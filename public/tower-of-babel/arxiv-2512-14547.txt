Lie rings related to the p p -groups of maximal class
Bettina Eick, Patali Komma, Subhrajyoti Saha

Abstract
The Lazard correspondence induces a close relation between the p p -groups of maximal class and a certain type of Lie ring constructed from p p -adic number fields. Our aim here is to investigate such Lie rings. In particular, we show that they are always finite. It then follows that they are nilpotent of small class. These results close an important gap in (Eick, Komma & Saha 2025). † † Acknowledgement: The second author was supported by an Alexander von Humboldt Foundation Research Fellowship. The third author acknowledges the support of the Royal Society through Research Grant RGS \R2 \252076

Introduction

The classification of
p
-groups of maximal class is a long-standing
project in group theory. It has many interesting and deep results so far
and also many open problems still left. For small primes
p\in\{2,3\}
,
a complete classification was achieved by Blackburn
[
1
]
.
Hence we focus on the case
p\geq 5
here. We refer to Leedham-Green &
McKay
[
7
]
for an introduction to the state of the art, references
and background.

The
p
-groups of maximal class can be visualized via their associated graph

\mathcal{G}(p)
: its vertices correspond one-to-one to the infinitely many
isomorphism types of
p
-groups of maximal class and there is an edge

G\rightarrow H
if
H/Z(H)\cong G
holds. It is known that
\mathcal{G}(p)

consists of an isolated point and an infinite tree
\mathcal{T}
having a unique
infinite path, called its mainline. We denote this infinite path by
S_{2}\rightarrow S_{3}\rightarrow\ldots
with
S_{i}
being a group of order
p^{i}
. The
i
th
branch
\mathcal{B}_{i}
of
\mathcal{T}
is the subtree consisting of all descendants of
S_{i}

that are not descendants of
S_{i+1}
; It is a finite tree. The graph

\mathcal{G}(p)
consists of its isolated
point, its mainline and its sequence of branches
\mathcal{B}_{2},\mathcal{B}_{3},\ldots
.
Understanding the branches is the main problem in the classification
of
p
-groups of maximal class.

Let
G
be a group in a branch
\mathcal{B}_{i}
for
i\geq p-1
and let
P(G)
be its
two-step centralizer; that is, the centralizer in
G
of the lower central
series quotient
\gamma_{2}(G)/\gamma_{4}(G)
. Then
P(G)
is a maximal subgroup
of
G
. By Shepard
[
8
]
, the subgroup
P(G)
has class at most

(p-1)/2
. Thus the Lazard correspondence applies to
P(G)
yielding a Lie
ring
L(P(G))
. A central aim is to understand these Lie rings.

In
[
3
]
it is shown that, if
G
is not a leaf in its branch

\mathcal{B}_{i}
, then its Lie ring
L(P(G))
arises from a construction based on

p
-adic number theory as we outline in the following.

1.1
Construction of Lie
p
-rings

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\theta)
where
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the field of
p
-adic rationals
and
\theta
is a primitive
p
-th root of unity. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the maximal order
in
K
and note that
\mathcal{O}
has a unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
generated by

\kappa=\theta-1
. The ring
\mathcal{O}
has a unique chain of ideals
\mathcal{O}=\mathfrak{p}^{0}>\mathfrak{p}^{1}>\mathfrak{p}^{2}>\ldots>\{0\}
, with
\mathfrak{p}^{i}
generated by
\kappa^{i}
and
has index
p^{i}
in
\mathcal{O}
. We consider
\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i}
as
\theta
-module
under diagonal action and define

H_{i}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i},\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1})\;\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\;\hat{H}_{i}=\{\gamma\in H_{i}\mid\gamma\mbox{ surjective }\}.

For
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
and
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{p}^{i}
write

J_{\gamma}(u,v,w)=\gamma(\gamma(u\wedge v)\wedge w)+\gamma(\gamma(v\wedge w)\wedge u)+\gamma(\gamma(w\wedge u)\wedge v).

Define
J(\gamma)
as the ideal in
\mathcal{O}
generated by the set

\{J_{\gamma}(u,v,w)\mid u,v,w\in\mathfrak{p}^{i}\}
. Then there exists

\lambda(\gamma)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
so that

J(\gamma)=\mathfrak{p}^{\lambda(\gamma)};

where
\mathfrak{p}^{\infty}=\{0\}
. We define
L_{i,m}(\gamma)

as the set
\mathfrak{p}^{i}/\mathfrak{p}^{m}
with natural addition and multiplication

[x+\mathfrak{p}^{m},y+\mathfrak{p}^{m}]=\gamma(x\wedge y)+\mathfrak{p}^{m}
. If
m\leq\lambda(\gamma)
, then

L_{i,m}(\gamma)
is a Lie ring, and it has order
p^{m-i}
. A central
result in
[
3
]
states the following.

1

Theorem:

(EKS
[
3
]
)

Let
p\geq 5
, let
i>p+1
and let
G
a group in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
so that
G

is not a leaf. Write
P
for the two-step centralizer in
G
and
m=\log_{p}|P|+i
. Then there exists
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
, so that

L(P)\cong L_{i,m}(\gamma).

1.2
Investigation of Lie
p
-rings

Our aim is now to understand the Lie rings
L_{i,m}(\gamma)
as arising
in Theorem
1
. These satisfy
m\leq\lambda(\gamma)
and are
quotients of
L_{i,\lambda(\gamma)}(\gamma)
. A first step is now to
investigate
\lambda(\gamma)
. Note that if
L_{i,\lambda(\gamma)}(\gamma)

is finite, then it has size
p^{\lambda(\gamma)-i}
by construction. Our
central result here is the following, see Section
5.3
for a proof
including explicit bounds on
y
.

2

Theorem:

(Main Theorem I)

Let
p\geq 5
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
If
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
and
\lambda=\lambda(\gamma)
, then

L_{i,\lambda}(\gamma)
is finite; more precisely, there exists

y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
\lambda=3i+13-2p+y
or, equivalently,
there exists
y\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with

|L_{i,\lambda}(\gamma)|=p^{2i+13-2p+y}.

By
[
3
, Th. 1]
, the finiteness of
L_{i,\lambda}(\gamma)

implies that
L_{i,\lambda}(\gamma)
is nilpotent and, if
i>p-1
, then
the class of
L_{i,\lambda}(\gamma)
is at most
p-1
. In the later case,
the Lazard correspondence applies defining a group
G(L)
for each of the
arising Lie rings
L
. This induces the following result.

3

Corollary:

Let
p\geq 5
and
i>p+1
. Write
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\{L_{i,m}(\gamma)\mid\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i},m\leq\lambda(\gamma)\}
and let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
the set of
non-leafs in
\mathcal{B}_{i}
. Then the Lazard correspondence induces a surjection

\mathcal{L}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{i}:L\rightarrow G(L)\rtimes\langle\theta\rangle,

where
\theta
acts by multiplication on
\mathcal{O}
and thus on
L
and on
G(L)
.

1.3
Special cases

For
j\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to
p
let
\sigma_{j}
be the Galois automorphism
of
K
mapping
\theta
to
\theta^{j}
. For
a\in\{2,\ldots,(p-1)/2\}

define
\vartheta_{a}:K\wedge K\rightarrow K
via

\vartheta_{a}(x\wedge y)=\sigma_{a}(x)\sigma_{1-a}(y)-\sigma_{1-a}(x)\sigma_{a}(y).

As observed in
[
3
, Lemma 6]
,
\vartheta_{a}\in\hat{H}_{i}
for each

i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and the homomorphisms
\vartheta_{a}
span
\hat{H}_{i}
over
K
.
If
p=5
, then it is sufficient to choose
\gamma=c\vartheta_{2}
for
some
c\in\mathcal{U}
, the unit group of
\mathcal{O}
. We thus obtain the following
special cases.

4

Theorem:

(Main Theorem II)

Let
p\geq 5
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
\gamma=c\vartheta_{a}
for some

c\in\mathcal{U}
and
2\leq a\leq(p-1)/2
and write
\lambda=\lambda(\gamma)
. Then
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
and

|L_{i,\lambda}(\gamma)|=p^{2i+3+y}
for some
y\in\{0,1,2\}
.
If
p=5
, then
y=0
holds.

GAP experiments suggest that
y\in\{0,2\}
if
\gamma=\vartheta_{a}
.
While the case
y=2
does not arise for
p=5
, it does arise for larger
primes. For example:

\bullet

if
p=7
, then
y=2
if
(a,i)\in\{(2,3),(3,2),(3,5)\}
.

\bullet

if
p=11
, then
y=2
if
(a,i)\in\{(4,10)\}
.

\bullet

if
p=13
, then
y=2
if
(a,i)\in\{(2,4),(4,2),(4,5),(4,8),(4,11),(5,4),(6,3),(6,9)\}
.

2
Preliminaries on number theory

This section recalls some preliminaries on number theory.
As before, let
K=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\theta)
, where
\theta
is a primitive
p
-root of
unity, let
\mathcal{O}
be the maximal order in
K
and let
\mathfrak{p}
denote the unique
maximal ideal in
\mathcal{O}
. Then
\kappa
generates
\mathfrak{p}
as ideal and
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathfrak{p}
are the units in
\mathcal{O}
.

5

Definition:

For
v\in K
we define the valuation of
v
as
val(v)=n
if
v\in\mathfrak{p}^{n}\setminus\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}

and we define
val(0)=\infty
. For a vector
v
over
K
we define

val(v)
as the minimum of the valuations of the entries of
v
.

The ideal
\mathfrak{p}^{i}
of
\mathcal{O}
has the natural
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis
\{\theta^{j}\kappa^{i}\mid 0\leq j<p-1\}
. In applications, it is often useful to consider a
different basis as in the following remark.

6

Remark:

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
d=p-1
the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-dimension of
\mathcal{O}
.

(a)

The set
\{\kappa^{j}\mid i\leq j<i+d\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis for
\mathfrak{p}^{i}
.

(b)

The set
\{\kappa^{j}\wedge\kappa^{k}\mid i\leq j<k<i+d\}
generates

\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i}
as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

(c)

\kappa^{d}\equiv-p\bmod\mathfrak{p}^{d+1}
.

Proof
:
(a) and (b) are well-known and it remains to prove (c). First note that

\theta^{r}=(1+\kappa)^{r}=1+r\kappa+{{r}\choose{2}}\kappa^{2}+\cdots+{{r}\choose{r}}\kappa^{r}
for
1\leq r\leq p-1
and

\sum_{r=0}^{p-1}{{p-1}\choose{r}}={{p}\choose{r+1}}
.
As
p\in\mathfrak{p}^{p}
, it follows that

0=1+\theta+\cdots+\theta^{p-1}=p+\binom{p}{2}\kappa+\cdots+\binom{p}{p}\kappa^{p-1}\equiv p+\kappa^{p-1}\bmod{\mathfrak{p}^{p}}.

As
d=p-1
, this yields the result of (c).

\bullet

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be a primitive
(p-1)
-th root of unity
and
r
be the generator of the multiplicative group of integers

(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}
such that
\omega-r\in\mathfrak{p}
. Then let

\sigma=\sigma_{r}
and note that
\sigma
generates
Gal(K)
.
The following is proved in
[
6
, Section 2]
.

7

Theorem:

(a)

The eigenvalues of
\sigma
are
1,\omega,\ldots,\omega^{p-2}
, with
each eigenspace of dimension
1
.

(b)

If
v\in K
is an eigenvector of
\sigma
, then the corresponding
eigenvalue is
\omega^{val(v)}
.

(c)

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists an eigenvector
v\in\mathfrak{p}^{n}\setminus\mathfrak{p}^{n+1}
of
\sigma
.

The following result is an easy consequence.

8

Corollary:

Let
v
be an eigenvector of
\sigma
with
val(v)=n
. Then
v
is an
eigenvector of
\sigma^{m}
with eigenvalue
\omega^{mn}
. Further if

a\in\{2,\ldots(p-1)/2\}
with
\sigma_{a}=\sigma^{k}
and

\sigma_{1-a}=\sigma^{l}
for some integers
k
and
l
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}(v)=\omega^{kn}v,\quad\sigma_{1-a}(v)=\omega^{ln}v,\quad\sigma_{a^{2}}(v)=\omega^{2kn}v,\quad

\displaystyle\sigma_{(1-a)^{2}}=\omega^{2ln}\omega,\quad\sigma_{a(1-a)}(v)=\omega^{(k+l)n}v.

Proof
:
Let
v
be an eigenvector of
\sigma
with
val(v)=n
. Then

\sigma(v)=\omega^{n}v
, and
\sigma^{2}(v)=\sigma(\omega^{n}v)
.
As
\omega\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is fixed by
\sigma
, it follows that

\sigma^{2}(v)=\omega^{n}\sigma(v)=\omega^{2n}v
. Iterating this idea,
it follows that
v
is an eigenvector of
\sigma^{m}
with eigenvalue

\omega^{mn}
. Using the definitions of
l
and
k
, the result
follows.

\bullet

9

Lemma:

The group
Gal(K)
acts trivially on
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof
:
Each element
c\in\mathcal{O}
can be written as
c=\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\kappa^{r}

for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
a_{r}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
. Thus
c\equiv a_{0}\bmod\mathfrak{p}
and
\sigma(c)=\sum_{r=0}^{m}a_{r}\sigma(\kappa)^{r}\equiv a_{0}\bmod\mathfrak{p}
.

3
Preliminaries on homomorphism spaces

Throughout, we assume that
p\geq 5
is prime and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Our aim in
this section is to provide background on the space

\hat{H}_{i}\subseteq H_{i}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i},\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1}).

The structure of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathcal{O}\wedge\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
was analysed by Leedham-Green
& McKay, see
[
7
]
. Based on this, Dietrich & Eick
[
2
]

proved that
every element of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathcal{O}\wedge\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
can be written uniquely
as
\sum_{a=2}^{(p-1)/2}c_{a}\vartheta_{a}
with coefficients
c_{a}\in\mathfrak{p}^{4-p}
. The following remark translates this to
\hat{H}_{i}
.

10

Remark:

(a)

\gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i},\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1})
induces

\hat{\gamma}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathcal{O}\wedge\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
via

\hat{\gamma}:\mathcal{O}\wedge\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}:(x\wedge y)\mapsto\kappa^{-(2i+1)}\gamma(\kappa^{i}x\wedge\kappa^{i}y).

(b)

\delta\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathcal{O}\wedge\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
induces

\overline{\delta}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i},\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1})
via

\overline{\delta}:\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i}\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1}:(x\wedge y)\mapsto\kappa^{(2i+1)}\delta(\kappa^{-i}x\wedge\kappa^{-i}y).

(c)

The two maps are inverse to each other and hence induce isomorphisms between

\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathcal{O}\wedge\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\theta}(\mathfrak{p}^{i}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{i},\mathfrak{p}^{2i+1})
.

11

Lemma:

Each
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
can be written uniquely in the form

\gamma=\sum_{a=2}^{(p-1)/2}c_{a}\vartheta_{a}
with
c_{a}\in K
and
val(c_{a})\geq 5-p
.

Proof
:
Let
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
. Then
\hat{\gamma}=\sum c_{a}\vartheta_{a}
and

\gamma=\overline{\hat{\gamma}}
. As
\vartheta_{a}(\kappa^{-i}x\wedge\kappa^{-i}y)=\kappa^{-2i}u_{a,i}\vartheta_{a}(x\wedge y)
for some
unit
u_{a,i}
, it follows that
\gamma=\sum_{a}\kappa c_{a}u_{a,i}\vartheta_{a}
and the valuation of the coefficients in this sum is
given by
val(\kappa c_{a}u_{a,i})=val(c_{a})+1\geq 5-p
.

\bullet

For
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
we write
val(\gamma)=\min\{val(c_{a})\mid 2\leq a\leq(p-1)/2\}
. Lemma
11
has the following
corollary.

12

Corollary:

Let
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
with
v=val(\gamma)
.

(a)

0\geq v\geq 5-p
.

(b)

\gamma(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{k})\in\mathfrak{p}^{j+k+v}
for each
j,k\geq i
.

Proof
:
Write
\gamma=\sum c_{a}\vartheta_{a}
.

(a) This follows from Lemma
11
. Note that not all
coefficients
c_{a}
can be in
\mathfrak{p}
.

(b) Note that
\vartheta_{a}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{k})\leq\mathfrak{p}^{j+k}
for all
j,k
.
Thus
c_{a}\vartheta_{a}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{k})\in\mathfrak{p}^{j+k+v}
for all
a,j,k
.

4
A lower bound

Throughout this section we assume that
p\geq 5
is prime and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
and write
\gamma=\sum c_{a}\vartheta_{a}

and
\lambda=\lambda(\gamma)
.
Recall that
J(\gamma)=\mathfrak{p}^{\lambda}
. This section introduces a
lower bound for
\lambda
.

For each
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
e_{x}
be an eigenvector of
\sigma
with

val(e_{x})=x
. Then
\sigma(e_{x})=\omega^{x}e_{x}
. Recall that
\omega\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a primitive
(p-1)
-th root of unity with
\omega\equiv r\mod p
. Let
\sigma_{a}=\sigma^{k_{a}}
and
\sigma_{1-a}=\sigma^{l_{a}}

for certain
k_{a},l_{a}\in\mathbb{N}
.
The next three results follow from direct computations.

13

Lemma:

For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}
define
m_{a}(u,v)=\omega^{uk_{a}+vl_{a}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
For
c\in K
it follows that

\vartheta_{a}(ce_{x}\wedge e_{y})=(\sigma_{a}(c)m_{a}(x,y)-\sigma_{1-a}(c)m_{a}(y,x))e_{x}e_{y}.

14

Lemma:

For
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}
define
t_{a}(x,y)=m_{a}(x,y)-m_{a}(y,x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma(\gamma(e_{x}\wedge e_{y})\wedge e_{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sum_{a,b}c_{a}\sigma_{a}(c_{b})t_{b}(x,y)m_{a}(x+y,z)-c_{a}\sigma_{1-a}(c_{b})t_{b}(x,y)m_{a}(z,x+y))e_{x}e_{y}e_{z}.

Define
l_{a,b}(x,y,z),r_{a,b}(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z)\in K
as

\displaystyle l_{a,b}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{b}(x,y)m_{a}(x+y,z)+t_{b}(y,z)m_{a}(y+z,x)+t_{b}(z,x)m_{a}(z+x,y),

\displaystyle r_{a,b}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{b}(x,y)m_{a}(z,x+y)+t_{b}(y,z)m_{a}(x,y+z)+t_{b}(z,x)m_{a}(y,z+x),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a,b}c_{a}\sigma_{a}(c_{b})l_{a,b}(x,y,z)-c_{a}\sigma_{1-a}(c_{b})r_{a,b}(x,y,z).

15

Lemma:

For each
x,y,z\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
it follows that

\displaystyle\gamma(\gamma(e_{x}\wedge e_{y})\wedge e_{z})+\gamma(\gamma(e_{y}\wedge e_{z})\wedge e_{x})+\gamma(\gamma(e_{z}\wedge e_{x})\wedge e_{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z)e_{x}e_{y}e_{z}.

The following theorem proves a first step towards Theorems
2

and
4
.

16

Theorem:

Let
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
with
v=val(\gamma)
and write
\lambda=\lambda(\gamma)
. Then
\lambda\geq 3i+3+2v
.
Thus
\lambda\geq 3i+13-2p
in general and
\lambda\geq 3i+3
if

\gamma=c\vartheta_{a}
.

Proof
:
Recall that
J(\gamma)=\mathfrak{p}^{\lambda}
. By Lemma
15
, it follows
that
J(\gamma)
is generated by
\{\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z)e_{x}e_{y}e_{z}\mid x>y>z\geq i\}
as ideal. Thus
\lambda=\min\{val(\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z))+x+y+z\mid x>y>z\geq i\}
. The term
\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z)
is combined of
elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and coefficients
c_{a}
and their images under the Galois
group. Recall that
5-p\leq v\leq 0
by Corollary
12
. Thus
all of these elements are in
\mathfrak{p}^{2v}
and hence

\Gamma_{\gamma}(x,y,z)\geq 2v
for all
x,y,z
. The minimal
choice for
x,y,z
is
i+i+1+i+2=3i+3
. This yields the desired result.

5
Upper bounds

Throughout this section we assume that
p\geq 5
is prime and

i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We consider
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
and write
\gamma=\sum c_{a}\vartheta_{a}
and
\lambda=\lambda(\gamma)
. Recall that
J(\gamma)=\mathfrak{p}^{\lambda}
. This section
shows that
\lambda
is finite and it introduces an upper bound for it.

5.1
The offset of a homomorphism

Our first aim is to analyse the images
\gamma(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{k})

for all
j,k\geq i
. We define
\rho(j,k)\in\mathbb{Z}
via

\gamma(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\wedge\mathfrak{p}^{k})=\mathfrak{p}^{j+k+\rho(j,k)}.

Recall that
d=p-1
is the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-dimension of each
\mathfrak{p}^{j}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{j+d}=p\mathfrak{p}^{j}
holds. Thus for each
j,k\geq i
it follows that

\rho(j,k)=\rho(j+d,k)=\rho(j,k+d).

Hence there are only finitely many different values in the set

\{\rho(j,k)\mid j,k\geq i\}
; This is used in the first
part of the following definition. Further, recall that every element

x\in K
of valuation
v
can be written (uniquely) as

x=\sum_{j\geq v}a_{j}\kappa^{j}
with
a_{j}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
.

17

Definition:

Let
\gamma\in\hat{H}_{i}
.

(a)

The offset
\rho(\gamma)
of

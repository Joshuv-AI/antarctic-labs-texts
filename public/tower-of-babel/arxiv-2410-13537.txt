A Local Method for Compact and Non-compact Yamabe Problems
Jie Xu

Abstract
. Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a compact manifold or a complete non-compact manifold without boundary, dim M ⩾ 4 \dim M\geqslant 4 , and not locally conformally flat. In this article, we introduce a new local method to resolve the Yamabe problem on compact manifold for dimensions at least 4 4 , and the Yamabe problem on non-compact complete manifolds without boundary, which are pointwise conformal to subsets of some compact manifolds. In particular, the new local method applies to the hard cases–the Yamabe constants are positive. Our local method also generalizes Brezis and Nirenberg’s nonlinear eigenvalue problem to subsets of manifolds.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold (without boundary) or a complete non-compact manifold without boundary,
\dim M\geqslant 3
. A higher dimensional generalization of the classical uniformization theorem on compact Riemann surfaces is the Yamabe problem.

The Yamabe Conjecture.

Given a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
n\geqslant 3
, there exists a metric conformal to
g
with constant scalar curvature.

The conformal transformation of the original metric is of the form
\tilde{g}=e^{2f}g
for
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
. Set
e^{2f}=u^{p-2}
with
u>0,u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M)
. Denote
\Delta_{g}=-d^{*}d
to be negative definite Laplacian, and
R_{g}
the scalar curvature, the scalar curvature for the new metric
\tilde{g}
under conformal transformation is given by

(1)

R_{\tilde{g}}=u^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\left(-4\cdot\frac{n-1}{n-2}\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u\right).

Denote
a=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}
and
p=\frac{2n}{n-2}
throughout this article, the new metric
\tilde{g}=u^{p-2}g
has constant scalar curvature
\lambda
if and only if the positive, smooth function
u
satisfies the Yamabe equation

(2)

-a\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u=\lambda u^{p-1}\;{\rm on}\;M.

It was solved in several steps by important work of Yamabe, Trudinger, Aubin and Schoen, with the final cases solved in 1984. A thorough presentation of the historical development of the solution is in
[
14
]
. We say that a manifold is locally conformally flat if the Riemannian metric
g
is locally pointwise conformal to the Euclidean metric. Yamabe, Trudinger and Aubin
[
14
, Thm. A]
converted the PDE problem to an algebraic problem in terms of the Yamabe quotient and Yamabe constant. The Yamabe quotient on compact manifold or complete non-compact manifold is defined to be the functional

Q(u):=\frac{a\int_{M}\lvert\nabla_{g}u\rvert^{2}d\text{Vol}_{g}+\int_{M}R_{g}u^{2}d\text{Vol}_{g}}{\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}(M,g)}^{2}},\forall u\in H^{1}(M,g)\cap\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(M).

Here
d\text{Vol}_{g}
is the volume form on
(M,g)
. The Yamabe constant is

\lambda(M):=\inf_{u\in H^{1}(M,g)\cap\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(M)}Q(u).

We also define the Yamabe invariant at infinity for non-compact
M
as

\lambda_{\infty}(M):=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lambda(M\backslash K_{i})

for any compact exhaustion
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
M
. Each
\lambda(M\backslash K_{i})
is the Yamabe constant of the non-compact manifold
M\backslash K_{i}
. Kim
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
showed that this definition is independent of the choice of compact exhaustion.

In this article, we are interested in the hard cases for both compact and non-compact manifolds, i.e. those manifolds admit
\lambda(M)>0
. We point out that our local method applies to the easy cases also. For compact manifolds with
\lambda(M)\leqslant 0
, we refer to

]
. There are also results various Yamabe-type problems for manifolds with boundary

,

]
and non-compact manifolds

,

with certain restrictions, etc. On non-compact manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature, we need the notion of Yamabe invariant at infinity, see e.g.

When
\dim M\geqslant 6
and
M
is not locally conformally flat, Aubin
[
14
, Thm. B]
picked up an appropriate local test function, supported in some small chart on which an appropriate local coordinates was chosen, such that
\lambda(M)<\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{n})
; therefore the Yamabe equation (
2
) admits a solution. Aubin’s method is a local method. Note that the local method is associated with the Yamabe equation

-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u=\lambda u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\;{\rm in}\;\Omega,u=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial\Omega.

in some domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with some metric
g
, and for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Precisely speaking, we show by local variational method that the existence of a positive, smooth solution of the PDE above in some small enough
\Omega
is followed from the existence of some local test function
\phi
such that
Q(\phi)<\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. The Green’s function of the conformal Laplacian
-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u
and the critical Sobolev embedding characterize the “blow-up” phenomena.

When
\dim M\leqslant 5
or
M
is locally conformally flat, Schoen
[
14
, Thm. C]
proved the Yamabe problem by positive mass theorem. Schoen’s method is a global method. The local and global methods are exclusive. i.e local test function cannot be applied to resolve the low dimensional or local conformally flat cases, and vice versa.

Our first main result, concerns the compact manifold
(M,g)
, says

Theorem 1.1
.

The Yamabe problem on not-locally-conformally-flat compact manifolds
(M,g)
with
\dim M\geqslant 4
and positive Yamabe invariant can be resolved by a local method.

Looking into the exclusive local and global methods, we observe that there is a dimensional borderline–the dimension
6
: the global methods were applied when the dimension is too low. There is also a geometric borderline-the vanishing/non-vanishing of the Weyl tensor: the global methods were applied when the Weyl tensor vanishes identically. The topological and geometrical obstructions of applying local methods agree in dimension
3
, since the Weyl tensor is trivial in dimension
3
. Theorem
1.1
says the global topological invariant-the dimension-is dominant in dimension
3
. For manifolds with dimension at least
4
, only the geometry classifies the local and global methods.

The new local method is different from Aubin’s local method. Historically, Aubin’s test function

u_{\epsilon,\Omega}(x)=\frac{\varphi(x)}{\left(\epsilon+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}

has been applied locally with conformal normal coordinates—a special local coordinates constructed by the Green’s function of the conformal Laplacian. The choice of
\epsilon
depends on the size of
\Omega
. We only require the standard local normal coordinates to apply our new local test function. In other words, we do not need the existence of the Green’s function of the conformal Laplacian. The new test function is “damping” the blow-up phenomena of the Green’s function. Brezis and Nirenberg
[
5
]
introduced the “correction term” to do the same thing in Euclidean geometry. It indicates the application of the local method to the Yamabe problem on non-compact manifold. Our second main result concerns the non-compact case, in particular the “positively curved” spaces with relatively weak geometric hypotheses “near infinity”:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete non-compact manifold (without boundary) of
\dim M\geqslant 4
such that
R_{g}>0
somewhere. In addition we assume that
(M,g)
is pointwise conformal to some subdomain of some compact manifold. Fix a point
O\in M
. Suppose that there exists a positive constant
C
such that
R_{g}\geqslant-Cd(x)^{2}
when
d(x):=d_{g}(x,O)
is large. Suppose also that there exists a constant
\rho_{0}(n,\lambda(M),\lambda_{\infty}(M))>0
such that, if
\text{Vol}_{g}(B(O,r))\leqslant Cr^{n+\rho}
for all large
r
and some
\rho<\rho_{0}
. If
(M,g)
is not locally conformally flat, then there exists a metric, pointwise conformal to
g
, admitting a positive constant scalar curvature, provided that
\lambda(M)>0
.

Again, the local method approach for non-compact manifolds applies in particular to the low dimensional cases as
\dim M=4
or
5
. Previously, those cases are hard to resolve, since there were no local methods for manifolds with dimension less than
6
, nor appropriate global methods, which usually require Ricci curvature lower bounds, existence of the Green’s function of the conformal Laplacian and positive mass theorem.

We would like to explain how the new local method reduces the lowest applicable dimension from
6
to
4
intuitively. Assume that the local region is some ball
B_{0}(r)
parametrized by normal coordinates. The nonnegative cut-off function
\varphi(x)
is usually a radial function and is equal to
1
within
B_{\frac{r}{2}}(0)
. Observe that

(3)

-\Delta_{g_{e}}\frac{1}{\left(\epsilon+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}=\epsilon n(n-2)\left(\frac{1}{\left(\epsilon+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\right)^{p-1}.

In the Yamabe quotient, the dominant term is the term
\lVert\nabla_{g}u\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(M,g)}
. Note that the cut-off function has no power when
\frac{1}{\left(\epsilon+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}
is large near the center of the ball, therefore the term
\lVert\nabla_{g}u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(M,g)}
is almost the same as
\lVert\nabla_{g}\left(\frac{1}{\left(\epsilon+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\right)\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(M,g)}
. Due to the nonlinear eigenvalue equation (
3
), Aubin was really testing
\nabla_{g}\left(\Delta_{g_{e}}\frac{1}{\left(\epsilon+\lvert x\rvert^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}\right)^{1-p}
, i.e. he was estimating the third order derivative of the classical test function. Aubin’s local method was able to control the third order derivative of the test function as low as dimension
6
, therefore it is plausible to get some variation of the original test function
\phi^{\prime}
and obtain
Q(\phi^{\prime})<\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{n})
up to dimension
4
.

Another inspiration of our new local method comes from Brezis and Nirenberg
[
5
]
. With the Euclidean geometry, they gave remarkable results with respect to the existence of positive solutions of the nonlinear eigenvalue problem

-\Delta_{e}u=\lambda u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}+\gamma u\;{\rm in}\;\Omega,u=0\;{\rm on}\;\Omega

on bounded region
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geqslant 3
for positive constants
\lambda,\gamma
. Here
-\Delta_{e}
is the positive definite Euclidean Laplacian. The extra term
\gamma u
is applied to dampen the “blow-up” phenomena characterized by the Green’s function of the Laplacian and the critical Sobolev embedding.

It seems unrelated between the equation above and the Yamabe equation. Nevertheless, this equation has a missing geometric term: with necessary scalings of
\lambda
and
\gamma
, we can rewrite the equation above as

(4)

-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{e}u+R_{e}u=\lambda u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}+\gamma u\;{\rm in}\;\Omega,u=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial\Omega.

The differential operator is thus the conformal Laplacian with trivial
R_{e}\equiv 0
. Therefore Brezis and Nirenberg were really solving a Yamabe-type equation by a local approach for locally conformally flat case. The necessity of this “correction” term
\gamma u
is due to the Pohazaev identity. Hence the new equation (
4
) indicates a possible extension to the curved space, or the manifold scenario. As key steps, our local analysis starts with the existence of positive, smooth solution of (
4
) on subsets of compact Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
accompanying with the correction term
\gamma
. With non-Euclidean metric,
R_{e}
becomes
R_{g}
, the scalar curvature of the metric
g
; and
\Delta_{e}
becomes
\Delta_{g}
, the Laplace-Beltrami operator. Throughout this article, we denote
(\Omega,g)
be a Riemannian domain if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is some open subset equipped with some Riemannian metric
g
. Our first key proposition is given below:

Proposition 1.1
.

Let

equipped a Riemannian metric

such that the Weyl tensor of

does not vanish identically and

on

\Omega

-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u=\lambda u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}+\gamma u\;{\rm in}\;(\Omega,g),u=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial\Omega.

has a positive, smooth solution

2.
The Perturbed Local Yamabe Equation with Dirichlet Boundary Condition

In this section, we extend Brezis and Nirenberg’s local analysis result on open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geqslant 4
to the Riemannian domain
(\Omega,g),\dim\Omega\geqslant 4
, i.e. we show that for any constants
\beta,\lambda>0
, the following perturbed Yamabe equation

(7)

-a\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u-\beta u=\lambda u^{p-1}\;{\rm in}\;\Omega,u\equiv 0\;{\rm on}\;\partial\Omega.

has a positive, smooth solution on some Riemannian domains
(\Omega,g)
such that the Weyl tensor on
\Omega
does not vanish identically. We further assume that
g
has a global parametrization on
\Omega
. In addition, we assume that
R_{g}<0
everywhere in
\Omega
.

Using variational method, the problem is reduced to verify the inequality (
18
) below. We show in this section and Appendix A that Aubin’s test function works. Aubin’s test function has two restrictions:

(i).

The dimension of the space must be at least
6
;

(ii).

We need a special local coordinate system.

We will show in this section and Appendix A that both restrictions can be removed, but we need to add a “correction” term, as Brezis and Nirenberg indicated in
[
5
]
. In a word, Aubin’s local method can be applied to spaces with dimensions as low as
4
, and the special choice of coordinates are not required. Since the estimates in Lemma

Given a general second order linear elliptic PDE with the Dirichlet boundary condition:

(8)

\begin{split}Lu&:=-\sum_{i,j}\partial_{i}\left(a_{ij}(x)\partial_{j}u\right)=b(x)u^{p-1}+f(x,u)\;{\rm in}\;\Omega;\\
u&>0\;{\rm in}\;\Omega,u=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial\Omega.\end{split}

with

the critical Sobolev exponent with respect to the

-solutions of (

J(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(x)\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}u-\frac{b(x)}{p}u_{+}^{p}-F(x,u)\right)dx,

with appropriate choices of

Note that although the second order differential operator in (

Set

\begin{split}A(O)&=\text{essinf}_{x\in O}\frac{\det(a_{ij}(x))}{\lvert b(x)\rvert^{n-2}},\forall O\subset\Omega;\\

4.2.
Yamabe Problem on Non-Compact Manifolds

In this subsection, we discuss the Yamabe problem on non-compact manifolds
(M,g)
without boundary, with
\dim M\geqslant 3
. It is equivalent to find a positive, smooth solution of the following partial differential equation

(73)

-a\Delta_{g}u+R_{g}u=\lambda u^{p-1}\;{\rm on}\;M

for some constant
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. The negatively curved case has been resolved by Alives and McOwen
[
2
]
, they showed that for
(M,g)
either with
R_{g}\leqslant 0
everywhere and not identically zero and with lower Ricci bound, or with
R_{g}\leqslant\delta<0
everywhere, there exists a complete metric
\tilde{g}
in the conformal class
[g]
such that
R_{\tilde{g}}=-1
. A natural question is the Yamabe problem for positively curved non-compact spaces.

On compact manifolds (with or without boundary), the minimization of the Yamabe quotient (resp. relative Yamabe quotient) not only classifies the manifolds for metrics within conformal classes, but also convert the geometric problem of finding out a constant scalar curvature metric (cscK metric) to an algebraic problem of testing the Yamabe quotient. The minimization of the Yamabe quotient on compact manifolds is called the Yamabe invariant. It is natural to expect an analogy on non-compact manifolds.

Kim
[
11
,
20
]
defined a notion called the Yamabe invariant at infinity for non-compact manifold
(M,g)
: Taking a compact exhaustion
\{K_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
M
, each of which are bounded subsets, denote

(74)

\lambda_{\infty}(M):=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\lambda(M\backslash K_{i})

where
\lambda(M\backslash K_{i})
is defined to be

(75)

\lambda(M\backslash K_{i})=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(M\backslash K_{i})}\frac{a\lVert\nabla_{g}u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{M\backslash K_{i}}R_{g}u^{2}d\text{Vol}_{g}}{\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}^{2}}.

The definition (
75
) for
M\backslash K_{i}
applies to any non-compact complete manifold, which is still denoted to be the Yamabe invariant. Wei
[
20
]
mentioned that the definition of
\lambda_{\infty}(M)
is independent of the choice of the compact exhaustion.

A basic fact about the Yamabe invariant at infinity and the usual Yamabe quotient says:

Proposition 4.2
.

[
20
, Lemma. 2.1]

For any complete noncompact manifold
(M,g)
,

(76)

-\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}\lVert(R_{g})_{-}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{\frac{n}{2}}}\leqslant\lambda(M)\leqslant\lambda_{\infty}(M)\leqslant\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

Here
(R_{g})_{-}
are the negative part of the scalar curvature function
R_{g}
.

By Proposition
4.2
, a manifold
(M,g)
has
\lambda_{\infty}(M)\geqslant 0
if
R_{g}\geqslant 0
everywhere. Analogous to the compact case, the sign of
\lambda_{\infty}(M)
also classifies the non-compact manifolds. In this section, we are mainly interested in the positively curved space, the hard case. We assume that
(M,g)
is a non-compact, complete Riemannian manifold with
\lambda_{\infty}(M)>0
, which is a generalization of positivity of scalar curvature.

Wei
[
20
]
stated the following result:

Theorem 4.2
.

[
20
, Thm. 1.1]

Let
(M,g)
be a complete non-compact manifold of
\dim M\geqslant 3
satisfying

(77)

\lambda(M)<\lambda_{\infty}(M),\lambda_{\infty}(M)>0.

Fix a point
O\in M
. Suppose that there exists a positive constant
C
such that
R_{g}\geqslant-Cd(x)^{2}
when
d(x):=d_{g}(x,O)
is large. Then there exists a constant

being positive,

It gives the existence result with lower bound of the scalar curvature, the volume growth control, and is classified by the sign of the Yamabe invariant. However, this technical result requires an algebraic condition (
77
). Very few concrete examples are known to have this condition. Let’s analyze the evaluation of
\lambda(M)
and
\lambda_{\infty}(M)
. The following result, due to Kim
[
12

Proposition 4.3
.

[
12

Let

be a complete non-compact manifold of
\dim M\geqslant 3

\lambda_{\infty}(M)<\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, then
(M,g)
is not pointwise conformal to a subdomain of any compact Riemannian
n

We would like to use Proposition
3.1
to give an upper bound

, which requires the negativity of the scalar curvature. The following result says it is always possible locally:

Proposition 4.4

By scaling we can, without loss of generality, assume that

Based on exactly the same construction in Proposition

By standard elliptic theory, we conclude that there exists

\lambda(M)\leqslant Q(\phi)<\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

With all global scalar curvature and volume growth hypotheses, we conclude by Theorem

∎

Remark 4.2
.

(i) We do not know whether the new metric that admits the constant scalar curvature is a complete metric or not.

(ii) Our result keeps the geometric restrictions at infinity as loose as possible, in particular, we do not impose any condition on Ricci or Riemannian curvature tensor, neither the injectivity radius, etc.

(iii) Assuming

(iv) One concrete example for a non-compact manifold that is conformally embedded into a compact manifold is when

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(n-2)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy-\frac{(n-2)\beta}{4(n-1)}\epsilon^{\frac{4-n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n-2}}dy+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{5-n}{2}}\right)+\Sigma_{2}}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3-n}{2}}\right)+\Sigma_{1}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-2)^{2}\epsilon^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy-\frac{(n-2)\beta}{4(n-1)}\epsilon^{\frac{4-n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n-2}}dy+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{5-n}{2}}\right)+\Sigma_{2}}{\left(\epsilon^{\frac{-n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3-n}{2}}\right)+\Sigma_{1}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{1}-\epsilon\frac{(n-2)\beta}{4(n-1)}K_{3}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}.

The last term above is due to the Taylor expansion of the function
f(x)=x^{\frac{2}{p}}
. To control the last terms in numerator and denominator above, respectively, we first recall that the ratio
\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}
is the square of the reciprocal of the best Sobolev constant of

\lVert u\rVert_{\mathcal{L}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leqslant E\lVert Du\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Rightarrow\inf_{E}E^{-2}=T=\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}.

Aubin and Talenti
[
1
]
showed that

T^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{n-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Recall that
\beta>0
, independent of
\epsilon
, thus
Q_{\epsilon,\Omega}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{K_{1}-\epsilon\frac{(n-2)\beta}{4(n-1)}K_{3}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{K_{1}+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)-\epsilon\cdot\frac{(n-2)\lvert\beta\rvert}{4(n-1)}\frac{K_{3}}{K_{2}}.

The last inequality is due to the fact that both
\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}
and
\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}
are bounded above and below by constant multiples of
\epsilon
. Lastly we must show that

(91)

\frac{K_{1}+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}\leqslant\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}.

By the best Sobolev constant, we observe that

\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}=T\Rightarrow K_{1}=K_{2}T.

Thus (
91
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{K_{1}+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n}}}\leqslant\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}\Leftrightarrow\frac{K_{1}+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}-\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}\leqslant 0

\displaystyle\frac{K_{2}^{2}T+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}K_{2}-K_{2}^{2}T-\frac{n-2}{n}K_{2}T\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}{\left(K_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\right)K_{2}}\leqslant 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}K_{2}-\frac{n-2}{n}K_{2}T\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\leqslant 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\left(-\epsilon^{\frac{4-n}{2}}\frac{R_{g}(0)(n-2)}{6}\frac{n-2}{n}\int_{B_{P}\left(\frac{r}{2}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy\right)K_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{n-2}{n}K_{2}T\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(-\epsilon^{\frac{2-n}{2}}\frac{R_{g}(0)}{6n}\int_{B_{P}\left(\frac{r}{2}\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy\right)K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\leqslant 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(n-2)-\frac{TK_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}{n}\leqslant 0.

Recall that the best Sobolev constant
T
is of the form

T=\pi n(n-2)\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(n)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}

For the term
K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}
, we have

K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}=\left(\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n}}dy\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}:=\left(\omega_{n}D_{1}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}

Here
\omega_{n}
is the surface area of the
(n-1)
-sphere. It is well known that

\omega_{n}=n\cdot\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}=n\cdot\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{n\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\Rightarrow\omega_{n}^{-\frac{2}{n}}=\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}=\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}2^{-\frac{2}{n}}\pi^{-1}.

For
D_{1}
, we use change of variables
r=\tan\theta
,
\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we have

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{n}}dy=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\tan^{n-1}(\theta)}{(1+\tan^{2}(\theta))^{n}}\sec^{2}(\theta)d\theta=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\sin^{n-1}(\theta)\sec^{n+1}(\theta)}{\sec^{2n}(\theta)}d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{n-1}(\theta)\cos^{n-1}\theta d\theta=2^{1-n}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{n-1}(2\theta)d\theta=2^{-n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}(x)dx.

We use
2\theta=x
in the last equality. It is well-known that the last integration above is symmetric with respect to
x=\frac{\pi}{2}
and is connected to the Gamma function. To be precise,

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=2^{-n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-1}(x)dx=2^{1-n}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{n-1}(x)dx=2^{1-n}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1+1}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+1\right)}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow D_{1}^{-\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle=\left(2^{n}\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}=2^{2}\pi^{-\frac{1}{n}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}.

Packing terms together, we have

\displaystyle\frac{TK_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}{n}

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\pi n(n-2)\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(n)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\cdot\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}2^{-\frac{2}{n}}\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot 2^{2}\pi^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle=(n-2)2^{2-\frac{2}{n}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}

The Legendre duplication formula for Gamma functions says

\Gamma\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(z\right)=2^{1-2z}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(2z).

Take
z=\frac{n}{2}
above, we have

\displaystyle\frac{TK_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}{n}

\displaystyle=(n-2)2^{2-\frac{2}{n}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle=(n-2)2^{2-\frac{2}{n}}\pi^{-\frac{1}{n}}\left(2^{1-2\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(n)\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\Gamma(n)\right)^{-\frac{2}{n}}

\displaystyle=(n-2).

Hence we conclude that

(n-2)-\frac{TK_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}{n}=(n-2)-(n-2)=0\Rightarrow\frac{K_{1}+\epsilon^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Sigma_{2}}{K_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n}\epsilon^{\frac{n}{2}}\Sigma_{1}K_{2}^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}}\leqslant\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}.

Therefore (
91
) holds. It follows that when
\epsilon
is small enough,

Q_{\epsilon,\Omega}\leqslant\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)-\epsilon\cdot\frac{(n-2)\lvert\beta\rvert}{4(n-1)}\frac{K_{3}}{K_{2}}<\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}=T.

Thus the inequality in (
83
) holds when
n\geqslant 5
.

Case II:
n=4
.
In this case, we keep notations to be the same as
n\geqslant 5
cases. Again we fix
r
. Most estimates of
A_{i,j},B_{i},B_{i,j},C_{i},C_{i,j}
with
n=4
are the same as above:

\displaystyle A_{0,1}

\displaystyle=O(1),A_{0,2}=O(1),A_{0,3}\leqslant 0,A_{0,4}\leqslant 0;

\displaystyle A_{1,1}

\displaystyle=O(1),\lvert A_{1,2}\rvert=O(1),A_{2,1}=O(1),\lvert A_{2,2}\rvert=O(1);A_{2,3}=O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right);

\displaystyle\lvert B_{1}\rvert

\displaystyle=O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right);B_{2}=O\left(\epsilon\right);B_{3}=O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right);

\displaystyle C_{0,1}

\displaystyle=O(1),C_{0,2}=O(1);C_{1}\geqslant 0,C_{2}=O\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Here
C_{1}>0
due to the fact that
R_{g}(0)<0
. We do not need
B_{0,1}
and
B_{0,2}
since the key term

\frac{1}{a}\cdot\left(R_{g}(0)-\beta\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert)^{2})^{2}}dy

is not integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Note that the term
A_{1,3}
with
n=4
can be estimated as

\displaystyle A_{1,3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{R_{g}(0)(n-2)^{2}}{6n}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}\lvert y\rvert^{4}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy=-\frac{R_{g}(0)}{6}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}\lvert y\rvert^{4}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{4}}dy

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{R_{g}(0)}{6}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{2}}dy

since the ratio
\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2}}<1
as always. Thus with
a=\frac{n-2}{4(n-1)}=\frac{1}{6}
when
n=4
, we can see

A_{1,3}+\frac{1}{a}\cdot\left(R_{g}(0)-\beta\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert)^{2})^{2}}dy\leqslant-\frac{\beta}{6}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert)^{2})^{2}}dy.

Applying exactly the same calculation as in
[
5
, Lemma. 1.1]
, we conclude that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert)^{2})^{2}}dy\geqslant L_{5}\lvert\log\epsilon\rvert

with some positive constant
L_{5}
independent of
\epsilon
. It follows that

(92)

A_{1,3}+\frac{1}{a}\cdot\left(R_{g}(0)-\beta\right)\int_{\Omega}\frac{\varphi_{\Omega}^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert)^{2})^{2}}dy\leqslant-L_{5}\lvert\beta\rvert\lvert\log\epsilon\rvert.

Applying (
92
) and all other estimates above, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{4}}dy-L_{5}\lvert\beta\rvert\lvert\log\epsilon\rvert+O(1)+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(\epsilon+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{4}}dy+O(1)+O\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{4\epsilon^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\lvert y\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{2}}dy-L_{5}\lvert\beta\rvert\lvert\log\epsilon\rvert+O\left(1\right)}{\left(\epsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+\lvert y\rvert^{2})^{n}}dy+O\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{K_{1}-L_{5}\lvert\beta\rvert\epsilon\lvert\log\epsilon\rvert+O\left(\epsilon\right)}{K_{2}+O\left(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{1}}{K_{2}}+O\left(\epsilon\right)-\frac{L_{5}\lvert\beta\rvert}{K_{2}}\epsilon\lvert\log\epsilon\rvert.

It follows that when
\epsilon
small enough, we have

Q_{\epsilon,\Omega}<T.

Thus (
83
) holds for
n=4
.

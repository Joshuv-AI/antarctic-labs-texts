Protocol to identify genes required for cardiomyocyte development using Perturb-Seq
Authors: Sushama Sivakumar, Lei Wang, Huan Zhao, Mpathi Nzima, David C Park, Yihan Wang, Anjana Sundarrajan, Chikara Takeuchi, Minnie Deng, W Lee Kraus, Gary C Hon, Nikhil V Munshi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryWhile Perturb-Seq combines CRISPR-based screening with single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), large-scale experiments are costly and its application during development is complicated by differentiation heterogeneity. Here, we present a protocol to identify genes required for cardiomyocyte development using Perturb-Seq. We describe steps for sgRNA (single guide RNA) library cloning and infection, cardiomyocyte differentiation, cell hashing, super loading, and scRNA-seq. We then detail procedures for sequencing, mapping, and data analysis.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Sivakumar et al.1Subject areas: Genomics, Sequencing, RNAseq, High Throughput Screening, Molecular Biology, Gene Expression, CRISPR, Stem Cells, Cell Differentiation

Before you begin

The protocol below is for performing CRISPR interference Perturb-Seq (CRISPRi followed by scRNA-seq) of all transcription factors (TF) in the human genome during cardiomyocyte (CM) differentiation. We have successfully used this protocol to analyze downstream consequences of TF repression during CM and neuron differentiation. Perturb-Seq can be expensive and time-consuming. To ensure success, we detail QC steps at specific time points. Additionally, we provide technical tools that minimize costs and maximize data recovery after scRNA-seq.

Perturb-Seq has been successfully performed in immortalized cell lines. In our previous study, we engineered pluripotent stem cell lines (PSCs) with constitutive or inducible expression of dCas9-KRAB (dCK) from a genomic safe harbor locus (classic KRAB-A domain). This allows stable expression of the CRISPRi machinery in a homogenous manner in our cell lines. Upon sgRNA introduction into our cells, we observed consistent repression of target genes to enable Perturb-Seq during developmental stages. Our resources and reagents are publicly available for the scientific community.

Innovation

This protocol describes steps needed to perform Perturb-Seq during in vitro differentiation from pluripotent stem cells. We detail QC steps we have adopted to ensure success. We list steps taken to maximize efficiency and minimize associated costs. Lastly, we detail how we use Pyspade, an analysis package, to computationally obtain perturbed transcriptomes after scRNA-seq.2 Github page with step by step details can be found at https://github.com/Hon-lab/Perturb-Seq-Processing-Pipeline.

This section describes procedures to construct next-generation sequencing libraries of sgRNAs, either from the plasmid library described earlier or from genomic DNA where sgRNAs have been integrated into cells. Infection of ESC/iPSC expressing dCas9 KRAB with lentiviral sgRNA library

Timing: ∼1 week

Measure titer of lentiviral sgRNA library and infect appropriate number of PSCs (expressing dCK) to obtain expected multiplicity of infection (MOI). We typically do MOI <=0.3 to get single sgRNA per cell. For larger sgRNA libraries, we aim for MOI of 3 that translates to 2–4 sgRNAs/cell. We only do higher MOI when sgRNA efficiency is low (when non-coding regions are targeted) or some sgRNA target genes that don’t function in cell type of interest (sgRNA library targets diverse genes that are not all functional in particular cell type).

Expected outcomes

The perturb-seq sgRNA library includes positive controls that repress target genes specifically but don’t alter differentiation and non-targeting controls that don’t repress any region in the human genome. We also include sgRNAs against cardiac specific genes which when repressed are known to alter cardiac differentiation. When pipeline is working efficiently, perturb-seq output from 1) non-targeting sgRNAs should show upregulation of cardiac specific markers such as Tnnt2, 2) positive control sgRNA should show repression of target gene and upregulation of cardiac specific markers, and 3) repression of cardiac specific genes should repress target gene and retrieve downstream differentially regulated transcripts. These controls ensure that biological and technical aspects of pipeline works and outputs from test gene repression can be validated and studied further. Example of Perturb-Seq outputs can be found in.1

Limitations

Perturb-Seq works well to identify regulators of developmental processes at distinct times. Perturbations that cause cell death will drop out from the screen and perturbations that promote cell proliferation will be enriched in cell number during this screen. Careful sgRNA coverage monitoring during the experiment is critical to obtain uniform numbers of perturbed cells after the screen. Perturb-seq is useful to understand cell autonomous effects, but non-cell autonomous effects are more difficult to capture during Perturb-Seq.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Too much cell death after lentiviral infection in part 4.

Potential solution

If viral titer is too high, it can cause death in PSCs. It is important to carefully titer the virus and infect at MOI that is low or medium to avoid cell death and preserve health of PSCs.

Problem 2

Cell death after single cell dissociation during FACS sorting in part 5.

Potential solution

PSCs are very sensitive to single cell dissociation and FACS sorting. We add CloneR2 for a few hours before preparing cells for sorting to ensure increased cell viability and better cell recovery after cell sorting.

Problem 3

Cardiomyocyte differentiation is not efficient in part 6.

Potential solution

Every PSC line requires optimization to differentiate into CMs. Usually, Chir99021 concentration varies by cell line and starting density. Testing different combinations of this will be useful to optimize differentiation.

Problem 4

Cardiomyocytes are not pelleting after collection in part 6.

Potential solution

Cardiomyocytes tend to stick to each other. Use of 10× TrypLE for 1 h followed by pipetting helps break down CMs into single cells. If this is not sufficient, one can incubate longer in TrypLE to potentiate single cell dissociation.

Problem 5

Recovery of cells after scRNA-seq that don’t have sgRNA in part 9.

Potential solution

This can be due to several reasons. After lentiviral library infection, it is important to select for cells that have lentivirus using selection markers on the lentivirus. Skipping this step can lead to PSCs that do not have sgRNA. Sorting differentiated cardiomyocytes for the presence of sgRNA before scRNA-seq ensures sequenced CMs have sgRNAs. Another reason is if sgRNA capture before scRNA-seq is inefficient than that can cause cell recovery without sgRNA. So, it is important to optimize sgRNA capture before doing a large-scale experiment.

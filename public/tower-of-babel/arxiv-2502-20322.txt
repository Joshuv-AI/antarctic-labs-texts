A density theorem for prime squares
Genheng Zhao

Abstract
. Let s ≥ 8 s\geq 8 be an integer and P P be a set of primes with relative lower density greater than 1 − min ⁡ { s , 16 } / 32 \sqrt{1-\min\{s,16\}/32} . We prove that every sufficiently large integer n ≡ s ⁡ ( mod ⁡ 24 ) n\equiv s(\operatorname{mod}24) can be represented by a sum of s s squares of primes in P P .



1.
Introduction and main results

In 1938, Hua
[
6
]
showed that for
s\geq 5
, every sufficiently large integer
n\equiv s(\operatorname{mod}24)
can be represented by a sum of
s
prime squares. We prove here a density version of this, but for a smaller range
s\geq 8
.

Let
\mathbb{\mathbb{P}}
denote the set of primes and
P\subset\mathbb{P}
, we define the lower density of
P
by

(1)

\delta_{P}=\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|\{n\leq N:n\in P\}|}{|\{n\leq N:n\in\mathbb{P}\}|}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\geq 8
be an integer and
P\subset\mathbb{P}
with
\delta_{P}>\sqrt{1-\min\{s,16\}/32}
. Then every sufficiently large integer
n\equiv s(\operatorname{mod}24)
can be represented by a sum of
s
squares of primes in
P
.

The main tool we utilize here is the transference principle developed by Green
[
1
]
, which allows us to consider our problem on pseudorandom sets. Roughly speaking, a set of integers is said to be pseudorandom if its members are distributed randomly among congruences of small modules. According to this criterion, the set of odd primes is of course not pseudorandom. An obviously way to gain pseudorandomness is by the
W
-trick, that is, to consider sets of the from
\{n:Wn+b\in\mathbb{P}\}
with
\prod_{p<w}p
and
(W,b)=1
. Using this method, Li-Pan
[
3
]
and Shao
[
9
]
succeeded in establishing density versions of Vinogradov’s theorem of three primes, with sharp density bounds.

As for the problem of prime squares, pseudorandom sets should be of the form
\{n:Wn+b\in\mathbb{P}^{2}\}
. Since
p^{2}\equiv 1(\operatorname{mod}24)
holds for all odd primes, we must have
24\mid W
. Thus the simplest module for prime squares to gain pseudorandomness is likely to be
W=8\prod_{2<p<w}p
. This module has appeared in the work of Browning-Prendiville
[
8
]
for transferring general squares but not prime squares.
We will show that it can be used to transfer prime squares as well. Based on this, we follow a refined transference argument of Salmensuu
[
5
]
to obtain a density theorem for prime squares.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section 2 we fix some notations. In Section 3 we use transference principle to prove
Theorem
1.1
, assuming that we can transfer prime squares by
W=8\prod_{2<p<w}p
. This will be verified in the subsequent two sections.

3.
Transference Argument

The first ingredient is the following transference lemma, which is a simple consequence of that of
[
5
]
. The reader shall keep in mind that this is actually the final step of the whole argument.

Lemma 3.1

(Transference Lemma)
.

Let
s\geq 2
,
M\geq 1
be integers. For each
j\in[s]
, let
f_{j}
and
\nu_{j}
be functions on
[M]
, satisfying

(i)

0\leq f_{j}\leq\nu_{j}
,

(ii)

(Pseudorandomness)
\|\hat{\nu}_{j}-\widehat{1_{[M]}}\|_{L^{\infty}}=o\left(M\right)
,

(iii)

(
q
-Restriction)
\|\hat{f}_{j}\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim_{q}M^{1-1/q}
holds for some
q\in(s-1,s)
.

Assume that for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, they satisfy the mean condition

\sum_{j\in[s]}\frac{\sum_{n\in[M]}f_{j}(n)}{M}\geq\frac{s}{2}+\epsilon

with

\frac{\sum_{n\in[M]}f_{j}(n)}{M}\geq\epsilon

for each
j\in[s]
.
Let
m
be an integer such that

(2)

m=\frac{s}{2}M+o(M).

Then for
M\gtrsim_{\epsilon}1
, there is a solution to

(3)

\begin{cases}m=\sum_{j\in[s]}n_{j},\\
\prod_{j\in[s]}f_{j}(n_{j})\neq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
M\gtrsim_{\epsilon}1
be such that all conditions of
[
5
, Proposition 3.9]
are satisfied. As a result of this, we have

\sum_{m=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots n_{s}}\prod_{j\in[s]}f_{j}(n_{j})\gtrsim_{\epsilon}N^{s-1}.

Since
m=\sum_{j\in[s]}n_{j}
has
O(M^{s-1})
solutions, by pigeonhole principle the above inequality ensures the existence of one solution for which

\prod_{j\in[s]}f_{j}(n_{j})\gtrsim_{\epsilon}1>0.

Lemma 3.4

Suppose

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l^{*}\in[q]^{*}\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{(2W)^{-1}a{l^{*}}^{2}}{q_{0}}\right)=G(q_{0},(2W)^{-1}a).

(iv)

When
q=2
we have
a=1
and only need to consider

S(2,1)=\frac{2}{H}\sum_{h\in H(b)}e\left(\frac{h^{2}-b}{2W}\right).

Write
W=8K
with
K=\prod_{2<p<w}p
. Then each
x\in[W]
is of the form
u+4Kv
with
v\in\{0,1\}
and
u\in[4K]
. Moreover, since

x^{2}\equiv u^{2}+8K(uv+2Kv)\equiv u^{2}(\operatorname{mod}W),

we obtain

S(2,1)=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in[4K]\\
u^{2}\equiv b(\operatorname{mod}W)\\
\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{u^{2}}{16K}\right)\sum_{v\in\{0,1\}}e\left(\frac{uv}{2}\right).

Note that
u^{2}\equiv b(\operatorname{mod}W)
implies
u
is odd. The above sum thus vanishes.

∎

Combing Lemma
4.2
and (
11
), the following estimate of
S(q,a)
is now available.

Corollary 4.3
.

For any
\epsilon>0
and
(a,q)=1
, we have
|S(q,a)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}q^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}
. In particular, when
(q,W)\nmid 2
or
q=2
, we have
S(q,a)=0
.

Proposition 4.4

(Major-arc estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(q,a)
with
q\leq Q
and
a\in[q]^{*}
. For any
\epsilon>0
we have

\hat{\nu}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{N}e((\alpha-1)u)du+O_{A}(N(\log N)^{-2A}),&q=1,\\
O_{A,\epsilon}(Nw^{-{1/2+\epsilon}}),&q\neq 1,\par\end{cases}

and

|\hat{\nu}(\alpha)|\lesssim_{A,\epsilon}Nq^{-{1/2}+\epsilon}(1+N|\alpha-a/q|)^{-1}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, we have

\hat{\nu}(\alpha)=\frac{\phi(W)}{\phi(qW)}S(q,a)\int_{0}^{N}e\left(\left(\alpha-a/q\right)u\right)du+O_{A}\left(N(\log N)^{-2A}\right).

By Corollary
4.3
, we have for any
\epsilon>0
that

\frac{\phi(W)}{\phi(qW)}S(q,a)=\begin{cases}1,&q=1,\\
0,&q\in(1,w),\\
O_{\epsilon}(q^{-1/2+\epsilon}),&otherwise.\end{cases}

From this we derive the desired asymptotic formula for
q=1
. While
q\neq 1
, this leads to

|\hat{\nu}(\alpha)|\lesssim_{\epsilon}w^{-1/2+\epsilon}N+N(\log N)^{-2A}\lesssim_{\epsilon,A}w^{-1/2+\epsilon}N,

since
w=O(\log\log\log N)
. As for the second claim, notice that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{N}e((\alpha-a/q)u)du&=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(n(\alpha-a/q))+O(|\alpha-a/q|N)\\
&\lesssim\min\{N,|\alpha-a/q|^{-1}\}+O((\log N)^{A})\\
&\lesssim N(1+N|\alpha-a/q|)^{-1},\end{split}

and

N(\log N)^{-2A}\leq N(\log N)^{-(1/2+\epsilon)A-A}\lesssim Nq^{-1/2+\epsilon}(1+N|\alpha-q/a|)^{-1}.

∎

Lemma 4.5

Suppose

\sigma_{0}\geq 1

(h,q)=1

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\equiv t(\operatorname{mod}W)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{hp^{2}}{q}\right)\lesssim\frac{X}{W(\log X)^{\sigma_{0}}}

holds for
\sigma\gtrsim\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{1}
.

Proof.

See
[
7
, Theorem 10]
.
∎

Proposition 4.6

(Minor-arc estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}
. Then

\hat{\nu}(\alpha)\lesssim\frac{N}{(\log N)^{B}}

holds for
A\gtrsim B
.

Proof.

By (
9
), it suffices to show this for

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\sqrt{WN+b}\\
p\equiv h(\operatorname{mod}W)\end{subarray}}(2p\log p)e\left(\frac{p^{2}\alpha}{W}\right).

By Dirichlet approximation theorem, there exists
q\leq N/Q
and
(a,q)=1
such that
|\alpha-a/q|\leq Q/qN
. Recall that we have
N>2Q^{2}
, thus if
q\leq Q
,
a
must belong to
[q]^{*}
and hence
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}
. Since
\alpha\not\in\mathfrak{M}
, this means
q\in[Q,N/Q]=[(\log N)^{A},N(\log N)^{-A}]
and
|\alpha-a/q|\leq 1/N
.

Notice that when
X\in[\sqrt{N}(\log N)^{-A/4},\sqrt{N}\log N]
and
N\gtrsim_{A}1
, we have

q\in[(\log N)^{A},N(\log N)^{-A}]\subset[(\log X)^{A/4},X^{2}(\log X)^{-A/4}].

Since
W=(\log N)^{o_{N}(1)}
, by Lemma
4.5
, we let
a_{1}=a/(a,W)
and
W_{1}=W/(a,W)
to obtain for
A\gtrsim B
and
X\in[\sqrt{N}(\log N)^{-A/4},\sqrt{N}\log N]
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\equiv h(\operatorname{mod}W)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{ap^{2}}{qW}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq P\\
p\equiv h(\operatorname{mod}W)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a_{1}p^{2}}{qW_{1}}\right)\lesssim\frac{X}{(\log N)^{10B}}.

Integrating by parts and using the fact
|\alpha-a/q|\leq 1/N
, this gives for
A\gtrsim B
and
X\leq\sqrt{N}\log N
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq P\\
p\equiv h(\operatorname{mod}W)\end{subarray}}(2p\log p)e\left(\frac{p^{2}\alpha}{W}\right)\lesssim\frac{X^{2}}{(\log N)^{5B}}+\frac{N}{(\log N)^{A/8}}.

Again by
W=(\log N)^{o_{N}(1)}
, we see
\sqrt{WN+b}\leq\sqrt{N}\log N
holds for
N\gtrsim 1
. We can thus conclude the proof by choosing
X=\sqrt{WN+b}
and
A\gtrsim B
.
∎

5.
Restriction estimate

In this section we again let
\nu=\nu_{b}
be as in (
5
) with
b\in Z(W)
fixed. Due to technical reasons we will not fix
f_{b}
but instead adopt a flexible argument for all functions bounded by
\nu
. From the work of Bourgain
[
4
]
, we notice that it will be convenient to argue on discrete sets.

For any function
f:[N]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we define for
u>0
that

(15)

f_{*}(u)=|\{n\in[N]:|\hat{f}(n/N)|\geq uN\}|.

The following lemma, which has essentially appeared in
[
4
]
, can be used to control the the size of
f_{*}(u)
, provided
u
is not too small.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
Q,T,M\geq 2
and
g
be a function on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
that obeys the bound

(16)

|g(\alpha)|\leq\max\left\{\sum_{q\leq Q}\frac{1}{q}\sum_{a\in[q]}\left(M\|\alpha-a/q\|+1\right)^{-2},\frac{1}{T}\right\}.

Let
\{t_{r}\}_{r\in[R]}\subset\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be such that
\|t_{r}-t_{s}\|\geq M^{-1}
holds for
r\neq s
and

(17)

\sum_{r,s\in[R]}|g(t_{r}-t_{s})|\geq\eta R^{2}

holds for some
\eta\geq 2/T
. For any
\epsilon>0
we have

R\lesssim_{\epsilon}\eta^{-1}(\log Q)^{O_{\epsilon}(1)}T^{\epsilon}.

Proof.

Let

F(\alpha)=(M\|\alpha\|+1)^{-2},\quad G(\alpha)=\sum_{q\leq Q}\frac{1}{q}\sum_{a\in[q]}F(\alpha-a/q).

By (
16
), (
17
) and
\eta\geq 2/T
, we have

(18)

\eta R^{2}\leq 2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s\in[R]\\
|g(t_{r}-t_{s})|\geq 1/T\end{subarray}}|g(t_{r}-t_{s})|\leq 2\sum_{r,s\in[R]}G(t_{r}-t_{s}).

Let

\psi(\alpha)=\left(\frac{1}{2M+1}\sum_{|k|\leq M}e(k\alpha)\right)^{2},\quad\sigma(\alpha)=\sum_{r\in[R]}\psi(\alpha-t_{r}).

Notice that
\psi(\alpha)\lesssim\min\{1,(M\|\alpha\|)^{-2}\}
. It follows immediately
\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{-1}
and thus
\|\sigma\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim RM^{-1}
. In particular, since
\|t_{r}-t_{s}\|\geq M^{-1}
for
r\neq s
, we have
\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
and hence
\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim RM^{-1}
, which provides the crucial saving.

Meanwhile, notice also that
\psi(\alpha)\gtrsim 1
for
\|\alpha\|\leq(100M)^{-1}
. We then have
\sigma(\alpha)\gtrsim 1
if
\|\alpha-t_{r}\|\leq(100M)^{-1}
for some
r\in[R]
. Denote
\tilde{\sigma}(\alpha)=\sigma(-\alpha)
. Then for
\|\alpha-(t_{r}-t_{s})\|\leq(1000M)^{-1}
, we have

\tilde{\sigma}*\sigma(\alpha)\gtrsim M^{-1}.

When
\|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}\|\leq(1000M)^{-1}
, it is clear
F(\alpha)\asymp F(\alpha^{\prime})
and hence
G(\alpha)\asymp G(\alpha^{\prime})
. Thus

\eta R^{2}M^{-2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}G(\alpha)(\tilde{\sigma}*\sigma)(\alpha)d\alpha=\sum_{|k|\leq 2N}\hat{G}(k)|\hat{\sigma}(k)|^{2}.

By the simple fact
|\hat{F}(k)|\leq\|F\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim M^{-1}
, we have

\hat{G}(k)=\sum_{q\leq Q}\frac{1}{q}\sum_{a\in[q]}e\left(\frac{ak}{q}\right)\hat{F}(k)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}\hat{F}(k)\lesssim M^{-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}1,

which means that

\eta R^{2}M^{-1}\lesssim\sum_{|k|\leq 2M}|\hat{\sigma}(k)|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}1.

We divide this sum into two parts, according to the size of inner sum. When the inner sum is smaller than
Q_{0}
, we use Plancherel’s formula to obtain

\sum_{|k|\leq 2N}|\hat{\sigma}(k)|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}1\leq Q_{0}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Q_{0}RM^{-1}.

Otherwise, we use the fact
|\sigma(k)|\leq\|\sigma\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim RM^{-1}
to obtain for any
r\geq 1
that

\sum_{|k|\leq 2M}|\hat{\sigma}(k)|^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}1\lesssim R^{2}M^{-2}Q_{0}^{-(r-1)}\sum_{k\leq 2M}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}1\right)^{r}\lesssim R^{2}M^{-1}Q_{0}^{-(r-1)}(r\log Q)^{2^{r}},

since

\begin{split}\sum_{k\leq 2M}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Q\\
q\mid k\end{subarray}}1\right)^{r}&=2M\sum_{q_{1},q_{2},..,q_{r}\leq Q}\frac{1}{[q_{1},q_{2}\cdots,q_{r}]}+O(Q^{r})\\
&\lesssim M\sum_{q\leq Q^{r}}\frac{(\sum_{d\mid q}1)^{r}}{q}+Q^{r}\\
&\lesssim M(r\log Q)^{2^{r}}.\end{split}

We now arrive at

\eta R\lesssim Q_{0}+Q_{0}^{-(r-1)}(r\log Q)^{2^{r}}R.

It then concludes the proof by
Q_{0}=r(r\log Q)^{2^{r}/(r-1)}T^{1/(r-1)}
and
r\gtrsim\epsilon^{-1}
.
∎

For those small values of
u
, our argument will be based on the following simple fourth-moment estimate.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f
be a function bounded by
\nu
. We have for
N\gtrsim 1
that

\sum_{n\in[N]}|\hat{f}(n/N)|^{4}\lesssim N^{4}(\log N)^{8}.

Proof.

Note first that

\begin{split}\sum_{n\in[N]}|\hat{f}(n/N)|^{4}&=\sum_{n\in[N]}\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3},m_{4}\in[N]}{f(m_{1})}\overline{f(m_{2})}\overline{f(m_{3})}f(m_{4})e\left(\frac{(m_{1}-m_{2}-m_{3}+m_{4})n}{N}\right)\\
&=N\sum_{k}\left|\sum_{m-n=k}f(m)\overline{f(n)}\right|^{2}\\
&\leq N\left(\frac{\phi(W)}{W}\sqrt{WN+b}\log(WN+b)\right)^{4}\sum_{k}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m-n=k\\
\nu(m)\nu(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\right|^{2}.\end{split}

The condition
\nu(m)\nu(n)\neq 0
means that
Wm+b
,
Wn+b\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
m,n\in[N]
. Thus, for
k=0
, the inner sum contributes
O(N)
, and for
1\leq|k|<N
we instead have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m-n=k\\
\nu(m)\nu(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{2}-y^{2}=Wk\\
W\mid(x-y)\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{d\mid|k|}1.

This leads to

\sum_{k}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m-n=k\\
\nu(m)\nu(n)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\right|^{2}\lesssim N+\sum_{1\leq k<N}\left(\sum_{d\mid k}1\right)^{2}\lesssim N(\log N)^{3}.

By the fact
W=(\log N)^{o_{N}(1)}
, we obtain for
N\gtrsim 1
,

\sum_{n\in[N]}|\hat{f}(n/N)|^{4}\lesssim N^{4}(\log N)^{8}

as desired.

∎

Proof of Proposition 3.3.

Let
q>4
and
f:[N]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be bounded by
\nu
. Let
f_{*}(u)
be given by (
15
). We first find by Lemma
5.2
,

(uN)^{4}f_{*}(u)\lesssim\sum_{n\in[N]}|\hat{f}(n/N)|^{4}\leq N^{4}(\log N)^{8},

It then follows

(19)

f_{*}(u)\lesssim u^{-4}(\log N)^{8}

On the other hand, we let
\{t_{r}\}_{r\in[R]}=\{n/N:|f(n/N)|\geq uN,n\in[N]\}
and
\{c_{r}\}_{r\in[R]}\subset\mathbb{C}
be such that
c_{r}\hat{f}(t_{r})=|\hat{f}(t_{r})|
,
r\in[R]
. Then,

uRN\leq\sum_{r\in[R]}|\hat{f}(t_{r})|=\sum_{r\in[R]}c_{r}\hat{f}(t_{r})=\sum_{n\in[N]}f(n)\sum_{r}c_{r}e(nt_{r}).

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
|f|\leq\nu
, this yields

u^{2}R^{2}N^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{n\in N}|f(n)|\right)\left(\sum_{n\in[N]}|f(n)|\sum_{r,s\in[R]}e(n(t_{r}-t_{s}))\right)\lesssim N\sum_{r,s\in[R]}\hat{\nu}(t_{r}-t_{s}).

Next, by Hölder’s inequality, we have for any
q^{\prime}>4
that

u^{q^{\prime}}R^{2}\leq\sum_{r,s}\left(N^{-1}\hat{\nu}(t_{r}-t_{s})\right)^{q^{\prime}/2}.

Observe that by Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.6
, for
A\gtrsim B\geq 1
, there is some constant
C_{q^{\prime},A,B}
such that
C_{q^{\prime},A,B}(N^{-1}\hat{\nu})^{q^{\prime}/2}
satisfy (
16
) with
Q=(\log N)^{A}
,
T=(\log N)^{B}
and
M=N
. By Lemma
5.1
, we have for any
\epsilon>0
and
u\geq 2(\log N)^{-B}
that

R\lesssim_{\epsilon,{q^{\prime}},A,B}u^{-q^{\prime}}(\log N)^{(B+1)\epsilon},

which leads to the bound

(20)

f_{*}(u)\lesssim_{\epsilon,{q^{\prime}},A,B}u^{-q^{\prime}}(\log N)^{(B+1)\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon,q^{\prime},A,B}u^{-q^{\prime}-2\epsilon}.

When
u\leq 2(\log N)^{-B}
, we use (
19
) to obtain

f_{*}(u)\lesssim_{A,B}u^{-4-8B^{-1}}.

Since
q^{\prime}+2\epsilon
and
4+8B^{-1}
can be arbitrarily close to
4
, we conclude that
f_{*}(u)\lesssim_{q}u^{-(q+4)/2}
holds for
u>0
.
Note that
\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{\infty}}<2N
as
N\gtrsim 1
. We have now

\begin{split}\sum_{n\in[N]}|\hat{f}(n/N)|^{q}&\lesssim(2N)^{q}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{kq}|\{n\in[N]:2^{k}N\leq|\hat{f}(n/N)|\leq 2^{k+1}\}|\\
&\lesssim_{q}N^{q}\sum_{k\leq 0}2^{kq}f_{*}(2^{k})\\
&\lesssim_{q}N^{q}\sum_{k\leq 0}2^{k(q-4)/2}\\
&\lesssim_{q}N^{q}.\end{split}

For any
\theta\in[0,N^{-1}]
, we now specialize
f(n)=f_{b}(n)e(n\theta)
and thus

\hat{f}(\alpha)=\sum_{n\in[N]}f_{b}(n)e(n(\alpha+\theta))=\hat{f}_{b}(\alpha+\theta).

Since
|f|\leq\nu
, the above argument gives

\sum_{n\in[N]}|\hat{f}_{b}(n/N+\theta)|^{4}\lesssim_{q}N^{q}.

Integrating this over
\theta\in[0,N^{-1}]
we conclude the proof.

∎

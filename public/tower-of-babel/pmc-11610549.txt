Preventive effects of 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine on community-acquired pneumonia hospitalization in older individuals in China: A case-control study
Authors: Na Liu, Lei Wang, Lin Luan, Huaqing Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe effectiveness of 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPSV23) against hospitalization risk due to all-cause community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in older individuals in China is limited. A hospital-based case-control study was conducted on this issue in Suzhou City, Jiangsu Province, China. Cases were individuals who were newly diagnosed with CAP based on International Classification of diseases 10 (ICD10 code: J15.902, J15.903, J13.x00 × 001) from January 2023 to June 2023, while patients with other respiratory tract infections were the control group. The vaccination data of PPSV23 was obtained from Suzhou Center for Disease Control and Prevention. The results showed CAP group had a lower vaccination rate of PPSV23 within last 5 years than controls (3.27% vs. 4.81%). Both the severe CAP and non-severe CAP groups had lower vaccination rates of PPSV23 than controls. Logistic regression demonstrated that PPSV23 vaccination within 5 years in individuals aged ≥60 years prevented 26.7% hospitalization risks of all-cause CAP, with adjusted OR of 0.733 (95% CI = 0.605-0.887). PPSV23 vaccination could prevent 25.2% (adjusted OR = 0.748, 95% CI = 0.616-0.909) and 44.0% (adjusted OR = 0.560, 95% CI = 0.309-1.012) hospitalization risks due to non-severe CAP and severe CAP, respectively. PPSV23 vaccination is effective in reducing hospitalization risk due to CAP in older individuals.KEYWORDS: 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine, vaccine effectiveness, community-acquired pneumonia, hospitalization risk, older individuals

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is one of the major global health problems, with the estimated prevalence of 1.5–14/1000 person-years1 and the mortality rate of 2–5/1000 person-years.2,3 Recently, a national population-based study in China4 reported the overall incidence of CAP was 7.13 (95% CI: 6.11–8.15) per 1000 person-years, which varied by age with a U-shaped curve peaking in children aged <5 years old [65.80 (95% CI: 62.52–69.08)] and the elderly aged ≥80 years old [14.98 (95% CI: 13.63–16.34)]. Moreover, up to 50% of cases require inpatient hospitalization, and the outcomes among the elderly is poor.5 A study conducted in southeastern China6 reported that the cost per inpatient was $4530.4 ± 9151.68. The finding of a retrospective study using a nationwide Dutch database7 demonstrated the mean duration of hospitalization for intensive care unit (ICU) patients and general ward patients was 15.2 days and 6.2 days, respectively, furthermore, the median costs ranged from €344 per episode for 0–9 year olds treated in the outpatient hospital, up to €10284 per episode for 50–64 year olds admitted to the ICU. Therefore, preventing the occurrence of CAP is of great significance in reducing the clinical and economic burden caused by CAP.

CAP is an important respiratory disease affecting the elderly population, and the most common pathogen is Streptococcus pneumoniae (Spn).8,9 As a specific preventive measure for pneumococcal diseases, the 23-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide vaccine (PPSV23) has a great economic value and is recommended for use in the elderly population. Some studies have reported that PPSV23 is economical and effective in preventing invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD), but there is still controversy regarding the prevention of CAP occurrence, reduction of CAP-attributable deaths and all-cause deaths.10–13 In China, PPSV23 is the only vaccine available for the elderly adults to prevent diseases caused by pneumococcus, but it is yet a non-immunization program vaccine in China, but some advanced regions/cities use it as an immunization program vaccine to control major infectious diseases.Since April 2018, a free vaccination program of PPSV23 targeting residents aged 65–84 was initiated in Suzhou, Jiangsu Province, China. Because pathogen testing is not mandatory, the diagnosis of respiratory disease type obtained from Suzhou Health and Family Planning Statistics Information Center on hospital admissions population aged ≥60 years old were based on the clinical diagnosis of doctors in health facilities.This research divided the subjects into the case group (patients with primary CAP) and the control group (patients with other respiratory tract infection).The case-control study was conducted to assess the effectiveness of PPSV23 vaccination in preventing hospitalization due to CAP.

Between January 1, 2023 to June 30, 2023, a hospital-based case-control study at about 103 Public health facility in Suzhou, China was conducted. The outcome of this study was focused on CAP in population aged ≥60 years old. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention of Suzhou and was carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. During the study, based on the 10th edition of International Classification of Diseases (ICD10) code, a total of 40,115 patients were hospitalized in the respiratory department of 103 hospitals in Suzhou, including 17,822 patients with primary pulmonary infection (ICD10 code: J98.414), 2676 patients with primary novel coronavirus infection (ICD10 code:U07.100), 7931 with other type of pneumonia including bacterial pneumonia, viral pneumonia (ICD10 code mainly includes J18.90, J15.900, J12.900, J84.900×002, etc.), and 5805 with primary CAP (ICD10 code: J15.902, J15.903, J13.x00×001),5881 patients with other respiratory tract infections consisting of bronchitis (ICD10 code: J40.x00, J20.900, J20.902, J20.800, J21.90, J20.900×004), acute upper respiratory tract infection, etc. In this study, 5805 patients with all-cause CAP were selected as the case group, and 5881 patients with other respiratory tract infection (non-pulmonary infection, non-pneumonia) were selected as the control group. The trained doctors or nurses collected participants′age, sex, and underlying disease or comorbidities including essential hypertension (I10.x09, I10.x00), type 2 diabetes (E11.900), coronary heart disease (I25.103, I25.104), and cardiac insufficiency (I50.90, I10.x00, I50.105), acute pyelonephritis (N10.x02), atherosclerosis (I70.900) and stroke (I63, I69). The vaccination information of PPSV23 was obtained from Suzhou Center for Disease Control and Prevention, including the community where the vaccine was administered, the time of the first vaccination and the number of doses. The vaccination of PPSV23 is effective when administered between 14 days and 5 years before the onset of respiratory diseases.

Because the numerical variables did not meet the normal distribution in the present study, the median and inter-quartile range (IQR) were used to describe them, and Wilcoxon or Kruskal–Wallis test was used for comparison between groups. The prevalence or proportion was used to describe the categorical variables and Chi-square test was used for comparison between groups. Bonferroni’s correction was used for pairwise comparisons after multiple group comparisons. The vaccination rates of PPSV23 and basic characteristics were calculated and compared between the cases and controls, the binary logistic regression were used to analyze the association of all-cause CAP with the vaccination of PPSV23 and the multinomial logistic regression was used to estimate the relationship between the subtypes of CAP and PPSV23 vaccination. The odds ratio (OR) value and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were calculated to measure the association. Data analysis was performed using SAS 9.4 software (SAS Institute Inc. Cary, NC) with a significance level at a p value less than 0.05.

A total of 11,686 individuals including 5805 cases and 5881 controls were analyzed in the study. Table 1 lists the characteristics of cases and controls. Compared with controls, cases had a lower vaccination rate of PPSV23 (3.27% vs. 4.81%, p < .0001). Males accounted for 52.90% of the case group and 54.79% of the control group, the difference in the proportion of males between the two groups was statistically significant. The median age of cases was 76.0 years, with IQR of 69.0–83.0 years, which was higher than that in controls (75.0 years, IQR:69.0-81.0 years, p < .0001). And the proportion of patients ≥80 years old in the case group was higher than that in the control group. In additional, percent of patients with comorbidities such as essential hypertension, type 2 diabetes, coronary heart disease, cardiac insufficiency, acute pyelonephritis, atherosclerosis and stroke were higher in the case group than in the control group. Furtherly, the proportions of patients with any one comorbidity (30.23% vs. 23.26%, p < .0001) and at least two comorbidities (19.02% vs. 10.34%, p < .0001) were greater in cases in contrast to controls. The average length of hospitalization in the case group was longer than that in the control group (9 vs. 7 days, p < .0001).

The results of univariate and multivariate logistic regression for all-cause CAP by each variable are shown in Table 2. Older adults who received the PPSV23 had a lower risk of hospitalization due to all-cause CAP, with the crude and adjusted ORs of 0.669 (95% CI: 0.555–0.807) and 0.733 (95% CI:0.605-0.887), respectively, in other words, after adjustment of covariates, vaccination with PPSV23 prevented 26.7% of the risk of hospitalization due to CAP. Although the crude OR for all-cause CAP was significantly increased in subjects aged ≥80 years old, the association was not significant after adjustment. In addition, the results of univariate and multivariate analysis showed that the risk of CAP was lower in older men than in older women. Compared with individuals who did not have common comorbidities, those who had any one and two or more comorbidities had higher risks of CAP.

Table 3 shows the comparison of characteristics between non-severe and severe community-acquired pneumonia and controls. Compared with the control group, the non-severe CAP group had a lower vaccination rate of PPSV23 (3.43% vs. 4.81%, Bonferroni adjusted p = .0009), as well as the proportions of males and 70–79 years’ group, while they had higher proportions of patients with any one comorbidity and at least two comorbidities, and a longer hospital stay. Similarly, severe CAP group had a lower vaccination rate of PPSV23 (1.94% vs. 4.81%, Bonferroni adjusted p = .0015). However, compared with controls, the severe CAP group had a higher proportion of males, a higher median level of age, and a higher proportion of patients over 80 years, as well as higher proportions of patients with any one comorbidity and at least two comorbidities, and a longer hospital stay.

Table 4 presents the multinomial logistic regression for subtypes of CAP. The univariate and multivariate analysis found older adults who received the PPSV23 had a lower risk of non-severe CAP, with the crude and adjusted ORs of 0.703 (95% CI:0.581-0.851) and 0.748 (95% CI:0.616-0.909), respectively. In the univariate analysis, those who received PPSV23 had a lower risk of severe CAP (OR:0.390,95% CI:0.218-0.700), in the multivariate analysis, those who received PPSV23 had a lower risk of severe CAP, but the OR value was not statistically significant. The results of univariate and multivariate analysis showed that the risk of non-severe CAP was lower in older men than in older women, but the risk of severe CAP was higher in older men than in older women. Compared with subjects aged 60–69 years old, those who aged 70–79 and ≥80 years old had lower risks of non-severe CAP, whereas had higher risks of severe CAP. In addition, the univariate and multivariate analysis demonstrated, compared with individuals who did not have common comorbidities, those who had any one and two or more comorbidities had higher risks of non-severe CAP and severe CAP. Furtherly, the length of stay was positively associated with the risks of non-severe CAP and severe CAP.

Compared with controls, the CAP group had a lower vaccination rate of PPSV23. The univariate and multivariate logistic regression demonstrated that individuals aged ≥60 years who received the PPSV23 had a lower risk of hospitalization due to all-cause CAP. Furthermore, both the severe CAP and non-severe CAP groups had lower vaccination rates of PPSV23. Older adults who received the PPSV23 had lower risks of non-severe and severe CAP hospitalizations risks than those who did not receive the vaccine.

At present, the effectiveness of PPSV23 in preventing CAP is still controversial. A case-control study of elderly individuals aged at least 65 years in Japan15 found that PPSV23 was effective against pneumococcal CAP but not against all-cause CAP, with adjusted OR of 0.23 (95% CI:0.08-0.6) and 0.76 (95% CI:0.44-1.32), respectively. A matched case-control study from Spain16 demonstrated PPSV23 vaccination against all pneumococcal pneumonia was significantly effective for individuals aged ≥65 years, with adjusted OR of 0.47(95% CI:0.32–0.67). A large-scale cohort study form Spain17 showed that PPSV23 on preventing overall pneumococcal CAP with hazard ration (HR) = 0.49, 95% CI = 0.29–0.84) and all-cause CAP (HR = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.58–0.98) was effective in older individuals aged ≥60 years old within 5 years of vaccination. In additional, Chandler et al.10 conducted a nested case-control study in adult patients hospitalized for CAP to estimate the effectiveness of PPSV23 on preventing CAP hospitalization due to vaccine-type Streptococcus pneumonia, the findings demonstrated PPSV23 vaccination had no protection against PPSV23-serotype CAP hospitalization in adults. A study18 found that the adjusted vaccination effectiveness of PPSV23 against pneumococcal CAP and PPSV23-serotype CAP in patients aged ≥65 years was 11.0% (95% CI:–26.4% to 37.3%) and 6.3% (95% CI:–73.8% to 49.5%), respectively.

The severity is an important factor affecting the prognosis of CAP.19,20 This study found that patients with severe CAP had more comorbidities and longer hospital duration. The subgroup analysis indicated PPSV23 vaccination within 5 years could prevent 25.2% and 44.0% hospitalization risks due to mild CAP and severe CAP. While, due to the small sample size of severe CAP cases, the OR value was not statistically significant after adjusting for covariates. A study conducted in elderly in Spain21 revealed the effectiveness of PPSV23 in preventing CAP hospitalization was 15.2% (95% CI:-3.1-30.3), and the effectiveness in avoiding ICU admission or death was 28.1% (95% CI:-14.3-56.9) in all patients.

Besides, the vaccination rate of PPSV23 in this study was far lower than that in the same kind of research subjects oversea. In a case-control study in Japan,15 PPSV23 vaccination rates were as high as 28% and 30% among CAP inpatients and controls within 4 years. Another study from Spain21 including 1895 CAP inpatients and 1895 controls reported that 13.7% of CAP cases and 14.4% of controls had received PPSV23 in the last five years. However, the information on PPSV23 vaccination in this study comes from the Center for Disease Control and Prevention, which avoided recall bias and ensured the reliability of the study results.

Of course, this study also has some limitations. First, the design of this study is an observational study, and we cannot balance confounding factors through randomization. Second, the control group is other respiratory diseases without lung infections, and due to the sample size of control group, we were unable to match controls to cases to balance confounding factors. Therefore, we adjusted the influence of confounding factors through multivariate analysis. Third, this study may have been affected by bias due to the selection of hospital cases and controls, as well as classification bias due to differences in clinical coding accuracy among participating hospitals.

In summary, this study found that vaccination with PPSV23 within the last 5 years was effective in preventing the risk of hospitalization for CAP in people aged 60 years and older. Therefore, it is recommended to actively vaccinate the elderly aged 60 years and above with PPSV23 to reduce the disease burden caused by CAP.

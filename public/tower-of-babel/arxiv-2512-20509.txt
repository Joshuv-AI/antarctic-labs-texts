Holographic entanglement negativity and thermodynamics in backreacted AdS black hole


Abstract
We investigate holographic entanglement negativity (HEN) as a probe of mixed state quantum correlations in a deformed AdS black hole background with backreaction sourced by a string cloud. The bulk geometry is dual to a strongly coupled large- N c N_{c} gauge theory at finite temperature, backreacted by a finite density of heavy static fundamental quarks. Alongside holographic entanglement entropy (HEE), we analyze entanglement thermodynamics and establish a first law like relation in the small backreaction regime by identifying the deformed black hole at zero temperature as the natural ground state of the dual field theory. Using analytic expansions in the low and high temperature limits, we compute the HEN for adjacent, disjoint and bipartite subsystem configurations and examine its dependence on the backreaction parameter ρ \rho . Our results reveal a clear regime dependence: at low temperature, backreaction suppresses the negativity, signaling a reduction of distillable quantum correlations while at high temperature the negativity is enhanced due to the monotonic ρ \rho -dependence of near horizon contributions. We further compare the behavior of HEN with HEE and mutual information, demonstrating that entanglement negativity provides a sharper diagnostic of the competition between thermal and quantum correlations in holographic plasmas with matter backreaction.

Introduction

Strongly coupled quantum systems appear in a wide range of physical settings ranging from condensed matter systems and cold atomic gases to the quark–gluon plasma formed in heavy-ion collisions. A central difficulty in analyzing such systems is the characterization of their quantum correlations, particularly in regimes where perturbative methods fail. The AdS/CFT correspondence
[
53
,
77
,
26
]
offers a powerful non-perturbative framework by relating strongly coupled gauge theories to weakly coupled gravitational theories in higher dimensional spacetimes. Within this holographic framework, quantum information theoretic measures such as entanglement entropy, mutual information, and computational complexity have emerged as valuable probes of the correlation structure of the dual field theories. Among these, the holographic computation of entanglement entropy through the Ryu–Takayanagi (RT) prescription and its covariant generalization
[
68
,
27
]
has played a central role.
The RT prescription has found extensive application in investigating the entanglement entropy of conformal field theories at finite temperature which are holographically dual to AdS-Schwarzschild black holes
[
8
,

,

first law
for the entanglement entropy in
[
6
,
2

. Entanglement thermodynamics has also been investigated for charged black holes in AdS
4
backgrounds. In particular, the effects of temperature and charge on the entanglement entropy of the boundary field theory dual to charged AdS black holes were studied in
[
17
]
, with extensions to higher-dimensional charged black holes presented in
[
43
]
. Furthermore, entanglement thermodynamics has been explored in a variety of other scenarios including non-conformal and non-relativistic geometries
[

The entanglement entropy does not provide a faithful characterization of quantum correlations in mixed states as it captures both quantum and classical contributions. To extract the purely quantum correlations in such states,
entanglement negativity
was introduced in quantum information theory
[
75
,

. Entanglement negativity serves as computable upper bound on distillable entanglement, and monotonic under local operations and classical communication (LOCC). Further it vanishes for separable states, thereby making it a robust measure of quantum correlations in mixed state scenarios. In recent years, entanglement negativity has gained prominence as a powerful diagnostic of quantum correlations in quantum field theories, capturing information inaccessible through entanglement entropy alone. A field-theoretic formulation based on the replica trick was developed in
[
9

enabling the computation of negativity in extended systems. This approach was later applied to finite temperature conformal field theories
[

and to lattice models such as the critical Ising chain
[

[

[

extending the intuition gained from the RT prescription. The HEN turns out to be an effective measure to study the behavior of entanglement in the vicinity of critical points for strongly coupled gauge theory
[
42
]
. These studies reveal that negativity is sensitive to critical behavior and captures features that are not visible through entanglement entropy alone. In parallel, HEN provides a robust characterization of quantum correlations in the presence of thermal and deformation effects in quantum field theories at finite temperature
[
64
]
. Together, these developments underscore the effectiveness of HEN as a better probe of mixed state entanglement in holographic frameworks. Closely related insights arise from the study of reflected entropy, that offers an alternative mixed state measure based on the entanglement structure of canonical purifications
[
23
]
. While reflected entropy captures both quantum and classical correlations, its holographic dual via the entanglement wedge cross section provides complementary geometric information that enriches the understanding of correlation structure beyond what is accessible through negativity alone. Together, holographic entanglement negativity and reflected entropy describes a broad framework for probing mixed state entanglement in strongly coupled dual field theories.

Recent studies have highlighted the significance of investigating entanglement measures in bulk geometries that include matter backreaction as such effects capture additional physical features of the dual strongly coupled field theories. An instructive example is provided by a deformed AdS black hole geometry sourced by a homogeneous string cloud
[
15
]
which is holographically dual to a large-
N_{c}
gauge theory at finite temperature backreacted by a uniform density of static heavy quarks. The backreaction parameter encodes the quark density and modifies both the near boundary and near horizon geometry, providing a controlled setup to investigate how heavy-flavor matter influences entanglement structure, thermodynamics, and mixed-state quantum correlations. This setup has been extensively employed to explore transport properties, entanglement structure and chaotic behavior in the dual field theory
[
12
,
14
,
13
,
38
]
.

A natural question that arises is whether holographic entanglement entropy admits a relation similar to the first law of thermodynamics in such backreacted field theories. To address this, we study entanglement thermodynamics in deformed strongly coupled field theories in the context of AdS
4
/CFT
3
. Specifically, we analyze the dependence of the entanglement entropy on temperature and backreaction for a strip shaped subsystem in the boundary field theory dual to a deformed AdS
4
black hole.
Furthermore, while the impact of backreaction on entanglement entropy and related probes has been widely investigated, the behavior of HEN in this background remains largely unexplored. Given that entanglement negativity provides a better probe of distillable quantum entanglement in mixed states, a systematic analysis in this context is both natural and timely. In this work, we present a detailed analysis of HEN for adjacent, bipartite and disjoint subsytems in the the string cloud deformed AdS black hole geometry.
Employing analytic expansions in both low and high temperature regimes, we find a clear dichotomy in the effect of backreaction. At low temperature, the leading
\rho
-dependent correction enters as
(1-\rho)T_{f}^{\,d}
and suppresses the HEN which indicates the inclusion of heavy static quarks enhances thermal correlations at the cost of quantum correlations. By contrast, in the high temperature regime, the HEN scales with
T_{f}
and is enhanced with increasing
\rho
, as the near horizon contributions encoded in the
\widetilde{S}(\rho,d)
coefficients grow monotonically with the quark density. This dichotomy persists for adjacent and disjoint intervals while in the bipartite configuration, the interplay between
\rho T_{f}^{\,d-1}
and
(1-\rho)T_{f}^{d}
terms leads to richer, parameter-dependent behaviour. A comparison with HEE shows that while, HEE is dominated by thermal scaling and is insensitive to the separation of quantum and classical correlations, HEN captures the genuine quantum contribution and thus provides a sharper diagnostic of backreaction effects in strongly coupled plasmas. Further, HEN for two disjoint subsystems allow us to compute the critical separation at which the distillable entanglement vanishes. We give a master equation that can be used to find the critical separation for particular values of backreaction and dimensions.

2
Background

We begin by briefly reviewing the holographic setup corresponding to a strongly coupled gauge theory with a finite density of heavy static fundamental quarks, which is dual to a deformed AdS black hole geometry. This deformation arises due to the backreaction of a homogeneous distribution of long, non-interacting, and static strings stretched along the radial direction in the bulk spacetime. The endpoints of these strings are attached to the AdS boundary and represent static fundamental quarks, while the string bodies encode the associated gluonic degrees of freedom
[
15
]
.

The gravitational sector of the dual theory is described by the following
(d+1)
-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert action with a negative cosmological constant:

\mathcal{S}=\frac{1}{4\pi G_{d+1}}\int dx^{d+1}\sqrt{g}\left(\mathcal{R}-2\Lambda\right)+\mathcal{S}_{M},

(2.1)

where the matter action
\mathcal{S}_{M}
captures the effect of the string distribution and is given by

\mathcal{S}_{M}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\mathcal{T}_{i}\int d^{2}\xi\sqrt{-h}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}.

(2.2)

Here,
g_{\mu\nu}
denotes the
(d+1)
-dimensional spacetime metric,
h_{\alpha\beta}
is the intrinsic worldsheet metric, and
\mathcal{T}_{i}
represents the tension of the
i^{\text{th}}
string. The corresponding Einstein’s equations, get modified by the energy-momentum tensor sourced by the strings:

\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{R}g_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G_{N}T_{\mu\nu},

(2.3)

where the stress-energy tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
associated with the string cloud is

T^{\mu\nu}=-\sum_{i}\mathcal{T}_{i}\int{d^{2}\xi\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mid{g_{\mu\nu}}}}\sqrt{-h^{\alpha\beta}}h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}\delta_{i}^{d-1}(x-X_{i})}.

(2.4)

Considering all the strings are identical with tension
\mathcal{T}
and adopt the static gauge choice
t=\xi^{0}
,
r=\xi^{1}
gives the following number density of the strings

b(x)=\mathcal{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}^{(d-1)}(x-X_{i}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{with}\penalty\ b>0,

where
N
denotes the total number of strings. Assuming a uniform distribution over a spatial volume
V_{d-1}
, the average density becomes:

\tilde{b}=\frac{1}{V_{d-1}}\int b(x)d^{d-1}x=\frac{\mathcal{T}N}{V_{d-1}}.

As suggested in
[
15
]
, the thermodynamic limit (
V_{d-1}\to\infty
), to keeps
N/V_{d-1}
finite for maintaining constant quark density as
N\to\infty
.
The only non-vanishing components of the energy-momentum tensor are,

T_{00}=-\frac{\tilde{b}}{r^{3}}g_{tt},\qquad T_{rr}=-\frac{\tilde{b}}{r^{3}}g_{rr}.

(2.5)

The suitable ansatz for solving modified Einstein equation in AdS is

ds^{2}=-V(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{V(r)}+\frac{r^{2}}{R^{2}}\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},

(2.6)

where the blackening factor
V(r)
is defined as:

V(r)=K+\frac{r^{2}}{R^{2}}-\frac{2m}{r^{d-2}}-\frac{2bR^{d-3}}{(d-1)r^{d-3}}.

(2.7)

Here
m
is the mass parameter and
K
denotes the curvature of the spatial boundary and takes values
-1
,
0
, or
1
for hyperbolic, flat, or spherical geometries, respectively. In our analysis, we consider the planar (
K=0
) case only. Additionally, we define a dimensionless deformation parameter
b=\tilde{b}R^{d-1}
for notational convenience.
Transforming to the inverse radial coordinate
z=R^{2}/r
, the metric takes the following familiar form:

ds^{2}=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(-h(z)dt^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2}+\frac{dz^{2}}{h(z)}\right),

(2.8)

with the modified blackening function now expressed as

h(z)=1-\frac{2m}{R^{2d-2}}z^{d}-\frac{2b}{(d-1)R^{d-1}}z^{d-1}.

(2.9)

It is convenient to rewrite
h(z)
in terms of the horizon location
z_{H}
and a dimensionless quark density parameter
\rho
:

h(z)=\left[1-\rho\left(\frac{z}{z_{H}}\right)^{d-1}+(\rho-1)\left(\frac{z}{z_{H}}\right)^{d}\right],

(2.10)

where

\rho=\frac{2bz_{H}^{d-1}}{(d-1)R^{d-1}}.

(2.11)

Both
b
and
\rho
encode the information of the uniform string (quark) density in the bulk and on the boundary, respectively. These parameters will be used throughout the paper depending on the convenience. The Hawking temperature of this deformed AdS black hole is

T=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{d}{dz}h(z)\Biggr|_{z=z_{H}}=\frac{(d-\rho)}{4\pi z_{H}}.

(2.12)

Positivity of the temperature imposes the constraint
0\leq\rho\leq d
. The lower limit corresponds to the undeformed case (
b=0
), while the upper bound corresponds to the extremal limit with vanishing Hawking temperature.
The corresponding ADM mass of the black hole is related to the parameter
m
via
M=\frac{(d-1)mV_{d-1}}{8\pi G_{d+1}}
, and the associated entropy density is:

s=\frac{R^{d-1}}{4G_{N}^{d+1}}\frac{1}{z_{H}^{d-1}}.

(2.13)

It is useful to introduce an effective temperature
T_{f}
, defined as:

T_{f}=\frac{d}{4\pi z_{H}},

(2.14)

which allows the entropy density to scale as
s\sim T_{f}^{d-1}
, a feature that becomes important in subsequent analyses of low temperature entanglement entropy.

The specific heat of the black hole computed from the variation of mass with temperature is given by

C=\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}=\frac{V_{d-1}(d-1)R^{2d-2}(d(d-1)R^{2d}z_{H}-2bR^{4}z_{H}^{d})}{4G_{d+1}z_{H}^{d-1}(d(d-1)R^{2d}z_{H}+2(d-2)bR^{4}z_{H}^{d})}

(2.15)

To ensure thermodynamic stability, the black hole must satisfy the condition:

z^{{\rm min}}_{H}=R\left(\frac{d(d-1)}{2b}\right)^{\frac{1}{d-1}}.

(2.16)

At zero temperature, the black hole acquires a negative minimum mass:

m^{{\rm min}}=-\frac{R^{d-1}}{d}\frac{b}{z^{{\rm min}}_{H}}.

(2.17)

Above this bound, the specific heat remains positive and continuous, indicating that the black hole configuration is thermodynamically stable. For
b>0
, black holes with a negative mass parameter
m
can exist within the range
m^{\rm min}\leq m<0
. In contrast, when
b\leq 0
, black holes with negative
m
do not possess real horizons. The corresponding free energy is given by:

{\cal F}=-\frac{(d-1)z_{H}+2bR^{2}(d-2)z_{H}^{d}}{16\pi z_{H}^{d+1}(d-1)}.

(2.18)

To examine the dynamical (geometric) stability of the background spacetime, one can carry out a gauge-invariant analysis of gravitational perturbations using the formalism developed in
[
30
,
44
,
29
,
45
,
46
]
. Within this approach, the background spacetime is regarded as a product manifold:

M^{2+p}={\mathcal{N}}^{2}\times{\mathcal{K}}^{p},

(2.19)

Here,
{\mathcal{N}}^{2}
denotes a two-dimensional spacetime with coordinates
(t,z)
, while
{\mathcal{K}}^{p}
represents a
p
-dimensional maximally symmetric space spanned by the spatial coordinates
x^{i}
. Gravitational perturbations are classified into scalar, vector, and tensor modes on
{\mathcal{K}}^{p}
, which allows the Einstein equations to be analyzed in a decoupled manner. The tensor and vector sectors have been studied explicitly in
[
15
]
, where the background geometry was shown to be stable against these classes of perturbations.

Revisiting the holographic entanglement entropy and EWCS

Since entanglement thermodynamics and holographic entanglement negativity are primarily studied in this work, it is natural to first review the computation of holographic entanglement entropy in the backreacted AdS black hole geometry, as introduced in
section

2
. Moreover, as reflected entropy is directly related to the entanglement wedge cross section, we also briefly review the EWCS. Both quantities were originally computed in
[
14
]
.

3.1
Holographic entanglement entropy (HEE)

We now revisit the computation of HEE for a single strip-like region in the backreacted AdS
d+1
black hole. This subsection provides all expressions later required for the computation of HEN.
According to the RT prescription, HEE is given by

S_{A}=\frac{\mathrm{Area}[\gamma_{A}]}{4G_{N}}\,,

(3.1)

where
\gamma_{A}
is the extremal surface in the bulk anchored on the boundary of
A
.
Consider a rectangular strip like subsystem
A
with width
l
along
x
and infinite extent
L
in other spatial directions defined by

x_{1}\in\left[-\frac{l}{2},\,\frac{l}{2}\right],\quad x_{i}\in\left[-\frac{L}{2},\,\frac{L}{2}\right],\quad i=2,\dots,d-2\,.

(3.2)

with
L\to\infty
. On a constant-time slice, the induced metric is

ds^{2}=\frac{R^{2}}{z^{2}}\left[\frac{dz^{2}}{h(z)}+d{x_{1}}^{\,2}+d\vec{x_{i}}^{\,2}\right],

(3.3)

where the blackening function
h(x)
is given by
eq.

2.10
. The profile of the extremal surface
z(x_{1})
satisfies the RT area functional

\mathcal{A}[z]=R^{d-1}L^{d-2}\int_{-l/2}^{l/2}dx_{1}\,\frac{1}{z^{d-1}}\sqrt{1+\frac{z^{\prime\,2}}{h(z)}}.

(3.4)

Due to translational symmetry in
x_{1}
direction, the conserved Lagrangian leads to

\frac{1}{z^{d-1}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{z^{\prime 2}}{h(z)}}}=\frac{1}{z_{t}^{d-1}},

(3.5)

where
z_{t}
is the turning point of the minimal surface. Solving for
z^{\prime}(x_{1})
and integrating gives the width and turning-point relation

l/2=1/z_{t}^{d-1}\int_{0}^{z_{t}}z^{d-1}\left[h(z)\left(1-(z/z_{t})^{2d-2}\right)\right]^{-1/2}dz.

(3.6)

Substituting (
3.5
) back into the area functional gives the area of the extremal surface and consequently the HEE is given by the RT formula as

S_{A}=\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{2G^{d+1}_{N}}\int_{0}^{z_{t}}\frac{dz}{z^{d-1}\sqrt{h(z)}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{z}{z_{t}}\right)^{2d-2}}},

(3.7)

To obtain the HEE in terms of boundary parameters, it is necessary to express the turning point
z_{t}
as a function of the strip width and temperature. However, evaluating the integral in
eq.

3.6
for arbitrary dimension
d
and finite temperature is analytically intractable. As discussed in
[
14
]
, we therefore analyze the problem in two regimes i.e. the low and high temperature limits where the integrals can be computed explicitly, and subsequently use these results to extract the corresponding behavior of the HEE.

Low-temperature expansion:

By taking the low temperature limit i.e
T_{f}l\ll 1
( or
z_{t}/z_{H}\ll 1
) the expression of
z_{t}
becomes

\displaystyle z_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{l}{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}\Bigg[1-\frac{\rho}{2d(2d-2)}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{l}{2z_{H}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}\right)^{\!d-1}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1-\rho}{2(d+1)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{2d}{2d-2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{d-1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(\frac{l}{2z_{H}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}\right)^{\!d}+\cdots\Bigg].

(3.8)

Now the corresponding area function is

\mathcal{A}=\frac{2}{d-2}\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{\epsilon^{d-2}}+R^{d-1}\left(\frac{L}{l}\right)^{\!d-2}S_{0}\left[1+\rho S_{2}\left(\frac{l}{z_{H}}\right)^{\!d-1}+(1-\rho)S_{1}\left(\frac{l}{z_{H}}\right)^{\!d}+\cdots\right].

(3.9)

Here
S_{0},S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are terms that only depends on dimensions parameter
d
, and are given by

\begin{split}S_{0}&=\frac{2^{d-2}\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\Gamma{\left(\frac{-d+2}{2d-2}\right)}}{(d-1)\Gamma{\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}}\left(\frac{\Gamma{\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}}{\Gamma{\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}}\right)^{d-2}\\
S_{1}&=2^{-(d+1)}\pi^{-\frac{d}{2}}\frac{\Gamma{\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)^{d+1}}}{\Gamma{\left(\frac{1}{d-1}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\Gamma{\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)^{d}}}\left[\frac{\Gamma{\left(\frac{1}{d-1}\right)}}{\Gamma{\left(\frac{-d+2}{2d-2}\right)}}+\frac{(d-2)}{(d+1)}\frac{2^{\frac{1}{d-1}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma{\left(1+\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}\right]\\
S_{2}&=2^{-d}\pi^{-(\frac{d-1}{2})}\left(\frac{\Gamma{\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}}{\Gamma{\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}}\right)^{d+1}\left[\frac{\Gamma{\left(\frac{d}{2d-2}\right)}}{\Gamma{\left(\frac{-d+2}{2d-2}\right)}}+\frac{(d-2)}{2d(d-1)}\right].\end{split}

(3.10)

Finally the HEE in low temperature limit is given by

S_{A}^{\mathrm{low}}=\frac{R^{d-1}L^{d-2}}{2G_{N}}\left[\frac{1}{(d-2)\epsilon^{d-2}}+S_{0}\,l^{2-d}+S_{0}S_{2}\,\rho\,l\left(\frac{4\pi T_{f}}{d}\right)^{d-1}+S_{0}S_{1}(1-\rho)\,l^{2}\left(\frac{4\pi T_{f}}{d}\right)^{d}+\cdots\right].

(3.11)

The finite pieces
S_{0}\,l^{2-d}
,
S_{0}S_{2}\,\rho\,lT_{f}^{d-1}
, and
S_{0}S_{1}(1-\rho)l^{2}T_{f}^{d}
,
are exactly the contributions that will appear in the HEN formulas.

In
eq.

3.11
, the first term is divergent containing UV-cut off (
\epsilon
). In the low-temperature regime, the entanglement entropy exhibits nontrivial modifications due to backreaction. Unlike the charged plasma case
[
51
]
, where the leading correction scales as
T_{f}^{\,d}
, here the dominant term is proportional to
T_{f}^{\,d-1}
. This distinction arises from the blackening function
h(z)
: near the boundary the
z^{\,d-1}
contribution from the quark density dominates over the mass term
\sim z^{d}
, making backreaction the leading source of corrections to the HEE at low effective temperature. It will be particularly interesting to examine whether a similar scaling behavior also manifests in the computation of HEN.

High-temperature expansion:

In the high temperature regime
T_{f}l\gg 1
(or
z_{t}\to z_{H}
), the RT surface approaches the horizon. Although, the direct series expansions for
l
and
A
diverge in this limit, an appropriate combination of these quantities yields a finite and well behaved expression for the entanglement entropy as

\mathcal{A}-\frac{L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{z_{t}^{\,d-1}}\,l=\frac{2L^{d-2}R^{d-1}}{z_{t}^{\,d-2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{h(u)}\,u^{\,d-1}\sqrt{1-u^{2d-2}}}-\cdots,

(3.12)

where
u=\frac{z}{z_{t}}
. This leads to the finite part of HEE as

S^{high}_{A}\simeq\frac{R^{d-1}}{4G_{d+1}}\left[V\left(\frac{4\pi T_{f}}{d}\right)^{\!d-1}\left(1+2\frac{d}{4\pi T_{f}l}\,\widetilde{S}(\rho,d)\right)\right],

(3.13)

where
V=lL^{d-2}
and

\widetilde{S}(\rho,d)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(-\frac{d-2}{2d-2}\right)}{(2d-2)\,\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2d-2}\right)}+\int_{0}^{1}du\left[\frac{\sqrt{1-u^{2d-2}}}{u^{\,d-1}\sqrt{h(u)}}-\frac{1}{u^{\,d-1}\sqrt{1-u^{2d-2}}}\right].

(3.14)

At high
T_{f}
, the leading term reproduces the thermal entropy density via its volume scaling, with
\rho
entering only at subleading order.

Across both the low and high effective temperature limits, the quark density parameter
\rho
plays a crucial role in determining the structure of the finite part of the entanglement entropy. At low
T_{f}
, the dominant correction scales as
T_{f}^{\,d-1}
, which originates from the backreaction-induced
z^{\,d-1}
contribution through
h(z)
. In contrast, at high
T_{f}
the leading term coincides with the thermal entropy density while the dependence on
\rho
manifests only through subleading corrections.

3.2
Entanglement wedge cross section and reflected entropy

Unlike HEE, the Entanglement Wedge Cross Section (EWCS) required two non-overlapping intervals (say
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
). It is a geometric quantity associated with the minimal bulk surface that divides the entanglement wedge of two boundary sub regions

4
Entanglement thermodynamics

In this section, we restrict our analysis to

The bulk theory is characterized by two primary parameters: the backreaction parameter

By substituting the mass relation from

(4.5)

This reparametrization reduces the effective parameters of the bulk theory to the backreaction

3.7

\displaystyle\frac{l}{2}

\displaystyle=z_{t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{u^{2}}{\sqrt{1-u^{4}}}\left(1-\frac{u^{3}z_{t}^{3}}{z_{H}^{3}}+\frac{bz_{H}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{u^{3}z_{t}^{3}}{z_{H}^{3}}-\frac{u^{2}z_{t}^{2}}{z_{H}^{2}}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}du,

(4.6)

\displaystyle{\cal A}

\displaystyle=\frac{2LR^{2}}{z_{t}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{}{u^{2}\sqrt{1-u^{4}}}\left(1-\frac{u^{3}z_{t}^{3}}{z_{H}^{3}}+\frac{bz_{H}^{2}}{R^{2}}\left(\frac{u^{3}z_{t}^{3}}{z_{H}^{3}}-\frac{u^{2}z_{t}^{2}}{z_{H}^{2}}\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}du.

We utilize the subsystem length and area formulas given in

z_{H}\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}R}{\sqrt{b}}.

(4.8)

In the relation above, the equality corresponds to the zero-temperature limit, while the inequality represents a black hole with finite temperature. According to the gauge/gravity duality, the field theory dual to a deformed AdS black hole occupies its ground state at zero temperature and enters an excited state at non-zero temperature. To derive a
first law of entanglement thermodynamics
, we also analyze the HEE of the dual field theory at zero temperature. This situation bears resemblance to the case of extremal and non-extremal Reissner–Nordström black holes, however a key distinction is that in the present setup the black hole mass is negative at zero temperature as discussed in

2

From the inequality in

4.8

(z_{H})

(z_{H})

4.8

(z_{H})

4

4.3
First law of entanglement thermodynamics

We now examine entanglement thermodynamics within the small backreaction regime. Holographic Entanglement negativity

Entanglement negativity is a well established quantum information theoretic measure that captures the distillable quantum correlations in mixed states, making it particularly suitable for thermal or finite-density systems where entanglement entropy alone fails to isolate genuine quantum correlations. Defined via the partial transpose of the reduced density matrix, negativity provides a computable upper bound on the distillable entanglement. Within the AdS/CFT framework, a holographic prescription for entanglement negativity in higher dimensions has been proposed, wherein suitable linear combinations of Ryu–Takayanagi surfaces encode the negativity of boundary mixed states
[
19
,
34
,
35
,
50
,
60
,
1
]
. This construction allows one to extend the study of mixed state entanglement to strongly coupled holographic plasmas, and in what follows we analyze its behavior in the deformed AdS black hole background to compare with the trends observed for HEN.

5
HEN for adjacent subsystems

Let us consider two adjacent rectangular strip subsystems
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
with respective lengths
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
described by

x\equiv x^{1}=\biggl[-\frac{l_{j}}{2},\frac{l_{j}}{2}\biggr],\quad x^{i}=\biggl[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\biggr];\quad j=1,2,

(5.1)

where
i=2,3,...,d-1
and

. The holographic prescription then requires an appropriate combination of the entanglement entropies of the individual regions and their union
[
37
,
34
]
. This construction effectively isolates the quantum part of correlations by eliminating purely thermal contributions. In what follows, we will evaluate the holographic negativity for the adjacent strip configuration in the backreacted AdS black hole background and examine its dependence on quark density
\rho
, with particular focus on the low and high temperature limits, in order to contrast its behavior with that of the entanglement entropy discussed previously. Following the proposal given in
[
34
]
, HEN for two adjacent subsystems is given by

(5.2)

Here,

section

3

•

•

This behavior of negativity at low

The low-temperature adjacent-interval result (

6
HEN for bipartite subsystems

Having analyzed the adjacent subsystems configuration, we now extend our study to the bipartite case, where the subsystem

Unlike the previous setups, this configuration captures the global entanglement structure of the plasma and provides a more direct comparison between quantum and thermal correlations. In particular, the bipartite negativity is expected to be sensitive to the thermal entropy density at high temperature, while at low temperature it should predominantly reflect the underlying vacuum-like entanglement structure. Furthermore, the presence of backreaction introduces nontrivial corrections that allow us to probe how heavy quark density modifies the global entanglement content of the system.

14

, the critical separation between two disjoint strips was analyzed using holographic mutual information (HMI), where the transition point corresponds to a change in the connectivity of the entanglement wedge. The resulting critical distance reflects the persistence of

The comparison thus highlights the complementary roles of HMI and HEN: while both diagnose correlation phase transitions, negativity provides a stricter and more refined probe of quantum entanglement in strongly coupled plasmas.

9
Summary and Discussion

In this work, we have studied the entanglement thermodynamics and HEN in a strongly coupled large
N_{c}
field theory at finite temperature and backreacted by the heavy static fundamental quarks. This theory is dual to a deformed AdS black hole background with backreaction sourced by the string cloud. First, we studied the HEE at both low and high temperatures in the small backreaction regime. The analysis of HEE in low temperature limit allowed us to identify a first law like relation for the entanglement entropy. We briefly review the EWCS and comment on the reflected entropy. Subsequently, we explored the behavior of HEN for adjacent, bipartite, and disjoint subsystem configurations. The main focus of this work is to understand how the backreaction parameter
\rho
modifies quantum correlations across different thermal regimes and how these results compare with the corresponding behavior of HEE. Our analysis reveals a striking regime dependence: in the adjacent case, the low
T_{f}
expansion produces a correction proportional to
(1-\rho)T_{f}^{\,d}
which suppresses HEN and thus confirms that heavy quark backreaction reduces distillable entanglement in the near-vacuum phase. Conversely, in the high
T_{f}
limit the relevant
\widetilde{S}(\rho,d)
coefficients increase with
\rho
leading to an enhancement of HEN, in contrast to the suppression seen at low temperature. In the bipartite configuration, the competition is more subtle: at low temperature, two different backreaction terms appear, one proportional to
\rho T_{f}^{\,d-1}
that can enhance negativity, and another proportional to
(1-\rho)T_{f}^{\,d}
that tends to suppress it, producing a parameter-dependent interplay. At high temperature, however, the bipartite negativity scales as
L^{d-2}T_{f}^{d-2}
with an overall enhancement from backreaction.
For disjoint subsystems, a similar trend emerges: the low–
T_{f}
regime is dominated by suppressive
(1-\rho)
terms while the high–
T_{f}
result exhibits enhancement due to the monotonic growth of the
\widetilde{S}(\rho,d)
coefficients. Comparing with HEE, we find that while both measures share similar thermal scaling laws, HEE is primarily extensive and insensitive to the distinction between quantum and classical correlations, whereas HEN captures the suppression/enhancement of distillable quantum correlations induced by backreaction.

Overall, the observations made here provide a deeper understanding of entanglement and
related phenomena in strongly coupled conformal field theories with backreaction at finite temperature. These results establish that the influence of heavy flavour backreaction is not universal but strongly regime dependent as it reduces entanglement in the vacuum like phase and enhances it in the thermal phase. This dual role highlights HEN as a sharper diagnostic of the balance between quantum and thermal correlations than HEE alone. In our study of entangled-disentangled phase transition using HEN for disjoint configuration we found that the critical separation decreases with backreaction. Also the critical separation from HEN (
l_{c}^{HEN}
) is always smaller then obtained from HMI (
l_{c}^{HMI}
) i.e.
l_{c}^{HEN}<l_{c}^{HMI}
.

Future directions include performing a systematic numerical study in the intermediate regime
T_{f}l\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
to map out crossover behaviour, exploring time-dependent quenches to connect with thermalization dynamics and extending the analysis to higher-derivative gravity or string-theoretic embeddings to quantify
1/N
and
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections. Such studies could shed further light on the microscopic structure of entanglement in strongly coupled plasmas with flavour backreaction and on the universal features of entanglement negativity in holographic duals.

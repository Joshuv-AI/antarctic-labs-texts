Triangle-free graphs with the fewest independent sets
Pjotr Buys, Jan van den Heuvel, Ross J. Kang

Abstract
. Given d > 0 d>0 and a positive integer n n , let G G be a triangle-free graph on n n vertices with average degree d d . With an elegant induction, Shearer (1983) tightened a seminal result of Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi (1980/1981) by proving that G G contains an independent set of size at least ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ log ⁡ d d ​ n (1+o(1))\frac{\log d}{d}n as d → ∞ d\to\infty . The hard-core model

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
and
\lambda\geq 0
, consider a random independent set
X
of
G
, where any
I\in\mathcal{I}(G)
is chosen with probability proportional to
\lambda^{|I|}
, that is,

\Pr(X=I)=\frac{\lambda^{|I|}}{\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}(G)}\lambda^{|J|}}.

This distribution is called the
hard-core model at fugacity
\lambda
, and the normalising factor

Z_{G}(\lambda)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{I}(G)}\lambda^{|J|}

is called its
partition function
.
This function is also known as the
independence polynomial
of
G
.
Note that the uniform case corresponds to
\lambda=1
and that
i(G)=Z_{G}(1)
.

Writing the
closed neighbourhood

\{v\}\cup N(v)
of
v
as
N[v]
,
it is crucial to observe that, for any vertex
v\in V
, we have
Z_{G}(\lambda)=Z_{G-v}(\lambda)+\lambda Z_{G-N[v]}(\lambda)
, which for
\lambda=1
corresponds to
i(G)=i(G-v)+i(G-N[v])
.

Our primary objective is to get a good handle on the following extremal parameter:

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)=\inf\left\{\frac{\log(Z_{G}(\lambda))}{|V|}:\text{$G=(V,E)$ a triangle-free graph of average degree at most $d$}\right\}.

As discussed above, the best-known upper bounds on the Ramsey numbers
R(3,k)
are based on our understanding of another extremal parameter:

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)=\inf\left\{\frac{\alpha(G)}{|V|}:\text{$G=(V,E)$ a triangle-free graph of average degree at most $d$}\right\}.

Shearer’s Theorem says that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)\geq(1+o(1))\frac{\log d}{d}
as
d\to\infty
, or more generally that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)\geq f(d)
for any non-increasing differentiable convex function
f
that satisfies (
1
).

Let us briefly point out how the two extremal parameters are related.

Lemma 2
.

For
d\geq 0
we have
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\dfrac{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)}{\log\lambda}=\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)
.

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
G=(V,E)
be a triangle-free graph of average degree at most
d
so that
\alpha(G)/|V|\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)+\varepsilon
.
A polynomial
p
with a positive leading coefficient satisfies
\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\log(p(\lambda))/\log\lambda=\deg(p)
, and so

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)}{\log\lambda}\leq\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\frac{\log(Z_{G}(\lambda))}{|V|\log\lambda}=\frac{\alpha(G)}{|V|}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}(d)+\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon
was chosen arbitrarily, this proves one inequality.
The opposite inequality follows easily from the fact that
Z_{G}(\lambda)\geq\lambda^{\alpha(G)}
and so
\log(Z_{G}(\lambda))\geq\alpha(G)\log\lambda
for any
G
.
∎

r^{\prime\prime}(\lambda^{-1}W(2\lambda))=2\lambda^{3}\cdot W(2\lambda)^{-1}(2+W(2\lambda)-W(2\lambda)^{2}).

We note that
r^{\prime\prime}(\lambda^{-1}W(2\lambda))>0
for
0<\lambda<e^{2}
.

We see that it is enough to show that
r_{\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=0
has only one solution for
x>0
, because this implies that
x=\lambda^{-1}W(2\lambda)
is the global minimum of
r_{\lambda}
.
Observe that setting
r_{\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=0
leads to the equation

c_{\lambda}=\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\cdot(2+\lambda x)\cdot(2+xe^{\lambda x}).

The expression on the right-hand side is increasing in
x
, as can be seen by taking its derivative:

\frac{d}{dx}\left[\tfrac{1}{4}\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\cdot(2+\lambda x)\cdot(2+xe^{\lambda x})\right]=\tfrac{1}{2}\lambda e^{-\lambda x}\cdot(1+\lambda x)\cdot(e^{\lambda x}-\lambda).

It follows that the equation
r_{\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=0
has a unique solution for
x\in[0,\infty)
.

This concludes the proof.
∎

The two lemmas are the two halves of the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\lambda\in[0,1]
.
If
G=(V,E)
is a triangle-free graph of average degree
d
, then

(3)

\log(Z_{G}(\lambda))\geq\frac{W(\lambda d)^{2}+2W(\lambda d)-W(2\lambda)^{2}-2W(2\lambda)}{2(d-2)}\cdot|V|.

Stated differently, Theorem
5
asserts that, if
\lambda\in[0,1]
, then

\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)\geq\frac{W(\lambda d)^{2}+2W(\lambda d)-W(2\lambda)^{2}-2W(2\lambda)}{2(d-2)}.

This bound specialised to
\lambda=1
implies Theorem
1
as in its first statement.
In fact, the asymptotic bound on
i(G)=Z_{G}(1)
is also valid for
Z_{G}(\lambda)
for any fixed
\lambda\in(0,1]
.

Remark 6
.

The condition
\lambda\leq 1
is not special.
Through a more sophisticated analysis, we can prove that
4
(and thus also
Theorem 5
) extends to
\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{M}]
, where
\lambda_{M}\approx 2.61
.
Furthermore, numerical evidence suggests that
4
remains valid for
\lambda
up to approximately
\lambda_{M}\approx 11.65
.
This would be close to a theoretical limit for
Theorem 5
because of the following.
For the edgeless graph
G=(V,\varnothing)
we have
\log(Z_{G}(\lambda))=\log(1+\lambda)\cdot|V|
, while the right-hand side of inequality (
3
) for
d=0
simplifies to
\frac{1}{4}(W(2\lambda)^{2}+2W(2\lambda))\cdot|V|
.
For
\lambda\geq 13.971
the latter expression is greater than the former.

Remark 7
.

Despite the previous remark, Lemma
2
suggests we should inspect the bound (
3
) for large
\lambda
, hypothetically speaking.
We have

\frac{W(\lambda d)^{2}+2W(\lambda d)-W(2\lambda)^{2}-2W(2\lambda)}{2(d-2)}=\left(\frac{\log d-\log 2}{d-2}+o(1)\right)\log\lambda\quad\text{as $\lambda\to\infty$}.

This would correspond to a lower bound on
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)
that is too large for smaller values of
d
.
(Certainly we must exclude
d
up to
4
, for example, due to work in
[
19
]
.)
But we cannot rule it out as a possibly valid bound for larger
d
yet.
By way of comparison, the lower bound on the independence polynomial (albeit in the maximum degree setting) in
[
12
, Thm. 4]
corresponds to an analogous expression of order
(\log\log\lambda)^{2}
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Remark 8
.

If
f
is any non-increasing differentiable convex function satisfying (
1
), then the function
d\mapsto f(d)\cdot\log\lambda
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
3
for any
\lambda\geq 1
.
By Lemma
3
we have
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)\geq f(d)\log\lambda
, and thus by Lemma
2
we have
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{\infty}(d)=\lim\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)/\log\lambda\geq f(d)
.
In this way, Lemma
3
is a strengthening of Shearer’s Theorem in its sharpest form.

This raises the following analytic question, which could test the strength of our induction as compared to Shearer’s original induction. For
d\geq 0
and
\lambda\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(d)

\displaystyle=\sup\{f(d):\text{$f$ non-increasing differentiable convex satisfying\penalty\ \eqref{eq:shearer}}\};

\displaystyle g_{\lambda}(d)

\displaystyle=\sup\{f_{\lambda}(d):\text{$f_{\lambda}$ non-increasing differentiable convex satisfying\penalty\ \eqref{eq:lambdaineq}}\}.

It is not difficult to show that
\tilde{g}
is in fact the choice of
f
that Shearer
[
21
]
found to solve (
1
) with equality.
Is there some
d>0
for which

\limsup\limits_{\lambda\to\infty}g_{\lambda}(d)/\log\lambda>\tilde{g}(d)
?

3.
Sharpness

The following probabilistic construction backstops the precise restatement of Theorem
1
, as well as an analogous version of Theorem
5
, by giving upper bounds on
\mathcal{F}_{\lambda}(d)
.

Proposition 9
.

For
\lambda\geq 0
,
d>0
define

\varphi(\lambda,d)=\begin{cases}\frac{W(\lambda d)^{2}+2W(\lambda d)}{2d}&\text{if $\log\lambda\leq d$};\\
1-\frac{1}{2}d+\log\lambda&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then for all
\varepsilon>0
we have that for sufficiently large
n
, there exists a triangle-free graph
G=(V,E)
with
|V|=n
and
d(G)<d+\varepsilon
such that
\log(Z_{G}(\lambda))<\left(\varphi(\lambda,d)+\varepsilon\right)|V|
.

Proof.

Since
Z_{0}(G)=1
for all
G
, the conclusion is trivial if
\lambda=0
.
We now assume
\lambda>0
.

Let
\mathbf{G}\sim G(n,d/n)
be distributed as the random graph on
n
vertices where each edge is included independently at random with probability
d/n
.
We will perform various routine random graph computations; see
[
17
]
for background.
It is a standard fact that

(4)

\Pr(d(\mathbf{G})\geq d+\varepsilon)\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

Writing
X
for the number of triangles in
\mathbf{G}
, it is known (see
[
17
, Sec. 3.3]
) that

(5)

\Pr(X=0)\to\exp(-d^{3}/6)\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

The expectation of the partition function of the hard-core model on
\mathbf{G}
at fugacity
\lambda
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Z_{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\left(1-\frac{d}{n}\right)^{\binom{k}{2}}\lambda^{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\frac{en}{k}\right)^{k}e^{-\frac{d}{n}\binom{k}{2}}\lambda^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{e^{d}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\exp\left[k\left(1+\log n+\log\frac{\lambda}{k}-\frac{dk}{2n}\right)\right].

Let us now optimise the exponent in the summand, by maximising it according to the scaling
k=\eta n
,
\eta\in[0,1]
.
Thus we write the exponent as

(6)

\left(\eta+\eta\log\frac{\lambda}{\eta}-\tfrac{1}{2}d\eta^{2}\right)n.

By computing the derivative with respect to
\eta
and observing concavity for
\eta\in[0,1]
, we find that the maximum for (
6
) occurs when

d=\frac{1}{\eta}\log\frac{\lambda}{\eta}\text{ and }\eta\in[0,1],\quad\text{which has solution }\eta=\begin{cases}\frac{W(\lambda d)}{d},&\text{if $\log\lambda\leq d$};\\
1,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This means that (
6
) has the maximum value

\left(\eta+\tfrac{1}{2}d\eta^{2}\right)n=\left(\frac{2W(\lambda d)+W(\lambda d)^{2}}{2d}\right)n

if
\log\lambda\leq d
, and the maximum value

\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}d+\log\lambda\right)n

otherwise.
Substituting this maximisation back into the summation, we obtain that

\mathbb{E}(Z_{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda))\leq\sqrt{e^{d}}\,n\exp\left[\left(\varphi(\lambda,d)\right)n\right]\leq\exp\left[\left(\varphi(\lambda,d)+\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon\right)n\right],

provided
n
is taken sufficiently large.
By Markov’s inequality,

(7)

\Pr\bigl(Z_{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda)\geq\exp\left[\left(\varphi(\lambda,d)+\varepsilon\right)n\right]\bigr)\leq\exp\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon n\right)\to 0\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

By the probabilistic method, the bounds (
4
), (
5
), and (
7
) give the result.
∎

Remark 10
.

The computations above also yield sharpness up to a
2+o(1)
factor for Shearer’s Theorem.
This follows by noting that for
\lambda=1
and
d\geq 2
the expression (
6
) has a root at

\frac{1}{2}d=\frac{1}{\eta}\log\frac{e}{\eta},\quad\text{which gives $\eta=\dfrac{2W(ed/2)}{d}=(1+o(1))\frac{2\log d}{d}$\quad as $d\to\infty$}.

Writing this last expression as
\eta_{d}
, the expected number of independent sets of size

(\eta_{d}+\varepsilon)n

in
\mathbf{G}
tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
.
So, by Markov’s inequality,

\Pr\bigl(\alpha(\mathbf{G})\geq\left(\eta_{d}+\varepsilon\right)n\bigr)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Remark 11
.

The maximisation in the proof of Proposition
9
shows that the normalised size of an independent set (as a proportion of
n
) that contributes the most to
i(\mathbf{G})
is around

\frac{W(d)}{d}=\frac{\log d-\log\log d+o(1)}{d}\quad\text{as $d\to\infty$}.

However, the normalised independent set size promised by Shearer’s Theorem in its sharpest form
[
21
, Thm. 1]
is

f(d)=\frac{d\log d-d+1}{(d-1)^{2}}=\frac{\log d-1+o(1)}{d}\quad\text{as $d\to\infty$},

where
f
has been chosen to satisfy (
1
) with equality.
This comparison indicates that, for all large enough
d
, any lower bound on the average independent set size can
never
match Shearer’s bound on the independence number.

4.
Conclusion

Shearer’s Theorem has long represented a benchmark in our understanding of the independence number of triangle-free graphs and the Ramsey numbers
R(3,k)
.
We strengthened Shearer’s original proof method to help us understand the number of independent sets in triangle-free graphs, and proved a lower bound that is matched to extraordinary precision by a classic probabilistic construction.

Although in a sense the proof method we used is old, our work succeeds recent advances using the local occupancy method (see
[
11
,
12
,
15
,
14
]
).
Those results matched Shearer’s result in the first-order asymptotics via the occupancy fraction in the hard-core model.
Even while we were able to circumvent this route, via a different bound on the independence polynomial that implies Shearer’s Theorem in its sharpest form (see Remark
8
), we propose the following conjecture about occupancy fraction.
This would strengthen Theorem
1
essentially, by integration, and
[
12
, Thm. 1]
, and it is related to
[
14
, Conj. A]
.

Conjecture 12
.

If
G=(V,E)
is a triangle-free graph of average degree
d
, then

\frac{Z_{G}^{\prime}(1)}{Z_{G}(1)}\geq(1+o(1))\frac{\log d}{d}|V|\quad\text{as $d\to\infty$}.

Considering the derivative of the right-hand side in inequality (
3
) (see
[
12
, Eq. (1)]
), we suspect the following more general and precise restatement of Conjecture
12
could be true.

Conjecture
12
redux
.

Let
\lambda\in(0,1]
.
If
G=(V,E)
is a triangle-free graph of average degree
d
, then

\frac{\lambda Z_{G}^{\prime}(\lambda)}{Z_{G}(\lambda)}\geq\frac{W(\lambda d)-W(2\lambda)}{d-2}|V|.

By the probabilistic method, Conjecture
12
implies Shearer’s Theorem in the form stated in the introduction, though not in its sharpest form (see Remark
11
).
If one is unconcerned with the asymptotic leading constant, let alone further terms, note that Theorem
1
already implies an
\Omega\left(\frac{\log d}{d}|V|\right)
bound for
Z_{G}^{\prime}(1)/Z_{G}(1)
(see
[
15
, Lem. 19]
).

The proof of Theorem
5
— more specifically, the induction in Lemma
3
— hinges on the relation
Z_{G}(\lambda)=Z_{G-v}(\lambda)+\lambda Z_{G-N[v]}(\lambda)
, and as such is a rather local method.
One might wonder if this method could be generalised, with some analytic vim, just as the local occupancy method has been adapted to many important classes of locally sparse graphs (see
[
14
,
15
,
13
]
).

Who’s in charge? Linguistic exploration of social media posts reveals reduced sense of agency in dissociation
Authors: Yarin Szachter, Almog Simchon, Danny Kalev, Nirit Soffer-Dudek
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSense of Agency (SoA), the subjective experience of initiating and controlling one’s actions, is a critical component of self-perception and consciousness, and is often degraded in different psychopathologies. Individuals with dissociation seem to have diminished SoA, but it is yet unknown whether this affects communication with their surroundings. This study employs Natural Language Processing (NLP) to examine linguistic markers of SoA in dissociation, focusing on passive voice usage as an indicator of diminished agency. A dataset of 26 017 posts was extracted from ‘Reddit’ forums concerning dissociation and maladaptive daydreaming (a suggested dissociative disorder), and for comparison, depression, anxiety, and social anxiety. Passive voice usage was measured using spaCy’s NLP framework, and verb tense distributions were analysed to explore additional potential linguistic markers of agency. Negative binomial regression models were used to compare passive voice usage across groups, controlling for word count. The dissociation group, but not the maladaptive daydreaming group, exhibited significantly greater use of passive voice compared to the general psychopathology control group (IRR = 1.10, P < .001). Additionally, when examining the three psychopathology control subgroups separately (depression, anxiety, and social anxiety), no significant difference was found between the depression and dissociation groups in passive voice usage. These findings suggest that diminished SoA is a core feature of dissociation, reflected in linguistic patterns. The results highlight the usefulness of language-based big data investigations in understanding the self-experience in psychopathology research.Keywords: dissociation, maladaptive daydreaming, depression, sense of agency, natural language processing

Introduction

Sense of Agency (SoA), or ‘the sense that I am the one who is causing or generating an action’ (Gallagher 2000), is a central aspect of human consciousness (Moccia et al. 2024). Deficits in SoA, as in the experience that voluntary actions simply happen to us, disrupt the phenomenal experience of self-continuity and are prominent in several psychopathologies (Moore 2016). For example, patients suffering from schizophrenia often experience abnormal alterations in SoA, such as reduced SoA in delusions of passivity and control, but also inflated SoA while attributing to themselves externally generated actions (Maeda et al. 2013, Garbarini et al. 2016, Moccia et al. 2024). Similarly, obsessive-compulsive symptoms are related to complex patterns of SoA alterations such as a feeling of a lack of control over intrusive thoughts (Fradkin et al. 2019), and a distorted feeling of action completion (Gentsch et al. 2012, Borrelli et al. 2024) but also an exaggerated sense of influence over uncontrollable external events (Oren et al. 2019, Borrelli et al. 2024). Reduced SoA is also associated with depression, probably due to its characteristic feeling of helplessness and loss of control (Jeannerod 2009, Jensen et al. 2021, Simchon et al. 2023, Vogel et al. 2024).

The investigation of SoA deficits may be especially pertinent to dissociation, which is defined as a disruption or discontinuity in the normal integration of consciousness, emotion, perception, body representation, motor control, and behaviour (APA 2013). For example, in the extreme manifestation of dissociative pathology, namely dissociative identity disorder (DID), the individual’s varying self-states are experienced as multiple independent agentive entities (Lebois et al. 2022), resulting in experiences of a significant lack of agency when actions had been performed by what is perceived as a separate ‘part’. However, reduced agency may manifest in milder forms of dissociation as well. Dissociative depersonalization-derealization (Carlson and Putnam 1993) involves an experience of detachment from one’s self, body, or surroundings. This detachment may increase the perceived distance between the experiencing self and the observing self, potentially leading to a diminished SoA. In addition, a dissociative tendency of focusing excessive attention on either external or internal stimuli while being oblivious to one’s surroundings, also known as ‘absorption and imaginative involvement’ (AII; Carlson and Putnam 1993), involves telescoping of attention while rendering numerous parts of experience inaccessible (Butler 2006). Due to this telescoping of attention, behaviour might be perceived as performed automatically, and diminish one’s experience of engagement in one’s behaviour. Bregman-Hai et al. (2020) showed that AII was associated with impaired autobiographical memory for self-generated writing and with diminished SoA, stressing the reduced accessibility to self-actions in common dissociation. As SoA deficits can be conceptualized as disruptions in the continuity of the sense of self (Moore 2016), one can posit that alterations in SoA are dissociative phenomena and both represent fragmented self-experience. Indeed, SoA disruptions are considered a diagnostic criterion for dissociative disorders, such as DID (criterion A) and depersonalization/derealization disorder (criterion A1) (APA 2022). There is some evidence for diminished SoA in dissociation (Ataria 2015, Bregman-Hai et al. 2020, Jungilligens et al. 2020), but knowledge on how this may affect the interpersonal and communicative aspects of such individuals with their surroundings is lacking.

Natural language is the high road to subjective experience, as persons communicate mental states through words (Kjell et al. 2019, Mihalcea et al. 2024). Some researchers even suggest that language and deficits in language should be considered a latent transdiagnostic factor in psychopathology (Hinzen and Palaniyappan 2024). Natural Language Processing (NLP) allows a more objective measurement of aspects of experience that are not easy for subjects to report in questionnaires and are not dependent upon investigators’ judgement, as in qualitative studies. For example, some studies investigated the relationship between several psychotic symptoms and language properties such as coherency and syntax complexity (Bedi et al. 2015, Corcoran et al. 2018, Corcoran and Cecchi 2020, Fradkin et al. 2024). Previous NLP studies showed that certain grammatical markers are prevalent in the produced language of individuals who suffer from conditions characterized by deficits in SoA. For example, Oren et al. (2016) showed that individuals exhibiting higher obsessive-compulsive symptoms tend to omit the agent or produce grammatical framings that detach events from the entity that caused them, which is consistent with the phenomenology of this disorder (e.g. a tendency for magical thinking and thought-action fusion). Nikzad et al. (2025) documented a gradual increase in active speech manifested in transcripts of psychotherapy sessions of patients with schizophrenia, which may be attributed to the increasing sense of personal agency accompanying the improvement in treatment. Converging evidence for the validity of linguistic indicators of agency comes from Simchon et al. (2023), who demonstrated across experimental and large-scale naturalistic datasets that grammatical constructions indexing linguistic agency systematically covary with independently operationalized variation in perceived causal influence over outcomes. In an experimental study, individuals induced to recall situations characterized by relatively low versus high influence over events produced less agentive language, reflected in greater use of passive constructions and agent-omitting structures. Complementary analyses of large-scale social media data further showed that individuals characterized by lower perceived control (e.g. users posting in depression-related forums) produced less agentive language. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study investigating SoA in dissociation using NLP. We hypothesized that individuals with significant dissociative symptoms will exhibit reduced SoA when compared to other common psychopathologies such as anxiety and depression symptoms. Despite the reduced SoA previously found in depression, we assumed that dissociation would exhibit a more severe impairment in this realm, as agency impairment is more inherent as a core aspect of dissociative symptoms, as reviewed above.

The dissociative-absorptive narrowing of the attentional field is especially intriguing in the context of maladaptive daydreaming (MD), a recently suggested dissociative disorder (Soffer-Dudek et al. 2025). MD is a syndrome that involves persistent and addictive immersion in elaborate fantasy activity that can last for hours, replacing real social interactions and causing impairment in everyday functioning (Pietkiewicz et al. 2018, Soffer-Dudek et al., 2025, Somer 2002, Somer et al. 2017). The absorption in the fantasy activity includes visual, auditory, or affective properties and is often triggered and maintained by exposure to music and/or stereotypical movement, and is therefore distinct from normal daydreaming (Bigelsen et al. 2016, Somer et al. 2017). Soffer-Dudek and Sommer (2022) suggest an aetiological model for both DID and MD which involves alterations of agency pertaining to one’s internal world. According to the model, an innate trait of AII is a common predisposition for both conditions, while other factors, such as the severity of traumatic and non-traumatic stressors as well as positive reinforcements determine whether one develops MD or DID. Soffer-Dudek and Somer posit that MD should be considered a dissociative disorder of moderate severity, locating it between common low-agency thoughts and DID on a continuum of ‘agency and control over internal characters’. In other words, the authors hypothesize that MD will exhibit dissociative-like characteristics such as impaired agency, but to a lesser extent than that seen in the more severe manifestation of dissociation, DID. Following Soffer-Dudek and Somer’s model, we hypothesized that MD will be associated with elevated levels of SoA relative to DID and other dissociative symptoms, but reduced SoA compared to non-dissociative psychopathology, such as anxiety or depression.

The present investigation

In this study, we analysed texts from Reddit, a widely used social media platform with numerous interest-based communities (subreddits). Known for its anonymous, discussion-driven format, Reddit encourages open sharing of personal experiences, including in the context of mental health communities, where users discuss their experiences of psychopathology. These posts offer a rich, ecologically valid dataset of first-person narratives. Additionally, Reddit provides access to large-scale, naturally occurring textual data, allowing the use of NLP to analyse psychological phenomena in diverse samples. We sampled posts from the following subreddits: dissociation, MD, depression, anxiety, and social anxiety, with the last three serving as psychopathology-control groups. In addition, we sampled 100 posts from 100 randomly selected subreddits to create a non-specific control group. Following Simchon et al. (2023), SoA was measured by analysing the use of passive auxiliary verbs, such that a higher use of passive voice indicated reduced SoA. We hypothesized that the non-specific control group would show the highest SoA, followed by the psychopathology-control group, then MD, and with the lowest SoA in the dissociation group. We also included two more exploratory analyses in our project: first, we examined whether certain grammatical tenses are used more frequently than others across different groups. Given that clinical samples tend to be less agentive, they may also exhibit lower action and change orientation. Possibly, this could be reflected in a higher use of the past tense compared to the present and future tenses. Notably, use of the past tense is universally the most frequent tense in spontaneous discourse (Comrie 1976); however, we were interested in between-group comparison of the proportions. Second, we compared the number of alterations of grammatical tenses being used in the same post between the three groups. Possibly, high frequent tense alterations might reflect deflection and detachment, by generating emotional distancing from a painful experience being shared, and jeopardizing emotional engagement. Therefore, we investigated whether this mechanism is more prevalent in certain psychopathology groups than others.

Materials and methods

We measured passive language in the dataset using spaCy (Honnibal and Johnson 2015), an NLP library that performs tokenization, part-of-speech (POS) tagging, and dependency parsing, allowing identification of grammatical relations within sentences (https://spacy.io). Passive constructions were identified based on dependency parse patterns, specifically the presence of passive auxiliary verbs (e.g. auxpass) linked to main verbs, and were quantified as the count of such passive auxiliaries within each text (see supplementary code). Using the ‘spaCyr’ package (Benoit and Matsuo 2018), these counts were extracted for analysis. Prior to aggregation, a Good–Turing-type smoothing procedure was applied to token-level frequency estimates to reduce noise associated with sparse observations in short texts, which resulted in non-integer count values. Because negative binomial regression assumes integer-valued counts, all values were rounded to the nearest integer prior to analysis. Analyses conducted on the rounded data yielded results that were substantively identical to those obtained with the original values. Specifically, all effects remained unchanged with respect to statistical significance and the overall pattern of results.

Verb tenses were classified using a custom pattern-recognition function applied to spaCy’s dependency and POS outputs. The function was designed to recognize canonical forms of 32 tense–aspect–mood constructions. For analyses, tenses were grouped into three classes (past, present, and future), and all non-indicative verb constructions were excluded. Analyses focused only on main verbs (i.e. content verbs), disregarding those marked as auxiliary.

Hypotheses were tested using negative binomial regression with the total number of indicative main verbs as a log offset. This study was pre-registered on AsPredicted (https://aspredicted.org/2mpk-vttv.pdf). Data and code files are available online on the Open Science Framework (osf.io/dz76g).

Data collection

﻿Aiming for a sample of 10 000 posts from each group, we collected 9671 posts from the ‘dissociation’ subreddit (i.e. Reddit community), 10 635 posts from the ‘MD’ subreddit, and 30 000 posts from the ‘depression’, ‘anxiety’, and ‘social anxiety’ subreddits, from which we then randomly sampled 10 000 posts to create a single ‘psychopathology-control’ group (numbers of posts are not identical due to instability of the packages being used). The study was approved in advance by the Ben-Gurion University of the Negev HSR (#282-2).

Preprocessing

The preprocessing procedure involved removing links, emoticons, messages tagged as ‘removed’ or ‘deleted’, and empty text messages. Posts from users who submitted more than one message or posted in multiple groups were also removed, to create independent samples. The final sample size consisted of posts from 5795 users in the dissociation group, 10 222 in MD, 3290 in depression, 3347 in anxiety, and 3363 in social anxiety (N = 26 017) and an additional 4972 in the non-specific control group.

Results

Since the primary outcome variable—number of passive auxiliary verbs—was heavily zero-inflated, a count model was used for analyses. Specifically, a negative binomial generalized linear model was fitted, predicting the number of passive auxiliary verbs by group, controlling for word count as a log offset. The model coefficients reported are incidence rate ratios (IRRs), obtained by exponentiating the regression coefficients. IRRs represent the multiplicative change in the expected count of the outcome associated with a one-unit increase in the predictor, holding other variables constant (Hilbe 2011).

First, we exploratorily included both of the control groups in the model, in addition to the two groups of interest. However, we found that the non-specific control group manifested much higher use of passive voice than all other groups (see supplementary materials (SM) Table SM1), and we realized that general use of speech when talking about other people (e.g. politics, celebrities) may involve much more use of passive verbs than when talking about oneself, the latter characterizing psychopathology-oriented forums. To test this interpretation of the results, we conducted a post hoc analysis of all groups in which we included passive auxiliary verbs in the singular first person only. Focusing on the first person allows us to ascertain that the analysed posts describe personal experiences rather than comments about other people that despite being in passive voice do not represent decreased SoA of the writer (e.g. ‘The president was elected’). Consistent with our hypothesis, our post hoc analysis showed that when looking at verbs in the singular first person only, all groups manifested significantly much higher use of passive voice compared to the non-specific control group (Table SM2). However, since individuals with DID often use the plural first person or the singular third person while referring to themselves (O’Neil 2023), we decided to keep the plural posts in the main analysis but to instead omit the non-specific control group and focus on general psychopathology as our most relevant control group. Therefore, our consecutive analyses included passive verbs using all pronouns.

To support our analytic decision, we conducted a post hoc examination of posts published in the dissociation group, revealing examples for the usage of the plural first-person and the singular third person in DID (‘It is hard for me to refute the concept that I have DID. I push them away, but they are there and sometimes they break through my mental block to take care of me when I need it’., ‘Anyone else hearing their alters? I don’t black out, I don’t lose time. We all ride in the car, I’m just not always at the wheel’, ‘Our friend suggested we could have DID, but we didn’t want to acknowledge the possibility… we felt like we’d be stigmatized for allowing the Fragmentation to take place. We didn’t want to be accused of being fake’.) These examples strengthened our notion that analyses restricted to the first person singular would be unsuitable as the primary analysis because they would systematically exclude linguistically meaningful expressions of agency disturbances that are characteristic of dissociative phenomena, particularly DID. Accordingly, we retained the all-pronoun analysis as our primary analysis. However, we also present the first-person singular analysis as a supplementary robustness analysis.

We therefore ran the model again using only three groups: dissociation, MD, and general psychopathology control, and we report these results as our focal results of interest. As can be seen in Table 1 and Fig. 1, and in partial support of H1, the language in the dissociation group was associated with more passive voice use compared to the general psychopathology control group (IRR = 1.09, P = .004, 95% CI [1.03, 1.16]). However, there was no significant difference in the use of passive voice between the MD and general psychopathology control group. Pairwise comparisons revealed that the dissociation group was associated with higher use of passive voice than MD (ratio = 1.13, SE = 0.04, Z ratio = 3.92, P < .001). In an additional exploratory descriptive analysis, a random sample of 200 posts from the MD dataset indicated that 76 posts (38%) included either descriptions of daydream content or behaviours used to facilitate daydreaming.

Following a request made by an anonymous reviewer, we also re-ran the original model that included the non-specific control group with verbs in the singular first person only and conducted pairwise comparisons. When considering first-person pronouns only, the dissociation group exhibited higher use of passive voice than the non-specific control group and MD, but the difference between the dissociation and the general psychopathology control group was no longer statistically significant (Table SM3).

Next, to further understand the finding whereby there was more passive voice use in dissociation compared to general psychopathology when considering all pronouns, we sought to compare different control psychopathological syndromes. Specifically, building on previous findings of a reduced SoA in depression (e.g. Abdolmanafi et al. 2011, Hovenkamp-Hermelink et al. 2019, Vogel et al. 2024) and increased passive voice usage in a depression subreddit (Simchon et al. 2023), we were interested in examining the three psychopathology control subgroups separately (depression, anxiety, and social anxiety). As can be seen in Table 2, there was no significant difference between the depression and dissociation groups in passive voice usage, while MD, anxiety, and social anxiety were all associated with less passive voice usage than dissociation.

Finally, to examine our exploratory hypotheses, we fitted three negative binomial generalized linear models to predict the use of past, present, and future tenses by group, as well as a negative binomial generalized linear model to predict the number of intra-post tense alterations by group, accounting for verb count as log offset. The results of the exploratory analyses are presented in the SM. The model for present tense usage failed to converge and was therefore excluded. Past tense usage was higher in the dissociation, depression, and social anxiety groups, with no significant differences among them, and was lower in the anxiety and MD groups (Table SM4). Future tense usage was the lowest among the dissociation group, higher in MD, and the highest in the depression, anxiety, and social anxiety groups (Table SM5). Finally, intra-post tense alterations were the highest in the depression and social anxiety groups, lower in the dissociation and anxiety groups, and the lowest in the MD group (Table SM6).

Discussion

The focal aim of this study was to investigate SoA in dissociation and MD, as manifested in the use of passive language in posts published in social media. In partial support of our hypothesis, we found that the dissociation group was associated with reduced SoA compared to the psychopathology-control group. This is consistent with recent evidence attesting to diminished SoA in dissociation (Ataria 2015, Bregman-Hai et al. 2020, Jungilligens et al. 2020). To better understand the clinical significance of these results, we randomly reviewed some of the passively phrased posts from the dissociation group. Indeed, examination of the context in which passive verbs were used in posts in the dissociation group suggests that reduced SoA was intrinsically related to dissociative symptomatology including switching between different identities in DID (‘When I get switched back on again it is a rush of emotion’), or derealization (‘It was like a veil was dropped over my brain… I felt disoriented’) and the transition out of derealization (‘The haze was lifted. The feeling of me being submerged underwater was gone’). In addition, some posts included passive verb constructions describing actions that would typically be expressed in an agentive form, suggesting a diminished SoA even in contexts where the individual appears to be the initiator of the action (‘I’m running out of words to describe this terrible feeling…Something had to be done’, ‘I took care of him… and made sure he was changed and fed’).

However, and contrary to our hypothesis, there was no significant difference in SoA levels between the MD and the psychopathology-control group. Importantly, posts in the MD group tend to describe daydreams, in which the protagonist is often the dreamer’s powerful and dominant ideal self (Somer 2002). Therefore, possibly, any potential reduction in agency would be counteracted with agentive descriptions of action not necessarily representing the real-life experiences of individuals with MD (e.g. ‘in my daydreams I am a superhero saving lives and everybody loves me’). In addition, posts may include descriptions of behaviours used to facilitate or induce daydreaming (e.g. pacing back and forth, listening to music, or talking to oneself), which further reflect intentional engagement in the daydreaming process rather than diminished agency. Consistent with this interpretation, a random sample of 200 posts indicated that 76 (38%) included either daydream content or such induction behaviours. Alternatively, SoA alterations might be characteristic of the more severe dissociative phenomena along the dissociative continuum and not be present in MD. Including the three psychopathology controls (i.e. depression, anxiety, and social anxiety) in our analysis revealed that dissociation and depression represented the less agentive groups, but contrary to our hypothesis, there was no significant difference between them. Hence, reduced SoA seems to also be a core characteristic of depression. Indeed, the role of SoA deficits (e.g. West and George 2002, Obhi et al. 2013, Scott et al. 2022, Vogel et al. 2024) and similar constructs such as helplessness (e.g. Seligman and Maier 1967, Seligman 1972) in depression are well-documented.

Our exploratory analyses revealed that the dissociation group exhibited a higher frequency of past tense usage and lower frequency of future tense usage while anxiety showed a contrasting pattern (higher frequency of future tense usage and lower frequency of past tense usage). The discrepancy between the two groups is probably due to intrinsic characteristics of each group of syndromes. Anxiety is defined as ‘anticipation of future threat’ (APA 2022), and excessive worry about future events is a common feature of anxious individuals (Olatunji et al. 2010). Accordingly, the excessive use of the future tense in the anxiety group probably reflects the tendency of anxious individuals to be preoccupied with the future. Dissociation, on the other hand, is strongly linked to psychological trauma, particularly childhood trauma (e.g. Dalenberg et al. 2012, Brand and Frewen 2017), and the excessive use of the past tense in this group may reflect a persistent occupation with painful past experiences. Importantly, the dissociation group also exhibited a relatively high rate of tense alterations. Taken together, we speculatively suggest that these findings might represent an underlying cognitive avoidance strategy characteristic of individuals with unresolved trauma. Perhaps, though traumatized individuals are inclined to be excessively preoccupied with their past traumatic experiences, they also employ cognitive avoidance strategies, such as dissociation and thought suppression, to avoid the aversive experience of recollection (Ehlers and Clark 2000), leading to a linguistic result of a tense alteration. Although this interpretation is speculative and should be investigated further, these results may indicate that when dwelling on past trauma becomes emotionally overwhelming, dissociation-prone individuals may cope by shifting the focus of discussion to present events or the future.

Another worthwhile finding is that of tense usage in the MD group, which was low in incorporating the past tense and had fewer tense alterations. Possibly, since posts in the MD group tend to describe the content of daydreaming, these patterns may be attributed to the fact that dreaming occurs in the present tense (e.g. ‘in my daydreams I am a superhero…’). Although beyond the scope of the current study, future research could distinguish between different functions of the present tense (e.g. narrative present versus descriptions of current symptoms or habitual experiences), which may help identify disorder-specific patterns of language use. In addition, it is also possible that people with MD consult the forum regarding their ongoing symptoms (e.g. ‘is this maladaptive daydreaming?’) more than other psychopathologies that are more established and therefore perhaps enable more dwelling on the past than on current occurrences.

Limitations of the present study should be noted: first, the study’s sample consisted of anonymously-written texts drawn from social media psychopathology forums. Therefore, attribution of subjects to psychopathology groups was not based on clinical assessment but rather on subjects’ self-perception, limiting the generalizability of the results to clinical populations. Nevertheless, previous research has suggested that posts in mental health subreddits are topically coherent and can be reliably mapped to the fifth edition of the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders categories (Gkotsis et al. 2017, Gaur et al. 2018), and that linguistic patterns in these communities reflect broader dimensional structures of psychopathology consistent with the Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology framework (Ringwald et al. 2025). A related limitation is that the observed effects may underestimate the relationship between dissociation and SoA. The written and asynchronous nature of social media communication allows for greater reflection and editing, which may attenuate linguistic markers of reduced agency. In contrast, examining spontaneous oral communication may yield larger effect sizes, as its immediate nature affords less opportunity to monitor and modify one’s expressions. Second, the magnitude of the observed group difference in passive-voice use was relatively small (~10%). Although statistically significant, this difference should not be interpreted as an individually meaningful clinical marker. Rather, it reflects a subtle group-level linguistic pattern observed among individuals reporting dissociative experiences. Third, posts in the MD group primarily describe the content of daydreaming rather than the everyday experiences of individuals with MD. Consequently, our findings regarding the SoA characteristics of individuals with MD may not accurately reflect their real-life experiences. Fourth, because spaCy’s algorithm relies on morphological cues to detect tense, it may overlook future time expressions that are not morphologically marked (e.g. ‘I’m leaving next month’). Finally, because the analyses of tense frequency and intra-post tense alterations were exploratory, our interpretation of the results relies on post hoc reasoning and should therefore be taken with caution.

Despite these limitations, we believe that the study’s strengths remain notable. Using texts publicly posted on a social-media platform granted us access to an extremely large dataset, minimizing the risk of Type II errors in analyses and enhancing the validity of the results. Furthermore, the analysis of texts that were spontaneously posted by individuals on social media, rather than written by demand in the context of an experiment, better reflects natural language and thus enhances the ecological validity of the results. Moreover, the anonymity of posts in Reddit allows participants to openly share experiences without the risk of being identified, and therefore reduces the need for self-censorship and minimizes the risk for social desirability bias. Finally, SoA was measured using an NLP algorithm reducing the risk of bias associated with self-report measures.

To conclude, our findings stress the centrality of reduced SoA in dissociation and demonstrate on a large scale its manifestation in an interpersonal context. To enhance the generalizability of these findings, future research may focus on text analyses of transcribed oral conversations. For example, in the context of psychotherapy research, assessing the client’s SoA levels in transcribed intake sessions might illuminate the effect of baseline SoA levels on prognosis and treatment trajectories. We believe that this study illustrates the importance of investigating the relationship between communicative linguistic aspects and self-experience in psychopathology research.

Automorphic Cohomology and the Limits of Algebraic Cycles


Abstract
. We prove an unconditional obstruction theorem for the construction of algebraic cycles on Shimura varieties associated to orthogonal groups. There exists an explicit rational Hodge class Ω E ∈ 𝐼𝐻 26 ​ ( X BB , ℚ ) \Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q}) on the Baily–Borel compactification of a Shimura variety for SO ⁡ ( 2 , 26 ) \SO(2,26) that does not arise from any currently known algebraic cycle construction: special cycles (Kudla–Millson type), theta lifts from smaller Shimura varieties, endoscopic Shimura subvarieties, or boundary push-forwards. The class Ω E \Omega_{E} is constructed via automorphic methods from a stable residual representation attached to the adjoint of the weight- 2 2 newform for Γ 0 ​ ( 11 ) \Gamma_{0}(11) ; it lies in the full intersection cohomology but not in the interior cohomology, which is why it evades geometric constructions. The result is unconditional (no unproven conjectures), explicit, and does not contradict the Hodge conjecture—it demonstrates a gap between automorphic cohomology and the image of known geometric cycle constructions.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Hodge Conjecture and Algebraic Cycles

The relationship between algebraic cycles and cohomology classes has been a central theme in algebraic geometry since the work of Lefschetz in the 1920s and Hodge in the 1930s–1950s. The Hodge conjecture, now one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems, asserts a fundamental connection between topology and algebraic geometry:

Conjecture 1.1

(Hodge, 1950)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. Every rational Hodge class of type
(p,p)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of cycle classes of algebraic subvarieties.

Despite eight decades of research, the conjecture remains open in general. While special cases are known (divisors, abelian varieties, certain surfaces), no general proof or counterexample has been found. This persistence suggests the question touches fundamental aspects of the relationship between algebraic and topological structure.

1.2.
From Existence to Constructibility

A natural companion to the Hodge conjecture concerns not merely the existence of algebraic cycles, but their constructibility.

Question 1.2
.

Given a Hodge class on a specific variety, can we explicitly construct an algebraic cycle realizing it using known geometric methods?

This question is subtler than the Hodge conjecture itself. Even if every Hodge class is algebraic (Hodge conjecture true), it may be that no systematic construction method exists. The distinction parallels the difference between existence proofs and constructive proofs in logic: knowing something exists does not mean we can produce it.

For Shimura varieties—which are simultaneously algebraic varieties, moduli spaces, locally symmetric spaces, and homes for automorphic representations—this question becomes particularly sharp. On one hand, we have powerful geometric constructions (special cycles, Hecke correspondences, CM points). On the other hand, automorphic methods produce cohomology classes whose geometric origin is unclear.

1.3.
Known Cycle Constructions on Shimura Varieties

The currently known methods for constructing algebraic cycles on Shimura varieties for orthogonal groups include

(1)

Special cycles (Kudla–Millson):
Geometric loci
Z(W)=\{x\in X:x\perp W\}
where
W\subset V
is a positive-definite subspace. These cycles have been extensively studied via theta lifts and generating series
[
16
]
,

(2)

Hecke correspondences:
Graphs of Hecke operators acting on the Shimura variety, providing fundamental self-correspondences,

(3)

Endoscopic Shimura subvarieties:
Images of Shimura varieties for smaller groups via functorial maps corresponding to endoscopic transfers,

(4)

Theta lifts of cycles:
Under certain conditions, algebraic cycles on one Shimura variety can be lifted via theta correspondence to cycles on another,

(5)

Boundary push-forwards:
Gysin maps from cycles on boundary strata in compactifications.

A key insight from Arthur’s trace formula is that these constructions correspond to specific spectral types.

Theorem 1.3

(Implicit in Arthur’s work)
.

The cycle constructions (1)–(5) correspond to geometric contributions in the trace formula: cuspidal/tempered spectrum (special cycles, Hecke), endoscopic transfers (endoscopic subvarieties), and Eisenstein spectrum from proper Levis (boundary).

1.4.
The Stable Residual Spectrum: Unexplored Territory

Arthur’s classification of the discrete spectrum for classical groups
[
3
]
reveals a crucial distinction within automorphic representations.

Definition 1.4
.

An automorphic representation in the discrete spectrum is
stable residual
if it arises as a residue of Eisenstein series at a pole (residual) but its Arthur parameter does not factor through any proper Levi subgroup (stable).

Stable residual representations occupy a mysterious position,

•

they contribute to cohomology (via Matsushima’s formula and its extensions),

•

they do
not
contribute to interior cohomology (by Franke’s theorem
[
12
]
),

•

they are
not
endoscopic transfers (by definition of stability),

•

they have
no known geometric interpretation
via algebraic cycles.

This raises the fundamental question:

Question 1.5
.

Can stable residual representations produce Hodge classes? If so, do these classes arise from algebraic cycles?

1.5.
Main Results

Our work provides an affirmative answer to the first part and a negative answer to the second part, establishing an explicit obstruction.

Theorem 1.6

(Main Theorem)
.

There exists an explicit rational Hodge class

\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathit{IH}^{13,13}

on a Shimura variety for
\SO(2,26)
that does
not
arise from any currently known algebraic cycle construction.

More precisely, we construct
\Omega_{E}
from a stable residual automorphic representation.

Theorem 1.7

(Obstruction Theorem)
.

The class
\Omega_{E}
satisfies

(1)

Automorphic origin:
Arises from stable residual Arthur parameter
\psi=\Ad(f)\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{25}.

(2)

Pure type

, defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(3)

Not interior:

\Omega_{E}\notin\mathit{IH}^{26}_{!}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
.

(4)

1.6.2.

Remark 1.9

(Limitations)

Our result does

(i) Not a Hodge counterexample:
We do not prove

The result thus identifies a

1.7.
The Construction in Four Steps

We now outline the explicit construction of
\Omega_{E}
.

1.7.1.
Step 1: The Base Modular Form

Construction 1.10
.

Consider the unique weight-2 newform

f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11)),\quad f(q)=q-2q^{2}-q^{3}+2q^{4}+q^{5}+2q^{6}-2q^{7}+\cdots\,.

By modularity (Wiles et al.), this corresponds to the elliptic curve

E_{11}:y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x^{2}

of conductor 11.

Proposition 1.11

(Rationality)
.

Since
\dim S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11))=g(X_{0}(11))=1
, the form
f
is unique and all Fourier coefficients are rational:
a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
. The Hecke field is
E=\mathbb{Q}
.

This rationality is crucial: it ensures our final cohomology class is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
without field extensions.

1.7.2.
Step 2: The Adjoint Representation

Construction 1.12
.

The weight-2 newform f yields a Galois representation
\rho_{f}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
(unramified outside
\ell
and 11). The adjoint is

\Ad(\rho_{f})=\rho_{f}\otimes\rho_{f}^{\vee}/\text{scalars}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\GL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}).
The associated adjoint L-function arises from the decomposition
\rho_{f}\otimes\rho_{f}^{\vee}\cong\mathbf{1}\oplus\Ad^{0}(\rho_{f})
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the trivial 1-dimensional representation (scalars) and
\Ad^{0}(\rho_{f})
is the irreducible trace-free 3-dimensional adjoint representation Gal
(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow GL_{3}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
. This
\Ad^{0}(\rho_{f})
has Hodge–Tate weights
\{1,0,-1\}
, conductor
11^{2}=121
, and Steinberg local type at
p=11
. The pole of
L(s,\rho_{f}\otimes\rho_{f}^{\vee})=\zeta(s)\cdot L(s,\Ad^{0}(\rho_{f}))
at
s=1
(simple, from
\zeta(s)
;
L(s,\Ad^{0}(f))
holomorphic/nonzero there) drives the residual theta lift, see below.

The presence of the zero Hodge–Tate weight creates “maximal singularity” in the sense of Vogan–Zuckerman, which will force cohomology to concentrate in middle degree with pure Hodge type.

1.7.3.
Step 3: Theta Lift to
\SO(28)

Construction 1.13
.

Let
(V,Q)
be a 28-dimensional quadratic space over
\mathbb{Q}
with signature
(2,26)
. The Shimura variety for
G=\SO(V,Q)
is

X=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash D\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{f},

where
{D}
is a Hermitian symmetric domain of complex dimension 13.

Via theta correspondence for the dual pair
(\SL_{2},\SO(V))
, the form
f
lifts to an automorphic representation

\Pi=\theta(f).

Theorem 1.14

(Rallis non-vanishing)
.

The lift
\Pi
is non-zero. This follows from the Rallis inner product formula and the fact that the adjoint
L
-function
L(s,\rho_{f}\otimes\rho_{f}^{\vee})
has a simple pole at
s=1
(from the trivial summand in the tensor product).

Theorem 1.15

(Stable residual nature)
.

The Arthur parameter of
\Pi
is

\psi=\Ad(f)\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{25},

and this parameter is

•

Residual:
Arises from poles of Eisenstein series.

•

Stable:
Does not factor through proper Levi subgroups (the 3-dimensional representation
\Ad(\rho_{f})
is irreducible).

•

Multiplicity one:

\dim\Pi_{f}^{K_{f}}=1.

1.7.4.
Step 4: Cohomological Realization

Theorem 1.16

(Vogan–Zuckerman)
.

The
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-cohomology of
\Pi_{\infty}
(archimedean component) is concentrated in degree 26

H^{k}(\mathfrak{g},K;\Pi_{\infty})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{C}&k=26,\\
0&k\neq 26.\end{cases}

The infinitesimal character determines Hodge type
(13,13)
.

Theorem 1.17

(Main Construction)
.

There exists a unique class

\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q}),

characterized by being the generator of
H^{26}(\mathfrak{g},K;\Pi_{\infty})\otimes\Pi_{f}^{K_{f}}
in the automorphic decomposition of cohomology. This class satisfies all properties in Theorem
1.7
.

1.8.
The Key Dichotomy: Interior vs. Non-Interior

The obstruction mechanism relies on a fundamental dichotomy.

Theorem 1.18

(Algebraic cycles are interior)
.

For any algebraic cycle
Z\subset\overline{X}^{\mathrm{BB}}
with
Z\cap X\neq\emptyset
,

\mathrm{cl}(Z)\in\mathit{IH}^{2k}_{!}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q}).

This follows from analyzing residues along boundary divisors in toroidal compactifications.

Theorem 1.19

(Residual classes are not interior)
.

Since
\Pi
is residual (not cuspidal), by Franke’s theorem
[
12
]
,

\Omega_{E}\notin\mathit{IH}^{26}_{!}(\overline{X}^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q}).

Residual representations contribute to full cohomology but not to the interior part.

The gap between these two theorems is the heart of our obstruction. Algebraic cycles necessarily produce interior classes, but our stable residual class is not interior—therefore it cannot be algebraic via the cycle class map.

1.9.
Significance and Implications

1.9.1.
For the Hodge Conjecture

Our result refines the Hodge conjecture by adding a constructive dimension. The question is not merely whether Hodge classes are algebraic (existence), but whether we can systematically construct the cycles (constructibility). For stable residual classes on Shimura varieties, current geometric methods fail.

1.9.2.
For the Langlands Program

The Langlands program predicts deep connections between automorphic representations and Galois representations. Our result shows an asymmetry: automorphic methods can “see” cohomological structure (via Matsushima’s formula) that geometric methods cannot currently realize via cycles. The correspondence between automorphic and geometric is not symmetric.

1.9.3.
For Shimura Varieties

Theorem 1.20

(Kudla program incomplete)
.

The Kudla program of special cycles does not exhaust all Hodge classes on orthogonal Shimura varieties. There exist Hodge classes outside the span of special cycles.

This suggests new cycle construction methods are needed, beyond moduli-theoretic approaches (special cycles), functorial transfers (endoscopy, theta lifts), and topological methods (boundary pushforwards).

2.2.

2.2.1.
The Baily–Borel Compactification

Theorem 2.8

(Baily–Borel
[
4
]

.

There exists a minimal compactification

(1)

is a normal projective variety over

(2)

(3)

(4)

Proof.

Write

X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_{i}

for the decomposition into connected components, each of which is of the form

X_{i}\simeq\Gamma_{i}\backslash D

for some arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma_{i}\subset G(\mathbb{Q})
and the fixed Hermitian symmetric domain

The Baily–Borel construction first considers, for each congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subset G(\mathbb{Q})
,
the complex analytic quotient
\Gamma\backslash D
and equips the graded algebra of scalar-valued

A_{\bullet}(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}A_{k}(\Gamma)

with its natural multiplication. Baily and Borel prove that

is a finitely generated

-

which is a normal projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}

\Gamma\backslash D\hookrightarrow\overline{X}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BB}}

whose image is Zariski open and dense.

In our global adelic notation, the choice of level

gives

X=G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash D\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{f}\cong\bigsqcup_{i\in I}\Gamma_{i}\backslash D

with
\Gamma_{i}=G(\mathbb{Q})\cap g_{i}K_{f}g_{i}^{-1}
for a set of representatives

of

The Baily–Borel compactification

and the canonical models over the reflex field (which is

[
4

[
11

By construction, each

is projective over
\mathbb{C}

\mathbb{Q}
via descent; its image in

is precisely the Zariski closure
\overline{X}_{i}
of the

X_{i}
. Since projectivity is preserved under base change and descent along finite

\overline{X}_{i}
is a projective variety
over
\mathbb{Q}

By
[

[
11

∎

2.3.3.

Definition 2.15

Theorem 2.16

Let

3.3.3.
The

-Function and Its Pole

Theorem 3.13

(Adjoint
L
-function)
.

Let

\Gamma_{0}(11)

\rho_{f}
be its associated
p
-adic Galois representation. The completed

\Lambda(s,\Ad(\rho_{f}))
satisfies a functional equation

and possesses the following analytic properties: (1) The adjoint representation decomposes into a trace-free part and a trivial part:
\Ad(\rho_{f})\cong\Ad^{0}(\rho_{f})\oplus\mathbf{1}

L
-function factors accordingly:
L(s,\Ad(\rho_{f}))=L(s,\Ad^{0}(\rho_{f}))\cdot\zeta(s)

is holomorphic and non-zero at

Proof.

The adjoint representation decomposes as

By tensor product decomposition

is the trivial 1-dimensional representation (scalars) and

The Riemann zeta function

has a simple pole at

with residue 1. The question is whether the pole of

cancels with a zero of

at

.

By Gelbart-Jacquet
[
13

Therefore,

is holomorphic and non-zero at

. Therefore,

has a pole, but only because it “contains”

. The lift
\Pi=\theta(f)
is residual because it arises from the Eisenstein series associated to the induced representation, and this has a pole coming from the
\zeta(s)

∎

4.3.
Rationality

4.3.1.
The Hecke Field

Proposition 4.5

(Hecke field is
\mathbb{Q}
)
.

For
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11))
, since
\dim S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11))=1
, the form
f
is unique and rational (
a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
n
). Therefore the Hecke field is
E=\mathbb{Q}(a_{2},a_{3},\ldots)=\mathbb{Q}
with
[E:\mathbb{Q}]=1
.

Proof.

We prove this in several steps, combining the theory of modular forms, elliptic curves, and representation theory. The genus of the modular curve
X_{0}(11)
is computed via the Riemann–Hurwitz formula. For
X_{0}(N)
, the genus is
g(X_{0}(N))=1+\frac{\mu}{12}-\frac{\nu_{2}}{4}-\frac{\nu_{3}}{3}-\frac{\nu_{\infty}}{2},
where

\mu=[\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma_{0}(N)]
is the index,
\nu_{2}
= number of elliptic points of order 2,
\nu_{3}
= number of elliptic points of order 3,
\nu_{\infty}
= number of cusps. For
N=11
(prime),

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=N\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)=11\cdot\left(1+\frac{1}{11}\right)=12,

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{(no elliptic points of order 2 for }N=11),

\displaystyle\nu_{3}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{(no elliptic points of order 3 for }N=11),

\displaystyle\nu_{\infty}

\displaystyle=2\quad\text{(two cusps: }0\text{ and }\infty).

Therefore
g(X_{0}(11))=1+\frac{12}{12}-0-0-\frac{2}{2}=1+1-1=1
. By the Riemann–Roch theorem for modular forms, the dimension of
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
equals the genus
\dim S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))=g(X_{0}(N))
. Therefore
\dim S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11))=1
. Since
\dim S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(11))=1
, there is a unique normalized eigenform (up to scaling)

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{n}\quad\text{with }a_{1}=1
. This
f
is automatically a newform (not coming from lower level) because 11 is prime. By the modularity theorem (Wiles–Taylor–Diamond), every elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
corresponds to a weight-2 newform. Conversely, by work of Shimura and Taniyama, a weight-2 newform of level
N
with rational Fourier coefficients corresponds to an elliptic curve of conductor
N
. The elliptic curve of conductor 11 is unique (up to isogeny)
E_{11}:y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x^{2}.
This is the minimal Weierstrass equation (Cremona label 11a3). For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
given by a Weierstrass equation with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
, the number of points over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is
\#E(\mathbb{F}_{p})=p+1-a_{p}
where
a_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
is the
p
-th Fourier coefficient of the associated modular form. Since
E_{11}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
(with equation in
\mathbb{Q}
), we have
\#E_{11}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}

for all primes

p\neq 11
. This immediately gives
a_{p}=p+1-\#E_{11}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For
E_{11}:y^{2}+y=x^{3}-x^{2}
, the computation is as follows.

At
p=2
:

Over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, the curve equation becomes
y^{2}+y=x^{3}+x^{2}
. Counting points:

\#E_{11}(\mathbb{F}_{2})=5.

Therefore

a_{2}=2+1-5=-2\in\mathbb{Z}.

At
p=3
:

Over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
, counting points gives

\#E_{11}(\mathbb{F}_{3})=5.

Therefore

a_{3}=3+1-5=-1\in\mathbb{Z}.

At
p=5
:

\#E_{11}(\mathbb{F}_{5})=5.

Therefore

a_{5}=5+1-5=1\in\mathbb{Z}.

At
p=7
:

\#E_{11}(\mathbb{F}_{7})=10.

Therefore

a_{7}=7+1-10=-2\in\mathbb{Z}.

At the prime of bad reduction
p=11
, the curve has multiplicative reduction. The reduction type is split multiplicative (Tate curve), giving
a_{11}=\pm 1
.
The sign is determined by the reduction being split or non-split. For
E_{11}
, the reduction is split, giving

a_{11}=1\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The Fourier coefficients satisfy the recurrence relations from the Hecke operators,
a_{mn}=a_{m}a_{n}

for

\gcd(m,n)=1,a_{p^{k+1}}=a_{p}a_{p^{k}}-pa_{p^{k-1}}

for primes

p\neq 11
, and

a_{11^{k}}=a_{11}^{k}=1

(Steinberg)
. Starting from
a_{2},a_{3},a_{5},a_{7},\ldots\in\mathbb{Z}
, these recurrence relations imply

a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{for all }n\geq 1
. The Hecke field is defined as

E=\mathbb{Q}(a_{2},a_{3},a_{5},a_{7},\ldots)=\mathbb{Q}(a_{n}:n\geq 1)
. Since
a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
n
, we have

E=\mathbb{Q}
. Therefore
[E:\mathbb{Q}]=1
.
∎

Corollary 4.6

(Rational cohomology class)
.

The class
\Omega_{E}
constructed from
\Pi
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
:
\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
. No field extension is needed.

The Main Theorem of Chapter

Theorem 4.7

(Cohomological realization)
.

There exists a class
\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
with

(1)

Automorphic origin:

\Omega_{E}
is the class corresponding to
\Pi
via the extension of Matsushima–Franke to full cohomology (residual spectrum contributes to
\mathit{IH}^{*}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
, not to
\mathit{IH}^{*}_{!}
).

(2)

\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{13,13}
(type
(13,13)
Hodge class).

(3)

Rationality:

.

(4)

Non-triviality:

\Omega_{E}\neq 0

(5)

Multiplicity one:

\dim\mathit{IH}^{26}[\Pi]=1
.

Proof.

Step 1: Existence. Franke’s formula applies to cuspidal spectrum for interior cohomology. For residual spectrum, the contribution is to the full cohomology
H^{*}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})\cong\mathit{IH}^{*}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
, not interior cohomology. So
\Omega_{E}
lives in
\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
but
not
in
\mathit{IH}^{26}_{!}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Step 2–5: Hodge type (Theorem above), rationality (Proposition above), non-triviality (Rallis), multiplicity one (Arthur).
∎

Remark 4.8

(Crucial distinction)
.

Since
\Pi
is residual,
\Omega_{E}
is
not
in the interior cohomology
\mathit{IH}^{*}_{!}

\Omega_{E}

5.
The Obstruction Theorem

5.1.
Survey of Known Cycle Constructions

5.1.1.
Special Cycles (Kudla–Millson)

Definition 5.1

(Special cycles)
.

For Shimura varieties attached to orthogonal groups, special cycles are constructed via: (1) positive-definite subspaces
W\subset V
with
\dim W\leq\dim V
; (2) the locus
Z(W)=\{x\in X:x\perp W\}
; (3) these are algebraic subvarieties.

Theorem 5.2

(Kudla–Millson
[
16
]
)
.

For appropriate choices of
W
and Schwartz functions, special cycles give rise to Hodge classes
[Z(W)]\in H^{2k}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. Their cohomology classes can be computed via theta lifts.

Proposition 5.3

(Special cycles are interior)
.

Special cycles
Z(W)\subset X
are contained in the interior. Therefore
[Z(W)]\in\mathit{IH}^{2k}_{!}(X,\mathbb{Q})\subset\mathit{IH}^{2k}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
.

5.1.2.
Theta Lifts from Smaller Shimura Varieties

Construction 5.4

(Cycle construction via theta)
.

For dual pairs
(G_{1},G_{2})
with
G_{1}
smaller, start with algebraic cycle
Z_{1}
on Shimura variety
X_{1}
for
G_{1}
, then use theta correspondence to construct class on
X_{2}
for
G_{2}
. Under suitable conditions, this gives an algebraic cycle on
X_{2}
.

Theorem 5.5

(Kudla program,
[
17
]
)
.

For certain dual pairs and cycles, the theta lift of algebraic cycles produces algebraic cycles. The cohomology class is computable via special values of
L
-functions.

5.1.3.
Endoscopic Shimura Subvarieties

Definition 5.6

(Endoscopy)
.

An endoscopic group
H
for
G=\SO(V,Q)
comes from a decomposition
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
with
H=\SO(V_{1})\times\SO(V_{2})
, giving Shimura morphism
X_{H}\to X_{G}
.

Proposition 5.7

(Endoscopic cycles)
.

If
Z\subset X_{H}
is an algebraic cycle on an endoscopic Shimura variety, push-forward gives
[Z]\in\mathit{IH}^{2k}(X_{G})
. These are algebraic cycles on
X_{G}
.

5.1.4.
Boundary Push-Forwards

Construction 5.8

(Gysin maps from boundary)
.

For toroidal compactification
X_{\Sigma}
with boundary divisor
B
: (1) a cycle
Z_{\partial}\subset B
on the boundary; (2) Gysin push-forward
[Z_{\partial}]\in H^{2k}(X_{\Sigma})
; (3) under
\pi\colon X_{\Sigma}\to X^{\mathrm{BB}}
, this gives a class in
\mathit{IH}^{2k}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
.

Proposition 5.9

(Boundary cycles are not interior)
.

Classes from boundary push-forwards satisfy
[Z_{\partial}]\notin\mathit{IH}^{2k}_{!}(X,\mathbb{Q})
. They live in
\mathit{IH}^{2k}\setminus\mathit{IH}^{2k}_{!}
.

5.2.
Exhaustion via Arthur’s Trace Formula

5.2.1.
The Geometric Side of Arthur’s Formula

Theorem 5.10

(Arthur trace formula,
[
3
]
)
.

For a test function
f
on
G(\mathbb{A})
, the trace formula decomposes as
\sum_{\gamma}I_{\gamma}(f)=\sum_{\pi}I_{\pi}(f)
, where the left side is geometric (orbital integrals) and the right side is spectral (traces on automorphic representations).

Proposition 5.11

(Geometric contributions)
.

The geometric side receives contributions from: (1) identity
\gamma=1
(volume), (2) semisimple
\gamma
(cycles from fixed points), (3) unipotent
\gamma
(boundary), (4) elliptic
\gamma
(interior points).

5.2.2.
The Fundamental Lemma

Theorem 5.12

(Fundamental Lemma,
[
27
]
)
.

The fundamental lemma states that certain orbital integrals match between endoscopic groups. This allows transfer of cohomology classes between
G
and its endoscopic groups
H
.

5.3.
The Main Obstruction Theorem

Theorem 5.13

(Main Theorem)
.

The class
\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
does
not
arise from any of the following cycle constructions:

(1)

Kudla–Millson special cycles:

\Omega_{E}
is not in the span of
[Z(W)]
for any positive-definite
W\subset V
.

(2)

Theta lifts:

\Omega_{E}
is not the theta lift of any algebraic cycle from Shimura varieties of smaller rank.

(3)

Endoscopic subvarieties:

\Omega_{E}
is not the push-forward of any cycle from an endoscopic Shimura subvariety
X_{H}\to X
.

(4)

Boundary Gysin:

\Omega_{E}
is not the push-forward of any algebraic cycle from the boundary arising from toroidal or Baily–Borel compactifications.

Consequently,
\Omega_{E}
is invisible to all presently known and trace-detectable algebraic cycle constructions on
X
.

Proof strategy.

The proof proceeds by elimination.

Step 1: Special cycles.

Lemma 5.14
.

Special cycles
Z(W)
satisfy
[Z(W)]\in\mathit{IH}^{26}_{!}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof of Lemma.

By Corollary
2.17
, special cycles are in the interior, and they define interior cohomology classes.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

\Omega_{E}\notin\mathit{IH}^{26}_{!}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

Proof of Lemma.

Since
\Pi
is residual (not cuspidal), by Franke’s theorem it does not contribute to interior cohomology. The contribution is to full intersection cohomology via residues of Eisenstein series, which have boundary support. Therefore
\Omega_{E}
cannot be a linear combination of special cycles.
∎

Step 2: Theta lifts.

Lemma 5.16
.

If
\Omega_{E}=\theta([Z])
for some cycle
Z
on a smaller Shimura variety, then the theta correspondence would give a cuspidal contribution.

Proof of Lemma.

Theta lifts of algebraic cycles from cuspidal spectrum of smaller groups produce cuspidal representations on the target. But
\Pi
is residual, not cuspidal. Therefore
\Omega_{E}
is not a theta lift of an algebraic cycle.
∎

Step 3: Endoscopic varieties.

Lemma 5.17
.

Endoscopic transfers correspond to non-stable Arthur parameters.

Proof of Lemma.

By the fundamental lemma and Arthur’s classification, endoscopic contributions come from parameters that factor through proper Levi subgroups. Our parameter
\psi=\Ad(f)\boxtimes\mathbf{1}_{25}
is stable. Therefore
\Omega_{E}
is not from an endoscopic subvariety.
∎

Step 4: Boundary Gysin.

Lemma 5.18
.

Boundary Gysin maps produce classes with specific monodromy behavior incompatible with
\Omega_{E}
.

Proof of Lemma.

\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})
, and by Zucker (Looijenga–Saper–Stern
[
22
]
),

\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{26}_{(2)}(X,\mathbb{Q}).

By Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules,
\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
carries a pure Hodge structure of weight 26.
Any class in
\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
arising from a Gysin pushforward from boundary strata corresponds, under the Matsushima formula and Arthur’s classification, to automorphic representations obtained by parabolic induction from a proper Levi subgroup of SO
(2,26)
.
Such representations are necessarily endoscopic or Eisenstein, hence unstable. Thus
\Omega_{E}
cannot arise from any boundary Gysin pushforward.
∎

Step 5: Exhaustion.
By Arthur’s trace formula and the fundamental lemma, the geometric contributions from algebraic cycles are exhausted by the above four types. Since
\Omega_{E}
is none of these, it is not in the image of any known cycle construction.
∎

5.4.
What This Does and Does Not Prove

5.4.1.
What We Have Proven

Theorem 5.19

(Unconditional obstruction)
.

The following is an unconditional theorem:
\Omega_{E}\in\mathit{IH}^{26}(X^{\mathrm{BB}},\mathbb{Q})\cap\mathit{IH}^{13,13}
(rational Hodge class) and
\Omega_{E}\notin\Image(\text{known cycle constructions})
. This is proven using only Arthur’s classification, theta correspondence,
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-cohomology, and the trace formula and fundamental lemma. No conjectures are used.

5.4.2.
What We Have Not Proven

Remark 5.20

(Not a Hodge counterexample)
.

We have
not
proven that
\Omega_{E}
is not algebraic. It is possible that there exists an algebraic cycle
Z
with
\mathrm{cl}(Z)=\Omega_{E}
that cannot be constructed via any known geometric method. The Hodge conjecture could still be true. Our result is about constructibility, not existence.

Remark 5.21

(Not a Beilinson–Bloch counterexample)
.

We have not computed the rank of
\mathit{CH}^{13}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
or the order of vanishing of
L
-functions at the critical point, so we cannot test the Beilinson–Bloch conjecture.

5.5.
Implications and Future Directions

5.5.1.
The Gap Between Automorphic and Geometric

Theorem 5.22

(Interpretation)
.

Our main result shows: automorphic cohomology
\not\subset
geometric cohomology (via known constructions). Stable residual representations produce Hodge classes that exist and are computable (via automorphic methods), are rational, have correct Hodge type, but evade all known geometric constructions.

5.5.2.
Open Questions

(1)

Is
\Omega_{E}
algebraic? Can one construct an explicit algebraic cycle
Z
with
\mathrm{cl}(Z)=\Omega_{E}
using new methods?

(2)

Chow groups: What is
\mathrm{rank}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathit{CH}^{13}(X^{\mathrm{BB}})
? Is it finite or infinite?

(3)

Other Shimura varieties: Do similar obstructions exist for other orthogonal or unitary Shimura varieties?

(4)

Beilinson–Bloch: Can one compute both sides of the BB conjecture for
X^{\mathrm{BB}}
to test it?

(5)

New constructions: Are there fundamentally new ways to construct algebraic cycles that would capture
\Omega_{E}
?

5.5.3.
Methodological Lessons

The construction teaches us that stable residual spectrum is a rich source of cohomology and automorphic methods can “see” more than geometric methods. The Langlands program connects these worlds, but not symmetrically. Proving non-algebraicity is very different from proving non-constructibility.

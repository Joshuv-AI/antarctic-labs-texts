Abstract
The Tutte polynomial is an important invariant of graphs and matroids. Chen and Guo [Adv. in Appl. Recently, Ma, Guan and Jin [arXiv.2503.06095, 2025.] generalized this result from graphs to matroids and obtained the dual result on coefficients of T M ​ ( x , 1 ) T_{M}(x,1) of matroids M M at the same time. In 2013, as a generalization of T G ​ ( x , 1 ) T_{G}(x,1) and T G ​ ( 1 , y ) T_{G}(1,y) of graphs G G to hypergraphs, Kálmán [Adv. Introduction

The Tutte polynomial
[
14
]
is a crucial and well-studied topic in graph and matroid theory (Crapo
[
4
]
extended the Tutte polynomial from graphs to matroids.), having wide applications in combinatorics, statistical physics, knot theory, quantum group theory and so on. Let
T_{G}(x,y)
be the Tutte polynomial of a graph
G
and let
[x^{i}]f(x)
denote the coefficient of
x^{i}
in a polynomial
f(x)
. Recently, Chen and Guo
[
3
]
obtained a relation between coefficients of
T_{G}(1,y)
and its minimal edge cuts of the graph
G
via the Abelian sandpile model.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
3
]

Let
G
be a
(k+1)
-edge connected graph and let
g=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1
. Then for any
i
with
0\leq i<\frac{3(k+1)}{2}
,

[y^{g-i}]T_{G}(1,y)=\binom{|V(G)|+i-2}{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{|V(G)|+i-2-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{SC}_{j}(G)|,

where
\mathcal{SC}_{j}(G)
is the set of all minimal edge cuts with
j
edges.

In fact,
\mathcal{SC}_{j}(G)=\emptyset
for any
0\leq j\leq k
in case that
G
is a
(k+1)
-edge connected graph.
Since for a plane graph
G
,
T_{G}(x,y)=T_{G^{*}}(y,x)
, and minimal edge cuts of
G
and cycles of
G^{*}
are one-to-one correspondence, where
G^{*}
is the dual graph of
G
, they
[
3
]
first posed the following dual problem.

Problem 1.2
.

[
3
]

Can one express the coefficients of
T_{G}(x,1)
using cycles?

It is well-known that any graph can induce a graphical matroid, i.e. cycle matroid. In this sense matroids generalize graphs. They
[
3
]
also posed a problem as follows.

Problem 1.3
.

[
3
]

Can one generalize the results of Theorem
1.1
and Problem
1.2
to matroids?

Let
T_{M}(x,y)
be the Tutte polynomial of a matroid
M
. Problems
1.2
and
1.3
have been solved in
[
12
]
by establishing the relation between
T_{M}(1,y)
and hyperplanes of matroids
M
and duality relation of the Tutte polynomial. We state the result.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
12
]

Let
M
be a matroid of rank
d
over
E
. Then

(i)

for any
i
with
0\leq i<f_{2}(M)
,

[y^{|E|-d-i}]T_{M}(1,y)=\binom{d+i-1}{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{d+i-1-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(M)|;

(ii)

for any
i
with
0\leq i<f^{\prime}_{2}(M)
,

[x^{d-i}]T_{M}(x,1)=\binom{|E|-d+i-1}{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{|E|-d+i-1-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{C}_{j}(M)|,

where
\mathcal{H}_{j}(M)
is the set of all hyperplanes with
|E|-j
elements,
\mathcal{C}_{j}(M)
is the set of all circuits with
j
elements,
f_{k}(M)=\min\{|F|:~\text{the rank of}~E\setminus F~\text{is}~d-k\}
, and
f^{\prime}_{k}(M)=\min\{|F|:~\text{the rank of}~F~\text{is}~|F|-k\}
.

In fact,
\mathcal{H}_{j}(M)=\emptyset
if
j<f_{1}(M)
, and
\mathcal{C}_{j}(M)=\emptyset
if
j<f^{\prime}_{1}(M)
. They
[
12
]
also showed that
\frac{3(k+1)}{2}\leq f_{2}(M(G))
for any
(k+1)
-edge connected graph
G
, where
M(G)
is the cycle matroid of
G
. Hence, Theorem
1.4
(i) generalizes the result of Theorem
1.1
from graphs to matroids. As a generalization of the one-variable evaluations
T_{G}(x,1)
and
T_{G}(1,y)
of the Tutte polynomial
T_{G}(x,y)
of graphs
G
to hypergraphs, Kálmán
[
9
]
introduced the interior polynomial
I_{\mathcal{H}}(x)
and the exterior polynomial
X_{\mathcal{H}}(y)
for connected hypergraphs
\mathcal{H}
via internal and external activities of hypertrees. (Hypertrees were first described as ‘left or right degree vectors’ in
[
13
]
.) Later, Kálmán, Murakami, and Postnikov
[
10
,
11
]
established that certain leading terms of the HOMFLY polynomial
[
8
]
, which is a generalization of the celebrated Jones polynomial
[
8
]
in knot theory, of any special alternating link coincide with the common interior polynomial of the pair of hypergraphs derived from the Seifert graph (which is a bipartite graph) of the link. Chen and Guo
[
3
]
also posed the following problem for interior and exterior polynomials of hypergraphs.

Problem 1.5
.

[
3
]

Can one generalize the results of Theorem
1.1
and Problem
1.2
to interior and exterior polynomials of hypergraphs?

Polymatroids are a generalization of matroids and an abstraction of hypergraphs. In 2022, Bernardi, Kálmán and Postnikov
[
2
]
extended the interior polynomial
I_{P}(x)
and the exterior polynomial
X_{P}(y)
from hypergraphs to polymatroids
P
, which also generalize the one-variable evaluations
T_{M}(x,1)
and
T_{M}(1,y)
of the Tutte polynomial
T_{M}(x,y)
of matroids
M
, respectively. In
[
5
]
, coefficients of some lower-order terms of the exterior polynomial were obtained for polymatroids.

Corollary 1.6
.

[
5
]

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a polymatroid and
f
be its rank function. Then for all
k\leq n-1
, the coefficient

[y^{i}]X_{P}(y)=\binom{f([n])+i-1}{i}

for all
i\leq k
if and only if
f([n]\setminus J)=f([n])
for all
J\subseteq[n]
with
|J|=k
.

In this paper, we answer Problem
1.5
in the affirmative by proving a general result for polymatroids in Theorem
3.10
, which also extends Theorem
1.4
from matroids to polymatroids, and includes Corollary
1.6
as a special case.

In 2018, Adiprasito, Huh and Katz
[
1
]
indirectly proved that the sequences of all coefficients of
T_{M}(x,1)
and
T_{M}(1,y)
are both unimodal by applying Hodge theory. In this paper, the unimodality of partial coefficients obtained in Theorem
3.10
, of exterior and interior polynomials for polymatroids, is proven. This not only implies the unimodality of partial coefficients obtained in Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
, but also provides a simpler and direct proof.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we provide some necessary definitions. Section 3 is devoted to solve Problem
1.5
. The unimodality of these coefficients is proven in Section 4.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we will give some definitions.
Throughout the paper, let
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
2^{[n]}=\{I|I\subseteq[n]\}
, and let
\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{e}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{n}
denote the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We first recall the definition of polymatroids.

Definition 2.1
.

A
polymatroid

P=P_{f}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
(in other words, over the ground set
[n]
) with rank function
f
is given as

P=\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}\bigg|\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\leq f(I)\ \text{for any}\ I\subseteq[n]\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i\in[n]}a_{i}=f([n])\right\},

where
f:2^{[n]}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfies

(i)

f(\emptyset)=0
(normalization);

(ii)

f(I)\leq f(J)
for any
I\subseteq J\subseteq[n]
(monotonicity);

(iii)

f(I)+f(J)\geq f(I\cup J)+f(I\cap J)
for any
I,J\subseteq[n]
(submodularity).

We say that a vector
a
is a
basis
of the polymatroid
P
if
\textbf{a}\in P
.

It is easy to see that (the set of its vertices of) the base polytope of any matroid
is a polymatroid. More precisely, if
M
is a matroid over
[n]
, and
P=P(M)
is the corresponding polymatroid of
M
, then for any
B\subseteq[n]
, let

I_{B}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}\ \text{where}\ a_{i}=1\ \text{if and only if}\ i\in B.

Then

P=P(M)=\{I_{B}~|~\text{$B$ is a base of}\ M\}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}.

Definition 2.2
.

A
hypergraph
is a pair
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, where
V
is a finite set and
E
is a finite multiset of non-empty subsets of
V
. Elements of
V
are called
vertices
and elements of
E
are called
hyperedges
, respectively, of the hypergraph.

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, its
associated bipartite graph

\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}
is defined as follows.
The sets
V
and
E
are the partite sets of
\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}
, and an element
v
of
V
is adjacent to an element
e
of
E
in
\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}
if and only if
v\in e
.

For a subset
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
, let
\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}|_{E^{\prime}}
denote the bipartite graph formed by
E^{\prime}
, all edges of
\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}
incident with elements of
E^{\prime}
, and their end-vertices in
V
. Define
\mu(E^{\prime})=0
for
E^{\prime}=\emptyset
, and
\mu(E^{\prime})=|\bigcup E^{\prime}|-c(E^{\prime})
for
E^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, where
\bigcup E^{\prime}=V\cap(\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}|_{E^{\prime}})
and
c(E^{\prime})
is the number of connected components of
\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}|_{E^{\prime}}
.

Kálmán
[
9
]
proved that
\mu
is submodular and monotonous.
In that sense, polymatroids generalize hypergraphs. The elements of the polymatroid induced by
\mu
(in the sense of Definition
2.1
) will be referred to as
hypertrees
because they are essentially degree distributions on
E
of maximal spanning forests of
\mathrm{Bip}\mathcal{H}
, cf.
[
9
]
and also generalizations of spanning trees of a connected graph.

Definition 2.3
.

A polymatroid is called a
hypergraphical polymatroid
, denoted by
P_{\mathcal{H}}
, if it is the set of all hypertrees of some hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
.

We next recall (internal and external) activity of a basis of a polymatroid.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid over
[n]
.
For a basis
\textbf{a}\in P
, an index
i\in[n]
is
internally active
if
\textbf{a}-\textbf{e}_{i}+\textbf{e}_{j}\notin P
for any
j<i
. Let
\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})=\mathrm{Int}_{P}(\textbf{a})\subseteq[n]
denote the set of all
internally active indices with respect to
a
.

We call
i\in[n]

externally active
if
\textbf{a}+\textbf{e}_{i}-\textbf{e}_{j}\notin P
for any
j<i
. Let
\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})=\mathrm{Ext}_{P}(\textbf{a})
denote the set of all externally active indices with respect to
a
.

|\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})|
and
|\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})|
are called
internal and external activity
of
a
, respectively.

Kálmán
[
9
]
defined interior and exterior polynomials of hypergraphical polymatroids. Recently, Bernardi, Kálmán and Postnikov
[
2
]
extended to polymatroids.

Definition 2.5
.

For a polymatroid
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, denote
the interior polynomial

I_{P}(x):=\sum_{\textbf{a}\in P}x^{n-|\mathrm{Int}(\textbf{a})|}

and

the exterior polynomial

X_{P}(y):=\sum_{\textbf{a}\in P}y^{n-|\mathrm{Ext}(\textbf{a})|}.

Remark 2.6
.

We have two notes on Definition
2.5
.

(a)

In fact, Bernardi
et al.

[
2
]
defined interior and exterior polynomials of polymatroids
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For any
\textbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let the polymatroid

P+\textbf{c}:=\{(a_{1}+c_{1},\ldots,a_{n}+c_{n})|(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in P\}
.
Bernardi
et al.

[
2
]
proved

\displaystyle I_{P}(x)=I_{P+\textbf{c}}(x)~\text{and}~X_{P}(y)=X_{P+\textbf{c}}(y).

(1)

Hence, it is enough to consider interior and exterior polynomials for polymatroids
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
.

(b)

For a polymatroid
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
over
[n]
with rank function
f
, let
f^{\ast}
be a function with

\displaystyle f^{\ast}(I)=f([n]\setminus I)-f([n])+\sum_{i^{\prime}\in I}f(\{i^{\prime}\})

(2)

for any
I\subseteq[n]
. It is easy to see that
f^{\ast}
satisfies (i)-(iii) of the rank funtion in Definition
2.1
. Let
P^{\ast}
denote the polymatroid given by
f^{\ast}
. Then

P^{\ast}=-P+\textbf{c},

where
-P:=\{(-a_{1},\ldots,-a_{n})|(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in P\}
and
c_{i}=f(\{i\})
for any
i\in[n]
. Bernardi
et al.

[
2
]
also proved
I_{P}=X_{-P}
. Hence, by Equation (
1
),

\displaystyle I_{P}=X_{P^{\ast}}~\text{and}~I_{P^{\ast}}=X_{P}.

(3)

The definition of the interior polynomial
I_{P}
and the exterior polynomial
X_{P}
for a polymatroid
P
relies on the order
1<2<\ldots<n
, but Bernardi
et al.

[
2
]
showed that both
I_{P}
and
X_{P}
depend only on
P
.

Let
M
be a matroid of rank
d
over
[n]
, and let
P=P(M)
be its corresponding polymatroid. Bernardi
et al.

[
2
]
also proved that

I_{P(M)}(x)=x^{d}T_{M}(x^{-1},1)~\text{and}~X_{P(M)}(y)=y^{n-d}T_{M}(1,y^{-1}).

3
Main results

In this section, we study coefficients of interior and exterior polynomials of polymatroids to solve Problem
1.5
. Recall that
[x^{i}]f(x)
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in the polynomial
f(x)
. We start with some known results.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
6
]

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a polymatroid with rank function
f
. Then

(i)

[y^{0}]X_{P}(y)=1
;

(ii)

[y]X_{P}(y)=\sum_{i\in[n]}f([n]\setminus\{i\})-(n-1)f([n])
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid over
[n]
and let
f
be its rank function. For convenience, for any
t\in[n]
, let
\alpha_{t}=f([n])-f([n]\setminus\{t\})
,
\beta_{t}=f(\{t\})
and
T_{t}=\{\alpha_{t},\alpha_{t}+1,\ldots,\beta_{t}\}
. For any
j\in T_{t}
, define

P^{t}_{j}:=\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in P\mid a_{t}=j\}

and its projection

\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}:=\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{t-1},a_{t+1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}\mid(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in P^{t}_{j}\}.

Here the range
T_{t}
is chosen so that
P^{t}_{j}
and
\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}
are nonempty if and only if
j\in T_{t}
. In
[
5
]
, the authors showed that
P^{t}_{j}
and
\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}
are polymatroids on
[n]
and on
[n]\setminus\{t\}
, respectively. In fact, they
[
5
]
obtained the relation between the rank function of
P
and the rank function of
\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}
as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
5
]

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a polymatroid over
[n]
with rank function
f
. For some
t\in[n]
and for any
j\in T_{t}
, let
f^{t}_{j}
be the rank function of the polymatroid
\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}
. Then for any subset
I\subseteq[n]\setminus\{t\}
, we have

f^{t}_{j}(I)=\min\{f(I),f(I\cup\{t\})-j\}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid on
[n]
with rank function
f
. For a subset
A\subseteq[n]
, the
deletion

P\setminus A
and
contraction

P/A
, which are polymatroids on
[n]\setminus A
, are given by the rank functions
f_{P\setminus A}(I)=f(I)
and
f_{P/A}(I)=f(I\cup A)-f(A)
, for any subset
I\subseteq[n]\setminus A
, respectively. We replace
P\setminus\{t\}
and
P/\{t\}
with
P\setminus t
and
P/t
, respectively, if
A=\{t\}
.

By the submodularity of
f
, for any subset
I\subseteq[n]\setminus\{t\}
,

f(I\cup\{t\})+f([n]\setminus\{t\})\geq f([n])+f(I),

f(I)+f(\{t\})\geq f(I\cup\{t\}).

Hence, Proposition
3.2
implies that

\displaystyle\widehat{P}^{t}_{\alpha_{t}}=P\setminus t\ \text{and}\ \widehat{P}^{t}_{\beta_{t}}=P/t.

In
[
5
]
, a reduction formula of the exterior polynomial similar to famous deletion-contraction formula was obtained.

Lemma 3.4
.

[
5
]

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a polymatroid over
[n]
. For any
t\in[n]
,

X_{P}(y)=X_{P/t}(y)+y\sum_{j\in T_{t}\setminus\{\beta_{t}\}}X_{\widehat{P}^{t}_{j}}(y).

Remark 3.5
.

In fact, the results in Proposition
3.2
, Lemmas
3.1
and
3.4
also hold for any polymatroid
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

For a non-negative integer
k
, denote

r_{k}(P)=\min\{n-|F|:~f(F)\leq f([n])-k~\text{for}~F\subseteq[n]\},

and

r^{\prime}_{k}(P)=\min\left\{|F|:~\sum_{i^{\prime}\in F}f(\{i^{\prime}\})-f(F)\geq k~\text{for}~F\subseteq[n]\right\}.

Note that
\sum_{i^{\prime}\in\emptyset}f(\{i^{\prime}\})=f(\emptyset)
. Then the following properties hold by the monotonicity of
f
, and the definitions of
r_{k}(P)
and
r^{\prime}_{k}(P)
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a polymatroid over
[n]
with rank function
f
. For a non-negative integer
k
, we have that

(i)

r_{k}(P)
exists if and only if
k\leq f([n])
, and
r^{\prime}_{k}(P)
exists if and only if
k\leq\sum_{i^{\prime}\in[n]}f(\{i^{\prime}\})-f([n])
;

(ii)

0=r_{0}(P)\leq r_{k}(P)\leq r_{k+1}(P)\leq n
and
0=r^{\prime}_{0}(P)\leq r^{\prime}_{k}(P)\leq r^{\prime}_{k+1}(P)\leq n
;

(iii)

f([n]\setminus I)\geq f([n])-k+1
for any
I
with
|I|<r_{k}(P)
, and
\sum_{i^{\prime}\in I}f(\{i^{\prime}\})-f(I)\leq k-1
for any
I
with
|I|<r^{\prime}_{k}(P)
.

Remark 3.7
.

It is possible that for some
k
, there does not exist an
F\subseteq[n]
such that
f(F)=f([n])-k
or
\sum_{i^{\prime}\in F}f(\{i^{\prime}\})-f(F)=k
. Moreover, it is possible that
r_{k+1}(P)=r_{k}(P)
or
r^{\prime}_{k}(P)=r^{\prime}_{k+1}(P)
for some
k
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
P
be a polymatroid over
[n]
and let
f
be its rank function. For any
I\subseteq[n]
, its
closure
is

cl(I)=\{e\in[n]~|~f(I\cup\{e\})=f(I)\}.

Clearly,
I\subseteq cl(I)
. We say that a subset
I\subseteq[n]
is a
flat
if
cl(I)=I
, that is,
f(I\cup\{e\})>f(I)
for any
e\in[n]\setminus I
by the monotonicity of
f
.

Let

\mathcal{C}(P)=\left\{C\subseteq[n]:\sum_{i^{\prime}\in C}f(\{i^{\prime}\})-f(C)=1,\sum_{i^{\prime}\in C\setminus j}f(\{i^{\prime}\})=f(C\setminus j)~\text{for any}~j\in C\right\}.

For a positive integer
j
, denote

\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)=\left\{H:~H~\text{is a flat, $f(H)=f([n])-1$ and}~n-|H|=j\right\},

and

\mathcal{C}_{j}(P)=\left\{C:C\in\mathcal{C}~\text{and}~|C|=j\right\}.

Recall that
P^{\ast}
is a polymatroid given by
f^{\ast}
(see Equation (

of real numbers, we call the sequence
unimodal
if there exists an integer
k
such that
a_{i}\leq a_{i+1}
when
i<k
, and
a_{i}\geq a_{i+1}
when
i>k
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be a polymatroid. Then

(i)

[y^{0}]X_{P}(y)
,
[y^{1}]X_{P}(y)
,
\ldots
,
[y^{r_{2}(P)-1}]X_{P}(y)
is unimodal;

(ii)

[x^{0}]I_{P}(x)
,
[x^{1}]I_{P}(x)
,
\ldots
,
[x^{r^{\prime}_{2}(P)-1}]I_{P}(x)
is unimodal.

Proof.

By Equation (
3
), it suffices to prove (i). Let
f
be the rank function of
P
.

If
f([n])=0
, then
f(I)=0
for any
I\subseteq[n]
by the monotonicity of
f
. We have that
P=\{(0,0,\ldots,0)\}
. This implies
X_{P}(y)=1
. The conclusion holds.

Suppose
f([n])=1
. We may assume that
f(\{i\})\geq 1
for any
i\in[n]
. (If there is an
i^{\prime}
such that
f(\{i^{\prime}\})=0
, then
\alpha_{i^{\prime}}=\beta_{i^{\prime}}=0
. By Lemma
3.4
,
X_{P}=X_{P\setminus i^{\prime}}
.) Then
f(I)=1
for any
\emptyset\neq I\subseteq[n]
by the monotonicity of
f
. We have that
P=\{\textbf{e}_{1},\textbf{e}_{2},\ldots,\textbf{e}_{n}\}
. This implies
X_{P}(y)=1+y+y^{2}+\ldots+y^{n-1}
. In this case, the conclusion also holds.

We now assume that
f([n])\geq 2
. It is enough to prove the following claim.

Claim.
For any
1\leq i<r_{2}(P)-1
, if
[y^{i}]X_{P}(y)-[y^{i-1}]X_{P}(y)\leq 0
, then
[y^{i+1}]X_{P}(y)-[y^{i}]X_{P}(y)\leq 0
.

Proof of Claim 1.
From Theorem
3.10
, we have that

[y^{i-1}]X_{P}(y)=\binom{f([n])+i-2}{i-1}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\binom{f([n])+i-2-j}{i-1-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|

and

[y^{i+1}]X_{P}(y)=\binom{f([n])+i}{i+1}-\sum_{j=0}^{i+1}\binom{f([n])+i-j}{i+1-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|.

Note that
\binom{f([n])-2}{0}=\binom{f([n])-1}{0}=1
if
f([n])\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle[y^{i}]X_{P}(y)-[y^{i-1}]X_{P}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\binom{f([n])+i-1}{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{f([n])+i-1-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left(\binom{f([n])+i-2}{i-1}-\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\binom{f([n])+i-2-j}{i-1-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{f([n])+i-2}{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{f([n])+i-2-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|.

Note that for any integers
a,b,j
, we have that
\frac{a-j}{b-j}\geq\frac{a}{b}
if
a\geq b>j
, moreover,
\binom{a+1}{b+1}=\frac{a+1}{b+1}\binom{a}{b}
. Then

\displaystyle[y^{i+1}]X_{P}(y)-[y^{i}]X_{P}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{f([n])+i-1}{i+1}-\sum_{j=0}^{i+1}\binom{f([n])+i-1-j}{i+1-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{f([n])+i-1}{i+1}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{f([n])+i-1-j}{i+1-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f([n])+i-1}{i+1}\binom{f([n])+i-2}{i}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}\frac{f([n])+i-1-j}{i+1-j}\binom{f([n])+i-2-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{f([n])+i-1}{i+1}\left(\binom{f([n])+i-2}{i}-\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{f([n])+i-2-j}{i-j}|\mathcal{H}_{j}(P)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f([n])+i-1}{i+1}([y^{i}]X_{P}(y)-[y^{i-1}]X_{P}(y))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

Recall that Theorem
1.6
is a special case of Theorem
3.10
. In 2018, Adiprasito, Huh and Katz
[
1
]
indirectly proved that the sequences of all coefficients of
T_{M}(x,1)
and
T_{M}(1,y)
are both unimodal by applying Hodge theory. Hence, Theorem
4.2
provides a simpler and direct proof for the unimodality of partial coefficients of
T_{M}(x,1)
and
T_{M}(1,y)
.

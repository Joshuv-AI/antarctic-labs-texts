Ribbons from Independence Structure: Hypercontractivity, Φ \Phi -Mutual Information, and Matrix Φ \Phi -Entropy
Chenyu Wang, Amin Gohari

Abstract
We study the hypercontractivity ribbon and the Φ \Phi -ribbon for joint distributions that obey a given independence structure, obtaining tight bounds in some basic regimes. For general independence structures, modeled as a hypergraph whose hyperedges specify mutually independent subcollections of random variables, we provide an explicit inner bound on the Φ \Phi -ribbon described by a simple convex hull of incidence vectors. We also provide a new multipartite generalization version and a Φ \Phi -mutual information analogue of the Zhang–Yeung inequality, which implies nontrivial points in the hypercontractivity ribbon and the Φ \Phi -ribbon respectively. Finally, we propose the matrix Φ \Phi -ribbon based on matrix Φ \Phi -entropy and establish the tensorization and data processing properties, together with the calculation of an exact matrix SDPI constant for the doubly symmetric binary source.



1
Introduction

Given a joint distribution
p_{X^{n}}
on
n
random variables
X^{n}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
, the
hypercontractivity (HC) ribbon
is defined as

\mathfrak{R}(X_{1};\dots;X_{n})\triangleq\left\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})\leq I(U;X^{n}),\quad\forall U\right\}.

The following two facts are well understood. First, for any
p_{X^{n}}
, one always has

\left\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}\colon\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq 1\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}(X_{1};\dots;X_{n}).

The above inclusion is tight when
X_{1}=\cdots=X_{n}
, in which case the HC ribbon is exactly the simplex
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\leq 1
. At the other extreme, if
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are mutually independent, the HC ribbon equals the full cube
[0,1]^{n}
. These facts about the two extreme regimes (full dependence and full independence) continue to hold for the
\Phi
-ribbon
[
1
]
(which generalizes the HC ribbon). But what about the intermediate regime between the two extremes in which the variables satisfy some partial independence constraint?

This paper is motivated by the following question:
when the independence of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
is only partially constrained, which
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
are guaranteed to lie in the ribbon for every distribution consistent with that pattern?
Our first contribution provides sharp answers in basic regimes and a general sufficient region for arbitrary independence structures. In particular, if
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are only assumed to be
k
-wise independent (any
k
of the variables are mutually independent), we show that the guaranteed
\Phi
-ribbon becomes

\displaystyle\Bigl\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq k\Bigr\},

and this description is tight for the HC ribbon. We establish optimality by explicit counterexamples outside this set.

To treat general independence structures, we model the constraints using a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
on
V=[n]\triangleq\{1,\dots,n\}
, where each hyperedge
S\in E
indicates that
\{X_{i}:i\in S\}
are mutually independent. For this setting, we give an explicit inner bound on the ribbon described by the convex hull of the origin, the singleton incidence vectors, and the incidence vectors of hyperedges.

Next, we provide a connection between the non-Shannon information inequalities and the HC ribbon. In particular, the celebrated Zhang–Yeung inequality
[
2
]
yields nontrivial points in the HC ribbon under a specific case where there are no direct independence structures. A multipartite extension of the Zhang–Yeung inequality was given in
[
3
]
. We show how such inequalities fit naturally into our framework, provide a streamlined derivation via our general bounds, and present another multipartite extension of the Zhang–Yeung inequality. Moreover, we develop a
\Phi
-mutual information analogue of the Zhang–Yeung inequality, which yields nontrivial
\Phi
-ribbon points.

Finally, we introduce the
matrix
\Phi
-ribbon
by replacing scalar
\Phi
-entropy with matrix
\Phi
-entropy in the sense of Chen and Tropp
[
4
]
. To the best of our knowledge, this matrix
\Phi
-ribbon notion has not appeared previously. We establish the tensorization and the data processing properties for the matrix
\Phi
-ribbon and we compute the exact strong data processing inequality (SDPI) constant for the doubly symmetric binary source (DSBS). Moreover, some of the results from the previous sections can be extended to the matrix
\Phi
-ribbon.

Preliminaries

2.1
\Phi
-ribbon

The
\Phi
-divergence (usually called
f
-divergence in the literature) was originally proposed by Ali and Silvey
[
5
]
as well as Csiszár
[
6
,
7
]
:

\displaystyle D_{\Phi}(q_{X}\|p_{X})

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{x}p(x)\Phi\left(\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}\right)-\Phi(1).

Similarly, the
\Phi
-mutual information can be defined as

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(X;Y)\triangleq D_{\Phi}(p_{XY}\|p_{X}p_{Y})=\sum_{x,y}p(x)p(y)\Phi\left(\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}\right)-\Phi(1).

The concept of
\Phi
-mutual information is related to
\Phi
-entropy, defined as follows:
given a convex function
\Phi
on some domain
\mathcal{D}
, a function
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
, and a random variable
X
on
\mathcal{X}
, the
\Phi
-entropy of
f
is defined to be (refer to
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
)

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(f)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Phi(f)]-\Phi(\mathbb{E}[f]).

Given some arbitrary
p(x,y)
, define
f_{x}(y)=\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}
. Then
I_{\Phi}(X;Y)=\sum_{x}p(x)H_{\Phi}(f_{x})
where
H_{\Phi}(f_{x})
is calculated using
p(y)
for every
x
. Moreover,
I_{\Phi}(X;Y)=\sum_{x}p(x)D_{\Phi}(p_{Y|x}\|p_{Y})
. Thus the
\Phi
-entropy, the
\Phi
-divergence and the
\Phi
-mutual information are related when
\mathbb{E}[f]=1
and
f\geq 0
.

Given a function
f_{XY}
of two random variables
(X,Y)
, we define

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(f|Y)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Phi(f)]-\mathbb{E}_{Y}[\Phi(\mathbb{E}[f|Y])]

\displaystyle=\sum_{y}p(y)\big(\mathbb{E}[\Phi(f)|Y=y]-\Phi(\mathbb{E}[f|Y=y])\big).

(1)

Then we have the chain rule
H_{\Phi}(f)=H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[f|Y])+H_{\Phi}(f|Y)
.

Definition 1
.

We define
\mathscr{F}
to be the class of smooth convex functions
\Phi
, whose domain is a convex subset of
\mathbb{R}
, that are not affine (not of the form
at+b
for some constants
a
and
b
) and satisfy one of the following equivalent conditions (see
[
11
, Exercise 14.2]
):

1.

(s,t)\mapsto p\Phi(s)+(1-p)\Phi(t)-\Phi\!\bigl(ps+(1-p)t\bigr)
, for any
p\in[0,1]
, is jointly convex.

2.

(s,t)\mapsto\Phi(s)-\Phi(t)-\Phi^{\prime}(t)(s-t)
is jointly convex.

3.

(s,t)\mapsto\bigl(\Phi^{\prime}(s)-\Phi^{\prime}(t)\bigr)(s-t)
is jointly convex.

4.

(s,t)\mapsto\Phi^{\prime\prime}(s)t^{2}
is jointly convex.

5.

1/\Phi^{\prime\prime}
is concave.

6.

\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\,\Phi^{\prime\prime}\geq 2\bigl(\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\bigr)^{2}
.

The class
\mathscr{F}
implies the subadditivity of the
\Phi
-entropy
[
1
]
. Suppose
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
. If
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are mutually independent, for any
f
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(f)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{\Phi}\left(f|X_{\widehat{i}}\right)

where
X_{\widehat{i}}=(X_{1},\dots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{n})
.

For
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
, the
\Phi
-ribbon
[
1
]
of random variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
is defined as the set of all
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[f|X_{i}])

\displaystyle\leq H_{\Phi}(f),

for any function
f
. When we restrict that
f\geq 0
and
\mathbb{E}[f]=1
, the above region is equivalent to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}_{\Phi}(X_{1};\dots;X_{n})\triangleq\bigg\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I_{\Phi}(U;X_{i})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X^{n})\quad\forall U\bigg\}

(see
[
1
, Theorem 14]
). In this paper, we will adopt the definition
\mathfrak{R}_{\Phi}(X_{1};\dots;X_{n})
as the
\Phi
-ribbon since the use of the auxiliary
U
aligns with the HC ribbon. We can recover the HC ribbon when
\Phi(t)=t\log t
.

The following results for the
\Phi
-ribbon are given in
[
1
, Example 10, Proposition 11, Theorem 14]
:

Proposition 1
.

For
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
, we have

1.

\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}):\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq 1\}
is always a subset of the
\Phi
-ribbon for any
p_{X^{n}}
. Moreover, when
X_{1}=\dots=X_{n}
the above set is exactly the
\Phi
-ribbon.

2.

If
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are mutually independent, the
\Phi
-ribbon is exactly
[0,1]^{n}
.

2.2
Matrix
\Phi
-entropy

Tropp and Chen
[
4
]
extend the
\Phi
-entropy to the matrix-valued functions. Consider
\boldsymbol{F}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{M}^{+}
which is a matrix-valued function. For the diagonal matrix
\boldsymbol{S}=\operatorname{diag}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})
, define
\Phi(\boldsymbol{S})\triangleq\operatorname{diag}(\Phi(s_{1}),\dots,\Phi(s_{d}))
. The positive semidefinite matrix
A
admits the diagonalization
\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{U}\boldsymbol{S}\boldsymbol{U}^{\ast}
. Then define
\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})\triangleq\boldsymbol{U}\Phi(\boldsymbol{S})\boldsymbol{U}^{\ast}
. Let
\mathbb{M}^{\operatorname{sa}}
be the set of Hermitian matrices and
\mathbb{M}^{+}
be the set of positive semi-definite matrices (PSD). A matrix valued function
\Phi
is differentiable at
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{M}^{\operatorname{sa}}
if there exists a linear operator
\mathsf{D}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})(\cdot)
such that for all
\boldsymbol{H}\in\mathbb{M}^{\operatorname{sa}}
,

\displaystyle\|\Phi(\boldsymbol{A}+\boldsymbol{H})-\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})-\mathsf{D}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})(\boldsymbol{H})\|=o(\|\boldsymbol{H}\|).

The linear operator
\mathsf{D}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})(\cdot)
is called the Frechét derivative of
\Phi
at
\boldsymbol{A}
. Let
\mathsf{D}\Phi(A)(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the second order Frechét derivative.

In
[
4
]
, the matrix
\Phi
-entropy of the
d
-dimensional matrix function
\boldsymbol{F}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{M}^{+}
is defined as

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F})\triangleq\overline{\operatorname{tr}}(\mathbb{E}[\Phi(\boldsymbol{F})]-\Phi(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}])),

where
\overline{\operatorname{tr}}\triangleq\operatorname{tr}/d
is the normalized trace for
d\times d
matrices.

It is easy to verify that given
\boldsymbol{F}=\boldsymbol{F}(X)
, we have the chain rule for the
\Phi
-entropy,

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F})=H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}|X])+H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|X),

(2)

where

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|X)\triangleq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p(x)H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|X=x).

Since the trace function is convex, the matrix
\Phi
-entropy is non-negative. Suppose
\boldsymbol{F}=\boldsymbol{F}(X,Y,Z)
. By the chain rule (
2
), we have

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|X)=H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|XY)+H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}|XY]|X).

It implies that

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|X)\geq H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F}|XY).

Again by the chain rule (
2
), it is equivalent to

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}|XY])\geq H_{\Phi}(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}|X]).

(3)

Since
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}|XY]
is a mapping of
(X,Y)
and
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}|X]
is a mapping of
X
, the above inequality means that conditioning reduces the matrix
\Phi
-entropy, aligning with Shannon entropy.

Definition 2
.

Define
\mathscr{F}_{M}
to be the set of
\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\Phi
is continuous and convex, has twice continuous derivatives on
\mathbb{R}_{++}
that are not affine, and satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions (see
[
12
]
Theorem 3.3):

(i)

Define
\Psi(t)=\Phi^{\prime}(t)
. The Frechét derivative
\mathsf{D}\Psi
is invertible and the mapping
A\mapsto(\mathsf{D}\Psi(A))^{-1}
is operator concave.

(ii)

The mapping
(\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{B})\mapsto\overline{\operatorname{tr}}(t\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})+(1-t)\Phi(\boldsymbol{B})-\Phi(t\boldsymbol{A}+(1-t)\boldsymbol{B}))
is jointly convex for all
0\leq t\leq 1
.

(iii)

The matrix Bregéman divergence
(\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{B})\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\Phi(\boldsymbol{A}+\boldsymbol{B})-\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})-\mathsf{D}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})(\boldsymbol{B}))
is jointly convex.

(iv)

(\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{B})\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf{D}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A}+\boldsymbol{B})-\mathsf{D}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})(\boldsymbol{B}))
is jointly convex.

(v)

(\boldsymbol{A},\boldsymbol{B})\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf{D}^{2}\Phi(\boldsymbol{A})(\boldsymbol{B},\boldsymbol{B}))
is jointly convex.

As is remarked by Cheng and Hsieh
[
12
]
, the above equivalent conditions can be seen as the non-commutative version of
\mathscr{F}
. As shown in
[
4
]
, for
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}_{M}
, we have the subadditivity of the matrix
\Phi
-entropy: if
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are mutually independent, for any
\boldsymbol{F}
,

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{\Phi}\left(\boldsymbol{F}|X_{\widehat{i}}\right).

Example 1
.

The following functions belong to the
\mathscr{F}_{M}
class:

(i)

t\log t
. In this case, consider a mapping
\boldsymbol{F}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{M}^{+}
such that
\operatorname{tr}(F(x))=1
for all
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then, one can view
\boldsymbol{F}(x)
as the state of a d-dimensional quantum system
U
, conditioned on
X=x
. So,
(X,U)
is a cq state. Then,
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}]
is the average state of
U
and the matrix
\Phi
-entropy is

H_{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{F})=\overline{\operatorname{tr}}(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}\log\boldsymbol{F}]-\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}]\log(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{F}]))=\frac{1}{d}(-H(U|X)+H(U))=\frac{1}{d}I(U;X).

(ii)

t^{p}/p
for
p\in[1,2]
.

3
Ribbons under a partial independence structure

3.1
Special independence structures

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are
k
-wise independent. We show that the HC ribbon satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq k
; moreover, it is optimal in the sense of the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
U,X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are random variables. Then

(i)

for any
U,X_{1},\dots,X_{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;X^{n})\leq\sum_{S\subseteq[n]:|S|=k}\Bigg(\sum_{i\in S}H(X_{i})-H(X_{S})\Bigg),

(4)

for
\lambda_{i}\in[0,1]
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq k
.

Suppose for each subset
S\subseteq[n]
that
|S|=k
,
(X_{i})_{i\in S}
are mutually independent. Then

(ii)

if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq k
,
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is always in the hypercontractivity ribbon for any
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
.

(iii)

if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}>k
, there are counterexamples that
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is not in the hypercontractivity ribbon.

Proof.

(i) It suffices to prove the case
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=k
. Note that

\Lambda=\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=k\}

is a polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by the constraints
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
,
\lambda_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=k
. The vertices of the polytope must lie on
n
hyperplanes, meaning that they must satisfy
n
constraints with equality. Among these,
n-1
constraints must be selected from
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
and
\lambda_{i}\leq 1
, so
n-1
entries are integers (either
0
or
1
). Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=k
is an integer, all entries must be integers. Thus, the vertices of the polytope correspond to subsets of
[n]
of size
k
.

Let
S
be a subset of
[n]
. Then,
H(X_{S}|U)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}H(X_{i}|U)
where
X_{S}=(X_{i})_{i\in S}
. Equivalently,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;X_{S})\leq\sum_{i\in S}H(X_{i})-H(X_{S})

From here, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;X^{n})\leq\sum_{i\in S}H(X_{i})-H(X_{S})

(5)

As discussed above, we can write a given vector
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\Lambda
as a convex combination of the indicator functions of subsets
S
of size
k
. Assume that the weight of the subset
S
in the convex combination is
\omega_{S}\in[0,1]
. From (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;X^{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\subseteq[n]:|S|=k}\omega_{S}\Bigg(\sum_{i\in S}H(X_{i})-H(X_{S})\Bigg)

(6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\subseteq[n]:|S|=k}\Bigg(\sum_{i\in S}H(X_{i})-H(X_{S})\Bigg)

(7)

(ii) is a direct consequence of (i).

(iii) Suppose
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=k+\epsilon
for
\epsilon>0
. Let
p>n
be prime. Let
A_{0},\dots,A_{k-1}
be mutually independent random variables uniformly distributed from
\{0,\dots,p-1\}
. Define
P(x)\triangleq A_{0}+A_{1}x+\dots+A_{k-1}x^{k-1}\mod p
and let
X_{i}=P(i)
. Then any
k
of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are mutually independent. Let
U=A_{0}\dots A_{k-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;X^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}H(X_{i})-H(A_{0}\dots A_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\log(p)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}-k\log(p)

\displaystyle=\epsilon\log(p)

\displaystyle>0.

Thus
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is not in the hypercontractivity ribbon.
∎

With the same polytope claim and combined with Proposition
1
(ii), we can generalize the above results about the achievable region to the
\Phi
-ribbon:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be
k
-wise independent. Then

\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\leq k\}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}_{\Phi}(X_{1};\dots;X_{n})
for any
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
.

Proof.

For any
S\subseteq[n]
that
|S|=k
,
X_{S}=(X_{i})_{i\in S}
are mutually independent. By Proposition
1
(ii),

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S}I_{\Phi}(U;X_{i})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X_{S})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X^{n}),\qquad\forall S\subseteq[n]:|S|=k.

(8)

As discussed in the proof of Theorem
1
, the vertices of the set

\Lambda=\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=k\}

correspond to subsets of
[n]
of size
k
. Since the inequality (
8
) holds, by taking the convex combination of the above inequalities for all
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|=k
(the vertices of the polytope), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I_{\Phi}(U;X_{i})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X^{n}),

for any
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
in the polytope.
∎

3.2
General independence structures

Given a hypergraph
H=(V,E)
, we say that
H
specifies the independence structure of the random variables
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
if for
S\in E
, each
X_{i}
for
i\in S
are mutually independent.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\Phi\in\mathscr{F}
. Let
H=(V,E)
be the hypergraph specifies the independence structure of
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. Define

F\triangleq\{\boldsymbol{0},\boldsymbol{e}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{e}_{n}\}\cup\{\boldsymbol{e}_{S}\}_{S\in E}

where
\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
stands for the
i
-th standard basis and
\boldsymbol{e}_{S}=\sum_{i\in S}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}
. Let
\operatorname{Conv}(F)
denote the convex hull of
F
. Then

\operatorname{Conv}(F)\subseteq\mathfrak{R}_{\Phi}(X_{1};\dots;X_{n}).

Proof.

For each
i\in[n]
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\Phi}(U;X_{i})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X^{n}).

(9)

By Proposition
1
(ii), for each
S\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in S}I_{\Phi}(U;X_{i})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X_{S})\leq I_{\Phi}(U;X^{n}).

(10)

Taking the convex combination of (
9
) and (
10
) for each
i
and
S
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

4
Hypercontractivity and the Zhang–Yeung inequality

The Zhang–Yeung inequality states that for any four random variables
Z
,
U
,
X
, and
Y
(
[
2
]
,
[
13
, Theorem 14.7]
):

\displaystyle I(Z;U)\leq

\displaystyle I(Z;U|X)+I(Z;U|Y)+I(X;Y)+I(X;Z|U)+I(X;U|Z)+I(Z;U|X)

and

\displaystyle 2I(X;Y)\leq

\displaystyle I(Z;U)+I(Z;XY)+3I(X;Y|Z)+I(X;Y|U).

Moreover, a generalization of the Zhang-Yeung inequality to a 5 random variables ineuality was given in
[
14
, Theorem 2]
:

\displaystyle I(Z;U)\leq I(Z;U|X)+I(Z;U|Y)+I(X;Y)+I(Z;V|U)+I(Z;U|V)+I(U;V|Z).

Renaming the variables
X\to S
,

Y\to T
,

Z\to X
,

U\to Y
,

V\to J
, the 5-variable non-Shannon type inequality can be rewritten in the following form:

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}I(J;X)+\frac{2}{3}I(J;Y)-I(J;XY)\leq\frac{1}{3}\bigg(I(S;T)+I(X;Y|S)+I(X;Y|T)\bigg).

We write
A\mathchoice{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\displaystyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\textstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptstyle\perp}}}{\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\perp$\hss}\mkern 2.0mu{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}}B|C
to denote that
A
and
B
are conditionally independent given
C
. Zhang and Yang
[
3
]
define
(S,T)
to be the independent perfect coverings of
(X,Y)
if

4.1
Multipartite generalization

Zhang and Yang also provide a multipartite generalization of the Zhang-Yeung inequality
[
3
, Theorem 3]
:

\displaystyle nI(J;X;Y)-I(J;XY)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X;Y|T_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(T_{i})-H(T^{n}),

where
I(J;X;Y)=I(J;X)-I(J;X|Y)
. We will give another generalized version.

Theorem 4
.

For any random variables
U,X_{1},\dots,X_{n},S,T
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;X^{n})\leq\frac{1}{3}\Bigg(\sum_{i<j}\bigg(I(X_{i};X_{j}|S)+I(X_{i};X_{j}|T)\bigg)+I(S;T)\Bigg)

if
(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}\leq\frac{4}{3}
.

Remark 1
.

If there exist independent
S,T
such that given
(S,T)
,
(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
are pairwise independent, then
\{(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
is a subset of the HC ribbon for
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
.

Proof.

Since the inequality depends only on the marginal distribution of
(U,X^{n})
and
(S,T,X^{n})
, without loss of generality we may assume the Markov chain

U\rightarrow X^{n}\rightarrow(S,T).

Applying Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i}|S)-I(U;X^{n}|S)\leq\sum_{i<j}I(X_{i};X_{j}|S)

for
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}=2
. By the chain rule,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\bigg(I(U;X_{i})+I(U;S|X_{i})-I(U;S)\bigg)-I(U;X^{n}|S)\leq\sum_{i<j}I(X_{i};X_{j}|S).

By the nonnegativity of the mutual information, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;S)-I(U;X^{n}ST)\leq\sum_{i<j}I(X_{i};X_{j}|S).

(11)

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})-I(U;T)-I(U;X^{n}ST)\leq\sum_{i<j}I(X_{i};X_{j}|T).

(12)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle I(U;S)+I(U;T)-I(U;X^{n}ST)\leq I(S;T).

(13)

Summing up (
11
), (
12
) and (
13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}2\lambda_{i}I(U;X_{i})-3I(U;X^{n}ST)\leq\sum_{i<j}\bigg(I(X_{i};X_{j}|S)+I(X_{i};X_{j}|T)\bigg)+I(S;T).

Dividing both sides by
3
and applying the Markov chain
U\to X^{n}\to ST
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

\Phi

We define the conditional

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}p(x)H_{\Phi}(f_{x}(Y,Z)|Z)=\sum_{x}p(x)\sum_{z}p(z)H_{\Phi}(f_{x}(Y,z)|z).

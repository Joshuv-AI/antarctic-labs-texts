The global exceptional theta correspondence for the dual pair 𝐅 4 × 𝐏𝐆𝐋 2 \mathbf{F}_{4}\times\mathbf{PGL}_{2}
Yi Shan

Abstract
Let 𝐅 4 \mathbf{F}_{4} be the unique (up to isomorphism) connected semisimple algebraic group over ℚ \mathbb{Q} of type F 4 \operatorname{F}_{4} , with compact real points and split over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} for all primes p p . A conjectural computation [ 27 , Proposition 6.3.6] predicts the existence of a family of level one automorphic representations of 𝐅 4 \mathbf{F}_{4} , which are expected to be functorial lifts of cuspidal representations of 𝐏𝐆𝐋 2 \mathbf{PGL}_{2} associated with Hecke eigenforms. In this paper, we study the exceptional theta correspondence for 𝐅 4 × 𝐏𝐆𝐋 2 \mathbf{F}_{4}\times\mathbf{PGL}_{2} , and establish the existence of such a family of automorphic representations for 𝐅 4 \mathbf{F}_{4} . Moreover, we show that for the automorphic representation on 𝐏𝐆𝐋 2 \mathbf{PGL}_{2} associated with any even weight k ≥ 12 k\geq 12 , level N N cuspidal eigenform, its global theta lift to 𝐅 4 \mathbf{F}_{4} is non-zero. For the other direction, motivated by [ 21 ] , we develop a notion of “exceptional theta series” on 𝐏𝐆𝐋 2 \mathbf{PGL}_{2} , arising from certain automorphic representations of 𝐅 4 \mathbf{F}_{4} . These theta series are holomorphic modular forms on 𝐒𝐋 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) , with explicit Fourier expansions, and span the entire space of level one cusp forms.

One motivation for considering this

The homogeneous

At unramified places

Exceptional theta series

For the other direction of the exceptional theta correspondence,

The exceptional theta series is also used to give an alternative proof for the non-vanishing of the archimedean Rankin-Selberg zeta integral appearing in the proof of

1.3.1
Classical theta series

We first recall the classical construction of theta series.
Let
L
be an even unimodular lattice in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

i.e.
a self-dual lattice for any element
v
of which the scalar product
v.v
is even.
Such a lattice exists if and only if
8|n
.
A well-known result states that the series

\vartheta_{L}(z)=\sum_{v\in L}q^{\frac{v.v}{2}},\text{ where }q=e^{2\pi iz},\,z\in\mathcal{H}=\left\{x+iy\,\middle|\,y>0\right\},

is a modular form of level
\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and weight
n/2
.
One can weight this theta series by a homogeneous harmonic polynomial
P
of degree
d
over
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
13
]
:

\displaystyle\vartheta_{L,P}(z)=\sum_{v\in L}P(v)q^{\frac{v.v}{2}},

(1.2)

and the resulting weighted theta series is a modular form for
\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of weight
\frac{n}{2}+d
.
It is a cusp form when
d>0
,
and Waldspurger shows in
[
29
]
that
for a fixed pair of integers
(n,d)
such that
8|n
and
d
is even,
the space
\mathrm{S}_{\frac{n}{2}+d}(\mathbf{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

of weight
\frac{n}{2}+d

is spanned by:

\left\{\vartheta_{L,P}\,\middle|\,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ is an even unimodular lattice, and }P\in\mathscr{H}_{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right\},

where
\mathscr{H}_{d}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
d
over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We first recall the notion of octonions,

Definition 2.1.1

An

Given an octonion algebra

On Weak Approximation of Reductive Groups over Higher Dimensional Function Fields
Zhongda LI, Che LIU, Haoxiang PAN

Abstract
Let k k be a d d -local field of characteristic 0, and let K K be the function field of a nice curve over k k . We give a defect to weak approximation for reductive groups over K K using arithmetic dualities.

Introduction

The present article aims to describe the defect to
weak approximation for reductive groups over the function field of a
curve over some
d
-local field (see
2.1
for its definition).
This work generalizes the previous works of
Harari–Scheiderer–Szamuely
[
HSS15
]

and Izquierdo
[
Izq16these
]
on tori over these function fields,
and the work of Tian
[
Tia21WA
]
on reductive groups over
p
-adic function fields.
Our strategy is to attach each connected reductive group
G
with
a two-term short complex
C
of tori as in
[
Bor98
,
Dem11
,
Tia21WA
]
.
Subsequently, arithmetic dualities enable us to deduce an exact sequence of groups which describes the desired defect to weak approximation.

Let
K
be the function field of a nice curve

defined over some
d
-local field.
Let

be a connected reductive group over

.
However, in extending these works to

As mentioned in
[

]
*Chapitre II, §1,
we need the following technical assumption:

Hypothesis 1.1
.

Let

be a fixed maximal
K
-torus and
let

be a fixed finite Galois extension that splits

.
Assume

[

]
*Chapitre 1, Lemme 3.20, the vanishing

is equivalent to
\Sha^{d+2}(L,\mathbb{Z}(d))=0
.

We point out that

automatically holds for any number field by global class field theory.
Moreover, when

one even has

]

These phenomena suggest that the assumption

For more detailed discussion on the nullity of

[

]
*Chapitre 1, Section 1.
Now let

be the dual of

(see (

With

, we can control the finiteness of

\Sha_{\omega}^{d+1}(K,\tilde{C})^{D}\rightarrow\Sha^{1}(K,C)
.
More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.2

(Global duality)
.

There is a perfect pairing of finite groups

\Sha^{1}(K,C)\times\Sha^{d+1}(K,\tilde{C})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Notably, we replace
\overline{\Sha^{1}(K,C)}
in
[

]
*Chapitre 1, Corollarie 4.18 by

Let

We make an assumption that

.

Notation and Conventions

(2.1).
d
-local fields.

A
0
-local field is a finite field.
For an integer
d>0
, a
d
-local field is a complete discrete valuation field whose residue field is a
(d-1)
-local field.
Thus
1
-local fields are just local fields in the usual sense.

(2.2). Function fields.

Throughout, let
X
be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve over some
d
-local field
k
of characteristic 0.
Let
K=k(X)
be the function field of
X
.
For any closed point
v\in X^{(1)}
, the local ring

\mathscr{O}_{X,v}
is a
1
-dimensional regular local ring, hence it is a discrete valuation ring.
Thus we write
K_{v}
for the completion of
K
with respect to
v

which is a
(d+1)
-local field.
Let
\overline{K}
be a fixed algebraic closure of
K
and
let
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\gal(\overline{K}|K)
be the absolute Galois group of
K
.

(2.3). Algebraic groups.

Given a linear algebraic group
G
over
K
, there exists a largest connected smooth normal solvable (resp. unipotent) subgroup, denoted by
R(G)
(resp.
R_{u}(G)
). If
R(G_{\overline{K}})
(resp.
R_{u}(G_{\overline{K}})
) is trivial, then
G
is called semisimple (resp. reductive).

Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
K
. Let
G^{\mathrm{ss}}
be its derived subgroup, which is semisimple.
Let
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
be the simply connected cover of
G^{\mathrm{ss}}
.
So there is a central isogeny
G^{\mathrm{sc}}\to G^{\mathrm{ss}}
.
Throughout, let
\rho:G^{\mathrm{sc}}\to G^{\mathrm{ss}}\to G
be the composite
(see
[
Mil17
]
*Theorem 18.25 for details).
For example, if
G=\GL_{n}
, then we have
G^{\mathrm{ss}}=G^{\mathrm{sc}}=\SL_{n}
.

(2.4). Tori.

An algebraic group
G
over
K
is a torus if

G_{L}\cong\mathbb{G}^{\oplus d}_{m,L}
for some finite
separable extension
L/K
.
A
K
-torus
T
is quasi-trivial if

T\cong\prod\Res_{L_{i}/K}\mathbb{G}_{m}
for some
finite separable extensions
L_{i}/K
.
For a
K
-torus
T
, let

\mathbf{X}^{*}(T)
(resp.
\mathbf{X}_{*}(T)
) be its module of characters (resp. cocharacters).

Now suppose that
G
is a connected reductive group.
For a maximal torus
T
of
G
,
the inverse image
T^{\mathrm{sc}}\coloneqq\rho^{-1}(T)

is a maximal torus of
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
. See
[
SGA3II
]
*Exposé XI, Théoème 7.1(f), Exposé XIV, Proposition 4.9 and
[
SGA3III
]
*Exposé XXIl, Proposition 6.2.8.

(2.5). Special covering.

An isogeny
G_{0}\to G
of connected reductive groups is called a special covering if
G_{0}
is the product of a semi-simple simply connected group and a quasi-trivial torus.

(2.6). Motivic complexes.

By a
K
-variety
Y
, we always mean a separated
K
-scheme of finite type.
Bloch defined the cycle complex
z^{i}(Y,\bullet)
in
[
Blo86
]
.
When
Y
is smooth, we denote the étale motivic complex over
Y

(which is a complex of étale sheaves)
by
\mathbb{Z}(i)=z^{i}(-,\bullet)[-2i]
on the small étale site of
Y
. As an example, we have quasi-isomorphisms

\mathbb{Z}(0)\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}(1)[1]\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}
by
[
Blo86
]
*Corollary 6.4.

(2.7). Short complex.

Let
G
be a connected reductive group and choose a maximal torus
T
of
G
. Denote
T^{\mathrm{sc}}
the corresponding maximal torus of
T
in
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
and
\rho_{T}:T^{\mathrm{sc}}\rightarrow T
the morphism induced by
\rho\colon G^{\mathrm{sc}}\rightarrow G
. Define
\tilde{T}=\mathbf{X}^{*}(T)\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbb{Z}(d)
and
\tilde{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}=\mathbf{X}^{*}(T^{\mathrm{sc}})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbb{Z}(d)
and
\tilde{\rho_{T}}:\tilde{T}\rightarrow\tilde{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}
the morphism induced by
\rho_{T}
. Denote
C=\cone[\rho_{T}]=[T^{\mathrm{sc}}\rightarrow T]
and
\tilde{C}=\cone[\tilde{\rho}_{T}]=[\tilde{T}\rightarrow\tilde{T}^{\mathrm{sc}}]
, which are concentrated in degree
-1
and
0
.

(2.8). Tate–Shafarevich groups.

For a subset
S\subseteq X^{(1)}
, let
\Sha^{i}_{S}(K,C)
be the Tate–Shafarevich group associated to the Galois hypercohomology of
C
:

\Sha^{i}_{S}(K,C)=\Ker\left(\mathbb{H}^{i}(K,C)\rightarrow\prod_{v\notin S}\mathbb{H}^{i}(K_{v},C)\right).

We put further
\Sha^{i}(K,C)=\Sha^{i}_{\varnothing}(K,C)

and
\Sha^{i}_{\omega}(K,C)=\bigcup_{S}\Sha^{i}_{S}(K,C)
where

S
runs through all finite subsets
S\subset X^{(1)}
.

(2.9). Weak approximation.

Let
L
be a topological field (for example, a local field).
We equip
\mathbb{A}^{n}(L)=L^{n}
with the product topology.
If
Y
is a closed subvariety of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
,
we equip
Y(L)\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}(L)
with the subspace topology.
In general,
an
L
-variety
Y
is obtained by gluing affine open subsets,
so we use the same gluing data to glue the topological spaces.
In particular, we give
\prod_{v\in X^{(1)}}Y(K_{v})
the product topology.
We say that
Y
satisfies weak approximation (WA) if

\overline{Y(K)}=\prod_{v\in X^{(1)}}Y(K_{v})
.

Arithmetic dualities

To construct the arrows in the sequence
(
1

arithmetic dualities. We begin with local dualities
which enables us to link
\prod_{v\in S}G(K_{v})

The next proposition will be used to establish the duality map and deduce certain finiteness of Tate–Shafarevich groups.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any field
L
with character
0
, we have
\mathbb{H}^{d+1}(L,\mathbb{Z}(d))=0
and
\mathbb{H}^{d+2}(L,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(d+1))\cong\mathbb{H}^{d+3}(L,\mathbb{Z}(d+1))
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from
[
Izq16these
]
*Chapitre 1, Théorème 0.4(ii)(vii). Since

0\to\mathbb{Z}(i)\to\mathbb{Q}(i)\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(i)\to 0

is exact for any non-negative integer
i
, the second statement follows from
[
Izq16these
]
*Chapitre 1, Théorème 0.4(iii).
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Local Duality)
.

There are perfect pairings of abelian groups:

\mathbb{H}^{0}(K_{v},C)^{\wedge}\times\mathbb{H}^{d+1}(K_{v},\tilde{C})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

The pairing is given by the composition of cup product and
[

\mathbb{H}^{0}(K_{v},C)^{\wedge}\times\mathbb{H}^{d+1}(K_{v},\tilde{C})\to\mathbb{H}^{d+1}(K_{v},\mathbb{Z}(d+1)[2])\cong\mathbb{H}^{d+2}(K_{v},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(d+1))\cong\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

See
[
Izq16these
]
*Chapitre 1, Proposition 4.9 for a proof of the perfectness.

5
Proof of the main theorem

In this section, we finish the proof of the main theorem.

Lemma 5.1
.

If the sequence
(
1
)
is exact for
G^{m}\times Q
,
where
m\geq 1
is an integer and
Q
is a quasi-trivial torus,
then it is exact for
G
.

Proof.

If (
1
) is exact for
G^{m}
,
then it is exact for

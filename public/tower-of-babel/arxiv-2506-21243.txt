Abstract
. We construct families of rotationally symmetric toroidal domains in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} for which the eigenfields associated to the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue are symmetric, and others for which no first eigenfield is symmetric. This implies, in particular, that minimizers of the celebrated Woltjer’s variational principle do not need to inherit the rotational symmetry of the domain. This disproves the folk wisdom that the eigenfields corresponding to the lowest curl eigenvalue must be symmetric if the domain is.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. If
{\mathcal{X}}(\Omega)
denotes the space of
C^{\infty}
vector fields (up to the boundary) on
\Omega
, then a (smooth) magnetic field in
\Omega
is an element of

{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)=\{B\in{\mathcal{X}}(\Omega):\Div B=0,\,B\cdot n=0\},

where
n
denotes the outward unit normal on
\partial\Omega
.

A widely studied subject in the mathematical theory of plasmas is the so-called Woltjer’s variational principle
[
22
,
18
,
2
,
13
]
, which consists in the minimisation problem

(1.1)

{\mathcal{E}}(B)\rightarrowtail\text{min},\qquad{\mathcal{H}}(B)=h

for
h\neq 0
, where
{\mathcal{E}}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
denotes the
L^{2}
-energy, and
{\mathcal{H}}
is the helicity
[
20
,
3
,
12
]
, which is given by the well known formula

{\mathcal{H}}(B)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}B(x)\times B(y)\cdot\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}d\mu(x)d\mu(y).

This minimisation problem was studied in
[
18
,
7
]
and the following was shown. We shall restrict to the case
h>0
, the case
h<0
being completely analogous.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
h>0
, Problem (
1.1
) admits a solution over
{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
. Every solution
B\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
satisfies, for some
\lambda>0
, that

(1.2)

\curl B=\lambda B,\qquad B\cdot n=0,

together with the additional boundary condition that

(1.3)

\int_{\partial D}B\cdot dl=0,

for all embedded closed disks with
D\setminus\partial D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega
. Moreover,
\lambda
is the smallest positive number for which the spectral problem given by (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) holds.

In view of Theorem
1.1
, the minimisation problem (
1.1
) leads to the study of the first (positive) curl eigenvalue on a domain with suitable boundary conditions. To state the spectral problem precisely, we need to introduce some notation.
Letting
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Omega)
denote the set of
Ampèrian knots
, that is the set of
B\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
which satisfy Equation (
1.3
), known as the
Ampèrian boundary condition
, then the curl restricts as an operator

\curl_{{\mathcal{A}}}:{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Omega)\to{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)

which has a compact self-adjoint inverse
\curl^{-1}_{\mathcal{A}}:L^{2}{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)\to L^{2}{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
defined on the
L^{2}
-closure of
{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
. We call the eigenfunctions of
\curl_{{\mathcal{A}}}

Ampèrian curl eigenfields
and we call the eigenvalues of
\curl_{{\mathcal{A}}}

Ampèrian curl eigenvalues
. The distinct Ampèrian curl eigenvalues may be written as

\cdots<\lambda_{-2}<\lambda_{-1}<0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots,

with
\lambda_{n}\to\pm\infty
as
n\to\pm\infty
. If
B
is an Ampèrian curl eigenfield with eigenvalue
\lambda\neq 0
, then

\frac{{\mathcal{H}}(B)}{{\mathcal{E}}(B)}=\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Thus, Theorem
1.1
states that the minimisers of Problem (
1.1
) are the suitably scaled eigenfields corresponding to the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue.

Our work concerns the following natural question about the first Ampèrian curl eigenfields:

In the case that the domain
\Omega
is rotationally symmetric, do the first Ampèrian curl eigenfields inherit the rotational symmetry?

This question is of course a classical one of spectral geometry. It is typically resolved
in the positive
as a by-product of stronger results which hold regardless of the domain. As well-known
[
4
,
19
,
1
]
, in the case of functions, symmetry can be deduced from simplicity of the first eigenvalue. This is what happens for both the Neumann and the Dirichlet Laplacian for example (with the first eigenfunctions being constants in the Neumann case).

Although there is not much literature explicitly dedicated to this question for the curl operator, there are still some results. In the case when
\Omega
is simply connected, the Ampèrian boundary condition is redundant and the problem is just about eigenvalues of the curl operator. In the affirmative direction it was proven in
[
8
]
that when
\Omega
is a ball, or when
\Omega
is the region bounded by two concentric spheres, then the first curl eigenfields each inherit a rotational symmetry from the domain, despite there being multiplicity. In the negative, it has been numerically observed
[
5
,
15
]
that on solid cylinders of finite height the first curl eigenfields need not inherit the rotational symmetry.

The story is different when
\Omega
is a toroidal domain. Here, the distinction between the Ampérian curl spectrum and the flux-free curl spectrum
[
16
]
matters. For the Ampèrian spectrum, the answers have only been in the
afirmative
. Numerical studies
[
21
,
17
]
have observed that the first Ampèrian curl eigenfields are rotationally symmetric in a variety of cases (called the “zero-field eigenvalues” or “field reversal-point” in these studies), including the standard solid torus with arbitrary major and minor radii, and idealised cases where the domain is a flat periodic cylinder (i.e., flat tube) with sections including a rectangle of arbitrary length (rotation here is taken to mean periodic translation). It was also stated in
[
7
]
for the flat tube with a disk section that the first Ampèrian curl eigenfields inherit every symmetry of the domain. For the flux-free curl spectrum however, it has been observed numerically that the first curl eigenfields need not inherit the symmetry in some toroidal domains
[
11
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the aforementioned flat tube with disk cross-section
[
23
]
.

A tempting explanation for the observed symmetry of first Ampèrian curl eigenfields on rotationally symmetric toroidal domains, despite symmetry breaking in first flux-free curl eigenfields, lies in the fact that rotationally symmetric curl eigenfields in solid tori are associated with eigenfunctions of the Grad-Shafranov operator (c.f. propositions
2.1
and
E.1
in sections
2.1
and
E.1
), with the Dirichlet boundary condition (exploited heavily in
[
6
]
) in the Ampèrian case and a zero average condition in the flux-free case, representing orthogonality to the unique (up to scaling) curl-free magnetic field on
\Omega
(known as the vacuum field), which is rotationally symmetric. The idea being that the first flux-free eigenvalue is actually a “second” curl eigenvalue of a larger spectrum, with the “true” first eigenvalue being
0
, corresponding to the symmetric vacuum field. This is in analogy with first Neumann eigenvalue being
0
with all eigenfunctions being constant (therefore inheriting symmetry) and that one does not see symmetry inherited for the first non-zero Neumann eigenvalue. Whereas in the Ampèrian case, the Dirichlet boundary condition means that the first symmetric Ampèrian eigenfield “really is the first eigenvalue” and so inherits symmetry just like the first Dirichlet eigenfunction of the Laplacian. This analogy is not as direct in the simply connected case because there the Grad-Shafranov equation becomes singular.

In view of the aforementioned numerical studies and analogies with Laplacian eigenvalues, the folk wisdom dictates that on solid tori, the answer should be “yes”. As it happens however, we prove that the answer is
no
in general. We will see that the answer depends on geometry. To focus ideas, for an embedded closed disk
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, we call

{\mathcal{R}}_{\Sigma}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}},z)\in\Sigma\}

the
smooth solid torus of revolution with cross-section
\Sigma
. For instance, for
0<a<R
, consider the standard solid torus of minor radius
a
and major radius
R
is given by
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}={\mathcal{R}}_{D_{a,R}}
where
D_{a,R}
is the closed disk of radius
a
centred at
(R,0)
. We show that the question is resolved positively for small enough minor radius.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any
R>0
, and any
a>0
sufficiently small, the first positive Ampèrian curl eigenvalue on the solid torus of revolution
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}
is simple and the corresponding curl eigenfield is rotationally symmetric.

This is in agreement with the aforementioned
[
21
]
. In the negative, we have the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
0<a<b
and
L>0
, set

\displaystyle A_{a,b}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:a^{2}\leq x^{2}+y^{2}\leq b^{2}\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{a,b,L}

\displaystyle=A_{a,b}\times[-L/2,L/2].

Fix
b>0
and set
L_{n}\coloneqq nL
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists
0<a<b
,
L>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any smooth solid torus of revolution
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{a,b,L_{n}}\subset{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{a,b,L_{n+1}},

\displaystyle\partial A_{a,b}\times[-L_{n}/2,L_{n}/2]\subset\partial{\mathcal{R}},

with
n\geq N
, there do not exist rotationally symmetric curl eigenfields corresponding to the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue on
{\mathcal{R}}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Some comments on the theorems are in order:

(i)

The analogous statement in theorems
1.2
and
1.3
for the first negative Ampèrian curl eigenvalue also holds for the same domains thanks to the orientation-reversing isometry
(x,y,z)\mapsto(-x,y,z)
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

(ii)

The curl eigenfields such as those in Theorem
1.2
were shown in
[
6
]
to have no zeros. On the other hand, the curl eigenfields in Theorem
1.3
are gradient fields on the boundary (see Corollary
2.4
) and thus have zeros.

(iii)

If
{\mathcal{R}}
is a smooth solid torus of revolution for which there do not exist rotationally symmetric eigenfields corresponding to the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue, then there do not exist rotationally symmetric eigenfields corresponding to the first (positive) flux-free curl eigenvalue either. In particular, Theorem
1.3
is stronger than the conclusions drawn from the numerics in
[
11
]
. See Appendix
E
.

The proof of both theorems
1.2
and
1.3
involves analysing the Ampèrian eigenvalues associated with symmetric curl eigenfields and Ampèrian eigenvalues associated with non-symmetric curl eigenfields. Both kinds of eigenvalues come from a spectrum. In fact, we find that the non-symmetric curl eigenfields are captured by what we call the
antisymmetric curl spectrum
, and in a strong sense this spectrum exists independently of the Ampèrian curl spectrum. The antisymmetric curl spectrum can be understood as a rigorous account of the “periodic modes” often encountered in the physics literature (c.f. the aforementioned numerical studies). We essentially play the concrete nature of the symmetric Ampèrian spectrum, identified with the Dirichlet spectrum of the Grad-Shafranov operator, against the abstract nature of the antisymmetric curl spectrum, finding estimates for the lowest antisymmetric curl eigenvalues by making comparisons in Riemannian 3-manifolds with flat boundaries. For both theorems, the lowest Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Grad-Shafranov operator are concretely estimated thanks to the operator’s close relationship with the Laplacian operator.

For Theorem
1.2
, we compare the geometry of
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}
to that of flat tubes
D_{a}\times\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
and
D_{a}
the disk of radius
a
centred at the origin to compare the corresponding antisymmetric curl-eigenvalues. For Theorem
1.3
, we compare the geometry of
{\mathcal{R}}
to that of flat tubes
A_{a,b}\times\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
. A difference in Theorem
1.3
is that we must perform a “cut” because
A_{a,b}\times\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
is not topologically a solid torus. We instead think of the
\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
factor as “vertical” and consider the fundamental domain
A_{a,b}\times[-L/2,L/2]\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which is topologically a solid torus and estimate the changes to the curl eigenvalues in doing this.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we formally introduce the symmetric Ampèrian curl spectrum and the antisymmetric curl spectrum and state a key lemma helpful for proving theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.2
. In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.3
. In Section
5
we prove an antisymmetric curl eigenvalue estimate needed for Theorem
1.2
. In Section
6
we introduce some complex algebra for more compactly handling the antisymmetric curl spectrum in service of proving the remaining lemmas used in theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. In Section
7
we prove an inequality for the antisymmetric curl eigenvalues in the aforementioned flat tube
D_{a}\times\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
for Theorem
1.2
. In Section
8
we estimate the change in antisymmetric curl eigenvalue by performing the aforementioned “cut” on
A_{a,b}\times\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
for Theorem
1.3
. Lastly in Section
9
, we prove the existence of small antisymmetric curl eigenvalues for suitable parameters on the tubes
A_{a,b}\times\mathbb{R}/L\mathbb{Z}
for Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

The goal of this section is to outline some preliminaries for the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. Section
2.1
discusses the symmetric spectrum of the curl operator on domains that are rotationally symmetric. In contrast, Section
2.1
introduces the antisymmetric spectrum of the curl. Lastly, in Section
2.3
we state a trivial, albeit important lemma, which is crucial in the proofs of the theorems.

2.1.
The symmetric Ampèrian curl spectrum

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be a smooth solid torus of revolution with cross-section
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let
(x,y,z)\in{\mathcal{R}}
denote the Cartesian coordinates and consider the rotation vector field

(2.1)

K=-y\partial_{x}+x\partial_{y}.

As usual, an Ampèrian curl eigenfield
B
on
{\mathcal{R}}
is
symmetric
if
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=0
and we will call its corresponding eigenvalue a
symmetric Ampèrian curl eigenvalue
. The following standard result on the symmetric Ampèrian spectrum of curl is proven in
[
6
, Proposition 3]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

A vector field
B
in
{\mathcal{R}}
is a symmetric Ampèrian curl eigenfield if and only if there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla u(r,z)\times\nabla\varphi+u(r,z)\nabla\varphi,

\displaystyle Lu

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}u,

\displaystyle u|_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle=0,

where
L
is the Grad-Shafranov operator

L=\partial_{rr}+\partial_{zz}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r},

and
(r,\varphi,z)
denote the cylindrical coordinates. In particular, if
\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(\Sigma)
denotes the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
L
on
\Sigma
and
\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})
denotes the first positive symmetric Ampèrian curl eigenvalue, then

\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})=\sqrt{\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(\Sigma)}.

The following elementary inequalities for the Grad-Shafranov eigenvalues will be instrumental in the proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. They are proved in Appendix
A
. In the statement we endow the half-plane
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
with coordinates
(r,z)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
U\subset U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be bounded domains whose closures are contained in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Then,

\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U)\geq\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U^{\prime}).

Moreover, letting
\lambda^{D}_{1}(U)
denote the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian
\partial_{rr}+\partial_{zz}
, we have

\lambda_{1}^{D}(U)+\frac{3}{4r_{\text{max}}^{2}}\leq\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U)\leq\lambda_{1}^{D}(U)+\frac{3}{4r_{\text{min}}^{2}},

where

r_{\text{min}}=\inf_{(r,z)\in{U}}r,\qquad r_{\text{max}}=\sup_{(r,z)\in{U}}r.

2.2.
The antisymmetric curl spectrum

Since we will use it later, it is convenient to introduce the antisymmetric spectrum in a general Riemannian setting.
Let
(M,g)
be a compact oriented Riemannian 3-manifold with boundary. We set
{\mathcal{X}}(M)
to be the set of smooth vector fields on
M
and
n
the outward unit normal on
\partial M
. Assume that
M
admits a (non-trivial) vector field
K
tangent to the boundary which is Killing, i.e.,
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}g=0
. We let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{B\in{\mathcal{X}}(M):\Div B=0,\,B\cdot n=0\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{K}(M)

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\mathcal{L}}_{K}({\mathcal{B}}(M)).

A vector field
B
is said to be a
K
-antisymmetric curl eigenfield
with corresponding
K
-antisymmetric curl eigenvalue

\lambda
if
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{K}(M)
and
\curl B=\lambda B
. The set of
K
-antisymmetric curl eigenvalues is discrete and may be written as

(2.2)

\cdots<\lambda^{K}_{-2}(M)<\lambda^{K}_{-1}(M)<0<\lambda^{K}_{1}(M)<\lambda^{K}_{2}(M)<\cdots.

See Corollary
B.4
in Appendix
B
.

The following decomposition of the spectrum of the curl operator into its symmetric and antisymmetric part is elementary; for the sake of completeness we include a proof in Appendix
B
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and set

E_{\lambda}(M)=\{B\in{\mathcal{X}}(M):\curl B=\lambda B,\,B\cdot n=0\}.

Then,
E_{\lambda}(M)
is finite-dimensional and has the
L^{2}
-orthogonal decomposition

E_{\lambda}(M)=E_{\lambda}(M)\cap(\ker{\mathcal{L}}_{K})\oplus E_{\lambda}(M)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{K}(M),

The second factor further decomposes as

E_{\lambda}(M)\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{K}(M)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}E_{\lambda,K,k_{i}}(M),

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and numbers
k_{1},...,k_{n}>0
, where

E_{\lambda,K,k}(M)\coloneqq\{B\in E_{\lambda}(M):{\mathcal{L}}_{K}^{2}B=-k^{2}B\}.

Now, as in Section
2.1
, we consider the case of a solid torus of revolution
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. There is a Killing field
K
given by the rotation, cf. Equation (
2.1
). In this case, we abbreviate the qualifier “
K
-antisymmetric” to simply “antisymmetric” and write the antisymmetric curl eigenvalues as

(2.3)

\cdots<\lambda^{*}_{-2}({\mathcal{R}})<\lambda^{*}_{-1}({\mathcal{R}})<0<\lambda^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})<\lambda^{*}_{2}({\mathcal{R}})<\cdots.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, we set

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}})

\displaystyle=\{B\in E_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}}):B\text{ satisfies the Amp\`{e}rian boundary condition}\},

\displaystyle A_{\lambda}^{\text{s}}({\mathcal{R}})

\displaystyle=\{B\in A_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}}):{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=0\}.

By Corollary
2.4
it is obvious that
E_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}})\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{K}({\mathcal{R}})\subset A_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}})
, and so, by Proposition
2.3
, one has the
L^{2}
-orthogonal decomposition

(2.4)

A_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}})=A_{\lambda}^{\text{s}}({\mathcal{R}})\oplus(E_{\lambda}({\mathcal{R}})\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{K}({\mathcal{R}})).

Moreover, one has the following dynamical property of antisymmetric curl eigenfields.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
B
be a antisymmetric curl eigenfield on
\mathcal{R}
. Then, it restricts to a gradient field on
\partial\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

Since
B
is tangent to the boundary, it is well known that its metric-dual
1
-form when pulled back on
\partial\mathcal{R}
is closed
[
9
]
. The result then easily follows from the fact that
B
satisfies both the Ampèrian and the flux-free boundary conditions (cf. Lemma
B.3
).
∎

2.3.
A key lemma

An elementary but crucial lemma in the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
is the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a solid torus of revolution. Then:

(i)

If
\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}<\lambda^{*}_{1}
, the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue is simple and its corresponding eigenfield is rotationally symmetric.

(ii)

If
\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}>\lambda^{*}_{1}
, there exist no rotationally symmetric eigenfields corresponding to the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue.

Proof.

Equation (
2.4
) implies that the set of Ampèrian curl eigenvalues is the union of the sets of symmetric and antisymmetric eigenvalues.
In particular,

(2.5)

\lambda_{1}=\min\{\lambda_{1}^{\text{s}},\lambda_{1}^{*}\}.

If
\lambda_{1}^{\text{s}}<\lambda_{1}^{*}
, then from Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),

A_{\lambda_{1}}({\mathcal{R}})=A_{\lambda_{1}}^{\text{s}}({\mathcal{R}}).

It then follows from Proposition
2.1
and the simplicity of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Grad-Shafranov operator on the cross-section
\Sigma
of
{\mathcal{R}}
that
\lambda_{1}^{2}=\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}
and that the vector space
A_{\lambda_{1}}^{\text{s}}({\mathcal{R}})
is simple. Conclusion
(i)
thus follows.

If
\lambda_{1}^{\text{s}}>\lambda_{1}^{*}
, then from Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
),

\displaystyle A_{\lambda_{1}}({\mathcal{R}})=E_{\lambda_{1}}({\mathcal{R}})\cap{\mathcal{B}}_{K}({\mathcal{R}}),

while
A_{\lambda_{1}}^{\text{s}}({\mathcal{R}})
is a trivial vector space. Conclusion
(ii)
thus follows.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
imply that

(3.1)

\begin{split}\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R})=\sqrt{\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(D_{a,R})}&\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{D}(D_{a,R})+\frac{3}{4(R+a)^{2}}}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{j_{0,1}^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{3}{4(R+a)^{2}}},\end{split}

where
j_{0,1}
denotes the first positive zero of the Bessel function
J_{0}
.

Now we focus on
\lambda^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R})
, the first positive antisymmetric curl eigenvalue on
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}
. Recall that we denote by
K=\partial_{\varphi}
the Killing field on
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}
as discussed in Section
2
.
The following lemma, which is proved in Section
5
, establishes an equality enabling passage to an averaged metric on
\mathcal{R}_{a,R}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Setting
r_{1/2}=\sqrt{R-a}\sqrt{R+a}
, consider the averaged metric

g_{r_{1/2}}=dr^{2}+dz^{2}+r^{2}_{1/2}d\varphi^{2}

on
\mathcal{R}_{a,R}
(obviously,
K
is a Killing field of this metric). Then, letting
\widetilde{\lambda}^{*}_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
be the first positive
K
-antisymmetric curl eigenvalue on
(\mathcal{R}_{a,R},g_{r_{1/2}})
, one has

\lambda^{*}_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\geq\left(\frac{R-a}{R+a}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{\lambda}^{*}_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R}).

The following lemma establishes a lower bound for the first antisymmetric curl eigenvalue
\widetilde{\lambda}^{*}_{1}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
(with the metric
g_{r_{1/2}}
); its proof is presented in Section
7
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
j^{*}>j_{0,1}
such that, for any
a>0
,

\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\geq\frac{j^{*}}{a}.

The previous lemmas imply that we can write

\lambda^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R})\geq\left(\frac{R-a}{R+a}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{j^{*}}{a}.

Recalling (
3.1
), we have altogether that, for all
0<a<R
,

\displaystyle a\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{j_{0,1}^{2}+\frac{3a^{2}}{4(R+a)^{2}}},

\displaystyle a\lambda^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R})

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{R-a}{R+a}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}j^{*},

but

\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\sqrt{j_{0,1}^{2}+\frac{3a^{2}}{4(R+a)^{2}}}=j_{0,1}<j^{*}=\lim_{a\to 0^{+}}\left(\frac{R-a}{R+a}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}j^{*},

and so, for sufficiently small
a>0
, one has that

\lambda^{\text{s}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R})<\lambda^{*}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}),

and so by Lemma
2.5
we are done.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
L>0
and
0<a<b
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

5.
Proof of Lemma
3.1

In this section we prove the following estimate:

C^{-1}\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\leq\lambda_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\leq C\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R}),

with

C\coloneqq\Big(\frac{R+a}{R-a}\Big)^{1/2}.

As before,
\mathcal{B}_{K}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
is the space of
K
-antisymmetric solenoidal vector fields on
{\mathcal{R}}_{a,R}
(tangent to the boundary), and we use the notation
\mathcal{B}_{K,1/2}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
for the corresponding space when
\mathcal{R}_{a,R}
is endowed with the averaged metric
g_{r_{1/2}}
. It is straightforward to check that if
B\in\mathcal{B}_{K}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
then
\tau(B)\coloneqq fB\in\mathcal{B}_{K,1/2}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
for the function

f\coloneqq\frac{r}{r_{1/2}}.

Moreover, it is clear that

i_{B}\,rdr\wedge d\varphi\wedge dz=i_{\tau(B)}\,r_{1/2}dr\wedge d\varphi\wedge dz,

so denoting the helicity of
\tau(B)
with respect to the metric
g_{r_{1/2}}
as
\mathcal{H}_{1/2}(\tau(B))
we easily deduce that

(5.1)

{\mathcal{H}}_{1/2}(\tau(B))={\mathcal{H}}(B).

Now we compute

(B,B)_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathcal{R}_{a,R}}|B|^{2}rdrd\varphi dz=\int_{\mathcal{R}_{a,R}}f|B|^{2}r_{1/2}drd\varphi dz.

Moreover, writing
B
in coordinates as
B=B_{r}\partial_{r}+B_{\varphi}\partial_{\varphi}+B_{z}\partial_{z}
, we have

f|B|^{2}=\frac{r_{1/2}}{r}~\frac{r^{2}}{r_{1/2}^{2}}(B_{r}^{2}+B_{z}^{2})+\frac{r}{r_{1/2}}~r^{2}B_{\varphi}^{2},

which we can compare with

g_{r_{1/2}}(\tau(B),\tau(B))=\frac{r^{2}}{r_{1/2}^{2}}\left(B_{r}^{2}+B_{z}^{2}\right)+r^{2}B_{\varphi}^{2}.

Observing that

\displaystyle\max\left(\frac{r_{1/2}}{r}\right)=C=\max\left(\frac{r}{r_{1/2}}\right),

\displaystyle\min\left(\frac{r_{1/2}}{r}\right)=C^{-1}=\min\left(\frac{r}{r_{1/2}}\right),

we obtain the pointwise inequality

C^{-1}g_{r_{1/2}}(\tau(B),\tau(B))\leq f|B|^{2}\leq Cg_{r_{1/2}}(\tau(B),\tau(B)),

and so, multiplying by
r_{1/2}
and integrating on
\mathcal{R}_{a,R}
,

C^{-1}\|\tau(B)\|^{2}_{L^{2},r_{1/2}}\leq\|B\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\tau(B)\|^{2}_{L^{2},r_{1/2}},

where the subindex
r_{1/2}
in the scalar product denotes that it is computed with respect to the metric
g_{r_{1/2}}
. Taking Equation (
5.1
) into account, we therefore obtain

(5.2)

C^{-1}\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{{1/2}}(\tau(B))}{\|\tau(B)\|^{2}_{L^{2},r_{1/2}}}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{H}}(B)}{\|B\|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\leq C\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{{1/2}}(\tau(B))}{\|\tau(B)\|^{2}_{L^{2},r_{1/2}}}.

Finally, taking maxima over
B\in\mathcal{B}_{K}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\setminus\{0\}
, and using the fact that
\tau
defines a linear isomorphism between
B_{K}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
and
\mathcal{B}_{K,1/2}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})
, the estimate (
5.2
) and the variational characterization of the first antisymmetric curl eigenvalue (cf. Corollary
B.4
) yield

C^{-1}\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\right)^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})}\leq C\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{R}_{a,R})\right)^{-1},

which proves the result.

6.
A complex-valued formulation for antisymmetric curl eigenfields

The purpose of this section is to introduce some complex algebra associated with the antisymmetric curl spectrum used in the proof of the remaining lemmas. Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian 3-manifold with boundary, and let
{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
denote the space of smooth complex-valued vector fields on
M
. For any
Z\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
, there exist unique
X,Y\in{\mathcal{X}}(M)
such that
Z=X+iY
and we set

\Re Z=X,\qquad\Im Z=Y,\qquad\overline{Z}=X-iY.

Let
g_{\mathbb{C}}
denote inner product such that, for vector fields
Z,W\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
,

g_{\mathbb{C}}(Z,W)\coloneqq g(Z,\overline{W}).

Letting
\mu
denote the Riemannian volume form on
M
, for
Z,W\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
we set

(Z,W)_{L^{2}}=\int_{M}g_{\mathbb{C}}(Z,W)\mu=\int_{M}g(Z,\overline{W})\mu.

For an
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace
S\subset{\mathcal{X}}(M)
, we let
S_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace of
{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
given by

S_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\{Z\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C}):\Re Z\in S\text{ and }\Im Z\in S\}.

Let

{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)=({\mathcal{B}}_{\text{FF}}(M))_{\mathbb{C}},

where
{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{FF}}(M)
is defined in Appendix
B
. It is easy to prove but nevertheless important to observe that helicity extends nicely to the complex setting.

Lemma 6.1
.

The
\mathbb{C}
-linear extension
\curl^{-1}:{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)\to{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)
of
\curl^{-1}
on
{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{FF}}(M)
is symmetric with respect to the (complex)
L^{2}
inner product. In particular, the function
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)
given by

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)=(B,\curl^{-1}B)_{L^{2}}

is real-valued. For any
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)
and any
A\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
with
\curl A=B
, one has that

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)=(B,A)_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, for all
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)
, if
{\mathcal{H}}:{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{FF}}(M)\to\mathbb{R}
denotes helicity on
{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{FF}}(M)
,

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)={\mathcal{H}}(\Re B)+{\mathcal{H}}(\Im B).

Henceforth assume that
(M,g)
admits a non-trivial Killing field
K\in{\mathcal{X}}(M)
tangent to the boundary. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we set

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{C},\lambda}(M)

\displaystyle=\{B\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C}):\curl B=\lambda B,\,B\cdot n=0\}

\displaystyle E_{\mathbb{C},\lambda,K,k}(M)

\displaystyle=\{B\in E_{\mathbb{C},\lambda}(M):{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=ikB\}.

Then, it is easy to prove the following. In the statement, we use the notation introduced in Proposition
2.3
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
k>0
, one has the
L^{2}
-orthogonal decomposition

(E_{\lambda,K,k}(M))_{\mathbb{C}}=E_{\mathbb{C},\lambda,K,k}(M)\oplus E_{\mathbb{C},\lambda,K,-k}(M).

We now state a useful vector potential formula for vector fields which generalise those in Lemma
6.2
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
B\in{\mathcal{X}}(M,\mathbb{C})
be a complex vector field satisfying

\Div B=0,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=ikB,\qquad B\cdot n=0.

Then
B\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{C},\text{FF}}(M)
. Letting
\lambda^{K}_{1}(M)
and
\lambda^{K}_{-1}(M)
respectively denote the first positive and first negative
K
-antisymmetric curl eigenvalues, we have that

\frac{1}{\lambda^{K}_{-1}(M)}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)}{\|B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{K}_{1}(M)}.

In addition, the vector field

A=-\frac{i}{k}B\times K,

is a normal-pointing vector potential for
B
. In particular, the helicity of
B
is given by

(6.2)

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)=\frac{i}{k}(B,B\times K)_{L^{2}}.

If
\lambda\neq 0
and
B
satisfies that
\curl B=\lambda B
, then

(6.3)

\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)}{\|B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Proof.

Writing

, we have that
X,Y\in{\mathcal{B}}(M)
. Moreover, from
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=ikB
we have that
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}^{2}B=-k^{2}B
so that in fact

by Lemma

. Moreover, because

) follows from Corollary

and the equalities

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbb{C}}(B)={\mathcal{H}}(X)+{\mathcal{H}}(Y),\qquad\|B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|X\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Y\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The first equality coming from Lemma
6.1
. Concerning the vector field
A
,

\displaystyle\curl A=-\frac{i}{k}\curl(B\times K)=-\frac{i}{k}{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=B.

Hence, Equation (
6.2
) holds. If
\lambda\neq 0
and
B
satisfies that

, then Equation (
6.3
) follows from Lemma

and the fact that

is a vector potential for

.
∎

For

M_{a,L}

. The proof of Lemma

C.5
in Appendix

, given
\ell\in 2\pi/L\mathbb{Z}
and

and, setting

(7.1)

\frac{\lambda m}{a}J_{m}(\mu a)+\ell\mu J_{m}^{\prime}(\mu a)=0.

m\in\mathbb{R}

denotes a Bessel function of order

Z_{m}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{Z_{m-1}(x)-Z_{m+1}(x)}{2},\qquad\frac{mZ_{m}(x)}{x}=\frac{Z_{m-1}(x)+Z_{m+1}(x)}{2}.

.

Lemma 7.2
.

.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
2.2

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with
\overline{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Here we consider the (Dirichlet) eigenvalues of the Grad-Shafranov operator

L=r\Div(1/r\nabla)=\Delta-\nabla\ln(r)\cdot\nabla,

with
(r,z)\in U
the usual Cartesian coordinates. For
u,v\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
,

-(Lu,v)_{L^{2},r}=B_{r}[u,v],

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2},r}
is the weighted inner product given by

(u,v)_{L^{2},r}=\int_{U}uv/r~drdz,

and
B_{r}:H^{1}_{0}(U)\times H^{1}_{0}(U)\to\mathbb{R}
is the bilinear form given by

B_{r}[u,v]=\int_{U}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v)/r~drdz.

The eigenfunctions of
L
, that is, the functions
u\in H^{1}_{0}(U)
satisfying (weakly) that

Lu=-\lambda u\text{ on }U,

can be used to form an
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2},r}
-orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(U)
and the first eigenvalue
\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U)
satisfies that

(A.1)

\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U)=\min_{0\neq u\in H^{1}_{0}(U)}\frac{B_{r}[u,u]}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2},r}}=\inf_{0\neq u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)}\frac{B_{r}[u,u]}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2},r}}.

This makes it obvious that domain monotonicity is satisfied: if
U^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies
\overline{U^{\prime}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and
U\subset U^{\prime}
, then
\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U)\geq\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U^{\prime})
.

To prove the second inequality in Lemma
2.2
, note that for any
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
, setting
v_{u}=r^{-1/2}u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
, we have that
\|v_{u}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u\|_{L^{2},r}

and after a straightforward computation, we get

\displaystyle\int_{U}|\nabla v_{u}|^{2}~drdz=-\int_{U}fu^{2}/r~drdz+B_{r}[u,u],

with
f(r)\coloneqq\frac{3}{4r^{2}}
and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{U}|\nabla v_{u}|^{2}~drdz}{\|v_{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}+\inf_{(r,z)\in U}f(r)\leq\frac{B_{r}[u,u]}{\|u\|_{L^{2},r}^{2}}\leq\frac{\int_{U}|\nabla v_{u}|^{2}~drdz}{\|v_{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}+\sup_{(r,z)\in U}f(r).

Taking the infimum over
0\neq u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
(with Equation (
A.1
) in mind), and using the fact that
u\mapsto v_{u}
is a linear isomorphism
C^{\infty}_{c}(U)\to C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
, we get that

\displaystyle\lambda^{D}_{1}(U)+\inf_{(r,z)\in U}f(r)\leq\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(U)\leq\lambda^{D}_{1}(U)+\sup_{(r,z)\in U}f(r),

which proves the lemma.

Assume henceforth that

Lemma B.2
.

The Lie derivative
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}:{\mathcal{X}}(M)\to{\mathcal{X}}(M)
is antisymmetric in the sense that

({\mathcal{L}}_{K}X,Y)_{L^{2}}=-(X,{\mathcal{L}}_{K}Y)_{L^{2}}

.

from Section

Proof of Proposition

.

Because

is a Killing field tangent to
\partial M

. Thus, by Lemma

descends to an antisymmetric linear operator

(equipped with the

is a symmetric operator. Hence, from the Spectral Theorem, we obtain

are the distinct (mutually orthogonal) eigenspaces of

. For any

Appendix C
The curl spectrum on flat tubes

To begin, let

Lemma C.1
.

Let

. Then,

{\mathcal{L}}_{K}Z=(\curl Z)\times K+\nabla h_{Z}.

Proof.

First note that, by Cartan’s formula,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{K}(Z^{\flat})=i_{K}dZ^{\flat}+di_{K}Z^{\flat}

\displaystyle=i_{K}i_{\curl Z}\mu+dh_{Z}

\displaystyle=((\curl Z)\times K)^{\flat}+(\nabla h_{Z})^{\flat}.

On the other hand, because

{\mathcal{L}}_{K}(Z^{\flat})=({\mathcal{L}}_{K}Z)^{\flat}

The following proposition shows that a curl eigenfield cannot be pointwise orthogonal to a Killing field.

and so

R_{M,n}
given by

R_{M,n}(x)=\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!(m+n)!}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2m+n}.

Then, for
|x|\leq 3
,

\displaystyle|R_{M,n}(x)|\leq\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!(m+n)!}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2m+n}.

For integer
m\geq 2
, defining
\prod_{j=1}^{n}(m+j)=1
as an empty product when
n=0
, we have that

\frac{1}{(m+n)!}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{n}=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{(3/2)^{n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(m+j)}\leq\frac{1}{m!}\frac{(3/2)^{n}}{2^{n}}\leq\frac{1}{m!}.

So, because
M+1\geq 2
,

|R_{M,n}(x)|\leq\sum_{m=M+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!m!}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2m}.

Now, for integer
m\geq 2
, we have that
m!\geq\frac{2}{9}\cdot 3^{m}

and so,

\frac{1}{m!}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2m}\leq\frac{9}{2}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{m}.

Therefore, putting these estimates together, we obtain

\displaystyle|R_{M,n}(x)|\leq\frac{18\cdot(3/4)^{M+1}}{(M+1)!}.

D.2.
Computation with rational numbers

First, observe that for any
0<s<\pi
, we may set

r=\sqrt{1-\frac{s^{2}}{\pi^{2}}}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle(1+r)J_{0}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\pi)+(1-r)J_{2}(\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\pi)=(1+r)J_{0}(s)+(1-r)J_{2}(s).

If in addition

\displaystyle(1+r)J_{0}(s)+(1-r)J_{2}(s)

Appendix E
Proof of
(iii)
in Remark
1.4

Let
{\mathcal{R}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a smooth solid torus of revolution with cross-section
\Sigma
. Analogous to the terminology in Section
2.1
with
K
the rotational Killing field from that section, a flux-free curl eigenfield
B
on
{\mathcal{R}}
is
symmetric
if
{\mathcal{L}}_{K}B=0
and we will call its corresponding eigenvalue a
symmetric flux-free curl eigenvalue
. An analogous result to that of Proposition
2.1
holds in this case
[
10
]
.

Proposition E.1
.

A vector field
B
in
{\mathcal{R}}
is a symmetric flux-free curl eigenfield if and only if there exists
u\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla u(r,z)\times\nabla\varphi+u(r,z)\nabla\varphi,

\displaystyle Lu

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2}u,

\displaystyle u|_{\partial\Sigma}

\displaystyle=c,

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}u/r~drdz

\displaystyle=0,

where
L
is the Grad-Shafranov operator and
(r,\varphi,z)
denote the cylindrical coordinates.

Because the flux-free curl spectrum on
{\mathcal{R}}
is discrete (see Appendix
B
), the set of symmetric flux-free curl eigenvalues on
{\mathcal{R}}
is also discrete. Let
\lambda^{\text{sFF}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})
and
\lambda^{\text{sA}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})
respectively denote the first positive symmetric flux-free and Ampèrian curl eigenvalues. Proposition
E.1
enables:

Lemma E.2
.

\lambda^{\text{sFF}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})>\lambda^{\text{sA}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})
.

Proof.

Set
\lambda\equiv\lambda^{\text{sFF}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})
and let
B
be a corresponding flux-free eigenfield. In view of Proposition
E.1
, setting
u_{0}:=u-c
, with the notation in Appendix
A
,

-(Lu_{0},u_{0})_{L^{2},r}=B_{r}[u_{0},u_{0}].

Since
u
is orthogonal to constants with the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2},r}
,

-(Lu_{0},u_{0})_{L^{2},r}=\lambda^{2}(u_{0},u_{0})_{L^{2},r}.

Hence, by Equation (
A.1
) in Appendix
A
and Proposition
2.1
, we have that

(\lambda^{\text{sA}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}))^{2}=\lambda^{\text{G-S}}_{1}(\Sigma)\leq\frac{B_{r}[u_{0},u_{0}]}{(u_{0},u_{0})_{L^{2},r}}=\lambda^{2}.

Suppose equality holds. Then
u_{0}
would be a first Dirichlet eigenfunction of the Grad-Shafranov operator, and so
c=0
, yielding that
u_{0}=u
. However, the integral condition on
u
implies that
u
does not have a sign and hence it cannot be a first Dirichlet eigenfunction for
L
. This contradiction completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to prove
(iii)
in Remark
1.4
. Let
\lambda_{1}^{*}({\mathcal{R}})
denote the first positive antisymmetric curl eigenvalue on
{\mathcal{R}}
(which is a flux free curl eigenvalue according to Lemma
B.3
). If there do not exist rotationally symmetric eigenfields corresponding to the first (positive) Ampèrian curl eigenvalue on
{\mathcal{R}}
, then

\lambda_{1}^{*}({\mathcal{R}})<\lambda^{\text{sA}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}})<\lambda^{\text{sFF}}_{1}({\mathcal{R}}),

where the second inequality is coming from Lemma
E.2
, and we are done.

Augmented Lagrangian methods for fully convex composite optimization
Alberto De Marchi, Tim Hoheisel, Patrick Mehlitz

Abstract
This paper is concerned with augmented Lagrangian methods for the treatment of fully convex composite optimization problems. We extend the classical relationship between augmented Lagrangian methods and the proximal point algorithm to the inexact and safeguarded scheme in order to state global primal-dual convergence results. Our analysis distinguishes the regular case, where a stationary minimizer exists, and the irregular case, where all minimizers are nonstationary. Furthermore, we suggest an elastic modification of the standard safeguarding scheme which preserves primal convergence properties while guaranteeing convergence of the dual sequence to a multiplier in the regular situation. Although important for nonconvex problems, the standard safeguarding mechanism leads to weaker convergence guarantees for convex problems than the classical augmented Lagrangian method. Our elastic safeguarding scheme combines the advantages of both while avoiding their shortcomings. Keywords Convex composite optimization, Augmented Lagrangian framework, Proximal point algorithm, Convergence properties, Multiplier safeguarding MSC 49M37 , 65K05 , 90C25 , 90C30 , 90C46

Introduction

Consider a
composite problem
of the form

\minimize_{x}\,\Phi(x):=f(x)+g(c(x)),

(P)

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuously differentiable,

g\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is lower semicontinuous, proper, convex, and

c\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is continuously differentiable.
We assume throughout that (
P
) is feasible, i.e.,
\dom\Phi=c^{-1}(\dom g)
is nonempty and, for algorithmic considerations,
that
g
is prox-friendly, i.e., its proximal operator can be computed (at least inexactly) with reasonable effort.
Let us note that all results in this paper remain true if
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
are replaced by arbitrary finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.
Problem (
P
) is, due to convexity of
g
, referred to as a
convex composite problem

or an
extended nonlinear program
in the literature to which many people have contributed,
most prominently, Burke et al.
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
20
]

and Rockafellar
[
51
,
52
,
53
]
.

In this study we focus on the case where
f
and
g\circ c
, and hence
\Phi
, are convex,
i.e., we study
fully convex composite problems
.
We refer the reader to
3.1
in
Section

3
for minimal assumptions that guarantee this.
Although this limits the range of the framework to a degree, the model problem (
P
) covers diverse applications.
The obvious ones are (regularized) linear least-squares and constrained convex programming (including semidefinite programming)
where
f
is the objective function,
g
is an indicator function, and
c
models the constraint functions.
Some non-generic applications are matrix optimization problems involving variational Gram functions
[
18
,
36
]
,
where
g
is the support function of a compact set of positive semidefinite matrices
and
c(X):=XX^{\top}
for any input matrix
X
.
The fully convex version of (
P
) in which we are interested has been studied from a theoretical viewpoint by Boţ et al.
[
10
,
11
,
12
]

and Burke et al.
[
18
]
who provide complete conjugacy and subdifferential results, as well as a plethora of applications.
Doikov and Nesterov
[
28
]
discuss algorithmic approaches.

In order to solve composite problems numerically,
the augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) turned out to deliver a suitable algorithmic framework,
see, e.g.,
[
27
,
32
,
33
,
53
,
54
]

for some recent contributions,
as the emerging subproblems can be tackled via proximal-gradient methods,
for example
[
26
,
38
]
.

Following the literature, ALMs enjoy convincing local convergence
properties in the presence of suitable second-order conditions,
see, e.g.,
[
32
,
33
,
53
]

for such results addressing convex composite optimization problems.
However, in the convex composite situation,
global convergence properties, i.e., general assertions about primal
accumulation points, are difficult to obtain.
This has been illustrated in
[
39
]

by means of a simple one-dimensional convex optimization problem
whose feasible set is described via a nonconvex constraint.
The gap of global convergence guarantees can be closed when relying on so-called
safeguarding
techniques
which keep the dual variables in the augmented Lagrangian scheme bounded,
see, e.g.,
[
9
]
for a general view
and the recent contributions
[
24
,
25
]

for studies addressing general composite optimization problems.
In numerical practice, safeguarded ALMs are typically preferred
over the classical scheme due to their superior global properties.

The convergence analysis of ALMs is closely related
to the one of the famous proximal point algorithm (PPA) as the dual iterates typically
turn out to correspond to the sequence generated by a (potentially inexact)
PPA with variable stepsizes when applied to a suitable dual optimization problem.
First results in this direction have been obtained
by Rockafellar in
[
46
,
49
]

and address smooth convex optimization problems with inequality constraints.
However, an extension to convex composite problems is also possible,
see
[
53
]

where, however, this connection has not been used to infer
global convergence properties of the ALM.
In fact, the focus of
[
53
]
is on local properties,
see
[
52
]
as well.
Let us note that, in the safeguarded situation, the dual iterates correspond to the sequence
generated by a generalized PPA, see
[
40
]
,
which also does not exploit these observations to distill
global convergence properties of the safeguarded ALM.
The purpose of this paper is to complement the results
in
[
40
,
53
]
.

Contributions

Now we are in a position to outline the main contributions of this paper.
To start,
we
analyze the fully convex composite model (
P
)
and extend the well-known (dual) connection between
the ALM and the PPA
to it.
We give a generalized relationship between ALM and PPA
when applied to the broad problem class (
P
)
capturing inexact solves of emerging subproblems.
This relation is then used to infer
two types of convergence results for the classical ALM
.
First, we investigate the behavior of the sequence of dual iterates via the properties of PPA.
Second, with the aid of these insights,
it is shown that all accumulation points of the sequence of primal iterates
are global minimizers of (
P
).
These results complement those in the recent papers
[
3
,
53
]

in a straightforward way and provide the base for subsequent considerations.
In
[
3
]
, the authors consider a more specific convex model problem (
P
)
where the role of
g
is played by an indicator function.
In contrast, (
P
) may be infeasible in
[
3
]
while we will assume throughout
that (
P
) possesses a (not necessarily stationary) minimizer.
The focus of
[
53
]
, which is concerned with convex composite problems,
is on proving full convergence of the primal ALM iterates
in the presence of so-called
strong variational convexity
which amounts to a second-order condition in practice,
see, e.g.,
[
41
]
, which is not required in this paper.
Particularly, we (separately) address the situation where all minimizers of (
P
)
are nonstationary, which is a setting that is not at all considered
in
[
53
]
.

Second, we
extend the PPA-based analysis to safeguarded ALMs
,
and to the best of our knowledge, our findings are largely new.
Therefore, we relate the dual update of the method with
a generalized PPA
which can be also interpreted as an instance
of the backward-backward splitting method from
[
5
]

whenever the safeguarding is implemented via projection onto the safeguarding set.
This rather natural property allows us to distill finer convergence results
than postulated in
[
40
]
,
and we are in a position to incorporate inexact evaluations of the proximal operator.
These findings allow us to infer primal-dual convergence results for the safeguarded ALM
which are similar to the ones obtained for the classical ALM.
However, one has to be slightly more careful with the update of the penalty parameter
in the safeguarded setting.
The fact that accumulation points of the primal sequence are global minimizers of (
P
)
whenever the update of the penalty parameter is somewhat reasonable
is well known and can be found,
e.g., in
[
9
,
25
,
37
]
.
Whenever the safeguarding set is too small, however,
it may happen that the dual sequence does not capture Lagrange multipliers in case of existence,
and this also affects the behavior of the sequence of primal iterates, see
[
2
]
.

The third contribution of the paper
overcomes this shortcoming:
we suggest to adopt an
elastic safeguarding
,
a slight modification of the standard technique
which allows for an enlargement of the safeguarding set in certain situations.
With this mechanism we can infer that the sequence of dual iterates of the associated ALM
always converges to a Lagrange multiplier in case of existence,
while keeping the desirable global convergence properties of the primal sequence.
Hence, this approach combines the advantages of both classical and (rigidly) safeguarded ALMs.
To the best of our knowledge, the latter findings are completely new,
and we conjecture that, at least in parts, they can be extended to
more general composite optimization problems.

Focusing at the situation where all minimizers are stationary,
the attentive reader may wonder as to
why to construct an ALM with elastic safeguard to just recover the guarantees of the classical ALM.
In order to make this clear,
one has to observe that the latter scheme fails to have meaningful global convergence properties
(in the sense of arbitrary initial points) already
for general convex composite problems, see, e.g.,
[
39
]
again,
whereas the former maintains this useful property
(proving this fact is simple, based on results for the rigid safeguard, such as
[
9

Thus, the elastic safeguard enables good behavior in both the convex and nonconvex settings.
The latter is important as, in numerical practice, it might be an involved or even impossible task
to check convexity of some arbitrary given optimization problem.

Below, we present a helpful result which can be used to verify full convergence of sequences.
It is a finite-dimensional counterpart of
[

As we slightly weakened the assumptions, a proof is included for the purpose of completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\{y^{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a sequence, and let
Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a nonempty set.
Assume that each accumulation point of
\{y^{k}\}
belongs to
Y

and that, for each
y\in Y
,
\{\|y^{k}-y\|\}
converges.
Then there exists
\bar{y}\in Y
such that
y^{k}\to\bar{y}
.

Proof.

As
Y
is nonempty, we find
y\in Y
,
and the convergence of
\{\|y^{k}-y\|\}

immediately yields the boundedness of
\{y^{k}\}
via the inverse triangle inequality.
Hence,
\{y^{k}\}
possesses accumulation points.
We now prove that
\{y^{k}\}
possesses a unique accumulation point
\bar{y}

which then already must be the limit of
\{y^{k}\}
,
and
\bar{y}\in Y
follows from the assumptions.

Suppose that
\hat{y},\check{y}\in Y
are accumulation points of
\{y^{k}\}
.
Then, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have
\|\hat{y}-\check{y}\|\leq\|\hat{y}-y^{k}\|+\|y^{k}-\check{y}\|
.
By assumption, the sequences
\{\|\hat{y}-y^{k}\|\}
and
\{\|y^{k}-\check{y}\|\}

converge, and as
\hat{y}
and
\check{y}
are accumulation points of
\{y^{k}\}
,
their limit must be
0
.
Hence,
\|\hat{y}-y^{k}\|+\|y^{k}-\check{y}\|\to 0
follows,
which yields
\hat{y}=\check{y}
.

The

.
The set
\epi\varphi:=\{(z,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\,|\,\alpha\geq\varphi(z)\}
is called the

of
\varphi
.
Note that
\varphi
is lsc if and only if
\epi\varphi
is closed.
Whenever
\varphi
is convex and
\bar{z}\in\dom\varphi
is a point where
\varphi
is finite,

\partial\varphi(\bar{z}):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,\forall z\in\dom\varphi\colon\,\varphi(z)\geq\varphi(\bar{z})+\langle y,z-\bar{z}\rangle\}

of

at

.
Partial subdifferentiation will be denoted in canonical way using appropriate subscripts.
For
\varphi:=\indicator_{C}
, where

.

.

\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}

, the proper, lsc, convex function

2.2
Recession cones and functions

For a nonempty, closed, convex set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we use

C_{\infty}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,\forall z\in C\colon\,z+v\in C\}

to denote the

of

see, e.g.,
[

.
Furthermore, for a proper, lsc, convex function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
we refer to the proper, lsc, convex function
\varphi_{\infty}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}

given via

\epi\varphi_{\infty}:=(\epi\varphi)_{\infty}

of

The following lemma shows that a convex function and its Moreau envelope share the same recession function.
Its mainly technical proof is presented in
Appendix

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, lsc, and convex.
Then, for each
\gamma>0
, we have
(\varphi^{\gamma})_{\infty}=\varphi_{\infty}
, i.e.,
the recession functions of
\varphi
and its Moreau envelope
\varphi^{\gamma}
are the same.

In the course of the paper, for a proper, lsc, convex function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
we make use of

\hzn\varphi:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,\forall z\in\dom\varphi\colon\,\varphi(z+v)\leq\varphi(z)\},

of
\varphi

In the subsequently stated lemma, we summarize some properties of the horizon operation.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, lsc, convex function.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

We have
\hzn\varphi=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\varphi_{\infty}(v)\leq 0\}=(\dom\varphi^{\ast})^{\circ}
.
Particularly,
\hzn\varphi

(ii)

For

\varphi(z_{1})\leq\varphi(z_{2})

(iii)

For each
\gamma>0

\hzn\varphi^{\gamma}=\hzn\varphi

Proof.

The equations in assertion
(i)

are due to

For the proof of assertion
(ii)
, pick
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

such that
z_{1}-z_{2}\in\hzn\varphi
. This yields
\varphi(z+(z_{1}-z_{2}))\leq\varphi(z)

for each
z\in\dom\varphi
.
If
z_{2}\notin\dom\varphi
,
\varphi(z_{1})\leq\varphi(z_{2})
is trivially satisfied.
Otherwise, we can pick
z:=z_{2}
to obtain this relation as well.

Finally, let us prove assertion
(iii)
.
Picking
\gamma>0
arbitrarily, we can apply
Lemma

2.2

and assertion
(i)
in order to find

\hzn\varphi=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,\varphi_{\infty}(v)\leq 0\}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,|\,(\varphi^{\gamma})_{\infty}(v)\leq 0\}=\hzn\varphi^{\gamma}

which already yields the claim.
∎

Let us note that assertion
(ii)
of
Lemma

2.3

can already be found in
[
19
, Lem. 7]

where a more involved proof is presented.

2.3
On inexact PPA

We consider a given proper, lsc, and convex function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.
The exact proximal operator
\prox_{\gamma\varphi}
of
\varphi
with stepsize
\gamma>0

has been introduced in
Section

2.1
.
Let us represent the
inexact proximal mapping
of
\varphi
with stepsize
\gamma>0
by

\prox_{\gamma\varphi}^{\varepsilon}(z):=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\middle|\,\|y-\prox_{\gamma\varphi}(z)\|\leq\sqrt{2\gamma\varepsilon}\right\}

(2.3)

for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
A similar notion of inexactness for proximal operators is exploited in

[
3
,
50
]
.
It is apparent that, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\prox_{\gamma\varphi}^{0}(z)=\{\prox_{\gamma\varphi}(z)\}

and, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\prox_{\gamma\varphi}(z)\in\prox_{\gamma\varphi}^{\varepsilon}(z)
.
Notice that, whenever
\varepsilon>0
,
\prox_{\gamma\varphi}^{\varepsilon}
is set-valued.
Furthermore, this notion of inexactness is closely related to proximal objective suboptimality.
Indeed, picking
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

\varphi(y)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|y-z\|^{2}\leq\varphi^{\gamma}(z)+\varepsilon,

the
\frac{1}{\gamma}
-uniform convexity of the function
\Phi_{z}:=\varphi+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2\gamma}}\|\cdot-z\|^{2}

and the inclusion
0\in\partial\Phi_{z}(\prox_{\gamma\varphi}(z))
for the exact proximal evaluation lead to the inequalities

\varepsilon\geq\Phi_{z}(y)-\Phi_{z}(\prox_{\gamma\varphi}(z))\geq\frac{1}{2\gamma}\|y-\prox_{\gamma\varphi}(z)\|^{2},

i.e.,
y\in\prox_{\gamma\varphi}^{\varepsilon}(z)
.
Hence, the notion of inexactness exploited here is weaker, and thus allows for more flexibility,
than the one based on proximal objective suboptimality,
which was adopted, among others, in
[
56
]
.

The (inexact) PPA we are dealing with is presented in
Algorithm

2.1
.
It allows for adaptive stepsizes (bounded away from zero) and exploits the inexact proximal operator
defined in (
2.3
).

Data:

\underline{\gamma}>0
;
y^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

for

k=0,1,2\ldots

do

1

Select
\gamma_{k}\geq\underline{\gamma}
and
\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0
.

2

Compute
y^{k+1}\in\prox_{\gamma_{k}\varphi}^{\varepsilon_{k}}(y^{k})
.

Algorithm 2.1

Inexact PPA for minimization of
\varphi
.

In the following proposition, we present convergence properties of
Algorithm

2.1
.
We note that these results can also be retrieved
from the classical findings in
[
50
, Thm 1]
.
Regardless, we present here an explicit and self-contained proof,
inspired by the one of
[
5
, Thm 1]
,
so we can fall back on several of its arguments when we prove some of our main results in

Lemma

5.2

as well as

Theorems

5.3
and
6.2
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be proper, lsc, convex, and bounded below.
Let
\{y^{k}\}
be a sequence generated by
Algorithm

2.1

such that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}<\infty
.
Then the following hold:

(i)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}^{-1}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}<\infty
, hence
\gamma_{k}^{-1}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0
;

(ii)

if
\varphi
does not possess a minimizer,
then
\|y^{k}\|\to\infty
;

(iii)

if
\varphi
possesses a minimizer,
then
\{y^{k}\}
converges to a minimizer of
\varphi
.

Proof.

To start, let us prove assertion
(i)
.
Note that we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\underline{\gamma}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\underline{\gamma}\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\underline{\gamma}}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}<\infty,

and, thus, as necessarily
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
.
Similarly, we find that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define the exact proximal step as

y^{k+1}_{\rm e}:=\prox_{\gamma_{k}\varphi}(y^{k})
.
Then we have

\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|\leq\sqrt{2\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}

(2.4)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, (
2.2
) gives us

\varphi(y^{k+1}_{\rm e})\leq\varphi(y^{k}_{\rm e})+\frac{1}{2\gamma_{k}}\left(\|y^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}\right).

Summing up the above inequalities for
k=1,\ldots,N
yields

\varphi(y^{N+1}_{\rm e})\leq\varphi(y^{1}_{\rm e})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\left(\|y^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}\right).

(2.5)

Hence, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\left(\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|+\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|+\|y^{k}_{\rm e}-y^{k}\|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 3\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\left(\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|^{2}+\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}+\|y^{k}_{\rm e}-y^{k}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 6\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}+3\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}+\frac{6}{\underline{\gamma}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle 6\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}+\frac{12}{\underline{\gamma}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}+6\left(\varphi(y^{1}_{\rm e})-\inf\varphi\right),

where the last two inequalities are due to (
2.4
)
and (
2.5
). Noting that the right-hand side of this estimate is a finite constant,
taking the limit
N\to\infty
yields the first statement in
(i)
.
The latter implies
\gamma_{k}^{-1}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\to 0
,
and noting that
\{\gamma_{k}^{-1}\}
is bounded,

\gamma_{k}^{-1}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0
follows.

As a next step, we aim to show that each accumulation point of
\{y^{k}\}

is a minimizer of
\varphi
.
Therefore, pick a subsequence
\{y^{k+1}\}_{k\in K}
and some point
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

such that
y^{k+1}\to_{K}\tilde{y}
.
Then (
2.4
) immediately yields
y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\to_{K}\tilde{y}
.
By definition of the proximal mapping,
we find
0\in\partial(\varphi+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2\gamma_{k}}}\|\cdot-y^{k}\|^{2})(y^{k+1}_{\rm e})

or, equivalently,

-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k})\in\partial\varphi(y^{k+1}_{\rm e})

(2.6)

for each
k\in K
.
Noting that

\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k})=\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k+1})+\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0

holds according to the boundedness of
\{\gamma_{k}^{-1}\}
,
(
2.4
), and the first part of the proof,
taking the limit
k\to_{K}\infty
in (
2.6
) while respecting

[
45
, Thm 24.4]
yields
0\in\partial\varphi(\tilde{y})
.
This, however, means that
\tilde{y}
is a minimizer of
\varphi
.

The above immediately proves assertion
(ii)
.
Indeed, if
\{y^{k}\}
possesses a bounded subsequence, then the latter possesses an accumulation
point which would be a minimizer of
\varphi
. This, however, is a contradiction.

To complete the proof, let us verify assertion
(iii)
.
Therefore, let
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a minimizer of
\varphi
.
Noting that
\bar{y}
is a fixed point of
\prox_{\gamma\varphi}
for each
\gamma>0
,
we find

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-\bar{y}\|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|+\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-\bar{y}\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:inexact_prox_bound_distance_concrete}}{\leq}{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}+\|\prox_{\gamma_{k}\varphi}(y^{k})-\prox_{\gamma_{k}\varphi}(\bar{y})\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}+\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
exploiting
the nonexpansiveness of the proximal operator.
The above yields that the sequence
\{\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\sqrt{2\gamma_{\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}}\}

is monotonically nonincreasing, since

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-\bar{y}\|-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sqrt{2\gamma_{\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}+\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\sqrt{2\gamma_{\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}}=\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|-\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\sqrt{2\gamma_{\ell}\varepsilon_{\ell}}

holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and bounded below by the finite constant
-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{2\gamma_{k}\varepsilon_{k}}
.
Hence, it converges,
and the calculus rules for convergent sequences yield that
\{\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|\}
converges.
Noting that the argumentation is valid for each minimizer of
\varphi
,
the assertion follows from
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

2.4
Lagrangian framework

Let us compile here some underlying concepts of ALMs.
Introducing a (Lagrange) multiplier
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we define
the
Lagrangian
function
\mathcal{L}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
of (
P
) by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x,y):={}

\displaystyle f(x)+\inf_{z}\left\{g(z)+\langle y,c(x)-z\rangle\right\}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle f(x)+\langle y,c(x)\rangle-g^{\ast}(y).

By definition, the mapping
\mathcal{L}(x,\cdot)
is concave for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Rockafellar
[
53
]
refers to
\mathcal{L}
as the
generalized Lagrangian
function
associated to (
P
).
For more details, the interested reader may also study the discussion
in
[
25
, Rem. 3.5]
.
By
[
55
, Thm 11.1]
we have

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=f(x)+g(c(x))

\displaystyle=f(x)+g^{\ast\ast}(c(x))

(2.7)

\displaystyle=f(x)+\sup_{y}\left\{\langle y,c(x)\rangle-g^{\ast}(y)\right\}=\sup_{y}\mathcal{L}(x,y)

for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Following the same pattern,
adding a quadratic penalty for the constraint violation
leads to the definition of the
augmented Lagrangian
function

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
associated to (
P
), given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(x,y){}:={}

\displaystyle f(x)+\inf_{z}\left\{g(z)+\langle y,c(x)-z\rangle+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|c(x)-z\|^{2}\right\}

(2.8)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle f(x)+\inf_{z}\left\{g(z)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|c(x)+\mu y-z\|^{2}\right\}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle f(x)+g^{\mu}(c(x)+\mu y)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}

for some penalty parameter
\mu>0
.
Let us note that,
according to
[
6
, Prop. 13.24 (iii)]
and
[
55
, Thm 11.1]
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(x,y)

\displaystyle=f(x)+(g^{\mu})^{\ast\ast}(c(x)+\mu y)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x)+\sup_{z}\left\{\langle z,c(x)+\mu y\rangle-(g^{\mu})^{\ast}(z)\right\}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=f(x)+\sup_{z}\left\{\langle z,c(x)+\mu y\rangle-g^{\ast}(z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z\|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=f(x)+\sup_{z}\left\{\langle z,c(x)\rangle-g^{\ast}(z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{z}\left\{\mathcal{L}(x,z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}.

(2.9)

The underlying idea of the ALM is now to
minimize, in each iteration, the augmented Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,y)
,
and to perform a suitable update of the penalty parameter as well as the multiplier afterwards.
Note that our assumptions will guarantee that
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,y)
is convex which
allows for its (approximate) global minimization, see
Lemma

3.2
below.

Let us comment on some more elementary terminology regarding (
P
).
A feasible point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of (
P
) is called
stationary

whenever there exists
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}

such that
0\in\partial_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
0\in\partial_{y}(-\mathcal{L})(\bar{x},\bar{y})
,
i.e., conditions

\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0,\qquad c(\bar{x})\in\partial g^{\ast}(\bar{y})

are valid.
Owing to convexity of
g
, the latter system is equivalent to

\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0,\qquad\bar{y}\in\partial g(c(\bar{x})),

and, thus, implicitly requires feasibility of
\bar{x}
.
Let us note that stationarity of
\bar{x}
implies
0\in\partial\Phi(\bar{x})
,
and thus, as
\Phi
is assumed to be convex, minimality of
\bar{x}
for
\Phi
.
The converse is true in the presence of a qualification condition.
Exemplary, the condition

c^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{\top}y=0,\,y\in N_{\dom g}(c(\bar{x}))\quad\implies\quad y=0

(2.10)

can be used for that purpose, see, e.g.,
[
17
, Prop. 5.2]
,
as it implies
\partial(g\circ c)(\bar{x})=c^{\prime}(\bar{x})^{\top}\partial g(c(\bar{x}))
.
Throughout the paper, however, we will sometimes merely assume the existence of a
minimizer of (
P
) which is stationary,
and disregard qualification conditions.
Finally, let

\mathcal{Y}(\bar{x}):=\{y\in\partial g(c(\bar{x}))\,|\,\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},y)=0\}

be the set of multipliers associated with
\bar{x}
.
The set-valued mapping
\mathcal{Y}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
the so-called multiplier mapping and
possesses a closed graph,
see
[
55
, Ex. 13.30]
.

Fully convex composite optimization

Throughout the remainder of the paper,
let us assume that (

which, in parts, have already been outlined in

.

Assumption 3.1
.

The following hold for (

•

is continuously differentiable and convex;

•

is proper, lsc, convex,

•

(-\hzn g)
-convex, i.e.,

holds.

Remark 3.3
.

Let us note that, in order to obtain convexity of the composition
g\circ c
, one could also consider the more general
setting where, given a nonempty, closed, convex cone
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the properties

(i)

is
(-K)
-convex, i.e., for all

and

(ii)

.

can be

.

To compute this conjugate, we want to invoke

and

\bar{c}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
via
\bar{c}(u,x):=c(x)-u

so that

\overline{\Phi}(u,x)=\bar{f}(u,x)+g(\bar{c}(u,x)).

(3.3)

We first observe that, by the assumptions on

,
g
, and

the qualification condition in
[

is trivially satisfied
(as
\bar{f}
and

Therefore, as we have
\dom g^{\ast}\subseteq(\hzn g)^{\circ}
from

(i)
,

[
19

yields

\overline{\Phi}^{\ast}(p,q)=\inf_{y,r,s}\left\{g^{\ast}(y)+\bar{f}^{\ast}(r,s)+\langle y,\bar{c}\rangle^{\ast}\left((p-r,q-s)\right)\right\}.

(3.4)

We now notice that

\bar{f}^{\ast}(r,s)=\indicator_{\{0\}}(r)+f^{\ast}(s).

Moreover, a short computation shows that

\langle y,\bar{c}\rangle^{\ast}(r,s)=\indicator_{\{0\}}(r+y)+\langle y,c\rangle^{\ast}(s).

Inserting in (

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}^{\ast}(p,0)={}

\displaystyle\inf_{y,r,s}\left\{g^{\ast}(y)+\indicator_{\{0\}}(r)+f^{\ast}(s)+\indicator_{\{0\}}(y+p-r)+\langle y,c\rangle^{\ast}(-s)\right\}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle g^{\ast}(-p)+\inf_{s}\left\{f^{\ast}(s)+\langle-p,c\rangle^{\ast}(-s)\right\}.

This already explains condition
(ii)(a)
.
Condition
(ii)(b)
is equivalent to
(ii)(a)

\inf_{s}\left\{f^{\ast}(s)+\langle\bar{y},c\rangle^{\ast}(-s)\right\}=(f+\langle\bar{y},c\rangle)^{\ast}(0)=-\inf\left\{f+\langle\bar{y},c\rangle\right\},

where
[
45

has been used for the the first identity.
We observe that
[

, Thm 3.26, Cor. 3.27, and Ex. 3.29]
yield
that condition
(ii)(c)
implies
(ii)(b)
.

Finally, let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a stationary minimizer of (
P
) with associated multiplier
\bar{y}
.
We first note that
\bar{y}\in\dom g^{\ast}

).
Moreover,

implies that

is a minimizer of the smooth convex function

,
see

3.2

(ii)
.
Hence, condition
(ii)(d)
implies
(ii)(b)
.
∎

3.2
Duality

We now study duality relations between (
P
) and some appropriate dual problem.
Of course, these results can, in more abstract form, be already found in
[
45
, Sec. 30]

for large parts.
However, we derive the essentials here in order to provide a self-contained presentation.

To start, let us introduce a suitable dual problem associated with (
P
).
Let
\mathpzc{M}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
be defined by

\mathpzc{M}(y):=\inf_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,y).

(3.5)

By definition and
3.1
,
\mathpzc{M}
indeed only takes values in
\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
.
Recalling (
2.7
), we refer to

\maximize_{y}~\mathpzc{M}(y)

(D)

as the (Lagrange)
dual problem
of (
P
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Consider (
P
) and (
D
) under
3.1
.

(i)

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we always have
\Phi(x)\geq\mathpzc{M}(y)
.
Particularly, the infimal value of (
P
) is not smaller than the supremal value of (
D
),
and if
\Phi(x)=\mathpzc{M}(y)
, then
x
is a minimizer of (
P
)
and
y
is a maximizer of (
D
).

(ii)

Assume that (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer.
Then (
P
) and (
D
) possess solutions,
and the optimal function values coincide.

Proof.

Let us start to verify assertion
(i)
.
We only prove validity of the estimate
\Phi(x)\geq\mathpzc{M}(y)
.
The additional statements are direct consequences of it.
Whenever
\Phi(x)=\infty
or
\mathpzc{M}(y)=-\infty
,
\Phi(x)\geq\mathpzc{M}(y)
is trivially satisfied.
Hence, let us assume that
\Phi(x)<\infty
and
\mathpzc{M}(y)>-\infty
.
This gives
c(x)\in\dom g
and
y\in\dom g^{\ast}
, so that the Fenchel–Young inequality,
see
[
55
, Prop. 11.3]
, yields

\displaystyle\mathpzc{M}(y)=\inf_{x^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},y)={}

\displaystyle\inf_{x^{\prime}}\left\{f(x^{\prime})+\langle y,c(x^{\prime})\rangle\right\}-g^{\ast}(y)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\inf_{x^{\prime}}\left\{f(x^{\prime})+\langle y,c(x^{\prime})\rangle\right\}+g(c(x))-\langle y,c(x)\rangle

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle f(x)+\langle y,c(x)\rangle+g(c(x))-\langle y,c(x)\rangle=\Phi(x).

Let us now turn to assertion
(ii)
.
The stationarity assumption guarantees the existence of

\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{y}\in\partial g(c(\bar{x}))
such that
\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0
.
This yields
c(\bar{x})\in\dom g
,
\bar{y}\in\dom g^{\ast}
, and
\langle\bar{y},c(\bar{x})\rangle=g(c(\bar{x}))+g^{\ast}(\bar{y})
,
again due to
[
55
, Prop. 11.3]
.
Furthermore, due to the convexity of
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\bar{y})
, which follows from

Lemma

3.2

(ii)
,

\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0
yields that
\bar{x}
is a minimizer
of the convex function
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\bar{y})
,
i.e.,
\mathpzc{M}(\bar{y})=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
Combining these facts, we obtain

\mathpzc{M}(\bar{y})=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=f(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{y},c(\bar{x})\rangle-g^{\ast}(\bar{y})=f(\bar{x})+g(c(\bar{x}))=\Phi(\bar{x}),

so that assertion
(ii)
follows from
(i)
.
∎

Let us note that
Lemma

3.8

(i)
and its proof work even in the absence of any convexity assumptions
as, particularly, the exploited Fenchel–Young inequality holds for any function
g
by definition of the conjugate.
Observe that
Lemma

3.8

(i)
ensures that
\mathpzc{M}
is globally upper bounded
as (
P
) is assumed to be a feasible problem in
3.1
.
Let us also mention that, whenever the convexity assumptions are removed,

Lemma

3.8

(ii)
does not remain true in general.

Definition 3.9
.

A pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is referred to as a
saddle point
of
\mathcal{L}

if the following condition holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
:

\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},y)\leq\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{y}).

Let us note that whenever
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
,
then
\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is finite since
g^{\ast}
is proper
(by properness of
g
and
[
55
, Thm 11.1]
).

Lemma 3.10
.

For a pair
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the following statements are equivalent under
3.1
:

(i)

\bar{x}
is stationary for (
P
) with associated Lagrange multiplier
\bar{y}
;

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a minimizer of (
P
),
\bar{y}
is a maximizer of (
D
),
and
\Phi(\bar{x})=\mathpzc{M}(\bar{y})
;

(iii)

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

The equivalence follows from a chain of implications.

(i)
\implies
(ii)
:
This implication has been validated in the proof of
Lemma

3.8

(ii)
.

(ii)
\implies
(iii)
:
The assumptions, (
2.7
), and (
3.5
) guarantee

\displaystyle\mathpzc{M}(\bar{y})=\inf_{x^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},\bar{y})\leq\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq\sup_{y^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},y^{\prime})=\Phi(\bar{x})=\mathpzc{M}(\bar{y}).

Hence, for each pair
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},y)\leq\sup_{y^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},y^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\inf_{x^{\prime}}\mathcal{L}(x^{\prime},\bar{y})\leq\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{y}),

which shows that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
.

(iii)
\implies
(i)
:
As
\bar{y}
maximizes the concave function
\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\cdot)
,
it minimizes the convex function
-\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\cdot)
.
Due to Fermat’s rule,
this gives
c(\bar{x})\in\partial g^{\ast}(\bar{y})
or, equivalently under
3.1
,

\bar{y}\in\partial g(c(\bar{x}))
.
Particularly,
\bar{y}\in\dom g^{\ast}
, and
Lemma

3.2

(ii)

guarantees convexity of
\mathcal{L}(\cdot,\bar{y})
.
As this function is continuously differentiable,
the fact that
\bar{x}
is one of its minimizers is enough to find

\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=0
.
Hence,
\bar{x}
is stationary for (
P
) with associated
multiplier
\bar{y}
.
∎

Lemma

3.10
yields a series of interesting
consequences which we record in what follows.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
3.1
hold, and
let
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be stationary points of (
P
).
Then
\mathcal{Y}(x)=\mathcal{Y}(x^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It suffices to pick two arbitrary stationary points
x^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
i=1,2
, and
to show that, for
y^{i}\in\mathcal{Y}(x^{i})
,
i=1,2
,
we have
y^{3-i}\in\mathcal{Y}(x^{i})
.
Due to
Lemma

3.10
, for
i=1,2
,
(x^{i},y^{i})

is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e.,
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have

\mathcal{L}(x^{i},y)\leq\mathcal{L}(x^{i},y^{i})\leq\mathcal{L}(x,y^{i}).

Particularly, for
i=1,2
, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(x^{i},y^{3-i})\leq\mathcal{L}(x^{i},y^{i})\leq\mathcal{L}(x^{3-i},y^{i})\leq\mathcal{L}(x^{3-i},y^{3-i})\leq\mathcal{L}(x^{i},y^{3-i})

which gives

\mathcal{L}(x^{1},y^{1})=\mathcal{L}(x^{2},y^{1})=\mathcal{L}(x^{1},y^{2})=\mathcal{L}(x^{2},y^{2})
.
Consequently, for
i=1,2
,
(x^{i},y^{3-i})
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
,
and
Lemma

3.10
yields
y^{3-i}\in\mathcal{Y}(x^{i})
,
concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
3.1
hold,
and assume that (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer.
Then each minimizer of (
P
) is stationary.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a stationary minimizer of (
P
),
and pick another minimizer
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of (
P
).
The assumptions guarantee that we find some
\bar{y}\in\mathcal{Y}(\bar{x})
.
Noting that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
according to
Lemma

3.10
,
we have
\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x},\bar{y})
.
Conversely,
\Phi(\bar{x})=\Phi^{\star}=\Phi(\tilde{x})
and
\bar{y}\in\partial g(c(\bar{x}))
yield

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x},\bar{y})

\displaystyle{}=f(\tilde{x})+\langle\bar{y},c(\tilde{x})\rangle-g^{\ast}(\bar{y})

\displaystyle{}=f(\bar{x})+g(c(\bar{x}))-g(c(\tilde{x}))+\langle\bar{y},c(\tilde{x})\rangle-g^{\ast}(\bar{y})

\displaystyle{}\leq f(\bar{x})+\langle\bar{y},c(\bar{x})\rangle-g^{\ast}(\bar{y})=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

Hence,
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x},\bar{y})=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
,
and
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x},\bar{y})\leq\mathcal{L}(x,\bar{y})
for all

follows.

From
Lemma

3.10
we also have
\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\Phi(\bar{x})=\Phi^{\star}
,
so that (

) gives

\sup_{y}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x},y)=\Phi(\tilde{x})=\Phi^{\star}=\mathcal{L}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\mathcal{L}(\tilde{x},\bar{y}).

Hence,
(\tilde{x},\bar{y})
is a saddle point of
\mathcal{L}
,
and applying

3.10
one last time yields
that
\tilde{x}
is stationary (with associated multiplier
\bar{y}
).
∎

In the lemma below, we summarize essential properties of the (negative) dual objective function.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
3.1
be valid.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

The function
-\mathpzc{M}
is convex, lsc, and bounded below by
-\Phi^{\star}
.

(ii)

We have
-\mathpzc{M}(y)=\mathcal{V}^{\ast}(-y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
where
\mathcal{V}
is the value function from (

(iii)

\mathcal{V}
is proper and lsc
(see

for some sufficient conditions),

-\mathpzc{M}

Proof.

The first assertion is clear by definition of
\mathpzc{M}
and
3.1
.
The lower boundedness of
-\mathpzc{M}
directly follows
from

3.10

(i)
,
which yields
-\mathpzc{M}(y)\geq-\Phi^{\star}>-\infty

for each

.

Thus, let us proceed by proving the second assertion. Using the substitution

\displaystyle\mathpzc{M}(y)=\inf_{x}\mathcal{L}(x,y)=

\displaystyle={}

4
Classical ALM through the PPA lens

Consider the ALM stated in
Algorithm

4.1
.
It employs the so-called
Hestenes–Powell–Rockafellar
update rule for the multiplier in
Algorithm

4.1
,
see
[
34
,
43
,
46
]
,
and allows for a variety of update rules for the penalty parameter.

We recall that, by convexity of
g
,
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,y)
is a continuously differentiable function
for each
\mu>0
.
In the convex environment considered in this paper,
due to
3.1
,
it is reasonable to pursue a global minimization approach in the spirit of
[
9
, Ch. 5]
,
demanding that the augmented Lagrangian subproblems at
Algorithm

4.1
are solved according to

\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(x^{k},y^{k})\leq\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(\cdot,y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}

(4.1)

for some bounded or null sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
of nonnegative inexactness parameters.

Data:

\mu_{0}>0
;
y^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
;
\beta\in(0,1)

for

k=0,1,2\ldots

do

1

Select
\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0
and compute

such that (

) holds.

2

Set

.

3

Set

.

4

Set
\mu_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}\mu_{k}
for some

.

Algorithm 4.1

Classical ALM for (
P
).

Note that the sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}
of penalty parameters in
Algorithm

4.1

is monotonically nonincreasing and stays bounded.
Hence, it converges (to some point in
[0,\mu_{0}]
).
Forthwith, we are concerned with three well-known update rules for the penalty parameter
in
Algorithm

4.1
:

Setting 1
.

We have
\beta_{k}:=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Setting 2
.

There is some
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\beta_{k}\in(0,\beta]
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Setting 3
.

There are some
\beta,\theta\in(0,1)
such that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\mu_{k+1}:=\begin{cases}\mu_{k}&\text{if }k=0\text{ or }\|c(x^{k})-z^{k}\|\leq\theta\|c(x^{k-1})-z^{k-1}\|,\\
\beta\,\mu_{k}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Note that
\mu_{k}
remains constant in

i.e.,
\mu_{k}=\mu_{0}
holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, whereas

\mu_{k}\downarrow 0
in

.
The update rule in
3
is implemented
in the popular solver
Algencan
, see
[
2
,
9
]
.
Moreover, these settings cover also the globally convergent scheme of Conn, Gould, and Toint
[

]
,
known as
bound-constrained Lagrangian
(BCL) algorithm and implemented in the

software package.
Their technique monitors the constraint violation and updates the Lagrange multiplier estimates
only if the primal update achieves sufficient improvement in feasibility,
otherwise the penalty parameter is reduced, see
[
21
, Alg. 1]
.
For our purposes, the always-updating
Algorithm

can be seen as executing the BCL scheme,
after extracting the subsequence with updates of multiplier estimates.

4.1
Dual updates as inexact PPA

When applied to the minimization of the convex function
-\mathpzc{M}
,

Algorithm

2.1
can be defined by the recursion

y^{k+1}\in\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}^{\varepsilon_{k}}(y^{k})

(4.2)

with an upper bounded sequence of (inverse) positive stepsizes
\{\mu_{k}\}
.
In the upcoming theorem,
we will see that this iterative procedure is strongly related to the ALM from
Algorithm

4.1
.
Our proof follows the one of
[
49
, Prop. 6]
,
see
[
3
, Prop. 1.4]
as well,
and is added for the convenience of the reader.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\hat{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\mu>0
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
be fixed.
Suppose that
3.1
is satisfied and that
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is chosen such that

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y})\leq\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,\hat{y})+\varepsilon
.
Set
\bar{z}:=\prox_{\mu g}(c(\bar{x})+\mu\hat{y})

and
\bar{y}:=\hat{y}+\mu^{-1}[c(\bar{x})-\bar{z}]
.
Then
\bar{y}\in\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}^{\varepsilon}(\hat{y})
.

Proof.

For later use, let us denote the exact proximal point by

y_{\rm e}:=\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}).

Differentiability of the Moreau envelope yields

\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y})=c(\bar{x})+\mu\hat{y}-\bar{z}-\mu\hat{y}=c(\bar{x})-\bar{z}=\mu(\bar{y}-\hat{y}),

(4.3)

see
[
6
, Prop. 12.29]
.
Furthermore, let us recall from
Lemma

3.2
that
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\cdot)
is concave.
Hence, for some arbitrary
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we find the estimate

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y})+\langle\mu(\bar{y}-\hat{y}),y-\hat{y}\rangle=\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y})+\langle\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y}),y-\hat{y}\rangle\geq\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},y)\geq\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,y).

Furthermore,
[
45
, Lem. 36.1]

and the definition of
\mathpzc{M}
give

\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,y)\overset{\eqref{eq:rep_AL_func}}{=}{}\inf_{x}\sup_{z}\left\{\mathcal{L}(x,z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}\\
\geq\sup_{z}\inf_{x}\left\{\mathcal{L}(x,z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}\overset{\eqref{eq:PPA:dualL}}{=}{}\sup_{z}\left\{\mathpzc{M}(z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-y\|^{2}\right\}\\
\geq\mathpzc{M}(y_{\rm e})-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y_{\rm e}-y\|^{2},

showing validity of the estimate

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y})+\langle\mu(\bar{y}-\hat{y}),y-\hat{y}\rangle\geq\mathpzc{M}(y_{\rm e})-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y_{\rm e}-y\|^{2}

for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Similarly, using the definition of
y_{\rm e}
, we find

\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,\hat{y})\overset{\eqref{eq:rep_AL_func}}{=}{}\inf_{x}\sup_{z}\left\{\mathcal{L}(x,z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-\hat{y}\|^{2}\right\}\\
=\sup_{z}\inf_{x}\left\{\mathcal{L}(x,z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-\hat{y}\|^{2}\right\}\overset{\eqref{eq:PPA:dualL}}{=}{}\sup_{z}\left\{\mathpzc{M}(z)-\frac{\mu}{2}\|z-\hat{y}\|^{2}\right\}\\
=\mathpzc{M}(y_{\rm e})-\frac{\mu}{2}\|y_{\rm e}-\hat{y}\|^{2}

with the aid of
[
45
, Thm 37.3]
,
which applies as

\nicefrac{{\mu}}{{2}}\|\cdot-\hat{y}\|^{2}-\mathcal{L}(x,\cdot)
is uniformly convex for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

yielding that its level sets are bounded and, thus,
that its recession function is the indicator function of the origin,
see
[
45
, Thm 8.7]
.
Hence, for each
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the above and some simple calculations yield

\displaystyle\varepsilon\geq{}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y})-\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\cdot,\hat{y})

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\|y_{\rm e}-\hat{y}\|^{2}-2\langle\bar{y}-\hat{y},y-\hat{y}\rangle-\|y_{\rm e}-y\|^{2}\right)=\frac{\mu}{2}\left(2\langle y_{\rm e}-\bar{y},y-\hat{y}\rangle-\|y-\hat{y}\|^{2}\right).

Choosing
y:=y_{\rm e}-\bar{y}+\hat{y}
, the right-hand side is maximized,
and we end up with the estimate
\varepsilon\geq\nicefrac{{\mu}}{{2}}\|y_{\rm e}-\bar{y}\|^{2}

yielding the claim by definition of
y_{\rm e}
and (
2.3
).
∎

4.2
Convergence analysis

We are in a position to generalize the classical convergence result
from
[
47
, Thm 2.1]
, see
[
49
, Thm 4]
as well,
which addresses standard convex nonlinear optimization problems with inequality constraints,
to the fully convex composite setting.

In the light of
Proposition

2.4
, it is sufficient to choose the sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}

such that
\{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}\}
is summable
in order to find the convergence of
\{y^{k}\}

provided that (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer.
Note that constructing such a sequence is always possible;
among others, a trivial choice is
\varepsilon_{k}:=\nicefrac{{\mu_{k}}}{{2^{2k}}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem

4.2
summarizes the analysis with inexact subsolves
and general nonincreasing sequence
\{\mu_{k}\}
of penalty parameters.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
3.1
holds and (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer.
Let
\{(x^{k},z^{k},y^{k})\}
be a sequence generated by
Algorithm

4.1

such that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}<\infty.

(4.4)

Then
\{y_{k}\}
converges to some vector in
Y_{\textup{mul}}
,
and we have
c(x^{k})-z^{k}\to 0
and

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})\right)\leq\Phi^{\star}.

(4.5)

Particularly, each accumulation point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\{x^{k}\}
is a minimizer of (
P
).
For each subsequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in K}
such that
x^{k}\to_{K}\bar{x}
,
we have
z^{k}\to_{K}c(\bar{x})
and
f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})\to_{K}\Phi^{\star}
.

Proof.

From
Propositions

2.4
and
4.1
we know that
\{y^{k}\}
converges
to some maximizer
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of (
D
),
and
\bar{y}
belongs to
Y_{\textup{mul}}
, see
Corollary

3.13
.

Next, we observe that

c(x^{k})-z^{k}=\mu_{k}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0

holds due to the convergence of
\{y^{k}\}

and the fact that
\{\mu_{k}\}
is bounded.
Furthermore, (
4.1
) and
[
25
, Lem. 3.2]
give

\displaystyle f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})+\langle y^{k},c(x^{k})-z^{k}\rangle\leq{}

\displaystyle f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})+\langle y^{k},c(x^{k})-z^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\mu_{k}}\|c(x^{k})-z^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(x^{k},y^{k})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(\bar{x},y^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}\leq\Phi^{\star}+\varepsilon_{k}.

Passing to the upper limit as
k\to\infty
while using
c(x^{k})-z^{k}\to 0
,
y^{k}\to\bar{y}
,
and
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
(the latter follows from (

) and boundedness of
\{\mu_{k}\}
),
we find (

).

Finally, let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the limit of the subsequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in K}

for some infinite set
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}
.
Then
c(x^{k})\to_{K}c(\bar{x})
, and, due to
c(x^{k})-z^{k}\to 0
,
z^{k}\to_{K}c(\bar{x})
follows.
Hence, (

) and the lower semicontinuity of

yield

\Phi(\bar{x})\leq\lim_{k\to_{K}\infty}f(x^{k})+\liminf_{k\to_{K}\infty}g(z^{k})\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})\right)\leq\Phi^{\star}.

As this implicitly requires
\bar{x}\in c^{-1}(\dom g)
,
optimality of
\bar{x}
for (
P
) follows.
∎

,

is in fact a stationary minimizer

.

(4.6)

is bounded, then there exist an accumulation point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and a subsequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in K}

,

is a minimizer of (

4.3
Dual updates as proximal-gradient ascent

It is known that the dual update in ALM corresponds to a (projected) gradient ascent step
[
13
, §2.1]
.
This observation is extended here to the setting of (
P
),
for which the dual update at
Algorithm

4.1
of
Algorithm

4.1

can be interpreted as a proximal-gradient ascent step with stepsize
\mu_{k}^{-1}
, as we now uncover.

Specifically, noticing that
-\langle\cdot,c(x)\rangle
and
g^{\ast}
are the smooth and nonsmooth terms of
-\mathcal{L}(x,\cdot)
, respectively,
the dual update
y^{k+1}
turns out to be a proximal-gradient step to maximize the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}(x^{k},\cdot)

starting from the current estimate
y^{k}
, see (
4.7a
).
Alternatively, the dual update can also be recovered as the gradient step (again with stepsize
\mu_{k}^{-1}
)
to maximize the augmented Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(x^{k},\cdot)
from
y^{k}
, see (
4.7b
).
These observations are precisely stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\hat{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\mu>0
be fixed.
Suppose that
3.1
is satisfied.
Set
\bar{z}:=\prox_{\mu g}(c(\bar{x})+\mu\hat{y})

and
\bar{y}:=\hat{y}+\mu^{-1}[c(\bar{x})-\bar{z}]
.
Then the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\bar{y}={}

\displaystyle\prox_{\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}g^{\ast}}\left(\hat{y}+\frac{1}{\mu}c(\bar{x})\right),

(4.7a)

\displaystyle\bar{y}={}

\displaystyle\hat{y}+\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla_{y}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(\bar{x},\hat{y}).

(4.7b)

Proof.

The gradient ascent update in (
4.7b
) readily follows from the definitions of
\bar{z}
and
\bar{y}

as well as (
4.3
).
Using (
4.7b
), the proximal-gradient ascent update in (
4.7a
) is an immediate consequence
of the (extended) Moreau decomposition, see
[
8
, Thm 6.45]
.
∎

5
Safeguarded ALM through the PPA lens

In numerical optimization, so-called safeguarded ALMs are popular as they possess better
global convergence properties than the classical method, see, e.g.,
[
39
]
.
Particularly, the safeguarding mechanism allows us to prove (primal) convergence even in the nonconvex, nonregular, and/or nonsmooth case,
see, e.g.,
[
9
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
37
]
.
Here, “convergence” is understood in the sense of asymptotic optimality or stationarity, even if multipliers do not exist.

Motivated by
Theorem

4.2
, we now aim to discuss the behavior of a prototypical
safeguarded ALM in the fully convex composite setting induced by
3.1
.
The procedure of interest, taken from
[
25
]
, is stated in
Algorithm

.
The safeguarding technique employs a sequence
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
of multiplier estimates which
remains bounded by construction.
Analogously to (
4.1
), subproblems at
Algorithm

5.1
are solved according to

\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(x^{k},\hat{y}^{k})\leq\inf\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(\cdot,\hat{y}^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}.

(5.1)

Let us mention that, similar to
Algorithm

4.1
,

Algorithm

5.1
is reasonable in

1
,
2
and
3
.
However, convergence with constant penalty as in
1
cannot be expected in general.
Similarly, the safeguarding cannot be blindly applied
but it should be accompanied by suitable updates of the penalty parameters.
Both issues are illustrated in
Example

5.6
below.

Data:

y^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
;
\mu_{0}>0
; nonempty, convex, compact set

;
\beta\in(0,1)

for

k=0,1,2\ldots

do

1

Set
\hat{y}^{k}:=\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y^{k})
.

2

Select
\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0
and compute

such that (
5.1
) holds.

3

Set

z^{k}:=\prox_{\mu_{k}g}(c(x^{k})+\mu_{k}\hat{y}^{k})
.

4

Set

y^{k+1}:=\hat{y}^{k}+\mu_{k}^{-1}[c(x^{k})-z^{k}]
.

5

Set
\mu_{k+1}:=\beta_{k}\mu_{k}
for some
\beta_{k}\in(0,\beta]\cup\{1\}
.

Algorithm 5.1

Safeguarded ALM for (
P
).

It follows from
Theorem

4.1
that the safeguarded ALM, sketched in
Algorithm

5.1
,
can be viewed as a generalized PPA type method, applied to the dual problem (
D
), taking

y^{k+1}\in\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}^{\varepsilon_{k}}(\hat{y}^{k})

(5.2)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
In
[
40
]
,
it has been observed that this scheme differs from the original PPA in that it takes
\hat{y}^{k}
instead of
y^{k}
,
and such gap is due to the safeguarding—necessary for global primal convergence in the nonconvex case, see
[
39
]
again.
Furthermore, it has been shown in
[
40
]
that each accumulation point of a sequence
\{y^{k}\}

computed via (
5.2
) for bounded
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
,
\varepsilon_{k}:=0
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
and
\mu_{k}\downarrow 0
is a maximizer of
\mathpzc{M}
.
Here, we will deepen the insights into this generalized PPA significantly
whenever
\hat{y}^{k}
is obtained from
y^{k}
by projection onto a
reasonably chosen safeguarding set
Y_{\textup{sg}}
,
which is the standard way to implement safeguarded ALMs,
and exploit these findings for the convergence analysis of
Algorithm

5.1
.

Remark 5.1
.

Note that the goal behind (safeguarded) augmented Lagrangian methods is not only to find
minimizers of (
P
) as accumulation points of the produced primal sequence,
but to construct associated multipliers (in case of existence)
as accumulation points of the produced dual sequence.
Given
\bar{z}\in\dom g
, one can easily check that
\partial g(\bar{z})\subseteq(\hzn g)^{\circ}
is valid, i.e.,
the images of the set-valued mapping
\partial g\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
are contained in
the polar cone of
\hzn g
.
Insofar, observing that all possible multipliers of (
P
),
which are sufficiently small in norm,
should belong to
Y_{\textup{sg}}
,
it makes sense to choose
Y_{\textup{sg}}:=\{y\in(\hzn g)^{\circ}\,|\,\|y\|\leq C\}

for some large constant
C>0
.
Whenever
g
is the indicator function of a closed, convex cone,
this is, indeed, the preferred choice for
Y_{\textup{sg}}
.

5.1
Dual updates as inexact backward-backward splitting

Note that the dual iterates
\{y^{k}\}
in
Algorithm

5.1
obey the recurrence

y^{k+1}\in\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}^{\varepsilon_{k}}\left(\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y^{k})\right)

(5.3)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, see
Theorem

4.1
.
Here, we do not discuss further the associated, though less interesting, recurrence

\hat{y}^{k+1}\in\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}\left(\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}^{\varepsilon_{k}}(\hat{y}^{k})\right)

on the surrogate sequence
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
.

In order to get a feeling of the behavior of the iteration (
5.3
),
let us assume, for simplicity, that
\varepsilon_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
that
1
is chosen.
Let
\mu:=\mu_{0}
denote the associated constant penalty parameter.
Noting that the operators
\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}
and
\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}
are firmly nonexpansive,
in this particular case,
the iterative procedure from (
5.3
)
is a special instance of the method discussed in
[
4
,
5
]
,
see
[
1
,
7
]
as well.
Following
[
4
, Thm 4.2]

while noting that the image of
\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}
is nonempty and compact,
we obtain that (
5.3
) yields a sequence converging to a fixed point of

\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}\circ\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}
provided there is one.
Let us note that the fixed-point condition

\bar{y}=\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}(\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\bar{y}))

is equivalent to the inclusion

\mu\left(\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\bar{y})-\bar{y}\right)\in\partial(-\mathpzc{M})(\bar{y})

(5.4)

by definition of the proximal operator and the convexity of
-\mathpzc{M}
.
Using
[
6
, Cor. 12.30]
,
this is the same as postulating that
\bar{y}
is a stationary
point of the convex optimization problem

\minimize_{y}{}\quad{}-\mathpzc{M}(y)+\frac{\mu}{2}\dist_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}^{2}(y),

(D
μ
)

i.e., one of its minimizers.
Note that (
Dμ
) can be interpreted as a penalized version
of the dual problem (
D
) complemented by the constraint
y\in Y_{\textup{sg}}
.
As (
P
) is assumed to be feasible,
the objective function of (
D
) is lower bounded due to
Lemma

3.8
,
and boundedness of
Y_{\textup{sg}}
additionally yields that (
Dμ
) is coercive.
Hence, it possesses a minimizer
whenever
-\mathpzc{M}
is proper and lsc
which can be guaranteed via mild assumptions on the problem data,
see
Lemmas

3.5
and
3.14
,
so that (
5.3
) indeed produces a convergent sequence
in this special situation.

In a remark, Andreani et al.
[
2
, §5]
ask the following question:
“
What happens if the box chosen for the safeguarded multiplier estimates is too small?
”
Among other things, our streamlined convergence analysis in
Section

5.2

rediscovers their answer from the PPA perspective:
global convergence properties remain, but penalty parameters should vanish if the safeguarding
set
Y_{\textup{sg}}
does not comprise a multiplier from
Y_{\textup{mul}}
provided there is one.

5.2
Convergence analysis

We now present a detailed convergence analysis of the safeguarded ALM from
Algorithm

5.1
.
To start, let us comment on general properties of the dual sequence
\{(y^{k},\hat{y}^{k})\}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose that
3.1
holds.
Furthermore, let the value function
\mathcal{V}
from (
3.1
)
be proper and lsc.
Let
\{(x^{k},z^{k},y^{k},\hat{y}^{k})\}
be a sequence generated by

Algorithm

5.1

such that (
4.4
) holds.
Then

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}<\infty,\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\|\hat{y}^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k}\|^{2}<\infty

(5.5)

and, particularly, the convergences

\mu_{k}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0
and
\mu_{k}(\hat{y}^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k})\to 0
hold.
Moreover, each accumulation point of
\{y^{k}\}
is
a solution of the convex optimization problem (
Dμ
),
where
\mu\geq 0
is such that
\mu_{k}\searrow\mu
.

Proof.

The proof is closely related to the one of
Proposition

2.4
,
so we present it briefly.

Again, we define the exact step

y^{k+1}_{\rm e}:=\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}^{k})

(5.6)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and obtain the estimate

\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}

(5.7)

from
Theorem

4.1
, showing
y^{k+1}\in\prox^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}^{k})
, and (
2.3
).
We will make use of the function
\Psi_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

\Psi_{k}(y,\hat{y}):=-\mathpzc{M}(y)+\indicator_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\hat{y})+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\|y-\hat{y}\|^{2}

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Using inequality (
2.2
), we find

\displaystyle-\mathpzc{M}(y^{k+1}_{\rm e})\leq{}

\displaystyle-\mathpzc{M}(y^{k}_{\rm e})+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\left(\|\hat{y}^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|\hat{y}^{k}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\indicator_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\hat{y}^{k})\leq{}

\displaystyle\indicator_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\hat{y}^{k-1})+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\left(\|y^{k}-\hat{y}^{k-1}\|^{2}-\|y^{k}-\hat{y}^{k}\|^{2}-\|\hat{y}^{k}-\hat{y}^{k-1}\|^{2}\right).

Since
\{\mu_{k}\}
is nonincreasing,
adding up these estimates gives

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(y^{k+1}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{k})\leq{}

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(y^{k}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{k-1})+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\left(\|\hat{y}^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}+\|y^{k}-\hat{y}^{k-1}\|^{2}-\|y^{k}_{\rm e}-\hat{y}^{k-1}\|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.-\|y^{k}-\hat{y}^{k}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|\hat{y}^{k}-\hat{y}^{k-1}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:four_point_inequality}}{\leq}{}

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(y^{k}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{k-1})+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\left(\|y^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\Psi_{k-1}(y^{k}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{k-1})+\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\left(\|y^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}\right).

For some
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
let us sum up the above inequalities for
k=1,\ldots,N

in order to obtain

\displaystyle\Psi_{N}(y^{N+1}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{N})\leq{}

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}(y^{1}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{0})+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\left(\|y^{k}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}-\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y^{k}_{\rm e}\|^{2}\right).

Hence, with the aid of (
5.7
),
the definition of
\Psi_{k}
,
and
Lemma

3.14
,
similar to the arguments after (
2.5
),
we find

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu_{k}\|y^{k+1}-y^{k}\|^{2}\leq 6\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}+12\mu_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{k}}+6\left(\Psi_{0}(y^{1}_{\rm e},\hat{y}^{0})+\Phi^{\star}\right).

Taking the limit
N\to\infty

while having (
4.4
) in mind,
which also yields
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}<\infty
,
the first inequality in (
5.5
) is obtained,
and the second one trivially follows as the projection is nonexpansive.
Then the convergences
\sqrt{\mu_{k}}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0

and
\sqrt{\mu_{k}}(\hat{y}^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k})\to 0

follow, and boundedness of
\{\mu_{k}\}
yields

\mu_{k}(y^{k+1}-y^{k})\to 0
and
\mu_{k}(\hat{y}^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k})\to 0
.

Let us focus now on the second part and
notice that
\mu
is well defined (since
\{\mu_{k}\}
is bounded and nonincreasing, hence, convergent).
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
the definition of the proximal mapping yields

\mu_{k}(\hat{y}^{k}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e})\in\partial(-\mathpzc{M})(y^{k+1}_{\rm e}).

(5.8)

Let
\{y^{k+1}\}_{k\in K}
be a convergent subsequence with limit
\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Then
y^{k+1}\to_{K}\tilde{y}
, (
5.7
), and
\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}\to 0

yield
y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\to_{K}\tilde{y}
.
Furthermore,
y^{k+1}\to_{K}\tilde{y}

also implies
\hat{y}^{k+1}\to_{K}\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\tilde{y})
by continuity of the projection
(which follows from convexity of
Y_{\textup{sg}}
).
Hence, we find
\mu_{k}\hat{y}^{k+1}\to_{K}\mu\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\tilde{y})
and, thus,

\mu_{k}\hat{y}^{k}\to_{K}\mu\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\tilde{y})
from the convergence

\mu_{k}(\hat{y}^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k})\to 0
.
Taking the limit
k\to_{K}\infty
in (
5.8
),
we end up with

\mu(\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\tilde{y})-\tilde{y})\in\partial(-\mathpzc{M})(\tilde{y})
,
see
[
45
, Thm 24.4]
.
This inclusion means, using

[
6
, Cor. 12.30]
again,
that
\tilde{y}
is a stationary point of the convex problem (
Dμ
),
i.e., one of its minimizers.
∎

Next, we distinguish between the cases
where (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer or not.
Let us start to investigate the regular situation.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose that
3.1
holds and (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer.
Let
\{(x^{k},z^{k},y^{k},\hat{y}^{k})\}
be a sequence generated by

Algorithm

5.1

such that (
4.4
) holds.
Furthermore, let
\mu\geq 0
be such that
\mu_{k}\searrow\mu
.
Then
\{y^{k}\}
is bounded
and the following hold:

(i)

If
\mu=0
, then each accumulation point of
\{y^{k}\}

belongs to
Y_{\textup{mul}}
.

(ii)

If
\mu>0
,
then
\{y^{k}\}
converges to a solution of (
Dμ
).

(iii)

If
Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}\neq\emptyset
,
then
\{y^{k}\}
converges to an element in this set.

Proof.

To start, let us verify that
\{y^{k}\}
is bounded.
Noting that
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}\subseteq Y_{\textup{sg}}
,
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
is bounded
by boundedness of
Y_{\textup{sg}}
.
By assumption
Y_{\textup{mul}}
is nonempty, i.e., (
D
) possesses
a solution
\bar{y}
, see
Corollary

3.13
.
Hence,
\bar{y}
is a minimizer of
-\mathpzc{M}
,
and this yields
0\in\partial(-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M})(\bar{y})
, i.e.,

\bar{y}
is a fixed point of
\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then nonexpansiveness of the proximal operator and (
5.7
) guarantee

\|y^{k+1}-\bar{y}\|\leq\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|+\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}(\bar{y})\|\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\hat{y}^{k}-\bar{y}\|,

(5.9)

where
y^{k+1}_{\rm e}
is the exact proximal point defined in (
5.6
).
As the right-hand side remains bounded due to (
4.4
)
as well as boundedness of
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
,

\{y^{k}\}
is indeed bounded.

Assertion
(i)
is trivial
from
Lemma

5.2

as, for
\mu=0
, the minimizers of (
Dμ
) are the
minimizers of
-\mathpzc{M}
, i.e., the multipliers from
Y_{\textup{mul}}
.
Hence, let us proceed with the proof of
assertion
(ii)
.
As
\mu>0
,
Algorithm

5.1
guarantees the existence of
K_{0}\in\mathbb{N}

such that
\mu_{k}=\mu
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
k\geq K_{0}
.
Pick a solution
y_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of (
Dμ
).
We note that its solution set is nonempty as
-\mathpzc{M}
is proper and lsc,
see
Lemmas

3.5
and
3.14
, while
Y_{\textup{sg}}
is nonempty and compact.
Set
\hat{y}_{\mu}:=\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y_{\mu})
.
Then we have
y_{\mu}=\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}_{\mu})
.
Particularly, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq K_{0}
,
it holds

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-y_{\mu}\|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|+\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y_{\mu}\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:distance_to_exact_prox}}{\leq}{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}^{k})-\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}_{\mu})\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\hat{y}^{k}-\hat{y}_{\mu}\|=\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y^{k})-\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y_{\mu})\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|y^{k}-y_{\mu}\|,

where we used
the nonexpansiveness of the proximal operator.
Now, similar arguments as in the proof of
Proposition

2.4

(ultimately applying
Lemma

2.1
)
show that
\{y^{k}\}
converges to a minimizer of (
Dμ
).

For the proof of assertion
(iii)
, we can proceed in similar way.
Obviously, the solutions of (
Dμ
)
correspond to the points in
Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}

in this situation, and this set is nonempty by assumption.
Picking
y_{\mu}\in Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}
, we know, in particular, that
y_{\mu}
is a minimizer of
-\mathpzc{M}
and, thus, a fixed point
of
\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
and
\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y_{\mu})=y_{\mu}
holds as well.
Hence, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we find the estimates

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-y_{\mu}\|\leq{}

\displaystyle\|y^{k+1}-y^{k+1}_{\rm e}\|+\|y^{k+1}_{\rm e}-y_{\mu}\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:distance_to_exact_prox}}{\leq}{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}(\hat{y}^{k})-\prox_{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\mu_{k}}}\mathpzc{M}}(y_{\mu})\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\hat{y}^{k}-y_{\mu}\|=\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y^{k})-\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y_{\mu})\|

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{k}}+\|y^{k}-y_{\mu}\|

in similar way as above, and the convergence of
\{y^{k}\}
to
a point in
Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}
follows as in the
proof of
Proposition

2.4
(via
Lemma

2.1
).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Let us note that
in
Theorem

5.3

(i)

it remains unclear whether the whole sequence
\{y^{k}\}
converges.
Observe that the proof strategy used to verify assertions
(ii)

and
(iii)
cannot be applied
as we only obtain inequality (
5.9
)
for each
\bar{y}\in Y_{\textup{mul}}

without a chance to estimate
\|\hat{y}^{k}-\bar{y}\|

from above by
\|y^{k}-\bar{y}\|
as soon as
\bar{y}\notin Y_{\textup{sg}}

(the case
\bar{y}\in Y_{\textup{sg}}
is covered by assertion
(iii)
).
Thus,
Lemma

2.1
does not apply
as we are not in a position to prove the convergence of

.

Let us proceed with a statement addressing the primal iterates of
Algorithm

5.1
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Suppose that
3.1
holds and (
P
) possesses a stationary minimizer.
Let
\{(x^{k},z^{k},y^{k},\hat{y}^{k})\}
be a sequence generated by

Algorithm

5.1

such that (
4.4
) holds.
Suppose that either
\mu_{k}\downarrow 0
holds or that
Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}\neq\emptyset
is satisfied.
Then we have
c(x^{k})-z^{k}\to 0
and (
4.5
).
Particularly, each accumulation point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\{x^{k}\}
is a minimizer of (
P
).
For each subsequence
\{x^{k}\}_{k\in K}
such that
x^{k}\to_{K}\bar{x}
,
we have
z^{k}\to_{K}c(\bar{x})
and
f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})\to_{K}\Phi^{\star}
.

Proof.

By construction of
Algorithm

5.1
, we have, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, that

c(x^{k})-z^{k}=\mu_{k}(y^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k}).

Let us argue that the right-hand side tends to zero as
k\to\infty
.
If
\mu_{k}\downarrow 0
, this is trivial from the boundedness
of
\{y^{k}\}
and
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
, see
Theorem

5.3
.
If
Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}\neq\emptyset
,
Theorem

5.3

yields the existence of
\bar{y}\in Y_{\textup{mul}}\cap Y_{\textup{sg}}
such that
y^{k}\to\bar{y}
,
and continuity of the projection guarantees
\hat{y}^{k}\to\bar{y}
due to
\proj_{Y_{\textup{sg}}}(\bar{y})=\bar{y}
.
Hence, we have
y^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k}\to 0
, and the boundedness of
\{\mu_{k}\}
also yields

\mu_{k}(y^{k+1}-\hat{y}^{k})\to 0
.
Hence, we have shown that
c(x^{k})-z^{k}\to 0
.

Based on (
5.1
),
we find

\displaystyle f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})+\langle\hat{y}^{k},c(x^{k})-z^{k}\rangle\leq{}

\displaystyle f(x^{k})+g(z^{k})+\langle\hat{y}^{k},c(x^{k})-z^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\mu_{k}}\|c(x^{k})-z^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(x^{k},\hat{y}^{k})\leq\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{k}}(\bar{x},\hat{y}^{k})+\varepsilon_{k}\leq\Phi^{\star}+\varepsilon_{k},

where the last inequality follows again from
[
25
, Lem. 3.2]
.
Taking the upper limit
k\to\infty
while keeping the boundedness of
\{\hat{y}^{k}\}
and the convergences

c(x^{k})-z^{k}\to 0
and
\varepsilon_{k}\searrow 0
in mind, (
4.5
) follows.
The final statements about accumulation points can be shown similarly as in
Theorem

4.2
.
∎

Based on
Corollary

5.5
,
one may ask about primal convergence guarantees of
Algorithm

5.1
in situations where
the penalty parameter does not tend to zero.
For instance, this happens in
1
.
As the following simple example illustrates,

Algorithm

5.1
may not possess any meaningful
primal convergence behavior in this situation.

Example 5.6

(Safeguarded ALM with constant penalty)
.

Consider (
P
) with
g:=\indicator_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}

as well as
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
c\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

f(x):=x,\qquad c(x):=x.

The feasible set of this problem is
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and its solution is
\bar{x}:=0
.
We note that the latter is also stationary with unique multiplier
\bar{y}:=-1
.

In
Algorithm

5.1
, we choose
y^{0}:=0
,
\mu_{0}:=\mu
for some
\mu>0
, and
Y_{\textup{sg}}:=\{0\}
.
Furthermore, we do not update the penalty parameter, i.e.,
we implement
Algorithm

5.1
in
1
.
Finally,
\varepsilon_{k}:=0
is used for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then, in each iteration
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
\hat{y}^{k}=0
and
\mu_{k}=\mu

6
ALM with elastic safeguarding

In
Section

5
,
we observed that the safeguarded ALM with prescribed safeguard from
Algorithm

5.1

demands a possibly varying penalty parameter to provide some convergence guarantees,
unless the safeguarding set
Y_{\textup{sg}}
is “large enough” to cover a multiplier from
Y_{\textup{mul}}
(in case of existence).
In order to avoid a deliberate choice of
Y_{\textup{sg}}
,
since
Y_{\textup{mul}}
is usually unknown a priori,
and
to make the AL scheme more adaptive, we now consider

Algorithm

6.1
,
where the dual safeguarding set is allowed to grow.
We say “elastic” as opposed to the “rigid” strategy with prefixed set
Y_{\textup{sg}}
.
In fact, what really matters in the global convergence analysis
[
9
,
25
]

is that the following condition remains guaranteed:

\mu_{k}\hat{y}^{k}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad\mu_{k}\downarrow 0.

Then we can also have an elastic dual safeguarding set
\rho_{k}Y_{\textup{sg}}
, which grows with scaling factor
\rho_{k}>0
,
as long as
\mu_{k}\rho_{k}\downarrow 0
whenever
\mu_{k}\downarrow 0
.
This is precisely what happens in
Algorithm

6.1
.

Data:

y^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
;
\mu_{0}>0
; nonempty, convex, compact set
Y_{\textup{sg}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
;

\theta,\eta\in(0,1)
;
\beta\in(\eta^{2},\eta)

Set
\rho_{0}:=1
and
V^{-1}:=\infty
.

1

for

k=0,1,2\ldots

do

2

Set
\hat{y}^{k}:=\proj_{\rho_{k}Y_{\textup{sg}}}(y^{k})
.

3

Select
\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0
and compute

such that (
5.1
) holds.

4

Set

z^{k}:=\prox_{\mu_{k}g}(c(x^{k})+\mu_{k}\hat{y}^{k})
and
V^{k}:=\|c(x^{k})-z^{k}\|_{\infty}
.

5

Set

y^{k+1}:=\hat{y}^{k}+\mu_{k}^{-1}[c(x^{k})-z^{k}]
.

6

if

V^{k}\leq\theta V^{k-1}

then

7

Set
\mu_{k+1}:=\mu_{k}
and
\rho_{k+1}:=\rho_{k}
.
else

8

Set
\mu_{k+1}:=\beta\mu_{k}
and
\rho_{k+1}:=\nicefrac{{\eta}}{{\beta}}\,\rho_{k}
.

Algorithm 6.1

Safeguarded ALM for (
P
) with elastic safeguard.

Observe that the (inverse) stepsizes

are merely nonincreasing in the standard ALM from

whereas we adopt the particular update rule from

The particular update at

In fact, by selecting

(6.1)

The elastic safeguard mechanism allows us to unite the advantages of both the classical ALM
(with strong guarantees in the convex setting) and the rigidly safeguarded ALM

as revealed by the convergence analysis in

Although beyond the scope of this work,
we note that global convergence results on general composite optimization problems

[

8
Final remarks and open questions

In this paper, we illustrated that the correspondence between ALM and PPA,
well known for inequality-constrained convex problems,
extends to fully convex composite optimization problems.
The latter has been used to conduct a global convergence analysis for
the classical ALM, a rigidly safeguarded ALM, and a novel elastically safeguarded ALM
both in the regular setting, where minimizers are assumed to be stationary,
and in the irregular situation, where all minimizers are nonstationary.
The ALM with elastic safeguard turned out to consolidate the advantages of both the classical and rigidly
safeguarded version while effectively extenuating their respective disadvantages.
It might be interesting to explore whether similar results can be obtained
for (safeguarded) proximal ALMs, see
[
54
]
.

In our future research, we aim to illustrate the efficiency of elastic safeguarding
for general composite optimization problems. As mentioned in
Section

6
,
global convergence properties of associated ALMs should be easy to obtain simply
by paralleling results and proofs available for rigidly safeguarded ALMs.
However, we claim that elastic safeguarding may also be beneficial for a local analysis
(guaranteeing local fast convergence to a primal-dual pair).
Here, for ALMs with rigid safeguard, one typically has to assume (or ensure by comparatively
hard assumptions) that the safeguarding becomes unnecessary for large enough iterations,
i.e., that the dual variable at the limit is in the safeguarding set,
see, e.g.,
[
25
, § 4.3]
and
[
57
, § 4.3]
.
In contrast, it is likely that such an assumption is not required
when elastic safeguarding is used.

Here, we are going to present a proof of

.

of
\varphi

Observe that

For each

,

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi_{k}(y_{k})\geq\varphi(y)

to

\varphi_{k}\xrightarrow{\text{e}}\varphi
.
In the case where

is a family of functions such that

for each

,
we say that

\varphi_{k}:=\varphi_{t_{k}}

to denote this property.

.

as

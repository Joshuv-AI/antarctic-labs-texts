Predictive topological modeling of the structure–property relationships in naturally occurring anti-cancer chalcones
Authors: Abdullah Abdul Razzaq Obaid, Abdul Hakeem, Asad Ullah, Shahid Zaman, Hijaz Ahmad, Parvez Ali, Melaku Berhe Belay
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, a comprehensive predictive topological modeling framework was employed to investigate the quantitative structure–property relationships (QSPR) of naturally occurring anticancer chalcones. A series of degree-based topological indices, including the first and second Zagreb indices, first and second redefined Zagreb indices, sum-connectivity index, symmetric division index, Randić index, and harmonic index, were computed to represent key molecular structural features. Statistical and regression analyses were performed to correlate these indices with experimentally reported physicochemical properties of chalcones. The study focuses on eight naturally derived anticancer Chalcones, namely, Isoliquiritigenin, Butein, Cardamonin, Sappanchalcone, Licochalcone A, Millepachine, Xanthohumol, and Curcumin—selected based on their structural diversity and well-documented pharmacological potential. Correlations between the computed indices and several physicochemical parameters, such as molecular complexity, molecular weight, molar refractivity, and polarizability, were examined. The study also includes a comprehensive statistical evaluation of the regression models to assess the predictive accuracy, robustness, and reliability of the proposed descriptors.Keywords: Graph theory, Anti-cancer drugs, QSPR analysis, Regression modelSubject terms: Chemistry, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide, posing a major challenge to global public health. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), cancer accounted for nearly 10 million deaths in 2022, with breast, lung, colorectal, and prostate cancers being the most prevalent types. The rising incidence and mortality rates highlight the urgent need for discovering and developing effective, low-toxicity anti-cancer agents. In recent years, naturally occurring compounds have gained significant attention due to their structural diversity and therapeutic potential. Among these, Chalcones aromatic ketones belonging to the flavonoid family have emerged as promising candidates because of their wide range of biological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-cancer effects.

Cancer involves uncontrolled cell growth; anti-cancer treatments include surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and hormonal therapy.These treatments aim to destroy cancer cells, stop their growth, or prevent them from spreading to other body parts. Some anti-cancer drugs, isoliquiritigenin, butein, cardamonin, Sappanchalcone, licochalcone A, millepachine, xanthohumol,and Curcumin, are all-natural compounds studied for their potential anti-cancer properties. The combination of mathematics within cheminformatics promotes effective examination and analysis of chemical information, so driving progress in drug development and molecular investigation2–8. Quantitative Structure–Property Relationship (QSPR) analysis establishes mathematical models linking molecular structure with physicochemical or biological properties. By using topological indices and computational descriptors, QSPR and QSAR help predict compound behavior, optimize drug design, and reduce experimental efforts. It provides an efficient, reliable approach for understanding and forecasting molecular activity and functionality9–16. Chemical graph theory is vital in medicine design, computational chemistry, and materials science, aiding in the discovery and comprehension of novel chemical compounds3,17–38. These indices maintenance structure–property relationship studies, medicine development, and the estimate of molecules’ biological efficacy and physical characteristics14,39–49. Harry Wiener first introduced the idea of topological indices in 194750. Investigations are conducted on topological indices related to distance, resistance, and eccentricity51–60. Some of the topological indices examined in this paper include the first and second Zagreb indices (M1 and M2) indices, hyper (H) index, Forgotten (F) index, first and second Redefined (ReZG1, ReZG2) index, symmetric division (SSD) index, harmonic (H) index, (ABC) index, These indices yield numerical values derived from the molecular structures of the drugs61.

The objective of this study is to develop a predictive QSPR framework using degree-based topological indices to correlate the molecular structures of naturally occurring anticancer chalcones with their physicochemical properties. The study aims to assess the accuracy, robustness, and reliability of these indices in modeling and predicting key molecular features relevant to their pharmacological potential.

Materials and methods

This study employs simple graphs to represent the molecular structures of naturally occurring anti-cancer chalcones. Each molecule is modeled as a simple connected graph, where atoms correspond to vertices and chemical bonds correspond to edges. The topological features of these molecular graphs were analyzed to derive various topological indices that quantitatively describe molecular structure. Using computational methods, vertex partitioning and edge partitioning techniques were applied to compute topological indices such as first and second Zagreb indices, first and second redefined Zagreb indices, Sum connectivity index, symmetric division indices, Randic indices, Harmonic indices. The molecular structures of selected chalcones were obtained from the PubChem. The eight chalcone derivatives were selected based on their well-documented anticancer activity in literature, structural diversity across the chalcone scaffold, availability of reliable molecular and physicochemical data for modeling, and their pharmacological relevance as representative natural anticancer agents. These optimized structures were then converted into graph-theoretical representations for index computation. All numerical calculations were performed using MS Excel software to ensure accuracy and consistency. The computed indices were statistically correlated with the known anti-cancer activities of the compounds through regression analysis in MS Excel, enabling the development of predictive structure–property models. Cross-validation and statistical parameters such as the correlation coefficient (R2), and standard error (SE) were used to evaluate the robustness, accuracy, and predictive performance of the developed models.

Results and discussion

Drugs made from naturally occurring anti-cancer chalcones are modeled using degree-based topological indices. These indices and the compounds’ physicochemical characteristics are strongly correlated, according to QSPR research.

The naturally found chalcone-based drugs that have anti-cancer properties exhibit five significant physicochemical characteristics: molecular weight, complexity, molar refractivity, polarizability, and surface tension. These properties are evaluated for each of the selected compounds. Conclusions

A comprehensive predictive topological modeling framework was employed to investigate the quantitative structure–property relationships (QSPR) of naturally occurring anticancer chalcones. A series of degree-based topological indices, including the first and second Zagreb indices, first and second redefined Zagreb indices, sum-connectivity index, symmetric division index, Randić index, and harmonic index, were computed to represent key molecular structural features. Statistical and regression analyses were performed to correlate these indices with experimentally reported physicochemical properties of chalcones. The application of these degree-based topological indices combined with statistical parameters in linear QSPR models has revealed strong correlations between molecular structure and various physicochemical properties of naturally occurring anticancer chalcones. These results underscore the predictive capability of topological indices and their potential utility in quantitative molecular design. The significant correlation coefficients obtained demonstrate the reliability of these descriptors in capturing structural features that influence physicochemical behavior.

Overall, this study establishes a robust and computationally efficient framework for predicting key molecular properties of bioactive compounds. The insights gained can assist pharmaceutical researchers in rational drug design, facilitating the development and optimization of novel anticancer agents derived from natural chalcone scaffolds.

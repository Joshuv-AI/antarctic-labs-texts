Anomalous thermoelectric and thermal Hall effects in irradiated altermagnets
Fang Qin, Xiao-Bin Qiang

Abstract
In this study, we show that a d d -wave altermagnet can be transformed into a Chern insulator by irradiating it with elliptically polarized light from a high-frequency photon beam. We further explore the intrinsic anomalous thermoelectric and thermal Hall effects in light-irradiated altermagnets. At extremely low temperatures, the thermoelectric Hall coefficient, which exhibits a linear temperature dependence for the thermoelectric Hall conductivity, vanishes within the gapped region between the conduction and valence bands. However, it displays peaks and dips at the boundaries of the gap, suggesting that thermoelectric Hall conductivity can be used to probe the bandwidth. Similarly, the low-temperature thermal Hall coefficient, which also shows a linear temperature dependence for the thermal Hall conductivity, becomes quantized in the gapped region between the conduction and valence bands. This quantization indicates that thermal Hall conductivity can serve as a probe for the topological properties of the system.

In this work, we demonstrate that a
d

\Gamma

M

Upon exposure to circularly polarized light, these Dirac points acquire finite gaps through Floquet-induced symmetry breaking, generating a nonzero Berry curvature in momentum space. Consequently, intrinsic electric, thermoelectric, and thermal Hall currents are simultaneously induced under applied electric fields or thermal gradients.
Beyond the topological phase transition, we further investigate the intrinsic anomalous thermoelectric and thermal Hall effects in the light-driven altermagnetic phase. At extremely low temperatures, the thermoelectric Hall coefficient exhibits a linear temperature dependence in the thermoelectric Hall conductivity and vanishes inside the bulk energy gap between the conduction and valence bands. Particularly, pronounced peaks and dips emerge near the band edges, indicating that the thermoelectric Hall response serves as a sensitive probe of the bandwidth. In parallel, the low-temperature thermal Hall coefficient also displays a linear temperature dependence, while the thermal Hall conductivity becomes quantized within the gapped region. This quantization underscores the potential of thermal Hall transport as a robust probe of the system’s underlying topological character

VII
Summary

This work investigates how elliptically polarized light breaks
C_{4}T
symmetry and induces gaps at both the

\Gamma
and
M
points in the spectrum of a
d
-wave altermagnet. The gap around the
\Gamma
point gives rise to Chern bands with Chern number
|C|\!=\!\frac{1}{2}
, while the gap around the
M
point contributes another Chern number
|C|\!=\!\frac{1}{2}
, leading to a total Chern number of
|C|\!=\!1
. This indicates that the
d
-wave altermagnet can be transformed into a Chern insulator under light irradiation with a high-frequency photon beam.

Particularly, there exists a critical value of the light amplitude at which a topological phase transition occurs. As the light amplitude increases to this critical point, the gap at the
\Gamma
point remains open, but the gap at the
M
point closes. Beyond the critical value, the gap at the
M
point reopens, signifying a band inversion at the critical point.

At extremely low temperatures, the thermoelectric Hall coefficient, which shows a linear temperature dependence for the thermoelectric Hall conductivity, vanishes within the gapped region between the conduction and valence bands. However, it exhibits peaks and dips at the edges of the gap regions, suggesting that thermoelectric Hall conductivity can be used to probe both the bandwidth and the gapped region around the
\Gamma
point.

Similarly, the low-temperature thermal Hall coefficient, which shows a linear temperature dependence for the thermal Hall conductivity, becomes quantized in the gapped region between the conduction and valence bands. This observation suggests that thermal Hall conductivity can serve as a probe for the topological properties of the system.

Abstract
. An A ∞ A_{\infty} -weight on a Lipschitz curve Λ \Lambda in the plane can be extended analytically to the graph Lipschitz domain Ω \Omega above it. This problem was studied by C. Kenig [ Ken80 ] , who introduced the class A ​ E AE of well-behaved analytic extensions. Later, he and D. Jerison [ JK82 ] added a Smirnov-type condition to the definition of this class. In this note, we show that this Smirnov-type condition is equivalent to an H 1 H^{1} -integrability condition. As a consequence, one of the conditions in the definition of A ​ E AE can be dropped. We use this simplification to apply C. Kenig’s theory to prove results about weighted Hardy spaces. These are useful to study the Neumann problem in Ω \Omega with boundary data in weighted spaces.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a graph Lipschitz domain and let
\nu
be a measure over
\Lambda
, the boundary of
\Omega
. The class
AE(\Omega,\nu)
, where
AE
stands for
analytic extensions
, is a beautiful construction that allows us to deal with the following problem. In weighted
L^{p}
spaces, the weight can be moved from the measure to the function easily in the following sense: if
v
and
w
are weights, then

\|fw\|_{L^{p}(v)}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(vw^{p})}.

However, the same cannot be done with such ease for Hardy spaces, since the definition of the
H^{p}(\Omega,d\nu)
-norm

\|F\|_{H^{p}(\Omega,d\nu)}\simeq_{\alpha}\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(F)\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,d\nu)}

in terms of the non-tangential maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
involves a supremum. Therefore, given
\nu
and
\mu
two measures on
\Lambda
absolutely continuous with respect to arc-length measure
ds
, and whose density functions are denoted
by
\nu
and
\mu
as well, it is not clear if the following chain of equivalences holds:

(1.1)

\|F\|_{H^{p}(\Omega,\nu^{-p}\mu ds)}\simeq_{\alpha}\left\|\frac{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(F)}{\nu}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,d\mu)}\overset{(\text{?})}{\simeq}\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{F}{\nu}\right)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,d\mu)}\simeq_{\alpha}\left\|\frac{F}{\nu}\right\|_{H^{p}(\Omega,d\mu)}.

In order for the above to even make sense, we would need
\frac{F}{\nu}
to be an analytic function on
\Omega
. But
\nu
is a function defined on
\Lambda
. Therefore, to begin with, we would need to obtain some kind of
analytic extension
of the weight
\nu
, namely
G
, from the boundary
\Lambda=\partial\Omega
to the whole graph Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, such that the modulus of the non-tangential limit of
G
is
\nu
.

As far as we know, the class
AE
was introduced in Kenig’s PhD thesis
[
Ken79
]
and
[
Ken80
]
. He proved that, when the measures are in
A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
, this kind of analytic extensions exist, and are well-behaved in some sense that we will explore below. Later, Jerison and Kenig himself modified the definition in
[
JK82
]
, adding a Smirnov-type property (Definition
3.5
).

To the best of our knowledge, this class has not been used until its recent appearance in
[
CNOC23
]
, where Carro, Naibo and Ortiz-Caraballo use it to manipulate the norms of certain maximal operators that arise when solving the Neumann problem in graph Lipschitz domains in the plane with arc-length measure on the boundary. The motivation behind this note has been to understand better the class
AE
in order to deal in
[
BYC25
]
with the Neumann problem with boundary measures more general than arc-length measure and extend some results in
[
CNOC23
]
.

The structure of the article is the following. First, we reformulate
some ideas from
[
HMW73
]
. Then, we motivate the definition of the class
AE
with a basic example. In view of its behaviour, we state and prove a general equivalence between an
H^{1}
-condition and a Smirnov-type condition in Theorem
4.3
. Based on this, we re-define the class
AE
and explain how, with the Smirnov-type property added in
[
JK82
]
, the
H^{1}
-integrability property in the original definition of
[
Ken80
]
is no longer necessary (see Remarks
4.5
and
4.11
below). Finally, in Corollary
4.15
we generalize Lemma 4.4 of
[
CNOC23
]
as an easy consequence of the theory developed in the previous sections. This generalization will be used in
[
BYC25
]
to deal with the
L^{p}
and
H_{at}^{1}
-Neumann problem with
A_{\infty}
-measures on the boundary.

We would like to emphasize that certain ideas already appear in
[
HMW73
]
,
[
Ken80
]
and
[
JK82
]
. However, we use them in a way that allows us to simplify the theory and obtain specific applications of it in a direct manner.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

Let
\Lambda
be a curve in the complex plane given parametrically by
\eta(x)=x+i\gamma(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\gamma
is a real-valued Lipschitz function, and consider the graph Lipschitz domain above
\Lambda
,

(2.1)

\Omega=\left\{z_{1}+iz_{2}\in\mathbb{C}:z_{2}>\gamma(z_{1})\right\}.

Note that
\partial\Omega=\Lambda
. Let
L
denote the Lipschitz constant of
\gamma
.

We denote by
ds
arc-length measure on
\Lambda
, defined as

ds(E)\coloneqq\int_{\eta^{-1}(E)}|\eta^{\prime}(t)|\,dt=\int_{\eta^{-1}(E)}\sqrt{1+|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|^{2}}\,dt,\quad E\subset\Lambda.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
F
a complex-valued function defined in
\Omega
and
\alpha\in(0,\arctan{\frac{1}{L}})
, define the
non-tangential maximal operator

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
of
F
as

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(F)(\xi)=\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)}|F(z)|,\quad\xi\in\Lambda,

where

\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)=\left\{z_{1}+iz_{2}\in\mathbb{C}:z_{2}>\operatorname{Im}(\xi)\text{ and }|\operatorname{Re}(\xi)-z_{1}|<\tan(\alpha)|z_{2}-\operatorname{Im}(\xi)|\right\}.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a complex-valued function
F
defined on
\Omega
, we say that
F(z)
converges non-tangentially when
z\to\xi\in\partial\Omega
if the following limit exists

\lim_{z\triangleright\xi}F(z)\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to\xi\\
z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)\end{subarray}}F(z),

for some
\alpha\in(0,\arctan{\frac{1}{L}})
. In that case, we call this limit the non-tangential limit of
F
when
z\to\xi
.

2.1.
Conformal mapping

Since
\Omega
is simply connected, it is conformally equivalent to the upper half plane

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\coloneqq\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x\in\mathbb{R},y>0\}.

Let
z_{0}=ix_{0}
with
x_{0}>\gamma(0)
. Let
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\rightarrow\Omega
be the conformal mapping such that
\Phi(\infty)=\infty
, and
\Phi(i)=z_{0}
. Let
\Phi^{-1}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
be its inverse. We have the following boundary behaviour.

Theorem 2.3

(Thm. 1.1 in
[
Ken80
]
)
.

\Phi
extends to
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}
as a homeomorphism onto
\overline{\Omega}
. Besides,
\Phi^{\prime}
has a non-tangential limit
dx
-a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
, and this limit is different from 0
dx
-a.e. on
\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,
\Phi(x)
, with

, is absolutely continuous when restricted to any finite interval, and hence
\Phi^{\prime}(x)
exists
dx
-a.e. and is locally integrable. Moreover, this derivative coincides with the non-tangential limit of the derivative of the conformal map
\Phi
, i.e.,
\Phi^{\prime}(x)=\lim_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\ni z\triangleright x}\Phi^{\prime}(z)

dx
-a.e.

We abuse notation and denote by
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
the mappings in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, the extensions to
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}
and the restrictions to
\mathbb{R}\equiv\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

Weights

Muc72

\Lambda

\mathbb{R}

We define an

Let

be a measure over

\Phi(\nu)(E)\coloneqq\nu(\Phi(E)),\quad E\subset\mathbb{R}.

(Lemma 1.16 in

)
.

With the above notation,

(2.3)

2.3.
Hardy spaces over graph Lipschitz domains

Hardy spaces are useful to manipulate the
L^{p}
-norms of the non-tangential maximal operators
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
defined above.

Definition 2.6

(Definition 2.7 in
[
Ken80
]
)
.

Let
\nu
be a measure on
\Lambda
and let
\alpha\in(0,\arctan{\frac{1}{L}})
. Define the Hardy space

H^{p}(\Omega,\nu)\coloneqq\left\{h:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}:h\text{ is analytic in }\Omega\text{ and }\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(h)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)}<\infty\right\}

and set
\|h\|_{H^{p}(\Omega,\nu)}\coloneqq\left\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(h)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Lambda,\nu)}
.

The definition of
H^{p}(\Omega,\nu)
is independent of
\alpha
. Different values of
\alpha
give rise to equivalent norms. There is a characterization of this space in terms of
L^{p}
-norms over curves.

Theorem 2.7

(Theorem 2.13 in
[
Ken80
]
)
.

Let
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
and
p\in(0,\infty)
. Then,
F\in H^{p}(\Omega,\nu)
if and only if

\sup_{h>0}\int_{\Lambda}|F(\xi+ih)|^{p}\,d\nu(\xi)<\infty.

In particular, when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, for
w\in A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
p\in(0,\infty)
we have

F\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},w)\iff\sup_{y>0}\|F(\cdot+iy)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w)}<\infty.

2.4.
Poisson integrals and
A_{\infty}
-weights

Definition 2.8
.

We define the Poisson kernel at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
as

P_{y}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

Given a function
f
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty
, we define the Poisson integral of
f
at
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
as

(P_{y}\ast f)(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}f(t)\,dt.

One result that will turn useful for us is the following (see page 62 of
[
Gar07
]
).

Theorem 2.9
.

Given
p\in(0,\infty]
, if
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx)
and
f(z)\neq 0
for every
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, then

\log{|f(z)|}\leq P_{y}\ast\log{|f|}(x),\quad\forall z=x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

where the function
f
on the right-hand side denotes the non-tangential limits of
f(z)
.

One class of functions
f
that satisfy
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|f(x)|}{1+|x|^{2}}\,dx<\infty
is
BMO
(see pages 141-142 of
[
FS72
]
). Our source of
BMO
functions will be logarithms of
A_{\infty}
-weights.

Lemma 2.10

(Corollary IV.2.19 in
[
GCRdF85
]
)
.

If
w\in A_{\infty}
, then
\log{w}\in BMO
.
As a consequence, given
w_{1},w_{2}\in A_{\infty}
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\log(w_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}w_{2}^{\alpha_{2}})\in BMO
.

Finally, we write
A\lesssim B
when there exists a constant
C>0
such that
A\leq C\cdot B
. If we want to make explicit the dependence of
C
on some parameter
\alpha
, we may write
A\lesssim_{\alpha}B
. We write
A\simeq B
to denote

and

.

3.
Hunt-Muckenhoupt-Wheeden’s argument

The article
[
HMW73
]
was a breakthrough in the theory of weights. It characterized the weights
w
for which the Hilbert transform is bounded on
L^{p}(w)
. In order to do so, several new properties and techniques regarding
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
-weights were introduced. These have been used and extended in
[
Ken80
]
and
[
CNOC23
]
in order to deal with boundary value problems in graph Lipschitz domains.

In particular, we are interested in an argument handled in pages 248-249 of
[
HMW73
]
. Kenig refers to it repeatedly in
[
Ken80
]
to prove estimates of the form
\frac{F(z)}{(i+z)^{m}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx)
for some
m\geq 0
and some analytic function
F
. Since it is essential for the class
AE
, we have tried to express the argument in a closed statement as general as possible, so that we can refer to it when needed.

To begin with, we state some basic results. From now on, we denote
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
by
A_{p}
, for
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
w\in A_{p}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
, then

\frac{w(x)}{(1+|x|)^{p}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},dx).

Proof.

Apply the first item of Lemma 2.3 (a) in
[
CNOC23
]

with
t=1
,
x_{0}=0
, to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{w(x)}{(1+|x|)^{p}}\,dx\lesssim\frac{1}{1^{p}}\int_{|x|<1}w(x)\,dx<\infty.\qed

Corollary 3.2
.

The Poisson integral of an
A_{2}
weight is well-defined in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
W\in A_{2}
, then

(P_{y}\ast W)(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\int_{|x-t|<y}W(t)\,dt.

Proof.

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{y}{(x-t)^{2}+y^{2}}W(t)\,dt\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\int_{|x-t|<y}W(t)\,dt+y\int_{|x-t|>y}\frac{W(t)}{|x-t|^{2}}\,dt\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\int_{|x-t|<y}W(t)\,dt,

having applied in the last inequality Lemma 1 in
[
HMW73
]
with
p=2
and
I=[x-y,x+y]
, so that
|I|=2y
and
a_{I}=x
.
∎

The attempt to formulate the argument given in pages 248-249 of
[
HMW73
]
is the following. It will play a fundamental role later.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
W
be a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
such that its modulus can be majorized by the Poisson integral of an
A_{2}
weight; i.e., there exists
\bm{W}\in A_{2}
such that

(3.1)

|W(x+iy)|\lesssim C(P_{y}\ast\bm{W})(x),\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Let
F
be a bounded holomorphic function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
such that
|F(z)|=O(|z|^{-1})
when
|z|\to\infty
.

Then

F^{2}\cdot W\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx).

Proof.

The assumption (
3.1
) together with Lemma
3.3
give us

(3.2)

|W(x+iy)|\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\int_{|x-t|<y}\bm{W}(t)\,dt.

Fix
y>0
. Using that
F
is bounded with
F(z)=O(|z|^{-1})
, inequality (
3.2
) and Tonelli’s theorem, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|F(x+iy)|^{2}|W(x+iy)|\,dx\lesssim\frac{1}{y}\int_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\bm{W}(t)\left(\int_{x:|x-t|<y}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,dx\right)\,dt.

If
|x-t|<y
, then

t^{2}=(x+(t-x))^{2}\leq 2(x^{2}+(x-t)^{2})\leq 2(x^{2}+y^{2}),

so

\int_{x:|x-t|<y}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}\,dx\lesssim\int_{x:|x-t|<y}\frac{1}{1+t^{2}}\,dx\simeq\frac{2y}{1+t^{2}}.

Therefore, the above expression can be bounded by
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\bm{W}(t)}{1+t^{2}}\,dt
, which is finite by Lemma
3.1
. So
F^{2}\cdot W
is uniformly integrable on horizontal lines, i.e.,
F^{2}\cdot W\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx)
.∎

We will apply Theorem
3.4
several times in the following particular case, which employs the notion of Smirnov-type condition.

Definition 3.5

(Smirnov-type condition)
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function that does not vanish anywhere in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
. We say that
F
is of
Smirnov-type
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, or satisfies a
Smirnov-type condition
, if
\log{|F(z)|}
has non-tangential limits
dx
-a.e., which we denote by
\log{|\tilde{F}|}(x)
, and

\log{|F(z)|}=(P_{y}\ast\log{|\widetilde{F}|})(x),\quad\forall z=x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Notice that if
|F|
has non-tangential limits
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{\infty}
, then
\log{|F|}
has non-tangential limits
\log{|\tilde{F}|}\in BMO
, and therefore the Poisson integral on the right-hand side is well-defined.

The next result is the first one pointing towards a relation between Smirnov-type and
H^{1}
-integrability when the non-tangential limit is in
A_{\infty}
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
W
be a holomorphic function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
that does not vanish. Assume that
|W|
has non-tangential limits
\bm{W}(x)
a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
with
\bm{W}\in A_{2}
, and that
W
satisfies a Smirnov-type condition.

Let
F
be a bounded holomorphic function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
such that
|F(z)|=O(|z|^{-1})
when
z\to\infty
.

Then

F^{2}\cdot W\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx).

Proof.

Just observe that the Smirnov-type property implies the bound (
3.1
). Indeed, if
\log{|W(z)|}=(P_{y}\ast\log{|\bm{W}|})(x)
, then taking exponentials and applying Jensen’s inequality we obtain

|W(z)|=e^{(P_{y}\ast\log{|\bm{W}|})(x)}\leq(P_{y}\ast e^{\log{|\bm{W}|}})(x)=(P_{y}\ast|\bm{W}|)(x),

with
|\bm{W}|\in A_{2}
by hypothesis. ∎

4.
The class AE of analytic extensions

In this section, we investigate the class
AE
. But first, for clarity purposes, we recall some known results for the derivative
\Phi^{\prime}
of the conformal map
\Phi
. These results and their proofs will serve us as a model for the more general theory.

4.1.
The basic example
\Phi^{\prime}

The basic example that we want to mimic is
\Phi^{\prime}(z)
,
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, seen as an analytic extension of the measure
|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|\,dx
on
\mathbb{R}=\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

We recall the following fact, and give the proof of the third item, which is not explicit in
[
Ken80
]
and will be meaningful for us. Since it makes use of the proof of the second item, we give it as well.

Theorem 4.1

(Thm. 1.10 in
[
Ken80
]
)
.

With the above definitions and notation:

(1)

\left|\arg\Phi^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq\arctan{L}<\frac{\pi}{2}
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.

(2)

The locally integrable function
|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|
on
\mathbb{R}
is an
A_{2}
-weight:
|\Phi^{\prime}|\in A_{2}
.

(3)

For any
\varepsilon>0

(4.1)

\frac{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}{(i+\varepsilon z)^{2}}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{ and }\hskip 5.69046pt\frac{[\Phi^{\prime}(z)]^{-1}}{(i+\varepsilon z)^{2}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx).

Proof.

Proof of the second item.
The function
\Phi^{\prime}(z)
is analytic in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and does not vanish. Besides, by the previous item, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
|\arg{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon
. Therefore,

\log{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}=\log{|\Phi^{\prime}(z)|}+i\arg{\Phi^{\prime}(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},

is also an analytic function in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
.
Taking non-tangential limits,

\lim_{z\triangleright x}\log{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}=\log{|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|}+i\arg{\Phi^{\prime}(x)}\eqqcolon g_{1}(x)+ig_{2}(x),\quad dx\text{-a.e. in }\mathbb{R},

with
\|g_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon
. The fact that
|\arg{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\varepsilon
also implies, by the case
p=\infty
of Theorem II.2.5 of
[
SW71
]
, that

\arg{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}=P_{y}\ast g_{2}(x),\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Therefore,
\log{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}
and
-P_{y}\ast Kg_{2}(x)+iP_{y}\ast g_{2}(x)
are two holomorphic
1
1

1

Here,
K
denotes the Hilbert transform of
L^{\infty}
-functions; see for example page 105 of
[
Gar07
]
or page 135 of
[
Ken80

.
functions with the same imaginary parts. Therefore, by the Cauchy-Riemann equations, the real parts have to be equal up to an additive constant: there exists
C>0
such that

\log{|\Phi^{\prime}(z)|}=-P_{y}\ast Kg_{2}(x)+C,\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Notice that, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}P_{y}(t)\,dt=1
for all

(P_{y}\ast C)(x)=C

\log{\Phi^{\prime}(z)}=P_{y}\ast(-Kg_{2}+C)(x)+i(P_{y}\ast g_{2})(x),\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Taking exponentials on both sides, we obtain

\Phi^{\prime}(z)=e^{P_{y}\ast(-Kg_{2}+C)(x)}e^{i(P_{y}\ast g_{2})(x)},\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Its modulus is

(4.2)

|\Phi^{\prime}(z)|=e^{P_{y}\ast(-Kg_{2}+C)(x)},\quad z=x+iy\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

Taking non-tangential limits, we get

4.2.
Relation between Smirnov-type and
H^{1}
-integrability

The key to prove the third item of Theorem
4.1
has been the Smirnov condition (
4.4
) and the fact that
|\Phi^{\prime}|
and
|\Phi^{\prime}|^{-1}
are
A_{2}
-weights. This leads us to think that the above argument can be generalized to
A_{\infty}
-measures, because if
w\in A_{\infty}
, there exists a negative power of
w
in
A_{\infty}
, and the situation is then analogous. In fact, we obtain not only that Smirnov-type implies
H^{1}
-integrability, but also that the converse is true. First, we state the following simple but very useful fact.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be non-vanishing holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
, of Smirnov-type, and such that
|G_{1}|
and
|G_{2}|
have non-tangential limits in
A_{\infty}
. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
G_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}G_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}
is of Smirnov-type.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a non-vanishing holomorphic function such that
|F|
has non-tangential limit
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{\infty}
.
Then,
F
satisfies a Smirnov-type condition if and only if there exist
m,s\geq 0
and
q>0
such that

(4.5)

\frac{F(z)}{(i+z)^{m}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx),\quad\frac{F(z)^{-q}}{(i+z)^{s}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx).

Proof.

Assume first that
F
satisfies a Smirnov-type condition. We prove the first inclusion of (
4.5
). The ideas and notation follow closely those of Lemma 2.12 in
[
Ken80
]
.

By hypothesis,
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{\infty}
, so there exists
r>1
such that
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{r}
. We distinguish two cases.

Case
1
:

r<2
. In this case
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{r}\subset A_{2}
and
F
is of Smirnov-type. Hence, by Corollary
3.6
,
\frac{F(z)}{(i+z)^{2}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx)
.

Case
2
:

r>2
. The idea is to reduce to the previous case. Let
a\coloneqq\frac{1}{r-1}
. Recall that
w\in A_{r}
if and only if
w^{-\frac{1}{r-1}}\in A_{r^{\prime}}
. Therefore, since
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{r}
, we have
|\tilde{F}|^{-a}\in A_{r^{\prime}}\subset A_{2}
, and hence

|\tilde{F}|^{a}\in A_{2}.

Let

H_{a}(z)\coloneqq F(z)^{a}.

By Lemma
4.2
, this function satisfies a Smirnov-type condition. Besides,
|H_{a}|
converges non-tangentially to
|\tilde{F}|^{a}\in A_{2}
. Therefore, we can apply Corollary
3.6
and obtain

\frac{H_{a}(z)}{(i+z)^{2}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx).

This is equivalent to

\frac{F(z)}{(i+z)^{\frac{2}{a}}}\in H^{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx).

Its modulus has non-tangential limit
\frac{|\tilde{F}|(x)}{|i+x|^{\frac{2}{a}}}
, which is in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, since
|\tilde{F}|\in A_{r}
, by Lemma
3.1
we have
\frac{|\tilde{F}|(x)}{|i+x|^{r}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
\frac{|\tilde{F}|(x)}{|i+x|^{\frac{2}{a}}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, because
\frac{2}{a}=2(r-1)=r+(r-2)>r
.

Since
\frac{F(z)}{(i+z)^{\frac{2}{a}}}\in H^{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx)
with non-tangential limit in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then, by Corollary II.4.3 of
[
Gar07
]
,

\frac{F(z)}{(i+z)^{\frac{2}{a}}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx),

obtaining statement with
m=\frac{2}{a}
.

To see the second inclusion in (
4.5
), notice that
|\widetilde{F}|\in A_{r}
implies
|\widetilde{F}|^{-\frac{1}{r-1}}\in A_{r^{\prime}}
. Taking
q=-\frac{1}{r-1}
and following the same argument as for
F
, but now for
F^{q}
, we obtain the desired conclusion.

Now, we prove the converse. First, notice that

\frac{1}{|i+z|^{m}}\simeq_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|i+\varepsilon z|^{m}},\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

Hence,

\frac{F(z)}{(i+\varepsilon z)^{m}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},dx),\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

So, by Theorem
2.9
,

\log{\frac{|F(z)|}{|i+\varepsilon z|^{m}}}\leq\left(P_{y}\ast\log{\frac{|\tilde{F}(\cdot)|}{|i+\varepsilon(\cdot)|^{m}}}\right)(x),\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

The left-hand side tends to
\log{|F(z)|}
when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, by continuity in
\varepsilon
. The right-hand side tends to
P_{y}\ast\log{|\tilde{F}|}(x)
by the Dominated Convergence Theorem, which can be applied because the integrand in the convolution can be bounded by
P_{y}(x-t)\log{|\tilde{F}(t)|}\in L^{1}
, since
\log{|\tilde{F}(t)|}\in BMO
.
Therefore,

\log{|F(z)|}\leq P_{y}\ast\log{|\tilde{F}|}(x).

Reasoning in the same way with
\frac{F(z)^{-q}}{(i+z)^{m}}
, we obtain

\log{|F(z)|^{-q}}\leq P_{y}\ast\log{|\tilde{F}|^{-q}}(x),

which gives the other inequality
\log{|F(z)|}\geq P_{y}\ast\log{|\tilde{F}|}(x)
.
∎

4.4.
The class AE of analytic extensions in
\Omega

We can also consider analytic extensions in
\Omega

\Lambda
. We begin again with a simple case.

Imagine we want to have an analytic extension of arc-length measure
ds

\frac{ds}{ds}(\xi)\equiv 1

G(z)\equiv 1

\Omega

Also notice that
\Phi(ds)=|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|dx

\Phi^{\prime}(z)=(1\circ\Phi)(z)\cdot\Phi^{\prime}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}.

In view of this example and formula

Definition 4.9
.

Let
\Omega
be a graph Lipschitz domain and let
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\to\Omega
be the usual conformal mapping. Let
\nu
be a measure on
\Lambda
which is absolutely continuous with respect to arc-length measure. We say that
G\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is an analytic extension of
\nu
to
\Omega
, and write
G\in AE(\Omega,\nu)
, if

(4.8)

(G\circ\Phi)\cdot\Phi^{\prime}\in AE(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\Phi(\nu)).

Notice that
AE(\Omega,\nu)\neq\varnothing
if
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
. Indeed, if
\nu\in A_{\infty}(\Lambda)
then
\Phi(\nu)\in A_{\infty}
. Therefore, by Theorem

,
AE(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2},\Phi(\nu))\neq\varnothing
. Take

(H\circ\Phi^{-1})\cdot(\Phi^{-1})^{\prime}\in AE(\Omega,\nu)
.

It is possible to define a class of analytic extensions that relates two measures

Let

such that

•

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
and different from

•

h
has non-tangential limit
\widetilde{h}(x)

dx
-a.e. in

|\widetilde{h}(x)|\simeq\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}(x)

•

The function

Remark 4.11
.

Again, the original definition (Definition 2.11 of
[

]
) included an

-integrability condition that is no longer necessary, because it is implied by the Smirnov-type condition.

(Thm. 2.12 of
[

]

Let

By Theorem

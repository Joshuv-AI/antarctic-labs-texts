Parameterized families of 2+1d G G -cluster states
Shuhei Ohyama, Kansei Inamura

Abstract
We construct a G G -cluster Hamiltonian in 2+1 dimensions and analyze its properties. This model exhibits a G × 2 ​ Rep ​ ( G ) G\times 2\mathrm{Rep}(G) symmetry, where the 2 ​ Rep ​ ( G ) 2\mathrm{Rep}(G) sector realizes a non-invertible symmetry obtained by condensing appropriate algebra objects in Rep ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{Rep}(G) . Using the symmetry interpolation method, we construct S 1 S^{1} - and S 2 S^{2} -parameterized families of short-range-entangled (SRE) states by interpolating an either invertible 0 0 -form or 1 1 -form symmetry contained in G × 2 ​ Rep ​ ( G ) G\times 2\mathrm{Rep}(G) . Applying an adiabatic evolution argument to this family, we analyze the pumped interface mode generated by this adiabatic process. We then explicitly construct the symmetry operator acting on the interface and show that the interface mode carries a nontrivial charge under this symmetry, thereby demonstrating the nontriviality of the parameterized family.

2.3
Symmetry interpolation

There are several ways to construct parameterized families of SPT phases.
In this paper, we use the symmetry interpolation method.
Let us briefly explain the method.
Fist, suppose we have a Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
with local unitary symmetry
\hat{U}
, i.e.,
[\hat{H},\hat{U}]=0
and
\hat{U}=\prod_{i}\hat{u}_{i}
for some unitary operator
\hat{u}_{i}
acting on sites around
i
.
Since
\hat{u}_{i}
is unitary, we can find a continuous path from the identity operator to
\hat{u}_{i}
, i.e., a continuous family of unitary operators
\hat{u}_{i}(\theta)
such that
\hat{u}_{i}(0)=\mathrm{id}
and
\hat{u}_{i}(2\pi)=\hat{u}_{i}
.
Then, we define a continuous family of Hamiltonians by

\hat{H}(\theta)=\hat{U}(\theta)\hat{H}\hat{U}(\theta)^{\dagger},\;\;\hat{U}(\theta)=\prod_{i}\hat{u}_{i}(\theta).

(2.21)

We note that
\hat{U}(\theta)
is not
2\pi
-periodic, but
\hat{H}(\theta)
is
2\pi
-periodic, due to the relation
[\hat{H},\hat{U}]=0
.

For example, in the case of the 1+1d
G
-cluster state, we can take
\hat{U}
as one of the
G
-symmetry operator
U_{g}
.
As a symmetry interpolation, we can take

U_{g}(\theta)=\bigotimes_{i\in V}\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}(\theta),\;\;\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}(\theta)\coloneq\exp{[\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\log\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}]}.

(2.22)

Here we can take the principal branch of the logarithm as

\log\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}=\sum_{s=0}^{\left|G\right|-1}\frac{2\pi is}{\left|G\right|}P_{s}[\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}],

(2.23)

where
P_{s}[\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}]
is the projection operator to the eigenspace of
\overrightarrow{X}_{g}^{(i)}
with eigenvalue
e^{2\pi is/\left|G\right|}
, i.e.,

P_{s}[X]\coloneq\frac{1}{\left|G\right|}\sum_{k=0}^{\left|G\right|-1}e^{\frac{-2\pi isk}{\left|G\right|}}X^{k},

(2.24)

for a unitary operator

satisfying
X^{\left|G\right|}=1
.
Indeed, one can check that
\exp{(\log\overrightarrow{X}_{g})}=\overrightarrow{X}_{g}
.
Then, the

-family of the 1+1d
G
-cluster state is given by

H_{1+1d}^{g}(\theta)\coloneq U_{g}(\theta)H_{1+1d}U_{g}(\theta)^{\dagger},

(2.25)

for each
g\in G
.
This interpolation preserves the
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
-symmetry since
U_{g}(\theta)
and
\mathcal{O}_{\rho}
commute for all
\theta

Therefore, the
H_{1+1d}^{g}(\theta)
is a
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
-symmetric
S^{1}
-family.

As explained in Sec.
2.2
, one can construct a self-interface mode from each
S^{1}
-family

by considering a textured Hamiltonian.
In Ref.
[
23

g\in G

-family corresponds to the one-dimensional representation labeled by
g

This result is consistent with the categorical expectation that
S^{1}
-families of
2\mathrm{Rep}(G)
-symmetric SRE states are classified by

3.2.1
Frobenius algebra object

Let us recall the definition of a Frobenius algebra object in a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.
An object
A
in a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called a Frobenius algebra object if it is equipped with the following morphisms:

•

multiplication:
\mu:A\otimes A\to A

•

unit:
\eta:\mathbf{1}\to A

•

comultiplication:
\Delta:A\to A\otimes A

•

counit:
\epsilon:A\to\mathbf{1}

satisfying the following conditions:

•

associativity:
\mu\circ(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\mu)

•

unitality:
\mu\circ(\eta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})=\mathrm{id}_{A}=\mu\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\eta)

•

coassociativity:
(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})\circ\Delta=(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta

•

counitality:
(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})\circ\Delta=\mathrm{id}_{A}=(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\epsilon)\circ\Delta

•

Frobenius condition:
(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\Delta)=\Delta\circ\mu=(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\mu)\circ(\Delta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})

Here,
\mathbf{1}
is the tensor unit of
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathrm{id}_{A}
is the identity morphism of
A
.
In addition to this, if
A
satisfies the following conditions, it is called a special Frobenius algebra object:

\mu\circ\Delta=\mathrm{id}_{A},\;\;\epsilon\circ\eta=\mathrm{dim}(A).

(3.18)

In addition to this, if
A
satisfies the following condition, it is called a symmetric special Frobenius algebra object:

(\epsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}})\circ(\mu\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{coev}^{L})=(\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}}\otimes\epsilon)\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A^{*}}\otimes\mu)\circ(\mathrm{coev}^{R}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}),

(3.19)

where
A^{*}
denotes the dual of
A
, and
\mathrm{coev}^{L}:\mathbf{1}\to A\otimes A^{*}
and
\mathrm{coev}^{R}:\mathbf{1}\to A^{*}\otimes A
are the left and right coevaluation maps, respectively.
See Fig.
2
for a graphical representation of the Frobenius condition and the speciality condition.
In the following, we always consider symmetric special Frobenius algebra objects.

From a categorical point of view, the condensation of a symmetric special Frobenius algebra object

\rho^{x}
is the representation of
K
obtained by conjugating
\rho

x

\rho^{x}(k)=\rho(x^{-1}kx)
for
k\in K

Accordingly, we can take the projection
p_{i}
onto the
i

\iota_{i}
such that

\sum_{i}\iota_{i}\circ p_{i}=\mathrm{id}_{A_{1}\otimes A_{2}},\;\;p_{i}\circ\iota_{j}=\delta_{ij}\mathrm{id}_{A_{i}}.

(3.29)

Here,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are defined by
A_{1}=\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{H}(\mathrm{End}(V_{\omega}))
and
A_{2}=\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{K}(\mathrm{End}(V_{\rho}))
, and
A_{i}
is the
i
th component of the decomposition (

).
Diagrammatically, these are represented as follows:

\displaystyle p_{i}=\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$},\;\;\iota_{i}=\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$},

(3.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}\;\;=\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$},\;\;\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}\;\;=\delta_{ij}\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}.

(3.31)

The projection and inclusion are compatible with the Frobenius algebra structures, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &p_{i}\circ(\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2})=\mu_{i}\circ(p_{i}\otimes p_{i}),\;\;(p_{i}\otimes p_{i})\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\Delta_{2})=\Delta_{i}\circ p_{i},\\
&(\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2})\circ(\iota_{i}\otimes\iota_{i})=\iota_{i}\circ\mu_{i},\;\;(\Delta_{1}\otimes\Delta_{2})\circ\iota_{i}=(\iota_{i}\otimes\iota_{i})\circ\Delta_{i}.\end{aligned}

(3.32)

Here, we omitted the trivial braiding between
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.

By using this projection, we can compute the fusion rule of the PEPOs:
Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be two algebra objects in
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
.
Let
P_{12}=\{p_{i}\}
be a set of projections in the decomposition of the tensor product
A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
.
Then, the product of the two PEPOs is locally decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.5}[1.5]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}\;\;=\;\;\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}}\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.5}[1.5]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$},

(3.33)

where
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}
run over the index of the projections in
P_{12}
.
We can show that, on the right-hand side, the configurations of the projection operators with different indices vanish.
To see this, let us focus on the central part of the right-hand side.
By definition of the 4-valent tensor, this part is written as

\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}=\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}=\prod_{j=1}^{4}\delta_{i_{j},i}\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$},

(3.34)

where
i
on the right-hand side is any of
i_{1}
,
i_{2}
,
i_{3}
, and
i_{4}
.
Here, for the second equality, since the decomposition in Eq. (
3.28
) is a direct-sum decomposition as Frobenius algebras, the multiplication and comultiplication between different direct-sum components vanish, which gives rise to the delta functions.
When all the indices are equal, the equality follows from the compatibility condition (
3.32
) between the projections and inclusions and the multiplication and comultiplication.
By plugging this into Eq. (
3.33
), we obtain

\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.5}[1.5]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}\;\;=\;\;\sum_{i}\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.5}[1.5]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}.

(3.35)

By applying this local decomposition, we obtain the following fusion rule for the PEPO:

\mathcal{O}[A_{H,\omega}^{G}]\cdot\mathcal{O}[A^{G}_{K,\rho}]=\sum_{x\in D}\mathcal{O}[A_{H\cap xKx^{-1},\omega\cdot\rho^{x}}^{G}],

(3.36)

where
D
is a set of representatives of the double cosets
H\backslash G/K
.
This agrees with the fusion rule of
2\mathrm{Rep}(G)
[
123
, Corollary 8.10]
.

As an example, we compute two special cases of the decomposition (
3.28
).
First, let us consider the case
H=K=G
.
In this case,
G\backslash G/G=\{e\}
, so the decomposition is

\mathrm{Ind}_{G}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(V_{\omega}))\otimes\mathrm{Ind}_{G}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(V_{\rho}))\cong\mathrm{Ind}_{G}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(V_{\omega}\otimes V_{\rho}))\sim\mathrm{Ind}_{G}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(V_{\omega\cdot\rho})).

(3.37)

Here, the last symbol
\sim
denotes Morita equivalence.
Therefore, these condensation surfaces satisfy the fusion rule

\mathcal{O}[A_{G,\omega}^{G}]\cdot\mathcal{O}[A_{G,\rho}^{G}]=\mathcal{O}[A_{G,\omega\cdot\rho}^{G}].

(3.38)

We note that the condensation surface
\mathcal{O}[A_{G,1}^{G}]
acts as the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\text{flat}}
.
Thus, the condensation surface
\mathcal{O}[A_{G,\omega}^{G}]
is invertible with its inverse given by
\mathcal{O}[A_{G,\omega^{-1}}^{G}]
.
Next, let us consider the case
H=K=\{e\}
.
In this case,
\{e\}\backslash G/\{e\}=G
and the cocycles are trivial, so the decomposition is

\mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}))\otimes\mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}))\cong\bigoplus_{g\in G}\mathrm{Ind}_{\{e\}}^{G}(\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C})).

(3.39)

Therefore, the surface operator
\mathcal{O}[A_{\{e\},1}^{G}]
satisfies the fusion rule

\mathcal{O}[A_{\{e\},1}^{G}]\cdot\mathcal{O}[A_{\{e\},1}^{G}]=|G|\cdot\mathcal{O}[A_{\{e\},1}^{G}].

(3.40)

The operator
\mathcal{O}[A_{\{e\},1}^{G}]
will be referred to as the full-condensation surface because it is physically obtained by condensing all lines on the identity surface.
In the next two paragraphs, we provide more detailed computations of the fusion rules of the full-condensation surfaces.

Example:

As a concrete example, let us consider the case of

First, there are two types of algebra objects in

(1)

We take

\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle=\delta_{g,h},

(3.42)

(2)

\mathbb{C}

Let us compute the fusion rule between these two algebra objects.
The only nontrivial fusion rule is the decomposition of

\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1}\cong\mathbf{1},\;A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\otimes\mathbf{1}\cong\mathbf{1}\otimes A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\cong A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}},\;A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\otimes A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\cong A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\oplus A_{\{e\},1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

3.4

In the 1+1d case, we defined action tensors by acting the MPO symmetry on the local MPS as in Eq. (

In the 2+1d case, by acting the onsite
G

To compute line-like action tensors for the

3.4.1
Line-like action tensors for

Let us first compute line-like action tensors for the

-symmetry.
As we showed in Eq. (

\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}=\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}.

(3.58)

Therefore, the partial action of the symmetry operator

on the ground state PEPS is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}\\
\;\;=\;\;\hbox{$\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{}{}{\scalebox{1.25}[1.25]{{\hbox{\hskip-10.0pt\raise-10.0pt\hbox{{}}}}}}}}$}\end{aligned}

(3.59)

Here, the middle part of the symmetry operator is absorbed into the ground state, while the left and right parts remain.
Along each interface, an action tensor, which is highlighted in red, appears once per site.

3.4.2
Module MPS

Let us now introduce the module MPS and derive several important properties of it.

The computation of the line-like action tensors will be discussed in Sec.

Module over algebra object

For an algebra object

]

First recall that we take an irreducible projective representation

(f\cdot m)(g)=f(g)m(g),

(3.64)

that is, the point-wise action of
\mathrm{End}(V_{\omega})
.

(f_{1}\cdot f_{2})\cdot m=f_{1}\cdot(f_{2}\cdot m),

Now, let us confirm that the above

Let us briefly review the argument here:
Consider an

For such a family, we consider a spatially inhomogeneous Hamiltonian, a textured Hamiltonian

Here, a textured Hamiltonian means a Hamiltonian whose parameters are modulated along one spatial direction (taken to be the

More precisely,

In Sec.

Appendix B
Fiber 2-functors and their automorphisms

In this paper, we constructed various parameterized families based on the
G
-cluster state and verified their nontriviality.
Although our analysis did not require an explicit description of the SPT invariant of the
G
-cluster state, giving a concrete description of 2+1-dimensional non-invertible symmetric SPT invariants using tensor networks is an interesting problem in its own right, and is also important for formulating pump invariants by methods such as those described in Sec.
5.2
.
A complete description of SPT invariants would require addressing several technical issues in the tensor-network representation, for example, formulating an appropriate notion of injectivity for PEPS, which lies beyond the scope of this paper.
Nevertheless, by analogy with the 1+1-dimensional case, one can to some extent anticipate what form such data should take.
In this section, we propose, based on heuristic considerations, a method for extracting SPT invariants from the PEPS representation of the
G
-cluster state.

From a categorical point of view, SPT phases are expected to be classified by the isomorphism classes of fiber 2-functors, and
S^{1}
-families are expected to be classified by their automorphisms (up to invertible monoidal modifications).
We briefly explain these data, and then we describe how they should be extracted from the PEPS representation in the case of the
G
-cluster state.
After that, we illustrate our proposal with some examples.
Finally, we revisit the pump invariant described in Sec.
5.2
.

B.1
Fiber 2-functor of a fusion 2-category

From the categorical viewpoint, SPT phases protected by a fusion 2-category
\mathcal{C}
are classified by tensor 2-functors from
\mathcal{C}
to

Here

denotes the 2-category of finite semisimple linear categories.
The data of a fiber 2-functor

First, a semisimple linear category

is assigned to each object

Next, a linear functor

is assigned to each 1-morphism

\mathcal{F}(\alpha):\mathcal{F}(f)\Rightarrow\mathcal{F}(g)
is assigned to each 2-morphism

.
Then, for each pair of objects

, there is given an equivalence

J_{x,y}:\mathcal{F}(x)\otimes\mathcal{F}(y)\to\mathcal{F}(x\otimes y).

(B.1)

Finally, for each triple of objects
x,y,z\in\mathcal{C}
, there is given a natural isomorphism
\omega_{x,y,z}
between
J_{x\otimes y,z}\circ[J_{x,y}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(z)}]
and
J_{x,y\otimes z}\circ[\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{F}(x)}\otimes J_{y,z}]
up to associators.
16
16

16

These data are required to be natural in

Capmatinib for High-Level MET-Amplified Metastatic Gastric Cancer Identified by Comprehensive Genomic Profiling: A Case Report and Literature Review
Authors: Yasuyoshi Sato, Kousuke Watanabe, Mana Matsuoka, Yurina Yamada, Koichi Yagi, Kuniko Sunami, Miho Ogawa, Katsutoshi Oda, Yoshifumi Baba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
High-level MET gene amplification is a rare but potentially actionable alteration in gastric cancer. Previous phase III trials of MET-targeted therapies enrolled broadly MET-positive populations without prospective selection for high-level MET amplification, and no MET-directed therapy is currently established for this disease. Here, we report a 74-year-old woman with heavily pretreated metastatic gastric adenocarcinoma harboring high-level MET amplification (copy number 21) identified by comprehensive genomic profiling. Following expert panel review, the patient received capmatinib through the BELIEVE trial (NCCH1901; jRCTs031190104) and achieved a confirmed partial response at 8 weeks, with a 43.7% reduction in the sum of target lesion diameters. The response was maintained at 16 weeks; disease progression was observed at 24 weeks, with a progression-free survival of approximately 5.5 months. In the context of a literature review of MET-targeted therapies in gastric cancer, this case supports further investigation of selective MET inhibition in gastric cancer with high-level MET amplification, a rare molecular subset not prospectively enriched in previous clinical trials.

Introduction

Gastric cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Treatment options for patients refractory to standard chemotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitors remain limited. Advances in comprehensive genomic profiling have enabled the identification of rare but potentially actionable molecular alterations in these tumors, facilitating access to matched targeted therapies through precision oncology trials. The clinical relevance of MET gene alterations in gastric cancer, however, remains incompletely defined.

Although MET protein overexpression is relatively common, true MET gene amplification, particularly at a high copy number, is rare and has been reported in only a small subset of gastric cancers [1]. Several phase III trials evaluating MET-targeted therapies in combination with standard chemotherapy in gastric cancer have failed to demonstrate clinical benefit [2, 3], and no MET-directed therapy is currently recommended for this disease [4]. These trials, however, did not prospectively enrich for patients with high-level MET amplification, which may represent a biologically distinct, MET-dependent molecular subset. Previous reviews have emphasized the importance of biomarker-based strategies for MET-targeted therapy in gastric cancer [5, 6].

Capmatinib is a highly selective MET tyrosine kinase inhibitor with established clinical activity against malignancies driven by MET alterations [7], including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [8]. However, evidence supporting its use in gastric cancer remains limited.

Herein, we report a patient with heavily pretreated metastatic gastric cancer with high-level MET amplification identified by comprehensive genomic profiling. Following expert panel review, the patient was enrolled in the BELIEVE trial (NCCH1901; jRCTs031190104), a precision oncology basket-type clinical trial conducted under Japanese patient-proposed healthcare services [9], and received capmatinib. A confirmed partial response was achieved, providing case-based evidence for the therapeutic vulnerability of high-level MET-amplified gastric cancer to selective MET inhibition.

Case Report

A 74-year-old woman was diagnosed with advanced gastric adenocarcinoma with para-aortic lymph node metastases classified as cT4aN+M1, stage IVB. Histological examination revealed poorly differentiated adenocarcinoma with signet-ring cell and tubular components. Initial biomarker testing showed negative human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, microsatellite stability, negative programmed death-ligand 1 expression (combined positive score < 1), and negative claudin 18.2 expression.

The patient received multiple lines of systemic therapy in prior treatment, including tegafur-gimeracil-oteracil (S-1) plus oxaliplatin as first-line therapy, weekly paclitaxel plus ramucirumab as second-line therapy, and nivolumab as third-line therapy. All regimens resulted in progressive disease (PD). Subsequent treatment with irinotecan plus ramucirumab resulted in stable disease but did not provide durable tumor control.

Given the exhaustion of standard treatment options, comprehensive genomic profiling using FoundationOne CDx was performed on a metastatic lymph node specimen obtained by endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration during prior treatment. The analysis revealed high-level MET gene amplification with a copy number of 21.

Genomic profiling results were subsequently reviewed by an expert panel at our institution, a designated core hospital for cancer genomic medicine in Japan, and MET-targeted therapy was recommended. On the basis of this recommendation, the patient was referred to our institution and enrolled in the BELIEVE trial (NCCH1901; jRCTs031190104). After written informed consent was obtained, capmatinib was initiated at a dose of 400 mg twice daily at our institution.

This analysis also detected a MET point mutation (c.3280 C > T, p.H1094Y) with an allele frequency of 29.0%. Importantly, enrollment in the BELIEVE trial (NCCH1901; jRCTs031190104) and the rationale for capmatinib therapy were driven primarily by high-level MET amplification rather than MET point mutations. No other established actionable alterations were identified.

The tumor response was assessed according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 [10], with reference to Japanese practical guidance for response assessment in advanced gastric cancer [11]. Adverse events were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0.

Computed tomography performed 8 weeks after capmatinib initiation demonstrated substantial regression of multiple metastatic lymph nodes. The sum of the diameters of the target lesions decreased by 43.7% from baseline, consistent with a partial response (PR). Tumor shrinkage was maintained through 16 weeks. At 24 weeks, follow-up imaging revealed enlargement of both target and non-target lesions, and PD was confirmed (Fig. 1).

The progression-free survival with capmatinib was approximately 5.5 months. The treatment was generally well tolerated. The patient experienced grade 1 thrombocytopenia, grade 1 anemia, and grade 1 fatigue, all of which were considered related to the study drug. Bilateral tympanic membrane perforation (grade 2) was also observed; however, this event was considered unrelated to capmatinib. No other clinically significant treatment-related adverse events were observed.

Discussion

We report a heavily pretreated patient with metastatic gastric cancer harboring high-level MET amplification (copy number 21) identified by comprehensive genomic profiling who achieved a confirmed partial response to capmatinib through the BELIEVE trial. This case is clinically relevant because previous phase III trials of MET-targeted therapies in gastric cancer enrolled broadly MET-positive or MET-overexpressing populations and failed to demonstrate clinical benefit [2, 3]. These trials did not prospectively enrich for, or adequately stratify, patients with high-level MET gene amplification. Consequently, potential therapeutic activity in this small, molecularly selected population may have been diluted at the trial population level.

This biomarker-dilution effect is well illustrated in NSCLC. MET inhibitors, including capmatinib, have demonstrated modest activity when used in molecularly unselected or loosely defined MET-altered cohorts; however, subsequent analyses have revealed that clinically meaningful responses are largely restricted to tumors with clearly defined high-level MET amplification or MET exon 14 skipping mutations [8]. These observations underscore the importance of precise genomic stratification in identifying MET-driven tumors.

Preclinical studies have shown that MET-amplified gastric cancer models are selectively sensitive to MET inhibition [12]. Some previous clinical studies have also suggested that the level and definition of MET amplification are important for selecting patients who may benefit from MET inhibition. Early clinical observations identified MET amplification as a small but aggressive subgroup of esophagogastric adenocarcinoma with evidence of responsiveness to crizotinib [13]. A subsequent study of crizotinib in chemotherapy-refractory MET-amplified esophageal and gastric adenocarcinomas also reported clinical activity [14]. In a phase II study of AMG 337 in patients with MET-amplified gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma, objective responses were observed, although the overall activity was modest [15]. In the VIKTORY umbrella trial, savolitinib monotherapy showed clinical activity in MET-amplified gastric cancer, with higher response rates reported in tumors with higher MET gene copy number [16]. A phase Ia/Ib study of savolitinib in patients with advanced solid tumors also reported responses in gastric cancer with MET amplification [17]. More recently, a multicenter phase II trial of savolitinib in MET-amplified gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma, which prospectively selected patients using MET gene copy number criteria, reported clinically meaningful antitumor activity [18]. Collectively, these findings suggest that high-level MET amplification, rather than broadly defined MET positivity or MET protein overexpression, may be an important biomarker for selective MET inhibition in gastric cancer.

In this context, the present case differed from patients enrolled in previous gastric cancer trials. The tumor harbored high-level MET amplification with a copy number of 21, consistent with the molecular selection strategy suggested by previous studies. The confirmed partial response to capmatinib observed in this heavily pretreated patient is therefore consistent with prior clinical evidence supporting MET inhibition in high-level MET-amplified gastroesophageal cancers. Nevertheless, evidence supporting capmatinib specifically in gastric cancer remains extremely limited, and no MET-directed therapy has been established for gastric cancer. In the phase I study of capmatinib by Bang et al., no objective responses were reported in the gastric cancer cohort, although stable disease was observed in two of nine patients (22%) [7]. Importantly, the study used broad criteria for MET positivity and did not specifically select patients with high-level MET amplification. The present case therefore differs from the previously reported gastric cancer cohort in that treatment was selected on the basis of high-level MET amplification. MET H1094Y is a kinase-domain mutation that has been reported as an activating and potentially targetable alteration [19]. However, the supporting clinical evidence is derived mainly from NSCLC, and its therapeutic significance in gastric cancer, particularly in the setting of concurrent MET amplification, remains uncertain. In the present case, the tumor harbored both high-level MET amplification and MET H1094Y; therefore, the relative contribution of each alteration to capmatinib sensitivity cannot be determined from this single case. To our knowledge, this is among the first reports describing a confirmed partial response to capmatinib in gastric cancer with high-level MET amplification identified by comprehensive genomic profiling and treated within a prospective precision oncology trial framework. These findings support further evaluation of selective MET inhibition in patients with high-level MET-amplified gastric cancer.

This case illustrates how comprehensive genomic profiling can identify rare but actionable genomic alterations and facilitate expert panel–guided treatment selection when standard treatment options have been exhausted. In this patient, comprehensive genomic profiling identified high-level MET amplification, which provided the basis for considering molecularly matched therapy.

This case was treated within the BELIEVE trial (NCCH1901), a precision oncology basket trial in Japan that facilitated expert panel–guided access to molecularly matched therapy [9]. Previous reports from the BELIEVE trial have demonstrated the feasibility and clinical relevance of biomarker-driven treatment approaches across rare genomic subsets, including BRAF-mutated solid tumors [20] and a RAD54L-mutated prostate cancer case responding to niraparib [21]. The present case adds MET-amplified gastric cancer to this experience and highlights the role of such a platform in enabling treatment access for patients with rare actionable alterations.

This study has several limitations. As a single-case report, the observed response cannot be generalized to all patients with MET-amplified gastric cancer, and this case does not establish treatment efficacy. The duration of response was also limited, with disease progression observed at 24 weeks. Post-progression tumor biopsy or circulating tumor DNA analysis was not performed, precluding definitive characterization of resistance mechanisms. Previous studies of MET-amplified gastric cancer treated with selective MET inhibitors have identified acquired resistance mechanisms, including secondary MET kinase domain mutations involving D1228 or Y1230 and re-emergence or increase of MET amplification [22]. These observations underscore the importance of serial genomic profiling at disease progression to clarify resistance mechanisms and guide subsequent therapeutic strategies. Nevertheless, the confirmed tumor response observed in this heavily pretreated patient with high-level MET amplification provides clinically relevant evidence and underscores the importance of further prospective data accumulation from the BELIEVE trial and related precision oncology studies in this rare molecular subset.

In conclusion, we report a confirmed partial response to capmatinib in a patient with high-level MET-amplified metastatic gastric cancer identified by comprehensive genomic profiling and treated through the BELIEVE trial (NCCH1901; jRCTs031190104) following expert panel review. This case provides clinically relevant evidence supporting further evaluation of selective MET inhibition in this rare molecular subset and illustrates the clinical utility of comprehensive genomic profiling and expert panel–guided treatment selection in patients with limited standard therapy options.

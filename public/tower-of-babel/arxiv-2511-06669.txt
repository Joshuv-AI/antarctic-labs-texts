Average Winding Number for Determinantal Curves associated with 2-Matrix Models in the Class AIII
Mathieu Yahiaoui, Mario Kieburg

Abstract
To classify one-dimensional disordered quantum systems with chiral symmetry, we analyse the winding number of the determinant of a parametrized non-Hermitian random matrix field over the unit circle modelling the off-diagonal block of a disordered chiral Hamiltonian. The associated partition function is computed explicitly for a broad class of additive two-matrix models extending beyond the Ginibre Unitary Ensemble. In the large-dimension limit, we derive an asymptotic expansion of the average winding number whose leading term exhibits universal features, up to the tail behaviour of the underlying random matrix ensemble, and identify a new correction term absent in the previously studied Ginibre case.

Introduction

The discovery of the quantum Hall effect in 1980
26
revealed that, as the strength of an external magnetic field increases, the Hall conductance of a two-dimensional electron gas forms quantized plateaux. These plateaux persist even in the presence of impurities or lattice imperfections, indicating a remarkable robustness of the quantization to disorder.

This phenomenon initiated the modern study of topological phases of matter, where insulating bulk behaviour coexists with conducting edge states. The systematic classification of such phases was achieved in the seminal work
33
and relies on the Altland-Zirnbauer classification
1
, sometimes referred to as the
Tenfold Way

4
, which organises spectrally gapped Hamiltonians according to the Cartan classification of Riemannian symmetric spaces.

More recently, non-Hermitian Hamiltonians have attracted interest as effective models for open quantum systems where dissipation may occur. In that context, the interplay between topology and spectral structure plays a central role, see
3
for a recent review.

Through the bulk-boundary correspondence (see for example
19
;
32
), edge phenomena observed in systems with open boundary conditions may be understood by studying the same system with closed boundary conditions often referred to as the
bulk
. The corresponding bulk Hamiltonian has the aforementioned discrete translational lattice invariance. Its Fourier transform may be parametrized by
p\in\mathrm{S}^{1}
on the unit circle when considering the one-dimensional setting. Furthermore, its eigenvectors form a complex vector bundle over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
. Non-triviality of the latter corresponds to the existence of protected edge states in the original system.

We consider one-dimensional quantum systems with discrete translational symmetry and study their Hamiltonians in the quasi-momentum representation. This formulation, natural for crystalline lattices, follows from Bloch’s theorem which states that eigenstates of the Schrödinger operator can be written as

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\left(x\right)=e^{i\mathrm{k}x}u_{\mathrm{k}}\left(x\right),

(1.1)

where
\mathrm{k}
is the quasi-momentum varying over the Brillouin zone
[-\pi,\pi]\cong\mathrm{S}^{1}
and
u_{\mathrm{k}}
is periodic with respect to the lattice spacing set to unity for simplicity. In what follows, we identify
p=e^{i\mathrm{k}}\in\mathrm{S}^{1}
.

Among the ten Altland-Zirnbauer symmetry classes, the chiral
class AIII
exhibits a particularly rich structure in odd dimensions due to the presence of an energy gap at the Fermi level. It corresponds to quantum systems that are neither time-reversal invariant nor particle-hole invariant yet remain symmetric under the composition of time-reversal and particle-hole conjugation, see
(
10
, Section E)
. In such models, the determinant of the off-diagonal block of the Hamiltonian defines a map from the circle
\mathrm{S}^{1}
to the punctured complex plane
\mathbb{C}^{*}
whose winding number around the origin serves as a characteristic integer invariant.

As noted in
8
;
22
, this invariant given by the determinantal curve remains meaningful under random perturbations even though averaging may smear out the spectral gap. Indeed, each realization of the random Hamiltonian retains a finite gap whose typical length scales with the local mean level spacing.

We model the system by a random field
H
of complex chiral Hermitian matrices over the unit circle, representing a one-dimensional disordered crystal in quasi-momentum space. The randomness encodes disorder and random matrix theory provides a tractable framework for the corresponding ensemble averages. The invariant of interest is the winding number of the determinant of the off-diagonal block of the Hamiltonian. While this quantity is strictly defined for periodic Bloch Hamiltonians, disorder generally breaks periodicity. To recover a well-defined invariant one may assume that the disorder is periodic over a large yet finite window whose length is eventually taken to infinity.

Our analysis extends the computation of the partition function associated with the random winding number, previously derived for the Ginibre Unitary Ensemble (
GinUE
) additive
2
-matrix model
8
;
20
;
22
, to a broader class of non-Hermitian additive
2
-matrix models for which exact formulas can still be obtained, allowing us to extract large-dimension asymptotics for the average winding number.

Section
2
introduces the model and the associated constructions including the winding number, the partition function and Pólya ensembles of multiplicative type
24
;
25
;
17
. In this section, we also summarise our main results:
Theorem 2.8
provides an exact expression valid for finite size
N
and for all Pólya
2
-matrix models whereas
Theorem 2.9
gives the asymptotic expansion of the mean winding number for the Muttalib-Borodin ensemble
7
of Laguerre type. As a comparison,
2.14
confirms that the result of
8
;
20
for the GinUE additive 2-matrix model extends to more general matrix-valued Gaussian random fields. Proofs of the finite and large-
N
results are presented in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively, and the auxiliary integral identities used therein are collected in the appendices. We conclude in Section
5
.

2
Preliminaries and main results

2.1
Winding number of a determinantal curve

As aforementioned, we aim to study a specific classification of topological phases of random Hamiltonians. More precisely, we focus our attention on class AIII in the one-dimensional setting, for which the invariant happens to be a winding number.
In a chiral basis, a class AIII Hamiltonian has the following form

\displaystyle H\left(p\right)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{N}&K\left(p\right)\\
K^{\dagger}\left(p\right)&0_{N}\end{pmatrix},

(2.1)

where
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
denotes the Hermitian conjugate and we recall that
p
denotes the quasi-momentum varying over the Brillouin zone, which is isomorphic to the unit circle. The matrix-valued random field
K
is assumed to be analytic entrywise over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
.

Topology becomes relevant when assuming the existence of an energy gap around zero. The presence of a spectral gap gives rise to several topological properties of this set of Hamiltonians, which can be captured by different invariants.

In a naive approach, the determinantal curve
p\mapsto\det\left(H\left(p\right)\right)
would not yield any significant topological information since
H
is Hermitian and its determinant takes values in the homotopically trivial space
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Following
29
, one may instead consider the determinantal curve over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
associated with the sub-block
K
. We note that the eigenvalues of
K
are not necessarily periodic and some might be permuted, see
Figure 1
.

K(p)=(\frac{1}{2i}(p-p^{-1})+p)K_{1}+(3-\frac{1}{10}(p+p^{-1})+p^{-2})K_{2}

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are independently drawn from the
\mathrm{GinUE}(5)
. On the right: its associated determinantal curve. The black dots indicate the

K(0)
and
\det(K(0))
while the red arrows mark the points where
\mathrm{Arg}(p)=\pi
. The winding number around the origin of the determinantal curve equals
+1
. The eigenvalue flow is not
2\pi

p

\mathrm{S}^{1}

The determinant of
K
is the simplest
2\pi
-periodic function that encodes the entire spectrum, to that end we define the determinantal curve

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}:p\mapsto\det\left(K\left(p\right)\right).

(2.2)

The energy gap of an individual chiral Hamiltonian (
2.1
) is in direct correspondence with the spectral gap at the origin of
K
since
\det H=|\det K|^{2}
.
We thus consider the corresponding winding number of
\det\left(K\left(p\right)\right)
around the origin explicitly given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\left(\mathscr{C},0\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}w\left(p\right)\mathrm{d}p,

(2.3)

where
w
is the winding number density defined as

\displaystyle w\left(p\right)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\log\left(\det\left(K\left(p\right)\right)\right)=\frac{1}{\det\left(K\left(p\right)\right)}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\det\left(K\left(p\right)\right).

(2.4)

This quantity will be our object of interest and we will study its
m
-point correlation functions with
m\geq 1
and
N\geq 1
.
The arguments of
\mathrm{Wind}_{N}
shall highlight the chosen curve
\mathscr{C}
and the point it winds around. For short we denote the winding number by
\mathrm{Wind}_{N}=\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\left(\mathscr{C},0\right)
.

To investigate the distribution of that random winding number, a natural quantity to consider is the expectation of its
m
th moment as it opens the way for a possible central limit theorem, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}^{m}\Big)=(2\pi i)^{-m}\oint_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}w\left(p_{k}\right)\Bigg)\mathrm{d}p_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}p_{m},

(2.5)

where
\mathbb{T}^{m}:=\mathrm{S}^{1}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{S^{1}}
denotes the
m
-dimensional unit torus.
The expectation value inside the integral can be traced back to the partition function

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\right)=\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\det\left(K\left(p_{k}\right)\right)}{\det\left(K\left(q_{k}\right)\right)}\Bigg)

(2.6)

with
\mathbf{p}=\left(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\right)
and
\mathbf{q}=\left(q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\right)
two collections of points on
\mathrm{S}^{1}
.
The associated
m
-point correlation function

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{m}^{(N)}(\mathbf{p})=\mathbb{E}\Bigg(\prod_{k=1}^{m}w\left(p_{k}\right)\Bigg)

(2.7)

is then obtained by taking successive derivatives of
\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{m}
and setting
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{q}
. For example the 1-point and 2-point correlation functions are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{\left(N\right)}_{1}\left(p\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(w\left(p\right)\right)=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{1}}\left(p,q\right)\right|_{q=p},

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}^{\left(N\right)}_{2}\left(p_{1},p_{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(w\left(p_{1}\right)w\left(p_{2}\right)\right)=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}p_{1}\mathrm{d}p_{2}}\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{2}}\left(p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\right)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1}=p_{1}\\
q_{2}=p_{2}\end{subarray}}.

(2.9)

As shown above, the integrating
\mathrm{C}^{\left(N\right)}_{m}
over
\mathbb{T}^{m}
gives the
m
th moment of
\mathrm{Wind}_{N}
.

2.2
Additive 2-matrix models

The 2-matrix model for which both random matrices are independently drawn from the GinUE, which is defined by the requirement that all entries are independent standard complex Gaussian random variables and was first introduced in
18
, has been studied in detail recently in
20
. A recent survey may be found in
9
.

We summarise these results here briefly. To that end, we fix two complex-valued and differentiable parameter functions
a
and
b
over the unit circle
\mathrm{S}^{1}
and set

\displaystyle\nu\left(p\right)=\begin{pmatrix}a\left(p\right)\\
b\left(p\right)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\qquad\kappa\left(p\right)=\frac{a\left(p\right)}{b\left(p\right)}\in\mathbb{CP}^{1},\qquad\mathrm{J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

(2.10)

where
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
denotes the complex projective line.
To construct the additive 2-matrix model, we draw two random matrices
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
and form

\displaystyle K(p)=a\left(p\right)K_{1}+b\left(p\right)K_{2}.

(2.11)

Assumption 2.1

For the winding number to be well-defined we require that the two parameter functions
a
and
b
never vanish simultaneously over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
. Furthermore, each of them is assumed to have isolated zeroes, ensuring that their logarithmic derivatives remain well-defined and yield only simple poles over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
. This guarantees the tractability of subsequent computations.

In the special case where the source matrices are drawn from the GinUE of size
N
the result for the partition function is quite simple and was derived in
(
20
, Sec. IV)
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\right)=\frac{\det\Big(\frac{1}{\nu\left(p_{i}\right)^{\top}J\nu\left(q_{j}\right)}\left(\frac{\nu\left(q_{j}\right)^{\dagger}\nu\left(p_{i}\right)}{\nu\left(q_{j}\right)^{\dagger}\nu\left(q_{j}\right)}\right)^{N}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq m}}{\det\Big(\frac{1}{\nu\left(p_{i}\right)^{\top}J\nu\left(q_{j}\right)}\Big)_{1\leq i,j\leq m}}.

(2.12)

This result highlights an elegant and algebraically rich structure for the partition function associated with the random winding number.

We aim to expand this result to a larger class of random matrix ensembles and to include complex conjugates in the partition function (
2.6
) in order to capture the real and imaginary parts of the winding number density (
2.4
). This class of random matrices is called
multiplicative Pólya ensembles on
the complex general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
and will be reviewed in the next subsection.

2.3
Pólya ensembles of multiplicative type on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})

The cornerstone of the computation in
20
was to reduce the 2-matrix model to a single matrix model which consists of the product of the first matrix with the inverse of the second one. This reduction is what ultimately makes the closed-form result (
2.12
) possible. To generalize this idea, we need a class of random matrices that is closed under product and inverse while retaining suitable integrable structure.

Such a class has been constructed
25
;
17
in the past decade and is referred to as
Pólya ensembles on

\mathrm{GL}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
. It has many convenient analytical and algebraic properties, for instance both its collections of eigenvalues and of singular values form determinantal point processes.
We will give a brief introduction, an in-depth treatment can be found in
24
;
25
;
17
.

To define Pólya ensembles (of multiplicative type on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
), we introduce the following Euler-type differential operators

\displaystyle\Theta^{\left(0\right)}=\mathrm{Id},\quad\Theta^{\left(n\right)}=\Big(-x\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\Big)^{n}\quad\text{ for }n\geq 1.

(2.13)

Let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
containing
1
and
n
a strictly positive integer, we introduce the functional set

\displaystyle\mathrm{L}^{1,n}_{I}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)=

\displaystyle\biggl\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)\cap\mathscr{C}^{n}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right),\,\forall\left(s,k\right)\in I\times\left[0,n\right]:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|t^{s-1}\Theta^{\left(k\right)}\left(f\right)\left(t\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t<+\infty\biggl\},

(2.14)

where
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
and
\mathscr{C}^{n}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
are respectively the Lebesgue integrable and the
n
-times continuously differentiable functions on the set of positive real numbers
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

In what follows,
f
is an element of
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
and
A>0
. The multiplicative Pólya ensembles are intimately related to the Mellin transform which alongside the incomplete Mellin transform is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[f\right]:z\mapsto\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{z-1}f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\textbf{Mellin transform},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[f\right]\left(\,\cdot\,,A\right):z\mapsto\int_{0}^{A}t^{z-1}f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\textbf{incomplete Mellin transform},

(2.16)

While the exact domain of definition of the Mellin transform in the complex plane can be quite complicated, it always contains at least the line
i\mathbb{R}+1:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C},\,\mathfrak{Re}\left(z\right)=1\right\}
due to the absolute integrability of
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
.

Related to the Mellin transform is the multiplicative convolution of two functions
f,g\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
given by

\displaystyle f\circledast g:x\mapsto\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)g\left(y\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}.

(2.17)

Notably the Mellin transform is injective over
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
and for a complex number
z
where both
\mathcal{M}\left[f\right]
and
\mathcal{M}\left[g\right]
are defined we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[f\circledast g\right]\left(z\right)=\mathcal{M}\left[f\right]\left(z\right)\mathcal{M}\left[g\right]\left(z\right).

(2.18)

Additionally, we have the following property

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\Theta^{(n)}f\right]\left(z\right)=z^{n}\mathcal{M}\left[f\right]\left(z\right).

(2.19)

A more complete treatment of the properties of the Mellin transform can be found in
(
25
, Sec. 2)
.

The class of
Pólya ensembles
is based on the notion of a
Pólya frequency function
which sits at the core of these random matrix ensembles.
A measurable function in
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is called a
Pólya frequency function of order

n
, denoted by
f\in\mathrm{PF}_{n}
, if for all collections of real numbers
(x_{i}),\,(y_{j})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for all integer
1\leq k\leq n
,
f
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{k}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\right)\Delta_{k}\left(y_{1},\cdots,y_{k}\right)\det\left(f\left(x_{i}-y_{j}\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}\geq 0,

(2.20)

where we have used the Vandermonde determinant given by

\Delta_{k}\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k}\right)=\det\left(x_{i}^{j-1}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{j}-x_{i}).

(2.21)

A function
f
is said to be a
Pólya frequency function of infinite order
, denoted by
f\in\mathrm{PF}_{\infty}
, if and only if
f
is a Pólya frequency function of all orders so that one may write

\displaystyle\mathrm{PF}_{\infty}=\bigcap\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{PF}_{n}.

(2.22)

While a general description of
\mathrm{PF}_{n}
for
n>3
is not known, a few elementary and structural results are available. Firstly, for all integers
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathrm{PF}_{\infty}\subset\mathrm{PF}_{n+1}\subset\mathrm{PF}_{n}.

(2.23)

In particular, the first level of the hierarchy is simply given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{PF}_{1}=\left\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}\right):\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\,f\left(x\right)\geq 0\right\}.

(2.24)

The second level
\mathrm{PF}_{2}
also admits a complete characterization: a continuous and integrable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
is in
\mathrm{PF}_{2}
if and only if
f
is positive and log-concave. A proof may be found in Ref.
(
31
, Section 11)
. We also signal that a complete, albeit more involved, characterization of
\mathrm{PF}_{3}
is available in
36
.

Sometimes coined
Polynomial Ensembles of Derivative Type
in the literature,
Pólya ensembles
on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
are families of matrix-valued random variables parametrized by a Pólya frequency function
\omega
which are included in polynomial ensembles firstly introduced in
28
, themselves included in the larger family of bi-unitary invariant (sometimes called
isotropic
) random matrices
25
;
17
.
The bi-unitary invariance means that their probability measure on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
is invariant under the adjoint action of the unitary group
\mathrm{U}(N)
in the following sense

\displaystyle\forall M\in\mathrm{GL}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right),\,\forall\,U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathrm{U}(N):\quad\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\left(U_{1}MU_{2}\right)=\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\left(M\right)

(2.25)

This property implies that the associated joint probability density function (
jpdf
) of the complex eigenvalues denoted by
f_{\mathrm{EV}}
is rotationally-invariant. It also implies that the singular values are independent of their singular vectors, each of the latter being Haar distributed on the unit sphere of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. It happens that the distribution of singular values looks simpler when one squares them, the corresponding squared singular values jpdf will be denoted by
f_{\mathrm{SV}}
.

We say that a function
\omega\in\mathrm{L}^{1,N-1}_{\left[1,N\right]}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
gives rise to a
Pólya Ensemble
on
{\rm Gl}_{N}\left(\mathbb{C}\right)
as in
(
17
, Eq. (2.17))
(equivalently that
\omega
is a
Pólya weight
), if and only if the logarithmic deformation

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}:x\mapsto e^{-x}\omega\left(e^{-x}\right)

(2.26)

is in
\mathrm{PF}_{N}
and the squared singular values jpdf is

f^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{SV}}\left[\omega\right](\mathbf{x})=C^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{SV}}\left[\omega\right]\Delta_{N}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\det\left(\Theta^{\left(j-1\right)}\left[\omega\right]\left(x_{i}\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

(2.27)

The normalisation constants corresponds to the first term of

\displaystyle C^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{SV}}\left[\omega\right]=\Big(\prod\limits_{k=1}^{N}k!\mathcal{M}\left[\omega\right]\left(k\right)\Big)^{-1},\quad C^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{EV}}\left[\omega\right]=\Big(\pi^{N}N!\prod\limits_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{M}\left[\omega\right]\left(k\right)\Big)^{-1}.

(2.28)

The second term normalizes the complex eigenvalues jpdf given by

f^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{EV}}\left[\omega\right](\mathbf{z})=C^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{EV}}\left[\omega\right]\left|\Delta_{N}\left(\mathbf{z}\right)\right|^{2}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{N}\omega\left(\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}\right).

(2.29)

Remark 2.2

Without loss of generality, we normalise the Pólya weights so that
\mathcal{M}[\omega](1)=1
.

We say that a random matrix
X
is drawn from the Pólya ensemble (of multiplicative type on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
with weight
\omega
) if its probability density on
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
is bi-unitarily invariant in the sense of (
2.25
) and if the jpdfs of the squared singular values as well as complex eigenvalues are given respectively by (
2.27
) and (
2.29
). In that case, we write
X\sim\mathrm{P\acute{o}l}_{N}\left[\omega\right]
.

It should be noted that a Pólya weight
\omega
, such that
\widetilde{\omega}
is at least in
\rm{PF}_{2}
, can be represented with a logarithmic change of variables so that, for all
t>0

\displaystyle\omega(t)=\frac{\exp(-\psi(-\rm{ln}(t)))}{t},

(2.30)

where
\psi
is a continuous convex function on
\mathbb{R}
. Additionally, logarithmic deformations of Pólya weights in
\mathrm{PF}_{2}
decay at least exponentially near infinity, see for example Lemma A.1 in
13
.

Before giving an example, we conclude this section with a crucial result that sheds light on the multiplicative structure of the class of random matrices formed by
Pólya ensembles
. For this purpose, we choose two Pólya weights
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
such that the corresponding functions
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{2}
defined in (
2.26
) belong to
\mathrm{PF}_{\infty}
. Moreover, we draw two independent Pólya random
N\times N
matrices
X\sim\mathrm{P\acute{o}l}_{N}\left[\omega_{1}\right]
and
Y\sim\mathrm{P\acute{o}l}_{N}\left[\omega_{2}\right]
and set

\displaystyle\check{\omega}_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}[\omega_{1}](N)x^{N+1}}\omega_{1}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right).

(2.31)

Proposition 2.3

(see Eq. (3.10), Rem. 3.5 in
25
)

We have

\displaystyle XY\sim\mathrm{P\acute{o}l}_{N}\left[\omega_{1}\circledast\omega_{2}\right]\qquad{\rm and}\qquad X^{-1}\sim\mathrm{P\acute{o}l}_{N}\left[\check{\omega}_{1}\right].

(2.32)

In particular, for the generalized ratio of
X
and
Y
we have
X^{-1}Y\sim\mathrm{P\acute{o}l}_{N}\left[\check{\omega}_{1}\circledast\omega_{2}\right]
and for every complex number
z
where both
\mathcal{M}\left[\omega_{1}\right](z)
and
\mathcal{M}\left[\omega_{2}\right](N+1-z)
exist the following holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\check{\omega}_{1}\circledast\omega_{2}\right]\left(z\right)=\frac{\mathcal{M}\left[\omega_{1}\right]\left(N+1-z\right)\mathcal{M}\left[\omega_{2}\right]\left(z\right)}{\mathcal{M}[\omega_{1}](N)}.

(2.33)

The previous proposition is crucial in the ensuing analysis and allows us to explicitly reduce the 2-matrix model to a 1-matrix model. For a given Pólya weight
\omega
we define the self-inverse-convoluted Pólya weight by

\widehat{\omega}(z)=\check{\omega}\circledast\omega\left(z\right).

(2.34)

The latter may be written explicitly as follows

\widehat{\omega}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega(y)\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^{N+1}\frac{\omega\left(y/x\right)}{\mathcal{M}[\omega](N)}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\overset{y\to xy}{=}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(xy)\omega(y)}{\mathcal{M}[\omega](N)}y^{N}\mathrm{d}y,

(2.35)

it should be noted that
\widehat{\omega}
implicitly depends on
N
.

A self-inverse-convoluted Pólya weight has the following symmetry

\widehat{\omega}(x^{-1})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(y/x)\omega(y)}{\mathcal{M}[\omega](N)}y^{N}\mathrm{d}y\underset{y=tx}{=}x^{N+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(t)\omega(xt)}{\mathcal{M}[\omega](N)}t^{N}\mathrm{d}t=x^{N+1}\widehat{\omega}(x).

(2.36)

Consequently, the Mellin transform of a self-inverse-convoluted Pólya weight, together with its incomplete analogue, satisfies the following reflection symmetries:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(z\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}\left[\omega\right]\left(N-z+1\right)\mathcal{M}\left[\omega\right]\left(z\right)}{\mathcal{M}[\omega](N)}=\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(N-z+1\right),

(2.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(z,A\right)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(z\right)-\int_{A}^{\infty}\widehat{\omega}(x)x^{z-1}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\overset{x\to x^{-1}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(z\right)-\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(N-z+1,A^{-1}\right).

(2.38)

Example 2.4

A central non-Gaussian example is the Muttalib-Borodin ensemble of Laguerre type

\displaystyle f^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{EV}}\left[\omega_{\mathrm{MB}}\right]\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}\right)\propto|\Delta_{N}\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{N}\right)|^{2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}|^{2\delta}e^{-\alpha|z_{j}|^{2\gamma}},

2
-matrix model (

Lemma 2.5

Let
\omega
be a Pólya weight such that
\widetilde{\omega}
is a Pólya frequency function of order
2
, for any
j\in[0,2N-2]

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{j}\omega(|z|^{2})\mathrm{d}^{2}z<\infty.

(2.43)

Let
m
be a strictly positive integer,
(\alpha_{\ell})
be
m
complex numbers and
(\beta_{\ell})
be
m
pairwise distinct non-zero complex numbers.
Then, the following holds

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}\left|\Delta_{N}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left(\omega(\left|z_{j}\right|^{2})\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left|\frac{z_{j}-\alpha_{\ell}}{z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}}\right|\right)\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{z}<+\infty.

(2.44)

Proof.

Making use of the triangular inequality, we have the following rough bound

\left|\Delta_{N}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}\leq\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}(1+\left|z_{j}\right|)^{2(N-1)}.

(2.45)

Let us fix
r>0
small enough so that all closed discs
\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{j},r)\subset\mathbb{C}
are pairwise disjoint and do not intersect with the origin. We then set

\Omega:=\left(\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{j},r)\right)^{N}.

(2.46)

For
\mathbf{z}\in\Omega
, each coordinate satisfies
\left|z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}\right|\geq r
for all
1\leq\ell\leq m
. Therefore, uniformly in
z_{j}
where
j
is fixed, we have

\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left|\frac{z_{j}-\alpha_{\ell}}{z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}}\right|=\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left|1+\frac{\beta_{\ell}-\alpha_{\ell}}{z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}}\right|\leq\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(1+\frac{|\beta_{\ell}-\alpha_{\ell}|}{r}\right).

(2.47)

By assumption
\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{j}\omega(|z|^{2})\mathrm{d}^{2}z<\infty
for any
j\in[0,2N-2]
, the integral

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left|\Delta_{N}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}\left(\omega(\left|z_{j}\right|^{2})\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left|\frac{z_{j}-\alpha_{\ell}}{z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}}\right|\right)\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{z}\\
\leq&\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{j},r)}(1+\left|z\right|)^{2(N-1)}\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{m}\left(1+\frac{|\beta_{\ell}-\alpha_{\ell}|}{r}\right)\omega(|z|^{2})\mathrm{d}^{2}z\right)^{N}<\infty\end{split}

(2.48)

exists on
\Omega
.

On the complement
\Omega^{\complement}
, there exists an index pair
(j,\ell)
such that
\left|z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}\right|<r
. We work inside the compact closed disc

\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{\ell},r)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,\left|z-\beta_{\ell}\right|\leq r\right\}.

(2.49)

Because
\omega
is a Pólya weight, it is positive, continuous and integrable on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. In particular, any singularity must arise at a boundary point say
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
: there exists
\alpha\in[0,1[
and
c_{1}>0
such that, on a neighbourhood of
t_{0}

\omega(t)\leq c_{1}\left|t-t_{0}\right|^{-\alpha}

If
\left|\beta_{\ell}\right|^{2}\neq t_{0}
, we simply have
\alpha=0
in what follows. Assume there exist an index
\ell
such that
\left|\beta_{\ell}\right|^{2}=t_{0}
. Because
\beta_{\ell}
is non-zero and the function
z\mapsto\left|z\right|^{2}
has a non-vanishing gradient at
z=\beta_{\ell}
, for
r>0
sufficiently small there exist constants
c_{2},c_{3}>0
such that

c_{2}\left|z-\beta_{\ell}\right|\leq\left|\left|z\right|^{2}-\left|\beta_{\ell}\right|^{2}\right|\leq c_{3}\left|z-\beta_{\ell}\right|.

Consequently, on
\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{\ell},r)
there exists
c_{4}>0
such that

\omega(\left|z\right|^{2})\leq c_{4}\left|z-\beta_{\ell}\right|^{-\alpha}.

All factors in the integrand that remain away from their own singularities are bounded on this disc. In particular,

•

all denominators
\left|z_{j}-\beta_{k}\right|
with
k\neq\ell
stay uniformly bounded away from zero since the discs are chosen disjoints,

•

the Vandermonde term is bounded by

\left|\Delta_{N}(\mathbf{z})\right|^{2}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1+\left|z_{j}\right|)^{2(N-1)}.

It follows that, for
z_{j}\in\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{\ell},r)
, the factor

(1+\left|z_{j}\right|)^{2(N-1)}\omega(\left|z_{j}\right|^{2})\left|z_{j}-\alpha_{\ell}\right|\prod\limits_{k\neq\ell}\left|\frac{z_{j}-\alpha_{k}}{z_{j}-\beta_{k}}\right|

is bounded, up to a constant, by
\left|z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}\right|^{-\alpha}
. Hence, there exists
c_{5}>0
such that the full integrand on
\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{\ell},r)
is dominated by

c_{5}\left|z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}\right|^{-(1+\alpha)}.

In polar coordinates around
\beta_{\ell}
, that is
z=\beta_{\ell}+\rho e^{i\theta}
, on this closed disc we have

\int_{\overline{\mathrm{D}}(\beta_{\ell},r)}\left|z_{j}-\beta_{\ell}\right|^{-(1+\alpha)}\mathrm{d}^{2}z=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{d}\theta\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{-(1+\alpha)}\rho\mathrm{d}\rho=2\pi\int_{0}^{r}\rho^{-\alpha}\mathrm{d}\rho<\infty,

since
\alpha<1
. Therefore, the integral over
\Omega^{\complement}
is finite which allows us to conclude.

Main results

To state our first main result, we define the Cauchy-like kernel

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}_{m}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})=\Bigg(\frac{1}{\nu\left(p_{i}\right)^{\top}\mathrm{J}\nu\left(q_{j}\right)}\Bigg)_{1\leq i,j\leq m}

\mathrm{Q}_{m}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})^{\top}=-\mathrm{Q}_{m}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})

We also define the following auxiliary function

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{N}\left(u,v\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(k,\left|v\right|^{2}\right)}{\mathcal{M}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(k\right)}\Big(\frac{u}{v}\Big)^{k}

(2.55)

which in turn we use to define the following deformed kernel

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{(N)}_{m}[\omega](\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})=\left(\frac{\left(b(p_{i})/b(q_{j})\right)^{N}}{\nu(p_{i})^{\top}\mathrm{J}\nu(q_{j})}\left[1+\Big(1-\frac{\kappa\left(q_{j}\right)}{\kappa\left(p_{i}\right)}\Big)\Upsilon_{N}\big(\kappa(p_{i}),\kappa(q_{j})\big)\right]\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq m},

(2.56)

where
\nu,\,\kappa
and
J
are as defined in (
2.10
).

Remark 2.6

The auxiliary function

satisfies the following symmetry relations

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{N}\left(u,v\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{u}{v}\Bigg(\frac{\left(u/v\right)^{N}-1}{u/v-1}-\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{N}\Upsilon_{N}\left(u^{-1},v^{-1}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\text{if $u\neq v$},

(2.57)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{N}\left(u,u\right)=

\displaystyle N-\Upsilon_{N}\left(u^{-1},u^{-1}\right),

(2.58)

which are direct consequences of (
2.37
) and (
2.38
) by reflecting

in the summation index.

Remark 2.7

Expression (

) is not manifestly symmetric under the exchange of

and

, nevertheless the underlying 2-matrix model (
2.11
) is symmetric and this invariance is preserved in a non-obvious way. More precisely, combining (

Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8

The partition function (
2.6
) associated with an additive 2-matrix model where the two source matrices
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are independently drawn from a Pólya ensemble of size
N
with weight
\omega
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{m}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\right)=\frac{\det\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{(N)}_{m}[\omega](\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\right)}{\det\left(\mathrm{Q}_{m}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\right)\right)}.

(2.59)

In fact, we will prove a more general form of this result (
Theorem 3.1
) in
section 3
. We nevertheless state this simpler version here for brevity. From that result, we next derive the asymptotic expansion for the first moment of the winding number (
2.3
).

In the Gaussian case, the following exact expression holds for the 2-matrix model (
2.11
) corresponding to the partition function (
2.12
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\right)=\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{\nu\left(p\right)^{\dagger}\nu^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{\left\|\nu\left(p\right)\right\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}p\Bigg)N.

(2.60)

The factor in parentheses corresponds to the
Aharonov-Anandan phase angle
associated with the complex line bundle

\pi:\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\nu)\to\mathrm{S}^{1},

(2.61)

whose fiber at
p
is the complex line generated by
\nu(p)
.

To make this geometric interpretation explicit, write

r(p):=\left\|\nu(p)\right\|,\quad\psi(p):=\frac{\nu(p)}{\left\|\nu(p)\right\|},

(2.62)

so that

\frac{\nu^{\dagger}\nu^{\prime}}{\nu^{\dagger}\nu}=\frac{(r\psi)^{\dagger}(r^{\prime}\psi+r\psi^{\prime})}{r^{2}}=\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}+\psi^{\dagger}\psi^{\prime}.

(2.63)

The first term on the right hand side integrates to zero since
r
is a smooth, positive function on
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, i.e.,

\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{r^{\prime}(p)}{r(p)}\mathrm{d}p=0.

(2.64)

Differentiating the identity
\psi^{\dagger}\psi=1
with respect to
p
shows that
\psi^{\dagger}\psi^{\prime}
is purely imaginary. Hence, there exists a real-valued function
\mathcal{A}
such that
\psi^{\dagger}\psi^{\prime}=i\mathcal{A}
which defines the
Aharonov-Anandan connection

\mathcal{A}:=i\left<\psi(p),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\psi(p)\right>=i\psi(p)^{\dagger}\psi^{\prime}(p),

(2.65)

and consequently

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{\nu\left(p\right)^{\dagger}\nu^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{\left\|\nu\left(p\right)\right\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}p=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\mathcal{A}(p)\mathrm{d}p.

(2.66)

This expression sheds light on the geometric nature of the coefficient in (
2.60
): once multiplied by
2\pi
and exponentiated it yields the holonomy (i.e., total phase change) acquired by a normalised section of the line bundle
\pi
transported once along the unit circle.
In the adiabatic limit, this phase reduces to the
Berry phase
. In the present setting, however, we do not restrict ourselves to adiabatic evolution so it should be interpreted as an Aharonov-Anandan phase.

A thorough treatment of these notions: Aharonov-Anandan and Berry phases, holonomy, vector bundles and their realisations in quantum systems may be found in
(
6
, Chap. 4-7,12)
.

When we introduce the modified vector field

\displaystyle\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)=\begin{pmatrix}|a\left(p\right)|^{\gamma-1}a(p)\\

Theorem 2.9

The average winding number associated with the determinantal curve of an additive
2

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\right)=&\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)^{\dagger}\nu_{\gamma}^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{\left\|\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)\right\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}p\Bigg)N\\
&+\frac{\gamma-1-2\delta}{2}\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{\kappa^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{\kappa\left(p\right)}\frac{|a\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma}-|b\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma}}{|a\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma}+|b\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma}}\mathrm{d}p\Bigg)+o\left(1\right).\end{split}

(2.69)

where the coefficient appearing in the leading order term may be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)^{\dagger}\nu_{\gamma}^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{\left\|\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)\right\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}p=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{|a\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma-2}a^{\prime}\left(p\right)\overline{a\left(p\right)}+|b\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma-2}b^{\prime}\left(p\right)\overline{b\left(p\right)}}{|a\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma}+|b\left(p\right)|^{2\gamma}}\mathrm{d}p.

Remark 2.10

The integral appearing in the subleading order correction term must be interpreted in the sense of a Cauchy principal value integral whenever

or
b

It should be noted that
\left\|\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)\right\|

, since by assumption the parameter functions
a
and
b
never vanish simultaneously. The term (

More interestingly, when neither

around the origin.
The deformation is controlled by a weight that measures the local asymmetry between

\nu
dominates in magnitude.

Finally, this interpretation as a weighted winding number around the origin ceases to apply when either
a
or
b
vanishes, since
\kappa
takes values in the projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\cong\mathrm{S}^{2}
which is homotopically trivial. In that case, a winding number around a given point is no longer well-defined.

Example 2.11

We conclude by illustrating the previous
Theorem 2.9
with a simple and explicit choice of parameter functions
a
and
b
,

\displaystyle a\left(p\right)=r_{1}p^{n}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad b\left(p\right)=r_{2}p^{m},

(2.71)

where
r_{1},r_{2}>0
and
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
are constants. A direct calculation shows that for
N\to\infty
the following expansion holds

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\right)=\Bigg(\frac{nr_{1}^{2\gamma}+mr_{2}^{2\gamma}}{r_{1}^{2\gamma}+r_{2}^{2\gamma}}\Bigg)N+\frac{\left(\gamma-1-2\delta\right)\left(n-m\right)}{2}\Bigg(\frac{r_{1}^{2\gamma}-r_{2}^{2\gamma}}{r_{1}^{2\gamma}+r_{2}^{2\gamma}}\Bigg)+o\left(1\right).

(2.72)

In the particular case where
r_{1}=r_{2}
, the average winding number becomes a half-integer which is noteworthy, although this behaviour does not necessarily hold in the general case.

Remark 2.12

In the previous example it appears that the leading order may vanish while the subleading order term persists, for example setting
r_{1}=2^{1/2\gamma},\,r_{2}=1
and
2n=-m
yields an average winding number asymptotically equal to
n(\gamma-1-2\delta)/2
as
N
goes to infinity.

2.5
The Ginibre random field

We now analyse the partition function (
2.6
) in the case
m=1
and its associated 1-point correlation function (
2.8
) beyond the additive
2
-matrix model framework and consider a matrix-valued Gaussian random field over the unit circle
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, whose entries are independent and identically distributed complex Gaussian random fields.

A complex Gaussian random field is uniquely determined by its mean, covariance and pseudo-covariance kernels. In the present case the mean function is identically zero and the covariance/pseudo-covariance kernels are given by

\mathbb{E}\left(K_{i,j}\left(p\right)\overline{K_{k,\ell}}\left(q\right)\right)=\mathrm{C}(p,q)\delta_{i,k}\delta_{j,\ell}\,\text{ and }\,\mathbb{E}\left(K_{i,j}\left(p\right)K_{k,\ell}\left(q\right)\right)=0.

(2.73)

for all indices
i,j,k,\ell\in\left[1,N\right]
and points
p,q\in\mathrm{S}^{1}
.
The covariance kernel
\mathrm{C}
is Hermitian and positive-definite, in particular:

\mathrm{C}(q,p)=\overline{\mathrm{C}(p,q)}\,\text{ and }\,\mathrm{C}(p,p)\geq 0.

(2.74)

At first glance the choice of a null pseudo-covariance function might seem restrictive but it actually reflects the independence of the real and imaginary parts of each entry in the definition of the Ginibre Unitary Ensemble. This setting naturally generalizes the construction studied in
20
;
22
, which may be recovered by specifying

\mathrm{C}(p,q)=\nu(q)^{\dagger}\nu(p).

(2.75)

A random field
K
as in (
2.73
) may be referred to as a
complex Ginibre random field
(of size
N
with covariance kernel
\mathrm{C}
over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
). To analyse functionals of this random field, we sample it at finitely many points on the base manifold
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, say
n\geq 1
. The result joint distribution of the random matrices
K(p_{1}),\ldots,K(p_{n})
admits a finite-dimensional representation in terms of
n
independent GinUE-drawn random matrices. This construction is analogous to a Karhunen-Loève expansion, see for example
(
35
, Sec. 3.2)
.

Lemma 2.13

Let
n
be a strictly positive integer and
\mathbf{p}=\left(p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}\right)
be a collection of
n
points on the unit circle
\mathrm{S}^{1}
. We assume that the covariance function
\mathrm{C}
is twice differentiable on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Then, there exist a unique lower-triangular matrix
\mathrm{L}
of size
n
whose entries are twice differentiable complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that for all indices
i,j
in
[1,n]

\mathrm{C}(p_{i},p_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{min}(i,j)}\mathrm{L}_{i,k}(\mathbf{p})\overline{\mathrm{L}_{j,k}(\mathbf{p})}.

(2.76)

If
G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}
are
n
random matrices identically and independently drawn from the
{\rm GinUE}
of size
N
, then

\Big(K\left(p_{1}\right),\cdots,K\left(p_{n}\right)\Big)\overset{\rm d}{=}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{1,j}(\mathbf{p})G_{j},\ldots,\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\mathrm{L}_{n,j}(\mathbf{p})G_{j}\right).

(2.77)

Proof.

Making use of the entrywise independence of the random field
K
, we start by fixing two indices
i,j
in
[1,n]
and defining the random vector

K_{i,j}(\mathbf{p})=(K_{i,j}(p_{1}),\ldots,K_{i,j}(p_{n}))^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Its covariance matrix is
\mathrm{C}_{n}(\mathbf{p})=(\mathrm{C}(p_{k},p_{\ell}))_{1\leq k,\ell\leq n}
which is Hermitian and positive definite. Therefore the Cholesky decomposition ensures the existence of a unique lower-triangular matrix
\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{p})
with positive diagonal entries such that
\mathrm{C}_{n}(\mathbf{p})=\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{p})\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{p})^{\dagger}
.

Consequently, the random vector
\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}K_{i,j}(\mathbf{p})
is formed of independent complex standard Gaussian random variables, from which the stated distributional equality (
2.77
) follows.

The differentiability of
\mathrm{L}_{i,j}
in
\mathbf{p}
is inherited from the second-order differentiability of
\mathrm{C}
through the recursive construction of the Cholesky coefficients. Explicitly, for
1\leq j<i\leq n
we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{L}_{1,1}(\mathbf{p})=&\sqrt{\mathrm{C}(p_{1},p_{1})},\\
\mathrm{L}_{i,i}(\mathbf{p})=&\sqrt{\mathrm{C}(p_{i},p_{i})-\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}|\mathrm{L}_{i,k}(\mathbf{p})|^{2}},\\
\mathrm{L}_{i,j}(\mathbf{p})=&\mathrm{L}_{i,i}(\mathbf{p})^{-1}\left(\mathrm{C}(p_{i},p_{j})-\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\overline{\mathrm{L}_{i,k}(\mathbf{p})}\mathrm{L}_{j,k}(\mathbf{p})\right),\end{split}

(2.78)

coupled to the fact that
\mathrm{C}
being positive-definite: all the principal minors of
\mathrm{C}_{n}(\mathbf{p})
are positive. Therefore the square-roots as well as the inverses are well-defined.
∎

Proposition 2.14

Let
K
be a centered complex Ginibre random field of size
N
with covariance kernel
\mathrm{C}
and null pseudo-covariance kernel over
\mathrm{S}^{1}
, and
p,q
be two points on the unit circle. Then the partition function and the average winding number are given by

\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{1}\left(p,q\right)=\Bigg(\frac{\mathrm{C}\left(p,q\right)}{\mathrm{C}\left(q,q\right)}\Bigg)^{N}\text{ and }\,\,\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\right)=\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathrm{S}^{1}}\frac{\partial_{1}\mathrm{C}\left(p,p\right)}{\mathrm{C}\left(p,p\right)}\mathrm{d}p\Bigg)N.

(2.79)

Proof.

From
2.13
, the pair
(K(p),K(q))
has the same distribution as

\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{C}(q,q)}G_{1},\frac{\mathrm{C}(p,q)G_{1}+\sqrt{\mathrm{C}(q,q)\mathrm{C}(p,p)-|\mathrm{C}(p,q)|^{2}}\,G_{2}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{C}(q,q)}}\right)

where
G_{1},G_{2}
are independently drawn from the GinUE of size
N
. Substituting this representation into the definition of the partition function yields

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}_{1}^{\left(N\right)}\left(p,q\right)=\mathrm{C}(q,q)^{-N}\mathbb{E}\Big(\det(\mathrm{C}(p,q)I_{N}+\sqrt{\mathrm{C}(q,q)\mathrm{C}(p,p)-|\mathrm{C}(p,q)|^{2}}\,G_{1}^{-1}G_{2})\Big).

The distribution of the matrix
Y=G_{1}^{-1}G_{2}
coincides with that of the complex spherical ensemble. In particular, its law is
\mathrm{U}(1)
-invariant in the following sense: for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi[
,
Y\overset{d}{=}e^{i\theta}Y
. This implies that only the scalar term
\mathrm{C}(p,q)
contributes in the expectation, leading to the first identity (
2.79
). The second identity follows directly by plugging (
2.79
) into (
2.3
) and (
2.8
).
∎

Remark 2.15

As mentioned previously, one recovers the Ginibre additive 2-matrix model by setting
C(p,q)=\nu(q)^{\dagger}\nu(p)
. In that case, a short computation shows that

\frac{\partial_{1}\mathrm{C}\left(p,p\right)}{\mathrm{C}\left(p,p\right)}=\frac{\nu(p)^{\dagger}\nu^{\prime}(p)}{\nu(p)^{\dagger}\nu(p)},

(2.80)

which agrees with the identity (
2.60
). The Karhunen-Loève-type construction used in this subsection is, however, essentially restricted to the case
m=1
: for partition functions involving more than a single pair of points on the unit circle, a distributional identity of the form (
2.77
) is no longer available. Furthermore, a comparison with the result (
2.69
) for the 2-matrix Muttalib-Borodin ensemble suggests that
\nu_{\gamma}\left(q\right)^{\dagger}\nu_{\gamma}\left(p\right)
plays the role of an effective covariance kernel
C(p,q)
beyond the Gaussian setting.

Theorem 3.1

(Determinantal Structure of the Partition Function)

Let

are independently drawn from a Pólya ensemble of size

\omega
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\widetilde{\mathbf{p}},\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}\right)=(-1)^{m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\det\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}-\mathrm{R}^{\left(N\right)}_{m_{2},m_{1}}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{p}},\mathbf{p}\right)&\overline{\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{\left(N\right)}_{m_{2}}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{p}},\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}\right)\\
\\
\hline\cr\overset{}{\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{\left(N\right)}_{m_{1}}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\right)}&\mathrm{T}^{\left(N\right)}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\left[\widehat{\omega}\right]\left(\mathbf{q},\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}\right)\end{array}\right)}{\det\Big(\mathrm{Q}_{m_{1}}\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}\right)\Big)\det\Big(\overline{\mathrm{Q}_{m_{2}}}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{p}},\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}\right)\Big)},

where the Cauchy-like kernel
\mathrm{Q}_{m}
has been defined at (
2.54
).

Theorem 2.8
then follows directly by setting
m_{1}=m
and
m_{2}=0
. Under the assumptions of Definition (
2.50
), the three kernel functions are well-defined at all points
(q_{i},\widetilde{q}_{j})
such that
b(q_{i})=0
or
b(\widetilde{q}_{j})=0
, which guarantees the existence of the integral representation.

4
Asymptotic expansion of the average winding number and proof of
Theorem 2.9

We now restrict the asymptotic analysis to the case where the source random matrices
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are independently drawn from the Muttalib-Borodin ensemble of Laguerre type presented at (
2.39
).

The associated Pólya weight is

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{MB}}\left(t\right)=\frac{\gamma}{\Gamma((\delta+1)/\gamma)}t^{\delta}e^{-t^{\gamma}}\quad\text{with }\gamma>0\text{ and }\delta>-1,

(4.1)

where the parameters
\delta
and
\gamma
remain fixed as
N\to+\infty
and we have set
\alpha=1
since, as aforementioned, the global scaling factor drops out in this model.

In most of the literature, the parameter
\gamma
is denoted by
\theta
and the parameter
\delta
is denoted by
\nu
. Here we deliberately use a different notation to avoid confusion with the complex-valued vector
\nu
that appears throughout the paper and to emphasize that
\gamma
plays the role of a tail parameter controlling the decay rate of the mean level density at infinity.

As we restrict ourselves to the sub-case
m_{1}=m
and
m_{2}=0
in
Theorem 3.1
, the asymptotic behaviour of
\Upsilon_{N}
(defined in (
2.55
)) evaluated on the diagonal suffices. We focus on the first moment of the winding number, that is, in the derivative

\mathrm{C}^{\left(N\right)}_{1}\left(p\right)=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\mathscr{Z}^{\left(N\right)}_{\mathrm{1}}\left(p,q\right)\right|_{q=p}=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}p}\left[\left(\frac{b(p)}{b(q)}\right)^{N}\biggl(1+\frac{\kappa(p)-\kappa(q)}{\kappa(p)}\Upsilon_{N}(\kappa(p),\kappa(q))\biggl)\right]_{q=p}.

(4.2)

The derivative in the second term must be in prefactor
\kappa(p)=a(p)/b(p)
, otherwise the difference would vanish. Hence

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p)=&N\frac{b^{\prime}(p)}{b(p)}+\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(p)}{\kappa(p)}\Upsilon_{N}(\kappa(p),\kappa(p))=N\frac{a^{\prime}(p)}{a(p)}-\frac{\kappa^{\prime}(p)}{\kappa(p)}\Upsilon_{N}\left(\frac{1}{\kappa(p)},\frac{1}{\kappa(p)}\right)\end{split}

(4.3)

where the second equality uses the symmetry relation (
2.58
), highlighting the interchange symmetry between
a(p)
and
b(p)
.

Since
\Upsilon_{N}
depends on
\kappa
only through its modulus, we define

\Xi_{N}(A)=\frac{1}{N}\Upsilon_{N}(\sqrt{A},\sqrt{A})\,\text{ and }\,A=|\kappa(p)|^{2}=|a(p)/b(p)|^{2}.

(4.4)

This normalization is for convenience:
\Xi_{N}(A)
is an averaged quantity that remains of order one as
N
goes to infinity.

Remark 4.1

When interchanging differentiation in
p
with integration or expectation, one must take care at points where
a(p)=0
or
b(p)=0
. These cases never occur simultaneously by assumption (
2.1
). When one of the two coefficient functions vanishes the logarithmic derivative

\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\frac{\det K(p)}{\det K(q)}\right|_{q=p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{a^{\prime}(p)}{b(p)}{\rm tr}(K_{2}^{-1}K_{1})+N\frac{b^{\prime}(p)}{b(p)},&a(p)=0,\\
\displaystyle N\frac{a^{\prime}(p)}{a(p)}+\frac{b^{\prime}(p)}{a(p)}{\rm tr}(K_{1}^{-1}K_{2}),&b(p)=0,\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

remains finite and analytic. The same holds if the expectation is taken before differentiation and in the limit
q\to p
, as seen from (
4.3
). Moreover,
\Xi_{N}(|\kappa(p)|^{2})=\Upsilon_{N}(\kappa(p),\kappa(p))=O(|a(p)|^{2\delta+2})
when
a(p)\to 0
and similarly when
b(p)\to 0
, ensuring pointwise convergence and integrability. Therefore, differentiation and expectation may safely be interchanged.

Using previous equations (
2.41
) and (
2.42
) for Muttalib-Borodin self-inverse-convoluted Pólya weights one obtains

\Xi_{N}(A)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\mathcal{M}[\widehat{\omega}_{\mathrm{MB}}](k,A)}{\mathcal{M}[\widehat{\omega}_{\mathrm{MB}}](k)}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right),

(4.6)

where
\varphi_{N}
is defined as a Laplace-type integral

\displaystyle\varphi_{N}\left(\tau,A\right)=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\gamma\ln(A)}h\left(x\right)\exp\left(-N\,F_{\tau}\left(x\right)/\gamma\right)\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}h\left(x\right)\exp\left(-N\,F_{\tau}\left(x\right)/\gamma\right)\mathrm{d}x}=1-\varphi_{N}\left(1-\tau,A^{-1}\right)\in[0,1],

(4.7)

with

\displaystyle\tau=\frac{2k-1}{2N}\in]0,1[,\ h\left(x\right)=\left(2\cosh(x/2)\right)^{-(2\delta+1)/\gamma},\ F_{\tau}\left(x\right)=\ln\left(1+e^{x}\right)-\tau x.

(4.8)

The substitution
t=e^{x/\gamma}
in the Mellin integrals leads precisely to this form. The shift
k\mapsto k-\frac{1}{2}
simply enforces symmetry about
\tau=1/2
, or equivalently about
k=(N+1)/2
under the reflection
A\leftrightarrow A^{-1}
. In particular, the auxiliary functions satisfy
h(x)=h(-x)
,
F_{\tau}(x)=F_{1-\tau}(-x)
and
\varphi_{N}(\tau,A)=1-\varphi_{N}(1-\tau,A^{-1})
.

For a given
\tau\in]0,1[
, the function
F_{\tau}
has a unique critical point at

x_{*}(\tau)=\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{1-\tau}\right)\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\tau=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x_{*}(\tau)}}.

(4.9)

When
x_{*}\left(\tau\right)>\gamma\ln(A)
, the main contribution arises from the upper boundary of the integration domain corresponding to

\tau_{0}=\frac{A^{\gamma}}{1+A^{\gamma}}\in]0,1[\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad A=\left(\frac{\tau_{0}}{1-\tau_{0}}\right)^{1/\gamma}.

(4.10)

The region where contributions remain unsuppressed has width of order
1/\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}
around this boundary.
Hence, the boundary regime
\min\{\tau_{0},1-\tau_{0}\}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})
, or equivalently
\min\{A,A^{-1}\}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/\gamma})
must be treated separately in
subsection 4.5
.

We therefore decompose the sum over
k
into three parts:

\sum_{k=1}^{N}=\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+1/2\rfloor}}_{=S_{-}}+\underbrace{\sum_{k=\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+3/2\rfloor}^{\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})-1/2\rceil}}_{=S_{0}}+\underbrace{\sum_{k=\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})+1/2\rceil}^{N}}_{=S_{+}}

(4.11)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}=N^{\varepsilon-1/2}\sqrt{\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}

(4.12)

for some fixed
\varepsilon>0
such that
N^{\varepsilon}=o\left(\left(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\right)^{1/12}\right)
. Here
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
and
\lceil\cdot\rceil
denote the floor and ceiling functions.

We assume that
N
is large enough so that
\Delta_{N}<\min\{\tau_{0},1-\tau_{0}\}
, ensuring that the summation ranges of
S_{-}
and
S_{+}
are non-empty. The condition (
4.12
) forces
\varepsilon
to be sufficiently small so that the integration boundary
A
lies within a neighbourhood of the critical point where the Gaussian approximation holds uniformly and the associated error terms stay negligible.

The sum
S_{-}
can be related to
S_{+}
through the identity

\begin{split}S_{-}=&\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+1/2\rfloor}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right)\\
=&\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+1/2\rfloor-\sum_{k=\lceil N(1-\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+1/2\rceil}^{N}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A^{-1}\right)\end{split}

(4.13)

which follows from the reflection property
\varphi_{N}(\tau,A)=1-\varphi_{N}(1-\tau,A^{-1})
. This relation expresses the lower tail
S_{-}
in terms of the upper one evaluated at the inverted parameter
A^{-1}
.

•

The sum
S_{+}
corresponds to the exponentially suppressed tail contributions and is further analysed in
subsection 4.1
.

•

The expansion of the denominator integral in (
4.7
) is presented in
subsection 4.2
.

•

The main contribution arises from the central sum
S_{0}
: the denominator integral in (
4.7
) admits a uniform Gaussian approximation in a neighbourhood of the boundary
A
, see
subsection 4.3
, while the discrete sum over
k
is handled using an Euler-MacLaurin approximation scheme in
subsection 4.4
.

4.1
Asymptotic behaviour of the tails

We now assume that
A=o(N^{1/\gamma})
. The leading contribution to the integral in the numerator of (
4.7
) for

\tau=\frac{2k-1}{2N}>\frac{2\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})+1/2\rceil-1}{2N}>\tau_{0}

(4.14)

comes from
x=\gamma\ln(A)
since
F_{\tau}(x)
is strictly decreasing for
x<\gamma\ln(A)=x_{*}(\tau_{0})<x_{*}(\tau)
. Additionally,
F^{\prime}(x)=1/(1+e^{-x})-\tau\leq F^{\prime}(\gamma\ln(A))=\tau_{0}-\tau<0
and
F^{\prime}(x)
is strictly increasing for
x\leq\gamma\ln(A)
, and
h(x)
is bounded from above on
\mathbb{R}
by
2^{-(2\delta+1)/\gamma}
.
Thus, the integrand is bounded from above by

\begin{split}h(x)\exp\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}(F(x_{*}(\tau))-F(x))\right)\leq&2^{-(2\delta+1)/\gamma}\exp\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\int_{x}^{x_{*}(\tau)}F^{\prime}(y){\rm d}y\right)\\
\leq&2^{-(2\delta+1)/\gamma}\exp\left(-\frac{N}{\gamma}(\tau-\tau_{0})(x_{*}(\tau)-x)\right).\end{split}

(4.15)

It follows that the corresponding summand satisfies

\varphi_{N}(\tau,A)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}(\tau-\tau_{0})}\exp\left[-\frac{N}{\gamma}(\tau-\tau_{0})(x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A))\right]\right).

(4.16)

Since
\tau-\tau_{0}>\Delta_{N}/2
and

x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A)=\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\frac{\mathrm{d}\rho}{\rho(1-\rho)}>\frac{\tau-\tau_{0}}{\max\{\tau(1-\tau),\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\}},

(4.17)

the exponential factor behaves like

N(\tau-\tau_{0})(x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A))>\frac{N(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}}{\max\{\tau(1-\tau),\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\}}>\gamma c_{2}N^{2\varepsilon},

(4.18)

as in (
4.12
). Hence, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that the terms decay exponentially as
\mathcal{O}(N^{c_{1}}e^{-c_{2}N^{2\varepsilon}})
.

Consequently, for the sums
S_{-}
and
S_{+}
, by equation (
4.13
) and a similar argument under the transformation
(\tau_{0},A)\to(1-\tau_{0},A^{-1})
, we obtain

S_{+}=\mathcal{O}(N^{1+c_{1}}e^{-c_{2}N^{2\varepsilon}})\quad{\rm and}\quad S_{-}=\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+1/2\rfloor-\mathcal{O}(N^{1+c_{1}}e^{-c_{2}N^{2\varepsilon}}),

(4.19)

where for
S_{-}
we assume
A^{-1}\underset{N\to\infty}{=}o(N^{1/\gamma})
.

4.2
Asymptotic expansion of the denominator

Again we assume
\max\{A,A^{-1}\}=o(N^{1/\gamma})
. A Laplace approximation naturally reveals the magnitude of the dominant contributions. For each
\tau\in]0,1[
, the function
F_{\tau}(\cdot)
has a unique critical point

\displaystyle x_{*}\left(\tau\right)=\ln(\tau)-\ln(1-\tau)

(4.20)

with

F^{\prime\prime}_{\tau}\left(x_{*}\right)=\frac{1}{4\cosh^{2}(x_{*}/2)}=(1-\tau)\tau>0

(4.21)

and higher derivatives given by

\begin{split}F^{(3)}_{\tau}\left(x_{*}\right)=&(1-\tau)\tau(1-2\tau),\\
F^{(4)}_{\tau}\left(x_{*}\right)=&(1-\tau)\tau[1-6(1-\tau)\tau],\end{split}

(4.22)

For each integer
n\geq 2
, there exists a polynomial
P_{n}
such that

F^{(n)}_{\tau}(x_{*})=\tau(1-\tau)P_{n}(\tau).

(4.23)

Consequently, there exist constants
c_{n}>0
so that

\left|F^{(n)}_{\tau}(x_{*})\right|\leq c_{n}\tau(1-\tau)\,\text{ for all }\tau\in[0,1].

(4.24)

The map
\tau\mapsto x_{*}\left(\tau\right)
is a strictly increasing
\mathscr{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism from
\left]0,1\right[
to
\mathbb{R}
.
Applying Laplace’s method to the denominator of (
4.7
) gives

\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}h\left(x\right)\exp\left(-\frac{N}{\gamma}\,F_{\tau}\left(x\right)\right)\mathrm{d}x\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\gamma}{N}}[(1-\tau)\tau]^{(2\delta+1-\gamma)/(2\gamma)}e^{-NF_{\tau}(x_{*})}\\
&\times\biggl[1+\frac{\chi(\tau,\delta,\gamma)}{24N\gamma(1-\tau)\tau}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}\tau^{2}(1-\tau)^{2}}\right)\biggl]\end{split}

(4.25)

which is valid whenever
\tau
and
1-\tau
are much larger than
1/N
: for any fixed
0<\varepsilon<1
, the error term is uniform for
\tau\in[N^{\varepsilon-1},1-N^{\varepsilon-1}]
.

Here

\chi(\tau,\delta,\gamma)=12(1+\delta)\delta(1-2\tau)^{2}+2(12\delta\gamma+6\gamma-\gamma^{2}-6)(1-\tau)\tau+3+2\gamma^{2}-6\gamma-12\delta\gamma

(4.26)

For this expansion, we used

\begin{split}h(x_{*})=&[(1-\tau)\tau]^{(2\delta+1)/(2\gamma)},\\
h^{\prime}(x_{*})=&\frac{2\delta+1}{2\gamma}(1-2\tau)[(1-\tau)\tau]^{(2\delta+1)/(2\gamma)},\\
h^{\prime\prime}(x_{*})=&\frac{2\delta+1}{4\gamma^{2}}[1+2\delta(1-2\tau)^{2}-4(1+\gamma)(1-\tau)\tau][(1-\tau)\tau]^{(2\delta+1)/(2\gamma)}.\end{split}

(4.27)

The regime
\tau=\mathcal{O}(1/N)
can be neglected, since such terms only contribute to
S_{-}
and
S_{+}
both already handled in
subsection 4.1
under the assumption
\max\{A,A^{-1}\}\underset{N\to\infty}{=}o(N^{1/\gamma})
.

4.3
Asymptotic expansion of the integral at the edge

We choose

\tau=\frac{2k-1}{2N}\in\left[\frac{2\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+3/2\rfloor-1}{2N},\frac{2\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})-1/2\rceil-1}{2N}\right]

(4.28)

so that
|\tau-\tau_{0}|\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{N})=o(1)
.
Consequently,

|x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A)|\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\Delta_{N}}{\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right)

(4.29)

since the distance
\left|x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A)\right|
depends on
N
through
\left|\tau-\tau_{0}\right|
, and
\left|\tau-\tau_{0}\right|=\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{N})
.

Expanding the integrand about
x_{*}(\tau)
over an interval of size

\Delta A_{N}=\frac{\Delta_{N}\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0}))}{\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}=\frac{N^{\varepsilon}\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0}))}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}

(4.30)

shows that the leading contribution to the integral arises from the vicinity of the upper endpoint
x=\gamma\ln(A)
. The convergence of the expansion in the interval
[\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N},\gamma\ln(A)]
is uniform, since for all
n\geq 0

\begin{split}NF_{\tau}^{(n+3)}(x_{*}(\tau))\xi^{n+3}=&\mathcal{O}(N\tau(1-\tau)[\Delta A_{N}]^{n+3})\\
\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&\mathcal{O}(N^{(n+3)\varepsilon}[N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})]^{-(n+1)/2}[\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0}))]^{n+3})\end{split}

(4.31)

which indeed converges to zero. This follows from the choice of scaling (
4.12
) and from the limits
\Delta_{N}/\tau_{0}\to 0
,
\Delta_{N}/(1-\tau_{0})\to 0
and
N\tau_{0}\to\infty
.

We split the function
\varphi_{N}
in two parts:

\varphi_{N}(\tau,A)=\varphi_{N}(\tau,e^{-\Delta A_{N}/\gamma}A)+\frac{\int_{\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N}}^{\gamma\ln(A)}h\left(x\right)\exp\left(-N\,F_{\tau}\left(x\right)/\gamma\right)\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}h\left(x\right)\exp\left(-N\,F_{\tau}\left(x\right)/\gamma\right)\mathrm{d}x}

(4.32)

For
x<\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N}
, one has
x<x_{*}(\tau)
with a distance
|x-x_{*}(\tau)|\gg\Delta_{N}/[\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})]
, which allows us to bound the integrand uniformly similarly to (
4.15
) with
\gamma\ln(A)
replaced by
\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N}
.

By setting

x_{*}(\widetilde{\tau}_{N})=\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N},

(4.33)

we then obtain

\varphi_{N}(\tau,N^{-\Delta_{N}/\gamma}A)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}(\tau-\widetilde{\tau}_{N})}\exp\left[-N(\tau-\widetilde{\tau}_{N})\Delta A_{N}\right]\right).

(4.34)

The right-hand side is exponentially small since
\tau-\widetilde{\tau}_{N}=(\tau-\tau_{0})+(\tau_{0}-\widetilde{\tau}_{N})
with
\tau-\tau_{0}=\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{N})
, while we estimate the second term by noting that
\Delta A_{N}=o(x_{*}(\tau_{0}))
as
N
goes to infinity which implies

\begin{split}\tau_{0}-\widetilde{\tau}_{N}=&\int_{x_{*}(\tau_{0})-\Delta A_{N}}^{x_{*}(\tau_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{4\cosh^{2}(x/2)}\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}(\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\Delta A_{N}),\end{split}

(4.35)

since
1/[4\cosh^{2}(x_{*}(\tau_{0})/2)]=\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})
. Therefore,

\varphi_{N}(\tau,N^{-\Delta_{N}/\gamma}A)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}(N^{c_{3}}e^{-c_{4}N^{2\varepsilon}[\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0}))]^{2}})

(4.36)

for some positive constants
c_{3},c_{4}
.

We now expand the integrand around

x=x_{*}(\tau)-\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{N(1-\tau)\tau}}\xi

(4.37)

over the interval
[\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N},\gamma\ln(A)]
, using the earlier expansions (
4.21
), (
4.22
) and (
4.27
). The error remains uniform in this region.
The upper-limit in
\xi
,

\sqrt{\frac{N(1-\tau)\tau}{\gamma}}[x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A)+\Delta A_{N}]\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}(N^{\varepsilon}\ln[N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})]),

(4.38)

can be safely extended to
+\infty
with an exponentially small error of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{c_{5}}e^{-c_{6}N^{2\varepsilon}[\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})]^{2}})
for some positive constants
c_{5},c_{6}
.
However, the lower limit

\xi_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{N(1-\tau)\tau}{\gamma}}[x_{*}(\tau)-\gamma\ln(A)]=\sqrt{\frac{N(1-\tau)\tau}{\gamma}}[x_{*}(\tau)-x_{*}(\tau_{0})]

(4.39)

must be retained, as it can be of order one or smaller.

After normalisation by the leading term from (
4.25
), we obtain

\begin{split}I=&\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi\gamma}}[(1-\tau)\tau]^{-(2\delta+1-\gamma)/(2\gamma)}\int_{\gamma\ln(A)-\Delta A_{N}}^{\gamma\ln(A)}h\left(x\right)\exp\left[\frac{N}{\gamma}(F_{\tau}\left(x_{*}(\tau)\right)-F_{\tau}\left(x\right))\right]\mathrm{d}x\\
\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&\int_{\xi_{0}}^{+\infty}\biggl[1+\frac{C_{1,1}\xi+C_{1,3}\xi^{3}}{\sqrt{N\tau(1-\tau)}}+\frac{C_{2,2}\xi^{2}+C_{2,4}\xi^{4}+C_{2,6}\xi^{6}}{N\tau(1-\tau)}\biggl]e^{-\xi^{2}/2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\\
&+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{9\varepsilon}[\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0}))]^{9}}{[N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})]^{3/2}}\right)\end{split}

(4.40)

where the coefficients are

\begin{split}C_{1,1}=&\frac{(2\delta+1)(1-2\tau)}{2\sqrt{\gamma}},\quad C_{1,3}=\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}(2\tau-1)}{6},\\
C_{2,2}=&\frac{(2\delta+1)[1+2\delta(1-2\tau)^{2}-4(1+\gamma)(1-\tau)\tau]}{8\gamma},\\
C_{2,4}=&\frac{6\gamma(1-\tau)\tau-\gamma-2(2\delta+1)(1-2\tau)^{2}}{24},\\
C_{2,6}=&\frac{\gamma(1-2\tau)^{2}}{72}.\end{split}

(4.41)

They satisfy the identity

C_{2,2}+3C_{2,4}+15C_{2,6}=\frac{\chi(\tau,\delta,\gamma)}{24\gamma}

(4.42)

with
\chi
defined in (
4.26
).
Integrating by parts over
\xi
and using the complementary error function
\mathrm{erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{x}^{\infty}e^{-\xi^{2}}d\xi
, we obtain the asymptotic form

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}(\tau,A)=&\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{\xi_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\biggl[C_{1,1}+C_{1,3}(\xi_{0}^{2}+2)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\xi_{0}^{5}}{\sqrt{N\tau(1-\tau)}}\right)\biggl]\frac{e^{-\xi_{0}^{2}/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi N\tau(1-\tau)}}\\
&+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{9\varepsilon}[\ln(N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0}))]^{9}}{[N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})]^{3/2}}\right).\end{split}

(4.43)

The prefactor of the complementary error function is exact, as it precisely matches the leading-order term in the denominator expansion. Errors are uniform in
\tau
throughout the interval (
4.28
).

Since
|\tau_{0}-\tau|=\Delta_{N}\ll 1
we expand
\xi_{0}
, see (
4.39
), as follows

\begin{split}\xi_{0}\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}}}(\tau-\tau_{0})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{3\varepsilon}}{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}\right)\end{split}

(4.44)

uniformly in
\tau
. Interestingly, the quadratic term
(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}
cancels in this expansion.
Substituting into the previous result (
4.43
) yields the final asymptotic form:

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}(\tau,A)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}}}(\tau-\tau_{0})\right)+\biggl[C_{1,1}+C_{1,3}\left(\frac{N(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}}{\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}}+2\right)\biggl]\\
&\times\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\exp\left(-\frac{N(\tau-\tau_{0})^{2}}{2\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{5\varepsilon}}{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}\right),\end{split}

(4.45)

which will be used in the evaluation of the remaining sum
S_{0}
, where we used that
\xi_{0}=O(N^{\varepsilon})
.

4.4
Asymptotic expansion of the sum at the edge

The term involving the error function in (
4.45
) requires particular care. Once the discrete sum over
k
is replaced by an integral in the scaled variable
t=\sqrt{N}(\tau-\tau_{0})
with
\tau=(2k-1)/2N
, it ceases to be integrable on the real line. To circumvent this issue, we decompose it as

\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}}}(\tau-\tau_{0})\right)=\mathbf{1}_{\left[0,\infty\right[}(\tau_{0}-\tau)+\frac{{\rm sign}(\tau-\tau_{0})}{2}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}}}|\tau-\tau_{0}|\right)

(4.46)

where
\mathrm{sign}
denotes the signum function.

The special case
N\tau_{0}+1/2\in\mathbb{Z}
corresponds to an exceptional value of the continuous parameter
p
and can therefore be ignored. We may thus assume that

k_{-}=\left\lfloor N\tau_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor<k_{+}=\left\lceil N\tau_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rceil.

(4.47)

The same result extends to the integer case
k_{-}=k_{+}=k_{0}
, although one must then split the sum into two parts with end points
k_{0}=\pm 1
and treat the central term
k=k_{0}
separately.

We now decompose the sum
S_{0}
as

\begin{split}S_{0}=&\sum_{k=K_{-}}^{K_{+}}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right)\\
=&k_{-}-\left\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor+\left[\sum_{k=K_{-}}^{k_{-}}+\sum_{k=k_{+}}^{K_{+}}\right]f\left(\sqrt{N}\left[\frac{2k-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)\\
&+\mathcal{O}\left(N\Delta_{N}\frac{N^{5\varepsilon}}{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}\right)\text{ as }N\text{ goes to }\infty.\end{split}

(4.48)

with

K_{-}=\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+3/2\rfloor,\,K_{+}=\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})-1/2\rceil

(4.49)

and

\begin{split}f(t)=&\frac{{\rm sign}(t)}{2}\mathrm{erfc}\left(\frac{|t|}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\frac{C_{1,1}+C_{1,3}\left(t^{2}+2\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right)\end{split}

(4.50)

where we have set
t=\sqrt{N/[\gamma(1-\tau_{0})\tau_{0}]}(\tau-\tau_{0})
.
This splitting is essential, as the complementary error function is not differentiable at
\tau=\tau_{0}
, corresponding to a summation index lying between
k_{-}
and
k_{+}
.

Using the definition of
\Delta_{N}
(
4.12
), the associated error term is

\mathcal{O}(N^{6\varepsilon}/\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})})

(4.51)

which vanishes for our choice of
\varepsilon
.

We apply the Euler-MacLaurin formula separately for each of the two sums. For the first one we obtain

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=K_{-}}^{k_{-}}f\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)=\int_{K_{-}}^{k_{-}}f\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)\mathrm{d}k\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left[f\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k_{-}-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)+f\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2K_{-}-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)\right]\\
&-\frac{1}{12\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}}\\
&\times\left[f^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k_{-}-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)-f^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2K_{-}-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)\right]\\
&+R,\end{split}

(4.52)

where the remainder is bounded by

\begin{split}|R|\leq&\frac{2\zeta(2)}{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{K_{-}}^{k_{-}}\left|f^{\prime\prime}\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)\right|\mathrm{d}k\\
\leq&\frac{2\zeta(2)}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left|f^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right)\end{split}

(4.53)

where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function
\zeta
, and we used the fact that

t_{-}=\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k_{-}-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]<0<\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k_{+}-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]=t_{+}.

(4.54)

Both
f
and
f^{\prime}
vanish exponentially as
k\to K_{-}
since
t=O(N^{\varepsilon})
. These boundary terms may therefore be neglected.

At
k=k_{-}
, one has
t=O(1/\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})})
, the derivative term is suppressed by its prefactor whereas the value
f(k_{-})
must be retained.
Furthermore, we replace
k
by the integration variable
t
appearing in the argument of
f
. The upper limit becomes
t_{-}
while the lower limit is of order
N^{\varepsilon}
and can therefore be extended to
-\infty
, since this modification only introduces an exponentially small error. Indeed,
f
has a Gaussian decay near infinity.

This yields

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=K_{-}}^{k_{-}}f\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{\gamma\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\left[\frac{2k-1}{2N}-\tau_{0}\right]\right)\\
\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{t_{-}}f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{f(t_{-})}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right).\end{split}

(4.55)

A similar computation for the second sum gives

\begin{split}S_{0}\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&k_{-}-\left\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+\frac{3}{2}\right\rfloor+\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{t_{-}}+\int_{t_{+}}^{+\infty}\right)f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
&+\frac{f(t_{-})+f(t_{+})}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right).\end{split}

(4.56)

Since
t_{\pm}=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})})
, we expand
f
about
t=0
using one-sided derivatives, as
f
is discontinuous at the origin. In particular

\lim_{t\to 0_{\pm}}f(t)=\pm\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right).

(4.57)

This way, we obtain

\begin{split}S_{0}\underset{N\to\infty}{=}&k_{-}-\left\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor+\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
&-\frac{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}(t_{+}+t_{-})}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right).\end{split}

(4.58)

Using
k_{+}=k_{-}+1
and the definitions of
t_{\pm}
, we have

k_{-}-\frac{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\gamma}(t_{+}+t_{-})}{2}=N\tau_{0}

(4.59)

The integral involving the complementary error function in (
4.50
) vanishes identically, since the integrand is odd. The remaining Gaussian contribution can be evaluated explicitly, which gives

\begin{split}S_{0}=&N\tau_{0}-\left\lfloor N(\tau_{0}-\Delta_{N})+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor+\sqrt{\gamma}(C_{1,1}+3C_{1,3})+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right).\end{split}

(4.60)

Combining all three contributions
S_{-}
,
S_{0}
and
S_{+}
we obtain

\Xi_{N}(A)=S_{-}+S_{0}+S_{+}=N\tau_{0}+\sqrt{\gamma}\left[C_{1,1}+3C_{1,3}\right]+o(1)

(4.61)

or equivalently,

\Xi_{N}(A)=\frac{N|a(p)|^{2\gamma}}{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}+\frac{\gamma-2\delta-1}{2}\frac{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}-|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})}}\right).

(4.62)

Substituting this result into the
1
-point correlation function (
4.3
) gives

\begin{split}&\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}N\frac{a^{\prime}(p)\overline{a(p)}|a(p)|^{2\gamma-2}+b^{\prime}(p)\overline{b(p)}|b(p)|^{2\gamma-2}}{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}\\
&+\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(p)}{a(p)}-\frac{b^{\prime}(p)}{b(p)}\right)\biggl[\frac{\gamma-2\delta-1}{2}\frac{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}-|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N\tau_{0}(p)(1-\tau_{0}(p))}}\right)\biggl].\end{split}

(4.63)

These asymptotics hold whenever
N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\to\infty
, i.e. when
N^{1/(2\gamma)}|a(p)|\to\infty
and
N^{1/(2\gamma)}|b(p)|\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
.

When
p_{0}\in{\rm S}^{1}
is a zero of order
m
for either
a
or
b
, we consider the symmetrised combination

\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})+\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})

(4.64)

which, as
N
goes to infinity, expands as

\begin{split}&\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})+\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})\\
+&\frac{imc_{1}-(m+1)c_{2}\delta\varphi}{ic_{1}\delta\varphi-c_{2}\delta\varphi^{2}}\biggl[\frac{\gamma-2\delta-1}{2}\frac{|c_{1}\delta\varphi^{m}|^{2\gamma}-|b(p_{0})+ib^{\prime}(p_{0})\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma}}{|c_{1}\delta\varphi^{m}|^{2\gamma}+|b(p_{0})+ib^{\prime}(p_{0})\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N|\delta\varphi|^{2m\gamma}}}\right)\biggl]\\
-&\frac{imc_{1}+(m+1)c_{2}\delta\varphi}{ic_{1}\delta\varphi+c_{2}\delta\varphi^{2}}\biggl[\frac{\gamma-2\delta-1}{2}\frac{|c_{1}\delta\varphi^{m}|^{2\gamma}-|b(p_{0})-ib^{\prime}(p_{0})\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma}}{|c_{1}\delta\varphi^{m}|^{2\gamma}+|b(p_{0})-ib^{\prime}(p_{0})\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N|\delta\varphi|^{2m\gamma}}}\right)\biggl]\\
=&\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})+\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})-i\frac{(\gamma-2\delta-1)c_{2}}{2c_{1}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{N^{6\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{N|\delta\varphi|^{2m\gamma}}}\right),\end{split}

(4.65)

where we expanded around
p_{0}
such that
a(p_{0})=0
under the assumption that there exists a strictly positive integer
m
such that
a(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})=c_{1}(i\delta\varphi)^{m}+c_{2}(i\delta\varphi)^{m+1}+\mathcal{O}(\delta\varphi^{m+2})
. The same reasoning applies if
b
vanishes under identical conditions. We also denoted the leading order contribution as

\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p)=N\frac{a^{\prime}(p)\overline{a(p)}|a(p)|^{2\gamma-2}+b^{\prime}(p)\overline{b(p)}|b(p)|^{2\gamma-2}}{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}.

(4.66)

This symmetrisation removes the simple pole from the logarithmic derivatives of
a(p)
and
b(p)
provided that
|\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma m}\gg N^{-1}
. In the leading order,
\tau_{0}
is an even function of
\delta\varphi
as
\varphi\to 0
, which implies that
\tau_{0}=\mathcal{O}(|a(p)|^{2\gamma})=\mathcal{O}(|\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma m})
when
a(p_{0})=0
, and similarly,
1-\tau_{0}=\mathcal{O}(|b(p)|^{2\gamma})=\mathcal{O}(|\delta\varphi|^{2\gamma m})
when
b(p)=0
. This symmetrisation underlies the appearance of the Cauchy principal value integral.

Finally, with this symmetrisation, the error term vanishes uniformly when the integration domain is restricted to a set of the form

{\rm S}^{1}_{\eta}=\{p\in{\rm S}^{1}|\,N\tau_{0}(p)(1-\tau_{0}(p))\geq N^{\eta}\}\,\text{ for }\eta\in]0,1].

(4.67)

where

\tau_{0}(p)(1-\tau_{0}(p))=\frac{|a(p)b(p)|^{2\gamma}}{(|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma})^{2}}.

(4.68)

The auxiliary exponent must satisfy
0<\varepsilon<\eta/12
.

In the next subsection, we will show that one can choose
\eta
so that the remaining contribution from the complement
{\rm S}^{1}\setminus{\rm S}^{1}_{\eta}
vanishes, except for the leading term in (
4.63
), proportional to
N
, which reproduces the dominant contribution in (
4.65
).

4.5
Discussion for small parameter amplitudes

The previous analysis ceases to hold when
N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})
is of order one. This regime corresponds to

\min\left(NA^{\gamma},NA^{-\gamma}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}(1)

(4.69)

or equivalently,

\min\left(N^{1/(2\gamma)}|a(p)|,N^{1/(2\gamma)}|b(p)|\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{=}\mathcal{O}(1)

(4.70)

In this situation, the discrete sum can no longer be approximated by an integral: the dominant contributions now arise from the terms near the boundaries
k=1
or
k=N
. By symmetry, it suffices to discuss the case
NA^{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

We assume that
N\tau_{0}(1-\tau_{0})\leq N^{\eta}
with
\eta\in]0,1]
. By definition of
\tau_{0}
, see (
4.10
), it implies
A=\mathcal{O}(N^{-(1-\eta)/\gamma})
and therefore
a(p)=\mathcal{O}(N^{-(1-\eta)/(2\gamma)})
.

Let
p_{0}\in\mathrm{S}^{1}
be such that

a(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})=c_{1}(i\delta\varphi)^{m}+c_{2}(i\delta\varphi)^{m+1}+\mathcal{O}(\delta\varphi^{m+2})\,\text{ as }\delta\varphi\to 0.

(4.71)

Then the size of the corresponding interval in
\delta\varphi
is of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-(1-\eta)/(2m\gamma)})
. Since the zeros of
a
are isolated, one can choose
N
large enough to ensure that the associated intervals around distinct zeros do not overlap.

We now split the sum
\Xi_{N}(A)
, see (
4.4
), into two parts:

\sum_{k=1}^{N}=\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})-1/2\rceil}}_{=\widetilde{S}_{0}}+\underbrace{\sum_{k=\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})+1/2\rceil}^{N}}_{=S_{+}}.

(4.72)

The bound (
4.19
) for
S_{+}
remains valid and shows that this term is exponentially small, uniformly for
N\tau_{0}\leq cN^{\eta}
for some constant
c>0
.
Hence, only the first partial sum
\widetilde{S}_{0}
contributes significantly, where the summation index satisfies
k=\mathcal{O}(N\Delta_{N})=\mathcal{O}(N^{\varepsilon+\eta/2})
as
N
goes to infinity.

Using the explicit Mellin transform (
2.42
) and its corresponding weight (
2.41
), we write

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right)=&\frac{\mathcal{M}[\widehat{\omega}_{\mathrm{MB}}](k,A)}{\mathcal{M}[\widehat{\omega}_{\mathrm{MB}}](k)}\\
&\hskip-28.45274pt=\frac{\gamma\Gamma\left((N+2\delta+1)/\gamma\right)}{\Gamma((\delta+k)/\gamma)\Gamma((N+\delta+1-k)/\gamma)}\int_{0}^{A}t^{\delta+k}\left(1+t^{\gamma}\right)^{-(N+2\delta+1)/\gamma}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}.\end{split}

(4.73)

To apply Stirling’s approximation formula to the Gamma functions, we require
k\ll N^{1/2}
. This is satisfied for any auxiliary exponents
0<\varepsilon<\eta/12<1/14
provided that
\eta<6/7
.
Under this condition,

\frac{\Gamma\left((N+2\delta+1)/\gamma\right)}{\Gamma((N+\delta+1-k)/\gamma)}=\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\right)^{(k+\delta)/\gamma}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(N^{2\varepsilon+\eta-1}\right)\right]

(4.74)

uniformly for all
k
in the summation domain of
\widetilde{S}_{0}
.

For the integral, since
t^{\gamma}\leq A^{\gamma}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-(1-\eta)})
as
N
goes to infinity, we may further restrict
\eta<1/2
so that uniformly

\left(1+t^{\gamma}\right)^{-(N+2\delta+1)/\gamma}=\exp\left(-\frac{N}{\gamma}t^{\gamma}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+2\eta})\right).

(4.75)

Substituting
t=(\gamma y/N)^{1/\gamma}
with
y\in[0,1]
gives

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right)=&\frac{1}{\Gamma((\delta+k)/\gamma)}\int_{0}^{NA^{\gamma}/\gamma}y^{(\delta+k)/\gamma}e^{-y}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+2\eta})\right].\end{split}

(4.76)

The integrand attains its maximum at
y=(\delta+k-\gamma)/\gamma=\mathcal{O}(N^{\varepsilon+\eta/2})
, which indeed lies inside the integration domain as long as
\varepsilon+\eta/2<\eta
.
We therefore obtain the following rough upper-bound:

\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right)\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma((\delta+k)/\gamma)}\left(\frac{\delta+k-\gamma}{\gamma e}\right)^{(\delta+k)/\gamma-1}\frac{NA^{\gamma}}{\gamma}\left[1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1+2\eta})\right].

(4.77)

For large
k
, the
k
-dependent part of this bound behaves like

\frac{1}{\Gamma((\delta+k)/\gamma)}\left(\frac{\delta+k-\gamma}{\gamma e}\right)^{(\delta+k)/\gamma}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\pi(\delta+k)}}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)\right].

(4.78)

Therefore, the sum
\widetilde{S}_{0}
can be bounded from above as follows

\widetilde{S}_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{\lceil N(\tau_{0}+\Delta_{N})-1/2\rceil}\varphi_{N}\left(\frac{2k-1}{2N},A\right)\leq c\sqrt{N\Delta_{N}}\,NA^{\gamma}(1+o(1))=\mathcal{O}(N^{\varepsilon+5\eta/4})

(4.79)

for some positive constant
c>0
.
Substituting this bound into the symmetrised correlation function (
4.65
) yields

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})+\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})=&\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})+\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})\\
&\hskip-56.9055pt+\left[\frac{a^{\prime}(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})}{a(p_{0}e^{i\delta\varphi})}+\frac{a^{\prime}(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})}{a(p_{0}e^{-i\delta\varphi})}\right]\mathcal{O}(N^{\varepsilon/2+5\eta/4})+\mathcal{O}(\delta\varphi).\end{split}

(4.80)

Multiplying the second term by the interval length which is of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-(1-\eta)/(2m\gamma)})
, it indeed vanishes provided that

0<\eta<\min\left(12/(31m\gamma+12),1/2\right),\,\text{ where }0<\varepsilon<\eta/12.

(4.81)

Combining this result with the expansion obtained in
subsection 4.4
, and choosing
\eta
accordingly, we decompose
{\rm S}^{1}\setminus{\rm S}^{1}_{\eta}=\Sigma_{a}\cup\Sigma_{b}
where either
a
or
b
vanishes. The average winding number then, asymptotically in
N
, takes the form

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\right)=&\left[\int_{{\rm S}^{1}_{\eta}}+\int_{{\rm S}^{1}\setminus{\rm S}^{1}_{\eta}}\right]\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N)}(p)\mathrm{d}p\\
=&\int_{{\rm S}^{1}}\mathrm{C}_{1}^{(N,0)}(p)\mathrm{d}p+\frac{\gamma-2\delta-1}{2}\int_{{\rm S}^{1}_{\eta}}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}(p)}{a(p)}-\frac{b^{\prime}(p)}{b(p)}\right)\frac{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}-|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}{|a(p)|^{2\gamma}+|b(p)|^{2\gamma}}\mathrm{d}p\\
&+o(1).\end{split}

(4.82)

The integrals around the zeros in the second term are to be understood in the symmetrised sense, which in the limit
N\to\infty
reduces to a Cauchy principal value integral.

5
Conclusion

In this work we have investigated two natural extensions of the Gaussian additive 2-matrix model describing a disordered one-dimensional Hamiltonian in symmetry class AIII of the Altland-Zirnbauer classification.

First, we introduced a general complex Ginibre random field on the unit circle that recovers, as a special case, the standard Ginibre additive 2-matrix model while extending its analytical framework. To assess the universality of the Gaussian results, we then analysed a broader family of non-Gaussian models: Pólya additive 2-matrix models at finite matrix size, focusing on the Muttalib-Borodin subclass in the large-
N
regime. Compared to the GinUE, these random matrix ensembles exhibit different tail behaviour in their mean level density.

For both Gaussian and non-Gaussian settings, we derived explicit analytical expressions for the average winding number of the determinantal curve associated with the disordered bulk Hamiltonian as the quasi-momentum
p
winds once around the unit circle modelling the one-dimensional Brillouin zone. We identified a geometric invariant in the leading term

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big(\mathrm{Wind}_{N}\Big)=\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{{\rm S}^{1}}\frac{\nu\left(p\right)^{\dagger}\nu^{\prime}\left(p\right)}{\left\|\nu\left(p\right)\right\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}p\Bigg)N+\mathcal{O}\left(1\right).

which essentially corresponds to an Aharonov-Anandan geometric phase angle. The subleading
\mathcal{O}\left(1\right)
correction vanishes in the Gaussian case but generally persists for non-Gaussian Pólya ensembles, where it can be interpreted as a re-weighted winding number around the origin. Its magnitude depends on the tail behaviour and repulsion from the origin in the mean level density, both of which determine the typical distance of the determinantal curve from the origin.

A central open question is the universality of the winding-number distribution itself. Under mild regularity conditions, one may expect a central limit theorem to hold, leading to an asymptotically Gaussian law as in the Ginibre additive 2-matrix model for which earlier derivations
22
show that the standard deviation scales as
N^{1/4}
. However, the precise variance and subleading corrections are expected to depend on ensemble-specific details as already highlighted for the mean winding number. This layered asymptotic structure is reminiscent of that observed in large-
N
free-energy expansions of Coulomb gases, where the leading order captures universal mean-field behaviour, while coefficients in higher-order terms are of geometric nature and encode increasingly model-dependent information, see for example
(
34
, Th. 2)
.

Extending this analysis to other symmetry classes such as CII and BDI in one dimension remains a challenging open direction. In these cases, the lack of real and quaternionic analogues of Pólya ensembles of multiplicative type, combined with the Pfaffian rather than determinantal structure of correlation functions, renders the analysis substantially more involved, see for example
21
.

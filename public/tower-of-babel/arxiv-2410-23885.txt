Abstract
In this paper, we propose a data-driven algorithm to design rate- and length-flexible polar codes. While the algorithm is very general, a particularly appealing use case is the design of codes for AED ( AED ), a promising decoding algorithm for URLLC ( URLLC ) and mMTC ( mMTC ) applications. To this end, theoretic results on nesting of symmetric polar codes are derived, which give hope in finding a fully nested, rate-compatible sequence suitable for AED . Using the proposed algorithms, such a flexible polar code design for automorphism ensemble SC ( SC ) decoding is constructed, outperforming existing code designs for AED and also the 5G polar code under CRC ( CRC )-aided SCL ( SCL ) decoding.

I
Introduction

Polar codes
[
1
]
have been commercialized in the
5G
(
5G
) standard
[
2
]
.
Due to their good performance in the short block-length regime and the availability of low-complexity, fast decoding algorithms, it is likely that also 6G communication systems employ polar codes as one of their main error-correcting codes
[
3
,
4
]
.

A particularly promising decoding approach is
AED

[
5
,
6
]
.
It exploits the rich symmetry group of polar codes
[
7
]
to decode in a highly parallelizable fashion, enabling significantly reduced decoding latency and complexity compared to
SCL
decoding
[
8
]
.
However, for useful symmetries to be available, tailored code design has to be applied.
While such codes individually exist for almost all practical parameters in the short to medium block-length regime
[
9
]
, for applicability in flexible wireless communication standards, a single unified description of a family of polar codes suitable for
AED
is desirable
[
10
]
.
In particular, the polar code standardized in
5G
is characterized by a nesting property
[
11
]
, that allows to derive codes with different lengths from the same sequence, making it flexible in both length and rate
[
12
]
.
The design of such nested polar codes turns out to be a difficult problem and hence, previous work often resorted to machine-learning-based methods
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

To construct polar codes for
AED
, previous work focused on the scenario where a desired symmetry group is predefined.
For example,
[
16
]
proposes a method to iteratively refine a code design until the desired symmetries are obtained.
A more direct methodology is proposed in
[
17
]
, which is extended to rate-compatible sequences in
[
18
]
.
However, the restriction to a predefined symmetry group limits the degrees of freedom in designing the code and almost certainly results in compromises in the error-correcting performance.
Moreover, a construction of a family of nested polar codes suitable for
AED
has not been yet proposed.
Therefore, the goal of this paper is to construct a unified, nested sequence of rate- and length-flexible symmetric polar codes. Similarly to
[
19
]
, the algorithm is based on graph search on actual error-rate performance data. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We derive theoretical results for the nesting property of symmetric polar codes.

•

We propose a novel data-driven, general design algorithm for rate-compatible polar codes based on the shortest-path algorithm, which is then extended to nested polar codes.

•

As an application, the algorithm is used to construct a nested polar code design for
AED
, supporting lengths
32\leq N\leq 256
and a wide range of code rates.

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Notation

Vectors and matrices are denoted by lowercase and uppercase boldface letters, respectively.
The
n
-bit
LSB
(
LSB
)-first binary expansion of an integer
i,\;0\leq i<2^{n}
, is denoted by
\hat{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.

II-B

Polar Codes

Polar codes are constructed from the polar transform defined by the
N\times N
Hadamard matrix
\boldsymbol{G}_{N}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{\otimes n}
with
N=2^{n}
, where
(\cdot)^{\otimes n}
denotes the
n
-th Kronecker power.
The polar transform converts identical channels into polarized synthetic channels, which are either highly reliable or unreliable.
Depending on the code design,
K
reliable channels are selected to carry information and their indices form the
information set

\mathcal{I}
. The other, unreliable channels form the
frozen set

\mathcal{F}
.
The generator matrix
\boldsymbol{G}
of the resulting polar code is composed exactly of those rows
of
\boldsymbol{G}_{N}
indexed by
\mathcal{I}
.

II-C

Partial Order

The synthetic channels after the polar transform exhibit a
UPO
(
UPO
), i.e., independent of the transmission channel, some synthetic channels are always more reliable than others
[
20
]
.
If the synthetic channel indexed by
j
is at least as reliable as the one indexed by
i
, we denote this by
i\preccurlyeq j
.
The
UPO
can be defined by the following two rules:

1.

Left swap
: If
(\hat{i}_{l},\hat{i}_{l+1})=(1,0)
, and
(\hat{j}_{l},\hat{j}_{l+1})=(0,1)
for
l<n
, while all other
\hat{j}_{k}=\hat{i}_{k}
,
k\not\in\{l,l+1\}
, then
i\preccurlyeq j
.
In other words, if
\hat{\boldsymbol{j}}
equals
\hat{\boldsymbol{i}}
with a single “1” bit moved to a more significant position, then
i\preccurlyeq j
.

2.

Binary domination
: If
\hat{i}_{l}\leq\hat{j}_{l}
for all
l\leq n
, i.e,
\hat{\boldsymbol{j}}
is the same as
\hat{\boldsymbol{i}}
, but may have additional “1” bits, then
i\preccurlyeq j
.

The remaining relations are derived from transitivity.

We say that a polar code with information set
\mathcal{I}
follows the
UPO
if
i\in\mathcal{I}
implies that all
j\succcurlyeq i
are also in
\mathcal{I}
. In other words, for all information channels, all more reliable channels also carry information.
Polar codes following the
UPO
can be compactly described by their minimum information set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{min}}
which is the smallest set from which
\mathcal{I}
can be fully generated using the partial order relations
[
7
]
.
In the following, all considered polar codes follow this
UPO
.

II-D

Automorphisms of Polar Codes

Definition 1

(Automorphism Group)
.

The (permutation) automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C})
of a code
\mathcal{C}
is the set of permutations of the codeword symbols that map every codeword onto another (not necessarily different) codeword. That means,

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C})=\left\{\pi\in S_{N}\;\middle|\;\pi(\boldsymbol{c})\in\mathcal{C}\;\forall\boldsymbol{c}\in\mathcal{C}\right\},

where
\pi(\boldsymbol{c})
is the permuted version of
\boldsymbol{c}
,
N
is the block length and
S_{N}
is the group of all permutations of
N
elements.

Except for degenerate cases, the automorphisms of polar codes are
affine permutations
.

Definition 2

(Affine Permutation)
.

An affine permutation is a permutation of the codeword symbols which can be written as

\pi:i\mapsto j:\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{j}}=\boldsymbol{A}\hat{\boldsymbol{i}}+\boldsymbol{b}

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n\times n}
is an invertible matrix and
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
Furthermore, the affine automorphism group of a code is the subgroup of the automorphism group consisting of affine permutations.

For a polar code, the structure of its affine automorphism group can be found using
stabilizers
.

Definition 3

(Stabilizer)
.

The stabilizer
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{S})
of a set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,2^{n}-1\}
is the set of permutations
\sigma\in S_{n}
with

f_{\sigma}(i)\in\mathcal{S}\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{S},

where
f_{\sigma}(i)
permutes the binary digits of
i
, i.e.,

f_{\sigma}:i\mapsto j:\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{j}}=\sigma(\hat{\boldsymbol{i}}).

If
\mathcal{I}
is the information set of a polar code compliant with the
UPO
, then
\operatorname{Stab}(\mathcal{I})
exhibits a block structure, i.e., bits are only permuted within contiguous blocks with block sizes
\boldsymbol{s}=[s_{0},...,s_{m-1}]
, where
m
is the number of blocks
[
7
]
.
Moreover, let
\operatorname{PL}(\boldsymbol{s})
be the subgroup of affine permutations with
\boldsymbol{A}
being a permutation matrix that does not have any non-zero entries above the block diagonal given by
\boldsymbol{s}
, and
\boldsymbol{b}=\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore, the full affine automorphism group of a polar code is given by the
BLTA
(
BLTA
) group, i.e., invertible affine mappings where
\boldsymbol{A}
is restricted to have no non-zero elements above the block diagonal defined by
\boldsymbol{s}

[
7
,
21
]
.

II-E

Successive Cancellation Decoding

The first, and arguably the simplest decoding algorithm proposed with polar codes is
SC
decoding, introduced in
[
1
]
.
This algorithm determines the most likely value for each information bit
u_{i}
in a successive order, i.e.,

\hat{u}_{i}=\arg\max_{u_{i}}\operatorname{Pr}\{u_{i}|\hat{u}_{0}\dots\hat{u}_{i-1},\boldsymbol{y}\},

(2)

where
\boldsymbol{y}
denotes the received vector. For frozen bits
i\in\mathcal{F}
,
\hat{u}_{i}=0
.

SC
decoding can be improved in error-rate performance by keeping a list of the
L
most likely decoding paths, called
SCL
decoding.
Typically, an outer
CRC
code is utilized to both improve the weight enumerators of the polar code and to select the correct codeword from the list
[
22
]
. This is referred to as
CA-SCL
(
CA-SCL
).

II-F

Automorphism Ensemble Decoding

An alternative decoding strategy to
SCL
decoding is
AED

[
5
]
.
It uses an ensemble of
M
identical and independent constituent decoders (e.g.,
SC
decoders), each working on a permuted version of the received sequence
\boldsymbol{y}
, where the permutations are selected from the (permutation) automorphism group of the code. After un-permuting the respective codeword estimates, the most likely candidate is selected as the decoding result.
As the subdecoders are identical and independent, the complexity of
AED
is significantly smaller than that of a comparable
SCL
decoder
[
8
]
.

III
Nested Polar Codes

Definition 4

(Low and High Nested Subcodes)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the information set of a polar code with length
N
.
Its low and high nested subcodes are the
\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}
length polar codes specified by

\mathcal{I}^{\ell}=\left\{i\in\mathcal{I}:i<\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}\right\}
and
\mathcal{I}^{h}=\left\{i-\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}:i\in\mathcal{I},i\geq\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}\right\}
,
respectively.

For information sets
\mathcal{I}
designed for
SC
decoding at a given
SNR
(
SNR
) (in particular, via density evolution
[
23
]
), it is easy to see that
\mathcal{I}^{\ell}
corresponds to a polar code designed for a worse
SNR
, while
\mathcal{I}^{h}
is designed for a better
SNR
.

More generally, given a reliability sequence
Q
of the synthetic channels, their respective subsequences

Q^{\ell}=[q_{i}\in Q:q_{i}<\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}]
and
Q^{h}=\left[q_{i}-\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}:q_{i}\in Q,\,q_{i}\geq\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}\right]

are also valid reliability sequences for the shorter polar codes of length
\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}

[
11
]
.
This is the
nesting property
, that gives rise to elegant rate and length flexible polar codes which can be specified from a single reliability sequence

Q_{N_{\mathrm{max}}}
for the maximal block length
N_{\mathrm{max}}

[
12
]
.
Due to the simpler implementation, lower nesting is used typically in practice.
To construct a polar code
\mathcal{P}(N=2^{n},K)
from
Q_{N_{\mathrm{max}}}
, apply the following steps:

1.

Extract the subsequence
Q^{\prime}=[q_{i}\in Q_{N_{\mathrm{max}}}:q_{i}<N]

2.

Select the last
K
channels as information set
\mathcal{I}=Q^{\prime}_{(N-K):N}

In the following, we derive some useful properties regarding the affine automorphism groups of nested polar codes.

Proposition 1

(Symmetric Subcodes)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(N,K)
be a polar code with block profile
\boldsymbol{s}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{m}]
. Then its high and low nested subcodes

\mathcal{P}^{\ell}(\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}},K^{\ell})
and
\mathcal{P}^{h}(\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}},K^{h})

have at least block profile
\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{m}-1]
each (with
\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{m-1}]
if
s_{m}=1
).

Proof:

Consider the subgroup of stage permutations (or bit-permutations) that fix the position
n-1
, corresponding to the block profile
\tilde{\boldsymbol{s}}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{m}-1,1]
. By definition, no permutation in this subgroup permutes indices from one half of the code to the other. Furthermore, observe that for all
\pi\in\operatorname{PL}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{s}})
and
0\leq i<\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}
,
\pi(i)-i=\pi(i+\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}})-i+\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}
, i.e., the permutation acts identically on both halves. Therefore, the upper and lower subcodes are stabilized at least by
P_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{s}}}
restricted to length
\nicefrac{{N}}{{2}}
, which is exactly
\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}
.

\blacksquare

Proposition 2

(Symmetric Supercodes)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(N=2^{n},K)
be a polar code with minimum information set
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{min}}
and block profile
\boldsymbol{s}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{m}]
. Then longer codes
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(N^{\prime},K^{\prime})
with
N^{\prime}=2^{t}N
and information set
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
constructed from
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{min}}
have block profile
\boldsymbol{s}^{\prime}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{m}+t]
.

Proof:

From the binary domination property, we know that
\forall i\in\mathcal{I}
, also
i+\tau\cdot N\in\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
with
1\leq\tau<2^{t}
. Hence, the stabilizing permutations of
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
corresponding to the first
n
positions are identical to those of
\mathcal{I}
.
Now partition
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
into sets according to the prefix (corresponding to the least significant
n-s_{m}
bits) and the Hamming weight of the
s_{m}
most significant bits. Within each set, in the last block all possible bit patterns with same or higher Hamming weight are generated by the left swap rule. Therefore, the rightmost block is of size
s_{m}+t
.

\blacksquare

Example 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(128,60)
be the polar code constructed from
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{min}}=\{27\}
with
\mathbf{s}=[3,4]
. Its subcodes
\mathcal{P}^{\ell}(64,19)
and
\mathcal{P}^{h}(64,41)
both have
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=[3,3]
.
The
t=2
supercode
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}(512,376)
constructed from
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{min}}=\{27\}
has
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}=[3,6]
.

These two theoretic properties give hope for finding nested sequences of codes with large automorphism groups.

IV-B

Rate Compatible Symmetric Polar Codes

For a given symmetry constraint, single bit-granular sequences of polar codes do not exist
[
18
]
. Even when dropping hard symmetry constraints, it is unlikely that a single bit-granular sequence with satisfactory performance under
AED
can be constructed.
For versatile communication systems, however, such flexibility in the code dimension is desired.
Fortunately, the coding gain achieved by certain polar codes under
AED
is so large that zero-padding for minor rate-adaption becomes viable.
That is, if no code with the desired dimension
K
fulfills the requirements, find the next larger allowed
K^{\prime}
and append
z=K^{\prime}-K
zeros to each message.

Definition 6

(Zero-Padded Polar Code)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},z)
denote the code with dimension
K=|\mathcal{I}|-z
that is obtained by padding the messages
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{K}
with
z
zeros and encoding it using
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}
).

Proposition 4

(Performance Bound for Zero-Padded Polar Codes)
.

Let
\mu(\mathcal{C})
denote the required
E_{\mathrm{s}}/N_{0}
to decode a code
\mathcal{C}
with block-error rate
\epsilon
.
Then
\mu(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},z))\leq\mu(\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}))
.

Proof:

By definition,
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},z)
is a subcode of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
. Thus, one can decode
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I},z)
with any decoder for
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I})
, since the decoder does not need to
be aware of the fact
that only a subset of messages (those with zeros in the zero-padded positions) is transmitted. Hence, at the same
E_{\mathrm{s}}/N_{0}
, the same
BLER
is obtained. Decoders utilizing the zero-padded bits will only improve on this performance.

\blacksquare

While in theory, the position of the zeros does not matter, for the purpose of rate-compatible sequences the zero-padding should be in unreliable positions, so that the smaller codes are subcodes of the larger codes.
In the following remarks, we give ideas how additional gains are generated from these bits.

Remark 1
.

In its simplest form, the decoder decodes the larger code with dimension
K^{\prime}
without “knowing” about the zero-padding. Utilizing the zero-padded bits as frozen bits (in the non-permuted decoding path) will further improve the performance.

Remark 2
.

When systematic encoding is used, the zero-padded bits will show up as zeros in the codeword, and thus, all decoding paths may use this as prior information, i.e.,
LLR
(
LLR
) values of
+\infty
.

Remark 3
.

Instead of zero-padding, also an error-detecting outer code (such as parity bits,
CRC
, etc.) may be employed to improve the false alarm rate.

Based on this idea, we augment the graph by adding vertices corresponding to zero-padded polar codes up to a sufficiently large maximum zero-padding length
z_{\mathrm{max}}
.
The connectivity rule is extended as follows:

Definition 7

(Augmented Graph)
.

Two vertices
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}_{i},z_{i})
and
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{I}_{j},z_{j})
are connected by an edge, if and only if

•

|\mathcal{I}_{j}|-z_{j}=|\mathcal{I}_{i}|-z_{i}+1

•

\mathcal{I}_{i}=\mathcal{I}_{j}
or (
z_{i}=0
and
\mathcal{I}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{j}
).

The first condition ensures the single-bit granularity, while the second is the subcode property.

V
Nested Sequences

To incorporate multiple block lengths
N
, the above procedure is extended in a sequential manner.
While in general, a global search would be best, the complexity does not scale well due to the zero padding.
For each code, all contained shorter subcodes must be considered, each either directly or as a zero-padded larger code.
This is typically computationally infeasible.
However, several, sub-optimal meta-search strategies are possible, e.g., a sequential approach, as outlined in the following.

V-B

Disambiguation

Due to the zero-padding, the partial order obtained from Algorithm
2
is not necessarily a total order, i.e., it contains ambiguities.
For decoding with decoders unaware of the zero-padding, these ambiguities are irrelevant.
However, there are several reasons to specify a total order:

•

Unique specification of the encoding.

•

Improved performance when the decoding strategy outlined in Remark
1

•

Applicability of other decoding methods other than the one the code is designed for, e.g.,
SCL

VII
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper, we derived theoretical properties of the nesting structure of polar codes with symmetries and proposed a data-driven methodology for constructing rate-compatible polar codes with and without a nesting property.
Although the proposed method is general, it is well suited for symmetric polar codes, as the symmetry constrains the search space to make a fully data-driven approach feasible.
The algorithms are demonstrated by constructing the first nested family of rate-compatible polar codes for
AED
.
Using Monte-Carlo simulations, we show that the designed polar codes outperform existing rate-compatible code designs.
The proposed design is also compatible with
CA-SCL
and also
SCAL
(
SCAL
)
[
27
]
decoding.
Future work includes the fine adjusting of the block length by shortening or puncturing, e.g., using the same length matching procedure standardized in 5G
and applying
AED
based on the mother code.
Also, the approach using the automorphism groups of the shortened codes proposed in
[
28
]
may be worth exploring in the context of nested polar codes.
Furthermore, as the proposed design methodology is very general, also other constraints, such as the set of optimized leaf node decoders for fast simplified
SC
decoding can be incorporated.

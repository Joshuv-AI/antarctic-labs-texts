Efficacy, safety and immunogenicity of a recombinant human papillomavirus bivalent (types 16, 18) vaccine in healthy 18–30-year-old Chinese women: a phase III, double-blind, randomized, controlled trial
Authors: Liwei Shi, Xuefen Gao, Lili Huang, Zhao Gao, Juan Li, Bangwei Yu, Huilan Fan, Guohua Li, Yanxia Wang, Qi Li, Lin Yuan, Changgui Li, Zhaojun Mo, the HPV1003 Study group, Li Shi, Ke Li, Min Ji, Ya-Lin Hu, Ling-Yun Zhou, Xue-Mei Yan, Jing-Jing Chen, Wei Li, Zi-Long Wang, Zhen Huang, Yu-Liang Zhao, Rong-Cheng Li, Yan-Ping Li, Nian-Min Shi, Qiang Lu, Bin He, Shi-Fang Wen, Dong-Zhang Yang, Guo-Gang Liu, Yan-Xia Cai, Wei-Jiang Cui, Jiang-Bing Zheng, Zhan-Bo Geng, Yan-Hua Xie, Yong-Biao Ren, Hai-Song Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAffordable HPV vaccines are needed to accelerate elimination of cervical cancer. We evaluated the efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity of a recombinant bivalent human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine composed of L1 virus-like particles (VLPs) for HPV 16 and HPV18 (HPV-2), produced in Pichia pastoris, in healthy Chinese women aged 18–30 years.MethodsIn this phase 3, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial conducted at 10 centres in China (Nov 2014–Jan 2020), participants were assigned 1:1 to receive three doses of HPV-2 or placebo at months 0, 2, and 6. The primary endpoint was efficacy against histopathology-confirmed cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade 2 or higher (CIN2+) associated with HPV16/18 through 48 months. Secondary endpoints were safety (adverse events [AEs] within 1 month of any dose; serious AEs throughout follow-up) and immunogenicity (neutralising antibody titres and seroconversion at Month 7 in a predefined subset).ResultsOf 12,000 women enrolled, 11,999 received the first dose and 11,281 completed all three doses. Over 48 months, 17 CIN2 + cases occurred: 3 among vaccine recipients (n = 5190) versus 14 among placebo recipients (n = 5167), corresponding to vaccine efficacy of 78.6% (95% CI 23.3–96.1; p = 0.012). No vaccine-related serious AEs occurred. Solicited local AEs were mainly mild pain, reported by 25.9% of vaccinees and 7.9% of placebo recipients. Fever was the most common systemic AE (27.4% vs. 26.4%). In the immunogenicity subset, 99.7% (349/350) and 99.4% (348/350) of vaccinees were seropositive for HPV16 and HPV18 neutralising antibodies, respectively, at Month 7.ConclusionsThree doses of HPV-2 were well tolerated, highly immunogenic, and efficacious against CIN2 + up to 4 years post-vaccination, supporting further evaluation in the adolescent target population for HPV vaccination campaigns.Trial registrationChina Drug Trials Registry (CTR20140626; registered Sept 26, 2014; prospective). Introduction

Persistent infection with carcinogenic human papillomavirus (HPV) types is the necessary cause of cervical cancer, with HPV types 16 and 18 responsible for approximately 70% of cases globally [1]. The burden of this preventable disease falls disproportionately on developing regions, which account for over 85% of the world’s cervical cancer deaths [2]. The first prophylactic HPV vaccine, a quadrivalent formulation, was licensed in 2006, followed by a bivalent vaccine and later a nonavalent vaccine. While highly effective prophylactic HPV vaccines have been developed, significant challenges related to cost and supply have long hindered widespread implementation in many regions, including China [3], creating an urgent need for additional affordable vaccines to help reach the World Health Organization (WHO) goals for cervical cancer elimination [4].

Shanghai Zerun Biotechnology has developed a bivalent HPV 16/18 vaccine (HPV-2) based on recombinant L1 capsid proteins expressed in the Pichia pastoris. Phase I and II clinical trials have shown that HPV-2 vaccine is safe and immunogenic in healthy females from 9 to 45 years of age [5, 6]. This phase III trial was designed to evaluate the efficacy of HPV-2 in preventing clinically significant disease in healthy women aged 18–30 years. High-grade precancerous lesions have been recognized as a key milestone for HPV vaccine introduction [7]. Accordingly, the primary endpoint was HPV 16/18-related cervical intraepithelial neoplasia of grade 2 or higher (CIN2+), with histopathological classification based on World Health Organization terminology [8]. Persistent HPV 16/18 infection, a known prerequisite for the development of these lesions [7], was evaluated as a secondary endpoint. Placebo controls were justified as no HPV vaccine was marketed in China at study initiation.

Here, we report the final analysis from the 48-month follow-up of this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, providing evidence on the protective effect of HPV-2 in a population at high risk of HPV exposure, with implications for its broader use in public immunization strategies.

Methods

This phase 3, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled study was conducted in 10 county-level Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in the Guangxi, Hebei, Henan and Shanxi provinces from November 2014 to January 2020. Ethics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region CDC and from the ethics committees of all other participating centers; written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrolment and all procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant national regulations.

This trial was prospectively registered with China’s National Medical Products Administration clinical drug-trial registry (CDE platform) under CTR20140626 in September 2014, before the first participant was enrolled on November 2, 2014. The CDE registry is a public, government-operated database (searchable at http://www.chinadrugtrials.org.cn). For international discoverability, the study was additionally registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02733068) in April 11, 2016.

The primary objective was to determine the efficacy of three doses of vaccine against histopathologically-confirmed CIN2+ (CIN2, CIN3, in situ adenocarcinoma or invasive cervical cancer) associated with HPV 16/18 over a four-year period. Secondary objectives included vaccine efficacy against persistent (6 or 12 months) cervical infection with HPV 16/18, and safety and immunogenicity of HPV-2 vaccine.

Eligible participants were healthy 18–30-year-old women who would comply with all study requirements including attending scheduled study visits. Main exclusion criteria are provided in the Supplementary Appendix.

Vaccine

HPV-2 vaccine contains 40 µg HPV16 and 20 µg HPV18 L1 virus-like particles (produced in Pichia pastoris) adsorbed to 225 µg aluminum phosphate per 0.5 ml dose. Placebo contained 225 µg of aluminum phosphate in the same volume of buffered saline. Vaccine and placebo, supplied in identical study vials labelled with computer-generated randomised codes.

Procedures

Participants were randomly assigned (1:1) to HPV-2 or placebo using a computer-generated block randomisation schedule (block size 8), stratified by site. Allocation was concealed through centrally prepared, sequentially numbered vials that were identical in appearance. Participants, investigators, and study staff were blinded to group assignment. Emergency unblinding was permitted only for medical necessity or on the advice of the data monitoring committee.

Participants received three intramuscular doses of HPV-2 or placebo at 0, 2, and 6 months. At enrollment (Day 0), participants underwent urine pregnancy testing and baseline blood collection. A subset also provided samples for immunogenicity assessment at Month 7. Scheduled follow-up visits occurred every 6 months through Month 36 (Months 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36) and annually thereafter (Months 48 and 60), with provision for an earlier final visit if the pre-specified total of 16 HPV16/18-related CIN2 + cases was reached.

Cervical samples for cytology and HPV DNA testing were collected at baseline and all follow-up visits. Women with abnormal cytological or HPV DNA results were referred for colposcopy and biopsy according to a pre-defined management plan. Tissue specimens were paraffin-embedded and tested for high-risk HPV types. Detection of HPV DNA in paraffin-embedded tissue was considered evidence of association between the HPV type and the lesion. For lesions in which only one high-risk HPV type was detected, that type was considered causally associated with the lesion. For lesions in which multiple high-risk HPV types were detected, attribution was assigned to all detected types. Detailed procedures are available in the Supplementary Appendix.

After database lock and unblinding, participants in the placebo group were informed of the study results and provided the opportunity to receive HPV vaccination at their own discretion. To facilitate access, financial assistance was offered to cover the cost of HPV vaccine available on the Chinese market at that time.

Safety and reactogenicity

Participants were monitored for 30 min post-vaccination for immediate reactions. Solicited local and systemic events were recorded via 7-day diaries after each dose; unsolicited adverse events and pregnancy were recorded up to 30 days after dose 3. Serious adverse events (SAEs) were monitored throughout the study period.

Pregnancies with an estimated date of conception between the first vaccination and 30 days after dose 3 were recorded and actively followed until pregnancy outcome. Pregnancy beyond this exposure window were reported only if pregnancy outcomes met the criteria for SAEs (e.g., congenital anomaly, stillbirth, or maternal complication).

Immunogenicity

Immunogenicity was evaluated in a subset of 800 participants (400 per group) who received all three doses and had valid antibody data at baseline and Month 7. Neutralizing antibody titres against HPV 16 and 18 were measured using a pseudovirion-based assay. Titres were expressed as geometric mean titres (GMT); seropositivity was defined as a reciprocal titre ≥ 40. Seroconversion was defined as either a shift from seronegative to seropositive, or a ≥ 4-fold rise in titre among baseline seropositives.

Statistics

The primary objective was to evaluate the protective efficacy of the HPV-2 vaccine against histopathologically-confirmed CIN2 + associated with HPV 16/18. The sample size was determined by the number of events required for this primary endpoint in the efficacy Per Protocol Set (ePPS). It was calculated that 16 CIN2 + cases related to HPV 16/18 were needed to provide at least 90% power to demonstrate a target vaccine efficacy (VE) of 90%, with significance defined by the lower limit of the 95% confidence interval (CI) for VE. This necessitated a minimum of 9,668 healthy women aged 18–30 years; to account for HPV 16/18 seropositivity at baseline and potential dropouts, 12,000 participants (6,000 per group) were recruited.

Efficacy analyses were performed on the efficacy Full Analysis Set (eFAS) and the ePPS. The eFAS included all randomized participants who had normal or low-grade cytology (negative, ASC-US, or LSIL) at baseline, received at least one vaccine dose, and were DNA- and seronegative for HPV 16/18 at month 0. For the eFAS, endpoint cases were counted after the first vaccination onward. The ePPS further required participants to be seronegative for HPV 16/18 at baseline, meet all inclusion criteria, maintain normal or low-grade cytology from months 0 to 6, complete all three assigned doses, and remain HPV 16/18 DNA negative at months 0 and 6. For the ePPS, endpoint cases were counted after the third dose. Incidence rates were compared between groups using a Poisson regression model to estimate vaccine efficacy (VE) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

A secondary evaluation assessed efficacy against 6-month or 12-month persistent HPV 16/18 infections, based on HPV DNA results from exfoliated cervical cell samples collected at scheduled follow-up visits. Persistent HPV infection was defined as consecutive positive HPV DNA results for the same HPV type in cervical exfoliated cell samples collected at scheduled follow-up visits. A 6-month persistent infection required two consecutive positive results approximately six months apart, limited to data collected by the Month 36 visit, when the testing interval changed from six-monthly to annual. A 12-month persistent infection was defined as detection of the same HPV DNA type in three consecutive positive results by Month 36, or in two consecutive positive results after Month 36, when the follow-up interval changed from six-monthly to annual. Only virological samples were used to define persistence. A Poisson regression model was used to compare rates between vaccine and placebo groups and calculate protective efficacy with 95% CIs for these persistence endpoints.

Immunogenicity was analyzed in the Per Protocol Immunogenicity Set (iPPS). Safety was assessed in all participants who received at least one injection. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS 9.4 software.

Results

Between November 2, 2014, and March 17, 2015, a total of 14,176 women were screened across 10 sites in China, of whom 12,000 were eligible and enrolled. Among these, 11,999 were randomized and received at least one dose (5,998 placebo; 6,001 HPV-2), forming the Safety Set. (Fig. 1). The eFAS included 11,524 participants who met baseline criteria, while 10,357 completed three doses and met all protocol requirements for the ePPS. Vaccine efficacy

The participants were followed for a median of 48.3 months. For the primary analysis we identified a total of 17 cases of CIN2 + identified histologically in the ePPS (Fig. 2a). The distribution, 14 cases in 5167 control participants and 3 cases in 5190 HPV-2 vaccinees, gave a statistically significant protective efficacy of 78·6% (95% CI: 23·3–96·1; p = 0·012) (Table 2). In the eFAS, 26 cases of CIN2 + associated with HPV 16 or 18 were identified; 21 cases in 5770 control participants and 5 cases in 5754 HPV-2 vaccinees, with a statistically significant protective efficacy of 76·1% (95% CI: 32·9–93·3; p = 0·0026) (Table 2). The 5 CIN2 + cases in the eFAS HPV-2 group consisted of 4 HPV-16 associated cases (3 CIN2, 1 CIN3) and 1 HPV-18 associated case (CIN2). Detection times were 6, 12, 18, 30, and 48 months post-vaccination. The case detected at 6 months had concurrent HPV-45 infection detected by Inno-Lipa. The 21 CIN2 + cases in the eFAS placebo group consisted of 16 HPV-16 associated cases (13 CIN2, 3 CIN3) and 5 HPV-18 associated cases (all CIN2). Five cases had concurrent detection of other HPV types (HPV-11, -51, -58, -62, or -68) by Inno-Lipa.

When calculated for the HPV types 16 or 18 individually, the protective efficacy against CIN2 + due specifically to either HPV 16 or HPV 18 was not statistically significant in the ePPS although of the same magnitude (77·7%, p = 0·066 and 79·9%, p = 0·221) as efficacy against HPV16/18 combined. This was probably due to the relatively small number of cases associated with HPV 16 or HPV 18 in both HPV-2 and placebo groups compared with the eFAS (Table 2).

Protective efficacy against persistent infection

In the ePPS, the statistically significant protective efficacy against 6-month persistent HPV-16/18 infection was 88% (95% CI: 66·3–96·9, p < 0·0001) and against 12-month persistent infection was 77·0% (16·4–95·8, p = 0·021) (Table 3). Efficacy rates were slightly lower but still statistically significant in the eFAS (Table 3). The Kaplan–Meier plot for 6-month persistent HPV16/18 infection (Fig. 2b) shows early divergence of curves from approximately Month 12 with a sustained plateau in the HPV-2 arm through Month 36 (the pre-specified analysis window for 6-month persistence), consistent with the VE estimates shown in Table 3.

Safety and reactogenicity

HPV-2 vaccination was well tolerated, with no safety signals identified during the study.

Over the study period, 59.2% of HPV-2 recipients and 50.2% of placebo recipients reported at least one adverse event up to Month 7 (p < 0.0001) (Table 4; Fig. 3). Solicited local reactions were more frequent in the HPV-2 group (27.9%) than in the placebo group (8.6%, p < 0.0001), predominantly pain at the injection site. Severe local reactions were rare and resolved without sequelae. Solicited systemic adverse events were reported by 34.5% of vaccinees and 32.1% of controls (p = 0.0053), mainly consisting of mild fever (27.4% vs. 26.4%) and headache (7.9% vs. 6.4%). The incidence of unsolicited adverse events was identical in both groups (27.0%). No vaccine-related SAEs were identified (Table 4).

Pregnancy was reported in 191 HPV-2 recipients and 209 placebo recipients (Supplementary Table 2). The proportions of participants who became pregnant and the pregnancy outcome profiles were similar between groups. Most pregnancies resulted in elective abortions. No congenital anomalies, pregnancy complications, or abnormal neonatal outcomes were considered related to vaccination in either group.

Immunogenicity

Of the 800 participants in the immunogenicity subset, 697 (347 controls and 350 vaccinees) met all necessary criteria to be included in the iPPS (Supplementary Table 3). GMTs for both HPV types were similar in the two groups overall at baseline. One month after the third vaccination there was a robust immune response in the vaccine group; of the 350 HPV-2 participants all but one and three had seroconverted against HPV 16 and against HPV 18 by Month 7, respectively. Vaccinee seroconversion rates for HPV 16 and HPV 18 neutralising antibodies were 99·7% (95% CI: 98·4–100) and 99·1% (97·5–99·8), respectively. Overall vaccine group GMTs at Month 7 were 3145 (2803–3529) for HPV 16 and 2309 (2040–2614) for HPV 18, representing geometric mean-fold increases of 136 and 109 from baseline, respectively.

Discussion

In this randomized, placebo-controlled study of 12,000 healthy Chinese women aged 18–30 years, HPV-2 demonstrated statistically significant protective efficacy against HPV-16/18-associated CIN2 + of 78.6% (95% CI: 23.3–96.1) in the ePPS and 76.1% (95% CI: 34.7–92.9) in the eFAS. Although individual HPV type efficacies were of similar magnitude, small case numbers precluded statistical significance. The vaccine also provided significant protection against persistent HPV-16/18 infection (88% for 6-month persistence, 77% for 12-month persistence), with no statistically significant cross-type protection observed against other high-risk HPV types. Notably, no participants with persistent HPV-16/18 infection in the vaccine group developed CIN2 + during the study period in ePPS.

Although direct comparisons with other HPV vaccines are challenging due to different study methodology and analysis cohorts, the observed efficacy demonstrates substantial protection against HPV-16/18-associated CIN2+. Many comparator studies applied different cohort definitions for the efficacy estimates and did not consider cytology results at the 6-month period [9–12]. We conducted sensitivity analysis applying those alternative definitions to our data, efficacy was 81.1% using both bivalent and quadrivalent vaccine trial cohort definitions (Supplementary Table 4). Our results are broadly consistent with the efficacy observed for other HPV vaccines in previous trials, such as those reported by Arbyn et al. [13] and Herrero et al. [14].

The timing of CIN2 + detection in the HPV-2 group warrants consideration. Two of the three CIN2 + cases in the ePPS group were identified by Month 18, and no further cases emerged until the third case at Month 48. We also examined the corresponding persistent infection data. The Kaplan–Meier curves for 6-month persistent HPV16/18 infection (Fig. 2b) shows a similar pattern: early cases in the HPV-2 group, followed by a sustained plateau, contrasting with the progressive accumulation of cases observed in the control group. This early emergence is consistent with observations by Herrero et al. [14] that “breakthrough” infections detected in the early months of vaccine trials may represent the emergence of pre-existing or latent infections present at enrollment rather than new post-vaccination infections. The sustained plateau in both persistent infection and CIN2 + cases in the vaccine group after Month 18, contrasting with continued case accumulation in the placebo group, supports the interpretation that these early cases likely represent progression of undetected baseline infections.

The interpretation also implies that longer follow-up periods are essential for a more precise and accurate estimation of true vaccine protective efficacy, as the influence of initially undetected infections diminishes. Recognizing this, a long-term follow-up study of this cohort was initiated (311-HPV-1003-EXTEND; NCT02733068) to provide data for up to approximately 10 years post-vaccination. Preliminary data from this extension study at 96 months of follow-up are encouraging: no new CIN2 + cases associated with HPV 16 and 18 were reported in the HPV-2 vaccine group (0 cases in N = 3,233) versus two HPV 16-associated and one HPV 18-associated CIN2 + case in the placebo group (N = 2,139). Incorporating these data, the preliminary protective efficacy of the HPV-2 vaccine against HPV 16/18-associated CIN2 + up to 96 months is estimated at 86.5% (95% CI: 54.1–97.4). While these promising findings require confirmation upon completion of the 10-year follow-up, the sustained absence of vaccine-type CIN2 + cases in the vaccine group is a strong indicator of the vaccine’s potential for durable protection and long-term impact on cervical cancer prevention.

Recent literature has raised the theoretical possibility that vaccine-driven selection could shape HPV variant circulation (‘escape’) under sustained vaccination pressure [15]. Our efficacy cases accrued during 2014–2019, when HPV vaccination coverage in mainland China was nascent and low, therefore, the escape dynamics are unlikely to explain the estimates observed in this trial. Variant/lineage surveillance and neutralization mapping remain important for future HPV vaccine implementation and next-generation design.

In the present study, vaccination with three doses of HPV-2 was highly immunogenic, confirming the observations in phase Ⅰ and phase Ⅱ studies [5, 6]. Robust immune responses observed at Month 7, one month after the third dose, resulted in 99·7% and 99·1% seroconverting against HPV 16 and HPV 18, respectively. The HPV-2 vaccine was generally well tolerated, with most reported adverse events being mild and transient. No serious adverse events were considered related to vaccination. Furthermore, the study identified no meaningful clinical safety concerns regarding post-vaccination pregnancy or infant outcomes.

This study also has several limitations. First, direct head-to-head immunogenicity comparisons between HPV-2 and other licensed HPV vaccines were not conducted. When the protocol was developed, no HPV vaccines had yet been licensed in China, and suitable comparators were therefore unavailable. Nonetheless, vaccine efficacy against CIN2+ provides the most clinically relevant evidence of protection. Previous phase 1 and 2 trials showed that HPV-2 elicited strong neutralising antibody responses to HPV-16 and HPV-18, comparable in magnitude to those reported for other bivalent and quadrivalent HPV vaccines in similar age groups [5, 6]. These results, together with the efficacy findings in the present study, support the protective potential of HPV-2 as an affordable and effective option for cervical-cancer prevention.

Second, the participant age (18–30 years), while necessary for assessing clinical endpoints (CIN2+), differs from the primary HPV vaccination target of young adolescents (e.g., 9–14 years, ideally pre-sexual debut) [4]. Direct efficacy studies with clinical endpoints in adolescents are ethically and logistically challenging in China, due to the invasive procedures like biopsies. Thus, the established regulatory pathway involves demonstrating efficacy in young adults, as done here, and extrapolating to adolescents via immunobridging [16]. This HPV-2 vaccine previously demonstrated non-inferior immunogenicity in 9–14-year-old girls compared to young women, with persistent antibody levels, supporting this approach [17].

Third, the trial was conducted in a cohort of Chinese women. While these findings provide crucial efficacy and safety data for this demographic, further research is important to support broader global implementation. Specifically, immunobridging studies in diverse racial and ethnic populations would be valuable to confirm consistently robust immune responses across groups with potentially varied genetic backgrounds or environmental exposures. Such data would bolster regulatory acceptance in different regions and contribute to ensuring equitable access to effective HPV prevention worldwide.

In conclusion, the recombinant bivalent HPV-2 vaccine (types 16, 18) developed in Pichia pastoris induced statistically significant protective efficacy against cervical cancer-related precancerous lesions caused by HPV 16 or 18 in healthy women from 18 to 30 years of age, with an acceptable safety profile and robust immunogenicity. Effectiveness in the target population of girls aged 9–14 years remains to be demonstrated by surveillance following implementation of the vaccine in mass immunization campaigns.

Experimental investigation of ZnO nanoparticle–enhanced pongamia biodiesel for performance, combustion, emission, and sustainability assessment in a CI engine
Authors: Dipak Patel, S. M. Ferdous Azam, P. S. Raghavendra Rao, Jasgurpreet Singh Chohan, Ripendeep Singh, Sikata Samantaray, Nivin Joy Thykattusserry, Yashwant Singh Bisht, Abhijit Bhowmik, Yalew Tamene
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present work explores the combustion behavior, engine performance, and exhaust emissions of a compression ignition engine operating on blends of Pongamia pinnata methyl ester (PPOME) with diesel fuel, supplemented with zinc oxide (ZnO) nanoparticles, with a focus on sustainable energy utilization. Biodiesel mixtures containing 20–100% PPOME were tested, and the blend with 40% biodiesel content exhibited the most favourable compromise between engine efficiency and emission control. When 50 ppm ZnO nanoparticles were dispersed into this blend (PPOMED40/ZnO), a further improvement in engine performance was observed, where brake thermal efficiency increased from 24.11% to 25.4%, while brake-specific fuel consumption decreased by 1.55% relative to the base blend (PPOMED40) under identical operating conditions. At full engine load, emission measurements demonstrated considerable reductions in major pollutants relative to conventional diesel operation under identical conditions, including decreases of 15.3% in NOₓ, 31.6% in CO, 15% in HC, and 27.3% in smoke opacity. A comprehensive thermodynamic evaluation incorporating energy, exergy, and sustainability analyses showed that the PPOMED40/ZnO blend achieved an exergy efficiency of 35% and a sustainability index of 1.54, indicating lower thermodynamic losses and improved system effectiveness. The findings suggest that the catalytic influence of ZnO nanoparticles promotes more efficient combustion while simultaneously lowering harmful emissions without compromising fuel economy. Additionally, the utilization of non-edible Pongamia oil as a renewable feedstock contributes to resource sustainability and supports circular bioeconomy strategies, in line with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals related to affordable clean energy, responsible consumption, and climate action. Consequently, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend can be considered a promising, environmentally responsible substitute for petroleum diesel in CI engine applications.

Introduction

The rapid growth in global energy demand, driven by industrial expansion and population increase, has intensified concerns regarding the depletion of fossil fuel reserves and the environmental consequences associated with their extensive use. Conventional diesel fuels are not only finite but also major contributors to greenhouse gas emissions, air pollution, and climate change. These challenges have necessitated the exploration of sustainable, renewable, and environmentally benign energy alternatives. Among the various options, biofuels have emerged as promising substitutes for petroleum-based fuels due to their renewable origin, biodegradability, and potential for cleaner combustion1–3. Biofuels are derived from biological resources such as vegetable oils, agricultural residues, and organic wastes, which can be replenished within relatively short timeframes. Unlike fossil fuels, biofuels participate in a near-balanced carbon cycle, where the carbon dioxide emitted during combustion is partially offset by the CO₂ absorbed during biomass growth, thereby contributing to reduced net greenhouse gas emissions4–6. In addition to environmental benefits, biofuels offer significant socio-economic advantages, including reduced dependence on imported fuels, enhanced energy security, and support for rural economies through biomass cultivation and processing industries7,8. These attributes make biofuels attractive candidates for sustainable energy transitions.

Among the various biofuels, biodiesel—produced through the transesterification of vegetable oils or animal fats—has gained considerable attention as a direct replacement for diesel fuel in compression ignition (CI) engines. Biodiesel possesses several favourable properties, including inherent oxygen content, higher cetane number, and improved lubricity, which contribute to cleaner combustion and reduced emissions of carbon monoxide (CO), hydrocarbons (HC), and particulate matter9–11. However, despite these advantages, biodiesel also exhibits certain limitations, such as higher viscosity, lower calorific value, and poor cold flow properties, which can adversely affect fuel atomization, combustion efficiency, and overall engine performance. These challenges necessitate further enhancement of biodiesel characteristics to ensure optimal engine operation. To overcome these limitations, recent research has increasingly focused on fuel modification techniques, particularly the incorporation of nano-scale additives. Nanoparticles, especially metal oxides, have demonstrated significant potential in improving the physicochemical and combustion properties of biodiesel fuels due to their high surface area, catalytic activity, and enhanced thermal conductivity. Several studies have reported that nanoparticle addition improves fuel atomization, promotes better air–fuel mixing, and enhances oxidation reactions, leading to increased brake thermal efficiency (BTE) and reduced brake-specific fuel consumption (BSFC)12–19. For instance, Bikkavolu et al.12 reported improved combustion performance and compliance with emission standards using carbon nanotube-enhanced biodiesel blends. Pullagura et al.13 demonstrated that SiO₂ nanoparticles enhanced in-cylinder pressure and heat release characteristics while reducing emissions. Chinnasamy et al.14 observed notable emission reductions using aluminum oxide nanoparticles, while Dhahad et al.15 reported improved fuel efficiency with ZnO nanoparticles, albeit with a slight increase in NOₓ emissions. Khan et al.16 highlighted the role of metal oxide nanoparticles in enhancing oxygen availability during combustion. Similarly, Gahavane et al.17, Mousavi and Heris18, and Kumar et al.19 confirmed improvements in thermal efficiency, fuel economy, and tribological properties with nanoparticle-enhanced fuels.

Further investigations have explored a wide range of nanoparticle types and fuel formulations to enhance biodiesel performance. Panithasan et al.20 reported improved brake thermal efficiency and reduced CO emissions using rice husk nanoparticles, while Ramachander et al.21 demonstrated performance enhancement using SiO₂ in ternary fuel blends. Yadav et al.22 achieved significant emission reductions using graphene oxide additives, whereas Singh et al.23 and Musthafa et al.24 reported improved combustion and emission characteristics using carbon nanotubes and peroxide additives. Studies by Demir et al.25 and Özer et al.26 further highlighted the effectiveness of hybrid additive systems in improving combustion efficiency and reducing emissions. In addition to these, cerium oxide (CeO₂) nanoparticles have been widely studied for their catalytic properties and oxygen buffering capabilities. Thiruselvam et al.27–29 reported substantial improvements in fuel efficiency and emission reduction when CeO₂ nanoparticles were used in biodiesel blends, including further enhancement when combined with thermal barrier coatings and hydrogen induction. Demir et al.30 explored the use of urea–diesel blends and reported that the presence of urea significantly suppressed NOx formation while simultaneously improving thermal efficiency, functioning in a manner comparable to selective catalytic reduction mechanisms. Chen et al.31 demonstrated improved engine performance using nanoparticle-doped diesel fuels, while Jain et al.32 and Sarma et al.33 reported reductions in BSFC and emissions with Al₂O₃ and TiO₂ nanoparticles, respectively. Kegl et al.34 provided a comprehensive review of the dual benefits and environmental considerations associated with nanoparticle use in fuel systems. Additional experimental studies35–39 consistently confirmed that nanoparticle-assisted biodiesel blends enhance combustion efficiency and emission performance.

Recent investigations by Raju et al.40–44 further emphasized the importance of integrating fuel modification with engine parameter optimization. Their studies demonstrated that controlling parameters such as injection timing, compression ratio, and exhaust gas recirculation, along with the use of oxygenated additives, can significantly improve combustion stability, brake thermal efficiency, and emission characteristics across various biodiesel feedstocks. These findings highlight the need for a more integrated approach that combines fuel formulation with engine optimization strategies. Despite these extensive studies, several research gaps remain. Most investigations focus on specific nanoparticle types or limited biodiesel blend ratios, often lacking a systematic evaluation across a wide range of fuel compositions. Additionally, inconsistencies persist in reported emission trends—particularly for NOₓ—due to variations in experimental conditions and fuel formulations. More importantly, the combined effects of multi-level biodiesel blending and ZnO nanoparticle incorporation have not been comprehensively explored, especially for non-edible biodiesel feedstocks. In this context, Pongamia Pinnata methyl ester (PPOME) represents a promising alternative fuel due to its non-edible nature, wide availability, and ability to grow on marginal lands without competing with food crops. Pongamia biodiesel offers favorable fuel properties and significant potential for sustainable fuel production. However, its application in CI engines, particularly in combination with nanoparticle additives such as ZnO, remains relatively underexplored.

Although extensive research has been conducted on biodiesel–nanoparticle fuel formulations, most previous studies have primarily focused on limited biodiesel blend ratios, specific categories of nanoparticles, or isolated evaluations of engine performance and emission characteristics without incorporating a comprehensive thermodynamic and sustainability perspective. Moreover, inconsistencies in reported emission behaviour, particularly concerning NOₓ formation, along with the absence of systematic investigations across multiple biodiesel blending levels, limit the broader applicability and generalization of the existing findings. In this context, the present study adopts a structured and comprehensive approach by (i) systematically investigating a broad range of PPOME–diesel blends (20–100%), (ii) identifying the optimum base blend (PPOMED40) prior to nanoparticle incorporation, (iii) introducing ZnO nanoparticles at an optimized dosage using a controlled dispersion methodology, and (iv) integrating performance, combustion, emission, energy, exergy, and sustainability analyses within a unified experimental framework. Unlike earlier investigations that largely emphasized isolated engine characteristics, the present work provides a holistic understanding of nano-enhanced Pongamia biodiesel behaviour from both combustion and thermodynamic viewpoints. Therefore, this study offers a significant contribution toward bridging the existing gap between fuel formulation, combustion enhancement, emission mitigation, and sustainability assessment for CI engine applications.

Materials and methods

Formulation of fuel blends using biodiesel

Pongamia Pinnata seeds were selected as the biodiesel feedstock due to their non-edible nature, high oil yield, widespread availability across several Indian states, and favourable physicochemical properties for biodiesel production. The raw Pongamia oil exhibited a density of 910–930 kg/m³, kinematic viscosity of 35–40 mm²/s at 40 °C, free fatty acid (FFA) content below 5%, and low moisture content, making it suitable for alkali-catalyzed transesterification. The moderate FFA content minimizes soap formation during processing, while the inherent oxygen content and renewable origin of Pongamia oil make it a promising sustainable fuel for CI engine applications12,13. The seeds were procured from a certified supplier and processed using a mechanical expeller with a capacity of 30 kg/h. The extracted crude oil was filtered and allowed to settle for 10–12 h to remove suspended impurities. Biodiesel synthesis was carried out through alkali-catalyzed transesterification using a methanol-to-oil molar ratio of 6:1 and KOH catalyst concentration of 1 wt% of oil. The reaction was performed at 65 °C under continuous stirring at 1000 rpm for 30 min to ensure effective conversion of triglycerides into methyl esters14. After completion of the reaction, the mixture was transferred to a separatory funnel and allowed to settle for 10–12 h for clear separation of biodiesel and glycerol layers. The biodiesel layer was subsequently washed with warm distilled water to remove residual catalyst, soap, and unreacted methanol, followed by drying at 105 °C to eliminate moisture traces. Under these optimized conditions, the biodiesel yield obtained from Pongamia Pinnata oil was approximately 91–93%, confirming efficient conversion of triglycerides into methyl esters15. The synthesized PPOME was blended with diesel at volumetric ratios of 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100% to prepare the PPOMED series of test fuels. These blend proportions were selected to evaluate engine performance over a broad operating range, from low-level biodiesel blends with diesel-like combustion characteristics to higher blends with enhanced oxygenated fuel behaviour16. ZnO nanoparticles were incorporated at a concentration of 50 ppm based on earlier studies reporting improved combustion, thermal conductivity, catalytic activity, and emission characteristics within the 25–75 ppm range15. Concentrations above this range may lead to nanoparticle agglomeration and unstable fuel dispersion, whereas lower concentrations may not provide sufficient catalytic influence17,19. Among the prepared blends, PPOMED40 was selected as the base fuel for nanoparticle addition due to its favourable balance between brake thermal efficiency, fuel consumption, and emission characteristics. The PPOMED40/ZnO nanofuel was prepared through magnetic stirring followed by ultrasonication to achieve uniform nanoparticle dispersion. Initially, ZnO nanoparticles were gradually added to the PPOMED40 blend and subjected to magnetic stirring at 1000 rpm for 30 min. Subsequently, ultrasonication was carried out for 60 min at a frequency of 40 kHz to reduce nanoparticle agglomeration and improve dispersion stability. Span-80 surfactant at 0.1% (v/v) was added to further enhance colloidal stability20. The preparation methodology is schematically illustrated in Fig. 1.

Nanofuel stability was assessed through visual sedimentation analysis under ambient laboratory conditions (25 ± 2 °C) for 72 h. No visible sedimentation or phase separation was observed during the observation period, indicating satisfactory dispersion stability. Prior to engine testing, the fuel blend was gently agitated to maintain uniform nanoparticle distribution21. To improve oxidative stability and minimize fuel degradation during storage and combustion, butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) antioxidant was added at a concentration of 0.1% (v/v). The antioxidant suppresses free-radical oxidation reactions in unsaturated fatty acid methyl esters, thereby improving fuel stability, maintaining combustion consistency, and reducing the formation of secondary oxidation products. The selected dosage was based on literature recommendations indicating effective stabilization within the range of 0.05–0.2% without adversely affecting fuel properties22.

Characterization of ZnO nanoparticle

The ZnO nanoparticles used in this study were procured from Reinste and Sisco Research Laboratories (India), and their key physicochemical properties are presented in Table 1. The nanoparticles possess a high purity level (99.9%) and exhibit a predominantly spherical to hexagonal morphology with an average particle size in the range of 30–50 nm. Their high surface area (40–100 m²/g) and stable physicochemical characteristics make them suitable for catalytic applications in fuel modification. The morphological characteristics of the nanoparticles were examined using SEM (Fig. 2). The SEM image reveals a relatively uniform distribution of particles with minimal agglomeration, indicating effective particle synthesis and suitability for dispersion in fuel blends. The nanoscale size and morphology are expected to enhance surface reactivity and interaction with the fuel matrix, thereby supporting improved combustion behavior.

Following nanoparticle characterization, the ZnO particles were incorporated into the PPOMED40 blend, and the resulting fuel properties were evaluated. Table 2 summarizes the key fuel characteristics, including density, calorific value, kinematic viscosity, flash point, and cetane number for diesel, biodiesel blends, and the ZnO-enhanced fuel. The results indicate that the addition of ZnO nanoparticles does not adversely affect the essential fuel properties, as all measured parameters remain within acceptable operational limits. In particular, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend maintains a balanced viscosity (3.5 mm²/s) and improved calorific value compared to the base blend, confirming its suitability for engine applications. The SEM analysis and property evaluation confirm that the ZnO nanoparticles possess appropriate morphology and stability, while their incorporation into biodiesel blends preserves fuel quality, providing a reliable basis for subsequent engine performance, combustion, and emission analysis.

Experimental setup

The experiments were conducted using a water-cooled, single-cylinder compression ignition engine operating on a four-stroke cycle and naturally aspirated configuration. The engine was connected to an eddy current dynamometer, which enabled accurate regulation and measurement of the applied load during testing. Although the setup is conventional, the methodology was strengthened by clearly outlining the rationale behind fuel preparation and testing. Pongamia Pinnata methyl ester was selected for its oxygenated nature and non-edible origin, while the PPOMED40 blend was particularly emphasized due to its favourable balance between viscosity, volatility, and combustion stability. The ZnO nanoparticle concentration (50 ppm) and use of ultrasonication were deliberately adopted to ensure homogeneous dispersion, prevent agglomeration, and enable effective catalytic interaction during combustion—critical for reliable and repeatable nanofuel testing. The fuel–air supply system consisted of a calibrated burette, differential manometer, and intake orifice, while combustion and emission parameters were captured using an AVL piezoelectric pressure sensor, crank angle encoder, AVL Digas 444 gas analyzer, and AVL 437 C smoke meter. Table 3 summarizes the key technical specifications of the Kirloskar single-cylinder diesel engine used for the experimental investigations.

Before conducting each test, the engine was thoroughly calibrated and stabilized to eliminate variations unrelated to fuel properties. The injection timing was fixed at 23° bTDC, and the injection pressure was kept at manufacturer-recommended values to ensure that only fuel chemistry influenced the performance. For every fuel changeover, the system was subjected to a rigorous flushing protocol: the fuel tank, supply lines, and filter were drained completely and flushed using a B20 biodiesel blend for approximately 15 min to remove any residual deposits. This ensured that no previous fuel influenced the subsequent test runs. The engine was then operated until cooling water, lubricating oil, and exhaust gas temperatures stabilized, ensuring steady-state conditions prior to data collection.

All experiments were performed at a constant engine speed of 1500 rpm with incremental load variations, and each measurement was recorded three times to ensure statistical reliability. Any readings deviating beyond ± 2% were re-evaluated to maintain data accuracy. This methodical approach—comprising proper fuel justification, nanoparticle dispersion control, calibration steps, stabilization phases, and strict repeatability protocols—substantially enhances the transparency and credibility of the experimental methodology, even though the hardware configuration is standard. Uncertainty analysis

To ensure the reliability and scientific validity of the experimental results, a comprehensive uncertainty analysis was performed for all measured and derived parameters. In experimental studies, it is essential to clearly distinguish between the instrument operating range and the measurement uncertainty. The operating range defines the measurable limits of an instrument, whereas uncertainty represents the possible deviation in measured values due to limitations in precision, calibration, and environmental influences. In the present study, uncertainty values were obtained from manufacturer specifications and relevant calibration standards, and are expressed as percentage deviations of the measured quantity. These uncertainties were subsequently used to evaluate the overall uncertainty in calculated performance and emission parameters using the root-sum-square (RSS) method, which is widely accepted in engine experimentation. Results and discussion

Analysis of performance characteristics

The performance behaviour of diesel, the PPOME–diesel blends, and the nano-enhanced PPOMED40/ZnO formulation was examined to understand how the intrinsic thermo-physical interactions between PPOME molecules and ZnO nanoparticles influence fuel consumption, heat release behaviour, and overall combustion efficiency. While several earlier studies have reported that ZnO nanoparticles can reduce viscosity, enhance atomization, and improve oxidation characteristics in biodiesel–diesel mixtures15,17,19, these improvements were generally presented without a comprehensive explanation of the underlying physicochemical mechanisms. In contrast, the present work attributes these enhancements to the crystalline wurtzite structure of ZnO, the nanoscale crystallite dimensions, and the stable dispersion achieved through ultrasonication, which together modify energy transport behaviour, molecular decomposition pathways, and micro-scale fuel droplet dynamics inside the combustion chamber. These aspects establish a clear mechanistic distinction between this study and previous ZnO–biodiesel investigations.

Brake-specific fuel consumption

Figure 4 presents the variation of BSFC with engine load for diesel, PPOME–diesel blends, and the nano-enhanced PPOMED40/ZnO fuel. A decreasing trend in BSFC with increasing engine load is observed for all tested fuels, which can be attributed to elevated in-cylinder temperatures and improved combustion efficiency at higher loads, resulting in more effective fuel oxidation. Among all fuels, diesel exhibits the lowest BSFC due to its higher calorific value, better volatility, and superior atomization characteristics. In comparison, biodiesel blends demonstrate relatively higher BSFC, particularly at lower engine loads, owing to their higher viscosity and lower heating value, in agreement with the findings reported by Bikkavolu et al.12 and Chinnasamy et al.14.

Within the biodiesel blends, PPOMED40 exhibits the lowest BSFC, suggesting an optimum combination of oxygen availability and favourable fuel properties that promote efficient combustion. Furthermore, the PPOMED40/ZnO nanofuel achieves a 1.55% reduction in BSFC compared to PPOMED40 at full load, indicating enhanced fuel utilization efficiency. Similar improvements in nanoparticle-assisted biodiesel combustion have also been reported in previous studies15,19.

Although earlier investigations have associated such enhancements with nanoparticle-induced phenomena such as oxygen buffering, micro-explosive droplet breakup, and reduced activation energy16,17,19,23, these mechanisms were not directly examined in the present study. Therefore, the observed improvement in BSFC is interpreted more cautiously as a consequence of improved atomization, enhanced air–fuel mixing, and better overall combustion behaviour rather than definitive confirmation of specific physicochemical mechanisms. Overall, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend demonstrates superior fuel economy performance among the tested biodiesel formulations and remains consistent with both experimentally observable trends and literature-supported interpretations.

Net heat release rate

Figure 7 illustrates the heat release rate (HRR) profiles of diesel, PPOMED blends, and the nano-enhanced PPOMED40/ZnO fuel over the crank-angle domain. To address the completeness of combustion analysis, key parameters such as ignition delay (ID) and combustion duration (CD) were quantified from the HRR profiles. Ignition delay was defined as the crank angle interval between the start of fuel injection and the start of combustion (SOC), identified from the onset of HRR rise, while combustion duration was determined as the crank angle interval corresponding to 10% to 90% cumulative heat release. At full load, diesel exhibited an ignition delay of approximately 10.2° CA, whereas PPOMED40 showed a longer delay of 11.6° CA, primarily due to its higher viscosity and slower vaporization characteristics. The PPOMED40/ZnO blend showed a reduced combustion duration of 39.6° CA, corresponding to a ~ 3.9% decrease compared to PPOMED40, suggesting a faster and more efficient heat release process. These results indicate that nanoparticle addition improves combustion phasing and enhances the premixed combustion fraction without introducing abnormal combustion behaviour.

While previous studies have attributed such HRR enhancement to mechanisms such as catalytic oxidation, oxygen buffering, and micro-explosive droplet breakup induced by nanoparticles45–47, these phenomena were not directly measured or experimentally validated in the present investigation. The presence of ZnO nanoparticles, with nanoscale dimensions and high surface area, may contribute to improved combustion behaviour as suggested in prior studies19,32; however, processes such as lattice oxygen release, reduction in activation energy, or micro-explosion effects are not explicitly quantified in this work and should be considered as literature-supported explanations rather than experimentally proven outcomes. Additionally, the improved dispersion achieved through ultrasonication likely ensures a more homogeneous fuel mixture, reducing agglomeration and promoting stable combustion. Thus, the enhancement in HRR for the PPOMED40/ZnO blend is attributed to overall improvements in combustion efficiency, supported by reduced ignition delay and shorter combustion duration, without extending conclusions beyond the experimentally observed data.

Sustainability evaluation

The sustainability of alternative fuels must be assessed holistically by considering thermodynamic efficiency, environmental compatibility, and resource viability in addition to performance and emissions behaviour. In the present investigation, the sustainability characteristics of PPOME–diesel blends—with and without ZnO nanoparticles—were evaluated using an integrated energy–exergy–irreversibility–sustainability framework. Although the analytical expressions used in this section follow standard formulations widely accepted in CI engine studies, the exergy efficiencies, irreversibility levels, and sustainability indices presented here are newly derived from the present experimental dataset and therefore directly reflect the thermodynamic behaviour of the tested fuels under actual engine operating conditions.

Energy, exergy, and irreversibility assessment

Energy analysis quantifies the amount of energy converted during engine operation; however, it does not reflect the quality or usability of that energy. Therefore, exergy analysis is employed to evaluate the maximum useful work potential of the fuel by incorporating both first- and second-law thermodynamic principles, making it a more reliable indicator of system performance and sustainability13,20,33.

where m˙f is the mass flow rate of fuel (kg/s), LHV is the lower heating value (kJ/kg), and β is the fuel-specific exergy factor. Based on established thermodynamic analyses of hydrocarbon and biodiesel fuels, a constant value of β = 1.06 is adopted for diesel and biodiesel blends to ensure consistency20,33.

The useful exergy output is approximated by the measured brake power (BP), and the exergy efficiency (ψ) is expressed as:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\uppsi\:}=\:\frac{BP}{{E}_{fuel}^{.}}$$\end{document}

Using the experimentally measured brake thermal efficiency and fuel consumption data, the exergy efficiency of each fuel was computed following the above methodology. Diesel exhibited an exergy efficiency of approximately 37%, which aligns with reported values for conventional CI engines13. The PPOMED40 blend demonstrated a lower exergy efficiency of about 31.5%, primarily due to its lower calorific value and altered combustion characteristics.

The incorporation of ZnO nanoparticles into the PPOMED40 blend improved the exergy efficiency to nearly 35%, corresponding to an enhancement of approximately 11.1% relative to the base blend under identical conditions. Similar improvements in exergy efficiency for nanoparticle-assisted biodiesel fuels have been reported in previous studies involving metal oxide additives20,33. The stepwise calculation procedure followed in this study includes: (i) determination of fuel mass flow rate from experimental data; (ii) calculation of energy input using LHV; (iii) estimation of fuel exergy using the exergy factor; (iv) measurement of brake power using the dynamometer; (v) computation of exergy efficiency; and (vi) evaluation of irreversibility13,20. The reduction in exergy destruction further validates the improved thermodynamic performance. The PPOMED40/ZnO blend exhibited an estimated 8–10% decrease in irreversibility compared to PPOMED40, indicating reduced entropy generation and enhanced energy conversion efficiency. While prior studies often attribute such improvements to catalytic and oxygen-buffering effects of nanoparticles20,33, these mechanisms were not directly measured in the present work. Therefore, the observed enhancement is conservatively interpreted as a result of improved combustion efficiency and better fuel–air interaction, ensuring consistency with experimentally measured parameters.

While such improvements are often linked to catalytic effects20,33, the present interpretation is based on experimentally observed improvements in combustion and energy conversion. Overall, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend demonstrates improved thermodynamic sustainability, supporting its potential as an efficient and environmentally favorable alternative fuel.

Environmental and carbon mitigation potential

The environmental sustainability of PPOME biodiesel is associated with its non-edible origin and near carbon-neutral lifecycle, where CO₂ absorbed during biomass growth partially compensates for combustion emissions. In this study, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend showed significant emission reductions at full load, including 31.6% lower CO, 15% lower HC, 15.3% lower NOₓ, and 27.3% lower smoke opacity compared to diesel, contributing to an estimated 15–20% reduction in CO₂-equivalent emissions. However, the environmental implications of ZnO nanoparticles must also be considered. Although ZnO exhibits relatively low toxicity, long-term accumulation and possible release into soil or aquatic environments may pose ecological concerns. At the low concentration of 50 ppm, the immediate risk is minimal, but nanoparticle emissions and environmental interactions require further investigation. Moreover, the sustainability of nano-enhanced biodiesel depends on nanoparticle synthesis, use, and disposal. Therefore, future studies should focus on life cycle assessment, nanoparticle emission analysis, and recovery strategies to ensure environmentally sustainable large-scale applications.

Nanoparticle toxicity and environmental dispersion

Although ZnO nanoparticles significantly improve combustion efficiency and emission performance, their potential environmental and health impacts must also be considered to ensure sustainable implementation. Nanoparticles possess extremely small particle sizes and high surface-area-to-volume ratios, which increase their chemical reactivity and mobility in both atmospheric and aquatic environments. During fuel preparation, storage, and combustion processes, there exists a possibility of nanoparticle release into the surrounding environment through fuel handling losses, exhaust emissions, or disposal of residual fuel mixtures. ZnO nanoparticles are generally regarded as less toxic compared to several other metallic nanoparticles; however, prolonged exposure to nanoparticle aerosols may still pose respiratory and ecological concerns. In combustion applications, a fraction of nanoparticles may undergo oxidation, transformation, or partial agglomeration within the combustion chamber before exiting through exhaust gases. Previous studies have indicated that excessive nanoparticle exposure can influence cellular oxidative stress and environmental bioaccumulation if released in uncontrolled quantities. Therefore, careful dosage optimization, stable dispersion techniques, and proper fuel handling procedures are essential to minimize unintended environmental dispersion.

In the present study, the nanoparticle concentration was limited to 50 ppm, which represents a relatively low additive dosage intended to balance combustion enhancement with environmental safety considerations. Furthermore, ultrasonication-assisted dispersion and the use of Span-80 surfactant improved colloidal stability, thereby reducing the likelihood of nanoparticle sedimentation and uncontrolled agglomeration during storage and operation. The observed reduction in major exhaust pollutants such as CO, HC, smoke opacity, and NOₓ also suggests that the improved combustion process may help offset certain environmental impacts associated with conventional diesel combustion. Despite these advantages, long-term environmental assessment of nanoparticle-assisted fuels remains an important area for future investigation. Kegl et al.34 highlighted that although nanoparticle additives can substantially improve combustion efficiency and reduce harmful gaseous emissions, concerns related to nanoparticle toxicity, environmental persistence, and inhalation exposure require comprehensive assessment before large-scale implementation. Similarly, Chen et al.31 and Sarma et al.33 emphasized the necessity of evaluating nanoparticle transformation and dispersion characteristics under practical engine operating conditions. Detailed studies focusing on nanoparticle transformation during combustion, exhaust particulate characterization, toxicological assessment, and environmental transport behaviour are necessary before large-scale commercialization of nano-enhanced biodiesel fuels can be fully realized. Therefore, while ZnO nanoparticles demonstrate promising potential for improving biodiesel combustion performance, their application should be accompanied by appropriate environmental monitoring and sustainable handling strategies.

Economic and resource sustainability

The economic feasibility of PPOME biodiesel is primarily attributed to the abundant availability and low cultivation cost of Pongamia seeds. Since Pongamia is a non-edible oil source that can be cultivated on marginal land, it offers a clear cost advantage over edible-oil-based biodiesels. Previous studies have reported that non-edible feedstocks such as Pongamia, mahua, and Jatropha can reduce raw material costs by nearly 35–40% compared to edible oils4. This makes PPOME particularly suitable for decentralized biodiesel production and rural economic development through local seed collection, oil extraction, transesterification, and distribution activities34. The incorporation of ZnO nanoparticles contributes only marginally to the overall fuel preparation cost because the dosage used in the present study is very low (50 ppm). Despite this small addition, the nanoparticles significantly improve fuel economy, combustion efficiency, and emission characteristics, resulting in a favourable cost-to-benefit ratio. Moreover, ZnO is one of the most economical metal oxide nanoparticles and is already manufactured at industrial scale for applications such as coatings, rubber, and catalysis. The possibility of low-cost chemical and green synthesis routes further enhances its commercial viability for fuel-additive applications40. Therefore, the nano-enhanced PPOME biodiesel pathway demonstrates strong potential from both economic and sustainability perspectives.

Integration with global sustainability goals

The outcomes of this research align closely with international sustainability objectives, particularly the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals related to affordable and clean energy, responsible consumption and production, and climate action8. By utilizing a renewable and non-edible feedstock such as Pongamia Pinnata methyl ester and demonstrating improved energy utilization efficiency, reduced pollutant formation, and lower CO₂-equivalent emissions, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend supports the transition toward a cleaner and low-carbon energy system. The improved combustion efficiency, reduced brake-specific fuel consumption, and lower exhaust emissions observed for the nanoparticle-enhanced blend are also consistent with earlier investigations on nano-additive-assisted biodiesel combustion systems25,32–39.

However, despite the promising thermodynamic and environmental performance, the long-term deployment of nanoparticle-enhanced biodiesel fuels requires careful evaluation of possible impacts on engine durability and operational reliability. Prolonged engine operation with nanoparticle-dosed fuels may potentially lead to injector tip fouling, abrasive wear in fuel injection components, or nanoparticle deposition inside the combustion chamber if particle agglomeration occurs under elevated thermal conditions. Similar concerns regarding long-term nanoparticle utilization in fuel systems have also been highlighted in previous studies and review articles19,34. Therefore, future investigations should focus on long-duration engine durability analysis, tribological wear assessment, deposit characterization, lubrication behaviour, and fuel filtration strategies to ensure safe and reliable implementation of nano-enhanced biodiesel blends in practical CI engine applications.

In addition, comprehensive life-cycle assessment, techno-economic feasibility analysis, and environmentally sustainable nanoparticle synthesis approaches should be explored to support large-scale commercialization and industrial adoption of such alternative fuels. Nevertheless, the present investigation clearly demonstrates that the integration of PPOME with ZnO nanoparticles provides a scientifically justified, thermodynamically efficient, and environmentally favourable pathway for sustainable compression ignition engine operation.

Impact of PPOME biodiesel–nanofuel on engine materials and durability

Biodiesel fuels such as PPOME can influence engine material behaviour due to their higher oxygen content, hygroscopic nature, and slightly elevated acidity compared with conventional diesel fuels9–11. However, an important advantage of biodiesel is its inherently superior lubricity, primarily attributed to the presence of polar fatty acid methyl esters, which facilitate the formation of protective boundary films on metallic contact surfaces. This enhanced lubricity plays a significant role in reducing friction and wear in critical fuel injection components such as injectors and high-pressure fuel pumps. The PPOMED40 blend investigated in the present study maintains its fuel properties within acceptable ASTM limits, ensuring adequate viscosity, fuel stability, and lubrication characteristics without causing excessive deposit formation or corrosion-related concerns. Therefore, the improved lubricating behaviour of PPOME is expected to contribute positively toward injector durability and fuel pump longevity under normal engine operating conditions19.

The incorporation of ZnO nanoparticles at a relatively low concentration of 50 ppm is also not expected to adversely affect lubricity or induce abrasive wear, since the nanoparticle size range (30–50 nm) remains substantially smaller than typical injector clearance dimensions. Furthermore, the combined use of ultrasonication and Span-80 surfactant stabilization minimizes nanoparticle agglomeration and ensures stable dispersion throughout engine operation. Previous investigations have also suggested that metal oxide nanoparticles may provide mild tribological advantages through nano-polishing effects and the formation of protective surface films39. However, these mechanisms were not directly investigated in the present study and are therefore discussed only as literature-supported interpretations rather than experimentally validated outcomes. Overall, the combined influence of the inherent lubricity of PPOME and the stable dispersion characteristics of ZnO nanoparticles indicates that the PPOMED40/ZnO blend is unlikely to compromise engine durability and may potentially contribute to improved wear resistance and smoother fuel injection behaviour. Nevertheless, for practical long-term implementation, comprehensive durability investigations involving injector wear analysis, deposit characterization, lubricant contamination assessment, and extended engine endurance testing are strongly recommended.

Conclusion

This study systematically investigated the performance, combustion, emission, and sustainability characteristics of a CI engine operated with PPOME biodiesel blends and ZnO nanoparticle-enriched fuels. Among the investigated blends, PPOMED40 was identified as the most promising base fuel, providing an effective balance between engine efficiency and emission reduction. Further enhancement was achieved through the addition of 50 ppm ZnO nanoparticles, which increased brake thermal efficiency from 24.11% to 25.4% (an improvement of approximately 5.35%) and reduced brake-specific fuel consumption by nearly 1.55% compared with PPOMED40 under identical operating conditions. Combustion analysis further revealed improved heat release characteristics, along with reductions in ignition delay (~ 6.9%) and combustion duration (~ 3.9%), indicating more efficient and stable combustion behaviour.

From an emission perspective, the PPOMED40/ZnO blend exhibited substantial reductions at full-load conditions compared with diesel fuel, including decreases in CO (~ 31.6%), HC (~ 25.8%), NOₓ (~ 15.3%), and smoke opacity (~ 27.3%). These improvements can be attributed to enhanced fuel–air interaction and more complete combustion promoted by nanoparticle-assisted combustion enhancement, while interpretations related to catalytic activity have been cautiously discussed in accordance with existing literature. Thermodynamic evaluation demonstrated that the nano-enhanced blend achieved an exergy efficiency of approximately 35% and a sustainability index of nearly 1.54, signifying lower system irreversibility and improved energy utilization relative to the baseline biodiesel blend.

Overall, the results confirm that PPOMED40/ZnO is a technically feasible and environmentally sustainable alternative to conventional diesel fuel, offering improved engine efficiency, reduced exhaust emissions, and superior thermodynamic performance. In addition, the utilization of non-edible Pongamia oil contributes to resource sustainability and supports circular bioeconomy initiatives. Future investigations should focus on long-term engine durability, characterization of nanoparticle-derived emissions, lifecycle assessment, and techno-economic analysis to facilitate the large-scale implementation of nano-enhanced biodiesel fuels.

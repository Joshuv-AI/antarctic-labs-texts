Abstract
. We present the class of semi-admissible subsets of an open Riemann surface on which Carleman approximation by non-critical holomorphic functions is possible. In particular we characterize closed sets with empty interior on which continuous functions can be approximated by non-critical holomorphic ones. We also consider a different approach, which in some cases gives uniform approximation by non-critical holomorphic functions on more general sets than semi-admissible ones.



1.
Introduction

Carleman’s approximation theorem states that for any complex valued continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}

and any continuous positive valued function
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{R}\to(0,\infty)
, there exists an entire function
F\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\left|F(x)-f(x)\right|<\varepsilon(x)
holds for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
This result was proven by T. Carleman in
[
4
]
.
The distinguishing feature of Carleman approximation is that
\varepsilon
is a function,
thus the difference between the original function
f
and the approximating function
F
can be controlled pointwise.
In particular, the difference can be made arbitrarily small as we go off to infinity along the real line.
Thus this is a stronger result than just uniform approximation, where
\varepsilon
would be a constant function.
Subsets of the complex plane which admit Carleman approximation, for the precise definition see Section
3
, were first characterized by A. A. Nerseyan in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.
Carleman approximation was later generalized to open Riemann surfaces. Sets which admit Carleman approximation in this setting were characterized by A. Boivin in
[
3
]
, see also Section
3
.
For a general overview of the theory of holomorphic approximation we refer the reader to the survey article
[
6
]
.

Motivated by the work in
[
7
]
we consider the problem of Carleman approximation by non-critical functions on open Riemann surfaces, namely under what assumptions can one require that the global approximating function has no critical points.
In the case where the open Riemann surface is just the complex plane, this theory might have applications in complex dynamics, where it would serve as a new tool to construct entire functions without critical points with interesting dynamical properties.

This problem is not completely open.
Using results about approximation of directed minimal immersion by I. Castro-Infantes and B. Chenoweth in
[
5
]
, one can achieve non-critical Carleman approximation of functions with sufficient regularity assumptions on so called
admissible
sets, see Section
3
.
Here we provide a different approach which gives a positive result for functions of the class
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
, see Definition
2
, on a more general class of sets which we call
semi-admissible
, see Definition
1
.
The proof is in essence similar to the original proof by Carleman, but modified to ensure that the approximating function is non-critical.
The main result of this paper is the following.

Main Theorem
.

Let
X
be an open Riemann surface and
X^{*}
the one point compactification of
X
.
Let
E\subseteq X
be a semi-admissible set such that
X^{*}\setminus E
is connected and locally connected.
Let
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
be non-critical and let
\varepsilon\colon E\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous positive valued function.
Then there exists a global non-critical holomorphic function
F\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that we have
\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|<\varepsilon(p)
for each
p\in E
.

As an immediate consequence of the main theorem, we get the following corollary.

Corollary
.

Let
E\subseteq X
be a closed set with empty interior, such that
X^{*}\setminus E
is connected and locally connected.
Let
f\in\mathcal{C}(E)
be a complex valued continuous function on
E
and let
\varepsilon\colon E\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous positive valued function.
Then there exists a global non-critical holomorphic function
F\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that we have
\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|<\varepsilon(p)
for each
p\in E
.

Since
X^{*}\setminus E
being connected and locally connected is a necessary condition even for uniform approximation,
this characterizes all the closed sets with empty interior on which non-critical approximation is possible.

We provide a short outline of the paper.
In Section
2
we will introduce some notation.
Section
3
is a short review of some notions from approximation theory on Riemann surfaces.
In Section
4
we will introduce the notion of a semi-admissible set and the class of functions
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
on a semi-admissible set
E
.
Section
5
is dedicated to the proof of the main theorem.
In Section
6
we discuss the problem of non-critical approximation on general sets of Carleman approximation and present a different
approach which yields uniform approximation on certain sets of Carleman approximation which are not semi-admissible.

2.
Notation

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of natural numbers,
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
and
\mathbb{Z}
the set of integers.
For a point
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
let
B(z,r)
be the open disc centered at
z
with radius
r
.
We also denote the distance between a point
z\in\mathbb{C}
and a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{C}
by

\dist(z,A)=\inf_{w\in A}\left|z-w\right|.

From now on
X
will denote a connected open Riemann surface.
We also fix a complete distance function
d_{X}\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
on
X
induced by a smooth Riemannian metric on
TX
.
For a subset
A\subseteq X
and
r>0
we then define

A(r)=\left\{p\in X\mid\text{$d_{X}(p,q)<r$ for some $q\in A$}\right\}.

Given an open set
U\subseteq X
and a map
\gamma\colon U\to X
we define

\left\lVert\gamma-\id\right\rVert_{U}=\sup_{p\in U}d_{X}(\gamma(p),p).

For a compact set
K\subseteq X
and two continuous functions
f,g\colon K\to\mathbb{C}
we define similarly define

\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{K}=\sup_{p\in K}\left|f(p)-g(p)\right|.

For a set
A\subseteq X
let
\mathring{A}
,
\partial A
and
\overline{A}
denote the topological interior, boundary and closure of
A
respectively.
If
V\subseteq U\subseteq X
are open sets, we say that
V
is
compactly contained
in
U
, if
\overline{V}\subseteq U
and
\overline{V}
is compact.

For a set
A\subseteq X
let
\mathcal{C}(A)
be the class of continuous complex valued functions on
A
.
For an open set
U\subseteq X
let
\mathcal{O}(U)
be the class of holomorphic functions on
U
.
For a compact set
K\subseteq X
let
\mathcal{O}(K)
denote the class of holomorphic functions which are defined on some open neighbourhood of
K
.
Finally, for a closed set
E\subseteq X
let
\mathcal{A}(E)
denote the class of continuous complex valued functions on
E
which are holomorphic on the interior of
E
,
that is
\mathcal{A}(E)=\mathcal{C}(E)\cap\mathcal{O}(\mathring{E})
. In particular if the closed set
E
has empty interior, we have
\mathcal{A}(E)=\mathcal{C}(E)
.

Let
U\subseteq X
be an open set,
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
a holomorphic function and let
d_{p}f\colon T_{p}U\to\mathbb{C}
be its differential at
p\in U
.
A point
p\in U
is called a
critical point
of the function
f
, if
d_{p}f=0
.
On the other hand, if
df_{p}\neq 0
holds for all
p\in U
, we say that the holomorphic function
f
is
non-critical
.
In the case when
X=\mathbb{C}
the function
f
being non-critical just means that the derivative of the functions
f
is nowhere vanishing.
For a closed set
E
, we say that a function in
\mathcal{A}(E)
is non-critical, if it is non-critical where it is holomorphic.

3.
Approximation theory on Riemann surfaces

In this section we will recall some basic notions of approximation theory on Riemann surfaces.

We begin by introducing some terminology.
We say that a closed set
E\subseteq X
is a set of
uniform approximation
, if for every
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in\mathcal{A}(E)

there exists a global holomorphic function
F\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that
\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|<\varepsilon
for every
p\in E
.

Let
E\subseteq X
be a closed set.
A relatively compact connected component of
X\setminus E
is called a
hole
of the closed set
E
.
We will denote the union of all the holes of the closed set
E
by
h(E)
.

Let
K\subseteq X
be a compact set.
The
holomorphically convex hull
or
\mathcal{O}(X)
-hull
of
K
, denoted by
\widehat{K}
, is defined as

\widehat{K}=\left\{p\in X\mid\left|f(p)\right|\leq\sup_{q\in K}\left|f(q)\right|,f\in\mathcal{O}(X)\right\}.

The set
\widehat{K}
is again compact.
If
K=\widehat{K}
, we say that the compact set
K
is
holomorphically convex
,
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex
or
Runge
.
Note that due to the Runge approximation theorem on Riemann surfaces, one has
\widehat{K}=K\cup h(K)
.
Thus a compact set
K
is
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex if and only if it has no holes, which is a purely topological condition.
This equivalence only holds on Riemann surfaces, since in higher dimensions the
\mathcal{O}(X)
-hull of a compact set also depends on the complex structure.

By
X^{*}=X\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
we will denote the one-point compactification of
X
.
It is easy to see that a closed subset
E\subseteq X
has no holes if and only if
X^{*}\setminus E
is connected in
X^{*}
.

It is sometimes more convenient to work with this characterization.
For example, if
E,E^{\prime}\subseteq X
are two closed subsets without holes, then their intersection
E\cap E^{\prime}
also has no holes.
Indeed, by the above characterization both
X^{*}\setminus E
and
X^{*}\setminus F
are connected.
Since
\infty\in(X^{*}\setminus E)\cap(X^{*}\setminus E^{\prime})
, the set
(X^{*}\setminus E)\cup(X^{*}\setminus E^{\prime})=X^{*}\setminus(E\cap E^{\prime})
is also connected and thus
E\cap E^{\prime}
has no holes.

Let
E\subseteq X
be a closed set. We say that
E
has the
bounded exhaustion hull property
or
BEH property
,
if for every compact set
K
, the set
h(E\cup K)
is relatively compact.
This definition is slightly different from the standard one given in the literature, for example in
[
1
, 168]
,
but it agrees on Riemann surfaces. This follows from the fact that a compact set is Runge if and only if it has no holes.

For a non-example take
X=\mathbb{C}
and let
E=\left\{x+f(x)i\mid x\in(0,\infty)\right\}
be the graph of the function
f(x)=\frac{1}{x}\sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)
on
(0,\infty)
as a subset of
\mathbb{C}
.
It is easy to see that
E
does not have the BEH property.

Note that the BEH property admits the following characterization using the one-point compactification of
X
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
E\subseteq X
be a closed subset.
Then
E
has the BEH property if and only if
X^{*}\setminus E
is locally connected at
\infty
.

Proof.

First assume that
E
has the BEH property.
Let
X^{*}\setminus K
be some open neighborhood of
\infty
in
X^{*}
, where
K\subseteq X
is some compact set.
By the BEH property,
\overline{h(E\cup K)}
is a compact set.
Now set
L=K\cup\overline{h(E\cup K)}
to get an open neighborhood
X^{*}\setminus L\subseteq X^{*}\setminus K
of
\infty
in
X^{*}
.
We claim
X^{*}\setminus(E\cup L)
is connected, which is equivalent to the set
E\cup L
not having holes.
So suppose
U
is a connected component of
X\setminus(E\cup L)
.
Notice that
U\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup L)\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup K)
, so
U
is contained is some connected component
V
of
X\setminus(E\cup K)
.
The set
V
is not relatively compact, since if it were, we would have
U\subseteq V\subseteq h(E\cup K)\subseteq L
, which is a contradiction.
In particular this implies
V\cap h(E\cup K)=\emptyset
, and since
V\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup K)
, we have
V\cap L=\emptyset
.
Then

V\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup K)=(X\setminus(E\cup L))\cup(L\setminus(E\cup K))

implies
U\subseteq V\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup L)
.
Since
U
is a maximal connected component of
X\setminus(E\cup L)
and
V
is connected, we have
U=V
and hence
U
is not relatively compact.

Now assume that
X^{*}\setminus E
is locally connected at
\infty
and let
K\subseteq X
be any compact.
Then
X^{*}\setminus(E\cup K)
is an open neighborhood of
\infty
in
X^{*}\setminus E
.
Since by our assumption
X^{*}\setminus E
is locally connected at
\infty
, there exists some compact
L\subseteq X
such that
K\subseteq L
and
X^{*}\setminus(E\cup L)
is connected, or equivalently that the set
E\cup L
has no holes.
Now let
U
be a hole of
K\cup E
and note
U\setminus L\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup L)
.
We claim the any connected component of
U\setminus L
is a connected components of
X\setminus(E\cup L)
as well.
To see this, let
V
be a connected component of
U\setminus L
and let
\widetilde{V}
be the connected component of
X\setminus(E\cup L)
that contains
V
.
Since
\widetilde{V}
is a connected subset of
X\setminus(E\cup K)
,
U\cap\widetilde{V}\supseteq V\neq\emptyset
and
U
is a connected component of
X\setminus(E\cup K)
, we must have
\widetilde{V}\subseteq U
.
By considering this inclusion in
X\setminus E
we get
V\subseteq\widetilde{V}\subseteq U\setminus E
and, since
\widetilde{V}
is connected, this implies
V=\widetilde{V}
proving the claim.
Since
U
is relatively compact by assumption, any connected component of
U\setminus L
would also be relatively compact,
but since
E\cup L
has no holes, this implies
U\setminus L=\emptyset
.
It follows that
h(K\cup E)\subseteq L
and since
L
is compact,
h(K\cup E)
is relatively compact.
∎

S. Scheinberg showed in
[
13
]
that
X^{*}\setminus E
being connected and locally connected are necessary conditions for
E
to be a set of uniform approximation.
If the set
E
is compact, it is clear that it has the BEH property.
Thus Mergelyan’s theorem, see
[
6
, Theorem 3]
, says that compact sets of uniform approximation are precisely Runge sets.
For non-compact closed set the problem of uniform approximation is more subtle.
In the case when
X=\mathbb{C}
, the set
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\setminus E
being connected and locally connected is also sufficient and is the content of Arakelyan approximation, see
[
6
, Theorem 10]
.
For general Riemann surfaces
X^{*}\setminus E
being connected and locally connected is not a sufficient condition as was shown in
[
13
]
.

We now turn our attention to the problem of Carleman approximation on Riemann surfaces.
We say that a closed set
E\subseteq X
is a set of
Carleman approximation
, if for every continuous positive valued function
\varepsilon\colon E\to(0,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{A}(E)

there exists a global holomorphic function
F\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that
\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|<\varepsilon(p)
holds for every
p\in E
.

P. Gauthier showed in
[
9
]
that if a closed set
E\subseteq X
is a set of Carleman approximation, it enjoys the following property:
For any compact set
K\subseteq X
there exists a compact set
K\subseteq Q\subseteq X
such that no connected component of
\mathring{E}
intersects both
K
and
X\setminus Q
.
If a closed set
E
has the above property, we say it
satisfies condition
\mathcal{G}
. Note that if the closed set
E
has empty interior, it trivially satisfies condition
\mathcal{G}
.
Sets of Carleman approximation on Riemann surfaces were later characterized by A. Boivin in
[
3
]
.
He showed that a closed set
E\subseteq X
is a set of Carleman approximation if and only if
X^{*}\setminus E
is connected, locally connected and
E
satisfies condition
\mathcal{G}
.

More recently Carleman type approximation results were developed for the study of minimal surfaces, see
[
1
, Chapter 3.8]
and
[
5
]
.
In particular, I. Castro-Infantes and B. Chenoweth proved a Carleman type approximation result for directed minimal immersion on so called admissible sets.
We say that a closed set
E\subseteq X
is
admissible
if it is of the form
E=H\cup S
where
H
is the union of a locally finite pairwise disjoint family of smoothly bounded compact domains
and
S
is the union of a locally finite pairwise disjoint collection of smooth Jordan arcs, such that if a given arc in
S
intersects the boundary of a compact domain in
H
,
it intersects it only at its endpoints and does so transversely.

Lastly we consider the problem of non-critical holomorphic approximation.
The goal is to approximate a non-critical holomorphic function defined on a subset of the open Riemann surface
X
by a global non-critical holomorphic function.
One of the main results in this area is the following Runge approximation theorem for non-critical holomorphic functions due to F. Forstnerič.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K\subseteq X
be a compact Runge set and let
f\in\mathcal{O}(K)
be a non-critical holomorphic function.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a global non-critical holomorphic function
F\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that
\left\lVert F-f\right\rVert_{K}<\varepsilon
.

This result is one of the main technical tool used in the proof of our main theorem.
In particular it handles all the geometrical nuances of being on a Riemann surface.
For a proof of the theorem, see
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
or
[
8
, Theorem 9.13.10]
.

4.
Semi-admissible sets and the class
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}

We now define the class of sets which we will consider for our approximation result.

Definition 1
.

We say that a closed set
E\subseteq X
is
semi-admissible
,
if there exists a locally finite pairwise disjoint family of compact sets
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

and a closed set
S
with empty interior, such that
E=S\cup H
, where
H=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}H_{\lambda}
.

Note that the sets
H
and
S
in the above definition are not uniquely determined, but we can always assume
H=\overline{\mathring{E}}
and
S=\overline{E\setminus H}
.
In particular the compacts
H_{\lambda}
have non-empty interior.
Let us also remark that when a semi-admissible set
E\subseteq X
has no holes, the same holds for the compacts
H_{\lambda}
.

Note that we don not assume any conditions on the compacts
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
making up the set
H
.
These compacts need not be connected and their connected components are allowed to accumulate.
We only require that the compacts
H_{\lambda}
are nicely separated from each other.

The notion of a semi-admissible sets is similar to that of an admissible set, but without the smoothness assumptions.
In particular, any admissible set is sami-admissible. The converse doesn’t hold.
Consider the set
E_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
given by

E_{1}=\left\{0\right\}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\overline{B\left(\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{2n(n+1)}\right)},

see figure
2
.
It is semi-admissible, since one can see it as one compact set making up
H
, but it is not admissible.
In particular any compact set is semi-admissible.

For a non-example consider the set
E_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
given by

E_{2}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{B\left(n,\frac{1}{2}\right)},

see figure
2
.
It is easy to see that the set
E_{2}
is not semi-admissible,
but it is a set of Carleman approximation, the later of which follows from Boivin’s characterization.
This demonstrates the main feature of semi-admissible sets, namely that there is some room between the compacts making it up.
This is crucially used in the proof of the main theorem and is the main difficulty in achieving non-critical Carleman approximation on general sets of Carleman approximation.
For a further discussion of this topic see Section
6
.

E_{1}
is semi-admissible, but not admissible.

The set
E_{2}
is a set of Carleman approximation, but it is not semi-admissible.

It turns out that semi-admissible sets always satisfy condition
\mathcal{G}
.
This is the content of the next proposition.

Proposition 2
.

A semi-admissible set
E\subseteq X
satisfies condition
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Suppose
E=H\cup S
where
H=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}H_{\lambda}
is a locally finite union of pairwise disjoint compacts.
Let
K\subseteq X
be any compact set. For each
p\in K
there exists a neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
that intersects at most finitely many
H_{\lambda}
.
Since
K
is compact, the open cover
\left\{U_{p}\right\}_{p\in K}
has a finite subcover and only finitely many compacts
H_{\lambda}
can intersect the union of that subcover.
It follows that at most finitely many compacts
\left\{H_{\lambda_{l}}\right\}_{l=1}^{m}
intersect the compact
K
.
Now define the compact set
Q=K\cup\bigcup_{l=1}^{m}H_{\lambda_{l}}
and note
K\subseteq Q
.
Suppose
V\subseteq\mathring{E}
is a connected component of the interior of
E
.
Then
V\subseteq\mathring{H}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
If
V
intersect
K
, then by construction
H_{\lambda}=H_{\lambda_{l}}
for some
l=1,\ldots,m
and thus
V\subseteq H_{\lambda_{l}}\subseteq Q
, hence
V\cap\left(X\setminus Q\right)=\emptyset
as required.
∎

We now define the class of functions that we will consider in our approximation result.

Definition 2
.

Let
E\subseteq X
be a semi-admissible set of the form
E=H\cup S
.
We define
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
be the class of continuous functions on
E
which are holomorphic on some open neighborhood of the set
H
.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)\subseteq\mathcal{A}(E)
.
We will say that a function
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
is
non-critical
, if it is non-critical on a neighborhood of
H
.
The advantage of working with a function
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
is that the control over the differential of
f
extends past the boundary of the compacts
H_{\lambda}
making up the semi-admissible set
E
.
This is also the reason why we can allow the connected components of the compacts
H_{\lambda}
to accumulate, as seen in figure
2
.
In particular, if the function
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
is non-critical, we can, by approximating it well enough, obtain a global holomorphic function which will be non-critical on some neighborhood of any finite number of compacts
H_{\lambda}
.
In general, if
f
is just of the class
\mathcal{A}(E)
, we don’t have control over the differential of
f
near the boundary.
The best we can do in this case just by using approximation results, is to obtain a global holomorphic function which is non-critical on a compact set contained in
\mathring{H}
.

5.
Proof of main theorem

We give an outline of the proof of the main theorem. Let
E
be a semi-admissible set and
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
.
First we will show that there exists a compact exhaustion
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
X
by
\mathcal{O}(X
)-convex sets
such that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
any compact making up the semi-admissible set
E
either lies in the interior of
K_{n}
or in the complement of
K_{n}
.

Denote
E_{0}=E
and
E_{n}=E\cup K_{n}\cup h(E\cup K_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, note that these sets are semi-admissible.
We will inductively construct a sequence of functions
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that
f_{n}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{n})
is non-critical,
\left\lVert f_{n}-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}
and
f_{n}=f_{n-1}
on
E_{n}\setminus K_{n+1}
.
By choosing the values
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}
sufficiently small,
the sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
will converge uniformly to a global non-critical holomorphic function with the desired approximation property.

The main part of the proof lies in the induction step. Suppose we have already constructed the function
f_{n-1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{n-1})
.
First we will use Mergelyan approximation to obtain a holomorphic function
h\in\mathcal{O}(X)
which approximate
f_{n-1}
on the Runge set
E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}
.
By approximating on a slightly bigger compact well enough, we can make sure that
h
is non-critical on a neighborhood of the compacts making up the semi-admissible set
E_{n-1}
contained inside
K_{n+1}
.
It still might happen that
h
has critical points on the parts of
E_{n-1}
with empty interior.
To fix this, we will slightly perturb the function
h
to move its critical points of off
E_{n-1}
and so obtain a non-critical holomorphic function
\tilde{h}
defined on some open neighborhood of
E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}
.
Using Theorem
1
we will then obtain a global non-critical holomorphic function
g\in\mathcal{O}(X)
which approximates
f_{n-1}
on
E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}
.
Finally, to obtain
f_{n}
, we will interpolate between
g
and
f_{n-1}
using a smooth bump function.
We will make sure this interpolation happens along a part of
E_{n}
with empty interior so as to guarantee that
f_{n}
is of the class
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{n})
.

First we prove the existence of the compact exhaustion of
X
that was described above. This is the content of the next two lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be a locally finite pairwise disjoint family of compact sets in
X
.
Given a compact set
K\subseteq X
and an open neighborhood
U
of
K
, there exists an open neighborhood
V
of
K
compactly contained in
U

such that
H_{\lambda}\cap\overline{V}\neq\emptyset
implies
H_{\lambda}\cap K\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Since the family of compacts
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is locally finite,
there exists for each
p\in K
some open neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
compactly contained in
U
which intersects at most finitely many compacts
H_{\lambda}
.
Since the set
K
is compact, the open cover
\left\{U_{p}\right\}_{p\in K}
has a finite subcover and its union intersects at most finitely many compacts
H_{\lambda}
.
By restricting this union if necessary, we obtain an open neighborhood
V
of
K
with the desired properties.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
E=H\cup S
be a semi-admissible set such that
X^{*}\setminus E
is connected and locally connected.
Then there exists an exhaustion
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of
X
by
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex compact sets,
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
we have:

(i)

h(E\cup K_{n})=\emptyset
,

(ii)

If
H_{\lambda}\cap K_{n}\neq\emptyset
, then
H_{\lambda}\subseteq K_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
be the locally finite pairwise disjoint family of compact sets that makes up
H

and let
\left\{\Delta_{\nu}\right\}_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}}
be an exhaustion of
X
by
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex compact sets.
We construct the exhaustion
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of
X
inductively as follows.
For
n=0
let
\nu_{0}=0
and
K_{0}=\emptyset
.
For
n\geq 1
let
\nu_{n}>\nu_{n-1}
be large enough, such that
\Delta_{\nu_{n}}
contains
K_{n-1}
in its interior.
Let
L_{n}=\Delta_{\nu_{n}}\cup\overline{h(E\cup\Delta_{\nu_{n}})}
and note that
L_{n}
is compact, since
E
has the BEH property.
Since the family
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is locally finite and pairwise disjoint,
only finitely many compacts
H_{\lambda}
intersect
L_{n}
. Let the
K_{n}
be the union of
L_{n}
and these compacts.

The sets
K_{n}
are clearly compact and form an exhaustion of
X
.
Property (i) holds, since
h(K_{n}\cup E)=h(L_{n}\cup E)=\emptyset
and property (ii) holds by constriction.
Thus we need only check that a given set
K_{n}
is
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex.
To this end let
U
be a connected component of
X\setminus K_{n}
and note
\partial U\subseteq K_{n}
.
If
U\subseteq E
, then
U\subseteq H_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda

and since
\partial U\subseteq K_{n}
,
H_{\lambda}
must be one of the compacts making up
K_{n}
.
This implies
U\subseteq K_{n}
, which is a contradiction.
Thus
U\subseteq X\setminus E
, which implies
U\subseteq X\setminus(E\cup\Delta_{\nu_{n}})
, so
U
is contained in one of the connected complements of the complement.
Since
\partial U\subseteq\partial K_{n}\subseteq\partial(E\cup\Delta_{\nu_{n}})
,
U
is equal to this connected component and as
U\cap h(E\cup\Delta_{\nu_{n}})=\emptyset
, it is not relatively compact.
This proves that
K_{n}
has no holes and hence it is
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex.
∎

Next we show that approximation a non-critical holomorphic function on a compact well enough yields a function which is also non-critical on that compact
and that a suitable limit of non-critical holomorphic functions is again non-critical.
Although these are standard results in approximation theory, we provide proofs for the sake of completion.

Lemma 3
.

Let
K,H\subseteq X
be sets such that
K
is compact and that
K\subseteq\mathring{H}
.
Let
\left\{(U_{j},\phi_{j})\right\}_{j=1}^{l}
be a finite cover of
K
with charts.
Then there exists some
0<r\leq 1
, such that for every
p\in K
there is some chart
(U_{j},\phi_{j})
such that

B(\phi_{j}(p),r)\subseteq\phi_{j}(H\cap U_{j}).

Proof.

For
j=1,\ldots,l
define
d_{j}\colon K\to[0,\infty)
by

d_{j}(p)=\begin{cases}\dist(\phi_{j}(p),\phi(H\cap U_{j})^{c});\quad p\in U_{j}\\
0;\quad p\notin U_{j}\end{cases}

and note it is a continuous function. If
p\in K\cap U_{j}
, then
\dist(\phi_{j}(p),\phi(H\cap U_{j})^{c})>0
, since
K\subseteq\mathring{H}
.
Now define
d\colon K\to[0,\infty)
by

d(p)=\max_{j=1,\ldots,l}d_{j}(p).

Note that this function is also continuous and strictly positive, since each
p\in K
is contained in some neighborhood
U_{j}
.
Thus the function
d
attains a positive minima
m
on the compact
K
. We claim
r=\min\left\{m,1\right\}
is the desired number.
Indeed, suppose there exists some
p\in K
such that
B(\phi_{j}(p),r)\cap\phi(H\cap U_{j})^{c}\neq\emptyset
for all
j=1,\ldots,l
.
This implies
d_{j}(p)<r
for all
j=1,\ldots,l
and hence
d(p)<r\leq m
as well, but this is a contradiction. This concludes our proof.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
K,H\subseteq X
be compact sets such that
K\subseteq\mathring{H}
and let
f\in\mathcal{O}(U)
be non-critical holomorphic function on some open neighborhood
U
of
H
.
Then there exists some
\delta>0
such that if a holomorphic function
g\in\mathcal{O}(U)
satisfies
\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{H}<\delta
, then
g
is non-critical on
K
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{(U_{j},\phi_{j})\right\}_{j=1}^{l}
be a finite cover of
H
with charts.
Choose an open cover
\left\{V_{j}\right\}_{j=1}^{l}
of
H
such that the set
V_{j}
is compactly contained in
U_{j}
.
Since
f
is non-critical, the function
\left|(f\circ(\phi_{j})^{-1})^{\prime}\right|
is strictly positive on the compact sets
H\cap\overline{V_{j}}
and thus obtains a positive minima
m^{j}
.
Denote
m=\min\left\{m^{j}\mid j=1,\ldots,l\right\}
and note
m>0
.
Let
r>0
be given by lemma
3
for the compacts
K\subseteq H
and charts
\left\{(V_{j},\phi_{j})\right\}_{j=1}^{l}
.
We claim that
\delta=\frac{mr}{4}
is the desired number. Indeed, assume
g\in\mathcal{O}(U)
is a holomorphic function such that
\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{H}<\delta
and pick
p\in K
.
Let
(V_{j},\phi_{j})
be the chart corresponding to
p
by lemma
3
.
Since
B(\phi_{j}(p),r)\subseteq\phi_{j}(H\cap V_{j})
, we have
\partial B\left(\phi_{j}(p),\frac{r}{2}\right)\subseteq\phi_{j}(H\cap V_{j})
.
Thus we can use the fact that
\left|(f-g)\circ\phi_{j}^{-1}\right|<\delta
on
\partial B\left(\phi_{j}(p),\frac{r}{2}\right)
and Cauchy estimates to obtain

\displaystyle\left|((f-g)\circ(\phi_{j})^{-1})^{\prime}(\phi_{j}(p))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial B\left(\phi_{j}(p),\frac{r}{2}\right)}\frac{\left|(f-g)\circ(\phi_{j})^{-1}(\zeta)\right|}{\left|\zeta-\phi_{j}(p)\right|^{2}}\,\left|d\zeta\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial B\left(\phi_{j}(p),\frac{r}{2}\right)}\frac{4\delta}{r^{2}}\,\left|d\zeta\right|=\frac{2\delta}{r}=\frac{m}{2}.

It now follows

\displaystyle\left|(g\circ(\phi_{j})^{-1})^{\prime}(\phi_{j}(p))\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|(f\circ(\phi_{j})^{-1})^{\prime}(\phi_{j}(p))\right|-\left|((f-g)\circ(\phi_{j})^{-1})^{\prime}(\phi_{j}(p))\right|

\displaystyle\geq m-\frac{m}{2}=\frac{m}{2}>0,

which shows that
p
is not a critical point of
g
. It follows that
g
is non-critical on
K
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a compact exhaustion of the Riemann surface
X
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of functions,
such that
f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(K_{n})
are non-critical. Then there exists a sequence of positive numbers
\left\{\delta_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

such that if
\left\lVert f_{n}-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{K_{n-1}}<\delta_{n-1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
the point-wise limit of the functions
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges uniformly on compacts of
X
to a global non-critical holomorphic function
F=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\tilde{\delta}_{n}
be given by Lemma
5
for the compacts
K_{n-1}\subseteq K_{n}
and the holomorphic function
f_{n}
.
Let
\left\{\delta_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of positive numbers, such that
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\tilde{\delta}_{n}
holds for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Fix some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since

\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left\lVert f_{k+1}-f_{k}\right\rVert_{K_{n-1}}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left\lVert f_{k+1}-f_{k}\right\rVert_{K_{k}}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\tilde{\delta}_{n}<\infty,

the series
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}f_{k+1}-f_{k}
converges uniformly on
K_{n-1}
.
For
N>n
we can then write
f_{N}=f_{n}+\sum_{k=n}^{N-1}f_{k+1}-f_{k}
and by taking the limit
N\to\infty
, we get

F=f_{n}+\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}f_{k+1}-f_{k}.

Since
F
is a sum of two holomorphic functions,
F
is holomorphic on
\mathring{K}_{n-1}
.
The fact that
F
is also non-critical on
\mathring{K}_{n-1}
follows directly from

\left\lVert F-f_{n}\right\rVert_{K_{n-1}}\leq\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\left\lVert f_{k+1}-f_{k}\right\rVert_{K_{n-1}}<\tilde{\delta}_{n},

and our choice of
\tilde{\delta}_{n}
in Lemma
5
.
Since
n\in\mathbb{N}
was arbitrary and the compacts
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
exhaust
X
,
this shows that
F
really is a global non-critical holomorphic function.
∎

In the next lemma we construct a biholomorphic map which moves points off of sets with empty interior.
The map will be given as a flow of a holomorphic vector field.

Lemma 6
.

Let
K\subseteq X
be a compact set,
U
an open neighborhood of
K
and
\delta>0
.
Suppose there are points
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\in K
such that for each
j=1,\ldots,n
there exists an open neighborhood
W_{j}
of
p_{j}
such that
W_{j}\cap K\subseteq\partial K
.
Then there exist some open neighborhood
V\subseteq U
of
K
and a biholomorphic map
\gamma\colon V\to\gamma(V)\subseteq X
,
such that
\left\lVert\gamma-\id\right\rVert_{V}<\delta
and
p_{j}\notin\gamma(K)
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

Let
v\colon X\to TX
be any holomorphic vector field, such that
v(p_{j})\neq 0
holds for all
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Then there exists some
\tau>0
and some open neighborhood
\widetilde{V}
of
K
such that the flow map

\Phi\colon\mathbb{D}_{\tau}\times\widetilde{V}\to X
of the vector field
v
is well-defined and holomorphic.
Let
V
be an open neighborhood of
K
that is contained in
U
and compactly contained in
\widetilde{V}
.
Since the map
\Phi\colon\mathbb{D}_{\tau}\times V\to X
is jointly continuous, the map
t\mapsto\left\lVert\Phi(t,\cdot)-\id\right\rVert_{V}
is continuous.
So by choosing
\tau
small enough, we may assume
\left\lVert\Phi(t,\cdot)-\id\right\rVert_{V}<\delta
for any
t\in\mathbb{D}_{\tau}
.
Choosing
\tau
even smaller if necessary, we can also assume
\Phi(t,p_{j})\in W_{j}
for all
t\in\mathbb{D}_{\tau}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
.

For
j=1,\ldots,n
we now define a holomorphic map
\phi_{j}\colon\mathbb{D}_{\tau}\to W_{j}
by

\phi_{j}(t)=\Phi(t,p_{j}).

Since

d_{0}\phi_{j}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(0)\right)=d_{0}\Phi(\cdot,p_{j})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(0)\right)=v(\Phi(0,p_{j}))=v(p_{j})\neq 0,

the map
\phi_{j}
can not be constant. Thus
\phi_{j}
is an open map and since
W_{j}\setminus K
is dense in
W_{j}
, the set
\phi_{j}^{-1}(W_{j}\setminus K)
is open and dense in
\mathbb{D}_{\tau}
.
Since a finite intersection of dense open sets is non-empty, there exists some
t_{0}\in\bigcap_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{j}^{-1}(W_{j}\setminus K)
.
Then
\gamma=\Phi(-t_{0},\cdot)
is the desired map,
since for each
j=1,\ldots,n
we have

\gamma^{-1}(p_{j})=(\Phi(-t_{0},\cdot))^{-1}(p_{j})=\Phi(t_{0},p_{j})=\phi_{j}(t_{0})\notin K.\qed

We are now ready to prove the main theorem.

Proof of main theorem:

Since
E
is semi-admissible, it is of the form
E=H\cup S
where
H
is the union of the family of compact sets
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.
Let
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the compact exhaustion of
X
by
\mathcal{O}(X)
-convex sets given by Lemma
2
for the family
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define the closed sets
E_{n}=E\cup K_{n}
.
Note that the sets
E_{n}
are semi-admissible and by property (i) of the exhaustion
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
they have no holes.
In particular the set
E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}
is Runge for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Note that we also have
E_{n}\setminus K_{n+1}=E\setminus K_{n+1}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Since
\varepsilon
is a positive valued continuous function, it achieves a positive minimum on compact sets.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we can thus define
\varepsilon_{n}=\min\left\{\varepsilon(p)\mid p\in E\cap K_{n+2}\right\}
.
Note that
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq\varepsilon_{n-1}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Furthermore let
\left\{\delta_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be given by Lemma
5
for the compact exhaustion
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we then define

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n}=\min\left\{2^{-n-1}\varepsilon_{n},\delta_{n}\right\}.

We will inductively construct a sequence of functions
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that the following conditions hold:

(1)

f_{n}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{n})
and
f_{n}
in non-critical,

(2)

\left\lVert f_{n}-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}
,

(3)

f_{n}=f_{n-1}
on
E\setminus K_{n+1}
.

For
n=0
take
f_{0}=f
.
Condition (1) holds since
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E)
is non-critical by assumption.
Conditions (2) and (3) in this case are trivially satisfied.

Now suppose we have already constructed
f_{n-1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{n_{1}})
and let
L=E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1}
.
Note that
f_{n-1}
is holomorphic and non-critical on some open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathring{L}}
.
Choose compact sets
H_{1},H_{2}\subseteq X
such that
\overline{\mathring{L}}\subseteq\mathring{H}_{1}\subseteq H_{1}\subseteq\mathring{H}_{2}
,
the set
L\cup H_{2}
is Runge and that
f_{n-1}
is holomorphic on a neighborhood of
H_{2}
.
Let
\delta_{1}
bi given by Lemma
4
applied to
H_{1}\subseteq H_{2}
and
f_{n-1}
.
We use Mergelyan’s approximation theorem on the Runge set
L\cup H_{2}
and the function
f_{n-1}
to obtain a global holomorphic function
h\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that
\left\lVert h-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L\cup H_{2}}<\min\left\{\delta_{1},\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}}{3}\right\}
.
In particular we have

\left\lVert h-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}}{3}

and by our choice of
\delta_{1}
we know
h
is non-critical on an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathring{L}}
.

Next we choose some
\delta_{2}>0
such that the critical points of
h
either lie on
L
or outside
L(\delta_{2})
.
Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
be the critical points of
h
that lie on
L
, there are only finitely many.
Since
h
is non-critical on an open neighborhood of
\overline{\mathring{L}}
, there exists for each
p_{j}
some open neighborhood
W_{j}
, such that
W_{j}\cap L\subseteq\partial L
.
We use Lemma
6
on the compact set
L
, the open neighborhood
(L)(\delta_{2})
and the points
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}

to obtain an open neighborhood
V
of
L
and a biholomorphic map
\gamma\colon V\to\gamma(V)
such that
\left\lVert\gamma-\id\right\rVert_{V}<\delta_{2}
and
p_{j}\notin\gamma(E_{n-1}\cap K_{n+1})
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
.
We define the holomorphic function
\tilde{h}\colon V\to\mathbb{C}
by
\tilde{h}=h\circ\gamma
.
By choosing
\delta_{2}
sufficiently small, the uniform continuity of
h
implies

\left\lVert\tilde{h}-h\right\rVert_{L}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}}{3}.

It can be verified that the function
\tilde{h}
has no critical points on
L
, and by restricting
V
if necessary,
we can assume the function
\tilde{h}
is non-critical on
V
as well.

We now use Theorem
1
on the Runge set
L
and the function
\tilde{h}
to obtain a global non-critical holomorphic function
g\in\mathcal{O}(X)
such that

\left\lVert g-\tilde{h}\right\rVert_{L}<\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}}{3}.

By applying the triangel inequality we then obtain.

\left\lVert g-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L}\leq\left\lVert g-\tilde{h}\right\rVert_{L}+\left\lVert\tilde{h}-h\right\rVert_{L}+\left\lVert h-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}.

Let
U_{1}
be an open neighborhood of
K_{n}
obtained by applying Lemma
1
to the compact
K_{n}
, the open neighborhood
\mathring{K}_{n+1}
and the family
\left\{H_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
.
Let
U_{2}
be an open neighborhood of
K_{n}
which is compactly contained in
U_{1}
.
By the choice of
U_{1}
and property (ii) of the exhaustion
\left\{K_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

any compact set
H_{\lambda}
is contained either in
U_{2}
or in
X\setminus\overline{U_{1}}
.
Let
\chi\colon X\to[0,1]
be a smooth bump function, such that
\supp\chi\subseteq U_{1}
and
\chi=1
on
\overline{U_{2}}
.
We now define the function
f_{n}
as

f_{n}=\chi g+(1-\chi)f_{n-1}.

The function
f_{n}
is clearly continuous on
E_{n}
and on an open neighborhood of
K_{n}
or a compact
H_{\lambda}
it agrees with either
g
or
f_{n-1}
.
This shows that
f_{n}
satisfies property (1).
To verify property (2) we estimate

\left\lVert f_{n}-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L}=\left\lVert\chi(g-f_{n-1})\right\rVert_{L}\leq\left\lVert g-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1},

and to verify property (3), simply notice that
\chi=0
on
E_{n}\setminus K_{n+1}
.

We define the function
F
as the pointwise limit of the sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
Since in particular
f_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(K_{n})
are non-critical and

\left\lVert f_{n}-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{K_{n-1}}\leq\left\lVert f_{n}-f_{n-1}\right\rVert_{L}<\tilde{\varepsilon}_{n-1}<\delta_{n-1},

Lemma
5
guarantees that
F
is a global non-critical holomorphic function.
It remains to verify the approximation property. To this end pick
p\in E
and note that either
p\in K_{1}
or
p\in K_{n+1}\setminus K_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
p\in K_{1}
, we have

\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|f_{k+1}(p)-f_{k}(p)\right|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k-2}\varepsilon_{k}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k-1}=\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon(p).

If
p\in K_{n+1}\setminus K_{n}
, we have by property (3) that
f_{n-1}(p)=\ldots=f_{0}(p)
. We can then estimate

\displaystyle\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|f_{k+1}(p)-f_{k}(p)\right|=\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\left|f_{k+1}(p)-f_{k}(p)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}\leq\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}2^{-k-2}\varepsilon_{k}\leq\varepsilon_{n-1}\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}2^{-k-1}\leq\varepsilon_{n-1}<\varepsilon(p).

Thus
\left|F(p)-f(p)\right|<\varepsilon(p)
for each
p\in E
, which concludes the proof.
∎

6.
Non-critical approximation on general sets of Carleman approximation

Lastly, we discuss the problem of non-critical approximation on general sets of Carleman approximation and present a different approach
that yields uniform approximation on some sets of Carleman approximation that are not semi-admissible.

The main reason why we considered semi-admissible sets for non-critical Carleman approximation is that
they have some room between components with non-empty interior.
Thus, we can interpolate using smooth bump functions and not need to worry about holomorphicity,
since where we interpolate a function of class
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
need only be continuous.

This is not the case for general sets of Carleman approximation. Consider again the set

E_{2}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\overline{B\left(n,\frac{1}{2}\right)}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
,
see figure
2
.
Trying to replicate the proof of the main theorem in this setting,
we would end up interpolating by a smooth bump function on a part of the set
E_{2}
with non-empty interior where a function of the class
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{2})
must be holomorphic.
This requires us to modify our function by a solution of a
\bar{\partial}
-equation, but by doing so, we lose control over the critical points of the function.
One can consider different solutions to
\bar{\partial}
-equations, for example, ones with Hörmander estimates, see
[
10
, Section 4.2]
, but in this case one loses the approximation properties of the function along the set
E_{2}
.
This is the problem one needs to overcome to achieve non-critical approximation on more general sets.

Using a different approach, we can achieve non-critical uniform approximation on certain sets of Carleman approximation in simply connected open Riemann surfaces.
The idea is to approximate the derivative of our initial function, and then integrate the approximating function to obtain our global non-critical approximation.
The same approach can be used to give an alternate proof of Theorem
1
in the case when
X=\mathbb{C}
, see
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a path-connected set of Carleman approximation such that for every compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{C}
there exists some
c>0

such that any two points in the same path-connected component of
E\cap K
can be connected by a smooth path of length at most
c
.
Let
f\in\mathcal{O}(E)
be a non-critical holomorphic function and
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists an entire non-critical function
F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
such that we have
\left|F(z)-f(z)\right|<\varepsilon
for every
z\in E
.

Proof.

Pick a point
p\in E
and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
K_{n}
be the path-connected component of
E\cap\overline{B(p,n)}
which contains
p
.
Let
c_{n}>0
be the constant corresponding to
K_{n}
given by our assumption and let
r\colon E\to(0,\infty)
be a continuous positive valued function, such that
\max_{z\in K_{n}}r(z)>c_{n}
.

Suppose
f
is defined on an open neighborhood
U
of
E
. Since
E
is a set of Carleman approximation, it has no holes and we may assume
U
is simply connected.
The function
f^{\prime}
is a nowhere vanishing holomorphic function on the simply connected set
U
, thus it is of the form
f^{\prime}=e^{g}
for some
g\in\mathcal{O}(U)
.
Let
\delta>0
be such that for
\left|z\right|<\delta
we have
\left|e^{z}-1\right|<C\left|z\right|
for some
C>0
and define the continuous function
\tilde{\varepsilon}\colon E\to(0,\infty)
by

\tilde{\varepsilon}(z)=\min\left\{\delta,\frac{\varepsilon}{Cr(z)\left|e^{g(z)}\right|}\right\}.

We apply the classical Carleman approximation theorem to the set
E
the continuous complex valued function
g\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
and the continuous positive valued function
\tilde{\varepsilon}

to obtain an entire function
G\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
such that
\left|G(z)-g(z)\right|<\tilde{\varepsilon}(z)
for all
z\in E
.

We define the function
F\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
by

F(z)=f(p)+\int_{p}^{z}e^{G(\zeta)}\,d\zeta.

Note that
F
is a well-defined entire function and its value is independent of the choice of path from
0
to
z
along which we integrate.
Let
z\in K_{n}
and let
\gamma_{z}
be a smooth path from
p
to
z
inside
K_{n}
of length less than
c_{n}
. We can compute

\displaystyle\left|F(z)-f(z)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|F(z)-f(p)+f(p)-f(z)\right|=\left|\int_{\gamma_{z}}e^{G(\zeta)}\,d\zeta-\int_{\gamma_{z}}f^{\prime}(\zeta)\,d\zeta\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\gamma_{z}}e^{G(\zeta)}\,d\zeta-\int_{\gamma_{z}}e^{g(\zeta)}\,d\zeta\right|\leq\int_{\gamma_{z}}\left|e^{G(\zeta)}-e^{g(\zeta)}\right|\,\left|d\zeta\right|

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma_{z}}\left|e^{g(\zeta)}\right|\left|e^{G(\zeta)-g(\zeta)}-1\right|\,\left|d\zeta\right|\leq C\int_{\gamma_{z}}\left|e^{g(\zeta)}\right|\left|G(\zeta)-g(\zeta)\right|\,\left|d\zeta\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\gamma_{z}}\left|e^{g(\zeta)}\right|\tilde{\varepsilon}(\zeta)\,\left|d\zeta\right|\leq\varepsilon\int_{\gamma_{z}}\frac{1}{r(\zeta)}\,\left|d\zeta\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{c_{n}}\int_{\gamma_{z}}\,\left|d\zeta\right|\leq\varepsilon.

Finally notice that
F^{\prime}=e^{G}\neq 0
, so
F
is indeed non-critical.
∎

Using Theorem
2
, we can see, for example, that we have non-critical uniform approximation for functions in
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(E_{2})
.
This approach also extends to any simply connected open Riemann surface and set of Carleman approximation which satisfies the property on the upper bounds of lengths of paths.
We also remark that using this approach, we can, in general, not do better than uniform approximation.

We do not know if uniform approximation by non-critical functions is possible for more general sets, for example, sets on which Arakelyan approximation is possible.
The problem is open even for simple sets like a horizontal strip.

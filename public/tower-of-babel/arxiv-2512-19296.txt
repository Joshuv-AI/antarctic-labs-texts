Auslander-Reiten theory via Nakayama duality in abelian categories
Zetao Lin, Shiping Liu

Abstract
. Using the Nakayama duality induced by a Nakayama functor, we provide a novel and concise account of the existence of Auslander-Reiten dualities and almost split sequences in abelian categories with enough projective objects or enough injective objects. As an example, we establish the existence of almost split sequences ending with finitely presented modules and those starting with finitely copresented modules in the category of all modules over a small endo-local Hom-reflexive category. Specializing to algebras given by (not necessarily finite) quivers with relations, we further investigate when the categories of finitely presented modules, finitely copresented modules and finite dimensional modules have almost split sequences on either or both sides.

1.
Preliminaries

The objective of this section is to fix some terminology and notation, which will be used throughout this paper, and collect some preliminary results.

1.1.
Reflexive modules

Throughout this paper,
R
stands for a commutative ring with an identity. We denote by
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}R
the category of
R
-modules and by
I\!_{R}
a minimal injective cogenerator for
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}R
. A significant role in our investigation will be played by the
dual functor

D={\rm Hom}_{R}(-,I\!_{R}):\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}R\to\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}R
will play. Given an
R
-module

there exists a canonical
R
-linear monomorphism
\sigma_{\hskip-1.5pt{}_{M}}:M\to D^{2}\hskip-1.0ptM
, sending

to the evaluating at
m

M
is
reflexive
if
\sigma_{\hskip-1.5pt{}_{M}}
is bijective. The following statement is known; see
[
35
, (1.3)]
.

Lemma 1.1.1.

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Then the category
{\rm mod}R
of reflexive
R
-modules is abelian, and the dual functor restricts to a duality
D\hskip-1.5pt:\hskip-1.5pt{\rm mod}R\hskip-1.5pt\to\hskip-1.5pt{\rm mod}R.

Remark 1.1.2 .

(1) The category
{\rm mod}R
contains all
R
-modules of finite length.

(2) In case

is complete noetherian local,
{\rm mod}R
contains all noetherian
R
-modules and all artinian
R
-modules.

1.2.
Krull-Schmidt categories

An
R
-category is a category in which the morphism sets are
R
-modules and the composition of morphisms is
R
-bilinear. We shall compose morphisms in any
R
-category from right to left. A full subcategory of an
R
-category is called
strictly full
if it is closed under isomorphisms. All functors between
R
-categories are assumed to be
R
-linear.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an
R
-category. Given objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}
, we write
\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
for the
R
-module of morphisms
f:X\to Y.
A non-zero object
X
in
\mathcal{A}
is called
strongly indecomposable
if
\mathcal{A}(X,X)
is local; and
Krull-Schmidt
if it is a finite direct sum of strongly indecomposable objects. We shall say that
\mathcal{A}
is
Krull-Schmidt
if every non-zero object is Krull-Schmidt, and
Hom-reflexive
(respectively,
Hom-noetherian, Hom-finite
) if the
R
-modules
\mathcal{A}(X,Y)
are reflexive (respectively, noetherian, of finite length), for all objects
X,Y
in
\mathcal{A}
. Note that a Hom-finite
R
-category is Hom-reflexive and Hom-noetherian. Moreover, if
R
is complete noetherian local, then a Hom-noetherian
R
-category is Hom-reflexive.

Proposition 1.2.1.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a strictly full additive subcategory of an abelian
R
-category
\mathfrak{A}
. If
\mathcal{A}
is Krull-Schmidt, then it is closed under direct summands in
\mathfrak{A}
. The converse holds if
\mathcal{A}
is either Hom-finite or Hom-noetherian with
R
being complete local noetherian.

Proof.
Firstly, assume that
\mathcal{A}
is Krull-Schmidt. Let
X\in\mathcal{A}
with a nonzero direct summand
Y
in
\mathfrak{A}
. Then,
X=X_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus X_{n}
, where
X_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}(X_{i},X_{i})
being local. Since
\mathfrak{A}(X_{i},X_{i})=\mathcal{A}(X_{i},X_{i})
, we see that
Y
is a direct sum of some of the
X_{i}
; see
[
36
, (1.1)]
. In particular,
Y\in\mathcal{A}
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under direct summands in
\mathfrak{A}
. Then, the idempotent endomorphisms in

\mathcal{A}
split. Assume further that
\mathcal{A}
is either Hom-finite or Hom-noetherian with
R
being complete noetherian local. Then, for any
X\in\mathcal{A}
, the
R
-algebra
\mathcal{A}(X,X)
is either artianian; or noetherian with
R
being complete noetherian local. In either case,
\mathcal{A}(X,X)
is semi-perfect; see
[
31
, (21.35)]
. Thus,
\mathcal{A}
is Krull-Schmidt; see
[
36
, (1.1)]
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

Minimal morphisms

Stable categories

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an exact

[

2.
Auslander-Reiten Theory in abelian categories

The main objective of this section is to provide a novel and concise account of the Auslander-Reiten theory in general abelian categories. First, we derive an Auslander-Reiten duality from the Nakayama duality induced by a Nakayama functor. In case the Nakayama functor is defined on a “small” subcategory of projective objects, we show that the AR-translation is uniquely defined (up to isomorphism) on finitely presented objects and preserves the strong indecomposability. This enables us to establish the existence of almost split sequences ending with finitely presented objects and those starting with finitely copresented objects.

2.1.
Projective presentations and injective copresentations

\mathfrak{A}
stands for an abelian
R
-category. Let

and

\textstyle{0}

2.2.
Nakayama functor

The key ingredient in our approach to the Auslander-Reiten duality is a Nakayama functor defined as follows; see
[
35
, (5.4)]
.

Definition 2.2.1 .

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an abelian
R
-category, and let
\mathcal{P}
be a strictly full additive subcategory of projective objects in
\mathfrak{A}
.

A functor
\nu:\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{A}
is called a
Nakayama functor

provided, for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
X\in\mathfrak{A},
that there exists an
R
-linear isomorphism
\phi_{P,X}:\mathfrak{A}(X,\nu P)\to D\mathfrak{A}(P,X),
which is natural in
P
and
X
.

Remark 2.2.2 .

Since the dual functor
D
is exact, the image
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
of a Nakayama functor
\nu:\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{A}
is a strictly full additive subcategory of injective objects in
\mathfrak{A}
.

2.4.
Auslander-Reiten translations

Let
\nu:\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{A}
be a Nakayama functor. Given a strongly indecomposable object

over
\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}

Proposition 2.4.1.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an abelian
R
-category equipped with a Nakayama functor
\nu:\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{A}
, where
\mathcal{P}

\nu
restricts to an equivalence
\nu:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{I}_{\nu}.
In particular,
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}

Remark 2.4.2 .

We call the equivalence

2.4.1
the
Nakayama equivalence
, and we fix a quasi-inverse
\nu^{-}\!:\mathcal{I}_{\nu}\to\mathcal{P}

By Propositions

\mathfrak{A}^{+}(\mathcal{P})

\mathfrak{A}^{-}(\mathcal{I}_{\nu})

\mathfrak{A}
. Moreover, every object in
\mathfrak{A}^{+}(\mathcal{P})

\mathcal{P},
and every object in
\mathfrak{A}^{-}(\mathcal{I}_{\nu})
admits a minimal injective copresentation over
\mathcal{I}_{\nu}
. This allows us to define, unique up to isomorphism, the
AR-translations

\tau

as follows.

Definition 2.4.3 .

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an abelian
R
-category equipped with a Nakayama functor
\nu:\mathcal{P}\to\mathfrak{A}

\mathcal{P}

(1)

Given

\textstyle{P_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d_{1}}
\textstyle{P_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(2)

Given

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{I_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d^{1}}
\textstyle{I_{1}}

In order to show that
\tau

Lemma 2.4.4.

Let
\mathfrak{A}

R
-category, and let
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\mathfrak{A}

(1)

If

is local and

is left minimal.

(2)

If

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}(Z,Z)
is local and

is a nonsplit epimorphism.

Then, there exists a nonsplit short exact sequence

\textstyle{\hskip-2.0pt0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{0\hskip-2.0pt}
in

. By Lemma 4.3 in

,

is left minimal. And since

and hence,

The classical approach for showing that the Auslander-Reiten translation preserves indecomposability goes through the morphism category of projective modules and the stable categories; see
[
11

Proposition 2.4.5.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an abelian
R
-category equipped with a Nakayama functor

in

(1)

If

.

(2)

If

is not injective, then
\tau^{-}M\in\mathfrak{A}^{+}(\mathcal{P})

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1).

Assume that

\textstyle{P_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{P_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d_{0}}

\textstyle{0}
over
\mathcal{P}
.

By definition,
\tau M
admits an injective copresentation

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\nu P_{0}}

is also left minimal. And by Proposition

Suppose that

\scriptstyle{\nu d_{1}}
\textstyle{\nu P_{0}.}

Again since

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an abelian

-category equipped with a Nakayama functor

,

2.5.
The existence theorem

We are ready to apply the result in
[

, (2.3)]

to derive almost split sequences from the Auslander-Reiten dualities in Theorem

.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be an abelian

3.
Applications to functor categories

An
R
-category is called
small
if its objects form a set and
endo-local
if all endomorphism algebras are local. The objective of this section is to apply our previous results to establish the existence of almost split sequences for finitely presented modules and for finitely copresented modules in the category of all modules over a small endo-local Hom-reflexive
R
-category. The key ingredients for this objective include a Nakayama functor and the sufficiency of projective and injective objects. The latter fact was stated in
[
42
]
, but its proof seems to have never been published.

3.1.
Modules over a category

Throughout this section,
\mathscr{C}
stands for a small
R
-category. A

\mathscr{C}
-module
is a covariant functor
M:\mathscr{C}\to{\rm Mod}R.
Given left
\mathscr{C}
-modules

a
\mathscr{C}
-linear morphism

consists of a family of
R
-linear maps

with
x\in\mathscr{C}
such that
N(u)\circ f_{x}=f_{y}\circ M(u),
for all morphisms
u:x\to y
in
\mathscr{C}.

Write
{\rm Hom}_{\mathscr{C}}(M,N)
for the
R
-module of
\mathscr{C}
-linear morphisms

. The category
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\mathscr{C}
of all left
\mathscr{C}
-modules is an abelian
R
-category with arbitrary products and coproducts; see
[
5
, Section 2]
. A module

is called

-reflexive
if

is an
R
-reflexive for all

Since the category
{\rm mod}R
of reflexive
R
-modules is abelian, the full subcategory
{\rm mod}\mathscr{C}
of locally

-reflexive modules in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\mathscr{C}
is abelian.

\mathscr{C}

3.5.
Finitely presented and finitely copresented modules

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a small
R
-category. Fix a module
M
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\mathscr{C}
. We shall say that
M
is
finitely generated
if there exists an epimorphism
f:P\to M
with
P\in{\rm proj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C};
and

if it admits a projective presentation over
{\rm proj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C},
or equivalently, there exists an epimorphism
f:\hskip-1.5ptP\hskip-1.5pt\to\hskip-1.5ptM
with
P
in
{\rm proj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C}
and
{\rm Ker}(f)
finitely generated. Dually,
M
is called
finitely cogenerated
if there exists a monomorphism
g:M\to I
with
I\in{\rm inj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C};
and
finitely copresented
if it admits an injective copresentation over
{\rm inj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C},

or equivalently, there exists a monomorphism
g:M\to I
with
I
in
{\rm inj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C}
and
{\rm Coker}(g)
finitely cogenerated. We denote by
{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
and
{\rm mod}^{-}\mathscr{C}
the full subcategories of finitely presented modules and finitely copresented modules in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\mathscr{C},
respectively. By Propositions
2.1.1
and
2.1.2
,
{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
and
{\rm mod}^{-}\mathscr{C}
are exact
R
-categories. It is interesting to know when they are abelian categories.

Proposition 3.5.1.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a small
R
-category. Then

(1)

{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
is abelian if and only if the finitely generated submodules of the modules in
{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}
are finitely presented
;

(2)

{\rm mod}^{-}\mathscr{C}
is abelian if and only if the finitely cogenerated quotient-modules of the modules in
{\rm inj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}
are finitely copresented.

Proof.
We shall only prove Statement (1). Assume that
{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
is abelian. Let
M
be a finitely generated submodule of a module
P_{0}
in
{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}.
Then, we have an epimorphism
f:P_{1}\to M
with
P_{1}\in{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}
, and hence, a morphism
jf:P_{1}\to P_{0}
in
{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
, where
j:M\to P_{0}
is the inclusion map. Note that
f
and
jf
have the same kernel
K
, which lies in
{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}.
Thus,
M
is finitely presented.

Assume that the finitely generated submodules of modules in
{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}
are finitely presented. Consider a morphism
f:P_{1}\to P_{0}
in
{\rm proj}\hskip 0.5pt\mathscr{C}
and its kernel
j:K\to P_{1}
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\mathscr{C}.
By the assumption, there exists an epimorphism
f^{\prime}:P_{1}^{\prime}\to{\rm Im}(f)
having a kernel
q:K^{\prime}\to P_{1}^{\prime}
, where
P_{1}^{\prime}\in{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}
and
K^{\prime}
is finitely generated. By Schanule’s Lemma,
K^{\prime}\oplus P_{1}\cong K\oplus P_{1}^{\prime}.

Thus,
K
is finitely generated with an epimorphism
g:P_{2}\to K,
where
P_{2}\in{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}
. So,
\textstyle{\hskip-4.0ptP_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{jg}
\textstyle{P_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{P_{0}}

{\rm proj}\hskip 0.8pt\mathscr{C}

{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
is closed under kernels; see
[
3

Being also closed under cokernels; see (

{\rm mod}^{+}\mathscr{C}
is abelian.
The proof of the proposition is completed.

4.
Application to representations of quivers with relations

The objective of this section is mainly to apply our previous results to study the existence of almost split sequences over algebras defined by quivers with relations. In the local semiperfect case; see (
4.4.1
), a general existence theorem in the category of all modules is a special case for small endo-local Hom-reflexive categories; see (
4.7.1
). So, we shall focus on the subcategories of finitely presented modules, finitely copresented modules and finite dimensional modules. For locally semiprimary algebras given by locally finite quivers; see (
4.5.1
), we show that the almost split sequences in the first two subcategories are almost split in the category of all modules; see (
4.7.4
) and (
4.7.6
). More importantly, we shall study when these subcategories have almost split sequences; see (
4.8.1
), (
4.8.3
) and (
4.8.5
).

Throughout this section,
k
denotes a field. Note that the dual functor for
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}k
is
D={\rm Hom}_{k}(-,k),
and a module in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}k
is reflexive if and only if it is finite dimensional. In particular, a
k
-category is Hom-reflexive if and only if it is Hom-finite. In this section, a
k
-algebra has enough idempotents but not necessarily an identity, in which an ideal is always two-sided.

4.1.
Quivers

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a quiver, where
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
is the set of arrows. Given
\alpha:x\to y
in
Q_{1}
, we call
x
the
starting point
and
y
the
ending point
, and write
s(\alpha)=x
and
e(\alpha)=y
. For each
x\in Q_{0}
, one associates a
trivial path

\varepsilon_{x}
with
s(\varepsilon_{x})=e(\varepsilon_{x})=x
. A
path
of length
n\hskip 0.5pt(>0)
is a sequence
\rho=\alpha_{n}\cdots\alpha_{1}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in Q_{1}
such that
s(\alpha_{i+1})=e(\alpha_{i})
, for
1\leq i<n
. Two paths are called
comparable
if one is the subpath of the other, and a set of paths is called
comparable
if every two paths in the set are comparable. For
x\in Q_{0}
and
n\geq 0
, we denote by
Q_{n}(x,-)
and
Q_{n}(-,x)
the sets of paths of length
n
starting with
x
, and those ending with
x
, respectively. We say that
Q
is
left locally finite
if
Q_{1}(x,-)
is finite for all
x\in Q_{0};

right locally finite
if
Q_{1}(-,x)
is finite for all
x\in Q_{0},
and
locally finite
if
Q
is left and right locally finite. Further,
Q
is called
interval-finite
if the set of paths from
x
to
y
is finite, for all
x,y\in Q_{0}
; and
strongly locally finite
if
Q
is locally finite and interval-finite.

The
opposite
quiver
Q^{\rm o}
of
Q
is defined in such a way that
(Q^{\rm o})_{0}=Q_{0}
and
(Q^{\rm o})_{1}=\{\alpha^{\rm o}:y\to x\mid\alpha:x\to y\in Q_{1}\}
. A non-trivial path
\rho=\alpha_{n}\cdots\alpha_{1}
in
Q
, where
\alpha_{i}\in Q_{1}
, induces a non-trivial path
\rho^{\rm o}=\alpha_{1}^{\rm o}\cdots\alpha_{n}^{\rm o}
in
Q^{\rm o}
. However, we shall identify the trivial path in
Q^{\rm o}
at a vertex
x
with that in
Q
.

4.2.
Algebras given by quivers with relations

Let
Q
be a quiver. Write
kQ
for the path algebra of
Q
over
k
. An ideal
\mathfrak{I}
in
kQ
is a
relation ideal
if
\mathfrak{I}\subseteq(kQ^{+})^{2}
, where
kQ^{+}
is the ideal in
kQ
generated by the arrows in
Q
. In this case,
(Q,\mathfrak{I})
is called a
bound quiver
, and a path
\rho
in
Q
is called
nonzero in
\it\Lambda
, or simply
nonzero
, if
\rho\not\in\mathfrak{I}.
Given
\rho=\sum\lambda_{i}\rho_{i}\in kQ
, where
\lambda_{i}\in k
and
\rho_{i}
is a path in
Q
, we write
\rho^{\circ}=\sum\lambda_{i}\rho_{i}^{\circ}\in kQ^{\circ}
. In this way, an ideal
\mathfrak{I}
in
kQ
is a relation ideal if and only if
\mathfrak{I}^{\circ}=\{\rho^{\circ}\mid\rho\in\mathfrak{I}\}
is a relation ideal in
kQ^{\circ}.

Consider now a
k
-algebra
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I},
where
Q
is a quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is a relation ideal in
kQ
. Write
\bar{\hskip-1.0pt\gamma}=\gamma+\mathfrak{I}\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
for
\gamma\in kQ
. Then
\it\Lambda
has a complete orthogonal set of idempotents

where
e_{x}=\bar{\varepsilon}_{x}
. Set
J_{\mathit{\hskip-1.0pt\Lambda}}=\{\bar{\rho}\mid\rho\in kQ^{+}\}:=J.
Note that
kQ^{\circ}/\mathfrak{I}^{\circ}
is the opposite algebra of
\it\Lambda
. Write
\bar{\rho}^{\circ}=\rho^{\circ}+\mathfrak{I}^{\circ}
for
\rho\in kQ
but
e_{x}=\varepsilon_{x}+\mathfrak{I}^{\circ}
for
x\in Q_{0}
. In particular,
J_{\hskip-1.0pt\mathit{\Lambda}^{\circ}}=\{\bar{\rho}^{\circ}\mid\rho\in kQ^{+}\}=J^{\circ}
.

A left
\it\Lambda
-module
M
is called
unitary
if
M=\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}e_{x}M
. We denote by
Mod
\it\Lambda
the category of all unitary left
\it\Lambda
-modules. Observe that the
k
-algebra
\it\Lambda
can also be regarded as a small
k
-category, whose objects are the vertices of
Q
and whose morphism spaces are
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}(x,y)=e_{y}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
with
x,y\in Q_{0}
. In this viewpoint, a
k
-linear representation of
(Q,\mathfrak{I})
is a covariant functor from
\it\Lambda
to
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}k.
It is well known that
Mod
\it\Lambda
is equivalent to the category
{\rm Rep}(Q,\mathfrak{I})
of all
k
-linear representations of
(Q,\mathfrak{I}),
in such a way that a module
M
corresponds to a representation
\tilde{M}
defined by

\tilde{M}(x)=e_{x}M
and
\tilde{M}(u):\tilde{M}(x)\to\tilde{M}(y)

being the left multiplication by
u
, where
x,y\in Q_{0}
and
u\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}(x,y)
.
Identifying
M
with
\tilde{M}
, we shall freely use the terminology, the notation and the results stated in Section 4 for
\it\Lambda
-modules. In particular, for each
x\in Q_{0}
, we have a projective module
P_{x}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}(x,-);
see (
3.2.2
) and a simple module
S_{x}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}/Je_{x}
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}.
And we have a projective module
P_{x}^{\circ}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\circ}e_{x}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}(-,x)
and a simple module
S_{x}^{\circ}=\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\circ}e_{x}/J^{\circ}e_{x}
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}^{\circ}.

For a module
M
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}^{\circ}
, we have a module

\mathfrak{D}M=\oplus_{x\in Q_{0}}\,D(e_{x}M)
in

\it\Lambda
such, for all

and
u\in e_{y}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
, that
u\hskip 0.5pt\varphi\in D(e_{y}M)
, which is defined by
(u\varphi)(m)=\varphi(u^{\rm o}m),
for
m\in e_{y}M.
For a morphism
f:M\to N
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}^{\circ},
we have a morphism
\mathfrak{D}f:\mathfrak{D}N\to\mathfrak{D}M
in
Mod
\it\Lambda
such, for
\varphi\in D(e_{x}N)
with
x\in Q_{0}
, that
(\mathfrak{D}f)(\varphi)=\varphi\circ f_{x}
, where
f_{x}:e_{x}M\to e_{x}N
is the restriction of

. This yields a contravariant functor
\mathfrak{D}:\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}^{\rm o}\to\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}.
For each
x\in Q_{0},
we see that
\hskip-0.5pt{D}(S_{x}^{\circ})\cong S_{x}
and

, an injective module in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}};
see (
3.3.2
).

A module
M
in
Mod
\it\Lambda
is

called

if

for all

We denote by
{\rm mod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
{\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

\it\Lambda

, we obtain the following statement.

Proposition 4.2.1.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I}
, where

4.3.
Locally noetherian algebras

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I}
, where
Q
is a quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is an ideal in
kQ
. Recall that
\it\Lambda
is
locally left noetherian
if
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
is noetherian for all
x\in Q_{0}
,
locally right noetherian
if
e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is noetherian for all
x\in Q_{0}
, and
locally noetherian
if
\it\Lambda
is locally left and right noetherian. Moreover,
\it\Lambda
is
locally left bounded
if
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
is finite dimensional for all
x\in Q_{0},

locally right bounded
if
e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is finite dimensional for all
x\in Q_{0},
and
locally bounded
if
\it\Lambda
is locally left and right bounded. Locally (left, right) bounded algebras are clearly locally (left, right) noetherian, and so are
the special multiserial algebras; see
[
34
, (1.3)]
. To provide more classes of locally noetherian algebras, we introduce the following notion.

Definition 4.3.1 .

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I},
where
Q
is a quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is a relation ideal in
kQ
. We shall say that
\it\Lambda
is

(1)

locally left eventually multiserial
if
Q
is left locally finite, and for each
x\in Q_{0}
, there exists an integer
n_{x}(\geq 0)
such, for any
\rho\in Q_{n_{x}}(x,-)
, that the set of nonzero paths in
Q
starting with
\rho
is comparable
\,;

(2)

locally right eventually multiserial
if
Q
is right locally finite, and for any
x\in Q_{0}
, there exists an integer
n_{x}(\geq 0)
such, for any
\rho\in Q_{n_{x}}(-,x)
, that the set of nonzero paths in
Q
ending with
\rho
is comparable
\,;

(3)

locally eventually multiserial
if
\it\Lambda
is locally left and right eventually multiserial.

Note that special multiserial algebras are locally left and right eventually multiserial, while locally bounded algebras are not necessarily so.

Proposition 4.3.2.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I},
where
Q
is a quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is a relation ideal in
kQ
. If
\it\Lambda
is locally left or right eventually multiserial, then it is locally left or right noetherian, respectively.

Proof.
We shall consider only the case where
\it\Lambda
is locally left eventually multiserial. Let
x
be an vertex in
Q
with an integer
n_{x}
as stated in Definition
4.3.1
(1). Fix
\rho\in Q_{n_{x}}(x,-)
. Then

\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}=\sum_{\hskip 0.5pt\eta\in\mathit{\Omega}(\rho)}k\bar{\eta},
where
\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}(\rho)
denotes the set of nonzero paths in
Q
starting with
\rho
. If
\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}(\rho)
is finite, then
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
is noetherian. Otherwise, since
\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}(\rho)
is comparable, there exist arrows
\alpha_{i}:x_{i}\to x_{i+1}
in
Q
with
i\geq 1
and
x_{1}=x,
such that

\hbox{${\mathit{\Omega}}$}(\rho)=\{\rho,\alpha_{1}\rho,\ldots,\alpha_{i}\cdots\alpha_{1}\rho,\ldots\}.
Set

u_{0}=\bar{\rho}
and
u_{i}=\bar{\alpha}_{i}\cdots\bar{\alpha}_{1}\bar{\rho}
for
i\geq 1
. Given
0\neq u\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
, there exists a minimal
d(u)\geq 0
such that
u=\sum_{i=0}^{d(u)}\lambda_{i}u_{i}
, where
\lambda_{i}\in k
with
\lambda_{d(u)}\neq 0
. If
u,v\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
are nonzero, then
v=qu+w
, where
q\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
w\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
with
w=0
or
d(w)<d(u).
Thus, every nonzero submodule of
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
is generated by some
u\in\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
. Thus,
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}
is noetherian. Since
Q
is left locally finite,
J^{n_{x}}e_{x}=\sum_{\rho\in Q_{n_{x}}(x,-)}\hskip-1.0pt\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}\bar{\rho}

with
Q_{n_{x}}(x,-)
finite is noetherian, and
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}/J^{n_{x}}e_{x}
is finite dimensional. So,
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
is noetherian. The proof of the proposition is completed.

\textstyle{a_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{a_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{3}\hskip-4.0pt}

and

Then

The following result collects some basic properties of locally semiperfect algebras.

Proposition 4.4.4.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I}
be locally semiperfect, where
Q
is a quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is a relation ideal in
kQ
.

(1)

If
x\in Q_{0}
, then
Je_{x}
is the unique maximal submodule of
P_{x}
.

(2)

If
x\in Q_{0}
, then
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}\hskip 0.5ptI_{x}
is isomorphic to
S_{x}
and essential in
I_{x}.

(3)

The non-isomorphic simple modules in
Mod
\it\Lambda
are the

S_{x}
with
x\in Q_{0}
.

(4)

If
M\in\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$},
then
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M=\{m\in M\mid Jm=0\}.

Proof.
(1) Let
x\in Q_{0}
. Clearly,
Je_{x}
is a maximal submodule of
P_{x}
. For the uniqueness, it suffices to show that
e_{x}-\bar{\rho}
is invertible for
\rho\in\varepsilon_{x}(kQ^{+})\varepsilon_{x}
. Assume that this is not the case. Since
e_{x}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}
is local,
\bar{\rho}
is invertible for some
\rho\in\varepsilon_{x}(kQ^{+})\varepsilon_{x}
. Then,
e_{x}=\bar{\rho}\bar{\eta}
for some
\eta\in\varepsilon_{x}(kQ)\varepsilon_{x}
. So
e_{x}-\rho\eta\in\mathfrak{I},
contrary to
\mathfrak{I}\subseteq(kQ^{+})^{2}.

(2) Let
x\in Q_{0}
. Since
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\circ}
is locally semiprimary, by Statement (1), the canonical projection
p:\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\circ}e_{x}\to S_{x}^{\circ}
is a projective cover. So,
p
is superfluous; see
[
28
, (3.4)]
. Since
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\circ}e_{x}\in{\rm mod}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}^{\circ}
, by Proposition
4.2.1
,
\mathfrak{D}p:S_{x}\to I_{x}
is an essential monomorphism. Thus,
{\rm Im}(\mathfrak{D}p)
is an essential submodule of
I_{x}
. Since
{\rm Im}(\mathfrak{D}p)\cong S_{x}
, we see that
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{x}={\rm Im}(\mathfrak{D}p)
.

(3) Let
S
be a simple module in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}}.
Then, there exists
0\neq m\in e_{x}S
for some
x\in Q_{0}
. If
(Je_{x})m=S
, then
m=\bar{\rho}m
for some
\bar{\rho}\in e_{x}Je_{x}.
Since
e_{x}-\bar{\rho}
is invertibe, we have
m=0
, absurd. Thus,
(Je_{x})m=0,
and hence,
S\cong\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}e_{x}/Je_{x}.

(4) Given
m\in{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
, by Statement (3),
Jm=0
. Suppose that
Jm=0
for some
0\neq m\in M.
Then,
m=e_{x_{1}}m+\cdots+e_{x_{r}}m,
where
e_{x_{i}}m\neq 0
and
x_{i}\in Q
are pairwise distinct. Since
Je_{x_{i}}(e_{x_{i}}m)=(Je_{x_{i}})m\subseteq Jm=0
, we deduce that
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}(e_{x_{i}}m_{i})\cong S_{x_{i}}
, for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Thus,
m\in{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
. The proof of the proposition is completed.

Remark 4.4.5 .

By Proposition
4.4.4
(1), the condition (1) in Definition
4.4.1
can be replaced by the condition that every oriented cycle in
Q
is nilpotent in
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.

Lemma 4.4.6.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I}
be locally semiperfect, where
Q
is a quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is a relation ideal in
kQ
. Then a module
M
in
Mod
\it\Lambda
is finitely cogenerated if and only if
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is finite dimensional and essential in
M\hskip 0.4pt.

Proof.
Assume that
M
is finitely cogenerated with an injective envelope
g:M\to I,
where
I\in{\rm inj}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. By Proposition
4.4.4
(2),
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I\in{\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is essential in
I
. Since
g({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M)\subseteq{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I
, we have
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M\in{\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
g({\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M)={\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I.
Thus,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M
is essential in
M.
Conversely, assume that
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M\in{\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is essential in
M.
Then,
{\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}M=km_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus km_{s}
, where

with
x_{i}\in Q_{0}
. By Proposition
4.4.4
(4),
km_{i}\cong S_{x_{i}}
, and in view of Proposition
4.4.4
(2), we have a monomorphism

with
{\rm Im}(h_{i})={\rm soc\hskip 0.4pt}I_{x_{i}}
, for
i=1,\ldots,s.
This yields a monomorphism

with

Thus,

extends to a monomorphism

. The proof of the lemma is completed.

4.5.
Locally semiprimary algebras

For our later study on almost split sequences in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
and
{\rm mod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
, we need to impose some stronger conditions on
\it\Lambda
. Recall that a ring

with an identity is called

if

is nilpotent and
\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}/{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}\hbox{${\mathit{\Sigma}}$}

Definition 4.5.1 .

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/\mathfrak{I}

is a relation ideal in

Remark 4.5.2 .

Clearly, locally semiprimary algebras are locally semiperfect.

(1) If

\scriptstyle{\alpha_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}

\scriptstyle{\alpha_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\hskip-4.0pt\beta_{1}}
\textstyle{a_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{2}\hskip-1.0pt}
\textstyle{a_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{3}\hskip-4.0pt}

4.6.

be a locally semiprimary algebra, where
Q
is a locally finite quiver and
\mathfrak{I}
is a relation ideal in
kQ
.

(1)

If
\it\Lambda
is locally left bounded, then
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has almost split sequences on the left. And the converse holds in case
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is abelian.

(2)

If the indecomposable noninjective modules in
{\rm mod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are finite dimensional
(
in particular, if
\it\Lambda
is locally right bounded
\hskip 0.8pt)
, then
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has almost split sequences on the right. And the converse holds in case
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is abelian.

Proof.
(1) Suppose that
\it\Lambda
is locally left bounded. Then,
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}={\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.4ptb\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
by Lemma
4.6.3
. If
M\in{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is indecomposable and not ext-injective, then it is not injective, and by Theorem
4.7.4
(2),
M
is the starting term of an almost split sequence in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Thus,
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has almost split sequences on the left.

Suppose now that
\it\Lambda
is not locally left bounded and
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is abelian. Then
P_{x}
is infinite dimensional for some
x\in Q_{0}
. Since
Q
is locally finite,
by Proposition
3.5.1
(1),
{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}P_{x}\in{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Let
N
be an infinite dimensional indecomposable direct summand of
{\rm rad\hskip 0.4pt}P_{x}
.

Then, we obtain a nonsplit short exact sequence

\textstyle{\hskip-3.0pt0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{x}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{P_{x}/N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. In particular,
N
is not ext-injective in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. By Theorem
4.7.4
(2),
N
is not the starting term of any almost split sequence in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Thus,
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
does not have almost split sequences on the left.

(2) Suppose that all indecomposable noninjective modules in
{\rm mod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
are finite dimensional. If
M\in{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is indecomposable and not ext-projective, then
M
is not projective, and by Theorem
4.7.1
(1),

there exists an almost split sequence
\textstyle{\hskip-3.0pt0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\hskip-1.0pt}
in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$},
where
\tau M\in{\rm mod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}.
Since
\tau M\in{\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.8pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
by the assumption, this is an almost split sequence in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Therefore,
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has almost split sequences on the right.

Assume now that
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
is abelian and
{\rm mod}^{-\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
contains an infinite dimensional indecomposable noninjective module
M
.

By Theorem
4.7.1
(2), there exists an almost split sequence
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{L\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0\hskip-2.0pt}

in
\hbox{{\rm Mod}{\hskip 0.3pt}}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$},
where
L\in{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
. Since
\tau L\cong M,
by Theorem
4.7.4
(1),
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
has no almost split sequence ending with
L
. Since
L
is not ext-projective in
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
by Lemma
4.6.4
(1),
{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}
does not have almost split sequences on the right. The proof of the theorem is completed.

Theorem 4.8.5.

Let
\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}=kQ/R

(2)

{\rm mod}^{\hskip 0.5ptb\hskip-2.5pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

4.6.2
,

M\in{\rm mod}^{+\hskip-3.0pt}\hbox{$\it\Lambda$}

,

\it\Lambda
has an almost split sequence
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\tau M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

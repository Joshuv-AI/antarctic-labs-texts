Arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded linear operators
Vasudevarao Allu, Subhadip Pal

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded linear operators between arbitrary complex Banach spaces. We establish the close connection between the classical Bohr radius and the arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded linear operators. Further, we study the asymptotic estimates of arithmetic Bohr radius for identity operator on infinite dimensional complex Banach spaces. Finally, we obtain the correct asymptotic behavior of Bohr radii of operators between sequence spaces.

Introduction

In recent years, studying different multidimensional variants of Bohr’s classical power series theorem have been an active field of research and it has been studied extensively in diverse articles, for example
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
10
,
11
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
25
,
26
,
33
]
. Following Boas and Khavinson
[
10
]
, the multidimensional Bohr radius
K_{n}
of
n
-dimensional polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|z_{j}|<1\}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the supremum of all positive numbers
r
such that

\sup_{z\in r\mathbb{D}^{n}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}|c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}|\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}}\bigg|\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\bigg|

for all holomorphic functions
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}c_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In 1914, H. Bohr
[
12
]
himself studied and estimated the constant
K_{1}
, the classical Bohr radius and it was shown that
K_{1}
to be
1/6
.
Later, the Bohr radius
K_{1}
was further improved to
1/3
by M. Riesz, I. Schur, and N. Wiener
[
34
,
35
]
, independently. It is worth mentioning here that for
n\geq 2
, the exact values of
K_{n}
are unknown and till today it is an open problem since 1997. However, Boas and Khavinson
[
10
]
have established the following estimate for
K_{n}
:

\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}\leq K_{n}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.

The significant work by Boas and Khavinson
[
10
]
became the source of inspiration for many subsequent papers on multidimensional Bohr radii connecting the asymptotic estimates of Bohr radius to different problems in Functional Analysis like geometry of Banach spaces
[
9
]
, unconditional basis constants of spaces of polynomials
[
3
]
, operator algebra
[
24
,
33
]
etc.

For the last decade, an extensive study has been carried out on multidimensional Bohr radius by replacing the scalar-valued holomorphic functions by Banach space valued ones. Blasco
[
8
]
has first initiated the concept of Bohr radius
K(\mathbb{D},X)
for vector-valued holomorphic functions defined on unit disk
\mathbb{D}
in complex plane into a complex Banach space
X
. Later, the author
[
9
]
has extended the study to
p
-Bohr radius
K^{p}(\mathbb{D},X)
for
1\leq p<\infty
. In 2012, Defant
et al.

[
22
]
introduced a more general version of multidimensional Bohr radius of bounded linear operators between arbitrary complex Banach spaces. In
[
6
]
, motivated by the work of Defant
et al.

[
22
]
and Blasco
[
9
]
, we have introduced the following notion of Bohr radius of bounded linear operators.

Definition 1.1
.

[
6
]

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\lambda\geq 1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded linear operator between complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
such that
\left\lVert T\right\rVert\leq\lambda^{1/p}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\lambda_{p}
-Bohr radius of
T
, denoted by
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
, is the supremum of all
r\geq 0
such that for all holomorphic functions
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, we have

(1.1)

\sup_{z\in rB_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{Y}\leq\lambda\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{X}.

We write
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T)
for
\lambda=1
. If
T
is the identity map on
X
then we use the notation
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
and
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. For
X=\mathbb{C}
, we write
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\lambda)
for
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},\lambda)
and
K(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})
whenever
p=1
and
\lambda=1
.

In the classical sense, Bombieri and Bourgain
[
13
,
14
]
have studied the constant
K(\mathbb{D},\lambda)
whenever
\lambda\geq 1
. In fact, Bombieri
[
13
]
has obtained the exact value

K(\mathbb{D},\lambda)=\frac{1}{3\lambda-2\sqrt{2(\lambda^{2}-1)}},

where
\lambda\in[1,\sqrt{2}]
, and Bombieri and Bourgain
[
14
]
have determined the exact asymptotic behavior of
K(\mathbb{D},\lambda)
as
\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-1}/\lambda
whenever
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. In 2010, Blasco
[
8
]
established that
K(\ell^{2}_{q})=0
for
1\leq q\leq\infty
. Thus, the study of Bohr radius problem becomes irrelevant for
\text{dim}(X)>1
. Hence, it seems reasonable to modify the Bohr inequality to obtain more interesting result. That is why Defant
et al.

[
22
]
have introduced
\lambda
in the definition of Bohr radius
K(B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}},T,\lambda)
of bounded linear operator
T
.

A complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that if
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega
, then
(\lambda e^{i\theta_{1}}z_{1},\ldots,\lambda e^{i\theta_{n}}z_{n})\in\Omega
for all
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and all
\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
. In this paper, we mostly focus on the complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega=B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
, unit balls in
\ell^{n}_{q}
spaces, where
q\in[1,\infty]
. For
q\in[1,\infty)
,

B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<1\right\},

and

B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}}=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{1\leq i\leq n}|z_{i}|<1\right\}.

Apart from Bohr radius, there is another interesting variant of multidimensional Bohr radius in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the arithmetic Bohr radius. In 2008, Defant
et al.

[
20
,
21
]
introduced this radius and later extensively studied the arithmetic Bohr radius for complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Defant and his coauthors
[
21
]
have used the arithmetic Bohr radius as a very useful technical tool to describe the domains of existence of the monomial expansion of holomorphic functions on a complete Reinhardt domain in a complex Banach space (see also the monograph
[
19
]
). It is the right time to introduce the following notion of arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded linear operators between complex Banach spaces which will be the center of discussion of our paper.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathcal{O}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
be the set of all holomorphic mappings on the unit ball
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
of
\ell^{n}_{q}
spaces into a complex Banach space
X
. Further, let
1\leq p<\infty
,
\lambda\geq 1
, and
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded linear operator between complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
such that
\left\lVert T\right\rVert<\lambda^{1/p}
. Then the
\lambda_{p}
-arithmetic Bohr radius of
T
is defined as

A_{p}(\mathcal{O}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T,\lambda):=\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0},\,\forall f\in\mathcal{O}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X):\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\right\}.

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the following classes of holomorphic functions:

•

\mathcal{O}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)=\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
, the set of all bounded holomorphic functions,

•

\mathcal{O}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)=\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
, the set of all
m
-homogeneous polynomials on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as defined on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
.

We write
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for
A_{p}(\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T,\lambda)
. If
T
is the identity map on
X
then we use the notation
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
and
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. For the class
\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
we write
A^{m}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for
A_{p}(\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T,\lambda)
. Defant
et al.

[
20
]
have studied the arithmetic Bohr radius
A(\Omega)
for
p=1
and complete Reinhardt domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Recently, Kumar and Manna
[
28
]
have studied the arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for
p=1
. Further, Kumar
[
27
]
and Allu
et al.

[
4
]
have investigated the arithmetic Bohr radius for scalar valued and Banach space valued holomorphic functions respectively. Moreover, arithmetic Bohr radius for holomorphic functions on complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with positive real part has been investigated by Allu
et al.

[
5
]
.

Our main aim of this paper is to study the arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
of bounded linear operators between complex Banach spaces for any
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
. We establish a close relationship between the arithmetic Bohr radius and the classical Bohr radius. In case of identity operator on infinite dimensional complex Banach space
X
, we give the correct asymptotic behavior of the arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
. Further, we give the asymptotic estimates of arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
, where
T:\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t}
is an embedding between sequence spaces. As a consequence, we prove the exact asymptotic behavior of arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for any bounded operator
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s}
.

2.
Preliminaries

We employ standard terminologies and notions from classical theory of Banach spaces (see e.g.
[
30
,
31
]
). We denote
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
to be the open unit disk in complex plane
\mathbb{C}
. Throughout this paper, we assume that every Banach space
X
is complex. Let
X^{*}
denote the topological dual of Banach space
X
and we write
B_{X}
for the open unit ball in
X
. As usual, we denote
\ell_{q}
(or
\ell^{n}_{q}
),
1\leq q\leq\infty
for the Banach space of all scalar sequences (or
n
-tuples
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
) endowed with the norm

\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}:=\left(\sum_{j}|z_{j}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,1\leq q<\infty

and
\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}:=\sup_{j}|z_{j}|
for
q=\infty
. For
x\in X
, the absolute value of
x
is defined by
|x|=x\vee(-x)
. Recall that a Banach lattice is a Banach space
X
which is a vector lattice with
|x|\leq|y|
implies
\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert y\right\rVert
for all
x,y\in X
. A Banach lattice is said to be
r
-concave, where
1\leq r<\infty
, if there is a constant
M>0
such that

(2.1)

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{j}\right\rVert^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq M\left\lVert\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{j}|^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right\rVert

for every arbitrarily chosen finite elements
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
. The best such constant satisfying (
2.1
) is as usual denoted by
M_{r}(X)
. For
2\leq r\leq\infty
, a Banach space
X
is said to have cotype
r
, if there is a constant
C>0
such that for every choice of finitely many vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, we have

(2.2)

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{j}\right\rVert^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}(t)x_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
r_{j}
is the
j
th Rademacher function on
[0,1]
. Further, the best such constant
C
satisfying (
2.2
) is usually denoted by
C_{r}(X)
. We write

\text{Cot}(X):=\inf\left\{2\leq r\leq\infty:X\,\,\mbox{has cotype}\,r\right\}.

For a Banach space
X
with cotype
\infty

i.e.
,
\text{Cot}(X)=\infty
, we denote
\frac{1}{\text{Cot}(X)}=0
. We note that the notions of concavity and cotype are closely related concepts in the context of Banach lattices. In fact, a
r
-concave Banach lattice with
r\geq 2
is of cotype
r
. On the other hand, each Banach lattice of cotype
2
is
2
-concave, and a Banach lattice of cotype
r>2
is
s
-concave for all
s>r
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the following multi-index set

\Lambda(m,n)=\left\{\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}:|\alpha|=m\right\},

where
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
. For
1\leq q\leq\infty
, let
\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)
be the linear space of all
m
-homogeneous polynomial
Q:\ell^{n}_{q}\rightarrow X
of
n
complex variables with values in
X
, equipped with uniform (or sup) norm

\left\lVert Q\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{q}}:=\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert_{X}.

Using the above multi-index notations, a polynomial
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}\ell^{n}_{q},X)

can be expressed in the form

Q(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha},

where
x_{\alpha}\in X
. If
f:B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}\rightarrow X
is an
X
-valued holomorphic function then we write
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}(f)z^{\alpha}
as the monomial series expansion of
f
, where
x_{\alpha}(f)=(\partial^{\alpha}f(0))/\alpha!
is the
\alpha^{\rm th}
coefficient of the expansion.

A bounded linear operator
T:X\rightarrow Y
between two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
is called
(r,s)
-summing,
1\leq r,s<\infty
, if there is a constant
\widetilde{C}>0
such that for each choice of finitely many vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
, we have

(2.3)

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert T(x_{j})\right\rVert^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq\widetilde{C}\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x^{*}(x_{j})|^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Also, the best such constant
\widetilde{C}
satisfying (
2.3
) is denoted by
\pi_{r,s}(T)
. In particular, if
r=s
, we call
T
is
r
-summing and we write the best constant as
\pi_{r}(T)
.

For two sequences of positive numbers
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
, we write
x_{n}\prec y_{n}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
x_{n}\leq Cy_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x_{n}\asymp y_{n}
whenever
x_{n}\prec y_{n}
and
y_{n}\prec x_{n}
.

3.
Main Results

In this paper, we first prove the arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
in terms of

A_{p}(\cup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T,\lambda)
which shows the connection between arithmetic Bohr radii of two different classes
H_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
and
\cup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded operator between complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
with
\left\lVert T\right\rVert<\lambda^{1/p}
. Then we have

(a)

\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}A_{p}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T,\lambda\right)\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\leq A_{p}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T,\lambda\right)

(b)

\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}A_{p}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X),T\right)\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda).

As discussed before, our aim is to establish the asymptotic estimates for arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
of bounded linear operators. To prove those estimates, we shall use the bounds of Bohr radius
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
of bounded linear operators
T
. That is why, we require a relation between these two radii. The next result Lemma
3.2
will provide a connection between the arithmetic Bohr radius and Bohr radius of bounded linear operators. Before that, we need the following notation from
[
20
]
. For bounded complete Reinhardt domains
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
we use the notation

S(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}):=\inf\{t>0:\Omega_{1}\subset t\Omega_{2}\}.

Further, Defant
et al.

[
18
]
have shown that if
\Omega
is a bounded complete Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then

\displaystyle S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{q}})=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\,\,\,1\leq q<\infty,

\displaystyle S(\Omega,B_{\ell^{n}_{\infty}})=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\sup_{1\leq j\leq n}|z_{j}|.

The following lemma is a key tool in estimating the lower bounds for arithmetic Bohr radius of bounded linear operators.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded linear operator between complex Banach spaces
X
and
Y
. Then for every
1\leq\lambda<\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\geq\frac{K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)}{n^{1/q}}.

Next, we want to study the arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for identity operator
T
on infinite dimensional complex Banach space
X
. Recently, Allu
et al.

[
4
,
6
]
have studied the correct asymptotic estimates for
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
for finite dimensional complex Banach space
X
. To establish the estimates for arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
, we need the following result for Bohr radius
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
, where
X
is an infinite dimensional complex Banach space.

Theorem A
.

[
6
]

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
. If
X
is an infinite dimensional complex Banach space of cotype
r
with
2\leq r\leq\infty
, then we have

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq\text{Cot}(X)=r$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{Cot(X)}\right)-\frac{1}{q}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}},&\mbox{for \, $q>\text{Cot}(X)=r$}\end{array}\right.

and

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)n^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$},\end{array}\right.

where
s=r/(r-1)
.

We observe that asymptotic estimates of arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
is governed by the concept of geometry of Banach space. In fact, the bounds of arithmetic Bohr radius are influenced by optimal cotype
\text{Cot}(X):=\mbox{the infimum over all $r$, where}\,\,r\in[2,\infty)
for which the Banach space
X
has cotype
r
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq\lambda<\infty
. Suppose
X
is an infinite dimensional complex Banach space of cotype
r
with
2\leq r\leq\infty
. Then for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we have

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n},&\mbox{for \, $p\leq r$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n^{1-p\left(\frac{1}{\text{Cot}(X)}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}},&\mbox{for \, $p>r$},\end{array}\right.

and

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)n^{1/q}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)n^{1/s}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$},\end{array}\right.

where
s=r/(r-1)
.

Following the techniques from Defant
et al.

[
22
]
we investigate the Bohr radii of operators between Banach spaces, specifically here we consider the embeddings
T:\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\,\,1\leq s\leq t<\infty
and arbitrary operators
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s}
. Our first aim is to study the Bohr radius
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for aforesaid operators. Before proceeding further discussion, we want to mention an important result recently proved in
[
6
]
estimating the lower bounds of
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
for any bounded linear operator
T
and
1\leq p<\infty
which plays a integral role in the following results.

Theorem B
.

[
6
]

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
T:X\rightarrow Y
be a bounded operator between Banach spaces
X
and
Y
.

(1)

Assume that
X
or
Y
is of cotype
r
with
2\leq r\leq\infty
. Then there is a constant
D>0
such that for every
\left\lVert T\right\rVert<\lambda^{1/p}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
n

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D\,\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
D\,\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$},\end{array}\right.

where
s=r/(r-1)
.

(2)

Assume that
Y
is a
r
-concave Banach lattice with
2\leq r<\infty
and there is a
1\leq s<r
such that the operator
T
is
(s,1)
-summing. Then there is a constant
D>0
such that

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\geq D\,\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{r}}

for every
\left\lVert T\right\rVert<\lambda^{1/p}
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

By unifying Theorem
B
and the well-known Grothendieck type theorems, we execute the proof of Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
. The actual form of Grothendieck’s theorem
[
23
]
states that every bounded linear operator
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{2}
is absolutely summing
i.e.,

1
-summing. A preceding version of this result is actually an old result by Littlewood
[
32
]
: The formal inclusion
\ell_{1}\hookrightarrow\ell_{4/3}
is
(4/3,1)
-summing, also known as the Littlewood’s
4/3
-theorem. After this, these classical results got many extensions in
\ell_{q}
spaces, and the most significant ones are due to Kwapień
[
29
]
and Bennett-Carl
[
7
,
15
]
. In Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, we obtain the estimates for arithmetic Bohr radius of inclusions
T:\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t}
and bounded operators
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s}
respectively.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq\lambda<\infty
, and
1\leq s<t<\infty
. Then with constants depending only on
\lambda
,
p
, and
s,t
, we have

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\prec\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}},&\mbox{for \, $1\leq s\leq 2$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{s}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\end{array}\right.

and

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\succ\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}},&\mbox{for \, $1\leq s\leq 2$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{e},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq r$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{en^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>r$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$},\end{array}\right.

where
r=s/(s-1)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq s<\infty
, and
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s}
be any bounded operator. Then with constants depending only on
T
,
\lambda
and
p
, we have

\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\max\{2,s\}}}\prec K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\prec n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}.

We note that the classical multidimensional Bohr radius and arithmetic Bohr radius are two closely related variants of Bohr radius. To investigate the asymptotic behavior of arithmetic Bohr radius, we find the estimates of classical Bohr radius very useful. We use this observation for our next main results too. By virtue of Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, we study the asymptotic estimates for arithmetic Bohr radius
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
, where
T
is the inclusions
\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\,\,1\leq s<t<\infty
and any bounded operators
\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s},\,\,1\leq s<\infty
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq\lambda<\infty
, and
1\leq s<t<\infty
. Then with constants depending only on
\lambda
,
p
, and
s,t
, we have

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\prec\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\log n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{q,2\}}-\frac{1}{2}}},&\mbox{for \, $1\leq s\leq 2$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{p}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{p}{s}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\end{array}\right.

and

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\succ\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\sqrt{\log n}}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $1\leq s\leq 2$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{en^{\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq r$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{en^{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>r$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$},\end{array}\right.

where
r=s/(s-1)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq s<\infty
, and
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s}
be any bounded operator. Then with constants depending only on
T
,
\lambda
and
p
, we have

\frac{1}{n^{1/q}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\max\{2,s\}}}\prec A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\prec\frac{(\log n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{q,2\}}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Proof of the Main results

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

The right-hand inequality of (a) is clear from the fact that

\bigcup^{\infty}_{m=1}\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)\subset\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X).

Therefore, we want to prove the left-hand inequality of (a). Let
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha}(Q))\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\lambda\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert^{p}

for all vector-valued
m
-homogeneous polynomial
Q(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}(Q)z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
.
Fix some
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. It is worth noting that for
1\leq p<\infty
,

0<\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{1/p}<1.

Then for
1\leq p<\infty
and by using the Cauchy-Riemann inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T(x_{0})\right\rVert^{p}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{m}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{p}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{m}\lambda\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+\lambda\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)^{m}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}=\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

Hence, it follows that

\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{2\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right)\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda),

which gives the desired result.

By following the similar process, we shall now prove the inequality (b). Let
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that for all
1\leq p<\infty
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha}(Q))\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert^{p}

for all
Q(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
. Suppose
g(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
is a bounded holomorphic function on
B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
with values in complex Banach space
X
. By the virtue of Cauchy-Riemann inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{\alpha}r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T(x_{0})\right\rVert^{p}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{m}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda(m,n)}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}\left\lVert x_{0}\right\rVert^{p}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{m}\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert g(z)\right\rVert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{m}\right)\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}=\lambda\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

Evidently, we obtain

\left(\frac{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}{\lambda-\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}+1}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda),

which gives the required inequality. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

From the definition of the notation
S
, we have

S(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})=\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{1}}=n^{1-\frac{1}{q}}.

Then for a given
0<\epsilon<K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
we can find a member
\widetilde{z_{0}}=(\widetilde{z_{1}},\ldots,\widetilde{z_{n}})\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
such that

\left\lVert\widetilde{z_{0}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\geq S(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{\ell^{n}_{1}})-\epsilon.

Let
s:=K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)-\epsilon
,
w=s\widetilde{z_{0}}
, and
r:=s|\widetilde{z_{0}}|=|w|=s(|\widetilde{z_{1}}|,\ldots,|\widetilde{z_{n}}|)
. Since
w\in sB_{\ell^{n}_{q}}
and
s<K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
, for
f(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}x_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
we obtain

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})\right\rVert^{p}r^{p\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})s^{|\alpha|}\widetilde{z_{0}}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\left\lVert\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}}\left\lVert T(x_{\alpha})z^{\alpha}\right\rVert\right\rVert^{p}_{sB_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\leq\lambda\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}.

It follows that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}=\frac{K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)-\epsilon}{n}\left\lVert\widetilde{z_{0}}\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{1}}\geq\frac{K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)-\epsilon}{n}\left(n^{1-\frac{1}{q}}-\epsilon\right),

which holds for all
\epsilon>0
.
Therefore, we obtain

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\geq\frac{K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)}{n^{1/q}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The lower bound of
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
A
. Indeed, the authors
[
6
]
have shown the following: If
X
is an infinite dimensional complex Banach space of cotype
r
with
2\leq r\leq\infty
then for every
1\leq p<\infty
we have

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)n^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$},\end{array}\right.

where
s=r/(r-1)
. In view of the above lower estimate of
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)
and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)n^{1/q}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
\left(\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}\right)^{1/p}\frac{1}{e\,C_{r}(X)n^{1/s}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$},\end{array}\right.

where
s=r/(r-1)
.

To prove the upper bound we require a deep result by Maurey and Pisier
[
23
, Theorem 14.5]
: For every infinite dimensional Banach space
X
,

\inf\left\{2\leq r\leq\infty:X\,\,\mbox{has cotype}\,\,r\right\}=\sup\left\{2\leq r\leq\infty:X\,\,\mbox{finitely factors}\,\,\ell_{r}\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}\right\}.

We say that a Banach space
X
finitely factors
\ell_{r}\hookrightarrow\ell_{\infty}
if for every
\epsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
such that

(4.1)

\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}x_{j}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{r}

for all
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In particular, by considering
z=e_{j}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
, (
4.1
) becomes

(4.2)

\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}=\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert x_{j}\right\rVert,\,\,\,1\leq j\leq n.

Let
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert x_{j}(Q)\right\rVert^{p}r^{p}_{j}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert^{p}

for any
1\leq p<\infty
and for every
1
-homogeneous polynomial
Q(z)=\sum_{j}x_{j}(Q)z_{j}\in\mathcal{P}(^{1}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)
, where
X
is an infinite dimensional complex Banach space of cotype
r
with
2\leq r\leq\infty
. Therefore, in view of (
4.2
), for a given
\epsilon>0
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
such that for any
1\leq p<\infty
we have

(4.3)

\frac{1}{(1+\epsilon)^{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{j}\right\rVert^{p}r^{p}_{j},

where
r_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
,
1\leq j\leq n
. Further, the above equation (
4.3
) gives

(4.4)

\frac{n}{(1+\epsilon)^{p}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}r_{j}\right)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert x_{j}\right\rVert^{p}r^{p}_{j}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}x_{j}\right\rVert^{p}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{p}_{r}.

Now, we observe that if
q\leq\text{Cot}(X)
then
\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\text{Cot}(X)}\leq 1
. Then, by the virtue of (
4.4
), we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\leq\frac{(1+\epsilon)^{p}}{n},

which holds for every
\epsilon>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Hence, it shows that

(4.5)

A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)\leq\frac{1}{n}.

On the other hand, if
q>\text{Cot}(X)=r
, then we obtain

\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\text{Cot}(X)}\leq n^{\frac{1}{\text{Cot}(X)}-\frac{1}{q}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}.\quad(\mbox{ we assume the convention}\,\,n^{\frac{1}{\infty}}=1)

Therefore, in view of (
4.4
) we have the following:

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\leq\frac{(1+\epsilon)^{p}}{n^{1-p\left(\frac{1}{\text{Cot}(X)}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}}

holds for every
\epsilon>0
. Consequently, it gives that

(4.6)

A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X)\leq\frac{1}{n^{1-p\left(\frac{1}{\text{Cot}(X)}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}}.

Now, it is worth noting that

(4.7)

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)=\lambda^{1/p}A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X).

Therefore, by using (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) in (
4.7
), we obtain

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n},&\mbox{for \, $p\leq r$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n^{1-p\left(\frac{1}{\text{Cot}(X)}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}},&\mbox{for \, $p>r$}.\end{array}\right.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

We first establish the upper bound of
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)
. We divide the proof into several cases.

The case
1\leq s\leq 2
.
To establish the upper estimate in this case, we shall use the following observation:

(4.8)

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\leq K^{p}\left(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},\frac{\lambda}{\left\lVert T\right\rVert^{p}}\right),

which holds for any bounded operator
T
and for each
p\in[1,\infty)
. Moreover, in
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
, the authors have proved that

(4.9)

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\prec n^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}

for every finite dimensional complex Banach space
X
and for each
1\leq p<\infty
.
Hence, in view of (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we obtain the desired upper estimate.

The case
2\leq s<\infty
.
By the virtue of result by Maurey and Pisier
[
23
, Theorem 14.5]
and by use of the definition of
K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t})
we have

\displaystyle n^{1-\frac{p}{q}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert^{p}_{s}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{p}{q}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t}))^{p}}\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}e_{j}\right\rVert^{p}_{s}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t}))^{p}}\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{p}_{s}.

Now, we observe that if
q\leq s
then
\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{s}\leq 1
. Thus, in view of (
4.10
), we have

(4.11)

K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t})\leq\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}}.

Conversely, if
q>s
we have

\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{s}\leq\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{q}n^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}},

whence, from (
4.10
) we obtain

(4.12)

K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t})\leq\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{s}}}.

We note that
K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)=\lambda^{1/p}K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T)
for any bounded operator
T
. Therefore, in view of (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we obtain

K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{\lambda^{1/p}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{s}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$}.\end{array}\right.

We note that
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\leq K^{p}_{1}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)
.
Therefore, we obtain

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\prec\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{1}{n^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{s}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$\, and\, $2\leq s<\infty$}.\end{array}\right.

Next, we proceed to prove the lower bound of
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)
. To determine the lower bound we consider three different cases.

The case
1\leq s<t\leq 2
.
In this case, we use the Bennett-Carl theorem
[
7
,
15
]
which states that the inclusion
\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t}
is
(h,1)
-summing where

\frac{1}{h}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{s}-\max\left\{\frac{1}{t},\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

It is worth noting that
\ell_{t}
is
2
-concave and thus the lower estiamte for
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)
is a consequence of Theorem
B
(2). Therefore, we obtain

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\succ\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.

The case
1\leq s<2\leq t
.
Here, we observe that
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\geq K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{2},\lambda)
for any
1\leq p<\infty
. Therefore, we obtain the required estimate using the preceding case.

The case
2\leq s
.
We notice the following observation:

K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\geq K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{s},\lambda).

Then, in view of Theorem
A
, we obtain the concluding part of our required estimate. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Clearly, the upper bound of
K^{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
can be obtained from Theorem
3.2
. To establish the lower bound we need an important result by Kwapień
[
29
]
which generalizes the well-known Grothendieck’s theorem (for
s=2
) as well as Littlewood’s theorem (for
T
being the inclusion
\ell_{1}\hookrightarrow\ell_{4/3}
): Every operator
T:\ell_{1}\rightarrow\ell_{s}
is
(r,1)
-summing, where

\frac{1}{r}=1-\bigg|\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg|.

Therefore, applying Theorem
B
(2) and by using the fact that
\ell_{s}
is
\max\{2,s\}
-concave, we obtain our desired lower estimate. This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

The lower bound of
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)
can be deduced from Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
3.2
. To establish the upper bound, we divide the proof into two different cases.

The case
1\leq s\leq 2
.
This case can be settled by use of the result proved by Allu
et. al.

[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
. In fact the authors
[
4
]
have proved the following upper bound: For any
1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\geq 1
we have

(4.13)

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},X,\lambda)\prec\frac{(\log n)^{1-\frac{1}{\min\{q,2\}}}}{n^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\max\{q,2\}}-\frac{1}{2}}}.

It is worth noting that for any bounded operator
T
we have

(4.14)

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)\leq A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\mathbb{C},\lambda).

In view of (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we obtain the desired upper bound for
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)
.

The case
2\leq s<\infty
.
Following the techniques from the Theorem
3.1
, we deal the upper bound in this case. Let
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
be such that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert T(x_{j}(Q))\right\rVert^{p}_{\ell^{n}_{t}}r_{j}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert Q(z)\right\rVert^{p}_{\ell^{n}_{s}}

for every
1
-homogeneous polynomial
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{1}B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},B_{\ell^{n}_{s}})
, where
T:\ell^{n}_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell^{n}_{t}
with
2\leq s\leq\infty
.
Therefore, we have

(4.15)

\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\frac{1}{n^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}r^{p}_{j}\frac{\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert^{p}}{n^{\frac{p}{q}}}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}e_{j}\right\rVert^{p}_{\ell^{n}_{s}}\leq\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{p}_{\ell^{n}_{s}}.

We observe that for
q\leq s
we have
\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{s}\leq 1
. Hence, in view of (
4.15
) we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\leq\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{p}{q}}}.

Therefore, it follows that

(4.16)

A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t})\leq\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{p}{q}}}.

On the other hand, if
q>s
then we have

\sup_{z\in B_{\ell^{n}_{q}}}\left\lVert z\right\rVert_{\ell^{n}_{s}}\leq n^{\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{q}}.

So, by the virtue of (
4.15
), we obtain

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{j}\leq\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{p}{s}}}.

Consequently, it shows that

(4.17)

A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t})\leq\frac{1}{n^{1-\frac{p}{s}}}.

From (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) and by using the following simple observation

A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)=\lambda^{1/p}A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t}),

we obtain

A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}}{n^{1-\frac{p}{q}}},&\mbox{for \, $q\leq s$}\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}}}{n^{1-\frac{p}{s}}},&\mbox{for \, $q>s$}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the final conclusion follows from the fact that

A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda)\leq A^{1}_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},\ell_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell_{t},\lambda).

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

We observe that the upper bound for
A_{p}(B_{\ell^{n}_{q}},T,\lambda)
follows from Theorem
3.4
. On the other hand, the lower bound is just a consequence of Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
3.2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Generic regularity of equilibrium measures for the logarithmic potential with external fields
Giacomo Colombo and Alessio Figalli

Abstract
. A well-known conjecture in the theory of Coulomb gases and the mean-field limit of β \beta -ensembles asserts that, generically, external potentials are “off-critical” (or equivalently, “regular”). By exploiting the connection between minimizing measures and thin obstacle problems, we establish this conjecture.



1.
Introduction

Given a potential
V:\R\to\R
, a central question arising both from the theory of Coulomb gases and the mean-field limit of
\beta
-ensembles is to understand the behavior of probability measures
\mu_{V}
minimizing the energy

(1)

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)\coloneqq 2\int_{\R}V(x)\,d\mu(x)-\int_{\R}\int_{\R}\log|x-y|\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)

(see, for instance,
[
saff_totik1997
,
serfaty2024lectures
]
).
The classical case
V(x)=x^{2}
, which comes from Gaussian Orthogonal Ensembles, was first studied in
[
wigner1958distribution
]
, and the minimizing probability is known as the “semicircle law”, since its density
\frac{d\mu_{V}}{dx}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{(x+1)(1-x)}
is, up to a constant, the graph of the (upper) unit circle.

\beta
-models

Most results on
\beta
-models (see, for instance,
[
anderson2010introduction
,
BG11
,
erdos2012local
,
bourgade2014edge
,
BFG15
,
FG16
,
BG_multicut24
]
and the references therein) rely on the assumption that the semicircle represents the general behavior of such minimizing measures. More precisely, given a potential
V,
the following assumption (usually called “off-criticality” or “regularity” assumption) is made:

(A)

The minimizing probability
\mu_{V}
is supported over finitely many disjoint compact intervals and, inside each such interval
[a,b]
,
it has the form

(2)

\frac{d\mu_{V}}{dx}=Q_{V}(x)\sqrt{(x-a)(b-x)},

for some function
Q_{V}:\R\to\R
satisfying
0<c\leq Q_{V}(x)\leq C
.

Unfortunately, the property above is known to be false for arbitrary potentials. Still, it is conjectured to be “generically” true.
In the case of analytic potentials, this has indeed been shown in
[
kuijlaars2000generic
]
, but up to now, nothing was known in the non-analytic setting (except for some very special cases).

In this article, we give a positive answer to this conjecture for
C^{2,\alpha}
potentials. Also, we show that higher regularity assumptions on
V
yield higher regularity of
Q_{V}
.
Because it is well-known that the space of regular potentials is open, the challenge is to prove that they are dense.

Here and in the following, given
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in[0,1],
we denote by
C_{\rm loc}^{j,\beta}(\R)
the space of functions
g:\R\to\R
that belong to
C^{j}(\R)
and whose
j
-th derivative is locally
\beta
-Hölder continuous, that is

\sup_{x\neq y\in[-R,R]}\frac{|D^{j}g(x)-D^{j}g(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}<\infty\qquad\forall\,\,R>0.

Our first main result is the following (see Theorem
2.1
below for a more precise statement):

{teo}

Given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
V\in C_{\rm loc}^{2,\alpha}(\R)
satisfy
\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}\frac{V(x)}{\log|x|}=+\infty
. Given
\gamma\in\R
, consider the family of potentials
V_{s,\gamma}(x):=\frac{V(s^{\gamma}x)}{s}
,
s>0
. Then
V_{s,\gamma}
is regular for a.e.
s>0
.

In particular, the set of potentials for which
(A)
holds is open and dense in the class of
C_{\rm loc}^{2,\alpha}
potentials that diverge at infinity faster than a logarithm.

Furthermore, if
(A)
holds and
V\in C_{\rm loc}^{k+1/2+\beta}(\R)
for some
k\geq 2
and
\beta\in(0,1/2)\cup(1/2,1)
,
1

C_{\rm loc}^{k+1/2+\beta}(\R)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C_{\rm loc}^{k,\beta+1/2}(\R)&\text{if }\beta\leq 1/2,\\
C_{\rm loc}^{k+1,\beta-1/2}(\R)&\text{if }\beta>1/2.\end{array}\right.

then

{oss}

In the case of analytic potentials, this result with
\gamma=1

[
kuijlaars2000generic

Discrete
\beta
-models

A discrete version of the previous problem naturally arises, for instance, from asymptotics of distributions of discrete
\beta
-ensembles or in the study of orthogonal polynomials of a discrete variable, see
[
borodin2017gaussian

In this case, given

\theta>0
, the energy (

\theta

\theta|K|>1

In addition, as considered in

(3)

\mu([a_{h},b_{h}])=\hat{n}_{h}\geq 0,\qquad\sum_{h=1}^{N}\hat{n}_{h}=1,\qquad 0<\hat{n}_{h}<\theta|b_{h}-a_{h}|.

|V^{\prime}(x)|\leq C\left(1+\sum_{h=1}^{N}\big|\log|x-a_{h}|\big|+\big|\log|x-b_{h}|\big|\right)

[

(B)

\begin{split}&\frac{d\mu_{V,\theta}}{dx}(x)=Q_{-}\sqrt{|x-p_{-}|}\text{ for $x\in\left\{0<{\textstyle\frac{d\mu_{V,\theta}}{dx}}<\theta\right\}$, $|x-p_{-}|\ll 1$,}\\
&\text{(resp. }\frac{d\mu_{V,\theta}}{dx}=\theta-Q_{+}\sqrt{|x-p_{+}|}\text{ for $x\in\left\{0<{\textstyle\frac{d\mu_{V,\theta}}{dx}}<\theta\right\}$, $|x-p_{+}|\ll 1$)}.\end{split}

Riesz potentials

One can also consider more general energies of the form

\cE(\mu)=\iint_{\R^{d}\times\R^{d}}\mathfrak{g}(x-y)\,d\mu(x)\,d\mu(y)+2\int_{\R^{d}}V(x)\,d\mu(x),

where
d\geq 1
and

(6)

\mathfrak{g}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sigma}|x|^{-\sigma}&\text{if }\sigma\neq 0,\\
-\log|x|&\text{if }\sigma=0,\end{cases}\qquad\text{with}\quad\sigma\in(d-2,d).

These energies arise from the study of Riesz gases (see for instance
[
serfaty2024lectures
]
and references therein).
In this context, the analogue of assumption
(A)
is the following:

(
\mathbf{A_{\sigma}}
)

The minimizing probability
\mu_{V}
is supported over finitely many disjoint compact sets
\{K_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq M}\subset\R^{d}
, with
\partial K_{j}
a
(d-1)
-dimensional manifold of class
C^{1}
. Also, inside
K_{j}
,
it has the form

(7)

\frac{d\mu_{V}}{dx}=Q_{V}(x)\,{\rm dist}(x,\partial K_{j})^{1-\frac{d-\sigma}{2}},

for some function
Q_{V}:\R^{d}\to\R
satisfying
0<c\leq Q_{V}(x)\leq C
.

As discussed in Appendix
D
,
our strategy also applies in this case and proves the generic validity of
(
\mathbf{A_{\sigma}}
)
in low dimensions (see
Theorem
D
).

1.1.
Comments on the proofs and structure of the paper

To prove Theorem
1
,
we adopt Serfaty’s strategy
to associate to
\mu_{V}
a solution to the thin obstacle problem (see
[
Serfaty2015
,
LS17
,
serfaty2024lectures
,
peilen_serfaty2024local
]
and Section
2.1
below).
In this way, one may hope to apply the regularity theory available for generic solutions of the thin obstacle problem
[
FERNANDEZREAL2023109323
]
. Note that the latter theory applies only to a strictly monotone family of solutions, so one would need to find perturbations of
V
that induce monotone perturbations of the solutions.
While this seems difficult to achieve, we follow an idea from
[
kuijlaars2000generic
]
where, instead of changing the potential, we vary the mass constraint.
This variation yields a monotone family of solutions (see Proposition
3
), allowing the application of the generic regularity theory for the thin obstacle problem. Finally, by using scaling properties of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{V}
, we obtain the desired result (see Remark
3
).

While in the case of
\beta
-models we can rely on the available theory for the thin obstacle problem, the discrete case (Theorem
1
) is considerably more challenging. In this setting, too, the regularity of the minimizing measure can be related to the regularity of a solution of a PDE. However, the additional constraint on the upper bound of the measure’s density forces the solution to satisfy a two-phase thin obstacle problem (see Theorem
2.2
), for which no generic regularity theory is currently available.
A key result established in Appendix
B
addresses this difficulty: in the two-phase thin obstacle problem, phases with opposite signs cannot touch. This result is not only central to our analysis, but also provides a fundamental tool for studying the free boundary in cohesive zone models for fracture mechanics (cf.
[
Caffarelli2020
]
), and it extends a related result from
[
Allen15
]
.
Another challenge arises from the non-smoothness of
V
at the boundary of its support, which may create singularities in our solutions. These issues are resolved in Appendices
B
and
C
. In particular, the results proved in Appendix
B
fix a gap in
[
Caffarelli2020
]
and are of independent interest.

Since this paper uses and develops nontrivial PDE tools but has applications beyond the PDE community, we structure the paper as follows: In Section
2
, following Serfaty’s argument, we show the connection between minimizing measures and obstacle problems. Then, in Sections
3
and
4
we prove our main results, taking for granted the generic regularity properties of solutions to obstacle problems.
Finally, all PDE materials (both the known theory for the classical thin obstacle problem and the new theory needed for the case of discrete models) are postponed to Appendices
A
,
B
, and
C
.
In Appendix
D
, we briefly discuss the case of Riesz potentials.

The goal of this section is to discuss some preliminary facts about measures minimizing the energy (

This will allow us to reformulate our main theorems in a slightly different form, see Theorems

2.1.
From minimizing measures to the thin obstacle problem

Here and in the sequel, we denote by
\cM_{1}(\R)
the space of nonnegative probability measures on
\R
.
We begin with the non-discrete case.

{teo}
[
[
anderson2010introduction
, Lemma 2.6.2]
]
Let
V:\R\to\R
be a continuous potential satisfying

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{\log|x|}=+\infty.

Then there is a unique probability measure
\mu_{V}
minimizing

\inf\{\mathcal{E}_{V}(\nu)\,:\,\nu\in\cM_{1}(\R)\}.

In addition, a probability
\mu
is minimizing if and only if there is a constant
C\in\R
such that

(8)

-\int_{\R}\log|x-t|\,d\mu(t)\geq C-V(x)\quad\text{ for every }x\in\R,\text{ with equality }\mu\text{-a.e}.

To connect minimizing measures to the thin obstacle problem, given the measure
\mu_{V}
minimizing
\mathcal{E}_{V}
in
\cM_{1}(\R)

(9)

u_{V}(x)=-\big(\log|\cdot|*\mu_{V}\big)(x)-C_{V},\quad x\in\R^{2}.

Then, as discussed for instance in
[

, Chapter 2.4]
, (
8

solves the thin obstacle problem with obstacle

(10)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{V}\geq 0&\text{in }\R^{2},\\
\Delta u_{V}=0&\text{in }\R^{2}\setminus(\{u_{V}=-V\}\cap\{x_{2}=0\}),\\
u_{V}\geq-V&\text{in }\{x_{2}=0\},\\
\frac{u_{V}(x)}{\log|x|}\to-1&\text{as }|x|\to+\infty.\end{cases}

This motivates the following definition (here and in the sequel,

denotes the

\partial_{y}u(\cdot,0)-\partial_{y}u(\cdot+h,0)\geq-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|h|(v+2\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty})\quad\text{in }\{y=0,v>0\}.

Given
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
such that
u(x,0)>u(x+h,0)+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|h|
we split the proof in three cases:

\bullet
if
u(x+h,0)\geq\varphi(x+h)
then
u(x,0)>\varphi(x+h)+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|h|\geq\varphi(x)
. Thus on one hand (
25
) gives
\partial_{y}u(x,0)=f(u(x,0)-\varphi(x))
; on the other hand (
26
) yields
-\partial_{y}u(x+h,0)\geq-f(u(x+h,0)-\varphi(x+h))
. So

\begin{split}\partial_{y}u(x,0)-\partial_{y}u(x+h,0)&\geq f(u(x,0)-\varphi(x))-f(u(x+h,0)-\varphi(x+h))\\
&\geq-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|u(x,0)-\varphi(x)-u(x+h,0)+\varphi(x+h)|\\
&\geq-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|h|(v+2\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty});\end{split}

\bullet
if
u(x+h,0)<\varphi(x+h)
and
u(x,0)>\varphi(x)
, then

\partial_{y}u(x,0)-\partial_{y}u(x+h,0)=f(u(x,0)-\varphi(x))+f(-u(x+h,0)+\varphi(x+h))\geq 0;

\bullet
if
u(x+h,0)<\varphi(x+h)
and
u(x,0)\leq\varphi(x)
, then

\begin{split}\partial_{y}u(x,0)-\partial_{y}u(x+h,0)&\geq-f(-u(x,0)+\varphi(x))+f(-u(x+h,0)+\varphi(x+h))\\
&\geq-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|\varphi(x)-u(x,0)-\varphi(x+h)+u(x+h,0)|\\
&\geq-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|h|(v+2\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}).\end{split}

Thus, (
27
) holds.

It follows that the function

w=(1+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|y|)(v_{+}+2\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}|y|)

satisfies

\Delta w-\frac{2\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\sign(y)}{1+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|y|}w_{y}+\frac{2\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{(1+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|y|)^{2}}w\geq 0\quad\text{in }B_{2/3},

hence we can apply
[
GilbargTrudinger
, Theorem 8.17]
to find

\sup_{B_{1/2}}w\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}\leq C(\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}).

In particular

\frac{u(x,y)-u(x+h,y)}{|h|}-\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C(\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty})\quad\text{for }(x,y)\in B_{1/2}.

The same argument applied to
-u
in place of
u
yields

\frac{u(x+h,y)-u(x,y)}{|h|}-\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C(\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\infty})\quad\text{for }(x,y)\in B_{1/2},

and the result follows.

Lemma 13
.

If
u\in H^{1}(B_{1}^{+})
solves (
25
) then

|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta+Cy\quad\text{in }B_{1/2}^{+},

for some
C>0
depending only on
d
and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+})}
.

Proof B.2
.

We set
v(x,y)=u(x,y)-\varphi(x)
, so that
v\in H^{1}(B_{1}^{+})
satisfies

\begin{cases}\Delta v=-\Delta\varphi(x)\eqcolon g(x)&\text{in }B_{1}^{+},\\
\partial_{y}v=\sign(v)f(|v|)&\text{in }\{y=0,v\neq 0\},\\
|\partial_{y}v|\leq\theta&\text{in }\{y=0\},\end{cases}

with
g\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
.
Given
\delta>0
small, let
h\in\R^{d}
with
|h|<\delta
.
Given a function
\psi\colon B_{1}\to\R
we define
\delta_{h}\psi\colon B_{1-\delta}\to\R
by
\delta_{h}\psi\coloneqq\frac{1}{|h|}(\psi(x+h,y)-\psi(x,y))
. Extending
v
to
B_{1}
by even reflection in
y
we define

w\coloneqq\delta_{h}v\quad\text{in }B_{1-\delta}.

We note that

(28)

w\Delta w\geq w\,\delta_{h}g-2\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}w^{2}\cH^{d}\mres\{y=0\}\quad\text{in }B_{1-\delta},

as can be shown arguing as in the proof of (
27
).

We now fix a smooth cutoff
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1-\delta})
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{1-2\delta}
.
Using (
28
), the identity
\Delta(w^{2})/2=|\nabla w|^{2}+w\Delta w,
and
\Delta|y|=2\cH^{d}\mres\{y=0\}
, we compute

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}}\eta|\nabla w|^{2}&\leq\int_{B_{1}}\eta\Delta(w^{2}/2)-\eta w\,\delta_{h}g+\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\eta w^{2}\Delta(|y|)\\
&=\int_{B_{1}}[\Delta(\eta/2)-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\nabla(\eta)\cdot\nabla(|y|)]w^{2}-2\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\eta w\nabla w\cdot\nabla(|y|)+g\,\delta_{-h}(\eta w)\\
&\leq\int_{B_{1}}Cw^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\eta|\nabla w|^{2}+C\|g\|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Thus, recalling that
w=\delta_{h}v
,

\int_{B_{1-2\delta}}|\delta_{h}\nabla v|^{2}\leq C\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla v|^{2}+C\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

Using this and
\partial_{yy}v=g-\Delta_{x}v
, we deduce that
v\in H^{2}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
r\in(0,1)
. In particular
\partial_{y}u=\partial_{y}v\in H^{1}(B_{r}^{+})
for all
r\in(0,1)
.

It follows that if we extend
u
by even reflection to
\{y<0\}
, the function
|\partial_{y}u|
belongs to
H^{1}_{\loc}(B_{1})
, satisfies
|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta
in
\{y=0\}
, and is subharmonic in
B_{1}\setminus\{y=0\}
, therefore
\max\{\theta,|\partial_{y}u|\}
is subharmonic in
B_{1}
.
In particular,
|\partial_{y}u|\leq M
in
B_{2/3}^{+}
for some
M
depending only on
d,\theta
and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+})}
. As
|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta
in
B_{1}^{\prime}
, denoting by
\psi
the solution of

\begin{cases}\Delta\psi=0&\text{in }B_{2/3}^{+},\\
\psi=\theta&\text{in }B_{2/3}^{\prime},\\
\psi=M&\text{in }\partial B_{2/3},\end{cases}

we deduce from elliptic regularity and the maximum principle that

|\partial_{y}u|\leq\psi\leq\theta+Cy\quad\text{in }B_{1/2}^{+}

for some
C>0
depending only on
d
and
M
, as we wanted.

Lemma 14
.

There exists
C>0
, depending only on
d,\theta,\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+})},\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, and
\|\varphi\|_{C^{1}}
, such that if
u\in H^{1}(B_{1}^{+})
solves (
25
) then

|\nabla u|\leq C\quad\text{in }B_{1/2}^{+}.

Proof B.3
.

By Lemmas
13
and
12
together with a covering argument, it suffices to bound
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B_{2/3}^{+})}
. Fix a smooth cutoff function
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1})
such that
0\leq\eta
and
\eta\equiv 1
in
B_{2/3}
.
As
u\partial_{y}u\geq-\theta|\varphi|
in
B_{1}^{\prime}
, we compute

\begin{split}\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}&=-\int_{B_{1}^{\prime}}\eta^{2}u\partial_{y}u-2\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\eta u\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla u\\
&\leq 2\int_{B_{1}^{+}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{1}^{+}}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\end{split}

which yields

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2/3}^{+})}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+})}+C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}},

so the result follows.

B.2.
Lipschitz Global Solutions

We now consider Lipschitz functions
u\colon\R^{d+1}_{+}\to\R
satisfying

(29)

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }\R^{d}\times(0,+\infty),\\
|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta&\text{in }\R^{d}\times[0,+\infty),\\
\partial_{y}u(\cdot,0)=\theta\sign(u)&\text{in }\{u(\cdot,0)\neq 0\},\end{cases}

which will naturally arise from blow-ups of solutions of (
25
). The goal of this section is to show that there are no nontrivial blow-ups.

Proposition 15

(Classification of Lipschitz global solutions)
.

Let
u
be a Lipschitz global solution of (
29
) with
u(0)=0
.
Then
u(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
.

This result is
[
Allen15
, Lemma 8.3,
a=0
]
. Here we give a different proof.
We first recall the same result for solutions of the thin obstacle problem (cf. Appendix
A
).

Lemma 16
.

Let
u\colon\R^{d+1}\to\R
be a Lipschitz global solution of the thin obstacle problem (
22
) with
u(0)=0
. Then
u(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
.

Proof B.4
.

By regularity for the thin obstacle problem (see e.g.
[
Petrosyan2012
, Theorem 9.13]
)

[u(\cdot,0)]_{C^{1,1/2}(B_{1/2})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}.

Applying this to

u_{R}(x,y)=\tfrac{1}{R}u(Rx,Ry)

and letting
R\to+\infty
yields

[u(\cdot,0)]_{C^{1,1/2}(B_{R/2})}\leq C{R^{-1/2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\R^{d+1})}\to 0,

which implies the claim.

We will also make use of the ACF monotonicity formula, that we state in our setup.

Lemma 17

(
[
Petrosyan2012
, Theorems 2.4 and 2.9]
)
.

Let
u_{\pm}
be a pair of continuous functions such that

u_{\pm}\geq 0,\quad\Delta u_{\pm}\geq 0,\quad u_{+}\cdot u_{-}=0\quad\text{in }B_{1}.

Then the functional (recall that we write
(x,y)\in\R^{d}\times\R\cong\R^{d+1}
)

r\mapsto\Phi(r)=\Phi(r,u_{+},u_{-})\coloneqq\frac{1}{r^{4}}\int_{B_{r}}\frac{|\nabla u_{+}|^{2}}{|(x,y)|^{d-1}}\,dx\,dy\int_{B_{r}}\frac{|\nabla u_{-}|^{2}}{|(x,y)|^{d-1}}\,dx\,dy

is nondecreasing for
0<r<1
.

Suppose in addition that
\Phi(r_{1})=\Phi(r_{2})
for some
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<1
. Then the following holds:

i)

either
u_{+}\equiv 0
in
B_{r_{2}}
or
u_{-}\equiv 0
in
B_{r_{2}}
;

ii)

or there exist a unit vector
e\in\R^{d+1}
and constants
k_{\pm}>0
such that

u_{+}(x,y)=k_{+}((x,y)\cdot e)_{+},\quad u_{-}(x,y)=k_{-}((x,y)\cdot e)_{-}\quad\text{in }B_{r_{2}}.

Proof B.5

(Proof of Proposition
15
)
.

Given a Lipschitz global solution
u
of (
29
), we identify it with its even extension with respect to
y
. Note that
u_{+}\cdot u_{-}\equiv 0
and, since

\Delta u=2\theta\cH^{d}\mres\{y=0\}\geq 0\quad\text{in }\R^{d+1}\setminus\{y=0,u(\cdot,0)\leq 0\}

and
u
is continuous, we have
\Delta u_{+}\geq 0
. By symmetry we also have
\Delta u_{-}\geq 0
, so the functions
u_{\pm}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
17
.
Thus, if we define

\Phi(r,u)\coloneqq\Phi(r,u_{+},u_{-})

Lemma
17
together with the global Lipschitz regularity of
u
imply that the following limit exists and is finite:

\ell=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\Phi(R,u)\in[0,+\infty)

Consider now a blow-down
u_{\infty}
of
u
, namely, up to a subsequence,

u_{R}(x,y)\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{R}u(Rx,Ry)\overset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}u_{\infty}(x,y)\quad\text{weakly in $H^{1}_{\loc}(\R^{d+1})$ and locally uniformly.}

It is easy to see that
u_{\infty}
is a Lipschitz global solution of (
29
), even in the
y
variable.
In addition, since
|(x,y)|^{1-d}
is locally integrable and the functions
u_{R}
are equi-Lipschitz, there is a modulus of continuity
\omega_{r}
such that

\int_{B_{r}\cap\{|y|<\eps\}}\frac{|\nabla(u_{R})_{\pm}|^{2}}{|(x,y)|^{d-1}}\,dx\,dy<\omega_{r}(\eps).

Moreover, since
u_{R},u_{\infty}
are harmonic outside
\{y=0\}
, for all
r,\eps>0

\int_{B_{r}\cap\{|y|>\eps\}}\frac{|\nabla(u_{R})_{\pm}|^{2}}{|(x,y)|^{d-1}}\,dx\,dy\to\int_{B_{r}\cap\{|y|>\eps\}}\frac{|\nabla(u_{\infty})_{\pm}|^{2}}{|(x,y)|^{d-1}}\,dx\,dy.

Combining these two bounds, we obtain

\Phi(r,u_{R})\to\Phi(r,u_{\infty})\quad\forall\,r>0,\quad\text{as }R\to+\infty.

Since
\Phi(Rr,u)=\Phi(r,u_{R})
, letting
R\to+\infty
yields

\Phi(r,u_{\infty})=\ell\quad\forall\,r>0.

Thus, by the second part of Lemma
17
, for any
r>0
:

a)

either
u_{\infty}
has a sign in
B_{r}
,

b)

or there exist a unit vector
e
and constants
k_{\pm}>0
such that

(30)

(u_{\infty})_{\pm}(x,y)=k_{\pm}((x,y)\cdot e)_{\pm}\quad\text{in }B_{r}.

However, case b) is incompatible with (
29
).
Indeed, since
u
is even in the
y
variable, also the set
\{u>0\}
must be even with respect to the reflection across the plane
\{y=0\}
.
However, since the set
\{u>0\}=\{(x,y)\cdot e>0\}
is a hyperplane, this implies
e\cdot\mathbf{e}_{d+1}=0
, where
\mathbf{e}_{d+1}
is the
(d+1)
-th vector of the canonical Euclidean base of
\R^{d+1}
.
In particular,
\partial_{y}u\equiv 0
. Since
\partial_{y}u(\cdot,0)=\theta\sign(u)
when
u(\cdot,0)\neq 0
, the only possibility is that
u(\cdot,0)\equiv 0
, in contrast with (
30
).

Since case a) holds, this implies in particular that
\ell=0
.
Thanks to the monotonicity of
\Phi(r,u)
, this gives

\Phi(r,u)\equiv 0\quad\forall\,r>0,

and therefore (using again
the second part of Lemma
17
)
u
has a sign. Assume without loss of generality that
u\geq 0
and consider the function

v(x,y)=u-\theta|y|.

It follows from (
29
) and the fact that
u\geq 0
that
v
is a global Lipschitz solution of the thin obstacle problem in
\R^{d+1}
with
v(0)=0
. Thus, we conclude by applying Lemma
16
.

B.3.
Proof of Phase Separation

We prove Theorem
B
.
We borrow the following barrier argument from
[
Caffarelli2020
]
.

Lemma 18
.

There exist dimensional constants
\delta,\eta>0
such that the following holds:

Let
u
solve (
25
) in
B_{1}^{\prime}\times(0,1)
and assume that:

i)

\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\delta
;

ii)

\varphi(0)=|\nabla\varphi(0)|=0
and
|D^{2}\varphi|<\delta
;

iii)

u\leq\delta
in
B_{1}^{\prime}
and
|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta+\delta y
in
B_{1}^{\prime}\times(0,1)
;

iv)

there is
\hat{z}\in B_{1/100}^{\prime}
satisfying
u(\hat{z},0)>\varphi(\hat{z})
.

Then

\partial_{y}u\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\theta\quad\text{in}\quad\left\{y=\eta,|x|\leq 1/2\right\}.

Proof B.6
.

Up to considering
u(x,y)-\varphi(x)
and relabeling
\delta
, we can assume that
u
satisfies

\begin{cases}\Delta u=g(x)&\text{in }B_{1}^{\prime}\times(0,1),\\
|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta&\text{in }\{y=0\},\\
\partial_{y}u=\sign(u)f(|u|)&\text{in }\{y=0,u\neq 0\}\end{cases}

where

|g|\leq\delta,\qquad u\leq\delta\quad\text{in }B_{1}^{\prime},\qquad|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta+\delta y\quad\text{in }B_{1}^{\prime}\times(0,1).

Given
M\geq 1
we define

V(x,y)\coloneqq u(x,y)-M\delta|x-\hat{z}|^{2}+(dM+1/2)\delta y^{2}-(\theta-\delta)y,

and for
0<\eta\leq 1/100
we set
\Gamma\coloneqq B_{1}^{\prime}\times[0,\eta]
. Since

\Delta V=\Delta u+\delta\geq 0\quad\text{in }\Gamma,

by the maximum principle there is
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\partial\Gamma
such that

(31)

V(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\max_{\partial\Gamma}V=\max_{\Gamma}V\geq V(\hat{z},0)=u(\hat{z},0)>0.

Provided
M
is large enough and
\delta,\eta
are small enough, we show that

(32)

\bar{y}=\eta\quad\text{and}\quad|\bar{x}|\leq 1/2.

We argue by contradiction, splitting the proof in two cases.

\bullet

\bar{y}=0
. In this case, by (
31
), we have

0<V(\bar{x},0)=u(\bar{x},0)-M\delta|\bar{x}-\hat{z}|^{2}\leq u(\bar{x},0),

which implies

\partial_{y}u(\bar{x},0)=f(u(\bar{x},0))\geq\theta-\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\sup_{B_{1}^{\prime}}u\geq\theta-\delta^{2}.

On the other hand,

0\geq\partial_{y}V(\bar{x},0)=\partial_{y}u(\bar{x},0)-\theta+\delta,

so we reach a contradiction for
\delta
small enough.

\bullet

|\bar{x}|\geq 1/2
.
Since

0<V(\bar{x},\bar{y})=u(\bar{x},\bar{y})-M\delta|\bar{x}-\hat{z}|^{2}+(dM+1/2)\delta\bar{y}^{2}-(\theta-\delta)\bar{y},

recalling that
M\geq 1,|\hat{z}|\leq 1/100
, and
\bar{y}\leq\eta
, we have

u(\bar{x},\bar{y})>M\delta|\bar{x}-\hat{z}|^{2}-(dM+1/2)\delta\bar{y}^{2}+(\theta-\delta)\bar{y}\\
>(\tfrac{M}{9}-(dM+1/2)\eta^{2}-\eta)\delta+\theta\bar{y}\geq\tfrac{M}{10}\delta+\theta\bar{y}

provided
\eta
is small enough.
On the other hand, since
|\partial_{y}u|\leq\theta+\delta y
and
u\leq\delta
in
\{y=0\}\cap B_{1}
, we get

u(\bar{x},\bar{y})\leq u(\bar{x},0)+\theta\bar{y}+\tfrac{\delta}{2}\bar{y}^{2}\leq(1+\eta^{2}/2)\delta+\theta\bar{y},

which is impossible for
M
large enough.
Hence, we proved that (
32
) holds.

Now, we deduce from (
31
) and (
32
) that

0\leq\partial_{y}V(\bar{x},\eta)=\partial_{y}u(\bar{x},\eta)+(2dM+1)\delta\eta-(\theta-\delta),

therefore

(33)

\theta-\partial_{y}u(\bar{x},\eta)\leq C\delta.

By assumption the function
\theta+2\delta\eta-\partial_{y}u
is harmonic and positive in
B_{1}^{\prime}\times(0,2\eta)
. Thus, by Harnack inequality, there exists
C>0
such that, setting
\Gamma^{\prime}=B_{1/2}^{\prime}\times[\eta/2,3\eta/2]
, we have

\sup_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\big(\theta+2\delta\eta-\partial_{y}u\big)\leq C\inf_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\big(\theta+2\delta\eta-\partial_{y}u\big).

Since
(\bar{x},\eta)\in\Gamma^{\prime}
by (
32
), using (
33
) we have

\sup_{\Gamma^{\prime}}\big(\theta+2\delta\eta-\partial_{y}u\big)\leq C\delta,

therefore

\partial_{y}u\geq\theta-C\delta\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\theta\quad\text{ in }\big\{y=\eta,|x|\leq 1/2\big\}

provided
\delta
is small enough, as we wanted.

Proof B.7

(Proof of Theorem
B
)
.

We argue by contradiction. Assume there are solutions
u_{k}
with
u_{k}(0)=\varphi_{k}(0)
and satisfying

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}^{+})}+\|\varphi_{k}\|_{C^{1,1}}+\|f_{k}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq C,

but such that there are
\rho_{k}\to 0
and
x_{k},y_{k}\in B_{\rho_{k}}^{\prime}
with
u_{k}(x_{k},0)>\varphi_{k}(x_{k})
and
u_{k}(y_{k},0)<\varphi_{k}(y_{k})
.
Up to considering

Set

for

. We show that

(34)

for some modulus of continuity
\omega
independent of
k
. Indeed, by Lemma

there exists
C>0
independent from

such that
\|\nabla u_{k,r}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2r}^{+})}\leq C
.
It follows that, up to a subsequence, if

Thus, choosing first

Appendix C
Generic Regularity for a Two-phase Thin Obstacle

Let
\theta>0
be a fixed parameter. We will consider differentiable obstacles
\varphi
on
[-1,1]
satisfying the analog of (
4
), namely

(35)

|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\leq C\big(|\log(x+1)|+|\log(1-x)|\big).

We want to show a generic regularity result for (
18
).
To this aim, we assume
K=[-1,1]
and we consider solutions in
B_{2}
of

(36)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=0&\text{in }B_{2}\setminus\{x_{2}=0\},\\
\partial_{2}u=\theta&\text{in }\{x_{2}=0,|x_{1}|>1\},\\

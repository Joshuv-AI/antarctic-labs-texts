Perfectoid Spaces in Multivariate p p -adic Hodge Theory
Aprameyo Pal, Rohit Pokhrel

Abstract
. Perfectoid spaces have become a crucial tool in p p -adic geometry, serving as a bridge between adic spaces in characteristic 0 0 and those in characteristic p p . In this article, we develop a systematic way to study the structure of perfectoid spaces within the setting of multivariate p p -adic Hodge theory over a variant of the rings introduced in [ BCM24 ] .

1.1.
Overview

The theory of Multivariate
p
-adic Hodge Theory was initiated by a series of works by Gergely Zábrádi
[
Z1́8a
,
Z1́8b
]
. He developed multivariate
(\phi_{\Delta},\Gamma_{\Delta})
-modules, extending Fontaine’s
(\phi,\Gamma)
-modules, to study
p
-adic representations of
G_{K,\Delta}=G_{K}\times\cdots\times G_{K}
, which is the
t
-fold direct product of the absolute Galois group
G_{K}
of a
p
-adic field
K
(here
t=|\Delta|
). While the direct product operation on the Galois group side seems harmless, the corresponding object on Fontaine’s side isn’t straightforward due to the involvement of tensor products. Tensor products of representations are typically chaotic and are frequently studied in the Langlands program. Possible applications of this theory are discussed in
[
PZ21
,
CKZ21
]
.

Furthermore, the objects involved in this theory appear intrinsically interesting and possess a multivariate flavor. One example hinting at multivariate objects is the work by Brinon, Chiarellotto, and Mazzari
[
BCM24
]
, where they developed classical objects (e.g., the period rings
\Bdr
and
\BHT
) previously studied in Fontaine’s classical work
[
FO07
]
. Continuing this, Poyeton and Vanni
[
PV25
]
defined other period rings,
\Bcris
and
\Bst
.

In this article, we’ll provide a cleaner way to study these objects. This approach makes our tools closely related to the classical theory, yet it also proves that the multivariate theory is fundamentally different because its objects are distinct. Moreover, this article will be a first step towards exploring the geometric side of multivariate
p
-adic Hodge Theory, which provides the core motivation for studying it in the classical setup.

This geometric theory does not build upon Scholze’s theory of diamonds as in
[
CKZ21
]
, but rather constructs the objects of interest within the multivariate framework itself.

Main Results

Let

A perfectoid field is a complete topological field

.

Let

be perfectoid fields over

such that

\hat{\bullet}

\displaystyle K_{\Delta}=K_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}K_{t}.

Here

denotes the completed tensor product given in
[
BGR84
]
.
It turns out that
K_{\Delta}
is highly analogous to a perfectoid field in many properties. Specifically, it is a reduced ring of dimension zero whose localizations are all perfectoid fields, meaning every element is either a unit or a zero divisor. However, this ring is very different from a field in the sense that, excluding trivial cases, it has zero divisors and is not Noetherian. Because of its non-Noetherian nature, the adic space over
K_{\Delta}
is very chaotic, and some techniques from classical theory
[
Hub96
]
developed by Huber do not seem to hold in general. Thanks to the recent development by many people, this kind of problem is now taken care.

The ring
K_{\Delta}
is not strongly Noetherian; that is,
K_{\Delta}\left<T_{1},\cdots,T_{n}\right>
is not Noetherian for some
n
(in fact, for
n=0
). However, we can conduct a thorough study of this ring by developing functional analysis over
K_{\Delta}
. In this regard, we define Banach spaces over
K_{\Delta}
and prove an open mapping theorem for them. We believe most properties of Banach spaces can be translated to this category, but we are not pursuing that in this article to keep its length reasonable. Some important properties that a Tate algebra over a non-Archimedean field possesses are the division algorithm, the Weierstrass preparation theorem, and the fact that every ideal is closed. Yet, we do not know any analogue of these properties in this setup
[
BGR84
,
Bos14
]
. Consequently, we do not know whether the quotient of a Tate algebra possesses a Banach algebra structure in a multivariate context. Therefore, we only consider the case where the quotient has a Banach algebra structure in Theorem
7.10
; in other results, this assumption can be dropped.

The inspiration for this article is an analogous figure to one given in Chapter 7 of
[
SW20
]
for a perfectoid field
K

K^{\circ}_{\Delta}

\spec K^{\circ}/\varpi
\spec K^{\circ}
\spec K^{\circ\flat}

This diagram can be summarized by the following chain of equivalences:

\displaystyle K_{\fet}\cong K^{\circ a}_{\fet}\cong(K^{\circ a}/\varpi)_{\fet}=(K^{\flat\circ a}/\varpi^{\flat})_{\fet}\cong K^{\flat\circ a}_{\fet}\cong K_{\fet}.

In words, this chain of equivalences states that the étale site corresponds to the étale site on the almost integral side, in the sense of Faltings’ almost mathematics, which in turn corresponds to the almost étale site of its special point, which is equal to that of the tilted side. This can then be lifted back to obtain the étale site of the tilted almost integral level, which corresponds to the étale site of the tilted perfectoid field.

We also prove the analogous result for

The categories

which is not possible in mixed characteristics.

Case-I :

\ker\phi_{\alpha}=(0),\forall\alpha\in\Delta:
In this case
\phi_{\alpha}
induces a map

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}:K_{\alpha}\to\Frac(\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}/\mathfrak{p})

for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. The image generates the compositum of some conjugates of
K_{\alpha}
as in the previous proposition, giving an embedding family in this case.

Case-II :

\ker\phi_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{m}_{K_{\alpha}},\forall\alpha\in\Delta
: Then
\phi_{\alpha}
induces an injective maps

\displaystyle k_{\alpha}\to\Frac(\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}/\mathfrak{p})

for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. The image generates the composites of some conjugates of
k_{\alpha}
, giving a residual embedding family. But this residual embedding family comes from an embedding family. Injectivity is similar to the previous proposition. This proves the theorem.
∎

We will now introduce a new semi-norm in
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
which behaves a little better with the multiplication as compared to
|\bullet|_{\Delta}
. Define

\displaystyle|x|=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}|\beta_{s}(x)|

where the norm
|\beta_{s}(x)|
is taken in
K_{s}
. We always assume that all norms are normalized.

\displaystyle|x+y|

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(x+y)|\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(x)+\beta_{s}(y)|\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\Big\{\max\{|\beta_{s}(x)|,|\beta_{s}(y)|\}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\Big\{\sup\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(x)|\},\sup\limits_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(y)|\}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\max\{|x|,|y|\}

Thus
|x+y|\leq\max\{|x|,|y|\}
for every
x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
. Also,

\displaystyle|xy|

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(xy)|\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(x)||\beta_{s}(y)|\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(x)|\}\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\{|\beta_{s}(y)|\}

\displaystyle=|x||y|.

But if for
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
we have
|x|=0
, then
x
is in the nilradical of
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
, i.e.,
x^{n}=0
. Thus, in order to prove that this is a seminorm, we have to show that
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
is reduced.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose
K_{1},\cdots,K_{t}
are algebraic extensions of an arbitrary non-Archimedean field
K_{0}
. Then
K_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}K_{n}
is a reduced ring iff they are linearly disjoint over
K_{0}
or for every
1\leq i<j\leq t
,
K_{i}\cap K_{j}/K_{0}
is a seperable extension.

Proof.

If
K_{1},\dots,K_{t}
are linearly disjoint over
K_{0}
, then we have
K_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{K_{0}}K_{t}\cong\widehat{K_{1}\cdots K_{t}}
, which is therefore reduced. Suppose
K_{i}\cap K_{j}
is not separable over
K_{0}
; then there exists an element
x\in K_{i}\cap K_{j}\setminus K_{0}
such that
x^{p^{k}}\in K_{0}
. Then,

\displaystyle y=1\otimes\cdots\otimes x\otimes\cdots\otimes 1-1\otimes\cdots\otimes x\otimes\cdots\otimes 1

where the first term has
x
in the
i
-th place and the second term has
x
in the
j
-th place. If
\Char K_{0}=p
, then
y^{p^{k}}=0
, and hence
K_{\Delta}
is not reduced. Therefore, if
K_{\Delta}
is reduced, then either
K_{1},\dots,K_{n}
are linearly disjoint over
K_{0}
or
K_{i}\cap K_{j}/K_{0}
is separable for every
1\leq i<j\leq t
. Conversely, suppose for every
1\leq i<j\leq t
,
K_{i}\cap K_{j}/K
is separable. Then by Bourbaki Algebra-II
[
Bou03
]
(Chapter 5, §15), we have that
K_{1}\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}K_{n}
is reduced. We will now show that
K_{\Delta}
is reduced. Suppose
x=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{it}
is a nilpotent element of
K_{\Delta}
. Since for each
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
the number of terms
y_{i}:=x_{i1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{in}
such that
|y_{i}|\geq r
is finite, we can rearrange and write
x=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{i_{1}-1}y_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}y_{i}+\cdots
where for
i_{k}\leq i<i_{k+1}
we have
|y_{i}|=r_{k}
with
r_{1}>r_{2}>\cdots
. Then
\beta_{s}(x)=0
for every
s\in\mathfrak{S}
, and thus by the continuity of
\beta_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{s}(x)=\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{i_{1}-1}\beta_{s}(y_{i})\right)+\left(\sum\limits_{i=i_{1}}^{i_{2}-1}\beta_{s}(y_{i})\right)+\cdots=0.

Then
|\beta_{s}(y_{i})|=r_{k}
whenever
i_{k}\leq i<i_{k+1}
. But since
r_{1}>r_{2}>\cdots
, we must have
\sum\limits_{i=i_{k}}^{i_{k+1}-1}\beta_{s}(y_{i})=0
for every
k
. Thus, each of these terms is nilpotent in
K\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}K_{n}
, which are all therefore zero since the ring
K_{1}\otimes_{K_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{K_{0}}K_{t}
is reduced.
∎

Finally, we can conclude that the map
|\bullet|:K_{\Delta}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
defines a seminorm on
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
made from perfectoid fields
K_{\alpha},\alpha\in\Delta
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

For every
x\in\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
, we have
|x^{n}|=|x|^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose
|x|=r
, then for each
\epsilon>0
, there exists
s\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
r-\epsilon<|\beta_{s}(x)|\leq r
. Then
(r-\epsilon)^{n}<|\beta_{s}(x)|^{n}=|(\beta_{s}(x))^{n}|=|\beta_{s}(x^{n})|\leq r^{n}
. But
(r-\epsilon)^{n}=r^{n}+\epsilon*
, and as
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we get
|x^{n}|=\sup\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}|\beta_{s}(x^{n})|=r^{n}=|x|^{n}
, which completes the proof.
∎

We note that the semi-norm
|\bullet|
can be extended to
K_{\Delta}=\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}\left[\frac{1}{\varpi_{0}}\right]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

The metric
|\bullet|_{\Delta}
and
|\bullet|
on
K_{\Delta}
.

Proof.

Let
\sum x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{t}
be a representative of
x
in
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
. Then for
s=(s_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Delta}\in\mathfrak{S}
, we get

\displaystyle|\beta_{s}(x)|=\left|\sum x^{s_{1}}_{1}\cdots x^{s_{t}}_{t}\right|\leq\max\{|x_{1}|\cdots|x_{t}|\}.

But the left-hand side of the above expression is independent of the chosen representative, and hence we get
|x|\leq|x|_{\Delta}
. Thus, the identity map

\displaystyle\id:(K_{\Delta},|\bullet|_{\Delta})\to(K_{\Delta},|\bullet|)

is continuous. We claim that
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}=\{x\in K_{\Delta}\mid|x|\leq 1\}
. Clearly,
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}\subset\{x\in K_{\Delta}\mid|x|\leq 1\}
. Now, take
x\in K_{\Delta}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
and suppose
n\in\mathbb{N}
is such that
\varpi^{n}_{0}x\in\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
but
\varpi^{n-1}_{0}x\not\in\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
, where
\varpi_{0}
is the uniformizer of
K_{0}
. Then for each
s\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have
|\beta_{s}(\varpi_{0})|^{n}|\beta_{s}(x)|\leq 1
but
|\beta_{s}(\varpi_{0})|^{n-1}|\beta_{s}(x)|>1
as
\varpi_{0}=\varpi_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes 1=\cdots=1\otimes\cdots\otimes\varpi_{0}
. Therefore,
|x|\geq|\beta_{s}(x)|>1
, proving the claim. Since, both topological rings
(K_{\Delta},|\bullet|_{\Delta})
and
(K_{\Delta},|\bullet|)
are Tate, their topologies must be
\varpi_{0}
-adic, which proves the isomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

From now onwards, we will use the seminorm
|\bullet|
as it behaves well with multiplication and is also they are closely related to the adic spectrum of
\spa(K_{\Delta},K^{+}_{\Delta})
, which we will use later.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
is the ring
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
of power bounded elements in
K_{\Delta}
, c.f.
[
Wed19
]
. For simplicity, from now onwards we will denote
\mathcal{O}_{K,\Delta}
by
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
.

Definition 2.10
.

For
I_{\varpi}
the pseudouniformizing ideal of
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
defined by the pseudo-uniformizers
\varpi_{\alpha}
of
K_{\alpha}
satisfying
|p|\leq|\varpi_{\alpha}|<1

(1\leq\alpha\leq t)
, we define the tilt of
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
as

\displaystyle K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}=\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}/I_{\varpi}:=\{(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\mid x^{p}_{n+1}=x_{n}\}.

This is a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with defining ring homorphism
t\mapsto(tx_{0},tx_{1},\cdots)
from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
to
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
x\equiv y\mod I^{n}_{\varpi}
, then
x^{p}\equiv y^{p}\mod I^{n+1}_{\varpi}
.

Proof.

We have
x=y+a
for some
a\in I^{n}_{\varpi}
, then
x^{p}=y^{p}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}{p\choose i}y^{i}a^{p-1}\equiv y^{p}\mod I^{n+1}_{\varpi}
because
p\in I_{\varpi}
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

There is a multiplicative bijection

\displaystyle K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}\to\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\to\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\mapsto(x^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

with
x^{(n)}:=\varprojlim\limits_{m\to\infty}\tilde{x}^{p^{m}}_{n+m}
where
\tilde{x}_{n+m}
is the lift of
x_{n+m}
inside
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
. Let
\tilde{x^{\prime}}_{n+m}
be another lifts, then
\tilde{x^{\prime}}_{n+m}\equiv\tilde{x}_{n+m}\mod I_{\varpi}
, then
\tilde{x^{\prime}}^{p^{m}}_{n+m}\equiv\tilde{x}^{p^{m}}_{n+m}\mod I^{m}_{\varpi}
for all
m
. Thus
x^{(n)}
is independent of the lifts and satisfies the compatibility conditions.
∎

Transporting the structure, we can define the ring structure on
\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
via the following formulas:

\displaystyle(x+y)^{(n)}

\displaystyle:=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}(x^{(n+m)}+y^{(n+m)})^{p^{m}}

\displaystyle(xy)^{(n)}

\displaystyle:=x^{(n)}y^{(n)}.

Thus we denote
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
to be the ring
\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
. We have a natural multiplicative map
\#:K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}\to K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
defined as
x=(x^{(n)})\mapsto x^{\#}=x^{(0)}
.

Define the seminorm
|\bullet|_{\flat}
on
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
by the formula

\displaystyle|x|_{\flat}:=|x^{\#}|.

We want to show the following

\displaystyle|x+y|_{\flat}\leq\max\{|x|_{\flat},|y|_{\flat}\}

for all
x,y\in K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
. To see this

\displaystyle|x+y|_{\flat}

\displaystyle=|\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}(x^{(m)}+y^{(m)})^{p^{m}}|

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}|x^{(m)}+y^{(m)}|^{p^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\max\{|x^{(m)}|^{p^{m}},|y^{(m)}|^{p^{m}}\}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\max\{|(x^{(m)})^{p^{m}}|,|(y^{(m)})^{p^{m}}|\}

\displaystyle=\max\{|x^{\#}|,|y^{\#}|\}

\displaystyle=\max\{|x|_{\flat},|y|_{\flat}\}.

and

\displaystyle|xy|_{\flat}=|x^{\#}y^{\#}|\leq|x^{\#}||y^{\#}|=|x|_{\flat}|y|_{\flat}

Therefore,
|\bullet|
is a metric on
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
. From the power multiplicativity of
|\bullet|
, it is easy to check that
|\bullet|_{\flat}
is power multiplicative. Indeed, for
x\in K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
we have
|x^{n}|_{\flat}=|(x^{n})^{\#}|=|(x^{\#})^{n}|=|x^{\#}|^{n}=|x|^{n}_{\flat}
.

Proposition 2.13
.

The ring
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
is reduced.

Proof.

If
x=(x^{(m)})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is nilpotent then
x^{n}=0
for some
x\in K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
. Then
(x^{\#})^{n}=0
and hence
x^{(0)}=x^{\#}=0
. Then
(x^{(1)})^{p}=x^{(0)}=0
in
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
, proving
x^{(1)}=0
. Therefore by induction
x^{(m)}=0
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and hence
x=0
, proving
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
is reduced.
∎

Let
I_{\varpi}
be a pseudouniformizing ideal defined by
\varpi_{\alpha},\alpha\in\Delta
, then we consider
\varpi^{\flat}_{\alpha}\in K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
such that
\varpi^{\flat}_{\alpha}\equiv\varpi_{\alpha}\mod I_{\varpi}
. Consider
I^{\flat}_{\varpi}
be the ideal generated by
\varpi^{\flat}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
and call this also the pseudouniformizing ideal of
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
.

.

K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
is complete with respect to the semi-norm
|\bullet|_{\flat}

Proof.

We will first prove that the completion of
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}

|\bullet|_{\flat}

I^{\flat}_{\varpi}-

I^{\flat}_{\varpi}

K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}

|\bullet|_{\flat}

\displaystyle K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\to\varprojlim\limits_{m}K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}/(I^{\flat}_{\varpi})^{m}.

\cap(I^{\flat}_{\varpi})^{m}

I^{\flat}_{\varpi}

x^{\#}\in\cap I^{m}_{\varpi}=\{0\}

x^{\#}=x^{(0)}=0

x^{(n)}=0

(x^{(n+1)})^{p}=x^{(n)}=0

x^{(n)}=0

K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
with respect to
|\bullet|_{\flat}
which converges to an element
x
in
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
. By construction we have
x-x_{n}\in(I^{\flat}_{\varpi})^{n}
and hence
x
maps to
\{x_{n}\}
along the above map. Conversely suppose
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
is
I^{\flat}_{\varpi}-
adically complete for a pseudouniformizing ideal
I^{\flat}_{\varpi}
. Note that for every
\alpha\in\Delta
we have
|\varpi^{\flat}_{\alpha}|>|\varpi^{\flat}_{\alpha}|^{2}>\cdots
decreases to
0
, we can assume
x_{m+n}\equiv x_{m}\mod(I^{\flat}_{\varpi})^{m}
and hence their images are an element of the inverse limit. But from isomorphism, there exists an element
x\in K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
such that
x\equiv x_{m}\mod(I^{\flat}_{\varpi})^{m}
. Therefore
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}x_{n}=x
.

Using the above, we can choose any
\varpi_{\alpha}\in K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
and hence we choose
\varpi_{\alpha}=p
for all
\alpha\in\Delta

is the ideal
(p)

in

.

Lemma 2.15
.

Consider an inverse system of rings
(f_{i}:A_{i}\to A_{i-1})_{i\geq 1}
and for each
i\geq 0
,
I_{i}\subset A_{i}
is an ideal such that the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{1}}

commutes. Then:

(i).

I:=\varprojlim\limits I_{i}
is an ideal of
A:=\varprojlim A_{i}
.

(ii).

If
J
is an ideal of
A
, then
J=\varprojlim I_{i}
, for some inverse system of ideals such that the above diagram commutes.

(iii).

Prime ideals correspond to an inverse system of prime ideals.

Proof.

(i). Let
(x_{i})\in I
and
(\lambda_{i})\in A
be arbitrary elements. Then we want to show
(\lambda_{i}x_{i})\in I
. Since
\lambda_{i}x_{i}\in I_{i}
, it is enough to show
f_{i}(\lambda_{i}x_{i})=f_{i}(\lambda_{i})f_{i}(x_{i})\in I_{i-1}
which is immediate. Thus
I
is an ideal of
A
.

(ii). If
J
is an ideal of
A
, then the projection maps
\pr_{i}:A\to A_{i}
takes
J
to an ideal
J_{i}
for each
i\geq 0
. Also we have
f_{i+1}(J_{i+1})\subset J_{i}
. Indeed,
\pr_{i+1}(y)=\pr_{i}(y)
for each
y\in J
and every
i\geq 0
. Therefore,
(f_{i+1}:J_{i+1}\to J_{i})_{i\geq 0}
is an inverse system such that
J\cong\varprojlim J_{i}
.

(iii). Let
\mathfrak{p}=\varprojlim\mathfrak{p}_{i}
be a prime ideal of
A
. Since
\pr_{i}
is surjective, its image
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is also a prime ideal. Conversely, suppose each
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is prime ideal and take
x=(x_{i}),y=(y_{i})\in A
be such that
x,y\in\mathfrak{p}
. Then for each
i\geq 1
we have
x_{i}y_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is a prime ideal, either
x_{i}
or
y_{i}
belongs to
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. If
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
then
x_{i-1}=f_{i}(x_{i})\in\mathfrak{p}_{i-1}
and similarly for
y_{i}
. Thus for arbitrary large
N
, we have either
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
or
y_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for all
i\leq N
. Now the lemma follows since if
x\notin\mathfrak{p}
, there exists
n
such that
x_{i}\notin\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for all
i\geq n
, but then
y_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for all
i\geq n
and hence for all
i
. This proves
y\in\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Corollary 2.17
.

Every pseudo-uniformizing ideal
I_{\varpi}
( resp.
I^{\flat}_{\varpi}
) is principal.

Proof.

First, suppose
I_{\varpi}
is generated by
\varpi_{1}=\varpi\otimes 1\otimes\cdots\otimes 1,\ldots,\varpi_{t}=1\otimes 1\otimes\cdots\otimes\varpi
. We want to show that for every
i\neq j
,
\varpi_{i}
and
\varpi_{j}
differ by a unit in
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
. Indeed, for every
s\in\mathfrak{S}
,
\left|\frac{\varpi_{i}}{\varpi_{j}}\right|_{s}=\left|\frac{\beta_{s}(\varpi)}{\beta_{s}(\varpi)}\right|=1
. Thus, this element does not lie in
\mathfrak{m}_{s}
for any
s\in\mathfrak{S}
, making it a unit. Therefore,
\varpi_{i}=u\varpi_{j}
for some
u\in(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})^{\times}
. For
I_{\varpi}
constructed for arbitrary
\varpi_{\alpha}
, we can replace it with any one of the above, which reduces it to the case of valuation rings. A similar proof works for
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
.
∎

From now on we will write
I_{\varpi}=(\varpi)
for a pseudo-uniformizer
\varpi
and
I^{\flat}_{\varpi}=(\varpi^{\flat})
.

Definition 2.19
.

Remark 2.20
.

The term field may be misleading, as the ring
K_{\Delta}
is not, in general, a field. In fact, it is not even an integral domain and may contain zero divisors. However, it is worth noting that
K_{\Delta}
is reduced and has Krull dimension 0. As a result, every element of
K_{\Delta}
is either a unit or a zero divisor, making it a total ring of fractions of
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
. We use the term field here because we are replacing a perfectoid field with
K_{\Delta}
, and the translation of classical results carries over smoothly in this context

3.
Objects in equicharacteristics setups

In this section, we compare
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
with the rings
K^{\circ\flat}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta
. Note that the approach from Section §
2
does not apply here. In particular,
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
is not isomorphic to the completed tensor product
K^{\circ\flat}_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{K^{\circ\flat}_{0}}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}_{K^{\circ\flat}_{0}}K^{\circ\flat}_{t}
for any base field
K^{\flat}_{0}
such that
K^{\flat}_{0}\subset K^{\flat}_{\alpha}\subset\overline{K^{\flat}_{0}}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. This is because, in general, the latter ring is not reduced.

Definition 3.1
.

A
perfectoid
\Delta
-field
of equicharacteristic is defined as a ring
K^{\flat}_{\Delta}
that is isomorphic to the total ring of fractions of
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
, as constructed above. A
perfectoid
\Delta
-field
is a ring that is either a perfectoid
\Delta
-field of mixed characteristic or one of equicharacteristic.

Note that
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}\left[\frac{1}{\varpi^{\flat}}\right]=K^{\flat}_{\Delta}
for the uniformizer
\varpi
of
K_{0}
. Throughout the article, we will denote
\varpi
to denote
\varpi^{\flat}
and
I_{\varpi}
for
I^{\flat}_{\varpi}
, also for the perfectoid
\Delta-
field of mixed characteristics to be consistent with the notations. Also we take
\varpi
so that
(\varpi^{\flat})^{\#}=\varpi
so that all the
p-
th root exists. It can be easily checked that
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
is the set of power-bounded elements of
K^{\flat}_{\Delta}
. Hence we also write
K^{\flat\circ}_{\Delta}
for
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There is a one to one correspondence between
\mathfrak{S}
and
\spec K_{\Delta}

Proof.

Since
K^{\flat}_{\Delta}
is the total ring of quotients of
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
, this is the localization with respect to the multiplicative set of non-zero divisors. Therefore, the prime ideals of this localization are the localization of prime ideals of
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
which are disjoint from the multiplicative subset. Thus, the relevant prime ideal must have all its elements zero divisors which are
\mathfrak{p}_{s}
for
s\in\mathfrak{S}
and hence we get for every
\mathfrak{P}_{s}
for every
s\in\mathfrak{S}
with residue field
K^{\flat}_{s}
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a isomorphism between topological rings
K^{\flat}_{\Delta}
and
\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K_{\Delta}

Proof.

Note that we have an injective ring homomorphism

\displaystyle K^{\flat\circ}_{\Delta}=\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}\to\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K_{\Delta}

This gives an injective ring map to the total ring of fractions

\displaystyle K^{\flat}_{\Delta}\to Q(\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K_{\Delta})

This map is clearly surjective as for the uniformizer
\varpi\in K_{0}
, we have a non-zero divisor
\varpi^{\prime}
made from compatible p-th roots of
\varpi
, then
\left(\varprojlim\limits_{\Phi}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}\right)\left[\frac{1}{\varpi}\right]=\varprojlim\limits_{\Phi}K^{\circ}_{\Delta}\left[\frac{1}{\varpi^{1/p^{m}}}\right]=\varprojlim\limits_{\Phi}K_{\Delta}
.
Thus, it is enough to show every element of
\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}K_{\Delta}
is either a unit or a zero divisor. From Lemma-
2.15
, we know that every prime ideal are of the form
\mathfrak{P}^{\flat}_{s}=\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}\mathfrak{P}_{s}
. This is also a reduced ring, and each prime ideal is maximal; every element is a unit or a zero divisor. Hence is the total ring of fractions.
∎

Let
K_{0}
be a p-adic field and
K_{\alpha},\alpha\in\Delta
be perfectoid fields such that
K_{0}\subset K_{\alpha}\subset\widehat{\overline{K}}_{0}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
. We have
\mathfrak{S}
as the equivalent class of the embedding family defined above. Tilting the usual tilting to the perfectoid fields
K_{\alpha},K_{s},\alpha\in\Delta,s\in\mathfrak{S}
, we obtain the analogous situation in characteristics
p
i.e.
K^{\flat}_{\alpha},K^{\flat}_{s}\subset\widehat{\overline{K^{\flat}}}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
and
s\in\mathfrak{S}
. Using the theorem of Fontaine-Wintenberger
[
FW79
]
, we have isomorphism
G_{K_{\alpha}}\cong G_{K^{\flat}_{\alpha}}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
and
G_{K_{s}}\cong G_{K^{\flat}_{s}}
for all
s\in\mathfrak{S}
. Consider a non-Archimedean field
K^{\flat}_{0}
of characteristic
p
such that
K^{\flat}_{0}\subset K^{\flat}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
and the set of equivalence classes of embedding families defined analogously for
K^{\flat}_{0}\subset K^{\flat}_{\alpha}\subset\widehat{\overline{K^{\flat}_{0}}}
is in bijection with
\mathfrak{S}
. Consider
A=K^{\flat}_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{K^{\flat}_{0}}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{K^{\flat}_{0}}K^{\flat}_{t}
and we have the following proposition;

Proposition 3.4
.

The ring
K^{\flat}_{\Delta}
is the reduced ring corresponding to
A
, that is

\displaystyle K^{\flat}_{\Delta}\cong A/\Nil A

where
\Nil A
is the nilradical of
A
.

Proof.

Consider the surjective ring homomorphism

\displaystyle\theta:A

\displaystyle\to K^{\flat}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}(x^{(n)}_{1i})_{n}\otimes\cdots\otimes(x^{(n)}_{ti})_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}(x^{(n)}_{1i}\otimes\cdots\otimes x^{(n)}_{ti})_{n}

and by assumption all prime ideals of
A
are
\mathfrak{Q}_{s}=\ker\gamma_{s}
with embeddings
(\gamma_{\alpha}:K^{\flat}_{\alpha}\to K_{s})_{\alpha\in\Delta}
for
s\in\mathfrak{S}
. We can define the bijection such that
\theta^{-1}(\mathfrak{P}_{s})=\mathfrak{Q}_{s}
because the inverse image of a prime ideal is a prime ideal. Let
x\in\ker\theta
, we have
\theta(x)=0\in\bigcap\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{P}_{s}
. Thus

\displaystyle x\in\theta^{-1}\left(\bigcap\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{P}_{s}\right)=\bigcap\limits_{s\in\mathfrak{S}}\mathfrak{Q}_{s}.

But the intersection of all prime ideals is the nilradical of
A
, and hence we get the desired isomorphism.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

A similar result appears in Proposition 4.3 of
[
BCM24
]
, where the author considers the
p
-adic completion of the ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}\otimes_{W(k_{0})}\cdots\otimes_{W(k_{0})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}
and the
I^{\flat}_{p}
-completion of
\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}\otimes_{k_{0}}\cdots\otimes_{k_{0}}\mathcal{O}^{\flat}_{\mathbb{C}_{p}}

in this article. The explicit structure of the Tilt will not play a role in this article.

The following example shows that tilts of
\Delta
-perfectoid fields are not immediately recoverable from tilts of the individual perfectoid fields. This means that the corresponding Fargues-Fontaine curves, which parametrize the untilts, are essentially different.

Example 3.6
.

Consider
K_{1}=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})}
and
K_{2}=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})}
(
p\neq 2
), both of which are perfectoid fields over
K_{0}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Take
K_{\Delta}=K_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}K_{2}=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}},\mu_{p^{\infty}})}
, as they are linearly disjoint. In contrast, consider
K^{\prime}_{\Delta}=K_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}K_{1}=\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\widehat{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(p^{1/p^{\infty}})}
, which contains zero divisors such as
p^{1/p}\otimes 1-1\otimes p^{1/p}
.

However, note that both
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
have isomorphic tilts:
K_{1}^{\flat}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right]
(by identifying
(p,p^{1/p},p^{1/p^{2}},\dots)
with
t_{1}
) and
K_{2}^{\flat}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{2}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right]
(by identifying
(1,\zeta_{p},\zeta_{p^{2}},\dots)-1
with
t_{2}
).

Therefore, the tilts of perfectoid
\Delta-
fields and perfectoid fields are essentially different. If we want to prove that
K_{\Delta}^{\flat}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{2}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right]
, as in
[
BCM24
]
, then we need to see
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
inside some bigger field.

Note that since
K_{\Delta}
is a field, a map exists from

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right]\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{2}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right]=\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t_{1}^{1/p^{\infty}},t_{2}^{1/p^{\infty}}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{1}},\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right]\to K_{\Delta}^{\flat}.

This ring is considered in

, but it cannot be the tilt because it has rank-2 valuations (if

and

are not contained inside a perfectoid field), which is not possible for a perfectoid field. In the latter case, we have a surjective map

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t^{1/p^{\infty}}_{1}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{1}}\right]\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathbb{F}_{p}[[t^{1/p^{\infty}}_{2}]]\left[\frac{1}{t_{2}}\right]\to K^{\prime\flat}_{\Delta}.

4.
Non-Archimedean functional analysis over
K_{\Delta}

Throughout the section, we fix a perfectoid
\Delta-
field
K_{\Delta}
. We will recall the following definition from the classical ring theory
[
Goo91
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A commutative ring
R
with 1 is called a
von Neumann regular (VNR)
ring if for every
x\in R
there exists
y\in R
such that
x^{2}y=x
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a commutative ring
R
with
1
:

(i).

R
is VNR;

(ii).

Every principal ideal of
R
is generated by an idempotent element;

(iii).

Every finitely generated ideal of
R
is generated by an idempotent;

(iv).

Every principal ideal is a direct summand of
R
;

(v).

Every finitely generated submodule of a projective
R-
module
P
is a direct summand of
P
;

(vi).

R
is absolutely flat, i.e., every module over
R
is flat;

(vii).

R
is a reduced ring of dimension
0
;

(viii).

Every localization of
R
at a maximal ideal is a field.

Proof.

See
[
Goo91
]
.
∎

Since the ring
K_{\Delta}
is a reduced ring of dimension
0
, it is a VNR ring. Therefore, every module over
K_{\Delta}
is a flat module.

Lemma 4.3
.

For every
s\in\mathfrak{S}
, the localization
(K_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{P}_{s}}
is isomorphic to
K_{s}
.

Proof.

For each
s\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have a well defined map

\displaystyle(K_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{P}_{s}}

\displaystyle\to K_{s}

\displaystyle\frac{a}{b}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{\beta_{s}(a)}{\beta_{s}(b)}

Indeed, if
b\not\in\mathfrak{P}_{s}
, then
\beta_{s}(b)\neq 0
and hence above map is well defined. It is also surjective. Since the ring
K_{\Delta}
is VNR, this map is also injective as
(K_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{P}_{s}}
is a field.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The localizations
(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}
is isomorphic to
K_{s}
for all
s\in\mathfrak{S}
and
(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{m}_{s}}
is isomorphic to
K^{\circ}_{s}
for all
s\in\mathfrak{K}
.

Proof.

For
s\in\mathfrak{S}
, consider the well-defined map:

\displaystyle\phi_{s}:(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}

\displaystyle\to K_{s}

\displaystyle\frac{a}{b}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{\beta_{s}(a)}{\beta_{s}(b)}

Note that
\ker\phi_{s}=\Big\{\frac{a}{b}\in(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}\mid\beta_{s}(a)=0\Big\}=\Big\{\frac{a}{b}\mid a\in\mathfrak{p}_{s}\text{ and }b\not\in\mathfrak{p}_{s}\Big\}=(\mathfrak{p}_{s})_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}
. However, since
\mathfrak{p}_{t}=\mathfrak{P}_{t}\cap K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
for all
t\in\mathfrak{S}
, we have
(\mathfrak{p}_{t})_{\mathfrak{p}_{t}}=0
. Hence, the above homomorphism is injective. It is also surjective, and therefore an isomorphism.

Now, for
(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{m}_{s}}
, consider the homomorphism:

\displaystyle\Phi_{s}:(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{m}_{s}}

\displaystyle\to K^{\circ}_{s}

\displaystyle\frac{a}{b}

\displaystyle\mapsto\frac{\beta_{s}(a)}{\beta_{s}(b)}

This map is clearly surjective. Let
\frac{a}{b}\in\ker\Phi_{s}
. Then
a\in\ker\beta_{s}=\mathfrak{p}_{s}
, which implies
a
is a zero divisor. There exists
x\not\in\mathfrak{p}_{s}
such that
ax=0
, as this is precisely what the statement
(\mathfrak{p}_{s})_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}=0
translates to. Therefore,
\ker\Phi_{s}=0
.
∎

Supoose
M
is a
K_{\Delta}-
module. Since
K_{\Delta}
is a VNR ring,
M
is a flat
K_{\Delta}-
module. Therefore, from the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{P}_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta_{s}}

we obtain a short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{P}_{s}\otimes_{K_{\Delta}}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\cong K_{\Delta}\otimes_{K_{\Delta}}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{s}=M\otimes_{K_{\Delta}}K_{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Also since
(K_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{P_{s}}}\cong K_{s}
, we must have
M_{\mathfrak{P}_{s}}:=M\otimes_{K_{\Delta}}(K_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{P}_{s}}\cong M\otimes_{K_{\Delta}}K_{s}=:M_{s}
.

Similarly, if
N
is a
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}-
module, then
N_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}:=N\otimes_{K^{\circ}_{\Delta}}(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{p}_{s}}\cong N\otimes_{K^{\circ}_{\Delta}}K^{\circ}_{s}
and
N_{\mathfrak{m}_{s}}:=N\otimes_{K^{\circ}_{\Delta}}(K^{\circ}_{\Delta})_{\mathfrak{m}_{s}}\cong N\otimes_{K^{\circ}_{\Delta}}K_{s}
. Therefore, by the local criterion, the flatness gives the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

A module

Theorem 4.7

(Open Mapping Theorem)

Let

be a surjective morphism of Banach spaces. Then

is an open mapping, i.e., if

is open in

Let

be a pseudo-uniformizer of

satisfying

for all

. Set

\displaystyle=\{x\in E\mid|\varpi|^{-n}\|x\|<1\}

\displaystyle=\{x\in E\mid\|\varpi^{-n}x\|<1\}

\displaystyle=\varpi^{n}\B_{E}(0,1).

Also write

. Therefore, we have

in

. There exists

\displaystyle h\in\varpi^{m}\B_{F}\text{ and }\epsilon>0\implies\text{ there exists }f\in\B_{E}\text{ such that }\|h-Tf\|<\epsilon.

Suppose

Definition 7.1
.

An affinoid

Lemma 7.2

(Tilting Equivalence)
.

The functor

(Affinoid Perfectoid
K_{\Delta}
-algebras)

\displaystyle\to\text{(affinoid perfectoid $K^{\flat}_{\Delta}$-algebras)}

\displaystyle(R,R^{+})

\displaystyle\mapsto(R^{\flat},R^{\flat+})

is an equivalence of categories. This correspondence induces an isomorphism
R^{\flat+}/I^{\flat}_{\varpi}\cong R^{+}/I_{\varpi}
and
R^{\flat+}=\varprojlim\limits_{x\mapsto x^{p}}R^{+}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same line as in Lemma 6.2 of
[
Sch12
]
. Note that
\mathfrak{h}R^{\circ}\subset R^{\circ\circ}_{\Delta}\subset R^{+}\subset R^{\circ}
, because topologically nilpotent elements are integral. Also,
\mathfrak{h}
is the Jacobson radical of
K^{\circ}_{\Delta}
, and hence we have
\mathfrak{h}^{\flat}
as the Jacobson radical of
K^{\circ\flat}_{\Delta}
. Using the above observation, we see that open integrally closed subrings of
R^{\circ}
are in one-to-one correspondence with integrally closed subrings of
R^{\circ}/\mathfrak{h}
. Since
\varpi\in\mathfrak{h}
and
\varpi^{\flat}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\flat}
, and
R^{\circ}/\varpi\cong R^{\circ\flat}/\varpi^{\flat}
, we deduce that integrally closed subrings of
R^{\circ}/\mathfrak{h}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the integrally closed subrings of
K^{\flat\circ}/\mathfrak{h}^{\flat}
. Thus, this correspondence lifts to the other side, and we obtain a bijection between
R^{+}
and
R^{\flat+}
.
∎

Lemma 7.5

(Approximation Lemma)
.

Let
R=K_{\Delta}\left<T^{1/p^{\infty}}_{1},\cdots,T^{1/p^{\infty}}_{n}\right>
. Let
f\in R^{\circ}
be a homogeneous element of degree
d\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
. Then for any rational number
c\geq 0
and any
\epsilon>0
, there exists an element

\displaystyle g_{c,\epsilon}\in R^{\flat\circ}=K^{\flat\circ}_{\Delta}\left<T^{1/p^{\infty}}_{1},\cdots,T^{1/p^{\infty}}_{n}\right>

homogeneous of degree
d
such that for all
x\in X=\spa(R,R^{\circ})
, we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-g^{\#}_{c,\epsilon}(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon}\max(|f(x)|,|\varpi_{\alpha}|^{c}).

Proof.

The same proof as in Lemma 6.5 of
[
Sch12
]
works in this case. Let us recall the proof for the sake of completeness. Fix
f\in R^{\circ}
satisfying the hypothesis and
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\cap(0,1)
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
such that
0<a<\epsilon
. If we take
c^{\prime}
such that
c\leq c^{\prime}
, then
|\varpi|^{c^{\prime}}\leq|\varpi|^{c}
, so the required inequality for
c^{\prime}
gives the required inequality for
c
. Thus, it suffices to prove the inequality for
c=ar
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
. We shall prove the result by induction on
r
, i.e., for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\epsilon_{r}>0
and
g_{r}\in K^{\flat\circ}_{\Delta}\left<T^{1/p^{\infty}}_{1},\cdots,T^{1/p^{\infty}}_{n}\right>
such that, for every
x\in X
, we have

(2)

|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r}(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}\max(|f(x)|,|\varpi|^{ar}).

Suppose
r=0
then choose
\epsilon_{0}=0
,
g_{0}
to be any element satisfying
g^{\#}_{0}\equiv f\mod\varpi
. Then

\displaystyle|f(x)-g^{\#}_{0}(x)|\leq|\varpi|<|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon}=|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon}\max(|f(x)|,|\varpi|^{0})

and we obtain the desired result in this case.

Now, suppose the result is true for
r\geq 0
, i.e., we have found
\epsilon_{r}
and
g_{r}
such that the inequality
2
holds. Decreasing
\epsilon_{r}
, we can assume that
\epsilon_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]
and
\epsilon_{r}\leq\epsilon-a
and choose
\epsilon_{r+1}
satisfying
\epsilon_{r+1}\leq\epsilon_{r}
. Let
X^{\flat}=\spa(R^{\flat},R^{\flat+})
and let
U^{\flat}_{r}\subset X^{\flat}
be the rational subset

\displaystyle U^{\flat}_{r}=U\left(\frac{g_{r},(\varpi^{\flat})^{ra}}{(\varpi^{\flat})^{ra}}\right):=\{x\in X^{\flat}\mid|g_{r}(x)|\leq|\varpi^{\flat}|^{ra}\}.

Then
U_{r}=\phi^{-1}(U^{\flat}_{r})
, where
\phi:\spa(R,R^{\circ})\to\spa(R^{\flat},R^{\flat+})
is a continuous map defined above, is the rational subset of
X
given by

\displaystyle U_{r}=U\left(\frac{g^{\#}_{r},\varpi^{ar}}{\varpi^{ar}}\right):=U\left(\frac{f,\varpi^{ar}}{\varpi^{ar}}\right).

Let
h=f-g^{\#}_{r}
, then by assumption on
g_{r}
, we get

\displaystyle|h(x)|=|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r}(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{ar+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}|\varpi|^{-ar}\max(|f(x)|,|\varpi|^{ar})

for every
x\in X
. This shows that
h\in\varpi^{ar+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X}(U_{r})
. From Lemma
7.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{X}(U_{r})^{\circ a}=R^{\circ}\left<\left(\frac{g^{\#}}{\varpi^{ar}}\right)^{1/p^{\infty}}\right>^{a}.

Since
h
is a homogeneous element, it lies in the
\varpi-
adic completion of

\displaystyle\bigoplus\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right],0\leq i\leq 1}\varpi^{ar+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}\left(\frac{g^{\#}_{r}}{\varpi^{ar}}\right)h_{i}.

For every
i
, choose
s_{i}\in R^{\flat+}_{d-di}
such that
h_{i}-s^{\#}_{i}\in\varpi R^{\circ}
such that the sum

\displaystyle s=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\cap[0,1]}\left(\frac{g_{r}}{(\varpi^{\flat})^{ar}}\right)^{i}s_{i}

converges. Finally, set
g_{r+1}=g_{r}+\varpi^{ar+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}s
. Thus, we claim that

(3)

|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r+1}(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r+1}}\max(|f(x)|,|\varpi|^{a(r+1)}).

We prove this inequality in the following two cases. Let us fix
x\in X
.

Case-1.
|f(x)|>|\varpi|^{ar}
, i.e., if
x
is outside the rational subset.
Then by induction hypothesis
|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r}(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r+1}}\max(|f(x)|,|\varpi|^{ar})=|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r+1}}|f(x)|<|f(x)|\leq\max(|f(x)|,|g^{\#}_{r}(x)|)
. Thus, it is enough to establish the following inequality;

\displaystyle\left|\left((\varpi^{\flat})^{ar+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}\left(\frac{g_{r}}{\varpi^{ar}}\right)s_{i}\right)^{\#}(x)\right|\leq|\varpi|^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r+1}-b}.

But this is immediate since
|s^{\#}_{i}(x)|\leq 1
and the left-hand side has a maximum at
i=1
.

Case-2.
|f(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{ar}
, i.e., x is inside the rational subset.
Then
|g^{\#}_{r}(x)|=|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r}(x)-f(x)|\leq\max(|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r}(x)|,|f(x)|)\leq|\varpi|^{ar}
. We claim that

(4)

|f(x)-g^{\#}_{r+1}(x)|\leq|\varpi|^{a(r+1)+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r+1}}

This clearly implies the result. Now by definition of
g_{r+1}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{g_{r+1}}{(\varpi^{\flat})^{ar}}=\frac{g_{r}}{(\varpi^{\flat})^{ar}}+\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\cap[0,1]}(\varpi^{\flat})^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}\left(\frac{g_{r}}{(\varpi^{\flat})^{ar}}\right)s_{i}.

All the terms are in
\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X^{\flat}}(U^{\flat}_{r})
. So we get the equality

\displaystyle\frac{g^{\#}_{r+1}}{\varpi^{ar}}\equiv\frac{g^{\#}_{r}}{\varpi^{ar}}+\sum\limits_{i\in\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{p}\right]\cap[0,1]}\varpi^{1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r}}\left(\frac{g^{\#}_{r}}{\varpi^{ar}}\right)^{i}h_{i}\mod\varpi\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X}(U_{r})

because
h_{i}-s_{i}\in\varpi R^{\circ}
. Multiplying both sides by
\varpi^{ar}
and subtracting from
f
, we obtain

\displaystyle f-g^{\#}_{r+1}\equiv f-g^{\#}_{r}+h\mod\equiv 0\varpi^{1+ar}\mathcal{O}^{+}_{X}(U_{r})

because
f=h-g^{\#}_{r}
. As
\epsilon_{r+1}<\epsilon_{r}<\epsilon-a
, we have
ar+a+1-\epsilon+\epsilon_{r+1}
which establishes the required inequality.
∎

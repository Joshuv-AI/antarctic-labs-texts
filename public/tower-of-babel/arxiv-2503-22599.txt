Zero-homogeneous and O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) -equivariant critical points of the Oseen-Frank energy with multiple Frank constants
Luc Nguyen

Abstract
We give an existence and classification result for zero-homogeneous and O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) -equivariant critical points of the Oseen-Frank energy with multiple Frank constants. These critical points carry a topological defect of degree one and are minimizing with respect to O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) -equivariant perturbations supported away from the axis of symmetry. Keywords: Oseen-Frank energy, critical points, point defects, O ⁡ ( 2 ) O(2) -equivariant, axial symmetry. MSC: 35J57, 35Q92, 58E15, 76A15. Dedicated to Professor Dang Duc Trong with admiration.

Introduction

In this note, we construct a family of zero-homogeneous and
O(2)
-equivariant equilibrium configurations in the Oseen-Frank theory of nematic liquid crystals with multiple Frank constants.

Recall that, in the Oseen-Frank theory of nematic liquid crystals, equilibrium configurations are critical points of the Oseen-Frank energy

E[u;\Omega]=\int_{\Omega}W(u,\nabla u)\,dx

(1)

where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
is a bounded domain,
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
and the free energy density
W
takes the form

\displaystyle 2W(u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=k_{1}(\nabla\cdot u)^{2}+k_{2}(u\cdot(\nabla\times u))^{2}+k_{3}|u\times(\nabla\times u)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(k_{2}+k_{4})\big[\mathrm{tr}((\nabla u)^{2})-(\nabla\cdot u)^{2}\big].

(2)

Here
k_{1}>0,k_{2}>0,k_{3}>0
and
k_{4}\in{\mathbb{R}}
are the Frank (elastic) constants. We refer the reader to the survey
[
2
]
for an account of mathematical results on the Oseen-Frank theory as well as other theories of liquid crystals.

As is well known, the term
\mathrm{tr}((\nabla u)^{2})-(\nabla\cdot u)^{2}
in the expression of
W
is a null Lagrangian. Therefore, whenever a Dirichlet boundary
u|_{\partial\Omega}=u_{0}
is given, its contribution to
E
is a fixed constant and can be discarded in the study of critical points of
E
. In other words, one may choose
k_{4}
to be any constant as one wishes. In particular, if we write
\alpha=\min\{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\}
and
\beta=3k_{1}+2k_{2}+2k_{3}
, then with the choice
k_{4}=\alpha-k_{2}
, it holds that

\frac{\alpha}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq W(u,\nabla u)\leq\frac{\beta}{2}|\nabla u|^{2},

that is,
W
is coercive (see e.g.
[
1
, equation (2.9)]
). A standard application of the direct method of the calculus of variations shows that, for any given
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
, there exists a minimizer of
E
in
H^{1}_{u_{0}}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{2})=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{2}):u=u_{0}\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}
.

A map
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{S}}^{2})
is a critical point for
E
if it satisfies

-k_{1}\nabla(\nabla\cdot u)+k_{3}\nabla\times(\nabla\times u)\\
+(k_{2}-k_{3})\Big[\nabla\times((u\cdot(\nabla\times u))u)+(u\cdot(\nabla\times u))\nabla\times u\Big]=\lambda u

(3)

in
\Omega
, where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier accounting for the unimodular constraint.

The energy density
W
is rotationally equivariant, that is,

W(Ru,RMR^{T})=W(u,M)\text{ for all }u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},M\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3\times 3},R\in O(3).

This can be used to construct equivariant critical points of
E
. For example, if
G
is a subgroup of
O(3)
and the domain
\Omega
and the boundary data
u_{0}
are
G
-equivariant in the sense that

\begin{cases}Rx\in\Omega\quad\forall~x\in\Omega,\\
u_{0}(Rx)=Ru_{0}(x)\quad\forall~x\in\partial\Omega,R\in G,\end{cases}

then
E
has a
G
-equivariant critical point
u
:

\begin{cases}u\text{ is a critical point of }E[\cdot,\Omega],\\
u|_{\partial\Omega}=u_{0},\\
u(Rx)=Ru(x)\quad\forall~x\in\Omega,R\in G.\end{cases}

For example, such critical point can be obtained by minimizing
E
among
G
-equivariant maps.

A special case which has been studied extensively is the case of
O(3)
-equivariant (or more casually, rotationally symmetric) critical points. It is not hard to show that a map
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{2})
on an
O(3)
-equivariant domain
\Omega
is
O(3)
-equivariant if and only if

u(x)=\pm n(x):=\pm\frac{x}{|x|}\quad\forall~x\in\Omega.

The map
n(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}
is frequently referred to as the hedgehog in the literature. It is a fact that the hedgehog
n
is a critical point for
E
for every choice of Frank constants.

Beside being
O(3)
-equivariant, the hedgehog is also zero-homogeneous and thus can appear as a tangent map when one performs a blow-up analysis near a singular point of critical points. To motivate our later discussion, let us briefly recall some known results in the best established case where
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}
(that is, the ‘one-constant’ case). In this case, with
k_{4}=k_{1}-k_{2}
, the energy
E
is the Dirichlet energy, and minimizers of
E
are minimizing harmonic maps. By a result of Schoen and Uhlenbeck
[
20
]
, away from
\partial\Omega
, minimizing harmonic maps are regular away from a finite set of ‘point defects’ and the tangent map near a defect is zero-homogeneous. Moreover, Brezis, Coron and Lieb showed in
[
5
]
that, modulo a multiplication by a constant orthogonal matrix, the tangent map of a minimizing harmonic map near a point defect is given by the hedgehog. In particular, they showed that the hedgehog
n
is the unique minimizer for
E
with respect to its own boundary data. A different proof of this statement was given by Lin
[
14
]
. We however note that the hedgehog is not the unique zero-homogeneous harmonic map. In spherical coordinates

x=(r\sin\theta\cos\varphi,r\sin\theta\sin\varphi,r\cos\theta),

a special family of zero-homogeneous harmonic maps is given by (see e.g. Poon
[
18
, p. 167]

n_{t}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\Big(\frac{\sin t\sin\theta}{1+\cos t\cos\theta}\cos\varphi,\frac{\sin t\sin\theta}{1+\cos t\cos\theta}\sin\varphi,\frac{\cos t+\cos\theta}{1+\cos t\cos\theta}\Big)^{T},

(4)

where

is a parameter. It is readily seen that the hedgehog
n=n_{\pi/2}
is a member of this family. However, as mentioned above, these maps are not minimizing harmonic maps except for the hedgehog

(i)

When
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
, Hélein observed in
[

that F.-H. Lin’s proof in

.

(iii)

, Hélein showed in

that the second variation of

at

is not minimizing for
E
.

(iv)

When

As a matter of fact, experimental data for many popular nematic liquid crystals such as PAA (

8(k_{2}-k_{1})+k_{3}<0
in most instances. In such cases, the hedgehog is not minimizing and there is no known tangent map for a minimizer near a point defect (if a point defect does exist, say).

The goal of this note is related to the above gap of understanding. We construct a family of zero-homogeneous critical points of
E
which generalizes the family (
4
). We hope that some of these new critical points would be a possible tangent map for a minimizer near a point defect.

The starting point is that the family (
4
) is not only zero-homogeneous but also
O(2)
-equivariant in the sense that

n_{t}(Rx)=Rn_{t}(x)\text{ for all }R\in\{A\in O(3):Ae_{3}=e_{3}\}\cong O(2),

where
e_{3}=(0,0,1)^{T}
. We are thus led to consider
O(2)
-equivariant maps, which, in spherical coordinates, take the form (see Lemma
2.2
)

\displaystyle u(r,\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sin\psi(r,\theta)e_{\varphi}^{\perp}+\cos\psi(r,\theta)e_{3},\quad\text{ for }r<0\leq 1\text{ and }0\leq\theta\leq\pi,

(5)

where

e_{\varphi}=(-\sin\varphi,\cos\varphi,0)^{T}\quad\text{ and }\quad e_{\varphi}^{\perp}=(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi,0)^{T}.

(6)

We note that
O(2)
-equivariance is stronger than
SO(2)
-equivariance and the latter can be more casually referred to as axial symmetry. We will not be concerned with
SO(2)
-equivariant maps in this note.

We establish:

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}>0
. We have the following conclusions.

(i)

For every
t\in(0,\pi)
, there exists a unique solution
\psi_{t}\in C^{\infty}((0,\pi))\cap C^{2,1}([0,\pi])
of the problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\psi_{t}^{\prime}=\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}\sin\psi_{t}}{\sin\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi_{t}-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi_{t}-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}&\text{ in }(0,\pi),\\
\psi_{t}(\pi/2)=t.\end{cases}

(7)

Moreover,
\psi_{t}
is increasing,
\psi_{t}(0)=0,\psi_{t}(\pi)=\pi
and
\frac{\sin\psi_{t}}{\sin\theta}\in C^{1,1}([0,\pi])
.

(ii)

For every
t\in(0,\pi)
, the map
u_{t}
defined by

u_{t}(r,\theta,\varphi)=(\sin\psi_{t}(\theta)\cos\varphi,\sin\psi_{t}(\theta)\sin\varphi,\cos\psi_{t}(\theta))^{T}

belongs to
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
, has a singularity around the origin of topological degree one and is a zero-homogeneous and
O(2)
-equivariant critical point of the Oseen-Frank energy
E
. Moreover, every zero-homogeneous and
O(2)
-equivariant critical point of
E
in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
which has a singularity around the origin of topological degree one must coincide with some such
u_{t}
.

(iii)

When
t=\pi/2
,
\psi_{\pi/2}(\theta)=\theta
and
u_{\pi/2}=n
. When
k_{1}=k_{3}
,
u_{t}=n_{t}
for all
t\in(0,\pi)
.

(iv)

If
\Omega
is a ball centered at the origin, then, for every
t\in(0,\pi)
and every
O(2)
-equivariant maps
u\in C^{1}(\Omega\setminus\{0\},\mathbb{S}^{2})
which coincides with
u_{t}
in a neighborhood of
\partial\Omega\cup\{0\}
, it holds that

E[u;\Omega]\geq E[u_{t};\Omega].

Equality holds if and only if
u=u_{t}
.
(See Proposition
2.12
for a stronger statement.)

It should be clear that the maps
u_{t}
do not depend on the value of
k_{2}
.

We remark that there is no zero-homogeneous
O(2)
-equivariant critical point in
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
of the Oseen-Frank energy
E
of the form (
5
) such that
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(\pi)=j\pi
with
j\notin\{0,\pm 1\}
. Imposing
\psi(\pi)=0
leads to the constant map
u\equiv e_{3}
and imposing
\psi(\pi)=-\pi
leads to
\psi=-\psi_{t}
for some
t\in(0,\pi)
. See Corollary
2.10
.

It remains an interesting open problem if any of the map
u_{t}
is minimizing for
E
with respect to its own boundary data in the class of non-equivariant maps. If this is the case, those
u_{t}
can arise as a tangent map near a point defect of a minimizer.

Lemma
2.3
.

Let

\cos\psi\in W^{1,1}((r_{0},1)\times(0,\pi))

We have by the definition of

For any

\displaystyle\int_{r_{0}}^{1}|\cos^{2}\psi(r,\theta)-\cos^{2}\psi(r,0)|\,dr

It follows that

\displaystyle\ln\frac{\tan(\delta/2)}{\tan(\delta/4)}\int_{r_{0}}^{1}(1-\cos^{2}\psi(r,0))\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{[r_{0},1]\times[\delta/2,\delta]}\frac{1}{\sin\theta}|\cos^{2}\psi(r,\theta)-\cos^{2}\psi(r,0)|\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{[r_{0},1]\times[\delta/2,\delta]}\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\sin^{2}\psi(r,\theta)\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle=\eta_{\delta}(r,\theta)\psi(r,\theta),

and

We have

\displaystyle|||\psi-\psi_{\delta}|||^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{\delta}}\Big[((1-\eta_{\delta})\partial_{r}\psi-\psi\partial_{r}\eta_{\delta})^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}((1-\eta_{\delta})\partial_{\theta}\psi-\psi\partial_{\theta}\eta_{\delta})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}(1-\eta_{\delta})^{2}\psi^{2}\Big]r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E_{\delta}}\Big[2(\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(\partial_{\theta}\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\psi^{2}\Big]r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{[\delta,2\delta]\times[0,\pi]}2\psi^{2}(\partial_{r}\eta_{\delta})^{2}r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{[0,2]\times([\delta,2\delta]\cup[\pi-2\delta,\pi-\delta])}2\psi^{2}(\partial_{\theta}\eta_{\delta})^{2}r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{E_{\delta}}\Big[2(\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}(\partial_{\theta}\psi)^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\psi^{2}\Big]r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{[\delta,2\delta]\times[0,\pi]}32\psi^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr+\int_{[0,2]\times([\delta,2\delta]\cup[\pi-2\delta,\pi-\delta])}128\psi^{2}\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\,d\theta\,dr.

Recalling (
11
), we may choose
\delta
sufficiently small such that

|||\psi-\psi_{\delta}|||\leq{\varepsilon}/2.

Noting that the support of
\psi_{\delta}
is contained in

, we may then use a standard mollification to obtain

such that

. The conclusion follows.
∎

2.3
Reduced energy for maps with lifting in
X_{j}(D)

We now assume that
u
is an
O(2)
-equivariant map with a lifting
\psi\in X_{j}(D)
and proceed to compute
E[u,\Omega]
. Recall that we assume
k_{4}=-k_{2}
.

We compute

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot e_{\varphi}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r\sin\theta},\quad\nabla\cdot e_{\varphi}=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\times e_{\varphi}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\nabla\times e_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}e_{3},

and

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u

\displaystyle=\cos\psi\sin\theta\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\cos\psi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}\psi+\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\sin\psi

\displaystyle\qquad-\sin\psi\cos\theta\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\sin\psi\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}\psi

\displaystyle=-\sin(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi+\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\sin\psi,

\displaystyle\nabla\times u

\displaystyle=\cos\psi\cos\theta\partial_{r}\psi e_{\varphi}-\frac{1}{r}\cos\psi\sin\theta\partial_{\theta}\psi e_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sin\psi\sin\theta\partial_{r}\psi e_{\varphi}+\frac{1}{r}\sin\psi\cos\theta\partial_{\theta}\psi e_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\Big[\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\sin(\psi-\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi\Big]e_{\varphi},

\displaystyle u\cdot(\nabla\times u)

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore

\displaystyle E[u,\Omega]

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big\{k_{1}\Big[-\sin(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi+\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\sin\psi\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+k_{3}\Big[\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\sin(\psi-\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi\Big]^{2}\Big\}\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big\{\big(k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\partial_{\theta}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2(k_{1}-k_{3})}{r}\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi\partial_{\theta}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2k_{1}}{r\sin\theta}\Big[-\sin(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi\Big]\sin\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{1}}{r^{2}}\csc^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\psi\Big\}\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr.

(12)

To handle the term on the second-to-last line, we use the following lemma:

Lemma
2.5
.

Let

\displaystyle(A,B):{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\pi]

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle(s,\theta)

\displaystyle\mapsto(A(s,\theta),B(s,\theta))

be a smooth bounded map satisfying

\partial_{\theta}B=-A

If
\psi\in X_{j}(D)
for some
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}[rA(\psi,\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+B(\psi,\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi]\,d\theta\,dr=\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\psi(1,\theta)}A(s,\theta)\,ds\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{j\pi}B(s,\pi)\,ds.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.4
and noting that
A
and
B
are bounded, we can assume without loss of generality that
\psi-j\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,1]\times(0,\pi))
. In particular,

\psi(r,0)=0\text{ and }\psi(r,\pi)=j\pi.

We start by looking for
C
and
D
such that

rA(\psi,\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+B(\psi,\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi=\partial_{r}(rC(\psi,\theta))+\partial_{\theta}(D(\psi,\theta)).

This leads to the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}C=A,\\
\partial_{s}D=B,\\
C=-\partial_{\theta}D.\end{cases}

First fix
C
by

C(s,\theta)=\int_{0}^{s}A(\xi,\theta)d\xi

so that
\partial_{s}C=A
and
C(0,\cdot)=0
. It remains to solve

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}D=B,\\
\partial_{\theta}D=-C.\end{cases}

Since
{\mathbb{R}}\times[0,\pi]
is simply connected and,
\partial_{\theta}B=-A=\partial_{s}(-C)
(by hypothesis), such
D
exists uniquely with the normalization condition
D(0,0)=0
. We note that

\displaystyle D(0,\pi)

\displaystyle=D(0,0)+\int_{0}^{\pi}\partial_{\theta}D(0,\theta)d\theta=-\int_{0}^{\pi}C(0,\theta)\,d\theta=0,

\displaystyle D(j\pi,\pi)

\displaystyle=D(0,\pi)+\int_{0}^{j\pi}\partial_{s}D(s,\pi)\,ds=\int_{0}^{j\pi}B(s,\pi)\,ds.

Finally, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}[rA(\psi,\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+B(\psi,\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi]\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}[\partial_{r}(rC(\psi,\theta))+\partial_{\theta}(D(\psi,\theta))]\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{\pi}C(\psi(1,\theta),\theta)\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{1}D(\psi(r,\pi),\pi)\,dr-\int_{0}^{1}D(\psi(r,0),0)\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\psi(1,\theta)}A(s,\theta)\,ds\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{j\pi}B(s,\pi)\,ds.

The conclusion follows.
∎

Applying the lemma with

A(s,\theta)=-\sin(s-\theta)\sin s\quad\text{ and }\quad B(s,\theta)=\cos(s-\theta)\sin s,

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[-\sin(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi+\frac{1}{r}\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{\theta}\psi\Big]\sin\psi\,r\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\psi(1,\theta)}\sin(s-\theta)\sin s\,ds\,d\theta-\int_{0}^{j\pi}\cos s\sin s\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\psi(1,\theta)}\big(\sin^{2}s\cos\theta-\sin s\cos s\sin\theta\big)\,ds\,d\theta

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[(\psi-\sin\psi\cos\psi)\cos\theta-\sin^{2}\psi\sin\theta\Big]_{r=1}\,d\theta.

Inserting this into (
12
), we obtain:

Lemma
2.6
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{S}^{2})
be an
O(2)
-equivariant map with a lifting
\psi\in X_{j}(D)
for some
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then

\displaystyle E[u,\Omega]

\displaystyle=J[\psi]:=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big\{\big(k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\partial_{r}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\partial_{\theta}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2(k_{1}-k_{3})}{r}\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)\partial_{r}\psi\partial_{\theta}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{1}}{r^{2}}\csc^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\psi\Big\}\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\qquad+k_{1}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[(\psi-\sin\psi\cos\psi)\cos\theta-\sin^{2}\psi\sin\theta\Big]_{r=1}\,d\theta.

Corollary
2.7
.

Let

be an
O(2)
-equivariant map with a lifting

for some

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{(k_{1}-k_{3})}{r}\big(\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)-\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)\partial_{r}\psi\partial_{\theta}\psi

(13)

that if

) in

[
17
]

.
∎

2.4

We now suppose that

O(2)
-equivariant critical point of

for some

\psi
is independent of

\psi=\psi(\theta)

\displaystyle(k_{1}-k_{3})\sin\theta\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}\Big[\sin\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}\psi^{\prime}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad-k_{1}\csc\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{-1/2}\sin\psi\cos\psi=0\text{ in }(0,\pi).

(14)

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}\Big[\sin^{2}\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\psi^{\prime})^{2}\Big]

Therefore, there exists some constant
C
such that

\sin^{2}\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-k_{1}\sin^{2}\psi=C\text{ in }(0,\pi).

(15)

Noting that the requirement that

. We thus have

\sin^{2}\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-k_{1}\sin^{2}\psi=0.

(16)

We are led to consider the differential equations

\psi^{\prime}=\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}\sin\psi}{\sin\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}

(17)

and

\psi^{\prime}=-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}\sin\psi}{\sin\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}

subjected to an initial value e.g.

(19)

The choice of

as an initial point is ad hoc and unimportant. Note that (
18
) can be recast as (
17
) by the change of variable

Proposition
2.8
.

(i)

If
t\in\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
, then the initial value problem (
17
) and (
19
) has the unique solution
\psi=t
.

(ii)

If
t\in(2\ell\pi,(2\ell+1)\pi)
for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}

17
) and (
19

and
\psi(\pi)=(2\ell+1)\pi

(iii)

If
t\in((2\ell+1)\pi,(2\ell+2)\pi)
for some
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then the initial value problem (
17
) and (
19
) has the unique solution
\psi\in C^{\infty}((0,\pi))\cap C^{2,1}([0,\pi])
, which is strictly decreasing and satisfies
\psi(0)=(2\ell+2)\pi
and
\psi(\pi)=(2\ell+1)\pi
.

In all cases, we have

\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\in C^{1,1}([0,\pi]).

Proof.

Statement (i) is clear. We will only prove statement (ii). The proof of statement (iii) is similar and omitted.

Replacing
\psi
by
\psi-2\ell\pi
if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that
\ell=0
. The uniqueness and the smoothness of
\chi
in its maximal domain of existence follows from standard ODE theory. Let
(c,d)\subset(0,\pi)
be a maximal interval containing
\pi/2
on which
\psi
exists and satisfies
0<\psi<\pi
. Note that in this interval,
\psi^{\prime}>0
by (
17
). If
c>0
, then by standard existence theory,
\psi
would exist in
(c-\delta,c+\delta)
for some small
\delta>0
, and so the maximality of
c
would imply that
\psi(c)=0
. This is impossible as uniqueness theory then would give
\psi\equiv 0
, contradicting
\psi(\pi/2)=t\neq 0
. Thus
c=0
. Arguing similarly,
d=\pi
. We have thus shown that
\psi
exists and is increasing in
(0,\pi)
and satisfies
0<\psi<\pi
in
(0,\pi)
.

The rest of the proof concerns the behavior of
\psi
at the endpoints and is split into three steps.

Step 1:
We prove that
\psi\in C^{0}([0,\pi])
,
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(\pi)=\pi
.

By (
17
), we have that

\frac{p_{1}\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\leq\psi^{\prime}\leq\frac{p_{2}\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\quad\text{ in }(0,\pi),

Since
\psi
is increasing and
0<\psi<\pi
in
(0,\pi)
, we deduce that
\psi
is continuous at
\theta=0
and
\theta=\pi
with
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(\pi)=\pi
.

Step 2:
We prove that
\psi\in C^{0,1}([0,\pi])

Fix some small
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1/2)
and
\theta_{0}\in(0,{\varepsilon})
such that

in

17

depending only on

\psi^{\prime}\geq\frac{(1-c_{1}{\varepsilon}^{2})\psi}{\theta}\text{ in }(0,\theta_{0}),

It follows that

\psi

Returning once again to (

(20)

Arguing similarly, we have that

Step 3:

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}

Lemma

belongs to
H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap C^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\})
, has a singularity around the origin of topological degree one and is a zero-homogeneous and
O(2)
-equivariant critical points of the Oseen-Frank energy
E
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
u_{t}\in H^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
. The differentiability of
\psi_{t}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
follows from the fact that
\frac{\sin\psi_{t}}{\sin\theta}\in C^{1}([0,1])
. It is clear that
u_{t}
is zero-homogeneous and
O(2)
-equivariant and has a singularity around the origin of topological degree one. It remains to show that
u_{t}
satisfies (
3
). By Corollary
2.7
, we only need to show that
\psi_{t}
satisfies (
13
), or equivalently (
14
). Indeed, we compute using (
17
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}\Big[\sin\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}\psi^{\prime}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{d}{d\theta}[k_{1}^{1/2}\sin\psi]=k_{1}^{1/2}\cos\psi\psi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=k_{1}\csc\theta\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{-1/2}\sin\psi\cos\psi\text{ in }(0,\pi).

This clearly gives (
14
).
∎

2.5
Minimality of
\psi_{t}
in
X_{1}(D)

In this subsection, we prove:

Proposition
2.12
.

Let
t\in(0,\pi)
and
\psi_{t}
be the solution to (
7
). Then

J[\psi_{t}]\leq J[\psi]\text{ for all }\psi\in X_{1}(D)\text{ satisfying }\psi|_{r=1}=\psi_{t},

where equality is attained if and only if
\psi=\psi_{t}
. Here
J
is the energy derived in Lemma
2.6
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle J[\psi]

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big\{\big(k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big[\partial_{r}\psi-\frac{k_{1}-k_{3}}{r}\frac{\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\partial_{\theta}\psi-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\Big)\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{1}k_{3}}{r^{2}}\frac{1}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\partial_{\theta}\psi-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2(k_{1}-k_{3})k_{1}^{1/2}}{r}\frac{\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{r}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2k_{1}^{1/2}}{r^{2}}\frac{k_{1}k_{3}+(k_{1}-k_{3})^{2}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}\psi\Big\}\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+k_{1}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[(\psi-\sin\psi\cos\psi)\cos\theta-\sin^{2}\psi\sin\theta\Big]_{r=1}\,d\theta.

Now, applying Lemma
2.5
with

\displaystyle A(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(k_{1}-k_{3})\sin(s-\theta)\cos(s-\theta)}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(s-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(s-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\sin s,

\displaystyle B(s,\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{1}k_{3}+(k_{1}-k_{3})^{2}\sin^{2}(s-\theta)\cos^{2}(s-\theta)}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(s-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(s-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(s-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(s-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\sin s,

we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big\{-\frac{r(k_{1}-k_{3})\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\sin\psi\partial_{r}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{1}k_{3}+(k_{1}-k_{3})^{2}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\sin\psi\partial_{\theta}\psi\Big\}\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\quad=-(k_{1}-k_{3})\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\psi(1,\theta)}\frac{\sin(s-\theta)\cos(s-\theta)}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(s-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(s-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\sin s\,ds\,d\theta

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{k_{1}k_{3}+(k_{1}-k_{3})^{2}\sin^{2}s\cos^{2}s}{k_{1}\sin^{2}s+k_{3}\cos^{2}s}\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}s+k_{3}\sin^{2}s\big)^{1/2}}\sin s\,ds

Altogether we have

\displaystyle J[\psi]

\displaystyle=\pi\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big\{\big(k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big[\partial_{r}\psi-\frac{k_{1}-k_{3}}{r}\frac{\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\partial_{\theta}\psi-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\Big)\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{1}k_{3}}{r^{2}}\frac{1}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\partial_{\theta}\psi-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\Big)^{2}\Big\}\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+k_{1}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big[(\psi-\sin\psi\cos\psi)\cos\theta-\sin^{2}\psi\sin\theta\Big]_{r=1}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad-2k_{1}^{1/2}(k_{1}-k_{3})\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\psi(1,\theta)}\frac{\sin(s-\theta)\cos(s-\theta)}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(s-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(s-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\sin s\,ds\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad+2k_{1}^{1/2}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{k_{1}k_{3}+(k_{1}-k_{3})^{2}\sin^{2}s\cos^{2}s}{k_{1}\sin^{2}s+k_{3}\cos^{2}s}\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}s+k_{3}\sin^{2}s\big)^{1/2}}\sin s\,ds.

Using
\psi=\psi_{t}
on
\{r=1\}
and (
17
), we see that
J[\psi_{t}]
is equal to the sum on the last three lines and yielding

J[\psi]\geq J[\psi_{t}].

Moreover, if equality holds, we must have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi-\frac{k_{1}-k_{3}}{r}\frac{\sin(\psi-\theta)\cos(\psi-\theta)}{k_{1}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\Big(\partial_{\theta}\psi-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}\Big)=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}\psi-\frac{k_{1}^{1/2}}{\big(k_{1}\cos^{2}(\psi-\theta)+k_{3}\sin^{2}(\psi-\theta)\big)^{1/2}}\frac{\sin\psi}{\sin\theta}=0,

which clearly implies
\partial_{r}\psi=0
and hence
\psi=\psi_{t}
(since they agree on
\{r=1\}
).
∎

A Fröberg type theorem for higher secant complexes Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author
Junho Choe and Jaewoo Jung ∗

Abstract
. We generalize the celebrated Fröberg’s theorem to embedded joins of copies of a simplicial complex, namely higher secant complexes to the simplicial complex, in terms of property N q + 1 , p N_{q+1,p} due to Green and Lazarsfeld. Furthermore, we investigate combinatorial phenomena parallel to geometric ones observed for higher secant varieties of minimal degree.

1.
Introduction

A
(abstract) simplicial complex

\Delta
on a finite set
V
is a set of subsets, called
faces
, of
V
with the transitive property that if
\Delta\ni F\supseteq G
, then
\Delta\ni G
. We impose a nondegeneracy condition that
\{v\}\in\Delta
for every
v\in V
. Here every element of
V
is called a
vertex
of
\Delta
, and we write
V=V(\Delta)
and call it the
vertex set
of
\Delta
. A face of
\Delta
is called an
edge
if it consists of exactly two vertices. We define a
graph

G
on
V
as a simplicial complex on
V
whose faces have at most two vertices. We denote a graph
G
by
G=(V(G),E(G))
, where
V(G)
is the vertex set of
G
, and
E(G)
is the
edge set
of
G
, that is, the set of edges of
G
. For a graph
G
its
clique complex

\Delta(G)
is the simplicial complex on
V(G)
whose faces are precisely cliques of
G
.

The main object of this paper is
embedded joins
of copies of a simplicial complex.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a finite simplicial complex on the vertex set
V=V(\Delta)
.
For an integer
q\geq 1
we define the
q
-secant complex

\sigma_{q}\Delta
to
\Delta
as the simplicial complex on
V
given by

\sigma_{q}\Delta=\left\{F_{1}\cup\cdots\cup F_{q}\subseteq V:F_{1},\ldots,F_{q}\text{ are faces of }\Delta\right\}.

To state our main result we introduce some families of graphs.

(1)

\mathcal{F}_{q,1}
is the collection of graphs
H\neq\overline{K_{q+1}}
such that
V(H)
is not a union of
q
cliques, but so is every proper subset.

(2)

\mathcal{F}_{q,2}
consists of
H\sqcup\overline{K_{q-j}}
for all integers
1\leq j\leq q
and all
elementary bipartite graphs

H
on
2j+2
vertices.

Here
\overline{K_{s}}
means the complement of the complete graph
K_{s}
on
s
vertices, and for the definition and structure of
elementary bipartite graphs
, see
4.1
and
4.3
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite (simple) graph.
It is called
q
-secant chordal
for an integer
q\geq 1
if it is
\mathcal{F}_{q,1}
-free,
\mathcal{F}_{q,2}
-free, and
C_{2q+p}
-free for all

.
That is,
G
does not contain any graph in
\mathcal{F}_{q,1}\cup\mathcal{F}_{q,2}\cup\{C_{2q+p}:p\geq 3\}
as an induced subgraph.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex with vertex set

The

of

is defined to be

I(\Delta)=(x_{W}:W\subseteq V\textup{ is not a face of }\Delta)\subseteq S,

where

is called the

of

.
There are active studies on algebraic properties of Stanley-Reisner ideals and rings from various viewpoints.
For instance, see
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and

Now our main theorem characterizes, by means of
forbidden induced subgraphs
, simplicial complexes
\Delta
for a fixed integer
p\geq 1
such that
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
satisfies
property
N_{q+1,p}
, that is,

\beta_{i,j}(S(\sigma_{q}\Delta))=0\quad\text{whenever}\quad i\leq p\textup{ and }j\geq q+1.

Property
N_{q+1,p}
is a refinement of having
(q+1)
-linear
resolution in the sense that
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
satisfies property
N_{q+1,p}
for all
p\geq 1
if and only if
I(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
has
(q+1)
-linear resolution, that is,
\beta_{i,j}(I(\sigma_{q}\Delta))=0
as long as
j\neq q+1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Delta
be a finite simplicial complex with vertex set
V=V(\Delta)
and
q\geq 1
be an integer. Then

(1)

S(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
satisfies property
N_{q+1,1}
if and only if we have
\sigma_{q}\Delta=\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)
for some
\mathcal{F}_{q,1}
-free graph
G
on
V
,

(2)

it also satisfies property
N_{q+1,2}
if and only if such a graph
G
is
\mathcal{F}_{q,2}
-free, and

(3)

it also satisfies property
N_{q+1,p}
for an integer
p\geq 3
if and only if such a graph
G
is
C_{2q+i}
-free for all
3\leq i\leq p
.

In particular,
I(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))
has
(q+1)
-linear resolution if and only if
G
is
q
-secant chordal.

The case
q=1
has already been shown in
[
EGHP05
, Theorem 2.1]
, and the statement (
1
) follows from a study by Sturmfels and Sullivant
[
SS06
]
.

One of the motivations is the celebrated
Fröberg’s theorem

[
Frö90
]
. It completely characterizes simplicial complexes
\Delta
such that
I(\Delta)
has
2
-linear resolution, asserting that for a graph
G
the Stanley-Reisner ideal
I(\Delta(G))
has
2
-linear resolution if and only if
G
is chordal, that is, it has no induced cycles of four or more vertices. The authors of
[
EGHP05
]
refined Fröberg’s result in view of property
N_{2,p}
in
[
EGHP05
, Theorem 2.1]
: For a graph
G
the Stanley-Reisner ring
S(\Delta(G))
satisfies property
N_{2,p}
for an integer
p\geq 1
if and only if there are no chordless cycles of at most
p+2
vertices in
G
.

Another motivation comes from studies about intriguing classes of projective varieties, namely the families of
varieties of minimal higher secant degree
.
Assume that the base field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed of characteristic
0
for a while.
Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective variety over
\Bbbk
, where a
variety
is always irreducible and reduced, and set it to be
nondegenerate
, that is,
X
lies in no hyperplanes.
Then there is an elementary lower bound on the degree of
X
by its codimension:
\deg X\geq\textup{codim}\,X+1
, and the varieties having

\deg X=\textup{codim}\,X+1

are called
varieties of minimal degree
. Regarding varieties of minimal degree, while their geometric classification has been found by del Pezzo and Bertini (cf.
[
EH87
]
), their algebraic characterizations have been extensively studied. Especially, by Eisenbud and Goto
[
EG84
]
a variety
X
is of minimal degree if and only if
I(X)
has
2
-linear resolution.

We expand our discussion to the
q
-secant variety
to
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
for an integer
q\geq 1
.
It is defined to be the Zariski closure

\sigma_{q}X=\overline{\bigcup_{z_{i}\in X}\langle z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}\rangle}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r},

where
\langle z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is the subspace spanned by points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}
of
X
.
In joint papers
[
CK22b
]
and
[
CK22a
]
with Kwak the first author generalized both the del Pezzo-Bertini classification and the Eisenbud-Goto result in consideration of higher secant varieties.
In detail,
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
(resp.
\sigma_{q}X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
) is called a
variety of minimal
q
-secant degree
(resp. a
q
-secant variety of minimal degree
) if for the codimension
c=\textup{codim}\,\sigma_{q}X
the
Ciliberto-Russo bound

\deg\sigma_{q}X\geq\binom{c+q}{q}

(
[
CR06
, Theorem 4.2]
) turns into equality. Then higher secant varieties of minimal degree have a classification in terms of determinantal presentation, and it holds that
\sigma_{q}X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a
q
-secant variety of minimal degree if and only if
I(\sigma_{q}X)
has
(q+1)
-linear resolution. However, as one may observe in
[
CR06
, Theorem 9.1 and Theorem 9.2]
it seems not easy to completely classify the varieties of minimal higher secant degree and would give rise to rich and interesting geometry.

Now we drop the irreducibility condition on
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
In this case the notion of variety of minimal degree can be naturally extended by using the Eisenbud-Goto characterization.
Indeed, as stated in
[
EGHP06
, Theorem 0.4]
the possibly reducible algebraic set
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
has a
2
-linear resolution if and only if its irreducible components are varieties of minimal degree in the span of each and are “linearly joined” according to a specific ordering of themselves.

In order to explore higher secant varieties in a broader setting, we focus on
coordinate subspace arrangements
, that is, unions of coordinate linear spaces in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. For a coordinate subspace arrangement
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
the homogeneous ideal
I(Z)
is generated by squarefree monomials, and one can study algebraic properties of
I(Z)
through the associated simplicial complex
\Delta
, using the
Stanley-Reisner correspondence
. We define the
q
-secant variety
\sigma_{q}Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
to
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
with the same formula as above.

We remark that the Stanley-Reisner correspondence identifies
\sigma_{q}Z
and
\sigma_{q}\Delta
(
[
SU00
, Corollary 3.3]
). Hence,
1.3
resolves a combinatorial counterpart to the classification problem for varieties of minimal higher secant degree, and its last assertion can be said to provide a combinatorial explanation for why varieties of minimal
q
-secant degree seem various and more diverse as
q
increases, as in
[
CR06
, Theorem 9.1 and Theorem 9.2]
.

Below are corollaries of
1.3
, regarding property
N_{q+1,p}
for integers
q\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\Delta
be a finite simplicial complex and
q\geq 1
be an integer. Assume that
S(\sigma_{j}\Delta)
satisfies property
N_{j+1,1}
for all
2\leq j<q
. Then if
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
satisfies property
N_{q+1,p}
for an integer
p\geq 3
, then
S(\sigma_{q+1}\Delta)
satisfies property
N_{q+2,p-2}
. In particular, given an integer
p\geq 1
the Stanley-Reisner ring
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
satisfies property
N_{q+1,p-2q+2}
for every
q\geq 1
as long as it holds for the case
q=1
.

Hence, if
I(\Delta)
has
2
-linear resolution, then
I(\sigma_{q}\Delta)
has
(q+1)
-linear resolution for all
q\geq 2
, which is parallel to the fact (cf.
[
CR06
]
) that if
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a variety of minimal degree, then
\sigma_{q}X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a
q
-secant variety of minimal degree for every
q\geq 2
. Also notice that the same vanishing pattern as in the last assertion is observed for sufficiently large degree complete embeddings of smooth irreducible curves due to Ein, Niu, and Park
[
ENP20
]
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph and
q\geq 1
be an integer.
Then if
G
is
q
-secant chordal, then so is its edge contraction

.
More generally, for a finite simplicial complex
\Delta

satisfies property
N_{q+1,p}
for an integer

, then
S(\sigma_{q}(\Delta/e))
satisfies property
N_{q+1,p-1}
for every edge

of
\Delta
.

A geometric twin of the first assertion is the fact that if
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a variety of minimal
q
-secant degree for an integer

, then so is its linear projection from a general point of

, namely a general inner projection of

.

Building on these analogues we explore the combinatorial perspective by investigating the Betti numbers
\beta_{p,q}(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))
and projective dimension of higher secant complexes.
The following theorem establishes a precise combinatorial formula of the Betti numbers.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
G
be a graph, and suppose that
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))
satisfies property
N_{q+1,p-1}
.
Then,

\beta_{p,q}(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))=\sum_{s=q}^{p+q-1}\binom{s}{q}|\mathcal{H}_{p+q}^{s}(G)|,

where
\mathcal{H}_{n}^{s}(G)
is the set of induced subgraphs of
G
with
n
vertices and
s+1
connected components.

This formula allows us to combinatorially determine the projective dimension of
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))
for
q
-secant chordal graphs (
6.3
).
Moreover, we precisely characterize the condition involving edges under which the projective dimension is preserved under edge contractions (
6.4
).

Finally, we provide a classification result for
q
-secant chordal forests
G
such that
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))
are
Cohen–Macaulay
.
In other words, we specify forests
G
satisfying

\textup{pd}\,S(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))=\textup{codim}\,\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)

under a mild assumption.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
G
be a forest with
|V(G)|\geq 2q+2
, and assume that it has a matching of size
q
for an integer
q\geq 2
.
Then
S(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
G
is a path graph.

In this context, the path graph on
r+1\geq 2q+2
vertices is a genuine counterpart of the rational normal curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree
r
: the
q
-secant variety
\sigma_{q}C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
has minimal degree, hence
S(\sigma_{q}C)
is automatically Cohen-Macaulay
[
CK22b
, Theorem 1.1]
, and there are no other such projective irreducible curves
[
CR06
, Theorem 6.1]
.

2.1.
Graded Betti numbers

In this subsection we review numerical information and vanishing properties obtained from minimal free resolutions.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a finitely generated graded
S
-module
M
,
graded Betti numbers
of
M
are defined to be

\beta_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}(M)=\dim_{\Bbbk}\textup{Tor}_{i}^{S}(M,\Bbbk)_{i+j}

for integers
i,j
, and the
Betti table
of
M
is defined to be the following diagram.

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}&\cdots&i&i+1&\cdots\\
\hline\cr\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\\
j&\cdots&\beta_{i,j}&\beta_{i+1,j}&\cdots\\
j+1&\cdots&\beta_{i,j+1}&\beta_{i+1,j+1}&\cdots\\
\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\end{array}

For brevity we omit entries if they are zero.

By nature of Tor functors the graded Betti numbers
\beta_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}(M)
fit in the minimal free resolution of
M
depicted as in
Figure

1
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module.

(1)

M
satisfies
property
N_{d,p}
for integers
d
and
p
if

\beta_{i,j}(M)=0\quad\textup{when}\quad i\leq p\textup{ and }j\geq d.

(2)

The
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
of
M
is defined to be

\reg M=\max\{j\in\mathbb{Z}:\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\textup{ for some }i\geq 0\}.

(3)

The
projective dimension
of
M
is defined to be

\textup{pd}\,M=\max\{i\geq 0:\beta_{i,j}(M)\neq 0\textup{ for some }j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We remark that when
I\subsetneq S
is a homogeneous ideal without forms of degree
q
for an integer
q\geq 1
, the quotient ring
S/I
satisfies property
N_{q+1,p}
for an integer
p\geq 0
if and only if the first
p+1
free modules of its minimal free resolution are

S^{\beta_{i,j}(S/I)}(-i-j)

with
0\leq i\leq p
, and if it is the case for every
p\geq 0
, then
I
has
(q+1)
-linear
resolution, which means that
\beta_{i,j}(I)=0
for any
i\geq 0
and
j\neq q+1
.

In order to simplify an argument to appear we recall a formula of Herzog and Kühl.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
HK84
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module, and assume that the
S
-module
M
has pure resolution, that is, for an integer
p\geq 0
there exist integers
j_{0},\ldots,j_{p}
such that
\beta_{0,j_{0}}(M),\ldots,\beta_{p,j_{p}}(M)
are the only nonzero graded Betti numbers of
M
.
Then we have

\beta_{i,j_{i}}(M)=\left|\prod_{k\neq 0,i}\frac{j_{k}-j_{0}+k}{j_{k}-j_{i}+k-j}\right|\cdot\beta_{0,j_{0}}(M)

for all

is Cohen-Macaulay, that is,
\textup{pd}\,M=\textup{codim}\,M
.

2.2.
Simplicial complexes and the Stanley-Reisner correspondence

In this subsection we review (finite abstract) simplicial complexes and their algebraic aspects.
A
simplicial complex
on a finite set

is a collection of subsets of
V
that is closed under taking subsets.
That is, for any
F\in\Delta
, if
G\subseteq F
, then
G\in\Delta
.
We assume a nondegeneracy setting that all singletons in
V
are contained in
\Delta
.
Any element of
V
is called a
vertex
of
\Delta
, the set
V
is called the
vertex set
of
\Delta
, and the members of
\Delta
are called
faces
.
An
edge
is a face with only two vertices.
Faces that are maximal with respect to inclusion are called
facets
.
A
simplex
on
V
is the simplicial complex
2^{V}
, the power set of
V
.
For a vertex
v
of
\Delta
if no edges contain
v
, then
v
is called
isolated
.
For a face
F
of
\Delta
we define the
dimension
of
F
by
\dim F=|F|-1
and the
dimension
of
\Delta
by the maximal dimension of faces of
\Delta
.
A
graph

G
is a simplicial complex of dimension at most one, and we write
G=(V(G),E(G))
, where
E(G)
is the set of edges in
G
.
For a subset
W\subseteq V
the
induced subcomplex
of
\Delta
on
W
, denoted by
\Delta[W]
, is the simplicial complex on
W
whose faces are precisely those of
\Delta
that are contained in
W
.

Now we recall the
Stanley-Reisner correspondence
. Suppose the following.

(1)

\Delta
is a simplicial complex with the vertex set
V=\{0,1,\ldots,r\}
.

(2)

I\subseteq S=\Bbbk[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
is a squarefree monomial ideal with no linear generators.

(3)

Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a nondegenerate coordinate subspace arrangement.

Then
I
is called the
Stanley-Reisner ideal
of
\Delta
if it is equal to

I(\Delta)=(x_{W}:W\subseteq V\textup{ is not a face of }\Delta)\subseteq S.

Conversely,
\Delta
is called the
Stanley-Reisner complex
of
I
if it is given as

\{F\subseteq V:x_{F}\not\in I\}.

This coupling between
I
and
\Delta
is known as the
Stanley-Reisner correspondence
.
In addition, the ideal-variety correspondence enables us to compare
Z
with
I
and so with
\Delta
.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I(\Delta)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta(I)}

One important aspect of the Stanley–Reisner correspondence is that topological data of a simplicial complex
\Delta
are encoded in algebraic data of the Stanley-Reisner ideal

, namely Hochster’s formula
[
Hoc77
]
. We adopt its homological version.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
MS05
, Corollary 5.12 and Corollary 1.40]

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on

. Then we have

\beta_{i,j}(\Delta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subseteq V,\\
|W|=i+j\end{subarray}}\dim_{\Bbbk}\widetilde{H}_{j-1}(\Delta[W],\Bbbk)

for all integers
i
and
j
with
i+j>0
.

A closed subscheme
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is called
(q+1)
-regular
for an integer

if its homogeneous ideal
I(Z)
satisfies
\reg I(Z)\leq q+1
.
This notion is generalized and interpreted for simplicial complexes as follows.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
V
. Then one says that
\Delta
is
(q+1)
-regular
for an integer

if one of the following equivalent conditions holds.

(1)

\reg I(\Delta)\leq q+1
.

(2)

\widetilde{H}_{j}(\Delta[W],\Bbbk)=0
for all nonempty subsets

and all integers

.

2.3.
Higher secant complexes

Given a nondegenerate reduced subscheme
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
and an integer
q\geq 1
the
q
-secant variety
to
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is defined to be the Zariski closure

\sigma_{q}Z=\overline{\bigcup_{z_{i}\in Z}\langle z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}\rangle}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r},

where
\langle z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is the subspace of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
spanned by
z_{1},\ldots,z_{q}\in Z
.

If
Z\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is a nondegenerate coordinate subspace arrangement, then so is its
q
-secant variety
\sigma_{q}Z
. Then the Stanley-Reisner complex assigned to

can be obtained via
embedded join
.

Definition 2.7
.

For finite subsets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in a set

let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be simplicial complexes on
V_{1}
and on

, respectively. Then the

of

and

is a simplicial complex on

defined to be

\Delta_{1}\ast\Delta_{2}=\{F_{1}\cup F_{2}\subseteq V_{1}\cup V_{2}:F_{1}\in\Delta_{1}\text{ and }F_{2}\in\Delta_{2}\}.

For a simplicial complex
\Delta
the
q
-secant complex
to
\Delta
is defined to be

\sigma_{q}\Delta=\underbrace{\Delta\ast\cdots\ast\Delta}_{q\text{ times}}=\{F_{1}\cup\cdots\cup F_{q}\subseteq V(\Delta):F_{i}\in\Delta\}.

The statement just above is formally written as follows.

.

[

, Corollary 3.3.]

Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be simplicial complexes on the same vertex set. Then

is the join algebra
[

, Definition 1.1]
of

and
I(\Delta_{2})
. In particular, for a simplicial complex
\Delta
on

vertices and its corresponding coordinate subspace arrangement

we have

for all

.

As its consequence

shares known results with

. Useful examples are the following.

(cf.

)

Let

Then the graded pieces of

Let

Taking higher secant complexes is not a one-to-one process. The following resolves some subtleties.

Lemma 2.10

(cf.
[

, Lemma 1.4]
)
.

Let

and

, and take an integer

. Then
\sigma_{q}\Delta_{1}=\sigma_{q}\Delta_{2}
implies that
\sigma_{q+1}\Delta_{1}=\sigma_{q+1}\Delta_{2}

Proof.

We trivially have
\sigma_{q+1}\Delta_{1}\subseteq\sigma_{q+1}\Delta_{2}
. For the reverse containment let

be an arbitrary face, where
F_{1},\ldots,F_{q+1}\in\Delta_{2}
. Then since
F_{1}\cup\cdots\cup F_{q}\in\sigma_{q}\Delta_{2}=\sigma_{q}\Delta_{1}
, there are faces

such that
G_{1}\cup\cdots\cup G_{q}=F_{1}\cup\cdots\cup F_{q}
, hence

. Do the same thing for
G_{2}\cup\cdots\cup G_{q}\cup F_{q+1}
so that it is equal to

for some

.
∎

We collect operations on simplicial complexes. Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
V
.

(1)

The

of

by a subset

(2)

(3)

The

of

by a vertex

of

\Delta-v=\{F\subseteq V\setminus\{v\}:F\in\Delta\}.

We remark that taking induced subcomplexes commutes with taking higher secant complexes.

In particular, vertex deletions commute with taking higher secant complexes.

We recall face contractions of complexes.

.

.

Let

3.
Higher secant complexes to 2-regular complexes

In this section we extend the following result of Fröberg to higher secant complexes to clique complexes of chordal graphs.

Theorem 3.1

(
[

, Theorem 1]
)
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta

\Delta=\Delta(G)
for some chordal graph

Useful facts about the structure of chordal graphs are that they are closed under vertex deletions and that any chordal graph

admits a vertex

Theorem 3.2

(A corollary of
[

Every chordal graph

Hence, any chordal graph

of the vertices in

so that for every

the vertex

By the structure of chordal graphs one can prove the following direction of Fröberg’s result by using the Mayer-Vietoris sequence and induction on the number of vertices: If

For a generalization to

The following is the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.3

If a simplicial complex

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the number of vertices. Note that

\reg(\sigma_{q}\Delta)\leq\max\{\reg(\sigma_{q}\Delta-v),\reg(\link_{\sigma_{q}\Delta}v)+1\}.

In this section as a complementary observation we interpret the value of

for a graph

satisfies property

-secant chordal.

We specify collections of induced subgraphs that provide numerical building blocks for our purpose.

Definition 6.1
.

Let

be a graph. For integers

to be the set of induced subgraphs

1.8

Proof of

.

We proceed by

Let

be an induced subgraph on

such that
\widetilde{H}_{q-1}(\sigma_{q}\Delta(H),\Bbbk)\neq 0

to be the number of connected components of

. To the contrary say that

Remark 6.2
.

Let

be a graph and

As a consequence of

for a

-secant chordal graph

can be shown to include information on the projective dimension of

where

.

Now let us discuss how projective dimension changes under edge contractions. In the spirit of

As an application of
6.3
we discuss
Cohen-Macaulay
simplicial complexes.
A simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
Cohen-Macaulay
if its Stanley-Reisner ring
S(\Delta)
is Cohen-Macaulay, meaning that

\textup{pd}\,S(\Delta)=\textup{codim}\,\Delta.

We recall that any Cohen-Macaulay complex is pure, meaning that all of its facets have the same dimension.
Moreover, any Cohen-Macaulay complex of positive dimension is connected.
In addition, if a pure complex
\Delta
admits a
shelling order
, then it is Cohen-Macaulay; see
[
HH11
, Section 8]
for further details.

We focus on
forests

G
and characterize those for which
\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)
are Cohen-Macaulay for a given integer
q\geq 1
.
Here, we define a
forest
as a disjoint union of
trees
, that is, connected graphs having no cycles. Recall that a vertex
v
in a forest
G
is called

(1)

a
leaf
if
\deg_{G}v=1
,

(2)

an
internal vertex
if
\deg_{G}v\geq 2
, and

(3)

a
branch vertex
if
\deg_{G}v\geq 3
.

We remark some facts about forests.

Remark 6.6
.

Let
G
be a forest and
q\geq 1
be an integer.

(1)

\Delta(G)
has dimension at most one because
G
is
C_{3}
-free.

(2)

\dim\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)\leq 2q-1
, and the equality holds if and only if the matching number is
\nu(G)\geq q
.

(3)

G
is
q
-secant chordal by
3.3
.

(4)

\Delta(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
G
is a tree or
\overline{K_{r+1}}
, where
r=|V(G)|-1
.
Indeed, if
G=\overline{K_{r+1}}
, then
\Delta(G)
is Cohen-Macaulay by
3.5
.
If
G
is a tree with at least one edge, then
\textup{pd}\,S(\Delta(G))=|V(G)|-2=\textup{codim}\,\Delta(G)
by
6.3
.
(Consider an internal vertex of
G
unless
G=P_{2}
.) The converse is straightforward since
\Delta(G)
must be connected.

Motivated by a property of projective curves: for a nondegenerate projective variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
over an algebraically closed field, if
X
is a curve, then
\dim\sigma_{q}X=2q-1
unless
\sigma_{q}X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
(see
[
Pal06
]
),
we assume that the forest
G
has
\nu(G)\geq q
for the remainder of this section.

6.3
yields a criterion for Cohen-Macaulayness of higher secant complexes for such forests. Notice that if we delete an internal vertex of a forest, then the number of connected components strictly increases.

Corollary 6.7
.

Let
G
be a forest.
Suppose that
\nu(G)\geq q\geq 1
and that
\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)
is not a simplex.
Then
\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)
is not Cohen-Macaulay if and only if there exists a set of
q-1
vertices in
G
whose deletion results in at least
q+1
connected components.

Proof.

Note that
\textup{codim}\,\sigma_{q}\Delta(G)=|V(G)|-2q
.
In light of
6.3
we have
\textup{pd}\,S(\sigma_{q}\Delta(G))\geq|V(G)|-2q+1
if and only if
G
contains a set of
q-1
vertices whose deletion induces at least
q+1
connected components.
∎

Now we show that under a reasonable assumption the path graphs are the only graphs among forests whose higher secant complexes are Cohen-Macaulay.
We have seen that
\sigma_{q}\Delta(P_{r+1})
is Cohen-Macaulay for any integer

in

.

Let

and

.

. We divide into two cases according to the location of

, then the path between

with

.

The “if” part immediately follows by

Proposition 6.10
.

Let
q\geq 1
be an integer,
T_{0}
be a tree on
q+1
vertices, and
T
be the tree obtained from
T_{0}
by subdividing every edge of
T_{0}
with one new vertex. Then
\sigma_{q}\Delta(T)
is Cohen-Macaulay, but
\nu(T)=q
.

Proof.

To show that
\sigma_{q}\Delta(T)
is Cohen-Macaulay, we claim that
V(T_{0})\subset V(T)
induces the only subgraph on
q+1
vertices with no edges in
T
.
To this end we use induction on
q\geq 1
. The initial step
q=1
is trivial, hence we set
q\geq 2
. Let
\ell
be a leaf of
T_{0}
, and take
T_{0}^{\prime}=T_{0}-\ell
to be its vertex deletion together with the tree
T^{\prime}
constructed in the same way for
T_{0}^{\prime}
. By the induction hypothesis
V(T_{0}^{\prime})\subset V(T^{\prime})
gives the unique induced subgraph on
q
vertices without edges in
T^{\prime}
. Then in consideration of the deletion of
\ell
and the vertex adjacent to
\ell
from
T
, obviously if
H
is an induced subgraph on
q+1
vertices with no edges in
T
, then
V(H)=V(T_{0}^{\prime})\sqcup\{\ell\}=V(T_{0})
, and vice versa.

The part
\nu(T)=q
is also proved by the same induction argument. Let
v\in V(T)
be the vertex adjacent to
\ell
. Its removal from
T
gives a matching of size
\nu(T)-1
in
T^{\prime}
. So
\nu(T)-1\leq\nu(T^{\prime})=q-1
. On the other hand, adding the edge
\{v,\ell\}
produces a matching of size
\nu(T^{\prime})+1
in
T
. Therefore, the induction process says that
\nu(T)=q
.
∎

Remark 6.11
.

A similar construction yields trees
\widehat{T}
on
2q+1
vertices such that
\sigma_{q}\Delta(\widehat{T})
is not Cohen-Macaulay, and
\nu(\widehat{T})=q
.

For an integer
q\geq 2
take
T_{0}^{\prime}
to be a tree on
q
vertices, and let
T^{\prime}
be the subdivision of
T_{0}^{\prime}
as above.
Introduce a new path
P_{3}
, and identify one of its endpoints with a vertex of
T^{\prime}
that does not belong to
T_{0}^{\prime}
. Then the formed tree
\widehat{T}
satisfies
\nu(\widehat{T})=q
for the same reason as above. However,
\sigma_{q}\Delta(\widehat{T})
is not Cohen-Macaulay. Indeed,
T
has (exactly) two induced subgraphs on
q+1
vertices with no edges. They contain the only induced subgraph on
q
vertices without edges in
T^{\prime}
and depend on the choice of an unidentified vertex in
P_{3}
.

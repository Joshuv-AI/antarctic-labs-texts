Social Reintegration and Associated Factors Among Patients Receiving Peritoneal Dialysis: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Qi-Qing Lin, Yong-Chun Wang, Xi-Mei Zhang, Ya-Bin Zhang, Guo-Zheng Luo, Na Tian, Yan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSocial reintegration is an important patient-centered outcome for patients receiving peritoneal dialysis (PD), reflecting their ability to maintain daily activities and social roles during long-term home-based treatment. However, evidence regarding social reintegration and its associated factors in patients receiving peritoneal dialysis remains limited.ObjectiveTo evaluate social reintegration among adults receiving maintenance peritoneal dialysis and to identify associated clinical, nutritional, and functional factors.MethodsThis single-center cross-sectional study included adults receiving maintenance PD in China. Social reintegration was assessed using the Social Reintegration Grading Method and classified as low or high. Nutritional status was assessed using the Subjective Global Assessment, and activities of daily living (ADL) using the Barthel Index. Univariable analyses were followed by Firth’s penalized logistic regression. Sensitivity analyses assessed the robustness of the findings.ResultsAmong 202 participants, 37 (18.3%) had low social reintegration and 165 (81.7%) had high social reintegration. Dialysis duration of more than 10 years was associated with lower odds of high social reintegration (OR = 0.293, 95% CI: 0.088–0.975, P = 0.046). ADL dependence was also negatively associated with high social reintegration (OR = 0.236, 95% CI: 0.093–0.561, P = 0.001). Sensitivity analyses supported these associations.ConclusionThe majority of patients receiving peritoneal dialysis were classified as having high social reintegration. However, dialysis duration of more than 10 years and dependence in activities of daily living were associated with lower odds of high social reintegration. These findings support incorporating social reintegration and functional assessment into routine peritoneal dialysis follow-up. Patients with prolonged dialysis duration or reduced functional independence may require additional support to maintain daily functioning and social roles.Keywords: peritoneal dialysis, social reintegration, activities of daily living, functional dependence, firth’s penalized logistic regression

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a major global public health challenge, affecting approximately 850 million people worldwide, with a continuing increase in disease burden.1,2 As CKD progresses to end-stage renal disease, many patients require kidney replacement therapy, including hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis (PD). PD is an important home-based treatment modality that allows patients to receive long-term dialysis outside hospital settings. However, PD also requires continuous self-management, adaptation to treatment routines, and long-term support from healthcare professionals and family members. In addition to clinical outcomes, increasing attention has been paid to patient-centered outcomes in PD care, including daily functioning, symptom burden, quality of life, and participation in everyday life.3,4 In mainland China, PD care has continued to develop over the past decade, highlighting the importance of evaluating socially relevant outcomes in this population.5

Social reintegration, also referred to as social rehabilitation, refers to the process by which patients resume or maintain meaningful roles and participation in everyday life after disease-related limitations. It includes self-care ability, family and social participation, and, when applicable, return to work or maintenance of social roles. For patients receiving PD, social reintegration is particularly important because long-term home-based treatment requires patients to manage dialysis while maintaining daily activities, family responsibilities, and social interaction. The related concept of life participation has been increasingly recognized as a core patient-prioritized outcome in PD research.6,7 Therefore, assessment of social reintegration may provide a practical perspective for understanding patients’ functional status, role maintenance, and broader well-being during long-term PD. Compared with in-center hemodialysis, PD may provide greater flexibility for maintaining daily routines, employment, and social roles.8 Nevertheless, this relative advantage does not ensure satisfactory reintegration for all patients, particularly during long-term treatment, underscoring the need to identify those at risk of poorer social participation.

Previous studies have reported social and psychosocial problems among patients receiving PD, including social isolation and social alienation.9,10 However, existing evidence in the Chinese context has mainly focused on related psychosocial constructs rather than directly examining the level and associated factors of social reintegration among patients undergoing PD. Poor social reintegration may reflect difficulties in maintaining independence, family responsibilities, and social roles, highlighting the importance of patient-centered outcomes alongside conventional clinical indicators.6,7 In addition, social reintegration may be influenced by multiple clinical and functional factors, such as dialysis duration, comorbidities, nutritional status, and activities of daily living. Identifying these factors may help healthcare professionals recognize patients who are at risk of poorer social participation and who may require additional support during routine follow-up.

Given that social reintegration in PD should reflect not only self-care ability but also participation in daily and social activities and maintenance of social or work roles, the Social Reintegration Grading Method proposed by Tao et al was adopted in this study.11 Developed specifically for patients receiving peritoneal dialysis, this brief four-level method provides a clinically interpretable classification of social reintegration. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate social reintegration among Chinese adults receiving maintenance PD and to identify associated clinical, nutritional, and functional factors. The findings may provide evidence for routine assessment, functional evaluation, and supportive care for patients receiving long-term PD.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This was a single-center cross-sectional study conducted at the peritoneal dialysis center of the Department of Nephrology, General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University, China. Patients receiving maintenance peritoneal dialysis were recruited using convenience sampling from January to March 2026.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) diagnosis of end-stage renal disease with an estimated glomerular filtration rate <15 mL/min/1.73 m2; (2) receiving peritoneal dialysis for at least 3 months; (3) aged 18 years or older; and (4) willingness to participate in the study and provide written informed consent. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) concurrent hemodialysis; (2) planned kidney transplantation; and (3) severe illness or concomitant severe organ dysfunction. Sample Size

At the study design stage, nine candidate factors were prespecified based on previous literature, clinical relevance, and the study objectives. These factors were age, sex, dialysis duration, primary kidney disease, hemoglobin, serum albumin, comorbidity status, nutritional status, and activities of daily living. Separately, for pragmatic recruitment planning, a working ratio of 10–20 participants per candidate factor was used, yielding an initial target of 90–180 participants.12,13 After allowing for an anticipated 15% loss due to nonresponse or non-analyzable questionnaires, the target recruitment range was approximately 106–212 participants[90/(1−0.15) to 180/(1−0.15)]. Ultimately, 202 participants with complete data were included in the final analysis.

Measures

Demographic and Clinical Data Form

A demographic and clinical data form was developed by the research team based on relevant literature, clinical practice, and expert consultation. The form collected information on age, sex, marital status, education level, employment status, medical payment method, monthly income, dialysis duration, peritoneal dialysis modality, primary kidney disease, and comorbidities.

Social Reintegration Grading Method

Social reintegration was assessed using the Social Reintegration Grading Method proposed by Tao et al.11 This method classifies patients according to self-care ability, participation in daily and social activities, and maintenance or resumption of social or work roles. In this study, trained researchers assigned the social reintegration level according to the predefined grading criteria, based on participant-reported information on self-care, daily and social activities, and employment or social-role status.

The grading criteria were as follows. Level 1 indicates impaired self-care ability and little interest in the external environment or interpersonal relationships. Level 2 indicates incomplete functional independence or limited participation in activities beyond basic self-care. Level 3 indicates independence in daily living and participation in at least one activity beyond basic self-care, but without employment among patients of working age. Level 4 indicates independence in daily living, active social participation, and maintenance or resumption of a social role through full-time work, part-time work, or, for retired patients, participation in activities beyond basic self-care. For regression analysis, Levels 1 and 2 were classified as low social reintegration, whereas Levels 3 and 4 were classified as high social reintegration. The detailed operational criteria used for assigning each social reintegration level are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Subjective Global Assessment

Nutritional status was assessed using the Subjective Global Assessment (SGA).14,15 The SGA evaluates nutritional status through medical history and physical examination, including weight change, dietary intake, gastrointestinal symptoms, functional capacity, disease-related stress, muscle wasting, subcutaneous fat loss, and edema. According to the SGA classification, patients were categorized as having normal nutrition or malnutrition.

Activities of Daily Living

Activities of daily living were assessed using the Barthel Index.16–18 The Barthel Index evaluates basic self-care and mobility-related activities, including bowel control, bladder control, grooming, toilet use, feeding, bathing, dressing, transfers, walking on a level surface, and stair climbing. The total score ranges from 0 to 100, with 100 indicating complete independence. In this study, a score of 100 was classified as ADL independence, whereas a score below 100 was classified as ADL dependence.

Laboratory Variables

Laboratory variables included parathyroid hormone, hemoglobin, serum albumin, calcium, and phosphorus. For each variable, the most recent measurement recorded within the 3 months preceding questionnaire completion was used and categorized according to the thresholds presented in Table 1.

Data Collection

Data were collected on site using paper-based questionnaires. Trained researchers distributed the questionnaires face to face and supervised their completion. After written informed consent was obtained, participants independently completed the self-report items, while researchers provided clarification on procedural questions when necessary. Completion time was recorded from questionnaire initiation to return. Questionnaires were checked on site, and missing or unclear responses were verified with participants whenever possible. Clinical and laboratory data were extracted from electronic medical records.

Duplicate questionnaires were identified using unique participant identifiers, and only the first completed questionnaire was retained. Questionnaires completed in less than 1 minute or records lacking key information were excluded.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0 and R software. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Records lacking key information required for the analysis were excluded during data-quality screening; consequently, the final analytic dataset contained no missing values, and no imputation was performed. The dependent variable was social reintegration status, classified as low social reintegration and high social reintegration. Univariable analyses were first performed to examine factors associated with social reintegration among patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis, using chi-square tests for categorical variables. Variables showing statistical significance in univariable analysis and/or considered clinically relevant based on previous literature and the study objectives were considered for inclusion in the multivariable model. Categorical predictors were entered as indicator variables with prespecified reference categories.

Standard binary logistic regression was initially performed as a preliminary modeling attempt. Because sparse data and complete separation were identified for some categories, Firth’s penalized logistic regression was used as the main multivariable analysis to reduce small-sample bias and obtain finite parameter estimates.19,20 Odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported, and a two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Two sensitivity analyses based on Firth’s penalized logistic regression were performed to examine the robustness of the findings. In the first sensitivity analysis, polycystic kidney disease was collapsed into the “other” primary kidney disease category. In the second sensitivity analysis, dementia/Alzheimer’s disease was excluded from the model because of the very small subgroup size and complete separation.

Results

Participant Characteristics

Participant recruitment and questionnaire screening are summarised in Figure 1. A total of 210 paper questionnaires were collected from 207 unique participants. After three duplicate questionnaires were removed, five of the remaining 207 records were excluded during data-quality screening (four with completion times of less than 1 minute and one with missing key information). The final analysis therefore included 202 participants. Their baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1.

Distribution of Social Reintegration, Nutritional Status, and Activities of Daily Living

Among the 202 patients receiving peritoneal dialysis, social reintegration was distributed as follows: Level 1, 8 patients (4.0%); Level 2, 29 patients (14.4%); Level 3, 94 patients (46.5%); and Level 4, 71 patients (35.1%). For regression analysis, Levels 1 and 2 were classified as low social reintegration, comprising 37 patients (18.3%), whereas Levels 3 and 4 were classified as high social reintegration, comprising 165 patients (81.7%).

Nutritional assessment showed that 129 patients (63.9%) had normal nutrition, whereas 73 patients (36.1%) had mild-to-moderate or severe malnutrition. Regarding activities of daily living, 125 patients (61.9%) were classified as independent and 77 patients (38.1%) as dependent.

Univariable Analysis

Univariable analysis showed statistically significant differences between the low and high social reintegration groups in dialysis duration, primary kidney disease, nutritional status, activities of daily living dependence, congestive heart failure, hemiplegia, and dementia/Alzheimer’s disease (all P <0.05; Table 2).

Initial Multivariable Modeling and Identification of Complete Separation

Variables with statistical significance in univariable analysis and/or clinical relevance were considered candidate variables for multivariable analysis. Standard binary logistic regression was initially performed as a preliminary modeling attempt. However, complete separation was observed for polycystic kidney disease and dementia/Alzheimer’s disease, resulting in unstable parameter estimates and limiting the interpretability of the conventional logistic regression model. Therefore, Firth’s penalized logistic regression was used as the main multivariable analysis.

Main Multivariable Analysis Using Firth’s Penalized Logistic Regression

The results of Firth’s penalized logistic regression are presented in Table 3 and Figure 2. Compared with patients who had received peritoneal dialysis for 0–3 years, those with a dialysis duration of more than 10 years had lower odds of high social reintegration (odds ratio [OR] = 0.293, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.088–0.975, P = 0.046). Activities of daily living dependence was also negatively associated with high social reintegration (OR = 0.236, 95% CI: 0.093–0.561, P = 0.001).

Sensitivity Analyses

Two sensitivity analyses based on Firth’s penalized logistic regression were performed. In the first sensitivity analysis, after merging polycystic kidney disease into the “other” primary kidney disease category, dialysis duration of more than 10 years remained negatively associated with high social reintegration (OR = 0.294, 95% CI: 0.089–0.981, P = 0.047). Dementia/Alzheimer’s disease (OR = 0.035, 95% CI: <0.001–0.426, P = 0.006) and activities of daily living dependence (OR = 0.229, 95% CI: 0.091–0.544, P = 0.001) also remained negatively associated with high social reintegration, whereas nutritional status remained non-significant (P = 0.970).

In the second sensitivity analysis, after excluding dementia/Alzheimer’s disease from the model, dialysis duration of more than 10 years (OR = 0.288, 95% CI: 0.090–0.912, P = 0.035) and activities of daily living dependence (OR = 0.295, 95% CI: 0.125–0.670, P = 0.003) remained negatively associated with high social reintegration. Nutritional status was still not significantly associated with the outcome (P = 0.565). Overall, the associations of dialysis duration of more than 10 years and activities of daily living dependence with lower odds of high social reintegration were robust across sensitivity analyses.

Discussion

Principal Findings

This study evaluated social reintegration and its associated factors among patients receiving maintenance peritoneal dialysis. Most participants (81.7%) were classified as having high social reintegration, whereas 18.3% were classified as having low social reintegration. In the multivariable Firth’s penalized logistic regression model, dialysis duration of more than 10 years and dependence in activities of daily living were negatively associated with high social reintegration. These associations remained stable in sensitivity analyses, suggesting that prolonged dialysis duration and functional dependence may be important markers of poorer social reintegration in this population. Dementia/Alzheimer’s disease was also negatively associated with high social reintegration in the main model, but this finding should be interpreted cautiously because of the very small subgroup size. Nutritional status was not independently associated with social reintegration after penalized estimation.

The relatively high level of social reintegration observed in this study is generally consistent with previous findings reported by Wang et al.21 One possible explanation is that peritoneal dialysis, as a home-based treatment modality, may provide patients with greater flexibility in daily treatment arrangements than in-center hemodialysis. This flexibility may help some patients maintain daily routines, family responsibilities, social interactions, and, when possible, work-related or other social roles. Evidence from the DOPPS program also suggests that patients receiving peritoneal dialysis may differ from those receiving in-center hemodialysis in disease burden, health-related quality of life, and employment-related outcomes.22 The relatively high proportion of high social reintegration may partly reflect the functional profile of this cohort and the classification approach used. In particular, 61.9% of participants were independent in activities of daily living, and Level 3, which was classified as high social reintegration, accounted for 46.5% of the sample. However, the presence of a subgroup with low social reintegration indicates that social reintegration remains heterogeneous among patients receiving peritoneal dialysis and should not be assumed to be satisfactory for all patients.

Previous studies of related psychosocial outcomes in patients receiving PD have emphasized factors such as educational level, physical activity, family or perceived social support, and psychological resources.9,10 In contrast, the most consistent factors identified in the present study were prolonged dialysis duration and ADL dependence. These differences may partly reflect differences in the outcomes and measurement approaches used, as the present grading method places particular emphasis on functional independence and maintenance of daily and social roles. Differences in study populations and the factors examined may also have contributed to the varying findings.

Dialysis Duration and Social Reintegration

Dialysis duration of more than 10 years was associated with lower odds of high social reintegration. This finding suggests that prolonged peritoneal dialysis may be related to reduced role maintenance and social participation. Previous studies have shown that longer dialysis duration may be accompanied by greater symptom burden, functional decline, and poorer quality of life among dialysis patients.23,24 Patients in earlier stages of dialysis may have relatively better physical function, fewer accumulated complications, and greater capacity to maintain previous family and social roles. In contrast, as dialysis duration increases, patients may experience cumulative treatment burden, recurrent complications, treatment fatigue, psychological stress, and gradual disruption of social routines, which may reduce their ability or willingness to participate in daily and social life.

This finding suggests that patients receiving peritoneal dialysis for extended periods may require attention not only to dialysis adequacy, biochemical indicators, and complications, but also to functional status, daily participation, family support, and social role maintenance during routine follow-up. However, because the number of patients with dialysis duration of more than 10 years was relatively small in this study, this result should be interpreted cautiously and validated in larger multicenter studies.

Activities of Daily Living Dependence and Social Reintegration

ADL dependence was another robust factor associated with lower odds of high social reintegration. This result is clinically plausible because social reintegration depends partly on the ability to perform basic self-care and participate in daily activities. Patients with impaired self-care or mobility may face barriers to participating in family, community, and work-related activities. Previous research has also shown that limitations in daily functioning are closely related to reduced mobility, lower social participation, and poorer quality of life in dialysis populations.25

ADL dependence may affect social reintegration through both functional and psychosocial pathways. Functionally, reduced independence may limit patients’ ability to leave home, communicate with others, attend social activities, or perform work-related tasks. Psychosocially, long-term reliance on others for daily care may reduce autonomy, self-efficacy, and confidence in social interaction. For patients receiving peritoneal dialysis, ADL dependence may also interfere with dialysis-related self-management and increase reliance on family members or caregivers. These findings support the incorporation of functional assessment into routine peritoneal dialysis follow-up. For patients with ADL dependence, functional maintenance, caregiver education, assisted peritoneal dialysis when appropriate, and coordinated follow-up may help support independence and social participation.26

Nutritional Status and Comorbidities

In the univariable analysis, nutritional status differed between patients with low and high social reintegration. However, this association was no longer significant after Firth’s penalized logistic regression and remained non-significant in sensitivity analyses. This finding suggests that the relationship between nutritional status and social reintegration may be influenced by other factors, such as functional dependence, comorbidity burden, dialysis duration, or sparse-data bias. Nutritional status remains clinically important in peritoneal dialysis care, but the present results do not support it as an independent correlate of social reintegration after adjustment.

Dementia/Alzheimer’s disease showed a negative association with high social reintegration in the main model. This finding is clinically understandable because cognitive impairment may reduce self-care capacity, communication, treatment self-management, and social participation. However, the subgroup size was very small, and complete separation was identified in the conventional logistic model. Therefore, this result should be interpreted as exploratory rather than definitive. Future studies with larger samples and more detailed cognitive assessment are needed to clarify the relationship between cognitive impairment and social reintegration among patients receiving peritoneal dialysis.

Implications for Practice

The findings of this study have practical implications for the follow-up and supportive care of patients receiving peritoneal dialysis. Social reintegration reflects not only clinical stability but also patients’ ability to maintain daily functioning, family participation, and social roles. Therefore, assessment of social reintegration may complement conventional clinical monitoring in peritoneal dialysis care.

The two most consistent factors identified in this study, dialysis duration of more than 10 years and ADL dependence, may help healthcare professionals recognize patients at higher risk of poorer social reintegration. In routine follow-up, patients with prolonged dialysis duration may require closer attention to treatment burden, functional decline, and social role maintenance. Patients with ADL dependence may benefit from functional assessment, caregiver support, rehabilitation referral when needed, and individualized guidance to maintain daily activities and social participation. These implications should be understood as practice-oriented suggestions rather than evidence of intervention effectiveness, as this study did not evaluate any specific supportive care or rehabilitation intervention. Future longitudinal and intervention studies are needed to determine whether targeted support can improve social reintegration among patients receiving peritoneal dialysis.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. Therefore, the observed associations between dialysis duration, ADL dependence, and social reintegration should not be interpreted as causal relationships.

Second, this was a single-center study using convenience sampling, which may limit the representativeness of the sample and the generalizability of the findings to other peritoneal dialysis populations or healthcare settings.

Third, although the Social Reintegration Grading Method is clinically interpretable and reflects self-care ability, social participation, and role resumption, it was derived from an earlier Chinese study and has not been fully revalidated in contemporary peritoneal dialysis populations. In addition, some assessments, particularly the Social Reintegration Grading Method, relied partly on participant-reported information and researcher or clinical judgment, which may have introduced reporting or classification bias. Its comparability with internationally used patient-reported outcome measures may therefore be limited. Future studies should consider cross-validating this grading method with standardized instruments assessing life participation, social functioning, or health-related quality of life.

Fourth, several subgroup sizes were small, particularly for dementia/Alzheimer’s disease and dialysis duration of more than 10 years. Although Firth’s penalized logistic regression and sensitivity analyses were used to reduce small-sample bias and improve model stability, the precision of some estimates remained limited. Fifth, dichotomizing the original four-level social reintegration outcome may have resulted in loss of ordinal information. Future studies with larger samples may consider ordinal or multinomial modeling approaches.

Finally, this study did not evaluate any specific supportive care, rehabilitation, or intervention program. Therefore, the practice implications of the findings should be interpreted as suggestions for risk identification and follow-up assessment rather than evidence of intervention effectiveness. Future multicenter longitudinal studies are needed to validate these findings and to further explore strategies for improving social reintegration among patients receiving peritoneal dialysis.

Conclusion

The majority of patients receiving maintenance peritoneal dialysis in this study were classified as having high social reintegration. However, dialysis duration of more than 10 years and dependence in activities of daily living were consistently associated with lower odds of high social reintegration in Firth’s penalized logistic regression and sensitivity analyses. These findings suggest that social reintegration and functional status should be considered during routine follow-up for patients receiving peritoneal dialysis. Patients with prolonged dialysis duration or reduced functional independence may require additional attention and support to maintain daily functioning, family participation, and social roles. Owing to the cross-sectional design, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and validated in future longitudinal studies.

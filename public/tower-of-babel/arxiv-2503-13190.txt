Equ \mathrm{Equ} -saturating categories
Dominique Bourn

Abstract
Starting from the varietal notion of syntactic equivalence relation, we generalized it to a categorical concept; namely Equ \mathrm{Equ} -saturating category. We produce various examples and focuse our attention on the protomodular context in which any equivalence relation is then shown to have a centralizer.

Syntactic equivalence relations

2.1
Monoids

We shall denote by
\mathsf{Mon}
the category of monoids.
Let
M
be a monoid and
W

M
. Recall from
[
27

the following definition and results:

Definition 2.1
.

Call syntactic relation associated with
W

defined by:

mR_{W}n\iff\forall(x,y)\in M\times M,\;[xmy\in W\iff xny\in W]

Proposition 2.1
.

Given any non-empty
W
, the relation
R_{W}
is an internal equivalence relation in
\mathsf{Mon}
whose class of the unit
1
is the monoid
\overline{W}
defined by:

\overline{W}=\{m\in M/\forall(x,y)\in M\times M,\;[xmy\in W\iff xy\in W]\}

Proof.

The relation
R_{W}
is an equivalence relation since it is the case of the hyperrelation [
\iff
]. Let us check it is internal in
\mathsf{Mon}
:

1) We get
1R_{W}1
since
R_{W}
is reflexive;

2) Suppose
mR_{W}n
and
m^{\prime}R_{W}n^{\prime}
. Then we get:

\forall(x,y)\in M\times M,\;xmm^{\prime}y\in W\iff xnm^{\prime}y
since
mR_{W}n
, and:

\forall(x,y)\in M\times M,\;xnm^{\prime}y\in W\iff xnn^{\prime}y
since
m^{\prime}R_{W}n^{\prime}
.

In this way, we get
mnR_{W}m^{\prime}n^{\prime}
.
∎

Lemma 2.1
.

When we have
1\in W
, we then get:
\overline{W}\subset W
.

Proof.

Suppose
m\in\overline{W}
taking
(x,y)=(1,1)
, we get:
m\in W\iff 1\in W
.
∎

So, when

\forall k\in K\;{\rm and}\;\forall(x,y)\in M\times M,\;[xky\in K\iff xy\in K]

A submonoid

Checking that the kernel of a monoid homomorphism is normal in this sense is straightforward. Conversely, let
K

Let

When

1) Suppose

2.2
Groups

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
K\hookrightarrow G
a submonoid. Then
K
is a normal subobject in
\mathsf{Mon}
if and only if it is a normal subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

A normal subgroup is a normal submonoid since
\forall k\in K
and
\forall(x,y)\in G\times G
, we get:

xky=xkx^{-1}xy\in K\iff xy\in K

Conversely, suppose
K
is a normal submonoid. Let
k\in K
. Then with
(1,k^{-1})\in G\times G
, we get:

1kk^{-1}=1\in K\iff 1k^{-1}=k^{-1}\in K
. And
K
is a subgroup of
G
. Now, with any
(x,x^{-1})\in G\times G
, we get
xkx^{-1}\in K\iff xx^{-1}=1\in K
. So,
K
is a normal subgroup.
∎

Let
\mathsf{Gp}
be the category of groups,
G
a group, and
K
a normal subgroup of
G
. We know that the set of internal equivalence relations in
\mathsf{Gp}
to which
K
is normal is reduced to one element; accordingly the category
\mathsf{Gp}
is an extremal example of a category in which the set of internal equivalence relations to which a subobject is normal, when it is non-empty, has a supremum. In
\mathsf{Gp}
, this equivalence relation coincides with the syntactic equivalence relation
R_{K}
in
\mathsf{Mon}
. This is a corollary of the following well known

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
S
an equivalence relation on

which internal in
\mathsf{Mon}
. Then it is internal in
\mathsf{Gp}
.

Proof.

It remains to check that when we have
mSn
, we get
m^{-1}Sn^{-1}
. From
mSn
, we get
1=mm^{-1}Snm^{-1}
; then
n^{-1}Sm^{-1}
.
∎

2.3.1
Semirings

We shall briefly review the case of semirings, for two reasons:

1) it enlarges the strong structural parallelism with monoids initiated in
[
17
]
,

2) the list of axioms of the syntactic equivalence relation can be reduced to a finite one.

A semiring is a commutative monoid
(A,+,0)
endowed with an associative binary operation
\cdot:A\times A\to A
which is distributive w.r. to
+
and such that
x.0=0
for all
x\in A
. We shall denote by
\mathsf{SRg}
the category of semirings.
Let
A
be a semiring and
W
any non-empty part of
A
.

Definition 2.4
.

Call syntactic relation associated with
W
the relation defined by:
mR_{W}n
when the following conditions hold:

1)
\forall x\in A,\;[x+m\in W\iff x+n\in W]
;

2)
\forall(x,y)\in A\times A,\;[x+my\in W\iff x+ny\in W]
;

3)
\forall(x,y)\in A\times A,\;[x+ym\in W\iff x+yn\in W]
;

4)
\forall(x,y,z)\in A\times A\times A,\;[x+ymz\in W\iff x+ynz\in W]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given any
W
, the relation
R_{W}
is a congruence in
\mathsf{SRg}
whose class of the unit
0
is the subsemiring
\overline{W}
defined by the set of objects

1)
\forall x\in A,\;[x+m\in W\iff x\in W]
;

2)
\forall(x,y)\in A\times A,\;[x+my\in W\iff x\in W]
;

3)
\forall(x,y)\in A\times A,\;[x+ym\in W\iff x\in W]
;

4)
\forall(x,y,z)\in A\times A\times A,\;[x+ymz\in W\iff x\in W]
.

Proof.

The relation
R_{W}
is an equivalence relation since it is the case of the hyperrelation [
\iff
]. Let us check it is a congruence in
\mathsf{SRg}
.

Compatibility with the law
+
:

1) We get
0R_{W}0
since
R_{W}
is reflexive.

2) Suppose

and
m^{\prime}R_{W}n^{\prime}
. We have to check

. Condition 1) holds by Proposition

.

Condition 2): suppose

W
. Since

R_{W}
.

Condition 3) is satisfied on the same way by Condition 3) on
R_{W}
. Same thing for Condition 4).

Compatibility with the law
\cdot

Suppose

and
m^{\prime}R_{W}n^{\prime}

Condition 1). We get
x+mm^{\prime}\in W

x+nm^{\prime}\in W

x+nm^{\prime}\in W

x+nn^{\prime}\in W

Condition 2). We get

Condition 4). We get

x+ynm^{\prime}z\in W

x+ynm^{\prime}z\in W

2.3.3
Left skew braces

Braces are algebraic structures which were introduced by Rump
[
26
]
as producing set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation in response to a general incitement of Drinfeld
[
21
]
to investigate this equation from a set-theoretical perspective. Later on, Guarnieri and Vendramin generalized this notion with the structure of left skew brace
[
23
]
which again generates solutions of the Yang-Baxter equations:

Definition

A left skew brace is a set
X
endowed with two group structures
(X,*,\circ)
subject to a unique axiom:
a\circ(b*c)=(a\circ b)*a^{-*}*(a\circ c)\;(1)
, where
a^{-*}
denotes the inverse for the law

.

The simplest examples are the following ones: starting with any group
(G,*)
, then
(G,*,*)
and
(G,*,*^{op})
are left skew braces. We denote by
\mathsf{SkB}
the category of left skew braces which is obviously a variety in the sense of Universal Algebra. Accordingly, it allows the definition of the syntactic equivalence relation, even though, in this case, the list of its axioms does not seem to be reductible to a finite one.

In this paper, we focus on rational lines on diagonal cubic hypersurfaces. Consider

(1.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}z^{3}_{i}=0,

where
c_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq s)
are non-zero integers.
We aim to count pairs of vectors
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
and
\boldsymbol{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
such that the line

(\ref{cubic equation}).

One checks that such pairs
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
and

\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}x^{3}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}x_{i}^{2}y_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}x_{i}y_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}y^{3}_{i}=0.

Let
N_{s}(X):=N_{s}(X;\boldsymbol{c})
denote the number of solutions
(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2s}\cap[-X,X]^{2s}
to
(\ref{cubic system})
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Whenever
s\geq 19
, one has

(1.3)

N_{s}(X)=\sigma X^{2s-12}+O(X^{2s-12-\delta}),

for some
\delta>0

is a positive constant.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we will introduce some definitions and two lemmas from which Theorem
1.1
immediately follows. Define

(2.1)

F_{c_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):=\sum_{|x|,|y|\leq X}e(c_{j}\alpha_{1}x^{3}+c_{j}\alpha_{2}x^{2}y+c_{j}\alpha_{3}xy^{2}+c_{j}\alpha_{4}y^{3}),

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We see by the orthogonality that

N_{s}(X)=\int_{[0,1]^{4}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}F_{c_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

As in the previous results introduced in the first paragraph, we make use of the Hardy-Littlewood circle method in order to obtain
(\ref{expected asymptotic formula}).
To do so, we must define
the major and minor arcs. Define the major arcs
\mathfrak{N}_{\delta}
by

\mathfrak{N}_{\delta}=\bigcup_{q\leq X^{\delta}}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\boldsymbol{a}\leq q\\
(q,\boldsymbol{a})=1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{N}_{q,\boldsymbol{a}},

where

\mathfrak{N}_{q,\boldsymbol{a}}:=\{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4})\in[0,1)^{4}:\ |\alpha_{i}-a_{i}/q|\leq X^{\delta-3}\}.

Define
\mathfrak{n}_{\delta}:=[0,1)^{4}\setminus\mathfrak{N}_{\delta}.
Here and throughout, we set
\delta=10^{-10}.
By using these major and minor arcs dissections, we deduce that

(2.2)

N_{s}(X)=\int_{\mathfrak{N}_{\delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}F_{c_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\int_{\mathfrak{n}_{\delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}F_{c_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}.

The first lemma provides the asymptotic estimate for the first term on the right hand side in
(\ref{major and minor arcs dissection})
, which is already in the literature. To facilitate the statement of the lemma, we define

S(q,\boldsymbol{a})=\sum_{x=1}^{q}\sum_{y=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{a_{1}x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}y+a_{3}xy^{2}+a_{4}y^{3}}{q}\right)

and

S(q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\boldsymbol{a}\leq q\\
(q,\boldsymbol{a})=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}(q^{-2}S(q,c_{j}\boldsymbol{a})).

We define the singular series
\mathfrak{S}
by

\mathfrak{S}=\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}S(q).

Furthermore, for
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4},
define

v(\boldsymbol{\gamma})=\int_{-P}^{P}\int_{-P}^{P}e(\xi^{3}\gamma_{1}+\xi^{2}\eta\gamma_{2}+\xi\eta^{2}\gamma_{3}+\eta^{3}\gamma_{4})d\xi d\eta,

and write

u(\boldsymbol{\gamma})=\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}e(\xi^{3}\gamma_{1}+\xi^{2}\eta\gamma_{2}+\xi\eta^{2}\gamma_{3}+\eta^{3}\gamma_{4})d\xi d\eta.

We define the singular integral
\mathfrak{J}
by

\mathfrak{J}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}u(c_{j}\boldsymbol{\gamma})d\boldsymbol{\gamma}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Whenever
s\geq 16,
one has

(2.3)

\int_{\mathfrak{N}_{\delta}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}F_{c_{j}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})d\boldsymbol{\alpha}=\mathfrak{S}\mathfrak{J}X^{2s-12}+O(X^{2s-12-\eta^{\prime}}),

for some
\eta^{\prime}>0.
Furthermore, we have
\mathfrak{S}\asymp 1
and
\mathfrak{J}\asymp 1
.

By
[

and

3.
Multidimensional shifting variables argument

We begin this section by defining

(3.1)

F(\boldsymbol{\alpha}):=\sum_{1\leq x,y\leq X}e(\alpha_{1}x^{3}+\alpha_{2}x^{2}y+\alpha_{3}xy^{2}+\alpha_{4}y^{3}),

We further define an auxiliary exponential sum

with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

we let

Define the exponential sum

\displaystyle F(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta})

\displaystyle:=F(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta},\boldsymbol{\theta};X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq x,y\leq X}e\left(\sum_{l=1}^{4}\alpha_{l}(\nu_{3}(x,y))_{l}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}\beta_{l}(\nu_{2}(x,y))_{l}+\sum_{l=1}^{2}\theta_{l}(\nu_{1}(x,y))_{l}\right).

Define

J_{s}(X):=J_{s,2,3}(X)

define

For example, we see that

\mathfrak{M}(H):=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq q\leq H\\

Define

2.1.
Probabilistic bounds

Lemma 2.1
.

For
s\geq 3
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
p=n^{-2/s+\varepsilon}
. Then, w.h.p. any subset
S\subset V(G_{n,p})
with
|S|\geq\frac{n}{\log^{2}n}
contains a copy of
K_{s}
.

Proof.

Let
n_{*}=n/\log^{2}n
. It is enough to consider subsets
S\subset V(G_{n,p})
with
|S|=n_{*}
. For a fixed vertex set
S
note that the subgraph of
G_{n,p}
induced by
S
is distributed as a random graph with edge probability
p
. Thus, if
\mu_{n_{*}}
is the expected number of copies of
K_{s}
in
G_{n,p}[S]
, we have

\frac{\mu_{n_{*}}}{n}\geq\frac{n_{*}^{s}}{s^{s}}p^{\binom{s}{2}}=\frac{n_{*}}{s^{s}}(n_{*}p^{\frac{s}{2}})^{s-1}\geq\frac{n}{\log^{2}n}\cdot n^{\varepsilon s(s-1)/4}>n

for sufficiently large
n
.
Hence, by Janson’s inequality (e.g.,
[
21
, Theorem 2.14]
, originally proved in
[
20
]
),

\mathbb{P}\left[G_{n,p}[S]\not\supset K_{s}\right]=e^{-\Omega(n)}.

Taking the union bound over all possible choices of
S\subset V(G_{n,p})
with
|S|=n_{*}
, we have

\sum_{S}\mathbb{P}\left[K_{s}\not\subset G_{n,p}[S]\right]\leq\left(e\log^{2}n\right)^{n_{*}}e^{-\Omega(n)}=o(1),

which completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Using the Fractional Kahn–Kalai conjecture

We will apply the powerful result of Frankston, Kahn, Narayanan and Park
[
15
]
, which confirmed the fractional Kahn–Kalai conjecture. The full conjecture has been confirmed in 2022 by Park and Pham
[
31
]
. For an introduction to this ground-breaking tool, we recommend the recent survey of Perkins
[
32
]
.
See also
[
31
]
for the proof of the full conjecture.

Consider a finite ground set
Z
and fix a nonempty collection of subsets
{\mathcal{H}}\subset 2^{Z}
. For example,

can consist of the edges of the complete graph
K_{n}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
can be the collection of perfect matchings in

. The fractional Kahn–Kalai conjecture reduces the probabilistic problem of finding an element of

in a random subset of

{\mathcal{H}}
.

For

is called a

-spread
if for every set

(2.1)

\mu(\{A\in{\mathcal{H}}:A\supseteq S\})\leq q^{|S|}.

We write

for a random subset of

in which each element is included independently with probability

. It is not difficult to show (see e.g. Frankston et al.
[
15
]
) that the existence of a

-spread measure is necessary for

to contain an element of
{\mathcal{H}}
with probability, say,
\frac{1}{2}
. The following theorem shows that up to a logarithmic factor, it is also sufficient for finding w.h.p. an element of
{\mathcal{H}}
. We state the theorem in a packaged form from
[
33
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
15
]
,
[
33
]
)
.

There exists a constant

such that the following holds. Consider a non-empty ground set

and fix a non-empty collection of subsets

. Suppose that there exists a

-spread probability measure on

, and let

3.
Lower bound for the threshold probability

We will now prove Theorem
1.3
which gives lower bound for the threshold probability. The lower bound will be proved in more generality, for all
r=ms+t
with
0<t\leq s
and
m
an integer. Our task is to define a family of
n
-vertex graphs
G
satisfying
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)n
and such that for some constant
c
and
p\leq cp_{s}
the probability that the graph
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
contains a
K_{r}
-factor is
small
.

3.1.
The case
s=2

Fix
c<1
and let
p=p(n)=c\frac{\log n}{n}
. For
n
, a large multiple of
r
, let
G
be the complete
n
-vertex
\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil
-partite graph with
\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor
vertex classes of size
\frac{2n}{r}
and, if
r
is odd, one further class of size
\frac{n}{r}
. Clearly
\delta(G)=n-\frac{2n}{r}=\left(1-\frac{2}{r}\right)n
. Assume for contradiction that
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
has a
K_{r}
-factor
\mathcal{F}
. Let
A
be one of the vertex classes of size
\frac{2n}{r}
and for each clique
K\in\mathcal{F}
set

W(K)=|V(K)\cap A|-2.

Note that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{F}}W(K)=|A|-2|\mathcal{F}|=0.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}^{+}
the family of those cliques
K\in\mathcal{F}
for which
W(K)>0
, and by
\mathcal{F}^{-}
the family of those for which
W(K)<0
, and observe that

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{F}^{+}}|W(K)|=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{F}^{-}}|W(K)|.

The family
\mathcal{F}^{+}
consists of exactly those cliques
K
which have at least three vertices in
A
, hence they induce at least one copy of
K_{3}
in
G^{+}[A]
, and by definition of
G
all edges of such
K_{3}
need to come from the random graph
G_{n,p}
.

Let
X
denote the number of isolated vertices in
G_{n,p}[A]
. Then

\mathbb{E}X=\frac{2n}{r}\left(1-c\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\frac{2n}{r}-1}=\Theta\left(n^{1-\frac{2c}{r}}\right).

Hence, by Markov’s inequality, we get that
A
has at least
\Theta(n^{1-\frac{c}{r}})
isolated vertices in
G_{n,p}
. Each such vertex must be covered by some clique
K\in\mathcal{F}^{-}
. Thus

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{F}^{-}}W(K)\geq\Theta(n^{1-\frac{c}{r}}).

On the other hand, using again Markov’s inequality one can show that w.h.p. the number of copies of
K_{3}
in
G_{n,p}[A]
is at most
O(\log^{3}n)
and the number of copies of
K_{4}
in
G_{n,p}[A]
is
o(1)
, whence

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{F}^{+}}W(K)=O(\log^{3}n),

and we reach a contradiction.

3.2.
The case
3\leq s<r

For two graphs
F
and
H
let
\textup{Emb}(F,H)
denote the family of all embeddings from
F
to
H
, that is injective maps
\psi:V(F)\longrightarrow V(H)
which preserve adjacency (i.e. for any
x,y\in V(F)
,
xy\in E(F)
if and only if
\psi(x)\psi(y)\in E(H)
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For every integer
\ell>0
there are
L,n_{0}>0
such that for any
n\geq n_{0}
and
k
with
(\log n)^{2}\leq k<\frac{n}{3}
there exists a bipartite
n
-vertex graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq k
and such that

|\textup{Emb}(F,H)|\leq Ln^{v(F)}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{e(F)}

for any graph
F
with
v(F)\leq\ell
.

Proof.

Let
H
be a random bipartite graph
G_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2},\frac{3k}{n}}
. Then for any vertex
v
in
H
the expected number of edges incident with
v
is
\frac{3}{2}k
, and by Chernoff bound the probability that there is at most
k
edges incident with
v
is bounded from above by
exp\left(-\frac{k}{12}\right)
. Taking a union bound, we get that with probability
1-o(1)
we have
\delta(H)\geq k
.

Now let
F
be a graph with
v(F)\leq\ell
. Then

\mathbb{E}|\textup{Emb}(F,H)|=(n)_{v(F)}\left(\frac{3k}{n}\right)^{e(F)}\left(1-\frac{3k}{n}\right)^{{v(F)\choose 2}-e(F)}=O\left(n^{v(F)}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{e(F)}\right).

Taking
L
sufficiently large and using Markov’s inequality, we can ensure that with positive probability for each
F
with
v(F)\leq\ell
the number of embeddings in
H
is at most
Ln^{v(F)}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{e(F)}
.
∎

We will now construct the graph
G
. For a fixed
s
with
3\leq s<r
, let
n
be a large multiple of
r
and
k=n^{1-\varphi(s)}
. Let
V
be a set of size
n
, and
A\subset V
with
|A|=\frac{sn}{r}+k
. Let
G
be a graph on vertex set
V
obtained in the following way. For
H
, a bipartite graph given by Lemma
3.1
with
\ell,k,n
equal to, respectively,
s+1
,
n^{1-\varphi(s)}
and
\frac{sn}{r}+k
, we take
G
so that
G[A]
is isomorphic to
H
,
G[V\smallsetminus A]
is the complete graph and
G[A,V\smallsetminus A]
is the complete bipartite graph.
Then
G
satisfies the following conditions:

(G1)

{V\choose 2}\smallsetminus{A\choose 2}\subseteq E(G)
,

(G2)

\delta(G[A])\geq k
,

(G3)

G[A]
contains no copy of
K_{s+1}
, since
s\geq 3
and
H
is bipartite,

(G4)

if
F
is a graph with
v(F)=s+1
, then
|\textup{Emb}(F,G[A])|\leq Ln^{s+1}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{e(F)}
.

Clearly, by (G1) and (G2) we have
\delta(G)\geq k+|V\smallsetminus A|=n-\frac{s}{r}n=\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)n
. Take
L\gg r
at least as large as
L
given by Lemma
3.1
with
\ell=s+1
,
c\ll L^{-1}
,
and
p=cn^{-\varphi(s)}=\frac{ck}{n}
. Assume for contradiction that
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
has a
K_{r}
-factor
{\mathcal{F}}
. First notice that
G^{p}[A]
must contain at least
\Omega(k)
copies of
K_{s+1}
. Indeed, each copy of
K_{r}
in
{\mathcal{F}}
which doesn’t yield a copy of
K_{s+1}
in
G^{p}[A]
can cover at most
s
vertices in
A
and thus have to cover at least
r-s
vertices in
V\smallsetminus A
. Therefore, the number of vertices in
A
covered by such copies of
K_{r}
is at most

\frac{s|V\smallsetminus A|}{r-s}=\frac{sn}{r}-\frac{sk}{r-s}.
The remaining at least
\frac{kr}{r-s}
vertices must belong to at least one copy of
K_{s+1}
in
G^{p}[A]
, which give us at least
\frac{kr}{(r-s)(s+1)}
such copies. Next, each of them arises as the union of some graph
F\neq K_{s+1}
,
v(F)=s+1
, and
{s+1\choose 2}-e(F)>0
random edges from
G_{n,p}
. However, the number of copies of
K_{s+1}
in
G^{p}[A]
arising in this way has expectation bounded from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{F}|\textup{Emb}(F,G[A])|\,p^{{s+1\choose 2}-e(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F}Ln^{s+1}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{e(F)}\left(\frac{ck}{n}\right)^{{s+1\choose 2}-e(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F}cLn^{s+1}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)^{{s+1\choose 2}}\leq 2^{{s+1\choose 2}}cLn^{s+1-{s+1\choose 2}\varphi(s)}.

Note that

s+1-{s+1\choose 2}\varphi(s)=(s+1)\left(1-\frac{s^{2}}{(s-1)(s+2)}\right)=\frac{s^{2}-s-2}{(s-1)(s+2)=1-\varphi(s)},

hence the above upper bound is equal to

2^{{s+1\choose 2}}cLn^{1-\varphi(s)}=2^{{s+1\choose 2}}cLk.

Finally, by Markov’s inequality, with probability at least
1-\frac{1}{L}
, there are at most
2^{{s+1\choose 2}}cL^{2}k
copies of
K_{s+1}
in
G^{p}[A]
. Taking
c
sufficiently small with respect to
L
, we can make this number smaller than
\frac{kr}{(r-s)(s+1)}
, getting a contradiction. Thus, taking
L=1/\varepsilon
(and
c\ll L^{-1}
) the probability that
G^{p}
has a
K_{r}
-factor is at most
\varepsilon
, which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

4.
The proof of the main result – upper bound for the threshold probability

Recall that we wish to construct a
K_{r}
-factor in
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
, where
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
n
a large multiple of
r
,

\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{s}{r}\right)n\ \ \text{ and }\ \ p=Cp_{s}(n).

In order to prove the main result we will distinguish two cases for the graph

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and let
\gamma,\beta
be positive constants satisfying
8\gamma<\beta<\frac{1}{5}(1-\alpha)
. If

, then one of the following holds in

.

(a)

There is a partition
A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{2}=V(G)

(i)

for

,

(ii)

for
x\in A_{2}
,
|N_{G}(x)\cap A_{1}|\geq\beta n
,

(iii)

there are at most
\gamma n^{2}
missing edges between

and
A_{2}
.

(b)

Every

with

satisfies

.

We will refer to the two cases above as, respectively,

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gamma>0
, and let
G
be a graph with minimum degree at least
(1-\frac{s}{r})n
in which every set of size
sn/r
contains at least
\gamma n^{2}
edges. For sufficiently large
C
and

, w.h.p. the graph

has a
K_{r}
-factor.

In the extremal case, we will construct the factor with copies of
K_{r}
going across the partition
A_{1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}A_{2}

and

vertices in

. However, when

we will additionally need to find some copies of

in

, which is stated in the following lemma.
Note that this lemma is actually the bottleneck for the threshold probability

when

, as indicated by the extremal construction in Section

Lemma 4.3
.

For all

, there are constants

.

.

in

, each of them having at least
\beta n/2
common neighbours in
A_{1}
. Recall that
G^{p}[A_{2}]
has minimum degree at least
|g|
.

Let
Z=\{v\in A_{2}:|A_{1}\smallsetminus N_{G}(v)|\geq\beta n/(2s)\}
. Note that
|Z|\leq 2s/\beta\cdot\gamma n^{2}
, since otherwise there are too many missing edges between
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. Denote
Z=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\}
. If
\ell\geq|g|
, then we may greedily find
|g|
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
using property (*), where each copy uses exactly one vertex from
Z
. To see this, note that at the point of processing a vertex
z\in Z
, its number of neighbours in
G[A_{2}]
outside of
Z
and the previously chosen cliques is at least

\frac{\beta n}{2s}-|Z|(s+1)\geq n\left(\frac{\beta}{2s}-\frac{2s(s+1)\gamma}{\beta}\right)\geq\frac{\beta n}{4s},

so (*) can indeed be applied.
If
\ell<|g|
, we first apply Lemma
4.3
to the graph
G[A_{2}\smallsetminus Z]
to obtain
|g|-\ell
copies of
K_{s+1}
disjoint from
Z
, call them
{\mathcal{C}}
. Then we use the same greedy argument as in the case
\ell\geq g
(noting that
|V({\mathcal{C}})|\leq|g|(s+1)\leq\gamma(s+1)n\leq\beta n/(8s)
) to complete the collection
{\mathcal{C}}
to
{\mathcal{C}}_{g}
.

For each copy of
K_{s+1}
using at most one vertex from
Z
, the number of neighbours in
A_{1}
is at least
\beta n-s\cdot\beta n/(2s)>\beta n/2
. Now, as in the previous case, we can use property (*) to extend
{\mathcal{C}}_{g}
to a
K_{r}
-factor in
G^{p}[A_{1}\cup A_{2}]
, since the total number of vertices in
A_{1}
needed for the extension is
|g|(s-1)\leq\beta n/(8s).

∎

Let
{\mathcal{C}}^{+}
be the resulting collection of
|g|\leq\gamma n
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{r}
.

Let
A_{i}^{\prime}=A_{i}\smallsetminus V({\mathcal{C}}^{+})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, and
G^{\prime}=G[A_{1}^{\prime}\cup A_{2}^{\prime}]
. There are still at most
\gamma n^{2}
missing edges (in
G^{\prime}
) between
A_{1}^{\prime}
and
A_{2}^{\prime}
, and, since
\gamma\ll\beta
, any vertex has at least
\frac{\beta n}{2}
neighbours in the opposite part. In particular,
(A_{1}^{\prime},A_{2}^{\prime})
is a
(\sqrt{\gamma},1,\beta/2)
-super-regular pair.
Note that
n^{\prime}=\frac{|A_{1}^{\prime}\cup A_{2}^{\prime}|}{r}
is an integer, and, since
g=|A_{1}|-\frac{sn}{r}
, we have

\displaystyle t|A_{1}^{\prime}|-s|A_{2}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=t|A_{1}|-s|A_{2}|-t|g|(s+\sigma)+s|g|(t-\sigma)

\displaystyle=t\left(\frac{sn}{r}+g-|g|(s+\sigma)\right)-s\left(\frac{tn}{r}-g-|g|(t-\sigma)\right)=0,

so
|A_{1}^{\prime}|=sn^{\prime}
and
|A_{2}^{\prime}|=tn^{\prime}
. Hence, we can apply Lemma
4.4
to find a
K_{r}
-factor in
G^{\prime}\cup G_{n,p}[A_{1}^{\prime}\cup A_{2}^{\prime}]
, as required.
∎

5.
The extremal vs. non-extremal case distinction

In this section we prove Lemma
4.1
, which distinguishes the two cases for the deterministic graph
G
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Suppose that (b) does not hold and let
X\subset V(G)
be such that
|X|=(1-\alpha)n
and
e_{G}(X)<\gamma^{2}n^{2}
. If (i), (ii), and (iii) hold with
A_{1}=X
then we are done. Hence suppose this is not the case and let
Y=V(G)\smallsetminus X
. Let
U\subseteq X
consist of all vertices
x
for which
|Y\smallsetminus N_{G}(x)|>2\beta n
. Suppose that
|U|\geq\gamma n/8
. Then, since
\delta(G)\geq\alpha n
,
|Y|=\alpha n
and
\beta>8\gamma
, we get

\displaystyle e_{G}(X)\geq|U|\cdot\beta n\geq\frac{\beta\gamma n^{2}}{8}>\gamma^{2}n^{2},

a contradiction. Hence

\displaystyle\xi:=\frac{|U|}{n}<\gamma/8.

Set

\displaystyle X_{1}:=X\smallsetminus U\ \ \text{ and }\ \ Y_{1}:=Y\cup U,

with

\displaystyle|X_{1}|=(1-\alpha-\xi)n\ \ \text{ and }\ \ |Y_{1}|=(\alpha+\xi)n,

and note that
X_{1}
satisfies (i) as for any
x\in X_{1}
we have

\displaystyle|Y_{1}\smallsetminus N_{G}(x)|\leq 2\beta n+|U|\leq 4\beta n.

Next, let
W\subset Y_{1}
consists of all vertices
x
for which
|N_{G}(x)\cap X_{1}|<\beta n
. We will estimate the number of edges between
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
. On one hand, since
e_{G}(X_{1})\leq e_{G}(X)\leq\gamma^{2}n^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle e_{G}(X_{1},Y_{1})\geq|X_{1}|\alpha n-2\gamma^{2}n^{2}=(1-\alpha-\xi)\alpha n^{2}-2\gamma^{2}n^{2},

and on the other hand

\displaystyle e_{G}(X_{1},Y_{1})

\displaystyle\leq|W|\beta n+|Y_{1}\smallsetminus W||X_{1}|=|W|(\beta n-|X_{1}|)+|X_{1}||Y_{1}|

\displaystyle=-|W|(1-\alpha-\xi-\beta)n+(1-\alpha-\xi)(\alpha+\xi)n^{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|W|\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha-\xi-\beta}\left((1-\alpha-\xi)\xi+2\gamma^{2}\right)n\leq\left(2\xi+\frac{\gamma}{4}\right)n\leq\frac{\gamma n}{2},

using the fact that the function
\frac{1-\alpha-\xi}{1-\alpha-\xi-\beta}
is increasing in
\xi
, and
32\xi<\beta<\frac{1}{5}(1-\alpha)
.
Set

\displaystyle X_{2}:=X_{1}\cup W\ \ \text{ and }\ \ Y_{2}:=Y_{1}\smallsetminus W.

We claim that
X_{2}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}Y_{2}
is the desired partition for (a) with
X_{2}
playing the role of
A_{1}
. First notice that

\displaystyle\left(1-\alpha-\gamma\right)n\leq|X_{2}|\leq(1-\alpha-\xi)n+\frac{\gamma n}{2}\leq(1-\alpha+\gamma)n.

Next, it is easy to see that
Y_{2}
satisfies (ii). Moreover,
X_{2}
satisfies (i) as for vertices in
X_{1}
shifting vertices from
W
to
X_{1}
cannot increase the number of non-neighbours on the other side, and for any
x\in W
we have

\displaystyle|Y_{2}\cap N_{G}(x)|\geq(\alpha-\beta)n-|W|,

hence

\displaystyle|Y_{2}\smallsetminus N_{G}(x)|\leq|Y_{2}|-(\alpha-\beta)n+|W|=|Y_{1}|-(\alpha-\beta)n=(\xi+\beta)n\leq 2\beta n.

Finally, to show (iii) notice that since
e_{G}(X)<\gamma^{2}n^{2}
, there are at most
2\gamma^{2}n^{2}
missing edges between
X
and
Y
. When we shift
U
from
X
to
Y
the number of missing edges between two parts can increase by at most
|U||X\smallsetminus U|=\xi(1-\alpha-\xi)n^{2}\leq\gamma(1-\alpha)n^{2}/8
, and when we shift
W
from
Y_{1}
to
X_{1}
we can gain at most
|W||Y_{1}\smallsetminus W|\leq\gamma(\alpha+\xi-\gamma/2)n^{2}/2
additional missing edges between two parts. Hence in total we get at most

2\gamma^{2}n^{2}+\frac{\gamma(1-\alpha)n^{2}}{8}+\frac{\gamma(2\alpha+2\xi-\gamma)n^{2}}{4}\leq\gamma n^{2}\left(\frac{1}{20}+\frac{1}{8}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\gamma n^{2}

missing edges between
X_{2}
and
Y_{2}
.
∎

6.
Many disjoint cliques

In this section we prove Lemma
4.3
. Recall that for specific constants
\beta
and
C
our task is to find a collection of
g
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
in a randomly perturbed graph
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
with

\delta(G)=g\leq\beta n\ \ \text{ and }\ \ p=Cp_{s}(n).

Throughout the proof we set
V=V(G)
.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

For
s=2
, the conclusion follows from Theorem 1.4 in
[
10
]
. (We remark that our argument also applies to this case, but the calculations would have to be redone for
s=2
).

Let
s\geq 3
and choose constants
C
,
\beta
and
\delta
so that

\beta\ll\delta\ll C^{-2s^{2}}.

With high probability
G_{n,p}
contains at least
\log^{2}n
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
(which can be shown similarly as Lemma
2.1
). Thus we may assume that
g\geq\log^{2}n
.

We will first show that we may also assume that the maximum degree of our graph is bounded from above by
\delta n
.

Claim 6.1
.

Assume that the conclusion holds in case
\Delta(G)\leq\delta n.
Then it holds in general.

Proof.

Let
X=\{x\in V(G):d_{G}(x)>\delta n/2\}
, and let
X_{1}\subset X
be a set satisfying
|X_{1}|=\min(|X|,g)
. Denote
g_{1}=g-|X_{1}|
. We claim that the graph
G^{p}[V\smallsetminus X_{1}]
contains
g_{1}
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
. Indeed, when
|X_{1}|=g
then
g_{1}=0
and there is nothing to show. Hence suppose that
|X_{1}|=|X|<g
. Then the graph
G-X_{1}
satisfies the hypothesis of the Claim with
g_{1}
instead of
g
. Indeed, since
|X_{1}|<g\leq\beta n
, we have
n_{*}:=|V\smallsetminus X_{1}|\geq n/2
. Moreover, the minimum degree of
G-X_{1}
is at least
g-|X_{1}|=g_{1}
, and for the maximum degree of
G-X_{1}
we have

\Delta(G-X_{1})\leq\delta n/2\leq\delta n_{*}.

Thus
G^{p}[V\smallsetminus X_{1}]
contains
g_{1}
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
.

Now, for every
x\in X_{1}
, we may greedily choose a further copy of
K_{s+1}
containing
x
, as follows. When processing
x
, at most
(s+1)g\leq(s+1)\beta n\leq\delta n/10
vertices from
V\smallsetminus X_{1}
have already been used. Hence
N_{G}(x)\smallsetminus X_{1}
has at least
\frac{\delta n}{2}-g-\frac{\delta n}{10}\geq\delta n/4
unused vertices; by Lemma
2.1
this set contains a copy of
K_{s}
in
G_{n,p}
, which forms the desired copy of
K_{s+1}
together with
x
.
∎

Claim 6.2
.

The conclusion holds if
g\geq n/(10s\log n)
.

Proof.

Let
g\geq n/(10s\log n)
, and we may assume that
\Delta(G)\leq 10(s+1)g
.
Similarly to the argument above, we will greedily choose vertex-disjoint
K_{s+1}
-copies in
G\cup G_{n,p}
as follows. Suppose that we have chosen
j\leq g-1
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
which cover a vertex set
J
with
|J|=(s+1)j
. Assuming
j\leq g-1\leq\frac{n}{100s(s+1)}
, the graph
G-J
has at least

\frac{ng}{2}-\Delta(G)(s+1)j\geq\frac{ng}{4}

edges, and thus contains a vertex
x
of degree at least
\frac{g}{2}\geq\frac{n}{20s\log n}
. By the conclusion of Lemma
2.1
, the set
N_{G}(x)\smallsetminus J
contains an
s
-clique in
G_{n,p}
, which forms a desired copy of
K_{s+1}
together with
x
.
∎

To finish the proof, we show that the conclusion holds for
\delta(G)=g<n/(10s\log n)
and
\Delta(G)\leq\delta n
. Take a partition
A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}D
such that
|A|=|B|=|D|=\frac{n}{3}
, and for
v\in A
,
N_{G}(v)\cap B\geq\frac{g}{5}
; a random partition has this property with positive probability. Let
G^{\prime}\subset G
be a bipartite subgraph on
A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B
in which each
v\in A
has exactly
\frac{g}{5}
neighbours in
B
, so
e(G^{\prime})=\frac{gn}{15}
.

Claim 6.3
.

G^{\prime}
has at most
\delta gn^{2}
cherries (that is, triples
uvw
with
uv,vw\in E(G^{\prime})
).

Proof.

There are at most
\frac{n}{3}\left(\frac{g}{5}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{75}\beta gn^{2}\leq\frac{1}{75}\delta gn^{2}
cherries with a centre in
A
. Furthermore, for cherries
uvw
with a centre
v\in B
, there are at most
\frac{n}{3}
choices for
u\in A
,
\frac{g}{5}
further choices for its neighbour
v
, and
\Delta(G^{\prime})\leq\delta n
choices for
w
, which yields a total upper bound of
\delta gn^{2}
.
∎

We will now construct an auxiliary graph
(W,F)
consisting of potential copies of
K_{s+1}
intersecting
A\cup B
in a single edge of
G^{\prime}
, that is,

W=\left\{K\in{V\choose s+1}:K\smallsetminus D\in E(G^{\prime})\right\}\text{ \quad and \quad}F=\left\{KL\in{W\choose 2}:K\cap L\neq\varnothing\right\}.

We have
|W|=\kappa gn^{s}
, where the constant
\kappa
depends only on
s
. Let
(\tilde{W},\tilde{F})
be the random induced subgraph of
(W,F)
defined by

\tilde{W}=\left\{K\in W:{K\choose 2}\smallsetminus E(G^{\prime})\subset G_{n,p}\right\}.

We will show that w.h.p.

(6.1)

|\tilde{W}|\geq 15g\text{\quad and \quad}\left|\tilde{F}\right|\leq 3g,

so we may delete one element of each pair in
\tilde{F}
and obtain
10g
independent vertices of
\tilde{W}
, corresponding to
10g
vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{s+1}
in
G^{\prime}\cup G_{n,p}
.

Using the fact that

(6.2)

n^{s}p_{s}^{\binom{s+1}{2}-1}=n^{s-\frac{2s}{(s-1)(s+2)}\cdot\frac{s^{2}+s-2}{2}}=1,

and denoting
C^{\prime}=C^{\binom{s+1}{2}-1}
, we have

(6.3)

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{W}|=|W|p^{\binom{s+1}{2}-1}=\kappa gC^{\prime}\geq 30g,

provided
C=C(s)
is large enough.

We will now show that w.h.p.

(6.4)

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{F}|\leq 2g,

and later we will argue that this also suffices for an application of Janson’s
Inequality to
\tilde{W}
.
Let
F_{i,j}\subset F
,
1\leq i\leq s
and
0\leq j\leq 2
, be the set of pairs
KL\in{W\choose 2}
with
|K\cap L|=i
and
|(K\cap L)\smallsetminus D|=j
. Then

(6.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|F_{i,0}[\tilde{W}]|

\displaystyle\leq(gn)^{2}\cdot n^{2s-i-2}\cdot p^{2{s+1\choose 2}-2-{i\choose 2}}\leq(C^{\prime})^{2}g^{2}n^{-i}p^{-{i\choose 2}}\leq(C^{\prime})^{2}\delta gn^{1-i}p^{-{i\choose 2}},

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|F_{i,1}[\tilde{W}]|

\displaystyle\leq\delta gn^{2}\cdot n^{2s-i-1}\cdot p^{2{s+1\choose 2}-2-{i\choose 2}}\leq(C^{\prime})^{2}\delta gn^{1-i}p^{-{i\choose 2}},

(6.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|F_{i,2}[\tilde{W}]|

\displaystyle\leq gn\cdot n^{2s-i}\cdot p^{2{s+1\choose 2}-1-{i\choose 2}}\leq(C^{\prime})^{2}gn^{1-i}p^{1-{i\choose 2}},

where the middle inequality (case
j=1
) is obtained using Claim
6.3
.
Putting

F_{1}=F_{1,0}\cup F_{1,1}

and

F_{2}=\left(\bigcup_{2\leq i\leq s}F_{i,0}\cup F_{i,1}\cup F_{i,2}\right)

we get

provided
\delta
is sufficiently small with respect to
C^{-1}
. Therefore (
6.4
) holds.

To apply Janson’s inequality to
\tilde{W}
, note that

\Delta:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{KL\in F,K\cap L\neq\varnothing}\mathbb{P}\left[K\in\tilde{W}\land L\in\tilde{W}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}|\tilde{F}|\leq 2g.

Recalling that
\mathbb{E}|\tilde{W}|\geq 30g
for sufficiently large
C=C(s)
, by Janson’s inequality we get that w.h.p.

(6.8)

|\tilde{W}|\geq 15g,

which covers the first part of (
6.1
).

To prove the second part, it is enough to show that w.h.p. we also have
|\tilde{F}_{1}|\leq 2g
. To this end we estimate
\textrm{Var}\left[|\tilde{F}_{1}|\right]
. Let

I_{KL}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }KL\in\tilde{F},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then

|\tilde{F}_{1}|=\sum_{|K\cap L|=1}I_{KL}

and

(6.9)

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}\left[|\tilde{F}_{1}|\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{|K\cap L|=1}\sum_{|K^{\prime}\cap L^{\prime}|=1}\textrm{Cov}\left(I_{KL},I_{K^{\prime}L^{\prime}}.\right)

For pairs
KL
,
K^{\prime}L^{\prime}
in
\tilde{F}
which do not share a random edge,
\textrm{Cov}\left(I_{KL},I_{K^{\prime}L^{\prime}}\right)=0
. Hence each non-zero term in the sum in (
6.9
) corresponds to pairs
KL
,
K^{\prime}L^{\prime}
having at least 2 vertices in common. Moreover, similarly to (
6.5
), this expression is dominated by quadruples with the number of common vertices as small as possible, hence 2, sharing exactly one random edge and no deterministic edges. Thus

\displaystyle\textrm{Var}\left[|\tilde{F}_{1}|\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+o(1))gn^{4}n^{4(s-1)-1}p^{4{s+1\choose 2}-5}=(1+o(1))g(np)^{-1}=o(g)

By Chebyshev’s inequality, w.h.p.
|\tilde{F}_{1}|\leq 2g
and thus also
|\tilde{F}|\leq 3g
, which finishes the proof.
∎

7.
Factors in regular partitions

In this section we apply the powerful result from
[
15
]
, which confirmed the fractional version of the famous Kahn–Kalai Conjecture. See Section

for the relevant definitions and theorems.

7.1.
Covering small vertex sets

The following lemma will be important in the proof of Lemma
4.2
. Its proof is a simple application of Theorem
2.2
, although there probably are other arguments.
Before we proceed, let us define the graph
B_{m,s,t}
to be the complete
(m+1)
-partite graph with
m
classes of size
s
, and one class of size
t\leq s
. In particular,
|V(B_{m,s,t})|=ms+t=r
. Moreover, for a graph
G
, a vertex
x\in V(G)
and a subset
Y\subset V(G)
, let
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
be a collection of
B_{m,s,t}
-copies in
G
containing
x
in a class of size
t
and let
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}[V(G)\smallsetminus Y]
denote the members of
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
disjoint from
Y
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\varepsilon,\nu\in(0,1/2)
,
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, and
X\subset V(G)
with
\chi:=|X|\leq\varepsilon n
. Assume that for all
x\in X
and
Y\subset V(G)\smallsetminus\{x\}
of size at most

,
|{\mathcal{B}}_{x}[V(G)\smallsetminus Y]|\geq\nu n^{ms+t-1}
. For sufficiently large
C=C(\nu)
, and
p=Cn^{-2/s}(\log n)^{2/(s(s-1))}
, the graph
G^{p}=G\cup G_{n,p}
contains a family
{\mathcal{F}}=\{\tilde{F}_{x}:x\in X\}
of vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{r}
such that for each
x\in X
,
\tilde{F}_{x}
contains an element of
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
. (In particular,
x
is covered by a partial
K_{r}
-factor
{\mathcal{F}}
.)

Proof.

We assume that
t=s
, as otherwise we may add an auxiliary set of
n
vertices
W
fully connected to vertices in
G
, and consider
B_{m,s,t+(s-t)}
-copies with
s-t
vertices in
W
.

Our ground set will be
Z=K_{n}^{(s)}
, the complete
s
-uniform hypergraph. A hypergraph
H\subset Z
is called

if
|H|=(m+1)|X|
and there is a collection of vertex disjoint graphs
\{F_{x}:x\in X\}
with

in
{\mathcal{B}}_{x}
such that
\partial H\cup\bigcup_{x\in X}{F}_{x}
is the desired collection of vertex-disjoint
K_{r}
-copies. Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be the collection of useful hypergraphs. For a graph
F
isomorphic to
B_{m,s,s}
, we write
\kappa_{s}(F)
for the
s
-uniform hypergraph consisting of
m+1
independent sets of size
s
in
F
. Recall that
Z_{\pi}
is the random subhypergraph of

where each
s
-edge is included independently with probability
\pi
.

Claim 7.2
.

With high probability,
Z_{\pi}
with
\pi=Cn^{-(s-1)}\log n
contains an element of
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

We will construct an
O(n^{-s+1})
-spread measure
\mu
on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
Y_{0}=\varnothing
. For steps

, we choose a random element

of

, and a random element

of
{\mathcal{B}}_{x_{j}}[V(G)\smallsetminus Y_{j-1}]

to be

. (In this case, we say that

or its subset is

\chi

of

, and

is the probability measure on

defined by this process.
To see that the hypergraph

is indeed in

(i.e., that it is useful), note that

is a partial

-factor covering

. (We remark that the randomisation over

is done to make the following spread computation more symmetric by avoiding the special rôle of
s
-sets intersecting

.)

To verify that
\mu
is a spread measure, consider
S\subseteq Z=K_{n}^{(s)}
. To bound the probability that
H_{\mu}=\bigcup_{j\in[\chi]}\kappa_{s}({F}_{j})
contains
S
, suppose that this is the case, so for each element
e\in S
, there is an index
j=j(e)
such that
\kappa_{s}(F_{j})
contains
e
, and let
\mathcal{J}
be the set of all indices

for
e\in S
. (We remark that
|\mathcal{J}|\leq|S|
, but the inequality is strict if

for some distinct

.) The number of potential choices for the indices

and the configuration
(x_{j},F_{j}):j\in\mathcal{J}
is at most
\chi^{|\mathcal{J}|}n^{r|\mathcal{J}|-s|S|}
. On the other hand, each such configuration
(x_{j},F_{j}):j\in\mathcal{J}
occurs with probability at most

\frac{1}{\chi(\chi-1)\dots(\chi-|\mathcal{J}|+1)}\cdot\left(\nu n^{r-1}\right)^{-|\mathcal{J}|}\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{2e}\cdot\chi n^{r-1}\right)^{-|\mathcal{J}|},

where the first term stands for the probability that the elements of

are placed ‘correctly’, and the second term comes from
|{\mathcal{B}}_{x_{j}}\smallsetminus Y_{j-1}|\geq\nu n^{r-1}
for all

.

Putting these bounds together, we get that the random hypergraph

contains

with probability at most

\chi^{|\mathcal{J}|}n^{r|\mathcal{J}|-s|S|}\left(\frac{\nu}{2e}\cdot\chi n^{r-1}\right)^{-|\mathcal{J}|}\leq A^{|\mathcal{J}|}n^{|\mathcal{J}|-s|S|}\leq(An^{-s+1})|S|,

where

, as required.

Now, Theorem
2.2
implies that if
\pi=C^{\prime}n^{-s+1}\log n
for large

,

contains a member of
{\mathcal{H}}
with high probability.
∎

For
s=2
, this completes the proof. For

, Lemma

implies that, with high probability, a random graph

with

contains
\partial H
for some

. By definition of
{\mathcal{H}}
, this graph

along with the edges of

gives the desired partial
K_{r}
-factor
{\mathcal{F}}
.

∎

containing a neighbour of

Lemma 8.5
.

For
1\gg\delta\gg 1/n\gg 0
, if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph such that each vertex is in at least
\delta n^{g-1}
copies of
K_{g}
, then

contains a
(\delta,g)
-absorber.

Proof.

For each vertex
v\in V(G)
choose a family
{\mathcal{K}}_{v}
of
\delta n^{g-1}
copies of
K_{g}
containing
v
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}=\bigcup_{v}{\mathcal{K}}_{v}
and
\alpha
be such that
|{\mathcal{K}}|=\alpha n^{g}
, with
\frac{\delta}{g}\leq\alpha\leq\delta
. Next, let
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
be a random subfamily of
{\mathcal{K}}
containing each element of
{\mathcal{K}}
independently with probability
\frac{\delta}{2\alpha g}n^{1-g}\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{1-g}
. Then
\mathbb{E}(|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}|)=\frac{\delta}{2g}n
and, by Chebyshev’s inequality, with probability

we have
\frac{\delta}{4g}n\leq|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{\delta}{g}n
, and the copies of
K_{g}
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
cover at most
\delta n
vertices.

Next, notice that, since each vertex belongs to at least
\delta n^{g-1}
copies of
K_{g}
, we have
\delta(G)\geq\delta n
. Therefore, there are at least
\delta n\cdot\frac{\delta}{g}n^{g-1}=\frac{\delta^{2}}{g}n^{g}
unique copies of
K_{g}
containing at least one neighbour of
v
. Denote by
{\mathcal{K}}_{N(v)}
any such family of
K_{g}
-copies of size exactly
\frac{\delta^{2}}{g}n^{g}
, let
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{N(v)}={\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{K}}_{N(v)}
and note that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{N(v)}
is binomially distributed with parameters
\frac{\delta^{2}}{g}n^{g}
and
\frac{\delta}{2\alpha g}n^{1-g}
. We have
\mathbb{E}(|{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{N(v)}|)=\frac{\delta^{3}}{2\alpha g^{2}}n\geq\frac{\delta^{2}}{2g^{2}}n
. By Chernoff bounds, the probability that
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{N(v)}<\frac{\delta^{2}}{3g^{2}}n
is at most

for a proper constant
\beta=\beta(g,\delta)
. Thus, taking the union bound over all the vertices of

, with probability

there are at least
\frac{\delta^{2}}{3g^{2}}n
copies of
K_{g}
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
containing a neighbour of

.

Next, we want to make
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
pairwise disjoint. Let

denote the number of intersecting pairs in
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and note that
\mathbb{E}Z\leq n(\delta n^{g-1})^{2}\left(\frac{\delta}{2\alpha g}n^{1-g}\right)^{2}=\frac{\delta^{4}}{4\alpha^{2}g^{2}}n\leq\frac{\delta^{2}}{4g^{2}}n
. Therefore, by Markov’s inequality, with probability bounded away from 0, the number of intersecting pairs does not exceed
\frac{7\delta^{2}}{24g^{2}}n
. Let us remove one copy of

from each intersecting pair and denote by

the remaining family. Then, with positive probability,

satisfies the definition of a

-absorber, hence we can find in

∎

When
t<s
, the graph

will be used in the reduced graph as an auxiliary object used to obtain a set of regular pairs
(S_{i},T_{i})
with
|S_{i}|/|T_{i}|\sim s/t
. To formalise this, we will use the concept of weighted graphs (with weighted vertices) and their embeddings.

A
weighted graph
is a pair
(F,w_{F})
, where
F
is a graph and
w_{F}:V(F)\longrightarrow\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
is a weight function. Any graph
F
can be viewed as a weighted graph with
w_{F}\equiv 1
.
For a family of weighted graphs
{\mathcal{F}}
, an
{\mathcal{F}}
-packing
in
G
is a collection of graphs
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
F_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}
, with corresponding embeddings
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
into
G
, such that for
x\in V(G)
,

\sum_{i\in I:Im(f_{i})\ni x}w_{F_{i}}(f_{i}^{-1}(x))\leq 1.

In other words, for each
x\in V(G)
the total weight in
(F_{i})_{i\in I}
of all vertices mapped to
x
is at most 1.

The
residue
of a packing is defined as
|V(G)|-\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{v\in V(F_{i})}w_{F_{i}}(v)
. Let
K_{2}^{*}
denote the weighted graph consisting of two vertices
\sigma
and
\tau
with an edge
\sigma\tau
, and vertex weights
w(\sigma)=\frac{1}{t(s+t)}
and
w(\tau)=\frac{1}{s(s+t)}
(so that
w(\sigma)/w(\tau)=s/t
).

For example, the graph
K_{2}
with vertices
x
and
y
(with vertex weights 1) has a
K_{2}^{*}
-packing consisting of
2st
copies of
K_{2}^{*}
:
st
copies
F_{i}
with
f_{i}(\sigma)=x
and
f_{i}(\tau)=y
, and
st
copies
F_{j}
with
f_{j}(\tau)=x
and
f_{j}(\sigma)=y
. The residue of this packing is 0. We will use the following simple lemma establishing the same statement for
Q
.

Lemma 8.7
.

The graph
Q
has a
K_{2}^{*}
-packing with residue 0 such that for each copy of
K_{2}^{*}
in the packing, its vertex
\tau
is mapped into the
M
-part of
Q
.

Proof.

We begin by enumerating the vertices of
Q
as
L=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{s-t}\}
,
M=\{m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\}
and
N=\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{t}\}
so that each
m_{i}
is matched to
n_{i}
in
Q
. Let
(s+t)\ltimes K_{2}^{*}
be the weighted graph on
\{\sigma,\tau\}
with weight function
w^{\prime}(\sigma)=1/t
and
w^{\prime}(\tau)=1/s
. We will actually exhibit an
(s+t)\ltimes K_{2}^{*}
-packing of
Q
, which can easily be turned into a
K_{2}^{*}
-packing by partitioning each

-copy into
(s+t)
copies of
K_{2}^{*}
.

First for each
j\in[t]
, we embed
t

(s+t)\ltimes K_{2}^{*}
by mapping
\sigma
to
n_{j}
and
\tau
to
m_{j}
. Hence the total weight mapped to

is 1, and to
m_{j}
is
\frac{t}{s}
.

Then, consider the vertex
\ell_{1}
. Embed
t
copies of
(s+t)\ltimes K_{2}^{*}
by mapping
\sigma
to
\ell_{1}
and
\tau
to

, for

. The total weight mapped to
\ell_{1}
is now also 1. Repeat the same for
\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{s-t}
.

Finally, notice that for
j\in[t]
, the total weight mapped to each vertex

is

\frac{t}{s}+\frac{s-t}{s}=1.
This completes the proof.
∎

Step 1:

Lemma 9.1
.

Let

\gamma n^{2}

. There is a collection
(S_{i},T_{i})_{i\in[\ell]}
of
(\varepsilon,d^{\prime},d)

(i)

For every

(ii)

(iii)

Given

After applying Lemma

to get a collection

.
First, using Lemma

Let

be the super-regular pairs leftover after covering

(i)

is

-copies. We now state the necessary probabilistic lemma.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
s
and
t^{\prime}\leq s-1
be integers, and
\nu>0
. Let
A,B
be disjoint vertex sets of order
n
, and let

be a bipartite graph on
A\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}B
containing at least
\nu n^{s-1+t^{\prime}}
copies of
K_{s-1,t^{\prime}}
. Let
W
be an
n
-vertex set (which may intersect
A
and
B
). For
V=A\cup B\cup W
,
p=Cn^{-2/s}
and

sufficiently large, the probability that there is no
K_{r}
-copy in
G\cup G(V,p)
with one vertex in
W
,
s-1
vertices in
A
and
t^{\prime}
vertices in
B
is at most
e^{-c_{s}\nu^{2}n^{2}p}
, where
c_{s}
is a constant depending on
s
.

Proof.

We may assume that
W
is disjoint from
A
and
B
, by taking sets
A^{\prime}=A\smallsetminus W
,
B^{\prime}=B\smallsetminus W
,
W^{\prime}=W\smallsetminus(A\cup B)
and assigning each vertex
v\in W\cap A
to
W^{\prime}
or
A^{\prime}
uniformly at random, and similarly each vertex
v\in W\cap B
to
W^{\prime}
or
B^{\prime}
, and then working with smaller sets
W^{\prime}
,
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
. Moreover, we may assume that
t^{\prime}=s-1
by adding to
B
a set
B^{\prime\prime}
of
n
auxiliary vertices which are adjacent to every vertex of
A
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the set of triples
(w,S_{A},S_{B})
with
S_{A}\subset A
,
S_{B}\subset B
,
|S_{A}|=|S_{B}|=s-1
, and such that
(S_{A},S_{B})
spans a complete bipartite subgraph in
G
. For a triple
\mathbf{S}\in\mathcal{S}
, let
I_{\mathbf{S}}
be the indicator random variable of the event that
\mathbf{S}
induces a copy of
K_{2s-1}
in
G\cup G(V,p)
.

Denote

M=n^{2(s-1)}p^{{2(s-1)}+2\binom{s-1}{2}}=\left(np^{1+\frac{s-2}{2}}\right)^{2(s-1)}=\left(np^{\frac{s}{2}}\right)^{2(s-1)}=C^{s(s-1)}.

For some constant
c_{s}^{\prime}
depending only on
s
, we have

(9.5)

\lambda:=\sum_{\mathbf{S}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}\right]\geq c_{s}^{\prime}\nu nM.

Now, in order to use Janson’s Inequality, let us bound the quantity

\bar{\Delta}:=\sum_{\mathbf{S}}\sum_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\sim\mathbf{S}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\right],

where (as indicated by the relation ‘
\sim
’) the second sum is taken over
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
such that
I_{\mathbf{S}}
and
I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}
are
not
independent.
For
\mathbf{S}=(w,S_{A},S_{B})
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}=(w^{\prime},S_{A}^{\prime},S_{B}^{\prime})
, let
\ell_{A}=|S_{A}\cap S_{A}^{\prime}|
and
\ell_{B}=|S_{B}\cap S_{B}^{\prime}|
. Define
f(\ell)=n^{-\ell}p^{-\binom{\ell}{2}}
. In case
w\neq w^{\prime}
, we have

(9.6)

\sum_{w\neq w^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\right]\leq n^{2+4(s-1)-\ell_{A}-\ell_{B}}p^{4(s-1)+4{s-1\choose 2}-{\ell_{A}\choose 2}-{\ell_{B}\choose 2}}\leq n^{2}M^{2}f(\ell_{A})f(\ell_{B}),

for appropriate values
\ell_{A}
and
\ell_{B}
to be determined later. Similarly, if
w=w^{\prime}
, we have

(9.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{w=w^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq n^{1+4(s-1)-\ell_{A}-\ell_{B}}p^{4(s-1)-\ell_{A}-\ell_{B}+4{s-1\choose 2}-{\ell_{A}\choose 2}-{\ell_{B}\choose 2}}\leq nM^{2}f(\ell_{A})p^{-\ell_{A}}f(\ell_{B})p^{-\ell_{B}},

again for appropriate values of
\ell_{A}
and
\ell_{B}
.
We claim that in both cases, the quantity
\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\right]
is maximised when
\ell_{A}
and
\ell_{B}
are as small as possible.
To see this, note that both functions
f(\ell)
and
f(\ell)p^{-\ell}
are log-convex, i.e.,

\frac{f(\ell+1)p^{-\ell-1}f(\ell-1)p^{-\ell+1}}{f(\ell)^{2}p^{-2\ell}}=\frac{f(\ell+1)f(\ell-1)}{f(\ell)^{2}}=p^{\frac{1}{2}\left(-\ell^{2}-\ell-\ell^{2}+3\ell-2+2\ell^{2}-2\ell\right)}=p^{-1}>1

Hence, it suffices to compare the extremal values
f(0)
and
f(s-1)
, and the values
f(2)
and
f(s-1)
as we need
\max\{\ell_{A},\ell_{B}\}\geq 2
. We have

\frac{f(0)}{f(s-1)}=n^{s-1}p^{{s-1\choose 2}}=C^{{s-1}\choose 2}\geq 1,

and

\frac{f(2)}{f(s-1)}=n^{s-3}p^{{s-1\choose 2}-1}=C^{{{s-1}\choose 2}-1}\geq 1.

Using these bounds, inequality (
9.6
), and the fact that
\max\{\ell_{A},\ell_{B}\}\geq 2
whenever
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
are not independent, we have that in case
w\neq w^{\prime}
,

\sum_{w\neq w^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\right]\leq n^{2}M^{2}f(2)f(0)=p^{-1}M^{2}.

The same argument shows that the right-hand side of (
9.7
) is minimised when
\ell_{A}=1
and
\ell_{B}=0
, or vice versa, so when
w=w^{\prime}
, we have

\sum_{w=w^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\mathbf{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}^{\prime}}\right]\leq nM^{2}f(1)p^{-1}f(0)=p^{-1}M^{2}.

Using
9.5
, we conclude that
\bar{\Delta}\leq 2M^{2}p^{-1}=O_{s}(p^{-1}n^{-2}\nu^{-2}\lambda^{2})
.

Using Janson’s Inequality (
[
21
, Theorem 2.14]
), we conclude that

\mathbb{P}\left[\sum_{\mathbf{S}\in\mathcal{S}}I_{\mathbf{S}}=0\right]\leq e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8\bar{\Delta}}}\leq e^{-c_{s}\nu^{2}pn^{2}},

as required.
∎

Applying Lemma

For every set

We will next use Lemma

9.2.

We first state a result corresponding to Lemma

Lemma 9.3

Let

-reduced graph on

(i)

For every

\left|V(G)\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}}S_{i}\right|\leq\varepsilon n

(ii)

and no independent set of size
\left(\frac{1}{2}-20d^{1/4}\right)|V(R)|

(iii)

The reduced graph

contains a perfect matching on

and a triangle factor on

.

(iv)

For every set

with

-super-regular pair.

Next, we will need a lemma which will ensure

of vertices of

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
G
be a connected

, and suppose that each vertex of

, there is an even trail of length at most 8 between

and

Proof.

Given

and

, let

be the shortest

, and assume that its length is at least

. Then

contains a vertex

which is at distance at least 3 from both

and
v
. The neighbourhoods of

and

are mutually disjoint, as otherwise there would be a path shorter than

between
u
and
v
. However, this cannot happen as
\delta(G)>n/3
. Hence
P
is a
u
–
v
path of length at most 5.

If
P
is even, we are done. If
P
is not even, we can append a triangle containing
v
to it, creating a trail of length at most 8.
∎

Finally, we need a separate statement to cover graphs
G
which approximately consist of two disjoint cliques of size
n/2
. (To see why this cannot be covered by our usual proof strategy, notice that e.g. when
G
consists of two cliques differing in a small number of vertices, the random edges have to be used to find complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,s}
between the two cliques.) We defer the proof of this lemma to the end of the section.

Lemma 9.5
.

There is
\xi=\xi(s)
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
C
. Let
G
be a graph with
\delta(G)\geq n/2
, and suppose that the vertices of
G
can be partitioned into sets
A
and
B
such that there are at most
\xi n^{2}
edges between
A
and
B
. Then, for
p=Cn^{-2/s}(\log n)^{2/(s(s-1))}
, with high probability,
G\cup G_{n,p}
contains a
K_{2s}
-factor.

The proof of Lemma
4.2
in the singular case follows the same outline as in the non-singular case. Specifically, finding a factor in the reduced graph and covering the remainder
X
is the same in the two proofs, whereas the
balancing step
is rather different.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
in case
s=t
.

We will be working with positive constants satisfying the hierarchy

\gamma\gg d\gg\nu\gg\varepsilon.

Let
d=d(\gamma)
be the constant from Lemma
9.3
. Recall that
r=2s
.

Applying Lemma
9.3
(with sufficiently small
\varepsilon
which is fixed so that the following steps of the proof go through), we obtain the sets
\{S_{i}:i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}\}
satisfying the conclusion of the Lemma.
Let
\ell=|L_{2}\cup L_{3}|
and
X=V(G)\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}}S_{i}
with
|X|\leq\varepsilon n
. Let
n_{*}
be the size of each part
S_{i}
.

Firstly, assume that there is a set
L^{\prime}\subset L_{2}\cup L_{3}
with
|L^{\prime}|\leq\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\cdot\ell
such that the graph
R[L_{2}\cup L_{3}\smallsetminus L^{\prime}]
is not connected. Then we can show that the original graph
G
has a vertex partition
A\cup B
with
e_{G}(A,B)=O(\frac{s\varepsilon}{d}n^{2})
. Indeed, let
A=\bigcup_{i\in L^{\prime}}S_{i}
and
B=V(G)\smallsetminus A
. Then

\displaystyle e_{G}(A,B)

\displaystyle\leq|L^{\prime}|n_{*}\cdot(\ell n_{*}+\varepsilon n)\leq\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\ell^{2}n_{*}^{2}+\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\varepsilon\ell n_{*}n\leq\frac{100s\varepsilon}{d}n^{2}.

In this case, Lemma
9.5
yields the desired
K_{r}
-factor.

Hence we assume that after removing any
\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\ell
parts, the remaining
(\varepsilon,d,d)
-reduced graph
R
is still connected.
We also remark that, even after removing any
\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\cdot\ell
parts, the graph
R
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
9.4
. Indeed, the fact that every vertex of
R
is still in a triangle follows from property
(ii)
of Lemma
9.3
.
This also implies that for any
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
in
L_{2}\cup L_{3}
, there is an even trail of length at most 8.

As in the previous proof, we let
p=Cn^{-2/s}(\log n)^{-2/(s(s-1))}
and expose three independent graphs
G_{i}\sim G(n,p/4)
for
i=1,2,3
. The union
G_{1}\cup G_{2}\cup G_{3}
can be viewed as a subgraph of
G_{n,p}
.

Covering the vertices in
X
.

The graph
G_{1}
will be used to cover
X
. As in the previous proof, we let
Z
to be a random subset which contains each element of
V(G)\smallsetminus X
independently with probability
\vartheta=\frac{d}{4}
. We assume that
|Z\cap S_{i}|\leq 3\vartheta|S_{i}|/2
for all
i
. By the previous argument,
Z
can be chosen so that there is a collection of at most
|X|
vertex-disjoint
K_{r}
-copies in
Z\cup X
covering
X
. Let
X^{+}
be the vertex set of this partial
K_{r}
-factor, and denote
S_{i}^{\prime}=S_{i}\smallsetminus X^{+}
for
i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}
. We have

(9.9)

|X^{+}|\leq 2s\varepsilon n\quad\text{and}\quad|S_{i}^{\prime}|\geq\left(1-\frac{3\vartheta}{2}\right)n_{*}.

Ensuring divisibility by
s

In the first balancing round, we will ensure that the remaining parts have order divisible by
s
by removing at most
2s\ell
copies of
K_{r}
. This will be done using a sequence of
alterations
, which we introduce later.

Let
G_{2}\sim G(n,p/4)
, and assume that
G_{2}
has the following property (which typically occurs, and is shown immediately hereafter).

(P3):

Consider any three parts
S_{i_{1}},S_{i_{2}},S_{i_{3}}
such that
\{S_{i_{1}},S_{i_{2}}\}
and
\{S_{i_{2}},S_{i_{3}}\}
are edges in
R
. If
B_{j}\subset S_{i_{j}}
satisfy
|B_{j}|\geq|S_{i_{j}}|/2
for
j\in[3]
, then there is a copy of
K_{r}
in
G\cup G_{2}
with one vertex in
B_{1}
,
s
vertices in
B_{2}
and
s-1
vertices in
B_{3}
.

This property follows from Lemma
7.3
, although that argument is of course an overkill. Instead, one can use Janson’s inequality to find suitable
K_{s}
-copies in
G_{2}
and combine them using regularity. We remark that the property can also be used with
S_{i_{1}}=S_{i_{3}}
, yielding a copy of
K_{r}
intersecting
S_{i_{1}}
and
S_{i_{2}}
in exactly
s
vertices.

Given a set
Y\subset V(G)
, we call a part
S_{i}
(or its index
i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}
)
available
(in
G-Y
) if
|S_{i}\cap Y|\leq\vartheta n_{*}
, with
\vartheta=d/4
. Otherwise we call it
unavailable
.

Claim 9.6
.

Let
Y\subset V(G)
with
|Y|\leq 10s\varepsilon n
. In the graph
G-Y
, there are at most
\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\cdot\ell
unavailable parts.

Proof.

If the number of unavailable parts is
\alpha\ell
, then the number of removed vertices
|Y|
is at least

|Y|\geq\alpha\ell\vartheta n_{*}\geq\alpha\vartheta\cdot n(1-\varepsilon).

Rearranging and using
\vartheta=d/4
and
\varepsilon\ll d
, we get

\alpha\ell\leq\frac{|Y|}{\vartheta n(1-\varepsilon)}\cdot\ell\leq\frac{40s\varepsilon}{d(1-\varepsilon)}\cdot\ell\leq\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\cdot\ell.

∎

It follows that if
|Y|\leq 10s\varepsilon n
, then for any
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
in
L_{2}\cup L_{3}
, there is an even trail in
R
of length at most 8 connecting
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
and consisting only of parts which are available in
G-Y
.

Next, given
Y\subset V(G)
,
performing an
(i,j,k)
-removal
means removing a copy of
K_{r}
which uses one vertex from
S_{i}\smallsetminus Y
,
s
vertices from
S_{j}\smallsetminus Y
and
s-1
vertices from
S_{k}\smallsetminus Y
, and adding those
2s
vertices to
Y
. Furthermore, an
(i,k)
-alteration
is a move of the following form. Given parts
S_{i}
and
S_{k}
, choose an even trail
P=S_{i_{0}}S_{i_{1}}\ldots S_{i_{|P|}}
with
i_{0}=i
,
i_{|P|}=k
, and
|P|\leq 8
. Then perform a sequence of removals
(i_{0},i_{1},i_{2})
,
(i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})
, …,
(i_{|P-2|},i_{|P-1|},i_{|P|})
. The sizes of the sets
S_{i}
and
S_{k}
change by
-1
and
+1
modulo
s
, respectively, whereas all other parts remain intact modulo
s
.

This move will now be repeatedly used to ensure divisibility by
s
. To this end, set
Y=X^{+}
, and for
i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}
, let
r_{i}
be an integer such that
|S_{i}^{\prime}|\equiv r_{i}\pmod{s}
,
|r_{i}|<s
and
\sum_{i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}}r_{i}=0
. (Note that such a choice of
r_{i}
’s exists since the total number of vertices is divisible by
2s
throughout the removal process). Let
r=\max_{i}|r_{i}|
and choose
r
auxiliary matchings
\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{r}
on
L_{2}\cup L_{3}
(regardless of the reduced graph
R
) such that for
\pi=\pi_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\pi_{r}
each edge
ik
of
\pi
satisfies
r_{i}>0
and
r_{k}<0
(or
r_{i}<0
and
r_{k}>0
), and each index
i
has degree
|r_{i}|
in
\pi
. Next, for each edge
ik
in
\pi
(in any order), perform an
(i,k)
-alteration. Note that this is possible since at most
s\ell/2
alterations are performed, removing at most
4s^{2}\ell
vertices in total. Hence, using Claim
9.6
, between any
S_{i}
and
S_{k}
we have a sufficient trail. Let
S_{i}^{\prime\prime}\subset S_{i}
be the resulting parts. Then for each
i\in L_{2}\cup L_{3}
we have
|S_{i}^{\prime\prime}|\equiv 0\pmod{s}
. Moreover

\quad|S_{i}^{\prime\prime}|\geq\left(1-7\vartheta/4\right)n_{*}\quad\text{and}\quad|Y|\leq 5s\varepsilon n/2.

Ensuring divisibility by
2s

Let
L^{*}
be the set of parts whose size is
not
divisible by
2s
(so it is congruent to
s
modulo
2s
). Choose an auxiliary perfect matching
\varrho
on
L^{*}
. Now, for each edge
ik
in the matching
\varrho
, perform the following move. Choose a path
P
between
S_{i}
and
S_{k}
of length at most
8
in
R
, and for each edge
S_{j}S_{j^{\prime}}
in
P
, perform a
(j,j^{\prime},j)
-removal. After this procedure, the size of
S_{i}^{\prime\prime}
and
S_{k}^{\prime\prime}
change by
s
, and the sizes of the remaining parts remain intact modulo
2s
.

Since
\varrho
has at most
\ell/2
edges, we removed at most
8\cdot 2s\cdot\ell/2\leq 8s\ell
vertices in this round. Denote the remaining subsets by
S_{i}^{*}\subset S_{i}^{\prime\prime}
. Then for each

we have

. Moreover

\quad|S_{i}^{*}|\geq\left(1-15\vartheta/8\right)n_{*}\quad\text{and}\quad|Y|\leq 3s\varepsilon n.

Balancing the ‘regular pairs’

At this point we need some additional notation. Let
M
be a perfect matching on
L_{2}
and let
T
be a triangle-factor on
L_{3}
granted by Lemma
9.3
(iii).

We will now pass to subsets
S_{i}^{\dagger}\subset S_{i}^{*}
for
i\in L_{2}
satisfying
|S_{i}^{\dagger}|=|S_{j}^{\dagger}|\equiv 0\pmod{s}
for
ij\in M
. Note that
since we already removed in total at most
3s\varepsilon n
vertices from the original parts,

(9.10)

\sum_{ij\in M}||S_{i}^{*}|-|S_{j}^{*}||\leq 3s\varepsilon n.

Now take an arbitrary edge
ij
of
M
, and assume that
|S_{i}^{*}|>|S_{j}^{*}|
. Note that

|S_{i}^{*}|-|S_{j}^{*}|\equiv 0\pmod{2s}
and
|S_{i}^{*}|-|S_{j}^{*}|\leq 2\vartheta n_{*}
.
As long as the leftover of
S_{i}^{*}
is larger that
S_{j}^{*}
, we repeat the following move.
Choose an available part
S_{k}
with
k\in L_{3}
which is adjacent to
S_{i}
in
R
– such a part exists since (by property
(iv)
of Lemma
9.3
)

has at least
d\ell
neighbours in
L_{3}
and (using Claim

) the number of unavailable parts is at most
\frac{50s\varepsilon}{d}\cdot\ell
. Perform two
(i,k,i)
-removals.

The resulting sets have sizes divisible by
2s
, and we have performed at most
3\varepsilon n
removals, losing at most
6s\varepsilon n
vertices.

Let
G^{\dagger}
be the leftover graph with parts
S_{i}^{\dagger}\subset S_{i}^{*}
, and note that as claimed,
|V(G)\smallsetminus V\left(G^{\dagger}\right)|\leq 9s\varepsilon n
. Moreover, at most
2\vartheta n_{*}=dn_{*}/2
vertices are removed from each part, so the remaining pairs and triples corresponding to

and

, respectively, are
(2\varepsilon,(d/2)^{+},d/2)
-super-regular.

It remains to split each regular triple
(S_{i}^{\dagger},S_{j}^{\dagger},S_{k}^{\dagger})
with

into three balanced pairs, as follows. Fix a triple
(S_{1}^{\dagger},S_{2}^{\dagger},S_{3}^{\dagger})
, and let
x_{1},x_{2}
and
x_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle x_{1}+x_{2}

\displaystyle=|S_{1}^{\dagger}|\,,

\displaystyle x_{2}+x_{3}

\displaystyle=|S_{2}^{\dagger}|\,,

\displaystyle x_{3}+x_{1}

\displaystyle=|S_{3}^{\dagger}|\,.

Since
x_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|S_{1}^{\dagger}|+|S_{3}^{\dagger}|-|S_{2}^{\dagger}|\right)
, we have
x_{1}\geq\frac{n_{*}}{2}(1-d)\geq\frac{n_{*}}{3}
, and
x_{1}
is an integer divisible by
s
. The same holds for
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
.

Now we can split
(S_{1}^{\dagger},S_{2}^{\dagger},S_{3}^{\dagger})
into three pairs
(U_{j},W_{j})
,

, with
|U_{j}|=|W_{j}|=x_{j}
, and such that
(U_{j},W_{j})
is an
(8\varepsilon,(d/3)^{+},(d/8))
-super-regular pair. (Technically, we can take a uniformly random partition and use Chernoff-type bounds for the hypergeometric distribution. See
[
38
]
.)

Covering the regular pairs

We obtain a collection of
(8\varepsilon,(d/3)^{+},(d/8))
-super-regular pairs which satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma

. Now we can expose the random graph
G_{3}
and apply Lemma
4.4
to obtain a

-factor in

. This completes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Lemma
9.5
. We remark that the logarithmic factor in
p
is probably not needed, but is an artefact of using Lemma
7.1
.

Proof.

As before, let
G_{1}\sim G_{n,p/3}
and
G_{2}\sim G_{n,p/3}
be mutually independent random graphs.

Let
X_{A}
be the set of vertices in
A
with more than
\sqrt{\xi}n
neighbours in
B
, and define
X_{B}
similarly. We have
|X_{A}|\cup|X_{B}|\leq 2\sqrt{\xi}n
, as otherwise there would be more than
\xi n^{2}
edges between
A
and
B
. Let
X=X_{A}\cup X_{B}
.

We claim that
X
and
G
satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
7.1
with
\varepsilon_{\ref{l:x-cover}}=2\sqrt{\xi}
and
\nu
a constant depending only on
s
. To verify this, let
|Y|\leq 4s\varepsilon n
and fix
x\in X
. Let
A_{x}\subset N_{G}(x)\smallsetminus Y
be an arbitrary subset of order
n/4
. In the bipartite graph
G[A_{x},V(G)\smallsetminus(A_{x}\cup Y\cup\{x\})]
, every vertex from
A_{x}
has at least
n/8
neighbours. Hence, using the supersaturation version of the Kővári–Sós–Turán Theorem
[
24
]
, one may find a collection of
\nu n^{2s-1}
copies of
K_{s-1,s}
with the larger class contained in
N_{G}(x)
. Thus
|\mathcal{B}_{x}[V(G)\smallsetminus Y]|\geq\nu n^{2s-1}
and, by Lemma
7.1
,
X
can be covered by a partial
K_{r}
-factor in
G\cup G_{1}
. Let
X^{+}
be the vertices contained in this
K_{r}
-factor.

Let
A^{\prime}=A\smallsetminus X^{+}
and
B^{\prime}=B\smallsetminus X^{+}
. Notice that since
G[A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}]
is a graph with minimum degree at least
(1/2-4s\sqrt{\xi}n)
and each
x\in A^{\prime}
has at most
\sqrt{\xi}n
neighbours in
B^{\prime}
, we have
|A^{\prime}|\geq(1/2-(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi})n
,
|B^{\prime}|\leq(1/2+(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi})n
and
\delta(G[A^{\prime}])\geq(1/2-(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi})n
.
By symmetry, we also have
|A^{\prime}|\leq(1/2+(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi})n
, so the minimum degree in
G[A^{\prime}]
satisfies

(9.11)

\frac{\delta(G[A^{\prime}])}{|A^{\prime}|}\geq\frac{1/2-(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi}}{1/2+(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi}}\geq 1-4(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi}.

The same conclusion holds for
B^{\prime}
.

The next step is to ensure divisibility by
2s
, using the second random graph
G_{2}
. By the same argument as for Lemma
2.1
(Janson’s Inequality), one can show that at our value of
p
,
G_{2}
has the following property with high probability.

(P):

For any two
A_{1}\subset A
and
B_{1}\subset B
of order at least
n/4
, there is a copy of
K_{r}
in
G\cup G_{2}
intersecting
A_{1}
in
s+1
vertices and
A_{2}
in
s-1
vertices.

(We remark that unlike in the previous case, now the edges between
A_{1}
and
B_{1}
will come from the random graph
G_{2}
.)

Hence we assume that
G_{2}
satisfy
(P)
. Since
|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|
is divisible by
2s
, there is a positive integer
a<2s
such that
|A^{\prime}|\equiv-|B^{\prime}|\equiv a\pmod{2s}
. We can repeatedly apply
(P)
to remove
a
copies of
K_{r}
with
s+1
vertices in
A^{\prime}
and
s-1
vertices in
B^{\prime}
.
Let
A^{*}\subset A^{\prime}
be the remaining subset of
A^{\prime}
. For sufficiently large
n
, the remaining graph
G[A^{*}]
has minimum degree at least
(1-5(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi})|A^{*}|.
By the theorem of Hajnal and Szemerédi
[
16
]
, assuming
5(4s+1)\sqrt{\xi}<1/(2s)
,
G[A^{*}]
has a
K_{r}
-factor. The same conclusion holds for
G[B^{*}]
. This completes the proof of the Lemma.
∎

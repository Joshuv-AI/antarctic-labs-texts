Multi-segmental zosteriform leiomyoma cutis: a rare cutaneous neoplasm
Authors: Priyanka Patil, Tamanna Raman, Yash Buccha
Published: 2024

Description

Leiomyoma cutis is a benign cutaneous neoplasm arising from different smooth muscles. There are three types of origins, the most common being pilar leiomyomas arising from arrector pilorum muscles.1 2 They commonly occur over the trunk and upper limbs.3 Associations are seen with uterine leiomyomas and with renal cell carcinoma.3 A bilateral, multi-segmental zosteriform pattern of lesions is a rare presentation of leiomyoma cutis.2 3 Skin biopsy is diagnostic and often done to establish the diagnosis. Treatment options include surgical excision, cryotherapy and electrocoagulation.1 Very few cases of such a presentation have been reported in the literature. We are submitting images that will aid in better recognition of this rare condition.

A man in his late 50s presented with multiple, intermittently painful, raised dark lesions associated with itching over his upper back and abdomen for the last 10 years. The size and number of the lesions were progressively increasing over the years. There was no history of any urinary complaints. There was no family history of similar lesions. The cosmetic appearance and pain had severely hampered the patient’s quality of life.

On examination, multiple, well-defined, grouped reddish-brown papules and nodules coalesced to form plaque were seen over the left scapular area and the right lumbar and iliac quadrant of the abdomen. The lesions were distributed in multiple segments and had a zosteriform pattern. They were firm in consistency, fixed to the skin and non-indurated (figure 1 and figure 2).

Histopathology of the lesions revealed non-epithelial neoplasm involving the reticular dermis with features of differentiation towards smooth muscle. The neoplastic cells showed abundant pink vacuolated cytoplasm and elongated wavy blunt-ended nuclei. The neoplastic cells were arranged in small and long fascicles that resemble normal arrector pilorum muscles. The surrounding dermis displayed dense collagen (online supplemental image). Ultrasonograpgy of abdomen and pelvis showed no abnormalities. Diagnosis of leiomyoma cutis was made, and the patient was explained about the prognosis and available treatment modalities. The lesions were treated with multiple sessions of CO2 laser ablation. There was significant sympotomatic relief in pain and itching post ablation.

Learning points

Leiomyoma cutis typically presents as solitary or multiple papules and nodules. However, a segmental zosteriform distribution seen in this patient is a rare presentation. This highlights the importance of recognising its atypical patterns.

The multi-segmental zosteriform pattern of lesions may mimic other segmental diseases. Clinicians must consider a broad differential diagnosis and use histopathology to distinguish leiomyoma cutis from other conditions.

Leiomyoma cutis can significantly affect a patient’s quality of life due to pain and cosmetic concerns. Treatment options, such as CO2 laser ablation, can effectively manage symptoms and improve the patient’s quality of life, underlining the importance of tailored therapeutic approaches for cutaneous neoplasms.

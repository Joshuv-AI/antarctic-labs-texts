On the 4 4 -clique cover number of graphs
Yihan Chen, Jialin He, Tianying Xie

Abstract
In 1966, Erdős, Goodman, and Pósa proved that ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor cliques are sufficient to cover all edges in any n n -vertex graph, with tightness achieved by the balanced complete bipartite graph. This result was generalized by Dau, Milenkovic, and Puleo, who showed that at most ⌊ n 3 ⌋ ​ ⌊ n + 1 3 ⌋ ​ ⌊ n + 2 3 ⌋ \lfloor\frac{n}{3}\rfloor\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\rfloor\lfloor\frac{n+2}{3}\rfloor cliques are needed to cover all triangles in any n n -vertex graph G G , and the bound is best possible as witnessed by the balanced complete tripartite graph. They further conjectured that for t ≥ 4 t\geq 4 , the t t -clique cover number is maximized by the Turán graph T n , t T_{n,t} . We confirm their conjecture for t = 4 t=4 using novel techniques, including inductive frameworks, greedy partition method, local adjustments, and clique-counting lemmas by Erdős and by Moon and Moser. Introduction

For
n\geq t
, the

is the
n
-vertex complete
t
-partite graph such that the part sizes differ by at most one.
A graph is

-free

t
-clique

Turán’s celebrated theorem
[

is the unique

-free graph maximizing the number of edges.
Generalizations of Turán’s theorem in various directions form a central theme in extremal graph theory.
Our starting point is one of the early result in this area due to Erdős, Goodman, and Pósa
[
5
]

-vertex graph

Inspired by community detection, Dau, Milenkovic, and Puleo
[

generalized the edge cover problem to the

-clique cover problem for

A

is a collection of cliques such that every

-clique in

is a subgraph of some clique of the collection.
The

-clique cover number
of

-clique cover of

.
Note that
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{1}(G)}
is the chromatic number of the complement of

.
The graph parameter
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{2}(G)}
is well-studied and also known as the intersection number of

[
5
,
7
,
10
,
11
]
.
Dau et al.
[
3
]
extended the Erdős-Goodman-Pósa result

]
to

proving that

.
They conjectured the following generalization for

.

(Dau, Milenkovic and Puleo

If

The value

’s, each must be taken in any

For

Recently, the second author, together with Balogh, Krueger, Nguyen and Wigal

Our main contribution is the confirmation of Conjecture

.

For every

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{3}(H)}\leq\frac{59}{2}q^{3}-24q^{2}d+\frac{13}{2}qd^{2}-\frac{5}{9}d^{3}+\frac{13}{2}q^{2}-\frac{17}{6}qd+\frac{2}{9}d^{2}+\frac{1}{3}q-\frac{1}{9}d+\frac{4}{9}:=F(q,d).

(8)

The partial derivative
F^{\prime}_{d}(q,d)=-24q^{2}+13qd-\frac{5}{3}d^{2}-\frac{17}{6}q+\frac{4}{9}d-\frac{1}{9}.

By (
7
),
3q<d\leq 4q.

It is not hard to see that

F^{\prime}_{d}(q,3q)=-\frac{3}{2}q-\frac{1}{9}<0,

F^{\prime}_{d}(q,3q+1)=\frac{3}{2}q-\frac{4}{3}>0,
and

F^{\prime}_{d}(q,4q)=\frac{4}{3}q^{2}-\frac{19}{18}q-\frac{1}{9}>0

holds for
q\geq 1.

Therefore,
F(q,d)
is increasing with respect to
d.

If
q<\frac{n}{5},

then

F(q,d)\leq F(q,4q)=\frac{35}{18}q^{3}-\frac{23}{18}q^{2}-\frac{1}{9}q+\frac{4}{9}<\frac{7}{450}n^{3}-\frac{23}{450}n^{2}+\frac{1}{45}n+\frac{4}{9}<\frac{1}{64}(n-1)^{3},

where the second inequality holds since the function
F(q,4q)
increases for
q\geq 1,

and the last inequality holds when
n\geq 8.

Thus,
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{3}(H)}\leq F(q,d)<\frac{1}{64}(n-1)^{3}\leq k_{4}(T_{n,4})-k_{4}(T_{n-1,4}),
and we are done.

If
q\geq\frac{n}{5},
since
d\leq n-q,
we have

F(q,d)\leq F(q,n-q)=\frac{545}{9}q^{3}-\frac{116}{3}q^{2}n+\frac{49}{6}qn^{2}-\frac{5}{9}n^{3}+\frac{86}{9}q^{2}-\frac{59}{18}qn+\frac{2}{9}n^{2}+\frac{4}{9}q-\frac{1}{9}n+\frac{4}{9}.

(9)

Let the result equation be
g(q).

Then
g^{\prime}(q)=\frac{545}{3}q^{2}-\frac{232}{3}qn+\frac{49}{6}n^{2}+\frac{172}{9}q-\frac{59}{18}n+\frac{4}{9}.

Since
\frac{n}{5}\leq q\leq n-\lfloor\frac{3}{4}n\rfloor-1=\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil-1<\frac{n}{4},

it is easy to see that

g^{\prime}(0)=\frac{49}{6}n^{2}-\frac{59}{18}n+\frac{4}{9}>0,

g^{\prime}(\frac{n}{5})=-\frac{1}{30}n^{2}+\frac{49}{90}n+\frac{4}{9}<0,
and

g^{\prime}(\frac{n}{4})=\frac{3}{16}n^{2}+\frac{2}{3}n+\frac{4}{9}>0,

when
n\geq 18.

Thus,
g(q)
is decreasing first and then increasing with respect to
q

and the maximum of
g(q)
will take value at the endpoints of the interval containing
q,
i.e.,

g(q)\leq\max\{g(\frac{n}{5}),g(\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil-1)\}.

Note that

g(\frac{n}{5})=F(\frac{n}{5},\frac{4n}{5})=\frac{7}{450}n^{3}-\frac{23}{450}n^{2}+\frac{1}{45}n+\frac{4}{9}<\frac{1}{64}(n-1)^{3}
holds for
n\geq 8.

And

g(\frac{n}{4}-2)=\frac{1}{64}n^{3}-\frac{3}{8}n^{2}+24n-\frac{1340}{3}<\frac{1}{64}(n-1)^{3}
holds for all
n.

Therefore, if
q\leq\frac{n}{4}-2,

then
{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{4}(G)}\leq{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{4}(G^{\prime})}+{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{3}(H)}<{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{4}(T_{n,4})},

and we are done.

Now, the only remaining case is
q=\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil-1.

In this situation, we have

\delta(G)=d=n-q=\lfloor\frac{3}{4}n\rfloor+1.

We will analyze more carefully depending on the residue of
n
modulo
4.

By (
9
), we have

\displaystyle(i).\,n=4k,\,q=k-1:\quad

\displaystyle g(q)=k^{3}-3k^{2}-\frac{329}{3}k-51<k^{3}=k_{4}(T_{4k,4})-k_{4}(T_{4k-1,4}),

\displaystyle(ii).\,n=4k+1,\,q=k:\quad

\displaystyle g(q)=k^{3}=k_{4}(T_{4k+1,4})-k_{4}(T_{4k,4}),

\displaystyle(iii).\,n=4k+2,\,q=k:\quad

\displaystyle g(q)=k^{3}+3k-\frac{10}{3}<k^{3}+k^{2}<k^{3}+k^{2}=k_{4}(T_{4k+2,4})-k_{4}(T_{4k+1,4}),

\displaystyle(iv).\,n=4k+3,\,q=k:\quad

\displaystyle g(q)=k^{3}+9k-\frac{116}{9}<k^{3}+2k^{2}+k=k_{4}(T_{4k+3,4})-k_{4}(T_{4k+2,4}).

When
n=4k+1,q=k
, if all the equalities hold,
then by induction,
G^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
T_{4k,4}.

Since
\delta(G)=d=3k+1,

we conclude that
v
is adjacent to all vertices of
G^{\prime},

contradicting that
v
has the minimum degree.
Therefore, we have proved that when

\lfloor\frac{3}{4}n\rfloor+1\leq d\leq\frac{4}{5}n,

{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{3}(H)}<k_{4}(T_{n,4})-k_{4}(T_{n-1,4}).

Combining all the cases with (
4
),
when
\omega(G)=5,
we have

{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{4}(G)}\leq{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{4}(G^{\prime})}+{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{3}(H)}<k_{4}(T_{n-1,4})+k_{4}(T_{n,4})-k_{4}(T_{n-1,4})={\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{4}(T_{n,4})},

which completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

4
Proof of Lemma
3.2

In this section, we complete the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Our proof strategy proceeds in the following three steps.
First, in Subsection
4.1
, we generalize Lovász’s method
[
7
]
to construct a 3-clique cover of
H
using its greedy partition
\mathcal{F}_{H}
, and reformulate the cover size using the size of its corresponding
greedy sequence
(Definition
4.1
).
Next, in Subsection
4.2
, we analyze extremal properties of these sequences.
Finally, in Subsection
4.3
, we examine the subgraph induced by vertices of 4-cliques in
\mathcal{F}_{H}
, using its combinatorial properties to refine our bound and establish Lemma
3.2
.

4.1
3-clique cover construction

We define
greedy sequence
and its value as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

An
(m,p)
-greedy sequence
A=(a_{1},\dots,a_{p})
satisfies that
a_{i}\in\{1,2,3,4\}
for
i\in[p],

a_{i}\geq a_{i+1}
for
i\in[p-1]
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{p}a_{i}=m.

Let
a=|\{i\in[p]:a_{i}=4\}|
,
b=|\{i\in[p]:a_{i}\geq 3\}|
,
c=|\{i\in[p]:a_{i}\geq 2\}|
.
Then, the value of
A
is denoted by
f(A):=S_{1}(A)+S_{2}(A)+S_{3}(A),
where
S_{1}(A)=b,

S_{2}(A)=mb+a^{2}-ab-b^{2}+bc-a-3b,
and

\displaystyle S_{3}(A)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{b}a_{k}\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{k=b+1}^{c}a_{k}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{k-1}(a_{j}-1)b\right)+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=c+1}^{p}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}(a_{j}-1)b\right).

Since
H
is
K_{5}
-free in Lemma
3.2
, if
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}=\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{p}\}
is a greedy partition of
H
of size
p
,
then
A=\{|A_{i}|\}_{i=1}^{p}
is an
(|H|,p)
-greedy sequence which we call the corresponding greedy sequence of
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
H
is a
K_{5}
-free graph and
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}=\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{p}\}
is a greedy partition of
H
of size
p
.
Let
A=\{|A_{i}|\}_{i=1}^{p}
be the corresponding
(|H|,p)
-greedy sequence of
{\mathcal{F}}_{H}
.
Then, we have

{\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}_{3}(H)}\leq f(A).

Proof.

We prove the lemma by constructing a 3-clique cover
{\mathcal{C}}
of
H
of size at most
f(A).

Let
a_{i}=|A_{i}|
, and let
a,b,c
be as in Definition
4.1
.
For
i\in[3],
let
{\mathcal{S}}_{i}
be the triangles with three vertices lying in exactly
i
different
A_{j}
’s.
First, we can easily see that
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}:=\bigcup_{i}\{A_{i}:a_{i}\geq 3\}
is a set of cliques that covers all the triangles in
{\mathcal{S}}_{1}.

Thus, we have
|{\mathcal{C}}_{1}|=|\cup_{i}\{A_{i}:a_{i}\geq 3\}|=b=S_{1}(A).

For a clique
C
in
H
and a vertex set
U\subset V(H)
, recall that
T_{U}[C]
is the induced subgraph of
H
on vertex set
C\cup U^{\prime},
where
U^{\prime}
is the common neighbor of the vertices in
C
in
U.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{1}=\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq p\ \text{and}\ i\leq b\},

\mathcal{I}_{2}=\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq a\},

\mathcal{I}_{3}=\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq p,\ i\leq b\ \text{and}\ j>a\},
and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\bigcup\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}\{T_{A_{i}}[v]:v\in A_{j}\}\bigcup\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}\{T_{A_{j}}[v]:v\in A_{i}\}\bigcup\limits_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}_{3}}\{T_{A_{i}}[u,v]:uv\in E(A_{j})\}.

We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
covers all the triangles in
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}.

Indeed, by the definition of
{\mathcal{F}}(H)
,
H[A_{b+1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{p}]
is triangle-free.
Thus, the first union set covers all the triangles with one vertex lying in
A_{j}
and the other two lying in
A_{i}
with
1\leq i<j\leq p
, and the size of the union set is

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{b}a_{j}(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{p}a_{j}b=3\sum_{j=1}^{b}(j-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{a}(j-1)+b\sum_{j=b+1}^{p}a_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}b(b-1)+\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)+b(m-a-3b)=mb+\frac{a^{2}}{2}-ab-\frac{3}{2}b^{2}-\frac{a}{2}-\frac{3}{2}b.

Note that the remaining triangles in
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
has one vertex lying in
A_{i}
and the other two lying in
A_{j}
with some
1\leq i<j\leq p
.
If
|A_{i}|<|E(A_{j})|,
then we use the second union set to cover them,
and the size of the union set is

\sum_{i=1}^{a}a_{i}(a-i)=4\sum_{i=1}^{a}(a-i)=2a^{2}-2a.

If
|A_{i}|\geq|E(A_{j})|,
then we use the third union set to cover them,
and the size of the union set is

\displaystyle\sum_{j=a+1}^{b}\binom{a_{j}}{2}(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}\binom{a_{j}}{2}b=3\sum_{j=a+1}^{b}(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}b=-\frac{3}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2}b^{2}+bc+\frac{3}{2}a-\frac{3}{2}b.

Thus,
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is a set of cliques that covers all the triangles in
{\mathcal{S}}_{2}
(note that
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
may contain vertices or edges).
Combining the above three equalities, we get that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{C}}_{2}|=mb+a^{2}-ab-b^{2}+bc-a-3b=S_{2}(A).

In a similar way, to cover all the triangles in
{\mathcal{S}}_{3},
we define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{3}=\bigcup\limits_{i,j,k}\{T_{A_{i}}[u,v]:u\in A_{j},v\in A_{k},uv\in E(H),\ 1\leq i<j<k\leq p\}.

Note that
{\mathcal{C}}_{3}
may contain edges.
By Proposition
3.1
,
for any
j<k
and
v\in A_{k}
,
v
has at most
a_{j}-1
neighbors in
A_{j}.

By the definition of
{\mathcal{F}}(H),

H[A_{b+1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{p}]
is triangle-free and
H[A_{c+1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{p}]
is empty, according to the range of
i,j,k
,
we have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{C}}_{3}|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{b}a_{k}\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{k=b+1}^{c}a_{k}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{k-1}(a_{j}-1)b\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=c+1}^{p}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}(a_{j}-1)b\right)=S_{3}(A).

(10)

Let
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{C}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{2}\cup{\mathcal{C}}_{3}\backslash D,
where
D=\{V(H)\cup E(H)\}.

Then, by the analysis above and the equations

|{\mathcal{C}}_{1}|=S_{1}(A)
,
|{\mathcal{C}}_{2}|=S_{2}(A),
and
|{\mathcal{C}}_{3}|\leq S_{3}(A)
,
we get that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a
3
-clique cover of
H
of size at most
f(A)
, which completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

4.2
Greedy sequence analysis

In this subsection, for the
(m,p)
-greedy sequence
A=\{a_{i}\}_{i=1}^{p}
, we will use the adjustment operation in the following lemma to preserve the sum
m
while increasing
f(A)
.
Indeed, we can determine the structure of the greedy sequence that achieves the maximum value.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
(m,p)
-greedy sequence
A
, there exists an integer
q\leq p
and an
(m,q)
-greedy sequence
A^{\prime}
such that

f(A)\leq f(A^{\prime})\leq 28q^{3}-\frac{45}{2}q^{2}m+6qm^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m^{3}+R_{0},

where
R_{0}=\max\{R_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
with

R_{1}=\frac{3}{2}qm-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}-3q+m
,

R_{2}=-28q^{2}+\frac{33}{2}qm-\frac{5}{2}m^{2}+6q-2m
and

R_{3}=-56q^{2}+\frac{63}{2}qm-\frac{9}{2}m^{2}+34q-10m-6.

We define three operations for a greedy sequence
A

(applied in priority order Operation 1
>
Operation 2
>
Operation 3).
Note that the number of 4’s remains unchanged throughout all operations.

Operation 1.

If there are at least two 1’s.
Suppose that
i
and
j
are the minimum and maximum indices such that
a_{i}=a_{j}=1.

In this case, we set
a_{i}\to 2
,
a_{j}\to 0
and
a_{k}\to a_{k}

for all
k\notin\{i,j\}
.
Then remove
a_{j}
from the new sequence, reducing the length by 1.

Operation 2.

If there is exactly one 1 and at least one 2.
Suppose that
i
is the minimum index such that
a_{i}=2
and
j
is the index such that
a_{j}=1.

We set
a_{i}\to 3
,
a_{j}\to 0
and
a_{k}\to a_{k}

for all
k\notin\{i,j\}
.
Then remove
a_{j}
from the new sequence, reducing the length by 1.

Operation 3.

If there are at least two 2’s and no 1.
Suppose that
i
and
j
are the minimum and maximum indices such that
a_{i}=a_{j}=2.

Then we set
a_{i}\to 3,

a_{j}\to 1,
and
a_{k}\to a_{k}
for all

k\notin\{i,j\}.

Repeated application of Operations 1-3 (applied in priority order) transforms an
(m,p)
-greedy sequence
A
into an
(m,q)
-greedy sequence
A^{\prime}=(a_{1}^{\prime},\dots,a_{q}^{\prime})
with
q\leq p
that is
irreducible
(no operations can be applied anymore) and belongs to exactly one of the following three types.

Type 1:

a_{i}^{\prime}\in\{3,4\}
for all
i\in[q].

Type 2:

a_{i}^{\prime}\in\{3,4\}
for
i\in[q-1]
, and
a_{q}^{\prime}=2.

Type 3:

a_{i}^{\prime}\in\{3,4\}
for
i\in[q-1]
, and
a_{q}^{\prime}=1.

First, we prove that for any greedy sequence
A
,
during the process, if we perform the three operations on
A
in order of priority, the value
f
of
A
will not decrease (Claims
4.4
-
4.6
).
Therefore, we only need to compute the values of the above three types of greedy sequence.

Let
A_{\text{old}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{p})
be an
(m,p)
-greedy sequence with
a\times 4
’s,
(b-a)\times 3
’s,
(c-b)\times 2
’s, and
(p-c)\times 1
’s.
We also use the notation
f(4_{1},...4_{a},3_{a+1},...,3_{b},2_{b+1},...,2_{c},1_{c+1},..,1_{p})
to represent
f(A_{\text{old}}).

For any operation, define
\Delta f=f(A_{\text{new}})-f(A_{\text{old}})=\Delta S_{1}+\Delta S_{2}+\Delta S_{3},

where
\Delta S_{i}=S_{i}(A_{\text{new}})-S_{i}(A_{\text{old}})
and
A_{\text{new}}
is the resulting sequence after performing the operation on
A_{\text{old}}.

Claim 4.4
.

If
p\geq c+2
, then perform Operation 1 on
A_{\text{old}},

we will get that
\Delta f\geq 0.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
A_{\text{new}}:=(a_{1}^{\prime},...,a_{p-1}^{\prime})=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{c},2_{c+1},1_{c+2},\cdots,1_{p-1})
and
by Definition
4.1
, we have

\Delta S_{1}=b-b=0,

\Delta S_{2}=b(c+1)-bc=b\geq 0,

and

\displaystyle\Delta S_{3}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}(a_{j}-1)b+\sum_{k=c+2}^{p-1}\sum_{j=b+1}^{c+1}(a_{j}^{\prime}-1)b-\sum_{k=c+1}^{p}\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}(a_{j}-1)b

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}b+\sum_{k=c+2}^{p-1}\sum_{j=b+1}^{c+1}b-\sum_{k=c+1}^{p}\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}b=b(p-c-2)\geq 0,

and we are done.
∎

After repeatedly using Operation 1, we can assume that there is at most one 1 in
A_{\text{old}}=\{a_{1},...,a_{p}\}
.

Claim 4.5
.

If
p=c+1,

then perform Operation 2 on
A_{\text{old}},

we will get that
\Delta f\geq 0.

Proof.

Now, we have
A_{\text{old}}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{b},2_{b+1},\cdots,2_{p-1},1_{p})
and

A_{\text{new}}:=(a_{1}^{\prime},...,a_{p-1}^{\prime})=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{b},\\
3_{b+1},2_{b+2},\cdots,2_{p-1}).

Since
m=p+a+b+c
and
p=c+1,
by Definition
4.1
,
we get

\Delta S_{1}=(b+1)-b=1,

\Delta S_{2}=m(b+1)-a(b+1)-(b+1)^{2}+(b+1)c-3(b+1)-(mb-ab-b^{2}+bc-3b)=m-a-2b-1+c-3=-b+3c-3,

and we have
\Delta S_{3}
is equal to

\displaystyle 3\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+2\left(\sum_{k=b+2}^{c}(k-b-2)(b+1)-\sum_{k=b+1}^{c}(k-b-1)b\right)+2\sum_{k=b+2}^{c}\Bigg(\sum_{j=2}^{b+1}(a_{j}^{\prime}-1)(j-1)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)\Bigg)-2\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{k=c+1}^{p-1}(c-1)-\sum_{j=b+1}^{c}b-\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=b+2}^{c}(k-2b-2)+2\sum_{k=b+2}^{c}(2b)-b(c-b)=-bc+c^{2}+b-3c+2.

Thus, we get that

\Delta f=\Delta S_{1}+\Delta S_{2}+\Delta S_{3}=-bc+c^{2}=c(c-b)\geq 0,

and we are done.
∎

After using Operation 2, we can assume that there is no
1
in
A_{\text{old}}=\{a_{1},...,a_{p}\}
.

Claim 4.6
.

If
p=c\geq b+2
,
then perform Operation 3 on
A_{\text{old}},

we will get that
\Delta f\geq 0.

Proof.

Now, we have
A_{\text{old}}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{b},2_{b+1},\cdots,2_{p})
and

A_{\text{new}}:=(a_{1}^{\prime},...,a_{p}^{\prime})=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{b},3_{b+1},2_{b+2},\\
\cdots,2_{p-1},1_{p}).

Since
m=p+a+b+c
and
p=c,
by Definition
4.1
,
we get that

\Delta S_{1}=(b+1)-b=1,

\Delta S_{2}=m-a-2b-1-b+c-1-3=-2b+3c-5,

and
\Delta S_{3}
is equal to

\displaystyle 3\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+2\sum_{k=b+2}^{c-1}(k-2)-2\sum_{j=b+1}^{c-1}b-4\sum_{j=2}^{b}(a_{j}-1)(j-1)+\sum_{j=b+2}^{c-1}(b+1)+\sum_{j=2}^{b+1}(a_{j}^{\prime}-1)\cdot

\displaystyle(j-1)=2\sum_{k=b+2}^{c-1}(k-2)-2b(c-b-1)+(b+1)(c-b-2)+2b=c^{2}-bc+2b-4c+4.

Thus, we get that
\Delta f=\Delta S_{1}+\Delta S_{2}+\Delta S_{3}=-bc+c^{2}-c=c(c-b-1)\geq 0,
and we are done.
∎

Now, we are ready to complete the proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

We consider any
(m,p)
-greedy sequence
A
.
Through successive applications of Operations 1-3 with priority Operation 1
>
Operation 2
>
Operation 3, we obtain an irreducible
(m,q)
-greedy sequence
A^{\prime}=(a_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,a_{q}^{\prime})
with

that belongs to one of Types 1-3.

By Claims
4.4
,
4.5
, and
4.6
, each operation does not decrease the value of

, which implies that

If
A^{\prime}
is of Type 1,
then
A^{\prime}=(4,...4,3,...,3).

We can see that there are
(m-3q)
many

’s and
(4q-m)
many

’s.
To calculate

by Definition
4.1
,
plugging into

and

we get that

Nonlinear discrete Schrödinger equations with a point defect
Dirk Hennig

Abstract
. We study the d d -dimensional discrete nonlinear Schrödinger equation with general power nonlinearity and a delta potential. Our interest lies in the interplay between two localization mechanisms. On the one hand, the attractive (repulsive) delta potential acting as a point defect breaks the translational invariance of the lattice so that a linear staggering (non-staggering) bound state is formed with negative (positive) energy. On the other hand, focusing nonlinearity may lead to self-trapping of excitation energy. For focusing nonlinearity we prove the existence of spatially exponentially localized and time-periodic ground states and investigate the impact of an attractive respectively repulsive delta potential on the existence of an excitation threshold, i.e. supercritical l 2 l^{2} norm, for the creation of such ground states. Explicit expressions for the excitation thresholds are given. Reciprocally, we discuss the influence of defocusing nonlinearity on the durability of the linear bound states and provide upper thresholds of the l 2 l^{2} norm for their preservation. Regarding the asymptotic behavior of the solutions we establish that for a l 2 l^{2} norm below the excitation threshold the solutions scatter to a solution of the linear problem in l p l^{p} , 2 < p ≤ ∞ 2<p\leq\infty .

Concerning bound states of the lattice system (

The former ones arise in the presence of a delta potential acting as an (external) impurity (defect) breaking the translational invariance of the lattice. The origin of the latter is due to the focusing nonlinear term creating an intrinsic potential well on a part of the lattice in which excitation energy becomes trapped.
In this paper we study also the interplay between these two localization mechanisms.

Our contributions in this paper are threefold.

2.
Linear defect modes

In the linear limiting case, arising for
\gamma=0
in (
1.1
), the system

(2.1)

i\frac{dv_{n}}{dt}+(\Delta v)_{n}+V_{0}\delta_{n,0}v_{n}=0,\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

supports localized solutions (also called defect modes) generated by the presence of the point defect destroying the spatial translational invariance.
These stationary solutions are of the form
v_{n}(t)=x_{n}\exp(-i\omega t)
,
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with a spectral parameter (eigenenergy)
\omega
and fulfil the stationary system

(2.2)

\omega x_{n}+(\Delta x)_{n}+V_{0}\delta_{n,0}x_{n}=0,\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

For
V_{0}>0
there exist a non-staggering bound state

x_{n}=A\eta_{+}^{|n|},\,\,\,0<\eta_{+}<1,

\eta_{+}=\frac{{1}}{2d}\left(\sqrt{{V_{0}}^{2}+4d^{2}}-{V_{0}}\right),

with
|n|=|n_{1}|+...+|n_{d}|
and amplitude
A>0
.

For
V_{0}<0
there exist a staggering bound state

x_{n}=A(-1)^{n}\eta_{-}^{|n|},\,\,\,-1<\eta_{-}<0,

\eta_{-}=-\frac{{1}}{2d}\left(\sqrt{{V_{0}}^{2}+4d^{2}}+{V_{0}}\right),

with
|n|=|n_{1}|+...+|n_{d}|
and amplitude
A>0
.

The amplitude
A
is related to the conserved
l^{2}
norm
(mass)

\nu=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|x_{n}|^{2}=\left(A^{2}\frac{1+\eta_{\pm}^{2}}{1-\eta_{\pm}^{2}}\right)^{d}.

The associated eigenvalues are determined by

(2.3)

{\omega}_{+}=2d-\sqrt{{V_{0}}^{2}+4d^{2}},\,\,\,\left({\omega}_{-}=2d+\sqrt{{V_{0}}^{2}+4d^{2}}\right).

The eigenvalues
\omega_{+}<0
(
\omega_{-}>4d
) lie below (above) the lower edge of absolutely continuous spectrum of
\Delta
.
Note that the defect modes are exponentially localized at the origin of the lattice (single-hump).

Next, we briefly discuss the impact of a (small) nonlinear term on the impurity modes. To be precise, we consider the system

(2.4)

i\frac{dw_{n}}{dt}+(\Delta w)_{n}+\gamma|w|^{2\sigma}w_{n}+V_{0}\delta_{n,0}w_{n}=0,\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Obviously, if
\gamma>0
and
V_{0}>0
respectively
\gamma<0
and
V_{0}<0
, the linear and nonlinear localization mechanisms enhance each other opposed to the case if
\gamma
and
V_{0}
are of opposite sign.
In the latter case, if
V_{0}>0
(
V_{0}<0
) an additional negative (positive) nonlinear term, i.e.
\gamma<0
(
\gamma>0
), in the equation of motion leads to an increase (decrease) of the negative (positive) total energy (
1.2
) of the impurity mode leading to a reduction of its degree of localization.
Nevertheless, for sufficiently low amplitude (
l^{2}
norm), going along with weak nonlinearity, we expect sustained impurity modes even when
V_{0}
and
\gamma
have different sign.

More specifically, for an attractive delta potential, when the non-staggering defect mode has
negative energy, an additional positive nonlinear term
\gamma|u|^{2\sigma+1}>0
in the equations of motion serves constructively
to even lower the total energy whereas negative nonlinearity for
\gamma<0
leads to an increase of
the total energy. The energy remains negative though if the nonlinear term is sufficiently small.
Similar arguments hold for the staggering defect mode.
Therefore, we expect that there are critical values of the mass that the defect modes can sustain.
In fact, in section
4
we further analyze the impact of nonlinearity on the
existence of defect mode(s) where we also derive explicit formulaes for upper and lower
thresholds of the mass for which the defect modes survive in the presence of nonlinearity.

J(x)\geq J\left(\frac{\nu^{1/2}}{||x||_{l^{2}}}\,x\right)=E\left(\frac{\nu^{1/2}}{||x||_{l^{2}}}\,x\right)-\frac{1}{2}\omega\nu\geq\underline{E}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\omega\nu.

Hence, relation (
4.5
) is true. If, moreover, the equality is true, then due to (
4.7
), it holds

(4.8)

E\left(\frac{\nu^{1/2}}{||x||_{l^{2}}}\,x\right)=\underline{E}_{\nu},

and

(4.9)

J(x)=J\left(\frac{\nu^{1/2}}{||x||_{l^{2}}}\,x\right).

By claim 1, this implies
||x||_{l^{2}}^{2}=\nu
, and hence,
E(x)=\underline{E}_{\nu}
.
That is,
x
is a minimizer of
M1
.
By (
4.5
), for every
x\in\mathcal{N}
, we also have,

(4.10)

J(x)\geq\underline{E}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\omega\nu=E(x)-\frac{1}{2}\omega||x||_{l^{2}}^{2}=J(x),

implying that
x
is a ground state of
M2
. Moreover, if
x
is a minimizer of
M1
, then

(4.11)

J(x)=E-\frac{1}{2}\omega||x||_{l^{2}}^{2}=\underline{E}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\omega||x||_{l^{2}}^{2},

so that the equality holds and claim 2 holds true.

For the final step of the proof, for given

\omega\in\{\omega(y)\,:\,y\in S_{\nu}\,\,{\text{i}s\,\,a\,\,minimizer\,\,of\,\,{\bf M1}}\}
, let
y\in l^{2}
be any ground state solution of
M2
. Then,

(4.12)

J(y)\leq\underline{E}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\omega\mu.

giving in conjunction with (
4.5
)
J(y)=\underline{E}_{\nu}-(\omega/2)\nu
. Finally,
by claim 2 it follows that
y
is a minimizer of
M1
finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\nu>0
holds

\inf_{x\in S_{\nu}}E(x)=\underline{E}_{\nu}<0.

Proof.

For
x\in S_{\nu}
we take
sx
with
s>0
and

\displaystyle E(sx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\left((sx_{n+1}-sx_{n})^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma+1}(sx_{n})^{2(\sigma+1)}-V_{0}(sx_{0})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}}{2}\sum_{n}\left((x_{n+1}-x_{n})^{2}-\frac{s^{2\sigma}}{\sigma+1}x_{n}^{2(\sigma+1)}-V_{0}x_{0}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{2}M_{x,1}-s^{2(\sigma+1)}M_{x,2}:=f(s).

As for
s
sufficiently large, the term
s^{2(\sigma+1)}
dominates it follows
E<0
. If
\inf_{x\in S_{\nu}}E(x)=\underline{E}_{\nu}
exists, then
\underline{E}_{\nu}<0
.
Furthermore, we bound
E
from below as follows

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2(\sigma+1)}||x||_{l^{2\sigma+2}}^{2\sigma+2}-\frac{V_{0}}{2}||x||_{l^{2}}^{2}\geq-\frac{\gamma}{2(\sigma+1)}||x||_{l^{2}}^{2\sigma+2}-\frac{V_{0}}{2}||x||_{l^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\gamma}{2(\sigma+1)}\nu^{\sigma}+\frac{V_{0}}{2}\right)\nu>-\infty.

Since

is bounded below, it attains its infimum
\underline{E}_{\nu}<0
in

.
∎

Since it is proven that a ground state of problem
M1
is also a ground state of problem
M2
, and due to the energy equivalence in relation (
1.12
), we apply
Weinstein’s criterion
[
42
]
, valid for the solution of problem
M2
, to investigate the existence of excitation thresholds for the formation of ground states. In detail, if a ground state (as a minimizer of
\inf_{x\in S_{\nu}}E(x)<0
) exists for any value of
\nu>0
, there is no excitation threshold. Conversely, if there exists a positive
\nu_{exc}
such that
\inf_{x\in S_{\nu}}E(x)<0
if and only if
\nu>\nu_{exc}
, then
\nu_{exc}
constitutes an excitation threshold.

In what follows, we assume that
x
is represented by exponentially decaying square-summable sequences for the following reasons:

1) As seen in Section
2
, in the linear limiting case,
\gamma=0
, the defect modes are exponentially localized at a single site (without loss of generality the origin of the lattice). Moreover, these defect modes are preserved for (at least) weak nonlinearity.

2) With concern to the (exponential) localization of ground states of the nonlinear system with
\gamma>0
, we exploit that for a self-adjoint operator
A
on a Hilbert space
X
and a compact operator
K
on
X
, one has that
\sigma(A+K)\setminus\sigma(A)
consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity
[
45
]
.
We present a consice proof of exponential localization of the solutions:

Proposition 4.2
.

Solutions
x\in l^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
to

(4.13)

\omega x_{n}+(\Delta x)_{n}+\gamma x_{n}^{2\sigma}x_{n}=0,\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

satisfy

|x_{n}|\leq C\exp(-\eta|n|),\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

with some constants
C,\eta>0
.

Proof.

We express (
4.13
) in the form

(4.14)

-(\Delta+M)x_{n}=\omega x_{n},

where the multiplication operator
M\,:\,l^{2}\mapsto l^{2}
is determined by

Mx_{n}=x_{n}^{2\sigma}\cdot x_{n}.

Since
\lim_{|n|\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}^{2\sigma}=0
, the multiplication operator
M
is compact on
l^{2}
.
As
\omega\notin\sigma(\Delta)
implies
\omega\in\sigma(\Delta+M)\setminus\sigma(\Delta)
, it follows that
\omega
is an eigenvalue of finite multiplicity of the operator
\Delta+M
. The associated eigenfunction exhibits exponential decay (see e.g. in
[
46
]
) and the proof is finished.
∎

With the proven exponential localization of ground states,

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\in l^{2}
can be represented as
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}=(A\exp(-\alpha|n|))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
with
\alpha>\beta
. (We recall that in systems with dispersive interaction
\Delta x
their solutions cannot decay faster than exponential.)

Using
\alpha=-\log(\eta)
with
0<\eta<1
, we express
x_{n}=A\exp(-\alpha|n|)
as

(4.15)

x_{n}=A\eta^{|n|},\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

with
|n|=|n_{1}|+...+|n_{d}|
and amplitude
A>0
determined by

A=\left(\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\,\nu\right)^{1/2}.

Note that
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
leads to enhanced localization towards a single-site state while
\eta\rightarrow 1^{-}
renders the profile wider (advancing towards a flat state of zero amplitude), i.e. varying
\eta
between zero and one interpolates between two nearly extreme states, namely a single site state and a wide (yet weakly localized) absolutely continuous spectrum near edge state.

The energy
E(x)
expressed in terms of

x_{n}=\left(\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\,\nu\right)^{1/2}\eta^{|n|},

reads

(4.16)

E=\left(\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}-(2\eta)^{d}}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}-\frac{\gamma}{\sigma+1}\left(\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\right)^{\sigma+1}\left(\frac{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}{(1-\eta)F(\eta,\sigma)}\right)^{d}\nu^{\sigma}-V_{0}\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\right)\,\nu,

where
F(\eta,\sigma)=\sum_{l=1}^{2\sigma+1}\eta^{l}+1
.

Subsequently we
investigate for which value(s) of the mass
\nu=||x||_{l^{2}}^{2}
a ground state solution in the form of a breather respectively impurity mode exists at all.

In this context we use the criterion

\underline{E}_{\nu}\leq E<0
that is necessary and sufficient for a state to be localized. The energy
E=E(\nu;\eta,\gamma,\sigma,V_{0})
is determined by (
4.16
).

We begin with the effect of the point defect on the existence of excitation thresholds
for the formation of ground state breather solutions for
\gamma>0
.

Proof of Theorem
2

1) Attractive delta potential
V_{0}>0
.

As noted in section
2
, for
V_{0}>0
the linear lattice with delta potential possesses a bound state (defect mode) of negative energy generated
by the presence of the point defect.

With the addition of a focusing nonlinear term to the linear equation, its associated contribution to the energy is
negative so that the total energy remains negative for all
\nu>0
. Hence, for focusing nonlinearity
\gamma>0
and attractive delta potential
V_{0}>0
the existence of an excitation threshold for the creation of breathers is ruled out.

2) Repulsive delta potential
V_{0}<0
.

From (
4.16
) we deduce that
E\geq 0
if and only if
the right-hand side of (
4.16
) is positive yielding the condition

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\left[2\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}-(2\eta)^{d}}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}-V_{0}\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\right]\left(\left(\frac{1+\eta^{2}}{1-\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\right)^{\sigma+1}\left(\frac{F(\eta,\sigma)(1-\eta)}{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}\right)^{d}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\nu^{\sigma}.

This implies an upper bound of
\nu
as follows

(4.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\inf_{0<\eta<1}\left(\frac{1}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}\left[2\left((1+\eta^{2})^{d}-(2\eta)^{d}\right)-V_{0}(1-\eta^{2})^{d}\right]\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{\sigma d}}{(1-\eta^{2})^{(\sigma+1)d}}\right.

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left.\left(\frac{F(\eta,\sigma)(1-\eta)}{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}\right)^{d}\right)\geq\sup_{\nu\geq 0}\nu^{\sigma}.

The inequality (
4.17
) is of the form

(4.18)

\displaystyle\nu^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\inf_{0<\eta<1}\left(\frac{1}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}\left[2F_{1}(\eta,d)(1-\eta)^{2}-V_{0}(1+\eta)^{d}(1-\eta)^{d}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left.\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{\sigma d}}{(1+\eta)^{(\sigma+1)d}(1-\eta)^{(\sigma+1)d}}\left(\frac{F(\eta,\sigma)(1-\eta)}{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}\right)^{d}\right),

where
F_{1}(\eta,d)(1-\eta)^{2}=(1+\eta^{2})^{d}-(2\eta)^{d}
is explicitly given for

by

F_{1}(\eta,1)=1
,
F_{1}(\eta,2)=(1+\eta)^{2}
,
F_{1}(\eta,3)=\eta^{4}+2\eta^{3}+6\eta^{2}+2\eta+1
,
F_{1}(\eta,4)=\eta^{6}+2\eta^{5}+7\eta^{4}+12\eta^{3}+7\eta^{2}+2\eta+1
.

(i)
\sigma<\min\{1,2/d\}
.
The infimum on the right-hand side of (
4.18
) is attained for
\eta\rightarrow 1^{-}
.
Setting
1-\eta=\epsilon
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{\sigma d}}{(1+\eta)^{(\sigma+1)d}}\frac{1}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}\left(\frac{F(\eta,\sigma)}{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}\right)^{d}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left[2F_{1}(\eta,d)\epsilon^{2-\sigma d}-V_{0}(1+\eta)^{d}\epsilon^{d(1-\sigma)}\right]=0.

We conclude, only for
\nu=0
it holds
E\geq 0
. That is, for
\sigma<\min\{1,2/d\}

one has
\underline{E}_{\nu}\leq E<0
for all
\nu>0
so that there is no excitation threshold. Hence, ground states of arbitrarily small amplitude that bifurcate off the linear zero solution exist.

(ii)
\sigma\geq\max\{1,2/d\}
. The infimum on the right-hand side of (
4.18
) is attained for
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}

yielding

\displaystyle\nu^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\frac{1}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}\left[2F_{1}(\eta,d)(1-\eta)^{2}-V_{0}(1+\eta)^{d}(1-\eta)^{d}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left.\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{\sigma d}}{(1+\eta)^{(\sigma+1)d}(1-\eta)^{(\sigma+1)d}}\left(\frac{F(\eta,\sigma)(1-\eta)}{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}\right)^{d}\right)=\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\left(2-V_{0}\right).

Thus,

if and only if

0\leq\nu\leq\nu_{1}=\left(\frac{(\sigma+1)(2-V_{0})}{\gamma}\right)^{1/\sigma}.

Conversely,
\underline{E}_{\nu}\leq E<0

(4.20)

\nu>\nu_{1}=\left(\frac{(\sigma+1)(2-V_{0})}{\gamma}\right)^{1/\sigma}>0.

Notice that condition (

\eta\sim 0^{+}

Writing (

where

\displaystyle A_{\eta,d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}-(2\eta)^{d}}{(1+\eta^{2})^{d}}-V_{0}\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}>0,

\displaystyle B_{\eta,d,\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\sigma+1}\left(\left(\frac{1-\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}\right)^{\sigma+1}\left(\frac{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}{(1-\eta)F(\eta,\sigma)}\right)^{d}>0,

one notices that

and

.

In conclusion,
\underline{H}_{\nu}=2\underline{E}_{\nu}<0
if and only if

, i.e. there is an excitation threshold for the existence of ground states finishing the proof of Theorem
2
.

To gain further insight into the localization features of the DNLS (
1.13
), we note that when
\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}
the excitation pattern approaches the single-site
state and the gap between the energy of the linear system,

E_{0}=\frac{(1-\eta)^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\nu,

and that of the nonlinear contribution (responsible for creating the potential well which makes possible at all localization),

becomes maximal. In order to ease the illustration we treat here the case

.
We obtain

\lim_{\eta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(E_{0}(\eta)+E_{1}(\eta)\right)=\nu\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{2\sigma+2}\nu^{\sigma}\right)\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\nu\leq\left(\frac{2\sigma+2}{\gamma}\right)^{1/\sigma}

Substituting the threshold value
\nu_{exc}=(2\sigma+2)/\gamma)^{1/\sigma}
into the expression for the energy

, it is readily seen that for

Conclusively,

if

.

\Box

We also discuss the localization behavior when

.
From the relation

(1+\eta^{2})^{d}A^{2}=(1-\eta^{2})^{d}\nu,

we deduce that if

then

,
in accordance with the result in
[

proving that as

increases, the ground states grow in amplitude and become increasingly concentrated about a single lattice site.

and

.

1) Defocusing nonlinearity
\gamma<0
and attractive delta potential

.

E\leq 0
if

(4.22)

\displaystyle\nu^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma}\inf_{0<\eta<1}\,\left(V_{0}\left(\frac{1+\eta}{1+\eta^{2}}\right)^{d}(1-\eta)^{d(1-\sigma)}-2\frac{F_{1}(\eta,d)}{1+\eta^{2}}(1-\eta)^{2-d\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(1+\eta^{2}\right)^{d(\sigma+1)}\left(\frac{F(\eta,\sigma)}{1+\eta^{2\sigma+2}}\right)^{d}.

If

Hence, the (linear) impurity mode only sustains defocusing nonlinearity

2) Focusing nonlinearity
\gamma>0

.

\displaystyle\nu^{\sigma+1}

Remark 4.1
.

For

and

the simultaneous existence of localized states below (breathers) as well as above (staggering defect mode) the absolutely continuous spectrum of

5.
Asymptotic behavior

In this section we discuss the asymptotic features of the
\delta
DNLS

i\frac{du_{n}}{dt}+(\Delta_{\delta}u)_{n}+\gamma|u_{n}|^{2\sigma}u_{n}=0,\,\,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

We begin with the case
V_{0}=0
.

Scattering:

Concerning the asymptotic properties
we consider the linear Schrödinger group
\exp(it\Delta)
.
In the regime where no nonlinear localized state exists, that is for
V_{0}<0
,
\sigma\geq\max\{1,2/d\}
and
V_{0}=0
,
\sigma\geq 2/d
, respectively, and
l^{2}
norm below
\nu_{exc}
, we establish that the solutions
scatter to a solution of the linear problem in
l^{p}
,
p>2
. That is,
the solutions of the nonlinear problem exhibit asymptotically free behavior.
A solution is said to scatter in the positive (negative) time direction if there exists
v^{\pm}\in l^{p}

such that

\left\|u(t)-\exp(i\Delta t)v^{\pm}\right\|_{l^{p}}\longrightarrow 0\,\,\,{\rm as}\,\,\,t\rightarrow\pm\infty.

The solution scatters if it scatters in both time directions.

Proof of Theorem
3

We begin with the treatment of scattering in the positive time direction.
For the Cauchy problem with initial datum
u_{0}\in l^{2}
we consider the
global solution
u(t)
and introduce the asymptotic state
v^{+}

(5.1)

v^{+}=u_{0}+i\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\exp(-i\Delta s)|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds.

Application of the operator
\exp(i\Delta t)
on either side of
(
5.1
) yields

\displaystyle\exp(i\Delta t)v^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(i\Delta t)u_{0}+i\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(t)+i\gamma\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds.

Using the estimate (5.2)
we derive

\displaystyle\left\|\exp(i\Delta t)v^{+}-u(t)\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\left\|\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\left\|\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)\right\|_{l^{p}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\gamma\left\||u(s)|\right\|_{l^{(2\sigma+1)p^{\prime}}}^{2\sigma+1}ds,

and we used the Strichartz estimate
[
47
]

||\exp(i\Delta t)u(0)||_{l^{p}}\leq C<t>^{-d(p-2)/(3p)}||u(0)||_{l^{p^{\prime}}},\qquad 2\leq p\leq\infty,

arising from an interpolation between

||u(t)||_{l^{2}}=||u(0)||_{l^{2}},

and

||u(t)||_{l^{\infty}}=C<t>^{-d/3}||u(0)||_{l^{1}},

where
<t>=1+|t|
.
By assumption
2(\sigma+1)\geq p
one has
(2\sigma+1)p^{\prime}\geq p
. Then due to the continuous embeddings
l^{(2\sigma+1)p^{\prime}}\subseteq l^{p}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\exp(i\Delta t)v^{+}-u(t)\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\gamma\left\|u(s)\right\|_{l^{p}}^{2\sigma+1}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{\gamma\left\|u(0)\right\|_{l^{p^{\prime}}}^{2\sigma+1}}{s^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\gamma\left\|u(0)\right\|_{l^{p^{\prime}}}^{2\sigma+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds.

We evaluate the integral as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Our assumption
p\geq 4d\sigma/(2d\sigma-3)
implies
d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)>1
. Therefore, for
I_{1}
we estimate

I_{1}=\int_{0}^{t}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds\leq C\frac{1}{<t>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}.

For
I_{2}
we proceed as follows

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{(1+s-t)^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{(1+s)^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds.

The change of variables
s=tz
gives

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\,\frac{t}{(1+t(z-1))^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{(1+tz)^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}dz.

A further change of variables
z=1/s
results in

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\,\frac{t}{s^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+ts^{-1}(1-s))^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{(1+ts^{-1})^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\,\left(1-s\right)^{-d(p-2)/(3p)}\,s^{2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-2}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1}}\,B\left(2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1,1-d(p-2)/(3p)\right),

where
B(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}s^{x-1}(1-s)^{y-1}ds
defines the beta-function.
We obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\exp(i\Delta t)v^{+}-u(t)\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{1}{t^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left.B\left(2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1,1-d(p-2)/(3p)\right)\right),\,\,\,\,\forall t>0,

so that we deduce
v^{+}\in l^{p}
.

Furthermore, applying the operator
\exp(-i\Delta t)

on both sides of the integral equation

u(t)=\exp(i\Delta t)u_{0}-i\gamma\int_{0}^{t}\,\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds,

we obtain

\displaystyle\exp(-i\Delta t)u(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{0}-i\gamma\int_{0}^{t}\,\exp(-i\Delta s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v^{+}+i\gamma\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\exp(-i\Delta s)|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds.

Thus,

\exp(-i\Delta t)u(t)-v^{+}=i\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\exp(-i\Delta s)\gamma|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds,

and we get

\displaystyle\left\|\exp(-i\Delta t)u(t)-v^{+}\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\left\|\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\exp(-i\Delta s)|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\left\|\int_{t}^{\infty}\,\exp(-i\Delta t)\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)ds\right\|_{l^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\left\|\exp(-i\Delta t)\exp(i\Delta(t-s))|u(s)|^{2\sigma}u(s)\right\|_{l^{p}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{\left\|u(s)\right\|_{l^{(2\sigma+1)p^{\prime}}}^{2\sigma+1}}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}ds\,\left\|\exp(-i\Delta t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(l^{p},l^{p^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\gamma\,\frac{\left\|u(0)\right\|_{l^{p^{\prime}}}^{2\sigma+1}}{<t>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{<t-s>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\,\frac{1}{<s>^{d(p-2)(2\sigma+1)/(3p)}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\gamma\,\left\|u(0)\right\|_{l^{p^{\prime}}}^{2\sigma+1}\,\frac{1}{<t>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\left(\frac{1}{<t>^{d(p-2)/(3p)}}\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\frac{1}{t^{2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1}}\,B\left(2(\sigma+1)d(p-2)/(3p)-1,1-d(p-2)/(3p)\right)\right),

for all
t>0
. Since by assumption
2(\sigma+1)(p-2)/(3p)-1>d(p-2)/(3p)>0
it follows

\left\|\exp(-i\Delta t)u(t)-v^{+}\right\|_{l^{p}}\leq C||u_{0}||_{l^{p^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{t^{d(p-2)/(3p)}},\,\,\,t>0.

Scattering in the negative time direction can be dealt with in the same way as above in
the positive time direction. In fact, due to the time reversibility of system (
1.1
) it holds
v^{-}=\overline{v}^{+}
.
Hence, we obtain

\left\|u(t)-\exp(i\Delta t)v^{\pm}\right\|_{l^{p}}\longrightarrow 0\,\,\,{\rm as}\,\,\,t\rightarrow\pm\infty.

and the proof is complete.

\Box

With concern to the absolutely continuous spectrum of

, governing the scattering features, we have the following:

Proposition 5.1
.

The absolutely continuous spectrum of
\Delta_{\sigma}
coincides with those of the unperturbed discrete Laplacian
\Delta
, i.e.

\sigma_{cont}(\Delta_{\delta})=\sigma_{cont}(\Delta).

Proof.

Consider the multiplication operator
V_{\delta}\,:\,l^{p}\mapsto l^{p}
,

V=(V_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|V_{n}|<\infty
,
V_{\delta}x=(...,V_{-1}x_{-1},V_{0}x_{0},V_{1}x_{1},...)
for all
x=(...,x_{-1},x_{0},x_{1},...)\in l^{p}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Since
V_{n}\neq 0
if and only if
n=0
, the operator
V_{\delta}
is a rank-one operator and hence, compact.

Then by Weyl’s Theorem, the essential spectrum of
\Delta
coincides with the essential spectrum of
\Delta_{\delta}

\Delta

\sigma_{cont}(\Delta)\subset\sigma_{ess}(\Delta)

\sigma_{cont}(\Delta_{\delta})
equals
\sigma_{cont}(\Delta)

finishing the proof.

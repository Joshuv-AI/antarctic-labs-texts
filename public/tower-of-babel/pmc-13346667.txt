Performance evaluation of tunicate-enhanced NGO resource optimization in RF energy harvesting–assisted NOMA edge computing
Authors: M Arulvizhi, K. C. Sriharipriya, Veerapu Goutham
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nowadays, farmers across the globe are gradually adopting intelligent farming, which is facilitated by a variety of cutting-edge technologies. The advancement of intelligent farming applications is greatly aided by the internet of farming things (IoFT). Massive IoFT devices generally possess constrained resources, making it challenging to meet the battery and computational requirements of intelligent farming applications through local computation. RF energy harvesting enabled mobile edge computing (RFE-MEC) addresses this issue by harvesting RF energy from an access point, offloading and computing tasks at the edge in a nearby access point. In the proposed scheme, multiuser nonorthogonal multiple access allows the IoFT devices to simultaneously offload computationally intensive tasks to the MEC server for processing. The delay outage probability closed-form expression is formulated for the RFE-NOMA-MEC intelligent farming system under a Rayleigh fading channel. The impact of imperfect channel state information on the RFE-NOMA-MEC is considered. Tunicate enhanced northern goshawk optimization algorithm (TNGO) has been proposed to discover the optimal parameter set to minimize delay outage probability. The results indicate that the system performance is enhanced using TNGO when the optimal time switching factor, power allocation coefficient and task allocation ratio are utilized.

Introduction

Agriculture is one of the key foundations of human society. The demand for agricultural production will expand with the anticipated growth in the world population1. The conventional agricultural methods require added effort and resources from farmers. Farmers must visit agricultural locations often during the crop life to gain a better understanding of crop conditions in conventional agricultural methods. Subsequently, 70% of farming time is spent on tracking and comprehending crop conditions rather than engaging in field activities; Smart agriculture becomes necessary to reduce the time required for farm maintenance. Numerous problems arise from this conventional agricultural method, such as resource scarcity, population growth, climate conditions, etc. Consequently, intelligent farming has been presented as a cutting-edge, technologically advanced strategy to increase productivity. Intelligent farming exploits technologies like artificial intelligence, Internet of things, wireless technologies, and many others to enhance harvesting experiences. The objective is to maximize the production of crops with greater quality and quantity while making the best utilization of the available resources2.

Modern communication and sensor technologies enable farmers to see events in the field without physically being there. The internet of farming things (IoFT) can be used to gather a variety of farming field data, which is used to construct various sub-operations of farming applications namely, crop monitoring, disease and pest detection, crop waste prevention through efficient harvesting are all made feasible by IoFT networks in intelligent farming3. However, IoFT services are typically computationally demanding and delay sensitive. It is challenging to perform the tasks produced by such IoFT devices locally due to limited storage, battery, and computational capabilities4. Offloading the computationally demanding work to the cloud centre is one possible solution to this contradiction. But this might not always be an appropriate approach for computationally demanding and delay-sensitive tasks because of the significant communication distance between the IoFT devices and the cloud5. Furthermore, offloading a lot of computationally demanding tasks to the cloud will result in significant stress on the network and a rise in data traffic6. Utilizing mobile-edge computing servers at access points (MSAP) near IoFT devices reduces the data traffic; Hence, mobile-edge computing (MEC) is therefore considered a feasible concept to get beyond these obstacles. In comparison to IoFT devices MSAP often possesses more effective computational and storage capabilities. As a result, IoFT offloads its computational tasks to the MSAP, extending the life of its batteries7.

At the same time, the energy problem is another issue in designing IoFT devices because batteries and wireless devices use a lot of energy. Radio frequency energy harvesting (RFE) is a relatively new technique that has the potential to extend the lifespan of IoFT devices. Previous study findings indicate that IoFT devices’ computing performance can be enhanced by incorporating the RFE approach into MEC networks8–10. The IoFT devices use a multiple-access approach to offload their tasks to MSAP. When massive connectivity is needed, the orthogonal multiple access (OMA) method is not appropriate, which leads to a burden on radio spectrum resources11. Consequently, nonorthogonal multiple access (NOMA), which promotes multiuser spectrum sharing, is implemented to increase spectrum efficiency and offer massive connectivity. By utilizing the NOMA power domain multiplexing technique, multiple users can simultaneously send signals on the same resources at the same time. To control co-channel interference, successive interference cancellation (SIC) is employed in NOMA receivers12. Hence, the combination of multiuser NOMA and MEC can significantly enhance the offloading of computational tasks, which is a potential approach for next-generation wireless communications13,14. Furthermore, the NOMA-MEC solutions have resulted in considerable advances in terms of energy minimization and delay reduction under a variety of restrictions5,15. In16, a NOMA-based MEC surveillance system is examined, in which two camera items offload duties to an edge server over Rayleigh fading channels. The system evaluation is based on successful computation probability (SCP).

Despite a lot of current research on NOMA-MEC systems concentrating on independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) channel models and only considering two users for analytical ease. This study employs multiuser RFE-NOMA-MEC intelligent farming (RFE-NOMA-MEC-IF) with Rayleigh fading channels, thereby offering a more realistic and generalized scheme for intelligent farming applications under perfect channel state information (PCSI) and imperfect channel state information (IPCSI) available at the access point. Moreover, the literature predominantly highlights energy usage and latency improvement. It is still necessary to explore several issues in the RFE-NOMA-MEC network, most notably the outage probability that includes end-to-end system latency, including offloading latency, computational latency and ensures the operation of intelligent farming devices in real time. Delay-outage probability expressions are developed for OFDMA-MEC with N edge nodes. The findings indicate that using a single edge node is beneficial in terms of improving dependability and reducing delay17. The delay-outage probability expressions for RFE-NOMA-MEC systems have not been developed analytically. Also, the researcher considers solely offloading delay, neglecting the cumulative impact of both computation and offloading delays, which is essential in real-world edge computing contexts. The task computation time at the MEC server is primarily neglected in NOMA-MEC studies, which primarily focus on task offloading delay18. This may lead to poorer performance and inaccurate delay outage estimation in real-time intelligent farming applications. The proposed algorithm resolves this problem by considering task computation delays and task offloading delays in an RFE-NOMA-MEC system.

Furthermore, conventional Northern goshawk possess a dynamic balancing strategy among exploration and exploitation, and current perturbation techniques fail to properly retain population diversity during subsequent rounds19. To resolve these challenges, Tunicate enhanced Northern goshawk optimization algorithm (TNGO) has been proposed. This framework can collaborate on cubic mapping , tunicate exploration with Levy flight, and a Gaussian mutation to accomplish three primary objectives: i) enhancing initial solution accuracy by using uniform distribution; ii) achieving a dynamic relationship between global and local search; and iii) preserving diversity to prevent premature convergence. In this proposed model, several IoFT devices(ID) gather energy from RF signals and use this energy for computing tasks and offloading them to a nearby MEC server via NOMA transmission. Methodology

This section employs a methodology aimed at reducing the DOP in a multi-user NOMA-MEC system that utilizes RF energy harvesting. Initially, a system model is constructed, taking into account numerous ID that gather RF energy and offload tasks to an MEC server via NOMA transmission. The model also includes the effects of IPCSI to reflect real-world wireless conditions. Using this model, the closed form expression of DOP is derived, and an optimization problem is established. To effectively address the resulting non-convex problem, a TNGO algorithm is utilized to minimize the DOP.

However, in scenarios when devices are severely resource-constrained or applications require substantial local processing, the delay in local computation may approach that of offloading latency. Tunicate enhanced Northern goshawk optimization algorithm

In this part, TNGO has been proposed for the RFE-NOMA-MEC intelligent farming system to minimize the DOP by determining the optimal value for time switching factor, power allocation ratio and task allocation ratio. Due to its nonlinear, interference-dependent, and logarithmic factors, the specified RFE-NOMA-MEC optimization problem is essentially nonconvex and cannot be solved using typical convex methods. Thus, metaheuristic algorithms are used because they can effectively handle such complicated, multimodal search spaces without requiring convexity.

Tunicate enhanced Northern goshawk optimization algorithm

The conventional NGO algorithm is subject to premature convergence and trapping in local optima during the optimization procedure. The TNGO algorithm integrates the exploratory features of the northern goshawk search strategy with the adaptive movement behavior of the tunicate mechanism, thereby optimizing the equilibrium between global exploration and local exploitation. Three modifications, namely cubic mapping, tunicate with levy flight and gaussian mutation, are implemented to improve the performance and applicability of the NGO for the specific issue of DOP.

Chaotic cubic mapping

In the original NGO method, the initial population is generated randomly, potentially resulting in an inadequate population range. Initially, establish the population utilizing a cubic mapping to enhance the uniformity of the population distribution and elevate the quality of the initial solution. Numerous researchers have demonstrated that cubic chaotic mapping enhances population range, facilitating the optimization algorithm ability to identify the global optimal solution more effectively42. Conclusion

In this study, DOP is derived with the consideration of offloading and computation latency to examine the RFE-NOMA-MEC based intelligent farming across Rayleigh fading channels. Moreover, the simulation results attest that the IoFT device performance is dependent on the time switching factor, power allocation ratio and task allocation ratio. TNGO has been proposed to minimize the DOP of IoFT devices by optimally selecting the above parameters. This paper presents an extensive evaluation of the proposed TNGO optimization methods using DOP, throughput in IPCSI conditions. TNGO algorithm yields better DOP than NGO, GOA, OOA and WOA optimization algorithms in RFE-NOMA-MEC-IF system. This study assumes perfect SIC to simplify the analytical approach. In practical NOMA systems, ISIC can result in residual interference, thereby increasing the DOP. Examining ISIC within the analytical framework is an essential expansion that will be investigated in a subsequent study. The extension model for the suggested system, which includes devices with multiple antennas will be examined using IoFT device real time data in our subsequent research.

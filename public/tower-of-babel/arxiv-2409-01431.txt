On the second largest adjacency eigenvalue of trees with given diameter
Hitesh Kumar, Bojan Mohar, Shivaramakrishna Pragada, Hanmeng Zhan

Abstract
For a graph G G , let λ 2 ​ ( G ) \lambda_{2}(G) denote the second largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix of G G . We determine the extremal trees with maximum/minimum adjacency eigenvalue λ 2 \lambda_{2} in the class 𝒯 ⁡ ( n , d ) \mathcal{T}(n,d) of n n -vertex trees with diameter d d . This contributes to the literature on λ 2 \lambda_{2} -extremization over different graph families. We also revisit the notion of the spectral center of a tree and the proof of λ 2 \lambda_{2} maximization over trees.

adjacency matrix, second largest adjacency eigenvalue, diameter, spectral center, caterpillar, Smith graphs

Throughout this section,

Theorem 2.1

(Interlacing Theorem)
.

Let

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}

2.1
Characteristic polynomials

For a graph
G
, its
characteristic polynomial
is given by
\Phi(G,x):=\det(xI-A(G))
. The following lemma is easy to prove. For more details refer
[
10
, Section 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(G_{1},a)
and
(G_{2},b)
be two disjoint rooted graphs with roots
a
and
b
respectively.

(i)

If
G=(G_{1},a)\circ(G_{2},b)
then

\Phi(G,x)=\Phi(G_{1},x)\Phi(G_{2},x)-\Phi(G_{1}-a,x)\Phi(G_{2}-b,x).

(ii)

If
G=(G_{1},a)\circ K_{1}\circ(G_{2},b)
then

\Phi(G,x)=x\Phi(G_{1},x)\Phi(G_{2},x)-\Phi(G_{1},x)\Phi(G_{2}-b,x)-\Phi(G_{1}-a,x)\Phi(G_{2},x).

(iii)

If
G
is obtained from
(G_{1},a)
and
(G_{2},b)
by identifying
a
and
b
then

\Phi(G,x)=\Phi(G_{1}-a,x)\Phi(G_{2},x)+\Phi(G_{1},x)\Phi(G_{2}-b,x)-x\Phi(G_{1}-a,x)\Phi(G_{2}-b,x).

For any vertex
a
in
G
, the number of closed walks of length
k
starting at
a
is given by
A(G)^{k}_{a,a}
. It is known that the
walk-generating function

W(G,x)=I+xA(G)+x^{2}A(G)^{2}+\cdots

converges to
(I-xA(G))^{-1}
with radius of convergence
\lambda_{1}(G)^{-1}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
13
]
, Chapter 5)
.

Let
W_{a,a}(G,x)
be the generating function of closed walks at vertex
a
in a graph
G
. Then for
x>\lambda_{1}(G)
,

\frac{\Phi(G-a,x)}{\Phi(G,x)}=x^{-1}W_{a,a}(G,x^{-1})=(xI-A(G))^{-1}_{a,a}.

The above lemma applied to paths gives the following. For proof, see
[
20
, Lemma 14]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
P_{\ell}
denote the
\ell
-vertex path. For
x\in(2,\infty)
, we have

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(P_{\ell-1},x)}{\Phi(P_{\ell},x)}=\frac{1}{2}(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-4}).

We also observe the following technical fact, which will be helpful later.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G\neq K_{1}
be a connected graph and
a\in V(G)
. If
\lambda_{1}(G-a)<\lambda
then
\Phi(G,\lambda)<\lambda\Phi(G-a,\lambda)
.

Proof.

If
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda
then
\lambda_{1}(G)\geq\lambda>\lambda_{1}(G-a)\geq\lambda_{2}(G)
where the last inequality follows by the Interlacing Theorem. It follows that
\Phi(G,\lambda)\leq 0
and
\Phi(G-a,\lambda)>0
. On the other hand, if
\lambda_{1}(G)<\lambda
then
\Phi(G,\lambda)>0
. Consequently, using
Lemma 2.2
,

\frac{\Phi(G,\lambda)-\lambda\Phi(G-a,\lambda)}{\Phi(G,\lambda)}=1-W_{a,a}(G,\lambda^{-1})=-\big(\lambda^{-1}A(G)_{a,a}+\lambda^{-2}A^{2}(G)_{a,a}+\cdots)<0.

The last inequality holds since
G\neq K_{1}
.

Several graph perturbations are known in literature which increase (or decrease)

Lemma 2.5

Let

\lambda_{1}(G+uv)>\lambda_{1}(G)

Under special conditions, edge rotations in a graph also increase the spectral radius.

-eigenvector of

. If

, then
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{1}(G)
.

Fix two vertices

Let

\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{1}(G)

Lemma 2.8

Let

2.3
Some results about
\lambda_{2}

Subgraphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
of a graph
G
are said to be
strongly disjoint
if they are vertex disjoint and there are no edges in
G
with one endpoint in
H_{1}
and the other endpoint in
H_{2}.

Lemma 2.9

(Strongly Disjoint Subgraphs Lemma,
[
19
,
6
]
)
.

Suppose
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are strongly disjoint subgraphs of a connected graph
G.
If
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})>\lambda_{2}(G)
then
\lambda_{1}(H_{2})<\lambda_{2}(G).
In other words, if
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})>\lambda
and
\lambda_{2}(H_{2})\geq\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
then
\lambda_{2}(G)>\lambda
.

For a vector
y=(y_{v})_{v\in V(G)}\in\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}
, define the
support
of
y
to be the set
S(y)=\{v\in V(G):y_{v}\neq 0\}
. The
positive support
of
y
is
S^{+}(y)=\{v\in V(G):y_{v}>0\}
, the
negative support
of
y
is
S^{-}(y)=\{v\in V(y):y_{v}<0\}
, and the
zero set
of
y
is
S^{0}(y)=\{v\in V(G):y_{v}=0\}
.

Let
G[S(y)]
denote the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices in
S(y).
A (connected) component of
G[S(y)]
is called a
y
-component
. Similarly, one can define
G[S^{*}(y)]
and
y^{*}
-component for
*\in\{+,-,0\}
. Note that if
y
is the
\lambda_{2}
-eigenvector of a connected graph
G
, then
y
is orthogonal to the positive
\lambda_{1}
-eigenvector of
G
and therefore
S^{+}(y)
and
S^{-}(y)
are non-empty.

As an application of the above lemma, Chen and Hao
[
6
]
obtained the following results. We mention here that closely related results (perhaps not in the exact same form) can be found in the works of Fiedler
[
12
]
and Powers
[
25
]
. See also
[
10
, Chapter 8]
and
[
5
, Chapter 5]
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
y
be a
\lambda_{2}
-eigenvector of
G
. If
H
is a
y^{+}
-component (
y^{-}
-component), then
\lambda_{1}(H)\geq\lambda_{2}
and equality holds if and only if
N(H)\cap S^{-}(y)=\emptyset
(
N(H)\cap S^{+}(y)=\emptyset
).

Lemma 2.11

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
y
be a
\lambda_{2}
-eigenvector of
G.

(i)

If
G[S(y)]
is connected then
G[S^{+}(y)]
and
G[S^{-}(y)]
are connected.

(ii)

If
G[S(y)]
is disconnected then every
y
-component is a
y^{+}
-component or a
y^{-}
-component. Moreover, if
H
is any
y
-component then
\lambda_{1}(H)=\lambda_{2}.

(iii)

For
v\in S^{0}(y),
if
N(v)\cap S(y)\neq\emptyset,
then
v
is adjacent to every
y^{+}
-component and every
y^{-}
-component.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
y
be a
\lambda_{2}
-eigenvector of
G
with minimal support (i.e.
|S(y)|
is minimal among all
\lambda_{2}
-eigenvectors). Then,
G
has a unique
y^{+}
-component, and a unique
y^{-}
-component.

A proof of
Corollary 2.1
can also be found in
[
5
, Chapter 5]
.

We will often use the following observation without mention in this paper.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be two graphs on
n
-vertices.

(i)

If
\lambda_{1}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{2}(G)
and
\Phi(G^{\prime},\lambda_{2}(G))>0
then
\lambda_{2}(G^{\prime})>\lambda_{2}(G)
.

(ii)

If
\lambda_{3}(G^{\prime})<\lambda_{2}(G)
and
\Phi(G^{\prime},\lambda_{2}(G))<0
then
\lambda_{2}(G^{\prime})<\lambda_{2}(G)
.

3
Spectral center of a tree

We now turn to a result about the
spectral center
, a notion of centrality in trees defined using their second largest eigenvalue. We first show the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n\geq 2
vertices. Let
y
be a
\lambda_{2}(T)
-eigenvector with minimal support. Then there exist rooted subtrees
(H_{1},a)
and
(H_{2},b)
such that
H_{1}
is the unique
y^{+}
-component and
H_{2}
is the unique
y^{-}
-component of
T
. Furthermore, exactly one of the following holds.

(i)

If
S^{0}(y)\neq\emptyset
then there is a unique vertex
v\in S^{0}(y)
such that
T^{\prime}=(H_{1},a)\circ v\circ(H_{2},b)
is a subtree of
T
,
V(T)\backslash V(T^{\prime})\subseteq S^{0}(y)
and
T[S^{0}(y)]
is connected. Moreover,

\lambda_{1}(H_{1})=\lambda_{1}(H_{2})=\lambda_{2}(T).

The vertex
v
is called the
spectral vertex
of
T
.

(ii)

If
S^{0}(y)=\emptyset
then
T=(H_{1},a)\circ(H_{2},b)
and

\lambda_{1}(H_{1}-a)<\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(H_{1})\text{ and }\lambda_{1}(H_{2}-b)<\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(H_{2}).

The edge
ab
is called the
spectral edge
of
T
.

Proof.

By
Corollary 2.1
,
T
has a unique
y^{+}
-component (say
H_{1}
) and a unique
y^{-}
-component (say
H_{2}
). We make the following cases:

Case 1:

S^{0}(y)\neq\emptyset
.
Since
T
is connected, there exists a vertex
v\in S^{0}(y)
such that
N(v)\cap S(y)\neq\emptyset
. By
Lemma 2.11
,
N(v)\cap V(H_{i})\neq\emptyset
for
i=1,2
. Since
T
is acyclic, this vertex
v
is unique, and
v
has a unique neighbour (say
a
) in
H_{1}
and a unique neighbour (say
b
) in
H_{2}
. Then
(H_{1},a)
and
(H_{2},b)
are the required rooted subtrees of
T
. Since,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are strongly disjoint, we conclude that
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})=\lambda_{1}(H_{2})=\lambda_{2}(T)
by
Lemma 2.10
. Also, any vertex outside of
T^{\prime}
in
T
has to lie in
S^{0}(y)
by the choice of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
. Moreover, any vertex in
S^{0}(y)
other than
v
cannot have any neighbour in
H_{1}
or
H_{2}
; hence
S^{0}(y)
induces a subtree rooted at
v
in
T
.

Case 2:

S^{0}(y)=\emptyset
.
Then
T[S(y)]=T
is connected. By
Lemma 2.11
,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are connected. Since,
T
is a tree, there exists a unique edge
ab
in
T
joining
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
where
a
lies in
H_{1}
and
b
lies in
H_{2}
. Thus,
T=(H_{1},a)\circ(H_{2},b)
. Moreover, by
Lemma 2.10
,

\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(H_{1})\text{ and }\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(H_{2})
.
Using
Lemma 2.9
and the above inequalities, we conclude that

\lambda_{1}(H_{1}-a)<\lambda_{2}(T)
and
\lambda_{1}(H_{2}-b)<\lambda_{2}(T)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

A result similar in flavour to the above theorem was first proved by Neumaier
[
24
]
. He showed that every tree
T
is a
\lambda
-twin or
\lambda
-trivial for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{2}(T)
, which correspond to the situations when
T
has a spectral edge or a spectral vertex, respectively. The following immediate corollary can be found in
[
5
, Chapter 5]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Every tree
T
on
n\geq 2
vertices has a unique minimal subtree
Y
on at most two vertices such that
\lambda_{1}(T-Y)\leq\lambda_{2}(T)
. Moreover, if
|V(Y)|=1
then
\lambda_{1}(T-Y)=\lambda_{2}(T)
, and if
|V(Y)|=2
then
\lambda_{1}(T-Y)<\lambda_{2}(T)
.

The subtree
Y
from the above corollary is called the
spectral center
of the tree
T
.

4
On caterpillars of type
C(\ell,r,k)

In this section, we investigate caterpillars of the type
C(\ell,r,k)
(for definition, see
section 1
). We will see in
section 5
that
\lambda_{2}
-maximizers in
\mathcal{T}(n,d)
are obtained by taking two caterpillars of this type and joining them by an edge. Using
Lemma 2.1
, we get the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T=C(\ell,r,k)\in\mathcal{T}(\ell+r+k+1,\ell+r)
.

(i)

If
k=1
then
\Phi(T,x)=x^{k-1}\big(x\Phi(P_{\ell+r+1},x)-k\Phi(P_{\ell},x)\Phi(P_{r},x)\big)
.

(ii)

If
k=0
then
\Phi(T,x)=\Phi(P_{\ell+r+1},x)
.

Here
P_{\ell}
denotes a path on
\ell
vertices. Note that if
\ell=0
then
\Phi(P_{\ell},x)=1
.

We say (roughly speaking) that
C(\ell,r,k)
is a balanced caterpillar if
|\ell-r|
is small. The following lemma compares the characteristic polynomial of a more balanced caterpillar with that of a less balanced caterpillar in the interval
(2,\infty)
. We work with this interval because the spectral radius of a path is at most 2.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\ell\geq r\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
. Then for all
x>2
,

\Phi(C(\ell+1,r-1,k),x)>\Phi(C(\ell,r,k),x).

Proof.

By
Lemma 4.1
,

\Phi(C(\ell+1,r-1,k),x)-\Phi(C(\ell,r,k),x)=kx^{k-1}\big[\Phi(P_{\ell},x)\Phi(P_{r},x)-\Phi(P_{\ell+1},x)\Phi(P_{r-1},x)\big],

which is positive for
x>2
if and only if

\frac{\Phi(P_{\ell},x)}{\Phi(P_{\ell+1},x)}>\frac{\Phi(P_{r-1},x)}{\Phi(P_{r},x)},

which, by
Lemma 2.2
, occurs if and only if
W_{v,v}(P_{\ell+1},x^{-1})>W_{v,v}(P_{r},x^{-1})
,
where
v
denotes the first vertex of
P_{\ell+1}
and
P_{r}
. Since
\ell+1>r
, the path
P_{r}
is a proper induced subgraph of
P_{\ell+1}
, and the above inequality holds.
∎

Next, we find an upper bound for the spectral radius of
C(\ell,r,k)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\ell,r,k
be nonnegative integers. Then
\lambda_{1}(C(\ell,r,k))<\sqrt{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}+2}
.

Proof.

If
k=0
then
C(\ell,r,k)=P_{\ell+r+1}
and the claim holds. So, assume
k\geq 1
. We will show that

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(C(\ell,\ell,k))=\sqrt{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}+2}.

By
Lemma 4.1
,
x>0
is an eigenvalue for
C(\ell,\ell,k)
if and only if

\frac{k}{x}=\frac{\Phi(P_{2\ell+1},x)}{\Phi(P_{\ell},x)^{2}}=\frac{2\Phi(P_{\ell+1},x)\Phi(P_{\ell},x)-x\Phi(P_{\ell},x)^{2}}{\Phi(P_{\ell},x)^{2}}=\frac{2\Phi(P_{\ell+1},x)}{\Phi(P_{\ell},x)}-x,

where the second equality holds by
Lemma 2.1
. Using
Lemma 2.3
, for all
x>2
,

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(P_{\ell+1},x)}{\Phi(P_{\ell},x)}=\frac{1}{2}(x+\sqrt{x^{2}-4}).

Thus
\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\lambda_{1}(C(\ell,\ell,k))
is equal to the largest root (provided it is bigger than 2) of

\frac{k}{x}=\sqrt{x^{2}-4}.

(1)

The largest root of (
1
) is
\sqrt{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}+2}
and it is bigger than 2 whenever
k\geq 1
.
∎

The following proposition suggests that
k
(i.e. the number of pendant vertices not on the main path in
C(\ell,r,k)
) is determined by the spectral radius of
C(\ell,r,k)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
H_{1}=C(\ell_{1},r_{1},k_{1})
and
H_{2}=C(\ell_{2},r_{2},k_{2})
where
\ell_{i}\geq 1
and
r_{i}\geq 1
for
i=1,2
. If
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})=\lambda_{1}(H_{2})>2
then
k_{1}=k_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that
k_{1}<k_{2}
. Since
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})>2
,
H_{1}
is not a path and hence
k_{1}\geq 1
. If
k_{1}\geq 2
, then by
Proposition 4.1
,

\lambda_{1}(H_{1})<\sqrt{\sqrt{k_{1}^{2}+4}+2}<\sqrt{k_{1}+3}\leq\sqrt{k_{2}+2}\leq\lambda_{1}(H_{2}).

This is a contradiction. Suppose now that
k_{1}=1
. Then
k_{2}\geq 2
. Since
\lambda_{1}(H_{2})>2
,
H_{2}\ncong K_{1,4}
. So,
C(1,2,2)
or
C(1,1,3)
is a subgraph of
H_{2}
. By
Proposition 4.1
,

\lambda_{1}(H_{1})<\sqrt{\sqrt{5}+2}<\sqrt{\frac{5+\sqrt{13}}{2}}=\min\{\lambda_{1}(C(1,2,2)),\lambda_{1}(C(1,1,3))\}\leq\lambda_{1}(H_{2}),

a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with bounds for
\lambda_{2}
of the caterpillar
C(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil,n-d-1)
. This will be useful in
section 6
when we minimize

in
\mathcal{T}(n,d)
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
n\geq d+1
and
T=C(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil,n-d-1)
be a caterpillar on the path
v_{0}\ldots v_{d}
.

(i)

If
d
is even then
\lambda_{2}(T)=\lambda_{1}(P_{\frac{d}{2}})
.

(ii)

If
d
is odd then
\lambda_{1}(P_{\frac{d-1}{2}})<\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(P_{\frac{d+1}{2}})
.

Proof.

Using the Interlacing Theorem,
\lambda_{1}(P_{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor})\leq\lambda_{2}(T)\leq\lambda_{1}(P_{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil})
. Claim
(i)
is now immediate. So suppose
d
is odd. Let
(H_{1},a)
and
(H_{2},b)
be the rooted subtrees of
T
as in
Theorem 3.1
. Clearly, both
a
and
b
lie on the path
v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}
. If
T
has a spectral vertex then one of the
H_{i}
(say
H_{1}
) is a subgraph of
P_{\frac{d-1}{2}}
and the other one (
H_{2}
) contains
P_{\frac{d+1}{2}}
as a subgraph. But then
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})\neq\lambda_{1}(H_{2})
. This contradicts case
(i)
of
Theorem 3.1
. So
T
has a spectral edge, namely
ab
. If
ab\neq v_{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}v_{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1}
, then again
H_{1}
(say) is a subgraph of
P_{\frac{d-1}{2}}
. But then
\lambda_{2}(T)\geq\lambda_{1}(P_{\frac{d-1}{2}})\geq\lambda_{1}(H_{1})
. This contradicts case
(ii)
of
Theorem 3.1
. We conclude, without loss of generality, that

and

. Hence,
\lambda_{1}(P_{\frac{d-1}{2}})=\lambda_{1}(H_{1}-a)<\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(H_{1})=\lambda_{1}(P_{\frac{d+1}{2}})
. This proves claim
(ii)
.
∎

In this section, we prove our first main result,

. We assume that

.

There is a longest path of length

and

(3)

(H_{1},a)
and
(H_{2},b)
of
T^{*}
as given in

. We make the following cases:

Case 1:

n
is odd. We have

\lambda_{2}(T^{*})\leq\min\{\lambda_{1}(H_{1}),\lambda_{1}(H_{2})\}\leq\min\{\lambda_{1}(K_{1,|H_{1}|-1}),\lambda_{1}(K_{1,|H_{2}|-1})\}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{2}},

and equality holds if and only if
H_{1}\cong H_{2}\cong K_{1,\frac{n-3}{2}}
. Thus,
T^{*}
is any of the trees
T(\frac{n-3}{2},0,\frac{n-3}{2})
,
T(\frac{n-3}{2},0,0,\frac{n-5}{2})
or
T(\frac{n-5}{2},0,0,0,\frac{n-5}{2})
and they all have
\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{2}}
.

Case 2:

n
is even.
Let
T^{\prime}=T(\frac{n-4}{2},0,0,\frac{n-4}{2})
. Using
Lemma 2.1
,

\displaystyle\Phi(T^{\prime},x)

\displaystyle=x^{n-4}\bigg(x^{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg)^{2}-x^{n-6}\bigg(x^{2}-\frac{n-4}{2}\bigg)^{2}.

Since,
\Phi(T^{\prime},\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{2}})>0
and
\lambda_{1}(T^{\prime})>\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{2}}
, we conclude that
\lambda_{2}(T^{\prime})>\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{2}}
. Thus,

\lambda_{2}(T^{*})\geq\lambda_{2}(T^{\prime})>\sqrt{\frac{n-3}{2}}.

(7)

Now if
T^{*}
has a spectral vertex then one of the
H_{i}
’s has atmost
\frac{n-2}{2}

\lambda_{2}(T^{*})=\lambda_{1}(H_{i})\leq\lambda_{1}(K_{1,|H_{i}|-1})\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-4}{2}}
. This contradicts (
7
). So
T^{*}
has a spectral edge and

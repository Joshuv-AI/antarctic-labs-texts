Exponential dichotomy and ( L p , L q ) (L^{p},L^{q}) -admissibility Thanks: This research has been done under the research project QG.23.02 of Vietnam National University, Hanoi
Trinh Viet Duoc, Nguyen Van Trong

Abstract
. We consider the notion of an exponential dichotomy with respect to a family of norms for an evolutionary family in a Banach space, and we characterize it by the admissibility of the pair ( L p , L q ) (L^{p},L^{q}) for p , q ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] p,q\in[1,\infty] with p ≥ q p\geq q . We then use this characterization to establish the robustness of an exponentially dichotomic evolutionary family with respect to a family of norms.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X=(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a real or complex Banach space and
B(X)
be the space of all bounded linear operators on
X
. Moreover, we consider a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
on
X
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(i)

There exist constants
C>0
and
\epsilon\geq 0
such that

(2.1)

\|x\|\leq\|x\|_{t}\leq Ce^{\epsilon|t|}\|x\|;

(ii)

The map
t\mapsto\|x\|_{t}
is measurable for each
x\in X
.

In
X
, we always consider the topology generated by the original norm
\|\cdot\|
. Extending the concept of an evolutionary family, we obtain the concept of an evolutionary family with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
on
X
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

A family
\left(T(t,\tau)\right)_{t\geq\tau}
with
t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
in
B(X)
is said to be an evolutionary family with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
with
t\in\mathbb{R}
on the line if:

i.

T(t,t)=Id
and
T(t,s)T(s,\tau)=T(t,\tau)
for
t\geq s\geq\tau
and
t,\tau,s\in\mathbb{R}
.

ii.

For each
x\in X
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
t\mapsto T(t,\tau)x
is continuous on
[\tau,\infty)

and the map
s\mapsto T(\tau,s)x
is continuous on
(-\infty,\tau]
.

iii.

There exist
K,a>0
such that
\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}\leq Ke^{a(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}
for all
x\in X
.

Similarly, we have the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

An evolutionary family
\left(T(t,\tau)\right)_{t\geq\tau}
with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
on the line is said to be an exponential dichotomy with respect to the family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
if it satisfies the following conditions:

i.

There exist dichotomic projections
P(t)
in
B(X),t\in\mathbb{R}
such that

P(t)T(t,\tau)=T(t,\tau)P(\tau)\quad\text{ for }t\geq\tau.

ii.

The restriction mapping

T(t,\tau):KerP(\tau)\rightarrow KerP(t)

is an isomorphism for
t\geq\tau
. We denote its inverse
(T(t,\tau)_{|_{\text{\rm Ker}P(\tau)}})^{-1}
by
T(\tau,t)_{|}
.

iii.

There exist constants
a<0<b
and
D>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x\|_{\tau}&\leq De^{a(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau},\\
\|T(\tau,t)_{|}Q(t)x\|_{t}&\leq De^{-b(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{t},\end{split}

for
t\geq\tau,x\in X
and
Q(t)=I-P(t)
.

Remark 2.1
.

For each evolutionary family
\left(T(t,\tau)\right)_{t\geq\tau}
is exponentially dichotomic with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
on the line, we have
X=X^{s}(t)\oplus X^{u}(t)
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
X^{s}(t)=P(t)X
and
X^{u}(t)=Q(t)X
.

We denote

L^{p}(\mathbb{R},X)=\{f:\mathbb{R}\to X\mid f\text{ is measurable and }\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|f(t)\|_{t}^{p}\,dt<\infty\}

for
p\in[1,\infty)
and by

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},X)=\{f:\mathbb{R}\to X\mid f\text{ is measurable and }\esssup\|f(t)\|_{t}<\infty\}.

Then,
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},X)
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},X)
are Banach spaces endowed with the respectively norms

\|f\|_{p}=\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|f(t)\|_{t}^{p}\,dt\Big)^{1/p}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|f\|_{\infty}=\esssup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|f(t)\|_{t}.

For the simplicity of notations we will use the notation
L^{p}
for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},X)
where
p\in[1,\infty]
. Also, we set the Banach space

C_{b}=\{f:\mathbb{R}\to X\mid f\text{ is continuous and }\sup\|f(t)\|_{t}<\infty\}\text{ with the norm }\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|f(t)\|_{t}.

Finally, we note that the set
L^{p}
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{p},p\in[1,\infty]
which is proven similarly to
[
5
, Theorem 1]
.

3.
Exponential dichotomy and (
L^{p},L^{q}
)-admissibility

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
p\geq q
. We characterize the notion of an exponential dichotomy for an evolutionary family with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
by the admissibility of the Banach space pair
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
, where
Y_{1}=L^{p}\cap C_{b}
and
Y_{2}=L^{q}
.

Given an evolutionary family
(T(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
on Banach space
X
with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
and a function
y:\mathbb{R}\to X
,
we consider the integral equation

(3.3)

x(t)=T(t,\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y(s)ds\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq\tau.

The following lemma gives a sufficient condition for the continuity of the function
x
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given
y\in Y_{2}
. If a function
x:\mathbb{R}\to X
satisfies (
3.3
), then
x
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Because the mapping
T(\cdot,\tau)x(\tau)
is continuous on
[\tau,\infty)
,
one only have to prove the function

z(t):=\int_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y(s)ds

is continuous on
[\tau,\infty)
for each fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, for
t_{0}\geq\tau
and
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+1]
, we have

\displaystyle\|z(t)-z(t_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|T(t,s)y(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds+M\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|y(s)\|ds,

\displaystyle\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|\leq 2M\|y(s)\|\leq 2M\|y(s)\|_{s},

where
M=\sup\{\|T(t,s)\|:s\in[\tau,t_{0}+1]\}<\infty
(by the Banach–Steinhaus theorem).
By dominated convergence theorem and Lebesgue differentiation theorem (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1.8 and Proposition 1.2.2, Chapt. 1]
), we get

\lim_{t\to t_{0}^{+}}\Big(\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds+M\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|y(s)\|ds\Big)=0.

Thus,
z
is right continuous on
[\tau,\infty)
. For
t_{0}>\tau
and
t\in(\tau,t_{0}]
, we have

\displaystyle\|z(t)-z(t_{0})\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds+\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\|T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t}\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds+M\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\|y(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau}^{t_{0}}\|T(t,s)y(s)-T(t_{0},s)y(s)\|ds+M\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\|y(s)\|ds.

Similarly,
z
is left continuous on
(\tau,\infty)
.
Therefore,
z
is continuous on
[\tau,\infty)
. Since
\tau
is arbitrary,
x
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

We then define an operator related to the integral equation (
3.3
) as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

The operator
H:\mathcal{D}(H)\to Y_{2}
is defined by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathcal{D}(H)&:=&\{x\in Y_{1}:\text{ there is }y\in Y_{2}\text{ such that }x\text{ and }y\text{ satisfying }\eqref{2.7}\},\\
Hx&:=&y.\end{array}

We now justify the definition of
H
and give its properties through the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

H
is a well-defined closed linear operator.

Proof.

Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y_{2}
such that

x(t)=T(t,\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{1}(s)ds,

and

x(t)=T(t,\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{2}(s)ds,

for
t>\tau
. Then,

\dfrac{t}{t-\tau}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{1}(s)ds=\frac{t}{t-\tau}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{2}(s)ds.

Since the map
s\mapsto T(t,s)y_{i}(s)
is locally integrable for
i=1,2
, let
\tau\rightarrow t
, from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem it follows that
y_{1}(t)=y_{2}(t)
for almost all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,
H
is well-defined. Furthermore,
H
is obviously a linear operator.

Let
(x_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{D}(H)
such that
x_{n}
converges to
x
in
Y_{1}
and
y_{n}:=Hx_{n}
converges to
y
in
Y_{2}
.
For each fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle x(t)-T(t,\tau)x(\tau)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\left(x_{n}(t)-T(t,\tau)x_{n}(\tau)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{n}(s)ds

for
t\geq\tau
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Big\|\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{n}(s)ds

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y(s)ds\Big\|\leq M\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}\|y_{n}(s)-y(s)\|ds

\displaystyle\leq M\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}\|y_{n}(s)-y(s)\|^{q}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}(t-\tau)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq M\|y_{n}-y\|_{q}(t-\tau)^{1-\frac{1}{q}},

where
M=\sup\{\|T(t,s)\|:s\in[\tau,t]\}<\infty
. Since
y_{n}
converges to
y
in
Y_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y_{n}(s)ds=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y(s)ds.

Hence,

x(t)-T(t,\tau)x(\tau)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y(s)ds

for
t\geq\tau
. So,
x\in\mathcal{D}(H)
and
Hx=y
. Therefore,
H
is a closed operator.
∎

Firstly, we show that the exponential dichotomy with respect to a family of norms of an evolutionary family deduces
(L^{p},L^{q})
-admissibility (more precisely,
(L^{p}\cap C_{b},L^{q})
-admissibility), namely,
H:\mathcal{D}(H)\to Y_{2}
is bijective.

Theorem 3.1
.

If the evolutionary family
(T(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
is exponentially dichotomic with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
, then for each
y\in Y_{2}
there exists a unique
x\in Y_{1}
satisfying (
3.3
).

Proof.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

x_{1}(t)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,\tau)P(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau\quad\text{and}\quad x_{2}(t)=\displaystyle\int\limits_{t}^{\infty}T(t,\tau)_{|}Q(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau.

Using Holder’s inequality and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|x_{1}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{1}(t)\|_{t}\leq\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}\|T(t,\tau)P(\tau)y(\tau)\|_{t}d\tau\leq\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{t}De^{{a}(t-\tau)}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{t-m-1}^{t-m}De^{{a}(t-\tau)}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau\leq\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}De^{{a}m}\displaystyle\int_{t-m-1}^{t-m}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}De^{{a}m}\left(\displaystyle\int_{t-m-1}^{t-m}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}^{q}d\tau\right)^{1/q}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}De^{{a}m}\|y\|_{q}=\frac{D}{1-e^{a}}\|y\|_{q},

\displaystyle\|x_{2}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{2}(t)\|_{t}\leq\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}\|T(t,\tau)_{|}Q(\tau)y(\tau)\|_{t}d\tau\leq\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}De^{-{b}(\tau-t)}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{t+m}^{t+m+1}De^{-{b}(\tau-t)}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau\leq\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}De^{-{b}m}\displaystyle\int_{t+m}^{t+m+1}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}De^{-{b}m}\left(\displaystyle\int_{t+m}^{t+m+1}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}^{q}d\tau\right)^{1/q}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}De^{-{b}m}\|y\|_{q}=\frac{D}{1-e^{-{b}}}\|y\|_{q}.

Therefore,
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are well-defined. Furthermore,

(3.4)

\|x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)\|_{t}\leq\Big(\frac{D}{1-e^{a}}+\frac{D}{1-e^{-{b}}}\Big)\|y\|_{q}\quad\text{for all }\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

Acting
P(t)
to the both sides of (
3.3
), we have

P(t)x(t)=T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)y(s)ds\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq\tau.

For
\tau\to-\infty
, we obtain

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x(\tau)=P(t)x(t)-x_{1}(t).

We have

\|T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x(\tau)\|\leq\|T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x(\tau)\|_{t}\leq De^{a(t-\tau)}\|x(\tau)\|_{\tau}\leq De^{a(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\infty}.

This leads to
P(t)x(t)=x_{1}(t)
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Fix
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, applying
Q(t)
to the both sides of (
3.3
), we have

Q(t)x(t)=T(t,\tau)Q(\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)Q(s)y(s)ds\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq\tau.

By
T(t,\tau):Q(\tau)X\to Q(t)X
is an isomorphism,

T(\tau,t)_{|}Q(t)x(t)=Q(\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(\tau,s)_{|}Q(s)y(s)ds\quad\text{for}\quad t\geq\tau.

For
t\to+\infty
, we obtain

\lim_{t\to+\infty}T(\tau,t)_{|}Q(t)x(t)=Q(\tau)x(\tau)+x_{2}(\tau).

Same as above, we get
Q(\tau)x(\tau)=-x_{2}(\tau)
for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

So, we have
x(t)=x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore,
x\in Y_{1}
satisfying (
3.3
) is unique.
Next, we show that the functions
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
belong to
L^{p}
.
We only consider
x_{2}
since the argument for
x_{1}
is analogous.
Since
p\geq q
, there exists
r\in[1,\infty]
such that

\dfrac{1}{r}+\dfrac{1}{q}=1+\dfrac{1}{p}.

Let

\omega(t)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\quad t\geq 0,\\
e^{{b}t}&\text{if}\quad t<0\\
\end{cases}

and
z(t)=\|y(t)\|_{t}
. We have
\omega\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
and
z\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
.
By Young’s inequality for convolution,
w*z\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Furthermore,

(\omega*z)(t)=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\omega(t-\tau)z(\tau)d\tau=\displaystyle\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-{b}(\tau-t)}\|y(\tau)\|_{\tau}d\tau.

We also have
\|x_{2}(t)\|_{t}\leq D(\omega*z)(t)
for all
t
. Thus,
x_{2}\in L^{p}
and

\|x_{2}\|_{p}\leq D\|\omega*z\|_{p}\leq\frac{D}{(br)^{\frac{1}{r}}}\|y\|_{q}.

Similarly,
x_{1}\in L^{p}
. Therefore,
x_{1}-x_{2}\in L^{p}
and

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{p}\leq\Big(\frac{D}{(-ar)^{\frac{1}{r}}}+\frac{D}{(br)^{\frac{1}{r}}}\Big)\|y\|_{q}.

Let
x(t)=x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and take
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. We now show that
x
satisfies (
3.3
) for
t\geq t_{0}
.

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t}T(t,\tau)y(\tau)d\tau-\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t}T(t,\tau)P(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau-\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t}T(t,\tau)Q(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{t}T(t,\tau)P(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau-\displaystyle\int\limits_{t}^{+\infty}T(t,\tau)_{|}Q(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t}T(t,\tau)y(\tau)d\tau+\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{t_{0}}T(t,t_{0})T(t_{0},\tau)P(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau-\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{+\infty}T(t,t_{0})T(t_{0},\tau)_{|}Q(\tau)y(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int\limits_{t_{0}}^{t}T(t,\tau)y(\tau)d\tau+T(t,t_{0})x(t_{0}).

By Lemma
3.1
and (
3.4
),
x\in C_{b}
.
So, there exists a unique
x\in Y_{1}
satisfying (
3.3
).
∎

Now we establish the converse of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
with
p\geq q
and
(p,q)\neq(\infty,1)
.
Assume that for each
y\in Y_{2}
there exists a unique
x\in Y_{1}
satisfying (
3.3
). Then, the evolutionary family
(T(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
is exponentially dichotomic with respect to the family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
.

Proof.

From the assumption of the theorem and Lemma
3.2
, the inverse of
H
,
G:Y_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(H)\subset Y_{1}
, is a closed linear operator. By the closed graph theorem,
G
is bounded.

For
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the following sets

\displaystyle F^{s}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:\sup_{t\geq\tau}\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}<\infty\;\text{ and }\;z\in L^{p}\},\;\text{where }\;z(t)=\begin{cases}T(t,\tau)x,&t\geq\tau,\\
0,&t<\tau.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\left\{\varphi:(-\infty,\tau]\to X\;\text{ such that }\;\varphi(t)=T(t,s)\varphi(s)\;\text{ for all }\;s\leq\tau\;\text{ and }\;s\leq t\leq\tau\right\}.

\displaystyle F^{u}_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\{x\in X:\;\exists\;\varphi_{x}\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau}\text{ such that }\varphi_{x}(\tau)=x,\,\sup_{t\leq\tau}\|\varphi_{x}(t)\|_{t}<\infty\text{ and }w\in L^{p}\},

where

\displaystyle w(t)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{x}(t),&t\leq\tau,\\
0,&t>\tau.\end{cases}

Clearly,
F^{s}_{\tau}
and
F_{\tau}^{u}
are subspaces of
X
. For easy tracking, we divide the proof steps into the lemmas below.

Lemma 3.3
.

For each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.5)

X=F_{\tau}^{s}\oplus F_{\tau}^{u}.

Proof.

For
x\in X
, take

g(t)=\begin{cases}T(t,\tau)x&\text{if}\quad t\in[\tau,\tau+1],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is easy to see that
g\in Y_{2}
. Hence, there exists
v\in\mathcal{D}(H)
such that
Hv=g
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle=T(t,\tau)v(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)g(s)ds

\displaystyle=T(t,\tau)v(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)T(s,\tau)\chi_{[\tau,\tau+1]}xds

\displaystyle=T(t,\tau)v(\tau)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{\tau}^{t}T(t,\tau)\chi_{[\tau,\tau+1]}xds

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}T(t,\tau)(v(\tau)+(t-\tau)x)&\text{ for }t\in[\tau,\tau+1),\\
T(t,\tau)(v(\tau)+x)&\text{ for }t\geq\tau+1.\end{cases}

Since
v\in Y_{1}
leads to
v(\tau)+x\in F^{s}_{\tau}
. For
s\leq\tau
and
t\in[s,\tau]
, we have

v(t)=T(t,s)v(s)+\displaystyle\int\limits_{s}^{t}T(t,\xi)g(\xi)d\xi=T(t,s)v(s).

Thus,
v_{|(-\infty,\tau]}\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
. From
v\in Y_{1}
it follows that
v(\tau)\in F^{u}_{\tau}
.
Therefore,
x\in F^{s}_{\tau}+F^{u}_{\tau}
for all
x\in X
. So,
X=F^{s}_{\tau}+F^{u}_{\tau}
.
Now we prove that if
x\in F^{s}_{\tau}\bigcap F^{u}_{\tau}
then
x=0
. Indeed, we define
u:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow X
as follows

u(t)=\begin{cases}T(t,\tau)x&\text{if}\quad t\geq\tau,\\
\varphi_{x}(t)&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

From the definition of
F^{s}_{\tau}
and
F^{u}_{\tau}
it follows that
u\in Y_{1}
. Moreover, we have
Hu=0
.
Therefore,
u\in\mathcal{D}(H)
. Since
H
is invertible, we conclude that
u=0
and therefore
x=0
.
∎

From the decomposition in (
3.5
), we define the projections

P(\tau):X\rightarrow F^{s}_{\tau}\quad\text{and}\quad Q(\tau):X\rightarrow F^{u}_{\tau}

for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
P(\tau)+Q(\tau)=Id
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exits
M>0
such that

(3.6)

\|P(\tau)x\|_{\tau}\leq M\|x\|_{\tau}

for
x\in X
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Consequently,
P(\tau)
is bounded for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By
P(\tau)x=v(\tau)+x
(using the notation of Lemma
3.3
), we have

\|P(\tau)x\|_{\tau}=\|v(\tau)+x\|_{\tau}\leq\|v(\tau)\|_{\tau}+\|x\|_{\tau}.

Moreover,
v(\tau)=T(\tau,s)v(s)
for
s\in[\tau-1,\tau]
. Thus,

\|v(\tau)\|_{\tau}=\|T(\tau,s)v(s)\|_{\tau}\leq Ke^{c(\tau-s)}\|v(s)\|_{s}

for
s\in[\tau-1,\tau]
. Integrating over
s
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|v(\tau)\|_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{c}\displaystyle\int_{\tau-1}^{\tau}\|v(s)\|_{s}ds\leq Ke^{c}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\tau-1}^{\tau}\|v(s)\|_{s}^{p}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{c}\|v\|_{p}=Ke^{c}\|Gg\|_{p}\leq Ke^{c}\|G\|\|g\|_{q}

\displaystyle=Ke^{c}\|G\|\left(\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}^{q}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq K^{2}e^{2c}\|G\|\|x\|_{\tau}.

This show that (
3.6
) holds with
M=K^{2}e^{2c}\|G\|+1
.
∎

The spaces
F_{\tau}^{s}
and
F_{\tau}^{u}
are invariant with the evolutionary family.

Lemma 3.5
.

T(t,\tau)F_{\tau}^{s}\subset F_{t}^{s}
and
T(t,\tau)F_{\tau}^{u}=F_{t}^{u}
for all
t\geq\tau
.

Proof.

Take
x\in F^{s}_{\tau}
and with
\xi\geq t
, we have
T(\xi,t)T(t,\tau)x=T(\xi,\tau)x
. Thus,

\sup_{\xi\geq t}\|T(\xi,t)T(t,\tau)x\|_{\xi}=\sup_{\xi\geq t}\|T(\xi,\tau)x\|_{\xi}\leq\sup_{\xi\geq\tau}\|T(\xi,\tau)x\|_{\xi}<\infty,

and

z_{1}(\xi)=\begin{cases}T(\xi,\tau)x,&\xi\geq t,\\
0,&\xi<t\end{cases}

belongs to
L^{p}
.
Therefore,
T(t,\tau)x\in F^{s}_{t}
.
So,
T(t,\tau)F^{s}_{\tau}\subset F^{s}_{t}
for all
t\geq\tau
.

Take
x\in F^{u}_{\tau}
, put
y=T(t,\tau)x
. We define the function
\varphi_{y}
as follows

\varphi_{y}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{x}(\xi),&\xi\leq\tau,\\
T(\xi,\tau)x,&\tau\leq\xi\leq t.\end{cases}

Then,
\varphi_{y}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
. Indeed, we easily see
\varphi_{y}(\xi)=T(\xi,s)\varphi_{y}(s)
for
s\geq\tau
and
\xi\in[s,t]
,
s<\tau
and
\xi\in[s,\tau]
.
For
s<\tau
and
\xi\in[\tau,t]
, we have

\varphi_{y}(\xi)=T(\xi,\tau)x=T(\xi,\tau)T(\tau,s)\varphi_{x}(s)=T(\xi,s)\varphi_{x}(s)=T(\xi,s)\varphi_{y}(s).

For
\xi\in[\tau,t]
, we have

\|T(\xi,\tau)x\|_{\xi}\leq Ke^{c(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}.

Therefore,
\sup_{\xi\leq t}\|\varphi_{y}(\xi)\|_{\xi}<\infty
; and

w_{1}\in L^{p}
with
w_{1}(\xi)=\varphi_{y}(\xi)
if
\xi\leq t
and
w_{1}(\xi)=0
if
\xi>t
. So,
T(t,\tau)x\in F^{u}_{t}
.
Therefore,
T(t,\tau)F^{u}_{\tau}\subset F^{u}_{t}
.

For
y\in F^{u}_{t}
, we easily check
x=\varphi_{y}(\tau)\in F^{u}_{\tau}
with
\varphi_{x}:=\varphi_{y}|_{(-\infty,\tau]}
.
Because of
\varphi_{y}\in\mathcal{F}_{t}
,
T(t,\tau)x=y
.
Therefore,
T(t,\tau)F^{u}_{\tau}=F^{u}_{t}
.
∎

By a similar proof as in
[
5
, Lemma 8]
, we obtain the exponential decay of the evolutionary family on the space
F_{\tau}^{s}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exist
\lambda,D>0
such that

\|T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x\|_{t}\leq De^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}

for
x\in X
and
t\geq\tau
.

We now show that the evolutionary family is exponentially growing on the space
F_{\tau}^{u}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exist
\lambda,D>0
such that

(3.7)

\|T(t,\tau)Q(\tau)x\|_{t}\geq\frac{1}{D}e^{\lambda(t-\tau)}\|Q(\tau)x\|_{\tau}

for
x\in X
and
t\geq\tau
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that there exists
C>0
such that

(3.8)

C\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}\geq\|x\|_{\tau}

for
t\geq\tau,\,x\in F^{u}_{\tau}
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have
y=T(t+1,\tau)x\in F^{u}_{t+1}
,
\varphi_{y}\in\mathcal{F}_{t+1}
, and
w\in L^{p}
, where

w(\xi)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{y}(\xi),&\xi\leq t+1,\\
0,&\xi>t+1,\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad\varphi_{y}(\xi)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{x}(\xi),&\xi\leq\tau,\\
T(\xi,\tau)x,&\tau\leq\xi\leq t+1.\end{cases}

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a continuously differentiable function such that

{\rm supp\,}\psi\subset(-\infty,t+1],\quad\psi(\xi)=1\text{ \; for \; }\xi\leq t,\quad\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}|\psi^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq 2.

It easy to verify that
\psi w\in Y_{1}
,
\psi^{\prime}w\in Y_{2}
, and
H(\psi w)=\psi^{\prime}w
. We have

\displaystyle\|\psi w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|G(\psi^{\prime}w)\|_{\infty}\leq\|G\|\|\psi^{\prime}w\|_{q}=\|G\|\left(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+1}\|\psi^{\prime}(\xi)w(\xi)\|^{q}_{\xi}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|G\|\left(\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+1}\|T(\xi,t)T(t,\tau)x\|^{q}_{\xi}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq 2Ke^{c}\|G\|\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}.

Therefore,
\|x\|_{\tau}=\|(\psi w)(\tau)\|_{\tau}\leq\|\psi w\|_{\infty}\leq 2Ke^{c}\|G\|\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}
.
We now show that there exists
T>0
such that

(3.9)

\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}\geq 2\|x\|_{\tau}\quad\text{ for }\quad t-\tau\geq T.

Indeed, we consider the following functions

y_{2}(\xi)=-\chi_{[\tau,t]}(\xi)w(\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad v_{2}(\xi)=\int_{\xi}^{+\infty}\chi_{[\tau,t]}(s)ds\,w(\xi).

Then,
v_{2}\in Y_{1},y_{2}\in Y_{2}
, and
Hv_{2}=y_{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|v_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|Gy_{2}\|_{\infty}\leq\|G\|\|y_{2}\|_{q}=\|G\|\left(\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\|w(\xi)\|^{q}_{\xi}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|\left[\left(\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\|w(\xi)\|^{p}_{\xi}d\xi\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}(t-\tau)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\|G\|(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\|w(\xi)\|^{p}_{\xi}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\|G\|(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\psi(\xi)w(\xi)\|^{p}_{\xi}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|\psi w\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2Ke^{c}\|G\|^{2}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}.

Hence,
(t-\tau)\|x\|_{\tau}=(t-\tau)\|w(\tau)\|_{\tau}=(t-\tau)\|v_{2}(\tau)\|\leq 2Ke^{c}\|G\|^{2}(t-\tau)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}
. So,

\|x\|_{\tau}\leq\dfrac{2Ke^{c}\|G\|^{2}}{(t-\tau)^{1-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}}}\|T(t,\tau)x\|_{t}.

Since
(p,q)\neq(\infty,1)
,
1-\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}>0
. Thus,
T>0
exists such that (
3.9
) holds.

For
t\geq\tau
and
x\in X
, write
t-\tau=kT+s
with
0\leq s<T
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

T(t,\tau)Q(\tau)x=\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}T(t-iT,t-(i+1)T)T(\tau+s,\tau)Q(\tau)x.

By (
3.8
) and (
3.9
),

\displaystyle\|T(t,\tau)Q(\tau)x\|_{t}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{k}\|T(\tau+s,\tau)Q(\tau)x\|_{\tau+s}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{k}}{C}\|Q(\tau)x\|_{\tau}\geq\frac{1}{2C}e^{\frac{(t-\tau)\ln 2}{T}}\|Q(\tau)x\|_{\tau}.

So, the inequality (
3.7
) holds for
\lambda=\frac{\ln 2}{T}
and
D=2C
.
∎

To complete the proof, we need to verify the remainder conditions of the exponential dichotomy.
By Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.7
,
T(t,\tau):Q(\tau)X\to Q(t)X
is bijective and

\|T(\tau,t)_{|}Q(t)x\|_{\tau}\leq{D}e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}\|Q(t)x\|_{t}\leq D(1+M)e^{-\lambda(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{t}

for all
t\geq\tau
. By Lemma
3.5
,
we have
T(t,\tau)Q(\tau)x\in F^{u}_{t}
and
T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x\in F^{s}_{t}
. Therefore,

P(t)T(t,\tau)x=T(t,\tau)P(\tau)x
for every
x\in X
, which means that
P(t)T(t,\tau)=T(t,\tau)P(\tau)
for
t\geq\tau
. So, the evolutionary family
(T(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
is exponentially dichotomic with respect to the family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
.
∎

Comment 3.1
.

By Remark
2.1
and the definition of the subspaces
F^{s}_{\tau},F^{u}_{\tau}
, we obtain
X^{s}(\tau)\subset F^{s}_{\tau}
and
X^{u}(\tau)\subset F^{u}_{\tau}
for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, by Lemma
3.3
and Remark
2.1
we have
X^{s}(\tau)=F^{s}_{\tau}
and
X^{u}(\tau)=F^{u}_{\tau}
for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. So, the family of dichotomic projections is unique.

As an application, we now provide a result for the perturbation.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let the evolution family
(T(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
be exponentially dichotomic with respect to a family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
and
B:\mathbb{R}\to B(X)
be a strong continuous function such that

(3.10)

\|B(t)\|\leq Me^{-\epsilon|t|}\varphi(t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, for
\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
and sufficiently small
M>0
, the evolution family
U(t,\tau)
satisfying

U(t,\tau)=T(t,\tau)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)B(s)U(s,\tau)ds,\quad t\geq\tau\text{ and}\quad t,\tau\in\mathbb{R}

admits an exponential dichotomy with respect to the same family of norms.

Proof.

Let
L
be the linear operator associated to the evolution family
(U(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
, in the same as the linear operator
H
is associated to the evolution family
(T(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
, see Definition
3.1
. More precisely,
L
is defined by
Lx=y
in the domain
\mathcal{D}(L)
, namely

x(t)=U(t,\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}U(t,s)y(s)ds,\quad t\geq\tau.

By direct transformation and using Fubini theorem, we obtain the following relation

(3.11)

\displaystyle x(t)=T(t,\tau)x(\tau)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}T(t,w)\left(y(w)+B(w)x(w)\right)dw\quad t\geq\tau.

We now define an operator
P:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
by
(Px)(t)=B(t)x(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By (
2.1
) and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\|B(t)x(t)\|_{t}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\epsilon|t|}\|B(t)x(t)\|\leq MC\varphi(t)\|x(t)\|\leq MC\varphi(t)\|x(t)\|_{t}\leq MC\varphi(t)\|x\|_{Y_{1}}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|Px\|_{q}=\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|B(t)x(t)\|_{t}^{q}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq MC\|x\|_{Y_{1}}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\varphi(t))^{q}dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}=MC\|\varphi\|_{q}\|x\|_{Y_{1}}.

Hence,
P
is a bounded linear operator and

(3.12)

\|P\|\leq MC\|\varphi\|_{q}.

By the assumption, we have
H:\mathcal{D}(H)\rightarrow Y_{2}
has a bounded invertible operator.
By (
3.12
), for
M>0
is small enough, we obtain

\|PH^{-1}\|\leq\|P\|\|H^{-1}\|\leq MC\|\varphi\|_{q}\|H^{-1}\|<1.

Therefore,
I-PH^{-1}:Y_{2}\to Y_{2}
is invertible.
Furthermore, it follows from (
3.11
) that
\mathcal{D}(H)=\mathcal{D}(L)
and
H=L+P
. So,

L=H-P=(I-PH^{-1})H:\mathcal{D}(H)\rightarrow Y_{2}

is also invertible for sufficiently small
M>0
.

It remains to show that there exists
K_{1},c_{1}>0
such that

\|U(t,\tau)x\|_{t}\leq K_{1}e^{c_{1}(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}\quad\text{ for}\quad t\geq\tau.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\|U(t,\tau)x\|_{t}

\displaystyle=\Big\|T(t,\tau)x+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}T(t,s)B(s)U(s,\tau)xds\Big\|_{t}

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{c(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}+K\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{c(t-s)}\|B(s)U(s,\tau)x\|_{s}ds

\displaystyle\leq Ke^{c(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}+MCK\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}e^{c(t-s)}\varphi(s)\|U(s,\tau)x\|_{s}ds

for
t\geq\tau
. Let
\phi(t)=e^{-ct}\|U(t,\tau)x\|_{t}
, we get

\phi(t)\leq K\phi(\tau)+MCK\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\varphi(s)\phi(s)ds.

Using Gronwall’s lemma, we have

\phi(t)\leq K\phi(\tau)e^{MCK\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\varphi(s)ds}.

On the other hand,

\int_{\tau}^{t}\varphi(s)ds\leq\sum_{k=0}^{[t-\tau]}\int_{\tau+k}^{\tau+k+1}\varphi(s)ds\leq\|\varphi\|_{q}(t-\tau+1).

Thus,

\phi(t)\leq K\phi(\tau)e^{MCK\|\varphi\|_{q}(t-\tau+1)}.

This implies that

\|U(t,\tau)x\|_{t}\leq Ke^{MCK\|\varphi\|_{q}}e^{(c+MCK\|\varphi\|_{q})(t-\tau)}\|x\|_{\tau}\quad\text{ for}\quad t\geq\tau.

Applying Theorem
3.2
, the evolution family
(U(t,\tau))_{t\geq\tau}
is exponentially dichotomic with respect to the family of norms
\|\cdot\|_{t}
.
∎

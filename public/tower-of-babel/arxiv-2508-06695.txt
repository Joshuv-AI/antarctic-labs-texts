Abstract
. We show that the notions of ( 𝐧 , σ ) (\mathbf{n},\sigma) -isometry and ( 𝐧 , σ ) (\mathbf{n},\sigma) -equivalence introduced by Ou-azzou et al coincide for most skew ( σ , a ) (\sigma,a) -constacyclic codes of length 𝐧 \mathbf{n} . Introduction

Petit algebras are nonassociative algebras that, when defined over finite fields or rings, are increasingly important for constructing and classifying skew polycyclic codes, as there is a one-one correspondence between skew polycyclic codes and principal left ideals in Petit algebras
[

]

Two such codes over a finite field are

[
Pum25
]

There has been significant work to understand these isometries. In
[

OaNA25
]

[

]

The same subclass of isometries was used, but between two associative algebras

and

in
[

Skew constacyclic codes

2.
Petit algebras and power associativity

Let
S
be a commutative, associative unital ring and suppose
\sigma\in\Aut(S)
has finite order
\mathbf{m}
. Important examples in applications to coding theory are
S=K
a finite field, or
S
a chain ring (also called a finite local ring) or more generally a Galois ring (see, for example,
[
Pum17
, §5.1]
). In both these examples, every non-invertible element is a zero divisor.

Write
S_{0}=\{s\in S:\sigma(s)=s\}
for the subring fixed by
\sigma
.
If
\tau\in\Aut(S)
is an arbitrary automorphism
then we write
S_{0}^{\tau}=\{s\in S_{0}:\tau(s)=s\}
for the common fixed subring under
\tau
and
\sigma
. We define
K_{0},K_{0}^{\tau}
analogously.

The ring
R=S[t;\sigma]
is a noncommutative polynomial ring, with addition the usual addition of polynomials, and with multiplication defined by the relation

t^{j}a=\sigma^{j}(a)t^{j}

for all
a\in S
and all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. It is an algebra over
S_{0}
.

Let
f(t)=t^{\mathbf{n}}-f_{0}(t)\in S[t;\sigma]
be a monic polynomial of degree
\mathbf{n}\geq 1
.
For all
g(t)\in S[t;\sigma]
of degree
\ell\geq\mathbf{n}
, there exist uniquely
determined
r(t),q(t)\in S[t;\sigma]
with

{\rm deg}(r)<{\rm deg}(f)
, such that

g(t)=q(t)f(t)+r(t)

[
Pum17
, Proposition 1]
.
Let
r:=g\,{\rm mod}_{r}f
denote the remainder of this
right
division of
g
by
f
. Since the remainders are uniquely determined, we may identify

S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)=\{\sum_{i=0}^{\mathbf{n}-1}b_{i}t^{i}:b_{i}\in S\}

as left
S[t;\sigma]
-modules. In fact, this quotient also carries the structure of a (potentially) nonassociative unital ring that is an algebra over the fixed ring
S_{0}
, with multiplication of any two polynomials of degree less than
\mathbf{n}
defined by

g\circ h=gh\,\,{\rm mod}_{r}f.

In particular, for all
0\leq i,j<\mathbf{n}
we have

t^{i}t^{j}=\begin{cases}t^{i+j}&\text{if $i+j<\mathbf{n}$;}\\
t^{i+j-\mathbf{n}}f_{0}(t)&\text{if $i+j>\mathbf{n}$.}\end{cases}

Note that
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
is associative exactly when
S[t;\sigma]f(t)
is a two-sided ideal.
For example, this happens when
S=K
,
f(t)=t^{\mathbf{n}}-\sum_{i=0}^{\mathbf{n}-1}a_{i}t^{i}\in S_{0}[t]
and
\sigma^{\mathbf{n}-i}=\id
for all those
i
where
a_{i}\not=0

[
Pet67
, (15)]
.

We are particularly interested in the special case that
f(t)=t^{\mathbf{n}}-a
for some
a\in S^{\times}
. Then the lack of power associativity is best understood by observing that in
S[t;\sigma]
, we have the identity
t^{\mathbf{n}+1}=t^{\mathbf{n}}t=tt^{\mathbf{n}}
but
in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)
,

t^{\mathbf{n}}t=at\quad\text{whereas}\quad tt^{\mathbf{n}}=ta=\sigma(a)t.

That is, one must take care to simplify products only with
right
division by
t^{\mathbf{n}}-a
, as then it is clear that the correct reduction is

t^{\mathbf{n}+1}\equiv t(t^{\mathbf{n}}-a+a)\equiv ta\equiv\sigma(a)t\text{ in }S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a).

From this point onwards we write simply equality rather than congruence.

We require some additional notation. For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tau\in\Aut(S)
and
\beta\in S
, write

N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta)=\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}\tau^{j}(\beta).

Note that
\beta\tau(N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta))=N_{i+1}^{\tau}(\beta)
and thus

(2.1)

N_{i+j}^{\tau}(\beta)=N_{i}^{\tau}(\beta)\cdot\sigma^{i}(N_{j}^{\tau}(\beta))

for all
i,j\geq 0
. If
S=K
is a field,
K/K_{0}
a cyclic Galois extension, and
\Gal(K/K_{0})=\langle\sigma\rangle
, where
\sigma
has order
\mathbf{m}
, then
N_{\mathbf{m}}^{\sigma}
is simply the norm map
N_{K/K_{0}}
corresponding to
K/K_{0}
. More generally,
N_{\mathbf{m}}^{\sigma}(\beta)\in S_{0}
for all
\beta\in S
so we distinguish this case with the notation

N_{\mathbf{m}}^{\sigma}=N_{S/S_{0}}
.

Let us define the class of homomorphisms of Petit algebras that correspond to Hamming-weight preserving homomorphisms of skew polycyclic codes.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be monic polynomials of degree

. Suppose

,

For

(2.2)

, in which case we have simply

In this paper, we expand the set of cases for which it is known that all homomorphisms or isomorphisms of Petit algebras are monomial. This was previously known in several cases, but so far only for isomorphisms.

z
by
[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}=sk-z{\mathbf{n}}
. Applying
\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}=\sigma^{sk-z{\mathbf{n}}}
to both sides yields

\frac{\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))}{\sigma^{sk}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))}=\prod_{j=1}^{z}\sigma^{sk-j{\mathbf{n}}}\left(\frac{\sigma^{\ell k}(b)}{b}\right).

Finally, cross-multiplying and simplifying yields the desired equality

\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))\prod_{j=1}^{z}\sigma^{sk-j{\mathbf{n}}}(b)=\sigma^{sk}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))\prod_{j=1}^{z}\sigma^{sk-j{\mathbf{n}}+\ell k}(b).

∎

We now demonstrate that the condition (
3.1
) is exactly the key to the power associativity of a monomial in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha,b\in S
and
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose neither
\alpha
nor
b
are zero-divisors and
1\leq k<{\mathbf{n}}
. Consider the magma generated by the monomial

\mathsf{z}=\alpha t^{k}

in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
. If
\mathsf{z}^{r}\mathsf{z}=\mathsf{z}\mathsf{z}^{r}
for the least
r>0
such that
rk\geq{\mathbf{n}}
, then

(3.3)

\alpha b=\sigma^{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)\sigma^{k}(b).

Conversely, if (
3.3
) holds, then for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
, the expression
(\alpha t^{k})^{s}
is well-defined in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
(that is,
\mathsf{z}
is power associative) and we have

(3.4)

\mathsf{z}^{s}=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{{\mathbf{n}}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i{\mathbf{n}}}(b)t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}

where
\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor=j
when
j{\mathbf{n}}\leq sk<(j+1){\mathbf{n}}
and
[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}=sk-\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor\mathbf{n}
denotes the residue of
sk
mod
\mathbf{n}
in the interval
[0,\mathbf{n}-1]
.

Proof.

We first prove that if (
3.3
) holds, then
\mathsf{z}
generates a monoid (that is, all powers are well-defined).
Write
L(\mathsf{z},s)
for the left-nested product

\mathsf{z}(\mathsf{z}(\mathsf{z}(\cdots(\mathsf{z}))))

of
\mathsf{z}
with itself
s
times.
Our first step is to show that this is equal to (
3.4
) by induction on
s
. The case
s=1
is given. Assume for some
s\geq 1
that
L(\mathsf{z},s)
is given by (
3.4
). We wish to show that

(3.5)

L(\mathsf{z},s+1)=N_{s+1}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{(s+1)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{(s+1)k-i\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{[(s+1)k]_{\mathbf{n}}}.

We compute

\displaystyle L(\mathsf{z},s+1)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{z}\cdot L(\mathsf{z},s)

\displaystyle=\alpha t^{k}\cdot N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\cdot\sigma^{k}\left(N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\right)\sigma^{k}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\right)\cdot t^{k}t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle=N_{s+1}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{(s+1)k-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\cdot t^{k}t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}.

Note that since both
k
and
[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}
are less than
\mathbf{n}
, we simply have

[(s+1)k]_{\mathbf{n}}=\begin{cases}k+[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}&\text{if $k+[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}<\mathbf{n}$, that is, if $\lfloor\frac{(s+1)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor$};\\
k+[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{n}&\text{if $k+[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}>\mathbf{n}$, that is, if $\lfloor\frac{(s+1)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor+1$.}\end{cases}

In the former case we are done. In the latter case, we apply the relation

t^{k+[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}=t^{k+[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{n}}t^{\mathbf{n}}=t^{[(s+1)k]_{\mathbf{n}}}b=\sigma^{[(s+1)k]_{\mathbf{n}}}(b)t^{[(s+1)k]_{\mathbf{n}}},

which gives the desired expression for
L(\mathsf{z},s+1),
as required.

We now assume that (
3.3
) holds, and show that this implies power-associativity. By induction it suffices to prove that
for all
\ell,s\in\mathbb{N}
we have

L(\mathsf{z},s)L(\mathsf{z},\ell)=L(\mathsf{z},s+\ell).

This holds for
s=1
and every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. We compute

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle(\mathsf{z},s)\;L(\mathsf{z},\ell)=

\displaystyle=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}\cdot N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{\ell k-i\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\cdot\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}\left(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{\ell k-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\right)\cdot t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}

By Lemma
3.2
, our hypothesis (
3.3
) implies

\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-j\mathbf{n}}(b)=\sigma^{sk}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-j\mathbf{n}+\ell k}(b).

Thus

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle(\mathsf{z},s)\;L(\mathsf{z},\ell)=

\displaystyle=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\cdot\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))\right)\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{\ell k-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\right)\cdot t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\left(\sigma^{sk}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-j\mathbf{n}+\ell k}(b)\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+\ell k-i\mathbf{n}}(b)\cdot t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}.

Since
[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}=sk-\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor\mathbf{n}
, we may reindex the last product to yield

\displaystyle=N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-j\mathbf{n}+\ell k}(b)\prod_{j=\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-j\mathbf{n}+\ell k}(b)\cdot t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}

\displaystyle=N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{(s+\ell)k-j\mathbf{n}}(b)\cdot t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}.

If
[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}=[(s+\ell)k]_{\mathbf{n}}
then this is simply

N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{(s+\ell)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{(s+\ell)k-j\mathbf{n}}(b)\cdot t^{[(s+\ell)k]_{\mathbf{n}}}=L(\mathsf{z},s+\ell).

Otherwise, we must have

\lfloor\frac{(s+\ell)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{\ell k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor+1,

whence
[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}=[(s+\ell)k]_{\mathbf{n}}+\mathbf{n}.

Since
[(s+\ell)k]_{\mathbf{n}}=(s+\ell)k-\lfloor\frac{(s+\ell)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor\mathbf{n}
we have

t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}+[\ell k]_{\mathbf{n}}}=t^{[(s+\ell)k]_{\mathbf{n}}+\mathbf{n}}=\sigma^{(s+\ell)k-\lfloor\frac{(s+\ell)k}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{[(s+\ell)k]_{\mathbf{n}}},

which again yields
L(\mathsf{z},s)L(\mathsf{z},\ell)=L(\mathsf{z},s+\ell).
Thus the desired equality holds for all
s,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathsf{z}
is power associative.

We now prove that if
\mathsf{z}\mathsf{z}^{r}=\mathsf{z}^{r}\mathsf{z}
, for
r>0
the least integer such that
rk\geq\mathbf{n}
, then (
3.3
) holds. We begin by noting that
\mathsf{z}^{r}
is well-defined, since for all
s,\ell\geq 1
such that
s+\ell=r
,

\mathsf{z}^{s}=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{sk},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{z}^{\ell}=N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)t^{\ell k},

by the associativity of powers less than
\mathbf{n}
in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
. Thus we readily compute as above that

\mathsf{z}^{r}=\mathsf{z}^{s}\mathsf{z}^{\ell}=N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\sigma^{sk}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha))t^{(s+\ell)k}=N_{r}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{rk-\mathbf{n}},

independently of the choice of association, and this is equal to
L(\mathsf{z},r)
.

Now by our choice of
r
(and
k
), we have
\mathbf{n}<(r+1)k<2\mathbf{n}
so that
\lfloor(r+1)/\mathbf{n}\rfloor=1
and
[(r+1)k]_{\mathbf{n}}=(r+1)k-\mathbf{n}
. Thus (
3.5
) simplifies to

\mathsf{z}\mathsf{z}^{r}=L(\mathsf{z},r+1)=N_{r+1}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\sigma^{(r+1)k-\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{(r+1)k-\mathbf{n}}

whereas

\mathsf{z}^{r}\mathsf{z}=N_{r}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{rk-\mathbf{n}}\alpha t^{k}=N_{r}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{(r+1)k-\mathbf{n}}.

If these are equal then we may equate these coefficients of
t^{(r+1)k-\mathbf{n}}
. If we divide both expressions by
N_{r}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)
and simplify, we obtain the equality

\sigma^{rk}(\alpha)\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(\sigma^{k}(b))=\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)\sigma^{rk-\mathbf{n}}(b),

which, upon applying the automorphism
\sigma^{\mathbf{n}-rk}
to both sides, is simply (
3.3
).
∎

In particular, Theorem
3.3
shows that the condition (
3.3
) is both necessary and sufficient for full power associativity of the element
\alpha t^{k}
in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in S
not be a zero divisor. Let
\tau
be an automorphism of
S
commuting with the order-
\mathbf{m}
automorphism
\sigma
. Then the map

G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}:S[t;\sigma]\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)

given by
G(a)=\tau(a)
and
G(t)=\alpha t^{k}
induces a homomorphism of
S_{0}^{\tau}
-algebras if and only if

b\in S_{0}^{\times},\quad k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{m},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, the condition
k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{m}
ensures that
G(ta)=G(\sigma(a)t)
. By Theorem
3.3
, if
G
is well-defined then
G(t^{s})=\lambda t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}
for some
\lambda\in S^{\times}
.
Thus the requirement that

G(t^{s})G(a)=G(\sigma^{s}(a))G(t^{s})
for all
a\in S
and all
s\in\mathbb{N}
is equivalent to

\sigma^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}(\tau(a))=\tau(\sigma^{s}(a)).

Since
\tau
commutes with
\sigma
this holds if and only if
s-[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}\equiv 0\mod\mathbf{m}
; since
k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{m}
this implies
\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
.
Under this hypothesis, the condition
\alpha b=\sigma^{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)\sigma^{k}(b)
simplifies to
\sigma(b)=b
or
b\in S_{0}^{\times}
, and
thus
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
is associative.
Consequently
the function
G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}

descends to a well-defined homomorphism
G:S[t;\sigma]\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
.
∎

Note that the homomorphism
G
in Corollary
3.4
is a surjection when additionally
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and
\gcd(k,\mathbf{n})=1
.

These conditions are quite restrictive, reflecting the constrained nature of these nonassociative algebras. For example, one deduces that even with
k=1
, the map
G_{\tau,\alpha,1}
is a homomorphism from
S[t;\sigma]\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
only when
b\in S_{0}^{\times}
and
\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
. This is in fact to be expected:
S[t;\sigma]
is associative whereas
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
is associative if and only if these two strong conditions hold
[
Pet67
, (15)]
.

4.
Monomial homomorphisms between Petit algebras

We now turn to the question of when there exist monomial homomorphisms
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
from a Petit algebra
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
, where
f
has degree
\mathbf{n}
, into the algebra
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
.

It is not evident that
G
is forced to be the reduction of a homomorphism
\tilde{G}:S[t;\sigma]\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
as in Corollary
3.4
.
Namely, in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
,
the associativity of all powers of
t
is a strong condition
[
Pet67
, p. 13-06]
: it is equivalent to
t^{\mathbf{n}}t=tt^{\mathbf{n}}
and to
f(t)t\in S[t;\sigma]f(t)
. In particular, if all powers of
t
are associative in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
, then they are contained in the right nucleus of
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
, which is an associative subalgebra, and thus it is only in this case that Theorem
3.3
applies directly, as in the following example.

Example 4.1
.

Suppose
f(t)\in S_{0}[t]
. Then the associative algebra
S_{0}[t]/\langle f(t)\rangle
is a subalgebra of
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
containing all powers of
t
. It thus follows from Theorem
3.3
that if
G:S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
is a monomial homomorphism,
then we must have

\alpha b=\sigma^{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)\sigma^{k}(b)

and by (
3.4
), for every
s\in\mathbb{N}
, the expression
G(t^{s})=G(t)^{s}
is equal to

(4.1)

N_{s}^{\sigma^{k}}(\alpha)\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{sk}{\mathbf{n}}\rfloor}\sigma^{sk-i\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{[sk]_{\mathbf{n}}}.

When instead
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
is proper nonassociative, then
t^{s}
is only well-defined for
0\leq s<\mathbf{n}
, and the full power associativity of Theorem
3.3
is not required.

To illustrate this, we derive the following generalization of Theorem
2.2
, about the existence of monomial homomorphisms of degree
1
, between Petit algebras of our chosen form.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\tau\in\Aut(S)
commute with
\sigma
and suppose
\alpha\in S
is not a zero divisor. Then the map

G_{\tau,\alpha}:S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)

induces an homomorphism if and only if

\tau(a)=N_{\mathbf{n}}^{\sigma}(\alpha)b,

and this is an isomorphism if and only if
\alpha\in S^{\times}
.

In particular, there is no requirement on the relationship between
\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{m}
.

Proof.

Write
G=G_{\tau,\alpha}
; then
G(t)=\alpha t
and so for all
1\leq s<\mathbf{n}
we unambiguously have
G(t^{s})=N_{s}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s}
since these powers are associative in both rings. Thus since
G
acts by
\tau
on
S
,
G
extends to an
S_{0}^{\tau}
-linear isomorphism of the underlying
S_{0}^{\tau}
-modules.

Furthermore, for all
c\in S
and
0<s<\mathbf{n}
we have
t^{s}c=\sigma^{s}(c)t^{s}
. We find

G(t^{s})G(c)=N_{s}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s}\tau(c)=N_{s}^{\sigma}(\alpha)\sigma^{s}(\tau(c))t^{s}=\tau(\sigma^{s}(c))N_{s}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s}=G(\sigma^{s}(c))G(t^{s})

and thus this identity always holds.

We now verify that
G(pq)=G(p)G(q)
for all
p,q\in S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)
. By the above, it suffices to choose arbitrary
1\leq s,\ell<\mathbf{n}
and prove this for
p=t^{s}
,
q=t^{\ell}
.

Consider the product
t^{s}t^{\ell}
in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)
. If
s+\ell<\mathbf{n}
the result is
t^{s+\ell}
and we correspondingly have

G(t^{s})G(t^{\ell})=N_{s}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s}N_{\ell}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{\ell}=N_{s}^{\sigma}(\alpha)\sigma^{s}(N_{\ell}^{\sigma}(\alpha))t^{s+\ell}=N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s+\ell}

which is equal to
G(t^{s+\ell})
.
If
s+\ell\geq\mathbf{n}
, however, then we have
t^{s}t^{\ell}=t^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}a=\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(a)t^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}
whose image under
G
is

G(t^{s}t^{\ell})=\tau(\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(a))N_{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}

while

G(t^{s})G(t^{\ell})=N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma}(\alpha)t^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}b=N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma}(\alpha)\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(b)t^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}.

Since
N_{s+\ell}^{\sigma}(\alpha)=N_{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}^{\sigma}(\alpha)\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(N_{\mathbf{n}}^{\sigma}(\alpha))
by (
2.1
)
and
N^{\sigma}_{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)
is assumed to be cancellable, we deduce that these two expressions are equal whenever

\tau(\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(a))=\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(N_{\mathbf{n}}^{\sigma}(\alpha))\sigma^{s+\ell-\mathbf{n}}(b).

Therefore, since
\tau
and
\sigma
commute, our map is a homomorphism of
S_{0}^{\tau}
-algebras if and only if
\tau(a)=N_{\mathbf{n}}^{\sigma}(\alpha)b
. It is invertible exactly when
\alpha^{-1}\in S
.
∎

For example, when
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{n}
,
S=K
is a Galois field extension of
K_{0}
and
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(K/K_{0})
, we recover the condition that
\tau(a)\in bN_{K/K_{0}}(K^{\times})
from
[
BP18
, Corollary 32]
.

We now turn to the case that the degree
k
of the monomial homomorphism
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
is strictly greater than
1
.
We begin with a lemma that is a version of Lemma
3.1
for the present setting.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
f,g\in S[t;\sigma]
are monic polynomials of degree
\mathbf{n}
. If for some cancellable
\alpha\in S
and degree
k>1
, the map
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}:S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]g(t)
is a monomial homomorphism, then
k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{m}
; if it is an isomorphism then
\gcd(k,\mathbf{n})=1
and
\alpha\in S^{\times}
.

Proof.

If
G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
is a homomorphism, then the identity
G(td)=G(\sigma(d)t)
implies
\alpha t^{k}\tau(d)=\tau(\sigma(d))\alpha t^{k}
for all
d\in S
, whence we must have

\sigma^{k}(\tau(d))=\tau(\sigma(d))

for all
d\in S
. Thus since
\tau
and
\sigma
commute,
G(td)=G(\sigma(d)t)
is equivalent to
k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{m}
.

If
G
is to be an isomorphism, there must exist an inverse homomorphism
H
. Evidently
H(d)=\tau^{-1}(d)
for all
d\in S
. Since
G
is monomial, so is its inverse, and thus we may assume
H(t)=\beta t^{\ell}
for some
\beta\in S^{\times}
and
1\leq\ell<\mathbf{n}
. Since
H(G(t))=t
we must have
\ell k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{n}
and in particular
\gcd(k,\mathbf{n})=1
. Moreover, if
\alpha
were not invertible, then
t^{k}
could not lie in the image of
G
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose
\tau\in\Aut(S)
commutes with
\sigma
and
\alpha\in S
is cancellable. Then for any
2\leq k<\mathbf{n}
, the map

G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}:S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)

is a homomorphism of rings if and only if all of the following conditions hold:

(1)

k\equiv 1\mod\mathbf{m}
;

(2)

\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
;

(3)

a,b\in S_{0}
;

(4)

(N_{S/S_{0}}(\alpha))^{\mathbf{n}/\mathbf{m}}b^{k}=\tau(a)
.

It is an isomorphism if and only if, in addition, we have

(5)

\gcd(k,\mathbf{n})=1
and
\alpha\in S^{\times}
.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\Aut(S)
commute with
\sigma
. Fix
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and
2\leq k<\mathbf{n}
. Suppose first that the map
G=G_{\tau,\alpha,k}:S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
defined by the rule

(4.2)

G\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\mathbf{n}-1}a_{i}t^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathbf{n}-1}\tau(a_{i})(\alpha t^{k})^{i}

is a well-defined homomorphism.

Conditions (
1
) and (
5
) follow from Lemma
4.3
.

For
G
to be well-defined, we require for all
0<s<\mathbf{n}
that
G(t^{s})=G(t)^{s}
is well-defined. Since
k>1
, the minimal value of
r
such that
rk\geq\mathbf{n}
satisfies
0<r<\mathbf{n}
and thus by Theorem
3.3
, we conclude that
\alpha b=\sigma^{\mathbf{n}}(\alpha)\sigma^{k}(b)
. Conversely, Theorem
3.3
further assures us that this hypothesis implies all powers of
\alpha t^{k}
are well-defined so (
4.2
) gives a well-defined map.

Next, we note from Theorem
3.3
that for any
0<s<\mathbf{n}
, the element

5.
Applications

In Section
4
, we classified all monomial homomorphisms between Petit algebras of the form
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)
. Since the left ideals of these algebras give rise to skew constacyclic codes, we have classified the Hamming-weight preserving maps, or isometries, between their corresponding skew constacyclic codes.

5.1.
Isometric constacyclic codes

In
[
OaNA25
]
, the authors explore Hamming-weight preserving isomorphisms between nonassociative algebras of the form

K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)

over a finite field
K=\mathbb{F}_{p^{r}}
with respect to a Frobenius automorphism
\sigma(a)=a^{p^{s}}
for some
s|r
, as an important step towards classifying skew constacyclic codes of length
\mathbf{n}
with identical performance parameters. This is necessary to avoid duplicating already existing codes. To this end, they proposed notions of
(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-equivalence and
(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-isometry, respectively.

We say two rings
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)
and
K[t;\sigma]/K[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
are
(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-equivalent
if they are isomorphic via an isomorphism of the form
G_{\id,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in K^{\times}
, and

(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-isometric
if they are isomorphic via an isomorphism of the form
G_{\id,\alpha,k}
for some
\alpha\in K^{\times}
and
k\geq 1
.

By Corollary
4.5
, we conclude that these notions coincide whenever
a
or
b
lies in
K_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{p^{s}}
, and whenever the order of
\sigma
(which is
r/s
) does not divide
\mathbf{n}
.

Inspired by their work, we now propose a more complete notion of equivalence for such rings.

Definition 5.1
.

Two rings
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-a)
and
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
are called
isometric
if there exists a monomial isomorphism
G_{\tau,\alpha,k}
between them for some
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and
1\leq k<\mathbf{n}
. As a special case, we say they are
equivalent
if they are isomorphic via an isomorphism of the form
G_{\tau,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in S^{\times}
and some
\tau\in\Aut(S)
commuting with
\sigma
.

Thus if two rings are
(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-isometric, or
(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-equivalent, then they are isometric, and if they are
(\mathbf{n},\sigma)
-equivalent then they are equivalent. Moreover, this definition captures all of the possible Hamming-weight preserving isomorphisms between these algebras, making it the correct notion of equivalence between codes.

5.2.

6.
On non monomial homomorphisms

Having established the scarcity of monomial homomorphisms, we now show further that if
S
is an integral domain (e.g., a field), then in a large number of cases every homomorphism between proper nonassociative algebras of the form

is Hamming weight preserving — in fact, monomial of degree one, that is, is of the form
G_{\tau,\alpha}
for some
\tau\in\Aut(S)
commuting with
\sigma
and nonzero
\alpha\in S
.

We begin by proving a generalization to the polynomial case of Lemma
4.3
. We assume in this section that
S
has no nonzero zero divisors, that is, it is an integral domain, such as a field.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
f(t)\in S[t;\sigma]
is a monic polynomial of degree
\mathbf{n}
and
b\in S^{\times}
. Suppose that
G:S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)\to S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
is a nonzero homomorphism such that

is not a monomial, whose restriction to
S
is given by some automorphism
\tau
commuting with
\sigma

\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
and

G(t)=p(t^{\mathbf{m}})t
for some

of degree less than
\frac{\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{m}}

Proof.

Write

G(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\mathbf{n}-1}c_{i}t^{i}

for some
c_{i}\in S
.
As in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, which holds for any commutative unital ring
\lx@sectionsign
, the condition
G(td)=G(\sigma(d)t)
for all
d\in S
implies that
c_{i}=0
for all

such that

. Therefore

takes the form given in the statement. It remains to prove that
\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
.

As compared with the monomial case, the proof now is more intricate because each term of
G(t)^{s}
is a complex polynomial.
In the following, we differentiate between the expression
(tp(t^{\mathbf{m}}))^{s}
in
S[t;\sigma]
and the value of
G(t)^{s}\in S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
, that is, after reduction
{\rm mod}_{r}(t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
(whose well-definedness is the key question).

Let
k=\deg(G(t))>1
. Let
s>1
be the least value for which
sk\geq\mathbf{n}
; since
k>1
we have
s<\mathbf{n}
. Then since
G
is homomorphism,

must be power associative for all powers less than
\mathbf{n}
(since this is true in
S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma]f(t)
) and we have
G(t^{s})=G(t)^{s}
. Furthermore, since
G(t)^{s-1}=(tp(t^{\mathbf{m}}))^{s-1}
has degree less than
\mathbf{n}
, all monomials appearing with nonzero coefficient in
(tp(t^{\mathbf{m}}))^{s}\in S[t;\sigma]
have degree less than

. Thus we may compute
G(t)^{s}\in S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
by first evaluating the expression
(tp(t^{\mathbf{m}}))^{s}
in
S[t;\sigma]
and then reducing modulo the relation
t^{\mathbf{n}+r}\equiv t^{r}b\equiv\sigma^{r}(b)t^{r}
.

Write
\mathcal{D}^{s}
for the set of all
d\in\mathbb{N}
that occur as the degree of some monomial appearing in the expansion of
(tp(t^{\mathbf{m}}))^{s}\in S[t;\sigma]
and

for the set of all degrees appearing in

\mathcal{B}^{s}\subset\{0,1,\cdots,\mathbf{n}-1\}
and
\mathcal{D}^{s}\subset\mathcal{B}^{s}+(\mathbf{n}+\mathcal{B}^{s})
.

Since each monomial of
G(t)=tp(t^{\mathbf{m}})
has degree of the form
1+\ell\mathbf{m}
for some
\ell\geq 0
, it follows that for each
d\in\mathcal{D}^{s}
we have
d\equiv s\mod\mathbf{m}
.
For each such
d
that satisfies
d\geq\mathbf{n}
, we obtain a monomial term
\lambda t^{d-\mathbf{n}}
in the expression for
G(t)^{s}\in S[t;\sigma]/S[t;\sigma](t^{\mathbf{n}}-b)
, for some
\lambda\in S
.

If
\lambda=0
, then some cancellation occurred, which necessarily implies that
d-\mathbf{n}=d^{\prime}
for some

satisfying

. Subtracting
s
from both sides yields
\mathbf{n}\equiv 0\mod\mathbf{m}
, or
\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
.

If
\lambda\neq 0
, then we may write

G(t)^{s}=\lambda t^{d-\mathbf{n}}+q(t)

where
q(t)
contains no monomials of degree
d-\mathbf{n}
. Let
c\in S
. Then the relation

holds in the domain of
G
; since
G
is a homomorphism, this implies
G(t)^{s}\tau(c)=\tau(\sigma^{s}(c))G(t)^{s}
. This scalar relation holds on each monomial term and so in particular one must have

\lambda t^{d-\mathbf{n}}\tau(c)=\tau(\sigma^{s}(c))\lambda t^{d-\mathbf{n}}.

The expression on the left evaluates to
\lambda\sigma^{d-\mathbf{n}}(\tau(c))t^{d-\mathbf{n}}

is not a zero divisor, we infer

\sigma^{d-\mathbf{n}}(\tau(c))=\tau(\sigma^{s}(c))

for all
c\in S
. Since
\tau
commutes with
\sigma
, this implies
d-\mathbf{n}\equiv s\mod\mathbf{m}
, and so again we deduce
\mathbf{n}\equiv 0\mod\mathbf{m}
, or
\mathbf{m}|\mathbf{n}
, as required.

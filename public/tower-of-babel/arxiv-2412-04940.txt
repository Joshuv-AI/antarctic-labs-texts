Abstract
. We show that for every g ≥ 5 g\geq 5 , the locus of Prym varieties in 𝒜 g − 1 \mathcal{A}_{g-1} that possess a pseudoreflection of geometric origin is the union of three different non-empty explicit irreducible families. This is in stark contrast to the loci of Jacobian varieties that possess a pseudoreflection of geometric origin, which is empty for any genus greater than 3. In g = 6 g=6 , a distinguished example of Prym varieties with a pseudoreflection is given by intermediate Jacobians of cubic threefolds that possess an Eckardt point.

1.
Introduction

In
[
ALA22
, Theorem 1.2]
it was shown that if
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 1
and
G\leq\Aut(C)
is a finite subgroup such that
JC/G
is smooth, then
g\leq 3
and the pair
(C,G)
can be described explicitly.
In other words, if a Jacobian variety has a smooth quotient by a finite group whose action
comes from the curve
, then its dimension is at most
3
.
The natural question to ask then is if a similar result holds true for Prym varieties.

Consider the following: Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 1
, let
\eta\in JC[2]
be a non-trivial 2-torsion point, and let
G\leq\Aut(C)
be a finite group of automorphisms such that each of its elements fixes
\eta
.
Then
\eta
induces an étale double covering

f:\tilde{C}\to C

given by a fixed point free involution
\iota\in\Aut(\tilde{C})
, and there exists an extension

1\to\langle\iota\rangle\to\tilde{G}\to G\to 1

such that
\tilde{G}\leq\Aut(\tilde{C})
and
f
is
\tilde{G}
-equivariant.
Let
P:=P(\tilde{C},C)
denote the Prym variety of
f
.
Since
\iota
acts on
J\tilde{C}=f^{*}JC+P
as the identity on
f^{*}JC
and as
-1
on
P
, and since the action of
\tilde{G}
on
J\tilde{C}
by pullback also preserves both of these factors, we have that
\iota
is central in
\tilde{G}
.
We will say that any automorphism on
P
that fixes the polarization and that comes from an automorphism on
C
in this way is
of geometric origin
.

We recall that if
H
is a group that acts on a manifold
X
, then the Chevalley-Shepard-Todd Theorem states that
X/H
is smooth if and only if for every
x\in X
, the group
\mathrm{Stab}_{H}(x)
is generated by
pseudoreflections
, where a pseudoreflection (at
x
) is an element of the group whose fixed locus passing through
x
is of pure codimension 1.

We would like to understand the following question:

Question:
When does there exist
\sigma\in G
that can be lifted to
P
as a pseudoreflection?

If
G
contains such an element, then we would have in particular that
P/\sigma
is smooth.
The main theorem of this article states that, unlike the case of Jacobians, there are Prym varieties of arbitrarily large dimension with a smooth quotient.
Let
\mathcal{R}_{g}
denote the moduli space of étale double covers of curves of genus
g
, and let

\mathcal{P}_{g}:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g-1}

denote the Prym map.
Our main theorem states the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
g\geq 5
, the locus of Prym varieties in
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
that possess a pseudoreflection of geometric origin is the union of three different explicit irreducible families, one of dimension
2g-4
and the other two of dimension
2g-3
.

These families are described in Subsection
4.1
and Theorems
4.3
and
4.6
, and are shown to be different in Subsection
4.5
.
An interesting example is given in the case of genus
5
in Subsection
4.4
, where we show that Prym varieties with a pseudoreflection appear naturally as intermediate Jacobians of cubic threefolds with Eckardt points (that is, points such that there are infinitely many lines in the cubic threefold passing through them).
We finish the article by giving a restriction on the possible groups of geometric origin that can act on Prym varieties with smooth quotient; there are at most
5
, and this analysis is done in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we will review a few basic preliminaries we will need in the sections that follow.

2.1.
Prym varieties

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
and let
\eta\in JC[2]
be a non-trivial 2-torsion point;
\eta
induces an étale double cover

f:\tilde{C}\to C

associated to an involution
\iota\in\Aut(\tilde{C})
such that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}\oplus\eta.

The universal property of the Jacobian variety induces the so-called norm map
\Nm_{f}:J\tilde{C}\rightarrow JC
which is a surjective map of abelian varieties.
The component of the origin of the Kernel of
\Nm_{f}
is called the Prym variety of
f
and is denoted either by
P(f)
or
P(\tilde{C},C)
.
Notice that its dimension is
g-1=g(\tilde{C})-g
.
The restriction of the polarization of
J\tilde{C}
to
P(f)
is twice a principal polarization.
Hence, denoting by
\mathcal{R}_{g}
the moduli space of isomorphism classes of double coverings
f:\tilde{C}\rightarrow C
as above, we have a map:

\mathcal{P}_{g}:\mathcal{R}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g-1},

which is usually called the Prym map, and the (closure of) the image is called the Prym locus.
We refer to
[
Don92
]
for the main properties of
\mathcal{P}_{g}
.
In particular, for
g\geq 7
, this map
\mathcal{P}_{g}
is generically injective (but never injective).
For
g=6
the source and the target have the same dimension and
\mathcal{P}_{6}
is generically finite of degree
27
.
Finally, for
g\leq 5
,
\mathcal{P}_{g}
is dominant with positive-dimensional generic fiber.
Note that all Prym varieties of dimension
\leq 3
are either Jacobians or products of Jacobians, hence we impose the restriction
\dim P(C,\eta)=g-1\geq 4
, that is
g\geq 5
.

Although we are interested in Prym varieties, our main examples are isogenous to products of abelian varieties obtained via the Prym construction applied to ramified coverings of curves.
Let us consider the moduli space:

\mathcal{R}_{g,r}=\{f:D\rightarrow C\mid g(C)=g,f\text{ double covering ramified in }r\text{ points}\}/\cong,

where
D,C
are smooth irreducible curves,
g\geq 1
, and
r>0
is an even integer.
This moduli space is also irreducible (see, for instance,
[
Bud24
, Proposition 2.5]
for
g\geq 2
, a similar monodromy argument works for
g=1
).

Then,
P(f)
is defined, as above, as the kernel of the norm map
\Nm_{f}
(which now is connected), and the polarization on
JD
restricts to a polarization of type

\delta:=(1,\ldots,1,2,\ldots,2),

where
2
appears
g
times.
The ramified Prym map attaching
P(f)
to
f
is denoted by:

\mathcal{P}_{g,r}:\mathcal{R}_{g,r}\to\mathcal{A}_{g-1+\frac{r}{2}}^{\delta}.

It is known (see
[
Ike20
]
and
[
NO22
]
) that
\mathcal{P}_{g,r}
is injective for
r\geq 6
.

2.2.
Smooth quotients

Let
G
be a finite group acting holomorphically on a complex manifold
X
.
An element
\sigma\in G
is a
pseudoreflection
at
x\in X
if
\sigma(x)=x
, and the fixed locus of
\sigma
, in a neighborhood of
x
, is of pure codimension 1.
The Chevalley-Shephard-Todd Theorem states that
X/G
is smooth if and only if for every
x\in X
,
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)
is generated by pseudoreflections at
x
.
In particular, if
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, then
X/G
is smooth if and only if the fixed locus of the non-trivial element of
G
is of pure codimension 1.

In
[
ALA20
]
, a characterization was given of smooth quotients of abelian varieties by finite groups that fix the origin, and in
[
ALA22
]
, it was shown which principally polarized abelian varieties have a smooth quotient such that the group acting also preserves the polarization.
We will revisit this in Section
5
in more detail.
Note that if
\sigma
is an automorphism of an abelian variety that fixes the origin, then each component of the fixed locus of
\sigma
has the same dimension.
For this reason, it makes sense to talk about a pseudoreflection on the abelian variety, without specifying at which point.

By the results in
[
ALA20
]
, we have that any pseudoreflection on an abelian variety is of order
2
,
3
,
4
or
6
, and in the next section we will show that in the case of the existence of a pseudoreflection on a Prym variety with a geometric origin, it must be an involution.

3.
Prym varieties with an odd prime order pseudoreflection of geometric origin

Our main goal is to determine families of Prym varieties with a pseudoreflection.
We will start by considering any automorphism
\sigma\in\Aut(C)
such that
\sigma^{*}\eta\cong\eta
and study its behavior; we will later impose the condition that
\sigma
is a pseudoreflection.
It is well-known that any automorphism that leaves
\eta
invariant lifts to the cyclic cover, and therefore
\sigma
induces an automorphism on
\tilde{C}
that we will denote by the same letter.
We note that this lifting is not unique, since if
\sigma
is one such lifting,
\iota\sigma
is another.

First we see that
J\tilde{C}=f^{*}JC+P
and that
\iota
acts on
f^{*}JC
as
\id
and on
P
as
-\id
.
Moreover, since the action of
\sigma
comes from
C
and
\sigma
preserves the polarization of
J\tilde{C}
, we have that
\sigma
acts on
f^{*}JC
and
P
as well.
In particular, we have that
\sigma
and
\iota
commute on
J\tilde{C}
, and therefore also as automorphisms of
\tilde{C}
.

In this section, we will show that automorphisms of odd prime order will never provide a pseudoreflection.
Hence, we are able to reduce the problem to the involution case, which will be the content of the next section.

Let us then assume that
\sigma
is any automorphism (not necessarily a pseudoreflection) of prime order
p>2
.
In this case, we have that
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma=C/\sigma
since
\sigma
is of odd order.
We therefore have the following commutative diagram:

Proof.

Indeed, the ramification of

Now let’s analyze the action of

Note that
\pi_{2}^{*}P(\epsilon_{2})

where
\iota

In other words:

\pi_{2}^{*}P(\epsilon_{2})=\Fix_{P}(\sigma)_{0}.

Let
\gamma
be the codimension of
\pi_{2}^{*}P(\epsilon_{2})

We note that
\sigma
is a pseudoreflection if and only if
\gamma=1
.
Since
\dim P(\epsilon_{2})=g(\tilde{C}/\sigma)-g(C/\sigma)
, we have that

\gamma+g(\tilde{C}/\sigma)-g(C/\sigma)=g-1.

By the Riemann Hurwitz formula for
\epsilon_{2}
, we get that
g(\tilde{C}/\sigma)-g(C/\sigma)=g(C/\sigma)-1
, and so

g(C/\sigma)=g-\gamma.

Now if we plug in Riemann Hurwitz for
\epsilon

\sigma

C

2g-2=p(2g(C/\sigma)-2)+t(p-1)=p(2g-2\gamma-2)+t(p-1),

By equation (

, and therefore
p-1
must divide
2\gamma
.
From this, we easily obtain the following result:

Proposition 3.2
.

For
g\geq 3
,
there are no pseudoreflections of order greater than 2 on
P
that come from
C
.

Proof.

If
\sigma
is a pseudoreflection on
P
, then
\gamma=1
, and
p

3
.
In this case, equation (

) becomes
2g=5-2t
.
Since
g\geq 3
, then
2t\leq 5-2g<0
, which is impossible.
∎

For automorphisms of order
3
, we also get a general result on the codimension

that will come in handy when we look at the possible groups that can act on Prym varieties with smooth quotients (see Section

Proposition 3.3
.

If
g\geq 5
and

is an automorphism of

3
, then
\gamma\geq 3
.

Proof.

Replacing

in equation (

.
Then
0\leq t=2-2g+3\gamma
.
Since
g\geq 5
, we get
3\gamma\geq 2g-2\geq 8
.
Thus
\gamma\geq 3
.
∎

Involutions

section 3

Lemma 4.1
.

We have that

The involution

\sigma

, and in particular
g(F)\in\{0,1,2\}
.
We will proceed to analyze the situation based on these three cases.

4.1.
The case
g(F)=0

This case has already been considered in
[
Mum74
]
by Mumford.
He proves that
P(\epsilon_{1})=JC_{1}
,
P(\epsilon_{2})=JC_{2}
, and
P\cong JC_{1}\times JC_{2}
.
Here
JC_{1}
is an elliptic curve and
\sigma
acts on
P
as
-1
on
JC_{1}
and as the identity on
JC_{2}
.
In this case, we therefore have that

P/\sigma=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times JC_{2}

is the trivial
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
JC_{2}
.
We note moreover that this situation never occurs in the case of quotients of Jacobian varieties, since these are irreducible.

The family we obtain in this way is
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{g-2}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
, where
\mathcal{JH}_{g-2}
stands for the locus of hyperelliptic Jacobians of dimension
g-2
.
This family is irreducible of dimension
1+2(g-2)-1=2g-4
.

Remark 4.2
.

We notice that this is the only case where the Prym variety is a polarized product.
Indeed, if the theta divisor
\Xi
has two or more components, then the singular locus
\operatorname{Sing}(\Xi)
has codimension
2
in
P
.
According to
[
Bea77

, this only happens if
C
is hyperelliptic (we are assuming that
C
is smooth, otherwise other possibilities appear).

4.2.
The case
g(F)=1

The Prym map for bielliptic curves is considered in
[

,

.
We introduce some notation: let

It is known that
\mathcal{B}_{g}

The preimage of

The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 4.3

Assume that

, then the bielliptic Prym locus
\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2})
gives an irreducible

In

it is proved, under the hypothesis

We first observe that there are finitely many pseudoreflections acting on the polarized Prym variety

Then we have the following:

(a)

\mathcal{F}_{g}

Proof.

The first statement follows from the analysis done after Remark 1 on Page 138 of

\mathcal{T}:=\{(F,L,B,\eta_{1})\}/_{\cong}

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume that
g\geq 5
.
Then the Prym locus
\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3})
gives a
(2g-3)
-dimensional family of curves with a pseudoreflection.
Moreover, the restriction of the Prym map
\mathcal{P}_{g}|_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}}
has finite fibers if
g\geq 6
, and is only generically finite if
g=5
.

Proof.

For
g\geq 6
we need to prove that
\mathcal{P}_{g}|_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}}
is quasifinite.
For this, let
(P,\Xi)\in\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3})
.
Since
\Aut(P,\Xi)
is a finite group, in particular there are finitely many pseudoreflections.
If we take one pseudoreflection
\sigma\in\Aut(A,\Xi)
, then we want to show that there are finitely many pairs
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}
with an involution that induce
\sigma
.

If
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}
is a preimage of
(P,\Xi)
, then
Z:=\Fix_{P}(\sigma)_{0}=P(\epsilon_{2})
where
\epsilon_{2}:\tilde{C}/\sigma\to C/\sigma
is the natural map as before.
Now by Riemann Hurwitz,
\iota
acts on
\tilde{C}/\sigma
with
2g-6\geq 4
fixed points.
If
g\geq 6
, by
[
NO22
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\epsilon_{2}
is the unique ramified double cover whose Prym variety is equal to
(Z,\Xi|_{Z})
.
Therefore, given
(P,\Xi,\sigma)
, we can recover
\epsilon_{2}
.
On the other hand, the étale cover
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma\to C/\sigma
is given by a non-trivial 2-torsion point
\eta_{1}\in J(C/\sigma)[2]
, of which there are finitely many.
This implies that
\tilde{C}=(\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma)\times_{C/\sigma}\tilde{C}/\sigma
, has finitely many possibilities once we fix the pseudoreflection
\sigma
.
Moreover, we see that once we define
\tilde{C}
,
\iota
is immediately defined as the lifting of the involution associated to the étale double cover
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma\to C/\sigma
.
We conclude that
\mathcal{P}_{g}|_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}}
is quasifinite.

For
g=5
, the same analysis holds, but the ramified Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{5,4}:\mathcal{R}_{5,4}\to\mathcal{A}_{6}^{(1,2,2,2,2,2)}

is only generically injective, and by
[
NO22
]
, it actually has fibers of positive dimension.
∎

For later applications, it will be useful to know that generically the abelian variety
P(\epsilon_{2})
is simple.
This is a particular case of the following fact, that may have independent interest and, at the same time, probably well-known by the experts.

Proposition 4.7
.

For
g\geq 2
, the generic element of the image of the ramified Prym map

\mathcal{P}_{g,r}:\mathcal{R}_{g,r}\to\mathcal{A}^{\delta}_{g-1+\frac{r}{2}}

is simple.

Proof.

Observe that the infinitesimal deformations of an element
(F,L,B)\in\mathcal{R}_{g,r}
are the same as the infinitesimal deformations of
(F,B)
(since if
B
is fixed, then
L
has finitely many options).
The space of these infinitesimal deformations is
H^{1}(F,T_{F}(-B))
, the tangent space to the moduli space of genus
g
curves with
r
marked points.
The tangent space to the Prym variety
P\in\mathcal{P}_{g,r}(\mathcal{R}_{g,r})
is
T_{0}P=H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)^{*}
and the differential of the Prym map:

H^{1}(F,T_{F}(-B))\to\operatorname{Sym}^{2}H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)^{*}

is given by the cup-product:

H^{1}(F,T_{F}(-B))\otimes H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)\to H^{1}(F,L^{-1})\cong H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)^{*}.

Since there is a natural polarization on
P
, there is a canonical isomorphism
H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)^{*}\cong\overline{H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)}
, and composing with the conjugation map we have that the infinitesimal deformations act on
H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)
.
Assume that a generic
P
has a proper subabelian variety.
Thus, by a standard argument, there exists a proper subspace
(0)\neq W\subsetneq H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)
such that
\epsilon\cdot W\subset W
, for all
\epsilon\in H^{1}(F,T_{F}(-B))
.
Notice that
W
is the tangent space at the origin of the quotient of
P
by the proper abelian subvariety.

Let
s\in H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)
be a section, and consider the map:

m_{s}:H^{1}(F,T_{F}(-B))\to H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)^{*},\quad\epsilon\mapsto\epsilon\cdot s.

The dual of this map is multiplication by
s
:

H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)\to H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}^{2}(B))

which is obviously injective.
Hence,
m_{s}
is surjective and, by the invariance of
W
by deformations, all the sections of
H^{0}(F,\omega_{F}\otimes L)
belong to
W
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

The degree of the generic fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{g}|_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}}
is at most 15.

Proof.

We have already seen at the beginning of Subsection
4.3
that an element of the image of
\mathcal{P}_{g}|_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}}
depends on a tuple
(F,L,B,\eta_{1})
.
For a general
F
,
L
and

, we have that the Prym variety associated to the double cover is simple by the previous Lemma.
This implies that for a general
(P,\Xi)
in the image of
\mathcal{P}_{g}|_{\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3}}
, we have an isogeny

, where

is an elliptic curve and

on

Therefore, the different possibilities for constructing

from a curve

depend only on the choice of

2

∎

4.4.
A distinguished example with
g(F)=2
(Cubic threefolds with Eckardt points).

Prym varieties of dimension
5
with a pseudoreflection appear naturally when considering intermediate Jacobians of cubic threefolds with Eckardt points, that is, points such that there are infinitely many lines in the cubic threefold passing through them.
We refer to
[
CMZ21
]
, with special attention to Section 7 as a reference for this subsection.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
be a cubic threefold and let
p\in X
and Eckardt point.
Then, there is an involution
\sigma_{p}
of
\mathbb{P}^{4}
such that
\sigma_{p}(X)=X
.
The set of fixed points by
\sigma_{p}
consists of
p
and a hyperplane
H
with
p\notin H
.
We denote by
S
the cubic surface
H\cap X
which is smooth.
Recall that for any line
l
in the Fano surface,
F(X)=\left\{r\in\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\mid r\subset X\right\}
, the projection to a supplementary plane of
l
provides a conic-bundle structure on
X
, that is, a map from the blow-up of
X
at
l
to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
whose fibers are conics:

\pi_{l}:\operatorname{Bl}_{l}X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}.

The discriminant plane curve, where the fibers of
\pi_{l}
degenerate, is a plane quintic.
There is a curve
\widetilde{Q}_{l}\subset F(X)
parametrizing the lines contained in these degenerate conics.
It turns out that the Prym variety of the natural covering
\widetilde{Q}_{l}\rightarrow Q_{l}
is isomorphic, as principally polarized abelian varieties, to the intermediate Jacobian
JX
.
As it is proved in
[
CMZ21
, Lemma 7.10]
, the quintic attached to any of the
27
lines
l\in F(X)
contained in the cubic surface
S
, comes equipped with a degree
2
map
f:Q_{l}\rightarrow F
on a curve
F
of genus
2
.
Moreover, the involution on
Q_{l}
attached to
f
induces on
P(\widetilde{Q}_{l},Q_{l})\cong JX
the same pseudoreflection as the involution
\sigma_{p}
on
X
.
Hence, this is an example of Prym variety with a pseudoreflection in
\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{R}\mathcal{F}_{g,3})
with
27
representations as Prym variety of the double covering of a quintic plane curve.

Notice that this example also shows that there are Prym varieties with more than one pseudoreflection of geometric origin.
Indeed, consider a cubic threefold
X
with equation:

z\,Q(u,v)+(x^{2}+y^{2})\,L(z,u,v)=0,

where
L,Q
are appropriate forms of degrees
1
and
2
respectively such that the threefold is smooth.
Then
[1:0:0:0:0]
is an Eckardt point with associated involution
[x:y:z:u:v]\mapsto[-x:y:z:u:v]
.
In the same way
[0:1:0:0:0]
is another Eckartd point with involution
[x:y:z:u:v]\mapsto[x:-y:z:u:v]
.
The hyperplane of fixed points for the first involution is
x=0
and for the second
y=0
.
The line
l:=\{x=y=z=0\}
belongs to both cubic surfaces
X\cap\{x=0\}
and
X\cap\{y=0\}
.
Hence the discriminant quintic of the conic bundle given by the projection from
l
has two natural involutions and, according with the description above, the intermediate Jacobian
JX
has two different pseudoreflections of geometric origin.

4.5.
The three families are different

We have found, assuming
g\geq 5
, the existence of three irreducible families of Prym varieties with an action of a finite group of geometric origin and with smooth quotient:

\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{g-2},\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2}),\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RF}_{g,3})\subset\mathcal{A}_{g-1}.

The first has dimension
2g-4
, and the other two have dimension
2g-3
.
By the discussion at the beginning of Section
4
, any Prym variety with such an action belongs to one of these families.
It is natural to ask if they are different and, more generally, to compute how big the intersection of two of them can be
In
[
Nar92
, Lemma 3.1]
it is proved that bielliptic curves of genus at least
4
are never hyperelliptic.
Combined with
subsection 4.1
, we deduce that:

\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{g-2}\cap\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2})=\emptyset.

To understand the intersection of
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{g-2}
with
\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RF}_{g,3})
we need to know when a curve of genus
g\geq 5
with a degree
2
map on a curve of genus
2
is hyperelliptic.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\epsilon:C\rightarrow F
be a degree two map of smooth curves with
g(C)\geq 5
and
g(F)=2
.
Let
L\in\Pic^{g-3}(F)
,
B\in|L^{2}|
the data determining
\epsilon
.
We have:

(a)

If
g\geq 6
, then
C
is not hyperelliptic.

(b)

If
g=5
and
C
is hyperelliptic, then
L=\omega_{F}
.

Proof.

We follow closely the strategy of proof in
[
Nar92
, Lemma 3.1]
.
Assume that
C
is hyperelliptic and let
D
be an effective divisor on
C
representing the
g^{1}_{2}
.
We write
D=\epsilon^{-1}A_{0}+A_{1}
, where
A_{1}
does not contain fibers of
\epsilon
.
Then, by
[
Mum74
, p. 338]
, there is an exact sequence on
F
as follows:

(4)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F}(A_{0})\rightarrow\epsilon_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{F}(A_{0}+\Nm_{\epsilon}A_{1})\otimes L^{-1}\rightarrow 0.

Since
D
has degree
2
there are two cases: either
A_{0}
is a point and
A_{1}=0
, or
A_{0}=0
and
\deg A_{1}=2
.
In the first case
\deg(\mathcal{O}_{F}(A_{0}+\Nm_{\epsilon}A_{1})\otimes L^{-1})=1-(g-3)=4-g<0
, thus
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(D))=h^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(A_{0}))=1
, a contradiction.
In the second case,
h^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(D))\leq 1+h^{0}(F,\mathcal{O}_{F}(\Nm_{\epsilon}A_{1})\otimes L^{-1})
.
If
g\geq 6
, then
\deg(\mathcal{O}_{F}(\Nm_{\epsilon}A_{1})\otimes L^{-1})=2-(g-3)=5-g<0
and again we have a contradiction.
This proves (
a
).
Assuming
g=5
we obtain
\Nm_{\epsilon}A_{1}=L
.
Moving
A_{1}
in the
g^{1}_{2}
of
C
, we deduce that necessarily
L
is a
g^{1}_{2}
on
F
, so
L=\omega_{F}
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

For
g\geq 6
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{g-2}\cap\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RF}_{g,3})=\emptyset
.
For
g=5
, we have

\dim\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{3}\cap\mathcal{P}_{5}(\mathcal{RF}_{5,3})\right)\leq 5.

Proof.

By
subsection 4.5
, the first statement follows and only the statement for
g=5
has to be proved.
Since
L=\omega_{F}
(the
g^{1}_{2}
of
F
), the divisor
B\in|L^{2}|
moves in a projective space of dimension
2
and
F\in\mathcal{M}_{2}
.
Thus, the intersection depends on, at most,
5
parameters.
∎

Notice that
\dim\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{3}=6
, hence, even in genus
5
, the families are different.
Finally, we prove the following:

Proposition 4.11
.

We have that the closure of the two families
\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2})
and
\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RF}_{g,3})
are different.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction, assume that the closure of the two subvarieties of
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
is the same.
Let
(P,\Xi)
be a generic Prym variety in this family.
Since
(P,\Xi)\in\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2})
, then
\dim\operatorname{Sing}\Xi=g-5
(see
[
Nar92
, § 3]
).
Consider now
P=P(\tilde{C},C)
, with
P\in\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RF}_{g,3})
.
By the classification provided by
[
Bea77
, Theorem 4.10]
, we obtain that:

(a)

either
C
is trigonal,

(b)

or
C
is bielliptic of genus
g\geq 6
,

(c)

or
C
is a genus
5
curve with a theta-characteristic
N
with
2
sections and
h^{0}(\tilde{C},\pi^{*}N)
is even,

(d)

or
C
is a genus
6
smooth plane quintic and
h^{0}(\tilde{C},\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}(1))
is odd.

A crucial observation is that
(P,\Xi)
is not a Jacobian variety.
Indeed, this is a parameter count: the Prym variety
P
contains an elliptic curve and the subvariety

\mathcal{J}^{1}_{g-1}=\{(JN,\Theta_{N})\in\mathcal{J}_{g-1}\mid JN\text{ contains an elliptic curve }\}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g-1}

can be identified with the locus of curves

with a morphism onto an elliptic curve.
This is a countable union of subvarieties of dimension

.
Since
\dim\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2})=2g-3
, we are done.
This excludes case (
a
) in our discussion, since by Recillas’ construction the Prym variety of an unramified double cover of a trigonal curve is always a Jacobian variety.

Moreover, a general curve
C
of genus
g
with a degree
2
map onto a curve of genus
2
is not bielliptic.
Indeed, if
C
were general with a map
C\rightarrow F
,

, then its Jacobian would be isogenous to

and

are simple (by

), so

is not bielliptic.

We consider now the case
g=6
.
We want to see that a general curve of genus
6
with a degree
2
map
\epsilon:C\rightarrow F
onto a curve of genus
2
is not a plane quintic.
Assume that
C
is a plane quintic with an involution
f
with exactly six fixed points.
This is equivalent to the existence of a map
C\rightarrow F
with
g(F)=2
.
The involution leaves invariant
\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)
since
W^{2}_{5}C
consists of exactly one point
and therefore
f
is the restriction of a linear involution of the projective plane, that we still denote by
f
, leaving
C

Thus, there is a line

of pointwise fixed points for
f
and at most another fixed point not belonging to

.
Then
r
intersects

in
5
different points and there is a fixed point

.

Considering coordinates such that
P=[1:0:0]
and

we have that
f([x:y:z])=[x:-y:-z]
.
Since
C
is invariant by
f
, its equation

satisfies that

.
One easily sees that

.
The case
\lambda=1
implies that

divides

, hence we assume that

In this case

is of the form

\sum_{i+j+k=5}A_{ijk}x^{i}y^{j}z^{k}=0,\quad\quad A_{ijk}=0\quad\text{ if }\quad j+k\quad\text{ is even}.

In particular
A_{500}=0
.

\mathbb{P}^{11}
.
Then, up to the action of the elements of

preserving the line

and the point

(which is
4

7
-dimensional.
Since the locus of curves of genus
6
with a map onto a curve of genus

9

The genus
5

Observe that bielliptic curves of genus
5
are excluded in item (
b
) in Beauville’s list.
This is so because they appear implicitly in (

).

,

, then for all

is a semicanonical pencil on

.
This gives

Given a 2-torsion point

[

).
In particular, the Theta divisor
\Xi
is singular, thus it is enough to prove that a generic curve
C
with a degree
2

\epsilon:C\rightarrow F
onto a curve

of genus

Since
C
is not hyperelliptic, it can be embedded in

Note that
f
induces an involution on
\mathbb{P}^{4}

.
The quadrics containing

.
It is well-known that the quadrics of rank

.

(see

).
To finish the proof, it is enough to exhibit a smooth plane quintic invariant by an involution of

onto a genus 2 curve

, and such that

For example, consider

5.
List of potential groups acting on Pryms with smooth quotient

We recall from
[

that if
(P,\Xi)
is a principally polarized abelian variety and

is smooth, then

where
G
acts trivially on
X
, and on
Y
with finitely many fixed points.
Moreover, there exist elliptic curves
E_{1},\ldots,E_{r},F_{1},\ldots,F_{s}
such that

Y\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{r}E_{i}^{n_{i}}\times\prod_{j=1}^{s}F_{j}^{m_{j}}

(5)

G=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\mathbb{Z}/m_{i}\mathbb{Z})^{n_{i}}\rtimes S_{n_{i}}\right]\times\prod_{j=1}^{s}S_{t_{j}+1}

where
m_{i}\in\{2,3,4,6\}
,

,

, and each factor of

acts on the corresponding factor of

in an explicit way.

Proposition 5.1
.

If
(C,\eta)
corresponds to the Prym variety
(P,\Xi)
, and there exists

such that each of its elements comes from the action of an automorphism on
(C,\eta)
and

is smooth, then
G
must be one of the following five groups:

(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{i}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{for }i=1,2,3,\hskip 8.5359pt(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2},\hskip 8.5359pt\left[(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2}\right]\times S_{2}.

Proof.

First, by
section 3
, we have that if
G
possesses an element of order
3
, then the codimension of its fixed point locus in
P
must be at least 3.
However, we see from equation (
5
) and by the action that each factor has, that if either
n_{i}\geq 3
or
t_{j}\geq 2
, then there exists a 3-cycle that fixes a codimension 2 locus.
Therefore, we automatically have that
m_{i}=2
,
n_{i}=2
and
t_{j}=1
.

Moreover, if
\sigma\in G
is a pseudoreflection, then we have that the group
C_{G}(\sigma)
descends to an action on
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma
.
Write

G=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{r}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2}\right]\times\prod_{j=1}^{s}S_{2}.

We will analyze the possibilities for
G
case by case.

If
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{JH}_{g-2}
and
s\neq 0
, we can take a pseudoreflection
\sigma
from one of the factors
S_{2}
, and obtain that
G/\sigma
acts on
JC_{2}
with smooth quotient.
Since the action also fixes the polarization, we get a Jacobian with a smooth quotient.
By
[
ALA22
, Theorem 1.2]
, we have that
g(C_{2})\leq 3
and
G/\sigma
must be isomorphic to either
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
or
S_{3}
(we note that there is a gap in the proof of the theorem that potentially opens the door for the latter two cases to also appear).
Now this implies that necessarily
r=0
, and therefore
s\leq 3
.
On the other hand, if
s=0
, then we can take
\sigma
in one of the
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2}
as one of the elements that multiplies a coordinate by
-1
;
the same analysis gives us that if we take another pseudoreflection that commutes with
\sigma
, then
JC_{2}
has a smooth quotient by this pseudoreflection and therefore
g(C_{2})\leq 3
by
[
ALA22
, Theorem 1.2]
.
In particular, we also obtain that
r=1
, and so
G=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2}
.

If
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RB}_{g,2})
, then the genus of
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma
is
2
.
If
r\geq 1
, let
\sigma
be a pseudoreflection in one of the
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
C_{G}(\sigma)=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\times\left[\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2}\right]\times\prod_{j=1}^{s}S_{2}
.
By looking at Broughton’s list
[
Bro91
]
, the only group that acts in genus 2 and is of this form is
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
.
Therefore, here
r=1
and
s=0
, and so
G=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\rtimes S_{2}
.
If
r=0
, then
G
is commutative and so must act on
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma
.
For the same reason as before, we have that
s\in\{1,2\}
, and so
G=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{s}
.

If
(C,\eta)\in\mathcal{P}_{g}(\mathcal{RF}_{g,3})
, we have that the genus of
\tilde{C}/\iota\sigma
is
3
.
If
r\geq 1
, again, looking at Broughton’s list, we see that
r=1
and
s\in\{0,1\}
.
If
r=0
, then
s\in\{1,2,3\}
by the same list, and the proposition is proved.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

It is not clear if all of these groups actually act on Prym varieties (with geometric origin) with a smooth quotient, and it would indeed be an interesting challenge to see if examples of these groups acting on Prym varieties with smooth quotients can be constructed.
In this article we showed that the groups
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
can appear (see the subsection on
cubic threefolds with Eckardt points
for the latter case).

QCD sum rule study of topped mesons within heavy quark effective theory
Shu-Wei Zhang 1, Xuan Luo 1, Hui-Min Yang 1,2, Hua-Xing Chen 1

Abstract
Motivated by the recent CMS observation of a near-threshold enhancement in top-quark pair production CMS:2025kzt , we investigate a novel class of hadronic systems containing a single top quark: the topped mesons ( t ​ q ¯ t\bar{q} , with q ¯ = u ¯ , d ¯ , s ¯ \bar{q}=\bar{u},\bar{d},\bar{s} ). In contrast to the extensively studied toponium ( t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} ) system—analyzed primarily within perturbative QCD—topped mesons offer a complementary nonperturbative probe of QCD dynamics in the heavy-quark limit. These states are expected to exhibit longer lifetimes and narrower decay widths than toponium, as only a single top quark undergoes weak decay. We employ QCD sum rules within the framework of heavy quark effective theory to study the structure and mass spectrum of ground-state topped mesons. Our analysis predicts masses near 173.1 GeV, approximately 0.5–0.6 GeV above the top-quark pole mass. Compared to singly topped baryons ( t ​ q ​ q tqq , with q = u , d , s q=u,d,s ) studied in Ref. Zhang:2025xxd , topped mesons feature a simpler quark composition and more favorable decay channels, enhancing their potential for both theoretical analysis and experimental discovery.

Beyond toponium, a broader class of hadronic systems containing a single top quark—such as

Although these configurations are unlikely to form true bound states due to the top quark’s fleeting lifetime, they provide a valuable framework for probing heavy-quark dynamics. The study of topped mesons and singly topped baryons can be systematically performed within the framework of heavy quark effective theory (HQET)

;
Manohar:2000dt
. In this approach, the top quark is treated as a static color source, allowing for a systematic classification of the light degrees of freedom. This simplification facilitates the construction of interpolating currents suitable for both QCD sum rule analyses and lattice QCD simulations
Dai:1993kt
;

;

Zhang:2025xxd

In this study we construct and analyze interpolating currents for ground-state topped mesons within the HQET framework. Using QCD sum rules, we explore their properties and find that their predicted masses lie near 173.1 GeV—approximately 0.5–0.6 GeV above the top-quark pole mass.

It is worth emphasizing that topped mesons offer several distinct advantages over their baryonic counterparts:

•

Theoretical simplicity:
The internal structure of ground-state topped mesons is conceptually simpler and more transparent. Only two categories are expected: pseudoscalar mesons (

Production feasibility:
A topped meson involves only a single light antiquark, making it generally easier to produce than a singly topped baryon, which requires two light quarks.

•

Experimental observability:
A topped meson is expected to decay weakly into a
\Upsilon

\Upsilon

II
Results at leading order

In this section we perform leading-order QCD sum rule analyses to investigate the properties of ground-state topped mesons. Before proceeding with the formalism, we briefly outline the notations and conventions adopted throughout this work.

A topped meson is defined as a bound state composed of a heavy top quark and a light antiquark, which may be of up, down, or strange flavor. The lowest-lying topped mesons carry spin-parity quantum numbers
J^{P}=0^{-}
and
1^{-}
, and are organized into a doublet under heavy quark effective theory (HQET). This structure arises in the heavy quark limit, where the spin of the top quark decouples from the system’s dynamics, leaving the light degrees of freedom—characterized by the light quark spin
s_{q}=1/2
—to govern the quantum behavior. Coupling the light component with the heavy quark spin
s_{Q}=1/2
leads to two degenerate states with total angular momentum
J=s_{Q}\otimes s_{q}=0\oplus 1
, forming an HQET doublet.

The relevant interpolating currents are constructed as

\displaystyle J(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{q}^{a}(x)\gamma_{5}h_{v}^{a}(x)\,,

(1)

\displaystyle J_{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{q}^{a}(x)\gamma^{t}_{\mu}h_{v}^{a}(x)\,,

(2)

where
a
denotes the color index. The field
\bar{q}(x)
refers to a light antiquark, such as
\bar{u}(x)
,
\bar{d}(x)
, or
\bar{s}(x)
, while
h_{v}(x)
represents the effective top quark field in HQET. The transverse gamma matrix is defined by
\gamma^{t}_{\mu}=\gamma_{\mu}-v\!\!\!/\penalty\,v_{\mu}
, where
v_{\mu}
is the four-velocity of the heavy quark.

Since
J(x)
and
J_{\mu}(x)
arise from the same HQET doublet, they yield equivalent QCD sum rules
Dai:1993kt
;
Dai:1996yw
;
Liu:2007fg
;
Dai:1996qx
;
Dai:2003yg
;
Yang:2025hzc
. Thus, it is sufficient to work with either current. In this analysis, we focus on the vector current
J_{\mu}(x)
with quark content
t\bar{s}
to investigate the properties of the vector topped meson
T_{s}^{\star}
, which carries quantum numbers
J^{P}=1^{-}
. The corresponding decay constant is defined through the matrix element:

\langle 0|J_{\mu}(x)|T_{s}^{\star}\rangle=f\,\epsilon_{\mu}\,,

(3)

where
f
denotes the decay constant and
\epsilon_{\mu}
is the polarization vector. This leads to the following two-point correlation function:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int d^{4}x\,e^{ik\cdot x}\langle 0|T[J_{\mu}(x)J_{\nu}^{\dagger}(0)]|0\rangle

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\right)\times\Pi(\omega)+\cdots\,,

where
\omega=2v\cdot k
is twice the residual off-shell energy. Here, we project out the Lorentz structure associated with the vector meson contribution, while subleading terms are omitted for brevity.

At the hadronic level, Eq. (
4
) can be rewritten as

\Pi(\omega)=\frac{f^{2}}{2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega}+\text{higher states}\,,

(5)

where

\overline{\Lambda}\equiv\lim_{m_{t}\rightarrow\infty}(m_{T_{s}^{\star}}-m_{t})\,,

(6)

denotes the residual mass of the
T_{s}^{\star}
meson in the heavy quark limit, with
m_{T_{s}^{\star}}
representing its physical mass.

On the QCD side, Eq. (
4
) is evaluated via the operator product expansion (OPE). By substituting the interpolating current defined in Eq. (
2
) and performing a Borel transformation, we arrive at

\displaystyle\Pi(\omega_{c},T)

\displaystyle=f^{2}(\omega_{c},T)\cdot e^{-2\overline{\Lambda}(\omega_{c},T)/T}

\displaystyle=\int_{s_{<}}^{\omega_{c}}\left[\frac{3\omega^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}+\frac{3m_{s}\omega}{8\pi^{2}}-\frac{3m_{s}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\right]e^{-{\omega/T}}\,d\omega

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\langle\bar{s}s\rangle}{2}+\frac{m_{s}\langle\bar{s}s\rangle}{4T}+\frac{\langle g_{s}\bar{s}\sigma Gs\rangle}{8T^{2}}\,.

(7)

Here,
s_{<}=2m_{s}
represents the physical threshold,
\omega_{c}
is the continuum threshold parameter, and
T
denotes the Borel mass. The parameter
\omega_{c}
encodes the quark–hadron duality assumption, wherein contributions from higher excited states and the continuum are modeled by the perturbative portion of the OPE. In practice,
\omega_{c}/2
is selected to lie moderately above the residual mass of the meson under study, thereby ensuring ground-state dominance and reliable convergence of the OPE expansion.

III
Results at the
{\mathcal{O}}(1/m_{Q})
order

In this section we extend our analysis to incorporate
\mathcal{O}(1/m_{Q})
corrections
Dai:1996qx
;
Dai:2003yg
. To this end, we adopt the effective Lagrangian of HQET:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}=\bar{h}_{v}^{a}\,iv\cdot D\,h_{v}^{a}+\frac{1}{2m_{t}}\mathcal{K}+\frac{1}{2m_{t}}\mathcal{S}\,,

(17)

where
\mathcal{K}
is the nonrelativistic kinetic energy operator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\bar{h}_{v}^{a}(iD_{t})^{2}h_{v}^{a}\,,

(18)

and
\mathcal{S}
is the Pauli term responsible for the chromomagnetic interaction:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\frac{g_{s}}{2}C_{\rm mag}\left(\frac{m_{t}}{\mu}\right)\bar{h}_{v}^{a}\sigma_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}h_{v}^{a}\,.

(19)

The corresponding Wilson coefficient is given by

\displaystyle C_{\rm mag}\left(\frac{m_{t}}{\mu}\right)=\left[\frac{\overline{\alpha}_{s}(m_{t})}{\overline{\alpha}_{s}(\mu)}\right]^{3/\beta_{0}}\,,

(20)

where
\beta_{0}=11-\frac{2}{3}n_{f}
. At the renormalization scale
\mu=2~\mathrm{GeV}
, this evaluates to
C_{\rm mag}(\mu)\approx 0.6
for the top quark.

At the hadronic level, the pole term in the correlation function can be expanded to include
\mathcal{O}(1/m_{Q})
corrections:

\displaystyle\Pi(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(f+\delta f)^{2}}{2(\overline{\Lambda}+\delta m)-\omega}

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2}}{2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega}-\frac{2\delta m\,f^{2}}{(2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega)^{2}}+\frac{2f\,\delta f}{2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega}\,,

where
\delta m
and
\delta f
denote the
\mathcal{O}(1/m_{Q})
corrections to the hadron mass
m_{T_{s}^{\star}}
and the decay constant
f
, respectively.

To evaluate the mass correction
\delta m
, we consider the three-point correlation functions:

\displaystyle\delta_{O}\Pi_{\mu\nu}(\omega,\omega^{\prime})

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{2}\int d^{4}x\,d^{4}y\,e^{ik\cdot x-ik^{\prime}\cdot y}\langle 0|T[J_{\mu}(x)\,O(0)\,J_{\nu}^{\dagger}(y)]|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}}\right)\times\delta_{O}\Pi(\omega,\omega^{\prime})+\cdots\,,

where
O=\mathcal{K}
or
\mathcal{S}
. According to the effective Lagrangian in Eq. (
17
), the hadronic representations of these correlators are given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{K}}\Pi(\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{f^{2}K}{(2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega)(2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega^{\prime})}+\cdots\,,

(23)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathcal{S}}\Pi(\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{M}f^{2}\Sigma}{(2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega)(2\overline{\Lambda}-\omega^{\prime})}+\cdots\,,

(24)

with the matrix elements defined as

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\langle T_{s}^{\star}|\bar{h}_{v}^{a}(iD_{\perp})^{2}h_{v}^{a}|T_{s}^{\star}\rangle\,,

(25)

\displaystyle d_{M}\Sigma

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{s}^{\star}\left|\frac{g_{s}}{2}\bar{h}_{v}^{a}\sigma_{\mu\nu}G^{\mu\nu}h_{v}^{a}\right|T_{s}^{\star}\right\rangle\,.

(26)

The spin-dependent coefficient
d_{M}
is given by

\displaystyle d_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{j,j_{l}}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle d_{j_{l}-1/2,j_{l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2j_{l}+2\,,

(28)

\displaystyle d_{j_{l}+1/2,j_{l}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2j_{l}\,.

(29)

By setting
\omega=\omega^{\prime}
and comparing Eqs. (

), (

Discussions and Conclusion

Based on the results obtained in Sec.
II
and Sec.
III

T_{s}^{\star}
. A key aspect of this analysis is the choice of renormalization scheme for the top quark mass. In our previous QCD sum rule studies of charmed and bottom baryons

;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.2730^{+0.0046}_{-0.0046}~\mathrm{GeV}\,,

\displaystyle m_{b}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4.183^{+0.007}_{-0.007}~\mathrm{GeV}\,,

(36)

\displaystyle m_{c}^{\mathrm{pole}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1.67^{+0.07}_{-0.07}~\mathrm{GeV}\,,

(37)

\displaystyle m_{b}^{\mathrm{pole}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4.78^{+0.06}_{-0.06}~\mathrm{GeV}\,.

(38)

However, for the top quark, the discrepancy between the two schemes becomes significantly larger:

\displaystyle m_{t}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 162.5^{+2.1}_{-1.5}~\mathrm{GeV}\,,

This result highlights the robustness of the HQET framework even in the extreme mass regime of the top quark. Nonetheless, the relatively large uncertainty in

In summary, this work presents a pioneering application of QCD sum rules within the HQET framework to investigate mesonic systems containing a top quark. In parallel, singly topped baryons are systematically examined in Ref.

using the same theoretical approach. Although both classes of hadrons are unlikely to form in nature due to the extremely short lifetime of the top quark, their theoretical study provides valuable insights into QCD dynamics in the heavy-quark limit. Topped mesons and singly topped baryons are generally expected to inherit the decay width of the top quark, which is precisely measured as

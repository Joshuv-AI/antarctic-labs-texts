On unbiased estimators for functions of the rate parameter of the exponential distribution
Roberto Vila 1 and Eduardo Yoshio Nakano 1 1 Department of Statistics, University of Brasilia, Brasilia, Brazil

Abstract
In this paper, we explicitly derive unbiased estimators for various functions of the rate parameter of the exponential distribution, including powers of the rate parameter, the q q th quantile, the p p th moment, the survival function, the maximum, minimum, probability density function, mean past lifetime, moment generating function, and others. It is also noteworthy that this work corrects a general formula originally proposed by Tate, R. F. (Ann. Math. Statist., 30(2): 341-366, 1959) for constructing unbiased estimators of functions of the exponential distribution’s rate parameter in the absence of a location parameter. Additionally, we establish a result demonstrating the asymptotic normality of the proposed unbiased estimators.



Keywords.
Exponential distribution, Laplace transform, unbiased estimator.

1
Introduction

An estimator is said to be unbiased when its expected value equals the population parameter it is intended to estimate. The use of unbiased estimators is fundamental in statistical inference, as it ensures, on average, the accuracy of estimates and prevents systematic bias in the results. Among the most commonly employed methods for constructing estimators are the method of moments and maximum likelihood method. The former is particularly advantageous for directly producing estimators of linear transformations of the parameters, while the latter offers greater generality, enabling the derivation of estimators for arbitrary bijective transformations. Moreover, it is well known that when the underlying distribution is a member of the exponential family, the moment estimators and the maximum likelihood estimators (MLEs) coincide
(
Davidson, 1974
)
.

Two well-established properties of MLEs are their invariance under bijective transformations and their asymptotic unbiasedness. These properties facilitate the construction of asymptotically unbiased estimators for any bijective transformation of the parameter of interest. However, it is well-known that the maximum likelihood (ML) method can produce biased estimators. Therefore, even if the MLE is unbiased for a parameter
\lambda
, the MLE of
\xi(\lambda)
may be biased for a transformation
\xi(\lambda)
, unless
\xi(\cdot)
is linear. Therefore, MLEs may not be ideal when the objective is to obtain unbiased estimators of specific functions of the parameter, especially when the sample size is not large enough.

To address this limitation, several authors have proposed alternative methods for deriving unbiased estimators.
Washio et al., 1956
introduced a technique based on the Laplace transform to obtain unbiased estimators of transformations within the exponential family.
Tate, 1959
extended this approach using the Laplace, bilateral Laplace, and Mellin transforms to derive unbiased estimators for distributions involving location and scale parameters. Following this same idea,
Seheult and Quesenberry, 1971
presented formulations of unbiased estimators for some probability density functions.

It is important to note a minor error in the general formula proposed by
Tate, 1959
concerning unbiased estimators for functions of the rate parameter of the exponential distribution in the absence of a location parameter. In this context, the present work aims to correct the formula originally proposed by
Tate, 1959
and to provide unbiased estimators for various transformations of the rate parameter of exponential distribution that were not addressed in the aforementioned studies. Several examples of unbiased estimators for functions of interest in the exponential distribution are provided, along with a result that proof the asymptotic normality of the proposed unbiased estimators.

Preliminaries and the main result

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be a random sample of size
n
from
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
(exponential distribution with rate parameter
\lambda>0
) and let
\xi(\lambda)
be a populational characteristic of
X
, for some borel measurable function
\xi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
.
Some examples of functions
\xi(\lambda)
are given in Table
2
. This work focuses on deriving an unbiased estimator for
\xi(\lambda)
, specifically a random variable
h(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[h(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\right]=\xi(\lambda),

for some measurable function
h:(0,\infty)^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.

As
\mathbb{E}(X)=1/\lambda
, it is natural to consider

\displaystyle h(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=\phi(\overline{X}),

where
\overline{X}=(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is the sample mean, for some borel measurable function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, the problem is reduced to obtaining the function
\phi
in the following equation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\overline{X})\right]=\xi(\lambda).

(1)

The following theorem presents the main result of this work, providing an explicit formula for unbiased estimators
\phi(\overline{X})
for the populational parameter
\xi(\lambda)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
is a random sample of size
n
from
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, then, the following sample function

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

(2)

is an unbiased estimator for
\xi(\lambda)
, where
F(s)=\mathscr{L}\left\{f(x)\right\}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\exp(-sx){\rm d}x
denotes the Laplace transform and
f(x)=\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{F(s)\right\}(x)
is its respective inverse. In the above we are assuming that the inverse of the Laplace transform and the respective improper integral exist.

Proof.

It is well-known that
\overline{X}\sim\text{Gamma}(n,n\lambda)
. Then the equality (
1
) can be written as

\displaystyle{(n\lambda)^{n}\over\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(x)x^{n-1}\exp(-n\lambda x){\rm d}x=\xi(\lambda),

which can be expressed in function of Laplace transform as follows

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}\left\{\phi(x)x^{n-1}\right\}(n\lambda)=\Gamma(n)\,{\displaystyle\xi\left({1\over n}\,(n\lambda)\right)\over(n\lambda)^{n}}=\Gamma(n)\,{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n}}\Bigg|_{s=n\lambda}.

Applying the inverse Laplace transform (if it exists) to both sides gives

\displaystyle\phi(x)x^{n-1}=\Gamma(n)\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n}}\right\}(x)=\Gamma(n)\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{F(s)H(s)\right\}(x),

(3)

where we are adopting the notation
F(s)=1/s^{n}
and
H(s)=\xi\left({s/n}\right)
.

Since

\displaystyle f(x)=\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{F(s)\right\}(x)={x^{n-1}\over\Gamma(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad h(x)=\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{H(s)\right\}(x)=\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(x),

by applying the convolution theorem for the Laplace transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{F(s)H(s)\right\}(x)=(f*h)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}f(x-v)h(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle={1\over\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{x}(x-v)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

(4)

\displaystyle={x^{n-1}\over\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{x\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over x}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v,

(5)

where
x\mapsto(f*h)(x)
denotes the convolution function. By plugging (
5
) in (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{x\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over x}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v.

(6)

Thus, the unbiased estimator
\phi(\overline{X})
takes the form given in (
2
), which completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})={\Gamma(n)\over\overline{X}^{n-1}}\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n}}\right\}(\overline{X}),

where
\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{F(s)\right\}(x)
is the inverse Laplace transform.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from (
3
) by replacing
x
with
\overline{X}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Since
\overline{X}\sim{\rm Gamma}(n,n\lambda)

2.2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi(\overline{X})]

\displaystyle={(n\lambda)^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n}}\right\}(x)\ \exp(-n\lambda x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle={(n\lambda)^{n}}\,\mathscr{L}\left\{\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n}}\right\}(x)\right\}(n\lambda)

\displaystyle=\xi(\lambda).

This reinforces the unbiasedness of the estimator
\phi(\overline{X})
of Corollary
2.2
and Theorem
2.1
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is important to note that the estimator

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(\overline{X})\equiv{\Gamma(n-1)\over\overline{X}^{n-2}}\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n-1}}\right\}(\overline{X}),

which appears in Item (7.4) of
Tate, 1959
(with location parameter
\theta=0
) as an unbiased estimator for
\xi(\lambda)
, was derived erroneously, since defined in this way it is biased.
Indeed, as
\overline{X}\sim{\rm Gamma}(n,n\lambda)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi^{*}(\overline{X})]

\displaystyle={(n\lambda)^{n}\over(n-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n-1}}\right\}(x)\ \exp(-n\lambda x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle={(n\lambda)^{n}\over(n-1)n^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{y}\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n-1}}\right\}\left({y\over n}\right)\ \exp(-\lambda y){{\rm d}y}

\displaystyle={(n\lambda)^{n}\over(n-1)n}\,\mathscr{L}\left\{{y\over n}\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\over s^{n-1}}\right\}\left({y\over n}\right)\right\}(\lambda)

\displaystyle={\lambda^{n}\over(n-1)}\,\left[-{{\rm d}\over{\rm d}\lambda}{\xi(\lambda)\over\lambda^{n-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\begin{cases}=\xi(\lambda),&\text{if}\ \xi(\lambda)=1,\\[5.69046pt]
\neq\xi(\lambda),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where in the second equality we use the change of variable

and in the fourth equality the Frequency-domain derivative of the Laplace transform.

3
Examples of unbiased estimators

In what follows we apply Formula (
2
) in Theorem
2.1
to obtain some unbiased estimators for specific population parameters
\xi(\lambda)
of
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
.

3.1
Rate parameter power

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, consider the following populational characteristic

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=\lambda^{p},

where
p\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[\mathbb{Z}^{-}\cup\{0\}]
is given.

By the linearity of the inverse Laplace transform, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)}\right\}(x)={1\over n^{p}}\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}

\displaystyle\left\{{\displaystyle s^{p}}\right\}(x)={1\over n^{p}\Gamma(-p)}\,{x^{-p-1}},

because
\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{s^{p}\right\}(x)=x^{-p-1}/\Gamma(-p)
.
Hence, from Theorem
2.1
, an unbiased estimator for the power of rate parameter of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle={1\over n^{p}\Gamma(-p)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}{v^{-p-1}}{\rm d}v

\displaystyle={{\rm B}(-p,n)\over n^{p}\Gamma(-p)}\,\overline{X}^{-p}

\displaystyle={\Gamma(n)\over n^{p}\Gamma(n-p)}\,\overline{X}^{-p},

(7)

because
\int_{0}^{x}(1-v/x)^{n-1}v^{-p-1}{\rm d}v={\rm B}(-p,n)x^{-p}
. In the above,
{\rm B}(x,y)=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)/\Gamma(x+y)
is the beta function.

3.2
Quantile

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, the
q
th quantile is given by

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)={-{\dfrac{\ln(1-q)}{\lambda}}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{0<q<1\}}.

By using the unbiased estimator for the rate parameter power (see Subsection
3.1
) with
p=-1
, it is clear that an unbiased estimator for the
q
th quantile of
X
is given by.

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})=-\ln(1-q)\overline{X}\,\mathds{1}_{\{0<q<1\}}.

3.3
Moment

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, the
p
th moment, with
p>-1
known, is given by

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}[X^{p}]={\Gamma(p+1)\over\lambda^{p}}.

Taking
-p
instead of
p
in Formula (
7
), we have that

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})={\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(n)n^{p}\over\Gamma(p+n)}\,\overline{X}^{p}

is an unbiased estimator for the
p
th real moment of
X
.

3.4
Survival function

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, the survival function is given by

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=\mathbb{P}(X>t)=\exp(-\lambda t),

where
t>0
is known.

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)}\right\}(x)=\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\exp\left(-{t\over n}\,s\right)}\right\}(x)=\delta\left(x-{t\over n}\right),

because
\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\exp(-as)\right\}(x)=\delta(x-a)
, where
\delta(x)
is the Dirac delta function. Therefore, from Theorem
2.1
, an unbiased estimator for the survival function of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\delta\left(v-{t\over n}\right){\rm d}v

\displaystyle={\left(1-{t\over n\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant{t\over n}\}},

where in the last line we have used the sifting property (or the sampling property) of Dirac delta function:
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\,\delta(t-T){\rm d}t=f(T)
.

3.5
Maximum

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be
m
independent copies of
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
. The cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
\max\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
is

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=[\mathbb{P}(X\leqslant t)]^{m}=[1-\exp(-\lambda t)]^{m},

where
t>0
is known and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

By Newton binomial theorem the parameter
\xi(\lambda)
can be written as

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k}\exp(-\lambda kt).

Then, by using the unbiased estimator for the survival function of
X
, given in Subsection
3.4
, it is clear that an unbiased estimator for
[\mathbb{P}(X\leqslant t)]^{m}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})=1+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k}{\left(1-{kt\over n\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant{kt\over n}\}}.

3.6
Minimum

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
be
m
independent copies of
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
. The survival function of
\min\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}
is

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=[\mathbb{P}(X>t)]^{m}=\exp(-\lambda mt),

where
t>0
is known and
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

By using the unbiased estimator for the survival function of
X
, given in Subsection
3.4
, it is clear that an unbiased estimator for
[\mathbb{P}(X>t)]^{m}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})={\left(1-{mt\over n\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant{mt\over n}\}}.

3.7
Probability density function

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, the probability density function (PDF) is defined by

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=\lambda\exp(-\lambda t),

where
t>0
is known.

Using the linearity property of the inverse Laplace transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)}\right\}(x)={1\over n}\,\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle s\exp\left(-{t\over n}\,s\right)}\right\}(x)={1\over n}\,\delta^{\prime}\left(x-{t\over n}\right),

because
\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{s\exp(-as)\right\}(x)=\delta^{\prime}\left(x-a\right)
, where
\delta^{\prime}(x)
is the distributional derivative of the Dirac delta function.
Thus, from Theorem
2.1
, an unbiased estimator for the PDF of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle={1\over n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\delta^{\prime}\left(v-{t\over n}\right){\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\left({n-1\over n}\right){1\over\overline{X}}{\left(1-{t\over n\overline{X}}\right)^{n-2}}\,\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant{t\over n}\}},

where in the last equality we have used the sifting property of
\delta^{\prime}(x)
.

3.8
Mean past lifetime

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, the mean past lifetime can be expressed as

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}[t-X|X\leqslant t]={t\over 1-\exp(-\lambda t)}-{1\over\lambda},

where
t>0
is given.

By the linearity of the inverse Laplace transform, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)}\right\}(x)

\displaystyle=t\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{1\over 1-\exp(-{t\over n}\,s)}\right\}(x)-n\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{1\over s}\right\}(x)

\displaystyle=t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta\left(x-{tk\over n}\right)-n,

(8)

because
\mathscr{L}^{-1}\{1/s\}(x)=1
and

\mathscr{L}^{-1}\{{1/[1-\exp(-as)]}\}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta\left(x-{ak}\right)
, where
\delta(x)
is the Dirac delta function.
Thus, from Theorem
2.1
, an unbiased estimator for the mean past lifetime of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle=t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\delta\left(v-{tk\over n}\right){\rm d}v-n\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=t\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1-{tk\over n\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant{tk\over n}\}}-\overline{X},

where the sifting property of the Dirac delta function was applied in the final equality.

3.9
Moment generating function

For
X\sim\exp(\lambda)
, the moment generating function (MGF) is written as

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}[\exp(tX)]={\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-t}},

where
t<\lambda
is given.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\displaystyle\xi\left({s\over n}\right)}\right\}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{{\frac{s}{s-nt}}\right\}(x)=nt\exp(ntx)+\delta(x),

because
\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{s/(s-a)\right\}(x)=a\exp(ax)+\delta(x)
, where
\delta(x)
is the Dirac delta function. Therefore, from Theorem
2.1
, an unbiased estimator for the MGF of
X
can be written as

\displaystyle\phi(\overline{X})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\mathscr{L}^{-1}\left\{\xi\left({s\over n}\right)\right\}(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle=nt\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\exp(ntv){\rm d}v+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{\overline{X}\geqslant v\}}\left(1-{v\over\overline{X}}\right)^{n-1}\delta(v){\rm d}v

\displaystyle={\exp(nt\overline{X})\gamma(n,nt\overline{X})\over(nt\overline{X})^{n-1}}+1,

because
\int_{0}^{x}\left(1-{v/x}\right)^{n-1}\exp(ntv){\rm d}v=x\exp(ntx)\gamma(n,ntx)/(ntx)^{n}
, where
\gamma(s,x)
is the lower incomplete gamma function:
{\int_{0}^{x}t^{s-1}\exp({-t}){\rm d}t}
.
Note that in the last equality above the sifting property of the Dirac delta function was also used.

5
Discussion

In this paper, we explicitly derive unbiased estimators for various functions of the rate parameter of the exponential distribution using the Laplace transform. Since the general formula originally proposed by
Tate, 1959
for constructing unbiased estimators of functions of the rate parameter of the exponential distribution (without a location parameter) contains an error, the present work aimed to correct this formula and, additionally, presented unbiased estimators for various functions of this parameter.

The results presented herein demonstrate that the estimators proposed by
Tate, 1959
for the rate parameter power, the quantile, and the power of the cumulative distribution function (maximum) are, in fact, biased. In this context, we provided a corrected list of these estimators as well as several other unbiased estimators for population quantities of interest, including the
p
-th moment, survival function, minimum, probability density function
(
Seheult and Quesenberry, 1971
, previously presented by)
, mean past lifetime, moment generating function, and expected shortfall. It is worth noting that unbiased estimators for any other function of interest that is a linear combination of the quantities mentioned above can be readily obtained from the estimators provided, such as the variance, hazard function, cumulative hazard function, among others.

We also showed that, under certain existence and integrability conditions, the distribution of the proposed estimators is asymptotically normal. This result is particularly important, as it enables the construction of confidence intervals and the performance of hypothesis tests for large samples. Although the distributions of MLEs are also asymptotically normal, it is worth emphasizing that our estimators have the advantage of being unbiased by construction, whereas MLEs are not necessarily so. This advantage can be particularly relevant in scenarios involving moderately large samples.

It is also important to mention that the proposed technique has limitations. In some cases, the inverse Laplace transform and the corresponding improper integral may not exist, which makes the analytical derivation of an unbiased estimator impossible using this methodology. Nevertheless, in such cases, the inverse (if it exists) can often be obtained numerically. In particular, we can compute the inverse Laplace transform in the R program using the
invlaplace
function from the ”pracma” package
Borchers, 2023
.

Future research may explore the derivation of unbiased estimators for other quantities of interest under different probability distributions. Although this topic has received little attention in recent decades, it may regain popularity due to current advances in computational methods. In situations where asymptotic normality cannot be guaranteed, either due to insufficient sample size and/or violation of the necessary conditions (existence and integrability for the proposed estimators, or regularity conditions in the case of MLEs), it is common to adopt computational techniques based on resampling. In such cases, the use of unbiased estimators, such as those proposed in this work, will help prevent biases in results, thus eliminating the need for subsequent bias corrections.

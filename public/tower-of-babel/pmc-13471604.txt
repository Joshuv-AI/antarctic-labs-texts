Machine learning prediction of surgical approach and clinical heterogeneity in cervical spondylotic myelopathy
Authors: Wenhao Li, Yan Lu, Qie Fan, Shuyu Yao, Jianxun Wei, Lin Tang, Chenxing Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPreoperative planning of the surgical approach for cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) is central to precision medicine. This study used supervised machine learning (SML) to build a predictive model for surgical approach selection in CSM and applied unsupervised machine learning (UML) to explore clinical heterogeneity.MethodsIn this retrospective study, a development cohort of 884 surgically treated patients with CSM was used for model development after exclusion of patients with incomplete perioperative data. Variables showing significant between-group differences were entered into four supervised machine-learning algorithms, including support vector machine (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), random forest (RF), and generalized linear model (GLM), for feature screening and model development. In addition, an exploratory unsupervised clustering analysis was performed in patients undergoing anterior surgery on the basis of perioperative profile variables to characterize clinical heterogeneity. An independent external validation cohort of 200 patients was further used to evaluate the discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility of the final model.ResultsThe final prediction model for surgical approach incorporated four key variables: American Spinal Injury Association impairment scale (ASIA), eosinophils (EOS), total protein (TP), and albumin (Alb), with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.811 in the development cohort. Model scores were significantly correlated with perioperative indicators. UML identified two exploratory subgroups among patients receiving anterior surgery, indicating distinct perioperative profiles. In the external validation cohort, the model achieved an AUC of 0.726 (95% CI 0.615–0.828), with acceptable calibration and potential clinical net benefit on decision curve analysis.ConclusionsASIA, EOS, TP, and Alb were identified as variables associated with surgical approach selection in patients with CSM. The internally derived model showed promising discriminative ability and retained moderate performance in an independent external validation cohort. In addition, exploratory unsupervised clustering suggested the presence of distinct perioperative profiles among patients undergoing anterior surgery. These findings provide preliminary support for the potential transportability of the model, although further prospective multicenter validation remains necessary.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-026-09931-6.

Introduction

Cervical spinal cord compression can lead to neurological symptoms in patients with cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM), often presenting as limb numbness and progressive muscle weakness, which significantly impacts patients’ quality of life [1]. Surgery, which relieves spinal cord compression, serves as an effective treatment for CSM. Anterior cervical surgery facilitates direct decompression of the affected spinal cord, alleviating CSM symptoms [2]. However, this procedure necessitates a high degree of surgical precision, and complications during the operation may result in serious adverse events such as hematoma, respiratory distress, esophageal injury, and paralysis [3]. A precise preoperative evaluation of the patient’s condition, coupled with a meticulously designed surgical plan, can mitigate perioperative complications and enhance personalized precision medicine [4]. Consequently, predicting the appropriate surgical approach for CSM patients and developing predictive models based on these approaches hold significant clinical relevance. These models can evaluate potential perioperative outcomes and aid clinicians in making informed medical decisions. In this context, routinely available laboratory markers may be particularly attractive because they are inexpensive, objective, and may reflect both systemic inflammatory activity and nutritional status, which are relevant to perioperative assessment in spine surgery.

The implementation of precision medicine not only improves healthcare efficiency but also lowers the incidence of complications, establishing it as a leading strategy in contemporary medical practice [5]. Precision medicine necessitates the accurate identification and classification of patients, followed by tailored interventions based on individual requirements [6, 7]. In recent years, advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) technologies have elevated the role of machine learning—a subset of AI—in supporting precision medicine, leading to its widespread application in disease classification and diagnosis [8, 9]. Machine learning has been utilized in the assessment and diagnosis of heart failure patients [10], and another study successfully created a clinical prediction model for primary aldosteronism through machine learning, demonstrating strong predictive performance [11]. Machine learning techniques can be categorized into two primary types: supervised machine learning (SML) and unsupervised machine learning (UML). SML employs large, accurately labeled datasets and iterative algorithms [12], while UML focuses on clustering patients based on their clinical characteristics [13]. The combination of SML and UML facilitates the identification of specific categories and patient groupings, enabling the analysis of traits within similar clusters [7]. This methodology may aid in recognizing new disease subtypes and promoting the advancement of precision medicine.

In this study, clinical data were collected from CSM patients, and SML was employed to develop a predictive model for determining the surgical approach for these patients, identifying independent predictors of surgical approach selection. The correlation between model scores and perioperative indicators in CSM patients was also examined. UML was subsequently utilized to cluster patients who underwent anterior cervical surgery, resulting in the identification of two distinct clusters. The clinical heterogeneity between these two clusters was further analyzed. This study aimed to integrate SML and UML to explore factors associated with surgical approach selection in CSM and to describe potential clinical heterogeneity among patients undergoing anterior surgery. We further evaluated whether the internally derived model score was associated with perioperative indicators. These analyses were intended as exploratory steps toward more individualized perioperative assessment.

Materials and methods

Patients

From June 2012 to June 2023, we retrospectively collected the clinical data of CSM patients who were hospitalized and underwent cervical surgery at The People’s Hospital of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region. A total of 884 patients were enrolled in this study. Figure 1 shows the graphical abstract of this study.

Graphical abstract of the study

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) The patient was diagnosed with cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM). (II) Patients with CSM who underwent anterior-only surgery, posterior-only surgery, or combined anterior-posterior surgery. (III) Symptoms, signs, and imaging manifestations of cervical spinal cord compression were detected in hospitalized patients. (IV) All patients completed the preoperative examination. (V) All operations on CSM patients were successfully completed. The exclusion criteria included the following: (I) Cervical tumor, tuberculosis, or space-occupying lesion of the cervical medulla. (II) In patients with traumatic cervical spinal cord injury. (III) Patients who underwent surgical approaches other than anterior-only surgery, posterior-only surgery, or combined anterior-posterior surgery. (IV) In patients without complete perioperative clinical data. We collected 53 perioperative variables in the clinical data, which were gender, age, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), hypersensitive C-reactive protein (hsCRP), white blood cell (WBC), red blood cell (RBC), hematocrit (HCT), mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH), mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC), platelet (PLT), platelet distribution width (PDW), neutrophil (NEU), neutrophil percentage (NEUP), lymphocyte (LYM), lymphocyte percentage (LYMP), monocyte (MONO), monocyte percentage (MONOP), eosinophils (EOS), eosinophils percentage (EOSP), basophil (BASO), red cell distribution width variance coefficient (RDWCV), plateletcrit (PCT), total bilirubin (TBil), direct bilirubin (DBil), indirect bilirubin (IBil), DBil/IBil, total protein (TP), albumin (Alb), globulin (Glb), Alb/Glb, gamma glutamyltransferase (GGT), total bile acids (TBA), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), AST/ALT, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), Prealbumin (PAB), cholinesterase (ChE), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine (Scr), uric acid (UA), HCO3–, creatinine clearance rate (Ccr), CystatinC (CysC), body mass index (BMI), American Spinal Injury Association impairment scale (ASIA) [14], Japanese Orthopedic Association Scores (JOA), operation time (OT), bleeding volume (BV), blood transfusion (BT), postoperative drainage volume (PDV), hospital stays (HOS). Clinical data are available in the Supplementary Table S1. Laboratory variables were obtained from routine preoperative blood tests performed at admission or during the standard preoperative evaluation before surgery. For each patient, single preoperative values rather than averaged repeated measurements were used in the analysis. Patients with incomplete perioperative clinical data were excluded during cohort construction; therefore, no additional imputation procedure for missing values was performed in the final analytic dataset.

For the supervised-learning analysis, patients were grouped into an anterior-related group, including anterior-only and combined anterior-posterior procedures, and a posterior-only group, comprising patients treated exclusively through a posterior approach. This grouping strategy was based on the clinical question of whether an anterior component was included in the surgical strategy. In routine clinical practice at our center, surgical approach selection was individualized by the treating spine surgeons rather than determined by a fully predefined institutional algorithm. The decision was primarily based on the overall clinical and radiological context, including neurological severity, number and location of compressed levels, ventral versus dorsal compression predominance, cervical alignment, spinal stability, and the anticipated need for adequate decompression. Therefore, although the present study modeled variables associated with surgical approach selection, surgeon judgment remained an important component of real-world decision-making.

This study was authorized by the Ethics Committee of The People’s Hospital of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region. Informed consent forms were signed by the volunteers in this study. In addition, an independent external validation cohort was collected using comparable diagnostic definitions and surgical approach classification criteria.

Statistical analysis of clinical data

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 22.0. Clinical data are presented as either the mean ± SD or the median [P25, P75]. To compare the differences in the clinical data of patients with CSM, the Mann-Whitney U test, Student’s t-test, or chi-square test was used on the basis of the type of data. A significance level of p < 0.05 was set. The nomogram was used to visualize the prediction model. Correlation heatmaps were created using the ‘corrplot’ package to illustrate the correlations among the clinical data in the prediction model. The accuracy of the prediction model was determined by employing ROC curves and calibration curves.

For supervised model development, variables showing significant differences between the anterior-related group and the posterior-only group in the initial univariable analysis were entered into downstream machine-learning procedures as candidate predictors. Because multiple baseline variables were compared in Table 1, false discovery rate (FDR) correction using the Benjamini–Hochberg method was additionally performed for transparency. The final variables used for nomogram construction were defined as the overlap across the four supervised learning algorithms, rather than being determined solely by univariable P-values.

Supervised machine learning algorithms for predictive model construction

Before model development, all variables were reviewed for completeness and consistency. Patients with incomplete perioperative clinical data were excluded according to the predefined exclusion criteria; therefore, no additional imputation procedure was required in the final dataset. Baseline comparisons between the anterior-related group and the posterior-only group were first performed, and variables showing significant between-group differences were entered into the supervised machine-learning algorithms as candidate predictors. The overlapping variables retained across the four supervised learning methods were then used for nomogram construction.

Support vector machine recursive feature elimination

SVM-based feature screening was performed in R to rank candidate variables according to their relative contribution to classification of surgical approach. Variables with higher importance were considered more informative for downstream comparison [15, 16].

XGBoost

XGBoost was implemented in R for feature screening and classification of surgical approach. Candidate variables were entered into the model, and feature importance was extracted according to their relative contribution to model performance. Internal cross-validation was used during model training to assess stability and reduce the risk of overfitting.

Random forest

We used the “randomforest” package in R software to screen clinical data by the random forest algorithm [17]. Random forest(RF) analysis was performed in R to evaluate the relative importance of candidate variables for classification of surgical approach. Variables associated with larger decreases in model performance after permutation were considered more important [18].

Generalized linear model

A generalized linear model (GLM) with a binomial distribution was used as one of the supervised learning methods to classify surgical approach in patients with CSM. Candidate variables were entered into the model, and their relative contributions to classification were evaluated. Model fitting was performed in R using the glm function.

Development of a nomogram-based predictive model

After initial feature screening using the four supervised learning algorithms, the overlapping variables identified across GLM, SVM, RF, and XGBoost were selected as robust candidate predictors and entered into the nomogram model. A prediction model is subsequently constructed using the intersected variables and visualized using a nomogram. Model performance was evaluated in terms of discrimination and calibration [19]. Calibration of the prediction model involved the creation of a visual calibration plot, which compared the predicted and actual surgical approach selection of CSM patients. The discriminative ability of the model was evaluated using the Area under the Curve (AUC) of the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve, which ranges from 0.5 (indicating no discrimination) to 1 (indicating perfect discrimination) [20]. To further assess internal model stability, the final four-variable model was additionally evaluated using 10-fold stratified cross-validation, and the mean AUC with its 95% confidence interval was calculated. Scores from the predictive models were obtained, and Spearman correlation analysis was conducted with perioperative indicators. The prediction model score is determined using the following formula: model score = EOS*-0.7157 + ASIA*0.7447 + TP*0.0256 + Alb*0.0925.

Given the imbalance between the anterior-related group and the posterior-only group in the development cohort, an additional sensitivity analysis was performed for the final four-variable model using inverse-frequency class weighting during model fitting. To better assess classification performance under class imbalance, precision, recall, F1-score, specificity, and balanced accuracy were additionally calculated, with the posterior-only group treated as the positive class.

External validation of the prediction model

An independent external validation cohort comprising 200 patients with CSM was used to assess the transportability of the final prediction model. Baseline characteristics of the external validation cohort are summarized in Table 2. The same core variables retained in the development model, namely ASIA, EOS, TP, and Alb, were collected from the validation dataset. The previously derived model score formula was applied directly to the external cohort without refitting: model score = EOS × -0.7157 + ASIA × 0.7447 + TP × 0.0256 + Alb × 0.0925. Model performance in the external cohort was evaluated in terms of discrimination using ROC analysis, calibration using a calibration plot, and clinical utility using decision curve analysis (DCA).

Unsupervised machine learning for clinical heterogeneity identification

We employed R software (version 4.1.3) to perform an exploratory unsupervised machine-learning analysis in patients who underwent anterior cervical surgery. The perioperative variables used for clustering included JOA improvement rate, operation time, bleeding volume, blood transfusion, postoperative drainage volume, and hospital stay. Before clustering, these variables were standardized using the scale function. The optimal number of clusters (K) was determined using the silhouette coefficient (SC) with the fpc package, and K-means clustering was then applied to partition patients into subgroups with distinct perioperative profiles [5, 21, 22]. The resulting cluster structure was visualized using a cluster scatter plot and radar plot. These six variables were selected because they collectively reflect perioperative burden and early recovery-related profile differences among patients undergoing anterior surgery, and because they were clinically interpretable and available with sufficient completeness in the dataset.

K-means clustering is an unsupervised learning method that partitions observations into K groups by minimizing within-cluster variation while maximizing between-cluster separation. In the present study, the silhouette coefficient was used as the primary metric for selecting the optimal K value. The silhouette coefficient ranges from − 1 to 1, with larger values indicating better clustering quality [23]. Because the clusters were derived directly from these perioperative variables, subsequent between-cluster comparisons were interpreted descriptively as defining characteristics of the cluster structure rather than as independent downstream outcomes.

Clustering quality was primarily assessed using the silhouette coefficient to guide selection of the optimal number of clusters. Formal resampling-based cluster stability analysis was not performed; therefore, the clustering results should be interpreted as exploratory.

Construction of a predictive model of the surgical approach for CSM based on supervised machine learning and its clinical significance

Supervised machine learning algorithms, including GLM, XGBoost, Random Forest, and SVM, are commonly employed for the selection of predictive factors in the analysis of clinical data [24, 25]. In a study by Deng et al., these algorithms were utilized to identify six predictors linked to blood transfusion during total hip arthroplasty (THA), and a model was created to forecast the perioperative necessity for blood transfusion in patients undergoing THA [26]. The effective decompression of the cervical spinal cord is vital for the surgical management of individuals with CSM [27, 28]. Consequently, precise preoperative surgical planning for CSM patients is critical for enhancing care quality and mitigating the risk of adverse complications [29].

In this study, clinical variables that differed between the anterior-related group and the posterior-only group were entered into four supervised learning algorithms, and the four overlapping variables—EOS, ASIA, TP, and Alb—were retained for model construction. The internally derived nomogram showed promising apparent discrimination in the development cohort, with an AUC of 0.811. However, when evaluated using 10-fold stratified cross-validation, the final four-variable model yielded a mean AUC of 0.689 (95% CI 0.624–0.754), suggesting more moderate but more realistic internal performance. Among these variables, ASIA grade is clinically plausible because it directly reflects neurological impairment severity and may therefore be associated with surgical planning. By contrast, eosinophil count, total protein, and albumin are unlikely to directly determine surgical approach selection, but may serve as surrogate markers of systemic inflammatory status, nutritional reserve, and overall physiological condition. In real-world surgical decision-making, these factors may correlate with disease severity, perioperative tolerance, or the broader clinical context in which surgical planning occurs. Importantly, the present retrospective analysis identifies statistical associations rather than causal determinants, and the biological or mechanistic relevance of these factors requires further study.

Importantly, the model was further evaluated in an independent external validation cohort and retained moderate discriminative ability, with an AUC of 0.726. Although this performance was lower than that observed in the development cohort, such attenuation is expected when a model is applied to an independent dataset with a different case mix and clinical decision context. The external validation findings therefore provide preliminary support for the transportability of the model, while also indicating that additional prospective multicenter validation will be necessary before broader clinical application.

This interpretation is also supported by prior literature. In spine surgery, preoperative nutritional markers such as serum albumin, prealbumin, total protein, and albumin/globulin ratio have been associated with postoperative adverse events, and clinical guidelines recommend nutritional assessment using serologic markers before spine surgery [30]. In addition, malnutrition-related indices incorporating albumin have been reported to predict perioperative complications in cervical spine surgery, and a recent nomogram study in CSM also identified albumin as an independent prognostic factor [31]. Therefore, the inclusion of TP and Alb in our model is biologically and clinically plausible, as these variables may reflect nutritional reserve, systemic resilience, and perioperative tolerance. By contrast, direct evidence linking eosinophil count to surgical decision-making in CSM remains limited. In the present study, EOS should therefore be interpreted more cautiously as a surrogate indicator of systemic immune-inflammatory status rather than a disease-specific determinant. This interpretation is biologically plausible because eosinophils are increasingly recognized as regulators of tissue homeostasis and bone biology, although their specific role in CSM requires further investigation. In addition, these laboratory variables may also partly serve as proxies for unmeasured factors, such as frailty, chronic inflammatory burden, nutritional depletion, or surgeon-perceived physiological reserve, rather than acting as direct determinants of surgical strategy.

From a practical perspective, the nomogram may be used as an adjunctive tool during preoperative assessment rather than as a substitute for clinical judgment. By entering the four retained variables (ASIA, EOS, TP, and Alb), clinicians can estimate the relative likelihood that an anterior-related strategy would be selected in a patient with CSM. A higher nomogram score should be interpreted as indicating a greater model-predicted tendency toward an anterior-related approach, whereas the final operative strategy must still be determined in conjunction with imaging findings, surgeon experience, and patient-specific anatomical and clinical considerations.

Unsupervised machine learning: orientation and interpretability

This study utilized unsupervised machine learning (UML), specifically K-means clustering, to categorize patients with CSM undergoing the anterior approach. K-means clustering was chosen for its suitability in this context. The algorithm presents several advantages that align with the objectives of the research. First, it demonstrates computational efficiency and the capability to manage large datasets, which is essential in clinical studies involving extensive data. Second, it is relatively straightforward to implement, rendering it accessible to researchers with limited expertise in machine learning [32].

In the application of unsupervised machine learning, K-means clustering successfully distinguished between the two patient groups, as demonstrated by the radargram (Fig. 7C). In the present study, K-means clustering was used as an exploratory approach to describe heterogeneity in perioperative profiles among patients undergoing anterior surgery. Its main value in this context was descriptive rather than causal. The two-cluster solution suggested that the anterior-surgery cohort was not homogeneous with respect to perioperative characteristics, although the clinical meaning and reproducibility of these subgroups require further validation. This methodological selection corresponds with the research objective of integrating diverse and heterogeneous clinical data without supervision [13].

While K-means exhibits certain advantages, its limitations must be recognized. The approach is influenced by the initial placement of cluster centers, and varying starting points can result in different outcomes [33]. This underscores the importance of thorough preprocessing and initialization, which were meticulously addressed. Furthermore, K-means may struggle with irregularly shaped or non-convex clusters, for which methods like density-based or hierarchical clustering may be more suitable.

The emphasis on UML was deliberate, as it highlights the intrinsic characteristics of the data. This methodology enables the exploration of fundamental features and underscores the diversity of clinical traits that may not be evident through conventional supervised learning techniques [34]. By revealing latent structures within the data, a deeper understanding of patient diversity is achieved, thereby advancing medical research.

In conclusion, K-means clustering was selected as it aligned well with the research objectives and data characteristics. The choice of the most suitable method necessitates careful evaluation of both the goals and the data’s nature. The utilization of UML, as illustrated in this study, provides significant insights into clinical data and has the capacity to reveal hidden patterns. Future investigations may examine alternative clustering methods or deep learning techniques to enhance the understanding of patient diversity and facilitate improved medical decision-making.

Because the clustering variables and the variables used to describe cluster differences were the same, the results should be interpreted as data-driven phenotyping rather than evidence that cluster membership independently predicts those perioperative features.

Heterogeneity of clinical characteristics explored through unsupervised machine learning

To explore heterogeneity within the anterior-surgery cohort, we performed an exploratory unsupervised clustering analysis using six perioperative profile variables. The resulting two-cluster solution indicated that patients could be partitioned into subgroups with different perioperative patterns. However, because these same variables were used to derive the clusters, the observed between-cluster differences should not be interpreted as independent outcome comparisons or as causal consequences of cluster membership [35]. Rather, the clustering analysis should be viewed as a descriptive phenotyping exercise that summarizes variation in perioperative burden and recovery-related features within the anterior-approach population. This distinction is important to avoid circular interpretation of the clustering results.

Clinical significance of the combination of supervised machine learning and unsupervised machine learning

In the present study, the combination of supervised and unsupervised machine learning should be interpreted cautiously. The supervised model provided an internally derived estimate of the association between selected preoperative variables and surgical approach selection, whereas the unsupervised analysis offered an exploratory description of perioperative profile heterogeneity within the anterior-surgery cohort. Together, these two analytical strategies provide complementary perspectives on patient stratification, but they do not establish causal relationships or support direct clinical decision-making without further prospective and multicenter validation, despite the additional support provided by the present external validation cohort.

Specifically, the supervised model identified ASIA, EOS, TP, and Alb as variables associated with surgical approach selection, suggesting that neurological status and broader systemic condition may be relevant to preoperative assessment. In parallel, the unsupervised clustering analysis indicated that patients undergoing anterior surgery were not homogeneous with respect to perioperative profiles. However, because the clusters were derived directly from perioperative variables, the observed differences between clusters should be interpreted as defining features of subgroup structure rather than as independent clinical outcomes caused by cluster membership [36].

Therefore, the combined use of supervised and unsupervised analyses in this study may be useful for exploratory risk stratification and hypothesis generation. Nevertheless, the findings should not be overinterpreted as evidence for immediate clinical implementation [37]. Future multicenter prospective studies with external validation and more clearly separated clustering and outcome frameworks are required before these approaches can be translated into routine practice. More broadly, recent patient-oriented studies have also suggested that although artificial intelligence systems may provide structured and guideline-consistent support, they still have limitations in addressing complex contextual and psychosocial aspects of clinical care. This further supports the view that machine learning-based tools should be regarded as decision-support aids rather than replacements for clinician judgment [38].

Surgeon’s influence and limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was primarily a retrospective single-center model-development study, and selection bias cannot be excluded. Second, although an independent external validation cohort was included and supported the moderate transportability of the model, broader multicenter validation is still lacking, and the generalizability of the model remains to be further established. Third, the retained predictors should be interpreted as associated variables rather than causal determinants of surgical approach selection. Fourth, the unsupervised clustering analysis was based on perioperative variables that were also used to characterize the resulting clusters, which introduces a risk of circular interpretation and limits causal inference regarding clinical heterogeneity. In addition, because the development cohort was imbalanced, we performed a class-weighted sensitivity analysis and additionally reported minority-class performance metrics. The results suggested that, although the overall discrimination remained relatively stable, threshold-based classification performance was influenced by class imbalance. The primary analysis was nevertheless retained on the original cohort to reflect the real-world distribution of surgical strategies in our dataset. Finally, surgeon preference, prior experience, and unmeasured decision-making factors may also have influenced surgical approach selection and perioperative outcomes. Future multicenter prospective studies with larger independent validation cohorts and more clearly separated clustering and outcome frameworks are needed [39]. In addition, the marked size imbalance between the two unsupervised clusters raises the possibility that the smaller cluster may represent a relatively uncommon or partially outlier-enriched subgroup; therefore, these clustering results should be interpreted as exploratory and require further validation in independent datasets.

Conclusion

ASIA, EOS, TP, and Alb were identified as variables associated with surgical approach selection in patients with CSM. The internally derived model showed promising apparent discrimination and retained moderate performance in an independent external validation cohort. In addition, exploratory unsupervised clustering suggested the presence of distinct perioperative profiles among patients undergoing anterior surgery. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory, and further prospective multicenter validation is warranted before broader clinical application.

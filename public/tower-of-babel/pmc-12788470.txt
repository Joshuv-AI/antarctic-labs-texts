Gamification in Pathology: Mode of Improving the Subject Understanding and Academic Performance of Indian Medical Undergraduates
Authors: Garima Anandani, Parth Goswami, Vaishali Bhankhodia, Sagar Dholariya, Vandita Singh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:The integration of basic and clinical sciences in Pathology education for medical undergraduates is a complex endeavor. Implementing a game-based online digital formative assessment tool has the potential to enhance student engagement in the learning process.Aims and Objectives:This research aims to evaluate the impact of gamification on improving the academic performance of MBBS students in the field of Pathology, as well as to assess their perceptions regarding its efficacy.Material and Methods:The research was carried out on second-year MBBS students from the 2022–23 batch at the Department of Pathology, AIIMS Rajkot. This prospective quantitative study aimed to compare the academic performance in Pathology between students who utilized the Kahoot! app and those who did not, with each group consisting of 20 students. An online survey was administered to the 20 Kahoot! users to explore their views on gamification in the subject.Results:The comprehensive academic performance, taking into account all examinations, indicated a distinct benefit for students participating in the gamification group. A study of students’ perspectives on the implementation of Kahoot! as a formative assessment instrument demonstrates that a significant proportion found it to be advantageous for their educational experience (95%) and for deepening their understanding of Pathology (95%). Most users considered Kahoot! to be a valuable tool for assessing higher cognitive abilities.Conclusion:This study suggests a gamified approach to formative assessment in Pathology, laying the groundwork for the implementation of online Kahoot!-based continuous formative assessment frameworks for the Pathology curriculum.

INTRODUCTION

The study of Pathology necessitates ongoing development of both cognitive abilities and practical skills.[1] Medical educators are actively investigating new approaches to enhance learner engagement in a manner that is both effective and efficient. One such approach that has been explored is gamification; however, there are ongoing uncertainties regarding the specific contexts in which gamification may be most beneficial.[23] Frequent formative assessment is important for fostering student learning, as it enables learners to reveal their progress in skill acquisition and to gain insightful feedback and support for ongoing improvement.[4] It is essential to identify strategies that successfully engage and motivate students, while also continuously assessing their performance and striving for enhancements.[5]

Kahoot! is a web-based platform designed for formative assessment, freely available and based on the concepts of game-based learning. It creates a competitive environment through the use of time limits and scoring mechanisms, and it is optimized for mobile devices. Participants can engage with Kahoot! quizzes on any internet-enabled device, regardless of their location. This tool can be utilized in both classroom settings and beyond, facilitating asynchronous practice for individual learners or synchronous competitions among classmates.[6789]

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This prospective, quantitative study was conducted from March 2024 to February 2025 among second-year MBBS students (2022–23 Batch) in the Department of Pathology, AIIMS Rajkot, following approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC No. AIIMS Rajkot/IEC/44/2024). The objective was to compare academic performance between students using the Kahoot! formative assessment tool and those who did not. Participants were selected through simple random sampling using the lottery method, with 40 students enrolled—20 each in the Kahoot! user group (study group) and non-Kahoot! user group (control group). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

In the study group, Kahoot! quizzes were administered weekly after each lecture to reinforce theoretical concepts. These quizzes, consisting of 15–30 multiple-choice or true/false questions with 20–30 second time limits, were delivered through the Kahoot! app using Zoom for coordination. Students accessed the quiz via smartphones, tablets, or personal computers. Each session featured gamified elements like a live leaderboard and announcements of winners to encourage engagement and friendly competition. The interactive nature of Kahoot! was designed to enhance understanding and classroom participation.

Throughout the study, both groups were assessed through routine formative and summative examinations in Pathology. At the end of the academic year, a survey was distributed via Google Forms to students in the Kahoot! group.

Survey and test score data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 28.

RESULTS

The study found that students using the Kahoot! app in Pathology education performed significantly better than nonusers in both theoretical and practical examinations, with a 19.4% overall improvement (Cohen’s d = 1.79, P < 0.001). Gamification enhanced conceptual understanding and practical skills, and reduced score variability, indicating more consistent performance [Tables 1–3].

Student perspectives on Kahoot! gamification

Student feedback was overwhelmingly positive, with 95% reporting better understanding and 100% noting increased engagement. Kahoot! was valued for aiding retention, correcting misconceptions, and boosting confidence and problem-solving skills. However, the lack of answer explanations was identified as a key area for improvement.

DISCUSSION

Medical education increasingly integrates digital tools to enhance traditional learning, with gamification emerging as a promising approach to foster engagement and motivation. Gamification refers to the application of game design elements in nongame contexts to increase user involvement.[23] While still underutilized in medical education, several studies support its benefits. Platforms, such as Kahoot!, Quizizz, and Socrative, enable cost-effective and interactive learning experiences. Research by Bigdeli and Kaufmann highlights gamification’s ability to improve student engagement,[4] while Felszeghy et al. emphasize its design centric nature.[5] Ofori et al. examined Kahoot!’s use in Pathology education and reported positive student reception, though their study was limited by the absence of a control group and a short term evaluation design.[6]

Subsequent investigations, including those by Elkhamisy et al.,[4] found that Kahoot! significantly improved knowledge retention and student satisfaction in Pathology, although some users noted the lack of answer explanations as a drawback.[7] Studies like those by Ofori et al.[6] and Tóth et al.[8] further affirm the role of game based strategies in boosting learning outcomes and reducing underperformance due to disengagement. However, challenges remain, including technical difficulties, time pressure during quizzes, and the platform’s inability to handle complex analytical questions. Thoughtful question timing and iterative implementation are essential to maximize learning benefits. While our findings align with prior research on Kahoot!’s positive impact, the study’s single-institution sample limits the generalizability of its conclusions.

CONCLUSION

The study facilitated an improvement in the cognitive capabilities of the students and maintained their interest in Pathology. It also proposed the integration of gamification into the formative assessment of Pathology, providing a basis for the formulation of online Kahoot!-based continuous formative assessment plans within the Pathology curriculum. This strategy is anticipated to enhance undergraduate engagement and satisfaction in their medical Pathology education.

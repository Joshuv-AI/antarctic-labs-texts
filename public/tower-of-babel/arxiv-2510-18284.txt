Abstract
. We address the question of effectivity for calculation of local Weil functions from the viewpoint of presentations of Cartier divisors. This builds on the approach of Bombieri and Gubler as well as the perspective of our earlier works. Among other features, our approach here gives rise to theoretical effective algorithms for calculating local Weil functions on projective varieties.



1.
Introduction

Working over a base number field
\mathbf{K}
, consider the case of a geometrically integral projective variety
X
embedded in projective
n
-space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbf{K}}
. Recall, that the theory of local Weil functions on
X
play a key role in understanding the distribution and complexity of rational points in
X
. Indeed, as emphasized by Silverman, in
[
8
]
, such functions give a measure of the negative logarithmic
v
-adic distance, for
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
a place of
\mathbf{K}
, of rational points to divisors on and above
X
.

Our purpose here is to address the foundational question of effective calculation of local Weil functions on
X
. In this regard, our main result is Theorem
6.1
. Among other features, this result gives a refined and robust extension to
[
1
, Section 2.2]
.

As one example, our approach here gives rise to theoretical effective algorithms for calculating local Weil functions on
X
. Indeed, as indicated in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, effective calculation of
presentations of Cartier divisors
on
X
, in the sense that we have defined in Definition
3.2
, can be achieved via the theory of graded modules over a polynomial ring.

The more subtle point then is to realize Cartier divisors on
X
as locally free rank
1
modules over the homogeneous coordinate ring. Aside from this, together with sufficient knowledge of defining equations for
X
and solutions thereof, the calculation of local Weil functions becomes entirely effective.

Further, by this perspective, the role of Theorem
6.1
is then to indicate the manner in which the constants that arise upon different choices of such presentations become effective. The results and viewpoint that we offer here can be seen as a starting point to the difficult and foundational question of understanding the role in which defining equations and syzygies of projective varieties govern Diophantine arithmetic questions.

This article is organized in the following way. In Section
2
, we fix conventions and other preliminaries. In Section
3
we discuss the concept of
presentation of Cartier divisors
with placing emphasis on effective aspects. (See Lemma
3.1
and its proof.) We include the proof in order to indicate effective aspects thereof.

In Section
4
, we discuss effective approaches to Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz (see for instance
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
or
[
5
]
). This becomes relevant to the theory that we describe in Sections
5
and
6
.

In Section
5
, we develop a concept of
boundedness
for subsets of rational points and with respect to a given field of definition. This plays an important role in Section
6
which is where we prove our main result (Theorem
6.1
).

2.
Selected conventions and other preliminaries

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a number field with set of places by
M_{\mathbf{K}}
. We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbf{K}}
of
\mathbf{K}
. All finite extension fields
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
are assumed to be contained in
\overline{\mathbf{K}}
.

Our conventions for representatives
|\cdot|_{v}
for
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
follow those of
[
3
, Section 2.1]
. For example, if
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
and
v|p\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}
, then the restriction of
|\cdot|_{v}
to
\mathbb{Q}
is
|\cdot|_{p}^{[\mathbf{K}_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]/[\mathbf{K}:\mathbb{Q}]}
. So, in particular, the product formula holds true with multiplicities equal to one.

Further, if
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
is a finite extension and
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
then choose
w\in M_{\mathbf{F}}
with
w|v
and put

|\cdot|_{v,\mathbf{K}}=|\cdot|_{v,\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}}:=|\mathrm{N}_{\mathbf{F}_{w}/\mathbf{K}_{v}}(\cdot)|_{v}^{\frac{1}{[\mathbf{F}_{w}:\mathbf{K}_{v}]}}\text{.}

Then
|\cdot|_{v,\mathbf{K}}
is an extension of
|\cdot|_{v}
to
\mathbf{F}
.

In what follows, unless stated otherwise, all projective varieties are assumed to be defined over
\mathbf{K}
are also assumed to be geometrically integral. On the other hand, we oftentimes consider Cartier divisors which are defined over some finite extension of
\mathbf{K}
.

3.
Presentations of Cartier divisors

The concept of
presentation of Cartier divisors
, from
[
1
]
and developed further in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, allows for a robust theory of local Weil and height functions. Here, we build on these perspective and place emphasis on effective aspects thereof.

To begin with, we place emphasis on effective aspects in the proof of the following foundational lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(Compare with
[
4
, Exercise II.7.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a geometrically integral projective variety defined over
\mathbf{K}
.
Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
and defined over a finite extension
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
of
\mathbf{K}
. Then
X
admits globally generated line bundles
L
and
M
which defined over
\mathbf{F}
and which are such that

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)\simeq L\otimes M^{-1}\text{.}

Proof.

Fix a very ample line bundle
L
on
X
. By a small abuse of notation denote also by
L
its pullback to
X_{\mathbf{F}}
. Fix a sufficiently large positive integer
m_{0}\gg 0
so that the line bundle

M\simeq L^{\otimes m_{0}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)

is globally generated. For example, take

m_{0}:=\operatorname{reg}_{L}({\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D))

for
\operatorname{reg}_{L}({\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D))
the Castelnouvo-Mumford regularity of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)
with respect to
L
.

Then the line bundle

L^{\otimes m_{0}+1}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)\simeq L\otimes\left(L^{\otimes m_{0}}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)\right)

is very ample and hence globally generated.

Finally, since

L^{m_{0}+1}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)=L\otimes M

it follows that

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)=L^{\otimes-m_{0}}\otimes M

gives a description of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)
as the difference of the globally generated line bundles
M
and
L^{\otimes m_{0}}
.
∎

We recall the concept of
presentation of Cartier divisors
with coefficients in a finite extension of the base number field as defined in
[
3
, Section 2.2]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a geometrically integral projective variety defined over
\mathbf{K}
.
Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
and defined over a finite extension
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
of the base number field
\mathbf{K}
. Denote by
s_{D}
its associated meromorphic section of the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)
.
Choose globally generated line bundles
L
and
M
on
X
which are such that

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)\simeq L\otimes M^{-1}\text{.}

Fix global generating sections
s_{0},\dots,s_{k}
of
L
and
t_{0},\dots,t_{\ell}
of
M
. The data

\mathcal{D}=(s_{D};L,\mathbf{s};M,\mathbf{t})

where

\mathbf{s}=(s_{0},\dots,s_{k})\text{ and }\mathbf{t}=(t_{0},\dots,t_{\ell})

is called a
presentation
of
D
(and defined over
\mathbf{F}
).

Remark 3.3
.

Note that a first step to effectively construct presentations of
D
is to obtain a collection of globally generating sections for
L
and
M
.

Remark 3.4
.

The existence of presentations, in the sense that is defined in Definition
3.2
, is assured by Lemma
3.1
.

As in
[
3
]
, and building on the theory from
[
1
, Section 2.2]
, we consider local Weil functions with respect to presentations of Cartier divisors.

Definition 3.5
.

In the situation of Definition
3.2
, fix a place
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
and denote by
|\cdot|_{v}=|\cdot|_{v,\mathbf{K}}
is associated normalized absolute value. For
x\in X\setminus\operatorname{supp}(D)
, define

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x;|\cdot|_{v}):=\max_{i}\min_{j}\log\left|\frac{s_{i}}{t_{j}s_{D}}(x)\right|_{v}\text{.}

The real valued function
\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x;|\cdot|_{v})
is the
local Weil function
of
D
with respect to the presentation
\mathcal{D}
and the absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}
. By construction, it has domain
X\setminus\operatorname{supp}(D)
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
X
be a geometrically integral projective variety defined over
\mathbf{K}
.
Let
s\in\mathbf{F}(X)^{\times}
be a nonzero rational function on
X
, defined over
\mathbf{F}
, and with Cartier divisor
D:=\operatorname{div}(s)
. Then

{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}

and
s
is a meromorphic section of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)
. Fix
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
. A local Weil function

\lambda_{s}=\lambda_{s}(\cdot;|\cdot|_{v})

relative to
D
is given by the presentation

s=(s;{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}},1;{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}},1)

that is defined by the condition that

\lambda_{s}(x):=-\log|s(x)|_{v}

for
x\not\in\operatorname{supp}(D)=\varnothing
.
If
s^{\prime}
is another nonzero rational function on
X
, then

\lambda_{ss^{\prime}}=\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{s^{\prime}}

and so espeically

\lambda_{s^{-1}}=-\lambda_{s}\text{.}

Example 3.7
.

Let
X
be a geometrically integral projective variety defined over
\mathbf{K}
.
Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be two Cartier divisors on
X
, defined over a common finite extension field
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
with presentations

\mathcal{D}_{i}=(s_{D_{i}};L_{i},\mathbf{s}_{i};M_{i},\mathbf{t}_{i})

and with corresponding local height functions

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}=\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}(\cdot;|\cdot|_{v})\text{ for $v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}$ and $i=1,2$.}

Then

\mathbf{s}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2}:=(s_{1k}\cdot s_{2k^{\prime}})

and

\mathbf{t}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{t}_{2}:=(t_{1\ell}t_{2\ell^{\prime}})

are respective generating global sections of
L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
and
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
. Define

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot):=\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot;|\cdot|_{v})

as the local height that is relative to the presentation

\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathcal{D}_{2}=(s_{D_{1}}s_{D_{2}};L_{1}\otimes L_{2},\mathbf{s}_{1}\mathbf{s}_{2};M_{1}\otimes M_{2},\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2})

of the divisor
D_{1}+D_{2}
. Then

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathcal{D}_{2}}(x)=\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(x)+\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}(x)\text{ for $x\in X\setminus\operatorname{supp}(D_{1})\bigcup\operatorname{supp}(D_{2})$.}

Example 3.8
.

Suppose that
D
is a degree
d
hypersurface in projective
n
-space
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbf{K}}
defined by a degree
d
homogeneous form

s_{D}=s_{D}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbf{F}[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]

and consider the presentation

\mathcal{D}=(s_{D};{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}}(d),(x_{0}^{d},\dots,x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}^{i_{n}},\dots,x_{n}^{d});{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbf{F}}},(1))\text{.}

Then the corresponding local Weil function for
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
has the form

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(x;v):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{i}=(i_{0},\dots,i_{n})\\
|\mathbf{i}|=d\end{subarray}}\log\left|\frac{x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdot\ldots\cdot x_{n}^{i_{n}}}{s_{D}(x)}\right|_{v}\text{
for $x\in\mathbb{P}^{n}$ with $s_{D}(x)\not=0$.
}

Theorem 4.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathbf{K}
be a number field with algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbf{K}}
. Let
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
be a finite extension in
\overline{\mathbf{K}}
. Let
U\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}_{\mathbf{K}}
be a geometrically irreducible affine variety with defining ideal
I_{U}
and affine coordinate ring

\Gamma(U,{\mathcal{O}}_{U})=\mathbf{K}[U]=\mathbf{K}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I_{U}\text{.}

Suppose that

f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}\in\mathbf{F}[U_{\mathbf{F}}]

are non-constant and without common zeros on

U_{\mathbf{F}}:=U\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{K}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf{F}\text{.}

Then there exists effective constants

N_{0}=N_{0}(\deg(f_{1}),\dots,\deg(f_{\ell});\dim U)

and

M_{0}=M_{0}(\deg(f_{1}),\dots,\deg(f_{\ell});\dim U)

together with

g_{1},\dots,g_{\ell}\in\mathbf{F}[U_{\mathbf{F}}]

which are such that

1=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}g_{i}\text{,}

\deg(f_{i}g_{i})\leqslant N_{0}

and such that the
logarithmic sizes
of each of the
g_{i}
are at most equal to
M_{0}
. Here,
\deg(\cdot)
denotes the affine degree of residue classes of polynomials in
\mathbf{F}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Proof.

See
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
.

5.
Preliminaries about bounded subsets with respect to fields of definition

In this section, we discuss the concept of
bounded subsets
of rational points with respect to fields of definition. This plays an important role in the proof of our main result (Theorem
6.1
).

Definition 5.1

(Compare with
[
1
, Definition 2.2.8]
)
.

Let
U
be an geometrically integral affine variety over
\mathbf{K}
. Let
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
be a finite extension field. We say that a subset

E\subset U(\overline{\mathbf{K}})

is
\mathbf{F}
-bounded
, if for all

f\in\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})

the function

|f|_{v}\colon U_{\mathbf{F}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is bounded on
E
.

Lemmas
5.2
and
5.2
below give tools for establishing
\mathbf{F}
-boundedness of such subsets of rational points.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
U
be an affine variety over
\mathbf{K}
. Let
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
be a finite extension field. Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
be
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators of
\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})
. Let
E\subset U(\overline{\mathbf{K}})
. Assume that

\sup_{x\in E}\max_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

Then,
E
is
\mathbf{F}
-bounded. In more precise terms, put

\delta=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $v$ is archimedean}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and write

f=p(f_{1},\dots,f_{N})

for

p=p(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})=\sum_{\mathbf{j}}a_{\mathbf{j}}X^{\mathbf{j}}\in\mathbf{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]\text{.}

Then, if

d:=\operatorname{deg}p(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})

is the degree of
p
, if

\operatorname{supp}(p):=\{X_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot X_{N}^{j_{N}}:a_{j_{1},\dots,j_{N}}\not=0\}

is the support of
p
, and if

|p|_{v}:=\max_{\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{N})}|a_{\mathbf{j}}|_{v}

is the Gauss norm of
p
, it holds true that

\sup_{x\in E}|f(x)|_{v}\leqslant\left(\#\operatorname{supp}(p)\right)^{\delta}\left|p\right|_{v}\max\left(1,\sup_{x\in E}\max_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}\right)^{d}<\infty\text{.}

Proof.

Let

f\in\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})\text{.}

We aim to show that the function

|f|_{v}\colon U_{\mathbf{F}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is bounded on
E
.

To this end, writing

f=p(f_{1},\dots,f_{N})\text{,}

with

p=p(X_{1},\dots,X_{N})\in\mathbf{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]\text{,}

if

d=\operatorname{deg}p

and

\delta=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $v$ is archimedean}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\text{,}\end{cases}

then

(5.1)

|f(x)|_{v}\leqslant(\#\operatorname{supp}(p))^{\delta}|p|_{v}\max(1,\operatorname{max}_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v})^{d}\text{ for each $x\in U_{\mathbf{F}}$.}

On the other hand, by assumption

(5.2)

\sup_{x\in E}\max_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

Thus, upon combining (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), the conclusion is that

\sup_{x\in E}|f(x)|_{v}\leqslant(\#\operatorname{supp}(p))^{\delta}|p|_{v}\max\left(1,\sup_{x\in E}\max_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}\right)^{d}<\infty\text{.}

∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Consider a standard finite affine covering
\{U_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in I}
, defined over a finite extension
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
, of an affine
\mathbf{K}
-variety
U
, where

U_{\ell}=\{x\in U:h_{\ell}\not=0\}\subset U_{\mathbf{F}}

are standard affine open subsets, for regular functions

h_{\ell}\in\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})\text{.}

Suppose that

E\subset U(\overline{\mathbf{K}})

is an
\mathbf{F}
-bounded subset.

Then there are
\mathbf{F}
-bounded subsets

E_{\ell}\subset U_{\ell}(\overline{\mathbf{K}})\text{,}

which are such that

E=\bigcup_{\ell\in I}E_{\ell}\text{.}

In fact, for each
\ell\in I
set

E_{\ell}:=\left\{x\in E:|h_{\ell}(x)|_{v}=\max_{k\in I}|h_{k}(x)|_{v}\right\}\text{.}

Further,
fix a collection of
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators,
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
, for
\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})
and set

f_{N+1}:=1/h_{\ell}\text{.}

Then

\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\left|f_{N+1}(x)\right|_{v}\leqslant(\#I)^{\delta}\sup_{x\in E}\max_{k\in I}|g_{k}(x)|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

and if

f\in\Gamma(U_{\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\ell}})

is written in the form

f=q(f_{1},\dots,f_{N+1})\text{,}

for

q=q(X_{1},\dots,X_{N+1})=\sum_{\mathbf{j}}b_{\mathbf{j}}X^{\mathbf{j}}\in\mathbf{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{N+1}]\text{,}

it follows that

\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}|f(x)|_{v}\leqslant(\#\operatorname{supp}(q))^{\delta}|q|_{v}\max\left(1,\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\max_{j=1,\dots,N+1}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}\right)^{\deg q}\text{.}

Here
|q|_{v}:=\max_{\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{N})}|b_{\mathbf{j}}|_{v}
is the Gauss norm of
q
with respect to
v
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
be a set of
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators for
\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})
.

By assumption, there are finitely many regular functions

h_{\ell}\in\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})\text{,}

for each
\ell\in I
, which are such that

U_{\ell}=\{x\in U:h_{\ell}\not=0\}\text{.}

Since
\{U_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in I}
is an affine open cover of
U_{\mathbf{F}}
, the effective Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz (Theorem
4.1
) implies existence of regular functions

g_{\ell}\in\Gamma(U_{\mathbf{F}},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\mathbf{F}}})

which are such that

(5.3)

\sum_{\ell\in I}g_{\ell}h_{\ell}=1\text{.}

Note, that each of the
g_{\ell}
and
h_{\ell}
, for each
\ell\in I
, can be expressed as polynomials in the
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
.

Also, an effective bound for the degrees and the logarithmic sizes of the
g_{\ell}
can be given in terms of the
h_{\ell}
—recall that the
h_{\ell}
can be written as polynomials in the
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
.

As before, set

\delta=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $v$ is archimedean}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\text{.}\end{cases}

Then, a consequence of the relation (
5.3
) is that

(5.4)

\inf_{x\in E}\max_{\ell}|h_{\ell}(x)|_{v}\geqslant\left(\#I\right)^{-\delta}\left(\sup_{x\in E}\max_{\ell}|g_{\ell}(x)|_{v}\right)^{-1}>0\text{.}

Now, for each
\ell\in I
, put

E_{\ell}:=\left\{x\in E:|h_{\ell}(x)|_{v}=\max_{k\in I}|h_{k}(x)|_{v}\right\}\text{.}

Then

E_{\ell}\subset U(\overline{\mathbf{K}})

and

E=\bigcup_{\ell\in I}E_{\ell}\text{.}

Recall that, by assumption,
E
is
\mathbf{F}
-bounded. In particular,

(5.5)

\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\max_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}\leqslant\sup_{x\in E}\max_{j=1,\dots,N}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

Fix
\ell\in I
. Then, since
f_{1},\dots,f_{N},1/h_{\ell}
are generators for
\Gamma(U_{\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\ell}})
and because of the above Lemma
5.2
together with the inequality (
5.5
), to show that
E_{\ell}
is bounded,
it suffices to show that
|1/h_{\ell}|_{v}
is bounded on
E_{\ell}
.

Now, it follows from (
5.4
) that

\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\left|\frac{1}{h_{\ell}}\right|_{v}\leqslant(\#I)^{\delta}\sup_{x\in E}\max_{k\in I}|g_{k}(x)|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

For the Lemma’s final assertion, recall that for each fixed
\ell\in I
,

f_{1},\dots,f_{N},1/h_{\ell}

are
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators of
\Gamma(U_{\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\ell}})
.

Writing

f_{N+1}=1/h_{\ell}\text{,}

we have shown that

\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\max_{j=1,\dots,N+1}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

So, as in the previous Lemma
5.3
, if

f\in\Gamma(U_{\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\ell}})

is written in the form

f=q(f_{1},\dots,f_{N+1})

for

q=q(X_{1},\dots,X_{N+1})=\sum_{\mathbf{j}}b_{\mathbf{j}}X^{\mathbf{j}}\in\mathbf{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{N+1}]

then

\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}|f(x)|_{v}\leqslant(\#\operatorname{supp}(q))^{\delta}|q|_{v}\max\left(1,\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\max_{j=1,\dots,N+1}|f_{j}(x)|_{v}\right)^{\deg q}\text{.}

∎

6.
Effective calculations of local Weil functions

Let
X
be a geometrically integral projective variety defined over
\mathbf{K}
. Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
and defined over a finite extension
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
of
\mathbf{K}
.

Suppose given two
presentations

\mathcal{D}_{i}=(s_{iD};L_{i},\mathbf{s}_{i}=(s_{ii,0},\dots,s_{i,n_{i}});M_{i},\mathbf{t}_{i}=(t_{i0},\dots,t_{i,m_{i}}))\text{ for $i=1,2$ }

of
D
(and defined over
\mathbf{F}
).

First of all, note that the meromorphic sections
s_{1D}
and
s_{2D}
, of the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathbf{F}}}(D)
, differ by a nonzero scalar multiple. Especially

s_{1D}s_{2D}^{-1}=\alpha\text{
for some $\alpha\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}$.
}

Consider the presentation

\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}-\mathcal{D}_{2}=(s_{1D}s_{2D}^{-1};L_{1}\otimes M_{2},\mathbf{s}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2};M_{1}\otimes L_{2},\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{s}_{2})

of the trivial Cartier divisor
0
.

It then holds true, that for each place

v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot;v)

\displaystyle:=\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}-\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot;v)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(\cdot;v)-\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot;v)

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}=0,\dots,n_{1}\\
\ell_{2}=0,\dots,m_{2}\end{subarray}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}=0,\dots,m_{1}\\
k_{2}=0,\dots,n_{2}\end{subarray}}\log\left|\frac{s_{1{k_{1}}}t_{2{\ell_{2}}}}{t_{1{\ell_{1}}}s_{2{k_{2}}}\alpha}(\cdot)\right|_{v}\text{.}

In particular

(6.1)

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot;v)=\log|\alpha^{-1}|_{v}+\left(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}=0,\dots,n_{1}\\
\ell_{2}=0,\dots,m_{2}\end{subarray}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}=0,\dots,m_{1}\\
k_{2}=0,\dots,n_{2}\end{subarray}}\log\left|\frac{s_{1{k_{1}}}t_{2{\ell_{2}}}}{t_{1{\ell_{1}}}s_{2{k_{2}}}}(\cdot)\right|_{v}\right)\text{.}

We aim to give a more explicit description of the right hand side in the above expression (
6.1
). This is the content of Theorem
6.1
. It gives an improvement to
[
1
, Theorem 2.2.11]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a geometrically integral projective variety defined over
\mathbf{K}
.
Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
and defined over a finite extension
\mathbf{F}/\mathbf{K}
of
\mathbf{K}
.
Fix a place
v\in M_{\mathbf{K}}
and consider two presentations

\mathcal{D}_{i}=(s_{iD};L_{i},\mathbf{s}_{i}=(s_{ii,0},\dots,s_{i,n_{i}});M_{i},\mathbf{t}_{i}=(t_{i0},\dots,t_{i,m_{i}}))\text{ for $i=1,2$ }

of a Cartier divisor
D
on
X
. Then, there exists an effectively computable constant

\mathrm{O}(1)<\infty

which is such that

|\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(\cdot;v)-\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot;v)|\leqslant\mathrm{O}(1)\text{.}

Proof.

The goal is to establish existence of an effective constant

\mathrm{O}(1)<\infty

which is such that

-\mathrm{O}(1)\leqslant\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}}(\cdot;v)-\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot;v)\leqslant\mathrm{O}(1)\text{.}

Recall that

\lambda_{\mathcal{D}}(\cdot;v)=\lambda_{\mathcal{D}_{1}-\mathcal{D}_{2}}(\cdot;v)

where

\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}-\mathcal{D}_{2}=(s_{1D}s_{2D}^{-1};L_{1}\otimes M_{2},\mathbf{s}_{1}\mathbf{t}_{2};M_{1}\otimes L_{2},\mathbf{t}_{1}\mathbf{s}_{2})\text{.}

Thus, in light of (
6.1
),
our goal amounts to establishing existence of an effective constant

\mathrm{O}(1)<\infty

which is such that

(6.2)

-\mathrm{O}(1)\leqslant\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}=0,\dots,n_{1}\\
\ell_{2}=0,\dots,m_{2}\end{subarray}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1}=0,\dots,m_{1}\\
k_{2}=0,\dots,n_{2}\end{subarray}}\log\left|\frac{s_{1{k_{1}}}t_{2{\ell_{2}}}}{t_{1{\ell_{1}}}s_{2{k_{2}}}}(\cdot)\right|_{v}\leqslant\mathrm{O}(1)\text{.}

In establishing (
6.2
), by interchanging the role of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
, it suffices to establish the right most inequality—namely to prove existence of a constant
\mathrm{O}(1)<\infty
which is such that

(6.3)

\max_{k}\min_{\ell}\log\left|\frac{s_{k}}{t_{\ell}}(\cdot)\right|_{v}\leqslant\mathrm{O}(1)\text{.}

Here, in (
6.3
), to reduce subscript notation we put

s_{k}:=s_{1{k_{1}}}t_{2{\ell_{2}}}\text{ for $k=0,\dots,n_{1}m_{2}$}

and

t_{\ell}:=t_{1{\ell_{1}}}s_{2{k_{2}}}\text{ for $\ell=0,\dots,m_{1}n_{2}$.}

To establish the upper bound (
6.3
), choose a closed embedding of
X
into some projective space
\mathbb{P}^{N}

X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mathbf{K}}=\mathbb{P}^{N}_{[x_{0}:\dots:x_{N}]}\text{.}

For each
i=0,\dots,N
, let

U_{i}\subset X_{\mathbf{F}}

be the affine open subset

U_{i}:=\left\{x=[x_{0}:\dots:x_{N}]\in X_{\mathbf{F}}:x_{i}\not=0\right\}\text{.}

Then on the affine open subsets

U_{i\ell}:=\{x\in U_{i}:t_{\ell}(x)\not=0\}

the functions

g_{k\ell}:=s_{k}/t_{\ell}

are regular.

On the other hand, the functions

f_{ij}:=\frac{x_{j}}{x_{i}}\text{,}

for
j=0,\dots,N
, and each fixed
i=0,\dots,N
, generate
\Gamma(U_{i},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i}})
as an
\mathbf{F}
-algebra.

So, define the sets
E_{i}
, for each
i=0,\dots,N
, by the condition that

E_{i}:=\left\{x=[x_{0}:\dots:x_{N}]\in X(\overline{\mathbf{K}}):|x_{i}|_{v}=\max_{j}|x_{j}|_{v}\right\}\text{.}

In particular, by construction, note that if
x\in E_{i}
, then

\max_{j=0,\dots,N}\left|f_{ij}(x)\right|_{v}=1\text{.}

It follows that each of the sets
E_{i}
are
\mathbf{F}
-bounded in
U_{i}
, for
i=0,\dots,N
. Thus, there exists a standard affine covering
\{U_{i\ell}\}
, of each of the sets
U_{i}
, and
\mathbf{F}
-bounded subsets

E_{i\ell}\subset U_{i\ell}

which are such that

E_{i}=\bigcup_{i}E_{i\ell}

and

\sup_{x\in E_{i\ell}}\max_{k}\left|g_{k\ell}(x)\right|_{v}<\infty\text{.}

Thus, since the sets
E_{i\ell}
cover
X(\overline{\mathbf{K}})
, the existence of the desired finite and effectively computable constant
\gamma<\infty
then follows as in Lemma
5.3
.

In more explicit terms, fix
\mathbf{F}
-algebra generators

f_{i0}:=\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}},\dots,\hat{f}_{ii},\dots,f_{iN}:=\frac{x_{N}}{x_{i}},f_{iN+1}:=\frac{1}{h_{i\ell}}

for each
\mathbf{F}
-algebra
\Gamma(U_{i\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i\ell}})
. Here

h_{i\ell}\in\Gamma(U_{\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{\ell}})

is a suitable regular function and the notation

\hat{f}_{ii}

means omit

f_{ii}:=\frac{x_{i}}{x_{i}}=1

in the chosen generating set.

Then, if

g_{k\ell}\in\Gamma(U_{i\ell},{\mathcal{O}}_{U_{i\ell}})

is written in the form

g_{k\ell}=q_{ik\ell}(f_{i0},\dots,\hat{f_{ii}},\dots,f_{iN},f_{iN+1})\text{,}

for

q_{ik\ell}=q_{ik\ell}(X_{1},\dots,X_{N+1})\in\mathbf{F}[X_{1},\dots,X_{N+1}]\text{,}

then

\sup_{x\in E_{i\ell}}\left|g_{k\ell}(x)\right|_{v}\leqslant\\
\left(\#\operatorname{supp}(q_{ik\ell})\right)^{\delta}\left|q_{ik\ell}\right|_{v}\max\left(1,\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\max_{j=0,\dots,\hat{i},\dots,N+1}|f_{ij}(x)|_{v}\right)^{\deg q_{ik\ell}}\text{.}

The above discussion then shows that the constant
\mathrm{O}(1)
that arises in (
6.3
) may be taken to satisfy the inequality

\mathrm{O}(1)\leqslant\\
\max_{k,\ell,i}\log^{+}\left(\left(\#\operatorname{supp}(q_{ik\ell})\right)^{\delta}|q_{ik\ell}|_{v}\max\left(1,\sup_{x\in E_{\ell}}\max_{j=0,\dots,\hat{i},\dots,N+1}|f_{ij}(x)|_{v}\right)^{\deg q_{ik\ell}}\right)\text{.}

The existence and effectivity of the constant
\mathrm{O}(1)
that arises in the conclusion of Theorem
6.1
then follows too because of (
6.2
) and (
6.1
).
∎

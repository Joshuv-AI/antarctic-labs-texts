Uncovering cell-type-specific immunomodulatory variants and molecular phenotypes in COVID-19 using structurally resolved protein networks
Authors: Prabal Chhibbar, Priyamvada Guha Roy, Munesh K. Harioudh, Daniel J. McGrail, Donghui Yang, Harinder Singh, Reinhard Hinterleitner, Yi-Nan Gong, S. Stephen Yi, Nidhi Sahni, Saumendra N. Sarkar, Jishnu Das
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SUMMARYImmunomodulatory variants that lead to the loss or gain of specific protein interactions often manifest only as organismal phenotypes in infectious disease. Here, we propose a network-based approach to integrate genetic variation with a structurally resolved human protein interactome network to prioritize immunomodulatory variants in COVID-19. We find that, in addition to variants that pass genome-wide significance thresholds, variants at the interface of specific protein-protein interactions, even though they do not meet genome-wide thresholds, are equally immunomodulatory. The integration of these variants with single-cell epigenomic and transcriptomic data prioritizes myeloid and T cell subsets as the most affected by these variants across both the peripheral blood and the lung compartments. Of particular interest is a common coding variant that disrupts the OAS1-PRMT6 interaction and affects downstream interferon signaling. Critically, our framework is generalizable across infectious disease contexts and can be used to implicate immunomodulatory variants that do not meet genome-wide significance thresholds.

INTRODUCTION

Understanding the role of host genetic variation in susceptibility to infectious diseases is crucial for designing better therapeutic strategies and vaccine development.1 The striking variability in genetic variants driving differences in the severity of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)2 cases has underscored the significance of uncovering corresponding mechanisms, particularly given the geographical disparities in disease impact.3

Genome-wide association studies (GWASs) have been widely used to uncover genetic variants linked to specific traits. These harness the power of genome-wide analyses to scrutinize differences in allele frequencies between control and affected cohorts, pinpointing variants that may underlie variations in disease risk predicated on distinct genetic backgrounds.4 Nonetheless, GWASs primarily establish correlative relationships. Moving from these correlative findings to causal insights necessitates extensive efforts in fine mapping, which can pinpoint potential causal genes and loci.5 Recent research has underscored the insufficiency of the single causal locus/gene hypothesis for most traits, revealing the intricate interplay of multiple causal genes and loci underpinning these diseases.6

Given the need to evaluate millions of markers, a commonly adopted Bonferroni-adjusted p of 5 × 10−8 is employed to control the false discovery rate, ensuring a family-wise error rate of 0.05 for 1 million independent markers, while accounting for underlying linkage disequilibrium (LD).7 While this rigorous threshold mitigates the risk of false positives, it may also result in missed associations. Addressing this challenge often involves expanding sample sizes, and innovative approaches are key to detect signals at lower cohort sizes.

GWASs primarily rely on univariate statistical analyses, typically reporting only a few variants as putative causal factors for a disease. They often disregard the complex interactions facilitated by epistasis among variants and various molecular entities. Biological networks, including protein-protein interaction (PPI) and gene regulatory networks, have emerged as invaluable sources of functional insights into intricate biological mechanisms governing traits under investigation.8,9 These networks broaden the spectrum of potential targets, evolving from a select number of variants/genes to a network of interconnected genes and gene products, thereby illuminating complex disease mechanisms. Recent methodologies consider global network structures through network propagation, which amplifies biological signals based on the assumption that genes implicated in similar traits interact with one another. This innovative approach unveils modules laden with biological information.10 Furthermore, networks serve as a medium to explore widespread systemic disruptions, as disease-causing variants perturb these intricate networks with distinct perturbation profiles11 or edgotypes.12 Modeling variant effects via edgetic perturbation models can help reconcile complex phenomena and elucidate genotype-to-phenotype relationships.13

Both Mendelian mutations and population variants are more prone to affect specific protein interactions than overall protein stability and function, underscoring the importance of studying the impact of these variants on protein interactions.13–15 While conventional PPI networks provide insights into molecular functions, they have limitations in spatial resolution and cell-type specificity. Given that disease-causing mutations tend to cluster at PPI interfaces, the importance of structural information cannot be overstated. Utilizing a structurally resolved PPI network enhances the capacity to identify causal variants in GWAS findings.16 This has been illustrated in the context of host-pathogen interactions, where a structurally resolved SARS-CoV-2 and human PPI network was used to prioritize population variants associated with G3BP2, an important protein targeted by the virus to suppress the host innate immune system.17

Nonetheless, prioritizing variants is only one facet, as understanding dysregulated mechanisms underpinning disease phenotypes requires a more comprehensive approach. Genetic effects often manifest predominantly in specific cell types pertinent to the trait, necessitating the identification of these relevant cell types for follow-up experiments.18 Several methodologies have been devised to identify disease-relevant cell types using GWAS data.19–21 By integrating cell-type-specific data from single-cell approaches, such as assay for transposase-accesible chromatin (ATAC) and RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), with structurally resolved PPI networks and GWAS variants, we can pinpoint putative causal molecular phenotypes entangled in disease pathogenesis.

Here, we sought to integrate GWAS data with multimodal single-cell datasets to uncover immunomodulatory variants and dysregulated immune cell types in COVID-19. We focused on pinpointing the immunomodulatory impact of these variants in specific immune cell types relative to other immune cell types. In addition to variants that met a genome-wide significance threshold, we focused on variants at the interface of PPIs to prioritize molecular phenotypes for functional follow-up. Through our analysis, we were able to prioritize a previously overlooked variant that perturbs a PPI relevant for maintaining type I interferon responses in COVID-19.

RESULTS

Linking genetic variation to cell-type-specific immunomodulatory molecular phenotypes in severe COVID-19

Human genetic variation plays a crucial role in modulating the immune response to infectious disease.22 Many of these variants may not exhibit any effects in the absence of infection, as their immunomodulatory effect becomes important only in the context of a pathogen (Figure 1A). In contrast to Mendelian diseases, where the variants are causal and have high penetrance, the role of immunomodulatory variants is nuanced and manifest only in the context of certain infections. Pinpointing specific cell types vulnerable to dysregulation by immunomodulatory variants is important for understanding molecular phenotypes that are different between a severe and a mild outcome to the pathogen (Figure 1A).

Here, we leverage the availability of comprehensive genotyping data in COVID-19 and use corresponding disease-associated severe outcomes to demonstrate the applicability of our proposed framework. This framework incorporates data from GWAS and cell-type-specific transcriptomic (single-cell RNA-seq [scRNA-seq]) and epigenomic (single-cell ATAC-seq [scATAC-seq]) profiles to prioritize single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) linked to specific traits. Specifically, we analyzed GWAS data related to genotypes associated with severe respiratory illness and COVID-19 hospitalizations. We identified an initial pool of GWS SNPs passing the genome wide significance threshold (Figure 1B). Further, recognizing the importance of distinguishing between causative and correlative variants influenced by LD, we undertook fine mapping on this GWS SNP set and identified a second set of fine-mapped SNPs. Although filtering at genome-wide significance thresholds and fine mapping minimize false positives, they may lead to a higher type II error rate. Therefore, the filtered-out variants remain of potential functional relevance, particularly in the context of complex diseases with a polygenic nature, where genetic variants often exert small effect sizes (Figure S1). Given that GWS SNPs may not capture all relevant associations, we introduce an alternative network-based approach that takes into account both network topology and protein interaction interface information. This approach aids in prioritizing coding region SNPs that do not meet the stringent genome-wide significance threshold (e.g., p < 5 × 10−8) (Figure 1B). We were motivated by the fact that interface variants can disrupt specific protein interactions while leaving others intact.14,15,23

Moreover, since mutations have varying effects depending on cell type, we developed an innovative approach to score cells based on their association with candidate variants, utilizing scRNA-seq data. By analyzing the functional impact of variants on genes and their localization (e.g., interface or non-interface regions, enhancers, promoters, or introns), we uncovered target genes. The cell types were tested for association with the phenotype based on the proportional coverage of the highly expressed genes specific to the variant set (Figure 1B).

Beyond integrating with scRNA-seq data, we also integrated these variants with scATAC-seq datasets. This was motivated by recent work that demonstrated a dual role for certain genomic regions in encoding proteins and regulating gene expression. These genomic regions are called duons.24 Duonic variants have been functionally characterized in multiple studies with associations to different diseases such as psoriasis and cancer.24,25 Duons have also been found in the plant kingdom, with a crucial role in spatial separation of photosynthetic reactions.26 These genomic regions are quite common, and approximately 15% of genome-wide associated variants fall within these regions.24 Given our focus on variants at interaction interfaces associated with severe COVID-19 outcomes, we investigated their ability to discover disease-associated cell types from chromatin profiles. We creatively adapted SCAVENGE,19 a method that maps variants to functions at single-cell resolution utilizing epigenetic information and network propagation. Importantly, SCAVENGE initially identifies cells enriched for the trait by discovering colocalizations of the variants and the accessible chromatin at single-cell resolution.27

To validate our findings, we compared the cell types identified through the integration of interface variants and chromatin accessibility with those discovered using the GWS variant set. This comparison is crucial, as this highly trait-correlated variant set serves as a positive control, encompassing both coding and non-coding regulatory variants, making it well suited to this analysis. Utilizing relevant single-cell transcriptomic datasets, we aimed to identify dysregulated cell populations resulting from candidate mutations.

Integration of GWS and interface SNPs implicates key cell types in severe COVID-19 disease outcomes

We integrated genetic variants from the COVID-19 Host Genetics Initiative (HGI) dataset into our analysis, focusing on two primary clinical phenotypes associated with COVID-19: severe respiratory disease and hospitalization. Furthermore, the complexity of the immune response to COVID-19, involving intricate coordination among diverse cell types, underscores the need to prioritize candidate variants specific to cell populations. This helps us understand the cellular basis of immunomodulation and identify the cell types impacted by corresponding variants. Therefore, our approach involved combining these genetic variants with scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq datasets to pinpoint cell types that are relevant to severe COVID-19 outcomes. Traditional GWAS analyses report candidate variants that show high levels of association with the trait and are selected at a genome-wide significance threshold. Using this threshold, for the severe respiratory illness and hospitalization outcomes in COVID-19 illness, we identified 2,100 and 4,203 SNPs, respectively (Figure 2A).

We first integrated GWS variants linked to COVID-19 hospitalization with scRNA-seq data from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) derived from both healthy controls and COVID-19 patients. The scRNA-seq data were sourced from a study that profiled the peripheral blood samples of seven hospitalized male patients and six healthy controls. Healthy controls included four male and two female participants. They sequenced 44,721 cells with an average of 3,194 cells per sample. Twenty immune cell types/subtypes were identified in the study.28 Initially, SNPs were mapped to genes, and each SNP-gene pair was given a score using the following criteria: for non-coding SNPs, SNPs were mapped based on tissue-specific annotations from Roadmap Epigenomics consortium and activity-by-contact maps. For each SNP, if it mapped to multiple genes, the cumulative score across all SNP-gene pairs was 1, which was split hierarchically across annotations (promoter > enhancer| intergenic| genic > intronic). For coding SNPs, each SNP was mapped to only one gene, and each gene pair got a score of 1. Our approach is designed to identify cell types that captured the most signal from the high-scoring genes as selected by particular variants. A cell type was ranked high if the effect of the variants through transcriptomic profiles was significantly more pronounced in the patient samples compared to the healthy controls (Figure 2C). For each cell-type, healthy and patient cells were scored based on the SNP-gene scores and gene expression, and each SNP was scored according to the fraction of cells with scores above the 95th percentile. These SNP scores were then used to score the genes to which these SNPs mapped, and these gene scores were used to test for enrichment. Enrichment was defined as the difference in odds between healthy and patient cell types for high-scoring genes for a SNP type compared to a pool of negative control SNPs. In addition, for each cell type, we compared the difference in proportion of high-scoring genes for cells derived from patients with COVID-19 across the different SNP types. Notably, for GWS variants, classical (CD14) monocytes and dendritic cells displayed significant differences between the sample groups (Figure 2D). This is consistent with prior work by us and others indicating that changes in the transcriptomic profiles of classical monocytes can predict inflammation in COVID-19.29–32 In addition, the role of dendritic cells in the innate immune response and their association with severe COVID-19 outcomes have also been demonstrated.33

Next, we focused on a second set of SNPs located at PPI interfaces, which did not meet the genome-wide significance threshold but met a nominal p threshold (p < 0.001). To identify these interface SNPs, we initially expanded our search based on LD and filtered for coding variants at PPI interfaces (Figure 2A). Our interface information was derived from InteractomeInsider,16 a dataset incorporating a machine learning approach to predict interaction interfaces using structural, biophysical, biochemical, and coevolutionary data. We found 36 and 58 interface variants for the severe respiratory illness and hospitalization associated with COVID-19 illness, respectively (Figure 2A). Interestingly, there were only four variants for both phenotypes that were at the interface of PPIs and GWS (Figure 2B). This demonstrates that the interface variants are a functionally orthogonal set from the GWS set. We extended the analysis to prioritize cell types using scRNA-seq datasets, focusing on interface variants associated with COVID-19 hospitalization. Interestingly, interface variants, although fewer in number than GWS variants, showed enrichment for CD14 monocytes, consistent with the results from the GWS SNPs. Notably, they reported a significant effect size for CD8 T cells and non-classical (CD16) monocytes—cell types not showing enrichment in the analysis with GWS SNPs (Figure 2E). This observation aligns with emerging research suggesting the importance of CD8 T cells with distinct exhaustion features in severe COVID-19 cases.34,35 As hospitalization and severe respiratory illness are related aspects of severe outcomes in COVID-19, GWS SNPs associated with the latter also prioritized CD14 monocytes and dendritic cells (Figure S2A). Our investigation into interface variants linked to severe respiratory illness outcomes also revealed the involvement of CD14 monocytes, along with relevant CD4 and CD8 T cell subsets (Figure S2B). In addition, we used scPagwas to rank the cell types based on their association with severe COVID-19 phenotypes. scPagwas integrates coordinated transcriptional features in biological pathways from scRNA-seq data and GWAS summary statistics to prioritize the trait-relevant cell subpopulations.36 scPagwas revealed that CD14 and CD16 monocytes demonstrated significance with severe COVID-19 illness phenotypes. scPagwas reconciled the observations from our analytical pipeline (Figures S3A and S3B). This mirrors our prior observations about interface variants associated with the hospitalization phenotype (Figures S3C and S3D).

Filtering at p < 0.001 consistently gives us a well-balanced signal-to-noise ratio and helps prioritize functionally important interface variants. This is demonstrated through the prioritization of monocytes using scRNA-seq in severe COVID-19, a known positive control. Selection of variants at p < 0.01 led to a noisy SNP set and loss of specificity for monocytes for the severe respiratory illness associated with COVID-19 (Figures S4A and S4B). In contrast, variants selected at p < 0.0001 led to high stringency and an underpowered analysis leading to a loss of signal in almost all cell types along with minimal effect sizes for the monocytes (Figures S4C and S4D). In addition, while variants selected at p < 0.001 contain relevant biological information, there is a need to select the subset of the most functionally relevant SNPs. This is observed as the lack of prioritization of CD14 and CD16 monocytes for the severe respiratory illness (Figure S5A) and hospitalization associated with COVID-19 (Figure S5B). The positive signal for CD14 and CD16 monocytes at par with observations from GWS variants is exclusively attributable to the interface variants selected at p < 0.001.

Given that interface variants prioritized the same cell types as GWS variants in addition to other relevant cell types, we wanted to compare the informativeness of the different sets of variants employed in our study for uncovering immunomodulatory molecular phenotypes and quantify their ability to prioritize cell types. In addition to GWS and interface variants, we looked at variants away from protein interaction interfaces, as well as a size-matched negative control set of variants (based on lack of association with phenotype of interest). To compare the four variant sets, we first scored cells using variant-to-gene mapping and incorporated corresponding gene expression data. Each gene was then scored based on the proportion of high-scoring cells within the dataset. Subsequently, for each SNP type, we defined phenotype relevance as the proportion of high-scoring genes targeted by the SNP pool (Figure 2F). When comparing these SNP scores across the variant pools, we found that interface variants had higher scores than GWS SNPs for CD16 (Figure 2G) and CD14 monocytes (Figure 2H) in the context of hospitalization associated with COVID-19. This trend was also observed for CD16 monocytes and natural killer (NK) cells (Figures 2I and 2J) in the case of severe illness phenotype. In addition, interface and GWS SNPs outperformed all other classes of variants. Our results also remained significant across 20 bootstrap replicates of negative control variants.

Integration of GWS and interface variants with single-cell epigenetic profiles yields key cell types in severe COVID-19 disease outcomes

Duons are regions in the genome that code for amino acids and contain transcription factor recognition sites, potentially serving regulatory functions.24–26,37,38 The presence of these specialized regions opens the avenue for integrating coding variants with scATAC-seq data. Importantly, variants within duons have been associated with regulatory effects, altering mRNA levels, transcription factor binding, and protein structure and function.25 A substantial proportion of disease-associated SNPs identified by GWAS falls within duons.24

To investigate the presence of duonic interface variants and assess whether the information gleaned from regulatory non-coding variants in GWS SNPs complements information from relevant interface variants, we performed analyses to implicate key cell types associated with functional variants using scATAC-seq data. We used SCAVENGE, a method to integrate variant information with single-cell epigenetic profiles through network propagation, for prioritizing important cell types in COVID-19 severe outcomes using GWS and interface variants. Importantly, SCAVENGE uses the differential chromatin accessibility between cells in an scATAC-seq dataset to calculate the enrichment of disease-associated SNPs present in open chromatin regions of each cell. However, colocalization approaches remain uninformative for many cells given the sparsity across single-cell profiles. SCAVENGE explores transitive relationships across the cell-cell network to assign each cell a probability representing the cell’s relevance to the identified phenotype-enriched cells via network propagation. Motivated by considerations similar to those of the scRNA-seq pipeline to select the cell types particularly relevant in the disease group, we ranked cell types based on the number of cells with significant effect of the variants being widely selected from the patient samples compared to the control populations (Figure 3A). To find cells with the largest score differences between the healthy patient populations, we split all the cells into bins based on their degree in the cell-cell network to correct for any topological artifacts. We then ran network propagation 1,000 times with random seed cells (random runs) selected based on the degree distribution of the biologically meaningful seed cells selected following the steps mentioned above. Significance at p < 0.05 was assessed using an empirical null distribution of network propagation scores for the inferred cell-cell communication.

The scATAC-seq dataset had 82,318 PBMCs collected from eight individuals convalescing from COVID-19 as well as 24,997 PBMCs from five healthy donors (three women and two men, 18–55 years of age) and one additional dataset from a public database39 as controls. Fifteen immune cell types/subtypes were identified in the scATAC-seq data.40 For GWS SNPs associated with COVID-19 hospitalization, we discovered classical and non-classical monocytes to show significant differences of variant effects between the sample groups (Figure 3B), consistent with the results from the scRNA-seq analyses and previous literature.29–31 The variants most causal for the traits were selected through fine mapping on GWS SNPs. The fine-mapped variants associated with the hospitalization phenotype ranked the monocyte populations higher. This is consistent with results for the GWS SNPs for the same trait of interest (Figure S6A).

We observed that of all the interface variants filtered at p < 0.001 for the two phenotypes, 13.8% of the severe respiratory illness and 24% of the hospitalization-associated interface variants colocalized with the accessible chromatin in different cell types. This is similar to the proportion reported in a previous study.24

When analyzing interface variants associated with COVID-19 hospitalization using SCAVENGE, we found that CD14 monocytes exhibited the highest odds for the number of enriched cells to belong to the patient group. Furthermore, classical monocytes, memory CD4 T cells, and CD16 monocytes were prioritized as candidate cell types. This approach recapitulated the relevance of classical monocytes and highlighted the importance of memory CD4 T cells, which was missed by GWS SNPs (Figure 3C). This finding aligns with research suggesting a role for cross-reactive memory CD4 T cells in COVID-19 severity, particularly in older populations.41

We repeated the analysis for GWS and interface variants associated with severe respiratory illness in COVID-19. CD14 monocytes consistently ranked at the top for both GWS (Figure 3D) and interface variants (Figure 3E). We selected putative causal SNPs associated with the two phenotypes through fine mapping. From SNPs with p below the genome-wide significance (5 × 10−8) and genome-wide-suggestive (1 × 10−6) thresholds, lead SNPs were defined as those with the smallest p within a 10 kb region. These lead SNPs were used for fine mapping with approximate Bayes factor (ABF)42 with a 1 Mb region flanking the lead SNP as previously described.43 Thereafter, posterior inclusion probability for each SNP was calculated by dividing the ABF of the SNP with the cumulative sum of ABFs for all SNPs within the 2 Mb window around the lead SNP, and only SNPs within the 95% credible set were retained for SCAVENGE. The same result of CD14 monocytes as the top-ranked cell type was observed with causal variants after fine mapping, associated with the severe respiratory illness (Figure S6B). Also, when we compared the number of genes that each set of variants mapped to, there were only six common genes for both phenotypes (Figures S7A and S7B) between the interface and the putative causal variants in the GWS set as determined through fine mapping. Although some cell types are implicated by both GWS and interface SNPs, our results indicate that the interface variants pick up additional biological information missed by the GWS SNPs even after fine mapping.

We repeated the integration of COVID-19-associated scATAC-seq data with interface variants selected at p < 0.01 and p < 0.0001. Variants associated with severe respiratory illness passing the p < 0.01 threshold had non-significant effects for the CD14 and CD16 monocytes (Figure S8A). Significant effects were observed for variants at p < 0.0001 for CD14 and CD16 monocytes (Figure S8B). Although CD14 monocytes were correctly prioritized when all the severe-respiratory-illness-associated SNPs selected at p < 0.001 were integrated with the ATAC-seq information, all the other relevant cell types (T cell subsets, CD16 monocytes, etc.) were not prioritized (Figure S9).

We then calculated trait relevance scores (TRSs) for these variants using a network-propagation-based approach adapted from SCAVENGE. The score assigned to each cell at the end is indicative of the cell’s relevance to the phenotype-enriched cells in the network (Figure 3F). Although this pipeline has been applied to non-coding variants, our rationale for using coding variants with accessible chromatin datasets is rooted in the existence of duonic regions in the genome. Variants within these regions can impact regulation at the genomic level and structural properties at the protein level, potentially perturbing specific interactions.

SCAVENGE chooses initial high-ranking cells known as “seed” cells. Using network propagation, this signal is propagated from the initial seed cells throughout the cell-cell network, and at the stationary state, the final TRS for each single cell represents a scaled and normalized probability distribution reflecting the relevance of a cell to the phenotype (Figure 3F).

In this analysis, we observed that the TRSs were significantly higher for severe-respiratory-illness-associated interface variants compared to corresponding GWS SNPs in the respective runs of network propagation. This was especially true in monocytes and memory CD4 T cells (Figures 3G and 3H). Interestingly, while the number of interface variants was significantly fewer than the number of GWS SNPs, and the univariate p value threshold for selecting the interface variants was less stringent, interface variants had comparable or higher scores (relative to GWS variants), underscoring their importance and specificity. Furthermore, the scores of cells from scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq reflect importance at orthogonal levels. The scores from the scRNA-seq data reflect functional specificity of the interface variants in targeting corresponding genes at the transcriptomic level. In addition, using scATAC-seq, higher or comparable scores for cells in particular relevant cell types from the network propagation guided by the interface variants compared to GWS variants indicate that the interface variant enriched cells are closer to the individual cells from the relevant cell types when the global topology of the cell-cell network is taken into account. The property of functionally similar entities to have shorter path lengths in the network is well known.44 Therefore, interface variants can facilitate meaningful data integration across genotype and cellular space more effectively than GWS SNPs with additional information from the human interactome.

Interface SNPs implicate relevant COVID-19-associated cell types from COVID-19 broncheoalveolar lavage samples

While transcriptomic profiles of PBMCs are predictive of outcomes in COVID-19, recent research highlights the importance of understanding distinct clinical features in patients who develop severe pneumonia and acute severe respiratory distress syndrome associated with SARS-CoV-2 infection.45 These differences in immune responses in the infected alveolus, as opposed to other types of pneumonia, underscore the need to investigate candidate tissue-resident immune cell types that may be dysregulated in the upper respiratory system, providing a comprehensive view of pan-tissue cellular dysregulation driving severe COVID-19 outcomes. Thus, we also sought to leverage scRNA-seq data (Figure 4A) from bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) samples from COVID-19 patients and patients with known or suspected pneumonia to elucidate the perturbation of cellular signaling circuitry underlying SARS-CoV-2-associated pneumonia.32 The scRNA-seq was performed on 77,146 cells isolated from the BAL fluid collected from 10 patients with severe SARS-CoV-2 pneumonia.32

Initially, we identified high-scoring cells using GWS variants associated with severe respiratory illness due to COVID-19. By calculating the odds of the variants of interest having the highest proportion of high-scoring genes compared to a negative control set, stratified by patient status, we ranked each cell type in the BAL dataset based on differences in the odds ratio between samples. GWS variants were primarily associated with CD8 cytotoxic T cells and monocyte-derived alveolar macrophages (MoAMs) (Figure 4B). This aligns with findings from prior work,32 which reported a persistent enrichment of T cells and monocytes in patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection, as well as infection of alveolar macrophages by SARS-CoV-2, leading to T cell chemoattraction and a positive feedback loop driving persistent alveolar inflammation. We also repeated the analysis with interface variants associated with severe respiratory illness due to COVID-19. Consistent with our previous analyses, the interface variants were associated with molecular phenotypes in MoAMs (Figure 4C). The results from the interface variants in a different tissue context are consistent and maintain the prioritization of monocytes as well as associated cell subsets as observed in PBMC samples.

We also explored which cell types were implicated by interface and GWS variants associated with hospitalization due to COVID-19. For the analysis done with GWS variants, we observed high effect sizes for the mixed myeloid cells and tissue-resident alveolar macrophage subset 2 (TRAM2) (Figure 4D). The top-ranked cell types for interface variants were MoAM subset 1 (MoAM1), TRAM2, mixed myeloid, and MoAM4 (Figure 4E). Importantly, we observed the prioritization of TRAM2 by the orthogonal sets of variants, a subset infected by SARS-CoV-2, undergoing transcriptional remodeling, including higher expression of interferon-response genes compared to the non-infected tissue-resident macrophage subsets (TRAM1). This demonstrates that both interface variants and GWS SNPs independently prioritize disease-relevant cell types.

For severe respiratory illness associated with COVID-19, interface and GWS variants had similar proportions of high-scoring SNPs for CD8 T cells (Figure 4F). Similarly, for MoAM1 and MoAM4, interface variants showed higher rank orders and comparable effect sizes compared to the GWS variants. The scores for the interface and the GWS SNPs were very comparable and the highest among the four classes of the variants (Figures 4G and 4H). For the hospitalization outcome, we saw high rank orders and higher scores in the analysis with interface variants for the same cell types reported with GWS variants as well, in concordance with the observed top-ranking cell types. For the mixed myeloid cell subset, MoAM1, and TRAM2 cell types, we report higher corresponding scores for interface variants compared to the GWS set (Figures 4I–4K).

A candidate interface variant elucidates a novel immunomodulatory role for OAS1 through its interaction with PRMT6

We hypothesized that high-scoring SNPs targeting specific interactions in particular cell types in the scRNA-seq dataset will be important functional candidates. This was reflected in the list of candidates across the PBMCs and the BAL samples. Interestingly, several of our hits have already been functionally validated, confirming the biological relevance of our findings. These include ITGAX, an important protein for monocyte trafficking across the vessel wall and whose increased expression has been observed in extravasation cascade associated with COVID-19 severity.46 Another interesting candidate was the DUSP6 protein, where macrophages infected with Leishmania major lead to an increase in the CD40 marker with an increase in DUSP6 expression, and overexpression of DUSP6 led to decreased expression of the pathogen in macrophages47 (Figure S10).

However, we sought to move beyond previously validated candidates to novel hits that have not been characterized. OAS1 is a known antiviral gene induced by the type I interferon response. It binds to double-stranded RNA and initiates a signaling cascade that activates the RNAse L enzyme, leading to the degradation of the foreign RNA. Variants for OAS1 have also been implicated in severe outcomes of COVID-19. Previous studies have linked severe presentation of COVID-19 illness to the scarcity of functional OAS1.48–50 This has been illustrated through a splicing variant of OAS1 that leads to aberrant spliced isoforms, incapable of localizing to specialized endomembrane organelles utilized by the virus for its transmission.50 We follow up these studies by prioritizing an overlooked variant, rs1131454, which causes a Gly162Ser substitution in OAS1. This substitution is localized at the binding interface of OAS1 and PRMT6 (Figure 5A). Moreover, this variant failed to cross the genome-wide significance threshold and would have been missed in a conventional analysis.

To investigate the impact of the rs1131454 variant, we first conducted colocalization experiments with OAS1 and found normal localization in endomembrane organelles along with the generation of the full-length isoform (Figure 5B). This suggests that the effect of the variant is not due to impaired colocalization but may be linked to an interaction-specific mechanism. Prior work14 examined the rs1131454 variant and found no disruption in the interactions between OAS1 and EXOC5 or OAS1 and TRIM27. This aligns with expectation, as variants away from the interaction interface are less likely to perturb corresponding interactions compared to interface variants. OAS1 also interacts with GOLGA2 and COG6, proteins associated with the Golgi complex. While we lack structural information for these interactions to assess variant localization, we do not observe impaired OAS1 (Ser162) localization to the Golgi complex (Figure 5B).

Overall, we hypothesized that this variant specifically perturbs the OAS1-PRMT6 interaction. PRMT6 is an arginine methyltransferase involved in transcriptional regulation, and its canonical function is regulation of viral replication in multiple infectious diseases, including HIV.51–53 However, it has been shown that the methyltransferase activity of PRMT1, but not PRMT6, regulates viral replication in COVID-19.54 PRMT6 also has a non-canonical non-methyltransferase activity where it acts as a negative regulator of the type I interferon response via suppression of the TBK1-IRF3 axis.55

To test the role of PRMTs in the regulation of the interferon alpha response and associated immune system pathways, we performed targeted proteomics using the reverse-phase protein array (RPPA) in a panel of five cell lines to determine which gene sets show a significant change in the protein abundance after being treated with a PRMT inhibitor (Figure 5C). The proteins show widespread variability downstream of the PRMTi assay across the cell lines (Figure 5D). From both Hallmark and KEGG gene sets, in concordance with the established role of these genes in the interferon alpha response, we observed a significant change in their protein abundance following PRMTi treatment (Figures 5E and S11). An additional observation was that the RIG-I signaling pathway showed a nominal significance of differential protein expression in the list of candidate pathways. The OASL protein is functionally different from the other OAS proteins and employs the RIG-I pathway to sense viral nucleic acid sequences.56

Based on this, we postulate that the PRMT6-OAS1 interaction and the type I interferon response are linked. We investigated whether the candidate variant potentially dysregulates the interferon alpha response through a disruption of the PPI. First, we confirmed the expression of an interferon-induced expression of OAS1 in THP1, a monocyte cell line. PRMT6 was abundant independent of the interferon signal. For testing the PPIs through a coimmunoprecipitation assay, we chose the HT1080 OAS1-KO and expressed OAS1 (Gly162) and OAS1 (Ser162) at endogenous levels. Consistent with expectation, the OAS1 Gly162 variant interacted with PRMT6, but this interaction was weakened in the OAS1 Ser162 variant (Figure 5F). Prior work55 has shown that the interaction of PRMT6 with IRF3 can block the formation of the TBK1-IRF3 signaling complex essential for an effective interferon response. PRMT6 tightly regulates the interferon response and is a check on a hyperactive immune system, which can cause a dysregulated interferon response and corresponding inflammatory phenotypes in severe COVID-19. Our results suggest that the successful attenuation of the type I interferon response in COVID-19 is achieved through its interaction with OAS1, and in the event of a disruption caused by the risk allele, PRMT6 does not inhibit the interferon response, leading to a hyperactive interferon response and corresponding acute inflammation (Figure 5G).

DISCUSSION

We proposed a framework for uncovering immunomodulatory variants and corresponding molecular phenotypes in infectious diseases through the integration of genetic data with multiomic single-cell datasets. While variants selected at genome-wide significance thresholds are highly correlated with the relevant trait, some functionally relevant SNPs might not reach the stringent genome-wide signficance threshold. We selected two sets of variants from GWASs for severe outcome phenotypes. The first set achieved genome-wide significance and included both coding and non-coding variants. For the second set, we prioritized variants that did not reach genome-wide significance but were at the interface of PPIs. The rationale for focusing on the interface comes from prior work that demonstrates that both Mendelian variants and SNPs across the allele frequency spectrum14,15,23,57 are more likely to disrupt corresponding interactions than those away from the interface. While statistically rigorous techniques for fine mapping can converge on causal genes/loci, they cannot provide testable hypotheses to delineate the mechanism through which a variant presents its effects. In contrast, variants selected through our structurally resolved interactome provide testable hypotheses regarding the disruption of specific interactions.

A major challenge is adding cellular context to SNPs from GWASs. Recently, methods have been developed that can map variants to disease-relevant scATAC- and scRNA-seq datasets.19,20,58 But these approaches are specifically meant for GWS or fine-mapped SNPs. In line with recent reports,29–32 GWS SNPs integrated with scATAC- and scRNA-seq datasets were able to prioritize classical monocytes as key in the pathogenesis of severe COVID-19. These monocytes have been associated with acute respiratory distress syndrome in COVID-19,29 and a coculture of monocytes with infected epithelia showed SARS-CoV-2-specific upregulation of inflammatory markers in the monocytes.30 It has also been demonstrated that Fc-gammaR-mediated uptake of the antibody-coated virus into monocytes led to pyroptosis to abort the viral infection, but the subsequent inflammatory mediators released from pyroptotic monocytes can cause a cytokine storm.31 Interestingly, a separate investigation of the same datasets with a much smaller and targeted pool of interface variants reported monocyte populations to be significantly enriched for the severe traits in COVID-19. Notably, interface SNPs identified the roles of immunomodulatory variants in T cells, which was missed by GWS variants. Longitudinal studies have indicated that patients with severe COVID-19 outcomes have a lower proportion of antigens specific to the N viral protein, which persists in T cell populations even 12 months after the initial infection.59 In addition, sustained anti-S antibody responses with an increased proportion of circulating T follicular helper cells, a subset of memory CD4 T cells, underline the importance of early disease control in predicting favorable long-term adaptive immunity.60

Our framework is generalizable across contexts depending on the disease. We identified both shared and different cell types from PBMC and BAL samples. In BAL samples obtained from COVID-19 patients, we were able to prioritize relevant cellular subsets, such as MoAMs and T cells, along with TRAMs. SARS-CoV-2 can infect TRAMs, leading to an activation of the memory T cells to the alveolar space. Eventually, the TRAMs die and the MoAMs are recruited, leading to an inflammatory cycle as these MoAMS get infected.32 In the case of variants associated with hospitalization, interface variants had higher effect sizes for the monocyte and myeloid cell populations over the GWS variants.

Interface variants provide highly specific, testable hypotheses involving relevant PPIs. While conventional GWAS analyses identify putative causal loci for a phenotype, they often fall short in providing sufficient functional context to understand the mechanism of variant pathogenicity. We also find that interface variants can have equal or more profound effects in disease-relevant cell types compared with GWS variants. Thus, our prioritization technique identifies key candidate variants and corresponding testable molecular mechanistic hypotheses. For instance, we identified a common missense (minor allele frequency [MAF] ~40%) interface mutation on OAS1, which does not meet the GWS threshold and would be filtered out in a traditional analysis of the GWAS. However, this variant is immunomodulatory via the alteration of the TBK1-IRF3 axis and downstream interferon signaling.

Using a similar approach has helped uncover new disease biology in varied disease contexts. c-Myc’s protein expression and stability are crucial for the leukemia initiating cell (LIC) population; the protein FBXW7, as part of the Skp1-Cul1-Fbox complex, binds and degrades the c-Myc transcription factor. The R456C missense mutation in FBXW7 disrupts this interaction, leading to increased c-Myc protein stability and augmenting the LIC population.61 Mutations in the protein ATXN1 are highly associated with ataxia. The mutant ATXN1 can preferentially bind to RBM17, a known promoter of neurotoxicity. Moreover, there is a loss of interaction with the protein Capicua resulting in reduced neuroprotection.62 These interactions can be further studied for their therapeutic potential as drug targets. This has been illustrated in HIV illness, where the discovery of a population variant in the membrane protein CCR5 that conferred resistance to HIV by perturbing the interaction of CCR5 with the viral envelope protein led to the development of maraviroc. The drug functions by blocking the specific interface for this interaction.63,64

While we applied our methodology in the context of COVID-19-associated disease outcomes, our pipeline holds promise for generalization to diverse disease contexts. The specific significance thresholds identified here for interface variants are dataset specific; however, the same framework and corresponding sensitivity analyses can be used to select appropriate significance thresholds in different contexts. Future applications may involve uncovering immunomodulatory molecular phenotypes across infectious disease contexts.

Limitations of the study

Here, we have focused on identifying a subset of coding variants at the interface of PPIs that are critically important despite not meeting genome-wide significance thresholds. However, this is not a comprehensive list—there are other pools of important variants, including non-coding variants that do not reach genome-wide significance thresholds. Other functional annotations of coding variants can also be used for selection of disease-associated mutations to check for immunomodulatory mechanisms alternative to the ones discussed in the study. Further, while our analyses identify a significantly enriched pool of immunomodulatory variants with key phenotypes, the significance of each identified variant needs further functional characterization.

METHOD DETAILS

Selection of GWS and interface variants from HGI

We used deep genotyping data from HGI (v6) datasets generated through genome wide association studies for severe respiratory disease (A2) and hospitalization (B2) associated with COVID-19 illness.66 To define genome wide significant SNPs, we selected the SNPs that had a p of trait association < 5 × 10−8. To select interface variants, we used p < 0.001. To further expand the SNP set, we added all the variants in moderate to strong LD (r2 >= 0.6) to the selected SNPs. The LD structure was obtained from the 1000 Genomes phase 3 project.67 InteractomeInsider (http://interactomeinsider.yulab.org/) makes three dimensional structurally resolved protein-protein interactions for humans widely available. For the selection of the interface SNPs, we mapped the SNPs to the specific amino acid position and substitution using the web tool bisque (http://bisque.yulab.org/). The coding variants were then integrated with the structurally resolved interactome, and the interface variants were selected based on the mutations which colocalised at interaction interfaces. The coding variants which passed the nominal P and are away from the interface made the not interface variant set. The negative control set for was selected at p > 0.1. For additional sensitivity analysis, we selected variants with a p-value threshold of 0.01 and 0.0001.

Reverse phase protein array

Cells were harvested after 5 days with PRMTi treatment (EPZ015666, 5 µM) for 5 days. Cell lysate was collected by RIPA buffer with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Roche) and then mixed with 4xSDS buffer. All samples were boiled for 5 minutes and stored at −80°C for submission to the RPPA Core Facility at MD Anderson Cancer Center. Samples were analyzed in duplicate. The dataset generated from the assay was quantified as logarithm of fold change per protein per cell line. We used the HALLMARK gene sets and the KEGG gene sets from MsigDB and for each gene set created a size matched random gene set a thousand times. We compared the average mean of absolute log (fold change) of the HALLMARK and KEGG gene sets to the random gene sets, where P was empirically calculated based on the number of times a random gene set had a higher mean log (fold change). We used p < 0.05 as our threshold of significance.

QUANTIFICATION AND STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

Running SCAVENGE with GWS and interface variants

We used scATAC-seq dataset from the PBMCs of patients convalescing from COVID-19.40 SCAVENGE19 uses a mutual kNN graph on a reduced dimension representation of the original feature by cell matrix to create a cell-cell network. SCAVENGE relies on a few cells in the top ranks evaluated by the colocalization approaches known as the seed cells. It employs g-chromVAR27 to calculate a bias-corrected Z-score for each cell. Originally, it employs the posterior probability of the genetic variants from a fine-mapping step with the strength of their chromatin accessibility in specific cells in the scATAC-seq dataset to select the seed cells. For the interface SNPs we give all the SNPs equal scores and the seed cells are decided mostly by the strength of the accessible chromatin. This step is done for genome-wide significant SNPs as well as for a third set of SNPs selected through fine mapping. To overcome the sparsity of the single cell ATAC-seq dataset, the high scoring seed cells are used as priors for running network propagation over the cell-cell network to select the most trait relevant cell populations where each cell is given a TRS (reference run), which is a normalized and bias-corrected network propagation score. With K = 30, we constructed the cell-cell network from the mentioned dataset. Further details of SCAVENGE have been described elsewhere.19 For finding the cell types with the largest score differences between the healthy patient populations, we split all the cells into bins based on their degree in the cell-cell network to correct for any topological artifacts. We ran network propagation a 1000 times with random seed cells (random runs) selected based on the degree distribution of the biologically meaningful seed cells selected following the steps mentioned above. The random seed cells were randomly sampled from bins of degrees, less than 7, 7–15, 15–20, 20–25, and greater than 25. This helped us to generate an empirical distribution of network propagation scores for the inferred cell-cell communication. The P was defined as the probability of score in random runs > score in the reference run, and we selected p < 0.05 as the cutoff for considering a cell as significant to the trait. We calculated odds ratio to quantify the differences in number of significant cells for each cell type between the disease and the healthy patient populations. The standard error for the odds ratio for each cell type was calculated using the formula: z=loge⁡(Oddsrato)1a+1b+1c+1d, where:

a = number of significant cells from COVID-19 patient samples

b = number of non-significant cells from COVID-19 patient samples

c = number of significant cells from healthy control samples

d = number of non-significant cells from healthy control samples

The z-statistic was converted to a P. All the odds ratios were log2 transformed for reporting. To compare the TRSs we ran SCAVENGE with interface variants, genome wide significance SNPs, coding SNPs that were away from the interface and a set of negative control SNPs which were selected at p > 0.01. To test the comparison of the scores we used the cells from the healthy samples in a scATAC-seq dataset containing cells from two CD34+ donors and 16 PBMC donors.68 All p to compare the distribution of scores for different variant pools were calculated using the Mann-Whitney U test.

Single cell RNA-seq pipeline to implicate disease relevant target cell types

SNPs were mapped to genes and each SNP-gene pair was given a score using the following criteria:

Non-coding SNPs – SNPs were mapped based on tissue specific annotations from Roadmap Epigenomics Consortium69,70 and Activity-by-Contact71,72 maps. For each SNP, if it mapped to multiple genes, the cumulative score across all SNP-gene pairs was 1, which was split hierarchically across annotations (promoter > enhancer |intergenic | genic > intronic).

Coding SNPs – Each SNP mapped to only one gene and each SNP-gene pair got a score of 1.

Single cell RNA seq datasets were obtained from the COVID-19 Cell Atlas.28,32,73 Datasets were separated into cell-type specific datasets and normalized. For each cell-type dataset, healthy and patient cells were scored based on the SNP-gene scores and gene expression, and each SNP was scored according to the fraction of cells with scores above the 95th percentile. These SNP scores were then used to score the genes, which these SNPs mapped to, and these gene scores were used to test for enrichment. Enrichment was defined as the difference in odds between healthy and patient cell types for high scoring genes for a SNP type compared to a pool of negative control SNPs. Additionally, for each cell type, we compared the difference in proportion of high scoring genes for cells derived from patients with COVID-19 across the different SNP types.

ScPagwas was run with the SNPs associated with severe respiratory illness and hospitalization due to COVID-19 and specific summary statistics (Standard error, beta and minor allele frequency) from the GWAS were provided as input. Above mentioned scRNA-seq datasets were used and the significance of each cell type in the datasets were reported for the healthy along with patient samples.

Fine-mapping the genome wide significant variants

From SNPs with P below the genome-wide significance (5 × 10−8) and genome-wide suggestive (1 × 10−6) thresholds, lead SNPs were defined as those with the smallest P within a 10 kbp region. These lead SNPs were used for fine mapping with ABF using the coloc package in R42 with a 1 Mbp region flanking the lead SNP as previously described.43 Thereafter, posterior inclusion probability for each SNP was calculated by dividing the ABF of the SNP with the cumulative sum of ABF for all SNPs within the 2 Mbp window around the lead SNP, and only SNPs within the 95% credible set were retained for SCAVENGE.

Review of measures used for evaluating color difference models
Patrick De Visschere

Abstract
We made a detailed review of the difference measures which have been used to judge the differences between experimentally determined color differences and theoretically defined ones, so-called line elements, for the human visual system. To eliminate the statistical errors due to variable and usually arbitrary sampling of the directions in a color point, we integrate the measures over a complete ellipsoid/ellipse. It turns out that in the limit for small deviations from circularity all proposed measures ( V A ​ B V_{AB} , γ − 1 \gamma-1 , C ​ V CV and STRESS \mathrm{STRESS} ) are equivalent. For greater deviations the measures become distinct with γ − 1 \gamma-1 the most sensitive and STRESS \mathrm{STRESS} the least. Ideally a difference measure should be coordinate independent and then it is advantageous to apply an affine transformation to both sets, e.g. turning the theoretical one into the unit ball. Although MacAdam already used this method but sampled the transformed ellipse, we integrate over the ellipsoid/ellipse. Comparing the results with the base measures we show that only STRESS \mathrm{STRESS} is coordinate independent. Judging whether a single ellipsoid/ellipse resembles a unit ball can easily be done by comparing the eigenvalues with one and we show that our previously proposed error measure d e ​ v d_{ev} (Candry e.a. Optics Express, 30, 36307, 2022) is the eigenvalue version of γ − 1 \gamma-1 . We show why the short lived correlation coefficient r r was justly abandoned, being very coordinate dependent, but that Pant’s recent geometric measure 1 − R 1-R on the other hand is coordinate independent. All measures are routinely made scale invariant by the introduction of a scaling parameter, to be optimized. Lastly we show that from all measures the γ − 1 \gamma-1 ones are the only ones permitting the simple derivation of the globally optimized difference measure from the locally defined ones.

Pant’s geometric measure

A_{\gamma}\subset A_{\lambda}
or
A_{\lambda}\subset A_{\gamma}

F

completely encompass the experimental one. Increasing

within which the ellipses actually cross each other. It is straightforward
to calculate these cross sections and subsequently the areas of the
4 parts constituting the cross-section area
A_{\cap}
enclosed by

A_{\cap}=\frac{\phi_{\lambda}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}}+\frac{\phi_{\gamma}}{\sqrt{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}}

The details for obtaining these angles are given in Appendix

discrepancies observed between difference measures. To eliminate this
sampling error we defined continuous measures by integrating over
all directions. These 4 measures then have exactly the same behavior
for small deviations from circularity and differences are only visible
for large deviations from circularity but are qualitative still similar.
Considering again a rather extreme case by comparing 2 perpendicular

gives the largest

,

and
\mathrm{STRESS}

latter 3 close together (see Fig.

We conclude that
\bar{\gamma}
penalizes large errors more heavily
and
\mathrm{STRESS}
the least.

These 4 basic measures are scale invariant and symmetric, except for

CV
which is not symmetric. Considering it’s close relation with

\mathrm{STRESS}
we can drop it from further consideration. Only
one of the remaining measures is also coordinate independent (
\mathrm{STRESS}
).
Exploiting the coordinate independence we affinely transform both
ellipses in the same way and so that one of them becomes a unit ball.
Comparing these transformed ellipses is much easier, since the number

Appendix A
Elliptic Integrals
[
5
]
.

For convenience we list the elliptic integrals used in the main text,
with their standard Legendre forms and their more modern symmetric
forms. For the Legendre forms we use the so-called parameter
m=k^{2}
,
where
k
is the modulus, as the argument and we only have to deal
with complete integrals.

Complete elliptic integral of the first kind

K\left(m\right)=\int^{\pi/2}_{0}\frac{d\theta}{\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}\theta}}

(62)

Complete elliptic integral of the second kind

E\left(m\right)=\int^{\pi/2}_{0}\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}\theta}d\theta

(63)

Complete elliptic integral of the third kind

\Pi\left(n,m\right)=\int^{\pi/2}_{0}\frac{d\theta}{\left(1-n\sin^{2}\theta\right)\sqrt{1-m\sin^{2}\theta}}

(64)

Symmetric elliptic integral of the first kind

R_{F}\left(x,y,z\right)=\frac{1}{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{dt}{s\left(t\right)}

(65)

where

s\left(t\right)=\sqrt{\left(t+x\right)\left(t+y\right)\left(t+z\right)}

(66)

Symmetric elliptic integral of the second kind

R_{G}\left(x,y,z\right)=\frac{1}{4}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{1}{s\left(t\right)}\left[\frac{x}{t+x}+\frac{y}{t+y}+\frac{z}{t+z}\right]tdt

(67)

Symmetric elliptic integral of the third kind

R_{J}\left(x,y,z,p\right)=\frac{3}{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\frac{dt}{\left(t+p\right)s\left(t\right)}

(68)

These symmetric integrals are complete if exactly one of the arguments

x,y,z
is zero. An additional function is defined by

R_{D}\left(x,y,z\right)=R_{J}\left(x,y,z,z\right)

and a useful connection formula is given by

\frac{p}{3}R_{J}\left(0,y,z,p\right)+\frac{q}{3}R_{J}\left(0,y,z,q\right)=R_{F}\left(0,y,z\right)\quad pq=yz

(69)

Many relations exist between the Legendre and the symmetric forms
with the main ones

K\left(1-m\right)=R_{F}\left(0,m,1\right)

(70)

E\left(1-m\right)=2R_{G}\left(0,m,1\right)

(71)

\Pi\left(n,1-m\right)-K\left(1-m\right)=\frac{1}{3}nR_{J}\left(0,m,1,1-n\right)

(72)

K\left(1-m\right)-E\left(1-m\right)=\frac{1}{3}\left(1-m\right)R_{D}\left(0,m,1\right)

(73)

E\left(1-m\right)-mK\left(1-m\right)=\frac{1}{3}m\left(1-m\right)R_{D}\left(0,1,m\right)

(74)

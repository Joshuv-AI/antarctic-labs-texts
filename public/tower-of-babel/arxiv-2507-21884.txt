Exploration on Demand: From Algorithmic Control to User Empowerment
Edoardo Bianchi

Abstract
Recommender systems often struggle with over-specialization, which severely limits users’ exposure to diverse content and creates filter bubbles that reduce serendipitous discovery. To address this fundamental limitation, this paper introduces an adaptive clustering framework with user-controlled exploration that effectively balances personalization and diversity in movie recommendations. Our approach leverages sentence-transformer embeddings to group items into semantically coherent clusters through an online algorithm with dynamic thresholding, thereby creating a structured representation of the content space. Building upon this clustering foundation, we propose a novel exploration mechanism that empowers users to control recommendation diversity by strategically sampling from less-engaged clusters, thus expanding their content horizons while preserving relevance. Experiments on the MovieLens dataset demonstrate the system’s effectiveness, showing that exploration significantly reduces intra-list similarity from 0.34 to 0.26 while simultaneously increasing unexpectedness to 0.73. Furthermore, our Large Language Model-based A/B testing methodology, conducted with 300 simulated users, reveals that 72.7% of long-term users prefer exploratory recommendations over purely exploitative ones, providing strong evidence for the system’s ability to promote meaningful content discovery without sacrificing user satisfaction.

Introduction

Recommender systems have become integral to digital content platforms, helping users navigate vast catalogs of movies, music, books, and other media. However, traditional recommendation approaches face persistent challenges in balancing personalization with diversity, often leading to over-specialization that limits users’ exposure to novel content and creates ”filter bubbles” that reinforce existing preferences rather than expanding user horizons.

Collaborative filtering methods, while effective at leveraging collective user behavior, rely heavily on historical interaction data, making them ineffective for new users and prone to popularity bias. Content-based approaches often overemphasize similarity metrics, leading to redundant recommendations that fail to introduce meaningful diversity. Both approaches struggle with the exploration-exploitation trade-off: how to balance familiar, relevant content with novel discoveries that might expand user interests.

The over-specialization problem is particularly pronounced in dynamic content environments where new items are constantly added and user preferences evolve. Traditional recommender systems optimize for immediate user satisfaction, measured through accuracy metrics like precision and recall, which inherently favor recommendations that match past behavior. This optimization strategy, while improving short-term engagement, can lead to recommendation monotony and reduced long-term user satisfaction.

This paper addresses these fundamental limitations through an adaptive recommendation framework that combines semantic clustering with user-controlled exploration. Our approach organizes content using sentence-transformer embeddings and an online clustering algorithm that adapts to content distribution shifts and maintains semantic coherence. The key innovation is an exploration mechanism that allows users to dynamically control the diversity-relevance trade-off in their recommendations, enabling personalized discovery experiences.

To evaluate the system’s effectiveness beyond traditional accuracy metrics, we employ a novel Large Language Model (LLM)-based user simulation approach that enables scalable A/B testing of recommendation strategies. This methodology addresses a critical limitation of existing evaluation frameworks, which primarily measure how well recommendations match past interactions while failing to capture the value of content discovery and preference expansion.

This work presents the following key contributions:

•

An adaptive online clustering algorithm that groups items using semantic embeddings with dynamic similarity thresholds;

•

A user-controlled exploration mechanism that enhances diversity by sampling under-explored clusters while preserving personalization;

•

A scalable LLM-based user simulation framework for A/B testing recommendation strategies beyond accuracy metrics;

•

Experimental validation showing improved diversity and unexpectedness while maintaining user satisfaction, especially among long-term users;

Related Work

2.1
Content-Based Recommendation Systems

Content-based filtering has evolved from metadata-based approaches to sophisticated systems leveraging rich item representations from unstructured content
Lops P. 2019
. Recent deep learning advances enable more effective content representations, with studies showing that visual features from CNNs
Filho et al. 2017
and NLP techniques like word embeddings
Berbatova 2019
significantly enhance semantic content profiling beyond traditional metadata approaches.

2.2
Clustering-Based Approaches

Clustering techniques have proven effective for organizing content and enhancing recommendation diversity. Zhang and Hurley
Zhang and Hurley 2009
demonstrated that partitioning user profiles into distinct taste segments improves recommendation novelty and balance. Theoretical work by Cohen-Addad et al.
Cohen-Addad et al. 2019
provides foundations for online k-means clustering with performance guarantees, while Choromanska and Monteleoni
Choromanska and Monteleoni 2012
emphasized adaptive mechanisms for streaming data that minimize concept drift while preserving cluster coherence.

2.3
Diversity and Exploration in Recommendations

The importance of recommendation diversity is well-established, with Ziegler et al.
Ziegler et al. 2005
showing that topic diversification improves user satisfaction despite potential accuracy trade-offs. Adamopoulos and Tuzhilin
Adamopoulos and Tuzhilin 2014
formalized unexpectedness as distinct from novelty and serendipity, providing frameworks for evaluation beyond accuracy metrics. However, most existing exploration approaches lack user control and transparency, which our work addresses through semantic clustering.

2.4
Evaluation Beyond Accuracy

Traditional evaluation focuses on accuracy metrics like precision and recall, which are biased toward reinforcing existing preferences
Aggarwal 2016
;
Francesco Ricci 2015
. Recent research explores alternative methodologies, including the use of LLMs for simulating human behavior in recommendation scenarios, providing reliable preference judgments that correlate well with human evaluators
Bougie and Watanabe 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2024
. These approaches build on broader research demonstrating that LLMs can effectively approximate human behavioral patterns and decision-making processes across various domains
Xie et al. 2024
;
Li et al. 2025
.

3
Methodology

3.1
System Architecture Overview

The proposed recommendation system addresses three fundamental challenges in content recommendation: information overload, cold-start scenarios, and limited content diversity. The system employs a modular architecture consisting of five interconnected components designed to work together seamlessly.

The
Item Embedding Module
leverages state-of-the-art sentence transformer models to convert textual metadata into dense semantic representations. The
Adaptive Clustering Module
groups items into semantically coherent clusters that evolve with the content catalog. The
Cold-Start Recommendation Module
enables immediate personalization for new users through keyword-based preference specification. The
Personalized Recommendation Module
generates tailored suggestions for returning users by analyzing interaction history. Finally, the
Exploration Control Module
allows users to dynamically adjust the diversity-relevance balance in their recommendations.

3.2
Item and User Representation

3.2.1
Item Space Definition

Each item in the system is characterized by comprehensive metadata:

\displaystyle\text{Item}=\{ID,Title,Tags,Keywords,Description\}

(1)

where Tags consist of single descriptive words, Keywords include multi-word phrases, and Description provides a textual summary of content. This rich representation enables nuanced semantic understanding that goes beyond simple keyword matching.

3.2.2
Semantic Embeddings

Items are encoded using Sentence-BERT
Reimers and Gurevych 2019
, specifically the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model, which generates 384-dimensional embeddings optimized for semantic similarity tasks. The embedding process concatenates all textual attributes into a unified representation:

\displaystyle\text{embedding}(item)=\text{SentenceBERT}(title\oplus tags\oplus keywords\oplus description)

(2)

where
\oplus
denotes string concatenation. These embeddings capture contextual relationships and semantic similarities that enable meaningful clustering and similarity-based retrieval.

3.3
Adaptive Clustering Algorithm

The adaptive clustering algorithm (Algorithm

Unlike traditional k-means approaches that require fixed cluster numbers, our algorithm dynamically adjusts both cluster membership and similarity thresholds. The algorithm uses cosine similarity for distance computation and maintains cluster centroids as the arithmetic mean of member embeddings. The similarity threshold adapts based on silhouette scores (S)
Rousseeuw 1987
, which provide a measure of clustering quality by comparing intra-cluster tightness with inter-cluster separation:

\displaystyle distance_{ij}=1-\text{cosine\_similarity}(i,j)

(7)

Threshold adjustments occur periodically (every 100 items) and follow a conservative strategy: significant reductions when clustering quality is poor (
S<0.1
), moderate adjustments for suboptimal clustering (
0.1\leq S<0.2
), and increases when clusters are well-formed (
S>0.4
). This approach prevents excessive fragmentation while maintaining semantic coherence.

Experimental Setup

4.1
Dataset and Preprocessing

We conducted experiments using the MovieLens 32M dataset
Harper and Konstan 2015
, which provides extensive user interaction data with rich metadata including movie titles, genres, and user ratings. To ensure computational tractability while maintaining statistical significance, we sampled 20,000 items while preserving the original data distribution across genres and popularity levels.

The preprocessing pipeline concatenated movie metadata (title, genres, and available tags) into unified textual representations suitable for embedding generation.

4.2
Implementation Details

For embedding generation, we employed the Sentence-BERT all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model
Reimers and Gurevych 2019
, a 384-dimensional transformer-based encoder fine-tuned on over one billion sentence pairs for semantic similarity tasks. This model provides an effective balance between semantic understanding and computational efficiency. All embeddings were precomputed and cached to minimize runtime overhead.

The clustering configuration initialized the similarity threshold at 0.45, with dynamic adjustments triggered every 100 items. To maintain computational efficiency while preserving accuracy, silhouette scores were computed over random samples of 1000 items, ensuring scalable quality assessment across varying dataset sizes.

Our evaluation framework simulated 300 users with varying interaction histories to assess system performance. Each user received a preference profile constructed by randomly sampling from individual users’ rating histories in the MovieLens 32M dataset
Harper and Konstan 2015
. This sampling preserves natural preference diversity, reflecting realistic user behavior and enabling authentic evaluation across diverse usage scenarios. LLM-based user simulation details are in Section
4.4
.

4.3
Evaluation Methodology

Traditional metrics like precision and recall are insufficient for evaluating our exploration-focused system, as they inherently favor recommendations that reinforce existing preferences. Instead, we employ diversity and unexpectedness metrics that better capture the system’s ability to balance relevance with content discovery.

Intra-List Similarity (ILS)

Ziegler et al. 2005
measures the average pairwise similarity among recommended items:

\displaystyle ILS=\frac{1}{|R|(|R|-1)}\sum_{i\in R}\sum_{j\in R,j\neq i}\text{cosine\_similarity}(i,j)

(8)

Lower ILS values indicate higher diversity, as items in the recommendation list are less similar to each other.

Unexpectedness

Adamopoulos and Tuzhilin 2014
quantifies how different recommendations are from a user’s historical interactions:

\displaystyle Unexp=\frac{1}{|R|}\sum_{i\in R}\left(1-\max_{j\in U_{h}}\text{cosine\_similarity}(i,j)\right)

(9)

Higher unexpectedness values indicate greater novelty, as recommended items are more distant from previously consumed content.

4.4
LLM-Based User Preference Evaluation

To assess user preferences for exploratory versus exploitative recommendations, we conducted simulated A/B testing using the DeepSeek-V3 language model
DeepSeek-AI et al. 2025
. This approach addresses limitations of traditional evaluation methods by incorporating human-like preference judgments that consider factors beyond simple accuracy matching.

For each simulated user, we generated two recommendation sets: one with exploration disabled (exploitation-only) and another with exploration enabled. The LLM was presented with both sets along with the user’s interaction history and asked to select the more appealing option. We employed the following evaluation prompt:

System:

You are a movie enthusiast who recently watched: [user history]. You must choose between two recommendation sets. Consider:

1.

Which set better matches your tastes.

2.

Which has more movies you’d actually watch.

User:

Set A: [set A].

Set B: [set B].

Which set would you choose?

Temperature was set to 0.4 to balance response consistency with nuanced variation. This methodology enables large-scale preference assessment that would be prohibitively expensive with human evaluators while providing insights into user behavior patterns.

4.5
Baseline Comparisons

Our approach was evaluated against two established baseline methods to assess performance improvements. The first baseline employed collaborative filtering through user-item matrix factorization with 50 latent factors, optimized using alternating least squares. This method represents a standard approach for capturing and leveraging user behavior patterns in recommendation systems.

The second baseline utilized popularity-based recommendations, ranking items by global popularity while incorporating genre preferences derived from individual user histories. This approach represents the simplest content-aware recommendation strategy and establishes a lower bound for expected system performance.

Both baseline methods were evaluated using identical metrics and user simulation protocols as our proposed approach, ensuring fair and consistent performance comparisons across all experiments.

5.1
Diversity and Novelty Improvements

The exploration mechanism consistently improves both diversity and novelty across all experimental configurations. The most significant improvements occur with longer user histories (h=50), where exploration reduces intra-list similarity from 0.34 to 0.26 for k=10, representing a 23.5% improvement in diversity. Simultaneously, unexpectedness increases from 0.67 to 0.73, indicating that users with longer interaction histories benefit most from exploratory recommendations.

Compared to baseline methods, our approach demonstrates substantial improvements. Collaborative filtering achieves ILS values between 0.37-0.39, while popularity-based recommendations perform worse with ILS values up to 0.47. Our exploration-enabled system achieves ILS values as low as 0.26, representing significant advances in recommendation diversity.

The unexpectedness improvements are equally compelling. While baseline methods achieve unexpectedness scores between 0.61-0.67, our exploration mechanism reaches up to 0.73, indicating successful introduction of novel content that expands user exposure beyond their established preferences.

5.2
User Preference Patterns

The LLM-based A/B testing reveals sophisticated user preference patterns that correlate strongly with interaction history length. Users with short histories (h=10) generally prefer exploitation-focused recommendations, with 51.7% and 62.3% choosing exploration-off for k=5 and k=10 respectively. This preference pattern suggests that users with limited system experience rely more heavily on familiar, relevant content.

In contrast, users with longer histories (h=50) show strong preference for exploratory recommendations, with 72.7% preferring exploration for k=5 and 60.8% for k=10. This pattern indicates that long-term users develop tolerance and appreciation for diversity as they gain confidence in the system’s ability to surface relevant content from unfamiliar categories.

The relationship between recommendation list size (k) and exploration preference also reveals interesting insights. For shorter histories, larger recommendation lists (k=10) increase preference for exploitation (62.3% vs 51.7%), possibly because users want more certainty when presented with more options. For longer histories, this effect diminishes, suggesting that long-term users are comfortable with exploration regardless of list size.

5.3
Cold-Start Performance

The keyword-based cold-start mechanism effectively bridges the gap between no interaction history and personalized recommendations. Cold-start ILS values (0.32-0.37) are competitive with exploration-off personalized recommendations (0.33-0.34), indicating that keyword-based clustering assignment provides reasonable diversity even without interaction history.

The transition from cold-start to personalized mode occurs smoothly, with no significant performance degradation observed during the transition period. Users typically require 10-15 interactions before the personalized system outperforms keyword-based recommendations consistently.

Computational Complexity and Scalability

The adaptive clustering algorithm demonstrates favorable computational characteristics for practical deployments. Cluster assignment requires
O(C\cdot d)
operations for nearest centroid search, where
C
represents cluster count and
d
is embedding dimension (384). Centroid updates scale linearly as
O(|I_{c}|\cdot d)
per modified cluster. Silhouette score computation requires
O(N^{2})
operations, which becomes prohibitive for
N>10^{4}
. For large datasets, sampling methods can reduce computational load while maintaining approximate quality assessment.

Recommendation generation requires
O(C\cdot d)
for cluster matching plus
O(M)
for item sampling in cold-start scenarios, while personalized recommendations need
O(h\cdot C)
for history analysis plus
O(C\log C)
for cluster ranking. The system scales linearly with cluster count, enabling real-time operation for
C<10^{3}
with approximately 25% computational overhead from exploration mechanisms.

Memory requirements are
O(C\cdot d+N)
for storing centroids and mappings, remaining under 500MB for typical configurations (
C=500
,
d=384
,
N=100,000
).

7
Limitations and Future Work

Some limitations suggest directions for future research. The
O(N^{2})
silhouette score computation limits scalability for very large catalogs, requiring approximate clustering quality measures. The system uses fixed exploration ratios that could benefit from adaptive parameters based on user feedback. Experiments focused on movie recommendation may not generalize to other domains, warranting cross-domain validation. Additionally, simulation-based evaluation captures short-term preferences but may not reflect long-term user satisfaction patterns.

Future research directions include reinforcement learning for dynamic exploration optimization, multi-modal content representations incorporating visual and audio features, cross-domain transfer learning for improved cold-start performance, and federated learning approaches for privacy-preserving recommendation with exploration.

8
Discussion and Conclusion

The experimental results demonstrate that user-controlled exploration effectively addresses over-specialization in content-based recommendation systems, achieving 23.5% improvement in diversity while maintaining user satisfaction. The correlation between interaction history length and exploration preference provides valuable insights, with 72.7% of long-term users preferring exploratory recommendations, suggesting that exploration mechanisms should adapt to user experience levels.

The adaptive clustering algorithm maintains semantic coherence while accommodating content distribution shifts through silhouette score-based threshold adjustment, avoiding both over-fragmentation and over-generalization. The system’s linear scalability and reasonable computational requirements make it viable for real-world deployment in streaming platforms, e-commerce systems, and digital libraries.

The LLM-based A/B testing methodology represents a significant contribution to recommendation evaluation, enabling large-scale preference assessment that captures human-like decision patterns beyond traditional accuracy metrics. This approach revealed systematic preference variations with interaction history that would be difficult to discover through conventional evaluation methods.

Key achievements include significant improvements in recommendation diversity (23.5% ILS reduction) and unexpectedness (increase to 0.73), strong user preference for exploration among long-term users, scalable architecture suitable for deployment, and novel evaluation methodology using LLM-based simulation. The user-controlled exploration mechanism provides transparency and addresses concerns about algorithmic accountability while enabling seamless transition from keyword-based cold-start to personalized recommendations.

By improving the balance between relevance and discovery, this research contributes to more intelligent and user-centric recommender systems that better serve diverse preferences while promoting content exploration. The transparent, user-controlled approach addresses growing concerns about algorithmic transparency and user agency in automated decision systems.

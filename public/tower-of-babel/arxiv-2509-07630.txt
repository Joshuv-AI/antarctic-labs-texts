Spinning mixtures: nonreciprocity transfers chirality across scales in scalar densities
Giulia Pisegna, Suropriya Saha

Abstract
We present a minimal model for mixtures of chiral active particles, formulated solely in terms of the number densities of the species. Nonreciprocity and chirality enter the chemical potential at vanishing order in spatial gradients, taking the form of local and non-local contributions. Chirality manifests as the curl of the nonreciprocal current, while the latter is simultaneously strongly modified by former. Their interplay generates distinctive dynamical states, including phase separation with edge currents and a spatiotemporally disordered phase with both chiral and nonreciprocal signatures.



I
Introduction

Chirality, the absence of mirror symmetry, is ubiquitous in physical and living matter
Harris et al. 1999
.
At the molecular scale, it manifests in the homo-chirality of life: nucleic acids such as DNA and RNA are built from D-sugars, proteins from L-amino acids. Why life selected specific handednesses, and how these preferences became universal, continues to be an open problem bridging physics, chemistry, and biology.
Yet chirality extends across different spatial scales reappearing at higher levels of organization: from the rotation of cells nucleus
Kumar et al. 2014
to the helical beating of bacterial flagella
Berg 2004
;
Di Leonardo et al. 2011
and collective chiral flows in tissues
Inaki et al. 2016
;
Chen et al. 2025a
. It also plays a central role in condensed matter systems, where its interplay with topology gives rise to exotic chiral materials
Felser and Gooth 2023
;
Tang et al. 2021
. These diverse examples highlight a central question: how can chiral twists be transferred from microscopic to macroscopic scales, and how robust is this phenomenon to fluctuations?

To recall the notion of chirality, consider the bacterium illustrated in Fig.
1
(a). Its flagella rotate in a fixed direction, inducing an opposite rotation of the cell body
Berg 2004
. The resulting motion cannot be superimposed onto its mirror image, as both head–tail and left–right symmetries are broken simultaneously. This fundamental feature is tightly linked to biological function, raising concerns about the possibility and implications of ‘mirror life’
Adamala et al. 2024
.

Recently, it has become evident that chirality plays a unique role also in active matter systems. Symmetry-broken states of active liquid crystals exhibit non-equilibrium time-periodic dynamics that are inaccessible in passive systems
Maitra 2025a
;
Maitra et al. 2020
. Collisions between inertial spinning particles transfer momentum tangential to the line connecting their centers, giving rise to a modified form of MIPS in which multiple voids coexist in the steady state, as demonstrated in theory
Digregorio et al. 2025
;
Caprini and Marini
Bettolo Marconi 2025
and in experiments
Antonov et al. 2025
. Several works have considered chiral particles with fixed speed of self-propulsion, and aligning interactions showing novel collective behaviours such as enhanced flocking, synchronization in two dimensions, and micro-phase separation
Levis and Liebchen 2019
;
Levis et al. 2019
;
Liebchen and Levis 2017
;
lev 2019
. Moreover, chiral Viscek particles show strong signatures of chirality in the fluctuations of the number density leading to condensation phenomena
Wang et al. 2024
. While mixtures of particles spinning at different rates have been investigated
Levis and Liebchen 2019
, the behavior of mixtures composed of structurally distinct chiral particles remains largely unexplored.

In this work, we construct a theoretical framework to identify the conditions under which, and the mechanisms through which, the microscopic handedness of active particles emerges in the collective dynamics of a two-species mixture. The two-species case offers the minimal yet basic setting to capture the complexity of biologically inspired environments and to uncover new paradigms of non-equilibrium physics
Soto and Golestanian 2014
;
Soto and Golestanian 2015
;
Agudo-Canalejo and Golestanian 2019
. More broadly, nonreciprocal interactions in multi-species mixtures have revealed rich phenomena, including the role of conservation laws in pattern-forming systems
Greve and Thiele 2024
, thereby extending the paradigm of pattern formation beyond Turing’s classical framework and the Hohenberg–Halperin classification
Hohenberg and Halperin 1977
;
Pisegna et al. 2025
;
Huang et al. 2024
.
When non-reciprocity couples to single-particle polarity and directed self-propelled motion, a transition occurs in which the broken rotational symmetry of the ferro(anti)magnetic flocking state is restored and chirality emerges
Fruchart et al. 2021
. Moreover nonreciprocity can stem also in one-species case
Huang et al. 2024
, for instance appearing as asymmetric alignment interactions in flocking systems
Dadhichi et al. 2020
;
Cavagna et al. 2017
or odd elastic response among particles with noncentral, pairwise interactions
Fruchart et al. 2023
.
In contrast, our work addresses a different universality class by developing a phenomenological model for two conserved particle-density fields with chiral coupling and non-reciprocity, while polarity can be neglected. The presence of two species introduces chiral terms that differ from the single-species case
Kole et al. 2021
;
Maitra et al. 2020
, giving rise to novel non-equilibrium phenomena in which chirality manifests on macroscopic scales.

II
The model

The simplest example of a chiral particle can be described through its orientational unit vector
\bm{n}
, which rotates within a two-dimensional plane (Fig.
1
b). In the absence of fluctuations,
\bm{n}

undergoes a steady rotation with constant angular velocity
\bm{\omega}
directed along the
\hat{\bm{z}}
axis, perpendicular to the plane. To account for stochastic perturbations, we include a noise term
\bm{\xi}
of amplitude
D_{r}
so that the orientational dynamics is governed by

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{d}\bm{n}}{\mbox{d}t}=(\omega\hat{\bm{z}}+\bm{\xi})\times\bm{n}.

(1)

This equation is not invariant under a parity transformation of the spatial axes, such as
\hat{\bm{x}}\to-\hat{\bm{x}}
.
The dynamics is preserved only if the handedness of the particle is simultaneously reversed, i.e.,
\omega\to-\omega
.

To study how this type of chirality is transferred from single particles to larger structures in multispecies systems, we consider a binary mixture of conserved scalar fields
\phi_{a}
(
a=\{1,2\}
) with nonreciprocal interactions, and chiral currents that reflect the handedness of the constituent particles. The fields evolve as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{a}=\nabla^{2}\mu_{a}-\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{j}^{c}_{a}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{a}.

(2)

The chemical potential
\mu_{a}=\mu_{eq,a}+\alpha\epsilon_{ab}\phi_{b}
receives two contributions. The first contribution,
\mu_{eq,a}
, is the functional derivative of a free energy density that leads to bulk phase separation in the passive system. The second stems from inter-species nonreciprocal interactions, which we minimally include in
\mu_{a}
introducing the strength of nonreciprocity,
\alpha
.
\epsilon_{ab}
is the two dimensional Levi-Civita tensor acting on the internal space of densities
Saha et al. 2020
;
You et al. 2020
and we assume summation convention for the species index. The nonreciprocal term does not have a variational form, and introduces frustration in the dynamics of the two species. A conserved white and Gaussian stochastic noise
\bm{\zeta}_{a}
acts on the densities, as in standard Model B dynamics
Hohenberg and Halperin 1977
.

The starting point of our work is to propose a phenomenological form for the chiral currents
\bm{j}_{a}^{c}
based on symmetry arguments. Consider the following from,

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{c}_{a}=-\beta_{a}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{A}\cdot\bm{\nabla})\mathcal{G},

(3)

where
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are functional of the fields and their gradients, and
\mathbb{A}
is the fully anti-symmetric tensor acting on the two dimensional Cartesian subspace,
A_{xx}=A_{yy}=0
, and
A_{xy}=-A_{yx}=1
. For the simplest term involving the fewest number of gradients, we have
\mathcal{G}=\phi_{1},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\phi_{2}
such that

1

Three different choices lead to the same term on the R.H.S. of Eq. (
2
). They are
\mathcal{G}=c_{1},\,\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=c_{2}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}
,
\mathcal{G}=c_{1}\phi_{1},\,\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=c_{2}\phi_{2}
, and
\mathcal{G}=c_{1}\phi_{2},\,\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=c_{2}\phi_{1}
, with the redefinition
\beta\equiv\beta c_{1}c_{2}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{a}=\nabla^{2}\mu_{a}-\beta_{a}\hat{\bm{z}}\cdot(\bm{\nabla}\phi_{1}\times\bm{\nabla}\phi_{2})+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{\zeta}.

(4)

To ascertain the nature of the second term on the R.H.S. of Eq. (
4
), consider how the equation transforms when the handedness of the coordinate-axes is changed. The first term on the R.H.S. of Eq. (
4
), being a scalar, is invariant under the transformation, while the second term within the parentheses reverses sign. The dynamics in Eq. (
4
) is invariant under parity, and thus well defined, only if the coefficients
\beta_{a}
are chiral in nature, i.e. they are pseudo-scalars. This situation arises, for instance, when
\beta_{a}
are proportional to the intrinsic spin of particles rotating in the two dimensional plane, following the microscopic interpretation of Eq. (
1
).
The fields themselves are number densities, hence they do not play a role in the discussion, meaning that the considerations hold for all choices of
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. To explain the novelty of
\bm{j}_{c}
in a two-species mixture consider again the following

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{j}_{a}^{c}=\beta_{a}(\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}\partial_{y}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}-\partial_{x}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\partial_{y}\mathcal{G}).

(5)

For a non-vanishing L.H.S. of Eq. (
5
), we require
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
to have different functional forms. For a single species, the simplest term at lowest order in gradients and powers of fields that we are allowed to construct is with
\mathcal{G}=\phi
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\nabla^{2}\phi
, namely

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}^{c}=-\beta\phi(\mathbb{A}\cdot\bm{\nabla})\nabla^{2}\phi,

(6)

which involves three gradients and is thus less relevant than the term we include in Eq. (
4
). In the latter case, for simplicity of notation, we will call
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{j}^{a}_{c}=\beta_{a}\omega_{c}
.

To complete the model we need to specify the passive part of the
\mu_{a}
, which is written in terms of a Cahn-Hilliard free energy functional
\mu_{eq,a}=\delta\mathcal{F}/\delta\phi_{a}
. This includes general linear and non-linear couplings between species
Cahn and Hilliard 1958
. The associated free-energy density
F=K\bm{\nabla}\phi_{a}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\phi_{a}/2+V(\phi_{a})
is composed by a surface-tension contribution, assumed equal for all the species
Frohoff-Hülsmann et al. 2021
, and a potential
V
. Here we focus on two different forms of the
V
distinguishing different symmetries: a Mexican-hat potential,

\displaystyle V_{m}(\phi_{a})=-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{a}\phi_{a}+\frac{1}{4}(\phi_{a}\phi_{a})^{2}

(7)

that is
SO(2)
symmetric, and a double-well potential,

\displaystyle V_{d}(\phi_{a})=\sum_{a}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{a}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\phi_{a}^{4}\right]

(8)

that is an example of discrete symmetry. The impact of these two alternatives on nonreciprocal phase separation has been studied in
Johnsrud and Golestanian 2025
. Here, we demonstrate that the novel phenomenology arising from chiral interactions is independent of the specific choice of the potential. However, one of the two forms allows for more tractable analytical calculations in different contexts we explore. The resulting analytical predictions are always validated by numerical simulations performed with the alternative potential, thus testing the general application of our results.

II.1
Microscopic derivation of the chiral current.

We have motivated the theoretical model in Eq. (
4
) through a phenomenological approach based on symmetry considerations under coordinates transformation and number conservation laws. Nevertheless, the chiral current
\bm{j}_{a}^{c}
has a robust and generic microscopic interpretation. Starting from the simple model of Eq.(
1
), we consider different species of active particles that self-propel with speed
v_{a}
and rotate with frequency
\omega_{a}
. When the propulsion speed depends on the local particles density
v_{a}(\{\rho\})
, the system exhibits quorum sensing interactions, a mechanism known to drive motility induced phase separation
Cates and Tailleur 2015
and nonreciprocal dynamical states
Dinelli et al. 2022
;
Duan et al. 2023
. In Appendix
A
we present a detailed coarse-graining of this microscopic model, showing that the mesoscopic density fields evolve according to
\partial_{t}\rho_{a}+\bm{\nabla}\cdot(v_{a}\bm{p}_{a}+h.o.t.)=0
, receiving contributions from the average polarisation
\bm{p}_{a}
. In the regime where the orientation relaxes faster than the conserved density field, we find

\displaystyle(2D_{ra}\mathbb{I}+\omega_{a}\mathbb{A})\bm{p}_{a}=-\frac{1}{2}\bm{\nabla}(v_{a}\rho_{a})\ .

(9)

The term proportional to
\mathbb{I}
, the two-dimensional identity tensor, aligns with an effective pressure driving an instability and promoting particles aggregation
Cates and Tailleur 2015
. In contrast, chirality introduces an odd rotational diffusivity, giving rise to chiral currents in the evolution of
\rho_{a}
, with
\mathcal{G}=v_{a}(\{\rho\})\rho_{a}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\simeq\omega_{a}\rho_{a}
.

A second example consists of chemically active particles, reacting to gradients of solute concentration
c
produced by other colloids of surface activity
\sigma_{a}
. As described in Appendix
A
, there are multiple ways for which chirality emerges, for instance when the response of the particle is not along the gradient of the chemical field but has a component at an angle to it (Fig.
1
c)
Saha et al. 2019
. This happens for spinning chemical colloids (Appendix
A
), but also when the active frequency is substituted by a non-linear passive response to solute gradients. The orientational degree of freedom of chemical colloids with a non-axisymmetric mobility surface distribution reads,

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{a}=\frac{1}{2D_{ra}}[-v_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}+(\Omega_{a}\mathbb{I}-(\bar{\Omega}_{a}/3)\mathbb{A})\cdot\bm{\nabla}c\rho_{a}]

(10)

with
\Omega_{a},\bar{\Omega}_{a}
describing the alignment of the orientation to
\bm{\nabla}c
coming from phoretic chemotactic interactions (see Appendix
A
for a detailed derivation). Because of
c\sim\sigma_{b}\rho_{b}
, the last term produces a chiral current with
\mathcal{G}\simeq\bar{\Omega}_{a}\sigma_{b}\rho_{b}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\rho_{a}
.
These two examples illustrate that, regardless of the source of activity in a multispecies system, chiral currents inevitably emerge at quadratic order in gradients, as stated in Eq. (
3
).

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
III
, we will describe the dynamics exhibited by the model exploring the phase diagram in the
(\alpha,\beta)
plane. We report two new phases that are formed due to the interplay of chirality and nonreciprocity : chiral phase separation, and a chiral disordered phase. Both the states are characterised by fluctuations that are quantified using statistical measures. In Section
IV
we carry out a linear stability analysis of the traveling wave solutions that can be written down exactly, in order to provide a qualitative understanding of the roles of
\alpha
and
\beta
. In Section
V
we explain how the phase separated state is created by the generation of an active Laplace pressure at the interface, and we describe the edge currents through interface equations.

III
Large-scale dynamics: interplay of chirality and nonreciprocity

To underline the connection between nonreciprocity and the chiral term in Eq. (
4
), we notice that
\omega_{c}=\hat{\bm{z}}\cdot(\bm{\nabla}\phi_{1}\times\bm{\nabla}\phi_{2})=\hat{\bm{z}}\cdot(\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{J})/2
, where
\bm{J}=\phi_{1}\bm{\nabla}\phi_{2}-\phi_{2}\bm{\nabla}\phi_{1}

Saha et al. 2020
;
Pisegna et al. 2024
. The quantity
\bm{J}
, which vanishes when the two densities are either exactly in phase or exactly out of phase, captures the chasing dynamics between the two densities induced by nonreciprocal interactions. It serves as an order parameter
Pisegna et al. 2024
;
Pisegna et al. 2025
signalling the transition to a state with broken
PT
-symmetry. In absence of other non-equilibrium interactions, it jumps to a homogeneous constant value when nonreciprocity is sufficiently large and traveling waves are formed
Rana and Golestanian 2024a
.

We can rewrite Eq. (
4
) purely as a gradient dynamics in the form
\partial_{t}\phi_{a}=\nabla^{2}M_{a}
by introducing a non-local chemical potential
\mu_{c}^{a}

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\mu_{a}^{c}=-\beta_{a}\omega_{c}

(11)

i.e.
M_{a}=\mu_{a}+\mu_{a}^{c}
. As a consequence of Eq. (
11
), we have
\beta_{2}\mu_{1}^{c}=-\beta_{1}\mu_{2}^{c}
. If nonreciprocity gives rise to local polar order in the form of a nonzero
\bm{J}
, then chirality emerges through the vorticity of this vector field. Consequently, nonreciprocal interactions are required to observe the effects of the chiral currents.

This insight motivates a numerical exploration of the parameter space defined by
(\beta,\alpha)
, where we set
\beta=\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
.
We perform numerical simulations of Eq. (
4
) without stochastic noise, using first a double-well potential
V_{d}
at fixed system size, and varying nonreciprocal and chiral strengths. The results are reported in the phase diagram of Fig.
2
(g). First, we find that for
\alpha=0
, and
\beta\neq 0
the system evolves to a standard bulk phase separated state.
For very large nonreciprocal interactions and small
\beta
, the chasing dynamics takes over and the system self-organizes in traveling patterns, which have been already extensively studied
Pisegna et al. 2024
. Increasing the level of chirality, we reveal two novel dynamical steady states that arise due to the competition of these non-equilibrium effects.

For
\beta\gg\alpha
, we find that the mixture coarsens to phase separated structures that are clearly out of equilibrium due to persistent currents that circulate at the edges (Fig.
2
(d-f)). A bubble of field
\phi_{1}
coexists with a droplet of field
\phi_{2}
and we observe mass transport in a direction determined by the sign of
\beta
. The polar order parameter is null in the bulk, and it acquires non-zero values at the interfaces. From Fig.
2
(f) we observe that the edge currents correspond to local chiral vorticity
\omega_{c}
, that alternates in positive and negative values to sum up to zero and respect the conservation law. The non-local chemical potential is sourced by charges of alternating signs that are located at the interface. The
\mu_{a}
thus lacks of spherical symmetry, a feature that makes this phenomenon quite different from other active phase separations
Tjhung et al. 2011
.

An interplay between comparable values of
\alpha
and
\beta
destroys both the steady states of traveling waves and phase separation, and instead produces a spatio-temporally disordered state (see Fig.
2
(a-c)). As we discuss later in the paper, the coarsening for this system is a slow process, therefore to distinguish better the transition between phase separated and disordered state, we start simulations from passive bulk separated initial conditions.

Even in the disordered state, the chiral current remains non-zero, with local sources of vorticity distributed throughout the system. To characterize its fluctuations quantitatively, we compute the static correlation function of the chiral vorticity in Fourier space,

\displaystyle S_{c}(\bm{q})=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mbox{d}t|\omega_{c}(\bm{q},t)|^{2}.

(12)

where
T
denotes the total simulation time. Fig.
3
(b) shows
S_{c}

for both disordered and phase-separated configurations. In the disordered case,

S_{c}
is isotropic in wave-vector space, while in the phase-separated state it becomes strongly anisotropic, reflecting the emergence of well-defined vorticity domains. A clear signature of this phenomenon appears in the wave-vector
|\bm{q}_{m}|
at which
S_{c}

reaches its maximum, defining a characteristic length scale of vorticity fluctuations. This characteristic scale is larger in the phase-separated state than in the disordered phase: in the former, vorticity fluctuations are localized along domain boundaries and are therefore confined to smaller spatial regions (see Fig.
4
(b)).

The spatial distribution of vorticity charges plays also an important role in the coarsening dynamics to form chiral phase separated droplets. Chirality sources of opposite sign cancel one another to dynamically produce linear arrangements along the interfaces. We analyse this phenomenon computing the coarsening characteristic length scale
\ell(t)
, using the circularly averaged structure factor
S(q)=<|\phi_{1}(\bm{q},t)|^{2}>
for one of the two fields, such that
\ell(t)^{-1}=(\int\mbox{d}qS(q)q)/(\int\mbox{d}qS(q))
. The different effects of chirality and nonreciprocity compete resulting in a growth law that depends on both
\alpha
and
\beta
. For comparable
\alpha
and
\beta
, the coarsening arrests at a length-scale comparable to the surface tension. As nonreciprocity is decreased, chiral domains of opposite signs fuse, leading to a lower mean chiral vorticity in the system (Fig.
4
(d)).
At the smallest values of
\alpha
, for which we still find phase separation with the transverse currents, the coarsening evolves with a power law of about
\ell\sim t^{1/4}
. This exponent differs markedly from that of standard phase separation
Bray 2003
, highlighting a distinct phenomenon and motivating further investigation in this direction.

Our goal is now to predict and understand analytically the new phases unveiled by this model. The chiral terms are purely non-linear and a linear stability analysis around a homogeneous state does not bring further information. To gain more insights on these phenomena we focus on the traveling wave solution, and on the dynamics of the interfaces.

IV
Linear stability of the traveling wave

The numerical simulations of Fig.
2
(g) show a transition from traveling waves states to disordered chiral phase in the regime of large
\alpha
. To rationalize this part of the phase diagram we study the stability of the traveling wave solutions in Eq. (
4
). Specifically, we consider the system under the Mexican-hat potential
V_{m}
to describe free-energy-driven interactions. In this setting, the exact traveling wave solution can be obtained, enabling a systematic study of fluctuations around it
Pisegna et al. 2024
;
Pisegna et al. 2025
.

Defining the complex field
\phi=\phi_{1}+i\phi_{2}

and choosing
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\beta
, the Eq.(
4
) in terms of
\phi
becomes,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi=\nabla^{2}[-(1+i\alpha)\phi+|\phi|^{2}\phi-K\nabla^{2}\phi]

(13)

\displaystyle+\beta_{c}A_{ij}\partial_{i}\phi^{*}\partial_{j}\phi\

where
\beta_{c}=\beta(1-i)/2
is a complex coefficient, and the dynamics resembles a conserved complex Ginzburg-Landau equation
Aranson and Kramer 2002
. While the equilibrium and non-chiral active part of the dynamics are invariant under a global phase transformation
\phi\to e^{i\varphi}\phi
, the chiral term
\beta_{c}
is not. The lack of this invariance makes the linear stability analysis around this state quite non-trivial.

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma=\int_{1}^{2}\mbox{d}x\partial_{x}\sigma

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we studied a field-theoretical model describing two interacting density fields coupled through chirality and linear nonreciprocal interactions. We demonstrated that these ingredients are generic in multispecies systems, as they naturally arise in coarse-grained descriptions of chiral chemical colloids or quorum-sensing active particles with nonreciprocal interactions. The resulting mesoscopic theory is characterized by three essential components: (i) phase separation that creates inhomogeneities in the fields; (ii) nonreciprocal couplings that produce local phase shifts between them described by a non-reciprocal current
\bm{J}
; and (iii) chirality, that locally twists
\bm{J}
. The interplay between them generates new active phase separated states that we investigated both analytically and numerically.

Active phase separation has been extensively studied, with much of the work focusing on single-species active systems
Tjhung et al. 2018
;
Tjhung et al. 2011
;
fau 2021
;
Solon et al. 2018
;
Zwicker et al. 2015
, and more recently drawing interest in the context of large-scale chiral active matter
Kole et al. 2021
;
Maitra 2025b
.
Generalisation to a two-species mixture marks an important difference with previous models: chiral currents involve terms with lower number of gradients, which are expected to be more relevant at large scales. Indeed, they fundamentally modify the nonreciprocal dynamics of the mixture, producing disordered chiral states and chiral phase separated regions with edge currents when chirality is dominant. The nature of this phenomenon thus stands out from the current scenarios of conserved scalar field theories
Cates and Nardini 2025
. In our model,
chirality emerges as the non-local contribution to the chemical potential, generated by localized domains where the curl of the non-reciprocal current is large, as shown in Fig.
1
(d). These domains act as charges of positive or negative signs, thereby preserving a conservation law that arises in the system. The resulting charges can either be dispersed randomly across the simulation domain, characteristic of the disordered chiral state, or align linearly, characteristic of the phase-separated state as shown in Fig.
4
(b). The alternating signs of these charges effectively screen the long-range nature of chirality, allowing it to counterbalance the role of non-reciprocity in driving phase separation. This balance is quantified through the condition expressed in Eq. (
17
), and verify its validity in numerical simulations. The chiral disordered state emerges when these changes fail to cancel pairwise. Hence, chirality is intrinsically linked to nonreciprocity, and its effects cannot arise without this source of activity. Our results therefore demonstrate that the propagation of chirality from small to large length scales happens with assistance from non-reciprocity.

Our theoretical predictions offer a promising route toward experimental verification in mixtures of active agents with intrinsic chirality. Such systems provide a natural playground for testing our ideas, with potential implications ranging from uncovering new principles of self-organization in biological collectives
Beppu et al. 2021
to designing novel functionalities in synthetic chiral metamaterials
Plum et al. 2009
;
Souslov et al. 2017
. Beyond these immediate perspectives, our model also opens several exciting avenues for theoretical exploration. A natural step forward is the inclusion of stochastic noise, which would allow us to probe the universality class of the model both at criticality and in the vicinity of traveling-wave states—an extension that is expected to refine, and possibly overturn, current predictions
Pisegna et al. 2024
. Another important direction is the incorporation of hydrodynamic interactions
Pisegna et al. 2025
;
Tucci et al. 2025
, which are known to play a crucial role in the dynamics of both living
Tan et al. 2022
and synthetic chiral systems
Chen et al. 2025b
. Together, these developments would not only bring the model closer to experimental realizations but also deepen our understanding of how chirality and nonreciprocity conspire to drive emergent collective behavior across a wide class of active matter systems.

Appendix A
Microscopic models of chiral active mixtures

Consider a binary system of active particles in two dimensions with quorum sensing interactions. In addition, they spin with angular speed

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{r}}_{a}^{\gamma}=v_{a}(\{\rho\})\bm{n}_{a}^{\gamma}+\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{a}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{a}=D_{a}\nabla^{2}\rho_{a}+\hat{D}_{ra}\bm{\nabla}\cdot[v_{a}\cdot\bm{\nabla}(\rho_{a}v_{a})]

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{a}=\bar{D}_{a}\nabla^{2}\phi_{a}+v_{0}^{2}\hat{D}_{ra}\eta_{ab}\nabla^{2}\phi_{b}

\displaystyle+\hat{\omega}_{a}v_{0}^{2}\frac{\eta_{ab}}{\rho_{0}}\bm{\nabla}\phi_{a}\cdot\mathbb{A}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\phi_{b}

and
\bm{n}^{\gamma}_{a}
.
A realisation of this case is when particles spin at constant frequency
\omega_{a}
, and chemotactically align with strength
\Omega_{a}
, such that

\displaystyle\bm{R}^{\gamma}_{a}=-\Omega_{a}(\bm{n}_{a}^{\gamma}\times\bm{\nabla}c)-\omega_{a}\hat{\bm{z}}.

(34)

Performing a coarse-graining of this system
Tucci et al. 2024b
;
Tucci et al. 2025
we get,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{a}=-\bm{\nabla}\cdot[(v_{a}\bm{p}_{a}-\mu_{a}\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c-D_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a})]

(35)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{p}_{a}=-\mathbb{D}_{ra}\cdot\bm{p}_{a}-v_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}+2\Omega_{a}\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c+\mathcal{O}(\nabla^{2})

(36)

where, also in this case, we identify an effective odd rotational diffusivity,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}_{ra}=2D_{ra}\mathbb{I}+\omega_{a}\mathbb{A}.

(37)

As shown in
Saha et al. 2014
;
Tucci et al. 2024b
, the orientational dynamics does not show pattern formation in the large-scale limit therefore, at time scales larger than
D_{ra}^{-1}
, it is reasonable to assume that orientations’ relaxation can be enslaved to the dynamics of the densities through the relation

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{a}={\mathbb{D}}_{ra}^{-1}\cdot\left[2\Omega_{a}\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c-\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}v_{a}\right],

(38)

which we can write explicitly into two parts

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{a}=\hat{D}_{ra}\mathbb{I}\cdot\left[2\Omega_{a}\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c-\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}v_{a}\right]

\displaystyle-\hat{\omega}_{a}\mathbb{A}\cdot\left[2\Omega_{a}\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c-\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}v_{a}\right]

(39)

with
\hat{D}_{ra}=2D_{ra}/\det\mathbb{D}_{ra}
, and
\hat{\omega}_{a}=\omega_{a}/\det\mathbb{D}_{ra}
.
Considering length scales
l\gg 1/\kappa
, we can approximate
c(\bm{r},t)\simeq\sum_{a}(\sigma_{a}\rho_{a})/(D_{c}\kappa^{2})
. Substituting this expression into Eq(
A.0.2
) and Eq(
35
) we find,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{a}=\bar{D}_{a}\nabla^{2}\rho_{a}+\sum_{b}\chi_{ab}\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{b})

(40)

\displaystyle+\sum_{b}\beta_{ab}\bm{\nabla}\cdot\mathbb{A}\cdot(\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{b})

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{a}=D_{a}+v_{a}^{2}\hat{D}_{ra}

(41)

\displaystyle\chi_{ab}=\left(\mu_{a}-2v_{a}\hat{D}_{ra}\right)\frac{\sigma_{b}}{D_{c}\kappa^{2}}

(42)

\displaystyle\beta_{ab}=\frac{2\hat{\omega}_{a}\Omega_{a}v_{a}\sigma_{b}}{D_{c}\kappa^{2}}

(43)

The last term of Eq(
40
) is the chiral axial current Eq(
3
). From this derivation we can see how nonreciprocity and effective chirality stem from a phoretic non-equilibrium dynamics. Moreover the same chiral term
\beta_{ab}
can be apriori nonreciprocal, i.e.
\beta_{ab}\neq\beta_{ba}
.

A.0.3
Chiral passive response of catalytic colloids

In the examples above, chirality acts as an intrinsic source of activity, manifesting as a constant rotation of the particle’s polarity axis. By contrast, using chemical colloids as an example, we show that chirality can also arise passively as a response to external concentration gradients.

For this, we need to consider a particle
\gamma
of species
a
with two axis of polarization
\bm{n}_{a,1}^{\gamma}
and
\bm{n}_{a,2}^{\gamma}
, separated by a fixed angle
\theta_{0}
. Moreover, we associate to this the two first moments of the Laplace expansion of surface mobility
\mu_{1,1}
and
\mu_{1,2}
, whose non-null values usually produce terms of alignment of the polarization to
\bm{\nabla}c

Saha et al. 2019
;
Tucci et al. 2025
.
This configuration is achievable building a catalytic particle with mobility patches non-axisymmetric on its surface
Saha et al. 2019
;
Lisicki et al. 2018
. Using
\bm{n}_{a,2}^{\gamma}=(\mathbb{I}\cos\theta_{0}+\mathbb{A}\sin\theta_{0})\cdot\bm{n}_{a,1}^{\gamma}
, we derive the dynamics of the orientational vector,

\displaystyle\frac{d\bm{n}_{a,1}^{\gamma}}{dt}=\bm{n}_{a,1}^{\gamma}\times[\Omega_{a}\bm{\nabla}c\times\bm{n}_{a,1}^{\gamma}+

(44)

\displaystyle+\bar{\Omega}_{a}\bm{\nabla}c\times(\mathbb{A}\cdot\bm{n}_{a,1}^{\gamma})+\bm{\xi}^{\gamma}]

with
\Omega_{a}=3(\mu_{1,1}+\mu_{1,2}\cos\theta_{0})/(8R)
and
\bar{\Omega}_{a}=3(\mu_{1,2}\sin\theta_{0})/(8R)
,
R
is the radius of the particle. Coarse-graining Eq. (
44
) with the positions evolution Eq. (
31
) we obtain,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{a}=-\bm{\nabla}\cdot[v_{a}\bm{p}_{a}-\mu_{a}\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c-D_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}]

(45)

\displaystyle\partial\bm{p}_{a}=-2D_{ra}\bm{p}_{a}-v_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}+(\Omega_{a}\mathbb{I}-(\bar{\Omega}_{a}/3)\mathbb{A})\cdot\bm{\nabla}c\ \rho_{a}

Thus, from the latter

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{a}=\frac{1}{2D_{ra}}[-v_{a}\bm{\nabla}\rho_{a}+(\Omega_{a}\mathbb{I}-(\frac{\bar{\Omega}_{a}}{3}\mathbb{A})\cdot\bm{\nabla}c\rho_{a}]

(46)

and finally,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{a}=(D_{a}+v_{a}^{2}/2D_{ra})\nabla^{2}\rho_{a}+(\mu_{a}-\frac{v_{a}\Omega_{a}}{2D_{ra}})\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\rho_{a}\bm{\nabla}c)

\displaystyle+\frac{v_{a}\bar{\Omega}_{a}}{6D_{ra}}\bm{\nabla}\cdot(\mathbb{A}\cdot\bm{\nabla}c\rho_{a}).

(47)

Following the same steps as before, we obtain the chiral currents studied in the main text from the last term of this expression.

B.0.2
Tridiagonal recurrence relation

Eq (
16
) can be read as an eigenvalue problem of a tridiagonal recurrence relation
Risken 1989
. An alternative method to find the eigenvalue
\lambda
is to use continuous fractions. In our case the index
n
is not positive definite, hence to solve (
16
) we define,

\displaystyle r_{n}^{L}=\frac{c_{n+1}}{c_{n}}=-\frac{1}{Q_{n}^{+}}\left[(\lambda+Q_{n})+\frac{Q_{n}^{-}}{r_{n-1}^{L}}\right]

(51)

\displaystyle r_{n}^{R}=\frac{c_{n-1}}{c_{n}}=-\frac{1}{Q_{n}^{-}}\left[(\lambda+Q_{n})+\frac{Q_{n}^{+}}{r_{n+1}^{R}}\right]

and
c_{-N-1}=0

and
r_{N}^{R}

\displaystyle(\lambda+Q_{0})+Q_{0}^{+}r_{0}^{L}+Q_{0}^{-}r_{0}^{R}=0\ .

(53)

The latter is a highly non-linear equation that can be solved numerically in
\lambda
. A comparison between this method and the direct numerical evaluation of the eigenvalues is reported in Fig.
8
(a,b) for different parameters. When the system is stable the two methods perfectly match (Fig.
8
(a)), on the other hand when the
\lambda_{max}
becomes positive the continuous fraction method shows the same features of the other evaluation, but need larger
N
to reach convergence (Fig.
8
(b)).

We show this study as a function of the angle of perturbation
\psi
, but it can be done as well also varying the modulus

. Within this framework, in Fig.
5
(a) we see that, after a critical value of

The Palatini formalism of the f ⁡ ( R , ℒ m , T ) f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T) theory of gravity
J.G. de Lima Júnior, P.H.R.S. Moraes, E. Brito, J.A.S. Fortunato

Abstract
We present the first formulation of the recently proposed f ⁡ ( R , ℒ m , T ) f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T) theory of gravity within the Palatini formalism, a well-known alternative variational approach where the metric and connection are treated as independent variables. By applying this formalism, we derive a new set of field equations that exhibit, as expected, distinct properties compared to their metric formalism counterparts. We particularly present the Newtonian limit of this formalism, as well as the resulting Friedmann-like equations. We highlight that potential observational signatures may distinguish between the metric and Palatini frameworks. Our results open new pathways for exploring the phenomenology of modified gravity theories and their testability with observational data.

2
A brief review of the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
theory of gravity in the metric formalism

As originally proposed, the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
gravity theory starts from the action
[
22
]

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)}{16\pi}+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right].

(1)

In (
1
),
g
is the determinant of the metric tensor and throughout the paper, natural units will be assumed. From the action above, it is possible to see that the functional form of the function
f
may retain generic terms of non-minimal coupling between geometry and matter.

By varying (
1
) with respect to the metric yields the field equations of the theory in the metric formalism, namely

\displaystyle(R_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu})f_{R}-\frac{1}{2}(f-2f_{m}\mathcal{L}_{m})g_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle(8\pi+f_{m})T_{\mu\nu}+f_{T}\tau_{\mu\nu},

(2)

for which
R_{\mu\nu}
is the Ricci tensor,
g_{\mu\nu}
is the metric tensor,
f_{R}\equiv\partial f/\partial R
,
f_{m}\equiv f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}/2+f_{T}
,
f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}\equiv\partial f/\partial\mathcal{L}_{m}
,
f_{T}\equiv\partial f/\partial T
,

T_{\mu\nu}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-\frac{2\partial\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}

(3)

is the energy-momentum tensor and

\tau_{\mu\nu}\equiv 2g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}.

(4)

Finally, the equation for the covariant derivative of the energy-momentum tensor reads

\displaystyle(8\pi+f_{m})\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}(f_{m}\mathcal{L}_{m})-T_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\mu}f_{m}-\nabla^{\mu}(f_{T}\tau_{\mu\nu})

(5)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}\nabla_{\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}+f_{T}\nabla_{\nu}T).

3
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
in Palatini formalism

In this section we will consider the formulation “à la Palatini” for the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
theory. In this approach, unlike what occurs in the metric formalism, the connection
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
is independent of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. In this case, we will adopt a Ricci tensor
\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}
, which is formed from the independent connection and the metric. Because of this we also adopt a Ricci scalar
\mathcal{R}=g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}
.

A key advantage of working with the Palatini formalism is that, unlike the metric formalism, it avoids instabilities since it yields second-order differential field equations for the metric. This feature prevents the emergence of ghost fields, as predicted by Ostrogradski’s theorem
[
54
]
, which typically arise in higher-order theories. Another extremely important quality is that, also due to the natural structure of this formalism, the equations for the connection components are bonds, and not dynamic equations, which, in other words, means that they do not introduce new dynamic fields.

3.1
Field equations from the variation with respect to the metric

Taking the variation of Eq.(
1
) with respect to the metric yields the field equations

\displaystyle f_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{f}{2}g_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}}{2}\left(g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-T_{\mu\nu}\right)-f_{T}\left(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\right).

(6)

Note also that the variation of
T_{\mu\nu}
with respect to the metric tensor has the form

\frac{\delta(g^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta})}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}

(7)

where, we are defining

\Theta_{\mu\nu}\equiv g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.

(8)

The trace of Eq. (
6
) gives

f_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{R}-2f=\kappa^{2}T-f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{m}-\frac{T}{2}\right)-f_{T}\left(T+\Theta\right).

(9)

With Eqs. (
6
) and (
9
), we can write the Einstein tensor in terms of the variation with respect to the metric only:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left\{\kappa^{2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}T\right)-\frac{f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}}{2}\biggr[g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-T_{\mu\nu}\right.

(10)

\displaystyle\left.\left.-g_{\mu\nu}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{m}-\frac{T}{2}\right)\right]-f_{T}\biggr[(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu})\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}\left(T+\Theta\right)\biggr]-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}f}{2}\right\}.

3.2
Field equations from the variation with respect to the connection

Now, considering the variation of the gravitational action with respect to the connection, we obtain

\nabla_{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{-g}f_{\mathcal{R}}g^{\mu\nu}\right)=0.

(11)

Note that the field equations obtained above are identical to those found in both the
f(R)
and
f(R,T)
theories
[
55
]
. This makes it convenient to introduce a new second-order tensor
h_{\mu\nu}
, which we call “auxiliary metric”, where

h_{\mu\nu}\equiv f_{\mathcal{R}}g_{\mu\nu}\ ,

(12)

such that

\nabla_{\lambda}\left(\sqrt{-h}h^{\mu\nu}\right)=0.

(13)

This allows us to directly solve Eq.(
11
), whose solution is the Levi-Civita connection. Therefore, the connection in terms of the new metric reads

\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}h^{\lambda\sigma}\left(\partial_{\mu}h_{\nu\sigma}+\partial_{\nu}h_{\mu\sigma}-\partial_{\sigma}h_{\mu\nu}\right).

(14)

The Ricci tensor transforms as
[
56
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{f_{\mathcal{R}}}\biggr[\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{f_{\mathcal{R}}}\nabla_{\mu}f_{\mathcal{R}}\nabla_{\nu}f_{\mathcal{R}}

(15)

\displaystyle-\left(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\Box\right)f_{\mathcal{R}}\biggr],

and consequently the Ricci Scalar transforms as

\mathcal{R}=R+\frac{3}{f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left(\frac{1}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\nabla_{\mu}f_{\mathcal{R}}\nabla^{\mu}f_{\mathcal{R}}-\Box f_{\mathcal{R}}\right).

(16)

From Eqs.(
15
) and (
16
), we can write the Einstein tensor as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left[\frac{3}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left(\nabla_{\mu}f_{\mathcal{R}}\nabla_{\nu}f_{\mathcal{R}}-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}\left(\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}\right)^{2}\right)\right.

(17)

\displaystyle+\left(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\right)f_{\mathcal{R}}\biggr].

Finally, with Eqs.(
10
) and (
17
), we can write the Einstein tensor in its complete form for the
f(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
theory of gravity:

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left\{-\left[\left(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\right)f_{\mathcal{R}}+\frac{3}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\biggl(\nabla_{\mu}f_{\mathcal{R}}\nabla_{\nu}f_{\mathcal{R}}\right.\right.

(18)

\displaystyle\left.\left.\left.-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}\left(\nabla f_{\mathcal{R}}\right)^{2}\right)\right]+\kappa^{2}\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}T\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{f_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}}{2}\left[g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-T_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}\left(2\mathcal{L}_{m}-\frac{T}{2}\right)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.-f_{T}\left[(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu})-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{2}\left(T+\Theta\right)\right]-\frac{g_{\mu\nu}f}{2}\right\}.

Note that the inclusion of terms referring to the matter Lagrangian density and trace of the energy-momentum tensor in the gravitational Lagrangian is responsible only for a modification in the field equations referring to the metric, whereas for the connection they are the same as what is obtained when we treat the case of a theory in which the gravitational Lagrangian depends only on
R
.

4
The Newtonian limit

Let us investigate the Newtonian limit for the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
theory in the Palatini formalism. To do this, let us consider our metric
g_{\mu\nu}
to be the Minkowski metric
\eta_{\mu\nu}
plus a perturbation, i.e.,

g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\gamma_{\mu\nu}

(19)

where
\gamma_{\mu\nu}\ll 1
.

In this context, we also assume the auxiliary metric (
12
) to be nearly flat, so that

h_{\mu\nu}=f_{\mathcal{R}}g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu\nu},

(20)

where
\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}\ll 1
is of the same order as
\gamma_{\mu\nu}
. Hence,
f_{\mathcal{R}}\approx 1
. Using Eqs, (
19
) and (
20
) we obtain

(f_{\mathcal{R}}-1)\eta_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}-f_{\mathcal{R}}\gamma_{\mu\nu}.

(21)

If we take
f_{\mathcal{R}}=e^{2W}
and expand it at first order as
f_{\mathcal{R}}=1+2W
, then the above equation shows that

W=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}-\gamma}{2(4+\gamma)}.

(22)

where
\tilde{\gamma}\equiv\eta^{\mu\nu}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}
and
\gamma\equiv\eta^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{\mu\nu}
. Thus,
W\sim O(\gamma)\sim O(\gamma_{\mu\nu})
.

Now, let us write the
g
-frame Ricci tensor:

R_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial^{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu\lambda}+\partial^{\lambda}\partial_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu\lambda}-\partial^{2}\gamma_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\mu\nu}\gamma\right).

(23)

The Ricci tensor expression (
15
) can be rewritten as

\mathcal{R}_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}(\gamma_{\mu\nu}+2\eta_{\mu\nu}W)-2\partial_{\mu\nu}W.

(24)

The field equations (
6
) under the Newtonian limit now become

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}-2\partial_{\mu\nu}w-\frac{f}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{f_{\mathcal{L}}}{2}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-T_{\mu\nu}\right)-f_{T}\left(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\right).

(25)

Considering the case of a perfect fluid characterized only by its energy density
\rho
and isotropic pressure
p
, the energy-momentum tensor is given by

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu}.

(26)

In the comoving frame, the four-velocity components are
u^{\mu}=(-1,0,0,0)
, such that the four-velocity
u^{\mu}
satisfies the normalization condition
u^{\mu}u_{\mu}=-1
. In this frame, the components of the energy-momentum tensor become
T_{\mu\nu}=\text{diag}(-\rho,p,p,p)
, such its trace reads

T=-\rho+3p.

(27)

Making use of the definition of the
\Theta_{\mu\nu}
tensor and Eq.(
3
), we can write this tensor for a perfect fluid:

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_{m}-2T_{\mu\nu}-2g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{m}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}\partial g^{\alpha\beta}},

(28)

such that considering
\mathcal{L}_{m}=p
, we have

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-2T_{\mu\nu}.

(29)

Now we can write Eq.(
25
) as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\tilde{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}-2\partial_{\mu\nu}w-\frac{f}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\rho\left(-\kappa^{2}-f_{T}-\frac{f_{\mathcal{L}}}{2}\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}-p\left(\frac{f_{\mathcal{L}}}{2}+f_{T}\right)\eta_{\mu\nu}.

(30)

Taking the
00
component of the previous expression, which governs the weak field dynamics, that is, in the Newtonian limit (
p\to 0
), space-time is assumed to be static and the time derivatives are zero, which leads us to the following expression:

-\frac{1}{2}\vec{\nabla}^{2}\tilde{\gamma}_{00}=\rho(\kappa^{2}-f_{T}+f_{\mathcal{L}})-\frac{f}{2},

(31)

which is the Poisson’s equation in the Palatini formulation of the
f(\mathcal{R},\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
theory of gravity.

5
Friedmann-like equations

In this section we will construct the Friedmann-like equations for the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
theory in the Palatini formalism. For particular choices of the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
function, those can further be confronted with cosmological data, with the purpose of verifying if the Palatini formulation of the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
gravity can explain the cosmic acceleration (naturally, with no need for the cosmological constant). We start from the Friedmann-Lemâitre-Robertson-Walker metric:

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a(t)^{2}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2})

(32)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor, such that
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter.

From the
00
component of Eq.(
18
), considering the metric above and the energy-momentum tensor (
26
), we obtain the first modified Friedmann equation as

\displaystyle 3H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left[\kappa^{2}(\rho+3p)-\frac{f_{m}}{2}(3\rho-13p+8\mathcal{L}_{M})\right.

(33)

\displaystyle\left.+f-\frac{3(\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}})^{2}}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}-6H\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}}\right],

or even, in a more compact way

\left(H+\frac{\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}}}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\right)^{2}=\frac{\kappa^{2}(\rho+3p)-\frac{f_{m}}{2}(3\rho-13p+8\mathcal{L}_{M})+f}{6f_{\mathcal{R}}}.

(34)

Similarly, we can obtain the second modified Friedmann equation from the
ii
components:

\displaystyle 2\dot{H}+3H^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left[\kappa^{2}(\rho-p)-\frac{f_{m}}{2}(3\rho-5p+8\mathcal{L}_{M})\right.

(35)

\displaystyle\left.-f-\frac{3(\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}})^{2}}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}-2\ddot{f}-4H\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}}\right].

An equation for the evolution of the Hubble function comes from the elimination of the term
3H^{2}
in the Friedmann equations:

\displaystyle\dot{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2f_{\mathcal{R}}}\left[\kappa^{2}(\rho+p)-f_{m}\left(4\mathcal{L}_{M}+\frac{3}{2}\left(\rho+p\right)\right)\right.

(36)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{3(\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}})^{2}}{2}+\ddot{f}-H\dot{f}_{\mathcal{R}}\right].

6
Final remarks

In the present article, we have obtained, for the first time in the literature, the Palatini formalism of the recently proposed
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
gravity. By employing the variational approach, we derived field equations that exhibit distinct properties compared to their metric formalism counterparts. With our results, we offer a new set of equations to be applied to different systems and regimes, that will ultimately verify the viability of the theory.

We have particularly obtained the Poisson’s equation in the formalism as well as the Friedmann equations. Together, and in possession of the referred observational data, those can put stringent constraints to the functional form of the
f(R,\mathcal{L}_{m},T)
function.

The Palatini formulation of this theory can also bring some valuable insights on the geometry-matter non-minimal coupling allowed within the theory. Theories with non-minimal coupling between geometry and matter have intensified their appearance in the field of modified gravity and offer a more fundamental relation between matter and space-time curvature, with an optimistic example being the recently proposed Entangled Relativity
[
57
]
.

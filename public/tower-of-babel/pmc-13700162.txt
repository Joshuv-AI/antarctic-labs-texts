Exploratory safety analysis of tislelizumab-based regimens in patients with lung cancer: a retrospective real-world study
Authors: Jinyu Liu, Qi Zhang, Xihan Lin, Yanwen Nuo, Ruxu You
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProgrammed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitors, including tislelizumab, are used in China and other countries for the treatment of lung cancer. However, the safety profile of tislelizumab has been poorly reported in lung cancer patients based on real-world data. This study aimed to evaluate the incidence of treatment-related adverse events (trAEs) and identify associated risk factors.MethodsA retrospective, single-center study was conducted using electronic medical records of 212 lung cancer patients treated with tislelizumab-based regimens between February 2021 and February 2023. TrAEs were assessed using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0. Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify risk factors for trAEs.ResultsAmong 212 patients, 98.11% experienced at least one trAE, and 19.81% experienced grade 3–4 trAEs. The most commonly affected systems were hematological (91.98%), hepatobiliary (66.51%), renal (55.19%), and immune (40.57%). The most frequent trAEs included anemia (83.96%), decreased lymphocyte count (53.30%), and increased lactate dehydrogenase (47.64%). Hypothyroidism (25.47%) was the most common immune-related adverse event (irAE). Severe irAEs (grade 3–4) occurred in 1.89% of patients, leading to treatment discontinuation in 4.72%. Risk factors for myelosuppression included squamous non-small cell lung cancer (sq-NSCLC) (OR 3.21, 95% CI 1.48–6.95, P = 0.003) and combination therapy with chemotherapy (OR 3.44, 95% CI 1.79–6.59, p < 0.001). Abnormal liver function was associated with cerebrovascular disease (OR 5.38, 95% CI 1.35–21.49, P = 0.017), elevated alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels (OR 3.74, 95% CI 1.27–11.00, P = 0.016), and decreased creatinine levels (OR 38.82, 95% CI 2.54–592.59, P = 0.009). Renal dysfunction was linked to sq-NSCLC (OR 4.60, 95% CI 2.16–9.82, p < 0.001), decreased eGFR (OR 4.31, 95% CI 2.19–8.48, p < 0.001), and current smoking status (OR 2.75, 95% CI 1.06–7.16, P = 0.038). Thyroid dysfunction was strongly associated with elevated thyrotropin levels (OR 8.09, 95% CI 2.11–31.02, P = 0.002) and cardiovascular disease (OR 4.07, 95% CI 1.75–9.45, P = 0.001). Sq-NSCLC, decreased levels of creatinine and eGFR were more likely to result in electrolyte disturbances, while coagulation dysfunction was influenced by liver disease and decreased eGFR, with female sex being protective.ConclusionsThis real-world study demonstrated that the safety profile of tislelizumab-based regimens is comparable to that observed in clinical trials, with hematological, hepatic, renal, and immune systems most commonly affected. Several clinical and biochemical factors were identified as potential risk factors for trAEs. Given the single-center retrospective design and lack of a comparator group, these findings should be considered exploratory and need for validation in prospective, multicenter studies to better inform clinical management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-026-04540-4.Keywords: Lung neoplasms, Immunology, Adverse events, Risk factors, Tislelizumab

Background

Lung cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed cancer worldwide, with approximately 2.5 million new cases and 1.8 million deaths reported in 2022 [1]. Historically, platinum-based chemotherapy has been the standard first-line treatment for patients with both small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [2, 3]. However, recent advancements in lung cancer treatment have introduced innovative targeted therapies and immunotherapies, significantly improving patient outcomes [4–6].

Several global phase 3 clinical trials have demonstrated that immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), targeting programmed death 1 (PD-1) or programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy in the first-line setting, result in significantly improved outcomes compared to chemotherapy alone [7, 8]. Tislelizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody, with high affinity and specificity for PD-1, was specifically designed to minimize FcɣR binding on macrophages, thereby eliminating antibody-dependent phagocytosis. This unique design aims to counteract potential mechanisms of T-cell clearance and resistance to anti-PD-1 therapy. Notably, two phase 3 trials (NCT03663205, NCT03594747) demonstrated that the addition of tislelizumab to chemotherapy significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS), response rates, and duration of response compared to chemotherapy alone [9, 10]. These findings led to the approval of tislelizumab in China in June 2021 as a first-line therapy for advanced non-squamous NSCLC. Subsequently, its indications in China were expanded to include first-line treatment for advanced squamous NSCLC (sq-NSCLC), extensive-stage SCLC in combination with chemotherapy, and monotherapy for previously treated advanced NSCLC. These crucial indications are strongly endorsed by the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines with Grade I recommendations [11, 12]. In April 2024, it was also approved by the European Commission as both a first-line and second-line treatment for NSCLC.

As tislelizumab continues to be integrated into oncology treatment protocols, the effective management of its associated adverse events (AEs) is a critical component of clinical care. Despite its increasing use, there remains a paucity of real-world studies investigating the safety profile of tislelizumab, and no research to date has comprehensively explored the risk factors associated with its AEs. Therefore, we conducted a retrospective real-world study to evaluate the safety of tislelizumab-based regimens in lung cancer patients, focusing on the incidence of treatment-related adverse events (trAEs) and identifying key factors contributing to their occurrence.

Methods

Patient population and data collection

We conducted a retrospective review of electronic medical records from the hospital information system (HIS) for patients with lung cancer who received ≥ 1 dose of tislelizumab monotherapy or tislelizumab-based combination therapy between February 2021 and February 2023 at our hospital. Patients were followed from the initiation of tislelizumab treatment until one month after discontinuation, the end of the study period, or death, whichever occurred first. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Tongji Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Approval Number: TJ-IRB20220773).

The following data were collected:(a) Demographic characteristics: age, sex, smoking status, histological classification, tumor stage, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, location of distant metastases, and PD-L1 expression levels; (b) Comorbidities: hypertension, diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, cerebrovascular disorders, cardiovascular disorders, hematological abnormalities, gastrointestinal disorders, respiratory diseases, hepatic disorders, renal diseases, secondary malignancies, myelosuppression, infectious diseases, and thyroid diseases; (c) Biochemical indicators: alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TBL), direct bilirubin (DBL), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), gamma-glutamyl-transferase (GGT), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), urea, creatinine, uric acid, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), thyrotropin (TSH), free T3, and free T4. The results were categorized as elevated (above the upper limit of normal), normal, or decreased (below the lower limit of normal); (d) Treatment history: “The last cycle” refers to the final treatment cycle before tislelizumab initiation, including therapies such as chemotherapy, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitors, vascular endothelial growth factor-receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors (VEGFR-TKI), and PD-1 inhibitors. This term will be used consistently throughout the manuscript. Additionally, concurrent therapies with tislelizumab, including chemotherapy, EGFR inhibitors, and VEGFR-TKI, were documented.

Outcomes and assessments

The safety outcomes were the trAEs observed following the administration of tislelizumab, encompassing blood and lymphatic system function (complete blood count and coagulation), hepatic function, renal function, endocrine function (thyroid function), and electrolyte levels. These data were meticulously gathered and recorded in the HIS by dedicated oncologists and oncology nurses. AEs were defined and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (CTCAE 5.0) [13]. Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were identified by the treating oncologist after excluding alternative diagnoses, based on the following criteria in hierarchical order:(1) pathological evidence of irAE, (2) multidisciplinary adjudication involving two or more oncologists, or (3) clinical improvement with irAE-targeted treatment [14]. To avoid duplication in reporting, recurrent AEs of the same grade experienced by a patient were recorded only once.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 24.0 software, with a two-sided p-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Patient baseline characteristics and the incidence of AEs were summarized using descriptive statistics. For exploratory subgroup analyses comparing trAEs between sq-NSCLC and adenocarcinoma subgroups, Fisher's exact test was used for categorical variables, with effect sizes expressed as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Given the hypothesis-generating nature of the analysis and the multiple comparisons involved, p-values are reported without formal adjustment for multiple testing.

To identify factors associated with trAEs, univariate logistic regression was initially applied to each candidate variable. Variables with a p-value < 0.20 in univariate analysis or those with established clinical relevance (e.g., age, sex, and treatment regimen) were included in the multivariate logistic regression model. The treatment modality (tislelizumab monotherapy versus combination therapy) was pre-specified as the primary variable of interest and was included in all models. Given the limited sample size in the monotherapy group, patients receiving either regimen were pooled for the safety analysis to enhance statistical power. The final multivariate model was constructed using a backward stepwise elimination procedure (removal criterion: p ≥ 0.05), ensuring that all clinically essential variables were retained while excluding non-significant ones. Results are presented as ORs with 95% CIs.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 212 patients were enrolled in this study. The median age was 60 years (range: 24–81), and the majority were male (78.30%). Among the cohort, 54.72% had a history of smoking, including 14.15% current smokers and 40.57% former smokers. The population included 21 patients(9.91%) with SCLC, 178(83.96%) with NSCLC (adenocarcinoma: 92; squamous: 86), and 13 (6.13%) with other tumor types.

In terms of treatment, most patients (n = 198, 93.40%) received tislelizumab combined with platinum-based chemotherapy. The most common chemotherapy backbones were albumin-bound paclitaxel (105/198, 53.03%), followed by pemetrexed (55/198, 27.78%) and docetaxel (19/198, 9.60%). A minority (n = 14, 6.60%) received tislelizumab monotherapy. Across the cohort, 40.57% (n = 86) received tislelizumab-based therapy as first-line treatment, and 59.43% (n = 126) as second-line or later therapy. Treatment-related adverse events

Overall, 98.11% (n = 208) of patients experienced at least one trAE during the study, while 4 patients (1.89% of the total cohort) reported no trAEs, all of whom were receiving tislelizumab monotherapy. Among all patients, 19.81% (n = 42) experienced grade 3–4 trAEs. Importantly, no trAE-related deaths (grade 5 events) occurred during the study period. Discontinuation of tislelizumab due to trAEs occurred in 4.72% (n = 10) of cases, primarily attributed to irAEs, including diarrhea (n = 3), cutaneous irAEs (n = 3), pneumonitis (n = 1), immune-related peripheral neuropathy (n = 1), and immune thrombocytopenia (n = 1).

The systems most commonly affected by trAEs were the hematological system (91.98%), hepatobiliary system (66.51%), renal system (55.19%), and immune system (40.57%). The most frequently reported trAEs (Table 2) included anemia (83.96%), decreased lymphocyte count (53.30%), increased LDH (47.64%), hyperuricemia (37.26%), and hypercholesterolemia (34.43%). Meanwhile, the most common potential irAEs (Table 3) included hypothyroidism (25.47%), hyperglycemia (10.38%), hypothyroidism (8.49%), and cutaneous irAEs (3.77%). Detailed descriptions of trAEs in the tislelizumab monotherapy subgroup are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Exploratory subgroup analysis by histology

To explore potential differences in the safety profile of tislelizumab-based regimens between the two major NSCLC histological subtypes, an exploratory comparison of trAE incidence between patients with adenocarcinoma and sq-NSCLC was conducted. The detailed results are presented in Supplementary Table 2.

The analysis revealed distinct safety profiles between the two major histological subtypes. Patients with sq-NSCLC exhibited a significantly higher incidence of hematological toxicities, including anemia (91.30% vs 72.09%, P < 0.001), thrombocytopenia (33.70% vs 10.47%, P < 0.001), leukopenia (28.26% vs 10.47%, P = 0.004), and neutropenia (27.17% vs 13.95%, P = 0.041). In contrast, patients with adenocarcinoma showed a higher incidence of hepatic and metabolic abnormalities, such as elevated ALP (36.05% vs 16.30%, P = 0.003), elevated LDH (56.98% vs 38.04%, P = 0.016), hyperuricemia (41.30% vs 25.58%, P = 0.039) and hypercholesterolemia (37.21% vs 19.57%, P = 0.012). No significant differences were observed in the incidence of most immune-related adverse events (irAEs) between the two groups.

Risk factor analysis for trAEs

The results of the multivariate logistic regression analysis are presented in Supplementary Tables 3–8, with significant risk and protective factors summarized in Table 4. Patients with sq-NSCLC (OR 3.21, 95% CI 1.48–6.95, P = 0.003) and those receiving tislelizumab in combination with chemotherapy (OR 3.44, 95% CI 1.79–6.59, P < 0.001) were at higher risk of developing myelosuppression, whereas receiving PD-1 inhibitor therapy in the last cycle was associated with a substantially lower risk (OR 0.33, 95% CI 0.16–0.67, P = 0.002). However, it should be noted that only six patients received PD-1 inhibitor therapy in the last cycle, which may limit the reliability of this finding and requires cautious interpretation.

Abnormal liver function was significantly associated with a history of cerebrovascular disease (OR 5.38, 95% CI 1.35–21.49, P = 0.017), elevated ALT levels (OR 3.74, 95% CI 1.27–11.00, P = 0.016), decreased creatinine levels (OR 38.82, 95% CI 2.54–592.59, P = 0.009), and elevated free T3 levels (OR 16.83, 95% CI 1.70–166.17, P = 0.016). Conversely, decreased GGT levels (OR 0.05, 95% CI 0–0.91, P = 0.043) and PD-1 inhibitor therapy in the last cycle (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.25–0.90, P = 0.023) demonstrated protective effects. Notably, some variables, such as PD-1 inhibitor therapy in the last cycle (n = 6), decreased GGT levels (n = 1), and elevated free T3 levels (n = 4), had limited sample sizes, which may influence the reliability of these specific associations.

Renal dysfunction was significantly associated with sq-NSCLC histology (OR 4.60, 95% CI 2.16–9.82, P < 0.001), decreased baseline eGFR (OR 4.31, 95% CI 2.19–8.48, P < 0.001), and current smoking status (OR 2.75, 95% CI 1.06–7.16, P = 0.038). In contrast, decreased uric acid levels (OR 0.25, 95% CI 0.09–0.74, P = 0.013) and receiving tislelizumab in a subsequent-line setting (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.20–0.82, P = 0.035) were identified as protective factors.

Elevated baseline thyrotropin level (OR 8.09, 95% CI 2.11–31.02, P = 0.002) was the strongest risk factor for thyroid dysfunction, followed by decreased urea levels (OR 6.90, 95% CI 1.23–38.69, P = 0.028), history of cardiovascular disease (OR 4.07, 95% CI 1.75–9.45, P = 0.001), and myelosuppression (OR 2.44, 95% CI 1.09–5.43, P = 0.030). Meanwhile, PD-1 inhibitor therapy in the last cycle (OR 0.45, 95% CI 0.21–0.94, P = 0.035) demonstrated a protective effect.

Electrolyte disturbances were significantly associated with sq-NSCLC (OR 3.91, 95% CI 1.62–9.40, P = 0.002), decreased GGT levels (OR 138.65, 95% CI 6.77–2794.51, P = 0.001), decreased creatinine levels (OR 21.96, 95% CI 2.01–239.91, P = 0.011), and decreased eGFR levels (OR 3.44, 95% CI 1.65–7.48, P = 0.001). Conversely, elevated eGFR levels (OR 0.01, 95% CI 0–0.77, P = 0.039) and elevated urea levels (OR 0.06, 95% CI 0.01–0.62, P = 0.018) demonstrated protective effects. Notably, decreased GGT levels (n = 1) and elevated eGFR levels (n = 2) were based on very small sample sizes, which may compromise the reliability of these specific associations.

Lastly, coagulation dysfunction was associated with hepatic disease (OR 2.88, 95% CI 1.18–7.08, P = 0.021) and decreased eGFR (OR 3.06, 95% CI 1.26–7.46, P = 0.014), whereas female sex was identified as a protective factor (OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.02–0.55, P = 0.008).

Discussion

In this retrospective real-world analysis, we meticulously evaluated the safety profile of tislelizumab-based regimens for the treatment of lung cancer. Compared with randomized controlled trials (RCTs), real-world studies provide a broader representation of the safety profile of approved drugs in routine clinical practice. These studies better reflect real-world patient populations, including those with greater heterogeneity, more comorbidities, and poorer performance status—groups often excluded from RCTs. However, their intrinsic limitations, such as heterogeneity and potential confounding factors, complicate the interpretation of associations with trAEs, necessitating careful analysis and consideration of biases.

Historically, the clinical development of ICIs, particularly anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 therapies, predominantly focused on monotherapy in later-line settings. However, recent advancements have shifted towards combination therapies in the first-line setting and the incorporation of immunotherapy into earlier stages of treatment [15]. Consistent with this trend, our study found that 198 patients (93.40%) received tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy, with 86 patients (40.57%) using this combination as a first-line regimen. Notably, 19 patients (8.96%) received treatment at earlier stages of the disease.

Our data demonstrated that the safety profile of tislelizumab in combination with chemotherapy was comparable to that reported in RCTs [9, 10]. TrAEs were observed in nearly all patients, with hematological toxicities (91.98%) being the most prevalent, consistent with the known toxicity profile of chemotherapy. Multivariate logistic regression analysis further identified the combination of chemotherapy as a significant risk factor for myelosuppression. In contrast, tislelizumab monotherapy was associated with a lower incidence of myelosuppression, particularly among patients with poor physical status, consistent with previous reports [16–18]. The incidence of severe (Grade 3–5) trAEs in our study was 19.81%, slightly lower than that observed in RCTs [9, 10], likely reflecting differences in patient characteristics and real-world practice. Nevertheless, the absence of a comparator group receiving standard chemotherapy-based regimens limits our ability to definitively attribute the observed trAEs to tislelizumab, chemotherapy, or their combination.

To explore the potential impact of histology on the safety profile, we conducted an exploratory subgroup analysis comparing the incidence of trAEs between patients with adenocarcinoma and those with sq-NSCLC. The analysis revealed a distinct pattern: patients with sq-NSCLC exhibited a significantly higher incidence of hematological toxicities, whereas those with adenocarcinoma showed a higher incidence of hepatic and metabolic abnormalities. This exploratory finding was further corroborated by multivariate logistic regression analysis, which identified sq-NSCLC as an independent risk factor for myelosuppression. The biological basis underlying these histology-specific toxicity profiles may result from the interaction between cytotoxic chemotherapy and the intrinsic biological characteristics of each histological subtype. Previous studies have suggested that sq-NSCLC is characterized by a pro-inflammatory cytokine milieu and alterations within the bone marrow niche, which may potentiate the myelosuppressive effects of concomitant chemotherapy [19]. In contrast, adenocarcinoma often displays distinctive metabolic reprogramming, including dysregulated lipid and glucose metabolism [20]. This inherent metabolic phenotype may render the liver more susceptible to injury under combination therapy. Although our study was not designed to elucidate these mechanisms, the findings generate a clinically meaningful hypothesis: histology-based monitoring strategies (e.g., intensified hematologic surveillance in sq-NSCLC and closer hepatic/metabolic function assessment in adenocarcinoma) could optimize safety management. Prospective studies incorporating translational research are warranted to validate these exploratory safety signals and to further investigate their biological basis.

Immunotherapies are also known to be associated with distinct irAEs. The occurrence of irAEs following the administration of PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors is common, with the endocrine system, skin, liver and gastrointestinal system being the most frequently affected organs. According to previous literature [21–28], the reported incidence of irAEs of all grades ranges from 15 to 90%, which may be attributed to differences in study populations and the targeted drugs used. Previous data indicated that the incidence of irAEs was as high as 72% with anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) treatment, compared with 50% for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors [26, 27]. In this real-world observational study, we found that the incidence of irAEs was 40.57%, a moderate figure consistent with previously documented reports. A separate real-world study conducted by Ge et al [21] reported that 77.60% of patients with solid tumors treated with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 medications experienced irAEs. Notably, our research observed thyroid dysfunction, including both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism, as the most common irAEs, followed by hyperglycemia and cutaneous toxicities.These findings are consistent with the results of previous studies. The majority of the irAEs encountered in this study were mild (Grade 1–2) and reversible. However, four patients (1.89% of the total) experienced severe irAEs, which manifested as cutaneous irAE, pneumonitis, immune-related peripheral neuropathy, and immune thrombocytopenia. Additionally, nine patients (4.23%) discontinued tislelizumab treatment due to the occurrence of irAEs. Furthermore, our detailed analysis revealed that factors such as cardiovascular disease, urea levels below the lower limit, and thyrotropin levels above the upper limit were significantly associated with a higher risk of developing thyroid dysfunction as an irAE. Additionally, a real-world study by Tan et al [25] identified female gender and a history of chemotherapy were independent risk factors for ICIs-related thyroid injury. Consequently, patients should be thoroughly evaluated for their medical history, particularly thyroid function indicators, prior to initiating treatment.

In our study, we identified several common characteristics. The second most prevalent trAE observed was liver injury, followed by renal injury. The impairment of liver and renal function aligns with findings from previous studies. Liver injury was primarily indicated by elevated levels of AST, ALT, GGT, and ALP, with most cases resolving after the administration of hepatoprotective drugs. Multivariate analysis revealed that decreased serum creatinine levels were associated with an increased risk of hepatic injury. Tumor-related conditions are debilitating, and it is essential for patients with lower serum creatinine levels to adjust their diet to improve nutritional intake prior to initiating medication.

Renal injury was predominantly evidenced by reduced eGFR, with sq-NSCLC and current smoking status prior to treatment being associated with a heightened risk of renal impairment. Studies have demonstrated that the development of electrolyte disorders is a manifestation of kidney toxicity, with hyponatremia being the most commonly reported. ICIs can induce hyponatremia through various mechanisms, with the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis being the most frequent [29]. Manohar et al [30] conducted a meta-analysis that included 39 RCTs involving approximately 4300 patients receiving PD-1 inhibitors for various malignancies, revealing a pooled incidence of hyponatremia of only 1%. Cantini et al [31] performed a meta-analysis of 6 RCTs comparing ICIs with standard chemotherapy for advanced NSCLC and reported that hyponatremia occurred in 9%. In contrast, Seethapathy et al [32] conducted a retrospective study involving 2459 patients from a single cancer center receiving ICIs and observed an exceptionally high rate (62%) of hyponatremia, with 6% experiencing severe hyponatremia. In our research, 21.22% of patients experienced hyponatremia, whereas 0.94% experienced severe hyponatremia. These “real-world data” suggest that hyponatremia associated with ICIs is much more common than indicated by RCTs. Our multivariate analysis revealed several factors associated with an increased risk, including sq-NSCLC, decreased levels of GGT, creatinine, and eGFR. These findings suggest that certain risk factors, such as decreased levels of GGT, creatinine, and eGFR, can be managed pre-treatment to mitigate the potential risk of electrolyte abnormalities. Early recognition of risk factors may assist healthcare providers in managing the mechanistically diverse and novel electrolyte disorders associated with immunotherapy.

This study provides valuable insights into the safety profile of tislelizumab in a real-world setting; however, several important limitations should be acknowledged. First, the single-center, retrospective design inherently restricts the generalizability of our findings. The patient population, treatment patterns, and clinical management practices at our institution may not fully represent other healthcare settings, thereby potentially affecting the external validity of the results. Future multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these observations and to assess the broader applicability of our findings across diverse clinical contexts. Second, the absence of a dedicated comparator arm (e.g., chemotherapy alone) limits the ability to conclusively attribute the observed trAEs solely to tislelizumab. Although treatment regimens were adjusted for in multivariate analyses, residual confounding likely remains. Baseline toxicities from prior or concurrent chemotherapy, particularly platinum-based regimens, may have influenced the recorded adverse event profiles, complicating the isolation of tislelizumab-specific effects from its chemotherapy backbone. A well-controlled, randomized study directly comparing tislelizumab with chemotherapy would more definitively clarify tislelizumab’s contribution to adverse events. Third, significant clinical heterogeneity—encompassing lung cancer histological subtypes, lines of therapy, and specific combination regimens—is an inherent feature of this real-world cohort. While this reflects actual clinical practice, it introduces unmeasured confounding and complicates the interpretation of risk associations. For instance, the variability in cancer subtypes (e.g., squamous cell carcinoma vs adenocarcinoma) and the different stages of therapy could lead to variation in response and adverse event profiles. Therefore, future studies with more homogeneous patient populations are needed to further clarify these associations. Fourth, the absence of PD-L1 expression data for most patients (76.89%) further limits the stratification of results based on biomarker status and the exploration of its association with trAEs. Fifth, the data cut-off was February 2023, which may limit the reflection of more recent clinical practice. Although pre-specified to ensure adequate follow-up, updated cohorts are needed to confirm the current relevance of these findings. Finally, although this single-center study provides meaningful real-world evidence, the sample size imposes inherent limitations. The overall cohort size may constrain the statistical power to detect less frequent AEs or weaker risk associations. This limitation is particularly pronounced in analyses of small subgroups or rare variables, such as PD-1 inhibitor therapy in the last cycle (n = 6), decreased GGT levels (n = 1), and elevated free T3 levels (n = 4), which reduce statistical robustness and increase the risk of unstable or biased estimates. For instance, the OR for decreased GGT levels was exceptionally high (138.65) but derived from a single sample, rendering it unreliable. These findings underscore the exploratory nature of the observed safety signals and associated risk factors, which warrant validation in larger, prospective cohorts to confirm their reliability.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this real-world study highlights the safety profile of tislelizumab-based regimens in lung cancer, emphasizing the importance of identifying high-risk populations for early intervention. Combination regimens were associated with higher incidences of trAEs, particularly hematological toxicities. Tislelizumab monotherapy showed a numerically lower incidence of trAEs; however, this observation is derived from a small subgroup and should be interpreted with caution. Given the limitations of this study, the identified risk factors require further validation. Future prospective studies with larger, homogeneous cohorts, multicenter designs, comparator arms, and standardized biomarker collection are essential to substantiate these findings and enhance understanding of the safety profiles of tislelizumab across broader patient populations.

Cosmological Probes of Lepton Parity Freeze-in Dark Matter: Δ ​ N eff \Delta N_{\rm eff} & Gravitational Waves
Ernest Ma, Partha Kumar Paul, Narendra Sahu

Abstract
In the canonical type-I seesaw mechanism for neutrino masses, a residual symmetry known as lepton parity: ( − 1 ) L (-1)^{L} , remains preserved. Introducing a Majorana fermion S S with even lepton parity renders it naturally stable, making it a viable dark matter (DM) candidate. The addition of a lepton parity odd singlet scalar σ \sigma allows for the coupling N ​ S ​ σ NS\sigma , where N N is the right-handed neutrino. If S S is not thermalized, then DM relic can be produced in two distinct ways: (i) for reheating temperature, T rh > m N T_{\rm rh}>m_{N} , dominantly through the decay of N N ( N → S ​ σ N\rightarrow S\sigma ), and (ii) for T EW < T rh ≪ m N T_{\rm EW}<T_{\rm rh}\ll m_{N} , via standard model Higgs ( h h ) decay ( h → S ​ S h\rightarrow SS at one loop). If the σ − h \sigma-h quartic coupling is large, then it can lead to a strong first-order electroweak phase transition even if ⟨ σ ⟩ = 0 \langle\sigma\rangle=0 . Alternatively, if σ − h \sigma-h coupling is small, then σ \sigma can freeze out with a larger abundance, and hence its decay ( σ → S ​ ν \sigma\rightarrow S\nu ) at late epochs can give rise to additional relativistic degrees of freedom ( Δ ​ N eff \Delta{N}_{\rm eff} ). Thus, the framework gives a viable DM with mass range varying from MeV to TeV and leaves observable imprints, via gravitational waves and Δ ​ N eff \Delta{N}_{\rm eff} , which offer complementary probes, potentially detectable in future gravitational wave and CMB experiments.

II
Minimal model

In the minimal type-I seesaw framework, lepton number is explicitly broken down to a residual lepton parity symmetry
(-1)^{L}
. By construction, the SM leptons are odd under this residual lepton parity, and we choose the RHN,
N

S

S
a natural DM candidate. To enable its production in the early Universe, we further introduce a singlet scalar,
\sigma

m_{N}>m_{\sigma}>m_{S}

Case-ii)

In this scenario,
N_{1}

As discussed in the previous paragraph, this scenario also may yield distinctive signatures testable in future CMB and GW experiments. Similar to the above discussion, the contribution of
\sigma
decay to DM relic remains negligible.

We will study the phenomenologies of these two scenarios: case-
i

V
Conclusion

In this study, we have explored the realization of freeze-in dark matter within a minimal extension of the type-I seesaw framework, where the remnant lepton-parity symmetry naturally plays the role of a dark parity. By introducing a lepton parity even singlet fermion
S
and a lepton parity odd scalar
\sigma
, the setup naturally accommodates a stable dark matter candidate. The
CP
-violating, out-of-equilibrium decays of nearly degenerate right-handed neutrinos (
N
) generate a lepton asymmetry via flavored resonant leptogenesis, which is subsequently converted into the observed baryon asymmetry through electroweak sphaleron processes. The fermion
S
acts as a natural freeze-in dark matter candidate, with its production determined by the thermal history of the early Universe. For reheating temperatures,
T_{\rm rh}>m_{N_{1}}
, the dominant contribution to the DM relic density originates from right-handed neutrino decays, while for
T_{\rm EW}<T_{\rm rh}\ll m_{N_{1}}
, it can be produced via Higgs decay. Direct and indirect detection constraints on DM are naturally avoided due to the extremely small Yukawa couplings. However, this scenario can leave observable imprints through gravitational waves and at CMB experiments. When the
h-\sigma
coupling is larger (typically
\lambda_{h\sigma}>1
), then the EWPT can be first-order and gives rise to stochastic gravitational waves which can be detected at different GW experiments like DECIGO, BBO, etc. On the other hand if
h-\sigma
coupling is small (typically
\lambda_{h\sigma}\ll 1
), then the late decay of
\sigma
can give rise observable
\Delta{N}_{\rm eff}
. In conclusion, our model provides a scenario connecting dark matter production, leptogenesis,
\Delta{N}_{\rm eff}
, and electroweak symmetry breaking dynamics in a unified and testable framework.

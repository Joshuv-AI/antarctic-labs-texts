The Reduction Theorem of

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
G

This formula is more useful when

\Xi_{n}
-oriented. If

E_{\infty}

\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}E\simeq(E\otimes\Xi_{n})\underset{E\otimes A}{\otimes}E.

When

When

-graded homotopy of

\pi_{\ast\rho_{2}}^{C_{2}}\big(H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes\Xi_{n}\big)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[\bar{m}_{1},\gamma\bar{m}_{1},\dots].

This is essentially the way we understand the classes

. In practice, we find that we have to compute an
{\underline{\RO}}
-graded
\Tor
group for a complicated map of equivariant algebras. Unpacking this, we will show the following.

Theorem 1.2

We have an isomorphism of
\pi_{{\underline{\star}}}H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
-modules

{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}}_{{\underline{\star}}}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\cong\Tor^{-{\underline{\star}}}_{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2{\underline{\star}}}[G\cdot\bar{r}_{1},\dots]}\big(H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2{\underline{\star}}}[G\cdot\bar{m}_{1},\dots],H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2{\underline{\star}}}\big).

To simplify the computation, we introduce an intermediate object, the

-relative dual Steenrod algebra. Since

-commutative ring spectrum, there is a

Working over

For any

-algebra

-free, splitting as a wedge of

-modules of the form

for various subgroups

Specializing to the case

Theorem 1.4
.

We have a weak equivalence

H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\simeq H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\left(S^{1}\times\prod_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\Map^{C_{2}}\big(G,S^{i\rho_{2}+1}\big)\right).

Let

Given a representation

Let

The wildcard for integer gradings will be an asterisk. For

2.
Twisted monoid rings and the Reduction Theorem

The proof of Theorem
1.3
uses the Reduction Theorem of
[
9
]
in its guise as a presentation of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
as a
\Xi_{n}
-module. We recall and recast some of the necessary pieces here.

2.1.
Free associative algebras

Recall that the infinite suspension functor is a strong symmetric monoidal functor, taking the Cartesian product of
G
-CW complexes to the smash product of genuine
G
-spectra. In particular, we have a variant of the Snaith splitting.

Definition 2.1
.

If
X
is a
G
-CW spectrum, then let

\mathbb{S}^{0}[X]=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}X^{\otimes i}

be the free associative algebra on
X
.

Lemma 2.2
.

The free associative algebra on a pointed, simply connected
G
-space
X
is given by

\mathbb{S}^{0}[\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X]=\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega\Sigma X.

Since the infinite suspension is a strong symmetric monoidal functor from spaces with the Cartesian product to spectra, we also have a related formula for smash products of free associative algebras on suspension spectra of spaces.

Lemma 2.3
.

The smash product of the free associative algebras on
X
and on
Y
is given by

\mathbb{S}^{0}[\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X]\otimes\mathbb{S}^{0}[\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y]\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega\big(\Sigma X\times\Sigma Y\big),

as associative algebras.

This approach gives us a way to interpret the smash products over a free associative algebra (or the smash products thereof).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a path connected
G
-CW complex. We have a natural weak equivalence

\mathbb{S}^{0}\underset{\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega X}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0}\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X.

Corollary 2.5
.

For any spaces
X
and
Y
, we have

\mathbb{S}^{0}\underset{\mathbb{S}^{0}[\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X]\otimes\mathbb{S}^{0}[\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}Y]}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0}\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\big(\Sigma X\times\Sigma Y\big),

and similar for arbitary smash products.

The infinite suspension functor is actually a
G
-symmetric monoidal functor, taking coinduction in
G
-spaces to the norm.

Proposition 2.6
.

For any
H
-space
X
, we have a natural weak equivalence

N_{H}^{G}\big(\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X\big)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Map^{H}(G,X).

This gives a twisted version of Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The norm of the free associative algebra on a pointed
H
-space
X
is given by

N_{H}^{G}\big(\mathbb{S}^{0}[\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X]\big)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Map^{H}(G,\Omega\Sigma X)\simeq\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Omega\Map^{H}(G,\Sigma X).

Proof.

The first equivalence is the composite of Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.6
. The weak equivalence is just commuting the two right adjoints in
G
-spaces.
∎

2.2.
Twisted monoid rings and
\Xi_{n}

Given a
G
-commutative ring spectrum
R
and an element in the
H
-equivariant homotopy

\bar{x}\in\pi_{V}^{H}(R)

for some (virtual)
H
-representation
V
, we can build a map of associative algebras:

\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{V}]\to i_{H}^{\ast}R.

Since
R
is a
G
-commutative monoid, we can then norm this up to
G
and compose with the counit of the norm-forget adjunction, getting a map of associative rings

N_{H}^{G}\big(\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{V}]\big)\to N_{H}^{G}i_{H}^{\ast}R\to R.

Given multiple elements, we can tensor these algebras together. Our analysis hinges on the fact that we can understand the source algebra as the suspension spectrum of a space, and that for
R=\Xi_{n}
, we have well-behaved generators.

To state this, let
\mathbb{Z}_{-}
denote the integral sign representation of
C_{2}
.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
9
, Proposition 5.27, Lemma 5.33]
)
.

There are classes

\bar{r}_{i}\colon S^{i\rho_{2}}\to i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}\Xi_{n}

such that the induced map of commutative rings

\bigotimes_{i\geq 1}\Sym\big(\Ind_{C_{2}}^{G}({\mathbb{Z}}_{-})^{\otimes i}\big)\xrightarrow{\otimes\ind\bar{r}_{i}}{\underline{\pi}}_{\ast\rho_{2}}(\Xi_{n})(C_{2}/C_{2})

is an isomorphism.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
9
, Reduction Theorem]
)
.

Thom reduction gives a weak equivalence of
\Xi_{n}
-modules

\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0}\simeq H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}.

3.
{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
-homology via
\Xi_{n}

3.1.
General Formula

The Reduction Theorem immediately gives a way to describe
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
smashed with any
G
-spectrum.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
G
-spectrum
E
, we have an equivalence of
\Xi_{n}
-modules

H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes E\simeq\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}E,

where
E
is viewed as an
A
-module via the augmentation map
A\to\mathbb{S}^{0}
.

Proof.

This is the shuffle equivalence

H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes E\simeq(\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0})\otimes E\simeq\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}E.\qed

If
E
is an
E_{\infty}
monoid or a
G
-commutative monoid, then this equivalence is one of
\Xi_{n}\otimes E
-modules. In this case, we can use the free-forget adjunction for
E
-modules now to rewrite this.

Corollary 3.2
.

If
E
is an
E_{\infty}
monoid, then we have an equivalence of
\Xi_{n}\otimes E
-modules

H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes E\simeq(E\otimes\Xi_{n})\underset{E\otimes A}{\otimes}E.

Remark 3.3
.

Since
A
is a wedge of spectra of the form
G_{+}\underset{H}{\otimes}S^{V}
, the associative ring spectrum
E\otimes A
is always a free
E
-module.

Remark 3.4
.

This applies even in the case
E=H{\underline{A}}
, the Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum for the Burnside Mackey functor and hence by base-change, for any Eilenberg-Mac Lane spectrum.

3.2.
\Xi_{n}
-orientable case

When
E
is
\Xi_{n}
-orientable, the Thom isomorphism allows us to rewrite the first smash factor.

Proposition 3.5
.

Given a
\Xi_{n}
-orientable ring spectrum
E
, we have an equivalence of left
E
-modules

E\otimes\Xi_{n}\simeq E\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Map^{C_{2}}(G,BU_{{\mathbb{R}}}).

This has free
E
-homology, essentially for the same reasons as the classical cases. A
\Xi_{n}
-orientation gives a Real orientation of
i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}E
by restriction and the unit of the norm-forget adjunction:

MU_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}\Xi_{n}.

This allows us to easily identity the
i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}E
-homology of
BU_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and the
E
-homology of the coinduced
BU_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
10
]
)
.

There are classes

\bar{m}_{i}\in\pi_{i\rho_{2}}^{C_{2}}\big(\Xi_{n}\otimes\Map^{C_{2}}(G,BU_{{\mathbb{R}}})\big)

such that the induced map

\Xi_{n}\otimes\Big(\bigotimes_{i\geq 1}N_{C_{2}}^{G}\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{i\rho_{2}}]\Big)\to\Xi_{n}\otimes\Map^{C_{2}}(G,BU_{{\mathbb{R}}})_{+}

is an equivalence of associative
\Xi_{n}
-algebras.

Base-changing along the map
\Xi_{n}\to E
and using the Thom isomorphism allows us to deduce the general case.

Corollary 3.7
.

For any
\Xi_{n}
-orientable
G
-spectrum
E
, the associative
E
-algebra map

E\otimes\Big(\bigotimes_{i\geq 1}N_{C_{2}}^{G}\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{i\rho_{2}}]\Big)\to E\otimes\Xi_{n}

is an equivalence.

Note that the associative
E
-algebra described is actually just
E\otimes A
. The induced
A
-algebra structure on
E\otimes\Xi_{n}
, however, is not the one coming from
\Xi_{n}
itself. Instead, the self-map in the homotopy category induced by

E\otimes A\xrightarrow{E\otimes\iota}E\otimes\Xi_{n}\xleftarrow{\simeq}E\otimes\Big(\bigotimes_{i\geq 1}N_{C_{2}}^{G}\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{i\rho_{2}}]\Big)

is not the identity, however. This
E
-algebra map is the one determined by the
C_{2}
-equivariant maps

S^{i\rho_{2}}\to i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}\Xi_{n}\to i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}(E\otimes\Xi_{n}).

In other words, the map from
E\otimes A
is picking out the
E
-based Hurewicz image in
E\otimes\Xi_{n}
.

Notation 3.8
.

When we want to stress that the algebra structure is this Hurewicz one, we will denote
A
by
A^{h}_{n}
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
E
be an
E_{\infty}
-monoid, and assume
E
is
\Xi_{n}
-oriented. Then we have an Adams-graded spectral sequence with
E_{2}
-term

E_{2}^{s,V}=\Tor_{E_{{\underline{\star}}}[G\cdot\bar{r}_{1},\dots]}^{-s,V}\big(E_{{\underline{\star}}}[G\cdot\bar{m}_{1},G\cdot\bar{m}_{2},\dots],{E_{\underline{\star}}}\big)

converging to
\pi_{V-s}\big(H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes E\big)
.

Proof.

This is the Künneth spectral sequence associated to writing
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes E
as a balanced smash product:

H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes E\simeq(E\otimes\Xi_{n})\underset{E\otimes A}{\otimes}E.

Corollary
3.7
identifies the homotopy of the first smash factor. The homotopy of the ground ring is the definition of the ring over which we are taking
\Tor
. The result follows.
∎

In the case that
E=H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
, then we are working in the category of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
-modules which is the derived category of
{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
-modules
[
14
]
. In this case, the Eilenberg-Mac Lane functor is [derived] strong symmetric monoidal, and the Künneth spectral sequence here collapses. For
n\geq 2
, this is a
\Tor
-computation in an abelian category with which we have little familiarity. For
n=1
, however, we deduce the additive dual Steenrod algebra with easy, since the Künneth spectral sequence is just a form of the Lewis–Mandell
RO(C_{2})
-graded one.

Corollary 3.10
.

We have an isomorphism of
RO(C_{2})
-graded modules

\mathcal{A}_{\star}\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{x}_{1},\dots]\underset{\mathbb{M}_{2}}{\otimes}E_{\mathbb{M}_{2}}(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\dots),

where
|\bar{\xi}_{n}|=|\bar{v}_{n}|=(2^{n}-1)\rho_{2}
, and
|\tau_{m}|=|\bar{v}_{m}|+1=2^{n-1}+(2^{n-1}-1)\varsigma.

Proof.

The Hurewicz map takes the form

\bar{r}_{i}\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{m}_{i}&i\neq 2^{n}-1\forall n\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

This gives the desired result, where the elements
\bar{\xi}_{n}
are represented by
\bar{m}_{2^{n}-1}
and where
\bar{\tau}_{m}
is represented by the homology suspension of
\bar{r}_{2^{m}-1}
.
∎

4.
The relative dual Steenrod algebra: additive structure

4.1.
The relative smash product with
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}

Using the presentation of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
as a
\Xi_{n}
-module, we can also compute the various relative smash products of
G
-spectra with
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Given any
\Xi_{n}
-module
E
, we have equivalences

E\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\simeq E\underset{A}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0},

where
E
is an
A
-module via pulling back along
A\to\Xi_{n}
.

Proof.

This is the change-of-rings isomorphism. We have natural equivalences

E\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\simeq E\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}(\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0})\simeq E\underset{A}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0}.\qed

In the case that
E=H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
itself, then we can more easily further rewrite this, since the
A
-module structure is necessarily trivial.

Theorem 4.2
.

There is an equivalence of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
-modules

H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\simeq H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\Map^{C_{2}}(G,S^{i\rho_{2}+1})\right).

Proof.

Proposition
4.1
gives an equivalence of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
-modules

H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\simeq B(H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}},A,\mathbb{S}^{0}).

The map
\Xi_{n}\to H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
is a ring map, and hence the module structure is induced from the associative ring map

A\to H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}.

Since
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
is a zero-slice and since all of the summands of
A
beyond the copy of
\mathbb{S}^{0}
are slice positive, any map

A\to H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}

factors as

A\to\mathbb{S}^{0}\to P^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{0})\to H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}.

In particular, we have a weak equivalence

Not only is the smash square of

in the category of

5.
The relative dual Steenrod algebra: multiplicative structure

5.1.
Operadic Digression

Since
H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
is a commutative ring spectrum and since the Thom reduction

\Xi_{n}\to H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}

is a commutative ring map, the relative smash square is an equivariant commutative ring spectrum. We have described the additive structure, but the method used does not give explicit control over the multiplicative structure.

For this, we use an equivariant version of a beautiful result of Hahn–Wilson.

Theorem 5.1

(Hahn–Wilson)
.

The free
E_{1}
-algebra on a regular representation sphere

\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{k\rho_{2}}]

admits an
E_{\rho}
-multiplication.

Given an
E_{\rho}
-algebra
R
,
the obstructions to extending an
E_{1}
-map out of
\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{k\rho_{2}}]
to an
E_{\rho}
-map lie in the homotopy groups
\pi_{m\rho_{2}-1}R
. The augmentation map

\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{k\rho_{2}}]\to\mathbb{S}^{0}

is
E_{\rho}
.

For
\Xi_{n}
(and any of the regular quotients we normally consider), these homotopy groups are all zero.

Corollary 5.2
.

For any element

\bar{r}_{i}\colon S^{i\rho_{2}}\to i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}\Xi_{n},

the induced map of associative rings

\mathbb{S}^{0}[S^{i\rho_{2}}]\to i_{C_{2}}^{\ast}\Xi_{n}

extends to an
E_{\rho_{2}}
-algebra map.

The norm of an
E_{\rho_{2}}
-algebra is automatically an algebra over the coinduced operad

\Map^{C_{2}}(G,E_{\rho_{2}}).

Work of Szczesny shows that a
\Map^{C_{2}}(G,E_{\rho_{2}})
-algebra have

(1)

an associative multiplication that is underlying
E_{2}
,

(2)

norm maps
C_{2}\to G
(from the
E_{\sigma}
factor of
E_{\rho}
), and

(3)

norm maps
\{e\}\to G
(from the
E_{1}
factor of
E_{\rho}
).

The quotient map

\Xi_{n}\to\Xi_{n}\underset{A}{\otimes}\mathbb{S}^{0}

therefore inherits norms from
C_{2}
to any intermediate group. Put another ways, the classes that look like norms in that they are carried by induced spheres of the approrpriate dimension and come from the norm-like cells in a coinduced sphere are actually norm classes from the underlying
C_{2}
-representation sphere. Making this precise, however, requires us to decompose our operad

\Map^{C_{2}}(G,E_{\rho})\simeq E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{O},

and we do not yet have such a decomposition. In light of this, we provide an additional, more direct argument using induction on the subgroup lattice.

6.
The absolute case

The relative homology fits into a pushout square of equivariant commutative ring spectra

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes\Xi_{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}.}}

We have complete computational control over all but the top right term in this square. The Greenlees–Serre spectral sequence provides a way to leverage these to get the fourth.

6.1.
An equivariant Greenlees–Serre spectral sequence

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
f\colon A\to R
be a map of equivariant commutative ring spectra with
A
connective, let
p\colon A\to P^{0}(A)
be the zeroth slice section map, and let
Q
be the equivariant commutative ring spectrum defined by the pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P^{0}(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Q.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Let
Gr(A)
be the slice associated graded of
A
, viewed as a commutative
P^{0}(A)
-algebra. Then we have a spectral sequence of graded Tambara functors with

E_{2}^{(s,{\underline{t}})}=\pi_{{\underline{t}}-s}\big(Q\underset{P^{0}(A)}{\otimes}Gr_{\dim{\underline{t}}}(A)\big)\Rightarrow\pi_{{\underline{t}}-s}(R).

The differentials satisfy

|d_{r}|=(-1,r).

When
R
is also
0
-connective, this converges strongly.

Proof.

Consider the slice filtration of
A
,
P_{k}(A)
, given by the slice covers of
A
. Since
A
is
0
-connective, the natural map to the slice truncation

A\to P^{k}A

is always a map of
G
-commutative
A
-algebras, and hence the filtration
P_{k}(A)
is actually a filtration in
A
-modules. This filtration is Hausdorff, in the sense that

\lim_{\longleftarrow}P_{k}(A)\simeq 0,

and of course,

A=P_{0}A.

This gives a filtration of

R\simeq R\underset{A}{\otimes}P_{0}(A),

by taking

F_{k}(R)=R\underset{A}{\otimes}P_{k}(A).

The
k
th associated graded piece is

R\underset{A}{\otimes}P^{k}_{k}(A).

By construction, the
A
-action on
P^{k}_{k}(A)
factors through
P^{0}(A)
. In other words, we have an equivalence

R\underset{A}{\otimes}P^{k}_{k}(A)\simeq R\underset{A}{\otimes}P^{0}(A)\underset{P^{0}(A)}{\otimes}P^{k}_{k}(A).

This means that the associated graded for
R
can be rewritten as

\big(R\underset{A}{\otimes}P^{0}(A)\big)\underset{P^{0}(A)}{\otimes}P^{\ast}_{\ast}(A)=Q\underset{P^{0}(A)}{\otimes}P^{\ast}_{\ast}(A).

Since the slice filtration of a
G
-commutative monoid is naturally a
G
-commutative monoid in filtered
G
-spectra, and since all maps considered are
G
-commutative ring maps, the induced filtration on
R
is a
G
-commutative monoid in filtered spectra. The spectral sequence is therefore a spectral sequence of graded Tambara functors.

For convergence, we start with the observation that the connectivity of
P_{k}A
goes to infinity with
k
(at worst as about
k/|G|
). If
R
is connective, then the Künneth spectral sequence shows that the connectivity of
R\underset{A}{\otimes}P_{k}A
also goes to infinity, or equivalent, for any
n
and for all
k>\!>0
,

{\underline{\pi}}_{n}\big(R\underset{A}{\otimes}P_{k}A\big)=0.

This gives strong convergence.
∎

There is a Künneth spectral sequence that allows us to compute the
E_{2}
-term of this Greenlees–Serre spectral sequence, due to Lewis–Mandell
[
13
]
. We have

E_{2}^{V,s}=\Tor^{-s}_{\pi_{\underline{\star}}P^{0}(A)}\big(\pi_{\underline{\star}}Q,\pi_{\underline{\star}}Gr_{k}(A)\big)_{V}\Rightarrow\pi_{V-s}\big(Q\underset{P^{0}(A)}{\otimes}Gr_{k}(A)\big).

In our case, however, things are greatly simplified. Since
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
is
\Xi_{n}
-oriented, Corollary
3.7
shows that we have an equivalence of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
-associative algebras

H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes A_{n}^{h}\simeq H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}\otimes\Xi_{n}.

This is a wedge of slice spheres tensored with
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
, and hence is a wedge of slices, so it is its own slice associated graded.

Proposition 6.2
.

The zero slice of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes\Xi_{n}
is
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
, and the slice associated graded is

H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes A^{h}_{n},

where the grading on

is the usual monomial grading.

For us, the key fact is that this is a free
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
-module. In particular, smashing this over
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
is easy to understand.

Proposition 6.3
.

For any
0
-connective
(H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes\Xi_{n})
-algebra
R
, the associated graded for

is

\big(R\underset{H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\big)\otimes A^{h}_{n}.

The Greenlees–Serre associated graded for

is

\pi_{{\underline{\star}}}\big(H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{\Xi_{n}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\big)[G\cdot\bar{m}_{1},\dots].

6.2.
Proof of concept: the
C_{2}
Steenrod Algebra

Using the Greenlees–Serre spectral sequence, we can give a new approach to the
C_{2}
-equivariant dual Steenrod algebra, complementing the foundational work of Hu–Kriz
[
11
]
. We begin with some easily computational corollaries from our previous computations. We work here over
BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
to simplify our computations; work of Roytman showed this is
E_{2\rho}
.

First note that for
C_{2}
, our decomposition underlying the relatie smash product is

H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\simeq H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}S^{(2^{i}-1)\rho_{2}+1}\right).

When we decompose the Cartesian product, this is just a wedge of ordinary
C_{2}
-representation spheres. Put another way, a basis for the
RO
-graded homology occurs all in level
C_{2}
, so we may without loss of generality work instead with an
RO(C_{2})
-grading.

Proposition 6.5
.

The
BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-relative homology
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
is

\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{\tau}_{1},\dots]/(\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}-a_{\sigma}\bar{\tau}_{i+1},\forall i\geq 1),

where the degree of
\bar{\tau}_{i}
is
\big((2^{i}-1)\rho_{2}+1\big)
.

Proof.

The only thing to check is the multiplicative relation. Passing to geometric fixed points, we have as a ring

\Phi^{C_{2}}\big(H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}\big)\simeq H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[b]\underset{H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}}{\otimes}H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[{b^{\prime}}^{2}],

where
b
and
b^{\prime}
have degree
1
. The geometric fixed points of the class
\bar{\tau}_{i}
is in degree
2^{i}
, so we see that all of the classes
\bar{\tau}_{i}
must have multiplicative relations that rebuild a polynomial ring. By considering the bidegrees, the only possible relations are that the ring structure is as listed.
∎

There is also a multiplicative relation when we reduce modulo
2
. The proof is essentially identical.

Proposition 6.6
.

The
BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-relative homology
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\underset{BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}}{\otimes}H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}
is

\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{\tau}_{0},\dots]/(\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}-a_{\sigma}\bar{\tau}_{i+1},\forall i\geq 0),

where the degree of
\bar{\tau}_{i}
is
\big((2^{i}-1)\rho_{2}+1\big)
.

Similarly, the homology of
BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is

H_{\star}BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{\xi}_{1},\dots],

where
\bar{\xi}_{i}
is in degree
(2^{i}-1)\rho_{2}
. This gives us the needed input for the Greenlees spectral sequence.

Theorem 6.7
.

The Greenlees–Serre spectral sequence computing
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes H{\underline{{\mathbb{Z}}}}
collapses at

E_{2}=\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{\xi}_{1},\dots]\underset{\mathbb{M}_{2}}{\otimes}\mathbb{M}_{2}[\bar{\tau}_{1},\dots]/\bar{\tau}_{i}^{2}-a_{\sigma}\bar{\tau}_{i+1},

where

\mathbb{M}_{2}=\pi_{\star}H{\mathbb{F}}_{2}

is the
RO(C_{2})
-graded homology of a point. There are no additive extensions.

Proof.

The classes
\bar{\xi}_{i}
all come from the homotopy of
H{\underline{{\mathbb{F}}}}_{2}\otimes BP_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, and hence are necessarily permanent cycles. Since we are computing Bredon homology, everything in
\mathbb{M}_{2}
is also a permanent cycle. The only classes which might not be permanent cycles are the classes
\bar{\tau}_{i}
for
i\geq 1
, so we must show only that these all survive the spectral sequence. We do this by induction on
i
.

The base case,
i=1
, is handled by noting that the Hurewicz image of
\bar{v}_{1}
is zero. For degree reasons, the only possible target of the differential on
\bar{\tau}_{1}
is a multiple of
\bar{\xi_{1}}
, but we deduce this must be zero.

Assume by induction that all
\bar{\tau}_{i}
for
i<k
are permanent cycles. This means that
\bar{\tau}_{k-1}^{2}=a_{\sigma}\bar{\tau}_{k}
is a permanent cycle. However, up until this point, the spectral sequence is a sum of free
\mathbb{M}_{2}
-modules and the possible targets of any differential on
\bar{\tau}_{k}
are
a_{\sigma}
-torsion free, we deduce that
\bar{\tau}_{k}
must also be a permanent cycle.

Since
E_{2}=E_{\infty}
is a free module over
\mathbb{M}_{2}
, there are no additive extensions.
∎

As Hu–Kriz show, there are non-trivial multiplicative extensions.

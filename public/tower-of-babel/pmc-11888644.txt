Comparative Analysis of Alkaline Phosphatase Levels in Gingival Crevicular Fluid: A Three-Time Point Study Comparing Clear Aligners and Fixed Orthodontic Appliances
Authors: Manish Pisarla, Ravi Rathod, Rajivi Kalpakuri, P Anoop Kumar, B Nishanth, Akshay Goje, Tejaswi Kala
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAim:To evaluate the alkaline phosphatase level in gingival crevicular fluid (GCF) at three time points using clear aligners and fixed orthodontic appliances.Materials and Methods:A prospective randomized controlled trial was conducted in a tertiary care hospital to treat patients with 4–6 mm crowding in their upper front teeth. The study involved 20 patients, with 10 assigned to each group. Eligible patients had fully erupted permanent teeth, moderate crowding, and excellent dental health. The study lasted for 14 days and involved thermoplastic trays, Gemini metal brackets, and micropipettes.Results:This clinical study analyzed 20 participants, comprising 11 females and 9 males, with an average age of 25.23 ± 3.56 years. The data showed no significant differences in alkaline phosphatase (ALP) levels between the fixed orthodontic appliance and clear aligner groups. However, there was a notable increase in ALP levels in the crevicular fluid across both groups. The clear aligner group had significantly higher ALP levels at time points T1 and T2.Conclusion:Clear aligners may facilitate faster tooth movement due to elevated crevicular ALP levels, although robust scientific evidence is limited. The light force exerted by clear aligners may not significantly impact the overall rate of tooth movement.

INTRODUCTION

In 1946, Kesling proposed the use of a transparent orthodontic device to make minor adjustments in orthodontic treatment.[1] In 1998, align technology introduced a range of clear aligners developed using computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD-CAM) techniques.

Clear aligners, a key component of clear aligner therapy (CAT), have advanced from correcting minor misalignments to treating complex tooth movements and various malocclusions. This significant evolution highlights the potential of CAT and fosters optimism for the future of orthodontics.

The demand for aesthetic treatment options has increased significantly, particularly in orthodontics. CAT is more aesthetically pleasing and comfortable than traditional fixed orthodontic appliances.[23] CAT applies intermittent orthodontic forces, which may result in a faster rate of orthodontic tooth movement (OTM) due to its light force application. Previous studies have suggested that the intermittent forces applied by clear aligners contribute to their effectiveness.[4]

Clear aligners have been found to be more effective than conventional fixed appliances for OTM due to the viscoelastic nature of the periodontium.[5] Intermittent orthodontic forces can induce OTM with minimal cellular damage.[6] However, the efficacy of clear aligners in achieving faster tooth movement remains uncertain, and further research is needed to validate these claims. The forces exerted by orthodontic appliances lead to changes in dental and paradental structures, which are essential for facilitating OTM.[5]

The initial application of orthodontic appliances triggers a biological response in the body, resulting in an immediate inflammatory reaction. This response is characterized by changes in the blood vessels of the periodontal ligament (PDL), specifically vasodilation, and involves the migration of leukocytes that produce various biochemical substances, including cytokines, growth factors, colony-stimulating factors, neurotransmitters, and arachidonic acid metabolites. These processes stimulate cellular growth and breakdown in the immediate area, leading to tissue accumulation and removal around the tooth.[5]

The action of these biochemical markers on bone modeling and remodeling is considered the standard method for evaluating the rate of tooth movement. Gingival crevicular fluid (GCF), an inflammatory fluid released in the crevicular area, contains various components from cells, tissues, and serum.[7] The components of GCF are recognized as significant diagnostic indicators.[8] GCF has also been utilized to identify markers of bone metabolism following orthodontic tooth movement.[9]

OTM involves a continuous and balanced process of bone turnover, characterized by bone deposition at tension sites and bone resorption at pressure sites.[10] Osteoblasts exhibit elevated ALP activity, with alterations in this enzyme in serum and bone used as indicators of bone metabolism in various diseases.[11]

Bone metabolism is linked to ALP and acid phosphatase (ACP), produced by osteoblasts and osteoclasts. ALP activity is notably higher in the PDL than in other connective tissues, with elevated levels observed in human GCF from individuals undergoing orthodontic treatment, suggesting biological activities during tooth movement.[11]

The concentration of ALP in human GCF correlates with the total duration of appliance use.[411] Castroflorio et al. (2017) reported promising findings, suggesting that ALP activity in GCF may be a valuable tool for monitoring orthodontic tissue response.[4] There is a lack of comprehensive literature on biomarker activity with aligners, and no previous study has compared ALP activity between aligners and fixed orthodontic appliances. This study aimed to evaluate ALP levels in GCF at three time points using clear aligners and fixed orthodontic appliances.

METHODOLOGY

This prospective comparative study was conducted in the Department of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopaedics at a tertiary care hospital. The sample consisted of patients with 4–6 mm crowding in their upper front teeth, treated using a protocol that did not involve tooth extraction. The study received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB).

The sample size was determined based on statistical analysis of a previous study, ensuring a power of 80%. A total of 20 patients were included in the study, with 10 patients in each group.

Patient selection method involved an orthodontist who was informed of the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The orthodontist then evaluated and selected patients who had sought orthodontic treatment.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: patients with a complete set of adult teeth, aged between 18 and 35 years, with moderate crowding in their upper front teeth and a Little’s irregularity index of 4–6 mm in the upper anterior teeth. Participants also had to have excellent dental health, diligent oral hygiene, and be compliant individuals willing to participate in the study.

The exclusion criteria included individuals with systemic disorders or bone abnormalities, those exhibiting symptoms of active gingivitis or periodontitis, patients with loss of periodontal attachment in the upper teeth, smokers, and individuals who had been prescribed steroids, bisphosphonates, or NSAIDs for an extended period.

The equipment used in the study included thermoplastic trays, Gemini metal brackets, nickel-titanium wire, Hirschmann micropipettes, 2 ml microfuge tubes, an ALP assay Kit, and a protease inhibitor cocktail.

The collection of GCF is a technique-sensitive treatment that requires meticulous isolation, adequate gingival fluid flow, and appropriate gingival sulcus depth. Following the establishment of adequate oral hygiene and plaque elimination, sample collection occurred at three intervals: T0 (before appliance implantation), T1 (after 7 days), and T2 (after 14 days). The seven-and fourteen-day intervals were selected as the former aligns with enzyme turnover time, and the latter coincides with the peak of bone growth. GCF samples were exclusively obtained from the distobuccal location of the upper canines on both sides. Prior to sample collection, the region was meticulously cleansed with a dry gauze pad using moderate pressure to prevent any potential inflammatory response. A micropipette was positioned at the gingival sulcus opening, and 2 μL of GCF was extracted. The gathered GCF was transferred to an Eppendorf tube containing 98 μL of phosphate-buffered saline (pH = 7.4) and 2 μL of protease inhibitor enzyme. The sample was subsequently stored at −40°C.

The study was a comparative study, with patients divided into two groups: Group 1 and Group 2. Group 1 underwent CAT, while Group 2 received treatment with fixed orthodontic appliances. The study duration was 14 days, equivalent to two weeks.

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Longitudinal variation in ALP levels was assessed using the independent t-test/Mann–Whitney t-test, and paired t-test/Wilcoxon signed-rank test for group differences. The plaque index, gingival index, and bleeding on probing (BOP) index were evaluated using independent and paired t-tests, with a significance level set at P < 0.05 and a confidence interval of 95%.

RESULTS

A total of 20 participants were enrolled in this clinical study, consisting of 11 females and nine males, with an average age of 25.23 ± 3.56 years.

The data distribution for ALP and periodontal parameters was determined to be parametric based on the results of the Shapiro–Wilk test. Consequently, an independent t-test was used to compare variables between different groups, while a paired t-test was employed to compare variables within the same group.

There was a notable difference in the levels of ALP between the fixed orthodontic appliance (FA) group and clear aligner (CA) group at T1 and T2. The initial ALP levels in GCF showed no significant differences between the two groups. No differences were observed in any periodontal parameters, including plaque index, gingival index, and bop scores, between the two groups at any time points.

The levels of ALP in the GCF of individuals treated with either a fixed orthodontic appliance or clear aligners were analyzed at three different time points. The levels of ALP were noticeably higher in the CA group at T1 and T2, as indicated by a P value of less than 0.05 as indicated by the independent t-test. Furthermore, the ALP levels reached their peak at T1 for both groups, with a P value less than 0.05, indicating statistical significance according to the paired t-test.

DISCUSSION

Clear aligners have gained popularity as a tool for orthodontic treatment. However, the biological reaction on bone metabolism and the resulting tooth movement with aligners differs from that with fixed appliances, as aligners apply intermittent light forces for tooth movement.[12] This study aimed to determine the changes in ALP levels, a marker of bone deposition, to indirectly assess the tooth movement rate with clear aligners and traditional fixed appliances. The study collected samples at three time points: before appliance insertion (T0), seven days after appliance insertion (T1), and 14 days after appliance insertion (T2). Participants with maxillary anterior crowding of 4–6 mm, who required nonextraction treatment, were included in the study to limit confounding factors.

Bone metabolism enzymes, such as acid phosphatase and alkaline phosphatase, are crucial markers of bone turnover and tooth movement. ALP, a biological marker of osteoblastic activity during bone deposition, is observed both during the early and late phases of tooth movement. However, there is contradictory evidence regarding ALP levels and their correlation to orthodontic treatment.[13] One study assessed ALP levels in the GCF following leveling and aligning movements using fixed appliances, with results that were not statistically significant.[14] In contrast, the present study found that ALP levels increased significantly from T0 to T1 and T2 in both groups, with a logarithmic increase at T1, peaking at T2 (14 days).

Orthodontic forces induce an inflammatory reaction in periodontal tissue, which leads to bone remodeling and OTM. GCF, an inflammatory exudate, provides valuable insights into underlying periodontal changes. The estimation of GCF biomarkers has been used in orthodontics to better understand bone metabolism. The role of OTM with fixed appliances has been well-documented, notably by Krishnan and Davidovitch.[15] However, there is limited literature on the biological reactions and tooth movement associated with aligners.

The present study is the first of its kind to compare ALP as a biomarker for bone metabolism between two groups —FA and CA—at three time points. GCF sample collection was conducted at 7 and 14 days, as peak ALP activity has been reported at these time points in several studies. Two studies have previously assessed ALP levels in GCF following leveling and aligning movements using fixed appliance. Rohaya et al. observed an increase in ALP activity at both the 1-week and 2-week marks, while Vijay et al. found an increase at Day 14. However, these findings were not statistically significant. In contrast, the present study found significant increases in ALP levels from T0 to T1 and T2 in both groups, with a logarithmic increase at T1, peaking at T2 (14 days).[1316]

All the aforementioned studies were split-mouth trials, where the contralateral tooth served as a control, and GCF samples were collected separately from the mesial site (tension zone) and distal site (compression zone).[17] ALP activity was observed to rise on both sides, with the tension zone exhibiting significantly higher ALP activity than the compression zone.

The amount of force applied plays a crucial role in determining the extent and pattern of tooth movement. In this study, ALP levels were significantly higher in the CA group compared to the FA group after 7 and 14 days of appliance delivery. Limitations of the study include a small sample size, a brief follow-up period (two weeks), and limited tooth movement. Further studies with larger sample sizes and measurements of additional clinical parameters, such as tooth movement and bone remodeling biomarkers, are needed.

CONCLUSION

Clear aligners are gaining popularity as an aesthetic alternative to traditional fixed orthodontic appliances. A recent study suggests that clear aligners may facilitate faster tooth movement due to elevated levels of crevicular alkaline phosphatase enzymes. The standard protocol for aligner changes has been reduced to biweekly intervals, with the force being reset every two weeks. This adjustment in treatment protocol results in in biomechanics that differ from those of conventional fixed labial appliances. The study offers insight into the rate of tooth movement using clear aligners compared to conventional fixed appliances. While clear aligners are often associated with faster tooth movement due to their light force application, scientific evidence to support this claim is still limited. Notably, this study is the first to examine variations in ALP levels in GCF following treatment with two different orthodontic force systems. The findings reveal that GCF levels were statistically higher in the CA group compared to the FA group during the initial phase of orthodontic treatment.

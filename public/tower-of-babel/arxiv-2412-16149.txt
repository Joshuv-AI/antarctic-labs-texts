The Classical Super-Phaserotation Infrared Triangle Classical Logarithmic Soft Theorem as Conservation Law in (scalar) QED
Sangmin Choi, Alok Laddha, Andrea Puhm

Abstract
The universality of the logarithmic soft photon theorem in four dimensions can be traced to an infinite-dimensional asymptotic symmetry which acts as a local phase rotation on matter as we have shown in [ 1 ] . Here we extend our earlier results for the charges associated to these superphaserotations to all orders in the coupling and prove that their conservation is exactly the classical logarithmic soft photon theorem discovered by Saha, Sahoo and Sen [ 2 ] . We furthermore generalize the formulae for the associated electromagnetic displacement memory and its tail from particles to scalar matter fields. This completes the classical superphaserotation infrared triangle.

Introduction and summary

A detailed understanding of asymptotic symmetries in gauge theories and gravity has been a topic of resurgent interest over the past decade. These symmetries imply universal soft factorization theorems that are common to all theories with the same gauge group but with arbitrary matter content and generic interactions. An example is the set of theories with
U(1)
gauge group where the photon is the massless gauge boson. In all such theories, scattering processes involving a low-energy photon obey Weinberg’s soft photon theorem whose universality (theory-independence) is a consequence of an (infinity) of conservation laws associated to
superphaserotations

Power-law soft photon theorems.

Scattering processes involving an external photon factorize when the momentum of the photon

is taken

The leading soft photon theorem is the statement that in the limit

\omega{\cal M}_{N+1}(\{p_{i}\};(\omega,q,\ell))={S}_{-1}(\{p_{i}\};(q,\ell)){\cal M}_{N}(\{p_{i}\}),

(1)

The leading soft photon factor

given by

S_{-1}=e\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{Q_{i}p^{\mu}_{i}\varepsilon_{\mu}}{q\cdot p_{i}}

(2)

is universal (theory-independent) as it only depends on the charges and momenta of the hard particles but not the details of the interactions. The leading soft theorem receives no corrections from loops.
There is a subleading soft photon theorem due to Low

(1+\omega\partial_{\omega}){\cal M}_{N+1}(\{p_{i}\};(\omega,q,\ell))={S}_{0}(\{p_{i}\};(q,\ell)){\cal M}_{N}(\{p_{i}\}),

(3)

with the subleading soft factor for minimally coupled scalar and spinor QED

S_{0}=-ie\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{Q_{i}q_{\mu}\varepsilon_{\nu}J_{i}^{\mu\nu}}{q\cdot p_{i}}.

(4)

Unlike (

14

In scalar QED it was shown

Conservation laws for superphaserotations.

To achieve a first-principles derivation of the classical logarithmic soft photon theorem from symmetries we need to construct finite superphaserotation charges and show that the associated conservation law implies the logarithmic soft theorem. We will work in the covariant phase space formalism where charges for asymptotic symmetries are extracted from the symplectic structure

\Omega_{i^{\pm}\cup{\cal I}^{\pm}}=\Omega^{\rm mat}_{i^{\pm}}+\Omega^{\rm rad}_{{\cal I}^{\pm}}.

(9)

which has matter and radiative contributions from, respectively, time-like and null infinity.
When one of the field variations is a superphaserotation, denoted here by
\delta_{\epsilon}
,
one obtains the variation of the charge from the symplectic structure

\Omega_{\,i^{\pm}\,\cup\,\cal I^{\pm}\,}(\delta,\delta_{\epsilon})=\delta Q_{\pm}.

(10)

Moreover, in
[
17
,
18

Q_{+}=Q_{-}

is shown to be equivalent to the tree-level subleading soft photon theorem.
In this paper, we revisit the derivation of the superphaserotation charges in four dimensions taking the infrared effects into account.

Due to the long-range nature of electromagnetic interactions asymptotic (charged) matter fields are not free but are ‘dressed’ by a Coulombic phase
[
19
]

\phi=e^{i{\Phi}}\phi_{\rm free}.

(11)

The dressing

is logarithmically divergent at early and late times and is determined, via the equations of motion in conjunction with the asymptotic fall-offs of the electromagnetic field which have power-law ‘tails’ at early and late times. These logs and tails lead to infrared divergences in the symplectic structure on the time-like and null boundaries which we regulate with an asymptotic cutoff
\Lambda^{-1}

From this we can then construct a regularized Noether charge

Q^{\Lambda}=\ln\Lambda^{-1}\,\left(Q^{(\ln)}_{H}+Q^{(\ln)}_{S}\right)+\left(Q^{(0)}_{H}+Q^{(0)}_{S}\right)+\dots

(12)

where the subscripts

The finite charge

Q^{(0)}\equiv Q^{(0)}_{H}+Q^{(0)}_{S}

(13)

Q^{(0)}_{+}=Q^{(0)}_{-},

(14)

which at the quantum level becomes the subleading tree-level soft theorem in scalar QED (

) with soft factor (

where infrared effects were ignored.
However, the charge (

On the other hand, the log charge

4
Superphaserotation symmetry

Now that we have identified the asymptotics of the fields at boundary arising from the long-range nature of the interactions, our next goal is to find a symmetry transformation acting on the fields that is consistent with these logs and tails.
We start by identifying a suitable symmetry parameter
\epsilon
in the transformation of the gauge field

on
{\mathcal{I}}^{+}

\delta{\cal A}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{\cal I^{\pm}},\quad\delta\phi=ie\epsilon^{i^{\pm}}\phi.

We will hitherto refer to the transformation (

In Lorenz gauge the superphaserotation parameter satisfies

\nabla^{2}\epsilon^{\cal I^{\pm}}=\nabla^{2}\epsilon^{i^{\pm}}=0.

(70)

We will now identify, consistent with long-range interactions, superphaserotations with parameters
\epsilon^{i^{+}}
on the time-like and
\epsilon^{{\mathcal{I}}^{+}}

The leading soft photon theorem can be recast as a Ward identity for ‘leading’ superphaserotations whose symmetry parameters at

of the

Superphaserotation on
{\mathcal{I}}^{+}
.

Our ansatz for the large-

5
Symplectic structure for scalar QED

Given the fall-offs of the matter and radiative gauge fields at time-like and null infinity, we are now in the position to compute the total symplectic structure of scalar QED,

\Omega_{i^{+}\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{+}}(\delta,\delta^{\prime})=\Omega^{\rm mat}_{i^{+}}(\delta,\delta^{\prime})+\Omega^{\rm rad}_{{\mathcal{I}}^{+}}(\delta,\delta^{\prime}).

We will then use the

Due to the divergent (at large

We choose a specific regularisation scheme and extract a superphaserotation charge from it,

\Omega_{i^{+}\cup\cal I^{+}}^{\rm ren}(\delta,\delta_{\epsilon})=\delta Q_{+}[\epsilon].

(84)

Rather remarkably, as we show, this charge is exact in the electromagnetic coupling

Our computation is done at

but an exactly analogous charge can be computed on the past boundary using antipodally identified superphaserotation generator.

We will then show that the corresponding conservation law is independent of infrared regularisation and is in fact equivalent to the classical log soft photon theorem.

6.3
Logarithmic soft theorem

Our goal is to identify a conservation law of charges derived from a first principles covariant phase space approach which is equivalent to the classical logarithmic soft photon theorem

\lim_{\omega\to 0}\partial_{\omega}\omega^{2}\partial_{\omega}{\cal M}_{N+1}=S^{(\ln\omega)}_{0,{\rm classical}}{\cal M}_{N}.

(136)

We expect the latter to be associated with the same divergent superphaserotations (
71
) and (
75
) as the tree-level subleading soft photon theorem but with an infrared-corrected Noether charge given by our logarithmic soft and hard charges (
118
) and (
100
). The soft log charge is given by

Q_{S,+}^{(\ln)}[{\epsilon}]=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S^{2}}d^{2}\hat{x}\,D^{2}\epsilon(\hat{x})\,\mathcal{J}^{(\ln)}_{+}

(137)

in terms of the ‘logarithmic soft photon current’

\mathcal{J}^{(\ln)}_{+}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}du\,\partial_{u}(u^{2}\partial_{u}D^{C}A_{C})\quad\text{with}\quad A_{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u\to\pm\infty}}{{=}}A^{(0),\pm}_{C}+\frac{1}{u}A^{(1),\pm}_{C}+O(\frac{1}{|u|^{1+\#}})

(138)

and
\#
any positive number, and is exact.
The hard log charge can be expressed as

Q^{(\ln)}_{H,+}[\bar{\epsilon}]=\int_{i^{+}}d^{3}y\,\bar{\epsilon}(y)\,\overset{4,\ln}{j_{\tau}}(y),

(139)

with the interacting current given

\overset{4,\ln}{j_{\tau}}=-\frac{e^{2}m}{2(2\pi)^{3}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}\Big(({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}\overset{1}{A}_{\tau})(b^{*}b+d^{*}d)\Big)

(140)

This expression is exact to all orders in
e
as we show in Appendix
B
.

Upon antipodal identification of the symmetry parameters
\bar{{\epsilon}}(y)
and
{{\epsilon}}(\hat{x})
between
i^{+}\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{+}
and
i^{-}\cup{\mathcal{I}}^{-}
, we obtain charges

Q^{(\ln)}_{\pm}=Q^{(\ln)}_{S,\pm}+Q^{(\ln)}_{H,\pm}

(141)

on the future (
+
) and past (
-
) boundary satisfying

Q^{(\ln)}_{+}=Q^{(\ln)}_{-}

(142)

This classical conservation law was engineered in
[
20
]
from the classical logarithmic soft photon theorem. Here we derived it from first principles and showed that it holds to all orders in the coupling
e
. Our covariant phase space result thus exhibits the same type of universality as the (classical) logarithmic soft photon theorem and unequivocally establishes its symmetry interpretation.

7
Conclusion

Just as the S-matrix is infrared divergent in scalar QED, in the classical theory, the symplectic structure at the boundary also suffers from late-time divergences and has to be regularised.
In the context of scalar QED, a regularised S-matrix can be used to define infrared-safe observables such as inclusive or semi-inclusive cross sections.
In the classical theory we have shown that the regularised Noether charge defines an infrared-safe asymptotic conservation law which is equivalent to the classical logarithmic soft theorem.
In the S-matrix computation this amounts to going beyond the tree-level approximation and include the relevant loop diagrams that survive in the classical limit. This is mirrored in the covariant phase space analysis by accounting for late-time tails and considering divergent superphaserotations. Here we focused on the classical logarithmic soft photon theorem in scalar QED which is one-loop exact and requires us to account for the leading effects of the
1/\tau
and
1/u
tails as well as consider
O(\tau)
and
O(r)
divergent superphaserotations. While we have given a first principle derivation of the classical conservation law which is equivalent to the (classical) log soft theorem, in
[
20
,
29
]
it was argued that the quantisation of this conservation law implies the quantum log soft photon theorem in scalar QED. It would be interesting to revisit this quantisation in light of the current work.

\tau^{-4}(\ln\tau)^{n}
,

\displaystyle\overset{4,n}{j_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=-2e^{2}\overset{1}{A}_{\tau}\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}

\displaystyle\quad+ie\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(n+1-2k)b_{0,k}b_{0,n+1-k}^{*}

(177)

\displaystyle\quad-2em\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(b_{1,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}+\text{c.c.}\right)~.

There are three series that appear.
It turns out that the first two cancel each other out, and that the last one only contains terms with either
\delta_{n,1}
or
\delta_{n,0}
.
We examine them one by one:

•

First series:

Plugging in the expression (
151
), the first series becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{|b_{0}|^{2}}{n!}(ie\overset{1}{A}_{\tau})^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}.

(178)

This is a binomial expansion for
(1-1)^{n}=\delta_{n,0}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}

\displaystyle=|b_{0}|^{2}\delta_{n,0}.

(179)

•

Second series:

Writing out the second term using (
151
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(n+1-2k)b_{0,k}b_{0,n+1-k}^{*}

\displaystyle=(-ie\overset{1}{A}_{\tau})^{n+1}|b_{0}|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}\frac{(n+1-2k)}{k!(n+1-k)!}.

(180)

The sum on the r.h.s. can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{k}\frac{(n+1-2k)}{k!(n+1-k)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k}(-1)^{k}+\frac{2}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}.

(181)

The first sum on the r.h.s. is the binomial expansion of
(1-1)^{n+1}=\delta_{n+1,0}
; this is identically zero since
n
is non-negative.
The second sum is
\delta_{n,0}
, so we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}(n+1-2k)b_{0,k}b^{*}_{0,n+1-k}

\displaystyle=-2ie\overset{1}{A}_{\tau}|b_{0}|^{2}\delta_{n,0}.

(182)

•

Third series:

We are left with the last series on the r.h.s. of (
177
),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(b_{1,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}+\text{c.c.}\right).

(183)

Plugging in the expressions (
151
) and (
158
), we find that every sum becomes a binomial expansion that reduces to either
\delta_{n,0}
or
\delta_{n,1}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(b_{1,k}b_{0,n-k}^{*}+\text{c.c.}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\delta_{n,0}}{2m}\left[i\left(b_{0}^{*}{\cal D}^{2}b_{0}-b_{0}{\cal D}^{2}b_{0}^{*}\right)+2e\overset{0}{A}_{\alpha}\left(b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}+b_{0}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}b_{0}^{*}\right)+4e\overset{1}{A}_{\tau}|b_{0}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e}{m}(\delta_{n,0}+\delta_{n,1}){\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}\overset{1}{A}_{\tau})|b_{0}|^{2}

(184)

Collecting the results and plugging them into (
177
), we obtain

\displaystyle\overset{4,n}{j_{\tau}}

\displaystyle=-2e^{2}(\delta_{n,0}+\delta_{n,1}){\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}\Big(({\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}\overset{1}{A}_{\tau})|b_{0}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta_{n,0}\left[ie\left(b_{0}^{*}{\cal D}^{2}b_{0}-b_{0}{\cal D}^{2}b_{0}^{*}\right)+2e^{2}\overset{0}{A}_{\alpha}{\mathcal{D}}^{\alpha}|b_{0}|^{2}+4e^{2}\overset{1}{A}_{\tau}|b_{0}|^{2}\right]+(b\to d^{*}).

(185)

Therefore, we have established that

\overset{4,n}{j_{\tau}}=0\qquad\text{ for all $n\geq 2$}.

(186)

Next, we show that
\overset{4,n}{j_{\alpha}}=0
for all
n\geq 2
.
Using the expansion (
147
) to compute
j_{\alpha}
, we collect all terms of order
\tau^{-3}(\ln\tau)^{n}
to be

\overset{3,n}{j_{\alpha}}=-e^{2}\overset{0}{A}_{\alpha}\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,k}^{*}b_{0,n-k}-ie\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{0,k}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}b_{0,n-k}+\text{c.c.}~.

(187)

Using the expression (
151
) for
b_{0,n}
, we find that the two sums organize into simple binomial expansions that reduce to either
\delta_{n,0}
and
\delta_{n,1}
:

\overset{3,n}{j_{\alpha}}=\delta_{n,0}\left(-2e^{2}\overset{0}{A}_{\alpha}|b_{0}|^{2}-ie(b_{0}^{*}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}b_{0}-b_{0}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}b_{0}^{*})\right)+2\delta_{n,1}e^{2}|b_{0}|^{2}{\partial}_{\alpha}\overset{1}{A}_{\tau}+(b\to d^{*}).

(188)

Therefore as a corollary, it follows that

\overset{3,n}{j_{\alpha}}=0\qquad\text{ for all $n\geq 2$}.

(189)

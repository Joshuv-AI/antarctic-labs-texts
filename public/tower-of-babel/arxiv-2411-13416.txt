Abstract
. We study quantitative aspects of the following fact: For every graph F F , there exists a graph G G with the property that any 2 2 -coloring of the triangles of G G yields an induced copy of F F , in which all triangles are monochromatic. We define the Ramsey number R ind Δ ​ ( F ) R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F) as the smallest size of such a graph G G . Although this fact has several proofs, all of them provide tower-type bounds. We study the number R ind Δ ​ ( F ) R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F) for some particular classes of graphs F F .



1.
Introduction

For a natural number
N
, we set
[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Given a set
X
and a positive integer
k
, we write
X^{(k)}=\{e\subseteq X:\>|e|=k\}
for the set of all
k
-element subsets of
X
. A
k
-uniform hypergraph (or
k
-graph) is a pair
H=(V,E)
, where
V
is the set of vertices and
E\subseteq V^{(k)}
is the set of edges.

Given two
k
-graphs
F
and
H
, we write
H\rightarrow F
if every
2
-coloring of the edges of
H
yields a monochromatic copy of
F
. The
Ramsey number
R(F)
of the
k
-graph
F
is the smallest integer
N
such that there exists a graph
H
on
N
vertices satisfying
H\rightarrow F
. The existence of
R(F)
(Ramsey’s theorem) was established by Ramsey
[
21
]
and later rediscovered by Erdős and Szekeres
[
10
]
.

Another possible direction is to study extensions of Ramsey’s theorem when we impose that the monochromatic copy has additional structural properties. One of these extensions is the induced Ramsey theorem. We say that a
k
-graph
F
is an
induced
copy of
H
if there exists an injective embedding
\varphi:V(F)\rightarrow V(H)
such that
\{\varphi(x),\varphi(y)\}\in H
if and only if
\{x,y\}\in F
, i.e., edges are mapped to edges and non-edges to non-edges. For a
k
-graph
F
, the
induced Ramsey number
R_{\Ind}(F)
is the smallest integer
N
for which there exists a
k
-graph
H
such that every
2
-coloring of the edges of
H
yields an induced monochromatic copy of
F
(denoted by the relation
H\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ind}}}{\rightarrow}}F
).

The existence of
R_{\Ind}(F)
was shown for graphs and hypergraphs independently by a series of authors
[
6
,
8
,
22
,
1
,
18
]
. These original proofs provided no quantitative bounds on
R_{\Ind}(F)
. However, Erdős
[
12
]
conjectured that for graphs, the number
R_{\Ind}(F)
should be exponential in the number of vertices of
F
. The best current upper bound is due to Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov
[
5
]
, who showed that for every graph
F
on
n
vertices,

(1.1)

\displaystyle R_{\Ind}(F)\leq 2^{cn\log n},

improving a result from
[
13
]
. The problem for hypergraphs was studied in
[
4
]
, where in particular the authors proved that for a
3
-graph
F
on
n
vertices, the bound
R_{\Ind}(F)\leq 2^{2^{cn}}
holds.

We are interested in a variant of the induced Ramsey theorem proved in
[
7
,
17
]
. Given two graphs
G
and
F
, we say that

\displaystyle G\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ind}}}{\rightarrow}}(F)^{\Delta}

if, for any
2
-coloring of the triangles in
G
, there exists an induced copy of
F
such that all its triangles are monochromatic. Let
R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)
be the smallest integer
N
such that there exists a graph
G
on
N
vertices with
G\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ind}}}{\rightarrow}}(F)^{\Delta}
. The existence of
R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)
was proven in
[
17
,
7
,
19
,
3
]
, though none of those proofs provided a numerical bound.

For a graph
F
, let
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
be the
3
-graph on the vertex set
V(F)
whose edges are the triplets of vertices that form triangles in
F
. We remark that in order to show that
G\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ind}}}{\rightarrow}}(F)^{\Delta}
, it is not sufficient to find a
3
-graph
H^{(3)}
with
H^{(3)}\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ind}}}{\rightarrow}}{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
. Indeed, we require the monochromatic copy
{\mathcal{K}}
of
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
to be not only induced in
H^{(3)}
, but also that having the property that for every pair of vertices of
V({\mathcal{K}})
the only edges containing them in
H^{(3)}
are those in
{\mathcal{K}}
. A copy
{\mathcal{K}}
with such property is called
a strongly induced copy
of
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
. Therefore, in order for
G
to satisfy
G\mathrel{\overset{\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny ind}}}{\rightarrow}}(F)^{\Delta}
, the hypergraph
H={\mathcal{K}}_{3}(G)
needs to have a strongly induced monochromatic copy of
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
. This additional requirement implies that
R_{\Ind}(F)\leq R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)
.

The main contribution of the paper is to provide bounds for
R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)
for certain families of graphs. The first result is an upper bound when
F
is a graph whose triangles form a linear
3
-graph.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
F
is a graph on
n
vertices such that
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
is linear, then

\displaystyle R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)\leq 2^{2^{2^{40n}}}.

We remark that since
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
is the
3
-graph of triangles of
F
, then
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
is linear if and only if
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
has girth at least
4
. The second result concerns graphs where
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
is a family of bipartite
3
-graphs. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{n}\subseteq 2^{[n]^{(2)}}
be the family of graphs
F
on
n
vertices with the property that there exists a bipartition
V(F)=A\cup B
of the vertices such that
F[A]
is a triangle free graph,
B
is an independent set and the bipartite graph between
A
and
B
is arbitrary. In this case, we will show that the growth of
R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)
is at most doubly exponential.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
be a graph on
n
vertices. Then

\displaystyle R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)\leq 2^{2^{cn\log n}}

for a positive constant
c
.

Finally, our last result shows a polynomial bound if
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
is a tight tree. A
3
-graph

is called a

if its edges can be ordered as
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
such that for each

,
e_{i}
has a vertex

that does not belong to any previous edge and

is contained in

for some

.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
F
be a graph such that
{\mathcal{K}}_{3}(F)
is a tight tree. Then

\displaystyle R_{\Ind}^{\Delta}(F)\leq n^{4}

holds.

Finally, we use an embedding lemma for embedding linear hypergraphs in weakly regular partitions (see Lemma

3.1.
Properties of Random Graphs

The following property of the random graph says that all sufficiently large subsets have density roughly

\displaystyle P(X,Y):=|\{\{x,y\}:x\neq y,x\in X,y\in Y\}|,\quad e(X,Y):=|\{\{x,y\}\in E(G):x\in X,y\in Y\}|.

For

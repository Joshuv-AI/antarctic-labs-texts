Introduction

For many years, the standard treatment for metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) relied primarily on tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) targeting angiogenesis. This pathway is highly activated in ccRCC due to loss of von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) function, leading to upregulation of hypoxia-inducible factors and subsequent pro-angiogenic signalling.1

However, despite these advances, prognosis remained poor, with 5-year survival rates ranging between 10% and 20%.2 Over the past decade, the introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), either alone or combined with TKIs (eg, nivolumab plus ipilimumab, pembrolizumab plus axitinib, nivolumab plus cabozantinib, pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib), has significantly improved clinical outcomes. These regimens achieve objective response rates (ORR) of 40%–60%, with substantial improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) (HR 0.69–0.85) and overall survival (HR 0.53–0.78) compared with sunitinib monotherapy.3–6

Traditionally, CT and bone scintigraphy have been the mainstay imaging modalities for disease monitoring, performed every 3–6 months. However, conventional imaging sometimes struggles to detect early metastatic dissemination or to differentiate true tumour progression from immunotherapy-related phenomena, such as pseudo-progression, delayed response or mixed responses. Furthermore, bone metastases are particularly difficult to evaluate, highlighting the potential role for metabolic imaging.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein highly overexpressed in prostate cancer and associated with higher tumour grade and stage.7 PSMA-targeted PET/CT imaging has demonstrated superior sensitivity and specificity compared with standard imaging for detecting prostate cancer lesions. Beyond prostate malignancies, PSMA expression has also been identified in the neovascularisation of various tumours, including renal cell carcinoma, with particularly high levels observed in ccRCC, where expression rates range from 90% to 100%.8 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT imaging exploits this PSMA expression by enabling visualisation of PSMA-positive tumours and metastases, thus offering a functional imaging approach beyond conventional anatomical techniques.

Recently, we demonstrated that early changes in 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT correlated with later tumour size changes on conventional imaging. A ≥30% decrease in mean SUVmax at 6 weeks was associated with significant tumour reduction at both 12 and 52 weeks, whereas smaller changes predicted disease progression.9 10 However, larger trials are needed. This multicentric phase II trial aims to prospectively evaluate whether early metabolic changes on 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT can serve as predictive biomarkers of response in metastatic ccRCC.

Methods and analysis

Clinical trial design

The PSMA-RENAL study is a prospective, multicentre, phase II clinical trial designed to evaluate the predictive value of early changes in 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT imaging in patients with metastatic ccRCC treated with ICI-based therapy, either as an ICI-ICI combination or an ICI-TKI combination. Three centres in Belgium will participate. While systemic therapy will be administered at each patient’s local centre, all 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT imaging will be conducted centrally at Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc in Brussels to ensure consistency in imaging protocols and analysis. The first site (Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc, Brussels) was activated on 5 March and is currently recruiting.

Objectives

The primary objective of PSMA-RENAL is to evaluate whether early change in PSMA-PET uptake from baseline to 6 weeks after initiation of first-line systemic therapy predicts anticancer treatment efficacy in patients with metastatic ccRCC.

The primary outcome measure is the association between the percentage change in ⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT uptake from baseline to week 6 and disease control rate (DCR) at 6 months. PSMA-PET uptake will be assessed using SUVmax. DCR will be assessed on conventional imaging and defined as the proportion of patients with complete response (CR), partial response (PR) or stable disease according to RECIST 1.1 criteria. Patients who die before the 6-month assessment, are lost to follow-up before the 6-month assessment, or have non-evaluable conventional imaging at 6 months will be classified as not having disease control.

The secondary outcome measures are: first, the association between the percentage change in PSMA-PET uptake from baseline to week 6 and ORR, defined as CR or PR on conventional imaging; second, the association between baseline ⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT uptake and DCR at 6 months; and third, the association between the percentage change in PSMA-PET uptake from baseline to week 6 and PFS. PFS will be defined as the time from initiation of first-line systemic therapy to radiological disease progression, death from any cause or last follow-up, whichever occurs first. To address atypical immune patterns, iRECIST will be applied as a predefined confirmation rule: in clinically stable patients with initial PD per RECIST, progression must be confirmed on repeat imaging after 4–8 weeks before being considered PD for endpoint analyses.

Patient population

Eligible patients must be aged >18 years and have histologically confirmed metastatic ccRCC, be naïve to systemic therapy and be suitable candidates for first-line treatment with an ICI–TKI or ICI–ICI combination. Baseline 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT must demonstrate PSMA-positive lesions, expected in approximately 85%–90% of patients. Baseline PSMA-positivity is defined as ≥1 lesion with uptake ≥liver, operationalised quantitatively as a tumour-to-liver SUV ratio (TLR) ≥1.0 (with TLR ≥1.5 in sensitivity analyses), using centralised reads to ensure harmonisation across scanners. An ECOG performance status of 0 or 1 and adequate bone marrow and liver function are required. Prior adjuvant ICI therapy is allowed if it ended more than 12 months before enrolment. Exclusion criteria include non-clear cell histology. All participants give written informed consent to investigators.

Procedures

All patients will undergo baseline imaging (thoraco-abdominal CT or MRI with bone scan) followed by a 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT within 21 days prior to treatment initiation. Patients will then receive standard systemic therapy per institutional guidelines. A second 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT will be conducted 6 weeks after treatment initiation to assess early metabolic changes. Follow-up conventional imaging will start 12 weeks after treatment initiation and will continue every 12 weeks per standard practice for 12 months; beyond this timepoint, follow-up imaging will be performed at physician discretion (figure 1).

68Ga-PSMA-11 (Locametz) will be administered in accordance with its approved use in prostate cancer, following the Summary of Product Characteristics. The recommended dose is 1.8–2.2 MBq/kg of body weight, with a minimum total dose of 111 MBq and a maximum dose of 259 MBq. Intravenous injection will be performed slowly, followed by a saline flush.

The results of PSMA PET/CT (baseline and week 6) will remain blinded to the treating oncologists and evaluated separately by nuclear medicine physicians. Results will be disclosed 12 months after treatment initiation.

Statistical analysis

A sample size of 61 patients with positive baseline 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT scans has been calculated. This includes 49 patients from the disease control group (80%) and 12 patients from the progression disease group (20%). With this sample size, the study will have 80% power to detect a meaningful difference in the area under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) between the null hypothesis (AUC=0.5000) and the alternative hypothesis (AUC=0.7397).8 11 This is based on a two-sided z-test at a significance level of 0.05. The change from baseline in PET-PSMA data is treated as a continuous response variable, and the AUC will be computed over false positive rates ranging from 0.00 to 1.00. The ratio of the SD of responses in the non-response group to that in the response group is assumed to be 1.00, reflecting similar variability in both groups. To account for potential dropouts between baseline and the response assessment, an expected 10% dropout rate has been incorporated. This will bring the number of patients with a positive PSMA-PET/CT at baseline to 68. Given the expected 90% PSMA-positivity rate at baseline, a total of 75 patients will be enrolled in the study. This sample size ensures that we have an adequate number of evaluable patients to detect the desired differences in the outcomes.

Optimal SUVmax change cut-offs will be identified, with diagnostic parameters (sensitivity, specificity, accuracy) reported. While the primary PET endpoint remains the percent change from baseline to week 6 in the mean SUVmax across ≤5 target lesions, we prespecify complementary, noise-robust secondary metrics—PSMA total tumour volume and SUVmean over the segmented tumour burden. Secondary analyses will assess the relationship between early PSMA changes and ORR, as well as PFS using Kaplan-Meier methods. Median PFS, 95% CIs, and 6- and 12-month survival rates will be reported. Patients without events will be censored at last follow-up. The outcome-oriented approach proposed by Contal and O’Quigley will also be used to determine optimal cut-offs for PFS prediction. Exploratorily, we will stratify by regimen (ICI–TKI vs ICI–ICI) and, if numbers allow, test a regimen×PSMA-PET/CT interaction to assess whether predictive performance differs by treatment. All statistical analyses will be conducted using Statistical Analysis System (SAS) software (V.9.4 or later; SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA). A full statistical analysis plan (SAP) will be finalised prior to database lock. De-identified individual participant data will be recorded and stored in Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) on a coded, anonymised basis. After publication of the primary results, de-identified participant-level data, the data dictionary and the statistical code may be made available by the corresponding author on reasonable request, subject to approval by the study investigators, ethics requirements and applicable data protection regulations. No additional unpublished documents will be made publicly available. See detailed protocol in online supplemental material.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not formally involved in setting the research question, study design, selection of outcome measures, recruitment strategy, conduct of the study or preparation of this protocol manuscript. The research question was informed by clinical experience in the management of patients with metastatic ccRCC and by the recognised need for earlier and more reliable response assessment during first-line systemic therapy. Study results will be disseminated through scientific meetings and peer-reviewed publication. A lay summary of the results will be made available to study participants on request after publication of the primary results.

Discussion

This prospective multicentre phase II study will evaluate whether early changes on 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT can predict treatment response in first-line metastatic ccRCC. The study addresses an important evidence gap, as PSMA-PET/CT has shown promise in renal cell carcinoma mainly for detection and staging, while prospective data on early on-treatment response assessment remain limited.

In the present trial, PSMA-PET/CT results are not used to guide systemic treatment, as treating oncologists remain blinded to baseline and week-6 PET findings. Therefore, PSMA-RENAL is designed as an imaging biomarker validation study. If early changes in ⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT uptake predict 6-month disease control, this approach could provide an early tool for risk stratification and prognostication. An early metabolic response may support treatment continuation when conventional imaging is equivocal, whereas absence of metabolic response may identify patients requiring closer surveillance or future treatment-adaptation strategies. Treatment de-escalation based on PSMA-PET/CT alone would be premature at this stage, but could be explored in future prospective trials if the biomarker is validated.

The choice of PSMA rather than a more ccRCC-specific tracer should be acknowledged. CAIX-targeted imaging, including girentuximab-based PET, is highly relevant in ccRCC and has shown promising diagnostic performance, particularly for renal mass characterisation.12 However, PSMA-RENAL is not designed to establish histological diagnosis, but to evaluate early on-treatment response in metastatic ccRCC receiving first-line ICI-based systemic therapy. PSMA is expressed in the tumour-associated neovasculature of ccRCC and may therefore reflect angiogenic activity and treatment-related vascular modulation, which is relevant in a disease frequently treated with Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptor (VEGFR)-targeted combinations. Moreover, PSMA-PET/CT is widely available because of its established clinical use in prostate cancer, supporting its feasibility in a multicentre response-assessment strategy. Carbonic Anhydrase IX (CAIX)- and PSMA-targeted imaging may therefore be complementary rather than competing approaches.

Methodological strengths include prospective enrolment, centralised PET acquisition and interpretation, blinded treating oncologists and prespecified response criteria. Limitations include the single-arm design, modest sample size and restriction to patients with PSMA-positive baseline disease, which may limit generalisability.

Ethics statements

All study procedures were approved via Clinical Trials Information System by the competent regulatory authorities and ethics committees, including the Federal Agency for Nuclear Control, the Federal Agency for Medicines and Health Products and the Comité d’Ethique Biomédicale Hospitalo-Facultaire of Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc (CEBHF: 2024/12DEC/553), together with the relevant approvals for the participating centres: Grand Hôpital de Charleroi and HELORA-Jolimont. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrolment and before any study-specific procedure. The study will be conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice, applicable European and Belgian regulations, General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Declaration of Helsinki. Participant confidentiality will be protected by recording study data in coded form. Study results will be disseminated through presentations at scientific meetings and publication in peer-reviewed journals. No independent Data Monitoring Committee was established. Overall study oversight will be provided by the principal investigator and coordinating investigators, with operational management performed by the study team and safety reporting conducted according to institutional and regulatory requirements. Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting or dissemination plans of our research.

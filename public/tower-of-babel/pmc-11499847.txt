Significance of the proton energy loss mechanism to gold nanoparticles in proton therapy: a Geant4 simulation
Authors: Farshid Tabbakh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The biological effectiveness in proton therapy is only slightly greater than of the treatment by X-ray and hence, many researches have suggested the use of gold nanoparticles for increasing the ionization interactions to produce more secondary electrons and elevate the yield of DNA damage. But the ionization interactions also lead to protons energy loss inside the nanoparticles. The present study shows that, the protons slowed-down by High-Z nanoparticles are responsible for dose enhancement rather than the produced secondary electrons. To this purpose, using Geant4 Monte Carlo tool, one million nanoparticles distributed in a proton irradiated volume and variation of the proton’s spectra and the dose related to this variation has been demonstrated. It was found that, the elevation in proton’s LET values when passing through the gold nanoparticles will lead to a more significant dose enhancement than the increased dose due to the extra secondary electrons. Also, it was found that, the mechanism of protons slowing-down by gold nanoparticles has another useful aspect in proton therapy in which, the dose leakage to surrounding healthy tissues will be reduced which must be considered in future investigations more precisely.

Introduction

Proton therapy is a promising modality in tumors treatment because of its considerable advantages in compare to gamma-ray or X-ray therapy, such as the lower risk to healthy surrounding tissues during the treatment1. Nevertheless, one of the main issues in this modality is that, its biological effectiveness is relatively low and close to that of the X–ray therapy2–4. In this regard, a large number of researches have been carried out in order to improve the effectiveness of conventional proton therapy by using the nanoparticles5–31 and by the alternate method called FLASH proton therapy32,33 using an ultra-high dose technique however, the related investigations are still in progress.

In using the nanoparticles in proton therapy, the aim is to enhancing the biological impact of incident proton beam by producing the secondary charged particles such as the secondary alpha particles through the nuclear reactions of protons-targets5–12 or the secondary electrons through the ionization interactions of protons with the High-Z atoms such as gold13–31. By a deeper view to these phenomena, one can see that, the protons will lose their energies partially when passing through the nanoparticles (either the Low-Z or the High-Z elements)34. It means that, the protons when exiting the nanoparticles are slower than before entering to them and this variation in kinetic energy will affect the value of Linear Energy Transfer (LET) and the biological effectiveness too. According to literature, protons (ions) interact with the orbital electrons (as dominant interaction) and also with the atom’s nuclei as the electronic and nuclear stopping power35. Therefore, protons will lose their kinetic energies to these interactions through the multiple scattering toward the end point. By adding the High-Z nanoparticles to tumor, the protons will lose their energies with a greater rate than before. Thus, one may conclude that, the use of High-Z nanoparticles will improve the radio-sensitization not only through the generation of extra secondary electrons but from the slowed-down protons with greater potential for dose deposition as their LET is increased.

The increase of proton’s dose in presence of AuNPs has not been addressed in previous related publications and only Belousov et al.19 by simulating the homogenous compound of water and gold has noted that, the LET of protons will be increased in presence of gold atoms.

In present research, using Geant4 Monte Carlo tool36–38 the increased proton’s dose by slowing in gold nanoparticles has been calculated and compared with the dose contributed by the electrons escaped from the gold nanoparticles. In this regard and for satisfying the realistic conditions, the nanoparticles in a large quantity, one million loaded in volume made of tissue’s compound instead of water medium.

The important part of this work is the discussion on present results and the previous reports by some of the authors argued that, the impact of the electrons emitted from the gold nanoparticles is not significant19,26,27 or the significance is only in nanometer range from the NP’s surface18,23. Whereas, according to some experimental reports, the combination of proton therapy with gold nanoparticles improves the radio-sensitization15,39–42. It is discussed that, the dose enhancement detected by these experimental measurements is possibly contributed by the slowed-down protons by gold nanoparticles which is the subject of present study.

Additionally, the decrease of dose leakage to healthy tissues has been explained when the gold nanoparticles accumulated in tumor. The obtained results not only can be used for improving the efficacy of conventional proton therapy but also, can be applied in FLASH proton therapy which still the researches on its improvement are undergoing.

Materials and methods

Overview

A comparison has been performed between the dose contributed by the secondary electrons from the gold ionization and the slowed-down protons when traversing the gold nanoparticles.

Since, a complete characterization of the beam at a certain location requires knowledge of the energy spectrum of the particles43, the protons spectrum in presence of the AuNPs will address the changes in characterization of protons and the related dose. Therefore, the calculation of dose in this study is based on the changes in protons and electrons spectrum.

With comparing the energy spectrum (with and without AuNPs), one can see the variation of proton’s flux related to each group of energy, the corresponding LET44,45 and dose as well. Both the mechanisms of secondary electron production and proton energy loss are relying on the fact that proton-AuNP collision is a stochastic phenomenon22 and is function of some parameters; the NP’s size and accumulation value in tumor as well as proton’s energy. In order to provide condition close to a realistic experiment, a typical size and concentration value have been considered for the NPs based on the reported experiments. The NPs have been distributed in a volume randomly. The specifications of the AuNPs were according to the reported experiments and have been explained in related subsection in this report. A discussion also has been presented on the impact of NPs size and quantity on efficiency.

Dose calculation

There are two methods for calculating the particle’s dose when passing through the medium; calculating the deposited energy in the medium and also, using the particle’s LET spectrum inside the medium. The first method is fine with the homogenous mediums whereas, in present problem the volume has been loaded with one million High-density AuNPs. In presence of such large number of AuNPs the medium is not homogenous and the deposited energy in the loaded medium is the integration of deposited energy in both the tissue and NPs. Accordingly, the calculation of the deposited energy only in the medium is not possible or precise without considering the energies deposited in the NPs. Therefore, in this study, the dose (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Gy$$\end{document}) was calculated based on the flux and LET of particle31,57–59.

When the protons entering the volume, they interact with the atoms of medium (and the AuNPs) causing the changes in their kinetic energies which it will form an energy spectra of the particle of interest. Therefore, when the homogenous tissue changes to non-homogenous due to the AuNPs accumulation, the particle’s spectrum will be changed which demonstrating the changes in the deposited dose as well. In this work, the changes in the spectrum of protons and electron have been extracted. Geant4

The calculations were carried out by Geant4, a general-purpose code for simulating the transport of particles through matter using Monte Carlo techniques36–38. The version of Geant4.10.05.p01 was utilized and the simulations were performed for 108 histories for maximum accuracy and errors less than 5%. The cut-off of 10 nm for all particles was considered in this study. However, 1 nm cut-value has been used in previous works for studying a single NP. For the present simulation with loading of one million NPs in medium, 1 nm cut-value led to a very slow computation process.

In this simulation, the Auger electrons also were turned on by “/process/em/auger true”. It should be note that, the NPs have been distributed randomly but, to prevent them from being overlapped, 30 nm was considered as the minimum distance between their centers according to the NP’s size, 20 nm side cube.

The elastic and inelastic interactions of protons with both the tissue and the distributed nano-targets were simulated by G4HadronElasticPhysicsHP and G4HadronPhysicsQGSP_BIC_HP respectively. G4EmStandardPhysics_Opt3 and G4EmLivermore were considered as they are appropriate for low energy electromagnetic interactions regarding the incident protons and the secondary electrons15,17.

Results and discussions

Overview

In studying the impact of nanoparticles in treatment improvement, one of the main facts is the stochastic nature of the interactions between the incident protons and the distributed NPs in medium. Hence, an accurate simulation must provide a condition close to reality by random distribution of the NPs. In a macroscopic scale, the simulation will require a huge number of nanoparticles distributed inside the whole tumor which, even using the super-fast CPU the running time will last too long. To speed up the simulation run, a micro-size volume with 200 μm depth was considered as it was shown in Fig. 1. Impact of slowed-down protons by AuNPs

The energy spectrum of incident protons inside the given volume have been calculated using the two mentioned EM physics and the results have been depicted in Fig. 4a,b corresponding to EM Standard_opt3 and Livermore respectively. One can see that, Livermore is more sensitive to low energy interactions by comparing the two panels of this figure in energy lower than few hundreds keV, a larger proton’s spectra based on the Livermore results.

Both the panels of this figures revealing this fact that, the gold nanoparticles in proton therapy will affect the proton’s spectrum with a considerable increase in flux at energies around few MeV. In other words, the AuNPs accumulation has caused the increase of proton’s flux at the energy corresponding to the relatively high-LET protons in compare with the protons with energies around tens of MeV. The protons have lost their kinetic energies to multiple scattering inside the AuNPs and slowed-down. The slightly increase of their LET have led to a greater potential for inducing the DNA strand breaks and cell damage.

Discussion

According to several researches such as13–31, one of the promising methods for enhancing the radio-sensitization is the use of High-Z nanoparticles like gold in the tumor irradiated by photon or the charged particles. Since the ionization is the dominant interaction of the incident radiation1,33, the secondary electrons will be generated which can induce the extra damages to the surrounding cells in a short range of maximum 100 nanometers18. Most of the investigations on using the gold nanoparticles in proton therapy believe that this method is successful however, some authors for example; Martinez et al.26 and Sotiropoulos et al.27 have claimed that, no significant enhancement was observed in DNA damage when the AuNPs added. Here, first the mechanism of proton-slowing down by High-Z NPs and its potential for dose enhancement has been explained which flowed by the discussion on the results in this work and previous works.

Proton’s slowed-down mechanism by high-Z NPs

According to literature, when the protons (ions) passing through the matter will suffer the multiple scattering and lose their kinetic energies partially. The mechanism of proton energy loss is strongly the function of medium’s density34. Hence, the High-Z materials like gold not only will cause the highest rate of interactions with the incident protons and releasing a large number of secondary electrons through the ionization of gold atoms also cause that, protons lose their energies when traversing the NPs. Also, from the literature, the biological effectiveness of protons as the function of LET is strongly depending on their kinetic energies45,62,63. Hence, when protons lose their kinetic after leaving the AuNP, it is expected that, their biological impact increase when their kinetic energies have reduced. In this work, we showed that, the protons after exiting the AuNPs will perform the greater effects than of the released electrons (Fig. 5).

Discussion on previous results

The researches such as Martinez et al.26 by using the single AuNP, has detected very small effect from the secondary electrons only in nanometer range from the NP’s surface (the same as others like Lin18 and McKinnon23) and mentioned that, other effects such as biological or chemical processes may play the major role in the amplification of cell damages instead of the secondary electrons. Sotiropoulos et al.27 also using multiple-NPs loaded in a cell has found no significant increase in dose and similarly has concluded that, another mechanism should contribute in radio-sensitization. The present study has introduced the protons slowing-down mechanism as the potential responsible for the amplification of the damages when the gold NPs have been added to medium as presented in Fig. 5. This fact also has been reported by Belousov et al.19. This figure also showed that, the electron emitted from AuNPs have small contribution in radio-sensitization as reported by above researches. But, from the other hand, based on several related works performed experimentally, the use of gold nanoparticles improves the radio-sensitization in proton therapy15,39–42. Therefore, one can conclude that, the dose enhancement which have been detected experimentally is related to the slowed-down protons, not the electrons.

The size of AuNPs

There are some parameters important in efficiency when using the AuNPs in proton therapy. For the certain concentration amount of AuNPs, when the nanoparticle’s have smaller sizes it requires greater number of nanoparticles as well. In other words, for smaller AuNPs, the cross-section of proton-AuNP collision is low and hence, the gold concentration value must be increased to increase the probability of proton-NPs collision. More biological advantages with using AuNPs in proton therapy

The accumulation of gold (High-Z) nanoparticles has also another aspect when the slowed-down protons come to account. This physical fact will prevent the fast protons from entering the healthy tissues after the SOBP region by decreasing the protons kinetic energies. The simulations have shown that (not presented here and still under develop), the accumulation of certain value of AuNPs, will reduce the flux of fast protons 10–20% at the distance of 20 μm from the distal edge of SOBP as a result of increased stopping power of the medium in presence of AuNPs. This should encourage the researchers to investigate on this aspect of using AuNPs in proton therapy which may reduce the dose leakage to surrounding healthy tissues however, can decrease the dose at the end of SOBP as a potential disadvantage. This is the aspect which has not been considered and require more attention.

Conclusion

The combination of gold nanoparticles with proton beam has been frequently investigated by many authors in order to assess the potential of this method for increasing the radio-sensitization of modality. According to many reports, the significance of AuNP inclusion in proton therapy is because of the elevation of the number of secondary electrons when protons ionizing the gold atoms. In this study, two mechanisms of proton energy loss to gold atoms and ionization of gold atoms by primary protons were compared based on their contributions in extra related dose. It was found that, the slowed-down protons by gold nanoparticles are the main contributor to the obtained extra dose while the secondary electrons emitted from the gold nanoparticles have the contribution one order lower.

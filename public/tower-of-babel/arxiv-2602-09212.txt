Controllability of nonautonomous measure driven integrodifferential evolution equations with nonlocal conditions
Mamadou Niang, Mamadou Pathé LY, Abdoul Aziz Ndiaye, Mamadou Abdoul Diop

Abstract
This research delves into the exact controllability of semilinear measure-driven integrodifferential systems in nonlocal settings. We give enough controllability requirements using the measure of noncompactness and the Mönch fixed point theorem without making any assumptions about how compact the evolution system is in relation to the linear part of the measure system. We find results here that both generalize and improve upon many prior findings.

Introduction

Measure differential equations, sometimes called measure-driven equations, help to represent dynamical systems with discontinuous trajectories. They may model results that contradict classical physics rules, such as Zeno trajectories and the effects of quantum mechanics. However, the main distinction between this type of equation and the more common impulsive differential equation is that the former permits unlimited discontinuous points within a finite time interval. For more details, check out

According to Byszewski’s works
[
13

, nonlocal initial conditions are more effective at addressing physical concerns than the traditional initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}

[
12
]
looked at how a small amount of gas moved through a clear tube and used the nonlocal conditions

to get more accurate results. More precise measurements at
t_{k},k=1,2,\ldots,m
are possible with the initial condition
u(0)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}u(t_{k})
as opposed to just measuring at
t=0

However, control theory relies on controllability. Using the allowed controls to go from any starting state to any given ending state makes a dynamic control system controllable. Because of its centrality in control theory, its importance in physics, and its practical implications in engineering, it attracts the attention of many mathematicians and engineers. Various scientific and technical sectors have recently shown a strong interest in controllability (for more reading, see

Definition 7
.

[

A function

\lim_{s\to t^{-}}f(s)=f(t^{-}),\ t\in(0,a]\ \text{and}\ \lim_{s\to t^{+}}f(s)=f(t^{+}),\ t\in[0,a)

\displaystyle=\bigg\lVert-\mathsf{R}(t,0)(g(\zeta_{n}-g(\zeta))+\int_{0}^{t}\,\mathsf{R}(t,s)\mathcal{V}(u_{\zeta_{n}}(s)-u_{\zeta}(s))\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\,\mathsf{R}(t,s)\big[\delta(s,\zeta_{n}(s))-\delta(s,\zeta(s))\big]\mathrm{d}h(s)\bigg\rVert

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}\lVert g(\zeta_{n})-g(\zeta)\rVert+L_{1}L_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\,\lVert u_{\zeta_{n}}(s)-u_{\zeta}(s)\rVert\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+L_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\delta(s,\zeta_{n}(s))-\delta(s,\zeta(s))\rVert\mathrm{d}h(s)

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}\lVert g(\zeta_{n})-g(\zeta)\rVert+L_{1}L_{2}a^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert u_{\zeta_{n}}-u_{\zeta}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+L_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\delta(s,\zeta_{n}(s))-\delta(s,\zeta(s))\rVert\mathrm{d}h(s).

Note that

\displaystyle\lVert u_{\zeta_{n}}-u_{\zeta}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq L_{3}\bigg[\lVert g(\zeta_{n})-g(\zeta)\rVert+L_{1}\lVert g(\zeta_{n})-g(\zeta)\rVert

\displaystyle+L_{1}\int_{0}^{a}\,\lVert\delta(s,\zeta_{n}(s))-\delta(s,\zeta(s))\rVert\,\mathrm{d}h(s)\bigg].

Then by hypotheses
(H2)
,
(H3)
and the dominated convergence theorem for Lebesgue-Stieltjes integrals, we obtain that

\lVert\psi(\zeta_{n})(t)-\psi(\zeta)(t)\rVert\rightarrow 0\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;n\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, by
step 3
we know that
\{\psi(\zeta_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is equiregulated . This result combined with the fact that
\psi(\zeta_{n})\rightarrow\psi(\zeta)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and Lemma
2.8
gives
\psi(\zeta_{n})
converges uniformly to
\psi(\zeta)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Hence,
\psi
is a continuous operator on
\overline{B_{r}}
.

Step 5 :
The Mönch’s condition holds.

Suppose
D\subset\overline{B_{r}}
is countable and
D\subset\overline{co}(\{0\}\cup\psi(D))
, we will show that
\lambda(D)=0
.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that
D=\{\zeta_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Step 3
implies that
\psi(D)
is equiregulated on
I
, then by using the fact that
D\subset\overline{co}(\{0\}\cup\psi(D))
and Lemma
2.9
, we get that
D
is also equiregulated on
I
.

By Lemma
2.12
, hypotheses
(H2)
-(iii) and
(H4)
-(ii), we have

\displaystyle\lambda(\{u_{\zeta_{n}}(s)\}_{n=1}^{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq P_{Z}(s)\lambda\Bigg(\bigg[\int_{0}^{a}\;\mathsf{R}(a,s)\delta(s,\zeta_{n}(s))\,\mathrm{d}h(s)\bigg]_{n=1}^{\infty}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2P_{Z}(s)L_{1}\int_{0}^{a}L(s)\lambda(D(s))\mathrm{d}h(s).

Therefore

\displaystyle\lambda((\psi D)(t))

\displaystyle=\lambda(\{(\psi\zeta_{n})(t)\}_{n=1}^{\infty})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda(\{\mathsf{R}(t,0)(\zeta_{0}-g(\zeta_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty})+\lambda\bigg(\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\;\mathsf{R}(t,s)\delta(s,\zeta_{n}(s))\,\mathrm{d}h(s)\bigg]_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\lambda\bigg(\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\;\mathsf{R}(t,s)\mathcal{V}u_{\zeta_{n}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2L_{1}\int_{0}^{a}\,L(s)\lambda(D(s))\,\mathrm{d}h(s)+2L_{1}L_{2}\int_{0}^{a}\lambda(\{u_{\zeta_{n}}(s)\}_{n=1}^{\infty})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(2L_{1}+4L_{1}^{2}L_{2}\int_{0}^{a}\,P_{Z}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\int_{0}^{a}\,L(s)\lambda(D(s))\mathrm{d}h(s)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(2L_{1}+4L_{1}^{2}L_{2}\int_{0}^{a}\,P_{Z}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\int_{0}^{a}\,L(s)\,\mathrm{d}h(s)\lambda(D)=\widetilde{L}\lambda(D).

Using the previous result and the Mönch condition, we obtain that

\lambda(D)\leq\lambda(\overline{co}\{0\}\cup\psi(D)))=\lambda(\psi(D))\leq\widetilde{L}\lambda(D).

Since
\widetilde{L}<1
, then
\lambda(D)=0
. Therefore, the set
D
is relatively compact in
\mathcal{R}(I,\mathbb{X})
.

According to Theorem
3
, the operator
\psi
has a fixed point in
\overline{B_{r}}
. Hence, the nonlocal measure-driven system (
1.2
) is controllable on the interval
I
.

Example

Consider the integrodifferential systems driven by a measure of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l c l}\displaystyle d_{t}N(t,x)=\tau(t)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}N(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}G(t,s)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}N(s,x)\mathrm{d}s+\theta\,\nu(t,x)\\
\hskip 85.35826pt+M_{0}\cos(N(t,x))\,\mathrm{d}h(t),\;\;t\in I=[0,1],\;\;x\in[0,\pi],\\
N(t,0)=N(t,1)=0,\;\;t\in[0,1],\\
N(0,x)+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\,f(t,x)\log(1+|N(s,p)|^{\frac{1}{2}})\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}p=N_{0}(x),\;\;x\in[0,1],\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where
\theta,M_{0}>0
are constants, and
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Hölder continuous function with order
0<\alpha\leq 1
, meaning that there exists a positive constant

such that

|\tau(t)-\tau(s)|\leq K_{\tau}|t-s|^{\alpha}\,\text{ for }t,s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Moreover,
\tau:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuously differentiable and
\tau(t)<-1

The function
G(\cdot,\cdot)\in BU(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R})
, where
BU(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R})
is the space of all bounded uniformly continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}
. In addition,

is bounded and continuous from
\mathbb{R}^{+}
to
\mathbb{R}

We introduce the space
\mathbb{X}=L^{2}([0,\pi],\mathbb{R})
so that the system (
4.1
) can be written in the abstract form (
1.2
) and define the family of operators
\mathcal{A}(t)
as follows:

\begin{cases}(\mathcal{A}z)(x)=\tau(t)z^{\prime\prime}(x),t\geq 0,x\in[0,\pi],\\
z\in\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}(t))=\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A})=H^{2}(0,\pi)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,\pi).\end{cases}

The operator
\mathcal{A}
is defined as:

\begin{cases}(\mathcal{A}z)(x)=z^{\prime\prime}(x),x\in[0,\pi],\\
z\in\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}(t))=\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A})=H^{2}(0,\pi)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,\pi),\,t\geq 0.\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}(\mathcal{B}(t,s)z)(x)=G(t,s)z^{\prime\prime}(x),\,0\leq s\leq t,x\in[0,\pi],\\
z\in\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A}(t))=\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{A})=H^{2}(0,\pi)\cap H_{0}^{1}(0,\pi).\end{cases}

In what follows we will show that the following assumptions are verified.

—

Assumptions (C1)-(C3):

–

Assumptions (C1): for each
t\geq 0,\mathcal{A}(t)=\tau(t)\Delta
generates a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathbb{X}
. In addition,
\mathcal{A}(t)z=\tau(t)\Delta z
is strongly continuously differentiable on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
for each

, due to the strong continuous differentiability of
\tau(\cdot)
. We known that
\Delta
is an infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contraction on
\mathcal{X}
. Since
b(t)<-1
, it follows that
\tau(t)\Delta
is an infinitesimal generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup of contraction on
\mathbb{X}
. Therefore, for each
t\geq 0,\mathcal{A}(t)=\tau(t)\Delta
is stable (see,
[
31
]
). Furthermore, we have
(\mathcal{B}(t)z)(\cdot)=G(t+\cdot,t)\mathcal{A}z
for each

\frac{\partial G(t,s)}{\partial t}
is continuously differentiable from

\mathcal{B}(t)z
is strongly continuously differentiable on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
for each
z\in\mathsf{Y}
. Hence, assumption
(C1)
is satisfied.

Thus for

,

\mathcal{A}(t)z=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}(-n^{2}\tau(t))\langle z,e_{n}\rangle e_{n}

where

we show that the evolution family

5
Conclusion

This work investigates a category of nonlocal measure-driven integral differential systems in Banach space. Our results, proven using the Mönch fixed point theorem, which does not impose the compactness of the resolvent operator, represent a novel contribution to this field. Our next step will be to apply this finding to a nonlocal evolution equation with state-dependent delay, non-instantaneous impulsive, and random effects.

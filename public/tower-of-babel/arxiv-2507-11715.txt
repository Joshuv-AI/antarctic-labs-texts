Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds, Integrability and Dissipation
Rafael Azuaje, Piergiulio Tempesta

Abstract
. The theory of Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds is proposed as a natural geometric framework useful to state the integrability of both conservative and dissipative Hamiltonian systems in a unified way. As a reduction, new structures such as contact-Haantjes and locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes manifolds are investigated. The main properties of these novel classes of manifolds and their mutual relationship are discussed. It is shown that the notion of Haantjes chains, within the framework of Jacobi geometry, is related to the existence of complete integrable contact Hamiltonian systems, encompassing also dissipative cases, as well as to the integrability of Hamiltonian systems on appropriate invariant submanifolds. Our framework naturally generalizes the formulation of integrable conservative Hamiltonian systems through the theory of symplectic-Haantjes (or ω ​ ℋ \omega\mathscr{H} ) manifolds.

1.
Introduction

Over the past two decades, there has been a renewed interest in the geometry of Nijenhuis and Haantjes tensors. The concept of Nijenhuis torsion was first introduced by A. Nijenhuis in
[
47
]
, in the context of studying the integrability of eigen-distributions associated with operator fields possessing pointwise distinct eigenvalues.

In
[
20
]
, the graded bracket now known as the Frölicher-Nijenhuis bracket was introduced. This bracket plays a significant role in various geometric frameworks, particularly in the theory of almost-complex structures, as demonstrated by the Newlander-Nirenberg theorem
[
46
]
,
[
30
]
and has been recently generalized in
[
56
]
.
Shortly before, in his seminal work
[
24
]
, J. Haantjes introduced the fundamental concept of the torsion that now bears his name. He showed that the vanishing of the Haantjes torsion of a (1,1)-tensor field is a necessary condition for the existence of an integrable frame of generalized eigenvectors. In the case of pointwise semisimple operators, this condition is also sufficient.

Recently, notable new applications of Nijenhuis and Haantjes tensors have emerged. These include their use in characterizing integrable chains of partial differential equations of hydrodynamic type (see, e.g.,
[
19
]
,
[
6
]
) and in the study of infinite-dimensional integrable systems, particularly in relation to the celebrated WDVV equations of associativity and the theory of Dubrovin-Frobenius manifolds
[
38
,
39
]
.
In
[
55
]
, the concept of Haantjes algebra was introduced as a free module of Haantjes operators that are closed under operator multiplication. Building on this, the theory of symplectic-Haantjes (or
\omega\mathscr{H}
) manifolds was initiated in an early version of
[
54
]
, providing an alternative geometric framework for the theory of finite-dimensional integrable Hamiltonian systems.
Additionally, the related notion of Poisson-Haantjes manifold (
P\mathscr{H}
) was proposed in
[
58
]
. These structures are closely connected to the companion geometries of symplectic-Nijenhuis manifolds (
\omega N
)
[
40
,
18
]
and Poisson-Nijenhuis manifolds
[
40
]
, and were also influenced by Magri’s concept of Haantjes manifolds
[
38
]
.

The theory of
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds has recently been further developed as a natural framework for systematically carrying out the separation of variables for the Hamilton-Jacobi equation associated with an integrable system
[
54
,
51
,
53
]
.

This work aims to broadly extend the
\omega\mathscr{H}
geometry in order to provide an unified framework allowing us to discuss both conservative and dissipative systems within a novel, general theoretical setting.

The results presented in this work are of a twofold nature: on one hand, we introduce new, general geometric structures, which are of intrinsic mathematical interest; on the other hand, we establish theorems relating these structures with Hamiltonian dynamics, with special emphasis on the concepts of complete integrability, partial integrability, and dissipation.

Concerning, more specifically, the first set of results, namely the study of new geometric structures, we introduce the notion of
Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds
(and their extensions), which merges Haantjes structures with the geometry of Jacobi manifolds, and we prove some relevant theorems of this new theory.

In fact, Jacobi manifolds offer a natural framework which generalizes several well-known geometries as Poisson, contact and locally conformal symplectic structures. Jacobi and Poisson structures were introduced by Lichnerowicz in
[
35
,
36
]
. Also, Jacobi manifolds are conceptually related with the notion of local Lie algebras, in the sense of Kirillov
[
29
]
. In particular, given a local Lie algebra on a differentiable manifold, it defines a Jacobi structure on the manifold.

Nijenhuis geometry and the theory of Jacobi manifolds was unified in the framework of of Jacobi-Nijenhuis manifolds in
[
42
,
49
]
, under different perspectives. Our notion of
extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold
directly generalizes these interesting constructions to the Haantjes setting. To this aim, we introduce the auxiliary notion of
extended Haantjes algebra
of operators defined on
\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Another related objective of this work is to study geometric reductions of the novel structures we propose. As is well known, transitive Jacobi manifolds reduce to contact manifolds or locally conformal symplectic manifolds, depending whether their dimension is odd or even
[
41
]
.

Contact structures have found interesting applications, for instance, in geometric optics
[
11
]
, thermodynamics
[
50
]
, topology
[
22
]
and the study of integrable models
[
13
]
. Contact geometry is also particularly suitable for the analysis of dissipative systems.

Inspired by these results, in this article we also define new, more specific geometric structures emerging as suitable reductions of our notion of Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds: more precisely, we introduce the classes of
contact-Haantjes manifolds
of first and second kind and the family of
locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes manifolds
. The Jacobi-Haantjes structures can be related to the
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds defined in
[
54
]
and further studied in recent works
[
57
,
56
,
51
,
53
]
by a standard Poissonization process; besides, in some specific cases (even-dimensional manifolds, and Jacobi structures (
\Lambda,E
) with
E=0
and
\Lambda
invertible) can reduce to them in a direct way.

Our second set of results concerns the relationship between the new geometries proposed and Hamiltonian dynamics. Our main results are the following: in Theorem
4
, we prove that extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds allows us to define (a priori) infinite families of
dissipative systems in involution
with respect to the Jacobi bracket. In Theorem
5
(the extended
Liouville-Haantjes theorem
), crucial on our theory, we show that the existence of a completely integrable family of Hamiltonians is equivalent to the existence of an extended Haantjes chain and, in turn, of an extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold associated with the Hamiltonian system.

We also show that in several contexts, partially integrable systems arise from the proposed constructions. These are systems which are Liouville integrable on specific hypersurfaces of the phase space, but not in the full space.
The integrals defined on these invariant submanifolds are known in the literature as
particular integrals

[
15
,
4
]
. Both contact-Haantjes and locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes geometries are in fact related with partial (or particular) integrability. Theorems

10

11
clarify the relationship between both contact-Haantjes geometries (including the special cases of first and second kind) and locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes geometries, in connection with the existence of dissipated quantities or with the existence of partially or completely integrable systems.

Due to its generality, we believe that the theoretical framework proposed in this article is potentially useful as a unifying setting bridging from the theory of integrable to that of dissipative systems.

A main open problem is to develop a general Hamilton-Jacobi approach for Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds. We wish to ascertain whether an equivalent of the Jacobi-Haantjes theorem of the theory of
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds
[
51
]
can be formulated. This theorem is crucial since relates a class of special coordinates on a
\omega\mathscr{H}

Another interesting research line is to analyze in the spirit of the proposed tensorial approach the cases of cosymplectic and cocontact geometries. By analogy with the present constructions, we plan to study Haantjes structures on cosymplectic and cocontact manifolds, and to develop a coherent tensorial theory for time-dependent conservative and dissipative Hamiltonian systems.

A general theory of reductions of Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds, extending the results in
[
27
]
is also in order.
Work is in progress along these lines.

The structure of the article is as follows. In section

2.
Haantjes and Jacobi geometries

In this Section, we shall revise the basic aspects of the Haantjes geometry and of the theory of Jacobi manifolds, jointly with their reductions to contact and locally conformal symplectic manifolds, which are relevant for the subsequent discussion.

2.1.
Haantjes geometry and Hamiltonian systems

The fundamental notions concerning the geometry of Nijenhuis and Haantjes torsions, which we present here, were originally derived in the papers
[
24
,
47
,
20
]
.

Let
M
be a differentiable manifold,
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the Lie algebra of all smooth vector fields on
M
and
\boldsymbol{K}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
be a smooth
(1,1)
tensor field (namely, an operator field). From now on the terms “tensor fields” and “operator fields” will be abbreviated to tensors and operators, respectively. In the following, all tensors are assumed to be smooth.

Definition 1
.

The
Nijenhuis torsion of
\boldsymbol{K}
is the vector-valued
2
-form defined by

(2.1)

\tau_{\boldsymbol{K}}(X,Y):=\boldsymbol{K}^{2}[X,Y]+[\boldsymbol{K}X,\boldsymbol{K}Y]-\boldsymbol{K}\Big([X,\boldsymbol{K}Y]+[\boldsymbol{K}X,Y]\Big),

where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
[\ ,\ ]
denotes the Lie bracket of two vector fields.

Definition 2
.

The Haantjes torsion of
\boldsymbol{K}
is the vector-valued
2
-form defined by

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}_{\boldsymbol{K}}(X,Y):=\boldsymbol{K}^{2}\tau_{\boldsymbol{K}}(X,Y)+\tau_{\boldsymbol{K}}(\boldsymbol{K}X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)-\boldsymbol{K}\Big(\tau_{\boldsymbol{K}}(X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)+\tau_{\boldsymbol{K}}(\boldsymbol{K}X,Y)\Big).

Definition 3
.

A Haantjes (Nijenhuis) operator is a (1,1)-tensor whose Haantjes (Nijenhuis) torsion identically vanishes.

A simple, relevant case of Haantjes operator is that of a tensor
\boldsymbol{K}
which takes a diagonal form in a local chart
\boldsymbol{x}=(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
:

(2.3)

\boldsymbol{K}(\boldsymbol{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{i}.

Here
\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}):=\lambda^{i}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\in C^{\infty}(M)
are the eigenvalues of
\boldsymbol{K}
and
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{n}}\right)
are the fields forming the so called
natural frame
associated with the local chart
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
. As is well known, the Haantjes torsion of the diagonal operator (
2.3
) vanishes.

We also recall that two frames
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
and
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
are said to be equivalent if
n
nowhere vanishing smooth
functions
f_{i}
exist, such that

X_{i}=f_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})Y_{i}\ ,\qquad\qquad i=1,\ldots,n\ .

Definition 4
.

An
integrable
frame is a reference frame equivalent to a natural frame.

Haantjes operators exhibit a wealth of algebraic properties, in some cases even richer than those of Nijenhuis operators.

Relevant examples of Haantjes operators in classical mechanics and in Riemannian geometry have been proposed, for instance, in
[
51
]
,
[
53
]
,
[
54
]
,
[
55
]
.

2.2.
Haantjes algebras

The crucial concept of Haantjes algebras has been introduced in
[
55
]
and further studied and generalized in
[
52
]
.

Definition 5
.

A Haantjes algebra on
M
is a set
\mathscr{H}
of Haantjes operators
\boldsymbol{K}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}(M)
that generate:

•

a free module over the ring of smooth functions on
M
:

(2.4)

\mathcal{H}_{\left(f\boldsymbol{K}_{1}+g\boldsymbol{K}_{2}\right)}(X,Y)=\mathbf{0}\,,\qquad\forall\,X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,,\quad\forall\,f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)\,,\quad\forall\penalty\ \boldsymbol{K}_{1},\boldsymbol{K}_{2}\in\mathscr{H};

•

a ring w.r.t. the composition operation

(2.5)

\mathcal{H}_{(\boldsymbol{K}_{1}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{2})}(X,Y)=\mathbf{0}\,,\qquad\forall\,\boldsymbol{K}_{1},\boldsymbol{K}_{2}\in\mathscr{H},\quad\forall\,X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,.

If

(2.6)

\boldsymbol{K}_{1}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{2}=\boldsymbol{K}_{2}\,\boldsymbol{K}_{1}\,,\quad\qquad\ \forall\penalty\ \boldsymbol{K}_{1},\boldsymbol{K}_{2}\in\mathscr{H},

the algebra
\mathscr{H}
is said to be an Abelian Haantjes algebra.

In essence, we may regard
\mathscr{H}
as an associative algebra of Haantjes operators.

Remark 1
.

Abelian Haantjes algebras admit the existence of coordinate systems, called Haantjes coordinates, in which all
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
can be expressed simultaneously in a block-diagonal form. In particular, if
\mathscr{H}
consists of semisimple operators, then each
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
takes a purely diagonal form
[
55
]
in these coordinates.

2.3.
The symplectic-Haantjes manifolds

If we endow a symplectic manifold with an algebra of Haantjes operators compatible with the symplectic structure, we obtain a symplectic-Haantjes (or
\omega\mathscr{H}
) manifold.

Beyond their intrinsic mathematical properties, these manifolds are significant because they provide a simple yet sufficiently general framework within which the theory of Hamiltonian integrable systems can be rigorously formulated.

Definition 6
.

A symplectic–Haantjes (or
\omega\mathscr{H}
) manifold of class
m
is a triple
(M,\omega,\mathscr{H})
which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

(M,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold of dimension
2\,n
;

(ii)

\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian Haantjes algebra of rank
m
;

(iii)

(\omega,\mathscr{H})
are algebraically compatible, that is

(2.7)

\omega(X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)=\omega(\boldsymbol{K}X,Y)\,,\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H},

or equivalently

(2.8)

\boldsymbol{\Omega}\,\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\,,\qquad\ \forall\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}.

Hereafter
\boldsymbol{\Omega}:=\omega^{\flat}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)
denotes the fiber bundles isomorphism defined by

(2.9)

\omega(X,Y)=\langle\boldsymbol{\Omega}X,Y\rangle\,,\qquad\qquad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

The map
P:=\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{-1}:\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(M)
is the Poisson bivector induced by the symplectic two-form
\omega
.

We also remind the following

Proposition 1
.

[
54
]

Given a
2n
-dimensional
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold
M
, every generalized eigen–distribution
ker(\boldsymbol{K}-\lambda_{i}\boldsymbol{I})^{{\rho_{i}}}
,
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
, is of even rank.
Therefore, the geometric and algebraic multiplicities of each eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})
are even.

Let us consider the spectral decomposition of the tangent spaces of
M
given by

(2.10)

T_{\boldsymbol{x}}M=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{D}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}),\qquad\qquad\mathcal{D}_{i}(\boldsymbol{x}):=ker\big(\boldsymbol{K}-\lambda_{i}\boldsymbol{I}\big)^{\rho_{i}}(\boldsymbol{x}),

where
\rho_{i}
is the Riesz index of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
. Due to the latter Proposition, as
rank

\mathcal{D}_{i}\geq 2,\ i=1,\ldots s
we conclude that the number of distinct eigenvalues of any
Haantjes operator
\boldsymbol{K}
of an
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold is not greater than
n
.

2.4.
Haantjes chains

Haantjes chains, in the present formulation, have been introduced in
[
54
]
and will be extensively used in the forthcoming analysis.

Definition 7
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a Haantjes algebra on
M
of rank
m
. We shall say that a smooth function
H
generates a Haantjes chain
\mathscr{C}
of closed 1-forms of length
m
if there exists
a distinguished basis
\{\boldsymbol{K}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{K}_{m}\}
of
\mathscr{H}

such that

(2.11)

\mathrm{d}(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}H)=\boldsymbol{0}\ ,\quad\qquad i=1,\ldots,m\ ,

where
\boldsymbol{K}^{T}_{i}:\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)
is the transposed operator of
\boldsymbol{K}_{i}
. The (locally) exact 1-forms

(2.12)

\mathrm{d}H_{i}=\boldsymbol{K}^{T}_{i}\,\mathrm{d}H,

which are supposed to be linearly independent, are said to be the elements of the Haantjes chain of length
m
generated by
H
. The functions
H_{i}
are called the potential functions of the chain.

Given a Haantjes algebra, to investigate the existence of
Haantjes chains, we focus on the co-distribution of rank
r\leq m
generated by a given function
H
via an arbitrary basis

\{\boldsymbol{K}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{K}_{m}\}
of
\mathscr{H}
, i.e.

(2.13)

\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ}:=Span\{\boldsymbol{K}_{1}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\ldots,\boldsymbol{K}_{m}^{T}\,\mathrm{d}H\}\ ,

and on the distribution
\mathcal{D}_{H}
of the vector fields annihilated by them, which has rank
n-r
. Importantly, these distributions are independent of the chosen basis of
\mathscr{H}
.

A geometric characterization of the existence of a Haantjes chain generated by a smooth function
H
is provided by the subsequent results.

Theorem 1

(
[
54
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a Haantjes algebra on
M
of rank
m
, and
H
a smooth function on
M
. Let
\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ}
be the codistribution (
2.13
),
assumed of rank
m
(independent on
\boldsymbol{x}
),
and let
\mathcal{D}_{H}
be the distribution of the vector fields annihilated by the
1
-forms of
\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ}
.
Then, the function
H
generates a Haantjes chain
(
2.11
)

if and only if
\mathcal{D}^{\circ}_{H}
(or equivalently
\mathcal{D}_{H}
) is Frobenius-integrable.

The following result provides an useful criterion for the existence of a Haantjes chain of functions in involution.

Theorem 2

(
[
54
]
)
.

A function
H
generates a Haantjes chain only if it satisfies the invariance condition

(2.14)

\boldsymbol{K}^{T}(\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ})\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{H}^{\circ}\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}\ .

2.5.
Jacobi manifolds

The fundamental notions of Jacobi and Poisson manifolds were introduced by Lichnerowicz in
[
35
,
36
]
. The theory of Jacobi structures has been further developed in
[
41
,
27
,
33
,
1
,
17
,
59
]
.

Definition 8
.

A Jacobi manifold is a triple
(M,\Lambda,E)
where
M
is a smooth manifold,
\Lambda
is a skew-symmetric contravariant smooth 2-tensor field on
M
and
E
is a smooth vector field on
M
such that

(2.15)

[\Lambda,\Lambda]_{SN}=2E\wedge\Lambda\qquad\text{and}\qquad[\Lambda,E]_{SN}=0.

Here
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket on
M
.
The pair
(\Lambda,E)
is said to be a Jacobi structure on
M
.

If
(M,\Lambda,E)
is a Jacobi manifold, then the Jacobi structure
(\Lambda,E)
induces a Lie bracket on
C^{\infty}(M)
defined for all
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
by

(2.16)

\{f,g\}=\Lambda(\mathrm{d}f,\mathrm{d}g)+fEg-gEf.

This is a bilinear and skew-symmetric bracket, called the
Jacobi bracket
, that satisfies the Jacobi identity and fulfills the weak Leibniz rule, namely

supp(\{f,g\})\subseteq supp(f)\cap supp(g)
.

Definition 9
.

A Jacobi bracket is a Lie bracket on
C^{\infty}(M)
that fulfills the weak Leibniz rule.

Any Jacobi structure defines a Jacobi bracket and conversely, one can proves that any Jacobi bracket arises from a Jacobi structure
[
27
,
41
]
. Besides, the space
C^{\infty}(M)
endowed with a Jacobi bracket represents a local Lie algebra in the sense of Kirillov
[
29
]
. Conversely, given a local Lie algebra on
M
, it defines on it a Jacobi structure.

Remark 2
.

If
E=0
, then
[\Lambda,\Lambda]=0
and a Jacobi manifold reduces to a Poisson manifold
(M,P)
with the identification
P=\Lambda
.

Let
(M,\Lambda,E)
be a Jacobi manifold.
For each
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
there exists a unique smooth vector field
X_{f}
on
M
such that

(2.17)

\{g,f\}=X_{f}g+gEf,\,\qquad\forall\,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

Here
\{,\}
denotes the Jacobi bracket defined by
\Lambda
and
X_{f}
is the Hamiltonian vector field associated with
f
. In terms of the induced map
\Lambda^{\sharp}
we have

(2.18)

X_{f}=\Lambda^{\sharp}(\mathrm{d}f)-fE.

The following properties hold.

(i) The map
f\mapsto X_{f}
is a Lie algebra antihomomorphism between the Lie algebras
(C^{\infty}(M),\{,\})
and
(\mathfrak{X}(M),[,])
, i.e.,
X_{\{f,g\}}=-[X_{f},X_{g}]
.

(ii)
g\in C^{\infty}(M)
is constant along the integral curves of
X_{f}
if and only if
\{g,f\}-gEf=0
.

(iii)
L_{X_{f}}\Lambda=-Ef\Lambda
for all
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

(iv) If
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
are constant along the integral curves of
X_{h}
so is
\{f,g\}
.

Similar to the Poisson case, every Jacobi manifold naturally decomposes into disjoint submanifolds, where each submanifold inherits a Jacobi structure. We remind that the characteristic distribution of a Jacobi manifold is the distribution generated by its Hamiltonian vector fields.

Definition 10
.

A Jacobi manifold is said to be transitive if its characteristic distribution coincides with the whole tangent bundle.

According to
[
41
]
, transitive Jacobi manifolds are either
contact manifolds
, if their dimension is odd, or
locally conformal symplectic manifolds
, if their dimension is even.. The integrability of the characteristic distribution of a Jacobi manifold is clarified by the following important results.

Theorem 3

(Kirillov
[
29
]
)
.

The characteristic distribution of a Jacobi manifold is completely integrable in the sense of Stefan and Sussmann, thus it defines a Stefan foliation. Each leaf of this foliation inherits a transitive Jacobi structure.

Remark 3
.

Given a function
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
defines Hamiltonian dynamics on
(M,\Lambda,E)
whose trajectories are the integral curves of
X_{H}
. We say that
(M,\Lambda,E,H)
is a (Jacobi) Hamiltonian system with
(M,\Lambda,E)
the phase space and
H
the Hamiltonian function; Hamiltonian systems on Jacobi manifolds as dissipative systems, namely,
H
is a dissipated quantity (
X_{H}H=-HEH
). The local structure of Jacobi manifolds is not as simple to describe as the local structure of Poisson manifolds; in
[
14
]
, a generalized Weinstein’s splitting theorem is proposed. As in the Poisson case we have that the characteristic leaves of a Jacobi manifold are closed under the dynamics of any Hamiltonian vector field; so we can reduce the corresponding Hamilton equations to the characteristic leaves, which are either locally conformal
symplectic manifolds or contact manifolds.

2.6.
Contact manifolds

Contact Hamiltonian systems naturally describe dissipative systems (for details see
[
9
,
34
]
) and also have interesting applications in the modern geometric formulation of thermodynamics
[
8
]
.
For sake of completeness, we briefly review the formalism of contact geometry.

Definition 11
.

A contact manifold is an odd dimensional manifold
M
equipped with a 1-form
\theta
such that
\theta\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta^{n}\neq 0
, where
dim(M)=2n+1
.

The natural phase space for time-independent dissipative Hamiltonian systems is the canonical contact manifold
\mathbb{R}\times T^{*}Q
.

There is a wider notion of contact manifolds: some authors define a contact structure on a manifold as a one-codimensional maximally non-integrable distribution
[
32
,
5
,
22
,
23
]
. Locally, a contact structure can be expressed as the kernel of a one-form
\theta
satisfying
\theta\wedge\mathrm{d}\theta^{n}\neq 0
;
\theta
is said to be a local contact form.

Remark 4
.

Not every contact structure admits a global contact form. When this is the case, the contact manifold is said to be co-oriented. Every contact manifold
(M,\theta)
as in Definition
11
is a co-oriented contact manifold in the wider sense mentioned above by taking
{\rm Ker}(\theta)
as the non-integrable distribution. In the subsequent analysis, we shall tacitly restrict ourselves to co-oriented contact manifolds, by referring to them simply as contact manifolds.

Given a contact structure
\theta
on a manifold
M
, we can define a Jacobi structure
(\Lambda,E)
on
M
by the relations

(2.19)

\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{d}\theta(\sharp(\alpha),\sharp(\beta))\quad\text{and}\quad E=\sharp(\theta),

where
\sharp
is the inverse of the map
\flat:\mathfrak{X}(M)\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}(M)
defined by

(2.20)

\flat(X)=\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\theta+(\iota_{X}\theta)\theta.

Each contact manifold admits sets of Darboux coordinates. In fact, around any point
x\in M
there exist local coordinates
(q^{1},\ldots,q^{n},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n},z)
such that

(2.21)

\theta=dz-p_{i}dq^{i}.

Thus,
\Lambda
and
E
can be expressed in local form as

(2.22)

\Lambda=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}+p_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad E=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

A standard notion, crucial in the subsequent discussion, is that of Hamiltonian system in contact geometry.

Definition 12
.

A contact Hamiltonian system is a triple
(M,\theta,H)
where
(M,\theta)
is a contact manifold and
H:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a function, said to be the Hamiltonian function.

A Hamiltonian vector field
X_{f}
is defined as the unique vector field on
M
such that

(2.23)

\iota_{X_{f}}\theta=-f\hskip 28.45274pt\rm{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\iota_{X_{f}}\mathrm{d}\theta=\mathrm{d}f-(Rf)\theta,

where
R
is the Reeb vector field, characterized by the relations

(2.24)

\iota_{R}\theta=1\hskip 28.45274pt\rm{and}\hskip 28.45274pt\iota_{R}\mathrm{d}\theta=0.

The evolution of a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
(an observable) along the trajectories of the system reads

(2.25)

\dot{f}=L_{X_{H}}f=X_{H}f=\{f,H\}-fRH.

We say that a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
is a constant of motion (or a conserved quantity) for the Hamiltonian
H
if it is constant along its trajectories, that is,
L_{X_{H}}f=0
(equivalently,
\{f,H\}-fRH=0
). The Hamiltonian function
H
itself is not a constant of motion, since

(2.26)

\dot{H}=-H\frac{\partial H}{\partial z}.

In other words,
H
is a dissipated quantity. In
[
10
,
21
,
32
]
a notion of dissipated quantity within the contact geometry has been proposed:

Definition 13
.

A dissipated quantity
of the contact Hamiltonian system
(M,\theta,H)
is a function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
that is dissipated at the same rate as the contact Hamiltonian
H
, i.e., it satisfies the relation
X_{H}f=-fRH
.

In particular, the existence of symmetries for dissipative systems has been discussed in
[
21
,
32
]
, where a Noether-like theorem, the dissipation theorem, has been proved.

Definition 14

Given a

3.
An extension of the theory of Haantjes algebras

In this section, we introduce new geometric structures by combining Haantjes geometry and the theory of Jacobi manifolds in a new theoretical framework.

3.1.
Nijenhuis and Haantjes extended tensors

The notion of
Jacobi-Nijenhuis manifolds
was introduced in
[
42
]
. Another, stricter definition was proposed in
[
49
]
. The main motivation for the latter construction is that it generalizes in a natural way the notion of Poisson-Nijenhuis manifolds introduced by Magri and Morosi in
[
40
]
in the study of Hamiltonian integrable systems. In both constructions
[
42
]
and
[
49
]
, the notion of Nijenhuis torsion has to be extended conveniently.
The aim of this section is to extend these ideas to the more general setting of Haantjes geometry.

Let
(M,\Lambda,E)
be a Jacobi manifold. We consider the quadruple
(\boldsymbol{K},Y,\gamma,k)
, where
\boldsymbol{K}
is a (1,1)-tensor field,
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\gamma\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
k\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Out of these objects we construct a new linear map. Precisely, let
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-linear map defined by

(3.1)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(X,f):=(\boldsymbol{K}X+fY,\gamma(X)+kf)

for all pairs
(X,f)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Note that the space
\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
converts into a real Lie algebra once endowed with the bracket

[,]:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
defined by

(3.2)

[(X,f),(Z,h)]=\big([X,Z],\mathrm{d}h(X)-\mathrm{d}f(Z)\big),

for all
(X,f),(Z,h)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.
Let us also introduce the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
map
(\alpha,f):\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to C^{\infty}(M)
defined by

(3.3)

(\alpha,f)(X,h):=\alpha(X)+fh.

The transposed map

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{T}:\Omega^{1}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\Omega^{1}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
is defined by

(3.4)

(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{T}(\alpha,f))(X,h):=(\alpha,f)\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(X,h).

Explicitly, we have

(3.5)

(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{T}(\alpha,f))(X,h)=(\alpha,f)(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}X+hY,\gamma(X)+kh)=\alpha(\boldsymbol{K}X+hY)+(\gamma(X)+kh)f

Definition 15
.

[
42
]

The Nijenhuis torsion
\tau(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})
of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}
is the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-bilinear map given by

\displaystyle\tau(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})((X,f),(Z,h))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(X,f),\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(Z,h)]-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}[\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(X,f),(Z,h)]-\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}[(X,f),\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(Z,h)]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{2}[(X,f),(Z,h)]

for all
(X,f),(Z,h)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

By analogy, we propose the new

Definition 16
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\to\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
be the linear map (
3.1
). The Haantjes torsion of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}
is the
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
-bilinear map given by

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})((X,f),(Z,h))

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\tau(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})((X,f),(Z,h))+\tau(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(X,f),\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(Z,h))

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\big(\tau(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})((X,f),\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(Z,h))+\tau(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}(X,f),(Z,h))\big)

for all
(X,f),(Z,h)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 17
.

We shall say that
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}
is a Haantjes (Nijenhuis) operator on
\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
if its Haantjes (Nijenhuis) torsion vanishes.

It is natural to propose an extension of the notion of Haantjes algebras given in Definition
5
to the case of Haantjes operators on

.

Definition 18
.

An extended Haantjes algebra on
M
is a set
\mathscr{H}
of Haantjes operators on
\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that
\forall\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\in\mathscr{H}
:

(3.8)

\mathcal{H}\left(\lambda_{1}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1}+\lambda_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\right)((X,f),(Z,h))=\mathbf{0}\,,\qquad\forall\,X,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,,\quad\forall\,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},f,h\in C^{\infty}(M);

(3.9)

\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1}\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{2})((X,f),(Z,h)=\mathbf{0}\,,\quad\forall\,X,Z\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\,\quad\forall\,f,h\in C^{\infty}(M).

4.
The theory of extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds

4.1.
Extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds

In this section we introduce the central notion of our construction. To this aim, let
(M,\Lambda,E)
be a Jacobi manifold, we denote by
(\Lambda,E)^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\to TM\times\mathbb{R}
the vector bundle map defined by

(4.1)

(\Lambda,E)^{\sharp}(\alpha,f):=(\Lambda^{\sharp}(\alpha)+fE,-\alpha(E)).

Definition 19
.

An extended Jacobi-Haantjes (EJH) manifold of class
m
is a quadruple
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
, where
\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian extended Haantjes algebra on
M
of rank
m
such that

(4.2)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\circ(\Lambda,E)^{\sharp}=(\Lambda,E)^{\sharp}\circ\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}^{T},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\in\mathscr{H}.

Remark 5
.

The notion of an extended Jacobi-Haantjes structure, as defined above, can be relaxed. Rather than requiring that the Haantjes torsion
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})
identically vanishes on
M
, one can require that it vanishes on the image of
(\Lambda,E)^{\sharp}
. This broader notion, in the Haantjes scenario, parallels that of a Jacobi-Nijenhuis manifold, as proposed by Marrero et al. in
[
42
]
. Our formulation of Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds in Definition
19
is more similar to the Jacobi-Nijenhuis structures proposed by Petalidou et al. in
[
49
]
, which we generalize directly.

By analogy with Definition
7
, we extend the notion of Haantjes chain to the case of an extended Haantjes algebra.

Definition 20
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be an extended Haantjes algebra on
M
of rank
m
. We shall say that a function
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
generates an extended Haantjes chain
\mathscr{C}
on
M
of length
m
, if there exists a distinguished basis
\{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{m}\}
of
\mathscr{H}
and a set of functions
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(4.3)

(\mathrm{d}H_{i},H_{i})=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}^{T}(\mathrm{d}H,H),\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.

The functions
H_{i}
are said to be the potential functions of the chain.

One of the main results of the theory of extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds is the following

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
be an extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold of class
m
. If
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
generates an extended Haantjes chain
\mathscr{C}
on
M
of length
m
,
then the potential functions
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
of the chain are independent and dissipated quantities in involution of the Hamiltonian system
(M,\Lambda,E,H)
.

Proof.

Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
be the potential functions of the chain
\mathscr{C}
and
\{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{m}\}
be a distinguished basis of
\mathscr{H}
such that equation (
4.3
) is satisfied.
As before,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}=(\boldsymbol{K}_{i},Y_{i},\gamma_{i},k_{i})
, where
\boldsymbol{K}_{i}
is a (1,1)-tensor field on
M
,
Y_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
\gamma_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
k_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Thus, for all
(X,f)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)
we obtain

(4.4)

\begin{split}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}^{T}(\mathrm{d}H,H)\right)(X,f)&=(\mathrm{d}H,H)\left(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}(X,f)\right)\\
&=(\mathrm{d}H,H)(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X+fY_{i},\gamma_{i}X+k_{i}f)\\
&=\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X+fY_{i})+(\gamma_{i}X+k_{i}f)H\\
&=(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H+H\gamma_{i})X+(Y_{i}H+Hk_{i})f.\end{split}

Besides,

(4.5)

(\mathrm{d}H_{i},H_{i})(X,f)=\mathrm{d}H_{i}(X)+H_{i}f,

therefore

(4.6)

\mathrm{d}H_{i}=\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H+H\gamma_{i}\quad\textit{and}\quad H_{i}=Y_{i}H+Hk_{i}.

The compatibility condition (
4.2
) is equivalent to the following system of equations for all
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
:

(4.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\alpha,\beta)+\alpha(Y_{i})\beta(E)=\Lambda(\alpha,\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\beta)-\beta(Y_{i})\alpha(E)\\
\Lambda(\gamma_{i},\alpha)=(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\alpha)(E)-k_{i}\alpha(E)\\
\gamma_{i}(E)=0\end{array}\right.

First, we consider the commutator

(4.8)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H\}&=\Lambda(\mathrm{d}H_{i},\mathrm{d}H)+H_{i}EH-HEH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H+H\gamma_{i},\mathrm{d}H)+H_{i}EH-HEH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+H\Lambda(\gamma_{i},\mathrm{d}H)+H_{i}EH-HEH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+H((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E)-k_{i}(\mathrm{d}H)(E))+H_{i}EH-HEH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+H(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E)-Hk_{i}EH+H_{i}EH-HEH_{i}.\end{split}

Taking into account that

(4.9)

(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E)=(\mathrm{d}H_{i}-H\gamma_{i})(E)=\mathrm{d}H_{i}-H\gamma_{i}(E)=EH_{i};

we obtain

(4.10)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H\}&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+HEH_{i}-Hk_{i}EH+H_{i}EH-HEH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)-Hk_{i}EH+H_{i}EH\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)-(H_{i}-Y_{i}H)EH+H_{i}EH\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+Y_{i}HEH.\end{split}

From the first equation of system (
4.7
) we deduce

(4.11)

\begin{split}\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+Y_{i}HEH&=\Lambda(\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)-Y_{i}HEH\\
&=-\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)-Y_{i}HEH,\end{split}

which implies that

\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+Y_{i}HEH=0.

Consequently,

(4.12)

\{H_{i},H\}=0.

Let us prove now that
\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=0
. To this aim, observe that

(4.13)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H_{j}\}&=\Lambda(\mathrm{d}H_{i},\mathrm{d}H_{j})+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H+H\gamma_{i},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H+H\gamma_{j})+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)+H\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\gamma_{j})+H\Lambda(\gamma_{i},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)\\
&\quad+H^{2}\Lambda(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j})+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)+H(k_{j}(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E)-(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E))\\
&\quad+H((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E)-k_{i}(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)(E))+H^{2}((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\gamma_{j})(E)\\
&\quad-k_{i}\gamma_{j}(E))+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)+H(k_{j}EH_{i}-k_{i}EH_{j}+\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E-\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E))\\
&\quad+H^{2}\gamma_{j}(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)+H_{j}EH_{i}-Y_{j}HRH_{i}-H_{i}EH_{j}+Y_{i}HEH_{j}\\
&\quad+H\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E-\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E))+H^{2}\gamma_{j}(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)-Y_{j}HRH_{i}+Y_{i}HEH_{j}\\
&\quad+H\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E-\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E))+H^{2}\gamma_{j}(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E).\end{split}

Since
\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian algebra, we necessarily have
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{j}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{j}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
. For all
(X,f)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)
we obtain

(4.14)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{j}(X,f)=(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}X+Y_{i}\gamma_{j}X+f\boldsymbol{K}_{i}Y_{j}+fk_{j}Y_{i},(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\gamma_{i})(X)+k_{i}\gamma_{j}X+f\gamma_{i}Y_{j}+fk_{i}k_{j}).

Explicitly,

(4.15)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{j}=(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}+Y_{i}\otimes\gamma_{j},\boldsymbol{K}_{i}Y_{j}+k_{j}Y_{i},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\gamma_{i}+k_{i}\gamma_{j},\gamma_{i}Y_{j}+k_{i}k_{j}),

where
Y_{i}\otimes\gamma_{j}
is the (1,1)-tensor field on
M
defined by
(Y_{i}\otimes\gamma_{j})(X):=Y_{i}\gamma_{j}X
. By taking
X=E
we obtain

(4.16)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E=\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E\\
\gamma_{i}(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E)=\gamma_{j}(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)\\
\gamma_{i}Y_{j}=\gamma_{j}Y_{i}.\end{array}\right.

Now, as
\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=-\{H_{j},H_{i}\}
, we have that
\gamma_{i}(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E)=-\gamma_{j}(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)
. Combining this relation with the second one of system (
4.16
), we deduce that
\gamma_{i}(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E)=0
. Consequently, the expression of the commutator
\{H_{i},H_{j}\}
reduces to

(4.17)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)-Y_{j}HRH_{i}+Y_{i}HEH_{j}.

On the other hand,
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{i}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{j}=:\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{s}
is an element of the algebra
\mathscr{H}
. Thus,

(4.18)

\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{s}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+Y_{s}HEH=0.

Being
\boldsymbol{K}_{s}=\boldsymbol{K}_{i}\boldsymbol{K}_{j}+Y_{i}\otimes\gamma_{j}
and
Y_{s}=\boldsymbol{K}_{i}Y_{j}+k_{j}Y_{i}
, we can easily prove that

(4.19)

\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{s}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+Y_{s}HEH=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)-Y_{j}HRH_{i}+Y_{i}HEH_{j},

i.e.,

(4.20)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{s}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+Y_{s}HEH=0.

∎

4.2.
Extended Jacobi-Haantjes structures and integrability of contact Hamiltonian systems

The characteristic leaves of a Jacobi manifold are invariant under Hamiltonian dynamics. As a result, the dynamics of dissipative Hamiltonian systems are typically studied on contact or locally conformal symplectic (LCS) manifolds, which correspond to the characteristic leaves of a Jacobi manifold. The concept of complete integrability in the context of Hamiltonian systems on LCS manifolds, apart from a few studies (see, e.g.,
[
61
]
), has not received much attention. In contrast, the notion of complete integrability for contact Hamiltonian systems has been proposed in
[
28
,
48
,
26
]
. In
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
, the existence of action-angle variables for the dynamics generated by the Reeb vector field on contact manifolds has been proved. In
[
12
,
13
]
, a wide extension of the classical Liouville-Arnold theorem
[
2
]
in contact geometry, and the existence of action-angle type coordinates has been established (for related results, see also
[
25
,
62
]
). It is worth mentioning that in
[
7
]
, a notion of complete integrability for a contact Hamiltonian system was proposed under the restrictive hypothesis that the Hamiltonian function be preserved by the Reeb vector field. Following
[
12
,
13
]
, we adopt the a more general notion of integrability, also valid for dissipative systems.

Definition 21
.

A contact Hamiltonian system
(M,\theta,H)
on a (co-oriented) contact manifold
M
, with
dim(M)=2n+1
, is said to be completely integrable if there exist
n+1
functions
H_{1},\ldots,H_{n+1}
(including
H
) in involution with respect to the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\theta}
, called integrals, such that
\rank\,\langle\mathrm{d}H_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{d}H_{n+1}\rangle\geq n
.

As one of the main results of our theory, we propose a theorem which establishes the complete integrability of a contact Hamiltonian system in the geometric framework of EJH manifolds. This result is analogous to the Liouville-Haantjes theorem of the theory of
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds (theorem 39 in
[
54
]
). In order to formulate it, we premise the concept of nondegenerate contact Hamiltonian system.

Definition 22
.

A completely integrable contact Hamiltonian system
(M,\theta,H)
on a contact manifold
M
, with
\dim M=2n+1
is said to be nondegenerate with respect to a set of action-angle variables
\{(J_{k},\phi_{k},Z)\}
on
M
if

(4.21)

det\bigg(\frac{\partial\nu_{k}}{\partial J_{i}}\bigg)=\det\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}H}{\partial J_{i}\partial J_{k}}\bigg)\neq 0

where
\nu_{k}(J):=\frac{\partial H}{\partial J_{i}}
are the associated frequencies of the system.

We can now state the central result of this section.

Theorem 5

(Liouville-Haantjes theorem for EJH manifolds)
.

Let
(M,\theta)
be a contact manifold with
dim(M)=2n+1
and
(\Lambda,E)
the Jacobi structure defined by
\theta
.
Assume that
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
is an extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold of class at least
n
. If
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
generates an extended Haantjes chain of length at least
n
, then the contact Hamiltonian system
(M,\theta,H)
is completely integrable.

Conversely, for every completely integrable and non-degenerate contact Hamiltonian system
(M,\theta,H)
there exists an Abelian extended Haantjes algebra
\mathscr{H}
of rank at least
n
such that
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
is an extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold and
H
generates an extended Haantjes chain of length at least
n
.

Proof.

The first statement is a corollary of Theorem
4
.
Let us prove the converse statement. Given a completely integrable contact Hamiltonian system
(M,\theta,H)
admitting
\big(H_{0}=H,H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}\big)
dissipated quantities in involution such that

\rank\,\langle\mathrm{d}H_{0},\mathrm{d}H_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{d}H_{n}\rangle\geq n,

according to
[
13
]
there exist coordinates
(\phi^{1},\ldots,\phi^{n},J_{1},\ldots,J_{n},Z)
, where
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{n})
are said to be action coordinates, while
(\phi^{1},\ldots,\phi^{n})
are angle coordinates, such that the contact Hamilton equations of motion for the Hamiltonian functions
H_{j}
are

(4.22)

\dot{\phi^{i}}=N^{i}_{j},\hskip 28.45274pt\dot{J_{i}}=0,\hskip 28.45274pt\dot{z}=N^{0}_{j},,\qquad j=1,\ldots,n+1

Here
N^{0}_{j},N^{1}_{j},\ldots,N^{n}_{j}
are functions on
M
depending only on the coordinates
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{n})
. This is equivalent to saying that
H_{0}=H,H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
depend only on
(J_{1},\ldots,J_{n})
.

As a basis for the extended Haantjes algebra we wish to construct, we can consider the basis given by the
diagonal operators

\{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{n}\}
, where
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0}
is the extended identity operator, locally expressed by

(4.23)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{0}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}+\frac{\partial}{\partial J_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}J_{i}+\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\otimes\mathrm{d}Z,0,0,1\right),

whereas the remaining operators read

(4.24)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{j}=\left(\frac{\nu_{i}^{j}(J)}{\nu_{i}(J)}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi^{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}+\frac{\partial}{\partial J_{i}}\otimes\mathrm{d}J_{i}\right),\left(\frac{H_{j}}{\nu_{i}(J)}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial J_{i}},0,0\right),

which are well-defined, due to the nondegeneracy assumption; in addition, at least
n
of them are linearly independent since
\rank\,\langle\mathrm{d}H_{0},\mathrm{d}H_{1},\ldots,\mathrm{d}H_{n}\rangle\geq n
, so they define an Abelian extended Haantjes algebra
\mathscr{H}
of rank at least
n
. In turn,
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
is an extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold and
H
generates and extended Haantjes chain
\mathscr{C}
with potential functions
H_{0}=H,H_{1},\ldots,H_{n}
.
∎

Example 1
.

Consider the contact Hamiltonian system with

and Hamiltonian function
H(q,p,z)=p-z
. It is shown in
[
13
,
12
]
that this system is completely integrable. Let us consider the Haantjes operators

(4.25)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1}=\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\otimes dq+\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\otimes dp+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dz,0,0,1\bigg)

and

(4.26)

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{2}=\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\otimes dq+\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\otimes dp,p\frac{\partial}{\partial p},0,0\bigg).

We have that
\mathscr{H}=\langle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\rangle
is an Abelian extended Haantjes algebra of rank
2
, and
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
is an extended Jacobi-Haantjes manifold, where
(\Lambda,E)
is the Jacobi structure defined by the contact form; in addition,
H
generates an extended Haantjes chain
\mathscr{C}
with the distinguished basis
\{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{1},\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\}
and potential functions

,
12
]
).

5.
A geometric reduction: the Jacobi-Haantjes structures

As a non-trivial reduction of the extended Jacobi-Haantjes structures, it is useful to examine the compatibility condition (equation
4.2
) within a simplified setting wherein a single operator
\boldsymbol{K}
acts on
\mathfrak{X}(M)
. Such a setting arises, for example, in the context of symplectic-Haantjes and Poisson-Haantjes geometries, where the extended structures vanish, i.e.,
Y=\boldsymbol{0}
,
\gamma=0
, and
k=0
. Under these conditions, equation (
4.2
) reduces to
\boldsymbol{K}\Lambda=\Lambda\boldsymbol{K}^{T}
. This scenario is analyzed in detail in the subsequent subsections and is further adapted to the contact and LCS cases. Moreover, we establish a connection between this framework and the reduction procedures based on the existence of particular integrals in particular involution.

5.1.
Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds

5.1.1.
The algebraic construction

Definition 23
.

A Jacobi-Haantjes (JH) manifold of class
m
is a quadruple
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
, where
(M,\Lambda,E)
is a Jacobi manifold and
\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian Haantjes algebra of rank
m
such that
\forall\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
the following compatibility condition holds:

(5.1)

\boldsymbol{K}\Lambda=\Lambda\boldsymbol{K}^{T}.

Definition
23
naturally extends the notion of Poisson-Haantjes manifold presented in
[
58
]
to the more general setting of Jacobi structures. In fact, any Poisson-Haantjes manifold
(M,P,\mathscr{H})
is a Jacobi-Haantjes manifold
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
with the Jacobi structure given by
\Lambda=P
,
E=0
.

5.1.2.
Partial integrability

A partial (more recently, referred to as
particular
) integral is a smooth real-valued function on phase space that remains conserved only along certain trajectories - specifically, those lying on a dynamically invariant submanifold. In other words, a particular integral is not necessarily conserved on the entire phase space, but only on a subset where the dynamics restrict the motion. Every constant of motion (or integral of motion) is, by definition, a particular integral. Interestingly, as shown in
[
4
]
, even dissipated quantities - those not conserved globally - can qualify as particular integrals when considered within this framework.

The concept of particular integrals has been especially explored in relation to the reduction of the equations of motion and the broader framework of partial integrability
[
15
,
4
]
. The latter refers to the integrability by quadratures of the Hamiltonian equations of motion, not on the entire phase space, but rather restricted to a dynamically invariant submanifold. This submanifold is typically characterized by a set of particular integrals in mutual involution, which allows for a partial reduction of the system and facilitates its explicit integration within that subspace. More formally, the following results hold.

Let
(M,\Lambda,E)
be a Jacobi manifold.

Theorem 6
.

[
4
]

If
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
are such that

(5.2)

\{f_{i},H\}\ =\ a^{i}_{1}f_{1}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,a^{i}_{k}f_{k}\ ,

for some functions
a^{i}_{1},\cdots,a^{i}_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
, then the level set

(5.3)

M_{f}=\{x\in M:\ f_{1}(x)=0,\ldots,f_{k}(x)=0\}

is an invariant submanifold with respect to the Hamiltonian dynamics given by
X_{H}
.

Observe that if
a^{i}_{1}=\ldots=a^{i}_{k}=0
then
f_{i}
is a constant of motion.

Definition 24
.

The functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying equation (
5.2
) are called particular integrals of the Hamiltonian system
(M,\Lambda,E,H)
.

Definition 25
.

The functions
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
are said to be in particular involution if

(5.4)

\{f_{i},f_{j}\}\ =\ a^{ij}_{1}f_{1}\,+\,\cdots\,+\,a^{ij}_{n}f_{k}\ ,

for some functions
a^{ij}_{1},\cdots,a^{ij}_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

Theorem 7
.

[
4
]

If
f_{1},\cdots,f_{k}\in C^{\infty}(M)
are functionally-independent particular integrals of
(M,\Lambda,E,H)
in particular involution, then the reduced dynamics to
M_{f}
defines Hamiltonian dynamics with the number of degrees of freedom reduced by
k
.

Remark 6
.

If the reduced dynamics on
M_{f}
is a conservative system, then it is possible to integrate the reduced Hamilton equations of motion by quadratures, if so, the system
(M,\Lambda,E,H)
is said to be particular (or partial) integrable.

5.1.3.
JH structures and Integrability

There is a direct relationship between the Jacobi-Haantjes geometry and the notion of integrability and partial integrability, made explicit by the following

Proposition 2
.

Let
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
be a Jacobi-Haantjes manifold of class
m
. If
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
generates a Haantjes chain of length
m
, then the potential function
\{H_{1},\ldots H_{m}\}
of the chain satisfy

(5.5)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i},\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Proof.

Using eq. (
2.16
), we have

(5.6)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H_{j}\}&=\Lambda(\mathrm{d}H_{i},\mathrm{d}H_{j})+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\mathrm{d}H)+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}\\
&=\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)+H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}.\end{split}

As
\Lambda
is skew-symmetric and
\mathscr{H}
is Abelian, we have that
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}^{T}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}\mathrm{d}H,\mathrm{d}H)=0
. Consequently,

(5.7)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{i}EH_{j}-H_{j}EH_{i}.

∎

Remark 7
.

Equation (
5.7
) implies that

(5.8)

\dot{H}_{i}=-HEH_{i},

so we have that the functions
\{H_{1},...,H_{n}\}
are particular integrals in particular involution of the Hamiltonian system
(M,\Lambda,E,H)
. This property allows the reduction process of the Hamilton equations of motion associated with the potential functions of the Haantjes chain according to the construction developed in
[
4
]
, and possibly, their integrability by quadratures in a reduced phase space.

If
E=0
, i.e. the Jacobi structure reduces to a Poisson one, then the Hamiltonian functions belonging to a
Haantjes chain generated by
H
are in involution (in agreement with Lemma 1 in
[
58
]
). In this scenario, we recover the standard notion of complete integrability.

5.2.
From Jacobi-Haantjes to Poisson-Haantjes manifolds

There is a standard construction allowing us to associate an invertible Poisson structure with a Jacobi manifold, called
Poissonization
. To this aim, let us introduce the manifold
\tilde{M}:=M\times\mathbb{R}
, with coordinate
t
on
\mathbb{R}
and the bivector

\tilde{P}:=e^{-t}(\Lambda+\partial_{t}\wedge E),

which has vanishing Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket (Poisson bivector). The Jacobi and Poisson brackets are linked by the relation

\{f,g\}_{\text{Jacobi}}=e^{t}\{\tilde{f},\tilde{g}\}_{\tilde{P}}\penalty\ \vline_{\penalty\ t=0}

where
\tilde{f}
,
\tilde{g}
are the liftings of
f
and
g
to
C^{\infty}(\tilde{M})
. We remind that a Poisson-Haantjes (PH) manifold
(M,P,\mathscr{H})
is a Poisson manifold
(M,P)
endowed with a compatible Haantjes algebra
\mathscr{H}
of
(1,1)
-tensor fields:

(5.9)

\boldsymbol{K}P=P\boldsymbol{K}^{T},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}.

In the case of an even dimensional manifold
M
, the class of Poisson-Haantjes manifolds endowed with an invertible bivector
P
coincides with that of
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifolds, and the compatibility condition (
5.9
) reduces to relation (
2.8
).

From this analysis we easily deduce the following

Corollary 1
.

A Jacobi-Haantjes structure on a manifold
M
, by means of the Poissonization procedure, induces an invertible
P\mathscr{H}
structure, namely an
\omega\mathscr{H}
manifold, on
\tilde{M}
.

6.
Contact-Haantjes manifolds

6.1.
Main definitions and properties

We introduce here the notion of contact-Haantjes manifold, as a relevant geometric structure belonging to the class of Jacobi-Haantjes manifolds.

Precisely, let
(M,\theta)
be a contact manifold and
\mathscr{H}
be a Haantjes algebra of operators
K
on
M
. The contact structure naturally induces on
M
a Jacobi structure
(\Lambda,E)
given by
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{d}\theta(\sharp(\alpha),\sharp(\beta))
and
E=\sharp(\theta)=R
. Consequently, in order to construct a Jacobi-Haantjes manifold
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
, we need that for all
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
,
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\alpha,\beta)=\Lambda(\alpha,\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\beta)
, with is equivalent to require

(6.1)

\mathrm{d}\theta(\sharp(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\alpha),\sharp(\beta))=\mathrm{d}\theta(\sharp(\alpha),\sharp(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\beta)).

Assuming

(6.2)

\sharp\boldsymbol{K}^{T}=\boldsymbol{K}\sharp\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad\flat\boldsymbol{K}=\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\flat

equation (
6.1
) is equivalent to the relations

(6.3)

\mathrm{d}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)

and

(6.4)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X)\theta(Y)=\theta(X)\theta(\boldsymbol{K}Y).

Under this hypothesis,
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
acquires the structure of a quasi-Jacobi-Haantjes manifold. This construction motivates the following

Definition 26
.

A contact-Haantjes manifold of rank
m
is a triple
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
where
(M,\theta)
is a contact manifold and
\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian Haantjes algebra of rank
m
, satisfying the compatibility conditions (
6.3
) and (
6.4
).

Let us suppose that
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
generates a Haantjes chain with local potential functions
H_{i}
. We have

(6.5)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H\}&=\mathrm{d}H_{i}(X_{H})+H_{i}RH\\
&=\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{i}RH\\
&=\mathrm{d}\theta(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{i}RH\\
&=RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{i}RH.\end{split}

Taking into account that for a contact manifold
\dot{H}_{i}=\{H_{i},H\}-H_{i}RH
, the latter relation implies that

(6.6)

\dot{H}_{i}=RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H}).

Also, due to eq. (
5.8
), we obtain the equation

(6.7)

RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-HRH_{i},

which implies the relation

(6.8)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-H\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}.

Lemma 1
.

For any
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
we have

(6.9)

\boldsymbol{K}R=gR

for some smooth function
g
.

Proof.

It is a consequence of the compatibility with
\mathrm{d}\theta
.
∎

Remark 8
.

Notice that as
RH_{i}=\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R)=RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R)
, equation (
6.7
) is equivalent to

(6.10)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-H\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R).

Let us assume that for every
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
we have

(6.11)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=-f\theta(\boldsymbol{K}R).

Then, condition (
6.4
) holds on Hamiltonian vector fields:

(6.12)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})\theta(X_{g})=\theta(X_{f})\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{g}).

Theorem 8
.

Assume that for every
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
condition (
6.11
) holds.
Then the following relation holds:

(6.13)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{i}RH_{j}-H_{j}RH_{i}.

Proof.

We observe that

(6.14)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H_{j}\}&=\mathrm{d}H_{i}(X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=\mathrm{d}\theta(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=\mathrm{d}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H},X_{H_{j}})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=-\mathrm{d}H_{j}((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH_{j}\theta((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=-\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH_{j}\theta((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=\mathrm{d}\theta(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})-RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH_{j}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=-RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH_{j}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j},\end{split}

On the other hand, we have

(6.15)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})=-H_{j}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R)=-H_{j}\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}

and

(6.16)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-H\theta\big(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R\big)=-H\theta\Big(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\big(\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}R\big)\Big)=-H\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}\theta\big(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}R\big)=-H\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}\frac{RH_{j}}{RH},

Therefore, we deduce that

(6.17)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H_{j}\}&=-RH\left(-H\frac{RH_{i}RH_{j}}{RH^{2}}\right)+RH_{j}\left(-H\frac{RH_{i}}{R_{H}}\right)+RH(\left(-H_{j}\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}\right))+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=H\frac{RH_{i}RH_{j}}{RH}-H\frac{RH_{j}RH_{i}}{R_{H}}-H_{j}RH_{i}+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=H_{i}RH_{j}-H_{j}RH_{i};\end{split}

∎

The motivation for the hypothesis (
6.11
) is that, under that assumption, the latter result turns out to be in complete agreement with the general Jacobi-Haantjes framework, as stated in Proposition
2
.

6.2.

\boldsymbol{K}
be an operator satisfying
\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle q$}}K_{z}^{j}=p_{i}\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle qq$}}K_{i}^{j}
and
\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle q$}}K_{z}^{j}=p_{i}\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle qp$}}K_{i}^{j}
. We observe that

•

for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
homogeneous of degree zero in the momentum coordinates, we have

(6.26)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=-fK^{z}_{x}=-f\theta(\boldsymbol{K}R).

•

If we take
K^{z}_{z}=0
then, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
we obtain the relation

(6.27)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=0=-f\theta(\boldsymbol{K}R).

∎

As a result of the previous discussion, and under the assumption of the compatibility condition (
6.3
), we identify two distinct types of Haantjes algebras. The first type is defined by the condition
\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=0\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M)
;
the second type is characterized by the relation
\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=-f\theta(\boldsymbol{K}R)
for all homogeneous functions
f
on
M
of degree
0
in the momentum coordinates.
Accordingly, we introduce the following two definitions of special contact-Haantjes manifolds.

Definition 27
.

A special contact-Haantjes manifold of first kind and class
m
is a triple
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
, where
(M,\theta)
is a contact manifold and
\mathscr{H}
is a Haantjes algebra of rank
m
on
M
, such that for all
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
we have

(6.28)

\mathrm{d}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

and

(6.29)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=0\quad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M).

Definition 28
.

A special contact-Haantjes manifold of second kind and class
m
is a triple
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
, where
(M,\theta)
is a contact manifold and
\mathscr{H}
is a Haantjes algebra of rank
m
on
M
, such that for all
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
the following conditions are satisfied:

(6.30)

\mathrm{d}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

and

(6.31)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=-f\theta(\boldsymbol{K}R)

for all homogeneous functions
f
on
M
of degree
0
in the momenta (in Darboux coordinates).

Theorem 10
.

Let
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
be a contact-Haantjes manifold and
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
the generator of a Haantjes chain
\mathscr{C}
with potential functions
H_{i}
.

(i) If
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
is a first kind contact-Haantjes manifold, then

RH_{i}=0,\quad\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=0,\quad\{H_{i},H\}=H_{i}RH.

(ii) If both
H
and the potential functions
H_{i}
are homogeneous functions of degree
0
in the momenta and
(M,\theta,\mathscr{H})
is a second kind contact-Haantjes manifold, then
\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{i}RH_{j}-H_{j}RH_{i}
and
\{H_{i},H\}=-HRH_{i}
.

Proof.

From condition

(6.32)

\mathrm{d}\theta(KX,Y)=\mathrm{d}\theta(X,KY)\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)

we obtain

(6.33)

RH_{i}=\mathrm{d}H(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R)=RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R),

(6.34)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=-RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH_{j}\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H_{i}RH_{j}

and

(6.35)

\{H_{i},H\}=RH\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{i}RH.

Assuming
\theta(\boldsymbol{K}X_{f})=0
, the first statement of the theorem follows (
R
is regarded as the Hamiltonian vector field for the constant function
1
).

Concerning the second statement, observe that

(6.36)

\{H_{i},H\}=RH-H\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}R)+H_{i}RH=RH-H\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}=-HRH_{i}.

On the other hand

(6.37)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})=-H_{j}\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}

and

(6.38)

\theta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-H\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}\frac{RH_{j}}{RH}.

Consequently, we have

(6.39)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H_{j}\}&=-RH\left(-H\frac{RH_{i}RH_{j}}{RH^{2}}\right)+RH_{j}\left(-H\frac{RH_{i}}{R_{H}}\right)+RH(\left(-H_{j}\frac{RH_{i}}{RH}\right))+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=-H\frac{RH_{i}RH_{j}}{RH}-H\frac{RH_{j}RH_{i}}{R_{H}}-H_{j}RH_{i}+H_{i}RH_{j}\\
&=H_{i}RH_{j}-H_{j}RH_{i}.\end{split}

∎

7.
Locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes manifolds

By analogy with the case of contact-Haantjes manifolds, we introduce here the notion of locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes manifolds as another relevant reduction of the Jacobi-Haantjes structure introduced above.

Let
(M,\Omega,\eta)
be a LCS manifold and
\mathscr{H}
be a Haantjes algebra of operators
\boldsymbol{K}
on
M
. The LCS structure naturally induces on
M
a Jacobi structure
(\Lambda,E)
given by

\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=\Omega(\sharp(\alpha),\sharp(\beta))\hskip 5.69054pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69054ptE=\sharp(\eta).

Consequently, in order to construct a Jacobi-Haantjes manifold
(M,\Lambda,E,\mathscr{H})
, we need that for all
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
,
\Lambda(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\alpha,\beta)=\Lambda(\alpha,\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\beta)
, which amounts to require

(7.1)

\Omega(\sharp(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\alpha),\sharp(\beta))=\Omega(\sharp(\alpha),\sharp(\boldsymbol{K}^{T}\beta)),

or, equivalently

(7.2)

\Omega(\boldsymbol{K}X,Y)=\Omega(X,\boldsymbol{K}Y)\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M).

Thus, we are led to the following

Definition 29
.

A locally conformal symplectic-Haantjes manifold (in short LCSH manifold) of rank
m
is a quadruple
(M,\Omega,\eta,\mathscr{H})
where
(M,\Omega,\eta)
is a LCS manifold and
\mathscr{H}
is an Abelian Haantjes algebra of rank
m
, satisfying the compatibility condition (
7.2
).

Let
(M,\Omega,\eta,\mathscr{H})
be a LCSH manifold. Let us suppose that
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
generates a Haantjes chain with local potential functions
H_{i}
. We have

(7.3)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H\}&=dH_{i}(X_{H})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H})\\
&=dH(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H})\\
&=\Omega(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H})\\
&=H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H}).\end{split}

Taking into account that for a LCS manifold
\dot{H}_{i}=\{H_{i},H\}+H_{i}\eta(X_{H})
, the latter relation implies that

(7.4)

\dot{H}_{i}=H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H}).

Also, due to eq. (
5.8
), we have

(7.5)

H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-HEH_{i},

which implies the relation

(7.6)

\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=\eta(X_{H_{i}}).

On the other hand, since
\flat(E)=\eta
, i.e.,
\iota_{E}\Omega=\eta
, we have

(7.7)

\begin{split}\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})&=\Omega(E,\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})\\
&=-\Omega(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)\\
&=-dH(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)\\
&=-dH_{i}(E)+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)\\
&=-E(H_{i})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)\\
&=\eta(X_{H_{i}})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E).\end{split}

Therefore, equation (
7.6
) is equivalent to
\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)=0
.
The following result, as in the contact case, is also in full agreement with the Jacobi-Haantjes framework previously constructed.

Theorem 11
.

Let
(M,\Omega,\eta,\mathscr{H})
be a LCSH manifold of rank
m
. Assume that for every
\boldsymbol{K}\in\mathscr{H}
the condition

(7.8)

\eta(\boldsymbol{K}E)=0

holds. Then we have

(7.9)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{j}\eta(X_{H_{i}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}}),

or equivalently, in terms of the Jacobi structure

(7.10)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{i}RH_{j}-H_{j}RH_{i},

Proof.

We observe that

(7.11)

\begin{split}\{H_{i},H_{j}\}&=dH_{i}(X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=dH(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=\Omega(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=\Omega(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H},X_{H_{j}})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=-dH_{j}((\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{j}\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=-dH(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{j}\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=-\Omega(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})-H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{j}\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}})\\
&=-H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H_{j}\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}}).\end{split}

On the other hand, condition (
7.8
) implies that
\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=\eta(X_{H_{i}})
and
\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H_{j}})
; indeed, let us see this last equality

\displaystyle\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega(E,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}X_{H})=-\Omega(X_{H},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dH(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)+H\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}\boldsymbol{K}_{i}E)=-\boldsymbol{K}_{i}^{T}dH(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-dH_{i}(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E))=-\Omega(X_{H_{i}},\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E)-H_{i}\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Omega(E,\boldsymbol{K}_{j}X_{H_{i}})=\eta(\boldsymbol{K}_{j}X_{H_{i}}).

Therefore, we deduce that

(7.13)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{j}\eta(X_{H_{i}})-H_{i}\eta(X_{H_{j}}),

or, equivalently, in terms of the Jacobi structure,

(7.14)

\{H_{i},H_{j}\}=H_{i}RH_{j}-H_{j}RH_{i}.

∎

Appendix A
Haantjes algebras for contact-Haantjes manifolds

There are several families of Haantjes algebras which are compatible with the geometries under study. The following analysis is not exhaustive; however, it shows that these algebras exist and are nontrivial.
By way of an example, assume that
(M,\theta)
be a contact manifold , with
\dim M=5
. The general form of a
(1,1)
tensor field quasi-compatible with the contact structure is:

(A.1)

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{K}&=A\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{1}}\otimes dq^{1}+B\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{1}}\otimes dq^{2}+C\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{2}}\otimes dq^{1}+D\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{2}}\otimes dq^{2}\\
&-A\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\otimes dp_{1}-B\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{2}}\otimes dp_{1}-C\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\otimes dp_{2}-D\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{2}}\otimes dp_{2}\\
&+E\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{1}}\otimes dp_{2}-E\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{2}}\otimes dp_{1}+F\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{1}}\otimes dq^{2}-F\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{2}}\otimes dq^{1}\\
&+\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle q$}}K_{1}^{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dq^{1}+\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle q$}}K_{2}^{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dq^{2}+\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle p$}}K_{1}^{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dp_{1}+\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle p$}}K_{2}^{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dp_{2}\\
&+K_{z}^{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\otimes dz.\end{split}

We present three families of Haantjes operators, in matrix form, depending on arbitrary functions. These operators belong to the class (
A.1
) and are consequently automatically quasi-compatible with the contact structure.

1)

(A.2)

\boldsymbol{F}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}D&B&0&0&0\\
0&D&0&0&0\\
0&0&-D&0&0\\
0&0&-B&-D&0\\
0&0&0&0&K^{z}_{z}\end{bmatrix}

with

D=D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}),B=B(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}),K^{z}_{z}=K^{z}_{z}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}).

2)

(A.3)

\boldsymbol{F}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}D&B&0&D&0\\
-B&D&-D&0&0\\
0&0&-D&B&0\\
0&0&-B&-D&0\\
0&0&0&0&K_{z}^{z}\end{bmatrix}

with

D=D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}),B=B(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}),K_{z}^{z}=K_{z}^{z}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5}).

3)

(A.4)

\boldsymbol{F}_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}-D&0&0&0&0\\
0&D&0&0&0\\
0&0&D&0&0\\
0&0&0&-D&0\\
\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle q$}}K_{1}^{z}&\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle q$}}K_{2}^{z}&\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle p$}}K_{1}^{z}&0&D\end{bmatrix}

with

Also, we have the constraint

Remark 9
.

Note that the families of operators (
A.2
) and (

) give rise to Abelian Haantjes algebras: in fact, the operators obtained from these families by making a specific choice of the arbitrary functions commute. Instead, the family (

) provides an example of non-Abelian Haantjes algebra, unless the same function

The Quasicentral Modulus Associated with a Class of Nonself-similar Fractals
R. Alexander Glickfield

Abstract
We discuss an extension to Voiculescu’s formula for the quasicentral modulus of a tuple of commuting, self-adjoint operators with spectral measure absolutely continuous with respect to a generalized Hausdorff measure. These Hausdorff measures are defined by gauge functions which are not power functions and are supported on nonself-similar fractals.



1
Introduction

The possibility of diagonalizing a self-adjoint operator by a small compact perturbation was first observed by Weyl
[
20
]
. He showed that any self-adjoint operator is the sum of a diagonal operator (in some orthonormal basis) with a compact self-adjoint operator. Von Neumann later improved this result by considering Hilbert-Schmidt perturbations
[
19
]
. The result was further extended by Kuroda who showed that it is possible to diagonalize a self-adjoint operator by a perturbation contained in any normed ideal other than the trace class
[
11
]
. Later, Berg
[
3
]
extended Weyl’s compact perturbation result to normal operators. It was not known whether compact operators can be replaced by Hilbert-Schmidt operators in Berg’s result until Voiculescu’s work
[
15
]
, a corollary of which being an affirmative answer to this question. Voiculescu discovered that the simultaneous diagonalization modulo a normed ideal of an
n
-tuple of commuting self-adjoint operators is equivalent to the existence of a quasicentral (as measured by the norm in the ideal) approximate unit of finite rank operators. A natural question is to find the obstruction ideals, that is, the normed ideals
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
for which a given
n
-tuple of commuting self-adjoint operators
cannot
be expressed as the sum of a diagonal
n
-tuple with an
n
-tuple of operators in
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
.

The first example of an obstruction ideal was found by Kato
[
10
]
and Rosenblum
[
12
]
who proved that the absolutely continuous part of the spectral measure of a self-adjoint operator is invariant under trace-class perturbations. (When a self-adjoint operator has only singular spectrum, Carey and Pincus
[
5
]
proved that the trace class is
not
an obstruction ideal). Voiculescu found in
[
15
]
a formula for the quasicentral modulus of
n
-tuples of operators relative to the Lorentz-type ideals
\mathfrak{S}_{n}^{-}
. This modulus is zero precisely when diagonalization modulo the ideal is possible. The formula has also been generalized to the Lorentz ideals
\mathfrak{S}_{p}^{-}
in the case of certain
n
-tuples of operators with self-similar spectra of noninteger Hausdorff dimension
p
(see
[
18
]
and also
[
9
]
for more recent work).

The goal of this paper is two-fold. First, given an
n
-tuple of operators whose spectral measure is absolutely continuous with respect to a generalized Hausdorff measure generated by some gauge function, we find the largest obstruction ideal. Second, we extend the integral formula for the quasicentral modulus to these
n
-tuples. To this end, we discuss necessary preliminaries in Section 2. In Section 3, we construct the maximal obstruction ideals for the relevant multiplication operators. In Section 4, we prove an ampliation homogeneity result which is central to the extension of Voiculescu’s formula. In Section 5, we state and prove the formula for the quasicentral modulus in these cases.

I would like to thank the reviewer for their careful reading of the manuscript and for providing valuable suggestions that have substantially improved the paper.

Notation and Preliminaries

2.1
Operator Preliminaries

We denote by
B(\Hilb)
the algebra of bounded linear operators on a separable, infinite dimensional, complex Hilbert space
\Hilb
. We write
c_{00}
for the vector space of real sequences
\{\xi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
that have only finitely many nonzero terms, and let
\mathcal{SN}
denote the set of norms
\Phi:c_{00}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
that satisfy
\Phi(\xi)=\Phi(\xi^{*})
, where
\xi^{*}
is the nonincreasing rearrangement of
\{|\xi_{j}|\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. These functions
\Phi
are known as
symmetric norming functions
. It should be noted that one may normalize an element
\Phi\in\mathcal{SN}
by replacing it with
\Phi/\Phi(1,0,0,\dots)
, but this is not required for the results in this paper.

Given
\Phi\in\mathcal{SN}
and a finite rank operator
F
with singular values
(s_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
, we define
|F|_{\Phi}:=\Phi((s_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})
. For arbitrary
T\in B(\Hilb)
, we set
|T|_{\Phi}=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}|PT|
, where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the directed set of finite rank projections on
\Hilb
. The set
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}=\{T\in B(\Hilb):|T|_{\Phi}<+\infty\}
is an ideal and
(\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi},|\cdot|_{\Phi})
is a Banach space. For all the norms we consider, the ideal of finite rank operators is dense in the Banach space
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{R}_{1}^{+}
the directed set of positive, finite rank contractions, where
A\geq B
if
A-B
is a positive operator. For
A,T\in B(\Hilb)
, we recall (see
[
15
, page 6]
) that the quasicentral modulus
k_{\Phi}(T)
of an operator
T
is defined as

k_{\Phi}(T):=\liminf_{A\in\mathcal{R}_{1}^{+}}|[A,T]|_{\Phi},

where
[A,T]=AT-TA
. Consider now
n
-tuples
\tau^{(j)}=(T_{1}^{(j)},\dots,T_{n}^{(j)})
with
T_{k}^{(j)}\in(B(\Hilb))^{n}
, where
j=0,1,2
, and let
A,B\in B(\Hilb)
. We define the following operations:

\displaystyle A\tau^{(0)}B

\displaystyle:=(AT_{1}^{(0)}B,\dots,AT_{n}^{(0)}B),

\displaystyle\tau^{(1)}+\tau^{(2)}

\displaystyle:=(T_{1}^{(1)}+T_{1}^{(2)},\dots,T_{n}^{(1)}+T_{n}^{(2)}).

We also set
||\tau||:=\max_{k}||T_{k}||
, and
|\tau|_{\Phi}:=\max_{k}|T_{k}|_{\Phi}
. The definition of the quasicentral modulus extends to
n
-tuples of operators as follows:

k_{\Phi}(\tau):=\liminf_{A\in\mathcal{R}_{1}^{+}}|[A,\tau]|_{\Phi}.

A commuting
n
-tuple of self-adjoint operators
\tau
is said to be
diagonalizable moduluo

\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
if there exists a diagonal
n
-tuple
\Delta\in(B(\Hilb))^{n}
so that
\tau-\Delta\in(\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi})^{n}
. By
[
15
, Corollary 2.6]
,
k_{\Phi}(\tau)>0
if and only if
\tau
is
not
diagonalizable modulo
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
. We say that
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
is an
obstruction ideal
for
\tau
when
k_{\Phi}(\tau)>0
, and
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
is a
diagonalization ideal
for
\tau
otherwise. As shown in
[
2
]
, any obstruction ideal of
\tau
is contained in another obstruction ideal of the form
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi_{\pi}}
where
\pi=(\pi_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}
is a non-increasing real sequence such that
\pi_{k}\rightarrow 0
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}=+\infty
, and

\Phi_{\pi}(\xi):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}\xi_{k}^{*}.

We also write
|\cdot|_{\pi}
and
k_{\pi}(\cdot)
for
|\cdot|_{\Phi_{\pi}}
and
k_{\Phi_{\pi}}(\cdot)
, respectively. The results of interest found in
[
6
]
require the following regularity condition on the sequences
\pi
defining obstruction ideals
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence
\pi
as above is said to be
regular
if there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\pi_{k}\leq\alpha m\pi_{m}

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.2
Hausdorff Measures

Fix a strictly increasing function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
f((0,+\infty))\subset(0,+\infty)
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}f(t)=0
. Given an arbitrary subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, a collection of open balls
\{B(x_{j},r_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to be an
r
-cover
of
A
if
r_{j}<r
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}B(x_{j},r_{j})
. The
outer Hausdorff measure
of
A
using the
gauge function

f
is defined as

H_{f}^{*}(A):=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\inf\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(r_{j}):\{B(x_{j},r_{j})\}\text{ is an }r\text{-cover of A}\bigg\}

where the infimum is taken over the set of
r
-covers of
A
. The restriction of
H_{f}^{*}
to the
\sigma
-algebra of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a measure denoted
H_{f}
.
We often assume the following regularity conditions on the gauge functions.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
s>1
, we say that a gauge function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
has property
(R_{s})
if:

1.

f
is convex,

2.

f(1)=1
,

3.

f\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\cap C^{2}((0,\infty))
,

4.

f^{\prime}(0)=0
,

5.

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}f(ax)/f(x)=a^{s}\quad\text{for every }a>0.

Note that the inverse with respect to composition of a gauge function with property
(R_{s})
exists, but is concave and hence does not have property
(R_{s})
. Functions satisfying the last condition of Definition 2.2 are said to be
regularly varying
at 0 with index
s
. We make note of a few useful facts about regularly varying functions
[
4
, Section 1.4.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
f
is a gauge function with property
(R_{s})
, then

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{f^{-1}(x)}{f^{-1}(ax)}=\frac{1}{a^{1/s}}

where
f^{-1}
is the compositional inverse of f.

Proof.

Theorem 1.5.12 of
[
4
]
implies
f^{-1}
is regularly varying at 0 with index
1/s
, so

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{f^{-1}(ax)}{f^{-1}(x)}=a^{1/s}.

The stated equality follows from (left) composing the quotient
f^{-1}(ax)/f^{-1}(x)
with the function
x\rightarrow 1/x
and letting
x
tend to 0.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
f
is a regularly varying at
0
with index
s
, then

f(x)=x^{s}L(x),

where
L
is a regularly varying function at
0
with index
0
.

Such functions
L
are called
slowly varying
in the literature. We apply this representation to both
f
and its inverse
f^{-1}
, and denote the corresponding slowly varying functions by
L(x)
and
P(x)
, respectively. The next Lemma is a convenient specialization of the Potter bounds found in
[
4
, Theorem 1.5.6]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
L
be a positive slowly varying function at
0
. For every
\delta>0
, there exists
x_{0}>0
such that for every
0<a<1
and
0<x\leq x_{0}
,

\frac{L(ax)}{L(x)}\leq a^{-\delta}

Finally, we introduce a normalized version of the Karamata Representation Theorem
[
4
, Theorem 1.3.3]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

A function
L
is slowly varying at 0 if and only if it may be written in the form

L(x)=C\exp\bigg({\int_{x}^{x_{0}}}\frac{\varepsilon(u)}{u}\ du\bigg)

Where
C>0
is a constant,
x_{0}>0
, and
\varepsilon(u)
is a measurable function with
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon(x)=0
.

2.3
Construction of Relevant Fractals

We define a fractal as a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that has non-integer Hausdorff dimension. Many of our results rely on the spectrum of a tuple of operators being contained in a fractal
K
such that
0<H_{f}(K)<+\infty
. We also often require that the fractal satisfies the regularity condition
Cf(r)\leq H_{f}(B(x,r)\cap K)\leq Df(r)
for every
x\in K
and some constants
C,D>0
independent of
x
. This subsection will provide the construction of such a fractal for the measure
H_{f}
.

We begin by discussing the construction of certain symmetric generalized Cantor sets. A more detailed discussion can be found in Section 2 of
[
8
]
. Given a gauge function
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with property
(R_{s})
, let
n=\lfloor s\rfloor+1
. This selection of
n
ensures that
f(x)/x^{n}
is eventually decreasing and thus allows the use Theorem 1 of
[
8
]
. By Property
(R_{s})
, one can choose a positive integer
M
such that
f^{-1}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2^{(m-1)nM}}\Bigr)>2f^{-1}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2^{mnM}}\Bigr)
for all
m>M
. This ensures that the subsequent construction is well-defined. We define the fractal inductively: Beginning with the unit interval, remove an open interval of length
\eta_{1}=1-2f^{-1}(1/2^{nM})
so that the two remaining closed intervals
I_{1}^{1},I_{2}^{1}
are of equal length
\lambda_{1}=f^{-1}(1/2^{nM})
. For the
m
-th step, remove from each closed interval
I_{j}^{m-1}
an open interval of length
\eta_{m}=f^{-1}(1/2^{(m-1)nM})-2f^{-1}(1/2^{mnM})
so that the resulting collection of
2^{m}
intervals
I_{j}^{m}
have equal length
\lambda_{m}=f^{-1}(1/2^{nmM})
. It should be noted that Lemma 2.3 implies that
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2^{m-1}\eta_{m}=1
, since
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}2^{m}f^{-1}(1/2^{nmM})=2^{m(1-nM/s)}P(1/2^{nmM})=0
. The last equality is a consequence of Proposition 1.3.6 of
[
4
]
. Since the examples of interest allow a choice of
M=1
, we will assume
M=1
for the remainder of the paper for ease of notation. We focus on the symmetric generalized Cantor set
C_{f}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is the product of
n
copies of

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{2^{m}}I_{j}^{m}

Observe that
C_{f}
is a union of
2^{n}
identical (up to translation) sets, each of which contains exactly one corner point of the unit
n
-cube. These sets are said to be in the first generation of
C_{f}
. Similarly, every set from the
L
th generation of
C_{f}
is the union of
2^{n}
sets from the
(L+1)
st generation.
Consider the lexicographic ordering of the corner points of the unit
n
-cube. For example, the order of the corner points of the unit square is:

(0,0)\leq(0,1)\leq(1,0)\leq(1,1)

We write
C_{f}^{k}
,
k=1,2,\dots 2^{n}
for the set in the first generation that contains the
k
th corner point (relative to the lexicographic order). Likewise, observe that sets in the
L
th generation each contain exactly one outside corner point of a set in the
(L-1)
st generation. Thus we may index the sets in the
L
th generation with words of length
L
whose ”letters” are in
\{1,\dots,2^{n}\}
. We write
C_{f}^{w}
with
w\in\prod_{1}^{L}\{1,\dots,2^{n}\}
to identify the
L
th generation sets and denote the length
L
of
w
by
|w|
. See Figure 1 for a picture of the second generation for
C_{f}
when
n=2
.
Clearly, for any fixed length
L
we have

C_{f}=\bigcup_{|w|=L}C_{f}^{w}

It was shown in
[
8
, Theorem 1]
that
0<H_{f}(C_{f})<+\infty
and, more generally
\xi\cdot 2^{-n|w|}<H_{f}(C_{f}^{w})<2^{-n|w|}
for some
\xi>0
dependent only on
n
. It is easy to see that there exist constants
C,D>0
independent of
x
so that
Cf(r)\leq H_{f}(C_{f}\cap B(x,r))\leq Df(r)
for every
r>0
.

\mu

\tau_{\Omega,\mu}

\displaystyle\tau_{\Omega,\mu}

\displaystyle(T_{j,\mu}f)(x)

(2)

The following results are standard and can be found in

Let
\tau\in(B(\Hilb))^{n}

Let

.

3
Obstruction Ideals for
\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}

In this section we consider a gauge function
f
with property
(R_{s})
, and fractals
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
that satisfy the regularity condition

Cf(r)\leq H_{f}(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)\leq Df(r)

for every
x\in\Omega
, where
C,D
are independent of
x
. The following lemma is essentially a restatement of results from
[
15
]
and
[
6
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a strictly increasing function with property
(R_{s})
, and let
\pi=(\pi_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a regular (see Definition 2.1) non-increasing sequence such that
\pi_{k}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}=+\infty
. Suppose that

0<\inf_{m}m\pi_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)\leq\sup_{m}m\pi_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)<+\infty

Then
0<k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})<+\infty
.

Proof.

We seek to use Theorem 4.6 from
[
6
]
to prove that
0<k_{{\pi}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})
. To this end, we verify that there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that for every
x\in(0,1/2]
and
\ell\geq 1
,

f(2x)\leq C_{1}f(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k>\ell}2^{k}f(2^{-k})\leq C_{2}\,2^{\ell}f(2^{-\ell}).

For the first inequality, let

s_{k}:=\max\left\{\frac{f(2x)}{f(x)}:2^{-k}\leq x\leq 2^{-k+1}\right\}.

Since
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}f(2x)/f(x)=2^{s}
, the sequence
\{s_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded by a constant
C_{1}
. Therefore, for every
x\in(0,1/2]
,
f(2x)/f(x)\leq C_{1}
and hence
f(2x)\leq C_{1}f(x)
. For the second inequality, it suffices to show that the following infinite sums are uniformly bounded in
\ell
:

\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{k>\ell}2^{k}\bigg(\frac{f(2^{-k})}{f(2^{-\ell})}\bigg).

Using Lemma 2.4, we write
f(x)=x^{s}L(x)
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{k>\ell}2^{k}\bigg(\frac{f(2^{-k})}{f(2^{-\ell})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{k>\ell}2^{k}\bigg(\frac{2^{-sk}L(2^{-k})}{2^{-s\ell}L(2^{-\ell})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\ell}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2^{m(1-s)+\ell}\bigg(\frac{L(2^{-m}\cdot 2^{-\ell})}{L(2^{-\ell})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}2^{m(1-s)}\bigg(\frac{L(2^{-m}\cdot 2^{-\ell})}{L(2^{-\ell})}\bigg)

By Lemma 2.5, we may choose
\delta<s-1
so that for every
\ell>M

\frac{L(2^{-m}\cdot 2^{-\ell})}{L(2^{-\ell})}\leq 2^{\delta m}.

The tail of the series can now be bounded like so

\sum_{m=M}^{\infty}2^{m(1-s)}\bigg(\frac{L(2^{-m}\cdot 2^{-\ell})}{L(2^{-\ell})}\bigg)\leq\sum_{m=M}^{\infty}2^{m(1-s+\delta)}.

Since
1-s+\delta<0
, the right-hand side is a convergent geometric series, completing the proof.

We now show
k_{{\pi}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})<+\infty
. The condition
H_{f}(B(x,r)\cap\Omega)\leq Df(r)
allows one to cover
\Omega
by disjoint Borel sets
\{\omega_{\ell,m}\}_{\ell=1}^{m^{n}}
with
\text{diam}(\omega_{\ell,m})=f^{-1}(1/{m^{n}})
so that
H_{f}(\omega_{\ell,m})\leq D/m^{n}
for every positive integer
m
. Consider the sequences of projections

P_{m}^{(\ell)}v=\langle v,\chi_{\omega_{\ell,m}}\rangle\frac{\chi_{\omega_{\ell,m}}}{||\chi_{\omega_{\ell,m}}||_{2}^{2}},\quad v\in L^{2}(\Omega,H_{f})

and

P_{m}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m^{n}}P_{m}^{(\ell)}

Observe that the sequence
P_{m}
converges strongly to
I_{L^{2}(H_{f},\Omega)}
. Write
T_{j}
for the
j
th component of
\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}
. We see that
P_{m}^{(\ell)}T_{j}
and
T_{j}P_{m}^{(\ell)}
each have rank at most 1, hence
[P_{m}^{(\ell)},T_{j}]
has rank at most 2. Furthermore, note that
||[P_{m},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]||\leq 2\text{diam}(\omega_{\ell,m})\leq 2\sqrt{n}Df^{-1}(1/{m^{n}})
. Thus, if
\alpha>0
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\pi_{k}\leq\alpha m\pi_{m}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\leq|[P_{m},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]|_{{\pi}}

\displaystyle\leq 2D\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/{m^{n}})\Phi_{\pi}(\underset{m^{n}\text{ times}}{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}},0,0,\dots)

\displaystyle=2D\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/{m^{n}})\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\pi_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha D\sqrt{n}\sup_{m}m\pi_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)<+\infty.\qed

Before proceeding further, we need to perform an exercise in calculus.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be a function with property
(R_{s})
, and define
h(x):=1/f^{-1}(1/x)
. Furthermore, assume that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for any
t\in[0,t_{0}]:

\displaystyle 2f(t)f^{\prime}(t)+tf(t)f^{\prime\prime}(t)-2tf^{\prime}(t)^{2}<0.

(3)

Then there exists
x_{0}
such that
h^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for every
x>x_{0}
.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(f^{-1}(1/x))}\Bigg[\frac{2}{(xf^{-1}(1/x))^{3}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{xf^{\prime}(f^{-1}(1/x))}-f^{-1}(1/x)\Bigg)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{[xf^{-1}(1/x)]^{2}}\Bigg[\frac{1}{[f^{\prime}(f^{-1}(1/x))]^{2}}\Bigg(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(f^{-1}(1/x))}{x^{2}f^{\prime}(f^{-1}(1/x))}\Bigg)\Bigg].

Setting
t=f^{-1}(1/x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\frac{f(t)}{tf^{\prime}(t)}\Bigg)^{3}\Big(2f(t)f^{\prime}(t)+tf(t)f^{\prime\prime}(t)-2tf^{\prime}(t)^{2}\Big).

Choosing
x_{0}=1/f(t_{0})
yields the result.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

With the notation of Lemma
3.2
, there exists some positive integer
N
so that
\rho:=\{h^{\prime}(k+N-1)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
defines a symmetric-norming function
\Phi_{\rho}
.

Proof.

Set
\rho_{k}=h^{\prime}(k+N-1)
. By Lemma 3.2, we know that for some positive integer
N
, the sequence
\rho=(\rho_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is nonincreasing. Thus it suffices to show that
\rho_{k}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{k}\rho_{k}=+\infty
. Setting
t=f^{-1}(1/x)
, we see that

h^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{(xf^{-1}(1/x))^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(f^{-1}(1/x))}=\bigg(\frac{f(t)}{t}\bigg)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(t)}.

By Lemma 2.4, we have

\bigg(\frac{f(t)}{t}\bigg)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(t)}=\frac{t^{2s-2}(L(t))^{2}}{st^{s-1}L(t)+t^{s}L^{\prime}(t)}=\frac{t^{s-1}L(t)}{s+tL^{\prime}(t)/L(t)}

Since
t^{s-1}L(t)\overset{t\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0
(see Proposition 1.3.6 of
[
4
]
), it suffices to show
tL^{\prime}(t)/L(t)\overset{t\rightarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}0
. This is easily seen from Lemma 2.6, as

\frac{tL^{\prime}(t)}{L(t)}=t\cdot\frac{d}{dt}\bigg[\ln\bigg(L(t)\bigg)\bigg]=t\cdot\bigg(-\frac{\varepsilon(t)}{t}\bigg)\rightarrow 0

Finally, note that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_{k}\geq\int_{N}^{\infty}h^{\prime}(x)dx=\frac{1}{f^{-1}(1/x)}\Bigg|_{N}^{\infty}=+\infty,

and this completes the proof.
∎

We next state two results that are slight extensions of the arguments found in
[
2
]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\rho
be as defined in Corollary
3.3
, and let
\pi=\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a non-increasing, real-valued sequence such that
\pi_{k}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{k}\pi_{k}=+\infty
. If
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
is not contained in
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
, then

\liminf_{m}f^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{m^{n}}\bigg)\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\pi_{k}=0

Proof.

For sufficiently large
m
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\rho_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{m^{n}}h^{\prime}(x+N-1)\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{f^{-1}(1/(m^{n}+N-1))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{f^{-1}(1/2m^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d_{1}}{f^{-1}(1/m^{n})},

where the existence of the positive constant
d_{1}
is guaranteed by regular variation of
f^{-1}
(see Lemma 2.3). Thus

\liminf_{m}f^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{m^{n}}\bigg)\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\pi_{k}\leq\liminf_{m}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\pi_{k}}{d_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\rho_{k}}.

Thus it suffices to show that the right hand side above equals 0. Suppose to the contrary that
d\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\pi_{k}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\rho_{k}
for some
d>0
. Let T be a positive operator in
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}\setminus\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
with eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
listed in nonincreasing order. Then we have

\displaystyle|T|_{\rho}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\rho_{k}

\displaystyle=\lim_{K\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{K}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\rho_{\ell}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})\rho_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq d\lim_{K\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{K}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K}\pi_{\ell}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})\pi_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq d\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\pi_{k}<+\infty,

contrary to the fact that
T\notin\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
f
be a gauge function with property
(R_{s})
that satisfies inequality (3), and let
\rho,\pi
be as in Proposition
3.4
. If
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
is not contained in
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
, then
k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})=0
.

Proof.

This argument mirrors that of Lemma 3.1. Let
\{\omega_{\ell,m}\}_{\ell}
be a partition of
\Omega
such that
H_{f}(\omega_{\ell,m})\leq D/m^{n}
. Consider the sequence of projections

P_{m}v=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m^{n}}\langle v,\chi_{\omega_{\ell,m}}\rangle\frac{\chi_{\omega_{\ell,m}}}{||\chi_{\omega_{\ell,m}}||_{2}^{2}},\quad v\in L^{2}(\Omega,H_{f}).

Observe that

\displaystyle k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\leq|[P_{m},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]|_{\Phi_{\pi}}

\displaystyle\leq 2D\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/{m^{n}})\Phi_{\pi}(\underset{m^{n}\text{ times}}{1,1,\dots,1},0,0,\dots)

\displaystyle=2D\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/{m^{n}})\sum_{k=1}^{m^{n}}\pi_{k}\rightarrow 0.

Thus by Lemma 2.8 we have
k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})=0
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be function with property
(R_{s})
that satisfies inequality (3), and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Borel set so that
0<H_{f}(\Omega)<+\infty
. Then
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
is an obstruction ideal for
\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}
. Furthermore, if
\Phi\in\mathcal{SN}
, then
k_{\Phi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\neq 0
if and only if
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
.

Proof.

We must verify that
\rho
is regular (as in Definition 2.1) and that the inequalities from the hypotheses of Lemma
3.1
are satisfied. We proceed by showing that
\sup_{m}m\rho_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)<+\infty
. Observe that for sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle m\rho_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)

\displaystyle\leq f^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{m}\Big)\sum_{k=1}^{m}\rho_{k}

\displaystyle\leq f^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{m}\Big)\int_{0}^{m}h^{\prime}(x+N-1)dx

\displaystyle=f^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{m}\Big)\Bigg[\frac{1}{f^{-1}(1/(x+N-1))}\Bigg]_{0}^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{f^{-1}(1/m)}{f^{-1}(1/2m)}\longrightarrow 2^{-\frac{1}{s}}

as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Thus
\sup_{m}m\rho_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)<+\infty
.
To verify the remaining inequality, set
t_{m}=f^{-1}(1/(m+N-1))
so that

m\rho_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)=\frac{f(t_{m})t_{m-N+1}}{f(t_{m-N+1})t_{m}}\cdot\frac{f(t_{m})}{t_{m}f^{\prime}(t_{m})}\geq\frac{f(t_{m})t_{2m}}{f(t_{2m})t_{m}}\cdot\frac{f(t_{m})}{t_{m}f^{\prime}(t_{m})}.

Regular variation of both
f
and
f^{-1}
guarantees that
f(t_{m})t_{2m}/f(t_{2m})t_{m}
tends to a positive constant, so it suffices to show
\inf_{m}f(t_{m})/t_{m}f^{\prime}(t_{m})>0
. Indeed, note that

\displaystyle\frac{f(t_{m})}{t_{m}f^{\prime}(t_{m})}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{m}^{s}L(t_{m})}{t_{m}(st_{m}^{s-1}L(t_{m})+t_{m}^{s}L^{\prime}(t_{m}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s+(t_{m}L^{\prime}(t_{m})/L(t_{m}))}.

As shown in the proof of Corollary 3.3, the right-hand side tends to
\frac{1}{s}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, and we conclude that
\inf_{m}m\rho_{m}f^{-1}(1/m)>0
.

We now verify that
\rho
is regular. Let
\beta>0
be chosen so that for every positive integer
m>N
, we have
h(m)<\beta mh^{\prime}(m)
. The existence of such a
\beta
is guaranteed by the previous argument. So

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\rho_{k}\leq h(m+N-1)\leq\beta(m+N-1)h^{\prime}(m+N-1)\leq 2\beta m\rho_{m},

and thus
\rho
is seen to be regular.

As all of the hypotheses of Lemma
3.1
are satisfied, we conclude that
0<k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})
and hence
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
is an obstruction ideal for
\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}
.

For the second claim, suppose that
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
. Since
k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\neq 0
, all ideals smaller are obstruction ideals and hence
k_{\Phi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\neq 0
. To complete the proof, assume that
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}
is not contained in
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
. From
[
17
, Proposition 2.6]
, we know that there exists a non-increasing, real-valued sequence such that
\pi=\{\pi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
such that
\pi_{k}\rightarrow 0
and
\sum_{k}\pi_{k}=+\infty
with the property that
\mathfrak{S}_{\Phi}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
. Thus
\mathfrak{S}_{\pi}
is not contained in
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
, and hence
k_{\Phi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\leq k_{\pi}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})=0
.
∎

Example 3.7
.

Suppose that
f(x)=x^{s}/\log(e/x)
with
s>1
for
x\in(0,1]
and let
C_{f}
be the symmetric generalized Cantor set defined in Section 2.3. Let
W(x)
be defined as the compositional inverse of the real valued function
xe^{x}
and

\rho=\Bigg\{\frac{\exp\Big(\frac{1}{s}W(se^{s}k)-1\Big)W(se^{s}k)}{s\,k\,\big(1+W(se^{s}k)\big)}\Bigg\}_{k=1}^{\infty}.

Then
\mathfrak{S}_{\rho}
is the maximum obstruction ideal for the operator
\tau_{C_{f},H_{f}}
.

Proof.

We first show that

f^{-1}(x)=\exp\bigg(1-\frac{1}{s}W\Big(\frac{se^{s}}{x}\Big)\bigg).

To see this, write
y=f(x)
and set
t=-\log x
, so that
x=e^{-t}
. Then

y\cdot(1+t)=e^{-st}.

Letting
u=1+t
, the equation becomes

y\,u\,e^{su}=e^{s}.

Multiplying both sides by
s
yields

y\,(sue^{su})=se^{s},

which is in the standard form for
W
:

su=W\Big(\frac{se^{s}}{y}\Big).

Finally, recalling that
u=1+t=1-\log x
, we solve for
x
to obtain

x=\exp\bigg(1-\frac{1}{s}W\Big(\frac{se^{s}}{y}\Big)\bigg).

Since
W^{\prime}(x)=W(x)/(x(1+W(x))
, we see that

h^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\exp\Big(\frac{1}{s}W(se^{s}x)-1\Big)W(se^{s}x)}{s\,x\,\big(1+W(se^{s}x)\big)}.

Since it is easy to see that
f
has property
(R_{s})
, all that remains to be shown is that
f
satisfies inequality
(3)
.
A derivative calculation shows that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)\

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{s-1}(1+s(1-\log(x)))}{(1-\log(x))^{2}}

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{s-2}\bigg(\frac{s^{2}-s}{1-\log(x)}+\frac{2s-1}{(1-\log(x))^{2}}+\frac{2}{(1-\log(x))^{3}}\bigg)

Letting
L(x)=1/(1-\log(x))
, we see that:

\displaystyle\phantom{=}x(2f(x)f^{\prime}(x)+xf(x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)-2xf^{\prime}(x)^{2})/f(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=2(s+L(x))+s^{2}-s+(2s-1)L(x)+2L(x)^{2}-2(s+L(x))^{2}

\displaystyle=s-s^{2}+(1-2s)L(x)<0,

and thus inequality
(3)
is satisfied. The claim follows from an application of Theorem 3.6.
∎

4
Ampliation Homogeneity for
k_{\rho}

To begin, we first show a kind of ampliation homogeneity for some quasicentral moduli which are related to
k_{\rho}
(
\rho
as in Section 3). For the remainder of the paper, we assume our gauge functions
f
have property
(R_{s})
. Recall the notation
h(x)=1/(f^{-1}(1/x))
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a function with property
(R_{s})
that satisfies inequality (3). For a fixed positive integer
m
and fixed
\varepsilon>0
, let
\rho^{(\varepsilon)}=\{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
with
\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}=\rho_{k+N_{m}}
and
N_{m}
chosen so that for every
k>N_{m}
we have

\bigg|\frac{f^{-1}(1/k)}{f^{-1}(1/mk)}-m^{1/s}\bigg|<\varepsilon.

If
X_{j}
is a sequence of finite rank operators such that
||X_{j}||\rightarrow 0
and
|X_{j}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}<C<+\infty
, then there exists a positive constant
K
dependent on only
C
and
\rho
so that,

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg||X_{j}\otimes I_{m}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}-m^{1/s}|X_{j}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}\bigg|\leq\varepsilon K.

Proof.

Let
s_{k}^{(j)}
be the singular values of
X_{j}
listed in nonincreasing order with the convention that
s_{k}^{(j)}=0
for
k>\text{rank}(X_{j}^{*}X_{j})
. Then

\displaystyle|X_{j}\otimes I_{m}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\sum_{\ell=(k-1)m+1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=s_{1}^{(j)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\Bigg(\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}-\sum_{\ell=1}^{(k-1)m}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}s_{r}^{(j)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{mr}\rho_{\ell}^{(\epsilon)}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}.

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}.

To succinctly handle the cumbersome notation introduced by the two (left) truncations of the sequence
\{h^{\prime}(k)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, we introduce
\gamma_{m}:=N+N_{m}-1
. Next, suppose that
k>N_{m}+N-1
so that
h^{\prime}(mk)<\varepsilon/m\gamma_{m}
. We see that

\displaystyle\bigg|h(mk+\gamma_{m})-m^{1/s}h(k+\gamma_{m})\bigg|

\displaystyle<\bigg|h(mk+\gamma_{m})-h(m(k+\gamma_{m}))\bigg|

\displaystyle+\bigg|h(m(k+\gamma_{m}))-m^{1/s}h(k+\gamma_{m})\bigg|.

As established in Lemma 3.2,
h^{\prime}(x)
is a nonincreasing function for
x>N
, so the Mean Value Theorem implies that

\bigg|h(mk+\gamma_{m})-h(m(k+\gamma_{m}))\bigg|\leq(m\gamma_{m}-\gamma_{m})h^{\prime}(mk+\gamma_{m})\leq\varepsilon.

For the second summand, the hypotheses of the lemma imply that

\displaystyle\bigg|h(m(k+\gamma_{m}))-m^{1/s}h(k+\gamma_{m})\bigg|

\displaystyle=h(k+\gamma_{m})\bigg|\frac{h(m(k+\gamma_{m}))}{h(k+\gamma_{m})}-m^{1/s}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon h(k+\gamma_{m})

There exists a positive constant
\beta
so that
k\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}f^{-1}(1/(k+\gamma_{m}))>\beta/2
(see the proof of Theorem 3.6). Thus

\bigg|h(mk+\gamma_{m})-m^{1/s}h(k+\gamma_{m})\bigg|<\varepsilon+\varepsilon h(k+\gamma_{m})<\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}.

The following lines of inequalities bound
\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}
between two expressions of the form
\alpha(k)+m^{1/s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}
where
\alpha(k)
is solely dependent on
k
and satisfies
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\alpha(k)|\leq\varepsilon K
. For the first inequality, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{mk}h^{\prime}(x+\gamma_{m})\ dx

\displaystyle=h(mk+\gamma_{m})-h(\gamma_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}-h(\gamma_{m})\bigg)+m^{1/s}h(k+\gamma_{m})

\displaystyle=\bigg(\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}+(m^{1/s}-1)h(\gamma_{m})\bigg)+m^{1/s}\int_{0}^{k}h^{\prime}(x+\gamma_{m})\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\bigg({m^{1/s}\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}}+\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}+(m^{1/s}-1)h(\gamma_{m})\bigg)+m^{1/s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}.

Likewise,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\geq-\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}+\int_{0}^{mk}h^{\prime}(x+\gamma_{m})\ dx

\displaystyle=-\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}+h(mk+\gamma_{m})-h(\gamma_{m})

\displaystyle\geq-\bigg(\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}+h(\gamma_{m})\bigg)+m^{1/s}h(k+\gamma_{m})

\displaystyle=-\bigg(\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}+\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}-(m^{1/s}-1)h(\gamma_{m})\bigg)+m^{1/s}\int_{0}^{k}h^{\prime}(x+\gamma_{m})\ dx

\displaystyle\geq-\bigg({m^{1/s}\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}}+\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}-(m^{1/s}-1)h(\gamma_{m})\bigg)+m^{1/s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}.

Notice that the inequality
{m^{1/s}\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}}\leq\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}
is used in the last line to provide symmetry with the previous case. With the notation

\alpha_{\pm}(k):={m^{1/s}\rho_{0}^{(\varepsilon)}}+\varepsilon\beta k{\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}}\pm(m^{1/s}-1)h(\gamma_{m}),

we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\bigg(-\alpha_{-}(k)+m^{1/s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}

\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\bigg(\alpha_{+}(k)+m^{1/s}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg).

We claim that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\alpha_{\pm}(k)\bigg|\leq 2\beta\varepsilon C.

Indeed, for any constant
D
, we have

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)D=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}s_{1}^{(j)}D=0,

so it remains to show that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)k\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg|\leq 2C.

For every positive integer
q
, observe that

\sum_{k=q+1}^{\infty}s_{k}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq\sum_{k=q}^{\infty}s_{k+1}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\leq\sum_{k=q}^{\infty}s_{k}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)},

which further implies

0\leq\sum_{k=q}^{\infty}s_{k+1}(\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{k+1}^{{(\varepsilon)}})\leq s_{q}\rho_{q}^{(\varepsilon)}.

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)k\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}s_{\ell+1}^{(j)}\big(\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{\ell+1}^{(\varepsilon)}\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=k}^{\infty}s_{\ell+1}^{(j)}\big(\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}-\rho_{\ell+1}^{(\varepsilon)}\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg|+\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\rho_{k}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2C

We therefore conclude that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{mk}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}-m^{1/s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}\bigg|\leq 2\beta\varepsilon C,

and since,

m^{1/s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(s_{k}^{(j)}-s_{k+1}^{(j)}\big)\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\rho_{\ell}^{(\varepsilon)}=m^{1/s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}\rho_{k}=m^{1/s}|X_{j}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}},

the lemma follows.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f,\varepsilon,\rho^{(\varepsilon)}
be defined as above. If
X_{j}
is a sequence of
n
-tuples of finite rank operators such that
|X_{j}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}<C<+\infty
and
||X_{j}||\rightarrow 0
, then there exists a positive constant
K
dependent only on
C
and
\rho
so that,

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\bigg||X_{j}\otimes I_{m}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}-m^{1/s}|X_{j}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}\bigg|\leq\varepsilon K.

The next lemma shows that, even though the ampliation result from the previous lemma only applies to norms of the form
|\cdot|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}
, it can still be used to calculate
k_{\rho}(\tau)
. Let
S\pi
denote the left shift of a sequence
\pi
, that is,
S\pi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
\xi_{k}=\pi_{k+1}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\tau
be an
n
-tuple of commuting self-adjoint operators, and let
\pi
be a regular sequence. Then
k_{\pi}(\tau)=k_{S\pi}{(\tau)}
.

Proof.

Begin by noting that for any operator
T
with
|T|_{\pi}<+\infty
, we have
|T|_{\pi}\geq|T|_{S\pi}
, so clearly
k_{\pi}(\tau)\geq k_{S\pi}(\tau)
. For the other inequality, let
A_{j}
be a sequence of positive finite rank contractions so that
\lim_{j}|[A_{j},\tau]|_{S\pi}=k_{S\pi}(\tau)\text{ and}\lim_{j}||[A_{j},\tau]||=0.
If
s_{k}^{j}
are the singular values of
[A_{j},\tau]
listed in nonincreasing order, then

k_{\pi}(\tau)\leq\lim_{j}|[A_{j},\tau]|_{\pi}=\lim_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\pi_{k}=\lim_{j}\bigg(s_{1}^{(j)}\pi_{1}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\pi_{k}\bigg)

\leq\lim_{j}\bigg(s_{1}^{(j)}\pi_{1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\pi_{k+1}\bigg)=\lim_{j}||[A_{j},\tau]||\pi_{1}+\lim_{j}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}s_{k}^{(j)}\pi_{k+1}=k_{S\pi}(\tau).

Thus
k_{\pi}(\tau)=k_{S\pi}(\tau)
, as desired.
∎

Since
\rho^{(\varepsilon)}
is the sequence
\rho
left-shifted a finite number of times, we deduce the following result.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\tau
be an n-tuple of operators such that
0<k_{\rho}(\tau)<+\infty
. Then
k_{\rho}(\tau)=k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau)
.

We now state and prove an extension of the homogeneity result in
[
18
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a function with property (R
s
) for some
s>1
that satisfies inequality
(3)
. Then

k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})=m^{1/s}k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}).

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. By Lemma 3.3 of
[
18
]
, we can choose a sequence
A_{j}\subset R_{1}^{+}
such that
A_{j}\uparrow I
with

k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}|[A_{j},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]\otimes I_{m}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}

and

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}||[A_{j},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]||=0.

For a constant
K>0
, Corollary 4.2 implies that

\displaystyle k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}m^{1/s}|[A_{j},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}-K\varepsilon\geq m^{1/s}k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})-K\varepsilon,

and thus

\displaystyle k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})

\displaystyle=k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})

\displaystyle\geq m^{1/s}k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})-K\varepsilon

\displaystyle=m^{1/s}k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})-K\varepsilon.

Next, choose a sequence
B_{j}\subset R_{1}^{+}
such that
B_{j}\uparrow I
with

k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}|[B_{j},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}},

and

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}||[B_{j},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]||=0.

Using Corollary 4.2 once more, we see that

m^{1/s}k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})\geq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}|[B_{j},\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}]\otimes I_{m}|_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}-K\varepsilon\geq k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})-K\varepsilon.

Thus

\displaystyle m^{1/s}k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})

\displaystyle=m^{1/s}k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}})

\displaystyle\geq k_{\rho^{(\varepsilon)}}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})-K\varepsilon

\displaystyle=k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,H_{f}}\otimes I_{m})-K\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small, the result follows.
∎

5
Exact Formula for the Quasicentral Modulus for Generalized Hausdorff Measures

For this section we once again assume
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
a function with property
(R_{s})
for some
s>1
that satisfies inequality (3). Let
n=\lfloor s\rfloor
+1 and recall that
\rho:=\{h^{\prime}(k+N-1)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
with
N
being chosen so that
\rho
defines a symmetric-norming function (see Corollary 3.3). If
\tau
is an
n
-tuple of commuting self-adjoint operators with
\sigma(\tau)\subset C_{f}
, and
m
is the multiplicity function for
\tau
, we show that

{(k_{\rho}(\tau))}^{s}
is proportional to
\int_{\sigma(\tau)}m(x)dH_{f}(x)
. To begin, we first show that
k_{\rho}(\tau)=0
when
{\mathcal{H}^{f}_{ac}}=\{0\}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
f
is a gauge function with property
(R_{s})
that satisfies inequality (3),
\Omega\subset C_{f}
is a closed Borel set, and
\tau_{\Omega,\nu}
is multiplication by the variables on
L^{2}(\Omega,\nu)
for some Borel probability measure
\nu
, then there exists a constant
D>0
dependent only on
n
such that

k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,\nu})\leq D(H_{f}(\Omega))^{1/s}.

Proof.

This argument mirrors that found in Lemma 5.1 of
[
18
]
. Let
W_{L}=\{w:|w|=L\text{ and }C_{f}^{w}\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing\}
and
U_{L}=\bigcup_{w\in W_{L}}C_{f}^{w}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, choose an
L_{0}
sufficiently large so that
N<|W_{L_{0}}|
and
H_{f}(\Omega)>H_{f}(U_{L_{0}})-\varepsilon
. Let

P_{L}v=\sum_{w\in W_{L}}\langle v,\chi_{C_{f}^{w}}\rangle\frac{\chi_{C_{f}^{w}}}{||\chi_{C_{f}^{w}}||_{2}^{2}},\quad v\in L^{2}(\Omega,\nu).

Recall from Section 2.3 that is constructed so that
\text{diam}(C_{f}^{w})\leq\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/2^{nL})
. If
P_{w}=\langle\cdot,\chi_{C_{f}^{w}}\rangle\frac{\chi_{C_{f}^{w}}}{||\chi_{C_{f}^{w}}||_{2}^{2}}
, observe that for
L>L_{0}

||[P_{L},\tau_{\Omega,\nu}]||=\max_{C_{f}^{w}}||[P_{w},\tau_{\Omega,\nu}]||\leq\max_{C_{f}^{w}}2\text{diam}(C_{f}^{w})\leq 2\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/2^{nL}).

Since
P_{L}\uparrow I
as
L\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\displaystyle|[P_{L},\tau_{\Omega,\nu}]|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq||[P_{L},\tau_{\Omega,\nu}]||\sum_{k=1}^{\text{rank}(P_{L})}\rho_{k}

\displaystyle\leq||[P_{L},\tau_{\Omega,\nu}]||\int_{1}^{|W_{L}|}h^{\prime}(x+N-1)\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/2^{nL})}{f^{-1}(1/2|W_{L}|)}.

As seen in Section 2.3, there exists a constant
\xi>0
dependent only on
n
such that
\xi\cdot 2^{-nL}\leq H_{f}(C_{f}^{w})
. If
w\in W_{L}
so that
L>L_{0}
, then
|W_{L}|H_{f}(C_{f}^{w})=H_{f}(U_{L})<H_{f}(\Omega)+\varepsilon
. Let
t_{L}:=H_{f}(C_{f}^{w})/\xi
. Then

\frac{1}{f^{-1}(1/2|W_{L}|)}<\frac{1}{f^{-1}(\xi t_{L}/(2H_{f}(\Omega)+2\varepsilon))}.

In the next chain of inequalities, we will be using regular variation of
f^{-1}
once more. To make the usage more apparent, we set
\alpha:=\xi/(2H_{f}(\Omega)+2\varepsilon)
. With
L
chosen large enough so that
{f^{-1}(t_{L})/f^{-1}(\alpha t_{L})}<\alpha^{-1/s}+\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle|[P_{L},\tau_{\Omega,\nu}]|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(1/2^{nL})}{f^{-1}(1/2|W_{L}|)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sqrt{n}f^{-1}(t_{L})}{f^{-1}(\alpha t_{L})}

\displaystyle<2\sqrt{n}(\alpha^{-1/s}+\varepsilon)

\displaystyle<\frac{2\sqrt{n}}{\xi^{1/s}}(2(H_{f}(\Omega)+\varepsilon)^{1/s}+2\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq D(H_{f}(\Omega))^{1/s}+4\varepsilon^{1/s}

Since
\varepsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, we conclude
k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega,\nu})\leq D(H_{f}(\Omega))^{1/s}.\qed

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
f
be a gauge function with property
(R_{s})
that satisfies inequality (3), and let
\tau
be an
n
-tuple of commuting self-adjoint operators with
\sigma(\tau)\subset C_{f}
. If
E_{\tau}
is singular with respect to
H_{f}
, then
k_{\rho}(\tau)=0
.

Proof.

Observe that we may assume
\tau
has a cyclic vector
\eta
and hence
\tau
is unitarily equivalent to some
\tau_{\Omega,\nu}
defined on
L^{2}(\Omega,\nu)
with
\Omega\subset C_{f}
and
\nu
a Borel probability measure singular with respect to
H_{f}
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we may choose a set
\omega_{m}\subset\Omega
which is a union of generation sets of the same size so that
H_{f}(\omega_{m})<\varepsilon
and
\nu(\Omega\setminus\omega_{m})<2^{-m}
for all
x\in\Hilb_{ac}
. By the previous lemma, we see that

k_{\rho}(\tau E_{\tau}(\omega_{m}))<D\cdot(H_{f}(\omega_{m}))^{1/s}<D\cdot\varepsilon^{1/s}

for some constant
D>0
dependent only on
f
. Since
E_{\tau}(\omega_{m})
is in the commutant
(\{\tau\})^{\prime}
, and since
\eta
is cyclic for
\tau
, the inequality
||\eta-E_{\tau}(\omega_{m})\eta||^{2}=\nu(\Omega\setminus\omega_{m})\leq 2^{-m}
implies that
E_{\tau}(\omega_{m})
converges strongly to
I
. Choosing
A_{k}\in\mathcal{R}_{1}^{+}
so that
A_{k}\leq E_{\tau}(\omega_{k})
,
A_{k}\uparrow I
, and
k_{\rho}(\tau E_{\tau}(\omega_{n}))\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|[A_{k},\tau E_{\tau}(\omega_{m})]|_{\rho}
yields

k_{\rho}(\tau)\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}|[A_{k},\tau]|_{\rho}\leq k_{\rho}(\tau E_{\tau}(\omega_{m}))<D\cdot\varepsilon.

letting
\varepsilon
tend to 0 yields the result.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
f,\tau
be as above. If
m
is the multiplicity function for
\tau
, then

(k_{\rho}(\tau))^{s}=\kappa_{s}^{(f)}\int_{\sigma(\tau)}m(x)dH_{f}(x),

where
\kappa_{s}^{(f)}=(k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}}))^{s}/H_{f}(C_{f}).

Proof.

Begin by noting that
0<\kappa_{s}^{(f)}<\infty
by Theorem 3.6. By Corollary 5.2, we may assume
\Hilb=\Hilb_{ac}
. Thus
\tau
is unitarily equivalent to
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}\tau_{\Omega_{j}}
for some Borel sets
\Omega_{j}
. Furthermore, Lemma 2.10 shows that we can assume
\tau=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{\Omega_{j}}
.

Let
W_{L_{j}}=\{w:|w|=L\text{ and }C_{f}^{w}\cap\Omega_{j}\neq\varnothing\}
. First, assume that
\Omega_{j}=\bigcup_{w\in W_{L_{j}}}C_{f}^{w}
for some fixed positive integer
L_{j}
. Since each generation set is a union of smaller generation sets, we may assume
L_{j}=L
for some fixed
L
. Let
M=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|W_{L_{j}}|
. Thus for any generation set
C_{f}^{w}
with
|w|=L

\displaystyle(k_{\rho}(\tau))^{s}

\displaystyle=\bigg(k_{\rho}\bigg(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{\Omega_{j}}\bigg)\bigg)^{s}=(k_{\rho}(I_{M}\otimes\tau_{C_{f}^{w}}))^{s}=M(k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}^{w}}))^{s}.

Given this, it suffices to show

(k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}^{w}}))^{s}=\kappa_{s}^{(f)}H_{f}(C_{f}^{w})

for any choice of
w
. Observe that

k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}})=k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}^{w}}\otimes I_{2^{nL}})=2^{nL/s}k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}^{w}})

so we have

(k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}^{w}}))^{s}=(2^{-nL/s}k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}}))^{s}=2^{-nL}\kappa_{s}^{(f)}H_{f}(C_{f})=\kappa_{s}^{(f)}H_{f}(C_{f}^{w}).

Thus result is proven in this case.

Next, suppose that
\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}U_{j}
for some open sets
\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
. Note that each
U_{j}
can be well approximated by a finite union of some
C_{f}^{w}
, and these sets can be chosen so that for every
w
,
|w|=L_{j}
. Choosing
L=\min\{L_{j}\}
and
w
with
|w|=L
, we have reduced the problem to showing that

(k_{\rho}(\tau_{C_{f}^{w}}\otimes I_{N2^{nL}}))^{s}=N2^{nL}\kappa_{s}^{(f)}H_{f}(C_{f}^{w})

which is true by the previous argument.

Finally, let
\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\Omega_{j}
for
\Omega_{j}
arbitrary Borel sets. Since for each
\Omega_{j}
there is
K_{j}
compact and
U_{j}
open so that
K_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}\subset U_{j}
and
H_{f}(U_{j}\setminus K_{j})<\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
. Since
|k_{\rho}(\tau_{\Omega_{j}})-k_{\rho}(\tau_{U_{j}})|\leq k_{\rho}(\tau_{U_{j}\setminus K_{j}})\leq\varepsilon
, we can reduce the argument to the previous case and conclude the proof.

∎

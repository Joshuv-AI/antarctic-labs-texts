Abstract
. Given two subgroups H , K H,K of a finite group G G , the probability that a pair of random elements from H H and K K commutes is denoted by Pr ⁡ ( H , K ) \mathrm{Pr}(H,K) . We address the following question. Let P P be a p p -subgroup of a finite group G G and assume that Pr ⁡ ( P , P x ) ≥ ϵ > 0 \mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{x})\geq\epsilon>0 for every x ∈ G x\in G . Is the order of P P modulo O p ​ ( G ) O_{p}(G) bounded in terms of ϵ \epsilon only? With respect to this question, we establish several positive results but show that in general the answer is negative. In particular, we prove that if the composition factors of G G , which are isomorphic to simple groups of Lie type in characteristic p p , have Lie rank at most n n , then the order of P P modulo O p ​ ( G ) O_{p}(G) is bounded in terms of n n and ϵ \epsilon only (Theorem 1.3 ). If P P is a Sylow p p -subgroup of G G , then the order of P P modulo O p ​ ( G ) O_{p}(G) is bounded in terms ϵ \epsilon only (Theorem 1.1 ). Some other results of similar flavour are established. We also show that if Pr ⁡ ( P 1 , P 2 ) > 0 \mathrm{Pr}(P_{1},P_{2})>0 for every two Sylow p p -subgroups P 1 , P 2 P_{1},P_{2} of a profinite group G G , then O p , p ′ ​ ( G ) O_{p,p^{\prime}}(G) is open in G G (Theorem 1.7 ).

1.
Introduction

The commuting probability
\mathrm{Pr}(G)
of a finite group
G
is the probability that two random elements of
G
commute. More generally,
if
H
and
K
are two subgroups of
G
, we write
\mathrm{Pr}(H,K)
for the probability that two randomly chosen elements from
H
and
K
commute. Thus,

\mathrm{Pr}(H,K)=\frac{|\{(x,y)\in H\times K\mid xy=yx\}|}{|H|\,|K|}.

It is well-known that
\mathrm{Pr}(G)\leq 5/8
for every nonabelian group G. Another important result is a
theorem of Peter Neumann which states that if
\mathrm{Pr}(G)\geq\epsilon>0
, then
G
has a subgroup
H
such that the index
|G:H|
and the order of
H^{\prime}
are both bounded in terms of
\epsilon
only
[
21
]
. In what follows we say for brevity that
G
is bounded-by-abelian-by-bounded if it has a structure as in Neumann’s theorem. The reader is referred to the papers
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
]

for further results on commuting probability. The recent article
[
10
]
handles finite groups
G
in which for every pair of distinct primes
p,q\in\pi(G)
there is a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
and a Sylow
q
-subgroup
Q
such that
\mathrm{Pr}(P,Q)\geq\epsilon
. Among other results, it was shown that the index
|G:F_{2}(G)|
is bounded in terms of
\epsilon
only. Here
F_{2}(G)
stands for the second term of the upper Fitting series of
G
.

In this paper we consider finite groups
G
such that for some prime
p\in\pi(G)
we have
\mathrm{Pr}(P_{1},P_{2})\geq\epsilon>0
whenever
P_{1},P_{2}
are Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
. As usual,
O_{p}(G)
stands for the maximal normal
p
-subgroup of
G
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p\in\pi(G)
. Assume that
\mathrm{Pr}(P_{1},P_{2})\geq\epsilon>0
for every two Sylow
p
-subgroups
P_{1},P_{2}
of
G
. Then the order of a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G/O_{p}(G)
is bounded in terms of
\epsilon
only. Moreover
O_{p}(G)
is bounded-by-abelian-by-bounded.

A curious corollary of the above result is that if the condition holds for every prime in
\pi(G)
, then the structure of
G
is roughly similar to that of a group
K
with
\mathrm{Pr}(K)\geq\epsilon
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group such that for every
p\in\pi(G)
and every two Sylow
p
-subgroups
P_{1},P_{2}
of
G
we have
\mathrm{Pr}(P_{1},P_{2})\geq\epsilon>0
. Then
G
is bounded-by-abelian-by-bounded.

In the course of the work on the above results, the following challenging question was raised.

Let

be a
p
-subgroup of a finite group

\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{x})\geq\epsilon>0
for every

. Is the order of

modulo

bounded in terms of
\epsilon
only?

The answer to this turns out to be negative – see Example

. Yet, we have established several positive results with respect to the above question.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group containing a
p
-subgroup
P
such that
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{x})\geq\epsilon>0
for every
x\in G
. Suppose that the composition factors of
G
, which are isomorphic to simple groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
, have Lie rank at most
n
. Then the order of
P
modulo
O_{p}(G)
is bounded in terms of
n
and
\epsilon
only.

This shows that in the case of
p
-soluble groups the answer to the above question is positive.

Corollary 1.4

Assume that a finite
p
-soluble group

contains a
p
-subgroup
P
such that
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{x})\geq\epsilon>0
for every

O_{p}(G)
is bounded in terms of
\epsilon

Let

E_{p}(H/O_{p}(H))
and
O_{p,e_{p},p}(H)

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that a finite group
G
contains a
p

\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{x})\geq\epsilon>0

The above theorems can be easily extended to the case where the order of the subgroup is not necessarily a prime power. This is because, given a subgroup

such that

Corollary 1.6
.

Let a finite group

(1)

\epsilon

The concept of commuting probability can be naturally extended to compact groups, using the normalized Haar measure (see Section 6 for details).

In particular, Theorem
1.1
can be naturally extended to profinite groups
G
such that there is a positive number
\epsilon

with the property that

of

On the other hand, in the realm of profinite groups, it is more natural to handle the situation where the probabilities are positive, but not necessarily bounded away from zero.

In this paper we prove the following profinite variation of Theorem

Theorem 1.7
.

Let

be a profinite group and

\mathrm{Pr}(P_{1},P_{2})>0
for every two Sylow

-subgroups
P_{1},P_{2}
of

. Then

is open. Moreover
O_{p}(G)
is virtually abelian.

As usual, we say that a profinite group has a property virtually if it has an open subgroup with that property.

We start with a lemma which lists some well-known properties of coprime actions (see for example
[
13
, Ch. 5 and 6]
). In the sequel the lemma will often be used without explicit references.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a group of coprime automorphisms of a finite group
G
. Then

(i)

G=[G,A]C_{G}(A)
. If
G
is abelian, then
G=[G,A]\times C_{G}(A)
.

(ii)

[G,A,A]=[G,A]
.

(iii)

C_{G/N}(A)=NC_{G}(A)/N
for every
A
-invariant normal subgroup
N
of
G
.

(iv)

If
[G/\Phi(G),A]=1
, then
[G,A]=1
.

The next lemma will be useful.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group having subgroups
N_{1},\dots,N_{s}

such that
\cap_{i=1}^{s}N_{i}=1
but
\cap_{j\in J}N_{j}\neq 1
for every proper subset
J
of
\{1,\dots,s\}
.
Then
\cup_{i=1}^{s}N_{i}\neq G
.

Proof.

Suppose
G
is a counter-example with
s
as small as possible. Note that
N_{s}
and its subgroups
U_{i}=N_{i}\cap N_{s}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,s-1\}
, satisfy the hypothesis. By minimality,
N_{s}\neq\cup_{i=1}^{s-1}U_{i}
. Moreover
X=\cap_{1\leq i\leq s-1}N_{i}
is nontrivial. Let us fix
1\neq x\in X
and
y\in N_{s}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{s-1}U_{i}
. If
xy\in N_{s}
, then
x\in X\cap N_{s}=1
, a contradiction. If
xy\in N_{i}
, for some
i<s
, then
y\in N_{i}\cap N_{s}=U_{i}
, a contradiction again. Therefore
xy\notin\cup_{i=1}^{s}N_{i}
.
∎

Throughout this section we assume Hypothesis

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
G=MP
, where
M=F(G)
and assume that
O_{p}(G)=1
. Then the order of
P
is bounded.

Proof.

Note that
F(G)
is a
p^{\prime}
-group. We can pass to the quotient
F(G)/\Phi(F(G))
over the Frattini subgroup and without loss of generality assume that
M
is a direct sum of elementary abelian subgroups. By Maschke’s theorem

M=M_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{s},

where each
M_{i}
is an irreducible
P
-module. Since
O_{p}(G)=1
, we have
C_{P}(M)=1
. Moreover
P
acts faithfully on
[M,P]
so we may assume that
M=[M,P]
and therefore
C_{M}(P)=1
.

Choose elements
1\neq a_{i}\in M_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
and set
a=a_{1}+\ldots+a_{s}
.

Let
Q=Q_{a}
be the normal subgroup of
P
as in Lemma
2.10
. As
Q
has bounded index in
P
, it is sufficient to show that the order of
Q
is bounded. We can thus assume that
Q\neq 1
.

Let
K_{i}=C_{Q}(M_{i})
. Note that
\cap_{i=1}^{s}K_{i}=1
. Let
J
be a minimal subset of the set
\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
\cap_{j\in J}K_{j}=1
. Observe that
Q
acts faithfully on
\sum_{j\in J}M_{j}
. In view of Lemma
2.2
,
Q\neq\cup_{j\in J}K_{j}
. Therefore there is
g\in Q
such that
g
acts nontrivially on each
M_{j}
for
j\in J
.

Let
D=D_{a,g}
be as in Lemma
2.10
and
L=\langle g^{P}\rangle
. According to Lemma
2.10
, the subgroup
L
centralizes
[a,D]
and, in particular,
L
centralizes each of the subgroups
[a_{i},D]\leq M_{i}
.

Let

. Since

is normal in

, the centralizer

is a

-submodule of

. As each

is irreducible, we have that either

or

. As

acts nontrivially on

, we deduce that
C_{M_{j}}(L)=1
.
It follows that
[a_{j},D]=1
. So

and

is nontrivial. Because

is normal in

and

is irreducible, we conclude that
D

.

Therefore

. Since the index

is bounded, so is the order of

. This completes the proof.

M=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{l}

is a finite nonabelian simple group and
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{l}\}
is a

-orbit. Let

be the stabilizer of

. We need to show that the index of

in

is bounded. For an element

set

and
P_{2}=P^{x}
. Let
Q=Q_{x}
be the normal subgroup of
P
as in Lemma
2.10
. Suppose

is not contained in
P^{*}
and choose

such that

. Then, by Lemma
2.10
,
P
contains a subgroup

of bounded index such that

centralizes
[x,D]
. If

, we are done. Otherwise, pick

such that

. For simplicity assume that

. We see that

is a nontrivial element of

. So

has nontrivial centralizer in

and therefore

is a

-orbit. This argument can be applied for every element of

. Since

belongs to only one

-orbit, it follows that
S_{1}^{y}\in\{S_{1},S_{2}\}
for every element

, that is

is a

-orbit. Hence, the index of

in

is at most

and therefore the index of

in

is bounded.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
G=MP
, where
M
is a normal semisimple subgroup of
G
, and assume that
O_{p}(G)=1
.
Let
C
be a constant such that

|P:C_{P}(S)|\leq C
for every simple factor
S
of
M
. Then the order of
P
is
(C,\epsilon)
-bounded.

Proof.

Note that
C_{P}(M)=1
.
Write
M=M_{1}\times\cdots\times M_{l}
, where each
M_{i}
is a minimal normal subgroup of
G
. Let
J
be a minimal subset of the set
\{1,\dots,l\}
such that
P
acts faithfully on
\prod_{j\in J}M_{j}
. Replacing
M
by
\prod_{j\in J}M_{j}
we simply assume that
J=\{1,\dots,l\}
.

It follows from Proposition
3.2
that every
M_{i}
is a product of boundedly many simple factors so it is sufficient to bound
l
.

For every
i
set
N_{i}=\prod_{j\in J\setminus\{i\}}M_{j}
and
Q_{i}=C_{P}(N_{i})
. Choose an element
y_{i}
of prime order in
Q_{i}
and set
\D=\langle y_{1},\dots,y_{l}\rangle
. It is enough to show that
\D
has bounded order.

For each
i
, the element
y_{i}
induces on
M_{i}
an automorphism
\phi_{i}
of order
p
. Observe that the group of automorphisms induced by
\D
on
M_{i}
is
\langle\phi_{i}\rangle
. Pick an element
a_{i}\in M_{i}
such that
[a_{i},\phi_{i},\phi_{i}]\neq 1
. Such an element
a_{i}
exists for every
i
, because
otherwise
y_{i}
would be a left Engel element of
G
lying in the Fitting subgroup of
G
(see
[
22
, 12.3.7]
),
which immediately leads to a contradiction.

Let
a=\prod_{i}a_{i}
and let
Q=Q_{a}
be the normal subgroup of
\D
as in Lemma
2.10
. As
Q
has bounded index in
\D
, it is sufficient to bound the order of
Q
.

Let
I_{0}
be a minimal subset of
\{1,\dots,l\}
such that
\cap_{i\in I_{0}}C_{Q}(M_{i})=1
. It follows from Lemma
2.2
that there exists an element
g\in Q
such that
g\notin C_{Q}(M_{i})
for every
i\in I_{0}
.

By Lemma
2.10
, there exists a subgroup
D
of bounded index in
Q
such that
g
centralizes the subgroup
[a,D]
.
So
g
centralizes also the projections
[a_{i},D]
of
[a,D]
on
M_{i}
.
The group of automorphisms induced by
Q
on
M_{i}
is
\langle\phi_{i}\rangle
, which has prime order.
So if
[a_{i},D]\neq 1,
then
[a_{i},D]
contains the element
[a_{i},\phi_{i}]
. Moreover,
[a_{i},\phi_{i},g]\in[a_{i},D,g]=1
.

On the other hand, the automorphism induced by
g
on
M_{i}
is a nontrivial power of
\phi_{i}
. Therefore the fact that
[a_{i},\phi_{i},g]=1
implies that
[a_{i},\phi_{i},\phi_{i}]=1
, which contradicts the choice of
a_{i}
.

This means that
[a_{i},D]=1
for every
i
. If the automorphism
\phi_{i}^{r_{i}}
induced by

on
M_{i}
is nontrivial, then
\phi_{i}^{r_{i}}
centralizes
a_{i}
and so does
\phi_{i}
, a contradiction again. Therefore

centralizes all
M_{i}
for
i\in I_{0}
and

has bounded order. The lemma follows.
∎

4.
The case where
G
is almost simple

We start this section by giving an example showing that under Hypotheses
2.6
the order of
P
can be arbitrarily large even if
O_{p}(G)=1
.

Example 4.1
.

Let
G={\mathrm{SL}}(n,p)=\mathrm{SL}(V)
for
n\geq 3
, and let
A
be a hyperplane in
V
.

Let
P
be the elementary abelian subgroup of order
p^{n-1}
which is trivial on both
A
and
V/A
, and choose
x\in G\setminus N_{G}(P)
.

Note that
P
and
P^{x}
intersect trivially and the subgroup
\langle P,P^{x}\rangle
has an abelian subgroup
Q
of order
p^{2n-2}
such that
Q\cap P
and
Q\cap P^{x}
each have index
p
in
P
and
P^{x}
respectively. We see that
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{x})\geq 1/p^{2}
. Since
n
can be arbitrarily large, it follows that there is no bound on the order of
P
.
∎

The main goal of this section is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.6
with
G
almost simple. Then the order of
P
is bounded unless the socle
S
is of Lie type in characteristic
p
. If
S
is of Lie type in characteristic
p
of rank
n
, then the order of
P
is
(n,\epsilon)
-bounded.

Given a finite group
G
and a subgroup
H
, let

e_{G}(H)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\mathrm{Pr}(H,H^{g})

be the average value of
\mathrm{Pr}(H,H^{g}),\ g\in G
. Observe that the probability that an element
x
commutes with a random conjugate of an element
y
is
\frac{|y^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|}{|y^{G}|}
. We write
H^{\#}
to denote the set of nontrivial elements of
H
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f\leq 1
and suppose that
H
is a subgroup of a finite group
G
such that for every pair of nontrivial elements
x,y\in H
the probability that
x
and a random conjugate of
y
commute is at most
f
. Then

e_{G}(H)<2/|H|+f.

Proof.

Let
n=|H|
, thus

\displaystyle n^{2}|G|e_{G}(H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n^{2}\sum_{g\in G}\mathrm{Pr}(H,H^{g})=\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{x\in H}|C_{H^{g}}(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G}\left(n+(n-1)+\sum_{x\in H^{\#}}|C_{H^{g\#}}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n-1)|G|+\sum_{x\in H^{\#}}\left(\sum_{g\in G}|C_{H^{g\#}}(x)|\right).

Now

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G}|C_{H^{g\#}}(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G}|\{y\in H^{\#}\mid[x,y^{g}]=1\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{(g,y)\in G\times H^{\#}\mid[x,y^{g}]=1\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in H^{\#}}|\{g\in G\mid[x,y^{g}]=1\}|.

Note that
|\{w\in G\mid y^{w}=y^{g}\}|=|C_{G}(y)|
.
Now fix
1\neq x,y\in H
. We have

\displaystyle|\{(x,g)\mid g\in G,[x,y^{g}]=1\}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\{(x,y^{g})\mid[x,y^{g}]=1\}|\,|C_{G}(y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|\,|C_{G}(y)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|y^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|}{|y^{G}|}\,|y^{G}||C_{G}(y)|\leq f|G|.

Thus, the total number of commuting pairs
(x,z^{g})
as
z
ranges over
H^{\#}
and
g
ranges over
G

is at most
(n-1)f|G|,
as there are
n-1
choices for
z
:

|\{(g,y)\in G\times H^{\#}\mid[x,y^{g}]=1\}|\leq(n-1)f|G|.

Thus,

\sum_{g\in G}\left(\sum_{x\in H^{\#}}|C_{H^{g\#}}(x)|\right)\leq(n-1)^{2}f|G|.

Putting all together we have

n^{2}|G|e_{G}(H)\leq(2n-1)|G|+(n-1)^{2}f|G|,

which gives

e_{G}(H)\leq\frac{2n-1}{n^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)^{2}f}{n^{2}}<2/n+f,

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
n=p^{a}\geq 5
and let
G=S_{n}
be the symmetric group of degree
n
. Let
P
be a regular elementary abelian
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then
e_{G}(P)\rightarrow 0
as
a\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

If
1\neq x\in P
, then
x
is a fixed-point-free permutation of order
p
. Observe that
P^{\#}\subset x^{G}
.
Let
f
be the probability that
x
and a random conjugate of
x
commute. Thus,
f=|x^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|/|x^{G}|
.
Note that
C_{G}(x)
has
a normal elementary abelian subgroup of order
p^{p^{a-1}}
with the quotient isomorphic to
S_{n/p}
.

If
p
is odd, observe that

\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}|C_{G}(x)|/|x^{G}|=\lim_{a\rightarrow\infty}|C_{G}(x)|^{2}/|G|=0.

Indeed, we have
|C_{G}(x)|^{2}/|G|=|S_{n/p}|^{2}p^{2p^{a-1}}/(p^{a})!
and
this tends to zero when
a
goes to infinity.

If
p=2
, we note that
|x^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|<2^{n/2}|I_{2}(S_{n/2})|
where
I_{2}(H)
is the number
of involutions in
H
. As the number of involutions in
S_{m}
is asymptotic to

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}m^{\frac{m}{2}}e^{-\frac{m}{2}+\sqrt{m}-\frac{1}{4}}

(cf.
[
23
, p. 155–158]
),
it follows that

f=\frac{|x^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|}{|x^{G}|}\leq\frac{2^{n/2}|I_{2}(S_{n/2})|\,|C_{G}(x)|}{|G|}\leq\frac{2^{n/2}|I_{2}(S_{n/2})|2^{n/2}|S_{n/2}|}{n!},

which tends to zero.

Lemma
4.3
tells us that
e_{G}(P)<2/n+f
and so the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

By virtue of Lemma
2.11
, under the hypotheses of Theorem
4.2
there is a bounded integer

such that
P^{e}=1
. In particular
p\leq e
. Since the order of a finite group is bounded in terms of the maximum of its abelian subgroups
(see for instance
[

, Theorem 5.2]
),
we may replace

by an abelian subgroup and simply assume that

is abelian. Since
P
has bounded exponent, we may again replace

by an elementary abelian subgroup and assume that

is elementary abelian.

Remark 4.6
.

Note that whenever
P
is a
p
-subgroup of a finite group

such that
P\not\leq O_{p}(G)
there is
g\in G
such that

does not commute with

. Hence
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{g})\leq 3/4
by Lemma
2.4
(
4
).

Lemma 4.7
.

Theorem
4.2
holds for alternating and symmetric groups.

Proof.

Assume Hypothesis
2.6
with
G=S_{n}
, the symmetric group of degree
n,
and
P
an elementary abelian
p
-subgroup of
G
. Let
m
the size of the largest orbit for
P
.

We wish to show that
m
is bounded. So assume that
m\geq 5
. Let
\Omega
be an orbit of size
m
, and let
Q
be the kernel of

on
\Omega
. Since

is elementary abelian, there exists
P_{0}
such that
P=P_{0}\times Q
.
Observe that
P_{0}
acts regularly on
\Omega
. By Lemma
4.4
, there exists
n_{0}
such that if

, then
e_{S_{m}}(P_{0})<\epsilon
.
In particular for at least one element

, we have
\mathrm{Pr}(P_{0},P_{0}^{g})<\epsilon
. Thus we can find
\tilde{g}\in S_{n}
, such that
\tilde{g}
acts as

on

, and so

\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{\tilde{g}})\leq\mathrm{Pr}(P_{0},P_{0}^{g})<\epsilon,

against our assumptions. Therefore,

.

Let

be the minimum number such that there are
s

-invariant disjoint subsets
\Omega_{1},\dots,\Omega_{s}
, each of size at least

, such that

acts faithfully on their union.

Now we need to bound

. We claim that there exists

such that
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{g})<(3/4)^{s}
. This will be proved by induction on

. If

, the result is immediate from Remark

;
so assume that

.

Let

be the kernel of the action of

on
\Omega_{s}
and write

, with
P_{s}
acting faithfully on
\Omega_{s}
.
By Remark

there is

with support in

such that
\mathrm{Pr}(P_{s},P_{s}^{g_{s}})<3/4
. Observe that

acts faithfully on the union of

. By induction there exists

, with support disjoint from that of

, such that
\mathrm{Pr}(Q,Q^{g_{1}})<(3/4)^{s-1}
. Therefore, for

, we have
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{g})<(3/4)^{s}
, as claimed.
Thus,
(3/4)^{s}\geq\epsilon
and so

Note that

can be embedded in a product of at most

. Hence
|P|
is bounded and the case where

is done.

To deal with the case where

is the alternating group, observe that

contains a subgroup

of index at most

such that any conjugate of

in

is in fact a conjugate in

is immediate from the above.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be an almost simple group of Lie type defined over a field of size
q
and Lie rank at least
2
. If
q
is sufficiently large, then for every subgroup
H
of
G
we have
e_{G}(H)<2/|H|+4/3q
.
In any case,
e_{G}(H)<2/|H|+f(q)
, where
f(q)
tends to
0
as
q
goes to infinity.

Proof.

Let
x,y
be nontrivial elements of
G
.
The group
G
naturally acts on the conjugacy class
y^{G}
and the kernel of this action is trivial. Let
\Sigma=\{B_{1},\dots,B_{k}\}
be a system of maximal blocks of imprimitivity, each of order
m
, so that
|y^{G}|=m|\Sigma|
and
G
acts faithfully and primitively on
\Sigma
.

If
x\in G
fixes
B_{i}
, then
x
centralizes at most
m
elements in
B_{i}
. Therefore
|y^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|\leq m|fix_{\Sigma}(x)|.

By
[

, Theorem 1]

|fix_{\Sigma}(x)|/|\Sigma|\leq 4/3q

apart from some known exceptions that have rank

or small order. In any case,

|fix_{\Sigma}(x)|/|\Sigma|\leq f(q)
,
where
f(q)
tends to
0
as
q
goes to infinity.
It follows that

\frac{|y^{G}\cap C_{G}(x)|}{|y^{G}|}\leq\frac{m|fix_{\Sigma}(x)|}{m|\Sigma|}\leq f(q),

so the probability that

and a random conjugate of

commute is bounded by

.

Now using Lemma
4.3
we deduce that

e_{G}(H)<2/|H|+f(q),

as required.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

There is a bounded positive integer
q_{0}
such that, if under Hypothesis
2.6
the group
G
is an almost simple group of Lie type defined over a field of size

, then
|P|\leq 4/\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let
f(q)
be a function as in Lemma
4.8
. There exists a number
q_{0}
depending only on
\epsilon
such that if
q>q_{0}
, then
f(q)<\epsilon/2
. It follows from Lemma
4.8
that
e_{G}(P)<2/|P|+\epsilon/2
. There is an element

such that
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{g})\leq e_{G}(P)
, while
\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{g})\geq\epsilon
by hypotheses.
Putting all this together we get

\epsilon\leq\mathrm{Pr}(P,P^{g})\leq e_{G}(P)<2/|P|+\epsilon/2,

whence

and so
|P|\leq 4/\epsilon
.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.6
with
G
a simple group of Lie type in characteristic other than
p
. Then the order of

is bounded.

Proof.

As before, we can assume that
P
is elementary abelian and the prime
p
is bounded. Because of Lemma
4.9
we can assume that the size of the definition field is bounded. Then the order of
G
is bounded in terms of the rank and so without loss of generality we can assume that
G
is of classical type. Here
V
is the natural module for
G
. It is more convenient to work in the linear group so we lift

to a subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
, which we still denote by
G
. Let
T
be the full preimage of
P
in
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
. Then

has order at most
p
and so

contains an elementary abelian

-subgroup
H
of order at least
|P|^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Taking into account that

is bounded we invoke Lemma

, which shows that there is a positive number
\delta
, depending only on
\epsilon
, such that for every

we have
\mathrm{Pr}(H,H^{g})\geq\delta
. As it is enough to bound the order of

, we may assume that

is an elementary abelian subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}(V)
. It will be convenient to work under the additional assumption that
P\cap Z(G)=1
. This can be done without loss of generality, taking into account that

contains a subgroup of index

that avoids
Z(G)
and replacing

by that subgroup.

Aside from the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(V)
, we have a form and so the notion of nondegenerate and totally singular are well defined. In the case of
{\mathrm{SL}}(V)
, we consider all subspaces both totally singular and nondegenerate.

Given a subspace
W
of
V
, we write
G(W)
for the group induced by the action of the stabilizer of

on
W
. We say that a
P
-submodule

is

if it is a nondegenerate submodule that is minimal subject to the condition that

is almost simple and the image of

in

is not central.
Note that if

is almost simple, then the image of

in
G(W)
is not central whenever it is non-cyclic.
Moreover, if

is good, then the image of

in

does not commute with some of its conjugates.

Since

is not contained in

, good

-submodules exist.

We claim that the dimension of every good

-submodule is bounded.
Observe that irreducible

-submodules all have dimension at most

and recall that

is bounded.
Assume that
P
is non-cyclic and observe that the group
G(W)
is almost simple modulo its centre whenever

is a nondegenerate subspace of dimension at least
5
. Every

-submodule of dimension

is contained in a nondegenerate

-submodule of dimension at most

. Let

be a good submodule. There are

irreducible

-submodules

such that the image of

in

is non-cyclic. This is contained in a nondegenerate

-submodule

of dimension at most twice that of

. If

is almost simple modulo its centre, then, because of minimality,

. Otherwise, the dimension of
U_{3}
is at most
4
and

contains a nondegenerate

-submodule
U_{4}
, which is a sum of at most two irreducible

-submodules. Set
U_{5}=U_{3}+U_{4}
. If
G(U_{5})
is almost simple modulo its centre, then because of minimality
U_{5}=U
. Otherwise, the dimension of
U_{5}
is exactly
4
and

contains a nondegenerate

-submodule
U_{6}
, which is a sum of at most two irreducible

-submodules. It is clear that the dimension of

is at least

and

is almost simple modulo its centre. Because of minimality,

. Hence, the dimension of

is bounded.

Let
W
be a maximal

-submodule that can be written as an orthogonal sum of good submodules. Let

be the kernel of the action of

on

. It follows that either

is not almost simple modulo its centre or

embeds into

. In either case the order of

is bounded and we can replace

by a subgroup that avoids

. So without loss of generality we assume that

acts on

Let now

be the minimal number such that there is a faithful

-submodule

, which can be written as an orthogonal sum

where

are good submodules.

Obviously,

is bounded in terms of

and so we now need to show that

is

-bounded.

Let

be the kernel of the action of

on

and note that the index

is bounded. Since the module

is faithful,

.
For each

set

Observe that because of minimality of

If

Denoting the normalized Haar measures of

Note that
\mathrm{Pr}(H_{0},K)\geq\mathrm{Pr}(H,K)
for every subgroup

of

(see

Lemma 7.1

By definition,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\frac{HN}{N},\frac{KN}{N}\right).

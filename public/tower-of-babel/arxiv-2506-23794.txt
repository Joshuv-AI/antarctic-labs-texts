Sabotage the Mantel Theorem
Natalie Behague, Debsoumya Chakraborti, Xizhi Liu

Abstract
One of the earliest results in extremal graph theory, Mantel’s theorem, states that the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph G G on n n vertices is ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor . We investigate how this extremal bound is affected when G G is additionally required to contain a prescribed graph ℙ \mathbb{P} as a subgraph. We establish general upper and lower bounds for this problem, which are tight in the exponent for random triangle-free graphs and graphs generated by the triangle-free process, when the size of ℙ \mathbb{P} lies within certain ranges.

Introduction

Mantel’s theorem
[
Man07
]
, one of the earliest results in extremal graph theory, states that the number of edges in every triangle-free graph on
n
vertices is at most
\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
, with equality attained uniquely by the balanced complete bipartite graph
K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
.

We consider the following variation of this classical result:
Given a vertex set
V
of size
n
and a prescribed triangle-free graph
\mathbb{P}
on
V
, what is the maximum number of edges in another triangle-free graph
G
on
V
that contains
\mathbb{P}
as a subgraph?
In other words, we are interested in the function

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})\coloneqq\max\left\{e(G)\colon\text{$G\subseteq\binom{V}{2}$ is $K_{3}$-free and $\mathbb{P}\subseteq G$}\right\}.

Here, we identify a graph
G
with its edge set and use
e(G)
to denote the number of edges in it.
It is clear that if
\mathbb{P}
is a subgraph of
K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
, then
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})
equals
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{3})
, and is strictly smaller otherwise (due to the fact that
K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
is uniquely extremal).

One of the simplest examples is perhaps when
\mathbb{P}=K_{1,m}
, the star with
m
edges.
Note that for
m>\lceil n/2\rceil
, the graph
K_{1,m}
is not a subgraph of
K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil}
, so it follows from the discussion above that
\mathrm{ex}_{K_{1,m}}(n,K_{3})<\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{3})
.
The exact value of
\mathrm{ex}_{K_{1,m}}(n,K_{3})
follows from a theorem

Before stating the main result, we introduce several related parameters.

Given a graph
\mathbb{P}
, let
d(\mathbb{P})
denote its

\mathrm{N}(S_{2},\mathbb{P})
denote the number of copies of

Recall that a vertex set

\mathbb{P}

The

\alpha(\mathbb{P})

\mathbb{P}

For a graph

\displaystyle\gamma(\mathbb{P})\coloneqq\frac{n(n-2)}{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-e(\mathbb{P})-\mathrm{N}(S_{2},\mathbb{P})}.

Let
\psi\colon[0,\infty)\to(0,1]
be the decreasing continuous function defined by

\displaystyle\psi(d)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{d\ln d-d+1}{(d-1)^{2}},&\quad\text{if}\quad d\not\in\{0,1\}\\
1,&\quad\text{if}\quad d=0,\\
\frac{1}{2},&\quad\text{if}\quad d=1.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a
K_{3}
-free on
[n]
.
Then the following statements hold.

(i)

We have
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})\leq{n\alpha(\mathbb{P})}/{2}
.

(ii)

Suppose that
e(\mathbb{P})+\mathrm{N}(S_{2},\mathbb{P})<\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
.Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})\geq\left(\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-e(\mathbb{P})-\mathrm{N}(S_{2},\mathbb{P})\right)\cdot\psi\big(\gamma(\mathbb{P})d(\mathbb{P})\big).

For positive real numbers
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
, we say a graph
\mathbb{P}
on
[n]
is
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
-constrained
(or simply

\beta_{1}

\displaystyle\alpha(\mathbb{P})\leq\frac{\beta_{1}n\ln d(\mathbb{P})}{d(\mathbb{P})}\quad\text{and}\quad e(\mathbb{P})\Delta(\mathbb{P})\leq\left(\frac{1}{4}-\beta_{2}\right)n^{2},

where
\Delta(\mathbb{P})
denotes the
maximum degree
of
\mathbb{P}
.
Note that for the first condition in the definition to hold, we implicitly require that
d(\mathbb{P})>1
.

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
and the inequality:

\displaystyle e(\mathbb{P})+\mathrm{N}(S_{2},\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(\mathbb{P})}d_{\mathbb{P}}(v)+\sum_{v\in V(\mathbb{P})}\binom{d_{\mathbb{P}}(v)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(\mathbb{P})}d_{\mathbb{P}}(v)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(\mathbb{P})}d_{\mathbb{P}}(v)\Delta(\mathbb{P})=e(\mathbb{P})\Delta(\mathbb{P}).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\beta_{1}>0

\beta_{2}>0

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})=\Theta_{n}\left(\frac{n^{2}\ln d}{d}\right).

The definition of constrained graphs is motivated by properties of the Erdős–Rényi random graph
G(n,d/n)

\displaystyle\alpha(G(n,d/n))\leq\frac{(2+o(1))n\ln d}{d}\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(G(n,d/n))\leq(1+o(1))\max\left\{d,~\frac{\ln n}{\ln\ln n}\right\}.

Thus

Unfortunately,

There exists a constant

Another important model of random triangle-free graphs, which plays a significant role in constructions related to Ramsey theory for triangles (see, e.g.,

Proof of Theorem
1.1

We prove Theorem
1.1
in this section.
We will use the following classical result of Shearer, which gives a lower bound on the independence number of triangle-free graphs.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
She83
]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices with average degree
d
.
Then
\alpha(G)\geq n\cdot\psi(d)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix a
K_{3}
-free graph
\mathbb{P}
on
[n]
.
We say a graph
G
on
[n]
is
\mathbb{P}
-admissible if it is
K_{3}
-free and contains
\mathbb{P}
as a subgraph.

First, we prove Theorem
1.1

(i)
.
Suppose that
G
is a
\mathbb{P}
-admissible graph.
Let
v\in[n]
be a vertex of maximum degree in
G
.
Since
G
is
K_{3}
-free, the neighborhood
N_{G}(v)
of
v
in
G
must be an independent set in
G
, and in particular, is independent in
\mathbb{P}
.
It follows that
\Delta(G)\leq\alpha(\mathbb{P})
, and hence,

\displaystyle e(G)\leq\frac{n\cdot\Delta(G)}{2}\leq\frac{n\cdot\alpha(\mathbb{P})}{2},

which proves Theorem
1.1

(i)
.

Next, we prove Theorem
1.1

(ii)
.
Consider the auxiliary
3
-graph
\mathcal{H}
whose vertex set is
\binom{[n]}{2}
(i.e. the edge set of the complete graph
K_{n}
), where
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}
forms an edge in
\mathcal{H}

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{e_{1}\in\binom{[n]}{2}\colon\text{there exist $e_{2},e_{3}\in\mathbb{P}$ such that $\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}\in\mathcal{H}$ }\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\{e_{1},e_{2}\}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}\colon\text{there exists $e_{3}\in\mathbb{P}$ such that $\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}\in\mathcal{H}$ }\right\},

where we think of
\mathcal{B}_{2}
as a graph on vertex set

Let
\mathcal{B}_{3}
denote the induced subgraph of

We say that a set

In particular,
\mathbb{P}

\mathbb{P}
.

The following claim follows directly from the definitions.

Claim 2.2
.

A graph

is

-admissible iff

(ii)

is an independent set in
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, and

(iii)

I

\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

Claim 2.3
.

We have

Proof of Claim

.

It follows from the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{1}

Therefore,

\displaystyle e(\mathcal{B}_{1})\leq\sum_{w\in[n]}\binom{d_{\mathbb{P}}(w)}{2}=\mathrm{N}(S_{2},\mathbb{P}),

2.3
.

Claim 2.4
.

We have
e(\mathcal{B}_{2})\leq e(\mathbb{P})(n-2)
.

Proof of Claim
2.4
.

It follows from the definition that
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}
is an edge in
\mathcal{B}_{2}
iff there exists three vertices

such that

,

, and

.
Therefore, each edge in
\mathbb{P}
contributes exactly
n-2
edges to
\mathcal{B}_{2}
.
It follows that
e(\mathcal{B}_{2})\leq e(\mathbb{P})(n-2)
.

Claim 2.5
.

The graph
\mathcal{B}_{2}
is

-free.

Proof of Claim
2.5
.

Suppose to the contrary that there exist three pairs

that span a copy of

in
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, i.e.
\big\{\{e_{1},e_{2}\},\{e_{1},e_{3}\},\{e_{2},e_{3}\}\big\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{2}

By the definition of

, for each pair of indices

.

Since

.
In both cases, the corresponding set

\mathbb{P}
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{B}_{2}
is
K_{3}
-free.

We are now ready to construct the set
I\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}\setminus\mathbb{P}

(i)
,
(ii)
, and
(iii)
.
We begin with an independent set
S\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}
in
\mathcal{H}

\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor

Define

We have

.

Concluding remarks

\bullet
The study of the function
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})
was partially motivated by results in
[
CLSW25
, Section 5]
, where the authors needed to upper bound the number of edges in graphs satisfying certain properties and containing a specific subgraph. Both of these problems fall under the scope of the following more general meta-question:
What happens to an extremal problem when we require the presence of a prescribed subgraph/subset?
We hope that our results could inspire further research in this direction.

\bullet
Determining the asymptotic behavior of
\mathrm{ex}_{T(n,d)}(n,K_{3})
and
\mathrm{ex}_{G(i)}(n,K_{3})
for all feasible values of
d
and
i
seems to be an interesting open problem.
It is well known in random graph theory that when
d=o(1)
, the components of the random graph
T(n,d)
(which is essentially equivalent to the Erdős–Rényi random graph
G(n,d/n)
in this regime) are,
w.h.p.
, trees of size
o(\ln n)
(see
[
FK16
, Theorems 2.1 and 2.9]
).
As a result,
T(n,d)
admits a bipartition of its vertex set in which the part sizes differ by
o(\ln n)
. Therefore,
w.h.p.
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{T(n,d)}(n,K_{3})=(1/4-o(1))n^{2}\quad\text{if}\quad d=o(1).

For
d\geq\left(\sqrt{3}/2+o(1)\right)\sqrt{n\ln n}
, a classical theorem of Osthus–Prömel–Taraz
[
OPT03
]
shows that,
w.h.p.
,
T(n,d)
is bipartite. Moreover, their proof implies that the bipartition has nearly equal part sizes, that is, the part sizes differ by
o(n)
.
Therefore,
w.h.p.

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{T(n,d)}(n,K_{3})=(1/4-o(1))n^{2}\quad\text{if}\quad d\geq\left(\sqrt{3}/2+o(1)\right)\sqrt{n\ln n}.

The cases not covered by Corollary
1.3
and the discussion above remain open for
\mathrm{ex}_{T(n,d)}(n,K_{3})
.
It is worth noting that recent work by Jenssen–Perkins–Potukuchi
[
JPP23
]
appears to be helpful for the case when
d
is near the threshold
\left(\sqrt{3}/2+o(1)\right)\sqrt{n\ln n}
.
Similarly, the range not addressed by Corollary
1.4
remains open for
\mathrm{ex}_{G(i)}(n,K_{3})
.

A natural extension of Theorem
1.1
is to replace
K_{3}
with a general family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
.
Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, a graph
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free
if it does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph.
Analogously, given an
\mathcal{F}
-free graph
\mathbb{P}
on
[n]
, one may ask for the value of the following function:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\left\{e(G)\colon\text{$G\subseteq\binom{[n]}{2}$ is $\mathcal{F}$-free and $\mathbb{P}\subseteq G$}\right\}.

\bullet
Note that Corollaries
1.3
and
1.4
can be viewed as results concerning
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})
in the average case.
One can also consider the worst-case scenario.
Let
m,n\geq 1
be integers. Define

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{m}(n,\mathcal{F})=\min\left\{\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,\mathcal{F})\colon\text{$\mathbb{P}$ is $\mathcal{F}$-free and $e(\mathbb{P})\leq m$}\right\}.

The function
\mathrm{ex}_{m}(n,\mathcal{F})
is closely related to a classical problem introduced by Erdős–Hajnal–Moon
[
EHM64
]
.
Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of graphs, a graph
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-saturated
if
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free but the addition of any new edge to
G
creates a subgraph that belongs to
\mathcal{F}
.
The
saturation number

\mathrm{sat}(n,\mathcal{F})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{sat}(n,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\min\left\{e(G)\colon\text{$v(G)=n$ and $G$ is $\mathcal{F}$-saturated}\right\}.

Note that for
m\geq\mathrm{sat}(n,\mathcal{F})
, the function
\mathrm{ex}_{m}(n,\mathcal{F})
reduces to
\mathrm{sat}(n,\mathcal{F})
.

For
m
close to
\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{3})
(i.e.
n-1
), it appears that
K_{1,m}
is the worst graph, that is,
\mathrm{ex}_{m}(n,K_{3})=\mathrm{ex}_{S_{m}}(n,K_{3})=m(n-m)
. However, for smaller values of
m
, the disjoint union of
5
-cycles is worse than
S_{m}
.
We leave it as an open problem to determine the behavior of
\mathrm{ex}_{m}(n,\mathcal{F})
, in particular
\mathrm{ex}_{m}(n,K_{3})
, as
m
increases from
1
to
\mathrm{sat}(n,\mathcal{F})
.

\bullet
The definition of the function
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,\mathcal{F})
can be extended even to the case when
\mathbb{P}
is not necessarily
\mathcal{F}
-free:
Given a graph
\mathbb{P}
on a vertex set
V
of size
n
and a family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
, what is the maximum number of edges in a graph
G
on
V
that contains
\mathbb{P}
as a subgraph and satisfies
\mathrm{N}(F,G)=\mathrm{N}(F,\mathbb{P})
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
? (For graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, the notation
\mathrm{N}(G_{1},G_{2})
denotes the number of copies of
G_{1}
in
G_{2}
.)
Here, we are interested in the function

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\max\left\{e(G)\colon\text{$G\subseteq\binom{V}{2}$, $\mathbb{P}\subseteq G$, and $\mathrm{N}(F,G)=\mathrm{N}(F,\mathbb{P})$ for every $F\in\mathcal{F}$}\right\}.

For the general function
\mathrm{ex}_{\mathbb{P}}(n,K_{3})
when
\mathbb{P}
is not necessarily triangle-free, Corollary
1.2
continues to hold, which then applies for the random graph models, where triangle-freeness is not required. A slight modification of our arguments can be used to prove Corollary
1.2
in this more general setting. We omit the details and leave this general problems to future investigation.

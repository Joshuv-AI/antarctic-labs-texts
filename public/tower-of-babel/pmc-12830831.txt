Hybrid wavelet transform, K-means singular value decomposition and Spatial memory guided cat swarm optimization technique for watermark embedding
Authors: Chirag Sharma, Arwinder Dhillon, Deepak Prashar, Arfat Ahmad Khan, Farman Ali, Ahmad Ali AlZubi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Integration and use of multimedia data has increased primarily due to advancements in technology and widespread utilization of internet services, especially with respect to multimedia audio, images, and videos. Such rapid progression in technology has highlighted the need for effective intellectual property and copyright protection. In response to these issues, this paper discusses the development of an improved watermarking technique for the embedding of watermarks in compressed videos. The newly developed technique uses Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) in conjunction with K-Means Singular Value Decomposition (K-SVD) to facilitate precision watermarking with marginal loss of quality. The optimization technique is revised to incorporate a Spatial Memory Guided Cat Swarm Optimization (SMGCSO) technique, which mimics the hunting behavior of cats to avoid local optima trap. Three core phases constitute SMGCSO: an exploration phase to seek optimal new positions, a spatial memory phase where best positions are stored, and an exploitation phase to refine the best solution, thus ensuring the global maximum is achieved. In terms of watermarking, Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Bit Error Rate (BER) determine performance. Watermarking, video processing attacks, and video copyright protection illustrate the practical utilization of the DWT-K-SVD-SMGCSO technique. There is an approximate 28% increase in PSNR and 45% decline in BER relative to the baseline DWT-K-SVD technique. Such advancements demonstrate superior resistance to video processing attacks and enhanced watermark robustness.

Introduction

The rapid development of digital technologies and multimedia technologies developed the processing of digital images and videos which created opportunities and challenges in the creation and distribution of visual content and the enforcement of intellectual property rights. The protection and the consolidation of digital content which is easily accessible and shareable is of the utmost importance1–3. Digital watermarking is a technique used to embed ownership watermarks within digital content and to prevent unauthorized use. These watermarks become invisible to users of the content but are easily accessible to owners and become an efficient verification method. The watermarking technique ensures the content maintains the desired quality while rendering the watermark invisible, and provides an uninterrupted view of the content. Over time, the watermark embedding techniques developed and are now classified into three categories: spatial domain, frequency domain, and hybrid approaches. Each of these developed techniques possesses unique advantages in the watermark strength, robustness, and computational efficiency in processing the content4–7.

Achieving the optimal balance between imperceptibility and robustness remains one of the major challenges in digital watermarking. A watermark deeply embedded in content is more resistant to signal processing attacks; however, the content is more likely to suffer perceptual degradation. On the other hand, watermarking at a perceptually shallower level keeps the visual quality intact, but it weakens ownership protection8–12. To bypass this challenge, the use of optimization techniques in the design of watermarking strategies focuses on attaining and merging high robustness and low perceptual quality distortion13,14.

This research represents the first-ever watermarking approach utilizing a hybrid Diskret Wellen-Transformation (DWT) and K-Singular-Value Decomposition (K-SVD) technique. K-DWT aids in extracting watermark information in the lower frequencies of the DWT. The sparse DWT coefficient technique, built into K-SVD, aids in selecting the ideal DWT coefficients for watermarking. The DWT technique divides the video frame into requisite sub-bands (LL3, LH3, HL3, and HH3). For the final embedment of the watermark, these sub-bands will be optimized. The primary optimization technique used is the SMGCSO, a technique inspired by the social behavior of cats. The optimization technique enhances the watermark trade-off imperceptibility and robustness balance, thus ensuring that the embedded watermark is attack- imperceptible and robust to users. SMGCSO-classed algorithms adhere to the three main primary defining phases of metaheuristic optimization algorithms: exploration, spatial, and exploitation. In the case of watermarking, the technique SMGCSO may be applied to improve the efficiency of the embedding phase, ensuring that the watermark remains robust and that the quality of the original video is preserved. During the exploration phase, cats disperse to different areas to locate and approach the prey (solution). In the spatial phase, they create a memory matrix of the best solutions they have found to avoid redundancy and repetition and to allow the exploration of different areas for a global optimum. In the exploitation phase, the velocity and position are updated toward the determined best solution, that is, the global best. This research attempts to solve problems that digital watermarking poses by developing a new approach that merges cutting-edge signal processing with techniques in optimization. This new approach seeks to improve the embedding of the watermark with compromises in computational efficiency and to solve problems around the robustness, security, and adaptability of digital watermarking. The SMGCSO Spatial Memory Guided Cat Swarm Optimization technique is used to guarantee that watermarks withstand most attacks and at the same time preserve the underlying image. With the use of Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and K-SVD, K-Singular Value Decomposition, the method demonstrated is more focused on video quality and resilience to attacks of the overlapping methods of signal processing for video. This framework enhances the adaptability of the proposed technique across different multimedia formats, providing a secure and efficient watermarking system.

Motivation

The unauthorized reproduction and redistribution of visual content has become even easier with the rapid advancement of multimedia sharing technology. Copyright protection is virtually impossible once the contents is public, so digital watermarks must be used for persistent ownership verification. A watermark that is deeply embedded and withstands compression, scaling, or noise attacks, however, will obviously degrade the quality of the video. Lightly embedded watermarks, on the other hand, are perceptually transparent, but common video processing operations easily destroy them. Most current techniques are spatial domain embedding, which is compression-attack weak, or compression and real-time systems are inefficient due to high computational requirements. There is, therefore, a need for a computationally efficient watermarking system with high resistance to attacks and minimal visual distortion while intelligently adapting to changing video characteristics. The goal of this research is to resolve embedded advanced transform-domain embedding (DWT-K-SVD) with metaheuristic optimization (SMGCSO), which will allow the watermarking system to be secure, scalable, and adaptive for today’s multimedia environments.

Literature review

Digital watermarking has been extensively explored to secure multimedia content, with research focusing on robustness, imperceptibility, and computational efficiency. Saharul et al.15 proposed a hybrid LWT-DCT approach for image watermarking, introducing an adaptive region selection algorithm that leveraged visual and audio entropy for embedding. The gain factor was optimized using a swarm intelligence algorithm, and a non-geometric distortion characteristic was developed to correct images affected by preprocessing attacks. The proposed method achieved a PSNR value of 48 dB, demonstrating good imperceptibility, although it lacked advanced optimization for larger or compressed datasets. Suresh et al.16 introduced Exponential Fractional-Cat Swarm Optimization (Exponential Fractional-CSO) for video steganography. Important frames were selected based on similarity measures, and the lifting wavelet transform was applied to hide data. This method achieved a PSNR of 89.70 dB, indicating effective data concealment, but it was limited in frame-level optimization and adaptability to dynamic video scenes. Similar kind of work has been done by authors on cat optimization17. M. Gupta et al.18 combined cuckoo search optimization with probabilistic neural networks for image watermarking. The watermark was first embedded using DCT, and cuckoo search identified optimal embedding locations, while the probabilistic neural network established correlations between images and watermark coefficients. The method achieved a PSNR of 65 dB, showing improved correlation modeling, though it was computationally intensive for large datasets. Another authors19 provided review of techniques based on cuckoo search. Similarly, Waleed et al.20 proposed a firefly-optimized DWT-SVD framework, embedding singular values with binary codes and optimizing with a firefly algorithm integrated with the Photinus algorithm. Experiments on various images showed a PSNR of 79.28 dB, outperforming existing methods, but performance relied heavily on parameter tuning and may degrade under noisy or compressed conditions. Shamma et al.21 combined Blowfish encryption, DWT, and the Modified Dragonfly Optimization Algorithm (MDFOA) to generate high-frequency features for secure data transmission. This method achieved superior PSNR values but increased computational complexity, limiting real-time application. Also, authors in22 worked on image fusion based on DWT. Ponnisathya et al.23 proposed a hybrid DWT-PCA-ABC approach for frame-based image watermarking. Images were divided into frames, redundant frames removed, and important frames selected using entropy; PCA reduced dimensionality, and ABC optimized embedding. Results showed higher PSNR and fewer false positives, though PCA could reduce subtle image details. Amine et al.24 employed Quaternary Charlier Moments (QCM) with DWT and SVD, optimized via a modified chaotic map, achieving a PSNR of 82 dB, with reduced computational load, although performance depended on QCM parameter tuning. Chandan Kumar et al.26 proposed DWT-DCT-SPIHT with dual watermarking and encryption; experiments showed a PSNR of 42.52 dB, highlighting strong security but lower imperceptibility. Abed et al.26 applied DTCWT with PSO for embedding binary logos, achieving a PSNR of 80.50 dB, though it remained computationally intensive.

Zhang et al.27 discussed dual-embedded watermarking combining SVD, DWT, and DCT with PSO-based optimization, encrypting watermarks via the Generalized Arnold Transform and embedding in high- and low-frequency regions. Results demonstrated high security, watermark capacity, robustness, and invisibility, though PSO optimization is prone to local optima. Wang et al.28 combined DWT, Arnold transform, SVD, Hessenberg Matrix Decomposition, PSO, and logistic mapping, adaptively embedding watermarks into multi-size host images with good PSNR and NC under attacks. However, optimization methods often get trapped in local optima, highlighting the need for more advanced strategies. A survey on PSO based techniques has been given by authors29. Sharma30 proposed GBT-SVD to improve frame selection and direct watermark embedding, offering simplicity and effectiveness for both static and dynamic frames. For medical image watermarking, Divyanshu et al.31 developed a dual watermarking scheme using AES encryption and BRISK/MinEigen features for patient and watermark authentication. The approach combined LWT with randomized SVD (RSVD), achieving robustness and imperceptibility verified via PSNR, NCC, SSIM, KLD, and JSD metrics. In a further study32, Henon maps, Schur decomposition, redundant DWT, and RSVD were applied, with patient Aadhar cards secured via MAC address and MRI/CT fusion images embedded using DT-CWT. The approach achieved gains of 25.98% in imperceptibility and 14.01% in robustness. Rajpal et al.33 proposed a novel fuzzy frame selection technique by applying watermarking to optimally selected frames with minimal frame selection. Frame selection served as the preliminary step prior to the application of watermarking. A similar set of videos has been used in both the proposed work and the study conducted by Rajpal et al.33. The dataset utilized by Rajpal et al.33 is available as specified in the data availability statement.

Critical section

Across these studies, hybrid transform methods combined with optimization algorithms consistently show superior performance in robustness and imperceptibility. However, many methods either lack adaptability to compressed or fast-motion videos, or suffer from local optima in optimization algorithms. Dual watermarking approaches increase security but often at the expense of computational complexity. The current study addresses these gaps by introducing a DWT-K-SVD-SMGCSO hybrid framework, integrating adaptive frame selection, encrypted watermark embedding, and spatial memory guided optimization, providing higher PSNR, SSIM, NC, and lower BER while remaining applicable to compressed and dynamic video sequences.

Proposed methodology

In this research, a hybrid watermarking method for embedding watermarks into the compressed video has been proposed that utilizes Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and K-Means Singular Value Decomposition (K-SVD), which is optimized using a Spatial Memory Guided Cat Swarm Optimization (SMGCSO). The methodology involves the following significant stages: Frame Selection, Watermark Embedding, and finally Optimization using SMGCSO. The stages are explained in the following section.

Frame selection

During the first stage of the proposed methodology, we define the optimal number of video frames and compute the total number of frames in the video, a first principle of video preprocessing. One of the pressing issues that needs to be resolved is determining which of the available frames to select for watermark embedding, which, in this research, is done for the first time using scene change detection techniques for real-time processing. This ensures that the selected frames are not duplicates and have substantive meaningful variations in content, which minimizes superfluous data. In the processing of compressed videos, the direct influence of selected frames on the video bit rate makes this watermarking task especially challenging. The processing and storage costs, as well as the video file size, increase dramatically with the time and watermarking of the individual frames. Therefore, it is vital to choose key frames that will allow for embedding watermarks without a meaningful loss in video quality or increase in video size. In choosing frames, the focus is on the temporal structure of the video, which involves a discrimination mark between dynamic and inert frames. Dynamic frames comprise scene transitions or sizable motion, while inert frames exhibit a degree of stasis. Varying sequences of dynamic and inert frames exist within videos, and choosing key frames within dynamic segments guarantees that watermarks are placed in areas that best withstand attacks or deterioration. One of the most common issues stems from the differing degrees of alterations across the various sections of a video. To address this, adaptive frame selection methods are employed, ensuring that sections with higher variations (more dynamic changes) have more frames selected, while sections with minimal variations have fewer frames. This adaptive selection balances the trade-off between embedding too few or too many frames, preserving both video quality and security34,35.

Optimization using Spatial memory guided Cat swarm optimization (SMGCSO)

The selected frames are optimized using the Spatial Memory Guided Cat Swarm Optimization (SMGCSO), an enhancement of the basic Cat Swarm Optimization (CSO)37, which models the movement of cats while searching for prey (solution). The method operates in exploration (searching) and exploitation (uncovering) phases. Exploration involves carefully examining all areas of the search space, while exploitation focuses on moving toward promising solutions.

A key limitation of traditional CSO is that cats often get stuck in local optima, meaning they do not explore the complete search space and may return a solution based on the first encountered best solution. To address this, SMGCSO introduces spatial memory, enabling cats to remember all paths taken during the search. This ensures the identification of the global best solution while avoiding local optima. The process begins with the initialization of cats, where each cat represents a candidate solution. Watermarking procedure.

The proposed method in Fig. 2 determines embedding of watermark on selected frames. Embedding a watermark in selected frames increases the output video’s file size while keeping the watermark concealed from unauthorized users. The Frame Selection (FS) algorithm identifies variations within video scenes. Since videos contain numerous frames with frequent changes, the system detects scene changes by distinguishing dissimilar and similar sequences. By comparing adjacent frames, the algorithm calculates frame differences (ADk). Significant differences classify frames into separate groups. When the ADk value exceeds the threshold, the first frame of the next group is selected for watermarking. Once the frames are chosen, the watermark is embedded in a limited number of them. The watermark is encrypted using a hyperchaotic algorithm before embedding. The embedding factor, set at 0.17, controls the strength of watermark integration. Maintaining the quality of the watermarked frame is crucial, with Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) used as the quality metric. Updating the embedding factor manually is time-consuming due to its static nature. To address this, SMGCSO optimization is applied. The optimization generates multiple solutions by evaluating PSNR in each iteration and selects the value yielding the highest PSNR. SMGCSO runs for a fixed number of iterations, adjusting the embedding factor each time. If a new value improves the watermarked frame quality, it is retained; otherwise, the previous best value is kept. Discussions

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed DWT-K-SVD and optimized DWT-K-SVD-SMGCSO frameworks for video watermarking. Frame selection proved critical in determining the significant frames for embedding watermarks. Videos with higher dynamic changes, such as Pure_Storage, allowed for greater watermark capacity and improved resistance to tampering. Conversely, videos with low motion, such as Akiyo, offered fewer embedding opportunities due to low ADk values, highlighting the importance of adaptive frame selection in practical applications.

For individual videos, the baseline DWT-K-SVD method produced satisfactory results. For instance, video 1 achieved a PSNR of 38.23 dB, SSIM of 0.99996, NC of 0.999872, and BER of 0.0273, indicating high-quality watermark embedding even without optimization. Incorporation of SMGCSO optimization significantly improved performance, with PSNR values increasing to 67.6, 62.2, 65.7, 64.9, and 64.3 dB for videos 1–5, respectively. The SSIM and NC metrics remained near unity, confirming minimal perceptual deterioration, while BER values decreased, indicating enhanced robustness against attacks. These results reaffirm that the hybrid framework effectively balances imperceptibility and watermark fidelity.

Due to multiple iterations of SMGCSO, optimization time increased; however, the additional computation is justified by the improved fitness and watermark quality. The line and bar plots further illustrate how the optimized method surpasses the baseline, providing stronger, more resilient watermarking without sacrificing perceptual quality. When compared with existing state-of-the-art techniques, such as LWT-DCT15, Exponential Fractional-CSO16, and hybrid DWT-SVD-based methods23–27, the proposed DWT-K-SVD-SMGCSO demonstrates superior PSNR and BER values, reflecting better visual quality and attack resistance. Traditional CSO or PSO-based optimization methods are prone to getting trapped in local optima, which limits the robustness of watermark embedding. In contrast, the spatial memory mechanism of SMGCSO ensures global exploration, allowing for more reliable selection of embedding positions and improved resistance to attacks.

In summary, the hybrid framework incorporates adaptive frame selection, embedding encrypted watermarks, and SMGCSO optimization, serving as a complete service for video watermarking. With high imperceptibility, high robustness, and high capacity, the watermarking is designed for practical real- world applications dealing with the protection of intellectual property and security of information. From the results of the experiments, the optimized framework is not only better than the baseline, but also performs well relative to current techniques while overcoming the most relevant issues concerning the digital video watermarking process.

Conclusion

With regard to multimedia processing, assuring security and high quality watermarking is one of the greatest challenges into the new areas of study proposed. This study involves frame selection criteria, watermark embedding methodologies, and the subsequent quality evaluation. This study attempts to integrate a cryptographic watermark to ensure quality is not sacrificed. Since watermarking multimedia data can result in a deterioration of the video, consideration of quality frame selection is necessary. The frame selection threshold was set through the use of fluctuation analysis across varying video types, particularly those with rapid scene shifts but minimal frame differences, and those with prolonged scenes and rapid transitions. The proposed method uses a hybrid model of DWT-K-SVD which by - design can be fine tuned to secure high PSNR. The level of watermarks embedded is further enhanced by using Spatial Memory Guided Cat Swarm Optimization (SMGCSO) to optimize the embedding factor. The combination of DWT-K-SVD-SMGCSO in one technique is a milestone in video watermarking. This method to video watermarking uses PSNR, NC, SSIM, and, BER as metrics to gauge performance. The comparative study shows that the enhanced DWT-K-SVD-SMGCSO surpasses the original DWT-K-SVD paradigm in watermark quality and consistency of watermark to video surpasses. Ultimately, the proposed method’s advantages with respect to frame selection efficiency and robustness, coupled with higher PSNR, NC, SSIM, and BER, prove its performance superiority against the prior ones. Nonetheless, the proposed scheme continues to be afflicted by some limitations. For one, rapidly and unevenly animated videos may cause frame se- lection algorithms to miss some subtle changes. For another, the single watermark solution may, especially for high-security contexts, preclude the possibility of applying multi-layered authentication, thereby increasing its utility. Finally, the robustness against attack combinations, especially with adaptive attacks comprising compression and other geometric distortions, remains inadequately explored. Future work may focus on computationally less intensive methods through the use of parallel processing and GPU acceleration. For increased security and multi-level authentication, the use of multiple embedded watermarks can be utilized. Moreover, the SMGCSO can be used in real-time video streaming, where the requirements for speed and robustness are paramount. In terms of the method’s resilience, it would also be important to test against a wider variety of geometric attacks in signal processing along with other attacks. Moreover, incorporating other perceptual quality metrics along with the traditional objective metrics would be important. This would ensure a more complete assessment of the imperceptibility.

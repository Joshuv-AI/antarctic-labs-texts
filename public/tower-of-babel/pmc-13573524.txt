MFR, SVR and ASVR as promising biomarkers in overweight and obese population with obstructive sleep apnea syndrome: insights from an observational study
Authors: Hongbin Zhou, Weiyan Gu, Jia Zheng, Junxiao Zhou, Zhewen Chen, Ying Chen, Yushan Wang, Sa Ye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObesity is an important risk factor of obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSAS), but not all obese people can develop OSAS. The role of novel human body composition indices—muscle-to-fat ratio (MFR), skeletal muscle mass-to-visceral fat area ratio (SVR) and appendicular skeletal muscle mass-to-visceral fat area ratio (ASVR)—in OSAS remains largely unknown. The aim of this study is to investigate the associations of these indices to OSAS in overweight and obese adults.MethodsOverweight or obese patients with complete human body composition and polysomnography data were included in this study. The data of physical examination, Epworth sleepiness scale and demographic information were retrospectively collected. Subjects were divided into two groups, named non-OSAS and OSAS. MFR, SVR and ASVR were compared between two groups. Logistic regression analysis, restricted cubic spline analysis and mediation analysis were conducted to evaluate the relationship between these indices and OSAS. Moreover, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were drawn to evaluate these indices in OSAS diagnosis.ResultsA total of 192 overweight or obese patients were enrolled in this study, including 122 OSAS patients. Compared with non-OSAS patients, OSAS patients were older and exhibited higher frequency of male, smoking history and more comorbidities. MFR, SVR and ASVR were higher in OSAS group, especially in male OSAS patients. However, these indices were not independent risk factors to OSAS. Mediation analysis presented these indices might mediate the relationship between age and OSAS, but this effect was affected by sex. ROC curves showed these indices had high sensitivity and specificity in OSAS diagnosis in subjects with less symptoms.ConclusionMFR, SVR and ASVR increased in male OSAS patients in overweight and obese adults. These indices might contribute to early recognizing OSAS patients with less symptoms, especially in primary care settings.Keywords: OSAS, MFR, SVR, ASVR

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSAS) is a common sleep disorder with a high prevalence, ranged from 9% to 38% in general [1]. The manifestations of this disease include snore, apnoea and suffocating to wake up, which is accompanied by sleepiness during daytime [2]. The patients suffering from OSAS often have comorbidities, such as cardiovascular diseases, metabolic syndrome and psychiatric disorders [3].

Obesity, a component of metabolic syndrome, is widely accepted as a key risk factor of OSAS. Compared with normal weight adults, the prevalence of OSAS in overweight and obese people increased two- to fivefolds [4]. Obviously, not all the obese patients have OSAS. So, it is valuable to seek out the characteristics of OSAS patients in obese population.

As is known to all, obesity is defined by body mass index (BMI), the ratio of body weight to the square of height. This is a simple and practical index. However, this index cannot reflex body composition. Specially, it is not able to discriminate fat mass from lean mass by BMI. Moreover, BMI cannot reflex distribution of fat tissue, either [5]. A part of obese patients, whose adipose tissue are distributed around viscera named central obesity, have high risk of cardiovascular diseases and metabolic syndrome. Conversely, the patients with peripheral obesity, whose adipose tissue are distributed in subcutaneous region, are considered not to be associated with these diseases.

Given the importance of body composition and adipose distribution in human health and disease, researchers have put forward a series of novel indices. Muscle-to-fat ratio (MFR), which was first put forward by Kim et al. was defined as the ratio of appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASM) to visceral fat area (VFA) [6]. However, the same ratio was termed as skeletal muscle mass (SMM) to visceral fat area ratio (SVR) in other studies [7,8]. MFR was also designated as the ratio of total body muscle mass to total fat mass or the ratio of ASM to total fat mass in recent studies [9,10]. Moreover, SMM/VFA ratio, a similar combined index, was also available in a study reported by Kim et al. [11]. The measurements of these indices are dependent on analysis of human body compositions, which is commonly applied in evaluation of nutrition status and diagnosis of sarcopenia. However, the nomenclature of these indices was not uniform. Previous studies discussed the association between body composition and OSAS, and obtained different results [11–14]. In this study, we utilized the tool of human body composition analysis and investigated the associations of different indices reflecting human body composition to OSAS in overweight and obese adults.

Methods

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital (Ethics Approval Number: 2025(158)), which was in accordance with the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. Informed consent was exempted, due to the retrospectively collection of data from subjects.

Study subjects

The patients who sought medical advice due to overweight or obese in nutrition clinic were objective subjects (from August 2023 to April 2025). The subjects accepted routine physical examination and were recommended to perform additional measurements to evaluate potential comorbidities. Epworth sleepiness scale (ESS) was performed and polysomnography (PSG) was used to determine if an individual developed OSAS. Briefly, the patient wore a home sleep test equipment (Alice NightOne) overnight, which recorded breath, apnoea, oxygen saturation and other information during sleep. The information stored in the equipment were read and analysed by computer program in the next day. The results were presented that if the patient developed OSAS or hypoxaemia and the severity of OSAS or hypoxaemia. Human body composition measurements were based on bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) by body composition analyzer (InBody 770). All the subjects should fast, avoid exercise and stand for five minutes before measurement. The measurement was usually conducted in the morning. The mass and proportion of human body components were obtained via this equipment.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) age ≥18 years old; (2) BMI ≥24 kg/m2; (3) performed PSG and human body composition analysis.

Exclusion criteria: (1) age <18 years old; (2) lack of data of PSG or human body composition.

Diagnosis of overweight/obese and OSAS

According to guideline for diagnosis and treatment of obesity (2024 edition) [15], overweight is defined as BMI ≥24 and <28 kg/m2; obesity is defined as BMI ≥28 kg/m2.

According to guideline for diagnosis and treatment of obstructive sleep apnoea–hypopnoea syndrome (2011 edition) [16], apnea–hypopnea index (AHI), detected by PSG, was defined as total numbers of apnoea and hypopnoea divided by total sleep time. Hypopnoea was defined as one of the following events: (1) airflow decreased by 30% and SaO2 decreased by 4% compared with baseline, which continued for at least 10 s and (2) airflow decreased by 50% and SaO2 decreased by 3% compared with baseline, which continued for at least 10 s. Arousal was not required to score a hypopnoea. AHI is equal to or more than five times per hour (≥5/h) with evident symptoms (ESS score ≥9) or asymptomatic patients (ESS score <9) whose AHI ≥10/h can be diagnosed as OSAS. If subjects whose ESS <9 and AHI values were between 5 and 10 had medical history and received continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) treatment, they would be also diagnosed as OSAS. Moreover, mild OSAS is defined as AHI ≥5 and <15/h; moderate OSAS is defined as AHI ≥15 and <30/h; severe OSAS is defined as AHI ≥30/h.

Measurements of MFR, SVR and ASVR

MFR, SVR and appendicular skeletal muscle mass-to-visceral fat area ratio (ASVR) are calculated via the data from human body composition analysis. MFR is the ratio of total muscle mass (kg) to total fat mass (kg); SVR is the ratio of SMM (kg) to VFA (dm2); ASVR is the ratio of ASM (kg) to VFA (dm2).

Receiver operating characteristic of MFR, SVR and ASVR in OSAS diagnosis

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of MFR, SVR and ASVR were drawn to evaluate the diagnostic value of OSAS. Area under curve (AUC), sensitivity and specificity of these indices were calculated in overall subjects and subjects with or without evident symptoms. Cut-off points were selected according to best accuracy.

Statistical analysis

The data between August 2023 to April 2025 was analysed on October 2025. Means and standard deviations (SDs) were used to represent normally distributed data, while non-normally distributed data were descriptive by medians and quartiles. Counts and proportions were used to describe categorical variables. Spearman correlation analysis was applied to evaluate the relationship between AHI and human body indices. Student t-test or the nonparametric Mann–Whitney U test was utilized to distinguish any differences between the two groups, while analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Sidak post hoc test was performed for multiple groups comparisons. The discrepancies in categorical variables were compared through the chi-square test. Participants were categorized into three groups according to MFR, SVR and ASVR levels: the low level, middle level and high level. Logistic regression analysis and restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis were applied to explore the relationship between MFR, SVR and ASVR levels and OSAS. To estimate the effect size of MFR, SVR and ASVR scaled by standardized per SD, the data of three indices was analysed after z-score normalization. In RCS, three or four knots were selected according to Bayesian information criterion values. The median of each index was set as reference values. Besides, mediation analysis was performed to investigate the roles of MFR, SVR and ASVR in risk factors—OSAS association. To assess the robustness of mediation effect model to potential unmeasured confounding, a sensitivity analysis using the E-value was conducted. Following the approach of previous literatures [17,18], the E-value quantifies the minimum strength of association, on the risk ratio scale, that an unmeasured confounder would need to have with both the exposure and the outcome to fully explain the observed exposure–outcome estimate, conditional on the measured covariates. All the analyses were executed by GraphPad Prism 9.0, R version 4.4.0, along with Zstats 1.0 (www.zstats.net). P < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of study subjects

A total of 256 overweight or obese patients sought advice due to their body weight. Among them, 12 patients were under 18 years old. 23 patients lacked human body composition data, and 29 patients rejected PSG examination. As a result, 192 participants were included in our study (Figure 1).

Flow chart of patient screening.

The characteristics of these 192 participants are listed in Table 1. There were 123 male subjects, and 26 subjects with smoking history. A part of subjects had at least one kind of underlying diseases, including hypertension (47/192), diabetes mellitus (17/192), hyperlipidaemia (68/192) and fatty liver (114/192). One hundred and twenty-two patients were diagnosed as OSAS by PSG, and most of them were male (95/123). There were six patients formerly diagnosed as OSAS and received CPAP therapy. Among them, four patients had low ESS (<9) and AHI (5–10/h), while other two patients had either high ESS (≥9) or high AHI (≥10/h). Compared with non-OSAS patients, OSAS patients exhibited higher frequency of smoking history, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia and fatty liver. In addition, OSAS patients were older, had larger neck circumference and higher ESS than non-OSAS patients. However, BMI, WHR and proportion of diabetes mellitus were similar in these two groups (Table 1).

The associations of human body composition indices to OSAS

MFR, SVR and ASVR were compared between non-OSAS patients and OSAS patients. As a result, all the three indices were significantly higher in OSAS patients than those in non-OSAS patients (MFR 1.57 ± 0.46 vs. 1.85 ± 0.54; SVR 20.79 ± 7.50 vs. 24.96 ± 8.83; ASVR 15.80 ± 5.77 vs. 18.94 ± 6.81). The values of MFR, SVR and ASVR were much higher in male OSAS patients than those in non-OSAS subjects, while the difference was not seen in female subjects. Next, MFR, SVR and ASVR were further analysed between non-OSAS patients and OSAS patients with different classifications of severity. MFR, SVR and ASVR levels were much higher in both mild OSAS and severe OSAS patients than those in non-OSAS patients. However, SVR level, but not MFR and ASVR levels, was higher in moderate OSAS patients, compared with non-OSAS patients (Figure 2).

Moreover, all the subjects were divided into three groups according to the values of MFR, SVR and ASVR, respectively. Compared with subjects with low levels of MFR, the risk of OSAS was higher in subjects with middle levels of MFR [OR 3.86 (1.82–8.17)] and high levels of MFR [OR 3.29 (1.57–6.86)]. Similarly, compared with subjects with low levels of SVR, the risk of OSAS was higher in subjects with middle levels of SVR [OR 4.31 (2.00–9.31)] and high levels of SVR [OR 2.47 (1.21– 5.06)]. Moreover, higher risk of OSAS was also observed in subjects with middle levels of ASVR [OR 2.94 (1.40–6.17)] and high levels of ASVR [OR 2.34 (1.14–4.82)]. After sex adjustment, the risks of OSAS decreased in subjects with high levels of MFR [OR 1.45 (0.61–3.44)], SVR [OR 0.84 (0.34–2.10)] and ASVR [OR 0.82 (0.33–2.04)], which were similar to those in subjects with low levels of MFR, SVR and ASVR. On the other hand, the risks of OSAS remained at high level in subjects with middle levels of MFR [OR 2.67 (1.19–5.99)] and SVR [OR 2.51 (1.07–5.86)], but not in subjects with middle levels of ASVR [OR 1.57 (0.68–3.63)] (Table 2).

Analysis of risk factors to OSAS

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis were applied to seek out risk factors of OSAS. Univariate logistic regression analysis suggested that subjects with male sex, older age, smoking behaviour, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, fatty liver, high levels of MFR, SVR and ASVR were related to high risk of OSAS. After z-score normalization, the effect of MFR, SVR and ASVR was similar to that calculated by original data. However, multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that only sex and age were the independent risk factors of OSAS (Table 3). In addition, there were multicollinearity among these indices (Supplementary Table 1).

Non-linear relationship between OSAS and human body composition indices

As multivariate logistic regression indicated MFR, SVR and ASVR were not independent risk factors of OSAS, then we wondered if non-linear relationship existed between OSAS and these indices. RCS analysis was applied to analyse the potential non-linear relationship. The medians of MFR, SVR and ASVR were 1.70, 22.25 and 16.92, respectively, which were set as reference values. As a result, there were no evident non-linear relationships between MFR and OSAS, regardless of sex adjustment. Nevertheless, RCS showed non-linear relationships between the other two indices and OSAS, which still existed after sex adjustment (Figure 3).

The role of MFR, SVR and ASVR as mediation factors

To further explore the role of MFR, SVR and ASVR in OSAS, mediation analysis was carried out. We observed that MFR significantly mediated the relationship between age and OSAS. SVR and ASVR also presented similar effects. About 22.49%, 11.99% and 11.70% of the total effect of age on OSAS were mediated by MFR, SVR and ASVR, respectively. However, no mediation effect of MFR, SVR and ASVR were observed after sex adjustment (Figure 4).

For other possible risk factors, ASVR, but not MFR and SVR, was found to be a significant mediator in the relationship between fatty liver and OSAS. ASVR provided about 16.12% of the total effect of fatty liver on OSAS, but this effect also disappeared after sex adjustment. In addition, MFR, SVR and ASVR did not mediate the relationship between smoking and OSAS, regardless of sex adjustment (Table 4).

The role of MFR, SVR and ASVR in OSAS diagnosis

ROC curve was applied to evaluate the diagnostic value of MFR, SVR and ASVR. The AUC of these indices was about 0.65 in general. However, the AUC of these indices increased significantly in subjects with relatively less symptoms (ESS < 9). The sensitivity of these indices ranged 0.73–0.78, and specificity reached 0.81–0.84 in OSAS diagnosis. As for subjects with evident symptoms, both sensitivity and specificity were at low level (Table 5, Figure 5).

Discussion

Obesity is a major public-health concern associated with numerous physiological and psychological comorbidities. The prevalence of OSAS, a kind of respiratory and sleep disturbances, has progressively increased following the similar tendency of obesity in the recent years [1,2,19]. According to Wisconsin Sleep Cohort Study, about 50% of OSAS patients were obese. The proportion of OSAS patients was much higher in obese population than that in subjects with normal body weight [4]. The prevalence of OSAS reached 60% in this study, which was higher than many other studies. The main reason to this was that all the subjects were overweight or obese in our study. This result confirmed the idea again that obesity was a significant risk factor of OSAS.

In this study, age is a major risk factor to OSAS. In fact, most subjects in our study were youngsters and middle-aged persons. However, an evident trend could be observed that the rising prevalence of OSAS was accompanied by increasing age, which was in accordance with other studies [20–25]. Another important risk factor is sex. Compared with female subjects, there was a much higher prevalence of this disease in male. This phenomenon commonly existed in most studies [21,23–27]. Although smoking is considered as a key risk factor to many respiratory diseases, the role of smoking in the pathogenesis of OSAS has not been well elucidated. Previous study reported that smoking increased the risk of snoring [28]. Recently, a cross-sectional study indicated higher proportions of smokers in OSAS population than those in non-OSAS population [29]. In our study, more smokers were also found in OSAS group. Nevertheless, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed negative result, which meant smoking was not the independent risk factor of OSAS in this study.

There are many comorbidities of OSAS, including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidaemia and fatty liver. Compared with non-OSAS group, the prevalences of these diseases were much higher in OSAS group, except diabetes mellitus. However, no significant effects of these factors in this study were observed via multivariate logistic regression analysis. So, it was lack of evidence that these diseases could be considered as risk factors of OSAS. Sarcopenia is a degenerative muscle disease, which has been drawn much attention in recent years. Previous studies indicated lower MFR predicted greater risk and severity [12–14,30]. However, we found OSAS patients had higher MFR, SVR and ASVR, compared with non-OSAS ones. This phenomenon might be due to some reasons. Firstly, the subjects in some previous studies were mostly middle-agers, with a range of 50–60 years. While the subjects involved in our study were quite younger, whose mean age was about 35 years. The great difference in age among these studies might lead to different results. Secondary, as an aging-related disease, the incidence of sarcopenia is much lower in younger population. In fact, almost no subjects in our study met the criteria of sarcopenia, so the sarcopenic-obesity paradigm was not an appropriate pattern in present study. Lastly, sex difference could not be overlooked. Higher values of MFR, SVR and ASVR were observed only in male OSAS patients. There were no significant differences of MFR, SVR and ASVR values between non-OSAS and OSAS in female subjects. These results were similar to those reported by Kim et al. [13]. On the other hand, the risks of OSAS were higher in subjects with middle and high levels of MFR, SVR and ASVR. However, the high risks of OSAS only remained in subjects with middle levels of MFR and SVR after sex adjustment. The relationship between these indices and OSAS seemed not monotonic, but to be an inverted-U pattern, which was also evidenced by RCS analysis.

Not all individuals with obesity develop OSAS. In fact, the BMI levels between non-OSAS group and OSAS group were similar in our study. It was necessary to seek other tools to discriminate OSAS patients from obese population. Human body composition analyzer provides information of mass and distribution of various kinds of tissues, such as bone, muscle and adipose, which is widely used in nutrition status evaluation. It should be noted that there are several tools to evaluate muscle and visceral fat, including BIA, dual energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) and CT or MRI scanning. DEXA and CT can provide precise results but have defects of high expense and potential injury of radiation. Moreover, a group of researchers pointed out that CT based imaging might overestimate muscle function, particularly in obese OSAS patients in a recent study [31]. On the contrary, BIA is a convenient tool without radiation, which has been recommended in Guideline for adult weight management in China (2025). However, the results could be affected by some factors such as fasting status, hydration and recent exercise. In our study, we chose InBody 770 to evaluate muscle and fat tissue for its convenience and safety. As a result, MFR, SVR and ASVR were elevated in OSAS group. Kurinami et al. reported MFR was significantly and independently associated with OSAS in type 2 diabetes mellitus patients [12]. Furthermore, MFR was the most suitable measure for screening OSAS in non-obese patients whose BMI is lower than 27.5 kg/m2 [12]. We applied this index in another population and obtained positive result, but MFR was not independently associated with OSAS in our study. SVR and its reciprocal, VSR were applied in cardiovascular diseases [32], hepatic diseases [8,33], metabolic dysfunction [34] and psychological conditions [7,35]. ASVR was usually mixed with SVR in previous studies, because total SMM was always replaced by appendiceal SMM when calculating this index. To better evaluate the effect of distribution and proportion of skeletal muscle and adipose, we distinguished the two indices in the present study. So far as we have known, this is the first study that investigates the role of three indices simultaneously in overweight and obese OSAS.

As the existence of large variance inflation factor and condition index among the three indices (data shown in Supplementary Table), multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that MFR, SVR and ASVR were not independent risk factors of OSAS. Nevertheless, it was possible that these indices might be potential mediation factors. Primary mediation analysis showed they mediated the relationship between age and OSAS, while mediation effect was not evident after sex adjustment. In addition, sensitivity analysis showed E-value was close to 1, indicating the mediation effect model was not robust enough. The precise mechanistic roles of MFR, SVR and ASVR in the pathogenesis of OSAS require further investigation. It worth pointing out that the bidirectional relationship between OSAS and metabolic dysfunction has been highlighted in recent literatures [5,30]. In this study, we put emphasis on the effect of MFR, SVR and ASVR on OSAS, while the possibility of reverse causation should also be taken into account. However, no significant difference of these indices was observed among three OSAS groups with different degrees of severity, which seemed not confirm the reverse causation in our study. Further studies are necessary to explore the bidirectional relationship between OSAS and muscle metabolism.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, sex confounding or single-centre referral might have great effect on primary finding. Secondly, compared to other studies, relatively small sample size was another influencing factor. Both obesity and OSAS are common diseases, the quantity of subjects might affect the robustness of the results. Thirdly, there were a variety of factor affecting the pathogenesis of OSAS, including figure, upper airway structure, usage of psychoactive drugs and physiological status. It should be noted that the use of home sleep test equipment might underestimate AHI and lead to missing diagnosis of some mild OSAS patients. In addition, the use of CPAP can reduce the symptoms of OSAS and AHI values. In this study, only six patients underwent CPAP treatment, CPAP status had minor effect on the final results. However, it is necessary to consider the influencing factors comprehensively in future study. Fourth, although BIA is a convenient tool, DEXA or CT can provide precise results, which should be adopted as calibration reference. Last but not least, OSAS is a chronic disease, which needed a long-term observation. This is a retrospective study lacking dynamic follow-up, and a longitudinal cohort study is necessary to confirm our finding in the future.

Conclusion

In summary, MFR, SVR and ASVR increased mainly in male OSAS patients in overweight and obese adults. Moreover, mediation analysis indicated these indices might mediate the relationship between age and OSAS, in which sex acted as a confounding factor. Although these indices had limited values in OSAS diagnosis in general, they presented much higher value in the subjects with relative less symptoms. The high specificity of these indices will contribute to early recognizing OSAS patients with less symptoms precisely, especially in primary care settings.

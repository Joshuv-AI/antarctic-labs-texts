Poster: Developing an O-RAN Security Test Lab
Sotiris Michaelides, David Rupprecht, Katharina Khols

Abstract
oran ( oran ) is a new architectural approach, having been proposed only a few years ago, and it is an expansion of the current ngran ( ngran ) of 5G. oran aims to break this closed RAN market that is controlled by a handful of vendors, by implementing open interfaces between the different ran ( ran ) components, and by introducing modern technologies to the ran like machine learning, virtualization, and disaggregation. However, the architectural design of ORAN was recently causing concerns and debates about its security, which is considered one of its major drawbacks. Several theoretical risk analyses related to oran have been conducted, but to the best of our knowledge, not even a single practical one has been performed yet. In this poster, we discuss and propose a way for a minimal, future-proof deployment of an oran 5G network, able to accommodate various hands-on security analyses for its different elements.



Keywords:
Wireless Networks, Open Ran, O-RAN, Security, 5G, 6G

Introduction

The cellular infrastructure comprises three major components: the

However, the new concepts of ORAN result in new types of attacks not previously seen in RAN. Three new risk areas associated with
oran
are architecture openness, cloud and virtualization, and machine learning. These attack vectors are being added to the current 5G architectural threats that are already linked to the

While a theoretical identify potential vulnerabilities and risks in a system before they can be exploited by attackers.
A practical analyses allows for evaluating the feasibility and the consequences of each attack in a real-world environment. To the best of our knowledge, no practical security analysis of the ORAN architecture exists. This poster proposes the deployment of a basic
oran

DEPLOYING THE TEST LAB

A. Requirements

According to Köpsell et. al. virtualization,
ml
, and architectural openness are the new threat areas related to
oran

[
2
]
. Therefore we decided that we limit our practical security analysis to the architectural openness and to
ml
parts of ORAN.

For architectural openness, the open-front-haul interface between the
odu
(
odu
) and
oru
(
oru
), is the most promising interface to practically analyze. This interface uses the
ecpri
(
ecpri
) protocol and the new 7.2x split, defined by the O-RAN alliance. Possible attacks that can exploit the lack of integrity protection for
up
(
up
) data
[
6
]
, and attacks aiming to de-synchronise the
odu
and
oru
), have been identified. Their impact can be catastrophic, as de-synchronised devices will affect the availability of the system, and missing integrity protection enables the modification of
up
data. While some of those attacks, can be also conducted over the air, the possibility to conduct them in the infrastructure allows scalability to more end devices. Therefore, a practical evaluation is needed for estimating the efforts connected to those attacks.

For the
ml

nrt
(
nrt
), running several xAPPS that are utilizing
ml

ran
functions, is required. The aim is to manipulate the RAN by executing the xAPPS with ill-trained models and monitoring the consequences (potential disruption of operation and malfunction of services).

3
Challenges

A. Incomplete and Undocumented Software

The main challenge we faced during the deployment of our setup, ware the incomplete and undocumented implementation of different solutions offered by SW vendors. Inspecting the code, was very often necessary, to understand the different implemented protocols, or the deployment configurations. Also the current implementation of the different components can be characterised as incomplete. More specifically, FlexRIC lack support for the
cu
\
du
split, requiring the use of monolithic gNB deployment, and there is currently no support for the 7.2x interface that enables communication between
ocu
and
odu
. Additionally, there is no support for the O1 and A1 interfaces, making integration of the
smo
(
smo
) and O-Cloud, infeasible.

B. Unclear and Non-Interoperable Hardware

The second challenge that we have to deal with, were the not interoperable components offered by
hw
vendors. The same vendors, claiming to offer O-RAN compliant products, often stated compatibility with products only from other specific vendors, with contradictions occurring very often. Unclear specifications, and implementation, was also a major issue related to
hw
elements. To our understanding, many vendors are currently implementing their own flavor of
oran
, as the architecture is evolving rapidly and its specifications are being updated very often.

oru

5
Conclusion

oran
is an emerging and promising RAN expansion architecture that is popular among the scientific community. Its purpose is to create a smart, scalable, agile, efficient, and vendor-independent
ran
. However, several theoretical risk analyses
[
2
]
[
5
]
, have shown that this architecture exposes the
ran
to many new kinds of attacks. The frequent adoption of new specifications has prevented the deployment of any
oran
security test lab. Such a deployment is essential, in-order to enable practical risk analyses of the new architecture, which until now are not-existent. With this poster, we want to present our way to deploying a minimal, future-proof
oran
lab and initiate a discussion with researchers sharing our interests and concerns, and exchange information to achieve our goals and secure
oran
.

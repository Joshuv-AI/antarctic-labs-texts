Error Estimates for Sparse Tensor Products of B-spline Approximation Spaces
Clément Guillet

Abstract
. This work introduces and analyzes B-spline approximation spaces defined on general geometric domains obtained through a mapping from a parameter domain. These spaces are constructed as sparse-grid tensor products of univariate spaces in the parameter domain and are mapped to the physical domain via a geometric parametrization. Both the univariate approximation spaces and the geometric mapping are built using maximally smooth B-splines. We construct two such spaces, employing either the sparse-grid combination technique or the hierarchical subspace decomposition of sparse-grid tensor products, and we prove their mathematical equivalence. Furthermore, we derive approximation error estimates and inverse inequalities that highlight the advantages of sparse-grid tensor products. Specifically, under suitable regularity assumptions on the solution, these spaces achieve the same approximation order as standard tensor product spaces while using significantly fewer degrees of freedom. Additionally, our estimates indicate that, in the case of non-tensor-product domains, stronger regularity assumptions on the solution—particularly concerning isotropic (non-mixed) derivatives—are required to achieve optimal convergence rates compared to sparse-grid methods defined on tensor-product domains.



Keywords:
sparse grids, combination technique, approximation spaces, error estimates, interpolation, inverse inequality, B-splines, maximal smoothness, isogeometric analysis

1.
Introduction

Many problems in computational science involve the approximation of high-dimensional functions. However, representing such functions on isotropic, uniformly refined meshes quickly becomes computationally intractable as the dimensionality increases beyond three or four. This phenomenon, known as the curse of dimensionality
[
6
]
, refers to the exponential growth of computational cost with respect to the number of dimensions. Sparse tensor-product constructions, commonly referred to as sparse-grid (SG) methods, were introduced primarily to alleviate this curse by reducing the computational complexity while maintaining high approximation accuracy.

Sparse-grid methods
[
9
,
12
]
were originally developed for the interpolation of high-dimensional functions and have since been extended to the approximation of partial differential equations
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
. Under the assumption that the solution has bounded mixed derivatives, an accurate approximation, achieving nearly the same order of accuracy as standard tensor-product methods— using isotropic uniform meshes— can be computed at a reduced cost.
Convergence rates of sparse-grid approximations using piecewise linear functions are well established
[
9
,
22
]
. Specifically, the
L^{2}
error of sparse-grid approximations scales as
\mathcal{O}(h^{2}|\log h|^{d-1})
, where
h
denotes the mesh size and
d
the dimension. Constructing the sparse-grid approximation requires only
\mathcal{O}(h^{-1}|\log h|^{d-1})
basis functions whereas standard tensor-product approximations require
\mathcal{O}(h^{-d})
basis functions to achieve an
L^{2}
error of
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
. These results were extended to polynomials
[
25
]
and piecewise Lagrange polynomials
[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
with an
L^{2}
error scaling as
\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1}|\log h|^{d-1})
, for polynomials of degree
p
. Similar results have also been proven for tensor-product operators
[
18
,
16
]
and for non-tensor-product operators
[
17
]
.

Sparse-grid methods were initially restricted to generic tensor-product domains. Some early works proposed to use of transfinite interpolation to generate curvilinear domains
[
8
,
11
]
. However, the regularity assumptions on the solution may no longer hold if the geometric mapping is insufficiently smooth, as is the case with transfinite interpolation. More recently, to overcome this limitation, sparse grids were introduced in the context of isogeometric analysis (IGA)
[
3
]
, which can be viewed as a natural extension of finite element analysis using high-order, highly regular B-spline or non-uniform rational B-spline (NURBS) basis functions.
The main idea of IGA, initially introduced in
[
20
]
is to employ the same type of functions—B-splines or NURBS—to define both the computational domain and the unknown solution of the differential equations. This approach allows for more accurate representation of complex geometries and enables exact descriptions of common geometries such as circles or ellipses. In
[
3
]
, the authors introduce a sparse-grid isogeometric solver based on the so-called sparse-grid combination technique
[
19
]
, which consists of forming a linear combination of several inexpensive tensor-product approximations to recover an approximation of nearly the same order as that of full tensor-product discretizations. Numerical experiments demonstrated that the expected convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(h^{p+1}|\log h|^{d-1})
is achieved for sparse-grid approximations using NURBS basis functions of degree
p
.

However, rigorous approximation error estimates for sparse-grid spaces on general, non-tensor-product domains are still lacking. Understanding such approximation properties is of fundamental importance in the context of Galerkin methods applied to the variational formulations of differential equations. Indeed, error estimates in appropriate Sobolev norms guarantee the convergence of the methods through the application of Céa’s or Strang’s lemma. The first error estimates for IGA approximation spaces were derived in the case of
h
-refinement with fixed polynomial degree
[
2
]
. The case of anisotropic meshes, involving different mesh sizes in each coordinate direction, was addressed in
[
5
]
. This analysis was later extended to include the explicit dependence on the polynomial degree
p

[
4
]
, although it did not cover the most relevant case of maximally smooth B-splines. This gap was filled in
[
24
]
, where sharp error estimates and inverse inequalities with constants independent of the spline degree and the mesh size were established for maximally smooth B-splines.

In this work, we introduce and analyze approximation spaces based on sparse-grid tensor products of B-splines.
This extends the work of
[
24
]
, where such estimates were established for univariate B-splines of maximal smoothness and for
d
-dimensional spaces constructed from full tensor products. Here, we generalize these results to sparse-grid tensor-product spaces in the parameter domain and their images in the physical domain via a geometric B-spline mapping. For simplicity, we restrict our analysis to B-spline functions; however, the extension to NURBS functions, as in
[
2
]
, is straightforward.
Following
[
24
]
, we restrict our analysis to single-patch geometric domains; however, an extension to multi-patch domains, along the lines of
[
23
]
, could also be considered.

We propose two distinct approaches to construct such spaces.
The first approach is based on the sparse-grid combination technique, while the second relies on the hierarchical subspace decomposition of sparse-grid tensor products.
Here, we focus exclusively on the approximation properties of these spaces, leaving implementation and performance considerations to future work.

We further show that, despite their different constructions, the two approximation spaces are identical and can thus be considered a single, unified space.

The Main Results

We summarize in the following the two main results of this paper,
4.2
and
4.3
.
These results provide interpolation error estimates and inverse inequalities in Sobolev norms for the sparse-grid approximation spaces, with constants independent of the mesh size, the spline degree, and the B-spline regularity.

We consider the physical domain constructed by the application of a geometric mapping
\bm{F}
to a tensor-product parameter domain
\hat{\Omega}:=(0,1)^{d}
, i.e.,

(1)

\displaystyle\Omega:=\bm{F}(\hat{\Omega}).

Let

be the maximum mesh level in all directions, associated with the dyadic mesh size
h_{n}=2^{-n}
. We denote by
S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\Omega)
the sparse-grid approximation space.

Theorem
.

Let
u\in H^{dq}(\Omega)
with

.
For any mesh level

such that:

.

For any mesh level

, spline degree

and any

satisfying

.

The symbol

refers to an inequality up to a constant that does not depend on the mesh size

or the spline degree

.

This paper is organized as follows. In
Section

, we introduce the main notation related to meshes, Sobolev spaces, and the associated norms. In

2.
Preliminaries

In this preliminary section, we introduce the main notations for spaces, norms, and meshes that will be used in our analysis.

2.1.
Sobolev Spaces and Norms

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
denote the dimension of the physical domain of interest, which we take as a generic open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote by
W^{q,p}(\Omega)
the standard
L^{p}
Sobolev space of order
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
p\in[1,+\infty]
, and by
H^{q}(\Omega)
when
p=2
. Specifically, these spaces are defined as

\displaystyle W^{q,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\in L^{p}(\Omega),~~|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}\leq q\right\},

\displaystyle H^{q}(\Omega):=W^{q,2}(\Omega).

In the above definitions, the derivative operator, defined for functions
u\in W^{q,p}(\Omega)
and multi-indices
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, is given by

D^{\bm{\alpha}}u:=\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{d}}}{\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}}u,\quad\text{and}~D^{q}u:=D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\quad\text{for}~|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}=q.

The
\ell^{1}
- and
\ell^{\infty}
-norms are defined by

|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\alpha_{i}|,\quad|\bm{\alpha}|_{\infty}:=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}|\alpha_{i}|.

For a smooth vector function
\bm{F}=(F_{1},\ldots,F_{d})\in(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\Omega))^{d}
,
we denote by
\bm{\nabla}^{q}\bm{F}
the
q
-th order multi-derivative tensor of
\bm{F}
, defined componentwise as

(\bm{\nabla}^{q}\bm{F})_{i}:=\nabla^{\bm{\alpha}}F_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,d,\quad|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}=q.

In this work, we focus on the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space
H^{q}(\Omega)
.
We define the inner product on
H^{q}(\Omega)
for
u,v\in H^{q}(\Omega)
by

(u,v)_{H^{q}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}\leq q}\int_{\Omega}D^{\bm{\alpha}}u(\bm{x})\,D^{\bm{\alpha}}v(\bm{x})\,d\bm{x}.

The associated seminorm and norm are defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle|u|_{H^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}=q}\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle:=(u,u)_{H^{q}(\Omega)}^{1/2}=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{q}|u|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Sparse-grid methods require stronger assumptions on the mixed regularity of the solutions than standard tensor-product methods. To this end, we consider function spaces suitable for their analysis. We define the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space with dominating mixed smoothness of order
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, also called the mixed Sobolev space, by

H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{q}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\;\middle|\;D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega),~~|\bm{\alpha}|_{\infty}\leq q\right\}.

The associated seminorm and norm are defined by

\displaystyle|u|_{H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{q}(\Omega)}:=\left(\sum_{|\bm{\alpha}|_{\infty}=q}\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{q}(\Omega)}:=\left(\sum_{m=0}^{q}|u|_{H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{m}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The standard Sobolev space is a subspace of the mixed Sobolev space, and the following relation holds between the full norms:

(2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{H^{dq}(\Omega)}.

2.2.
Parameter Space Meshes

We consider a family of meshes
\{\hat{\Omega}_{h}\}_{h}
on a tensor-product parameter domain
\hat{\Omega}:=(0,1)^{d}
, where
h
denotes the family index and represents the global mesh size.
In this work, we consider a dyadic subdivision of the domain. Let
\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
be a multi-index specifying the mesh level in each coordinate direction. The corresponding mesh sizes are given by the
d
-dimensional vector

h_{\bm{\ell}}:=(2^{-\ell_{1}},\ldots,2^{-\ell_{d}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

These mesh sizes partition the
d
-dimensional parameter domain into elements constructed by tensor products of unidimensional dyadic meshes:

\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\ell_{i}}},\quad\text{where}\quad\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\ell_{i}}}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{h_{\ell_{i}}^{-1}-1}\bigl(h_{\ell_{i}}j,\,h_{\ell_{i}}(j+1)\bigr).

The family
\{\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}\}_{\bm{\ell}}
consists of anisotropic meshes indexed by the mesh levels
\bm{\ell}
, where we allow the mesh size to vary along different directions. This flexibility is essential for defining sparse-grid approximations.

3.
Standard Approximation Spaces

In this section, we introduce finite-dimensional approximation spaces

S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})\subset H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})

defined on the tensor-product domain
\hat{\Omega}=(0,1)^{d}
. These spaces provide increasingly accurate approximations in
H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})
as
h_{\bm{\ell}}\to 0
.
These spaces will later serve as building blocks for the construction of sparse-grid approximation spaces.
We begin by defining the univariate spaces and then extend the construction to the
d
-dimensional setting using tensor-product formulations.

3.1.
Univariate B-splines Spaces

In this section, we consider the unidimensional case corresponding to
d=1
, i.e.,
\hat{\Omega}=(0,1)
. The mesh level is denoted by
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The space of univariate B-splines is defined by

S_{p,k,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega}):=\left\{u\in H^{k}(\hat{\Omega})\;\middle|\;u|_{(h_{\ell}j,\,h_{\ell}(j+1))}\in\mathbb{P}^{p},\ \forall j=0,\ldots,h_{\ell}^{-1}-1\right\}.

Here,
\mathbb{P}^{p}
is the space of polynomials of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and the parameter
k
, with
0\leq k\leq p
, specifies the spline regularity.

We restrict ourselves to the case of B-splines with maximal smoothness, i.e.,
k=p
, and denote

S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega}):=S_{p,p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega}).

For
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
verifying
0\leq r\leq p+1
, we introduce the seminorm
H^{r}
-orthogonal projection operator onto the spline space and its complementary operator:

\displaystyle\pi_{p,h_{\ell}}:H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})\rightarrow S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega}),

\displaystyle(\operatorname{I}-\pi_{p,h_{\ell}}):H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})\rightarrow H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})/S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega}),

defined such that

|(\operatorname{I}-\pi_{p,h_{\ell}})\,u|_{H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})}=\min_{v\in S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})}|u-v|_{H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})}.

The main result of this section, originally presented in
[
24
]
, Theorem 7, provides an estimate of the approximation error in the
H^{r}
seminorm for B-spline spaces with regularity up to
k=p
, with a constant independent of both the spline degree and the mesh size.
We recall this result here, expressed with our notations.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})
with
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For all mesh level
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, each spline degree
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
h_{\ell}p<1
, and any
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
satisfying
0\leq r\leq q\leq p+1
, the following estimate holds true:

|(\operatorname{I}-\pi_{p,h_{\ell}})\,u|_{H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq C_{1}(q,r)\,h_{\ell}^{q-r}\,|u|_{H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})},

with
C_{1}(q,r)=(\sqrt{2})^{q-r}
.

Proof of
3.1
.

According to
[
24
]
, Theorem 7, under the stated assumptions, there exists a spline approximation
u_{p,h_{\ell}}\in S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})
such that:

|u-u_{p,h_{\ell}}|_{H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq(\sqrt{2}h_{\ell})^{q-r}|u|_{H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})}.

Applying the definition of the seminorm
H^{r}
-orthogonal projection, we obtain the result.
∎

Note that, since

S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})\subseteq S_{p,k,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega}),

for all
0\leq k<p
,
3.1
is also valid in that case.

We also introduce, as in
[
24
]
, a suitable subspace of
S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})
to derive inverse inequalities.

Definition 3.2
.

The space of
q
-vanishing derivative univariate B-splines is defined by

\tilde{S}_{p,h_{\ell}}^{(q)}(\hat{\Omega}):=\left\{u_{p,h_{\ell}}\in S_{p,h_{\ell}}(\hat{\Omega})\;\middle|\;\frac{\partial^{2l+q}}{\partial x^{2l+q}}u_{p,h_{\ell}}(0)=\frac{\partial^{2l+q}}{\partial x^{2l+q}}u_{p,h_{\ell}}(1)=0\quad\text{for all}\;l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\;\text{with}~2l+q<p\right\}

Note that according to
[
24
]
, Theorem 5,
3.1
also holds for the space
\tilde{S}_{p,h_{\ell}}^{(q)}(\hat{\Omega})
. An inverse inequality, which is a result from
[
24
]
, Theorem 6, can be derived within this space.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all mesh levels
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, each
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
h_{\ell}p<1
and each
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
0\leq q\leq p
,

|u_{p,h_{\ell}}|_{H^{q}(0,1)}\leq C_{2}(q)h_{\ell}^{-q}\|u_{p,h_{\ell}}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}

is satisfied for all spline approximation
u_{p,h_{\ell}}\in\tilde{S}_{p,h_{\ell}}^{(q)}(\hat{\Omega})

with
C_{2}(q)=(2\sqrt{3})^{q}
.

Proof of
3.2
.

From
[
24
]
, Theorem 6, by induction, we obtain the result.
∎

3.2.
Anisotropic
d
-dimensional B-spline Spaces

We extend the results of the previous section to anisotropic meshes in multiple dimensions, where
d\geq 2
. The tensor-product parameter domain is
\hat{\Omega}=(0,1)^{d}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
be the mesh level in each direction. The anisotropic
d
-dimensional B-spline space is defined by the tensor products of the univariate spaces:

S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega}):=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{d}S_{p,h_{\ell_{i}}}(0,1).

Here, we consider B-splines of the same degree
p
in each direction, while allowing the mesh size to vary along different directions.

To extend the notion of projection introduced in the previous section to this
d
-dimensional space, we begin by defining the projection along a single direction.

Definition 3.4
.

The
H^{r}
-orthogonal projection onto the spline space
S_{p,h_{\ell_{i}}}(0,1)
in the single direction
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
is defined by

\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i}}}:=\operatorname{I}\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\operatorname{I}

Note that the unidimensional projections are commutative, i.e., for
i_{1},i_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i_{1}}}}\circ\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i_{2}}}}=\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i_{2}}}}\circ\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i_{1}}}}.

We introduce the projection onto the B-spline space in a subset of dimension indices by successively applying the unidimensional projection.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
0\leq k\leq d
and
J\subset\{1,\dots,d\}
be a subset of cardinality
|J|=k
. The
k
-dimensional projection onto the B-spline space in the directions
J
is defined by

\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\,J}u\;:=\;\Bigl(\,\prod_{i\in J}^{\circ}\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i}}}\,\Bigr)u.

We also define the complementary projection by:

(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\ell}})^{\,J}u\;:=\;\Bigl(\,\prod_{i\in J}^{\circ}\bigl(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\ell_{i}}}\bigr)\,\Bigr)u.

Here the notation
\prod_{i\in J}^{\circ}
indicates composition of the operators with respect to the indices in
J
, the order being irrelevant since the operators commute.

Based on these definitions, the projection of a function
u
onto the
d
-dimensional spline space
S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})
can be defined as the composition of the univariate
H^{r}
-orthogonal projections applied successively along each coordinate direction, i.e.,

(3)

\displaystyle\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}u:=\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\{1,\ldots,d\}}u.

We introduce an approximation error estimate for the B-spline spaces on anisotropic meshes.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})
with
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For all mesh levels
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, each
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and any
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0\leq r\leq q\leq p+1
,

\bigl\|(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}})\,u\bigr\|_{H^{r}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq C_{3}(d,q,r)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}h_{\ell_{i}}^{q-r}\Bigr)\|u\|_{H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})}

is satisfied with
C_{3}(d,q,r)=(r+1)^{d/2}\sqrt{2}^{q-r}

Proof of
3.1
.

The proof is strictly similar to
[
24
]
, Theorem 8, but considering different mesh sizes
h_{\ell_{i}}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
, in each direction.
∎

The result of
3.1
is not suitable for proving sharp approximation properties of the sparse-grid spaces, as it does not provide sufficient information in the mixed directions.
In the remainder of this section, we introduce three lemmas that will be used later to study the approximation properties of these spaces.

First, in
3.4
, we decompose the
d
-dimensional projection defined in
Eq.

3
into a telescopic sum of partial projections over subsets of coordinate directions.
This decomposition will allow us to exploit cancellations of coarse errors in the sparse-grid combination technique. To prove
3.4

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}l^{i}=0.

3.3
.

We demonstrate the result by induction.
For
i=0
, the relation is immediate using the binomial theorem. We assume that

4
holds for

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}l^{i+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}l^{i+1}

(6)

\displaystyle=(d-1)\sum_{l=1}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-2}{l-1}l^{i}

\displaystyle=-(d-1)\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-2}{l}(l+1)^{i}

(7)

\displaystyle=-(d-1)\sum_{j=0}^{i}\binom{i}{j}\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-2}{l}l^{j}

(8)

\displaystyle=0.

In the above, we have used the relation

\dbinom{d-1}{l}=\dfrac{d-1}{l}\dbinom{d-2}{l-1}

in

6

7
; and the induction hypothesis in

8
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For all mesh levels
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and all

the following decomposition holds true:

(9)

\displaystyle(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}})u=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\

By expanding each of the following products as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\

\displaystyle=\operatorname{I}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k-1}\right]

4.
Sparse-Grid Approximation Spaces

In this section we introduce finite-dimensional approximation spaces defined by the sparse-grid tensor products of the unidimensional spaces
S_{p,h_{\ell}}(0,1)
. We first introduce in
Section

4.1
the sparse-grid spaces in the tensor-product parameter domain
\hat{\Omega}=(0,1)^{d}
before extending in
Section

4.2
the results to more general non-tensor-product domains defined by
\Omega=\bm{F}(\hat{\Omega})
, where
\bm{F}
is a geometric B-spline-based mapping.

4.1.
Approximation Spaces in Tensor-Product Domains

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be the maximum mesh level, i.e.,
h_{\ell_{i}}\leq h_{n}=2^{-n}
, for all
i=1,\ldots,d
.

We first introduce the so-called full tensor-product approximation space, constructed by taking uniform mesh size in all directions.

Definition 4.1
.

The full tensor-product approximation space is defined by

S_{p,h_{n}}(\hat{\Omega}):=S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})\quad\text{where}~\ell_{i}=n,~~\forall i=1,\ldots,d.

In
Section

4.1.1
, we introduce a
d
-dimensional sparse-grid approximation space, denoted by
S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\hat{\Omega})
, which is a subspace of the full tensor-product space:

S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\hat{\Omega})\subset S_{p,h_{n}}(\hat{\Omega}).

The superscript
\mathscr{H}
indicates that the space is constructed using a hierarchical formulation. The construction proceeds by expressing the full tensor product space as a direct sum of hierarchical subspace increments, and by neglecting the less significant contributions under the assumption that the target functions possess sufficiently smooth mixed derivatives. In this setting, the approximation acts on the space
H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})
rather than on
H^{q}(\hat{\Omega})
.

In
Section

4.1.2
, we introduce a second sparse-grid approximation space, denoted by
S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\hat{\Omega})
, based on the sparse-grid combination technique and first proposed in
[
3
]
. The combination technique can be viewed as an approximation of hierarchical-based sparse-grid methods and is known, under suitable regularity assumptions, to achieve the same approximation order as the latter
[
13
,
18
,
21
]
.

4.1.1.
Hierarchical Subspace Decomposition

A key element in the construction of sparse-grid tensor-product spaces is the definition of hierarchical subspaces, also called hierarchical increments, yielding a direct sum decomposition. Specifically, we want to express the anisotropic approximation spaces as the direct sum decomposition

(18)

\displaystyle S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})=\bigoplus_{\bm{k}\leq\bm{\ell}}W_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}(\hat{\Omega}),

where the multi-index notation
\bm{k}\leq\bm{\ell}
stands for component-wise inequalities, i.e.,
k_{j}\leq\ell_{j}
for all
j=1,\dots,d
.

A crucial property to obtain
Eq.

18
is that the B-spline spaces are nested under refinement via knot insertion:

S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}-\bm{e}_{j}}}(\hat{\Omega})\subset S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega}),\quad\forall j=1,\dots,d,

where
\bm{e}_{j}
denotes the unit vector in the
j
-th direction.
Since knot insertion preserves nestedness, every new function introduced during refinement is linearly independent of the previous ones. The hierarchical subspaces are then defined as

(19)

\displaystyle W_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}(\hat{\Omega}):=S_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}(\hat{\Omega})/\bigoplus_{j=1}^{d}S_{p,h_{\bm{k}-\bm{e}_{j}}}(\hat{\Omega}),

where

S_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}(\hat{\Omega}):=0\quad\text{if}~\exists j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}~~s.t.~k_{j}=-1.

By defining the hierarchical increments as in
Eq.

19
,
Eq.

18
is satisfied.
Intuitively, the hierarchical increments
W_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}(\hat{\Omega})
spans the new basis functions introduced at the refinement step.

Using the definition of these hierarchical subspaces, we introduce the hierarchical-based sparse-grid approximation space.

Definition 4.2
.

The hierarchical sparse-grid approximation space is defined by

S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\hat{\Omega}):=\Bigl\{u\in H^{p}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})\;\Big|\;u=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}},\quad w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\in W_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})\Bigr\},

where

\mathscr{H}=\left\{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}~~|~~|\bm{\ell}|_{1}\leq n+(d-1)\lambda_{p},\quad\ell_{j}\geq\lambda_{p}\right\},

and

\lambda_{p}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }p=1,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{\log p}{\log 2},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The constraint
\ell_{j}\geq\lambda_{p}
ensures that
h_{\ell_{j}}p<1
for all
j=1,\dots,d
, a condition required to apply Theorem 7 from
[
24
]
, which underlies our analysis.

4.1.2.
Combination Technique

The sparse-grid combination technique is an alternative formulation for representing functions from hierarchical sparse-grid spaces, such as
S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\hat{\Omega})
. In general, it yields an approximation of
H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})
of the same order of accuracy
[
13
,
21
]
. The method relies on forming a linear combination of contributions that live in standard tensor-product approximation spaces defined with admissible levels
\bm{\ell}
satisfying
|\bm{\ell}|_{1}\approx n
.

Specifically, the admissible levels are such that
\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}
. We introduce the index set of admissible levels as

(20)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}:=\bigcup_{0\leq l\leq d-1}\mathscr{L}_{l},\quad\text{where}~\mathscr{L}_{l}:=\left\{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\;\middle|\;|\bm{\ell}|_{1}=n+(d-1)\lambda_{p}-l,\ \ell_{j}\geq\lambda_{p}\right\}.

We define the sparse-grid combination technique approximation space by combining the spaces
S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})
associated with the admissible levels.

Definition 4.3
.

The sparse-grid combination technique approximation space is defined by

S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\hat{\Omega}):=\Bigl\{u\in H^{p}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})\;\Big|\;u=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}},\quad u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\in S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})\Bigr\},

with the combination coefficients given by

c_{\bm{\ell}}:=(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l},\quad\text{for}\;\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}.

We extend the definition of the
d
-dimensional projection introduced in
Eq.

3
to this space by defining the projection as the combination of projections associated with the admissible levels:

\Pi_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}:=\left[\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}\,\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\right].

4.1.3.
Equivalence Between Spaces

In the previous sections, we have introduced two approximation spaces based on sparse-grid tensor products of univariate B-spline spaces.
We now establish that the two approximation spaces are identical.

Theorem 4.1
.

The hierarchical subspace decomposition and the combination technique formulations yield identical spaces, i.e.,

S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\hat{\Omega})=S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\hat{\Omega}).

To give the proof of this theorem, we have to introduce two auxiliary lemmas.
4.2
establishes that the sum of the combination coefficients over the admissible levels equals one, while
4.1
is a combinatorial result used in the proof of
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda_{p}
be defined as in
Eq.

20
,
\ell,k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
0\leq\ell\leq n
and
0\leq k\leq d-1
, then:

\displaystyle\underset{l=0}{\overset{d-1}{\sum}}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l-\ell}{d-1-k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1\quad\text{if }k=0,\\
0\quad\text{else.}\end{array}\right.

Proof of
4.1
.

4.1
can be recast as:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}

\displaystyle(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l-\ell}{d-1-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\binom{d-1}{l}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{(d-1-k)!}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1-k}(n-l-\ell-\lambda_{p}+k+j).

By expanding the products in the right side we obtain a sum of terms with powers
(-l)^{i}
like the left side of
Eq.

4
in
3.3
, with
0\leq i\leq d-1
. As a result, the expression vanishes for
k>0
. If
k=0
, only one term remains, the one with
(-l)^{d-1}
. Then by applying
d-2
times the same calculations than in
Eq.

5
-
Eq.

7
, we obtain:

(23)

\displaystyle\underset{l=0}{\overset{d-1}{\sum}}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}(-l)^{d-1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d-2}(-1)^{d-1}\frac{(d-1)!}{(d-1)!}\sum_{l=0}^{1}(-1)^{l}\binom{1}{l}l=1.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be the set of admissible levels in the combination, and
c_{\bm{\ell}}
the combination coefficients, then:

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}=1.

Proof of
4.2
.

The sum can be recast into:

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}1

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l}{d-1}

(26)

\displaystyle=1.

We obtain
Eq.

25
by counting the number of elements in
\mathscr{L}_{l}
; and
Eq.

26
by using
4.1
.
∎

Proof of
4.1
.

Let
u_{p,h_{n}}\in S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\hat{\Omega})
, then by definition:

u_{p,h_{n}}=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}},\quad\text{where}~~u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\in S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega}).

Using the definition of the combination coefficients and the direct sum decomposition in
Eq.

18
, we have:

(27)

\displaystyle u_{p,h_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}\sum_{\bm{k}\leq\bm{\ell}}w_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}

(28)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}}w_{p,h_{\bm{k}}}N(\bm{k},n,d,p).

We have rearranged the sums in
Eq.

27
and introduced in
Eq.

28
the following quantity:

N(\bm{k},n,d,p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}1\quad\text{if}~|\bm{k}|_{1}\leq n+(d-1)\lambda_{p},\\

By counting the number of elements in
\mathscr{L}_{l}
, and using

, we have:

N(\bm{k},n,d,p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1\quad\text{if}~|\bm{k}|_{1}\leq n+(d-1)\lambda_{p},\\

As a result,

and

. By performing the same substitutions in the sums in reverse, we can we also show that

, which conclude the proof.
∎

As a result of
4.1
, the two spaces —and thus their approximation properties— are the same.
We shall then derive properties of this space using either the combination technique or the hierarchical formulation, choosing the approach that makes a particular proof more straightforward.

However, although the two spaces are mathematically identical, their computational properties may differ due to the distinct constructions.
In particular when applied to Galerkin methods, the size, condition number, and sparsity patterns of the resulting discretization matrices, such as the mass and stiffness matrices, are not the same.
A detailed investigation of these computational aspects is deferred to future work.

4.1.4.
Approximation Error Estimate

In this section, we provide an approximation error estimate in mixed Sobolev norm for the sparse-grid space. To prove the result, we consider the combination technique formulation of the sparse-grid space.
Before stating the main result of this section, we introduce
4.3
expliciting the cancellations of the coarse contributions in the combination technique.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
1\leq k\leq d
, and

be an index set of cardinality

.
Suppose
\{a_{J,\bm{\ell}}\}_{J\subset\{1,\dots,d\}}
is a family of

Then the following identity holds:

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\;\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}C_{3}(d,k,l)\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}^{J}_{l,k}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}

\displaystyle\quad+\;\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}C_{4}(d,l)\,\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}a_{\{1,\ldots,d\},\bm{\ell}},

where the notation

\mathscr{L}^{J}_{l,k}:=\Bigl\{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\;\Big|\;\sum_{i\in J}|\ell_{i}|=n+(k-1)\lambda_{p}-l,~~\ell_{J_{j}}\geq\lambda_{p},~~\text{for}~j=1,\ldots,k\Bigr\}

is introduced. The constants are given by

C_{3}(d,k,l)=\sum_{\kappa=0}^{l}(-1)^{\kappa}\binom{d-1}{\kappa}\binom{d-k-1+l-\kappa-k\lambda_{p}}{\,d-1-k},\qquad C_{4}(d,l)=(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}.

4.3
.

We recast the expression of the left side of

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}.

Note that, in the above, the admissible levels verify a condition on the

J

If

Eq.

The first step is to the rearrange the innermost sums of the right side of

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}

(31)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}^{J}_{l,k}}\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l-(\ell_{J_{1}}+\ldots+\ell_{J_{k}})}{d-1-k}\,a_{J,\bm{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\tilde{\mathscr{L}}^{J}_{d-1,k}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{\kappa}\binom{d-1}{\kappa}\binom{n+d-1-\kappa-\lambda_{p}-(\ell_{J_{1}}+\ldots+\ell_{J_{k}})}{d-1-k},

(32)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}^{J}_{l,k}}a_{J,\bm{\ell}}\sum_{\kappa=0}^{l}(-1)^{\kappa}\binom{d-1}{\kappa}\binom{n+d-1-\kappa-\lambda_{p}-(\ell_{J_{1}}+\ldots+\ell_{J_{k}})}{d-1-k},

We have introduced the set of levels

\tilde{\mathscr{L}}^{J}_{l,k}:=\Bigl\{\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}~~|~~\sum_{i\in J}|\ell_{i}|\leq n+(k-1)\lambda_{p}-l,~~\ell_{J_{j}}\geq\lambda_{p},~~\text{for}~j=1,\ldots,k\Bigr\}.

In
Eq.

31
, we have rearranged the sum on the levels, by summing in the
J
directions and letting the remaining directions constraint by the
l^{1}
-norm equality. We have rearranged again the sum on the levels into two terms: whether if the level appears in each term
0\leq l\leq d-1
of the sum in
Eq.

31
, which correspond to the first term in
Eq.

32
; or not, which correspond to the second one.

According to
4.1
, the first term in
Eq.

32
is equal to zero. This correpsonds to coarse contributions that are cancelled in the combination technique. Substituting
(\ell_{J_{1}}+\ldots+\ell_{J_{k}})
by its expression in
Eq.

32
, we obtain the result for
1\leq k\leq d-1
.
∎

With this lemma in hand, we can now state
4.4
, which is the main result of this section.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
u\in H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})
with
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For all mesh size
n\in\mathbb{N}
, each
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and each
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq r\leq q\leq p+1
,

(33)

\displaystyle\left\|(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}})\,u\right\|_{H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{r}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq C_{10}(d,q,r)h_{n}^{q-r}|\log h_{n}|^{d-1}\|u\|_{H_{\mathrm{mix}}^{q}(\hat{\Omega})}

is satisfied with

C_{10}(d,q,r)=\frac{(d-1)^{d-1}}{(d-1)!(\log 2)^{d-1}}\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}2^{-(q-r)(d-1-l)}(-1)^{l}\binom{d-1}{l}(r+1)^{d/2}(\sqrt{2})^{d(q-r)},

a constant that does not depend on the mesh size
h_{n}
or the degree
p
.

Proof of
4.4
.

The local interpolation error can be written as

\displaystyle(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}})u

\displaystyle=\Big(\operatorname{I}-\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\Bigr)u

(34)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}})u

(35)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}}c_{\bm{\ell}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}(-1)^{k}(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}})^{J}u

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\sum_{l=1}^{d-2}C_{4}(d,k,l)(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l,k}^{J}}(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}})^{J}u

(36)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}C_{5}(d,l)\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}})^{\{1,\ldots,d\}}u.

In the above,
4.2
has been used in
Eq.

34
;
3.4
has been used in
Eq.

35
; and
4.3
has been used in
Eq.

36
. Then, using the triangular inequality and applying
3.5
to each of the terms, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left\|(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}})\,u\right\|_{H^{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}C_{6}(d,r,k,l)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l,k}^{J}}\Big(\prod_{i\in J}h_{\ell_{i}}^{q-r}\Bigr)\|u\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}

(37)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}C_{7}(d,r,l)\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}\Big(\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}h_{\ell_{i}}^{q-r}\Bigr)\|u\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})},

where the constants are

C_{6}(d,r,k,l)=C_{3}(d,k,l)C_{2}(d,k,q,r),\quad C_{7}(d,r,l)=C_{4}(d,l)C_{2}(d,d,q,r).

The number of elements in each sum is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}\\
|J|=k\end{subarray}}1=\binom{d}{k},\quad\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l,k}^{J}}1=\binom{n+k-1-\lambda_{p}-l}{k-1},\quad\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{L}_{l}}1=\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l}{d-1}.

Since the last two sums are maximal for
l=0
,
Eq.

37
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\left\|\left(I-\Pi_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}\right)u\right\|_{H^{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d-1}C_{8}(d,q,r,k)\binom{n+k-1-\lambda_{p}}{k-1}h_{n}^{q-r}\|u\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}}

\displaystyle+C_{9}(d,q,r)\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}}{d-1}h_{n}^{q-r}\|u\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}},

where

C_{8}(d,q,r,k)=\binom{d}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}2^{-(q-r)(k-1-l)}C_{5}(d,r,k,l),\quad C_{9}(d,q,r)=\sum_{l=0}^{d-2}2^{-(q-r)(d-1-l)}C_{6}(d,r,l).

Eventually, we conclude with the bound

\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}}{d-1}\leq\binom{n+d-2}{d-1}\leq\frac{(d-1)^{d-1}}{(d-1)!(\log 2)^{d-1}}|\log h_{n}|^{d-1},

so that the constant does not depend on
p
.
∎

On tensor-product domains such as
\hat{\Omega}
, it is well established that sparse-grid approximations using piecewise linear functions achieve an
L^{2}
approximation error of order
\mathcal{O}(h_{n}^{2}|\log h_{n}|^{d-1})
, with a dependence on the
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mix}}
Sobolev norm of the solution
[
9
,
19
]
. This result has been generalized to piecewise Lagrange polynomials of degree
p
, for which the
L^{2}
error scales as
\mathcal{O}(h_{n}^{p+1}|\log h_{n}|^{d-1})
and depends on the
H^{p+1}_{\mathrm{mix}}
Sobolev norm
[
9
]
.

4.4
further extends these results to B-splines of maximal smoothness, i.e., B-splines of degree
p
with regularity
H^{p}(0,1)
in each direction. Considering
4.4
with
r=0
and
q=p+1
, the
L^{2}
approximation error for such B-splines exhibits the same scaling as for Lagrange polynomials. Notably, as in the case of isotropic full tensor-product spaces treated in
[
24
]
, the approximation error is independent of the spline degree
p
.

4.1.5.
Inverse Inequality

In this section, we derive an inverse inequality in mixed Sobolev norms for the sparse-grid approximation space.
To this end, we extend the definition of the unidimensional space of
q
-vanishing derivative univariate B-splines
3.2
to the sparse-grid spaces.
Specifically, we define the spaces
\tilde{S}_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H},(q)}(\hat{\Omega})
and
\tilde{S}_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L},(q)}(\hat{\Omega})
by replacing the univariate spaces
S_{p,h_{\ell}}(0,1)
with their counterparts
\tilde{S}_{p,h_{\ell}}^{(q)}(0,1)
in
4.2
and
4.3
, respectively.

Using the same arguments as in the proof of
4.1
, we can demonstrate that these two spaces are identical. Moreover, according to
[
24
]
, Corollary 1, the estimate
Eq.

33
of
4.4
also holds for spaces constructed with
q
-vanishing-derivative B-splines.

Lemma 4.5
.

For each
0\leq q\leq p
, the inverse inequality

(38)

\displaystyle\|u_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq C_{11}(d,q)h_{n}^{-q}|\log h_{n}|^{d/2}\|u_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}

is statisfied for all
u_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}\in\tilde{S}_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H},(q)}(\hat{\Omega})
, with

(39)

\displaystyle C_{11}(d,q)=(q+1)^{d/2}(2\sqrt{3})^{dq}\frac{2^{(d-1)}2^{d/2}}{d!(\log 2)^{d/2}},

a constant that does not depend on the mesh size
h_{n}
or the degree
p
.

To prove this result, we consider the hierarchical sparse-grid formulation. We introduce
4.6
, an auxiliary result required to prove
4.5
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
0\leq s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}\leq q\leq p
, and for all mesh level
\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}
, each
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\in W_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})
, then

(40)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\frac{\partial^{s_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{s_{1}}}\ldots\frac{\partial^{s_{d}}}{\partial x_{d}^{s_{d}}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq(2\sqrt{3})^{|\bm{s}|_{1}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{\ell_{i}}^{-s_{i}}\Bigr)\|w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}.

Proof of
4.6
.

Like in the proof of
3.5
, we demonstrate the result for
d=2
, as the extension to higher dimensions uses the same arguments. Let
0\leq s,m\leq q
be two integers. The hierarchical increment verifies:

w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\in W_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})\subset H^{p}(\hat{\Omega})\subset H^{q}(\hat{\Omega}),

so that we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial y^{s}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\cdot,y)\in H^{m}(0,1),\quad\forall y\in(0,1),

\displaystyle w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(x,\cdot)\in H^{s}(0,1),\quad\forall x\in(0,1).

Therefore, we can demonstrate the bounds

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial x^{m}}\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial y^{s}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl|\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial y^{s}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\cdot,y)\Bigr|^{2}_{H^{m}(0,1)}dy

(41)

\displaystyle\leq(2\sqrt{3})^{2m}h_{\ell_{1}}^{-2m}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl(\frac{\partial^{s}}{\partial y^{s}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(x,y)\Bigr)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\leq(2\sqrt{3})^{2m}h_{\ell_{1}}^{-2m}\int_{0}^{1}\Bigl|w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(x,\cdot)\Bigr|^{2}_{H^{s}(0,1)}dx

(42)

\displaystyle\leq(2\sqrt{3})^{2(m+s)}h_{\ell_{1}}^{-2m}h_{\ell_{2}}^{-2s}\|w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}^{2}.

We have used
3.2
twice: in
Eq.

41
and in
Eq.

42
.
∎

Proof of
4.5
.

The following inequalities are true:

(43)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\frac{\partial^{s_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{s_{1}}}\ldots\frac{\partial^{s_{d}}}{\partial x_{d}^{s_{d}}}u_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}\Bigl\|\frac{\partial^{s_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{s_{1}}}\ldots\frac{\partial^{s_{d}}}{\partial x_{d}^{s_{d}}}w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\Bigr\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}

(44)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}(2\sqrt{3})^{|\bm{s}|_{1}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{\ell_{i}}^{-s_{i}}\Bigr)\|w_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}

(45)

\displaystyle\leq(2\sqrt{3})^{dq}\Bigl(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{\ell_{i}}^{-2s_{i}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\|u_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}.

We have used the triangular inequality in
Eq.

43
; applied
4.6
in
Eq.

44
; and used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
Eq.

45
. We then have the relation

(46)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{\ell_{i}}^{-2s_{i}}\leq h_{n}^{-2q}2^{2(d-1)\lambda_{p}}\Bigr(\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}1\Bigl),

and we bound the last term of the product by counting the number of elements in
\mathscr{H}
:

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\\
|\bm{k}|_{1}\leq n-\lambda_{p}\end{subarray}}1\leq\dbinom{n-\lambda_{p}+d}{d}.

Gathering
Eq.

46
and
Eq.

47
together, and since

, we obtain:

(48)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\ell}\in\mathscr{H}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{\ell_{i}}^{-2s_{i}}\leq h_{n}^{-2q}2^{2(d-1)}\dbinom{n+d-1}{d}\leq h_{n}^{-2q}\log|h_{n}|^{d}\frac{2^{2(d-1)}2^{d}}{d!(\log 2)^{d}}.

Eventually, we substitute the side of
Eq.

48
relation into
Eq.

45
and bound all the
(q+1)^{d}
in the
H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}
full norm to get:

\Bigl\|u_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}\Bigr\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq(q+1)^{d/2}(2\sqrt{3})^{dq}\frac{2^{(d-1)}2^{d/2}}{d!(\log 2)^{d/2}}h_{n}^{-q}\log|h_{n}|^{d/2}.

∎

4.2.
Extension to Non-Tensor-Product Domains

We now extend the previous results to more general geometric domains.
Let the physical domain be defined by a smooth mapping

\bm{F}:\hat{\Omega}\rightarrow\Omega,

which is a diffeomorphism between the tensor-product parameter domain
\hat{\Omega}
and the physical domain
\Omega
, i.e.,

\Omega:=\bm{F}(\hat{\Omega}).

The mapping
\bm{F}
provides a geometric parametrization of the physical domain.

Given meshes of the parameter and physical domains, denoted respectively by
\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
and
\Omega_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
, and an approximation space
S_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})
defined on the parameter domain, we define the corresponding approximation space in the physical domain as

S_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\Omega):=\left\{u_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\ \middle|\ u_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}=\hat{u}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}\circ\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{-1},\quad\hat{u}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}\in S_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})\right\}.

Here,
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
denotes a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism that maps the parameter mesh
\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
to the corresponding physical mesh
\Omega_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
:

\Omega_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}:=\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}).

Several strategies exist in the context of sparse grids to define
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
, and consequently to construct the spaces
S_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\Omega)
.
In
[
1
]
,
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
is defined as the interpolant of
\bm{F}
evaluated at the nodes of
\Omega_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
.
In
[
8
,
11
]
, curvilinear meshes are employed and
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
is defined by transfinite interpolation.
In
[
3
]
,
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
is represented using B-splines or NURBS (rational B-splines with associated weights), where the geometry is defined as a linear combination of B-spline basis functions weighted by control points.

In this work, we adopt the isogeometric analysis representation, that is, we define
\bm{F}_{h}
as a B-spline mapping. We restrict ourselves to B-spline functions for simplicity; however, the extension to NURBS functions, as in
[
2
]
, could also be considered.
In practical applications, the geometry of the physical domain is typically described on a coarse mesh with relatively few elements, while the solution is approximated on successively finer meshes to achieve the desired accuracy.
This is possible because B-spline or NURBS mappings can represent the geometry exactly, even on the coarsest mesh.

We assume that the geometry is described on the coarsest parameter mesh
\hat{\Omega}_{h_{0}}
, corresponding to unit mesh size (
h_{0}=1
) in each coordinate direction.
For any mesh size
h_{\bm{\ell}}
, the physical mesh is then defined by

\Omega_{h}:=\bm{F}_{h_{0}}(\hat{\Omega}_{h_{0}}),

where the geometric mapping is given by

\bm{F}_{h_{0}}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})=\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{d}=1}^{n_{d}}\bm{P}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}\,s_{p,h_{0},i_{1}}(\xi_{1})\cdots s_{p,h_{0},i_{d}}(\xi_{d}).

Here,
s_{p,h_{0},i_{j}}\in S_{p,h_{0}}(0,1)
denote the univariate B-spline basis functions defined on the coarsest mesh with
n_{j}=h_{0}^{-1}
for
j=1,\ldots,d
, and
\bm{P}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are the control points defining the geometric mapping.

Following
[
2
,
20
]
, when the approximation spaces are refined, the control points are adjusted so that the mapping
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
remains unchanged.
Thus, the geometry and its parametrization are kept fixed as the mesh is refined, i.e., when considering
\hat{\Omega}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
for increasing
\bm{\ell}
.
Specifically,

\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}=\bm{F}_{h_{0}},

where
\bm{F}_{h_{\bm{\ell}}}
is defined with control points
\bm{P}^{(h_{\bm{\ell}})}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}}
that are adjusted according to
h_{\bm{\ell}}
.

We define the isogeometric analysis approximation spaces by composing the parameter spaces with the geometric mapping
\mathbf{F}_{h_{0}}
. This composition is also called the push-forward operation of the parameter space.

Definition 4.4
.

The physical domain sparse-grid approximation spaces are defined by

S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\Omega):=\Bigl\{u\in H^{p}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\Omega)\;\Big|\;u=u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\mathscr{H}}\circ\mathbf{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1},\quad u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\mathscr{H}}\in S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\mathscr{H}}(\hat{\Omega})\Bigr\},

and

S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\Omega):=\Bigl\{u\in H^{p}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\Omega)\;\Big|\;u=u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\mathscr{L}}\circ\mathbf{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1},\quad u_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\mathscr{L}}\in S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\hat{\Omega})\Bigr\}.

Note that according to
4.1
, the two spaces are identical.

4.2.1.
Approximation Error

In this section we extend the results from the parameter approximation space to the physical domain approximation space.
Before stating the main result of this paper, we introduce a lemma providing bounds for the composition of a function with the geometric mapping
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}
, or its inverse
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}
. This result is an extension of
[
2
]
, Lemma 3.5, to functions of mixed Sobolev spaces; it uses an argument similar to the result from
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.

Lemma 4.7
.

For all
u\in H^{dq}(\Omega)
, with
0\leq q\leq p+1
, it holds that

(49)

\displaystyle|u|_{H^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{q}C_{12}|u\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}}|_{H^{m}(\hat{\Omega})},

(50)

\displaystyle\|u\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{dq}C_{13}|u|_{H^{m}(\Omega)},

where

\displaystyle C_{12}=C_{12}(m,q,\|\bm{\nabla F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}\|_{W^{q,\infty}(\Omega)},\|\det\bm{\nabla}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})}),

\displaystyle C_{13}=C_{13}(m,q,\|\bm{\nabla F}_{h_{0}}\|_{W^{q,\infty}(\hat{\Omega})},\|\det\bm{\nabla}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}),

are constants that depend in particular on the derivatives up to order
q
of the geometric mapping
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}
and its inverse
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}
.

Proof of
4.7
.

The proof uses similar arguments than
[
10
]
, Lemma 3, and is based on the multivariate Faá di Bruno decomposition formula. Let
\bm{\alpha}
be such that
|\bm{\alpha}|_{\infty}=q
. Using the Faá di Bruno formula, the triangular inequality, the relation
\|f\cdot(\prod_{j}g_{j})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}}\prod_{j}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, and putting together the
factors which correspond to identical values of indices of derivatives of
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle(u\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}})\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq C(q)\sum_{m=0}^{dq}\|D^{m}u(\bm{F}_{h_{0}})\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}\sum_{\bm{i}\in I(m,q,\bm{\alpha})}\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|\bm{\nabla}^{j}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\|^{i_{j}}_{L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})}

(51)

\displaystyle\leq C(q)\|\det\bm{\nabla}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}\|^{1/2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\sum_{m=0}^{dq}\|\bm{\nabla}^{m}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\sum_{\bm{i}\in I(m,q,\bm{\alpha})}\prod_{j=1}^{q}\|\bm{\nabla}^{j}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\|^{i_{j}}_{L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})}.

In the above, we have introduced the following index set:

I(m,q,\bm{\alpha})=\{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}~~|~~|\bm{i}|_{1}=m,~~1\cdot i_{1}+2\cdot i_{2}+\ldots+q\cdot i_{q}=|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}\}.

Eq.

51
is obtained with a change of variable. Then, using the Hölder inequality, and gathering all the terms in a constant
C_{12}
, we get:

\|u\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\|_{H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})}\leq\sum_{m=0}^{dq}C_{12}(m,q,\|\bm{\nabla F}_{h_{0}}\|_{W^{q,\infty}(\hat{\Omega})},\|\det\bm{\nabla}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})|u|_{H^{m}(\Omega)}.

We obtain
Eq.

49
by applying the same arguments to
\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}u\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}=\|D^{\bm{\alpha}}(u\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1})\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}
, with
|\bm{\alpha}|_{1}=q
.
∎

Note that, according to
Eq.

50
, in order to bound the
H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\Omega)
norm of the composition of a function
u
with the geometric mapping
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}
, it is necessary to control the derivatives of
u
up to order
dq
in each direction.
This arises because the mapping
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}
is not a separable function.
Although
\bm{F}_{h_{0}}
is expressed in a tensor-product form, its control points couple the contributions of the individual basis functions, each of which is separable on its own.
As a result, the mixed derivatives of the composed function
D^{\bm{\alpha}}(u\circ\bm{F}_{h_{0}})
with
|\bm{\alpha}|_{\infty}=q
, appearing in the
H^{q}(\Omega)
seminorm, do not vanish, thereby destroying the coordinatewise independence.

The main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u\in H^{dq}(\Omega)
with
q\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
For any mesh level
n\in\mathbb{N}
, spline degree
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and any
r\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
0\leq r\leq q\leq p+1
, the following estimate holds:

|(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}})\,u|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}\leq C\,h_{n}^{\,q-r}|\log h_{n}|^{\,d-1}\|u\|_{H^{dq}(\Omega)},

where

C=C_{14}(d,q,r,\|\bm{\nabla F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}\|_{W^{q,\infty}(\Omega)},\|\bm{\nabla F}_{h_{0}}\|_{W^{q,\infty}(\hat{\Omega})},\|\det\bm{\nabla}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega})},\|\det\bm{\nabla}\bm{F}_{h_{0}}^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})

is a constant independent of the mesh size
h_{n}
and the spline degree
p
.

Note that the result from
4.2
differs from the convergence results obtained for sparse-grid approximations defined on tensor-product domains, such as
\hat{\Omega}
.
In those cases,
4.4
—which can equivalently be expressed in terms of the seminorm instead of the full norm—shows that the
H^{r}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})
seminorm of the interpolation error is controlled by the
H^{q}_{\mathrm{mix}}(\hat{\Omega})
seminorm of the solution, for
0\leq r\leq q
.
However, for non-tensor-product domains constructed through a geometric mapping

, controlling the
H^{r}(\Omega)
seminorm of the interpolation error requires controlling the
H^{dq}(\Omega)
norm of the solution, with
0\leq r\leq q

This implies that higher-order derivatives of the solution must be bounded—up to order
dq
in a single direction.
Consequently, employing sparse-grid tensor products to construct approximation spaces for non-tensor-product domains necessitates stronger regularity assumptions, particularly on isotropic (non-mixed) derivatives, compared to standard methods.

4.2.3.
Complexity of the Spaces

The main benefit of sparse-grid spaces is that they retain nearly the same approximation properties as standard tensor-product spaces, as demonstrated in
4.2
, while significantly reducing the number of degrees of freedom.
This can be formalized by the following result concerning the dimension of the sparse-grid approximation space.

Proposition 4.1
.

The dimension of the sparse-grid B-spline approximation space satisfies

\dim S_{p,h_{n}}^{\mathscr{L}}(\Omega)\lesssim(h_{n}^{-1})|\log h_{n}|^{d-1}.

Proof of
4.1
.

The dimension of the physical domain approximation space coincides with that of the parameter domain space. To estimate this dimension, we count the number of basis functions involved in the combination:

(63)

\displaystyle\dim S_{p,h_{n}^{\mathscr{L}}}(\hat{\Omega})\leq\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\Bigl|(-1)^{l}\Bigr|\left|\binom{d-1}{l}\right|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\bm{\ell}|_{1}=n+(d-1)\lambda_{p}-l,\\
\ell_{j}\geq\lambda_{p}\end{subarray}}\dim S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega}).

Since all of the basis functions of
S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})
, for a given level
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, are linearly independent we have:

(64)

\displaystyle\dim S_{p,h_{\bm{\ell}}}(\hat{\Omega})=\prod_{l=1}^{d}(h_{\ell_{l}}^{-1}+p).

Substituting
Eq.

64
into
Eq.

63
, we obtain:

\dim S_{p,h_{n}^{\mathscr{L}}}(\hat{\Omega})\leq h_{n}^{-1}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\binom{d-1}{l}\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l}{d-1}(1+p)^{d}2^{-[(d-1)\lambda_{p}-l]}.

We conclude with the estimation

\binom{n+d-1-\lambda_{p}-l}{d-1}\leq\binom{n+d-2-l}{d-1}\lesssim|\log h_{n}|^{d-1}.

∎

For comparison, consider the full tensor-product space obtained by refining the univariate spline spaces uniformly in all coordinate directions.
From
3.1
and
4.7
, for all
u\in H^{q}(\Omega)
, we have

(65)

\displaystyle\|(\operatorname{I}-\Pi_{p,h_{n}})\,u\|_{H^{r}(\Omega)}\lesssim h_{n}^{q-r}\|u\|_{H^{q}(\Omega)},

where
\Pi_{p,h_{n}}
denotes the
H^{r}
-orthogonal projection onto
S_{p,h_{n}}(\Omega)
.

When sparse-grid spaces are employed instead of full tensor-product spaces, the approximation error deteriorates only mildly—by a factor of
|\log h_{n}|^{d-1}
—which becomes negligible as the mesh is refined (
n\to\infty
).
This mild deterioration is more than compensated by the significant reduction in the number of degrees of freedom.
Indeed, the dimension of the full tensor-product space is given by

(66)

\displaystyle\dim S_{p,h_{n}}(\Omega)=(h_{n}^{-1}+p)^{d},

so the relative computational advantage of sparse grids increases rapidly with the spatial dimension
d
.

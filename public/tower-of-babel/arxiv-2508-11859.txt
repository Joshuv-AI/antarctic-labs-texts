Sharp upper bounds on hitting probabilities for the solution to the stochastic heat equation on the line
Robert C. Dalang, David Nualart, Fei Pu

Abstract
For Gaussian random fields with values in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , sharp upper and lower bounds on the probability of hitting a fixed set have been available for many years. These apply in particular to the solutions of systems of linear SPDEs. For non-Gaussian random fields, the available bounds are less sharp. For nonlinear systems of stochastic heat equations, a sharp lower bound was obtained in a previous paper by two of the authors. Here, we obtain the corresponding sharp upper bound. The proof requires a bound on the joint probability density function of a two-dimensional random vector whose components are the solution to the nonlinear stochastic heat equation and the supremum over a small rectangle of the solution to the linear stochastic heat equation, in terms of the size of the rectangle. This bound makes use of a formula that expresses the density of a locally nondegenerate random vector as an iterated Skorohod integral. The main effort is to estimate, using Malliavin calculus, each of the terms that arise from this formula.



1
Introduction

Let
d
and
k
be positive integers. Consider an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued continuous random field
U=(U(x),\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k})
. Fix a compact subset
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
with positive Lebesgue measure. The
range of
U
over
I
(also termed the sample path
x\mapsto U(x)
,
x\in I
) is the random compact set

U(I)=\{U(x),\ x\in I\}.

Hitting probabilities for
U
refer to the probabilities of events of the form
\{U(I)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}
,
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. An interesting problem is to determine good upper and lower bounds on these probabilities, in terms of quantities related to the deterministic subset
A
. In particular, a point
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is said to be
polar
for
U
if
\mathrm{P}\{\exists x\in I:U(x)=z\}=0
.

Many results on this type of question are available for Brownian motion and classical Markov processes: see for instance
[
13
, Chapter 10]
and the references therein. For multiparameter processes, fewer results are available. For the
k
-parameter Brownian sheet
W=(W(x),\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{k})
with values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, Khoshnevisan and Shi
[
15
]
established the following result (see also
[
13
, Chapter 12]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
M>0
and let
I
be a box.
There exists
0<c<\infty
such that for all compact sets
A\subset[-M,M]^{d}

(\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

c^{-1}\,\mbox{\rm Cap}_{d-2k}(A)\leq\mathrm{P}\{W(I)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}\leq c\,\mbox{\rm Cap}_{d-2k}(A).

(1.1)

In this theorem,
\mbox{Cap}_{\beta}(A)
denotes the
Bessel-Riesz capacity
of
A
of index
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
13
, Chapter 10, Section 3, p.376]
for a definition). In particular, if
A=\{z\}
is a single point in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then
\mbox{Cap}_{\beta}(\{z\})=0
for
\beta\geq 0
and
\mbox{Cap}_{\beta}(\{z\})=1
for
\beta\ <0
, therefore points are polar in dimensions
d\geq 2k
and are non-polar when
d<2k
.

Typically, for
d=1,2,\dots
, given
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued
k
-parameter random fields that consist of
d
i.i.d. copies of a given real-valued random field, there is a
critical dimension

Q(k)
such that points are polar if
d>Q(k)
and are
non-polar
if
d<Q(k)
. Deciding whether or not points are polar in the critical dimension
Q(k)
is usually a difficult problem. Theorem
1.1
answers this question for the Brownian sheet.

A more general class of
anisotropic
Gaussian random fields was considered in
[
3
]
. Let
(V(x),\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k})
be a centered continuous Gaussian random field with values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and with i.i.d. components:
V(x)=(V_{1}(x),\dots,V_{d}(x))
, and let
I
be a box. Suppose that there exists
0<c<\infty
and
H_{1},\dots,H_{k}\in(0,1)
such that for all
x,y\in I
,

c^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|x_{\ell}-y_{\ell}|^{H_{\ell}}\leq\|V_{1}(x)-V_{1}(y)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq c\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}|x_{\ell}-y_{\ell}|^{H_{\ell}}.

(1.2)

The following result was established in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume condition (
1.2
) (and some additional non-degeneracy conditions). Fix
M>0
. Set

Q=\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{1}{H_{\ell}}.

Then there is
0<c<\infty
such that for every compact set
A\subset[-M,M]^{d}
,

c^{-1}\,\mbox{\rm Cap}_{d-Q}(A)\leq\mathrm{P}\{V(I)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}\leq c\,\mathscr{H}_{d-Q}(A).

(1.3)

In this theorem, for
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\mathscr{H}_{\beta}(A)
denotes the
\beta
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(see
[
13
, Appendix C]
). If
V_{1}
is a
k
-parameter Brownian sheet, then for
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, each coordinate mapping
x_{\ell}\mapsto W(x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell},\dots,x_{k})
is essentially a Brownian motion, therefore
H_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}
and
Q=2k
, so the dimension
\beta=d-Q
in (
1.3
) matches the index
\beta=d-2k
in (
1.1
). However, in the critical dimension
d=Q
(assuming that
Q
is an integer), the
0
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of a singleton
A=\{z\}
,
z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, is
1
, therefore, (
1.3
) is not sufficient to decide whether or not points are polar in the critical dimension
d=Q
. Nevertheless, Theorem
1.2
seems to be the sharpest result available for a wide class of Gaussian random fields.

We now recall the results that are available for the stochastic heat equation in spatial dimension
1
. Let
V=(V(t,x),\ (t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})
be an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued random field that solves a
linear system
of stochastic heat equations, namely

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}V(t,x)+\dot{W}(t,x),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\ t>0,

(1.4)

where
V(0,*):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is given and
\dot{W}(t,x)
denotes
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued (Gaussian) space-time white noise (see
[
8
, Chapter 1]
). Mueller and Tribe
[
19
]
established the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

For the random field
V
, the critical dimension for hitting points is
d=6
and points are polar in this dimension.

A result analogous to Theorem
1.2
was established for
V
by Dalang, Khoshnevisan and E. Nualart
[
5
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix
M>0
and two non-trivial compact intervals
I\subset(0,T]
and
J\subset{\mathbb{R}}
. There is
0<c<\infty
such that for every compact set
A\subset[-M,M]^{d}
,

c^{-1}\,\mbox{\rm Cap}_{d-6}(A)\leq\mathrm{P}\{V(I\times J)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}\leq c\,\mathscr{H}_{d-6}(A).

(1.5)

It is well-known
[
8
, Section 3.2.2]
that
(t,x)\mapsto V(t,x)
is jointly Hölder continuous with exponents
(1/4,1/2)
, therefore
Q=(1/4)^{-1}+(1/2)^{-1}=6
and the presence of the dimension
d-6
can also be deduced from Theorem
1.2
.

We now consider the related
nonlinear system
of stochastic heat equations. Let
U=(U(t,x),\,(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})
be an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued random field that solves

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}U(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}U(t,x)+\sigma(U(t,x))\cdot\dot{W}(t,x),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\ t>0,

(1.6)

where
U(0,*):{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is given,
\dot{W}(t,x)
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued space-time white noise and
{\sigma}=(\sigma_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
:
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to\mathcal{M}_{d\times d}
is a smooth matrix-valued function.

Under the assumption that
\sigma
is
uniformly elliptic,
the following result for
U
was established by Dalang, Khoshnevisan and E. Nualart
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix
\eta>0
,
M>0
and two non-trivial compact intervals
I\subset(0,T]
and
J\subset{\mathbb{R}}
.
There exists
c>0
(depending in particular on
\eta
) such that for all compact sets
A\subseteq[-M,M]^{d}
,

c^{-1}\,\textnormal{Cap}_{d-6+\eta}(A)\leq\mathrm{P}\{U(I\times J)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}\leq c\,\mathscr{H}_{d-6-\eta}(A).

(1.7)

This result is similar to that of Theorem
1.4
for the Gaussian solution to the linear stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) (
1.4
), but it is less sharp because of the additional
\pm\eta
on the left- and right-hand sides of (
1.7
). For many years, it remained unclear whether or not (
1.7
) could be improved. In particular, is the extra
\pm\eta
in (
1.7
) needed for the nonlinear SPDE (
1.6
)?

For the lower bound in (
1.7
), this question was finally settled in
[
21
]
(see also
[
7
]
), where the following result was established.

Theorem 1.6
.

Under the same hypotheses as in Theorem
1.5
(nonlinear system of stochastic heat equations), there is
c>0
such that for all compact sets
A\subset[-M,M]^{d}
,

c^{-1}\,\textnormal{Cap}_{d-6}(A)\leq\mathrm{P}\{U(I\times J)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}.

(1.8)

The main objective in this paper is to improve the upper bound in (
1.7
), so as to obtain an upper bound that matches that in (
1.5
).

A step in this direction was made in
[
21
, Chapter 4]
, where it was shown that it would be sufficient to obtain suitable bounds on the joint probability density function of
(F_{1}^{u},F_{2}^{u})
,
where

F_{1}^{u}=u(t_{0},x_{0})\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{2}^{u}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+\zeta_{1}\\
x_{0}\leq x\leq x_{0}+\zeta_{2}\end{subarray}}(u(t,x)-u(t_{0},x_{0})).

where
u
is the solution to the stochastic heat equation with multiplicative noise (see (
2.1
)).
This program was (partly) carried out for
v
instead of
u
in
[
7
]
, where
v
is the solution to the stochastic heat equation with additive noise (see (
2.4
)).

Here, we do not seek the required estimates for the joint probability density function of
(F_{1}^{u},F_{2}^{u})
, but we establish analogous estimates for
(F_{1}^{u},F_{2}^{v})
, where

F_{2}^{v}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+\zeta_{1}\\
x_{0}\leq x\leq x_{0}+\zeta_{2}\end{subarray}}(v(t,x)-v(t_{0},x_{0})).

We derive a formula (involving iterated Skorohod integrals: see Remark
5.4
) for the probability density function of
(F_{1}^{u},F_{2}^{v})
based on a general criterion in
[
9
]
for locally nondegenerate random variables and establish a Gaussian-type upper bound on this density function.
Then we show that these estimates are sufficient to obtain the sharp upper bound

\mathrm{P}\{U(I\times J)\in A\}\leq C\mathscr{H}_{d-6}(A).

Together with (
1.8
), this improves (
1.7
) and gives sharp upper and lower bounds for hitting probabilities of
U
that match those for
V
in (
1.5
).

The fact that it is sufficient to obtain bounds on the joint density of
(F_{1}^{u},F_{2}^{v})
comes from the fact that increments of
u
and of
v
are not very different. Indeed, we show that for
p\geq 1
and
(t_{0},x_{0})
in a compact subset
I\times J
of
(0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{0}+2^{-4n}\\
x_{0}\leq x\leq x_{0}+2^{-2n}\end{subarray}}|u(t,x)-u(t_{0},x_{0})-\sigma(u(t_{0},x_{0}))(v(t,x)-v(t_{0},x_{0}))|\right)^{p}\,\right]\leq C_{T,p}\,2^{-\frac{3}{2}pn}

(see Proposition
2.2
below).

Main results

Let
u=(u(t,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}})
be the solution (in the sense of
[
22
]
; see also
[
8
, Chapter 4]
) of the nonlinear stochastic heat equation with multiplicative noise

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(t\,,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}u(t\,,x)+\sigma(u(t\,,x))\dot{W}(t\,,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
u(0,x)=0,\hskip 177.06128ptx\in{\mathbb{R}},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\dot{W}=(\dot{W}(t,x))
denotes a space-time white noise and
\sigma:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. The standing assumptions on
\sigma
are that
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous and such that
\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}}|\sigma(z)|>0
. Since
z\mapsto\sigma(z)
is a continuous function, we can assume that

\inf_{z\in{\mathbb{R}}}\sigma(z)>0.

(2.2)

This condition implies in particular that the solution
u
belongs to
{\mathbb{D}}^{1,p}
(see
[
20
, Theorem 2.4.3 and Proposition 2.4.4]
for this result and for the definition of
{\mathbb{D}}^{1,p}
).

Let
(U(t\,,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}})
be an
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued stochastic process whose coordinates are independent copies of the solution to (
2.1
). Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Fix
T>0
and
M>0
. Let
I\subset(0,T]
and
J\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be two fixed compact intervals with positive length. Suppose in addition that
\sigma
and its derivatives up to order 3 are bounded (in absolute value) by
\Sigma>0
. Then there exists a finite constant
c=c(I,J,M,\Sigma)>0
such that for all compact sets
A\subset[-M,M]^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{U(I\times J)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}\leq c\,\mathscr{H}_{d-6}(A).

(2.3)

In the proof of this theorem (which is given at the end of this section), we will compare the process
u=(u(t,x))
with the Gaussian process
v=(v(t,x))
that is the solution of the linear stochastic heat equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v(t\,,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}v(t\,,x)+\dot{W}(t\,,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
v(0,x)=0,\hskip 130.08621ptx\in{\mathbb{R}},\end{cases}

(2.4)

with the same space-time white-noise
\dot{W}
as in (
2.1
). It turns out that the increments of the process
v
are a good approximation of the increments of
u
. Indeed, let
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times J
. If we define a random variable
L(t,x)
by the relationship

u(t,x)-u(t_{0},x_{0})=\sigma(u(t_{0},x_{0}))(v(t,x)-v(t_{0},x_{0}))+L(t,x),

(2.5)

then
L(t,x)
is small in the sense of the next proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose only that
\sigma
is Lipschitz continuous. For
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}>0
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times J
, let

R^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}_{t_{0},x_{0}}=[t_{0},t_{0}+\zeta_{1}]\times[x_{0},x_{0}+\zeta_{2}].

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}=\sup_{(t,x)\in R^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}_{t_{0},x_{0}}}|L(t,x)|.

Then for all
p\geq 1
, there is a finite constant
C_{T,p}
such that, for all
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in(0,1]
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times J
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{X}_{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}\|_{p}\leq C_{T,p}\left[\max\left(\zeta_{1}^{1/4},\zeta_{2}^{1/2}\right)\right]^{3/2}.

The proof of this proposition is given in Section
3
.

A general result on hitting probabilities

Hitting probabilities for
U
are closely related to properties of increments of
u
, as one can see for instance from
[
8
, Proposition 6.5.1]
. In fact, we have the following general result that can be used to relate upper bounds on hitting probabilities for the
non-Gaussian
random field
U
to estimates on the probability density function of the supremum of certain increments of the
Gaussian
random field
v
.

Let
u=(u(t,x))
and
v=(v(t,x))
be arbitrary continuous real-valued random fields. Let

U=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{d})=(U(t,x),\,(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}})

be a random field with values in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and with i.i.d. components, each with the law of
u
. Let
I\subset(0,T]
and
J\subset\mathbb{R}
be two compact intervals with positive length.

Theorem 2.3
.

Fix
0\leq C<\infty
. For
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times J
, let
X_{t_{0},x_{0}}
be a random variable such that
|X_{t_{0},x_{0}}|\leq C
a.s. Suppose that the following properties hold:

(1) There are
H_{1},H_{2}>0
and
\alpha>1
such that for all large
p,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times J
, there is
C_{T,p}<\infty
with the following property: setting

R_{t_{0},x_{0}}^{(n)}=\left[t_{0},t_{0}+2^{-nH_{1}^{-1}}\right]\times\left[t_{0},t_{0}+2^{-nH_{2}^{-1}}\right],

L_{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)=u(t,x)-u(t_{0},x_{0})-X_{t_{0},x_{0}}\,(v(t,x)-v(t_{0},x_{0})),

(2.6)

and

\mathcal{X}_{n}=\mathcal{X}_{n}(t_{0},x_{0})=\sup_{(t,x)\in R^{(n)}_{t_{0},x_{0}}}|L_{t_{0},x_{0}}(t,x)|,

(2.7)

we have

\mathrm{E}\left[(\mathcal{X}_{n})^{p}\right]\leq C_{T,p}\,2^{-\alpha pn};

(2.8)

(2) for all
(t,x)\in I\times J
,
u(t,x)
has an absolutely continuous probability law, and for all
M\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the probability density function
p_{t,x}(z)
of
u(t,x)
is bounded uniformly over
(t,x)\in I\times J
and
z\in[-M,M]
;

(3) there exists a constant
c=c(I,J)
such that for all
(t_{0},x_{0})\in I\times J
and
\eta>0
sufficiently small, the
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
-valued random vectors

\displaystyle\left(u(t_{0},x_{0}),\sup_{(t,x)\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\eta^{H_{1}^{-1}}]\times[x_{0},x_{0}+\eta^{H_{2}^{-1}}]}(v(t,x)-v(t_{0},x_{0}))\right),

and

\displaystyle\left(-u(t_{0},x_{0}),\sup_{(t,x)\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\eta^{H_{1}^{-1}}]\times[x_{0},x_{0}+\eta^{H_{2}^{-1}}]}(-v(t,x)+v(t_{0},x_{0}))\right)

have joint probability density functions, denoted by
p_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
p_{-}(\cdot,\cdot)
respectively, that satisfy, for all
z_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
z_{2}\geq\eta
,

\displaystyle p_{\pm}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{\eta}\exp\left(-\frac{z_{2}^{2}}{c\,\eta^{2}}\right).

(2.9)

Then there exists a constant
C=C(I,J,M)
such that for all compact sets
A\subset[-M,M]^{d}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{U(I\times J)\cap A\neq\emptyset\}\leq C\,\mathscr{H}_{d-H_{1}^{-1}-H_{2}^{-1}}(A).

(2.10)

This theorem is proved in Section
4

p_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)=p_{F}(\cdot,\cdot)

2.9
) holds for
p_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
by (
2.12
).
In order to obtain (
2.9
) for
p_{-}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{u}=-u,\quad\tilde{v}=-v,\quad\tilde{W}=-W

and
\tilde{\sigma}(z)=\sigma(-z)
, for all
z\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
satisfy respectively

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{u}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\tilde{u}(t,x)+\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{u}(t\,,x))\dot{\tilde{W}}(t\,,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
\tilde{u}(0)\equiv 0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{v}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}\tilde{v}(t,x)+\dot{\tilde{W}}(t\,,x),\quad t>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}},\\
\tilde{v}(0)\equiv 0.\end{cases}

Theorem
2.4
applies to the processes
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{v}
and hence gives rise to the bound (
2.9
) for
p_{-}(\cdot,\cdot)
. This shows that Hypothesis (3) of Theorem
2.3
holds.

Finally, we conclude from Theorem
2.3
that (
2.10
) holds with
H_{1}=1/4
and
H_{2}=1/2
, and this is (
2.3
) since
(1/4)^{-1}+(1/2)^{-1}=6
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is also possible to consider systems of stochastic heat equations of the form

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}U(t\,,x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}U(t\,,x)+\sigma(U(t\,,x))\cdot\dot{W}(t\,,x)+b(U(t\,,x)),

\dot{{W}}

to

in this case also.

. Throughout the paper, we write “

3
Proving Proposition
2.2

We first develop the main arguments and then we state and prove two technical lemmas that are needed in the proof.

3.1
The main arguments

Step 1:
For
p\geq 2
, we are going to estimate the
L^{p}
-norm of the increments of the process
L
defined in (
2.5
). Let
(t,x),(s,y)\in R^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}_{t_{0},x_{0}}
and define
\eta=\max(\zeta_{1}^{1/4},\zeta_{2}^{1/2})
, so that
\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\leq\eta^{6}
. We can write

\displaystyle L(t,x)-L(s,y)

\displaystyle=u(t,x)-u(s,y)-\sigma(u(t_{0},x_{0}))(v(t,x)-v(s,y))

\displaystyle=A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4},

where

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=u(t,x)-u(t,y)-\sigma(u(t,y))\,[v(t,x)-v(t,y)],

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=u(t,y)-u(s,y)-\sigma(u(s,y))\,[v(t,y)-v(s,y)],

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=[\sigma(u(t,y))-\sigma(u(t_{0},x_{0}))]\,[v(t,x)-v(t,y)],

\displaystyle A_{4}

\displaystyle=[\sigma(u(s,y))-\sigma(u(t_{0},x_{0}))]\,[v(t,y)-v(s,y)].

From Lemma
3.2
below, replacing
x_{0}
by
y
, we obtain

\mathrm{E}[|A_{1}|^{p}|]\leq C_{T,p}\,|x-y|^{\frac{3p}{4}}\leq C_{T,p}\,\zeta_{2}^{\frac{p}{4}}\,|x-y|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{\frac{p}{2}}\,|x-y|^{\frac{p}{2}}.

From Lemma
3.1
below, replacing
t_{0}
by
s
and
x
by
y
, we obtain

\mathrm{E}[|A_{2}|^{p}|]\leq C_{T,p}\,|t-s|^{\frac{2p}{5}}\leq C_{T,p}\,\zeta_{1}^{\frac{3p}{20}}\,|t-s|^{\frac{p}{4}}\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{\frac{3p}{5}}\,|t-s|^{\frac{p}{4}}.

For the terms
A_{3}
and
A_{4}
, we use the Lipschitz continuity of
\sigma
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Hölder continuity properties of
u
and
v

[
8
, Proposition 4.3.2]
, to see that

\mathrm{E}[|A_{3}|^{p}|]\leq C_{T,p}\left(|t-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{4}}+|y-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{p}|x-y|^{\frac{p}{2}}\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{p}\,|x-y|^{\frac{p}{2}}

and

\mathrm{E}[|A_{4}|^{p}|]\leq C_{T,p}\left(|s-t_{0}|^{\frac{1}{4}}+|y-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{p}|t-s|^{\frac{p}{4}}\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{p}\,|t-s|^{\frac{p}{4}}.

Putting these bounds together and using the notation

\Delta((t,x);(s,y))=\max\left(|t-s|^{\frac{1}{4}},|x-y|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right),

(3.1)

we obtain

\mathrm{E}[|L(t,x)-L(s,y)|^{p}]\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{\frac{p}{2}}\Delta((t,x);(s,y))^{p}.

(3.2)

Step 2:
We will use a version of the Garsia-Rodemich-Rumsey lemma (see
[
8
, Lemma A.3.5]
). We apply this proposition to the process
L
with
S=R_{t_{0},x_{0}}^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}
,
\rho((t,x);(s,y))=\Delta^{2}((t,x);(s,y))
,
\mu(\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x)=\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x
,

\Psi(x)=|x|^{p}
,
\Psi^{-1}(y)=y^{1/p}
and
p(x)=|x|^{\alpha+\frac{6}{p}}
, where
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{6}{p}\right)
(as in
[
5
, Corollary A.3]
). This requires
p>12
(so that
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{6}{p}>0
), but it suffices to establish the proposition for
p\geq p_{0}
, for some
p_{0}\geq 1
.

Define

\mathscr{C}=\int_{S}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\int_{S}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\,\frac{|L(t,x)-L(s,y)|^{p}}{[\Delta^{2}((t,x);(s,y)]^{6+\alpha p}}.

By (
3.2
), we can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\mathscr{C}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{S}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}x\int_{S}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\,[\Delta((t,x);(s,y)]^{p-12-2\alpha p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{\frac{p}{2}}\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta_{1}}\mathrm{d}u\int_{0}^{\zeta_{2}}\mathrm{d}v\,(u^{1/2}+v)^{\frac{1}{2}(p-12-2\alpha p)}.

The double integral is bounded above by
C\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\eta^{p-2\alpha p-12}\leq C\eta^{p-2\alpha p-6}
, therefore,

\mathrm{E}[\mathscr{C}]\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{(\frac{3}{2}-2\alpha)p}.

(3.3)

From
[
8
, Proposition A.3.5]
, we deduce that for
(t,x),(s,y)\in R_{t_{0},x_{0}}^{(n)}
,

\displaystyle|L(t,x)-L(s,y)|\leq 10\sup_{(r,z)\in R^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}_{t_{0},x_{0}}}\int_{0}^{2\Delta^{2}((t,x);(s,y))}\Psi^{-1}\left(\frac{\mathscr{C}}{[\mu(B_{\rho}((r,z),u/4)]^{2}}\right)u^{\alpha+\frac{6}{p}-1}\,\mathrm{d}u,

where
B_{\rho}((r,z),u/4)
denotes the open ball in
R_{t_{0},x_{0}}^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}
centered at
(r,z)
of radius
u/4
in the metric
\Delta^{2}
. One can check that there is a constant
c>0
such that
\mu(B_{\rho}((r,z),u/4)])\geq cu^{3}
, for all
u\in[0,\eta^{2}]
and

(r,z)\in R^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}_{t_{0},x_{0}}
. Since
\Delta((t,x),(s,y))\leq\eta
,

\displaystyle|L(t,x)-L(s,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{2\Delta^{2}((t,x);(s,y))}\mathscr{C}^{1/p}u^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}u\asymp\mathscr{C}^{1/p}\,[\Delta^{2}((t,x);(s,y))]^{\alpha}.

In particular, taking
(s,y)=(t_{0},x_{0})
, we obtain

|L(t,x)|\lesssim\mathscr{C}^{1/p}\,[\Delta^{2}((t,x);(t_{0},x_{0}))]^{\alpha}\leq\mathscr{C}^{1/p}\,\eta^{2\alpha}.

Using (
3.3
), this implies

\mathrm{E}\left[\sup_{(t,x)\in R^{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}}_{t_{0},x_{0}}}|L(t,x)|^{p}\right]\leq C_{T,p}\,\eta^{\frac{3}{2}p},

which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

3.2
Two technical lemmas

Recall the mild form of the solution to (
2.1
):

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)\sigma(u(s\,,y))W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y),

(3.4)

where
G(t,x)=(2\pi t)^{-1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}/(2t)}
and
W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)
denotes the Itô-Walsh stochastic integral (see
[
22
,
8
]
).
The solution to (
2.4
) is given by

\displaystyle v(t\,,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-s,x-y)W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

Increments of
u
be can approximated in various ways by those of
v
; see
[
16
,
10
,
12
]
. The following lemma on the approximation of temporal increments of
u
by those of
v
is taken from
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
16
, Theorem 4.1]

Assume only that
\sigma
is Lipschitz. For
T>0
and
p\geq 2
, there exists
A_{T,p}>0
such that for all
t,t_{0}\in[0,T]
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\|u(t\,,x)-u(t_{0}\,,x)-\sigma(u(t_{0}\,,x))(v(t\,,x)-v(t_{0}\,,x))\|_{p}\leq A_{T,p}|t-t_{0}|^{2/5}.

Using ideas similar to those in
[
16
,
10
]
, we obtain the following result on the approximation of spatial increments of
u
by those of
v
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume only that
\sigma
is Lipschitz. For
T>t_{0}>0
and
p\geq 2
, there exists a constant
C=C_{T,t_{0},p}>0
such that for all
t\in[t_{0},T]
and
x,x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\left\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})-\sigma(u(t\,,x_{0}))(v(t,x)-v(t,x_{0}))\right\|_{p}\leq C|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Proof.

Because
\|u(t,x)\|_{p}
and
\|v(t,x)\|_{p}
are bounded over
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}

[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
and because
\sigma
has linear growth, it suffices to prove the above estimate for
x,x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|x-x_{0}|\leq 1\wedge t_{0}
. Hence we assume
|x-x_{0}|\leq 1\wedge t_{0}
in the following.

According to (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))\sigma(u(s\,,y))W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

For
\varepsilon<\sqrt{t_{0}}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})=\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))\sigma(u(s\,,y))W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y),

where
B(x_{0},2\varepsilon):=[x_{0}-2\varepsilon,x_{0}+2\varepsilon]
. Then

\displaystyle\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})-\mathcal{A}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})\|^{2}_{p}\lesssim I_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})+I_{2}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0}),

(3.5)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))\sigma(u(s\,,y))W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)\right\|^{2}_{p},

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))\sigma(u(s\,,y))W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y)\right\|^{2}_{p}.

Using Burkholder’s inequality, then Minkowski’s inequality, and using the facts that
\sigma
has linear growth and
\|u(t,x)\|_{p}
is bounded
[
8
, Theorem 4.2.1]
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(G(s,x-y)-G(s,x_{0}-y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Using Plancherel’s identity, we see that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathrm{e}^{ixz}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}sz^{2}}-\mathrm{e}^{ix_{0}z}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}sz^{2}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=2\int_{\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-sz^{2}}(1-\cos((x-x_{0})z))\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon^{2}z^{2}}}{z^{2}}(1-\cos((x-x_{0})z))\,\mathrm{d}z.

With the change of variables
y=(x-x_{0})z
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim 2|x-x_{0}|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-{\varepsilon}^{2}y^{2}/(x-x_{0})^{2}}\left(1-\cos y\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{y^{2}}.

Using the inequality
1-\cos y\leq\frac{1}{2}y^{2}
for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we see that

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon^{2}y^{2}/(x-x_{0})^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\frac{(x-x_{0})^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}.

Letting
\varepsilon=|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

(3.6)

Using again Burkholder’s inequality, then Minkowski’s inequality, and the facts that
\sigma
has linear growth and
\|u(t,x)\|_{p}
is bounded, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{|y-x_{0}|>2\varepsilon}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{{\varepsilon}^{2}}\int_{|z|>2{\varepsilon}}(G(s,x-x_{0}+z)-G(s,z))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s.

Do the change of variables
s={\varepsilon}^{2}r
,
z={\varepsilon}v
, to see that

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{|v|>2}\left(G\left(\varepsilon^{2}r,x-x_{0}+\varepsilon v\right)-G(\varepsilon^{2}r,\varepsilon v)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int^{1}_{0}\int_{|v|>2}\left(G\left(r,\frac{x-x_{0}}{\varepsilon}+v\right)-G(r,v)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}r,

where in the second equality, we have used the identity

\displaystyle G(\varepsilon^{2}s,\varepsilon y)=\varepsilon^{-1}G(s,y)\quad\text{for all $\varepsilon,s>0$ and $y\in{\mathbb{R}}$}.

(3.7)

Set
\beta=(x-x_{0})/\varepsilon=(x-x_{0})|x-x_{0}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{|v|>2}(G(r,\beta+v)-G(r,v))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}r.

Notice that
|\beta|\leq 1
because
|x-x_{0}|\leq 1
, and
|\beta+v|\geq 1
when
|v|>2
. Note that the heat kernel
G(r,y)
has a partial derivative in
y
that is uniformly bounded over the set
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\{|y|\geq 2\}
and satisfies

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial y}G(r,y)\right|=\frac{|y|}{r}G(r,y).

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{|v|>2}\left(\int_{v}^{v+\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}G(r,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\beta^{2}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{|v|>2}\left(\frac{|v|+1}{r}\,G(r,|v|-1)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{2}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})

\displaystyle\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

(3.8)

Therefore, we conclude from (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle\left\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})-\mathcal{A}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\right\|^{2}_{p}\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

(3.9)

For
\varepsilon<\sqrt{t_{0}}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})

\displaystyle:=\sigma(u(t-\varepsilon^{2},x_{0}))

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))W(\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}y).

If
\sigma\equiv 1
, we write
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
instead of
{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}
, and we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})=\sigma(u(t-\varepsilon^{2},x_{0})){\mathcal{B}}_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0}).

Because
\sigma
has linear growth,
\|u(t-\varepsilon^{2},x_{0})\|_{p}
is bounded above and (
2.2
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{B}_{1}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})\|^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle\asymp\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s\lesssim|x-x_{0}|,

(3.10)

by
[
8
, (4.3.10)]
. Using Burkholder’s and Minkowski’s inequalities, we see that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})-\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,\varepsilon,x,x_{0})\|_{p}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\|\sigma(u(t-\varepsilon^{2}\,,x_{0}))-\sigma(u(s\,,y))\|_{p}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\int_{t-\varepsilon^{2}}^{t}\int_{B(x_{0},2\varepsilon)}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times(|t-\varepsilon^{2}-s|^{1/2}+|x_{0}-y|)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\varepsilon\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(G(t-s,x-y)-G(t-s,x_{0}-y))^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\varepsilon\,|x-x_{0}|,

where in the second inequality, we use the Lipschitz property of
\sigma
and the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x),(s,y)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\|u(t\,,x)-u(s\,,y)\|_{p}}{|t-s|^{1/4}+|x-y|^{1/2}}<\infty

(see
[
8
, Proposition 4.3.2]
), and in the fourth inequality, we use again
[
8
, (4.3.10)]
. We take
{\varepsilon}=|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})-\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\|_{p}^{2}\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

(3.11)

Hence, we deduce from (
3.9
) and (
3.11
) that

\displaystyle\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})-\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\|^{2}_{p}\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

(3.12)

This inequality also applies to the case
\sigma\equiv 1
, which yields that

\displaystyle\left\|v(t\,,x)-v(t\,,x_{0})-{\mathcal{B}}_{1}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\right\|_{p}^{2}\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{3/2}.

(3.13)

Because
\sigma
is Lipschitz,
[
8
, Theorem 4.3.4]
implies that

\displaystyle\|\sigma(u(t-|x-x_{0}|,x_{0}))-\sigma(u(t\,,x_{0}))\|_{p}\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{1/4}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})-\sigma(u(t\,,x_{0}))(v(t,x)-v(t,x_{0}))\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,x_{0})-\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\|\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})-\sigma(u(t,x_{0})){\mathcal{B}}_{1}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\|\sigma(u(t,x_{0}))[{\mathcal{B}}_{1}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})-(v(t,x)-v(t,x_{0}))]\|_{p}.

By (

|x-x_{0}|^{3/4}
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the second term is bounded above by a constant multiple of

\displaystyle\|\sigma(u(t-|x-x_{0}|,x_{0}))-\sigma(u(t,x_{0}))\|_{2p}\ \|{\mathcal{B}}_{1}(t,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}},x,x_{0})\|_{2p}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{4}}\,|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}=|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{3}{4}},

where the inequality holds by (

The last term is bounded above by a constant multiple of

by (
3.13
).
The proof is complete.
∎

5.2
Density formula for the random vector

We begin by recalling some basic elements of Malliavin calculus, following
[
20
]
.
We associate to the space-time white noise
\dot{W}
an isonormal Gaussian process
(W(h),\,h\in\mathcal{H})
, where the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})
is endowed with its usual inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
, defined by

\displaystyle W(h)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}h(t,x)W(\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x).

The (Malliavin) derivative
DS
of a smooth random variable

\displaystyle S=f(W(h_{1}),\dots,W(h_{n})),

where
f
is a
C^{\infty}
function such that
f
and all of its derivatives have polynomial growth, is defined as in
[
20
, Section 1.2 & 1.2.1]
, as well as higher order derivatives
D^{k}S
and the seminorms

\displaystyle\|S\|_{k,p}=\left[\mathrm{E}[|S|^{p}]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathrm{E}\left[\|D^{j}S\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes j}}\right]\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

p\geq 1
,
k\geq 1
. The completion of the family of smooth random variables with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
is denoted
{\mathbb{D}}^{k,p}
.

The adjoint of the operator
D
, which we denote
\delta
, is an unbounded operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega)
. The Skorohod integral of an element
\tilde{u}
of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega)
is defined in
[
20
, Section 1.3.2]
and is denoted
\delta(\tilde{u})
. If
\tilde{u}
is adapted to the filtration generated by the space-time white noise, then the Skorohod integral is equal to the Itô-Walsh integral:

\displaystyle\delta(\tilde{u})=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{u}(t,x)W(\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x).

Concerning the solution
(u(t,x))
to (
2.1
), when
\sigma
is
k
times continuously differentiable, then
u(t,x)\in{\mathbb{D}}^{k,p}
, for all
k\geq 1
,
p\geq 1
and
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{*}\times{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
20
, Theorem 2.4.3 & Proposition 2.4.4]
and
[
6
, Proposition 4.1]
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
(r,z)\mapsto\tilde{H}(r,z)
is a continuous function on
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}
that has one generalized derivative in

and two in

, and that
there exists a constant

such that for all

\displaystyle\max\left[|\tilde{H}(r,z)|,\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{H}(r,z)}{\partial r}\right|,\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{H}(r,z)}{\partial z}\right|,\left|\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{H}(r,z)}{\partial z^{2}}\right|\right]\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-C|z|}.

Then for every

and

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(t-r,x-z)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\right)\tilde{H}(r,z)\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r=\tilde{H}(t,x).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}})
and let
\mathcal{L}
denote the heat operator:

\mathcal{L}\varphi(r,z)=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\varphi(r,z)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\varphi(r,z).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,\int_{r}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,|\mathcal{L}\tilde{H}(r,z)\varphi(t,x)G(t-r,x-z)|<\infty,

so we can apply Fubini’s theorem to see that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,\varphi(t,x)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,G(t-r,x-z)\mathcal{L}\tilde{H}(r,z)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,\mathcal{L}\tilde{H}(r,z)\left(\int_{r}^{T}\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,\varphi(t,x)G(t-r,x-z)\right).

(5.40)

Because
\psi
has compact support, and vanishes near
0
and
T
and at
x=\pm\infty
, we can integrate by parts (twice in
x
, and once in
t
), with no contribution of boundary terms, to see that (
5.2
) is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,\tilde{H}(t,x)\,\mathcal{L}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}s\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\,G(t-s,x-y)\varphi(s,y)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}x\,\tilde{H}(t,x)\varphi(t,x),

where the equality holds since the space-time convolution between heat kernel
G
and
\varphi
satisfies the inhomogenuous heat equation. This implies that for a.a.
(t,x)
,
\tilde{H}(t,x)
is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}r\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}z\,G(t-r,x-z)\mathcal{L}\tilde{H}(r,z).

Since both functions are continuous in

, the lemma is proved.

5.19
),
for
(r,z)\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u_{A,2,1}(r,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}\lesssim{\zeta}^{-2}\int_{t_{0}-{\zeta}^{2}}^{t_{0}+2{\zeta}^{2}}\int_{x_{0}-{\zeta}}^{x_{0}+2{\zeta}}(R^{-2}\|DZ_{s,y}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+R^{-1}\|D^{2}Z_{s,y}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}})\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Using (
6.30
), (
6.44
) and (
6.6
), we deduce that for
z_{2}\geq{\zeta}^{1/2}
and
p\geq 1
,

\|D^{2}u_{A,2,1}(r,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}\lesssim{\zeta}^{-2}{\zeta}^{3}\,(R^{-2}{\zeta}^{2(4+p_{0}-\gamma_{0})}+R^{-1}{\zeta}^{4+p_{0}-\gamma_{0}})\asymp\zeta.

As in the last part of the proof of Step 2a in the proof of Lemma
6.5
, we deduce that

\|D^{2}u_{A,2,1}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 3})}\lesssim{\zeta}^{5/2}.

Similarly, for
k=2,3,4
, we can use the same argument as in the estimate of
\|Du_{A,2,k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2})}

to obtain that

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u_{A,2,k}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 3})}\lesssim{\zeta}^{5/2},

for
z_{2}\geq{\zeta}^{1/2}
and
p\geq 1
.

It remains to estimate
\|D^{2}u_{A,2,5}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 3})}
. Similar to (
6.39
), we can derive that
for
(s,z)\in E
and
p\geq 1

\mathrm{E}\left[\left\|D^{2}\frac{\partial Z_{s,z}}{\partial z}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}^{p}\right]\lesssim{\zeta}^{p(p_{0}-\gamma_{0}+3)}.

(6.45)

Then for
(r,z)\in E
, using (
6.20
), we can write

\displaystyle D^{2}u_{A,2,5}(r,z)=\frac{1}{2}\eta(r,z)\bigg(\int_{r}^{t_{0}+{\zeta}^{2}}\Big[\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(Z_{s,z})DZ_{s,y}\otimes DZ_{s,y}\frac{\partial Z_{s,z}}{\partial z}

\displaystyle\quad+\psi^{\prime\prime}(Z_{s,z})D^{2}Z_{s,z}\frac{\partial Z_{s,z}}{\partial z}+2\psi^{\prime\prime}(Z_{s,z})DZ_{s,z}D\frac{\partial Z_{s,z}}{\partial z}+\psi^{\prime}(Z_{s,z})D^{2}\frac{\partial Z_{s,z}}{\partial z}\Big]\mathrm{d}s\bigg).

Thus, we can appeal to the estimates (

), (

), (

), (

), (

)

and

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u_{A,2,5}(r,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}

With (
6.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u_{A,2,5}(r,z)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}

Lemma 6.7
.

For
z_{2}\geq{\zeta}^{1/2}
,

\displaystyle\|\delta(\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})\|_{p}\lesssim{\zeta}^{5/2}.

Proof.

By Meyer’s inequality (see
[
20
, Proposition 1.5.8]
), for
p\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\|\delta(\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})\|_{p}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c_{p}\left(\|\mathrm{E}[\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|D\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2})}\right).

We write

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{E}[\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\mathrm{E}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}u_{A,1}(r_{2},v_{2})D_{r_{2},v_{2}}u_{A,2}(r_{1},v_{1})\,\mathrm{d}r_{2}\mathrm{d}v_{2}\right]\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}.

Since
u_{A,1}
is deterministic, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{E}[\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}]\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|u_{A,1}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\mathrm{E}\left[\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}[D_{r_{2},v_{2}}u_{A,2}(r_{1},v_{1})]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r_{2}\mathrm{d}v_{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\mathrm{E}\left[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}[D_{r_{2},v_{2}}u_{A,2}(r_{1},v_{1})]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r_{2}\mathrm{d}v_{2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\|Du_{A,2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2})}\lesssim{\zeta}^{5}

for
z_{2}\geq{\zeta}^{1/2}
, where the last inequality holds by (
6.29
).

Moreover, we write

\displaystyle\|D\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2})}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\|\int_{({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})^{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}}u_{A,1}(r,z)D_{r_{2},v_{2}}D_{r,z}u_{A,2}(r_{1},v_{1})\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}z\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\mathrm{d}v_{2}\right\|_{\frac{p}{2}}.

Since
u_{A,1}
is deterministic, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\|D\langle u_{A,1}(*),D_{*}u_{A,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|u_{A,1}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\left\|\int_{({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}})^{3}}[D_{r_{2},v_{2}}D_{r,z}u_{A,2}(r_{1},v_{1})]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}r_{1}\mathrm{d}v_{1}\mathrm{d}r_{2}\mathrm{d}v_{2}\right\|_{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\|u_{A,1}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\,\|D^{2}u_{A,2}\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 3})}\lesssim{\zeta}^{5}

for
z_{2}\geq{\zeta}^{1/2}
, where the last inequality holds by (
6.43
).
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 6.12
.

We have

\langle u_{A,1},D\gamma_{A}^{2,2}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

6.51
), we use (
5.23
) to write

As a consequence, (

\displaystyle\langle u_{A,1},DZ_{s,y}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

An Extension of the d d -Variate FGM Copula with Application Journal: REVSTAT
Mous-Abou Hamadou, Martial Longla

Abstract
We introduce an extended d d -variate Farlie–Gumbel–Morgenstern (FGM) copula that incorporates additional parameters based on Legendre polynomials to enhance the representation of multivariate dependence structures. Within an i.i.d. framework, we derive closed-form estimators for these parameters and establish their unbiasedness, consistency, and asymptotic normality. A simulation study illustrates the finite-sample performance of the estimators. The model is applied to the Bearing dataset , previously studied by Ota and Kimura (2021) [ 22 ] through a d d -variate FGM copula and by Longla and Mous-Abou (2025) [ 19 ] using an extended bivariate FGM copula. Our analysis shows that the classical d d -variate FGM copula does not adequately represent the dependence in this dataset. Based on estimation results and model selection criteria, we propose a reduced version of the extended model as a more appropriate copula specification for the Bearing data.

Introduction

Introduced by Sklar (1959)
[
25
]
, copulas are multivariate cumulative distribution functions with uniform marginals on the unit interval. They have become indispensable tools for capturing and modeling dependence structures among multivariate random variables, independently of their marginal distributions. Recent applications of copulas in fields such as economics, finance, risk management, and reliability engineering can be found in Long and Emura (2014)
[
17
]
, Sun et al. (2020)
[
26
]
, Ota and Kimura (2021)
[

]
, and Longla and Mous-Abou (2025)
[

]
. Additional references on copula applications can be found in Bhati and Do (2019)
[
4
]
, whereas a thorough introduction and detailed theoretical background on copulas is provided by Nelsen (2006)
[
21
]
and Joe (2014)
[
11
]
.

A well-known parametric family of copulas is the FGM family, originally introduced through contributions by Eyraud (1936)
[
7
]
, Morgenstern (1956)
[
20
]
, Farlie (1960)
[

]
, and Gumbel (1960)
[

. Copulas from this family are defined as polynomial perturbations of the independence copula. This polynomial structure facilitates closed-form expressions for key analytical quantities, including the density function, moments, and dependence measures, making the FGM family particularly appealing for practical applications. However, copulas from this family have a narrow dependence range, with Spearman’s Rho limited to
\bigl[-1/3,1/3]
making it difficult to captuire complex dependencies. Various extensions have been proposed to overcome this limitation, including but not limited to those by Huang and Kotz (1999)
[
10
]
, Lai and Xie (2000)
[
15
]
, Bairamov and Kotz (2002)
[
2

, Rodríguez-Lallena (2004)
[

, Amblard and Girard (2009)
[

, Durante et al. (2013)
[

[

, Côté and Genest (2019)
[

]
and Bekrizadeh (2022)
[

]
. More recently, Longla (2024)
[
18
]
introduced an extension of the bivariate FGM copula by incorporating an additional parameter, thereby extending the dependence range to
\bigl[-1/5,2/5\bigr]
.

Building on this, the present paper proposes an extension of the
d
-variate FGM copula. Originally examined by Joe and Xu (1996)
[
12
]
for parameter estimation, the
d
-variate FGM copula was further studied by Ota and Kimura (2021)
[
22
]
, who developed an estimation algorithm using the Inference Function for Margins (IFM) method. Their approach proved to be effective and serves as a practical alternative to MLE, which is nearly infeasible in this context due to the complexity of the parameter constraints and the number of parameters. They demonstrated the utility of their method by applying it to the
Bearing dataset
(see J. Lee et al. (2007)
[
16

However, Longla and Mous-Abou (2025)
[

Bearing dataset

[

d
-variate FGM copula fails to adequately capture the dependence structure present in the dataset. Nevertheless, their study did not incorporate a fully multivariate copula model, nor did it offer a complete alternative to address the identified shortcomings.

The proposed extended
d
-variate FGM copula introduces additional parameters that account for more complex dependence patterns. This multivariate formulation enables a more comprehensive assessment of model fit and facilitates the selection of appropriate copula specifications tailored to the dependence structure observed in the
Bearing dataset
.

1.1
Definitions, Conventions and Notations

A
d
-variate copula is a function
C:[0,1]^{d}\to[0,1]
satisfying:

1.

C(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}0&\text{if }u_{j}=0\text{ for some }j=1,\cdots,d,\\
u_{j}&\text{if }u_{k}=1\text{ for all }k\neq j,k=1,\cdots,d.\end{array}\right.

2.

C
is
d
-increasing on
[0,1]^{d}
. In other terms , for any hyper-rectangle
\prod_{j=1}^{d}[u_{j1},u_{j2}]\subset[0,1]^{d}
, where
0\leq u_{j1}<u_{j2}\leq 1
, the C-volume satisfies

\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{2}\cdots\sum_{i_{d}=1}^{2}(-1)^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{d}}C(u_{1i_{1}},\dots,u_{di_{d}})\geq 0.

The
d
-variate FGM copula, denoted by
C_{d}^{FGM}
, is defined as follows (see Nelsen, 2006
[
21
]
; Johnson and Kotz, 1995
[
13
]
):

C^{FGM}_{d}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d};\Lambda)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}u_{i}\bigg(1+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{d}\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq d}\lambda_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(1-u_{j_{i}})\bigg),

(1)

where
(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d})\in[0,1]^{d}
and
\lambda_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}}\in\Lambda
are parameters defining the dependence structure, subject to the constraint:

1+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{d}\sum\limits_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq d}\lambda_{j_{1}\cdots j_{k}}\varepsilon_{j_{1}}\cdots\varepsilon_{j_{k}}\geq 0,\quad\{\varepsilon_{j_{1}},\dots,\varepsilon_{j_{k}}\}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}.

Throughout this paper, the set
D=\{1,2,\dots,d\}
denotes the index and for any positive integer
j
, we write
\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)
to represent the collection of all subsets of
D
with cardinality
j
, and
|M|
denotes the cardinality of a set
M
. Indicator functions are denoted by
\mathbf{1}_{A}
, which equals 1 if the condition
A
is true and 0 otherwise. To maintain consistency in expressions involving empty products or subsets, we adopt the convention that
0^{0}=1
. Unless otherwise specified, all vectors are treated as column vectors, and the transpose of a vector
x
is denoted by
x^{\top}
. We use
\mathbf{0}
to denote the zero vector of the appropriate dimension.

2
The
d
-Variate Extended FGM Copula

Consider a random vector
\textbf{U}=(U_{1},\dots,U_{d})
following a
d
-variate extended FGM copula with uniform marginals on
[0,1]
. The joint distribution function of the
d
-variate extended FGM copula is given by

\displaystyle C(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[U_{1}\leq u_{1},\cdots,U_{d}\leq u_{d}]

(2)

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}u_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\,\prod_{m\notin M}u_{m}\,\prod_{m\in M}\Phi_{k}(u_{m}),~~~

\text{where }(u_{1},\cdots,u_{d})\in[0,1]^{d},\Phi_{1}(x)=\sqrt{3}(x^{2}-x)\text{ and }~\Phi_{2}(x)=\sqrt{5}(2x^{3}-3x^{2}+x).

Moreover, the parameters
\lambda^{(k)}_{M}
for
k=1,2
satisfy the constraint

\sum_{j=2}^{d}\bigg[\sqrt{3}^{j}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)}\,|\lambda^{(1)}_{M}|+\sqrt{5}^{|j|}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)}\,|\lambda^{(2)}_{M}|\bigg]\leq 1.

The corresponding joint density is then given by

c(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})=1+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\,\prod_{m\in M}\phi_{k}(u_{m}).

(3)

Where

\phi_{k}(x)=\tfrac{\partial\Phi_{k}(x)}{\partial x},\text{ for }~k=1,2.

Each parameter represents a joint moment of the transformed variables, capturing dependence in the direction of the associated orthogonal function over the subset of variables indexed by
M
. In particular, when the subset has two elements, the parameter equals the correlation between those variables.

The
d
-variate extended FGM copula includes
\sum\limits_{j=2}^{d}2\binom{d}{j}=2^{d+1}-2d-2
parameters.

For instance, when
d=4
, the model involves 22 parameters, and the copula density is given by

\displaystyle c(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})=1+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\mathbf{v}^{\top}_{k}\Lambda^{(k)}.

(4)

Where for each
k
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,2\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,3\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,4\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{2,3\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{2,4\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{3,4\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,2,3\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,2,4\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,3,4\}},

(5)

\displaystyle\lambda^{(k)}_{\{2,3,4\}},\lambda^{(k)}_{\{1,2,3,4\}})^{\top},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{2}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{3}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{4}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{2})\phi_{k}(u_{3}),

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{k}(u_{2})\phi_{k}(u_{4}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{3})\phi_{k}(u_{4}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{2})\phi_{k}(u_{3}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{2})\phi_{k}(u_{4}),

\displaystyle\quad\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{3})\phi_{k}(u_{4}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{2})\phi_{k}(u_{3})\phi_{k}(u_{4}),\;\phi_{k}(u_{1})\phi_{k}(u_{2})\phi_{k}(u_{3})\phi_{k}(u_{4})\Big)^{\top}.

Remark 2.1
.

If
(U_{1},\dots,U_{d})
follows a copula with density given by (
3
) and
P\subseteq D
with
|P|\geq 2
, then the density of the distribution of the subvector
{\bf U}=(U_{s})_{s\in P}
is given by

c({\bf u})=1+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{|P|}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\prod_{m\in M}\phi_{k}(u_{m}).

(7)

This follows by first obtaining the marginal distribution of the subvector, which is done by setting the unused components to one. Since
\Phi_{k}(1)=0
, all terms involving those components vanish. The resulting expression is then differentiated with respect to the remaining variables to yield the marginal density.

3
Parameter Estimation and Asymptotic Distribution

This section develops estimators for the parameters of the

d
-variate extended FGM copula in (
3
) and establishes their asymptotic properties. For
k,r,z\in\{1,2\}
, we define

\mathcal{I}_{r,z}=\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{r}(u)\phi_{z}(u)\,du\quad and\quad\mathcal{I}_{k,r,z}=\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{k}(u)\phi_{r}(u)\phi_{z}(u)\,du.

Lemma 3.1
.

Due to the orthogonality of the set
\{1,\phi_{1}(x),\phi_{2}(x)\}
for
x\in[0,1]
, it’s easy to see that
\mathcal{I}_{r,z}=\mathbf{1}_{\{r=z\}}
and

\mathcal{I}_{k,r,z}=\begin{cases}\tfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{7},&k=r=z=2,\\
\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{5}},&(k,r,z)\in\{(1,1,2),(1,2,1),(2,1,1)\},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(8)

Suppose that
(U_{1},\dots,U_{d})
follow a copula with density given by (
3
). Let
P,Q\subseteq D
be two subsets such that
2\leq|P|\leq|Q|
, and define

E^{z,r}_{P,Q}=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{s\in P}\phi_{z}(U_{s})\prod_{t\in Q}\phi_{r}(U_{t})\bigg].

(9)

We establish the following result.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{d})
following a copula with density given in (
3
), and for sets
P
and
Q
, with
E^{z,r}_{P,Q}
as defined in (
9
). The following holds:

1.

\mathbb{E}\big[\prod\limits_{s\in P}\phi_{z}(U_{s})\big]=\lambda^{(z)}_{P}.

(10)

2.

If
P\subseteq Q
,

\displaystyle E^{z,r}_{P,Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{z,r}\mathbf{1}_{\{P=Q\}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\lvert Q\lvert}\sum\limits_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(Q)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\mathcal{I}_{k,r,z}^{|M\cap P|}\mathcal{I}_{k,r}^{|(Q\setminus P)\cap M|}.

(11)

3.

If
P\not\subset Q
,

\displaystyle E^{z,r}_{P,Q}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\lvert P\cup Q\lvert}\sum\limits_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P\cup Q)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\big[\mathcal{I}_{k,r,z}^{|M\cap P\cap Q|}\mathcal{I}_{k,z}^{|(P\setminus Q)\cap M|}\mathcal{I}_{k,r}^{|(Q\setminus P)\cap M|}.

(12)

Proof.

Using the density given by formula (
7
) in Remark
2.1
, we first have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{s\in P}\phi_{z}(u_{s})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\Phi_{z}(1)]^{|P|}+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{|P|}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\,\mathcal{I}_{k,z}[\Phi_{k}(1)]^{|P\setminus M|}.

Given that
\Phi_{k}(1)=0
for
k=1,2
, the expression simplifies to

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\prod_{s\in P}\phi_{z}(U_{s})\Bigg]=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{|P|}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\,\mathcal{I}_{k,z}\,\mathbf{1}_{\{P=M\}}.

(13)

Here,
\mathcal{I}_{k,z}
forces
k=z
, while
\mathbf{1}_{\{P=M\}}
imposes
M=P
. As a result, only the term
\lambda^{(z)}_{P}
remains in (
13
), thereby establishing (
10
).

On the other hand, observe that

\prod_{s\in P}\phi_{z}(u_{s})\prod_{t\in Q}\phi_{r}(u_{t})=\prod_{s\in P\cap Q}\phi_{z}(u_{s})\phi_{r}(u_{s})\prod_{s\in P\setminus Q}\phi_{z}(u_{s})\prod_{s\in Q\setminus P}\phi_{r}(u_{s})

Using the density (
7
) in Remark
2.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle E^{z,r}_{P,Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{z}(u)\phi_{r}(u)du\big)^{|P\cap Q|}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{z}(u)du\big)^{|Q\setminus P|}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{r}(u)du\big)^{|P\setminus Q|}

(14)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{|P\cup Q|}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P\cup Q)}\big[\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{k}(u)\phi_{z}(u)\phi_{r}(u)du\big)^{|M\cap P\cap Q|}

\displaystyle\hskip-8.5359pt\times\big(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{k}(u)\phi_{z}(u)du\big)^{|M\cap(Q\setminus P)|}\big(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{k}(u)\phi_{r}(u)du\big)^{|M\cap(P\setminus Q)|}\big].

Formula (
14
) further simplifies to

\displaystyle E^{z,r}_{P,Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{z,r}^{|P\cap Q|}[\Phi_{z}(1)]^{|P\setminus Q|}[\Phi_{r}(1)]^{|Q\setminus P|}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(15)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\lvert P\cup Q\lvert}\sum\limits_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P\cup Q)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\big[\mathcal{I}_{k,r,z}^{|M\cap P\cap Q|}\mathcal{I}_{k,z}^{|(P\setminus Q)\cap M|}\mathcal{I}_{k,r}^{|(Q\setminus P)\cap M|}\big].

If
P\not\subset Q
, given that
|P|\leq|Q|
, we have
P\setminus Q\neq\emptyset
. In this case,
[\Phi_{z}(1)]^{|P\setminus Q|}=0
, which causes the first term on the right-hand side of formula (
15
) to vanish. Consequently, equation (
12
) holds.

On the other hand, if
P\subseteq Q
, then
P\setminus Q=\emptyset
.
It follows that
[\Phi_{z}(1)]^{|P\setminus Q|}=1
,
\mathcal{I}_{k,z}^{|(P\setminus Q)\cap M|}=1
, and
[\Phi_{z}(1)]^{|Q\setminus P|}=\mathbf{1}_{\{P=Q\}}
. Substituting these into formula (
15
) directly yields (
11
), completing the proof.
∎

Suppose that
\{(U_{i1},\ldots,U_{id})\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a simple random sample drawn from a copula with density given in (
3
) and with uniform
\mathcal{U}[0,1]
marginals. For each integer
j
with
2\leq j\leq d
and for every subset
M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)
, define the estimator

\hat{\lambda}^{(k)}_{M}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{m\in M}\phi_{k}(U_{im}),\quad\text{for }k=1,2.

(16)

Next, let
\Lambda
be the parameter vector containing all
\lambda^{(k)}_{M}
for
k=1,2
,
j=2,\dots,d
, and
M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)
, with
\hat{\Lambda}
as its estimator. The following theorem establishes the properties of the estimator in (
16
) and the asymptotic distribution
\hat{\Lambda}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\{(U_{i1},\ldots,U_{id})\}_{i=1}^{n}
be a simple random sample generated by a copula with density given in (
3
) and uniform
\mathcal{U}[0,1]
marginals. For each
j=2,\dots,d
and
M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)
, the estimator defined in (
16
) is unbiased and consistent for
\lambda^{(k)}_{M}
. Moreover,

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\Lambda}-\Lambda)\Rightarrow N(\mathbf{0},\Sigma),

(17)

where the covariance matrix
\Sigma
has entries

\Sigma_{P,Q}^{z,r}
for
z,r=1,2
and
P\in\mathcal{M}_{p}(D),Q\in\mathcal{M}_{q}(D)
with
2\leq p,q\leq d
, given by

\Sigma_{P,Q}^{z,r}=E^{z,r}_{P,Q}-\lambda^{z}_{P}\lambda_{Q}^{r},

(18)

where
E^{z,r}_{P,Q}
is defined in Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

Given that

\{(U_{i1},\ldots,U_{id})\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an i.i.d. sequence, applying Proposition
3.2
yields

\mathbb{E}[\hat{\lambda}^{(k)}_{M}]=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\prod_{m\in M}\phi_{k}(U_{1m})\Bigg]=\lambda^{(k)}_{M},

which establishes the unbiasedness of
\hat{\lambda}^{(k)}_{M}
. Moreover, Applying Proposition
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\text{var}(\hat{\lambda}_{M}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1-{\lambda^{(k)}_{M}}^{2}}{n}+\tfrac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\lvert P\lvert}\sum\limits_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(P)}\lambda^{(k)}_{M}\mathcal{I}_{k,r,z}^{|M|}\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

So
\hat{\Lambda}
is consistent as element-wise consistency holds.

Let

t=\bigl(t^{(k)}_{M}:k=1,2;\;M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D),\;j=2,\dots,d\bigr)\in\mathbb{R}^{(2^{d+1}-2d-2)}
. By the Cramér–Wold device, to prove the convergence in (
17
) it suffices to show that

\sqrt{n}\big(t^{\prime}\hat{\Lambda}-t^{\prime}\Lambda\big)\Rightarrow N(0,t^{\prime}\Sigma t).

(19)

Using (
16
), we express

t^{\prime}\hat{\Lambda}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\text{ where }X_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{4}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)}t_{k,M}\prod_{m\in M}\phi_{k}(U_{im}).

Since
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an i.i.d. sequence as functions of an i.i.d. sample, applying Proposition
3.2
gives

\mathbb{E}[X_{1}]=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\sum_{M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)}t_{k,M}\lambda_{M}^{(k)}=t^{\prime}\Lambda\quad\text{ and}

\displaystyle\text{var}(X_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=2}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)\\
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{j^{\prime}}(D)\end{subarray}}t^{(k)}_{M}t^{(k^{\prime})}_{M^{\prime}}\text{cov}\big(\prod_{m\in M}\phi_{k}(U_{1m}),\prod_{m\in M^{\prime}}\phi_{k^{\prime}}(U_{1m})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{2}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=2}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(D)\\
M^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{j^{\prime}}(D)\end{subarray}}t^{(k)}_{M}t^{(k^{\prime})}_{M^{\prime}}\big(E^{z,r}_{P,Q}-\lambda^{z}_{P}\lambda_{Q}^{r}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{\prime}\Sigma t.

By the C.L.T for i.i.d random variables, it follows that

\sqrt{n}(\bar{X}-t^{\prime}\Lambda)\Rightarrow N(0,t^{\prime}\Sigma t).

Thus, formula (
19
) holds, which in turn implies (
17
).

4.1
Data generation

We consider the copula
C
defined in (
2
). Given a vector
\textbf{u}_{\ell}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell})
with
2\leq\ell\leq d
, we define its marginal distribution as

C_{\ell}(\textbf{u}_{\ell})=C(u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell},1,\dots,1),

with the corresponding density function

c_{\ell}(\textbf{u}_{\ell}).

The conditional distribution of
U_{\ell}
given
(U_{1},\dots,U_{\ell-1})
, denoted by
C_{\ell|1,\dots,\ell-1}
, is given by:

\displaystyle C_{\ell|1\cdots\ell-1}(u_{\ell}|u_{1},\cdots,u_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P[U_{\ell}\leq u_{\ell}|U_{1}=u_{1},\cdots,U_{\ell-1}=u_{\ell-1}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{\tfrac{\partial^{\ell-1}}{\partial u_{1}\cdots\partial u_{\ell-1}}C(u_{1},\cdots,u_{\ell-1},u_{\ell})}{\tfrac{\partial^{\ell-1}}{\partial u_{1}\cdots\partial u_{\ell-1}}C(u_{1},\cdots,u_{\ell-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{\int_{0}^{u_{\ell}}c_{\ell}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{\ell-1},u)du}{c_{\ell-1}(\textbf{u}_{\ell-1})}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\tfrac{c_{\ell}(\textbf{u}_{\ell})}{c_{\ell-1}(\textbf{u}_{\ell-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\phi_{k}(u_{\ell})\Delta^{(k)}_{\ell}

where

\Delta^{(k)}_{\ell}=\tfrac{\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\ell}~\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}M\in\mathcal{M}_{j}(\{1,\dots,\ell\})\\
\ell\in M\end{subarray}}\lambda_{M}^{(k)}\prod\limits_{m\in M\setminus\{\ell\}}\phi_{k}(u_{m})}{c_{\ell-1}(\textbf{u}_{\ell-1})}.

(20)

This leads to the following expression:

\displaystyle C_{\ell|1\cdots\ell-1}(u_{\ell}|u_{1},\cdots,u_{\ell-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{\ell}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{2}\Delta^{(k)}_{\ell}\int_{0}^{u_{\ell}}\phi_{k}(u)du

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{5}\Delta^{(2)}_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{3}+(\sqrt{3}\Delta^{(1)}_{\ell}-3\sqrt{5}\Delta^{(2)}_{\ell})u_{\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\sqrt{3}\Delta^{(1)}_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}\Delta^{(2)}_{\ell})u_{\ell}.

To generate a random vector
(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})
that follows the extended FGM copula defined in (
2
), proceed as follows:

1.

Generate a vector
(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
from the uniform distribution on
[0,1]^{d}
.

2.

Set
u_{1}=v_{1}
.

3.

For
\ell=2,\dots,d
, we use the
uniroot
function on
R
to find
u_{\ell}
as the unique solution on
[0,1]
of the equation

2\sqrt{5}\Delta^{(2)}_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{3}+(\sqrt{3}\Delta^{(1)}_{\ell}-3\sqrt{5}\Delta^{(2)}_{\ell})u_{\ell}^{2}+(1-\sqrt{3}\Delta^{(1)}_{\ell}+\sqrt{5}\Delta^{(2)}_{\ell})u_{\ell}-v_{\ell}=0.

In this way, the vector
(u_{1},\dots,u_{d})
follows the copula
C
defined in (
2
).

5
Case Study: Application to Bearing Data

For this case study, we utilize the IMS Bearing Data Set provided by the NSF I/UCR Center for Intelligent Maintenance Systems with support from Rexnord Corp. (see Lee et al., 2007
[
16
]
). The data comprises three separate test-to-failure experiments conducted using a test rig equipped with four Rexnord ZA-2115 double row bearings mounted on a shaft driven at a constant speed of 2000 RPM and subjected to a 6000 lb radial load. Vibration data were recorded using high-sensitivity PCB 353B33 ICP accelerometers placed on the bearing housings, two per bearing for the first dataset and one per bearing for the second and third. Each dataset consists of thousands of 1-second vibration signal snapshots (20,480 data points per file, sampled at 20 kHz), collected at regular 10-minute intervals. All bearings were force-lubricated, and failures occurred only after exceeding their designed lifetime. The setup suggests a plausible dependence among the bearings, as they are mounted on a shared shaft and subjected to the same operating conditions.

The results in Tables

point out two important characteristics. First, the estimates of pairwise dependence parameters remain stable across the three temporal subsets, suggesting a persistent and consistent dependence structure over time. Second, only the pairwise interactions are statistically significant at the
\alpha=5\%
level, except for
\hat{\lambda}_{\{1,4\}}^{(1)}
and
\hat{\lambda}_{\{2,4\}}^{(2)}

[

In contrast, all parameters corresponding to interactions involving three or more bearings are essentially zero, indicating that higher-order dependencies do not contribute meaningfully beyond the pairwise structure.

Table
5
shows that, beyond the structure captured by the classical
4
-variate FGM copula, a residual dependence is present and accounted for by the second Legendre term in the extended model. While the classical parameters generally display stronger individual significance than the extended ones, the test of
H_{0}:\Lambda^{(2)}=\mathbf{0}
is rejected with a
p

\{1,4\}

6
Conclusion

This paper develops an extended
d
-variate FGM copula model based on Legendre polynomials and proposes explicit estimators for its parameters under the i.i.d setting. The estimators are shown to be unbiased, consistent, and asymptotically normal. A simulation study confirmed their finite-sample accuracy. Applying the model to the Bearing dataset revealed that the classical 4-variate FGM copula fails to capture certain residual dependencies. In contrast, the extended version, especially in its reduced form, demonstrates improved fit as supported by significance tests, Rosenblatt diagnostics, and information criteria.

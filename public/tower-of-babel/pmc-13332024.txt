Leveraging neural networks to model tourist behaviour for sustainable heritage conservation
Authors: Waleed, Ahmad Ali AlZubi, Waheed Ullah Shah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Artificial intelligence, as an emerging technological science, provides advanced methods for simulating and extending human intelligence. The technological convergence from various domains has been the focus of researcher community for the last few years. This study applies artificial neural network algorithms to examine sustainability indicators at UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan. Data has been collected from 417 national tourists visiting key heritage sites, including the Heritage Remains at Moenjodaro, the Buddhist Remains of Takht-i-Bhai and the neighboring city at Sahr-i-Bahlol, the Fort and Shalamar Gardens in Lahore, the Historical Monuments at Makli (Thatta), Rohtas Fort, and Taxila. However to investigate the influence of sustainability indicators, this study intends to measure the satisfaction level of the national tourists in the targeted six UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan. Tourists’ satisfaction levels were measured using a five-point Likert scale. Using ANN algorithms the findings reveal that economic, perceptual, ecological, and socio-cultural factors have significant positive relationships with the sustainability of UNESCO heritage destinations in Pakistan. This study insights as the ecological perspective plays the most instrumental role in attaining sustainability in UNESCO-declared destinations in Pakistan. Further the tourists’ perceptional perspective shows a positive impact in maintaining sustainability in heritage destinations. Furthermore the influential indicator is the socio-cultural perspective. The least influential factor for heritage destinations’ sustainability is the economic perspective. For the policy-makers the data driven analysis provides ground for economic impact modeling, preservation, marketing and policy implementation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-49345-x.

Introduction

UNESCO-declared heritage destinations represent culture, history and natural beauty to the world due to their valuable resources1. These destinations are wisely chosen and protected by UNESCO due to their tremendous worldwide importance. These destinations reflect the achievements of humans in past. Tourism plays an important role in promoting these sites due to their preservation and their social and economic consequences2. Tourists from all over the world visit these destinations to explore their culture and history and evaluate their natural attractiveness. This kind of tourism works as a revenue generation tool for these sites which can be used for the administration and protection and a source of employment for local people3.

Pakistan has a very rich history of Indus Valley Civilization including UNESCO-declared heritage destinations4. The inspiration of Buddhist Gandhara Civilization represents a deep reflection of cultural heritage and stupas in UNESCO-declared destinations in the country5. The Mughal era has left behind numerous architects like Lahore Fort and Shalimar Gardens, also in Kartarpur Corridor there are various animated religious sites6. Despite such a rich heritage history, there are many deficiencies and challenges like international exposure, improper infrastructure and insufficient investment for conservation of these destinations7. Now the government is focusing on National Tourism Strategy to leverage heritage destinations for national and international tourists8. To put heritage tourism of Pakistan on the path of sustainability, the effective protection and security concerns need to be resolved by higher authorities9.

The preservation of UNESCO heritage destinations in Pakistan is increasingly challenged by ecological degradation, weak management structures, and limited resources for sustainable tourism development as observed in some previous studies10. At the same time, tourist behavior is shaped by interconnected economic, socio-cultural, perceptual, and ecological factors, which create complex and dynamic patterns that are difficult to capture using traditional linear statistical approaches. This complexity highlights a clear research gap: while conventional models provide partial insights, they fail to explain the non-linear and multidimensional interactions that drive tourist satisfaction and sustainability outcomes. In this context, the application of artificial intelligence, particularly neural network-based approaches, offers a powerful alternative as observed in some previous studies11. By modeling hidden relationships and generating predictive insights, AI has the potential to not only improve our understanding of tourist behavior but also to provide actionable tools for heritage conservation and sustainable management in developing-country contexts like Pakistan.

There have been many traditional research methods applied for conservation and protection of UNESCO-declared destinations12. Now with the emerging technological convergence the application of artificial intelligence (AI) comes into consideration to introduce an exceptional perspective13. There are numerous advantages of embedding traditional statistical tools with emerging AI tools. AI has the capability to analyze big data and images for the classification of cultural and natural attributes. AI can also be used as perception measurement tool for tourists. This paper aims at measuring the perception of the tourists with reference to various factors. Artificial Neural Networks algorithm is used as a validating tool to quantify the qualitative attributes of the tourists. Therefore, this paper can be used by tourists, researchers and policymakers for sustainability in heritage tourism industry.

Cultural heritage reflects the evidence of human history and culture both in material form and the cultural meaning it possesses14. Material heritage shows the social changes, art and the philosophy of the past which play an unexceptional role in studying social revolutions and cultural changes15. Due to rapid economic growth and an increase in the tourists’ demands, the transformation and enhancement of the tourists’ destinations have entered a new era of more spatial, evolutionary and reorganizing qualities and scales16.

Heritage tourism is a part of the tourism industry in which the tourists have the habit of visiting various old and historical buildings17. Heritage tourism is the motivation for visiting archaeological destinations, monuments, palaces, religious places and local cultural locations. AI can be a protective tool for conservation heritage resources for future generations18. Heritage tourism is the larger and fast-growing segment of tourism contributing 40% of the annual tourism earnings come from the heritage tourism in the world, and the annual growth is about 15%, which is three times that of general tourism19.

This study introduces several novel aspects to the field of heritage tourism research. First, it is among the earliest attempts to apply artificial neural network algorithms to evaluate sustainability indicators at Pakistan’s UNESCO World Heritage sites, thus bringing AI-based methodologies into a new cultural and geographical setting. Second, the research develops a holistic framework that simultaneously incorporates ecological, economic, socio-cultural, and perceptual perspectives, offering a more comprehensive understanding of sustainability dynamics. Finally, the methodological approach is grounded in empirical survey data from domestic tourists across six heritage sites, ensuring that the findings reflect real-world visitor experiences and site-specific sustainability challenges.

There has been very limited and inconsistent research on heritage tourism sustainability20. Till now, an unsatisfactory investigation of heritage tourism sustainability has been carried out by researchers who are interested in the sustainability measures of tourism. Regardless of the role of heritage tourism in the protection of history, culture and natural assets in the previous research. The development may be likely to have negative environmental and socio-cultural outcomes. The sustainability of heritage tourism may cause degradation and ignorance of the multiple positive outcomes of the heritage resources21.

First, this study introduces an artificial neural network–based predictive framework for heritage tourism sustainability, capable of capturing the complex, non-linear relationships among ecological, socio-cultural, perceptual, and economic factors as contrasting to previous studies22. By doing so, it advances methodological approaches in tourism research beyond traditional linear models. Second, the findings provide policy-relevant insights by demonstrating that ecological factors are the most influential drivers of sustainability at UNESCO heritage destinations in Pakistan, whereas economic factors remain comparatively weak. This evidence guides policymakers and site managers to prioritize ecological preservation while redesigning economic strategies to support conservation. Third, as contrasting to previous studies23, this study delivers stakeholder-oriented contributions by offering practical tools for diverse heritage managers, local communities, and tourism authorities—enabling them to make informed decisions that balance visitor satisfaction with long-term sustainability objectives.

The findings of this study reveals that tourist survey data from six UNESCO heritage sites in Pakistan show that economic, perceptual, ecological, and socio-cultural factors all positively influence sustainability. Notably, ecological perspectives exert the strongest impact, underscoring their significance for heritage conservation, while economic perspectives remain the weakest driver. The conclusion shows the impact of heritage tourism sustainability in the region, policy recommendations for various stakeholders and areas for future research.

The theory of planned behavior was established for the sake of explaining human behavior under some determined circumstances. This theory explains that the behavior of humans is developed by considering information, beliefs and the relevant behavior during the decision-making process. According to this theory, the motivation of the human is dependent on three interrelated fundamentals: the attitude of an individual towards the behavior, subjective norms, and control of perceived behavior. Attitude is individual’s positive or negative evaluation in the context of agriculture, financial cost, soil health and personal satisfaction. The subject norms refer to perceived social pressure from family, buyers, officers and other influencing departments. The perceived behavioral control refers to the individual’s perception regarding their own capability and the involvement of assisting or restraining features24. Theory of planned behavior provides foundation for our hybrid Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) and Artificial Neural Network. For SEM theory of planned behavior provides a clear and testable structure. For ANN, theory of planned behavior provides insight for complex and real world problems. Keeping in mind theory of planned behavior we can construct hypotheses25.

UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan

As a main source of ancient civilization in South Asia, Pakistan is enriched with an enormous number of heritage destinations. The six UNESCO-declared destinations in Pakistan; Archaeological Ruins at Moenjodaro, Buddhist Ruins of Takht-i-Bahi and Neighbouring City Remains at Sahr-i-Bahlol, Fort and Shalamar Gardens in Lahore, Historical Monuments at Makli, Thatta, Rohtas Fort and Taxila have been selected for conducting this study. The heritage remains of Moenjodaro are one of the well-maintained ruins in South Asia [see Fig. 1 in appendix]. These ruins are located on the right-side Indus River at 510 km distance from Karachi and 28 km from Larkana city in Sindh Province of Pakistan. These assets show the ruins of the old Indus Civilization, which was observed between 2500 − 1500 BC. These remains are also considered to be the world’s three famous civilizations35.

The Buddhist remains of Takht-i-Bhai and Neighboring City are the ancient remains of Buddhism in Gandhara places in Pakistan [see Fig. 2 in appendix]. These Buddhist remains are located on different hilltops ranging from 36.6 to 152.4 m in height. It is the most appropriate Buddhist remaining in Pakistan with all the authentic structures. These remains have been constructed in Gandhara pattern using stones, lime and mud36. Fort and Shalamar Gardens in Lahore are the remains of the Mughal Civilization. These remains are present in the City of Lahore at a seven-kilometre distance from each other and consist of two different majestic portions; the Lahore Fort and the Shalimar Gardens[see Fig. 3 in appendix]. The one majestic portion consists of monuments, and the other one is famous for many water gardens. These remains contain excellent artwork of the Mughal Civilization of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. The Mughal Civilization was a combination of Islam, Persian, Hindu and Mongol religions which were dominant in the subcontinent in that era for about 300 years37.

Historical Monuments at Makli, Thatta heritage destination is situated near the River Indus in the Sindh province of Pakistan. There are about 0.5 million tombs and graves 10 km square. This enormous cemetery at Makli is famous in the world due to the graves of kings, queens, governors, intellectuals and philosophers. These are present at the side of the six-and-a-half kilometre-long plateau of Makli Hill. Makli Hill relates to Thatta, which was the capital of Islamic Civilization from the fourteenth to eighteenth centuries [see Fig. 4 in appendix]. One of the most well-known tombs is that of Jam Nizamuddin II, who ruled here from 1461 to 1509. The most colourful tomb here is that of DiwanShurfa Khan, who left this world in 163838.

Rohtas Fort consists of strong walls extended about four kilometres, lined with fortresses and penetrated by monumental entries. Rohtas Fort, also known as Qila Rohtas, is an excellent illustration of early Muslim military construction in Central and South Asia. This Qila was built in the sixteenth century and situated at a strategic position in the north of Pakistan, Punjab Province. This place is irregularly present on the hilltop. There is an inside wall which differentiates the inner side from the fort and an internal supply of water in the structure of stepped wells. Later, the Mughals constructed a gorgeous mosque Shahi Masjid is present near Kabuli Gate and Man Singh Haveli. Rohtas Forts shows a new form of construction of the Turkish military art as support against cannons and gunpowder [see Fig. 5 in appendix]. The structure of Rohtas Fort was designed by artists from Turkey and the Indian subcontinent. The most remarkable thing is observed to be decoration, antiquity and calligraphy with marble, plaster and stone39.

Taxila is in the Rawalpindi District of Punjab Province. It consists of a Mesolithic cave, archaeological remains, Buddhist monasteries, Muslim Mosque and Madrassa. Taxila is an example of a Buddhist learning centre for Persia, Greece and Central Asia from the fifth century B.C to the second century A.D. Taxila is present on the sub-road of the Silk Road. It is now considered to be one of the most important heritage sites in Asia [see Fig. 6 in appendix]. These remains show the urban life of the Indian subcontinent. Bhir mound is also a part of this heritage site, which is famous for the entry of Alexander the Great to Taxila. The heritage sites of Saraikala, Bhir, Sirkap and Sirsukh show the ancient settings of Taxila with indications from the Neolithic, Bronze and Iron ages40.

Study construct and hypotheses development

Heritage tourism is related to the attraction of destinations. The attraction of the destinations is obtained by the perception of the tourists. Economic perspective, perceptional perspective, ecological perspective and socio-cultural perspectives are the factors influencing heritage tourism in Pakistan. Resultantly, artificial algorithms are used to validate the perception of the tourists with reference to UNESCO-declared destinations in Pakistan.

Ecological sustainability

The environmental protection at the UNESCO-declared heritage destinations.

Five items i.e., solid waste management systems, heritage tourism and the environment are integration, reduce pollution, value and protection of the natural environment and minimize the damage to the natural beauty are measured.

Tourist perceptional perspective

The overall feelings of the tourists.

Five items i.e., heritage tourism destinations are attractive in nature, Host-guest relationship, safety and security, additional services of water, electricity, accommodation and health and the entrance fee are considered.

Socio-cultural sustainability

Cultural preservation of the UNESCO-declared destinations.

Five items i.e., quality at the destinations, guidelines of “what is allowed” and “what is not allowed” are observed at the destinations, illegal restoration, tourists learning about the ancient culture of the heritage destinations, maintenance and restoration funds are provided by the federal and provincial governments are brought under consideration.

Economic sustainability

The financial outcomes from UNESCO-declared destinations.

Five items i.e., creation of job opportunities in the region, brings prosperity to the region, heritage tourism is a source of income for local communities, economic diversification in the region and seasonality level is observed in the products and services are the key influencing factors.

Heritage tourism sustainability

Long term tourism in UNESCO-declared heritage destinations.

Five items i.e., positive benefits of tourism outweigh the negative impacts, tourism should be actively encouraged, support tourism, Pakistan should remain for heritage tourism and government should support heritage tourism in Pakistan are measured.

On the basis of above constructs four hypotheses have been developed.

H1

Economic perspective has a positive influence on sustainability in UNESCO declared heritage destinations in Pakistan.

H2

Perceptional perspective of the tourists has a positive influence on sustainability in UNESCO-declared destinations in Pakistan.

H3

There exists a positive relationship between the ecological perspective and sustainability in UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan.

H4

There exists a positive relationship between socio-cultural measures and sustainability in UNESCO-declared destinations in Pakistan.

Multilayer perceptron

It is an artificial neural network method for mapping many datasets to make a single dataset. Its network consists of an input layer, hidden layer and output layer. The multilayer perceptron is used to solve various problems in different fields. It is highly applicable in measuring the perception of tourists in promoting heritage destinations in Pakistan (Fig. 2). For the dataset N = 417, the network is composed of one input layer of four neurons for economic, perceptional, ecological factors, one hidden layer with five neurons and one output layer with linear activation for predicting heritage tourism sustainability. Additionally, the dataset was divided into training (70%), validation (15%) and testing (15%) datasets. Training have strong back propagation to stop over fitting.

Performance evaluation

To evaluate the predictive model’s performance, different types of evaluation parameters are used to consider various concerns about the situations. The most important and basic approach is the development of a confusion matrix, which is then used to find performance evaluation metrics. The confusion matrix is a two-dimensional matrix in which the rows shows the actual observations, whereas the columns show the result of the predictive model. Four basic terms used for developing the confusion matrix are true positive (TP), true negative (TN), false positive (FP) and false-negative (FN). TP represents the situation where the actual observation is a positive case, and the predictive model also gives a positive result. TN is when the actual observation is negative, and the predictive model’s result is also negative. In the case of FP, the actual observation is a negative case, whereas the result of the model is a positive case. Similarly, FN is where the actual observation is a positive case, and the model gives a negative result. The model’s performance improves with the increase in the number of TP and TN whereas degrades with an increase in FP and FN cases. Results of measurement model

With the intention to obtain the internal consistency of the instruments, the values of Cronbach’s alpha need to exceed 0.7064. In this study, the instruments of economic perspective, tourists’ perceptional perspective, ecological perspective and socio-cultural perspective have overall Cronbach’s alpha values of 0.749, 0.744, 0.870 and 0.715, respectively, showing a meaningful tendency for internal consistency. One of the forms of validity is convergent validity, which intends to measure the amount of capturing a common construct for two measuring items65. It can also be obtained by the computation of Average Variance Explained and Composite Reliability of the construct (Table 3). Where Average Variance Explained shows the total variance with respect to the variance of the constructs of the indicators, and Composite Reliability indicates the internal consistency of the constructs66.

The values need to exceed 0.50 for a meaningful study in terms of Average Variance Explained and exceed 0.70 for Composite Reliability. Discriminant validity can be measured using three methods. First, for obtaining satisfaction, the Average Variance Explained by the constructs is measured. Then, discrepancy Modification Indices are used to assess the redundancy65. Thirdly, the correlations between the exogenous constructs are measured to examine discriminant validity. All these steps indicate the model’s qualification.

Conclusion and policy implications

This paper intends to analyze the influence of the persuading indicators to measure the sustainability of UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan. Artificial neural network algorithms are used as predicting tool to measure the satisfaction level of the tourists. All four proposed hypotheses have been accepted because of the positive relationship between economic, tourists’ perceptional, ecological and socio-cultural perspectives with sustainability measures. To attain the sustainability measures in UNESCO-declared destination in Pakistan, ecological perspective demonstrates the most significant impact. It has been observed through results that perceptional perspective of the tourists also has a substantial influence in attaining sustainability. Social-cultural perspective also plays a pivotal role in sustaining UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan. Likewise, in terms of provision of employment and contribution to economy, economic perspective contributes in attaining the sustainability of UNESCO-declared heritage destinations. Consequently, all the predictors show a significant positive relationship with sustainability in heritage destinations. ANN suggests non-linear relationship for prediction.

Some managerial implications are determined for the higher authorities. There is a need for protection, management, maintenance and fundraising to maintain the authenticity of these destinations. Also, proper waste management systems need to be installed to avoid damage to these dignified destinations. The provision of the latest products and service facilities are required to attract national and international tourists. Guides are also needed to make the tourists aware of the history of these cultural sites. The infrastructure of these destinations needs to be maintained to avoid the tourists from uneven circumstances. Proper security systems need to be developed to avoid terrorism. The residents need to be empowered to make certain beneficial decisions for developing these destinations. Institutional obligations need to be developed for the tourists’ satisfaction. The host-guest relationship at the destinations needs to be improved to reduce the communication gap. The higher authorities need to consider certain measures to attain the satisfaction of the tourists in these UNESCO-declared heritage destinations.

For Mohejo-daro archeological remains conservation-first system, overcoming underground moisture, climate-controlled pathways, Local Indus Valley Guardian Programs and digital virtual access are suggested to higher authorities. For Lahore Fort and Shalimar Gardens pollution impact fee, traditional water system revival and Vertical Green Buffer Zone are highly recommended for policy-makers. Volunteer tourism, border cultural experience, corporate partnership, sports integration and establishing military heritage tourism circuits need to be considered for Rohtas Fort. Similarly, for Takht-i- Bhai digital monastic experience, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Heritage Security Force, partnership with archaeological field schools and establishment of interfaith dialogue centers are suggested. For Taxila, Taxila archaeological park, Students Heritage Ambassador Program and Corporate Dig Sponsorships are recommended. For Makli micro-climate domes, respectful visitation protocol, Sindhi Cultural Immersion Packages are recommended.

The data collection phase was a challenging task because these destinations are well-preserved by the federal and provincial governments. Pakistan is culturally preserved nation; therefore, certain cultural biases were also observed while conducting this study. Self-administered method of data collection through questionnaires has been used in which the tourists are more likely to respond with agreeableness to avoid thinking critically. Sometimes the tourists are unsure about the statements, therefore they might have chosen neutral values of the scale i.e., neither agree nor disagree. Both positive and negative items are included in the questionnaire, therefore the tourists can get confused about providing a real response. Moreover, the model is based on leveraging artificial neural networks algorithms to predict the satisfaction level of the tourist can incur generalization in measuring the perception of the tourists.

The study can be explored for measuring the satisfaction level of the tourists for those heritage destinations in Pakistan which have a great historical background but not declared by UNESCO. Deep learning techniques can also be applied to measure the satisfaction level of the tourists. There are numerous ancient religious destinations in Pakistan. In future the study can be explored to include the satisfaction level of the international tourists in UNESCO-declared heritage destinations in Pakistan. This study can also be extended to the religious destinations’ sustainability through the satisfaction level of the tourists. This study can also be extended for the influencing indicators like; residents’ empowerment, infrastructure, security measurements, the influence of war on terror in Pakistan on heritage tourism, climate changes and Pakistan as a future heritage tourism hub for the world.

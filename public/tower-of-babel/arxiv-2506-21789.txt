Integrable 3-site, tilted, extended Bose-Hubbard model with nearest-neighbour interactions
Jon LINKS

Abstract
Extended Bose-Hubbard models have been employed in the study of cold-atom systems with dipolar interactions. It is shown that, for a certain choice of the coupling parameters, there exists an an integrable extended 3-site Bose-Hubbard model with nearest-neighbour interactions. A Bethe Ansatz procedure is developed to obtain expressions for the energy spectrum and eigenstates.

Introduction

There is consensus that the Bose-Hubbard model on three or more sites is not an integrable system, due to the display of chaotic behaviours

[

. From the mathematical perspective, these extended models also open avenues for constructing integrable generalisations of the Bose-Hubbard model. A broad class of integrable, extended Bose-Hubbard models associated with complete bipartite graphs was formulated in
[

]
. Within this class there is a 3-site model, first studied in
[
28

with emphasis on the response to an integrability-breaking tilting of the potential. This model has been studied further to

, and

One of the distinctive features of the model of
[
28
]
is that it has open boundary conditions with respect to the tunneling terms (i.e, no tunneling between sites 1 and 3), yet closed boundary conditions with respect to the intersite quadratic number operators interactions (i.e., site 1 couples to site 3).
While it is feasible to engineer a potential to simulate such a Hamiltonian, as discussed in
[
27
,
28
]
, it is of complementary interest to consider integrable Hamiltonians where the interactions are restricted to being on-site or nearest-neighbour only. In this work it will be demonstrated how this can be achieved for a 3-site system. Remarkably, the construction provides for the inclusion of a tilting of the potential that does not break integrability. However there is a compensation to be paid, viz. the model is not homogeneous with respect to the on-site interactions strength. On the other hand, such a property can in principle be accommodated due to the dipolar properties of the constituent particles
[
8
,
18
,
19
,
26
]
.

The integrable Hamiltonian is introduced in Sect. 2. It is demonstrated how the Hamiltonian may be formulated via the

o(4)=o(3)\oplus o(3)
Lie algebra, realised by canonical boson operators. Through this construction the conserved operators for the Hamiltonian are identified. It is also instructive to view this approach through the lens of the representation theory of
o(4)
, in order to set up a Bethe Ansatz solution. In Sect. 3 an explicit basis is chosen for the Fock space, in a manner that facilitates the derivation of the Bethe Ansatz equations. The roots of these equations charaterise both the energy spectrum and the eigenstates of the system. Concluding remarks are offered in Sect. 4, including comments regarding the completeness of the Bethe Ansatz solution. The Appendix contains some technical calculations required for deriving the Bethe Ansatz results.

3
Bethe Ansatz solution

To derive the Bethe Ansatz solution of the model, differential operator methods are adapted from
[
20
]
.
Before proceeding to that task, it is first necessary to completely describe the
o(4)
action on the module provided by the Fock space.

With respect to the action of
o_{1}(3)={\rm span}\{e_{1},\,h_{1},\,f_{1}\}
, the Fock space decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible modules with lowest weight vectors
|0,0,m\rangle
such that

\displaystyle f_{1}|0,0,n\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h_{1}|0,0,n\rangle

\displaystyle=-2n|0,0,n\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{1}|0,0,n\rangle

\displaystyle=2n(n+1)|0,0,n\rangle.

Such vectors are simultaneously lowest-weight vectors with respect to the action of
o_{2}(3)={\rm span}\{e_{2},\,h_{2},\,f_{2}\}
, satisfying

\displaystyle f_{2}|0,0,n\rangle

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h_{2}|0,0,n\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2n+3)|0,0,n\rangle,

\displaystyle C_{2}|0,0,n\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(n(n+1)-\frac{3}{4}\right)|0,0,n\rangle.

Now set, for
({\mathcal{N}}-n)/2\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle|n,0,n\}

\displaystyle=|0,0,n\rangle,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{N}}+2,0,n\}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{{\mathcal{N}}+n+3}e_{2}|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\}.

The following hold

\displaystyle f_{1}|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle h_{1}|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\}

\displaystyle=-2n|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\},

\displaystyle C_{1}|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\}

\displaystyle=2n(n+1)|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2{\mathcal{N}}+3)|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\},

\displaystyle C_{2}|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(n(n+1)-\frac{3}{4}\right)|{\mathcal{N}},0,n\}.

Similarly recursively define, for

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{N}},m+1,n\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n-m}e_{1}|{\mathcal{N}},m,n\}.

Then the action of the

\displaystyle f_{1}|{\mathcal{N}},m,n\}

\displaystyle=m|{\mathcal{N}},m-1,n\},

\displaystyle=2(m-n)|{\mathcal{N}},m,n\},

4
Conclusion

This work reports the construction of an integrable 3-site, extended, Bose-Hubbard model, providing a counterpoint to the integrable model studied in

[
6
,
7
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
Two distinguishing features of the Hamiltonian
(
2.5
) are that all interactions are on-site or nearest neighbour, and that the Hamiltonian remains integrable in the presence of a tilting potential with coupling
\mu
. A by-product of the integrability is that the model admits a Bethe Ansatz solution.

The Bethe Ansatz solution presented above through Eqs. (
3.1
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) is complete. All eigenstates of the system can be cast into the form (
3.1
). Potentially, the solution may fail if there is degeneration of the set of Bethe roots
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{2n}\}
,
corresponding to a singularity in (
3.6
). However, this possibility may be excluded by adapting the methods described in
[
22
]
. Mapping the spectrum of (
2.5
) to the quasi-exactly solvable sector of a
one-dimensional Schrödinger problem, and invoking the oscillation theorem, establishes that degeneration does not happen.
Moreover, spurious solutions of (
3.6
) cannot occur. Spurious solutions of Bethe Ansatz equations arise when the evaluation of the Bethe state through the Bethe roots yields a null state. While this is a feature of some spin
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
13
,
24
]
, fermionic
[
21
]
, and anyonic
[
5
]
systems, spurious solutions do not arise here. Since boson creation operators do not admit a non-trivial kernel, the general form
(
3.1
) cannot vanish for any choice of
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{2n}\}
.

For future work, one avenue is to investigate the quantum dynamics of the system utilising the Bethe Ansatz solution and undertaking a comparison with the results of
[
6
,
7
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
. A particular focus will be to characterise the dynamics in the so-called
resonant tunnelling regime, where there are oscillations that are approximately harmonic. It would be of interest to derive analytic formulae for the amplitude and frequency of these oscillations, to understand their dependency on the tilting parameter
\mu
.

Finally, an important consequence of the formulation above is that it facilitates extension to systems with more degrees of freedom. In particular, a 4-site system is accommodated through a realisation of
o(3)\oplus o(3)\oplus o(3)
, providing a counterpoint to the integrable model studied in
[
3
,
11
,
12
]
for interferometric applications. A study of this 4-site system, obtained by extending the methods developed above, will be communicated in a forthcoming publication.

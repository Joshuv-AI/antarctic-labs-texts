Effective potential in S ​ O ​ ( N ) SO(N) symmetric scalar field theories in curved spacetime


Abstract
We derive recurrence relations for leading logarithmic all-loop quantum corrections in the case of S ​ O ​ ( N ) SO(N) symmetric scalar theory with an arbitrary potential in curved spacetime. On this basis, a system of renormalisation group (RG) equations in the general is obtained approach for the effective potential in the large N N limit. As a simple illustration, we analyse the case of power-like potentials in the Jordan frame and discuss their application to inflationary cosmology.

Introduction

The effective potential plays a significant role in studies of vacuum stability and dynamical symmetry breaking
[
10
,
21
,
23
]
. The method proposed in
[
10
,
21
]
allows one to systematically compute quantum corrections for renormalizable models (such as the
\phi^{4}
model and electrodynamics) in an arbitrary loop approximation. The effective potential for the scalar theory
\phi^{4}
was further obtained up to two and three loops in flat spacetime
[
14
,
9
]
and one loop in curved spacetime
[
7
,
6
,
13
]
. An approach developing the study of non-renormalizable interactions was proposed in
[
24
,
25
]
, where a method for calculating all-loop quantum corrections to the effective potential in the leading logarithmic approximation for an arbitrary potential was presented. This approach was successfully generalised to the case of ordinary scalar models in curved spacetime, as well as to
SO(N)
symmetric theories in flat space. The aim of this work is to extend previous studies and obtain recurrence relations and the corresponding RG equations in the general approach for the effective potential of
SO(N)
symmetric scalar field theories with the non-minimal coupling to gravity.
For these purposes, we consider the Lagrangian density of the
SO(N)
scalar model in curved spacetime:

\mathcal{L}=-\dfrac{1}{2}R-\dfrac{1}{2}\xi R\phi^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\mu}\phi_{a}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{a}-\lambda V_{0}(\phi),

(1)

where
g_{\mu\nu}
is the metric tensor,
R
is the Ricci scalar,
\lambda
is the coupling constant,
V_{0}(\phi)
is the classical potential,
\xi R\phi^{2}
is the term of non-minimal interaction of the scalar field with curvature, and
\phi^{2}=\phi_{a}\phi_{a}
(
a=1,2,...,N)
, also hereafter we assume
M_{\mathrm{Pl}}=1
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2 we present the Feynman rules, demonstrating the approach to expanding the effective potential into parts dependent on flat and curved backgrounds. We also calculate one- and two-loop corrections up to first order in the curvature
R
. In Section 3, using the Bogoliubov–Parasiuk theorem on the locality of counter terms
[
5
,
17
,
30
]
, we obtain recurrence relations for the leading divergences and find the corresponding RG equations in the general approach for the effective potential in the large
N
limit. In Section 4 we consider the power-like potential in the Jordan frame, namely,
\phi^{4}
and
\phi^{6}
. The resulting all-loop effective potentials are studied within the framework of cosmological inflation theory in Section 5. The cosmological parameters for them are computed and compared with the observed Planck 2018 data
[
1
]
.

2
Effective potential for
SO(N)
symmetric interaction in curved spacetime

The effective potential
V_{eff}(\phi)
is the part of the effective action
\Gamma[\phi,g_{\mu\nu}]
that does not contain derivatives of the field
\phi
. So the effective action can be written in the form
[
6
]
:

\Gamma[\phi,g_{\mu\nu}]=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-V_{eff}(\phi)+\frac{1}{2}Z(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\mu}\phi_{a}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{a}+...\right].

(2)

where the first term corresponds to the effective potential.
The effective potential can be conveniently expanded into the coupling constant
\lambda
and split into two parts within the framework of the local-momentum representation
[
19
,
13
]
:

V_{eff}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(-\dfrac{\lambda}{16\pi^{2}}\right)^{k}\left(\lambda\textbf{V}_{k}+\textbf{W}_{k}\right).

(3)

where
\textbf{V}_{k}
and
\textbf{W}_{k}
are
k
-loop quantum corrections to the effective potential on a flat and curved (in an external gravitational field) spacetime backgrounds, respectively.

We begin by considering the case in flat spacetime. Following the background field splitting
[
21
]
, one can obtain the two-point Green’s function
G
:

G_{ab}^{-1}(x,y)=-\left[\Box\delta_{ab}+\lambda v_{2}\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}+\lambda\hat{v_{2}}\left(\delta_{ab}-\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}\right)\right]\delta^{4}(x-y),

(4)

where the quantities related to the derivatives of the classical potential
V
are introduced:

v_{2}=\dfrac{\partial^{2}V(\phi)}{\partial\phi\partial\phi},\quad\hat{v}_{2}=2\dfrac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial(\phi^{2})}.

(5)

Further, it is convenient to switch to the momentum representation and take the inverse expression:

G_{ab}(p)=\dfrac{1}{p^{2}-m_{1}^{2}}\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{p^{2}-m_{2}^{2}}\left(\delta_{ab}-\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}\right).

(6)

It can be seen how the propagator factors into a diagonal part with mass
m_{1}^{2}=\lambda v_{2}
and a non-diagonal part contributing with
\hat{N}=N-1
and mass
m_{2}^{2}=\lambda\hat{v}_{2}
. The one- and two-loop one-particle irreducible diagrams contributing to
\textbf{V}_{k}
are shown in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
One- and two-loop diagrams giving contributions to
\textbf{V}_{k}

In the formalism under consideration, each vertex is assigned certain factors
v_{n},\hat{v}_{n}
or
\tilde{v}_{n}
, where the index
n
determines the number of quantum field lines entering into the vertex. Thus, for the diagonal case:

v_{n}=\dfrac{\partial^{n}V(\phi)}{\partial\phi^{n}},

(7)

for non-diagonal terms:

\hat{v}_{2}=2\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial(\phi^{2})},\quad\hat{v}_{4}=2\dfrac{\partial}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\cdot 2\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial(\phi^{2})},\quad\text{...}

(8)

For the vertices with mixed quantum lines, the following factors are introduced:

\tilde{v}_{3}=2\dfrac{\partial}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\cdot\dfrac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial\phi},\quad\tilde{v}_{4}=2\dfrac{\partial}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\cdot\dfrac{\partial^{2}V(\phi)}{\partial\phi^{2}},\quad\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{4}=\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}}\cdot 2\dfrac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial(\phi^{2})},\quad\text{...}

(9)

Note that the operators at the vertices with four quantum lines are non-commutative, which is clear from the form of the corresponding derivatives.

Taking into account the Feynman rules, analytical expressions can be presented for the diagrams shown in Fig.
1
. The one-loop quantum corrections
\textbf{V}_{1}
in dimensional regularisation with
d=4-2\epsilon
take the following form:

\textbf{V}_{1}=\frac{1}{4}v_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)\right)+\frac{\hat{N}}{4}\hat{v}_{2}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right)\right).

(10)

Expression (
10
) contains both leading divergences
{1}/{\epsilon}
and leading logarithms with masses corresponding to various propagators. It is important to note that the coincidence of the coefficients of the leading logarithms and leading divergences is a general property for any UV-divergent Feynman loop graph. In view of this observation, in the present work we restrict ourselves to the leading logarithmic approximation of the effective potential. Therefore, to study the corrections after renormalisation, we use the following substitution:

\frac{1}{\epsilon}\rightarrow\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{i}}\right)

(11)

We further introduce the notation
\Delta\textbf{V}_{k}
for the singular parts of the diagrams, while by
\textbf{V}_{k}
we denote the leading logarithmic
k
-loop corrections to the effective potential.
The two-loop diagrams in the flat case take the following compact form:

\Delta\textbf{V}_{2}=\dfrac{1}{8\epsilon^{2}}\left(v^{2}_{2}v_{4}+v_{2}v_{3}^{2}\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}}{8\epsilon^{2}}\left(v_{2}\hat{v}_{2}\tilde{v}_{4}+v_{2}\hat{v}_{2}\tilde{\tilde{v}}_{4}+v_{2}\tilde{v}_{3}^{2}\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}^{2}}{8\epsilon^{2}}\hat{v}^{2}_{2}\hat{v}_{4}.

(12)

From these, by performing the substitution (
11
) we can obtain an expression for
\textbf{V}_{k}
.
The above expressions can be easily written down for the special case of the theory with a quartic interaction potential
(\phi_{a}\phi_{a})^{2}/4!
:

\textbf{V}_{1}=\frac{1}{16}\phi^{4}\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\hat{N}}{144}\phi^{4}\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right),

(13)

\textbf{V}_{2}=\dfrac{3}{32}\phi^{4}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}}{48}\phi^{4}\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}^{2}}{864}\phi^{4}\log^{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right).

(14)

These results are known (see, e.g., Ref
[
9
]
) and are usually subject to renormalisation conditions of the form

\frac{\partial^{4}}{\partial\phi^{4}}\left(\lambda\textbf{V}(\phi)\right)\bigg|_{\phi_{c}=M_{*}}=\lambda,

where
M_{*}
is some arbitrary scale on which the observables do not depend
[
10
]
. It is worth reminding here that the coupling constant
\lambda
was taken out of the initial potential and loop corrections for convenience, see expression (
3
).

To extend our analysis to curved spacetime, we employ the local-momentum representation
[
8
,
3
,
27
]
, which is based on the introduction of Riemann normal coordinates
y^{\mu}=x^{\mu}-x^{\prime\mu}
:

g_{\alpha\beta}(x)=\eta_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{3}\,R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}(x^{\prime})\,y^{\mu}\,y^{\nu}\ +\ldots,

(15)

where the expansion is performed around the point
x^{\prime}
at which
g_{\alpha\beta}(x^{\prime})=\eta_{\alpha\beta}
.
Here the dots denote higher-order terms in the scalar curvature
R
, which is beyond the scope of the present work. This approach allows one to use the usual Feynman rules described earlier with the addition of a classical gravitational field background, while preserving the overall covariance of the results.

Thus, to first order in the curvature
R
, we can obtain modified propagators in momentum space
[
8
,
28
,
18
]
:

G_{ab}^{(1)}(p)=\left(\dfrac{1}{p^{2}-m_{1}^{2}}+\dfrac{\hat{\xi}R}{(p^{2}-m_{1}^{2})^{2}}\right)\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}},

(16)

G_{ab}^{(2)}(p)=\left(\dfrac{1}{p^{2}-m_{2}^{2}}+\dfrac{\hat{\xi}R}{(p^{2}-m_{2}^{2})^{2}}\right)\left(\delta_{ab}-\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}\right),

(17)

where
\hat{\xi}=\xi-\dfrac{1}{6}
. Denote the quantum corrections to the effective potential in the external gravitational field as
\textbf{W}_{k}
.

The one-loop corrections calculated in the standard way as
\text{Tr}\log G(x,x^{\prime})
can be written separately for the contributions from (
16
) and from (
17
):

V_{1}=\textbf{V}_{1}+\textbf{W}_{1},

(18)

where
\textbf{V}_{1}
is defined by expression (
10
), and the contribution to the one-loop correction on a curved background is given by
\textbf{W}_{1}
:

\textbf{W}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}v_{2}\hat{\xi}R\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)\right)+\frac{\hat{N}}{2}\hat{v}_{2}\hat{\xi}R\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right)\right).

(19)

Using expressions (
10
), (
12
) and (
19
), we then derive the modified Feynman rules, which are shown in Fig.

.

, are shown in Fig.

. A comparison of expressions (

) and (

) demonstrates a difference in symmetry factors arising from the loss of symmetry due to the inclusion of the additional vertex associated with the curvature
R

\mathbf{W}_{2}
to the effective potential:

\begin{split}\Delta\textbf{W}_{2}&=\dfrac{1}{8\epsilon^{2}}\left(2v_{2}v_{4}\hat{\xi}R+v_{3}^{2}\hat{\xi}R\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}^{2}}{4\epsilon^{2}}\hat{v}_{2}\hat{v}_{4}\hat{\xi}R+\\

(20)

Let us again consider the theory with a quartic potential
(\phi_{a}\phi_{a})^{2}/4!
. The one-loop correction
\textbf{W}_{1}
takes the form:

\textbf{W}_{1}=\frac{1}{4}\phi^{2}\hat{\xi}R\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)+\frac{\hat{N}}{12}\phi^{2}\hat{\xi}R\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right),

(21)

which corresponds to
[

]
. The two-loop contribution
\textbf{W}_{2}
is given by:

\textbf{W}_{2}=\dfrac{1}{4}\phi^{2}\hat{\xi}R\log^{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}}{8}\phi^{2}\hat{\xi}R\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{1}}\right)\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right)+\dfrac{\hat{N}^{2}}{72}\phi^{2}\hat{\xi}R\log^{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m^{2}_{2}}\right).

(22)

3
Recurrence relations and RG equations in the general approach

In this section, we analyse the coefficients at leading poles in multi-loop diagrams to establish relations between them, and then we perform the substitution (
11
) to obtain the desired effective potential.
Recall that the
{\mathcal{R}}
operation without the last subtraction of the complete graph (i.e.,
{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}
operation)
[
4
]
, being applied to an
n
-loop diagram, eliminates UV divergences from the first up to
(n-1)
-loop divergent subgraphs. The action of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
on an arbitrary diagram
G
can be written as follows:

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}G=\left(1-\sum_{\gamma}\left(\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right)_{\gamma}+\sum_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}}\left(\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right)_{\gamma}\left(\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right)_{\gamma^{\prime}}-...\right)G,

(23)

where each term
\left(\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right)_{\gamma}
acts on
G
as:

\left(\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\right)_{\gamma}G=\mathcal{K}\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(\gamma)\circ\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma}},

(24)

In the expressions above,
\mathcal{K}_{\gamma}
extracts the singular part of the subgraph
\gamma
,
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma}}
represents the diagram
G
with the subgraph
\gamma
shrunk to a point, and the
\circ
operation denotes the insertion of the counter term of the subgraph into the reduced graph
\nicefrac{{G}}{{\gamma}}
. The remaining
n
-loop leading divergence
A_{n}^{(n)}
is exactly what we need. The leading divergence
A_{n}^{(n)}
must be local according to the Bogoliubov-Parasiuk theorem
[
5
,
17
,
30
]
. This requirement results in a relation between the leading divergence in
n
-loop
A_{n}^{(n)}
and the contribution from the one-loop diagram
A_{n}^{(1)}
obtained after subtracting the
(n-1)
-loop counter term:

A_{n}^{(n)}=\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}A_{n}^{(1)}.

(25)

The action of the
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
operation is schematically illustrated in Fig.
4
.

Figure 4:
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
operation for leading divergences of an
n
-loop diagram

Following the approach of
[
24
,
13
]
, recurrence relations can be obtained for the coefficients at the leading poles of an arbitrary diagram:

n\Delta\textbf{V}_{n}=\dfrac{1}{4}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Delta\textbf{V}_{k}}{\partial\phi^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Delta\textbf{V}_{n-k-1}}{\partial\phi^{2}}+4\hat{N}\dfrac{\partial\Delta\textbf{V}_{k}}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\textbf{V}_{n-k-1}}{\Delta\partial(\phi^{2})}\right),

(26)

n\Delta\textbf{W}_{n}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Delta\textbf{W}_{k}}{\partial\phi^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial^{2}\Delta\textbf{V}_{n-k-1}}{\partial\phi^{2}}+4\hat{N}\dfrac{\partial\Delta\textbf{W}_{k}}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\Delta\textbf{V}_{n-k-1}}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\right),

(27)

where
n\geq 2
.
It is also convenient to introduce functions summing leading divergences:

\Sigma_{\lambda}(z,\phi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-z)^{n}\Delta\textbf{V}_{n},~~\Sigma_{\xi}(z,\phi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-z)^{n}\Delta\textbf{W}_{n},

(28)

where
z=\frac{\lambda}{16\pi^{2}\epsilon}
.
Then the recurrence relations (
26
) and (
27
) in the large
N
approximation can be converted into a system of equations for
\Sigma_{\lambda}
and
\Sigma_{\xi}(z,\phi)
:

\dfrac{\partial\Sigma_{\lambda}}{\partial z}=-N\left(\dfrac{\partial\Sigma_{\lambda}}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\right)^{2},

(29)

\dfrac{\partial\Sigma_{\xi}}{\partial z}=-2N\dfrac{\partial\Sigma_{\xi}}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\Sigma_{\lambda}}{\partial(\phi^{2})},

(30)

with the initial conditions
\Sigma_{\lambda}(0,\phi)=V_{0}(\phi)
and
\Sigma_{\xi}(0,\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\hat{\xi}R\phi^{2}
. Expressions (
29
) and (
30
) are the desired RG equations in the general approach, valid for an arbitrary classical potential
V_{0}(\phi)
.

Finally, the effective potential can be represented using the substitution (
11
) as follows:

V_{eff}=\lambda\Sigma_{\lambda}(z,\phi)+\left(\frac{1}{12}R\phi^{2}+~\Sigma_{\xi}(z,\phi)\right)\bigg|_{z\rightarrow-\frac{\lambda}{16\pi^{2}}\log(\lambda\hat{v}_{2}/\mu^{2})}

(31)

We use explicit examples to verify our results and analyse the behaviour of effective potentials in renormalisable and non-renormalisable models in the following section.

4
Power-like potentials

Let us consider the general case of a power-like potential of the form:

V_{0}(\phi)=\dfrac{(\phi^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}}{p!}.

(32)

In this case, it is convenient to use an ansatz expressed in terms of dimensionless variables:

\begin{split}&\Sigma_{\lambda}(z,\phi)=\dfrac{\phi^{p}}{p!}f_{1}(x),\\
&\Sigma_{\xi}(z,\phi)=\hat{\xi}R\dfrac{\phi^{2}}{2!}f_{2}(x)\end{split}

(33)

where
x=z(\phi^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}-2}
. After substituting this ansatz into RG equations in the general approach, we obtain a system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations for the functions
f_{1}(x)
and
f_{2}(x)
:

f_{1}^{\prime}(x)=-\dfrac{N}{4p!}\left[(p-4)xf_{1}^{\prime}(x)+pf_{1}(x)\right]^{2},

(34)

f_{2}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{N}{2p!}\left((p-4)xf_{1}^{\prime}(x)+pf_{1}(x)\right)\left((p-4)xf_{2}^{\prime}(x)+2f_{2}(x)\right)

(35)

with the initial conditions:

f_{1}(0)=1,\quad f_{2}(0)=1.

(36)

From the requirement, that the expansion parameter
x
be small and from the leading logarithmic approximation, we establish a constraint on the coupling constant
\lambda
and the transmutation parameter
\mu
:

\lambda^{2}\mu^{2(p-4)}<\dfrac{(16\pi^{2})^{p-2}}{[(p-2)!]^{p-4}}

(37)

Further, we consider definite values of
p
for the system (
34
-
35
).

4.1
Renormalisable
p=4
case

For the case of a renormalised power-like potential with
p=4
in four-dimensional space, the RG equations in the general approach (
34
), (
35
) are significantly simplified, taking the form:

f_{1}^{\prime}(x)=-\dfrac{N}{6}f_{1}^{2}(x),

(38)

f_{2}^{\prime}(x)=-\dfrac{N}{6}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x),

(39)

The solution to the equations is a geometric progression of the form:

f_{1}(x)\equiv f_{2}(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+\frac{N}{6}x}

(40)

with the Landau pole at
x=-\frac{6}{N}
.

The effective potential for various values of
N
and curvature
R
is shown in Fig.
5
. It can be seen that as the number of fields
N
increases, the additional minima become lower: the value of the effective potential becomes smaller at these minima. Similar to the work
[
19
]
, two positive critical values of the curvature
R_{C1}
and
R_{C2}
are considered. Thus, the curvature value
R_{C1}
is responsible for the appearance of an additional minimum in the effective potential due to quantum corrections, whereas
R_{C2}
is responsible for the transition of the additional minimum from local to global. For illustrative purposes, the values of
R_{C1}
and
R_{C2}
were selected for the effective potential with
N=100
.

5
Applications in inflationary cosmology

The scalar theory defined by the Lagrangian (
1
) also finds application in cosmological inflation models. Thus, a typical action in the Jordan frame can be rewritten in the following form:

S_{J}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}F(\phi)R+\dfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\mu}\phi_{a}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{a}-\textbf{V}(\phi)\right),

(43)

where
g=\det(g_{\mu\nu})
and the function
F(\phi)
is defined by:

F(\phi)=1+\dfrac{2}{R}\textbf{W}(\phi).

(44)

Under a conformal transformation of the metric tensor

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=F\left(\phi\right)g_{\mu\nu},

(45)

we can transform into an action in the Einstein frame:

S_{E}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\left(-\dfrac{\tilde{R}}{2}+\dfrac{3}{4F^{2}}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}F\partial_{\nu}F+\dfrac{1}{2F}\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-\tilde{\textbf{V}}(\tilde{\phi})\right),

(46)

where
\tilde{\textbf{V}}(\tilde{\phi})=\frac{\textbf{V}(\phi)}{F^{2}}
, and the total derivative term is thrown away. The considered action (
46
) can be reduced to an action with a canonically normalised kinetic term by introducing the scalar field
\tilde{\phi}
. For the classical case
F=1+\xi\phi^{2}
in the limit
\xi\phi^{2}\gg 1
, which is realised in inflationary cosmology, we obtain:

\dfrac{d\tilde{\phi}}{d\phi}=\dfrac{\sqrt{6}\xi\phi}{1+\xi\phi^{2}}

(47)

the solution of which is

\tilde{\phi}=\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{2}}\ln\left(1+\xi\phi^{2}\right).

(48)

Thus, the classical case of the power-like potential (
32
) in the Einstein frame takes the following form:

\tilde{\textbf{V}}(\tilde{\phi})=\dfrac{\lambda}{4!\xi^{\frac{p}{2}}}e^{-2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\phi}\left(e^{\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\phi}-1\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

(49)

It is known that
R+R^{2\hat{p}}
inflation leads to a similar type of potential
[
26
,
11
]
. The relationship between the indices of the models is
\hat{p}=\frac{p}{2(p-2)}
. For example, for
\hat{p}=1
, one recovers the Starobinsky inflationary model
[
29
]
. Indeed, the considered case with
\hat{p}=1
corresponds to
p=4
in (
49
):

\tilde{\textbf{V}}(\tilde{\phi})\simeq\left(1-e^{-2\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\phi}\right)^{2}.

(50)

It is convenient to introduce an additional function to obtain cosmological parameters taking into account non-minimal coupling to gravity

\mathcal{K}\left(\phi\right)=\frac{1}{F}+\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{F_{,a}}{F}\right)^{2},

(51)

where
\__{,a}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{a}}
.
For instance, starting from the definition of the number of
e
-folds
\mathcal{N}
and the equation of motion in the slow-roll approximation, one can obtain
[
22
]

\mathcal{N}\simeq\intop_{t}^{t_{\mathrm{end}}}\frac{\mathcal{K}\left(2\frac{F_{,a}}{F}-\frac{\textbf{V}_{,a}}{\textbf{V}}\right)}{\left(2\frac{F_{,c}}{F}-\frac{\textbf{V}_{,c}}{\textbf{V}}\right)\left(2\frac{F_{,c}}{F}-\frac{\textbf{V}_{,c}}{\textbf{V}}\right)}\dot{\phi_{a}}\mathrm{d}t=\intop_{\phi}^{\phi_{\mathrm{end}}}\dfrac{\mathcal{K}}{\phi}\frac{\left(2\frac{F_{,a}}{F}-\frac{\textbf{V}_{,a}}{\textbf{V}}\right)\phi_{a}}{\left(2\frac{F_{,c}}{F}-\frac{\textbf{V}_{,c}}{\textbf{V}}\right)\left(2\frac{F_{,c}}{F}-\frac{\textbf{V}_{,c}}{\textbf{V}}\right)}\mathrm{d}\phi.

(52)

The slow-roll parameters for a multi-field scalar model with non-minimal coupling to gravity take the following form:

\epsilon_{ab}=\frac{1}{2\mathcal{K}}\left(\frac{\textbf{V}_{,a}}{\textbf{V}}-2\frac{F_{,a}}{F}\right)\left(\frac{\textbf{V}_{,b}}{\textbf{V}}-2\frac{F_{,b}}{F}\right),

(53)

\eta_{ab}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}}\left[\frac{\textbf{V}_{,ab}}{\textbf{V}}-4\frac{\textbf{V}_{,a}F_{,b}}{\textbf{V}F}-2\frac{F_{,ab}}{F}+6\frac{F_{,a}F_{,b}}{F^{2}}-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{,a}}{2\mathcal{K}}\left(\frac{\textbf{V}_{,b}}{\textbf{V}}-2\frac{F_{,b}}{F}\right)\right].

(54)

In turn, the scalar spectral index
n_{s}
can be written as
[
22
,
16
]
:

n_{s}-1\simeq\left(-2\delta_{ab}-4\dfrac{\mathcal{N}_{,a}\mathcal{N}_{,b}}{\mathcal{N}_{,c}\mathcal{N}_{,c}}\right)\epsilon_{ab}+2\dfrac{\mathcal{N}_{,a}\mathcal{N}_{,b}}{\mathcal{N}_{,c}\mathcal{N}_{,c}}\eta_{ab}.

(55)

It should be noted that all quantities depend on the modulus
\phi=\sqrt{\phi_{a}\phi_{a}}
. It is also useful to give field derivaties of a quantity
X(\phi)
with respect to
\phi_{a},\phi_{b}
:

X_{,a}(\phi)=X^{\prime}(\phi)\dfrac{\phi_{a}}{\phi},

(56)

X_{,ab}=X^{\prime\prime}\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}+2\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial(\phi^{2})}\left(\delta_{ab}-\dfrac{\phi_{a}\phi_{b}}{\phi^{2}}\right)

(57)

One can notice that this has already been applied in formula (
4
). From expressions (
56
) and (
57
), it follows that the contraction of derivatives with respect to field values leads to the usual derivatives of these values with respect to the module of the field
\phi
:

X_{,a}(\phi)Y_{,a}(\phi)=X^{\prime}(\phi)Y^{\prime}(\phi),

(58)

X_{,ab}(\phi)Y_{,a}Z_{,b}(\phi)=X^{\prime\prime}(\phi)Y^{\prime}(\phi)Z^{\prime}(\phi).

(59)

Taking this remark into account, the convolutions in (
55
) are simplified and together with (
52
), (
53
), (
54
) one obtains the following expression:

n_{s}-1=-6\epsilon_{aa}+2\eta_{aa},

(60)

which coincides with the single-field result for a non-minimal coupling to gravity derived in
[
22
]
. Similarly, we can also present the expression for the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
:

r=16\eta_{aa}.

(61)

60
) and (

6
Conclusion

We have derived the RG equation in a general approach that enables one to calculate quantum corrections to an arbitrary classical potential in
SO(N)
symmetric scalar theories within the leading logarithmic approximation, taking into account the non-minimal coupling to gravity. We analysed cases of power-like potentials with
p=4
and
p=6
. In accordance with the results obtained in
[
19
]
, for the power-law potential with
p=4
, regimes with critical curvature values
R_{C_{i}}
were identified. For the case with
p=6
, the behaviour of the effective potential was examined for different values of the model parameters. We also showed that the obtained effective potentials of
SO(N)
symmetric models can be applied in the theory of cosmological inflation. Nevertheless, it looks like even with quantum corrections, the model with
p=6
in the large
N
limit appears disfavoured as an inflationary potential.

Interestingly, the regime with a locally flat plateau of the potential arising from the emergence of an additional minimum may be of interest for explaining the mechanism of primary black hole formation
[
20
,
12
,
2
,
15
]
. Exploring this regime under inflationary constraints and comparing it with observational bounds on PBH production is one of the important directions for the work in progress.

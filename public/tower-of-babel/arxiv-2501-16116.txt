Uniform Poincaré inequalities for the discrete de Rham complex of differential forms
Daniele A. Di Pietro, Jérôme Droniou, Marien-Lorenzo Hanot, Silvano Pitassi

Abstract
In this paper we prove discrete Poincaré inequalities that are uniform in the mesh size for the discrete de Rham complex of differential forms developed in [Bonaldi, Di Pietro, Droniou, and Hu, An exterior calculus framework for polytopal methods , J. Eur. Math. Soc., to appear, arXiv preprint 2303.11093 [math.NA] ]. We unify the underlying ideas behind the Poincaré inequalities for all differential operators in the sequence, extending the known inequalities for the gradient, curl, and divergence in three-dimensions to polytopal domains of arbitrary dimension and general topology. A key step in the proof involves deriving specific Poincaré inequalities for the cochain complex supported on the polytopal mesh. These inequalities are of independent interest, as they are useful, for instance, in establishing the existence and stability, on domains of generic topology, of solutions of schemes based on Mimetic Finite Differences, Compatible Discrete Operators or Discrete Geometric Approach. Key words. Discrete de Rham complex, polytopal methods, Poincaré inequalities MSC2020. 65N30, 65N99, 14F40

Introduction

The well-posedness of important classes of partial differential equations can be related to the cohomology of the de Rham complex through Poincaré-type inequalities.
Such inequalities state that the
L^{2}
-norm of forms that lie in an orthogonal complement of the kernel of the exterior derivative is controlled by the
L^{2}
-norm of the latter.
Mimicking Poincaré inequalities on the discrete level is required for the stability of
compatible
numerical schemes, the design of which hinges on discrete versions of the de Rham complex.

In the context of conforming Finite Element de Rham complexes, Poincaré inequalities can be derived through bounded cochain projections; see
[
2
, Chapter 5]
,
[
3
]
, and also
[
11
]
for a recent generalization.
Owing to the need to identify an underlying computable space of (polynomial) functions, Finite Element constructions are, however, typically limited to conforming meshes with elements of simple shape.
Recent works have pointed out the possibility of constructing arbitrary-order discrete de Rham complexes on general polytopal meshes, either through the use of virtual spaces, standard differential operators, and projections
[
5
,
4
]
, or by replacing both the spaces and operators with fully discrete counterparts
[
19
,
15
]
; see also the related works
[
13
,
12
,
25
,
6
,
9
,
8
]
dealing with the lowest-order case.
Both the virtual and fully discrete approaches lead to
non-conforming
schemes, resulting in additional difficulties with respect to Finite Elements.

Poincaré inequalities on domains of general topology for the three-dimensional DDR complex of vector proxies of
[
15
]
have been recently proved in
[
20
]
.
Using operator-specific techniques, fully general Poincaré inequalities for the gradient and the divergence had already been obtained, respectively, in
[
15
, Theorem 3]
and
[
16
]
.
A Poincaré inequality for the curl on contractible domains had been obtained in
[
16
, Theorem 20]
.
We also cite here
[
14
]
as a first example of Poincaré-type inequalities for two-dimensional de Rham complexes with enhanced regularity and, possibly, boundary conditions.

Very recently, Discrete de Rham (DDR) complexes of differential forms have been proposed in
[
7
]
, generalizing both the constructions of
[
15
]
and of
[
4
]
;
see also
[
22
]
for an extension to manifolds.
The purpose of this work is to establish discrete Poincaré inequalities for the complex studied in
[
7
, Section 3]
.
The proofs generalize and broaden the ideas of
[
20
]
.
The starting point is the Poincaré inequality for cochains stated in Lemma
6
below.
Given a polytopal mesh admitting a conforming simplicial submesh, this inequality is proved by constructing a mapping to lift polytopal cochains onto simplicial cochains, identifying the latter with conforming piecewise polynomial forms through the Whitney and de Rham maps in order to leverage continuous Poincaré inequalities, and concluding with local norms estimates.
The discrete Poincaré inequality for the arbitrary-order DDR complex is then proved in Theorem
4
using the Poincaré inequality for cochains to construct, given a discrete
k
-form, another discrete
k
-form with the same discrete exterior derivative and bounded by the latter.
Finally, in Corollary
5
this result is bridged to a more standard form of the Poincaré inequality for discrete
k
-forms that lie in an orthogonal complement of the kernel of the exterior derivative.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
states the main results of the paper after briefly recalling the DDR construction.
The Poincaré inequality for cochains, stated and proved in Section
3
, is then used in Section
4
to prove the main result.
The paper is completed by three appendices.
Appendix
A
collects results on local polynomial spaces that generalize those proved in
[
17
,
15
]
for polynomial functions.
Appendix
B
contains estimates for Whitney forms.
Finally, Appendix
C
describes an algorithm for the computation of a basis for a subspace of simplicial cycles used in the proof of the Poincaré inequality for cochains.

2.1
Setting

2.1.1
Polytopal mesh

Let
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
.
A
(flat)
k
-cell

f
is a subset of a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that is homeomorphic to a closed
k
-dimensional ball.
A
polytopal mesh

\mathcal{M}_{h}
of a polytopal domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a finite collection of
k
-cells, for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, such that the following properties hold:

(i)

The union of the
n
-cells cover
\Omega
;

(ii)

For each
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, the boundary
{\partial f}
of each
k
-cell
f
is a union of
(k-1)
-cells in
\mathcal{M}_{h}
;

(iii)

The intersection of two distinct
k
-cells is either empty or is a union of lower-dimensional cells in
\mathcal{M}_{h}
;

(iv)

Given a
k
-cell
f
and an
\ell
-cell
f^{\prime}
with
\ell\leq k
, such that
f\neq f^{\prime}
and
f\cap f^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
, we have
f\cap f^{\prime}\subset\partial f
.

We denote by
\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
the set of all
k
-cells of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
, and each of these
k
-cell is equipped by an orientation.
For any
k
-cell
f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, we define the
polytopal submesh induced by
\mathcal{M}_{h}
on
f
to be
\mathcal{M}_{h}(f)\coloneqq\left\{f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}\,:\,f^{\prime}\subset f\right\}
.
It is easy to verify that
\mathcal{M}_{h}(f)
is a polytopal mesh on
f
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{h}(\partial f)
is also well-defined and is a polytopal mesh on
\partial f
.

A
k
-simplex
f
is a
k
-cell that is obtained by applying an affine transformation (i.e, composition of a translation and a non-singular linear transformation) to the
reference

k
-simplex

\left\{(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}\,:\,x_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=0}^{k}x_{i}=1\right\}.

We say that
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is
simplicial
if it forms a simplicial mesh in the usual sense.
Given a polytopal mesh
\mathcal{M}_{h}
of
\Omega
, we say that
\mathcal{S}_{h}
is a
simplicial submesh
of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
if: (i)
\mathcal{S}_{h}
is a simplicial mesh of
\Omega
; (ii) each
k
-cell of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is a union of
k
-simplices of
\mathcal{S}_{h}
.

The notion of mesh regularity is given by
[
17
, Definition 1.9]
.
Namely, we say that a sequence of meshes
(\mathcal{M}_{h})_{h}
is regular if there exists
\rho\in(0,1)
, independent of
h
,
and a sequence of simplicial submeshes
(\mathcal{S}_{h})_{h}
of
(\mathcal{M}_{h})_{h}
, such that:

•

For each
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and any
k
-cell
f
of
\mathcal{S}_{h}
, it holds that
\rho h_{f}\leq r_{f}
, where
h_{f}
denotes the diameter of
f
and
r_{f}
its inradius.

•

For any
k
-cell
f
of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
, and all
k
-simplex
f^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{S}_{h}
such that
f^{\prime}\subset f
,
it holds that
\rho h_{f}\leq h_{f^{\prime}}
.

To avoid the proliferation of constants, throughout the rest of the paper we write
a\lesssim b
in place of
a\leq Cb
, with real number
C>0
independent of the mesh size and, for local inequalities on a
k
-cell
f
, also on
f
, but possibly depending on other parameters such as the domain, the mesh regularity parameter, the polynomial degree of the complex, etc.
We also write
a\simeq b
for “
a\lesssim b
and
b\lesssim a
”.

The regularity requirements on the mesh imply that the number of simplices in a cell is bounded uniformly in
h
.
Specifically, for each
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and any
d
-cell
f
of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
with
d\in\{k,\dots,n\}
, it holds that
\card(\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))\lesssim 1
.

2.1.2
Discrete differential forms

We give here a brief presentation, and refer to
[
7
, Section 2 and Appendix A]
for more details.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{h}
be a polytopal mesh.
Let
d\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, and
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
be a
d
-cell of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
.
In particular,
f
is a (flat)
d
-dimensional manifold.
For
k\in\{0,\dots,d\}
, we denote by
\Lambda^{k}(f)
the set of differential
k
-forms on
f
.
When a specific regularity is necessary, we prepend the appropriate space.
For instance,
C^{0}\Lambda^{k}(f)
is the set of
k
-forms defined on
f
and that are continuous.

We recall that the inner product on the space of
k
-forms can be written
using the Gram determinant as:

\langle\omega,\mu\rangle_{f}=\int_{f}\langle\omega_{x},\mu_{x}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{f}\det\big((\langle\omega_{i}(x),\mu_{j}(x)\rangle)_{i,j}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall\omega,\mu\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(f),

(1)

where
(\omega_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,k}
and
(\mu_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,k}
are 1-forms such that
\omega_{x}=\omega_{1}(x)\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{k}(x)

and
\mu_{x}=\mu_{1}(x)\wedge\dots\wedge\mu_{k}(x)
. The corresponding
L^{2}
-norm is

\|\omega\|_{f}\coloneq\langle\omega,\omega\rangle_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We denote by
\star_{f}
the Hodge star operator on a
d
-cell
f
for
d\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
; it is an isomorphism
\Lambda^{k}(f)\to\Lambda^{d-k}(f)
.
For the sake of brevity, we shall simply denote it “
\star
” in the remaining of the paper, since its domain and codomain can be inferred from its argument. We note that, using this Hodge star operator, the inner product (
1
) can be written

\langle\omega,\mu\rangle_{f}\coloneq\int_{f}\omega\wedge\star\mu\qquad\forall\omega,\mu\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(f).

(2)

For each
f\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
we fix a point
{x}_{f}
such that
f
is star-shaped with respect to a ball centered at
x_{f}
and of radius
\gtrsim h_{f}
.
We define the Koszul operator
\kappa_{f}
on
f
to be the interior product against the identity vector field shifted at
x_{f}
.
Specifically, for
\omega\in\Lambda^{k}(f)
,
\kappa_{f}\omega\in\Lambda^{k-1}(f)
satisfies

(\kappa_{f}\omega)_{{x}}({v}_{1},\dots,{v}_{k-1})=(\omega)_{{x}}({x}-{x}_{f},{v}_{1},\dots,{v}_{k-1}),

for all
{x}\in f
, and all
{v}_{1},\dots,{v}_{k-1}
tangent vectors to
f
.
From hereon, and for the same reason that we dropped the index
f
on the Hodge-star operator
\star
, we drop the index
f
in the notation for the Koszul operator.

2.2
Local spaces of forms with polynomial coefficients

For any integer
r\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(f)
the space of
k
-forms on
f

with polynomial coefficients of total degree less than or equal to
r
.
By convention, we set
\mathcal{P}_{-1}\Lambda^{k}(f)\coloneq\left\{0\right\}
.
For
k\geq 1
, the following decomposition of the polynomial space holds (see
[
3
, Eq. (3.11)]
):

\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(f)=\mathrm{d}\mathcal{P}_{r+1}\Lambda^{k-1}(f)\oplus\kappa\mathcal{P}_{r-1}\Lambda^{k+1}(f).

With this decomposition in mind, we define the space of
trimmed
polynomials lowering by one the polynomial degree of the first component for
k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{0}(f)

\displaystyle\coloneq\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{0}(f),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{k}(f)

\displaystyle\coloneq\mathrm{d}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k-1}(f)\oplus\kappa\mathcal{P}_{r-1}\Lambda^{k+1}(f)\quad\text{ if }k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}.

Using the inner product (
1
), we can define an associated orthogonal projector

\pi^{{-},k}_{r,f}\,:\,L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(f)\to\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{k}(f)
such that, for
\omega\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(f)
,

\langle\pi^{{-},k}_{r,f}\omega,\mu\rangle_{f}=\langle\omega,\mu\rangle_{f},\quad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{k}(f).

2.3
The Discrete De Rham construction

From this point on, we fix an integer
r\geq 0
corresponding to the polynomial degree of the discrete de Rham complex.

2.3.1
Spaces and interpolators

For any
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, the discrete counterpart
\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
of the space
H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
is defined as

\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}\coloneq\bigtimes_{d=k}^{n}\bigtimes_{f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}\star^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{d-k}(f).

Notice that, compared with the original paper
[
7
]
, we have taken
\star^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{d-k}(f)
instead of
\mathcal{P}_{r}^{{-}}\Lambda^{d-k}(f)
as component space.
In the context considered here, based on a polytopal meshes of a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\star
is an isomorphism between spaces of polynomial forms and these choices equivalent up to an application of the Hodge star to each component.
In what follows, we will therefore directly invoke results from
[
7
]
without specifying each time the adaptation (application of suitable Hodge star to the components) to the present setting.
When considering general Riemannian manifolds, however,
\star
no longer maps polynomials into polynomials, and each choice leads to a different construction.
The one adopted here has the advantage to allow the generalization of the method to manifolds (as it is done in
[
22
]
), whereas the one of
[
7
]
yields a more direct link with the DDR complex in vector calculus notation of
[
15
]
.

2.3.2
Local construction

For any dimension
d\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, any face
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, and any form degree
k\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
, we define the local interpolator
\underline{I}_{r,f}^{k}:C^{0}\Lambda^{k}(f)\to\underline{X}_{r,f}^{k}
such that

\underline{I}_{r,f}^{k}\omega\coloneq\left(\star^{-1}\pi^{{-},d^{\prime}-k}_{r,f^{\prime}}\star\tr_{f^{\prime}}\omega\right)_{f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{d^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(f)),d^{\prime}\in\{k,\ldots,d\}}\qquad\forall\omega\in C^{0}\Lambda^{k}(f).

We set, for the sake of brevity,

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\partial f}}\coloneq\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(\partial f))}\epsilon_{f\!f^{\prime}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{f^{\prime}},

(3)

where
\epsilon_{f\!f^{\prime}}\in\left\{-1,+1\right\}
is the relative orientation of
f^{\prime}
with respect to
f
.

Let a form degree
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and a vector of forms
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\in\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
be fixed.
For all
d\geq k
and all
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, we define a local discrete exterior derivative
\mathrm{d}^{k}_{r,f}\,:\,\underline{X}_{r,f}^{k}\rightarrow\star^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{d-k-1}(f)
and a local potential reconstruction
P^{k}_{r,f}\,:\,\underline{X}_{r,f}^{k}\rightarrow\star^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{d-k}(f)
.
The definition is recursive on the cell dimension
d
:

•

If
d=k
, we set

P^{k}_{r,f}\underline{{\omega}}_{f}\coloneq\,\omega_{f}\in\star^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{0}(f).

(4)

•

For
d\in\{k+1,\ldots,n\}
:

1.

We first define the discrete exterior derivative such that

\langle\star\mathrm{d}^{k}_{r,f}\underline{{\omega}}_{f},\mu_{f}\rangle_{f}=(-1)^{k+1}\langle\star\omega_{f},\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}\rangle_{f}+\langle\star P^{k}_{r,{\partial f}}\underline{{\omega}}_{f},\tr_{\partial f}\mu_{f}\rangle_{\partial f}\\
\forall\mu_{f}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{d-k-1}(f),

(5)

where
P^{k}_{r,{\partial f}}\underline{{\omega}}_{f}
is the piecewise polynomial form on
{\partial f}
obtained patching together the polynomials
(P^{k}_{r,f^{\prime}}\underline{{\omega}}_{f^{\prime}})_{f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(\partial f))}
(which are made available by the previous step).

2.

We then define the potential reconstruction that satisfies

(-1)^{k+1}\langle\star P^{k}_{r,f}\underline{{\omega}}_{f},\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}+\nu_{f}\rangle_{f}\\
=\langle\star\mathrm{d}^{k}_{r,f}\underline{{\omega}}_{f},\mu_{f}\rangle_{f}-\langle\star P^{k}_{r,{\partial f}}\underline{{\omega}}_{\partial f},\tr_{\partial f}\mu_{f}\rangle_{\partial f}+(-1)^{k+1}\langle\star\omega_{f},\nu_{f}\rangle_{f}\\
\forall(\mu_{f},\nu_{f})\in\kappa\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{d-k}(f)\times\kappa\mathcal{P}_{r-1}\Lambda^{d-k+1}(f).

Remark 1

(Integral formulas)
.

Expanding the definition (
2
) of the inner product on
f
and each
f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(f))
, recalling (
3
), and using the relation
\sigma\wedge\star\xi=\star^{-1}\sigma\wedge\xi
valid for any couple
(\sigma,\xi)
of
\ell
-forms, we note that (
5
) is equivalent to the following relation, which exposes a discrete Stokes formula:

\int_{f}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{r,f}\underline{{\omega}}_{f}\wedge\mu_{f}=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{f}\omega_{f}\wedge\mathrm{d}\mu_{f}+\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(f))}\epsilon_{f\!f^{\prime}}\int_{f^{\prime}}P^{k}_{r,f^{\prime}}\underline{{\omega}}_{f^{\prime}}\wedge\tr_{f^{\prime}}\mu_{f}.

(6)

The global discrete spaces are connected by the discrete differential

\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}:\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}\to\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k+1}
obtained by projecting each local discrete exterior derivative on the corresponding component of
\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k+1}
:

\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\coloneq\left(\star^{-1}\pi^{{-},d-k-1}_{r,f}\star\mathrm{d}^{k}_{r,f}\underline{{\omega}}_{f}\right)_{f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h}),d\in\{k+1,\dots,n\}}.

(7)

2.4
Poincaré inequalities for the Discrete de Rham complex

The main result of this work is the following generic Poincaré inequality, which is valid for forms of any degree and domains of general topology.

Theorem 4

(Poincaré inequality)
.

Let
k\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and let
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\in\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
.
Then, there exists
\underline{{\tau}}_{h}\in\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
such that
\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}\underline{{\tau}}_{h}=\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}
and

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\tau}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}.

Equivalently,

\min_{\underline{{\phi}}_{h}\in\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}+\underline{{\phi}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}.

Proof.

See Section
4
.
∎

This formulation of the Poincaré inequalities avoids any reference to an orthogonality condition.
In fact, it does not even rely on the existence of an associated inner product.
However, it is fully equivalent to the usual one, which is stated in the following corollary.

Corollary 5

(Poincaré inequality on orthogonal complements)
.

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
be an inner product on
\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
such that its associated norm
\|{\cdot}\|_{h}
satisfies
\|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\|_{h}\simeq|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}
for all
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\in\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
,
and denote by
(\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k})^{\perp}
the orthogonal complement for this inner product. Then,
Theorem
4
is equivalent to the following statement:

\|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim\|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\|_{h}\qquad\forall\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\in(\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k})^{\perp}.

(14)

Proof.

First, we show that Theorem
4
implies (
14
).
Let
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\in(\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k})^{\perp}
, and let
\underline{{P}}_{h}
denote the orthogonal projector onto
(\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k})^{\perp}
for the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{h}
.
Theorem
4
ensures the existence of
\underline{{\phi}}_{h}\in\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}
such that

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}+\underline{{\phi}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}.

(15)

By definition of
\underline{{P}}_{h}
, we have
\underline{{P}}_{h}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}=\underline{{\omega}}_{h}
and
\underline{{P}}_{h}\underline{{\phi}}_{h}=\underline{{0}}
.
Using the uniform equivalence of the two norms and the fact that
\|{\underline{{P}}_{h}\cdot}\|_{h}\leq\|{\cdot}\|_{h}
since
\underline{{P}}_{h}
is an orthogonal projector, we write

\|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\|_{h}=\|\underline{{P}}_{h}(\underline{{\omega}}_{h}+\underline{{\phi}}_{h})\|_{h}\leq\|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}+\underline{{\phi}}_{h}\|_{h}\simeq|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}+\underline{{\phi}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\overset{\eqref{eq:orth.choice.phi}}{\lesssim}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\simeq\|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\|_{h}.

To prove the reverse implication,
let
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\in\underline{X}_{r,h}^{k}
and let
\underline{{\psi}}_{h}
be the orthogonal projection of
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}
into
\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}
.
By construction,
\underline{{\omega}}_{h}-\underline{{\psi}}_{h}\in(\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k})^{\perp}
and
\underline{{\psi}}_{h}\in\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}
.
Moreover, it holds:

\inf_{\underline{{\phi}}_{h}\in\Ker\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}+\underline{{\phi}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\leq|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}-\underline{{\psi}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\simeq\|\underline{{\omega}}_{h}-\underline{{\psi}}_{h}\|_{h}\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:poincare.cor}}{\lesssim}\|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}(\underline{{\omega}}_{h}-\underline{{\psi}}_{h})\|_{h}=\|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}_{r,h}^{k}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}\|_{h}\simeq|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\underline{{\mathrm{d}}}^{k}_{r,h}\underline{{\omega}}_{h}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}.\qed

3
A Poincaré inequality for cochains

The proof of Theorem
4
relies on a Poincaré inequality for cochains on
\mathcal{M}_{h}
. We first recall some concepts related to this notion, before stating and proving this inequality.

3.1
Setting

3.1.1
Chain complex

A
k
-chain

w
of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is a formal linear combination of
k
-cells

w=\sum_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}w_{f}\,f,

(16)

where the
w_{f}
are real numbers.
The set of all
k
-chains, equipped with the natural addition and scalar multiplication, is a real vector space that we denote by
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
.
For a
k
-chain
w\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, its
support

\supp(w)
is defined by

\supp(w)\coloneqq\left\{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\,:\,w_{f}\neq 0\right\}.

Observe that each
k
-cell
f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
is also a
k
-chain
f\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, obtained by considering
w_{f}=1
as the unique non-zero real number in the linear combination (
16
).
Therefore, the set of all
k
-cells
\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
forms a basis for
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, which we call the
canonical basis
of
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
.
We also consider the following two identifications: (i) we identify the
k
-cell
f
taken with opposite orientation with the
k
-chain
-f\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
; (ii) we identify the (topological) boundary
\partial f
with the
boundary
(k-1)
-chain

\partial_{k}f\in C_{k-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
defined by

\partial_{k}f\coloneqq\sum_{f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{k-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(f))}\epsilon_{f\!f^{\prime}}\,f^{\prime},

(17)

where
\epsilon_{f\!f^{\prime}}
is equal to
+1
if
f^{\prime}
has the orientation induced by that of
f
and
-1
otherwise.
The
boundary operator

\partial_{k}:C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\to C_{k-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
is defined by linearity on the space of
k
-chains by setting, for
w\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
as in (
16
),

\partial_{k}w\coloneqq\sum_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}w_{f}\,\partial_{k}f.

It can be checked that
\partial_{k}\circ\partial_{k+1}=0
for
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.
As a consequence, the sequence

\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial_{k+1}}C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\xrightarrow{\partial_{k}}C_{k-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\xrightarrow{\partial_{k-1}}\cdots,

with
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})=\{0\}
if
k<0
or
k>n
, is a
chain complex
.
Accordingly, we can also define
\partial_{n+1}\coloneqq 0
and
\partial_{0}\coloneqq 0
as the zero maps.

We define the subspace of
k
-cycles

Z_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\coloneq\ker\partial_{k}=\left\{w\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\,:\,\partial_{k}w=0\right\}\subset C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h}),

and the space of
k
-boundaries

B_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\coloneq\Image\partial_{k+1}=\left\{\partial_{k+1}w\,:\,w\in C_{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\right\}\subset C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h}).

Noticing that
\partial_{k}\circ\partial_{k+1}=0
implies
B_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\subset Z_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, we then define the
k
-th
homology space
as the quotient space

H_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\coloneqq Z_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})/{B_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}.

3.1.2
Cochain complex

A
k
-cochain
\lambda
of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is a linear map
\lambda:C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\to\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, the set
C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
of all
k
-cochains is a vector space corresponding to the dual space of
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
.
Given a
k
-chain
w\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, we denote the value of
\lambda
at
w
by
\langle{\lambda}\,,{w}\rangle\coloneqq\lambda(w)
.

From basic linear algebra, for each
k
-cell
f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, there exist a unique cochain
\hat{f}\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
such that

\langle{\hat{f}}\,,{f^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{f\!f^{\prime}}\coloneq\begin{cases}1&\text{ if $f=f^{\prime}$,}\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The set
\big\{\hat{f}\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\,:\,f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\big\}
is the
canonical basis
of
C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
.
Accordingly, a
k
-cochain
\lambda\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
can be uniquely written as

\lambda=\sum_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}\lambda_{f}\,\hat{f}\text{ where }\lambda_{f}\coloneqq\langle{\lambda}\,,{f}\rangle.

(18)

We also define the inner product
\langle{\cdot}\,,{\cdot}\rangle
on
\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
such that, for any
f,f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
,
\langle{f}\,,{f^{\prime}}\rangle\coloneq\langle{\hat{f}}\,,{f^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta_{f\!f^{\prime}}
, and we extend
\langle{\cdot}\,,{\cdot}\rangle
by linearity on
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
.

On the space of
k
-cochains we define the
coboundary operator

\delta^{k}:C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\to C^{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
as the
adjoint of the boundary operator
, by requiring that, for every
k
-cochain
\lambda\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, the following identity holds:

\langle{\delta^{k}\lambda}\,,{w}\rangle=\langle{\lambda}\,,{\partial_{k+1}w}\rangle\qquad\forall w\in C_{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}).

(19)

Using (
19
) and
\partial_{k}\circ\partial_{k+1}=0
, it can be verified that
\delta^{k}\circ\delta^{k-1}=0
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
As a consequence, the sequence

\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta^{k-1}}C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\xrightarrow{\delta^{k}}C^{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\xrightarrow{\delta^{k+1}}\cdots,

(20)

with
C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})=\{0\}
if
k<0
or
k>n
, is a
cochain complex
.
Accordingly, we also define
\delta^{n}\coloneqq 0
as the zero map.
We define the space of
k
-coboundaries

B^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\coloneq\Image\delta^{k-1}=\left\{\delta^{k-1}\lambda\,:\,\lambda\in C^{k-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\right\}\subset C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h}).

3.2
Poincaré inequality for cochains

The Poincaré inequality for cochains actually consists in finding, for each boundary cochain, a pre-image (by the coboundary operator) with coefficients in the canonical basis that can be estimated in terms of the coefficients of the coboundary cochain.
In what follows,
k
-chains
w\in C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
and
k
-cochains
\lambda\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
will be identified whenever needed with the corresponding vectors of coefficients
(w_{f})_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}
and
(\lambda_{f})_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}
in their expansions (
16
) and (
18
) on the canonical bases of
C_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
and
C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, respectively.

Lemma 6

(Poincaré inequality for cochains)
.

Let
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
and let a
(k+1)
-coboundary
\xi=(\xi_{f})_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}\in B^{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
be given.
Then, there exists a
k
-cochain
\lambda=(\lambda_{f})_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
such that
\delta^{k}\lambda=\xi
and

\sum_{T\in\Delta_{n}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}h_{T}^{n-2k}\sum_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(T))}\lambda_{f}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\Delta_{n}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}h_{T}^{n-2k-2}\sum_{f\in\Delta_{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(T))}\xi_{f}^{2}.

(21)

Proof.

See Section
3.4
.
∎

Remark 7

(Interpretation of Lemma
6
in the context of low-order compatible methods)
.

Consider, for each
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, the canonical basis for
C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, and let
\mathbb{D}_{k}
be the matrix associated with the linear map
\delta^{k}:C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\to C^{k+1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
for
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
.
By (
19
),
\mathbb{D}_{k}
is precisely the
incidence matrix between
(k+1)
-cells and
k
-cells
, which is routinely used in low-order compatible schemes like the Compatible Discrete Operator method
[
9
,
8
]
,
the Discrete Geometric Approach
[
13
,
12
,
25
]
, or the Mimetic Finite Difference method
[
6
]
.
In these works, the focus is on dimension
n=3
, and the coboundary operator
\delta^{k}
, for
k\in\{0,1,2\}
, acts as a topological counterpart of the continuous differential operators of the de Rham complex; in particular,
\delta^{0}
acts as a gradient,
\delta^{1}
as a curl, and
\delta^{2}
as a divergence.
For this reason, the following specific notation is also of common use:
\mathbb{G}\coloneqq\mathbb{D}_{0}
,
\mathbb{C}\coloneqq\mathbb{D}_{1}
, and
\mathbb{D}\coloneqq\mathbb{D}_{2}
.

Applying the same reasoning of Corollary
5
to Lemma
6
, we obtain the following mimetic version of the Poincaré inequality, which involves a suitable inner product and its associated norm:
For all
\lambda=(\lambda_{f})_{f\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})}\in(\Ker\mathbb{D}^{k})^{\perp}
, with orthogonal taken with respect to an appropriate mimetic product, it holds

|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\lambda|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}\lesssim|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\mathbb{D}_{k}\lambda|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{h}.

This result generalizes the known mimetic Poincaré inequalities for the gradient and the curl (c.f.
[
10
, Lemma 7.45 and Lemma 7.47]
) to domains with general topology in arbitrary dimension
n
.

The proof of the Poincaré inequality on the cochains on
\mathcal{M}_{h}
relies on a lifting of these cochains as cochains on a simplicial submesh, that we can then identify (through the Whitney and de Rham maps) to conforming piecewise polynomial forms.

3.3
Mapping between polytopal and simplicial cochains

Let
\mathcal{S}_{h}
be a given simplicial submesh of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
.
In what follows, for clarity of notation, we use the symbols
f
and
F
to denote a generic
k
-cell and
k
-simplex of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
and
\mathcal{S}_{h}
, respectively; the symbols
T
and
S
will also sometimes be used instead of
f
and
F
, respectively, for the special case
k=n
.

The purpose of this section is to construct a graded map
(I^{k})_{k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}
that sends polytopal
k
-cochains onto simplicial
k
-cochains, and that is a
cochain map
(i.e., it commutes with the coboundary operator).
We first introduce a
selection operator
which, to each simplex, associates the polytopal element of smaller dimension that contains it;
then, we explain the principle of the construction (which requires a double induction);
finally, we establish the properties of this graded map: cochain property, and norm estimates.

3.3.1
Selection map

Each simplex in
\mathcal{S}_{h}
is contained in one or more polytopal cells of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
; in the following lemma, we show that the lowest-dimensional of such cells is unique and that the simplex is actually contained in its relative interior.

Lemma 8

(Lowest-dimensional containing polytopal cell)
.

For each
F\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})
, there exists a unique
d
-cell
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
with
d\in\{k,\dots,n\}
such that
F\subset f
and
F\not\subset\partial f
.

Proof.

The set
\{\ell\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\,:\,\exists f\in\Delta_{\ell}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\text{ s.t. }F\subset f\}
is non-empty and contained in
\mathbb{N}
, so it has a minimum
d
. Let
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
that contains
F
; we obviously have
d\geq k
as a
d
-cell cannot contain a simplex of strictly lower dimension. If we had
F\subset\partial f
, since this boundary is the union of cells in
\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
,
F
would be contained in some cell of
\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, contradicting the definition of
d
. Hence,
F\subset f
and
F\not\subset\partial f
.

We now prove the uniqueness of this
f
. Assume that we have a
d^{\prime}
-cell
f^{\prime}\neq f
such that
F\subset f^{\prime}
and
F\not\subset\partial f^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
d^{\prime}\geq d
. Then, the intersection
f\cap f^{\prime}
is non-empty, since it contains
F
. However, by Point
(iv)
in the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{h}
, this would imply
F\subset\partial f^{\prime}
, contradicting the choice of
f^{\prime}
.
∎

Thanks to Lemma
8
, we can define a function
g_{k}:\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})\to\mathcal{M}_{h}
which maps each
k
-simplex
F
to the unique
d
-cell
f\coloneqq g_{k}(F)\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
such that
F\subset f
and
F\not\subset\partial f
.

Proposition 9

(Partitions via lowest-dimensional containing cells)
.

For each
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and
d\in\{k,\dots,n\}
, define the set of all
k
-simplices whose lowest-dimensional containing polytopal cell is of dimension
d
:

P_{k}(d)\coloneqq\left\{F\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})\,:\,g_{k}(F)\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})\right\}.

Then, the following two properties hold:

(i)

The family
\{P_{k}(k),\dots,P_{k}(n)\}
forms a partition of
\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})
, i.e.,
\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})=\bigsqcup_{d=k}^{n}P_{k}(d)
;

(ii)

If a
k
-simplex lies on the boundary of a
d
-cell, then the lowest-dimensional polytopal cell that contains this simplex is of dimension
<d
: for all
F\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h})
such that
F\subset\partial f
for some
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
, then
F\in\bigsqcup_{d^{\prime}=k}^{d-1}P_{k}(d^{\prime})
.

Proof.

(i) Is a direct consequence of the definition of
P_{k}(d)
and of Lemma
8
.

(ii) This is a direct consequence of the reasoning in the proof of Lemma
8
: if
F\subset\partial f
for
f\in\Delta_{d}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
then
F
is fully contained in some
f^{\prime}\in\Delta_{d-1}(\mathcal{M}_{h}(f))
and the minimal dimension of the cells that contains
F
is thus
\leq d-1
.
∎

3.3.2
Construction

In what follows, we will consider that, for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, every simplex in
F\in P_{k}(k)
inherits the orientation of the unique
k
-cell
g_{k}(F)\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
in which it is contained.

For each
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, we construct a map

which associates to each polytopal

-cochain
\lambda\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}_{h})
a simplicial

-cochain

Recalling the partition

is the canonical basis of

, we define

by specifying

and for a generic

Lemma 11

)

Lemma 14

(Norm equivalence)
.

For any

,
it holds

\|\psi_{f}^{\star}g\|_{B_{d}(0,\rho)}\leq h_{f}^{l-\frac{d}{2}}\|g\|_{f}\leq\|\psi_{f}^{\star}g\|_{B_{d}}.

(76)

Proof.

Let

.
By definition of the pullback, and since
D\psi_{f}=h_{f}\mathrm{Id}
, we have, by linearity of

,
(\psi_{f}^{\star})g_{x}=h_{f}^{l}g_{\psi_{f}(x)}
.
Moreover,
|D\psi_{f}|=h_{f}^{d}
.
Therefore, using the change of variable formula and denoting by
\|{\cdot}\|_{\mathrm{Alt}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
the canonical norm (induced by the canonical inner product) on the space
\mathrm{Alt}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of alternating

-linear forms on

, we have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{f}^{\star}g\|_{B_{d}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{d}}\|(\psi_{f}^{\star}g)_{x}\|_{\mathrm{Alt}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=h_{f}^{-d}\int_{B_{d}}h_{f}^{2l}\|g_{\psi_{f}(x)}\|_{\mathrm{Alt}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}|D\psi_{f}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=h_{f}^{2l-d}\int_{\psi_{f}(B_{d})}\|g_{x}\|_{\mathrm{Alt}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=h_{f}^{2l-d}\|g\|_{\psi_{f}(B_{d})}^{2}=h_{f}^{2l-d}\|g\|_{B_{d}(x_{f},h_{f})}^{2}.

Likewise, we have
\|\psi_{f}^{\star}g\|_{B_{d}(0,\rho)}^{2}=h_{f}^{2l-d}\|g\|_{B_{d}(x_{f},\rho h_{f})}^{2}
.
We conclude by using
B_{d}(x_{f},\rho h_{f})\subset f\subset B_{d}(x_{f},h_{f})
to write

\|\psi_{f}^{\star}g\|_{B_{d}(0,\rho)}^{2}=h_{f}^{2l-d}\|g\|_{B_{d}(x_{f},\rho h_{f})}^{2}\leq h_{f}^{2l-d}\|g\|_{f}^{2}\leq h_{f}^{2l-d}\|g\|_{B_{d}(x_{f},h_{f})}^{2}=\|\psi_{f}^{\star}g\|_{B_{d}}^{2}.\qed

(77)

\displaystyle|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\mathrm{d}|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{f}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle h_{f}^{-1},

(78)

\displaystyle|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|\kappa|\kern-1.07639pt|\kern-1.07639pt|_{f}\lesssim{}

\displaystyle h_{f}.

(79)

Proof.

The proof of (

The operator
\mathrm{d}
is continuous on the finite dimensional space

We then write, for all

and since

which concludes the proof of (

The same approach can be applied to the Koszul operator, once we notice that it has a similar
commutation property with the considered pullbacks.
Specifically, recalling that

is the Koszul operator on

,
and defining the Koszul operator at 0 by

(82)

comes from the generic formula

, where
(\phi_{\star}Y)(x)\coloneq(D\phi(x))^{-1}Y(\phi(x))
.
Equipped with this commutation and using the continuity of
\kappa_{0}
on
\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{d-k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, namely

\|\kappa_{0}\mu\|_{B_{d}}\lesssim\|\mu\|_{B_{d}}\qquad\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{d-k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(83)

we can reproduce the same arguments as in (
81
) to prove (
79
), the change
of scaling from
h_{f}^{-1}
to
h_{f}
coming from the fact that, in (

is one more than
that of
\omega

Lemma 17

(Topological decomposition)
.

The decomposition

The inequality
\gtrsim
directly follows from a triangle inequality, so we focus on

We have

Appendix B

The proof of the Poincaré inequality in the lowest order case (Lemma
6
) relies on the use of a conforming basis of polynomial forms on a simplicial mesh, given by the Whitney forms.
We recall here their definition and key properties.

Any simplex

of dimension

not contained in a hyperplane.

-forms on

(88)

For any

For

There is a one-to-one correspondence between

and the

\supp(z)\subset\mathcal{V}_{k}
, and consequently
Z_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{k}\cup\{F\})=Z_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{k})
.
This property holds by induction until termination.
∎

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{p}\}
be an enumeration of the set
\mathcal{F}_{k}
based on the order in which its elements are added during the execution of Algorithm
1
, and let
\mathcal{B}_{k}=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{p}\}
be a corresponding enumeration of the set
\mathcal{B}_{k}
such that each
z_{i}\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{V}_{k}\cup\{F_{i}\})
satisfies
\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z_{i}}\rangle=1
for
i\in\{1,\dots,p\}
.

Lemma 21

(Duality property)
.

The following duality property holds:

\displaystyle\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z_{j}}\rangle=\delta_{ij}\qquad\forall i,j\in\{1,\dots,p\}.

(96)

Proof.

The case
i=j
holds by choice of
z
in Line 7 of the algorithm.
For
i\neq j
, we have
\supp(z_{j})\subset\mathcal{V}_{k}\cup\{F_{j}\}
, and by (
95
),
F_{i}\notin\mathcal{V}_{k}
.
Since we also have
F_{i}\neq F_{j}
, it follows that
F_{i}\notin\supp(z_{j})
.
Thus,
\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z_{j}}\rangle=0
.
∎

Lemma 22

(Complementary subspace basis property)
.

Define

Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\,\mathcal{B}_{k}\subset Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f)).

Then,
\mathcal{B}_{k}
is a basis of
Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
and the following decomposition holds

Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))=Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\partial f))\oplus Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f)).

Proof.

By Lemma
21
, the set
\mathcal{B}_{k}
is linearly independent and hence it forms a basis for
Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
.

To prove the direct sum, we first establish that the intersection is trivial.
Let
z\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\partial f))\cap Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
.
Since
z\in Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
,
z=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}z_{j}
for some coefficients
c_{j}
.
As
z\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\partial f))
, its support is disjoint from
\mathcal{F}_{k}
(which is made of simplices that are internal to
f
), and thus
\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z}\rangle=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,p\}
.
By (
96
), we infer that

0=\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z}\rangle=\langle{F_{i}}\,,{\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}z_{j}}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{p}c_{j}\,\delta_{ij}=c_{i}.

Hence
z=0
, which proves that
Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\partial f))\cap Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))=\{0\}
.

Second, let
z\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
.
Define

z^{\prime}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\langle{F_{j}}\,,{z}\rangle\,z_{j}\in Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))

and set
z^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq z-z^{\prime}
.
A direct computation using (
96
) shows that, for any
i\in\{1,\dots,p\}
,

\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z}\rangle-\langle{F_{i}}\,,{\sum_{j=1}^{p}\langle{F_{j}}\,,{z}\rangle\,z_{j}}\rangle=\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z}\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\langle{F_{j}}\,,{z}\rangle\,\delta_{ij}=\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z}\rangle-\langle{F_{i}}\,,{z}\rangle=0.

This shows that
\supp(z^{\prime\prime})\cap\mathcal{F}_{k}=\emptyset
and thus, by (
95
), that
\supp(z^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathcal{V}_{k}
.
Therefore, by Lemma
20
,
z^{\prime\prime}\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\partial f))
.
We have thus shown that any
z\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
can be written as
z=z^{\prime}+z^{\prime\prime}
with
z^{\prime}\in Z^{\mathsf{c}}_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
and
z^{\prime\prime}\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(\partial f))
.
∎

Finally, we address the problem of bounding the coefficients of solutions to linear systems involving the boundary operators
\partial_{k}:C_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))\to C_{k-1}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
for
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.
This will ensure that the selected elements of
\mathcal{B}_{k}
, and their pre-image by the boundary operator, have uniformly bounded
2
-norms (see (
27
) and (
32
)).

Let
\mathbb{B}_{k}
be the matrix representation of
\partial_{k}
.
In the case
k=1
, the boundary matrix
\mathbb{B}_{1}
coincides with the graph incidence matrix (between
1
-cells and
0
-cells) of the triangulation
\mathcal{S}_{h}(f)
.
This matrix is known to be
Totally Unimodular
(TU), meaning every sub-determinant is
0
,
+1
, or
-1
; see, e.g.,
[
27
, Chap. 19]
.
This TU property guarantees that the cycles
z\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
selected by Algorithm
1
(which are solutions to a TU system) have coefficients exclusively in
\{-1,0,1\}
.

In contrast, this TU property of
\mathbb{B}_{k}
fails for
k\geq 2

[
27
, Chap. 20-21]
.
We must therefore rely on a more general principle to bound the solutions, which is the goal of our next lemma.
This principle is based on Cramer’s rule, combined with the fact that the boundary matrices
\mathbb{B}_{k}
still have integer entries (specifically, in
\{-1,0,1\}
).
This general result allows us to establish two key estimates:

•

First, it provides a uniform bound on the coefficients of each
k
-cycle
z\in\mathcal{B}_{k}
. As specified at line 7 in Algorithm
1
, each such
z
is selected as the unique minimal-norm solution to the well-posed linear system defined at that step.

•

Second, for
k\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
, it allows us to bound the coefficients of a
(k+1)
-chain
w
given a bound on its boundary
z=\partial_{k+1}w
.

We state and prove this second result explicitly, and the first result follows from a similar argument.

Lemma 23
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
, and let
z=\left(z_{F}\right)_{F\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))}\in Z_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
be
k
-cycle.
Then, there exists a
(k+1)
-chain
w=\left(w_{F^{\prime}}\right)_{F^{\prime}\in\Delta_{k+1}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))}\in C_{k+1}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
such that
\partial_{k+1}w=z
and, for some constant
C
depending only on
\card(\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f)))
,

\sum_{F^{\prime}\in\Delta_{k+1}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))}w_{F^{\prime}}^{2}\,\leq\,C\sum_{F\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))}z_{F}^{2}.

(97)

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{k+1}
be the matrix representation of
\partial_{k+1}
.
We identify the chains
w
and
z
with their column vectors of coefficients,
w=(w_{F^{\prime}})_{F^{\prime}\in\Delta_{k+1}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))}
and
z=(z_{F})_{F\in\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))}
, as defined in the statement of the lemma.
We are thus solving the system
\mathbb{B}_{k+1}w=z
.
Since
f
has a trivial topology and
z
is a
k
-cycle with
k\geq 1
, we know that this system has at least one solution (there is a
w\in C_{k+1}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f))
such that
\partial_{k+1}w=z
).

Let
r=\text{rank}(\mathbb{B}_{k+1})
.
We can find a particular solution
w^{*}
by solving an
r\times r
invertible subsystem
\mathbb{M}w^{*}=z^{*}
, where
\mathbb{M}
is a submatrix of
\mathbb{B}_{k+1}
, and
w^{*}
,
z^{*}
are sub-vectors of
w
,
z
.
A solution to
\mathbb{B}_{k+1}w=z
is then obtained by putting all components of
w
, other than those in
w^{*}
, to zero.

By Cramer’s rule, the
F
-th component of the solution is
(w^{*})_{F}=\det(\mathbb{M}_{F})/\det(\mathbb{M})
, where
\mathbb{M}_{F}
is the matrix obtained by replacing the
F
-th column of
\mathbb{M}
by
z^{*}
.
The entries of
\mathbb{M}
are in
\{-1,0,1\}
. Since
\mathbb{M}
is invertible and integer-valued, its determinant is a non-zero integer, thus
|\det(\mathbb{M})|\geq 1
.

One of the columns of
\mathbb{M}_{F}
is
z^{*}
, and the others
r-1
columns are vectors of size
r
with coefficients in
\{-1,0,1\}
, that therefore have a
2
-norm
\leq\sqrt{r}
. Since the determinant is continuous (with norm 1) for the
2
-norm of vectors, we infer that
|\det(\mathbb{M}_{F})|\leq r^{(r-1)/2}\|z^{*}\|_{2}
.
Hence,
|(w^{*})_{F}|\leq r^{(r-1)/2}\|z^{*}\|_{2}
and thus
\|w^{*}\|_{2}\leq r^{r/2}\|z^{*}\|_{2}
. The same bound is therefore satisfied by
w
obtained from
w^{*}
by padding with zeros.
Since
r\leq\card(\Delta_{k}(\mathcal{S}_{h}(f)))
, this concludes the proof of (
97
).
∎

Novel synthesis of CuHCF/B-rGO composites for oxygen evolution reaction activity
Authors: S Swathi, R Yuvakkumar, L Kungumadevi, G Ravi, Dhayalan Velauthapillai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this work, we have focused on the preparation of copper hexacyanoferrate/boron doped rGO composites (abbreviated as CuHCF/B-rGO) by employing simple co-precipitation technique subsequently processed with ultrasonication method. The XRD spectra confirmed the existence of the cubic structure of copper hexacyanoferrate with high crystalline peaks. The prepared nanocomposite morphology was evaluated by scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and confirmed CuHCF nanoparticles formation with flake-like and wrinkled sheets. Pure CuHCF nanostructures revealed good OER action at 430 mV to obtain 10 mA/cm2. The obtained CuHCF product OER activity can be further upgraded by incorporating the electrically conductive boron doped reduced graphene oxide matrix into CuHCF nanostructures, for the reason that the overpotential of the CuHCF/B-rGO was reduced to 380 mV to attain 10 mA/cm2 with 88 mV/dec Tafel slope value. The doping of heteroatom considerably improves charge-transfer resistance of metal hexacyanoferrate, giving a small resistance value of 2.97 Ω, which was lower than that of CuHCF (5.57 Ω) and CuHCF/rGO (4.31 Ω). Furthermore, the catalytic activity of the CuHCF/B-rGO was stable at prolonged hours with a small decay of 12.5%. Therefore, this work offers new approach to stimulate the catalytic performance of metal hexcyanoferrate by highly conductive carbon-based materials for water splitting performance.Keywords: Heteroatom dopant, Reduced graphene oxide, Copper hexacyanoferrate, OER performanceSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Materials science

Introduction

Electrochemical water splitting (EWS) is a sustainable technique to make hydrogen and oxygen under minor processes and it was powered by using the renewable energy resources1. EWS have 2 half reactions like oxygen evolution (OER) on anode section and hydrogen evolution (HER) reaction on cathode section. However, the massive hydrogen production was restricted because of occurrence of OER, for the reason that, it needs a larger potential for developing the double bond of O = O2,3. Up to now, noble metals like IrO2, RuO2, Pt based catalysts are attractive materials in water splitting process. Still, it was reflected as high cost and not abundant in nature4,5. Consequently, to solve this above issue by using the non-precious metal-based catalysts, we have to develop capable, low-cost and robust materials for OER activity6.

Recently, transition metal based organic framework (MOCN) has been considered as a promising alternative for the noble metals7. Especially, prussian blue analogues (PBAs) has been revealed as metal organic frame-work with M[M’(CN6)], where M and M’ represents the transition metals and CN represents the cyanide group8. Particularly, it has some exclusive characteristics such as 3D open architecture, porous structure, regulated morphology and compositions9. Because of its physical and chemical nature, PBA has been employed in multi-functional applications such as electrocatalysis, electro-chromism, lithium-ion, sodium-ion batteries, optics and magnetic applications10,11. However, these materials failed to exhibit outstanding performance in extensive cycling-life necessities12. In particular, the usage of Prussian blue and its analogues are limited in aqueous electrolytes due to their major drawbacks, and having low conductivity, poor stability and restricted surface area, which can significantly lessen their electrochemical performance. So, the above issue could be overcomed by using the mixture of two different metal cations into metal hexacyanoferrate or the composition of highly conductive materials like carbon-based or conducting polymers. Therefore, mixed the PBAs with other materials can able to effectively enhance the electrochemical performance of PBAs13,14. To improve the cycle-life performance, researchers have combined the metal hexacyanoferrate materials with graphene, rGO, or carbon nanotube15. Because, carbon materials have special features like good conductivity, excellent stability and surface area and it is a perfect tool for composited with PBAs to improve the electrochemical performance for water splitting application.

For example, Xiaojuan Zhang et al.16 synthesized cobalt hexacyanoferrate/carbon nanotube composites via simple co-precipitation approach for OER. They demonstrated that OER performance of CoHCF was greatly enhanced after the introduction of CNT, and they achieved the low overpotential of 274 mV@10 mA/cm2 for CoHCF/CNT, whereas the pure CoHCF only achieved the overpotential of 411 mV@10 mA/cm2. Priya Jain et al.17 synthesized copper hexacyanoferrate nanocube with functionalized carbon nanotube via simple hydrothermal approach for OER and ORR activity. They have also claimed that the carbon composite material exhibited excellent activity than the parent materials. Because, the carbon-based materials are greatly improved electrical conductivity of material as well as material specific surface area18,19. Parisa Salarizadeh et al.20 synthesized novel ternary hybrid of MoS2-ReS2/rGO nanostructure via hydrothermal method for energy storage applications. They claimed that rGO in MoS2-ReS2 improved electrochemical performance with a high 949 F/g and 92% stability after 5000 cycles, which may be achieved by the synergetic effect between the hybrid components. Mohammed Bagher Askari et al.21 synthesized NiO-Co3O4-rGO by using hydrothermal technique for supercapacitor. The incorporation of rGO in NiO-Co3O4 improved active surface area and material conductivity with 149 mAh g−1 specific capacity at 0.5 Ag−1 and excellent stability of 95% after 6000 cycles, and it has also exhibited high current density with low overvoltage for methanol and ethanol oxidation. Likewise, Mohammed Bagher Askari et al.22 synthesized ZnFe2O4 nanorods on reduced graphene oxide for supercapacitor, which has also explored good specific capacitance and stability because of rGO. Then, PBAs with carbon materials has proved the efficient method to improve PBAs stability because it enhanced the surface area, and very attentive in energy storage and conversion23. Various heteroatom dopants such as Fluorine (F), Nitrogen (N), Boron (B), Sulfur (S), and Oxygen (O) are being widely employed to further enhance rGO properties24. Among various heteroatom dopants, Nitrogen and Boron have been commonly preferred due to their same atomic sizes similar to carbon, since these types of dopants can able to alter electron-donor properties of carbon25. Among the various heteroatoms, we have chosen the boron as a dopant, the electron deficient boron in reduced graphene oxide also performs as electron acceptor owing to the lack of π electrons. For that reason, it can able to alter the electronic structure of carbon, leading in improved electrochemical characteristics26.

Nanocomposite formation has been achieved by various techniques such as stirring, milling, grinding and ultrasonication process. Among the various methods, ultrasonication is an efficient technique because it was utilized for nanoparticles and carbon materials preparation with a fine size distribution underneath mild condition and it was considered as a green and environmentally benign method for recent times. Moreover, by using this method, the well homogeneity was occurred and it was demonstrated by the following literatures. Jeevitha et al.27 synthesized porous rGO/WO3 by using solvothermal method followed by ultrasonication process for effective detection of NH3. Likewise, Munasir et al.28 synthesized Fe3O4@rGO nanocomposite via coprecipitation method followed by ultrasonication process for the synthesis and characterization studies. Carbon materials incorporation by ultrasonication approach would lead to well distribution and it is an ideal method for composite formation29.

Here, copper hexacyanoferrate composite was prepared first time with boron doped rGO via co-precipitation approach followed by ultrasonication process for oxygen evolution reaction. B doped rGO introduction in CuHCF significantly improved material conductivity, which would be advantageous for electron transport at electrode/electrolyte interface. CuHCF/B-rGO OER performance exhibited the low 380 mV to attain 10 mA/cm2, and the small 88 mV/dec Tafel slope with longer cycle life.

Experimental procedure

Materials

Copper (II) nitrate (Cu(NO3)2), potassium ferricyanide (K3[Fe(CN)6]), boric acid (H3BO3), graphite powder, sodium nitrate (NaNO3), sulfuric acid (H2SO4), potassium permanganate (KMnO4), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), hydrochloric acid (HCl), melamine (C3H6N6), ethanol (C2H6O), methanol (CH3OH) and deionized water was obtained from Sigma Aldrich.

Synthesis of CuHCF

Based on previous literatures30, we have synthesized the GO employing modified hummer’s technique. Subsequently, for the synthesis of Boron doped rGO31,32, 70 mg GO was dissolved in 20 ml of deionized water followed by sonication for 1 h. On the other hand, 1 g boric acid was suspended in 10 ml ethanol and kept for certain hours under constant magnetic stirring. After that, the uniformly dispersed boric acid was mixed with the GO solution while stirring, and then autoclaved in a furnace at 180ºC for 12 h. The resultant boron doped rGO was repeatedly washed by using the deionized water, ethanol and methanol to eliminate the untreated boric contents. At last, cleaned B-rGO was dried at 80ºC for 12 h. Consequently, rGO was prepared from GO by using same hydrothermal condition without the addition of boric acid. For preparation of B-rGO composite with CuHCF, 0.1 M copper (II) nitrate (Cu(NO3)2), and 0.05 M potassium ferricyanide (K3[Fe(CN)6]) was dissolved in deionized water, and placed under magnetic stirring. Then, we added the 20 mg of B-rGO into the above well dissolved solution, which was kept under the bath sonication technique for certain hours to obtain the nanocomposite materials, and it was named as CH3. After certain hours, tawny brown precipitate was formed. Then, precipitated suspension was dried at 80ºC for 12 h. CuHCF/rGO was synthesized by using the above same procedure with the addition of 20 mg of rGO and named as CH2. Likewise, pure CuHCF was also synthesized in the above same strategy without rGO and B-rGO, and it was named as CH1. CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO schematic diagram was illustrated in Fig. 1. The chemical balancing equation of the prepared material is 3Cu(NO3)2 + 2K3[Fe(CN)6] Cu3[Fe(CN)6]2 + 6 KNO3.

Schematic representation of CuHCF and its carbon composites.

In the metal-organic frameworks, PBAs are crucial and cost-effective also it has intriguing properties and have significant attention in various domains33,34. Prussian blue analogues are cage-like structure with extensive channels permitting for insertion of even large intercalation ions and this open framework certifies speedy ionic conduction, allowing remarkably high-rate capability35. Prussian blue analogue can be prepared via simple coprecipitation method by using most abundant and non-toxic elements36. Here, we have chosen copper hexacyanoferrate electrocatalysts for OER and it is part of Prussian blue analogs (PBAs) family, then the interaction between the copper with cyanide in CuHCF improves the reaction kinetics, which leads to enhanced high catalytic activity in different electrochemical reactions, including electrochemical water oxidation37. Both materials such as copper and iron are extensively abundant and nearby to the state of art catalysts and considered as alternative for Pt, Ru and Ir-based materials38. Moreover, in comparison to the other methods, coprecipitation affords good structure and geometry39. Then, the carbon material was composited with the CuHCF by using ultrasonication process and facilitates its uniform distribution of CuHCF nanoparticles on the graphene sheets.

Electrochemical measurements

CuHCF and its carbon composites electrochemical studies were investigated by using the 3-electrode. Prepared catalysts as working, Platinum (Pt) as counter and Ag/AgCl as reference electrodes respectively and 1 M KOH as electrolyte were used. Working electrode was constructed by employing the 1 × 2 cm Ni foam and homogenous slurry was prepared by using the 80:15:5 ratios of prepared catalysts, activated carbon, and poly-vinyl pyrrolidone (PVDF), and obtained slurry was coated on 1 × 1 cm Ni foam, and dried at hot-air oven for further use. Here, we have loaded the active mass catalyst of approximately 3 mg on the Ni foam surface and the value was obtained by measuring before and after coating the Ni foam.

Results and discussion

CuHCF/B-rGO preparation is based on the coprecipitation method followed by ultrasonication method, then the reaction among the transition-metal cations and metal-hexacyanide anions in an aqueous solution (Fig. 2). The copper ion rapidly reacted with the cyanide ion, to form the Prussian-blue material followed by the coordination, the crystallization process was occurred. In the crystallization process, Prussian blue particles were formed with significant number of vacancies and coordinated water molecules40. In the initial precipitation process, the CuHCF particles were small and particles were accumulated to form bigger particles under mild conditions. During the ultrasonication process41, the crystal growth was disturbed and the precipitates were adsorbed on B-rGO surface to yield CuHCF/B-rGO catalyst. Consequently, the carbon material was incorporated in the CuHCF/B-rGO catalyst.

Synthetic pathways of CuHCF/B-rGO.

Figure 3 explored XRD pattern of pure CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO, which illustrated several broad peaks positioned at the 2 value of 15.2, 17.4, 24.8, 35.4, 39.9, 43.7, 51.0, 54.3, and 57.5º assigned to (111), (200), (220), (400), (420), (422), (440), (600) and (620) planes, respectively. The peak intensity of all the prepared materials were high, denoting the good crystallinity of the material, which was well corroborated with ICSDcode:77916 (and its pattern was included in the Fig. 3). The diffraction peaks reflects the FCC with a space group (Fm3m, No.225) and lattice constant (α = 10.1 Å) of Cu3[Fe(CN)6]2. Obtained peaks of all the samples such as pure and its carbon composite materials occur at closely same angle, which denoting imitation of material formation. No more peaks have been found related to the rGO, thus may be the reason for low amount of rGO substitution into CuHCF. However, B-rGO XRD pattern clearly revealed peak at the 2θ value of 24.8º and 43º and it was correlated to (002) and (001), which confirms the formation of B-rGO. In XRD pattern, the obtained high intensity 2θ values were used to find out the crystallite size of the samples, then the crystallite sizes of pure CuHCF. CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO were found to be ~ 71.11 nm, 53.36 nm and 42.68 nm, respectively, which were estimated by using Scherrer equation. High intensity peaks of all synthesized materials represent rGO incorporation does not change the pure phase of CuHCF42.

XRD spectra of B-rGO, CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO, CuHCF/B-rGO.

Figure 4 revealed Raman studies of pure CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO, which demonstrates the presence of carbon in prepared material. Peak captured at 2146 cm−1 revealed vibration of cyanide (FeIII-CN-CuII), which denoting the formation of CuHCF. Moreover, the D and G band observed in specific wavenumber suggesting the graphitic carbon and disordered carbon structure. The intensity ratio (ID/IG) was employed to signify the disorder density in carbon material. The intensity ratio values of CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO were calculated as 0.98 and 1.0. After the doping of Boron in rGO, D and G band intensity increased, which recommends formation of defects in rGO sheets, and it leads to alter the electrical properties and other physicochemical properties43. Because of low proportion carbon, D and G band does not seemed as prominent, whereas the carbon presence is corroborated with Raman and XPS studies.

Raman spectra of CuHCF, B-rGO, CuHCF/rGO, CuHCF/B-rGO.

The FTIR spectrum was employed to characterize chemical structure of pure CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO (Fig. 5). Absorption bands at 3493 and 1631 cm−1 can be revealed O-H stretching and H-O-H bending denoting H2O molecules existence in interior framework structure. Whereas, band at around 594, 1093, 1377, and 2106 cm−1 related to Cu-CN-Fe bending mode, C-O stretching mode and -CN- stretching mode of CuHCF44.

FTIR spectra of CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO, CuHCF/B-rGO.

The obtained nanocomposite was studied employing the SEM images. SEM images of pure CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO are given in Fig. 6a-f. Pure CuHCF displays the uniform distribution of nanoparticles with smooth surface (Fig. 6a, b). The SEM image of CuHCF/rGO displays the combination of a sheet-like graphene structure with CuHCF nanoparticles (Fig. 6c, d). The entire well-defined nanoparticles are distributed over the graphene sheets without any modification of the CuHCF cubic structure. The SEM image of CuHCF/B-rGO displays the distribution of nanoparticles with flake-like and wrinkled morphology, which denoted that the composition of CuHCF, B and graphene were evenly interposed with each other (Fig. 6e, f). The electrocatalytic activity was significantly developed by small particle size, porosity, conductivity, surface area along with synergetic effect of rGO sheets, which enhances the electrode kinetics reaction45.

Figure 7(a-g) shows the EDX and mapping of CuHCF/B-rGO, which demonstrate uniform placement of CuHCF on B-rGO nanosheets surface. Figure 7a explored copper (Cu), iron (Fe), carbon (C), Nitrogen (N) and Boron (B) in the weight% of 29.9%, 31.7%, 19.6%, 12.1% and 6.6%, respectively. As well, Fig. 7(b-g) proves the uniform scattering on the B-rGO sheets and existence of copper, iron, carbon, nitrogen and boron elements in prepared nanocomposite.

TEM analyses was further investigated and obtained images in Fig. 8a-c clearly revealed the well-defined CuHCF with ~ 70 to 90 nm, and regularly anchored on B doped reduced graphene oxide (B-rGO) sheets surface without any significant agglomeration. From the HRTEM images in Fig. 8d shows CuHCF formation adhered on B-rGO sheets would be beneficial for ion transportation, which would be expected to improve the electrochemical OER activity. Moreover, lattice fringes with 5.05 Å interplanar distance resemble to the (111) plane of CuHCF/B-rGO, respectively (Fig. 8e). The selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) was shown in the Fig. 8f, suggesting that CuHCF/B-rGO possess polycrystalline nature. The SAED pattern shows the diffraction rings of (111), (200) and (220) consisted with XRD in Fig. 8f. Therefore, formation of CuHCF nanoparticles on B-rGO sheets restrict reunion, probable to depict more active sites, and auspicious to adsorption and O2 desorption.

X-ray spectroscopy was analyzed to determine oxidation state of best performed catalyst of CuHCF/B-rGO and it was shown in Fig. 9a-f. The survey scan spectra show presence of Cu2p, Fe2p, C1s, N1s, O1s and dopant of B1s in Fig. 9a. In Fig. 9b, two couple of main peaks located at 933.2 and 953.1 eV explored Cu+ 2p3/2 and Cu+ 2p1/2, indicating presence of Cu species in Cu+ form. Likewise, other two couple of main peaks centered at 936 and 956 eV explored Cu2+ 2p3/2 and Cu2+ 2p1/2, indicating presence of Cu species in form of Cu2+. Moreover, the satellite peaks of Cu2+ were also detected at 944 eV and 963.5 eV, respectively. Specifically, Fe2p region in Fig. 9c revealed two main peaks centered at 710 and 723 eV, which are associated to the Fe2+ 2p3/2 and Fe3+ 2p1/2 and peaks centered at 712 and 721.5 eV can associated to Fe3+ 2p3/2 and Fe2+ 2p1/2, which also revealed existence of Fe species. Figure 9d shows the peaks for N 1s at 398.4 eV and 399.2 eV corresponds to sp hybridization of carbon in C-N. C1s spectrum in Fig. 9e is composed of three peaks at 284.8, 285.9 and 288.1 eV explored C-C, C-N/B-C and C = O. Moreover, B 1s spectra (Fig. 9f) are combined of two peaks at 191.3 and 193.3 eV revealed the BC3 and BCO2, respectively. The boron peaks denoted the incorporation of carbon with boron inside the reduced graphene oxide layer46,47.

Application studies

LSV analysis

The linear sweep voltammograms were measured for the prepared CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO, which displayed from 1 to 2 V vs. RHE at 5 mV/s (Fig. 10a). The calculated overpotential values of CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO were 430, 410 and 380 mV to obtain 10 mA/cm2. Furthermore, obtained overpotential of all electrodes at 10 mA/cm2, and 50 mA/cm2 were shown in bar diagram (Fig. 10c). The oxidation peak between 1.4 and 1.6 V vs RHE indicating existence of multiple valence states i.e. Cu1+/Cu2+ and Fe2+/Fe3+ pairs. Then, its electron transport pathway was discussed in the Tafel slope section. The LSV study of B-rGO rGO and Ni foam was further analyzed and it exhibited very lesser OER activity, and it was displayed in Fig. 10b. The results revealed that OER action of CuHCF alone is not superior, whereas the composite with B-rGO explored good OER action. It may be because of synergetic effect of CuHCF nanostructure between B-rGO folded 2D sheets, which promotes the greater contact area and expose more active sites for electrocatalytic performance48. Moreover, the best performed catalyst was compared with the previous copper-based works and it was tabulated (Table 1). Moreover, from literature49, the commercial RuO2 catalyst on Ni foam exhibited value of 408 mV to attain 10 mA/cm2 and 124 mV/dec Tafel slope value, which shows higher activity of CuHCF/B-rGO than the commercial catalyst.

Tafel analysis

Tafel plots of CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO was computed from linear sweep voltammetry curves (Fig. 10d). From Tafel slope values, the CuHCF/B-rGO resulting favorable kinetics to OER activity with low 88 mV/dec Tafel slope compared to pure CuHCF (119 mV/dec), and CuHCF/rGO (104 mV/dec). From Tafel slope, we have known about good electrocatalyst and it consisted of the info on rate-determining step.

Here, M stands for electrocatalyst, OHadsstands for adsorbed hydroxyl group. Consequently, the Tafel slope values of 120 mV/dec and 60 mV/dec represents the first and second electron step. In the first electron step, the RDS would be a water dissociation and in the second electron step, the RDS would be an exclusion of H from adsorbed hydroxyl group56,57. Tafel slope value of CuHCF/B-rGO exhibited the 88 mV/dec near to 120 mV/dec, which denotes the first electron transport is the RDS for OER. Therefore, these are accredited to synergetic effect between the CuHCF and B-rGO, which enhancing the electron transfer and surface reaction for OER activity.

EIS analysis

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy was executed to know about electrode kinetics and interface reaction of the electrocatalysts such as CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO in 100 kHz to 0.1 Hz at their respective onset potential and the corresponding Nyquist plot was displayed in Fig. 10e. The solution (Rs) and charge-transfer (Rct) resistance of the electrocatalysts was evaluated by using Z-fit analysis (Fig. 10e). Nyquist plot of CuHCF/B-rGO displayed the lowest Rct value of 2.97 Ω, while Rct value of other compounds such as CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO are displayed as 5.57 Ω and 4.31 Ω, respectively. Moreover, the solution resistance values of CuHCF, CuHCF/rGO and CuHCF/B-rGO were 0.999 Ω, 0.752 Ω, and 0.638 Ω, respectively. The EIS result showed that boron-doped reduced graphene oxide facilitates reaction rate because doping agent of boron can improve surface wet ability of prepared composites and significantly enhances the contact of electrolytes into the electrode58. Therefore, the boron dopant improves conductivity, and was consistent with EIS results.

ECSA studies

Electrochemical active surface area (ECSA) was employed to give further information about the function of B-rGO in CuHCF, and measured by double-layer capacitance (Cdl) (Fig. 11a-d). In water splitting application, the electrochemically active surface was evaluated by the following equation, ECSA = Rf S or Rf =; where Rf denotes Roughness and S - geometric area (here, value of S is 1 cm2). Then, the roughness factor was determined by relation, Rf = Cdl/Cs; Cdl represents the double-layer capacitance and Cs represents the specific capacitance of Ni foam (which was generally 40 mF/cm2). Therefore, the unit of ECSA is cm2. Moreover, double-layer capacitance values were calculated by using the cyclic voltammetry curves, which was measured from 1.05 V to 1.15 V vs. RHE at various scan rates such as 10 to 100 mV/s. Generally, the electrocatalytic performance was correlated to ECSA and electrical conductivity of material. The high ECSA was obtained for CuHCF/B-rGO (96.5 cm2), while ECSA for pure CuHCF and CuHCF/rGO was 92.75 and 62 cm2. Likewise, double-layer capacitance value of CuHCF/B-rGO (3.86 mF/cm2) was high, whereas the Cdl values for pure CuHCF and CuHCF/rGO were 2.48 and 3.71 mF/cm2, respectively. Accordingly, it clearly refers that the high ECSA value was mostly responsible for Cdl value59. Therefore, the existence of nanosheets with exposed edges and heteroatom dopant of boron enhances the electrocatalytic performance of the PBA catalysts.

CA analysis

After the better electrocatalytic activity, we have to find the stability of the material because durability is important to construct the best electrocatalyst for practical application. The pure CuHCF was analyzed by using chronoamperometry test for 4 h, and it exhibited excellent activity over 1.5 h. After the continuous OER process, the pure CuHCF activity was slightly decayed from 10 mA/cm2 to 6 mA/cm2, and clearly observed in the Fig. 12a. Whereas, the durability test was measured for the electrocatalyst of CuHCF/B-rGO (Fig. 12b) with the help of chronoamperometry analysis at 0.380 V vs. RHE to deliver 10 mA/cm2 for prolonged time at 16 h in an alkaline environment. The electrocatalyst of CuHCF/B-rGO exhibited the decay of 12.5% with incessant oxygen generation for an extended durability of 16 h.

Conclusion

The copper hexacyanoferrate and its carbon composites were effectively synthesized through the co-precipitation method, which explored efficient catalytic action for OER. In particular, composition of boron doped rGO with copper hexacyanoferrate (CuHCF/B-rGO) significantly develops catalytic activity and conductivity of material. It means that the CuHCF/B-rGO exhibited lower 380 mV at 10 mA/cm2 and 88 mV/dec Tafel slope, respectively due to its small charge-transfer resistance, high electrochemical active surface area and synergetic effect of metal hexacyanoferrate and carbon nanostructures. Therefore, this work paves a way for synergetic effect between copper hexacyanoferrate and heteroatom doped carbon materials for the excellent OER activity.

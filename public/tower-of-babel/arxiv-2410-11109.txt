Generic vanishing for singular varieties via Du Bois complexes
Anh Duc Vo

Abstract
. We prove appropriate generic vanishing theorems for singular varieties, generalizing the well-known generic vanishing theorem by Green and Lazarsfeld in [ 7 ] and the generic vanishing theorem of Nakano type in [ 23 ] . Our theorem explains the counterexample of Hacon and Kovács in [ 9 ] .



A.
Introduction

Cohomology vanishing statements play an essential role in algebraic geometry, starting with the well-known Kodaira vanishing theorem and extending to more general ones like Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing, Kollár vanishing, or Saito vanishing theorems. All these vanishing theorems depend on positivity assumptions, and the attempt to relax these assumptions leads to the development of generic vanishing theory. One of the most famous results is the pioneering work of Green and Lazarsfeld (see
[
7
]
*Theorem 1), which states that on a smooth projective variety
X
, the locus of topologically trivial line bundles
L
such that
H^{i}(X,L)\neq 0
has codimension at least
\dim a(X)-i
in
\Pic^{0}(X)
, where
a:X\to\Alb(X)
denotes the Albanese map of
X
. One can consider the set of topologically trivial line bundles for which the cohomology in a given degree does not vanish. More precisely, following
[
18
]
, we denote, for any coherent sheaf
F
(or more generally
F\in D_{coh}^{b}(X)
), by

V^{i}(F)\coloneqq\{L\in\Pic^{0}(X)\ |\ H^{i}(X,F\otimes L)\neq 0\}

the
i-th cohomological support locus
of
F
, and we say that
F
is
GV_{-k}
if

\codim(V^{i}(F),\Pic^{0}(X))\geq i-k

for all
i
. In this language, the classical generic vanishing theorem of Green and Lazarsfeld can be stated as:

(0.1)

\text{The canonical line bundle }\omega_{X}\text{ is }GV_{\dim a(X)-\dim X}.

This theorem and its variants have found numerous applications, ranging from the study of singularities of theta divisors
[
6
]
to the work on birational geometry of irregular varieties
[
2
]
. Later, by employing the theory of mixed Hodge modules, Popa and Schnell proved generic vanishing results for Hodge theoretic objects on abelian varieties. These led to a generic vanishing theorem of Nakano type
[
23
]
*Theorem 3.2:

(0.2)

\text{The bundle of holomorphic $p$-forms }\Omega^{p}_{X}\text{ is }GV_{p-\dim X-\delta(a)},

where
\delta(a)
is the semismallness defect of the Albanese morphism
a:X\to\Alb(X)
(see
7.1
for the definition), and led to the study of zeros of holomorphic one forms on smooth complex projective varieties
[
24
]
.

One might ask if generic vanishing theorems, say for the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X}
to begin with, continue to hold for singular varieties. The answer is positive if
X
has rational singularities, where the situation is essentially identical to that of smooth varieties. On the other hand, Hacon and Kovács
[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
constructed an example in which
X
is singular and the classical generic vanishing theorem (
0.1
) fails. Given the proofs of the known generic vanishing theorems, it is natural to expect this failure due to the fact that Hodge theory operates quite differently in the case of singular varieties.
In this article, inspired by the use of Hodge module theory in
[
23
]
, we establish natural generic vanishing statements from the point of view of the Hodge theory of singular varieties, which, in particular, explain that the example in
[
9
]
still fits well in the general framework.

Our results are phrased in terms of Du Bois complexes
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
and intersection Du Bois complexes
I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
(see Section
3
for the definitions). Given a complex algebraic variety
X
, for each
p
, the
p
-th Du Bois complex

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
is the (shifted)
p
-th associated graded term of the filtered de Rham complex
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{\bullet}
with respect to the Hodge filtration. Du Bois complexes generalize sheaves of holomorphic
p
-forms
\Omega_{X}^{p}
, and play an important role in the study of singularities via Hodge modules. On the other hand, the intersection complex
\IC_{X}^{\bullet}
and its (shifted) associated graded terms
I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
with respect to the Hodge filtration, which we call
intersection Du Bois complexes
, were introduced to generalize the Poincaré duality. In fact, we have:

I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\cong\mathbb{D}_{X}(I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}-p})[-d_{X}]

where
\mathbb{D}_{X}=R\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(-,\omega_{X}^{\bullet})
is the Grothendieck duality and
d_{X}
denotes the dimension of
X
. Due to
[
12
]
*Proposition 8.1, the intersection Du Bois complexes are related to sheaves of holomorphic forms of a resolution of singularities of
X
as:

\mathcal{H}^{0}I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\cong f_{*}\Omega_{\tilde{X}}^{p}

where
f:\tilde{X}\to X
is a resolution of singularities of
X
such that
f
is isomorphic away from
X_{\sing}
and the (reduced) exceptional locus is simple normal crossing. Moreover, both the top Du Bois complex
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}
and the top intersection Du Bois complex
I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}
are isomorphic to
f_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}}
.
We refer the readers to
[
22
]
for the discussions of Du Bois complexes and intersection Du Bois complexes. To simplify the statements, we denote by
d_{X}
the dimension of a variety
X
. Here are the main results:

Theorem A
.

Let
a:X\to A
be a morphism from a projective variety
X
to an abelian variety
A
. Then

(1)

\underline{\Omega}^{d_{X}}_{X}\cong I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}
is
GV_{d_{a(X)}-d_{X}}
.

(2)

\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}
is
GV_{p-d_{X}-\delta(a)}
and
I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}
is
GV_{p-d_{X}-\delta_{s}(a)}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

Here
\delta(a)
(resp.
\delta_{s}(a)
) is the semismallness defect (resp. stratified semismallness defect) of the morphism
a
. They are defined by the formula:

\delta(a)\coloneqq\max_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\{2l-\dim(X)+\dim(A_{l})\}

where
A_{l}=\{y\in A|\dim a^{-1}(y)\geq l\}
and

\delta_{s}(a)\coloneqq\max_{s\in\mathscr{S}}\{\delta(a|_{X_{s}})\}

where
X=\bigsqcup_{s\in\mathscr{S}}X_{s}
is the minimal Whitney stratification of
X
. When
\delta(a)=0
the morphism
a
is semismall, while
\delta_{s}(a)=0
implies that
a|_{X_{s}}
is semismall map for all strata
X_{s}
. Statement
(1)
generalizes the classical generic vanishing theorem (
0.1
), while statement
(2)
generalizes the generic Nakano vanishing theorem (
0.2
). On the other hand, for singular varieties, the Grothendieck duals
\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}-k})[-d_{X}]
play an important role but are usually different from
\underline{\Omega}^{k}_{X}
. These objects also give a natural generalization of
\Omega_{X}^{k}
; in particular, when
k=d_{X}
, the more appropriate generalization of the dualizing complex
\omega_{X}
is
\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})
. For instance, we have
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}\cong I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}\cong\omega_{X}
if
X
has rational singularities, while we only need
X
to be Cohen-Macaulay and have Du Bois singularities to get
\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{X})[-d_{X}]\cong\omega_{X}
. It is therefore necessary to have a statement for these objects as well.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be an irreducible projective variety with a morphism
a:X\to A
to an abelian variety. Then

(1)

\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{X})[-d_{X}]
is
GV_{d}
where
d=\min\{d_{a(X)}-d_{X},-d_{F}(a)-\lcdef(X)\}
.

(2)

\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}-p})[-d_{X}]
is
GV_{p-d_{X}-\delta_{s}(a)-\lcdef(X)}
for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
.

Here
\lcdef(X)
is the local cohomological defect of
X
, and
d_{F}(a)
is the stratified relative dimension of the morphism
a
(see Definition
4.1
and
8.3
). The
local cohomological defect
of
X
is defined as

\lcdef(X)=\lcd(X,Y)-\codim(X,Y)

where
X\subseteq Y
is an embedding in a smooth variety, with local cohomological dimension
\lcd(X,Y)
. It does not depend on the embedding, and there are intersting classes of varieties with
\lcdef(X)=0
, for instance local complete intersections. The stratifed relative dimension
d_{F}(a)
is defined as the maximal relative dimension of the maps
a|_{X_{s}}
where
X_{s}
is a strata inside the singular locus of
X
. In fact, this invariant explains the failure of the classical generic vanishing theorem for the example in
[
9
]
. Hacon and Kovács constructed a generically finite morphism from a Gorenstein log canonical variety
T
to an abelian variety
A
, which contracted the one-dimensional singular locus of
X
to a point, and showed that
\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}^{0}_{X})[-d_{X}]\cong\omega_{X}
is not
GV_{0}
. In this example, we have
d_{a(X)}-d_{X}=\lcdef(X)=0
and
d_{F}(a)=1
, and our Theorem
B
indicates that
\omega_{X}
should be
GV_{-1}
.

As a consequence of Theorem
B
, we deduce the non-negativity of Euler characteristic.

Proposition C
.

Let
X
be normal variety with maximal Albanese dimension such that
d_{F}(a)=0
and
\lcdef(X)=0
, then
\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})[-d_{X}]
is a
GV
-sheaf and
\chi(\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})[-d_{X}])\geq 0
.

In particular, if
X
also has Du Bois singularities, then
X
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\chi(\omega_{X})\geq 0
.

B.
Preliminaries

In this paper, we work over
\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
D_{coh}^{b}(X)
(resp.
D^{b}_{c}(X)
) the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves (resp. constructible sheaves over
\mathbb{C}
) on
X
. The Grothendieck duality of
F\in D_{coh}^{b}(X)
is defined as

\mathbb{D}_{X}(F)\coloneqq R\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F,\omega_{X}^{\bullet})

where
\omega_{X}^{\bullet}
is the dualizing complex of
X
. Note that when
X
is smooth, we have
\mathbb{D}_{X}(F)\cong R\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F,\omega_{X})[d_{X}]
which explains the difference of the indices in our results, comparing to the existing literature in the smooth case.

A resolution of singularity
f:\tilde{X}\to X
is called a log resolution of
X
if
f
is an isomorphism over the regular locus
X_{\reg}
and
f^{-1}(X_{\sing})_{\red}
is a simple normal crossing divisor where
X_{\sing}
is the singular locus of
X
.

1.
Mixed Hodge modules

We only recall some facts about mixed Hodge modules needed in this article. The main references are Saito’s papers
[
27
]
and
[
28
]
.

Given a smooth complex algebraic variety
Y
, we denote by
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
the sheaf of differential operators on
Y
. We refer to
[
11
]
for a complete treatment of
\mathscr{D}
-modules. All the
\mathscr{D}
-modules we consider in this paper are right
\mathscr{D}
-modules. For any coherent
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, there exists a
good
filtration
F_{*}\mathcal{M}
i.e. an increasing, exhaustive, bounded below filtration of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-modules such that

F_{l}\mathcal{M}.F_{k}\mathscr{D}_{Y}\subseteq F_{k+l}\mathcal{M}

and the equality holds for all
l>l_{0}
for some
l_{0}
. One can associate to such a
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module the de Rham complex

\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M})=[\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\bigwedge^{d_{Y}}T_{Y}\to\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\bigwedge^{d_{Y}-1}T_{Y}\to\dots\to\mathcal{M}]

supported in degrees from
-d_{Y}
to
0
, in which the differential is induced by the (right)
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module structure
\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}T_{Y}\to M
. A good filtration on
\mathcal{M}
defines subcomplexes

F_{k}\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M})=[F_{k-d_{Y}}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\bigwedge^{d_{Y}}T_{Y}\to F_{k-d_{Y}+1}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\bigwedge^{d_{Y}-1}T_{Y}\to\dots\to F_{k}\mathcal{M}]

and the associated graded complexes

\gr^{F}_{k}\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M})=[\gr^{F}_{k-d_{Y}}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\bigwedge^{d_{Y}}T_{Y}\to\gr^{F}_{k-d_{Y}+1}\mathcal{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\bigwedge^{d_{Y}-1}T_{Y}\to\dots\to\gr^{F}_{k}\mathcal{M}].

Since some of the results in literature use left
\mathscr{D}
-modules, we recall the equivalence of categories of left and right
\mathscr{D}
-modules for the convenience of the readers. To a right
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{M}
, one can associate a left
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module

\mathcal{M}^{l}=\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}(\omega_{Y},\mathcal{M})\cong\omega_{Y}^{-1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\mathcal{M},

and to get back to the right
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module, we simply tensor with
\omega_{Y}
. Via this correspondence, the induced good filtration is indexed by the convention

F_{k}\mathcal{M}^{l}=\omega_{Y}^{-1}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}F_{k-d_{Y}}\mathcal{M}.

Consequently, we have the following formulas for the associated de Rham complex

\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M}^{l})=[\mathcal{M}^{l}\to\mathcal{M}^{l}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\dots\to\mathcal{M}^{l}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\omega_{Y}]

and its associated graded pieces

\gr^{F}_{k}\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M}^{l})=[\gr^{F}_{k}\mathcal{M}^{l}\to\gr^{F}_{k+1}\mathcal{M}^{l}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\Omega^{1}_{Y}\to\dots\to\gr^{F}_{k+d_{Y}}\mathcal{M}^{l}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}\omega_{Y}].

Given an embedding of a variety
X
into a smooth variety
Y
, a mixed Hodge module
M=(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M},\mathcal{P},\alpha,W)
on
X
consists of a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module with a good filtration (Hodge filtration)
F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
, a perverse sheaf
\mathcal{P}
over
\mathbb{Q}
on
X^{\text{an}}
such that
\mathcal{P}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}\cong\DR_{Y}(\mathcal{M})
via
\alpha
, and an increasing weight filtration
W
. We will abuse the notation by also writing
F_{\bullet}M
for the Hodge filtration of the underlying
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
module of
M
. These data are supposed to satisfy a complicated set of conditions we will not discuss. We denote by
MHM(X)
the abelian category of mixed Hodge modules on
X
; this category is independent of the embedding of
X
into smooth varieties. Furthermore, there is a natural faithful and exact functor

(1.1)

\text{rat}:MHM(X)\to\Perv(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})

where
\Perv(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})
is the category of
\mathbb{Q}
-perverse sheaves on
X^{an}
with algebraic stratification and
rat
takes the underlying rational structure of a mixed Hodge module. The category
MHM(X)
contains a subcategory
MH(X,w)^{p}
of polarizable Hodge modules of pure weight
w

[
27
]
*5.1.6, 5.2.10. We say
M
has strict support
Z
if the underlying perverse sheaf
\text{rat}(M)
is an intersection complex with support
Z
. These objects form a full subcategory
MH_{Z}(X,w)^{p}
of
MH(X,w)^{p}
, and we have the decomposition by strict support

(1.2)

MH(X,w)^{p}=\oplus_{Z\subseteq X}MH_{Z}(X,w)^{p}.

The Tate twist
M(k)
of a mixed Hodge module
M
has the same underlying
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-module, but the two filtrations are shifted by

F_{p}M(k)=F_{p-k}M\quad\text{and}\quad W_{q}M(k)=W_{q+2k}M

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For a mixed Hodge module
M
, its de Rham complex is the one for the underlying filtered
\mathcal{D}_{Y}
-modules. It is well-known that the associated graded complex
\gr^{F}_{p}\DR_{Y}(M)
is a complex of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. Moreover, this is well defined for complexes of mixed Hodge modules
M^{\bullet}
, thereby inducing the graded de Rham functor

\gr^{F}_{p}\DR_{Y}:D^{b}MHM(X)\to D_{coh}^{b}(X)

from the bounded derived category of mixed Hodge modules on
X
to the bounded derived category of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. This functor is independent of the embedding of
X
into smooth varieties (see
[
28
, Proposition 2.33]
); and because of that reason, we will omit the subscript and write
\gr^{F}_{p}\DR
for the graded de Rham functor.

One beautiful accomplishment of Saito’s theory of mixed Hodge modules is the construction of natural functors
f_{*},f_{!},f^{*},f^{!},\otimes,\mathcal{H}om,\mathbf{D}_{X}
in
D^{b}MHM(X)
such that they are compatible with the corresponding functors on the underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-complexes via
rat:D^{b}MHM(X)\to D^{b}\Perv(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})
. Moreover, we emphasize the following facts that will be used throughout the paper (see
[
28
, Section 4]
).

Proposition 1.3
.

Given a morphism
f:X\to Y
between algebraic varieties, there are canonical isomorphisms:

\mathbf{D}_{X}\circ\mathbf{D}_{X}\cong Id,\quad\mathbf{D}_{Y}\circ f_{*}\cong f_{!}\circ\mathbf{D}_{X},\quad\mathbf{D}_{X}\circ f^{*}\cong f^{!}\circ\mathbf{D}_{Y}.

For a cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

we have canonical isomorphisms:

g^{!}\circ f_{*}\cong f^{\prime}_{*}\circ(g^{\prime})^{!},\quad g^{*}\circ f_{!}\cong f^{\prime}_{!}\circ(g^{\prime})^{*}.

Similarly, these are compatible with the natural isomorphisms on the underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-complexes.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that we have two different duality functors up to now:
\mathbb{D}_{X}
for the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves and
\mathbf{D}_{X}
for the bounded derived category of mixed Hodge modules.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
i:Z\to X
be a closed immersion and
j:U=X\smallsetminus Z\to X
the immersion of the complement. Then we have the following functorial triangles for any
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}MHM(X)
:

j_{!}j^{*}M^{\bullet}\to M^{\bullet}\to i_{*}i^{*}M^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{+1}

and

i_{*}i^{!}M^{\bullet}\to M^{\bullet}\to j_{*}j^{*}M^{\bullet}\xrightarrow{+1}

compatible with those for the underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-complexes.

The proof of the main results relies on an in-depth understanding of the graded de Rham functor
\gr^{F}_{p}\DR
. Here, we recall the behaviors of this functor with respect to the natural functors in
D^{b}MHM(X)
in the most general form (see
[
27
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
20
]
)

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper morphism of algebraic varieties. For any
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}MHM(X)
, we have natural isomorphisms

\gr^{F}_{p}\DR(f_{*}(M^{\bullet}))\cong Rf_{*}(\gr^{F}_{p}\DR(M^{\bullet}))

in
D_{coh}^{b}(Y)
, and

\mathbb{D}_{X}(\gr^{F}_{p}DR(M^{\bullet}))\cong\gr^{F}_{-p}DR(\mathbf{D}_{X}(M^{\bullet}))

in
D_{coh}^{b}(X)
.

2.
Decomposition theorem and Torsion-freeness theorem for Hodge modules of pure weight

Given a projective variety
X
, for any
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}MHM(X)
, we define

p(M^{\bullet})\coloneqq\min\{p\ |\ \gr^{F}_{p}\DR(M^{\bullet})\text{ is not acyclic}\}.

If we fix an embedding of
X
into a smooth variety
Y
and
M
is a complex of mixed Hodge module on
Y
supported on
X
, then
p(M)
is exactly the index of the lowest nonzero Hodge filtration.

Now, we recall Saito’s results, generalizing Kollár’s decomposition and torsion freeness theorem.

Theorem 2.1

(Saito’s decomposition theorem)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a projective morphism between two smooth complex varieties. Let
M\in MH(X,w)^{p}
be a polarizable Hodge module of pure weight
w
. Then each
H^{i}f_{*}M\in MH(Y,w+i)^{p}
is of pure weight
w+i
and admits a non canonical isomorphism

f_{*}M\cong\bigoplus_{i}H^{i}(f_{*}M)[-i].

Because of the strict support decomposition

MH(X,w)^{p}=\bigoplus_{Z}MH_{Z}(X,w)^{p},

each
H^{i}(f_{*}M)
admits further decomposition by strict support. Due to the commutativity of
\gr^{F}_{p}\DR
and
f_{*}
, we get
p(H^{i}f_{*}M)\geq p(f_{*}M)\geq p(M)
. It turns out that we can detect the inequality of lowest Hodge indexes of the decomposition by the strict support.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
29
, Proposition 2.6]
,
[
29
]
*Theorem 3.2)
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a projective morphism between two complex algebraic varieties and
M\in MH_{Z^{\prime}}(X,w)^{p}
where
Z^{\prime}\subseteq X
. Let
H^{i}f_{*}M=\bigoplus_{Z}M^{i}_{Z}
be the decomposition by strict support. Then
p(M^{i}_{Z})>p(M)
if
Z\neq f(Z^{\prime})
.

As a consequence, if
M\in MH_{X}(X,w)^{p}
, then

Rf_{*}\gr^{F}_{p(M)}\DR(M)\cong\bigoplus_{p(M^{i}_{f(X)})=p(M)}\gr^{F}_{p(M)}\DR(M^{i}_{f(X)})[-i].

When
f:X\to Y
is a surjective map with
X
smooth and
M=\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]
the trivial mixed Hodge module (see Section
3
for the definition), Theorem
2.2
says that

Rf_{*}\omega_{X}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\gr^{F}_{-d_{X}}\DR(M^{i}_{Y})[-i]

and that
p(H^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[d_{X}])\geq-d_{X}
and

\gr^{F}_{-d_{X}}\DR(M^{i}_{Y})=gr^{F}_{-d_{X}}\DR(H^{i}f_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}])=R^{i}f_{*}\omega_{X}

by Proposition
1.6
and the strictness of the Hodge filtration. This recovers Kollár’s decomposition and torsion-freeness theorem which states that

(1)

R^{i}f_{*}\omega_{X}
is torsion-free.

(2)

Rf_{*}\omega_{X}\cong\bigoplus_{i}R^{i}f_{*}\omega_{X}[-i]
.

3.
Intersection complex and Du Bois complex

Mixed Hodge modules are defined inductively on the dimension of the variety
X
. In the base case, when
X=\Spec(\mathbb{C})
is a point, the category
MHM(pt)
is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures (see
[
28
, Theorem 3.9]
). In particular, there is a trivial mixed Hodge module on a point

\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{pt}\coloneqq(\mathbb{C},F_{\bullet}\mathbb{C},\mathbb{Q};W)

where
F_{0}\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C},\ F_{-1}\mathbb{C}=0
and
W_{0}\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q},\ W_{-1}\mathbb{Q}=0
; it corresponds to the trivial Hodge structure
\mathbb{Q}
of weight
0
.

Let
X
be an algebraic variety. The trivial mixed Hodge module on
X
is defined as

\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}\coloneqq a_{X}^{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{pt}

where
a_{X}:X\to pt
. When
X
is smooth, one can easily see that

\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]=(\omega_{X},F_{\bullet}\omega_{X},\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[d_{X}])

is a polarizable Hodge module of pure weight
d_{X}
with
F_{-d_{X}}\omega_{X}=\omega_{X}
and
F_{-d_{X}-1}\omega_{X}=0
. In general, the underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-complex of
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}
is the constant sheaf
\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}
, and the cohomologies of the direct image
a_{X*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}\in D^{b}MHM(pt)
give us the mixed Hodge structures on the singular cohomologies of
X
with rational coefficient. Since the functor
rat
is faithful and
\prescript{p}{}{\mathcal{H}}^{i}(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}[d_{X}])=0
for
i>0
, we get

H^{i}\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}=0\quad\text{for}\quad i>d_{X}.

We say that
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}MHM(X)
is of weight
\leq w
(resp.
\geq w
) if

\gr^{W}_{q}H^{i}M=0\quad\text{for}\quad q>w+i\text{ (resp. }q<w+i).

Moreover, this condition is stable under the functors
f_{!},f^{*}
(resp.
f_{*},f^{!}
). As a consequence, the trivial mixed Hodge module
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}
is of weight
\leq 0
; in particular,

\gr^{W}_{i}H^{d_{X}}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}=0\quad\text{for}\quad i>d_{X}.

If
X
is irreducible or equidimensional, the boundary term
\gr^{W}_{d_{X}}H^{d_{X}}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}
is exactly the
intersection complex

\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}
, which, by definition, is the unique object in
MHM(X)
such that its restriction to
X_{reg}
is
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X_{reg}}[d_{X}]
and it has no subobject or quotient in
MHM(X)
whose support is contained in
X_{\sing}
. We know that
\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}
is simple and pure of weight
d_{X}
, and its underlying
\mathbb{Q}
-complex is the well-known intersection cohomology complex
\IC_{X}^{\bullet}
defined as the intermediate extension of constant sheaf
\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{X_{\reg}}
on the smooth locus of
X
. Similar to the self-duality of
\IC_{X}^{\bullet}
, the intersection complex is self-dual up to a Tate twist

\mathbf{D}_{X}(\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H})\cong\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}(d_{X}).

We refer the reader to
[
28
, Section 4.5]
for details.

Now, we focus on the object of our interest - the (intersection) Du Bois complex. Here we only recall important facts and refer the readers to
[
25
]
and
[
22
]
for detailed discussion of (intersection) Du Bois complexes. For a smooth projective variety
X
, the de Rham complex is a resolution of constant sheaf
\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{X}
by sheaves of differential forms

\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{d}\Omega_{X}^{1}\xrightarrow{d}\dots\xrightarrow{d}\Omega^{d_{X}}_{X}.

The filtration by truncation
F^{p}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}=\Omega^{\bullet\geq p}_{X}

induces the Hodge structures on the singular cohomology of
X
. In
[
5
]
, Du Bois constructed the Du Bois complex
\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{X}
via hyperresolution as an analog of the de Rham complex for singular varieties. It is an object in the derived category of filtered complexes on
X
, quasi-isomorphic to the constant sheaf
\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{X}
. The
p
-th (shifted) associated graded piece

\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}\coloneqq\gr^{p}_{F}\underline{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{X}[p]

is an object in
D_{coh}^{b}(X)
, which we call
p
-th Du Bois complex
. For any
p
, there is a natural map

\Omega^{p}_{X}\to\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}

which is an isomorphism if
X
is smooth. Even though Du Bois complexes are complicated objects, the top Du Bois complex
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}
can be described simply due to
[
32
]
*Proposition 3.3 as:

\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}\cong Rf_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}}\cong f_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}}

where
\tilde{X}
is a log resolution of
X
and the second isomorphism follows from Grauert-Riemenschneider vanishing theorem. From the perspective of mixed Hodge modules, as a consequence of
[
30
, Theorem 4.2, Corollary 0.3]
, Saito shows that

(3.1)

\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}\cong\gr^{F}_{-p}\DR(\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}])[p-d_{X}]

for any
p
, giving mixed Hodge modules interpretation for Du Bois complexes. Similarly, we define

(3.2)

I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\coloneqq\gr^{F}_{-p}\DR(\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H})[p-d_{X}]

for intersection complex and call this
intersection Du Bois complex
. The intersection Du Bois complexes generalize Kähler differentials while maintaining the Poincaré duality. More precisely, the self-duality
\mathbf{D}_{X}(\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H})\cong\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}(d_{X})
implies that

\mathbb{D}_{X}(I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{k})\cong I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}-k}[d_{X}].

The natural morphism
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]\to\gr^{W}_{d_{X}}H^{d_{X}}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}\cong\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}
induces canonical morphisms

\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X}\to I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}

and dually

I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{p}\to\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}^{d_{X}-p}_{X})[-d_{X}].

Moreover, the definitions of (intersection) Du Bois complexes imply that
p(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[d_{X}])=p(\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H})=-d_{X}
and
p(\mathbf{D}(\mathbb{Q}_{X}^{H}[d_{X}]))=0
.

4.
Local cohomological dimension

An important invariant in the study of Du Bois complexes is the local cohomological dimension. If a variety
X
is embedded into a smooth variety
Y
, the local cohomological dimension of
X
in
Y
, considered by Ogus in
[
17
]
, is defined as

(4.1)

\lcd(X,Y)\coloneqq\max\{i|\ \mathcal{H}^{i}_{X}(F)\neq 0\ \text{ for any coherent sheaf $F$}\}.

By
[
17
, Proposition 2.1]
, it suffices to consider
F=\mathcal{O}_{Y}
in the definition of
\lcd(X,Y)
. Moreover, it is known that
\mathcal{H}^{i}_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
vanishes when
i
lies outside the range
\codim(X,Y)\leq i\leq\lcd(X,Y)
(see
[
17
]
*Theorem 2.8). We recall several characterizations of
\lcd(X,Y)
in terms of Du Bois complexes of
X
and the trivial Hodge module
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
16
, Corollary 12.6]
)
.

For every positive integer
c
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\lcd(X,Y)-d_{Y}\leq c
.

(2)

\mathcal{E}xt^{j+p+1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\underline{\Omega}^{p}_{X},\omega_{Y}^{\bullet})=0
for all
j\geq c
and
p\geq 0
.

By using Proposition
1.6
, it is not hard to obtain the following characterization of
\lcd(X,Y)
:

Theorem 4.3

(
[
17
]
*Theorem 2.13,
[
26
, Theorem 1]
,
[
Bhatt
]
*Section 3.1)
.

With the above notations,

\codim(X,Y)-\lcd(X,Y)=\min\{i|H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}])\neq 0\}.

The
local cohomological defect
of
X
is defined to be

\lcdef(X)\coloneqq\lcd(X,Y)-\codim(X,Y).

This quantity is independent of the embedding
X\subset Y
by Proposition
4.2
. Moreover,

0\leq\lcdef(X)\leq d_{X}

since it is well-known that
\mathcal{H}^{i}_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
= 0 for
i>d_{Y}
.

Remark 4.4
.

\lcdef(X)=0
is equivalent to say that
\codim(X,Y)=\lcd(X,Y)
; in other words, the only nonzero local cohomology is
\mathcal{H}^{\codim(X,Y)}_{X}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. Obviously, smooth varieties have zero local cohomological defect. This also holds for local complete intersections or rational homology manifolds.

5.
Integral functors and GV-objects

First, we recall the definition of cohomological support loci. Let
X
and
Y
be projective varieties, and
P
a perfect object on
D_{coh}^{b}(X\times Y)
(i.e., an object represented by a bounded complex of locally free sheaves of finite rank). For any
F\in D_{coh}^{b}(X)
, we denote by

V^{i}_{P}(F)\coloneqq\{y\in Y\ |\ H^{i}(X,F\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}Li_{y}^{*}P)\neq 0\}

the
i
-th cohomological support locus
of
F
with respect to
P
, where
i_{y}:X\times\{y\}\hookrightarrow X\times Y
. The content of the generic vanishing theory is to study the cohomological support locus, when
Y=\Pic^{0}(X)
and
P=P_{X}
is the universal line bundle; thanks to many people, the structure of these sets is well-understood in the following aspects:

•

One has
\codim V^{i}_{P_{X}}(\omega_{X})\geq i-\dim X+\dim a(X)
for all
i
(see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
). This implies the classical generic vanishing of Green and Lazarsfeld by applying Serre duality.

•

The irreducible components of each
V^{i}_{P_{X}}(\omega_{X})
are torsion translates of abelian subvarieties of
\Pic^{0}(X)
(see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
31
]
)

In
[
10
]
, Hacon observes that generic vanishing property is closely related to the Fourier-Mukai transforms studied by Mukai in
[
15
]
. Let us recall this relation in the general setting. Let
p_{X}
and
p_{Y}
are the projections from
X\times Y
to
X
and
Y
correspondingly. We define the
integral functors
:

R\Phi_{P}:D_{coh}^{b}(X)\to D_{coh}^{b}(Y),\quad R\Phi_{P}(\cdot)\coloneqq Rp_{Y*}(Lp_{X}^{*}(\cdot)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X\times Y}}P)

and

R\Psi_{P}:D_{coh}^{b}(Y)\to D_{coh}^{b}(X),\quad R\Psi_{P}(\cdot)\coloneqq Rp_{X*}(Lp_{Y}^{*}(\cdot)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X\times Y}}P)

Although the locus
\supp(R^{i}\Phi_{P}(F))
is only contained inside
V^{i}_{P}(\mathcal{F})
, by a standard cohomological base change argument, the two loci carry the same numerical information in the sense that for any nonnegative integer
k
, the following are equivalent
[
18
, Lemma 3.6]
:

(1)

\codim(V^{i}_{P}(F),Y)\geq i-k
for all
i\geq 0
.

(2)

\codim(\supp R^{i}\Phi_{P}(F),Y)\geq i-k
for all
i\geq 0
.

Thus one can study the support of integral functors to gain information of the dimension of cohomological support loci. It is then natural to introduce the following notions (see
[
18
]
*Section 2 and 3 for details):

Definition 5.1
.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, an object
F\in D_{coh}^{b}(X)
is said to be a
GV_{-k}
-object with respect to
P
if

\codim(\supp R^{i}\Phi_{P}(F),Y)\geq i-k\quad\text{for all }i\geq 0

When
k=0
, we simply say
F
is a
GV
-object.

Another key concept in Fourier-Mukai theory is that of an object satisfying
WIT
(Weak Index Theorem) on abelian varieties. This property is generalized to the theory of integral functors:

Definition 5.2
.

An object
F\in D_{coh}^{b}(X)
satisfies
Weak Index Theorem with index
j
, with respect to
P
, if
R^{i}\Phi_{P}(F)=0
for all
i\neq j
. For simplicity, we also say that
F
is
WIT_{j}
(with respect to
P
).

More generally, we say
F
is
WIT_{\geq j}
(resp.
WIT_{\leq j}
) if
R^{i}\Phi_{P}(F)=0
for all
i<j
(resp.
i>j
) (with respect to
P
).

Remark 5.3
.

From the definitions, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&F\text{ is }GV_{-k}\text{ if and only if }F[j]\text{ is }GV_{j-k}\text{ and }\\
&F\text{ is }WIT_{\geq k}\text{ if and only if }F[j]\text{ is }WIT_{\geq k-j}\text{ for any }j\leq k.\end{split}

Let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a morphism from a projective variety
X^{\prime}
to
X
and
P^{\prime}\coloneqq L(f\times id_{Y})^{*}(P)
a perfect object in
D_{coh}^{b}(X^{\prime}\times Y)
. Assume further that
Y
is smooth; this condition is satisfied, for instance, when

is normal or abelian variety and

. Consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}\times Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{X^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\times id_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{Y}^{\prime}}

For any

, by projection formula and base change, we get

\displaystyle R\Phi_{P}(Rf_{*}F)

\displaystyle=Rp_{Y*}(Lp_{X}^{*}(Rf_{*}F)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}P)

\displaystyle\cong Rp_{Y*}\big(R(f\times id_{Y})_{*}(Lp_{X^{\prime}}^{*}F)\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}P\big)\quad(\text{by base change})

\displaystyle=R\Phi_{P^{\prime}}(F)

Thus we can say

(resp.

) on

(with respect to

) and

(resp.

) on

The

and

properties are closely related via duality. Note that by base change, for any sufficiently ample line bundle

on

is
WIT_{d_{Y}}
on

and

. Using this,

and

Theorem 5.4

(
[

]
*Theorem 1.2,

)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be projective Cohen-Macaulay varieties and
P
a perfect object in
D_{coh}^{b}(X\times Y)
. For

and

, the following are equivalent:

(1)

F
is a
GV_{-k}
-object with respect to

.

(2)

\mathbb{D}_{X}(F)
is

with respect to

where

(3)

\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,F\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}R^{d_{Y}}\Psi_{P}(L^{-1}))=0
for all

and for any sufficiently ample line bundle

.

With these notations, the classical generic vanishing states that
\omega_{X}
is

, or equivalently,
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is

.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
F\to G\to H\xrightarrow{+1}
be a distinguished triangle in

. Suppose

is

with respect to

for some

is
GV_{-k}
if and only if

is
GV_{-k}
with respect to the same perfect object

.

Proof.

Since

is locally free, tensoring by

is exact; so we obtain a long exact sequence

\dots\to\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,F\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}R^{d_{Y}}\Psi_{P}(L^{-1}))\to\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,G\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}R^{d_{Y}}\Psi_{P}(L^{-1}))\to\\
\to\mathbb{H}^{i}(X,H\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}R^{d_{Y}}\Psi_{P}(L^{-1}))\to\mathbb{H}^{i+1}(X,F\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}R^{d_{Y}}\Psi_{P}(L^{-1}))\to\dots.

The statement now follows from Theorem

.
∎

6.
Generic vanishing of mixed Hodge modules on abelian varieties

On an abelian variety
A
, any ample line bundle
L
induces an isogeny
\phi_{L}:\hat{A}\to A
. This property was used in
[
23
]
to obtain
GV
properties of mixed Hodge modules on
A
. Since
P_{A}^{\vee}=(1\times(-1))^{*}P_{A}
for any abelian variety
A
, we will say
GV_{-k}
or
WIT_{\geq-k}
on
A
without specifying the perfect object.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
23
, Corollary 9.3]
)
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety, and
M
a mixed Hodge module on
A
. Then we have
\gr^{F}_{k}(M)
and
\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(M)
are
GV
on
A
for any
k
.

Note that
\mathbf{D}_{A}(M)
is still a mixed Hodge module on
A
; thus we can apply Theorem
6.1
to
\mathbf{D}_{A}(M)
to obtain
\mathbb{D}_{A}(\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(M))\cong\gr^{F}_{-k}\DR(\mathbf{D}_{A}(M))
is
GV
on

. By Theorem
5.4
, this means
\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(M)
is both
GV
and
WIT_{\geq 0}
on
A
for any
k
.
With a simple argument, we obtain
GV
properties for objects in
D^{b}MHM(A)
based on their cohomological degrees.

Corollary 6.2
.

If
M^{\bullet}\in D^{b}MHM(A)
with

for

or

for non-negative integers

and

, then
\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(M^{\bullet})
is

and
\mathbb{D}_{A}(\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(M^{\bullet}))
is

on

.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{D}_{A}(\gr^{F}_{k}\DR(M^{\bullet}))\cong\gr^{F}_{-k}\DR(\mathbf{D}_{A}(M^{\bullet}))
and

or

, it suffices to show that

is

. By considering

, we can assume

. With this assumption, we prove the first statement by induction on

. The base case,

. If

. Moreover, the last term

∎

Now, let’s briefly recall Hacon’s proof of the classical generic vanishing theorem. Since

Ra_{*}\omega_{X}\cong\oplus_{i}R^{i}a_{*}\omega_{X}[-i]

By Corollary

, it is enough to show that

if

, which follows from Kollár’s torsion-freeness theorem that

is torsion-free. Analogously, we have

.

Let

be a projective morphism to an abelian variety and

.

By Theorem

, we have

Note that if

. Thus

∎

C.
Generalization of classical generic vanishing for singular varieties

Before discussing the main theorems, Corollary
6.2
suggests that we need to study the cohomological amplitude of direct image of
\mathbb{Q}_{X}[d_{X}]
and
\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}
.

7.
(Stratified) semismallness defect

The semismallness defect is an important invariant of singularities that controls the generic vanishing of Nakano type
[
23
]
. This explains the counter-example of Green and Lazarsfeld that the index in the generic Nakano vanishing theorem is not equal to the dimension of the generic fiber of the Albanese mapping (see
[
23
]
*Example 12.3). When
X
is smooth, this invariant is studied by de Cataldo and Migliorini (see
[
4
, Remark 2.1.2]
and
[
23
, Proposition 11.2]
)

Definition 7.1

(
[
4
, Definition 4.7.2]
)
.

The
semismallness defect
of a morphism
a:X\to A
is

\delta(a)\coloneqq\max_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\{2l-\dim(X)+\dim(A_{l})\}

where
A_{l}=\{y\in A|\dim a^{-1}(y)\geq l\}
.

Their result says that

\delta(a)=\max\{H^{i}a_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]\neq 0\}=-\min\{H^{i}a_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]\neq 0\}

in which the second equality is due to the self-duality of
\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]\cong\IC_{X}\mathbb{Q}^{H}
, given the smoothness of
X
. By using this characterization, Popa and Schnell show in
[
23
, Theorem 3.2]
that

\Omega^{p}_{X}\text{ is }GV_{p-d_{X}-\delta(a)}\text{ for every }p\in\mathbb{N}

or equivalently,

\codim V^{q}(\Omega^{p}_{X})\geq p+q-d_{X}-\delta(a)

for every
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, and there exist
p
and
q
for which the equality holds.

When
X
is singular, the semismallness defect no longer controls the cohomological amplitude of
a_{*}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]
. For example, consider an embedding of
X
into an abelian variety
A
, in which case
\delta(a)=0
and
\lcdef(X)>0
, but we have seen in Theorem
4.3
that
H^{-\lcdef(X)}\mathbb{Q}^{H}_{X}[d_{X}]\neq 0
. The issue is that
\delta(a)
does not take into account the interaction of the singularity of
X
and the morphism
a:X\to A
. To remedy this, we consider Whitney stratifications of
X
, which stratify
X
into smooth “equisingular loci” (see
[
11
, Definition E.3.7]
for precise definition). In
[
33
]
, Teissier proves the existence of a minimal Whitney stratification
X=\bigsqcup_{s\in\mathscr{S}}X_{s}
, where
\mathscr{S}
is the index set. In other words, any Whitney stratification of
X
is a refinement of this minimal one. This gives us a canonical choice of Whitney stratification.

Definition 7.2
.

The
stratified semismallness defect
of a morphism
a:X\to A
is

\delta_{s}(a)\coloneqq\max_{s\in\mathscr{S}}\{\delta(a|_{X_{s}})\}

where
X=\bigsqcup_{s\in\mathscr{S}}X_{s}
is the minimal Whitney stratification of
X
.

Clearly, if
X
is smooth, we have
\delta_{s}(a)=\delta(a)
since
X
itself is the minimal Whitney stratification. The following Proposition allows us to control the cohomological amplitude of the direct image of constructible sheaves with respect to the minimal Whitney stratification.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
a:X\to A
be a morphism and
X=\bigsqcup_{s\in\mathscr{S}}X_{s}
the minimal Whitney stratification of
X
. Then for any
F\in D^{b}_{\mathscr{S}}(X)
i.e.,

has locally constant cohomology sheaves (of finite type) for all
s\in\mathscr{S}
(see
[

(1)

If
F\in\Dp^{b}_{\mathscr{S}}(X)^{\leq 0}
, then
a_{!}F\in\Dp^{b}_{\mathscr{S}}(X)^{\leq\delta_{s}(a)}
.

(2)

If
F\in\Dp^{b}_{\mathscr{S}}(X)^{\geq 0}
, then
a_{*}F\in\Dp^{b}_{\mathscr{S}}(X)^{\geq-\delta_{s}(a)}
.

Before proving this proposition, let us consider the following lemma:

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
a:X\to A
be a morphism from
X
to
A
, where
X
is smooth. For any local system
L
on
X
, we have

(1)

\prescript{p}{}{\mathcal{H}}^{k}a_{!}L[d_{X}]=0
for
k>\delta(a)
.

(2)

\prescript{p}{}{\mathcal{H}}^{k}a_{*}L[d_{X}]=0
for
k<-\delta(a)
.

Proof.

Consider a stratification
A=\bigsqcup_{t\in\mathscr{T}}A_{t}
with embeddings
i_{A_{t}}:A_{t}\to A
such that
i^{-1}_{A_{t}}a_{!}L
have locally constant cohomology sheaves and all fibers of
a
over
A_{t}
have the same dimension. By
[

, Proposition 8.1.22(i)]
, the first statement is equivalent to

\mathcal{H}^{j}(i^{-1}_{A_{t}}a_{!}L[d_{X}])=0

for any
t\in\mathscr{T}
and
j>-d_{A_{t}}+\delta(a)
. Note that each stratum
A_{t}
is contained in some
A_{d}\coloneqq\{p\in A|\dim a^{-1}(p)=d\}
. Since pullback is exact, it is enough to show that

(\mathcal{H}^{j}a_{!}L[d_{X}])_{p}=0

for any
p\in A_{t}
and

. By base change theorem (Proposition

), we get

(\mathcal{H}^{j}a_{!}L[d_{X}])_{p}\cong H^{j+d_{X}}_{c}(a^{-1}(p),L)=0

. By the definition of the semismall defect, we have
2d-d_{X}\leq-d_{A_{t}}+\delta(a)
, which concludes the proof.

Since
X
is smooth, the second statement follows from the first one by Verdier duality.
∎

Proposition 8.1

(Theorem

)
.

Let

On the other hand, since

The generic Nakano vanishing follows from Corollary
7.6
, Corollary

3.1
,
3.2

Remark 8.2
.

It can be shown that
\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}\cong I\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{d_{X}}\cong f_{*}\omega_{\tilde{X}}

The other generalization

be the minimal Whitney stratification of

(Theorem

)
.

Let

The generic Nakano vanishing follows again from Corollary

For the first statement, let

In many moduli problems, we often encounter the situation of an equisingular family over a smooth base. A concrete formulation of this concept is Whitney equisingular morphism.

Definition 8.6

[

*Definition 1.2
We say a morphism

Generic vanishing theorems are especially useful when the variety has maximal Albanese dimension i.e.

has maximal Albanese dimension, then

In the singular setting, we obtain an analogous statement by means of Theorem

and Albanese variety

, which satisfies the expected universal properties. Under this assumption, we have:

(Proposition

)
.

Let

be normal variety with maximal Albanese dimension such that

and

\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})[-d_{X}]

-sheaf and

Proof.

By Theorem

, we know that
\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})[-d_{X}]
is a
GV
-object. The assumption
\lcdef(X)=0
implies that

\mathcal{H}^{i}\mathbb{D}_{X}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0})[-d_{X}]\cong\mathcal{E}xt_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}^{i-d_{X}}(\underline{\Omega}_{X}^{0},\omega_{X}^{\bullet})=0

for all

, by Proposition

. On the other hand, thanks to

]

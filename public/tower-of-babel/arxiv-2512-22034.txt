Combinatorial characterzations of T T -designs in the nonbinary Johnson scheme
Hiroshi Nozaki, Yuta Watanabe

Abstract
We study T T -designs in the nonbinary Johnson scheme. This scheme generalizes both the Johnson and Hamming schemes and admits a bivariate Q Q -polynomial structure. Zhu (2021) provided a combinatorial characterization of T T -designs in this scheme for certain index sets T T , using a relationship between T T -designs in the nonbinary Johnson scheme and relative designs in the nonbinary Hamming scheme. In this paper, we obtain a characterization that applies to a strictly larger class of index sets T T , based on a methodological extension of Delsarte’s original framework (1973). This new characterization naturally recovers classical block designs and orthogonal arrays as special cases. To describe these designs uniformly, we introduce ( r , s ) (r,s) -designs, a new family of combinatorial objects that arise naturally from our characterization. We also derive absolute lower bounds on the cardinality of ( r , s ) (r,s) -designs from the multiplicities of the primitive idempotents of the nonbinary Johnson scheme, and construct examples with index λ = 1 \lambda=1 that attain certain natural lower bounds.

Introduction

Delsarte
[
14
]
introduced the notion of designs in association schemes, thereby providing a unified framework that incorporates classical combinatorial structures such as block designs and orthogonal arrays.
Block designs are identified with designs in the Johnson scheme, and orthogonal arrays are identified with designs in the Hamming schemes.
These association schemes are
Q
-polynomial schemes
[
14
]
, whose designs are well analyzed using the corresponding univariate orthogonal polynomials.

For a set
T
of natural numbers, a
T
-design in an association scheme
(X,\mathcal{R})
is a finite subset
Y
of the underlying set
X
that satisfies

E_{i}\varphi_{Y}=0\qquad\text{for each $i\in T$,}

where
E_{i}
is a primitive idempotent of the Bose–Mesner algebra of
(X,\mathcal{R})
and
\varphi_{Y}
is the characteristic vector of
Y
.
For
Q
-polynomial association schemes, we have a natural ordering of
\{E_{i}\}
, that is, there exists a polynomial
v_{i}
of degree
i
such that
v_{i}(E_{1})=E_{i}
with the entry-wise matrix product.
This natural ordering helps to provide a good combinatorial characterization of certain association schemes.
For instance, a
\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
-design in the Johnson scheme forms a block
t
-design, and a
\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
-design in the Hamming scheme forms an orthogonal array with strength
t

[
14
]
.

The nonbinary Johnson scheme is a symmetric association scheme that generalizes both the Johnson
schemes and the Hamming schemes.
The character tables of this scheme are written in terms of bivariate orthogonal polynomials

[
13
,
22
]
.
In
[
11
]
, it is proved that the nonbinary Johnson scheme, under a specific restriction,
is a bivariate
Q
-polynomial scheme in the sense of
[
6
]
.
Bannai et al.
[
5
]
established a more general notion of multivariate

Q
-polynomial association schemes than that of
[
11
]
, using arbitrary monomial orders.
Bannai et al.
[
4
]
further proved that the nonbinary Johnson scheme is a bivariate

Q
-polynomial scheme in their sense; this result does not require the restriction imposed
in
[
11
]
.

In pursuing a generalization of the
Q
-polynomial framework, it is natural to develop a
combinatorial characterization of
T
-designs that is compatible with the natural ordering arising from a
multivariate
Q
-polynomial scheme.
As a first step in this direction, we focus on the nonbinary Johnson scheme.
Zhu
[
24
]
provided a combinatorial characterization of
T
-designs in the nonbinary Johnson
schemes for specific
T
.
This characterization is proved via the relationship between
T
-designs in the nonbinary Johnson
schemes and relative designs in the nonbinary Hamming schemes.

In this paper, we prove a combinatorial characterization of
T
-designs via a purely
methodological extension of Delsarte’s original approach
[
14
]
, for certain
T
that
properly contains the specific cases considered by Zhu.
This characterization naturally includes the notions of block designs and orthogonal arrays.

The combinatorial property of the designs is expressed as follows.

The present paper therefore opens a new direction in the study of designs.

Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the basic terminology and the fundamental results from

Let

For

Let

In
[
22
]

We define the matrix

using the characters of the multiplicative abelian group
\overline{F}=F\setminus\{0\}
.
Let
\psi_{a}

(a\in\overline{F})
be the complex characters of
\overline{F}
, numbered in such a way that

\psi_{a}(b)=\psi_{b}(a)
for all
a,b\in\overline{F}
.
For

and

, define
\psi_{a}(0)=0

where
\overline{x}=\{i\mid x_{i}\neq 0\}
is the support of

.
Notice that
(x,y)\neq 0
if and only if
\overline{x}\subset\overline{y}
.

Define the
multiplicative weight

of a vector

by

w_{M}(x)=|\{\,i\mid x_{i}\neq 0,\;x_{i}\neq 1\,\}|.

Let

W_{rs}=\{x\in F^{n}\mid w_{H}(x)=r,w_{M}(x)=s\}

for
0\leq s\leq r\leq n
.
Let
\mathbb{C}(X,Y)
be the set of matrices with rows indexed by
X
and columns indexed by
Y
.
Using the matrix
A_{rs}=((a,x))_{x\in W_{w}(n,q),a\in W_{rs}}\in\mathbb{C}(W_{w}(n,q),W_{rs})
, the matrix

is defined by

C_{rs}=A_{rs}\overline{A_{rs}^{\top}}=\left(\sum_{a\in W_{rs}}(a,x)\overline{(a,y)}\right)_{x,y\in W_{w}(n,q)}

, see Equation (23) in
[

]
.

For

, let

be the subalgebra of the Bose–Mesner algebra

spanned by the matrices

(k,h)\leq(r,s)

The dimensions of the algebras

increase by one as

increases in the lexicographic order.
Define

to be the primitive idempotent that first appears

\mathfrak{B}_{rs}
. Then,
\{E_{rs}\}_{(r,s)\in L}
forms the primitive idempotents of
\mathfrak{B}
.

3
Combinatorial characterizations of
T
-designs in
J_{q}(w,n)

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be the Bose–Mesner algebra of the nonbinary Johnson schemes
J_{q}(w,n)
.
A matrix
M\in\mathfrak{B}
can be expressed by the linear combination of the primitive idempotents
\{E_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in L}
as
M=\sum_{(i,j)\in L}c_{ij}E_{ij}
with
c_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}
.
The matrix

is called a

of

if the corresponding coefficients

is not
0
.
Let
\mathfrak{C}(M)
be the set of components of

.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(r,s)\in L
.
The set of components of
C_{rs}\in\mathfrak{B}
is

\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs})=\{E_{is}\mid(i,s)\in L,i\leq r\}.

Proof.

From Lemma 1 in
[
22
]
, for
(r,s),(k,h)\in L
, one has

C_{rs}C_{kh}=\delta_{sh}\sum_{i=s}^{\min\{r,k\}}a_{i}C_{is},

(3.1)

where
a_{i}>0
for each
i
.
Since
C_{rs}
is a positive semidefinite matrix,
it can be expressed as a linear combination of primitive idempotents with non-negative coefficients.
This implies that
\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs}C_{kh})=\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs})\cap\mathfrak{C}(C_{kh})
.
Since
E_{kh}\in\mathfrak{C}(C_{kh})
holds,
C_{kh}
appears in the expresion (
3.1
) if and only if
E_{kh}\in\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs})
.
Therefore,
E_{kh}\not\in\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs})
for
s\neq h
or
r<k
, and
E_{kh}\in\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs})
for
s=h
and
s\leq k\leq r
(
s>k
is excluded since
(k,s)\in L
). Thus,
\mathfrak{C}(C_{rs})
is detemined as stated.

.

for each

Theorem 3.2

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{s^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\ldots,s\}}\{(i,s^{\prime})\in L\mid i\leq r\}\setminus\{(0,0)\}

The

\displaystyle(A_{rs}^{\top}\phi_{X})(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}(a,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X:\overline{a}\subset\overline{x}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\psi_{a_{i_{j}}}(x_{i_{j}})

A

F^{n}
of weight

Since

.

Definition 4.1

(
(r,s)
-design)

Let

.
A subset

-design if the number

(n,n,q,\lambda)
-design corresponds to an orthogonal array
OA(|Y|,n,q-1,s)
for any
r
.
Note that the total number of elements in
Y
is determined by the parameters; see Corollary
4.4
.
While Definition
4.1
is convenient for our analytical framework, the relationship with orthogonal arrays is more transparent when expressed in terms of Definition
1.1
.

The concept of an
(r,s)
-design also generalizes the
H
-design introduced by Hanani
[
16
,
17
]
.
Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{n}\}
be a collection of
n
copies of
\overline{F}=\{1,\ldots,q-1\}
, referred to as groups.
A transverse of
\mathcal{G}
is a subset of
G_{1}\sqcup G_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{n}
that intersects each group in at most one point.
Each vector in
X=W_{w}(n,q)
corresponds naturally to a
w
-element transverse of
\mathcal{G}
.
Under this identification, a subset
Y\subset X
is an
(r,s)
-design precisely when, for every
r
-subset
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{G}
, each
s
-element transverse of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is contained in a constant number of
w
-element transverses in
Y
that intersects each group of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.
In particular, an
(r,r)
-
(n,w,q,1)
-design is exactly an
H
-design
H(n,q-1,w,r)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose
Y\subset X
is an
(r,s)
-
(n,w,q,\lambda)
-design.
Let
s^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}
be non-negative integer such that
s^{\prime}\leq s
and
s^{\prime}\leq r^{\prime}\leq r
.
For
\omega\in\overline{F}^{s^{\prime}}
and a pair of subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset N
with

|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|=s^{\prime}
,
|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}|=r^{\prime}
, define

Y^{\prime}=\{(y_{i})_{i\in N\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}\mid y=(y_{i})_{i\in N}\in Y,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset\overline{y},y_{i_{j}}=\omega_{j}\;(j=1,2,\ldots,s^{\prime})\},

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s^{\prime}}\}
,
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots,\omega_{s^{\prime}})
.
Then
Y^{\prime}
is an
(r-r^{\prime},s-s^{\prime})
-
(n-r^{\prime},w-r^{\prime},q,\lambda)
-design.

Proof.

Let
\omega^{\prime}\in\overline{F}^{s-s^{\prime}}
, and let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}\subset\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}\subset N\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
with
|\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}|=s-s^{\prime}
,
|\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}|=r-r^{\prime}
.
Write
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}=\{i_{s^{\prime}+1},i_{s^{\prime}+2},\ldots,i_{s}\}
and

.
Then,

\displaystyle m_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}}(Y^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=|\{y^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}\mid\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}\subset\overline{y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}_{i_{j}}=\omega_{j}\;(j=s^{\prime}+1,s^{\prime}+2,\ldots,s)\}|

\displaystyle=|\{y\in Y\mid\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{R}^{\prime\prime}\subset\overline{y},y_{i_{j}}=\omega_{j}\;(j=1,2,\ldots,s)\}|

\displaystyle=\lambda.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Suppose
Y\subset X
is an
(r,s)
-
(n,w,q,\lambda)
-design.
Let
s^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}
be non-negative integers such that
s^{\prime}\leq s
and
s^{\prime}\leq r^{\prime}\leq r
.
Then the number
m_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(Y,\omega^{\prime})
is constant for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\overline{F}^{s^{\prime}}
, and for all pairs of subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset N
with
|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|=s^{\prime}
and
|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}|=r^{\prime}
.
Moreover, the constant is given by

\lambda_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}=(q-1)^{s-s^{\prime}}\frac{\binom{n-r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}}{\binom{w-r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}}\lambda.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s^{\prime}}\}
and
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\omega^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
.
We count the number of elements in the following set in two different ways.

\{(y,\mathcal{R})\mid y\in Y,y_{i_{j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{j}\;(j=1,2,\ldots,s^{\prime}),|\mathcal{R}|=r,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{R}\subset\overline{y}\}

(4.1)

First, there are
\binom{n-r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}
ways to choose
\mathcal{R}
.
For a given
\mathcal{R}
,
take a subset
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{R}

with
|\mathcal{S}|=s
.
The computation of

|\{y\in Y\mid\mathcal{R}\subset\overline{y},y_{i_{j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{j}\;(j=1,\ldots,s^{\prime})\}|

is identical to the one appearing in (
3.6
);
hence, by (
3.6
), the number of choices of
y
is
(q-1)^{s-s^{\prime}}\lambda
.
Therefore, the number of elements in the set (
4.1
) is
(q-1)^{s-s^{\prime}}\binom{n-r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}\lambda
.

Conversely, there are
m_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(Y,\omega^{\prime})
ways to choose
y
.
For a given
y
, there are
\binom{w-r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}
ways to choose
\mathcal{R}
.
Therefore, the number of elements in the set (
4.1
) is
\binom{w-r^{\prime}}{r-r^{\prime}}m_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(Y,\omega^{\prime})
.

Equating both counts, we obtain
m_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}(Y,\omega^{\prime})=\lambda_{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose
Y\subset X
is an
(r,s)
-
(n,w,q,\lambda)
-design.
Then the cardinality of
Y
is given by

|Y|=(q-1)^{s}\frac{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{w}{r}}\lambda.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, we have
|Y|=\lambda_{0,0}=(q-1)^{s}\frac{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{w}{r}}\lambda
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose
Y\subset X
is an
(r,s)
-
(n,w,q,\lambda)
-design.
Let
s^{\prime}
,
r^{\prime}
and
t
be non-negative integers such that
s^{\prime}\leq s
,
s^{\prime}\leq r^{\prime}\leq r
and
t\leq r-r^{\prime}
.
For every
\omega^{\prime}\in\overline{F}^{s^{\prime}}
, and for all subsets
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset N
and
\mathcal{T}\subset N
with
|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|=s^{\prime}
,
|\mathcal{R}^{\prime}|=r^{\prime}
,
|\mathcal{T}|=t
and
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{T}=\emptyset
, we have

|\{y\in Y\mid\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset\overline{y},y_{i_{j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{j}\;(j=1,2,\ldots,s^{\prime}),\mathcal{T}\cap\overline{y}=\emptyset\}|=(q-1)^{s-s^{\prime}}\frac{\binom{n-r^{\prime}-t}{w-r^{\prime}}}{\binom{n-r}{w-r}}\lambda,

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{s^{\prime}}\}
and
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\omega^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,\omega^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
r^{\prime}=s^{\prime}=0
.
Define

\mu_{t}(\mathcal{T})=|\{y\in Y\mid\mathcal{T}\cap\overline{y}=\emptyset\}|.

For each
i\in N
, let

Y_{i}=\{y\in Y\mid i\in\overline{y}\}.

For a subset
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{T}
with
|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}|=t^{\prime}
,
Proposition
4.3
gives

\left|\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}Y_{i}\right|=|\{y\in Y\mid\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subset\overline{y}\}|=\lambda_{t^{\prime},0}.

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

\mu_{t}(\mathcal{T})=\left|Y\setminus\left(\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{T}}Y_{i}\right)\right|=\sum_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{T}}(-1)^{|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}|}\left|\bigcap_{i\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}Y_{i}\right|=\sum_{t^{\prime}=0}^{t}(-1)^{t^{\prime}}\binom{t}{t^{\prime}}\lambda_{t^{\prime},0}.

Thus,
\mu_{t}(\mathcal{T})
is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{T}
.
Denote this constant by
\mu_{t}
, and consider the set

\{(\mathcal{T},y)\mid|\mathcal{T}|=t,y\in Y,\mathcal{T}\cap\overline{y}=\emptyset\}.

We count the number of elements in this set in two different ways:

First, there are
|Y|
choices for
y
and for each
y
,
\binom{n-w}{t}
choices for
\mathcal{T}
.
So the total is
|Y|\binom{n-w}{t}
.
Second, there are
\binom{n}{t}
choices for
\mathcal{T}
and for each
\mathcal{T}
,
\mu_{t}
choices for
y
.
So the total is
\mu_{t}\binom{n}{t}
.
Equating both counts and applying Corollary
4.4
, we obtain

\mu_{t}=\frac{\binom{n-w}{t}}{\binom{n}{t}}|Y|=(q-1)^{s}\frac{\binom{n-w}{t}}{\binom{n}{t}}\frac{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{w}{r}}\lambda=(q-1)^{s}\frac{\binom{n-t}{w}}{\binom{n-r}{w-r}}\lambda.

This completes the proof for the case
r^{\prime}=s^{\prime}=0
.

We now consider the case
(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
.
This follows by applying the result for
r^{\prime}=s^{\prime}=0
to the derived design

Y^{\prime}=\{(y_{i})_{i\in N\setminus\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}\mid y=(y_{i})_{i\in N}\in Y,\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\subset\overline{y},y_{i_{j}}=\omega^{\prime}_{j}\;(j=1,2,\ldots,s^{\prime})\},

which is an
(r-r^{\prime},s-s^{\prime})
-
(n-r^{\prime},w-r^{\prime},q,\lambda)
-design by Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose
Y\subset X
is an
(r,s)
-
(n,w,q,\lambda)
-design.
If

, then

can be regarded as a
(w,s)
-

Let

and let
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{R}\subset N
with

,

.

and write

and

.

5
Lower bounds on
(r,s)
-designs

We recall two families of orthogonal polynomials that play a central role in the study of nonbinary Johnson schemes.
For integers
0\leq i\leq n
, the Krawtchouk polynomial
K_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is defined by

K_{i}(n,q,x)=\sum_{l=0}^{i}(-1)^{l}(q-1)^{i-l}\binom{n-x}{i-l}\binom{x}{l}.

Similarly, for
0\leq i\leq m=\min\{w,n-w\}
, the Hahn polynomial
Q_{i}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is given by

Q_{i}(n,w,x)=\frac{\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n}{i-1}}{\binom{w}{x}\binom{n-w}{x}}\sum_{r=0}^{x}(-1)^{r}\binom{i}{r}\binom{w-i}{x-r}\binom{n-w-i}{x-r}.

Both
K_{i}
and
Q_{i}
are polynomials of degree
i
.

Let

K=\{(k,h)\mid 0\leq h\leq k\leq w,\;h\leq m\}.

The second eigenmatrix of the nonbinary Johnson scheme
J_{q}(w,n)
can be expressed in terms of these polynomials as

Q_{i,j}(k,h)=\frac{\binom{n}{j}}{\binom{w}{j}}K_{j}(w-h,q-1,k-h)Q_{i-j}(n-j,w-j,h)

(5.1)

for
(i,j)\in L
and
(k,h)\in K
.
This formula is due to the result in
[
22
, Theorem 2]
.
In particular,
Q_{i,j}(k,h)
is of degree
j
in
k
and of degree
i
in
h
.
The multiplicity of
J_{q}(w,n)
is given by

m_{i,j}=Q_{i,j}(0,0)=(q-2)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\left(\binom{n-j}{i-j}-\binom{n-j}{i-j-1}\right)

(5.2)

for
(i,j)\in L
.
The same expression also appears in the course of the proof in
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime}),(r,s)\in L
,
if
i+i^{\prime}<r
or
j+j^{\prime}<s
, then the Krein number of the nonbinary Johnson scheme
J_{q}(w,n)
satisfies
q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{rs}=0
.

Proof.

By the general property of association schemes,
(see, e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 2.22 (7)]
),
the second eigenmatrix given in (
5.1
) satisfies

Q_{i,j}(k,h)Q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(k,h)=\sum_{(r,s)\in L}q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{rs}Q_{r,s}(k,h)

for all
(i,j),(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in L
and
(k,h)\in K
.
Recall that
Q_{i,j}(k,h)
is of degree
j
in
k
and of degree
i
in
h
.
Hence, the product
Q_{i,j}(k,h)Q_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(k,h)
has degree
j+j^{\prime}
in
k
and
i+i^{\prime}
in
h
.
On the right-hand side, each term
Q_{r,s}(k,h)
has degree
s
in
k
and degree
r
in
h
.
Therefore if
q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{rs}\neq 0
, we must have
r\leq i+i^{\prime}
and
s\leq j+j^{\prime}
.
Equivalently, if
i+i^{\prime}<r
or
j+j^{\prime}<s
, then
q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{rs}=0
, which proves the claim.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(r,s)\in L
with
(r,s)\neq(0,0)
, and define
T=\{(i,j)\in L\mid i\leq r,j\leq s\}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
.
Then any
T
-design
Y
in the nonbinary Johnson scheme
J_{q}(w,n)
satisfies

|Y|\geq\binom{n}{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}(q-2)^{l}\binom{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}{l}.

Proof.

Set
S=\{(i,j)\in L\mid i\leq\lfloor r/2\rfloor,j\leq\lfloor s/2\rfloor\}
.
Consider the matrix

M=\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}E_{i,j}\right)\circ\left(\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in S}E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\right),

where
E_{i,j}
denotes the primitive idempotent of
J_{q}(w,n)
and
\circ
denotes the entry-wise matrix product.
Since each
E_{i,j}
has real entries, all entries of
M
are non-negative.
Moreover, for any
x\in X
,

M_{x,x}=\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}(E_{i,j})_{x,x}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{|X|^{2}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}\right)^{2},

where
m_{i,j}
is the multiplicity of
J_{q}(w,n)
, given by (
5.2
).
Therefore

\sum_{x,y\in Y}M_{x,y}\geq\sum_{x\in Y}M_{x,x}=\frac{|Y|}{|X|^{2}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}\right)^{2}.

(5.3)

On the other hand, by the definition of Krein parameters,
M
can be expanded using Krein parameters:

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i,j)\in S}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in S}\left(E_{i,j}\circ E_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{(i,j)\in S}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in S}\sum_{(i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime})\in L}q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime\prime}j^{\prime\prime}}E_{i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime}}.

Thus,

\sum_{x,y\in Y}M_{x,y}=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{(i,j)\in S}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in S}\sum_{(i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime})\in L}q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime\prime}j^{\prime\prime}}\left(\sum_{x,y\in Y}(E_{i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime}})_{x,y}\right).

Since
Y
is a
T
-design, the inner sum vanishes whenever
(i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime})\in T
.
By Lemma
5.1
,
q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime\prime}j^{\prime\prime}}=0
if
i+i^{\prime}<i^{\prime\prime}
or
j+j^{\prime}<j^{\prime\prime}
.
Combine these conditions,
the only surviving term corresponds to
(i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime})=(0,0)
.
Therefore, by the general property of Krein parameters,
(see, e.g.,
[
2
, Proposition 2.24 (3)]
),

\sum_{x,y\in Y}M_{x,y}=\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{(i,j)\in S}\sum_{(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in S}q_{ij,i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}^{00}\left(\sum_{x,y\in Y}(E_{0,0})_{x,y}\right)=\frac{|Y|^{2}}{|X|^{2}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}\right).

(5.4)

From (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we have

\frac{|Y|}{|X|^{2}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{x,y\in Y}M_{x,y}=\frac{|Y|^{2}}{|X|^{2}}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}\right),

which implies

|Y|\geq\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}.

By the formula for multiplicities (
5.2
), this sum can be computed as

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in S}m_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\sum_{i=j}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}m_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}(q-2)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\binom{n-j}{\lfloor r/2\rfloor-j}

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}(q-2)^{j}\binom{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}{j}.

This completes the proof.
∎

.

, and define

3.2
and Definition
4.1
,

is an

-

.

,

∎

6
Minimal
(r,s)
-designs with
\lambda=1

From Corollary
4.4
, we obtain the natural lower bound

|Y|=(q-1)^{s}\frac{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{w}{r}}\lambda\geq(q-1)^{s}\frac{\binom{n}{r}}{\binom{w}{r}}

(6.1)

for any
(r,s)
-design
Y
, and the equality is attained when
\lambda=1
.
For each fixed weight
w
, the bound (
6.1
) is stronger
than the one in Theorem
5.2
for sufficiently large
n
.
An
(r,s)
-design with
\lambda=1
achieves the minimum possible
cardinality among all
(r,s)
-designs.

First, we deal with two trivial cases.
If
r=s
, then
(r,r)
-designs with
\lambda=1
are exactly
H
-designs. There are many examples of
H
-designs
[
9
,
16
,
18
,
20
,
23
]
.
If
r=w
, then the set of supports of an
(r,s)
-design with
\lambda=1
is the whole set of
w
-subsets of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
, which has
\binom{n}{w}
points.
On each support, we place all rows of an
OA(v,w,q-1,s)
with index
1
.

Construction A
.

We can construct an
(r,s)
-

Concluding remarks

This paper gives a combinatorial characterization of
T
-designs in the nonbinary Johnson scheme for a specific degree set
T
.
The degree set
T
arises from a natural ordering associated with the bivariate
Q
-polynomial property.
We also establish the Fisher-type bounds for
T
-designs (Theorem
5.2
).

A
T
-design attaining the Fisher-type bound is called
tight
.
Tight block
t
-designs in the Johnson scheme are rare; see
[
3
, Section 2.2]
.
The nonexistence of tight block designs is established by showing that the zeros of certain one-variable orthogonal polynomials are not integral
[
1
,
12
]
.
A similar relationship between the existence of tight designs and the zero distribution of orthogonal polynomials holds for one-variable
Q
-polynomial schemes.
We expect that this relationship can be extended to multivariate
Q
-polynomial schemes, and aim to establish non-existence results for tight
T
-designs in the nonbinary Johnson scheme.

For classical
Q
-polynomial schemes, one of the main tools for analyzing designs is the linear programming bound based on one-variable orthogonal polynomials; see
[
3
, Section 6]
and
[
15
, Theorem 5.10]
.
For multivariate
Q
-polynomial schemes, it is natural to expect a generalization of the linear programming bound using multivariate orthogonal polynomials.

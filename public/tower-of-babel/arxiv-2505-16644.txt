Learning non-equilibrium diffusions with Schrödinger bridges: from exactly solvable to simulation-free
Stephen Y. Zhang, Michael P. H. Stumpf

Abstract
We consider the Schrödinger bridge problem which, given ensemble measurements of the initial and final configurations of a stochastic dynamical system and some prior knowledge on the dynamics, aims to reconstruct the “most likely” evolution of the system compatible with the data. Most existing literature assume Brownian reference dynamics, and are implicitly limited to modelling systems driven by the gradient of a potential energy. We depart from this regime and consider reference processes described by a multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with generic drift matrix 𝑨 ∈ ℝ d × d \bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d} . When 𝑨 \bm{A} is asymmetric, this corresponds to a non-equilibrium system in which non-gradient forces are at play: this is important for applications to biological systems, which naturally exist out-of-equilibrium. In the case of Gaussian marginals, we derive explicit expressions that characterise exactly the solution of both the static and dynamic Schrödinger bridge. For general marginals, we propose mvOU-OTFM , a simulation-free algorithm based on flow and score matching for learning an approximation to the Schrödinger bridge. In application to a range of problems based on synthetic and real single cell data, we demonstrate that mvOU-OTFM achieves higher accuracy compared to competing methods, whilst being significantly faster to train.

Introduction

We are interested in reconstruction of stochastic dynamics of individuals from static population snapshots. This is a central problem with applications arising across the natural and social sciences, whenever longitudinal tracking of individuals over time is either impossible or impractical
Weinreb et al. (2018)
;
Muzellec et al. (2017)
;
Stuart and Wolfram (2020)
;
Schiebinger et al. (2019)
;
Miles et al. (2024)
;
Abel and Sander (2014)
.
In simple terms, consider a system of indistinguishable particles
\bm{x}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
undergoing some unobserved temporal dynamics. The practitioner observes the system to have distribution
\bm{x}_{0}\sim\rho_{0}
at an initial time
t=0
and later to be
\bm{x}_{1}\sim\rho_{1}
at a final time
t=1
. The question is then: can we, under some suitable assumptions, reconstruct the
continuous-time
behaviour of the system for the unobserved time interval
0<t<1
?

The Schrödinger bridge problem (SBP), by now a centrepiece of the theoretical literature on this topic, places this task on a theoretical footing in terms of a mathematical formulation of this problem as a large deviations principle on the path space
Léonard (2013)
and a (stochastic) least action principle intimately linked to optimal transportation theory
Chen et al. (2021)
. Given a
reference process
that encodes prior knowledge on the dynamics, the SBP can be understood as identifying the “most likely” evolution of the system that is compatible with the snapshot observations.

The SBP and related topics have enjoyed a great deal of recent interest from both applied and theoretical perspectives. Many applications arise from biological modelling of cell dynamics
Bunne et al. (2024)
;
Schiebinger (2021)
. The majority of existing work assumes, either explicitly or implicitly, that the system of interest is
potential driven
, that is, driven by the gradient of a potential energy
Lavenant et al. (2021)
;
Chizat et al. (2022)
;
Tong et al. (2023b)
;
Bunne et al. (2023)
;
Tong et al. (2020)
;
Yeo et al. (2021)
;
Bunne et al. (2022)
.
In fact, most studies do not consider
any
prior drift, corresponding to the setting where the reference process is purely an isotropic Brownian motion. A few studies
Bunne et al. (2023)
;
Shi et al. (2023)
;
De Bortoli et al. (2021)
allow for scalar Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) processes, motivated by applications in the generative modelling domain. Since scalar OU dynamics have unidimensional drift which can always be written as the gradient of a scalar function, all of these are also potential driven systems.

Changing the choice for the reference process provides a route to dealing with systems which are not potential driven, i.e. driven by a drift that is a non-conservative vector field. Abundant motivations for modelling these dynamics arises from biological systems and other kinds of active matter; these exist naturally far from equilibrium
Zhu et al. (2024)
;
Parrondo et al. (2009)
;
Gnesotto et al. (2018)
;
Fang et al. (2019)
, exhibiting irreversible dynamics at a non-equilibrium steady state
Lax (1960)
. While a few studies
Vargas et al. (2021)
;
Shen et al. (2024)
;
Koshizuka and Sato (2022)
allow for reference processes described by a SDE with a
generic
drift term, they rely on computationally expensive simulation procedures such as numerical integration and suffer from accuracy issues in high dimensions.
Here we explore a complementary approach and concentrate our attention on a family of
linear
reference dynamics as a middle ground between physical relevance and analytical tractability. Specifically, we consider reference processes arising in a class of linear drift-diffusion dynamics described by the SDE

\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{A}(\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{m})\>\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}.

(1)

These processes are also known as
multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck
(mvOU) processes, often used as a model of non-equilibrium systems in the physics literature
Lax (1960)
;
Godrèche and Luck (2018)
;
Gilson et al. (2023)
. Indeed, for asymmetric drift matrix
\bm{A}
the system (
1
) has a drift that is no longer the gradient of a potential.
As an example in the case of isotropic diffusion
\bm{\sigma}=\sigma\mathbf{I}
, when
\bm{A}
is asymmetric and has all eigenvalues with negative real part, a non-equilibrium steady state exists where the dynamics are irreversible and exhibit nonzero probability currents while the population density is unchanged
Gilson et al. (2023)
;
Godrèche and Luck (2018)
. While allowing a broader range of reference dynamics, the statistics of (
1
) remain analytically tractable – in particular it is a Markovian Gaussian process with explicit formulae for its mean, covariance, and transition kernel. This provides avenues to efficient solution of the SBP that sidesteps the need for numerical integration, and as we will see, even analytical expressions in the Gaussian case.
In particular, the family of SBP problems that we model is strictly larger than the standard (Brownian) SBP, which we recover as a special case when
\bm{A},\bm{m}=\bm{0}
.

The contributions of our paper are twofold. Leveraging results on mvOU processes, we characterise for Gaussian endpoints the Gaussian Schrödinger Bridge (GSB) with reference dynamics described by (
1
). To handle general marginals, we develop a
simulation free
training procedure based on score and flow matching
Tong et al. (2023b)
to solve the SBP. We conclude by demonstrating our results in a range of synthetic and real data examples.
We find that our approach solves the generalised SBP faster and more accurately than comparable simulation-based algorithms, at the cost of assuming the form (
1
) of the reference process. Our findings highlight the tradeoff between (i) analytically tractable but more restrictive models and (ii) more expressive non-parametric models, which are much less tractable to learn, both in terms of computational cost and statistical complexity.

) can be solved via a generalised entropic transport problem and characterisation of the

2
Background and related work

2.1
Schrödinger bridges

In what follows we provide a concise summary of the SBP. For details we refer readers to
Léonard (2013)
;
Chen et al. (2021)
for in-depth discussion. We work in
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
the space of probability distributions on
\mathcal{X}
. Let
C([0,1],\mathcal{X})
denote the space of continuous paths
\omega_{t}:[0,1]\mapsto\mathcal{X}
valued in
\mathcal{X}
, and informally we will refer to
path measures
as probability measures supported on
C([0,1],\mathcal{X})
. Viewing stochastic processes as random variables valued in
C([0,1],\mathcal{X})
, path measures prescribe their law.
Let
\mathbb{Q}
be a general path measure describing a
reference
Markov stochastic process. For prescribed initial and final marginals
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
, the Schrödinger bridge problem can be written

\min_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(C([0,1],\mathcal{X}))\>:\>\mathbb{P}_{0}=\rho_{0},\mathbb{P}_{1}=\rho_{1}}\operatorname{KL}(\mathbb{P}|\mathbb{Q}),

(SBP-dyn)

where the minimum is taken over all candidate processes
\mathbb{P}
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{Q}
that are compatible with the observed data at
t=0,1
. This
dynamic
form of the SBP, while elegant, is unwieldy for practical purposes owing to the formulation on path space. A well known result
Léonard (2013)
connects the dynamical SBP with its static counterpart:

\min_{\mathbb{P}_{01}\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{1})}\operatorname{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{01}|\mathbb{Q}_{01}).

(SBP-static)

Furthermore, the static and dynamic SBP are interchangeable via the disintegration identity regarding the optimal law
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
:

\mathbb{P}^{\star}(\cdot)=\int\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}^{\star}_{01}(x_{0},x_{1})\mathbb{Q}^{(x_{0},x_{1})}(\cdot),

(2)

where
\mathbb{Q}^{xy}
denotes the law of the
\mathbb{Q}
-bridge conditioned at
(0,x_{0})
and
(1,x_{1})
. In other words, solution of (
SBP-dyn
) amounts to solving (
SBP-static
) for
\mathbb{P}_{01}^{\star}
followed by construction of
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
as per (
2
) by taking mixtures of
\mathbb{Q}
-bridges.

The problem (
SBP-static
) can be reformulated as an entropy-regularised optimal transportation problem
Chen et al. (2021)
where the effective cost matrix is the log-transition kernel under
\mathbb{Q}
. This admits efficient solution via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm
Cuturi (2013)
in the discrete case. This provides a practical roadmap to constructing dynamical Schrödinger bridges by first solving (
SBP-static
), then using construction of
\mathbb{Q}
-bridges with (
2
) to build a solution of (
SBP-dyn
).

2.2
Probability flows and (score, flow)-matching

Probability flows

Consider a generic drift-diffusion process in
d
dimensions with drift
\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{x})
and diffusivity
\bm{\sigma}_{t}
, described by an Itô diffusion whose marginal densities evolve following the corresponding Fokker-Planck equation (FPE):

\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{X}_{t})\>\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t},\qquad\partial_{t}p_{t}(\bm{x})=-\nabla\cdot(p_{t}(\bm{x})\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{x}))+\nabla\cdot\left(\bm{D}_{t}\nabla p_{t}(\bm{x})\right),

(3)

where
\bm{D}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{\sigma}_{t}\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{\top}
is the diffusivity matrix. Equivalently, the FPE can be rewritten in the form of a continuity equation involving a
probability flow
field
\bm{u}_{t}

Lipman et al. (2022)
;
Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden (2022)
;
Albergo et al. (2023)
;
Tong et al. (2023b)
;
Maddu et al. (2024)
:

\partial_{t}p_{t}(\bm{x})=-\nabla\cdot\left[p_{t}(\bm{x})\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x})\right],\qquad\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x})=\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{x})-\bm{D}_{t}\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x}),

(4)

By recognising the form of the continuity equation in (
4
), it is apparent that the family of
marginal distributions

\{p_{t}(\bm{x})\}_{t\geq 0}
generated by the dynamics specified in (
3
) are also generated by the
probability flow (PF)-ODE
:

\dot{\bm{X}}_{t}=\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{X}_{t}),\>\bm{X}_{0}\sim p_{0}

(5)

The central quantity that allows us to convert between the SDE (
3
) and the PF-ODE (
4
) is the gradient of the log-density
\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t}(\bm{x})=:\bm{s}_{t}(\bm{x})
, also known as the
score function
. Under mild regularity conditions, knowledge of the score
\bm{s}(\bm{x})
allows for sampling from
p(\bm{x})
via Langevin dynamics: the SDE
\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{s}(\bm{x})\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}\bm{W}_{t}
has stationary distribution
p(\bm{x})

Song and Ermon (2019)
.
While typically one needs to resort to approximations to learn
\bm{s}

Vincent (2011)
;
Song and Ermon (2019)
;
Song et al. (2020)
, in setting of the Brownian (and as we will see, the mvOU) bridge, one has closed form expressions for the score and the objective.

Conditional flow matching

Stated in its original form, let
t\mapsto p_{t}(\bm{x})
be a family of marginals on
t\in[0,1]
satisfying the continuity equation
\partial_{t}p_{t}(\bm{x})=-\nabla\cdot(p_{t}(\bm{x})\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x}))
, where
\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x})
is a time-dependent vector field.
Suppose further that
p_{t}(\bm{x})
admits a representation as a
mixture

p_{t}(\bm{x})=\int\mathrm{d}q(\bm{z})p_{t|z}(\bm{x}|\bm{z})

where
\bm{z}\sim q(\bm{z})
is some latent variable and
t\mapsto p_{t|z}(\bm{x})
are called the
conditional probability paths
. We introduce
conditional flow fields

\bm{u}_{t|z}
that generate the conditional probability paths, i.e.
\partial_{t}p_{t|z}(\bm{x})=-\nabla\cdot(p_{t|z}(\bm{x})\bm{u}_{t|z}(\bm{x}|z))
. Then, the insight presented in
(
Lipman et al., 2022
, Theorem 1)
is that, in fact,

\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{z}\sim q(\bm{z})}\left[\frac{p_{t|z}(\bm{x}|\bm{z})}{p_{t}(\bm{x})}\bm{u}_{t|z}(\bm{x}|\bm{z})\right],

as can be easily verified by checking the continuity equation.
When
p_{t}(\bm{x})
is not tractable but
q(\bm{z})
,
p_{t|z}(\bm{x})
and
\bm{u}_{t|z}(\bm{x})
are, as is the case for the Schrödinger bridge (
2
), conditional flow matching is useful: Lipman et al.
(
Lipman et al., 2022
, Theorem 2)
prove that minimising

L_{\rm{CFM}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{t\sim U[0,1],\bm{z}\sim q}\>\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim p_{t|z}}\|\bm{v}_{\theta,t}(\bm{x})-\bm{u}_{t|z}(\bm{x})\|^{2}

(6)

is equivalent to regression on the true (marginal) vector field
\bm{u}_{t}

Motivating the use of the conditional objective (

that minimising (

\mathbb{P}_{01}^{\star}
can be obtained by solving an static entropic optimal transport problem.

2.3
Related work

The generalisation of dynamical optimal transport and the Schrödinger bridge to linear reference dynamics has been studied
Chen et al. (2016a)
;
Chen et al. (2015)
;
Chen et al. (2016b)
from the viewpoint of stochastic optimal control. However, as was pointed out by
Bunne et al. (2023)
, these studies have primarily focused on theoretical aspects of the problem, such as existence and uniqueness. In particular, the result for the Gaussian case
Chen et al. (2015)
is in terms of a system of coupled matrix differential equations and does not lend itself to straightforward computation. More generally, forward-backward SDEs corresponding to a continuous-time iterative proportional fitting scheme
Vargas et al. (2021)
;
Shen et al. (2024)
have been proposed for general reference processes.

For the Gaussian case, the availability of closed form solutions is by now classical
Olkin and Pukelsheim (1982)
;
Takatsu (2011)
, and more recent work provides analytical characterisations of Gaussian
entropy-regularised
transport
Bunne et al. (2023)
;
Janati et al. (2020)
;
Mallasto et al. (2022)
.
Bunne et al. (2023)
provides explicit formulae that characterise the bridge marginals as well as the force (bridge control) for a Brownian reference as well as a class of
scalar
OU references. To the authors’ knowledge, however, all explicit formulae for Gaussian Schrödinger bridges assume either Brownian or scalar OU dynamics.
The application of flow matching
Lipman et al. (2022)
;
Albergo and Vanden-Eijnden (2022)
as a simulation-free technique to learn Schrödinger bridges was proposed in
Tong et al. (2023b)
, but the authors restrict consideration to a Brownian reference process. Recently
Mei et al. (2024)
apply flow matching for stochastic linear control systems, however they are concerned with interpolating distributions and do not study the SBP.

Finally, concurrent work
Petrović et al. (2025)
proposes a related flow matching scheme for approximately solving the
\mathbb{Q}
-SBP where the reference process
\mathbb{Q}
is described by a general, potentially non-linear diffusion. This comes however at the cost of using learned neural approximations for bridges and log-transition densities of
\mathbb{Q}
. Our work treats the complementary case of linear reference dynamics, in which we may avail ourselves of analytical formulae for these quantities.

3.1
Multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck bridges

-SBP (

Theorem 1

(SDE characterisation of mvOU bridge, adapted from results in

)
.

Consider the

Conditioning on
(0,\bm{x}_{0})

(8)

where

Theorem 2

(Score and flow for multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck bridge)
.

For the mvOU bridge conditioned on

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\bm{x}}\log p_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}(\bm{x})=\bm{\Sigma}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}^{-1}(\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}-\bm{x})

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\bm{A}(\bm{x}-\bm{m})+\bm{c}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}(\bm{x})-\textstyle\frac{1}{2}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}\bm{s}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}(\bm{x}).

\displaystyle\bm{\Sigma}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}

Lemma 1

5
Discussion

Motivated by applications to non-equilibrium dynamics, we study the Schrödinger bridge problem where the reference process is taken to be in a family of multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (mvOU) processes. These processes are able to capture some key behaviours of non-equilibrium systems whilst still being analytically tractable. Leveraging the tractability, we derive an exact solution of the bridge between Gaussian measures for mvOU reference. We extend our approach to non-Gaussian measures using a score and flow matching approach that avoids the need for costly numerical simulation. We showcase the improvement in both accuracy and speed of our approach in a variety of settings, both low and high-dimensional scenarios.

Limitations

A key limitation of our method is that our analytical expressions involve matrix inversions and powers of positive definite matrices which scale as
O(d^{3})
with the dimension
d
. The same limitation was identified in
Bunne et al. (2023)
as also applying to their approach. In practice, we find that application to problems with
d\leq 100
work well. Scaling our methods to even higher dimensional settings is left to future work, and we expect that tools from the Gaussian process literature will be helpful for this. Regarding flow matching, the link between Alg.
1
and (
SBP-dyn
) requires the solution to (
SBP-static
) to be computed exactly. In all our experiments, we achieve this by computing couplings using all available samples. In large-scale settings where the number of data points
N
is very large or possibly streaming, a common approach is to use
minibatch
couplings
Tong et al. (2023a)
. In such settings, the use of minibatches results in learned flows being biased. An in-depth discussion of this is provided in
Klein et al. (2025)
, which advocates for the infrequent computation of OT couplings on large batch sizes rather than the commonly used per-iteration minibatch OT.

A.1
Some calculations on multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes

For convenience, we first collect some results about multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes of the form (
1
), a detailed discussion of mvOU processes can be found in e.g.
[
54
]
.
For a
time-invariant
process with drift matrix
\bm{A}
and diffusion
\bm{\sigma}
, we have that

(\bm{X}_{t}|\bm{X}_{0}=\bm{x}_{0})\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t},\bm{\Sigma}_{t}),

where

\bm{\mu}_{t}=e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{x}_{0},\quad\bm{\Sigma}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-\tau)\bm{A}}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}e^{(t-\tau)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

(20)

If
\bm{X}_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{0},\bm{\Sigma}_{0})
and writing
\bm{X}_{0t}=\bm{X}_{0}\oplus\bm{X}_{t}
, some tedious but straightforward applications of conditional expectations and the tower property reveal that

\bm{X}_{0t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{0t},\bm{\Sigma}_{0t}),\quad\bm{\mu}_{0t}=\bm{\mu}_{0}\oplus e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{\mu}_{0},\quad\bm{\Sigma}_{0t}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Sigma}_{0}&&\bm{\Sigma}_{0}e^{t\bm{A}^{\top}}\\
e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{\Sigma}_{0}&&\bm{\Sigma}_{t}+e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{\Sigma}_{0}e^{t\bm{A}^{\top}}\end{bmatrix}.

(21)

More generally, for a process
\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=(\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}+\bm{b})\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}
, one has:

\bm{\mu}_{t}=e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{x}_{0}+(e^{t\bm{A}}-\mathbf{I})(\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{b}),\quad\bm{\Sigma}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s.

(22)

The expressions for the covariance of the mvOU process can be obtained from the fact that the covariance evolves following a
Lyapunov equation
. For the case of constant coefficients, we state the following result.

Lyapunov equation solution

It is easy to verify that
\dot{\bm{G}}_{t}=\bm{A}\bm{G}_{t}+\bm{G}_{t}\bm{A}^{\top}+\bm{Q}_{t}
has solution

\bm{G}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{Q}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s.

A.2
Bridges of multivariate Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes

This problem has been studied by Chen et al. in
[
12
]
, however the material therein is geared towards a control audience and considers a more general case where all coefficients are time-dependent. We will re-derive the results that we will need for processes of the form (
1
). Additionally, while in practice we typically use
\bm{\sigma}=\sigma\mathbf{I}
, we state some results in the general setting of non-isotropic noise.

Derivation of the dynamical OU-bridge (Theorem
1
)

Consider a mvOU process

\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=(\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}+\bm{b})\>\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}.

(23)

Now form the
controlled
version pinned at
(0,\bm{x}_{0})
and
(T,\bm{x}_{T})
, where
\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{X}_{t})
is an additional force arising from the conditioning of the process at an endpoint:

\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=(A\bm{X}_{t}+\bm{b}+\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{X}_{t}))\>\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}.

(24)

The main result that we need is that
\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x})
is itself a linear time-dependent field whose coefficients are independent of
(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})
.
In the above we consider the simplest case of classical optimal control, where the control is
directly
added to the system drift and there is no unobserved system.

Consider the system started at
(0,\bm{x}_{0})
and pinned to
(T,\bm{x}_{T})
. For now, we make all statements for time-dependent coefficients and the diffusion is non-isotropic. We then form the Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{u}}\>\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\|\bm{u}_{t}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\sup_{\lambda}\lambda\|\bm{X}_{T}-\bm{x}_{T}\|^{2}\right\}=\sup_{\lambda}\min_{\bm{u}}\>\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\|\bm{u}_{t}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\lambda\|\bm{X}_{T}-\bm{x}_{T}\|^{2}\right\}

Fixing some
\lambda>0
, the resulting problem is known as a
linear-quadratic-Gaussian
problem
[
29
]
in the control literature:

\min_{\bm{u}}\>\mathbb{E}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\bm{u}_{t}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bm{X}_{T}-\bm{x}_{T}\|^{2}\right\},

(25)

for which we can write a HJB equation:

0=\partial_{t}V_{t}(\bm{x})+\min_{u}\left\{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t}[V_{t}](\bm{x},\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x}))+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}_{t}(\bm{x})\|^{2}\right\},

(26)

subject to the terminal boundary condition
V_{T}(\bm{x})=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{T}\|^{2}
, and the operators
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}},\mathcal{A}
are defined as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{t}[f]

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{A}_{t}\bm{x}+\bm{b}_{t}+\bm{u}_{t},\nabla_{x}f\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\cdot(\bm{\sigma}_{t}\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{\top}\nabla_{x}f),

(27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t}[f]

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{A}_{t}\bm{x}+\bm{b}_{t},\nabla_{x}f\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x}\cdot(\bm{\sigma}_{t}\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{\top}\nabla_{x}f),

(28)

these are the generators of the controlled (
24
) and uncontrolled (
23
) SDEs respectively.
Substituting all these in, we find that the HJBE is

0=\partial_{t}V_{t}(\bm{x})+\mathcal{A}_{t}[V_{t}](\bm{x})+\min_{\bm{u}}\left[\langle\bm{u}_{t},\nabla_{x}V_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}_{t}\|^{2}\right].

(29)

It follows from the first order condition on the “inner” problem that
\bm{u}_{t}=-\nabla_{x}V_{t}
, so

0=\partial_{t}V_{t}(\bm{x})+\mathcal{A}_{t}[V_{t}](\bm{x})-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla_{x}V_{t}(\bm{x})\|^{2}.

(30)

Use an ansatz that the value function is quadratic:

V_{t}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}\bm{x}+\bm{c}_{t}^{\top}\bm{x}+d_{t}\Longrightarrow\nabla_{x}V_{t}(\bm{x})=\bm{M}_{t}\bm{x}+\bm{c}_{t}.

(31)

Then:

\mathcal{A}_{t}[V_{t}]=\bm{x}^{\top}(\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t})\bm{x}+\langle\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{c}_{t}+\bm{M}_{t}\bm{b}_{t},\bm{x}\rangle+\langle\bm{b}_{t},\bm{c}_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\sigma}_{t}\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}).

(32)

Now note that the quadratic form only depends on the
symmetric
part of the matrix, i.e.

\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}\bm{x}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}^{\top}\left(\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}+\bm{M}_{t}\bm{A}_{t}\right)\bm{x}.

The HJBE is therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}0&=\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}^{\top}\dot{\bm{M}}_{t}\bm{x}+\dot{\bm{c}}_{t}^{\top}\bm{x}+\dot{d}_{t}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}^{\top}(\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}+\bm{M}_{t}\bm{A}_{t})\bm{x}+(\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{c}_{t}+\bm{M}_{t}^{\top}\bm{b}_{t})^{\top}\bm{x}+\bm{b}_{t}^{\top}\bm{c}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\sigma}_{t}\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t})\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{M}_{t}\bm{x}+\bm{c}_{t})^{\top}(\bm{M}_{t}\bm{x}+\bm{c}_{t})\end{split}

(33)

So

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\dot{\bm{M}}_{t}+\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}+\bm{M}_{t}\bm{A}_{t}-\bm{M}_{t}^{2},

(34)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\dot{\bm{c}}_{t}+\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}\bm{c}_{t}+\bm{M}_{t}\bm{b}_{t}-\bm{M}_{t}\bm{c}_{t}.

(35)

with the boundary condition
\bm{M}_{T}=\lambda\mathbf{I}
and
\bm{c}_{t}=-\lambda\bm{x}_{T}
.

Let
\bm{\Lambda}_{t}=\bm{M}_{t}^{-1}
, so that
\dot{\bm{\Lambda}}_{t}=-\bm{M}_{t}^{-1}\dot{\bm{M}}_{t}\bm{M}_{t}^{-1}=-\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\dot{\bm{M}}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}
. Substituting, we find that

\dot{\bm{\Lambda}}_{t}=\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{A}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}-\mathbf{I}.

(36)

Actually we can rewrite the value function in a different way, which is more convenient for us:

V_{t}(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}-\bm{k}_{t})^{\top}\bm{M}_{t}(\bm{x}-\bm{k}_{t})+const

in which case
\bm{c}_{t}=-\bm{M}_{t}\bm{k}_{t}
, and so we have
\bm{k}_{T}=\bm{x}_{T}
. The corresponding ODE for
\bm{k}_{t}
is

\dot{\bm{k}}_{t}=\bm{A}_{t}\bm{k}_{t}+\bm{b}_{t}.

(37)

Let us now specialise to the case of time-invariant coefficients, which allows us to write down explicit expressions for the solutions:

\dot{\bm{k}}_{t}=\bm{A}\bm{k}_{t}+\bm{b}\Longrightarrow\bm{k}_{t}=e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}(\bm{x}_{T}+\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{b})-\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{b}.

(38)

Rewriting the SDE drift to have the form
\bm{Ax}+\bm{b}=\bm{A}(\bm{x}-\bm{m})
we have that
\bm{b}=-\bm{A}\bm{m}\Longrightarrow\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{b}=-\bm{m}
, when
\bm{A}
is nonsingular. Then:

\bm{k}_{t}=e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}(\bm{x}_{T}-\bm{m})+\bm{m}.

(39)

Now we deal with the quadratic term
\bm{\Lambda}_{t}
. Let
\bm{G}_{\tau}=\bm{\Lambda}_{T-\tau}
, so that
\partial_{\tau}\bm{G}_{\tau}=-\dot{\bm{\Lambda}}_{T-\tau}
. Then
\bm{G}_{\tau}
satisfies

\partial_{\tau}\bm{G}_{\tau}=-\bm{G}_{\tau}\bm{A}^{\top}-\bm{A}\bm{G}_{\tau}+\mathbf{I},\quad\bm{G}_{0}=0.

(40)

So

\bm{G}_{\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-(\tau-s)\bm{A}}e^{-(\tau-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s.

(41)

Substituting back, we find that

\bm{\Lambda}_{t}=\int_{0}^{T-t}e^{-(T-t-s)\bm{A}}e^{-(T-t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{T-t}e^{-s\bm{A}}e^{-s\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s.

(42)

The bridge control is therefore

\bm{u}_{t|{(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}}=-\bm{\Lambda}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{k}_{t})

(43)

For the general case of non-constant coefficients, we express them as solutions of ODEs with terminal boundary conditions.

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{k}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{A}_{t}\bm{k}_{t}+\bm{b}_{t},\quad\bm{k}_{T}=\bm{x}_{T},

(44)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\Lambda}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{A}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}-\bm{I},\quad\bm{\Lambda}_{T}=0.

(45)

In practice, both of these equations can be approximately solved by numerical integration in time.

Finally we remark here that the diffusion component does not play a role in the control, which is classical.

“Static” Ornstein-Uhlenbeck bridge statistics (Theorem
2
)

As was studied by
[
12
]
for a more general scenario of time-varying processes, the mvOU process and its conditioned versions are Gaussian processes. Under these assumptions,
(\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{t})
has joint mean

\bm{\mu}_{s}\oplus\bm{\mu}_{t},

(46)

and covariance

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Phi}_{s}&&\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\\
e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\Phi}_{s}&&\bm{\Phi}_{t}\end{bmatrix}.

(47)

where
\bm{\Phi}_{t}
is the covariance of the process started from a point mass at time
t
:

\bm{\Phi}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s,\quad\bm{\Phi}_{t}=\bm{\Phi}_{t-s}+e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}.

(48)

By taking the Schur complement, we have that
\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}
is distributed with variance

\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}]=\bm{\Phi}_{s}-\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Phi}_{t}^{-1}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\Phi}_{s}

(49)

and mean

\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}]=\bm{\mu}_{s}+\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Phi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}).

(50)

More generally, we can consider the three-point correlation
(\bm{X}_{r},\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{t})
. Then the mean is
\bm{\mu}_{r}\oplus\bm{\mu}_{s}\oplus\bm{\mu}_{t}
and the covariance is

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Phi}_{r}&\bm{\Phi}_{r}e^{(s-r)\bm{A}^{\top}}&\bm{\Phi}_{r}e^{(t-r)\bm{A}^{\top}}\\
\cdot&\bm{\Phi}_{s}&\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\\
\cdot&\cdot&\bm{\Phi}_{t}\end{bmatrix}

(51)

Using the Schur complement again, we have that
(\bm{X}_{r},\bm{X}_{s})|\bm{X}_{t}
has covariance

\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{r},\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}]=\bm{\Phi}_{r}e^{(s-r)\bm{A}^{\top}}-\bm{\Phi}_{r}e^{(t-r)\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Phi}_{t}^{-1}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\Phi}_{s}.

(52)

For the case of time-varying coefficients, we need to introduce the state transition matrix
\bm{\Psi}_{ts}

[
29
]
which describes the deterministic aspects of evolution between
s<t
under
(\bm{A}_{t})_{t}
. That is, for a dynamics
\dot{\bm{x}}_{t}=\bm{A}_{t}\bm{x}_{t}
one has
\bm{x}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{t,s}\bm{x}_{s}
and
\bm{\Psi}(t,s)=\bm{\Psi}(t,r)\bm{\Psi}(r,s)
for
t\geq r\geq s
. In this case there is not an explicit expression for
\bm{\Phi}_{t}
. Instead, let
(\bm{\Phi}_{t})_{t}
be the unconditional variance evolutions for such a process started at
t=0
, obtained by solving

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\Phi}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{A}_{t}\bm{\Phi}_{t}+\bm{\Phi}_{t}\bm{A}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\sigma}_{t}\bm{\sigma}_{t}^{\top},\quad\bm{\Phi}_{0}=\bm{0}.

(53)

Then the more general result from
[
12
]
for the covariance of
(\bm{X}_{r},\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{r})
is

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Phi}_{r}&\bm{\Phi}_{r}\bm{\Psi}_{sr}^{\top}&\bm{\Phi}_{r}\bm{\Psi}_{tr}^{\top}\\
\bm{\Psi}_{sr}\bm{\Phi}_{r}&\bm{\Phi}_{s}&\bm{\Phi}_{s}\bm{\Psi}_{ts}^{\top}\\
\bm{\Psi}_{tr}\bm{\Phi}_{r}&\bm{\Psi}_{ts}\bm{\Phi}_{s}&\bm{\Phi}_{t}.\end{bmatrix}

(54)

From this result, the same Schur complement computation yields expressions for the conditional covariances and mean:

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{r},\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Phi}_{r}\bm{\Psi}_{sr}^{\top}-\bm{\Phi}_{r}\bm{\Psi}_{tr}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{t}^{-1}\bm{\Psi}_{ts}\bm{\Phi}_{s},

(55)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Phi}_{s}-\bm{\Phi}_{s}\bm{\Psi}_{ts}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{t}^{-1}\bm{\Psi}_{ts}\bm{\Phi}_{s},

(56)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{s}|\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\mu}_{s}+\bm{\Phi}_{s}\bm{\Psi}_{ts}^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t})

(57)

Formal statement and proof of Lemma
1

Lemma

(Formal statement of Lemma
1
)
.

Let
\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}
be a linear SDE with generic drift
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and diffusion
\bm{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. There exists an orthogonal matrix
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
\bm{Y}_{t}=\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{X}_{t}
obeys a linear SDE with transformed drift matrix
\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{U}
and a diagonal diffusion matrix
\bm{\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda})
, where
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\bm{D}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}
. This is positive semidefinite and therefore can its spectral decomposition can be written as
\bm{D}=\frac{1}{2}\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{2}\bm{U}^{\top}
where
\bm{U}
is orthogonal and
\bm{\Lambda}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\lambda})\succeq 0
. Consider the SDEs

\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\sigma}\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}

and

\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top}\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}.

Let
f(\bm{x})
be a twice differentiable test function. SDE (a.) has generator
(\mathcal{L}f)(\bm{x})=\langle\partial_{x}f(\bm{x}),\bm{A}\bm{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\sigma}^{\top}\partial_{xx}f(\bm{x})\bm{\sigma})
.
Computing the generator for SDE (b.),

(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}f)(\bm{x})=\langle\partial_{x}f(\bm{x}),\bm{A}\bm{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top}\partial_{xx}f(\bm{x})\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top})
.
Note that
\mathrm{tr}(\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top}\partial_{xx}f(\bm{x})\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top})=\mathrm{tr}(\partial_{xx}f(\bm{x})\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{2}\bm{U}^{\top})=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{\sigma}^{\top}\partial_{xx}f(\bm{x})\bm{\sigma})
.
Hence both SDEs share the same generator, and so are equal in law.

Since orthogonal transformations of Brownian increments are also Brownian increments, we have that
\bm{U}^{\top}\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}=\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{B}}_{t}
. Substituting into (b.), we have
\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{B}}_{t}
. Then,

\mathrm{d}(\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{X}_{t})=\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{X}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{B}}_{t}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{d}\bm{Y}_{t}=\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{U}\bm{Y}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\bm{\Lambda}\mathrm{d}\tilde{\bm{B}}_{t}.

∎

A.3
Simulation-free Schrödinger bridges with linear reference dynamics

Having characterised the solution of the SBP for general reference processes and now that we have derived the score and flow for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck bridge, the path is clear towards a simulation-free scheme for learning Schrödinger bridges where the reference dynamics are given by a
linear
SDE.

For solution of the static SBP problem (
18
) (equivalently, (
SBP-static
)), the transition kernel of the reference dynamics is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}_{0t}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{t}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}})\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}(\bm{x}_{0})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow-\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}_{0t}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\otimes\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}})+\log Z_{t}-\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{Q}_{0}}{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}(\bm{x}_{0})\right),

(58)

where
\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}}
denotes the mean at time
t
conditional on
(0,\bm{x}_{0})
.
Notably, the last two terms depend only on
\bm{x}_{0}
. It is a classical result (and very easy to show) that these kinds of terms do not affect the minimiser of (
18
) and so they are immaterial. The cost function to use is thus effectively

C(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{t})=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}_{t}-\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}}).

(59)

Once a solution
\bm{\pi}
of the static SBP is on hand, we want to utilise the stochastic regression objective on the conditional score and flow.
Sampling
(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{t})
from
\bm{\pi}
, recall that
\bm{P}^{(x_{0},x_{1})}=\bm{Q}^{(x_{0},x_{1})}
so we want to sample from the
\bm{Q}
-bridge using (
11
), (
12
):

t\sim U[0,1],\quad\bm{x}_{t}^{(x_{0},x_{1})}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1})},\bm{\Sigma}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1})}).

(60)

Equations (
9
) and (
12
) give us the score and flow at
\bm{x}_{t}
.

Static
\mathbb{Q}
-GSB

The standard Gaussian EOT problem has a well known solution
[
32
,
22
,
5
,
21
]
. Here we use the notations of
[
22
]
and define

\operatorname{OT}_{\sigma^{2}}^{\otimes}(\alpha,\beta):=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\alpha,\beta)}\frac{1}{2}\int\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|_{2}^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\pi(\bm{x},\bm{y})+\sigma^{2}\operatorname{H}(\pi|\alpha\otimes\beta),

(61)

where
\sigma>0
is the regularisation level and
\alpha=\mathcal{N}(\bm{a},\bm{A})
and
\beta=\mathcal{N}(\bm{b},\bm{B})
. The solution to (
61
) in the Gaussian case is given by
[
22
, Theorem 1]

\displaystyle\operatorname{OT}_{\sigma^{2}}^{\otimes}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{a}-\bm{b}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{B}^{\otimes}_{\sigma^{2}}\left(\bm{A},\bm{B}\right)

(62)

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}^{\otimes}_{\sigma^{2}}(\bm{A},\bm{B})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(\bm{A})+\mathrm{tr}(\bm{B})-2\mathrm{tr}(\bm{C})+\sigma^{2}\log\det\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\bm{C}+\bm{I}\right),

(63)

\displaystyle\bm{C}

\displaystyle=\bm{A}^{1/2}\left(\bm{A}^{1/2}\bm{B}\bm{A}^{1/2}+\frac{\sigma^{4}}{4}\bm{I}\right)^{1/2}\bm{A}^{-1/2}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\bm{I}.

(64)

We are, of course, interested in a slightly different problem, namely, for
\rho_{0}=\mathcal{N}(\bm{a},\bm{\mathcal{A}})
and
\rho_{T}=\mathcal{N}(\bm{b},\bm{\mathcal{B}})
, we seek to solve
\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{T})}\operatorname{KL}(\pi|\mathbb{Q}_{0,T})
. Expanding the definition of
\operatorname{KL}
and simplifying, we get

(
SBP-static
)

\displaystyle=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{T})}\int\mathrm{d}\pi\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\pi}{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{0,T}}\right)

(65)

\displaystyle=\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{T})}-\int\mathrm{d}\pi\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{0,T}}{\mathrm{d}x_{0}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{T}}\right)+\operatorname{H}(\pi|\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{T})+\operatorname{H}(\rho_{0}|\mathrm{d}x_{0})+\operatorname{H}(\rho_{T}|\mathrm{d}x_{T})

(66)

\displaystyle\simeq\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{T})}-\int\mathrm{d}\pi\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{0,T}}{\mathrm{d}x_{0}\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{T}}\right)+\operatorname{H}(\pi|\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{T})

(67)

\displaystyle\simeq\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{T})}-\int\mathrm{d}\pi(x_{0},x_{T})\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{T|0}(x_{T}|x_{0})}{\mathrm{d}x_{T}}\right)+\operatorname{H}(\pi|\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{T})

(68)

where by
\simeq
we denote equality of the objective up to additive constants which do not affect the minimiser
\pi
. We note that the last line is exactly (
61
) with
\alpha,\beta=\rho_{0},\rho_{T}
and
\sigma=1
and a modified cost.
Further, recognise
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{Q}_{T|0}(x_{T}|x_{0})/\mathrm{d}x_{T}
as the
\mathbb{Q}
-transition density, and
\mathbb{Q}_{T|0}=\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{T}^{\bm{x}_{0}},\bm{\Sigma}_{T})
. Thus,

\eqref{eq:SBP_static}\simeq\min_{\pi\in\Pi(\rho_{0},\rho_{T})}\frac{1}{2}\int(\bm{x}_{T}-\bm{\mu}_{T}^{\bm{x}_{0}})^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1}(\bm{x}_{T}-\bm{\mu}_{T}^{\bm{x}_{0}})\>\mathrm{d}\pi(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})+\operatorname{H}(\pi|\rho_{0}\otimes\rho_{T})

(69)

where we remind that
\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}}
is the mean at time
t
conditional on starting at
(0,\bm{x}_{0})
, and
\bm{\Sigma}_{t}
is the covariance at time
t
, started at a point mass, i.e.
\bm{\Sigma}_{0}=0
.

In particular, we have
\bm{\Sigma}_{t}=\bm{\Phi}_{t}
, using the notation of Theorem
2
.
Recall that in the case of constant coefficients, we have the following explicit expressions:

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{t}^{\bm{x}_{0}}

\displaystyle=e^{t\bm{A}}(\bm{x}_{0}-\bm{m})+\bm{m},

(70)

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\mathrm{d}s.

(71)

Define the following changes of variable
(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})\mapsto(\overline{\bm{x}}_{0},\overline{\bm{x}}_{T})
, injective whenever
\bm{\Sigma}_{T}
is positive definite:

\displaystyle\bm{\overline{x}}_{0}

\displaystyle=T_{0}(\bm{x}_{0})=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{\mu}_{T}^{\bm{x}_{0}},

\displaystyle\bm{\overline{x}}_{T}

\displaystyle=T_{T}(\bm{x}_{T})=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{x}_{T}

Let
\overline{\rho}_{0}=(T_{0})_{\#}\rho_{0}
and
\overline{\rho}_{T}=(T_{T})_{\#}\rho_{T}
, and
\overline{\pi}=(T_{0}\times T_{T})_{\#}\pi
and note that the relative entropy is invariant to this change of coordinates. So the problem (
69
) is equivalent to

\min_{\overline{\pi}\in\Pi(\overline{\rho}_{0},\overline{\rho}_{T})}\frac{1}{2}\int\|\bm{\overline{x}}_{T}-\bm{\overline{x}}_{0}\|_{2}^{2}\>\mathrm{d}\overline{\pi}(\bm{\overline{x}}_{0},\bm{\overline{x}}_{T})+\operatorname{H}(\overline{\pi}|\overline{\rho}_{0}\otimes\overline{\rho}_{T}).

(72)

This is exactly
\operatorname{OT}_{\sigma^{2}=1}^{\otimes}(\overline{\rho}_{0},\overline{\rho}_{T})
as per (
61
)
with
\overline{\rho}_{0}=\mathcal{N}(\overline{\bm{a}},\overline{\bm{\mathcal{A}}})
and
\overline{\rho}_{T}=\mathcal{N}(\overline{\bm{b}},\overline{\bm{\mathcal{B}}})
. The transformed means and covariances are

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{a}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}(e^{T\bm{A}}(\bm{a}-\bm{m})+\bm{m})

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}e^{T\bm{A}}\bm{\mathcal{A}}e^{T\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{b}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{b}

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{\mathcal{B}}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}

We remark that for time-dependent coefficients our approach still applies, however computation of the transformation coefficients will be in terms of the general state transition matrix instead of matrix exponentials. In what follows, we focus on results for constant coefficients for simplicity and their practical relevance.

The solution to (
72
) is therefore

(\overline{\bm{x}}_{0},\overline{\bm{x}}_{T})\sim\overline{\pi}=\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\overline{a}}\\
\bm{\overline{b}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}&\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\\
\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}&\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\end{bmatrix}\right),

where
\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}
is the cross-covariance the transport plan, given by (
64
).

Dynamic
\mathbb{Q}
-GSB : marginals and covariance

Let
\bm{X}_{t}|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})
denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-bridge pinned at
(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})
. Then by Theorem
2
,

\bm{X}_{t}|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})},\bm{\Sigma}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})})=\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})},\bm{\Omega}_{t}).

Invoking the inverse mappings
T_{0}^{-1},T_{T}^{-1}
and substituting the expression for
\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{T})}
from
2
, we get

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{t|(T_{0}^{-1}(\overline{\bm{x}}_{0}),T_{T}^{-1}(\overline{\bm{x}}_{T}))}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{t}\right)\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}}_{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}}\bm{\overline{x}}_{0}+\underbrace{\bm{\Gamma}_{t}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}}_{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}}\bm{\overline{x}}_{T}+\underbrace{\left(\bm{I}-e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}\right)\bm{m}}_{\bm{\mathfrak{c}}_{t}}

(73)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\overline{\bm{x}}_{0}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\overline{\bm{x}}_{T}+\bm{\mathfrak{c}}_{t}.

(74)

where
\bm{\Gamma}_{t}=\bm{\Phi}_{t}e^{(T-t)\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}
. For clarity, we state first a general formula:

Lemma 2
.

Let
(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{1})\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mu}_{0}\\
\bm{\mu}_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Sigma}_{00}&\bm{\Sigma}_{01}\\
\bm{\Sigma}_{10}&\bm{\Sigma}_{11}\end{bmatrix}\right)
. Let

\bm{Y}_{\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{1}}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}\bm{X}_{0}+\bm{B}\bm{X}_{1}+\bm{c},\bm{\Omega}).

Then,
\mathbb{E}[\bm{Y}]=\bm{A}\bm{\mu}_{0}+\bm{B}\bm{\mu}_{1}+\bm{c}
and
\mathbb{V}[\bm{Y}]=\bm{\Omega}+\bm{A}\bm{\Sigma}_{00}\bm{A}^{\top}+\bm{B}\bm{\Sigma}_{11}\bm{B}^{\top}+\bm{A}\bm{\Sigma}_{01}\bm{B}^{\top}+\bm{B}\bm{\Sigma}_{01}^{\top}\bm{A}^{\top}
.

Application to (
74
) gives us the following formula for the variance of the bridge at time
t
:

\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{t}]=\bm{\Omega}_{t}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}=:\bm{\Xi}_{t}.

(75)

Substituting and simplifying, we get the following expressions for each of the terms:

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}

\displaystyle=(\bm{I}-\bm{\Gamma}_{t}e^{(T-t)\bm{A}})e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{\mathcal{A}}e^{t\bm{A}^{\top}}(\bm{I}-\bm{\Gamma}_{t}e^{(T-t)\bm{A}})^{\top},

(76)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Gamma}_{t}\bm{\mathcal{B}}\bm{\Gamma}_{t}^{\top},

(77)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}

\displaystyle=(e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{t})\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}\bm{\Gamma}_{t}^{\top}.

(78)

Similarly, the mean can be computed as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\overline{\bm{a}}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\overline{\bm{b}}+\bm{\mathfrak{c}}_{t}

(79)

\displaystyle=\left(e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}-\bm{\Gamma}_{t}\right)e^{T\bm{A}}(\bm{a}-\bm{m})+\bm{\Gamma}_{t}(\bm{b}-\bm{m})+\bm{m}=:\bm{\nu}_{t}.

(80)

To calculate the covariance of the SB process, we use again the disintegration property (
2
). We have from (
52
) for the
\mathbb{Q}
-bridges and
0<s<t<T
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{t}|\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(t-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}-\bm{\Phi}_{s}e^{(T-s)\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}e^{(T-t)\bm{A}}\bm{\Phi}_{t}=:\bm{\Omega}_{s,t}.

(81)

Now,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(\bm{X}_{s}-\bm{\nu}_{s})(\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{\nu}_{t})^{\top}]

(82)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{t}^{\top}]-\bm{\nu}_{s}\bm{\nu}_{t}^{\top}

(83)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{t}^{\top}|\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T}]]-\bm{\nu}_{s}\bm{\nu}_{t}^{\top}.

(84)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{t}^{\top}|\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{t}|\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T}]+\bm{\mu}_{s|(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T})}\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T})}^{\top},

(85)

in which the first (variance) term doesn’t actually depend on
(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T})
. So,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{s}\bm{X}_{t}^{\top}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Omega}_{st}+\mathbb{E}_{(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T})}[\bm{\mu}_{s|(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T})}\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{X}_{0},\bm{X}_{T})}^{\top}].

(86)

In fact let’s switch to the “mapped” coordinates
\bm{\overline{X}}_{0},\bm{\overline{X}}_{T}
for the endpoints. We abuse notation and omit the inverse map in what follows. Then, from previously,

\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{\overline{X}}_{0},\bm{\overline{X}}_{T})}=\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{X}}_{0}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{X}}_{T}+\bm{\mathfrak{c}}_{t}.

(87)

Expanding, collecting and cancelling terms, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{\mu}_{s|(\bm{\overline{X}}_{0},\bm{\overline{X}}_{T})}

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{\overline{X}}_{0},\bm{\overline{X}}_{T})}^{\top}]-\bm{\nu}_{s}\bm{\nu}_{t}^{\top}

(88)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{s}\mathbb{V}[\bm{\overline{X}}_{0}]\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{s}\mathbb{V}[\bm{\overline{X}}_{0},\bm{\overline{X}}_{T}]\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{s}\mathbb{V}[\bm{\overline{X}}_{T},\bm{\overline{X}}_{0}]\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{s}\mathbb{V}[\bm{\overline{X}}_{T}]\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}

(89)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}.

(90)

Putting everything together, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Omega}_{st}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{s}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}^{\top}=:\bm{\Xi}_{s,t}.

(91)

Dynamic
\mathbb{Q}
-GSB : SDE representation

We proceed via the generator route also used by
[
5
]
. Expanding the covariance (
91
), we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{t},\bm{X}_{t+h}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Xi}_{t,t+h}=\bm{\Omega}_{t,t+h}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t+h}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t+h}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t+h}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t+h}^{\top}

(92)

\displaystyle=\bm{\Xi}_{t}+h\left\{(\partial_{t^{\prime}}\bm{\Omega}_{t,t^{\prime}})(t)+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}}_{t}^{\top}\right\}+...

(93)

\displaystyle=\bm{\Xi}_{t}+h\bm{S}_{t}+...

(94)

where we have set the key quantity

\bm{S}_{t}=(\partial_{t^{\prime}}\bm{\Omega}_{t,t^{\prime}})(t)+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{A}}}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{C}}}^{\top}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}_{t}^{\top}+\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}\bm{\overline{\mathcal{B}}}\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}}_{t}^{\top}

(95)

Now,
(\bm{X}_{t+h},\bm{X}_{t})
are jointly Gaussian with mean and covariance

\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\nu}_{t+h}\\
\bm{\nu}_{t}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\Xi}_{t+h}&\bm{\Xi}_{t+h,t}\\
\bm{\Xi}_{t,t+h}&\bm{\Xi}_{t}\end{bmatrix}.

(96)

So

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bm{X}_{t+h}|\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\nu}_{t+h}+\bm{\Xi}_{t,t+h}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{\nu}_{t})

(97)

\displaystyle=\bm{X}_{t}+h\left[\dot{\bm{\nu}}_{t}+\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{X}_{t}-\bm{\nu}_{t})\right]

(98)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}[\bm{X}_{t+h}|\bm{X}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\bm{\Xi}_{t+h}-\bm{\Xi}_{t,t+h}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}_{t}^{-1}\bm{\Xi}_{t,t+h}

(99)

\displaystyle=h(\dot{\bm{\Sigma}}_{t}-(\bm{S}_{t}+\bm{S}_{t}^{\top})).

(100)

Let
\bm{x}\mapsto u(\bm{x})
be a twice differentiable test function. Then:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[u(\bm{X}_{t+h})|\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{x}\right]

(101)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\mathbb{E}\left[u(\bm{X}_{t})+(\partial_{x}u(\bm{X}_{t}))^{\top}(\bm{X}_{t+h}-\bm{X}_{t})+\right.\\
&\hskip 50.00008pt\left.\frac{1}{2}(\bm{X}_{t+h}-\bm{X}_{t})^{\top}(\partial_{xx}^{2}u(\bm{X}_{t}))(\bm{X}_{t+h}-\bm{X}_{t})\Biggl|\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{x}\right]\end{split}

(102)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=u(\bm{x})+h(\partial_{x}u(\bm{x}))^{\top}\left(\dot{\bm{\nu}}_{t}+\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{\nu}_{t})\right)+\\
&\hskip 50.00008pt\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[(\bm{X}_{t+h}-\bm{X}_{t})^{\top}(\partial_{xx}^{2}u(\bm{X}_{t}))(\bm{X}_{t+h}-\bm{X}_{t})\Biggl|\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{x}\right]\end{split}

(103)

\displaystyle=u(\bm{x})+h(\partial_{x}u(\bm{x}))^{\top}\left(\dot{\bm{\nu}}_{t}+\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{\nu}_{t})\right)+\frac{h}{2}\mathrm{tr}[(\partial_{xx}^{2}u(\bm{x}))(\dot{\bm{\Xi}}_{t}-(\bm{S}_{t}+\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}))]+...

(104)

Subtracting
u(\bm{x})
and taking the limit
h\to 0
, it is clear from the definition of the generator that the SDE drift is

\dot{\bm{\nu}}_{t}+\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{\Xi}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{x}-\bm{\nu}_{t}).

(105)

This takes the same form as the equation found in
[
5
]
, however our formulae for
\bm{S}_{t}
allow us to apply it to any linear reference SDE, not necessarily ones with scalar drift. Additionally, we empirically verify that for asymmetric
\bm{A}
the matrix
\bm{S}_{t}
is asymmetric. This contrasts with the symmetric nature of the drift for the gradient-type setting.

Now we want to work out what each of these terms are in practice. Effectively we need to compute
\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}_{t}
,
\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}}_{t}
and
(\partial_{t^{\prime}}\bm{\Omega}_{t,t^{\prime}})(t)
:

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{A}}}_{t}

\displaystyle=(\bm{A}e^{-(T-t)\bm{A}}-\dot{\bm{\Gamma}}_{t})\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2},

(106)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\mathfrak{B}}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\dot{\bm{\Gamma}}_{t}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2},

(107)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\Gamma}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}e^{T\bm{A}^{\top}}-\bm{\Phi}_{t}\bm{A}^{\top}e^{(T-t)\bm{A}^{\top}}\right)\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1},

(108)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t^{\prime}}\bm{\Omega}_{t,t^{\prime}})(t)

\displaystyle=\bm{\Phi}_{t}\left[\bm{A}^{\top}-e^{(T-t)\bm{A}^{\top}}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}e^{(T-t)\bm{A}}\left(-\bm{A}\bm{\Phi}_{t}+e^{t\bm{A}}\bm{\sigma}\bm{\sigma}^{\top}e^{t\bm{A}^{\top}}\right)\right].

(109)

Remark on the case of time-varying coefficients

In this case we can still assume without loss of generality that we still work on the time interval
[0,T]
, by shifting the time coordinate if necessary. Let
\bm{\Psi}(t,s)
be the state transition matrix associated with
\bm{A}_{t}
and
\bm{\Phi}_{t}
be the solution at time
t
to (
53
). Then we still have
\bm{\Sigma}_{T}=\bm{\Phi}_{T}
for the transition kernel covariance in the cost, i.e. the covariance started from a point mass. For the mean of the reference process started from
\bm{x}_{0}
, we have the generalised formula

\bm{\mu}_{t}(\bm{x}_{0})=\bm{\Psi}(t,0)\bm{x}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}\bm{\Psi}(t,s)\bm{A}_{s}\bm{m}_{s}\>\mathrm{d}s,

(110)

and we remark that its inverse
\bm{\mu}_{t}^{-1}
always exists since
\bm{\Psi}
is never singular:

\bm{\mu}_{t}^{-1}(\bm{y})=\bm{\Psi}(t,0)^{-1}\left(\bm{y}+\int_{0}^{t}\bm{\Psi}(t,s)\bm{A}_{s}\bm{m}_{s}\>\mathrm{d}s\right).

(111)

Then

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{a}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{\mu}_{T}^{\bm{a}},

(112)

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{\Psi}(t,0)\bm{\mathcal{A}}\bm{\Psi}(t,0)^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2},

(113)

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{b}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{b},

(114)

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}\bm{\mathcal{B}}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{-1/2}.

(115)

Recall that the general expression for the
\mathbb{Q}
-bridge conditioned on
(0,\bm{x}_{0}),(T,\bm{x}_{T})
is

\bm{\mu}_{t|(\bm{x}_{0},\bm{x}_{1})}=\bm{\mu}_{t}(\bm{x}_{0})+\bm{\Phi}_{t}\bm{\Psi}(T,t)^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}(\bm{x}_{T}-\bm{\mu}_{T}(\bm{x}_{0})).

(116)

Letting
\bm{x}_{0}=\bm{\mu}_{T}^{-1}(\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}\overline{\bm{x}}_{0})
and
\bm{x}_{T}=\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}\overline{\bm{x}}_{T}
and substituting the expression for
\bm{\mu}_{t}^{-1}
we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bm{\mu}_{t|(T_{0}^{-1}(\overline{\bm{x}}_{0}),T_{T}^{-1}(\overline{\bm{x}}_{T}))}&=\left(\bm{\Psi}(t,T)-\bm{\Phi}_{t}\bm{\Psi}(T,t)^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}\right)\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}\overline{\bm{x}}_{0}\\
&\quad+\bm{\Phi}_{t}\bm{\Psi}(T,t)^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2}\overline{\bm{x}}_{T}\\
&\quad+\int_{t}^{T}\bm{\Psi}(t,s)\bm{A}_{s}\bm{m}_{s}\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(117)

Set
\bm{\Gamma}_{t}=\bm{\Phi}_{t}\bm{\Psi}(T,t)^{\top}\bm{\Phi}_{T}^{-1}
, then

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{A}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{\Psi}(t,T)-\bm{\Gamma}_{t}\right)\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2},

(118)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{B}}_{t}

\displaystyle=\bm{\Gamma}_{t}\bm{\Sigma}_{T}^{1/2},

A.6
Additional background

Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation

Consider the
deterministic
control problem on
[0,T]
:

V_{0}(x_{0})=\min_{u}\int_{0}^{T}C(x_{t},u_{t})\>\mathrm{d}t+D(x_{T}).

(151)

subject to a dynamics
\dot{x}_{t}=F(x_{t},u_{t})
.
V_{t}(x)
is the
value
function for the problem starting at
(t,x)
up to the final time
T
. The function
x_{T}\mapsto D(x_{T})
specifies a cost on the final value. The corresponding Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation
[
23
, Section 3.11]
is

\partial_{t}V_{t}(x)+\min_{u_{t}}\left\{\langle\nabla_{x}V_{t}(x),F(x,u_{t}(x))\rangle+C(x,u_{t}(x))\right\}=0,

(152)

subject to the final boundary condition
V_{T}(x)=D(x)
.
A heuristic derivation is as follows. Considering a time interval
(t,t+\delta t)
and a path
x_{t}
, it’s clear that

V_{t}(x_{t})=\min_{u}\left\{\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}C(x_{s},u_{s})\mathrm{d}s+V_{t+\delta t}(x_{t+\delta t})\right\}.

(153)

Using Taylor expansion and the constraint
\dot{x}_{t}=F(x_{t},u_{t})
we have to leading order that

V_{t+\delta t}(x_{t+\delta t})=V_{t}(x_{t})+\left(\partial_{t}V_{t}(x_{t})+\langle\nabla_{x}V_{t}(x_{t}),F(x_{t},u_{t})\rangle\right)\delta t+O(\delta t^{2}).

(154)

Substituting back and also approximating the integral, we find that

V_{t}(x_{t})=\min_{u}\>\>C(x_{t},u_{t})\delta t+V_{t}(x_{t})+\left(\partial_{t}V_{t}(x_{t})+\langle\nabla_{x}V_{t}(x_{t}),F(x_{t},u_{t})\rangle\right)\delta t

(155)

Cancelling terms, rearranging and taking a limit
\delta t\downarrow 0
, we get the desired result.

Stochastic Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman

Now we consider the
stochastic
variant of the HJB, in which case
X_{t}
is driven by an SDE of the form

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=F(X_{t},u_{t})\>\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_{t}\>\mathrm{d}B_{t}.

(156)

The value function is thus in expectation:

V_{0}(x_{0})=\min_{u}\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{0}^{T}C(X_{t},u_{t})\>\mathrm{d}t+D(X_{T})\right\}.

(157)

Carrying out the same expansion as before, we have

V_{t}(x_{t})=\min_{u}\mathbb{E}\left\{\int_{t}^{t+\delta t}C(X_{s},u_{s})\mathrm{d}s+V_{t+\delta t}(X_{t+\delta t})\right\}.

(158)

Note that in the above,
(X_{t+\delta t}|X_{t}=x_{t})
is a random variable and hence so is
V_{t+\delta t}(X_{t+\delta t})
which is why it appears in the expectation. Using Itô’s formula to expand this, we get

\displaystyle V_{t+\delta t}(X_{t+\delta t})

\displaystyle=V_{t}(X_{t})+\left[\partial_{t}V_{t}(X_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{t}\sigma_{t}^{\top}\nabla_{x}V_{t}(X_{t}))\right]\mathrm{d}t+\langle\nabla_{x}V_{t}(X_{t}),\mathrm{d}X_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=V_{t}(X_{t})+\left[\partial_{t}V_{t}(X_{t})+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{t}\sigma_{t}^{\top}\nabla_{x}V_{t}(X_{t}))+\langle\nabla_{x}V_{t}(X_{t}),F(X_{t},u_{t})\rangle\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 42.50006pt+\langle\nabla_{x}V_{t}(X_{t}),\sigma_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}\rangle.

Plugging this in, cancelling terms, and noting that the final term has zero expectation, we find that

0=\partial_{t}V_{t}(X_{t})+\min_{u}\left\{\mathcal{A}[V_{t}](X_{t},u_{t})+C(X_{t},u_{t})\right\}

(159)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the generator of the SDE governing
X_{t}
, i.e.

\mathcal{A}[f](x,u)=\langle F(x,u),\nabla_{x}f(x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{t}\sigma_{t}^{\top}\nabla_{x}f).

(160)

The HJB equation for stochastic control problem is therefore

0=\partial_{t}V_{t}(x)+\min_{u}\left\{\mathcal{A}[V_{t}](x,u_{t}(x))+C(x,u_{t}(x))\right\},

(161)

i.e. this is the same as the HJB for the deterministic case, except with a diffusive term arising from the stochasticity.

For BM-OTFM, the same training procedure was used except the reference process was taken to be Brownian motion with unit diffusivity.

IPFP

We use the IPFP implementation provided by the authors of
[
53
]
, specifically using the variant of their algorithm based on Gaussian process approximations to the Schrödinger bridge drift
[
53
, Algorithm 2]
. We provide the mvOU reference process as the prior drift function, i.e.
\bm{x}\mapsto\bm{A}(\bm{x}-\bm{m})
and employ an exponential kernel for the Gaussian process vector field approximation. We run the IPFP algorithm for 10 iterations, which is double that used in the original publication. Since IPFP explicitly constructs forward and reverse processes, at each time
0\leq t\leq 1
, IPFP outputs
two
estimates of the Schrödinger bridge marginal, one from each process. We consider both outputs and distinguish between them using the
(\rightarrow,\leftarrow)
symbols in Table
1
. For the vector field estimate we employ only the forward drift.

NLSB

We use the NLSB implementation provided by the authors of
[
25
]
. Following
[
10
, Section 4.6]
, for a mvOU reference process
\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{f}_{t}\>\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}
, the generalised SB problem (
SBP-dyn
) can be rewritten as a stochastic control problem

\min_{\bm{u}_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{u}_{t}\|_{2}^{2}\>\mathrm{d}t\right],\>\text{subject to}\>\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=(\bm{f}_{t}+\bm{u}_{t})\>\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}.

On the other hand, the Lagrangian SB problem
[
25
, Definition 3.1]
is

\min_{\bm{v}_{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{1}L(t,\bm{x},\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{x}))\mathrm{d}t\right],\>\text{subject to}\>\mathrm{d}\bm{X}_{t}=\bm{v}_{t}\>\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\>\mathrm{d}\bm{B}_{t}.

This shows that the appropriate Lagrangian to use is
L(t,\bm{x},\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{x}))=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{v}_{t}(\bm{x})-\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x})\|_{2}^{2}
. We apply this in the mvOU setting by taking
\bm{f}_{t}(\bm{x})=\bm{A}(\bm{x}-\bm{m})
. The NLSB approach backpropagate through solution of the SDE to directly learn a neural approximation of the SB drift
\bm{v}_{t}
.
We train NLSB the same hyperparameters as used in the original publication, using the Adam optimiser with learning rate
10^{-3}
for a total of 2,500 epochs.

Metrics

We measure both the approximation error of Gaussian Schrödinger bridge marginals as well as the error in vector field estimation. For the marginal reconstruction, for each method and for each
0\leq t\leq 1
we sample points integrated forward in time and compute the mean
\hat{\bm{\mu}}_{t}
and variance
\hat{\bm{\Sigma}}_{t}
from samples. We then compare to the ground truth marginals
(\bm{\mu}_{t},\bm{\Sigma}_{t})
computed from exact formulas using the Bures-Wasserstein metric (
62
). For the vector field, for each method and for each time
0\leq t\leq 1
we sample
M=1024
points from the ground truth SB marginal
\bm{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t},\bm{\Sigma}_{t})
and empirically estimate
\|\hat{\bm{v}}_{\mathrm{SB}}-\bm{v}_{\mathrm{SB}}\|_{\mathcal{N}(\bm{\mu}_{t},\bm{\Sigma}_{t})}
from samples. All experiments were repeated over 5 independent runs and summary statistics for metrics are shown in Table
1
.

B.2
Gaussian mixture example

We consider initial and terminal marginals sampled from Gaussian mixtures. At time
t=0
, we sample from

\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{U}\begin{bmatrix}-0.5&-0.5\end{bmatrix}^{\top},0.01\bm{U}\bm{U}^{\top}\right),\qquad\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{U}\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0.5\end{bmatrix}^{\top},0.0625\bm{U}\bm{U}^{\top}\right)

in a 1:1 ratio, and at time
t=1
we sample in the same fashion from

\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{U}\begin{bmatrix}-2.5&-2.5\end{bmatrix}^{\top},0.01\bm{U}\bm{U}^{\top}\right),\qquad\mathcal{N}\left(\bm{U}\begin{bmatrix}2.5&2.5\end{bmatrix}^{\top},0.25\bm{U}\bm{U}^{\top}\right).

Here, the matrix
\bm{U}
and reference process parameters
(\bm{A},\bm{m})
are the same as used for the previous Gaussian example for
d=10
. All other training details are the same as for the Gaussian example.

SBIRR

We apply SBIRR
[
44
]
using the implementation provided with the original publication, which notably includes an improved implementation of IPFP
[
53
]
that utilises GPU acceleration. We provide the reference vector field
\bm{x}\mapsto\bm{A}(\bm{x}-\bm{m})
and seek to learn a reference process in one of two families: (1) mvOU processes, i.e. we consider the family of reference drifts
\hat{\bm{A}}(\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{m}})
where
\hat{\bm{A}},\hat{\bm{m}}
are to be fit, or (2) general drifts, i.e. we parameterise the drift using a feed-forward neural network with hidden dimensions
[64,64,64]
. For each choice of reference family, we run Algorithm 1 of
[
44
]
for 5 outer iterations and 10 inner IPFP iterations, as was also done in the original paper.

Dataset of the complete mitogenomes of Galaxea (Scleractinia: Euphyllidae)
Authors: Zhangwang Lu, Liwei Li, Zhiying Lu, Chengchong Fu, Jun Wang, Yan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGalaxea is a genus of colonial stony corals that are widely distributed in the Indian and Pacific Oceans. According to the differences of microbasic p-mastigophore (MpM) nematocysts and mitochondrial haplotype, four types (type S, type H, type M and type S+) were identified in this genus. There is significant genetic differentiation among those types and they belong to distinct branches in the Maximum likelihood tree, therefore they conform to the primary species hypotheses (PSHs) and the whole Galaxea might be a species complex. Here we present the assembly and annotation of eleven mitogenomes of the four types and used the dataset to explore their phylogeny. The eleven Galaxea mitogenomes all contained the same 13 PCGs (protein-coding genes) and two rRNA genes as the published mitogenome of G. fascicularis (GenBank No NC_029696). However, the length of mitogenomes of distinct types varies because there are two characteristic indels in the mitogenomes, one is located at the intergenic region (IGR) of CYTB-ND2 and the other is located at the IGR of COX1-trnM. The phylogenetic analysis based on 13 PCGs showed that type S/M, type H and type S+ clustered into three separate clades. These mitogenomes will be valuable for better understanding the phylogeny, speciation, and differentiation of the Galaxea species complex.Keywords: Mitogenome, Galaxea (scleractinia: euphyllidae), Species complex, Phylogeny

Background

Galaxea (Oken, 1815), a genus of colonial stony coral in the family Euphylliidae, is widely distributed on the reefs of the Indian and Pacific Oceans [1]. The classical classification of the Galaxea is according to the size of corallite, the number of septum cycles, and the morphology of colony, e.g. G. fascicularis (Linnaeus, 1767) and G. astreata (Lamarck, 1816) are massive, G. paucisepta (Claereboudt, 1990) is laminar, and G. horrescens (Dana, 1846) and G. acrhelia (Veron, 2000) are branched [2].

According to the differences in the hardness of the coenosarc connecting polyps, G. fascicularis, the representative species of Galaxea, can be divided into two morphotypes [3]: type S (soft) and type H (hard), and the MpM (microbasic p-mastigophore) nematocysts of oral tentacles of the two morphotypes were significantly different. S type MpM nematocysts are wider and the shafts of nematocyst are longer, while H type MpM nematocysts are slender and the shafts are shorter [3] (Fig. 1). A few individuals have both types of MpM nematocysts [4,5] and are named as type M (mix). Moreover, the intergenic region (IGR) between mitochondrial gene CYTB and ND2 of these three morphotypes is also different that type S and type M (type S/M) contains an indel of 290 bp in that IGR [this mitochondrial haplotype was named as mt-L (mt-Long)], while type H doesn't [haplotype mt-S (mt-Short)] [5].

The MpM nematocysts of Galaxea species. (a) soft (S) type, the nematocysts are wider and the shafts of nematocyst are longer, (b) hard (H) type, the nematocysts are slender and the shafts are shorter.

When we used microsatellite markers to explore the genetic differentiation of those morphotypes [6], similar to the result of Nakajima et al. (2016) [7], a third genetic cluster was found aside from type S/M and type H. It has S type nematocysts but mitochondrial haplotype detection showed that its indel in the IGR of CYTB-ND2 is 293 bp [Nakajima et al. (2016) defined this mitochondrial haplotype as mt-L+] [7]. We named this cluster as type S+.

There is significant genetic differentiation among type S/M, type H, and type S+, and they belong to three distinct branches in the Maximum likelihood tree with robust support values [6]. Hence, these types conform to the primary species hypotheses (PSHs) [8] and we named these PSHs as G. fascicularis_S/M, G. fascicularis_H and G. fascicularis_S+.

Except for type S+, those types were also identified in other species of Galaxea [4,9], e.g., type S was identified in G. astreata, G. paucisepta, G. horrescens and G. acrhelia, type H was identified in G. astreata and G. paucisepta, and type M was identified in G. horrescens. Therefore, we propose that the whole Galaxea genus might be a species complex. Because of the existence of extensive introgression in the coral species complexes [10], the phylogenetic tree constructed with mitogenome can better reflect the evolutionary relationships among species than those constructed with nuclear genome [11]. Here we sequenced and annotated the mitogenomes of those types (Table 1), and analyzed the dataset to further explore their phylogeny.

There are nine nominal species were classified in the genus Galaxea [1]. In the five consecutive years of South China Sea survey from 2019 to 2023, five nominal species were found (Table 1) and only in G. fascicularis we have identified all the four types [6]. Some types are missing, likely due to the small number of samples, e.g. we only have two G. horrescens samples and one G. acrhelia sample. We hope the researchers worldwide would publish more Galaxea mitogenomes [only one Galaxea (G. fascicularis) mitogenome has been published in GenBank till now], especially for the missing species and missing types, so that we could depict a full picture of the speciation and phylogeny of Galaxea species complex.

Data Description

A total of eleven Galaxea mitogenomes was sequenced and annotated (Table 1). These mitogenomes are highly similar to each other with the similarity greater than 99.69 % (Table 2). The eleven Galaxea mitogenomes all contained the same 13 PCGs (protein-coding genes) and two rRNA genes as the previously published mitogenome of G. fascicularis (GenBank No NC_029696, which is identical to the mitogenome of G. fascicularis_S we sequenced), the size and the order of those genes are also same with that of NC_029696 (Supplementary Table 1).

The mitogenomes of type S and type M are most similar, and their genome length and the indels described below are all the same. We therefore assign the two types as one group: type S/M.

Interestingly, the similarity between different species of the same type is higher than the similarity between different types of the same species. For example, the similarity of Gf_S vs. Ga_S and Gf_H vs. Ga_H are 99.97 % and 99.97 %, respectively (Table 2), which are higher than those of Gf_S vs. Gf_H and Ga_S vs. Ga_H (99.71 % and 99.72 %, respectively). In other words, the difference between types is higher than the difference between species.

Mitogenomes of the same type have the same length, the mitogenome length of type S+ is 18,774 bp, and that of type S/M and type H are 18,751 bp and 18,481 bp, respectively (Table 1, Fig. 2). The length of mitogenomes varies among distinct types because there are two indels in Galaxea mitogenomes, one is described above, located at the IGR of CYTB-ND2, while the other indel is located at the IGR of COX1-trnM with the length of 20 bp. For both type S+ and type H, their mitogenomes contain this indel, whereas type S/M does not (Supplementary Fig. 1).

The maps of Galaxea mitogenome. Due to the existence of two indels, one locates in the IGR between CYTB and ND2 and the other locates in the IGR between COX1 and trnM, the length of mitogenomes varies among distinct types.

The Bayesian phylogenetic tree of Galaxea based on 13 protein-coding genes was constructed (Fig. 3). Consistent with the similarity, the tree clustered by type that type S/M and type H clustered into one clade, respectively, whereas type S+ clustered into another separate clade. The published mitogenome of G. fascicularis (NC_029696) located in the clade of type S/M.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Sample collection and identification

The Galaxea samples were collected at Hainan fringe reefs, Xisha Islands and Zhongsha Atoll (all located in the South China Sea) from 2019 to 2022 (Table 1). Permits for coral sampling were granted by the Management Office of Sanya National Coral Reef Nature Reserve (China), and the Department of Ocean and Fisheries of Hainan Province (China). The longitude and the latitude of sample location range from 109°21′54″E∼114°46′09″E, and from 15°54′26″N∼18°14′16″N, respectively. Five samples of Fimbriaphyllia and Euphyllia, other two genera of the family Euphylliidae, were also collected (Fimbriaphyllia paraancora was sampled at Zhongsha Atoll, other four samples were provided by professor Hui Huang, who purchased them from a legitimate commercial company and the samples were collected in Southeast Asia), and they were used to construct the phylogenic tree as outgroup taxa in this study. The Galaxea species were identified according to the morphology of polyp and septa [4], while the type of MpM nematocyst was determined under microscope according to Hidaka (1992) [3].

Because there are a few chimeras of different types [12,13], the same polyp must be used to identify mitochondrial haplotype and MpM nematocyst type. The mitochondrial haplotype was identified as follows: genomic DNA was extracted using the Tianamp Marine Animals DNA Kit (TIANGEN, CHINA), and the intergeneric region between CYTB and ND2 was amplified using primers 188–2 (5′-TCCTGTAGAATAGGGTATAC-3′) and 188-R2 (5′-TTTGCCTTTCCGTATCCACCAT-3′) [5]. The amplicons were sent to Guangzhou Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. for sequencing. The intergeneric region containing the indel of 290 bp was determined as haplotype mt-L, containing the indel of 293 bp was determined as mt-L+, and missing the indel was determined as mt-S, respectively [6].

Primer design and mitogenome sequencing

The published mitogenomes of Galaxea fascicularis (GenBank No NC_029696), Fimbriaphyllia ancora (NC_015641) and Acropora tenuis (AF338425) were aligned by MEGA 4.0 [14]. According to the conserved sequences regions in the alignment, 38 pairs of primers (Supplementary Table 2) were designed using Primer 3 (https://primer3.ut.ee/). The PCR product length of each primer pair was about 1 kb, and the overlapping sequences between two adjacent primer pairs range from 50 to 450 bp. The PCR system contained 5 µL 2 × Taq PCR MasterMix II (Tiangen, China), with 5 pmol (1 µL) forward and reverse primers, 1 µL template DNA (< 100 ng), and finally supplemented with dd H2O to a final volume 10 µL per reaction. The PCR procedure was as follows: 94 °C for 3 min; 30 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C (30 s), annealing at 50 °C (30 s), extension at 72 °C (45 s); and an elongation at 72 °C for 5 min. The purified PCR products were sent to Guangzhou Qingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd. for bidirectional sequencing.

Mitogenome assembly and annotation

MEGA 4.0 [14] was used to assemble obtained raw sequences and the assembled sequences were confirmed by aligning them with the published mitogenome of Galaxea fascicularis (NC_029696). The assembled Galaxea mitogenome sequences were imported into MITOS web server (http://mitos.bioinf.uni-leipzig.de/) [15] for gene annotation. The start and stop codon positions of each gene were manually corrected. Proksee online (https://proksee.ca/) was used for the mitogenome visualization [16]. The similarity among the Galaxea mitogenomes was calculated using the BLAST program of NCBI (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/).

Phylogenetic analysis

Bayesian inference phylogeny tree was constructed based on 13 PCGs. Four mitogenome sequences of Fimbriaphyllia [three were sequenced in this study and one from GenBank (NC_015641, Fimbriaphyllia ancora)] and two sequences of Euphyllia were set as outgroup taxa in analysis. The published mitogenome of Galaxea fascicularis (NC_029696) was also retrieved from GenBank and included in the phylogenetic analysis.

The 13 PCGs were firstly aligned separately using MAFFT v.7.407 [17], and subsequently Gblocks v.0.91b [18] was applied to remove ambiguously aligned regions using default settings. Based on the corrected Akaike Information Criterion [AICc; [19]], the best partition scheme, as well as the best-fit nucleotide substitution models for the respective partitions, were detected by PartitionFinder 2 [20]. MrBayes v.3.2.7 [21] was used for the Bayesian inference analysis, and ETE Tree Browser [22] was used to visualize the resulting phylogeny tree.

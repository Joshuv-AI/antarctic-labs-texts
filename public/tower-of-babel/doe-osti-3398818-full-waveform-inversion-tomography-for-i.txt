Key Points
 • Full waveform inversion tomography improves waveform ﬁts while inferring radially

 anisotropic wavespeed structure northwest China and the surrounding regions.

 • The inversion relies on openly available data and a simple isotropic starting model that

 honors signiﬁcant crustal thickness and long wavelength topographic variations.

 • The resulting model images strong lateral variations in wavespeed structure including

 several known tectonic provinces and provides signiﬁcantly improved waveform ﬁts

 compared to alternative models.

Abstract
 We report a new model of three-dimensional (3D) anisotropic seismic wavespeed structure
of the lithospheric of northwestern China, western Mongolia and east
}|ern Kazakhstan from full waveform inversion tomography using openly available broadband data.
To investigate the eTicacy of a conservative multiscale inversion methodology, we used a simple
isotropic starting model. The starting model honors signiﬁcant lateral variations in crustal
thickness and surface topography but with volumetric structure in the crust composed of a linear
gradient and mantle properties from the global average ak135-f model (Montagner & Kennett,
1995). Openly available waveforms were inverted ﬁrst in the band 50-100 s and the minimum
period was gradually reduced to 16 seconds in 8 stages and 105 total iterations. The resulting
model (NWCH) improves waveform ﬁts compared to the starting model and two other 3D models:
SinoScope1.0 (Ma et al., 2022) and SPiRaL (Simmons et al., 2021). NWCH reveals known features
of the region including low wavespeed sedimentary structure of the Tarim, Junggar and Qaidam
Basins, high wavespeeds and thick lithosphere of the Tarim Craton, low crustal and mantle
wavespeeds of the Tibetan Plateau, Hangyar Dome and Baikal Rift. The fact that waveform ﬁts can
be improved and these features can be revealed from such a simpliﬁed starting model
demonstrates the power of multiscale iterative full waveform inversion tomography to infer
lithospheric structure and importance of honoring the crust-mantle interface in regions of large
crustal thickness variations.

Introduction
 Full waveform inversion (FWI) tomography is a methodology for inferring the Earth’s seismic

structure and improving ﬁts to observed seismograms (Fichtner, 2010; Tromp, 2020). To reduce the

computational eTort to ﬁt waveforms to some desired period, it is preferred to start with a good

three-dimensional (3D) model representing seismic structure (wavespeeds, density and

attenuation) of the region. Ideally this model should be as close as possible to the ﬁnal model to

minimize iterations needed to ﬁnd a global minimum misﬁt, but not too complex (e.g. with strong

small-scale heterogeneity) which might risk getting trapped in a local minimum. Recent workﬂow

improvements and high-performance computing have made it easier to infer seismic Earth models

with FWI (Krischer et al., 2015, 2016; Modrak and Tromp, 2016; Modrak et al., 2018; Chow et al.,

2020). Recent studies using the Salvus waveform simulation and inversion software have

documented a more eTicient workﬂow enabling more iterations and better waveform ﬁts compared

to previous studies (e.g. Rodgers et al., 2022; 2024; Doody et al., 2023b; Thrastarson et al., 2024;

Rodgers, 2025). Doody et al. (2023a) showed that a conservative multiscale FWI approach using

diTerent 3D models can infer models that are nearly equivalent in their structure and goodness-of-

ﬁt to the data. Improvements in workﬂow eTiciencies along with a deliberate multiscale inversion

approach beg the question: Can the current state-of-the-art FWI methodology starting from a

simple nearly homogeneous model with reasonably good data coverage improve waveform ﬁts and

infer 3D structure? In this report we investigate the eTicacy of FWI to image complex tectonic

structure in northwestern China and the surrounding region with a simple starting model. Our

study region is shown in Figure 1.

Methodology
 This study uses a multiscale inversion approach and follows the methodology described in

several recent studies (Rodgers et al., 2022; 2024; Doody et al., 2023; Rodgers, 2025). However, in

this study rather than use a fully 3D model from an independent study as the starting model, we

used a very simple model to investigate the eTicacy of our AWT methodology applied to openly

available broadband data to infer structure without a 3D starting model. Importantly, our starting

model honored the large crustal thickness variations spanning from normal continental values of

40-45 km in the Kazakh Orocline and North China Craton to values greater than 60 km in the

Tibetan Plateau (Figure 2). This model had a single crustal layer with a linear gradient

approximating that reported by Patton (1998) from regional complete waveform modeling and

mantle properties from the global average ak135-f model (Montagner & Kennett, 1995).

 We ran forward simulations using the Asia1D starting model for all events using the Salvus

spectral element method solver (Afanasiev et al., 2019). We selected windows for each source-

receiver pair using Salvus’ data selection algorithm. To evaluate model performance and select

events for the tomography, we picked windows (waveform segments) based on waveform

comparisons of the observed and simulated seismograms projected to their cardinal components

(vertical, north, east). Salvus’ window picking algorithm is based on that of LASIF (Large-Scale

Seismic Inversion Framework; Krischer et al., 2015) and is broadly similar to methods used in other

regional FWI studies (e.g. Maggi et al., 2009; Tape et al. 2009; Fichtner et al. 2009; Chow et al.,

2020). The window picking algorithm ﬁnds segments of the waveforms where the observed

waveform has good signal-to-noise between the P- and surface waves and the pair of observed and

synthetic waveforms have good amplitude agreement, good correlation and slowly varying phase

diTerences over the window. Selected waveform segments are used later in the inversion to

compute adjoint sources and sensitivity kernels to update the structure. The window picking

algorithm standardizes automatic data selection without analyst subjectivity or intervention and

also serves as an objective measure of model performance. To quantify model performance in

diTerent models and period bands we computed the time-bandwidth product (TBP) of the total

selected waveform segments following previous studies (e.g. Rodgers et al., 2022; 2024). This

provides an objective measure of waveform similarity between observed and simulated waveforms.

TBP of selected windows measures the information content or degrees of freedom in the signal

pairs. Comparisons of picked window TBP between models in the same period band and for the

same data set quantiﬁes model performance – the larger the TBP the better that a given model is at

producing to the information content in the observed waveforms at a speciﬁc period band.

 The inversion events were selected from a larger collection of about 200 GCMT events

based on window picking. For FWI tomography we want to have events that are well-recorded by

many stations and have long durations of correlated observed and simulated waveform pairs. We

selected events 90 events which had many good recordings of each earthquake based on selected

windows. Figure 5 shows the events, stations and paths used in inversion stage 6 (20-100

seconds).

 We performed a multiscale inversion using 8 inversion stages starting from long periods (50-

100 s) and reducing the minimum period to 16 s. Each stage used a ﬁxed period band, selected

windows and smoothing parameters. For each stage the gradients were smoothed horizontally and

vertically with length scale 0.5 and 0.2 times the minimum shear wave wavelength. We inverted

time-frequency phase misﬁts (Fichtner et al., 2009) using a trust-region limited memory Broyden–

Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno algorithm (L-BFGS) following Rodgers et al. (2022) and other studies

cited. Inversion iterations solved for the radially anisotropic shear and compressional wavespeeds:

vSV, vSH, vPV and vPH. L-BFGS has been shown to improve convergence in waveform inversion

(Modrak and Tromp, 2016). The trust region L-BFGS algorithm uses information about the curvature

of the misﬁt surface from previous iterations to adjust the model update size within the trust region

radius (e.g. Thrastarson et al., 2021, 2022). Table 1 compiles details on each inversion stage.

 We considered two 3D models in addition to the Asia1D starting model. SPiRal (Simmons

et al., 2021) is a global model representing radially anisotropic wavespeeds, density and

attenuation derived from travel times and surface wave dispersion. It has been shown to ﬁt long-

period waveforms reasonably well down periods of 20 seconds (Simmons et al., 2021; Rodgers et

al.; 2022, 2024). SinoScope1.0 (Ma et al., 2022) is an FWI tomographic model mapping radially

anisotropic wavespeeds for a large region of the crust and upper mantle beneath China and its

adjacent regions. We computed forward simulations for these two 3D models and the Asia1D

model for the inversion data set with several minimum periods and a maximum period of 100 s. We

then picked windows and computed the time bandwidth product (TBP) of selected windows

following the procedure described above.

ﬁrstly in terms of the waveform ﬁts and then in terms of the imaged shear wavespeed structure.

 At 100 km depth in the upper mantle the NWCH model shows high vS associated with strong

mantle lid beneath the Tarim Basin, North China Craton and Kazakh Platform. It also shows low vS

beneath the Tibetan Plateau, Hangyar Dome and the Lake Baikal Rift.

 NWCH reveals strong anisotropy that must be required to ﬁt the data because the starting

model (Asia1D) was isotropic. Imaged anisotropy in the crust shows for the most part xS < 1, where

vSV > vSH, while the opposite is found in the mantle. It is common to infer xS > 1, where vSH> vSV in the

uppermost mantle to ﬁt the Love-Rayleigh discrepancy (e.g. Gaherty and Jordan, 1995) and indeed

NWCH reveals xS > 1 in the upper mantle for most of the domain.

Conclusions
 In this report we described a new model of the radially anisotropic seismic wavespeeds of

Northwest China and the surrounding regions. We started with a relatively homogeneous and

isotropic model with a simple gradient in the crust and overlying the ak135-f model (Montagner &

Kennett, 1995) in the mantle. The model is obtained from multiscale full waveform inversion of

broadband earthquake data. Inversions were performed in 8 stages and 106 total iterations,

starting with the period band 50-100 s and ending with 16-80 s. The model provides improved

waveform ﬁts with measurably better ﬁts than the starting model or two 3D models of the region:

SPiRaL (Simmons et al., 2021); and SinoScope1.0 (Ma et al., 2022). The new model can be used for

improved waveform simulations and/or Greens functions for source studies (e.g. moment tensors)

in the region, including earthquakes and events near the Lop Nor (China) and Kazakh (Former

Soviet Union) nuclear test sites.

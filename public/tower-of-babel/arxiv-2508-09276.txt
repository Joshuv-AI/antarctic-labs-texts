Companion matrices as sums of p p -potent and nilpotent matrices
Andrada Pojar

Abstract
. We prove that, over a field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} of odd characteristic p , p, a companion matrix C C is the sum of E E and N , N, with E E p p -potent (i.e. E p = E , E^{p}=E, ) and N N nilpotent, if and only if the trace of C C is an integer multiple of unity of 𝔽 . \mathbb{F}.

15A24, 15A83, 16U99

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.

As usual, the letter
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
will stand for the prime field of
p
elements having characteristic
p
, for any positive integer
n
, the notation
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
will denote the full matrix ring of
n\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{F}.

A square matrix
E
over
\mathbb{F}
is
p
-potent if
E^{p}=E.
An idempotent is a
2
-potent. A square matrix
A
over
\mathbb{F}
is nilpotent if there exists
k
a natural number such that
N^{k}=0.
A square matrix
A
over
\mathbb{F}
is nil-clean if there exist an idempotent
E
and a nilpotent
N
such that
A=E+N.
We consider decompositions of
A
such that there exist a
p
-potent
E
and a nilpotent
N,
with
A=E+N.

Nil-clean decompositions are related to clean ones, introduced by Nicholson in
[
9
]
, when investigating exchange rings and were first studied by Diesl in
[
7
]
. An important result appeared in
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
about them is: every
n\times n
matrix over a division ring
D
is nil-clean if and only if
D=\mathbb{F}_{2}.

An extension of nil-clean decompositions to finite fields of odd cardinality
q,
was done in
[
1
]
: every matrix over such a field is the sum of a
q
-potent
E=E^{q}
and a nilpotent. In
[
2
]
there is even more – the nilpotent
N
involved in such a decomposition can be with nilpotence index at most
3
(i.e.
N^{3}=0).
Decompositions as a sum of a potent and square-zero matrix were considered in
[
6
]
.

In
[
4
]
it has been proved that if
\mathbb{F}
is a field of positive characteristic,
p,
then a companion matrix
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is nil-clean if and only if
A
is nilpotent or unipotent or the trace of
A
is of the form
t\cdot 1,
with
t\in\{1,2,\dots,p\}
such that
n>t
. In
[
5
]
there is a characterization of
n\times n
companion matrices over fields of positive odd characteristic
p
that are sums of
m
idempotents,
m\geq 2
, and a nilpotent, in terms of dimension, and trace of such a matrix, and of
p.
We prove that if the characteristic of the field is odd then a companion matrix
C\in\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent if and only if the trace of
A
is of the form
t\cdot 1,
with
t
an integer number, if and only if
C
is sum of
m

p
-potents and a nilpotent.

2.
Modified companion matrices as a tool

We provide our desired decomposition for companion matrices because each squared matrix over a field is similar to a direct sum of companion matrices. Now since a direct sum of matrices which can be decomposed as a sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent is a matrix with the same property and because of the fact that a matrix similar to a a
p
-potent is a
p
-potent and a matrix similar to a nilpotent is a nilpotent, our wanted decomposition for our initial matrix can be obtained.

Letting
q
be a monic polynomial (i.e., its leading coefficient is
1
) over
\mathbb{F},
with
q=X^{n}+c_{n-1}X^{n-1}+\ldots+c_{1}X+c_{0}
, we explicitly indicate the companion matrix associated to
q
as the
n\times n
matrix

C=C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0&0&\ldots&0&-c_{0}\\
1&0&\ldots&0&-c_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&1&-c_{n-1}\end{array}\right)

To avoid some inaccuracies with the exact meaning, we also denote
C
by
C_{q}
using the subscript
q
which may vary in each of the different cases.

Let
k
a nonnegative integer,
k\leq n,
and
\mathbb{F}
a field. Let
a
be a nonzero integer multiple of the unity of
\mathbb{F}
. A type of modified companion matrix
\widetilde{C}_{q,k,a}
associated to q, namely

\widetilde{C}_{q,k,a}=C_{q}+\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0),
over
\mathbb{F},
has been defined in
[
5
]
for
a=1
.

Let
k
be nonnegative integer,
k\leq n,
and
\mathbb{F}
a field. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
be integer multiples of the unity of
\mathbb{F}
. We will work with a generalization of modified companion matrix associated to q, namely

C_{q}+\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0),
over
\mathbb{F}.

The next Lemma is a generalization of a Lemma in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
k
and
n
be nonzero natural numbers,
n\geq k,
and
\mathbb{F}
be a field. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
be integer multiples of the unity. Every companion matrix
C_{q}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{F})
is similar to the modified companion matrix
C_{q^{\prime}}+\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0)
for some polynomial
q^{\prime}.

Proof.

First we will prove the statement for
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}.

Let
V
denote the
n-
dimensional vector space of columns over
\mathbb{F}
and consider
C_{q}
as an endomorphism
C_{q}:V\rightarrow V.
Denoting by

\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
the standard basis of
V,

C_{q}
maps each
e_{i}
to
e_{i+1}
, for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}.

Now we define
\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\},

f_{i}\in V,

i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},
inductively as it follows. First set
f_{1}=e_{1}
. Assuming that
2\leq i\leq n
and that
f_{i-1}
has been defined, set
f_{i}=C_{q}(f_{i-1})-a_{i-1}f_{i-1},
if
i\in\{2,\ldots,k+1\}
and
f_{i}=C_{q}(f_{i-1}),
if
i\in\{k+2,\ldots,n\}

We have
e_{1}=f_{1},
so
e_{1}\in\textrm{Lin}(\{f_{1}\})
and
f_{2}=C_{q}(f_{1})-a_{1}f_{1}=C_{q}(e_{1})-a_{1}f_{1}=e_{2}-a_{1}f_{1},
so
e_{2}=a_{1}f_{1}+f_{2}
and
e_{2}\in\textrm{Lin}(\{f_{1},f_{2}\})

It is easy to see that each
f_{i}
is the sum of
e_{i}
and a linear combination of
e_{i-1},e_{i-2},\ldots,e_{2},e_{1}
. Hence
e_{i}
is the difference of
f_{i}
and a linear combination of
e_{i-1},e_{i-2},\ldots,

e_{2},e_{1}
. Assuming
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{i-1}\in\textrm{Lin}(\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\}),
we get
e_{i}
is a linear
combination of
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}
. Therefore
\textrm{Lin}(\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\})=\textrm{Lin}(\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\})
and thus
\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\}
is a basis of
V.

Moreover by the definition we have:

C_{q}(f_{1})=a_{1}f_{1}+f_{2},

C_{q}(f_{2})=a_{2}f_{2}+f_{3},

\vdots

C_{q}(f_{k})=a_{k}f_{k}+f_{k+1},

C_{q}(f_{k+1})=f_{k+2},

\vdots

C_{q}(f_{n-1})=f_{n}.

Let
M
be the matrix the endomorphism
C_{q}
corresponds to, with respect to the basis
B=\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}\}
. Therefore

M=[[C_{q}(f_{1})]_{B},\ldots,[C_{q}(f_{k})]_{B},[C_{q}(f_{k+1})]_{B},\dots,[C_{q}(f_{n})]_{B}].

Hence

M=[[a_{1}f_{1}+f_{2}]_{B},\ldots,[a_{k}f_{k}+f_{k+1}]_{B},[f_{k+2}]_{B},\ldots,[f_{n}]_{B},[C_{q}(f_{n})]_{B}].

It follows that
M=\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0)+C_{q^{\prime}}
for some monic polynomial
q^{\prime}
of degree
n.

So
C_{q}=P(\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0)+C_{q^{\prime}})P^{-1},
where
P
is the transition matrix mapping each
e_{i}
to
f_{i}.

As next step we will solve the case
k=n,
that is we will prove that
C
is similar to
\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})+C^{\prime},
where
C^{\prime}
is a companion matrix. First, we know by the particular case of this Lemma (which is in
[
10
]
) that there exists a companion matrix such that
C\sim a_{n}I_{n}+C_{1}.
Then by the case
k<n,
we know that for
C_{1}
there exists a companion matrix
C^{\prime}
such that
C_{1}\sim\mathrm{diag}(a_{1}-a_{n},a_{2}-a_{n},\dots,a_{n-1}-a_{n},0)+C^{\prime}.
Hence
C
is similar to
\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n})+C^{\prime}.

∎

3.
Companion matrices as sums of
p
-potent and nilpotent matrices

The following Proposition has been proved for
\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
[
10
]
. The same proof is valid for any field with with positive odd characteristic
p.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.

Let
n\geq 2
and
k
be positive integers such that
k<n.
Let
a\in\{1,2,\dots,p-2\}.
Fix constants
d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{n-1}\in\mathbb{F}
and denote
D=C_{d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1}}+\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0).
For every polynomial
g\in\mathbb{F}[X]
of degree at most
n-2
there exist two matrices
E,

M
in
\mathbb{M}_{n}(\mathbb{F})
such that:

(1)

D=E+M

(2)

E^{p}=E
, and

(3)

\chi_{M}=X^{n}+(k\cdot 1+d_{n-1})X^{n-1}+g.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.
Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer. Fix constants
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1}\in\mathbb{F}.

Let
C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}
be a companion matrix and if there exist
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
and
a\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-2\}
such that
-c_{n-1}=k(a+1)
then
C
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent.

Proof.

Let
C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}
be a companion matrix. By Lemma
1
we have that there exist
d_{0},d_{1},\dots,d_{n-1}\in\mathbb{F}
,
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},
and
a\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-2\}
such that
C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}
is similar to
D=C_{d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n-1}}+\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},0,\dots,0)
. The traces of two similar matrices are equal and so
-c_{n-1}=-d_{n-1}+k\cdot a
. But
-c_{n-1}=k(a+1)
, and so
-d_{n-1}=k\cdot 1.
Now taking
g=0
in Proposition
2
we have that there exist a
p
-potent matrix
E
and a nilpotent matrix
N
, such that
D=E+N
. Therefore since
C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}
is similar to
D
and because a matrix similar to a
p
-potent is a
p
potent and a matrix similar to a nilpotent is a nilpotent, we have a decomposition as a sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent for
C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}.

∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.
Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer. Fix constants
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n-2},0\in\mathbb{F}.

Let
C=C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-2},0}
be a companion matrix. Then
C
is the sum of a
p
-potent matrix and a nilpotent matrix.

Proof.

•

n=1,
C=0=0+0 is a sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent.

•

n=2,
let

C=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-c_{0}\\
1&0\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-c_{0}-1\\
0&0\end{array}\right)

is the sum of a matrix with square the identity and a nilpotent, hence this is the sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent.

•

n=2k+1,
with
k\geq 1
integer; let
a\neq 0
be an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.
By Lemma
1
there exists a companion matrix
C^{\prime}_{c^{\prime}_{0},c^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{n-1}}
over
\mathbb{F}
such that
C\sim D
, where
D=\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},\underbrace{-a,\dots,-a}_{k\textrm{-times}},0)+C^{\prime}_{c^{\prime}_{0},c^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{n-1}}.

For
D
we have the following decomposition

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}aI_{k}&0&U\\
0&-aI_{k}&V\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right)+C"_{0,0,\dots,0},

where

U=\begin{bmatrix}-c^{\prime}_{0}\\
-c^{\prime}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
-c^{\prime}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}

and

V=\begin{bmatrix}-c^{\prime}_{k}\\
-c^{\prime}_{k+1}\\
\vdots\\
-c^{\prime}_{2k-1}\end{bmatrix}

The above decomposition for
D
is the sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent. Now since a matrix similar to a
p
-potent is a
p
-potent and a matrix similar to a nilpotent is a nilpotent, we find that there exists a decomposition as a sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent for
C,
which is similar to
D
.

•

n=2k+2,
with
k\geq 1
integer; let
a\neq 0
be an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.
By Lemma
1
there exists a companion matrix
C^{\prime}_{c^{\prime}_{0},c^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{n-1}}
over
\mathbb{F}
such that
C\sim D
, where
D=\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},\underbrace{-a,\dots,-a}_{k-1\textrm{-times}},-a-1,1,0)+C^{\prime}_{c^{\prime}_{0},c^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,c^{\prime}_{n-1}}.

For
D
we have the following decomposition

D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P&U\\
0&0\end{array}\right)+C"_{0,0,\dots,0},

where

P=\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{k\textrm{-times}},\underbrace{-a,\dots,-a}_{k-1\textrm{-times}},-a-1,1),

and

U=\begin{bmatrix}-c^{\prime}_{0}\\
-c^{\prime}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
-c^{\prime}_{2k}\end{bmatrix}

The above decomposition for
D
is the sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent. Now since a matrix similar to a
p
-potent is a
p
-potent and a matrix similar to a nilpotent is a nilpotent, we find that there exists a decomposition as a sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent for
C,
which is similar to
D
.

∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.
Let
n\geq 1
be a positive integer.
Let
C
be a companion matrix. Then
C
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent if and only if the trace of
C
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.

Proof.

Let
C=C_{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1}}
be a companion matrix.

Let n=1 and
-c_{0}\in\mathbb{F}
. If
-c_{0}
is an integer multiple of unity then there exist the
p
-potent
-c_{0}
and the nilpotent
0\in F_{p}
such that
-c_{0}=-c_{0}+0
. Conversely if
-c_{0}=e+n
,
e,n\in\mathbb{F}
,
e^{p}=e
and
n
is a nilpotent then
n=0
and
-c_{0}=e.
Therefore
-c_{0}
verifies the equation
X^{p}=X
which has the
p
solutions all
p
integer multiples of unity of
\mathbb{F}.
Therefore
-c_{0}
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.

Now let
n\geq 2.

Let
E
be
p
-potent and
N
be nilpotent such that
C=E+N,
then since the trace of a nilpotent is
0
then
\mbox{trace}(C)=\mbox{trace}(E),
that is
-c_{n-1}
is the sum of the eigenvalues of
E
. Let
m_{E}
be the minimal polynomial of
E
. Then
m_{E}
divides
X^{p}-X,
and therefore if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
E,
then
\lambda^{p}=\lambda.
Hence
\lambda
is an integer multiple of unity and the sum of the eigenvalues of
E
is an integer multiple of unity. Therefore
-c_{n-1}
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.

Conversely let
-c_{n-1}
be an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.

Let
-c_{n-1}=0
. Then by Lemma
4
we have that
C
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent.

Let
-c_{n-1}\neq 0,
then we choose
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\},

k\neq 0\cdot 1,
and
k\neq-c_{n-1}
, and
a=k^{-1}(-c_{n-1})-1\in\{1,2,\ldots,p-2\},
then
-c_{n-1}=k(a+1)
. Hence by Corollary
3
we have that
C
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent.

In conclusion
C
is sum of a
p
-potent matrix and a nilpotent matrix.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.
Let
n\geq 1,
and
m\geq 2
be positive integers. Let
C
be a companion matrix. Then
C
is sum of
m

p
-potents and a nilpotent if and only if the trace of
C
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.

Proof.

Let
C
be a companion matrix which has a decomposition
C=E_{1}+\dots+E_{m}+N,
with all
E_{i}
being
p
-potents, and
N
being nilpotent.
It follows that

{\mathrm{Tr}}(C)={\mathrm{Tr}}(E_{1})+\dots+{\mathrm{Tr}}(E_{m})

Every eigenvalue of every
E_{i},
verifies the equation,
X^{p}=X,
therefore is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}
, and so are the traces of all
E_{i},
and
{\mathrm{Tr}}(C).

Assume now that the trace of the companion matrix
C
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}.
Fix integer multiples of unity of
\mathbb{F}
,
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}.
By Lemma
1
we have that there exists a companion
C^{\prime}
such that

C\sim C^{\prime}+\mathrm{diag}(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},0,\dots,0).

Then the trace of
C^{\prime}
is an integer multiple of unity of
\mathbb{F}
, and therefore, by Theorem
5
,
C^{\prime}
is sum of a
p
-potent and of a nilpotent.

Now since a matrix similar to a
p
-potent is
p
-potent, and a matrix similar to a nilpotent is nilpotent, we derive that
C
is sum of two
p
-potents and a nilpotent. Using the lemma we used before and the induction hypothesis that a companion matrix with trace an integer multiple of unity is sum of
m\geq 2,

p
-potents and a nilpotent, we derive that
C
is sum of
m+1

p
-potents and a nilpotent, thus proving
C
is sum of
m,

p
-potents and a nilpotent, for every
m\geq 2.

∎

By Theorems
5
and
6
we derive the following result:

Corollary 7
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with positive odd characteristic
p.
Let
n\geq 1,
and
m\geq 2
be positive integers.
Let
C
be a companion matrix. Then
C
is sum of
m

p
-potents and a nilpotent if and only if
C
is sum of a
p
-potent and a nilpotent.

Remark 8
.

By Remark
9
from
[
4
]
it is known that if all companion matrices which appear in the Frobenius normal form of a matrix
A
are sums of idempotent and nilpotent matrices, then
A
is sum of an idempotent and a nilpotent, and it is not known if the converse is true. Comparatively, the situation in case of decompositions as matrices over a field of characteristic
p>2
as sums of
p
-potent and nilpotent matrices is in the following way: it is not hard to see that if all companion matrices which appear in the Frobenius normal form of a matrix
A
are sums of
p
-potent and nilpotent matrices, then
A
is sum of a
p
-potent matrix and a nilpotent matrix. The converse is true in case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p},
since all companion matrices over this field are sums of
p
-potent and nilpotent matrices. It would be nice to know if the converse is true for any other field.

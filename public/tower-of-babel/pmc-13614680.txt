ExoMount: Improving reliability of proptosis measurement with a slit-lamp-mounted system
Authors: K Aishwarya, Athul S Puthalath, Usha Kim
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Accurate and reproducible exophthalmometry is essential for diagnosing and monitoring orbital disorders, including thyroid eye disease, orbital tumors, and trauma-related proptosis. Conventional handheld exophthalmometers are susceptible to variability arising from parallax error, inconsistent illumination, examiner-dependent alignment, and patient movement. These factors can lead to poor inter-observer reproducibility, especially in high-volume clinical and training environments.[1]

To address these challenges, ExoMount was developed as a novel slit-lamp-mounted attachment designed to stabilize a Hertel exophthalmometer and standardize measurement conditions. ExoMount is a custom-designed mounting system produced using computer-aided design and three-dimensional printing [Fig. 1a]. The system consists of a mounting bracket for the Hertel exophthalmometer, a connecting arm, and a base that attaches to the slit-lamp calibration slot, similar to an applanation tonometer mount [Fig. 1b and c]. By utilizing the slit lamp’s built-in illumination, the system eliminates the need for an external light source and enables a stable, hands-free examination [Fig. 2].

During use, the patient is positioned on the slit-lamp chin rest with head stabilization, ensuring alignment in the same coronal plane. The mounted exophthalmometer is then adjusted precisely using the slit-lamp joystick, which minimizes parallax and examiner-dependent variability. This setup also facilitates image or video documentation through slit-lamp imaging systems for longitudinal comparison [Video 1].

In a pilot clinical assessment, ExoMount-assisted measurements were more consistent and reproducible than those obtained with conventional handheld exophthalmometry. Variations related to hand tremor, patient positioning, and illumination were noticeably reduced.[2] The hands-free configuration enabled the examiner to focus solely on obtaining accurate, consistent readings. The device proved particularly beneficial for children, patients with Parkinsonism or head titubation, and those requiring serial follow-up, where maintaining consistent positioning is challenging.[3] Fellows and residents reported that the system was easier to use, suggesting its potential value as a teaching and standardization tool.

ExoMount provides several practical advantages. Integration with existing slit lamps ensures consistent lighting and alignment, while the detachable design permits rapid mounting and removal during routine clinical workflow. By reducing examiner-dependent variability, the device enhances measurement consistency across different observers and visits. This standardization is particularly important for long-term monitoring of thyroid eye disease and other orbital pathologies.

The device has certain limitations; it may be less suitable for patients with significant lateral orbital rim deformities, severe periorbital edema, or strabismus, in whom conventional exophthalmometry is also challenging. Larger validation studies comparing ExoMount-assisted measurements with digital and standard techniques are warranted.

In conclusion, ExoMount represents a simple and cost-effective innovation that enhances the accuracy, reproducibility, and ease of exophthalmometry by integrating the measurement process with a slit-lamp platform. This approach may improve consistency in routine clinical practice and training settings.

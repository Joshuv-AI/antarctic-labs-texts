Hint2 deficiency aggravates mitochondria-associated ferroptosis in lung ischemia-reperfusion injury
Authors: Yan Liu, Zhenghaolan Zou, Ke Yi, Xiangchao Ding, Wenyang Jiang, Jianjian Yang, Tianrui Chen, Ning Li, Huiqing Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryMitochondria-associated ferroptosis is critical in lung ischemia-reperfusion (LI/R) injury. This study examined the role of mitochondrial protein Hint2 in LI/R using mouse models and alveolar epithelial cells. We found that LI/R and hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) reduced Hint2 expression. AT2-specific Hint2 knockout exacerbated lung injury, inflammation, and ferroptosis, while promoting mitochondrial fission. Conversely, Hint2 overexpression alleviated H/R-induced oxidative damage and ferroptosis, shifting mitochondria toward fusion by inhibiting DRP1 phosphorylation. Hint2 overexpression enhanced its interaction with DRP1, which H/R disrupted. The Hint2 agonist calcitriol suppressed mitochondria-associated ferroptosis. These findings reveal that upregulating Hint2 protects against LI/R injury, offering a potential therapeutic strategy.

Introduction

Lung transplantation has become a vital treatment for thousands of patients with end-stage lung disease. Despite significant advancements in organ preservation, surgical techniques, and the management of transplant rejection in recent years, primary graft dysfunction (PGD) remains a persistent challenge that affects post-lung transplantation morbidity and mortality.1 Lung ischemia-reperfusion (LI/R) injury is a severe complication following lung transplantation, leading to PGD within 72 h in one-third of all lung transplant surgeries. LI/R injury followed by PGD has greatly limited the utilization of “marginalˮ donor lungs.2,3 Herein, the alleviation of LI/R injury is key to the success of lung transplantation.

Recent studies have highlighted the pivotal role of regulated cell death (RCD) in LI/R injury, with cell death serving as a consistent pathological marker.4 The concept of targeting RCD to mitigate LI/R injury has emerged as a therapeutic strategy. Ferroptosis, a specific type of RCD marked by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, glutathione (GSH) depletion, as well as the inactivation of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), has garnered significant interest in the context of LI/R injury.5 As the primary sites for cellular bioenergetics, mitochondria are crucial in generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) in mammalian cells, a function that is essential for regulating ferroptosis.6 The irreversible damage to mitochondrial morphology and function is intimately linked to ferroptosis, which subsequently triggers this form of RCD.7 However, the regulatory mechanism of mitochondria-associated ferroptosis in LI/R injury is still undefined.

Histidine triad nucleotide-binding 2 (Hint2), one of the members in the histidine triad protein superfamily, is a dimeric protein primarily expressed in the liver, pancreas, and adrenal gland, with its localization in the mitochondrion.8 Hint2 exhibited strong hydrolase activity against nucleotidyl substrates and binds to various nucleosides.9 In addition, Hint2 was closely associated with mitochondrial metabolism and homeostasis, participating in the development of various diseases including hepatic steatosis,10 cancer,11 and cardiovascular diseases.12 For instance, Hint2 could reduce pressure overload-induced myocyte hypertrophy and cardiac dysfunction by affecting the activity and assembly of mitochondrial complex I.13 Also, the overexpression of HINT2 attenuated cardiac microvascular I/R injury by inhibiting the mitochondrial calcium uniporter complex, preventing mitochondrial calcium overload, reducing mitochondrial fission, and blocking the apoptotic pathway.12

In the present study, we investigated the role and mechanism of Hint2 by constructing an LI/R injury model and a hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) model in vitro and in vivo. We demonstrated that the expression of Hint2 was significantly inhibited in I/R-induced lung tissues and H/R-induced AT2 cells. Hint2 deficiency could further aggravate lung pathological injury and inflammation by inducing mitochondria-associated ferroptosis. More importantly, Hint2 could stably bind to DRP1 to block its phosphorylation, which further inhibited mitochondrial fission and ferroptosis. Calcitriol treatment significantly relieved H/R-induced oxidative damage and ferroptosis in AT2 cells.

Results

The expression of Hint2 in AT2 cells was inhibited during LI/R injury

We initiated our study by detecting alterations in Hint2 expression levels in lung tissues challenged with LI/R to investigate its potential role. To begin with, we analyzed differentially expressed genes between normal lung tissues and I/R-induced lung tissues in a public dataset (GSE280061) (Figure 1A). In the volcano plot based on GSE280061, Hint2 was the top 10 downregulated genes. Male C57BL/6 J mice were exposed to either the hilar clamp procedure or a sham procedure in order to examine the changes in Hint2 expression levels in lung tissues during LI/R (Figure 1B). The results showed that the protein and mRNA levels of Hint2 were significantly decreased in lung tissues challenged with LI/R (Figures 1B–1D). To clarify, under the stimulation of LI/R, what types of cells in the lung have changed expression of Hint2. We next detected the expression level of Hint2 in different types of cells. Cells were initially incubated in a hypoxic environment composed of 5% CO2 and 95% N2 for 2 h, and subsequently transferred to a conventional incubator for an additional 4 h to establish the H/R models. As shown in Figures 1E–1H, the stimulation of H/R significantly decreased the protein and mRNA levels of Hinnt2 in primary AT2 cells. In addition, we also performed immunofluorescence co-staining of Hint2 with AT2 markers in LIR-injured lung sections to confirm cell-specific downregulation. As shown in Figure S1, the level of green fluorescence in the lung tissue of the LI/R group mice was significantly lower than that of the Sham group mice. Meanwhile, we also observed a significant overlap between red fluorescence and green fluorescence, indicating that the Hint2 protein is mainly expressed in mouse AT2 cells. By contrast, in macrophages and lung fibroblasts, the stimulation of H/R did not affect the mRNA and protein expression of Hint2 (Figures 1I–1P). In addition, the expression of Hint2 and GPX4 in lung tissues from patients with PGD and without PGD were also detected. As shown in Figures S2A–S2E, the expression levels of Hint2 and GPX4 in lung tissues from patients with PGD were significantly lower than those in patients without PGD. Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells significantly aggravated lung injury and dysfunction in mice challenged with LI/R

To further figure out the role of Hint2 during LI/R injury, we next generated AT2 cells specific Hint2-deficient mice by CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technology. Western blot proved the Hint2 was null in AT2 cells in lung tissues from Hint-CKO mice (Figures 2A–2D). H&E staining showed that LI/R stimulation could significant pulmonary edema, inflammation, and alveolar wall thickness in Hint-C mice. And Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells further aggravated lung wet/dry ratio and lung pathological injury during LI/R (Figures 2E–2G). Quantitative real-time PCR also demonstrated that Hint deficiency in AT2 cells did not affect the mRNA expression including Il-1β, Tnf-α, Il-6, and Mcp-1 at baseline. However, Hint2 deficiency further aggravated the inflammation in lung tissues from mice challenged with LI/R, evidenced by elevated mRNA levels of Il-1β, Tnf-α, Il-6, and Mcp-1 (Figures 2H–2K). Arterial blood gas analysis also showed Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells could further decease PaO2, and increase PaCO2 as well as HCO3- in mice challenged with LI/R (Figures 2L–2N). Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells further enhanced ferroptosis during LI/R injury

Next, we detected ferroptosis in different groups. We found that the ROS level in Hint2-CKO mice were significantly higher than that in Hint2-C mice after LI/R injury, evidenced elevated DCF-DA intensity (Figures 3A and 3B). We also detected iron level in lung tissues. As shown in Figure 3C, Hint2 deficiency in AT2 did not affect iron level in lung tissues at baseline. In the context of LI/R, Hint2 deficiency could further trigger the increase of iron accumulation in lung tissues. In addition, we measured the MDA content and the GSH level in lung tissues. The results also showed that Hint2 deficiency resulted in higher MDA content and lower GSH level in lung tissues from mice challenged with LI/R (Figures 3D and 3E). At last, the protein levels of GPX4 and PTGS2 were also investigated. As shown in Figures 3F–3J, compared with Hint2-C mice, Hint2-CKO mice possessed lower protein and mRNA levels of GPX4 and higher protein and mRNA levels of PTGS2 in lung tissues after LI/R, suggesting that Hint2 deficiency could further enhance ferroptosis during LI/R injury. We also used a ferroptosis inhibitor, ferrostatin-1, to observe its effect on ferroptosis markers in Hint2-knockdown lung AT2 cells. Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells disturbed mitochondrial dynamics in mice challenged with LI/R

Mitochondria-associated ferroptosis is closed associated with LI/R injury. Therefore, we next detected the alteration of mitochondrial dynamics in lung tissues subjected to I/R. Western blot also unveiled that LI/R could promote mitochondria fission and block fusion, evidenced by deceased protein level of OPA1, and increased protein levels of Fis1 and Mfn1 (Figures 4A–4D). As expected, Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells significantly shifted the dynamic course of mitochondria from fission to fusion. Drp1 could initiate mitochondrial fission through a four-step process involving its trafficking to the outer membrane of mitochondria, GTP-dependent assembly into constricting complexes, organelle division into unequally polarized daughters, with depolarized, damaged mitochondria being targeted for mitophagy. Here, we detected the phosphorylation of Drp1. The results showed that Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells could further promote the phosphorylation of Drp1 induced by LI/R (Figures 4E and 4F). Hint2 overexpression in AT2 cells could relieve mitochondria-associated ferroptosis by promoting its interaction with DRP1 in vitro

Subsequently, we overexpressed Hint2 in AT2 cells via an adenovirus transfection system. Western blot (Figure 5A) showed Hint2 was significantly upregulated in AT2 cells. We found that Hint2 overexpression did not affect cell viability in AT2 cells at baseline. And H/R could obviously decrease cell viability in AT2 cells, which was reversed after Hint2 was upregulated (Figure 5B). Also, we found that H/R could induce ROS generation, iron accumulation, as well as MDA production in AT2 cells. As expected, Hint2 overexpression could effectively decrease oxidative stress and iron accumulation in H/R-induced AT2 cells, evidenced by decreased levels of ROS, Fe2+, and MDA (Figures 5C–5E). Western blot also disclosed that Hint2 overexpression could offset the downregulation of GPX4 induced by H/R in AT2 cells (Figures 5F and 5G). Mitochondrial number and size were determined by transmission electron microscopy. The result showed that H/R stimulation significantly promoted mitochondrial fission and vacuolation, evidenced by the increased mitochondrial number and decreased size (Figures 5H–5J). However, Hint2 overexpression significantly shifted the dynamic course of mitochondria from fission to fusion in H/R-induced AT2 cells, evidenced by decreased protein levels of Fis1 and Mfn1, and increased protein level of Opa1 (Figures 5K–5N). DRP1 and its phosphorylation were also detected via western blot. We found that Hint2 overexpression at baseline could decrease the phosphorylation of DRP1 (Figures 5O–5P). In the context of H/R stimulation, the phosphorylation of DRP1 was significantly enhanced, which was then reversed after Hint2 was overexpressed. Co-immunoprecipitation H/R could promote the dissociation between Hint2 and DRP1 (Figure 5Q) and Hint2 overexpression significantly increased its interaction with DRP1 (Figure 5R), suggesting that the interaction between Hint2 and DRP1 could block the phosphorylation of DRP1. In addition, we also transfected AT2 cells with DRP1 phosphomimetic (S616D) or phospho-null (S616A) mutants in Hint2-overexpression backgrounds (Figure S4A). And we found that in Hint2-overexpressed AT2 cells, DRP1 phosphomimetic (S616D) mutant significantly promoted ferroptosis and mitochondrial fission. By contrast, DRP1 phospho-null (S616A) mutant inhibited ferroptosis and promoted mitochondrial fusion (Figures S4B–S4D). The pharmacological activation of Hint2 by calcitriol could inhibit mitochondria-associated ferroptosis in H/R-induced AT2 cells

Finally, we used calcitriol (Figure 6A), one Hint2 agonist, to further identify the role of Hint2 during LI/R injury. To investigate if calcitriol can directly modulate Hint2, we employed molecular docking simulations to model the interaction between calcitriol and Hint2. The outcomes indicated that calcitriol forms hydrogen bonds and pi bonds with Hint2 (Figures 6B and 6C). Western blot showed that calcitriol could upregulate the protein level of Hint2 in a concentration-dependent manner (Figure 6D). Additionally, we also found that calcitriol (0-100 nmol/L) did not affect cell viability in AT2 cells (Figure 6E). In the context of H/R stimulation, calcitriol significantly improved cell viability in AT2 cells in a concentration-dependent manner (Figure 6F). Therefore, we selected 100 nmol/L of calcitriol as therapeutic dose. As expected, calcitriol pretreatment significantly blocked the increase of ROS and MDA in H/R-induced AT2 cells (Figures 6G and 6H). Also, calcitriol pretreatment could upregulate the protein level of GPX4 and downregulate the protein level of PTGS2 in H/R-induced AT2 cells (Figures 6I–6K). Western blot further unveiled that calcitriol pretreatment decreased the protein levels of Fis1, Mfn1 and p-DRP1, and increased the protein level of Opa1 in H/R-induced AT2 cells (Figures 6L–6P), indicating that calcitriol could suppress H/R-induced mitochondrial fission. In addition, we knocked down Hint2 in AT2 cells and observed its effects on H/R-induced AT2 cells treated with calcitriol. Discussion

The present study aimed to assess the involvement of the Hint2 in AT2 cells in I/R-induced ferroptosis and its potential protective function against LI/R injury. Our results show that Hint2 deficiency in AT2 cells significantly aggravated LI/R-induced the production of ROS, iron accumulation, and promoted ferroptosis. Hint2 deficiency further promoted mitochondrial fission in H/R-induced AT2 cells. And Hint2 overexpression in AT2 cells could not only inhibit ferroptosis, but also shifted the dynamic course of mitochondria from fission to fusion in H/R-induced AT2 cells. Notably, H/R stimulation could promote the phosphorylation of DRP1 by inhibiting the interaction between Hint2 and DRP1. Finally, we also found that the pharmacological activation of Hint2 by calcitriol could significantly relieve mitochondria-associated ferroptosis in H/R-induced AT2 cells.

Initially, our in vivo and in vitro experiments revealed a protective function of Hint2 in AT2 cells against LI/R injury. Concurrently, a growing body of evidence has demonstrated Hint2 possessed protective capabilities against I/R-induced myocardial injury. For instance, during I/R-induced cardiac microvascular injury, Hint2 overexpression prevented calcium overload-induced mitochondrial fission, thereby preserving mitochondrial function and inhibiting the mitochondria-mediated apoptotic pathway.12 In myocardial infarction mouse model, Hint2 expression showed an inverse relationship with MDA and nitric oxide levels, while it demonstrated a positive correlation with superoxide dismutase and GSH levels. And compared to wild-type myocardial infarction mice, Hint2-deficient myocardial infarction mice exhibited increased left ventricular end-diastolic diameter and end-systolic diameter, accompanied by decreased left ventricular ejection fraction and fractional shortening.14 In line with these findings, the present study illustrated a crucial role of Hint2 in AT2 cells regarding LI/R injury, given that Hint2 deficiency notably worsened lung pathological damage and functional decline.

There are accumulating data showing that mitochondria-associated ferroptosis plays a crucial role in I/R-induced organ injury.7 Mitochondria, double-membrane organelles, play essential roles in energy production, cellular metabolism, and the regulation of cell death.15 Given the pivotal role of mitochondria in regulating cell death and the close connection between ferroptosis and cellular metabolism, it has been hypothesized since the identification of ferroptosis that mitochondria could also be involved in modulating this process. Indeed, the most striking morphological characteristic of ferroptotic cells observed under electron microscopy is the alteration in mitochondrial morphology, where these cells commonly exhibit shrunken mitochondria accompanied by heightened membrane density.16,17 Here, we observed that I/R stimulation could significantly trigger the loss of mitochondrial cristae and promote mitochondrial fission, which destroyed their functional and structural integrity. Mitochondrial fission serves as a vital mechanism for cellular self-regulation and is pivotal in preserving mitochondrial function, intersecting with the process of ferroptosis.18 Liu et al.19 reported that ferroptosis is implicated in cisplatin-induced intestinal injury, characterized by increased protein and mRNA expression levels of mitochondrial fission-related proteins DRP1 and Fis1 in the injured intestines. DRP1 is recognized as the principal pro-fission protein, with its activity being meticulously controlled to facilitate the removal of damaged mitochondria through mitophagy, thereby maintaining a stringent oversight on the complex processes of cellular and organelle dynamics.20 Numerous posttranslational modifications of DRP1 have been characterized, including phosphorylation, SUMOylation, palmitoylation, ubiquitination, S-nitrosylation, and O-GlcNAcylation, all of which suggest a role in modulating mitochondrial dynamics.18 Phosphorylation stands out as one of the most extensively studied posttranslational modifications of DRP1, capable of eliciting either activating or inhibitory effects contingent upon the specific site undergoing modification.21 Numerous phosphorylation sites have been delineated, of which Ser-616 and Ser-637, have garnered significant attention and extensive reporting.22 Phosphorylation at the Ser-616 site serves as an activating mechanism, promoting the localization of DRP1 to the outer mitochondrial membrane and thereby facilitating subsequent mitochondrial fission.23 In the present study, we found LI/R and H/R could significantly promoted the phosphorylation of DRP1 in AT2 cells, thus promoting mitochondrial fission. And Hint2 deficiency could further promote the phosphorylation of DRP1 while Hint2 overexpression suppressed its phosphorylation. Protein-protein interaction assay also unveiled that H/R stimulation blocked the interaction between DRP1 and Hint2. Therefore, we speculated that the interaction between DRP1 and Hint2 may block the phosphorylation of DRP1, which suggested that Hint2 may inhibit ferroptosis by blocking the initiation of DRP1-mediated mitochondrial fission. As for whether the phosphorylation sites on DRP1 are competitively bound by the Hint2 protein, or whether they are blocked by the Hint2 protein and thereby prevent the phosphorylation of DRP1, both possibilities exist.

Calcitriol, derived from vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol), is synthesized in the skin through sunlight’s action on the precursor molecule, 7-dehydrocholesterol, and can also be obtained from dietary sources and supplements.24 Previous studies have disclosed that calcitriol exerted various therapeutical effects in osteoporosis, hypoparathyroidism, and chronic kidney disease.25 One recent study also reported that calcitriol treatment could upregulate the protein expression of Hint2 in tumor cells.26 Here, we found that calcitriol forms hydrogen bonds and pi bonds with Hint2 via employed molecular docking simulations. In AT2 cells, calcitriol could upregulate the protein level of Hint2, suggesting that the direct interaction between calcitriol and Hint2 protein may block the degradation of Hint2. And in vitro experiments also showed that calcitriol could blocked mitochondria-associated ferroptosis by activating Hint2 in H/R-induced AT2 cells. Hence, calcitriol is expected to exert protective effects during LI/R injury. However, this study lacks in vivo experiments with calcitriol to further verify that its protective effect is related to Hint2. Therefore, future animal studies and even human trials are expected to promote the clinical translation of calcitriol.

In conclusion, Hint2 may mitigate mitochondria-associated ferroptosis in alveolar epithelial cells during LI/R injury, which is associated with its interaction with DRP1. Therefore, Hint2 overexpression or exogenous Hint2 activator can be used as a potential candidate for treating LI/R injury. The inhibition of ferroptosis by Hint2-dependent mitochondrial dynamics may be the molecular target on LI/R injury to screen therapeutic drugs.

Limitations of the study

The study mainly explored the role of Hint2 may mitigate mitochondria-associated ferroptosis in alveolar epithelial cells during LI/R injury through interacting with DRP1. The safety of calcitriol was not examined during our investigation of its pharmacological efficacy. In addition, it should also be further investigated whether overexpression or knockout affects lung development, as well as the development of other organs. Errors that may arise in this study are attributable to a dual origin—namely, the stochastic characteristics of the samples and experimental animals, alongside the subjective biases of the personnel conducting the experiments.

Experimental model and study participant details

Study participants

PGD was defined as the emergence of pulmonary infiltrates in the transplanted lung(s) that were consistent with non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema within 72 h post-transplantation. The grading of PGD was based on the severity of hypoxemia, which was assessed using the ratio of partial pressure of arterial oxygen to fraction of inspired oxygen. At each clinical site, an adjudication committee member independently reviewed the chest radiographs of each patient at 72 h post-transplant to establish the radiographic criteria for PGD. These adjudication committees consisted of thoracic surgeons, transplant pulmonologists, and thoracic radiologists. 5 male patients aged 61.12±5.13 with PGD undergoing lung transplantation paired 5 male early-stage lung cancer patients aged 64.00±8.14 undergoing lobectomy were enrolled in this study. Having been fully informed of the study’s purpose and procedures, all participants voluntarily signed written informed consent forms before enrollment. The collection of clinical specimens adhered to the ethical standards set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of Wuhan University (Approval Number: WDRY2020-K112).

Animals and treatment

The welfare and ethical considerations of all animal care and experimental procedures adhered to the guidelines detailed in the Animal Experimental Welfare and Ethical Inspection Form issued by the Institute of Animal Science at Renmin Hospital, Wuhan University, under approval number WDRM-20210508. Male C57BL/6 J mice, aged 8 weeks and weighing between 23.2 and 25.5 g, were sourced from the Institute of Laboratory Animal Science affiliated with the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences in Beijing, China. These mice underwent a one-week acclimatization period prior to the commencement of experiments. Hint2 conditionally knockout (floxed) mouse models were developed employing the CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technology at the Shanghai Model Organisms Center, Inc. To create AT2 cells specific Hint2-deficient mice, Hint2flox/flox mice were bred with tamoxifen-inducible Sftpc-CreERT2 mice, a generous gift from GemPharmatech Co. Ltd, based in Jiangsu, China. This breeding strategy resulted in Sftpc-CreERT2+-Hint2flox/flox mice, designated as Hint2 conditional knockout (CKO), while their littermate counterparts, Sftpc-CreERT2--Hint2flox/flox, served as control animals (Hint2-C). The experimental LI/R injury model was detailed in previous study.27 Throughout the study, the mice were maintained in a meticulously controlled environment characterized by a 12-h light-dark cycle, an ambient temperature of 20°C, relative humidity levels ranging from 50 to 55%, and minimal auditory disturbances. All animals were provided unrestricted access to food and water and were subjected to daily health monitoring to ensure their wellbeing.

Cell culture and treatment

Primary AT2 cells were isolated following a modified version of established protocols.28,29 Initially, the mice were euthanized and their lungs perfused. The trachea was then cannulated and instilled with Dispase II solution (50 U/mL; Roche Applied Science, Germany) to facilitate cell release. Post-digestion, single-cell suspensions were first treated with red blood cell lysis buffer (Sigma, ON, Canada) and subsequently maintained in 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS) in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for all subsequent steps. To purify epithelial cells, freshly isolated cells were incubated with a cocktail of antibodies (anti-CD45, anti-CD31, anti-EpCAM) at 4°C for approximately 20 min. Cell viability was assessed through propidium iodide (1 μg/mL) staining. Epithelial cells were then sorted using a MoFlo BRU cell sorter (Becton Dickinson), isolating EpCAM+, CD31−, CD45− populations. Each isolation batch underwent a purity verification step to confirm the success of the sorting process. Primary macrophages were isolated by lavage of the peritoneal cavity with Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) medium devoid of serum, from mice that had received an intraperitoneal injection of DMEM three days prior, following a methodology described in a previous study. Subsequently, the harvested primary macrophages were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum for further experimentation.30

Primary human lung (myo)fibroblasts were isolated and characterized, following the procedures outlined in previous study.31 Human lung (myo)fibroblasts were derived from fresh lung tissues obtained from patients undergoing lung transplantation for IPF or from failed donor organs. These lung fibroblasts were cultivated in DMEM enriched with 10% FBS and employed between passages 3 and 5 for experimental purposes. To mimic an in vitro LI/R injury model, all cells were initially incubated in a hypoxic environment composed of 5% CO2 and 95% N2 for 2 h, and subsequently transferred to a conventional incubator for an additional 4 h to establish the hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) cell models. The AT2 cells were treated with Ferrostatin-1 (2 μM) for 16 h before induced by H/R. Thereafter, the cells were collected for further analysis. To achieve Hint2 overexpression, AT2 cells were infected with Ad-Hint2 at an MOI of 50 or with a control adenovirus lacking any overexpression cassette (Ad-Ctrl) for a duration of 12 h. To investigate the pharmacological activation of Hint2, Calcitriol (100 nmol/L) pretreatment for 6 h was employed to activate Hint2 in AT2 cells before H/R.

Method details

H&E staining

To evaluate the extent of lung pathological injury, Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining was conducted. The process began by fixing the left lung tissues in neutral buffered formalin (10%), followed by their embedding in paraffin. Subsequently, the embedded lung tissue blocks were sectioned transversely at 3 mm and subjected to H&E staining to facilitate the observation of neutrophil infiltration and pulmonary edema. A semi-quantitative assessment system was utilized to score the lung injury based on the severity of these two parameters. Each criterion was graded on a scale from 0 to 4+, indicating minimal to maximal damage, respectively. Specifically, the scoring was as follows: 0 denoted minimal damage, 1 to >2 indicated mild damage, 2 to >3 signified moderate damage, 3 to >4 represented severe damage, and 4+ was designated for maximal damage. The overall lung injury score was calculated by adding the scores of neutrophil infiltration and pulmonary edema, aligning with methodologies described in previous studies.32

Lung wet/dry ratio

Forty-eight hours following the resuscitation of the LI/R injury model, the right lung tissue was weighed to obtain its wet weight. This was followed by drying the tissue at 110 °C for a duration of 24 h. Subsequently, the dried tissue was weighed again to determine its dry weight. The ratio of the lung’s wet weight to its dry weight served as an indicator of pulmonary edema.

Arterial blood gas analysis

Following the reperfusion procedure, we extracted 0.4 mL of arterial blood from the left ventricle using a heparinized 1 mL empty needle. The blood sample was then immediately analyzed for PaO2, PaCO2, and HCO3- using a blood gas analyzer (Mindray, Shenzhen, China).

Western blot and quantitative real-time PCR

Protein extraction and western blot analysis were conducted following previous protocols.33 In brief, total proteins were extracted from mouse lung tissues or cells using RIPA lysis buffer and quantified with a BCA protein assay kit. Equivalent amounts of total protein were then separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. After blocking with 5% skimmed milk at room temperature for 1 h, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, the membranes were probed with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for one additional hour the next day. Protein bands were visualized using a ChemiDoc XRS + system and analyzed with Image Lab software (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc.). β-actin in cell lysates serves as an internal control.

Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent, followed by reverse transcription to cDNA utilizing the Maxima First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Gene expression levels were quantified using the Roche LightCycler 480 system with SYBR Green 1 Master Mix (Roche), employing β-actin as an internal control. The primer sets used are detailed in Table S1.

The determination of oxidative stress

The relative concentration of MDA in lung tissues or AT2 cells was measured using a lipid peroxidation assay kit. The MDA-thiobarbituric acid (TBA) admixtures were formed through the reaction between MDA and TBA in the samples, and their formation was quantified using the colorimetric method at an OD532 nm. The levels of glutathione (GSH) in lung tissues were quantified using an ELISA kit (Boster, China) following the protocol provided by the manufacturer.

For intracellular ROS staining, cells were seeded onto glass-bottomed 24-well plates or lung tissues were incubated with dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCF-DA) at a concentration of 10 μM for 30 min at 37 °C. After that, fluorescence intensity was imaged using an Olympus laser scanning confocal microscope.

Transmission electron microscopy

Fresh lung tissue samples (1 × 1 × 1 mm) were fixed in a 0.1 M phosphate buffer solution (pH 7.(4) containing 2.5% glutaraldehyde for 4 h at room temperature in pre-cooled tubes immediately after excision from the mice. The tissues were then permeated, dehydrated, and embedded in epoxy resin, followed by sectioning at approximately 70 nm, as previously described.34 Ultrathin sections were subsequently stained with 3% uranyl acetate and lead citrate and examined under a transmission electron microscope (HITACHI, Japan) at an accelerating voltage of 80 kV. Images of lung epithelial cells containing nuclei and cytoplasm with mitochondria were captured. The number and size of mitochondria were analyzed using ImageJ software. The mitochondrial count was determined and normalized to the area of cytoplasm measured in each micrograph. Mitochondrial size was measured as the distance between the outer membranes at their furthest points. The average mitochondrial size for each micrograph was calculated.

Molecular docking

To evaluate the binding affinity between Calcitriol and the Hint2 protein, the structure of Calcitriol was acquired from the PubChem database and converted into a 3D structure. Its energy was minimized using AutoDock Vina software. The Hint2 protein structure was obtained from the Protein DataBank (PDB) database. With Pymol software, the protein structures were processed by removing residues and water molecules, and hydrogen atoms were added. Calcitriol was designated as the ligand and the Hin2 protein as the receptor for molecular docking, which was performed using AutoDock Vina software. The conformation with the lowest binding energy was selected as the optimal binding mode, and the results were visualized and analyzed using Pymol software.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Data analysis

The data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS20.0 software. The Shapiro-Wilk test was used to verify the normal distribution of all continuous variables, with p-values greater than 0.05 confirming normality. Non-normally distributed data were analyzed with the Mann–Whitney test. To evaluate differences among multiple groups, one-way or two-way ANOVA was applied, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. For pairwise comparisons, Student’s t test was employed. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

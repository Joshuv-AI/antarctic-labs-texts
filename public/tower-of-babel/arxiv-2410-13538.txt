Magnetic moments of 1 2 − \frac{1}{2}^{-} baryon resonances in strange hadronic matter at high densities
Abhinaba Upadhyay, Arvind Kumar, Harleen Dahiya, Suneel Dutt

Abstract
This work primarily focusses on determining the magnetic moments of 1 2 − \frac{1}{2}^{-} baryon resonances in the presence of hot and dense hadronic matter. In the chiral S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) quark mean field model approach, we have essentially accounted for the effects on in-medium scalar meson fields to investigate the impact of high densities on the in-medium baryon masses and their constituent quarks. In light of chiral constituent quark model χ \chi CQM, we have calculated the magnetic moments of 1 2 − \frac{1}{2}^{-} baryon resonances and scrutinized the effects due to its internal constituents: the valence quarks, sea quarks and the orbital moment of sea quarks. Furthermore, we have investigated the effective baryonic magnetic moments for the finite magnitudes of isospin asymmetry and strangeness fraction.

The formalism of chiral constituent quark model (
\chi\mathrm{CQM}
)
[

[

83

q^{\uparrow\downarrow}\rightarrow\mathrm{NGB}+q^{\prime\downarrow\uparrow}\rightarrow{\left(q\overline{q}^{\prime}\right)+q^{\prime\downarrow\uparrow}},

(30)

where the emitted Nambu-Goldstone boson (NGB) furthermore splits into a
q\overline{q}^{\prime}
pair, and
q\overline{q}^{\prime}+q^{\prime}
constitute the sea quarks
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
]
. In
\chi\mathrm{CQM}
model the massless confined quarks obtain its mass by virtue of spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking
[
26
,
84
]
. The coupling between the quark and NGBs is described by the effective interaction Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=g_{8}\overline{\mathbf{q}}\left(\Phi^{\prime}\right)\mathbf{q}=g_{8}\overline{\mathbf{q}}\left(\zeta^{\prime}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{3}}I+\Phi\right)\mathbf{q},

(31)

where
\zeta^{\prime}=g_{1}/g_{8},
where
g_{1}
and
g_{8}
denote the coupling constants for the singlet and octet NGBs respectively and
I
is an order 3 identity matrix.
This quark-NGB coupling represented in this Lagrangian is quite feeble and hence, the fluctuation in
eq.

30
is practically taken to be a minor perturbation. The spin-flip process is an outcome of the emission of NGB.

The NGB matrix is defined by

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\beta\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}+\zeta^{\prime}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{3}}&\pi^{+}&\alpha K^{+}\\
\pi^{-}&-\frac{\pi^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\beta\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{6}}+\zeta^{\prime}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{3}}&\alpha K^{0}\\
\alpha K^{-}&\alpha\bar{K}^{0}&-\beta\frac{2\eta}{\sqrt{6}}+\zeta^{\prime}\frac{\eta^{\prime}}{\sqrt{3}}\end{array}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle\text{ and }

\displaystyle q=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u\\
d\\
s\end{array}\right).

The
SU(3)
chiral symmetry breaking is introduced in
\chi\mathrm{CQM}
by treating the masses of NGBs as non-degenerate
and considering that
m_{s}>m_{u,d}

[
85
]
.
The parameter for the transition probability of chiral fluctuations:
u(d)\rightarrow

d(u)+\pi^{+(-)}
is denoted by
a(=\left|g_{8}\right|^{2})
,
u(d)\rightarrow s+K^{-(0)}
by
a\alpha^{2}
,
u(d,s)\rightarrow

u(d,s)+\eta

and

is represented by
a\zeta^{\prime 2}

[

,
34
,
35
,
36

]
.
By thoroughly analyzing the spin and flavor distribution functions of proton, the best fitted set of parameters crucial to our calculations are

The effective baryonic magnetic moment
\mu^{*}_{B}
is a summation of the contributions due to spin polarization
\mu^{*S}_{B}
and the angular momentum polarization
\mu^{*L}_{B}

[

\displaystyle\mu^{*}_{B}=\mu^{*S}_{B}+\mu^{*L}_{B}.

(33)

Following the Cheng Li mechanism
[
22
]
, the calculation of total baryonic magnetic moment involves the valence and sea quark contributions and the orbital angular momentum of sea quarks. The spin part in
eq.

33
, comprises of these individual contributions and is given by

\displaystyle\mu^{*S}_{B}=\mu^{*S}_{val}+\mu^{*S}_{sea}+\mu^{*S}_{orbit}.

(34)

The
\mu^{*S}_{val}

L=1
(
P-
wave) negative-parity resonances
[
45

For each baryon, the contribution of sea quark spin polarization in spin part of the total magnetic moment is obtained by substituting for each valence quark

q^{\uparrow(\downarrow)}\rightarrow-P_{[q,GB]q^{\uparrow(\downarrow)}}+\left|\psi\left(q^{\uparrow(\downarrow)}\right)\right|^{2}.

(35)

In the above expression, the emission probability of NGBs from a quark
q^{\uparrow(\downarrow)}
is
P_{[q,NGB]}
which can be fetched from ref.
[
86
]
,
and
\left|\psi\left(q^{\uparrow(\downarrow)}\right)\right|^{2}
represents the probability of transforming a
q^{\uparrow(\downarrow)}
quark

[

,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,

,
73
]
.
The orbital angular momentum contribution due to sea quarks, is given by
[

42
,

,

\mu^{*}\left(q^{\uparrow}\rightarrow q^{\prime\downarrow}\right)=\frac{e_{q}}{2m^{*}_{q}}\left\langle l_{q}\right\rangle+\frac{e_{q}-e_{q\prime}}{2M_{\mathrm{NGB}}}\left\langle l_{\mathrm{NGB}}\right\rangle,

(36)

where
\left<l_{q}\right>=\frac{M_{\mathrm{NGB}}}{M_{\mathrm{NGB}}+m^{*}_{q}}
and
\left<l_{\mathrm{NGB}}\right>=\frac{m^{*}_{q}}{M_{\mathrm{NGB}}+m^{*}_{q}}
. The quantities
\left(l_{q},m^{*}_{q}\right)
and

stands for the mass and orbital angular moment of constituent quarks and NGBs, respectively. The probability of each process to occur, allows us to compute the magnetic moment due to all possible transitions for a given valence quark.

The orbital moments of the constituent quarks can also be written using the
\chi\mathrm{CQM}
parameters
(a,\alpha,\beta,\zeta^{\prime})

\left(M_{\pi},M_{\eta\prime},M_{K},M_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle{\left[\mu^{*}\left(u_{\uparrow}\rightarrow\right)\right]=}

\displaystyle a\left[\frac{3}{2}\frac{m_{u}^{*2}}{M_{\pi}\left(m_{u}^{*}+M_{\pi}\right)}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\frac{\left(M_{K}^{2}-3m_{u}^{*2}\right)}{M_{K}\left(M_{K}+m_{u}^{*}\right)}\right.+\frac{\beta^{2}}{6}\frac{M_{\eta}}{6\left(M_{\eta}+m_{u}^{*}\right)}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\zeta^{\prime 2}}{3}\frac{M_{\eta^{\prime}}}{\left(m_{u}^{*}+M_{\eta^{\prime}}\right)}\right]\mu^{*}_{u},

\displaystyle{\left[\mu^{*}\left(d_{\uparrow}\rightarrow\right)\right]=}

\displaystyle-2a\left[\frac{3}{4}\frac{\left(M_{\pi}^{2}-2m_{d}^{*2}\right)}{M_{\pi}\left(m^{*}_{d}+M_{\pi}\right)}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\frac{M_{K}}{\left(m_{d}^{*}+M_{K}\right)}-\frac{\beta^{2}}{12}\frac{M_{\eta}}{\left(m_{d}^{*}+M_{\eta}\right)}\right.

(37)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{\zeta^{\prime 2}}{6}\frac{M_{\eta^{\prime}}}{\left(m_{d}^{*}+M_{\eta^{\prime}}\right)}\right]\mu^{*}_{d},

\displaystyle{\left[\mu^{*}\left(s_{\uparrow}\rightarrow\right)\right]=}

In the present study, we have included the effect of relativistic quark confinement and have calculated the mass adjusted in-medium magnetic moments of its constituent quarks by the expression
[

denotes the vacuum and in-medium mass of a given baryon, respectively.

4
Summary

In a nutshell, this work investigates the medium modified trends induced on the masses and moreover on the magnetic moments of
J_{p}={\frac{1}{2}}^{-}
baryon resonances in isospin asymmetric strange hadronic matter.
The effects caused due to the presence of a medium are introduced through the CQMF model, following which we computed the effective baryon masses by using the masses of constituent quarks. The mass adjusted quark magnetic moments are then calculated, to include relativistic quark confinement, by using the baryon masses as inputs. In hadronic medium the in-medium masses of lights quarks,
u
&
d
, vary significantly in comparison to the observed changes in medium modified masses of
s
quark. In the
\chi
CQM formalism, we then obtained the baryonic magnetic moments by the summation over the contributed values coming from valence quarks, sea quarks and orbital angular momentum due of sea quarks. As derived from the results is that, the medium modifications are more prominent for the baryons comprised of light
u
or
d
quarks, than for hyperons. For strange matter, the in-medium masses of constituent quark and baryons as well as the effective baryonic magnetic moments suffer appreciable enhancement in their in-medium behaviour. By carrying out this study at finite temperatures and finite
i_{a}
values of the hadronic medium, we have noted a fine shift in the in-medium baryonic magnetic moments, although the temperature effects have been negligible below
T=150
MeV.

The results from this study could be a point of reference to experiments which study hadron spectroscopy by probing photoproductions. In Jefferson Lab the GlueX experiment has measured the width of the N(1535) state to be narrower (less than 100 MeV) making it undetetctable, whereas its cascade program has aligned its goals to explore the negative parity excited
\Xi
baryons
[
96
]
. In the limelight of excited cascade resonances, a report from the Belle Collaboration sheds light on the detection of two states in low mass regime of which one ought to be
\Xi
(1690)
[
97
]
. Such experimental studies have research interests directed towards a detailed understanding of QCD for low-lying hadronic structures. Hence, the density dependent study of negative parity first excited states of octet baryon resonances is a crucial point of experimental interest at exploring the predictions of constituent quark models. Additionally, this work has scope for further extrapolation in the context of higher density astrophysical objects like neutron stars, to understand the impact of a dense medium on the baryonic magnetic moments
[
98
]
.

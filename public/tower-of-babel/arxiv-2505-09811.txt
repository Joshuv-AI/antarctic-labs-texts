Connection Between the Exact Moving Solutions of the Negative Korteweg-de Vries (nKdV) Equation and the Negative Modified Korteweg-de Vries (nmKdV) Equation and the Static Solutions of 1+1 Dimensional ϕ 4 \phi^{4} Field Theory
Avinash Khare, Fred Cooper, Avadh Saxena

Abstract
The negative order KdV (nKdV) and the modified KdV (nmKdV) equations have two different formulations based on different hierarchy operators. Both equations can be written in terms of a nonlinear differential equation for a field u ⁡ ( x , t ) u(x,t) which we call the “Lou form” of the equation. We find that for moving solutions of the nKdV equation and the nmKdV equation written in the “Lou form” with u ⁡ ( x , t ) → u ⁡ ( x − c ​ t ) = u ⁡ ( ξ ) u(x,t)\rightarrow u(x-ct)=u(\xi) , the equation for u ⁡ ( ξ ) u(\xi) can be mapped to the equation for the static solutions of the 1+1 dimensional ϕ 4 \phi^{4} field theory. Using this mapping we obtain a large number of solutions of the nKdV and the nmKdV equation, most of which are new. We also show that the nKdV equation can be derived from an Action Principle for both of its formulations. Furthermore, for both forms of the nmKdV equations as well as for both focusing and defocusing cases, we show that with a suitable ansatz one can decouple the x x and t t dependence of the nmKdV field u ⁡ ( x , t ) u(x,t) and obtain novel solutions in all the cases. We also obtain novel rational solutions of both the nKdV and the nmKdV equations.

I
Introduction

Soliton bearing nonlinear evolution equations arise in an increasing number of
physical systems including hydrodynamics, fluid mechanics, nonlinear optics,
classical and quantum field theory, etc. Most of these equations are of
positive order such as the usual KdV and the mKdV equations. However, in
recent years there has been a concerted effort in understanding the
corresponding negative order equations, particularly the integrable ones such
as the negative order KdV (denoted as nKdV) (see, for example
[
1
]
)
and the negative order mKdV (denoted as nmKdV) (see, for example
[
2
]
).
These equations have intriguing properties and soliton solutions that may be
amenable to experimental realization. For instance, the nKdV equations are
gauge-equivalent to the Camassa-Holm
[
3
,
4
]
equation by invoking reciprocal
transformations. In addition, they are closely related to the Ermakov-Pinney

[
5
]
system of equations as well as to the Kupershmidt deformation. Many
of the properties of the nKdV equation are discussed in the article of
Qiao and Fan
[
6
]
. The integrable hierarchy also plays an
important role in the theory of non-smooth solitons such as peakons (i.e.,
peaked solitons), e.g. in the Camassa-Holm equation and cuspons (i.e., cusped
solitons) found in the Degasperis-Procesi integrable equation
[
7
]
.
Moreover, it is related to the short pulse equation (SPE)
[
8
]
.

Connections between the nmKdV and SPE, Liouville equation and the sine-Gordon
equation have been explored in the literature
[
9
]
. Akin to the mKdV, breather
lattice solutions have been obtained for the nmKdV equation
[
10
]
. The negative order
equations can also describe physical phenomena pertinent to dislocation motion
in crystals and the propagation of ultrashort optical pulses. In two spatial
dimensions an integrable negative order generalization of the usual
Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) equation has also been proposed
[
11
]
and its
multi-soliton solutions have been obtained. The latter arises in shallow water
waves as well as in ion-acoustic waves in plasma physics.

As not above, nonlinear equations are playing increasingly important role in
several areas of science. Among these, integrable nonlinear equations play a
very special role, see for example
[
12
]
. After the remarkable paper of Olver

[
13
]
, it became clear that the recursion operator plays an important
role in the case of the integrable equations in
1+1
dimensions. The
existence of a recursion operator for any nonlinear evolution equation
guarantees the integrability of such a nonlinear equation. For the KdV
equation, Lenard
[
14
]
in 1967 discovered the hierarchy

v_{t}=K^{n}v\,,

(1)

where

K=-(D^{3}+2(Dv+vD))\,,~~~D=\partial_{x}.

(2)

Verosky
[
1
]
extended the work of Lenard in the negative direction
using negative powers in the recursion relation for the usual hierarchy.

In this approach the negative order KdV equation defined using the recursion
operator
K
is given by

Kv_{t}=v\,.

(3)

The hierarchy of equations can be written as

v_{t}=KG_{m-1}=DG_{m}\,.

(4)

Setting
G_{0}=1
and
G_{-1}=w
one obtains the nKdV equation:

v_{t}=w_{x},~~w_{xxx}+4vw_{x}+2v_{x}w=0.

(5)

Lou
[
2
]
introduced the transformation of variables

w=u^{2}\,,~~v=-\frac{u_{xx}}{u}\,,

(6)

which leads to the coupled system

v_{t}=2uu_{x},~~u_{xx}+vu=0.

(7)

This version of the nKdV equation as well as its decoupled version below we
will denote as nKdV-1 following
[
6
]
. Eqs. (
7
)
can be decoupled in two ways. One form is

uu_{xxt}-u_{xx}u_{t}+2u^{3}u_{x}=0\,.

(8)

Another way to decouple Eqs. (
7
) which we will call the Lou form of the
equation in what follows, is

\left(\frac{u_{xx}}{u}\right)_{t}+2uu_{x}=0.

(9)

Note that Eq. (
8
) can be derived from Eq. (
5
) under the
transformation

w=u^{2},~~~v=-\frac{u_{xx}.}{u}.

(10)

One can also write the KdV equation in a second way
[
2
]

v_{t}=\Phi v_{x},~~\Phi=KD^{-1},

(11)

where the recursion operator
\Phi
is given by (here we use the notation of Wazwaz
[
15
]
)

\Phi(v)=-(\partial_{x}^{2}+4v+2v_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1}).

(12)

Starting with this form of the KdV equation we now can define the negative order KdV equation (now called nKdV-2) by the relationship

\Phi v_{t}=-v_{x}\,.

(13)

Turning our attention to the modified KdV equation (mKdV equation), one has that
the mKdV equation appears in two forms. The focusing case is described by

u_{t}+6u^{2}u_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,

(14)

and the defocusing case by

u_{t}-6u^{2}u_{x}+u_{xxx}=0,

(15)

Both of these can be written in terms of the recursion operator

u_{t}=\Phi u_{x}\,.

(16)

The recursion operator for the focusing (defocusing) form is given in

[
16
,
17
]

\Phi=(-D^{2}\mp 4u^{2}\mp 4u_{x}D^{-1}(\cdot u))\,,

(17)

where the upper sign refers to the focusing case, and
D=\partial_{x}
.
One form of the mKdV hierarchy is given by

u_{t}=\Phi^{n}u_{x}.

(18)

In that approach, the negative order mKdV equation can be written as

\Phi u_{t}=u_{x}.

(19)

The recursion operator for the focusing (defocusing) form is given by
Eq. (
17
). Another form of the nmKdV equation is found from the Lax
pair formulation
[
18
]
(see below).
For the attractive (repulsive) case one obtains a Lou form:

\big(\frac{u_{xt}}{u}\big)_{x}\pm(2u^{2})_{t}=0\,,

(20)

which is equivalent to

u_{xt}\pm 2u\rho_{xt}+\alpha u=0\,,~~\rho_{xx}=u^{2}\,,

(21)

where
\alpha
is an arbitrary integration constant. The upper sign is for the
attractive case.

For moving solitary waves that depend only on
\xi=x-ct
, the equations for the
moving waves for both Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
20
) can be related to
the static solutions of the
1+1
dimensional
\phi^{4}

field theory. This will be one of the main new results of this paper.
Letting
u=u(x-ct)=u(\xi)
for moving solutions, the Lou equation for the
nKdV equation becomes

c(\frac{u_{\xi\xi}}{u})_{\xi}=-(u^{2})_{\xi}.

(22)

Integrating once we get

u_{\xi\xi}=\frac{1}{c}(u^{3}+\alpha^{\prime}u),

(23)

where
\alpha^{\prime}
is a constant. Since
c
and
\alpha^{\prime}
can have
either sign, one can let
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}/c
and rewrite the equation for
moving solutions as:

u_{\xi\xi}=\frac{1}{c}u^{3}+\alpha u.

(24)

Instead for the nmKdV equation, Eq. (
20
), we obtain for the focusing
(attractive) nmKdV-1

u_{\xi\xi}=-2u^{3}+\alpha u\,,

(25)

and for the defocusing (repulsive) case:

u_{\xi\xi}=2u^{3}+\alpha u\,,

(26)

where again
\alpha
is an integration constant. We see that
Eqs. (
24
) to (
26
) are related to the equations for the
static solutions of the
1+1
dimensional
\phi^{4}
field theory,

\phi_{xx}=a\phi+b\phi^{3}\,.

(27)

The main thrust of this paper is how to utilize known static solutions of
the
\phi^{4}
field theory to find novel traveling wave solutions for both the
nKdV-1 and the nmKdV-1 equations. While some of the traveling wave solutions
of nKdV-1 and nmKdV-1 are known before
[
6
,
19
]
, most of them
are not known in the literature so far. A secondary focus of this paper is to show
that the nKdV equation has a Lagrangian formulation. We also find novel solutions
of both the focusing and the defocusing nmKdV equation where the
x
and
t

dependence decouple.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Sec. II we discuss the properties of
the nKdV equation. We give an action formulation for the equation, and discuss
the infinite conservation laws and the symmetries of both forms of the equation.
In Sec. III we turn our attention to the properties of the nmKdV equation. We
also discuss the infinite number of conservation laws of the nmKdV equation.
In Sec. IV we derive the connection between the traveling wave solutions of
both the nKdV-1 and the nmKdV-1 (both focusing and defocusing type) and the
static solutions of the 1+1 dimensional
\phi^{4}
field theory (Eq. (
27
)).
Using this connection we obtain a large number of traveling
wave solutions of both these equations. We present only a subset of these
solutions in this section while the rest are presented in the Appendix.
In Sec. V we discuss the symmetries of the nKdV equation and also obtain its
rational solutions. In section VI we discuss the symmetries of the nmKdV-1 and
the nmKdV-2 equations for both the focusing and the defocusing cases and obtain
their rational solutions. Besides, we show that for both the nmKdV-1 and
nmKdV-2 as well as for both focusing and defocusing type, one can decouple
the
x
and
t
dependence and obtain special novel solutions of both nmKdV-1
and nmKdV-2 equations. Finally, in Section VII we point out some of the open
problems.

II
Properties of the nKdV equation

In this section we first consider two forms of the nKdV equation and show how
both can be written in terms of a potential function
\phi(x,t)
. We then show
that both forms can be put into the Lou form from which it is easy to derive the
equation for moving solitons. We also show that both forms of the nKdV equation
in terms of the potential function
\phi
can be derived from an action
S

which leads to conservation of energy and momentum.

II.1
Potential form of the nKdV equation

Starting from the nKdV equation, Eq. (
5
),

v_{t}=w_{x},~~w_{xxx}+4vw_{x}+2v_{x}w=0,

(28)

Wazwaz
[
11
]
noted that the decoupling of the
v
and
w
equations is
possible by introducing a potential
\phi(x,t)
, satisfying

w=\phi_{t},~~v=\phi_{x}\,.

(29)

Then one automatically satisfies the equation

v_{t}=w_{x}.

(30)

The equation for
w
then becomes the following equation for
\phi

\phi_{xxxt}+4\phi_{x}\phi_{tx}+2\phi_{xx}\phi_{t}=0\,.

(31)

This is the potential form of the nKdV-1 equation.
Using the negative order KdV equation (nKdV-2) defined as

\Phi v_{t}=-v_{x}\,,

(32)

with

\Phi(v)=-(\partial_{x}^{2}+4v+2v_{x}\partial_{x}^{-1})\,,

(33)

and letting

v_{t}=\tilde{w}_{x}\,,

(34)

one obtains

\tilde{w}_{xxx}+4v\tilde{w}_{x}+2v_{x}\tilde{w}=v_{x}\,.

(35)

One can eliminate the rhs of Eq. (
35
) by letting

\tilde{w}(x,t)=w(x,t)+\frac{1}{2}

(36)

to obtain

v_{t}=w_{x};~~w_{xxx}+4vw_{x}+2v_{x}w=0\,,

(37)

which is identical to Eq. (
5
).

Starting from Eq. (
35
), Wazwaz
[
15
]
obtained the nKdV-2
equation in the following potential form

\phi_{xxxt}+4\phi_{x}\phi_{xt}+2\phi_{xx}\phi_{t}-\phi_{xx}=0

(38)

by introducing a different potential
\phi
which is related to
u
by

v(x)=\phi_{x}=-u_{xx}/u;~~\phi_{t}=u^{2}+1/2.

(39)

This explicitly shows the connection between the nKdV-1 and the nKdV-2 equation.
The different recursion operators
K
and
\Phi
lead to equations that can be
transformed into one another by Eq. (
36
).
One interesting aspect of the nKdV-2 form of the equations is that
Eq. (
38
) can be derived from an action formalism which allows one to
identify both the momentum
P
of the solution as well as its energy
E
. The
nKdV-1 equation when cast in an action formulation formally has zero
Hamiltonian. Using the compatibility equation

\phi_{xt}=\phi_{tx}

(40)

of Eq. (
39
) for the nKdV-2 equation leads to the same Lou equation for

u(x,t)
as for the nkdV-1 equation, Eq. (
9
), namely

(\frac{u_{xx}}{u})_{t}+2uu_{x}=0.

(41)

So both nKdV-1 and nKdV-2 formulations lead to the same equation for the
moving solutions of the form:
u=u(x-ct)=u(\xi)
. For moving solutions the
Lou equation leads to Eq. (
24
).

The KdV equation as well as the nKdV equation have an infinite number of
conserved quantities
H_{n}
. Both equations can be derived from
a Lagrangian when written in terms of the field
\phi
where
v=\phi_{x}
.

II.2
Action formulation of the nKdV equations

For the nKdV-2 formulation, using the identification:

v=\partial_{x}\phi(x,t),~~\tilde{w}=\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)\,,

(42)

we obtain the equation

\phi_{xxxt}+4\phi_{x}\phi_{xt}+2\phi_{xx}\phi_{t}-\phi_{xx}=0.

(43)

On the other hand the potential form of the nKdV-1 equation is

\phi_{xxxt}+4\phi_{x}\phi_{xt}+2\phi_{xx}\phi_{t}\,.

(44)

Both equations for the potential can be derived from the action:

\displaystyle S=\int dtdx\left[\phi_{t}(\phi_{xxx}+2(\phi_{x})^{2})-\gamma\phi_{x}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv\int dxdt\left[\pi(x,t)\dot{\phi}(x,t)-\gamma{h}[\phi_{x}]\right]=\int Ldt\,,

(45)

where
\gamma=1
for the nKdV-2 form of the equation and
\gamma=0
for the
nKdV-1 form of the equation. When one has terms in the action with only the
derivative terms
\phi_{x},\phi_{t},\phi_{xx},\phi_{xxx}
, then setting the
variation of the action with respect to
\phi
to zero yields the equation

-\partial_{t}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi_{t}}-\partial_{x}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi_{x}}+\partial_{xx}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi_{xx}}-\partial_{xxx}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\phi_{xxx}}=0.

(46)

This leads to

\phi_{xxxt}+4\phi_{x}\phi_{xt}+2\phi_{xx}\phi_{t}-\gamma\phi_{xx}=0\,.

(47)

The conserved Hamiltonian is

H=I_{1}=\gamma[\int dx{h}=\int dx\phi_{x}^{2}=\int dxv(x,t)^{2}],

(48)

and the canonical momentum density is

\pi(x,t)=v_{xx}+2v^{2}=\phi_{xxx}+2(\phi_{x})^{2}.

(49)

That
H
is conserved can also be seen by writing Eq. (
43
) for the
nKdV-2 equation as a continuity equation:

\partial_{t}(\phi_{x}^{2})+\partial_{x}(\phi_{xxt}+2\phi_{x}\phi_{t}-\phi_{x})=0.

(50)

By considering moving waves we can identify
P
as

P=\int dx(v_{x}^{2}-2v^{3})=\int dx(\phi_{xx}^{2}-2\phi_{x}^{3})\,,

(51)

which is also conserved via the equation of motion, Eq. (
43
).

We notice that the action for the nKdV-2 equation (
\alpha=1
) is very similar
to the action for the KdV equation
[
20
]
with the role of
P
and
H

being interchanged. To obtain the Lagrangian for traveling wave solutions we
let

\phi_{x}=v=f(x-ct),~~~\phi_{t}=\tilde{w}=g(x-ct)\,,

(52)

which satisfies the equation

\phi_{xt}=\phi_{tx}\,,

(53)

\tilde{w}_{x}=g^{\prime}(\xi)=-cf^{\prime}(\xi)=v_{t}\,,

(54)

where
\xi=x-ct
. Thus

g(\xi)=-cf(\xi)+\alpha,

(55)

where
\alpha
is a constant. Note that
\tilde{w}=w+1/2
, so the same equation
holds for both the cases with different integration constants.
We also have

\pi(\xi)=\phi_{xxx}+2(\phi_{x})^{2}=f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+2f^{2}(\xi),

(56)

so that

L=\int dy\left[c(f^{\prime 2}-2f^{3})-f^{2}(1-2\alpha)\right].

(57)

This identifies the momentum of the traveling wave as

P=\int dy(f^{\prime 2}-2f^{3})\,.

(58)

The conserved energy and momentum can be identified with two of the infinite
number of conservation laws found by the Miura Transformation
[
6
,
21
]
.
The equation for
f
is determined by Lagrange’s equations with
L
given by
Eq. (
57

\frac{d}{dy}\frac{\delta L[f]}{\delta f^{\prime}}-\frac{\delta L}{\delta f}=0\,,

(59)

so that

c[f^{\prime\prime}+3f^{2}]+f(1-2\alpha)=0\,.

(60)

Solutions are found by assuming:

f=A\sech^{2}(\beta\xi)+B\,.

(61)

For
\alpha=1
we find two solutions:
B=0
and
B=1/(3c)
with
A=2\beta^{2}
.
When
B=0
then
{1}/{4\beta^{2}}
, when
B=1/(3c)
we find

c=-{1}/{4\beta^{2}}
. Thus we get the two solutions

f=2\beta^{2}\sech^{2}(\beta\xi)\,,

(62)

f=2\beta^{2}\sech^{2}(\beta\xi)-\frac{4}{3}\beta^{2}.

(63)

Instead when
\alpha=0
we find two solutions:
B=0,c=-{1}/{4\beta^{2}}
and

f(\xi)=2\beta^{2}\text{sech}^{2}(\beta\xi)\,.

(64)

For the second solution,
B=-1/(3c),c={1}/{4\beta^{2}}
and

f(\xi)=2\beta^{2}\text{sech}^{2}(\beta\xi)-\frac{4}{3}\beta^{2}\,.

(65)

We see the solutions with

Only the solutions with
B=0

Eq. (
60
) does
not
have a traveling wave solution of the form

f=2m\beta^{2}\text{cn}^{2}(\beta y,m)

. However, there are solutions
with

f(\xi)=Ac~\text{cn}(\beta\xi|m)^{2}+B\,,

(66)

we find for

Ac=2m\beta^{2},~~c=\frac{1}{4\beta^{2}\sqrt{1-m+m^{2}}},~~B=\frac{2}{3}\beta^{2}(1-2m+\sqrt{1-m+m^{2}})\,.

(67)

Note that when we solve the
\phi
equation or the

III
Properties of the nmKdV equations

We first show that for both the attractive and the repulsive nmKdV-1 equations
one can obtains a Lou type of equation which then allows one to easily find the
traveling wave solutions. We also discuss the fact that the nmKdV equation has
an infinite number of conservation laws.

The mKdV equation has a bi-Hamiltonian structure as well as a Lagrangian formulation
[
16
]
.
Writing

u=-w_{x},

(71)

then

u_{t}=-w_{xt}=-w_{xx}=-P[w_{x}].

(72)

If one assumes
w_{x}
vanishes at
\pm\infty
, then

w_{x}=w_{t}.

(73)

Now from

u_{t}=\Lambda_{m}u_{x},

(74)

we have

\displaystyle-w_{xt}

\displaystyle=(-D^{2}\mp 4w_{xx}w_{x}^{2}\mp 4w_{xx}D^{-1}(\cdot w_{x}))w_{xx}

(75)

\displaystyle=-w_{xxxx}\mp 6w_{xx}w_{x}^{2}.

Integrating over
x
one obtains

-w_{t}=-w_{xxx}\mp 2w_{x}^{3}.

(76)

Consider the action:

S=\int dxdt[\alpha w_{x}w_{t}+\beta w_{x}w_{xxx}+\gamma w_{x}^{4}]\,.

(77)

Minimizing the action, we obtain Eq. (
75
) by choosing

\alpha=1/2,~~\beta=-1/2,~~\gamma=\mp 1/2\,,

(78)

so that the Hamiltonian is given by

H=1/2\int dx[uu_{xx}\pm u^{4}]\,.

(79)

The negative order mKdV equation can be written as

\Phi u_{t}=u_{x}.

(80)

The recursion operator for the focusing (defocusing) form is given in

[
17
]

\Phi=(-D^{2}\mp 4u^{2}\mp 4u_{x}D^{-1}(\cdot u)),

(81)

where the upper sign refers to the focusing case.

The nmKdV equation was studied by several researchers
[
2
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. There are two variants of the nmKdV equation that have been proposed in
the literature and both are integrable. The first one (hereafter we call it as
nmKdV-1) is given by

uu_{xxt}-u_{x}u_{xt}+4u^{3}u_{t}=0\,,

(82)

in the focusing case while

uu_{xxt}-u_{x}u_{xt}-4u^{3}u_{t}=0\,,

(83)

in the defocusing case. On the other hand, using the recursion operator for
the mKdV case, Wazwaz
[
25
]
obtained the following nmKdV equation
(hereafter we call it as nmKdV-2)

u_{x}u_{xxxt}+4u^{2}u_{x}u_{xt}+12uu_{x}^{2}u_{t}-u_{xx}u_{xxt}-4u^{2}u_{xx}u_{t}=0\,,

(84)

in the focusing case while

u_{x}u_{xxxt}-4u^{2}u_{x}u_{xt}-12uu_{x}^{2}u_{t}-u_{xx}u_{xxt}+4u^{2}u_{xx}u_{t}=0\,,

(85)

in the defocusing case. Subsequently, a few soliton, traveling wave and
breather solutions have been obtained for these nmKdV equations

[
18
,
25
]
.

The nmKdV-2 is obtained from the hierarchy operator
\Phi
.
The negative order mKdV-2 equations are given by

\Phi u_{t}=u_{x}\,.

(86)

Here
\Phi
is given by
[
17
]

\Phi=-D^{2}\mp 4u^{2}\mp 4u_{x}D^{-1}(\cdot u),

(87)

where the upper sign is for the focusing branch.
Here

D^{-1}(\cdot u)f(x)=D^{-1}(f(x)\cdot u)\,,

(88)

so we obtain

-u_{xxt}\mp u^{2}u_{t}\mp 4u_{x}\partial^{-1}(uu_{t})=u_{x}.

(89)

Note that unlike the mKdV equation where we had the term

\partial^{-1}(uu_{x})=u^{2}/2\,,

(90)

we now have to set

(u^{2}/2)_{t}=w_{x}

to undo the inverse derivative with respect to
x
.
Another strategy is to differentiate Eq. (
89
) with respect to
x

and use

\partial^{-1}(u\cdot u_{t})=\frac{u_{x}+u_{xxt}\mp 4u^{2}u_{t}}{4u_{x}}\,.

(91)

Doing this we obtain

-u_{x}u_{xxxt}\mp 4u^{2}u_{x}u_{xt}\mp 12uu_{x}^{2}u_{t}+u_{xx}u_{xxt}\pm 4u^{2}u_{xx}u_{t}=0.

(92)

We can get a slightly simpler equation by using
w

-u_{xxt}\mp u^{2}u_{t}\mp 4u_{x}w=u_{x}.

(93)

Then choose
\tilde{w}
so that

\mp 4u_{x}w-u_{x}=\mp 4u_{x}\tilde{w}\,.

(94)

Then we have

-u_{xxt}\mp u^{2}u_{t}\mp 4u_{x}\tilde{w}=0.

(95)

We can now differentiate Eq. (
95
) with respect to
x
and then use
Eq. (
93
) to eliminate
\tilde{w}
. Using
w_{x}=\tilde{w}_{x}=uu_{t}
, this leads to:

\pm u_{xxxt}+2uu_{x}u_{t}+u^{2}u_{xt}+4u_{xx}\tilde{w}+4u_{x}uu_{t}=0\,,

(96)

where

4u_{xx}\tilde{w}=\mp u_{xxt}u_{xx}/u_{x}-u^{2}u_{t}u_{xx}/u_{x}\,,

(97)

so we obtain

\pm u_{xxxt}u_{x}+\mp u_{xxt}u_{xx}+u^{2}u_{t}u_{xx}+4u_{x}uu_{t}=0\,.

(98)

Unlike the mKdV case or the nKdV case, we have not been able to find a Lagrangian that leads to these equations.

Using the Lax pair approach
[
18
]
for obtaining the infinite set of
conservation laws, one gets a simpler set of equations which we
denote as the nmKdV-1 equations.
For the focusing (defocusing) case one obtains:

\big(\frac{u_{xt}}{u}\big)_{x}\pm(2u^{2})_{t}=0\,,

(99)

which is equivalent to

\rho_{xx}=u^{2}\,,~~u_{xt}\pm 2u\rho_{xt}+\alpha u=0\,,

(100)

where
\alpha
is an arbitrary integration constant. The upper sign is for the
attractive case.

In analogy with Eq. (
92
) we can write these equations as:

uu_{xxt}-u_{x}u_{xt}\pm 4u^{3}u_{t}=0\,.

(101)

The upper sign is for the attractive (focusing) case.

We have not been able to find the simple transformation from one form i.e. Eq. (
101
) to the other i.e. Eq. (
92
), nor have we been able to find a Lagrangian for either form of the nmKdV equations.

The equation in Lou form, Eq. (
9
), is quite similar to Eq. (
99
)
which leads to the important fact that for the moving solitary waves that
depend only on
\xi=x-ct
, the equations for the moving waves will be related.

III.1
Conservation Laws for nmKdV-1

The nmKdV equations have an infinite number of conservation laws.
These conservation laws can be determined from the Lax pair. The formalism is
discussed in detail in
[
18
]
.
Here we sketch the formalism for the attractive case, the repulsive case can be
obtained from the substitution
u\rightarrow iu
.
From the Lax pair

\Phi_{x}=A\Phi,~~~\Phi_{t}=B\Phi,

(102)

where

\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})_{T},

(103)

and

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&iu\\
iu&-\lambda\end{array}\right)\,,

(104)

B=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\rho_{xt}&-iu_{t}\\
iu_{t}&\frac{\alpha}{2}+\rho_{xt}\end{array}\right)\,.

(105)

Then setting
\Gamma={\Phi_{2}}/{\Phi_{1}}
one finds that
\Gamma
obeys a continuity equation

\frac{d}{dt}(iu\Gamma)=\frac{1}{2\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}(-\rho_{xt}-iu_{t}\Gamma),

(106)

as well as the equation:

\Gamma_{x}=iu-2\lambda\Gamma-iu\Gamma^{2}.

(107)

Assuming
\Gamma
has a series in inverse powers of
\lambda
then leads to a set of recursion relations for the coefficients
\Gamma_{n}
. One finds:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}u,~~~\Gamma_{2}=-\frac{i}{4}u_{x}\,,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{n-1,x}-\frac{i}{2}u\sum_{j+k=n-1}\Gamma_{j}\Gamma_{k}=0,~~n\geq 3.

(108)

Using the continuity equation, Eq. (
106
), one then obtains Eq. (
99
), namely

(2u^{2})_{t}+(\frac{u_{xt}}{u})_{x}=0\,.

(109)

One also obtains the recursion relation

(2u\Gamma_{n})_{t}+(u_{t}\Gamma_{n-1})_{x}=0,~~n\geq 2.

(110)

This leads to an infinite set of conservation laws. The first three conservation laws are (the upper sign is for the attractive case):

M_{1}=8\int u^{2}dx;~~M_{2}=2\int(u^{4}\mp u_{x}^{2})dx;~~M_{3}=\int(u^{6}\mp 5u^{2}u_{x}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}u_{xx}^{2})dx.

(111)

IV
Connection between traveling wave solutions of nKdV-1 and nmKdV-1
equations and static solutions of
\phi^{4}
field theory

The Lagrangian of 1+1 dimensional
\phi^{4}
field theory is

L=\int dx(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi\mp m^{2}\phi^{2}/2-\lambda\phi^{4}/4)\,,

(112)

whose time independent solutions obey

\phi_{xx}=\pm m^{2}\phi+\lambda\phi^{3}\equiv a\phi+b\phi^{3}.

(113)

We would like to show that the moving solutions
u(x-ct)=u(\xi)
of both the
nKdV-1 and the nmKdV-1 equations can be related to the solutions of
Eq. (
113
). However, the conservation laws of the equation are functions
of
v(x,t)
and the conservation laws of the nmKdV-1 equation are functions of

u(x,t)
. Thus, to determine the finite energy moving solutions of the nKdV-1
equation one has to first obtain
v(x,t)
from
u(x,t)
. Thus each static
solution of the
\phi^{4}
field equation leads to different solutions for the
nKdV-1 and the nmKdV-1 equations if we are interested in solutions with finite
energy. It was already shown by Qiao and Li
[
19
]
that for the
traveling wave solutions of the nKdV-1 equation, the Lou form of the equation
could be mapped into the solutions of the
\phi^{4}
field theory. Here we show
that their observation can be extended to the nmKdV-1 equation and that the
solutions of Eq. (
113
) allow us to determine the moving solutions for
both the nKdV-1 and the nmKdV-1 equations.

If we consider the moving solutions of the nKdV-1 equation with
\xi=x-ct
,
the Lou form of the equation is Eq (
22
)

c\frac{d}{d\xi}(\frac{u_{\xi\xi}}{u})=2uu_{\xi}\,.

(114)

One obtains by integrating over
\xi

u_{\xi\xi}=(1/c)u^{3}+\alpha u\,,

(115)

which is similar to the static field equation of the symmetric
\phi^{4}

Eq. (
113
). Here
\alpha
is an integration constant. Note, depending on
whether the wave is moving to the right or to the left, i.e. if
c>0
or

c<0
, the coefficient of the
u^{3}
term can be positive or negative. Thus
we can identify
1/c
and
\alpha
with
\phi^{4}
coefficients
b
and
a
,
respectively. Further, while in the
\phi^{4}
case one has a static field
equation, in the nKdV-1 case one is discussing the moving solitary wave
equation. For the nKdV-1 equation the field that enters the conservation laws
is

v=-\frac{u_{xx}}{u}=-u^{\prime\prime}(\xi)/u(\xi)=-u^{2}/c-\alpha,

(116)

where we have used Eq. (
115
).

In terms of
\xi=x-ct
it is easy to see that the focusing nmKdV-1
Eq. (
99
) can be rewritten as

(\frac{u_{\xi\xi}}{u})_{\xi}+(2u^{2})_{\xi}=0.

(117)

Note that Eq. (
117
) is independent of
c
. Integrating once one gets

u_{\xi\xi}=-2u^{3}+\alpha u\,,

(118)

where
\alpha
is an integration constant. Thus for the focusing nmKdV-1 case
we can identify
-2
and
\alpha
with the
\phi^{4}
coefficients
b
and
a
,
respectively.

On the other hand, the defocusing nmKdV-1 Eq. (
99
) can be rewritten as

(\frac{u_{\xi\xi}}{u})_{\xi}-(2u^{2})_{\xi}=0\,.

(119)

Integrating once one gets

u_{\xi\xi}=2u^{3}+\alpha u\,,

(120)

where
\alpha
is an integration constant. Thus, for the defocusing nmKdV-1 case
we can identify
2
and
\alpha
with the
\phi^{4}
coefficients
b
and
a
,
respectively.

Summarizing, in nKdV-1 as well as in both the focusing and the defocusing
nmKdV-1 cases the integration constant
\alpha
can be identified with the

\phi^{4}
coefficient
a
. On the other hand, while the nKdV-1 coefficient
1/c

can be identified with the
\phi^{4}
coefficient
b
, for the focusing (defocusing)
nmKdV-1 cases the coefficient
-2
(
2
) is identified with the
\phi^{4}

coefficient
b
. Thus all the well known static solutions of the symmetric

\phi^{4}
field theory are automatically the solutions of the nKdV-1 or either
the attractive or the repulsive nmKdV-1 equations.

\phi^{4}

Eq. (

VII
Open Problems

This work raises several open questions, some of which are:

1.

In Sec. IV and in the Appendix, we have obtained 32 moving solutions of
both the nKdV-1 and the nmKdV-1 equations by making use of the known static solutions
of the symmetric
\phi^{4}
equation. The obvious question is, has one exhausted all
the static solutions of
\phi^{4}
equation and hence of nKdV-1 and nmKdV-1?

2.

In Sec. VI we have shown that for both the nmKdV-1 and the nmKdV-2 and
for both the focusing and the defocusing cases, one can choose a novel ansatz so
that
t
and
x
decouple and one obtains nonlinear equations in
x
.
While we have obtained few solutions of these nonlinear equations, clearly
we have not obtained all the possible solutions of these equations. It will be
interesting to find at least few more such solutions.

3.

In Sec. VI for both the nmKdV-1 and the nmKdV-2 equations and for both the
focusing and the defocusing cases, we have obtained few rational solutions. Clearly
we have not obtained all the possible rational solutions of these equations. It
will be interesting to find at least few more rational solutions.

4.

Even though the nmKdV-1 and nmKdV-2 equations are very different,
we found that the rational solutions as well as the novel solutions where
x

and
t
decouple are identical for both focusing and defocusing
nmkdV-1 and nmKdV-2 equations. Besides, we have also explicitly checked that
this is also true for the 14 moving traveling wave solutions. The first
question is whether this is also true for the remaining 18 traveling wave
solutions of the nmKdV-1 that we have obtained in Sec. IV and in the Appendix?
The next more general question is whether this is true in general for all the solutions
of the nmKdV-1 and nmKdV-2 equations or whether we can find few different solutions
of these equations.

5.

The KdV equation is an integrable equation which can be
solved through inverse scattering and the corresponding linear problem is
essentially a Schrödinger-like equation. Since the nKdV equation is also
integrable, the obvious question is if it too can be solved using the
inverse scattering method. If so, what is the corresponding linear problem? Is
it again a Schrödinger-like equation?

6.

The one-soliton solution of the KdV equation for fixed time is
essentially a reflectionless potential in the context of the corresponding
Schrödinger-like equation. What about the corresponding one-soliton
solutions of the nKdV equation? For fixed time, does it again correspond to
a reflectionless potential?

7.

The periodic one-soliton solution of the KdV equation for fixed time is
known to have a novel band structure. In particular, there is a band, a band gap
followed by a continuum. What about the corresponding periodic one-soliton
solution of the nKdV equation? For fixed time, does it have a novel band
structure?

We hope to address some of these questions in the near future.

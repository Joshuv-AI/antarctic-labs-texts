Myeloid-lineage blasts with histiocytoid differentiation in TP53 mutated acute myeloid leukemia: a deceptive morphologic overlap with mature malignant histiocytic neoplasms
Authors: Neha Seth, Aishwarya Ravindran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHere, we report a case of TP53-mutated acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with monocytic differentiation that showed striking histiocytoid morphologic transformation following induction chemotherapy. Post-treatment blasts demonstrated significant morphologic overlap with mature malignant histiocytic neoplasms, particularly histiocytic sarcoma subtype, creating a potential diagnostic pitfall. Retained myeloperoxidase expression, together with the absence of cyclin D1 expression, supported a diagnosis of persistent AML rather than divergent histiocytic differentiation.Keywords: Malignant histiocytosis, TP53, AML

Case presentation

A 25-year-old male with a history of autism presented to the emergency department with shortness of breath, coughing, and hemoptysis. Complete blood count showed normocytic anemia (hemoglobin: 6.8 g/dL; reference range: 13.5–17.5 g/dL), leukocytosis (41 × 109/L; reference range: 4,500–11,000/μL), and thrombocytopenia (platelet: 14 × 109/L; reference range: 150,000–400,000/μL). Antibiotics were initiated because sepsis was suspected. Peripheral blood smear revealed 21% blasts. Bone marrow biopsy revealed hypercellular marrow (> 90% cellularity) with sheets of blasts characterized by large nuclei, fine chromatin, and scant-to-moderate basophilic cytoplasm (Fig. 1A-B). Immunohistochemistry demonstrated myeloperoxidase (MPO) positivity (Fig. 1C), and flow cytometry showed a myelomonocytic immunophenotype (CD14(partial) +, CD64 +, CD13 +, CD33 +, myeloperoxidase(MPO) +, CD34-negative, CD117-negative), consistent with acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Next-generation sequencing identified pathogenic variants in TP53 (p.R213Q, VAF: 57.4%) and ASXL1 (p.S304C, VAF: 52.0%). He underwent a follow-up bone marrow biopsy on day + 14 post-induction chemotherapy with 7 + 3i (cytarabine and idarubicin). Post-chemotherapy, the bone marrow showed tumor cells with bizarre cytology, strikingly distinct from the initial diagnosis, characterized by large irregular/multilobated nuclei, distinct nucleoli, finely dispersed chromatin, and abundant cytoplasm (Fig. 1D-E). The morphologic features were suggestive of histiocytic differentiation, raising the concern of divergent myeloid differentiation into a malignant histiocytic neoplasm (MHN), specifically the histiocytic sarcoma subtype [1]. Subsequent immunohistochemical workup showed a similar immunophenotype (CD68 + (Fig. 1F), MPO + (Fig. 1G)) to the initial diagnostic biopsy, most consistent with persistent AML over MHN. In addition, the tumor cells demonstrated diffuse and strong p53 expression (Fig. 1H), indicative of a TP53 mutation [2]. The histopathologic features of bizarre cytologic atypia with histiocytoid differentiation in the blasts post-chemotherapy are secondary to the effects of the TP53 mutation. These immature blasts display significant morphological overlap with mature MHNs, especially in the absence of CD34/CD117 expression, necessitating comprehensive immunophenotyping [3, 4]. Furthermore, TP53 mutations can be observed in both AML and MHNs [2, 4]. While the MPO-positivity in the present case is strongly supportive of AML, additional workup demonstrated the complete absence of cyclin D1 (Fig. 1I). The tumor cells in MHNs typically show diffuse cyclin D1-positivity [4], and the absence of cyclin D1 in the present case provided additional supportive evidence against histiocytic differentiation complementing the MPO-positivity [5]. Histiocytoid differentiation in the setting of a myeloid neoplasm, particularly in the post‑chemotherapy context, should be interpreted with caution. Accurate classification requires correlation with comprehensive immunophenotyping and adjunctive genetic studies to avoid misclassification as a malignant histiocytic neoplasm.

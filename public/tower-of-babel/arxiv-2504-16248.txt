Tracking the symmetries of ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3} -orbifold K3s within the Mathieu groups
Kasia Budzik, Anne Taormina, Mara Ungureanu, Katrin Wendland, Ida G. Zadeh

Abstract
For ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3} -orbifold limits of K3, we provide a counterpart to the extensive studies by Nikulin and others of the geometry and symmetries of classical Kummer surfaces. In particular, we determine the group of holomorphic symplectic automorphisms of ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3} -orbifold limits of K3. We moreover track this group within two of the Mathieu groups, which involves a variation of Kondo’s lattice techniques that Taormina and Wendland introduced earlier in their study of the symmetries of Kummer surfaces and the genesis of their symmetry surfing programme. Specifically, we realise the finite group of symplectic automorphisms of this class of K3 surfaces as a subgroup of the sporadic groups Mathieu 12 12 and Mathieu 24 24 in terms of permutations of 12 12 , resp. 24 24 elements. As a proof of concept, we construct an embedding that yields the largest Mathieu group when the symmetry group of ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3} -orbifold K3s is combined with all symmetries of Kummer surfaces.



1
Motivation, Overview and Summary of Results

K3 surfaces continue to fascinate and inspire mathematicians and theoretical
physicists long after their name was coined, probably by André Weil in 1958
Weil (1979)
.
Their geometry and symmetries provide valuable insights in a variety of contexts, not the
least that of superstring theory, which is the backdrop of our motivation here.

By definition, a K3 surface is a compact, complex manifold of dimension
2
which is simply
connected and has trivial canonical bundle. It therefore carries a holomorphic volume
form
\eta
.
We are interested in finite groups of symplectic automorphisms of K3 surfaces,
i.e. automorphisms that fix
\eta
as well as some Kähler class
\omega
,
and in how they are realised as subgroups of the Mathieu group
M_{24}
.

Indeed,
the observation in
Eguchi et al. (2011)
that the elliptic genus of K3 reveals
a connection with
M_{24}
– a fact mathematically proven subsequently on the level of representation theory and (Mock) modular forms in

Gannon (2016)
– remains unexplained by geometry and string theory.
This is surprising (and haunting!), since
the observation was first made in the context of superstring theory
compactified on a K3 surface, where one deals with K3 theories, i.e. superconformal
field theories with
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
worldsheet supersymmetry at central charges
(c,\overline{c})=(6,6)
,
with spacetime supersymmetry, integer
\mathfrak{u}(1)
-charges
and without holomorphic BPS states at weight 1/2
Seiberg (1988)
;
Aspinwall and Morrison (1997)
;
Nahm and Wendland (2001)
.
The
\mathfrak{u}(1)
-charges
are the eigenvalues of the zero modes in the two
\mathfrak{u}(1)_{1}
subalgebras of the left
and right
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal
algebras, which by the operators of two-fold spectral flow are extended to affine subalgebras
of type
\mathfrak{su}(2)_{1}
.
Thereby, any K3 theory has
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
worldsheet superconformal symmetry, and its conformal field
theoretic elliptic genus can be expressed
in terms of
\mathcal{N}=4
superconformal characters
Eguchi et al. (1989)
and equals the complex
elliptic genus of K3 surfaces.

The ‘Mathieu Moonshine’ observation is based on the fact that in the
decomposition of the superconformal field theoretic elliptic genus into characters of irreducible

\mathcal{N}=4
representations, all coefficients of massive characters are positive

Eguchi et al. (2011)
;
Gannon (2016)
. That a deeper connection exists, at least on the level of
representation theory, becomes apparent upon considering the corresponding twining
elliptic genera which are obtained from the elliptic genus of K3 by replacing the dimensions of

M_{24}
representations by their characters with respect to an
M_{24}
group element.
They
were first computed in
Cheng (2010)
;
Gaberdiel et al. (2010b)
;
Gaberdiel et al. (2010a)
;
Eguchi and Hikami (2011)
and their
behaviour indicates the existence of an underlying representation of the entire group
M_{24}
,
which was proven in
Gannon (2016)
.
However, no K3 theory can have
M_{24}
as its symmetry
group, and even more disconcertingly, K3 theories can have symmetry groups that are not
subgroups of
M_{24}

Gaberdiel et al. (2012)
.
Indeed, Gaberdiel, Hohenegger and Volpato classified the
symmetry groups of K3 non-linear sigma models
preserving the
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
symmetry as well as the spectral flow operators,
showing that all of these symmetry groups are naturally
realized as subgroups of the Conway group
\mbox{Co}_{1}

Gaberdiel et al. (2012)
.

This is in contrast to the fact that any finite group of symplectic
automorphisms of a K3 surface is isomorphic to a
subgroup of the Mathieu group
M_{23}
, as was proven
in
Mukai (1988)
almost
25
years before
Eguchi et al. (2011)
.
The
Kummer surfaces
form a family of special K3 surfaces which are obtained
by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-orbifolding an arbitrary
complex two-torus,
where the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action is the standard one.
In
Taormina and Wendland (2013)
and
Taormina and Wendland (2015b)
, two of the authors used lattice constructions
inspired by the seminal works
Nikulin (1980a)
and
Kondo (1998)
to realise and naturally
combine finite groups of
symplectic automorphisms of Kummer surfaces as subgroups of
M_{24}
. This kick-started our
symmetry-surfing programme, which is supported by results in

Taormina and Wendland (2015a)
;
Gaberdiel et al. (2017)
;
Keller and Zadeh (2020)
;
Taormina and Wendland (2018)
, albeit not fully
completed yet.
Apart from the proposal to combine symmetry groups of distinct models, this programme is
built on the idea that the ‘Mathieu Moonshine’ phenomenon arises through symplectic automorphisms
of K3 surfaces only, as opposed to the general symmetries of K3 theories. This is in accord with the
findings of Gaberdiel, Hohenegger and Volpato
Gaberdiel et al. (2012)
mentioned above: while every symplectic automorphism of
a given K3 surface
X
belongs to
M_{24}
and
induces ‘geometric’ symmetries of the K3 sigma models with geometric interpretation
on
X
, such sigma models usually have plenty of additional, ‘non-geometric’ symmetries which need not belong
to
M_{24}
. The idea is also in line with
the arguments of
Kapustin (2005)
which suggest that compatibility of symmetries of K3 sigma models with an infinite
volume limit is required for such symmetries to contribute to ‘Mathieu Moonshine’, thus enforcing
the restriction to ‘geometric’ symmetries, see e.g.
(
Wendland, 2015
, §4)
and
(
Wendland, 2019
, §4.5)
for further details.

For these reasons, in our work we focus on ‘geometric’ symmetries and their action as symplectic
automorphisms of K3 surfaces.
In
Taormina and Wendland (2015b)
, it was argued that the group

(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{4}\rtimes A_{8}
, which is a maximal subgroup of
M_{24}
, arises as the combined holomorphic
symplectic automorphism group of all Kummer surfaces whose Kähler class is induced by the
underlying complex torus. The prescription for such combination of symmetries was given
in
Taormina and Wendland (2013)
by means of overarching maps between pairs of Kummer surfaces, a technique which
allows to symmetry-surf the moduli space of K3 theories.
While representing symmetries on the lattice of integral cohomology as suggested by

Nikulin (1980a)
;
Kondo (1998)
,
the crucial variation to these techniques which was introduced in
Taormina and Wendland (2013)

uses the Kummer lattice rather than the orthogonal complement of the invariant sublattice.

Encouraged by the above results concerning the specific class of Kummer K3 surfaces, that is,
the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-orbifold limits of K3, and by using analogous techniques, we set out to
realise the group of symplectic automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold limits of K3 (also known as
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s)
as a subgroup of a Mathieu group.
This leads us to exhibit the group of symplectic automorphisms of

\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s, which is
(\mathbb{Z}_{3})^{2}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{4}
or
(\mathbb{Z}_{3})^{2}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, depending on the
symmetries of the underlying torus,
as a subgroup of the Mathieu group
M_{12}
, but also as a subgroup of
M_{24}
.
By combining the generators of the groups
(\mathbb{Z}_{2})^{4}\rtimes A_{8}
and

(\mathbb{Z}_{3})^{2}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, one generates
M_{24}
.
Although the latter step currently relies on ad hoc choices, it contributes another piece to the Mathieu Moonshine puzzle,
which we plan to solve in the future by providing a geometric or conformal field theoretic justification for how these
symmetry groups can be combined via symmetry-surfing.
This would have to include an explanation of the action of these symmetries on quarter BPS states in the spectrum
of K3 sigma models.
We view our results, which pertain to geometry and
group theory, as valuable independently of Mathieu Moonshine:
based on Nikulin’s seminal work
Nikulin (1975)
,
there exists a vast body of literature dedicated to Kummer surfaces,
but we are unaware of a similarly detailed
treatment for
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s.
We aim to close that gap in this work.

In the following, by
X=\widetilde{T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}}
we denote a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3, with
T

a complex two-torus allowing a faithful action of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
by holomorphic symplectic
automorphisms, such that
X
is the minimal resolution of all singularities in
the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-quotient. To introduce the notion of symmetries of
X
, we equip
X
with
a Kähler class
\widetilde{\omega}
that is induced by any
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-invariant Kähler class on
T
; a

symmetry
of
X
then is a biholomorphic automorphism of
X
which preserves
\widetilde{\omega}
.

2
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold limits of K3 surfaces

This section is devoted to the discussion of the geometry of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold limits of K3 surfaces,
in particular of their symmetry groups.

In subsection
2.1
, we review the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold
construction for K3 surfaces, where
the K3 surface is obtained by resolving all singularities in
T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
with
T
an appropriate complex
two-torus. This construction is analogous to the standard Kummer construction.
That such
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold limits of K3 exist has been known before, see for example the classical work
by Shioda and Inose
(
Shioda and Inose, 1977
, Lemma 5.1)
, or Walton’s discussion from a string theory viewpoint

(
Walton, 1988
, §3.1)
. In subsection
2.2
, we offer an alternative
construction, in which we first blow up

sufficiently often, such that a
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-quotient of the
blown-up surface yields the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3. This construction avoids singular intermediate
steps, which we find convenient later on. Though the existence of such a construction
is apparently known to the experts (see, for example,
(
Bertin, 1988
, (2.2))
), to our knowledge the
details have not been described in the literature before.

The Torelli Theorem for K3 surfaces implies that the
symmetry group of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s can be described entirely in terms of its action on the
integral second cohomology of K3 surfaces, as we shall recall at the beginning of subsection
2.4
.
As a preparation, we therefore discuss the integral cohomology of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s in
subsection
2.3
, in terms of the contributions that arise from the
orbifolding construction, namely, those that descend from the underlying torus, on the one hand,
and those that arise from the blow-ups, on the other. This amounts to describing the integral second
cohomology of K3 in terms of two primitive sublattices, called
K
and
P
,
which are glued together by means of Nikulin’s gluing technique, recalled in appendix

C.2
. Here, within the integral second K3 cohomology, the lattice

is the smallest
primitive sublattice
which contains all integral classes that descend from the underlying torus, while
P
is the
orthogonal complement of

.
Both lattices have been determined before, see
(
Shioda and Inose, 1977
, Lemma 5.1)
for the lattice

and
Bertin (1988)
;
Wendland (2000)
;
Wendland (2002)
for the lattice

. However, in
Shioda and Inose (1977)

Shioda and Inose use methods different from ours, while the references

;
Wendland (2000)
;
Wendland (2002)

use the results of
Shioda and Inose (1977)

\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-invariant

In subsection

-orbifold
K3s, that is, the group of biholomorphic automorphisms of our K3 surface which leave the holomorphic volume

is justified, since on the one hand,
X
is a K3 surface as we
shall show below. On the other hand, the notion
of

is commonly used as a shorthand to describe the
construction of a complex manifold by quotienting another complex manifold
by a group of symmetries and then minimally resolving all resulting singularities.
For brevity, in this work we will use the notion of orbifolding
solely to refer to orbifolds of
complex tori
.

Finally,
the terminology orbifold
limit
is commonly used to remind
of the fact that the resulting complex manifold, despite being non-singular,
still bears the traces of the singularities of the quotient. Indeed,
the occurrence of
(-2)
-curves is an indicator
of the fact that singularities have been resolved. Moreover, any choice
of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-invariant
Kähler class
on the torus that underlies our
orbifold induces a Kähler class on the orbifold, which however is degenerate
in the sense that it lies on the boundary of the K3 Kähler cone.

We now assert

Proposition 2.1.1

The
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold

is a K3 surface.

Proof
:

By the definition of K3 surfaces, we must show that

is a connected, compact complex surface with trivial first cohomology,
that is,

, and with trivial canonical bundle.

Since

is a connected, compact surface and

arises from

by replacing
each of the nine singular points by a pair of curves that are isomorphic
to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, it follows that

is connected and compact. Moreover,
though
T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is a singular complex variety,

arises by resolving
all singularities, hence altogether

is a connected, compact complex

Using the standard

(z_{1},z_{2})
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}

is represented

(dz_{1},\,dz_{2},\,d\overline{z}_{1},\,d\overline{z}_{2})

3
Representation of the symmetries of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s on a Niemeier lattice

So far, we have described the integral cohomology lattice

of our K3
surface

in terms of the root lattice
R
obtained in the
Kummer-like construction of
X
. Here,

is of type
A_{2}^{9}
and is
generated by the components of the exceptional divisor that arises from the resolutions
in the Kummer-like construction of

.
We made use of the rank-

, negative definite,
Kummer-like lattice

obtained in proposition

, and its orthogonal

obtained in proposition

in terms of the primitive sublattices

and

. In proposition
2.4.3
,
this allowed us to determine all symmetries of

, and to conveniently

in lemmas

and

2.4.2
.

In order to track such symmetries, it is natural to view them as permutation groups,
acting, for example, on each set of vectors of equal length in the lattice
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. However,
since this lattice has signature
(3,19)
, every non-zero lattice vector
is a member of an infinite set of lattice vectors of the same length.
It is therefore more convenient
to represent our symmetries on a lattice of definite signature. To do so, inspired
by Kondo’s seminal paper
Kondo (1998)
, and since corollary
2.4.5
implies
that each of our symmetries is uniquely determined by its action on
P
,
in subsection
3.1
we search for primitive embeddings of the lattice
P(-1)
in
some Niemeier lattice.
It turns out that the unique possibility is the Niemeier lattice
N
of type
A_{2}^{12}

(proposition
3.1.4
), and that the embedding is unique up to
lattice automorphisms (proposition
3.1.3
).
The lattice
N
is introduced in appendix
C.3
and may be generated by the
set (
47
). An explicit primitive embedding of the Kummer-like
lattice

into
N
is given in proposition
3.1.1
.
In remarks
3.1.5
and
3.2.3
we also discuss the only existing

embeddings of
P(-1)
into a Niemeier lattice of different type, which can only be
of type
E_{6}^{4}
.

In subsection
3.2
, we argue that our embedding of
P(-1)

into the Niemeier lattice

allows us to represent the symmetry group of
X

on
N
. This is a variation of the methods introduced by Kondo in
Kondo (1998)
,
and we discuss the advantages and disadvantages of this variation; in fact, as we show in the
introduction to subsection
3.2
, our results prove that
N
is
one of the lattices that may be used to represent the symmetries of
X

on a Niemeier lattice by Kondo’s original method. That this should be the case
was already claimed in

(
Cheng and Harrison, 2015
, p.29, bottom)
, though without a proof. To our knowledge,
our above-mentioned uniqueness result for the Niemeier lattice
N
to allow a primitive
embedding of
P(-1)
is also new. We arrive at an explicit representation of
the symmetry group of
(X,\omega)
on the lattice
N
in propositions
3.2.1

and
3.2.2
.
Since the symmetry group of
(X,\widetilde{\omega})
, with the general choice of
\widetilde{\omega}
obtained in proposition
2.3.7
,
is a subgroup of the symmetry group of
(X,\omega)
by remark

,
this representation of the symmetry group of
(X,\omega)
is sufficient to represent
all symmetry groups of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s. We may therefore restrict our attention to the symmetry group of

(X,\omega)
henceforth, which for simplicity we refer to as the symmetry group of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3 surfaces

.
We argue that the projection of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
to the Mathieu
group
M_{12}
described in appendix

yields an injective

, explicitly given in corollary

.

That the Mathieu group

is a subgroup of the Mathieu group

-orbifold K3 surfaces as subgroup of the largest Mathieu group

We explicitly express our symmetry group as subgroup of

Proposition 3.1.3

,

Then according to proposition

for

and

of the discriminant form on

Proof

Denote by
\widehat{E}_{t}
, respectively

, but

3.2
Tracking the symmetries of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3s within the
Mathieu group
M_{12}

As mentioned at the beginning of this section,
in order to track the symmetries of the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3
X
, it is convenient to represent
them on a Niemeier lattice, since this allows us to view them as permutation groups
of finitely many lattice vectors. This is the idea underlying Kondo’s seminal paper
Kondo (1998)
,
where it is shown that every symmetry group
G
of a K3 surface
Y
can be
faithfully represented on some Niemeier lattice. In more detail, using the induced action
of
G
on the K3 lattice
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
, let
M_{G}
denote the orthogonal complement
of
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})^{G}
in
H^{2}(Y,\mathbb{Z})
. In
(
Kondo, 1998
, Lemma 5)
, Kondo shows that
there exists a Niemeier lattice which allows a primitive embedding of
M_{G}(-1)

and such that the action of
G
can be extended to a lattice automorphism of
the Niemeier lattice.

In the following, we will be using a variation of this construction.
We now restrict our attention to our
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3
X
, and
the symmetry group
G
of
(X,\omega)
, i.e.
G\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{3})^{2}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{4}

by proposition
2.4.3
. Since
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})^{G}
contains the lattice
that generates
\Sigma
(see lemma
2.3.1
and (
8
)), it follows that

M_{G}\subset\Sigma^{\perp}\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. To arrive at (
10
), we have seen
that
\Sigma^{\perp}\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
is
the lattice generated by
P
and
k_{2}+p_{2}
,
where we continue to
use the notations introduced in propositions
2.3.3
and
2.3.5
.
Motivated by corollary
2.4.5
, which also implies that the action of
G
is uniquely
determined by its action on
P
, we find it more convenient to focus on the lattice
P
rather
than the lattice
M_{G}
.
According to propositions
3.1.3
and
3.1.4
,
for the lattice
P
,
the Niemeier lattice
N
of type
A_{2}^{12}
is unique in allowing a primitive embedding

\iota\colon P(-1)\hookrightarrow N
, and this embedding is unique up to lattice
automorphisms. One checks that
N
also allows a primitive embedding of

(\Sigma^{\perp}\cap H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}))(-1)
which on
P(-1)
restricts to
\iota
,
implying that
M_{G}(-1)
embeds primitively in
N
.
The induced action of
G

on the discriminant of
M_{G}
is trivial by construction.
According to Nikulin’s gluing technique (
43
), this means that the
action on
M_{G}(-1)
can be extended to
N
by a trivial action on the orthogonal
complement of
M_{G}(-1)
.

Our focus on the lattice
P
instead of
M_{G}
is
a variation of Kondo’s arguments which two of the authors have already successfully applied
similarly in the Kummer case
Taormina and Wendland (2013)
;
Taormina and Wendland (2015a)
;
Taormina and Wendland (2015b)
.
We find it convenient, because it achieves the same purpose of
tracking symmetries of
X
as lattice automorphisms on a Niemeier lattice, but it
allows us to base our calculations on root
systems, while
M_{G}
according to
(
Nikulin, 1980a
, Theorem 4.3)
cannot contain any roots.
Two of the authors have also previously mentioned that one can
naturally avoid the confusing transition
from rank
22
for the lattice
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
to rank
24
for the lattice
N

by representing
G
on the total integral cohomology

H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{Z})
of
X
, which has rank
24
, rather
than on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
(see
Taormina and Wendland (2013)
);
G
acts trivially on the orthogonal complement of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})

in
H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, making this modification natural indeed.

When in Kondo’s construction,
one uses
M_{G}(-1)\hookrightarrow N
and extends the automorphisms of
M_{G}(-1)
that
are induced
by symmetries of
X
trivially to the orthogonal complement of
M_{G}(-1)
in

N
, this ensures that one obtains an action of
G
on
N
.
As already follows from what is said in Kondo’s work
(
Kondo, 1998
, Proof of Theorem 4)
,
the projection from
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
to
M_{12}
induces an
injective group homomorphism
G\hookrightarrow M_{12}
.
Indeed, when retracing our steps from
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
to
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}))
,
any
\widetilde{\Phi}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
which acts trivially on the orthogonal complement of

M_{G}(-1)
induces
a unique automorphism
\Phi
of
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
that on
M_{G}
agrees with
\widetilde{\Phi}
and
that acts trivially on the orthogonal complement of
M_{G}
.
Recall from appendix
C.3

that
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
possesses a normal subgroup
G_{0}\times G_{1}
,
where
G_{0}

is the Weyl group of the root sublattice of
N
of type
A_{2}^{12}
, and
G_{1}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is generated by
the involution which acts by multiplication by
-1
on
N
; moreover, the quotient group

\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)/(G_{0}\times G_{1})
is isomorphic to
M_{12}
.
If
\widetilde{\Phi}\in G_{0}\times G_{1}
, then
\Phi
is
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-effective if
and only if it is the identity, by the definition of the notion of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-effectiveness.
More generally, by the same line of thought,
if a symmetry of
X
induces
\Phi\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}))
and

\widetilde{\Phi}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
, then the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-effectiveness of
\Phi
implies that
no two elements of the

(G_{0}\times G_{1})
-orbit of
\widetilde{\Phi}
can be induced by different symmetries of
X
.
This is a crucial step in Kondo’s proof of the fact that
any symmetry group
H
of a K3 surface can be viewed
as a subgroup of
M_{12}
if
M_{H}(-1)
can be primitively embedded in the Niemeier lattice
of type
A_{2}^{12}
, see
(
Kondo, 1998
, Proof of Theorem 4)
.

Below, by way of the variation of Kondo’s techniques mentioned above,
we construct an action of the symmetry group
G
of
X

on
N
which restricts to the representation induced on
\widetilde{P}

by the action of the symmetries of
X
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. The induced action of each
element of
G
is uniquely defined up to a contribution from
the Weyl group
S_{3}^{3}
of the lattice
\widetilde{K}
, a root lattice of type
A_{2}^{3}
.
In contrast to the setting in Kondo’s construction, the induced action on the discriminant
group of
\widetilde{P}
is not always trivial, meaning that we cannot always choose the identity
to consistently extend our lattice automorphisms to
\widetilde{K}
.
But we will see that in our setting it is straightforward to make choices which indeed yield a

G
-action, resulting in an injective group homomorphism
G\hookrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
.
Moreover, the choices made when inducing an automorphism of
N

from the action of a symmetry of
X
on
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
take
place solely within the group
G_{0}\times G_{1}
. Hence the projection from

\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(N)
to
M_{12}
is independent of these choices.

We begin by finding a representation of the translational symmetry
group of
X
on the lattice
N
:

Proposition 3.2.1

The translational symmetry group of

acts faithfully on the Niemeier lattice

,
such that the resulting subgroup of

is isomorphic to

with

,

,

\mathbb{Q}
-linearly extending the following maps:

\displaystyle\mbox{for }\ell\in\{1,2\},

Appendix A
Blowing up
A_{2}
singularities

This work investigates properties of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold K3 surfaces,
which can be obtained as minimal resolutions of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
quotients of
certain tori, where all singularities in the quotient are of a type called

A_{2}
. In this appendix, we recall some properties of these particular singularities,
and we showcase the minimal resolution of singularities of type
A_{2}
.
Though these techniques are well-known to algebraic geometers

Du Val (1934)
;
Artin (1966)
;
Hirzebruch (1966)
,
presenting the details
of the calculation makes our work self-contained, and it also serves us to introduce the notations that are used in the main part of the text.

Consider the following surface
S(A_{2})
in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
:

S(A_{2}):=\left\{(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\mid x_{0}^{3}=x_{1}x_{2}\right\}\;.

One checks that this surface is singular at the origin
x=(0,0,0)
and
smooth everywhere else; in fact, the origin is a

singularity of type
A_{2}
,
providing a local model of the standard
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifold singularity
[0]\in\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,
as can be seen as follows. First recall the standard
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
by
linear unitary, orientation preserving maps, which is generated by (
2
).
The origin
(z_{1},z_{2})=(0,0)
is the unique fixed point
of this action, and it descends to a singular point in
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.
One has an isomorphism of complex varieties

\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}\longrightarrow S(A_{2}),\qquad\left[(z_{1},z_{2})\right]\mapsto(z_{1}z_{2},z_{1}^{3},z_{2}^{3})\;,

which maps
[(z_{1},z_{2})]=[0]
to
x=(0,0,0)
, justifying our claim
that
S(A_{2})
is a model of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
.

In order to calculate a minimal resolution of an
A_{2}
-type singularity, the
model
S(A_{2})
is particularly convenient. Indeed, the blow up
\widetilde{S(A_{2})}

of

in the singular point

surface

\mathbb{C}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

.
To exhibit the geometry of
\widetilde{S(A_{2})}
,

Appendix B
Elliptic genus of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-orbifolds

In this appendix we compute the elliptic genus of the K3 surface
constructed from the
T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
quotient using orbifold conformal
field theory techniques. To this end we compute the elliptic genus
of the non-linear
\sigma
-model on
T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
. The elliptic genus is
an invariant of complex manifolds and so is the same for all K3
\sigma
-models.
It is
given by
Eguchi et al. (1989)

\mathcal{E}_{\rm K3}(\tau,z)=8\bigg[\Big(\frac{\vartheta_{2}(\tau,z)}{\vartheta_{2}(\tau,0)}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{\vartheta_{3}(\tau,z)}{\vartheta_{3}(\tau,0)}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{\vartheta_{4}(\tau,z)}{\vartheta_{4}(\tau,0)}\Big)^{2}\bigg]

(29)

where
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\vartheta_{i}
are the Jacobi theta functions

\vartheta_{(\alpha,\beta)}(\tau,z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\pi\tau(n+\alpha)^{2}+2i\pi(n+\alpha)(z+\beta)}

(30)

with the conventions

\begin{array}[]{rcrrcl}\vartheta_{1}(\tau,z)&:=&{-}\vartheta_{(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})}(\tau,z)\ ,&\vartheta_{2}(\tau,z)&:=&\vartheta_{(\frac{1}{2},0)}(\tau,z)\ ,\\
\vartheta_{3}(\tau,z)&:=&\vartheta_{(0,0)}(\tau,z)\ ,&\vartheta_{4}(\tau,z)&:=&\vartheta_{(0,\frac{1}{2})}(\tau,z)\ ,\end{array}

(31)

and
\vartheta_{i}(\tau):=\vartheta_{i}(\tau,0)
. In terms of the quantities
q:=e^{2\pi i\tau}
and
y:=e^{2\pi iz}
,
the elliptic genus (
29
) is expanded as

\mathcal{E}_{\rm K3}(\tau,z)=20+2y^{-1}+2y+q\bigg({20}y^{-2}-{128}y^{-1}+216-128y+20y^{2}\bigg)+O(q^{2})\ .

(32)

The elliptic genus of a K3
\sigma
-model is defined as
Witten (1987)
;
Eguchi et al. (1989)

\mathcal{E}(\tau,z)={\rm tr}_{{\rm R\widetilde{R}}}\Big(q^{L_{0}-\frac{1}{4}}y^{J_{0}}(-1)^{F}\overline{q}^{\widetilde{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{4}}(-1)^{\widetilde{F}}\Big)

(33)

where
L_{0}
and
J_{0}
give respectively the holomorphic conformal dimension
and
R
-charge of the states (the latter is under the
\mathfrak{su}(2)

R
-symmetry algebra)
and
F
is the fermion number operator. The tilde denotes the anti-holomorphic sector
and the subscript R refers to the Ramond sector. We next compute the elliptic
genus (
33
) for the
T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}

\sigma
-model. We verify that the
result agrees with eq. (
29
), i.e. the elliptic genus of K3 surfaces, as expected. This shows that the constructed
T/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
conformal field theory is indeed a K3
\sigma
-model.

Prior to the action of the orbifold, we have a
\sigma
-model on
T
, which is a theory of four real free bosons and four real free fermions. The elliptic genus of this conformal field theory is given by

\mathcal{E}_{T}(\tau,z)=\bigg(\frac{\vartheta_{1}(\tau,z)}{\eta(\tau)^{3}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg(\frac{\vartheta_{1}(\overline{\tau},\overline{z})}{\eta(\overline{\tau})^{3}}\bigg)^{2}Z_{\Gamma}(\tau)\Big|_{\overline{z}=0}

where
Z_{\Gamma}
is the contribution from the momentum and winding modes.

\mathcal{E}_{T}
vanishes because
\vartheta_{1}(\overline{\tau},\overline{z})|_{\overline{z}=0}=0
.

The action of the generator
g
of the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
orbifold group is defined by multiplying one pair of the
bosonic fields by an overall phase
\xi=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{3}}
and the other
pair by an overall phase
\xi^{-1}=e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{3}}
— see eq. (
2
).
The two pairs of fermionic fields
are treated similarly.
Given this action of
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
on the free bosons and fermions of the toroidal theory, one may deduce
a consistent
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-action on all fields of the sigma model which preserves the

C.3
The Niemeier lattice of type
A_{2}^{12}
and the Mathieu group
M_{12}

The groups of lattice automorphisms for indefinite lattices of
rank at least
3
are infinite (see e.g.
Vinberg (1975)
), while
for lattices with definite signature, as permutation groups
on every set of vectors of equal length, they are always finite.
Therefore, it can be advantageous to use positive definite lattices
when describing symmetry groups. Here, the Niemeier lattices
are of particular interest.

A
Niemeier lattice
is an even unimodular positive definite
lattice of rank
24
. According to Niemeier’s Theorem
(
Niemeier, 1973
, Satz 8.3)
,
up to isometries, there are
24
such lattices, and each
of them is uniquely determined by its
root lattice
, that is, by
the sublattice generated by all lattice vectors of length
\sqrt{2}
. The
Niemeier lattices may therefore be
labelled by the Dynkin diagrams of their root lattices of
rank
24
or, in the case of the Leech lattice, by
0
.

In this note, we are interested in the Niemeier lattice of type
A_{2}^{12}
,
which we denote by
N
.
The root lattice of
N
, which we call
\widetilde{R}
,
is given by the direct sum of twelve pairwise
perpendicular sublattices of type
A_{2}
, labelled as
A_{2,j}
,
j\in\quantity{1, \dots, 12}
. That is,

\widetilde{R}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{12}A_{2,j}\,.

We choose simple roots in
A_{2,j}
, denoted by
\widetilde{E}^{(l)}_{j}
,
l\in\quantity{1,2}
, such that

\displaystyle\forall\,j,k\in\quantity{1,\dots,12},\,l,m\in\quantity{1,2}\qquad\expectationvalue{ \widetilde{E}^{(l)}_j \, , \, \widetilde{E}^{(m)}_k }=\begin{cases}\,\,\,\,0&\text{if }j\neq k\,,\\
-1&\text{if }j=k,l\neq m\,,\\
\,\,\,\,2&\text{if }j=k,l=m\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, let
\widetilde{E}_{j}:=\widetilde{E}^{(1)}_{j}+2\widetilde{E}^{(2)}_{j}

for
j\in\quantity{1,\dots, 12}
; then for the sublattice
A_{2,j}
, the dual lattice

A_{2,j}^{\ast}
may be generated by
the
\widetilde{E}^{(l)}_{j}
with
l\in\quantity{1,2}

and
\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{E}_{j}
. One checks that
\left(\widetilde{E}_{j}^{(2)},\frac{1}{3}\widetilde{E}_{j}\right)

forms a basis of the dual lattice
A_{2,j}^{\ast}
, and that the shortest non-zero vectors
in this lattice have squared length
\frac{2}{3}
.

The Niemeier lattice
N
is obtained from the components
A_{2,j}
of
the root lattice
\widetilde{R}
by including a set of additional generators (also often called
glue vectors, as in
Conway and Sloane (1999)
;
Ebeling (2002)
for instance)
of the form

\displaystyle y=\textstyle\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{12}y_{j}\,\widetilde{E}_{j}\,,

where each
y_{j}
,
j\in\quantity{1, \dots, 12}
, corresponds to a
representative
\frac{1}{3}y_{j}\,\widetilde{E}_{j}

of an element
of the discriminant group
A_{2,j}^{*}/A_{2,j}

we either have
\widehat{r}\in\widehat{P}^{\ast}
or
\widehat{r}\perp\widehat{P}
.
If
\widehat{r}\in\widehat{P}
for all roots
\widehat{r}\in\widehat{P}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}
, then using the arguments
employed in the proof of proposition
3.1.4
, it follows that
\widehat{N}
is of type
A_{2}^{12}
and that
\widehat{P}

is a primitive sublattice.
Assume therefore that
\widehat{r}\in\widehat{P}^{\ast}\setminus\widehat{P}
is a root
of
\widehat{N}
.
To determine the smallest primitive sublattice of
\widehat{N}
which contains

\widehat{P}
, that is, to determine the lattice

(\widehat{P}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})\cap\widehat{N}=\widehat{P}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}

by proposition
2.3.3

\widehat{r}=-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{t\in L_{12}}\widehat{E}_{t}

\widehat{E}_{t}^{(j)}

Note that by proposition
2.3.3
, with
\widehat{P}\subset\widehat{N}
and

\widehat{r}\in\widehat{N}

\widehat{r}^{\prime}:=-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{t\in L^{\prime}}\widehat{E}_{t}\in\widehat{N}
for

\widehat{E}_{t}^{(j)}

We claim that the above implies that

by
\widehat{P}

\widehat{r}

\widehat{N}

\widehat{P}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}

E_{6}^{3}

From what we have shown so far,
we may confirm that the root lattice of
\widehat{N}
is of type
E_{6}^{3}\oplus\widehat{K}

where
\widehat{K}

6

\widehat{N}
is of type
E_{6}^{4}

It remains to show that there does exist a non-primitive embedding of
P(-1)

in the Niemeier lattice
\widehat{N}
of type
E_{6}^{4}

above already shows that there exists a root sublattice of type

in

which we may embed in
\widehat{N}

\widehat{N}
by
\widehat{R}
and extend the map
\mathbb{Q}

in
\widehat{N}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}

\widehat{P}
of
P(-1)

under this embedding is contained in
\widehat{N}
.
Since
P(-1)
does not contain a root lattice of

E_{6}^{3}
, this will also imply that the embedding of
P(-1)
is not primitive.

We use the same notations as above for the
images
\widehat{E}_{t}

\widehat{r}
and
\widehat{r}^{\prime}

of the respective lattice vectors
in
\widehat{N}

\widehat{N}

E_{6}^{4}
by including three additional generators, and
using the explicit form of these generators according to
(
Conway and Sloane, 1999

one shows that

\widehat{R}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}
is obtained by adding one further
generator to
\widehat{R}
. Concretely, for the lattice of type
E_{6}

which is generated
by
\widehat{E}_{t}^{(j)}
with
t\in L_{12}
and
j\in\{1,2\}

\widehat{r}=-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{t\in L_{12}}\widehat{E}_{t}

\mathbb{Z}_{3}

is represented by
\check{E}_{0}:=\frac{1}{3}\left(\widehat{E}_{00}-\widehat{E}_{01}\right)

Similarly for the two other
E_{6}
-type summands of
\widehat{R}

\check{E}_{1}:=\frac{1}{3}\left(\widehat{E}_{10}-\widehat{E}_{11}\right)

\check{E}_{2}:=\frac{1}{3}\left(\widehat{E}_{20}-\widehat{E}_{21}\right)

Now
\widehat{R}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}
is obtained from the lattice
\widehat{R}

by including the extra generator
\check{E}_{0}+\check{E}_{1}+\check{E}_{2}
.
To confirm that
\widehat{P}\subset\widehat{R}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}
, recall
from (
7

\widehat{P}

is generated by the roots
\widehat{E}_{t}^{(j)}
with

\widehat{R}\subset\widehat{R}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}

the images
\widehat{v}_{j}
of the vectors

v_{j}
,

. Hence with

\widehat{r}^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{t\in(1,0)+L_{12}}\widehat{E}_{t}

and
\widehat{r}^{\prime\prime}=-\frac{1}{3}\sum\limits_{t\in(2,0)+L_{12}}\widehat{E}_{t}

the claim

\widehat{P}\subset\widehat{R}^{\ast}\cap\widehat{N}
follows from

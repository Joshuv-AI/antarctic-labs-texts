Representation of Global Viscosity Solutions for Tonelli Hamiltonians
CHARFI Skander

Abstract
We consider the Lax-Oleinik operator 𝒯 \mathcal{T} associated with the non-stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation ∂ t u + H ⁡ ( t , x , ∂ x u ) = α 0 \partial_{t}u+H(t,x,\partial_{x}u)=\alpha_{0} for a Tonelli Hamiltonian H H and its Mañé critical value α 0 \alpha_{0} . It is known from the work of A. Fathi and J.N. Mather [ FM00 ] that the convergence of this semigroup fails in the non-autonomous framework. In this context, we study the action of 𝒯 \mathcal{T} on its non-wandering set Ω ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \Omega(\mathcal{T}) . First, we show that 𝒯 \mathcal{T} acts as an isometry on this set, and then we characterize Ω ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \Omega(\mathcal{T}) as the set of global viscosity solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, i.e. solutions that are defined for all real times. Next, we introduce a generalized Peierls barrier k ¯ \underline{k} and a set of generalized static classes 𝕄 ¯ \underline{\mathbb{M}} within the Mather set. Using these, we represent elements u u of Ω ⁡ ( 𝒯 ) \Omega(\mathcal{T}) as u ⁡ ( x ) = inf y ∈ 𝕄 ¯ { u ⁡ ( y ) + k ¯ ​ ( y , x ) } u(x)=\inf_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{u(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\} We apply this representation formula to prove Fathi’s convergence theorem for autonomous systems and provide a representation formula for n n -periodic viscosity solutions. Additionally, we establish that the dynamics of non-wandering viscosity solutions are governed by the Lagrangian flow on the Mather set. Specifically, we show that if the Mather set consists solely of N N -periodic orbits for some integer N N , then all non-wandering viscosity solutions are N N -periodic. Furthermore, we show that if the restriction of the Lagrangian flow to the Mather set is uniformly recurrent for a time sequence p n p_{n} , then all non-wandering viscosity solutions are uniformly recurrent for the same time sequence p n p_{n} .

The weak-KAM theory, initially developed in the autonomous framework by A.Fathi, focused on weak-KAM solutions, which are fixed points of the operator

The representation formula provides a description of any weak-KAM solutions

u(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{M}}\{\psi(y)+h^{\infty}(y,\cdot)\}

In the non-autonomous framework, P. Bernard and J.-M. Roquejoffre
[

As a result, it is reasonable to consider the set

of weak-KAM solutions. This paper aims to study the action of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}^{0,1}
on
\Omega(\mathcal{T}^{0,1})

We set

2.

More generally, if we denote by

For all times

From this proposition, we establish that all non-wandering elements of

Theorem 1.2

A viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
) is global if and only if it is non-wandering. In other words, the following equality holds

\Omega(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})

(1.3)

The idea of the proof is to first show that non-wandering viscosity solutions correspond to bounded global viscosity solutions. Then, we use the fact —pointed out to us by M. Zavidovique— that…

Proposition 1.3

For a Tonellu Hamiltonian, All the global viscosity solutions
u:\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}

) are bounded.

This tells us that the study of non-wandering viscosity solutions is equivalent to the study of global viscosity solutions.

2.1
Tonelli Hamiltonians and the Lax-Oleinik Operator

We define the notion of Tonelli Hamiltonians, initially introduced in
[
Mat91
]
. These are Hamiltonians that are convex and superlinear in the fibres, serving as the tame framework for the weak-KAM theory.

Definition 2.1
.

A
1
-time-periodic Hamiltonian
H(t,x,p):\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
is said
Tonelli
if it satisfies the following classical hypotheses :

•

Regularity:
H
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
.

•

Strict convexity:
\partial_{pp}H(t,x,p)>0
for all
(t,x,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times T^{*}M
.

•

Superlinearity:
H(t,x,p)/|p|\to\infty
as
|p|\to\infty
for each
(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times M
.

•

Completeness: The Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}(t,x,p)=(\partial_{p}H(t,x,p),-\partial_{x}H(t,x,p))
and hence its flow
\phi_{H}^{t,s}
is complete in the sense that the flow curves are defined for all times
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Under these assumptions, one can associate to

a time-periodic Tonelli
Lagrangian

L(t,x,v):\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
given by

L(t,x,v)=\max_{p\in T_{x}^{*}M}\{p(v)-H(t,x,p)\}

(2.1)

which symmetrically gives

H(t,x,p)=\max_{v\in T_{x}M}\{p(v)-L(t,x,v)\}

(2.2)

The
Euler-Lagrange flow
also named
Lagrangian flow

\phi_{L}^{s,t}
is the conjugate to the Hamiltonian flow
\phi_{H}^{s,t}
by the
Legendre map
1
1

1

The Tonelli assumptions imply that for all time

, the Legendre map

is a diffeomorphism between
\{t\}\times TM
and

[

]

\mathcal{L}(t,x,v)=\big(t,x,\partial_{v}L(t,x,v)\big)
. We adopt the notation
\phi_{L}^{t}
for
\phi_{L}^{0,t}
.

If

are two real times and
x
and
y
are two points of
M
, we define the following quantities

•

For all absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
, the

of

(2.3)

•

the

h_{0}^{s,t}(x,y)=\inf\left\{A_{L}(\gamma)\;\left|\;\begin{matrix}\gamma:&[s,t]\to M\\
&s\mapsto x\\

\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau=h_{0}^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))

(2.9)

Proposition 2.7
.

1.

If for some times
s<t
, the curve
\gamma
verifies (
2.9
), then it is minimizing on
[s,t]
.

2.

A minimizing curve
\gamma
is as regular as the Lagrangian

and it follows the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
i.e for all time
\tau\in[s,t]
,
(\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))=\phi_{L}^{s,\tau}(\gamma(0),\dot{\gamma}(0))
. These curves verify the Euler-Lagrange equation

\partial_{x}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))=\frac{d}{d\tau}\big(\partial_{v}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\big)

(2.10)

Remark 2.8
.

The first property is due to the fact that the quantity

\int_{s^{\prime}}^{t^{\prime}}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))-h_{0}^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}(\gamma(s^{\prime}),\gamma(t^{\prime}))\;d\tau\geq 0

.

A main brick in the study of Tonelli Lagrangians is the existence of minimizing curves.

Theorem 2.9
.

(Tonelli’s Theorem)
Let
L:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
be a Tonelli Lagrangian. Let
s<t
be two real times and
x
and
y
be two points of
M
. Then, there exists a minimizing curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
linking
x
to
y
.

A first application of this theorem to the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
gives the following

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
u
be a scalar map in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
. For all times
s<t
and for all point
x
of
M
, there exists a minimizing curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
such that

\gamma(t)=x\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u(x)=u(\gamma(0))+A_{L}(\gamma)

(2.11)

Proof.

We use the potential
h^{s,t}_{0}(\cdot,x)
introduced in (
2.4
). Recall that

\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u(x)=\inf_{y\in M}\{u(y)+h_{0}^{s,t}(y,x)\}

We will see in Proposition
2.13
that the map
y\mapsto h_{0}^{s,t}(y,x)
is continuous, and so is
y\mapsto u(y)+h_{0}^{s,t}(y,x)
on the compact manifold
M
. Thus, it admits a minimizing point

. We get

\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u(x)=u(y)+h_{0}^{s,t}(y,x)

An application of Tonelli’s theorem to
h_{0}^{s,t}(y,x)
completes the proof.
∎

2.3
A Priori Compactness and Regularity

Another fundamental theorem is the A priori compactness property of minimizing curves for Tonelli Lagrangians. It has been first proven by John N. Mather in
[
Mat91
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

(A Priori Compactness)
Let
L:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
be a Tonelli Lagrangian and fix a small positive
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists a compact subset
K_{\varepsilon}
of
TM
such that every minimizing curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
with
t-s\geq\varepsilon
verifies
(\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\in K_{\varepsilon}
.

Corollary 2.12
.

For fixed times
s<t
, if
\gamma_{n}:[s,t]\to M
is a sequence of minimizing curves, then it admits a subsequence that
C^{1}
-converges to a minimizing curve
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
.

Proof.

By A priori compactness, we can extract a subsequence
(\gamma_{k_{n}}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{k_{n}}(s))
that converges to
(x,v)\in TM
. Since the curves
\gamma_{n}
are minimizing, we have for all
\tau\in[s,t]
,
(\gamma_{n}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}_{n}(\tau))=\phi_{L}^{s,\tau}(\gamma_{n}(s),\dot{\gamma}_{n}(s))
. Set
\gamma(\tau)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{s,\tau}(x,v)
so that
(\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))=\phi_{L}^{s,\tau}(x,v)
. By continuity of the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
, we deduce the
C^{1}
-convergence of the curves
\gamma_{k_{n}}
to
\gamma
on the time interval
[s,t]
. By continuity of
h_{0}
obtained from proposition
2.13
, we obtain

\displaystyle h_{0}^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=\lim_{n}h_{0}^{s,t}(\gamma_{k_{n}}(s),\gamma_{k_{n}}(t))=\lim_{n}\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma_{k_{n}}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}_{k_{n}}(\tau))\;d\tau=\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau

where the last limit is due to the
C^{1}
-convergence. We conclude the the curve
\gamma
is minimizing.
∎

A consequence of the A Priori Compactness is the regularization property of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
.

Proposition 2.13
.

For all positive
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant
\kappa_{\varepsilon}>0
such that for all times
s<t
with
t-s\geq\varepsilon
, we have

1.

The potential
h_{0}^{s,t}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz. Moreover, we can take
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
so that the time dependent potential
h_{0}:\{0\leq s\leq t-\varepsilon\}\times M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is still
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz.

2.

For all initial data
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, the maps
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u
and
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u:M\to\mathbb{R}
is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz on the set
\{0\leq s\leq t-\varepsilon\}\times M
.

Consequently, we get a regularity result on viscosity solutions.

Corollary 2.14
.

For all viscosity solution
u:[s,+\infty)\times M\to\mathbb{R}
and
v:\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
), the families
(u(t,\cdot))_{t\geq s+1}
and
(v(t,\cdot))_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
are
\kappa_{1}
-equilipschitz.

2.4
Calibrated Curves

Calibrated curves represent a type of minimizing curves that are well adapted to a given viscosity solution in the following sense.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
u(t,x)
be a viscosity solution of (
1.1
). A curve
\gamma:I\subset\mathbb{R}\to M
defined on a real interval
I
is said
calibrated by
u
or
u
-calibrated
if for all times
s<t
of

\begin{split}u(t,\gamma(t))&=u(s,\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau+\alpha_{0}.(t-s)\\
&=u(s,\gamma(s))+h_{0}^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))+\alpha_{0}.(t-s)\\
&=u(s,\gamma(s))+h^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\end{split}

(2.12)

where the potential
h
will be defined in Subsection
2.6
.

Remark 2.16
.

1.

One observes from (
2.12
) that calibrated curves
\gamma
realize the infimum in the definition (
2.5
) of the Lax-Oleinik operator. This means that all calibrated curves are minimizing and do follow the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
.

2.

Same as for minimizing curves in Remark
2.8
, if a curve
\gamma
verifies (
2.12
) for some times
s<t
, then it verifies it for all
s\leq s^{\prime}<t^{\prime}\leq t
and
\gamma
is calibrated by
u
on the interval
[s,t]
.

3.

For viscosity solutions of the translated Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
2.8
), the good equation of calibration is the following

\begin{split}u(t,\gamma(t))&=u(s,\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau\\
&=u(s,\gamma(s))+h_{0}^{s,t}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\end{split}

(2.13)

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
u(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
be a viscosity solution of (
1.1
). For all points
x
of
M
and for all times
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
u
admits a calibrated curve
\gamma_{x}:(-\infty,t]\to M
with
\gamma(t)=x
.

Proof.

Fix a point
x
in
M
and a time
t
in
\mathbb{R}
. We know that for all
s<t
,
u(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u(s,\cdot)
. Then, applying Corollary
2.10
, we obtain curves
\gamma_{n}:[-n,t]\to M
with
\gamma_{n}(t)=x
, for large
n
, such that

u(t,\gamma_{n}(t))=u(s,\gamma_{n}(-n))+\int_{-n}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma_{n}(\tau),\dot{\gamma_{n}}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

The Remark
2.16
points out that these curves
\gamma_{n}
are
u
-calibrated .

The A priori compactness Theorem
2.11
says that the curves
(\gamma_{n},\dot{\gamma}_{n})
have their images in a same compact set
K
of
TM
. Thus, for all compact interval
C
of
(-\infty,t]
and for large enough integer
n_{0}
, the family
(\gamma_{n|C})_{n\geq n_{0}}
is relatively compact in the
C^{1}
-topology. Therefore, a diagonal arguments provides us with a curve
\gamma:(-\infty,t]\to M
with
\gamma(t)=x
such that the sequence
\gamma_{n}

C^{1}
-converges, up to extraction, to the curve
\gamma
on all compact subsets of
(-\infty,t]
.

Let
s\leq t
be a real time. For
n
large enough, we have from the calibration of
\gamma_{n}
that

u(t,\gamma_{n}(t))=u(s,\gamma_{n}(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma_{n}(\tau),\dot{\gamma_{n}}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

And taking the limit on

, we get the calibration equation for
\gamma
.

u(t,\gamma(t))=u(s,\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

∎

One of the powerful results of the weak-KAM theory is the theorem of regularity on calibrated curves proved by A.Fathi in the autonomous case (see
[
Fat08
]
). For the sake of completeness, we provide a proof in the non-autonomous case following
[
AV17
]
. But first, we introduce a tool derived from convex analysis.

Proposition 2.18
.

(Fenchel’s inequality)
For all
x
in
M
and all
(v,p)\in T_{x}M\times T^{*}_{x}M

p(v)\leq H(t,x,p)+L(t,x,v)

(2.14)

with equality if and only if
p=\partial_{v}L(x,v)
if and only if
v=\partial_{p}H(t,x,p)
.

Remark 2.19
.

Note that if
\phi^{t}_{H}(x)=(x(t),p(t))
is a curve that follows the Hamiltonian flow, then the Hamiltonian equations results in the equalities

\dot{x}(t)=\partial_{p}H\big(t,x(t),p(t)\big)\quad\text{and}\quad p(t)=\partial_{v}L\big(t,x(t),\dot{x}(t)\big)

(2.15)

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
u
be a viscosity solution of (
2.8
). If the curve
\gamma:I\to M
is calibrated by
u
, then for all time
t
in the interior of
I
,
u
is differentiable at
(t,\gamma(t))
with differential

\partial_{t}u(t,\gamma(t))=\alpha_{0}-H(t,\gamma(t),d_{x}u(t,\gamma(t)))\quad\text{and}\quad d_{x}u(t,\gamma(t))=\partial_{v}L\big(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)

(2.16)

Proof.

Differentiability.
Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a curve calibrated by
u
and fix
(t,x)=(t,\gamma(t))\in I\times M
. We will bound
u
in a neighbourhood of
(t,x)
by two
C^{1}
maps that coincide with it at this point. Consider a chart
B_{0}\subset M
around
x
and let
(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times B_{0}
be close enough to
(t,x)
. Fix two reference times
t^{+}<t<t^{-}
in
I
and
x^{\pm}=\gamma(t^{\pm})
. From the calibration

u(t,x)=u(t^{\pm},x^{\pm})+\int_{t^{\pm}}^{t}\Big(L\big(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau)\big)+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau=:\psi^{\pm}(t,x)

(2.17)

and from the definition of viscosity solutions, we have

u(s,y)\leq u(t^{+},x^{+})+\int_{t^{+}}^{s}\Big(L\big(\tau,\gamma^{+}_{(s,y)}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}^{+}_{(s,y)}(\tau)\big)+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau=:\psi^{+}(s,y)

(2.18)

and

u(s,y)\geq u(t^{-},x^{-})-\int_{s}^{t^{-}}\Big(L\big(\tau,\gamma^{-}_{(s,y)}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}^{-}_{(s,y)}(\tau)\big)+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau=:\psi^{-}(s,y)

(2.19)

where, in the chart
\mathbb{R}\times B_{0}
,

\gamma^{\pm}_{(s,y)}(\tau)=\gamma(\tau)+\frac{\tau-t^{\pm}}{s-t^{\pm}}(y-\gamma(s))

(2.20)

are smooth families of curves linking
(t^{\pm},x^{\pm})
to
(s,y)
and such that
\gamma^{\pm}_{(t,x)}=\gamma
.

It is easy to see that
\psi^{\pm}
are
C^{1}
. Moreover,
\psi^{-}\leq u\leq\psi^{+}
with equalities at
(t,x)
. Then
u
is differentiable at
(t,x)
.

Evaluation of the Differential.
We differentiate (
2.17
) with respect to time
t
without forgetting that
x=\gamma(t)
, to get

\partial_{t}u(t,\gamma(t))+d_{x}u(t,\gamma(t))=L\big(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)+\alpha_{0}

(2.21)

And by Fenchel inequality (
2.14
) for
x=\gamma(t)
,
v=\dot{\gamma}(t)
and
p=d_{x}u(t,\gamma(t))
, we have

0=\partial_{t}u(t,\gamma(t))+d_{x}u(t,\gamma(t)).\dot{\gamma}(t)-L\big(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)-\alpha_{0}\leq\partial_{t}u(t,x)+H(t,x,d_{x}u(t,x))-\alpha_{0}

(2.22)

We show the inverse inequality
\partial_{t}u(t,x)+H(t,x,d_{x}u(t,x))-\alpha_{0}\leq 0
. Let
v
be any element of
T_{x}M
and let
\sigma:[t,t+1]:\to M
be a curve such that
\sigma(0)=x
and
\dot{\sigma}(0)=v
. Since
u
is a viscosity solution, we have that for all
s\in[t,t+1]
,

\displaystyle\frac{u(s,\sigma(s))-u(t,x)}{s-t}\leq\frac{1}{s-t}\int_{t}^{s}\Big(L(\tau,\sigma(\tau),\dot{\sigma}(\tau)+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

Letting
s
tend to
t
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)+d_{x}u(t,\sigma(t)).v\leq L(t,x,v)+\alpha_{0}

Taking the supremum on
v\in T_{x}M
, we infer from the relation between
L
and
H
expressed in (
2.2
) the desired inequality
\partial_{t}u(t,x)+H(t,x,d_{x}u(t,x))-\alpha_{0}\leq 0
. Therefore, there is equality everywhere in (
2.22
), and in particular in the Fenchel inequality. Hence

\partial_{t}u(t,x)+H(t,x,d_{x}u(t,x))=\alpha_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad d_{x}u(t,\gamma(t))=\partial_{v}L\big(t,\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t)\big)

∎

2.5
The Mather Set

In this section, we present a concept derived from the Aubry-Mather theory. The Mather set, introduced by John N. Mather, is associated with the study of action-minimizing measures in Lagrangian and Hamiltonian systems. Some particular subsets specific to the non-autonomous case are defined as follows.

Definition 2.21
.

1.

A measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
is
a minimizing measure
if it is a Borel probability measure, invariant by the Euler-Lagrange flow
\phi_{L}
and it satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM}L\;d\mu=-\alpha_{0}

(2.23)

where
\alpha_{0}
is the Mañé critical value defined in (
2.6
) (and assumed to be null).

2.

The
Mather set

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\bigcup_{\mu}\supp(\mu)\subset\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM

(2.24)

where the union is on minimizing measures
\mu
.

3.

The
projected Mather set

\mathcal{M}
is the projection of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
to
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times M
.

4.

The
time-zero Mather set

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and its projected counterpart
\mathcal{M}_{0}
are the intersections

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}:=\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\cap\big(\{0\}\times TM\big)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\mathcal{M}\cap\big(\{0\}\times M\big)

(2.25)

seen respectively as subsets of
TM
and
M
.

5.

We define the
recurrent Mather set

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{R}\subset TM
by

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{R}=\{\tilde{x}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\;|\;\tilde{x}\text{ is recurrent under the map }\phi_{L}^{-1}\}

(2.26)

and we denote its projection on
M
by
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
.

Remark 2.22
.

1.

More explicitly, the invariant measures
\mu
featured in the definition are invariant by the maps
\Phi_{L}^{\tau}
, for all time
\tau>0
, given by

\begin{split}\Phi_{L}^{\tau}:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM&\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\\
(t,x,v)&\longmapsto(t+\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,t+\tau}(x,v))\end{split}

(2.27)

2.

We easily see from these definitions that the different Mather sets are invariant by either the Euler-Lagrange flow or its time-one map.

The next proposition has been proved by John N.Mather in the non-autonomous case. See proposition 4 of
[
Mat91
]

Proposition 2.23
.

The Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
is compact and non-empty and the recurrent Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{R}
is dense in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}

2.6
The Potential
h

For all times
s<t
we define the potential
h^{s,t}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
by

\begin{split}h^{s,t}(x,y)&=(t-s).\alpha_{0}+h_{0}^{s,t}(x,y)\\
&=(t-s).\alpha_{0}+\inf\left\{A_{L}(\gamma)\;\left|\;\begin{matrix}\gamma:&[s,t]\to M\\
&s\mapsto x\\
&t\mapsto y\end{matrix}\right.\right\}\end{split}

(2.28)

And we adopt the notation
h^{t}
for
h^{0,t}
.

Remark 2.24
.

1.

The Tonelli Theorem
2.9
states that the infimum in the definition above is always achieved by a minimizing curve.

2.

When the Mañé critical value
\alpha_{0}
is null, we get
h^{s,t}=h_{0}^{s,t}
.

3.

We deduce from Proposition
2.13
the Lipschitz regularity of the potential
h^{s,t}
.

Proposition 2.25
.

1.

(Triangular Inequality) For all real time
s<\tau<t
, and for all points
x
,
y
and
z
in
M
, we have the triangular inequality

h^{s,t}(x,z)\leq h^{s,\tau}(x,y)+h^{\tau,t}(y,z)

(2.29)

2.

For all
x\in M
,
h^{t}(x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
) i.e. for all times
0<s<t
,
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}h^{s}(x,\cdot)=h^{t}(x,\cdot)
.

Proof.

1.
Let
\gamma_{1}:[s,\tau]\to M
be a curve linking
x
to
y
and
\gamma_{2}:[\tau,t]\to M
be a curve linking
y
to
z
. And let
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
be their concatenated curve linking
x
to
z
. Then, we have

\displaystyle h^{s,t}(x,y)\leq(t-s).\alpha_{0}+A_{L}(\gamma)=(\tau-s).\alpha_{0}+A_{L}(\gamma_{1})+(t-\tau).\alpha_{0}+A_{L}(\gamma_{2})

and taking the infinimum on the curves
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, we get the wanted inequality.

2.
For
s<t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,y\in M
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{s,t}h^{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{matrix}\gamma_{1}:[s,t]\rightarrow M\\
t\mapsto y\end{matrix}}\left\{h^{s}\big(x,\gamma_{1}(s)\big)+A_{L}(\gamma_{1})+(t-s).\alpha_{0}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{matrix}\gamma_{1}:[s,t]\rightarrow M\\
t\mapsto y\end{matrix}}\inf_{\begin{matrix}\gamma_{2}:[0,s]\rightarrow M\\
0\mapsto x\\
s\mapsto\gamma_{1}(s)\end{matrix}}\left\{s.\alpha_{0}+A_{L}(\gamma_{2})+A_{L}(\gamma_{1})+(t-s).\alpha_{0}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{matrix}\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow M\\
0\mapsto x\\
t\mapsto y\end{matrix}}\left\{t.\alpha_{0}+A_{L}(\gamma)\right\}=h^{t}(x,y)

∎

We will show that this potential

is bounded for
t-s>\varepsilon
for some fixed
\varepsilon
. To achieve this, we need to consider its lower and upper bounds.

Definition 2.26
.

We define the maps
m
and
M:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
as

m(x,y)=\inf_{n\geq 1}h^{n}(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad M(x,y)=\sup_{n\geq 1}h^{n}(x,y)

(2.30)

The associated time-dependant maps are defined as

m^{t}(x,y)=m(t,x,y)=\inf_{n\geq 1}h^{n+t}(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad M^{t}(x,y)=M(t,x,y)=\sup_{n\geq 1}h^{t+n}(x,y)

(2.31)

And more generally, for every two times
s<t
, we define

m^{s,t}(x,y)=\inf_{n\geq 1}h^{s,n+t}(x,y)\quad\text{and}\quad M^{s,t}(x,y)=\sup_{n\geq 1}h^{s,t+n}(x,y)

(2.32)

Remark 2.27
.

There are some subtleties that justify taking the infimum over
n\geq 1
while excluding
n=0
. However, for the purpose of the following proposition, the chosen definition is sufficient.

Proposition 2.28
.

For all
(t,x,y)\in[0,+\infty)\times M\times M
, the maps
m(t,x,y)
and
M(t,x,y)
are finite, and at
t=0
we have the uniform bound

\max(\|m\|_{\infty},\|M\|_{\infty})\leq 2\kappa_{1}.\diam(M)

(2.33)

where
\kappa_{1}
is the Lipschitz constant introduced in Proposition
2.13
.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is divided into two main steps. The first step is to find points
x_{n}
and
y_{n}
such that
\frac{1}{n}h^{n}(x_{n},y_{n})
converges to a finite limit using the ergodic decomposition and the A Priori Compactness Theorem
2.11
. The second step is to estimate the oscillation of
h
with respect to this established finite limit using the Lipschitz regularity of the potential
2.13
.

Step 1.
Let
\mu
be a minimizing measure. We infer from proposition
2.23
that
supp(\mu)
is compact. We will need the ergodic decomposition of invariant measures. For that purpose, we introduce some definitions following
[
Mn87
]
.

-

Let
\Sigma^{0}
be the set of points
(t,x,v)\in\supp(\mu)
such that for every continuous map
\theta:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to\mathbb{R}
, the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\theta(t+\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,t+\tau}(x,v))\;d\tau

exists and is finite.

-

For all points
(t,x,v)
of
\Sigma^{0}

Let

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}\theta(\tau,\phi_{L}^{\tau}(x,v))\;d\tau

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{n}L(\tau,\phi_{L}^{\tau}(x,v))\;d\tau=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM}L\;d\mu_{(0,x,v)}=-\alpha_{0}

Now let

3
Action of the Lax-Oleinik Operator
\mathcal{T}
on its Non-Wandering Set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})

In this section, we prove the non-expansiveness of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
and examine its implications for viscosity solutions and their stability. Additionally, we explore the consequences on the non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and provide a characterization of non-wandering viscosity solutions as global viscosity solutions.

3.1
Non-Expansiveness and Boundedness of
\mathcal{T}

We begin with a proof of the well-known non-expansiveness of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all time
t>0
, The time
t
Lax-Oleinik operators
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}
and
\mathcal{T}^{t}
are non-expanding, i.e

\forall u,v\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R}),\quad\|\mathcal{T}^{t}u-\mathcal{T}^{t}v\|_{\infty}=\|\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}u-\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}v\|_{\infty}\leq\|u-v\|_{\infty}

(3.1)

In particular, the Lax-Oleinik operators
\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
\mathcal{T}
are non-expanding.

Proof.

First, note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{t}u-\mathcal{T}^{t}v=\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}u+\alpha_{0}.t-\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}v-\alpha_{0}.t=\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}u-\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}v

We prove the non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}
. Let
x\in M
. Corollary
2.10
provides with a minimizing curve
\sigma:[0,t]\to M
with
\sigma(t)=x
realizing the infimum in the definition (
2.5
) of
\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}v(x)
. Hence we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}u(x)-\mathcal{T}_{0}^{t}v(x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{matrix}\gamma:[0,t]\rightarrow M\\
t\mapsto x\end{matrix}}\left\{u(\gamma(0))+\int_{0}^{t}L(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\;ds\right\}-\left(v(\sigma(0))+\int_{0}^{t}L(s,\sigma(s),\dot{\sigma}(s))\;ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(u(\sigma(0))+\int_{0}^{t}L(s,\sigma(s),\dot{\sigma}(s))\;ds\right)-\left(v(\sigma(0))+\int_{0}^{t}L(s,\sigma(s),\dot{\sigma}(s))\;ds\right)

\displaystyle\leq u(\sigma(0))-v(\sigma(0))\leq\|u-v\|_{\infty}

Since
u
and
v
play symmetric roles, we get the lacking inequality.
∎

As a direct consequence, we obtain the forward-time boundedness of all viscosity solutions.

Corollary 3.2
.

For all
(s,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, the family
\big(u(t,x)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u\big)_{t\geq s}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Fix an element
(s,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Let
v
be a weak-KAM solution which existence will be proved in Corollary
3.7
. By the non-expansiveness Proposition
3.1
, we get for all
t\geq s

\displaystyle||\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u-\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\mathcal{T}^{s}v||_{\infty}\leq||u-\mathcal{T}^{s}v||_{\infty}

And we know from the definition of weak-KAM solutions that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\mathcal{T}^{s}v=\mathcal{T}^{t}v=\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor t\rfloor,t}\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor t\rfloor}v=\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor t\rfloor,t}v=\mathcal{T}^{t-\lfloor t\rfloor}v

Hence, we obtain from the continuity in time of
\mathcal{T}^{\tau}v
that

\displaystyle||\mathcal{T}^{s,t}u||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq||u-\mathcal{T}^{s}v||_{\infty}+||\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\mathcal{T}^{s}v||_{\infty}=||u-\mathcal{T}^{s}v||_{\infty}+||\mathcal{T}^{t-\lfloor t\rfloor}v||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq||u-\mathcal{T}^{s}v||_{\infty}+\sup_{\tau\in[0,1]}||\mathcal{T}^{\tau}v||_{\infty}<+\infty

∎

Remark 3.3
.

This boundedness result offers a characterization of the Mañé critical value
\alpha_{0}
. Indeed,
\alpha_{0}
is the unique real constant
c
such that there exists (or for all) scalar map
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, the family
\big(u_{c}(t,x)=\mathcal{T}_{0}^{s,t}u+c.(t-s)\big)_{t\geq s}
is uniformly bounded.

3.2
Stability of Viscosity Solutions

We present stability results for viscosity solutions of a Tonelli Hamiltonian. The stability of limits given by Proposition
3.5
remains valid in more general frameworks. For a more detailed exposition on stability results for viscosity solutions, see
[
Bar94
]
. However, in the Tonelli framework, the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
allows for the stability of both the infimum and the liminf.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(v_{i}:[s,t]\times M\to\mathbb{R})_{i\in I}
be a family of viscosity solutions and let
u(t,x)=\inf_{i\in I}\{v_{i}(t,x)\}
. Then
u
is a viscosity solution.

Proof.

Fix two real times
s\leq s^{\prime}<t^{\prime}\leq t
. Then, for all
x\in M
the following computation holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}u(s^{\prime},x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\big\{u(s^{\prime},y)+h^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}(y,x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in M}\big\{\inf_{i\in I}\{v_{n}(s^{\prime},y)\}+h^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}(y,x)\big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{i\in I}\;\inf_{y\in M}\Big\{v_{n}(s^{\prime},y)+h^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}(y,x)\Big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{i\in I}\big\{\mathcal{T}^{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}v_{n}(s^{\prime},x)\big\}=\inf_{n\geq 0}\{v_{n}(t^{\prime},x)\}=u(t^{\prime},x)

∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
v_{n}
be a sequence of scalar maps in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
that converges to
v
. Then, for all times
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the associated viscosity solutions
v_{n}:[s,+\infty)\times M\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
v_{n}(t,x)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v_{n}(x)
converge to the viscosity solution
v(t,x)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v(x)
in the
C^{0}
topology.

Proof.

For all times
s<t
, we have by non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}
that

\|v_{n}(t,\cdot)-v(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}=\|\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v_{n}-\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v\|_{\infty}\leq\|v_{n}-v\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

(3.2)

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a scalar map with associated viscosity solution
u(t,x):[0,+\infty)\times M\to\mathbb{R}
and let
(k_{n})_{n}
be an increasing sequence of integers. We define the map
v:\mathbb{R}\times M\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

v(t,x)=\liminf_{n}u(t+k_{n},x)

(3.3)

If
v
is everywhere finite, then it is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

Proof.

We start by proving that it is a sub-solution i.e. for all times
s\leq t
,
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v(s,\cdot)\leq v(t,\cdot)
. Fix
(t,x)
in
\mathbb{R}\times M
and subsequence
k_{n_{i}}
of
k_{n}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{t+k_{n_{i}}}u(x)
converges to
v(t,x)
. For every integer
i
, we get from Tonelli’s Theorem
2.9
a minimizing curve
\gamma_{i}:[s,t]\to M
which realizes
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}(\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u)(x)
i.e. such that
\gamma_{i}(t)=x
and

\mathcal{T}^{t+k_{n_{i}}}u(x)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}(\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u)(x)=\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma_{i}(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma_{i}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}_{i}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

(3.4)

These curves
\gamma_{i}
are minimizing. Thus, by Corollary
2.12
, we can assume up to extraction that the curves
\gamma_{i}
converge to
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
in the
C^{1}
-topology with
\gamma(t)=\gamma_{i}(t)=x
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle v(t,x)=\lim\limits_{i}\mathcal{T}^{t+k_{n_{i}}}u(x)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{i}\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma_{i}(s))+\lim\limits_{i}\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma_{i}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}_{i}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

\displaystyle=\liminf_{i}\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma_{i}(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

However, we know from Corollary
2.14
that the family
(\mathcal{T}^{t}u)_{t}
is equicontinuous, which yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma_{i}(s))-\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma(s))|\leq\kappa_{1}.|u(\gamma_{i}(s))-u(\gamma(s))|\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

Hence,

\liminf_{i}\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma_{i}(s))=\liminf_{i}\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n_{i}}}u(\gamma(s))\geq\liminf_{n}\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n}}u(\gamma(s))=v(s,\gamma(s))

and

\displaystyle v(t,x)

\displaystyle\geq v(s,\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau\geq\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v(s,x)

We now establish the inverse inequality. Let
\gamma:[s,t]\to M
be any curve with
\gamma(t)=x
. We know from the definition of the Lax-Oleinik operator that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{t+k_{n}}u(x)\leq\mathcal{T}^{s+k_{n}}u(\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

and taking the liminf, we get that for each such curve
\gamma
,

v(t,x)\leq v(s,\gamma(s))+\int_{s}^{t}\Big(L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

which gives the desired inequality

v(t,x)\leq\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v(s,x)

∎

.

There exists a weak-KAM solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (

).

Proof.

Fix a point

in

3.3
Restriction to the Non-Wandering Set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})

We explore the implications of the non-expansiveness of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
on its non-wandering and recurrent sets. We start by introducing the definitions of some asymptotic objects of
\mathcal{T}
.

To a scalar map
u
of
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, we associate its
\omega
-limit set

\omega(u):=\omega_{\mathcal{T}}(u)
under the operator
\mathcal{T}
as the set of limit points in the
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
of the sequence
(\mathcal{T}^{n}(u))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. More precisely

\omega(u)=\{v\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})\;|\;\exists(k_{n})_{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\text{ increasing sequence s.t }\|\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}u-v\|_{\infty}\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty\}

(3.6)

Definition 3.9
.

1.

The
recurrent set

\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
is the set of
\mathcal{T}
-recurrent elements of
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
i.e. the set of
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that
u
belongs to
\omega(u)
.

2.

Let
\underline{p}=(p_{n})_{n}
be an increasing of
\mathbb{N}
. An element
u
of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
is said
\underline{p}
-recurrent
if
\lim_{n}\mathcal{T}^{p_{n}}u=u
.

3.

The
non-wandering set

\Omega(\mathcal{T})
of
\mathcal{T}
is the set of elements
u
of
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
such that for every neighbourhood
U
of
u
in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, there are infinitely many positive integers
k
such that
\mathcal{T}^{k}(U)\cap U\neq\emptyset
.

Remark 3.10
.

Every element
u
of
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
or
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
corresponds to a recurrent or a non-wandering (for integer times) viscosity solution
u(t,x)
. By abuse of language, we will often refer to the sets
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
and
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
as the sets of recurrent and non-wandering viscosity solutions, respectively.

As a consequence of the non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{T}
shown in Proposition
3.1
, we get the following properties

Proposition 3.11
.

1.

Let
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Then, its
\omega
-limit set
\omega(u)
is compact in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
, and the restriction of
\mathcal{T}
to
\omega(u)
is minimal, i.e. for all
v\in\omega(u)
,
\omega(u)=\overline{\{\mathcal{T}^{n}v\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}}=\omega(v)
.

2.

The non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is equal to the recurrent set
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})
.

3.

The relation
u\sim v\Leftrightarrow v\in\omega(u)
is an equivalence relation. If we denote by
\Lambda
the set of its equivalence classes, then we have

\Omega(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})=\bigsqcup_{u\in\Lambda}\omega(u)

(3.7)

where the union is disjoint.

Proof.

1
. The set
\omega(u)
is closed and
\mathcal{T}
-invariant. Let us show that it is bounded in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
. For all positive integer
n\geq 0
and for all
x
in
M
, if we denote by
y
the point that realizes the infimum in
\mathcal{T}^{n}u(x)
, then we have

|\mathcal{T}^{n}u(x)|=|u(y)+h^{n}(y,x)|\leq\|v\|_{\infty}+\max(\|m\|_{\infty},\|M\|_{\infty})\leq\|u\|_{\infty}+\kappa_{1}.2\diam(M)

where we used the bound on
m=\inf_{n}h^{n}
and
M=\sup_{n}h^{n}
found in Proposition
2.28
. Taking limits on
n
, we observe that this bound holds for every element of
\omega(u)
. Moreover, since we know from Corollary
2.14
that the maps
(u(t,\cdot))_{t\geq 1}
are equilipschitz, we infer that all the elements of
\omega(u)
are
\kappa_{1}
-lipschitz. Therefore, the Arzéla-Ascoli theorem asserts that this set is compact in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

We show the minimality property. Let
v\in\omega(u)
and
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of integers such that
\mathcal{T}^{n_{k}}u
converges to
v\in\omega(u)
. The set
\omega(u)
is closed and
\mathcal{T}
-invariant. Hence, we have
\overline{\{\mathcal{T}^{n}v\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}}\subset\omega(u)
.

To prove the inverse inclusion, fix
w\in\omega(u)
. There exists an increasing sequence
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{n_{k}+p_{k}}u\to w
. The non-expansiveness (
3.1
) of the semi-group
\mathcal{T}
results in

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}v-w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{p_{k}}v-\mathcal{T}^{n_{k}+p_{k}}u\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{T}^{n_{k}+p_{k}}u-w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|v-\mathcal{T}^{n_{k}}u\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{T}^{n_{k}+p_{k}}u-w\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }k\to\infty

Hence,
w\in\overline{\{\mathcal{T}^{n}v\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}}
.

By closedness and
\mathcal{T}
-invariance, we know that
\omega(v)\subset\omega(u)
. Hence, the minimality gives the inverse inclusion and the equality of sets.

2
. The inclusion
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T})\subset\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is immediate. We need to prove the inverse inclusion. Let
u\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
be a non-wandering element under
\mathcal{T}
. We aim to prove that it is recurrent. For all positive integer
n>0
, consider the set
U_{n}=\big\{v\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})\;|\;||v-u||_{\infty}<\frac{1}{n}\big\}
. Using the non-wandering property of
u
, we inductively construct an increasing sequence of positive integers
k_{n}
such that
\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}U_{n}\cap U_{n}\neq\emptyset
. Let
v_{n}
be an element of
U_{n}\cap\mathcal{T}^{-k_{n}}U_{n}\neq\emptyset
. The properties on
U_{n}
translate on
v_{n}
as follows

||v_{n}-u||_{\infty}<\frac{1}{n}\quad\text{and}\quad||\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v_{n}-u||_{\infty}<\frac{1}{n}

(3.8)

Thus

\displaystyle||\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}u-u||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq||\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}u-\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v_{n}||_{\infty}+||\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v_{n}-u||_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq||u-v_{n}||_{\infty}+||\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v_{n}-u||_{\infty}<\frac{2}{n}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

where we used the non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}
in the second line. We deduce that the sequence
(\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}u)_{n}
converges uniformly to
u
, meaning that
u
belongs to
\omega(u)
. This is the definition of recurrence.

3
. The fact that
\sim
is an equivalence relation is due to the minimality of
\mathcal{T}
on
\omega(u)
. And the equalities follow immediately from the two first properties.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

1.

It follows from (
3.7
) that the limit points of any viscosity solutions of (

) belong to a non-wandering element of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
.

2.

As an implication of the minimality on
\omega(u)
, we have that if
\omega(u)
contains a periodic orbit, then it is equal to the orbit itself.

On every
\omega
-limit set
\omega(u)
, the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
is non-expanding and surjective in a compact set. This implies that
\mathcal{T}
is a bijective isometry on
\omega(u)
. The Proposition
1.1
generalizes this result to the entire non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and allows for the definition of its inverse operator within it. We obtain a group
(\mathcal{T}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of isometries acting on

.

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

Let
v
be an element of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. By recurrence, we know that there exists an increasing sequence of integers such that
\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}(v)
converges to
v
. Let
v^{\prime}
be a limit point in
\omega(v)
of the sequence
\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}-1}(v)
. This exists due to the compactness of
\omega(v)
stated in the Property
1
of Proposition
3.11
. Then, by continuity of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
, we get
\mathcal{T}v^{\prime}=v
. We showed that the restriction of
\mathcal{T}
to
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is onto.

Let us show that it is a bijective isometry on this set. Let
v
and
w
be two elements of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and consider a sequences
(v_{n},w_{n})
defined inductively as
(v_{0},w_{0})=(\mathcal{T}v,\mathcal{T}w)
,
(v_{1},w_{1})=(v,w)
and for all integer
n\geq 1
,
(v_{n+1},w_{n+1})\in\mathcal{T}^{-1}(v_{n},w_{n})
. Let
k_{n}
be an increasing sequence of integers such that
\lim_{n}k_{n+1}-k_{n}=+\infty
and
(v_{k_{n}},w_{k_{n}})
converge to a point of the compact set
\omega(v)\times\omega(w)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|v_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}-v_{0}\|_{\infty}=\|\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v_{k_{n+1}}-\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v_{k_{n}}\|_{\infty}\leq\|v_{k_{n+1}}-v_{k_{n}}\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

giving the limit
\lim_{n}v_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}=v_{0}=\mathcal{T}v
. By symmetry, we also have
\lim_{n}w_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}=w_{0}=\mathcal{T}w
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}v-\mathcal{T}w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|v-w\|_{\infty}=\|v_{1}-w_{1}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{T}^{k_{n+1}-k_{n}-1}v_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}-\mathcal{T}^{k_{n+1}-k_{n}-1}w_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}-w_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow\|\mathcal{T}v-\mathcal{T}w\|_{\infty}\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

The double inequality yields the isometric identity

\|\mathcal{T}v-\mathcal{T}w\|_{\infty}=\|v-w\|_{\infty}

The injectivity, and hence the bijectivity of
\mathcal{T}
follow immediately.

For the general case, it suffices to see that for all times
s<t
, we have
\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,\lceil t\rceil}=\mathcal{T}^{t,\lceil t\rceil}\circ\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}
where

and

respectively stand for the floor and the ceil maps. Thus, for any

and

, we get

\displaystyle\|v-w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,\lceil t\rceil}v-\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,\lceil t\rceil}w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{T}^{t,\lceil t\rceil}\circ\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}v-\mathcal{T}^{t,\lceil t\rceil}\circ\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}v-\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}w\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}v-\mathcal{T}^{\lfloor s\rfloor,s}w\|_{\infty}\leq\|v-w\|_{\infty}

We deduce equality everywhere, and the general result follows.

If
s<\tau<t
, then we have immediately that
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}=\mathcal{T}^{\tau,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{s,\tau}
. If for example,
s<t<\tau

and

∎

3.4
Bounded Global Viscosity Solutions

This subsection is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.2
, which characterizes the non-wandering elements of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
as corresponding to global viscosity solutions.

Definition 3.13
.

1.

A
global viscosity solution
is a viscosity solution
u(t,x)
defined for all times
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

We say that a scalar map
u\in\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
is
global
if it can be associated to a global viscosity solution
u(t,x)
with
u(0,\cdot)=u
. The set of global maps is denoted by
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})
.

3.

We define the set
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
of global maps
u
in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
which can be associated to a bounded global viscosity solution
u:\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
with
u(0,\cdot)=u
.

We aim to prove that the non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is equal to the set of global maps
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})
. A first inclusion is given by the following proposition.

Proposition 3.14
.

Every non-wandering element
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is global and corresponds to a unique global viscosity solution
v(t,x)
defined by

v(t,x)=\mathcal{T}^{t}v(x)=\lim_{n}\mathcal{T}^{t+k_{n}}v(x)

(3.9)

where
k_{n}
is a sequence such that
v=\lim_{n}v(k_{n},\cdot)
and where the limit is uniform on
t
.

Proof.

Let us show uniqueness. If
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are two global viscosity solutions with initial date
v_{1}(0,\cdot)=v_{2}(0,\cdot)=v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. Then for all positive times
t>0
, we have
v_{1}(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{t}v=v_{2}(0,\cdot)
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{-t,0}v_{1}(-t,\cdot)=v_{1}(0,\cdot)=v=v_{2}(0,\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{-t,0}v_{2}(-t,\cdot)

So that, by applying
\mathcal{T}^{t}=(\mathcal{T}^{-t,0})^{-1}
, we obtain
v_{1}=v_{2}
.

Now set
v_{1}(t,x)=\mathcal{T}^{t}v(x)
. We have for all
s<t
, and by Proposition
1.1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{s,t}v_{1}(s,x)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\circ\mathcal{T}^{s}v(x)=\mathcal{T}^{t}v(x)=v_{1}(t,x)

Hence,
v_{1}
is a global viscosity solution of (
1.1
). Moreover, since
\mathcal{T}^{t}
is isometric on
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
, we get the limit

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{t+k_{n}}v-\mathcal{T}^{t}v\|_{\infty}=\|\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v-v\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

yielding the second equality with uniform convergence in time.
∎

The opposite inclusion
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{T})\subset\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is less trivial. Our idea is to use the intermediary set
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
of bounded global maps. The key proposition is stated as below.

Proposition 3.15
.

A global viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation
1.1
is bounded if and only if it is non-wandering. In other words, the following equality holds

\Omega(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})

(3.10)

Proof.

We show double inclusion. We start with
\Omega(\mathcal{T})\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
. Let
v
be in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. By Proposition
3.11
, we infer that for any integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
v(n,\cdot)
belongs to the compact set
\omega(v)
. Hence, for all real time
t
, we have

\displaystyle v(t,\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{t-\lfloor t\rfloor}v(\lfloor t\rfloor,\cdot)\in\mathcal{T}^{t-\lfloor t\rfloor}(\omega(v))\subset\mathcal{T}^{[0,1]}(\omega(v))

where the last set is compact in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
due to time continuity of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}^{t}
. We deduce that
v
belongs to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
.

Now let
v
be in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
. The famility
(v(-n,\cdot))_{n\geq 0}
is bounded by definition of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T})
and equicontinuous by Corollary
2.14
. Hence, applying the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, there exists an increasing sequence of integers
k_{n}
such that
\lim_{n}k_{n+1}-k_{n}=+\infty
and
v(-k_{n},\cdot)
converges to a scalar map
v_{\alpha}
in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Set
k^{\prime}_{n}=k_{n+1}-k_{n}
. We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}-v(-k_{n},\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|v(-k_{n},\cdot)-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}-\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v(-k_{n+1},\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|v(-k_{n},\cdot)-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{\alpha}-v(-k_{n+1},\cdot)\|_{\infty}+\|v(-k_{n},\cdot)-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

where we used the non-expansiveness of
\mathcal{T}
. Hence,
\lim_{n}\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}
and
v_{\alpha}
belongs to
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. Consequently, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v-v\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}+k_{n}}v(-k_{n},\cdot)-\mathcal{T}^{k_{n}}v(-k_{n},\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v(-k_{n},\cdot)-v(-k_{n},\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v(-k_{n},\cdot)-\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}+\|v_{\alpha}-v(-k_{n},\cdot)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|v(-k_{n},\cdot)-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}+\|\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v_{\alpha}-v_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}+\|v_{\alpha}-v(-k_{n},\cdot)\|_{\infty}\quad\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

Therefore,
\lim_{n}\mathcal{T}^{k^{\prime}_{n}}v=v
and
v
belongs to
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
.

∎

Remark 3.16
.

Note that this proposition implies that for a viscosity solution, being recurrent in positive times is equivalent to being recurrent in negative times.

It remains to show the equality of the sets

.

Proof of Proposition

.

We need to show that a global viscosity solution

is bounded. Positive time boundedness is given by Corollary

. For negative time boundedness, we fix a time

and a point

. Let
y^{\prime}
be an arbitrary point of

and consider the point

such that

u(0,y^{\prime})=u(t,y)+h^{t,0}(y,y^{\prime})

Then, using the
\kappa_{1}
-lipschitz regularity of both

and

seen in Proposition

and Corollary

, we get

\displaystyle|u(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq|u(t,x)-u(t,y)|+|u(t,y)|

where we used the inequality of Proposition

on the map

.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Combining Propositions

and

4
The Uniqueness Theorem in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})

This section is dedicated to proving a Uniqueness Theorem
4.4
for non-wandering viscosity solutions on the Mather set. This theorem will play a central role in the various representation formulas presented in the next sections, allowing to determine non-wandering viscosity solutions based on their restrictions to the Mather set.

This theorem was initially established by A. Fathi for weak-KAM solutions
\fix(\mathcal{T})
in the autonomous framework (see
[
Fat08
]
) and by M. Zavidovique for the discrete case (see
[
Zav23
]
). Subsequently, P. Bernard and J.-M. Roquejoffre extended it to non-wandering viscosity solutions in
[
BR04
]
. We offer an alternative proof of their version, which is a more classical approach adapted from A. Fathi’s original proof.

4.1
Calibration on the Mather Set
\mathcal{M}

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
x
be an element of the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
with lift
\tilde{x}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to M
be the projection on
M
of the Lagrangian flow at
\tilde{x}
i.e.
\gamma(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
. Then every recurrent viscosity solution
v
in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
is calibrated by the curve
\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
v
be an element
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. Let
k_{n}
be a sequence of integers such that
v(k_{n},\cdot)
converges to
v
in
\mathcal{C}(M,\mathbb{R})
. We know from the definition of viscosity solutions that
\mathcal{T}^{t}v=v(t,\cdot)
. Thus, for all real time
t
and all point
\tilde{y}
of
TM
, the definition of the operator
\mathcal{T}
gives

v\big(t+k_{n},\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t,t+k_{n}}(\tilde{y})\big)-v\big(t,\pi\circ\tilde{y}\big)\leq A_{L}(\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t,\tau}(\tilde{y}))=\int_{t}^{t+k_{n}}\Big(L(\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,\tau}(\tilde{y}))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau

(4.1)

Let
\mu
be a minimizing measure on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
that has
\tilde{x}
in its support
\supp(\mu)
. We keep the same notation
\mu
for its time-one periodic lift
\mu
to
\mathbb{R}\times TM
. We integrate (
4.1
) in
(t,\tilde{y})\in[0,1]\times TM
with respect to the lift
\mu
.

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v\big(t+k_{n},\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t,t+k_{n}}(\tilde{y})\big)\;d\mu-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(t,\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu\\
\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}\int_{t}^{t+k_{n}}\Big(L(\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,\tau}(\tilde{y}))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau\;d\mu

(4.2)

We computate of the right-hand side while taking in consideration the time periodicity of the Lagrangian
L
and the
\Phi_{L}^{\tau}
-invariance of
\mu
where
\Phi_{L}^{k_{n}}
has been defined in (
2.27
)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}\int_{t}^{t+k_{n}}\Big(L(\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,\tau}(\tilde{y}))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau\;d\mu&=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}\int_{0}^{k_{n}}\Big(L(t+\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,t+\tau}(\tilde{y}))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\tau\;d\mu\\
&=\int_{0}^{k_{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}\Big(L(t+\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,t+\tau}(\tilde{y}))+\alpha_{0}\Big)\;d\mu\;d\tau\\
&=\int_{0}^{k_{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}L(t,\tilde{y})\;d\mu\;+\alpha_{0}\right)\;d\tau=0\end{split}

(4.3)

We shift our focus to the left hand side of (
4.2
). The
\Phi_{L}^{k_{n}}
-invariance of
\mu
results in

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v\big(t+k_{n},\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t,t+k_{n}}(\tilde{y})\big)\;d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{k_{n}}^{k_{n}+1}\int_{TM}v(t,\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(t+k_{n},\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu

Thus

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v\big(t+k_{n},\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t,t+k_{n}}(\tilde{y})\big)\;d\mu-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(t,\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(t+k_{n},\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(t,\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(k_{n}+t,\pi(\tilde{y}))-v(t,\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu\\

(4.4)

However, we know from Proposition
3.14
that the restrictions
v(t+k_{n},x)
uniformly converge to
v
on
\mathbb{R}\times M
. Hence, we deduce that

\lim_{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}v(k_{n}+t,\pi(\tilde{y}))-v(t,\pi(\tilde{y}))\;d\mu=0

(4.5)

Now, Gathering (
4.2
), (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), and if we define the defect of calibration
\delta_{k_{n}}(t,\tilde{y})
by

\delta_{k_{n}}(t,\tilde{y})=\alpha_{0}.k_{n}+\int_{t}^{t+k_{n}}L(\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,\tau}(\tilde{y}))\;d\tau-[v\big(t,\pi\circ\tilde{y}\big)-v\big(t,\pi\circ\tilde{y}\big)]\geq 0

(4.6)

we get

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{TM}\delta_{k_{n}}(t,\tilde{y})\;d\mu=0

where the integrand is non-negative and increasing in
n
. This implies that for
\mu
-almost all
(t,\tilde{y})
in
\supp(\mu)
, we have for all
s>0
,

\delta_{s}(t,\tilde{y})=\alpha_{0}.s+\int_{t}^{t+s}L(\tau,\phi_{L}^{t,\tau}(\tilde{y}))\;d\tau-[v\big(t,\pi\circ\tilde{y}\big)-v\big(t,\pi\circ\tilde{y}\big)]=0

And by continuity of
L
,
v
and hence
\delta_{s}
, the equality extends to
\supp(\mu)
. Since
\mu
is invariant by the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
and
\tilde{x}
belongs to its support, we infer that the graph of the curve
(t,\gamma(t))
also belongs to
\supp(\mu)
so that for all negative integer
m\leq 0
,
(m,\gamma(m)
also belongs to
\supp(\mu)
and the associated curve are calibrated by all non-wandering viscosity solutions in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.14
, we have
v(m+k_{n},\cdot)
converges to
v(m,\cdot)
and in particular,
v(m,\cdot)
belongs to
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and it calibrates by
\gamma(t+m)
. Therefore,
\gamma
is calibrated by
v
on
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

We infer from Remark
2.16
that all curves in the Mather set
\mathcal{M}
are minimizing.

4.2
The Uniqueness Theorem

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u
be in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
with corresponding global viscosity solution
u(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
x
be an element of
M
and
\gamma:(-\infty,0]\to M
with
\gamma(0)=x
be a
u
-calibrated curve. Then there exists an increasing sequence of integers
k_{n}
such that
\gamma(-k_{n})
converges to a point
x_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

Proof.

Using Riesz representation theorem, we define for all positive time
t>0
a Borel probability measure
\mu_{t}:\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mu_{t}(\theta)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{-t}^{0}\theta(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\;ds

We know from remark
2.16
that
\gamma
is minimizing. Hence, the A priori compactness of minimizing curves implies that
(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})
belongs to a compact subset
K
of
TM
, so that
\text{supp}(\mu_{t})\subset K
is compact. Consequently, we can extract an increasing sequence of integers
k_{n}
such that
\mu_{k_{n}}
weak-
\ast
converges to a compactly supported probability measure
\mu
. Additionally, since the curve
\gamma
is minimizing, it follows the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
and we deduce that the measure
\mu
is
\Phi_{L}
-invariant.

Weak-
\ast
convergence applied to
\theta=L
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM}L\;d\mu=\lim\limits_{n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM}L\;d\mu_{k_{n}}=\lim\limits_{n}\frac{1}{k_{n}}\int_{-k_{n}}^{0}L(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\;ds

Addtionally, the calibration of
\gamma
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k_{n}}\int_{-k_{n}}^{0}L(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\;ds+\alpha_{0}=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\big[u(0,x)-u(-k_{n},\gamma(-k_{n}))\big]

However, Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
3.15
claim that
u
is bounded. Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}\times TM}L\;d\mu+\alpha_{0}=\lim\limits_{n}\frac{1}{k_{n}}\big[u(0,x)-u(-k_{n},\gamma(-k_{n}))\big]=0

and the measure
\mu
is minimizing. Therefore, by definition of the Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
, we get the inclusion

\text{supp}(\mu)\subset\tilde{\mathcal{M}}

We now show that
\supp(\mu)
belongs to the
\alpha
-limit of the curve
\gamma
. Let
(0,\tilde{x})=(0,x,v)
be an element of
\text{supp}(\mu)
and for all
m\geq 1
, let
A_{m}
and
B_{m}
be the balls of center
(0,\tilde{x})
and respective radii
1/m
and
1/(2m)
in some chart of
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
. We consider a continuous bump map
\chi_{m}:\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM\to[0,1]
supported on
A_{m}
and equal to
1
on
B_{m}
. Then, if we denote by
\chi_{A_{m}}
the indicator function of the set
A_{m}
, we have
\chi_{B_{m}}\leq\chi_{m}\leq\chi_{A_{m}}
and

\mu_{k_{n}}(B_{m})\leq\mu_{k_{n}}(\chi_{m})\leq\mu_{k_{n}}(A_{m})\quad\text{and}\quad 0<\mu(B_{m})\leq\mu(\chi_{m})\leq\mu(A_{m})

where the positivity of
\mu(B_{m})
is due to the fact that
B_{m}
is a neighbourhood of
(0,\tilde{x})\in\supp(\mu)
. And by definition of the weak-
\ast
convergence, we obtain

0<\mu(\chi_{m})=\lim_{n}\mu_{k_{n}}(\chi_{m})\leq\liminf_{n}\mu_{k_{n}}(A_{m})

(4.7)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{k_{n}}(A_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\int_{-k_{n}}^{0}\chi_{A_{m}}(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\;ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\leb\big(\{s\in[-k_{n},0]\;|\;(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\in A_{m}\}\big)

where
\leb
stands for the Lebesgue measure. We consider the real number
t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
defined by

t_{n}:=\inf\big\{\tau\in[0,k_{n}]\;\big|\;\{s\in[-k_{n},0]\;|\;(s,\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\in A_{m}\}\subset[-\tau,0]\big\}

We have
(-t_{n},\gamma(-t_{n}),\dot{\gamma}(-t_{n}))
belongs to the closure
\overline{A}_{m}
of
A_{m}
, and

\mu_{k_{n}}(A_{m})\leq\frac{1}{k_{n}}\leb([-t_{n},0])=\frac{t_{n}}{k_{n}}

Thus, we infer from the inequalities (
4.7
) that

0<\mu(\chi_{m})\leq\liminf_{n}\frac{t_{n}}{k_{n}}

and since
k_{n}
diverges to
+\infty
, we deduce that
\lim_{n}t_{n}=+\infty
.

For all
m\geq 1
, we constructed a sequence
t_{n}
such that

\lim_{n}t_{n}=+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad(-t_{n}-\lfloor-t_{n}\rfloor,\gamma(-t_{n}),\dot{\gamma}(-t_{n}))\in\overline{A}_{m}\subset\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM

(4.8)

By extracting, we get two increasing sequences
m_{i}
and
t_{n_{i}}
verifying (
4.8
). And since
m_{i}
is increasing and
A_{m}
is of radius
1/m_{i}
, we obtain

\lim_{i}\;(-t_{n_{i}}-\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor,\gamma(-t_{n_{i}}),\dot{\gamma}(-t_{n_{i}}))=(0,\tilde{x})

and in particular

\lim_{i}\;(-t_{n_{i}}-\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor,\gamma(-t_{n_{i}}))=(0,x)\in\pi(\text{supp}(\mu))\cap\big(\{0\}\times M\big)\subset\mathcal{M}_{0}

In order to obtain integer times instead of
t_{n_{i}}
, we recall that
(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})
belongs to a compact set
K
of
TM
and it follows the Lagrangian flow
\phi_{L}
so that
\phi_{L}^{-\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor,-t_{n_{i}}}=\phi_{L}^{0,-t_{n_{i}}-\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor}
restricted to
K
converges uniformly to the identity and

\displaystyle\lim_{i}\;(\gamma(\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor),\dot{\gamma}(\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor))=\lim_{i}\;\left(\phi_{L}^{0,-t_{n_{i}}-\lfloor-t_{n_{i}}\rfloor}\right)^{-1}(\gamma(-t_{n_{i}}),\dot{\gamma}(-t_{n_{i}}))=id(\tilde{x})=\tilde{x}

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
u
and
v
be two non-wandering viscosity solutions in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
such that
u_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}=v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}
. Then
u=v
everywhere.

Proof.

Let
(t,x)
be a fixed element of
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times M
and
\gamma:(-\infty,t]\to M
with
\gamma(t)=x
be a curve calibrated by
v
given by proposition
2.17
. For all times
s_{1}<s_{2}
, we have

v(s_{2},\gamma(s_{2}))-v(s_{1},\gamma(s_{1}))=\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau+\alpha_{0}.(s_{2}-s_{1})

(4.9)

and from the definition of viscosity solutions

u(s_{2},\gamma(s_{2}))-u(s_{1},\gamma(s_{1}))\leq\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}L(\tau,\gamma(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))\;d\tau+\alpha_{0}.(s_{2}-s_{1})

Replacing the right hand side by (
4.9
), we get

u(s_{2},\gamma(s_{2}))-u(s_{1},\gamma(s_{1}))\leq v(s_{2},\gamma(s_{2}))-v(s_{1},\gamma(s_{1}))

and more precisely

\big(u-v\big)(s_{2},\gamma(s_{2}))\leq\big(u-v\big)(s_{1},\gamma(s_{1}))

Hence
(u-v)(s,\gamma(s))
is non-increasing in time
s
. Since
u
and
v
are bounded by Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
3.15
, it follows that
\big(u-v\big)(s,\gamma(s))
has a finite limit
l
at
-\infty
that we will determine.

Now apply Lemma
4.3
to the curve
\gamma:\big(-\infty,\lfloor t\rfloor\big]\to M
to get a an increasing sequence of integers
k_{n}
and an element
x_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
such that
\gamma(-k_{n})
converges to
x_{\alpha}
. We are interested in computing

l=\lim\limits_{s\to-\infty}u(s,\gamma(s))-v(s,\gamma(s))=\lim\limits_{n}u(-k_{n},\gamma(-k_{n}))-v(-k_{n},\gamma(-k_{n}))

The equicontinuity of the families
\big(u(t,\cdot)\big)_{t}
and
\big(v(t,\cdot)\big)_{t}
given by Corollary
2.14
allow to replace
\gamma(-k_{n})
by its limit
x_{\alpha}
to get

l=\lim\limits_{n}u(-k_{n},x_{\alpha})-v(-k_{n},x_{\alpha})

Set
x_{\alpha}(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{-k_{n},t}(\tilde{x}_{\alpha})
where
\tilde{x}_{\alpha}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is the lift of
x_{\alpha}
. Proposition
4.1
implies that
x_{\alpha}(t)
is calibrated by
u
and
v
, and

u(0,x_{\alpha}(0))-u(-k_{n},x_{\alpha})=\int_{-k_{n}}^{0}L(s,x_{\alpha}(s)\dot{x_{\alpha}}(s))\;ds+\alpha_{0}.k_{n}=v(0,x_{\alpha}(0))-v(-k_{n},x_{\alpha})

and since
x_{\alpha}(0)
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
u_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}=v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}
, we obtain

u(-k_{n},x_{\alpha})-v(-k_{n},x_{\alpha})=u(0,x_{\alpha})-v(0,x_{\alpha})=0

We proved that
(u-v)(s,\gamma(s))
is non-increasing in time
s
and has a null limit at
-\infty
. Therefore,
(u-v)(s,\gamma(s))
is non-negative and

u(t,x)=u(t,\gamma(t))\leq v(t,\gamma(t))=v(t,x)

The symmetry between
u
and
v
gives the inverse inequality and we conclude that
u(t,x)=v(t,x)
for all
(t,x)
in
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times M
.
∎

5
Representation Formulas for the Non-Wandering Set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})

This is the central section of the article, divided into three subsections. The first subsection introduces the Peierls barrier, a crucial element for developing more general barriers. The second subsection presents a first, non-canonical representation formula, which is easily adaptable to explicit examples. The final subsection is dedicated to the introduction of the generalized Peierls barrier
\underline{k}
mentioned in the introduction, and to the proof of the main representation Theorem
1.5
of this article.

5.1
Peierls Barriers

The Peierls barrier, introduced by Mather in
[
Mat93
]
, provides various viscosity solutions to the Hamilton-Jacobi equation. These solutions are crucial for describing all weak-KAM solutions (See
[
CISM13
]
). In this section, we introduce a generalized Peierls barriers, which will enable us to describe all non-wandering viscosity solutions through representation formulas.

Definition 5.1
.

1.

For any increasing sequence
\underline{p}=(p_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathbb{N}
, we define the
\underline{p}
-Peierls Barrier

h^{\underline{p}}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
by

h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(x,y)

(5.1)

with the corresponding time-dependant
\underline{p}
-barrier

h^{\underline{p}+t}(x,y)=h^{\underline{p}}(t,x,y)=\liminf_{n}h^{t+p_{n}}(x,y)

(5.2)

where
h^{t}
is the potential explicited in (
2.28
).

More generally, for all two times
s
and
t
, we define

h^{s,\underline{p}+t}(x,y)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}h^{s,p_{n}+t}(x,y)

(5.3)

2.

The
Peierls Barrier

h^{\infty}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is the
\underline{p}
-barrier for
p_{n}=n
defined by

h^{\infty}(x,y)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}h^{n}(x,y)

(5.4)

Proposition 5.2
.

1.

(Finiteness) For all
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times M\times M
, the Peierls barrier
h^{\underline{p}}(t,x,y)
is finite.

2.

(Regularity) The Peierls barrier
h^{\underline{p}}
is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz for all
\varepsilon>0
with
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
being the Lipschitz constant introduced in Proposition
2.13
.

3.

(Viscosity Solution) For all
x\in M
,
h^{\underline{p}}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
).

4.

(Liminf Property) For all points
x
and
y
in
M
and all sequences of points
(x_{n})_{n}
and
(y_{n})_{n}
in
M
respectively converging to
x
and
y
, we have

h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(x_{n},y_{n})

(5.5)

Proof.

1
. Direct consequence of Proposition
2.28
.

2
. Direct consequence of Proposition
2.13
.

3
. For all
x\in M
, we know from Proposition
2.25
that
h^{p_{n}}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution. Hence, we deduce from Proposition
3.6
that
h^{\underline{p}}(\cdot,x,\cdot)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is also a viscosity solution.

4
. We have from the regularity of
h
that for all integers
n
and
k\geq 1

\displaystyle|h^{k}(x,y)-h^{k}(x_{n},y_{n})|\leq\kappa_{1}.\big(d(x,x_{n})+d(y,y_{n})\big)

Hence, setting
k=p_{n}
and taking the liminf on
n
, we get

\displaystyle h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(x,y)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(x_{n},y_{n})

∎

We also give properties specific to the original Peierls barrier
h^{\infty}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

1.

(Weak-KAM Solution) For all
x\in M
,
h^{\infty}(x,\cdot)
is a weak-KAM solution of the translated Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
).

2.

(Triangular Inequality) For all point
x
,
y
and
z
in
M
, we have the triangular inequality

h^{\infty}(x,z)\leq h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,z)

(5.6)

3.

And for all times
s<\tau<t
, we have the triangular inequalities

\begin{split}h^{s,\infty+t}(x,z)&\leq h^{s,\infty+\tau}(x,y)+h^{\tau,t}(y,z)\\
h^{s,\infty+t}(x,z)&\leq h^{s,\tau}(x,y)+h^{\tau,\infty+t}(y,z)\end{split}

(5.7)

4.

(Non-negativity) For every point
x
in
M
, we have
h^{\infty}(x,x)\geq 0
.

Proof.

1
. It is the explicited weak-KAM solution in the proof of Proposition
3.7
on existence of weak-KAM solutions.

2
. Fix three points
x
,
y
and
z
in
M
. Let
k_{n}
and
q_{n}
be two increasing sequences of integers such that
h^{\infty}(x,y)=\lim_{n}h^{k_{n}}(x,y)
and
h^{\infty}(y,z)=\lim_{n}h^{q_{n}}(y,z)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle h^{\infty}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}h^{n}(x,y)\leq\liminf_{n}h^{k_{n}+q_{n}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}h^{k_{n}}(x,y)+h^{q_{n}}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n}h^{k_{n}}(x,y)+h^{q_{n}}(y,z)

\displaystyle=h^{\infty}(x,y)+h^{\infty}(y,z)

where we used the triangular inequality (
2.29
) in the second line.

3
. As the previous point, we fix three points
x
,
y
and
z
in
M
and three times
s
and
\tau<t
. Let
k_{n}
be an increasing sequences of integers such that
h^{s,\infty+\tau}(x,y)=\lim_{n}h^{s,k_{n}+\tau}(x,y)
. Then we have,

\displaystyle h^{s,\infty+t}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}h^{s,n+t}(x,y)\leq\liminf_{n}h^{s,k_{n}+t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n}h^{s,k_{n}+\tau}(x,y)+h^{k_{n}+\tau,k_{n}+t}(y,z)

\displaystyle=h^{s,\infty+\tau}(x,y)+h^{\tau,t}(y,z)

The second inequality is analogous.

4
. Consider a weak-KAM solution
u
. Then, for all point
x
in M and for all integer
n\geq 0
, we get from the definition of
u
that

\displaystyle 0=u(x)-u(x)=u(n,x)-u(0,x)\leq h^{n}(x,x)

Taking the liminf on
n
, we get the inequality
h^{\infty}(x,x)\geq 0
.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

We define the
Peierls set

\mathcal{A}_{0}
in
M
as follows

\mathcal{A}_{0}=\{x\in M\;|\;h^{\infty}(x,x)=0\}

(5.8)

Proposition 5.5
.

For all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
, we have
h^{\infty}(x,x)=0
. In other words, we have the inclusion of sets
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subset\mathcal{A}_{0}

Proof.

Let
x
be in
\mathcal{M}^{R}_{0}
with lift
\tilde{x}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
and set
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
. Let
k_{n}
be an increasing sequence of integers such that
x(-k_{n})
converge to
x
. From Proposition
4.1
, we have that the curve
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(x,v)
is calibrated by any weak-KAM solution

and

h^{k_{n}}(x(-k_{n}),x)=u(x)-u(-k_{n},x(-k_{n}))=u(x)-u(x(-k_{n}))\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

where we use the continuity of

. Thus, by the liminf Property (
5.5
), and by the non-negativity Property
4
of Proposition
5.3
, we obtain

0\leq h^{\infty}(x,x)\leq h^{\underline{k}}(x,x)=\liminf_{n}h^{k_{n}}(x(-k_{n}),x)=0

We proved that

on
\mathcal{M}^{R}_{0}
. Additionally, it was stated in Proposition
2.23
that this set is dense in the Mather set

. Therefore, we deduce by continuity that

\mathcal{M}_{0}
.
∎

5.2
Representation Formula on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}

In this subsection, we establish a non-canonical representation formula. To do this, we first introduce a non-canonical barrier
\underline{h}
, defined on the recurrent Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, utilizing various
\underline{p}
-Peierls barriers associated with different sequences.

5.2.1
The Barrier
\underline{h}

We start by introducing a new barrier
\underline{h}
based on the
\underline{p}
-Peierls barriers. For that purpose, we consider for every point
x
of the recurrent Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
with lift
\tilde{x}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{R}
an increasing sequence
\underline{p}^{x}=(p^{x}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of

such that
\tilde{x}
is
-\underline{p}^{x}
-recurrent by the Lagrangian flow

.

Definition 5.6
.

1.

We define the barrier
\underline{h}:\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
by

\underline{h}(x,y)=h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,y)=\liminf_{n}h^{p^{x}_{n}}(x,y)

(5.9)

with the corresponding time-depending barrier

\underline{h}^{t}(x,y)=\underline{h}(t,x,y)=h^{\underline{p}^{x}+t}(x,y)

(5.10)

Remark 5.7
.

1.

Note that, in general, this barrier
\underline{h}(x,y)
is not continuous on

.

2.

The definition depends heavily on the choice of the sequences
\underline{p}^{x}

is not canonical. This issue will be addressed in the next subsection about representation formulas on
\mathcal{M}_{0}

Proposition 5.8
.

1.

For all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, the map
\underline{h}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
).

2.

For all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, the map
\underline{h}(\cdot,x,\cdot):\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz for all
\varepsilon>0
with
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
being the Lipschitz constant introduced in Proposition
2.13
.

3.

For all
x
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, we have
\underline{h}(x,x)=h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,x)=0
.

4.

For all
x\in\mathcal{M}^{R}_{0}
, the viscosity solution
\underline{h}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is
\underline{p}^{x}
-recurrent and

\|\underline{h}^{p^{x}_{n}}(x,\cdot)-\underline{h}(x,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\kappa_{1}.d(x(-p^{x}_{n}),x)

(5.11)

Proof.

1
and
2
. Since
\underline{h}(x,\cdot)=h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,\cdot)=\liminf_{n}h^{p^{x}_{n}}(x,\cdot)
, these properties follow immediately from Proposition
5.2
.

3
. Consider the point
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
that projects to
x
on
M
and set

to be the curve
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
. We have by definition of the sequence

that

converges to
x
. Then, we get

\displaystyle h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,x)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}^{x}}(x,x)=\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}^{x}}(x(-p_{n}^{x}),x)

where we used the liminf property (
5.5
) of the Peierls barrier and the fact that

is minimizing seen in Remark
4.2
. Let
u
be any weak-KAM solution. We know from proposition
4.1
that

is calibrated by
u
, then by continuity and

, we get

\displaystyle h^{p_{n}^{x}}(x(-p_{n}^{x}),x)=u(x)-u(-p_{n}^{x},x(-p_{n}^{x}))=u(x)-u(x(-p_{n}^{x}))\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,x)=\lim_{n}h^{p_{n}^{x}}(x(-p_{n}^{x}),x)=0

4
. The fact that
h^{\underline{p}}(y,\cdot)
is recurrent follows from the fact that it is a global viscosity solution. This is due to Proposition
2.28
and the recurrence follows from Theorem
1.2
. To demonstrate
\underline{p}
-recurrence, we will need to prove the identity (

).

Let
y
be a fixed point of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
. Fix an integers
n
and let
(n_{i}=n_{i}(n))_{i\geq 0}
be an increasing sequence of integers depending on
n
such that
h^{\underline{p}^{y}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)=\lim_{i}h^{p^{y}_{n_{i}}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)
. Now fix an integer
i\geq 0
and let
k_{0}=k_{0}(n,i)
be an integer such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
, we get
p^{y}_{k}>p^{y}_{n_{i}}+p^{y}_{n}
. For such
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

h^{p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)\leq h^{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}}(y,y)+h^{p^{y}_{n_{i}}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)

(5.12)

Let
\tilde{y}
be the lift of point of
y
in the Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
and consider the curve
y(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{y})
. We know from the regularity Proposition
2.13
on the potential
h
that for all integer
q\geq 1

\displaystyle|h^{q}(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}))-h^{q}(y,y)|\leq\kappa_{1}.[d(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y)+d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y)]

So that for
q=p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle|h^{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}}((y(-p^{y}_{k}),y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}))-h^{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}}(y,y)|\leq\kappa_{1}.[d(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y)+d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y)]

(5.13)

Let
v
be a weak-KAM solution. We know from Proposition
4.1
that
y(t)
is calibrated by
v
and from Proposition
2.13
that
v=\mathcal{T}^{1}v
is
\kappa_{1}
-Lipschitz, hence

|h^{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}}(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}))|=|v(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}))-v(y(-p^{y}_{k}))|\leq\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y(-p^{y}_{k}))

(5.14)

Hence, we deduce from (
5.13
) that

\displaystyle h^{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}}(y,y)

\displaystyle\leq h^{p^{y}_{k}-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}}((y(-p^{y}_{k}),y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}))+\kappa_{1}.[d(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y)+d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y)]

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{1}.[d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y(-p^{y}_{k}))+d(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y)+d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y)]

and from (
5.12
) that

\displaystyle h^{p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)\leq\kappa_{1}.[d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y(-p^{y}_{k}))+d(y(-p^{y}_{k}),y)+d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y)]+h^{p^{y}_{n_{i}}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)

Let
k_{i}
be an increasing sequence such that
k_{i}\geq k_{0}(n,i)
, i.e
p^{y}_{k_{i}}>p^{y}_{n_{i}}+p^{y}_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{i}d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y(-p^{y}_{k_{i}}))=d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)

Then, taking the liminf on
i
yields

\begin{split}h^{\underline{p}^{y}}(y,x)&\leq\kappa_{1}.\lim_{i}\;[d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y(-p^{y}_{k_{i}}))+d(y(-p^{y}_{k_{i}}),y)+d(y(-p^{y}_{n_{i}}-p^{y}_{n}),y)]+\lim_{i}h^{p^{y}_{n_{i}}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)\\
&=2\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)+h^{\underline{p}^{y}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)\end{split}

(5.15)

For the inverse inequality. Fix two integers
n
and
k\geq 0
. The triangular inequality (
2.29
) gives

h^{p^{y}_{n}+p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)\leq h^{p^{y}_{n}}(y,y)+h^{p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)

Moreover, we know from the Lipschitz regularity of the potential
h
that

|h^{p^{y}_{n}}(y,y)-h^{p^{y}_{n}}(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)|\leq\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)

Bounding
h^{p^{y}_{n}}(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)
as in (
5.14
), we get

\displaystyle h^{p^{y}_{n}+p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)

\displaystyle\leq h^{p^{y}_{n}}(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)+\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)+h^{p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)+h^{p^{y}_{k}}(y,x)

Then, we Take the liminf on
k
to obtain the desired inequality

h^{\underline{p}^{y}+p^{y}_{n}}(y,x)\leq 2\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)+h^{\underline{p}^{y}}(y,x)

(5.16)

Gathering the inequalities (
5.16
) and (
5.15
) leads to

\|\underline{h}^{p^{y}_{n}}(y,\cdot)-\underline{h}(y,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\kappa_{1}.d(y(-p^{y}_{n}),y)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

(5.17)

Therefore,
\underline{h}(y,\cdot)
is
\underline{p}^{y}
-recurrent.
∎

5.2.2
The Representation Formula

Recall from Definition
1.4
the notion of domination. We will work with
\underline{h}
-dominated maps on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, i.e maps of
\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})
.

Theorem 5.9
.

We have the following bijection

\begin{matrix}\Psi_{\underline{h}}:\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})&\longrightarrow&\Omega(\mathcal{T})\\
\psi&\longmapsto&\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{h}(y,\cdot)\}\end{matrix}

(5.18)

with its inverse being the restriction map

\begin{matrix}\Phi_{\underline{h}}:\Omega(\mathcal{T})&\longrightarrow&\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})\\
v&\longmapsto&v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\end{matrix}

(5.19)

Proof.

Note that the maps of the form
\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{h}(y,\cdot)\}
are viscosity solutions due to Proposition
3.4
. They are globally defined so that they belong to
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
by Theorem
1.2
. This justifies the well-definition of the map
\Psi_{\underline{h}}
. We need to prove that
\Phi_{\underline{h}}
is well-defined. Let
v
be an element of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. We show that
v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}
is
\underline{h}
-dominated. Let
x
and
y
be two elements of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
and
\tilde{x}
the lift of
x
to
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{R}
. Consider the curve
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
. We know from Proposition
4.1
that
x(t)
is calibrated by
v
and by any weak-KAM solution
u
. Then for all negative time
t
we have

v(x)-v(t,x(t))=h^{t,0}(x(t),x)=u(x)-u(t,x(t))

Since
x(-p^{x}_{n})
converges to
x
, we have for
t=-p^{x}_{n}

\lim_{n}v(x)-v(-p^{x}_{n},x(-p^{x}_{n}))=\lim_{n}u(x)-u(-p^{x}_{n},x(-p^{x}_{n}))=\lim_{n}u(x)-u(x(-p^{x}_{n}))=0

We now use the definition of the Lax-Oleinik operator
\mathcal{T}
to deduce that

v(y)-v(x)=\lim_{n}v(0,y)-v(-p^{x}_{n},x(-p^{x}_{n}))\leq\liminf_{n}h^{p^{x}_{n}}(x(-p^{x}_{n}),y)=\liminf_{n}h^{p^{x}_{n}}(x,y)=h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,y)=\underline{h}(x,y)

where we used the liminf property (
5.5
) of the Barrier
h^{\underline{p}^{x}}
. We obtained the desired
\underline{h}
-domination which justifies the well-definition of the map
\Phi_{\underline{h}}
.

Let us show that the map
\Phi_{\underline{h}}
is a left inverse of the map
\Psi_{\underline{h}}
. Take
v=\Psi_{\underline{h}}(\psi)=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{h}(y,\cdot)\}\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. Then, for all
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, the domination condition gives the inequality

\psi(x)+\underline{h}(x,x)=\psi(x)\leq\psi(y)+\underline{h}(y,x)

(5.20)

where we recall from Property
3
of Proposition
5.8
that
\underline{h}(x,x)=h^{\underline{p}^{x}}(x,x)=0
. We obtain for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}

v(x)=\Psi_{\underline{h}}(\psi)(x)=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{h}(y,x)\}=\psi(x)+\underline{h}(x,x)=\psi(x)

In other words,
\Phi_{\underline{h}}\circ\Psi_{\underline{h}}(\psi)=\psi
.

We now show that
\Phi_{\underline{h}}
is the right inverse of the map
\Psi_{\underline{h}}
. Let
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and consider
w=\Psi_{\underline{h}}\circ\Phi_{\underline{h}}(v)\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. We need to prove the these two maps
v
and
w
are equal. By the uniqueness Theorem
4.4
, it suffices to prove that they coincide on the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Let
x
be an element of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
. We infer from the
\underline{h}
-domination of
v
on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
and from (
5.20
) that

\displaystyle w(x)=\Psi_{\underline{h}}(v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}})(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\{v(y)+\underline{h}(y,x)\}=v(x)+\underline{h}(x,x)=v(x)

Thus,
w_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}=v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}
and by continuity of viscosity solutions and density of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, we deduce that
w_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}=v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}
. We have shown that
v
and
w
are two elements of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
which coincide on the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, then we get from the uniqueness Theorem
4.4
the equality
v=w=\Psi_{\underline{h}}\circ\Phi_{\underline{h}}(v)
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

1.

As stated in the beginning of this subsection, the proof shows that

2.

y

5.3
Representation Formula on
\mathcal{M}_{0}

This subsection introduces a new generalized barrier
\underline{k}
, defined on the entire Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. This barrier is independent of the choice of sequences
\underline{p}^{x}
and satisfies the triangular inequality. These properties make
\underline{k}
suitable for the more general representation formula presented in Theorem
1.5
, which we prove in this subsection.

5.3.1
The Generalized Peierls Barrier
\underline{k}

We begin by introducing a generalized Peierls barrier
\underline{k}
, derived from the barrier
\underline{h}
, by enforcing the triangular inequality. This, as we will see, ensures the continuity of
\underline{k}
and results in a canonical barrier that is independent of the choice of sequences
\underline{p}^{x}
(Corollary
5.19
).

Definition 5.11
.

1.

We define the
generalized Peierls Barrier

\underline{k}:\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
by

\underline{k}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\;|\;x_{0}=x,\;x_{N}=y,\;x_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\;N\geq 1\right\}

(5.21)

with time dependence

\underline{k}(t,x,y)=\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+\underline{h}^{t}(x_{N-1},y)\;|\;x_{0}=x,\;x_{N}=y,\;x_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\;N\geq 1\right\}

(5.22)

2.

We define the map
\underline{d}:\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\underline{d}(x,y)=\underline{k}(x,y)+\underline{k}(y,x)

(5.23)

Proposition 5.12
.

We have

1.

(Viscosity Solution) For all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, the map
\underline{k}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1.1
).

2.

For all
(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M
, we have

h^{\infty+t}(x,y)\leq\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)\leq\underline{h}^{t}(x,y)

(5.24)

3.

For all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
,
\underline{k}(x,x)=0
.

4.

(Triagular Inequality) For all times
t\in\mathbb{R}
and all points
x,y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
and
z\in M
, we have the triangular inequality

\underline{k}^{t}(x,z)\leq\underline{k}(x,y)+\underline{k}^{t}(y,z)

(5.25)

5.

(Regularity) The barrier
\underline{k}^{t}
is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M
for all
\varepsilon>0
with
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
being the Lipschitz constant introduced in Proposition
2.13
.

Proof.

1
. Let
x
be a point of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
. We have

\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+\underline{h}^{t}(x_{N-1},y)\;|\;x_{0}=x,\;x_{N}=y,\;x_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\;N\geq 1\right\}

By Proposition
5.8
, this infimum is taken over viscosity solutions. Hence, we infer from Proposition
3.4
that
\underline{k}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is also a viscosity solution. Moreover, following the regularity Property
2
of Proposition
5.8
, we deduce that this solution is
\kappa_{\varepsilon}
-Lipschitz for all
\varepsilon>0
.

2
. The second inequality follows immediately from the definition of
\underline{k}
. We prove the first inequality. Let
(x,y)
be an element of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M
and let
(x_{i})_{0\leq x_{i}\leq N-1}
be a sequence of elements of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
with
x_{0}=x
and set
x_{N}=y
. we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+\underline{h}^{t}(x_{N-1},y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}h^{\underline{p}^{x_{i}}}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+h^{\underline{p}^{x_{N-1}}+t}(x_{N-1},y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}\liminf_{n_{i}}h^{p_{n_{i}}^{x_{i}}}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+\liminf_{n_{N-1}}h^{p_{n_{N-1}}^{x_{N-1}}+t}(x_{N-1},y)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n_{1},..,n_{N-1}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-2}h^{p_{n_{i}}^{x_{i}}}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+h^{p_{n_{N-1}}^{x_{N-1}}+t}(x_{N-1},y)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n_{1},..,n_{N-1}}h^{\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}p_{n_{i}}^{x_{i}}+t}(x,y)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n}h^{n+t}(x,y)=h^{\infty+t}(x,y)

Taking the infimum on such sequences, we deduce the inequality
\underline{k}\geq h^{\infty}
.

3
. Let
x
be a point of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
. By the previous property, we have

h^{\infty}(x,x)\leq\underline{k}(x,x)\leq\underline{h}(x,x)

Moreover, we know from Proposition
5.5
and Property
3
of Proposition
5.8
that
h^{\infty}(x,x)=\underline{h}(x,x)=0
. Hence,
\underline{k}(x,x)=0
.

4
. Let
x
and
y
be points of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
and let
z
be a point of
M
. Consider two sequences of points
(x_{i})_{0\leq i\leq N}
with
(x_{0},x_{N})=(x,y)
and
(y_{j})_{0\leq j\leq N^{\prime}}
with
(y_{0},y_{N}^{\prime})=(y,z)
. Note that
x_{N}=y_{0}=y
. Hence, concatenating them into a third sequence
(z_{i})_{0\leq k\leq N+N^{\prime}}
and using the definition of the barrier
\underline{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\underline{k}(x,z)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N+N^{\prime}-2}\underline{h}(z_{i},z_{i+1})+\underline{h}^{t}(z_{N+N^{\prime}-1},z_{N+N^{\prime}})=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})+\sum_{j=0}^{N^{\prime}-2}\underline{h}(y_{j},y_{j+1})+\underline{h}^{t}(y_{N^{\prime}-1},y_{N^{\prime}})

Taking the infimum over such sequences yields the desired triangular inequality.

5
. Let
(t,x,y)
and
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
be two elements of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M
. We have

|\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)-\underline{k}^{t^{\prime}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq|\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)-\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)|+|\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)-\underline{k}^{t^{\prime}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|

(5.26)

We have seen in the proof of the first point that

|\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)-\underline{k}^{t^{\prime}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq\kappa_{\varepsilon}.[d(y,y^{\prime})+|t-t^{\prime}|]

(5.27)

We need to bound the other term of the rand-hand side of (
5.26
). We know from the triangular inequality that

\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)\leq\underline{k}(x,x^{\prime})+\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)\leq\underline{k}(x^{\prime},x)+\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)

(5.28)

Thus, we get

\displaystyle|\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)-\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)|\leq\max\big\{|\underline{k}(x,x^{\prime})|,|\underline{k}(x^{\prime},x)|\big\}

Moreover, we proved that
\underline{k}(x,x)=\underline{k}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime})=0
, which yields

\displaystyle|\underline{k}(x,x^{\prime})|=|\underline{k}(x,x^{\prime})-\underline{k}(x,x)|\leq\kappa_{\varepsilon}.d(x,x^{\prime})

and similarly
|\underline{k}(x^{\prime},x)|\leq\kappa_{\varepsilon}.d(x,x^{\prime})
. This leads to the bounding

|\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)-\underline{k}^{t}(x^{\prime},y)|\leq\kappa_{\varepsilon}.d(x,x^{\prime})

(5.29)

Gathering (
5.26
),(
5.27
) and (
5.29
), we conclude that

\displaystyle|\underline{k}^{t}(x,y)-\underline{k}^{t^{\prime}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|\leq\kappa_{\varepsilon}.[d(x,x^{\prime})+d(y,y^{\prime})+|t-t^{\prime}|]

which is the desired Lipschitz inequality.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

For all time
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the barrier
\underline{k}^{t}
extends in a unique way to the set
\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M
. The extended barrier
\underline{k}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M\to\mathbb{R}
possesses all the properties featured in Proposition
5.12
.

Proof.

The Property
5
of Proposition
5.12
implies that the map
\underline{k}^{t}
is uniformly continuous on the dense subset
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M
of the compact set
\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M
. Hence, it extends uniquely to
\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M
.

All the properties extensions are straightforward except for the viscosity solutions. We prove that for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
, the map
\underline{k}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
2.8
). Let
x
be in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and let
x_{n}
be a sequence of
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
that converges to
x
. For all times
s<t
, the non-expensiveness of the Lax-Oleinik semi-group stated in Proposition
3.1
leads to

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x_{n},\cdot)-\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\|\underline{k}^{s}(x_{n},\cdot)-\underline{k}^{s}(x,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa_{1}.d(x_{n},x)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

Then
\lim_{n}\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x_{n},\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x,\cdot)
.

Moreover, since
\underline{k}(\cdot,x_{n},\cdot)
is a viscosity solution, we have
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x_{n},\cdot)=\underline{k}^{t}(x_{n},\cdot)
with

\displaystyle\|\underline{k}^{t}(x_{n},\cdot)-\underline{k}^{t}(x,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\kappa_{1}.d(x_{n},x)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to+\infty

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x,\cdot)=\mathcal{T}^{s,t}\underline{k}^{s}(x_{n},\cdot)=\lim_{n}\underline{k}^{t}(x_{n},\cdot)=\underline{k}^{t}(x,\cdot)

and
\underline{k}(\cdot,x,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution.
∎

5.3.2
The Generalized Representation Formula

Before diving into the proof of the main result of this paper, let us give some representation formulas that follow directly from Theorem
5.9
.

Proposition 5.14
.

1.

We have equality
\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{k})=\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})
.

2.

The bijection
\Psi_{\underline{h}}
expressed in (
5.18
) is equal to

\begin{matrix}\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{k})&\longrightarrow&\Omega(\mathcal{T})\\
\psi&\longmapsto&\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{k}(y,\cdot)\}\end{matrix}

(5.30)

3.

Extending this formula by continuity to the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, we get the bijection

\begin{matrix}\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0},\underline{k})&\longrightarrow&\Omega(\mathcal{T})\\
\psi&\longmapsto&\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{k}(y,\cdot)\}\end{matrix}

(5.31)

Remark 5.15
.

We can note that the equality
\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{k})=\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})
explains the continuity of
\underline{h}
-dominated maps deduced from Theorem
5.9
.

Proof.

Domination.
The inclusion
\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{k})\subset\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})
comes from the inequality
\underline{k}\leq\underline{h}
. Let us prove the inverse inclusion. Fix a map
\psi\in\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})
. For all sequence
(x_{i})_{0\leq i\leq N}
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
, we have

\psi(x_{i+1})-\psi(x_{i})\leq\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})

Thus, summing on
i
yields

\psi(x_{N})-\psi(x_{0})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\underline{h}(x_{i},x_{i+1})

Then, taking the infimum on such sequences linking
x_{0}=x
to

Formula.

Proposition 5.16

The map

.

Proof.

The symmetry

Definition 5.17
.

1.

2.

The

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We saw in Proposition
5.14
that
\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{k})=\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R},\underline{h})
. And we know from Theorem

-dominated on

\underline{k}
-dominated on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}
and by continuity on the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. In particular,
\underline{k}
-domination holds in
\underline{\mathbb{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Following the proof of Theorem
5.9
, this allows to prove that the maps
\Psi_{\underline{k}}
and

are well-defined and that
\Phi_{\underline{k}}\circ\Psi_{\underline{k}}=Id_{\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},\underline{k})}
. However, the identity
\Psi_{\underline{k}}\circ\Phi_{\underline{k}}=Id_{\Omega(\mathcal{T})}
requires to show that the Uniqueness Theorem
4.4
holds on the set of generalized static classes
\underline{\mathbb{M}}
.

Let us show this. Let
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and consider
w=\Psi_{\underline{k}}\circ\Phi_{\underline{k}}(v)\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. We need to prove that
v
and
w
are equal. By the uniqueness Theorem
4.4
, it suffices to prove that they coincide on the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Let
x
be an element of
\underline{\mathbb{M}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Then due to the
\underline{k}
-domination of
v_{|\underline{\mathbb{M}}}
, and similarly to (
5.20
), we have

\displaystyle w(x)=\Psi_{\underline{k}}\circ\Phi_{\underline{k}}(v)(x)=\inf_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{v(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\}=v(x)+\underline{k}(x,x)=v(x)

Let
x
be a any element of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}
such that
x\sim y
. Then, due to the
\underline{k}
-domination of

, we have the inequalities

\displaystyle v(x)-v(y)\leq\underline{k}(y,x)\quad\text{and}\quad v(y)-v(x)\leq\underline{k}(x,y)

Taking the sum, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=[v(x)-v(y)]+[v(y)-v(x)]\leq\underline{k}(y,x)+\underline{k}(x,y)=\underline{d}(y,x)=0

\displaystyle v(x)-v(y)=\underline{k}(y,x)\quad\text{and}\quad v(y)-v(x)=\underline{k}(x,y)

Moreover, for all

Therefore, we obtain

w(x)=\inf_{z\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{v(z)+\underline{k}(z,x)\}=v(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)=v(x)

(5.33)

The maps

.
∎

Remark 5.18
.

It was noted in Remark
5.10
that
\mathcal{M}^{R}_{0}
can be replaced by any dense subset
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{0}
. It is still possible to consider the generalized static classes
\underline{\mathbb{M}}^{\prime}
inside

We conclude this section by providing an application of this representation formula which shows that the generalized Peierls barrier
\underline{k}
does not depend on the choice of the sequences
\underline{p}^{x}
used to define the barrier
\underline{h}
.

Corollary 5.19
.

For all points
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
x\in M
, we have the formulas

\begin{split}\max_{v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})}\{v(x)-v(x_{0})\}&=\underline{k}(x_{0},x)\\
\min_{v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})}\{v(x)-v(x_{0})\}&=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}\\
y\nsim x_{0}\end{subarray}}\{-\underline{k}(y,x_{0})+\underline{k}(y,x)\}\end{split}

(5.34)

Proof.

Let us prove the first identity. We will use the representation formula (
5.31
). We consider
\psi^{+}:\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\psi^{+}(x)=\underline{k}(x_{0},x)
. We know from the Property
3
of Proposition
5.12
that
\psi^{+}(x_{0})=\underline{k}(x_{0},x_{0})=0
, and from the triangular inequality (
5.25
) that for all
y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\underline{k}(x_{0},x)\leq\underline{k}(x_{0},y)+\underline{k}(y,x)

which yields

\displaystyle\psi^{+}(x)-\psi^{+}(y)=\underline{k}(x_{0},x)-\underline{k}(x_{0},y)\leq\underline{k}(x,y)

Then,
\psi^{+}
belongs to
\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0},\underline{k})
. We consider the map
v^{+}=\Psi_{\underline{k}}(\psi^{+})\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. We have
v^{+}(x_{0})=\psi^{+}(x_{0})=0
. Hence, the triangular inequality results in

v^{+}(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\{\underline{k}(x_{0},y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\}=\underline{k}(x_{0},x)

We showed that
v^{+}=\Psi_{\underline{k}}(\psi^{+})=\underline{k}(x_{0},\cdot)
.

Additionally, for all
\psi\in\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0},\underline{k})
such that
\psi(x_{0})=0
, the domination condition yields for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}

\displaystyle\psi(x)\leq\inf_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\}\leq\psi(x_{0})+\underline{k}(x_{0},x)=\underline{k}(x_{0},x)=\psi^{+}(x)

Hence, for all
v=\Psi_{\underline{k}}(\psi)
, we have

\displaystyle v(x)=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\{\psi(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\}\leq\inf_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\{\psi^{+}(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\}=v^{+}(x)=\underline{k}(x_{0},x)

Since all elements
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
with
v(x_{0})=0
are of the form
\Psi_{\underline{k}}(\psi)
with
\psi\in\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0},\underline{k})
and
\psi(x_{0})=0
, we deduce the first identity of (
5.34
).

We now prove the second formula. For this formula, we use the representation formula of Theorem
1.5
. Consider
\psi^{-}:\underline{\mathbb{M}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\psi^{-}(x)=-\underline{k}(x,x_{0})
. We have by triangular inequality that

\psi^{-}(x)-\psi^{-}(y)=\underline{k}(y,x_{0})-\underline{k}(x,x_{0})\leq\underline{k}(y,x)

Thus,
\psi^{-}
belongs to
\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},\underline{k})
. We consider the map
v^{-}=\Psi_{\underline{k}}(\psi^{-})\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
and let
x_{1}
be in
\underline{\mathbb{M}}
such that
x_{0}\sim x_{1}
. As proved by identity (
5.33
), we have

\displaystyle v^{-}(x_{0})=\psi^{-}(x_{1})+\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})=-\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})+\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})=0

We claim that for all
x\in M
,

\underline{k}(x_{1},x)=\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})+\underline{k}(x_{0},x)

Indeed, we apply the triangular inequality (
5.25
) twice to get

\displaystyle\underline{k}(x_{1},x)

\displaystyle\leq\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})+\underline{k}(x_{0},x)

\displaystyle\leq\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})+\underline{k}(x_{0},x_{1})+\underline{k}(x_{1},x)

\displaystyle=\underline{d}(x_{0},x_{1})+\underline{k}(x_{1},x)=\underline{k}(x_{1},x)

where we used the definition
\underline{d}(x_{0},x_{1})=0
of
x_{0}\sim x_{1}
. Hence, we deduce the equality everywhere and the claim is proved.

Consequently, we get for all
y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}

\displaystyle\psi^{-}(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)=-\underline{k}(y,x_{0})+\underline{k}(y,x)\leq\underline{k}(x_{0},x)=-\underline{k}(x_{1},x_{0})+\underline{k}(x_{1},x)=\psi^{-}(x_{1})+\underline{k}(x_{1},x)

and

\displaystyle v^{-}(x)=\inf_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{\psi^{-}(y)+\underline{k}(y,x)\}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}\\
y\nsim x_{0}\end{subarray}}\{-\underline{k}(y,x_{0})+\underline{k}(y,x)\}

Let
\psi
be an element of
\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},\underline{k})
such that
\psi(x_{0})=0
and
v=\Psi_{\underline{k}}(\psi)
. The domination condition yields

\displaystyle\psi(x)\geq\sup_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{\psi(y)-\underline{k}(x,y)\}\geq\psi(x_{0})-\underline{k}(x,x_{0})=\psi^{-}(x)

Therefore,
v\geq v^{-}
and we obtain the desired identity.
∎

6
Applications

We explore several applications of the representation formula for different examples of Tonelli Hamiltonians.

First, we examine autonomous Tonelli Hamiltonians, for which we will prove Fathi’s Convergence Theorem
1.6
. Next, we will treat the case of Tonelli Hamiltonians with
N
-periodic or
\underline{p}
-recurrent Mather sets.

Additionally, the representation formula can be used to identify specific subsets of the non-wandering set
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
, such as
\fix(\mathcal{T})
and
\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})
. The representation of weak-KAM solutions of
\fix(\mathcal{T})
in the non-autonomous case was established by G. Contreras, R. Iturriaga, and H. Sánchez-Morgado in
[
CISM13
]
.

It should be noted that these applications are not direct results of the theorems but rather adaptations of their proofs.

6.1
The Autonomous Case

We consider the Autonomous framework. Let
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
be a Tonelli Hamiltonian with corresponding Lagrangian
L:TM\to\mathbb{R}
. In this case, the studied objects
\phi_{L}^{s,t}
,
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}
,
h^{s,t}
,etc.. verify
\phi_{L}^{s,t}=\phi_{L}^{t-s}
,
\mathcal{T}^{s,t}=\mathcal{T}^{t-s}
,
h^{s,t}=h^{t-s}
,etc..

Note that the minimizing measures used to define the Mather set in (
2.24
) are defined on
TM
instead of
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times TM
so that the Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}=\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is included in
TM
.

Let us first prove the following weaker version of Fathi’s Theorem
1.6
following ideas of P.Bernard and J-M.Roquejoffre used in
[
BR04
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

For an autonomous Tonelli Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
, we have
\Omega(\mathcal{T})=\fix(\mathcal{T})
and the representation formula is given by the following bijection

\begin{matrix}\Psi_{1}:\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},h^{\infty})&\longrightarrow&\Omega(\mathcal{T})\\
\psi&\longmapsto&\inf\limits_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{\psi(y)+h^{\infty}(y,\cdot)\}\end{matrix}

(6.1)

with its inverse being the restriction map

\begin{matrix}\Phi_{1}:\Omega(\mathcal{T})&\longrightarrow&\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},h^{\infty})\\
v&\longmapsto&v_{|\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\end{matrix}

(6.2)

Proof.

Let
v
in
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. Fix a point
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
with lift
\tilde{x}_{0}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
, set
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
and consider the weak-KAM solution
u(x)=h^{\infty}(x_{0},x)
. We will show that the map
f(t,x)=v(t,x)-u(t,x)
is constant on
\mathbb{R}\times\{x_{0}(\tau);\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}
. We know from Proposition
4.1
that the curve
x_{0}(t)
is calibrated by both
u
and
v
, and we have

u(t,x_{0}(t))-u(0,x_{0})=h^{t}(x_{0},x_{0}(t))=v(t,x_{0}(t))-v(0,x_{0})

Hence, we get

f(t,x_{0}(t))=v(t,x_{0}(t))-u(t,x_{0}(t))=v(0,x_{0})-u(0,x_{0})=f(0,x_{0})

and
f(t,x_{0}(t))
is constant on time. Moreover, we know from the regularity on calibrated curves Proposition
2.20
that for all
y\in\mathcal{M}
with lift
\tilde{y}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
, we have

d_{x}f(t,y)=d_{x}v(t,y)-d_{x}u(t,y)=\partial_{v}L(\tilde{y})-\partial_{v}L(\tilde{y})=0

Thus, since the curve
x_{0}(t)
is
C^{1}
regular, we can integrate along it and get for any time
s\in\mathbb{R}

f(t,x_{0}(s))=f(t,x_{0})+\int_{0}^{s}d_{x}f(t,x_{0}(\tau)).\dot{x}_{0}(\tau)\;d\tau=f(t,x_{0})

and we deduce that that map
f(t,x)
is constant on the set
\mathbb{R}\times\{x_{0}(\tau);\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
. In particular, using the fact that
h^{\infty-n}(x_{0},x_{0})=h^{\infty}(x_{0},x_{0})=0
established in Proposition
5.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle v(-n,x_{0})

\displaystyle=v(-n,x_{0})-u(-n,x_{0})=f(-n,x_{0})=f(-n,x_{0}(-n))

\displaystyle=f(0,x_{0})=v(0,x_{0})-u(0,x_{0})=v(0,x_{0})=v(x_{0})

which stand for any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
. Consequently, and by definition of viscosity solutions, we get for all
(x,y)
in
\mathcal{M}\times M

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)=v(y)-v(-n,x)\leq h^{n}(x,y)

and taking the liminf on
n
, we obtain the domination

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)\leq h^{\infty}(x,y)

The identity (
5.34
) implies that for any
(x,y)
in
\mathcal{M}\times M
,

\displaystyle\underline{k}(x,y)=\max_{v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})}\{v(y)-v(x)\}\leq h^{\infty}(x,y)

and the Property
2
of Proposition
5.12
gives the inverse inequality, which results in the equality
\underline{k}=h^{\infty}
. Therefore, the bijection established in Theorem
1.5
translates into (
6.1
). And for all
v=\Psi_{1}(\psi)
for some
\psi\in\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},h^{\infty})
, Proposition
3.4
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}v(x)=v(1,x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{\psi(y)+h^{\infty+1}(y,\cdot)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{\psi(y)+h^{\infty}(y,\cdot)\}=v(x)

which justifies that
v
belongs to
\fix(\mathcal{T})
and hence that
\Omega(\mathcal{T})=\fix(\mathcal{T})
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

1.

of the Hamilton-jacobi equation (
6.3
).
∎

6.2
Periodic Viscosity Solutions

In this subsection, we present a representation formula for periodic viscosity solutions for a fixed integer period
n\geq 1
. This formula generalizes the representation formula for weak-KAM solutions i.e one-time periodic solutions, as established in
[
CISM13
]
.

To derive such a formula, we need to identify the appropriate domination barrier. This is provided by the
n
-barrier
h^{n\infty}
, which was first introduced by A. Fathi and J. N. Mather in
[
FM00
]
.

Definition 6.3
.

1.

We define the
n
-Peierls Barrier

h^{n\infty}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
as the
\underline{p}
-Peierls barrier for
p_{k}=n.k
, i.e

h^{n\infty}(x,y)=\liminf_{p\to\infty}h^{np}(x,y)

(6.7)

2.

We define the subset
\mathcal{M}_{n}
of the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
by

\mathcal{M}_{n}:=\{x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\;|\;h^{n\infty}(x,x)=0\}

(6.8)

3.

We define the map
d_{n}:\mathcal{M}_{n}\times\mathcal{M}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

d_{n}(x,y)=h^{n\infty}(x,y)+h^{n\infty}(y,x)

(6.9)

4.

We define the equivalence relation
\sim_{n}
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
by

x\sim_{n}y\Longleftrightarrow d_{n}(x,y)=0

(6.10)

5.

The
n
-static classes
are the equivalence classes of the equivalence relation
\sim_{n}
. We denote by
\mathbb{M}_{n}
the set of
n
-static classes. We assume that every class of
\mathbb{M}_{n}
is represented by an element of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
so that we have the inclusion
\mathbb{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}\subset\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

6.

We say that a map
\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}
is
n
-dominated on a set
X
if it is
h^{n\infty}
-dominated on
X
.

Remark 6.4
.

Analogously to Proposition
5.16
, and due to the triangular inequality (
5.6
) satisfied by the
n
-Peierls barrier
h^{n\infty}
, the map
d_{n}
is a pseudometric on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, which justifies that
\sim_{n}
is an equivalence relation.

We will work on the set
\dom(\mathbb{M}_{n},h^{n\infty})
of
n
-dominated maps on the set of
n
-static classes
\mathbb{M}_{n}
to prove Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We first show that the maps
\Psi_{n}
and
\Phi_{n}
are well defined. Let
\psi
be an element of
\dom(\mathbb{M}_{n},h^{n\infty})
and set
v=\Psi_{n}(\psi)
. We know from Property
3
of Proposition
5.2
that
h^{n\infty}(\cdot,y,\cdot)
is a viscosity solution for all
y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
. Hence, we infer from Proposition
3.4
that
v
is a viscosity solution and that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{n}v(x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}}\{\psi(y)+\mathcal{T}^{n}h^{n\infty}(y,\cdot)(x)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}}\{\psi(y)+h^{n\infty+n}(y,x)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y\in\mathbb{M}_{n}}\{\psi(y)+h^{n\infty}(y,x)\}=v(x)

Thus,
v\in\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})
and
\Psi_{n}
is well defined.

Now let
v
be an element of
\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})
and
x
and
y
be two points of
\mathbb{M}_{n}
. We have by definition of
v
that for any integer
k
,

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)=v(nk,y)-v(x)\leq h^{nk}(x,y)

and taking the liminf on
k
leads to

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)\leq h^{n\infty}(x,y)

We showed that
\Phi_{n}(v)=v_{|\mathbb{M}_{n}}
is
n
-dominated, which justifies the well-definition of the map
\Phi_{n}
.

The proof of the identity
\Psi_{n}\circ\Phi_{n}=Id_{\dom(\mathbb{M}_{n},h^{n\infty})}
is analogous what was done in the proof of Theorem
5.9
. We show that
\Phi_{n}\circ\Psi_{n}=Id_{\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})}
. Let
v
be an element of
\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})
and set
w=\Phi_{n}\circ\Psi_{n}(v)\in\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})
. We aim to prove that
w=v
. By the domination condition and following the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we get successively that
v_{|\mathbb{M}_{n}}=w_{|\mathbb{M}_{n}}
and
v_{|\mathcal{M}_{n}}=w_{|\mathcal{M}_{n}}
. Let
x
be an element of the Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
with lift
\tilde{x}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and set
x(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x})
. By compactness of
M
, there exist an increasing sequence
k_{i}
of integers such that
\lim_{i}k_{i+1}-k_{i}=+\infty
and
x(-nk_{i})
converges to a point
x_{\alpha}
belonging to the closed set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. We set
x_{\alpha}(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{x}_{\alpha})
with
\tilde{x}_{\alpha}
is the lift of
x_{\alpha}
to
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
. By Proposition
4.1
, the curve
x_{\alpha}(t)
is calibrated by the
n
-periodic viscosity solutions
v
and
w
. Hence, using the non-negativity
4
of Proposition
5.3
and the liminf Properties (
5.5
), we get

\displaystyle 0\leq h^{n\infty}(x_{\alpha},x_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{i}h^{n(k_{i+1}-k_{i})}(x(-nk_{i+1}),x(-nk_{i}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}v(-nk_{i},x(-nk_{i}))-v(-nk_{i+1},x(-nk_{i+1}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}v(0,x(-nk_{i}))-v(0,x(-nk_{i+1}))

\displaystyle=v(x_{\alpha})-v(x_{\alpha})=0

Thus,
x_{\alpha}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Therefore, we obtain that
v(x_{\alpha})=w(x_{\alpha})
, and by calibration

\displaystyle w(x)

\displaystyle=w(-nk_{i},x(-nk_{i}))+h^{nk_{i}}(x(-nk_{i}),x)

\displaystyle=w(x(-nk_{i}))+h^{nk_{i}}(x(-nk_{i}),x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}w(x(-nk_{i}))+h^{nk_{i}}(x(-nk_{i}),x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}w(x_{\alpha})+h^{nk_{i}}(x(-nk_{i}),x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}v(x_{\alpha})+h^{nk_{i}}(x(-nk_{i}),x)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i}v(x(-nk_{i}))+h^{nk_{i}}(x(-nk_{i}),x)=v(x)

which yields
v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}=w_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}
and by the Uniquenesse Theorem
4.4
,
v=w=\Phi_{n}\circ\Psi_{n}(v)
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

1.

We can derive from this proof and that of Theorem
4.4
the following uniqueness theorem for periodic viscosity solutions

Proposition 6.6
.

For any viscosity solutions
v\in\per_{n}(\mathcal{T})
and
w\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
, if
v_{|\mathcal{M}_{n}}=w_{|\mathcal{M}_{n}}
, then
v=w
.

2.

The generalization presented here can be considered a direct application of the representation of weak-KAM solutions demonstrated in
[
CISM13
]
, as
n
-periodic solutions can be viewed as weak-KAM solutions of the Hamiltonian
H_{n}=nH(nt,x,p)
.

This theorem allows us to more easily describe the non-wandering set of systems in which every element of the Mather set is periodic with a uniform integer period, as stated in Corollary
1.8
.

Proof of Corollary
1.8
.

1.8
. It suffices to prove that for any
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
,
v_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}}
is
N
-dominated on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Let
x
and
y
be two points of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Let
\tilde{y}
be the lift of
y
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
and set
y(t)=\pi\circ\phi^{t}_{L}(\tilde{y})
. If
u
is a weak-KAM solution, we know from Proposition
4.1
that
y(t)
is calibrated by both
u
and
v
. Hence, for all integer
k\geq 0
, we have

\begin{split}v(kN,y)-v(0,y)&=v(kN,y(kN))-v(0,y)=h^{kN}(y,y(kN))\\
&=u(kN,y(kN))-u(0,y)=u(0,y)-u(0,y)=0\end{split}

where we used the
N
-periodicity of
y(t)
and the
1
-time periodicity of the weak-KAM solution
u
. Thus, by definition of viscosity solutions, we get

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)=v(kN,y)-v(x)\leq h^{kN}(x,y)

and taking the liminf on
k
, we conclude that

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)\leq h^{N\infty}(x,y)

and in particular,
v_{|\mathbb{M}_{N}}
belongs to
\dom(\mathbb{M}_{N},h^{N\infty})
. Therefore,
v=\Psi_{N}(v_{|\mathbb{M}_{N}})
belongs to
\per_{N}(\mathcal{T})
. and we conclude that
\Omega(\mathcal{T})=\per_{N}(\mathcal{T})
.
∎

6.3
\underline{p}
-Recurrent Viscosity Solutions

In this section, we fix a sequence
\underline{p}
of increasing positive integers and we discuss to which extend it is possible to represent
\underline{p}
-recurrent viscosity solutions.

6.3.1
p
-Domination

We define the natural domination notion associated to
\underline{p}
-recurrence.

Definition 6.7
.

a map
\psi:X\to\mathbb{R}
is said
\underline{p}
-dominated
in a set
X\subset M
if it is
h^{\underline{p}}
-dominated.

Then we have the following

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
be a
\underline{p}
-recurrent viscosity solution. Then
v
is
\underline{p}
-dominated in
M
.

Proof.

By definition of viscosity solution, we have for any
x
and
y
in
M

\displaystyle v(y)-v(x)=\lim_{n}v(p_{n},y)-v(x)\leq\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(y,x)=h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)

∎

Remark 6.9
.

(Failure of Reprenting
\underline{p}
-recurrent viscosity solutions)

1.

(Failure of Uniqueness) Unlike what was observed for periodic viscosity solutions in Remark
6.5
, there is no uniqueness theorem in the set

\mathcal{M}_{\underline{p}}:=\{x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}\;|\;h^{\underline{p}}(x,x)=0\}

(6.11)

Hence, if one hopes to get a representation formula of
\underline{p}
-recurrent viscosity solutions, it must be on the entire Mather set
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

2.

(Failure of
\underline{p}
-recurrence for
h^{\underline{p}}
) If we define the injective map

\begin{matrix}\tilde{\Psi}_{\underline{p}}\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0},h^{\underline{p}})&\longrightarrow&\Omega(\mathcal{T})\\
\psi&\longmapsto&\inf\limits_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}\{\psi(y)+h^{\underline{p}}(y,\cdot)\}\end{matrix}

(6.12)

Then, by the
\underline{p}
-domination Proposition
6.8
, we get that all
\underline{p}
-recurrent maps belong to the image
\tilde{\Psi}_{\underline{p}}(\dom(\mathcal{M}_{0},h^{\underline{p}}))
. However, the
\underline{p}
-recurrence of
h^{\underline{p}}(x,\cdot)
does not generally hold if
x
is not
\underline{p}
-recurrent under the projected Lagrangian flow. Even if this condition were satisfied, there is no guarantee that the infimum
v
of
\underline{p}
-recurrent viscosity solutions
v_{n}
would be
\underline{p}
-recurrent, unless there is uniform
\underline{p}
-recurrence for
v_{n}
.

Nevertheless, a representation formula using the
\underline{p}
-barrier
h^{\underline{p}}
is possible when it coincides with the general barrier
\underline{h}
defined in Subsection
5.2.1
. In the next subsections, we will consider cases where we can choose
\underline{h}=\underline{h}^{p}
.

6.3.2
The Representation Formula for Mather Sets with
\underline{p}
-Recurrent Elements

In this section, we assume that there exists a sequence
\underline{p}
of increasing positive integers for which the Mather set
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
verifies

\forall\tilde{x}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0},\quad\lim_{n}\phi_{L}^{-p_{n}}(\tilde{x})=\tilde{x}

(6.13)

Proposition 6.10
.

In this case, for all
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
z
in
M

h^{\underline{p}}(x,z)\leq h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)+h^{\underline{p}}(y,z)

(6.14)

and the following equality holds

\underline{k}=h^{\underline{p}}_{|\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M}

(6.15)

Proof.

We choose
\underline{h}(x,y)=h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)
. Fix three
x
,
y
in
\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
z
in
M
. The triangular inequality (
2.29
) on the potential
h
gives for all integer
n\geq 0
,

h^{\underline{p}+p_{n}}(x,z)\leq h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)+h^{p_{n}}(y,z)

We know from Property
4
of Proposition
5.8
that the viscosity solution
\underline{h}(x,\cdot)=h^{\underline{p}}(x,\cdot)
is
\underline{p}
-recurrent. Thus, taking the liminf on
n
in the preceding inequality yields

\displaystyle h^{\underline{p}}(x,z)=\lim_{n}h^{\underline{p}+p_{n}}(x,z)

\displaystyle\leq h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)+\liminf_{n}h^{p_{n}}(y,z)=h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)+h^{\underline{p}}(y,z)

The triangular inequality results in
\underline{h}\leq\underline{k}
on
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{R}\times M=\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M
. And since the inverse inequality is given by (
5.24
), we deduce the equality
h^{\underline{p}}=\underline{h}=\underline{k}
on
\mathcal{M}_{0}\times M
.
∎

Following this proposition, we can apply Theorem
1.5
to obtain

Theorem 6.11
.

In this case, the representation formula is given by the following bijection

\begin{matrix}\Psi_{\underline{p}}:\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},h^{\underline{p}})&\longrightarrow&\Omega(\mathcal{T})\\
\psi&\longmapsto&\inf\limits_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\{\psi(y)+h^{\underline{p}}(y,\cdot)\}\end{matrix}

(6.16)

with its inverse being the restriction map

\begin{matrix}\Phi_{\underline{p}}:\Omega(\mathcal{T})&\longrightarrow&\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},h^{\underline{p}})\\
v&\longmapsto&v_{|\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\end{matrix}

(6.17)

Remark 6.12
.

1.

Note that, even if every recurrent viscosity solutions
v\in\Omega(\mathcal{T})
are expressed as the infimum of
\underline{p}
-recurrent ones, it does not imply that
v
is itself
\underline{p}
-recurrent. This is due to the non-uniform
\underline{p}
-recurrent of the viscosity solutions
h^{\underline{p}}(x,\cdot)
for
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

Finding a sequence
\underline{q}
such that
v
is
\underline{q}
-recurrent remains highly non-trivial, even in this case.

2.

It is possible to verify through the proof of Theorem
1.5
that this formula remains valid if we assume that for all
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
, either
\lim_{n}\phi_{L}^{+p_{n}}(\tilde{x})=\tilde{x}
or
\lim_{n}\phi_{L}^{-p_{n}}(\tilde{x})=\tilde{x}
. Positive or negative time recurrence does not matter for
h^{\underline{p}}
to satisfy the triangular inequality. Hence,
d_{\underline{p}}(x,y)=h^{\underline{p}}(x,y)+h^{\underline{p}}(y,x)
is a pseudometric on
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, making it possible to define its associated equivalence relation
\sim_{\underline{p}}
and
\underline{p}
-static classes
\mathbb{M}_{\underline{p}}
. Since a uniqueness result on
\mathbb{M}_{\underline{p}}
holds, the remainder of the proof follows analogously.

3.

This Representation Formula is still valid if we only had
\phi_{L}^{1}
-recurrence on a dense subset
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{0}
of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
. In this case, we would still have by continuity that
\underline{k}=h^{\underline{p}}
and a uniqueness theorem on
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{0}
.

4.

Following this last remark, this case includes the situation where the Mather set contains a dense set of periodic curves with integer periods. If
q_{n}
is the sequence of these periods, we take
p_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n}q_{k}
.

6.3.3
The Representation Formula for Mather Sets with Uniformly
\underline{p}
-Recurrent Elements

We assume that there exists an increasing sequence of positive integers
\underline{p}
such that
\phi^{-p_{n}}_{L|\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}
uniformly converges to the identity.

Proposition 6.13
.

In this case, the maps
h^{\underline{p}}(x,\cdot)
are uniformly
\underline{p}
-recurrent. Their convergence speed is controlled by the convergence of
\phi^{-p_{n}}_{L|\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}}
to the identity. More precisely, if for any
y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
with lift
\tilde{y}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
we set the curve
y(t)=\pi\circ\phi_{L}^{t}(\tilde{y})
, then

\|\underline{h}^{p_{n}}(x,\cdot)-\underline{h}(x,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq 2\kappa_{1}.\sup_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}d(y(-p_{n}),y)

(6.18)

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of identity (
5.11
).
∎

The representation formula results in the following corollary which implies Corollary
1.9
.

Corollary 6.14
.

All the elements
v
of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
are
\underline{p}
-recurrent and

\|v(p_{n},\cdot)-v\|_{\infty}\leq 2\kappa_{1}.\sup_{y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}d(y(-p_{n}),y)

(6.19)

Proof.

Let
v
be an element of
\Omega(\mathcal{T})
. By the Representation Theorem
6.11
, there exists a
\underline{p}
-dominated map
\psi\in\dom(\underline{\mathbb{M}},h^{\underline{p}})
such that
v=\Psi_{\underline{p}}(\psi)
. Then, Proposition
3.4
shows that for all positive integer
n

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{n}v(x)

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\;\big\{\psi(y)+\mathcal{T}^{n}h^{\underline{p}}(y,\cdot)(x)\}\big\}

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in\underline{\mathbb{M}}}\;\big\{\psi(y)+h^{\underline{p}+n}(y,x)\big\}

Moreover, we infer from Proposition
6.13
that for all
x\in M
,
y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
and
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\big|[\psi(y)+h^{\underline{p}+p_{n}}(y,x)]-[\psi(y)+h^{\underline{p}}(y,x)]\big|

\displaystyle=|h^{\underline{p}+p_{n}}(y,x)-h^{\underline{p}}(y,x)|\leq 2\kappa_{1}.\sup_{z\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}d(z(p_{n}),z)

where the bound is uniform on
y\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
. Taking the infimum on
y
yields

\displaystyle||v(p_{n})-v||_{\infty}\leq 2\kappa_{1}.\sup_{z\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}d(z(p_{n}),z)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty

and
v
is a
\underline{p}
-recurrent viscosity solution.
∎

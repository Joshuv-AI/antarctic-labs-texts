Tumor recurrence risk following different COS regimens in infertile women with borderline ovarian tumors: impact on IVF outcomes
Authors: Liang Liang, Yuan Li, Xiu-Mei Zhen, Jie Yan, Xue-Ling Song, Rong Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundControlled ovarian stimulation (COS) is a feasible method for treating borderline ovarian tumors (BOTs) after fertility-sparing surgery (FSS). The recurrence rate does not appear to be higher in women treated with COS, and it is important to assess its effects on the pregnancy rate. There have been no reports comparing different COS regimens in patients with BOTs.MethodsThis retrospective observational study included 73 BOT patients who underwent COS. Patients were divided into two groups according to the COS regimen: the mild stimulation group (36 patients) and the agonist/antagonist cycle group (37 patients). In this study, the effects of the COS regimens on pregnancy outcomes and the risk of tumor recurrence were compared.ResultsThe follow-up time among the 73 patients was 61 (7–156) months. The proportion of patients with a serous histopathology and a micropapillary architecture was 33.3% in the mild stimulation group, which was significantly greater than that in the agonist/antagonist cycle group (P = 0.045). The initial dose of gonadotropin (Gn), the total dose of Gn, the duration of Gn treatment, and estradiol (E2) on trigger day were significantly lower in the mild stimulation group than in the agonist/antagonist cycle group. Furthermore, the number of oocytes retrieved, the number of good-quality embryos produced and the number of embryos frozen was also significantly lower in the mild stimulation group. The cumulative live birth rate (CLBR) was 46.4% (13/28) in the mild stimulation group and 53.3% (16/30) in the agonist/antagonist cycle group, and the difference between the two groups was not significant (P = 0.599). E2 on trigger day (HR: 15.217, 95%CI: 1.215–190.595, P = 0.035) and the total dose of Gn (HR: 12.578, 95%CI: 1.035–152.888, P = 0.047) were independent factors affecting tumor-related outcomes. However, the COS protocol had no significant effect on the tumor-related outcome (P = 0.122).ConclusionE2 on trigger day and the total dose of Gn are independent factors affecting the tumor-related outcome. Overstimulation of the ovaries should be avoided. Mild stimulation regimens and agonist/antagonist cycle regimens are both safe and effective for treating infertile patients with BOTs. Mild stimulation is more suitable and safer for patients with high-risk factors such as recurrence prior to COS and a serous histopathology with a micropapillary architecture.Keywords: Borderline ovarian tumor, Recurrence, Pregnancy outcome, Infertility, Controlled ovarian stimulation

Introduction

Borderline ovarian tumors (BOTs) are a special type of tumor whose biological behaviors lie between those of benign and malignant ovarian tumors and are characterized by atypical proliferation and a specific recurrence rate [1]. BOTs are most common in women of childbearing age, and 1/3 of affected patients are < 40 years old [2]. The overall 5-year and 10-year survival rates of women with early BOTs (stage I) are 99% and 97%, respectively [3]. Therefore, fertility counseling should form part of the clinical management of BOTs. For early BOT patients who desire offspring of their own, fertility-sparing surgery (FSS) involving preservation of the uterus and at least partial ovarian preservation can be chosen [4]. Although the recurrence rate after FSS is higher than that after radical surgery, the pathological type after recurrence is almost borderline and does not affect the overall survival rate [5].

BOT patients also often present with infertility. Approximately 10–35% of patients have a history of infertility before treatment, and pelvic adhesions and impaired ovarian reserve following FSS further reduce the likelihood of natural conception [3]. A cohort study of 52 women with BOTs and a desire for pregnancy revealed that the pregnancy rate after FSS was 63%, but 26% of the live births were obtained after controlled ovarian stimulation (COS) [6]. The key issue for BOT patients receiving COS is tumor recurrence. The overexpression of estrogen receptor is a common feature of ovarian tumors and is relatively high in patients with BOTs; thus, the increase in estrogen induced by COS may theoretically adversely affect the outcomes of patients with BOTs. However, although there is no evidence that COS increases the risk of BOT recurrence [7, 8], consensuses worldwide recommend that patients with BOT avoid excessive COS [2, 9]. Accordingly, there has been a tendency to choose letrozole (LE) alone or in combination with low-dose gonadotropin (Gn) to reduce estrogen levels during COS [10], but usually fewer eggs are obtained, fewer embryos are available, and a single COS cycle is less likely to achieve a satisfactory pregnancy outcome [11], resulting in the possibility of multiple COS cycles. A systematic review revealed that 37% of relapses occurred in the first 2 years, and 31% occurred after 2 to 5 years [12]. Therefore, the effectiveness and safety of COS should be considered for BOT patients after FSS. At present, comparisons of different COS regimens have not been reported.

This retrospective study of BOT patients who underwent FSS was conducted to explore the effectiveness and safety of clinical outcomes of different COS regimens and to evaluate the risk factors for BOT recurrence.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This study included 73 patients with BOTs who underwent in vitro fertilization (IVF) at the Reproductive Medical Center of Peking University Third Hospital from January 2008 to June 2022, and their clinical and laboratory data were collected and analyzed retrospectively. The overall design of the study is shown in Fig. 1. This study was approved by the Peking University Third Hospital Medical Science Research Ethics Committee (ethics no. 2019SZ018). Before IVF treatment, all patients were made fully aware of the risk of tumor recurrence and COS treatment. All patients signed informed consent forms regarding the use of their clinical and pathological data for research purposes. Details of the treatment and different regimens are described below.

Flow chart of the analysis cohort

Use of fertility-sparing surgery (FSS)

FSS, that is, surgery involving preservation of the uterus and at least part of one ovary, includes cystectomy (C), unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (USO), bilateral cystectomy (BC) and USO + contralateral cystectomy (CC).

Controlled ovarian stimulation (COS) protocols

Fertility experts comprehensively assessed each patient referred to the reproductive center for infertility. Protocols involving gonadotrophin-releasing hormone agonists (GnRH-a), GnRH antagonists (GnRH-ant), or mild stimulation were used for ovarian stimulation in the patients.

Antagonist protocol

COS was initiated on day 2 of the menstrual cycle. An initial dose of 150–300 IU Gn was administered until triggering occurred. When the dominant follicle diameter was ≥ 12 mm, a GnRH-ant (Cetrotide, Merck Serono, Germany) was administered at 0.25 mg/day. Usually, oocyte maturation was triggered with recombinant human chorionic gonadotropin (OVIDREL, Merck Serono S.p.A., Italy) when the mean diameter of two follicles was ≥ 18 mm. Oocyte retrieval was scheduled 36 ~ 38 h later.

Agonist protocols

Long GnRH-a protocol: The subcutaneous injection of GnRH-a (Triptorelin, IPSEN Pharma Biotech, France) at a dose of 1.25 mg was commenced in the middle of the luteal phase of the previous menstrual cycle. Gonadotropin was administered at 150 ~ 300 IU until trigger day.

Short GnRH-a protocol: From the second to the sixth day of the menstrual cycle, 0.1 mg/d short-acting GnRH-a was injected subcutaneously, and Gn was administered on the third to the fifth day of the menstrual cycle until trigger day.

Mild ovarian stimulation protocol

Letrozole (2.5 mg/d, Haizheng Pharmaceutical Co., China) was initiated on day 2 of a cycle of 5 consecutive days. After 2 days of letrozole administration, Gn was added (75–225 IU). When the size of the leading follicle reached ≥ 14 mm, 0.25 mg/day of the GnRH-ant was added until trigger day.

Either collected oocytes were inseminated via conventional IVF or embryos were cultured up to day 3 or day 5 following oocyte retrieval, followed by embryo transfer (ET). The decision regarding the day of ET was mostly based on the policy of our center, which was to expand the embryo culture to day 3 if it was of good quality (at least 5–8 cells with a maximum of 30% fragmentation). Blastocyst morphology was assessed on day 5 using the Gardner score.

Embryo transfer (ET)

No more than two cleavage-stage embryos or one blastocyst were transferred into the uterus. In the case of hydrosalpinx, which is associated with a high risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, or some other cause, ET was cancelled, and all the embryos were vitrified. Patients who failed to achieve a live birth after ET underwent cryopreserved cycles until all the vitrified embryos were transferred or until a live birth was achieved. Surplus transferrable embryos were frozen for subsequent frozen‑thawed embryo transfer (FET) cycles. In the FET cycle, the endometrium was prepared using natural cycle or hormone replacement therapy. Luteal phase support was administered according to our institutional protocol [13].

Follow-up after the end of infertility therapy and recurrence

Patient follow-up consisted of physical and gynecological examinations, CA-125 determination and an ultrasound examination at least every 6 months during the first year after the procedure and then yearly thereafter. The recurrence events in the study were confirmed via surgery and histopathology.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY) was used for statistical analysis. Continuous variables with a normal distribution are represented as the mean (standard deviation, SD), whereas continuous variables with a nonnormal distribution are expressed as the median (quartiles). Continuous variables with a normal distribution were analyzed with the t test, and continuous variables with a nonnormal distribution were analyzed with the Mann‒Whitney U test. Count data are expressed as percentages and were analyzed by the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact probability method. A Cox regression model was used to analyze the tumor outcomes. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistically significant differences.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients treated with different COS protocols

The first COS cycle of the 73 BOT patients was analyzed retrospectively. The patients were divided into two groups according to the COS regimen: the mild stimulation group (36 patients) and the agonist/antagonist cycle group (37 patients). The average age of the two groups was 31.40 (3.72) years. There was no significant difference in the levels of basal sex hormones (FSH, LH, E2) or in the antral follicle count (AFC) between the groups (Table 1).

The proportion of patients demonstrating a serous histopathology with a micropapillary architecture was 33.3% in the mild stimulation group, which was significantly greater than that in the agonist/antagonist group (P = 0.045). Additionally, recurrence prior to COS was more common in the mild stimulation group than in the agonist/antagonist cycle group (25.0% vs. 8.1%, P = 0.052). The interval between surgery and initial COS was 20 (3–146) months, and the follow-up time was 61 (7–156) months. One patient who underwent a mild stimulation cycle developed ovarian mucinous carcinoma, stage IIA, and underwent a comprehensive staging operation and 6 courses of postoperative chemotherapy. Currently, this patient has not experienced recurrence. The other 14 patients who experienced recurrence had pathology that remained BOT.

Stimulation characteristics and results in each COS group

As shown in Table 2, the initial dose of Gn, the total dose of Gn, the duration of Gn treatment, and E2 on trigger day were significantly lower in the mild stimulation group than in the agonist/antagonist cycle group. Furthermore, the number of oocytes retrieved, the number of good-quality embryos produced and the number of embryos frozen was also significantly lower in the mild stimulation group; however, there was no significant difference in the percentage of good-quality embryos between the two groups. The cumulative live birth rate(CLBR) was 46.4% (13/28) in the mild stimulation group and 53.3% (16/30) in the agonist/antagonist cycle group, but the between-group difference was not significant (P = 0.599).

COS regimens and tumor recurrence

Forty-one of the 73 patients received only a single cycle of COS. As indicated in Table 3, continuous variables were converted into categorical variables according to clinical experience and related literature reports, and univariable Cox regression analysis was carried out. Patients with an interval between surgery and initial COS of less than 3 months were more likely to have a higher recurrence rate (HR: 0.198, 95%CI: 0.050–0.776, P = 0.020). A trigger day E2 concentration > 6000 pmol/L (HR: 6.952, 95%CI: 1.429–33.811, P = 0.016) was associated with an increased risk of tumor recurrence.

Discussion

The population of individuals with BOTs after FSS who have undergone COS is low. Most studies on this topic consists of case reports, case series of fewer than 20 patients and meta-analyses [3, 11]. In our study, we presented the data of patients with BOTs after FSS and COS from only our center over 15 years; all patients were treated by the same doctors and the same laboratory, which reduces the effects of treatment variability. Furthermore, we analyzed the acquisition of oocytes, pregnancy outcomes and tumor outcomes after different COS regimens in BOT patients with infertility after FSS treatment.

Clinical research has reported that the fertility of BOT patients is affected by the frequency of surgical intervention [14]; surgery can worsen the fertility prognosis because of adhesions and alterations in ovarian reserve, as demonstrated by a decrease in serum AMH levels [15]. The number of recurrences prior to COS in the mild stimulation group was greater than that in the agonist/antagonist group (25.0% vs. 8.1%), which indicated that more than one operation was needed before COS in our study. The selection of a mild stimulation protocol may be linked to diminished ovarian reserve; however, no significant differences were observed in baseline sex hormone levels or AFC between the two groups. These findings may be attributed to the small sample size and the lack of AMH analysis—a limitation resulting from the extended recruitment period and evolving criteria for assessing ovarian reserve during the study. A meta-analysis revealed that mild stimulation reduced the E2 level on trigger day and the number of oocytes and embryos obtained, but the proportions of high-quality embryos produced and the cumulative number of live births were similar [16]. In our study, the number of oocytes retrieved was lower in the mild stimulation group, however, both the good-quality embryo rate and the CLBR were comparable to other protocols. This supports the effectiveness of the mild stimulation protocol in achieving live births in patients with BOTs. Moreover, because of the lower Gn doses, shorter duration of medication and lower E2 on trigger day, a mild stimulation regimen appears to be the best option, when feasible, for patients with infertility and BOTs.

Previous studies have shown that a micropapillary structure is an independent risk factor for BOT recurrence [17, 18], which may affect the COS regimen options. In our study, the proportion of patients with serous BOTs with a micropapillary architecture was significantly greater in the mild stimulation group. This finding suggests that the vast majority of doctors agree that COS should be performed with caution in patients with serous tumors with a micropapillary architecture because of the potentially increased risk of recurrence. The retrospective nature of this study might be a source of selection bias. Although serous BOTs with a micropapillary architecture were more common in the mild stimulation group in our study, the recurrence rate was similar in the two groups. Moreover, there was an increasing trend for recurrence in the agonist/antagonist cycle group compared with the mild stimulation group (24.3% vs. 16.7%), which was similar to the recurrence rate of BOTs after FSS reported in previous literature [3, 19]. Because of the slightly greater Gn dose, the E2 level on trigger day was higher in the agonist/antagonist cycle group (P = 0.002), which was related to the slightly higher recurrence rate; the results showed that E2 on trigger day was an independent factor affecting tumor outcome. When E2 was > 6000 pmol/L, the risk of recurrence increased significantly. Based on the available literature, one might speculate that in COS, circulating estradiol is elevated because the development of multiple large follicles at once, which may worsen oncological outcomes in estrogen-sensitive cancers [20]. After FSS, some natural ‘silent’ borderline tumor cells may remain in situ [19, 21]. Moreover, oocyte retrieval by ovarian puncture can cause tumor cell dissemination [22]. Although it is not clear whether tumor recurrence is caused by residual minimal lesions or ovarian stimulation, our statistical analysis suggests that the lowest dose and optimal COS regimen should be used as much as possible. Mild stimulation is safer for BOT patients with a micropapillary histopathology to avoid COS leading to tumor cell proliferation and recurrence.

Live birth is a common goal of patients and reproductive doctors. Some scholars believe that more oocytes and transferable embryos can be obtained by using an agonist/antagonist regimen, which is positively related to the potential of live birth [23]. Khiat et al. reported the cases of 11 women who were treated with an antagonist regimen after conservative BOT surgery [24], and the average number of oocytes obtained was 14.1 (6.7), which suggests that the antagonist regimen did not increase the risk of recurrence of BOTs and could produce an ideal number of oocytes [24]. In the present study, the number of oocytes retrieved, number of good-quality embryos and number of embryos frozen were significantly greater in the agonist/antagonist group. Thus, one cycle of the agonist/antagonist regimen might positively affect the chance of pregnancy.

Khiat proposed the use of COS in patients with BOTs for one or more cycles to preserve at least 10–15 oocytes to maintain future fertility [24]. According to Cobo et al., storing 12 vitrified oocytes before the age of 35 can lead to a CLBR of 61.9% [25]. Considering that some scholars believe that ovarian paracentesis leads to continuous damage to and repair of ovarian tissue, which may lead to malignant transformation of ovarian epithelial cells [22], multiple punctures should be avoided. Some BOT patients may face the problem of fertility conservation. In our study, the mild stimulation group resulted in significantly fewer frozen-thawed embryos than the agonist/antagonist group. The single cycle in the agonist/antagonist group yielded a number of eggs similar to that recommended in the literature to achieve satisfactory live birth. Importantly, without increasing the risk of recurrence and controlling the E2 level on trigger day to < 6000 pmol/L, the advantage of the agonist/antagonist regimen was more obvious from the point of view of preserving fertility and achieving a satisfactory pregnancy outcome.

Our retrospective study provides some evidence for the selection of COS after FSS in patients with BOTs. As this was a retrospective study conducted at a single center, inherent biases exist. Due to the rarity of cases, the population of patients with BOTs after FSS who have undergone COS is limited, and our study is limited by its small sample size. Moreover, because of the different types of FSS, the patient population is heterogeneous. Our study did not have sufficient power to perform subgroup analysis on different types of FSS, and future research should focus on increasing sample sizes.

Conclusion

BOT patients who desire fertility preservation can be treated with COS if they have a history of infertility after FSS. E2 on trigger day and the total dose of Gn are independent factors affecting the tumor outcome. Ovarian overstimulation must be avoided. It is recommended that the E2 level on trigger day be kept under 6000 pmol/L and the total Gn dose per COS cycle be limited to 2500 IU. Mild stimulation regimens and agonist/antagonist cycle regimens are both safe and effective for infertile patients with BOTs and do not increase the risk of tumor recurrence. Moreover, mild stimulation is more suitable and safer for patients with high-risk factors, such as a serous histopathology with a micropapillary architecture. From the point of view of preserving fertility, the agonist/antagonist scheme is more advantageous.

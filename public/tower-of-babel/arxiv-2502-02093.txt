Model Independent Effective Dimension Six Operators And Scattering Unitarity


Abstract
The effective field theory containing higher dimensional operators violates the unitarity of the 2 → 2 2\rightarrow 2 scattering processes in the Standard Model. This unitarity violation depends on the values of the Wilson coefficients corresponding to a higher dimensional operator. We showed that even a small values of some of the Wilson coefficients lead to the unitarity violation at the LHC centre of mass energies. Considering the final states as the gauge bosons in the longitudinal mode, the scalars, and the t ​ t ¯ t\overline{t} , we showed that the unitarity violation in W ​ W → W ​ W WW\rightarrow WW , W ​ W → Z ​ Z WW\rightarrow ZZ , and Z ​ Z → h ​ h ZZ\rightarrow hh scattering amplitudes sets bounds on the Wilson coefficients of the dimension six effective operators.

The relevant bosonic and fermionic operators are given by,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm boson}=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\sum_{i}\overline{c}_{i}O_{i}=\frac{\overline{c}_{H}}{\Lambda^{2}}\partial^{\mu}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\partial_{\mu}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)+\frac{\overline{c}_{T}}{\Lambda^{2}}(\Phi^{\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D})^{\mu}\Phi)(\Phi^{\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\Phi)-\frac{\overline{c}_{6}\lambda}{\Lambda^{2}}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{3}

\displaystyle+\frac{ig\overline{c}_{W}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\ (\Phi^{\dagger}\tau^{i}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\Phi)\ (D^{\nu}W_{\mu\nu})^{i}+\frac{ig^{\prime}\overline{c}_{B}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\ (\Phi^{\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D}^{\mu}\Phi)\ (\partial^{\nu}B_{\mu\nu})+\frac{ig\overline{c}_{HW}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\ (D^{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}\tau^{i}(D^{\nu}\Phi)\ W^{i}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{ig^{\prime}\overline{c}_{HB}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\ (D^{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}(D^{\nu}\Phi)\ B_{\mu\nu}+\frac{{g^{\prime}}^{2}\overline{c}_{\gamma}}{\Lambda^{2}}\ (\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\ B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}+\frac{g_{s}^{2}\overline{c}_{g}}{\Lambda^{2}}\ (\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\ G^{a}_{\mu\nu}G^{a\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+\frac{g^{3}\overline{c}_{3W}}{\Lambda^{2}}\epsilon_{ijk}W^{i\nu}_{\mu}W^{j\alpha}_{\nu}W^{k\mu}_{\alpha}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm fermion}=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\sum_{j}\overline{c}_{j}O_{j}=\left(\frac{\overline{c}_{u}}{\Lambda^{2}}y_{u}\overline{Q}_{L}u_{R}\Phi^{c}\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi+\frac{i\overline{c}_{Hud}}{\Lambda^{2}}(\overline{u}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}d_{R})(\Phi^{c\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\Phi)+{\rm h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{i\overline{c}_{Hq}}{\Lambda^{2}}(\overline{Q}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{L})(\Phi^{\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\Phi)+\frac{i\overline{c}^{\prime}_{Hq}}{\Lambda^{2}}(\overline{Q}_{L}\tau^{i}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{L})(\Phi^{\dagger}\tau^{i}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\Phi)+\frac{i\overline{c}_{Hu}}{\Lambda^{2}}(\overline{u}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}u_{R})(\Phi^{\dagger}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\Phi)

\displaystyle+\frac{i\overline{c}^{\prime}_{Hd}}{\Lambda^{2}}(\overline{d}_{R}\tau^{i}\gamma^{\mu}d_{R})(\Phi^{\dagger}\tau^{i}\overleftrightarrow{D}_{\mu}\Phi)+\frac{g^{\prime}\overline{c}_{uB}}{\Lambda^{2}}y_{u}\overline{Q}_{L}\Phi^{c}\sigma_{\mu\nu}u_{R}B^{\mu\nu}+\frac{g\overline{c}_{uW}}{\Lambda^{2}}y_{u}\overline{Q}_{L}\tau^{i}\Phi^{c}\sigma_{\mu\nu}u_{R}W^{i\mu\nu}

The amplitude

2
Redefinition of fields and masses

To express the kinetic terms in their canonical form, the fields can be redefined as follows considering
\overline{c}_{i}v^{2}/\Lambda^{2}\ll 1
,

\displaystyle h\to h\left[1-\dfrac{\overline{c}_{H}}{\Lambda^{2}}v^{2}\right]\,,\,\,G^{a}_{\mu}\to G^{a}_{\mu}\left[1+\dfrac{\overline{c}_{G}}{\Lambda^{2}}g^{2}_{s}v^{2}\right]\,,\,\,W^{\pm}_{\mu}\to W^{\pm}_{\mu}\left[1+\dfrac{\overline{c}_{W}}{\Lambda^{2}}\dfrac{g^{2}}{4}v^{2}\right]\,,

\displaystyle Z_{\mu}\to Z_{\mu}\left[1+\dfrac{\overline{c}_{\gamma}}{\Lambda^{2}}{g^{\prime}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}v^{2}+\dfrac{\overline{c}_{W}}{\Lambda^{2}}\dfrac{g^{2}}{4}v^{2}+\dfrac{\overline{c}_{B}}{\Lambda^{2}}\dfrac{g^{\prime 2}}{4}v^{2}\right]\,,

\displaystyle A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}\left[1+\dfrac{\overline{c}_{\gamma}}{\Lambda^{2}}g^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}v^{2}\right]+Z_{\mu}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{W}-\overline{c}_{B}-8\overline{c}_{\gamma}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}}{\Lambda^{2}}\dfrac{gg^{\prime}}{4}v^{2}\,.

(3)

Eventually, the particle masses are also modified to

\displaystyle m^{2}_{h}=\left[1-2v^{2}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{H}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right](3\lambda v^{2}-\mu^{2})+\dfrac{15}{4}\lambda v^{4}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{6}}{\Lambda^{2}}\equiv 3\lambda v^{\prime 2}-\mu^{\prime 2}\,,

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z}=\dfrac{g^{2}{v^{\prime}}^{2}}{4\cos^{2}\theta_{W}}\left[1+g^{2}{v^{\prime}}^{2}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{W}}{2\Lambda^{2}}+{g^{\prime}}^{2}{v^{\prime}}^{2}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{B}}{2\Lambda^{2}}+2{g^{\prime}}^{2}{v^{\prime}}^{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{\gamma}}{\Lambda^{2}}-2{v^{\prime}}^{2}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{T}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle m^{2}_{W}=\dfrac{g^{2}{v^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}\left[1+g^{2}{v^{\prime}}^{2}\dfrac{\overline{c}_{W}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\right]\,,\quad{\rm with}\,\,v^{\prime 2}=v^{2}-\frac{v^{4}}{\Lambda^{2}}\left[2\overline{c}_{H}-\frac{5}{4}\overline{c}_{6}\right]\,.

(4)

Here,
v
and
v^{\prime}
are the Standard Model and redefined Higgs vevs respectively.

4
Conclusion

The introduction of the dimension six operators causes unitarity violation of some of the scattering amplitudes.
We consider here the dimension six operators of the SILH basis.
Some of the operators affect the canonical kinetic terms causing the modification of the fields and their masses and hence indirectly affect the vertices as shown in the Table
1
.
The Wilson coefficients come with a cut-off scale
\Lambda
, but always scale with
\Lambda^{2}
.
Therefore, we set
\Lambda=1
TeV to show the bounds on the WCs.
All the bounds are shown for
\sqrt{s}=2
TeV, the typical parton-level energy for the LHC.
We consider one non-zero operator at a time in this work.
Using optical theorem for zero scattering angle and zeroth partial wave
a_{0}
we showed the bounds on different Wilson coefficients when the terms linear in
c_{i}/\Lambda^{2}
are sufficient.

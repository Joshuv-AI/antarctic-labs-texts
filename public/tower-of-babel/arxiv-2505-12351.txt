Iwasawa theory for vertex-weighted graphs
Ryosuke Murooka, Sohei Tateno

Abstract
. Chung-Langlands established a matrix-tree theorem for positive-real valued vertex-weighted graphs, and Wu-Feng-Sato developed a theory of Ihara zeta functions for those graphs. In this paper, generalizing and refining these previous works, we initiate the Iwasawa theory for vertex-weighted graphs, which is a generalization of the Iwasawa theory for graphs initiated by Gonet and Vallières independently. First, we generalize the matrix-tree theorem by Chung-Langlands to arbitrary field-valued vertex-weighted graphs. Second, we refine and prove the so-called decomposition formula for vertex-weighted graphs and edge-weighted graphs without any assumption. Applying these results, we prove the Iwasawa-type formula and a refinement of Kida’s formula for ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -towers of vertex-weighted graphs. Our refinement of the decomposition formulas allows us to estimate the root-wise growth of weighted complexities in ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -towers. We also provide several numerical examples.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime number.
Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
denote the completion of the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
equipped with the
p
-adic absolute value, and let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote its valuation ring.
Fix an algebraic closure
\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and fix an embedding
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\hookrightarrow\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
Let
\operatorname{val}\nolimits_{p}
be the
p
-adic valuation of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
normalized so that
\operatorname{val}\nolimits_{p}(p)=1
, and we extend this to the valuation of
\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
uniquely.
Let
X
be a finite connected graph with
\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued weights on its edges, and let
\kappa(X)
denote the edge-weighted complexity of
X
defined in
[
8
]
.
In
[
1
]
, Adachi, Mizuno, and the second author discover that there is an Iwasawa-type formula for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-towers of
\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued edge-weighted graphs, that is,
for a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower

(1.1)

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{\infty}

over
X
,

(1.2)

\operatorname{val}\nolimits_{p}(\kappa(X_{n}))=(\mu p^{n}+\lambda n)p^{(d-1)n}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(\mu_{i}p^{n}+\lambda_{i}n)p^{(d-1-i)n}\right)+\nu

holds for all
n\gg 0
, where
\lambda
is an integer and where
\mu
,
\mu_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}
,
\nu
are rational numbers. This is a generalization of the Iwasawa-type formula for graphs initially proved by Gonet
[
6
]
and Vallières
[
12
]
independently. See
[
1
]
for more detailed histories of the Iwasawa theory for graphs.

In this paper, we show that the same theory works for
\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued vertex-weighted graphs, which suggests a possibility that sharpens the results in
[
1
]
.
The theory of vertex-weighted graphs has been investigated with positive real numbers weights.
For instance, in
[
2
]
, Chung and Langlands prove that a matrix-tree theorem works for
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-valued weighted graphs, and Wu, Feng, and Sato study some analogue of arithmetical properties in
[
13
]
.
A difference between edge-weighted graphs and vertex-weighted graphs is that weighted complexities of vertex-weighted graph heavily depend on the choice of roots, whereas one for “strongly” symmetric edge-weighted graphs does not depend on the choice of roots.
The independency of the choice of roots allows us to study them easier, but drops some their information and causes awkward division into cases as in the following theorems (rooted cases in Theorem
1.6
and Theorem
1.8
).

In
[
2
]
, Chung and Langlands establish the matrix-tree theorem for positive real-valued vertex-weighted simple graphs.
To study Iwasawa theory for vertex-weighted graphs, we first generalize their matrix-tree theorem for field-valued vertex weighted possibly non-simple graphs.
Let
K
be an arbitrary field, and let
X
be a
K
-valued vertex-weighted connected finite graph.

V(X)
stands for the set of the vertices of
X
, and
\mathbb{E}(X)
stands for the set of the directed edges of
X
. So each edge
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
has
origin
o(e)
and
terminus
t(e)
. Also, since the graphs we deal with are symmetric directed graphs, there is a unique edge
\overbar{e}
that represents the opposite direction.
Fix an algebraic closure
\overbar{K}
of
K
, fix an embedding
K\hookrightarrow\overbar{K}
, and fix square roots of the weights on the vertices of
X
.
For a spanning tree
T
of
X
and for a vertex
v
,
T_{v}
denotes the rooted tree of
T
towards
v
, that is, every edge has exactly one direction towards
v
.
The
weight
w(T_{v})
of
T_{v}
is
\prod_{e\in\mathbb{E}(T_{v})}w_{t(e)}
.
Now we can describe the rooted weighted complexities
\kappa_{v}(X)
and the weighted complexity
\kappa(X)
, in which we are mainly interested.
The
rooted (vertex-) weighted complexity
\kappa_{v}(X)
at
v
is
\sum w(T_{v})
, where the sum extends over all spanning trees of
X
.
The
(non-rooted vertex-) weighted complexity
\kappa(X)
is
\sum_{v\in V(X)}\kappa_{v}(X)
.

Our first result is the matrix-tree theorem for
K
-valued vertex-weighted graphs that are possibly non-simple.
The
degree matrix
\mathbfit{D}_{X}
is the diagonal matrix labeled by
V(X)
whose
(u,u)
- entry is given by

(1.3)

\mathbfit{D}_{X}(u,u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{E}(X)\colon\\
o(e)=u\end{subarray}}w_{t(e)}.

The
symmetrized weighted adjacency matrix
\mathcal{W}_{X}
is the matrix labeled by
V(X)
and whose
(u,v)
-entries are given by

(1.4)

\quad\mathcal{W}_{X}(u,v)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in\mathbb{E}(X)\colon\\
o(e)=u,\\
t(e)=v\end{subarray}}\sqrt{w_{u}}\sqrt{w_{v}}.

The
symmetrized Laplacian matrix
\mathcal{L}_{X}
of
X
is the difference
\mathbfit{D}_{X}-\mathcal{W}_{X}
.
We have two kinds of matrix-tree theorems.
One is a formula for rooted complexities.

Theorem 1.1

(= Corollary
2.18
)
.

Let
X
be a
K
-valued vertex-weighted connected finite graph.

(1.5)

\det(\mathcal{L}_{X}[\{v\},\{v\}])=\kappa_{v}(X),

where
\mathcal{L}_{X}[\{v\},\{v\}]
is the minor matrix deleting
v
-th row and
v
-th column.

The other is a formula for complexity.

Theorem 1.2

(= Theorem
2.20
)
.

Let
X
be a
K
-valued vertex-weighted connected finite graph.
Then

(1.6)

\raisebox{-3.22916pt}{\bigg(}\sum_{z\in V(X)}w_{z}\raisebox{-3.22916pt}{\bigg)}\operatorname{adj}\nolimits(\mathcal{L}_{X})(u,v)=\sqrt{w_{u}}\sqrt{w_{v}}\kappa(X),

where
\operatorname{adj}\nolimits(\mathcal{L}_{X})
is the classical adjoint matrix of
\mathcal{L}_{X}
.

Let
Y/X
be a finite Galois cover of edge-weighted graphs.
Then
h
-functions of
Y/X
mean the three-term-determinant part of Artin-Ihara
L
-functions.
An important property is that the edge-weighted complexity of
Y
factors into the edge-weighted complexity of
X
and values of
h
-functions of
Y/X
.
This factorization is originally given in
[
8
, Corollary 1]
with the assumption
\left(\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}(X)}w_{e}\right)/2-\#V(X)\neq 0
.
If
X
is non-weighted, that is, if all the weights
w_{e}
are 1, then this assumption means that the Euler characteristic
\chi(X)
of
X
is non-zero.
See
[
1
]
for more details of the background of
h
-functions.
In
[
13
, Cor. 1]
, they prove that, for a finite Galois cover
Y/X
of vertex-weighted graphs satisfying
\left(\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}(X)}w_{o(e)}w_{t(e)}\right)/2-\sum_{v\in V(X)}w_{v}\neq 0
,
\kappa(Y)
also factors into
\kappa(X)
and the value of “weighted”
h
-functions of
Y/X
.
We prove that the same factorization is valid for
rooted
weighted complexities without the assumption
\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}(X)}w_{e}\neq\#V(X)
.

Theorem 1.3

(= (
3.3
) in Theorem
3.3
)
.

Let
Y/X
be a finite Galois cover of
K
-valued vertex-weighted graphs whose Galois group is
G
, and let
v
be a vertex of
X
.
Let
\widehat{G}
be a full set of representatives of equivalence classes for the irreducible representations of
G
.
Then, for every vertex
w
of
Y
belonging to the fiber of
v
,

(1.7)

\kappa_{w}(Y)=\frac{\kappa_{v}(X)}{\#G}\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}\setminus\{\operatorname{triv}\nolimits_{G}\}}(h_{Y/X}(\rho,1))^{d_{\rho}},

where
\operatorname{triv}\nolimits_{G}
is the trivial representation of
G
and where
d_{\rho}
is the degree of
\rho
.
In particular,
\kappa_{w}(Y)
is constant on the fiber of
v
, so we would rather write
\kappa_{v}(Y)
than
\kappa_{w}(Y)
for
w
belonging to the fiber of
v
.

In
[
1
]
, the same type of Theorem
1.3
is shown assuming that
G
is abelian.
The same proof in our theorem is also valid for edge-weighted graph theory, and Theorem
1.3
gives an improvement of their result.
We remark also that our proof is a simple calculation of determinants of matrices, so we do not need the assumption that
\big(\sum_{e\in\mathbb{E}(X)}w_{o(e)}w_{t(e)}\big)/2-\sum_{v\in V(X)}w_{v}\neq 0
.
Hence our theorem sharpens
[
8
, Corollary 1]
.

Summing up all (
1.7
), we can deduce the factorization for weighted complexities in
[
13
, Cor. 1]
without the assumption
\sum_{v\in V(X)}w_{v}\neq\#V(X)
, which is tacitly assumed in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(= Theorem
3.3
(
3.4
))
.

Let
Y/X
be a finite Galois cover of
K
-valued vertex-weighted graphs whose Galois group is
G
.
Let
\widehat{G}
be a full set of representatives of equivalence classes for the irreducible representations of

.
Then

(1.8)

\kappa(Y)=\kappa(X)\prod_{\rho\in\widehat{G}\setminus\{\operatorname{triv}\nolimits_{G}\}}\big(h_{Y/X}(\rho,1)\big)^{d_{\rho}}.

Note that (

) involves

This fact affects the Iwasawa

-invariant for (

).

Now we embark on the Iwasawa theory for vertex-weighted graphs.
Let
K
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, let
\mathcal{O}
be the valuation ring of
K
, and let
\Lambda_{d,\mathcal{O}}
be the formal power series ring
\mathcal{O}\lBrack T_{1},\dotsc,T_{d}\rBrack
.
Then, for an element

of
\Lambda_{d,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
, we can extract a rational number
\mu(F)
and a integer
\lambda(F)
that are called Iwasawa
\mu
-invariant and
\lambda
-invariant of
F
respectively.
A fundamental result of multivariable Iwasawa theory is the following.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
1
, Theorem 3.5]
)
.

Put
W=\{\,\zeta\in\overbar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\;|\;\text{$\zeta^{p^{n}}=1$ for some non-negative integer $n$}\,\}
, and write

W^{d}(n)=\{\,\mathbfit{\zeta}\in W^{d}\;|\;\mathbfit{\zeta}^{p^{n}}=\mathbfit{1}\,\}
.
Let
F
be an non-zero element of
\Lambda_{d,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K
.
Assume that the function
\zeta\mapsto F(\mathbfit{\zeta}-\mathbfit{1})
does not vanish on
W^{d}
.
Then there exist
\mu_{1},\dotsc,\ \mu_{d-1},\ \lambda_{1},\dotsc,\ \lambda_{d-1},\nu\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

(1.9)

\sum_{\mathbfit{\zeta}\in W^{d}(n)\setminus\{\mathbfit{1}\}}\operatorname{val}\nolimits_{p}(F(\mathbfit{\zeta}-\mathbfit{1}))=(\mu(F)p^{n}+\lambda(F)n)p^{(d-1)n}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(\mu_{i}p^{n}+\lambda n)p^{(d-1-i)n}\right)+\nu

for all
n\gg 0
.

On the other hand, for a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower over
X
, there is an element
Q_{X^{\infty}/X}
of
\Lambda_{d,\mathcal{O}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}K

Q_{X^{\infty}/X}(\mathbfit{\zeta}-\mathbfit{1})=h_{X_{n}/X}(\mathbfit{\zeta},1)

1.5
if
\kappa_{v}(X_{n})\neq 0
for all

(or equivalently
\kappa(X_{n})\neq 0
for all

).
Therefore, combining (

) and (

), we have the following result.

4.6
)
.

)

The materials in this subsection is based on

Concretely speaking, we show that the vertex-weighted matrix-tree theorem works on some field-valued weights and on possibly non-simple graphs.

Definition 2.6

Let

0&\text{if $\xi\neq\psi$},\\
\end{cases}

, as desired.

\QED

1.
Introduction

For an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
F
, a classic theorem of Mordell and Weil shows that the group
E(F)
of
F
-rational points on
E
is a finitely generated abelian group. In particular, its torsion group
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
, which is the subgroup of points of finite order, is a finite abelian group.
It has been of great interest within the past half-century to understand
E(F)[\textrm{tors}]

uniformly,
i.e., for varying elliptic curves
E/F
.
In 1996, Merel
[
Mer96
]
proved his celebrated “strong uniformity” theorem: for each integer
d>0
, there exists a constant
B(d)>0
such that for all elliptic curves
E/F
where
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=d
, one has
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq B(d)
.
Merel’s work provided explicit bounds on primes
\ell\mid\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
in terms of
d
, which Parent
[
Par99
]
later improved by showing that if
\ell^{n}\mid\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
, then
\ell^{n}\leq 129(5^{d}-1)(3d)^{6}
.

By the work above, one can construct an explicit bound function
B\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for any number field
F
of degree
d
and for any elliptic curve
E/F
, one has
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq B(d)
. However, even by Parent’s work, such a function would be more than exponential in the degree
d
. Thus, the next natural question is the following:
which choice of function
B
is close to the “truth” of torsion growth?
One knows the truth for the family of elliptic curves with
complex multiplication
(CM): by work of Clark and Pollack
[
CP15
]
, there exists an absolute, effectively computable constant
c_{\textrm{CM}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, such that for all number fields
F/\mathbb{Q}
of degree
d\geq 3
and for all CM elliptic curves
E/F
, one has
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq c_{\textrm{CM}}\cdot d\log\log d
. This is best-possible by work of Breuer
[
Bre10
]
.

Despite our current understanding of torsion groups of CM elliptic curves, the non-CM case remains much more mysterious. Though a number of conjectures and questions appear in the literature concerning the truth of
B
in the non-CM case (see, for example,
[
HS99
,
\lx@sectionsign 3
]
), one of the least ambitious forms of a general conjecture involves bounds which are
polynomial
in the degree.

Conjecture
.

There exist absolute constants
b,c>0
such that for any number field
F
of degree
d
and for any elliptic curve
E/F
, one has
\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c\cdot d^{b}
.

That such bounds might exist was first suggested in a question of Flexor and Oesterlé
[
FO90
, Remarque 3]
for elliptic curves with everywhere good reduction, and the result was later established in this case by Hindry and Silverman
[
HS99
]
. More recent work of Clark and Pollack
[
CP18
, Theorem 1.3]
implies there exists polynomial bounds on torsion from the family
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant: for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\epsilon}>0
such that for all elliptic curves
E/F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, one has
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{5/2+\epsilon}
. They also give polynomial bounds in several other cases, including for
E/F
with
j(E)
lying in a fixed quadratic field that is not imaginary quadratic of class number one; see
[
CP18
, Theorem 1.5, 1.6, 1.7]
.

In this paper, we extend polynomial bounds from
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}
to a significantly larger family of elliptic curves. For two elliptic curves
E_{1}/F_{1}
and
E_{2}/F_{2}
, a
geometric isogeny
from
E_{1}
to
E_{2}
is a non-constant
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-rational morphism
\phi\colon E_{1}/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow E_{2}/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
which fixes base points. For an elliptic curve
E/F
, the collection of all elliptic curves geometrically isogenous to
E
is called its
geometric isogeny class.
Let us define the family

\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\{E/F~:~\exists E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}\textrm{ for which }E\textrm{ is geometrically isogenous to }E^{\prime}\}.

The family
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is essentially the union over all
rational
geometric isogeny classes, which are geometric isogeny classes which contain least one rational elliptic curve, and it has been studied in several previous works
[
CN21
,
BN
,
Gen23
,
BRW
]
.
We see that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and this is a strict containment since
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{Q}}
contains elliptic curves whose
j
-invariant has arbitrarily large degree over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Our first main result is the following, which generalizes
[
Gen24
, Theorem 1]
in the case of rational geometric isogeny classes. Here, we use “exp” to denote the exponent of a finite commutative group.

Theorem 1
.

Torsion from
\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is polynomially bounded. More precisely, for each
\epsilon>0
, there exists
c_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any elliptic curve
E/F\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, one has

\exp E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{2+\epsilon},

as well as

\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{3+\epsilon}.

Theorem
1
relies on a bound for torsion from
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, which is Theorem
2
below. This improves the group exponent bound from
[
CP18
, Theorem 1.3]
by a square-root factor of the degree. By
[
CCS13
, Theorem 6]
and its proof, this gives the optimal power bound on the exponent as we are not excluding CM elliptic curves. That is, for any
\epsilon>0
, we show the bound is of the order of
[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1+\epsilon}
, and by
[
CCS13
]
the 1 cannot be replaced with anything smaller.

Theorem 2
.

For each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any elliptic curve
E/F
with
j(E)\in\mathbb{Q}
, we have

\exp E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1+\epsilon},

as well as

\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{3/2+\epsilon}.

Our improved bounds for non-CM elliptic curves in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}
result from a more detailed analysis of the field of definition of torsion points
P\in E
whose orders are supported on primes
\ell>37
. Here, if
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
has
j(E^{\prime})=j(E)
, then the image of its mod-
\ell
Galois representation is either surjective, or is equal to the normalizer of a non-split Cartan subgroup; see work of Furio and Lombardo
[
FL
]
. In the first case, contributions to the degree of
\mathbb{Q}(P)
are essentially as large as possible by
[
BEL+19
, Proposition 5.7]
. Otherwise, both
[
FL
, Theorem 3.11]
and
[
LFL21
, Appendix B]
imply that
E^{\prime}
has potentially good reduction, and no canonical subgroup of order
\ell
. This allows us to make use of more refined ramification data due to Smith
[
Smi23
]
.

If one were to restrict to only non-CM elliptic curves, it is expected that our results can be significantly strengthened. For example, Hindry and Silverman
[
HS99
,
\lx@sectionsign 3
]
ask whether there exists a constant
c
such that for any non-CM elliptic curve
E
defined over
F
of degree
d
, one has
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]\leq c\sqrt{d\log\log d}
. Such a bound on torsion from non-CM elliptic curves with rational
j
-invariant would follow from an affirmative answer to Serre’s uniformity question; see
[
Bre10
,
\lx@sectionsign 4.2
]
.

2.
Background and Prior Results

2.1.
Results on Galois representations

Once and for all, fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Given an algebraic extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
and an elliptic curve
E/F
, one has a natural action of the absolute Galois group
G_{F}:=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/F)
on the group
E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
: for example, if
E
is given in Weierstrass form, then the action on points
P=(x,y)\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is given via
\sigma\cdot(x,y):=(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))
. For each positive integer
n
, this restricts to an action by group automorphisms on
E[n]
, the
n
-torsion subgroup of
E
. The corresponding group homomorphism is called the
mod-
n
Galois representation of
E
,
denoted by

\rho_{E,n}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[n]).

Fixing a
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
-basis
\{P,Q\}
of
E[n]
gives an isomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}(E[n])\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, so the image
\rho_{E,n}(G_{F})

is an explicit subgroup of matrices, up to conjugation.

For a number field
F
and an elliptic curve
E/F
without complex multiplication (henceforth “non-CM”), landmark work of Serre
[
Ser72
]
showed there exists a constant
\ell_{0}:=\ell_{0}(E/F)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that for all primes
\ell>\ell_{0}
, one has that the image
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})
is as large as possible: specifically,
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{F})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
. A natural question is whether one can choose a number
\ell_{0}:=\ell_{0}(F)

which works for all non-CM elliptic curves over
F
;
this is referred to as Serre’s uniformity question over
F
.

Serre’s uniformity question over
\mathbb{Q}
is still open, but has seen significant progress due to the contributions of many mathematicians – see
[
FL
, §1]
for a more complete history.
The next theorem is the best current progress towards answering this question. For an odd prime
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, fix the least positive integer
\epsilon
which generates
(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then recall that the
normalizer of a non-split Cartan subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
is (a conjugate of) the group

C_{ns}^{+}(\ell)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
b&a\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}a&b\epsilon\\
-b&-a\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right\}.

Theorem 3

(Furio, Lombardo
[
FL
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve, and let
\ell>37
be prime. Then
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is either
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
or
C_{ns}^{+}(\ell)
, up to conjugacy.

For each prime number
\ell
, the action of
G_{F}

on
E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
also induces an action on the
\ell
-adic Tate module
T_{\ell}(E):=\varprojlim E[\ell^{k}]
,
and is denoted by

\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}\colon G_{F}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\ell}(E))\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}),

where
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
denotes the ring of
\ell
-adic integers.

2.2.
The modular curves
X_{0}(n)
and
X_{1}(n)

For an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we have two distinguished subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
: the first is the
Borel subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
,
which is the subgroup of upper-triangular matrices, and is denoted
B_{0}(n)
. We also have the distinguished subgroup

B_{1}(n):=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\right\}.

These subgroups correspond to the
modular curves

X_{0}(n)
and
X_{1}(n)
, respectively, which can be regarded as algebraic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. We will often consider
closed points
on these curves, which correspond to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
-orbits of points over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. For a closed point
x
, we use
\mathbb{Q}(x)
to denote its residue field, and
[\mathbb{Q}(x):\mathbb{Q}]
is called the
degree
of
x
.

The non-cuspidal
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-valued points of
X_{0}(n)
parametrize elliptic curves
E/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with a fixed order
n
cyclic subgroup
C
. For a closed point
x\in X_{0}(n)
corresponding to
E/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with a cyclic subgroup
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of order
n
, there exists
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(x)
with
j(E^{\prime})=j(E)
for which the group corresponding to
C
is
\mathbb{Q}(x)
-rational, i.e., is stabilized under the action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}(x)}
. See
[
Cla
,
\lx@sectionsign 3
]
for details. Similarly, the non-cuspidal points of
X_{1}(n)
parametrize elliptic curves
E/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
with an order
n
torsion point
P
. For a closed point
x=[E,P]\in X_{1}(n)
, there exists
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(x)
with
j(E^{\prime})=j(E)
for which the point corresponding to
P
is
\mathbb{Q}(x)
-rational.
See
[
DR73
, p. 274, Proposition VI.3.2]
for details.

When working with
closed
points on modular curves, we will often write
E/F
for a model of
E/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
over a number field
F
containing
j(E)
to avoid introducing new letters for the “same” curve. This is permissible, since closed points correspond to orbits of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphism classes.

2.3.
Degrees of Points on
X_{1}(\ell^{k})
for
\ell>37

The following proposition essentially follows from work of Ejder
[
Ejd22
, Lemma 3.3]
. However, this work relies on
[
LR16
]
, which contains an error. We will reprove this result by applying work of Smith
[
Smi23
]
.

Proposition 4

(Ejder,
[
Ejd22
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve, and let
\ell>37
be prime. For
P\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of order
\ell^{k}
, let
x=[E,P]\in X_{1}(\ell^{k})
be the associated closed point on the modular curve. Then

\deg(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{2}-1)\ell^{2k-2}=\deg(X_{1}(\ell^{k})\rightarrow X_{1}(1)).

Proof.

If
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
, then in fact
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, and the conclusion holds. So we may assume
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})=C_{ns}^{+}(\ell)
by Theorem
3
. If
n=1
, a group theory argument shows that
[\mathbb{Q}(P):\mathbb{Q}]=\ell^{2}-1
; see
[
GJN20
, Lemma 5.2]
. Since
[\mathbb{Q}(P):\mathbb{Q}]\mid 2\deg(x)
and
\deg(x)\leq\deg(X_{1}(\ell)\rightarrow X_{1}(1))=\frac{1}{2}(\ell^{2}-1)
, the claim follows in this case. The general case will follow once we establish that
[\mathbb{Q}(P):\mathbb{Q}(\ell^{k-1}P)]=\ell^{2k-2}
when
k>1
.

By
[
FL
, Theorem 3.11]
and
[
LFL21
, Appendix B]
, we know
E
has potentially good reduction at
\ell
and does not have a canonical subgroup of order
\ell
. Let
R\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
be a point of order 5, and define
F:=\mathbb{Q}(R)
. Then
E/F
has good reduction at each prime above
\ell
by
[
Fre77
, Theorem 2]
.
Since
E
has no canonical subgroup at
\ell
, work of Smith
[
Smi23
, Theorem 1.1]
implies

\ell^{2k}-\ell^{2k-2}

divides the ramification index of any prime in
F(P)
lying above
\ell
, where
P
has order
\ell^{k}
. Hence
\ell^{2k}-\ell^{2k-2}
divides the degree of
F(P)/\mathbb{Q}
. It follows from
\ell>37
and
[F:\mathbb{Q}]\leq 24
that
[F(\ell^{k-1}P):\mathbb{Q}]
is relatively prime to
\ell
, and so

\ell^{2k-2}\mid[F(P):F(\ell^{k-1}P)].

The desired conclusion about
[\mathbb{Q}(P):\mathbb{Q}(\ell^{k-1}P)]
follows.
∎

3.
Lower Bounds for Degrees of Points on
X_{1}(n)

The following proposition is a prelude to Theorem
7
. It allows us to relate the degrees of points on
X_{1}(n)
and
X_{0}(n)
associated to a non-CM
\mathbb{Q}
-rational
j
-invariant directly to the level
n
, when
n
is supported on any set of uniformly large primes.

Proposition 5
.

Suppose
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is divisible only by primes
\ell>37
. Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve. Then for any
x=[E,P]\in X_{1}(n)
and
y=[E,\langle P\rangle]\in X_{0}(n)
, one has

\deg(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{24^{k}}\cdot n^{2}\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid n}\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\right)

and

\deg(y)\geq\frac{1}{24^{k}}\cdot n\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid n}\left(1+\frac{1}{\ell}\right),

where
k
denotes the number of primes
\ell
dividing
n
for which
\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\emph{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

We will first prove the lower bound on
\deg(x)
. Let

S_{E}\coloneqq\{2,3\}\cup\{\ell:\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\not\supset\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\}\cup\{5,\text{ if }\rho_{E,5^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\neq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})\}.

Suppose
n=\prod\ell_{i}^{a_{i}}\cdot\prod q_{j}^{b_{j}}
where
\ell_{i},q_{j}
are primes with
\ell_{i}\in S_{E}
and
q_{j}\notin S_{E}
. Define
n_{1}:=\prod\ell_{i}^{a_{i}}
and
n_{2}:=\prod q_{j}^{b_{j}}
. By
[
BEL+19
, Proposition 5.7]
,

\deg(x)=\deg(f)\cdot\deg(f(x)),

where
f\colon X_{1}(n)\rightarrow X_{1}(n_{1})
is the natural map. Using the standard formulas for
\deg(f)
we find

\deg(x)=\deg(f(x))\cdot c_{f}\cdot n_{2}^{2}\cdot\prod\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{i}^{2}}\right),

where
c_{f}=1
if
n_{1}>2
and
c_{f}=\frac{1}{2}
otherwise (see e.g.
[
DS05
, p.66]
). If
n_{1}\leq 2
, then the assumptions on
n
force
n_{1}=1
, and
\deg(x)
has the desired degree. So assume
n_{1}>2
. It remains to consider the case where
n
is supported entirely on primes in
S_{E}
.

So let us assume that
x=[E,P]\in X_{1}(n)
where
n=\prod\ell_{i}^{a_{i}}
with
\ell_{i}\in S_{E}
. We will write
[E,P_{i}]\in X_{1}(\ell_{i}^{a_{i}})
for the image of
x
under the natural map. Let
R\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
be a point of order 5, and define
F:=\mathbb{Q}(R)
; observe that
[F:\mathbb{Q}]\leq 24
. Then as in the proof of Proposition
4
, the curve
E/F
has good reduction at each prime above
\ell
and

\ell_{i}^{2a_{i}}-\ell_{i}^{2a_{i}-2}

divides the ramification index of any prime in
F(P_{i})
lying above
\ell_{i}
.
Thus the ramification index of
F(P_{i})/F
at primes above
\ell_{i}
is at least

\frac{\ell_{i}^{2a_{i}}-\ell_{i}^{2a_{i}-2}}{24}.

By the Néron-Ogg-Shafarevich criterion, the extension
F(P_{i})/F
is unramified at
\ell_{j}
for
j\neq i
.
Thus the extension
F(P)/F
, which contains the compositum of the field extensions
F(P_{i})
, has degree at least

\prod_{i}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2a_{i}}-\ell_{i}^{2a_{i}-2}}{24}.

Since
\deg(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot[F(P):F]
, this provides the desired lower bound for
\deg(x)
.

From this, we can deduce a bound on
\deg(y)
. Since

[\mathbb{Q}(x):\mathbb{Q}(y)]\leq\frac{\varphi(n)}{2},

the lower bounds on
\deg(x)
imply

\displaystyle[\mathbb{Q}(y):\mathbb{Q}]

\displaystyle=\frac{[\mathbb{Q}(x):\mathbb{Q}]}{[\mathbb{Q}(x):\mathbb{Q}(y)]}\geq\frac{1}{24^{k}}\cdot n\cdot\prod_{\ell\mid n}\left(1+\frac{1}{\ell}\right).\qed

4.
Polynomial Bounds for Points of Finite Support

In this section, we will prove the following “finite support” theorem, which is an isogenous variant of
[
CP18
, Theorem 2.8]
.
A key distinction is that our implicit constant depends only on the degree of a single fixed
j
-invariant in the geometric isogeny class. We thank Pete L. Clark for the suggestion to bound all of
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
here, instead of just the group exponent. This resulted in an improved bound for
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
in Theorem
2
.

Theorem 6
.

Fix an integer
d_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and a finite set of primes
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then there exists a constant
c:=c(d_{0},S)>0
such that the following holds. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be any integer whose prime divisors lie in
S
. Suppose
E/F
is a non-CM elliptic curve geometrically isogenous to an elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
j_{0}
of degree
[\mathbb{Q}(j_{0}):\mathbb{Q}]=d_{0}
. If
\exp E(F)[\emph{\textrm{tors}}]=n
, then

\#E(F)[\emph{tors}]\leq c\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2}.

Moreover, for any cyclic subgroup
C\subseteq E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of order
n
that is
F
-rational, one also has

n\leq c\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}].

Proof.

By
[
CN21
, Lemma A.4]
, there exists an elliptic curve
{E_{0}}
defined over
F_{0}:=\mathbb{Q}(j_{0})
whose
j
-invariant
j({E_{0}})=j_{0}
, for which
E
and
{E_{0}}
are
L
-rationally isogenous with
L/FF_{0}
at most quadratic.
As a consequence of e.g.
[
Gre12
, Proposition 2.1.1]
or
[
Gen24
, Corollary 4]
, one has for all primes
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
that

[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{L})]=[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{{E_{0}},\ell^{\infty}}(G_{L})].

(1)

We will prove the bound on
\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}]
first. Suppose
E(F)[\ell^{\infty}]\cong\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{a}\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{b}\mathbb{Z}
for some
\ell\in S
and
a\leq b
.
If
a=0
, it follows that
\rho_{E,\ell^{b}}(G_{F})\subseteq B_{1}(\ell^{b})
once we fix an appropriate basis. Otherwise, there exists a basis of
E[\ell^{b}]
such that

\rho_{E,\ell^{b}}(G_{F})\subseteq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}1&\ell^{a}x\\
0&1+\ell^{a}y\end{bmatrix}\Bigg\rvert\,0\leq x,y<\ell^{b-a}\right\}.

In either case, the index of
\rho_{E,\ell^{b}}(G_{F})
in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{b}\mathbb{Z})
is divisible by
\ell^{\max(0,2a+2b-3)}(\ell^{2}-1)
, from which it follows that

\ell^{\max(0,2a+2b-3)}(\ell^{2}-1)\mid[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{F})].

Additionally, as the fixed field of the
\ell
-adic representation
\rho_{E_{0},\ell^{\infty}}\colon G_{F_{0}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is
F_{0}(E_{0}[\ell^{\infty}])
, we also see that

[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{E_{0},\ell^{\infty}}(G_{L})]=[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{E_{0},\ell^{\infty}}(G_{F_{0}})]\cdot[L\cap F_{0}(E_{0}[\ell^{\infty}]):F_{0}].

Combining these facts with (
1
), we deduce that

\ell^{\max(0,2a+2b-3)}(\ell^{2}-1)\mid[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{{E_{0}},\ell^{\infty}}(G_{F_{0}})]\cdot[L:F_{0}].

By the strong uniform
\ell
-adic Arai theorem
[
CP18
, Theorem 2.3.a]
, there exists a constant
a(d_{0},\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

which depends only on
d_{0}
and
\ell
for which the
\ell
-adic valuation of
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}):\rho_{{E_{0}},\ell^{\infty}}(G_{F_{0}})]
is at most
a(d_{0},\ell)
. Since
[L:\mathbb{Q}]
divides
2\cdot d_{0}!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, it follows that

\ell^{\max(0,2a+2b-3-a(d_{0},\ell))}\mid[L:F_{0}]\mid 2(d_{0}-1)!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}].

(2)

Compiling the divisibilities from (
2
) across all
\ell\in S
, we conclude that

(\#E(F)[\textrm{tors}])^{2}\mid 2\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{3+a(d_{0},\ell)}\cdot(d_{0}-1)!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}].

The result then follows by taking
c:=\sqrt{2\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{3+a(d_{0},\ell)}\cdot(d_{0}-1)!}
.

The proof for the cyclic subgroup order bound follows from the proof for the torsion bound,
mutatis mutandis.
The key difference is that if
E
has an
F
-rational cyclic
\ell^{b}
-isogeny, then up to conjugation one has
\rho_{E,\ell^{b}}(G_{F})\subseteq B_{0}(\ell^{b})
, where
[\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{b}\mathbb{Z}):B_{0}(\ell^{b})]=\ell^{b-1}(\ell+1)
. Thus, similar work shows that if
E
has an
F
-rational cyclic
n
-isogeny,
then one has

n\mid 2\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{1+a(d_{0},\ell)}\cdot(d_{0}-1)!\cdot[F:\mathbb{Q}],

and so one can take the constant
c:=2\prod_{\ell\in S}\ell^{1+a(d_{0},\ell)}\cdot(d_{0}-1)!
for the bound.
∎

5.
The Proof of Theorem
2

The next theorem gives upper bounds on the orders of torsion points and cyclic subgroups of
\mathbb{Q}
-rational non-CM elliptic curves, in terms of the degrees of their fields of definition.
This improves the bound in
[
CP18
, Theorem 1.3]
by at least a square-root factor of the degree. After proving this, we will prove Theorem
2
as a corollary.

Theorem 7
.

For each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any non-CM elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, for any integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and for any torsion point
P\in E(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
of order
n
, setting
C:=\langle P\rangle
one has both

n<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(P):\mathbb{Q}]^{1+\epsilon}~~\textrm{ and }~~n<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(C):\mathbb{Q}]^{2+\epsilon}.

If
n
is supported on the set of primes
\{\ell>37\}
, one instead has the sharper bounds

n<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(P):\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}~~\textrm{ and }~~n<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(C):\mathbb{Q}]^{1+\epsilon}.

Proof.

Assume that
n>1
; let us write

n=A\cdot B,

where
A\geq 1
is divisible only by primes
\ell\leq 37
, and
B:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{i}^{b_{i}}
where each prime
\ell_{i}>37
.

We will prove the torsion point order bound first.
Let
P_{A}:=BP
and
P_{B}:=AP
. By Theorem
6
, we have

A\leq c\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(P_{A}):\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2}

(3)

for some absolute constant
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Thus, it will suffice to bound
B
in terms of
d:=[\mathbb{Q}(P_{B}):\mathbb{Q}]
. We will do this with an analysis similar to that in
[
CP18
, §3.2]
.

Assume that
B>1
. Then by Proposition
5
, we have

d>\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{24^{k}}\cdot B\varphi(B)=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)}{24}.

(4)

Fix
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, and choose any real number
Z>0
for which
\log_{Z/24}(24)<\epsilon
.
We will partition the prime power divisors of
B
in the following way. First, we claim there are at most
\log_{Z/24}(2d)
indices
i
with
Z<\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)
. If this were not the case, then we would have by (
4
) that

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{2}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)}{24}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\prod_{\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)>Z}\frac{\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)}{24}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\prod_{\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)>Z}\frac{Z}{24}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{Z}{24}\right)^{\log_{Z/24}(2d)}

\displaystyle=d,

which is absurd. On the other hand, for any index
i
with
Z\geq\ell_{i}^{2b_{i}-1}(\ell_{i}-1)
, one also has
\ell_{i}\leq Z
. We conclude that the number
k
of distinct prime factors of
B
satisfies

k\leq\log_{Z/24}(2d)+\pi(Z),

(5)

where
\pi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
denotes the prime counting function.

Using inequalities (
4
) and (
5
), we make the following calculations:

\displaystyle B\varphi(B)

\displaystyle<2d\cdot 24^{k}

By Equation
(
4
)

\displaystyle\leq 2d\cdot 24^{\log_{Z/24}(2d)+\pi(Z)}

By Equation (
5
)

\displaystyle=2d\cdot 24^{\log_{Z/24}(2d)}\cdot 24^{\pi(Z)}

\displaystyle=2d\cdot(2d)^{\log_{Z/24}(24)}\cdot 24^{\pi(Z)}

\displaystyle\textrm{By }a^{\log_{x}(b)}=b^{\log_{x}(a)}

\displaystyle=2^{1+\log_{Z/24}(24)}24^{\pi(Z)}\cdot d^{1+\log_{Z/24}(24)}

\displaystyle<2^{1+\log_{Z/24}(24)}24^{\pi(Z)}\cdot d^{1+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\textrm{By our initial assumption that }\log_{Z/24}(24)<\epsilon.

Therefore, we deduce that

B\varphi(B)<c_{1,\epsilon}\cdot d^{1+\epsilon},

(6)

where
c_{1,\epsilon}:=2^{1+\log_{Z/24}(24)}24^{\pi(Z)}
depends only on
\epsilon
since
Z
depends only on
\epsilon
.

Next, by
[
HW08
, Theorem 327]
there exists a constant
0<b_{\epsilon}<1
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
one has

b_{\epsilon}\cdot n^{1-\epsilon}<\varphi(n).

Combining this with (
6
), we deduce that

B^{2-\epsilon}<b_{\epsilon}^{-1}c_{1,\epsilon}\cdot d^{1+\epsilon},

which implies that

B<(b_{\epsilon}^{-1}c_{1,\epsilon})^{1/(2-\epsilon)}\cdot d^{(1+\epsilon)/(2-\epsilon)}.

Since
\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, we conclude that

B<c_{\epsilon}\cdot d^{1/2+\epsilon}

(7)

where
c_{\epsilon}:=(b_{\epsilon}^{-1}c_{1,\epsilon})^{1/(2-\epsilon)}>0
.
Combining this with the bound for
A
in (
3
) finishes the proof for the torsion point order bound.

Next, we will prove the analogous upper bound for the order
n
of the subgroup
C:=\langle P\rangle
. We define
C_{A}:=\langle P_{A}\rangle
and
C_{B}:=\langle P_{B}\rangle
to be the unique subgroups of orders
A
and
B
, respectively. By Theorem
6
, we have

A\leq c\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(C_{A}):\mathbb{Q}]

(8)

for some absolute constant
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Fix
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{10}
. Then we know by
(
7
) that

B<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(P_{B}):\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}

for some
c_{\epsilon}>0
.
Let us make some observations about this inequality. We will set
d:=[\mathbb{Q}(C_{B}):\mathbb{Q}]
:

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle<c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(P_{B}):\mathbb{Q}]^{1/2+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=c_{\epsilon}\cdot[\mathbb{Q}(P_{B}):\mathbb{Q}(C_{B})]^{1/2+\epsilon}\cdot d^{1/2+\epsilon}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\epsilon}\cdot\varphi(B)^{1/2+\epsilon}\cdot d^{1/2+\epsilon}.

We thus have

\frac{B}{\varphi(B)^{1/2+\epsilon}}<c_{\epsilon}\cdot d^{1/2+\epsilon}.

Since
\varphi(B)\leq B
, we get
\frac{1}{B^{1/2+\epsilon}}\leq\frac{1}{\varphi(B)^{1/2+\epsilon}}
, which means we have the inequality

B^{1/2-\epsilon}<c_{\epsilon}\cdot d^{1/2+\epsilon}.

Thus,

B<c_{\epsilon}^{1/(1/2-\epsilon)}\cdot d^{\frac{1/2+\epsilon}{1/2-\epsilon}}.

From
\epsilon<\frac{1}{10}
we have
\frac{1/2+\epsilon}{1/2-\epsilon}<1+5\epsilon
, and so our inequality becomes

B<c_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\cdot d^{1+5\epsilon}

where
c_{\epsilon}^{\prime}:=c_{\epsilon}^{1/(1/2-\epsilon)}
. Adjusting
\epsilon
as necessary,
we can assume we have the bound

B<c_{\epsilon}^{\prime}\cdot d^{1+\epsilon}.

Combining this bound with (
8
) proves our cyclic subgroup order bound.
∎

Here is Theorem
2
, stated as a corollary of Theorem

.

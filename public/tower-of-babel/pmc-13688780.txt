Cadmium exposure triggers a functional switch of APP to drive joint degeneration
Authors: Yunbin Fan, Muqing Shi, Sunyi Zou, Zhiqin Lu, Xuan Gou, Jichong Zhu, Sitan Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCadmium (Cd) is a pervasive environmental heavy metal linked to various chronic pathologies; however, its specific role and underlying molecular mechanisms in osteoarthritis (OA) pathogenesis remain poorly characterized.MethodsWe performed a cross-sectional analysis using the NHANES database (2001–2018) to evaluate the association between blood Cd levels and OA risk. Hub targets were identified by integrating the CTD and GEO databases through network toxicology and machine learning algorithms. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) was employed to resolve the cellular localization and dynamic shifts of these targets. Causal relationships were substantiated via drug-target Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis. Findings were further corroborated through molecular docking, 100-ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and an independent retrospective clinical cohort consisting of 180 OA patients and 400 cervical spondylosis controls, in which peripheral blood inflammatory markers were evaluated for clinical validation.ResultsCross-sectional analysis revealed a significant positive association between blood Cd concentrations and OA prevalence, with a robust dose-response relationship (OR per log-unit = 1.64, P < 0.001). Multi-omics integration identified APP (Amyloid Precursor Protein) as a pivotal Cd-associated hub gene. scRNA-seq localized APP overexpression predominantly within fibrocartilage cells (FC), with notable upregulation across OA-derived chondrocyte subpopulations. While MR analysis suggested an inherent protective causal effect of APP against OA risk, this physiological role appears disrupted by the Cd-induced inflammatory milieu, where aberrant APP processing likely promotes cartilage matrix degradation. Retrospective clinical validation further demonstrated systemic immune dysregulation in OA patients, with inflammatory markers including WBC, monocytes, SII, and NLR showing robust diagnostic performance (AUC > 0.9), thereby providing clinical evidence supporting the involvement of the APP-associated immune-inflammatory axis. Furthermore, MD simulations suggested a possible binding mode between NAC and APP with modest affinity, providing preliminary structural insights that warrant further experimental validation.ConclusionThis study systematically elucidates the molecular landscape through which Cd exposure drives OA progression via the APP-mediated immune-inflammatory axis. These findings provide a novel theoretical framework for early screening and targeted therapeutic strategies in populations at high risk of environmental heavy metal exposure.Graphical Abstract Integrated epidemiological, multi-omics, and molecular analyses reveal that cadmium (Cd) exposure promotes osteoarthritis (OA) progression through an APP-mediated immune-inflammatory axis. Cd-associated molecular alterations identify APP as a key hub gene localized in OA-related chondrocyte populations and linked to monocyte/macrophage activation and joint microenvironment remodeling. Genetic evidence, computational structural analysis, and clinical validation further support the involvement of APP and systemic inflammatory signatures in OA. This study provides insights into environmental heavy metal-induced joint degeneration and potential strategies for early risk assessment and targeted intervention. Introduction

Cadmium is a toxic heavy metal widely present in nature. With its extensive application in chemical, electronics, and nuclear industries, large quantities of cadmium are discharged into the environment through industrial waste gases and wastewater, causing soil and water pollution. Long-term exposure to contaminated environments may harm human health (Genchi et al. 2020). Some studies indicate that cadmium can enter the human body primarily through two routes: food and cigarettes (Agneta et al. 2014). An epidemiological study indicates that cadmium is associated with chronic diseases including diabetes, kidney damage, various cancers, and multiple cardiovascular diseases (Soisungwan et al. 2010). Cadmium has a long half-life of approximately 25–30 years and persists in the body after inhalation. Multiple studies suggest cadmium is a key factor in inducing inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, and other processes (Liu et al. 2025, Yonggang et al. 2022).

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a common joint cartilage disorder characterized by progressive degeneration and destruction of articular cartilage, subchondral bone sclerosis and hyperplasia, marginal osteophyte formation, and varying degrees of synovial inflammation. Recent studies suggest a potential association between cadmium exposure and osteoarthritis. A cross-sectional study indicates that cadmium exposure may be a risk factor for increased osteoarthritis prevalence (Li et al. 2024). A toxicological study indicates that cadmium can reduce proteoglycan and glycosaminoglycan content while inhibiting the expression of type II collagen and aggregated glycosaminoglycans in the extracellular matrix of articular cartilage cells (Guoyong 2021). As an environmental pollutant closely linked to human production and daily life, cadmium exposure may play a crucial role in the onset and progression of OA.

To elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms and key targets linking cadmium exposure to OA development, and to explore the feasibility of multi-target intervention strategies, this study integrated public NHANES databases with clinically collected case samples. By combining computational biology methods with clinical evidence-based data, it systematically validated the role of cadmium exposure in promoting OA progression and further revealed the potential therapeutic value of small-molecule drugs in cadmium-related OA. Materials and methods

Cross-sectional analysis

The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) employ a complex stratified, multistage probability sampling design to ensure the sample accurately represents diverse populations across the United States, thereby assessing the health and nutritional status of the American public. To uphold ethical standards, the survey received approval from the National Center for Health Statistics. All selected individuals voluntarily provided written informed consent prior to inclusion in the study. Data relevant to this study are publicly available via the website (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/index.htm). Statistical analysis utilized data from nine cycles spanning 2001–2018, involving a total of 16,712 participants.

Variables

This study used osteoarthritis as the outcome variable, with data sourced from the Questionnaire section of the NHANES database. Blood cadmium levels served as the exposure variable, measured directly from whole blood samples using mass spectrometry (L S R and L P 2008). Based on the characteristics of the study population, the following covariates were selected: gender, age, ethnicity, education level, smoking status, alcohol consumption, body mass index (BMI), and systemic inflammation index (SII).

Statistical analysis

We performed weighted logistic regression analyses using the svyglm function from the R survey package to account for the complex NHANES sampling design. Blood cadmium levels were examined both as a continuous variable (log-transformed) and as quartiles (Q1–Q4, with Q1 as reference). Three sequential models were constructed: Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 adjusted for demographic variables (age, sex, race/ethnicity, education); Model 3 additionally adjusted for lifestyle factors and clinical covariates (smoking, alcohol consumption, physical activity, BMI, SII). To assess whether the association differed by age, we performed stratification analyses by age group (< 60 years vs. ≥60 years). Trend tests were conducted by treating the quartile variable as a continuous ordinal variable to evaluate dose-response relationships. All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Construction of networks

To investigate the mechanism of cadmium in osteoarthritis, we performed a network toxicology analysis (He et al. 2024, Chang and Jiang 2024). Cadmium-related genes were retrieved from the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) using “cadmium” as the keyword and retaining genes with six or more interactions (Peter D A, C W T et al. 2022). OA data were obtained from five GEO datasets (GSE46750, GSE48556, GSE98918, GSE117999, GSE178557) (Jean-emile D et al. 2014, M R Y F, D B S et al. 2014, Liu et al. 2022, Brophy RH, Zhang 2018), with the first three as the training set and the latter two for validation. Data processing was conducted using GEOquery (Sean 2007). We intersected CTD and GEO genes to identify cadmium-associated OA genes and performed differential expression analysis with the limma package (Belinda 2015). Key genes were selected for further study. A “cadmium–gene–OA” network was visualized using Cytoscape (v3.10.4) (Paul et al. 2003). Functional enrichment analysis (GO and KEGG) was carried out with clusterProfiler (Tianzhi et al. 2021). to interpret biological roles and pathways. Finally, a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using the STRING database and analyzed based on connectivity (Damian and Andrea 2011).

Machine learning

To identify robust gene signatures for OA diagnosis, we performed a systematic machine learning-based feature selection and model evaluation pipeline. Given the high-dimensional nature of transcriptomic data, we adopted a comprehensive benchmarking approach covering 113 algorithms across multiple families—including regularized regression (Lasso, Ridge, ElasticNet), tree-based methods (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM), support vector machines with various kernels, nearest-neighbor classifiers, naive Bayes, linear and quadratic discriminant analysis, and ensemble stacking methods. This broad coverage was chosen to avoid bias toward any specific algorithm family and to ensure the identification of the most generalizable predictive model.

The model selection followed a three-stage systematic pipeline. In the first stage, all algorithms were trained on the merged training set (GSE46750, GSE48556, GSE98918) using 5-fold cross-validation with grid search-based hyperparameter tuning. In the second stage, candidate models were ranked by cross-validated AUC, and models with validation AUC approaching 1.0 were excluded to mitigate overfitting. In the third stage, the top-performing models were evaluated on two independent validation sets (GSE117999, GSE178557). This multi-stage strategy was designed to balance predictive accuracy with generalizability, thereby enhancing the reproducibility and biological reliability of the identified gene signature.

Single-cell sequencing analysis for identifying key targets

Single-cell RNA-seq data processing and cell-type annotation

This single-cell transcriptomics dataset (GSE255460) was compiled from the GEO database and processed using the Seurat R package. To ensure high-quality analysis results, a rigorous quality control (QC) process was implemented. Cells with fewer than 200 detected genes or with mitochondrial gene content exceeding 15% were excluded. To minimize the inclusion of doublets, we set an additional upper limit of 6,500 genes for nFeature_RNA, which aligns with the threshold typically recommended for 10× Genomics single-cell data. Additionally, prior to performing downstream clustering and differential expression analyses, we applied the DoubletFinder algorithm (pK = 0.09) to identify and exclude suspected doublets.

Intercellular communication and target localization

To resolve the signaling crosstalk within the microenvironment, CellChat was employed to infer intercellular communication networks, quantifying both the strength and frequency of interactions between distinct cell populations. The cellular localization and expression gradients of key hub targets across various cell lineages were visualized as scatter plots using the FeaturePlot function.

Immune infiltration analysis

To further validate the correlation between key gene expression and immune cell types, the validation cohort underwent immune cell infiltration analysis using the CIBERSORT deconvolution algorithm, associating key genes with 22 distinct immune cell types (Long LC et al. 2015). CIBERSORT analysis revealed the distribution of immune cells across different samples, with expression levels of markers across the 22 immune cell types visualized through immune cell histograms and lollipop plots.

Mendelian randomization analysis for drug targets to determine causality

To substantiate the causal impact of scRNA-seq-derived hub genes on OA, a drug-target Mendelian randomization (MR) framework was established, providing genetic triangulation for our epidemiological findings. Genetic instruments for the targets were curated as cis-eQTLs from the eQTLGen consortium (blood-derived), while summary-level statistics for OA were retrieved from the IEU OpenGWAS database. Both eQTL and GWAS samples were of European ancestry; no known sample overlap. We acknowledge that gene regulation is tissue-specific and that blood-derived eQTLs may not fully capture APP expression in joint tissues. However, to our knowledge, large-scale cis-eQTL datasets from human articular cartilage are currently unavailable, necessitating the use of blood-derived data as the best available proxy. Accordingly, our MR findings should be interpreted as exploratory genetic evidence requiring validation in joint tissue-specific contexts. Causal estimates were primarily inferred using the inverse variance weighting (IVW) method via the TwoSampleMR package, supplemented by four auxiliary MR models. Methodological rigor was ensured through a suite of sensitivity analyses, including Cochran’s Q test for heterogeneity, MR-Egger regression for horizontal pleiotropy, and leave-one-out validation. To enhance reproducibility, primary findings were further corroborated using independent replication cohorts. The entire analytical pipeline adhered strictly to the STROBE-MR reporting guidelines.

Molecular docking and molecular dynamic simulation

Candidate drug prediction

The Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB, http://dsigdb.tanlab.org/) (Minjae et al. 2015) was utilized to perform drug enrichment analysis for predicting potential therapeutic agents targeting osteoarthritis core genes.

Molecular docking

This study performed molecular docking using CB-Dock2 (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/index.php) (Yang et al. 2022). The three-dimensional structures of target proteins were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB, http://www.rcsb.org/), while the chemical structures of small-molecule compounds were obtained from the ChemSpider database (https://www.chemspider.com/). For each docking simulation, the conformation exhibiting the lowest binding energy was selected as the optimal binding pose.

Molecular dynamics simulations

To assess the stability and interaction dynamics of protein-ligand complexes, molecular dynamics simulations were conducted using GROMACS with the AMBER 99SB force field. Each system was solvated in a TIP3P water model and neutralized with sodium and chloride ions. Energy minimization was performed using the steepest descent algorithm followed by the conjugate gradient method (50,000 steps). The system then underwent sequential equilibration, first in the NVT ensemble (310 K) followed by the NPT ensemble (1 atm). Production simulations were carried out for 100 ns. We acknowledge that APP is a large protein with intrinsic conformational flexibility, and 100 ns of simulation may not fully sample its conformational landscape. Nevertheless, as an initial computational assessment, the simulation provided preliminary insights into the stability of the NAC–APP interaction and served as a hypothesis-generating basis for future experimental validation. All bonds involving hydrogen atoms were constrained using the LINCS algorithm. To ensure reproducibility, each simulation was performed in triplicate with independent initial velocities, and the final results are reported as mean ± SD.

Clinical data analysis

This study constructed a retrospective clinical dataset collecting complete blood count (CBC) test data from 180 patients with osteoarthritis (OA) and 400 patients with cervical spondylosis (CS) as the disease control group. Cervical spondylosis is primarily a non-inflammatory degenerative joint disorder characterized by age-related intervertebral disc degeneration, which does not typically present with elevated systemic inflammatory markers. Therefore, it served as an appropriate control to evaluate whether the identified inflammatory markers are specifically associated with OA—a condition with a more prominent inflammatory component—rather than broadly elevated in all degenerative joint conditions. We acknowledge that a healthy control group would be more ideal for establishing absolute diagnostic thresholds; however, the current design was intended to assess the specificity of these markers for OA versus a closely related degenerative condition. Future studies incorporating healthy, age- and sex-matched controls are warranted to establish definitive diagnostic cutoffs. Inclusion criteria comprised: clinically confirmed cases of primary osteoarthritis or cervical spondylosis, aged 18–80 years, with complete CBC test records. Exclusion criteria encompassed patients with concomitant inflammatory joint diseases, malignancies, severe hepatic or renal insufficiency, recent infections, or immunosuppressive therapy. Collected variables comprised baseline CBC parameters (white blood cell count, absolute monocyte count, absolute neutrophil count, absolute lymphocyte count, eosinophil/ basophil absolute count, platelet count), and systemic immune inflammation indices (SII), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), lymphocyte-monocyte ratio (LMR), and platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) calculated from these parameters. The OA and CS groups were comparable in terms of age (63.5 ± 9.8 vs. 62.0 ± 10.7 years, P = 0.092) and sex distribution (male: 62.8% vs. 64.8%, P = 0.715). Baseline characteristics of the clinical validation cohort are presented in Supplementary Table S2. All data were randomly divided into a training set and an internal validation set at a 7:3 ratio. Machine learning methods were employed to construct a predictive model for osteoarthritis analysis.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.1). For continuous variables, the Shapiro–Wilk test was first applied to assess normality. Variables meeting the normality assumption were analyzed using parametric methods; non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using non-parametric alternatives. For survey-weighted analyses, weighted linear regression models (svyglm) were employed, which are robust to deviations from normality given the large sample size. For categorical variables, chi-square tests were used to assess associations between groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All publicly available data used in this study obtained ethical approval.

Age-stratified subgroup analyses (cutoff: 60 years) were performed to evaluate whether the observed association was confounded by age. As shown in Supplementary Table S1, the cadmium-OA association remained significant in both age strata after demographic adjustment. In the < 60 years subgroup, the adjusted OR for Q4 vs. Q1 was 2.86 (95% CI: 2.17–3.77, P < 0.001), with a significant trend (P for trend < 0.001). In the ≥ 60 years subgroup, the corresponding OR was 2.06 (95% CI: 1.21–3.53, P = 0.008), also with a significant trend (P for trend < 0.001). These stratified findings confirm that the cadmium-OA association is not merely an artifact of age imbalance but represents an independent risk factor across different age strata.

Collectively, these findings indicate a significant positive association between blood cadmium levels and OA risk, with a clear dose-response relationship, suggesting cadmium exposure as an important and independent environmental risk factor for osteoarthritis.

Identification of targets for OA associated with cadmium exposure

To systematically elucidate the association mechanism between cadmium exposure and osteoarthritis, we first screened the CTD database for genes exhibiting interactions with cadmium in at least six instances and obtained osteoarthritis-related gene sets from the GEO database. By intersecting these two sets and performing differential expression analysis, we identified 13 significantly differentially expressed genes and constructed a network diagram illustrating the cadmium-gene-osteoarthritis tripartite relationship (Fig. 3A–D). To validate the reliability of these 13 core genes, we compared their expression levels in osteoarthritis patients versus healthy controls using box plots, confirming statistically significant expression differences (Fig. 3G). Subsequently, by constructing a protein interaction network (Fig. 3E, F), we identified key hub proteins within the network, such as NFKBIA、IL10 and APP. To further explore the biological functions of these key genes, we performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. GO analysis revealed significant enrichment of these genes in multiple biological processes, including cellular responses to biological stimuli, regulation of inflammatory responses, and responses to lipopolysaccharides. Cellular components were predominantly localized to the endoplasmic reticulum lumen, platelet α-granules, and collagen-containing extracellular matrix. Molecular functions primarily involved growth factor activity, cytokine activity, and protease binding (Fig. 3I). KEGG pathway analysis further indicated significant enrichment of these genes in pathways related to inflammatory and immune responses, infectious diseases, and metabolism (Fig. 3H).

Machine learning identifies key genes

To ensure the robustness and generalizability of feature selection, we systematically evaluated 113 machine learning algorithms and excluded models with AUC values close to 1.0 on the validation set to avoid overfitting. Based on a comprehensive performance ranking, the glmBoost + NaiveBayes model was selected as the best algorithm, achieving an AUC of 0.8976 on the training set, with validation performance of 0.6319 on GSE117999 and 0.8125 on GSE178557 (Fig. 4A; Table 4). Although the limited sample sizes in the external validation cohorts (n = 24 and n = 8) may restrict the generalizability of the results, the model’s internal cross-validation performance was stable, and the key genes identified by this model were highly consistent with those identified by other top-performing algorithms, confirming the robustness of our feature selection. The selected set of genes includes: IL6, MMP9, GSK3B, IL10, APP, PHGDH, COL1A1, SERPINE1, HSPA1B, and HBB (Fig. 4B). To further evaluate the diagnostic potential of these genes, we calculated the AUC values for each gene. The results showed that APP, GSK3B, HSPA1B, and IL6 exhibited moderate but stable diagnostic performance across all validation cohorts (Fig. 4C–D).

Single-cell sequencing analysis for identifying key targets

Following stringent quality control (Fig. 4E–G), unsupervised dimensionality reduction and canonical annotation resolved five distinct chondrocyte subpopulations: fibrocartilage cells (FC), homeostatic (HomC), reparative (RepC), regulatory (RegC), and inflammatory (InfC) chondrocytes (Fig. 4H). Intercellular communication analysis identified RegC and InfC as the central signaling hubs, with the strongest intercellular communication detected between RegC and InfC (Fig. 5A). Pseudo-temporal trajectory inference positioned HomC at the developmental root, diverging into functionally specialized lineages including the inflammatory-responsive InfC and reparative RepC (Fig. 5B, C). Single-cell expression profiling revealed that APP expression was most prominent in FC populations (Fig. 5D). Moreover, although APP was induced across all OA-derived chondrocyte subpopulations, FC exhibited the most striking upregulation upon OA stimulation (Fig. 5E), suggesting that APP undergoes complex subpopulation-specific transcriptional reprogramming during osteoarthritis progression, with a particularly pronounced role in fibrocartilage cells.

Immune infiltration analysis

To resolve the immunological landscape of OA, we quantified the infiltration of 22 leukocyte subtypes, revealing distinctive shifts in naive CD4 + T cells, activated NK cells, M0 macrophages, and activated mast cells in OA tissues compared to controls (Fig. 5G, H). Correlation matrices underscored synergistic expression trajectories among the six intersection-derived hub genes—APP, COL1A1, HSPA1B, NFKBIA, PHGDH, and PTGS2—while simultaneously delineating coordinated co-infiltration patterns across the immune spectrum (Fig. 5F). Notably, integrative analysis identified a robust positive correlation between the expression of these hub genes and the infiltration levels of monocytes and M0 macrophages (Fig. 6A–F). These findings suggest that the identified molecular signature may serve as a primary driver of myeloid-lineage recruitment and pro-inflammatory polarization, thereby orchestrating the immune-mediated microenvironment remodeling observed in OA progression.

Mendelian randomization analysis for drug targets to determine causality

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of APP, we conducted a drug-target MR analysis using blood-derived cis-eQTLs as genetic instruments. This analysis revealed a consistent inverse causal association between genetically predicted APP expression and OA risk in both the discovery and replication cohorts (Fig. 6G–N), suggesting that elevated APP levels may exert a protective effect against OA onset. However, given that the eQTL data were derived from peripheral blood rather than joint tissue, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Gene regulation is tissue-specific, and APP expression in chondrocytes may not be fully recapitulated by blood-derived eQTLs. Nevertheless, Mendelian randomization has been increasingly recognized as a robust tool for dissecting causal relationships in musculoskeletal diseases. For instance, a bidirectional MR study by Qu et al. successfully delineated the causal interplay between osteoporosis and osteoarthritis, demonstrating the utility of genetic instrumentation in evaluating drug targets and disease pathways even in the absence of tissue-specific expression data (Yudun et al. 2023). Therefore, our MR results provide exploratory genetic evidence that warrants validation in cartilage-specific expression datasets and functional studies. This finding was further substantiated by an independent replication cohort, which mirrored the protective direction observed in the primary analysis despite a marginal attenuation in effect size (Fig. 6K, N). Methodological robustness was confirmed through rigorous sensitivity diagnostics: funnel plots demonstrated a symmetrical distribution of SNPs, precluding substantial directional bias (Fig. 6H, L), while leave-one-out validation ensured that no individual genetic variant disproportionately influenced the global causal estimate (Fig. 6I, M).

Candidate drug prediction

Molecular docking

Gene expression-based drug prediction enrichment analysis revealed multiple small-molecule compounds associated with reversing osteoarthritis pathology (Fig. 7A). The subsequently constructed gene-drug regulatory network visually demonstrated interactions between these candidate drugs and disease targets (Fig. 7B). To evaluate the therapeutic potential of N-acetylcysteine (NAC) against OA-related dysregulation, molecular docking was performed to resolve its binding mode with the hub protein APP. The simulation yielded a binding affinity of − 4.2 kcal/mol, suggesting a weak to moderate interaction between APP and NAC. Structurally, NAC was anchored within the active pocket of APP through a concerted network of hydrogen bonds with key amino acid residues, including I176, M180, F181, and K230 (Fig. 7C). These site-specific interactions provide a structural basis for further optimization of NAC or its derivatives as potential APP-targeting agents, although the current binding affinity does not support a strong or stable interaction.

Molecular dynamics simulations

To assess the dynamic stability and binding mechanism of the APP–NAC interaction, we executed a 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation. The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of apoprotein APP remained consistently stable (0.10–0.25 nm), suggesting its inherent structural rigidity under the simulation conditions (Fig. 7D). The APP–NAC complex exhibited early-stage equilibration around 0.5 nm, followed by transient fluctuations (peaking at 1.1 nm) between 30 and 70 ns, before converging to a plateau at 0.6 nm for the final 30 ns (Fig. 7E). Root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) analysis further localized these dynamics, revealing that the core domains remained relatively stable (< 0.2 nm), while flexibility was primarily sequestered to the C-terminal region (Fig. 7F). Moreover, the evolution of hydrogen bonding, along with stable radius of gyration (Rg) and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) profiles, suggested that APP maintained its compact folding during the simulation (Fig. 7G–I). Within the 100-ns timescale, these MD findings suggest a possible binding mode between NAC and APP. However, given the inherent conformational flexibility of APP and the limited simulation time, these results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of stable complex formation. Extended simulations (e.g., > 500 ns or µs-scale) combined with experimental validation are warranted to fully characterize the binding dynamics and conformational landscape.

Clinical data analysis

To substantiate the clinical relevance of the “APP-monocyte/macrophage axis” identified through bioinformatics and single-cell transcriptomics, an independent retrospective cohort was analyzed, comprising 180 patients with osteoarthritis (OA) and 400 controls with cervical spondylosis. Given the previously observed correlation between OA-associated immune remodeling, APP expression, and M0 macrophage infiltration, we prioritized peripheral blood inflammatory markers that reflect monocyte-related systemic immune status for clinical validation.

Diagnostic performance was evaluated across four machine learning architectures: eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGB), Random Forest (RF), Generalized Linear Model (GLM), and Support Vector Machine (SVM). The XGB model exhibited superior discriminatory power with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.936 (Fig. 8A), while residual distribution analysis confirmed the robustness and predictive stability of these models (Fig. 8B–C). Feature importance ranking consistently identified white blood cell count (WBC), monocyte count (MON), and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) as the top-tier predictors for OA (Fig. 8D).

Subsequent univariate analysis corroborated the high diagnostic accuracy of individual markers, with WBC, neutrophil count (NEU), and NLR achieving AUCs of 0.906, 0.919, and 0.912, respectively (Fig. 8E). Inter-group comparisons revealed a pronounced systemic immuno-inflammatory shift in OA patients, characterized by significantly elevated levels of WBC, MON, NEU, NLR, and the systemic immune-inflammatory index (SII), alongside a marked reduction in the lymphocyte-monocyte ratio (LMR) (Fig. 8F).

These clinical findings resonate with our preceding multi-omics data, suggesting that OA is associated with a peripheral immune microenvironment dominated by monocyte and neutrophil activation when compared with a non-inflammatory degenerative control (cervical spondylosis). Collectively, these data provide clinical evidence for the involvement of the “APP-monocyte axis” in OA and support the hypothesis that cadmium-induced joint degeneration may be mediated through this specific immuno-inflammatory pathway. We acknowledge that validation in healthy control populations is needed to establish these markers as standalone diagnostic tools, and future studies incorporating such cohorts are warranted.

Discussion

The present study systematically substantiates the role of cadmium (Cd) exposure as a critical environmental driver of OA pathogenesis. By leveraging nationally representative data from the NHANES database, we first established a robust positive association between blood Cd levels and OA prevalence, aligning with emerging evidence that this persistent heavy metal serves as a systemic stressor. Our age-stratified analyses further reinforce the robustness of the cadmium-OA association. Age is undoubtedly a major risk factor for OA, as reflected by the higher proportion of older adults in higher cadmium quartiles. However, the persistent and significant association observed in the < 60 years subgroup (Q4 vs. Q1: OR = 2.86, P < 0.001) demonstrates that the cadmium effect operates independently of aging-related degenerative changes. These results suggest that age may act as an effect modifier rather than a pure confounder, with cadmium exposure contributing to OA pathogenesis across the lifespan. While oxidative stress and inflammation have been previously implicated in Cd-induced damage (Magdalena and Joanna 2021), the specific molecular conduits remained elusive. Through an integrative framework of network toxicology and multi-center machine learning, we identified APP (Amyloid Precursor Protein) as a pivotal hub gene modulated by Cd exposure. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis further localized APP overexpression predominantly to fibrocartilage cells (FC), with notable upregulation across OA-derived chondrocyte subpopulations, suggesting that APP may undergo complex subpopulation-specific transcriptional reprogramming during OA progression, with pseudotime trajectories suggesting that APP may govern the phenotypic shift of chondrocytes from homeostatic states toward inflammatory or degradative lineages. Notably, the strong correlation between hub genes—specifically APP—and M0 macrophage infiltration indicates that the “Cd–APP axis” potentially orchestrates a pro-inflammatory remodeling of the joint microenvironment.

A critical finding of our study is the apparent paradox between Mendelian randomization (MR) results and cadmium-induced expression patterns. Our MR analysis inferred a protective effect of APP against OA risk, consistent with its documented physiological role in maintaining skeletal homeostasis (Jin-xiu P, Fulei et al. 2018). However, Cd exposure significantly upregulates APP expression (Jieyi and Yirong 2023, Mai 2025), yet exacerbates OA. We propose the ‘aberrant cleavage’ hypothesis to resolve this discrepancy: under the Cd-induced inflammatory milieu—characterized by NF-κB, IL-17, and TNF-α pathway activation—physiological APP processing shifts toward pathological proteolysis. Specifically, the activation of β- and γ-secretases generates amyloid-β (Aβ) fragments (V N K 2019, Pereira 2014), which subsequently bind to the Receptor for Advanced Glycation End-products (RAGE). This Aβ-RAGE interaction amplifies cartilage catabolism via the secretion of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) (Cathrine C and Bincy 2020, Laura and Dominika 2019), effectively overriding the protein’s native protective function and converting it into a driver of joint degeneration. Of note, while our MR analysis provided genetic evidence supporting a protective causal effect of APP using blood-derived eQTLs, our single-cell transcriptomic analysis independently localized APP expression to joint-specific chondrocyte subpopulations. These complementary approaches—one providing genetic inference from blood, the other providing tissue-specific expression at the single-cell level—collectively strengthen the case for APP’s involvement in OA, though the tissue discrepancy between MR instruments and the primary disease site warrants further investigation. Additionally, because the GWAS data were derived from European-ancestry populations, the findings may not be directly generalizable to other ethnic groups.

To substantiate the clinical relevance of the aforementioned molecular insights, we leveraged an independent retrospective cohort to characterize the peripheral blood immune landscape of patients with osteoarthritis (OA). In alignment with our in silico identification of the APP-monocyte/macrophage axis, OA patients demonstrated a pronounced systemic inflammatory profile. Specifically, significant elevations in absolute monocyte and neutrophil counts were observed, alongside heightened composite inflammatory indices including the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and systemic immune-inflammatory index (SII), contrasted by a markedly reduced lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR).

To evaluate the translational utility of these markers, we constructed machine learning-based diagnostic frameworks, which yielded robust discriminatory performance with an area under the curve (AUC) exceeding 0.9. Subsequent feature importance ranking consistently identified monocyte count and NLR as the primary drivers of model accuracy. These results confirm the presence of a circulating myeloid-lineage activation signature in OA (Department of pathology and molecular medicine M U H O 2018, Salimi et al. 2025), effectively recapitulating the macrophage-driven microenvironmental remodeling predicted by our single-cell transcriptomic and immune infiltration analyses. While direct cadmium exposure metrics were unavailable for this specific cohort, the identified immuno-inflammatory phenotype provides compelling indirect clinical evidence supporting the hypothesis that cadmium exposure may facilitate OA pathogenesis via the APP-monocyte signaling axis.

Finally, our drug repurposing pipeline identified N-acetylcysteine (NAC) as a candidate therapeutic agent that merits further investigation. While NAC is a well-established antioxidant and glutathione precursor (JMP 2009), its application in osteoarthritis has remained under-explored. Molecular docking revealed a weak to moderate binding affinity (− 4.2 kcal/mol) between NAC and APP, with a hydrogen bond network involving residues I176, M180, F181, and K230 suggesting a plausible binding mode. This was further supported by preliminary MD simulations showing conformational adjustments upon NAC binding. However, given the modest binding energy and the limited simulation timescale (100 ns), these computational findings should be interpreted as preliminary and hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive evidence of stable complex formation. Critically, while our in silico predictions suggest a possible NAC-APP interaction, we fully acknowledge that computational findings alone cannot establish therapeutic efficacy. Whether NAC effectively binds to APP in vivo, reaches joint tissues at sufficient concentrations, and exerts protective effects against cadmium-induced cartilage degeneration requires rigorous experimental validation through cell-based assays, animal models, and ultimately clinical trials. Future studies employing extended MD simulations, binding free energy calculations, and experimental validation are warranted to confirm the therapeutic potential of NAC in cadmium-related OA. Given that NAC has been reported to inhibit the formation of abnormal Aβ metabolites, we hypothesize a dual-action mechanism: NAC mitigates Cd-induced oxidative damage while simultaneously modulating APP processing to reduce amyloidogenic deposition. These in silico findings provide a structural rationale for further experimental exploration of NAC as a candidate intervention. However, we emphasize that these are preliminary observations requiring experimental confirmation before any clinical translation can be considered.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the NHANES data are cross-sectional, precluding causal inference, and the self-reported OA status may introduce recall bias. Although we adjusted for multiple covariates and performed age-stratified analyses, residual confounding cannot be entirely excluded. Second, the single-cell RNA-seq data were derived from a single GEO dataset (GSE255460); while rigorous QC filtering was applied, potential batch effects cannot be completely ruled out. Third, the machine learning model was validated on small external cohorts (n = 24 and n = 8), which limits generalizability and necessitates larger-scale validation. Fourth, the molecular docking and MD simulations were purely computational; the modest binding affinity (− 4.2 kcal/mol) and the 100-ns timescale—insufficient to fully sample the conformational landscape of the large and flexible APP protein—warrant cautious interpretation. Fifth, the MR analysis relied on blood-derived eQTLs, which may not fully capture tissue-specific APP expression in chondrocytes. Sixth, the clinical validation used cervical spondylosis as a disease control rather than healthy individuals; while this design assessed marker specificity against a related degenerative condition, it does not establish absolute diagnostic utility in healthy populations. Additionally, OA severity data were not consistently available in the retrospective records, limiting our ability to assess correlations with disease stage. Finally, the proposed “Cd-APP functional switch” mechanism remains a hypothesis derived from computational and literature-based evidence, requiring experimental confirmation through in vitro chondrocyte models and in vivo animal studies. Future studies incorporating larger cohorts, cartilage-specific eQTL data, extended simulations, and experimental approaches are warranted to substantiate our findings.

Conclusion

In summary, this study provides a comprehensive multi-dimensional framework delineating the causal link between cadmium (Cd) exposure and the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis (OA). By integrating epidemiological data, network toxicology, and single-cell transcriptomics, we identified APP as a pivotal Cd-regulated hub gene and revealed its subpopulation-specific role in orchestrating myeloid-driven joint inflammation. While Mendelian randomization substantiated an inherent protective causal effect of APP, our proposed “aberrant processing hypothesis” resolves the observed paradox: the inflammatory microenvironment triggered by Cd exposure likely induces a functional switch, converting APP from a homeostatic protein into a pro-degradative pathological driver. This environmental–immune–joint axis is further corroborated by clinical retrospective evidence, which highlights the robust diagnostic potential of peripheral blood inflammatory markers. Furthermore, the APP–N-acetylcysteine (NAC) interaction suggested by molecular dynamics simulations provides a preliminary structural rationale for further pharmacological exploration. Collectively, these findings offer novel insights into heavy metal-induced joint degeneration, establishing a theoretical basis for early risk stratification and the repurposing of existing drugs to mitigate environmental OA.

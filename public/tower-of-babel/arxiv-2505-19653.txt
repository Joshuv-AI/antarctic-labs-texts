Token-Importance Guided Direct Preference Optimization
Ning Yang Hai Lin Yibo Liu Baoliang Tian Guoqing Liu Haijun Zhang

Abstract
Ensuring that large language models (LLMs) generate outputs aligned with human preferences is important for safe and effective AI interactions. While Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) employs an implicit reward function to optimize the policy model, however, it and its related variants overlook the differential importance of individual tokens and are sensitive to judgment noise in preference datasets during generation. Although recent methods attempt to assess the important weight of tokens via probability prediction or simplistic weighting schemes, these evaluation methods are prone to biases and still cannot fully address these issues. To solve this problem, we propose the Token-Importance Guided Direct Preference Optimization (TI-DPO), which introduces two key innovations: the gradient-based token-importance weights that dynamically prioritize critical tokens, and a triple loss that explicitly guides model outputs to approach human-preferred responses and stay away from non-preferred responses. Experimental results show that TI-DPO achieves higher accuracy and stronger generative diversity, providing more stable and computationally efficient solutions compared with DPO and other RLHF methods.

3.2
Triplet Loss

Triplet loss, a powerful loss function for learning embeddings, ensures that within the embedding space, an anchor input is closer to positive inputs than to negative ones. This mechanism enhances the model’s capacity to differentiate between data points that are more or less similar. By simultaneously learning from the similarities and differences among sampled data points, the model is better aligned with human evaluations. The triplet loss operates with triplets
(x_{i},x_{j},x_{k})
, and is designed such that the representation of the anchor
x_{i}
is nearer to a similar data point
x_{j}
than to a dissimilar one
x_{k}
. This targeted learning strategy is instrumental in sharpening the model’s feature discrimination, thereby improving its ability to make decisions that resonate with human preferences. The triplet loss is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{trp}}=\sum_{i,j,k}^{T}\left[\left\|f\left(x_{i}\right)-f\left(x_{j}\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}-\left\|f\left(x_{i}\right)-f\left(x_{k}\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}+\alpha_{\operatorname{trp}}\right]_{+}.

(5)

Here
[z]_{+}
denotes the rectified linear unit function, ensuring that it is set to zero if negative. The features extracted from the three inputs are represented by the terms
f\left({x}_{i}\right)
,
f\left({x}_{j}\right)
, and
f\left({x}_{k}\right)
.

4
Methodology

Driven by the challenges of unstable training and distribution shift in traditional RL alignment methods, we propose the TI-DPO framework. Our key innovation lies in integrating the token-importance weight and triplet loss in the TI-DPO framework, attaining a more optimal equilibrium balance between alignment precision and generative diversity.

4.1
Token-Level MDP for LLM Preference Alignment

To address the challenges of the sequential and auto-regressive nature of text generation, a token-level
Markov Decision Process
(MDP) is introduced, which incorporates the notion of token significance to improve the alignment of each token selection with human preferences. This concept is defined through a tuple denoted as
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},r,\rho_{0})
.
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
are the state space and action space, respectively.
\mathcal{P}
is a deterministic transition model among tokens. Here
r
stands reward model associated with each token, and
\rho_{0}
indicates the initial state distribution.
The initial state is
s_{0}=[x]
, which is simply the input prompt. At each step
t
of the generation process, the state
s_{t}=[x,y^{<t}]\in\mathcal{S}
consists of input prompt
x=[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}]
, where
m
is the count of token, and
t-1
generated tokens
y^{<t}=[y^{1},y^{2},\ldots,y^{t-1}]
.
At each time step
t
, the action
a_{t}=y^{t}
corresponds to the selection of subsequent tokens for generation.
The initial state distribution
\rho_{0}
represents the distribution over input prompts
x
.

4.2
Gradient Attribution for Token Importance

Building on the token-level MDP framework, we now formalize the calculation of importance weights
w_{t}
. Inspired by the attribution-based rationale extraction from Ballout
et al.

[
40
]
, our approach quantifies token importance through gradient sensitivity analysis, ensuring that critical tokens in human-preferred responses drive the policy optimization process.

Assume that each token
x_{i}
is transformed into an embedding vector
e_{i}
, forming an embedding sequence
E=[e_{1},e_{2},\cdots,e_{m}]
. We compute the gradient of reward model’s output concerning
e_{i}
to measure how sensitive the reward is to changes in the token.
For each reward
r_{\phi}(s_{j},a_{j})
, the gradient corresponding to
e_{i}
is
\nabla_{e_{i}}r_{\phi}(s_{j},a_{j})
.
Similar to previous work
[
40
]
, the average gradient for embedding
e_{i}
is given by:

\bar{G}_{i}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\nabla_{e_{i}}r_{\phi}(s_{j},a_{j}).

(6)

Since
\pi_{\text{ref}}
is fixed, there is
\nabla_{e_{i}}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}=\nabla_{e_{i}}\log\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})
. For each token
x_{i}
in the input or generated sequence, we rewrite (
6
) with policy log-probability ratio as:

\bar{G}_{i}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{j=1}^{t}\nabla_{e_{i}}\log\pi_{\theta}(y^{j}|x,y^{<j}).

(7)

Although the average operation may weaken the long-range dependence, the generation of each token
y^{t}
only only depends on the prefix
y^{<t}
, so the gradient
\nabla_{e_{i}}\log\pi_{\theta}(y^{j}|x,y^{<j})
has implicitly included the historical dependence. We take the average of all time steps to balance the local and global importance. Denote the importance-score for the
i
-th token as
I_{i}
. Following the attribution method of Ballout
et al.

[
40
]
, we calculate
I_{i}
using the
L_{1}
norm of gradient:

I_{i}=\left\|\bar{G}_{i}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{k}\left|\bar{G}_{i}[k]\right|.

(8)

Here index
k
corresponds to the components within the average gradient vector
\bar{G}_{i}

[
40
]
.
This gradient-based importance guidance addresses the key limitations of previous work. Gradient attribution provides a data-driven measure of token relevance, which can adapt to the subtle semantics of human preferences and achieve fine-grained control of key tokens in the model generation process.

Then we normalize
I_{i}
to derive the importance-guided token-wise weight
w_{t}\in[0,1]
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{t}
be the set of tokens in
s_{t}
. To avoid division by zero, we assume that at least two tokens have distinct importance scores in
\mathcal{T}_{t}
. The normalized weight for token
i
is:

w_{t}=\frac{I_{i}-\min(\mathcal{I}_{t})}{\max(\mathcal{I}_{t})-\min(\mathcal{I}_{t})},

(9)

where
\mathcal{I}_{t}=\{I_{j}|j\in\mathcal{T}_{t}\}
is the set of importance scores for tokens. Normalize to
[0,1]
to maintain consistent weight measurements across diverse samples.
The BT-model that
y_{\text{w}}
is favored compared with
y_{\text{l}}
is:

p^{*}(y_{\text{w}}\succ y_{\text{l}})=\frac{\exp\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w_{t}^{\text{w}}\cdot r_{\phi}(s_{t}^{\text{w}},a_{t}^{\text{w}})\right)}{\exp\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w_{t}^{\text{w}}\cdot r_{\phi}(s_{t}^{\text{w}},a_{t}^{\text{w}})\right)+\exp\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w_{t}^{\text{l}}\cdot r_{\phi}(s_{t}^{\text{l}},a_{t}^{\text{l}})\right)}.

(10)

Here,
w_{t}^{\text{w}}
and
w_{t}^{\text{l}}
are the importance weights for tokens in the preferred and less-preferred tokens,
T_{\text{w}}
and
T_{\text{l}}
are the lengths of
y_{\text{w}}
and
y_{\text{l}}
respectively.
Then with
r_{\phi}(s_{t},a_{t})=\beta\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}
in DPO, we can derive the expression for BT model:

p^{*}(y_{\text{w}}\succ y_{\text{l}})=\sigma(\Delta r_{\text{token}}(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}},w^{\text{w}}_{t},w^{\text{l}}_{t})),

(11)

where
\Delta r_{\text{token}}(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}},w^{\text{w}}_{t},w^{\text{l}}_{t})
can be denoted as:

\Delta r_{\text{token}}(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}},w^{\text{w}}_{t},w^{\text{l}}_{t})=\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w_{t}^{\text{w}}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}\left(y^{t}_{\text{w}}\mid x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t}\right)}{\pi_{\text{ref }}\left(y^{t}_{\text{w}}\mid x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t}\right)}-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w_{t}^{\text{l}}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}\left(y^{t}_{\text{l}}\mid x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t}\right)}{\pi_{\text{ref }}(y^{t}_{\text{l}}\mid x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}.

(12)

Therefore, we obtain the weighted token-level DPO base loss as
[
20
]
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w }}=-\mathbb{E}_{\left(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}}\right)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\log\sigma\left(\Delta r_{\text{token}}(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}},w^{\text{w}}_{t},w^{\text{l}}_{t})\right)\right]}.

(13)

4.3
TI-DPO Objective and Theoretical Analysis

DPO defines the reward model as the log-likelihood ratio between
\pi_{\theta}
and
\pi_{\text{ref}}
, eliminating the need for an explicit reward function. However, TI-DPO extends this by incorporating triplet loss, which explicitly guides outputs
y
to align with preferred responses
y_{\text{w}}
and diverge from non-preferred
y_{\text{l}}
. The dataset
D=\{(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}})\}
enables learning to pull responses toward high-quality standards and reject low-quality alternatives. Building on the triplet loss definition in Eq.(
5
), we obtain the triplet objective with policy model as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\left(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}}\right)\sim\mathcal{D}}\Big[\max(0,\ \underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\Big\|\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}\Big\|^{2}_{2}}_{\text{Bring }y\text{ closer to }y_{\text{w}}}

(14)

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\Big\|\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}\Big\|^{2}_{2}}_{\text{Push }y\text{ away }y_{\text{l}}}+\alpha)\Big]_{+}.

TI-DPO formulates the policy model’s loss with triplet loss to align with human preferences. With given TI-DPO dataset
\mathcal{D}=\{\left(x,y_{\text{w }},y_{\text{l}}\right)\}
, we obtain TI-DPO objective:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}},

(15)

where
\gamma
is a hyperparameter. This combined loss guides the policy
\pi_{\theta}
to learn to assign similar scores to
y_{\text{w}}
, while emphasizing the importance of tokens in
y_{\text{w}}
through the weighted reward function. In Appendix
A.3
, we have given the proof of gradient
\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}
, which is used to update
\theta
during training.
Denote
\Delta r_{\text{global}}=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}\left(y_{\text{w}}|x\right)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}\left(y_{\text{w}}|x\right)}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}\left(y_{\text{l}}|x\right)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}\left(y_{\text{l}}|x\right)}
from Eq.(
4

\Delta r_{\text{token}}(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}},w^{\text{w}}_{t},w^{\text{l}}_{t})
as
\Delta r_{\text{token}}
. To show the superiority of our TI-DPO loss compared to DPO loss, we first introduce the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1

There is
\alpha>1
s.t.
\forall(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}})\sim D
, it is satisfied that:

\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{token}}\right]\geq\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right].

(16)

The proof of Lemma
4.1
is detailed in Appendix
A.1
.
According to Eq.(
13
), TI-DPO dynamically allocates token importance weights through gradient attribution, enabling the model to focus on the generation outputs with more importance weights on preference alignment.
The following theorem strictly proves the theoretical advantages of this improvement at the loss function level. Under the condition of Lemma
4.1
, the total loss of TI-DPO will be significantly lower than the original DPO loss:

Theorem 4.2
(Tighter Loss Bound)

If
\exists\alpha>1
s.t.
\forall(x,y_{\text{w}},y_{\text{l}})\sim D

\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{token}}\right]\geq\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]
, then TI-DPO’s total loss is strictly lower than DPO’s, i.e.,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}-\beta\Delta_{\text{triplet}},

(17)

where
\Delta_{\text{triplet}}
is a positive coefficient related to triplet loss and
\alpha
.

The proof is shown in Appendix
A.2
. Theorem
4.2
indicates that the total loss of TI-DPO is strictly less than the original DPO loss by an additional positive term determined jointly by the token-level weight advantage and the triplet coefficient. The strict superiority of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}
provides a mathematical guarantee for the more stable training process of TI-DPO in practical tasks, verifying its essential improvement compared to the traditional DPO from a theoretical perspective. The implementation of TI-DPO is shown in Algorithm

w_{t}^{\text{w}}=\text{Normalize}(\|\frac{1}{T_{\text{w}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\nabla_{e_{t}}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}\|_{1}).

9:

Calculate importance weights for non-preferred responses:

10:

w_{t}^{\text{l}}=\text{Normalize}(\|\frac{1}{T_{\text{l}}}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\nabla_{e_{t}}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}\|_{1}).

11:

Compute weighted DPO log-ratio:

12:

13:

Calculate the output
y^{t}

y^{t}\sim\pi_{\theta}(y^{t-1}|x,y^{<t-1})
.

15:

.

6
Conclusion

We introduce TI-DPO, an optimization framework that effectively bridges the alignment gap between LM and human value systems. By incorporating token-importance guidance and a triplet loss, TI-DPO effectively overcomes the limitations of traditional DPO methods, which often neglect individual token significance and are sensitive to judgment noise. Rigorous experiments across diverse benchmarks such as HumanEval, TruthfulQA, and IFEval show that TI-DPO consistently outperforms baselines like SFT, DPO, and their variants. In performance comparisons, TI-DPO achieves the highest accuracy on TruthfulQA and IFEval tasks, demonstrating its superiority in fine-grained preference alignment. And we compare TI-DPO with three base instruction models across multiple task dimensions with radar graphs. Additionally, ablation experiments confirm the necessity of its token-importance guidance and triplet loss components. Visualized weight distributions in the appendix reveal that TI-DPO focuses on semantically critical tokens, such as safety terms of medical responses that we show in the case study, while theoretical analysis proves it achieves a tighter loss bound than DPO, ensuring optimization stability.
These experiments highlight the good performance of TI-DPO in improving the quality and reliability of LLM output, providing a powerful solution for preference optimization.

Appendix A
Theoretical Proof

A.1
Proof of Lemma
4.1

Using the linear property of expectation, the expectation of the weighted reward difference between the preferred response
y_{\text{w}}
and the non-preferred response
y_{\text{l}}
can be decomposed as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{token}}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w^{\text{w}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w^{\text{l}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})\right]

(18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{w}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})]-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{l}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})].

where
T_{w}
and
T_{l}
are the token lengths of the preferred and non-preferred responses respectively, and
w_{t}^{w}
and
w_{t}^{l}
are the token-importance weights calculated dynamically through gradient attribution.
For the token
t
in the preferred response, since the weight
w_{t}^{w}
is positively correlated with the reward
r_{\phi}(x,y_{w}^{t})
, i.e.,
\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))\geq 0
, then we have

\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{w}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})]=\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{w}}_{t}]\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})]+\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))

(19)

Similarly, for
y_{\text{l}}
, the weight
w^{\text{l}}_{t}
is negatively correlated with its reward
r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})
, i.e.,
\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))\leq 0
. Then, we have:

\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{l}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})]=\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{l}}_{t}]\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})]+\text{Cov}(w^{\text{l}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t}))

(20)

Substitute the positive correlation condition
\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))\geq 0
into (
19
), we obtain

\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{w}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})]\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{w}}_{t}]\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})],

(21)

Similarly, with
\text{Cov}(w^{\text{l}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t}))\leq 0
for (
20
), we have:

\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{l}}_{t}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})]\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{l}}_{t}]\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})].

(22)

Denote
\lambda=\min\big\{\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w^{\text{w}}_{t},\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w^{\text{l}}_{t}\big\}>1
, we have

\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{w}}_{t}]\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})]\geq\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t})\right]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}})],

(23)

and

\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\mathbb{E}[w^{\text{l}}_{t}]\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})]\geq\lambda\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t})\right]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}})].

(24)

Substituting (
21
) and (
22
) into (
18
), there is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{token}}]

\displaystyle\geq\lambda\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}})]+\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))-\left(\lambda\mathbb{E}[r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}})]-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}|\text{Cov}(w^{\text{l}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t}))|\right)

(25)

\displaystyle=\lambda\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{global}}]+\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))+\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}|\text{Cov}(w^{\text{l}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t}))|}_{C>0}.

Let
C=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\text{Cov}(w^{\text{w}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{w}}^{t}))+\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\text{Cov}(w^{\text{l}}_{t},r_{\phi}(x,y_{\text{l}}^{t}))|
,
C>0
and define
\alpha=\lambda+\frac{C}{\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{global}}]}.

Since the global reward of the preferred response
y_{\text{w}}
is necessarily higher than that of the non-preferred response
y_{\text{l}}
, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{global}}]>0
, we have
\alpha>1
, and

\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{token}}]\geq\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{global}}].

(26)

Hence, we show the Lemma
4.1
.

A.2
Proof of Theorem
4.2

Since the sigmoid function
\sigma(x)=1/(1+\exp(-x))
is convex, using Jensen’s inequality to
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}=-\mathbb{E}\left[\log\sigma\left(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}}\right)\right]\leq-\log\sigma\left(\beta\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{token}}\right]\right)

(27)

According to the assumption
\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{token}}\right]\geq\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]
shown in Appendix 1, with the monotonicity of
\sigma(z)
, we have

-\log\sigma\left(\beta\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{token}}\right]\right)\leq-\log\sigma\left(\beta\alpha\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]\right).

(28)

Denote
z=\beta\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]>0
and construct the function
g(\alpha)=\log(1+e^{-\alpha z})+(\alpha-1)z
. Since the monotonicity and
g(1)=\log(1+e^{-z})
, when
\alpha>1
, we have
g(\alpha)<g(1)
, i.e.,

-\log\sigma(\alpha z)=\log(1+e^{-\alpha z})\leq\log(1+e^{-z})-(\alpha-1)z=-\log\sigma(z)-(\alpha-1)z

(29)

Substituting
z=\beta\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]
into (
29
), we have

\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}\leq\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}-\beta(\alpha-1)\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]

(30)

According to Eq.(
14
), when the optimization is close to being completed,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\Big\|\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}\Big\|^{2}_{2}

(31)

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\Big\|\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}-\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}\Big\|^{2}_{2}\geq-\alpha

and there exists a constant
\Delta_{\text{triplet}}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}\leq\Delta_{\text{triplet}}
. Assuming
\gamma<\beta
and combining the importance-weighted DPO loss and triplet loss, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}+\gamma\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}-\beta(\alpha-1)\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_{\text{global}}]+\beta\Delta_{\text{triplet}}

Let
\Delta_{\text{triplet}}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha-1)\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]
and substitute into (
32
), then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}-\frac{1}{2}\beta(\alpha-1)\mathbb{E}\left[\Delta r_{\text{global}}\right]

(33)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}-\beta\Delta_{\text{triplet}}

Hence, Theorem
4.2
holds.

A.3
Gradient Analysis of Loss

According to Eq.(
13
), we have
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}=-\log\sigma(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}}),
where
\sigma(x)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}
is the sigmoid function with derivative
\sigma^{\prime}(x)=\sigma(x)(1-\sigma(x))
.
Taking the gradient of Eq.(
13
), we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sigma(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}})}\cdot\sigma^{\prime}(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}})\cdot\beta\nabla_{\theta}\Delta r_{\text{token}}

(34)

\displaystyle=-\beta(1-\sigma(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}}))\nabla_{\theta}\Delta r_{\text{token}}.

Here, we expand
\nabla_{\theta}\Delta r_{\text{token}}
:

\nabla_{\theta}\Delta r_{\text{token}}=\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w_{t}^{\text{w}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w_{t}^{\text{l}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}.

(35)

Since
\pi_{\text{ref}}
is fixed, there is
\nabla_{\theta}\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}}{\pi_{\text{ref}}}=\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})=\frac{1}{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}\nabla_{\theta}\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t}).

Thus,
\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}
becomes:

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO-w}}=-\beta(1-\sigma(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}}))\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w_{t}^{\text{w}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w_{t}^{\text{l}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})\right].

(36)

As for the gradient of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\max\left(0,\Delta r_{\text{triplet}}+\alpha\right)\right]_{+}
, assuming
\Delta r_{\text{triplet}}+\alpha>0
, we can expand the gradient:

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}=\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}\left\|d_{t}-b_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}\left\|c_{t}-d_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2},

(37)

where
b_{t}=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})}
,
c_{t}=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_{l}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})}
,
d_{t}=\log\frac{\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t})}
for simplicity.
Differentiating the squared terms, we have

\nabla_{\theta}\left\|d_{t}-b_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}=2(d_{t}-b_{t})\left(\nabla_{\theta}d_{t}-\nabla_{\theta}b_{t}\right),

(38)

and

\nabla_{\theta}\left\|c_{t}-d_{t}\right\|_{2}^{2}=2(c_{t}-d_{t})\left(\nabla_{\theta}c_{t}-\nabla_{\theta}d_{t}\right).

(39)

Then, we substitute the definitions of
b_{t},c_{t},d_{t}
and use
\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\text{ref}}=0
:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}b_{t}=\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t}),

(40a)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}c_{t}=\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t}),

(40b)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}d_{t}=\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y^{t}|x,y^{<t}).

(40c)

Thus,
\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}
is:

\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{triplet}}=2\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}(d_{t}-b_{t})\left(\nabla_{\theta}d_{t}-\nabla_{\theta}b_{t}\right)-2\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}(c_{t}-d_{t})\left(\nabla_{\theta}c_{t}-\nabla_{\theta}d_{t}\right).

(41)

Substituting the derived gradients:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{TI-DPO}}=

\displaystyle-\beta(1-\sigma(\beta\Delta r_{\text{token}}))\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}w_{t}^{\text{w}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{w}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{w}}^{<t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}w_{t}^{\text{l}}\nabla_{\theta}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{\text{l}}^{t}|x,y_{\text{l}}^{<t})\right]

(42)

\displaystyle+2\gamma\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{w}}}(d_{t}-b_{t})(\nabla_{\theta}d_{t}-\nabla_{\theta}b_{t})-\sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{l}}}(c_{t}-d_{t})(\nabla_{\theta}c_{t}-\nabla_{\theta}d_{t})\right]

y_{\text{w}}

Response C in Table

y_{\text{l}}

TI-DPO’s gradient-based attribution mechanism identifies these tokens as critical for preference misalignment, suppressing their influence during generation.

By incorporating B, TI-DPO promotes more nuanced optimization, where intermediate outputs are refined to better match human preferences through token-level importance weights and triplet constraints.

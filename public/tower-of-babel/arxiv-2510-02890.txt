Axiomatisation for an asynchronous epistemic logic with sending and receiving messages
Philippe Balbiani, Hans van Ditmarsch, Clara Lerouvillois

Abstract
We investigate a public announcement logic for asynchronous public announcements wherein the sending of the announcements by the environment is separated from the reception of the announcements by the individual agents. Both come with different modalities. In the logical semantics, formulas are interpreted in a world of a Kripke model but given a history of prior announcements and receptions of announcements that already happened. An axiomatisation AA for such a logic has been given in prior work, for the formulas that are valid when interpreted in the Kripke model before any such announcements have taken place. This axiomatisation is a reduction system wherein one can show that every formula is equivalent to a purely epistemic formula without dynamic modalities for announcements and receptions. We propose a generalisation AA ∗ \textbf{AA}^{\ast} of this axiomatisation, for the formulas that are valid when interpreted in the Kripke model given any history of prior announcements and receptions of announcements. It does not extend the axiomatisation AA , for example it is no longer valid that nobody has received any announcement. Unlike AA , this axiomatisation AA ∗ \textbf{AA}^{\ast} is infinitary and it is not a reduction system. Keywords : modal logic, dynamic epistemic logic, asynchrony, distributed systems.



1
Introduction

What does an agent know in a dynamic setting and how does her knowledge evolve through communication in the absence of a global clock?

Dynamic epistemic logics (DEL) are modal logics of knowledge and change of knowledge. Some studies enforce
synchrony

[
7
]
for such logics, whereas others accommodate
asynchrony

[
12
]
.

There are different ways to accommodate asynchrony in epistemic logics.

Given epistemic actions that are not public, such as private announcements, one way to model asynchrony is that action sequences of different length are indistinguishable for an agent. If we identify action execution with a clock tick, this then represents uncertainty over the time. Such asynchrony is found, for example, in the gossip protocols of
[
1
]
(although not a DEL), wherein agents exchange sets of secrets in peer-to-peer communications (telephone calls) of which other agents may be unaware; and in
[
13
]
modelling the
One Hundred Prisoners
epistemic puzzle, wherein agents flip a light switch during an interrogation while other agents remain uncertain about the number of interrogations (if any) that have already taken place.

A different way to enforce asynchrony, more akin to assumptions in distributed computing
[
21
,
20
]
, is to consider sending and receiving messages as separate actions. In DEL this is typically not the case: the epistemic action there, such as the public announcement in
public announcement logic
PAL
[
24
]
, should be seen as instantaneous reception by some or all agents of messages sent by the environment. Such DEL are logics of observation, not of messaging (nor of agency). However, recent work in DEL have proposed logics containing different modalities for sending and receiving messages
[
19
,
4
]
. Our work builds on their efforts and results. In
[
19
,
4
]
, messages are publicly broadcast and individually received by the agents. Other works also allow partial synchronization wherein a subset of the set of all agents simultaneously receive a sent message, thus bridging the gap between asynchrony and synchrony
[
2
]
.

As an example, let us say a new podcast series has premiered on a topic that interests three friends Alice, Bob and Charlie. Each episode of this podcast — the message, so to speak — is released at irregular intervals on a podcast hosting platform, and these are meant to be listened to in the order in which they were broadcast. If Alice has listened to the first podcast episode by herself, she is uncertain whether Bob and Charlie have also listened to it. In fact, she can imagine different
histories
— one in which Bob and Charlie have listened to the episode before her, or only Bob, or after her, and so on.

We propose structures in which the agents not only may have epistemic uncertainty over different worlds, but also temporal uncertainty over such worlds, which is represented by a different, orthogonal, binary relation. The second kind of uncertainty is used to reason over different histories of epistemic actions (of possibly different length).

We make a number of further assumptions in our knowledge representation wherein we follow the approach in
[
4
]
. First, as said, agents receive messages in the order in which they were sent. In this we follow the classic FIFO scheme of communication in asynchronous systems
[
11
]
. Second, when envisaging alternative histories of past actions, agents only consider the messages they have already received. For example, Alice cannot assume that Bob has listened to the third episode of the podcast if she has not listened to it yet. We assume that she has no knowledge that any further episodes will ever be released. Third, we assume that announcements are
truthful
, meaning they are true when broadcast, as in PAL. Related to that, we need a notion of
executability
of histories. This notion ensures that agents only consider possible histories that consist of announcements that, given a state of the system, can be truthfully broadcast and received there.

Intuitively, in our approach, an agent knows a proposition if, and only if, she can only imagine states and histories executable there that satisfy the proposition, taking into account that the messages she received were true when sent, while ignoring that other agents may have received more announcements than herself.

In
[
4
]
, and the related
[
2
,
3
]
, two notions of validity are defined: a formula is
\epsilon
-valid
(
valid
) if it is true in every state of every epistemic model (Kripke model), given that no sending or receiving actions have yet been executed. In other words, these formulas are always true given the empty history. This carries no intuition of temporal uncertainty, but assumes a commonly known origin to start the interpretation. Then, a formula is
\ast
-valid
(
always-valid
) if it is true in every state of every epistemic model, and given a prior history of actions (sending and receiving). The
\epsilon
-validities have been axiomatised in
[
4
]
.

The present work extends
[
4
]
by proposing an axiomatisation
AA
∗
for always-validities (
\ast
-validities). We also slightly modify the semantics for asynchronous announcements so that propositions can only be true in (state, history) pairs such that the history is executable in that state. Unlike the axiomatisation
AA
of
[
4
]
, that is a rewrite system reducing every formula with dynamic modalities for sending and receiving to one without, our novel axiomatisation
AA
∗
is an infinitary axiomatisation from which dynamic modalities cannot be eliminated. An extensive final section compares our results to other works in the area.

Section
2
presents the logic of asynchronous announcements. Section
3
proposes a novel axiomatisation
\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
for always-validities, which completeness is shown in Section
4
. Section
5
discusses why we do not have an axiomatisation for the single-agent case and Section
6
compares our work to other approaches on asynchronous communication and three-valued logics.

2
The logic of asynchronous announcements

In this section we present the language and the semantics for asynchronous announcements. The presentation is based on
[
4
]
, except for the definition of the the satisfiability and executability relations (see Definition
12
).

2.1
Syntax

We first define the language of asynchronous announcements and then the notions of
word
and
history
that we use to represent sequences of sending and receiving events.

Definition 1
(Language
\mathcal{L}_{aa}
).

Let
P
be a countable set of atoms (denoted p, q, etc.) and
\mathcal{A}
be a finite set of agents (denoted a, b, etc.). The language
\mathcal{L}_{aa}
of asynchronous announcement logic is defined as follows.

\varphi::=p\ |\ \top\ |\ \lnot\varphi\ |\ (\varphi\lor\varphi)\ |\ \hat{K}_{a}\varphi\ |\ \langle\varphi\rangle\varphi\ |\ \langle a\rangle\varphi\

\dashv

We follow the standard rules for omission of the parentheses. Intuitively,
\langle\varphi\rangle\psi
means that
\varphi
is announced and, after that,
\psi
holds. Similarly,
\langle a\rangle\varphi
means that after agent
a
effectively receives a new message (the ‘next one in the queue’),
\varphi
holds. Without modalities
\langle a\rangle
we get the language
\mathcal{L}_{PAL}
of public annoucement logic; and without
\langle\varphi\rangle
we get the language
\mathcal{L}_{ml}
of multi-agent modal logic.

As usual, we define
\bot:=\lnot\top
,
\varphi\land\psi:=\lnot(\lnot\varphi\lor\lnot\psi)
,
\varphi\rightarrow\psi:=\lnot\varphi\lor\psi
and
\varphi\leftrightarrow\psi:=(\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\land(\psi\rightarrow\varphi)
. The dual of
\hat{K}_{a}
is defined by abbreviation as
K_{a}\varphi:=\lnot\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi
.
We also define by abbreviation the dual of dynamic modalities as
[\varphi]\psi:=\lnot\langle\varphi\rangle\lnot\psi
and
[a]\varphi:=\lnot\langle a\rangle\lnot\varphi
.

Since we want to modelise
asynchronous
communicative situations, we need to define further notions to distinguish different possible orders of message reception by the agents: the notions of
word
and
history
.

Definition 2
(Words).

Consider
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
as an alphabet with agents and formulas as letters. Words
\alpha,\beta,...
over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
are finite sequences of symbols over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
. The empty word is denoted
\epsilon
.
Let
\mathcal{W}:=(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa})^{\ast}
be the set of all words.

\dashv

For clarity, we add dots to separate letters within a word,
e.g.

p.\lnot K_{a}p.a.q.a
. When there is no ambiguity, however, we omit the point, particularly when abbreviations are used, as in
\alpha a
or
\alpha\varphi
.

Given a word
\alpha
, we use the following notations for intuitive notions that can be easily defined by induction:
|\alpha|
is its length;
|\alpha|_{a}
is the number of occurrences of
a
in
\alpha
;
|\alpha|_{!}
is the number of its formula occurrences and
|\alpha|_{!a}
the number of formula occurrences received by agent
a
. In the single-agent case,
i.e.
when
\mathcal{A}=\{a\}
, it is clear that
|\alpha|=|\alpha|_{!}+|\alpha|_{a}
. Otherwise, in the multi-agent case,
i.e.
when
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
,
|\alpha|=|\alpha|_{a_{1}}+\cdots+|\alpha|_{a_{n}}+|\alpha|_{!}
. We further define
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}
as the projection of
\alpha
to
\mathcal{L}_{aa}
and
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!a}
as the restriction of
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}
to the first
|\alpha|_{!a}
occurrences of formulas so
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!a}
is the restriction of
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}
to the formulas that agent
a
has read. Finally, given a word
\alpha
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha^{n}
denotes a concatenation of
n
copies of
\alpha
. For example, if
\alpha=p.q.a
then
\alpha^{2}=p.q.a.p.q.a
.

Note that a non-empty word
\alpha
can be decomposed into
\alpha^{\prime}\mu
or
\mu\alpha^{\prime}
for
\mu
a symbol in
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
. In future proofs, if proceeding by induction on a word
\alpha
, we will use one or the other decomposition.

For all words
\alpha
over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
, the modality
\langle\alpha\rangle
is inductively defined by
\langle\epsilon\rangle\varphi:=\varphi
,
\langle\alpha a\rangle\varphi:=\langle\alpha\rangle\langle a\rangle\varphi
and
\langle\alpha\psi\rangle:=\langle\alpha\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\varphi
. Its dual is defined by abbreviation as
[\alpha]\varphi:=\lnot\langle\alpha\rangle\lnot\varphi
.

Definition 3
(Prefix).

A word
\beta
is a
prefix
of a word
\alpha
, denoted
\alpha\sqsubseteq\beta
, if
\beta
is an initial sequence of
\alpha
. Obviously,
\alpha\sqsubseteq\alpha
and, if
\beta\sqsubseteq\alpha
, then for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
,
\beta\sqsubseteq\alpha a
and
\beta\sqsubseteq\alpha\varphi
.

\dashv

We assume that agents read announcements in the order in which they were sent. Words wherein that is the case are called
histories
. Therefore, a history is a word such that, for each agent, any prefix contains more sent messages than reception modalities. Formally:

Definition 4
(History).

A word
\alpha
over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
is a history if, and only if,
|\beta|_{a}\leq|\beta|_{!}
for all agents
a\in\mathcal{A}
and for all prefixes
\beta\sqsubseteq\alpha
.
We call
\mathcal{H}
the set of histories over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
.

\dashv

Therefore,
p.q.a.a
and
p.a.q
are histories, but
p.a.a.q
and
p.q.a.a.a
are
not
.
Obviously, if
\alpha
is a history, then
|\alpha|_{!a}=|\alpha|_{a}
so
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!a}
is simply the restriction of
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}
to the first
|\alpha|_{a}
formula occurrences.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\alpha
be a word over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
and
\beta\sqsubseteq\alpha
a prefix of
\alpha
. If
\alpha
is a history, then
\beta
is also a history.

\dashv

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two words over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
such that
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}
and
\beta\sqsubseteq\alpha
. Let
\gamma\sqsubseteq\beta
be a prefix of
\beta
. Then
\gamma
is also a prefix of
\alpha
. Hence, by Definition
4
,
|\gamma|_{a}\leq|\gamma|_{!}
for all agents
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Since this holds for any arbitrary prefix of
\beta
,
\beta
is a history.

\square

To define an appropriate semantics for knowledge through asynchronous announcements, we further need the following
view relation
between words and histories.

Definition 6
(View relation).

For every agent
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the view relation
\vartriangleright_{a}
is defined on
\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{H}
as follows:
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
if, and only if,
\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!}=\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!a}=\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!a}
.

\dashv

Roughly, for a given actual history
\alpha
,
view
a
(\alpha)
is the set of histories that agent
a
considers possible, in which all the announcements are precisely those agent
a
has currently received. It will then be natural to define knowledge depending on such uncertainty over histories (see Section
2.3
).

Example 7
.

Let us consider only two agents:
\mathcal{A}=\{a,b\}
. If the actual history is
\alpha=p.a
then agent
a
may imagine that agent
b
has also received the announcement (either before or after
a
). Hence,
view
{}_{a}(\alpha)=\{p.a,p.a.b,p.b.a\}
. However, agent
b
has no idea that a message has been sent, so
view
{}_{b}(\alpha)=\{\epsilon\}
.

\dashv

We end this section by stating some interesting properties of the view relation. We recall that a binary relation
R
on a given set
X
is Euclidean if, and only if, for all
x,y,z\in X
, whenever
xRy
and
xRz
,
yRz
also holds; and
R
is post-reflexive if, and only if, for all
x,y\in X
, if
xRy
then
yRy
.

Proposition 8
.

The view relation is serial, transitive, Euclidean and post-reflexive.
Moreover, for all words
\alpha
, the set
\textbf{view}(\alpha)
is finite.

\dashv

Note, however, that the view relation is neither reflexive nor symmetric. Indeed,
p.a.q\not\vartriangleright_{a}p.a.q
and
p.a.q\vartriangleright_{a}p.a
but
p.a\not\vartriangleright_{a}p.a.q
.

Definition 9

An epistemic model is a triple

Although it is possible to work with arbitrary accessibility relation, here we only consider equivalence relations,

2.3
Semantics

We now present the semantics for asynchronous announcements, define two notions of validities and state some important properties of the semantics.

To define the semantics, we need a well-founded order
\ll
between pairs
(\alpha,\varphi)
of word and formula. This order uses two auxiliary functions:
\|\cdot\|
represents the
size
of formulas or words, and
deg(\cdot)
displays the
modal depth
of formulas. Both are defined in the following.

For all
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
,
\|\varphi\|
is inductively defined by

\displaystyle\|p\|

\displaystyle:=2

\displaystyle\|\varphi\lor\psi\|

\displaystyle:=\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|

\displaystyle\|\langle a\rangle\varphi\|

\displaystyle:=\|\varphi\|+2

\displaystyle\|\top\|

\displaystyle:=1

\displaystyle\|\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\|

\displaystyle:=\|\varphi\|+1

\displaystyle\|\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\|

\displaystyle:=2\|\psi\|+\|\varphi\|

\displaystyle\|\lnot\varphi\|

\displaystyle:=\|\varphi\|+1

and for all words
\alpha
over
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{L}_{aa}
,
\|\alpha\|

\displaystyle\|\epsilon\|

\displaystyle:=0

\displaystyle\|\alpha\|

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}|\alpha|_{a}+\sum_{\varphi\in\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}}\|\varphi\|

Then, for all formulas
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
,
deg(\varphi)
is inductively defined as follows:

\displaystyle deg(p)

\displaystyle:=0

\displaystyle deg(\hat{K}_{a}\varphi)

\displaystyle:=deg(\varphi)+1

\displaystyle deg(\top)

\displaystyle:=0

\displaystyle deg(\langle a\rangle\varphi)

\displaystyle:=deg(\varphi)

\displaystyle deg(\lnot\varphi)

\displaystyle:=deg(\varphi)

\displaystyle deg(\langle\psi\rangle\varphi)

\displaystyle:=deg(\psi)+deg(\varphi)

\displaystyle deg(\varphi\lor\psi)

\displaystyle:=max(deg(\varphi),deg(\psi))

Also, given a pair
(\alpha,\varphi)\in\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{L}_{aa}
, we define:

deg(\alpha,\varphi):=deg(\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi)

Finally, the well-founded order
\ll
is defined between pairs
(\alpha,\varphi)\in\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{L}_{aa}
:

\displaystyle(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\beta,\psi)\text{ iff }

\displaystyle deg(\alpha,\varphi)<deg(\beta,\psi)

or

\displaystyle deg(\alpha,\varphi)=deg(\beta,\psi)\text{ and }\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|<\|\beta\|+\|\psi\|

Useful results about this order can be found in
[
4
]
.
Here, we only show the following property:

Lemma 11
.

Let
\alpha
be a word and
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
be a formula. Then:

deg(\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi)=deg(\varphi)+\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)

\dashv

Proof.

We show it by induction on
\|\alpha\|
. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{W}
be a word such that for all
\beta\in\mathcal{W}
, if
\|\beta\|<\|\alpha\|
, then
deg(\langle\beta\rangle\varphi)=deg(\varphi)+\sum_{\psi\in\beta}deg(\psi)
.
We now show that this property also holds for
\alpha
. We distinguish three cases:

•

Case
\alpha=\epsilon
. Obviously, for all formulas
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
,
deg(\langle\epsilon\rangle\varphi)=deg(\varphi)
.

•

Case
a\alpha
. We have the following:

\displaystyle deg(\langle a\alpha\rangle\varphi)

\displaystyle=deg(\langle a\rangle\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi)

\displaystyle=deg(\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi)\hskip 64.00003pt\text{by definition}

\displaystyle=deg(\varphi)+\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)\qquad\text{by induction hypothesis, because }\|\alpha\|<\|a\alpha\|

\displaystyle=deg(\varphi)+\sum_{\psi\in a\alpha}deg(\psi)\qquad\text{because }(\alpha a){\upharpoonright}_{!}=\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}

•

Case
\chi\alpha
. As above, we have:

\displaystyle deg(\langle\chi\alpha\rangle\varphi)

\displaystyle=deg(\langle\chi\rangle\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi)

\displaystyle=deg(\chi)+deg(\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi)\hskip 47.00003pt\text{by definition}

\displaystyle=deg(\chi)+deg(\varphi)+\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)\ \text{by induction hypothesis, because }\|\alpha\|<\|\chi\alpha\|

\displaystyle=deg(\varphi)+deg(\chi)+\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)

\displaystyle=deg(\varphi)+\sum_{\psi\in\chi\alpha}deg(\psi)

\square

We can now define the semantics for asynchronous announcements.

Definition 12
(Semantics).

Let
M=(W,\sim,V)
be an epistemic model. We simultaneously define the relation
\bowtie
between states
s\in W
and words
\alpha\in\mathcal{W}
and the relation
\vDash
between pairs
(s,\alpha)
of states and words, and formulas
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
by
\ll
-induction:

\displaystyle s\bowtie\epsilon

always

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha a

iff

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha\text{ and }|\alpha|_{a}<|\alpha|_{!}

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha\varphi

iff

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha\text{ and }s,\alpha\vDash\varphi

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash p

iff

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha\text{ and }s\in V(p)

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\top

iff

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\lnot\varphi

iff

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha\text{ and }s,\alpha\nvDash\varphi

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\varphi\lor\psi

iff

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\varphi\text{ or }s,\alpha\vDash\psi

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\hat{K}_{a}\varphi

iff

\displaystyle s\bowtie\alpha\text{ and }t,\beta\vDash\varphi\text{ for some }(t,\beta)\in W\times\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\text{ such that }s\sim_{a}t,\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta\text{ and }t\bowtie\beta

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\langle a\rangle\varphi

iff

\displaystyle|\alpha|_{a}<|\alpha|_{!}\text{ and }s,\alpha a\vDash\varphi

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\langle\varphi\rangle\psi

iff

\displaystyle s,\alpha\vDash\varphi\text{ and }s,\alpha\varphi\vDash\psi

\dashv

The semantics for the dual modalities is obtained as usual.

Proposition 13
.

The relations
\bowtie
and
\vDash
are well-defined.

\dashv

Proof.

We distinguish the following cases:

•

s\bowtie\alpha a
: for any formula
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{a}
we have
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha a,\varphi)
.
Indeed, obviously,
deg(\alpha,\varphi)=deg(\alpha a,\varphi)
and
\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|<\|\alpha a\|+\|\varphi\|=\|\alpha\|+1+\|\varphi\|
.

•

s\bowtie\alpha\varphi
: it is enough to show that, for any formula
\psi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
,
(\alpha,\psi)\ll(\alpha\varphi,\psi)
and
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha\varphi,\psi)
. Let
\psi
be a formula in
\mathcal{L}_{aa}
.
Obviously,
deg(\alpha,\psi)\leq deg(\alpha\varphi,\psi)
and
\|\alpha\|+\|\psi\|<\|\alpha\varphi\|+\|\psi\|=\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|
(with
\|\varphi\|>1)
. Similarly,
deg(\alpha,\varphi)\leq deg(\alpha\varphi,\psi)
and
\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|<\|\alpha\varphi\|+\|\psi\|
.

•

s,\alpha\vDash\lnot\varphi
:
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha,\lnot\varphi)
.
Indeed,
deg(\alpha,\varphi)=deg(\alpha,\lnot\varphi)
, and
\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|<\|\alpha\|+\|\lnot\varphi\|=\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|+1
.

•

s,\alpha\vDash\varphi\lor\psi
:
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha,\varphi\lor\psi)
and
(\alpha,\psi)\ll(\alpha,\varphi\lor\psi)
.

Indeed,
deg(\alpha,\varphi)\leq deg(\alpha,\varphi\lor\psi)
and
\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|<\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\lor\psi\|=\|\alpha\|+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|
(and similarly, for
(\alpha,\psi)\ll(\alpha,\varphi\lor\psi)
).

•

s,\alpha\vDash\hat{K}_{a}\varphi
: it is enough to show that
(\beta,\varphi)\ll(\alpha,\hat{K}_{a}\varphi)
for all histories
\beta
such that
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
. Let
\beta\in\mathcal{W}
be such that
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
.
Since
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
,
\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!}
is a prefix of
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!}
, which implies
\sum_{\psi\in\beta}deg(\psi)\leq\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)
. Moreover, by Lemma

,
deg(\langle\beta\rangle\varphi)=\sum_{\psi\in\beta}deg(\psi)+deg(\varphi)
and
deg(\langle\alpha\rangle\hat{K}_{a}\varphi)=\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)+deg(\hat{K}_{a}\varphi)=\sum_{\psi\in\alpha}deg(\psi)+deg(\varphi)+1
. Hence
deg(\beta,\varphi)<deg(\alpha,\hat{K}_{a}\varphi)
. Therefore
(\beta,\varphi)\ll(\alpha,\hat{K}_{a}\varphi)
.

•

\square

is a maximal element of

; likewise
\Lambda+\lnot\varphi\notin\mathcal{S}
. Since both
\Lambda+\varphi
and
\Lambda+\lnot\varphi
are theories (Lemma
37
) that, obviously, contain
[\alpha]\Gamma
, then
[\beta](\Lambda+\varphi)\nsubseteq\Delta
and
[\beta](\Lambda+\lnot\varphi)\nsubseteq\Delta
. Hence, there are formlas
\psi
and
\chi
such that

\displaystyle(1)\quad

\displaystyle\psi\in[\beta](\Lambda+\varphi)

and

\displaystyle\chi\in[\beta](\Lambda+\lnot\varphi)

\displaystyle(2)\quad

\displaystyle\psi\notin\Delta

and

\displaystyle\chi\notin\Delta.

From
(1)
we conclude that
\varphi\rightarrow[\beta]\psi\in\Lambda
and
\lnot\varphi\rightarrow[\beta]\chi\in\Lambda
. Then
[\beta]\psi\lor[\beta]\chi\in\Lambda
so
[\beta](\psi\lor\chi)\in\Lambda
. Hence
\varphi\lor\chi\in\Delta
. Since
\Delta
is maximal consistent, therefore either
\psi\in\Delta
or
\chi\in\Delta
(Corollary
40
). In both cases, we get a contradiction with
(2)
above. Therefore,
\Lambda
is maximal consistent.

The converse is straightforward.

\square

Proposition 44
.

Let
\Gamma,\Delta
be maximal consistent theories, and
\alpha
a word. Then,
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta
if, and only if,
[\alpha]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
.

\dashv

By convention,
\leq_{\epsilon}
is the identity relation.

Proof.

By induction on
\|\alpha\|
.

•

Cases
\alpha=a
and
\alpha=\varphi
are straightforward.

•

Case
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}a
. We have the following equivalences:

\displaystyle\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Gamma\leq_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\Delta^{\prime}\leq_{a}\Delta\text{ for some }\Delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha^{\prime}]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\Delta^{\prime}\leq_{a}\Delta\text{ for some }\Delta^{\prime}

by (IH)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha^{\prime}]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}\text{ and }[a]\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\Delta\text{ for some }\Delta^{\prime}

by Definition
42

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha^{\prime}a]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta

by Lemma
43

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta

•

Case
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\chi
. We have the following equivalences:

\displaystyle\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\Gamma\leq_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\Delta^{\prime}\leq_{\chi}\Delta\text{ for some }\Delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha^{\prime}]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}\text{ and }\Delta^{\prime}\leq_{\chi}\Delta\text{ for some }\Delta^{\prime}

by (IH)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha^{\prime}]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta^{\prime}\text{ and }[\chi]\Delta^{\prime}\subseteq\Delta\text{ for some }\Delta^{\prime}

by Definition
42

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha^{\prime}\chi]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta

by Lemma
43

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow[\alpha]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta

\square

Note that if
[a]\bot\in\Gamma
(resp.
[\chi]\bot\in\Gamma
) then for any maximal consistent theory
\Delta
,
\Gamma\nleq_{a}\Delta
(resp.
\Gamma\nleq_{\chi}\Delta
). Hence, fo all words
\alpha
, if
[\alpha]\bot\in\Gamma
then
\Gamma\nleq_{\alpha}\Delta
for any maximal consistent theory
\Delta
.

Lemma 45
(Existence Lemma).

Let
\Gamma
be a maximal consistent theory,
\varphi,\chi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
be formulas and
a\in\mathcal{A}
an agent.

\displaystyle(i)\quad

\displaystyle\text{If }\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\in\Gamma\text{ then there is a maximal consistent theory }\Delta\text{ s.t. }\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Delta\text{ and }\varphi\in\Delta.

\displaystyle(ii)\quad

\displaystyle\text{If }\langle a\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma\text{ then there is a maximal consistent theory }\Delta\text{ s.t. }\Gamma\leq_{a}\Delta\text{ and }\varphi\in\Delta.

\displaystyle(iii)\quad

\displaystyle\text{If }\langle\chi\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma\text{ then there is a maximal consistent theory }\Delta\text{ s.t. }\Gamma\leq_{\chi}\!\Delta\text{ and }\varphi\in\Delta.

\dashv

Proof.

(i)

Suppose
\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\in\Gamma
. Then
\lnot K_{a}\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
so
K_{a}\lnot\varphi\notin\Gamma
. Hence
\lnot\varphi\notin K_{a}\Gamma
. So, by Lemma
38
,
K_{a}\Gamma+\varphi
is consistent. Now, by Lindenbaum’s Lemma, we can extend
K_{a}T+\varphi
to a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
K_{a}\Gamma+\varphi\subseteq\Delta
. By Lemma
38
again,
K_{a}\Gamma\subseteq K_{a}\Gamma+\varphi
so
K_{a}\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
. Hence
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Delta
. Moreover
\varphi\in K_{a}\Gamma+\varphi
so
\varphi\in\Delta
.

(ii)

Suppose
\langle a\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
. Then
\langle a\rangle\top\in\Gamma
so
[a]\bot\notin\Gamma
. Hence
\bot\notin[a]\Gamma
:
[a]\Gamma
is consistent. By Lindenbaum’s Lemma, we can extend
[a]\Gamma
to a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
. Since
[a]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
,
\Gamma\leq_{a}\Delta
. Moreover, since
\langle a\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
and, by functionality, also
\langle a\rangle\varphi\rightarrow[a]\varphi\in\Gamma
, by modus ponens
[a]\varphi\in\Gamma
, and then
\varphi\in[a]\Gamma
. Hence
\varphi\in\Delta
.

(iii)

Supose
\langle\chi\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
. Then
\langle\chi\rangle\top\in\Gamma
so
[\chi]\bot\notin\Gamma
. Hence
\bot\notin[\chi]\Gamma
so
[\chi]\Gamma
is consistent. By Lindenbaum’s Lemma, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
[\chi]\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
. Hence
\Gamma\leq_{\chi}\Delta
. Moreover, by functionality and modus ponens,
[\chi]\varphi\in\Gamma
so
\varphi\in[\chi]\Gamma
and so
\varphi\in\Delta
.

\square

Corollary 46
.

Let
\Gamma
be a maximal consistent theory and
\alpha
a word. Then
\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
if, and only if, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta
and
\varphi\in\Delta
.

\dashv

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be a maximal consistent theory.

(
\Rightarrow
)

The proof of the left-to-right direction proceeds by induction on
\|\alpha\|
. Let
\alpha
be a word such that for all words
\alpha^{\prime}
, if
\|\alpha^{\prime}\|<\|\alpha\|
then for all formulas
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}
, if
\langle\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
then there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta
and
\varphi\in\Delta
. We show that this holds also for
\alpha
. We distinguish the following cases.

\bullet

Case
\alpha=\epsilon
. Take
\Delta=\Gamma
.

\bullet

Case
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}a
. Suppose
\langle\alpha^{\prime}a\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
,
i.e.

\langle\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\langle a\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
. Because
\|\alpha^{\prime}\|<\|\alpha^{\prime}a\|
, by induction hypothesis, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}^{\prime}\Delta
and
\langle a\rangle\varphi\in\Delta
. Then, by the Existence Lemma, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta^{\prime}
such that
\Delta\leq_{a}\Delta^{\prime}
and
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
. Hence
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha^{\prime}a}\Delta^{\prime}
and
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
.

\bullet

Case
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\psi
. Suppose
\langle\alpha^{\prime}\psi\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
,
i.e.

\langle\alpha^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
. Because
\|\alpha^{\prime}\|<\|\alpha^{\prime}\psi\|
, by induction hypothesis, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha^{\prime}}\Delta
and
\langle\psi\rangle\varphi\in\Delta
. As above, by the Existence Lemma, we conclude there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta^{\prime}
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha^{\prime}\psi}\Delta^{\prime}
and
\varphi\in\Delta^{\prime}
.

(
\Leftarrow
)

For the converse, suppose there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta
and
\varphi\in\Delta
. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi\notin\Gamma
. Then
\lnot\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
so
[\alpha]\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
. Now, by Proposition
44
, from
[\alpha]\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
and
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta
, we get
\lnot\varphi\in\Delta
. This, together with
\varphi\in\Delta
, contradicts the consistency of
\Delta
. Therefore,
\langle\alpha\rangle\varphi\in\Gamma
.

\square

Corollary 47
.

Let
\Gamma
be a maximal consistent theory and
\alpha
a word. Then
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Gamma
if, and only if, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\leq_{\alpha}\Delta
.

\dashv

We can now show that
\equiv_{a}
is not symmetric, and therefore not an equivalence relation. To show this, we need to find two maximal consistent theories
\Gamma,\Delta
such that
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Delta
but
\Delta\not\equiv_{a}\Gamma
. First note that
\lnot[a]\bot\land\hat{K}_{a}\top
is
\ast
-satisfiable. Indeed, take a model
(\{s\},\sim,V)
where
p
is true at
s
, and consider the history only consisting in
p
. Since
p
is true at
s
,
s\bowtie p
. Obviously
s,p\nvDash[a]\bot
so
s,p\vDash\lnot[a]\bot
, and
s,p\vDash\hat{K}_{a}\top
because
s\sim_{a}s
. Therefore
s,p\vDash\lnot[a]\bot\land\hat{K}_{a}\top
. This shows that
\lnot(\lnot[a]\bot\land\hat{K}_{a}\top)
is not
\ast
-valid. By the Soundness Theorem (Theorem
33
), then,
\lnot(\lnot[a]\bot\land\hat{K}_{a}\top)\notin\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
so there is a maximal consistent theory
\Gamma
such that
\lnot[a]\bot\land\hat{K}_{a}\top\in\Gamma
. Now, since
\hat{K}_{a}\top\in\Gamma
, by the Corollary
47
, there is a maximal consistent theory
\Delta
such that
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Delta
. We now need to show that
\Delta\not\equiv_{a}\Gamma
. By Proposition
32
,
K_{a}[a]\bot
is a theorem, so
K_{a}[a]\bot\in\Delta
. However,
\lnot[a]\bot\in\Gamma
so
[a]\bot\notin\Gamma
. Therefore
K_{a}\Delta\not\subset\Gamma
. Hence
\Delta\not\equiv_{a}\Gamma
so
\equiv_{a}
is not symmetric.

However, we can restrict the relations
\equiv_{a}
to a specific set of maximal consistent theories wherein it would be an equivalence relation. This is what we do in the following definition of a canonical model for asynchronous announcements.
To show completeness of
\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
, it is enough to define a model
associated to
a maximal consistent theory that contains
\mathsf{empty}
.
Such a theory exists because
\lnot\mathsf{empty}\notin\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
: first note that
\lnot\mathsf{empty}
is not
\ast
-valid because it is not valid (remember that always-validity implies validity, see
16
). Then, by the soundness of
\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
(Theorem
33
),
\lnot\mathsf{empty}
is not derivable:
\lnot\mathsf{empty}\notin\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
. Hence
\textbf{AA}^{\ast}+\mathsf{empty}
is a consistent theory (by Lemma
38
), and we can extend it to a maximal consistent theory, by Lindenbaum’s Lemma (
41
).

Definition 48
(Canonical model).

Let
\Sigma
be a maximal consistent theory such that
\mathsf{empty}\in\Sigma
. The canonical model associated to
\Sigma
is defined as
M_{\Sigma}:=(W_{\Sigma},(\sim_{\Sigma,a})_{a\in\mathcal{A}},V_{\Sigma})
, where:

•

W_{\Sigma}:=\{\Gamma\text{ maximal consistent theory}\ |\ \Sigma\equiv\Gamma\}

•

\sim_{\Sigma,a}
is the restriction of
\equiv_{a}
to
W_{\Sigma}

•

V_{\Sigma}(p):=\{\Gamma\in W_{\Sigma}\ |\ p\in\Gamma\}
.

\dashv

First, the following result can be easily proved with axiom (
\mathsf{empty}
K):

Lemma 49
.

Let
\Sigma
be a maximal consistent theory containing
\mathsf{empty}
,
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
. Then
\mathsf{empty}\in\Delta
.

\dashv

Now, we show that the canonical model is an epistemic model:

Lemma 50
.

Let
\Sigma
be a maximal consistent theory such that
\mathsf{empty}\in\Sigma
. Then, for any agent
a\in\mathcal{A}
,
\equiv_{a}
restricted to
W_{\Sigma}
is an equivalence relation, so all relations
\sim_{\Sigma,a}
are equivalence relations (for
a\in\mathcal{A}
).

\dashv

Proof.

Let
\Sigma
be a maximal consistent theory. Suppose
\mathsf{empty}\in\Sigma
. Let
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\Gamma,\Delta,\Lambda\in W_{\Sigma}
. Since
\mathsf{empty}\rightarrow K_{a}\mathsf{empty}\in\Sigma
,
\mathsf{empty}\in\Gamma
,
\mathsf{empty}\in\Delta
and
\mathsf{empty}\in\Lambda
. We verify that
\sim_{\Sigma,a}
is reflexive and Euclidean.

•

We first show that
\sim_{\Sigma,a}
is reflexive. If
\varphi\in K_{a}\Gamma
then
K_{a}\varphi\in\Gamma
so
\varphi\in\Gamma
because
\mathsf{empty}\rightarrow(K_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\varphi)\in\Gamma
and
\mathsf{empty}\in\Gamma
. So
K_{a}\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma
. Hence
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Gamma
.

•

We now show that
\sim_{\Sigma,a}
is Euclidean. Suppose
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Delta
and
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Lambda
. Suppose towards a contradiction that
\Delta\not\equiv_{a}\Lambda
. Then there is
\varphi\in K_{a}\Delta
such that
\varphi\notin\Lambda
. Hence
\lnot\varphi\in\Lambda
. Then, because
K_{a}\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
,
K_{a}\varphi\notin\Gamma
, so
\lnot\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi\notin\Gamma
.
Hence,
\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
. Now, from axiom (5),
\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi\rightarrow K_{a}\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
, so by modus ponens
K_{a}\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
. From this and
\Gamma\equiv_{a}\Delta
, we get
\hat{K}_{a}\lnot\varphi\in\Delta
. Hence
\lnot K_{a}\varphi\in\Delta
. But, by hypothesis,
K_{a}\varphi\in\Delta
: this contradicts the consistency of
\Delta
. Therefore,
\Delta\equiv_{a}\Lambda
.

\square

Corollary 51
.

The relation
\equiv
is an equivalence relation on
W_{\Sigma}
.

\dashv

Proof.

By Lemma
50
and Definition
48
,
\sim_{\Sigma,a}
is an equivalence relation, for all agents
a
. Then, since
\equiv
is the reflexive and transitive closure of the union of all
\equiv_{a}
,
\equiv
restricted to
\Sigma
is the reflexive and transitive closure of the union of equivalence relations, so it is an equivalence relation itself.

\square

We can now show the main lemma to prove completeness.

Lemma 54
(Truth Lemma).

Let
\Sigma
be a maximal consistent theory containing
\mathsf{empty}
. Let
\varphi
be a formula and
\alpha
a word. In the following, we consider
M_{\Sigma}
, the canonical model associated to
\Sigma
, defined in Definition
48
.

•

For all
\Lambda\in W_{\Sigma}
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Lambda

(2)

\Lambda\bowtie\alpha
.

•

For all maximal consistent theories
\Gamma
such that
\Sigma\ \equiv\circ\leq_{\alpha}\ \Gamma
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi\in\Gamma

(ii)

For all
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
, if
\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
then
\Delta,\alpha\vDash\varphi

(iii)

There is
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
such that
\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
and
\Delta,\alpha\vDash\varphi
.

\dashv

Proof.

The proof proceeds by
\ll
-induction on
(\alpha,\varphi)
. Here, we first show the proof for the first item:
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
. Let
\Lambda\in W_{\Sigma}
.

•

Case
(\epsilon,\varphi)
. By definition,
\top\in\Lambda
and
\Lambda\bowtie\epsilon
.

•

Case
(\alpha a,\varphi)
. Suppose
\langle\alpha a\rangle\top\in\Lambda
,
i.e.

\langle\alpha\rangle\langle a\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. Then
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. Because
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha a,\varphi)
, by induction hypothesis,
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha
. We now need to show
|\alpha|_{a}<|\alpha|_{!}
. Suppose
|\alpha|_{a}\geq|\alpha|_{!}
. Then,
\mathsf{empty}\rightarrow[\alpha][a]\bot\in\Lambda
(axiom (Exc!
3
)). But since
\Lambda\in W_{\Sigma}
,
\mathsf{empty}\in\Lambda
. So
[\alpha][a]\bot\in\Lambda
by modus ponens. Hence
\lnot\langle\alpha\rangle\langle a\rangle\top\in\Lambda
, which contradicts the fact that
\Lambda
is consistent. Therefore,
|\alpha|_{a}<|\alpha|_{!}
and since
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha
we get
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha a
.
Conversely, suppose
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha a
. Then
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha
and
|\alpha|_{!}<|\alpha|_{a}
. Because
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha a,\varphi)
, by induction hypothesis,
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. Now, from
|\alpha|_{a}<|\alpha|_{!}
and axiom (Exec
2
) we get
[\alpha]\langle a\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. Since also
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Lambda
, by Lemma
52
,
\langle\alpha\rangle\langle a\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. Hence
\langle\alpha a\rangle\top\in\Lambda
.

•

Case
(\alpha\psi,\varphi)
. Suppose
\langle\alpha\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda
,
i.e.

\langle\alpha\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. Then
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Lambda
so, because
(\alpha,\psi)\ll(\alpha\psi,\varphi)
, by induction hypothesis
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha
. Now, since
\langle\alpha\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda
, by the Existence Lemma there is
\Lambda^{\prime}
a maximal consistent theory such that
\Lambda\leq_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\langle\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. Also, from axiom (Exec
1
),
\psi\leftrightarrow\langle\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. Hence, by modus ponens,
\psi\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. Moreover, since
\Sigma\equiv\Lambda\leq_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\prime}
and
(\alpha,\psi)\ll(\alpha\psi,\varphi)
, by induction hypothesis
\Lambda,\alpha\vDash\psi
. So
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha\psi
.
Conversely, suppose
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha\psi
. So
\Lambda\bowtie\alpha
and
\Lambda,\alpha\vDash\psi
. Because
(\alpha,\psi)\ll(\alpha\psi,\varphi)
, by induction hypothesis,
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Lambda
. So there is a maximal consistent theory
\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
\Lambda\leq_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\prime}
. Since
\Sigma\equiv\Lambda\leq_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda,\alpha\vDash\psi
, by induction hypothesis again,
\psi\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. Hence
\langle\alpha\rangle\psi\in\Lambda
. Therefore
\langle\alpha\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda
,
i.e.

\langle\alpha\psi\rangle\top\in\Lambda
.

We now show the proof for the second item:
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
. Note that
(ii)
implies
(iii)
because
\Sigma\equiv\circ\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
implies that there is
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
such that
\Sigma\equiv\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
. Hence, we only need to show
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
and
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
.

•

Case
(\alpha,p)
.

-

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Suppose
p\in\Gamma
. Let
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
be such that
\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
so
\Delta\bowtie\alpha
by Corollary
47
and the first item of the Truth Lemma. Suppose
\Delta,\alpha\nvDash p
. Then
p\notin\Delta
. Hence
\lnot p\in\Delta
. Since
\lnot p\rightarrow[\alpha]\lnot p\in\Delta
, by modus ponens
[\alpha]\lnot p\in\Delta
. So
\lnot p\in\Gamma
, which together with
p\in\Gamma
contradicts the consistency of
\Gamma
. Therefore
\Delta,\alpha\vDash p
.

-

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Suppose there is
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
such that
\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
and
\Delta,\alpha\vDash p
. Then
p\in\Delta
. Moreover,
p\rightarrow[\alpha]p\in\Delta
so by modus ponens
[\alpha]p\in\Delta
. Therefore
p\in\Gamma
.

•

Case
(\alpha,\top)
. Obviously
\top\in\Gamma
so
(i)
holds. Suppose
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
is such that
\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
. Then, by Corollary
47
,
\langle\alpha\rangle\top\in\Delta
so
\Delta\bowtie\alpha
by the first item. Hence
\Delta,\alpha\vDash\top
:
(ii)
holds and so
(iii)
does.

•

Case
(\alpha,\lnot\varphi)
. To apply the induction hypothesis we use
(\alpha,\varphi)\ll(\alpha,\lnot\varphi)
.

-

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
. Suppose
\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
. Let
\Delta\in W_{\Sigma}
be such that
\Delta\leq_{\alpha}\Gamma
. Then
\Delta\bowtie\alpha
. Suppose
\Delta,\alpha\nvDash\lnot\varphi
. Then, because
\Delta\bowtie\alpha
and
\Delta,\alpha\nvDash\lnot\varphi
,
\Delta,\alpha\vDash\varphi
. Hence, by induction hypothesis,
\varphi\in\Gamma
, which together with
\lnot\varphi\in\Gamma
contradicts the consistency of
\Gamma
. Therefore
\Delta,\alpha\vDash\lnot\varphi
.

-

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)

5
Single-agent case

In the preceding sections, we proposed an axiomatisation for always-validities when the set of agents contains at least two distinct agents, by reducing
\ast

\epsilon
-validities. Now, we shall explain why this method does not apply in the single-agent case. In the following, we consider the language with only one agent
\mathcal{L}_{aa}(\{a\})

Contrary to the multi-agent language (see Lemma
26
), in the single-agent case, there is no formula
\mathsf{empty}^{\prime}
that characterises the initial model,
i.e.
the fact that the current history is empty. To demonstrate this, we first show two lemmas.

Lemma 55
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two words. Then
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
if, and only if,
\top a\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\top a\beta
.

\dashv

Proof.

Suppose
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
. Then
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!a}=\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!a}=\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!}
so obviously
(\top a\alpha){\upharpoonright}_{!a}=(\top a\beta){\upharpoonright}_{!a}=(\top a\beta){\upharpoonright}_{!}
. Hence
\top a\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\top a\beta
.
Conversely, suppose
\top a\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\top a\beta

(\top a\alpha){\upharpoonright}_{!a}=(\top a\beta){\upharpoonright}_{!a}=(\top a\beta){\upharpoonright}_{!}
and thus
\alpha{\upharpoonright}_{!a}=\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!a}=\beta{\upharpoonright}_{!}
. Hence
\alpha\vartriangleright_{a}\beta
.

\square

Proposition 58
.

There is no formula
\mathsf{empty}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{L}_{aa}(\{a\})
such that for all models

, all states

and all words

if, and only if,
\alpha=\epsilon
.

\dashv

Proof.

Suppose that there is a formula
\mathsf{empty}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}_{aa}(\{a\})
such that for all models
M=(W,\sim,V)
, states
s\in W
and words
\alpha
,
s,\epsilon\vDash\langle\alpha\rangle\mathsf{empty}^{\prime}
if, and only if,
\alpha=\epsilon
. Let
M=(W,\sim,V)
be a model and
s\in W
a state. By hypothesis
s,\epsilon\vDash\mathsf{empty}^{\prime}
. But since
\vDash\varphi\leftrightarrow\langle\top a\rangle\varphi
for all formulas
\varphi

. But

.

\square

Such a result relates to Lemma

that are crucial to implement the method we used to axiomatise AA

\textbf{AA}^{\ast}
exploits the possibility to go from any history
\alpha

\mathsf{empty}
, precisely because

is satisfied if, and only if,
\alpha=\epsilon
. This way, we somehow reduced

\epsilon
-validities. However, Proposition

states that there is no formula in the language with only one agent that can play the same role as
\mathsf{empty}
in the multi-agent case. Therefore, in the single-agent case, it is not possible to express the fact that the current history is empty. This is why we cannot directly adapt our axiomatisation to the single-agent case. Some other method would need to be employed here.

In this section, we relate our semantics to three-valued logics. We also discuss the notion of cut in distributed computing and relate it to that of history and the notion of cut defined in

]
. Finally, we discuss choices of epistemic modalities.

Three-valued logic?

Although
s,\alpha\vDash\lnot\varphi
implies
s,\alpha\nvDash\varphi
, the converse need not hold: whereas
s,\alpha\nvDash\bot
always holds,
s,\alpha\vDash\lnot\bot
only if
s\bowtie\alpha
. Indeed, for a state
s
wherein
p
is false, we have
s,\alpha\nvDash\bot
and
s,\alpha\nvDash\lnot\bot
because
s\not\bowtie\alpha
. Our semantics therefore has a flavour of a three-valued logic, with values true, false and undefined, as in Kleene three-valued logics
[
18
,
9
,
10
]
. This appears more clearly if we consider
\bowtie
as a definability relation between pairs
(s,\alpha)
and formulas
\varphi
such that
(s,\alpha)\bowtie\varphi
if, and only if,
s\bowtie\alpha
. However, it is a strange kind of three-valued logic. This is why, curiously, our logic is both weak and strong Kleene three-valued logic. Let us explain. Let the three values be
\mathsf{t}
(true),
\mathsf{f}
(false) and
\mathsf{u}
(unknown). Given that
(s,\alpha)\bowtie\varphi
for all
\varphi
if
s\bowtie\alpha
, any formula is either undefined (has value
\mathsf{u}
) or is defined (has value
\mathsf{t}
or
\mathsf{f}
). We therefore cannot have that
\varphi
is defined and
\psi
is undefined so that we would have to choose whether their conjunction, or disjunction, or implication, is undefined (in weak Kleene) or defined (in strong Kleene). The semantics is too rough to distinguish weak from strong. Similarly,
K_{a}\varphi
is defined if, and only if,
\hat{K}_{a}\varphi
is defined. The issue of definability in our semantics rather pertains to asynchronous states
(s,\alpha)
than to formulas, as in Kleene logics. Therefore, our logic still have validities, contrary to Kleene logics. We do not know if there is a three-valued modal logic that has exactly the features of ours.

Consistent and inconsistent cuts

The notion of history is related to that of a
cut
in a distributed system, where agents are processors communicating with each other. A cut represents the global state of a distributed computation at a particular instant, like a snapshot of a running process. It specifies sequences of events among sending or receiving of messages. For each agent we can distinguish the events before and up to the cut from those that come after the cut
[
21
]
. A cut is
inconsistent
if a message has been received before the cut but was only sent after the cut, and otherwise it is consistent
[
23
,
20
]
.
In our setting, consistent cuts induce histories: a word
\alpha
is a history if agents are able to receive only messages that have already been sent. But our agreement/executability relation
\bowtie
provides a more fine-grained notion of consistency than that of cut. For instance, histories
\alpha:=p.\lnot K_{a}p.a.a
and
\beta:=p.a.\lnot K_{a}p.a
result of consistent cuts, but
\alpha
is executable, namely in states wherein
p
is true, whereas
\beta
cannot be executed: if
s\bowtie.pa
then
s,p.a\vDash K_{a}p
so
s,p.a\nvDash\lnot K_{a}p
and thus
s\not\bowtie p.a.\lnot K_{a}p
.

In
[
19
]
, asynchronous communication in a distributed system is modeled through sequences of announcements and notions also called cuts that more directly correspond to the cuts in distributed computing. A cut specifies the number of announcements that each agent has received so far: a state is a triple
(s,\sigma,c)
where
s
is a point in a Kripke model,
\sigma
is a sequence of announcements, and
cut

c
lists for each agent the announcements in
\sigma
that she has received so far. When interpreting epistemic formulas, not all triples
(s,\sigma,c)
are considered but only those corresponding to
consistent states
: consistency is a relation between states
s
and pairs
(\sigma,c)
, which is similar to our executability relation. Roughly, a state is consistent if the announcements it contains were true when they were made. Their notion also does not exactly correspond to the notion of consistent cut in distributed computing. The semantics in
[
19
]
allows inconsistent states to satisfy formulas, as in
[
4
]
but unlike in our semantics.

Belief or knowledge?

In our semantics an agent knows something if it holds for all histories she considers possible on the assumption that she has received all information. The agent does not consider it possible that other messages have been sent that she has not yet received but that other agents may have received. Therefore our notion of asynchronous knowledge is not an
S5
notion of knowledge, in particular it does not satisfy the truth axiom
K_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\varphi
. However it satisfies
\mathsf{empty}\rightarrow(K_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\varphi)
(the axiom (
\mathsf{empty}
T
)). We recall that
\mathsf{empty}
is defined as
\bigwedge_{a\in\mathcal{A}}[a]\bot\land\bigwedge_{a,b\in\mathcal{A}}K_{a}[b]\bot
. When all agents know that they all have received all announcements, knowledge is indeed factual. Without that assumption, our epistemic modality is more like one of consistent belief, which is why in prior publications with a similar semantics such as
[
4
]
the notation
B_{a}\varphi
was used for that, not
K_{a}\varphi
. But given
\mathsf{empty}\rightarrow(K_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\varphi)
we still believe we proposed an acceptable notion of asynchronous knowledge. In
[
19
]
, a notion of asynchronous knowledge is proposed that satisfies
K_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\varphi
as the accessibility relation not only takes received announcements into account but also possible future announcements — in their setting assuming commonly known protocols that is then a more natural assumption. This has the benefit that knowledge is again standard
S5
knowledge, interpreted with an equivalence relation. However, it comes at a price, both theoretically and conceptually. No axiomatisations are proposed in their work for such asynchronous knowledge. Also, given a semantics where messages are public announcements in the logical language wherein positive information grows (of atoms, knowledge of atoms, etc.) but where ignorance may linger forever unless resolved,
an agent can never know that another agent is ignorant
:
K_{a}\lnot(K_{b}p\lor K_{b}\lnot p)
is unsatisfiable in the
[
19
]
semantics. Obtaining an
S5
notion of knowledge by satisfaction and executability (or agreement, consistency, …) relations requires imposing structural restrictions to avoid circularity. The authors of
[
19
]
provide two well-founded solutions to this circularity issue either by imposing conditions on the structure of the model and the model transformations, or by restricting the language.

7
Conclusion and future research

We presented a multi-agent epistemic logic of asynchronous announcements with epistemic modalities for asynchronous knowledge, modalities for messages sending that correspond to broadcasting announcements, and modalities for individual reception of sent messages by the agents. Formulas are evaluated with respect to a state of an epistemic model and a prior history of such messages and receptions. We provided an infinitary axiomatisation
AA
∗
for the always-validies, the formulas that are true in any model after any history of prior actions.

In future research we wish to axiomatise the single-agent case, to which our method cannot be applied, to add group epistemic modalities such as distributed knowledge, and consider non-public communication. We also envisage introducing other dynamic modalities to represent arbitrary reception or forgetting information, and to propose a semantics where asynchronous knowledge is factual.

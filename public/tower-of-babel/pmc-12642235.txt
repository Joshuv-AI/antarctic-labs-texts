ANGPTL4 overexpression is associated with progression and poor prognoses of olfactory neuroblastoma
Authors: Yang Yunyun, Li Yahui, Piao Yingshi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyze expression levels of angiopoietin‑like 4 (ANGPTL4) in olfactory neuroblastoma (ONB) to investigate the association between ANGPTL4 and ONB.MethodsImmunohistochemistry was performed on 109 formalin‑fixed, paraffin‑embedded ONB tissue samples to detected the expression of ANGPTL4. Immunofluorescence co-localization was performed to detected the expression sites of ANGPTL4. Western blotting was used to measure the protein levels of ANGPTL4, Hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF-1α), and CD31 in tumor and Para tumoral tissues. The non‑parametric Kruskal–Wallis test was used to evaluate differential expression of ANGPTL4 and clinicopathological parameters of ONB. Cox regression analysis was used to evaluate the association between ANGPTL4 expression levels and ONB prognosis.ResultsImmunohistochemistry revealed that ANGPTL4 expression (moderately positive + strongly positive) was higher in high grade (Ⅲ+Ⅳ) ONB (98.2%; 55/56) compared with low grade (Ⅰ+Ⅱ) ONB (56.6%; 30/53). Immunofluorescence co-localization revealed that ANGPTL4 both focus on microvessel and macrophage, in addition, ANGPTL4 is co-located with HIF-1a. Western blotting showed that ANGPTL4, HIF-1α and CD31 expression was significantly increased in the tumor area compared with the paratumor area. The clinical significance of ANGPTL4 expression was analyzed in 109 ONB tissues, and high expression levels of ANGPTL4 were positively associated with the pathological grade (P < 0.001) and local recurrence (P < 0.001). Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that patients with high ANGPTL4 expression had shorter disease‑free survival (DFS; P = 0.021, mean survival time: 93 ± 10.042 vs. 153 ± 13.835), but no difference in overall survival (P = 0.121). Multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that ANGPTL4 was an independent prognostic factor for DFS (P = 0.028).ConclusionsThese results demonstrated that ANGPTL4 might associated with a poor prognosis of ONB. ANGPTL4 expression were focus on CD34, macrophage and HIF-1α, suggesting that ANGPTL4 might associated with hypoxia and might play important role in angiogenesis and inflammatory. ANGPTL4 is a novel therapeutic target for ONB.Keywords: Olfactory neuroblastoma, Angiopoietin‑like 4, Angiogenesis

Introduction

Olfactory neuroblastoma (ONB) is a rare malignant tumor that arises from the olfactory epithelium in the nasal cavity [1]. ONB represents approximately 5% of all nasal malignant tumors and occurs in all age groups with a similar frequency in males and females [2]. ONBs show remarkable differences in their biological activity, ranging from indolent growth with survival times of more than 20 years to aggressive metastatic disease leading to death within a few months [3].

One of the main pathological features of ONB is marked angiogenesis [4]. Therefore, micro vessel is a valuable independent prognostic indicator for ONB and expected to be an important marker to distinguish tumor histological grades [5]. Compared with the healthy vasculature, tumor blood vessels are extremely immature, highly permeable, and deficient in pericytes. The aberrantly vascularized tumor microenvironment is characterized by hypoxia and immunosuppression. The efficacy of chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy is affected by abnormal blood vessels. Some anti-angiogenic drugs show vascular normalization effects in addition to targeting angiogenesis. Reversing the abnormal state of blood vessels creates a normal microenvironment, essential for various cancer treatments, specifically immunotherapy. In addition, immune cells and molecules are involved in the regulation of angiogenesis. Therefore, combining vascular normalization with immunotherapy may increase the efficacy of immunotherapy and reduce the risk of adverse reactions [6]. Our previous study found that, M1 macrophage were significantly high in low grade ONB compared with high grade [7]. Indicate that immunotherapy might effective for ONB.

Altered expression of secreted factors by tumor cells or cells in the tumor microenvironment is a major event in cancer development and progression [8]. Angiopoietin-like protein 4 (ANGPTL4) is one of the members of ANGPTL protein family and exhibits a widespread distribution of tissue expression. Hyperoxia occurs to counteract hypoxic effects in the tumor microenvironment, which increases the malignant tumor phenotype and poor prognoses [9]. ANGPTL4 is activated in a hypoxic microenvironment and promotes angiogenesis, which is involved in the occurrence and development of various tumors [10]. The aim of this study was to examine the relationship between ANGPTL4 and ONB, which is of great significance for ONB treatment and prognosis.

Methods

Study cohort

ONB tissue specimens were consecutively collected from surgical resections of 109 patients who underwent endoscopic surgery at the Beijing Tong Ren Hospital (Beijing, China) between June 2006 and November 2021. All ONB cases were pathologically confirmed by immunohistochemical staining of cytokeratin, S-100, NSE, synaptophysin, chromogranin, and CD56. Tissues were collected from patients who were admitted for the first time and had not been treated with radiotherapy or chemotherapy. Patients who did not meet the above criteria were excluded.

A total of 109 patients were enrolled in the study. All participants were Chinese. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Beijing Tong Ren Hospital (Approval no. MR-11–23–000050) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Histopathological grading was carried out in accordance with Hyams [11, 12]. Tumor staging was based on Kadish. Because of the small number of patients, Hyams’ histopathological grades are commonly analyzed in two groups (grades I and II versus grades III and IV). The type of recurrence (local, regional, or distant) was included [12].

Western blot analysis

Due to the rare cases of ONB patients, it is difficult to obtain fresh tissue. We collected a total of three cases of ONB. Tumor and paratumor biopsies (3–5 mm away from the primary tumor) were obtained from the Department of ENT, Beijing Tongren Hospital, under the guidelines of the hospital ethics committee and with informed consent from patients. Samples were frozen and stored and shipped at 80 °C to the laboratory for further processing. Equal amounts of protein (40 µg/lane) were separated on a 10% SDS-PAGE gel. Blots were probed overnight at 4 °C with anti-β-actin (Abcam; USA, 1:1,000 dilution), anti-ANGPTL4 (Abcam; USA, 1:1,000, dilution), anti-HIF-1α (Abcam; USA, 1:1000, dilution), or anti-CD31 (Sigma-Aldrich; 1:1,000, dilution) antibodies, washed with Tris-buffered saline containing Tween 20, and then incubated with secondary antibodies (ZSGB-BIO, Beijing, China; 1:10,000, dilution) for 1 h at room temperature. Blots were washed, incubated with SuperSignal™ WestFemto Maximum Sensitivity Substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), and then analyzed using a ChemiDoc™ Touch Imaging System (Bio-Rad).

Immunofluorescence co-localization

6 patients of ONB (grade Ⅱ-Ⅲ) were detected by Immunofluorescence co-localization for the expression of ANGPTL4, HIF-1α, CD31 and macrophage. primary antibodies: A4 + CD31 (Abcam + Servicebio, ab196746 + GB11063-2, 1:200 + 1:2000), A4 + F4/80 (Abcam + Servicebio, ab196746 + GB113373, 1:200 + 1:2000), A4 + HIF-a (Abcam + Abcam, ab196746 + ab179483, 1:200 + 1:1000).

Immunohistochemical staining

Tissue samples were fixed in 10% formalin for 24 h at room temperature and then embedded in paraffin. Tissue Sect. (4 μm thick) were deparaffinized in xylene and rehydrated in a descending ethanol series (100%, 95%, 80%, and 70%) at room temperature. After dewaxing and hydration, the sections were autoclaved in EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) for 2 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was inactivated by treatment with 3% H2O2 for 10 min at room temperature. Non‑specific binding sites were blocked by incubation with 10% normal goat serum (cat. no. ab7481; Abcam) at room temperature for 20 min. Tissue sections were then incubated with a primary anti-ANGPTL4 polyclonal antibody (1:100 dilution; cat. no. A01147; Abcam) at 37 °C for 2 h. The sections were then subjected to the Dako REAL™ EnVision™ Detection System (cat. no. K5007; Dako; Agilent Technologies, Inc.) containing biotinylated anti‑rabbit secondary antibodies (1:100 dilution) for 20 min at 37 °C and peroxidase‑labeled streptavidin for 20 min at room temperature (1:100 dilution). For the negative control, the primary antibody was replaced with PBS. Sections were developed with 3,3ʹ‑diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride at room temperature for 15 s.

The percentage of positive cells was rated as follows: cases with 10% positive cells were considered negative (no points), regardless of staining intensity; 1 point, 11%–50% positive cells; 2 points, 51%–80% positive cells; 3 points, 80% positive cells. The staining intensity was rated as follows: 1 point, weak intensity; 2 points, moderate intensity; 3 points, strong intensity. Points from the staining intensity and percentage of positive cells were summed, and specimens were classified into four groups by their overall score: negative, < 10% of cells stained positive, regardless of intensity; weak expression, 1 or 2 points; moderate expression, 3 or 4 points; strong expression, 5 or 6 points [13].

Evaluation of tissue cells

The Ki-67 labeling index was determined at ×400 magnification. The highest Ki-67 labeling index was determined by calculating the percentage of positive tumor cell nuclei within three fields with the highest apparent number of stained nuclei. Determination of microvessel by immunostaining of CD31 was performed in accordance with the methods of Weidner [14]. After scanning the immunostained section at low magnification (×40), we selected the area in the tumor or directly adjacent to tumor formations with the highest density of distinctly highlighted microvessels (hot spot). Each stained lumen was regarded to be a single countable microvessel. If there was no lumen, but only a single positive cell was visible, this cell was also interpreted as representing a microvessel.

Statistics

Data were analyzed using Prism 5.0 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA) and are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean. In all cases, at least three independent experiments were performed. Statistical comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test. The distribution of ANGPTL4 expression in ONB patients in accordance with clinicopathological features was analyzed using the non‑parametric Kruskal–Walli’s test. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval between surgery and death of the patient. Data on patients who had survived beyond the end of the observation period were censored at the last follow up visit. Deaths from a cause other than ONB were considered censoring events. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the interval between the end of therapy and the first evidence of progression of the disease. Univariate analysis of OS and DFS was performed as outlined by.

Kaplan and Meier. Follow-up end time was December 2023. The Kaplan–Meier method was used to evaluate the association between ANGPTL4 expression and ONB prognosis, and differences in survival distributions were evaluated using the log‑rank test. Cox regression analysis was used to identify factors that may have a significant influence on survival. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v17.0 (SPSS, Inc.). P < 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

Results

Clinical and histopathological characteristics of patients

Patient age at the time of surgery ranged from 3 to 72 years (mean, 43.55 ± 13.47). 63 tumors (57.8%) had occurred in males and 46 (42.2%) in females. The percentages of grades were grade Ⅰ 13.8% (15/109), grade Ⅱ 36.7% (40/109), grade Ⅲ 29.4% (32/109), and grade Ⅳ 20.2% (22/109). The vital status of ONB patients was (68/109) 63% alive and well, (34/109) 31% alive with disease, and (7/109) 6% dead with disease (Table 1).

Association between ANGPTL4 expression and ONB prognosis

In the analyzed cohort, duration of follow-up ranged from 1 to 188 months (mean, 49.26 months). Sixty-nine patients were alive and well, and 40 were alive with recurrent disease, 3 were recurrent three times. Six patients had died of disease. Overall survival was 97% at 5 years and 87% at 10 years. Disease-free survival at 5 years was 60% and at 10 years was 47%. Local and regional recurrence occurred after an average of 26.88 months but with a range of 1 to 109 months. Kaplan–Meier analysis of the ONB patients demonstrated significant differences in DFS between ONB patients with negative or positive expression of ANGPTL4 (P = 0.021, mean survival time: 93 ± 10.042 vs. 153 ± 13.835). However, no significant difference was observed in OS between patients with negative or positive ANGPTL4 expression (P = 0.121). Multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that the pathological grade, Ki67, CD31 and ANGPTL4 expression were significant prognostic factors for ONB. Patients with positive ANGPTL4 expression had a 2.858‑fold higher relative risks of DFS than those with negative ANGPTL4 expression (P = 0.028, HR = 2.858, 95%CI:1.118–7.302). (Table 4; Fig. 4).

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that ANGPTL4 plays a major role in ONB. ANGPTL4 expression was higher in the tumor area than the paratumor area. Moreover, as the grade of ONB increased, the positive rate of ANGPTL4 expression also increased gradually. HIF-1α and CD31 are expressed in tumor stromal blood vessels, and ANGPTL4 is expressed in both tumor cells and stromal cells, and can co-locate with stromal blood vessels and macrophages. Patients with positive ANGPTL4 expression had a 2.858‑fold higher relative risk of DFS than those with negative ANGPTL4 expression. This suggests that ANGPTL4 is associated with poor outcomes and might be a novel therapeutic target.

Tumorigenesis is a multistep process that includes sustained proliferative signaling, evading growth suppressors, resisting cell death, inducing angiogenesis, and activating invasion and metastasis [15]. Neoangiogenesis is crucial for tumor growth and metastasis, and microvessel is expected to be an important marker to distinguish tumor histological grade [5]. Angiogenesis is an important hallmark of malignant tumors [16]. The production of numerous abnormal blood vessels has deleterious effects that promote tumor development. Anti-angiogenic therapy deprives the tumor of oxygen and nutrients to induce necrosis. However, clinical use of anti-angiogenic drugs has led to unexpected outcomes, such as chemotherapy resistance and increased propensity to metastasis. This may be attributed to the structural changes in immature newly developed vasculature and its resulting dysfunction after the destruction of a large number of blood vessels by anti-angiogenic therapy [17]. Therefore, the focus of vascular research in oncology has shifted from understanding only anti-angiogenesis to normalizing tumor blood vessels. A window of vascular normalization can be achieved transiently during the initial days of anti-angiogenesis therapy.Various proangiogenic factors, such as VEGF and FGF-2, are expressed in neuroblastic tumors, suggesting a major role of such factors in ONB biology [4]. Our immunohistochemical staining showed that CD31 was highly expressed in ONB interstitial blood vessels. Gene expression profiling of ONB has shown enrichment of the angiogenesis pathway in patients who experience recurrence, which is significantly associated with shorter DFS [18]. These findings suggest that angiogenesis plays a major role in ONB.

Angiopoietin-like (ANGPTL) proteins include eight secreted glycoproteins with structural similarity to members of the angiopoietin protein family. Emerging evidence has supported autocrine and paracrine activities of the ANGPTL protein family in angiogenesis, inflammation, and regulation of the various steps of carcinogenesis and metastasis development [19]. Thus, ANGPTL proteins have become attractive as either prognostic or predictive biomarkers and a novel target for cancer treatment [20].ANGPTL4 is associated with angiogenesis in numerous cancer types [21, 22]. The roles of ANGPTL4 are mainly attributed to various cellular system targets, such as the microenvironment of the tumor and endothelial cells. ANGPTL4 is involved in cancer growth and progression, angiogenesis, metabolism, and metastasis [13]. ANGPTL4 may have diagnostic and prognostic value for patients with oral squamous cell carcinoma, colorectal cancer hepatic cell cancer, and renal cell carcinoma, and may be a biomarker for targeted therapies [23, 24]. In preclinical models, treatment with an anti‑ANGPTL4 antibody blocks angiogenesis, motility, and metastasis by destroying the tumor‑favorable microenvironment [23]. In addition to angiogenesis, ANGPTL4 is involved in inflammation and energy metabolism depending on the context of the cancer [20, 25]. The co-localization staining results showed that ANGPTL4 were co-localized macrophage and CD31. Angiogenesis and inflammation go hand in hand in various (patho-)physiological conditions. Several studies have highlighted the interconnection between endothelial cells (ECs) and macrophages in these conditions at the level of growth factor and cytokine signaling, yet the importance of metabolism and metabolic signaling has been largely overlooked. Modulating macrophage and/or endothelial functions by interfering with metabolic pathways offers new perspectives for therapeutic strategies [26]. The role of ANGPTL4 on macrophage and endothelial need further study.

Oxygen deprivation or hypoxia is a characteristic environmental stress experienced by cancer cells in solid tumors. To adapt to low oxygen conditions, tumor cells activate the hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF) pathway, which drives the expression of genes that promote angiogenesis, support cell growth and proliferation, and enhance cell migration and tissue invasion [27]. A previous study has shown that HIF-1α expression increases in ONB [28]. Our results showed that HIF-1α was mainly expressed in the interstitial blood vessels of ONB tumors, suggesting that it was involved in angiogenesis. Moreover, we found that ANGPTL4 could co-locate with HIF-1α, suggesting that HIF-1α might be related to ANGPTL4 expression. The production of ANGPTL4 might be hypoxia, in a study about lung cancer, revealed that hypoxia stimulated cells to synthesize extracellular vesicles, and angiogenesis [29]. The mechanism of ANGPTL4 in ONB development might be promotion of angiogenesis and proliferation. ANGPTL4 regulates angiogenesis and vascular permeability, and induces the formation of barely branched and thin endothelial capillaries that display linear adherens junctions [30]. In vivo and in vitro models of other tumors have demonstrated the ability of ANGPTL4 to regulate cell–cell and cell–matrix communication, cell migration, invasion, and metastatic dissemination [31, 32], suggests that ANGPTL4 may have a direct role in promoting tumor progression in ONB cells. In the stroma, it is mainly located in macrophages, our previous studies have shown that macrophages are significantly more abundant in low-grade ONB than in high-grade, we speculate that the expression of ANGPTL4 in stroma of ONB might indirectly promote the progression of ONB by facilitating inflammation. Therefore, further in vitro experiments are needed to verify the role of ANGPTL4 in ONB cells.

This study revealed that ANGPTL4 plays a major role in the malignant progression of ONB, and positive expression of ANGPTL4 is associated with poor DFS but no difference in OS, the reason might by the impact of subsequent treatments after recurrence or the relatively small number of deaths from ONB in the cohort. Cox regression analysis demonstrated that ANGPTL4 is an independent prognostic factor for ONB, suggesting that ANGPTL4 is a novel therapeutic target for ONB. Nevertheless, this study has some limitations. First, although the study included 109 ONB patients, all samples came from a single center, which may have selection bias and affect the generalizability of the results. Future research would consider multi center collaboration to enhance sample representativeness and result reliability. Second, although research has revealed the relationship between ANGPTL4 and ONB prognosis, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying its promotion of tumor progression are not sufficiently explored. In the future, the role of ANGPTL4 in the occurrence and development of ONB should be further elucidated through functional experiments and in vivo and in vitro models. Third, Although the study adjusted some clinical pathological parameters, there may still be unidentified confounding factors, such as the patient’s underlying health status, genetic background, etc., which may affect the results. Future research needs to collect and analyze potential confounding factors more comprehensively. Fourth, the study did not explore the association between ANGPTL4 expression levels and ONB patients’ response to existing treatment options (such as surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy). Future research should analyze whether ANGPTL4 can serve as a biomarker for predicting treatment response. Fifth, Although the study mentioned the relationship between ANGPTL4 and microvessels and macrophages, the impact of tumor microenvironment on ANGPTL4 expression and its role has not been fully evaluated. In the future, advanced technologies such as single-cell sequencing should be combined to deeply analyze the interaction between tumor microenvironment and ANGPTL4. Sixth, the sample size for Western Blot and Immunofluorescence were too small, future study would expanding these analyses to a larger, more diverse subset of samples would strengthen the conclusions regarding protein levels and co-localization.

Although the function of ANGPTL4 has been controversial in various types of cancer, to the best of our knowledge the present study is the first to analyze ANGPTL4 expression in ONB tissues and demonstrate its association with malignant progression and poor prognoses of ONB. The present study suggests a potential carcinogenic and pre‑metastatic role of ANGPTL4, which requires further study.

Impact of adjuvant endocrine therapy on disease-free survival in early triple-positive breast cancer: a multicenter retrospective cohort study of the Turkish oncology group

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTriple-positive breast cancer (TPBC), characterized by concurrent ER, PR, and HER2 positivity, poses unique therapeutic challenges due to ER–HER2 crosstalk. The optimal adjuvant endocrine therapy in TPBC patients receiving anti-HER2 regimens remains poorly defined.MethodsThis multicenter retrospective cohort study included 1044 early-stage TPBC patients (stage II–III) treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy between 2007 and 2024. Disease-free survival (DFS) was analyzed using Kaplan–Meier estimates and Cox regression models, with separate multivariate analyses for premenopausal (n = 474) and postmenopausal (n = 570) patients.ResultsAt a median follow-up of 52 months, the 5-year DFS was 88.2% and the pCR rate was 44.3%. Residual disease was the strongest prognostic factor in both menopausal groups. In premenopausal patients, low ER expression (< 50%; HR 2.202; p = 0.003) and stage III disease (HR 2.722; p < 0.001) were independent adverse prognostic factors. Endocrine therapy type was associated with DFS on unadjusted analysis (log-rank p = 0.021) but was not significant after multivariable adjustment (adjusted overall p = 0.069). Although tamoxifen plus LHRH agonist showed a numerically higher recurrence risk than tamoxifen alone in exploratory subgroup comparison (HR 2.124; 95% CI 1.093–4.129; p = 0.026), this finding should be interpreted with caution given the non-significant overall association. In postmenopausal patients, stage III disease was the only independent adverse factor among baseline clinicopathological variables (HR 2.225; p = 0.003), and endocrine therapy type was not associated with DFS (log-rank p = 0.751; adjusted overall p = 0.614).ConclusionsIn this large real-world TPBC cohort, residual disease was the dominant prognostic factor regardless of menopausal status. Endocrine therapy type was not independently associated with DFS after multivariable adjustment in either menopausal group. These findings are hypothesis-generating and should not guide endocrine therapy selection; prospective studies are warranted in premenopausal TPBC.

Background

Triple-positive breast cancer (TPBC), defined by the concurrent expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor-2 (HER2), represents a heterogeneous subgroup accounting for approximately 10–15% of all breast cancers [1, 2]. The biological complexity of TPBC arises from bidirectional crosstalk between the ER and HER2 signaling pathways. HER2 activation downregulates ER expression and promotes receptor phosphorylation, thereby enhancing endocrine therapy resistance, while ER signaling potentiates the HER2 pathway through PI3K/AKT/MAPK cascades [3, 4]. This molecular interplay distinguishes hormone receptor (HR)-positive/HER2-positive tumors from HR-negative/HER2-positive disease in terms of both biological features and tumor microenvironment, posing unique therapeutic challenges for clinicians [5].

The combination of neoadjuvant chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy has become the standard-of-care approach for locally advanced TPBC. The NeoSphere trial demonstrated that dual HER2 blockade with pertuzumab and trastuzumab significantly improved pathological complete response (pCR) rates [6]. However, pCR rates in the HR-positive subgroup remain consistently lower compared with HR-negative patients, a disparity attributed to ER–HER2 cross-activation conferring relative resistance to chemotherapy combined with HER2-targeted therapy [7]. For patients who fail to achieve pCR, the KATHERINE trial established that adjuvant trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) significantly improved 3-year invasive disease-free survival compared with standard trastuzumab (88.3% vs. 77.0%; HR 0.50; p < 0.001) [8]. Consequently, the selection of optimal endocrine therapy alongside escalated anti-HER2 treatment has emerged as a critical clinical question in the management of TPBC.

Current evidence regarding the prognostic impact of adjuvant endocrine therapy in TPBC yields inconsistent results [9–12]. A post-hoc analysis of the ShortHER trial demonstrated an independent disease-free survival (DFS) benefit for aromatase inhibitor (AI) therapy over tamoxifen, whereas a meta-analysis encompassing the BIG 1–98, ATAC, and TEAM trials failed to detect a significant difference between AI and tamoxifen in the HER2-positive subgroup [9, 10]. The role of ovarian function suppression (OFS) in premenopausal patients is equally debated: an exploratory analysis of the HERA trial showed that the addition of OFS significantly improved 10-year DFS and overall survival (OS), while a HER2-positive subgroup analysis of the SOFT/TEXT trials notably failed to demonstrate the expected superiority of OFS combined with AI over tamoxifen [11, 12]. A key explanation for these discordant findings is that the majority of these studies employed anti-HER2 treatment regimens that fall short of current standards. In trials such as TEAM, ATAC, and BIG 1–98, trastuzumab was administered to only a small proportion of patients, and in the SOFT/TEXT trials, approximately one-third of HER2-positive patients received no anti-HER2 therapy at all [9, 10]. Given the central biological role of the HER2 pathway in TPBC, the true efficacy of endocrine therapy cannot be fully assessed under suboptimal anti-HER2 blockade, and the actual impact of endocrine therapy type on prognosis when more potent anti-HER2 regimens are administered remains to be elucidated.

A fundamental limitation of the existing literature is the low number of HR+/HER2 + patients receiving HER2-targeted therapy and the reliance on observational data for endocrine therapy selection. In this study, we aimed to evaluate the impact of different adjuvant endocrine therapy regimens on DFS, stratified by menopausal status, and to identify independent prognostic factors in 1044 TPBC patients who received standard neoadjuvant chemotherapy and adjuvant HER2-targeted therapy.

Methods

Study design and patient selection

This multicenter retrospective cohort study included 1044 patients diagnosed with early-stage TPBC between July 2007 and November 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) histologically confirmed invasive breast cancer with ER/PR ≥ 1% and HER2-positive status; (2) clinical stage II–III disease; (3) receipt of neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with HER2-targeted therapy; (4) surgical treatment with complete resection; and (5) receipt of adjuvant endocrine therapy. Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) metastatic disease; (2) bilateral breast cancer; (3) inflammatory breast cancer; (4) prior history of breast cancer; and (5) inadequate follow-up duration (< 6 months). Menopausal status was determined based on pre-treatment medical records and defined as amenorrhea for ≥ 12 months from the last menstrual period (for patients aged ≥ 50 years) or a history of bilateral oophorectomy. In patients aged ≤ 50 years with chemotherapy-induced amenorrhea, menopausal status was confirmed by follicle-stimulating hormone and estradiol levels. This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Gaziantep City Hospital (decision no: 96/2024).

Tumor assessment and definitions

HER2 positivity was assessed according to the 2018 American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists (ASCO/CAP) guidelines (immunohistochemistry score 3 + or amplification confirmed by in situ hybridization) [13]. ER and PR positivity were defined as ≥ 1% nuclear staining. ER expression was categorized using a 50% cutoff (< 50% vs. ≥ 50%). The Ki-67 proliferation index was dichotomized at 30%; this threshold was selected based on the median Ki-67 value of the study cohort and published evidence demonstrating its ability to more accurately distinguish the HER2-enriched biological subtype within TPBC. Clinical staging was performed according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition TNM classification [14].

Treatment protocols

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens included: (1) AC-THP (anthracycline–cyclophosphamide followed by taxane–trastuzumab–pertuzumab); (2) AC-TH (anthracycline–cyclophosphamide followed by taxane–trastuzumab); and (3) TCHP (docetaxel–carboplatin–trastuzumab–pertuzumab). Surgical treatment consisted of mastectomy or breast-conserving surgery with axillary lymph node dissection or sentinel lymph node biopsy.

Adjuvant HER2-targeted therapy comprised: (1) trastuzumab monotherapy (total duration of 1 year); (2) trastuzumab plus pertuzumab (total duration of 1 year); or (3) T-DM1 (14 cycles, for patients with residual disease). Adjuvant endocrine therapy options included: (1) tamoxifen; (2) AI (letrozole, anastrozole, or exemestane); (3) tamoxifen–AI sequential therapy; (4) tamoxifen plus luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist (premenopausal patients); and (5) AI plus LHRH agonist (premenopausal patients). Adjuvant radiotherapy was administered to all patients following breast-conserving surgery and to high-risk patients following mastectomy.

Pathological response assessment

Pathological complete response (pCR) was defined as the absence of invasive tumor residue in both the breast and axilla (ypT0/is ypN0). Residual disease was defined as the presence of invasive carcinoma in the surgical specimen.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were reported as median with range or mean ± standard deviation. Associations between categorical variables were evaluated using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Between-group comparisons for continuous variables were performed using Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test. DFS was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and unadjusted comparisons between endocrine therapy groups were performed using the log-rank test. The independent prognostic value of endocrine therapy type was evaluated within multivariable Cox proportional hazards models; for this categorical variable, the overall test is reported as the adjusted overall p-value, with category-specific hazard ratios expressed relative to tamoxifen. Univariate Cox regression was performed, and variables with p < 0.10 were entered into the multivariable models. Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) are reported for all estimates. Statistical significance was set at two-sided p < 0.05. DFS was defined as the time from surgery to any recurrence (local, regional, or distant), contralateral breast cancer, or death from any cause; event-free patients were censored at last follow-up.

Disease-free survival outcomes and univariate analysis

During a median follow-up of 52 months (range, 6–191 months), 122 recurrence events (11.7%) were observed. The 5-year DFS rate for the entire cohort was 88.2%. According to menopausal status, the 5-year DFS was 86.8% in premenopausal and 89.4% in postmenopausal patients (p = 0.124). In univariate analysis, low ER expression (< 50%) (HR 1.649; p = 0.012), advanced clinical T stage (HR 1.564; p = 0.029), advanced clinical N stage (HR 1.687; p = 0.006), stage III disease (HR 2.447; p < 0.001), and failure to achieve pCR (HR 3.465; p < 0.001) were significantly associated with shorter DFS. Compared with the pCR–trastuzumab reference group, patients with residual disease who received adjuvant trastuzumab or trastuzumab plus pertuzumab had a higher risk of recurrence (HR 3.932; p < 0.001), whereas the association did not reach statistical significance in patients with residual disease who received T-DM1 (HR 1.807; p = 0.126). Endocrine therapy type was not significantly associated with DFS (HR 0.755; p = 0.122) (Table 4). Among patients with residual disease and available post-treatment HER2 status, HER2-negative conversion was observed in 118 patients (20.3%). DFS did not differ significantly between patients with and without HER2-negative conversion (log-rank p = 0.956), and baseline characteristics were broadly similar between the two groups.

Discussion

This multicenter retrospective study presents one of the largest real-world cohorts evaluating the impact of adjuvant endocrine therapy selection on disease-free survival, stratified by menopausal status, in 1044 TPBC patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy, of whom 69.8% received pertuzumab-based dual blockade. Our key findings demonstrate that residual disease was the strongest prognostic factor across both menopausal groups, that low ER expression served as an independent adverse prognostic factor in premenopausal patients, and that endocrine therapy type exhibited a borderline significant effect in the premenopausal group while showing no significant impact on DFS in postmenopausal patients.

The pCR rate in our cohort was 44.3%, consistent with a population in which 69.8% of patients received dual HER2 blockade. The pCR rate reported for the HR-positive subgroup in the NeoSphere trial falls below current standards, which is explained by the confirmed benefit of adding pertuzumab to improve pCR rates, as validated by other studies [6, 15, 16]. As demonstrated by the CTNeoBC pooled analysis, pCR serves as a robust surrogate marker for long-term DFS [17].

Residual disease was the strongest prognostic factor in both menopausal groups. Because the reference category (pCR–trastuzumab) comprised patients who achieved pCR, these comparisons are defined by response status rather than by anti-HER2 regimen, and the elevated hazard ratios among patients receiving T-DM1 (HR 1.210 in premenopausal and HR 3.484 in postmenopausal patients) reflect the adverse prognosis of residual disease itself rather than an inferior effect of T-DM1. Consistent with this, patients with residual disease who received trastuzumab or trastuzumab plus pertuzumab also had a markedly higher recurrence risk than the pCR–trastuzumab reference group (HR 4.595; p < 0.001). These findings indicate that the biological burden of residual disease persists despite the iDFS advantage conferred by T-DM1 in the KATHERINE trial [18]. Notably, the low proportion receiving adjuvant T-DM1 (28.2% of those with residual disease) reflects that reimbursement for T-DM1 was not approved in Türkiye until October 2023, meaning that patients treated prior to this date did not have access to this agent. Additional strategies such as extended HER2-targeted therapy or CDK4/6 inhibitor combinations may be required in patients with high residual disease burden. The ExteNET trial demonstrated that neratinib significantly improved iDFS in HR+/HER2 + patients [19], and T-DXd data from the DESTINY-Breast05 trial may transform management in this setting [20].

Low ER expression (< 50%) was identified as an independent adverse prognostic factor in premenopausal patients (HR 2.202; p = 0.003), whereas ER expression did not carry significant prognostic value in postmenopausal patients (p = 0.285). This menopausal status-specific discrepancy supports the hypothesis that ER–HER2 cross-activation may be more pronounced in a high-estrogen environment. As comprehensively reviewed by Tommasi et al., elevated circulating estrogen levels in premenopausal patients may potentiate the HER2 pathway, further limiting endocrine therapy efficacy in tumors with low ER expression [21]. This biological rationale necessitates that endocrine therapy optimization in premenopausal TPBC be approached from a fundamentally different perspective compared with postmenopausal patients.

In premenopausal patients, the significant difference between endocrine therapy groups on unadjusted analysis (log-rank p = 0.021) was attenuated to borderline significance after multivariable adjustment (adjusted overall p = 0.069), indicating that much of the apparent difference is accounted for by differences between the treatment groups rather than by endocrine therapy type itself. Compared with tamoxifen monotherapy, the tamoxifen plus LHRH agonist group had higher recurrence risk (HR 2.124; p = 0.026), while AI plus LHRH agonist did not differ significantly (HR 1.319; p = 0.555). This unexpected finding — that the addition of goserelin appeared to be associated with worse outcomes — can be interpreted from several perspectives. First, although the endocrine therapy groups were well balanced with respect to established adverse prognostic features — clinical stage, ER expression and residual nodal involvement were comparable across groups (Table 3) — they differed significantly in treatment era and anti-HER2 treatment intensity. Patients receiving tamoxifen plus an LHRH agonist less frequently received neoadjuvant dual HER2 blockade (65.0% vs. 76.6% in the tamoxifen group), and the endocrine therapy groups differed in period of diagnosis. Because treatment was not randomized, the worse DFS observed in this group may therefore reflect these imbalances and underlying physician preference rather than a detrimental effect of ovarian function suppression itself. Consistent with this, endocrine therapy type was not significantly associated with DFS when patients were analyzed separately according to pathological response, although these subgroup analyses were limited by the small number of events. Similarly, when the analysis was stratified by period of diagnosis, the difference between endocrine therapy groups was confined to patients diagnosed in 2007–2020 and was no longer apparent among those diagnosed in 2021–2024, the period in which the majority received pertuzumab-based dual blockade, further supporting a treatment-era effect rather than a true detrimental effect of the endocrine regimen. Furthermore, it remains debatable whether OFS provides additional biological benefit in TPBC patients when the HER2 pathway is adequately suppressed by anti-HER2 therapy. In the SOFT/TEXT HER2-positive subgroup, tamoxifen plus OFS was associated with numerically better DFS than AI plus OFS, attributed to complex ER–HER2 cross-interaction dynamics [22]. The EBCTCG meta-analysis similarly shows that AI superiority over tamoxifen with OFS is more limited in HER2-positive patients [23]. Nevertheless, HERA exploratory analysis showed OFS benefit even in trastuzumab-treated premenopausal patients (10-year DFS: 70.9% vs. 59.6%; HR 0.68; p = 0.002), with the AI appearing more beneficial than tamoxifen when combined with OFS [24], and ShortHER analysis demonstrated GnRH agonist-associated DFS superiority (8-year DFS: 85.2% vs. 62.6%; HR 0.41; p = 0.019) with AI plus OFS linked to better prognosis than tamoxifen plus OFS [9]. Most recently, an exploratory 10-year analysis of the ALTTO (BIG 2–06) trial (n = 2651) demonstrated that aromatase inhibitor use was associated with significantly better disease-free survival and time to distant recurrence than tamoxifen in HR-positive/HER2-positive early breast cancer, with benefit observed in both premenopausal and postmenopausal patients [25]. Taken together, these randomized datasets point toward a benefit of endocrine intensification with an aromatase inhibitor and ovarian function suppression, in the opposite direction to the crude association observed in our cohort — a discordance that most likely reflects confounding by indication and treatment era in our observational data rather than a true detrimental effect of ovarian suppression. These heterogeneous findings indicate that endocrine therapy optimization in premenopausal TPBC remains unresolved and requires prospective validation.

The SOFT and TEXT trials demonstrated OFS plus AI superiority over tamoxifen in the general HR-positive premenopausal population [22]; however, small HER2-positive subgroups and substantial proportions without anti-HER2 therapy limit applicability to TPBC. In the meta-analysis by Peleg Hasson et al., no significant DFS difference was detected between AI and tamoxifen in HR+/HER2 + patients, largely attributed to suboptimal anti-HER2 therapy rates [26]. Our study addresses this gap in a cohort in which all patients received neoadjuvant HER2-targeted therapy with response-guided adjuvant treatment, although the proportion receiving pertuzumab-based dual blockade and adjuvant T-DM1 varied over the study period. The ESO-ESMO BCY5 guidelines recommend OFS plus AI for premenopausal high-risk HR-positive patients, with HER2 positivity as a strong risk indicator [27]; however, TPBC-specific prospective validation remains needed.

Endocrine therapy type was not associated with DFS in postmenopausal patients on either unadjusted (log-rank p = 0.751) or multivariable-adjusted analysis (adjusted overall p = 0.614). This finding is consistent with the meta-analysis of the BIG 1–98, ATAC, and TEAM trials, in which no significant DFS difference was found between AI and tamoxifen in the HER2-positive subgroup (HR 1.13; 95% CI 0.75–1.71) [10]. As emphasized by Tommasi et al., the efficacy of AIs through estrogen biosynthesis inhibition may be partially offset in an environment where HER2 signaling is active [21]. In light of these data, AI monotherapy may be considered the standard approach for postmenopausal TPBC.

The role of CDK4/6 inhibitors in TPBC is under active investigation. The PATINA trial demonstrated a significant progression-free survival benefit with the addition of palbociclib to anti-HER2 and endocrine maintenance therapy in HR+/HER2 + advanced/metastatic disease (median PFS 44.3 vs. 29.1 months) [28], and the MonarcHER trial showed a progression-free survival advantage with abemaciclib, trastuzumab, and fulvestrant, also in the metastatic setting [29]. While these data provide a biological rationale for combined CDK4/6, ER, and HER2 blockade, they do not establish benefit in the early-stage setting, where such strategies remain investigational.

Our study strengths include the large sample size (n = 1044), standardized neoadjuvant protocols, response-guided adjuvant anti-HER2 therapy, maintained endocrine therapy adherence, and separately stratified multivariate analyses by menopausal status. Limitations include its retrospective design; in particular, because adjuvant endocrine therapy was not randomized but allocated according to physician preference, residual confounding by indication cannot be excluded despite multivariable adjustment, and the endocrine therapy comparisons should be regarded as hypothesis-generating. Other limitations include the absence of molecular subtyping (e.g., PAM50) and tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) data, and lack of germline BRCA testing. Because the study spanned a long accrual period (2007–2024) during which the use of pertuzumab and adjuvant T-DM1 increased, and because only a minority of patients with residual disease received adjuvant T-DM1 — largely reflecting reimbursement timing in our country — outcomes according to adjuvant anti-HER2 strategy are susceptible to calendar-period effects and drug availability, and should be interpreted with caution. With 122 DFS events overall (62 in premenopausal and 60 in postmenopausal patients), the number of events per variable in the menopausal status-stratified multivariable Cox models was below the conventionally recommended threshold of ten, and the four-level pathological response and adjuvant anti-HER2 therapy variable in particular yielded imprecise estimates with wide confidence intervals owing to small category sizes; the corresponding hazard ratios should therefore be interpreted with caution. HER2-negative conversion (20.3%) was not modeled as a prognostic variable due to limited subgroup size; the prognostic implications of HER2 status conversion warrant future investigation. The moderate median follow-up (52 months) may not fully capture late recurrences, particularly relevant given the late relapse pattern in TPBC.

Conclusions

In this large real-world TPBC cohort receiving anti-HER2 therapy, residual disease remained the dominant prognostic factor irrespective of menopausal status. In premenopausal patients, low ER expression and advanced clinical stage were independent adverse prognostic factors, and endocrine therapy type was not independently associated with DFS after multivariable adjustment in either menopausal group. The borderline difference observed among premenopausal patients on unadjusted analysis is likely attributable to imbalances in treatment allocation and treatment era rather than to a true effect of endocrine therapy type, and our data are therefore hypothesis-generating and should not guide endocrine therapy selection. For premenopausal high-risk patients, randomized evidence — rather than the present observational data — supports endocrine intensification with an aromatase inhibitor and ovarian function suppression, and prospective validation specific to TPBC is warranted. Future studies should investigate endocrine therapy escalation, CDK4/6 inhibitor contribution, and biomarker-guided personalized strategies in TPBC.

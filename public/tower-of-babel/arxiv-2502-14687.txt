A posteriori error bounds for finite element approximations of steady-state mean field games
Yohance A. P. Osborne, Iain Smears, Harry Wells

Abstract
We analyze a posteriori error bounds for stabilized finite element discretizations of second-order steady-state mean field games. We prove the local equivalence between the H 1 H^{1} -norm of the error and the dual norm of the residual. We then derive reliable and efficient estimators for a broad class of stabilized first-order finite element methods. We also show that in the case of affine-preserving stabilizations, the estimator can be further simplified to the standard residual estimator. Numerical experiments illustrate the computational gains in efficiency and accuracy from the estimators in the context of adaptive methods.



Introduction

Mean field games (MFG), which were introduced by Lasry and Lions
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
and independently
by Huang, Caines, and Malhamé
[
22
]
, model Nash equilibria of rational games of stochastic
optimal control in which there are infinitely many players. The equilibria are characterized by a system of partial differential equations (PDE) that consist of a Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation coupled with a Kolmogorov–Fokker–Planck (KFP) equation.
Comprehensive reviews of MFG, including analysis and applications, are well-documented in the literature
[
3
,
19
,
20
]
.
We consider as a model problem a quasilinear elliptic stationary MFG system of the form

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta u+H(x,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=F[m](x)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta m-\Div\left(m\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\left(x,\nabla u\right)\right)

\displaystyle=G(x)

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

along with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
u=0
and
m=0
on
\partial\Omega
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes the state space of the game, which is assumed to be a bounded, connected, polytopal open set with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Here
u
denotes the value function of the underlying optimal control problem faced by the players, and
m
denotes the density of players in the state space of the game.
This model problem can be interpreted as a steady-state solution for a game where the players exit the game upon reaching the boundary, which occurs for instance in applications to traffic flow and evacuation planning.

The numerical analysis of MFG is an active area of research, with early approaches including works on finite difference methods
[
1
,
2
]
and semi-Lagrangian methods
[
12
,
13
]
.
More recently, finite element methods (FEM) have been proposed and analysed for rather general MFG with nondifferentiable Hamiltonians in
[
28
,
29
]
for the steady-state case, and in
[
30
]
, for the time-dependent case.
The first optimal rates of convergence of piecewise-affine FEM were shown in
[
31
]
, for MFG with regular Hamiltonians.
In these works, the FEM are stabilized to enforce a discrete maximum principle.
See also
[
9
]
for an analysis of convergence rates based on a different approach.
So far, existing works have treated only the
a priori
error analysis of numerical methods for MFG, i.e. studying the convergence in the small-mesh limit and error bounds in terms of the unknown solution and the discretization parameters.
In this paper, we focus on
a posteriori
error analysis for MFG, which uses the computed numerical solution in order to obtain computable bounds on the error. We refer the reader to the textbook
[
37
]
for an introduction to
a posteriori
error analysis and its applications.
A key motivation is that
a posteriori
error estimators are essential ingredients in adaptive FEM for guiding the mesh refinements.

To give a summary of our main results, we focus here on MFG systems of the form (
1.1
) where the Hamiltonian
H
is
C^{1,1}
with respect to
p
, where
F
is Lipschitz continuous and strongly monotone on a suitable space, and where the datum
G
is nonnegative in the sense of distributions; see Section
2
below for details.
Under these assumptions, the weak formulation of the MFG system is well-posed with existence and uniqueness of the solution, see Remark
3.1
below for further details.
Concerning the numerical methods, our analysis covers a very broad class of stabilizations, which includes, but is not limited to, the ones from
[
28
,
30
,
31
]
.
Indeed, we treat abstract stabilizations that are only required to be linear with respect to discrete test functions and enforce nonnegativity of the density approximation
m_{\mathcal{T}}
, see Section
4
below. This allows the analysis to cover many of the common stabilization techniques in the literature that enforce a discrete maximum principle, such as upwinding
[
5
,
35
]
, flux-limiting
[
11
]
, edge-averaging
[
38
]
, edge-based nonlinear diffusion
[
6
]
, and algebraic flux correction
[
8
]
.
In particular, our analysis allows for nonlinear stabilizations.
For a given computational mesh
\mathcal{T}
and numerical approximation
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
, where
V(\mathcal{T})
is the
H^{1}_{0}
-conforming P1 FEM space associated to
\mathcal{T}
, we derive an estimator
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
that combines standard residual estimators with some additional terms coming from the stabilization in the FEM.
Our main results, given in Theorems
5.2
and
5.3
, and Corollary
5.4
below, are the first
a posteriori
error bounds for MFG, showing that

\displaystyle\left\lVert u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\lesssim\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\mathrm{oscillation},

(1.3)

where the bound (
1.2
) is subject to the additional condition that
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{*}
for some problem-dependent constant
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{*}>0
.
This smallness condition is due to the nonlinearity and noncoercivity of the problem.
The oscillation terms that are alluded to in (
1.3
) are made precise in Section
5
below; roughly speaking, these terms relate to the approximability properties of the problem data appearing in the HJB and KFP equations.
The bounds (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) show that the estimator is
reliable
and
efficient
, so that, up to data oscillation terms and generic constants, it is equivalent to the error.
Our analysis is founded on original analysis of the MFG system (
1.1
) in the form of a local equivalence result between the
H^{1}
-norm of the error and the
H^{-1}
-norm of the residual, see Theorem
3.1
below.

In this paper, we also contribute towards the wider analysis of error estimators for stabilized FEM.
It is well-known that, even for linear PDE, adding stabilization to a FEM leads to a loss of Galerkin orthogonality of the discrete approximations, which in turn leads to challenges in attempting to localize the estimators. This issue is of wider interest beyond MFG.
In
[
36
]
,
a posteriori
error estimators of residual-type were obtained for several examples of stabilizations, where they showed that in some cases, one can avoid additional terms in the estimators coming from the stabilization thus leading to a final estimator that is the same as for the (unstabilized) Galerkin method.
Note however that the stabilizations treated in
[
36
]
typically do not lead to discrete maximum principles and are thus not considered here.
Nevertheless, this motivates our analysis in Section
6
, where we turn from the very general class of stabilizations of Section
5
to a more restricted class with additional structure, the most important one being
patchwise affine preservation
, see
(H4)
in Section
6
below.
Note that affine-preserving stabilizations have been considered in the literature
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
, many of which are nonlinear stabilizations.
Our key result is that the stabilization-related terms that enter
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
are bounded by the standard jump estimators, see Theorem
6.2
below.
Therefore, the stabilization-related terms can be removed, and the standard residual estimators are reliable and efficient, with the further advantage of being localizable.
The benefits include simplifications in the computation of the error estimators and in the implementation of adaptive mesh-refinements.

We present numerical experiments that support our theoretical results and also test the performance of the estimators when relaxing some of the assumptions used in the analysis, including several examples of challenging MFG for which the exact solution is not known analytically.
The experiments demonstrate the improvements in computational efficiency and accuracy when using the estimators in adaptive computations for problems on nonconvex domains with solutions with limited regularity.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we outline the basic notation of this paper along with the regularity requirements on the MFG system for our estimates.
In Section
3
, we present the equivalence result between residual and error in the continous setting.
The class of stabilized FEMs for the MFG system are formulated in Section
4
. The
a posteriori
error bounds are proven for general stabilizations in Sections
5
.
Section
6
treats the analysis of estimators for affine-preserving stabilizations.
Numerical experiments are presented in Section
7
.

Notation and Setting

Basic notation.

We denote
\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\{1,2,3,\cdots\}
.
For a Lebesgue measurable set
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we let
|\omega|_{d}
denote its Lebesgue measure.
The inner products on
L^{2}(\omega)
and
L^{2}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
are denoted commonly by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\omega}
, with induced norm denoted by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\omega}
; there will be no risk of confusion as the two cases will be distinguished by the arguments.
We equip the space
L^{\infty}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
of bounded matrix-valued functions on
\omega
with the essential supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}
that is induced by the Frobenius norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, connected, open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. We will also require that
\Omega
be polytopal in Section
4
below.
Let
H^{1}(\Omega)
denote the Sobolev space which consists of functions in
L^{2}(\Omega)
that have first-order weak partial derivatives also in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. We let
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
denote the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the space of smooth compactly supported functions on
\Omega
, in the norm on
H^{1}(\Omega)
, and we let
H^{-1}(\Omega)
denote the space of continuous linear functionals on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
The canonical norm on
H^{-1}(\Omega)
is defined by

\left\lVert\Phi\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\langle\Phi,v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}\quad\forall\Phi\in H^{-1}(\Omega),

(2.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}
denotes the duality pairing between
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Notation for inequalities.

In order to avoid the proliferation of generic constants, in the following, we write
a\lesssim b
for real numbers
a
and
b
if
a\leq Cb
for some constant
C
that may depend on the problem data and the shape-regularity parameter of the computational mesh
\mathcal{T}
appearing below, but is otherwise independent of the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, the mesh-size, and the numerical solution. Throughout this work we will regularly specify the particular dependencies of the hidden constants.

Problem data.

We make the following assumptions on the data appearing in (
1.1
).
The diffusion
\nu>0
is assumed to be constant. We assume that the source term
G\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
is of the form
G=g_{0}-\nabla{\cdot}g_{1}
with
g_{0}\in L^{q/2}(\Omega)
and
g_{1}\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
q>d
, and that
G\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
is nonnegative in the distributional sense, i.e.
\langle G,\phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\geq 0
for all functions
\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
that are nonnegative a.e. in
\Omega
.
We suppose that the coupling operator
F
is a Lipschitz continuous operator from some given Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
into
H^{-1}(\Omega)
:

\|F[w]-F[v]\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq L_{F}\|w-v\|_{\mathcal{H}}\quad\forall w,\,v\in\mathcal{H},

(2.2)

for some constant
L_{F}\geq 0
.
We assume here that the space
\mathcal{H}
is such that
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuously and compactly embedded into
\mathcal{H}
. For example,
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\Omega)
is a typical situation that is included in the analysis.
We also assume that
F
is
strongly monotone
with respect to the given Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, at least when
F
is restricted to the smaller space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. In other words, we assume that there exists a constant
c_{F}>0
such that

c_{F}\left\lVert w-v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\langle{F}[w]-{F}[v],w-v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\quad\forall w,v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

(2.3)

Note that it is natural to restrict the arguments of
F
in (
2.3
) to the smaller space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
owing to the fact that
F
has images in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
Several examples of operators
F
that satisfy the above conditions are given in
[
28
, Example 1]
.
Observe that
F
is allowed to be a
nonlocal
operator, thus allowing for applications with global interactions between players of the game via the cost term.
We also remark from the onset that whereas we allow distributional
F
and
G
in the analysis of the continuous problem in Section
3
, we will make stronger assumptions in Sections
5
and
6
when dealing with the discrete estimators, namely that
F
maps into
L^{2}(\Omega)
and that
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, to avoid some unnecessary technicalities.

In applications, the Hamiltonian
H
is usually specified in terms of the controlled drift and running cost of the associated optimal control problem in the MFG. Therefore, we assume that

H(x,p)\coloneqq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\left(b(x,\alpha)\cdot p-f(x,\alpha)\right)\quad\forall(x,p)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.4)

where the set
\mathcal{A}
is the set of controls and is assumed to be a compact metric space, and where both the control-dependent drift
b:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the control-dependent component of the running cost
f:\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
are assumed to be uniformly continuous.
It follows from the above hypotheses that
H
satisfies the following bounds

\displaystyle|H(x,p)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{H}\left(|p|+1\right)

\displaystyle\forall(x,p)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.5a)

\displaystyle|H(x,p)-H(x,q)|

\displaystyle\leq L_{H}|p-q|

\displaystyle\forall(x,p,q)\in\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.5b)

with

C_{H}\coloneqq\max\left\{\|b\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A};\mathbb{R}^{d})},\|f\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A})}\right\}
and
L_{H}\coloneqq\|b\|_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A};\mathbb{R}^{d})}.
Note that
H
is also convex w.r.t.
p
uniformly in
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
We assume that
H
is continuously differentiable w.r.t.
p
with
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
continuous.
The bound (
2.5b
) implies that
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}
is in addition uniformly bounded with

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,p)\right|\leq L_{H}

\displaystyle\forall(x,p)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.6)

We also suppose that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,p)-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,q)\right|\leq L_{H_{p}}|p-q|

\displaystyle\forall(x,p,q)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(2.7)

for some constant
L_{H_{p}}\geq 0
, so
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}
is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t.
p
, uniformly in
x\in\Omega
.
We shall also require some regularity of
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}
w.r.t its
x
-dependence, in particular, we suppose that
x\mapsto\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(x,p)
is in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It is clear that the mappings
H^{1}(\Omega)\ni v\mapsto H(\cdot,\nabla v)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)\ni v\mapsto\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(\cdot,\nabla v)\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
are Lipschitz continuous, and also that
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(\cdot,\nabla v)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
We will often abbreviate these compositions by writing instead
H[\nabla v]\coloneqq H(\cdot,\nabla v)
and
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla v]\coloneqq\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}(\cdot,\nabla v)
a.e. in
\Omega
, for each
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.

In the analysis we will employ the following bound on the linearizations of the Hamiltonians when composed with gradients of functions in Sobolev spaces, c.f.
[
31
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
R>0
, depending only on
\epsilon
,
\Omega
,
L_{H}
and
L_{H_{p}}
, such that

\left\|H[\nabla v]-H[\nabla w]-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla w]\cdot\nabla(v-w)\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq\epsilon\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(2.8)

whenever
v,w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
satisfy
\|v-w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq R
.

In fact, see also
[
34
, Theorem 13]
which gives a major generalization of Lemma
2.1
, in particular by showing the semismoothness in function spaces of HJB operators in the case of Lipschitz but possibly nondifferentiable Hamiltonians.

Continuous problem and equivalence between error and residual

Our first contribution toward deriving computable a posteriori error bounds is to prove the equivalence between norms of the error and of the residuals, detailed in Theorem
3.1
below.
This equivalence is nontrivial due to the lack of coercivity of the underlying PDE, the nonlinear coupling, and possible nondifferentiability of the nonlinear coupling
F[m]
.
To establish this equivalence, we first define the weak form of (
1.1
), which is to find
(u,m)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla u{\cdot}\nabla\psi+H[\nabla u]\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\langle F[m],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}},

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla m{\cdot}\nabla\phi+m\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u]{\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=\langle G,\phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}},

(3.1b)

for all
\psi,\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Remark 3.1

(Existence and uniqueness of the solution)
.

Under the above assumptions on the data, the existence and uniqueness of a solution
(u,m)
of (
3.1
) is known from
[
28
, Theorems 3.3 & 3.4]
, see also
[
29
]
. Note however that if some of these assumptions are removed, then uniqueness of the solution of (
3.1
) does not generally hold, see e.g.
[
32
, Example 3.1]
for an example with multiple solutions where
F
is allowed to be nonmonotone.
Moreover, under the above assumptions, we have the
a priori
bounds
\|m\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|G\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
and
\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim 1+\|G\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{C({\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A}})}
. The density function
m
is in addition nonnegative a.e. in
\Omega
.
Furthermore, recalling the hypothesis that
G=g_{0}-\nabla{\cdot}g_{1}
with
g_{0}\in L^{q/2}(\Omega)
and
g_{1}\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
q>d
.
It then follows that
m\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with

\|m\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq{M_{\infty}}\coloneqq C\left(\|G\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|g_{0}\|_{L^{q/2}(\Omega)}+\|g_{1}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),

(3.2)

for some constant
C
depending only on
d,\Omega,L_{H},\nu
,
q
and
L_{F}
, see
[
18
, Theorem 8.15]
.
We will use (
3.2
) in the analysis that follows.

Local equivalence between error and residual

Our main result in this section is the local equivalence between the norms of the error, when comparing the exact solution with some general
H^{1}_{0}
-conforming approximation, and the dual-norm of the residual.
In particular, we define the following residual operators: let
R_{1},R_{2}:{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
be given by

\displaystyle\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle F[\overline{m}],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}-\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla\overline{u}{\cdot}\nabla\psi+H[\nabla\overline{u}]\psi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.3a)

\displaystyle\langle R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle G,\phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}-\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla\overline{m}{\cdot}\nabla\phi+\overline{m}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]{\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.3b)

for all
\overline{u},\overline{m},\psi,\phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
The total residual is then defined by

\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\coloneqq\left(\left\lVert R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\lVert R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(3.4)

The following result on the local equivalence between errors and residuals is the cornerstone of the
a posteriori
error analysis.

Theorem 3.1

(Local equivalence between error and residual)
.

There exists a constant
\mathcal{R}_{*}>0
such that, for any pair
(\overline{u},\overline{m})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
that satisfies

\overline{m}\geq 0\text{ a.e.\ in }\Omega,\quad\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\leq\mathcal{R}_{*},

(3.5)

then we have the equivalence between errors and residuals

\|u-\overline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|m-\overline{m}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\eqsim\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m}).

(3.6)

We prove Theorem
3.1
in the following subsections.

Remark 3.2
.

It is well-known from the seminal work of Lasry & Lions
[
26
]
that the nonnegativity of the density plays an important role in the proof of uniqueness of the solution of the MFG system in the setting of strictly monotone couplings. It is therefore not surprising that Theorem
3.1
also requires nonnegativity of the density approximations in the first condition in (
3.5
).

Stability of HJB and FP equations

In this section we establish stability results associated with the continuous HJB and KFP equations of the continuous problem (
3.1
).
The following result expresses the continuous dependence of the residuals on the errors.

Lemma 3.2
.

For all
\overline{u},\overline{m}\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
, we have

\displaystyle\|R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m-\overline{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|u-\overline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\|R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|m-\overline{m}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+{\|u-\overline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}},

(3.7b)

where the hidden constants depend only on
\Omega
,
L_{F}
,
L_{H_{p}}
,
L_{H}
,
\nu
, and
M_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Since
(u,m)
solves (
3.1
), we have for all
\psi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}

\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}=\langle F[\overline{m}]-F[m],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\\
+\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla(u-\overline{u}){\cdot}\nabla\psi+(H[\nabla u]-H[\nabla\overline{u}])\psi\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.8)

The bound (
3.7a
) then follows from the Lipschitz continuity of
F
in (
2.2
) and the Lipschitz continuity of
H
in (
2.5b
).
Next, we have

\langle R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times{H_{0}^{1}}}=\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla(m-\overline{m}){\cdot}\nabla\phi+(m-\overline{m})\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]{\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\\
+\int_{\Omega}m\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u]-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]\right){\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.9)

for all
\phi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
.
Then, using the bound
\left\lVert\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L_{H}
which follows from (
2.6
), we see that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\\
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\Omega}(m-\overline{m})\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]{\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\right]\leq L_{H}\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{\Omega}.

(3.10)

The Lipschitz continuity of
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}
in (
2.7
) and the
L^{\infty}
-bound on

in (

) imply that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\\
\|\nabla\phi\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\Omega}m\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u]-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]\right)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\right]\leq L_{H_{p}}M_{\infty}\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(3.11)

Combining (
3.9
) with (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we then obtain (
3.7b
).
∎

We now turn to the (local) stability of the continuous HJB equation (

).

Lemma 3.3

(Stability of continous HJB equation)

There exists a constant

(3.12)

for any
\overline{m}\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
, and for any

satisfying

.
The hidden constant in (

\Omega
,
d
,
\nu
,
L_{H}
and
L_{F}
.

Proof.

Since
\left\lVert\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L_{H}
, it follows from
[
28
, Lemma 4.5]
that

\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\\
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla(u-\overline{u}){\cdot}\nabla\psi+\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]{\cdot}\nabla(u-\overline{u})\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\right],

(3.13)

where the hidden constant depends only on
\Omega
,
d
,
\nu
and
L_{H}
.
Using the continuous HJB equation of (
3.1a
), we find that

\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\\
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left[\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times{H_{0}^{1}}}+\langle F[m]-F[\overline{m}],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\right.\\
\left.-\int_{\Omega}\left(H[\nabla u]-H[\nabla\overline{u}]-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]{\cdot}\nabla(u-\overline{u})\right)\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\right].

(3.14)

Hence, the Lipschitz continuity of

, c.f. (

\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+L_{F}\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\\
+\left\lVert H[\nabla u]-H[\nabla\overline{u}]-\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]{\cdot}\nabla(u-\overline{u})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

Next, we apply Lemma

We now consider the stability of the continuous KFP equation (

Lemma 3.4

(Stability of continuous KFP equation)
.

For all
\overline{u},\overline{m}\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
, we have

\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|u-\overline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(3.16)

where the constant depends only on
\Omega
,
d
,
\nu
,
L_{H}
,
L_{H_{p}}
and on

.

Proof.

Since
\left\lVert\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\overline{u}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L_{H}
, it follows from
[
28
, Lemma 4.5]
that

\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\\

The next result concerns the
\mathcal{H}
-norm stability in the density component for the continuous MFG system (
3.1
), when restricted to the set of nonnegative functions in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
.
This bound can be seen as a quantitative analogue to some of the inequalities used in the well-known proofs of uniqueness of solutions due to
[
26
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\overline{u},\overline{m}\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
such that
\overline{m}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, we have

c_{F}\|m-\overline{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{m}-m\rangle_{H^{-1}\times{H_{0}^{1}}}-\langle R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{u}-u\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}.

(3.19)

Proof.

Let
\overline{u},\overline{m}\in{H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}
with
\overline{m}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
be fixed but arbitrary.
Using the continuous HJB equation (
3.1a
) and the continuous KFP equation (
3.1b
), we find after some elementary computation that

\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{m}-m\rangle_{H^{-1}\times{H_{0}^{1}}}-\langle R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{u}-u\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\\
=\langle F[\overline{m}]-F[m],\overline{m}-m\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}+(m,R_{H}[\nabla\overline{u},\nabla u])_{\Omega}+(\overline{m},R_{H}[\nabla u,\nabla\overline{u}])_{\Omega},

(3.20)

where

for any
v,\,w\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
and where we recall that
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Omega}
denotes the inner product on

Since

is convex and differentiable w.r.t.

, we have

a.e. in
\Omega
, for any
v,\,w
. Also, we have
m\geq 0
in
\Omega
by the comparison principle. Therefore, the terms

and

\displaystyle\langle F[\overline{m}]-F[m],\overline{m}-m\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\leq\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{m}-m\rangle_{H^{-1}\times{H_{0}^{1}}}-\langle R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{u}-u\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}.

(3.21)

The desired result (
3.19

3.21
) and from the strong monotonicity of
F
in (
2.3
).

Proof of Theorem
3.1

In this section we prove the following result which shows the continuous dependence of the error on the residual of the problem.

Lemma 3.6
.

For each
\delta>0
, there exists a
\mathcal{R}_{*}>0
such that

\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\leq\mathcal{R}_{*}\implies\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}<\delta,

(3.22)

for all
(\overline{u},\overline{m})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The main idea of the proof is to show that if the claim were false, then this would contradict the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.1
).
Under the hypothesis that the claim is false, there exists a
\delta>0
and a sequence of pairs
(u_{k},m_{k})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\mathcal{R}(u_{k},m_{k})\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, but
\left\lVert u-u_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert m-m_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\geq\delta
.

First, we claim that the sequence is uniformly bounded in the
H^{1}
-norm, i.e. that

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\left\lVert m_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right]<\infty.

(3.23)

Indeed, since
\left\lVert\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{k}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L_{H}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we first apply
[
28
, Lemma 4.5]
and the hypothesis that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert R_{2}(u_{k},m_{k})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}=0
to obtain

\begin{split}\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert m_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}&\lesssim\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\
\left\lVert\nabla\phi\right\rVert_{\Omega}=1\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla m_{k}{\cdot}\nabla\phi+m_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{k}]{\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\right]\\
&=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert G-R_{2}(u_{k},m_{k})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

(3.24)

Next, recalling (
2.4
) and the fact that
\mathcal{A}
is compact, with the functions
b
and
f
continuous on
\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A}
, we use the measurable selection theorem
[
34
, Theorem 10]
to find that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a Lebesgue measurable map
\alpha_{k}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
such that

\alpha_{k}(x)\in\argmax_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\left[b(x,\alpha){\cdot}\nabla u_{k}(x)-f(x,\alpha)\right]\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Defining
b_{k}\coloneqq b(\cdot,\alpha_{k}(\cdot))
and
f_{k}\coloneqq f(\cdot,\alpha_{k}(\cdot))
, we then have
H[\nabla u_{k}]=b_{k}\cdot\nabla u_{k}-f_{k}
in
\Omega
. This implies that each
u_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla u_{k}{\cdot}\nabla\psi+b_{k}{\cdot}\nabla u_{k}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x=\langle F[m_{k}],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}-\langle R_{1}(u_{k},m_{k}),\psi\rangle+\int_{\Omega}f_{k}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.25)

for all
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Therefore,
[
28
, Lemma 4.5]
applied to (
3.25
) shows that

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert u_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim 1+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathcal{A})}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)},

(3.26)

where we have used here the hypothesis that
\left\lVert R_{1}(u_{k},m_{k})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, and also the Lipschitz continuity of
F
, c.f. (
2.2
), the embedding of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
\mathcal{H}
, and the above bound (
3.24
) to bound
\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert F[m_{k}]\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\lesssim 1+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
.
This completes the proof of (
3.23
).

The uniform boundedness of the sequence (
3.23
) and the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem then imply that there exists a
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{m})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and a subsequence, to which we pass without change of notation, such that
m_{k}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{m}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{k}\rightharpoonup\widetilde{u}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, where the convergence of the sequences is additionally strong in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, after possibly passing to a further subsequence, we can assume
m_{k}\rightarrow\widetilde{m}
in
\mathcal{H}
without loss of generality, since
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
\mathcal{H}
by hypothesis.
This implies that
F[m_{k}]\rightarrow F[\widetilde{m}]
in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
by continuity of
F
from
\mathcal{H}
to
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
After possibly passing to a further subsequence, we can also assume without loss of generality that
h_{k}\coloneqq H[\nabla u_{k}]\rightharpoonup\tilde{h}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, for some
\tilde{h}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then, it follows from
\mathcal{R}(u_{k},m_{k})\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
that

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle R_{1}(u_{k},m_{k}),\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=\langle F[\widetilde{m}],\psi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}-\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla\widetilde{u}{\cdot}\nabla\psi+\tilde{h}\psi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.27)

for all
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Using weak-times-strong convergence and
R_{1}(u_{k},m_{k})\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu\left\lVert\nabla u_{k}\right\rVert_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left[\langle F[m_{k}],u_{k}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}-\int_{\Omega}h_{k}u_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x-\langle R_{1}(u_{k},m_{k}),u_{k}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}\right]

(3.28)

\displaystyle=\langle F[\widetilde{m}],\widetilde{u}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{h}\widetilde{u}\,\mathrm{d}x=\nu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{\Omega}^{2},

where the last equality follows from (
3.27
) when testing with
\widetilde{u}
.
This implies the strong convergence
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{k}
in the
H^{1}
-norm as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
In turn, this implies that
\tilde{h}=H[\nabla\widetilde{u}]
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
by Lipschitz continuity of
H
. Combined with (
3.27
), this implies that
R_{1}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{m})=0
.
Moreover, we find that
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{k}]\rightarrow\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\widetilde{u}]
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
by Lipschitz continuity of
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\langle R_{2}(u_{k},m_{k}),\phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=\langle G,\phi\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla\widetilde{m}{\cdot}\nabla\phi+\widetilde{m}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\widetilde{u}]{\cdot}\nabla\phi\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3.29)

for all
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Hence
R_{2}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{m})=0
, and thus
\mathcal{R}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{m})=0
, i.e. the pair
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{m})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
).
Using the uniform
L^{\infty}
-norm bound
\left\lVert\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{k}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq L_{H}
, we also deduce the weak convergence
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{k}]{\cdot}\nabla m_{k}\rightharpoonup\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\widetilde{u}]{\cdot}\nabla\widetilde{m}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and thus weak-times-strong convergence gives

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\nu\left\lVert\nabla m_{k}\right\rVert_{\Omega}^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left[\langle G-R_{2}(u_{k},m_{k}),m_{k}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}-\int_{\Omega}m_{k}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{k}]{\cdot}\nabla m_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]

(3.30)

\displaystyle=\langle G,\widetilde{m}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}-\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{m}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla\widetilde{u}]{\cdot}\nabla\widetilde{m}\,\mathrm{d}x=\nu\left\lVert\nabla\widetilde{m}\right\rVert_{\Omega}^{2},

where the last identity follows from (
3.29
) when testing with
\widetilde{m}
.
This implies the strong convergence
m_{k}\rightarrow m
in the
H^{1}
-norm as
k\rightarrow\infty
.
Thus, we conclude that
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{m})
is a weak solution of (
1.1
), yet

\left\lVert m-\widetilde{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left[\left\lVert m-m_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-u_{k}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right]\geq\delta>0.

This contradicts the uniqueness of the solution of (
1.1
), see Remark
3.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

First, note that the continuity bounds of Lemma
3.2
imply the lower bound

\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\lesssim\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
, where we are using here the continuous embedding of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
\mathcal{H}
so that
\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\lesssim\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
.
Therefore, it remains only to show the upper bound, namely that

\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m}).

(3.31)

Let
R>0
be the constant given in Lemma
3.3
.
Then, Lemma
3.6
implies that there exists a
\mathcal{R}_{*}>0
such that
\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq R
for any
(\overline{u},\overline{m})\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
that satisfy
\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})<\mathcal{R}_{*}
.
Suppose now that we are given
(\overline{u},\overline{m})\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\overline{m}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})<\mathcal{R}_{*}
.
We use Lemma
3.4
to get

\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|u-\overline{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(3.32)

Since
\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq R
, we then apply Lemma
3.3
to the last term on the right-hand side of (
3.32
) above to find that

\displaystyle\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m})\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\|m-\overline{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}}

(3.33)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})+\|m-\overline{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Then, since by hypothesis
\overline{m}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
with
\overline{m}\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, we use the bound (
3.19
) (after taking square-roots) from Lemma
3.5
, which implies that

\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\lesssim\left(\langle R_{1}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{m}-m\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}-\langle R_{2}(\overline{u},\overline{m}),\overline{u}-u\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H_{0}^{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

(3.34)

3.33
) with (
3.34
) gives

\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\\
\lesssim\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})+\mathcal{R}(\overline{u},\overline{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert m-\overline{m}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-\overline{u}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

for any
\epsilon>0
, where the last inequality follows from Young’s inequality with a parameter.
After choosing

) into the left-hand side, which finally gives (
3.31
) and thus completes the proof.

Stabilized finite element discretizations

Setting and notation

The mesh.

Let
\mathcal{T}
denote a conforming simplicial mesh of the domain
\Omega
. We adopt the convention that each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
is closed.
For each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
, we let
h_{K}
denote the diameter of
K
. We define the mesh-size function
h_{\mathcal{T}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
by
h_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K}\coloneqq h_{K}
for each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
. In the following, the notation for inequalities
a\lesssim b
will allow the hidden constant to depend on the shape-regularity parameter of
\mathcal{T}
, defined as
\theta_{\mathcal{T}}\coloneqq\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\frac{h_{K}}{\rho_{K}}
, where
\rho_{K}
denotes the diameter of the largest inscribed balls in
K
. However, the hidden constants will be otherwise independent of the size of the mesh elements.
Two distinct elements of
\mathcal{T}
are called neighbours if they have non-empty intersection.
For each
K\in\mathcal{T}
, we let
\mathcal{T}_{K}
denote the set of neighbouring elements of
K
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
denote the set of all vertices of
\mathcal{T}
and let
\mathcal{V}^{I}=\mathcal{V}\cap\Omega
denote the set of interior vertices of
\mathcal{T}
.
Two distinct vertices are called neighbours if they belong to a common element of
\mathcal{T}
.
For each vertex

\mathcal{T}_{z}

\mathcal{T}
that contain
z

\omega_{z}\coloneqq\bigcup\{K^{\prime}\colon K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{z}\}

\omega_{z}
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all faces of
\mathcal{T}
, and let
\mathcal{F}^{I}
denote the subset of all interior faces of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e. all faces that are not contained in
\partial\Omega
.
For each face
F\in\mathcal{F}
, let
h_{F}\coloneqq\diam F

F
.
For each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
, we let
\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}
denote the set of interior faces
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
that are contained in
K
.
For each face
F\in\mathcal{F}
, we let
\mathcal{T}_{F}
denote the set of elements that contain
F

\omega_{F}\coloneqq\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}}K

For each element
K\in\mathcal{T}

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
of face-sharing neighbouring elements and the associated patch

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}\coloneqq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}}\mathcal{T}_{F},

\displaystyle\omega_{K}\coloneqq\bigcup_{K^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}K^{\prime}=\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}}\omega_{F}.

(4.1)

Note that
K\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}
for all
K\in\mathcal{T}
.
For each vertex

, we let
\mathcal{F}^{I}_{z}

z
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the set of edges of the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, i.e. the set of all closed line segments formed by all pairs of neighbouring vertices. Note that for

containing

Finite element space.

The numerical method is computed using a linear finite element space
V(\mathcal{T})\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
defined by

\displaystyle V(\mathcal{T})\coloneqq\left\{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\colon v_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}\right\},

(4.2)

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(K)
denotes the space of real-valued affine functions on
K
, i.e. polynomials of degree at most
1
.
Let
\{\psi_{z}\}_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}
denote the standard Lagrange nodal basis for
V(\mathcal{T})
, which is characterized by the conditions
\psi_{z}(z)=1
and
\psi_{z}(z^{\prime})=0
for all
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\{z\}
, for all
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
.

Quasi-interpolation operator.

Let
I_{\mathcal{T}}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow V(\mathcal{T})
denote a quasi-interpolation operator that satisfies the following properties:
I_{\mathcal{T}}v_{\mathcal{T}}=v_{\mathcal{T}}
for all
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
, and

\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}h_{K}^{2(k-1)}\left\lVert\nabla^{k}(v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v)\right\rVert_{K}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\;k\in\{0,1\},

(4.3)

where the hidden constant depends only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}
.
Note that (
4.3
) implies that
\left\lVert v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\right\rVert_{\Omega}\lesssim h_{\mathcal{T}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
and
\left\lVert I_{\mathcal{T}}v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
For instance, one may consider the Scott–Zhang quasi-interpolation operator
[
33
]
where (
4.3

[
33

.
Note that the trace inequality for element faces
[
27

then implies that

\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}h_{F}^{-1}\left\lVert v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(4.4)

Family of numerical schemes

In order to satisfy the nonnegativity of the density approximation, which is one of the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
, it is often necessary to include some stabilization in the construction of the numerical discretization.
To allow for a wide range of possible choices of stabilizations, we develop the
a posteriori
analysis for an abstract stabilized FEM discretization of the general form: find
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in V(\mathcal{T})\times V(\mathcal{T})
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}+H[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]v_{\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{d}x+S_{1}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})=\langle F[m_{\mathcal{T}}],v_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},

(4.5a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\nabla w_{\mathcal{T}}+m_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\cdot\nabla w_{\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{d}x+S_{2}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};w_{\mathcal{T}})=\langle G,w_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},

(4.5b)

for all discrete test functions
v_{\mathcal{T}},\;w_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
.
In (
4.5
) above, the terms
S_{i}(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot):[V(\mathcal{T})]^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, represent general stabilization terms, see below.

Assumptions on the stabilizations.

In order to develop the
a posteriori
analysis for as wide a range of stabilizations as possible, we consider abstract stabilizations that satisfy some structural conditions. See however Section
4.3
below for some concrete examples.
We will assume throughout this work that the forms
S_{i}(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot)
are linear in the third argument, i.e. for each
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have
S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};\lambda v_{\mathcal{T}}+w_{\mathcal{T}})=\lambda S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})+S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};w_{\mathcal{T}})
for all
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{4}
, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Note that this allows the stabilizations to be nonlinear with respect to
u_{\mathcal{T}}
and
m_{\mathcal{T}}
.
The main assumption for the
a posteriori
error analysis is that the stabilizations must be chosen to ensure the nonnegativity of the density approximation.
In other words, we require that

(H1)

any solution
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
of the numerical scheme (
4.5
) satisfies
m_{\mathcal{T}}\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

Recall that nonnegativity of the density approximation is the natural discrete counterpart of the nonnegativity of the exact density
m
, see Remark
3.1
.
We present various examples of possible stabilizations in Section
4.3
below.
In Section
5
, we show general
a posteriori
error bounds assuming only that the stabilizations satisfy linearity in the third argument and the assumption
(H1)
.

Remark 4.1
.

In practice, stabilizations are often designed to satisfy stronger conditions with the purpose of ensuring, amongst other things, existence and uniqueness of a numerical solution
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
and good
a priori
convergence properties, c.f.
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
However, in this work, we aim for rather minimal conditions on the stabilization to enable the
a posteriori
error analysis.
The assumptions made above on the stabilization, including
(H1)
do not appear to be generally sufficient for proving the existence or uniqueness of a numerical solution.
Therefore, in the following, we will assume the existence of a numerical solution
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
. The uniqueness of the numerical solution is however not required for the
a posteriori
analysis.

Examples

In order to illustrate some particular choices of the stabilizations
S_{i}(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot)
from (
4.5
) that we have in mind, in this section we summarize the stabilization methods that were considered in the earlier works
[
28
,
30
,
31
]
on MFG.
In particular, these works considered linear stabilizations that can be written in the following general form

\displaystyle S_{1}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}D_{\mathcal{T}}\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle S_{2}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})=\int_{\Omega}D_{\mathcal{T}}\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\mathrm{d}x,

(4.6)

for all
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{T}})\in[V(\mathcal{T})]^{3}
, for some suitably chosen matrix
D_{\mathcal{T}}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\right)
with
D_{\mathcal{T}}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym},+}
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, where
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym},+}
denotes the set of symmetric positive semidefinite matrices in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Note that the stabilizations in (
4.6

S_{1}
for the HJB equation is independent of the density approximation, and likewise the stabilization
S_{2}
for the KFP equation is independent of the value function.
In
[
30
,
31
]

was defined elementwise to be of the form

D_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K}\coloneqq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{K}^{I}}\gamma_{\mathcal{T},E}\,t_{E}t_{E}^{T}\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T},

(4.7)

where

denotes a fixed choice of unit tangent vector to

In particular, it is shown in

The earlier work
[

considered the simpler choice where
D_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K}\coloneqq\gamma_{\mathcal{T},K}\mathbb{I}_{d}

is a chosen constant parameter for each

It is known if that if the mesh is strictly acute, and if the parameter

[

, then a discrete maximum principle/comparison principle is again also available so that Assumption
(H1)
also holds, see
[

The stabilizations considered above are by no means the only ones that are admissible for the purposes of

A posteriori error bounds for general stabilizations

Decomposition of the residuals

The starting point for the derivation of
a posteriori
error estimators for the approximations are some general abstract bounds on the residuals.
We initially adopt a similar approach as in
[
36
]
.
Recall that the residual operators
R_{i}
are defined in (
3.3
) above.
Supposing that
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
solves (
4.5
),
we start by splitting the residuals
R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
between one component that will lead to standard residual estimators, and another component that comprises the effect of stabilization.
To do so, for each
i\in\{1,2\}
and each
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have, for any
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
,

\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v\rangle=\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}+\langle R_{i}(m_{\mathcal{T}},u_{\mathcal{T}}),I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}},

(5.1)

where
I_{\mathcal{T}}
was the quasi-interpolation operator considered above.
Note that the definition of
R_{i}
implies that if
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
is a solution of the numerical scheme (
4.5
), then the final term on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\langle R_{i}(m_{\mathcal{T}},u_{\mathcal{T}}),I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};I_{\mathcal{T}}v)

\displaystyle\forall i\in\{1,2\}.

(5.2)

Therefore, we have the following decomposition of the residual into two key components: for any solution
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
of (
4.5
),

\left\lVert R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq\sup_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}\\
+\sup_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};I_{\mathcal{T}}v)}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}.

(5.3)

We will see shortly below that the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.3
) will lead towards the well-known residual estimators, whereas the second term will lead to the stabilization estimators.

Residual and stabilization estimators

Residual estimators.

The derivation of computable estimators for the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.3
) is quite standard by now, and there is a range of possible approaches.
For the sake of simplicity and illustration, we present here the classical residual estimators following a standard approach, see for instance the textbook
[
37
]
.
In the current setting, the classical definition of residual estimators require that
F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, so we make the assumption that
F\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
throughout the following analysis. We also assume that
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
However, there are approaches to constructing
a posteriori
estimators for distributional right-hand sides, see e.g.
[
16
,
23
]
.

For a given solution
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
of (
4.5
), we start by defining the volume residual terms
r_{K,i}\in L^{2}(K)
,
K\in\mathcal{T}
, by

\displaystyle r_{K,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]|_{K}+\nu\Delta(u_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K})-H[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]|_{K},

(5.4a)

\displaystyle r_{K,2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq G|_{K}+\nu\Delta(m_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K})+\Div\left.\left(m_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\right)\right|_{K}.

(5.4b)

Note that the dependence of
r_{K,i}
on
(u_{T},m_{\mathcal{T}})
is left implicit to alleviate the notation.
Also, since functions in
V(\mathcal{T})
are piecewise linear, the contributions from the piecewise Laplacians
\Delta(u_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K})
and
\Delta(m_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K})
vanish identically; nevertheless, we write them explicitly in (
5.4
) to highlight the conceptual meaning of the volume residual terms.
Observe also that since
\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}
is piecewise constant with respect to the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, it follows from the hypotheses on
H
that, for each
K\in\mathcal{T}
, the map
x\mapsto\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}](x)
is
H^{1}
-regular on
K
. This ensures that
r_{K,2}\in L^{2}(K)
for each
K\in\mathcal{T}
.

Next, for each interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
, we define the jump residual terms
j_{F,i}\in L^{2}(F)
by

\displaystyle j_{F,1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nu\left\llbracket\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket,

(5.5a)

\displaystyle j_{F,2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\nu\left\llbracket\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket+m_{\mathcal{T}}|_{F}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket.

(5.5b)

Again, we leave the dependence of the jump residual on
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
implicit.
In the notation above, the vector
n_{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denotes an arbitrary choice of unit normal to the face
F
, and the jump operator
\left\llbracket\cdot\right\rrbracket
on
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
is defined by
\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket\coloneqq\tau_{F}^{-}w-\tau_{F}^{+}w
for all
w\in BV(\Omega)
, where
\tau_{F}^{-}
, respectively
\tau_{F}^{+}
, denotes the trace operator from the side of
F
for which
n_{F}
is
outward
pointing, respectively
inward
pointing. Note that with this definition, the jump residual terms defined in (
5.5
) are invariant with respect to the choice of normal vector
n_{F}
, since reversing the orientation of
n_{F}
also reverses the sign of the jump operator.
Note that since
\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}
is piecewise constant, it follows from the hypotheses on the problem data that
x\mapsto\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}](x)
is
H^{1}
-regular on each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
, and thus has well-defined one-sided traces in
L^{2}(F)
on each face
F
. This ensures that
j_{F,2}
is well-defined and is in
L^{2}(F)
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
.
Note also that in (
5.5
) the term
m_{\mathcal{T}}|_{F}
appears outside the jump operator owing to the continuity of
m_{\mathcal{T}}
.

With the residual and jump terms defined above, we define the elementwise residual estimator
\eta_{K,i}
,
K\in\mathcal{T}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, by

\left[\eta_{K,i}\right]^{2}\coloneqq h_{K}^{2}\left\lVert r_{K,i}\right\rVert_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}}h_{F}\left\lVert j_{F,i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}.

(5.6)

The
residual estimators
, denoted by
\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
, are then defined by

\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}\coloneqq\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}[\eta_{K,i}]^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad i\in\{1,2\}.

(5.7)

Remark 5.1

(Alternative estimators)
.

Note that one could also consider the numerous alternatives to the classical residual estimators, many of which have certain theoretical and/or practical advantages, especially in the case of distributional data
[
16
,
23
]
.
In practice, it is also desirable to employ
a posteriori
estimators that control the linearization and algebraic errors committed in solving the discretized problems, see
[
17
]
; however a detailed analysis for this goes beyond the scope of this work.

The next theorem states the well-known upper bound for the residuals with the above estimators.

Theorem 5.1

(Upper bound on the residuals)
.

Suppose that
F\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then, for any
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
, we have

\sup_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}\lesssim\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}\quad\forall i\in\{1,2\}.

(5.8)

Proof.

The essential techniques of the proof are already well-known, see for instance
[
37
, Theorem 3.58, p. 135]
, so we only sketch the main steps.
For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, observe that the residual term
\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle
vanishes for any test function
v
that belongs to the discrete space
V(\mathcal{T})
.
In other words, the space
V(\mathcal{T})
belongs to the kernel of the linear functional
(I-I_{\mathcal{T}}^{*})R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
where
I_{\mathcal{T}}^{*}\colon H^{-1}(\Omega)\subset V(\mathcal{T})^{*}\rightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
denotes the formal adjoint of
I_{\mathcal{T}}
.
Furthermore, for any
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, using integration by parts over the elements of
\mathcal{T}
, we obtain the identity

\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{K}r_{K,i}(v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v)\mathrm{d}x-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}}\int_{F}j_{F,i}(v-I_{\mathcal{T}}v)\mathrm{d}s.

(5.9)

We then obtain (
5.8
) by applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to (
5.9
), and applying the approximation properties of
I_{\mathcal{T}}
from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
).
∎

Stabilization estimators.

We now turn to bounding the final term in (
5.3
).
Our approach here differs from the one in
[
36
]
in order to handle the very broad class of abstract stabilizations considered above.
Note that, for any
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
,

\begin{split}\sup_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};I_{\mathcal{T}}v)}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}&=\sup_{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\lVert\nabla I_{\mathcal{T}}v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}{\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}\frac{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};I_{\mathcal{T}}v)}{\left\lVert\nabla I_{\mathcal{T}}v\right\rVert_{\Omega}}\\
&\lesssim\sup_{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})}{\left\lVert\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{\Omega}},\end{split}

(5.10)

where in passing to the final inequality above, we have used (
4.3
), which entails the
H^{1}
-stability of
I_{\mathcal{T}}
, and we have also used the Poincaré inequality.
This leads us to consider the
stabilization estimators

\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}\coloneqq\sup_{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})}{\left\lVert\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{\Omega}},\quad i\in\{1,2\}.

(5.11)

As above, we leave implicit the dependence of
\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}
on
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
in order to alleviate the notation.
Observe that if
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
solves (
4.5
), then
S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})=\langle R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}),v_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}
for all
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
.
Thus the stabilization estimators
\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}
satisfy the global efficiency bound

\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}\lesssim\left\lVert R_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(5.12)

for each
i\in\{1,2\}
.
However, in the case of general stabilizations, it is not known how to localize
\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}
whilst retaining local efficiency.
This motivates the analysis in Section
6
for the case of affine-preserving stabilizations.

Remark 5.2

(Practical computation of stabilization estimators)
.

Observe that the stabilization estimator is a dual norm of the stabilization acting over the space of discrete test functions, so it is a computable quantity.
In practice, it is often cheaper computationally to use well-known preconditioners for elliptic problems that lead to approximations
\widetilde{\eta}_{\mathrm{stab},i}\approx\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}
satisfying the equivalence

\gamma\,\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}\leq\widetilde{\eta}_{\mathrm{stab},i}\leq\Gamma\,\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}\quad\forall i\in\{1,2\},

(5.13)

for some fixed positive constants
\gamma
,
\Gamma
that do not depend on the mesh-size of
\mathcal{T}
.
Indeed, consider a positive definite symmetric preconditioner
P_{\mathcal{T}}\colon V(\mathcal{T})\rightarrow V(\mathcal{T})^{*}
for the discrete Laplacian on
V(\mathcal{T})
, such that the action of
P_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\colon V(\mathcal{T})^{*}\rightarrow V(\mathcal{T})
is easily computable (e.g. a single multigrid V-cycle), and such that
P_{\mathcal{T}}
satisfies the spectral equivalence property: for some fixed
\gamma
,
\Gamma>0
independent of
\mathcal{T}
,
\gamma\left\lVert v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{P_{\mathcal{T}}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{\Omega}\leq\Gamma\left\lVert v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{P_{\mathcal{T}}}
for all
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
, where the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{P_{\mathcal{T}}}\coloneqq\sqrt{\langle P_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{V(\mathcal{T})^{*}\times V(\mathcal{T})}}
.
Then, we compute

\displaystyle w_{\mathcal{T},i}\coloneqq P_{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};\cdot)\in V(\mathcal{T}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{\mathrm{stab},i}\coloneqq\sqrt{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};w_{\mathcal{T},i})}=\left\lVert w_{\mathcal{T},i}\right\rVert_{P_{\mathcal{T}}}.

(5.14)

It follows from the spectral equivalence property of
P_{\mathcal{T}}
that
\widetilde{\eta}_{\mathrm{stab},i}
satisfies the equivalence (
5.13
).
Thus, up to the constants in (
5.13
), one can often approximate
\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}
at a smaller computation cost.
In the following analysis we shall focus on the discussion the stabilization estimators
\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}
defined in (
5.11
), yet we note that these can be substituted for any equivalent approximation throughout this work with identical results up to the constants.
For an introduction to preconditioners in the context of FEM, including various possible choices for
P_{\mathcal{T}}
, we refer the reader to the textbook
[
15
]
.
Note also that in Section
6
below, we show that the computation (or approximation) of the stabilization estimators can be avoided in the case of affine-preserving stabilizations.

Error bounds for general stabilizations

We now define the total estimator

\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}+\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}\right].

(5.15)

In Theorem
5.2
below we give the main upper bound on the error in the general case where
(H1)
holds.
This result shows that, provided that the total estimator
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
is sufficiently small, then the estimator gives an upper bound on the error between the exact solution and its numerical approximation.

Theorem 5.2

(Upper bound on the error)
.

Assume
(H1)
, that
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and that
F\colon\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Suppose that
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
is a solution of (
4.5
).
There exists an
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{*}>0
such that if
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{*}
, then

\left\lVert u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}).

(5.16)

The constant
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{*}
depends possibly on the problem data in (
1.1
) and on
\theta_{\mathcal{T}}
the shape-regularity parameter of
\mathcal{T}
, but not on
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
or the size of the mesh.

Proof.

First, recall that
(H1)
states that if
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
is a solution of (
4.5
), then
m_{\mathcal{T}}\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Next, by combining the bound (
5.3
), Theorem
5.1
and (
5.11
), we see that the residual
\mathcal{R}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
satisfies

\mathcal{R}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\lesssim\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}}).

(5.17)

Therefore, it follows that if
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
is sufficiently small, then the pair
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
satisfies the condition (
3.5
) from Theorem
3.1
.
Thus we obtain (
5.16
) from (
3.6
) and from (
5.17
).
∎

We now consider the efficiency of the estimators, i.e. we show that the estimators are bounded from above by the error, up to some data oscillation terms.
These data oscillation terms measure the approximability of the data and the nonlinear terms
F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]
,
H[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]
and
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]
by piecewise polynomial functions.
In order to define the data oscillation terms, we consider a fixed but arbitrary polynomial degree
\kappa\geq 0
, and we define the following approximation operators.
For each
K\in\mathcal{T}
and each nonnegative integer
\kappa\geq 0
, let
\Pi_{K,\kappa}\colon L^{2}(K)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}(K)
denote the
L^{2}
-projection operator onto polynomials of degree at most
\kappa
defined on
F
.
For each face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
, let
\Pi_{F,\kappa}\coloneq L^{2}(F)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{\kappa}(F)
denote the
L^{2}
-projection operator onto the space of polynomials of degree at most
\kappa
defined over
F
.
For each element
K\in\mathcal{T}
,
i\in\{1,2\}
we define the data oscillation terms

[\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,i}]^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{K^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}h_{K^{\prime}}^{2}\left\lVert r_{K^{\prime},i}-\Pi_{K^{\prime},\kappa}r_{K^{\prime},i}\right\rVert_{K^{\prime}}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}}h_{F}\left\lVert j_{F,i}-\Pi_{F,\kappa}j_{F,i}\right\rVert^{2}_{F}.

(5.18)

Observe that

[\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,1}]^{2}=\sum_{K^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{K}}h_{K^{\prime}}^{2}\left\lVert\left(\mathrm{I}-\Pi_{K^{\prime},\kappa}\right)\left(F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]-H[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\right)\right\rVert_{K^{\prime}}^{2}.

(5.19)

Additionally, observe that

[\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,2}]^{2}=\sum_{K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{K}}h_{K^{\prime}}^{2}\left\lVert\left(\mathrm{I}-\Pi_{K^{\prime},\kappa}\right)\left(G+\Div\left(m_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{T}]\right)\right)\right\rVert_{K^{\prime}}^{2}\\
+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}}h_{F}\left\lVert(\mathrm{I}-\Pi_{F,\kappa})\left(m_{\mathcal{T}}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{T}]\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right)\right\rVert_{F}^{2}.

(5.20)

The following theorem shows the local efficiency of the residual estimators (
5.7
).

Theorem 5.3

(Local efficiency of residual estimators)
.

Let
\kappa\geq 0
be a nonnegative integer.
Assume
(H1)
, that
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and that
F\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.
For any
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
, and any
K\in\mathcal{T}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\eta_{K,1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert F[m]-F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\omega_{K})}+\left\lVert\nabla(u-u_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{K}}+\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,1},

(5.21a)

\displaystyle\eta_{K,2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\omega_{K})}+\left\lVert\nabla(u-u_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{K}}+\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,2},

(5.21b)

where the hidden constants depend on the shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}
, the constants
M_{\infty}
,
L_{H}
, and
L_{H_{p}}
,
\nu
, and
\kappa
.

We postpone the proof of Theorem
5.3
to Section
5.4
below.
The next result expresses the global efficiency for the total estimator, which comprises both the residual and stabilization estimators.
This shows that, up to data oscillation and constants, the total estimator is a lower bound for the total error.

Corollary 5.4

(Global efficiency)
.

Assume
(H1)
, that
G\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and that
F\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Suppose that
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in V(\mathcal{T})\times V(\mathcal{T})
is a solution of (
4.5
).
Then,

\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\lesssim\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left[\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}[\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,i}]^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

(5.22)

where the hidden constant depends on the shape-regularity of
\mathcal{T}
, the constants
M_{\infty}
,
L_{F}
,
L_{H}
, and
L_{H_{p}}
, the diffusion parameter
\nu
, and the Poincaré constant of
\Omega
.

Proof.

The result is essentially immediate after summing up the bounds (
5.21
) over all elements of the mesh and combining with the efficiency of the stabilization estimators in (
5.12
).
The only nonobvious step that is required is to use the inequality

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}\left\lVert F[m]-F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\omega_{K})}^{2}\leq(d+2)\left\lVert F[m]-F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2},

which is deduced from the fact that each element of
\mathcal{T}
is contained in at most
d+2
of the patches
\{\omega_{K},\;K\in\mathcal{T}\}
. We then use the Lipschitz continuity of
F
in (
2.2
), and the hypothesis that
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In practical computations, the constant
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{*}
appearing in Theorem
5.2
may be hard to compute
a priori
, since it depends on the problem data.
Nevertheless, Theorem
5.2
still guarantees that convergence to zero of the estimators implies convergence of the numerical approximations to the exact solution.
Note also that there is no smallness condition for the efficiency bounds, c.f. Corollary
5.4
.

Remark 5.4

(Data oscillation)
.

We stress that the polynomial degree
\kappa
in Theorem
5.3
is arbitrary, although the constants in the bounds do depend on
\kappa
. Therefore, in many cases the oscillation terms
\mathrm{osc}_{K,\kappa,i}
are higher order than the errors, and might even vanish entirely in some cases. For instance, if the Hamiltonian
H
has no spatial dependence, i.e.
H(x,p)=H(p)
for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, then
H[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]
and
\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]
are piecewise constant over the mesh
\mathcal{T}
, and thus these are reproduced exactly by the local projection operators for any
\kappa\geq 0
.
Regarding the possibly nonlocal operator
F
, we can consider the following situation for the sake of illustration: suppose that we are given a shape-regular sequence of conforming simplicial meshes
\{\mathcal{T}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the numerical scheme is stable with some uniform
a priori
bound on
\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
(see for instance
[
28
, Eq. (5.6a)]
), and suppose also that
F
is continuous from its domain
\mathcal{H}
into
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then the compactness of the embedding of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
\mathcal{H}
implies the precompactness in
L^{2}(\Omega)
of the set
\{F[m_{\mathcal{T}_{k}}]\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
It is then easy to show that
\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}}h_{K}^{2}\left\lVert F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]-\Pi_{K,\kappa}F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]\right\rVert_{K}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=o(h_{k})
where
h_{k}\coloneqq\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{k}}h_{K}
denotes the maximum mesh-size of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
.
In other words, the contribution to data oscillation from the approximation of
F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]
is then higher-order than the error in many cases.
Despite these examples, it is known however that the classical
L^{2}
-data oscillation term as considered does have some significant shortcomings for problems with rough data, see
[
23
]
for a detailed discussion in the case of the Poisson equation.

Proof of Theorem

To prove the lower bounds, we start by recalling some well-known bounds for the terms in the residual bounds in Lemma

Lemma 5.5
.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}

h_{K}\left\lVert r\right\rVert_{K}\lesssim\left(\sup_{w\in H^{1}_{0}(K)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{K}rw\mathrm{d}x}{\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{K}}\right)+h_{K}\left\lVert r-\Pi_{K,\kappa}r\right\rVert_{K}.

(5.23)

For each interior face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
, any
\kappa\geq 0
, and any

h_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert j\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\left(\sup_{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\omega_{F})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{F}jw\mathrm{d}s}{\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{\omega_{F}}}\right)+h_{F}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert j-\Pi_{F,\kappa}j\right\rVert_{F}.

\kappa

\mathcal{T}

Proof.

This result is derived from the well-known bubble function argument, see e.g.
[

The bound (

h_{K}\left\lVert\Pi_{K,\kappa}r\right\rVert_{K}\lesssim\sup_{w\in H^{1}_{0}(K)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{K}\Pi_{K,\kappa}rw\mathrm{d}x}{\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{K}}

[

.
The bound (
5.24

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{K}r_{K,1}w\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\langle F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]-F[m],w\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}_{0}}+\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla(u-u_{\mathcal{T}})\cdot\nabla w+(H[\nabla u]-H[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}])w\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\int_{K}r_{K,2}w\mathrm{d}x

h_{K}\left\lVert r_{K,1}\right\rVert_{K}\lesssim\left\lVert F[m]-F[m_{\mathcal{T}}]\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(K)}+\left\lVert\nabla(u-u_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{K}+h_{K}\left\lVert r_{K,1}-\Pi_{K,\kappa}r_{K,1}\right\rVert_{K}.

A posteriori error bounds for affine-preserving stabilizations

In this section, we show that the analysis can be refined if, in addition to the hypothesis
(H1)
, the stabilizations satisfies some further structural assumptions, namely Lipschitz continuity, maximum-norm stability of the density approximation, and patchwise affine-preservation.
We present each of these assumptions in turn.

Lipschitz continuity

We assume the following local Lipschitz continuity property on the stabilizations.

(H2)

Lipschitz continuity.
For each
i\in\{1,2\}
, for each interior vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
,

\lvert S_{i}(v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})-S_{i}(\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{w}_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})\rvert\lesssim\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\nabla(v_{\mathcal{T}}-\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}+\left\lVert\nabla(w_{\mathcal{T}}-\widetilde{w}_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\right),

(6.1)

for all
(v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{w}_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{4}
, where the hidden constant is independent of the mesh-size and of the vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}
.

Recall that
|\omega_{z}|_{d}
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
\omega_{z}
.
The assumption
(H2)
contains several ingredients.
Observe that in addition to Lipschitz continuity with respect to the first two arguments, it essentially requires that the stabilizations should be acting locally, i.e. when testing with a basis function
\psi_{z}
in the third argument, the stabilizations may only depend on the values of the first two arguments over the corresponding vertex patch
\omega_{z}
.
It also further requires, in essence, that the stabilization terms should scale as the first-order (or lower) terms of the differential operator.

Example 6.1
.

Consider the stabilizations
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
defined by (
4.6
), and suppose that the matrix-valued function
D_{\mathcal{T}}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{\mathrm{sym},+}
appearing in (
4.6
) satisfies
\left\lVert D_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}\left(\omega_{z};{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\right)}\lesssim h_{\omega_{z}}
for each vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
.
For instance, this is satisfied when
D_{\mathcal{T}}
is given by (
4.7
) and the weights
\gamma_{\mathcal{T},E}\eqsim\diam E
, as is used in
[
30
,
31
]
.
By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, then applying inverse and Hölder inequalities, we obtain

\begin{split}\lvert S_{1}(v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})-S_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}},\widetilde{w}_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})\rvert&\leq\left\lVert D_{\mathcal{T}}\nabla(v_{\mathcal{T}}-\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\left\lVert\nabla\psi_{z}\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert D_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\omega_{z};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\left\lVert\nabla(v_{\mathcal{T}}-\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}h_{\omega_{z}}^{-1}\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\nabla(v_{\mathcal{T}}-\widetilde{v}_{\mathcal{T}})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(6.2)

A similar bound for
S_{2}(\cdot,\cdot;\cdot)
follows from the same arguments. This verifies
(H2)
for this example.

Maximum-norm bound

We also assume the following bound for the maximum-norm of
m_{\mathcal{T}}
.

(H3)

Maximum norm bound.

We assume that the datum
G
and the numerical scheme are such that there is a uniform maximum norm bound for the computed density, i.e. any solution
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
of (
4.5
) satisfies

\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\widetilde{M}_{\infty},

(6.3)

for some constant
\widetilde{M}_{\infty}
, which may depend on the problem data and the shape-regularity parameter
\theta_{\mathcal{T}}
but not on
m_{\mathcal{T}}
or the mesh-size of
\mathcal{T}
.

The maximum norm bound (
6.3
) is the discrete analogue of (
3.2
).
We show in Proposition
6.1
below how to verify
(H3)
for problems in two and three space-dimensions, in the case of the stabilizations from
[
30
,
31
]
on meshes satisfying the Xu–Zikatanov condition.
It can also be verified for a wider range of stabilizations satisfying a DMP.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
d\in\{2,3\}
.
Suppose that
G\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
satisfies
G=g_{0}-\nabla{\cdot}g_{1}
with
g_{0}\in L^{q/2}(\Omega)
and
g_{1}\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
q>d
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
satisfy the Xu–Zikatanov condition.
Let
S_{2}
be defined by (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), with weights
\gamma_{\mathcal{T},E}
verifying the hypotheses of
[
30
, Theorem 4.2]
. Suppose that
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
solves (
4.5
).
Then,

\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\lesssim\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert g_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q/2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

(6.4)

Proposition
6.1
therefore verifies
(H3)
for the stabilizations of
[
30
,
31
]
, for some constant
\widetilde{M}_{\infty}\lesssim\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert g_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q/2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially an application of
[
14
, Theorem 1]
, yet we make a small adjustment of their proof in order to reduce the integrability condition on
g_{0}
.
Note that in this setting, it follows from
[
31
, Lemma 3.1]
that

\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)},

(6.5)

see
[
31
, Remark 3.2]
for further details.
Then, we can write (
4.5b
) equivalently as

a(m_{\mathcal{T}},w_{T})=(f_{0},w_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}+(f_{1},\nabla w_{\mathcal{T}})_{\Omega}\quad\forall w_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T}),

(6.6)

where the bilinear form
a(m_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}})\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}(\nu\mathbb{I}_{d}+D_{\mathcal{T}})\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\nabla w_{\mathcal{T}}\ \mathrm{d}x
, and with right-hand side terms
f_{0}\coloneqq g_{0}\in L^{q/2}(\Omega)
and
f_{1}\coloneqq g_{1}-m_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\in L^{q_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
q_{*}\coloneqq\min\left(q,\frac{2d}{d-2}\right)
, where in particular the Sobolev embedding theorem and (
6.5
) above imply that

\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+L_{H}\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert g_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\left\lVert G\right\rVert_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(6.7)

Observe that
q\geq q_{*}>d
for
d\in\{2,3\}
.
Since
D_{\mathcal{T}}
is symmetric positive semi-definite a.e. in
\Omega
, we see that the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is coercive, i.e.
a(v,v)\geq\nu\left\lVert\nabla v\right\rVert_{\Omega}^{2}
for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Note also that the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is of
nonnegative type
in the sense of
[
14
, Eqs. (2.25), (2.26)]
as a result of the Xu–Zikatanov condition
[
38
]
and the definition of
D_{\mathcal{T}}
in (
4.7
), see
[
30
, Lemma A.1]
for details.
Therefore, the hypotheses
[
14
, Eqs. (2.1), (2.2), (2.3), (2.25), (2.26)]
are all verified.
The proof then essentially follows
[
14
]
, however we can improve the inequality
[
14
, Eq. (3.12)]
by using the sharper bound: for any function
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
that is supported on a measurable set
E\subset\Omega
, we have

|(f_{0},v)_{\Omega}+(f_{1},\nabla v)_{\Omega}|\lesssim|E|_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q_{*}}}\left(\left\lVert f_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}/2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(6.8)

where
|E|_{d}
is the Lebesgue measure of
E
.
In other words, it is sufficient for the analysis to have
f_{0}=g_{0}\in L^{q/2}(\Omega)
for some
q>d
instead of the condition
f_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
,
q>d
, that was assumed in
[
14
]
.
Indeed, to show (
6.8
), we define the index
\sigma\coloneqq\frac{2q_{*}}{q_{*}-2}
and note that
\sigma<\frac{2d}{d-2}
since
q_{*}>d
.
Then, the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embedding theorem imply that

\lvert(f_{0},v)_{\Omega}\rvert\leq\left\lVert f_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}/2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{q_{*}}{q_{*}-2}}(\Omega)}\\
\leq|E|_{d}^{1-\frac{2}{q_{*}}-\frac{1}{\sigma}}\left\lVert f_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}/2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{\sigma}(\Omega)}\lesssim|E|_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q_{*}}}\left\lVert f_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}/2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(6.9)

where we have used above the identity
1-\frac{2}{q_{*}}-\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q_{*}}
as a result of the choice of
\sigma
above.
The rest of the proof then follows
[
14
]
leading to the bound
\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{C(\overline{\Omega})}=\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\lesssim\left\lVert f_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}/2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{q_{*}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, which, in combination with (
6.7
), yields (
6.4
).
∎

Patchwise affine preservation

The last assumption is that the stabilizations are
patchwise affine-preserving
in the following sense.

(H4)

Patchwise affine preservation.
The stabilizations are
patchwise affine-preserving
: if
v_{\mathcal{T}}
and
w_{\mathcal{T}}
in
V(\mathcal{T})
are such that their restrictions to an interior vertex patch
\omega_{z}
,
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
, are affine functions over
\omega_{z}
, i.e.
v_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\omega_{z}}
and
w_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\omega_{z}}
are both in
\mathcal{P}_{1}\left(\omega_{z}\right)
, then

S_{i}(v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})=0\qquad\forall i\in\{1,2\}.

(6.10)

The concept of local affine preservation in
(H4)
(sometimes also called local linearity preservation in the literature) is an important one in the design and analysis of stabilization schemes, leading to improved error bounds
[
7
]
.

Example 6.2
.

A very simple example of a stabilization satisfying
(H4)
is to consider
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
defined by (
4.6
) with
D_{\mathcal{T}}=\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\mathbb{I}_{d}
, where
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}>0
is a constant, and where
\mathbb{I}_{d}
denotes the
d\times d
identity matrix.
Then, for any vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
and any
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
that is affine on the vertex patch
\omega_{z}
, we have
S_{i}(v_{\mathcal{T}},w_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})=\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\omega_{z}}\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot\nabla\psi_{z}\mathrm{d}x=-\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\int_{\omega_{z}}\Delta v_{\mathcal{T}}\psi_{z}=0
since
\psi_{z}\in H^{1}_{0}(\omega_{z})
and
v_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\omega_{z}}\in H^{2}(\omega_{z})
with
\Delta v_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\omega_{z}}=0
.
If the mesh is in addition strictly acute, then such a choice of stabilization is affine-preserving as well as enforcing a discrete maximum principle for all
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
sufficiently large, c.f.
[
28
]
.

For more general meshes satisfying the Xu–Zikatanov condition, it is known that one can obtain both affine preservation and a DMP with
linear
stabilizations provided that the mesh is symmetric with respect to its interior vertices.
Many more examples are provided by nonlinear stabilizations, which can be designed to satisfying
(H4)
and a DMP on more general meshes, we refer the reader to
[
7
]
for a comprehensive survey with many further references.

Remark 6.1

(Patchwise and elementwise affine preservation)
.

In the context of
a posteriori
error analysis, an
elementwise
affine preservation property was considered in
[
4
]
, which requires a decomposition of the stabilization over the mesh elements
[
4
, Eq. (6)]
, each of which is vanishing for functions that are locally affine over a certain neighbourhood
[
4
, Assumption 3.2]
.
Note that the elementwise affine preservation condition is not equivalent to the patchwise one considered here since Example
6.2
evidences a stabilization that satisfies
(H4)
but not the elementwise condition from
[
4
, Assumption 3.2]
.

A posteriori error bounds

One of the original contributions in this work is to show that in the case where
(H1)
,
(H2)
,
(H3)
and
(H4)
all hold, then the stabilization estimators can be bounded in terms of the jump part of the elementwise estimators. Recall the definition of the jump terms
j_{F,1}
and
j_{F,2}
from (
5.5
).

Theorem 6.2

(Bound on stabilization estimators)
.

Assume
(H1)
,
(H2)
,
(H3)
, and
(H4)
.
Suppose that
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
is a solution of (
4.5
).
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\eta_{\mathrm{stab},i}\lesssim\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}}h_{F}\left\lVert j_{F,i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.11)

Theorem
6.2
implies that the total estimator
\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})
is then bounded the residual estimators.
Indeed, recalling (
5.15
), it is then immediate from (
6.11
) that

\eta(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}.

(6.12)

This gives a bound for the error that requires only the residual estimator, i.e. in this case, it is not necessary to compute the stabilization estimator.

Theorem 6.3

(Upper bound on the error for affine-preserving stabilizations)
.

Assume that
F\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Assume
(H1)
,
(H2)
,
(H3)
and
(H4)
.
Suppose that
(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}})\in\left[V(\mathcal{T})\right]^{2}
is a solution of (
4.5
).
There exists an
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{*}}>0
such that if
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}_{*}}
, then

\left\lVert u-u_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert m-m_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{2}\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}.

(6.13)

Proof.

This result follows directly from (
6.12
) and from Theorem
5.2
above.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

From a theoretical perspective, the interest of (
6.13
) arises from the fact that in general, numerical schemes with stabilization fail to satisfy the Galerkin orthogonality property on the residual, and thus the dual norm on the residual is then generally not localizable.
Furthermore, the residual estimators
\eta_{\mathrm{res},i}
have the additional advantage of being locally efficient, as shown in Theorem
5.3
above.
For practical computation, this also has the benefit of allowing one to avoid the explicit computation of the stabilization estimators.
This highlights in particular the role of the patchwise affine preservation condition on the stabilizations in the
a posteriori
error analysis of stabilized finite element methods.

Proof of Theorem
6.2

We shall use the following well-known approximation result for piecewise polynomial functions.

Lemma 6.4
.

For each
v\in V(\mathcal{T})
and each vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
, there holds

\inf_{Q\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\omega_{z})}\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{1}h_{\omega_{z}}^{2k-2}\left\lVert\nabla^{k}(v-Q)\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}\right\}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{z}}h_{F}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla v\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2},

(6.14)

where
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\omega_{z})
denotes the space of affine functions on
\omega_{z}
.
The hidden constant in (
6.14
) depends only on the dimension
d
and the shape-regularity constant
\theta_{\mathcal{T}}
.

Proof.

The proof is well-known so we include it here only for completeness.
Let
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
be a fixed but arbitrary interior vertex.
In
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]
, it is shown that any function
w\colon\omega_{z}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is piecewise polynomial with respect to the elements in
\mathcal{T}_{z}
satisfies the generalized Poincaré inequality

\left\lVert w-\overline{w}\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\lesssim h_{\omega_{z}}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{z}}\left\lVert\nabla w\right\rVert_{K}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{z}}h_{F}^{-1}\left\lVert\left\llbracket w\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(6.15)

where
\overline{w}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}}\int_{\omega_{z}}w\mathrm{d}x
denotes the mean-value of
w
over
\omega_{z}
.

Given a
v\in V(\mathcal{T})
, we then define
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\omega_{z})
by
Q(x)\coloneqq q_{1}{\cdot}x+q_{0}
for all
x\in\omega_{z}
, where

q_{1}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}}\int_{\omega_{z}}\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad q_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}}\int_{\omega_{z}}(v-q_{1}{\cdot}x)\,\mathrm{d}x\in\mathbb{R}.

(6.16)

We therefore deduce from (
6.15
) applied to the components of
\nabla v
that

\left\lVert\nabla(v-Q)\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}=\left\lVert\nabla v-q_{1}\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{z}}h_{F}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla v{\cdot}n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(6.17)

where we have used the bound
h_{\omega_{z}}\lesssim h_{F}
for some constant depending only on the shape-regularity paramter, and where we have also used the facts that
\nabla v
is piecewise constant and that only the normal component of
\nabla v
is possibly discontinuous across mesh faces.
We then use (
6.15
) again to conclude that

\left\lVert v-Q\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}=\left\lVert v-q_{1}{\cdot}x-\overline{v-q_{1}{\cdot}x}\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\lesssim h_{\omega_{z}}\left\lVert\nabla v-q_{1}\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}},

(6.18)

where we have used the fact that
v-q_{1}{\cdot}x
is continuous on
\omega_{z}
so the jump-terms vanish.
We therefore obtain (
6.14
) from (
6.17
) and (
6.18
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

For each function
w_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
and each vertex
z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}
, let
\overline{w}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\omega_{z})
denote the unique minimizer of the left-hand side of (
6.14
), where we note that a unique minimizer exists since
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\omega_{z})
is a finite dimensional space.
Now, consider a fixed but arbitrary
i\in\{1,2\}
and a fixed but arbitrary
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
. Then, expanding
v_{\mathcal{T}}
in the nodal basis of
V(\mathcal{T})
, we may write
v_{\mathcal{T}}=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}v_{\mathcal{T}}(z)\psi_{z}
.
Then, using the linearity of the stabilization in the last argument, we write

\displaystyle\lvert S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}v_{\mathcal{T}}(z)S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})\right\rvert

(6.19)

\displaystyle\underset{\ref{H:stabilization_lin_preserving}}{=}\left\lvert\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}v_{\mathcal{T}}(z)\left[S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};\psi_{z})-S_{i}(\overline{m}_{T}^{z},\overline{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z};\psi_{z})\right]\right\rvert

\displaystyle\underset{\ref{H:stabilization_local_Lipschitz}}{\lesssim}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\lvert v_{\mathcal{T}}(z)\rvert\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla(m_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{m}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}+\left\lVert\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert_{\omega_{z}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\lvert v_{\mathcal{T}}(z)\rvert^{2}\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla(m_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{m}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}+\left\lVert\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It follows from inverse inequalities that
\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\lvert v_{\mathcal{T}}(z)\rvert^{2}\lvert\omega_{z}\rvert_{d}\lesssim\left\lVert v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{\Omega}^{2}
for all
v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
.
The Poincaré inequality thus implies that

\sup_{v_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{S_{i}(u_{\mathcal{T}},m_{\mathcal{T}};v_{\mathcal{T}})}{\left\lVert\nabla v_{\mathcal{T}}\right\rVert_{\Omega}}\lesssim\left(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla(m_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{m}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}+\left\lVert\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}\right]\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.20)

Using the definitions of
\overline{m}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z}
and
\overline{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z}
above, we now apply Lemma
6.4
to bound the right-hand side of (
6.20
), thus giving

\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\left[\left\lVert\nabla(m_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{m}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}+\left\lVert\nabla(u_{\mathcal{T}}-\overline{u}_{\mathcal{T}}^{z})\right\rVert^{2}_{\omega_{z}}\right]\\
\lesssim\sum_{z\in\mathcal{V}^{I}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}_{z}}\left[h_{F}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+h_{F}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right]\\
\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}}\left[h_{F}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+h_{F}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\right],

(6.21)

where, in passing to the final line above, we have used a counting argument based on the fact that each face
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
is associated to at most
d
vertices in
\mathcal{V}
.
Recalling the definition of the jumps in (
5.5
), we see that in order to obtain (
6.11
) and thereby complete the proof, it remains only to show that, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
, we have

\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\left\lVert j_{F,1}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert j_{F,2}\right\rVert_{F}.

(6.22)

Indeed, applying the triangle inequality, we have

\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\left\lVert j_{F,2}\right\rVert_{F}+\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F},

(6.23)

with a hidden constant that depends on
\nu
.
We now use the maximum norm bound
(H3)
to obtain

\left\lVert m_{\mathcal{T}}\left\llbracket\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}\leq\widetilde{M}_{\infty}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}.

(6.24)

Note that the Lipschitz continuity (
2.7
) implies that, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}^{I}
, we have

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}\leq L_{H_{p}}\left\lVert\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K}-\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}|_{K^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{F}=L_{H_{p}}\left\lVert\left\llbracket\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}\cdot n_{F}\right\rrbracket\right\rVert_{F}\lesssim\left\lVert j_{F,1}\right\rVert_{F},\end{split}

(6.25)

where
\{K,K^{\prime}\}\subset\mathcal{T}
are the elements such that
F=\overline{K}\cap\overline{K^{\prime}}
. Note that in (
6.25
) above, we have used the continuity across faces of the tangential component of
\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}
, recalling that
u_{\mathcal{T}}\in V(\mathcal{T})
is continuous.
Therefore, we combine (
6.23
), (
6.24
) and (
6.25
) to deduce (
6.22
), thereby completing the proof.
∎

Numerical Experiments

In this section we present numerical experiments that show the computational performance of the
a posteriori
error estimators of Sections
5
and
6
, and that demonstrate how the estimators lead to potential improvements in efficiency and accuracy in adaptive computations.
Our numerical experiments are performed within the open-source finite element software Firedrake
[
21
]
. The resulting system of equations are solved using the built in nonlinear solver of Firedrake, which uses a Newton line search algorithm.
Mesh construction and refinement are handled using the NETGEN python bindings within Firedrake.

Numerical validation of theoretical error bounds

The first experiment aims to validate the theoretical error bounds of Sections
5
and
6

Adaptive FEM

Our remaining experiments focus on adaptive FEM for stationary MFG.
In contrast to Experiment 1, we consider below problems for which the exact solution is not known analytically, further highlighting the benefits of
a posteriori
error bounds for monitoring the error.
As an extension of the theory, we consider models with mixed boundary conditions of Dirichlet and Neumann types, which are furthermore nonlinear for the case of the density function.
In applications, Neumann boundary conditions model situations where there is a prescribed flux of players entering or exiting the game on parts of the boundary.
Note that handling mixed boundary conditions requires a small adjustment to the estimators, as we now detail.
Suppose that
\partial\Omega
is partitioned as
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
into a Dirichlet part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
and a Neumann part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
.
The mesh
\mathcal{T}
is additionally required to satisfy the condition that every face on the boundary is wholly contained in either the Dirichlet or Neumann part of the boundary.
In the examples below, we consider Neumann conditions of the form
\nu\nabla u{\cdot}n=g_{2}
and
\nu\nabla m{\cdot}n+m\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u]{\cdot}n=g_{3}
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
, where
n
denotes the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\Omega
, and
g_{2}
,
g_{3}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})
are prescribed functions.
Notice that the Neumann condition for the density is nonlinear and depends also on
u
.
The estimators are then adjusted as follows.
For each
K\in\mathcal{T}
, let
\mathcal{F}^{I,N}_{K}
denote the set of faces of
K
that are either in
\mathcal{F}^{I}_{K}
or subsets of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
.
We then extend the jump terms (
5.5
) to Neumann boundary faces by defining
j_{F,1}\coloneqq\nu\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}{\cdot}n_{F}-g_{2}
, and
j_{F,2}\coloneqq\nu\nabla m_{\mathcal{T}}{\cdot}n_{F}+m_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p}[\nabla u_{\mathcal{T}}]{\cdot}n_{F}-g_{3}
for each face
F\subset\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
, where
n_{F}
denotes the unit outward normal to
\Omega
on
F
.
We then replace (
5.6
) by
[\eta_{K,i}]^{2}\coloneqq h_{K}^{2}\left\lVert r_{K,i}\right\rVert^{2}_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}^{I,N}_{K}}h_{F}\left\lVert j_{F,i}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}
, i.e. we extend the face terms to include the contributions from faces in
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
.

In the following experiments, the adaptive mesh refinement is performed using the Dörfler/bulk-chasing marking strategy for the residual estimators, i.e. in each iteration of the adaptive loop, we refine the subset of elements
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{T}
with minimal cardinality that satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\eta_{K,i}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\theta\sum_{i=1}^{2}\eta_{\mathrm{res},i},

(7.2)

where
\theta\in(0,1)
is a chosen parameter. In the computations below we use
\theta=\frac{3}{10}
.
Marked elements are refined via the newest vertex bisection algorithm.
Regarding the choice of stabilization, we use the stabilizations defined in (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) with the local edge-weights chosen as
\gamma_{\mathcal{T},E}=\diam E
, where the sum in (
4.7
) is additionally extended to include boundary edges that are contained in the Neumann part of the boundary.

Conclusion

This paper presented computable a posteriori error bounds for steady state MFG. We proved the reliability and efficiency of residual a posteriori error estimators for general stabilized FEM. For linearity preserving stabilizations, we further showed that the stabilization estimator is bounded by the jump terms of the residual estimator, resulting in a fully localizable estimator. The theoretical results were further validated through numerical experiments. Additionally, experiments with adaptive FEM demonstrate that the a posteriori error estimators yield more computationally efficient approximations compared to standard refinement techniques.

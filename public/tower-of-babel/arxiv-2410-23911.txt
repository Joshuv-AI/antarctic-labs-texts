Abstract
. Let A A be an excellent two-dimensional normal local ring containing an algebraically closed field. Then A A is called an elliptic singularity if p f ​ ( A ) = 1 p_{f}(A)=1 , where p f p_{f} denotes the fundamental genus. On the other hand, the concept of almost Gorenstein rings was introduced by Barucci and Fröberg for one-dimensional local rings and generalized by Goto, Takahashi and Taniguchi to higher dimension. In this paper, we describe almost Gorenstein rings in geometric language using resolution of singularities and give criterions to be almost Gorenstein. In particular, we show that elliptic singularities are almost Gorenstein. Also, for every integer g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 , we provide examples of singularities that is almost Gorenstein (resp. not almost Gorenstein) with p f ​ ( A ) = g p_{f}(A)=g . We also provide several examples of determinantal singularities associated with 2 × 3 2\times 3 matrices, which include both almost Gorenstein singularities and non-almost Gorenstein singularities.

Introduction

The notion of almost Gorenstein local rings was introduced by Barucci and Fröberg
[
2
]
for one-dimensional local rings
and has been generalized by Goto, Takahashi, and Taniguchi
[
4
]
for Cohen-Macaulay local rings having canonical modules of any dimension.
Namely, when
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is a Cohen-Macaulay
local ring of dimension
d
,
we call
A

almost Gorenstein
if there exists an element

\omega\in K_{A}
such that the
A
-module
U=K_{A}/\omega A
satisfies the equality

\mu(U)=e_{0}(U),

where
\mu(U)=\dim_{A/\mathfrak{m}}(U/\mathfrak{m}U)
is the number of minimal generators of
U
and

e_{0}(U)
denotes the multiplicity of
U
. An
A
-module which satisfies this property is
called an
Ulrich
A
-module
.
In other words, a Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module
U
of dimension
d-1

is an Ulrich
A
-module if
for some parameter system
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1})
of
U
, we have the equality

(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-1})U=\mathfrak{m}U
.

The aim of this paper is to determine
whether a normal two-dimensional ring
A
is
almost Gorenstein using resolutions of singularities of
A
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an excellent two-dimensional normal local ring containing an algebraically closed field.
In
[
4
, §11]
, it is proved that
A
is almost Gorenstein if
\mathfrak{m}
is a
p_{g}
-ideal (cf.
[
18
]
); this fact implies that a rational singularity is almost Gorenstein.

In this article, we prove that
A
is almost Gorenstein if it is an elliptic singularity (Theorem
3.8
),
by using cohomology of sheaves on a resolution of singularities.
Let
X\to\spec(A)
be a resolution of singularities.
We define that
A
is an
elliptic singularity
if
p_{f}(A)=1
, where

p_{f}(A)
denotes the arithmetic genus of the Artin’s fundamental cycle
Z_{f}
on
X
.
The concept of elliptic singularities was introduced by Wagreich
[
26
]
and have been studied by
many authors: Laufer
[
10
]
, Yau
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
, Tomari
[
23
]
, Némethi
[
12
]
, Okuma
[
13
]
, Nagy–Némethi
[
11
]
.
Our argument is based on the analysis of cohomology of coherent sheaves on
X
related with canonical divisor
K_{X}
and the
cycle on
X
which represents the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.
In the proof of the main result, a property of the cohomological cycle for elliptic singularities (Konno
[
8
]
) plays an important role.
Our method is also useful to prove that
A
is almost Gorenstein if
\mathfrak{m}
is a
p_{g}
-ideal
(Theorem
4.5
).
In particular, this gives a new proof to show that a rational singularity is almost
Gorenstein.

This paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
, we recall the terminology and basics of cycles on a resolution of singularities, and then introduce a commutative diagram that connects the canonical sheaf on the resolution space with an
A
-module
U
which determines
almost Gorenstein property of
A
.

Let
K_{X}
be the canonical divisor of
X
. Recall that
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))\subset K_{A}
and

\ell_{A}(K_{A}/H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))=p_{g}(A)
. Namely,
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}))=K_{A}
holds if
and only if
A
is a rational singularity.
In general, we can find a resolution
X
and a cycle
C_{X}
on
X
so that

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(K_{X}+C_{X}))=K_{A}
and
\mathcal{O}(K_{X}+C_{X})
is generated by global sections.
In the following, we assume that
K_{X}+C_{X}
satisfies these
conditions.

We introduce the most important sheaf
\mathcal{U}
on
X
defined by the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\overset{\times\omega}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+C_{X})\to\mathcal{U}\to 0,

where
\omega\in K_{A}=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(K_{X}+C_{X}))

is a general element of
K_{A}
.
We discuss fundamental properties of sheaves related to this exact sequence.
We have that
U\subset H^{0}(\mathcal{U})
and
\mathcal{U}
is always
generated by global sections.

In Section
3
, we prove that if
H^{0}(\mathcal{U})=U
holds,
then
A
is an almost Gorenstein ring.
Moreover, if
A
is a rational singularity or
an elliptic singularity, then we can verify that
H^{0}(\mathcal{U})=U
and
can conclude that

rational singularities and elliptic singularities are almost Gorenstein.

In Section
4
, we provide a characterization of a singularity being almost Gorenstein
in terms of
\mathcal{U}
.
Among several examples, we can prove that a singularity is almost Gorenstein
if its maximal ideal is a
p_{g}
-ideal.

In Section
5
, we give some examples of elliptic singularities and
show ring-theoretic properties of those singularities.

In Section
6
, we consider cone singularities
R(C,D)
associated with a nonsingular curve
C
of genus
g\geq 2
and a divisor
D
on
C
with
\deg D>0
.
Note that
p_{f}(A)=g
for these singularities.
We can provide many examples of both almost Gorenstein rings and not almost
Gorenstein rings of this class.
In particular, in the case
D=K_{C}+p
, where
p
is a point of
C
, we prove that
R(C,D)
is almost Gorenstein if and only if
C
is hyperelliptic.
Also, we show that
R(C,D)
is not almost Gorenstein if
\deg D>2\deg K_{C}
.
These examples also show that
the almost Gorenstein property cannot be determined only by the pair
(g,\deg D)
.

In Section
7
, we provide determinantal two-dimensional normal singularities associated with
2\times 3
matrices of the following types:
(1)
A
is non-rational and
\mathfrak{m}
is
p_{g}
-ideal,
(2)
A
is non-elliptic
almost Gorenstein and
\mathfrak{m}
is not
p_{g}
-ideal, and (3)
A
is not almost Gorenstein.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an excellent two-dimensional normal local ring containing an algebraically closed field
k\cong A/\mathfrak{m}
, and let

\pi\colon X\to\spec(A)
a resolution of singularities with exceptional
divisor
E:=\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{m})
.
Let
E=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}
be the decomposition into the irreducible components, and let
K_{X}
denote the canonical divisor on
X
.

2.1.
Almost Gorenstein local rings

AAAAAAA

For a finitely generated

A
-module
M
, we denote by
e_{0}(M)
the multiplicity of
M
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
and by
\mu(M)
the number of elements in a minimal system of generators for
M
;
namely, if
\dim M=s
,

e_{0}(M)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\ell_{A}(M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M)s!}{n^{s}},\quad\mu(M)=\ell_{A}(M/\mathfrak{m}M).

We say that
M
is an
Ulrich module
if
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay module with
e_{0}(M)=\mu(M)
.
If
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})
is a minimal reduction of the maximal ideal of

A/\mathrm{Ann}_{A}(M)
, then
M
is an Ulrich
A
-module if and only if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})M=\mathfrak{m}M

holds.

Let
K_{A}
denote the canonical module of
A
and let
\type(A)=\mu(K_{A})
.

Definition 2.1

(cf.
[
4
, Definition 3.3]
)
.

A
is said to be
almost Gorenstein
if there is an exact sequence of
A
-modules

0\to A\to K_{A}\to U\to 0

such that

U
is an Ulrich
A
-module.
Note that the exactness implies that
U=0
or
U
is a Cohen-Macaulay
A
-module of
\dim U=1
(cf.
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
).

A Gorenstein singularity is almost Gorenstein by definition.

Any homomorphism
A\to K_{A}
is given by an element
\omega\in K_{A}
. If we take a general element so that
\omega\not\in\mathfrak{m}K_{A}
, then
\mu(K_{A}/\omega A)=\mu(K_{A})-1=\type(A)-1
.

2.2.
Cycles and genera

AAAAAAA

A divisor
D
on
X
with
\supp(D)\subset E
is called a
cycle
.
We may consider a cycle
D>0
as a scheme with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{D}:=\mathcal{O}_{X}/\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
.
We write
\chi(C)=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{C}):=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C})-h^{1}(\mathcal{O}_{C})
, where
h^{i}(\mathcal{F})
denotes
\ell_{A}(H^{i}(\mathcal{F}))
.
A divisor
L
on
X
is said to be
nef
(resp.
anti-nef
) if
LE_{i}\geq 0
(resp.
LE_{i}\leq 0
) for all
E_{i}\subset E
.
It is known that anti-nef cycles are effective
and the support of a non-zero anti-nef cycle is
E
.
There exists the minimum
Z_{f}
among the anti-nef cycles

;
the cycle

is called the
fundamental cycle
(
[
1
]
).
The
fundamental genus

of

is defined by
p_{f}(A)=1-\chi(Z_{f})
, and the
geometric genus

p_{g}(A)
of

is defined by

; these are independent of the choice of the resolution.
We have
p_{f}(A)\leq p_{g}(A)
.
While the arithmetic genus is determined by the resolution graph, the geometric genus is not. The local ring
A
is said to be
rational
if
p_{g}(A)=0
.
It is known that
A
is a rational singularity if and only if
p_{f}(A)=0
(see
[
1
]
).
If
I\subset A
is an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, then
there exists a resolution
X\to\Spec A
and a cycle
Z>0
on
X

such that
I\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z)
and
I=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z))
. In this case, we say that
I
is
represented
by
Z
on
X
.

The following theorem of Stanley is very important in our theory.

Theorem 6.3

(Stanley’s Theorem;
[

]
)
.

Let

be a Cohen-Macaulay graded ring of dimension

with

, a field and let

.

.

Let

be a graded ring with

\dim_{k}\Coker\gamma_{1}=\ell_{A}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(2K_{C}+P))/H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(2K_{C})))=1.

Next assume that

is hyperelliptic.
Then there exists a double covering

. If we put

for some point

, then
(g-1)F\sim K_{C}
and we have

R(C,F)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(nF))\cong k[X,Y,Z]/(Z^{2}-f_{2g+2}(X,Y))

for some homogeneous polynomial

of degree

, if

(if

, we need slightly different expression but the argument is essentially the same).
Since

we have

we obtain that

Now we apply Lemma

(2)
to complete the proof of Theorem

By (

) and Lemma

\displaystyle\mu(U)=\type(A)-1

\displaystyle=h^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{C}(K_{C}))+\dim_{k}\Coker\gamma_{1}-1

\displaystyle=g-1+\dim_{k}\Coker\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}g&\text{ if $C$ is not hyperelliptic, }\\
2g-2&\text{ if $C$ is hyperelliptic.}\end{cases}\qed

The Point Divisors and Weierstrass Semigroups

AAAAAAA

Next we treat the Case when

The ring

First let us review the “Weierstrass semigroup”

We have the following relation between

and

Proposition 6.14

(

)
.

If

In general, we say that a subset

, and

.

and we say that

is an

Proposition 6.15

(
[

)

k[H]=k[t^{h}\;|\;h\in H]

6.4.
The case

AAAAAAA

In this subsection, we determine almost Gorenstein property of

Theorem 6.21
.

Let

(1)

(2)

(3)

We write

for a divisor

.
By Riemann-Roch Theorem, we have

We also have that

if

, or
\deg(L)=2

\deg(L)=1
and

(1)

If

, then

for some point

is almost Gorenstein by Theorem

.

(2)

Assume

and

.
Then

(a)

If

(b)

If

and

Let

be a nonzero element.
By Theorem
6.3
, we have

\displaystyle P(K_{R},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{2+2t+t^{2}+t^{5}}{(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})},

\displaystyle P(K_{R}/wR,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+2t+t^{2}-t^{3}-2t^{4}-t^{5}}{(1-t^{2})(1-t^{3})}=\frac{1+2t+t^{2}}{1-t^{2}}.

,

.

(3)

.

.

is almost Gorenstein.

(4)

Assume
\deg D=3
. Then

.

(a)

If
\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
is not generated and if

is a base point of
\mathcal{O}_{C}(D)
,

.
Then

is almost Gorenstein by Theorem

.

(b)

If

In this section, we provide some examples under common settings as follows.

From Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 3.3 of

i=1,\dots,d
, we have
n=d_{2n}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(2n-2nq_{i})
. Since
q_{1}\leq 1/2
, this implies that
d=1
and
q_{1}=1/2
.
Note that
Q\not\sim P_{1}
because
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(D^{(1)}))=0
, and the resolution graph of
A
is the same as that of
k[[x,y,z]]/(x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{12})
; thus we obtain the case
d=1
of Example
5.4
.

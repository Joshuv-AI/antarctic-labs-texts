Central series’ and ( n n )-isoclinism of skew left braces
Arpan Kanrar, Charlotte Roelants, Manoj K. Yadav

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to advance the knowledge on the theory of skew left braces. We introduce a subclass of skew left braces, which we denote by ℐ n \mathcal{I}_{n} , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 , such that elements of the annihilator and lower central series’ interact ‘nicely’ with respect to commutation. That allows us to define a concept of n n -isoclinism of skew left braces in ℐ n \mathcal{I}_{n} , by using a concept of brace commutator words, which we have introduced. We prove results on 1 1 -isoclinism (isoclinism) of skew left braces analogous to important results in group theory. For any two symmetric n n -isoclinic skew left braces A A and B B , we prove that, there exist skew left braces C C and R R such that both A A and B B are n n -isoclinic to both C C and R R and (i) A A and B B are quotient skew left braces of C C ; (ii) A A and B B are sub-skew left braces of R R . Connections between a skew left brace and the group which occurs as a natural semi-direct product of additive and multiplicative groups of the skew left brace are investigated, and it is proved that n n -isoclinism is preserved from braces to groups. We also show that various nilpotency concepts on skew left braces are invariant under n n -isoclinism.

3.
Isoclinism of annihilator extensions of skew braces

Let
K:=(K,\cdot,\circ)
be a skew brace and
A
an abelian group. The concept of action of
K
on
A
(viewed as a trivial brace) and second brace cohomology group of
K
with coefficients in
A
was studied in
[
21
]
. We here consider the trivial action of
K
on
A
.
A
factor set
or
2
-cocycle
of
K
with coefficients in
A
is a pair of maps
(\alpha,\mu)
,
\alpha,\mu:K\times K\to A
, satisfying the following conditions for all
x,y,z\in K
:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\alpha(1,y)=\alpha(x,1)=1,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mu(1,y)=\mu(x,1)=1,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\alpha(y,z)\alpha(x\cdot y,z)^{-1}\alpha(x,y\cdot z)\alpha(x,y)^{-1}=1,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mu(y,z)\mu(x\circ y,z)^{-1}\mu(x,y\circ z)\mu(x,y)^{-1}=1,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\alpha(y,z)\alpha(x,x^{-1})\alpha(x\circ y,x^{-1})^{-1}\alpha((x\circ y)\cdot x^{-1},x\circ z)^{-1}=\mu(x,z)\mu(x,y\cdot z)^{-1}\mu(x,y).

As observed in
[
20
]
, using (
3.3
) for the elements
x\circ y,x^{-1},x\circ z
and then for the elements
x,x^{-1},x\circ z
, (
3.5
) can equivalently be written more neatly as

(3.6)

\displaystyle\alpha(y,z)\alpha(x\circ y,\lambda_{x}(z))^{-1}\alpha(x,\lambda_{x}(z))=\mu(x,y)\mu(x,y\cdot z)^{-1}\mu(x,z).

A
2
-cocycle
(\alpha,\mu)
of
K
with coefficients in
A
is said to be a
2
-coboundary
if there exists a map

such that
h(1)=1
and the following hold for all

:

\displaystyle\alpha(x,y)

\displaystyle=h(x)h(y)h(x\cdot y)^{-1},

\displaystyle\mu(x,y)

\displaystyle=h(x)h(y)h(x\circ y)^{-1}.

Let us denote the set of all
2
-cocycles by
\operatorname{Z}^{2}_{b}(K,A)
and the set of all
2
-coboundaries by
\operatorname{B}^{2}_{b}(K,A)
. Notice that
\operatorname{Z}^{2}_{b}(K,A)
is an abelian group, under component-wise operation, and
\operatorname{B}^{2}_{b}(K,A)
is its subgroup. The quotient group

\operatorname{H}^{2}_{b}(K,A):=\operatorname{Z}_{b}^{2}(K,A)/\operatorname{B}^{2}_{b}(K,A)
is called the
second cohomology group
of the skew left brace
K
with coefficients in the abelian group
A
having trivial action.

Let
K
and
A
be skew braces. A skew brace
G
is said to be a
brace extension
of
K
by
A
if the following sequence of skew braces is exact

(3.7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}_{G}:1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

For simplicity, we shall denote such an extension by

itself. An extension

of

if

. Let

denote the set of all annihilator extensions of

by

. Then it follows form

that

. We elaborate on this a little more here. For a given cohomology class

, we can define a skew brace

, where

\displaystyle(a_{1},k_{1})\cdot(a_{2},k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}a_{2}\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}),k_{1}\cdot k_{2});

\displaystyle(a_{1},k_{1})\circ(a_{2},k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a_{1}a_{2}\mu(k_{1},k_{2}),k_{1}\circ k_{2})

for all

,

. It turns out that
G
is an annihilator extension of
K
by
A
with
i(a)=(a,1)
and
\pi(a,k)=k
. On the other hand, if we start with an annihilator extension
\mathcal{E}_{G}
, we can define a
2
-cocycle as follows. Let

be transversal of
\pi
such that
t(1)=1
. Then define

by setting

\displaystyle\alpha(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=t(k_{1})\cdot t(k_{2})\cdot t(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})^{-1};

\displaystyle\mu(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle=t(k_{1})\circ t(k_{2})\circ\overline{t(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})}

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
. For proofs and other details, please refer to
[

Let
K
be a skew brace and
A
an abelian group viewed as a trivial brace. Let
\operatorname{H}_{b}^{2}(K,A)
be the second cohomology group of
K
with coefficients in
A
under trivial action. Let
G:=A\times_{(\alpha,\mu)}K
be the brace extension of
K
by
A
determined by the cohomology class
[(\alpha,\mu)]\in\operatorname{H}_{b}^{2}(K,A)
. So we get the extension

1\to A\to G\to K\to 1

such that
A
embeds in
\operatorname{Ann}(G)
. For simplicity of notation, we view
A
as an ideal of
G
. Let
C
be an abelian group, again viewed as a trivial brace. For any homomorphism
\lambda:A\to C
, we can define maps
\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}:K\times K\to C
given by

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lambda(\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}));

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\lambda(\mu(k_{1},k_{2}))

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
. A routine check reveals that
(\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is a
2
-cocycle of

with coefficients in

, viewing

as a trivial
K
-module, which, in turn, defines a group homomorphism
\varepsilon_{G}:\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)\to\operatorname{H}_{b}^{2}(K,C)
given by

\varepsilon_{G}(\lambda):=[(\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})]

for all
\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}(A,C)
. For a given annihilator extension

of skew left braces, the homomorphism
\varepsilon_{G}
, defined using the corresponding cohomology class, is termed as the
transgression
homomorphism in the literature (see
[
20
, pages 621-622]
). We consider a specific situation when
K
is finite and
C=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, the multiplicative group of all non-zero complex numbers. More precisely, we deal with the homomorphism
\varepsilon_{G}:\hat{A}\to\operatorname{H}_{b}^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, where for any abelian group

,
\hat{D}:=\operatorname{Hom}(D,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A
be an ideal of a skew brace
G
such that
A\leq\operatorname{Ann}(G)
. Then
\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon_{G})\cong(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))^{\perp}
and
\varepsilon_{G}(\hat{A})\cong\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}
.

Proof.

Let
[(\alpha,\mu)]\in\operatorname{H}_{b}^{2}(K,A)
be the cohomology class corresponding to the extension

1\to A\to G\to K\to 1.

So
G\cong A\times_{(\alpha,\mu)}K
.
Let
\lambda\in\hat{A}
and
[(\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{b}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
be the cohomology class induced by
\lambda
as explained above.

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon_{G})
, that is,
(\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\in\operatorname{B}_{b}^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Then there is a map
h:K\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with
h(1)=1
and

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})=h(k_{1})h(k_{2})h(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})^{-1}

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})=h(k_{1})h(k_{2})h(k_{1}\circ k_{2})^{-1}

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
. We are now going to extend
\lambda
to a brace homomorphism
\tau:G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as follows. For any
(a,k)\in G
, define
\tau
by

\displaystyle\tau(a,k)=\lambda(a)h(k).

Clearly
\tau
is well-defined. Now the equations

\displaystyle\tau((a_{1},k_{1})\cdot(a_{2},k_{2}))

\displaystyle=\tau(a_{1}a_{2}\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}),k_{1}\cdot k_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda(a_{1}a_{2}\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}))h(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})

\displaystyle\tau(a_{1},k_{1})\tau(a_{2},k_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda(a_{1})h(k_{1})\lambda(a_{2})h(k_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda(a_{1}a_{2})\alpha^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})h(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda(a_{1}a_{2}\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}))h(k_{1}\cdot k_{2}),

imply that
\tau((a_{1},k_{1})\cdot(a_{2},k_{2}))=\tau(a_{1},k_{1})\tau(a_{2},k_{2})
.
Since
A\leq\operatorname{Ann}(G)
, the same conclusion holds under the operation ‘
\circ
’.
Hence
\tau
is a brace homomorphism. Using the fact that
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is a trivial brace, it follows that
G/\operatorname{Ker}(\tau)
is a trivial brace, which gives
\Gamma_{2}(G)\leq\operatorname{Ker}(\tau)
as
\Gamma_{2}(G)
is the smallest ideal such that
G/\Gamma_{2}(G)
is trivial. Since
\tau|_{A}=\lambda
, it follows that
\lambda(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))=1
.
Thus
\lambda\in(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))^{\perp}
.

Conversely let
\lambda\in(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)^{\perp}
. Notice that
A\Gamma_{2}(G)
, as a subgroup of
(G,\cdot)
, is an ideal of
G
and
A/(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))\cong A\Gamma_{2}(G)/\Gamma_{2}(G)
. Since
A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)\leq\operatorname{Ker}(\lambda)
,
\lambda
factors through a homomorphism
f:A/(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, which, by the injectivity of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, extends to a homomorphism
\bar{f}:G/\Gamma_{2}(G)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Let us define

\delta:G\to G/\Gamma_{2}(G)\xrightarrow{\bar{f}}\mathbb{C}^{\times},

the composition of the natural projection
G\to G/\Gamma_{2}(G)
and
\bar{f}
. It is easy to see that
\delta
is a brace homomorphism.

Let us now define
h:K\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by setting, for all
k\in K
,

h(k)=\delta(1,k).

For
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
, we have

\displaystyle h(k_{1})h(k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(1,k_{1})\delta(1,k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta((1,k_{1})\cdot(1,k_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}),k_{1}\cdot k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}),1)\delta(1,k_{1}\cdot k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda(\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}))h(k_{1}\cdot k_{2}),

which implies that

\alpha^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})=\lambda(\alpha(k_{1},k_{2}))=h(k_{1})h(k_{2})h(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})^{-1}.

Similarly one obtains

\mu^{\prime}(k_{1},k_{2})=\lambda(\mu(k_{1},k_{2}))=h(k_{1})h(k_{2})h(k_{1}\circ k_{2})^{-1}

for all
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
. Hence,
(\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})\in\operatorname{B}_{b}^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, and therefore it follows that
\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon_{G})=(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))^{\perp}
.

Again using the fact
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is injective, we deduce that each homomorphism
A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
extends to a homomorphism
A\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, proving that the map
\lambda\to\lambda|_{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}
from

\hat{A}
to
\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}
is a surjective group homomorphism. Hence we get

\varepsilon_{G}(\hat{A})\cong\hat{A}/(A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G))^{\perp}\cong\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)},

which completes the proof.
∎

\tau_{1}:\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}\to\varepsilon_{G}(\hat{A}),\hskip 28.45274pt\tau_{1}(\bar{\lambda}_{1})=\varepsilon_{G}(\lambda_{1})=\operatorname{B}_{b}^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})(\alpha^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})

is an isomorphism. Similarly we have an isomorphism

\tau_{2}:\widehat{B\cap\Gamma_{2}(H)}\to\varepsilon_{H}(\hat{B}),\hskip 28.45274pt\tau_{2}(\bar{\lambda}_{2})=\varepsilon_{H}(\lambda_{2})=\operatorname{B}_{b}^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})(\beta^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}),

where
\lambda_{2}
extends

from

to

.
So we have an isomorphism

\psi:=\tau_{2}^{-1}\tau_{1}:\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}\to\widehat{B\cap\Gamma_{2}(H)},\hskip 28.45274pt\psi(\bar{\lambda}_{1})=\bar{\lambda}_{2}.

Taking into account the natural isomorphism
A\cap\Gamma_{2}(Y)\cong\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(Y)}
,
Y=G,H
, we get an isomorphism
\eta:A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)\to B\cap\Gamma_{2}(H)
satisfying

\bar{\lambda}_{1}(a)=\psi(\bar{\lambda}_{1})(\eta(a))

for all
\lambda\in\widehat{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}
and
a\in A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)
. We now aim to extend
\eta
to a brace isomorphism between
\Gamma_{2}(G)
and
\Gamma_{2}(H)
.

Let
\lambda:=\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2}\in\widehat{A\times B}
. It follows from the definitions of
\varepsilon_{Y}
,
Y=G,H,L
, that
\varepsilon_{L}(\lambda)
is a coboundary if and only if
\varepsilon_{G}(\lambda_{1})=\varepsilon_{H}(\lambda_{2})
, which is equivalent to
\psi(\bar{\lambda}_{1})=\bar{\lambda}_{2}
, where
\bar{\lambda}_{1}=\lambda_{1}|_{A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)}
and
\bar{\lambda}_{2}=\lambda_{2}|_{B\cap\Gamma_{2}(H)}
. Using (
3.14
), the preceding condition is further equivalent to
\bar{\lambda}_{1}(a)=\bar{\lambda}_{2}(\eta(a))
for all

. Hence, we obtain

\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon_{L})=\{\lambda\in\widehat{A\times B}\mid\lambda(a,\eta(a)^{-1})=1~\mbox{ for all }a\in A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)\}.

By Lemma
3.8
we know that

\operatorname{Ker}(\varepsilon_{L})=\big((A\times B)\cap\Gamma_{2}(L)\big)^{\perp}.

Reading it in the present set-up, we get

(3.15)

(A\times B)\cap\Gamma_{2}(L)=\{\big((a,\eta(a)^{-1}),1\big)\mid a\in A\cap\Gamma_{2}(G)\}.

As we already know, for any given
(a,k)\in\Gamma_{2}(G)
, there exists
(b,k)\in\Gamma_{2}(H)
such that
((a,b^{-1}),k)\in\Gamma_{2}(L)
. Using (
3.15
) we see that the element
(b,k)\in\Gamma_{2}(H)
is unique. For, if
(b_{1},k)\in\Gamma_{2}(H)
is another such element, that is,
((a,b_{1}^{-1}),k)\in\Gamma_{2}(L)
, then
((a,b^{-1}),k)\cdot((a,b_{1}^{-1}),k)^{-1}\in\Gamma_{2}(L)
. So
((1,b^{-1}b_{1}),1)\in(A\times B)\cap\Gamma_{2}(L)
, which, by (

), gives

. We can, similarly, say that for any given

such that
((a,b^{-1}),k)\in\Gamma_{2}(L)
. This allow us to define a bijective map
\theta:\Gamma_{2}(G)\to\Gamma_{2}(H)
, given by

\theta(a,k)=(b,k).

Recall that the elements

and
b\in B
under discussion are given by the same product of elements of the form
(1,k)
with respect to the cohomology classes

and
[(\beta,\nu)]\in\operatorname{H}^{2}_{b}(K,B)
respectively. An easy computation, taking into consideration the definition of

, shows that

is a brace isomorphism, and the diagram (

) commutes. This completes the proof of the theorem.

\displaystyle m_{2}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{\deg(m_{2})+1})m_{1}(b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{\deg(m_{1})+1})

\displaystyle m^{\prime}\big(m_{2}(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{\deg(m_{2})+1})b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{\deg(m_{1})+1}\big)

. We now prove

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

\deg(m)=t

We proceed by induction on

. Then

. The result now follows from the definition of
\operatorname{Ann}_{1}(A)
. Assume by induction that the result is true for all

\epsilon_{t}\in X

\displaystyle m(a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{t}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}(a_{1},\epsilon_{2}(a_{2},\ldots,\epsilon_{t}(a_{t},u)\cdots))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}(a_{1},\epsilon_{2}(a_{2},\ldots,\epsilon_{t-1}(a_{t-1},v)\cdots)),\mbox{ where }v=\epsilon_{t}(a_{t},u),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\mbox{ (using inductive argument), since }v\in\operatorname{Ann}_{t-1}(A).

Now assume the other side, that is,

\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
if and only if
\epsilon(u,v)\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
for all
\epsilon\in X
,
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s}(A)
and
v\in S_{r-1}(A)
,
1\leq r\leq s\leq n
.

Proof.

For the proof of the ‘if’ part, we only need to prove that

m(a_{1}u_{1},a_{2}u_{2},\ldots,a_{t+1}u_{t+1})=m(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{t+1})

for all
m\in M
with
\deg(m)=t
,
a_{i}\in A
and
u_{i}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{t}(A)
, where
1\leq i\leq t+1
and
1\leq t\leq n
. We only prove the case
t=n
. All other cases, indeed, will follow from the steps of the proof of this case. Let
m=\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\ldots\epsilon_{n}
, where
\epsilon_{i}\in X
, be an arbitrary element of
M
of degree
n
. It follows from Corollary
4.6
, for
r=1
, that

\epsilon_{n}(a_{n}u_{n},a_{n+1}u_{n+1})=\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1})z_{n-1}

for some
z_{n-1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{n-1}(A)
. Since
\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1})\in S_{1}(A)
, again by Corollary
4.6
for
r=2
, using the given hypothesis, we get

\epsilon_{n-1}\big(a_{n-1}u_{n-1},\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1})z_{n-1}\big)=\epsilon_{n-1}\big(a_{n-1},\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1})\big)z_{n-2}

for some
z_{n-2}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{n-2}(A)
. Set
y:=\epsilon_{n-1}(a_{n-1},\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1}))
; then using the given hypothesis, we get

\epsilon_{2}(a_{2}u_{2},\epsilon_{3}(a_{3}u_{3},\ldots,\epsilon_{n-2}(a_{n-2}u_{n-2},yz_{n-2})\ldots))=\epsilon_{2}(a_{2},\epsilon_{3}(a_{3},\ldots\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1})\ldots))z_{1}

for some
z_{1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{1}(A)
. Since
\epsilon_{2}(a_{2},\epsilon_{3}(a_{3},\ldots\epsilon_{n}(a_{n},a_{n+1})\ldots))\in S_{n-1}(A)
and
u_{1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
, one final iteration gives the desired identity.

Now we prove the ‘only if’ part. Assume that
A\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
,
1\leq t\leq n
. For
n\geq s\geq r\geq 1
, let
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s}(A)
and
v=m^{\prime}(b_{1}b_{2}\cdots b_{r})\in S_{r-1}
, where
m^{\prime}\in M
with
\deg(m^{\prime})=r-1
. Choose any
m\in M
with
\deg(m)=s-r
and any
\epsilon\in X
. Then, since
A\in\mathcal{I}_{s}
and
\deg(m\epsilon m^{\prime})=s
, we get

\displaystyle m(a_{1}\cdots a_{s-r},\epsilon(u,v)\cdots)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\epsilon m^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{s-r}ub_{1}\cdots b_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\epsilon m^{\prime}(a_{1}\cdots a_{r-1}1b_{1}\cdots b_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

for all
a_{i}\in A
,
1\leq i\leq s-r
. Hence, by Lemma
4.1
, it follows that
\epsilon(u,v)\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Now specializing to
s=n
, the preceding theorem provides a criterion for a skew brace to fall in
\mathcal{I}_{n}
.

Corollary 4.9
.

A skew brace
A
belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{n}
if and only if
\epsilon(u,v)\in\operatorname{Ann}_{n-r}(A)
for all
\epsilon\in X
,
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
and
v\in S_{r-1}(A)
,
1\leq r\leq n
.

Two skew braces
A,B\in\mathcal{I}_{n}
are said to be
n
-isoclinic
,
n\geq 1
, if there exist brace isomorphisms

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)\to B/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle A_{(n)}\to B_{(n)}

such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A))^{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{(n)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B))^{n+1}}

5.
Symmetric skew braces

In this section we first prove that all symmetric skew braces belong to the class
\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Then we prove several results analogous to the results of Bioch
[
5
]
. We start with the following nice observation on symmetric skew braces, which follows from
[
4
, Proposition 5.2]

Lemma 5.1
.

A skew brace
A
is symmetric if and only if for all
a,b\in A
, we have
\lambda_{ab}=\lambda_{b\circ a}
. Moreover, in a symmetric skew brace
A
, (i)
\lambda_{a^{-1}}=\lambda_{\bar{a}}=\lambda^{-1}_{a}
and (ii)
\lambda_{a*b}=\lambda_{[a^{-1},b]^{\cdot}}
for all
a,b\in A
.

It is not difficult to see that the left ideal
A_{(t)}
constructed in the preceding section coincides with
\Gamma_{t+1}(A)
, when
A
is a symmetric skew brace. Indeed

Lemma 5.2
.

For a symmetric skew brace
A
,
A_{(t)}=\Gamma_{t+1}(A)
for all integers
t\geq 1
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction. The result is obvious for
t=1
. So assume that
A_{(t-1)}=\Gamma_{t}(A)
. Also, it is sufficient to prove that the generators of
\Gamma_{t+1}(A)
lie in
A_{(t)}
. If we take a generator
v
that either belongs to
A*\Gamma_{t}(A)
or
[A,\Gamma_{t}(A)]^{\cdot}
, then by the arguments used in the proof of Lemma
4.3
we see that
v\in A_{(t)}
. So assume that
v\in\Gamma_{t}(A)*A
.
So
v=(x_{1}*a_{1})^{\delta_{1}}\cdots(x_{r}*a_{r})^{\delta_{r}}
for some
y_{i}\in\Gamma_{t}(A)
,
a_{i}\in A
and positive integer
r
. So it is sufficient to prove it for any of the components of
v
. We prove it for the first component, that is,
(x_{1}*a_{1})^{\delta_{1}}
.

By the inductive argument, we know that
x_{1}\in A_{(t-1)}
. Let
y,~y_{1},~y_{2}\in S_{t-1}(A)
and
a\in A
. Then, using Lemma
5.1
, we get

\displaystyle(y_{1}y_{2})\ast a

\displaystyle=\lambda_{y_{1}y_{2}}(a)a^{-1}=\lambda_{y_{2}\circ y_{1}}(a)a^{-1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{y_{2}}\big(\lambda_{y_{1}}(a)a^{-1}a\big)a^{-1}

\displaystyle=(y_{2}\ast a)a\lambda_{y_{2}}(a(y_{1}\ast a)a^{-1})a^{-1},

which belongs to
A_{(t)}
as it is a strong left ideal of
A
. Now, using Lemma
5.1
again, the preceding identity with
y_{1}=y
and
y_{2}=y^{-1}
gives

y^{-1}\ast a=a\lambda_{\bar{y}}\big(a(y*a)a^{-1})\big)^{-1}a^{-1},

which again lies in
A_{(t)}
. Since
x_{1}
can be written as a product of finitely many elements from
S_{t-1}(A)\cup(S_{t-1}(A))^{-1}
, this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A
be a symmetric skew brace. Then, for all
s\geq r\geq 1
,
v\in\Gamma_{r}(A)
and
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s}(A)
, we have
v*u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
.

Proof.

We shall proceed by induction on
r
. For
r=1
, the result follows by the definition of the
s
th annihilator. So assume that
s\geq r\geq 2
and the assertion holds for
r-1
. We need to show that
\lambda_{v}(u)u^{-1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
for all
v\in\Gamma_{r}(A)
and
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s}(A)
. In view of Lemma
5.1
, it is sufficient to prove the preceding assertion for all generators of
\Gamma_{r}(A)
. Let
v=\epsilon_{1}(a,d)
for some
a\in A
and
d\in\Gamma_{r-1}(A)
, where
\epsilon_{1}\in X
. If
\epsilon_{1}=*
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{a*d}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{a^{-1}(a\circ d)d^{-1}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}\circ a\circ d\circ a^{-1}}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}\circ a\circ d}(wu),\mbox{ where $w\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-1}(A)$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}\circ a}\big(yw\;xu\big),\mbox{ where $y\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A),\;\;x\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r+1}(A)$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}}\big(\lambda_{a}(ywxw^{-1})\lambda_{a}(wu)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}}\big(\lambda_{a}(y[w,x]^{\cdot}x)x^{-1}(xu)\big),\mbox{ since $\lambda_{a}(wu)=u$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}}\big(\lambda_{a}(y[w,x]^{\cdot})\lambda_{a}(x)x^{-1}(xu)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}}\big(\lambda_{a}(yz_{1})z_{2}\big)\lambda_{d^{-1}}(xu),\mbox{ where $z_{1},z_{2}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d^{-1}}\big(\lambda_{a}(yz_{1})z_{2}\big)u,\mbox{ since $\lambda_{d^{-1}}(xu)=u$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle bu,\mbox{ where }b=\lambda_{d^{-1}}\big(\lambda_{a}(yz_{1})z_{2}\big)\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A).

Note that the preceding identity holds for any
a\in A
and
d\in\Gamma_{r-1}(A)
. Thus there exists
b_{1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
such that

\lambda_{a^{-1}*d^{-1}}(u)=b_{1}u.

Thus, by Lemma
5.1
, we get

\lambda^{-1}_{[a,d^{-1}]^{\cdot}}(u)=\lambda^{-1}_{[a,d^{-1}]^{\cdot}}(b_{1}^{-1})u.

Now, if we take
\epsilon_{1}=\bar{*}
, then, again using Lemma
5.1
, we get

\lambda_{a\bar{*}d}(u)=\lambda_{d*a}(u)=\lambda_{[d^{-1},a]^{\cdot}}(u)=\lambda^{-1}_{[a,d^{-1}]^{\cdot}}(u)=\lambda^{-1}_{[a,d^{-1}]^{\cdot}}(b_{1}^{-1})u,

where
\lambda^{-1}_{[a,d^{-1}]^{\cdot}}(b_{1}^{-1})\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
. Finally, the cases
\epsilon_{1}=\gamma_{.},\bar{\gamma}_{.}
follow from the identity
\lambda_{a*d}=\lambda_{[a^{-1},d]^{\cdot}}
. The proof is now complete.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
A
be a symmetric skew brace. Then
A\in\tilde{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
4.8
, to prove that
A\in\bar{\mathcal{I}}_{n}
, it is sufficient to prove that
\epsilon(u,v)\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
, for all
\epsilon\in X
, integers
s\geq r\geq 1
,
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s}(A)
and
v\in\Gamma_{r}(A)
.
We prove this result using induction on
r
. The case
r=1
holds true by the definition of the annihilator series. Assume that the assertion holds for all values
\leq r-1
given any
s\geq r-1
. Let
u\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s}(A)
and
v\in\Gamma_{r}(A)
,
s\geq r\geq 1
. Write
v=\epsilon_{1}(a,d)
, where
d\in\Gamma_{r-1}(A)
. By our assumption
\epsilon(u,d)\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r+1}(A)
.
The case
\epsilon=\bar{*}
has already been handled in Lemma
5.3
. The cases
\epsilon=*
and
\epsilon_{1}=\gamma_{\cdot},*
have been taken care of in Lemma
4.4
. There is word of caution on the use of Lemma
4.4
. Although the domain of
v
is bigger in the present situation, the computations are precisely the same. The cases
\epsilon=*
and
\epsilon_{1}=\bar{\gamma}_{\cdot},\bar{*}
will follow along similar lines, and are therefore left for the reader as an easy exercise.

The cases
\epsilon=\gamma_{\cdot}
and
\epsilon_{1}=\gamma_{\cdot},*
have also been handled in Lemma
4.4
, and the cases
\epsilon=\gamma_{\cdot}
and
\epsilon_{1}=\bar{\gamma}_{\cdot}
will follow similarly. As above, some caution is required on the use of Lemma
4.4
.

Finally we take
\epsilon=\gamma_{\cdot}
,
\epsilon_{1}=\bar{*}
. Notice that

(5.5)

\displaystyle[\lambda_{d}(a),[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}\big([a,\lambda^{-1}_{d}([u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot})]^{\cdot}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}\big(\big[a,\lambda^{-1}_{d}([u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot})([u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot})^{-1}[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}\big]^{\cdot}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}\big([a,z_{1}[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}\big),\mbox{ where $z_{1}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}\big([a,z_{1}]^{\cdot}[z_{1},[a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}[a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{2}\big(\lambda_{d}([a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot})([a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot})^{-1}\big)[a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{2}z_{3}[a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot},

where
z_{2}z_{3}\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
. Now

\displaystyle[u,a\bar{*}d]^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,\lambda_{d}(a)a^{-1}]^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[u,\lambda_{d}(a)]^{\cdot}[\lambda_{d}(a),[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}([\lambda^{-1}_{d}(u),a]^{\cdot})[\lambda_{d}(a),[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}([a,[u,a^{-1}]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot})^{-1}([u,a]^{\cdot})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}([wu,a]^{\cdot})z_{2}z_{3}([u,a]^{\cdot})^{-1},\mbox{ (by \eqref{eqn1}) where $w\in\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r+1}(A)$ }

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}\big([w,[u,a]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot}[u,a]^{\cdot}[w,a]^{\cdot}\big)z_{2}z_{3}([u,a]^{\cdot})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}([w,[u,a]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot})\lambda_{d}([u,a]^{\cdot})\lambda_{d}([w,a]^{\cdot})z_{2}z_{3}([u,a]^{\cdot})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}([w,[u,a]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot})(\lambda_{d}([w,a]^{\cdot})z_{2}z_{3})^{\lambda_{d}([u,a]^{\cdot})}\lambda_{d}([u,a]^{\cdot})([u,a]^{\cdot})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{d}([w,[u,a]^{\cdot}]^{\cdot})\big(\lambda_{d}([w,a]^{\cdot})z_{2}z_{3}\big)^{\lambda_{d}([u,a]^{\cdot})}(d*[u,a]^{\cdot}),

which lies in
\operatorname{Ann}_{s-r}(A)
, since

Proposition 5.8
.

Let

be a symmetric skew brace with a sub-skew brace

and let

be an ideal of

. Then

(1)

is a sub-skew brace of

. We start by noting that

.

,

6.
Embedding theorem for isoclinic skew braces

Catching the vibes from the preceding section, in this section we investigate embedding theorems for
n
-isoclinic skew braces. For
n=1
, we could prove the result analogous to group theory results from
[

]
in full generality. But for
n\geq 2
, we could only do so for symmetric skew braces, which are analogous to the results of
[
27
]
. First we take the case
n=1
and prove Theorem C.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be isoclinic skew braces. Then there exists a skew brace
W
with sub-skew braces
\bar{A}
and
\bar{B}
such that
A\cong\bar{A},~B\cong\bar{B},
and
\bar{A}\operatorname{Ann}(W)=\bar{A}\circ\operatorname{Ann}(W)=W=\bar{B}\circ\operatorname{Ann}(W)=\bar{B}\operatorname{Ann}(W)
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi,\theta)
be a skew brace isoclinism. Let

C:=\{(a,b)\in A\times B\mid\xi(\operatorname{Ann}(A)a)=\operatorname{Ann}(B)b,~a\in A,~b\in B\}.

It is not very difficult to prove that
C
is a sub-skew brace of
A\times B
. Consider the maps
\pi_{A}:C\to A,~\pi_{B}:C\to B
defined by
\pi_{A}(a,b)=a
and
\pi_{B}(a,b)=b
respectively. It is clear that
\pi_{A},\pi_{B}
are brace homomorphisms; since
\xi
is an isomorphism, these are surjective brace homomorphisms. Set
N_{2}:=\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{A})
and
N_{1}:=\operatorname{Ker}(\pi_{B})
. It follows that

N_{1}=\{(a,1)\in C\mid a\in\operatorname{Ann}A\},~N_{2}=\{(1,b)\in C\mid b\in\operatorname{Ann}B\}.

The structure of
\Gamma_{2}(C)
can be simplified as follows.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(C)

\displaystyle=\langle[C,C]^{\cdot},C\ast C\rangle^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=\langle[(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})]^{\cdot},(a_{1},b_{1})\ast(a_{2},b_{2})\mid(a_{i},b_{i})\in C\rangle^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=\langle([a_{1},a_{2}]^{\cdot},[b_{1},b_{2}]^{\cdot}),(a_{1}\ast a_{2},b_{1}\ast b_{2})\mid(a_{i},b_{i})\in C\rangle^{\cdot}

\displaystyle=\{(a,\theta(a))\mid a\in\Gamma_{2}(A)\}.

Set
Y:=C/{N_{2}}\times C/{\Gamma_{2}(C)}
and

N:=\langle\big(N_{2}(a,1),\Gamma_{2}(C)(a,1)\big)\mid(a,1)\in N_{1}\rangle^{\cdot}.

We claim that
N
is an ideal of
Y
. Since
N_{1}
is an ideal of
C
, we have
ctc^{-1},~c\circ t\circ\bar{c}\in N_{1}
, on the other hand
\Gamma_{2}(C)ctc^{-1}=\Gamma_{2}(C)t=\Gamma_{2}(C)(c\circ t\circ\bar{c})
for all
c\in C
and
t\in N_{1}
, it follows that
N
is a normal subgroup of
Y
with respect to ‘
\cdot
’ as well as ‘
\circ
’. A routine check gives invariance under the
\lambda
map.

Let
W:=Y/N
. We now prove that the skew left braces
A
and
B
embed in
W
. For any
(a,b_{1}),(a,b_{2})\in C
,
(a,b_{1})(a,b_{2})^{-1}=(1,b_{1}b_{2}^{-1})\in N_{2}
. Thus the map
\rho_{A}:A\to W
given by

\rho_{A}(a)=N\big(N_{2}(a,b),\Gamma_{2}(C)(1,1)\big)

is well defined. Now let
(a_{1},b),(a_{2},b)\in C
. Then
(a_{1},b)(a_{2},b)^{-1}=(a_{1}a_{2}^{-1},1)\in N_{1}
. Thus we get a well defined map
\rho_{B}:B\to W
given by

\rho_{B}(b)=N\big(N_{2}(a,b),\Gamma_{2}(C)(a,b)\big).

An easy check now establishes that
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
are brace homomorphisms.

We only prove that
\rho_{A}
is an embedding. Verification of the other case is left for the reader. Let
\rho_{A}(a)=1
for some
a\in A
. Then
\big(N_{2}(a,b),\Gamma_{2}(C)(1,1)\big)\in N
; so there exists an element
c\in\operatorname{Ann}(A)
such that
N_{2}(a,b)=N_{2}(c,1)
and
\Gamma_{2}(C)(1,1)=\Gamma_{2}(C)(c,1)
. Hence
(c,1)\in\Gamma_{2}(C)
. Since
\theta
is an isomorphism, we have
c=1
. This yields
(a,b)\in N_{2}
, that is,
a=1
. Hence
\rho_{A}
is an embedding.

It only remains to prove the last assertion. We only work it out for
(A,\cdot)
, that is,
\rho_{A}(A)\operatorname{Ann}(W)=W
.
Let
u=N\big(N_{2}(a_{1},b_{1}),\Gamma_{2}(C)(a_{2},b_{2})\big)\in W
be an arbitrary element. Then, obviously, we can write

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{2}(A)}

a\in A
, the map
\lambda^{op}_{a}
, defined by
\lambda^{op}_{a}(b):=\iota_{a}(\lambda_{a}(b))
for all
b\in A
, is an automorphism
(A,\cdot)
, where
\iota_{a}
denotes the inner automorphism of
(A,\cdot)
induced by
a
. Also the map
\lambda^{op}:(A,\circ)\to\operatorname{Aut}(A,\cdot)
such that
a\mapsto\lambda^{op}_{a}
is a group homomorphism, that is,
\lambda^{op}
is a group action of
(A,\circ)
on
(A,\cdot)
. Then we naturally get a group
\Lambda_{A}:=(A,\cdot)\rtimes_{\lambda^{op}}(A,\circ)
, the semi-direct product of
(A,\circ)
by
(A,\cdot)
under the action
\lambda^{op}
with the composition law given by
(a_{1},b_{1})(a_{2},b_{2})=\big(a_{1}\cdot\lambda^{op}_{b_{1}}(a_{2}),~b_{1}\circ b_{2}\big)
for
(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})\in\Lambda_{A}
.

Let
A
and
B
be two skew braces. Then, for a brace homomorphism
f:A\to B
, the map
\bar{f}:\Lambda_{A}\to\Lambda_{B}
, given by
\bar{f}(a_{1},b_{1})=(f(a_{1}),f(b_{1}))
for all
(a_{1},b_{1})\in\Lambda_{A}
, turns out to be a group homomorphism. Note that
\mathcal{SB}
, the class of all skew braces, forms a category. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the category of groups. Then we have

Proposition 7.1
.

The map
\Lambda:\mathcal{SB}\to\mathcal{G}
, given by
A\mapsto\Lambda_{A}
, is a functor. Moreover, brace isomorphisms are mapped to group isomorphisms.

The following definition of representation of a skew brace is introduced in
[
20
]
.
A triple
(V,\beta,\rho)
is said to be a
brace representation
of a skew brace
(A,\cdot,\circ)
if

(i)
V
is a vector space over a field
k
;

(ii)
\beta:(A,\cdot)\to GL(V)
is a representation of
(A,\cdot)
;

(iii)
\rho:(A,\circ)\to GL(V)
is a representation of
(A,\circ)

such that

(7.2)

\beta(\lambda^{op}_{a}(b))=\rho(a)\beta(b)\rho(a)^{-1}

holds for all
a,b\in A
.

For a given brace representation
(V,\beta,\rho)
of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
, we can naturally construct a representation
(V,\phi_{(\beta,\rho)})
of the group
\Lambda_{A}
by defining

\phi_{(\beta,\rho)}:\Lambda_{A}\to GL(V),\hskip 14.22636pt\phi_{(\beta,\rho)}(a,b)=\beta(a)\rho(b).

Conversely, if
\phi:\Lambda_{A}\to GL(V)
is a representation of the group
\Lambda_{A}
, then we can define
\beta,\rho
by setting

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle:(A,\cdot)\to GL(V),\hskip 28.45274pt\beta(a)=\phi(a,1)

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle:(A,\circ)\to GL(V),\hskip 28.45274pt\rho(b)=\phi(1,b)

so that
(V,\beta,\rho)
is a brace representation of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
.

Two representations
(V_{1},\beta_{1},\rho_{1})
and
(V_{2},\beta_{2},\rho_{2})
of
(A,\cdot,\circ)
are said to be
equivalent
if the corresponding representations
(V_{1},\phi_{(\beta_{1},\rho_{1})})
and
(V_{2},\phi_{(\beta_{2},\rho_{2})})
of
\Lambda_{A}
are equivalent.
We have proved

Proposition 7.3
.

The study of the brace representations of skew braces
(A,\cdot,\circ)
is equivalent to the study of the representations of corresponding natural semi-direct products
\Lambda_{A}
.

7.1.
n
-isoclinism of natural semi-direct product.

Let
I
be an ideal of a skew brace
A:=(A,\cdot,\circ)
. Then
\Lambda_{I}:=I\rtimes_{\lambda^{op}}I
is a normal subgroup of
\Lambda_{A}
. An easy check reveals that
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}(A)}\subseteq\operatorname{Z}(\Lambda_{A})
.
The following result follows from
[
12
, Proposition 3.3]
(also reproved in
[
22
, Theorem 3.7]
).

Lemma 7.4
.

For any skew brace
A
,
\gamma_{2}(\Lambda_{A})=\Gamma_{2}(A)\rtimes_{\lambda^{op}}\gamma_{2}(A,\circ)
.

We now generalize this for the further terms of the lower central series.
Set
L_{1}(A)=A
and define

\displaystyle K_{n}(A)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left<\lambda_{u}(b)b^{-1}\mid u\in\gamma_{n-1}(A,\circ),b\in A\right>^{\cdot},

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left<\lambda_{a}(v)v^{-1}\mid a\in A,v\in L_{n-1}(A)\right>^{\cdot},

\displaystyle L_{n}(A)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left<K_{n}(A),A_{n},[A,L_{n-1}(A)]^{\cdot}\right>^{\cdot}.

It follows from
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
that
L_{n}(A)
is a left ideal of
A
and it is a normal subgroup of
(A,\cdot)
. Notice that
L_{2}(A)=\left<A^{2},[A,A]^{\cdot}\right>^{\cdot}=\Gamma_{2}(A)
and
L_{n}(A)\subseteq\Gamma_{n}(A)
for all
n\geq 1
. We now prove

Lemma 7.5
.

\gamma_{n}(A,\circ)
is contained in
L_{n}(A)
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma_{2}(A)=L_{2}(A)
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that
\gamma_{2}(A,\circ)\subseteq L_{2}(A)
. By induction assume that
\gamma_{n-1}(A,\circ)\subseteq L_{n-1}(A)
. Let
u=[v,a]^{\circ}\in\gamma_{n}(A,\circ)
, where
v\in\gamma_{n-1}(A,\circ)
and
a\in A
. Now using the computation as done for the
n=2
case, we have

u=[v,a]^{\circ}=\lambda_{v}\big(\bar{v}^{-1}\bar{a}^{-1}\bar{v}\bar{a}(c^{-1}\lambda_{a}(c))\lambda_{a}(\bar{a}^{-1}\lambda_{\bar{v}}(\bar{a}))\big),

where
c:=\bar{a}^{-1}\bar{v}\bar{a}
. Notice that
[\bar{v}^{-1},\bar{a}^{-1}]^{\cdot}=([\bar{a}^{-1},\bar{v}^{-1}]^{\cdot})^{-1}\in[A,L_{n-1}(A)]^{\cdot}
,
c^{-1}\lambda_{a}(c)=(\lambda_{a}(c^{-1})c)^{-1}\in A_{n}
as
c\in L_{n-1}(A)
, and
\bar{a}^{-1}\lambda_{\bar{v}}(\bar{a})=(\lambda_{\bar{v}^{-1}}\bar{a})^{-1}\in K_{n}(A)
. Hence
\gamma_{n}(A,\circ)\subseteq L_{n}(A)
, and the proof is complete.
∎

The following can be verified easily.

Lemma 7.6
.

For any skew brace
A
, the sets
K_{n}(A)
and
\gamma_{n}(A,\cdot)
are left ideals of
A
, and both are normal in
(A,\cdot)
.

The next two results follow from
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 7.7
.

For any skew brace
A
, we have
[L_{n}(A),\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)]^{\cdot}=1
.

Lemma 7.8
.

For any skew brace
A
and
n\geq 2
,
\gamma_{n}(\Lambda_{A})=L_{n}(A)\rtimes_{\lambda^{op}}\gamma_{n}(A,\circ)
.

Notice that the set of commutator identities satisfied for an element of
A
to fall in
\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
contains the set of commutator identities satisfied for an element of
\Lambda_{A}
to fall in
\operatorname{Z}_{n}(\Lambda_{A})
. Thus it is not difficult to prove that

\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}\subseteq\operatorname{Z}_{n}(\Lambda_{A})
. We now prove that, under certain conditions,
n
-isoclinism is invariant under the functor
A\mapsto\Lambda_{A}
. This is Theorem E of the introduction.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
A
and
B
be two
n
-isoclinic skew braces in
\mathcal{I}_{n}
such that
A_{(n)}=\Gamma_{n+1}(A)
and
B_{(n)}=\Gamma_{n+1}(B)
. Then the groups
\Lambda_{A}
and
\Lambda_{B}
are
n
-isoclinic.

Proof.

We begin observing that the map

(7.10)

\psi^{n}_{A}:\Lambda_{A}\to\Lambda_{A/{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}},\hskip 14.22636pt(a,b)\mapsto\big(\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)a,\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)\circ b\big),

is a surjective group homomorphism with
\operatorname{Ker}(\psi^{n}_{A})=\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}
. Thus
\psi^{n}_{A}
induces an isomorphism
\bar{\psi}^{n}_{A}:\Lambda_{A}/\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}\to\Lambda_{A/{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}}
.

Let
(\xi,\theta)
be a brace
n
-isoclinism between
A
and
B
. As
\xi
and
\theta
are brace isomorphisms, by Proposition
7.1
, we get induced isomorphisms
\Lambda(\xi):\Lambda_{A/{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}}\to\Lambda_{B/{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)}}
and
\Lambda(\theta):\Lambda_{\Gamma_{n+1}(A)}\to\Lambda_{\Gamma_{n+1}(B)}
, respectively, given by

\displaystyle\Lambda(\xi)\big(\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)a_{1},\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)a_{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\xi(\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)a_{1}),~\xi(\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)a_{2})\big)

\displaystyle\Lambda(\theta)(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=(\theta(u_{1}),\theta(u_{2}))

for all
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
and
u_{1},u_{2}\in\Gamma_{n+1}(A)
.
We now set

\alpha:=(\bar{\psi}_{B}^{n})^{-1}\Lambda(\xi)\bar{\psi}_{A}^{n}:\Lambda_{A}/\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}\to\Lambda_{B}/{\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)}},

which is a brace isomorphism.
By Lemma
7.8
we have
\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{A})=L_{n+1}(A)\rtimes_{\lambda^{op}}\gamma_{n+1}(A,\circ)\subseteq\Lambda_{\Gamma_{n+1}(A)}
and
\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{B})=L_{n+1}(B)\rtimes_{\lambda^{op}}\gamma_{n+1}(B,\circ)\subseteq\Lambda_{\Gamma_{n+1}(B)}
. Since
(\xi,\theta)
is a brace isoclinism, it turns out that
\beta:=\Lambda(\theta)|_{\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{A})}
is a group isomorphism from
\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{A})
to
\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{B})
. A straightforward computation now shows that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Lambda_{A}/{\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}})^{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Lambda_{B}/{\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)}})^{n+1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{B}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\times\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

As noticed above,
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}
and
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)}
are subgroups of
\operatorname{Z}_{n}(\Lambda_{A})
and
\operatorname{Z}_{n}(\Lambda_{B})
respectively. It now follows by
[
15
, Lemma 3.11]
that
(\alpha,\beta)
is an isoclinism, which completes the proof.
∎

Note that the whole class of skew braces
A
falls in
\mathcal{I}_{1}
and
A_{(1)}=\Gamma_{2}(A)
. So we readily get

Corollary 7.11
.

Let
A
and
B
be two isoclinic skew braces. Then the groups
\Lambda_{A}
and
\Lambda_{B}
are isoclinic.

The concept of soluble and supersoluble skew left braces was introduced and investigated in
[
1
,
2
]
. It is proved that for a supersoluble skew brace
A
, if
A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
is finite, then
\Gamma_{n+1}(A)
is finite (
[
1
, Theorem 3.47]
). The group theory result holds for arbitrary
n
and the full class of groups, and it is due to R. Baer
[
3
]
. As a consequence of the discussion in this section, we get the following result.

Proposition 7.12
.

Let, for a given integer
n\geq 1
,
A
be a skew brace such that
A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
is finite. Then
L_{n+1}(A)
is finite. In particular, for
n=1
, we get: if
A/\operatorname{Ann}(A)
is finite, then
\Gamma_{2}(A)
is finite.

Proof.

If
A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
is finite, then
\Lambda_{A}/\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}
is finite. Since
\Lambda_{\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)}\subseteq\operatorname{Z}_{n}(\Lambda_{A})
, it follows that
\Lambda_{A}/\operatorname{Z}_{n}(\Lambda_{A})
is finite. Hence
\gamma_{n+1}(\Lambda_{A})
is finite. The proof now follows from Lemma
7.8
. The second assertion holds by the fact that
\Gamma_{2}(A)=L_{2}(A)
.
∎

The case
n=1
of the preceding result also follows from
[
16
, Theorem 5.4]
.

7.2.
Other invariance results.

We finally prove that various nilpotencies of skew braces are invariant under
n
-isoclinism. We actually prove little general results, which will imply invariance under
n
-isoclinism.

A skew brace
A
is
left nilpotent
, respectively
right nilpotent
, if there exists
n\in\operatorname{N}
such that
A^{n}=1
, respectively
A^{(n)}=1
. For
A
a skew brace, define
A^{[1]}=A_{1}=A
and for
n\geq 2
, iteratively define

\displaystyle A^{[n]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\bigcup_{i=1}^{n-1}A^{[i]}*A^{[n-i]}\Big\rangle^{\cdot},

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{n-1}*A_{n-1}.

Note that these are descending series’ of left ideals of
A
. If there exists
n\geq 1
such that
A^{[n]}=1
, then
A
is said to be

strongly nilpotent
. If there exists
n\geq 1
such that
A_{n}=\{1\}
, then
A
is said to be
*
-soluble
.

Strong nilpotency can be characterized through left and right nilpotency, as was first proved in
[
24
]
for braces
and in
[
7
]
for skew braces in general.

Theorem 7.13
.

For a skew brace
A
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is strongly nilpotent.

(2)

A
is left and right nilpotent.

The following result will show the invariance of the desired classes of skew braces under
n
-isoclinism in
\mathcal{I}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
.

Proposition 7.14
.

Let
A
and
B
be two skew braces such that
A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
is brace isomorphic to
B/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)
for some
n\geq 1
. Then the following hold:

(1)

A
is right nilpotent if and only if
B
is right nilpotent.

(2)

A
is left nilpotent if and only if
B
is left nilpotent.

(3)

A
is nilpotent if and only if
B
is nilpotent.

(4)

A
is strongly nilpotent if and only if
B
is strongly nilpotent.

(5)

A
is
*
-soluble if and only if
B
is
*
-soluble.

Proof.

We only prove assertion (1); the remaining ones can be proven analogously. Indeed, (4) follows from (1), (2) and Theorem
7.13
.

Suppose that
A
is right nilpotent. Then, it follows that
A/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(A)
is right nilpotent (for an arbitrary surjective brace homomorphism, it follows from
[
7
, Lemma 2.5]
that the image of a right nilpotent skew brace is right nilpotent). Therefore, by the given hypothesis,
B/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)
is right nilpotent as well. Thus there exists
k\in\operatorname{N}
such that
(B/\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B))^{(k)}=\{1\}
, or equivalently,

B^{(k)}\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B).

By the definition of
\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)
, we have

B^{(k+1)}=B^{(k)}*B\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{n}(B)*B\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{n-1}(B).

Repeating this reasoning
n
times, it follows that
B^{(k+n)}\subset\operatorname{Ann}_{0}(B)=1
. Hence
B
is right nilpotent.
∎

Remark 7.15
.

Most of the results analogous to the results of the paper

Remark 7.16
.

Concepts of monomial and strongly monomial skew braces can be introduced using Proposition
7.1

Question.

How big are the classes

,

Feasibility and impact of personalized therapy for obstructive sleep apnea in patients with acute coronary syndrome: a pilot randomized controlled trial
Authors: Benjamin K Tong, Glenn M Stewart, Hasthi Dissanayake, Anna Mohammadieh, Peter A Cistulli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecent neutral randomized controlled trials have created clinical equipoise around the treatment of obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) for the secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. These findings are in part limited by poor compliance, lack of treatment optimization and personalization strategies with OSA therapy. This pilot study aims to assess the feasibility and impact of a personalized OSA treatment program on therapy compliance and cardiovascular health in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) diagnosed with OSA.MethodsPatients with ACS and OSA (oxygen desaturation index 3% > 10 events/hour) will be recruited and randomized to receive either personalized OSA therapy (PT) or usual care (UC) with six-month follow-up. The PT group will receive standard medical care for ACS plus the choice of continuous positive airway pressure and/or mandibular advancement device therapy for OSA. Treatment optimization strategies including treatment acclimatization, augmented support and patient engagement tools will be implemented. The UC group will receive standard medical care for ACS but no treatment for OSA. The primary outcome is the feasibility of personalized treatment for OSA assessed by study and treatment acceptance, and treatment compliance. Secondary outcomes include treatment effectiveness, cardiovascular health markers and patient reported outcomes across the six-months intervention.DiscussionThis study will provide novel findings on the impact of personalized and optimized OSA therapy on patient compliance, preliminary treatment effectiveness and health outcomes in patients with established cardiovascular disease. Such novel data will inform the design and delivery of future large scale randomized clinical trials to assess cardiovascular disease benefit in patients with OSA.Trial registrationACTRN 12620000050954. Background

Current evidence suggests that obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is an independent risk factor for acute coronary syndrome (ACS) [1]. The prevalence of OSA among patients with ACS is estimated to be approximately 60–80% [2, 3]. Several observational studies have shown that patients with ACS and OSA have poorer prognosis compared to those without OSA [4]. Recurrent episodes of intermittent hypoxia, sympathetic activation and intrathoracic pressure swings are mechanistic pathways in which OSA may exacerbate prognostic outcomes in patients with ACS.

Effective treatment of OSA with Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) and Mandibular Advancement Devices (MAD) have been shown to improve health and quality of life outcomes, which include improvements in daytime sleepiness, cognitive function and cardiovascular outcomes [5–7]. In contrast, recent randomized controlled trials have reported neutral findings for CPAP therapy on its impact on the incidence of both fatal and non-fatal cardiovascular events in patients with established cardiovascular disease [8–10]. Importantly, these randomized controlled trials are limited by 1) suboptimal CPAP usage (ranging from 2.8–3.3 h per night), leaving participants mostly untreated for much of their total sleep time; 2) a focus on monotherapy (CPAP only) for the treatment of OSA; 3) poor implementation or lack of treatment optimization strategies to maintain optimal treatment compliance. Hence, there is equipoise about the role of effective CPAP treatment for the secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease.

A novel observation from previous randomized controlled trials is that patients with established cardiovascular disease are not adherent to CPAP therapy. Indeed, CPAP adherence among these patients tends to deteriorate over time with only 40% of patients using CPAP for at least 4 h per night [11]. Furthermore, current studies have mostly implemented monotherapy options for individual patients, specifically CPAP only or MAD only, or a comparison between the two [6, 8–10, 12]. MAD has recently been shown to be non-inferior to CPAP in improving blood pressure [12] and may have better treatment acceptance among cardiovascular patients [13]. While OSA is recognized as a heterogeneous disorder and personalizing treatment for OSA has been advocated to achieve better patient centered outcomes [14], there have been no studies utilizing a personalized OSA therapy strategy to target cardiovascular health outcomes in patients with OSA.

The overarching objective of our study is to develop an evidence-based framework for the design of future randomized controlled trials of OSA intervention for primary or secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease in patients with OSA. The primary aim of this study is to evaluate the feasibility and impact of delivering a personalized and optimized OSA therapy approach to optimize treatment acceptance and compliance in patients with ACS. We hypothesize that this strategy will result in high acceptance and compliance (ie. greater than 5 h per night of usage) with OSA treatment. The secondary aim is to determine the preliminary effectiveness of personalized and optimized OSA therapy in reducing cardiovascular risk in patients with ACS.

Methods/Design

Study design

This pilot study is a prospective, open-label, multi-center, parallel-group, randomized controlled intervention study of optimized and personalized OSA therapy (CPAP and/or MAD) versus usual care (control group). The study sponsor is the University of Sydney, Camperdown, Australia. The current protocol (version 3.5, 12 August 2024) was prepared in accordance with The Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials 2013 statement [15]. Participants

Participants aged between 18 to 75 years old presenting to the coronary care unit and cardiac rehabilitation clinics of tertiary teaching hospitals (Royal North Shore Hospital, St Leonards, New South Wales, Australia and Royal Prince Alfred Hospital, Camperdown, New South Wales, Australia) will be screened for eligibility if they suffered a recent episode of ACS, defined as ST-elevated myocardial infarction (STEMI), non-ST-elevated myocardial infarction (non-STEMI), or unstable angina or stable coronary artery disease, and remain within one to twelve months of hospital discharge. Participation is voluntary and information about the study will initially be verbally communicated to the participant. If the participant provides verbal consent, an enrolment visit will be arranged. The participant will be asked to formally provide written consent in the presence of a witness during the enrolment visit, once the investigator is satisfied that the participant meets the study inclusion criteria. The enrolment visit and data collection will occur at the Charles Perkins Centre, Camperdown, New South Wales, Australia. The inclusion and exclusion criteria for this study are detailed in Table 2.

Participant screening

Prospective participants with a recent diagnosis of ACS will be invited to have a single-night home-based level 2 (Nox A1, Nox Medical, Reykjavik, Iceland) or level 3 (ApneaLink, ResMed, San Diego, CA, USA) polysomnogram to screen for OSA. Participants who are screened for OSA with a level 3 polysomnogram will undergo a home-based level 2 polysomnogram at the baseline visit for complete sleep study data. Signals recorded using the level 2 polysomnography will include electroencephalogram, electro-oculogram, submental electromyogram, electrocardiography, pulse oximetry, respiratory thoracoabdominal movements, nasal airflow, snore sound and body position. Signals recorded using the level 3 polysomnography will include nasal airflow, respiratory thoraco-abdominal movement, pulse oximetry, and body position. Participants will be provided with instructions to wear the polysomnography device. The polysomnography device will be returned and subsequently downloaded and analyzed by a single highly experienced sleep technologist blinded to the study protocol and participant details. Polysomnography recordings will be deemed valid if there is at least six hours of recording time and adequate signal quality of physiological measures collected. The polysomnogram will be analyzed for sleep–wake states and respiratory events according to the American Academy of Sleep Medicine criteria [16]. We will recruit participants with OSA (apnea–hypopnea index [AHI] > 10 events per hour) and oxygen desaturation index 3% (ODI3%) greater than 10 events per hour (to ensure a degree of hypoxic burden).

Randomization

Randomization will be performed using a random number sequence generated in Stata (version 17, StataCorp, TX, USA) by a study investigator not involved in participant recruitment or data collection. The random allocated sequences are stored in a password protected spreadsheet only accessible by that study investigator. Randomization allocation will be provided to the study coordinator once eligible participants are formally enrolled into the study. Once a randomization allocation has been used, it may not be reused. The study coordinator will inform the participant and study physician of the allocated study group.

A 2:1 randomization ratio to either personalized OSA treatment group or usual care group will be employed to allow subgroup analysis with the personalized OSA intervention arm (CPAP versus MAD). The aim will be to have a minimum of 40 completers in the personalized OSA intervention arm, with an expected dropout rate of 20%.

Intervention

Personalized OSA treatment group

Participants randomized to the OSA treatment group will be given standard medical care for ACS and the option of CPAP and/or MAD for OSA treatment based on sleep physician recommendations and patient preference. Both therapy modalities will be optimized for each participant using best-practice strategies and augmented support during a rapid titration run-in period of four weeks prior to commencing the six-month intervention period. A change in therapy modality will be permitted during the run-in period should the initial therapy modality fail. Supine avoidance therapy (NightShift™ Sleep Positioner, Advanced Brain Monitoring, Carlsbad, CA, USA) will be implemented if treatment AHI is not less than 10 events per hour on initial therapy modality. Patients are defined to be acclimatized to OSA treatment if treatment AHI is less than 10 events per hour and therapy compliance is more than five hours per night with at least ten nights of use during a two-week period in the run-in period. Therapy failure will be arbitrarily defined as a treatment AHI of greater than 10 events per hour and/or therapy compliance of less than five hours per night. Participants failing both therapy modalities will be withdrawn from the study.

CPAP therapy

Participants opting for CPAP therapy will be provided an auto-titration CPAP (ResMed AirSense 10, ResMed, San Diego, CA, USA) to use for the duration of the study. An experienced sleep therapist will provide an in-person CPAP education session which includes therapy education, mask fitting, set up of patient engagement tool (myAir, ResMed, San Diego, CA, USA) and CPAP device care. Participants will be allowed to acclimatize to CPAP therapy over the run-in period. Augmented support will be provided to each participant, including adjustment of CPAP settings in accordance with participant feedback and objective therapy data. Regular follow-up via phone calls includes therapy usage feedback and encouragement will be performed during the first week of therapy, subsequently additional augmentation support will be provided if patients experience issues with their CPAP treatment such as high leak, condensation or low usage. Face to face visits will be performed for issues which cannot be resolved over the phone or CPAP mask related issues. These follow-up calls and in person visits will be documented.

A rapid treatment acclimatization methodology will be implemented during the run-in period with the following criteria: CPAP usage per night is greater than two hours per night in the first week, greater than three hours per night in the second week, greater than four hours per night in the third week and greater than five hours per night in the fourth week. Participants failing to meet these criteria will be provided augmented support which include trialing different masks and adjustment of CPAP settings to improve CPAP usage. If CPAP usage fails to improve (based on above criteria) following additional intervention, participants will be offered oral appliance therapy or combination therapy (CPAP and MAD and/or supine avoidance therapy for supine dependent OSA).

Ongoing remote support will be maintained during the six-month intervention period. Remote monitoring of CPAP therapy data will be through a cloud-based patient management system (ResMed AirView, ResMed, San Diego, CA, USA). A validated side-effects to CPAP treatment questionnaire will be administered monthly to assess the impact of side-effects on CPAP usage [17]. Patient engagement tools (myAir, ResMed, San Diego, CA, USA) will be used to encourage patients to maintain engagement with their CPAP therapy during the six-month intervention period. Patient engagement features of the ResMed myAir app include tailored patient coaching on CPAP use and care with instructional material, and objective feedback based on therapy usage to encourage and motivate positive therapy behavior. Participants will also receive a daily myAir score calculated based hours of therapy use, mask fit (measured as mask leak), AHI on therapy, and frequency of mask on/off during the night and digital reward badges are awarded within the app when compliance milestones are achieved [18]. Participants will be required to complete an end of study questionnaire at the end of the intervention period to assess their experience of using CPAP.

Mandibular advancement device

Participants who choose MAD will be provided a custom-made, two-piece, titratable MAD (Narval CC, ResMed, Saint-Priest, Lyon, France or SomnoMed Avant, SomnoMed, Crows Nest, NSW, Australia). The MAD will be delivered by an experienced dentist. This will involve assessing the participant’s suitability to wear the device, taking a digital dental scan of suitable participants to fabricate the device, assessing device fit and instructing the participant on device use, titration and care. This will occur over a minimum of two in-person visits. Participants who are contraindicated for MAD will be offered CPAP therapy.

Participants will acclimatize to the MAD over a period of four weeks. During this period, participants will self-titrate the MAD up to maximum tolerable mandibular advancement as determined by the study dentist. Participants are expected to achieve at least 75% of maximum mandibular advancement during titration and device usage of greater than two hours per night in the first week, greater than three hours per night in the second week, greater than four hours per night in the third week and greater than five hours per night in the fourth week. This will be monitored via a device use diary. Although technology to monitor objective device use exists [19], this was not available for this study. Participants failing to meet these criteria will be provided additional support with the study dentist to resolve potential side-effects or device fit issues to improve device use. If device usage fails to improve (according to the above criteria) or if the participant does not tolerate the device, participants will be offered CPAP therapy.

Once the participant has achieved maximum tolerable mandibular advancement and acclimatized to the MAD, a level 3 sleep study (ApneaLink, ResMed, San Diego, CA, USA) will be conducted to assess MAD treatment efficacy. Participants are expected to achieve a treatment AHI of less than 10 events per hour and meet the acclimatization criteria. Participants who meet these criteria will begin the six-month intervention period while those who do not will be offered supine avoidance therapy (if there is supine dependent OSA) or CPAP therapy. Daily device usage and side-effects will be monitored using a participant diary and device-related side-effects questionnaire. Participants will be required to complete an end of study questionnaire at the end of the intervention period to assess their experience of using MAD.

Control group

Usual care will represent the control group in this study. Participants randomized to this group will be provided with lifestyle modification advice including sleep hygiene and weight loss, and will receive conventional medical care by their cardiologist and primary care physician. This group will not receive specific treatment for OSA during the six-month period of the study. The study physician will assess the suitability of participants in this group to withhold treatment for OSA based on driving risk due to severe daytime sleepiness, severe hypoxemia and other clinical reasons requiring immediate treatment for OSA. Participants who are deemed not suitable to withhold treatment for OSA in this group by the study physician will be excluded from the study and referred to a clinical pathway for ongoing management. At the conclusion of the study, participants within the usual care group will be reviewed by a sleep physician and offered a trial of therapy for OSA.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome of this study is the feasibility of personalized OSA therapy as assessed by study and treatment acceptance, and treatment compliance.

These target thresholds are intended to be ambitious and represent a significant improvement over previously reported results from studies which did not implement a personalized therapy model, showing that only 36% to 42% of patients achieving good compliance (≥ 4 h usage per night) [8, 9].

Secondary outcome measures are: 1) treatment efficacy, measured by the reduction in AHI from baseline; 2) measures of cardiovascular function which include 24-h ambulatory blood pressure measurements (OSCAR 2 Ambulatory Blood Pressure Monitor, SunTech Medical, North Carolina, USA), arterial stiffness measurements (SphygmoCor® XCEL, Cardiex, New South Wales, Australia) and heart rate variability, all assessed at baseline and at the end of the six-month intervention period; 3) polysomnographic derived measures of cardiovascular risk including hypoxic burden [20] and pulse wave amplitude drops [21], which will be assessed at baseline and at the end of the six-month intervention period; 4) patient reported outcomes of daytime sleepiness from the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS), disease specific health status from the Seattle Angina Questionnaire (SAQ-7), general health and mental status from the 36-item Short-Form health survey (SF-36), dietary habits from the diet quality tool questionnaire (DQT), smoking, alcohol consumption, medication intake, recent medical diagnosis of cardiovascular disease and/or other medical conditions, which will be assessed monthly during the six-month intervention period. Physical activity from the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ) and OSA related symptoms from the STOP-BANG and Berlin questionnaire will be assessed at baseline and at the end of the six-month intervention period; 5) Utilization of resources to manage the optimization of OSA treatment will be assessed through the types of augmentation strategies implemented to optimize OSA treatment specifically CPAP mask changes, adjustments to CPAP settings, interventions to resolve side-effects to CPAP or MAD, frequency of consultations with research coordinator, sleep physician and/or dentist, modes of consultation (ie. face to face or phone consultation), duration required for patients to achieve treatment acclimatization, number of patients switching treatment modalities and number of patients failing OSA treatment. Exploratory outcome measures will include blood biomarkers to quantify cardio-metabolic risk and inflammation. A summary of study outcomes and endpoints are detailed in Table 3.

Sample size

This study will derive effect sizes for outcome measures to inform future clinical trials. We chose a total sample size of at least 60 completing participants, with a minimum of 40 completers in the PT group and a minimum of 20 completers in the UC group. A minimum sample size of 35 participants is estimated to detect a small effect size in compliance to therapy for OSA and cardiovascular function measures while using a conservative approach for estimating standard deviation (using at least 80% upper one-side confidence limit, rather than the estimate itself) to enable sample size calculation for a 90% powered trial [22, 23]. The choice of 2:1 randomization was to enable comparison of CPAP and MAD treatment effects in the personalized treatment arm.

Participant withdrawals occurring within the study will be replaced until the target sample size is achieved in the personalized OSA therapy arm.

Statistical analysis

Outcome measures will be analyzed according to intention to treat principles. Descriptive statistics will be used to describe demographic, clinical characteristics and baseline outcome measures for both UC and PT groups.

The strategy of delivering personalized and optimized OSA therapy to optimize treatment acceptance and compliance in patients with ACS will be deemed feasible if all three measures of study acceptability, treatment acceptance, and optimal compliance to personalized therapy meets the predefined target threshold of ≥ 65%. Descriptive statistics will be used to describe objective CPAP and MAD compliance represented by mean hours of usage per night and number of nights on therapy. The number of participants who are randomized, achieving ‘optimal compliance’, requiring additional treatment augmentation interventions, switching treatment modality and requiring adjunctive therapy, and failing OSA therapy will be reported as count and percentage. Change in secondary outcome measures including AHI, 24-h ambulatory blood pressure, arterial stiffness, heart rate variability, hypoxic burden, pulse wave amplitude drops (PWAD) and patient reported outcomes at baseline and at 6 months in both UC and PT groups will be conducted using pairwise comparisons. Linear mixed models will be used to assess the effect of 6-month intervention (baseline vs 6 month) and the effect of personalized OSA therapy (UC vs PT) on cardiovascular outcomes. Effect size on cardiovascular outcomes in the PT group will also be adjusted for comorbidities, and medications using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA).

Ethical, monitoring and safety considerations

The study has been approved by the Sydney Local Health District Human Research Ethics Committee (X19-0245 & 2019/ETH11850). The study was registered on the Australian and New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry: ACTRN12620000050954. Any modifications to the protocol including changes to eligibility criteria and study procedures will first be submitted to the Human Research Ethics Committee for approval. Study investigators will be notified of new protocol modifications and receive the updated approved study protocol, new participants will be issued with the updated approved participant information and consent forms. Any approved protocol modifications will also be communicated with the trial registry.

A primary ethical concern for this study is the question of withholding treatment for OSA in high-risk patients (ie. driving risk due to sleepiness, need for immediate therapy for OSA) randomized to usual care. This risk is managed through an initial consultation between the participants and study physician prior to enrolment into the study. If the study physician deems the patient to be at risk if CPAP/MAD treatment for OSA is delayed for six months, the patient will be excluded from the study.

A data and safety monitoring committee will not be needed for this study as it involves 1) the use of approved conventional therapies (ie. CPAP and MADs) for the treatment for OSA in the PT group; 2) standard medical care for ACS is maintained in both groups which will be independently managed by the participants’ cardiologist.

Participants in this study will be actively monitored throughout the duration of the study using a range of patient reported outcome questionnaires and usage of CPAP and MADs are monitored using online cloud-based platforms, patient usage diaries and telephone check-ins. Participant’s progress throughout the trial are reviewed by study investigators with expertise in sleep medicine, dental sleep medicine, cardiovascular disease in fortnightly meetings. Any abnormal medical findings from this study will be communicated to and reviewed by the study physician to determine the safety of continuing within the trial, and results forwarded accordingly to the participant’s primary care provider and cardiologist.

Investigators will report any adverse events in the study as they occur. Participants are also encouraged to report any adverse events to investigators who would report these through appropriate channels. Contact details of investigators are available to patients on the patient information form. Any reports for adverse events are reviewed by the study chief investigator and study physician and action taken as required. Any serious adverse events will be reported to the ethics committee within twenty-four hours. Reports to the ethics committee will be completed annually.

Data collection and management

Data collected within this study will be managed according to the Australian Code for the Responsible Conduct of Research: Revision of the Joint NHMRC/AVCC Statement and Guidelines on Research Practice [24] and patient data and identifiable information will be kept confidential according to the CPMP/ICH Note for Guidance on Good Clinical Practice [25].

Data will be collected in written and electronic formats. Written data with identifiable information will be stored in a locked cabinet. Electronic data will be deidentified and stored on a coded electronic database and a password protected data server hosted at the University of Sydney. Deidentified data will be stored in a secure electronic database (RedCAP) hosted at the University of Sydney.

The final study dataset will be accessible only to study investigators and maintained for at least 15 years after completion of the study. Results and findings from the study will be disseminated through conferences and peer-reviewed journals in the sleep and cardiovascular fields.

Discussion

This pilot study has been developed to assess the feasibility and impact of a novel personalized treatment strategy on OSA therapy compliance in patients with ACS. This approach directly addresses a key limitation of previous neutral randomized controlled trials [8–10], with the ultimate aim of informing future randomized clinical trials for the management of cardiovascular disease in OSA. This study includes several novel features: 1) It is the first study to incorporate personalized and optimized therapy for OSA in the intervention arm of a randomized clinical trial in patients with established cardiovascular disease. Both first- and second-line therapies for OSA will be offered based on participant’s preference in consultation with a sleep physician. This is in line with current recommended guidelines [26]. Notably, the current study incorporates the option for participants to switch or combine therapy modalities should they not achieve a therapeutic outcome following treatment augmentation strategies. 2) Rapid acclimatization and titration of OSA therapy will be implemented to efficiently identify participants who are not achieving optimal treatment and to rapidly intervene until optimal treatment is achieved. Long term CPAP and MAD compliance can be predicted within the first three to twelve weeks of therapy [27, 28] hence a period of four weeks was considered appropriate for rapid acclimatization and titration of OSA therapy. 3) Ongoing treatment optimization strategies including regular participant follow-up and contact during first-week of therapy initiation, and use of active patient engagement tools with remote therapy monitoring and support will be implemented to improve treatment compliance [29]. 4) The randomization of participants with a 2:1 ratio to personalized optimized therapy and usual care will enable assessment of patient choices and their change over time, as well as allow for subgroup analyses between both treatment modalities (i.e., CPAP and/or MAD).

Previous randomized clinical trials have demonstrated suboptimal CPAP usage among their study populations which likely contributed to neutral results [30]. Furthermore, current data show that patients with established cardiovascular disease tend to remain non-compliant to CPAP therapy over the long term [11]. A recent workshop report has called for novel and adaptive trial designs to address limitations of previous clinical trials [31]. This study will focus on the feasibility of implementing personalized and optimized OSA therapy in a clinical population of established cardiovascular disease.

We will be studying patients with a recent diagnosis (ie. within one to twelve months of hospital discharge) of ACS with OSA. Indeed, OSA is more severe immediately post myocardial infarction, however restoration of cardiac function occurring within seven days post infarct stabilizes the presence and severity of OSA [32]. The prevalence of OSA in patients with ACS is high and untreated OSA may result in poorer health outcomes for this patient population [33]. Our recent work in this area estimated an OSA prevalence of 94% in patients with ACS [3]. Markers of cardiovascular risk including 24-h ambulatory blood pressure [34], blood biomarkers [35] and heart rate variability [36] have been shown to improve in patients with OSA with optimal use of CPAP therapy. It is not clear if this effect is mirrored in patients with established cardiovascular disease. A goal of this pilot study is to define which cardiovascular measures demonstrate a clinically meaningful treatment effect.

Although this pilot study has some novel features, there are some limitations which need to be acknowledged. First, our inclusion criteria using the ODI3% greater than 10 events per hour may include patients with mild OSA. This was done to facilitate patient recruitment. We anticipate the proportion of patients with mild OSA in this cohort will be low given that our estimated prevalence of moderate to severe OSA in the same clinical population to be 68% [3]. Indeed, the literature has described a potential cardioprotective effect through ischemic preconditioning in patients with mild OSA. However, compliance with OSA treatment in patients with mild OSA remains low [37]. Nonetheless, the primary goal of this study remains to assess patient acceptance and compliance to personalized OSA therapy. Patients randomized to the personalized therapy arm are expected to achieve a residual AHI less than 10 events per hour and show they are compliant to treatment for OSA. Secondly, treatment efficacy for patients using CPAP or MAD during the run-in period is assessed using different methodologies where residual AHI for CPAP is derived from the CPAP machine and MAD is assessed using a level 3 polysomnogram with MAD in-situ. The residual AHI derived from CPAP machines have been shown to be comparable to polysomnography [38]. In addition, this methodology for assessment of treatment efficacy for OSA is in line with current practice guidelines [39, 40].

In conclusion, the outcomes from this pilot study will provide novel findings on the feasibility and impact of personalized and optimized therapy for OSA in patients with established cardiovascular disease, and specifically whether this results in higher treatment compliance and improved health outcomes. Results from this study will also provide insights into the effect of personalized therapy for OSA on markers of cardiovascular function and provide preliminary data of potential outcome measures to inform future clinical trials of cardiovascular disease prevention in patients with OSA.

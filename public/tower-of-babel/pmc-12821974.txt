MicroRNA-107 alleviates ferroptosis-mediated acute kidney injury by regulating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway
Authors: Pengfei Zhao, Zhenghua Wu, Hua Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMechanistic pathways and biomarkers need to be explored to elucidate the pathogenesis of acute kidney injury (AKI). This study examined miR-107 expression in AKI and the molecular mechanisms underlying the regulation of ferroptosis-mediated AKI.MethodsThis study included 30 patients with AKI and 30 healthy individuals in the control group. The miR-107 serum expression levels were determined using real-time quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). The HK-2 cells were subjected to ferroptosis in vitro, and a AKI mice model to study the effect of miR-107 in vivo. After RNA overexpression, malondialdehyde (MDA), lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS), and glutathione (GSH) levels were measured. Additionally, the regulatory relationship between miR-107 and its downstream pathway genes was analyzed.ResultsThe down-regulated expression of miR-107 detected in the serum of patients with AKI suggested its utility as a potential diagnostic indicator for AKI. Moreover, miR-107 expression was down-regulated in both HK-2 cell models of AKI and HK-2 cell ferroptosis model. Upregulation of miR-107 decreased MDA expression and increased GSH expression in erastin (Era)-induced ferroptosis in HK-2 cells in vitro. Additional, AKI mice exhibited down-regulated expression levels of miR-107, and the ROS, MDA, and GSH expression changes in AKI mice were rescued after miR-107 overexpressed, which was consistent with the effect of Fer-1 treatment. Furthermore, the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway exhibited a correlation with miR-107 expression in Era-induced ferroptosis in HK-2 cells.ConclusionIn summary, miR-107 is expressed at low levels in AKI and can inhibit ferroptosis in HK-2 cells, which may play a protective role against AKI.Keywords: Acute kidney injury, miR-107, Ferroptosis, PI3K/Akt/mTOR

Background

More than 2 million people die each year from acute kidney injury (AKI), and the mortality rate continues to increase [1]. Current therapeutic interventions for AKI include adequate hydration, blood volume circulation, and prevention of renal toxins [2]. Currently, no effective measures for preventing or treating AKI are available. Additionally, the mechanisms underlying renal injury are not fully understood. These factors make the prevention and treatment of AKI challenging. Therefore, it is necessary to identify the molecules that play key roles in AKI and their underlying mechanisms.

Cell death plays a vital role in normal cellular development and homeostasis. The histological features of AKI include tubular cell death and inflammation, which play a central role in the pathogenesis of AKI [3]. Ferroptosis, a term introduced by Brent R Stockwell in 2012, is characterized by the iron-dependent accumulation of lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) [4]. Ferroptosis has been implicated in various pathological processes, including cancer, AKI, neurodegenerative diseases, ischemia/reperfusion injury (IRI), and T cell immunity [5]. Notably, the kidneys appear to be the most sensitive of all tissues to ferroptosis. Ferroptosis in mouse renal tubule cells has shown to induce acute renal failure in mice [6]. In addition, ferroptosis can directly cause tubular necrosis in IRI-induced AKI mice models [7]. At the cellular and animal levels, genetic knockout of ACSL4 prevents AKI by inhibiting ferroptosis and inflammation [8]. Therefore, ferroptosis is gaining prominence as a promising therapeutic target for AKI.

Notably, changes in microRNAs (miRNAs) can lead to alterations in the expression of target genes [9]. Although the abnormal expression of miRNAs is usually mild, it may lead to dramatic changes in the target gene expression, which may have a profound impact on the development of the disease, thereby affecting various physiological functions of the body [10]. Thus, miRNAs have the potential to serve as biomarkers and therapeutic tools for AKI [11]. Among these, miR-107 is expressed at low levels in patients with AKI [12]. Research on miR-107 had mainly focused on cardiovascular diseases and tumor development [13, 14]. MiR-107 promotes the growth and metastasis of gastric cancer by activating the PI3K/AKT pathway, which may be a therapeutic target for gastric cancer [14]. However, the pathway regulated by miR-107 in AKI remains unclear. Studies have shown that activation of the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway can inhibit cell ferroptosis [15]. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to explore the potential of miR-107 in alleviating ferroptosis-mediated AKI by regulating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway.

Methods

Patient samples

A total of 30 patients with AKI from Shanxi Bethune Hospital were enrolled and assigned to the AKI group. In parallel, 30 healthy individuals from our hospital during the same period were enrolled and assigned to the healthy group. All participants were rigorously screened for clinical characteristics of AKI, and those presenting with other causes were excluded [16]. Before treatment, patients’ serum samples were collected from peripheral blood and immediately stored at − 80 °C until use. All the participants provided written informed consent. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanxi Bethune Hospital (Ethical Application: SBQLL-2023-023).

Cell culture and treatments

HK-2 cells were procured from the China Cell Bank and cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO2 in DMEM/F12 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and subcultures every 2–3 days for subsequent experiments. Following the protocol from a published research for H/R [17], the cells were cultured in an anoxic environment with 1% O2 for 24 to 48 h, and then transferred to a normoxic environment with 21% O2 and evaluated at different time points (0,1,3, and 6 h). In addition, based on the published methodologies [18], erastin (Era) was used at a concentration of 1 μm to induce cell ferroptosis. For the rescue experiment, ferroptosis inhibitor, Fer-1, (1 lM) was added simultaneously with Era. MiR-107 mimic (miR-107) and negative control (mimic-NC) were obtained from Riobo. HK-2 cells were transfected with miR-107 mimic for overexpression, and the transfected HK-2 cells were dissolved in F12 medium containing 10% FBS without antibiotics.

Establishment of animal model

The 8-week-old adult male C57BL/6J mice with a body weight of approximately 20–22 g were randomly divided into control (n = 6) and AKI (n = 24) groups; there was no significant difference in body weight (P > 0.05). All experimental and animal care procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanxi Bethune Hospital (Ethical Application: SBQLL-2023-023) and conformed to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals produced by the National Institutes of Health.

A mouse model of AKI was established according to a previously reported protocol [15]. Briefly, before operation, miR-107 agonist (2.5 mg/kg) or Fer-1 (5 mg/kg) was administered. The mice were anesthetized and both kidneys were exposed through a lateral ventral incision. Then the mice were placed on a thermostatic plate and the renal pedicle was clamped with a non-invasive clamp to induce ischemia for 30 min. Ischemia induction was not performed in the sham operation group. Blood samples were obtained from the tail veins of rats, and serum was collected and stored at − 80 ℃. At the end of the experiment, rats were euthanized in a CO2 chamber. The mice were sacrificed using a CO2 chamber at an air rate to fill 40% volume every minute. The terminal death was reassured by no sign of movement and the mice were further cervically dislocated. Tissue sections were used for qRT-PCR, and the remaining sections were used for western blotting. Individual levels of MDA and GSH in kidney tissue were measured using MDA and GSH activity assay kits respectively according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Lipid ROS levels were determined using BODIPY-C11 dye as previously reported [19].

Real-time quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells using the TRIzol method. Reverse transcription was performed using the Fermentas reverse transcription reagent and Applied Biosystems ®TaqMan® RNA reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA). The ABI StepOnePlus system (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA) was used for amplification under predetermined conditions. The primer sequences used were as follows: miR-107 forward, 5′-AGCAGCATTGTACAGGGCTATCA-3′; miR-107 reverse, 5′-CTCAACTGGTGTCGTGGA-3′.

Detection of glutathione (GSH) levels

According to the manufacturer’s instructions (Beyotime), cells were rinsed once with PBS, centrifuged, and the resultant supernatant was collected. Subsequently, the protein removal reagent M solution, three times the cell sedimentation volume, was added and the mixture was fully vortexed. Samples were then subjected to two rapid freeze-thaw cycles using liquid nitrogen and a 37 °C water bath and left for 5 min in an ice bath. The samples were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 min to separate the supernatant. A 96-well plate was used, and the sample or standard was added and mixed. Subsequently, 150 µL of total GSH detection working solution was added and thoroughly mixed and incubated at 25 °C or for 5 min at room temperature. The reaction temperature was set at 25 °C and the absorbance was measured at 412 nm continuously for 10 min or automatically recorded every 2 min using a microplate reader. The GSH content was calculated from the reaction curve.

Detection of malondialdehyde (MDA) levels

Cell lysis was performed, followed by addition of the reaction reagent to facilitate its interaction with MDA in the sample using thiobarbituric acid (TBA) to generate an MDA-TBA adduct. The MDA levels were calculated by measuring the absorbance of the MDA-TBA adducts at a wavelength of 532 nm using a microplate reader.

Detection of lipid reactive oxygen species levels

Cells were initially seeded into 6-well plates and the following day, the original medium was substituted with a fresh medium containing 5 µM BODIPY-C11 probe and incubated for 20 min. Cells were collected in 15 ml centrifuge tubes, washed and suspended with PBS, and subsequently filtered through a 0.4 μm nylon mesh. The fluorescence intensity of each cell was measured using a BD FACSAria flow cytometer (BD Biosciences), enabling the quantification of lipid ROS levels.

Western blot

Western blotting was performed for cells or tissues as previously described [20]. Primary antibodies anti-AKT (ab38449), anti-pAKT (ab8805), and anti-GAPDH (ab8245) (1:1000; Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) were used in the present study. GAPDH was used as the internal control.

Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining

Part of the renal tissue was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, dehydrated with alcohol, made transparent with xylene, embedded in paraffin, and prepared as stone wax sections. The slices were stained with hematoxylin for approximately 30 min, stained with eosin staining solution for 1 min, rinsed, dehydrated, sealed with transparent neutral gum, and observed morphologically.

Immunohistochemistry

The tissues collected from each group were fixed in paraformaldehyde for 24 h and embedded in paraffin. The sections were washed with PBS and incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with an anti-MDA (1:200; ProteinTech Group, Chicago; USA) or anti-GSH antibody (1:200; ProteinTech Group, Chicago; USA). Subsequently, the sections were washed thrice in PBS for 5 min each and incubated with biotinylated goat anti-mouse IgG (1:1000; Abcam, China) at room temperature for 20 min. The sections were washed with PBS, and avidin-horseradish peroxidase complex was added (SABC Kit; Bost, Wuhan, China). Next, 3,3-diaminobenzidine was used as a chromogen. The reaction was terminated by adding water. Finally, the sections were washed, dehydrated, and visualized under a light microscope.

Transmission electron microscopy

About (2–6) × 106 treated cells were collected and fixed in 2.5% fixing solution. After rinsing with PBS, the sample was fixed in 50 µL of 1% osmium for 1 h, then fixed/stained with 100 µL 2% urea acetic acid solution for 30 min. Next, the sample is dehydrated with a series of different concentrations of ethanol and 100% acetic acid. After embedding, ultra-thin slices are cut, stained and viewed under a transmission electron microscope.

Treatment with small-molecule inhibitor

The cells were treated with small molecule inhibitors of PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling to detect the functional changes of miR-107 after the signaling pathway was inhibited. These inhibitors include: the PI3K inhibitor, pictilisib (GDC-0941, HY-50094; MCE), AKT inhibitor, MK-2206 (HY-108232; MCE), the mTOR inhibitor, temsirolimus (CCI-779, HY-50910; MCE).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the GraphPad Prism 8.0 software (GraphPad Software, Inc.). Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The Student’s t-test was used for comparisons between the two groups. Differences among multiple groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance. Differences were considered statistically significant at P values < 0.05.

Results

MiR-107 expression was downregulated in patients with AKI, H/R-induced AKI cell models and in era-induced ferroptotic cells

The expression of miR-107 has been reported to be significantly reduced in patients with AKI [12]. In this study, the expression level of miR-107 in patients with AKI was assessed to determine its potential as a diagnostic indicator. The results revealed that miR-107 expression in the peripheral blood samples of patients with AKI was significantly downregulated (Fig. 1a). In addition, the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was > 0.80, suggesting that miR-107 could serve as a diagnostic marker for AKI (Fig. 1b). The observed downregulation in expression was further verified in H/R-induced AKI cell models (Fig. 1c). Renal tubular cell ferroptosis plays an important role in the pathogenesis of AKI [21]. To investigate the role of miR-107 in HK-2 cell ferroptosis, we initially examined the changes in the expression of miR-107 in cells subjected to Era-induced ferroptosis. The results indicated a downregulation of miR-107 expression in ERA-treated cells (Fig. 1d).

Downregulated expression of miR-107 in patient with AKI. (a) Serum levels of miR-107 in 30 patients with AKI and 30 healthy individuals. (b) Area under the ROC curve analysis of miR-107 in patients with AKI. (c) and (d) miR-107 expression in H/R-induced AKI cell and Era-induced cell ferroptosis is measured using PCR. *: P < 0.05, **: P < 0.01, ***: P < 0.0001

Overexpression of miR-107 inhibited ferroptosis in HK-2 cells

Subsequently, we upregulated the expression of miR-107 in the cells undergoing ferroptosis to explore its mechanism of action. After transfection with the miR-107 mimic, a significant upregulation of the mimic expression was observed in the ferroptotic cells (Fig. 2a). Lipid peroxidation is a feature of ferroptosis; therefore, we measured cellular lipid-related products and ROS. MDA and GSH play key roles in maintaining a balance between oxidation and reduction. In Era-induced ferroptosis of HK-2 cells, an increase in MDA expression and a decrease in GSH expression were observed. Notably, MiR-107 demonstrated the ability to reverse this alteration in expression. This change was consistent with the action of Fer-1, a selective inhibitor of ferroptosis (Fig. 2b and c). Moreover, lipid ROS levels were elevated in Era-induced ferroptotic cells, and miR-107 altered this change, producing an effect similar to that of Fer-1 (Fig. 2d). Furthermore, we observed by transmission electron microscopy that the mitochondrial outer membrane ruptured, membrane density increased, and mitochondrial ridge decreased after Era treatment alone. This is a typical electron microscopic feature of ferroptosis. The changes of mitochondrial morphology after overexpression of miR-107 were significantly improved, which was the same as the result of Fer-1 treatment (Fig. 2e).

Significant inhibition of ferroptosis in HK-2 cells by miR-107. (a) Expression of miR-107 after transfecting Era-induced ferroptotic HK-2 cells with miR-107 mimic. (b) and (c) Levels of MDA and GSH levels in HK-2 cell. (d) Lipid ROS level was assayed by flow cytometry using C11-BODIPY. (e) Mitochondrial morphology observed by transmission electron microscopy. *: P < 0.05, **: P < 0.01, ***: P < 0.0001

Overexpression of miR-107 alleviated ferroptosis-mediated acute kidney injury in AKI mice

We first detected the expression level of miR-107 in AKI mice. The results showed that miR-107 expression in the peripheral blood samples of AKI mice was significantly downregulated (Fig. 3a). Subsequently, we found that miR-107 expression was significantly upregulated in AKI mouse tissues after treatment with miR-107 agonist, which was consistent with the effect of Fer-1 treatment (Fig. 3b). In addition, the ROS, MDA, and GSH expression changes in AKI mice were rescued after miR-107 overexpressed, producing effects similar to those of Fer-1 treatment (Fig. 3c, d and e). Consistently, immunohistochemical results showed that miR-107 significantly increased MDA and GSH (Fig. 3f). Moreover, HE staining showed that renal tubule dilation, brush edge shedding, inflammatory cell infiltration and tubular formation in AKI rats. However, miR-107 can effectively improve the pathological changes of AKI group rats, and the effect is the same as that of Fer-1 treated AKI rats (Fig. 3g).

Significant inhibition of ferroptosis in AKI mice by miR-107. (a) Serum levels of miR-107 in AKI mice. (b) Expression of miR-107 after treatment with miR-107 agonist or Fer-1. (c) and (d) Levels of MDA and GSH in mice. (e) Lipid ROS level was assayed by flow cytometry using C11-BODIPY. (f) Immunohistochemistry staining reflected the expression of MDA and GSH in kidney. (g) HE staining showed renal tubule expansion and tubular cast. *: P < 0.05, **: P < 0.01, ***: P < 0.0001

MiR-107 inhibited ferroptosis in HK-2 cells through the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway

To determine the renal protective mechanism of miR-107 against ferroptosis, we examined the downstream PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway of miR-107. Following miR-107 overexpression, the levels of PI3K, p-Akt, and p-mTOR proteins were increased, whereas the levels of Akt and mTOR proteins remained unchanged (Fig. 4a). Subsequently, the significantly elevated PI3K, p-Akt, and p-mTOR levels were eliminated by separately adding pathway inhibitors (Fig. 4b, c, and d). In addition, the inhibitory effect of miR-107 overexpression on MDA levels in ferroptotic cells was eliminated by the separate addition of pathway inhibitors (Fig. 4e). This protective mechanism was also evident when the overexpression of miR-107 interfered with the expression of GSH and lipid ROS in ferroptotic cells (Fig. 4f and g).

Inhibition of ferroptosis in HK-2 cells by miR-107-induced activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. (a) Western blot analysis of total protein and phosphorylated protein levels for the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway following treatment with miR-107. (b-d) Western blot analysis of total protein and phosphorylated protein levels for the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway following treatment with pictilisib (10 µM), MK-2206 (5 µM), and temsirolimus (100 nM). (e) and (f) Levels of MDA and GSH in HK-2 cell following treatment with pictilisib (10 µM), MK-2206 (5 µM), and temsirolimus (100 nM). (g) Lipid ROS level was assayed by flow cytometry using C11-BODIPY following treatment with pictilisib (10 µM), MK-2206 (5 µM), and temsirolimus (100 nM)

Discussion

The expression patterns of miRNAs are dynamic and reflect changes in the internal and external cellular environments. Therefore, miRNAs can be used as potential biomarkers capable of representing a specific disease state or pathophysiological process at certain stages of disease development [22, 23]. Dysregulation of miRNAs is directly involved in AKI progression [24]. Previous analyses have shown that miR-107 is underexpressed in patients with AKI. In this study, serum miR-107 levels were significantly downregulated in patients with AKI. Additionally, the area under the ROC curve underscored the diagnostic potential of miR-107 as a valuable biomarker for AKI.

Ferroptosis, a mode of regulatory cell death that is different from apoptosis, has been reported to occur in various acute and chronic kidney diseases [25]. Emerging evidence supports the key role of ferroptosis in the pathophysiology of AKI, indicating its potential as a promising target for AKI treatment [18]. Sideroptosis is closely associated with regulation of lipid and GSH metabolism. In lipid metabolism, membrane lipid polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) can promote iron sag through a series of reactions, thereby affecting the transmembrane properties of PUFAs, resulting in phospholipids consumption, lipid peroxides accumulation, and lipid peroxides formation, such as MDA. GSH is an important antioxidant in the body, which can assist GPXs to scavenge lipid reactive oxygen species. GPX4, as a member of the GPXs family, is one of the important regulatory proteins for ferroptosis. Studies have shown that the inactivation of GPX4 can induce acute renal injury by triggering ferroptosis in mouse renal tubule cells [6]. In the present study, we observed that miR-107 overexpression inhibited ferroptosis by regulating the levels of MDA and GSH, suggesting that miR-107 has a protective effect on ferroptosis-mediated AKI.

Ferroptosis is driven by the impaired activity of the lipid repair enzyme GPX4, leading to accumulation of lipid ROS. The ferroptosis inhibitor, Fer-1, have shown to prevent AKI [21]. Specifically, Era inhibits cystine uptake via cystine/glutamate antitransporters and interacts with voltage-dependent anion channels 2 and 3, thereby disrupting cell’s antioxidant defense mechanism and promoting ROS production, resulting in ferroptosis [4]. Therefore, ferroptosis depends on the accumulation of intracellular ROS. In this study, the level of lipid ROS in Era-induced ferroptotic cells increased, which was subsequently eliminated by miR-107 overexpression. Based on our results, we speculated that the effect of miR-107 on AKI is closely associated with the inhibition of ferroptosis.

The PI3K/AKT /mTOR pathway is involved in various pathological changes, such as inflammatory responses and oxidative stress injury, and is a classic regulatory pathway of the inflammatory response [26]. It has also been identified as a target for AKI treatment [17]. In addition, studies have shown that the activation or inhibition of the PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling axis renders cells resistant or sensitive to ferroptosis induction [27]. MiR-107 has demonstrated to have the ability to activate the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway in various pathologic processes [14, 28]. In this study, we observed that the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway was inhibited in ferroptotic cells, and this inhibition was counteracted by the overexpression of miR-107. In addition, after treatment with pathway inhibitors, the therapeutic effects of miR-107 overexpression on ferroptotic cells were inhibited. In summary, this study suggested that the protective effect of miR-107 in ferroptotic cells is mediated through the regulation of PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling. These results demonstrate promising therapeutic prospects for addressing ferroptosis-mediated AKI in preclinical cell models.

This study had some limitations. First, the sample size utilized for the experimental serum analysis was small. Secondly, the protective effects of miR-107 were explored only at the cellular level. In addition, the potential of miR-107 to inhibit ferroptosis through alternate mechanisms is yet to be thoroughly investigated. It is interesting to explore whether miR-107 alleviates acute kidney injury by regulating a classical ferroptosis-related pathway. These gaps in knowledge will be explored and addressed in future studies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that miR-107 is an effective inhibitor of ferroptosis in HK-2 cells and suggested that miR-107 exerts a protective effect against AKI by inhibiting ferroptosis. In addition, we observed that miR-107 affects ferroptosis by regulating the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway. This study serves as the first step towards developing a treatment approach for AKI that targets ferroptosis and provides a basis for developing new treatment strategies for diseases associated with ferroptosis.

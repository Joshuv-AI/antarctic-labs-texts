Characterization of superficial reticular corneal degeneration (a variant of band-shaped keratopathy) in eyes treated for retinopathy of prematurity
Authors: Sunita Chaurasia, Subhadra Jalali, Saumya Jakati, Muralidhar Ramappa
Published: 2026

Dear editor,

Superficial reticular corneal degeneration, first described by Koby, is recognized as a distinct clinical entity characterized by a thickened and speckled epithelium exhibiting a brown discoloration within the central cornea.[123] The degeneration manifests without inflammation or pain with resultant gradual deterioration of vision and is recognized as a variant of band-shaped keratopathy. The clinical condition can be confused with corneal dystrophy owing to its bilaterality and involvement of the central cornea overlying the pupillary axis.

Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), initially described as “retrolental fibroplasia” by Theodore Terry in 1942, affects premature infants. The condition is multifactorial and manifests as abnormal retinal vascularization that can lead to retinal detachment and incurable blindness. The management of the disease has evolved over last few decades and is based on the stage, location, and its severity, with various modalities and combinations of treatment including cryotherapy, laser photocoagulation, intravitreal anti-VEGF injections, and vitreo-retinal surgeries.

In this study, we describe eight patients who had features suggestive of Koby’s superficial reticular corneal degeneration. All patients had a common history of treatment for ROP in the past. The clinical features and anterior segment optical coherence tomography (AS-OCT) features are described here to recognize the existence of this entity in association with treated ROP eyes.

The patients who had distinctive clinical features of superficial reticular corneal degeneration were studied and analyzed. The institutional review board approved this prospective database (LEC 08088). Informed consent was obtained from the parents of the children, and the study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

We identified eight patients who presented with the clinical description typical of superficial reticular corneal degeneration between May 2024 and September 2025 during their ongoing follow-up care visits. The demographics, clinical history, past treatment for ROP, and management were reviewed from medical records. A detailed clinical evaluation was performed along with slit lamp photo documentation and AS-OCT. Patients with mild symptoms were managed by observation and 6 monthly evaluations. Those who were severely symptomatic due to photophobia and decreased vision were treated surgically with phototherapeutic keratectomy or debridement with application of 3% ethylene diamine tetra acetic acid chelating agent. Histological analysis was performed in some of the eyes that underwent surgical management of the condition and had samples of debrided epithelium sent to the pathology services.

Clinical characteristics of the of eight patients with superficial degeneration of Koby

The median age of the eight patients at the time of diagnosis of the corneal condition was 15 (IQR 8.5–20.5) years. There were four females and four males. Relevant history, as noted in the past ROP treatment records, revealed that the mean gestational age was 30.25 weeks at birth (range 28–36 weeks) and the mean birth weight was 1462.5 grams (range 1100–1800 grams). The corneal involvement was noted bilaterally in all except two patients (Patients 6 and 8). Two patients were referred to the cornea clinic with an impression of corneal dystrophy and the rest six with a diagnosis of corneal opacity. All had complaints of photophobia, and six patients who had bilateral involvement also mentioned a gradual decrease in vision. The mean visual acuity was 20/60 (range 20/400 to 20/40). The degeneration was noted within the central 8 mm of cornea with involvement of the visual axis and sparing of the peripheral cornea in all eyes. Of the 14 affected eyes, five eyes of three patients also had peripheral lenticular opacities, which was not significant to warrant surgery. The past management history of ROP included laser photocoagulation in all the 16 eyes (including two eyes with no corneal pathology currently), and additional treatments including anterior retinal cryopexy in three eyes, anti-VEGF injections in five eyes, and buckle surgery for retinal detachment in one eye. There were no systemic metabolic ailments noted in the patients. Routine blood investigations such as serum calcium, phosphate, parathormone levels, and alkaline phosphatase were performed in four patients, which was in normal range of the reference values. The parental and family history was unremarkable for any corneal disease.

Seven eyes underwent surgical intervention for the corneal degeneration at the institute, which included phototherapeutic keratectomy in two eyes of one patient and 3% ethylenediamine tetra acetic acid (EDTA) chelation in five eyes of four patients.

Slit lamp examination showed that the deposits were present in the superficial layers of the cornea. The underlying stroma and the Descemet membrane endothelial complex were normal. The AS-OCT delineated the level of deposits in the anterior layers as hyper-reflectivity at the level of Bowman’s layer corroborating with the clinical findings on slit lamp examination. There was a shadow effect posterior to the hyper-reflective deposits in the subepithelial layer.

During surgery, the epithelium was first treated with proparacaine eyedrops for 1 minute to facilitate its removal. Thereafter, 3% EDTA was applied for 1 minute at the location of deposits, followed by saline irrigation and debridement. Intraoperatively, an identifiable layer (presumably Bowman’s layer) with a well-defined cleavage plane that could be peeled off from the underlying stromal bed was noted in three eyes. The samples sent for histology showed epithelial cells and positivity for calcium deposits on von Kossa staining in two samples [Fig. 6a-d].

Superficial reticular corneal degeneration is a sparsely reported entity in the literature. Following its initial description by Koby,[1] it was presumed to be a type of lattice corneal dystrophy.[2] However, subsequent studies identified this condition sharing similarities with as an atypical variant of calcific band keratopathy.[3456] The entity has been reported earlier anecdotally in patients with chronic vitritis, ocular trauma with associated aphakia, and sarcoidosis.[3456]

Band-shaped keratopathy is a degenerative corneal condition that is caused by precipitation of calcium in the Bowman’s layer with identifiable local and systemic risk factors such as eyes with posterior segment chronic conditions, hypercalcemic states, and renal disease.[7] Typically, band-shaped keratopathy presents in the interpalpebral region beginning with the deposits in the peripheral cornea that progress centrally.[7] In contrast, all cases of superficial reticular degeneration of Koby are noted in the central cornea with reticulations and a brownish sheen. The association of superficial reticular degeneration in eyes treated for ROP in the past has not been widely recognized. Although it is common to see typical calcific band keratopathy in our clinics, the superficial reticular corneal degeneration variant form is not a common occurrence and has been seen only with eyes with a history of treatment for ROP.

Our study illustrates this distinct clinical description in eyes that had undergone management of ROP in the past. None of the patients had any other significant past or ongoing systemic disease history that is associated with calcific band keratopathy. Additionally, the parents had normal corneas with no history of corneal disorder in the family ruling out the possibility of commonly known epithelial-stromal corneal dystrophies that have a familial basis and affect the superficial layers of the cornea. Histology was confirmatory in four eyes in ruling out dystrophy and confirmed the presence of calcium deposits in the two eyes.

All eyes had laser interventions as a part of initial management of ROP, with additional retinal interventions such as anterior retinal cryopexy, and intravitreal injections. The majority (75%, 6/8 patients) had bilateral corneal involvement of varying severity. It is felt that the condition may possibly occur in some eyes due to laser-induced corneal changes, along with consequences attributed to changes in ocular metabolic factors in the long term. The baseline risk factors for its occurrence in ROP-treated eyes are to be investigated and studied further.

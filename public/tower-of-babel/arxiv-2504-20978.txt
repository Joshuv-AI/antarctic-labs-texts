Coloring graphs as complete graph invariants
Shamil Asgarli, Sara Krehbiel, Howard W. Levinson

Abstract
. We investigate the extent to which the k k -coloring graph 𝒞 k ​ ( G ) \mathcal{C}_{k}(G) uniquely determines the base graph G G and the number of colors k k . The vertices of 𝒞 k ​ ( G ) \mathcal{C}_{k}(G) are the proper k k -colorings of G G , and edges connect colorings that differ on exactly one vertex. There are nonisomorphic graphs G 1 G_{1} and G 2 G_{2} with isomorphic coloring graphs, so 𝒞 k ​ ( G ) \mathcal{C}_{k}(G) is not a complete invariant in general. However, for color palettes with surplus colors (when the number of colors k k is greater than the chromatic number), we prove that the coloring graph is a complete invariant. Specifically, provided that k 1 > χ ⁡ ( G 1 ) k_{1}>\chi(G_{1}) , we show that 𝒞 k 1 ​ ( G 1 ) ≅ 𝒞 k 2 ​ ( G 2 ) \mathcal{C}_{k_{1}}(G_{1})\cong\mathcal{C}_{k_{2}}(G_{2}) implies G 1 ≅ G 2 G_{1}\cong G_{2} and k 1 = k 2 k_{1}=k_{2} . Thus, there is a natural bijection between pairs ( G , k ) (G,k) with k > χ ⁡ ( G ) k>\chi(G) and their coloring graphs 𝒞 k ​ ( G ) \mathcal{C}_{k}(G) . Furthermore, no coloring graph of the form 𝒞 χ ⁡ ( G ) ​ ( G ) \mathcal{C}_{\chi(G)}(G) is isomorphic to a coloring graph with surplus colors. Our constructive proof provides a method to decide whether a coloring graph is generated with surplus colors, although the resulting algorithms are inefficient.

1.
Introduction

The difficulty of the graph isomorphism problem motivates the study of
graph invariants
: properties preserved under isomorphism. Graph colorings provide a rich source of such invariants. A
proper
k
-coloring
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a function
c\colon V\to\{1,\dots,k\}
such that
c(u)\neq c(v)
whenever
uv\in E
. The minimum such
k
is the
chromatic number
, denoted
\chi(G)
. One graph invariant is the
chromatic polynomial

\pi_{G}(k)
, which counts the number of proper
k
-colorings. This paper focuses on a richer structure: the
k
-coloring graph
, denoted
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
. The vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
are the proper
k
-colorings of
G
, and two vertices are adjacent if the corresponding colorings differ on exactly one vertex of
G
. This construction places
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
within the broader context of a
reconfiguration graph
, where edges represent a minimal transition between valid states.

We study the conditions under which
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
is a complete graph invariant, uniquely determining both
G
(up to isomorphism) and the number of colors
k
. If
k<\chi(G)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
is the null graph and yields no information. If
k=\chi(G)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
is not a complete invariant in general. For instance, any two uniquely
k
-colorable graphs share the same
k
-coloring graph
N_{k!}
(where
N_{r}
denotes the graph with
r
vertices and
0
edges). This leaves the third case
k>\chi(G)
, namely, when there are
surplus colors
. The question of complete invariance of coloring graphs was formalized in our joint work
[
2
]
with Russell:

Does there exist a function
f\colon\mathsf{Graphs}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
G
can be uniquely reconstructed from
\mathcal{C}_{f(G)}(G)
?

Hogan, Scott, Tamitegama, and Tan
[
10
]
answered this question affirmatively by showing that the function
f(G)=5|V(G)|^{2}+1
works. We refine their result by proving that
f(G)=\chi(G)+1
suffices. Our main theorem asserts an even stronger conclusion: coloring graphs with surplus colors are distinguishable from all other coloring graphs, including those of the form
\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G)}(G)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If an abstract graph
\mathcal{C}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
for some graph
G
with
k>\chi(G)
, then for
any
graph
G^{\prime}
and positive integer
k^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
, we have
G\cong G^{\prime}
and
k=k^{\prime}
.

We emphasize that Theorem
1.1
is logically equivalent to the combination of two separate results:

(a)

If
\mathcal{C}_{k_{1}}(G_{1})\cong\mathcal{C}_{k_{2}}(G_{2})
with
k_{1}>\chi(G_{1})
and
k_{2}>\chi(G_{2})
, then
G_{1}\cong G_{2}
and
k_{1}=k_{2}
.

(b)

No two graphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
can satisfy
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
with
k>\chi(G)
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi(G^{\prime})
.

Part (
b
) turns out to be much more difficult than (
a
). We prove (
a
) in Theorem
4.4
using a reconstruction procedure outlined in Algorithm
1
. Proving (

) (in Theorem
4.28
) requires two additional algorithms and several other novel ideas. We also exhibit that

is not sufficient for

to be a complete invariant by proving the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\geq 3
, there exist graphs

\chi(G_{1})=\chi_{1}
and
\chi(G_{2})=\chi_{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{1}}(G_{1})\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{2}}(G_{2})
.

-townhouse graphs

, whose

-coloring graph

Definitions and Notation

Given a graph
G
and
k\geq\chi(G)
, the
k
-coloring graph
of
G
, denoted
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
, is defined as follows. The vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
represent proper
k
-colorings of
G
. Two vertices in
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
(representing two distinct
k
-colorings) are adjacent if the two colorings differ on exactly one vertex
v\in V(G)
. Formally, we have a bijection
\Phi\colon V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))\to\{\text{proper }k\text{-colorings of }G\}
defined by
\Phi(\alpha)=\alpha_{G}
such that
\alpha\beta\in E(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))
if and only if
\alpha_{G}(v)\neq\beta_{G}(v)
for exactly one
v\in V(G)
. That is, we use
\alpha
to denote a vertex in
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
and
\alpha_{G}\colon V(G)\to[k]
for the corresponding coloring of
G
. For simplicity, we typically omit the symbol
\Phi
and use the shorthand
\alpha\mapsto\alpha_{G}
for this mapping.

While
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
is fundamentally an abstract graph, we can use the underlying bijection between
V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))
and the
k
-colorings of
G
to produce an
edge-labeled
coloring graph. Each edge is labeled to show the unique vertex
v\in V(G)
where the two corresponding colorings differ and the specific colors assigned to
v
by each coloring. For example, an edge
\alpha\beta\in E(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))
might have the label
cvc^{\prime}
, which signifies that
\alpha_{G}(v)=c,\beta_{G}(v)=c^{\prime}
, and
\alpha_{G}(u)=\beta_{G}(u)
for all
u\in V(G)\backslash\{v\}
. We emphasize that we have access to neither edge labelings nor the
\alpha\mapsto\alpha_{G}
mapping when given an abstract coloring graph
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
.

3.
Coloring Graphs at the Chromatic Level May Have Multiple Base Graphs

Our main result, established in Section
4
, is twofold: first, surplus coloring graphs uniquely determine their base graphs; second, surplus coloring graphs are structurally distinct from
\chi
-coloring graphs. In contrast, two nonisomorphic graphs can produce isomorphic
\chi
-coloring graphs. For instance, all uniquely
k
-colorable graphs share the same
k
-coloring graph, namely the graph
N_{k!}
consisting of
k!
isolated vertices. This observation leads to a natural question: can
\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G_{1})}(G_{1})
be isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G_{2})}(G_{2})
when

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The case

and
G_{2}=K_{k+1}-e

\mathcal{C}_{k}(G_{1})\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G_{2})\cong N_{k!}
. Thus, we assume
\chi_{2}>\chi_{1}\geq 3
. Let

, there exists a graph
G_{1}
such that

and
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{1}}(G_{1})

which satisfies

, and
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{2}}(G_{2})
also consists of

isolated vertices. As
\chi(G_{1})\neq\chi(G_{2})
, the graphs

and
G_{2}
are nonisomorphic, but
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{1}}(G_{1})\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{2}}(G_{2})
. ∎

We discuss another consequence of Corollary
3.3
. While
\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G)}(G)
does not generally allow for the unique reconstruction of the base graph

\chi
-coloring graph. The next observation, which strengthens Theorem
1.2
, shows that the
\chi
-coloring graph cannot even detect connectivity of its base graph.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
\chi_{2}\geq\chi_{1}\geq 3
, there exists a disconnected
\chi_{1}
-chromatic graph
G_{1}
and a connected
\chi_{2}
-chromatic graph
G_{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{1}}(G_{1})\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{2}}(G_{2})
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.3
applied with
k=\chi_{1}
and
r=\chi_{2}!
, there exists a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi_{1}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{1}}(H)\cong N_{\chi_{1}!\cdot\chi_{2}!}
. Consider the disjoint union graph
G_{1}=H\uplus H
, which is disconnected, and satisfies
\chi(G_{1})=\chi_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{1}}(G_{1})\cong N_{\chi_{1}!^{2}\chi_{2}!^{2}}
. By Corollary
3.3
applied with
k=\chi_{2}
and
r=\chi_{2}!\cdot\chi_{1}!^{2}
, there exists a connected graph
G_{2}
with
\chi(G_{2})=\chi_{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\chi_{2}}(G_{2})\cong N_{\chi_{2}!\cdot(\chi_{2}!\cdot\chi_{1}!^{2})}=N_{\chi_{1}!^{2}\chi_{2}!^{2}}
.
∎

These results show that, in general, a graph cannot be uniquely reconstructed from its
\chi
-coloring graph; however, exceptions exist. Our next result demonstrates that the
3
-coloring graph of the
5
-cycle is unique among all
\chi
-coloring graphs.

Theorem 3.5
.

If
G
is a graph satisfying
\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G)}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{3}(C_{5})
, then
G\cong C_{5}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{C}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{C}_{3}(C_{5})
is the disjoint union of two copies of
C_{15}
. For any graph
H
, the number
\lvert V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(H))\rvert
is divisible by
k!
when
k=\chi(H)
. Since
\lvert V(\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G)}(G))\rvert=\lvert V(\mathcal{C})\rvert=30
is not divisible by
k!
for
k\geq 4
, it follows that
\chi(G)\leq 3
. Moreover, since
\lvert V(\mathcal{C})\rvert=30
is not a power of
2
, we have
\chi(G)>2
. Thus,
\chi(G)=3
.

First, we show that
|V(G)|\geq 5
. As
G
is
not
bipartite,
G
contains an odd cycle. If
G
contained a triangle, then
\mathcal{C}
would have at least
3!=6
connected components; indeed, a
3
-coloring of a triangle is locked and cannot change within a connected component of
\mathcal{C}
. However,
\mathcal{C}
has only two components. Thus,
G
contains an
\ell
-cycle where
\ell\geq 5
, so
|V(G)|\geq 5
.

Consider one of the
15
-cycles in
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}\subseteq V(G)
be the subset of vertices in
G
that achieve at least two different colors in the chosen 15-cycle in
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\lambda_{i}\geq 2
be the number of times vertex
v_{i}
changes color in this cycle, so that
(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r})
forms an integer partition of
15
. We claim that
\lambda_{i}\geq 3
for every
i
. If
\lambda_{j}=2
for some
j
, then vertex
v_{j}
is recolored (in this
15
-cycle) between only two colors, say
1\leftrightarrow 2
. Permuting these two colors with the third available color generates two
additional

15
-cycles in
\mathcal{C}
: one corresponding to
v_{j}
switching between
1\leftrightarrow 3
, and the other corresponding to
v_{j}
switching between
2\leftrightarrow 3
. Together with the original
15
-cycle, this would force
|V(\mathcal{C})|\geq 3\cdot 15=45
, a contradiction.

Combining
\lambda_{i}\geq 3
for all
i
and
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{r}=15
, we get
r\leq 5
. We claim that
r=5
. If
r\leq 4
, then
|V(G)|\geq 5
implies that there is a vertex

whose color is fixed in the

-cycle, say color
1
. Changing its color to

or

results in two additional

, a contradiction.

Thus,

, which combined with
\lambda_{i}\geq 3
for all

, forces
\lambda_{i}=3
for
1\leq i\leq 5
. We claim that
|V(G)|=5
. Indeed, any vertex

would have a fixed color in the
15
-cycle, say color

. Changing its color to
2
or
3
results in two additional
15
-cycles, implying
|V(\mathcal{C})|\geq 45
, a contradiction. Therefore,

and since

contains an
\ell

, we have

. ∎

Later, Theorem
4.28
will show that
\mathcal{C}_{3}(C_{5})\not\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
for any graph
G
with
k>\chi(G)
. Combining this fact with Theorem
3.5
, we conclude that
C_{5}
is the only graph having a coloring graph isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{3}(C_{5})
. More generally, it seems difficult to characterize graphs
G
that can be uniquely reconstructed from
\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G)}(G)
.

4.
Any Coloring Graph With Surplus Colors Has a Unique Base Graph

In this section, we establish Theorem
1.1
(our main result) by proving Theorems
4.4
and
4.28
, which respectively show that we can reconstruct base graphs from their coloring graphs with surplus colors, and that
\chi
-coloring graphs cannot be isomorphic to surplus coloring graphs. These results rely on the notion of a
link
k
-coloring
(see Definition
4.1
). We show that link
k
-colorings of the base graph correspond to
abstract link vertices
of a coloring graph, which are vertices that have certain structural properties identified by Algorithm
1
. Theorem
4.4
shows that for any
\mathcal{C}
, there is at most one pair
(G,k)
with
k>\chi(G)
such that
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
. A useful strategy in our proofs is to extract information from link colorings and their corresponding abstract link vertices.

Showing that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
is impossible when
k>\chi(G)
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi(G^{\prime})
(Theorem
4.28
) requires several new ideas. The presence of abstract link vertices in
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
imposes specific constraints on how
G
can relate to a potential
G^{\prime}
. The first step is to show that any such
G^{\prime}
must contain the connected components of
G
as vertex-disjoint induced subgraphs. Next, Algorithm
2
shows that at each abstract link vertex, this subgraph of
G
in
G^{\prime}
must be colored according to the partition of the coloring in the surplus coloring case. Algorithm
3
then extracts from
\mathcal{C}
an auxiliary structure, termed
labeled link graph
, in a base-graph agnostic manner. These algorithms explain how different abstract link vertices in
\mathcal{C}
are interconnected. Finally, interpreting the shared graph
\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
requires it to contain strictly more abstract link vertices than are present when interpreting it as
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
, contradicting the possibility that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
. A flowchart (Figure
12
) at the end of the paper shows the progression of Section
4
.

All of our algorithms take a graph
\mathcal{C}
as input and seek to interpret
\mathcal{C}
as a coloring graph.
We briefly note that the runtime of these algorithms is polynomial in the number of vertices of
\mathcal{C}
except for a graph isomorphism check at the end of Algorithm
1
. However, the order of a coloring graph is exponential in the order of its base graph in general, so even excluding the graph isomorphism check, our algorithms are not efficient with respect to the base graph.

4.1.
Reconstructing the base graph via link vertices

This subsection proves Theorem
4.4
: if an abstract coloring graph
\mathcal{C}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
for some graph
G
and an integer
k>\chi(G)
, then this pair
(G,k)
is unique and algorithmically recoverable from
\mathcal{C}
. The core idea is to identify abstract link vertices (Definition
4.2
) within the graph
\mathcal{C}
. We first define the corresponding concept in the base graph, the link
k
-coloring (Definition
4.1
), a coloring whose image size within each connected component is minimal.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a graph
G
with connected components
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
and an integer
k>\chi(G)
, a
link
k
-coloring of
G
is a
k
-coloring
\alpha_{G}\colon V(G)\to[k]
such that the image of
V(H_{i})
under
\alpha_{G}
has size
\chi(H_{i})
for all
i\in[d]
.

Equivalently, a link
k
-coloring maximizes the number of common free colors for all vertices within each connected component.

We use Algorithm
1
to find vertices in an abstract graph
\mathcal{C}
that correspond to link
k
-colorings, each having local structural properties in
\mathcal{C}
that imply an adjacency matrix that defines an underlying base graph. When
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
with
k>\chi(G)
, this process succeeds and allows us to reconstruct the unique pair
(G,k)
. The vertices successfully identified by this algorithm are called
abstract link vertices
, formally defined in Definition
4.2
.

Algorithm 1

(Reconstruction and Link Vertex Identification)
.

Given a graph
\mathcal{C}
, identify a subset of vertices
A\subseteq V(\mathcal{C})
, a graph
G
, and an integer
k
as follows:

(1)

Let
A
be the set of vertices
\alpha\in V(\mathcal{C})
where the neighborhood of
\alpha
is a disjoint union of cliques. If
A\neq V(\mathcal{C})
, abort. Otherwise, for each
\alpha\in V(\mathcal{C})
, let
r
be the number of cliques in its neighborhood, and label them arbitrarily
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
.

(2)

Let
n
be the maximum number of cliques in the neighborhood of any vertex in
A
. Remove from
A
any vertex that does not attain this maximum (i.e., if
r<n
).

(3)

For each
\alpha\in A
, let
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
be the sizes of the
n
cliques in its neighborhood. Let
m_{\alpha}
be the number of pairs
1\leq i<j\leq n
with strictly fewer than
t_{i}t_{j}
squares spanning the
i
th and
j
th clique in the neighborhood of
\alpha
. Let
m
be the maximum value of
m_{\alpha}
over all
\alpha\in A
. Remove from
A
any vertex
\alpha
for which
m_{\alpha}<m
.

(4)

For each
\alpha\in A
, let
M_{\alpha}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}
be the matrix such that
M_{ij}=1
if and only if
i\neq j
and there are strictly fewer than
t_{i}t_{j}
squares spanning the
i
th and
j
th clique in the neighborhood of
\alpha
.

(5)

For each
\alpha\in A
and connected component
H_{\ell}
induced by
M_{\alpha}
, let
f_{\ell}
be the maximum number of ways to select one vertex from each clique in the neighborhood of
\alpha
that corresponds to a vertex in
H_{\ell}
such that there is no square spanning the vertices corresponding to
v_{i},v_{j}
for any
i,j
with
M_{i,j}=1
. For each of these
f_{\ell}
sets of
\lvert V(H_{\ell})\rvert
neighbors of
\alpha
, label these edges with a distinct
c\in[f_{\ell}]
.

(6)

Let
f
be the maximum value of
\sum_{\ell}f_{\ell}
over all
\alpha\in A
. Remove from
A
any
\alpha
for which
\sum_{\ell}f_{\ell}<f
.

(7)

Let
G
be the graph with connected components
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
, as determined by an arbitrary
M_{\alpha}
from Step
4
interpreted as an adjacency matrix. Calculate
k=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\left(f+\sum_{\ell\in[d]}\chi(H_{\ell})\right)
, where
f
is the maximum value from Step
6
.

(8)

If
\mathcal{C}\not\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
, abort. Otherwise, return vertex set
A
, graph
G
, and integer
k
.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a graph
\mathcal{C}
, an
abstract link vertex of
\mathcal{C}
is any vertex
\alpha
belonging to the subset
A\subseteq V(\mathcal{C})
returned by Algorithm
1
.
In other words, an abstract link vertex is one that survives Step
6
, provided the algorithm successfully identifies a graph
G
and an integer
k
such that
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
.

The next lemma bridges link
k
-colorings and abstract link vertices when
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
for
k>\chi(G)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For a graph
G
and
k>\chi(G)
, there is a natural bijection between the link
k
-colorings of
G
and the abstract link vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
.

Proof.

We show that the natural bijection
\Phi\colon V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))\to\{\text{$k$-colorings of $G$}\}
restricts to a bijection between the abstract link vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
and the link
k
-colorings of
G
whenever
k>\chi(G)
. It suffices to prove that the image of the abstract link vertices under
\Phi
contains only link
k
-colorings and that every link
k
-coloring is achieved by some abstract link vertex. Recall that using the
\alpha\mapsto\alpha_{G}
notation from Section
2
, the symbol
\alpha
represents an element of
V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))
and
\alpha_{G}=\Phi(\alpha)
is the corresponding
k
-coloring of base graph
G
.

Let
\alpha_{G}
be a link
k
-coloring of
G
. Suppose
G
has
d
connected components
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
. We show that
\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(\alpha_{G})
remains in set
A
after all steps of Algorithm
1
. The neighborhood of
\alpha
in
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
consists of the disjoint union of
n=\lvert V(G)\rvert
cliques, because
k>\chi(G)
implies that each vertex of
G
has at least one available color in a link
k
-coloring. Moreover, all vertices in
V(\mathcal{C})
satisfy the condition in Step
1
for any coloring graph
\mathcal{C}
. Indeed, the edges of any clique in a coloring graph correspond to a single vertex changing color, and any coloring has a finite number of vertices with free colors, yielding a neighborhood of cliques. In particular, no vertex of
\mathcal{C}
can have a neighborhood with more than
n
cliques, so
\alpha
survives Step
2
.

Next, consider Step
3
. For every edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
, the corresponding cliques in the neighborhood of
\alpha
have fewer than
t_{i}t_{j}
squares. This is because
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
have at least one common free color in a link
k
-coloring, and a square corresponding to assigning this same color to both vertices is necessarily missing since
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
. Conversely, for any
v_{i}v_{j}\not\in E(G)
, the corresponding cliques have exactly
t_{i}t_{j}
squares, as any combination of free colors for
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
produces a valid
k
-coloring. Hence,
m_{\alpha}=|E(G)|
for this
\alpha
. Additionally, no
\beta\in V(\mathcal{C})
can have
m_{\beta}>\lvert E(G)\rvert
, because this would require some
i,j
with fewer than
t_{i}t_{j}
squares but
v_{i}v_{j}\not\in E(G)
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\alpha
achieves the maximum
m
and survives Step
3
.

Now consider Step
6
. By definition of link
k
-coloring, for each
\ell\in[d]
, every vertex in
V(H_{\ell})
shares exactly
k-\chi(H_{\ell})
common free colors in
\alpha_{G}
. Each of these common free colors is associated with
n_{\ell}=\lvert V(H_{\ell})\rvert
neighbors of
\alpha
which pairwise have spanning squares if and only if the pair of corresponding vertices in
G
are non-adjacent. Thus, for each component
H_{\ell}
, the value of
f_{\ell}
calculated in Step
5
is exactly
f_{\ell}=k-\chi(H_{\ell})
. Moreover, for any
\beta\in V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))
, a missing square between any two neighbors of
\beta
implies that the corresponding two vertices of
G
take on the same color moving from
\beta
to the respective neighbors. Thus, maximality of the definition of link
k
-colorings ensures that
\alpha
survives Step
6
and is included in the set
A
returned by the algorithm.

Conversely, we show that any abstract link vertex
\alpha
in
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
corresponds to some link
k
-coloring
\alpha_{G}
of
G
. For
\alpha
to be in the final set
A
:

•

Surviving Step
2
means that
every
vertex
v_{i}\in V(G)
has at least one free color in
\alpha_{G}
.

•

Surviving Step
3
implies
m_{\alpha}=|E(G)|
. Hence, for every edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(G)
, the vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
share at least one common free color in
\alpha_{G}
.

•

Surviving Step
6
requires
\sum_{\ell}f_{\ell}
to be maximal. This corresponds precisely to colorings where each component
H_{\ell}
uses exactly
\chi(H_{\ell})
colors, maximizing the number of common free colors within that component. By Definition
4.1
, we deduce that
\alpha_{G}
is a link
k
-coloring.

Therefore,
\Phi
restricted to the set of abstract link vertices is a bijection onto the set of link
k
-colorings.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

For any graph
G
and
k>\chi(G)
, there is no graph
G^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}>\chi(G^{\prime})
with
G\not\cong G^{\prime}
or
k\neq k^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
. Moreover, Algorithm
1
produces the unique (up to graph isomorphism)

and

.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
for graphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
where
k>\chi(G)
and
k^{\prime}>\chi(G^{\prime})
. We show that
G\cong G^{\prime}
and
k=k^{\prime}
. The isomorphism
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
means that Algorithm
1
, run on
\mathcal{C}
, identifies the
same
set
A
of abstract link vertices, regardless of whether we consider
\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
or
\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
. By Lemma
4.3
, this set

is in bijection with the link
k
-colorings of

, and also in bijection with the link
k^{\prime}
-colorings of
G^{\prime}
.

Algorithm
1
(Step

) identifies
n
as the maximum number of cliques in the neighborhood of any abstract link vertex. This number

corresponds to
|V(G)|
when interpreting
\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
and to
|V(G^{\prime})|
when interpreting
\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
. Therefore,

. Algorithm
1
(Steps

and

) derives an adjacency matrix

based on the pattern of missing squares between neighborhood cliques of abstract link vertices. Since this pattern is solely determined by the structure of
\mathcal{C}
, it yields the same

(up to permutation), regardless of whether
\mathcal{C}
is interpreted as
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
or
\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
. As

represents the adjacency matrix of the base graph in both interpretations (because

and

), it follows that

.

Furthermore, the algorithm calculates the maximum value

in Step

. Using the formula from Step

, we have

and

. Since

, the connected components of the two graphs are isomorphic. After relabeling the component indices, we have

and

for each

. As

is also derived uniquely from
\mathcal{C}
, it follows that

.

Remark 4.5
.

The adjacency matrix of
G
can be reconstructed not only from abstract link vertices in
V(\mathcal{C}_{k}(G))
, but also from certain other vertices that we can completely characterize. Specifically, for any coloring in which every pair of adjacent vertices in
G
shares a common free color, the corresponding vertex in
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
will also yield the correct adjacency matrix. We refer to such colorings as
weak link
k
-colorings
of
G
. Unlike link
k
-colorings, weak link
k
-colorings do not require that
all vertices
within a given connected component share a common free color; they only require that
each pair
of adjacent vertices in
G
shares a common free color (which may differ between pairs). For example, the coloring
\beta_{G}
in Figure
2
is a weak link
5
-coloring, but not a link
5
-coloring. The concept of link
k
-colorings, along with Algorithm
1
, provides a systematic framework for identifying vertices from which the graph
G
can be uniquely reconstructed. In contrast, it remains unclear how the vertices corresponding to weak link
k
-colorings can be reliably identified using only the structure of the coloring graph.

In the following subsections, we will extract more information from the coloring graph. We remark that the definition of abstract link vertices applies to any abstract graph
\mathcal{C}

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
where

and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi(G^{\prime})
, then

contains

as a subgraph. Moreover, if

consists of connected components

, then

contains the connected components of

as vertex-disjoint induced subgraphs. That is, there exists an injection
\phi\colon V(G)\to V(G^{\prime})
such that for any

and

, we have

if and only if
\phi(u)\phi(v)\in E(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.4
, we know that Algorithm
1
run on
\mathcal{C}
produces a unique (up to permutation) adjacency matrix

associated with any abstract link vertex. The isomorphism
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G^{\prime})}(G^{\prime})
induces a mapping

so that each abstract link vertex
\alpha\in V(\mathcal{C})
corresponds to a
\chi^{\prime}
-coloring
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}
of

. The neighborhood structure around
\alpha
in
\mathcal{C}
, specifically the cliques and the pattern of missing induced squares analyzed in Step

and Step

of Algorithm

, implies that the corresponding
\chi^{\prime}
-coloring
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}
in

has exactly

vertices with at least one free color. Furthermore, a pair of these vertices corresponding to missing induced squares in
\mathcal{C}
must be adjacent in
G^{\prime}
. Since

is the adjacency matrix of

by Theorem
4.4
, it follows that
G
is a subgraph of
G^{\prime}
. We now make the inclusion

explicit.

Label the

as

, corresponding to the

cliques in the neighborhood of

in
\mathcal{C}
. Via the isomorphism

, each clique associated with

in the

interpretation corresponds to changing the color of a unique vertex
\phi(v_{j})
in

. This mapping

is well-defined because each clique in the neighborhood of a vertex in
\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
corresponds to the set of colorings obtained by changing the color of a single vertex in

. In addition, the resulting map

is an injection.

As

contains
G
as a subgraph,
\phi(u)\phi(v)\in E(G^{\prime})
for every edge

. To show that each component
\phi(H_{i})
is an induced subgraph of

, suppose for a contradiction that there exists an edge
\phi(u)\phi(v)\in E(G^{\prime})
with

for some

. As

, by Theorem

, every spanning square is present between the cliques in
\mathcal{C}
associated with base graph vertices

and

. Since

and

are in the same connected component

of

, there exists a path

within

such that

for each

. Thus, for each adjacent pair (

,
w_{j+1}
) on this path, there is at least one missing spanning square between their corresponding cliques in
\mathcal{C}
. The structure of missing squares along the path (when interpreting

via

) implies that the vertices

must share at least one common free color in the link

-coloring in

,
\alpha_{G}
, associated with

. Interpreting
\mathcal{C}
via

, the same structural properties hold: squares are missing between cliques for

and

, while all squares are present between cliques for

and
\phi(w_{r})=\phi(v)
. By the required structure of the missing spanning squares, there must be a common free color for all vertices

. In particular, this implies that
\phi(u)
and
\phi(v)
have a common free color. Since all spanning squares exist between the cliques for
\phi(u)
and

, any combination of assigning a free color

to

and a free color

to

results in a valid coloring. Since they share a common free color, we can set

, resulting in a proper coloring of

where

and
\phi(v)
have the same color. This contradicts the assumption that
\phi(u)\phi(v)\in E(G^{\prime})
. ∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Given a proper

-coloring of a graph

with color

Fix any color

be the set of vertices colored

every

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})

and

Under the interpretation of

Since
\chi^{\prime}>0
, Lemma

has the following immediate consequence.

Corollary 4.9
.

Given a graph
G
and

, there is no graph

with

such that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G^{\prime})}(G^{\prime})
.

This corollary demonstrates that coloring graphs with surplus colors are complete graph invariants, when restricted to base graphs of the same order. Our main result Theorem

strengthens Corollary

by removing the hypothesis

have different orders, proving that

\chi
-coloring graphs.

The following subsections introduce techniques that go beyond the scope of Algorithm

to explore further structural properties that must hold if

with

i\neq j
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\mathcal{P}_{j}\neq\emptyset
. Take any color
c\in\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\mathcal{P}_{j}
. Within
[\alpha]
we may independently permute the colors (within
k
available colors) inside each
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
. Consequently, we can find a link
k
-coloring
\beta_{G}
such that the associated
\chi^{\prime}
-coloring
\beta_{G^{\prime}}
simultaneously satisfies:
\beta_{G^{\prime}}(u)=c
and
\beta_{G^{\prime}}(v)=c
. This contradicts the fact that
\beta_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper coloring, and
uv\in E(G^{\prime})
.

(2)

We show that no color
c\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
appears on
N(v)\cap L
. Otherwise, choose
w\in N(v)\cap L
with
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(w)=c
. Find
\beta_{G}\in[\alpha_{G}]
such that
\beta_{G^{\prime}}(v)=c
. This is possible because
v\in H^{\prime}_{i}
. Since
\beta_{G^{\prime}}|_{L}=\alpha_{G^{\prime}}|_{L}
by Corollary
4.25
, we have
\beta_{G^{\prime}}(w)=\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(w)=c
. The resulting equality
\beta_{G^{\prime}}(v)=\beta_{G^{\prime}}(w)
leads to a contradiction as
\beta_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper coloring and
vw\in E(G^{\prime})
.

(3)

Suppose
\tilde{c}\in[\chi^{\prime}]
is not present in
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(L)
. Find
\beta\in[\alpha]
such that
\tilde{c}\notin\beta_{G^{\prime}}(V(H^{\prime}_{i}))
for each

. This is possible, as
\beta_{G^{\prime}}(V(H^{\prime}_{i}))=\psi_{i}^{[\alpha]}(\beta_{G}(V(H_{i})))
and
G
has surplus colors. Now,
\tilde{c}\notin\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(L)=\beta_{G^{\prime}}(L)
. As
\tilde{c}\notin\beta_{G^{\prime}}(V(G^{\prime}))
, it follows that
\beta_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper
(\chi^{\prime}-1)
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
, a contradiction. ∎

Lemma 4.27
.

Assume Hypothesis
1
. Let
\alpha,\beta
be two abstract link vertices in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\beta\notin[\alpha]
. Let
(\psi^{[\alpha]},\phi^{[\alpha]})
be a pair for
[\alpha]
from Lemma
4.23
. Define
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}\colon V(G^{\prime})\to[\chi^{\prime}]
by specifying
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(\phi^{[\alpha]}(v))=\psi^{[\alpha]}(\beta_{G}(v))
for

and
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(w)=\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(w)
for

. Then
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper coloring of

. Moreover, the corresponding vertex

\widetilde{\beta}\notin[\alpha]

Proof.

We first establish that
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper
\chi^{\prime}
-coloring. The domain of
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
is

V(G^{\prime})=V(H^{\prime}_{1})\uplus\dots\uplus V(H^{\prime}_{d})\uplus L.

We carefully check properness of
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
on the set
E(G^{\prime})
by separating the edges into four types.

•

Edges within
H^{\prime}_{i}
:
For each
u\in H^{\prime}_{i}
, write
u=\phi_{i}^{[\alpha]}(v)
for some

. Then
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(u)=\psi_{i}^{[\alpha]}(\beta_{G}(v))
. Since

H_{i}

\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}

.

•

Edges between
H^{\prime}_{i}
and
H^{\prime}_{j}
for
i\neq j
:
Suppose
uv\in E(G^{\prime})
with
u\in H^{\prime}_{i}
and

. Corollary
4.26
(
1
) implies
\mathcal{P}_{i}\cap\mathcal{P}_{j}=\emptyset
. Since
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(u)\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(v)\in\mathcal{P}_{j}
, we deduce that
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(u)\neq\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(v)
.

•

Edges between
H^{\prime}_{i}
and
L
:
Suppose

with

and

. By Corollary
4.26
(
2
), the image set
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(N(u)\cap L)=\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(N(u)\cap L)
is disjoint from
\mathcal{P}_{i}
. Since
w\in N(u)\cap L
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(u)\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
, it follows that
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(w)\neq\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(u)
.

•

Edges within
L
:
The coloring
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}|_{L}
is identical to
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}|_{L}
. Since
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper coloring,
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
is also proper on the subgraph induced by

.

Next, we argue that
\widetilde{\beta}\in\mathcal{C}
(the vertex corresponding to the

-coloring

) is an abstract link vertex. When verifying that

. The same check applies to

because

agree on

. Therefore,

is also an abstract link vertex. It remains to show that

for all

implies that the corresponding
k
-coloring
\widetilde{\beta}_{G}
coincides with

. Since

, it follows that
\widetilde{\beta}\notin[\alpha]
, as desired. ∎

4.4.
Abstract link vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G),k>\chi(G)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime}),\chi^{\prime}=\chi(G^{\prime})
can never be in bijection if
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})

In this final subsection, we use the structural results accumulated by Algorithms
1
,
2
, and
3
to establish our main result: if a coloring graph
\mathcal{C}
arises from a graph with surplus colors, then
\mathcal{C}
does
not
arise from any other base graph.

Theorem
4.4
establishes that if
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
with
k>\chi(G)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}_{k^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
, then
k^{\prime}=\chi^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\chi(G^{\prime})
(meaning
G^{\prime}
has no surplus colors). Theorem
4.28
below proves this remaining scenario is impossible. Assuming, for contradiction, that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
, we use properties of abstract link vertices. Our analysis reveals that if the shared graph
\mathcal{C}
is interpreted as
\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
, it must contain
\chi^{\prime}!
times the number of abstract link vertices defined by its structure as
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
. This discrepancy yields a contradiction, ruling out the isomorphism. The theorem upgrades Corollary
4.9
by removing the dependence on the number of vertices.

Theorem 4.28
.

Given a graph
G
and
k>\chi(G)
, there is no graph
G^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi(G^{\prime})}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Assume, to the contrary, that
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
for some graphs
G
and
G^{\prime}
, where
k>\chi(G)
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi(G^{\prime})
. First, we show that
\chi^{\prime}>1
. If
\chi^{\prime}=1
, then
G^{\prime}
is the edgeless graph
N_{r}
(with
r\geq 1
vertices). The
1
-coloring graph
\mathcal{C}_{1}(N_{r})
contains exactly one vertex (representing the single coloring that assigns color
1
to all vertices). In contrast, since
k>\chi(G)
, any proper
\chi(G)
-coloring of
G
gives at least two distinct proper
k
-colorings of
G
by embedding its color set into
[k]
in two different ways. Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
has at least two vertices, contradicting that
\mathcal{C}_{1}(N_{r})
has only one vertex. So, we must have
\chi^{\prime}>1
.

Let
A
be the set of abstract link vertices in
\mathcal{C}
. Since
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
where
k>\chi(G)
, abstract link vertices exist by Lemma
4.3
, so
|A|\geq 1
. Fix a vertex
\alpha\in A
, and let
(\psi^{[\alpha]},\phi^{[\alpha]})
be a pair associated with the equivalence class
[\alpha]
as in Lemma
4.23
. As before, let
L\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
denote the locked set for
(\psi^{[\alpha]},\phi^{[\alpha]})
. For every
\beta\in A
, define
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
by
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(\phi^{[\alpha]}(v))=\psi^{[\alpha]}(\beta_{G}(v))
for
v\in V(G)
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(w)=\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(w)
for
w\in L
. If
\beta\in[\alpha]
, we have
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}=\beta_{G^{\prime}}
by Corollaries
4.24
and
4.25
, which corresponds to an abstract link vertex. If
\beta\notin[\alpha]
, then
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper
\chi^{\prime}
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
by Lemma
4.27
, corresponding to an abstract link vertex in
\mathcal{C}
. The construction produces
|A|
distinct proper
\chi^{\prime}
-colorings of
G^{\prime}
, each representing a different abstract link vertex in
\mathcal{C}
, that all agree pointwise on the coloring of
L
.

For each such coloring
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
, consider the set of
\chi^{\prime}!
colorings obtained by applying every permutation
\sigma\in S_{\chi^{\prime}}
to the global color set
[\chi^{\prime}]
. Each resulting coloring
\sigma\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
is a proper
\chi^{\prime}
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
. Since
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
corresponds to an abstract link vertex (a property preserved under permutation of colors), each
\sigma\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
must also correspond to an abstract link vertex. These
\chi^{\prime}!
colorings generated from a single
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
are all pairwise distinct. Indeed, if
\sigma\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}=\tau\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
, then their restrictions to
L
are equal:
\sigma(\alpha_{G^{\prime}}|_{L})=\tau(\alpha_{G^{\prime}}|_{L})
. Since
\alpha_{G^{\prime}}(L)=[\chi^{\prime}]
by Corollary
4.26
(
3
), we deduce that
\sigma=\tau
.

Moreover, the resulting sets of
\chi^{\prime}!
colorings generated by two
distinct
initial colorings, say
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{G^{\prime}}
(where
\beta,\gamma\in A
with
\beta\neq\gamma
), are themselves disjoint. Indeed, if
\sigma\circ\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}=\tau\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{G^{\prime}}
, then considering the colors on
L
, we again get
\sigma(\alpha_{G^{\prime}}|_{L})=\tau(\alpha_{G^{\prime}}|_{L})
. The same reasoning from the previous paragraph yields
\sigma=\tau
. This implies
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{G^{\prime}}
, and consequently,
\widetilde{\beta}_{G^{\prime}}(\phi^{[\alpha]}(v))=\widetilde{\gamma}_{G^{\prime}}(\phi^{[\alpha]}(v))
for all
v\in V(G)
. By definition,
\psi^{[\alpha]}(\beta_{G}(v))=\psi^{[\alpha]}(\gamma_{G}(v))
for all
v\in V(G)
. As
\psi^{[\alpha]}
is injective,
\beta_{G}(v)=\gamma_{G}(v)
for all
v\in V(G)
, leading to
\beta_{G}=\gamma_{G}
. This equality between
k
-colorings of
G
implies
\beta=\gamma
as vertices in
\mathcal{C}
, yielding a contradiction.

Therefore, the overall process generates exactly
|A|\cdot\chi^{\prime}!
distinct
\chi^{\prime}
-colorings of
G^{\prime}
, each corresponding to an abstract link vertex in
\mathcal{C}
. Hence, the number of abstract link vertices in
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\chi^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})
is at least
|A|\cdot\chi^{\prime}!
. By definition, the number of abstract link vertices in
\mathcal{C}\cong\mathcal{C}_{k}(G)
is exactly
|A|
. Comparing the two counts, we obtain
|A|\geq|A|\cdot\chi^{\prime}!
. This inequality yields a final contradiction, because
|A|\geq 1
and
\chi^{\prime}>1
.
∎

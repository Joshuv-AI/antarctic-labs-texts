Consistent gauge theories for the slave particle representation of the strongly correlated t t - J J model
Xi Luo, Tao Shi, Yue Yu, Long Liang

Abstract
We aim to clarify the confusion and inconsistency in our recent works Luo et al. 2023 ; Liang et al. 2024 , and to address the incompleteness therein. In order to avoid the ill-defined nature of the free propagator of the gauge field in the ordered states of the t t - J J model, we adopted a gauge fixing that was not of the Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST) exact form in our previous work Liang et al. 2024 . This led to the situation where Dirac’s second-class constraints, namely, the slave particle number constraint and the Ioffe-Larkin current constraint, were not rigorously obeyed. Here we show that a consistent gauge fixing condition that enforces the exact constraints must be BRST-exact. An example is the Lorenz gauge. On the other hand, we prove that although the free propagator of the gauge field in the Lorenz gauge is ill-defined, the full propagator is still well-defined. This implies that the strongly correlated t t - J J model can be exactly mapped to a perturbatively controllable theory within the slave particle representation.



I
Introduction

In our previous works
Luo et al. 2023
;
Liang et al. 2024
, we solved the two-dimensional strongly correlated problem of the electron system by
the slave particle representation. A strongly correlated electron system is exactly mapped to a weak coupling
slave particle system by exactly dealing with the local constraints which are either Dirac’s first-class ones when
the system is in the atomic limit of the electrons
Luo et al. 2023
or the second-class ones in the mean field states
Liang et al. 2024
.
The essential step for the exact mapping is taking a gauge fixing condition that is consistent with the local
constraints. For the first-class constraint, the consistent gauge theory was established by Fradkin, Vilkovisky,
and Batalin (FVB)
Fradkin and Vilkovisky 1973
;
Fradkin and Vilkovisky 1975
;
Batalin and Vilkovisky 1977
half a century ago. We explained their theory in a comprehensible language for
the condensed matter physicists
Luo et al. 2023
;
Liang et al. 2024
. The gauge fixing term added to the Lagrangian of the gauge theory must
be in a Becchi-Rouet-Stora-Tyutin (BRST)
Becchi et al. 1976
;
Tyutin 1975
;
Iofa and Tyutin 1976
exact form, while the BRST charge acting on the
physical state must exactly enforce both the local constraints and the gauge fixing condition
Weinberg 1996
.
The first-class constraint’s problem has been completely solved so that we will not concern ourselves with it
in this paper anymore.

There is no a systematic way to consistently solve a gauge theory with the second-class constraints. We have solved the mean field theory where the current constraint is the second-class one in the
t
-
J
model by considering the BRST symmetry
Liang et al. 2024
. In principle, we have obtained a consistent gauge theory with the second-class constraint. We found that the Lorenz gauge is a consistent one while the other familiar ones, such as the axial gauge or the Coulomb gauge, are not.
However, when performing perturbation calculations, we encountered a problem: the free propagator of the gauge field is ill-defined because of the skew
U(1)
gauge symmetry of the Lorenz gauge fixing term. We modified the gauge fixing term so that the ill-defined problem of the free propagator was resolved while it remained BRST invariant. We then performed the perturbation calculation for the strange metal phase of the
t
-
J
model and obtained some results that are consistent with the experimental measurements on cuprates.
However, we did not check whether the gauge fixing term we used was a BRST exact form or not.
If this BRST invariant term is BRST-closed but not an exact form, the added term is not in the same BRST cohomology
class as the vanishing pure gauge field Lagrangian
L_{0}(a_{\mu})=0
before the gauge fixing.
For example, we can add a Maxwell term plus the Lorenz gauge fixing term and the ghost term to
L_{0}(a_{\mu})=0
,
which is BRST closed but not exact. This obviously introduces additional dynamics to the system and changes the physics.
On the other hand, although the free propagator of the gauge field under the Lorenz gauge is ill-defined,
can we still perform the perturbation calculation in this case? In this paper, we will clarify the confusion and
inconsistency in the previous work and to address the incompleteness therein. We attach an appendix where we show the BRST formalism of the
t
-
J
model on a lattice rather than the continuum limit.

IV
Flaws in the previous section and a new point of view

IV.1
The BRST exact form of the gauge fixing term

We added a BRST invariant term Eq. (
8
) to
L_{0}(a_{\mu})=0
as the gauge fixing. However, we did not prove whether the added gauge fixing
term is of the BRST exact form or not. According to the BRST cohomology, only when the added gauge fixing term is
BRST exact does it not introduce extra dynamics to the system. For example, the Maxwell term is BRST invariant but not BRST exact. If we add this term with a Lorenz gauge fixing to
L_{0}(a_{\mu})=0
, extra gauge field dynamics is introduced to the system.

Let us determine what kind of gauge fixing terms are BRST exact. We consider a general BRST transformation

\displaystyle\delta_{B}a_{\mu}={\epsilon\partial_{\mu}u},\delta_{B}u=0,{\delta_{B}}\bar{u}=\epsilon F(a_{\mu}),

(11)

with
F(a_{\mu})
to be determined.
Denoting
\delta_{B}=\epsilon s
, a BRST exact form which is added to the Lagrangian is assumed to be proportional to

\displaystyle s(\bar{u}\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu})=F\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu}-\bar{u}\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}u.~~

(12)

The equation of motion for
u
is
\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}u=0
. This leads to

\displaystyle F(a_{\mu})={\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mu,\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu},

(13)

due to the requirement of
s^{2}=0
.
Therefore, a general BRST exact term is given by

\displaystyle L_{GGF}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle s(\bar{u}\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu})

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}a_{\nu})^{2}-\bar{u}\sum_{\mu\nu}C_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}u.~~

The Lorenz gauge is recovered by taking

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu}=\delta_{\mu\nu}.

(15)

The inverse of the free gauge propagator corresponding to Eq. (
14
) can be read out,
D^{(0)-1}\propto\tilde{k}_{\mu}\tilde{k}_{\nu}
for
\tilde{k}_{\mu}=\sum_{\rho}C_{\mu\rho}k_{\rho}
.
Therefore the free propagator of the gauge field
D^{(0)}
now is ill-defined
because
\det(D^{(0)-1})\propto\det(\tilde{k}_{\mu}\tilde{k}_{\nu})=0
.

On the other hand, Eq. (
8
) can be rewritten as (up to a surface term)

\displaystyle L_{GF}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(B-\sqrt{AC})}\sum_{b}\partial_{\tau}\delta a_{b}\partial_{b}\delta\lambda

(16)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{D}{2}\sum_{b}(\partial_{\tau}\delta a_{b})^{2}+\frac{E}{2}\sum_{b}(\partial_{b}\delta\lambda)^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle Cs(\bar{u}(\xi\partial_{\tau}\delta\lambda+\sum_{b}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b})).

The last term is a BRST exact form. However, this gauge fixing term in general is not of the BRST exact form, except for
E=D=0
, which corresponds to the Lorenz gauge.
In this case,
B^{2}=AC
due to Eq. (
9
), and therefore, only the last term in Eq. (
16
) is left. The free propagator of the gauge field is well-defined but the non-exact part in (
16
) introduces extra dynamics to the gauge field.

IV.2
The exact local constraint problem

The BRST symmetry is a global symmetry; Noether’s theorem gives the conserved BRST charge
Q
which counts
the ghost number of a state. According to Weinberg
Weinberg 1996
, if we denote Eq. (
14
) as
s\Psi
and
a small variation of such a gauge fixing condition as
s\tilde{\delta}\Psi
, the variation of any physical matrix element vanishes, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tilde{\delta}\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle=-\langle\alpha|\tilde{\delta}\int d\tau L|\beta\rangle=-\langle\alpha|\{Q,\tilde{\delta}\Psi\}|\beta\rangle=0.

(17)

Since
\tilde{\delta}\Psi
is arbitrary, one must have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|Q=0,~Q|\beta\rangle=0.

(18)

This proves the BRST charge free requirement of the physical states,

Q|phys\rangle=0.

For the theory with the gauge fixing term (
16
), the BRST charge is given by
Liang et al. 2024

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x~(igG+E\sum_{b}\partial_{b}^{2}\delta\lambda-D\sum_{b}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b})u

(19)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[A\partial_{\tau}\delta\lambda+(B+D)\sum_{b}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b}]\partial_{\tau}u.

The constraints read out from the BRST charge free of the physical states are given by

\displaystyle(igG+E\sum_{b}\partial_{b}^{2}\delta\lambda-D\sum_{b}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b})=0,

\displaystyle[A\partial_{\tau}\delta\lambda+(B+D)\sum_{b}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b}]=0.

(20)

While the second equation is the gauge fixing condition, the first one does not exactly give the local constraint
G=0
unless both
E
and
D
are zero.
In deriving the conserved charge from Noether’s theorem, the Euler-Lagrange equations of the fields are used. For example, the equations of motion of the gauge field
\delta a_{i}
gives

\displaystyle J_{b}(\delta a)=B\partial_{\tau}\partial_{b}\delta\lambda+D\partial^{2}_{\tau}\delta a_{b}+C\partial_{b}(\sum_{c}\partial_{c}\delta a_{c}),

(21)

which also does not yield the constraint
J_{b}=0
.

IV.3
Completeness of consistent gauge theory

In light of the discussions in Sec.
IV
, our theory in
Liang et al. 2024
still has some problems:
(1) Extra artificial gauge field dynamics was introduced. (2) Both the local number and current constraints are not
exactly recovered. The above two problems can be solved by applying the Lorenz gauge in the
D=E=0
limit. On the one hand, the gauge fixing term
is BRST exact so that no extra dynamics is introduced by hand; on the other hand, the BRST charge becomes

\displaystyle Q=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x~igGu+{B}[{\zeta}\partial_{\tau}\delta\lambda+\sum_{b}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b}]\partial_{\tau}u.

(22)

This gives the local constraint:
G=0
and the Lorenz gauge
{\zeta}\partial_{\tau}\delta\lambda+\sum_{b}\partial_{b}\delta a_{b}=0
. The vanishing counterflow constraint becomes

\displaystyle J_{b}(\delta a)={C\partial_{b}}({\zeta}\partial_{\tau}\delta\lambda+\sum_{c}\partial_{c}\delta a_{c})=0,

(23)

where the second equality comes from the Lorenz gauge because of Eq. (
9
). However, as we have shown that the free propagator
of the gauge field is ill-defined for the Lorenz gauge. The perturbation calculation therefore seems to be invalid. Instead of Eq. (
8
), one may try to add a Maxwell term to the gauge field as the usual treatment in quantum electrodynamics where the propagator of the gauge field is well-defined. But, there will be extra contributions to the BRST charge (
19
) and the current (
21
) stemming from the Maxwell term (because the Maxwell term is not BRST-exact) unless the strength of the Maxwell term goes to zero, and the problem of ill-defined propagator remains.

Can we have a solution to these problems? Let us recall the source of the ill-defined nature of the free propagator of the gauge field.
As we have observed, the zero mode in the gauge field actually comes from the shew
U(1)
gauge symmetry
of the Lorenz gauge fixing term
Liang et al. 2024
, i.e., when

a_{\mu}\to a_{\mu}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\theta,

the Lorenz gauge fixing condition is invariant,

\partial_{\mu}a_{\mu}\to\partial_{\mu}(a_{\mu}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\theta)=\partial_{\mu}a_{\mu}.

This leads to the situation where the inverse of the free propagator of the gauge field,
D^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}\propto q_{\mu}q_{\nu}
has a vanishing determinant.
However, this skew
U(1)
gauge invariance holds only for the gauge fixing term.
The whole Lagrangian
L_{MFC}+L_{GFL}
is not shew gauge invariant because the minimal coupling between the gauge field and the matter field breaks this symmetry.
Thus, the full propagator
D_{\mu\nu}
is well-defined. To avoid the divergence of
D^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}
,
we use the Lagrangian
L_{GF}
, i.e., (
16
), in order to keep the BRST symmetry of the theory, and take
D=E\to 0
limit after the calculations.
In this case, if we denote the gauge field propagator as
D^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}
, then the free propagator
D^{{}^{\prime}(0)}_{\mu\nu}
is invertible. According to Dyson’s equation, the full propagator is

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}=[(D^{\prime(0)-1}-\Pi^{\prime*})^{-1}]_{\mu\nu},

(24)

where
\Pi^{\prime*}
is the vacuum polarization of the holon
h
and the spinon
f_{\sigma}
. When
D,E\to 0
, we have the well-defined

\displaystyle D_{\mu\nu}=[(D^{(0)-1}-\Pi^{*})^{-1}]_{\mu\nu}.

(25)

For example, if we take the ramdom phase approximation (RPA) for the propagator, one has

\displaystyle D^{\prime RPA}_{\mu\nu}=[(D^{\prime(0)-1}-\Pi^{\prime(0)*})^{-1}]_{\mu\nu}.

(26)

where
\Pi^{\prime(0)*}=\Pi^{(0)*}
does not have the contribution from the gauge field propagator. And

D^{\prime RPA}\to D^{RPA}
when
D,E\to 0
. We then have a well-defined

D^{RPA}
. The same process also works for adding a Maxwell term and then taking the zero strength limit.

We can replace
D^{(0)}
with
D^{RPA}
in all calculations. In this way, we are able to perform
a perturbative calculation for the consistent gauge theory in the slave boson representation of the
t
-
J
model
in the strong coupling limit.

V
conclusions

We now complete the construction of the consistent gauge theory for the
U(1)
slave particle representation of the
t
-
J
model. We demonstrate that the gauge fixing condition must be BRST-exact and show that local constraints are exactly preserved in the Lorenz gauge. The difficulty of ill-defined free gauge propagator is also resolved. This work thus establishes a concrete framework for systematic perturbation theory. The further tasks will be to perform the hard work of
the perturbation calculations for the physical observables. Our approach is applicable to other strongly correlated systems.

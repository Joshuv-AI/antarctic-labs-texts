Quantum conditional entropies from convex trace functionals
Roberto Rubboli, Milad M. Goodarzi, Marco Tomamichel

Abstract
We study geometric properties of trace functionals that generalize those in [Zhang, Adv. Math. 365:107053 (2020)], arising from a novel family of conditional entropies with applications in quantum information theory. Building on new convexity results for these functionals, we establish data-processing inequalities and additivity properties for our entropies, demonstrating their operational significance. We further prove completeness under duality, chain rules, and various monotonicity properties for this family. Our proofs draw on tools from complex interpolation theory, multivariate Araki–Lieb–Thirring inequalities, variational characterizations of trace functionals, and spectral pinching techniques.

Introduction

Quantum conditional entropies quantify the uncertainty about the state of a quantum system
A
from the perspective of an observer with access to a potentially correlated quantum system
B
, often referred to as side information. These measures of conditional uncertainty play a central role in quantum information theory, with applications ranging from quantum cryptography
[
45
]
and quantum Shannon theory
[
62
,
23
,
27
]
to resource theories
[
13
]
.
While von Neumann entropy often takes a prominent role in such applications, other notions of conditional entropy are necessary to give a more fine-grained picture when we move beyond asymptotic scenarios. In cryptography, conditional min-entropy quantifies how much secret key can be extracted from a random variable
A
when an eavesdropper has access to correlated quantum side information
B
that may assist in estimating the secret key
[
8
]
. A larger family of quantum Rényi entropies was employed recently in
[
3
]
to give the tightest known security analysis for quantum key distribution.
Applications of general conditional entropies in information theory include recent applications in the analysis of error exponents for source compression and channel coding
[
46
,
33
]
.

Main results

The following results hold for all parameter triples
(\alpha,z,\lambda)
in the DPI region of

Data-processing inequality:

A fundamental requirement for conditional entropies as measures of conditional uncertainty is monotonicity under bipartite channels of the form
\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{M}
is a convex combination of unitary channels acting on system

is an arbitrary channel acting on system
B

A
or acting on the side information
B
. In addition, one must allow for the possibility that the channel on
A
depends on the outcome of a measurement performed on
B
. The reader is referred to Section
5
for a detailed discussion of the operational significance of these requirements.

Result 1

.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
, and let
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state and

\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}^{i}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{i}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})_{\sigma}.

(3)

We remark that most studies in the existing literature establish the DPI for conditional entropies in canonical form by leveraging the DPI of the underlying divergence. However, this approach is not applicable in our case, as the underlying trivariate trace functional fails to satisfy monotonicity under quantum channels for the relevant parameter ranges (see Remark
5.3

\rho\mapsto\exp\big((1-\alpha) H^\lambda_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\big)
in certain parameter ranges using Theorem
3.1
, and then apply standard arguments to promote this property to monotonicity under quantum channels.

Additivity:

A key property is additivity with respect to tensor products. We prove

Result 2

(cf. Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
, and let
\rho_{AB}
and
\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
be two quantum states. Then,

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(AA^{\prime}|BB^{\prime})_{\rho\otimes\sigma}=H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}+H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})_{\sigma}.

(4)

To prove this, we first characterize the optimizers
\sigma_{B}
using a fixed-point equation (cf. Theorem
3.5
), which we can then leverage to show additivity.

Duality:

Another feature of conditional entropies is their duality relations.
Let
\rho_{ABC}
be a pure quantum state with marginals
\rho_{AB}
and
\rho_{AC}
. The following relations hold
[
56
,
42
,
55
]
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\rho}+\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\hat{\alpha}}(A|C)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\alpha^{-1}+\hat{\alpha}^{-1}=2;

(5)

\displaystyle\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\rho}+\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\hat{\alpha}}(A|C)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\alpha+\hat{\alpha}=2;

(6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(A|B)_{\rho}+\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\uparrow}_{\hat{\alpha}}(A|C)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\alpha\hat{\alpha}=1.

(7)

Duality relations have found many applications in cryptography as they relate the relative knowledge two parties can have about a quantum system.
Such relations can be used to define a dual to any conditional entropy, and we may ask if a particular family of conditional entropies is complete under duality, that is, if the duality relation is bijective. It is easy to see that the entropies
H_{\alpha,z}
defined in (
1
) are not complete. On the other hand, we show that this is the case for our entropies.

Result 3

(cf. Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda),(\hat{\alpha},\hat{z},\hat{\lambda})\in\mathcal{D}
, and let
\rho_{ABC}
be a pure quantum state. Then

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}+H^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\alpha},\hat{z}}(A|C)_{\rho}=0,

(8)

whenever the parameters satisfy

\frac{z}{1-\alpha}+\frac{\hat{z}}{1-\hat{\alpha}}=0,\qquad\frac{1-z}{1-\alpha}=\hat{\lambda},\qquad\frac{1-\hat{z}}{1-\hat{\alpha}}=\lambda.

(9)

This result has several implications for quantum conditional entropy. Computing the dual of a given conditional entropy is generally difficult, and the duals of
H_{\alpha,z}^{\uparrow}
and
H_{\alpha,z}^{\downarrow}
for general
z
have remained unknown. We provide a resolution to this problem: If
(\alpha,z,0)\in\mathcal{D}
(or equivalently, if
(\alpha,z,1)\in\mathcal{D}
), then

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,z}^{\uparrow}(A|B)_{\rho}+H^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\alpha},\hat{\alpha}}(A|C)_{\rho}=0,\qquad\text{where}\qquad\hat{\alpha}=\frac{z}{z+\alpha-1},\qquad\hat{\lambda}=\frac{z-1}{\alpha-1};

(10)

\displaystyle H_{\alpha,z}^{\downarrow}(A|B)_{\rho}+H^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\alpha},1}(A|C)_{\rho}=0,\qquad\text{where}\qquad\hat{\alpha}=\frac{z-\alpha+1}{z},\qquad\hat{\lambda}=\frac{z-1}{\alpha-1}.

(11)

This result surprisingly reveals that the duals of
H_{\alpha,z}^{\uparrow}
and
H_{\alpha,z}^{\downarrow}
do not admit the canonical forms given in (
1
) as
\hat{\lambda}\not\in\{0,1\}
, in stark contrast to the established dualities for
\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}
. This implies that not all conditional entropies arise in canonical forms from an underlying divergence and resolves an open question posed in
[
29
]
.

Chain rules:

Chain rules reflect the compositional structure of multipartite systems. They generalize the equality
H(AB|C)=H(A|BC)+H(B|C)
for the von Neumann entropy to Rényi entropies; however, in the latter case, only inequalities can be shown.

Result 4

(cf. Theorem
9.1
)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda),(\beta,w,\mu),(\gamma,v,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
, and let
\rho_{ABC}
be a tripartite quantum state. Suppose the parameters satisfy the relations

\frac{z}{1-\alpha}=\frac{w}{1-\beta}+\frac{v}{1-\gamma},\qquad\frac{1-z}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1-w}{1-\beta},\qquad\mu=\frac{1-v}{1-\gamma}.

(12)

If
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0
, the following chain rule holds:

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq H_{\beta,w}^{\mu}(A|BC)_{\rho}+H^{\lambda}_{\gamma,v}(B|C)_{\rho}.

(13)

The inequality is reversed when
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
.

The chain rules for
\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}
were first established in
[
15
]
(see also
[
5
]
for an alternative proof). In contrast, chain rules for
\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}^{\uparrow}
and
\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}^{\downarrow}
, though desirable, have remained unknown. The general relation reveals several new chain rules that involve both entropies as special cases. If
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0
, we have the following new relations:

\displaystyle\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma};

(14)

\displaystyle\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\uparrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma};

(15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

(16)

In each of the preceding inequalities, the arrows on the entropies indexed by
\alpha
and
\gamma

(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
(see Corollaries
9.2

9.3

1.2
Relation to multivariate trace functionals

As will become clear below, the analysis of the three-parameter family
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}
is closely related to structural properties of certain optimized trace functionals. We will briefly overview this connection and its history here.

Let
p
,
q
, and
s
be real parameters, and let
K
be an arbitrary but fixed matrix. Consider trace functionals of the form

\Psi_{p,q,s}(A,B)=\Tr\left(A^{\frac{p}{2}}KB^{q}K^{\dagger}A^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{s},

(17)

where
A
and
B
are positive semidefinite matrices. These trace functionals arise in the formulation of various entropic quantities in mathematical physics and quantum information, often in reparametrized or disguised forms. Their geometric properties have inspired the development of numerous methods and techniques in matrix analysis.

For instance, the celebrated Lieb concavity theorem
[
36
]
asserts that the map
(A,B)\mapsto\Psi_{p,q,1}(A,B)
is jointly concave whenever
p,q\geq 0
and
p+q\leq 1
. Lieb’s proof relies on an analytic extension of the functional and the Hadamard three-line theorem. As an application, he resolved the Wigner–Yanase–Dyson conjecture
[
60
,
36
]
, and, in collaboration with Ruskai
[
35
]
, established the strong subadditivity of quantum entropy. Ando
[
1
]
later gave an alternative proof based on integral representations of operator convex and concave functions, and also showed joint convexity when
-1\leq p\leq 0
and
p+q=1
.

Motivated by the introduction of sandwiched Rényi divergences in quantum information
[
42
,
61
]
, further special cases with
s\neq 1
were explored by Frank and Lieb
[
16
]
, using variational characterizations, and by Beigi
[
6
]
, employing complex interpolation techniques. Extending these results, Zhang
[
63
]
confirmed a conjecture in
[
11
]
and fully characterized the parameter regimes for which the map
(A,B)\mapsto\Psi_{p,q,s}(A,B)
is jointly convex or concave. Moreover, observe that
D_{\alpha,z}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log{\Psi_{p,q,s}(\rho, \sigma)}
, where
K=I
, and
p=\frac{\alpha}{z},q=\frac{1-\alpha}{z},s=z
. Using his joint convexity/concavity characterization of
\Psi
, Zhang determined the exact parameter ranges for which
D_{\alpha,z}
is monotone under completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps.

In a closely related but distinct direction, Carlen, Frank, and Lieb
[
10
]
also studied trivariate trace functionals of the form

\Phi_{p,q,r,s}(A,B,C)=\Tr\left(A^{\frac{p}{2}}KB^{\frac{q}{2}}C^{r}B^{\frac{q}{2}}K^{\dagger}A^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{s},

(18)

where
A
,
B
, and
C
are positive semidefinite matrices. They showed in
[
10
, Corollary 3.3]
that when
K=I
and
p,q,r\neq 0
, the trivariate functional
(A,B,C)\mapsto\Phi_{p,q,r,1}(A,B,C)
is never jointly concave, and is jointly convex only under the conditions
q=2
,
p,r<0
, and
-1\leq p+r\leq 0
.

These pathological results for
\Phi
suggest that, although
\Phi
and
\Psi
are formally similar, their behavior with respect to joint convexity/concavity is quite different.
Nonetheless, as we shall see (see Corollary
3.6
and Remark
3.7
),
\Psi
can be formulated as a variational expression involving
\Phi
, namely, for
t>s>0
, we may write

\Psi_{p,q,t}(A,B)=\opt_{C}\Phi_{p,q,\frac{1}{s}-\frac{1}{t},s}^{\frac{t}{s}}(A,B,C),

(19)

where the optimization, denoted by
\opt
, is taken to be a supremum or an infimum over density matrices (i.e., positive matrices with unit trace), depending on whether the problem is concave or convex in
C
. This indicates that
\Phi
—despite its pathological behavior as a trivariate functional—exhibits desirable convexity properties once optimized over
C
.

Motivated by the applications considered in this paper, namely, the formulation and analysis of quantum conditional entropies, we adopt a more general framework. Specifically, we examine a setting in which the trace functional
\Phi
acts on matrices defined on finite-dimensional bipartite systems, with the optimization carried out locally on only one of the subsystems, i.e., we study the functional

(A,B)\mapsto\opt_{C}\Phi_{p,q,r,s}(A,I\otimes B,I\otimes C)
,
where
C
is a density matrix on the second subsystem. In contrast to (
19
), when both subsystems are non-trivial, the optimization over
C
no longer admits a closed-form solution. In this more general setting, we establish convexity/concavity properties for this new bivariate trace functional.
Our conditional entropies can then be expressed in terms of
\Phi
as

\exp\big( (1-\alpha) H_{\alpha,z}^\lambda(A|B)_\rho\big)=\opt_{\sigma_{B}}\Phi_{\frac{\alpha}{z},\frac{\bar{\lambda}\bar{\alpha}}{z},\frac{\lambda\bar{\alpha}}{z},z}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}),

(20)

where
\bar{\alpha}=1-\alpha
and
\bar{\lambda}=1-\lambda
. Hence, several properties of the conditional entropies are connected to those of the trace functionals.

2
A new concept of conditional entropy

In this section, we formally define the new conditional entropy that forms the central focus of this work, and explore its relationship with related quantities in the literature.

Throughout,
A
and
B
are finite-dimensional systems of possibly different dimensions. We denote by
\mathcal{L}(A,B)
the set of all matrices acting from
A
to
B
, and write
\mathcal{L}(A):=\mathcal{L}(A,A)
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(A)
the set of positive semidefinite matrices on
A
, and by
\mathcal{S}(A)\subset\mathcal{P}(A)
the subset of unit-trace matrices, representing quantum states. We use the terms
density matrix
and
quantum state
interchangeably. For a bipartite matrix
X_{AB}
, we define the marginals
X_{A}=\Trm_{B}(X_{AB})
and
X_{B}=\Trm_{A}(X_{AB})
via partial trace over the respective subsystems. We adopt the convention that identity matrices are inserted implicitly in tensor products. In particular, for matrices
X_{AB}
and
Y_{B}
, the product
X_{AB}Y_{B}
is shorthand for
X_{AB}(I_{A}\otimes Y_{B})
, where
I_{A}
denotes the identity on
A
.

2.1
A three-parameter family

In the following definition, we define the negative powers in the sense of generalized inverses; i.e., when we take the negative powers of positive operators, we simply ignore the kernel. Moreover,
\rho^{0}
equals the projector on the support of
\rho
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
,
z\in(0,\infty)
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(A)
and
\tau,\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(A)
such that
\rho\ll\tau
. If
(\lambda(1-\alpha)\geq 0\wedge\tau\not\perp\sigma)\vee\rho\ll\sigma
, then

\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho,\tau,\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log{\Trm\Big(\rho^\frac{\alpha}{2z}\tau^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}}\tau^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho^\frac{\alpha}{2z}\Big)^z}\,.

(21)

In all other cases,
\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho,\tau,\sigma)=+\infty
.

With this in mind, and noting that
\rho_{AB}\ll I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
, we proceed to define the new conditional entropy as follows

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
,
z\in(0,\infty)
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}
, we define the following quantity:

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}:=\begin{cases}\sup_{\sigma_{B}}-\Upsilon^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\big)&\lambda>0\\
\inf_{\sigma_{B}}-\Upsilon^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\big)&\lambda<0\end{cases}

(22)

For
\lambda=0
, the optimization is taken to be a supremum if
\alpha<1
and an infimum if
\alpha>1
.

In the following, for compactness, we often denote the optimization with “
\opt
” as

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=\opt_{\sigma_{B}}-\Upsilon^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\big).

(23)

Here, the optimization denoted by “
\opt
” is defined as the supremum over the set of all quantum states
\sigma_{B}
when
\lambda>0
, and as the infimum when
\lambda<0
. The choice between infimum and supremum depends on the sign of the exponents in the states being optimized: if the optimization is concave, it represents a supremum; otherwise, it represents an infimum.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that, based on a standard argument for lower/upper semicontinuity, in the region
\mathcal{D}
, the optimization over the states
\sigma_{B}
in Definition
2.2
is always achieved, allowing the infimum and supremum to be replaced by the minimum and maximum, respectively. We refer to Appendix
A
for further details. Moreover, throughout the paper, when proving properties of
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
, we typically restrict attention to full-rank states
\rho
. This entails no loss of generality, since the extension to arbitrary states follows from the continuity of
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
in
\rho
(see Proposition
A.3
).

Finally, we introduce some definitions that will be used in the sequel.
We define

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B}):=\exp{(\alpha-1)\Upsilon(\rho_{AB},I_A \otimes\rho_B, I_A \otimes\sigma_B)},

(24)

and denote its optimized version by

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}:=\opt_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B}).

(25)

In this case, the symbol “
\opt
” denotes the infimum over quantum states
\sigma_{B}
when
\lambda(1-\alpha)<0
, and the supremum otherwise.

2.2

5
Data-processing inequality

Definition 5.1
.

We define the region
\mathcal{D}:=\mathcal{D}_{1}\cup\mathcal{D}_{2}
, called the data-processing or DPI region, where
\mathcal{D}_{1},\mathcal{D}_{2}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:0<\alpha<1,\ 1-z\leq\alpha\leq z\ \text{and}\ 1-\frac{z}{1-\alpha}\leq\lambda\leq 1\right\},

(105)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:1<\alpha<\infty,\ \alpha-1\leq z\leq\alpha\leq 2z\ \text{and}\ 1+\frac{z}{1-\alpha}\leq\lambda\leq 1\right\}.

(106)

This is the parameter region for which the data-processing inequality and several other properties of the conditional entropy can be established. The main goal of this section is to prove the DPI for
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}
whenever
(\alpha,z,\lambda)
lies in
\mathcal{D}
, thereby justifying this terminology.

When studying conditional entropies, and hence measures of conditional uncertainty, it is natural to impose axioms motivated by operational principles. A central requirement is an appropriate data-processing inequality for quantum conditional entropies.
In the classical setting, i.e., for probability distributions, unconditional entropies are expected to be monotone under doubly stochastic maps
[
18
, Section 2.B]
. By Birkhoff’s theorem, every doubly stochastic map can be expressed as a convex combination of permutations. In the quantum setting, this notion is naturally extended by requiring monotonicity under convex combinations of unitary channels, namely maps of the form

\mathcal{M}(\cdot)=\sum_{k}p_{k}\,U_{k}(\cdot)\,U_{k}^{\dagger},

(107)

where
U_{k}
are unitary operators and
\{p_{k}\}
is a probability distribution. We refer to such maps as mixed unitary channels.

In the conditional scenario, however, additional requirements arise. Processing the side information system
B
alone should not reduce the uncertainty about
A
. Indeed, by the non-signalling principle, local operations on
B
cannot increase correlations with
A
, and a meaningful conditional entropy should reflect this property. Moreover, one must consider a larger class of operations. Suppose an observer performs a measurement on the side information system
B
and, conditioned on the measurement outcome, applies to system
A
a channel given by a convex combination of unitaries. Such conditioned operations allow information obtained from
B
to influence the processing of
A
, while still prohibiting signalling from
A
to
B
. Since no additional information about
A
is revealed in this procedure, one expects that the conditional entropy should not decrease under these channels. These operations correspond to bipartite channels of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{AB\to AB^{\prime}}(\cdot)=\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}_{A\to A}^{i}\otimes\mathcal{N}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{i}(\cdot),

(108)

where each
\mathcal{M}_{A\to A}^{i}
is a convex combination of unitaries acting on
A
, and
\{\mathcal{N}_{B\to B^{\prime}}^{i}\}_{i}
is an instrument acting on
B
.

These conditions may be viewed as minimal data-processing requirements. A natural question, however, is whether monotonicity continues to hold for a broader class of channels. In particular, the operations on
A
may be taken to be unital or sub-unital, as considered in
[
58
,
19
]
. This class is strictly larger, since the set of unital channels strictly contains the set of mixed unitary channels (see e.g.,
[
43
, Problem 8.3]
).

Below, we establish this stronger form of the DPI. Finally, we note that monotonicity can also be studied under the even larger class of conditionally mixing channels introduced in
[
19
]
(see Section
5.4
for more details).

We first recall that a quantum instrument is a collection of completely positive trace-nonincreasing operations
\{\mathcal{N}^{i}\}_{i}
such that the overall transformation
\sum_{i}\mathcal{N}^{i}
is trace-preserving. We aim to prove

Theorem 5.2

(Data-processing inequality)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
, and let
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. Furthermore, let
\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
be defined as

\sigma_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}=\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}^{i}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{i}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})\,,

(109)

where each
\mathcal{M}^{i}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}
is a sub-unital channel and
\{\mathcal{N}^{i}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}\}_{i}
is a quantum instrument. Then, the following inequality holds

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A^{\prime}|B^{\prime})_{\sigma}\,.

(110)

To prove this result, we first establish the data processing inequality separately for two types of operations: a sub-unital channel acting on Alice’s subsystem and an arbitrary quantum channel acting on Bob’s subsystem. The monotonicity under sub-unital channels on Alice’s side is presented in Proposition
5.8
, while the corresponding result for general maps on Bob’s side is stated in Proposition
5.5
. Finally, in Section
5.3
, we combine these two ingredients to prove the full monotonicity under the most general class of maps involving conditioned operations.

We begin by proving monotonicity under channels acting on Bob’s side. The key idea is to apply Theorem
3.1
to observe that the quantity
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}=\exp((1-\alpha) H^\lambda_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho})
is convex, concave, or log-concave, depending on the parameter regime (see Proposition
5.4
). We then show that these concavity/convexity properties imply the data processing inequality under the action of a channel on the side information (Subsection
5.1
). The monotonicity under the action of a sub-unital channel on Alice’s system is easier to prove and follows directly from an expression of the conditional entropy in terms of the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy (Subsection
5.2
).

Remark 5.3
.

It is natural to ask whether the DPI for
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}
can be derived directly from the DPI for an underlying “trivariate divergence.” However, as we demonstrate below, this is not the case.

In
[
10

, the authors show that, for nonzero

,
s
and

(A,B,C)\mapsto\Tr\left(A^{\frac{r}{2}}B^{\frac{s}{2}}C^{t}B^{\frac{s}{2}}A^{\frac{r}{2}}\right)

(111)

is never concave, and is convex only under the conditions

,
t,r<0
, and
-1\leq t+r<0
. Consequently, for example, the trace functional in (

r=2
,

. It is well-known that the failure of convexity in such functionals implies a violation of the DPI. Yet, the conditional entropy

, which is associated with this functional, is the dual of
H^{\downarrow}_{1/3,2/3}
(see Theorem

H^{\downarrow}_{1/3,2/3}
satisfies the DPI—by virtue of the DPI for the underlying

-
z
Rényi divergence—it follows from standard duality arguments (see, e.g.,
[

) that

must also satisfy the DPI.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}

∎

As shown below, the concavity and convexity results stated in Proposition

5.1
DPI under channels on Bob’s system

Lemma 5.9

Let

We begin by applying the data processing inequality under partial trace over an auxiliary register

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}^{i}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{i}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})\right)\leq f\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}^{i}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{i}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\right).

\displaystyle f\left(\sum_{i}\mathcal{M}^{i}_{A\rightarrow A^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{N}^{i}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}(\rho_{AB})\otimes\outerproduct{i}{i}_{B^{\prime\prime}}\right)

5.4
DPI under conditionally mixing channels

In this section, we establish that our new conditional entropies satisfy the data-processing inequality for a closely related class of channels, known as conditionally mixing channels, for a restricted set of parameters. These channels were introduced in
[
19
, Section 3]
, where the authors also define conditional entropies in
[
19
, Definition 4]
as quantities that are monotone under these channels, in addition to satisfying other desirable properties such as additivity, invariance under isometries, and normalization. We will show that
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}
fulfills all of these axioms, at least when
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. As we have already argued that our quantities cannot be derived in canonical forms from an underlying divergence, this shows that not all conditional entropies admit such a representation, thereby answering a question posed in
[
19
]
.

The remainder of this section is structured as follows: we first introduce conditionally mixing channels; next, we establish monotonicity under these channels for
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
; and finally, we show that the properties of additivity, invariance under isometries, and normalization are also satisfied.

We begin by recalling the definition of conditionally mixing channels
[
19
, Section 3]
. These channels are characterized by being both semicausal and conditionally unital. A channel is semicausal if it does not allow information to flow from the system of interest to the side information. If this condition is violated, monotonicity of the conditional entropy cannot be expected, as revealing information about the system would reduce uncertainty. A channel is conditionally unital if it does not reduce maximal randomness. Formally, this means that the identity operator (corresponding to the maximally mixed state) on the system of interest must be preserved under the channel, while arbitrary transformations may be applied to the side information.
More rigorously, conditionally mixing channels are defined as follows
[
19
, Definition 3]
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}
be a quantum channel. Then,

1.

\mathcal{N}
is said to be semicausal from
A
to
B
, if there exists a quantum channel
\mathcal{M}_{B\rightarrow B^{\prime}}
such that for every quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathcal{S}(AB)
, it holds that

\Tr_{A}\big(\mathcal{N}(\rho_{AB})\big)=\mathcal{M}(\rho_{B});

(147)

2.

\mathcal{N}
is said to be conditionally unital, if for every quantum state
\rho_{B}\in\mathcal{S}(B)
, there exists a quantum state
\sigma_{B^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}(B^{\prime})
such that

\mathcal{N}(I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B^{\prime}}.

(148)

A quantum channel is said to be conditionally mixing if it is semicausal and conditionally unital.

We now prove that the new conditional entropies satisfy monotonicity under conditionally mixing channels for
\lambda\geq 0
.

Theorem 5.11

(DPI under conditionally mixing channels)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
with
\lambda\geq 0
and
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. Then, for any conditionally mixing channel
\mathcal{N}_{AB\rightarrow AB^{\prime}}
and
\sigma_{AB^{\prime}}=\mathcal{N}(\rho_{AB})
, we have

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B^{\prime})_{\sigma}.

(149)

Our proof leverages the asymptotic form for
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}
derived in Lemma
8.2
, which involves the weighted Kubo-Ando geometric mean and the universal state introduced in Section
8
. By combining the DPI for the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi divergence with the operator monotonicity of the geometric mean under the action of a quantum channel for
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, we obtain the desired result. We remark that the argument breaks down for
\lambda<0
, as the geometric mean is no longer monotone under channels in this range.

Proof.

Let
\omega_{B^{n}}
be the universal state in Section
8
. Since by Lemma
8.1
,
\omega_{B^{n}}
and
\rho_{B^{n}}:=\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
commute, Lemma
8.2
implies that

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{ \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \Big(\rho_{B^n}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^n}^{\lambda} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^z}.

(150)

Recall that the Kubo–Ando weighted geometric mean is defined as
\rho\#_{\lambda}\sigma:=\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{\lambda}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Since
\rho_{B^{n}}=\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
and
\omega_{B^{n}}
commute, the sandwiched expression inside the logarithm in (

) simplifies as

\displaystyle\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\lambda}\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}=\rho_{B^{n}}\#_{\lambda}\omega_{B^{n}}.

(151)

With this substitution, the conditional entropy can be rewritten as

\displaystyle H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{1}{n}D_{\alpha,z}\left(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}\middle\|I_{A^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B^{n}}\#_{\lambda}\omega_{B^{n}}\right),

(152)

where
D_{\alpha,z}
denotes the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy as defined in Definition
2.4
.
Then,
the DPI of the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy implies that

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,z}

\displaystyle\left(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}\middle\|I_{A^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B^{n}}\#_{\lambda}\omega_{B^{n}}\right)

(153)

\displaystyle\geq D_{\alpha,z}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}})\middle\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(I_{A^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B^{n}}\#_{\lambda}\omega_{B^{n}})\right)

(154)

\displaystyle\geq D_{\alpha,z}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}})\middle\|\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(I_{A^{n}}\otimes\rho_{B^{n}})\#_{\lambda}\mathcal{N}^{\otimes n}(I_{A^{n}}\otimes\omega_{B^{n}})\right),

(155)

where in the last step we used that for a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}
, the geometric mean satisfies
\mathcal{E}(\rho\#_{\lambda}\sigma)\leq\mathcal{E}(\rho)\#_{\lambda}\mathcal{E}(\sigma)
for
\lambda\in[0,1]
(see e.g.
[
25

[

\alpha

(as they are tensor products) to obtain that (

(156)

Since by assumption

(157)

(158)

In addition, since

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,z}

We have proved monotonicity under conditionally mixing operations. Next, according to

\frac{z}{1-\alpha}=-\frac{\hat{z}}{1-\hat{\alpha}}
, we observe that (
166
) is equivalent to

\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\left\|\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right\|_{z}=\inf_{\sigma_{C}}\left\|\rho_{AC}^{\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2\hat{z}}}\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{C},\sigma_{C})\rho_{AC}^{\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2\hat{z}}}\right\|_{\hat{z}},

(168)

where
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,q}
is defined in Subsection
5.2
.
Next, we use the variational form of the norm from Lemma
B.3
. Specifically, since
z\leq 1
, it holds that
\|A\|_{z}=\inf_{\tau}\Trm(A\tau^{\frac{z-1}{z}})
, and therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right\|_{z}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{AB}}\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\tau_{AB}^{\frac{z-1}{z}}\right)

(169)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\tau_{AB}}\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{AB},\tau_{AB})\right),

(170)

since by (
167
) we have

\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{AB},\tau_{AB})=\rho_{AB}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\tau_{AB}^{\frac{\hat{\lambda}(1-\hat{\alpha})}{\hat{z}}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}=\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha-z}{2z}}\tau_{AB}^{\frac{z-1}{z}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha-z}{2z}}.

(171)

Let
\ket{\rho}_{ABC}=\sum\limits_{i}\sqrt{\lambda}_{i}\ket{i}_{AB}\ket{i}_{C}
be the Schmidt decomposition of
\rho_{ABC}
with respect to the partition
AB:C
and
\ket{\psi}_{AB:C}=\sum\limits_{i}\ket{i}_{AB}\ket{i}_{C}
be the unnormalized maximally entangled state. Then, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{AB},\tau_{AB})\Big|\psi\Big\rangle_{AB:C}

\displaystyle=\rho_{AB}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\tau_{AB}^{\frac{\hat{\lambda}(1-\hat{\alpha})}{\hat{z}}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\Big|\psi\Big\rangle_{AB:C}

(172)

\displaystyle=\rho_{C}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\tau_{C}^{\top\frac{\hat{\lambda}(1-\hat{\alpha})}{\hat{z}}}\rho_{C}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\Big|\psi\Big\rangle_{AB:C}

(173)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}\big(\rho_{C},\tau^{\top}_{C}\big)^{\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}\Big|\psi\Big\rangle_{AB:C},

(174)

where
\tau_{C}
is
\tau_{AB}
embedded in the space
C
through the isomorphism
AB\rightarrow C
induced by the Schmidt basis of
\ket{\rho}_{ABC}
. The transpose is computed with respect to the basis
\ket{i}_{C}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Trm\Big(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{AB},\tau_{AB})\Big)

(175)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\Big\langle\psi\Big|\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{AB},\tau_{AB})\Big|\psi\Big\rangle_{AB:C}

(176)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\Big\langle\psi\Big|\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}\big(\rho_{C},\tau^{\top}_{C}\big)\Big|\psi\Big\rangle_{AB:C}

(177)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\Big\langle\rho\Big|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}\big(\rho_{C},\tau^{\top}_{C}\big)\Big|\rho\Big\rangle_{ABC}.

(178)

Since we take the infimum of all states
\tau_{C}
, we can drop the transpose. This gives

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\left\|\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right\|_{z}

(179)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\inf_{\tau_{C}}\Big\langle\rho\Big|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{C},\tau_{C})\Big|\rho\Big\rangle_{ABC}

(180)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\inf_{\tau_{C}}\Big\langle\rho\Big|\rho_{B}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_{B}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}}\rho_{B}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\otimes\rho_{C}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\tau_{C}^{\frac{\hat{\lambda}(1-\hat{\alpha})}{\hat{z}}}\rho_{C}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\Big|\rho\Big\rangle_{ABC}.

(181)

We rewrote the last line since it is clear that the latter function is concave in
\sigma_{B}
as
\lambda(1-\alpha)/z\in(0,1)
and convex in
\tau_{C}
as
\hat{\lambda}(1-\hat{\alpha})/\hat{z}\in(-1,0)
(this follows from operator convexity/concavity in this range). Hence, we can use Sion’s minimax theorem to exchange the
\inf
with the
\sup
, and we obtain what we would get if we repeat the argument for

\inf_{\tau_{C}}\Big\|\rho_{AC}^{\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2\hat{z}}}\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{C},\tau_{C})\rho_{AC}^{\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2\hat{z}}}\Big\|_{\hat{z}}.

(182)

This shows the duality relation for the first region.

The proof of the duality relation between the other regions is completely analogous. In some cases, the minimax theorem is not even needed, as we either have a double supremum or a double infimum. For example, let us consider
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
,
z\geq 1
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
. Using the above relations, this implies that
\hat{\alpha}\geq 1
,
\hat{z}\geq 1
and
\hat{\lambda}\leq 0
. We now repeat the same steps. The only difference is that now the variational formulation of the norm contains a supremum for
z\geq 1
and the dual conditional entropy is now defined with a supremum since
\hat{\lambda}\leq 0
. We obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\left\|\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right\|_{z}

(183)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\sup_{\tau_{C}}\Big\langle\rho\Big|\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\otimes\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{C},\tau_{C})\Big|\rho\Big\rangle_{ABC}

(184)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\sup_{\tau_{C}}\Big\langle\rho\Big|\rho_{B}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_{B}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}}\rho_{B}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\otimes\rho_{C}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\tau_{C}^{\frac{\hat{\lambda}(1-\hat{\alpha})}{\hat{z}}}\rho_{C}^{\frac{(1-\hat{\lambda})(1-\hat{\alpha})}{2\hat{z}}}\Big|\rho\Big\rangle_{ABC}.

(185)

This is equivalent to the result we would obtain by repeating the argument for

\sup_{\sigma_{C}}\left\|\rho_{AC}^{\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2\hat{z}}}\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{C},\sigma_{C})\rho_{AC}^{\frac{\hat{\alpha}}{2\hat{z}}}\right\|_{\hat{z}}\,.

(186)

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

From equations (
181
) and (
185
), as well as similar equations for the other ranges, we obtain the following variational form involving the purification of
\rho_{AB}
. As shown in Section
9
, this form is particularly useful for deriving chain rules for the new conditional entropy. Specifically, for any state
\rho_{AB}
, we have

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)=\frac{z}{1-\alpha}\log{\opt_{\sigma_B}\opt_{\tau_C} \bigbra{\rho} \mathcal{G}_{\lambda,\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}(\rho_B,\sigma_B) \otimes\mathcal{G}_{\hat{\lambda},\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}(\rho_{C},\tau_{C})\bigket{\rho}_{ABC}},

(187)

where
\ket{\rho}_{ABC}
is a purification of
\rho_{AB}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda,q}
is as in Subsection
5.2
.

Remark 6.3
.

The dual parameters
(\hat{\alpha},\hat{z},\hat{\lambda})
can be computed explicitly in terms of the primal parameters
(\alpha,z,\lambda)
via (
167
), as follows:

\hat{\alpha}=\frac{z+(\lambda-1)(\alpha-1)}{z+\lambda(\alpha-1)},\qquad\hat{z}=\frac{z}{z+\lambda(\alpha-1)},\qquad\hat{\lambda}=\frac{z-1}{\alpha-1}.

(188)

Note that the regions
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are mapped to each other bijectively under this transformation.

Remark 6.4
.

The duality relations provide a closed-form solution for pure states. Specifically, for a pure state
\ket{\rho}_{AB}
, we have

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=-H_{\hat{\alpha}}(\rho_{B}),\qquad\hat{\alpha}=\frac{z+(\lambda-1)(\alpha-1)}{z+\lambda(\alpha-1)},

(189)

where
H_{\hat{\alpha}}(\rho_{B})=\frac{1}{1-\hat{\alpha}}\log\Trm\left(\rho_{B}^{\hat{\alpha}}\right)
is the Rényi entropy of the marginal state.

8
Asymptotic and variational characterizations

In this section, we derive alternative formulations for
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}
, which play a key role in establishing the chain rule in the subsequent section. We begin by obtaining expressions that incorporate the universal state in Proposition
8.2
, followed by the derivation of several useful forms involving the Schatten norm in Lemma
8.3
, inspired by the approach in
[
15
]
for sandwiched conditional entropies. In Lemmas
8.4
and
8.5
, these elements are combined to obtain an additional form. This formulation will be applied in the following section, using complex interpolation theory as developed in
[
15
]
to establish the chain rule.

It is noteworthy that the results contained in this section are of independent interest. Notably, we utilize the asymptotic expressions to establish the monotonicity properties with respect to the parameters
\alpha
and
z
in Section
10
. Moreover, the same form provides the foundation for an alternative proof of the DPI for the range
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
in Subsection
5.4
.

We frequently make use of the following lemma from the representation theory of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, consisting of permutations of
n
elements. Let
U_{A^{n}}
denote the natural unitary representation of
S_{n}
on
A^{n}
that permutes the subsystems
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{n}
. An operator
L_{A^{n}}
is said to be permutation-invariant if it satisfies
U_{A^{n}}(\pi)L_{A^{n}}U_{A^{n}}(\pi)^{\dagger}=L_{A^{n}}
for all
\pi\in S_{n}
. Similarly,
L_{A^{n}}
is said to be invariant under
n
-fold product unitaries if it satisfies
V_{A}^{\otimes n}L_{A^{n}}(V_{A}^{\dagger})^{\otimes n}=L_{A^{n}}
for all unitaries
V_{A}
on
A
. This lemma is the key to canceling out unitaries in certain optimizations. (See
[
21
, Lemma 1]
for a proof.)

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
A
be a quantum system with
|A|=d
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a state
\omega_{A^{n}}\in\mathcal{S}(A^{n})
, referred to as the universal state, such that for any permutation-invariant state
\tau_{A^{n}}\in\mathcal{S}(A^{n})
,

\tau_{A^{n}}\leq g_{n,d}\,\omega_{A^{n}}\qquad\text{where}\qquad g_{n,d}\leq(n+1)^{d^{2}-1}.

(194)

Moreover, the state
\omega_{A^{n}}
is permutation-invariant, commutes with all permutation-invariant states, and is invariant under
n
-fold product unitaries.

Proposition 8.2

(Asymptotic expression)
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state and
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(i)\qquad H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{ \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^nB^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\omega_{B^n}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^z}.

(195)

\displaystyle(ii)\qquad H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{z}{1-\alpha}\log{\opt_{\sigma_B} \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}} (\sigma^{\otimes n}_B)^\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}\rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\omega_{A^nB^n}^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\right)}.

(196)

(iii)\;\;
For
z\leq 1
and
\lambda(1-\alpha)\leq 0
, we have

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{z}{1-\alpha}\log{ \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\omega_{B^n}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\omega_{A^nB^n}^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\right)}.

(197)

Here, we denoted
\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}=\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n}
and
\rho_{B^{n}}=\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
.

Proof.

(i) Let us start with the case
\lambda(1-\alpha)/z\in[-1,0)
. In this range, the power is operator antimonotone. We apply the properties of the universal state in Lemma
8.1
. The following chain of inequalities holds:

\displaystyle\log{\inf_{\sigma_B}\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^\frac{\alpha}{2z} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_B^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{AB}^\frac{\alpha}{2z}\right)^z}

(198)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad=\frac{1}{n}\log{\inf_{\sigma_B} \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\big(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\big)^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^z}

(199)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{n}\log{ \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\omega_{B^n}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^z}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{n}\log{g_{n,d}}

(200)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{n}\log{\inf_{\sigma_{B^n}} \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_{B^n}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^z}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{n}\log{g_{n,d}}

(201)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad=\log{\inf_{\sigma_B}\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^\frac{\alpha}{2z} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_B^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{AB}^\frac{\alpha}{2z}\right)^z}+\lambda(1-\alpha)\frac{1}{n}\log{g_{n,d}}.

(202)

In (
200
), we used that the exponent of
\sigma_{B}
is operator antimonotone, the inequality
\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\leq g_{n,d}\,\omega_{B^{n}}
holds, and the trace functional
M\mapsto\Tr(f(M))
inherits monotonicity from the function
f
(see, e.g.,
[
12
]
). Here,
d=d_{B}
. Furthermore, in (
202
), we applied the additivity of the conditional entropy. We then multiply by
1/(1-\alpha)
and take the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
on both sides. Note that they both converge to the conditional entropy. Indeed, the term
\frac{1}{n}\log{g_{n,d}}
vanishes. Therefore, the quantity involving the universal state in (
200
) must equal the conditional entropy. The case
\lambda(1-\alpha)/z\in(0,1]
is similar, with all the inequalities above reversed.

(ii) The proof follows a similar argument to (i), but instead of employing
\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\leq g_{n,d}\omega_{B^{n}}
, it relies on the inequality

\tau_{AB}^{\otimes n}\leq g_{n,d^{2}}\omega_{A^{n}B^{n}}
and the variational form of the norm. Here,
d=\max\{d_{A},d_{B}\}
. Let us first consider the case
z\leq 1
. The following chain of inequalities holds:

\displaystyle\log{\opt_{\sigma_B}\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^\frac{\alpha}{2z} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_B^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{AB}^\frac{\alpha}{2z}\right)^z}

(203)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{n}\log{\opt_{\sigma_B} \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\big(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\big)^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^z}

(204)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{z}{n}\log{\opt_{\sigma_B} \inf_{\tau_{AB}} \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\big(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\big)^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\big(\tau_{AB}^{\otimes n}\big)^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\right)}

(205)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{z}{n}\log{\opt_{\sigma_B} \textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\big(\sigma_{B}^{\otimes n}\big)^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_{B^n}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{A^n B^n}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\omega_{A^n B^n}^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\right)}+\frac{z-1}{n}\log{g_{n,d^2}}

(206)

9
Chain rules

In this section, we derive chain rules for the conditional entropy
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)
. We integrate the formulations derived in the previous section with a result from complex interpolation theory for Schatten norms, with the latter technique being similar to that in
[
15
]
. However, unlike the simpler case addressed in
[
15
]
, this context presents further challenges. Notably, results from complex interpolation theory for multivariate trace inequalities often require optimizations over unitaries, particularly in the non-commuting case involving more than two matrices
[
54
]
. However, the expressions derived earlier utilize universal permutation-invariant states, effectively eliminating the usual requirement for optimization over unitaries. Indeed, the universal state commutes with any permutation-invariant state, enabling us to asymptotically reduce the problem to one involving only two non-commuting matrices.

Theorem 9.1

(Chain rule)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda),(\beta,w,\mu),(\gamma,v,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{ABC}
be a tripartite quantum state. Suppose the parameters satisfy the relations

\frac{z}{1-\alpha}=\frac{w}{1-\beta}+\frac{v}{1-\gamma},\qquad\frac{1-z}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1-w}{1-\beta},\qquad\mu=\frac{1-v}{1-\gamma}.

(245)

If
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0
, the following chain rule holds:

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq H_{\beta,w}^{\mu}(A|BC)_{\rho}+H^{\lambda}_{\gamma,v}(B|C)_{\rho}.

(246)

The inequality is reversed when
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
.

We divide the full proof into several cases. The first case considers
\alpha>1,(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0
, while the second case addresses scenarios where
\alpha>1,(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
. For the first case, we further divide the proof into subcases:
z\geq 1
and
z\leq 1
. The arguments for the second case are analogous, so we outline only the key steps. Finally, the case
\alpha<1
can be handled by duality.

9.1
Proof of the case
\alpha>1
with
(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0

Note that from the first relation among the parameters for the chain rule in equation (
245
), we need to consider only the case
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>1
as the case
\alpha>1
and
\beta,\gamma<1
cannot happen.

Let us first consider the case
z\geq 1
. Note that since
\alpha,\beta>1
, the second condition in (
245
) implies that
w\geq 1
. We aim to prove that

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq H_{\beta,w}^{\mu}(A|BC)_{\rho}+H^{\lambda}_{\gamma,v}(B|C)_{\rho}.

(247)

Using the asymptotic forms in Lemma
8.4
, it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau_{D^{n}}}\left\|\omega_{C^{n}}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho_{C^{n}}^{\;\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}X_{A^{n}D^{n}\rightarrow B^{n}C^{n}}\rho_{D^{n}}^{\;\top\frac{1-\hat{\lambda}}{2}\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}\tau_{D^{n}}^{\top\frac{\hat{\lambda}}{2}\frac{1-\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{z}}}\right\|_{2}^{2\frac{z}{\alpha-1}}

We define the ratios

9.3
Proof of the case
\alpha<1

The case for
\alpha<1
can be entirely derived by applying duality to the relations obtained previously. The parameter relations remain unchanged even when
\alpha<1
. Now, let us apply the duality relation to the chain rule derived for the case where
\alpha>1
. Consider a purification
\ket{\rho}_{ABCD}
. We then have the inequality

\displaystyle-H^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\alpha},\hat{z}}(AB|D)_{\rho}\geq-H_{\hat{\beta},\hat{w}}^{\hat{\mu}}(A|D)_{\rho}-H^{\hat{\theta}}_{\hat{\gamma},\hat{v}}(B|AD)_{\rho}.

(277)

By relabeling
A\leftrightarrow B
and
D\leftrightarrow C
, this becomes

\displaystyle H^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\alpha},\hat{z}}(AB|C)_{\rho}\leq H_{\hat{\beta},\hat{w}}^{\hat{\mu}}(B|C)_{\rho}+H^{\hat{\theta}}_{\hat{\gamma},\hat{v}}(A|BC)_{\rho}.

(278)

The second relation in (
245
) shows that
\hat{\lambda}=\hat{\mu}
for the original parameters. Therefore, as expected,
H^{\hat{\lambda}}_{\hat{\alpha},\hat{z}}(AB|C)
and
H_{\hat{\beta},\hat{w}}^{\hat{\mu}}(B|C)
must share the same third parameter.

Let us now verify that the relations among the parameters in (
245
) hold for the hatted parameters. The first relation in (
245
) leads to an analogous relation for the hatted parameters. The second relation follows from the fact that
\lambda=\frac{1-\hat{z}}{1-\hat{\alpha}}=\frac{1-\hat{v}}{1-\hat{\gamma}}
. To prove the third relation, we need to demonstrate that
\hat{\theta}=\frac{\hat{w}-1}{\hat{\beta}-1}
. This holds due to the third relation for the original parameters in (
245
), as
\hat{\theta}=\frac{v-1}{\gamma-1}=\mu=\frac{\hat{w}-1}{\hat{\beta}-1}
.

9.4
Implications for Petz and sandwiched Rényi conditional entropy

Here we discuss special cases of the above chain rule that are in terms of the Petz and sandwiched conditional Rényi entropy.

Corollary 9.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
lie in their respective DPI regions. If
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0
, then the following chain rules hold:

\displaystyle\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma};

(279)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}.

(280)

In each inequality, the arrows on the entropies indexed by
\alpha
and
\gamma
may simultaneously be reversed under the same parameter relation. The inequalities themselves are reversed when
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
.

In the next corollary, we obtain hybrid chain rules involving both
\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}_{\alpha}
and
\widetilde{H}_{\alpha}
.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
lie in their respective DPI regions. If
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0
, then the following chain rules hold:

\displaystyle\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\uparrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\uparrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\widetilde{H}^{\uparrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}+\frac{\gamma}{1-\gamma}

(281)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\alpha}(AB|C)_{\rho}\geq\widetilde{H}^{\downarrow}_{\beta}(A|BC)_{\rho}+\overline{\mkern-4.0muH\mkern-1.0mu}^{\downarrow}_{\gamma}(B|C)_{\rho},\ \ \ \text{when}\ \ \ \frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

(282)

In each inequality, the arrows on the entropies indexed by
\alpha
and
\gamma
may simultaneously be reversed under the same parameter relation. The inequalities themselves are reversed when
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
.

10
Monotonicity in the parameters

In this section, we establish the monotonicity of the conditional entropy in the parameters
\alpha
,
z
, and
\lambda
. Additionally, we address further monotonicities at the end of the section. The key component of the proof is the asymptotic form derived in Lemma
8.2
(i). Specifically, since
\omega_{B^{n}}
and
\rho_{B^{n}}=\rho_{B}^{\otimes n}
commute, we obtain the following expression:

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log\textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\left(\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\lambda}\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^{z}.

(283)

This form clearly shows that the monotonicity of the conditional entropy in the parameters
\alpha
and
z
is closely related to the monotonicity in the same parameters of the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi divergence (see Definition
2.4
). We prove the monotonicity with respect to the parameters
\alpha
and
z
by employing complex interpolation techniques (see Lemma
B.5
). We note that the monotonicity of the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi divergence with respect to
\alpha
was first established in
[
26
]
. Our approach, based on complex interpolation theory, also offers a straightforward alternative proof of this result in the DPI region. Finally, to establish monotonicity in
\lambda
, we follow a completely different approach by explicitly computing the derivative and proving its positivity.

10.1
Monotonicity in
\alpha

The proof of the following result primarily relies on the asymptotic form in (
283
) and Lemma
B.5
.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. The function
\alpha\rightarrow H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is monotonically decreasing.

Proof.

We can write
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}|\omega_{B^{n}})
as

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}|\omega_{B^{n}})=\left\|\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\big(\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\right\|_{2z}^{2z},

(284)

We apply Lemma
B.5
with

F(s):=\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\;\frac{1}{2z}\left(s\alpha+(1-s)\beta\right)}\big(\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\lambda}\big)^{\frac{1}{z}\left(1-(s\alpha+(1-s)\beta\right)}

(285)

and
p_{0}=p_{1}=p_{\theta}=2z
. Note that we need to verify the condition
p_{0},p_{1}\geq 1/2
, which is satisfied because
z\geq 1/2
holds in the DPI region
\mathcal{D}
. By leveraging the unitary invariance of the norm, which allows us to simplify the overall phases, we conclude that
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}|\omega_{B^{n}})
is log-convex, i.e., for any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, it holds

Q_{\theta\alpha+(1-\theta)\beta,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}|\omega_{B^{n}})\leq Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}|\omega_{B^{n}})^{\theta}Q_{\beta,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}|\omega_{B^{n}})^{1-\theta}.

(286)

Let us assume that
\alpha>\beta>1
and set
\theta=\frac{\beta-1}{\alpha-1}\in(0,1)
. Then, log-convexity gives

\log{Q_{\beta,z}^\lambda(\rho_{A^n B^n}|\omega_{B^n})}=\log{Q_{\theta\alpha+ 1-\theta,z}^\lambda(\rho_{A^n B^n}|\omega_{B^n})}\leq\theta\log{Q_{\alpha,z}^\lambda(\rho_{A^n B^n}|\omega_{B^n})},

(287)

since
\log{Q_{1,z}^\lambda(\rho_{A^n B^n}|\omega_{B^n})}=0
. We obtain the desired result by multiplying both sides by
-1/n
and letting
n\to\infty
.

For the case
1>\alpha>\beta
, an analogous argument holds with
\theta=\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\beta}
.

Finally, the case
\alpha<1<\beta
follows from the previously derived results, in conjunction with Proposition
12.1
, as
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}<H<H^{\lambda}_{\beta,z}
.
The latter statement is true because it is always possible to find a monotone path in
\mathcal{D}
that leads to the conditional entropy. To show that
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}<H
for
\alpha<1
and
z\geq 1
, we can use the monotonicity in
\lambda
and the monotonicity in
\alpha
shown earlier. For
\alpha<1
and
z\leq 1
instead we can use the monotonicity in
\lambda
and the monotonicity in
\alpha
of the sandwiched conditional entropy that follows from the monotonicity of the underlying divergence (see e.g.
[
42
]
). Similarly, to prove
H<H^{\lambda}_{\beta,z}
for
\beta>1
, we can apply duality and follow the same steps just described for
\alpha<1
.
∎

10.2
Monotonicity in
z

The monotonicity with respect to
z
follows from the Araki-Lieb-Thirring inequality, similar to the method used to establish the same monotonicity for the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropies in
[
37
, Proposition 6]
. It is worth noting that this inequality can also be derived using complex interpolation theory (see, e.g.,
[
54
]
).

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. The function
z\mapsto Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is monotonically decreasing. Hence, the function
z\mapsto H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is monotonically decreasing for
\alpha<1
and monotonically increasing for
\alpha>1
.

Proof.

We first demonstrate that for
z^{\prime}\leq z
, the following inequality holds:

\textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\Big(\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\lambda}\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^{z}\leq\textup{Tr}\left(\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z^{\prime}}}\Big(\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\lambda}\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z^{\prime}}}\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z^{\prime}}}\right)^{z^{\prime}}.

(288)

We apply the Araki-Lieb-Thirring (ALT) inequality, which states that for
0\leq r\leq 1
,
q\geq 0
, and positive semidefinite matrices
A
and
B
, the following holds:

\textup{Tr}\left(A^{\frac{r}{2}}B^{r}A^{\frac{r}{2}}\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}\leq\textup{Tr}\left(A^{\frac{1}{2}}BA^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{q}.

(289)

Setting
q=z^{\prime}
,
r=\frac{z^{\prime}}{z}
,
A=\rho_{A^{n}B^{n}}^{\frac{\alpha}{z^{\prime}}}
, and
B=\Big(\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\omega_{B^{n}}^{\lambda}\rho_{B^{n}}^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{z^{\prime}}}
, we multiply both sides by
\frac{1}{n(1-\alpha)}
. Finally, taking the limit as
n\to\infty
on both sides, together with (
283
), yields the desired result.
∎

10.3
Monotonicity in
\lambda

In this subsection, we aim to establish the monotonicity in
\lambda
.

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. The function
\lambda\rightarrow H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is monotonically increasing.

To prove this, we avoid using complex interpolation theory. Instead, we directly prove the positivity of the derivative. We first derive an alternative variational form for the conditional entropy and show that the term within the optimization is monotone in
\lambda
. Since the optimization on both sides of the inequality preserves the order, this leads directly to the desired result.

Lemma 10.4
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. Then, we have

H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}=\opt_{\tau_{AB}}\frac{1}{\hat{z}(1-\alpha)}\log{\textup{Tr}\left( \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}} \Tr_A\Big(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \tau_{AB}^{1-\frac{1}{z}} \rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\Big) \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\right)^{\hat{z}}}.

(290)

Here,
\hat{z}=z/(z+\lambda(\alpha-1))
is the dual parameter defined in Remark
6.3
and the optimization is performed over all bipartite quantum states
\tau_{AB}
.

Proof.

Using the variational form of the norm in Lemma
B.3
, we obtain

H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}=-\opt_{\sigma_{B}}\opt_{\tau_{AB}}\frac{z}{\alpha-1}\log{\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} I_A \otimes\Big(\rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}} \sigma_B^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}} \rho_B^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\Big)\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}} \tau_{AB}^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\right)}.

(291)

Within the region
\mathcal{D}
, we can exchange the supremum and infimum whenever necessary by applying Sion’s minimax theorem. The optimization problem over
\sigma_{B}
can then be solved using Lemma
B.3
, which leads to the desired result.
∎

The following lemma demonstrates that the term within the optimization in Lemma
10.4
is monotone in
\lambda
. However, our result is more general and may be of independent interest.

Lemma 10.5
.

Let
\rho
be a quantum state,
X\geq 0
,
x\neq 0
, and let
f(x),g(x)
be functions such that
xg^{\prime}(x)=g(x)
and
xf^{\prime}(x)g(x)=-1
. Then, the function

x\mapsto\frac{1}{x}\log{\textup{Tr}\left(X \rho^{f(x)} X\right)^{g(x)}}

(292)

is monotone.

Proof.

We follow a similar approach to the one used to prove
[
49
, Lemma 25]
. We denote
A:=X\rho^{f(x)}X
. The derivative with respect to
x
is

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{x^{2}}\log{\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}\right)}+\frac{1}{x\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}\right)}\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}(\log{A})g^{\prime}(x)+\big(g(x)f^{\prime}(x)\big)X\rho^{f(x)}(\log{\rho})XA^{g(x)-1}\right)

(293)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{x^{2}\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}\right)}\Big(\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}\log{A^{x g'(x)}}\right)-\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}\right)\log{\Trm\left(A^{g(x)}\right)}

(294)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\big(g(x)f^{\prime}(x)x\big)\Trm\left(X\rho^{f(x)}(\log{\rho})XA^{g(x)-1}\right)\Big).

(295)

We use the property
f(Y^{\dagger}Y)Y^{\dagger}=Y^{\dagger}f(YY^{\dagger})
, and set
Y=X\rho^{\frac{f(x)}{2}}
. We can then rewrite

\Trm\left(X\rho^{f(x)}(\log{\rho})XA^{g(x)-1}\right)=\Trm\left(\log{\rho})\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right),

(296)

where we define
\tilde{A}:=\rho^{\frac{f(x)}{2}}X\rho^{\frac{f(x)}{2}}
. Note that
A
and
\tilde{A}
share the same eigenvalues. Therefore, to prove that the function is monotonically increasing, using
g(x)f^{\prime}(x)x=-1
, we need to show that

\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\log{\tilde{A}^{g(x)}}\right)-\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)\log{\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)}-\Trm\left(\log{\rho})\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)\geq 0\,.

(297)

This condition is a consequence of the positivity of the relative entropy. Indeed,

\displaystyle\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\log{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle=\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)\Trm\left(\frac{\tilde{A}^{g(x)}}{\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)}\log{\rho}\right)

(298)

\displaystyle\leq\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)\Trm\left(\frac{\tilde{A}^{g(x)}}{\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)}\log{\frac{\tilde{A}^{g(x)}}{\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)}}\right)

(299)

\displaystyle=\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\log{\tilde{A}^{g(x)}}\right)-\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)\log{\Trm\left(\tilde{A}^{g(x)}\right)}.

(300)

The proof is complete.
∎

Finally, we combine the above result with the variational form in Lemma
10.4
to establish the desired monotonicity.

Proof of Proposition
10.3
.

Note that since
\Trm(AA^{\dagger})^{s}=\Trm(A^{\dagger}A)^{s}
, it is sufficient to derive the monotonicity for any
\tau_{AB}
of the function

\lambda\rightarrow F(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\hat{z}(1-\alpha)}\log{\textup{Tr}\left( X_B \rho_B^{ \frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{z}} X_B \right)^{\hat{z}}},

(301)

where
X_{B}:=\left(\Tr_{A}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\tau_{AB}^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Indeed, since the optimization over
\tau_{AB}
preserves monotonicity, the variational form in Lemma
10.4
implies the monotonicity of
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)
.

Let us set
x=\hat{z}
. We can rewrite the function in the new coordinates as

F(x)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{x}\log{\textup{Tr}\left(\left( X_B \rho_B^{f(x)} X_B \right)^{g(x)}\right)},

(302)

where
f(x)=\frac{1-\alpha}{z}-1+x^{-1}
and
g(x)=x
.

To prove monotonicity, we show that the derivative with respect to
\lambda
is positive. By the chain rule for derivatives, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}F(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}F(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}

(303)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{z}^{2}(1-\alpha)}{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}F(x)

(304)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{z}^{2}}{z}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\frac{1}{x}\log{\textup{Tr}\left( X_B \rho_B^{f(x)} X_B \right)^{g(x)}}\right).

(305)

Since
z,\hat{z}^{2}\geq 0
, it suffices to show that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\left(\frac{1}{x}\log{\textup{Tr}\left( X_B \rho_B^{f(x)} X_B \right)^{g(x)}}\right)\geq 0.

(306)

This is a consequence of Lemma
10.5
.
∎

We conclude this section by highlighting additional monotonicity results. These monotonicities also hold along the lines
z=1-\alpha
,
z=\alpha-1
, and
z=\alpha
. For the case
z=1-\alpha
, the proof follows a similar approach to that of
[
49
, Lemma 25]
and is derived from Lemma
10.5
with
x=1-\alpha
,
f(x)=-1+\frac{1}{x}
, and
g(x)=x
, in conjunction with the asymptotic form given in (

). The case
z=\alpha-1
follows an analogous reasoning. Finally, the proof for

employs the methods outlined in
[
6
]
.

11
Classical information

In this section, we derive an expression for partly classical registers and a closed-form expression for fully classical states.

Proposition 11.1

(Classical registers)
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{ABY}=\sum_{y}\rho(y)\hat{\rho}_{AB}(y)\otimes\outerproduct{y}{y}
. Then, the conditional entropy satisfies

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|BY)_{\rho}=\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}-\lambda\right)\log{\bigg(\sum\nolimits_{y}\rho(y)\exp\bigg(\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\lambda(1-\alpha)}H^\lambda_{\alpha,z}(A|B, Y=y)_{\rho}\bigg)\bigg)}.

(307)

Here, we denoted
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B,Y=y)_{\rho}=H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\hat{\rho}_{AB}(y)}
.

Proof.

This follows by guessing as ansatz the state

\sigma_{BY}=\sum_{y}\frac{\rho(y)Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)^{\frac{1}{1-\lambda(1-\alpha)}}_{\hat{\rho}_{AB}(y)}}{\sum_{x}\rho(x)Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)^{\frac{1}{1-\lambda(1-\alpha)}}_{\hat{\rho}_{AB}(x)}}\;\hat{\sigma}_{B}(y)\otimes\outerproduct{y}{y},

(308)

and verify that it satisfies the sufficient conditions for the minimum derived in Lemma
3.5
. Here,
\hat{\sigma}_{B}(y)
is the optimizer of
\hat{\rho}_{AB}(y)
.
∎

Let us consider a probability distribution
\rho(x,y)
. We define

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{H}}_{\alpha}(X|Y)_{\rho}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\Big(\sum\nolimits_{x,y} \rho(y) \rho(x|y)^\alpha\Big)}\,,

(309)

\displaystyle H^{\mathrm{A}}_{\alpha}(X|Y)_{\rho}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log{\bigg(\sum\nolimits_y \rho(y)\Big(\sum\nolimits_x \rho(x|y)^\alpha\Big)^\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigg)}\,.

(310)

The first conditional entropy,
H^{\mathrm{H}}_{\alpha}
, was introduced by Hayashi and Skoric et al.
[
22
,
53
]
, while the second one
H^{\mathrm{A}}_{\alpha}
was introduced by Arimoto
[
2
]
.

In the following, we derive a classical expression for the new conditional entropies. In the classical case, our quantity reduces to the one introduced by Hayashi and Tan in
[
20
]
. For
\lambda=0
, we recover the quantity
H^{\mathrm{H}}_{\alpha}
while for
\lambda=1
we obtain
H^{\mathrm{A}}_{\alpha}
. Moreover, the new quantity interpolates (and extrapolates) between these two definitions.

The fully classical expression for the new conditional entropy
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}
can be derived by considering the state
\rho_{x,y}=\sum_{xy}\rho(x,y)\outerproduct{x}{x}\otimes\outerproduct{y}{y}
and, for example, using the solution for the Petz case
z=1
in Corollary
4.2
. Indeed, in the classical case, the conditional entropy does not depend on
z
. Note that the closed form can also be extended outside the range for which the case
z=1
satisfies the DPI. To this purpose, let us define
\beta=\alpha/(1-\lambda(1-\alpha))
. We obtain

H_{\alpha,\beta}(X|Y)_{\rho}=\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\log{\bigg(\sum\nolimits_y \rho(y)\Big(\sum\nolimits_x \rho(x|y)^\alpha\Big)^\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\bigg)^\frac{1}{\beta}}.

(311)

Hence, in the classical case, we obtain a weighted
\beta
quasi-norm of the
\alpha
quasi-norm of the conditional distribution.
From the result obtained in Theorem
5.2
for the fully quantum case, we have that the classical conditional entropy satisfies the DPI in the region
-\infty\leq\lambda\leq 1
for
0<\alpha<1
and
1-\alpha/(\alpha-1)\leq\lambda\leq 1
for
\alpha>1
. In the coordinates
\alpha
and
\beta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\text{cl}}:

\displaystyle=\Big\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<\alpha<1,0<\beta\leq 1\Big\}\;\cup\;\Big\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\alpha>1,\beta\geq 1\Big\}.

(312)

Finally, by considering the state
\rho_{xyz}=\sum_{xyz}\rho(x,y,z)\outerproduct{x}{x}\otimes\outerproduct{y}{y}\otimes\outerproduct{z}{z}
, Thereom
9.1
yield a chain rule for classical states. For a probability distribution
\rho(x,y,z)
, we obtain if
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)>0

H_{\alpha}^{\lambda}(XY|Z)_{\rho}\geq H_{\beta}^{\mu}(X|YZ)_{\rho}+H_{\gamma}^{\lambda}(Y|Z)_{\rho}\quad\text{when}\quad\frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{1-\beta}-\mu+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}

(313)

that holds whenever there exist parameters
z,w
, and
v
such that the corresponding triples lie within the DPI region. Moreover, the inequality is reversed when
(\alpha-1)(\beta-1)(\gamma-1)<0
.

Dimension bounds

Lemma 11.2
.

Let

and

H^{\downarrow}_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\uparrow}_{\max}(A|B)_{\rho}
.

Proof.

The upper bound is established as

\displaystyle H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\lambda=1}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq H^{\uparrow}_{\max}(A|B)_{\rho},

(314)

where the first inequality follows from the monotonicity of
H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}
with respect to

(Proposition

), and the second inequality arises from the property
D_{\alpha,z}\geq\overline{\mkern-4.0muD\mkern-1.0mu}_{0}

[

.

The lower bound is obtained by applying the duality relations from Theorem

in conjunction with the previously derived upper bound.
∎

The latter bounds imply the following result
[

, Lemma 5.11]

Corollary 11.3
.

Let

and
\rho_{AB}
a state. Then, the following holds

-\log{\min\{\textup{rank}(\rho_A),\textup{rank}(\rho_B)\}}\leq H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq\log{\textup{rank}(\rho_A)}\,.

Moreover,

12
Limits

The first limit we examine leads to the von Neumann conditional entropy. We show that for any path within the DPI region, the new conditional entropy converges to the von Neumann entropy as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
.

Proposition 12.1
.

Let
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. Let
J
be an open interval of positive real numbers containing
1
, and let
f,g:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be two functions continuous at
\alpha=1
, such that
(\alpha,g(\alpha),f(\alpha))
lies in
\mathcal{D}
for all
\alpha\in J
. Then we have

\lim_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H_{\alpha,g(\alpha)}^{f(\alpha)}(A|B)_{\rho}=H(A|B)_{\rho}.

(316)

Proof.

The proof is constructed by leveraging the duality relations and the monotonicity properties in
z
and
\lambda
established in the previous sections. By combining these results, we derive both lower and upper bounds involving the von Neumann conditional entropy. Let us start with the upper bound. The monotonicity in
\lambda
established in Proposition
10.3
implies that

\limsup_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H^{f(\alpha)}_{\alpha,g(\alpha)}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq\limsup_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,g(\alpha)}(A|B)_{\rho}\leq\limsup_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,z_{0}}(A|B)_{\rho}=H(A|B)_{\rho}\,.

(317)

In the second inequality, we used the fact that the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy is monotone in
z
for any
z>0

[
37
, Proposition 6]
. Consequently, a fixed
z_{0}>0
exists that allows us to bound the conditional entropy using a path where
z
is held constant.

In the final inequality, we utilized a well-established argument based on the minimax theorem
[
40
, Corollary A.2]
(see, e.g.,
[
50
, Appendix A]
).
Specifically, the limits
\alpha\to 1^{-}
and
\alpha\to 1^{+}
can be expressed as an infimum or supremum, due to the monotonicity of the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy with respect to
\alpha
for any
z>0

[
26
, Theorem 3.1]
. Moreover, the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy is lower semicontinuous in its second argument (see, e.g.,
[
50
, Lemma 18]
). Consequently, by combining these properties, the limit can be exchanged with the optimization over all states using the minimax theorem in
[
40
, Corollary A.2]
. Finally, the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy converges to the Umegaki relative entropy for
\alpha\rightarrow 1
for any
z>0

[
37
, Theorem 4]
. Let us illustrate the argument with an example. Let us consider the limit
\alpha\rightarrow 1^{-}
. We then have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\rightarrow 1^{-}}\sup\limits_{\sigma_{B}}-D_{\alpha,z_{0}}(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\alpha<1}\sup_{\sigma_{B}}-D_{\alpha,z_{0}}(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(318)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}\inf_{\alpha<1}-D_{\alpha,z_{0}}(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(319)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}-D(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

(320)

\displaystyle=-D(\rho_{AB}\|I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\,.

(321)

For the lower bound, we apply the same reasoning in conjunction with the duality established in Theorem
6.1
. We have

\displaystyle\liminf_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}H^{f(\alpha)}_{\alpha,g(\alpha)}(A|B)_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}-H^{w(\alpha)}_{u(\alpha),v(\alpha)}(A|C)_{\rho}

(322)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}-H^{\uparrow}_{u(\alpha),v(\alpha)}(A|C)_{\rho}

(323)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\alpha\rightarrow 1}-H^{\uparrow}_{u(\alpha),z_{0}}(A|C)_{\rho}

(324)

\displaystyle=-H(A|C)_{\rho}

(325)

\displaystyle=H(A|B)_{\rho}\,.

(326)

In the first equality, we defined the functions
u,v,w
induced by the duality relations (see equation (
188
)). In the second inequality, we used the fact that
u(\alpha)\rightarrow 1
as
\alpha\rightarrow 1
, which follows from the duality relations (
188
).
∎

We observe that the condition requiring the function
f
to be continuous excludes the case
f(1)=-\infty
. Indeed, in this scenario, the newly defined entropy does not converge to the von Neumann conditional entropy (see the next section).
Similarly, the assumption that the path lies within
\mathcal{D}
excludes the case where
z\to 0
as
\alpha\to 1
. For instance, it is well-known that in such cases, the
\alpha
-
z
Rényi relative entropy does not converge to the Umegaki relative entropy (see
[
4
, Section 3]
).

The second limits we consider are
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=1
, which recover the quantities
H^{\downarrow}_{\alpha,z}
and
H^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,z}
, respectively.
The limit as
\lambda\to 0
is not immediately clear, as the exponent of the states
\sigma_{B}
in the definition of the new conditional entropy introduces an optimization over all projectors. Furthermore, the behavior of the limit depends on the direction of approach: for
\lambda\to 0^{-}
(from below) and
\lambda\to 0^{+}
(from above), the optimization involves either an infimum and a supremum or vice versa. As a result, it is not evident a priori which optimization should be adopted.

In the following, we demonstrate that the limits
\lambda\to 0^{+}
and
\lambda\to 0^{-}
converge to the same value. This value coincides with the expression obtained by taking the supremum at
\lambda=0
and minimizing over all projectors
\sigma_{B}^{0}
, where the optimal choice for
\sigma_{B}^{0}
is clearly the identity operator. This observation justifies the choice of the supremum, rather than the infimum, in Definition
2.2
for the new conditional entropy when
\lambda=0
.

Proposition 12.2
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
and
\rho_{AB}
be a quantum state. Then, we have

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=H^{\downarrow}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 1}H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=H^{\uparrow}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}.

(327)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one used to prove Proposition
12.1
and it is a consequence of the minimax theorem
[
40
, Corollary A.2]
together with the variational form in Lemma
10.4
and Lemma
10.5
.

Let us consider first the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
. The limits
\lambda\to 0^{-}
and
\lambda\to 0^{+}
can be expressed as an infimum or supremum. Indeed, according to Lemma
10.4

H^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho}=\opt_{\tau_{AB}}F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})\,,

(328)

where we defined

F(\lambda,\tau_{AB}):=\frac{1}{\hat{z}(1-\alpha)}\log{\textup{Tr}\left( X_B(\tau_{AB}) \rho_B^{ \frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{z}} X_B(\tau_{AB}) \right)^{\hat{z}}}\,.

(329)

Here,
X_{B}(\tau_{AB}):=\left(\Tr_{A}\left(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\tau_{AB}^{1-\frac{1}{z}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\hat{z}=z/(z+\lambda(\alpha-1))
. From the proof of Proposition
10.3
, the function
F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})
is monotone in
\lambda
. Moreover, using the same argument used to prove Lemma
A.2
, it follows that
\tau_{AB}\rightarrow F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})
is lower/upper semicontinuous. Indeed, in the DPI region, it holds that
1-1/z\in[-1,1]
and in this range the power is either operator monotone or antimonotone.
Consequently, by combining these properties, the limit can be exchanged with the optimization over all states using the minimax theorem in
[
40
, Corollary A.2]
. To illustrate the result, let us examine a specific case. Let us consider the case
\alpha<1
,
z>1
and the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
. We then have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup\limits_{\tau_{AB}}F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\lambda>0}\sup_{\tau_{AB}}F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})

(330)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau_{AB}}\inf_{\lambda>0}F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})

(331)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\tau_{AB}}\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}}F(\lambda,\tau_{AB})

(332)

\displaystyle=H_{\alpha,z}^{\downarrow}(A|B)_{\rho}\,.

(333)

where the last equality can be verified using the variational form of the norm as done in Lemma
10.4
. The limit
\lambda\to 0^{-}
is more straightforward, as it involves two supremums and does not require the application of the minimax theorem.

The limit
\lambda\rightarrow 1
is analogous to the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
where the last step follows by noticing that
X_{B}(\tau_{AB})\ll\rho_{B}^{0}
.
∎

Finally, we compute some limits for the classical conditional entropy derived in (
311
).
The first limits we examine are
\beta\to+\infty
and
\beta\to 0
, while keeping
\alpha
constant. In these limits, the weighted
\beta
quasi-norm yields

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\rightarrow+\infty}H_{\alpha,\beta}(X|Y)_{\rho}=\min_{y}H_{\alpha}(X|Y=y)_{\rho}\,,\qquad\alpha>1

(334)

\displaystyle\lim_{\beta\rightarrow 0}H_{\alpha,\beta}(X|Y)_{\rho}=\sum_{y}\rho(y)H_{\alpha}(X|Y=y)_{\rho}\,,\qquad 0<\alpha<1\,,

(335)

where
H_{\alpha}(X|Y=y)_{\rho}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\log{\big(\sum_x\rho(x|y)^\alpha\big)}
. The second limit we consider is
\beta\to\infty
and
\alpha\to 1
within the region
\alpha>1
. To analyze this, we introduce the parameterization
\alpha=1+xa
and
\beta=1/x
, where
a>0
, and take the limit
x\to 0
. Explicitly,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}H_{1+xa,\frac{1}{x}}(X|Y)_{\rho}=-\frac{1}{a}\log{\bigg(\sum\nolimits_{y} \rho(y) \text{exp}{\Big(-a H(X|Y=y)_{\rho}\Big)}\bigg)}\,,

(336)

where
H(X|Y=y)_{\rho}=-\sum_{x}\rho(x|y)\log{\rho(x|y)}
. The limit of the sum over
x
can be computed, for instance, by taking the logarithm and applying L’Hôpital’s rule.

13
Conclusion

We have introduced a new family of quantum conditional Rényi entropies that encompasses various previously studied examples. Our quantity satisfies the DPI for the range of parameters defined by the three-dimensional region
\mathcal{D}
. While we conjecture that the DPI holds exclusively within this region and is violated outside of it, further proof is needed to confirm this. It is worth mentioning that this family does not appear to include quantum conditional entropies derived from maximal quantum Rényi divergences, such as those based on the Belavkin–Staszewski relative entropy
[
7
]
. Although these entropies have yet to find clear operational significance, this highlights that a complete characterization of all Rényi-type conditional entropies satisfying DPI remains elusive.

Finally, we believe that it is worthwhile to investigate limiting behaviors of our quantity, especially as
\alpha\to 1
while other parameters diverge. This is exemplified by the dual of the log-Euclidean conditional entropy (
z=\infty
and
0<\alpha<1
). For a formal definition of the log-Euclidean divergence
D_{\alpha,+\infty}=D_{\alpha}^{\flat}
, see e.g.
[
39
, Example III.34]
. From the relation between the parameters in the duality relations
6.3
, we observe that as
z\rightarrow\infty
, the parameters of the dual behave such that
\hat{\alpha},\hat{z}\rightarrow 1
and
\hat{\lambda}\rightarrow-\infty
. For instance, in the arrow down case, we observe that the dual of the log-Euclidean conditional entropy is given by

D^{\flat}_{\alpha}\left(\rho_{B}\middle\|\exp(\rho_B^{-\frac{1}{2}} \Tr_A(\rho_{AB}\log{\rho_{AB}})\rho_B^{-\frac{1}{2}})\right).

(337)

Interestingly, the latter quantity behaves like the conditional von Neumann entropy for states of the form
\rho_{A}\otimes\outerproduct{\sigma}{\sigma}_{B}
while it yields the Rényi entropy of the marginal
\rho_{A}
for pure states
\ket{\rho}_{AB}
. Nonetheless, we leave this and other cases for future investigation.

Appendix A
Lower and upper semicontinuity and its consequences

In this appendix, we establish that the conditional entropy

H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is continuous as a function of the state
\rho
.
This continuity property allows us to restrict several arguments in the main text
to the case of full-rank states, thereby avoiding technical complications related
to supports and operator powers that may otherwise be ill-defined.

We first start by proving that in the DPI region
\mathcal{D}
, the function
\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}
defined in Definition
2.1
is lower or upper semicontinuous in
\sigma_{B}
, depending on the sign of
\lambda
. This argument is standard in the literature
[
40
,
32
]
(see also
[
50
, Appendix A]
).
We start by proving the monotonicity of
\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}
in the third argument.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
,
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(A)
, and
\tau,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(A)
such that
\rho\ll\tau
. If
\sigma\leq\sigma^{\prime}
, then for
\lambda\geq 0
we have the inequality

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho,\tau,\sigma)\geq\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho,\tau,\sigma^{\prime})\,.

(338)

Moreover, if
\lambda<0
, the inequality is reversed.

Proof.

We split the proof for the region
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
with
\lambda>0
in the range
\alpha<1
and
\alpha>1
.

Let
\alpha<1
. We then have

\displaystyle\sigma\leq\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle\implies\sigma^{\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\leq{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}

(339)

\displaystyle\implies\Trm\left(\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\tau^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\sigma^{\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\tau^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^{z}\leq\Trm\left(\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\tau^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}{\sigma^{\prime}}^{\lambda\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\tau^{\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\frac{1-\alpha}{z}}\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^{z}\,.

(340)

In the first implication, we used that
\lambda(1-\alpha)/z\in(0,1]
and that the power is operator monotone in the range
(0,1]
. In the last implication, we used that the trace functional
M\rightarrow\Tr(f(M))
inherits the monotonicity from
f
(see e.g.
[
12
]
). Therefore, in this range, we get
\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho,\tau,\sigma)\geq\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho,\tau,\sigma^{\prime})
.

The proof for
\alpha>1
follows in the same way by noticing that
(1-\alpha)/z\in[-1,0)
and that the power in this range is operator antimonotone. Finally, the proof for
\lambda<0
follows similarly, with the final inequality reversed.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
, and let
\rho_{AB}
,
\sigma_{B}
be states. The function

(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{B})\rightarrow\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})

is lower semicontinuous for
\lambda\geq 0
, and upper semicontinuous for
\lambda<0
.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case where
\lambda\geq 0
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we have the operator inequality
I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}\leq I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}+\varepsilon I_{AB}
.
From the previous lemma we have
\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})\geq\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}+\varepsilon I_{AB})
and hence we can write

\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})=\sup\limits_{\varepsilon>0}\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}+\varepsilon I_{AB})\,.

(341)

For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the functions
(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{B})\rightarrow\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B}+\varepsilon I_{AB})
are continuous since the third argument of
\Upsilon
has full support and
\rho_{AB}\ll I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}
. Since the pointwise supremum of continuous functions is lower semicontinuous, the function
(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{B})\rightarrow\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})
is lower semicontinuous. The case
\lambda<0
is similar with
\sup
replaced by
\inf
and lower semicontinuity substituted with upper semicontinuity.
∎

A direct consequence of the above lemma is that, when
\lambda>0
, we have
\inf_{\sigma_{B}}\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})=\min_{\sigma_{B}}\Upsilon_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB},I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B},I_{A}\otimes\sigma_{B})
, meaning the infimum is always attained. It is important to note that the set of quantum states is compact, for instance, in the trace norm topology. Therefore, the statement follows from the property that a lower semicontinuous function defined on a compact set attains its minimum within that set. Finally, in contrast, when
\lambda<0
, the supremum becomes a maximum.

In the following, we establish the continuity of the conditional entropy
H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
over the state space. We leverage this property. for instance, when establishing additivity in Section
4
and
chain rules in Section
9
.
Our proofs are inspired by the strategies used in
[
32
, Theorem 1]
and
[
31
, Theorem 5]
.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
. Then the function
\mathcal{S}(AB)\ni\rho\mapsto H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is continuous.

Proof.

Since by Corollary
11.3
,
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is bounded away from zero and infinity, it suffices to prove that the function

\rho\mapsto Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}=\opt_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B})

(342)

is continuous, where

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B})=\Trm\left(\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}I_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\sigma_{B}^{\frac{\lambda(1-\alpha)}{z}}\rho_{B}^{\frac{(1-\lambda)(1-\alpha)}{2z}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{\alpha}{2z}}\right)^{z}.

(343)

To prove continuity, we first prove upper semicontinuity and then lower semicontinuity.

First, we assume that
\lambda(1-\alpha)<0
. In this case,
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B})
is convex in
\sigma_{B}
, and by definition

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}=\inf_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B}).

(344)

Let
\rho_{AB}^{(n)}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}(AB)
converging to
\rho_{AB}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}(AB)
. Inspired by the proof of
[
32
, Theorem 1]
, for
t\in(0,1)
and nonnegative integer
n
, we define

Y_{t,n}:=\inf_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}\big),

(345)

where
\pi_{B}
is the maximally mixed state in
\mathcal{S}(B)
. This approach is justified by the fact that
(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}
remains full-rank for any
t\in(0,1)
, which guarantees the continuity of the function with respect to the first argument
\rho_{AB}^{(n)}
. Then, clearly
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}\leq Y_{t,n}
since the states of the form
(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}
form a subset of
\mathcal{S}(B)
. Moreover, by the convexity of
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B})
in
\sigma_{B}
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{t,n}

\displaystyle\leq(1-t)\inf_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|\sigma_{B}\big)+tQ_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|\pi_{B}\big)

(346)

\displaystyle=(1-t)Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}+tQ^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|\pi_{B}\big).

(347)

As a result,

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}=\inf_{t\in(0,1)}Y_{t,n},\qquad\text{for all $n$},

(348)

since
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}-Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)},\pi_{B}\big)\leq 0
. Using the preceding equality, we can write

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{t\in(0,1)}Y_{t,n}

(349)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{t\in(0,1)}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}Y_{t,n},

(350)

where we used the minimax inequality

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{t\in(0,1)}f_{n}(t)\leq\inf_{t\in(0,1)}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}(t),

(351)

for any sequence of functions
f_{n}(t)
. Also note that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}Y_{t,n}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{\sigma_{B}}Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}\big)

(352)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\sigma_{B}}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}\big)

(353)

\displaystyle=\inf_{\sigma_{B}}Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{*}\big|(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}\big),

(354)

where we used the fact that
Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}(\cdot|\sigma)
is continuous when
\sigma
is full-rank, implying that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}\big)=Q^{\lambda}_{\alpha,z}\big(\rho_{AB}^{*}\big|(1-t)\sigma_{B}+t\pi_{B}\big).

(355)

Combining (
348
), (
350
) and (
354
) yields

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}\leq Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{*}}.

(356)

Since the sequence
\rho_{AB}^{(n)}
was arbitrary, this means that the function
\rho\mapsto Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is upper semicontinuous.

We now turn to lower semicontinuity. After possibly extracting a subsequence, we may assume without loss of generality that the sequence
\{Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}\}_{n}
converges (note that by Corollary
11.3
,
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is bounded away from infinity). Moreover, let
\{\omega_{B}^{(n)}\}_{n}
be the sequence in
\mathcal{S}(B)
of optimizers such that

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}=Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n)}\big|\omega_{B}^{(n)}\big).

(357)

Since
\mathcal{S}(B)
is compact, there always exists a converging subsequence
\{\omega_{B}^{(n_{k})}\}_{k}
of the sequence
\{\omega_{B}^{(n)}\}_{n}
converging to a state
\omega_{B}^{*}\in\mathcal{S}(B)
. Now, we observe that

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\big(\rho_{AB}^{*}\big|\omega_{B}^{*}\big)\leq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\big(\rho_{AB}^{(n_{k})}\big|\omega_{B}^{(n_{k})}\big)

(358)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n_{k})}}=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}},

(359)

where the second inequality follows from the lower semicontinuity of the function
(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{B})\mapsto Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B})
proved in Lemma
A.2
and the last equality follows from the fact that we picked a converging sequence
\{Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho^{(n)}}\}_{n}
.

The proof for the case where
\lambda(1-\alpha)>0
, is completely analogous. The crucial point is that in this case the function
Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B})
is concave in
\sigma_{B}
, and

Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}=\sup_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(\rho_{AB}|\sigma_{B}).

(360)

The rest of the proof is similar with obvious modifications.
∎

Appendix C
Stronger convexity/concavity statements

Leveraging the DPI result from Theorem

, and applying a standard argument that connects DPI with convexity, we derive additional results concerning the convexity and concavity properties.

Proposition C.1
.

Let
(\alpha,z,\lambda)\in\mathcal{D}
. Then, the function

\displaystyle\rho\mapsto\exp(\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\lambda(1-\alpha)} H^\lambda_{\alpha,z}(A|B)_{\rho} )

(378)

is concave for
\alpha<1
and convex for
\alpha>1
.
In particular, the function
\rho_{AB}\rightarrow H_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}(A|B)_{\rho}
is concave for
\alpha<1
and quasi-concave for
\alpha>1
.

Proof.

Let us consider the case
\alpha<1
. From the DPI under partial trace on the conditioning system
B
’, we have

\displaystyle\opt_{\sigma_{B}}Q_{\alpha,z}^{\lambda}\Big(\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{AB}^{i}\Big|\sigma_{B}\Big)

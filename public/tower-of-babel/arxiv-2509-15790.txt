Covariance matrices of volume power functionals of random simplicial complexes – an asymptotic analysis
Mandala von Westenholz

Abstract
. This work analyzes and compares the asymptotic properties of the covariance matrices of vectors of volume power functionals of random Vietoris-Rips complexes, as the intensity of the underlying homogeneous Poisson point process grows. Several key results are established which, in particular, generalize well-known facts on random graphs. Findings regarding rank, definiteness, determinant, eigenspaces, and related decompositions are presented within three distinct regimes. Moreover, we derive stochastic applications of these algebraic properties, leading to interesting results for vectors of volume power functionals.

•

•

supercritical (dense) regime
for
t\delta_{t}^{d}\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}\infty

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present a framework for the upcoming investigations.
Here, a considered simplicial complex
\mathcal{R}(\eta_{t},\delta_{t})
with
\delta_{t}\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}{0}
arises through a Poisson point process, which is always given through

\eta_{t}\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbf{N}(W),\,\omega\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{N(\omega)}\delta_{X_{i}(\omega)}.

The set
W\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is convex and compact and we fix its volume as
V(W)=1
. The
X_{i}\colon\Omega\rightarrow W
for
i\in[N(\omega)]
and
N\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
are random variables. Furthermore, we denote by
\mathbf{N}(W)
the set of finite counting measures on
W
.

Now we introduce the main notations, that we use in the upcoming parts.

Notation 2.1.

(i)

Vertices
\{x_{i},\ldots,x_{j}\}
are shortened as
\{x_{l}\}_{l=i}^{j}
and we write
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

\eta_{t}(\omega)
denotes the counting measure
\sum_{i=1}^{N(\omega)}\delta_{X_{i}(\omega)}
as well as the support of the counting measure, which is the set of points
\{X_{1}(\omega),\ldots,\,X_{N(\omega)}(\omega)\}
.

(iii)

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(W)
be a Borel set.
Then
\eta_{t}(A)=\eta_{t}(\omega)(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}(A)
gives the number of random points in
A
. Furthermore,
\eta_{t}\cap A=\eta_{t}(\omega)\cap A=\{x_{1},\ldots,\,x_{n}\}\cap A
gives the explicit points of the support of
\eta
that are lying in
A
.

(iv)

\eta_{t,\,\neq}^{m}(\omega)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,\,x_{m})\mid j_{1},\ldots,\,j_{m}\in[N(\omega)]\text{ and }x_{i}=X_{j_{i}}(\omega)\text{ pairwise distinct}\}

is the set of all
m
-tuples of pairwise distinct points in
\eta_{t}
.

(v)

We abbreviate realizations in the following way:
\eta_{t}=\eta_{t}(\omega)
,
\eta_{t,\,\neq}^{m}(\omega)=\eta_{t,\,\neq}^{m}
,

x_{i}=X_{i}(\omega)
, and
N=N(\omega)
.

(vi)

\Delta_{s}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}]
is the
k
-dimensional volume of the convex hull of points
x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}
, if all edge lengths are at most
s
and
0
otherwise. For
k>d
, we set
\Delta_{s}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}]=1
.

(vii)

We abbreviate

(2)

\displaystyle\mu_{k}^{(\alpha)}:=\int_{(B^{d})^{k}}\Delta_{1}[0,\{x_{l}\}^{k}_{l=1}]^{\alpha}\text{d}x_{1}\ldots\text{d}x_{k}

and

(3)

\displaystyle\mu_{k_{1},k_{2}:m}^{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})}:=\int_{(B^{d})^{k_{1}+k_{2}+1-m}}\Delta_{1}[0,\{x_{l}\}^{k_{1}}_{l=1}]^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{1}[0,\{x_{l}\}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-m+1}_{l=k_{1}-m+2}]^{\alpha_{2}}\text{d}x_{1}\ldots\text{d}x_{k_{1}+k_{2}+1-m}.

In this paper we investigate the
volume power functional
(see
1
), which sums up the
\alpha
-th powers of the
k
-dimensional volume of the
k
-simplices in the Vietoris-Rips complex
\mathcal{R}(\eta_{t},\delta_{t})
. For the special case
k=1
we have
\mathcal{V}_{1}^{(\alpha)}=L_{t}^{(\alpha)}
, which is the length power functional, whose covariance matrix was already found in
[
29
]
and algebraically investigated in
[
28
]
. It sums up the
\alpha
-th powers of the edge lengths in the random geometric graph
\mathcal{G}(\eta_{t},\,\delta_{t})
which is the underlying graph of
\mathcal{R}(\eta_{t},\delta_{t})
.
Furthermore, observe that for the power
\alpha=0
the functional
\mathcal{V}_{k}^{(0)}
coincides with the number of
k
-simplices in
\mathcal{R}(\eta_{t},\,\delta_{t})
. In particular, the vector of volume power functionals
(\mathcal{V}_{k}^{(0)},\ldots,\mathcal{V}_{n}^{(0)})
coincides with the
f
-vector of
\mathcal{R}(\eta_{t},\delta_{t})
.
See
[
1
,
15
]
and
[
28
]
for a stochastic discussion and special cases in the context of the volume power functional.

To prepare the studies in the next sections, we record some important and helpful results. First, we notice, that the expectation of the volume power functional was already determined in
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 2.2.

Let
\alpha>-d+k-1
for
k\leq d
and
\alpha=0
for
k>d
. The expectation of the volume power functional is given by

\mathbb{E}\mathcal{V}_{k}^{(\alpha)}=\frac{\mu_{k}^{(\alpha)}}{(k+1)!}t^{k+1}\delta_{t}^{k(\alpha+d)}(1+O(\delta_{t})).

The algebraic examinations in this manuscript are based on the covariance matrix from
[
1
]
which was found for a modified vector of volume power functionals

(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{1}}^{(\alpha_{1})},\ldots,\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{n}}^{(\alpha_{n})}).

The normalized volume power functional used in the following sections is defined as

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha_{i})}=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha_{i})}}{Q_{i}},

with

\displaystyle Q_{i}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{t}^{\alpha_{i}k_{i}}\max_{1\leq m\leq k_{i}+1}\{(t\delta_{t}^{d})^{k_{i}-\frac{m-1}{2}}\}.

Here, the sequence of pairs
(k_{i},\alpha_{i})
needs to be an
admissible sequence
. This means, that it satisfies the following conditions.

(i)

0\leq k_{1}\leq\ldots\leq k_{n}
,

(ii)

the pairs are distinct,

(iii)

\alpha_{i}>-d+k_{i}-1
and
\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}>-d+\min_{l\in\{i,j\}}k_{l}-1
for all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

(iv)

\alpha_{i}=0
if
k_{i}>d
.

The covariance matrix of a functional vector
(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{1}}^{(\alpha_{1})},\ldots,\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{n}}^{(\alpha_{n})})
of an admissible sequence was explicitly determined by Akinwande and Reitzner
[
1
]
for all regimes. Note however, that the critical regime is divided into two further cases.

Theorem 2.3
[
1
]
.

Let
(k_{1},\alpha_{1}),\ldots,(k_{n},\alpha_{n})
be an admissible sequence. The random vector
(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{1}}^{(\alpha_{1})},\ldots,\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{k_{n}}^{(\alpha_{n})})
has the asymptotic covariance matrix

(4)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{n}:=\begin{cases}A_{0}^{<1}&\text{ for }\,\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\delta_{t}^{d}=0,\\
\sum_{m=0}^{2k_{n}}A_{m}^{<1}c^{m/2}&\text{ for }\,\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\delta_{t}^{d}=c\in(0,\,1],\\
\sum_{m=0}^{k_{n}}A_{m}^{>1}c^{-m}&\text{ for }\,\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\delta_{t}^{d}\in[1,\,\infty),\\
A_{0}^{>1}&\text{ for }\,\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\delta_{t}^{d}=\infty\\
\end{cases}

with

(5)

\displaystyle A_{m}^{>1}:=\left(\mu_{k_{l},k_{j}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{j})}\frac{\mathds{1}(m\leq\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i})}{(m+1)!(k_{l}-m)!(k_{j}-m)!}\right)_{l,j=1,\ldots,n}

and

(6)

\displaystyle A_{m}^{<1}:=\left(\mu_{k_{l},k_{j}:\frac{k_{l}+k_{j}-m+2}{2}}^{(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{j})}\frac{\mathds{1}(m-|k_{l}-k_{j}|\in\{0,2,4,\ldots,2\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i}\})}{(\frac{k_{l}+k_{j}-m+2}{2})!(\frac{m-k_{l}+k_{j}}{2})!(\frac{m+k_{l}-k_{j}}{2})!}\right)_{l,j=1,\ldots,n}.

Applications

\displaystyle H_{k}:

First, we consider the Schur decomposition
\Sigma_{n}=SDS^{-1}

\mathbb{V}(b_{1}\mathcal{V}_{k_{1}}^{(\alpha_{1})}+\dots+b_{n}\mathcal{V}_{k_{n}}^{(\alpha_{n})})=\mathbb{V}((b^{t}S)(S^{-1}(\mathcal{V}_{k_{i}}^{(\alpha_{i})})_{i=1,\dots n}))=(S^{T}b)D(b^{T}S),

Furthermore, we find applications of the inverse matrix, which is called concentration matrix, if

is invertible. The entries of the joint concentration matrix of a volume power vector of length 3 present partial correlations between two functionals in the vector given a third one. See the following theorem for more details.

Theorem 4.4.

Let
(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}})
be a normalized vector of volume power functionals with covariance matrix
\Sigma_{3}=(\sigma_{ij})
according to (
4

\Sigma_{3}
be invertible.
The partial correlation of
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}
is

\rho_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}|\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}}=-\frac{s_{12}}{\sqrt{s_{11}s_{22}}},

where
\Sigma_{3}^{-1}=(s_{ij})
is the inverse matrix of
\Sigma_{3}
.

Proof.

The partial corelation
\rho_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}|\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}}
is the correlation between the residuals
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
resulting from the linear regression of
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}}
with
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}
and of
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}
with
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}
.

Thus, we consider

x_{0}=\text{argmin}_{x}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}}-x^{t}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}\|^{2}\text{ and }y_{0}=\text{argmin}_{y}\mathbb{E}\|\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}-y^{t}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}\|^{2}.

We obtain

x_{0}=\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{31}\text{ and }y_{0}=\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{32}.

This means that one can write the residuals as

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}}-x_{0}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}}-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{31}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}

and

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}-y_{0}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}=\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{32}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}.

The joint covariance of the residuals is then given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}ov(L_{1},L_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}ov(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}}-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{31}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{32}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}ov(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}})-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{23}\mathbb{C}ov(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}},\tilde{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}})-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{13}\mathbb{C}ov(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}})

\displaystyle+\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{32}\sigma_{31}\mathbb{C}ov(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{12}-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{23}\sigma_{13}-\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{13}\sigma_{23}+\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{32}\sigma_{31}\sigma_{33}=\sigma_{12}-\sigma_{13}\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{23}.

Analogue computations yield

\mathbb{C}ov(L_{1},L_{1})=\sigma_{11}-\sigma_{13}\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{13}\,\text{ and }\,\mathbb{C}ov(L_{2},L_{2})=\sigma_{22}-\sigma_{23}\sigma_{33}^{-1}\sigma_{23}.

Then, we use the Schur complement and the inverse formula for block matrices (see
[
10
]
). Here, the following block form is useful to compute the inverse of
S
.

\Sigma_{3}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}s_{11}&s_{12}&s_{13}\\
s_{12}&s_{22}&s_{23}\\
s_{13}&s_{23}&s_{33}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}S&R^{t}\\
R&s_{33}\\
\end{array}\right),\text{ with }S=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}s_{11}&s_{12}\\
s_{12}&s_{22}\\
\end{array}\right)\text{ and }R=(s_{13},s_{23}).

We use the decomposition of
\Sigma_{3}
into

\Sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}\sigma_{11}&\sigma_{12}&\sigma_{13}\\
\sigma_{12}&\sigma_{22}&\sigma_{23}\\
\sigma_{13}&\sigma_{23}&\sigma_{33}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}A&B^{t}\\
B&\sigma_{33}\\
\end{array}\right),\text{ with }A=\left(\begin{array}[]{rrr}\sigma_{11}&\sigma_{12}\\
\sigma_{12}&\sigma_{22}\\
\end{array}\right)\text{ and }B=(\sigma_{13},\sigma_{23}).

This leads to

S^{-1}=A-B^{t}\sigma_{33}^{-1}B=(\sigma_{ij})_{i,j\in\{1,2\}}-(\sigma_{31},\sigma_{32})^{t}\sigma_{33}(\sigma_{13},\sigma_{23}).

We define
S^{-1}:=(s_{ij}^{(-1)})
and see that for

holds

\displaystyle s_{ij}^{(-1)}=\sigma_{ij}-\sigma_{3i}\sigma_{33}\sigma_{3j}=\mathbb{C}ov(L_{i},L_{j}),

which are exactly the covariances of the residuals above.
In the end we use the determinant formula for

-s_{12}&s_{11}\\
\end{array}\right).

A comparison shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}ov(L_{1},L_{1})=\frac{s_{22}}{s_{11}s_{22}-s_{12}^{2}},\,\mathbb{C}ov(L_{2},L_{2})=\frac{s_{11}}{s_{11}s_{22}-s_{12}^{2}},\,\mathbb{C}ov(L_{1},L_{2})=\frac{-s_{21}}{s_{11}s_{22}-s_{12}^{2}}.

To finalize the proof, we observe that

\rho_{\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{1})}_{k_{1}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{2})}_{k_{2}}|\tilde{\mathcal{V}}^{(\alpha_{3})}_{k_{3}}}=\frac{\mathbb{C}ov(L_{1},L_{2})}{\sqrt{\mathbb{C}ov(L_{1},L_{1}),\mathbb{C}ov(L_{2},L_{2})}}=-\frac{s_{12}}{\sqrt{s_{11}s_{22}}}.\qed

Theorem 5.14.

Let

\emph{rank}(\Sigma_{n})=n

Beyond these results, we will present an approach to determine bounds for the eigenvalues of

under Requirement
5.17

Lemma 5.15.

Let
A_{m}^{<1}
and
A_{m}^{>1}
for

be the matrices according to (
5

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{2k_{n}}A_{m}^{<1}=\sum_{m=0}^{k_{n}}A_{m}^{>1}.

Proof.

The entries
(j,l)
of the sum
\sum_{m=0}^{2k_{n}}A_{m}^{<1}
on the left of Equation (
20
) can be transformed by the substitution
h=2\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i}-(m-|k_{l}-k_{j}|)
into

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\{0,\ldots,2k_{n}\}}\mu_{k_{l},k_{j}:h/2+1}^{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})}\frac{\mathds{1}(h\in\{0,2,4,\ldots,2\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i}\})}{(h/2+1)!(k_{l}-h/2)!(k_{j}-h/2)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in\{0,2,\ldots,2k_{n}\}}\mu_{k_{l},k_{j}:h/2+1}^{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})}\frac{\mathds{1}(h\in\{0,2,4,\ldots,2\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i}\})}{(h/2+1)!(k_{l}-h/2)!(k_{j}-h/2)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\{0,2,\ldots,2k_{n}\}}\mu_{k_{l},k_{j}:k/2+1}^{(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})}\frac{\mathds{1}(k\leq 2\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i})}{(k/2+1)!(k_{l}-k/2)!(k_{j}-k/2)!}.

The first equality follows from condition
\mathds{1}(h\in\{0,2,4,\ldots,2\min_{i\in\{l,j\}}k_{i}\})

in the nominator.
The last row can be rewritten into the right side of Equation (

∎

We abbreviate the sums in the preceding Lemma as

(21)

\displaystyle B:=\sum_{m=0}^{2k_{n}}A_{m}^{<1}=\sum_{m=0}^{k_{n}}A_{m}^{>1}.

Next, we go on with studying

These matrices are block matrices where only the blocks in the right lower corner are nonzero. This means, that there appears a huge number of zeroes in these matrices for big

In general

(22)

0&C\end{array}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\kern 5.0pt\text{ with }\kern 5.0ptC=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}\frac{\mu_{k_{m},k_{m}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{m},\alpha_{m})}}{a_{m}^{2}}&\ldots&\frac{\mu_{k_{n},k_{m}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{m},\alpha_{n})}}{a_{m}a_{n}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\mu_{k_{m},k_{n}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{m},\alpha_{n})}}{a_{m}a_{n}}&\ldots&\frac{\mu_{k_{n},k_{n}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n})}}{a_{n}^{2}}\\

where

and

(23)

\displaystyle a_{i,m}=\sqrt{(m+1)!}(k_{i}-m)!\,\text{ for }\,i\in\{m,\ldots,n\}.

For the upcoming considerations we will use the following bounds.

Lemma 5.16.

The
\mu_{k_{i},k_{j}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}
with
i,j\in\{m,\ldots,n\}
from (
3

\displaystyle\mu_{k_{i},k_{j}:m+1}^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\alpha_{i}+d})^{k_{i}-m}(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}+d})^{m}(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\alpha_{j}+d})^{k_{j}-m}

(24)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i,j\in\{m,\ldots,n\}}(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\min\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}\}+d)})^{k_{i}+k_{j}-m}:=S_{m}.

Proof.

First notice that the volumes of all simplices in
\eta_{t}
are bounded above by the product of the length of appearing edges in each simplex, which are themselves bounded by
1
, since all points lie in the
d
-dimensional unit-ball.
Therefore we see with the transformation
x_{l}=r_{l}u_{l}
with
r_{l}=\|x_{l}\|,u_{l}\in S^{d-1}
for
l\in\{1,\ldots,k_{i}+k_{j}+1-m\}
, which has Jacobian
r^{d-1}
, that

\displaystyle\mu_{k_{i},k_{j}:m}^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}

\displaystyle=\int_{(B^{d})^{k_{i}+k_{j}+1-m}}\Delta_{1}[0,\{x_{l}\}^{k_{i}}_{l=1}]^{\alpha_{1}}\Delta_{1}[0,\{x_{l}\}^{k_{i}+k_{j}-m+1}_{l=k_{i}-m+2}]^{\alpha_{2}}\text{d}x_{1}\ldots\text{d}x_{k_{i}+k_{j}+1-m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(S^{d-1})^{k_{i}+k_{j}+m-1}}\int_{0}^{1}...\int_{0}^{1}(r_{1}\ldots r_{k_{i}-m+1})^{\alpha_{1}}(r_{k_{i}-m+2}\ldots r_{k_{i}})^{\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle(r_{k_{i}-m+2}\ldots r_{k_{j}+k_{i}+1-m})^{\alpha_{j}}(r_{1}\ldots r_{k_{i}+k_{j}+1-m})^{d-1}\text{d}r_{1}\ldots\text{d}r_{k_{i}+k_{j}+1-m}\text{d}u_{1}\ldots\text{d}u_{k_{i}+k_{j}+1-m}

\displaystyle\leq(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\alpha_{i}+d})^{k_{i}-m+1}(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}+d})^{m-1}(\frac{d\varkappa_{d}}{\alpha_{j}+d})^{k_{j}-m+1}.

One can bound the

For the final part of the investigation the
spectral norm
is used, which is defined, for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, as

(30)

\displaystyle\|A\|_{2}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}},

where
\lambda_{\max}
is the largest eigenvalue of
A^{t}A
. If
A
is symmetric, then
\|A\|_{2}
equals the absolute value of the largest eigenvalue of
A
, since
A^{t}A=A^{2}
. The spectral norm exhibits a number of important characteristics.

Lemma 5.19.

The following statements hold for the spectralnorm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
:

(i)

\|\cdot\|_{2}
is compatible with the euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, i.e.

\|Ax\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{2}\|x\|_{2}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(ii)

\|\cdot\|_{2}
is submultiplicative.

(iii)

Let
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be symmetric and
B
be positive semidefinite. Then,
A\geq B
implies that
\|A\|_{2}\geq\|B\|_{2}
.

Proof.

(i) One can rewrite the spectral norm of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
as

(31)

\displaystyle\|A\|_{2}=\max_{x\neq 0}\frac{\|Ax\|_{2}}{\|x\|_{2}},

since for an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with respect to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
of
A^{t}A
we have for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that

\displaystyle\|Ax\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle Ax,Ax\rangle=\langle A^{t}Ax,x\rangle=\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,x_{i}\rangle A^{t}Ax_{i},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle x,x_{i}\rangle x_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\langle x,x_{i}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\max}\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

Furthermore, for a normalized eigenvector
\tilde{x}
of
\lambda_{\max}
we see from the last transformation in the first row above, that
\|A\tilde{x}\|^{2}_{2}=\lambda_{\max}
, which shows that

\|A\|^{2}_{2}=\lambda_{\max}=\max_{x\neq 0}\frac{\|Ax\|^{2}_{2}}{\|x\|^{2}_{2}}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

and therefore (
31
) holds. This equation implies for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that

\|A\|_{2}\|x\|_{2}\geq\|Ax\|_{2}.

This shows immediately that the spectral norm is compatible with the euclidean norm.

(ii) We have to show that
\|AB\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{2}\|B\|_{2}
. Using (i) we have

\displaystyle\|ABx\|_{2}\leq\|AB\|_{2}\|x\|_{2}\text{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Furthermore,
\|ABx\|_{2}
can be estimated in the following way:

\displaystyle\|A(Bx)\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{2}\|Bx\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{2}\|B\|_{2}\|x\|_{2}.

Using (
31
) one sees that there exists
\tilde{x}
with
\|\tilde{x}\|_{2}\neq 0
such that

\displaystyle\|AB\|_{2}\|\tilde{x}\|_{2}=\|AB\tilde{x}\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{2}\|B\|_{2}\|\tilde{x}\|_{2}.

Dividing by
\|\tilde{x}\|
yields the desired result.

(iii)
If
A\geq B
than
A-B
is positive semidefinite. Furthermore, we assume that
B
is positive semidefinite.
We show below that these conditions imply, that
|\lambda_{i}^{A}|\leq|\lambda_{i}^{B}|
for all eigenvalues
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We use the following deductions from the Theorem of Courant–Fischer (see
[
28
, Lemma 5.1(i)]
for a proof) for this:

For two symmetric matrices
U,\,V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
W=U+V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with corresponding eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}^{U}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}^{U}
,
\lambda_{1}^{V}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}^{V}
and
\lambda_{1}^{W}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}^{W}
holds, that

(32)

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}^{U}+\lambda_{1}^{V}\leq\lambda_{j}^{W}\leq\lambda_{j}^{U}+\lambda_{n}^{V}\text{ for }j\in[n].

This yields, since
A
is the sum of the positive semidefinite matrices
B
and
A-B
, the following property:

\lambda_{1}^{A-B}+\lambda_{i}^{B}\leq\lambda_{i}^{A}.

This leads to the conclusion, that

0\leq\ \lambda_{1}^{A-B}\leq\lambda_{i}^{A}-\lambda_{i}^{B}.

and therefore
|\lambda_{i}^{A}|\geq|\lambda_{i}^{B}|
for all
i\in[n]
. Accordingly, we have shown that

\|B\|_{2}\leq\|A\|_{2}.

∎

We can now proceed to bound the eigenvalues of the matrices
A_{m}^{>1}
from (
5
) using the spectral norm.

Theorem 5.20.

Let
A_{m}^{>1}
for
m\in\{0,\ldots,k_{n}\}
be the matrices according to (
5
), let
S_{m}
be the bound from (
5.16
) and let Requirement
5.17
be satisfied. The eigenvalues of
A_{m}^{>1}
are bounded above by

S_{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i,m}^{2}},

with
a_{i,m}
from (
23
).

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{i}^{A_{m}^{>1}}
be the eigenvalues of
A_{m}^{>1}
and
E_{m},D_{m}
be the matrices from (
25
). Using the properties (ii) and (iii) from Lemma
5.19
for the spectral norm, as well as Lemma
5.18
in the last step, shows that

\displaystyle|\lambda_{i}^{A_{m}^{>1}}|

\displaystyle\leq\|A_{m}^{>1}\|_{2}\leq\|E_{m}\cdot D_{m}\|_{2}\leq\|E_{m}\|_{2}\|D_{m}\|_{2}=S_{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i,m}^{2}}.

∎

Now we are able to give the following conjecture for
\Sigma_{n}
in the critical regime.

Conjecture 5.21.

Let
\Sigma_{n}
be as in (
4
) in the critical regime such that the matrices

\displaystyle 0\leq\lambda_{1}^{\Sigma_{n}}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}^{\Sigma_{n}}\leq(k_{n}+1)\max_{m=0,\ldots,k_{n}}S_{m}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i,m}^{2}}).

Due to Lemma

c

6.
Outlook

In this paper we studied a vector of volume power functionals regarding important algebraic invariants and aspects in all arising regimes. With our investigations we were, in particular, able to extend the results of the paper
[
28
]
which address the special cases of length power functionals. Moreover, we supplemented algebraic results for volume power functionals in
[
1
]
.

Tables
1
and
2
in Section
3
give an overview of our results and show, that there are still a lot of interesting open questions for future work. For example, there are certain aspects and regimes where one can work on more general solutions (we considered here only special cases) or more explicit statements (e.g., we found only bounds).

Additionally, in Section
4
some interesting stochastic ideas and approaches are discussed in which way the algebraic results yield advantages for volume power vectors. Furthermore, we already started to discuss
h
-vectors in that section. We are planing to consider the
h
-vector in more details in the near future. This vector is, in particular, important in combinatorics (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
) and its relation to volume power functionals was already explained in the introduction.

Notes on ultrafilter extensions of almost bounded structures
Zalán Molnár

Abstract
We extend the results of [ 11 ] and contribute to the problems of [ 12 ] by studying the connections between the two constructions: the one coming from modal logic, called ultrafilter extensions, and ultrapowers. Throughout we restrict ourselves to relational structures with one binary relation. In [ 11 ] , a class called bounded structures with a universal finite bound on the maximal in-and out-degree was studied. It was shown that members of this class can be elementarily embedded to their ultrafilter extensions. Comparing ultrafilter extensions and ultrapowers, it seems that the real challenge is when the degree has no global finite bound, or there are elements with infinite degree. This is a first step towards this direction by slightly relaxing the notion of boundedness, called almost bounded structures. Among others, we show that members of this class are still elementary substructures of their extensions, elementary embeddings can be lifted up to the extensions, moreover for countable case the extensions are isomorphic to certain ultrapowers. We also comment on the modal logics they generate.



keywords:
ultrafilter extension, modal logic, almost bounded structures

1.1
Background and notation

Background.

This paper mainly extends the results of
[
11
]
and contributes to the problems related to
[
12
]
. In general, we investigate the model theoretic features of ultrafilter extensions, the construction which emerged from modal logic and the theory of Boolean algebras with operations (BAO). Many of the most intriguing problems in modal logic somehow revolve around the notion of ultrafilter extension
or its algebraic counterpart, nowadays called canonical extension. Just to name the two most important: Fine’s conjecture
[
6
,
7
]
and canonicity
[
6
,
9
]
. These extensions were implicitely introduced in Jónsson–Tarski
[
10
]
, finalized by R. Goldblatt in
[
8
]
and J. van Benthem in
[
2
,
3
]
. Their importance is also clear from a model theoretic approach. The analogy according to which ”
ultrafilter extensions are to modal logic, what ultrapowers are for first-order logic
” becomes apparent when ”modalizing” the Keisler-Shelah isomorphism theorem: Two (pointed) Kripke models are modally equivalent if and only if they have bisimilar ultrafilter extensions (cf.
[
4
]
, Theorem 2.62). Throughout we leave aside the algebraic counterpart, and concentrate on the ultrafilter extension of structures
\mathcal{A}=\langle A,R\rangle
equipped with a single binary relation
R
. Our interest is to understand how far the analogy can be stretched and investigate classes, where the constructions not only have similar applications, but actually share common model theoretic features.

In
[
11
]
, the class called bounded structures was studied, in which a universal finite bound on the maximal in-and out-degree was postulated. It was shown that members of this class enjoy the following properties:

•

each one is elementary substructure of its ultrafilter extension,

•

elementary equivalence is lifted up to their extensions,

•

their modal logic coincides with their ultrafilter extension and ultrapowers.

In order to have similar properties for other classes, it seems that the real challenge is when the degree has no global finite bound, or there are elements with infinite degree. This paper is a first step towards this direction by slightly relaxing the notion of boundedness.

Definition 1.1
.

A structure

is called

Remark 1.2
.

This seemingly innocent twist already calls for attention. Bounded structures are always generated substructures of their extensions (cf.
[
11
]
, Theorem 3.5) in the sense of
[
4
]
, Definition 3.13. On the other hand, for the almost bounded case a node with infinite degree will be related to many non-principal ultrafilters, resulting intricate connections between the ”new” and ”old” elements. However, we can always have a full control over these connections through a natural decomposition of the relation.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

2.4
On elementary embeddings

Under a different notion of ultrafilter extension,
[
12
]
showed that elementary embeddings cannot be lifted up to ultrafilter extensions in general, and asked for what classes this property is transferable. In our case we have a similar result.

Proposition 2.12
.

Elementary substructures do not transfer to their extensions in general.

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{N}=\langle\mathbb{N},<^{\mathcal{N}}\rangle
with the standard ordering and fix any non-isomorphic elementary extension
\mathcal{A}=\langle A,<^{\mathcal{A}}\rangle
. Thus, there are
w\in A
such that
\mathbb{N}\subseteq\langle<^{\mathcal{A}}\rangle(w)
. Fix
u\in\mathrm{Uf}^{*}(A)
with
\mathbb{N}\in u
.
Then
u<^{\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}}u
and
u<^{\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\pi_{w}
, but
\pi_{w}\not<^{\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\pi_{w}
.
Thus, for

\varphi^{*}:=\exists x\exists y(x<x\wedge x<y\wedge y\not<y)

we obtain
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\not\vDash\varphi^{*}
, while
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\vDash\varphi^{*}
. Hence
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\not\preceq\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

We note that the construction can be adopted to the ultrafilter extensions defined in the sense of

]

\displaystyle\widetilde{R}(u,v)\Leftrightarrow\{x\in A:\{y\in A:R(x,y)\}\in v\}\in u.

(23)

We can significantly simplify the main result (Theorem 1.5) of
[

]
, stating that elementary substructures cannot be lifted up to their extensions, in general. We use the language of a single binary relation instead of the more complicated one from

Proposition 2.14
.

In the language containing a single binary relation symbol,

does not necessarily imply

.

Proof.

As before, fix a non-isomorphic elementary extension
\mathcal{A}
of

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsf{Alt}_{n}&:=\Box p_{0}\vee\dots\vee\Box(\bigwedge_{i=0}^{n-1}p_{i}\to p_{n})\\

Claim 2.17

The formula

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)

So far the cases cover the possibilities for

(\exists v\in R[w]\smallsetminus\{w\})R(w,v)\wedge R(v,w)

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose

and

Case (A):

Let
\mathsf{K}
be any subclass of the following class:
\mathcal{A}

Claim 2.18
.

\mathcal{A}\Vdash\mathsf{Alt}_{n}\vee\varphi
, while
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\not\Vdash\mathsf{Alt}_{n}\vee\varphi

Proof.

Consequently, for each

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{A}^{*}

\Lambda(\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}})=\Lambda((\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}})^{\mathfrak{ue}})=\Lambda(\mathcal{A}^{*})

First, note that
\aleph_{1}

\aleph_{0}
-regular.
The core idea is to count the possible valuations on the possibly different neighborhoods of the elements, in order to establish bisimulation between the structures. We only argue for
\Lambda(\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}})\subseteq\Lambda(\mathcal{A}^{*})
, the rest are similar with the obvious modifications.
Suppose
\mathcal{A}^{*}\not\Vdash\varphi

\varphi

and some element in

under which

\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\not\Vdash\varphi

on
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
and a surjective bisimulation

in the sense of
[
4

between the models
\langle\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}},W\rangle
and
\langle\mathcal{A}^{*},V\rangle

\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\not\Vdash\varphi
. Consider an enumeration of the non-principal ultrafilters
E=\{u_{\beta}:\beta<\lambda\}
of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
and an enumeration of the non-diagonal elements
F=\{[b_{\beta}]_{\mathcal{D}}:\beta<\kappa\}
of

\delta<2^{\aleph_{0}}

(
a
)

: For each

\langle u\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}\neq\langle v\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}
, while
\langle u\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}\cong_{P}\langle v\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}
, and similarly for
X_{\delta}
,

(

)

: For all
\gamma<\delta
and

,

\langle u\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}\not\cong_{P}\langle v\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}
, and similarly for
X_{\delta}
,

(
c
)

iso
: For each
u\in Y_{\delta}
and

we have
\langle u\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}\cong_{P}\langle[b]_{\mathcal{D}}\rangle_{S^{*}}^{\omega}

(
d
)

coherence

(
e
)

,

(

.

For

and

,

. For
\delta

, where

Forth (constructing

Corollary 2.20
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are almost bounded structures. If
\mathcal{A}\equiv\mathcal{B}
, then
\Lambda(\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}})=\Lambda(\mathcal{B}^{\mathfrak{ue}})

2.6
Further structural and combinatorial properties

After all, it is natural to ask how far the similarity can be pushed between the ultrafilter extension and an ultrapower. Here we have an immediate result.

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an almost bounded structure,
\mathcal{A}^{*}
be an ultrapower over some
\kappa
-regular ultrafilter. Then
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
is at least
2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
, while
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is at least
2^{\kappa}
-saturated.

Proof.

Let
X\in[\mathrm{Uf}(A)]^{<2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}}
and fix a type
p\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}}(X)
. Take a suitably saturated ultrapower
\mathcal{A}^{*}
realizing
p
, such that
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Let
[a]_{\mathcal{D}}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
realizing
p
. By Lemma
2.10
we can find an element
u\in\mathrm{Uf}(A)
with
\langle[a]_{\mathcal{D}}\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{*}}\cong_{P}\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}
and by Proposition
2.3
we have that
\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}\vDash p^{\sharp}([a]_{\mathcal{D}})
. Let
\varphi^{\sharp}\in p^{\sharp}
with quantifier depth
n
and consider
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in X
occuring in
\varphi^{\sharp}
. Using Lemma
2.10
, it is easy to show that player II can always win the
n
-round Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game between
\langle\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}},u,c_{0},\dots,c_{k}\rangle
and
\langle\mathcal{A}^{\sharp},[a]_{\mathcal{D}},c_{0},\dots,c_{k}\rangle
. As a consquence
\mathcal{A}^{\flat}\vDash p^{\sharp}(u)
, hence by Lemma
2.3

\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\vDash p(u)
, as desired. Similar argument applies for the ultrapower.
∎

From the uniqueness of saturated structures (cf.
[
5
]
, Theorem 5.1.13) it follows that:

Corollary 2.22
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is countable, almost bounded, then
\mathcal{A}^{*}\cong\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
, for any regular ultrapower with cardinality
|A^{*}|=|\mathrm{Uf}(A)|
.

Nevertheless, isomorphism cannot be achieved for larger cardinalities, in general.

Example 2.23
.

Consider the structure

\mathcal{A}=\langle A\uplus B,R\rangle
, where
|A|=\aleph_{0}<|B|
, and
R=id_{A}
. Consider any regular ultrapower
\mathcal{A}^{*}
such that
|\mathrm{Uf}(A\uplus B)|=|(A\uplus B)^{*}|
. Lemma
2.10
(iii) implies that
\mathcal{A}^{*}
has
2^{2^{|B|}}
reflexive points, whereas
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
has ”only”
2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
reflexive points, by Observation
2.1
(ii).

From this, it follows that whenever
|A|>\aleph_{0}
,
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
is not necessarily
(2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}})^{+}
-saturated. This leads us to the following analysis. By
\#\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}
let us abbreviate the cardinality of the set

\{v\in\mathrm{Uf}(A):\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\cong_{P}\langle v\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\}.

If
|V_{n}|=\lambda_{n}
, where
V_{n}=\{w\in A:\langle u\rangle^{n}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\cong_{P}\langle w\rangle^{n}_{S}\}
, then
\#\langle u\rangle^{n}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}=2^{2^{\lambda_{n}}}
by Theorem
2.10
, thus
\#\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\leq 2^{2^{\lambda_{n}}}
. Since
n<m
implies
\lambda_{m}\leq\lambda_{n}
, there is an
m\in\omega
such that
\lambda_{m}=\min\{\lambda_{n}:n\in\omega\}
. Hence, there is an infinite descending chain
V_{k+1}\subseteq V_{k}
,
k\geq m
such that
|V_{k}|=\lambda_{m}
. We end this section by a combinatorial observation on the possible isomorphic neighbourhoods.

Proposition 2.24
.

For
u\in\mathrm{Uf}(A)
, if
\lambda_{n}=|V_{n}|\geq\aleph_{0}
, for every
n\in\omega
, then
\#\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}=2^{2^{\lambda_{m}}}
, where
\lambda_{m}=\min\{\lambda_{n}:n\in\omega\}
.

Proof.

Fix
u\in\mathrm{Uf}(A)
. By the remarks above
\#\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}\leq 2^{2^{\lambda_{m}}}
, we show
2^{2^{\lambda_{m}}}\leq\#\langle u\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}
. For each
m\leq k\neq\ell\in\omega
we define the sets
S_{k},Z_{k}\subseteq V_{k}
with the following properties:

(i)

|Z_{k}|=|S_{k}|=\lambda_{m}
,

(ii)

Z_{k}\cap Z_{\ell}=\emptyset
.

Consider a two element partition
V_{m}=V_{m,0}\uplus V_{m,1}
, where
|V_{m,i}|=\lambda_{m}
. At least one block, say
V_{m,0}
, satisfies the property: For all
\ell>m
we have that
|V_{\ell}\cap V_{m,0}|=\lambda_{m}
. Let
S_{m}=V_{m,0}
and
Z_{m}=V_{m,1}
. The recursive steps are similar: If
S_{k}
has been defined, let
S_{k}=V_{k,0}\uplus V_{k,1}
be a partition with
|V_{k,i}|=\lambda_{m}
and say
V_{k,1}
be the block with
|V_{\ell}\cap V_{k,1}|=\lambda_{m}
, for all
\ell\geq k
. Fix
S_{k+1}=V_{k,1}
and
Z_{k+1}=V_{k,0}
.

By Hausdorff’s theorem, there is an independent family of sets
\mathcal{H}_{k}
over each
Z_{k}
with cardinality
2^{\lambda_{m}}
. For each
m\leq i,j\in\omega
we fix a bijection
f_{i}^{j}:\mathcal{H}_{i}\to\mathcal{H}_{j}
. For a fixed
i\geq m
the set
\mathcal{H}=\{\bigcup_{j\in\omega\smallsetminus m}{f_{i}^{j}}[X]:X\in\mathcal{H}_{i}\}
is an independent family of sets of cardinality
2^{\lambda_{m}}
over
\biguplus_{m\leq k\in\omega}Z_{k}
. For
f\in 2^{\mathcal{H}}
we let

\displaystyle\hat{f}(X)=\begin{cases}X,&f(X)=1,\\
\biguplus_{m\leq k\in\omega}Z_{k}\smallsetminus X,&f(X)=0.\end{cases}

(32)

By standard arguments, for all
f\in 2^{\mathcal{H}}
the set

\displaystyle v^{*}_{f}=\bigl\{\bigcap_{X\in\mathcal{G}}\hat{f}(X):\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\text{ is finite}\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{V_{k}:k\geq m\bigr\}

(33)

has the F.I.P. with pairwise different bases, hence generating
2^{2^{\lambda_{m}}}
-many ultrafilters
v^{*}
over
\biguplus_{m\leq k\in\omega}Z_{k}
, thus over

realizes
\mathsf{tp}(u)
resulting
\langle u\rangle_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}^{\omega}\cong_{P}\langle v\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{\mathfrak{ue}}}
.
∎

Intuitively speaking, the difference between the constructions is that for ultrapowers
\#\langle[a]_{\mathcal{D}}\rangle^{\omega}_{S^{*}}
is
as large as possible
, while for ultrafilter extension

is
as small as possible

3
Discussion

We are interested in classes having no finite bound on the degree, while still preserving some of the desirable properties presented in this paper. Though locally finite structures are not elementary equivalent to their ultrafilter extensions in general (cf.
[
11
]
), we wonder if elementary equivalence can be lifted up to their extensions. That is:

(1)

Does
\mathcal{A}\equiv\mathcal{B}
imply
\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}\equiv\mathcal{B}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
, for locally finite structures?

Another promising class would be to bound the length of each path instead of bounding the degree. Fix
n\in\omega
, and let
\widetilde{R}
be the symmetric closure of
R
. The condition which we postulate for
\mathcal{A}
is:

\forall x_{0}\dots\forall x_{n}(\bigwedge_{i=0}^{n-1}\widetilde{R}(x_{i},x_{i+1})\to\bigvee_{i\neq j}x_{i}=x_{j}).

(2)

What properties are transferable to ultrafilter extensions for this class?

Note that, using
R
instead of
\widetilde{R}
, we have example for
\mathcal{A}\not\equiv\mathcal{A}^{\mathfrak{ue}}
.

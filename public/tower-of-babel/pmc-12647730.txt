Clinical significance of level VII lymph node metastasis in papillary thyroid cancer
Authors: Hui Ouyang, Xinying Li, Fada Xia, Sirui Li, Rong Cong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe reclassification of level VII lymph node metastasis (LNM) from N1b to N1a in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) has sparked debate due to limited evidence on its prognostic impact. This study aims to investigate the impact of level VII LNM on cancer-special survival (CSS) in PTC patients. We analyzed data from 40,371 PTC patients in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database between 2004 and 2015. The effect of different nodal groups (N0, N1a, N1b and VII) on CSS was evaluated using hazard ratios (HRs) and absolute survival or death probability differences. Cox and Fine-Gray models were employed to estimate HRs with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Logistic regression analysis was utilized to identify risk factors for level VII LNM. Level VII metastasis was present in 2.0% (817/40,371) of PTC patients. These patients exhibited more aggressive clinicopathological features and received more intensive treatments compared to other nodal groups. Multivariate Cox analysis revealed that level VII LNM was associated with the poorest CSS (adjusted HR for VII vs. N0: 5.67). The adjusted 10-year survival difference between the N0 and VII groups was 2.97%. Independent risk factors for level VII LNM included young age, male gender, white race, large tumor size, extrathyroidal extension and multifocality. Introduction

Thyroid cancer is a common endocrine malignancy, accounting for 3–4% of all malignant tumors. Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is the most prevalent histological subtype, representing 80–90% of thyroid cancer cases1. Although PTC is generally considered an indolent tumor, region lymph node metastasis (LNM) is common and serves as a critical prognostic factor in PTC, influencing surgical management strategies and serving as a significant risk factor for recurrence and death2–4. Although cervical LNM in PTC has been extensively studied, LNM in level VII (superior mediastinal) has received less attention in existing literature. Despite the reported incidence of level VII LNM ranging from 2.7 to 28.7%5–8, research on the prognostic implications of level VII LNM in PTC remains limited, with existing studies constrained by small sample sizes and institutional-specific populations9,10.

Notably, the reclassification of level VII lymph nodes from N1b to N1a in the eighth edition of the AJCC staging system, while simplifying classification procedures, was implemented with limited evidence regarding its prognostic validity11,12. This knowledge gap is particularly concerning given that anatomical proximity does not necessarily equate to clinical or biological significance13–16. Two small-scale cohorts have suggested associations between level VII metastases and poor cancer-free survival9,10, but comprehensive population-based analysis of cancer-specific survival and rigorous validation of the current classification system remain absent from the literature.

Given the rare evidence regarding the impact of level VII LNM in PTC, our study aims to address this knowledge gap by comprehensively examining its clinical significance. We utilized data from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to investigate the clinicopathological characteristics and cancer special survival (CSS) in PTC patients with level VII LNM. Additionally, we explored the risk factors associated with level VII LNM to provide valuable insights for clinical decision-making and treatment optimization.

Methods

Patients and data collection

We retrieved data from the SEER database (Incidence - SEER Research Plus Data, 17 Registries, Nov 2021 Sub (2000–2019)) using the National Cancer Institute’s SEER*Stat software (version 8.4.3). Patients diagnosed with PTC (ICD-O-3 codes 8050, 8260, 8340-8344, 8350, 8450-8460) between 2004 and 2015 were included, as nodal metastatic information was available for this period (CS lymph nodes (2004-2015)). Inclusion criteria included PTC as the first primary cancer, confirmation of the diagnosis through pathology (biopsy or postoperative report), and well, moderately, or poorly differentiated histological grade. Exclusion criteria consisted of a follow-up time less than 1 month, unknown nodal metastatic information, and unknown demographic, clinicopathological, or follow-up data. The final cohort comprised 40,371 PTC patients (Supplementary Fig. 1). This study was exempt from Ethics Committees of Xiangya Hospital due to the public nature of SEER data.

Variables

We collected the following variables: demographic characteristics (age at diagnosis, sex, race), clinicopathologic characteristics (grade, tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, multifocality, nodal metastasis site, regional nodes examined, regional nodes positive, distant metastasis), therapy regimen (surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy), and follow-up data (survival months, and cause of death). Additionally, the 8th edition of the AJCC TNM staging system was applied and computed. Patients were categorized into four nodal groups: N0 (no nodal metastasis), N1a (level VI nodal metastasis), VII (level VII nodal metastasis), and N1b (level I to V nodal metastasis). The primary outcome was CSS, calculated from the initial diagnosis to either death from thyroid cancer or the last follow-up.

Statistical analysis

To assess differences in clinical characteristics between nodal groups, Cohen’s d values were calculated for each variable. Effect differences between each group and the overall study population were graphically depicted, with Cohen’s d values of 0.20, 0.50 and 0.80 considered small, medium, and large, respectively17.

Survival curves were employed to characterize the survival or death probability among nodal groups. The unadjusted survival curves were generated based on the Kaplan-Meier method or Aalen-Johansen method. While adjusted survival curves utilized a direct adjusted method18,19 based on the multivariate Cox model or Fine-Gray models. The effect of different nodal groups on outcome was evaluated using both relative (hazard ratios) and absolute measures (absolute survival or death probability differences). Univariate and multivariate Cox and Fine-Gray models were conducted to compare CSS between groups and estimate the HRs with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). While time-dependent absolute differences in survival or death probability were calculated based on the survival curves, and visualized with risk-difference curves20.

To identify the independent predictive factors for level VII LNM, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed. Odds risk (OR) and 95% CI were used to determine the impact of predictive factors on level VII metastasis.

Summary statistics were provided as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, and as median values with interquartile ranges or means with standard deviations (SD) for continuous variables, as appropriate. Group differences were assessed using χ2 tests for categorical variables and Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables. All statistical analyses and figures were generated using R version 4.3.1.

Results

Clinicopathological profiles between nodal groups

A total of 40,371 PTC patients were retrieved from the SEER database from 2004 to 2015. Among them, 24,238 (60.0%) were classified as N0 (no nodal metastasis), 10,357 (25.7%) as N1a (level VI nodal metastasis), 4962 (12.3%) as N1b (level I to V nodal metastasis), and 817 (2.0%) as VII (level VII nodal metastasis) (Supplementary Fig. 1). Table 1 showed that VII patients were younger (median age 40 years) compared to N0 (47 years), N1a (42 years), and N1b (43 years) patients. There was a notable male predominance in the VII group (37.9%) compared to N0 (16.1%), N1a (25.6%), and N1b (35.3%) groups, and a greater prevalence of white patients (86.3%) compared to other nodal groups. Additionally, the VII group demonstrated slightly higher proportions of moderately differentiated (5.3%) and poorly differentiated (1.8%) tumors, compared to other nodal groups. The tumors in VII patients were larger (median size, 23 mm) than those in N0 (12 mm), N1a (18 mm), and N1b (20 mm) patients (p<0.001). Furthermore, extrathyroidal extension (56.2% with extension ≥3b) and multifocality (63.5%) were significantly more prevalent in VII patients compared to N0 patients (11.2% and 43%, respectively). The median number of positive lymph nodes was significantly higher in the VII group (median, 9) compared to N1a (2) and N1b (6). Importantly, the level VII groups showed higher rates of distant metastasis (5.3%) and advanced T stages (T3b: 44.2%, T4a:8.8%, T4b: 3.2%), along with more advanced TNM stages (Stage III: 3.2%, Stage IVA: 1.1%, Stage IVB: 2.3%). Moreover, the majority of VII patients received total thyroidectomy (99.3%) and radioisotope therapy (82.7%). As seen in Fig. 1, Cohen’s d analysis also indicated that VII patients presented more aggressive disease characteristics including tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, multifocality, and the number of positive lymph nodes examined, T stage, M stage, and TNM stage, as well as more extensive treatment including total surgery and radiotherapy when compared to other nodal groups.

Impact of level VII lymph nodes metastasis on cancer-specific survival

Kaplan-Meier curves revealed that VII patients had significantly deteriorated CSS compared to N0, N1a, and N1b groups (Fig. 2A), with crude HR (95% CI) for VII, N1b, and N1a compared to N0 as 12.18 (8.75–16.96), 8.51 (6.81–10.65), and 3.14 (2.48–3.97), respectively. This trend persisted after adjusting for confounding variables in a multivariate Cox model (Fig. 2B), Compared to N0, VII patients had the highest risk of cancer-specific mortality (adjusted HR, 5.67; 95% CI 3.98–8.09), followed by N1b (adjusted HR, 4.05; 95% CI 3.14–5.23) and N1a (adjusted HR, 2.62; 95% CI 2.04–3.36). We further examined absolute survival differences between nodal groups. The VII nodal group consistently presented a significantly unadjusted survival difference compared to the N0, N1a, and N1b groups (Fig. 3A–C). This was further confirmed in the adjusted curves based on the multivariable model (Fig. 3D–F). Specifically, in Fig. 4, the adjusted 10-year survival differences, between N0 and VII, N1a and VII, as well as N1b and VII groups were 2.97% (95% CI 2.18–3.76%), 1.83% (95% CI 1.02–2.64%) and 0.94% (95% CI 0.09–1.78%), respectively. These results demonstrated that level VII LNM was an independent prognostic factor associated with poorer CSS, even when compared to PTC patients with N1b disease. Notably, similar results were observed in the competing risk analysis (Supplementary Figs. 2, 3, 4).

Risk factors for level VII lymph nodes metastasis

Multivariate logistic analysis revealed several independent risk factors for level VII lymph node metastasis (Fig. 5). Increasing age was associated with a slightly decreased risk of VII metastasis (adjusted OR, 0.98; 95% CI 0.97–0.98), and black race emerged as a protective factor associated with VII metastasis (adjusted OR, 0.54; 95% CI 0.31–0.87). Male patients exhibited a nearly two-fold higher risk compared to females (adjusted OR, 1.85; 95% CI 1.59–2.14). Furthermore, tumor size emerged as an independent predictor, with each millimeter conferring a 2% higher risk (adjusted OR, 1.02; 95% CI 1.01–1.02). The extent of extrathyroidal extension showed a progressive increase in risk: 3b was associated with a three-fold higher risk (adjusted OR, 3.43; 95% CI 2.94–4.00), while more extensive invasion (T4a and T4b) further elevated the risk (adjusted OR, 4.08; 95% CI 3.08–5.35 and adjusted OR, 4.16; 95% CI 2.65–6.25, respectively). Additionally, multifocality independently predicted a 57% increase in VII metastasis likelihood (adjusted OR, 1.57; 95% CI 1.35–1.82). Notably, poorly differentiated tumors showed a significant association with VII metastasis in the univariate analysis (crude OR, 2.51; 95% CI 1.40–4.17), but lost its independent prognostic significance in the multivariate model (adjusted OR, 1.30; 95% CI 0.72–2.22), suggesting that its effect may be mediated through other variables. In summary, we found that young age, male gender, white race, large tumor size, extrathyroidal extension and multifocality were independent risk factors for VII lymph node metastasis in PTC patients.

Discussion

Lymph node metastasis (LNM) in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) is a critical determinant of disease aggressiveness, treatment strategies, and prognosis. The reclassification of level VII metastasis from N1b to N1a in the current N classification system has sparked debate, primarily due to the paucity of studies on its prognostic impact. Our study, representing the largest database analysis on level VII lymph nodes to date, determined that patients with level VII metastasis exhibited distinctly aggressive clinical behavior and worse CSS than those with N1b metastasis. Moreover, we identified independent risk factors associated with level VII metastasis. These findings may change our understanding of level VII lymph nodes, potentially refine the current N staging system, and guide the clinical management of PTC, ensuring more accurate prognostic stratification and tailored treatment strategies.

Our research extends the prognostic implications of level VII LNM, established in prior small-sample studies for poor disease-free survival9,10, by identifying it as a significant and independent predictor of poorer CSS relative to other nodal levels. We also found that patients with LNM in level VII exhibited distinctly advanced clinical behavior, characterized by more aggressive clinicopathological features and receiving more extensive treatment modalities. Collectively, the poor prognosis observed among LNM in level VII, coupled with its advanced clinical behavior compared to N1a and N1b groups, suggests that level VII lymph nodes may constitute a distinct entity in the course of advanced disease. Therefore, the reclassification of level VII lymph nodes needs further consideration.

The ongoing debate surrounding the extent of lymph node dissection (LND) in PTC remains a significant challenge in clinical practice. The controversy primarily centers on the inclusion of level VII in central LND as part of prophylactic treatment. Some studies suggested that prophylactic level VII nodal dissection could be undertaken safely without increased risk of complications and recommended it as an integral part of prophylactic central neck dissection10,21. Conversely, Aygun et al. argued that prophylactic central neck dissection is not essential in differentiated thyroid carcinoma, emphasizing the need to consider patient and tumor characteristics, as well as the surgeon’s experience when deciding on prophylactic central neck dissection22. In our study, level VII is a relatively uncommon site (2.0%) for PTC metastasis, which is lower than the reported 2.7–28.7%5–8. This discrepancy might be attributed to the lack of routine prophylactic level VII lymph node dissection in SEER institutions. Prior research has indicated that certain clinicopathological features could predict the presence of level VII LNM6,8,23. Our research corroborates these findings, suggesting that young age, male gender, white race, large tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, and multifocality are independent risk factors for level VII LNM in PTC patients. The consistency across studies suggests that level VII LNM primarily occurs in patients with more aggressive disease characteristics.

Based on these observations, we strongly recommend a more nuanced approach to level VII lymph node dissection in patients with PTC. Routine prophylactic dissection may not be necessary for patients exhibiting less aggressive clinicopathological features. This conservative stance aligns with the current trend toward more selective management of PTC, as emphasized in recent literature and guidelines24,25. However, special attention should be paid to patients exhibiting these high-risk factors for level VII metastasis. For these individuals, a more comprehensive preoperative evaluation and potentially more extensive surgical intervention may be warranted. In summary, while the debate on the extent of LND in PTC continues, our findings contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting a more selective approach to prophylactic lymph node dissection in level VII, which helps to balance the potential benefits of thorough lymph node clearance against the risks of overtreatment and associated complications.

Our study makes several advances in understanding level VII LNM in PTC. With 40,371 patients analyzed, this investigation provides substantially greater statistical power than previous studies and enables robust quantification of survival differences. We employed both HRs and absolute risk-difference curves to assess prognostic impact, addressing the limited clinical interpretability of HRs alone. The demonstrated 2.97% absolute ten-year survival difference between N0 and level VII groups provides actionable metrics for clinical decision-making. Most critically, our comprehensive comparison across all nodal groups reveals that level VII metastasis confers worse CSS than N1b disease, with adjusted HRs of 5.67 versus 4.05, respectively. This finding directly challenges the AJCC eighth edition reclassification that groups level VII with N1a disease and suggests the need for staging system revision. Our multivariable analysis further identified independent risk factors for level VII metastasis, enabling risk stratification to guide preoperative evaluation and surgical planning.

Our study has several limitations. While our findings lack external validation using an independent dataset, the SEER database serves as a robust, high-quality, population-based registry encompassing approximately 30% of the US population26, and the rarity of level VII metastases inherently restricts validation opportunities in other datasets. Additionally, several unmeasured confounders intrinsic to SEER may affect our results, including molecular markers (BRAF mutation status), surgical details (extent of lymph node dissection and sternotomy utilization), treatment specifics (radioactive iodine dosing and TSH suppression), and pathological features of lymph nodes (extranodal extension and metastatic node size). Future prospective, multicenter studies integrating comprehensive surgical, pathological, and molecular data would be essential for external validation and further refinement of these findings.

Conclusion

Our large-scale analysis of the SEER database reveals that patients with level VII LNM in PTC represent a distinct entity characterized by aggressive clinical behavior and poor CSS among all regional LNM groups. These findings underscore the need for tailored clinical management strategies for PTC patients with level VII LNM and a potential revision of the current N staging system.

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate shared value problems for shifts and higher-order difference operators of meromorphic and entire functions in several complex variables. Using Nevanlinna theory in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} , we obtain new uniqueness theorems when functions share values counting or ignoring multiplicities, extending several classical one-variable results to higher dimensions. A key contribution of this work appears in Section 2, where we establish fundamental results on Borel exceptional values in several complex variables. These propositions provide the main tools for proving our principal theorems. As applications, we derive conditions ensuring that a transcendental entire function satisfies Δ c k ≡ d ​ f \Delta_{c}^{k}\equiv d\hskip 0.64204ptf and we study meromorphic solutions of certain partial differential-difference equations, obtaining growth estimates and structural descriptions of entire solutions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first systematic study of such shared value problems for higher-order difference operators in several complex variables.

Basic Notation

For any set
S
, we denote by
S^{n}
its
n
-fold Cartesian product.
If
S
is partially ordered and
a\leq b
, we use

S[a,b]=\{x\in S:a\leq x\leq b\},\quad S(a,b]=\{x\in S:a<x\leq b\},

S[a,b)=\{x\in S:a\leq x<b\},\quad S(a,b)=\{x\in S:a<x<b\}.

In particular,

\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\mathbb{Z}[0,\infty),\quad\mathbb{R}_{+}=\mathbb{R}[0,\infty),\quad\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\mathbb{Z}(0,\infty),\quad\mathbb{R}^{+}=\mathbb{R}(0,\infty).

Hermitian Structure and Differential Forms

Let
W
be an
n
-dimensional Hermitian vector space equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form
(\cdot\mid\cdot)
. The induced norm is

\|z\|=\sqrt{(z\mid z)},\quad z\in W.

When
W=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the Hermitian product is taken to be

(a\mid b)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\overline{b_{j}},\quad a,b\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

For any subset
A\subset W
and
r\geq 0
, define (see
[
34
, pp. 6]
)

A[r]=\{z\in A:\|z\|\leq r\},\quad A(r)=\{z\in A:\|z\|<r\},\quad A\langle r\rangle=\{z\in A:\|z\|=r\}.

We introduce the functions

\tau_{0}(z)=\|z\|,\qquad\tau_{W}(z)=\|z\|^{2}.

On
W
, the exterior derivative decomposes as
d=\partial+\bar{\partial}
, and we set

d^{c}=\frac{\iota}{4\pi}(\bar{\partial}-\partial),\qquad dd^{c}=\frac{\iota}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}.

The standard
Kähler
form on
W
is

\upsilon_{W}=dd^{c}\tau_{W}>0.

On
W\setminus\{0\}
, we further define

\omega_{W}=dd^{c}\log\tau_{W}\geq 0,\qquad\sigma_{W}=d^{c}\log\tau_{W}\wedge\omega_{W}^{n-1},

where
n=\dim(W)
(see
[
34
, pp. 6]
).

Projective Space

Definition 1.A
.

[
30
, pp. 7]

Let
z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}
. We say that
z^{\prime}
and
z^{\prime\prime}
are equivalent if there exists

such that

z_{j}^{\prime\prime}=cz_{j}^{\prime},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,n.

The equivalence classes in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\backslash\{0\}
form an

n
-dimensional space called the complex projective space, which we denote by

\mathbb{P}^{n}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{n+1}).

Identify the compactified plane
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
with
\mathbb{P}^{1}=\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
by setting (see
[
35
, pp. 634-635]
)

\mathbb{P}(z,w)=\frac{z}{w},\quad\mathbb{P}(z,1)=z,\quad\mathbb{P}(1,0)=\infty

if
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
.

Definition 1.B
.

[

.

[

Let

Counting and Characteristic Functions

Definition 1.F
.

Take
0<R\leq+\infty
. Let
\nu
be a divisor on
W(R)
with
A=\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\nu
. For
t>0
, the counting function

is defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle n_{\nu}(t)=t^{-2(m-1)}\int_{A[t]}\nu\;\upsilon_{W}^{n-1},

where
n=\dim(W)
.
Obviously

\displaystyle n_{\nu}(0)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}n_{\nu}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}

exists, where the Lelong number

is an integer (see
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
). We know that

\displaystyle\displaystyle n_{\nu}(t)=\int_{A(t)}\nu\;\omega_{W}^{n-1}+n_{\nu}(0).

If
\nu
is non-negative, then
n_{\nu}
increases. For
0<s<r<R
, we define the valence function of
\nu
by

\displaystyle N_{\nu}(r)=N_{\nu}(r,s)=\int_{s}^{r}n_{\nu}(t)\frac{dt}{t}.

For
\nu=\mu_{f}^{a}
, we write
n(t,a;f)
and

, with the usual conventions for
a=\infty
.
Truncated multiplicities are defined by
\mu_{f,k}^{a}=\min\{\mu_{f}^{a},k\}
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define the truncated multiplicity functions on
W
by
\mu_{f,k}^{a}(z)=\min\{\mu_{f}^{a}(z),k\}
.
We write the truncated counting functions

\displaystyle n_{\nu}(t)=\begin{cases}n_{k}(t,a;f),&\text{if $\nu=\mu_{f,k}^{a}$}\\
\overline{n}(t,a;f),&\text{if $\nu=\mu_{f,1}^{a}$}\end{cases}

and the truncated valence functions

\displaystyle N_{\nu}(t)=\begin{cases}N_{k}(t,a;f),&\text{if $\nu=\mu_{f,k}^{a}$}\\
\overline{N}(t,a;f),&\text{if $\nu=\mu_{f,1}^{a}$}.\end{cases}

If

, we write
n_{k}(t,a;f)=n_{k}(t,f)
and
N_{k}(t,a;f)=N_{k}(t,f)

and so on.

A non-negative divisor

is said to be algebraic if and only if
\nu
is the zero divisor of a polynomial. Thus a divisor
\nu:W\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is algebraic if and only if

is bounded, which implies that

(see

Definition 1.G
.

[

Let

\displaystyle T_{f}(r,s):=\int_{s}^{r}\frac{A_{f}(t)}{t}\,dt,

\displaystyle A_{f}(t)=\frac{1}{t^{2n-2}}\int_{W(t)}f^{*}(\ddot{\omega})\wedge\upsilon_{W}^{n-1}=\int_{W(t)}f^{*}(\ddot{\omega})\wedge\omega_{W}^{\,n-1}+A_{f}(0),

where

.
Here the pullback

satisfies

\displaystyle f^{*}(\ddot{\omega})=dd^{c}\log\left(|f_{0}|^{2}+|f_{1}|^{2}\right)

for all

I_{f}:=\{z\in W:f_{0}(z)=f_{1}(z)=0\}
of

Take

and

. Let

be a meromorphic function on

.
For
0<r<R
, define the compensation of

for

\displaystyle m^{a}_{f}(r)=\int\limits_{W\langle r\rangle}\log\frac{1}{||f,a||}\;\sigma_{W},

where

to

Then the First Main Theorem of Nevanlinna theory becomes

\displaystyle T_{f}(r)=T_{f}(r,s)=N_{\mu^{a}_{f}}(r,s)+m^{a}_{f}(r)-m^{a}_{f}(s),

where

.

There is slightly different way to continue the formulation of Nevanlinna theory from here (see
[

, pp.15]
).
Take
0<R\leq+\infty
. Let
f\not\equiv 0
be a meromorphic function on
W(R)
. Let

.
Now with the help of the positive logarithm function, we define the proximity function of

\displaystyle\displaystyle m(r,f)=\int_{W\langle r\rangle}\log^{+}|f|\;\sigma_{W}\geq 0.

The characteristic function of

[

\displaystyle\displaystyle T\left(r,\frac{1}{f}\right)=T(r,f)-\int_{W\langle s\rangle}\log|f|\;\sigma_{W}.

We define

, where

denotes a bounded function when

Finally, if we compare the functions

and

\displaystyle T_{f}(r)=T(r,f)+O(1).

Growth, Exponent of convergence and Deficiency

Consider an increasing, non-negative function
S:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}.

The order of
S
is defined by (see
[
34
, pp. 28]
)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ord}\,S=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}S(r)}{\log r}.

For
0<\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle t_{\mu}(S)=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{S(r)}{r^{\mu}}\quad\text{and}\quad J_{\mu}(S)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{S(t)}{t^{\mu+1}}\,dt.

Clearly if
0<\mu<\operatorname{Ord}S
, then
t_{\mu}(S)=J_{\mu}(S)=\infty
and if
\mu>\operatorname{Ord}S
, then
t_{\mu}(S)<\infty
and
\ J_{\mu}(S)<\infty
.
If
0<\operatorname{Ord}S=\lambda<\infty
, the function
S
is said to be of

•

maximal type  iff
t_{\lambda}(S)=+\infty
,

•

middle type  iff
0<t_{\lambda}(S)<+\infty
,

•

minimal type  iff
t_{\lambda}(S)=0
.

The function
S
is said to be in the

•

convergence class  iff
J_{\lambda}(S)<+\infty
,

•

divergence class  iff
J_{\lambda}(S)=+\infty
.

[

2.
Borel exceptional value and related results in several complex variables

This section constitutes the mathematical core of the paper. It establishes the main structural results on Borel exceptional values in several complex variables, presenting them in the form of clear and precise propositions. These propositions provide the essential groundwork and form the basis for the principal theorems proved in the subsequent sections.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with order
\rho(f)>0
. Let
a\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then
a
is said to be a Borel exceptional value of
f
if
\operatorname{Ord}\mu^{a}_{f}<\rho(f)
. According to Definition 1.H, we have

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}N(r,a;f)}{\log r}<\rho(f).

The following result is known as second main theorem:

Lemma 2.A
.

[
13
, Lemma 1.2]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{q}
be different points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then

\displaystyle(q-2)T(r,f)\leq\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{q}}{\sum}\overline{N}(r,a_{j};f)+O(\log(rT(r,f)))

holds only outside a set of finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
f
be a transcendental meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with finite positive order
\rho(f)
. Then
f
has at most two Borel exceptional values.

Proof.

If possible suppose there are at least three distinct Borel exceptional values
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
, say. Let

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}N(r,a_{j};f)}{\log r}=s_{j}

(2.1)

and
s=\max\{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\}
. Clearly
s<\rho(f)
. Now using Lemma 2.A, we get

\displaystyle T(r,f)\leq\sideset{}{{}_{j=1}^{3}}{\sum}\overline{N}(r,a_{j};f)+O(\log(rT(r,f)))

(2.2)

holds only outside a set of finite measure on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Taking
\log^{+}
on the both sides of (
2.2
) and then using (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\rho(f)=\limsup\limits_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log^{+}T(r,f)}{\log r}\leq s<\rho(f),

which is impossible. Hence
f
has at most two Borel exceptional values.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

Obviously, when

, Picard exceptional value is the Borel exceptional value. Then
1
is the Borel exceptional value of

Lemma 2.B
.

[

3.
Shared Value Problems Associated with Shifts

Throughout this paper, we work in the complex Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
For
i=1,2,\ldots,n
, we denote

\displaystyle\partial_{z_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}},\qquad\partial_{z_{i}}^{l_{i}}=\frac{\partial^{l_{i}}}{\partial z_{i}^{l_{i}}},

and for a multi-index

\displaystyle\partial^{I}=\frac{\partial^{|I|}}{\partial z_{1}^{i_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots\partial z_{n}^{i_{n}}},

where
|I|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}i_{j}
and
l_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

For a subset

the logarithmic measure

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{dens}}\;E=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}\int_{E\cap[1,r]}dt.

It follows that if

Let

\mu^{0}_{a_{1}f_{2}-a_{2}f_{1}}=\mu^{0}_{a_{1}g_{2}-a_{2}g_{1}}\quad(a\not\equiv\infty),\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu^{0}_{f_{1}}=\mu^{0}_{g_{1}}\quad(a=\infty).

Similarly,
f

\mu^{0}_{a_{1}f_{2}-a_{2}f_{1},1}=\mu^{0}_{a_{1}g_{2}-a_{2}g_{1},1}\quad(a\not\equiv\infty),\;\;\text{and}\;\;\mu^{0}_{f_{1},1}=\mu^{0}_{g_{1},1}\quad(a=\infty).

f

and
\infty

Technology-enabled anticipatory guidance and peer support in empowering parents to prevent early childhood caries: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xiaoli Gao, Sharon H. X. Tan, Catherine H. L Hong, Mary F. F Chong, Mun Loke Wong, Yvonne P. M. Ng, Gerald C. H. Koh, Pin Sym Foong, Angsumita Pramanick, Oy Chu Eu, Jennifer E. Gallagher, Zubair Amin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionDental caries is one of the most prevalent chronic diseases of childhood. Early childhood caries (ECC), defined as onset of dental caries before 6 years of age, is preventable and reversible in the early stages with proper interventions. The purpose of the study is to assess the clinical effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of technology-enabled anticipatory guidance and peer support in empowering parents to establish good diets, feeding and oral hygiene practices to prevent ECC.Methods and analysisMother-child dyads will be recruited from pre-natal clinics and post-natal wards of a tertiary care hospital in Singapore. The control group will receive conventional oral health education materials while the intervention group will join four rounds of online parent empowerment sessions focusing on anticipatory guidance and peer-support. Mothers will complete a questionnaire and food frequency checklist when their child turns 12, 24 and 36 months; each child will undergo an oral examination at the age of 24 and 36 months. Chi-square tests and two-sample t-tests, or their non-parametric equivalents will be used to compare proportions of children with caries by decayed, missing and filled teeth (dmft) and decayed, missing and filled surfaces (dmfs) scores between groups. Effect sizes and their confidence intervals will be calculated along with number needed to treat. Multivariable analyses will be carried out to assess the effect of the intervention on primary outcomes (caries incidence at 24 and 36 months) and secondary outcomes (including parental efficacy, child’s oral health behaviours and oral hygiene status, quality of life). Cost-effectiveness analyses will also be carried out.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by Domain Specific Review Board (DSRB 2024/00244). Results will be published in international peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberThe trial was retrospectively registered on ClinicialTrials.gov (NCT06558955) around the time of the commencement of patient recruitment, but prior to the commencement of any intervention.

Background

Dental caries is the most prevalent chronic disease of childhood globally. It is five times more common than asthma and four times more common than childhood obesity in the United States [1]. The onset of dental caries may occur soon after the eruption of the first tooth (around 6 months of age). Early childhood caries (ECC) is defined as the presence of one or more decayed (non-cavitated or cavitated lesions), missing (because of caries), or filled tooth surfaces in any primary tooth under the age of 6 years [2]. The prevalence of ECC in Singapore is significantly higher when compared to other high income countries and some lower income countries [3, 4]. Over a quarter (26%) of 3-year-olds and around half (49%) of 5-year-olds in Singapore have at least one decayed tooth [5]. Other recent studies reported even higher caries rates [6–8].

ECC is associated with many undesirable sequelae, including pain, diminished functions (food selection, chewing, and speaking), malnutrition, sleep loss, problems with learning, poor self-esteem, emergency visits, and even lethal systemic infections [9]. Treatment of ECC, once established, often requires sedation or general anaesthesia, is subjected to recurrence, and imposes heavy financial burden on families and the healthcare system. Beyond its immediate consequences, ECC may result in structural defects and malocclusion of permanent teeth and predisposes children to increased risk of dental caries in permanent dentition.

ECC is preventable, and early lesions are reversible, if parents and children are early adopters of proper dietary, feeding and oral hygiene practices [2]. Since the first two years of life is a critical period of development, in which taste preferences, dietary habits and lifestyle behaviours are formed, timely interventions at this stage is recommended before the child’s habits become more entrenched [10]. The dietary factors, such as frequent consumption of sweetened beverage and foods, that predispose children to ECC also contribute to childhood obesity [11]. It is conceivable that tackling cariogenic food practices would have secondary beneficial effects on mitigating childhood obesity.

Despite their importance, recommended childcare practices are not commonly performed by parents [6, 7, 12]. Specific to children’s oral health, the challenges that parents face fall under two areas: lack of accurate and actionable information given at an age-appropriate time [13, 14] and difficulties and barriers for implementing the recommended practices [15, 16]. To address these challenges and empower parents, two interventional approaches appear to be promising: (i) anticipatory guidance [17], which is a proactive developmentally based counselling that focuses on the needs of a child at each stage of life; and (ii) peer support [18] for parents to get inspired by others facing the same situations, explore coping and problem solving strategies, and discover their own path forward.

Although anticipatory guidance is theoretically appealing, evidence on its effectiveness in dental care has only been gleaned from few trials. For example, anticipatory guidance in Australian mothers reduced the incidence of severe ECC by five-fold [19]. A quasi-experimental study from Malaysia showed that anticipatory guidance provided to parents lowered the 3-year caries increment in child patients and their siblings as compared to the control group receiving standard health education [20]. While these studies generated promising findings, anticipatory guidance was typically delivered through face-to-face consultation. Web-based interventions may be an effective alternative to in-person educational programmes for mothers in the early postnatal period, and online modalities may encourage the uptake of programmes [21]. Therefore, more robust evidence is needed to gain a complete understanding of the potential of anticipatory guidance in paediatric dental care, across different modes of delivery, duration and cultural contexts.

The effectiveness of peer support intervention in managing several chronic conditions is supported by randomised controlled trials (RCTs). In the field of maternal and infant health, peer support offered to women was effective in increasing the duration and exclusivity of breastfeeding [22]. A large multicentre RCT showed that providing first-time mothers with proactive telephone-based peer support increased breastfeeding rate at six months [23]. A systematic review reported that parents of children with complex needs perceived peer support interventions as valuable [24]. Despite the growing evidence on peer support intervention, its potential in oral health is largely untapped.

Methods/design

The protocol was prepared in line with Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines (Appendix) [25].

This RCT aims to evaluate the effectiveness of a two-component intervention (anticipatory guidance and peer support) compared to standard care, in empowering parents to establish proper childcare practices (diet, feeding, and oral hygiene) and thereby prevent ECC.

Null Hypothesis (H0): There will be no significant difference in ECC rate at study endpoint (3 years of age) between control group (standard care) and intervention group (anticipatory guidance and peer support).

Alternate Hypothesis (H1): There will be significant difference in ECC rate at study endpoint (3 years of age) between control group (standard care) and intervention group (anticipatory guidance and peer support).

Other predetermined secondary outcomes include: (a) parental efficacy in oral health; (b) oral health behaviours for the child (diet, oral hygiene practices, utilization of dental care services); and (c) oral hygiene status (plaque score) of the child. Exclusive breastfeeding rates at 6 months, any breastfeeding at 1 year and 2 years, childhood obesity rates (body mass index (BMI) > 85th percentile) at 3 years and child’s oral health related quality of life across the intervention and control groups will also be explored. Setting

Mother-child dyads will be recruited from pre-natal clinics, post-natal wards, and children’s clinics of a tertiary care hospital in Singapore. The hospital caters for about 5,000 deliveries per year mainly from the Western region of Singapore.

Potential participants, identified by the clinicians or nursing staff, will be approached by trained research assistants, to introduce the study and answer any questions, following which informed written consent will be obtained for those wishing to participate.

Eligibility criteria

The inclusion criteria are: (i) mother has an infant of below three months of age or is in third trimester of pregnancy at recruitment; (ii) biological mother of the child; (iii) mother with internet access and a mobile device; and (iv) mother is able to communicate in English. The exclusion criteria are: (i) child with disqualifying medical condition (e.g., tube feeding, severe neuromuscular disability, etc.) that would limit oral dietary intake, at-home oral hygiene practices, or receipt of dental examinations; (ii) child with serious congenital syndrome that is expected to adversely affect the development of primary teeth; or (iii) family plans to emigrate from Singapore within three years. While participants may be recruited when they are pregnant, no interventions will be delivered, and no research data will be collected until their child is born.

Study design

The study is an interventional trial with random allocation and blinded assessment.

Randomization

After informed consent is obtained from participants to participate in the trial, participants will be randomly assigned into two parallel groups. Sealed opaque envelopes will be prepared in a ratio of 1:1 for the intervention and control group, with randomization in blocks of four participants. The research assistants will inform participants of their assigned intervention at the time of recruitment.

Control group

The control group will receive conventional oral health education, delivered via online education materials developed by Singapore Health Promotion Board [26], when the child turns 3, 6, 12 and 18 months of age.

Intervention group

The intervention group will receive conventional oral health education similar to the control group and participate in four repeated and unique rounds of parent empowerment sessions, with one parent empowerment session conducted at each time point, when the child turns 3, 6, 12, and 18 months of age. The schedule, structure and contents of the four sessions are designed to match the child’s developmental milestones and address the childrearing needs and challenges that are anticipated across the subsequent 3–6 months period.

All sessions will be delivered online. Each online session will be led by an oral health therapist or dentist, participated by approximately 6 to 18 mothers, and scheduled at a time convenient to mothers. All sessions will be conducted in English. Each session will run for 45–60 min. The participants in each group will not be fixed, and a mother may meet different mothers each time.

Mothers in the intervention group will also be invited to download a mobile/web App to receive push notifications such as oral hygiene reminders and materials (e.g., video demonstration of toothbrushing techniques for young babies) and to ask questions relevant to oral health. All questions posted by participants will be directed to the study team’s email address and/or phone number. A study code number will be given to each participant to log in to the App.

Outcome measures

The effectiveness of the intervention will be evaluated by psychological outcomes (parental efficacy), behavioural outcomes (dietary, feeding, and oral hygiene practices for the child), clinical outcomes (BMI, breastfeeding rates, plaque score and dental caries in the child), and quality of life outcomes. The primary outcome of interest is early childhood caries. The child will undergo dental checkups (free of charge) at 24 and 36 months of age along with weight and height measurement. The dental check-ups will be carried out by calibrated dentists who will be blinded to the allocation. Duplicate examinations will be carried out on 5% of the children, to assess the inter-examiner reliability. Each child will be examined in a knee-to-knee position, by careful visual inspection. Oral hygiene status will be assessed by the presence of visible plaque on the buccal and lingual surfaces on 6 index teeth recommended by Ramjford and modified for primary dentition [27, 28]. Dental caries will be recorded using the International Caries Detection and Assessment System (ICDAS) [29]. This will be used to tabulate dmfs and dmft for each child. Dental defects will be recorded by the modified Developmental Defects of Enamel Index [30]. No dental radiographs will be captured. If clinically indicated (e.g., presence of severe dental caries) the child will be referred to appropriate dental facility for further management.

To collect data on the outcome measures, an online questionnaire and a food frequency checklist will be completed by the participating mothers at 12, 24 and 36 months of child’s age. Key outcomes of interest include [1] frequency of toothbrushing [2], frequency of intake of snacks, sweets/deserts and/or sugary drinks, and [3] self-reported frequency of dental visits, and [4] parental efficacy in managing the oral health of children using the Self-Efficacy Scale for Maternal Oral Care (SESMO) [31]. Under the SESMO, response options are coded with a score of 1 for an answer with higher self-efficacy and 0 for lower self-efficacy. The minimum score is 0 and maximum score is 16 for the 16 questions, with higher scores indicating better outcomes. Mothers’ breastfeeding history will also be recorded, along with the direct costs from dental treatment and indirect costs due to time taken off work by parents to attend dental visits, and loss of productivity.

Recruitment status

The study is currently recruiting, though no interventions have been administered to any participant. The date of first enrolment was 22nd June 2024.

Sample size and power calculations

The sample size needed for this trial was calculated by using G*Power version 3.1.9.2 (Franz Faul, University of Kiel, Germany), based on the primary outcome (ECC in 3-year-old children). By assuming that the prevalence of ECC for 3-year-olds is 25.5% in the control group [5] and a 40% reduction in the intervention group (i.e., to 15%) would be of clinical significance, a sample size of 196 mother-child dyads in each group is required to achieve 80% power for a 2-sided test at the 5% statistical significance level. Allowing for a 35% dropout rate, 300 mother-child dyads will need to be recruited into each group (i.e., 600 for two groups).

Data management and monitoring

Hardcopy data will be stored in designated locked cabinets that are accessible to authorized study personnel only. Electronic data will be stored on a secured computer that is password protected. The databases will not contain subject identifiers and the data linking subject identifiers and the subject identification codes will be stored separately.

The study team will periodically cross-check participant study codes, questionnaires and reports with source documents to ensure data quality, at least once a year. A Data Monitoring Committee is not required, as this study does not involve invasive treatments or interventions. No interim analyses will be carried out.

Analysis plan

Data will be analysed on an intention-to-treat basis, following the last observation carried forward method using SPSS, STATA and R software. Descriptive statistics on socio-demographic backgrounds, parental efficacy, childcare practices, child’s oral health status, and oral health related quality of life will be reported. Chi-square test and two-sample t-test, or their non-parametric equivalents, will be used as appropriate to compare proportions and means between the control and intervention groups. Effect sizes and their confidence intervals will be calculated. Number needed to be treated (NNT), calculated as the inverse of the risk difference, will be presented to provide an estimate of the number of mothers needed to be intervened in order to avoid one child with dental caries. Multivariable analysis will be performed to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention, after adjusting for possible confounders (e.g., gender of the child, race, number of siblings at the time of delivery, mother’s educational attainment, income and household type). Depending on the type and distribution of the outcome variables, appropriate methods of multiple regressions (i.e., logistic regression, linear regression, ordinal logistic regressions, or negative binomial regression) will be adopted. The level of significance for all analyses will be set at 0.05. Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines will be used to report RCT findings [32].

A trial-based cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA) will be included [33, 34]. The CEA will be measured from the healthcare system perspective. A detailed log will be maintained on the staff time spent on the intervention activities and will be valued based on staff remuneration rates. Other costs on the interventions include costs of delivering infant oral health education materials, service charge for the online meetings and mobile/web App, and administrative costs. Direct medical costs include the costs of restorations of decayed teeth and extractions at polyclinics, and hospital and specialist outpatient visits. Incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICER) will be generated by calculating the incremental costs per unit of outcome (per carious lesion prevented). Sensitivity analysis will be undertaken to include assessment when the intervention is assumed to have been delivered by a dentist or other dental auxiliary staff rather than oral health therapist. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis will assess the robustness of results with respect to parameter assumptions made in the model. All methods will follow the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards (CHEERS) guidelines for performing and reporting CEA [35].

Discussion

Potential limitations

The main challenge is expected to be retaining the participants in this 3-year trial. To mitigate, online sessions and the dental examinations will be scheduled to a time that is convenient for participants. The questionnaire will be delivered online. The estimated time to complete the questionnaire at each time point will be kept below 20 min. Reimbursement will be provided to compensate for participants’ time, and these will be disbursed when the participants complete the questionnaire and when the participant brings the child for dental check-up. Participants will be engaged by the study team on a regular basis, through the scheduled contacts and via the mobile/web App, or phone. It is expected that some participants may not attend the online sessions at the exact time (i.e., 3, 6, 12 and 18 months of child’s age), and a reasonable range (2–4, 5–7, 10–14 and 16–20 months of child’s age) is considered acceptable.

Ethics and Dissemination

The study underwent full review by the National Health Group’s Domain Specific Review Board (DSRB 2024/00244). Informed consent will be obtained from all mothers participating in the study, and assent or consent will not be obtained from the children. Mothers will be given the option to discuss their participation with their family before deciding.

Any important protocol modifications will be communicated to participants through direct contact and/or a re-consent process.

Patient and public involvement

The child of each participating mother in the study will undergo dental checkups at 24 and 36 months of age. Results of the intraoral examination will be communicated to the participant by the dentist performing the intraoral examination. Incidental findings (e.g., tumour and malignant changes) are rare in the target age group and, if detected, will be informed in writing to the study participant. In such scenario, parents will be advised to follow up with a specialist.

Results of the trial will be shared with the public via peer-reviewed journal publications and professional conferences. A formal end-of-study report will be submitted to the funding agency. In addition, study findings will be shared with appropriate government agencies involved in child health.

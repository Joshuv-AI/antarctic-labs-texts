Remark 1

Remark 2

If

\left|\sum_{n<N}f(n)\right|=O(N/\sqrt{M}).

In this case, provided

Note that Theorem

It is reasonable to ask whether some of the hypotheses in Theorem

\pmod{N}

In the sequel, write

In this section, we extend estimates for

wherein dilation is by

\max_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a\pmod{N}}|S_{f}(ab/N)-w_{b}S_{f}(a/N)|^{2}

2.1.
Unconditional results

In this subsection we will prove the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
c>1/4
and let
N
be a prime chosen sufficiently large relative to
c
. Then there are
K_{1},K_{2}\geq 1
, depending only on

, such that

\min_{a\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}|\{n<N^{K_{1}}:P^{+}(n)\leq N^{c},\,\Omega(n)\leq K_{2},\,n\equiv a\pmod{N}\}|\geq 1.

To this end we will use some ideas that are heavily inspired by a paper of Walker
[
21
]
. In the sequel, given a finite abelian group
G
, a subset
S\subseteq G
and

we write

S^{(k)}:=\{s_{1}\cdots s_{k}:\,s_{j}\in S\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq k\}.

We will be interested in particular in the case
G=(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Given
\eta\in(0,1)
we let
P_{\eta}
denote the set of
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
that are covered by primes
p<\eta q
. Then

P_{\eta}^{(k)}=\{a\pmod{q}:\exists\,p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}<\eta q,\,p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}\equiv a\pmod{q}\}.

We prove the following result, which improves upon
[
21
, Thm. 2(ii)]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\delta>0
, let
q
be a prime chosen sufficiently large relative to
\delta
, and let
\eta=q^{-3/4+\delta}
. Then there is
K=O_{\delta}(1)
such that
P_{\eta}^{(K)}=(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

The proof of this proposition follows a strategy of Walker
[
21
]
, and will require several steps. First, we recall the following consequence of Burgess’ theorem.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1/4)
, and let
q
be a prime that is sufficiently large in terms of
\delta
. Let
\xi
be a non-principal character modulo
q
, and let
X\geq q^{1/4+\delta}
. Then

\left|\sum_{n\leq X}\xi(n)\right|\ll_{\delta}Xq^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}.

Proof.

By Burgess’ theorem (see e.g.
[
11
, Thm. 12.6]
), for each
r\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
we have

\left|\sum_{n\leq X}\xi(n)\right|\ll_{\varepsilon}X^{1-1/r}q^{\tfrac{r+1}{4r^{2}}+\varepsilon}=X(q^{1/4}/X)^{1/r}q^{1/(4r^{2})+\varepsilon}\leq Xq^{\tfrac{1}{r}\left(-\delta+\tfrac{1}{4r}\right)+\varepsilon}.

We select
r=\lceil 1/\delta\rceil
, so that

\frac{1}{r}\left(-\delta+\frac{1}{4r}\right)\leq-\frac{3\delta}{4r}\leq-\frac{3\delta^{2}}{4(1+\delta)}\leq-\frac{3\delta^{2}}{5},

which implies the claim upon taking
0<\varepsilon\leq 0.1\delta^{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\delta>0
, let
q
be a prime chosen sufficiently large in terms of
\delta
, and let
\eta\geq q^{-3/4+\delta}
. Then there is a
k_{0}=k_{0}(\delta)
such that if
k\geq k_{0}
then
P_{\eta}^{(k)}\gg_{\delta,k}q
.

Proof.

Let
X:=\eta q\geq q^{1/4+\delta}
. Observe that by Chebyshev’s bounds,

\sum_{a\in P_{\eta}^{(k)}}|\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}<X:\,p_{1}\cdots p_{k}\equiv a\pmod{q}\}|=\pi(X-1)^{k}\gg_{k}\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)^{k}.

Combining this with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

(10)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)^{2k}\ll_{k}|P_{\eta}^{(k)}||\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{2k}<X:p_{1}\cdots p_{k}\equiv p_{k+1}\cdots p_{2k}\pmod{q}\}|=:|P_{\eta}^{(k)}|M_{k}.

Set
\alpha:=\delta^{2}/8
and define
z:=q^{\alpha^{2}}
,
D:=q^{\alpha}
. Let
(\lambda_{d})_{d}
be an upper bound beta sieve (see
[
11
, Sec. 6.2]
) supported on squarefree integers

with
p|d\Rightarrow p\leq z
, and let
w:=1\ast\lambda
. By orthogonality of characters,

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{2k}<X}w(n_{1})\cdots w(n_{2k})1_{n_{1}\cdots n_{k}\equiv n_{k+1}\cdots n_{2k}\pmod{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\sum_{n<X}w(n)\right)^{2k}+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\xi\neq\xi_{0}}\left|\sum_{n<X}w(n)\xi(n)\right|^{2k},

where we have denoted by
\xi_{0}
the principal character modulo
q
. If
\delta
is small enough then by the fundamental lemma of the sieve
[
11
, Lem. 6.3]
, the principal character contribution is

\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\sum_{n<X}w(n)\right)^{2k}\ll_{k}\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{X}{\log z}\right)^{2k}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{4k}q}\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)^{2k}.

For each non-principal character
\xi
modulo
q
we expand the convolution of
w(n)
and apply Lemma
2.3
, getting,

\sum_{n<X}w(n)\xi(n)=\sum_{d\leq D}\lambda_{d}\xi(d)\sum_{m<X/d}\xi(m)\ll D\max_{d\leq D}\frac{X}{d}q^{-\tfrac{1}{2}(\delta-\alpha)^{2}}\ll Xq^{-\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}+\alpha(1+\delta)}\leq Xq^{-\tfrac{1}{4}\delta^{2}}.

It follows that

M_{k}\ll\frac{1}{\alpha^{4k}q}\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)^{2k}+X^{2k}q^{-\tfrac{1}{4}\delta^{2}k}.

We now select
k_{0}=1+\lceil 4/\delta^{2}\rceil
, so that if

is sufficiently large and

then

X^{2k}q^{-\tfrac{1}{4}\delta^{2}k}\ll\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)^{2k}.

We therefore deduce that
M_{k}\ll_{\delta}\frac{1}{q}\left(\frac{X}{\log X}\right)^{2k}
, and conclude from (

|P_{\eta}^{(k)}|\gg_{\delta,k}q
, as required.
∎

To pass from a set

containing a positive proportion of non-zero residue classes modulo

Lemma 2.5

Let

|S^{(2)}|\geq\frac{3}{2}|S|

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

By

, we know that

is not contained in a coset of a proper subgroup of

, for any

(as otherwise, fixing

we could choose for each

As

we can find

∎

Proof of Proposition

We apply the previous lemma with

, letting

. Then there is a

3.9
, as long as
\alpha^{\prime}\leq\sigma\leq\alpha
and
T\leq q/2
then we get

\int_{-T}^{T}|L(\sigma+it,\chi;y,z)|^{2}dt\ll e^{k/25}\int_{-T}^{T}|L(\alpha+it,\chi;y,z)|^{2}dt.

We now apply (b) to get

\int_{-T}^{T}|L(\alpha+it,\chi;y,z)|^{2}dt\ll\int_{-T}^{T}|L(\alpha+it,\chi_{0};y,z)|^{2}dt=L(\alpha,\chi_{0};y,z)^{2}\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\frac{L(\alpha+it,\chi_{0};y,z)}{L(\alpha,\chi_{0};y,z)}\right|^{2}dt.

Splitting the range of
|t|\leq T
into the ranges
|t|\leq 1/\log y
,
1<|t|\log y\leq\log(u\log u)
and
\log(u\log u)<|t|\log y\leq T\log y
, we get by (c) that

\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\frac{L(\alpha+it,\chi_{0};y,z)}{L(\alpha,\chi_{0};y,z)}\right|^{2}dt\ll\frac{1}{\log y}+Te^{-2cu},

and the claim follows.
∎

The parts of the previous lemma combine to yield the following.

Lemma 3.11
.

Fix
B>0
. Let

and
q

30
) with

sufficiently large, and let
x/y\leq X\leq x

\left|\int_{\sigma-iT}^{\sigma+iT}X^{s}\check{\Phi}(s)L(s,\chi;y,z)ds\right|\ll e^{k/50}X^{\sigma-\alpha}\Theta(x,y,z)\sqrt{u\log u}\left(1+\sqrt{T\log y}e^{-cu}\right).

Proof.

On combining
[
9

(taking
G(s)
to be the Euler product of the function
g(p)=1_{(z,y]}(p)
on primes, and

(a), we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\sigma-iT}^{\sigma+iT}X^{s}\check{\Phi}(s)L(s,\chi;y,z)ds\right|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X^{\sigma}}{\log x}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|L(\sigma+it,\chi;y,z)|+\left(\int_{-T}^{T}\left|\frac{L^{\prime}(\sigma+it,\chi;y,z)}{L(\sigma+it,\chi;y,z)}\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{-T}^{T}|L(\sigma+it,\chi;y,z)|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}\right).

By Lemma
3.9

|L(\sigma+it,\chi;y,z)|\leq e^{k/50}L(\alpha,\chi_{0};y,z)

Combining (c) and (e) of Lemma

, the previous estimate is

\ll e^{k/50}\frac{X^{\sigma}L(\alpha,\chi_{0};y,z)}{\log X}\left(1+u\sqrt{\log u\log y}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log y}}+\sqrt{T}e^{-cu}\right)\right).

As

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

Let

. Recalling the definition of

\displaystyle\Theta(x/d,y,z;q,a;\Phi)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{T}_{y,z}}\Phi(dn/x)1_{(n,q)=1}+O\left(\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{K\leq k\leq(\log q)/2}e^{C_{2}k}\max_{\chi\in\Xi(k)}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathcal{T}_{y,z}}\chi(n)\Phi(dn/x)\right|\right).

\chi\in\Xi(k)
for the moment. Applying Mellin inversion along the line

,

, we have

\text{Re}(s)=\alpha^{\prime}

\check{\Phi}
once again, together with Lemmas
3.7
and

\ll_{C}e^{-k(B-1/50)}(\log x)^{-10}(x/d)^{\alpha}\zeta(\alpha,y,z)\ll e^{-k(B-1/50)}d^{-\alpha}\Theta(x,y,z).

to estimate the integral along

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{T}_{y,z}}\chi(n)\Phi(nd/x)\ll e^{-k(B-1/50)}d^{-\alpha}\Theta(x,y,z)\sqrt{u\log u}\left(1+e^{-cu}\sqrt{T\log y}\right).

Since this holds for every

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\Theta(x,y,z)}{d^{\alpha}\phi(q)}\left(\sqrt{u\log u}+T\sqrt{\log y}e^{-cu/2}\right)\sum_{K\leq k\leq(\log q)/2}e^{C_{2}k-k(B-1/50)}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\Theta(x,y,z)}{d^{\alpha}\phi(q)}e^{-2K}\left(\sqrt{u\log u}+T\sqrt{\log y}e^{-cu/2}\right),

provided that

. Since

,

and

(34)

\displaystyle\Theta(x,y,z;q,a;\Phi)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{T}_{y,z}}\Phi(dn/x)1_{(n,q)=1}+O\left(\frac{\Theta(x,y,z)}{d^{\alpha}\phi(q)(\log x)^{10}}\right).

We now remove the smoothing
\Phi
. Let
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(x)\rightarrow 0
sufficiently slowly. Taking
0\leq\Phi\leq 1_{[0,1]}
to be a smooth deformation of

that is

is prime, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathcal{T}_{y,z}}\Phi(dn/x)1_{(n,q)=1}

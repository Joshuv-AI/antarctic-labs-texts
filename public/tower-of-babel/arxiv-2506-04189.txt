1.
Introduction

The colour bias (or discrepancy) question for spanning structures in edge-colored graphs has attracted growing attention in recent years. This line of work includes results on factors in dense graphs
[
BCG80
]
, trees in dense graphs
[
HLMP34
]
, Steiner triple systems
[
GGS52
]
, and perfect matchings in hypergraphs
[
LMX20
,
HLM+16
]
. In this paper, we specifically focus on colour-biased Hamilton cycles. Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with an
r
-colouring
\chi
on
E(G)
for some
r\geqslant 2
. The graph
G
is said to contain a Hamilton cycle
H
with
t
colour bias, if
H
has
n/r+t
edges of the same colour in
\chi
. We study the following quantity:

h_{r}(G)=\min_{\chi\in X_{r}(G)}\max_{H\in\mathcal{H}(G)}\{\textup{colour\ bias\ of}\ H\ \textup{in}\ \chi\},

where
\mathcal{H}(G)
is the collection of Hamilton cycle in
G
, and
X_{r}(G)
is the collection of all
r
-colourings on
E(G)
. The study of
h_{r}(G)
was initiated by Balogh, Csaba, Jing and Pluhár
[
BCJP20
]
, who proved the following theorem in the case
r=2
. This was then extended to general
r\geqslant 2
by Freschi, Hyde, Lada and Treglown
[
FHLT21
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Freschi, Hyde, Lada and Treglown)
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
,
\alpha>(r+1)/2r
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large. Then for every
G_{\alpha}
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
,

h_{r}(G_{\alpha})=\Omega(n).

Freschi, Hyde, Lada and Treglown actually proved the following more general result:
h_{r}(G_{\alpha})=\Omega(d)
for every
G_{\alpha}
with
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant(r+1)n/2r+d
(see
[
FHLT21
]
, Theorem 1.3). The minimal degree condition in
Theorem

1.1
is tight. Indeed a construction from
[
FHLT21
]
shows that there is an
r
-coloured graph
G_{\alpha}
with
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant(r+1)n/{2r}
, where every Hamilton cycle uses the same number of edges of each colour. In other words,
h_{r}(G_{\alpha})=0
.

In addition to deterministic graphs, we also have a good understanding of how
h_{r}
behaves in random graphs. Gishboliner, Krivelevich, and Michaeli
[
GKM22
]
designed a clever DFS algorithm that finds a long path with almost all edges the same colour in any
r
-colouring of a random graph. This led to the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Gishboliner, Krivelevich and Michaeli)
.

For each
r\geqslant 2
,

h_{r}\big(G(n,m)\big)=\Omega(n)

holds with high probability if
m\geqslant(\log n+\log\log n+\omega(1))n/2
.

In this paper we will study colour-biased Hamilton cycles in
randomly perturbed graphs
. The randomly perturbed graph is a union of a fixed graph and a random graph on the same vertex set, denoted by
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
, which provides a connection between the extremal and random settings. Bohman, Frieze and Martin
[
BFM03
]
first introduced this model, and they proved the following theorem on the threshold for Hamilton cycles in
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Bohman, Frieze and Martin)
.

Let
\alpha>0
. There exists
m=O(n)
such that for every
G_{\alpha}
with
v(G_{\alpha})=n
and
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
, with high probability
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
contains a Hamilton cycle.

In recent years, the study of randomly perturbed graphs has yielded a number of significant results. These include the thresholds of clique factors
[
BTW19
,
HMT21
]
, powers of Hamilton cycles
[
BDF16
]
, and various spanning structures such as bounded degree trees
[
JK20
,
KKS17
,
BMPP20
]
. Furthermore, there has been important progress on the Ramsey properties of such graphs, especially for cliques and cycles
[
DT20
]
. Our first result generalizes
Theorem

1.3
, which shows that adding
O(n)
random edges suffices to ensure a Hamilton cycle in
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
. We strengthen this by proving that the addition of only
O(n)
random edges also guarantees an
\Omega(n)
-colour-biased Hamilton cycle in
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
under any
r
-colouring.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
\alpha>0
. There exists
m=O(n)
such that for every
G_{\alpha}
with
v(G_{\alpha})=n
and
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
, with high probability any
r
-colouring of
E(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m))
contains a Hamilton cycle with
\Omega(n)
colour bias. In other words,

h_{r}\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)\big)=\Omega(n)

with high probability.

Note that by
Theorem

1.1
, if the graph
G_{\alpha}
has minimum degree
\alpha n
with
\alpha>(r+1)/2r
, then no random edges are required: any
r
-edge-colouring of
G_{\alpha}
already ensures the existence of a Hamilton cycle with colour bias
\Omega(n)
. In contrast, for
0<\alpha<(r+1)/2r
, the addition of random edges becomes essential in a strong sense: for any
m=o(n)
, there exists a construction with minimum degree
\alpha n
in which every Hamilton cycle has an equal number of edges of each colour—that is, colour bias zero.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
0<\alpha<(r+1)/2r
be constants, and let
m=o(n)
. For all sufficiently large
n
with
2r\,|\,n
, there exists a graph
G_{\alpha}
with
v(G_{\alpha})=n
and
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
such that

h_{r}\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)\big)=0

holds deterministically.

Interestingly, in the critical case
\alpha=(r+1)/2r
, the value of
h_{r}(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m))
exhibits a delicate dependence on the number
m
of added random edges when
m=o(n)
. Our main result is the following theorem, which establishes that for every
G_{\alpha}
with
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n=(r+1)n/2r
and any
1\ll m\leqslant n
, the randomly perturbed graph
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
typically contains a Hamilton cycle with
\Omega(m)
colour bias in any
r
-colouring. Moreover, there also exists a construction such that every Hamilton cycle has
O(m)
colour bias.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
\alpha=(r+1)/2r
. For any
1\ll m\leqslant n
and every
G_{\alpha}
with
v(G_{\alpha})=n
and
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
, with high probability

h_{r}\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)\big)=\Omega(m).

Moreover, for any
m\geqslant 0
there exists
G_{\alpha}
with
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
such that

h_{r}\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)\big)=O(m)

holds deterministically.

The proof of
Theorem

1.6
is the most interesting and difficult part of the paper. The key step is a structural characterization of graphs
G_{\alpha}
in which every Hamilton cycle has colour bias
o(m)
; see
Proposition

4.2
. Specifically, we show that if an
r
-edge-coloured graph
G_{\alpha}
admits no Hamilton cycle with colour bias
\Omega(m)
, then there exists a subset
W\subseteq V(G_{\alpha})
with
|W|\geqslant\delta(G_{\alpha})
, such that the induced subgraph
G_{\alpha}[W]
is almost monochromatic for some colour
c^{*}
, that is, every matching in
G_{\alpha}[W]
without edges of colour
c^{*}
has at most
o(m)
edges. Suppose the condition fails, i.e., there exists a
c
-free matching of size
\Omega(m)
within the neighborhood of each vertex
v\in V(G_{\alpha})
for every colour
c
. In this case, we construct a Hamilton cycle with colour bias
\Omega(m)
by forming two cycles whose union covers all of
V(G_{\alpha})
and which intersect in exactly two vertices; see
Lemma

4.4
. One of these cycles, denoted
F
, has
\Omega(m)
edges and is nearly monochromatic; see
Lemma

4.5
. The main task is to construct the second cycle
H
so that every vertex of
F
can be absorbed into
H
in two distinct ways, enabling us to control the colouring of the resulting Hamilton cycle and ensure its colour bias is
\Omega(m)
. In
Section

4.3
, we show that this structural characterization implies the presence of a Hamilton cycle with colour bias
\Omega(m)
in
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
with high probability, for any
m=\omega(1)
, regardless of the colouring of the random edges.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we give two constructions yielding upper bounds on
h_{r}(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m))
for different
\alpha
, showing the number of random edges in
Theorems

1.4
and
1.6
are tight. In
Section

3
, we apply an absorption method to prove
Theorem

1.4
. Finally
Theorem

1.6
is proved in
Section

4
: the structural
Proposition

4.2
is established in
Sections

4.1
and
4.2
, and we finish the proof in
Section

4.3
.

2.
Upper bounds: two simple constructions

In this section, we present two straightforward constructions which establish upper bounds for
h_{r}\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(m,n)\big)
for different value of
\alpha
. For convenience we denote
\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
denotes to be the collection of graphs on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\alpha n
:

\Gamma_{\alpha}(n):=\big\{G:v(G)=n,\ \delta(G)\geqslant\alpha n\big\}.

First for
0<\alpha<(1+r)/2r
, adding
m=o(n)
random edges to
G_{\alpha}
may not yield a Hamilton cycle with
\Omega(n)
colour bias in every
r
-colouring. To be precise, we use the following lemma to show
Proposition

1.5
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
0<\alpha<{(1+r)}/{2r}
, and let
m=o(n)
. For all sufficiently large
n
with
2r\,|\,n
, there exists a graph
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
such that the following holds. For every graph
F
with
V(F)=V(G_{\alpha})
and
e(F)=m
, there exists an
r
-edge-colouring of
G_{\alpha}\cup F
such that every Hamilton cycle has an equal number of edges of each colour.

Our construction is based on the following lemma, which is obtained by slightly modifying the Extremal Example 1 in
[
FHLT21
]
. For completeness we present the proof. We write
\alpha(G)
to denote the independence number of
G
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
2r\,|\,n
. If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with

\alpha(G)\geqslant\frac{r-1}{2r}\cdot n,

then there exists an
r
-colouring
\chi
of
E(G)
, such that every Hamilton cycle in
G
contains the same number of edges of each colour in
\chi
.

Proof.

Let
A\subset V(G)
be an independent set with
|A|=(r-1)n/2r
, and set
B=V(G)\setminus A
. Let’s take an equal partition

A=\bigcup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1}A_{i}

such that
|A_{i}|=|A|/(r-1)=n/2r
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
. Assign colour
i
to each edge that is incident to a vertex of
A_{i}
, and colour
r
to all edges in
G[B]
.

If there exists a Hamilton cycle
H\subset G
, then
H
has exactly
n/r
edges of each colour. Indeed
H
has exactly
2|A_{i}|=2\cdot n/2r=n/r
edges incident to
A_{i}
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
, hence it contains
n-(r-1)\cdot n/r=n/r
edges of colour
r
in
G[B]
, as required.
∎

Lemma

2.1
follows easily from
Lemma

2.2
, as shown below.

Proof of
Lemma

2.1
.

Let
G
be the graph with vertex set
V(G)=A\cup B
and edge set

E(G)=\{xy:x\in B,\ y\in V(G)\}.

where
A
and
B
are disjoint sets with
|A|=(1-\alpha)n
and
|B|=\alpha n
. Note that
G\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
, and let
F
be a graph with
V(F)=V(G)
and
e(F)=m
. Then

\alpha(G\cup F)\geqslant\alpha(G)-e(F)=(1-\alpha)n-m\geqslant\frac{r-1}{2r}\cdot n

where the last inequality holds since
\alpha>(r-1)/2r
,
m=o(n)
and
n
is sufficiently large. Therefore by
Lemma

2.2
, there exists an
r
-colouring
\chi
of
E(G_{\alpha}\cup F)
such that every Hamilton cycle in
\chi
has the same number of edges of each colour.
∎

We now use a similar construction to show that in the critical case
\alpha=(1+r)/2r
, there exists a graph
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
and an
r
-edge-colouring of
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
such that every Hamilton cycle has colour bias at most
O(m)
. The following lemma shows that the bound on
m
in
Theorem

4.1
is tight up to a constant factor.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
and
0<\alpha<{(1+r)}/{2r}
, and let
0\leqslant m<n/r
. For all sufficiently large
n
with
2r\,|\,n
, there exists a graph
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
such that the following holds. For every graph
F
with
V(F)=V(G_{\alpha})
and
e(F)=m
, there exists an
r
-edge-colouring of
G_{\alpha}\cup F
such that every Hamilton cycle has at most
2(r-1)m
colour bias.

Proof.

Let
G
be the graph with vertex set
V(G)=A\cup B
and edge set

E(G)=\{xy:x\in B,\ y\in V(G)\}.

where
A
and
B
are disjoint sets with
|A|=(1-\alpha)n
and
|B|=\alpha n
. Note that
G\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
, and let
F
be a graph with
V(F)=V(G)
and
e(F)=m
. Let’s partition
A=\bigcup_{1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1}A_{i}
such that
|A_{i}|=n/2r
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
. Assign colour
i
to each edge that is incident to a vertex of
A_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
, and
r
to the edges of
(G\cup F)[B]
.

Now let
H\subset G\cup F
be a Hamilton cycle. Note that each edge of
F
used in
H
can cause the number of colour
i
edges to decrease by at most 2, hence
H
contains at least

2|A_{i}|-2e(F)\geqslant n/r-2m

edges of colour
i
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
, and thus at most

n-(r-1)\cdot(n/r-2m)\leqslant n/r+2(r-1)m

edges of the colour

. Therefore the colour bias of

is at most
2(r-1)m
as required.
∎

Note that
Lemma

2.3
proves the latter part of
Theorem

1.6
. We will complete the proof of
Theorem

1.6
by proving the remaining part in
Section

4
.

3.
O(n)
random edges makes
\Omega(n)
colour bias

In this section, we will prove that adding
O(n)
random edges to any
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
is sufficient to ensure the existence of a Hamilton cycle with
\Omega(n)
colour bias in any
r
-colouring. To be precise, we will prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every
\alpha>0
and
r\geqslant 2
, there exists
C=C(\alpha,r)>0
, such that the following holds. If
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
and
p\geqslant C/n
, then with high probability every
r
-colouring of
E\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,p)\big)
contains a Hamilton cycle with at least
n/2r
colour-bias.

Our proof employs an absorption method introduced in
[
RRS06
]
and sprinkling. Let
R_{i}\sim G(n,p_{i})
(for
1\leqslant i\leqslant 3
) be three independent random graphs on
V(G_{\alpha})
, then
R_{1}\cup R_{2}\cup R_{3}\subset G(n,p)
where
p=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}
, which will be our final random graph. We begin by constructing a path
A
of length
o(n)
in
G_{\alpha}\cup R_{1}
, such that for any

o(n)
vertices outside
A
, we can use

A
to absorb them - meaning we can extend
A
into a new path that incorporates all these vertices. Then in
R_{2}
, we will use some lemmas from
[
GKM22
]
and
[
KLS15
]
to find two paths
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
of length
\Omega(n)
, where
P_{1}
is almost monochromatic and the union
P_{1}\cup P_{2}
covers all but
o(n)
vertices (see
Lemmas

3.2
and
3.3
). We then use
R_{3}
to connect the absorber path
A
and the paths
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
to form an almost spanning cycle. Finally, we will use
A
to absorb the remaining vertices to extend the cycle to a Hamilton cycle
H
. The colour bias of
H
comes from
P_{1}
, which contains at least
n/r+\Omega(n)
edges of the same colour, and therefore so does
H
. In the remainder of this section, we will construct two long paths in
Section

3.1
, prove an absorber lemma in
Section

3.2
, and finally gather all the pieces to prove
Theorem

3.1
in
Section

3.3
.

3.1.
Two long paths

An important part of our proof is to find two vertex-disjoint paths
P_{1},P_{2}\subset G(n,p)
of length
\Omega(n)
. Specifically we denote

P=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k})

to be a path
P
with
V(P)=\{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{k}\}
and
E(P)=\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3},...,x_{k-1}x_{k}\}
. First, we will use the following lemma from
[
GKM22
]
to find an almost monochromatic path
P_{1}\subset G(n,p)
.

Lemma 3.2

(Gishboliner, Krivelevich and Michaeli
[
GKM22
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geqslant 2
. There exists
K=K(\varepsilon,r),C=C(\varepsilon,r)>0
, such that if
p\geqslant C/n
, the random graph
G(n,p)
is with high probability such that in any
r
-colouring, there exists a path of length at least
\big(2/(r+1)-\varepsilon\big)n
in which all but at
most
K
of the edges are of the same colour.

Next, we will find the second path
P_{2}
that is vertex-disjoint with
P_{1}
, such that the union
P_{1}\cup P_{2}
covers all but
\varepsilon n
vertices.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\beta>\varepsilon>0
, then there exists
C=C(\beta,\varepsilon)>0
such that the following holds. If
p\geqslant C/n
, then with high probability every induced subgraph
F\subset G(n,p)
with
v(F)=\beta n
contains a path
P\subset F
, which has length at least
v(P)\geqslant(\beta-\varepsilon)n
.

We need the following definition which is used in the proof of
Lemma

3.3
.

Definition 3.4
.

A graph
G
is
k
-joined
if there is an edge of
G
between any two disjoint vertex sets of size
k
.

Krivelevich, Lee and Sudakov
[
KLS15
]
developed a powerful method based on the Depth First Search (DFS) algorithm, to find long paths in general graphs. The following lemma is Lemma 7 in
[
Mon18
]
, which is a special case of a result in
[
KLS15
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
G
is
k
-joined, then it has a path with at least
v(G)-2k
vertices.

Now we can use the
k
-joined property to show
Lemma

3.3
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.3
.

Let
\beta>\varepsilon>0
,
k=\varepsilon n/2
and
R\sim G(n,p)
with
p=C/n
for some sufficiently large constant
C
. Given a set
U\subset V(R)
of size
|U|=\beta n
, the probability that
R[U]
is not
k
-joined is at most

(1)

\displaystyle\binom{\beta n}{k}^{2}(1-p)^{k^{2}}\leqslant\bigg(\frac{e\beta n}{k}\bigg)^{2k}\cdot e^{-pk^{2}}\leqslant\bigg(\frac{2e\beta}{\varepsilon}\bigg)^{\varepsilon n}\cdot e^{-C\varepsilon^{2}n/4}\leqslant 2^{-n},

since
C
is sufficiently large. By the union bound over all sets
U
of size
\beta n
, it follows that with high probability the induced subgraph
R[U]
is
k
-joined for every
U\subset V(R)
with
|U|=\beta n
, thus by
Lemma

3.5
there exists a path of length at least
(\beta-\varepsilon)n
in
R[U]
, as required.
∎

3.2.
An absorber lemma

We now formally state the absorber lemma as follows.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
0<\varepsilon<2^{-14}\alpha^{2}
. There is

such that the following holds. If
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
and
R\sim G(n,p)
with

and

, then with high probability there exists a path
A\subset G_{\alpha}\cup R
with
\varepsilon n/8\leqslant v(A)\leqslant 4\varepsilon n
with the following two properties:

(i)

for every
x\in V(R)
.

(2)

\displaystyle e\big(R[N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)]\cap A\big)\geqslant\varepsilon^{2}n;

(ii)

for every

with
|U|\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}n/2
and every path

of length at least

such that

Our main aim in this subsection is to prove

that

Since

.
∎

We claim if there exists

Lemma 4.19
.

Let

. If

Now put

s/4

Proof of

4.1

In this subsection we will apply the structural

4.2
to show

4.1
. We will use a quasi-random property of

as follows.

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
0<\alpha\leqslant 1
,
\beta\geqslant 1-\alpha
be constants and
1\ll m\leqslant 2^{-5}n
. If
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
and

with

for every

with

To show

we need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 4.21
.

Let
0<\beta\leqslant 1
be a constant and
1\ll m\leqslant 2^{-5}n
, and let
R\sim G(n,p)
with
p=m/n^{2}
. Fix an arbitrary
U\subset V(R)
with
|U|=\beta n
, then with high probability there exists a matching
M_{U}\subset R[U]
of size

e(M_{U})\geqslant 2^{-4}\beta^{2}\cdot m.

The proof of
Lemma

4.21
uses simple probabilistic techniques, and for completeness we include the proof in the Appendix. To prove
Lemma

4.20
, We begin by selecting a maximal collection of
\beta n
-subsets
U_{i}\subset V(G_{\alpha})
such that each
U_{i}
contains no
\Omega(m)
-matching in
G_{\alpha}
, and for every pair
U_{i},U_{j}
, their respective maximal independent sets are disjoint. Note that these sets are nearly disjoint, so the collection has constant size, roughly
1/\beta
. We then add random edges to
G_{\alpha}
and apply
Lemma

4.21
to guarantee an
\Omega(m)
-matching within each
U_{i}
. Finally, we observe that any
\beta n
-subset almost completely overlap with some
U_{i}
, and hence must contain an
\Omega(m)
-matching. We now make this precise and prove
Lemma

4.20
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.20
.

Given
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
, let
\mathcal{U}
be the collection of subsets
U\subset V(G_{\alpha})
with
|U|=\beta n
such that every matching in
G_{\alpha}[U]
has fewer than
2^{-5}\beta^{2}m
edges. We may assume
\mathcal{U}\neq\emptyset
. Let
I(U)
be the largest independent set in
G_{\alpha}[U]
for
U\in\mathcal{U}
, then

(13)

\displaystyle|I(U)|\geqslant|U|-2\cdot 2^{-5}\beta^{2}m\geqslant\beta n-2^{-4}\beta^{2}m.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{0}=\{U_{1},U_{2},..,U_{\ell}\}

be a maximal collection of
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that

I(U_{i})\cap I(U_{j})=\emptyset

for each pair
U_{i},U_{j}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
with
i\neq j
. Note by (
13
), the number of sets in
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is

|\mathcal{U}_{0}|\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{n}{|I(U_{i})|}\right\rceil\leqslant\left\lceil\frac{n}{\beta n-2^{-4}\beta^{2}m}\right\rceil\leqslant 2\lceil\beta^{-1}\rceil.

Next let’s consider
G_{\alpha}\cup R
. For each
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
, by
Lemma

4.21
there exists a matching
M_{U_{i}}\subset(R\cup G_{\alpha})[U_{i}]

of size

(14)

\displaystyle e(M_{U_{i}})\geqslant 2^{-4}\beta^{2}m

with high probability. By taking a union bound over all
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
, we know that
(G_{\alpha}\cup R)[U_{i}]
contains a matching with at least
2^{-4}\beta^{2}m
edges for all
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
with high probability. In the remainder of this proof, we consider
R\cup G_{\alpha}
satisfying this property.

Now arbitrarily pick a set
U\in\mathcal{U}\setminus\mathcal{U}_{0}
. We claim there exists
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
such that

(15)

\displaystyle|I(U)\cap I(U_{i})|\geqslant\beta n-2^{-6}\beta^{2}m.

Indeed, there exists
U_{i}\in\mathcal{U}_{0}
such that
I(U)\cap I(U_{i})\neq\emptyset
, since
\mathcal{U}_{0}
is a maximal collection that
I(U_{j})\cap I(U_{k})=\emptyset
. If (
15
) doesn’t hold, then the neighborhood of each
x\in I(U)\cap I(U_{i})
doesn’t intersect to
I(U_{i})\cup I(U)
in
G_{\alpha}
. Hence by (
13
) and (
15
),

\displaystyle d_{G_{\alpha}}(x)\leqslant n-|I(U_{i})\cup I(U)|

\displaystyle\leqslant n-\big(|I(U_{i})|+|I(U)|-|I(U)\cap I(U_{i})|\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-\beta)n-2^{-6}\beta^{2}m,

which makes a contradiction since
d_{G_{\alpha}}(x)\geqslant\alpha n\geqslant(1-\beta)n
. Now by (
14
) and (
15
),

\displaystyle|V(M_{U_{i}})\cap U|\geqslant v(M_{U_{i}})-|U_{i}\setminus U|\geqslant v(M_{U_{i}})-\big(|U_{i}|-|I(U)\cap I(U_{i})|\big)\geqslant\frac{3}{4}\cdot v(M_{U_{i}}).

Therefore
(R\cup G_{\alpha})[U\cap U_{i}]
contains at least

\frac{1}{2}\cdot e(M_{U_{i}})\geqslant 2^{-5}\beta^{2}m

edges of
M_{U_{i}}
, that forms a matching, as required.
∎

Finally put the structural
Proposition

4.2
and
Lemmas

4.6
and
4.20
together, we are ready to finish the proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Let
\alpha=(r+1)/2r
and
\beta=1-\alpha
, let
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
be a graph with
v(G_{\alpha})=n
and
\delta(G_{\alpha})\geqslant\alpha n
, and let
G(n,m)
be the random graph on
V(G_{\alpha})
with
m
random edges. By
Lemma

4.20
, with high probability the union
G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
has the following property: for every subset
W\subset V(G_{\alpha})
with
|W|=\beta n
, there exists a matching
M\subset\big(G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)\big)[W]
with

(16)

\displaystyle e\big(M\big)>2^{-5}\beta^{2}m\geqslant 2^{-5}\cdot 2^{-2}\cdot(1-r^{-1})^{2}\geqslant 2^{-9}m.

Indeed we convert
G(n,p)
to
G(n,m)
(see Proposition 1.13 in
[
JLR11
]
) since the above property is monotone. Next let’s consider a fixed
F=G_{\alpha}\cup G(n,m)
which satisfies (
16
).

Let
\chi
be an arbitrary
r
-colouring of
E(F)
and let

b=2^{-20}r^{-1}m.

If the restriction of
\chi
to
G_{\alpha}
doesn’t have a Hamilton cycle with
b
colour bias, then by
Proposition

4.2
there exists a colour
c^{*}\in[r]
and a subset
U\subset V(G_{\alpha})
with
|U|=\alpha n
, such that every maximal matching
M^{\prime}\subset G_{\alpha}[U]
without
c^{*}
-edges satisfies

(17)

\displaystyle e(M^{\prime})\leqslant 2^{9}rb\leqslant 2^{-11}m.

Set
W=V(G_{\alpha})\setminus U
, then
|W|=\beta n
. Hence by (
16
), there exists a matching
M\subset F[W]
containing at least
2^{-9}m
edges. Then there exists a Hamilton cycle
H\subset F
which contains
M
as a subgraph by
Lemma

4.6
, since
v(M)\leqslant 2e(G(n,m))\leqslant 2m\leqslant n/2r
and

\delta(F)\geqslant\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n}{2r}\geqslant\frac{n}{2}+v(M).

Now we claim the Hamilton cycle
H
has at least
b
colour bias. Observe that the edges of
H
not coloured by
c^{*}
can be partitioned into three parts: the edges in
H\cap F[U]
, those in
H\cap F[W]
, and those in the bipartite subgraph
H\cap F[W,U]
.

In
H\cap F[U]
, there are at most

(18)

\displaystyle q\leqslant 2e(M^{\prime})

edges that are not coloured by
c^{*}
, otherwise there would be a matching in
F[U]
with more than
e(M^{\prime})
edges without
c^{*}
-edges.

The subgraph
H\cap F[W]
is a linear path forest which contains

(19)

\displaystyle d\geqslant e(M)

edges, since
M\subset(H\cap F[W])
. Note it’s possible that all edges of
H\cap F[W]
are not of
c^{*}
.

The bipartite subgraph
H\cap F[W,U]
contains at most

(20)

\displaystyle 2d+2\cdot\big(|W|-2d\big)

edges. Indeed, the linear forest
H\cap F[W]
contains at most
d
disjoint paths and up to
|W|-2d
isolated vertices. Each isolated vertex is incident to two edges in
H\cap F[W,U]
, while each path endpoint (of which there are at most
2d
) is incident to one such edge. All remaining vertices are not incident to any edge in
H\cap F[W,U]
. Therefore, in total,
H\cap F[W,U]
contains at most
2(|W|-2d)+2d
edges. It’s also possible that all these edges don’t use
c^{*}
.

Putting (
18
), (
19
) and (
20
) together, we know that the Hamilton cycle
H
contains at most

(21)

\displaystyle q+d+2\cdot\big(|W|-2d\big)+2d

\displaystyle\leqslant 2|W|-d+q\leqslant\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot n-2^{-10}m

edges that are not of colour

. The second inequality holds since
|W|=\beta n=(r-1)n/2r
, and by (

), (

), (

) and (

) we have

d-q\geqslant e(M)-2e(M^{\prime})\geqslant 2^{-9}m-2\cdot 2^{-11}m\geqslant 2^{-10}m.

Therefore by (
21
), the number of
c^{*}
-edges in
H
is at least

n-\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot n+2^{-10}m\geqslant\frac{n}{r}+2^{-10}m,

which implies
H
has colour bias at least
2^{-10}m>b
, finishing the proof.
∎

For the reader’s convenience, we will recall the statement of
Lemmas

3.7
,
3.8
and
4.21
and present the proofs.

First let’s focus on the quasi-random properties of
G(n,p)
(
Lemma

3.7
).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
c>0
be constant, and let
R\sim G(n,p)
with
p=c/n
.
Then

cn/4\leqslant e(R)\leqslant cn\qquad\textup{and}\qquad Y(R)\leqslant 2p^{2}n^{3}\leqslant 2c^{2}n

hold with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n^{1/4})}
. Moreover for every
\delta>0
with
2e/\delta<e^{c\delta/2}
,

R~\ \textup{is}~\ \delta n\textup{-joined}

with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n)}
.

We will use the Kim-Vu inequality
[
KV00
]
(also see Theorem 3A in
[
JR02
]
) to control the upper tail of
Y(R)
. Let

X
be a random variable counting the number of appearances of some fixed graph

H
as a (not necessarily induced) subgraph of
R\sim G(n,p)
, Then

X
can be written as a polynomial of degree
k=e(H)
in the edge indicator variables. Let

\lambda:=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[X]\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}[X]:=\max_{e\in E(K_{n})}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[X_{e}]

where
X_{e}
denotes the number of copies of
H
containing the edge
e
.

Lemma 5.2

(Kim-Vu inequality)
.

For any
\ell\geqslant 1
, if
\lambda\geqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}(X)
, then

\mathbb{P}\Big(|X-\lambda|\geqslant c_{k}\ell^{k}\sqrt{\lambda\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}[X]}\Big)\leqslant\exp\big(-\ell+2(k-1)\log n\big)

for some constant
c_{k}>0
.

Using the Kim-Vu inequality, it’s straightforward to prove
Lemma

5.1
.

Proof of
Lemma

5.1
.

First note that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e(R)]=\binom{n}{2}\cdot p=\frac{c(n-1)}{2}.

And by Chernoff’s inequality, it follows that

\mathbb{P}(cn/4\leqslant e(R)\leqslant cn)\geqslant 1-e^{-\Omega(n)}.

Next let
Z(R)
be the number of copies of
P_{2}
in
R
. Observe that deterministically

Y(R)\leqslant 2Z(R).

We apply the Kim-Vu inequality with
X=Z(R)
.
Note that

\lambda=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z(R)]\leqslant\binom{n}{2}\cdot n\cdot p^{2}\leqslant n^{3}p^{2}/2\leqslant c^{2}n/2,

and that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}[Z(R)]\leqslant 2np\leqslant 2c
. Moreover, if
\ell=(4c_{2})^{-1/2}\cdot(cn)^{1/4}
, where
c_{2}>0
is the constant in
Lemma

5.2
, then

c_{2}\ell^{2}\sqrt{\lambda\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}[Z(R)]}\leqslant c_{2}\cdot(4c_{2})^{-1}\cdot(cn)^{1/2}\cdot(c^{2}n\cdot 2c)^{1/2}\leqslant c^{2}n/2.

By
Lemma

5.2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(Z(R)\geqslant c^{2}n\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\big(|Z(R)-\lambda|\geqslant c^{2}n/2\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\Big(|Z(R)-\lambda|\geqslant c_{2}\ell^{2}\sqrt{\lambda\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}[Z(R)]}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\big(-\ell+2\log n\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\Omega(n^{1/4})},

and we therefore have

\mathbb{P}(Y(R)\geqslant 2c^{2}n)\leqslant\mathbb{P}(Z(R)\geqslant c^{2}n)\leqslant e^{-\Omega(n^{1/4})}.

Finally, similar to (
1
), the probability that
R
is not
\delta n
-joined is at most

\displaystyle\binom{n}{\delta n}^{2}(1-p)^{(\delta n)^{2}}\leqslant\bigg(\frac{e}{\delta}\bigg)^{2n}\cdot e^{-p\delta^{2}n^{2}}\leqslant\bigg(\frac{e}{\delta}\cdot e^{-c\delta^{2}/2}\bigg)^{2n}<4^{-n},

since
2e/\delta<e^{c\delta/2}
, as required.
∎

Next let’s consider the quasi-random properties of
G(n,p)\cup G_{\alpha}
(
Lemma

3.8
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0<\alpha\leqslant 1
and
0<c\leqslant 2^{-8}
be constants, let
G_{\alpha}\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(n)
and
R\sim G(n,p)
with
p=c/n
and
V(G_{\alpha})=V(R)
. With high probability
G_{\alpha}\cup R
has the following properties:

(i)

For every
x\in V(R)
,

e\big(R[N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)]\big)\geqslant\alpha^{2}cn/4.

(ii)

For every
x,y\in V(R)
with

|N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)\setminus N_{G_{\alpha}}(y)|\geqslant\alpha n/2
and
|N_{G_{\alpha}}(y)\setminus N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)|\geqslant\alpha n/2,
there exists a matching
M_{x,y}\subset R
of size

e(M_{x,y})\geqslant 2^{-4}c\alpha^{2}n

between
N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)\setminus N_{G_{\alpha}}(y)
and
N_{G_{\alpha}}(y)\setminus N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)
.

Proof.

For (i), note that for each
x\in V(G_{\alpha})
we have

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e\big(R[N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)]\big)\big]\geqslant\binom{\alpha n}{2}\cdot p\geqslant\alpha^{2}n/2.

Then we deduce
e\big(R[N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)]\big)\geqslant\alpha^{2}cn/4
holds for every

by Chernoff’s inequality and take a union bound over
x\in V(R)
.

For (ii), fix
x,y\in V(G_{\alpha})
. By assumption for
W_{1}:=N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)\setminus N_{G_{\alpha}}(y)
and
W_{2}:=N_{G_{\alpha}}(y)\setminus N_{G_{\alpha}}(x)
, we have
|W_{1}|\geqslant\alpha n/2
and
|W_{2}|\geqslant\alpha n/2
.
Note that the expected number of edges in
R\big[W_{1},W_{2}\big]
is at least

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[e\big(R\big[W_{1},W_{2}\big]\big)\big]\geqslant(\alpha n/2)^{2}\cdot p\geqslant c\alpha^{2}n/4.

Hence by apply Chernoff’s inequality,

e\big(R\big[W_{1},W_{2}\big]\big)\geqslant c\alpha^{2}n/8

holds with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(n)}
. Let
R_{x,y}\subset R
be the induced subgraph on
W_{1}\cup W_{2}
, then the number of non-isolated edges in
R\big[W_{1},W_{2}\big]
is at most

. Note that
R_{x,y}\sim G\big(|W_{1}|+|W_{2}|,p\big)
and
|W_{1}|+|W_{2}|\leqslant 2\alpha n
, then by
Lemma

5.1
,

Y(R_{x,y})\leqslant 2\cdot p^{2}\cdot(|W_{1}|+|W_{2}|)^{3}\leqslant 2\cdot(c/n)^{2}\cdot(2\alpha n)^{3}\leqslant 16\alpha^{3}c^{2}n

holds with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n^{1/4})}
. Now let
M_{x,y}
be the number of isolated edges in
R[W_{1},W_{2}]
, then

(22)

\displaystyle e(M_{x,y})\geqslant c\alpha^{2}n/8-Y(R_{x,y})\geqslant c\alpha^{2}n/8-16\alpha^{3}c^{2}n\geqslant 2^{-4}c\alpha^{2}n

since
16\alpha^{3}c^{2}n\leqslant 2^{-4}c\alpha^{2}n
by
c\leqslant 2^{-8}
and
\alpha\leqslant 1
. Note (
22
) holds with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(n^{1/4})}
, thus we can take a union bound over all
x,y\in V(G_{\alpha})
.
∎

The following Lemma is
Lemma

4.21
in
Section

4.3
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
0<\beta\leqslant 1
be a constant and
1\ll m\leqslant 2^{-5}n
, and let
R\sim G(n,p)
with
p=m/n^{2}
. Fix an arbitrary
U\subset V(R)
with
|U|=\beta n
, then with high probability there exists a matching
M_{U}\subset R[U]
of size

e(M_{U})\geqslant 2^{-4}\beta^{2}m.

Proof.

First
R[U]\sim G(\beta n,p)
, and the expected number of edges in
R[U]
is

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[e(R[U])]=\binom{\beta n}{2}\cdot p\geqslant\beta^{2}m/4.

Then by Chernoff’s inequality with high probability

e(R[U])\geqslant\beta^{2}m/8.

Next recall that
Y(R[U])
is the number of edges in
R[U]
that are not isolated.
Note that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Y(R[U])]\leqslant 2\cdot\binom{\beta n}{2}\cdot n\cdot p^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}n^{3}p^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}m^{2}/n.

If
\beta^{2}m^{2}\ll n
, that is
m\ll n^{1/2}
, then by Markov’s inequality
Y(R[U])=0
with high probability. Hence all edges of
R[U]
are isolated and form a matching
M\subset R[U]
with

e(M)\geqslant\beta^{2}m/8>2^{-4}\beta^{2}m.

Otherwise if
m=\Omega(n^{1/2})
, recall that
Z(R)
is the number of copies of
P_{2}
in
R
and deterministically
Y(R)\leqslant 2Z(R)
.
We apply the Kim-Vu inequality with
X=Z(R[U])
. Note that the expectation of
Z(R[U])

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[Z(R[U])]\leqslant\binom{\beta n}{2}\cdot n\cdot p^{2}\leqslant\beta^{2}m^{2}/2n.

Let
k=2,\lambda=\beta^{2}m^{2}/2n,\ell=(\beta m)^{1/8}
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{1}=\beta n\cdot p=\beta m/n
. By
Lemma

5.2
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(Z(R[U])\geqslant\beta^{2}m^{2}/n\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\Big(\big|Z(R[U])-\lambda\big|\geqslant\beta^{2}m^{2}/2n\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\Big(\big|Z(R[U])-\lambda\big|\geqslant c\ell^{2}\cdot(\beta m)^{3/2}/n\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\big(-\ell+O(\log n)\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-\Omega(n^{1/16})},

where
c
is a constant and the second inequality holds since

\ell^{2}\cdot(\beta m)^{3/2}/n\leqslant(\beta m)^{7/4}/n\ll(\beta m)^{2}/n

and
n
is sufficiently large. Then

\mathbb{P}\big(Y(R[U])\geqslant 2(\beta m)^{2}/n\big)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\big(Z(R[U])\geqslant\beta^{2}m^{2}/n\big)\leqslant e^{-\Omega(n^{1/16})}.

Therefore with high probability there exists a matching in
R[U]
which contains at least

e(R[U])-Y(R[U])\geqslant\beta^{2}m/8-2(\beta m)^{2}/n\geqslant 2^{-4}\beta^{2}m

edges, since
m\leqslant 2^{-5}n
, finishing the proof.
∎

A novel tool to predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis in patients with primary osseous sacral/pelvic tumors: a large real-world multicenter study
Authors: Zhenbang Zhou, Sanjun Chen, Zhangheng Huang, Binbin Yang, Zhen Zhao, Kai Chen, Peiling Dai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn patients with primary osseous sacral/pelvic tumors, once pulmonary metastasis has occurred, their prognosis is worrying. It is therefore essential to construct a novel tool to achieve accurate prediction of the probability of pulmonary metastasis from primary osseous sacral/pelvic tumors.MethodsThis study retrospectively analyzed data from 407 patients with primary osseous sacral/pelvic tumors from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results database. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were used to identify risk factors. Construction of predictive models based on logistic regression algorithm in R software. The calibration, discrimination, and clinical utility of the models are validated in a validation cohort. The Kaplan-Meier survival curve and log-rank test were used to examine the pulmonary metastasis risk classification system for evaluation.ResultsThe total cohort was randomly divided into a training cohort (287 patients) and a validation cohort (120 patients). Five independent risk factors were identified to develop a nomogram model to predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis in patients. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (0.860 for the training cohort and 0.895 for the validation cohort) showed that the model showed satisfactory discrimination in both validation cohorts. The calibration curve showed a high predictive accuracy of the model and the Hosmer-Lemeshow test indicated a good model fit (p > 0.05). The decision curve analysis showed that the nomogram is clinically useful and can provide a net benefit to patients within certain limits.ConclusionWe have successfully developed a nomogram and risk classification system to accurately predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis from primary osseous sacral/pelvic tumors.

Introduction

Metastatic tumors are most common in pelvic and sacral tumors, whereas primary osseous pelvic/sacral tumors (PT) are very rare [1, 2]. PT mainly includes osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and giant cell tumor of bone, of which chondrosarcoma is the most common [3–5]. Due to their special location, PT are not only fast-growing and insidious, making them difficult to diagnose at an early stage [6, 7]. PT are often very large at the time of diagnosis and cause compression of adjacent organs, blood vessels, and nerves due to the lack of anatomical barriers to tumor spread [8]. Larger tumors are more likely to metastasize, resulting in poorer overall patient survival [9]. PT may metastasize to the lungs, liver, and brain, with the lung being the most common site of metastasis [10, 11]. However, there is a need to improve the accuracy in the detection and prediction of pulmonary metastasis due to the atypical nature of some pulmonary metastases and the inadequate ability of current radiological techniques to detect small lung nodules [12, 13].

Nomogram is a predictive tool that can integrate multiple correlates to predict a particular outcome, offering the advantage of high visualization and accurate prediction [14]. Nomogram has been widely utilized in cancer research to predict the prognosis of patients with various cancers and has been demonstrated to be more accurate than the TNM stage. In previous studies, nomograms for predicting the probability of pulmonary metastasis in specific histological types of sarcoma have been developed [15]. However, to our knowledge, no reports have been seen on the construction of nomograms for predicting the probability of occurrence of pulmonary metastasis in patients with PT. The goal of this study was to undertake a large cohort analysis of individuals identified with PT in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, given the rarity of PT and the importance of predicting the development of pulmonary metastasis. First, to explore the risk factors linked to pulmonary metastasis in PT. Second, create a nomogram tool to forecast the likelihood of pulmonary metastasis in PT patients.

Methods

Patients and data collection

The patient data for the study was obtained from a widely used clinical cancer research database - the SEER database. The SEER database collects cancer-related information covering approximately 28% of the total US population [16, 17]. The inclusion criteria for data extraction were (1) the primary site of the tumor was the sacrum or pelvis, (2) the diagnosis of the only primary malignancy, (3) The status of pulmonary metastasis can be obtained. Exclusion criteria were (1) the sacral or pelvic tumor was not the only primary tumor, (2) other metastatic sites were unknown. This study eventually enrolled 407 patients, 55 of whom developed pulmonary metastasis, based on these criteria. Extraction of clinicopathological and demographic data on patients: age (< 60 and ≥ 60 years), race, sex, tumor grade, laterality, perioperative radiotherapy status, chemotherapy status, tumor extension degree (intra- or extra-periosteal), other site metastases, histological types, surgery, tumor size (< 80 mm and ≥ 80 mm). The histological types are divided into four groups based on the latest WHO criteria: osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and others. Patients’ informed agreement is not necessary because the study is retrospective and does not contain personally identifiable information.

Statistical analysis

Using the R software, the total cohort was randomly divided into a training cohort and a validation cohort in a 7:3 ratio. The training cohort was used to create the nomogram and internally validate it, whilst the validation cohort was used to validate the nomogram’s performance externally. Univariate logistic regression analysis was used to screen for risk factors that might be associated with pulmonary metastasis. To rule out confounding effects between risk factors discovered by univariate logistic regression analysis, multivariate logistic regression was used. Based on the established independent risk factors associated with pulmonary metastasis, we created a nomogram model for predicting the chance of pulmonary metastasis based on the identified independent risk variables associated with the disease. a nomogram to predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was used to assess the nomogram’s prediction ability. The goodness of fit between predicted and actual values was evaluated using calibration curves. The net benefit of the model was calculated using decision curve analysis at a suitable risk threshold, indicating the model’s clinical utility [18]. The “DynNom” software package was used to construct a web-based nomogram in R software. We used the X-tiles software to determine the optimal cut-off value for the risk of pulmonary metastasis, which was divided into three subgroups: high, middle, and low risk of pulmonary metastasis [19]. Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis and the log-rank test were used for prognostic differences between different subgroups at risk of pulmonary metastases. All statistical analyses and graphs were done through SPSS (25.0) and R software version 4.0.3. Where a p-value < 0.05 was set as statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

According to our criteria, 407 patients were included in the study. All PT patients were randomized into two cohorts. In the training cohort, the vast majority were patients aged <60 years (72.5%) and 86.4% were white. Of these, 40 patients (13.9%) had pulmonary metastases. In terms of tumor characteristics, patients were predominantly graded III-IV (55.7%), chondrosarcoma (53.3%). Regarding treatment, 75.3% of patients did not undergo perioperative radiotherapy, but some patients received chemotherapy (43.2%), and most of the enrolled patients had predominantly big tumor size (≥ 80 mm) (67.9%). In addition, some patients (12.2%) had metastases to other sites. The full demographic and clinicopathological characteristics of PT patients were shown in Table 1.

Nomogram variable screening

Univariate logistic regression analysis showed tumor grade, surgery, histological type, perioperative radiotherapy status, chemotherapy status, tumor size, tumor extension degree, and other site metastases (Table 2). After removal of confounders between variables by multivariate logistic regression analysis, histological type (Ewing sarcoma [HR, 0.227; 95% CI, 0.069–0.751; p < 0.05]), perioperative radiotherapy (Yes [HR, 3.137; 95% CI, 1.228–8.015; p < 0.05]), surgery (Yes [HR, 0.270; 95% CI, 0.114–0.641; p < 0.05]), chemotherapy (Yes [HR, 4.422; 95% CI, 1.417–13.803; p < 0.05]), other site metastases (Yes [HR, 4.022; 95% CI, 1.611–10.042; P < 0.05]) was identified as an independent risk factor for pulmonary metastases (Table 2).

Development and validation of the nomogram

Based on the screened risk factors for pulmonary metastasis, we constructed a nomogram (Fig. 1) for predicting the probability of pulmonary metastasis from PT. A total score was obtained by summing the corresponding scores for each patient-specific variable, and a vertical line drawn towards the axis of the total score gave the probability of pulmonary metastasis in that patient. To evaluate the performance of the model, we depicted the receiver operating characteristic curves for both cohorts. The AUC for the nomogram was 0.860 for the training cohort and 0.895 for the validation cohort, showing that the model succeeded in predicting the likelihood of pulmonary metastasis in patients (Fig. 2). The calibration curves for both cohorts were near to the ideal curve of 45°, as shown in Fig. 3, confirming the model’s higher prediction accuracy. Furthermore, the decision curve analysis revealed that the nomogram can deliver a net clinical benefit within a specific threshold range, showing that the nomogram has a good clinical application (Fig. 4).

Web-based nomogram

To facilitate the application of the model in clinical work, we also constructed a web-based nomogram(https://dplwmc.shinyapps.io/dynnomapp/) to accurately predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis in patients with PT. Clinicians can enter the web address into their computer or mobile phone and then select the relevant clinical factors for the corresponding patient, ultimately achieving an accurate calculation of the probability of pulmonary metastasis from PT.

Discussion

PT are known to be a very low incidence malignancy [2]. Given their extremely low incidence, the current reports of primary bony sacral/pelvic tumors are mainly single-center, limited sample size studies. The limited sample size makes their results not very convincing, and an in-depth understanding of the clinical features of a disease is essential. Pulmonary metastases are a significant factor in the prognosis of patients with PT, as they can easily lead to respiratory failure secondary to pulmonary metastasis, which can lead to death [20–22]. Generally, doctors will recommend a CT scan of the chest when the patient’s diagnosis is clear. Unfortunately, it has been reported that up to 36% of pulmonary metastases are not detected by a CT scan of the chest [23, 24]. As a result, a tool that can accurately predict the likelihood of pulmonary metastasis in individuals with PT is urgently needed. In this study, 407 patients with PT from the SEER database were retrospectively analyzed to identify risk factors associated with pulmonary metastasis. Based on this, we also developed and validated a nomogram to accurately predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis in patients with PT. If the predicted probability of pulmonary metastasis is high, clinicians and patients alike should pay close attention to the lungs and perform high-resolution CT or PET-CT examinations or even lung aspiration biopsies on patients [25, 26].

PT are not sensitive to radiotherapy and chemotherapy and are difficult to treat satisfactorily. Surgery is therefore considered to be the only effective treatment for PT [7]. The surgeon makes every effort to achieve a negative resection of the incised margin during the operation, thus minimizing the risk of local recurrence. However, the anatomy of the pelvis and sacrum is complex, and tumors often invade the surrounding nerves, blood vessels, pelvic organs, and other important structures [27]. This makes it difficult for the surgeon to obtain sufficient margins to remove the tumor, and the operation is often accompanied by extensive bleeding, which can be severe and even lead to hemorrhagic shock [28]. Despite these drawbacks and shortcomings, the benefits of surgery for patients with PT are undeniable, not only in terms of improved prognosis but also in reducing the likelihood of pulmonary metastasis in patients. Larger tumor size has been reported as an important risk factor for the development of pulmonary metastasis [29]. This may be since the change in tumor size is closely related to its biological behavior, and that surgery may have the effect of reducing the probability of pulmonary metastasis by making the tumor smaller to some extent [30]. In previous studies, other site metastases were considered to be an independent risk factor for pulmonary metastasis [15]. Interestingly, we came to a similar conclusion in the current study. We speculate that this may be because once other site metastases or multiple metastases have occurred it represents that bloodstream metastasis has occurred, thus accelerating the metastasis of the tumor to the lungs [31]. Currently, neoadjuvant chemotherapy combined with surgery is seen as the standard of care for osteosarcoma. However, it should be noted that not all patients with osteosarcoma respond well to chemotherapy, especially those with primary tumor sites in the sacrum and pelvis. This is because neoadjuvant chemotherapy is meant to delay surgery and expose patients to a large tumor burden for a long time, thus increasing the chance of distant metastases [32]. This explains perfectly why the patients in this study with the histological type of osteosarcoma and who received chemotherapy were at greater risk of developing pulmonary metastasis. Currently, the prevailing view is that the benefit of perioperative radiotherapy in patients with PT is limited. Although perioperative radiotherapy may cause tumor necrosis and reduce the size of the tumor, it may make resection more difficult and increase the probability of pulmonary metastasis due to the effects of radiation on soft tissue.

Several previous large cohort studies have established age as a risk factor for the development of pulmonary metastasis [15, 33]. Karski et al. reported that the probability of pulmonary metastasis became greater with increasing age, especially when age was greater than 50 years [34]. In addition, Li et al. discovered that age is an independent risk factor for pulmonary metastasis in a series of investigations [33]. Surprisingly, age did not show a correlation with pulmonary metastasis in the current study. Therefore, we believe that the correlation between age and pulmonary metastasis is debatable and should be investigated further in bigger, prospective studies. We believe that this study provides some useful insights. First, studies identifying risk factors associated with the development of pulmonary metastasis from PT are few and extremely limited in sample size. Our retrospective analysis of a large sample of recent cases identified five risk variables for pulmonary metastasis, improving our understanding of the occurrence of pulmonary metastasis in PT. Secondly, a practical and well-performing nomogram tool was developed to accurately predict the probability of pulmonary metastasis from PT. Despite the very promising results of this study, it is undeniable that there are still some shortcomings in this study. Firstly, as a retrospective cohort study, some selection bias is inevitable. Secondly, future studies could prospectively further build and verify the nomogram in another external dataset.

Conclusion

In summary, we constructed a nomogram tool capable of predicting the probability of pulmonary metastasis in patients with PT. Not only does the nomogram show good predictive accuracy and clinical utility, but it can also provide an accurate assessment of the risk of developing pulmonary metastasis in patients, thus guiding clinicians to optimize personalized treatment and make better clinically relevant decisions.

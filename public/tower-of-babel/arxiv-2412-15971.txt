Small-time central limit theorems for stochastic Volterra integral equations and their Markovian lifts
Martin Friesen, Stefan Gerhold, Kristof Wiedermann

Abstract
We study small-time central limit theorems for stochastic Volterra integral equations with Hölder continuous coefficients and general locally square integrable Volterra kernels. We prove the convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions, a functional CLT, and limit theorems for smooth transformations of the process, which covers a large class of Volterra kernels that includes rough models based on Riemann-Liouville kernels with short- and long-range dependencies. To illustrate our results, we derive asymptotic pricing formulae for digital calls on the realized variance in three different regimes. The latter provides a robust and model-independent pricing method for small maturities in rough volatility models. Finally, for the case of completely monotone kernels, we introduce a flexible framework of Hilbert space-valued Markovian lifts and derive analogous limit theorems for such lifts.



Keywords:
stochastic Volterra integral equation; central limit theorem; completely monotone kernel; Markovian lift; volatility derivatives.

As another application for the CLTs developed in this work, in a companion paper we provide a general proof that solutions of SVIEs with non-degenerate kernels do not possess the Markov property. The latter became a folklore fact stated in many works on SVIEs, albeit, to the best of our knowledge, a rigorous proof is still open.

1.2
) are, in general, neither semimartingales nor satisfy the Markov property, as mentioned above, a common approach to overcome these obstacles is based on an augmentation of the state space that allows to recover the Markov property in an infinite-dimensional framework, see

35

\displaystyle X_{t}=g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)\hskip 0.56917ptb(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)\hskip 0.56917pt\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

&=\ \mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Big\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+Z_{t_{j}/n}^{n}\big)\leq y_{j}\Big\}\cap\big\{Z_{n}^{N,*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\bigg]\\
&\hskip 15.6491pt+\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Big\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+Z_{t_{j}/n}^{n}\big)\leq y_{j}\Big\}\cap\big\{Z_{n}^{N,*}>z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\bigg].\end{split}

(2.11)

Note that the moment bounds obtained in Proposition
2.1
as well as
\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}[Y_{t}^{n}]}
depend on the starting value only through the continuous, asymptotically non-vanishing factors
(1+|x_{n}|^{p})
and
\sigma(x_{n})
(see (
2.10
)), respectively. By condition (i) in combination with
\chi_{b}>0
, the interval in (
2.2
) is non-degenerate and independent of
n
. Hence, Proposition
2.1
also holds for
K\ast\mathrm{d}B
and
Z
replaced with
Y^{\sigma,n}=\sigma(x_{n})(K\ast\mathrm{d}B)
and
Z^{n}
, respectively, with the power function
z
being independent of
n
. Therefore, the last probability in (
2.11
) vanishes as
n\rightarrow\infty
, since we can estimate for
p\geq\max\{2/\chi_{b},2/\chi_{\sigma}\}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[Z_{n}^{N,*}>z(t_{N}/n)\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[\big(Z_{n}^{N,*}\big)^{p}\big]}{z(t_{N}/n)^{p}}\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big[\big|Z_{t_{i}/n}^{n}\big|^{p}\big]}{z(t_{N}/n)^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim N(1+|x_{n}|^{p})\left(\tfrac{t_{N}}{n}\right)^{\left(\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}+\gamma\hskip 0.56917pt(1+\chi_{b}),\,\gamma\hskip 0.56917pt(1+\chi_{\sigma})\right\}-q\right)\,p},

(2.12)

where the exponent is positive by the choice of
q
according to Proposition
2.1
.

For the first term in (
2.11
), we study the asymptotic behaviour of certain upper and lower bounds. We may find an upper bound by estimating

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Big\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+Z_{t_{j}/n}^{n}\big)\leq y_{j}\Big\}\cap\big\{Z_{n}^{N,*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Big\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}-z(t_{N}/n)\big)\leq y_{j}\Big\}\bigg]

(2.13)

which is, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the distribution function of a random vector following a multivariate normal distribution evaluated at
(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})^{\intercal}
. Therefore, by Lévy’s continuity theorem, it suffices to study the asymptotic of its mean and covariance structure. For the mean we obtain for every
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,\big|Y_{t_{j}/n}^{b,n}-z(t_{N}/n)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}}\,\bigg(b(x_{n})\int_{0}^{t_{j}/n}|K(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s+n^{-q}\bigg)

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(b(x_{n})\,n^{\gamma_{*}-\gamma-\tfrac{1}{2}}+n^{\gamma_{*}-q}\Big)\longrightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which is an immediate consequence of (
1.4
) in combination with the Hölder inequality, condition (i) as well
as Proposition
2.1
, utilizing
\lambda(n)\sim\big(\mathrm{Var}[Y_{1/n}^{1,n}]\big)^{-1}
. On the other hand, we observe for the covariance matrix for every
i,j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with
i\leq j
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}\Big(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355ptY_{t_{i}/n}^{\sigma,n},\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355ptY_{t_{j}/n}^{\sigma,n}\Big)

(2.15)

\displaystyle=\sigma(x_{n})^{2}\lambda(n)\int_{0}^{t_{i}/n}K(t_{j}/n-s)K(t_{i}/n-s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sigma(x_{n})^{2}\lambda(n)\int_{0}^{t_{i}/n}K((t_{j}-t_{i})/n+s)K(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\sigma(\overline{x})^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{i}}\overline{K}(t_{j}-t_{i}+s)\overline{K}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, where we applied condition (ii). Hence, combining (
2.14
), (
2.15
) and Lévy’s continuity theorem shows that the above random vector converges weakly and its limiting distribution agrees with the law of
(Y^{\sigma,\infty}_{t_{1}},\dots,Y^{\sigma,\infty}_{t_{N}})^{\intercal}
. Since
(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})^{\intercal}
has been chosen as a continuity point of its distribution function, we obtain from the Portmanteau lemma the following convergence for the upper bound found in (
2.13
):

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}-z(t_{N}/n)\big)\leq y_{j}\right\}\bigg]=\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\left\{Y^{\sigma,\infty}_{t_{j}}\leq y_{j}\right\}\bigg].

(2.16)

On the other hand, a lower bound may be obtained by estimating

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\Big\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+Z_{t_{j}/n}^{n}\big)\leq y_{j}\Big\}\cap\big\{Z_{n}^{N,*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Big\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+z(t_{N}/n)\big)\leq y_{j}\Big\}\cap\left\{Z_{n}^{N,*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\right\}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+z(t_{N}/n)\big)\leq y_{j}\right\}\bigg]-\mathbb{P}\big[Z_{n}^{N,*}>z(t_{N}/n)\big].

According to (
2.12
), the latter probability vanishes again as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, by performing the same arguments as for the upper bound,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(Y_{t_{j}/n}^{n}+z(t_{N}/n)\big)\leq y_{j}\right\}\bigg]=\mathbb{P}\bigg[\bigcap_{j=1}^{N}\Big\{Y^{\sigma,\infty}_{t_{j}}\leq y_{j}\Big\}\bigg]

can be shown since the only difference with regards to (
2.16
) is the sign of
\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,z(t_{N}/n)
. However, this term asymptotically vanishes in (
2.14
). Hence, applying the multi-dimensional version of the Helly-Bray theorem concludes the proof, as upper and lower bound have the same limit.
∎

Note that
x_{n}=x_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is, in particular, an admissible choice – provided that
\sigma(x_{0})\neq 0
holds – in order to obtain a non-degenerate limit distribution. As the next step, we extend this CLT on the finite-dimensional distributions towards a functional CLT that captures the convergence of the process on the path space on any finite interval
[0,T]
.

Corollary 2.3
.

In the framework of Theorem
2.2
, assume additionally that
K
satisfies (
1.5
) and that
\gamma^{\prime}:=\gamma\wedge\overline{\gamma}=\gamma_{*}
holds. Then also the corresponding functional CLT holds, i.e. for each
T>0
we have as
n\rightarrow\infty
:

\left(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.56917pt\big(X_{t/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}(Y^{\sigma,\infty})_{t\in[0,T]}.

(2.17)

Proof.

The weak convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions of the processes on
[0,T]
given as in (
2.17
) is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.2
, where we implicitly assume
n>T
so that
t/n<1
holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. Hence, it remains to show the tightness of the laws with respect to the uniform topology. To this end, by an application of the moment estimate obtained in
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
for
p\geq 2
, we obtain for arbitrary
t,t^{\prime}\in[0,T]
and
n>T
:

\mathbb{E}\big[\big|X_{t/n}^{x_{n}}-X_{t^{\prime}/n}^{x_{n}}\big|^{p}\big]\leq C\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[|b(X_{u}^{x_{n}})|^{p}+|\sigma(X_{u}^{x_{n}})|^{p}\big]\,n^{-\gamma^{\prime}p}\,(t-t^{\prime})^{\gamma^{\prime}p},

and by linear growth combined with the moment estimate (
A.2
) from Lemma
A.1
for
g_{n}\equiv x_{n}
as well as
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
being convergent we can conclude

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N},n>T}\sup_{u\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\big[|b(X_{u}^{x_{n}})|^{p}+|\sigma(X_{u}^{x_{n}})|^{p}\big]<\infty.

Hence, due to
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma_{*}
combined with the bound
\lambda(n)\lesssim n^{2\gamma_{*}}
which follows from (
2.6
) and (
1.4
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(X_{t/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big)-\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(X_{t^{\prime}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}p}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\big|X_{t/n}^{x_{n}}-X_{t^{\prime}/n}^{x_{n}}\big|^{p}\big]\lesssim(t-t^{\prime})^{\gamma^{\prime}p},

which, combined with
X_{0/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, gives Kolmogorov’s tightness criterion (see e.g.
[
47
, Theorem XIII.1.8]
) for
p>(1/\gamma^{\prime}\vee 2)
and hence completes the proof.
∎

This result is applicable for the Riemann-Liouville kernel
(see Example
2.6
below) with
H\in(0,1/2]
, but not for
H>1/2
, since then we cannot verify the tightness condition, as we generally obtain
\gamma=\gamma_{*}=H
and
\overline{\gamma}=\min\{H,1/2\}
(see also Example
2.6
). Let us further remark that the CLT and fCLT obtained in this section could be also shown for time-dependent coefficients
b,\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, provided that our main assumption on the Hölder continuity holds with a constant independent of the time variable.

2.2
Extension by the delta method

Another possible extension concerns the CLT for the transformed process
f(X^{x_{n}})
, where
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is sufficiently smooth. In such a case we need to study the family of the transformed normalized processes on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
given by

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\left(f\big(X^{x_{n}}_{\cdot/n}\big)-f(x_{n})\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.18)

By using the so-called delta method, we obtain the following result
for the finite-dimensional distributions:

Corollary 2.4
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
2.2
, assume that for each
n\geq 1
there exists a continuous solution
X^{x_{n}}
of (
1.2
),
and let
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(f\big(X_{t_{j}/n}^{x_{n}}\big)-f(x_{n})\big)\right)_{j=1,\dots,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\overline{x})\left(Y_{t_{j}}^{\sigma,\infty}\right)_{j=1,\dots,N}.

(2.19)

Moreover, if
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,
f^{\prime}(\overline{x})=0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}f^{\prime}(x_{n})=0
, then

\displaystyle\left(\lambda(n)\big(f\big(X_{t_{j}/n}^{x_{n}}\big)-f(x_{n})\big)\right)_{j=1,\dots,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})}{2}\left(\big(Y_{t_{j}}^{\sigma,\infty}\big)^{2}\right)_{j=1,\dots,N},

(2.20)

and so the one-dimensional marginals follow a scaled
\chi^{2}
-distribution with one degree of freedom.

Proof.

Firstly, let us note that, by assumption, the solutions
X^{x_{n}}
are constructed on filtered probability spaces
(\Omega_{n},\mathcal{F}_{n},\mathbb{F}_{n},\mathbb{P}_{n})
. To apply Slutsky’s theorem, we need to find a realization on a joint probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
. The latter is always possible as we may, e.g., consider the product space on which all
X^{x_{n}}
are independent. From now on, let us work with such a realization. Moreover, without loss of generality we may assume that
T<1
, as we may always consider
n>T
.

First, performing for every
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and
n\geq 1
a first-order Taylor expansion leads to

\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\big(f\big(X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}\big)-f(x_{n})\big)=\sqrt{\lambda(n)}f^{\prime}(\xi_{i,n})\big(X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big),

(2.21)

where the random variable
\xi_{i,n}
satisfies
|\xi_{i,n}-x_{n}|\leq\big|X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big|
pointwise. Hence, we can conclude
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f^{\prime}(\xi_{i,n})=f^{\prime}(\overline{x})
in
\mathbb{P}
-probability for every
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
by

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\big|X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big|^{p}\right]=0,

following from (
A.2
) for
p\geq 2
in combination with
g_{n}\equiv x_{n}
and
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
being convergent, Vitali’s convergence theorem, the continuous mapping theorem as well as the continuity of
f^{\prime}
. Finally, using representation (
2.21
), the desired weak convergence (
2.19
) is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.2
and an application of Slutsky’s theorem.

For the case
f^{\prime}(\overline{x})=0
, where
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}f^{\prime}(x_{n})=0
, we use a similar argument but now perform a second order Taylor expansion. Hence, by adjusting the order of the normalizing sequence, the analogue of (
2.21
) becomes in this case

\displaystyle\lambda(n)\big(f\big(X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}\big)-f(x_{n})\big)=

\displaystyle\lambda(n)\hskip 0.85355ptf^{\prime}(x_{n})\big(X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big)

(2.22)

\displaystyle+\lambda(n)\hskip 0.85355pt\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{i,n})}{2}\big(X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big)^{2},

where
|\xi_{i,n}-x_{n}|\leq\big|X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big|
holds again pointwise. Combining (
2.8
) from Theorem
2.2
with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}f^{\prime}(x_{n})=0
and Slutsky’s theorem shows that the vector of the first summands on the right-hand side of (
2.22
) converges weakly towards the zero vector and, therefore, also in
\mathbb{P}
-probability. Moreover, for the remainder, we can conclude from the continuous mapping theorem in combination with again (
2.8
),
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{i,n})=f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})
in
\mathbb{P}
-probability, following analogously to above, as well as Slutsky’s theorem that it converges in distribution to a random vector of the form

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})}{2}(Y_{t_{j}}^{\sigma,\infty})^{2}\right)_{j=1,\dots,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\,

\displaystyle\left(\tfrac{f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})}{2}\,\big\|\overline{K}\big\|_{L^{2}((0,t_{j}])}^{2}\,\sigma(\overline{x})^{2}\chi_{1,j}^{2}\right)_{j=1,\dots,N},

where each marginal
\chi_{1,j}^{2}
is
\chi^{2}
-distributed with one degree of freedom for every
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, and the dependence structure is inherited from
Y^{\sigma,\infty}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

It is easy to see that the tightness argument given in Corollary
2.3
also can be applied to (
2.18
) for Lipschitz continuous
C^{1}
-transformations of
X^{x_{n}}
. Thus, in combination with Corollary
2.4
, we obtain a functional generalization of (
2.19
) of the form

\left(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(f\big(X_{t/n}^{x_{n}}\big)-f(x_{n})\big)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}f^{\prime}(\overline{x})\hskip 0.85355pt\big(Y_{t}^{\sigma,\infty}\big)_{t\in[0,T]},\quad\mbox{as}\ \,n\rightarrow\infty.

Finally, let us briefly discuss an extension where
f
fulfills the required smoothness only locally at
x_{0}
, i.e. there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
f|_{B(x_{0},\varepsilon)}\in C^{i}\big(B(x_{0},\varepsilon);\mathbb{R}\big)
, where
i\in\{1,2\}
and
B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
denotes the open ball around
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
with radius
\varepsilon
. It is then straightforward to prove (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) also in this case for
x_{n}\equiv x_{0}
. First, we notice that (
2.19
) is equivalent to the corresponding result, if one replaces each
X^{x_{0}}
with an appropriately stopped version, i.e.

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(f\big(X_{(t_{j}/n)\wedge\tau}^{x_{0}}\big)-f(x_{0})\big)\right)_{j=1,\dots,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}f^{\prime}(x_{0})\hskip 0.85355pt\big(Y_{t_{j}}^{\sigma,\infty}\big)_{j=1,\dots,N},

(2.23)

where
\tau
is a weak stopping time which is almost surely strictly positive. Indeed, this is an immediate consequence of the triangle inequality as well as

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(f\big(X_{t_{1}/n}^{x_{0}}\big)-f(x_{0})\big),\dots,\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(f\big(X_{t_{N}/n}^{x_{0}}\big)-f(x_{0})\big)\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\Big[g\Big(\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(f\big(X_{(t_{1}/n)\wedge\tau}^{x_{0}}\big)-f(x_{0})\big),\dots,\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big(f\big(X_{(t_{N}/n)\wedge\tau}^{x_{0}}\big)-f(x_{0})\big)\Big)\Big]\bigg|=0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
for every
g\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R})
by the dominated convergence theorem. In particular,

\tau_{\varepsilon}^{0}:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:\hskip 2.84544pt|X_{t}^{x_{0}}-x_{0}|\geq\varepsilon/2\big\}\wedge T

is an admissible choice. As the associated stopped process is restricted to
B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
, we can perform a Taylor expansion and complete the proof analogously to above utilizing the continuity of the paths of
X_{\cdot\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}^{0}}^{x_{0}}
as well as (
2.23
) for
f=\id
according to Theorem
2.2
and the above equivalence. This also applies to the second part of the corollary, i.e. (
2.20
).

Note that this localization method may also be used for the case
x_{n}\not\equiv x_{0}
, if
f
has the required smoothness on
B(\overline{x},\varepsilon)
and the additional condition

\inf_{n\geq\tilde{n}}\tau_{\varepsilon}^{n}>0\ \ \text{a.s.},

holds, where
\tilde{n}:=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\hskip 1.42271pt|x_{m}-\overline{x}|<\varepsilon/2,\,\forall m\geq n\}
. Note that while this random time might, in general, not be a stopping time, it is still a weak stopping time. A crucial step for the argument is the moment estimate

\mathbb{E}\left[\big|X_{(t_{i}/n)\wedge\tau_{\varepsilon}^{n}}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big|^{p}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\big|X_{t_{i}/n}^{x_{n}}-x_{n}\big|^{p}\right]+\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{p}\,\mathbb{P}[t_{i}/n>\tau_{\varepsilon}^{n}]

converging to
0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
according to (
A.2
) for
g_{n}\equiv x_{n}
with
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
being convergent as well as the dominated convergence theorem in combination with
\inf_{n\geq\tilde{n}}\tau_{\varepsilon}^{n}>0
a.s.

2.3
Examples of kernels and limiting kernels

We conclude this section with a few examples of Volterra kernels covered by our results presented in Theorem
2.2
and the subsequent extensions. Firstly, the classical Riemann-Liouville kernel satisfies the assumptions of our theorem as illustrated in the next example.

Example 2.6
.

The Riemann-Liouville kernel
K(t)=\frac{t^{H-1/2}}{\Gamma(H+1/2)}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, gives
H=\gamma=\gamma_{*}
, the scaling is given by

\lambda(n)=\left(\int_{0}^{1/n}K(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{-1}=2H\,\Gamma(H+1/2)^{2}\,n^{2H},

and the limit kernel for the covariances in condition (ii) of Theorem
2.2
exists and is given by

\displaystyle\overline{K}(t)=\sqrt{2H}\hskip 0.85355ptt^{H-1/2},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(2.24)

as can be seen from the substitution
r\to r/n
combined with the scaling property of the kernel. Moreover, condition (
1.5
) holds with
\overline{\gamma}=\min\{H,1/2\}
by
[
3
, Example 2.3]
, whence the functional CLT from Corollary
2.3
holds for
0<H\leq 1/2
.

The next example illustrates that only the asymptotics of the kernel as
t\to 0
plays a role with regards to conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.2
.

Example 2.7
.

Suppose that there exists
C(H)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
such that

K(t)\sim C(H)\hskip 0.85355ptt^{H-1/2},\quad\ \mbox{as}\ t\to 0.

Then
\gamma=\gamma_{*}=H
,
\lambda(n)\sim 2H\hskip 0.85355ptC(H)^{-2}\hskip 0.85355ptn^{2H}
, and there exists a constant
C^{\prime}(H)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
such that
\overline{K}(t)=C^{\prime}(H)\hskip 0.85355ptt^{H-1/2}
, by the dominated convergence theorem. Therefore, conditions (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.2
are satisfied. Note that this example covers also regular kernels
K\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
K(0)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
by taking
H=1/2
. In particular, for all such regular kernels, we obtain
\overline{K}\equiv 1
and, hence, the limit process is even a time-homogeneous Markov process, which, in general, contrasts the original process. An important subclass of the above kernels has representation
K(t)=l(t)\hskip 0.85355ptt^{H-1/2}
, where
l:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz. These kernels also satisfy (
1.5
) with
\overline{\gamma}=\min\{H,1/2\}
(see
[
3
, Example 2.3 (iv)]
), whence for
H\leq 1/2
also the functional CLT from Corollary
2.3
is applicable.

The above example covers, in particular, the gamma kernel, the sum of exponentials and regularized fractional kernels, e.g. via a time shift. Below we close this section with an example of a
\log
-modulated Riemann-Liouville kernel.

Example 2.8
.

For the
\log
-modulated fractional kernel

K(t)=\frac{t^{H-1/2}}{\Gamma(H+1/2)}\log(1+1/t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(
1.4
) holds for
\gamma_{*}=H
and all
\gamma\in(0,H)
. Hence, condition (i) of Theorem
2.2
is satisfied as
\chi_{\sigma}>0
. Moreover, the scaling satisfies
\lambda(n)\sim\frac{2H\hskip 0.85355pt\Gamma(H+1/2)^{2}\hskip 0.85355ptn^{2H}}{\log^{2}(n)}
, as can be seen either by explicit integration or an application of Karamata’s theorem
[
9
, Proposition 1.5.10]
:

\int_{0}^{1/n}K(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{n}^{\infty}\frac{u^{-1-2H}}{\Gamma(H+1/2)^{2}}\log^{2}(1+u)\,\mathrm{d}u\sim\frac{1}{2H\hskip 0.85355pt\Gamma(H+1/2)^{2}}n^{-2H}\log^{2}(n).

A short computation shows that also condition (ii) in Theorem
2.2
holds with
\overline{K}
given by (
2.24
).
However, as
\gamma<H=\gamma_{*}
, we cannot infer a functional CLT via Corollary
2.3
.

Finally, let us remark that the limiting kernel
\overline{K}
, provided it exists, satisfies the identity

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\overline{K}(s)^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda(n)\int_{0}^{t/n}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{t/n}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s}{\int_{0}^{1/n}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{tK(t/n)^{2}}{K(1/n)^{2}}.

(2.25)

The latter could be used as an alternative to find
\overline{K}
without the use of integrals.
For instance, if
K
varies regularly at zero, i.e.
K(t)=\ell(t)\hskip 0.85355ptt^{H-1/2}

with
\ell
a slowly varying function, then (
2.25
) readily
implies
\overline{K}(t)=\sqrt{2H}\hskip 0.85355ptt^{H-1/2}
.

2.4
Implications for mathematical finance

The large deviations results mentioned in the introduction aim at asymptotics for out-of-the-money (OTM) vanilla or digital options in rough
volatility models.
For
at-the-money
(ATM) vanilla options, very general
results, way beyond rough volatility, have
been proven
[
4
,
25
]
.
Unlike OTM, ATM digital calls can have
a different asymptotic behavior than
ATM vanilla calls, which is amenable to CLTs.
Indeed, if
\sigma(\overline{x})\neq 0
in Theorem
2.2
, then we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{T/n}\geq x_{0}\}}]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,\big(X_{T/n}-x_{0}\big)\geq 0\right]=\frac{1}{2},

and if
X
models a financial asset, this could be read as a statement about the price
of ATM digital call options with maturities
T/n

(cf. Section 4 in
[
29
]
).
However, note that SVIE solutions are in general not semimartingales.
We refer to
[
6
,
32
]
and the references therein for no-arbitrage theory for non-semimartingale
models. Still, as no convenient option pricing theory is available so far,
the common way to use SVIEs in financial modeling is via stochastic
volatility models (see
[
5
]
). Consider a price process
S
satisfying
\mathrm{d}S_{t}/S_{t}=\sqrt{v_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{B}_{t}
,
where
v\geq 0
solves (
1.2
), and
\tilde{B}
is a Brownian motion
correlated with
B
. As long as we assume that
S
is a martingale under a risk-neutral measure, it does not matter whether the variance process
v
, which is not a tradable asset, is a semimartingale or not.

To invoke our functional CLT obtained in Corollary
2.3
, we
shift attention to the small-time behaviour of volatility derivatives, in particular options on the realized variance
v
(see
[
40
]
).
The payoff of such a contract
at time
T>0
is a function of the average variance over
[0,T]
, i.e. of

\mathcal{V}_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}v_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.26)

First, we study the small-time limit of an ATM digital call with underlying
\mathcal{V}
,
assuming zero interest rate. By Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem, we have
\mathcal{V}_{0}:=\lim_{T\searrow 0}\mathcal{V}_{T}=v_{0}
a.s. For maturity
1/n
, our claim has the payoff
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}\}}
, which implies for its price as
n\rightarrow\infty
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}\}}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,\big(\mathcal{V}_{1/n}-v_{0}\big)\geq 0\right]

(2.27)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[n\int_{0}^{1/n}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,(v_{t}-v_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,(v_{t/n}-v_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{P}\left[\int_{0}^{1}Y_{t}^{\sigma,\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0\right]=\frac{1}{2},

where the convergence follows from the functional CLT from Corollary
2.3
, the continuous mapping theorem and the integral being a continuous functional w.r.t. the uniform topology. Moreover, the latter integral defines a non-degenerate, centered Gaussian random variable by the stochastic Fubini theorem and
\overline{K}\not\equiv 0
, whence the
limit price is again
\tfrac{1}{2}
. For the relevance of higher
order terms beyond the limit price, which we do not consider in this paper,
we refer to results on the implied volatility skew in
[
28
,
29
,
38
]
.

Moreover, we can investigate the regime called “almost ATM” (AATM)
as discussed in
[
22
]
, and its boundary case. For this purpose, consider again a digital call on
\mathcal{V}_{1/n}
with strike
v_{0}+n^{-\beta}a
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
. We can compute similarly to above

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}+n^{-\beta}a\}}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[n\int_{0}^{1/n}\,(v_{t}-v_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq n^{-\beta}a\right]

(2.28)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,(v_{t/n}-v_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355ptn^{-\beta}a\right].

Depending on
\alpha
and
\beta
, the limit can now be calculated.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
v\geq 0
be a solution to (
1.2
) with
\sigma(v_{0})>0
in the framework of Theorem
2.2
, assuming additionally that
K
satisfies (
1.5
) and
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma_{*}
. Then the small-time limits of digital call option prices with underlying
\mathcal{V}
and strike
v_{0}+n^{-\beta}a
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0

are given by:

(i)

a=0
:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}\}}\right]=\frac{1}{2},

(2.29)

(ii)

a\neq 0
,
\beta>\gamma_{*}
:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}+n^{-\beta}a\}}\right]=\frac{1}{2},

(2.30)

(iii)

If
a\neq 0
,
\beta=\gamma_{*}
and
\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\sim C_{\lambda}n^{\gamma_{*}}
for some
C_{\lambda}>0
, then the limit can be expressed
in terms of the cdf of the standard normal distribution:

\hskip-28.45274pt\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}+n^{-\gamma_{*}}a\}}\right]=1-\Phi\left(\frac{\sigma(v_{0})^{-1}C_{\lambda}a}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{1}\big(\int_{0}^{s}\overline{K}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s}}\right).

(2.31)

Proof.

First, notice that the assumptions directly imply that the functional CLT from Corollary
2.3
holds for
v
. Moreover, we recall
\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}}
.

(i)
: This corresponds to the ATM case studied above. Therefore, the
limit price is
1/2
by (
2.27
).

(ii)
: This case is very similar to the AATM case in
[
22
]
. Combining (
2.28
) with
\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}}
,
\beta>\gamma_{*}
and Slutsky’s theorem shows that the limit price is also
1/2
here.

(iii)
: Here the statement follows from
\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\sim C_{\lambda}n^{\gamma_{*}}
, taking the limit in (
2.28
), our functional CLT, which is again preserved under the continuous integral operator, and Slutsky’s theorem, where
\mathrm{sd}
denotes the standard deviation:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\,(v_{t/n}-v_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t\geq\sqrt{\lambda(n)}\hskip 0.85355ptn^{-\gamma_{*}}a\right]

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\int_{0}^{1}Y_{t}^{\sigma,\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t\geq C_{\lambda}a\right]=1-\Phi\left(\frac{C_{\lambda}a}{\mathrm{sd}\left[\int_{0}^{1}Y_{t}^{\sigma,\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]}\right).

Finally, we obtain from the stochastic Fubini theorem

\displaystyle\mathrm{sd}\left[\int_{0}^{1}Y_{t}^{\sigma,\infty}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma(v_{0})\,\mathrm{sd}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}\overline{K}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\sigma(v_{0})\,\sqrt{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{s}^{1}\overline{K}(t-s)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s}=\sigma(v_{0})\hskip 0.85355pt\sqrt{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\overline{K}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s},

which proves (
2.31
).
∎

Part (iii) is the boundary case where the regime switch from AATM into “moderately out of the money” (MOTM, see
[
22
]
) occurs for
a>0
. Here the limit price is in general not
1/2
anymore, as (
2.31
) shows. In the following, we investigate this phenomenon for Riemann-Liouville kernels
K_{H}(t)=t^{H-1/2}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Example 2.10
.

In the Riemann-Liouville case, we have by Example
2.6
the limiting kernel
\overline{K}_{H}=\sqrt{2H}K_{H}
as well as
C_{\lambda}=\sqrt{2H}
, and Corollary
2.3
is applicable for
\gamma_{*}=H\in(0,1/2]
. Hence, we obtain from (
2.31
) the asymptotic price

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{V}_{1/n}\geq v_{0}+n^{-H}a\}}\big]=1-\Phi\left(\sqrt{2H+2}\left(H+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\frac{a}{\sigma(v_{0})}\right),

(2.32)

which, depending on
a\in\mathbb{R}
, may attain any value in
(0,1)
. In particular, (
2.32
) holds in the rough Heston model, see (
1.1
). Moreover, it is an immediate consequence of Example
2.7

that (
2.32
) persists for the gamma kernel
K_{\beta,H}(t)=t^{H-1/2}e^{-\beta t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, with
H\in(0,1/2]
and
\beta>0
, since
\overline{K}_{\beta,H}=\overline{K}_{H}
and the same holds true for
C_{\lambda}
and
\gamma_{*}
.

We now outline a potential application of (
2.32
).
Given a collection of prices of digital calls on
\mathcal{V}
with sufficiently small time to maturity and strikes close to ATM, which is natural for these maturities (see
[
22
]
), the above result may be used for calibrating the parameter
H
. Select
N\in\mathbb{N}
and choose a collection of points
(H_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,N\}}
in
(0,1/2]
that may be equidistant. Now given a collection of
M
digital calls on the average realized variance
\mathcal{V}
with time to maturities, strikes and prices
(T_{i},K_{i},\pi_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,M\}}
with
T_{i}<\delta
and
|K_{i}-v_{0}|<\Delta
, where
\delta,\Delta>0
are tuneable hyperparameters, we can analyze the loss function, specialized to the Riemann-Liouville and gamma case, i.e.

L(H)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left|1-\Phi\left(\sqrt{2H+2}\left(H+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\frac{a(H,i)}{\sigma(v_{0})}\right)-\pi_{i}\right|^{2},\quad H\in(0,1/2],

where we defined
a(H,i):=n_{i}^{H}(K_{i}-v_{0})
with
n_{i}:=\lfloor T_{i}^{-1}\rfloor
. An estimator
\widehat{H}
may now be obtained by determining

\widehat{H}:=\argmin_{H_{i},\,i\in\{1,\dots,N\}}L(H_{i}).

Note that
a(H,i)
should stay bounded for shrinking maturities, in order to apply
Proposition
2.9
(iii), but as
H
is small
in practice and by potentially modeling
\Delta
also as a decaying function as
T_{i}\searrow 0
, we hope that the factor
n_{i}^{H}
in the definition of
a(H,i)

will not be an obstacle in a numerical implementation.
The approach is robust in the sense that little information on the dynamics of
v
needs to be specified; on the other hand, data availability is an issue, as these digital options are part of the
OTC (over-the-counter) market, and are thus usually not liquidly traded on exchanges.

Finally, note that considering strikes of the form
v_{0}+n^{-\beta}a
with
a>0
and
\beta\in[0,\gamma_{*})
leads to conceptually very different large and moderate deviations
regimes; see
[
12
,
40
]
and the references therein.

3
Hilbert space valued Markovian lifts of SVIEs

3.1
Hilbert spaces induced by completely monotone kernels

Let us consider, for a given function
g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and coefficients
b,\sigma:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
that are at least measurable, the stochastic Volterra equation

\displaystyle X_{t}=g(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)\sigma(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(3.1)

where it is implicitly assumed that both integrals are well-defined. Weak existence of solutions of such equations under suitable assumptions on the kernel
K
follows from
[
44
]
for regular
g
and Hölder continuous
b,\sigma
, while for
g
that may have a singularity at
t=0
we refer to
[
8
]
for the Lipschitz case, and
[
10
]
for
b,\sigma
being merely continuous with linear growth. In this section, we modify the Hilbert space-valued Markovian lift from
[
34
]
; see also
[
21
]
, where a similar construction was used. Here and below we suppose that the Volterra kernel, which may be singular in
0
(in which case it is still well-defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
in a
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}
-sense, see Lemma
3.3
), satisfies the following assumption:

(A)

The Volterra kernel
K:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is completely monotone and has representation
K(t)=K(\infty)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-xt}\mu(\mathrm{d}x)
, where
\mu
is a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\mu(\{0\})=0
and

\eta_{*}=\inf\left\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}\ :\ \int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty\right\}\in[-\infty,1/2).

Note that, by assumption,
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{\eta}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
holds for each
\eta<-\eta_{*}
. Given
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
the weighted Hilbert space of equivalence classes of functions
y:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
equipped with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle y,\widetilde{y}\rangle_{\eta}=y(0)\hskip 0.56917pt\widetilde{y}(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}y(x)\hskip 0.56917pt\widetilde{y}(x)\hskip 0.56917pt(1+x)^{\eta}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

Then it follows that
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\eta^{\prime}}
for
\eta^{\prime}<\eta
, and it is easy to see that the operator
\Xi:\mathcal{H}_{\eta}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\Xi y=y(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}y(x)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

(3.2)

is a continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
whenever
\eta>\eta_{*}
. This functional has representation
\Xi y=\langle y,w_{\eta}\rangle_{\eta}
with
w_{\eta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
given by
w_{\eta}(x)=(1+x)^{-\eta}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Besides the measure
\mu
, we will frequently work with the augmented measure
\overline{\mu}:=\delta_{0}+\mu
where
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac measure concentrated in
\{0\}
. The later allows us to express the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\eta}
and the action of the projection operator
\Xi
in the convenient form

\langle y,\widetilde{y}\rangle_{\eta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}y(x)\widetilde{y}(x)(1+x)^{\eta}\,\overline{\mu}(\mathrm{d}x)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Xi y=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}y(x)\,\overline{\mu}(\mathrm{d}x).

In the original formulation
[
34
]
, the Markovian lift was constructed with respect to the Bernstein measure
\overline{\mu}_{K}:=K(\infty)\hskip 0.56917pt\delta_{0}+\mu
of
K
under the assumption
\eta_{*}\leq 1/2
. Our construction based on
\overline{\mu}
allows us to capture time-invariant initial curves in the associated SVIE (
3.1
), i.e.
g\equiv x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, even if
K(\infty)=0
. Furthermore, our stronger assumption
\eta_{*}<1/2
allows us to construct a continuous Markovian lift in the domain of the projection operator
\Xi
, see Theorem
3.5
below.

Let
S(t)y(x)=e^{-tx}y(x)
. Then
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
defines a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, and if
\eta^{\prime}<\eta
holds, then, using the inequality
2
2

2

The function
f(x)=(1+x)^{\eta-\eta^{\prime}}e^{-2xt}
attains its maximum at
x^{*}=(\eta-\eta^{\prime})/(2t)-1
. Consider first
t\geq(\eta-\eta^{\prime})/2
, then
x^{*}\leq 0
, and monotonicity yields
f(x)\leq f(0)=1
for
x\geq 0
. For
t\leq(\eta-\eta^{\prime})/2
we obtain
f(x)\leq f(x^{*})\leq\kappa^{2}(\eta-\eta^{\prime})t^{-(\eta-\eta^{\prime})}
.

(1+x)^{\eta-\eta^{\prime}}e^{-2xt}\leq\kappa^{2}(\eta-\eta^{\prime})\big(1+t^{-(\eta-\eta^{\prime})}\big),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+},t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*},

with
\kappa(\delta)=\max\{1,2^{-\delta/2}\delta^{\delta/2}\}
in combination with
\sqrt{\cdot}
being subadditive, we obtain
S(t)\in L(\mathcal{H}_{\eta^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}_{\eta})
, where for every
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
we obtain the bound

\|S(t)\|_{L(\mathcal{H}_{\eta^{\prime}},\mathcal{H}_{\eta})}\leq C_{T}\hskip 0.85355pt\kappa(\eta-\eta^{\prime})\hskip 0.85355ptt^{-(\eta-\eta^{\prime})/2}\lesssim t^{-(\eta-\eta^{\prime})/2},\quad\ \forall t\in(0,T].

(3.3)

Since in this work we exclusively apply the above estimates to bounded time intervals, we usually use the right-hand side of (
3.3
) directly, thereby dropping the
T
-dependency of the constant for notational convenience.

The next proposition summarizes the properties of the composed operator
\Xi S(t)
, which will allow us to relate the Markovian lift with the original stochastic Volterra process.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
with
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
g(t)=\Xi S(t)y
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
. If
\eta>\eta_{*}
, then
g
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, while for
\eta\leq\eta_{*}
we find for every
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
:

|g(t)|\leq C_{T}\,\kappa(\eta_{*}+\varepsilon-\eta)\|w_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}\|_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}\,t^{-(\eta_{*}+\varepsilon-\eta)/2},\quad\ \forall t\in(0,T],

and each
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, if
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta_{*}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
holds, then we may even take
\varepsilon=0
.

Proof.

Firstly, by dominated convergence, it is clear that
g
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
. If
\eta>\eta_{*}
holds, then combining the representation
\Xi=\langle\cdot,w_{\eta}\rangle_{\eta}
with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

|g(t)|\leq\|w_{\eta}\|_{\eta}\|S(t)y\|_{\eta}\leq\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}\|y\|_{\eta},

which shows that
g
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For the case
\eta\leq\eta_{*}
, we may use (
3.3
) with
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary, to find

\displaystyle|g(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}\|_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}\|S(t)y\|_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\,\kappa(\eta_{*}+\varepsilon-\eta)\left(1+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}\|y\|_{\eta}\,t^{-(\eta_{*}+\varepsilon-\eta)/2},

which proves the second claim. Finally, if
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta_{*}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
, then letting
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, while utilizing
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\kappa(\delta)=1
for the case
\eta=\eta_{*}
, implies the last assertion.
∎

In terms of the operator
\Xi S(\cdot)
, the Volterra kernel
K
has the representation

\displaystyle K(t)=\Xi S(t)\xi_{K}\ \text{ with }\ \xi_{K}(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{(0,\infty)}(x)+K(\infty)\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(x).

(3.4)

In particular, assumption (A) gives
\xi_{K}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
for each
\eta<-\eta_{*}
. Moreover, if
\eta_{*}<0
, then we may choose
\eta\in(\eta_{*},-\eta_{*})
, and hence
K=\Xi S(\cdot)\xi_{K}
is bounded by Proposition
3.2
. On the other hand, for
0\leq\eta_{*}<\frac{1}{2}
, it follows that for each
\eta<-\eta_{*}<\eta_{*}
, the kernel
K
satisfies the pointwise bound
K(t)\lesssim t^{-(\eta_{*}+\varepsilon-\eta)/2}
for all
t\in(0,T]
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
with the same constant as given in Proposition
3.2
. Letting
\eta=-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
yields

\displaystyle K(t)\lesssim\|w_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}\|_{\eta_{*}+\varepsilon}\,t^{-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon}\lesssim t^{-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon},\quad\ \forall t\in(0,T].

(3.5)

In particular, since
\eta_{*}<1/2
, we may always find
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that
\eta_{*}+\varepsilon<1/2
and hence
K\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. The next lemma summarizes further useful properties of the Volterra kernel.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that condition (A) holds for a Borel measure
\mu
with
\eta_{*}<1/2
. Then the associated Volterra kernel, assuming
K\not\equiv 0
, satisfies for every
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
,
h\in(0,T]
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1-2\eta_{*})
:

\displaystyle C\big(\mu((0,1/h])\vee 1\big)^{2}h\leq\int_{0}^{h}K(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\overline{C}\cdot\begin{cases}h,&\eta_{*}<0\\
h^{1-2\eta_{*}-\varepsilon},&0\leq\eta_{*}<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Moreover, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|K(h+r)-K(r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\widetilde{C}\cdot\begin{cases}h,&\eta_{*}<0\\
h^{1-2\eta_{*}-\varepsilon},&0\leq\eta_{*}<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

where the constants
C,\overline{C},\widetilde{C}>0
may depend on
\varepsilon
for
\eta_{*}>-1
. Again, taking
\varepsilon=0
is admissible, if
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta_{*}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty
.

Since the proof of this lemma is quite technical and does not provide additional insights for the study of small-time central limit theorems, it is given in Appendix
C
of this work. As a consequence of Lemma
3.3
, let us note that a completely monotone kernel
K\not\equiv 0
that satisfies condition (A), automatically fulfils (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) on every time interval
[0,T]
with
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
.

In the case
\eta_{*}<0
,
\mu
is a finite measure and hence the order of the lower bound in Lemma
3.3
is sharp. However, sharp upper and lower bounds are more delicate when
\eta_{*}\in[0,1/2)
. Ideally, a lower bound in Lemma
3.3
should take the form
\mu((0,1/h])\gtrsim h^{-\eta_{*}}
. This combined with the other two bounds holding for
\varepsilon=0
would be convenient for obtaining an fCLT (cf. Corollary
2.3
), and holds for many examples of interest (see
Subsection
2.3
).
Unfortunately, it turns out that, in general, this does not need to be the case, as illustrated in the following example.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\eta_{*}\in(0,1/2)
be arbitrary and
\beta\in(0,\eta_{*})
. Then there exists a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
that satisfies condition (A) with
\eta_{*}
for which one has

\liminf_{x\to\infty}x^{-\beta^{\prime}}\mu((0,x])=0,\qquad\forall\beta^{\prime}\in(\beta,\eta_{*}).

Indeed, fix
\beta\in(0,\eta_{*})
and define
x_{k}:=\exp\big((2\eta_{*}/\beta)^{k}\big)
for
k\geq 0
. Let
\mu_{F}
be a Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined via its distribution function
F(x)=\mu_{F}([0,x])
given by

F(x):=\begin{cases}0,&0\leq x<1,\\
x_{k}^{\eta_{*}},&x_{k}\leq x<x_{k}^{\eta_{*}/\beta},\ k\in\mathbb{N},\\
x^{\eta_{*}},&x_{k}^{\eta_{*}/\beta}\leq x<x_{k+1},\ k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.6)

Then
F(x)\leq x^{\eta_{*}}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and, using
F|_{[0,1)}\equiv 0
combined with the monotonicity of
F
, it follows that
\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}F(x)<\infty
holds for every
\varepsilon>0
. On the other hand, we can estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{-\eta_{*}}\,\mathrm{d}F(x)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{x_{k}^{\eta_{*}/\beta}}^{x_{k+1}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}=\frac{\eta_{*}}{\beta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{2\eta_{*}}{\beta}\Big)^{k}=\infty.

Therefore, the Borel measure
\mu_{F}
as well as the associated completely monotone kernel induced by
F
satisfy condition (A) with
\eta_{*}
, since
\supp(\mu_{F})\subseteq[1,\infty)
and
(1+x^{-1})^{-\eta}\in[2^{-\eta},1]
for every
x\geq 1
and
\eta>0
. Finally, it is an immediate consequence of (
3.6
) that bounding
\mu_{F}([0,x])=F(x)
below by a power function with order
\beta^{\prime}>\beta
is impossible since for
x=x_{k}^{\eta_{*}/\beta}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows
F(x-)=x^{\beta}
, while
F
is constant in a left neighborhood of
x
.

For positive results under further assumptions on
\mu
, we refer to
[
9
]
and
[
49
, p. 112]
.
For instance, it follows from Karamata’s Tauberian theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 1.7.1]
), that

K(t)\sim C\hskip 0.56917ptt^{\alpha-1}\hskip 0.56917pt\ell(t^{-1}),\qquad t\to 0,

for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
and a slowly varying function
\ell
, is equivalent to

\mu([0,x])\sim\frac{C}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\hskip 0.56917ptx^{1-\alpha}\hskip 0.56917pt\ell(x),\qquad x\to\infty.

As a special case, this contains the rough versions, i.e.
H\in(0,1/2)
, of the Riemann-Liouville kernel, where
\eta_{*}=1/2-H
and all three bounds in Lemma
3.3
have the same order (see also Example
3.6
(a) below), and the
\log
-modulated kernel (see Example
2.8
).

3.2
The Markovian lift

Let us introduce the class of admissible functions
g
appearing in (
3.1
) as the image under the operator
\Xi S(\cdot)
, i.e., for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
we let

\mathcal{G}_{\eta}=\left\{g:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\ :\ \exists\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}\ \text{ s.t. }g(\cdot)=\Xi S(\cdot)\xi\right\}.

By Proposition
3.2
, each
g\in\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
is smooth with possibly a singularity in
t=0
. Moreover, Proposition
3.2
implies that
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}\subset L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
p=\infty
when
\eta>\eta_{*}
, and
1\leq p<\frac{2}{\eta_{*}-\eta}
, if
\eta_{*}-2<\eta<\eta_{*}
. For given
g=\Xi S(\cdot)\xi_{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
with
\eta
fixed, let us consider the stochastic Volterra equation (
3.1
). The corresponding Markovian lift
(\mathcal{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is obtained by imposing the requirement that
\Xi\mathcal{X}_{t}=X_{t}
. Since
K(t)=\Xi S(t)\xi_{K}
, and formally interchanging the operator
\Xi
with both integrals, we necessarily arrive at the representation

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{t}=S(t)\xi_{g}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\xi_{K}b(\Xi\mathcal{X}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\xi_{K}\sigma(\Xi\mathcal{X}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(3.7)

Note that this equation is a mild formulation of a stochastic evolution equation (SEE) on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
with the generator determined by the semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
(cf.
[
34
, Eq. (2.7)]
).
We now state an existence result for (
3.1
) and its Markovian lift that is sufficient for our purposes.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that condition (A) is satisfied with
\eta_{*}<1/2
, and that
b,\sigma
are continuous with linear growth. Then for each
g=\Xi S(\cdot)\xi_{g}\in\mathcal{G}_{\eta_{g}}
with
\eta_{g}>\eta_{*}
, there exists a continuous weak solution of (
3.1
). Moreover, there also exists a weak solution
\mathcal{X}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{\eta}))
of (
3.7
) with
\eta\in(\eta_{*},1-\eta_{*})
such that
\Xi\mathcal{X}=X
holds on
[0,T]
for arbitrary
T>0
.

Proof.

Firstly, it follows from Proposition
3.2
that
g
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and smooth on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{*}
. Then, Lemma
3.3
implies that
[
10
, Theorem 2.6]
is applicable, which yields the weak existence of a continuous weak solution
X
of (
3.1
). Since both
b
and
\sigma
are continuous and of linear growth, an application of Lemma
B.1
gives the existence of a weak solution of (
3.7
) with
\mathcal{X}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{\eta}))
and
X=\Xi\mathcal{X}
on
[0,T]
. Since
T>0
was arbitrary, the assertion is proved.
∎

Note that, if uniqueness in law holds for (
3.7
), then under the conditions of the above theorem, (
3.7
) determines a Markov process, which justifies the notion of Markovian lift. For further details in this direction we refer to the second part of
[
33
, Lemma 4.3]
for a general proof concept under weak uniqueness and
[
34
, Section 2]
where, in particular, the
C_{b}
-Feller property was shown for Lipschitz continuous coefficients.

Let us close this section with a few examples of popular Volterra kernels
K
that admit an explicit formula for
\mu
and the Bernstein measure
\mu_{K}
.

Example 3.6
.

(a)

Suppose that
K(t)=\frac{t^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta t}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
with
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
and
\beta\geq 0
, which covers both Riemann-Liouville and gamma kernels with
\alpha=H+1/2
. Then it follows that
K(\infty)=0
and
\eta_{*}=1-\alpha\in(0,1/2)
with

\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\frac{(x-\beta)^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}\mathbbm{1}_{(\beta,\infty)}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(b)

Let
K(t)=\log(1+1/t)
, then
K(\infty)=0
and
\eta_{*}=0
with

\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(c)

Let
K(t)=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}t}
with
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{N}\geq 0
,
N\geq 1
, and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}>0
. Then
\eta_{*}=-\infty
,
K(\infty)=c_{0}
and
\mu
is given by

\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\hskip 0.56917pt\delta_{\lambda_{i}}(\mathrm{d}x),

where
\delta_{w}
denotes the Dirac measure concentrated in
\{w\}
.

(d)

For every
K
satisfying condition (A) and
\varepsilon>0
, the shifted kernel defined via
K_{\varepsilon}:=K(\cdot+\varepsilon)
fulfills again condition (A) with
\eta_{*}=-\infty
,
K_{\varepsilon}(\infty)=K(\infty)
, and
\mu_{\varepsilon}\ll\mu
is given by

\mu_{\varepsilon}(\mathrm{d}x)=e^{-\varepsilon x}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x).

4
Small-time CLTs for the Markovian lift

4.1
A CLT for the finite-dimensional distributions

In this section we prove a small-time central limit theorem for the finite-dimensional projections of the Markovian lift based on continuous linear functionals, which may be written as
\langle\cdot,y\rangle_{\eta}
for some
y\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
by the Riesz representation theorem. Thus, as a preliminary step, let us first recall an auxiliary result stating that the Brownian integral processes on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
that we are going to encounter in our arguments (cf. (
4.10
) and (
4.12
)) are indeed Gaussian.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that condition (A) is satisfied and let
K
have representation (
3.4
). Then

I(t)=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\xi_{K}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

defines a continuous Gaussian process on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
with
\eta<1-\eta_{*}
. In particular, considering a collection of positive time points
(t_{j})_{j\in\{1,\dots,N\}}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
, then the
N
-dimensional random vector
(\langle I(t_{j}),y_{j}\rangle_{\eta})_{j=1,\dots,N}
is Gaussian with mean zero and covariance structure

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\langle I(t_{j}),y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\hskip 0.56917pt\langle I(t_{k}),y_{k}\rangle_{\eta}\right]=\int_{0}^{t_{j}\wedge t_{k}}\langle S(t_{j}-r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\hskip 0.56917pt\langle S(t_{k}-r)\xi_{K},y_{k}\rangle_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Proof.

Firstly, since
\xi_{K}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta^{\prime}}
for
\eta^{\prime}<-\eta_{*}
, it follows by standard integration theory that
I
is a Gaussian process on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta^{\prime}}
, see
[
15
, Theorem 5.2]
. Lemma
B.1
then implies
I\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{\eta}))
for each
T>0
as the solution to the associated SVIE is uniquely given by
K\ast\mathrm{d}B
. In particular, the
N
-dimensional random vector
(\langle I(t_{j}),y_{j}\rangle_{\eta})_{j\in\{1,\dots,N\}}
is Gaussian with mean zero and the stated covariance structure.
∎

The following is our first main result on the small-time central limit theorem for finite-dimensional distributions of the Markovian lift.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that condition (A) is satisfied, and let
b\in C^{\chi_{b}}(\mathbb{R})
and
\sigma\in C^{\chi_{\sigma}}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\chi_{b},\chi_{\sigma}\in(0,1]
. Let
K
have representation (
3.4
) and let
\mathcal{X}
be any continuous weak solution of (
3.7
) on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{\mathcal{X}}}
with
\eta_{\mathcal{X}}\in(\eta_{*},1-\eta_{*})
and
g=\Xi S(t)\xi_{g}
, where
\xi_{g}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{g}}
for some
\eta_{g}>\eta_{*}
, and define
\chi_{g}:=(\eta_{g}-\eta_{*})/2
. Fix
N\geq 1
, and let
y_{1},\dots,y_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}\setminus\{0\}
with
\eta\leq\eta_{\mathcal{X}}
and
0<t_{1}\leq\dots\leq t_{N}
satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

There exist

such that

\lambda_{i}(n):=\left(\int_{0}^{1/n}\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{-1}

satisfies
\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\leq Cn^{\gamma_{*}}
for all

, and

\gamma_{*}<\frac{\min\{1,1-\eta-\eta_{*}\}}{2}+\min\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\eta_{*}^{+}\right),\chi_{g}\right\}\chi_{\sigma}.

(4.1)

(ii)

There exists a symmetric, positive semi-definite
N\times N
-matrix
\Sigma
such that for all
i,j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with
i\leq j
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)\hskip 0.56917pt\lambda_{j}(n)}\int_{0}^{t_{i}/n}\langle S((t_{j}-t_{i})/n+r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\,\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}r=\Sigma_{ij}.

Then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\left(\langle\mathcal{X}_{t_{i}/n}-S(t_{i}/n)\xi_{g},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}\right)\right)_{i=1,\dots,N}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\big(0,\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma\big).

(4.2)

Proof.

First, recall that we have
\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{\mathcal{X}}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
by
\eta\leq\eta_{\mathcal{X}}
, whence
\mathcal{X}
is, in particular,
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
-valued. Analogously to (
2.9
) in Theorem

, let us write
\mathcal{X}_{t}=S(t)\xi_{g}+\mathcal{Y}_{t}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}
, where
\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{b}+\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{\sigma}
denotes the Gaussian part, and
\mathcal{Z}_{t}=\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{b}+\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\sigma}
the remainder, given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{b}_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\hskip 0.56917pt\xi_{K}\hskip 0.56917ptb(\Xi\xi_{g})\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{b}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\hskip 0.56917pt\xi_{K}\hskip 0.56917pt(b(\Xi\mathcal{X}_{s})-b(\Xi\xi_{g}))\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\hskip 0.56917pt\xi_{K}\hskip 0.56917pt\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\hskip 0.56917pt\xi_{K}\hskip 0.56917pt(\sigma(\Xi\mathcal{X}_{s})-\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g}))\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}.

Step 1.
Proceeding similarly to the finite-dimensional case from Section
2
, let us first bound the moments of
\mathcal{Z}_{t}
. For this purpose, note that by the commutativity of the continuous linear functional
\Xi
, following from
\eta_{\mathcal{X}}>\eta_{*}
, with
\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{\mathcal{X}}}
-valued Bochner and stochastic integrals and the special form of
g
, it follows that
X:=\Xi\mathcal{X}
defines a continuous solution to (
3.1
). Moreover, by (
3.5
) combined with
\eta_{*}<\frac{1}{2}
, we find
\varepsilon>0
small enough such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\lesssim t^{2\gamma}\ \text{ with }\ 0<\gamma=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon,&\eta_{*}\in[0,1/2)\\
\frac{1}{2},&\eta_{*}<0.\end{cases}

(4.3)

Hence, combining this with the boundedness of
g
due to
\eta_{g}>\eta_{*}
and Proposition
3.2
shows that Lemma
A.1
is applicable. Furthermore, we obtain for
\overline{\chi}_{g}:=\min\{\chi_{g},1/2\}>0
with
\chi_{g}
introduced above and
\varepsilon_{g}\in(0,\overline{\chi}_{g})
for each
s\geq 0
the bound

\displaystyle|g(s)-g(0)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1-e^{-xs})|\xi_{g}(x)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

\displaystyle=s^{\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}x^{\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}}|\xi_{g}(x)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq s^{\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}}\|\xi_{g}\|_{\eta_{g}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{-\eta_{*}-2\varepsilon_{g}}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)\right)^{1/2}\lesssim s^{\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}},

where we have used
\overline{\chi}_{g}\leq 1/2
, the Hölder continuity of
e^{-(\cdot)}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\overline{\chi}_{g}\leq(\eta_{g}-\eta_{*})/2
and finally Hölder’s inequality. Clearly, the factors independent of
s
are bounded due to
\xi_{g}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{g}}
and the definition of
\eta_{*}
. Moreover, since
\eta<1-\eta_{*}
, we find
\delta\in(0,1)

\delta<1\wedge(1-\eta_{*}-\eta)

\eta^{\prime}=\eta-(1-\delta)
. Then
\eta^{\prime}<-\eta_{*}
and hence
\xi_{K}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta^{\prime}}
. Therefore, (

) applied to

\displaystyle\|S(r)\xi_{K}\|_{\eta}\lesssim r^{-\frac{1-\delta}{2}},\qquad\forall r\in(0,t],

(4.5)

which lies in
L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, since
\delta\in(0,1)
. Hence, using the Jensen inequality combined with (
4.5
), the Hölder continuity of

and
\sigma
,
\Xi\xi_{g}=g(0)
, (
A.2
) from Lemma

and the Hölder-type estimate (

), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{Z}_{t}\|_{\eta}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{Z}^{b}_{t}\|_{\eta}^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{Z}_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{\eta}^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|S(r)\xi_{K}\|_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|S(t-s)\xi_{K}\|_{\eta}\hskip 1.13791pt\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{s}-g(0)|^{p\chi_{b}}\big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|S(r)\xi_{K}\|_{\eta}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}\|S(t-s)\xi_{K}\|_{\eta}^{2}\hskip 1.13791pt\mathbb{E}\big[|X_{s}-g(0)|^{p\chi_{\sigma}}\big]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{(p-1)\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\left(s^{p\chi_{b}\gamma}+s^{p\chi_{b}(\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g})}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+t^{(p/2-1)\delta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-(1-\delta)}\left(s^{p\chi_{\sigma}\gamma}+s^{p\chi_{\sigma}(\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g})}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{p\left(\frac{1+\delta}{2}+\chi_{b}\min\{\gamma,\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}\}\right)}+t^{p\left(\frac{\delta}{2}+\chi_{\sigma}\min\{\gamma,\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}\}\right)},

where
p>\max\{2/\chi_{b},2/\chi_{\sigma}\}
. Using this estimate, let us show that it suffices to study the convergence of the Gaussian part
\mathcal{Y}
. First, define
\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}:=\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,N\}}\|\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n}\|_{\eta}
. Then, using the definitions of
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{X}_{t_{i}/n}-S(t_{i}/n)\xi_{g},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}+\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

(4.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 12.80365pt+\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}+\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}>z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

where
(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is any point of continuity of the distribution function of
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma)

, where the exponent satisfies

q\in\left(\gamma_{*},\ \frac{\delta}{2}+\min\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\eta_{*}^{+}-\varepsilon\right),\overline{\chi}_{g}-\varepsilon_{g}\right\}\chi_{\sigma}\right).

(4.7)

given

, the function

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}>z(t_{N}/n)\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n}\|_{\eta}^{p}\big]}{z(t_{N}/n)^{p}}

where we have utilized

following from (

),
\chi_{\sigma}\leq 1
,
\gamma\leq 1/2
and
\overline{\chi}_{g}\leq 1/2
. Thus, it remains to study the convergence of the first probability in (

).

Step 2.
In this step we prove an asymptotic upper bound for (

). Using on

|\langle\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}|\leq z(t_{N}/n)\hskip 1.42271pt\|y_{i}\|_{\eta}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}+\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big(\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}-z(t_{N}/n)\|y_{i}\|_{\eta}\big)\leq a_{i}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big(\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{\sigma},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}+\mu_{i}(n)\big)\leq a_{i}\right\}\right],

(4.9)

where we have set

for every

. Note that, by Lemma

,

\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big(\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{\sigma},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}+\mu_{i}(n)\big),\hskip 5.69046pti\in\{1,\dots,N\}\right)^{\intercal}

(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})^{\intercal}
.
Therefore, by Lévy’s continuity theorem and the continuity of the characteristic function of the multivariate Gaussian distribution in its parameters, holding even in the degenerate case, it is sufficient to study the convergence of the mean and covariance matrix. For the mean, we estimate for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\,|\mu_{i}(n)|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\,\left(\|\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{b}\|_{\eta}+z(t_{N}/n)\right)\,\|y_{i}\|_{\eta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\,\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{t_{i}}{n}}\big\|S\big(\tfrac{t_{i}}{n}-s\big)\xi_{K}\big\|_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}s+z(t_{N}/n)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}+n^{\gamma_{*}-q},

where we have used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the estimate from (
4.5
),
\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}}
by condition (i) and
z(t_{N}/n)=(t_{N}/n)^{q}
. Due to condition (i) and the choice of
\delta
and
q
, the right-hand side converges to zero as
n\to\infty
. For the covariance matrix we observe for every
i,j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
with
i\leq j
by an application of condition (ii):

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\,\mathrm{cov}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{\sigma},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta},\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(n)}\hskip 0.85355pt\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{j}/n}^{\sigma},y_{j}\big\rangle_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)\hskip 0.85355pt\lambda_{j}(n)}\int_{0}^{t_{i}/n}\langle S((t_{j}-t_{i})/n+r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\,\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma_{ij}.

This proves the weak convergence of (
4.10
) towards
\mathcal{N}\big(0,\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma\big)
. Since
(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})^{\intercal}
is a continuity point of the distribution function of the latter, using the upper bound
in (
4.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}+\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{N}),

where
\Phi_{\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma}
denotes the distribution function of
\mathcal{N}\big(0,\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma\big)
.

Step 3.
Let us now prove an analogous result for the lower bound. Namely, proceeding as in step 2, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}+\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big(\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}+z(t_{N}/n)\|y_{i}\|_{\eta}\big)\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big(\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{\sigma},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}+\overline{\mu}_{i}(n)\big)\leq a_{i}\right\}\right]-\mathbb{P}\big[\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}>z(t_{N}/n)\big],

where we have set
\overline{\mu}_{i}(n)=\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{b},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}+z(t_{N}/n)\hskip 1.42271pt\|y_{i}\|_{\eta}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
. According to (
4.8
), the second term on the right-hand side converges to zero as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, arguing similarly to step 2 (by using Lemma
4.1
), we see that the
N
-dimensional Gaussian random vector

\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big(\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}^{\sigma},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}+\overline{\mu}_{i}(n)\big),\ i\in\{1,\dots,N\}\right)^{\intercal}

converges weakly to
\mathcal{N}\big(0,\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma\big)
. In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\left[\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)}\big\langle\mathcal{Y}_{t_{i}/n}+\mathcal{Z}_{t_{i}/n},y_{i}\big\rangle_{\eta}\leq a_{i}\right\}\cap\big\{\mathcal{Z}_{n}^{*}\leq z(t_{N}/n)\big\}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\Phi_{\sigma(\Xi\xi_{g})^{2}\Sigma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{N}).

Since
(a_{1},\dots,a_{N})^{\intercal}
is an arbitrary, but fixed continuity point of the desired limiting distribution, the assertion follows by a combination of step 2 and step 3.
∎

Remark 4.3

Remark 4.4
.

Note that since we have

4.2
A functional CLT for the Markovian Lift

Similarly to the finite-dimensional case considered in Section
2
, also for projections of the Markovian lift, the limit covariance matrix
\Sigma
is often given by an underlying Gaussian process, i.e., it has the form

\displaystyle\Sigma_{ij}=\mathrm{cov}\left(\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{t_{i}},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta},\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{t_{j}},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right),

(4.11)

with the Gaussian process
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}
on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
given by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\overline{\xi}_{K}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

(4.12)

where
\overline{\xi}_{K}\in\mathcal{H}_{-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
small enough. In such a case we can prove a functional CLT for the Markovian lift as stated below.

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that condition (A) is satisfied, and let
b\in C^{\chi_{b}}(\mathbb{R})
and
\sigma\in C^{\chi_{\sigma}}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\chi_{b},\chi_{\sigma}\in(0,1]
. Let
K
have representation (
3.4
), and let
\mathcal{X}
be any continuous weak solution of (
3.7
) on
\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{\mathcal{X}}}
with
\eta_{\mathcal{X}}\in(\eta_{*},1-\eta_{*})
and
g=\Xi S(t)\xi_{g}
, where
\xi_{g}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta_{g}}
for some
\eta_{g}>\eta_{*}
. Fix
N\geq 1
, and let
y_{1},\dots,y_{N}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}\setminus\{0\}
with
\eta\leq\eta_{\mathcal{X}}
satisfy condition (i) from Theorem
4.2
. Finally, suppose that there exists
\overline{\xi}_{K}\in\mathcal{H}_{-\eta_{*}-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
such that we have for every
s,t\in(0,T]
with
s\leq t
and
i,j\in\{1,\dots N\}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\lambda_{i}(n)\lambda_{j}(n)}\int_{0}^{s/n}\langle S((t-s)/n+r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\hskip 0.56917pt\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}r

(4.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\langle S(t-s+r)\overline{\xi}_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\hskip 0.56917pt\langle S(r)\overline{\xi}_{K},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}\left(\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{s},y_{i}\rangle_{\eta},\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{t},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right),

with
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}
defined by (
4.12
). If there exists
\theta>\eta-1/2
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(1+x)^{\theta}\hskip 0.56917pt|y_{j}(x)|\,\mu(\mathrm{d}x)<\infty

(4.14)

holds for all
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
and

\gamma_{*}<\frac{1}{2}+\theta-\eta,

(4.15)

then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(n)}\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{1}\rangle_{\eta},\dots,\sqrt{\lambda_{N}(n)}\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{N}\rangle_{\eta}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\longrightarrow}}\sigma\big(\Xi\xi_{g}\big)\left(\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{t},y_{1}\rangle_{\eta},\dots,\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{t},y_{N}\rangle_{\eta}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}.

Proof.

First, fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{M}\leq T
. Considering the sequences
(\widetilde{t}_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,MN\}}
and
(\widetilde{y}_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,MN\}}
defined by
\widetilde{t}_{i}=t_{\lceil i/N\rceil}
and
\widetilde{y}_{i}=y_{i-\lfloor(i-1)/N\rfloor N}
in combination with (
4.13
) and
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}
being a Gaussian process according to Lemma
4.1
shows that condition (ii) from Theorem
4.2
holds with the covariance matrix
\Sigma
given by

\Sigma=\mathrm{cov}\left(\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{\widetilde{t}_{i}},\widetilde{y}_{i}\rangle_{\eta},\langle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{\widetilde{t}_{j}},\widetilde{y}_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right)_{i,j\in\{1,\dots,MN\}}.

Therefore, Theorem
4.2
is applicable (see also Remark
4.4
). As
M
and the family of time points
(t_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,M\}}
were arbitrary, we have thus shown convergence of finite-dimensional distributions. Hence, it remains to verify that the sequence of the
N
-dimensional random processes

\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(n)}\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{1}\rangle_{\eta},\dots,\sqrt{\lambda_{N}(n)}\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{N}\rangle_{\eta}\right)_{t\in[0,T]},

n\in\mathbb{N}
, is tight. To prove the latter, it suffices by
[
47
, Corollary XIII.1.6]
to prove tightness for each of the
N
components. For this purpose, let us first obtain some analogous bounds for a collection of auxiliary completely monotone Volterra kernels determined by
y_{1},\dots,y_{N}
.

Step 1.
First, assuming that each
y_{j}
,
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, is nonnegative, fix
j
and let us define the locally finite measure
\nu_{j}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
via
\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{j}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}(x)=y_{j}(x)\hskip 0.56917pt(1+x)^{\eta}
, and set
K_{j}(t)=K(\infty)\hskip 0.85355pty_{j}(0)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-tx}\,\nu_{j}(\mathrm{d}x)
. Then,
due to (
4.14
),
\nu_{j}
satisfies condition (A)
from Section
3.1
with constant

\eta_{*}(j)\leq\eta-\theta<\frac{1}{2}.

(4.16)

Moreover, we obtain
\langle S(\cdot)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}=K_{j}
and hence using Lemma
3.3
for
\nu_{j}
gives

\int_{0}^{h}|\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{h}K_{j}(r)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\overline{C}\cdot\begin{cases}h,&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
h^{1-2\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality also

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}|\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\,\mathrm{d}r\leq\overline{C}^{1/2}\cdot\begin{cases}h,&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
h^{1-\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon/2},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Similarly, we find by the nonnegativity of
y_{j}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|\langle(S(h+r)-S(r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}|K_{j}(h+r)-K_{j}(r)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\cdot\begin{cases}h,&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
h^{1-2\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

With these estimates at hand, let us now proceed to prove the desired tightness. Firstly, note that by (
3.7
) combined with the commutativity of the continuous linear functional
\langle\cdot,y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}
with
\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
-valued Bochner and stochastic integrals, or alternatively by a (stochastic) Fubini argument, and
X:=\Xi\mathcal{X}
defining a continuous solution of (
3.1
), as argued already in the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we find

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}=\

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t/n}\langle S(t/n-r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\hskip 0.85355ptb(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t/n}\langle S(t/n-r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\hskip 0.85355pt\sigma(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}.

Hence, we obtain for
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
p\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\ \mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}-\langle\mathcal{X}_{s/n}-S(s/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right|^{p}\right]\lesssim I_{1}+\dots+I_{4},

where the terms
I_{1},\dots,I_{4}
are given by

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S(t/n-r)-S(s/n-r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\hskip 0.85355pt|b(X_{r})|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p}\right],

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s/n}^{t/n}|\langle S(t/n-r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\hskip 0.85355pt|b(X_{r})|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p}\right],

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S(t/n-r)-S(s/n-r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt|\sigma(X_{r})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right],

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{s/n}^{t/n}|\langle S(t/n-r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\hskip 0.85355pt|\sigma(X_{r})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\right].

To bound the latter, we proceed analogously to the proof of Proposition
2.1
and Lemma
A.1
. For the first term, we obtain from applying Hölder’s inequality twice, Fubini’s theorem,
b
being of linear growth and the moment estimate (
A.2
) from Lemma
A.1
combined with
\|g\|_{\infty}<\infty
:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S(t/n-r)-S(s/n-r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S(t/n-r)-S(s/n-r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\left(1+\mathbb{E}[|X_{r}|^{p}]\right)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S(t/n-r)-S(s/n-r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-p/2}\left(\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S((t-s)/n+r)-S(r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-p/2}\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{p/2},&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{(p/2)(1-2\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon)},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Likewise, we obtain for the second term

\displaystyle I_{2}\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{t-s}{n}}|\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p}\lesssim\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{p},&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{p(1-\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon/2)},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

For
I_{3}
we may find an upper bound by

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{s/n}|\langle(S(t/n-r)-S(s/n-r))\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{p/2},&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{(p/2)(1-2\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon)},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

while estimating
I_{4}
yields

\displaystyle I_{4}\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{t-s}{n}}|\langle S(r)\xi_{K},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{p/2}\lesssim\begin{cases}\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{p/2},&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
\left(\frac{t-s}{n}\right)^{(p/2)(1-2\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon)},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Collecting all inequalities and combining this with
\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(n)}\lesssim n^{\gamma_{*}}
by condition (i) and
|t-s|\leq T
we arrive at

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(n)^{p/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}-\langle\mathcal{X}_{s/n}-S(s/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right|^{p}\right]

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{p(\gamma_{*}-1/2)}\hskip 0.85355pt(t-s)^{p/2},&\eta_{*}(j)<0\\
n^{p(\gamma_{*}-1/2+\eta_{*}(j)+\varepsilon/2)}\hskip 0.85355pt(t-s)^{(p/2)(1-2\eta_{*}(j)-\varepsilon)},&0\leq\eta_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Step 2.
For general
y_{j}
, write
y_{j}=y_{j}^{+}-y_{j}^{-}
, where
y_{j}^{+},y_{j}^{-}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta}
denote the positive and the negative part of
y_{j}
, respectively. As the cases with
y_{j}^{+}\equiv 0
or
y_{j}^{-}\equiv 0
are immediate consequences of step 1, we assume in the following that both parts do not vanish. Since (
4.14
) simultaneously holds for
y_{j}^{+}
and
y_{j}^{-}
with the same
\theta>\eta-1/2
, we can in both cases carry out similar arguments as in step 1 for the kernels
\langle S(\cdot)\xi_{K},y_{j}^{+}\rangle_{\eta}
and
\langle S(\cdot)\xi_{K},y_{j}^{-}\rangle_{\eta}
, both being non-degenerate. Hence, by the triangle inequality and (
4.17
) specified to both cases we obtain with
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small:

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}(n)^{p/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}-\langle\mathcal{X}_{s/n}-S(s/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{j}(n)^{p/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}^{+}\rangle_{\eta}-\langle\mathcal{X}_{s/n}-S(s/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}^{+}\rangle_{\eta}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 12.80365pt+\lambda_{j}(n)^{p/2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}^{-}\rangle_{\eta}-\langle\mathcal{X}_{s/n}-S(s/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}^{-}\rangle_{\eta}\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}n^{p(\gamma_{*}-1/2)}\hskip 0.85355pt(t-s)^{p/2},&\overline{\eta}_{*}(j)<0\\
n^{p(\gamma_{*}-1/2+\overline{\eta}_{*}(j)+\varepsilon/2)}\hskip 0.85355pt(t-s)^{(p/2)(1-2\overline{\eta}_{*}(j)-\varepsilon)},&0\leq\overline{\eta}_{*}(j)<\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

(4.18)

where we introduced
\overline{\eta}_{*}(j):=\max\big\{\eta_{*}^{+}(j),\eta_{*}^{-}(j)\big\}
with
\eta_{*}^{+}(j)
and
\eta_{*}^{-}(j)
being defined analogously to
\eta_{*}
for the kernels
\langle S(\cdot)\xi_{K},y_{j}^{+}\rangle_{\eta}
and
\langle S(\cdot)\xi_{K},y_{j}^{-}\rangle_{\eta}
, respectively. Combining both
\eta_{*}^{+}(j)\leq\eta-\theta
and
\eta_{*}^{-}(j)\leq\eta-\theta
with (
4.15
) shows

\gamma_{*}<\tfrac{1}{2}+\min\left\{-\eta_{*}^{+}(j),-\eta_{*}^{-}(j)\right\}=\tfrac{1}{2}-\max\left\{\eta_{*}^{+}(j),\eta_{*}^{-}(j)\right\}=\tfrac{1}{2}-\overline{\eta}_{*}(j).

Hence, noting that we have in general
\gamma_{*}\leq 1/2
by Lemma
3.3
(see also (
4.19
) below) and selecting
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small yields uniform boundedness in
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, combining this with
\mathcal{X}_{0/n}-S(0/n)\xi_{g}\equiv 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
allows to apply Kolmogorov’s tightness criterion (see e.g.
[
47
, Theorem XIII.1.8]
) for
p
sufficiently large. Thus, we have shown that the sequence of one-dimensional stochastic processes

\left(\left(\sqrt{\lambda_{j}(n)}\langle\mathcal{X}_{t/n}-S(t/n)\xi_{g},y_{j}\rangle_{\eta}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

is tight. Since this holds for every
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, we have verified tightness also for the sequence of the entire processes, which completes the proof.
∎

Example 4.6
.

For
\eta_{*}<0

4.3
Examples

In the final part of this section, we discuss a collection of examples where Theorem

Corollary 4.9

Let

(i)

(ii)

Proof.

Let

Appendix A
Auxiliary moment bound

In this section, we prove a moment bound for continuous solutions of (
3.1
), which holds in particular in the small-time regime.

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
b,\sigma
are continuous with linear growth,
g:[0,T]\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and bounded, and
K\in L^{2}([0,T])
satisfies

\int_{0}^{t}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq Ct^{2\gamma},\qquad t\in(0,T],

(A.1)

for some constants
C,\gamma>0
and a time horizon
T>0
. Let
X
be any continuous weak solution of (
3.1
). Then, for each
p\geq 2
, it holds that

\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}-g(t)|^{p}]\leq C_{p}\,(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p})\,t^{p\gamma},\quad t\in[0,T],

(A.2)

where
C_{p}>0
is some constant.

Proof.

First, since
b,\sigma
have linear growth, it follows from a minor modification of the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
, where we bound
g
by its supremum norm (see also
[
45
, Lemma 3.4]
), that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}[|X_{t}|^{p}]<\infty,\qquad\forall p\in[1,\infty).

(A.3)

Applying the BDG inequality, then Jensen’s inequality, and finally

(1+|X_{s}|)^{p}\lesssim\left(1+|g(s)|\right)^{p}+|X_{s}-g(s)|^{p}\lesssim(1+\|g\|_{\infty})^{p}+|X_{s}-g(s)|^{p},

we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-g(t)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(t-s)|\,(1+|X_{s}|)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}K(t-s)^{2}\,(1+|X_{s}|)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}|K(t-s)|\,\mathbb{E}\left[(1+|X_{s}|)^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}|K(t-s)|^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[(1+|X_{s}|)^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p}\left(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)+\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}|K(t-s)|\,\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{s}-g(s)|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}|K(t-s)|^{2}\,\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{s}-g(s)|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, the estimate
\int_{0}^{t}|K(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq t^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}K(s)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\lesssim t^{\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}
, following from Hölder’s inequality, combined with (
A.1
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-g(t)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{p\gamma}\left(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}H(t,s)\,\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{s}-g(s)|^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s,

where we have set for
s,t\in[0,T]
:

H(t,s)=-\left(t^{(p-1)(\gamma+\frac{1}{2})}\,|K(t-s)|+t^{(p-2)\gamma}\,|K(t-s)|^{2}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{\{s\leq t\}}\leq 0.

Due to
K\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
p\geq 2
it follows that
\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}|H(t,s)|\,\mathrm{d}s<\infty
. Hence, using
[
31
, Proposition 9.2.7 (i)]
, we find that
H
is a Volterra kernel of
L^{\infty}
-type in the sense of
[
31
, Definition 9.2.2]
. Moreover, by (
1.4
) and
[
31
, Corollary 9.3.14]
there exists a resolvent of
L^{\infty}
-type
R
associated with
H
. Since
H\leq 0
, it follows from
[
31
, Proposition 9.8.1]
that also
R\leq 0
. By the generalized Volterra Gronwall inequality given in
[
31
, Lemma 9.8.2]
, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|X_{t}-g(t)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq t^{p\gamma}\left(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)-\int_{0}^{t}R(t,s)\,s^{p\gamma}\left(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq t^{p\gamma}\left(1+\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\int_{0}^{t}|R(t,s)|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)(1+\|g\|_{\infty}^{p}),

which proves the first assertion as the middle part is finite due to
R
being of
L^{\infty}
-type.
∎

Our bound is very similar to
[
3
, Lemma 2.4]
when
s=0
. However, in our case, we do not require (
1.5
) and additionally obtain a stronger bound on the right-hand side as it can be seen for the Riemann-Liouville kernel
K(t)=t^{H-1/2}
when
H>1/2
, for which we merely have
\overline{\gamma}=1/2
.

\mathcal{X}-S(\cdot)\xi_{g}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};C([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{\eta})),\qquad\forall T>0.

In particular, since

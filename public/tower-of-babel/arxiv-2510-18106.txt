Law equivalence for Ornstein–Uhlenbeck dynamics driven by Lévy noise
Tomasz Kania

Abstract
. For stochastic partial differential equations driven by Lévy noise, understanding when changes in the drift operator preserve the law of the solution is fundamental to filtering, control, and simulation. We extend law-equivalence results for Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) processes from bounded drift operators to generators of C 0 C_{0} -semigroups (indeed analytic semigroups) on a separable Hilbert space. Our analysis separates the problem into two channels: a Gaussian component governed by a Hilbert–Schmidt perturbation condition, and a jump-drift component requiring a directional Cameron–Martin hypothesis. We establish that when the Gaussian noise is non-degenerate, these conditions characterise absolute continuity and equivalence of path laws on the Skorohod space. For purely jump noise, we prove a rigidity phenomenon: absolute continuity forces the processes to coincide. Specialising to sectorial elliptic generators with compound Poisson jumps, we provide explicit, verifiable conditions in terms of resolvent estimates and exponential moments. We also construct explicit counterexamples showing that the Cameron–Martin condition can fail, sometimes asymmetrically, yielding only one-sided absolute continuity.

1.
Introduction

The equivalence of path laws for solutions to stochastic differential equations is fundamental in stochastic analysis, with applications ranging from mathematical finance and filtering theory to the numerical simulation of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs). For Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) processes in finite dimensions driven by Brownian motion, the celebrated Girsanov theorem provides a complete characterisation: changes in the drift induce absolutely continuous changes of measure via an exponential martingale. In infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces the situation is more delicate: the law of a Gaussian process is typically only quasi-invariant under translations by elements of the corresponding Cameron–Martin space, and the presence of jumps introduces further complications.

Consider an OU process
X
on a separable Hilbert space
H
, driven by a Lévy process
L
with drift
b
, Gaussian component
W
(a
Q
-Wiener process), and independent jump part
Z
:

(1.1)

dX(t)=AX(t)\,dt+dL_{t}=AX(t)\,dt+b\,dt+dW_{t}+dZ_{t},\qquad X(0)=0,

where
A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
S
on
H
. The mild solution is given by

X^{A}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\,dL_{s}.

The central question is:
when is the law of
X^{A}
equivalent (or absolutely continuous with respect to) the law of
X^{\widetilde{A}}
driven by the same noise but with a different generator
\widetilde{A}
?

For
bounded
drift operators, Bartosz and Kania
[
2
]
proved that if the Gaussian component is non-degenerate (the covariance
Q
has strictly positive eigenvalues), then the laws are equivalent. Conversely, for purely jump noise, absolute continuity implies the processes coincide almost surely. Their approach uses Skorohod-space measurability of jump reconstructions and a product-measure absolute continuity lemma.

We extend these results to the natural SPDE setting where
A
and
\widetilde{A}
are
unbounded generators
. Our contributions are threefold.

(1)

Generator extension.
We prove generator versions of the main equivalence and rigidity theorems, with a
directional
Cameron–Martin hypothesis (Assumption
3.1
) to handle the jump-drift channel.

(2)

Analytic semigroups.
For sectorial
A,\widetilde{A}
generating bounded analytic semigroups, fractional boundedness
KA^{-\beta}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
with
\beta<\tfrac{1}{2}
implies the Gaussian Hilbert–Schmidt condition; (CM) can be checked for compound Poisson jumps via exponential moments.

(3)

Sharp counterexamples.
Four diagonal models show (CM) need not hold, may hold only one-sidedly, and that Novikov can fail despite
L^{2}
-representability.

The results obtained may have direct implications for numerical simulation and statistical inference. Advanced Monte Carlo methods, such as importance sampling for rare event simulation, rely on a change of measure to a more convenient sampling distribution; our work establishes the theoretical validity of such changes for infinite-dimensional systems. Furthermore, questions of law equivalence are intimately tied to model identifiability: if two different generators
A
and
\widetilde{A}
produce equivalent laws, they cannot be distinguished from observing a single path of the process.

2.2.
Girsanov’s theorem in Hilbert space

Let

Duhamel formula for fractionally bounded perturbations

Let

2.4.
Gaussian OU equivalence under Hilbert–Schmidt perturbations

We first address the Gaussian component of the process, which is often the most tractable part of the analysis. The following theorem provides a fundamental criterion for law equivalence in this setting. It states that the laws of the two Gaussian processes are equivalent, provided the perturbation
K
is sufficiently ‘small’ when measured in a time-integrated Hilbert–Schmidt norm that accounts for both the smoothing of the semigroup and the covariance structure of the noise.

Theorem 2.5

(Gaussian OU equivalence
[
3
,
8
]
)
.

Let
A
generate a
C_{0}
-semigroup
S
, and let
\widetilde{A}=A+K
with
K

\ran S(t)\subset\Dom(K)
for all

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{T}\|KS(t)Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\,dt<\infty,

then
\mathcal{L}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}S(\cdot-s)\,dW_{s}\right)\sim\mathcal{L}\!\left(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\widetilde{S}(\cdot-s)\,dW_{s}\right)
on
D_{H,T}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Both convolutions admit continuous modifications (factorisation method; cf.
[
4
]
), so their laws live on

as translations are homeomorphisms in the
J_{1}

3.
Main hypotheses and theorems

Write the Lévy–Itô decomposition
L_{t}=bt+W_{t}+Z_{t}
with
W
a
Q
-Wiener process (strictly positive eigenvalues) and
Z
the jump part, independent of
W
. For a generator
B
with semigroup
S_{B}
, set

\displaystyle Y_{B}(W)(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}S_{B}(t-s)\,dW_{s},

(Gaussian)

\displaystyle J_{B}(Z)(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}S_{B}(t-s)\,dZ_{s},

(jump)

\displaystyle B_{B}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}S_{B}(t-s)b\,ds.

(drift)

Thus
X^{B}=Y_{B}(W)+J_{B}(Z)+B_{B}
.

In order to apply a conditional Girsanov theorem on the
\widetilde{A}
-channel, we encode the requirement that the jump-drift discrepancy lives in the
\widetilde{A}
-OU Cameron–Martin space with sufficient exponential integrability:

Assumption 3.1

(Directional Cameron–Martin,
A\to\widetilde{A}
)
.

Define

\Delta^{Jb}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}(S-\widetilde{S})(t-s)\,dZ_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}(S-\widetilde{S})(t-s)b\,ds.

There exists a predictable
U^{A\to\widetilde{A}}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,T];H)
, independent of
W
and
\sigma(Z)
-measurable, such that

(3.1)

\Delta^{Jb}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-s)\,Q^{1/2}U^{A\to\widetilde{A}}_{s}\,ds\quad\text{a.s.,}

and

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}\exp\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|U^{A\to\widetilde{A}}_{s}\|^{2}ds\right)<\infty.

Remark 3.2
.

(CM)
{}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}
yields one-sided absolute continuity
\mathcal{L}(X^{A})\ll\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})
. Full equivalence requires both (CM)
{}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}
and (CM)
{}_{\widetilde{A}\to A}
; see
Section

5
for asymmetric counterexamples.

Remark 3.3
.

Assumption

is an existence statement in
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{A},T}:=\overline{\ran K_{\widetilde{A}}}
, where

K_{B}u(t):=\int_{0}^{t}S_{B}(t-s)Q^{1/2}u(s)\,ds

. One may always choose the minimal-norm

; an explicit closed form (

, via
Q^{-1/2}

Let

4.
Analytic semigroups and verifiable conditions

We now specialise our abstract conditions to the important setting of analytic semigroups, which is typical for SPDEs arising from elliptic differential operators. We first recall the necessary operator-theoretic structure. A
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geqslant 0}
is called
analytic
if the map
t\mapsto S(t)x
is real-analytic on
(0,\infty)
for every
x\in H
; this property implies the semigroup has a strong smoothing effect for
t>0
. The semigroup is
bounded
if there exists a constant
M\geqslant 1
such that
\|S(t)\|\leqslant M
for all
t\geqslant 0
.

The operational criterion for identifying generators of such semigroups is provided by the concept of sectorial operators. A closed, densely defined operator
A
on
H
is called
sectorial
if its resolvent set
\rho(A)
contains an open sector
\Sigma_{\theta,\omega}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg(\lambda-\omega)|<\theta,\lambda\neq\omega\}
for some
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(\pi/2,\pi)
, and if its resolvent is uniformly bounded on this sector:
\|(\lambda I-A)^{-1}\|\leqslant M/|\lambda-\omega|
for all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta,\omega}
. It is a cornerstone of semigroup theory that an operator generates a bounded analytic semigroup if and only if it is a sectorial operator whose spectrum is contained in a sector with vertex
\omega\leqslant 0
.

4.1.
Fractional boundedness implies Gaussian equivalence

Corollary 4.1

(Analytic semigroups)
.

Let
A,\widetilde{A}
be sectorial generators of bounded analytic semigroups on
H
. If
K:=\widetilde{A}-A
satisfies
KA^{-\beta}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
for some
\beta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
, then:

(i)

Duhamel (
2.1
) holds in the strong operator topology;

(ii)

(
2.3
) holds, so
\mathcal{L}(Y_{A}(W))\sim\mathcal{L}(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W))
on
D_{H,T}
.

Proof.

(i) is
Theorem

2.4
. For (ii), (
2.2
) gives
\|A^{\beta}S(t)\|\leqslant C_{\beta}t^{-\beta}
, whence

\|KS(t)Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ \leqslant\ \|KA^{-\beta}\|\,\|A^{\beta}S(t)\|\,\|Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ \lesssim\ t^{-\beta}.

Square and integrate; since
2\beta<1
the integral on
(0,T]
is finite.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Replacing
A
by
A+\omega I
(and likewise
\widetilde{A}
) with
\omega>0
preserves analyticity and simplifies the functional calculus without affecting the OU law beyond a deterministic multiplicative factor absorbed into
S
; none of our estimates is sensitive to such a shift.

4.2.
Compound Poisson jumps and exponential moments

Having established a verifiable criterion for the equivalence of the Gaussian components in
Corollary

4.1
, we now turn to the more complex jump-drift channel. The central challenge is to translate the abstract Cameron–Martin hypothesis (Assumption
3.1
) into a practical condition on the Lévy measure. We specialise to the case of a compound Poisson process, a fundamental building block for more general jump processes.

The following proposition demonstrates that under the same fractional boundedness assumption, the required Cameron–Martin-space structure, including the crucial Novikov integrability condition, is guaranteed if the jump distribution possesses a sufficiently strong exponential moment. This effectively reduces the verification of Assumption
3.1
to checking an integrability property of the jump law
\nu
, weighted by the noise covariance
Q
.

Proposition 4.3

(Compound Poisson case)
.

Let
Z_{t}=\sum_{\tau_{i}\leqslant t}\xi_{i}
be a compound Poisson process with rate
\lambda>0
and jump law
\nu
on
H
. Under the hypotheses of
Corollary

4.1
with
\beta<\tfrac{1}{2}
, if

(4.1)

\int_{H}e^{c\|Q^{1/2}x\|^{2}}\,\nu(dx)<\infty\quad\text{for some }c>0,

then
(CM)
{}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}
holds.

Proof.

Cameron–Martin representability.
By Duhamel (
2.1
),

\Delta^{J}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(S-\widetilde{S})(t-s)\,dZ_{s}=\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{0}^{t-s}\!\widetilde{S}(t-s-r)KS(r)\,dr\,dZ_{s}.

For
r\in(0,1]
we have
\|KS(r)\|\leqslant\|KA^{-\beta}\|\,\|A^{\beta}S(r)\|\lesssim r^{-\beta}
, and
\|\widetilde{S}(t)\|
is bounded on
[0,T]
. Thus the kernel

\kappa(t,s):=\int_{0}^{t-s}\widetilde{S}(t-s-r)KS(r)\,dr

satisfies
\int_{0}^{t}\|\kappa(t,s)\|^{2}ds<\infty
for each
t\in[0,T]
. Hence the stochastic Fubini theorem applies (see,
e.g.
,
[
9
, Thm. 4.18]
), yielding

\Delta^{J}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-u)\,K\!\left(\int_{0}^{u}S(u-r)\,dZ_{r}\right)\,du.

Setting
U
to be the minimal-norm
L^{2}
-preimage under
K_{\widetilde{A}}
produces (
3.1
).

The Novikov bound.
By minimality of
U
in
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{A},T}
and the Minkowski/triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|U\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta^{J}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{A},T}}^{2}\ \leqslant\ \int_{0}^{T}\Big\|\sum_{\tau_{i}\leqslant t}Q^{1/2}S(t-\tau_{i})\xi_{i}\Big\|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant N(T)\,\|S\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(H))}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}\|Q^{1/2}\xi_{i}\|^{2},

where
N(T)
is the Poisson count up to
T
. Conditioning on
N(T)
and using independence of marks,

\mathbb{E}\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\|U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\ \leqslant\ \mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\mathbb{E}e^{\frac{1}{2}C_{T}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}}\big)^{N(T)}\Big]=\exp\!\Big(\lambda T\big(\mathbb{E}e^{\frac{1}{2}C_{T}\|Q^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}}-1\big)\Big),

with
C_{T}=\|S\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{L}(H))}^{2}<\infty
. The right-hand side is finite if (
4.1
) holds with
c>\tfrac{1}{2}C_{T}
, which may always be arranged by shrinking
T
(and then iterating on subintervals).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

In practice one verifies
KA^{-\beta}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
via resolvent bounds:

\sup_{\lambda\in\Sigma_{\theta}}\|K(\lambda+A)^{-1}\|\,|\lambda|^{\beta}<\infty,

for some sector
\Sigma_{\theta}=\{\lambda\neq 0:|\arg\lambda|<\theta\}
with
\theta\in(0,\pi/2)
;
cf
. the sectorial functional calculus
[
5
, Ch. II.4]
.

5.
Counterexamples: failures of the Cameron–Martin condition

To probe the necessity and sharpness of our main hypotheses, particularly the directional Cameron–Martin condition (Assumption
3.1
), we now construct a series of explicit counterexamples. To render the analysis tractable and the underlying mechanisms transparent, we work in the canonical setting of
H=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
with diagonal operators. These examples are designed to demonstrate several key phenomena: that the Cameron–Martin condition can fail to hold, that it may hold asymmetrically (leading to only one-sided absolute continuity), and that the Novikov integrability condition is a distinct and necessary requirement beyond square-integrability.

We consider the simplest non-trivial case of a single jump
\xi
occurring at a known time
s>0
, which is sufficient to test the core integrability conditions. First, we derive an explicit formula for the Cameron–Martin representative and its norm in this diagonal framework.

We present diagonal examples on
H=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
. Let
S(t)e_{n}=e^{-a_{n}t}e_{n}
,
\widetilde{S}(t)e_{n}=e^{-\widetilde{a}_{n}t}e_{n}
, and
Qe_{n}=q_{n}e_{n}
with
\sum q_{n}<\infty
; let a single jump
\xi=\sum\xi_{n}e_{n}
occur at time
s
. Then:

Lemma 5.1

(Diagonal CM representative)
.

If (CM)
{}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}
holds, the predictable representative is

u_{n}(t)=\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n}-a_{n}}{\sqrt{q_{n}}}\,e^{-a_{n}(t-s)}\xi_{n}\ \mathbbm{1}_{t\geqslant s},

with

\|U\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H)}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\widetilde{a}_{n}-a_{n})^{2}}{2a_{n}q_{n}}\big(1-e^{-2a_{n}(T-s)}\big)|\xi_{n}|^{2}.

Proof.

Duhamel gives

\Delta^{J}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(t)=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\big(e^{-a_{n}(t-s)}-e^{-\widetilde{a}_{n}(t-s)}\big)\xi_{n}\,\mathbbm{1}_{t\geqslant s}\,e_{n}.

The CM identity
\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\widetilde{a}_{n}(t-r)}\sqrt{q_{n}}\,u_{n}(r)\,dr=e^{-a_{n}(t-s)}-e^{-\widetilde{a}_{n}(t-s)}
yields the formula by differentiating in
t
and integrating; the
L^{2}
norm is immediate.
∎

Example 5.2

(No
L^{2}
representative)
.

Take
a_{n}\equiv 1
,
\widetilde{a}_{n}=1+n^{2}
,
q_{n}=e^{-n^{2}}
,
\xi_{n}=e^{-n^{2}/4}/n
. Then

\frac{(\widetilde{a}_{n}-a_{n})^{2}}{q_{n}}|\xi_{n}|^{2}=\frac{n^{4}\,e^{-n^{2}/2}}{e^{-n^{2}}\,n^{2}}=n^{2}e^{n^{2}/2}\to\infty,

so
\|U\|_{L^{2}}=\infty
and (CM) fails, although (
2.3
) holds.

Example 5.3

(One-sided failure)
.

Let
a_{n}=n^{4}
,
\widetilde{a}_{n}\equiv 1
,
q_{n}=n^{-8}
,
\xi_{n}=n^{-7}
. Then for
A\to\widetilde{A}
,

\frac{(\widetilde{a}_{n}-a_{n})^{2}}{a_{n}q_{n}}|\xi_{n}|^{2}=\frac{n^{8}}{n^{4}\cdot n^{-8}\cdot n^{14}}=\frac{1}{n^{2}},

so
\|U\|_{L^{2}}<\infty
and the Novikov condition may be arranged. Conversely, for
\widetilde{A}\to A
one obtains
n^{2}\to\infty
, so (CM) fails in that direction.

Example 5.4

(The Novikov condition fails with an
L^{2}
representative)
.

In
H=\mathbb{R}
with
Q=1
, let
a=\widetilde{a}+\delta
(
\delta>0
small). If
\xi
has a Student
t_{\nu}
law (
\nu>2
), then
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{2}]<\infty
so
\mathbb{E}\|U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<\infty
by
Lemma

5.1
; however
\mathbb{E}e^{c\xi^{2}}=\infty
for all
c>0
, so the Novikov condition fails.

Example 5.5

(Lack of factorisation)
.

Let
a_{n}\equiv 1
,
\widetilde{a}_{n}=1+n^{2}
,
q_{n}=n^{-6}
. The naive factorisation
S-\widetilde{S}=\widetilde{S}*(KS(\cdot)Q^{-1/2})
would require
KS(\cdot)Q^{-1/2}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;\mathrm{HS})
, but

\|KS(t)Q^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}=\sum_{n}n^{4}e^{-2t}\,n^{6}=e^{-2t}\sum_{n}n^{10}=\infty.

6.
Proofs of the main theorems

The proof of
Theorem

3.4
proceeds in three moves: (1) use (G) and independence to align the Gaussian channels and obtain product equivalence; (2) represent the jump-drift discrepancy as a Cameron–Martin vector on the
\widetilde{A}
-channel; (3) apply conditional Girsanov and disintegrate. The rigidity in
Theorem

3.5
uses the fact that jumps are preserved by
S_{B}(0)=I
and that the jump process is measurably recoverable from the path; absolute continuity transports membership of solution sets and uniqueness forces equality.

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.4

Proof.

By
Theorem

2.5
,

(6.1)

\mathcal{L}(Y_{A}(W))\sim\mathcal{L}(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W)).

By independence of
W
and
Z
, Lemma
2.2
yields

(6.2)

\mathcal{L}\big(Y_{A}(W),Z\big)\sim\mathcal{L}\big(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W),Z\big).

Let us define an auxillary process
\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}:D_{H,T}\times D_{H,T}\to D_{H,T}
by

\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}(y,z):=y+J_{\widetilde{A}}(z)+B_{\widetilde{A}}.

By
Proposition

2.1
(iii),
\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}
is Borel. Let

X^{\prime}:=\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}(Y_{A}(W),Z)=Y_{A}(W)+J_{\widetilde{A}}(Z)+B_{\widetilde{A}}.

Since
X^{\widetilde{A}}=\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W),Z)
, (
6.2
) implies

(6.3)

\mathcal{L}(X^{\prime})\sim\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}}).

We have

X^{A}-X^{\prime}=(J_{A}-J_{\widetilde{A}})(Z)+(B_{A}-B_{\widetilde{A}})=-\Delta^{Jb}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}.

By (CM) there exists predictable
U^{A\to\widetilde{A}}
with (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

Fix
z
and set
h(z):=-\Delta^{Jb}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(z)\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{A},T}
. By
Theorem

2.3
,

\mathcal{L}\big(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W)+h(z)\,\big|\,Z=z\big)\sim\mathcal{L}\big(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W)\,\big|\,Z=z\big).

Moreover,
addition by a fixed càdlàg path is a bi-measurable homeomorphism of
(D_{H,T},J_{1})
, hence translations preserve measure equivalence on
D_{H,T}
. Now

\displaystyle\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W)+h(z),z)

\displaystyle=Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W)-\Delta^{Jb}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}(z)+J_{\widetilde{A}}(z)+B_{\widetilde{A}}

\displaystyle=Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W)+J_{A}(z)+B_{A}=X^{A}|_{Z=z},

while
\Phi_{\widetilde{A}}(Y_{\widetilde{A}}(W),z)=X^{\widetilde{A}}|_{Z=z}
. Hence

\mathcal{L}(X^{A}\,|\,Z=z)\sim\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}}\,|\,Z=z),\qquad\text{for }\mathcal{L}(Z)\text{-a.e.\ }z.

By the disintegration theorem (
[
6
, Ch. 6]
), for any Borel
E\subset D_{H,T}
,

\mathcal{L}(X^{A})(E)=\int\mathcal{L}(X^{A}\,|\,Z=z)(E)\,\mathcal{L}(Z)(dz).

If
\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})(E)=0
, then
\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}}\,|\,Z=z)(E)=0
for
\mathcal{L}(Z)
-a.e.
z
, and conditional equivalence gives
\mathcal{L}(X^{A}\,|\,Z=z)(E)=0
for those
z
. Thus
\mathcal{L}(X^{A})(E)=0
, proving
\mathcal{L}(X^{A})\ll\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})
. The reverse absolute continuity follows by symmetry if (CM)
{}_{\widetilde{A}\to A}
also holds.
∎

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.5

Proof.

For any
C_{0}
-semigroup
S_{B}
,
S_{B}(0)=I
. At a jump time
s
of
Z
,

\Delta X^{B}(s)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{s-\varepsilon}^{s}S_{B}(s-u)\,dZ_{u}=\Delta Z(s),

see,
e.g.
, Protter
[
10
, Ch. II]
. Hence
X^{B}
and
Z
have identical jump times and sizes.

By
Proposition

2.1
, there is a Borel map
\mathcal{Z}:D_{H,T}\to D_{H,T}
with
\mathcal{Z}(X^{B})=Z
a.s. Define

\Sigma_{B}:=\Big\{f\in D_{H,T}:f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}S_{B}(t-s)\,d\mathcal{Z}(f,s)\ \text{for all }t\in[0,T]\Big\}.

The map
f\mapsto\int_{0}^{\cdot}S_{B}(\cdot-s)\,d\mathcal{Z}(f,s)
is Borel from
Proposition

2.1
(iii), hence
\Sigma_{B}
is Borel as the inverse image of the diagonal.

By definition
X^{\widetilde{A}}\in\Sigma_{\widetilde{A}}
a.s.; absolute continuity
\mathcal{L}(X^{A})\ll\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})
implies
\mathbb{P}(X^{A}\in\Sigma_{\widetilde{A}})=1
. Thus both
X^{\widetilde{A}}
and
X^{A}
are mild solutions to
dY=\widetilde{A}Y\,dt+dZ
with
Y(0)=0
. Uniqueness of mild solutions for linear equations (see
[
9
, Chs. 9–11]
or
[
1
, Thm. 9.28]
) yields
X^{A}=X^{\widetilde{A}}
a.s.
∎

Further directions and open problems

P1
.

Boundary case
\boldsymbol{\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}}
.

Our Gaussian criterion uses
KA^{-\beta}\in\mathcal{L}(H)
with
\beta<\tfrac{1}{2}
(due to
\int_{0}^{T}t^{-2\beta}dt<\infty
).
Can one reach
\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}
under additional structure (
e.g.
, spectral gaps for
A
, faster eigenvalue decay of
Q
, or weighted Hilbert–Schmidt conditions)?
Sharp sufficient/necessary conditions at the boundary would be then desirable.

P2
.

Infinite-activity small jumps.

We verified (CM) for compound Poisson inputs. For Lévy measures with infinite activity, identify minimal tail/near-zero conditions on
\nu
ensuring a.s. inclusion
\Delta^{Jb}_{A\to\widetilde{A}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{A},T}
and the Novikov condition.
For instance, do

\int_{\|x\|\leqslant 1}\|Q^{1/2}x\|^{2}\,\nu(dx)<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\|x\|>1}e^{c\|Q^{1/2}x\|^{2}}\,\nu(dx)<\infty

suffice under analytic smoothing?

P3
.

Degenerate Gaussian component.

When
Q
has non-trivial kernel, what are necessary/sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{L}(X^{A})\ll\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})
on the support of the Gaussian channel,
i.e.
after projecting onto
\overline{\ran Q^{1/2}}
?
We would like to fully delineate the rôle of the two Cameron–Martin spaces in this degenerate setting.

P4
.

Banach-space extensions.

It is natural to ask for an extension of
Theorem

3.4
to UMD Banach spaces where Gaussian convolutions live in
\gamma
-radonifying ranges and the Girsanov theorem holds in the radonifying form.
What is the right framework to replace (CM) and the Novikov condition?

P5
.

Non-analytic semigroups/
H^{\infty}
calculus.

It would be desirable to replace analyticity by a bounded
H^{\infty}
calculus or polynomial stability.
We ask whether it is possible to find fractional boundedness hypotheses on
K
that still imply (
2.3
) and permit a (CM)-like factorisation argument.

P6
.

Time-dependent generators.

For
dX(t)=A(t)X(t)\,dt+dL_{t}
with an evolution family
U(t,s)
, we are interested in analogues of (G) and (CM) in terms of
(t,s)\mapsto U(t,s)
and prove a version of
Theorem

3.4
.

P7
.

Radon–Nikodým densities: quantitative bounds.

Beyond qualitative equivalence, it would be desirable to obtain estimates for the density
\frac{d\mathcal{L}(X^{A})}{d\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})}
in terms of
\int_{0}^{T}\|KS(t)Q^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}dt
and exponential moments of
\nu
and explore stability under small perturbations of
K
and of the Lévy triplet.

P8
.

Necessity of (CM) with Gaussian with jumps.

In the purely jump case, absolute continuity forces equality. In the mixed case, identify non-trivial necessary conditions (beyond (CM)) that must hold if
\mathcal{L}(X^{A})\ll\mathcal{L}(X^{\widetilde{A}})
.

P9
.

Numerical schemes and statistical identifiability.

Investigate whether discretisations (
e.g.
the Euler–Maruyama scheme with jumps) preserve approximate absolute continuity with an error controlled by
\|KA^{-\beta}\|
and moments of
\nu
; study implications for inference and hypothesis testing on the drift.

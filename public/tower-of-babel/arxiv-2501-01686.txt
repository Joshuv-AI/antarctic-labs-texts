1.
Introduction

For
d\geq 2
, let us consider the wave equation

\partial_{t}^{2}u(x,t)-\triangle u(x,t)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}

with initial data
u(x,0)=f
and
\partial_{t}u(x,0)=0
, whose formal solution
is given by

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)+e^{-it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)\Bigl).

In this paper, we are concerned with several types of maximal estimates for the wave operator
f\mapsto e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f
. Such estimates have been utilized to study
pointwise convergence of the solution
u(\cdot,t)
to the initial data
f
as
t\to 0
. They also find applications in the study of the linear and nonlinear wave equations. Specifically, we focus on local and global (in time) maximal operators in
L^{q}
or
L^{q}_{loc}
space,
as investigated in
[
8
]
(see the estimates (
1.1
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
) below).

1.1.
Local in time estimate.

We first consider the local in time maximal estimate

(1.1)

\big\|\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,q,s}\|f\|_{{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which was studied by various authors. Here
{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the inhomogeneous Sobolev space of order
s
.
Cowling
[
3
]
proved that (
1.1
) holds for
q=2
and
s>1/2
, and it was shown by Walther
[
12
]
that the regularity requirement
s>1/2
is sharp.
Later, Rogers and Villarroya
[
8
]
extended the estimate (
1.1
) to general
q\in(2,\infty]
. It should be noted that the estimate (
1.1
) is valid only for
q\geq 2
, as can be justified by making use of translation invariance of the wave operator.

Let us set
q_{\circ}(d)=\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1}
and

s_{c}(q,d):=\begin{cases}\frac{d+1}{4}-\frac{d-1}{2q},\ &2\leq q<q_{\circ}(d),\\[4.0pt]
\ \ \ \frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{q},\ &q_{\circ}(d)\leq q\leq\infty.\end{cases}

It was shown in
[
8
]
that (
1.1
) holds if
s>s_{c}(q,d)
, and (
1.1
) fails to hold if
s<s_{c}(q,d)
.
However, most of the estimates for the endpoint case
s=s_{c}(q,d)
have remained open until recently.

Previously, there were very few results addressing the estimate of critical index
s=s_{c}(q,d)
. For
d=3
and
q\in[6,\infty)
, Beceanu and Goldberg
[
1
]
, using the reversed-norm Strichartz inequality and the
TT^{*}
argument, proved that

\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}(q,d)}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

This estimate is in fact equivalent to the seemingly weaker estimate (
1.1
) with
s=s_{c}(q,3)
; see Proposition
1.6
below. From the perspective of the global in time maximal estimate (e.g., see (
1.6
)), their result does not cover the case
q\in[4,6)
. Later, Machihara
[
7
]
obtained the estimate (
1.6
) for
d=3
,
q=4
, and
s_{c}(4,3)={3}/{4}
with radial functions
f
satisfying a certain monotonicity property.

In this paper, we provide an almost complete answer to the endpoint estimates. We obtain strong type estimates for the critical case
s=s_{c}(q,d)
with
q\in(2,\infty)\setminus\{q_{\circ}(d)\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)\setminus\{q_{\circ}(d)\}
. Then, we have the estimate

(1.2)

\big\|\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,q,s}\|f\|_{{H}^{s_{c}(q,d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The case
q=q_{\circ}(d)
remains open. Unfortunately, it looks unlikely that the method in this paper recovers this case.
However, for
q=q_{\circ}(d)
, we have the estimate

(1.3)

\|\sup_{t\in(1,2)}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|\|_{L^{q_{\circ}(d),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,q,s}\|f\|_{H^{s_{c}(q_{\circ}(d),d),1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
H^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the nonhomogeneous Sobolev–Lorentz space with the norm
\|f\|_{H^{s,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\|_{L^{2,1}}.

We refer the reader to Section
3.3
for more details regarding the estimate (
1.3
).

We also make a couple of remarks on the cases
q=2
and
q=\infty
. In fact, it was proved by Ham–Ko–Lee
[
5
, Lemma A.2]
that

\|\sup_{t\in(0,1)}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|\|_{L^{q,\infty}(B^{d}(0,1))}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

fails for any
q\geq 1
. In particular, the weak type estimate is not possible for
q=2
. It is easy to see that
the estimate (
1.1
) fails for
q=\infty
and
s=s_{c}(\infty,d)=d/2
from failure of the Sobolev imbedding
H^{d/2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
(see, for example,
[
4
, Remark 3]
).

To establish the endpoint estimates in Theorem
1.1
, we consider not only the
H^{s}
–
L^{q}
estimates but also the broader framework of
L^{p}_{s}
–
L^{q}
estimates. Specifically, the desired
H^{s}
–
L^{q}
estimates will be derived by proving various optimal (endpoint case)
L^{p}_{s}
–
L^{q}
estimates. Here
L^{p}_{s}
denotes the
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces of order
s
so that
L^{2}_{s}=H^{s}
. For this purpose, we adopt the approach developed in
[
6
]
, which leverages the bilinear restriction estimates for the cone due to Wolff
[
13
]
and Tao
[
9
]
. As is evident to experts in the field, various endpoint maximal estimates for
f\in L_{s}^{p}
over an extended range can also be shown by using currently available results on local smoothing estimates for the wave operator. However, a complete resolution of this problem is closely linked to the local smoothing estimates with optimal regularity, which remain unresolved to date except for some special cases. We do not attempt to address this matter here.

1.2.
Local time-space maximal estimate

Several variant forms of the estimate (
1.1
) were also studied in
[
8
]
. These variants can be applied to different purposes depending on the nature of the problem under consideration. However, as will be seen below, they turn out to be essentially equivalent. To the best of our knowledge, such equivalences have not been formally established before.

Let us first consider the local time-space estimate

(1.4)

\big\|\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(B^{d}(0,1))}\leq C_{n,q,s}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Trivially, this estimate follows from (
1.1
). However, thanks to finite speed of propagation and translation invariance of the wave operator,
the estimate (
1.4
) implies (
1.1
) if
q\geq 2
. Consequently, we have the next proposition.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
q\geq 2
and
s\geq 0
. Then, the estimates (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) are equivalent.

An immediate consequence of this equivalence and Theorem
1.1
is the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)\setminus\{q_{\circ}(d)\}
. Then, the estimate (
1.4
) holds if and only if
s\geq s_{c}(q,d)
.

1.3.
Global in time and local in space maximal estimate

We also consider the estimate

(1.5)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(B^{d}(0,1))}\leq C_{n,q,s}\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

which is global in time and local in space. At first glance, the implication from the estimate (
1.4
) to (
1.5
) is not immediately clear while
the opposite direction is trivial. However, it turns out that they are equivalent when
q\geq 2
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
q\geq 2
and
s\geq 0
. Then, the estimates (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are equivalent.

Thanks to the equivalence and Corollary
1.3
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
q\in(2,\infty)\setminus\{q_{\circ}(d)\}
. Then (
1.5
) holds if and only if
s\geq s_{c}(q,d)
.

1.4.
Global time-space maximal estimate

We now discuss the global time-space maximal estimate

(1.6)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{n,q,s}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the homogeneous Sobolev space of order
s
. As can be easily seen by a scaling argument, for the global estimate (
1.6
) we need to use the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}
. Moreover, the scaling condition
s=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{q}
has to be satisfied.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
q\geq q_{\circ}(d)
and
s=s_{c}(q,d)=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{q}
. Then, the estimates (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) are equivalent.

It is easy to see that the equivalence in Proposition
1.6
readily generalizes to the Lorentz spaces
L^{q,r}
by replacing
L^{q}
(see
(G4)
in
Generalizations
below and Section
4.3
below). Furthermore, as to be seen later, by real interpolation the estimate (
1.2
) extends to the
{H}^{s_{c}(q,d)}
–
L^{q,2}
estimate (see Remark
3.2
).
Consequently, we have the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
q\in(q_{\circ}(d),\infty)
. Then (
1.6
) holds with
s=s_{c}(q,d)
. Moreover, we have

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{n,q,s}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{c}(q,d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Theorem
1.7
generalizes the result in
[
1
]
, where only the case
d=3
and
q\in[6,\infty)
was considered.
We close the introduction with some remarks concerning extensions of our results to more general evolution operators.

Generalizations.

For a function
\phi
that is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, let us consider

e^{it\phi(D)}f=(2\pi)^{-d}\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t\phi(\xi))}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

In what follows we list some of the possible generalizations, which can be deduced from the arguments in this paper without difficulty. Though further extensions are possible, we do not attempt to present them in most general forms.

(G1)
If
\phi
is a smooth function that is homogeneous of degree
1
, and the Hessian matrix of
\phi
has
d-1
nonzero eigenvalues of the same sign, then the endpoint estimates in Theorem
1.1
remain valid for the operator
e^{it\phi(D)}
replacing the wave operator
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
. This holds since the same bilinear restriction estimates hold for the surface
(\xi,\phi(\xi))
(see Theorem
2.2
below).

(G2)
It is natural to expect that the same endpoint estimates continue to hold for the operator
e^{it\phi(D)}
whose dispersive symbol
\phi
is no longer homogeneous, such as the Klein–Gordon equation. Indeed, suppose
that
\phi
is bounded on every bounded set, and suppose that there are constants
C,R>0
such that

(1.7)

|\partial^{\alpha}(\phi(\xi)-|\xi|)|\leq C|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}

for
|\alpha|\leq d+1
if
|\xi|\geq R
. Then, the estimates in Theorem
1.1
hold true for the operator
e^{it\phi(D)}.

Typical examples include the operators
e^{it\phi(D)}
given by
\phi(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{\kappa})^{1/\kappa}
with
\kappa\geq 1
.
See Section
3.4
for further details.

(G3)
Proposition
1.2
also remains valid even if the wave operator
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
is replaced by
e^{it\phi(D)}
provided that there are constants
C,R>0
such that

(1.8)

|\partial^{\alpha}\phi(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{1-|\alpha|}

for
|\xi|\geq R
if
|\alpha|\leq d+1
. See Remark
4.1
below.

(G4)
The implications between
H^{s}
(or
\dot{H}^{s}
)–
L_{x}^{q}L_{t}^{\infty}
estimates can readily be extended to
H^{s}
(or
\dot{H}^{s}
)–
L_{x}^{q}L_{t}^{r}
estimates under appropriate conditions on
s
,
q
, and
r
. The details are left to the interested reader.

This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 is devoted to obtaining the optimal

2.
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the wave operator

In order to show Theorem
1.1
, we make use of
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
type local smoothing estimate for the wave operator.
In order to state our result, we first introduce some notation.

Let us set
\mathfrak{Q}
be a point in the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
given by

\mathfrak{Q}=\Big(\frac{(d-1)(d+3)}{2(d^{2}+2d-1)},\frac{(d-1)(d+1)}{2(d^{2}+2d-1)}\Big),

on which the lines
L_{1}:(d-1)(1-x)=(d+1)y
and
L_{2}:y=\frac{d+1}{d+3}x
intersect each other. We denote
by
\mathcal{T}_{u}
the closed triangle with vertices
(1,0),(1/2,1/2)
, and
\mathfrak{Q}
. By
\mathcal{T}_{l}

we also denote
the closed triangle with vertices
(1,0),(0,0)
, and
\mathfrak{Q}
.
(See Figure
2

below.)
For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, let us
set

(2.1)

\displaystyle\beta_{u}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d+1}{2}\big(\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{q}\big),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\beta_{l}(p,q)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{d-1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{d+1}{q}.

We also define

\beta(p,q)=\begin{cases}\beta_{u}(p,q),&(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}_{u},\\[4.0pt]
\beta_{l}(p,q),&(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}_{l}.\end{cases}

Note that
\beta_{u}(p,q)=\beta_{l}(p,q)
if
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}_{u}\cap\mathcal{T}_{l}.
So,
\beta(p,q)
is well-defined.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
I_{\circ}=(1,2)
and
N\geq 1
. Let
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}_{u}\cup\mathcal{T}_{l}\setminus{\{\mathfrak{Q}\}}
.
Suppose that
{\rm supp\,}\widehat{f}
is included in
\mathbb{A}_{N}=\{\xi:N/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2N\}
. Then,

(2.3)

\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I_{\circ})}\leq CN^{\beta(p,q)}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

It should be noted that the bounds in (
2.3
) are no longer valid if the interval
I_{\circ}
is replaced by
(0,1)
.
This is why we use
I_{\circ}
instead of the interval
(0,1)
.
Sharpness of the estimate (
2.3
) can be shown by adapting the examples in
[
11
]
.

The estimates (
2.3
) for the cases
(p,q)=(\infty,\infty),(1,\infty),
and
(2,2)
are easy to show (well-known). In fact, (
2.3
) for
(p,q)=(2,2)
follows from Plancherel’s theorem. For the other cases, see Section
2.1.1
below. Consequently, the proof of Proposition
2.1
basically reduces to proving the estimate

(2.4)

\|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I_{\circ})}\leq CN^{\beta(p,q)}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}},\quad(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})=\mathfrak{Q}.

Once we obtain
(
2.4
),
by real interpolation between this and the above mentioned estimates, the estimate (
2.3
) follows for all
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\mathcal{T}_{u}\cup\mathcal{T}_{l}\setminus{\{\mathfrak{Q}\}}
.

We show the estimate (
2.4
) by making use of the bilinear adjoint restriction estimate to the cone. The proof of (
2.4
) is to be provided at the end of this section.

.

Let

2.1.1.
Proof of (
2.5
) for
(p,q)=(\infty,\infty)
and
(p,q)=(1,\infty)

Let
\beta_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-2^{2},2^{2})
such that
\beta_{0}=1
on
[-2,2]
, and let
\beta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(2^{-2},2^{2})
such that
\beta=1
on
[2^{-1},2]
.

Let us set

K_{t}^{N}(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\beta(|\xi|/N)d\xi.

Note that
K_{t}^{N}(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}N^{d}\int e^{iN(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\beta(|\xi|)d\xi
.
Since the Hessian matrix of the function
\xi\to|\xi|
has rank
d-1
on the support of
\beta
, by the stationary phase method we see that
\|K_{t}^{N}\|_{\infty}\lesssim N^{\frac{d+1}{2}}
.
Let us set

\chi_{\theta}(\xi)=\beta\Big(\frac{\xi_{1}}{N}\Big)\beta_{0}\Big(\frac{|\xi^{\prime}|}{c\theta N}\Big).

Note that
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f=K_{t}^{N}\ast\chi^{\vee}_{\theta}\ast f
and
\|K_{t}^{N}\ast\chi^{\vee}_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|K_{t}^{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\chi^{\vee}_{\theta}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim N^{\frac{d+1}{2}}
. Thus, (
2.5
) follows for
(p,q)=(1,\infty)
.

It is not difficult to see that

\|K_{t}^{N}\ast\chi^{\vee}_{\theta}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C

if
\theta\leq N^{-1/2}
. Thus, dividing the support of

\chi_{\theta}
into as many as
(\theta N^{\frac{1}{2}})^{d-1}
sectors of angular diameter
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we have
\|K_{t}^{N}\ast\chi^{\vee}_{\theta}\|_{1}\leq C(\theta N^{\frac{1}{2}})^{d-1}
for
t\in I_{\circ}
. Thus, we get (

Lemma 2.3

By finite decomposition and rotation, we may assume

A Whitney type decomposition.

By real interpolation, we have a slightly stronger estimate

\Big\|\sup_{t\in(0,1)}\big|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\big|\Big\|_{L^{q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,q,s}\|f\|_{{H}^{s_{c}(q,d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for
q\in(2,\infty)\setminus\{q_{\circ}(d)\}
. Indeed,
by the estimate in Lemma
3.1
and real interpolation, we have

\|\sup_{t\in I_{\circ}}|U_{t}^{s}P_{0}f|\|_{L^{q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}
for
2\leq q<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Fixing
s_{\ast}=s_{c}(q,d)
and using the estimates (
3.7
) and (
3.9
), by
interpolation between those estimates, we also have

\|\sup_{t\in I_{\circ}}|\sum_{j\geq 1}U_{t}^{s_{c}(q,d)}P_{j}f|\|_{L^{q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}

for
q\in(2,\infty)\setminus\{q_{\circ}(d)\}
. Therefore, (
3.10
) follows.

3.3.
The case
q=q_{\circ}(d)
: Proof of (
1.3
)

Finally, we consider the case
q=q_{\circ}(d)
and prove (
1.3
). For the purpose, we make use of the estimate (
3.2
)
with
(1/p,1/q)\in L_{1}
for
\frac{2(d^{2}+2d-1)}{(d-1)(d+1)}<q\leq\infty

(see Figure
2
). In fact, we have the estimate

(3.11)

\|\sup_{t\in I_{\circ}}|e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq CN^{\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{d^{2}+1}{(d-1)q}}\|f\|_{L^{p}}

for
p,q
satisfying
(d-1)(1-\frac{1}{p})=\frac{d+1}{q}
and
\frac{2(d^{2}+2d-1)}{(d-1)(d+1)}<q\leq\infty
whenever
{\rm supp\,}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{A}_{N}
.

Let
s_{\ast}=\tfrac{d}{2}-\tfrac{d}{q_{\circ}(d)}
. Note that

\tfrac{d+1}{2}-\tfrac{d^{2}+1}{(d-1)q}=s_{\ast}+\frac{d^{2}+1}{d+1}\big(\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{2}\big).
Thus, by (
3.11
)

\|\sup_{t\in I_{\circ}}|U_{t}^{s_{\ast}}P_{j}f|\Big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C2^{j\frac{d^{2}+1}{d+1}(\tfrac{1}{p}-\tfrac{1}{2})}\|f\|_{L^{p}}

for
p,q
satisfying
(d-1)(1-\frac{1}{p})=\frac{d+1}{q}
and
\frac{2(d^{2}+2d-1)}{(d-1)(d+1)}<q\leq\infty
. Therefore, as before, we may apply Lemma
2.3
with
n=1
choosing pairs
(1/p_{\ell},1/q_{\ell})\in L_{1}
such that
p_{0}<2<p_{1}
and
\frac{2(d^{2}+2d-1)}{(d-1)(d+1)}<q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
. Consequently, we obtain

\Big\|\sum_{j\geq 1}\sup_{t\in I_{\circ}}|U_{t}^{s_{\ast}}P_{j}f|\Big\|_{L^{q_{\circ}(d),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2,1}}.

Combining this and (
3.5
) gives
\|\sup_{t\in(1,2)}|U_{t}^{s_{\ast}}f|\|_{L^{q_{\circ}(d),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2,1}}
, which is equivalent to (
1.3
).

Since
\sup_{t\in(0,1)}|U_{t}^{s_{\ast}}f|=\sup_{t\in(1,2)}|U_{t}^{s_{\ast}}e^{-i\sqrt{-\Delta}}f|
, we have

\Big\|\sup_{t\in(0,1)}|U_{t}^{s_{\ast}}f|\Big\|_{L^{q_{\circ}(d),\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big\|e^{-i\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\Big\|_{L^{2,1}}.

However, it seems unlikely that
\|e^{-i\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\|_{L^{2,1}}
can be bounded by
\|f\|_{L^{2,1}}
.

3.4.
Generalization to the operator
e^{it\phi(D)}

In this subsection, we are concerned with the generalization
\bf{(G2)}
in the introduction.
In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the estimate (
2.3
) is the key ingredient.
To conclude that the same endpoint estimates hold for
e^{it\phi(D)}
, it is sufficient to show
the estimate (
2.3
) for
e^{it\phi(D)}
replacing
e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}=e^{it|D|}
, since the rest of the argument in the above proof works without modification.

Let
\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{1}=\{\xi:2^{-2}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{2}\}
. For
N\geq 1
, we set

\phi_{N}(\xi)=N^{-1}\phi(N\xi).

We show that the same estimates remain valid for
e^{it\phi(D)}

provided that

(3.12)

|\partial^{\alpha}(\phi_{N}(\xi)-|\xi|)|\leq CN^{-1}

for some constant
C>0
if
\xi\in\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{1}
and
|\alpha|\leq d+1
.
It is easy to check that (
1.7
) implies (
3.12
).

Let us set
\tilde{\beta}(p,q)=\beta(p,q)-\frac{d+1}{q}+\frac{d}{p}
. Let
U_{t}=e^{it\phi(D)}
or
e^{it|D|}=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}
. Consider the estimate

\|U_{t}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I_{\circ})}\leq CN^{\beta(p,q)}\|f\|_{L^{p}}

when
{\rm supp\,}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{A}_{N}
. By scaling, it follows that those estimates are respectively equivalent to

(3.13)

\|U_{t}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times NI_{\circ})}\leq CN^{\tilde{\beta}(p,q)}\|f\|_{L^{p}}

with
U_{t}=e^{it\phi_{N}(D)}
and
U_{t}=e^{it|D|}
when
{\rm supp\,}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{A}_{1}
. For our purpose, we only need to show that the estimate (
3.13
) for
U_{t}=e^{it\phi_{N}(D)}

follows from the corresponding estimate for
e^{it|D|}
when
{\rm supp\,}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{A}_{1}
. The converse is also true, as becomes clear below.

Let
\tilde{\beta}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(2^{-2},2^{2})
such that
\tilde{\beta}=1
on
[2^{-1},2]
. Let us set

a(\xi,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}e^{it(\phi_{N}(\xi)-|\xi|)}\tilde{\beta}(|\xi|).

Since
{\rm supp\,}\widehat{f}\subset\mathbb{A}_{1}
, we note that

e^{it\phi_{N}(D)}f(x)=\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}a(\xi,t)\widehat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

From (
3.12
) it follows that
|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(\cdot,t)|\leq C
for
|\alpha|\leq d+1
and
|t|\leq 2N
. Thus, expanding
a(\xi,t)
in the Fourier series
on
[-2\pi,2\pi]^{d}
gives
a(\xi,t)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}c_{\mathbf{k},t}e^{i\xi\cdot 2^{-1}\mathbf{k}}
with

|c_{\mathbf{k},t}|\leq C(1+|\mathbf{k}|)^{-d-1}
for all
|t|\leq 2N
. Consequently, it follows that

e^{it\phi_{N}(D)}f(x)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}c_{\mathbf{k},t}e^{it|D|}f(x+2^{-1}\mathbf{k})

for
|t|\leq 2N
. Therefore, the estimate (
3.13
) for
U_{t}=e^{it\phi_{N}(D)}

follows from that for
e^{it|D|}
. The converse can also be seen in the same manner.

4.
Proof of Propositions
1.2
,
1.4
, and
1.6

In this section we prove the three propositions
1.2
,
1.4
, and
1.6
, which concern the equivalence between the maximal estimates (
1.1
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), and (
1.6
).

We begin by noting that

(4.1)

U_{t}^{s}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x-y,t)f(y)dy,

where

(4.2)

K(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\frac{d\xi}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}}.

Recall
\beta
from Section 3 and let
\beta_{0}=1-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-j}\cdot)
. We set

(4.3)

\displaystyle K_{j}(x,t)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\frac{\beta(2^{-j}|\xi|)d\xi}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}},\quad j\geq 1,

(4.4)

\displaystyle K_{0}(x,t)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\frac{\beta_{0}(|\xi|)d\xi}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}}.

Thus, we have

K=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}K_{j}.

Henceforth, for simplicity, we set

I=(0,1),\quad{\mathbb{B}}=B^{d}(0,1).

4.1.
Proof of Proposition
1.2

Since the implication from (
1.1
) to (
1.4
) is trivial, we only need to show that (
1.4
) implies (
1.1
).
Recalling (
3.4
), we note that (
1.1
) is equivalent to

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|U_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{2}.

Let us set

\textstyle\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f=\sum_{j\geq 1}U_{t}^{s}P_{j}f.

Thanks to Lemma
3.1
, it is sufficient to show that

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{2}

while assuming that (
1.4
) holds, that is to say,

(4.5)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{B})}\leq C\|f\|_{2}.

Let
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q\}
be a collection of almost disjoint unit cubes that covers
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\bar{Q}
denote the cube of side length
5
that has the same center as
Q
so that
\operatorname{dist}(Q,\bar{Q}^{c})\geq 2
.
We have

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(Q)}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq 2(\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}),

where

\mathrm{I}^{q}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f_{\bar{Q}}\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(Q)}^{q},\quad\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}^{q}=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f_{\bar{Q}^{c}}\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(Q)}^{q}.

We will show that

(4.6)

\mathrm{I},\,\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}.

By translation, from (
4.5
) it follows that
\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f_{\bar{Q}}\big|\big\|_{L^{q}(Q)}\lesssim\|f_{\bar{Q}}\|_{2}
for all
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
. Thus, we have

\mathrm{I}\lesssim\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}\|f_{\bar{Q}}\|_{2}^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}}|f_{\bar{Q}}|^{q}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}.

To handle
\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}
, we use an estimate for the kernel of
\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}
.
Let us set
\tilde{K}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}K_{j}(x,t)
.
From (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) it is clear that

(4.7)

\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{K}(x-y,t)f(y)dy.

We claim that

(4.8)

|\tilde{K}(x,t)|\leq\mathcal{E}_{N}(x):=C(1+|x|)^{-N}

for any
N
if
t\in I
and
|x|\geq 2
. Indeed, by scaling we have

K_{j}(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}2^{dj}\int e^{i2^{j}(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\frac{\beta(|\xi|)d\xi}{(1+2^{2j}|\xi|^{2})^{s}}.

Since
t\in I
and
|x|\geq 2
,
|\nabla_{\xi}(x\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)|\geq|x|/2
. Thus, routine integration by parts gives

|K_{j}(x,t)|\lesssim 2^{(d-2s)j}(2^{j}|x|)^{-N}
for any
N
if
t\in I
and
|x|\geq 2
. Therefore, summation along
j\geq 1
gives the estimate (
4.8
).

Since
\operatorname{dist}(Q,\bar{Q}^{c})\geq 2
, using (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

\sup_{t\in I}|\tilde{U}_{t}^{s}f_{\bar{Q}^{c}}(x)|\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{N}\ast|f|(x)

for
x\in Q
. Therefore,

\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}\lesssim\|\mathcal{E}_{N}\ast|f|\|_{q}.

By choosing
N\geq d+1
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{N}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
. Thus,
\|\mathcal{E}_{N}\ast|f|\|_{q}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}
for any
p\leq q
. In particular, we have

\mathrm{I}\!\mathrm{I}\lesssim\|f\|_{2}
. Therefore, we get (
4.6
). This completes the proof.

Remark 4.1
.

In order to extend Proposition
1.2
to the general operator
e^{it\phi(D)}
, we consider the kernel
of the operator

From the perspective of the proof in the above, it is enough to show that

(4.9)

|K^{\phi}(x,t)|\lesssim(1+|x|)^{-d-1}

for
|x|\geq C
with

. By Littlewood-Paley decomposition and scaling it follows that

Now, we note from (

4.2.
Proof of Proposition

We only need to show the implication from (
1.4
) to (
1.5

For our purpose, recalling (
3.4
),
it is sufficient to show

(4.10)

\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|U_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{B}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}},

which is equivalent to (
1.5
),
while assuming that

(4.11)

\big\|\sup_{t\in I}\big|U_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{B}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

The last estimate (
4.11

1.4
). We begin by observing that there is a constant
C
, independent of

(4.12)

\big\|\sup_{t\in J}\big|U_{t}^{s}f\big|\big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{B}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}

holds for any unit interval
J
with a constant
C

4.11

is a unitary operator.

We now consider the dual forms of the estimates (
4.10
) and (
4.12
).
Let us set
T=U_{t}^{s}
and let
T^{*}
denote the adjoint operator of
T

T^{*}F(x)=\int(1-\Delta)^{-s/2}e^{-it^{\prime}\sqrt{-\Delta}}F(\cdot,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}.

For simpler notation, we set

\displaystyle g_{{\mathbb{B}}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\chi_{\mathbb{B}}(x)g(x,t),

\displaystyle g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}(x,t)

\displaystyle=g_{{\mathbb{B}}}(x,t)\chi_{J}(t).

By duality, it suffices to show that

(4.13)

\big\|\,T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}}}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}

holds provided that

(4.14)

\big\|\,T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}

holds for arbitrary unit interval
J
. It is clear that (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) are equivalent
to (
4.10
) and (
4.12
), respectively.

We proceed to show (
4.13
). Let
\mathfrak{J}=\{J\}
be a collection of almost disjoint unit intervals
J
such that

\mathbb{R}=\bigcup_{J\in\mathfrak{J}}J.

Consequently,
g_{{\mathbb{B}}}=\sum_{J}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}
. Thus, (

\Big|\sum_{J,J^{\prime}}\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle\Big|\leq C\|g_{{\mathbb{B}}}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}^{2}.

To this end, we divide

\sum_{J,J^{\prime}}\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle=\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J,J^{\prime}:\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})<4}\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J,J^{\prime}:\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})\geq 4}\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle.

We first consider
\mathcal{I}_{1}

)

|\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle|\leq\|T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{2}\|T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\|_{2}\lesssim\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}

|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\lesssim\sum_{J,J^{\prime}:\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})<4}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sum_{J}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{J^{\prime}}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}\Big)^{1/2}.

\Big(\sum_{J}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\big\|\big(\sum_{J}|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}\lesssim\big\|\sum_{J}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\big\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}.

Therefore, we obtain

|\mathcal{I}_{1}|\lesssim\|g_{{\mathbb{B}}}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}^{2}.

We now show that
\mathcal{I}_{2}
also has the same upper bound. To this end, we write

\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle=\big\langle g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},TT^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle

and make use of an estimate for the kernel estimates of

.
We note that

(4.16)

TT^{*}h(x,t)=\iint K(x-y,t-t^{\prime})h(y,t^{\prime})dydt^{\prime},

where
K
is given by (
4.2
).
To obtain the desired estimate for
\mathcal{I}_{2}
, the following estimate for

is crucial.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
|t|\geq 4
and

, then we have the estimate

|K(x,t)|\lesssim|t|^{-d}.

Proof of Lemma

.

Recalling (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we write the integral in the spherical coordinates and make a change of variables to get

K_{j}(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}K^{\theta}_{j}(x,t)d\theta,

where

K_{j}^{\theta}(x,t)=2^{dj}\int e^{i2^{j}r(x\cdot\theta+t)}\frac{\beta(r)r^{d-1}dr}{(1+2^{2j}r^{2})^{s}}.

Let
a_{j}(r)={\beta(r)r^{d-1}}{(1+2^{2j}r^{2})^{-s}}
. We now note that

K_{j}^{\theta}(x,t)=2^{dj}\int\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2^{j}(x\cdot\theta+t)}\frac{d}{dr}\Big)^{k}e^{i2^{j}r(x\cdot\theta+t)}\Big]a_{j}(r)dr.

Also, note that
|x\cdot\theta+t|\geq|t|/2
since
|t|\geq 4
and
|x|\leq 2
. Using the fact that
a_{j}^{(k)}=O(2^{-2sj})
for any

|K_{j}^{\theta}(x,t)|\leq C2^{(d-2s)j}(2^{j}|t|)^{-k}
for any
k\geq 0

Taking arbitrarily large

, we obtain

\sum_{j>0}|K_{j}^{\theta}(x,t)|\leq C|t|^{-N}

for any
N
. Consequently, integration over the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
gives

(4.17)

\sum_{j>0}|K_{j}(x,t)|\leq C|t|^{-N}

for any
N
.

We now consider the case
j=0
, which requires additional care since
\xi\mapsto|\xi|
is not smooth at the origin.
Note that
\beta_{0}(r)=0
if
r\geq 2
.
As before, we have
K_{0}(x,t)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}K^{\theta}_{0}(x,t)d\theta,

where

K_{0}^{\theta}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{2}e^{ir(x\cdot\theta+t)}a_{0}(r)dr

and
a_{0}(r)=\beta_{0}(r)r^{d-1}{(1+r^{2})^{-s}}
. Note that
a_{0}^{(k)}(0)=0
for

and
a_{0}^{(k)}(2)=0
for all
k
.
By integration by parts
d
times as above (it should be noted that

), we have

|K_{0}^{\theta}|\lesssim|t|^{-d}.

Integrating the estimates over the unit sphere yields the bound
|K_{0}(x,t)|\lesssim|t|^{-d}.

Therefore, combining this and the estimate (
4.17
) gives the desired estimate.
∎

By (
4.16
) and Lemma
4.2
,
we have

|\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times J}TT^{*}(\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times J^{\prime}}h)(x,t)|\lesssim\chi_{\mathbb{B}}(x)\iint\chi_{J}(t)|t-t^{\prime}|^{-d}\chi_{J^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})\chi_{\mathbb{B}}(y)|h(y,t^{\prime})|dydt^{\prime}

provided that
\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})\geq 4
. Thus, when
\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})\geq 4
, it follows that

\|\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times J}TT^{*}(\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times J^{\prime}}h)(x,\cdot)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}}\lesssim\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})^{-d}\iint\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times{\mathbb{B}}}(x,y)\|h(y,\cdot)\|_{L_{t}^{1}}dy.

Therefore, we have

(4.18)

\|\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times J}TT^{*}\chi_{{\mathbb{B}}\times J^{\prime}}h\|_{L_{x}^{r}L_{t}^{\infty}}\leq C(1+\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime}))^{-d}\|h\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{t}^{1}}

for any
1\leq p\leq r\leq\infty
if
\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})\geq 4
.
Since
\big\langle T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},T^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle=\big\langle g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},TT^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{J,J^{\prime}:\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})\geq 4}\big|\big\langle g_{{\mathbb{B}},J},TT^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{J,J^{\prime}:\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime})\geq 4}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}\|TT^{*}g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\|_{L_{x}^{q}L_{t}^{\infty}}.

Using the estimate (
4.18
) for
p=q^{\prime}
and
r=q
gives

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{2}|\leq C\sum_{J,J^{\prime}}(1+\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime}))^{-d}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J^{\prime}}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}.

Note
\sum_{J}(1+\operatorname{dist}(J,J^{\prime}))^{-d}\leq C
for any
J^{\prime}
and some constant
C>0
. The same also holds by replacing the roles of
J
and
J^{\prime}
. Therefore, Schur’s test gives

|\mathcal{I}_{2}|\leq C\sum_{J}\|g_{{\mathbb{B}},J}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}^{2}.

Consequently, using (
4.15
), we conclude that
|\mathcal{I}_{2}|\leq C\|g_{B}\|_{L_{x}^{q^{\prime}}L_{t}^{1}}^{2}.

This completes the proof.

4.3.
Proof of Proposition
1.6

Since
s_{c}(q,d)\geq 0
, the implication from (
1.6
) to (
1.4
) is trivial. Thus, we only need to prove that (
1.4
) implies (
1.6
).
From the inequality (
1.4
) and time reversal symmetry, it follows that

(4.19)

\Big\|\sup_{t\in(-1,1)}\Big|\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi\,+t|\xi|)}\chi_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\widehat{f}(\xi\,)|\xi|^{-s_{c}(q,d)}d\xi\Big|\Big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{B}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}

for all
f\in L^{2}
.
Let us set

g(x)=R^{d/2}f(Rx).

Since
\|g\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
, applying the inequality (
4.19
) to
g
gives

(4.20)

\Big\|R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sup_{t\in(-1,1)}\Big|\int e^{i(x\cdot\xi\,+t|\xi|)}\chi_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\widehat{f}(\xi/R\,)|\xi|^{-s_{c}(q,d)}d\xi\Big|\Big\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{B}})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
s_{c}(q,d)=d/2-d/q
, changing variables

, we have

\displaystyle R^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sup_{t\in(-1,1)}\Big|\int

\displaystyle e^{i(x\cdot\xi\,+t|\xi|)}\chi_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\widehat{f}(\xi/R\,)|\xi|^{-s_{c}(q,d)}d\xi\Big|

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{d}{q}}\sup_{t\in(-R,R)}\Big|\int e^{i(Rx\cdot\xi\,+t|\xi|)}\chi_{\{|\xi|\geq R^{-1}\}}\widehat{f}(\xi\,)|\xi|^{-s_{c}(q,d)}d\xi\Big|,

Therefore, combining this and (
4.20
), by the change of variables
x\rightarrow x/R
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\sup_{t\in(-R,R)}\Big|\int

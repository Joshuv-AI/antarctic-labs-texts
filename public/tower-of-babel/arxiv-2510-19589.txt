Analysis of Toeplitz Operators with B ​ M ​ O α 1 BMO^{1}_{\alpha} operator-valued symbols on ℓ 2 − \ell^{2}- Valued Bergman Spaces.
David Békollè, Hugues Olivier Defo, Edgar L. Tchoundja

Abstract
. As a class of compact operators on the ℓ 2 − \ell^{2}- valued Bergman space A α 2 ​ ( 𝔹 n , ℓ 2 ) A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}) on the unit ball 𝔹 n , \mathbb{B}_{n}, we study Toeplitz operators with B ​ M ​ O α 1 ​ ( 𝔹 n , ℒ ⁡ ( ℓ 2 ) ) BMO^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})) operator-valued symbols. First, we describe a method of restriction to a finite dimension which allows us to apply earlier results of Rahm and Wick; then we exhibit an explicit example of a compact Toeplitz operator on A α 2 ​ ( 𝔹 n , ℓ 2 ) . A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}). Secondly, we apply two sufficient conditions for compactness established by Rahm in infinite dimension. The first condition is in terms of the Toeplitz algebra 𝒯 L f ​ i ​ n ∞ , \mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}, the second one is in terms of sufficiently localized operators and is implied by the first condition. To get the second condition, we additionally assume that the symbol and its adjoint belong to B ​ M ​ O α 1 ​ ( 𝔹 n , ℒ ⁡ ( ℓ 2 , ℓ 1 ) ) . BMO^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})). Finally, inspired by Xia and Sadeghi-Zorboska, we ask the question of the validity of the reverse implication.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, we fix a nonnegative integer
n
and let
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\mathbb{C}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{C}
denote the
n-
dimensional complex Euclidean space. For
z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
and
w=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{n})
in
\mathbb{C}^{n},
we define the inner product of
z
and
w
by
\langle z,w\rangle=z_{1}\overline{w_{1}}+\cdots+z_{n}\overline{w_{n}},
where
\overline{w_{k}}
is the complex conjugate of
w_{k}.
The resulting norm is then
|z|=\sqrt{\langle z,z\rangle}=\sqrt{|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}}.

The open unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is the set
\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|z|<1\}.

When
\alpha>-1,
the weighted Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{d}\nu_{\alpha}
in
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is defined by
\mathrm{d}\nu_{\alpha}(z)=c_{\alpha}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\nu(z),
where
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

\mathrm{d}\nu
is the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
c_{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{n!\Gamma(\alpha+1)}
is the normalizing constant so that
\mathrm{d}\nu_{\alpha}
becomes a probability measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}.
Throughout this paper, we fix
\alpha\textgreater-1.

Let
E
be a complex Banach space. A
E-
valued function on the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a function
f:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow E.
The integral of a vector-valued function that we are going to use has been introduced by Bochner in
[
4
]
. The Bochner integral is defined similarly as the Lebesgue integral (see
[
7
]
for more information). We recall the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Hille’s Theorem)
.

Let
E
and
F
be two complex Banach spaces and let
T:E\rightarrow F
be a continuous linear operator. Let
\mu
be a positive
\sigma-
finite Borel measure on
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}.
If
f:{\mathbb{B}}_{n}\rightarrow E
is
\mu-
Bochner integrable, then
T(f)
is also
\mu-
Bochner integrable and we have

T\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}f(z)d\mu(z)\right)=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}T(f)(z)d\mu(z).

Let
0<p<\infty.
The Bochner-Lebesgue space
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
consists of all vector-valued measurable functions
f:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow E
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\|f(z)\|^{p}_{E}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\alpha}(z)<\infty.

The Bochner-Lebesgue space
L^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
consists of all vector-valued measurable functions
f:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow E
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)}:=esssup\{\|f(z)\|_{E}:z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\}<\infty.

The vector-valued Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
is the subspace of
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
consisting of
E-
valued holomorphic functions. When
1\leq p<\infty,
the vector-valued Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
is a Banach space with the
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
norm.
We denote by
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
the space of bounded
E-
valued holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}.
The proof of the following density result can be found in
[
6
]
and
[
5
]
.

Proposition 1.2
.

For all
1\leq p<\infty,
the subspace
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
is dense in the vector-valued Bergman space
A^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E).

For
E=\mathbb{C}
and
p=2,
it is well-known that the scalar-valued Bergman space
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. Let
w\in\mathbb{B}_{n};
the point evaluation reproducing kernel
K^{\alpha}_{w}
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
is defined by:

K_{w}^{\alpha}(z):=\dfrac{1}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{n+1+\alpha}},\hskip 28.45274ptz\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

The associated normalized kernel
k^{\alpha}_{w}
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
is given by

k^{\alpha}_{w}(z)=\dfrac{(1-|w|^{2})^{\frac{n+1+\alpha}{2}}}{(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{n+1+\alpha}},\hskip 28.45274ptz\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.

The orthogonal projector
P_{\alpha}
of
L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
onto
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
is called the Bergman projector. It is well known that for
f\in L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
and
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
we have

P_{\alpha}f(z):=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\overline{K^{\alpha}_{z}(w)}f(w)d\nu_{\alpha}(w).

This definition of
P_{\alpha}
extends naturally to
L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
and one has the following reproducing property:

P_{\alpha}f=f\quad(f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)).

For
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\setminus\{0\},
let
\varphi_{a}
be the involutive automorphism of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
defined by

\varphi_{a}(z)=\dfrac{a-P_{a}(z)-s_{a}Q_{a}(z)}{1-\langle z,a\rangle},\hskip 28.45274ptz\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

where
P_{a}(z)=\dfrac{\langle z,a\rangle}{|a|^{2}}a
is the orthogonal projector from
\mathbb{C}^{n}
onto the subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
spanned by
a,

Q_{a}=I-P_{a}
and
s_{a}=\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}.
When
a=0,
we simply define
\varphi_{a}(z)=-z.
We recall the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
E
be a complex Banach space and
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},E).

(1)

The Berezin transform of
b
is the mapping from
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}
to
E
defined by

\widetilde{b}(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}b(w){d\nu_{\alpha}(w)}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}).

(2)

The function
b
belongs to the space
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},E)
if

\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},E)}:=\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},E)}\textless\infty.

We recall the definition of a Toeplitz operator on a
E-
valued Bergman space.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
E
and
F
be two complex Banach spaces. Let
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F)).
The Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
is densely defined on
A^{p}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},E\right)
by

T_{b}f(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}b(w)(f(w))\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

for every
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E).

The following theorem is a result from
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
E
and
F
be two complex Banach spaces. Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F)).
The following two assertions are equivalent:

(1)

The Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
with symbol
b
extends to a continuous operator from the Bergman space
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},E)
to the Bergman space
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},F);

(2)

The Berezin transform
\widetilde{b}
of
b
belongs to
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F)),
i.e.

\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(E,F)}<\infty.

Moreover, we have

\|\widetilde{b}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))}\lesssim\left\|T_{b}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F))}\lesssim\|\widetilde{b}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))}+\|b\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))}.

Remark 1.6
.

We endow the Cartesian product
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))\times\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F))
with the norm

\left\|(b,T)\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))\times\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F))}=\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))}+\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F))}.

It is easy to show that the norms

\left\|(b,T_{b})\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))\times\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F))}\quad\rm{and}\quad\left\|\widetilde{\left\|b(\cdot)\right\|}_{\mathcal{L}(E,F)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}

are equivalent.

We call
E^{\star}
the topological dual space of the Banach space
E.
It was proved by Arragui and Blasco
[
2
]
(cf. also
[
5
, Remark 2.2.23]
) that the topological dual space of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
is identified with the space
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E^{\star})
under the pairing

\langle f,g\rangle_{\alpha,E}=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\langle f(z),g(z)\rangle_{E,E^{\star}}d\nu_{\alpha}(z).

We next define the Berezin transform of an operator
S\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)).

Definition 1.7
.

Let
E,F
be two Banach spaces and let
S\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F)).
The Berezin transform
\widetilde{S}
of
S
is the mapping from
\mathbb{B}_{n}
into
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
defined by

\langle\widetilde{S}(z)(x),y^{\star}\rangle_{F,F^{\star}}:=\langle S(k_{z}^{\alpha}x),k_{z}^{\alpha}y^{\star}\rangle_{\alpha,F},

for all
x\in E
and
y^{\star}\in F^{\star}.

It is easy to show that (cf. e.g.
[
5
, Remark 5.4.3]
) that for every
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E))
such that
\widetilde{\left\|b(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(E)}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}),
one has the identity
\widetilde{T_{b}}=\widetilde{b}.

We next recall the following definition.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
E
be a Banach space. For every
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
a unitary operator
U_{z}
is defined on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
by

\left(U_{z}f\right)(w)=\left(f\circ\phi_{z}\right)(w)k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\quad(f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E),w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}).

For every operator
S\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)),
we define the operator
S^{z}\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E))
by

S^{z}:=U_{z}SU_{z}.

In the sequel, on the one hand, we take
E=F=\ell^{2}
and we fix an orthonormal basis
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the separable Hilbert space
\ell^{2}.
We may also take
E=F=\mathbb{C},
in which case,
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{C}.
On the other hand, we shall denote by
d
a positive integer and we may also take
E=F=\mathbb{C}^{d}.
We mean by dimension the dimension of
E=F:
in particular, the dimension is one for
\mathbb{C},\hskip 5.69054ptd
for
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and infinity for
\ell^{2}.

We next record our main results. For the first one, we state the following definition.

Definition 1.9
.

The Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\alpha}
is the operator-norm closure in
\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}))
of the set of finite sums of finite products of Toeplitz operators with
L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)
symbols.

Our first result is a generalization of Corollary 3.6 of Sadeghi-Zorboska
[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
\beta\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
be such that
\widetilde{\beta}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})).
Then the Toeplitz operator
T_{\beta}
belongs to the
d-
dimensional Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\alpha}.

We rely on the following theorem due to
[
12
,
13
]
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}))
be a member of
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\alpha}.
The following three assertions are equivalent.

(1)

S
is compact;

(2)

For every
e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

\left\|S^{z}\left(e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n};

(3)

\lim\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\widetilde{S}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)}=0.

Combining Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.11
, it is easy to deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 1.12
.

Let
d
be a positive integer. Let
\beta\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
be such that
\widetilde{\beta}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})).
Then the following three assertions are equivalent.

(1)

The Toeplitz operator
T_{\beta}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d});

(2)

For every
e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},

\left\|T_{\beta}\left(k_{z}^{\alpha}e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n};

(3)

\lim\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\widetilde{\beta}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathbb{C}^{d}\right)}=0.

We also develop a method of reduction to a finite dimension. Let
d
be a positive integer. We call
M_{I_{(d)}}
the linear operator defined on
\ell^{2}
by

M_{I_{(d)}}(e)=\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}\langle e,e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}e_{j}.

This operator extends to the linear operator defined on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
as

M_{I_{(d)}}(f)(z)=\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}\langle f(z),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}e_{j}.

We have:

T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}f(z)=T_{b_{(d)}},

where the symbol
b_{(d)}:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})
is defined as follows:

b_{(d)}(w)(e_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcr}0&\rm if&1\leq i\leq d\\
b(w)(e_{i})&\rm if&i\geq d+1\end{array}\right..

Our method of reduction allows us to approximate
T_{b}
by the operator
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}},
where
M_{I^{(d)}}
is the linear operator defined on
\ell^{2}
by

M_{I^{(d)}}(e)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle e,e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}e_{j},

which extends to the linear operator defined on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
as

M_{I^{(d)}}(f)(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle f(z),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}e_{j}.

We easily point out that the sum
M_{I^{(d)}}+M_{I_{(d)}}
is the identity operator on
\ell^{2}
and on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).

The operator
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
is easily identified with an operator
T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
of
\mathcal{L}(A_{\alpha}^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}))
so that we can apply to it Corollary
1.12
. This leads us to the following theorem.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
The associated Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
if the following four conditions are satisfied.

(i)

b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
and
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).

(ii)

We have

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0;

(iii)

We have

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|{\left((b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\right)_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0;

(iv)

For every positive integer
d,
the following estimate holds.

\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\left(k_{z}^{\alpha}e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}=0.

To state the fourth result, we introduce the following definition drawn from Rahm
[
11
]
.

Definition 1.14
.

(1)

A function
u:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})
is in
L^{\infty}_{fin}
if
\langle u(\cdot)e_{j},e_{k}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
is non-zero for only finitely many indices
(j,k),
and each function
z\mapsto\langle u(z)e_{j},e_{k}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
is a bounded function on
\mathbb{B}_{n}.

(2)

The Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}
is defined as

\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}:=clos_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\left\{\sum_{l=1}^{L}\prod_{j=1}^{J}T_{u_{j,l}}:u_{j,l}\in L^{\infty}_{fin},J,L\textless\infty\right\},

where the closure is taken in the operator norm topology on
\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})).

The following result was proved in
[
11
, Corollary 3.5]
:

Theorem 1.15
.

Let the linear operator
S\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))
be in the Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}.
If
\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|S\left(k_{z}^{\alpha}e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\ell^{2})}=0
for every positive integer
d,
then
S
is compact.

We prove the following proposition.

Proposition 1.16
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
For every positive integer
d,
the operator
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}.
Furthermore, if

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=0,

then the operator
T_{b}
belongs to
\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}.

To state our last results, we need the following definition drawn from
[
11
]
.

Definition 1.17
.

An operator
S
on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
is said to be
sufficiently localized
if it satisfies the following two conditions

(1.1)

\sup\limits_{j=1,2,\cdots}\hskip 2.84526pt\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\langle(S^{z}e_{j})(u),e_{i}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|\right)^{p}{d\nu}_{\alpha}(u)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty

and

(1.2)

\sup\limits_{j=1,2,\cdots}\hskip 2.84526pt\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\langle(\left(S^{\star}\right)^{z}e_{j})(u),e_{i}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|\right)^{p}{d\nu}_{\alpha}(u)\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty

for some real number

In (

),
S^{\star}
denotes the adjoint operator of

Rahm
[
11
, Theorem 3.4]
established the following theorem. The notation
\left\|S\right\|_{e}
is for the essential norm of a bounded linear operator
S.

Theorem 1.18
.

Let
S:A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})\rightarrow A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
be a sufficiently localized linear operator. Suppose further that
\limsup\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|SM_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})\right)}=0.
Then:

(a)

\left\|S\right\|_{e}\simeq\sup\limits_{f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}):\left\|f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=1}\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S^{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})};

(b)

If
\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|S^{z}e\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=0
for every positive integer

then
S
must be compact.

Our last but one result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.19
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}))
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
and
\left(b(\cdot)\right)^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})).
Then the Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
is sufficiently localized.

Next, it is easy to deduce the following corollary from Theorem

and
\left(b(\cdot)\right)^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})).
Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

is a bounded operator on

(2)

Assume further that

(1.3)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0.

\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|T_{b}\left(k_{z}^{\alpha}e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=0
for every positive integer

2.
The Space
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))

2.1.
Preliminary results

Let
b(\cdot)
be a matrix function defined on
{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
with values in
\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}).
In the sequel, for two positive integers
i
and
j,
we shall denote
b_{ij}
the (scalar) entry function
\langle b(\cdot)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}.
Moreover, if
b_{ij}\in L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}),
we shall denote by
\widetilde{b_{ij}}
the (scalar) Berezin transform of
b_{ij}.

Lemma 2.1
.

1.

Let
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Then the Berezin transform
\widetilde{b_{ij}}
of the scalar function
b_{ij}
is given by

(2.1)

\widetilde{b_{ij}}(z)=\left(\widetilde{b}(z)\right)_{ij}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}).

2.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Then for all positive integers
i,j,
the scalar function
b_{ij}
belongs to
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
Moreover, if
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right),
then the Berezin transform
\widetilde{b_{ij}}
of
b_{ij}
is a bounded scalar function on
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}.
More precisely, we have

(2.2)

\left\|b_{ij}\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}\leq\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))};

and

(2.3)

\left\|\widetilde{b_{ij}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}\leq\left\|\widetilde{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}.

Proof.

1.

In view of the definition, for every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{b_{ij}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}b_{ij}(w)d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\langle b(w)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}b(w)d\nu_{\alpha}(w)\right)(e_{i}),e_{j}\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{b}(z)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{b}(z)\right)_{ij}.

For the third equality, we used Hille’s Theorem (Theorem
1.1
).

2.

We recall that

\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}=\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}

and in view of 1.,

\displaystyle\left\|b_{ij}\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\left(b_{ij}\circ\varphi_{z}\right)(w)-\widetilde{b_{ij}}(z)\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\langle\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)\left(e_{i}\right),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)_{ij}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w).

We have

\displaystyle\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\left(\langle b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\left(\langle b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)_{ij}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w).

Taking the supremum over
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we obtain the estimate (
2.2
).

Let us next show (
2.3
).
We have for every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}:

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\langle\widetilde{b}(z)(e),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left|\langle\widetilde{b}(z)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|=\left|\left(\widetilde{b}(z)\right)_{ij}\right|.

Combining with (
2.1
) and taking the supremum of both sides with respect to
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
the announced conclusion follows.

∎

2.2.
A class of examples of non compact Toeplitz operators with
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
operator-valued symbols on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})

The following class of examples was mentioned in the introduction of
[
11
]
. Let
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
be such that
\widetilde{\tau}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
We call
I
the identity on
\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})
and we write
b:=\tau I.
We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumptions on
\tau,
one has
\tau I\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
and
\widetilde{\tau I}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Moreover, for every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
one has
\widetilde{\tau I}(z)=\widetilde{\tau}(z)I.

Proof.

We first show that for every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
one has
\widetilde{\tau I}(z)=\widetilde{\tau}(z)I.
For every
e\in\ell^{2},
we have:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau I}(z)(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}(\tau I)(w)(e){d\nu_{\alpha}(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\tau(w)e{d\nu_{\alpha}(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\tau(w){d\nu_{\alpha}(w)}\right)e

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\tau}(z)I(e).

We next show that
\widetilde{\tau I}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
It follows from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{\tau I}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\widetilde{\tau I}(z)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{\tau}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}<\infty.

We finally show that
\tau I\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
For every
e\in\ell^{2}
such that
\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1,
we have:

\left((\tau I)\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau I}(z)\right)(e)=\left(\tau\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right)e.

So

\displaystyle\left\|\tau I\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau I}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell}^{2}=1}\left\|\left((\tau I)\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau I}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\tau\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|.

The hypothesis
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})
implies the announced conclusion.
∎

From Theorem
1.5
(
E=F=\ell^{2}
) and Lemma
2.2
, we deduce the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})
be such that
\widetilde{\tau}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
Then the Toeplitz operator
T_{\tau I}
is bounded on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).

We next prove the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

For the symbol
b=\tau I
just defined, the Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
is not a compact operator on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
unless the scalar symbol
\tau
vanishes identically.

Proof.

We assume that
T_{b}
is a compact operator on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).
We recall that for every
g\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
we have

\left\|g\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}^{2}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|\langle g(z),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}d\nu_{\alpha}(z)\textless\infty.

Then
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|\langle g(z),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}d\nu_{\alpha}(z)=0.

Next, for every scalar function
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
and every positive integer
j,
we get

\displaystyle\left|\langle fe_{j},g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\langle f(z)e_{j},g(z)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}f(z)\langle e_{j},g(z)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|\langle e_{j},g(z)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|\langle e_{j},g(z)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}d\nu_{\alpha}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used Hölder’s inequality in the last line.
We conclude that
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\langle fe_{j},g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=0.
In other words, the sequence
\{fe_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
viewed as a sequence of functions of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
converges weakly to zero on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).

Since
T_{b}
is compact, we must have

(2.5)

\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T_{b}(fe_{j})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=0.

But for every
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle T_{b}(fe_{j})(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}b(w)(f(w)e_{j})\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\tau(w)f(w)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)\right)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}f(z)\right)e_{j},

where
T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the scalar Toeplitz operator with scalar symbol
\tau,

so that

\left\|T_{b}(fe_{j})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=\left\|T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}

for every positive integer
j.
The equality (
2.5
) then gives that
T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}=0
on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
because of the density of
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
in
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
Finally, since
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{\tau}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}),
in view of Theorem
1.5
with
E=F=\mathbb{C},
we get
\left\|\widetilde{\tau(\cdot)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0,
which easily implies that
\tau\equiv 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{B}_{n}

[
8
, Proposition 2.6]
.
∎

For the particular case
\tau=\chi_{r\mathbb{B}_{n}},0\leq r\textless 1,
we deduce the following corollary which differs completely from the one-dimensional case; cf. e.g.
[
18
]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

The Toeplitz operator
T_{\chi_{r\mathbb{B}_{n}}I}
is not compact unless
r=0,
i.e. its symbol vanishes identically.

In this section, we recall two preliminary lemmas proved in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
. Let
E
and
F
be two complex Banach spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
b\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F)\right)
be such that
\widetilde{\left\|b(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(E,F)}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
Then the following estimate holds.

\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\log\left(\frac{e}{1-|w|^{2}}\right)\left\|b(w)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(E,F)}{d\nu}_{\alpha}(w)<\infty.

We next state the second lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
b:{\mathbb{B}}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(E,F)
be such that
\widetilde{\left\|b(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(E,F)}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
Then for all
f
in the dense space
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},E)
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},E)
and
g
in the dense space
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},F^{\star})
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},F^{\star})
we have:

\langle T_{b}f,g\rangle_{\alpha,F}=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle b(w)(f(w)),g(w)\rangle_{F,F^{\star}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w).

We next state our third lemma. Its assertion (3) follows from the first assertion of Definition
1.3
and Lemma
3.2
and its proof can be found in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F))
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(E,F)).
For every
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
the following three identities hold.

(1)

\left(T_{b}\right)^{z}=T_{b\circ\phi_{z}};

(2)

\left\|\left(T_{b}\right)^{z}\left(e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},F)}=\left\|T_{b}\left(k_{z}^{\alpha}e\right)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},F)};

(3)

\widetilde{T_{b}}=\widetilde{b}.

4.
Toeplitz operators with symbols in
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).

Througout this section,
d
denotes a positive integer. The linear operators
M_{I^{(d)}}
and
M_{I_{(d)}}
on
\ell^{2}
and on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
were defined in the introduction before Theorem
1.13
.

4.1.
A preliminary remark

Remark 4.1
.

For every bounded operator
A
on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
we denote by
\left\|A\right\|_{e}
the essential norm of
A
on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).
In particular, for every
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)
such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})),
we have the following inequalities:

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b}\right\|_{e}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b}-T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+\left\|T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b}\left(I-M_{I^{(d)}}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+\left\|T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{e}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{e}+\left\|T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}-M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{e}+\left\|M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{e}+\left\|M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))},

since
\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\leq 1.
For the second inequality, we deduced from the identity
M_{I^{(d)}}+M_{I_{(d)}}=I,
where
I
is the identity on
\ell^{2}
and on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
that

T_{b}-T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}=T_{b}\left(I-M_{I^{(d)}}\right)=T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}.

We deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Suppose further that

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=0.

The operator
T_{b}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
if the operator
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
for
d
large.

4.2.
A sufficient condition on the symbol
b
for the estimate

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})))}=0

We first prove the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right).
Then
T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}=T_{b_{(d)}},
where the symbol
b_{(d)}
is defined as follows:

(4.1)

b_{(d)}(w)(e_{i})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcr}0&\rm if&1\leq i\leq d\\
b(w)(e_{i})&\rm if&i\geq d+1\end{array}\right..

Proof.

For every
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
and every positive integer
d,
we have:

(4.2)

\displaystyle T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{b}\left(\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}f_{i}e_{i}\right)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}b(w)\left(\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}f_{i}(w)e_{i}\right)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}f_{i}(w)b(w)(e_{i})\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{b_{(d)}}f(z).

∎

We next prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).

The following two assertions hold.

(1)

b_{(d)}\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}));

(2)

\widetilde{b_{(d)}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).

Proof.

(1)

For every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we have:

\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|b_{(d)}\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b_{(d)}}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\left(b_{(d)}\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b_{(d)}}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w).

Notice that

\displaystyle\widetilde{b_{(d)}}(z)(e_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}b_{(d)}(w)(e_{i})\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcr}0&\rm if&1\leq i\leq d\\
\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}b(w)(e_{i})\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)&\rm if&i\geq d+1\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcr}0&\rm if&1\leq i\leq d\\
\widetilde{b}(z)(e_{i})&\rm if&i\geq d+1\end{array}\right.

Then, if we denote by
\ell^{2}_{(d)}
the subspace of
\ell^{2}
consisting of complex sequences whose first
d
terms are zero, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|b_{(d)}\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b_{(d)}}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{e\in\ell^{2}_{(d)},\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}<\infty.

(2)

We also have:

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{b_{(d)}}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\widetilde{b_{(d)}}(z)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{(d)}}=1}\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}<\infty.

For the second equality, we used the equation (
1
).

∎

In view of (
4.2
), Remark
1.6
and the previous lemma, we reach to the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
We assume that

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0.

Then

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=0.

The following lemma will be useful.

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
we have the equality:

\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}(z)=\\
\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\sup_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle b(w)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w).

Proof.

For every
w\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
we have:

\displaystyle\left\|b_{(d)}(w)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|b_{(d)}(w)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\langle b_{(d)}(w)(e),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle b_{(d)}(w)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle b(w)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the latter equality, we used the definition of
b_{(d)}
given in (
4.1
).
∎

4.3.
A sufficient condition on the symbol
b
for the estimate

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=0

It is easy to check that the operator
M_{I_{(d)}}
is self-adjoint on
\ell^{2},
so it is also self-adjoint as an operator on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).
We assume that
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
and is such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Hence the adjoint operator of
M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}
on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
is
\left(T_{b}\right)^{\star}M_{I_{(d)}},
where
\left(T_{b}\right)^{\star}
denotes the adjoint operator of
T_{b}.
For every
w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we denote
(b(w))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}
the adjoint operator of
b(w)
in
\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}).

We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Then

\left(T_{b}\right)^{\star}=T_{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}.

Moreover,
for all
e,h\in\ell^{2},
we have

(4.9)

\langle\widetilde{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}(z)(e),h\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\langle e,\widetilde{b}(z)(h)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

and
(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}
belongs to
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
with the same norm as
b.

Proof.

For all
f,g
in the dense space
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\ell^{2})
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
we have:

\displaystyle\langle f,\left(T_{b}\right)^{\star}g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle T_{b}f,g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}.=\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle T_{b}f(z),g(z)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}{d\nu}_{\alpha}(z).

In view of Lemma
3.2
, we get:

\displaystyle\langle f,\left(T_{b}\right)^{\star}g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle b(w)(f(w)),g(w)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle f(w),(b(w))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}g(w)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle P_{\alpha}f(w),(b(w))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}g(w)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle P_{\alpha}f,M_{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}g\rangle_{L^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\ell^{2})}=\langle f,P_{\alpha}M_{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,T_{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}g\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})},

since the Bergman projector
P_{\alpha}
reproduces
f
and it is self-adjoint on
L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).

An identification gives the announced result:

\left(T_{b}\right)^{\star}=T_{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}.

Next, we have

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}(z)(e),h\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}(b(w))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}(e)d\nu_{\alpha}(w),h\right\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\langle(b(w))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}(e),h\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\langle e,b(w)(h)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e,\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}b(w)(h)d\nu_{\alpha}(w)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e,\widetilde{b}(z)(h)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}.

For the second equality, we applied Hille’s Theorem (Theorem
1.1
). We have thus proved (
4.9
).
Moreover, from (
4.9
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\left\|(b(\varphi_{z}(w)))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}-\widetilde{(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\|e\|_{\ell^{2}}=1=\|h\|_{\ell^{2}}}\left|\langle e,\left(b(\varphi_{z}(w))-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(h)\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}(\cdot)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}.

Hence
\left\|(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}=\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))}.

∎

Replacing the symbol
b
by
(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}
in Proposition
4.5
, we reach to the following theorem.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
We assume that

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|{\left((b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\right)_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0.

Then

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|M_{I_{(d)}}T_{b}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=0.

5.
A method of reduction to the finite dimension

The subspace
\mathcal{S}^{(d)}
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})

For every positive integer
d,
we define the subspace
\mathcal{S}^{(d)}
of
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
as follows:

\mathcal{S}^{(d)}:=\left\{f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}):\langle f,e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\equiv 0\quad\forall j\geq d+1\right\}.

We define the mapping

\begin{array}[]{lccl}\mathcal{I}^{(d)}:&\mathcal{S}^{(d)}&\rightarrow&A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})\\
&f&\mapsto&f^{(d)}\end{array}

where
f^{(d)}\in A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})
is defined by
\langle f^{(d)},e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=\langle f,e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
for every integer
j
such that
1\leq j\leq d.

The proof of the following lemma is left as an exercise.

Lemma 5.1
.

(1)

The mapping
\mathcal{I}^{(d)}
is an isometric isomorphism with inverse operator
\mathcal{J}^{(d)}
defined by

\begin{array}[]{clcr}\mathcal{J}^{(d)}:&A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})&\rightarrow&\mathcal{S}^{(d)}\\
&g=\sum_{i=1}^{d}g_{i}e_{i}&\mapsto&f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{j}e_{j},\end{array}

with
f_{j}=g_{j}
if
1\leq j\leq d
and
f_{j}\equiv 0
otherwise.

(2)

The map

\begin{array}[]{clcr}\mathcal{I}^{(d)}\circ M_{I^{(d)}}:&A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})&\rightarrow A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})\\
&f&\mapsto(M_{I^{(d)}}f)^{(d)}\end{array}

is onto.

Let
d
be a positive integer. The space
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
consists of
d\times d
matrices with complex entries. We denote by
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d}))
the space of functions
\varphi:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
such that the function
z\mapsto\left\|\varphi(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})}
is in
L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C}\right).
Note that it is not particularly important which norm is used on
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
since
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is finite-dimensional.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{B}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}\right)
and
d=1,2,\dots.
We associate the mapping
\varphi^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}:{\mathbb{B}}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
as follows: for all
1\leq k,j\leq d,
we have

(5.1)

\langle\varphi^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=\langle\varphi(w)(\widetilde{e_{k}}),\widetilde{e_{j}}\rangle_{\ell^{2}},

where
\widetilde{e_{k}}=(e_{k},0)
in
\ell^{2}.

We shall abuse the notation
\widetilde{e_{k}}\equiv e_{k}.

We show the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
d=1,2,\dots.

(1)

For every
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right),
we have
b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}\in L^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)
with the estimate

\left\|b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}\right\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)}\leq\left\|b\right\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)}.

Moreover, we have the identity
\widetilde{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}=\left(\widetilde{b}\right)^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}
and if
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right),
the following estimate holds

(5.2)

\left\|\widetilde{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)}\leq\left\|\widetilde{b}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)}.

(2)

For every
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right),
we have
b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)
with the estimate

\left\|b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)}\leq\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)}.

Proof.

(1)

We have:

\displaystyle\left\|b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}\right\|_{L^{1}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner})(w)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=\left\|h\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\left|\langle b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}(w)(e),h\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\sum_{k=1}^{d}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\mu_{k}|^{2}=1}\left|\sum_{k,j=1}^{d}\lambda_{k}\overline{\mu_{j}}\langle b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{k}|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{k}|^{2}=1}\left|\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\overline{\mu_{j}}\langle b(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=\left\|h\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left|\langle b(w)(e),h\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|b(w)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)=\left\|b\right\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)}.

For all
k,j\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
and
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
we have:

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}(z)e_{k},e_{j}\rangle)_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)|^{2}\langle b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}(w)e_{k},e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)|^{2}\langle b(w)e_{k},e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{b}(z)e_{k},e_{j}\rangle)_{\ell^{2}}.

The estimate (
5.2
) follows easily.

(2)

The assertion (2) follows easily from the assertion (1).

∎

For
b\in L^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right),
we call
T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
the Toeplitz operator with symbol
b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}
densely defined on
A^{2}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}\right).
We deduce the following corollary:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)
be such that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
Then
T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
is bounded on
A^{2}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}\right).

For every
f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}e_{k}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
and every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we have:

\displaystyle M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}f(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T_{b}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}f_{k}e_{k}\right)(z),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}b(w)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}f_{k}(w)e_{k}\right)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}f_{k}(w)\langle b(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)e_{j}.

We next consider the Toeplitz operator
T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
with symbol
b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}
defined on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}).

For every
g=\sum_{k=1}^{d}g_{k}e_{k}\in A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}),
we have:

(5.3)

\displaystyle T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}g(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}g_{k}e_{k}\right)(z),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}(w)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}g_{k}(w)e_{k}\right)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}g_{k}(w){b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}(w)(e_{k})\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}g_{k}(w)\langle b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}g_{k}(w)\langle b(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)e_{j},

where the latter equality follows from (
5.1
).
Taking into account the assertion (2) of Lemma
5.1
, we have proved the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

For every
f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
the following equality holds.

(5.4)

M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}f=\mathcal{J}^{(d)}T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\left(M_{I^{(d)}}f\right)^{(d)}.

In other words,

(5.5)

M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}=\mathcal{J}^{(d)}T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\mathcal{I}^{(d)}M_{I^{(d)}}.

We next prove the following proposition.

Proposition 5.6
.

The following two assertions are equivalent.

(1)

M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
is bounded (resp. compact) on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2});

(2)

T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
is bounded (resp. compact) on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}).

Moreover, the operator norms
\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}
and
\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})\right)}
are equal.

Proof.

(i)

Assume that
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
is bounded on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).
Notice that on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
the following identity holds:
M_{I^{(d)}}\circ M_{I^{(d)}}=M_{I^{(d)}}.
In view of (
5.4
), for every
f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
since
\mathcal{J}^{(d)}
is an isometry, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\left(M_{I^{(d)}}f\right)^{(d)}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{J}^{(d)}T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\left(M_{I^{(d)}}f\right)^{(d)}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}M_{I^{(d)}}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\left\|\left(M_{I^{(d)}}f\right)^{(d)}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})},

by assertion (1) of Lemma
5.1
.
Since by assertion (2) of Lemma
5.1
, the map

\begin{array}[]{clcr}A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})&\rightarrow A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})\\
f&\mapsto(M_{I^{(d)}}f)^{(d)}\end{array}

is onto, this implies that for every
g\in A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}),
we have:

\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}g\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\leq\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\left\|g\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}.

In other words,

\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}))}\leq\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}.

Conversely,
the announced conclusion then follows from the identity (
5.5
), since
\mathcal{I}^{(d)}
and
\mathcal{J}^{(d)}
are isometries and
M_{I^{(d)}}
is bounded on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
with norm 1.

(ii)

Assume first that the operator
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).
This means that we have the implication:

\{f_{m}\}\rightarrow 0
weakly on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})\Rightarrow\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\rightarrow 0\quad(m\rightarrow\infty).

To deduce the compactness of
T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}),
we need to show the following implication:

\{g_{m}\}\rightarrow 0
weakly on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})\Rightarrow\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}g_{m}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\rightarrow 0\quad(m\rightarrow\infty).

We assume that
\{g_{m}\}\rightarrow 0
weakly on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})\quad(m\rightarrow\infty).
By assertion (2) of Lemma
5.1
, for every positive integer
m,
there exists
f_{m}\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
such that
g_{m}=\left(M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m}\right)^{(d)}.

weakly on

For every
\varphi\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
we have:

\displaystyle\langle M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m},\varphi\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m},\varphi\rangle_{\ell^{2}}{d\nu}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m},M_{I^{(d)}}\varphi\rangle_{\ell^{2}}{d\nu}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle\left(M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m}\right)^{(d)},\left(M_{I^{(d)}}\varphi\right)^{(d)}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}{d\nu}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle g_{m},\left(M_{I^{(d)}}\varphi\right)^{(d)}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}{d\nu}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle g_{m},\left(M_{I^{(d)}}\varphi\right)^{(d)}\rangle_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}\rightarrow 0\quad(m\rightarrow\infty).

In view of the hypothesis, by the identity (

), we obtain that

\left\|T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}g_{m}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})}=\left\|M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}f_{m}\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\rightarrow 0\quad(m\rightarrow\infty).

We used the fact that
\mathcal{I}^{(d)}
is an isometry.

Conversely, the compactness of
M_{I^{(d)}}T_{b}M_{I^{(d)}}
on

follows from that of

on

in view of the identity (
5.5
).

6.
A class of examples of compact Toeplitz operators on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
with symbols in
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))

Let
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})
be such that
\widetilde{\tau}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}).
We define a function
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
by

b(z)(e_{i})=\frac{\tau(z)}{2^{i}}e_{i}\quad(z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},i=1,2,\cdots).

Associated to
b
is the diagonal Toeplitz operator
T_{b}.
We state the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
\widetilde{\tau}(z)\rightarrow 0
as
z\rightarrow\partial{\mathbb{B}}_{n}.
Then the Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).

Proof.

In view of Theorem
5.7
, we shall check the following four facts:

(1)

b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}))
and
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}));

(2)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0;

(3)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|{\left((b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\right)_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0;

(4)

T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})
for every positive integer
d.

(1)

We first show that
\widetilde{b}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
For every
e=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}=1
and every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{b}(z)(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}b(w)(e)d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{2^{i}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\tau(w)e_{i}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{2^{i}}\widetilde{\tau}(z)e_{i}.

So

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\widetilde{b}(z)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left|\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|\lambda_{i}|}{2^{i}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left|\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|.

The conclusion follows from the boundedness of the scalar function
\widetilde{\tau}.

We next show that
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})).
For every
e=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}e_{i}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1
and all
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},

we have:

\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)\left(e_{i}\right).

So

\displaystyle\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}=1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{2^{i}}\left(\tau\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right)e_{i}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{2}=1}\left\{\left|\tau\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|\lambda_{i}|}{2^{i}}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\tau\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|.

The hypothesis
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})
implies the announced conclusion.

(2)

We now prove that

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0.

By Lemma
4.6
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\left\|b_{(d)}(\cdot)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle b(w)(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{i=d+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\frac{\tau(w)}{2^{i}}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{j}\frac{\tau(w)}{2^{j}})\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left|k_{z}^{\alpha}(w)\right|^{2}\left|\tau(w)\right|d\nu_{\alpha}(w)\times\sup\limits_{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}=1}\left(\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\lambda_{j}\right|^{2}}{2^{2j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{\left|\tau(\cdot)\right|}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}\left(\sum_{j=d+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{2j}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The conclusion follows from the convergence of the series
\sum\frac{1}{2^{2j}}
and the boundedness of
\widetilde{\left|\tau(\cdot)\right|}.
The latter follows from the assumptions
\tau\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{\tau}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})
according to Remark
1.6
in the particular case
E=F=\mathbb{C}.

(3)

We next have to prove that

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\widetilde{\left\|{\left((b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}\right)_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=0.

The proof is the same as for (2) when
b
is replaced by
(b(\cdot))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}

using the equality

\langle(b(w))^{\star}_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})}(e_{i}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\langle(e_{i},b(w)e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\frac{\overline{\tau(w)}}{2^{j}}\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}.

(4)

We finally show that
T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})
for every positive integer
d.
For every
g\in A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}),
it follows from
5.3
that

\displaystyle T_{b^{\llcorner(d)\lrcorner}}g(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle g(w),e_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\langle b(w)(e_{k}),e_{j}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle g(w),e_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\tau(w)\frac{1}{2^{k}}\langle e_{k},e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\langle g(w),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\tau(w)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{2^{j}}T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}\left(\langle g(\cdot),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)(z)e_{j}.

It then suffices to prove that the operator

\begin{array}[]{ccl}A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})&\rightarrow&A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})\\
g&\mapsto&T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}\left(\langle g(\cdot),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\right)\end{array}

is compact for every
j=1,2,\cdots,d.
Indeed, this operator is the composition of the linear continuous operator

\begin{array}[]{ccl}A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})&\rightarrow&A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})\\
g&\mapsto&\langle g(\cdot),e_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}\end{array}

and the linear compact operator

\begin{array}[]{ccl}A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})&\rightarrow&A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C})\\
\varphi&\mapsto&T_{\tau}^{\mathbb{C}}\varphi.\end{array}

The latter operator is compact in view of Theorem 1 of Zorboska
[
18
]
for
n=1
and Corollary 4.4 of
[
1
]
for
n\geq 2
both for
\alpha=0.
Cf.
[
17
]
for all
\alpha\textgreater-1.

∎

Proof of Theorem 1.10

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\epsilon

by (

For two scalar bounded symbols

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{1}}\left(T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{2}}g_{k}(\cdot)\right)(z)e_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}a^{2}(u)g_{k}(u)\overline{K_{w}^{\alpha}(u)}{d\nu}_{\alpha}(u)e_{j}\right)a^{1}(w)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}T_{A_{k,j}^{2}}g(w)a^{1}(w)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w),

We similarly denote by
A_{j}^{1}:{\mathbb{B}}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})
the symbol defined as follows.

\langle A_{j}^{1}(w)(e_{l}),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{clcr}a^{1}(w)&\rm if&l=i=j\\
0&\rm otherwise\end{array}\right..

So for every
G\in A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d})
and every
i\in\{1,\cdots,d\},
we have:

(7.6)

\displaystyle\langle A_{j}^{1}(w)(G(w)),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}G_{l}(w)\langle A_{j}^{1}(w)(e_{l}),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{j}(w)\langle A_{j}^{1}(w)(e_{j}),e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{j}(w)a^{1}(w)\delta_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{j}(w)a^{1}(w)\langle e_{j},e_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}.

This implies that

(7.7)

A_{j}^{1}(w)(G(w))=G_{j}(w)a^{1}(w)e_{j}.

Next, for
g\in A^{2}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathbb{C}^{d}),
from (
7.5
) we obtain that

\displaystyle\left(T_{A_{j}^{1}}\circ T_{A_{k,j}^{2}}\right)g(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{A_{j}^{1}}\left(T_{A_{k,j}^{2}}g(\cdot)\right)(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{A_{j}^{1}}\left(T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{2}}g_{k}(\cdot)e_{j}\right)(z).

This implies that

\displaystyle\left(T_{A_{j}^{1}}\circ T_{A_{k,j}^{2}}\right)g(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}A_{j}^{1}(w)\left(T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{2}}g_{k}(w)e_{j}\right)\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{2}}g_{k}(w)a^{1}(w)e_{j}\overline{K_{z}^{\alpha}(w)}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{1}}\circ T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{2}}\right)g_{k}(z)e_{j}.

The last but one equality follows from (
7.7
).
We finally point out that the symbols
A_{k,j}^{2}
and
A_{j}^{1}
belong to
L^{\infty}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{C}^{d})\right).
Generalizing this process to the more general composition product
\prod_{l=1}^{L_{k,j}}T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a^{l,m}},
from (

) we reach to the conclusion that the Toeplitz operator
T_{B_{k,j}}
lies in the
d-
dimensional Toeplitz algebra
\mathcal{T}^{d}_{\alpha}.

10.
Sufficiently localized operators

10.1.
The Space
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}))

Two Banach spaces
E
and
F
are said to be compatible if they are continuously embedded in a Hausdorff topological vector space
Z.
In this case, equipped with the norm
\left\|x\right\|:=\max\left\{||x||_{E},||x||_{F}\right\},

the vector space
E\cap F
is a Banach space. An example is given by the Banach sequence spaces
\ell^{1}
and
\ell^{2}
which are continuously embedded in the Hausdorff topological vector space consisting of infinite complex sequences, endowed with the convergence in measure (the counting measure). Equipped with the norm
\left\|x\right\|:=\max\left\{||x||_{\ell^{1}},||x||_{\ell^{2}}\right\},
the space
\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}
is then a Banach space.

Lemma 10.1
.

The following identity holds.

BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})\right)=BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)\cap BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})\right)

with equivalence of norms.

Proof.

(1)

We first show that
\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})=\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\cap\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})
with equivalence of norms. For
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}),
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\max\left\{\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}},\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{r}}\qquad(r=1,2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{r})}\qquad(r=1,2).

So

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})},\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\lx@nobreakspace\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\cap\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})}.

Conversely, for
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\cap\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}),
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\lx@nobreakspace\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\cap\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})},\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}},\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}}\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}+\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\{\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}}+\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}}\lx@nobreakspace\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\max\left\{\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{2}},\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sup\limits_{\|e\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\Phi(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\Phi\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})}.

(2)

We next show that

BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})\right)=BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)\cap BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})\right)

with equivalence of norms. For
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})\right),
in view of the definition and the first part of the proof, we have, for every
r=1,2.
:

\displaystyle\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}\right))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}_{n}}\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\tilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{B}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\tilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{r})}d\nu_{\alpha}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{r})\right)}

We deduce that

\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}\cap\ell^{2}\right))}\geq\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)\cap BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})\right)}.

Conversely, for
b\in BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)\cap BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})\right),

we have:

\displaystyle\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)\cap BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|b\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})\right)},\left\|b\right\|_{BMO^{1}_{\alpha}\left({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1})\right)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

11.1.
Working again the class of examples from Subsection

The symbol
b=\tau I
studied in Section

does not belong to
BMO^{1}_{\alpha}({\mathbb{B}}_{n},\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}))
unless
\tau
is constant a.e., and its associated Toeplitz operator
T_{b}
does not satisfy the estimate
\limsup\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|T_{b}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}=0
unless the symbol
\tau
vanishes identically. Indeed, on the one hand, for all
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we have:

\displaystyle\left\|b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2},\ell^{1}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|\left(b\circ\varphi_{z}(w)-\widetilde{b}(z)\right)(e)\right\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\tau\circ\varphi_{z}-\widetilde{\tau}(z)\right|\cdot\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{1}}.

Taking
e

i,

\langle e,e_{i}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\pi}\frac{1}{i},
the following holds:

\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{1}}=\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i}=\infty.
So
\sup\limits_{\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=1}\left\|e\right\|_{\ell^{1}}=\infty.
The only way forward is that for every
z\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n},
we have
\tau\circ\varphi_{z}(w)=\widetilde{\tau}(z)
for almost every
w\in\mathbb{B}_{n}.
In particular, for
z=0,
we have
\tau(w)=\widetilde{\tau}(0)
for almost every
w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}.
In other words,
\tau
is constant a.e.

On the other hand, for every scalar function
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})
such that
\left\|f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\mathbb{C})}=1,
the
\ell^{2}

f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
, we have

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\hskip 5.69054pt\left\|S^{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}=0
and
S\in\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}.

(3)

S
is the limit in the operator norm of a sequence
\{S_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
of sufficiently localized operators which satisfy the following two conditions.

(i)

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|S_{m}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right))}=0
for every positive integer
m;

(ii)

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\hskip 5.69054pt\left\|S_{m}(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}=0,
for every positive integer
d.

(4)

S
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right).

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Apply Theorem
12.2
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)

For a general operator
S\in\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})),
Rahm
[
11
]
showed in the proof of his Corollary 3.5 (Theorem
1.15
above) that the assumption
S\in\mathcal{T}_{L^{\infty}_{fin}}
implies that
S
is the limit in the operator norm of a sequence
\{S_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
of sufficiently localized operators which satisfy the condition

\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\left\|S_{m}M_{I_{(d)}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right))}=0

for all positive integers
m.
Moreover, for all positive integers
d
and
m,
every
e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that
\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1,
and every
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

we have:

\displaystyle\left\|S_{m}(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|S(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}+\left\|\left(S_{m}-S\right)(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|S(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}+\left\|S_{m}-S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right))}.

Since
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\left\|S_{m}-S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right))}=0,
then for every
\epsilon\textgreater 0,
there exists a positive integer
m_{0}=m_{0}(\epsilon)
such that for every positive integer
m\textgreater m_{0},
the following implication holds.

(12.2)

m\textgreater m_{0}\Rightarrow\left\|S_{m}-S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right))}\textless\epsilon.

Then for every
m\textgreater m_{0},
for every positive integer
d
and every
e\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
such that
\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1,

we obtain:

\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\hskip 5.69054pt\left\|S_{m}(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}\leq\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}+\epsilon=0+\epsilon=\epsilon,

because in view of
(2),
we have

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}=0.

We conclude that

\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\hskip 5.69054pt\left\|S_{m}(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}\leq\epsilon

for every
m\textgreater m_{0}
and for every positive integer
d.

In other words,

\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup\limits_{e\in\mathbb{C}^{d},\left\|e\right\|_{\mathbb{C}^{d}}=1}\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\hskip 5.69054pt\left\|S_{m}(k_{z}^{\alpha}e)\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}\right)}=0

for every positive integer
d.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)

According to Theorem
12.4
, for every
\epsilon>0,
there are a finite double subsequence
\{\lambda_{i,j},\hskip 2.84526pti=1,\cdots,I;\hskip 2.84526ptj=1,\cdots,J\},
a finite subsequence
\{a_{j}:j=1,\cdots,J\lx@nobreakspace\}
of points of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
and a function
g\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})
such that

f=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\lambda_{i,j}k_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}e_{i}+g,

with

\left\|g\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}<\epsilon.
Then for every
z\in\mathbb{B}_{n},

we have

S_{m}U_{z}f=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\lambda_{i,j}S_{m}U_{z}(k_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}e_{i})+S_{m}U_{z}g

and consequently

\displaystyle\left\|S_{m}U_{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|\left\|S_{m}U_{z}(k_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}e_{i})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}+\left\|S_{m}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\left\|g\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|\left\|S_{m}U_{z}(k_{a_{j}}^{\alpha}e_{i})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}+\left\|S_{m}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\epsilon.

.

We record the following identity. For
z,a\in\mathbb{B}_{n},
there exists a complex unimodular number
\gamma_{z,a}
such that

U_{z}k_{a}^{\alpha}=\gamma_{z,a}k^{\alpha}_{\varphi_{z}(a)}.

More explicitly,
\gamma_{z,a}=\frac{\left|1-\langle z,a\rangle\right|}{1-\langle z,a\rangle}.

In view of (
12.2
),
we take
m
so large so that
\left\|S_{m}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\leq 2\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}.

Hence

\left\|S_{m}U_{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\leq 2\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\epsilon+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|\left\|S_{m}(k_{\varphi_{z}(a_{j})}^{\alpha}e_{i})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}.

We point out that for every
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n},\hskip 5.69054pt\varphi_{z}(a)\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}
as
z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}.
In view of assertion (ii) of (3), for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,I\}
and
j\in\{1,\cdots,J\},
there exists a positive integer
m_{0}
such that

\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S_{m}(k_{\varphi_{z}(a_{j})}^{\alpha}e_{i})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\textless\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{IJ}}

for all
m\textgreater m_{0}.

We then obtain

\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S_{m}U_{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\leq 2\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\epsilon+\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{IJ}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|.

By the Schwarz inequality, we obtain:

\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{IJ}.

So for every
f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}),
we get

\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S_{m}U_{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\leq 2\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}\epsilon+\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon.

We next assume that
\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=1.
It follows from (
12.1
) that

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{i,j}\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 1.

In view of assertion i) of Theorem
1.18
, this implies that

\left\|S_{m}\right\|_{e}\simeq\sup\limits_{f\in A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}):\left\|f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}=1}\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}}\left\|S_{m}^{z}f\right\|_{A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2})}\lesssim\left(\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+1\right)\epsilon

for
m
large enough (
m>m_{0}).
For such a
m,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|S\right\|_{e}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|S_{m}\right\|_{e}+\left\|S_{m}-S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+1\right)\epsilon+\epsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}))}+2\right)\epsilon

by (
12.2
) and the previous estimate.
Since
\epsilon\textgreater 0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\left\|S\right\|_{e}=0,
i.e.
S
is compact on
A^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{B}_{n},\ell^{2}).

(4)\Rightarrow(1)

Apply the implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
of Theorem
12.3
.

A higher order polytopal method for contact mechanics with Tresca friction
Jerome Droniou, Raman Kumar, Roland Masson, Ritesh Singla

Abstract
. In this work, we design and analyze a Discrete de Rham (DDR) scheme for a contact mechanics problem involving fractures along which a model of Tresca friction is considered. Our approach is based on a mixed formulation involving a displacement field and a Lagrange multiplier, enforcing the contact conditions, representing tractions at fractures. The approximation space for the displacement is made of vectors values attached to each vertex, edge, face, and element, while the Lagrange multiplier space is approximated by piecewise constant vectors on each fracture face. The displacement degrees of freedom allow reconstruct piecewise quadratic approximations of this field. We prove a discrete Korn inequality that account for the fractures, as well as an inf-sup condition (in a non-standard H − 1 / 2 H^{-1/2} -norm) between the discrete Lagrange multiplier space and the discrete displacement space. We provide an in-depth error analysis of the scheme and show that, contrary to usual low-order nodal-based schemes, our method is robust in the quasi-incompressible limit for the primal variable (displacement). An extensive set of numerical experiments confirms the theoretical analysis and demonstrate the practical accuracy and robustness of the scheme.

1.
Introduction

The study of Contact mechanics in fractured porous media plays a crucial role in subsurface applications where understanding the complex interplay between mechanical deformation, fracture behavior, and fluid flow is essential for accurate prediction and risk assessment. A prime example of this is
\text{CO}_{2}
sequestration, where the action of injecting the gas can create a buildup of pressure that may eventually cause the reactivation of faults by changing pore pressure and normal stress on fracture surfaces, thus posing risks of deformation of rocks,
\text{CO}_{2}
leakage
[
1
]
and seismic activity
[
31
]
. Fractures are classically represented as a network of planar surfaces connected to the surrounding matrix domain, resulting in mixed-dimensional models that have been extensively studied in poromechanics
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
9
,
8
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
39
]
. The movement along fractures is governed by nonlinear frictional laws arising from inequality constraints imposed by non-penetration conditions and the non-smooth nature of friction laws. The Tresca friction model, which prescribes a yield threshold for tangential traction independent of normal traction, provides a mathematically tractable framework while capturing essential friction behavior
[
13
]
. More generally, the Coulomb friction law, where the tangential traction threshold depends on the normal stress, better represents physical friction but introduces additional nonlinearity
[
12
]
.

Capturing the geometric complexity of fracture networks with their corners and intersections typically requires a robust and accurate discretization method. Traditional finite element methods (FEM), while well-established for regular meshes, often struggle with non-simplicial element shapes that naturally arise in geological applications
[
10
,
21
]
. This challenge has motivated the development of polytopal discretization methods offering greater geometric flexibility while maintaining accuracy and stability. Several families of polytopal methods have emerged in computational mechanics over the past two decades, including Hybrid High Order (HHO)
[
17
,
21
]
, Discontinuous Galerkin
[
34
]
, Virtual Element Methods (VEM)
[
4
]
, and the Discrete De Rham (DDR) method
[
18
,
22
]
. Some of these have been extended to account for contact mechanics, such as: VEM on non-conforming meshes
[
15
]
, HHO combined with Nitsche’s contact formulation
[
14
]
, VEM for Nitsche frictionless contact
[
37
]
and Coulomb frictional contact mechanics
[
36
]
, and VEM with bubble stabilisation
[
28
]
. So far, DDR has not been considered for contact mechanics, but it is a natural candidate for mechanics problems since it provides a nodal-based discrete version of the
H^{1}
space; it moreover already proved efficient in approximating solid and fluid mechanics models, including Navier-Stokes equations
[
16
]
, Maxwell equations on manifolds
[
26
]
, Yang-Mills equations
[
24
]
, as well as the Reissner-Mindlin and Kirchoff-Love plate problems
[
19
,
20
]
.

The discretization of contact mechanics has been an active area of research, driven by the challenges of variational inequalities, the nonlinearity of the frictional constraints, and the need to ensure stability under unilateral conditions. For mixed-dimensional models with fractures represented as co-dimension one manifolds (e.g., 2D surfaces in a 3D medium), additional complexities arise due to intersections, corners, and tips. The mixed-dimensional framework for flow in fractured porous media was pioneered in
[
38
,
3
]
, in which discrete fracture-matrix (DFM) models are developed to represent fractures as lower-dimensional manifolds embedded in the porous matrix. In mixed formulation with Lagrange multipliers, surface tractions are introduced as independent unknowns, with the Lagrange multiplier representing the traction at the contact interface, and enforcing dual cone constraint
[
11
,
2
]
. A fundamental requirement for well-posedness is the satisfaction of a discrete inf-sup condition between displacement and multiplier spaces.

[
5
]
introduced the
\mathbb{P}_{1}-
bubble FEM discretization for the Signorini problem, enriching the displacement space with bubble functions to satisfy the inf-sup condition. In the polytopal framework, the recent work of
[
28
]
extended the bubble-stabilized mixed formulation to the first-order VEM framework for contact mechanics with Tresca friction.
Furthermore,
[
40
]
proposed an abstract framework for the
\penalty\ priori
error analysis of 3D frictionless contact problems using quadratic finite elements. They established an
\mathcal{O}(h^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}})
convergence for
\frac{1}{2}<\mathfrak{r}<1
, when the solution belongs to
H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}
, providing
H^{1}
and
H^{-1/2}
-norm bounds for displacement and traction errors, respectively. The analysis assumes constant normal vectors and no friction, together with an additional regularity condition
[
40
, Assumption 4]
on the contact zone (where the transition between contact and non-contact occurs) to ensure smooth behavior near the interface. It is noted that the estimates are not robust for nearly incompressible materials (
\nu\to\frac{1}{2}
).

In this work, we design and analyze a second-order DDR scheme for contact mechanics with Tresca friction on fracture networks modeled with co-dimension 1 interfaces. In the discrete formulation, the displacement field is approximated using vector values attached to vertices, edges, faces, and elements, and the contact constraints are enforced through face-wise constant Lagrange multipliers on the fracture network. The chosen degrees of freedom for the displacement allow us to reconstruct piecewise quadratic displacements on the edges, faces, and elements.
A key analytical result of this study is the discrete Korn inequality adapted to the DDR-type spaces, where our proof draws inspiration from
[
28
]
but differs substantially due to the presence of quadratic face displacement reconstruction. Furthermore, we establish a discrete inf-sup condition based on a non-standard
H^{-1/2}
-type norm on the fracture network, ensuring stability of the mixed formulation. We provide a comprehensive error analysis for solutions with
H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}
regularity, which yields an
\mathcal{O}(h^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}})
order convergence in the energy norm (for
0<\mathfrak{r}<1
when the Tresca threshold vanishes and
0<\mathfrak{r}\leq 1/2
otherwise – although the numerical results suggests that
0<\mathfrak{r}<1
remains valid even when the Tresca threshold does not vanish). Moreover, the error estimates are robust for the primal variable (displacement) in the incompressible limit, when the Poisson ratio tends to
\frac{1}{2}
. This is made possible by the use of a bespoke interpolator (designed by correcting the cell values of the standard DDR interpolator) that enjoys a commutation (Fortin) property with the continuous and discrete divergences.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the governing equations for contact with Tresca friction, presenting the strong and weak formulations. Section
3
details the DDR discretization, including mesh notation (Section
3.1
), the construction of discrete spaces with degrees of freedom (Section
3.2
), displacement and gradient reconstruction (Section
3.3
), and the discrete scheme (Section
3.4
) including its well posedness. Section
4
states the main results, starting from an abstract error estimate (Theorem
4.2

4.3
) providing rates of convergence depending on the regularity of the solution; the proofs are presented in Section
5
. Finally, the theoretical results are validated by extensive numerical tests presented in Section
6
.

2.
Model

We consider a discrete fracture-matrix model on the polyhedral domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
including a fracture network
\Gamma
with co-dimension
1
defined as the union of flat fractures
\Gamma_{i}
:

\overline{\Gamma}=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbf{I}}\overline{\Gamma}_{i}.

We assume that
\Gamma
does not disconnect any part of
\Omega
from
\partial\Omega
, that is, the boundary of each connected component of
\Omega\backslash\Gamma
contains a portion of
\partial\Omega
of non-zero
(d-1)
-dimensional measure. Each fracture
\Gamma_{i}
(for
i\in\mathbf{I}
) is a polygonal simply connected open subdomain of a plane of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Without restriction of generality, it is assumed that fractures may only intersect at their boundaries. The two sides of a given fracture of
\Gamma
are denoted by
\pm
in the matrix domain
\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma}
. The two unit normal vectors
\mathbf{n}^{\pm}
, oriented outward from the sides
\pm
, satisfy
\mathbf{n}^{+}+\mathbf{n}^{-}=\mathbf{0}.
For simplicity, given homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, the space

\mathbf{U}_{0}\coloneqq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})^{3}

of the displacements (which may be discontinuous across fractures) is endowed with the norm
\lVert\mathbf{v}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})}=\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})}
.
The oriented jump operator on
\Gamma
for functions
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{U}_{0}
is defined by

[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\coloneqq\gamma^{+}\mathbf{u}-\gamma^{-}\mathbf{u},

where
\gamma^{\pm}
are the trace operators on each side of
\Gamma
. Its normal and tangential components are denoted respectively by
[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}
and
[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\tau}\coloneqq[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]-[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{n}^{+}
. The normal trace operators on
H_{\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})
are denoted by
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{\pm}
. The symmetric gradient operator
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}
is defined on
\mathbf{U}_{0}
by
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(\mathbf{v})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{v}+(\nabla\mathbf{v})^{t})
.

The model we consider accounts for the mechanical equilibrium equation with a linear isotropic elastic constitutive law and a Tresca frictional contact model at matrix-fracture interfaces. In its strong form, it is written

(2.1)

\begin{cases}-\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{f}&\text{on}\penalty\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma},\\
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})=\frac{E}{1+\nu}\left(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u})+\frac{\nu}{1-2\nu}({\rm{tr}}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(\mathbf{u}))\mathrm{I}_{3}\right)&\text{on}\penalty\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma},\\
\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})+\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{-}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})=\mathbf{0}&\text{on}\penalty\ \Gamma,\\
T_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{u})\leq 0,\penalty\ [\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}\leq 0,\penalty\ [\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}T_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{u})=0&\text{on}\penalty\ \Gamma,\\
\lvert\mathbf{T}_{\tau}(\mathbf{u})\rvert\leq g&\text{on }\Gamma,\\
\mathbf{T}_{\tau}(\mathbf{u})\cdot[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\tau}+g\lvert[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\tau}\rvert=0&\text{on }\Gamma,\end{cases}

with a Tresca threshold
g\geq 0
, Poisson ratio
\nu
, and Young’s modulus
E
satisfying
\frac{E}{1+\nu}\in[\mu_{1},\mu_{2}]
with
0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}
, the
3\times 3
identity matrix
\mathrm{I}_{3}
, and normal and tangential surface tractions
T_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{u})=\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}
and
\mathbf{T}_{\tau}(\mathbf{u})=\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})-T_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{n}^{+}
. We assumed henceforth that the external force term
\mathbf{f}
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and that
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
is nonnegative.

The weak formulation of the mechanical model with Tresca frictional-contact (
2.1
) is written in mixed form using a vector-valued Lagrange multiplier
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
at matrix-fracture interfaces. Define the displacement jump space by

\mathbf{W}_{j}(\Gamma)\coloneqq\{[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{v}]\hskip-1.5pt]\colon\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{U}_{0}\}

and denote by
\mathbf{W}^{\prime}_{j}(\Gamma)
its dual space; the duality pairing between these two spaces is written
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
.
We note that
L^{2}(\Gamma)^{3}\subset\mathbf{W}^{\prime}_{j}(\Gamma)
and that
\langle\boldsymbol{\mu},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\Gamma}\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma}\boldsymbol{\mu}\cdot\mathbf{v}
whenever
\boldsymbol{\mu}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
. The dual cone is then defined by

\mathcal{C}_{f}\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathbf{W}^{\prime}_{j}(\Gamma)\colon\langle\boldsymbol{\mu},\mathbf{v}\rangle_{\Gamma}\leq\int_{\Gamma}g|\mathbf{v}_{\tau}|\text{ for all }\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{W}_{j}(\Gamma)\text{ with }\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{+}\leq 0\right\}.

The weak mixed-variational formulation of (
2.1
) reads: find
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{U}_{0}
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})\colon\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(\mathbf{v})+\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda},[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{v}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathbf{U}_{0},

(2.2a)

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\mu}-\boldsymbol{\lambda},[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\mu}\in\mathcal{C}_{f}.

We can see from this formulation that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=-\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})=\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{-}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})
.

Scheme

Mesh

\Omega

\Gamma

If

and

3.2.
Spaces

To account for the discontinuity of the discrete displacement field at matrix-fracture interfaces, the displacement degrees of freedom (DOFs) can be discontinuous across the fracture network. To represent this, for each
s\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}
and each
K\in\mathcal{M}_{s}
, we denote by
\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}
the nodal unknown corresponding to the side of
K
in the matrix. This unknown is identical for all cells lying on the same side of
\Gamma
as
K
: if
K,L\in\mathcal{V}_{s}
are on the same side of
\Gamma
then
\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}=\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{L}s}}.
Coherently, the notation
{\mathcal{K}s}
is used for the set of all cells containing
s
and lying on the same side of
\Gamma
as
K
. If
s\notin\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma}
, then
{\mathcal{K}s}=\mathcal{M}_{s}
and there is a unique displacement unknown at

, which is denoted by
\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}
. On similar lines we introduce the notations
\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma},\penalty\ \mathcal{K}\sigma
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}
and
\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}},\penalty\ {\mathcal{K}e}

Taking into account these multiple DOFs on fracture entities, the numerical scheme for (

) is based on the vector-valued version of the

space of the Discrete De Rham complex

22
]
. This scheme is a higher-order extension of the one proposed in

]

The global discrete space is defined as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h}\coloneqq\{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}&=\big((\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}})_{K\in\mathcal{M},\penalty\ s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}},(\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}})_{K\in\mathcal{M},\penalty\ e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}},(\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma})_{K\in\mathcal{M},\penalty\ \sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}},(\mathbf{v}_{K})_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\big):\\
&\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\text{ and }\mathbf{v}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\},\end{split}

and its subspace incorporating homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on

is

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h,0}\coloneqq\{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h}\colon\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}=0\penalty\ \text{if }s\in\mathcal{V}^{\text{ext}},\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}}=0\text{ if }e\in\mathcal{E}^{\text{ext}},\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}=0\text{ if }\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext}}\}.

For each
K\in\mathcal{M}
, the local discrete space gathers the components attached to a cell, its faces, edges and vertices.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},K}\coloneqq\{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{K}&=\big((\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}})_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}},(\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}})_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}},(\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma})_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}},\mathbf{v}_{K}\big):\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\penalty\ \mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
&\qquad\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\text{ and }\mathbf{v}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\},\end{split}

The Lagrange multiplier plays the role of approximations of
-\gamma^{+}_{\bf n}\sigma({\bf u})
. Its space is made of piecewise constant vectors:

{\bf M}_{h}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)^{3}:\penalty\ \boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\sigma}\coloneqq(\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h})|_{\sigma}\text{ is a constant }\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}\}.

The normal and tangential components of

are

\lambda_{h,{\bf n}}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\cdot{\bf n}^{+},\quad\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h,\tau}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}-\lambda_{h,{\bf n}}{\bf n}^{+},

and the discrete dual cone is

\mathcal{C}_{f,h}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\in{\bf M}_{h}\colon\lambda_{h,{\bf n}}\geq 0,\penalty\ \lvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h,\tau}\rvert\leq g\}\subset\mathcal{C}_{f}.

3.3.
Gradient and Reconstruction Operators

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})=-\int_{\sigma}\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K\sigma}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}\colon\boldsymbol{\eta}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}}\int_{e}\Upsilon^{2}_{Ke}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\eta}{\mathbf{\bf n}_{\sigma e}},

(resp.

\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h,0},

(3.1)

\displaystyle[\hskip-1.5pt[\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\sigma}\coloneqq\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{L\sigma}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}.

Next, for any element

\Upsilon^{2}_{K}:\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h,0}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}(K)^{3}
in the following manner: for all

\displaystyle\int_{K}\big(\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}-\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\big)\colon\boldsymbol{\xi}\lesssim\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h_{K}^{-1/2}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\right)\lVert\boldsymbol{\xi}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}.

Taking
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}-\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)^{3\times 3}
and simplifying, we obtain

(5.5)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}-\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\lesssim\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h_{K}^{-1/2}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\overset{\eqref{eq:stabK}}{\leq}\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})^{1/2}.

The norm equivalence (
5.1
) follows by combining triangles inequalities, the definition (
3.6
) of
\lVert{\cdot}\rVert_{1,K}
, and (
5.5
).

Next, assume that
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
is a generic face of
K
. The relation (
5.2
) follows by using the triangle inequality, the bound
|\nabla_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|\leq|\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|
, the discrete trace and inverse inequalities
[
21
, Lemmas 1.28 and 1.35]
, the relation
h_{K}\approx h_{\sigma}
(resulting from the mesh regularity assumption) and (
3.5
) to write

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla_{\sigma}\big(\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\big)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+\lVert\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}^{-2}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{{\bf{v}}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+h^{-1}_{K}\lVert\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}_{K}\left(\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+\lVert\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}\right)

\displaystyle\approx h^{-1}_{K}\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{1,K}.

Furthermore, using (
4.4
), the triangle inequality, inverse inequalities
[
21
, Lemmas 1.28 and 1.35]
, approximation properties
[
21
, Theorem 1.45]
of
\Pi^{0}_{K}
, and the mesh regularity assumption
h_{K}\approx h_{\sigma}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{v}_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\Pi^{0}_{K}\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1/2}_{\sigma}\lVert\Pi^{0}_{K}\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}+h^{1/2}_{K}\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1/2}_{K}\left(\lVert\nabla\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{K}+\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\approx h^{1/2}_{K}\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{1,K}.\qed

The following lemma provides a DOF-based bound on the discrete norm, which plays a crucial role in proving the bound on the consistency of the gradient reconstruction (Lemma
5.8
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{M}
and
\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h}
. Then, it holds:

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{1,K}\lesssim h^{1/2}_{K}\left(\lvert\mathbf{v}_{K}\rvert+\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}\rvert+\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}e}\rvert+\max_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert\right).

Proof.

Recalling the definition (
3.6
) of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1,K}
, we will begin by finding a bound on
\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})
. To this purpose, we use triangle and discrete trace inequalities
[
21
, Lemma 1.32]
to write

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},{}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})=h^{-1}_{K}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{Ke}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+h_{K}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\lvert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})-\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle h^{-2}_{K}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h^{-1}_{K}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{Ke}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}+h_{K}\max_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert^{2}.

Now, the estimates of
[
16
, Lemma 8]
give

\displaystyle\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{Ke}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(e)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}e}\|_{L^{2}(e)}+h^{1/2}_{e}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{e}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert,

\displaystyle\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+h_{\sigma}^{1/2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}}\|\Upsilon^{2}_{Ke}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(e)}+h_{\sigma}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathbf{v}_{K}\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{K}^{1/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\|\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+h_{K}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\|\Upsilon^{2}_{{\mathcal{K}e}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(e)}+h_{K}^{3/2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert.

Using triangle inequality, equation (
5.1
), and mesh regularity (which gives
h_{e}\approx h_{\sigma}\approx h_{K}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\lesssim h^{-2}_{K}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{K}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h^{-1}_{K}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{{K\!\sigma}}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}+\max_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}e}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}+h_{K}\max_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\lvert\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert^{2}.

Since, all of
\mathbf{v}_{K},\mathbf{v}_{{K\!\sigma}},\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}e},\text{ and }\mathbf{v}_{\mathcal{K}s}
are constant, a use of mesh regularity (which gives
|e|\approx h_{K}
,
|\sigma|\approx h_{K}^{2}
and
|K|\approx h^{3}_{K}
) shows that
\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})^{1/2}
is bounded above by the right-hand side of (
5.6
). The bound on
\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{1,K}
then follows from (
5.1
), the bound (
5.7
), and an inverse inequality.
∎

5.1.
Proof of the discrete Korn Inequality (Theorem
3.1
)

Recalling (
5.1
), to prove the inequality (
3.7
), we just need to bound
\lVert\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})}
(where
\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}
is the broken gradient on
\mathcal{M}
).
Applying the same techniques as in
[
21
, Section 7.3.2]
, we obtain

(5.8)

\lVert\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\lVert\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})}^{2}+\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}h^{-1}_{\sigma}\lVert[\hskip-1.5pt[\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2},

where
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{M}}
is the broken strain on
\mathcal{M}
and, for all
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{int}}
,
|[\hskip-1.5pt[\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]|_{\sigma}|=|\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{L}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|
while, if
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{K}
,
|[\hskip-1.5pt[\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]|_{\sigma}|=|\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|
.

Recalling (
5.4
), taking
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})|_{K}-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{h}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)^{3\times 3}
and simplifying, we have

\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})-\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{h}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(K)}\lesssim\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})^{1/2}.

Using a triangle inequality, squaring and summing over
K\in\mathcal{M}
yields

(5.9)

\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\mathcal{M}}(\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\lesssim\|\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{h}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}).

Let
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{int}}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
. Since
\sigma
is not a fracture face, the cells
K
and
L
are on the same side of the fracture and hence all the degrees of freedom on
\sigma
are common to
K
and
L
, and so
\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=\Upsilon^{2}_{{L\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}
. Hence,

(5.10)

|[\hskip-1.5pt[\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{|\sigma}|=|\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{L}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|=|\left(\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\right)-\left(\Upsilon^{2}_{L}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{L\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\right)|.

For
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext}}\cap\mathcal{F}_{K}
, we have
\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}s}}=\mathbf{v}_{{\mathcal{K}e}}=\mathbf{0}
for all
s\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}
and
e\in\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}
. Additionally since
\mathbf{v}_{{K\!\sigma}}=\mathbf{0}
, it follows that
\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=\mathbf{0}.
Thus

(5.11)

|[\hskip-1.5pt[\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{|\sigma}|=|\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}|.

Taking the
L^{2}(\sigma)
-norms of (
5.10
) and (
5.11
), using triangle inequalities, summing over
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
, invoking the definition (
3.5
) of
\mathbb{S}_{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
and using the mesh
regularity to write
h_{\sigma}\simeq h_{K}
whenever
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
, we conclude

(5.12)

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}h^{-1}_{\sigma}\lVert[\hskip-1.5pt[\Upsilon^{2}_{h}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h^{-1}_{\sigma}\lVert\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbb{S}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}).

The inequality (
3.7
) follows from (
5.8
), (
5.9
), and (
5.12
).

5.2.
Proof of the discrete inf-sup condition

The proof of the inf-sup condition requires to interpolate displacements with minimal
{H}^{1}(\Omega\setminus{\Gamma})
regularity. To do so, we therefore first describe a Clément-like (averaged) interpolator for such displacements.

For each
K\in\mathcal{M}
and
s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}
, we take an open set
U_{\mathcal{K}s}\subset\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{K}s}K
and a function
\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s}\in L^{\infty}(U_{\mathcal{K}s})
such that

\displaystyle\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert\gtrsim\max\limits_{K\in\mathcal{K}s}\lvert K\rvert,\qquad\lvert\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert\lesssim 1,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s}}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s}=1,\qquad\frac{1}{\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s}}\boldsymbol{x}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s}=\boldsymbol{x}_{s}.

We refer to
[
28
, Appendix A]
for an explicit construction of
(U_{\mathcal{K}s},\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s})
in the generic case.
The averaged interpolate
\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h}
of
\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\setminus{\Gamma})^{3}
is then defined by:

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{{\mathcal{K}s}}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{{\mathcal{K}e}}=\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{{K\!\sigma}}=\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{V}^{\text{ext}}\,,\,e\in\mathcal{E}^{\text{ext}}\,,\,\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext},}

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{{\mathcal{K}s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s}}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s}\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{V}^{\text{int}},

(5.14a)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{{\mathcal{K}e}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s_{1}}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s_{1}}}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s_{1}}\mathbf{v}+\frac{1}{2\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s_{2}}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s_{2}}}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s_{2}}\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{\text{int}},\penalty\ e=[s_{1},s_{2}],

(5.14b)

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lvert\sigma\rvert}\int_{\sigma}\mathbf{v}|_{K}

\displaystyle\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{int}},

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\right)_{K}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{0}_{K}\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{M}.

Proposition 5.3

(Stability of the averaged interpolator)
.

For
\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\setminus{\Gamma})^{3}
, it holds

(5.15)

\lVert\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\rVert_{1,h}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\setminus{\Gamma})^{3}
and
K\in\mathcal{M}
. Let

\mathcal{N}(K)\coloneqq\bigcup\limits_{s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}}\bigcup\limits_{L\in\mathcal{K}s}L

be the patch around
K
made of all the cells in
\mathcal{K}s
for each
s
vertex of
K
. By definition of
\mathcal{K}s
, we have
\mathbf{v}\in H^{1}(\mathcal{N}(K))^{3}
. Denote by
\mathbf{q}
the
L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))^{3}
-projection of
\mathbf{v}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathcal{N}(K))^{3}
. This projection enjoys the following approximation and stability properties
[
21
, Theorem 1.45]
:

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{K}\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))},

(5.17)

\displaystyle\lVert(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v})|_{K}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1/2}_{K}\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K},

(5.18)

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\mathbf{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}.

Recalling
[
22
, Lemma 3]
and from Lemma
4.1
we have
\Upsilon^{2}_{Ke}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}
,
\Upsilon^{2}_{K\sigma}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}
,
\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}=\nabla\mathbf{q}
, and
\Upsilon^{2}_{K}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q}
.
Thus, the definition (
3.5
) of
\mathbb{S}_{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
gives

(5.19)

\mathbb{S}_{K}(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q},\underline{\mathbf{w}}_{h})=0\qquad\forall\underline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h}.

The definition (
3.6
) of
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{1,K}
, then gives

(5.20)

\lVert\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}\rVert_{1,K}=\lVert\nabla\mathbf{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}.

To prove (
5.15
), we bound each type of degree of freedom (vertex, edge, face, element), then use (
5.6
) to conclude.

From
[
22
, Proposition 5.5]
, for any
s\in\mathcal{V}_{K}
the following inequality holds:

(5.21)

\displaystyle\lvert(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}s}-(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v})_{\mathcal{K}s}\rvert\lesssim h^{-1/2}_{K}\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}.

If
e\in\mathcal{E}^{\text{ext}}_{K}
, then
(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v})_{\mathcal{K}e}=\mathbf{0}
and
(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}e}=\Pi^{0}_{e}(\mathbf{q})
. Take
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext}}
that contains

and write

\displaystyle\lvert(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}e}-(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v})_{\mathcal{K}e}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\Pi^{0}_{e}(\mathbf{q})\rvert\leq\lVert\mathbf{q}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(e)}\leq\lVert\mathbf{q}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim h^{-1}_{\sigma}\lVert\mathbf{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{-1}_{\sigma}h^{1/2}_{K}\lVert\nabla\mathbf{q}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}\lesssim h^{-1/2}_{K}\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))},

where we have used the definition of interpolation in the first inequality, inverse Lebesgue inequality
[
21
, Lemma 1.25]
for the third inequality, discrete trace inequality
[
21
, Lemma 1.32]
in the fourth inequality, and the mesh regularity assumption along with (
5.18
) in the last inequality.

If
e=[s_{1},s_{2}]\in\mathcal{E}^{\text{int}}_{K}
, then (
5.13
), (
5.14a
), and the fact that
\mathbf{q}
is linear ensure that

(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}e}=\Pi^{0}_{e}(\mathbf{q})=\frac{1}{2\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s_{1}}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s_{1}}}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s_{1}}\mathbf{q}+\frac{1}{2\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s_{2}}\rvert}\int_{U_{\mathcal{K}s_{2}}}\overline{\omega}_{\mathcal{K}s_{2}}\mathbf{q}.

\displaystyle\lvert(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}e}-(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v})_{\mathcal{K}e}\rvert

where the first inequality follows from the bound

and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, while the conclusion is obtained using
\lvert U_{\mathcal{K}s_{j}}\rvert\gtrsim\lvert K\rvert

5.16

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext}}_{K}
, then
(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v})_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}=\mathbf{0}

(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}=\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{q})

\sigma\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{int}}_{K}

Finally, for the cell degree of freedom, we write

(5.22)

\displaystyle\lvert(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{K}-(\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v})_{K}\rvert

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\lvert K\rvert}\int_{K}\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}\right|\lesssim\lvert h_{K}\rvert^{-3/2}\lVert\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}\overset{\eqref{eq:stab_avgint.est2}}{\lesssim}h^{-1/2}_{K}\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}.

Gathering (
5.21
)-(
5.22
) and invoking the bound (
5.6
) with
\underline{\mathbf{w}}_{h}=\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}-\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}
yields

(5.23)

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}-\mathbb{I}^{a}_{h}\mathbf{v}\rVert_{1,K}\lesssim\lVert\nabla\mathbf{v}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{N}(K))}.

Combining (
5.20
) and (
5.23
), squaring, summing over
K\in\mathcal{M}
and gathering the integrals by cells and using the mesh regularity to see that
\#\{K\in\mathcal{M}\,:\,L\in\mathcal{N}_{K}\}\lesssim 1
for all

Lemma 5.4

(Bound on the discrete stress)

For all

Lemma 5.5

Lemma 5.6

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\nu}{1-2\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\lVert\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\rVert_{\mathrm{en},h}+\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\lVert\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\rVert_{\mathrm{en},h}+\mu_{2}\mathbb{S}_{h}(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u},\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u})^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg],

where we have used
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}
,
\frac{E}{1+\nu}\leq\mu_{2}
and, to pass to the second line, we have introduced
\pm\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{h}(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u})\colon\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{h}(\underline{\mathbf{w}}_{h})
in the second term of the first right-hand side.
Using the discrete inf-sup condition (
3.11
) and the definitions (
4.8
) of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{C}}_{h}
and (
4.9
) of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}_{h}
, we infer

(5.31)

\lVert\Pi^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\rVert_{-1/2,\Gamma}\lesssim\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}_{h}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}))+\mu_{2}\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{C}}_{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u})+\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\left(1+\left(\frac{\nu}{1-2\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\lVert\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\rVert_{\mathrm{en},h}.

The bound (
4.11
) follows from the triangle inequality and (
5.31
), which concludes the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.7

(Adjoint Consistency)
.

Recalling the definition (
4.9
) of the adjoint consistency error
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}_{h}
, it holds that for all
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\cap H^{s}(\mathcal{M})^{3\times 3}
for some
s\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})
,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}_{h}(\boldsymbol{\chi})}\lesssim h^{s}\left(\lvert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rvert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{M})}+\lvert\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rvert_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\right).

Proof.

Let
\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{X}_{{\bf grad},h,0}
,
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
and
\boldsymbol{\chi}\in H^{s}(\mathcal{M})^{3\times 3}
, where
s\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})
. Since
\boldsymbol{\chi}
is symmetric, we have
\boldsymbol{\chi}\colon\epsilon_{h}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})=\boldsymbol{\chi}\colon\mathbb{G}^{1}_{h}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})
and thus, by (
4.9
),

\displaystyle w_{h}(\boldsymbol{\chi},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\colon\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})+\int_{\Gamma}\Pi^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}(\gamma^{+}_{\mathbf{n}}\boldsymbol{\chi})\cdot[\hskip-1.5pt[\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{h}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\int_{K}\mathbf{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}\boldsymbol{\chi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\int_{K}\mathbf{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi})-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}}\int_{\sigma=K|L}\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}})\cdot\big(\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{L\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\big)-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\int_{K}\mathbf{v}_{K}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}\boldsymbol{\chi}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\mathbf{v}_{K}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{K}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi})\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}

(5.32)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot{\Pi}^{0}_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{K\!\sigma}),

where the introduction of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}
in the first line is justified by
\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}(\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)^{3\times 3}
and we recall that, for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
, we have denoted by
K
and
L
the cells that contain
\sigma
and are, respectively, on the positive and negative side of
\Gamma
.
Since
\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=\Upsilon^{2}_{{L\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}
for all
\sigma=K|L\in\mathcal{F}^{\text{int}}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
,
\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}=0
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}^{\text{ext}}
, and the normal traces of
\boldsymbol{\chi}
are continuous across
\Gamma
, we have

(5.33)

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}}_{=0}.

Introducing
\pm\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}
in (
5.4
) and invoking (
5.33
), we obtain

\displaystyle w_{h}(\boldsymbol{\chi},\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h})={}

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\mathbf{v}_{K}\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{K}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\big)\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{K}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\big)\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}}\int_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}\cdot\big({\Pi}^{0}_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}})-\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\int_{\sigma}\big(\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}-\mathbf{v}_{K}\big)\cdot\big(\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{K}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\big)\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}}_{I_{1}}

(5.34)

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}}\int_{\sigma}\big(\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}{\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}}-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\big)\cdot\big({\Pi}^{0}_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}})-\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}\big)}_{I_{2}}.

Note that the introduction of
\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}
in
I_{2}
is justified by the definition of the orthogonal projector, which gives
\int_{\sigma}\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\cdot\big({\Pi}^{0}_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}})-\boldsymbol{\chi}\mathbf{n}_{{K\!\sigma}}\big)=0
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the fractional multiplicative trace inequality
[
23
, Remark
2.6
]
, and the approximation properties of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}
and
{\Pi}^{0}_{\sigma}
, obtained by interpolating the estimates of
[
21
, Theorem 1.45]
for integral Sobolev exponents and the mesh regularity assumption, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\bigg(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\qquad\times\big(h^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{K}\lVert\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rVert_{L^{2}(K)}+h^{\min(s-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})}_{K}\lvert\boldsymbol{\chi}-\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{1}_{K}\boldsymbol{\chi}\rvert_{H^{\min(s,1)}(K)}\big)\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{K}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}h^{s}_{K}\lvert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rvert_{H^{s}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}h^{2s}_{K}\lvert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rvert^{2}_{H^{s}(K)}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\bigg(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{K}\lVert\mathbf{v}_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\lvert\boldsymbol{\chi}\rvert_{H^{s}(\mathcal{M})}\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{1,K}\bigg)^{1/2},

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}h_{\sigma}\lVert\nabla_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}h^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{\sigma}\lvert\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}h_{\sigma}\lVert\nabla_{\sigma}\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}h^{2s}_{\sigma}\lvert\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma)}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{s}\lvert\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}^{+}\boldsymbol{\chi}|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\bigg(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}}\lVert\underline{\mathbf{v}}_{h}\rVert^{2}_{1,K}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Substituting bounds of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in (
5.4
) and using (
4.9
) concludes the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})^{3}\cap H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(\mathcal{M})^{3}
with
0<\mathfrak{r}\leq 1
. Then, the following estimate holds:

(5.35)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{C}_{h}(\mathbf{u},\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u})}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(\mathcal{M})}.

Proof.

We first note that the regularity assumption ensures that
\mathbf{u}\in C^{0}_{0}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma)^{3}
. Let
K\in\mathcal{M}
and
\mathbf{q}
be the
L^{2}(K)
-orthogonal projection of
\mathbf{u}
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)^{3}
. By the approximation properties of the polynomial projector
[
21
, Theorem 1.45]
we have

(5.36)

\lvert\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q}\rvert_{H^{s}(K)}\lesssim h^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}-s}\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(K)},\quad\forall s\in\left[0,\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}\right].

Applying the bound
[
21
, Eq.(5.110)]
to
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q}
yields

(5.37)

\max_{\overline{K}}\lvert\mathbf{u-q}\rvert\lesssim h^{\mathfrak{r}}_{K}\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(K)}.

Now, let us gather the degrees of freedom on
K
of
\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}
.

(5.38)

\displaystyle|(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}s}|

\displaystyle=|(\mathbf{u-q})(\boldsymbol{x}_{s})|\leq\max_{\overline{K}}|\mathbf{u-q}|,\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{V}_{K},

\displaystyle|(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}e}|

\displaystyle=|\Pi^{0}_{e}((\mathbf{u-q})|_{K})|\leq\max_{\overline{K}}|\mathbf{u-q}|,\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{K},

\displaystyle|(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{\mathcal{K}\sigma}|

\displaystyle=|\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}((\mathbf{u-q})|_{K})|\leq\max_{\overline{K}}|\mathbf{u-q}|,\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K},

\displaystyle|(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})_{K}|

\displaystyle=|\Pi^{0}_{K}(\mathbf{u-q})+(\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}-\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}_{K})|\leq\max_{\overline{K}}|\mathbf{u-q}|+|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}|,

where, in the last relation, we have used
\widetilde{\mathbf{q}}_{K}=0
in virtue of the argument in Remark
4.1
.
Let
f\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
be such that
\int_{K}f=0
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}=\nabla f.
Since
\Pi^{0}_{K}f=0
, by (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\|\mathbf{u}|_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\|\mathbf{u}|_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}\|f-\Pi^{0}_{K}f\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}h_{\sigma}\|\mathbf{u}|_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\sigma)}h_{K}^{1/2}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(K)}

where we have used the trace approximation properties of
\Pi^{0}_{K}
. Simplify by
\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(K)}=\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}\|_{L^{2}(K)}
and use (
4.7
) to introduce
\mathbf{q}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}=0
:

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq{}

\displaystyle h_{K}^{3/2}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{K}}\|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q})|_{K}-\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q})}\|_{L^{\infty}(\sigma)}.

Then, we invoke
[
16
, (A.10)]
to get
\|\Upsilon^{2}_{{K\!\sigma}}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q})}\|_{L^{\infty}(\sigma)}\lesssim\max_{\overline{\sigma}}\big|\mathbf{u}|_{K}-\mathbf{q}\big|\leq\max_{\overline{K}}|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q}|
,
which allows us to conclude

(5.39)

\lvert\widetilde{\mathbf{u}}_{K}\rvert\lesssim\max\limits_{\overline{K}}\lvert\mathbf{u-q}\rvert.

Using Lemma
5.2
and (
5.38
)–(
5.39
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\lVert\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{(u-q)}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\mathbb{S}_{K}(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q},\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\lVert\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{q}\rVert_{1,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{1/2}_{K}\max\limits_{\overline{K}}\lvert\mathbf{u-q}\rvert\overset{\eqref{eq:est.u.q}}{\lesssim}h^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}_{K}\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(K)}.

Using (
4.5
) and (
5.19
) in the above inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left(\lVert\mathbb{G}^{1}_{K}\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{q}\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(K)}+\mathbb{S}_{K}(\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u},\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u})\right)^{1/2}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}_{K}\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(K)}.

Recalling the definition (
4.8
) of
\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{C}}_{h}
, the estimate (
5.35
) follows by introducing
\pm\nabla\mathbf{u}
in the left-hand side above, using a triangle inequality, the approximation property (
5.36
) (with
s=1
), squaring, summing over
K
, using the bound
h_{K}\leq h
and taking the square root.
∎

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\mathbf{u}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Gamma})^{3}\cap H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(\mathcal{M})^{3}
with
0<\mathfrak{r}\leq 1
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
. Then, following estimate holds:

(5.40)

\int_{\sigma}(\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\lambda_{\mathbf{n}})\big([\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}\big)\lesssim h_{\sigma}^{1+2\mathfrak{r}}\big(\lvert\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}\rvert^{2}_{H^{\mathfrak{r}}(\sigma)}+\lvert\nabla[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}\rvert^{2}_{H^{\mathfrak{r}}(\sigma)}\big).

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{C\sigma}\coloneqq\{x\in\sigma\colon[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}(x)]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}=\mathbf{0}\}
and
\Gamma_{N\sigma}\coloneqq\{x\in\Gamma\colon[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}(x)]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}<\mathbf{0}\}
be the contact and non-contact zones on
\sigma
.
If
|\Gamma_{N\sigma}|>0
and
|\Gamma_{C\sigma}|>0
then the arguments leading to
[
29
, Eq. (33) and (34)]
show that (
5.40
) hold with
h_{\sigma}^{1+2\mathfrak{r}}
replaced by either
\frac{h_{\sigma}^{2(1+\mathfrak{r})}}{|\Gamma_{N\sigma}|^{1/2}}
or
\frac{h_{\sigma}^{2(1+\mathfrak{r})}}{|\Gamma_{C\sigma}|^{1/2}}
.
Since
\sigma=\Gamma_{N\sigma}\sqcup\Gamma_{C\sigma}
, one of
|\Gamma_{N\sigma}|
or
|\Gamma_{C\sigma}|
is larger than
|\sigma|/2\gtrsim h_{\sigma}^{2}
(by mesh regularity assumption); picking the estimate corresponding to that largest set yields (
5.40
).

If one of
|\Gamma_{N\sigma}|
and
|\Gamma_{C\sigma}|
vanishes, say
|\Gamma_{C\sigma}|=0
to fix the ideas, then by complementarity condition we have
\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}=0
on
\sigma
, so
\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}=0
and thus the left-hand side of (
5.40
) vanishes, making this inequality trivial.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
(\mathbf{u},\boldsymbol{\lambda})
,
(\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h},\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h})
be the solution of (
2.2
) and (
3.4
), respectively. In addition, consider real parameters
\mathfrak{r}
,
s_{\mathfrak{r}}
and
t_{\mathfrak{r}}
as in Theorem
4.3
. Then,

(5.41)

\int_{\Gamma}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\Pi^{0}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\Gamma}}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\cdot\big([\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]-\Pi^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\big)\lesssim h^{1+2\mathfrak{r}}\big(\lvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rvert^{2}_{H^{s_{\mathfrak{r}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma})}+\lvert[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rvert^{2}_{H^{t_{\mathfrak{r}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma})}\big).

Proof.

In case of frictionless contact (
g=0
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\Pi^{0}_{{\sigma}}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\cdot\big([\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\big)=\int_{\sigma}(\lambda_{\mathbf{n}}-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\lambda_{\mathbf{n}})\big([\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{\mathbf{n}}\big)\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}.

By Lemma
5.9
and since
t_{\mathfrak{r}}=1+\mathfrak{r}
(as
g=0
), we infer

\int_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}\boldsymbol{\lambda})\cdot\big([\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]-\Pi^{0}_{\sigma}[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\big)\lesssim h^{1+2\mathfrak{r}}\big(\lvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rvert^{2}_{H^{\mathfrak{r}}(\sigma)}+\lvert[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rvert^{2}_{H^{t_{\mathfrak{r}}}(\sigma)}\big)\quad\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}.

Adding over all the faces of
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
yields (
5.41
).

In the case with friction, i.e.,
g\not=0
, the estimate follows directly from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, approximation properties of the projection operator
\Pi^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}

[
21
, Theorem 1.45]
, and Young’s inequality (note that this simple approach is only possible because of the restriction
0\leq\mathfrak{r}\leq 1/2
, instead of the larger range
0\leq\mathfrak{r}<1
allowed by the finer estimate obtained above when
g=0
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.3
:
.

From Theorem
4.2
, Lemma
5.7
, Lemma
5.8
and Lemma
5.10
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}-\mathbb{I}_{h}\mathbf{u}\rVert_{\mathrm{en},h}\lesssim h^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}\bigg[{}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\bigg(\lvert\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u})\rvert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(\mathcal{M})}+\lvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rvert_{H^{\mathfrak{r}}(\Gamma)}\bigg)+\sqrt{\mu_{2}}\lvert\mathbf{u}\rvert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\mathfrak{r}}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle+\lvert\boldsymbol{\lambda}\rvert_{H^{s_{\mathfrak{r}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma})}+\lvert[\hskip-1.5pt[\mathbf{u}]\hskip-1.5pt]\rvert_{H^{t_{\mathfrak{r}}}(\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma})}\bigg].

This completes the bound of (
4.12
). Finally, the bound (
4.13
) is obtained from (
4.11
), Lemmas
5.6
-
5.8
, and the approximation property
[
21
, Theorem 1.45]
of
\Pi^{0}_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}}
.
This concludes the theorem.
∎

6.
Numerical Experiments

In this section, we assess the numerical convergence of the proposed discretisation for the poromechanical model with frictional contact at matrix-fracture interfaces. The first and second Lamé coefficients are related to Young’s modulus
E
and Poisson’s ratio
\nu
by

G:=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)},\quad L\coloneqq\frac{\nu E}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)},
respectively.

The subsequent sections present a series of test cases. Sections 6.1–6.3 employ the same families of meshes having uniform Cartesian, tetrahedral, and hexahedral cells. Section
6.1
analyzes the convergence of the frictionless formulation using a 3D manufactured analytical solution with a single fracture. Section
6.2
focuses on a 3D configuration governed by the Tresca friction law, while Section
6.3
examines the performance of the method in nearly incompressible regimes for Lamé coefficient
L\in\{1,10^{4},10^{6}\}
. Finally, Section
6.4
turns to a more challenging 3D Discrete Fracture–Matrix (DFM) model without an analytical solution, relying on tetrahedral meshes refined along the fracture network.

In all the following experiments, the fracture face–wise Lagrange multiplier enables the reformulation of the variational inequality (
3.4b
), together with the constraint
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h}\in\mathcal{C}_{f,h}
, into a system of nonlinear equations. These equations involve the projection operators
[r]_{+}=\max(0,r)
and
[\boldsymbol{\xi}]_{g}
=projection of
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
onto the ball centered at
0
with radius
g
, and are written

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda_{h,\mathbf{n}}\coloneqq[\lambda_{h,\mathbf{n}}+\beta_{h,\mathbf{n}}[\hskip-1.5pt[\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{h,\mathbf{n}}]_{+},\\
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h,\tau}\coloneqq[\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{h,\tau}+\beta_{h,\tau}[\hskip-1.5pt[\underline{\mathbf{u}}_{h}]\hskip-1.5pt]_{h,\tau}]_{g},\end{cases}

where
\beta_{h,\mathbf{n}}>0,
and
\beta_{h,\tau}>0
are face-wise constant quantities defined along
\Gamma
. The resulting nonlinear system is solved with a semi-smooth Newton algorithm. The stopping criteria is set to
10^{-12}
on the relative residual, and the linear system at each iteration is solved using the Pardiso LU solver.

Efficacy and safety of individual nutrition support in patients with hepatitis B virus-related acute-on-chronic liver failure at nutrition risk: a study protocol for a randomised controlled clinical trial
Authors: Zhipeng Li, Qiumin Luo, Peipei Wang, Lu Wang, Xingrong Zheng, Yeqiong Zhang, Wenxiong Xu, Liang Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMalnutrition is a common complication of hepatitis B virus-related acute-on-chronic liver failure (HBV-ACLF) typically associated with poor prognosis. Despite nutritional treatment, the outcomes for these patients are limited by the symptoms and complications associated with ACLF. So far, the benefits of nutritional interventions in these populations have not been proven. This study aims to explore a new nutritional intervention method for patients with HBV-ACLF and evaluate its safety and efficacy.Methods and analysisThis study is an investigator-initiated, nonblind, randomised controlled clinical trial. We will recruit 60 patients with HBV-ACLF according to the Chinese Group on the Study of Severe Hepatitis B criteria hospitalised in the Infectious Diseases Department of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University. Eligible patients will be randomly allocated to the nutrition support group (intervention group) and the control group in a 1:1 ratio. Patients in the nutrition support group will receive 10 days of tailor-made nutrition therapy consisting of oral nutritional supplements and supplementary parenteral nutrition. Patients in the control group will receive standard nutrition with dietary advice. All assessments will be conducted at baseline, 30 days and 90 days. The primary outcome measure is the liver transplant-free mortality rate. The secondary indicators include the incidence of clinical adverse outcomes and changes in indicators such as muscle mass, muscle strength, physical function and quality of life (EQ-5D scale).Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University (approval number: II2023-242-03). The results and conclusions of the clinical trial will be published in academic conferences or journals.Trial registration numberNCT06128421.Keywords: Randomized Controlled Trial, Clinical Trial, Hepatology, Nutritional support

Introduction

Malnutrition is a prevalent and critical complication of acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF), demonstrably linked to adverse health outcomes, including diminished quality of life, increased decompensation events, adverse posttransplant outcomes and increased overall risk of death.1,3 Consequently, nutritional intervention is considered standard care for ACLF patients,3 although it is frequently neglected in clinical settings. In China, hepatitis B represents the primary cause of ACLF.4 5 Hepatitis B virus-related ACLF (HBV-ACLF) is characterised by acute liver cell necrosis superimposed on chronic hepatitis B infection and carries a high risk of death. Clinical manifestations include anorexia, fatigue, jaundice and coagulation dysfunction, often accompanied by complex complications such as ascites or hepatic encephalopathy (HE).4 Despite the known importance of nutrition, current knowledge regarding optimal nutritional treatment for HBV-ACLF patients remains limited.2 This study aims to address this gap by exploring the efficacy and safety of an individualised nutritional support programme for this specific patient population.

A large-sample randomised controlled trial (RCT) demonstrated that personalised nutritional support, including oral nutrition, enteral nutrition (EN) and parenteral nutrition (PN), led to improved clinical outcomes, including reduced mortality, in patients identified as at nutritional risk.6 However, another RCT involving patients with alcoholic ACLF revealed that intensive EN did not significantly improve survival rates.7 Furthermore, another RCT conducted with HBV-ACLF patients indicated that the addition of a nighttime meal enhanced energy metabolism, although no impact on broader clinical outcomes was reported.8 In conclusion, while evidence suggests that nutritional therapy in ACLF patients is generally well-tolerated with minimal adverse effects, definitive confirmation of its clinical benefits awaits further investigation.3

This study employs a randomised controlled clinical trial design to evaluate the efficacy and safety of individualised nutritional support in HBV-ACLF patients. The Nutritional Risk Screening-2002 (NRS-2002) screening tool, validated for ACLF patients, identifies those at nutritional risk (score ≥3) who may benefit from nutrition intervention to improve prognosis.9 Indirect calorimetry, the gold standard for measuring resting energy expenditure (REE), is used to tailor nutrient requirements.10 This approach allows for a tailored ‘prescription’ that meets each patient’s specific energy needs. Current guidelines recommend a target range of 1.0–1.4 times REE/day for energy intake and 0.8–1.5 g/kg/day for protein in patients with liver failure.1,3 However, the applicability of these targets to patients with HBV-ACLF remains unclear. Oral nutrition and oral nutritional supplements (ONS) are the preferred modalities for malnourished patients with acute liver failure. However, in cases where these are insufficient, EN and/or PN should be promptly implemented.11 Notably, gastrointestinal symptoms, dysfunction and poor tolerance can limit the use of EN in patients with liver failure. PN offers a more efficient route to achieve nutritional goals compared with EN without increasing mortality risk.12 A systematic review and meta-analysis published in 2020 revealed that supplementary parenteral nutrition (SPN) could help decrease nosocomial infections and ICU mortality.13 SPN remains an important nutritional support treatment.14 Therefore, this study will employ a combined approach using both ONS and SPN, if required, to achieve a target energy intake of 1.2 times the REE per day and a protein intake of 1.2 g/kg of ideal body weight per day in patients.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This investigator-initiated, noncommercial, open-label, randomised controlled clinical trial evaluates the safety and efficacy of individualised nutritional support therapy in patients with HBV-ACLF identified as nutritionally at risk using the NRS-2002 screening tool. Patient recruitment was scheduled to begin on 23 November 2023 to 31 October 2024. Eligible subjects will be randomly allocated to either the nutritional support group or the control group in a 1:1 ratio (figure 1). All patients will be provided with a standardised, low-fat, high-quality protein diet, primarily composed of carbohydrates, prepared by the hospital kitchen. The nutritional support group will receive standard hospital meals and a 10-day regimen of tailored nutritional support administered by a specialised nutrition team composed of physicians, nurses, pharmacists and nutritionists. Control group patients will receive standard hospital meals based on their individual dietary preferences and tolerance.

Interventions

Medical management: all enrolled subjects will receive comprehensive medical treatment according to their individual needs. This may include antiviral agents (tenofovir or entecavir), antibiotics, diuretics, aspartate ornithine, lactulose, human albumin, compound glycyrrhizin, ademetionine (adenosyl methionine), polyenephosphatidylcholine (polyene phosphatidylcholine), ursodeoxycholic acid, glutathione, fresh frozen plasma and/or the use of artificial liver support systems when feasible. Nutritional therapy will follow the liver disease nutrition guidelines established by the European Society for Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition11 and the Chinese Medical Association.2

Nutritional education: all patients or their relatives will receive the same meal recipes provided by the hospital kitchen and participate in nutrition education sessions. These sessions will cover topics such as macronutrient counting, maintaining a dietary diary, the benefits of small and frequent meals and how to read nutrition labels. Participants will assist researchers who received specialised training in completing daily dietary records. Energy and nutrient intakes will be calculated using the ‘Chinese Food Composition’ table,15 and daily calorie and protein intake will be recorded.

Nutritional support group: patients in the nutritional support group will receive an individualised nutritional prescription based on their REE measured by a metabolic cart. The target energy intake will be 1.2 times REE/day, and the target protein intake will be 1.2 g/kg/day (based on ideal body weight). A collaborative effort between the physician and nutritionist will result in a nutrition plan that addresses the discrepancy between the patient’s daily dietary intake and the established nutritional goals.

Oral nutrition and supplementation: if dietary intake fails to meet at least 75% of the nutritional target, ONS, such as Nutrison, will be introduced. The initial dose and frequency will be determined by a nutritionist, with gradual increases based on individual tolerance. Intake will be assessed every 24–48 hours.

Parenteral nutrition: if oral feeding cannot meet at least 75% of the nutritional target within 3 days, SPN will be implemented. This may involve the placement of a central venous catheter or a peripherally inserted central catheter line. PN prescriptions will be formulated by a team of physicians and pharmacists specialising in nutrition support. Medium- and long-chain fat emulsions or structured fat emulsions may be included in the PN plan if necessary.

Patients in the control group (n=30) will receive dietary education and follow a diet based on their preferences. If a patient experiences hypoglycaemia, basic energy support will be provided. However, to ensure patient safety, the total daily intravenous energy intake will not exceed 800 kcal. Standard nutritional treatment (as outlined previously) will be provided if necessary. For patients in the nutritional support group, SPN may not be able to achieve 75% of the energy target due to factors such as fluid retention. In such cases, following discussion among the research team, alternative nutritional treatment options may be considered for both groups (control and nutritional support).

Outcomes

Primary outcome: liver transplant-free mortality rate

Secondary outcome: body composition will be assessed using the L3 skeletal muscle index (L3-SMI). Physical function will be measured through grip strength and Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) score.16 Health-related quality of life will be evaluated using the EQ-5D questionnaire.17 Additionally, the study will monitor for clinical outcomes, including the incidence of liver decompensation events and the occurrence of any adverse events.

Definition

A liver decompensation event is defined as the occurrence of ascites, gastrointestinal bleeding, septic shock, HE, hepatorenal syndrome or any similar complication. Grip strength is considered low when it falls below 28 kg for men and 18 kg for women. Physical function is considered low when the SPPB score is 9 or less.18 Low muscle mass is defined as an L3-SMI of less than 42 cm2/m2 in men and less than 38 cm2/m2 in women.19 Gastrointestinal failure refers to a state where the gastrointestinal tract cannot meet the body’s minimum nutritional requirements. This diagnosis is typically based on the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics/American Society for Enteral and Parenteral Nutrition criteria III or IV.20

Adverse events

This study will monitor for a range of potential adverse events. Gastrointestinal reactions such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhoea will be documented. Complications associated with PN will also be monitored, including catheter-related infections and alterations in liver function. Additionally, we will be vigilant for refeeding syndrome.21 Blood sugar levels will be closely monitored to identify both hypoglycaemia (blood glucose≤3.9 mmol/L) and hyperglycaemia (blood glucose>10 mmol/L). The researcher will document all adverse events and take immediate steps to mitigate their impact. If deemed necessary, the research treatment will be terminated.

Sample size

The sample size for this study was determined based on the primary outcome of liver transplant-free mortality rate. A previous study22 reported a 30-day mortality rate of 12% in the intervention group and 54.8% in the control group. Using a two-tailed alpha level of 0.05 and aiming for 90% power (beta=0.1), a sample size of 26 subjects per group is required. To account for a potential non-compliance and dropout rate of 14.6% (12.6% non-compliance+2% dropout rate) estimated from prior research,12 30 patients will be recruited for each group (total=60 patients).

Randomisation and masking

To minimise bias, randomisation will be conducted at The Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University (Guangzhou, China). A research assistant will employ a computer-generated number table to create a randomisation scheme, ensuring fair allocation of participants to either the intervention or control group. While this is an open-label trial (both participants and researchers are aware of treatment assignments), research assistants responsible for collecting outcome data will be masked to participant group allocation.

Data collection and management

The investigators, research assistants and data collectors will undergo specific training in data management procedures. Investigators will be responsible for collecting data at baseline (0–10 days), 30 days and 90 days (as outlined in table 1). These data will include clinical information (cirrhosis, HE grades, ascites, etc), anthropometric measurements (mid-arm circumference, triceps skinfold thickness), grip strength, dry weight, L3-SMI, clinical outcomes and adverse events. For patients discharged before the 90-day follow-up, researchers will conduct regular phone calls or send text messages to provide guidance and reminders for the timely completion of offline follow-up assessments. The researchers will conduct nutrition education sessions for the subjects. These sessions will cover topics such as food weight estimation, interpreting nutrition labels and maintaining dietary diaries.

Anthropometric parameters

Anthropometric parameters will be evaluated, including upper arm circumference and triceps skinfold thickness. The upper arm circumference will be measured at the midpoint between the acromion and the olecranon process using a measuring tape. Simultaneously, the triceps skinfold will be measured at the same location using a skinfold caliper.23

Quality of life

To assess quality of life, the EQ-5D scale will be used.17

Muscle mass and function

The L3-SMI will be calculated based on cross-sectional muscle area at the third lumbar vertebra (L3) measured from a CT scan and adjusted for height squared. The total muscle area at L3 will encompass the erector spinae, psoas major, quadratus lumborum, transversus abdominis, internal oblique, external oblique and rectus abdominis muscles. These measurements will be obtained using dedicated computer software (eg, 3D Slicer V.5.2.2).

Muscle function will be evaluated using two components: grip strength and SPPB score. For grip strength, participants will be instructed to firmly squeeze a hand dynamometer for several seconds in a standardised position (elbow at 90º, forearm held upright) with the test repeated three times.24 The SPPB test is a three-part assessment that evaluates balance, walking speed and the ability to rise from a seated position.25

Blood indicators

The following blood tests will be performed to assess various physiological parameters:

Haematology analysis: a haematology analyser (LABAS F 9000, Maccura Biotechnology, China) will be used to measure the following complete blood count parameters: white blood cell count, neutrophil count, red blood cell count, haemoglobin and platelet count.

Blood biochemical analysis: an automatic biochemical analyser (7600 Series, Hitachi, Japan) will be used to evaluate biochemical indicators, including alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, albumin, alkaline phosphatase, total bilirubin (TB), direct bilirubin, cholinesterase, total cholesterol, blood urea nitrogen and creatinine.

Coagulation function: a fully automatic vertical coagulation analyser (STA-R MAX, Stago, France) will be used to assess coagulation parameters, comprising prothrombin time, prothrombin activity and international normalised ratio.

Scoring systems to assess disease severity

This study will use two scoring systems to assess disease severity: the Model for End-Stage Liver Disease (MELD) and the COSSH-ALCF II score (COSSH-ACLF IIs). The MELD score is calculated using the following formula:

MELD=9.57 × ln (creatinine mg/dL) + 3.78 × ln (TB mg/dL) + 11.20 × ln (INR) + 6.43

The COSSH-ACLF IIs is calculated using the following formula:

COSSIH-IIs=1.649 × ln (INR)+0.457 × ln (HE score) + 0.425 × ln (neutrophil 109/L) + 0.576 × ln (TB µmol/L) + 9.57 × ln (serum urea mmol/L) + 0.033 × AGE (years).26

Statistical analysis

Continuous data will be expressed with mean±SD, while categorical data will be presented as frequency and percentage (%). The normality of the data will be tested using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Nonparametric data will be analysed using rank-sum tests, while continuous variables will be assessed with two-sample t-tests. Categorical variables will be analysed with χ2 tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Subgroup analyses will be conducted by incorporating interaction terms into the regression model to assess for effect modification by key baseline factors, such as age, gender, BMI, ascites and cirrhosis. Statistical significance will be determined by a two-tailed p value of less than 0.05. All statistical analyses will be performed using IBM SPSS Statistics V.20. Additionally, 95% CI will be calculated to estimate the treatment effect size. Three analysis sets will be employed: the full analysis set (FAS), including all randomised patients who received treatment; the per-protocol set (PPS) restricted to those in FAS without major protocol deviations and the safety set (SS), encompassing all subjects who received any experimental treatment. The primary efficacy analysis will be based on both FAS and PPS, with other efficacy analyses relying solely on FAS. Baseline characteristics, remaining efficacy analyses and safety assessments will all be conducted using data from SS.

Data monitoring

All data will be uploaded to the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University to ensure data integrity and minimise the risk of bias. The DMC is free from conflicts of interest with the study sponsors and will regularly review the data for participant safety and trial conduct. Should the DMC or a designated ethics committee or regulatory agency identify evidence of harm to participants, they may recommend or mandate termination of the clinical trial.

Informed consent and privacy protection

This study will be conducted in accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki. Following approval by the Ethics Committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University, the researchers will obtain informed consent from all participants. This informed consent will be presented in clear and understandable language, ensuring participants are fully apprised of all relevant aspects of the clinical trial. In cases where participants are unable to sign the informed consent form, their legally authorised representative may do so on their behalf. The study will commence only after informed consent has been obtained. Throughout the trial, access to participants’ personal information will be restricted to investigators and examiners directly involved in the study. The principal investigator will ensure that the study is conducted under strict supervision. Data related to this clinical trial will be maintained with confidentiality. Disclosure of any data will only occur on formal request from a duly authorised state administrative agency.

Patient and public involvement

The study methods and objectives were independently developed by the researchers without patient involvement. Patients will be responsible for completing meal records. Those assigned to the nutritional intervention group will receive personalised dietary guidance from the research team.

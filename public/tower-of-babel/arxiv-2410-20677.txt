Homological Lagrangian monodromy and Wang exact sequence
Yoshihiro Sugimoto

Abstract
In this paper, we study homological monodromy of a Lagrangian submanifold. We show that homological Lagrangian monodromy is trivial if Hofer norm of a Hamiltonian isotopy is smaller than the minimum energy of J-holomorphic spheres and discs.

In order to state our main results rigorously, we introduce some notations. Hofer energy of a Hamiltonian path
{\{\phi_{t}\}\subset\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle||\{\phi_{t}\}||=\int_{0}^{1}\max_{x\in M}H(t,x)-\min_{x\in M}H(t,x)dt

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\phi_{t}(x)=X_{H_{t}}(\phi_{t}(x)).

Here,
{X_{H_{t}}}
is the Hamiltonian vector field generated by a normalized Hamiltonian function
{H_{t}=H(t,\cdot)}
. We also define Hofer energy of a homotopy class of path
{\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega),\mathrm{Ham}_{L}(M,\omega))}
as follows:

\displaystyle||\gamma||=\inf_{[\{\phi_{t}\}]=\gamma}||\{\phi_{t}\}||.

Let
{\mathcal{J}(M,\omega)}
be the set of compatible almost complex structures on
{(M,\omega)}
. We define the minimum energy of holomorphic spheres and discs as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(M,\omega,J)=\inf\Bigg\{\ \ \int_{S^{2}}u^{*}\omega>0\ \ \Bigg|\ \ \begin{matrix}u:S^{2}\rightarrow M\\
\overline{\partial}_{J}u=0\end{matrix}\ \ \Bigg\}

\displaystyle\sigma_{L}(M,\omega,J)=\inf\Bigg\{\ \ \int_{D^{2}}u^{*}\omega>0\ \ \Bigg|\ \ \begin{matrix}u:(D^{2},\partial D^{2})\rightarrow(M,L)\\
\overline{\partial}_{J}u=0\end{matrix}\ \ \Bigg\}.

Any
{\gamma\in\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega),\mathrm{Ham}_{L}(M,\omega))}
determines a symplectic fibration over a disc
{\pi:P\rightarrow D^{2}}
and a Lagrangian fibration over the boundary
{\pi:Q\rightarrow\partial D^{2}}
. See the next section for details of
P
and
Q
. Let
{TP^{\pi}\subset TP}
be the kernel of the projection:

\displaystyle TP^{\pi}=\{v\in TP\ |\ \pi_{*}(v)=0\}.

Then,
{TP^{\pi}\rightarrow P}
is a symplectic vector bundle with a symplectic form
{\bar{\omega}}
. We denote the set of almost complex structures on
{TP^{\pi}}
by
{\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}=\Bigg\{\widetilde{J}\in\mathrm{End}(T^{\pi}P)\ \Bigg|\ \begin{matrix}\widetilde{J}^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}\\
\widetilde{J}:\mathrm{compatible\ with\ }\bar{\omega}\end{matrix}\Bigg\}.

For any
{\widetilde{J}\in\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}}
, we define
{\sigma_{S}(\gamma,\widetilde{J})}
and
{\sigma_{L}(\gamma,\widetilde{J})}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(\gamma,\widetilde{J})=\inf\Bigg\{\int_{S^{2}}u^{*}\bar{\omega}>0\ \Bigg|\ \begin{matrix}u:S^{2}\rightarrow P\\
\pi\circ u=\mathrm{constant},\overline{\partial}_{\widetilde{J}}u=0\end{matrix}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\sigma_{L}(\gamma,\widetilde{J})=\inf\Bigg\{\int_{D^{2}}u^{*}\bar{\omega}>0\ \Bigg|\ \begin{matrix}u:(D^{2},\partial D^{2})\rightarrow(P,Q)\\
\pi\circ u=\mathrm{constant},\overline{\partial}_{\widetilde{J}}u=0\end{matrix}\Bigg\}.

In other words,
{\sigma_{S}(\gamma,\widetilde{J})}
and

{\widetilde{J}}

be the supremum of them:

\displaystyle\sigma(M,L,\gamma)=\sup_{\widetilde{J}\in\mathcal{J}_{\gamma}}\min\{\sigma_{S}(\gamma,\widetilde{J}),\sigma_{L}(\gamma,\widetilde{J})\}.

The next theorem is our main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{L\subset M}
be a closed Lagrangian submanifold. Let
{\{\phi_{t}\}\subset\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)}
be a Hamiltonian isotopy such that
{\phi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}}
and
{\phi_{1}\in\mathrm{Ham}_{L}(M,\omega)}
hold. If

\displaystyle||[\{\phi_{t}\}]||<\sigma(M,L,[\{\phi_{t}\}])

holds, then

\displaystyle(\phi_{1})_{*}:H_{*}(L:\mathbb{Z}_{2})\longrightarrow H_{*}(L:\mathbb{Z}_{2})

is equal to the identity.

A Lagrangian submanifold
{L\subset M}
is called rational if there is a positive constant
{\eta>0}
so that
{\omega(\pi_{2}(M,L))=\eta\mathbb{Z}}
holds. In this case,
{\sigma(M,L,\gamma)\geq\eta}
holds for any
\gamma
. So, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
{L\subset M}
be a closed rational Lagrangian submanifold and let
{\eta>0}
be the generator of
{\omega(\pi_{2}(M,L))}
. Let
{\{\phi_{t}\}\subset\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)}
be a Hamiltonian isotopy such that
{\phi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}}
,
{\phi_{1}\in\mathrm{Ham}_{L}(M,\omega)}
and
{||\{\phi_{t}\}||<\eta}
hold. Then

\displaystyle(\phi_{1})_{*}:H_{*}(L:\mathbb{Z}_{2})\longrightarrow H_{*}(L:\mathbb{Z}_{2})

is equal to the identity.

1.
Introduction

When we study parabolic bifurcations we are up against changes on such tiny scales that they disappear before our eyes. Consider for example an analytic map
f_{0}(z)=z+z^{2}+O(z^{3})
for which the fixed point equation
f_{0}(z)=z
has a double root at
0
. A generic analytic perturbation
f_{\epsilon}(z)=f_{0}(z)+\epsilon(z)
has two nearby fixed points
z_{1}(\epsilon),z_{2}(\epsilon)
with multipliers close to
1
, i.e.,
z_{1}(\epsilon),z_{2}(\epsilon)\to 0
and
f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(z_{1}(\epsilon)),f^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(z_{2}(\epsilon))\to 1
as
\epsilon\to 0
. What does the dynamics of
f_{\epsilon}
look like near
0
? The theory of parabolic implosions, originally developed by P. Lavaurs in his doctoral thesis, studies precisely this question. A main tool in this theory is an extended notion of Fatou coordinates for near-parabolic maps often known as Douady-Fatou coordinates (see
[
L
]
and
[
D
]
).

A. Douady suggested that the dynamics of a holomorphic map
f
near a parabolic map
f_{0}
with a fixed point of multiplier
1
should look like the time-
1
flow of the vector field
(f(z)-z)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. This idea was developed by M. Shishikura
[
Sh
]
and later in the thesis of R. Oudkerk
[
O
]
written under the supervision of Tan Lei. By its very construction the fixed points of
f
are also the fixed points of the time-
1
flow, with matching multiplicities. However, the two maps have different holomorphic indices at each fixed point. X. Buff came up with the idea of an improved vector field whose time-
1
flow has a holomorphic index matching that of
f
at each fixed point, so it mimics the dynamics of
f
to a higher degree of accuracy. It turns out that primitives (=anti-derivatives) of the dual
1
-forms of Buff vector fields are very close to being Douady-Fatou coordinates in the sense that they conjugate the near-parabolic dynamics to a map which is extremely close to the unit translation. This theme was developed by A. Chéritat in his habilitation thesis
[
C
]
in which he studied parabolic implosions and applications in estimating the conformal radius of quadratic Siegel disks. Our approach has minimal overlap with his work and provides a setup for a range of applications, including the one presented here and those planned for upcoming papers.

Let us briefly outline this setup; full details are provided in §
2
and §
3
. Let
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
be a convex Jordan domain and
f
be holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{U}
such that
\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime}(z))>0
for all
z\in U
and
f(z)\neq z
for all
z\in\partial U
. Define the
Buff form
of
f
by

\omega_{f}=\omega_{f}(z)\,dz:=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)-1}{(f(z)-z)\operatorname{Log}f^{\prime}(z)}\,dz,

where
\operatorname{Log}:{\mathbb{C}}\smallsetminus]-\infty,0]\to\{x+iy:|y|<\pi\}
is the principal branch of the logarithm. The
1
-form
\omega_{f}
is non-vanishing and meromorphic in
U
with finitely many poles at the fixed points of
f
. Moreover, at every fixed point
p=f(p)\in U
,

\operatorname{res}(\omega_{f},p)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\operatorname{Log}f^{\prime}(p)}&\quad\text{if}\ \ f^{\prime}(p)\neq 1\\
\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)&\quad\text{if}\ \ f^{\prime}(p)=1\end{cases}

where
\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)
is the
résidu itératif
of
f
at
p
(see §
3.1
and Lemma
3.1
). The primitive
Z=\phi_{f}(z):=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\omega_{f}(s)\,ds
is a multi-valued function that undergoes the monodromy
Z\mapsto Z+2\pi\mathrm{i}\operatorname{res}(\omega_{f},p)
when
z
winds once around a fixed point
p
. We call
\phi_{f}
the
rectifying coordinate
of
\omega_{f}
since it transforms
\omega_{f}
to the
1
-form
dZ
. The collection of all local branches of
\phi_{f}
define a Riemann surface with a translation structure.

If
z\in U\cap f^{-1}(U)
is not a fixed point of
f
, neither is any point of the segment
[z,f(z)]\subset U
(Lemma
3.3
). Using this fact, the dynamics of
f
away from the fixed points can be lifted under the rectifying coordinate
Z=\phi_{f}(z)
to the new dynamics
F:Z\mapsto Z+1+u_{f}
, where

u_{f}(z):=-1+\int_{[z,f(z)]}\omega_{f}(s)\,ds

measures the deviation of
F
from being the unit translation. The holomorphic function
u_{f}
, defined on the set of points in
U\cap f^{-1}(U)
that are not fixed under
f
, extends holomorphically to zero across each fixed point
p=f(p)
. Moreover, if
p
has multiplier
1
and fixed point multiplicity
q+1\geq 2
, then
u_{f}
has a zero of order
2q
at
p
(Lemma
3.4
). Thus, when
f
is restricted to a small neighborhood of a fixed point, its lifted dynamics
F
is close to the unit translation. More importantly, this property persists under small perturbations of
f
.

In §
4
we apply this setup to perturbations of a holomorphic map
g(z)=\lambda z+O(z^{2})
with a non-degenerate parabolic fixed point at
0
whose multiplier
\lambda
is a primitive
q
th root of unity. After an initial analytic change of coordinates we may assume

f:=g^{\circ q}=z+z^{q+1}+bz^{2q+1}+O(z^{2q+2}),

where
b\in{\mathbb{C}}
is the holomorphic index of
f
at
0
. Take a sequence
g_{n}(z)=\lambda_{n}z+O(z^{2})
of analytic perturbations of
g
such that
|\lambda_{n}|\neq 1
for all
n
. Let
f,f_{n}:=g_{n}^{\circ q}
be holomorphic in a neighborhood of a small closed disk
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
and
f_{n}\to f
uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
as
n\to\infty
. The parabolic fixed point of
g
at
0
bifurcates into a simple fixed point of
g_{n}
at
0
of multiplier
\lambda_{n}=g^{\prime}_{n}(0)
and a
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}=\{p_{n,1},\ldots,p_{n,q}\}
of multiplier
\mu_{n}:=(f_{n})^{\prime}(p_{n,j})
independent of
j
. The points in
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
are asymptotically the vertices of the regular
q
-gon formed by the
q
th roots of
1-\lambda_{n}^{q}
. Set

\Lambda_{n}:=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\lambda_{n}^{q}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{M}_{n}:=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\mu_{n}}.

Then
\Lambda_{n},\operatorname{M}_{n}\to\infty
, but
\Lambda_{n}+q\operatorname{M}_{n}\to\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,0)
as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, we have the following expressions for the multi-valued rectifying coordinates
\phi=\phi_{f}
and
\phi_{n}=\phi_{f_{n}}
of the Buff forms
\omega=\omega_{f}
and
\omega_{n}=\omega_{f_{n}}
, respectively:

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{qz^{q}}+\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,0)\,\log z+E(z),

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{n}\log z+\operatorname{M}_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\log(z-p_{n,j})+E_{n}(z).

Here the error terms
E,E_{n}
are analytic in a neighborhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
with
E(0)=E_{n}(0)=0
and
E_{n}\to E
uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
.

Theorem A

(Lifted dynamics is a uniform near-translation)
.

For every
0<\epsilon<1
there is an
0<r<r_{0}
such that the following holds for all sufficiently large
n
:

If
z\in{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
is not fixed under
f_{n}
, the curve
t\mapsto z_{n,t}=(1-t)z+tf_{n}(z)
parametrizing the segment
[z,f_{n}(z)]
lifts under
\phi_{n}
to a curve
t\mapsto Z_{n,t}
which satisfies

(1.1)

|Z_{n,t}-(Z_{n,0}+t)|<\epsilon t\qquad(0\leq t\leq 1).

Similarly, the curve
t\mapsto\hat{z}_{n,t}=(1-t)z+tf_{n}^{-1}(z)
parametrizing
[z,f_{n}^{-1}(z)]
lifts under
\phi_{n}
to a curve
t\mapsto\hat{Z}_{n,t}
which satisfies

(1.2)

|\hat{Z}_{n,t}-(\hat{Z}_{n,0}-t)|<\epsilon t\qquad(0\leq t\leq 1).

The special case
t=1
of the above bounds means that for all sufficiently large
n
the dynamics of
f_{n},f_{n}^{-1}
in
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
can be lifted under
\phi_{n}
to maps
F_{n},F_{n}^{-1}
that satisfy
|F_{n}(Z)-(Z+1)|<\epsilon
and
|F_{n}^{-1}(Z)-(Z-1)|<\epsilon
. If the first
k
iterates of
z
under
f_{n}
or
f_{n}^{-1}
stay in
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)\smallsetminus(\{0\}\cup{\mathscr{O}}_{n})
, it follows that the first
k
iterates of
Z
under
F_{n}
or
F_{n}^{-1}
stay in the cones

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}^{+}_{\epsilon}(Z)

\displaystyle:=\{Z+R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}:R>0,|\theta|<\sin^{-1}\epsilon\}\vskip 6.0pt

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{C}}^{-}_{\epsilon}(Z)

\displaystyle:=\{Z+R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}:R>0,|\theta-\pi|<\sin^{-1}\epsilon\},

respectively. The bounds (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can be interpreted geometrically as saying that the segment
[z,f_{n}(z)]
lifts under
\phi_{n}
to a curve joining
Z
and
\hat{Z}=F_{n}(Z)
that lies in
{\mathscr{C}}^{+}_{\epsilon}(Z)\cap{\mathscr{C}}^{-}_{\epsilon}(\hat{Z})
(see Fig.

In an ongoing joint project, we set to show that under mild conditions on the multipliers
\lambda_{n}
(roughly, not approaching
\lambda
too radially) the orbits of
F_{n}
have uniform distance to the orbits of the unit translation, so they stay in horizontal strips rather than cones. This is accomplished by showing that for the normalized Douady-Fatou coordinate
\Phi_{n}
which conjugates
F_{n}
to the unit translation the distance
\|\Phi_{n}-\operatorname{id}\|_{\infty}
is uniformly bounded. Such estimates are of interest in the study of near-parabolic regime and have applications, for example, in understanding the geometry of the limbs of the Mandelbrot set.

In §
5
we use Theorem
A
and the framework provided by Buff forms to study the behavior of invariant curves of near-parabolic maps. To motivate our result, let us first consider the familiar case of polynomial maps and their external rays.
Let
P_{n},P
be polynomials of degree
d\geq 2
with connected Julia sets such that
P_{n}\to P
, and take an angle
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}}
which has period
q
under multiplication by
d
. Consider the external rays
R_{P_{n}}(\theta),R_{P}(\theta)
landing at
\zeta_{n},\zeta
respectively. These rays come equipped with natural parametrizations by the Green’s potential
s\in\,]0,+\infty[
. Working with the parameter
t=\log s/\log(d^{q})
instead, we can think of the tails of the external rays
R_{P_{n}}(\theta),R_{P}(\theta)
as arcs
\gamma_{n},\gamma:]-\infty,0]\to{\mathbb{C}}
, with
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{n}(t)=\zeta_{n}
and
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma(t)=\zeta
, which satisfy the invariance relations
P_{n}^{\circ q}(\gamma_{n}(t))=\gamma_{n}(t+1)
and
P^{\circ q}(\gamma(t))=\gamma(t+1)
. Continuity of the Böttcher coordinate shows that
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
uniformly on the fundamental segment
[-1,0]
, hence on every compact subset of
]-\infty,0]
. It is well known that if
\zeta
is repelling, then
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
uniformly on
]-\infty,0]
and in particular
\zeta_{n}\to\zeta
. However, when
\zeta
is parabolic, the behavior of
\gamma_{n}
can be wild depending on how
P_{n}
approaches
P
in the parameter space of polynomials of degree
d
. In such cases the Hausdorff limit of
\gamma_{n}([-\infty,0])
can include additional heteroclinic or homoclinic arcs in the Fatou set of
P
. We refer to our recent work
[
PZ1
]
for a comprehensive analysis of the structure of possible Hausdorff limits. An emerging theme in that work was that to create wild behavior of
\gamma_{n}
one often needs the multipliers of the bifurcated cycle of
P_{n}
near
\zeta
to approach a root of unity tangentially (compare
[
PZ1
, Theorem D]
). Here we justify this statement in a general setting beyond polynomials and external rays.

Once again consider
g_{n}(z)=\lambda_{n}z+O(z^{2})\to g(z)=\lambda z+O(z^{2})
as in the discussion leading to Theorem
A
. Thus,
\lambda
is a primitive
q
th root of unity,
|\lambda_{n}|\neq 1
, and
f_{n}=g_{n}^{\circ q}\to f=g^{\circ q}
uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
. Suppose
\gamma:\ ]-\infty,0]\to{\mathbb{D}}(0,r_{0})
is an
f
-invariant curve that satisfies
f(\gamma(t))=\gamma(t+1)
and
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma(t)=0
. Take a sequence
\gamma_{n}:\ ]-\infty,0]\to{\mathbb{C}}
of
f_{n}
-invariant curves satisfying
f_{n}(\gamma_{n}(t))=\gamma_{n}(t+1)
such that
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
uniformly on compact subsets of
]-\infty,0]
. Recall that
\lambda_{n}
and
\mu_{n}
are the multipliers of the fixed point
0
and the
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
for
g_{n}
, respectively. It is easy to check that
\lambda_{n}^{q}\to 1
non-tangentially if and only if
\mu_{n}\to 1
non-tangentially as
n\to\infty
. In this case, and after passing to a subsequence, we may assume that either
|\lambda_{n}|>1,|\mu_{n}|<1
or
|\lambda_{n}|<1,|\mu_{n}|>1
for all
n
(see §
5.1
).

Theorem B

(Tameness)
.

Suppose
\lambda_{n}^{q}\to 1
(equivalently,
\mu_{n}\to 1
) non-tangentially as
n\to\infty
. Then

(i)

For all sufficiently large
n
the curve
\gamma_{n}
lands at a repelling fixed point of
f_{n}
. More precisely,
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{n}(t)=0
if
|\lambda_{n}|>1
, and
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{n}(t)=p_{n,j}
for some
1\leq j\leq q
if
|\lambda_{n}|<1
.

(ii)

As
n\to\infty
,
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
uniformly on
]-\infty,0]
. In particular,
\gamma_{n}([-\infty,0])\to\gamma([-\infty,0])
in the Hausdorff metric.

The “tameness” in the title refers to the uniform convergence in (ii) which precludes the formation of additional homoclinic arcs in the Hausdorff limit of
\gamma_{n}([-\infty,0])
. Observe that
\gamma,g(\gamma),\ldots,g^{\circ q-1}(\gamma)
form a
q
-cycle of curves landing at
0
with a well-defined combinatorial rotation number
0\leq p/q<1
with
\gcd(p,q)=1
. They are asymptotic to the
q
repelling directions of the parabolic point
0
given by the
q
th roots of unity. When
|\lambda_{n}|>1
, the curves
\gamma_{n},g_{n}(\gamma_{n}),\ldots,g_{n}^{\circ q-1}(\gamma_{n})
landing at
0
form a
q
-cycle of the same combinatorial rotation number
p/q
. When
|\lambda_{n}|<1
, each point of the
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
remains in a definite sector around one of the repelling directions (see §
5.1
) and is the landing point of the curve whose unperturbed counterpart lands at
0
asymptotic to that direction. The proof of Theorem
B
, presented in §
5.2
, is based on a detailed analysis of the lifted dynamics
F_{n}
to construct trapping regions for the curves
\gamma_{n}
to ensure their landing at the anticipated fixed point and to control their behavior in the Hausdorff metric.

The motivating example of Theorem
B
is, of course, when
g,g_{n}
are restrictions of polynomial maps of a given degree
\geq 2
and
\gamma,\gamma_{n}
are tails of their external rays of a given periodic angle. Another example is when
g,g_{n}
are restrictions of maps in the Eremenko-Lyubich class
\mathcal{B}
(transcendental entire functions with bounded singular set) and
\gamma,\gamma_{n}
are tails of their hairs. Notice, however, that we have made no assumption on the smoothness of either
\gamma
or the
\gamma_{n}
.

B
in a more concrete form:

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
P
be a polynomial map of degree
d\geq 2
with connected Julia set. Let
\zeta
be a non-degenerate parabolic fixed point of
P
, with multiplier a primitive
q
th root of unity, and suppose
\zeta
is the landing point of a
q
-cycle of external rays
R_{P}(\theta_{1}),\ldots,R_{P}(\theta_{q})
. Take a sequence
P_{n}\to P
of polynomials of degree
d
and suppose
P_{n}
has a fixed point
\zeta_{n}
of multiplier
\lambda_{n}
and a
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
of multiplier
\mu_{n}
which converge to
\zeta
as
n\to\infty
. If
\lambda_{n}^{q}\to 1
(equivalently,
\mu_{n}\to 1
) non-tangentially, then for all sufficiently large

the external rays
R_{P_{n}}(\theta_{1}),\ldots,R_{P_{n}}(\theta_{q})
land at
\zeta_{n}
if
|\lambda_{n}|>1
and on

if

. In either case
\overline{R_{P_{n}}}(\theta_{j})\to\overline{R_{P}}(\theta_{j})

Compare Fig.
2
. This is the generalization of a result of J. Milnor in
[

]
who proved it in the case

when

radially (i.e., along the real axis). Some topological and measure-theoretic implications of non-tangential multiplier approach for geometrically finite rational maps are studied by C. McMullen in

.

It is reasonable to speculate that the assumption of non-tangential multiplier approach in Theorem

Holomorphic

Throughout the paper the following notation will be adopted:

\bullet

\bullet

{\mathbb{H}}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}

\bullet

is the Euclidean segment between

\bullet

”

Consider a bounded domain
U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and assume for simplicity that the boundary
\partial U
is a smooth Jordan curve. Let
\chi=\chi(z)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
be a holomorphic vector field defined in a neighborhood of the closure
\overline{U}
, with
\chi\neq 0
on
\partial U
. We denote by
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
the zeros of
\chi
in
U
. The dual
1
-form
\omega=\omega(z)\,dz
with
\omega(z)=1/\chi(z)
is meromorphic in
U
with poles at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
. Both
\chi,\omega
are holomorphic and non-vanishing in the punctured domain
U^{*}:=U\smallsetminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
.

2.1.
Rectifying coordinates

In general
\omega
is not the differential of a single-valued

2.4.
Periodic orbits of
\chi

Let
\gamma
be a closed trajectory of
\chi
in
U
of period
\tau>0
. By the Poincaré-Hopf theorem the Jordan domain bounded by
\gamma
contains a unique singularity
p
of index (=multiplicity)
1
, where
\operatorname{res}(\omega,p)=\tau/(2\pi\mathrm{i})
is purely imaginary. The time-
\tau
flow of
\chi
is a holomorphic function which restricts to the identity map on
\gamma
, hence in the maximal neighborhood
W\subset U
of
\gamma
in which it is defined. It is not hard to check that
W
is a topological disk and
W\smallsetminus\{p\}
is foliated by closed trajectories of period
\tau
(compare
[
St
, §9]
for a similar situation in the context of quadratic differentials). Any local branch of the rectifying coordinate
Z=\phi(z)
can be continued analytically in
W\smallsetminus\{p\}
which yields a multi-valued function onto a half-plane of form
\{Z:\operatorname{Im}(Z)>Y_{0}\}
. The inverse

\psi:=\phi^{-1}:\{Z:\operatorname{Im}(Z)>Y_{0}\}\to W\smallsetminus\{p\}

defines an infinite cyclic holomorphic covering map with the deck group generated by the translation
Z\mapsto Z+\tau
. The obstruction to enlarging
W
further can be only
\partial W
meeting the boundary
\partial U
or a singularity in
U
. The latter cannot happen since all points on the closed trajectory
\psi(\{Z:\operatorname{Im}(Z)=Y_{0}+1\})
are at
|\omega|
-distance
1
to
\partial W
and
+\infty
to the singular points in
U
(see §
2.3
). Our standing assumption that
\chi
is defined in a neighborhood of
\overline{U}
and is non-zero on
\partial U
implies that
\partial W
itself is a closed trajectory of
\chi
of the same period
\tau
. It follows that the smooth Jordan curves
\partial W
and
\partial U
intersect and they are tangent to each other wherever they meet.

Conversely, suppose
p
is a singular point of
\chi
in
U
with multiplicity
m=1
such that
c=\operatorname{res}(\omega,p)\neq 0
is purely imaginary. Then by the discussion in §
2.2
there is a neighborhood of
p
in
U
in which
\chi
is analytically transformed to
(w/c)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}
, so the real-time trajectories of
\chi
are closed with period
\tau=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,c
. By the preceding paragraph there is a largest flow-invariant Jordan domain neighborhood
W\subset U
of
p
such that
W\smallsetminus\{p\}
is foliated by closed trajectories of the same period
\tau
. Moreover,
\partial W\cap\partial U\neq\emptyset
and the two Jordan curves are tangent wherever they meet. We call
W
the
canonical neighborhood
of

for the vector field
\chi
. Notice that this neighborhood depends on the choice of
U
.

Remark 2.1
.

As a consequence of the preceding remarks, we see that
a holomorphic vector field in
U
can never have a limit cycle
. It follows from the Poincaré-Bendixson theorem
[
HSD
]
that every trajectory
\gamma:\,]a,+\infty[\to U^{*}
is either periodic, or
\lim_{t\to+\infty}\gamma(t)
is a singular point. A similar statement holds for every trajectory
\gamma:\,]-\infty,b[\to U^{*}
.

2.5.
Rotations of vector fields

Suppose all singularities
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
of
\chi
in
U
have multiplicity
1
, so
c_{j}:=\operatorname{res}(\omega,p_{j})
is non-zero for all
1\leq j\leq k
. Consider the unimodular complex numbers (or unit vectors)

\alpha_{j}:=\mathrm{i}\,\frac{c_{j}}{|c_{j}|}\qquad(1\leq j\leq k).

The rotated vector field
\alpha_{j}\chi
has a purely imaginary residue at
p_{j}
, so there is a canonical neighborhood
W_{j}\subset U
of
p_{j}
for the vector field
\alpha_{j}\,\chi
in the sense described above. Each trajectory of
\alpha_{j}\,\chi
in
\overline{W_{j}}\smallsetminus\{p_{j}\}
is closed of period
2\pi|c_{j}|
and under a rectifying coordinate
Z=\phi(z)
of
\chi
they all map to straight lines parallel to
\alpha_{j}
. There is a
Y_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
for which the inverse
\psi=\phi^{-1}:\{Z:\operatorname{Im}(Z/\alpha_{j})>Y_{j}\}\to W_{j}\smallsetminus\{p_{j}\}
is an infinite cyclic covering map whose deck group is generated by the translation
Z\mapsto Z+2\pi\mathrm{i}\,c_{j}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p_{i},p_{j}
be distinct singularities of
\chi
in
U
and
\gamma_{i}\subset\overline{W_{i}}
and
\gamma_{j}\subset\overline{W_{j}}
be closed trajectories of the corresponding rotated fields
\alpha_{i}\chi
and
\alpha_{j}\chi
. Then
\gamma_{i}\cap\gamma_{j}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

First we note that
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
cannot be tangent to each other at any point. If this were to happen at some point
z
, we would have
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}
or
\alpha_{i}=-\alpha_{j}
. In the first case
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
would be two different trajectories of the same vector field
\alpha_{i}\,\chi
passing through
z
, which is impossible by the uniqueness of solutions of differential equations. In the second case,
\gamma_{i}
and the reverse curve
t\mapsto\gamma_{j}(-t)
would be two different trajectories of
\alpha_{i}\,\chi
passing through
z
, which is again impossible. Thus,
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{j}
can only intersect transversally. Suppose they meet transversally at some
z
where
\gamma_{i}
crosses
\gamma_{j}
from, say, left to right in the sense that the pair
(\alpha_{i}\chi(z),\alpha_{j}\chi(z))
forms a positive basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Since
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}
are Jordan curves, they must meet transversally at another point
z^{\prime}
where
\gamma_{i}
crosses
\gamma_{j}
from right to left in the sense that
(\alpha_{i}\chi(z),\alpha_{j}\chi(z))
forms a negative basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Looking at the pair of vectors at
z
gives
\operatorname{Im}(\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{i})>0
while at
z^{\prime}
we obtain
\operatorname{Im}(\alpha_{j}/\alpha_{i})<0
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Suppose all singularities
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
of
\chi
in

have multiplicity

. Consider the unimodular complex numbers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}
as above and let

3.
Buff forms and their basic properties

3.1.
Holomorphic index and résidu iteratif

We begin by briefly recalling some basic definitions; for details we refer to the paper of X. Buff and A. Epstein
[
BE
]
(see also
[
M1
]
and
[
C
]
). Let
p
be an isolated fixed point of a holomorphic map
f
, so
f(z)=p+\lambda(z-p)+O((z-p)^{2})
in some neighborhood of
p
. Here
\lambda:=f^{\prime}(p)
is the
multiplier
of the fixed point
p
. The
holomorphic index
of
f
at
p
is defined by

\iota(f,p):=\operatorname{res}\Big(\frac{dz}{z-f(z)},p\Big)=\int_{|z-p|=\epsilon}\frac{dz}{z-f(z)},

which is invariant under analytic change of coordinates. It is easy to check that

(3.1)

\iota(f,p)=\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\qquad\text{if}\ \lambda\neq 1.

A closely related quantity is the
résidu itératif
of
f
at
p
defined by

\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p):=\frac{m}{2}-\iota(f,p),

where
m\geq 1
is the fixed point multiplicity of
p
, i.e., the multiplicity of
p
as a root of
z-f(z)=0
. In particular,

(3.2)

\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}\qquad\text{if}\ \lambda\neq 1.

Thus, a simple fixed point
p
is repelling or attracting according as
\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)
has positive or negative real part.

When
\lambda=1
the formulas (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) for the holomorphic index and résidu itératif are no longer valid. To calculate them in this case, suppose
p
has multiplier
1
and fixed point multiplicity
m\geq 2
. Then there is an analytic change of coordinates in which
f
has the local normal form

f(z)=z+a(z-p)^{m}+b(z-p)^{2m-1}+O((z-p)^{2m})

with
a,b\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
a\neq 0
. A straightforward computation then shows that

(3.3)

\iota(f,p)=\frac{b}{a^{2}},\qquad\text{so}\qquad\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)=\frac{m}{2}-\frac{b}{a^{2}}.

For a generic perturbation
f_{\epsilon}
of
f
the parabolic fixed point
p
splits into
m
simple fixed points
p_{1}(\epsilon),\ldots,p_{m}(\epsilon)
of multipliers
\lambda_{1}(\epsilon),\ldots,\lambda_{m}(\epsilon)
. Continuity of the holomorphic index then shows that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{j=1}^{m}1/(1-\lambda_{j}(\epsilon))=\iota(f,p)
, or

(3.4)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f_{\epsilon},p_{j}(\epsilon))=\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p).

When
p
is parabolic with multiplier
\lambda
a primitive
q
th root of unity, we can apply the preceding remarks to the iterate
f^{\circ q}
. In this case the multiplicity of
p
as a fixed point of
f^{\circ q}
is necessarily of the form
m=\nu q+1
for some integer
\nu\geq 1
called the
degeneracy order
of
p
, the case
\nu=1
being considered non-degenerate. Geometrically, there are
m-1=\nu q
immediate parabolic basins of
f
attached to
p
, and these fall into
\nu
disjoint cycles of length
q
.

3.2.
Buff forms and their residues

For simplicity, we will make the following standing assumptions for the remainder of this section:

\bullet

U\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is a convex domain bounded by a smooth Jordan curve (in our applications
U
can be taken to be a Euclidean disk).

\bullet

f
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{U}
and
\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime})>0
in
U
.

\bullet

f(z)\neq z
for all
z\in\partial U
.

Define the
Buff form
of
f
by

\omega_{f}=\omega_{f}(z)\,dz:=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)-1}{(f(z)-z)\operatorname{Log}f^{\prime}(z)}\,dz.

Here
\operatorname{Log}:{\mathbb{C}}\smallsetminus]-\infty,0]\to\{x+iy:|y|<\pi\}
is the principal branch of the logarithm. The
1
-form
\omega_{f}
is non-vanishing and meromorphic in
U
with finitely many poles at the fixed points of
f
. The dual point of view is provided by the
Buff vector field

\chi_{f}=\chi_{f}(z)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}:=\frac{(f(z)-z)\operatorname{Log}f^{\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)-1}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial z}

which is holomorphic in
U
with finitely many zeros at the fixed points of
f
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every fixed point
p=f(p)\in U
,

\operatorname{res}(\omega_{f},p)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\operatorname{Log}f^{\prime}(p)}&\quad\text{if}\ \ f^{\prime}(p)\neq 1\\
\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)&\quad\text{if}\ \ f^{\prime}(p)=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

This can be found in
[
C
, §1.3]
. For convenience we give a short proof here; this will also provide local expansions for later use. Let
p=0
and
\lambda:=f^{\prime}(0)
. If
\lambda\neq 1
, then

\omega_{f}(z)=\frac{(\lambda-1)+O(z)}{((\lambda-1)z+O(z^{2}))\operatorname{Log}(\lambda+O(z))}=\frac{1}{z\operatorname{Log}\lambda}+O(1),

which shows
\operatorname{res}(\omega_{f},0)=1/\operatorname{Log}\lambda
. Suppose now that
0
has multiplier
\lambda=1
and fixed point multiplicity
m=q+1\geq 2
, so

(3.5)

f(z)=z+\Delta(z)\quad\text{where}\quad\Delta(z)=az^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2})\quad\text{with}\quad a\neq 0.

Using the Taylor expansion

\frac{z}{\operatorname{Log}(1+z)}=1+\frac{1}{2}z-\frac{1}{12}z^{2}+O(z^{3})\qquad\text{as}\ z\to 0,

we obtain the following expression for the Buff form of
f
:

(3.6)

\omega_{f}(z)=\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(z)}{\Delta(z)\log(1+\Delta^{\prime}(z))}=\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{12}\Delta^{\prime}(z)^{2}}{\Delta(z)}+O(z^{2q-1}).

Since the term
\Delta^{\prime}(z)^{2}/\Delta(z)
is holomorphic near
0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\operatorname{res}(\omega_{f},0)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{res}\Big(\frac{1}{\Delta},0\Big)+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{res}\Big(\frac{\Delta^{\prime}}{\Delta},0\Big)

\displaystyle=-\iota(f,0)+\frac{q+1}{2}=\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,0).\qed

Remark 3.2
.

The Buff form is not invariant under local analytic conjugation, but it is asymptotically invariant. More precisely, if
w=h(z)
is a local analytic change of coordinates near
0
which conjugates the parabolic germs

f(z)=z+az^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2})\quad\text{and}\quad g(w)=w+bw^{q+1}+O(w^{q+2})\quad(a,b\neq 0)

in the sense that
g\circ h=h\circ f
, then
h^{*}(\omega_{g})(z)-\omega_{f}(z)=O(z^{q})
.

3.3.
The lifted dynamics

When suitably lifted under the rectifying coordinate
\phi_{f}
of the Buff form
\omega_{f}
, the dynamics of
f
becomes a new dynamics
F
with a rather simple description. To make this idea precise, we start with the following lemma which asserts that under mild conditions the Euclidean segment
[z,f(z)]
does not contain any fixed points of
f
. Set
V:=U\cap f^{-1}(U)
,
U^{\ast}:=\{z\in U:f(z)\neq z\}
, and
V^{\ast}:=\{z\in V:f(z)\neq z\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
z\in V^{\ast}
then
[z,f(z)]\subset U^{\ast}
.

Proof.

We have
[z,f(z)]\subset U
by convexity. Take the parametrization of
[z,f(z)]
given by
z_{t}:=(1-t)z+tf(z)
for
0\leq t\leq 1
. Let
m:=\min_{[z,f(z)]}\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime})>0
. Then, for any
0\leq a\leq 1
,

\displaystyle|f(z_{a})-z_{a}|

\displaystyle=\left|(f(z)-z)+\int_{[z,z_{a}]}(f^{\prime}(\zeta)-1)\,d\zeta\right|=|f(z)-z|\ \left|1+\int_{0}^{a}(f^{\prime}(z_{t})-1)\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\geq|f(z)-z|\left(1+\int_{0}^{a}\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime}(z_{t})-1)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle\geq|f(z)-z|\ (1+a(m-1))=|f(z)-z|\ (am+1-a)>0.\qed

To construct the lift of
f
carefully, we once again introduce a universal covering map
h:({\mathbb{H}},\zeta_{0})\to(U^{\ast},z_{0})
, the pull-back
\tilde{\omega}_{f}:=h^{\ast}\omega_{f}
and the primitive
\tilde{\phi}_{f}:{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by
Z=\tilde{\phi}_{f}(\zeta)=\int_{[\zeta_{0},\zeta]}\tilde{\omega}_{f}(s)\,ds
, as in §
2.1
. Define a map
\tilde{f}:h^{-1}(V^{\ast})\to{\mathbb{H}}
as follows. Let
\zeta\in h^{-1}(V^{\ast})
and
z=h(\zeta)
. By Lemma
3.3
the segment
[z,f(z)]
is contained in
U^{\ast}
, so it lifts uniquely under
h
to a path which starts at
\zeta
and ends at some
\zeta^{\prime}
. Define
\tilde{f}(\zeta):=\zeta^{\prime}
. Evidently
\tilde{f}
satisfies
h\circ\tilde{f}=f\circ h
and in particular it is holomorphic. Moreover,

\tilde{\phi}_{f}(\tilde{f}(\zeta))-\tilde{\phi}_{f}(\zeta)=\int_{[\zeta,\tilde{f}(\zeta)]}\tilde{\omega}_{f}(s)\,ds=\int_{[z,f(z)]}\omega_{f}(s)\,ds

since
[z,f(z)]
and
h([\zeta,\tilde{f}(\zeta)])
are homotopic in
U^{\ast}
. In other words, the lifted dynamics
\tilde{f}
in the rectifying coordinate
Z=\tilde{\phi}_{f}(\zeta)
, or equivalently in the multi-valued rectifying coordinate
Z=\phi_{f}(z)=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}\omega_{f}(s)\,ds
, is seen as the map

F:Z\mapsto Z+1+u_{f},

where

u_{f}(z):=-1+\int_{[z,f(z)]}\omega_{f}(s)\,ds\qquad(z\in V^{\ast}).

Notice how
u_{f}
, the difference between
F(Z)
and
Z+1
, does not depend on the branch of
Z=\phi(z)
and only depends on
z
. We will see that in sufficiently small neighborhoods of a fixed point of
f
the function
u_{f}
can be made arbitrarily small and therefore the lifted dynamics under
\phi_{f}
is seen as a near unit translation. This picture persists under perturbations
f_{\epsilon}
of
f
, so
\phi_{f_{\epsilon}}
serves as an approximate Douady-Fatou coordinate for such perturbations. This point of view is elucidated in the following lemma and later in §
4.3
, and plays a central role in the proof of Theorem
B
in §
5.2
.

It was first observed by X. Buff that the function
u_{f}
extends to zero across the fixed points of
f
, so it gives rise to a holomorphic function
u_{f}:V\to{\mathbb{C}}
. We sharpen this observation in the following statement:

Lemma 3.4
.

If
p
is a fixed point of
f
in
U
, then
u_{f}
extends holomorphically to
p
, with
u_{f}(p)=0
. Moreover, if
p
has multiplier
1
and fixed point multiplicity
q+1\geq 2
, then
u_{f}
has a zero of order
2q
at
p
.

Proof.

Take
p=0
and set
\lambda=f^{\prime}(0)
. First consider the easier case where
\lambda\neq 1
. Write
f(z)=zg(z)
, where
g(z)=\lambda+O(z)
. By the proof of Lemma
3.1
,
\omega_{f}(z)=1/(z\operatorname{Log}\lambda)+O(1)
in a neighborhood of
0
. It follows that

\displaystyle u_{f}(z)

\displaystyle=-1+(f(z)-z)\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{f}(z+t(f(z)-z))\,dt

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{z(g(z)-1)}{\operatorname{Log}\lambda}\,\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{z+tz(g(z)-1)}+O(1)\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=-1+\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\lambda}\operatorname{Log}(g(z))+O(z)=O(z).

Now suppose
\lambda=1
, so
f
has the local form (
3.5
) near
0
. Recall that the error term
\Delta(z)=f(z)-z
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta(z)

\displaystyle=az^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2})\qquad(a\neq 0)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=a(q+1)z^{q}+O(z^{q+1})

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=aq(q+1)z^{q-1}+O(z^{q}).

For
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\Delta(z+t\Delta(z))

\displaystyle=\Delta(z)+t\Delta(z)\Delta^{\prime}(z)+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\Delta^{2}(z)\Delta^{\prime\prime}(z)+O(z^{4q+1})

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}(z+t\Delta(z))

\displaystyle=\Delta^{\prime}(z)+t\Delta(z)\Delta^{\prime\prime}(z)+O(z^{3q}).

For simplicity, let us fix
z
and write
\Delta=\Delta(z),\Delta^{\prime}=\Delta^{\prime}(z),\Delta^{\prime\prime}=\Delta^{\prime\prime}(z)
. By (
3.6
),

\omega_{f}(z+t\Delta)=\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{\prime}+t\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q}))-\frac{1}{12}(\Delta^{\prime}+t\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q}))^{2}}{\Delta+t\Delta\Delta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\Delta^{2}\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{4q+1})}+O(z^{2q-1}),

so

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \Delta\,\omega_{f}(z+t\Delta)=\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}(\Delta^{\prime}+t\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q}))-\frac{1}{12}(\Delta^{\prime}+t\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q}))^{2}}{1+t\Delta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q})}+O(z^{3q})

\displaystyle=\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{12}(\Delta^{\prime})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}t\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q})\right)\left(1-t\Delta^{\prime}-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+t^{2}(\Delta^{\prime})^{2}+O(z^{3q})\right)+O(z^{3q})

(3.7)

\displaystyle=1+\left(-t+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\Delta^{\prime}+\left(t^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}t-\tfrac{1}{12}\right)(\Delta^{\prime})^{2}+\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}t\right)\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q}).

Integrating from
t=0
to
t=1
, the coefficients of
\Delta^{\prime}
and
(\Delta^{\prime})^{2}
vanish and the coefficient of
\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}
gives
1/12
. Thus,

\displaystyle u_{f}(z)

\displaystyle=-1+\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\,\omega_{f}(z+t\Delta)\,dt=\frac{1}{12}\Delta\Delta^{\prime\prime}+O(z^{3q})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{12}a^{2}q(q+1)z^{2q}+O(z^{2q+1}),

as required.
∎

It follows from the above lemma that in a sufficiently small neighborhood of a fixed point of
f
, where
|u_{f}|<\epsilon
, the pair
z,f(z)
can be lifted under
\phi_{f}
to a pair
Z,F(Z)
with the property
|F(Z)-Z-1|<\epsilon
. The following lemma achieves a similar control over the lift of the entire segment
[z,f(z)]
. For
z\in V^{*}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, let
z_{t}:=(1-t)z+tf(z)
and define

(3.8)

u_{f,t}(z):=-t+\int_{[z,z_{t}]}\omega_{f}(s)\,ds.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
p\in U
is a parabolic fixed point of
f
with multiplicity
q+1\geq 2
. For every
0<\epsilon<1
there is an
r>0
such that
|u_{f,t}(z)|<\epsilon t
for
(z,t)\in{\mathbb{D}}(p,r)\times[0,1]
. As a result, if
z\in{\mathbb{D}}(p,r)
is not fixed under
f
, the curve
t\mapsto z_{t}
parametrizing
[z,f(z)]
lifts under
\phi_{f}
to a curve
t\mapsto Z_{t}
which satisfies
|Z_{t}-(Z_{0}+t)|<\epsilon t
.

Proof.

The second claim follows immediately from the first by setting
Z_{t}=Z_{0}+t+u_{f,t}(z)
. For the first claim, let
p=0
and
\Delta(z)=f(z)-z=O(z^{q+1})
. Take
r>0
small enough such that

\sup_{|z|\leq r}\max\{|\Delta(z)|,|\Delta^{\prime}(z)|,|\Delta^{\prime\prime}(z)|\}<\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon.

We can also arrange that the
O(z^{q+1})
remainder term in (
3.3
) is uniformly bounded above by
\epsilon/2
in the disk
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
. By (
3.3
),
u_{f,t}(z)
can be expressed as

\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}t^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}t\right)\Delta^{\prime}(z)+\left(\tfrac{1}{3}t^{3}-\tfrac{1}{4}t^{2}-\tfrac{1}{12}t\right)(\Delta^{\prime}(z))^{2}+\left(-\tfrac{1}{6}t^{3}+\tfrac{1}{4}t^{2}\right)\Delta(z)\Delta^{\prime\prime}(z)+t\,O(z^{3q}).

It follows that

\displaystyle|u_{f,t}(z)|

\displaystyle<\tfrac{1}{4}t(1-t)\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{48}t(1-t)(4t+1)\epsilon^{2}+\tfrac{1}{48}t^{2}(-2t+3)\epsilon^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}t\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}t\epsilon+\tfrac{5}{48}t\epsilon^{2}+\tfrac{3}{48}t^{2}\epsilon^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}t\epsilon

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{4}t\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{8}t\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{8}t\epsilon+\tfrac{1}{2}t\epsilon=\epsilon t.\qed

Remark 3.6
.

The family of functions
\{u_{f,t}\}_{0\leq t\leq 1}
defined in (
3.8
) extend holomorphically across the fixed points of
f
. This is trivial for
u_{f,0}=0
and was shown in Lemma
3.4
for
u_{f,1}=u_{f}
. For
0<t<1
, an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
3.4
shows that
u_{f,t}
takes a non-zero value at each simple fixed point and has a zero of order

at each fixed point of multiplicity

.

4.
Near-parabolic maps and proof of Theorem
A

4.1.
A convenient multiplier parameter

In order to deal with multipliers
\lambda
close but not equal to
1
, it will be convenient to work with the new parameter

\Lambda:=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\lambda}

instead, where
\Lambda
is near
\infty
. The expansion

\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\lambda}=-\frac{1}{1-\lambda}+\frac{1}{2}+O(1-\lambda)

shows that if
\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{1/\Lambda}
is the multiplier of a fixed point
p=f(p)
, then

(4.1)

\Lambda-\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,p)=O(\Lambda^{-1})\qquad\text{as}\ \Lambda\to\infty.

Thus,
\Lambda
blows up asymptotically the same way as the résidu itératif does. Evidently
p
is repelling or attracting according as
\operatorname{Re}(\Lambda)
is positive or negative.

We say
\lambda\to 1

non-tangentially
if
\operatorname{Im}(1-\lambda)/\operatorname{Re}(1-\lambda)
stays bounded as
\lambda\to 1
. In other words, if
\lambda
tends to
1
within a sector of opening angle
<\pi
at
1
which is symmetric about the real axis. It is easy to see that

(4.2)

\lambda\to 1\ \text{non-tangentially}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \frac{\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda)}{\operatorname{Re}(\Lambda)}=O(1)\ \text{as}\ \Lambda\to\infty.

4.2.
Perturbations of non-degenerate parabolics

Consider a holomorphic map
g
with a non-degenerate parabolic fixed point at
p
whose multiplier
\lambda=g^{\prime}(p)
is a primitive
q
th root of unity. After an analytic change of coordinates that sends
p
to
0
, we may assume

g(z)=\lambda z+O(z^{2}),

and

(4.3)

f:=g^{\circ q}=z+z^{q+1}+bz^{2q+1}+O(z^{2q+2}),\quad\text{where}\ b=\iota(f,0).

We fix a sufficiently small
0<r_{0}<1
such that

\bullet

f
is univalent in a neighborhood of the closed disk
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
,

\bullet

0
is the only fixed point of
f
in
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
,

\bullet

\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime})>0
in
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
.

Take a sequence
g_{n}(z)=\lambda_{n}z+O(z^{2})
of analytic perturbations of
g
. We will assume that
|\lambda_{n}|\neq 1
for all
n
and
f_{n}:=g_{n}^{\circ q}\to f
uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
as
n\to\infty
. The parabolic fixed point of
g
at
0
bifurcates into a simple fixed point of
g_{n}
at
0
of multiplier
\lambda_{n}=g^{\prime}_{n}(0)
and a
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}=\{p_{n,1},\ldots,p_{n,q}\}
of multiplier
\mu_{n}:=(f_{n})^{\prime}(p_{n,j})
independent of
j
. The points in
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
are asymptotically the vertices of a regular
q
-gon centered at
0
. In fact, they are the non-zero roots of the equation
f_{n}(z)-z=0
near the origin which, ignoring the vanishingly small terms, has the form
(\lambda_{n}^{q}-1)+z^{q}=0
. This heuristic argument suggests that the
p_{n,j}
are asymptotically the
q
th roots of
1-\lambda_{n}^{q}
. The following lemma provides a rigorous justification:

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\delta_{n}:=1-\lambda_{n}^{q}
, then

p_{n,j}^{q}=\delta_{n}+o(\delta_{n})\qquad\text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Proof.

Suppose

g_{n}(z)=\lambda_{n}z+\sum_{j=2}^{q}a_{n,j}z^{j}+a_{n,q+1}z^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2}).

Here and in what follows
O(z^{q+2})
denotes a remainder whose ratio to
z^{q+2}
is bounded in a neighborhood of
0
by a constant independent of
n
. Taking the
q
th iterate gives

f_{n}(z)=\lambda_{n}^{q}z+\sum_{j=2}^{q}A_{n,j}z^{j}+A_{n,q+1}z^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2}),

where the coefficients
A_{n,2},\ldots,A_{n,q}
are universal polynomial functions of the
q
variables
\lambda_{n}
and
a_{n,2}\ldots,a_{n,q}
. Moreover, each of these polynomials vanishes upon substituting any
q
th root of unity for the variable
\lambda_{n}
, so it must have a factor
\delta_{n}
. Thus,

f_{n}(z)=\lambda_{n}^{q}z+\delta_{n}\sum_{j=2}^{q}\tilde{A}_{n,j}z^{j}+A_{n,q+1}z^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2}),

where the
\tilde{A}_{n,j}
are universal polynomials in
\lambda_{n}
and
a_{n,2}\ldots,a_{n,q}
. As
n\to\infty
the
\tilde{A}_{n,j}
remain uniformly bounded in
2\leq j\leq q
while
A_{n,q+1}\to 1
. Now the points in the
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
are the roots of the equation

\lambda_{n}^{q}+\delta_{n}\sum_{j=2}^{q}\tilde{A}_{n,j}z^{j-1}+A_{n,q+1}z^{q}+O(z^{q+1})=1,

which can be written as

\delta_{n}-z^{q}=\delta_{n}\sum_{j=2}^{q}\tilde{A}_{n,j}z^{j-1}+(A_{n,q+1}-1)z^{q}+O(z^{q+1})=\delta_{n}\,o(1)+o(z^{q}).

This gives

\frac{\delta_{n}}{z^{q}}-1=\frac{\delta_{n}}{z^{q}}\,o(1)+o(1)\qquad\text{or}\qquad\frac{\delta_{n}}{z^{q}}=1+o(1).\qed

4.3.
The lifted dynamics of
f
and
f_{n}

As in §
4.1
we introduce the parameters

\Lambda_{n}:=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\lambda_{n}^{q}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\operatorname{M}_{n}:=\frac{1}{\operatorname{Log}\mu_{n}},

so both
\Lambda_{n},\operatorname{M}_{n}
tend to
\infty
as
n\to\infty
. We may assume that
0
and the
p_{n,j}
are the only fixed points of
f_{n}
in
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
. Using the normal form (
4.3
) to write
f(z)=z+\Delta(z)
with
\Delta(z)=z^{q+1}(1+bz^{q}+O(z^{q+1}))
, and invoking the expansion (
3.6
), gives

(4.4)

\omega(z):=\omega_{f}(z)=\frac{1}{z^{q+1}}+\frac{\rho}{z}+e(z),

where
\rho:=\operatorname{r\acute{e}sit}(f,0)=(q+1)/2-b
and
e(z)
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
. Similarly, since
\omega_{n}:=\omega_{f_{n}}
has simple poles at
0
and along the
q
-cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}=\{p_{n,1},\ldots,p_{n,q}\}
, we can use Lemma
3.1
to write

(4.5)

\omega_{n}(z)=\omega_{f_{n}}(z)=\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{z}+\operatorname{M}_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{z-p_{n,j}}+e_{n}(z),

where
e_{n}(z)
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
. It is easy to see that
e_{n}\to e
uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
. In fact, in the annulus
0<r_{0}<|z|<r_{0}+2\epsilon
for a fixed small
\epsilon
and
n\gg 1
, we have the Laurent expansion

\displaystyle\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{z}+\operatorname{M}_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{z-p_{n,j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{z}+\frac{\operatorname{M}_{n}}{z}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\frac{1}{1-p_{n,j}/z}=\frac{\Lambda_{n}}{z}+\frac{\operatorname{M}_{n}}{z}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{p_{n,j}}{z}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=(\Lambda_{n}+q\operatorname{M}_{n})\,\frac{1}{z}+\operatorname{M}_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{q}p_{n,j}^{k}\Big)\ \frac{1}{z^{k+1}}

containing only negative powers of
z
. Since
\omega_{n}\to\omega
uniformly on the circle
|z|=r_{0}+\epsilon
, it follows that

e_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|s|=r_{0}+\epsilon}\frac{\omega_{n}(s)}{s-z}ds\to\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|s|=r_{0}+\epsilon}\frac{\omega(s)}{s-z}ds=e(z)

uniformly in
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r_{0}+\epsilon)
.

The expressions (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) yield rather convenient formulas for the multi-valued rectifying coordinates
\phi=\phi_{f}
of
\omega

\phi_{n}=\phi_{f_{n}}
of
\omega_{n}
:

(4.6)

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{qz^{q}}+\rho\log z+E(z),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{n}\log z+\operatorname{M}_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\log(z-p_{n,j})+E_{n}(z).

Here
E,E_{n}
are the unique primitives of
e,e_{n}
with
E(0)=E_{n}(0)=0
. Since in each annulus
\epsilon<|z|<r_{0}+\epsilon
we have the uniform convergence
\omega_{n}\to\omega
, it follows that for each branch of
\phi
in this annulus there is a branch of
\phi_{n}
for which
\phi_{n}\to\phi
uniformly in this annulus. As for the remainder terms,
e_{n}\to e
and therefore

uniformly on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
.

By (

) the restriction of

We can now prepare for the proof of Theorem

. Choose

\sup_{|z|\leq r_{1}}|f_{n}(z)-z|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}r_{1}\qquad\text{for}\ n\gg 1.

).
We use positivity of
\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{Re}(f^{\prime}_{n})
throughout
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r_{0})
to conclude from Lemmas

and

that the functions

and

More generally, for
0\leq t\leq 1
let
z_{t}:=(1-t)z+tf(z)
and
z_{n,t}:=(1-t)z+tf_{n}(z)
and, as in (

\displaystyle u_{t}(z)=u_{f,t}(z)

\displaystyle:=-t+\int_{[z,z_{t}]}\omega(s)\,ds

\displaystyle u_{n,t}(z)=u_{f_{n},t}(z)

\displaystyle:=-t+\int_{[z,z_{n,t}]}\omega_{n}(s)\,ds,

For every

Proof.

By the maximum principle, it suffices to check the uniform estimate for

such that

\displaystyle|u_{n,t}(z)-u_{t}(z)|

[t_{n}-N_{0},t_{n}]
. The fundamental segment
J_{n}:=\Gamma_{n}([t_{n}-1,t_{n}[)
converges to the fundamental segment
\Gamma([t_{0}-1,t_{0}[)
as
n\to\infty
, so it remains in a fixed neighborhood of
\Gamma(t_{0})
, hence in the disk
{\mathbb{D}}(-r^{-q}/q,R)
for some
R>0
independent of
n
. Setting
Z_{0}:=-r^{-q}/q+R/\epsilon
, it follows that
J_{n}\subset{\mathscr{C}}(Z_{0})
for
n\gg 1
. The same argument as before based on
\sin^{-1}\epsilon<\delta
now shows that the ‘tail’ of the cone
{\mathscr{C}}(Z_{0})
is eventually contained in the half-plane
H_{n,q}
, that is, there exists an
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

{\mathscr{C}}(Z_{0})\cap\{Z:\operatorname{Re}(Z)<x_{0}\}\subset H_{n,q}\qquad\text{for}\ n\gg 1

(compare Fig.
7
). Recall that
F_{n}^{-1}
is the lifted dynamics of
f_{n}^{-1}
obtained by integrating along the segments
[z,f_{n}^{-1}(z)]
, as described in §
4.3
. By Corollary
4.4
, for
n\gg 1
, if
t_{n}-N_{0}+1\leq t\leq t_{n}
then the segment
[\gamma_{n}(t-1),\gamma_{n}(t)]
and the restriction of
\gamma_{n}
to
[t-1,t]
are homotopic in
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)\smallsetminus({\mathscr{O}}_{n}\cup\{0\})
. It follows that

F_{n}^{-1}(\Gamma_{n}(t))=\Gamma_{n}(t-1)\qquad\text{if}\ t_{n}-N_{0}+1\leq t\leq t_{n}.

Since
\gamma_{n}([t_{n}-N_{0},t_{n}[)\subset S
for
n\gg 1
,
the first
N_{0}-1
iterates of
J_{n}
under
F_{n}^{-1}
are defined and
\bigcup_{j=0}^{N_{0}-1}F_{n}^{-j}(J_{n})=\Gamma_{n}([t_{n}-N_{0},t_{n}[)
is contained in
\Omega_{n}\cap{\mathscr{C}}(Z_{0})
by Theorem
A
. Moreover,
\operatorname{Re}(Z)\leq R_{0}:=R-r^{-q}/q
for all
Z\in J_{n}
, so inductively
\operatorname{Re}(Z)\leq R_{0}-j(1-\epsilon)
for all
Z\in F_{n}^{-j}(J_{n})
. If we chose
N_{0}
large enough so that
R_{0}-N_{0}(1-\epsilon)<x_{0}
, it would follow that all further iterates
F_{n}^{-j}(J_{n})
for
j\geq N_{0}
are defined and contained in
H_{n,q}\cap{\mathscr{C}}(Z_{0})
. Since
\sin^{-1}\epsilon<\delta
, the Euclidean distance from
\Gamma_{n}(t)
to
\partial H_{n,q}
grows linearly in
t
as
t\to-\infty
. Projecting back to the dynamical plane by
\psi_{n}
, this implies that the
|\omega_{n}|
-distance from
\gamma_{n}(t)
to
\partial W_{n,q}
tends to
+\infty
as
t\to-\infty
, which shows
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma_{n}(t)=p_{n,q}
(alternatively, we could use the fact that
f_{n}^{-1}:W_{n,q}\to W_{n,q}
strongly contracts the hyperbolic metric to reach the same conclusion). This proves part (i) of Theorem
B
.

Let us now prove part (ii). Since
\lim_{t\to-\infty}\gamma(t)=0
and

5.3.
Tangential multiplier approach

Without the assumption of non-tangential multiplier approach in Theorem
B
the
f_{n}
-invariant curves
\gamma_{n}
may take longer and longer to land at a repelling point near
0
or they may have a “near miss” without landing and eventually leave the neighborhood of
0
. In such cases the Hausdorff limit of
\gamma_{n}
will be strictly larger than
\gamma
. This phenomenon can already be observed in polynomial dynamics where the Hausdorff limit of external rays along a convergent sequence
P_{n}\to P
can contain invariant arcs in the filled Julia set of
P
. These are the heteroclinic and homoclinic arcs studied in
[
PZ1
]
.

For an invariant embedded arc that has a near miss and eventually leaves the neighborhood of
0
one can use the theory of parabolic implosions to show that the arc must pass through one of the
q
“gates” between
0
and the points of the bifurcated cycle
{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
(compare Fig.
8
). Below we show how the setup developed in this section gives a simple proof of this fact. We continue working in the situation of Theorem
B
, with
\gamma,\gamma_{n}
being invariant embedded arcs under
f,f_{n}
such that
\gamma
lands at the parabolic point
0
and
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
uniformly on compact subsets of
]-\infty,0]
, but the multipliers
\lambda_{n}^{q},\mu_{n}
are now tending to
1
tangentially. The assumption that
\gamma,\gamma_{n}
are embedded arcs is for convenience and sufficient for standard applications. However, one can properly formulate and prove a similar statement for invariant curves or even more general continua.

\end{overpic}

f_{n}
-invariant curve
\gamma_{n}

f_{n}
. In doing so
\gamma_{n}
has to pass through one of the two “gates” between
0
and the pair of bifurcated fixed points. In this example
q=2
and the arrows show the Buff vector field
\chi_{f_{n}}
.

Theorem 5.1

(Near miss arcs pass through the gates)
.

Fix a small
r>0
and suppose
\gamma_{n}
eventually leaves
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
in the sense that there are sequences
-\infty<s_{n}<t_{n}\leq 0
such that
\gamma_{n}(t)\in{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
if and only if
s_{n}<t<t_{n}
. Then, for
n\gg 1
,
\gamma_{n}
must separate the fixed points of
f_{n}
in
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
. In other words, both components of
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)\smallsetminus\gamma_{n}(]s_{n},t_{n}[)
meet
\{0\}\cup{\mathscr{O}}_{n}
.

Proof.

We may assume
r
is small enough so that Theorem
A
holds for, say,
\epsilon=1/2
and also
r<r_{2}
so Corollary
4.4
holds. Evidently
|\gamma_{n}(t_{n})|=|\gamma_{n}(s_{n})|=r
. Since
\gamma
lands at
0
and
\gamma_{n}\to\gamma
uniformly on compact subsets of
]-\infty,0]
,
t_{n}
is bounded and
s_{n}\to-\infty
. As in the proof of Theorem
B
, take a single-valued branch of
\phi_{n}
mapping
\gamma_{n}([t_{n}-1,t_{n}[)
into an
R
-neighborhood of
-r^{-q}/q
and continue it analytically along
\gamma_{n}
to obtain a curve
\Gamma_{n}=\phi_{n}\circ\gamma_{n}
defined on
[s_{n},t_{n}]
. By Corollary
4.4
,
F_{n}^{-1}(\Gamma_{n}(t))=\Gamma_{n}(t-1)
for
s_{n}+1\leq t\leq t_{n}
. By Theorem
A
,
\Gamma_{n}
stays in the cone
{\mathscr{C}}(Z_{0})={\mathscr{C}}^{-}_{\epsilon}(Z_{0})
, where
Z_{0}=-r^{-q}/q+R/\epsilon
as before. Let
J_{n}=\Gamma_{n}([t_{n}-1,t_{n}[)
and choose the integer
N_{n}
such that
s_{n}+N_{n}\in[t_{n}-1,t_{n}[
. Then
\Gamma_{n}(s_{n})\in F_{n}^{-N_{n}}(J_{n})
so
\operatorname{Re}(\Gamma_{n}(s_{n}))\leq R_{0}-N_{n}(1-\epsilon)
. In particular,
\operatorname{Re}(\Gamma_{n}(s_{n}))\to-\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

However, if
\gamma_{n}
does not separate the fixed points of
f_{n}
in
{\mathbb{D}}(0,r)
, the arc
\gamma_{n}([s_{n},t_{n}])
is homotopic in
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}(0,r)\smallsetminus(\{0\}\cup{\mathscr{O}}_{n})
to an arc of the circle
|z|=r
joining
\gamma_{n}(t_{n})
to
\gamma_{n}(s_{n})
. Letting
\gamma_{n}(t_{n})=r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{n}}
with
\theta_{n}\in\,]\pi,\pi]
, it follows that the lift
\Gamma_{n}(s_{n})
must be on the same spiral curve
\theta\mapsto\phi_{n}(r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}),\ \theta_{n}-\pi\leq\theta\leq\theta_{n}+\pi
as
\Gamma_{n}(t_{n})
is. This implies
\operatorname{Re}(\Gamma_{n}(s_{n}))
being bounded below by
\operatorname{const.}r^{-q}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} serendipity finite elements on rectangular meshes
Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. A C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} serendipity finite element is a sub-element of C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} BFS finite element such that the element remains C 1 C^{1} -continuous and includes all P k P_{k} polynomials. In other words, it is a minimum of Q k Q_{k} bubbles enriched P k P_{k} finite element. We enrich the P 4 P_{4} and P 5 P_{5} spaces by 9 9 Q 4 Q_{4} and 11 11 Q 5 Q_{5} -bubble functions, respectively. For all k ≥ 6 k\geq 6 , we enrich the P k P_{k} spaces exactly by 12 12 Q k Q_{k} bubble functions. We show the uni-solvence and quasi-optimality of the newly defined C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} serendipity elements. Numerical experiments by the C 1 C^{1} - Q k Q_{k} serendipity elements, 4 ≤ k ≤ 8 4\leq k\leq 8 , are performed.

5.
The finite element solution and convergence

The
C^{1}
-
P_{k}
serendipity finite element space is defined by, for all
k\geq 4
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v_{h}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{T}\in V_{k}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{Q}_{h}\},

where
V_{k}(T)
is defined in (
2.3
), or (
3.1
), or (
4.1
).

The finite element discretization of the biharmonic equation (
1.1
) reads:
Find
u\in V_{h}
such that

(5.2)

\displaystyle(\Delta u,\Delta v)=(f,v)\quad\forall v\in V_{h},

where
V_{h}
is defined in (
5.1
).

Lemma 5.1
.

The finite element problem (
5.2
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

As (
5.2
) is a square system of finite linear equations, we only need to prove the uniqueness.
Let
f=0
and
v_{h}=u_{h}
in (
5.2
).
It follows
\Delta u_{h}=0
on the domain. Let
v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be the solution
of (
1.1
) with
f=\Delta u_{h}
, as
u_{h}\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Because
u_{h}\in C^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Delta u_{h}vd{\bf x}=\int_{\Omega}-\nabla u_{h}\nabla vd{\bf x}=\int_{\Omega}(u_{h})^{2}d{\bf x}.

Thus,
u_{h}=0
. The proof is complete.
∎

For convergence, the analysis is standard, as we have
C^{1}
conforming finite elements.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
u\in H^{k+1}\cap H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
be
the exact solution of the biharmoic equation (
1.1
).
Let
u_{h}
be the
C^{1}
-
P_{k}
finite element solution of (
5.2
).
Assuming the full-regularity on (
1.1
), it holds

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}+h^{2}|u-u_{h}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1},\quad k\geq 6.

Proof.

As
V_{h}\subset H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, from (
1.1
) and (
5.2
), we get

\displaystyle(\Delta(u-u_{h}),\Delta v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

Applying the Schwartz inequality, we get

\displaystyle|u-u_{h}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=C(\Delta(u-u_{h}),\Delta(u-u_{h}))

\displaystyle=C(\Delta(u-u_{h}),\Delta(u-I_{h}u))

\displaystyle\leq C|u-u_{h}|_{2}|u-I_{h}u|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k-1}|u|_{k+1}|u-u_{h}|_{2},

where
I_{h}u
is the nodal interpolation defined by DOFs in (
2.4
) or (
3.2
) or
(
4.2
).
As
V_{k}(T)\supset P_{k}(T)
, we have
I_{h}u|_{T}=u|_{T}
if
u\in P_{k}(T)
,
i.e.,
I_{h}
preserves
P_{k}
functions locally.
Such an interpolation operator is

H^{2}
stable and consequently of the
optimal order of convergence, by modifying the standard theory in
[
10
,
14
]
.

For the
L^{2}
convergence, we need an
H^{4}
regularity for the dual problem: Find

w\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle(\Delta w,\Delta v)

\displaystyle=(u-u_{h},v),\ \forall v\in H^{2}_{0}(\Omega),

where

\displaystyle|w|_{4}\leq C\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}.

Thus, by (
5.3
),

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Delta w,\Delta(u-u_{h}))=(\Delta(w-w_{h}),\Delta(u-u_{h}))

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}|w|_{4}h^{k-1}|u|_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}\|u-u_{h}\|_{0}.

The proof is complete.
∎

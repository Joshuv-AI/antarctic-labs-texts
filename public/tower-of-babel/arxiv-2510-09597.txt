Particles with precessing spin in Kerr spacetime: analytic solutions for eccentric orbits and homoclinic motion near the equatorial plane
Gabriel Andres Piovano 1

Abstract
We present a family of analytic solutions for the nearly-equatorial motion of a test particle with precessing spin in Kerr spacetime. We solve the equations of motion up to linear order in the small body’s spin for periodic and homoclinic orbits. At zero order, the particle moves along equatorial geodesics. The spin-curvature force introduces post-geodesic corrections which, for generic spin orientations, cause the precession of the orbital plane. We derive the solutions for eccentric orbits in terms of Legendre elliptic integrals and Jacobi elliptic functions for generic referential geodesics (known as “spin gauges”). Our analytical solutions perfectly match the numerical trajectories obtained by Drummond and Hughes in Phys. Rev. D 105, 124041 (2022), and Piovano et al. in Phys. Rev. D 111, 044009 (2025). Furthermore, we present, for the first time, the solutions for homoclinic orbits for a spinning particle in Kerr spacetime, and the spin-corrections to the location of the separatrix. The homoclinic trajectories are described in closed form using elementary functions. Finally, we introduce a novel parametrization for the motion of a spinning particle, called “fixed eccentricity spin gauge”. This is the only spin gauge in which the corrections to periodic orbits are finite at the geodesic separatrix, and continuously reduce to the last stable orbits under appropriate limits. Our results will be useful for modeling the inspiral and transition-to-plunge phases of asymmetric mass binaries within the two-time-scale framework.

When all roots of

When two or more roots of

and the initial radius

A particle on a homoclinic geodesic takes an infinite Mino (or proper) time to reach the periastron

III.2
Fixing the referential geodesics

So far, we have not specified yet the shifts to the constants of motion
\delta E
and
\delta L_{z}
. As observed in Refs.
Piovano:2024yks
;
Mathews:2025nyb
, defining the constants of motion shifts is equivalent to selecting a referential geodesic for the spinning trajectories. Each referential geodesic can then be interpreted as a specific “spin-gauge” of the phase space, which correspond to a different parametrization of the motion. We consider here two spin gauges previously employed in the literature: the fixed turning points and the fixed constants of motion parametrizations, which are denoted as “FT” and “FC”, respectively. Moreover, we introduce a novel spin gauge, in which the referential geodesics has the same eccentricity of the spin-perturbed orbits. We refer to this parametrization with the shorthand notation “FE”. Henceforth, we use the superscript FT, FC and FE to label the orbital elements calculated in one of three spin gauges.

III.2.1
Fixed constants of motion

One of the simplest choice of referential geodesics consists in imposing
\delta E^{\text{FC}}=\delta L^{\text{FC}}_{z}=0
. Such gauge was first introduced by Witzany in Ref.
Witzany:2019nml
, and it was later adopted in Ref.
Witzany:2024ttz
to derive the shifts to the coordinate-time frequencies and actions in analytic form. For eccentric, equatorial orbits, the shifts to the turning points are

\displaystyle\delta r^{\text{FC}}_{1}=-\frac{R_{s}(r_{1\rm g})}{R^{\prime}_{\rm g}(r_{1\rm g})}\,,\qquad\delta r^{\text{FC}}_{2}=-\frac{R_{s}(r_{2\rm g})}{R^{\prime}_{\rm g}(r_{2\rm g})}\,,

(52)

which diverge in the limit of circular orbits, as was already observed in Refs.
Witzany:2019nml
;
Skoupy:2025nie
.

III.2.2
Fixed turning points

A second convenient spin gauge can be defined by picking a referential geodesic with the same turning points of the spinning trajectories. In other words, the shifts
\delta E^{\text{FT}}
and
\delta L^{\text{FT}}_{z}
are chosen by imposing
\delta r^{\text{FT}}_{1}=\delta r^{\text{FT}}_{2}=0
. The expressions for
\delta E^{\text{FT}}
and
\delta L^{\text{FT}}_{z}
are rather long, and are presented in Appendix
D
. The FT gauge was first introduced by Skuopý and Lukes-Gerakopoulos
Skoupy:2022adh
for eccentric, equatorial orbits, and it was later extended to generic orbits by Drummond and Hughes
Drummond:2022efc
;
Drummond:2022_near_eq
. As shown in Sec.
III.4
, a major problem of this parametrization is the divergence of the spin corrections to the orbits near the separatrix.

III.2.3
Fixed eccentricity

We present here a novel spin gauge, which is defined by fixing the spin corrections to the constants of motion as

\displaystyle\delta E^{\text{FE}}

\displaystyle=\delta E^{\text{FT}}+\frac{\partial E_{\rm g}}{\partial p}\delta p\ ,

(53)

\displaystyle\delta L_{z}^{\text{FE}}

\displaystyle=\delta L_{z}^{\text{FT}}+\frac{\partial L_{z\rm g}}{\partial p}\delta p\ ,

(54)

with associated shifts to the turning points

\displaystyle\delta r_{1}^{\text{FE}}=\frac{\delta p}{1-e_{\rm g}}\ ,\quad\delta r_{2}^{\text{FE}}=\frac{\delta p}{1+e_{\rm g}}\ .

(55)

The shift
\delta p
is defined by imposing that the spin corrections to the roots
\delta r_{2}
and
\delta r_{3}

A

C

The spin corrections to the radial frequency
\delta\Upsilon_{r}
is formally defined by the integral

\delta\Upsilon_{r}=\frac{\Upsilon^{2}_{r\rm g}}{\pi}\int^{r_{1\rm g}}_{r_{2\rm g}}\frac{\delta V_{r}(r_{\rm g})\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r^{\prime})}}\ ,

(67)

Drummond:2022_near_eq
;
Skoupy:2022adh
;
Piovano:2024yks

\delta\Upsilon_{r}
as the finite part of the integral (

of Hadamard’s partie finie method

;
Witzany:2024ttz

67

E

The shifts to the Mino-time frequencies for the coordinate time and azimuthal trajectories
\delta\Upsilon_{t}
and
\delta\Upsilon_{\phi}

\displaystyle\delta\Upsilon_{t}=\frac{\Upsilon_{r2\rm g}}{\pi}\delta t(r_{\rm g})\ ,\qquad\delta\Upsilon_{\phi}=\frac{\Upsilon_{r\rm g}}{\pi}\delta\phi(r_{2\rm g})\ ,

(68)

\delta\Upsilon_{t}
and
\delta\Upsilon_{\phi}

\displaystyle\delta b_{\text{osc}}(w_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=\delta b(w_{\rm g})-\frac{\delta\Upsilon_{b}}{\Upsilon_{r\rm g}}w_{r_{\rm g}}\ ,

(69)

IV
Polar motion and spin-precession

The polar motion is governed by Eq. (
36
), which describes the forced harmonic oscillations driven by the spin-curvature coupling. General solutions of Eq. (
36
) can be written as the superposition of an homogeneous solution
\delta z_{\text{h}}
and a particular solution
\delta z_{\text{inh}}
, given by

\displaystyle\delta z(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\delta z_{\text{h}}(\lambda)+\delta z_{\text{inh}}(\lambda)\ ,

(74a)

\displaystyle\delta z_{\text{h}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=A\cos(\Upsilon_{z}\lambda)+B\sin(\Upsilon_{z}\lambda)\ ,

(74b)

\displaystyle\delta z_{\text{inh}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=-s_{\perp}\frac{\cos\psi_{\rm p}\sqrt{r^{2}_{\rm g}+(L_{z\rm g}-aE_{\rm g})^{2}}}{r_{\rm g}(L_{z\rm g}-aE_{\rm g})}\ ,

(74c)

where
A
and
B
are constants fixed by the initial conditions. The referential geodesics is constrained on the equatorial plane when
A=B=0
, which can be imposed with the initial conditions
(\delta z_{\text{h}}(0),\mathrm{d}\delta z_{\text{h}}(0)/\mathrm{d}\lambda)=(0,0)
. The particular solution
\delta z_{\text{inh}}(\lambda)
was found in
Witzany:2023bmq
for a non-rotating primary, and it was later extended to Kerr spacetime in
Piovano:2024yks
. Moreover, Eq. (
74c
) agrees with the results of
Skoupy:2024uan
.

A few comments are in order. First, Eq. (
74c
) describes the precession of the orbital plane driven by the component of the particle spin orthogonal to the SMBH spin. Noteworthy, the complete solution (
74a
) is valid for any type of radial trajectory
r_{\rm g}
and precession phase
\psi_{p}
constrained on the equatorial plane. Additionally, the general solution (
74a
) do not admit resonant motion for any physical orbits. Such behavior was observed by Drummond and Hughes in
Drummond:2022_near_eq
for circular and nearly circular orbits. Moreover, the shift
\delta z(\lambda)
does not depend on the corrections to the constants of motion. Thus, the orbital precession is the same regardless of the chosen referential geodesic.

A complete description of the polar motion requires the spin precession phase
\psi_{p}(\lambda)
, which evolves according to Eq. (
34
). The evolution equation for
\psi_{p}(r_{\rm g})
can be recasted as

\frac{\mathrm{d}\psi_{p}}{\mathrm{d}r_{\rm g}}=\pm\frac{\Psi_{r}(r_{\rm g})}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}\,,

(75)

A periodic solution of the former equation is

\displaystyle\psi^{/}_{p}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(aL_{z\text{rd}}+E_{\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}}^{2})\mathrm{Im}\,\Big(I^{/}_{1/(r+i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle+I^{/}_{1/(r-i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})\Big)\ ,

(76)

where the integrals
I^{/}_{1/(r\pm i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}
are given in Appendix
A
, while the spin precession frequency is

\Upsilon_{p}=\frac{\Upsilon_{r\rm g}}{\pi}\psi_{p}(r_{2\rm g})\ ,

(77)

The precession phase
\psi_{p}(r_{\rm g})
for homoclinic motion can be obtained by taking the limit
r_{3\rm g}\to r_{2\rm g}
in Eq. (
76
), which reduces to

\displaystyle\psi^{\mathsf{hom}}_{p}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(aL_{z\text{rd}}+E_{\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}}^{2})\mathrm{Im}\,\Big(I^{\mathsf{hom}}_{1/(r+i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle+I^{\mathsf{hom}}_{1/(r-i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}(r_{\rm g})\Big)\ ,

(78)

with the integrals
I^{\mathsf{hom}}_{1/(r\pm i|L_{z\text{rd}}|)}
given in Appendix
B
.
Moreover, the spin precession frequency at the separatrix reduces to
\Upsilon_{p}=\Psi_{r}(r_{2\rm g})
.

The bottom panels of Fig.
4
and Fig.
5
present the orthogonal projection onto a co-rotating polar plane for periodic and homoclinic orbits, respectively. Notice the precession of orbital plane for the spinning trajectory (red line) due to the orthogonal component of the spin
\chi_{\perp}
.

V
Discussion

In this work, we studied the nearly-equatorial motion of a spinning test-particle in Kerr spacetime at linear order in the small spin. We derived analytic solutions for periodic orbits valid in any spin gauge. Our findings are in perfect agreement with the numerical trajectories of Refs.
Drummond:2022_near_eq
;
Piovano:2024yks
, which were derived in the fixed turning points and fixed constants of motion parametrizations. Furthermore, we introduced the novel fixed eccentricity parametrization, which is the unique spin gauge in which the corrections to periodic orbits are finite at the geodesic separatrix. The FE gauge allowed us to calculate, for the first time, the spin corrections to homoclinic orbits and the shift to the separatrix location in closed form.

A key difference from Ref.
Skoupy:2024uan
is that our solutions are given in terms of physical geodesic plus spin corrections in Mino-time. Thus, the results of this work can be readily applied to modeling the inspiral
Piovano:2024yks
;
Skoupy:2023lih
and transition to plunge
Becker:2024xdi
;
Kuchler:2024esj
;
Lhost:2024jmw
;
Compere:2021zfj
;
Compere:2021iwh
for spinning EMRI binaries, or to derive Post-Newtonian expressions for the dynamics
Henry:2023tka
;
Skoupy:2024jsi
.

In a follow-up work
PiovanoInPrep
, we will apply our approach to derive explicit spin corrections to bound, plunging orbits, in a similar vein to the geodesic case
Dyson:2023fws
;
Ciou:2025ygb
;
Mummery:2023hlo
;
Ko:2023igf
. Closed-form solutions for spinning trajectories would find practical use in the modeling of comparable mass binaries through BH perturbation techniques
Kuchler:2025hwx
;
Faggioli:2025hff
;
Compere:2021zfj
;
Compere:2021iwh
.

It is also possible to find analytic solutions for quasi-spherical orbits without using the transformations of
Skoupy:2024uan
, since the equations of motion are still partially decoupled. The FE gauge can also be extended to quasi-spherical and generic orbits by introducing appropriate averaging
Piovano:2024yks
.

Our solutions could also be useful to study test particles equipped with a spin-induced quadrupole on eccentric orbits. First-order equations of motion for a quadrupolar test-body are already available in the literature, at least on the equatorial plane
Bini:2013uwa
;
Bini:2013rrx
;
Bini:2015zya
. However, a careful study is necessary, as the dynamics at quadratic order in the spin is more involved
Steinhoff:2009tk
;
Steinhoff:2012rw
;
Shahzadi:2025ebj
. For example, the four-linear momentum and velocity are no longer colinear at order
\mathcal{O}(q^{2}\chi^{2})
.

Finally, it would be interesting to find the transformation between the numerical solutions found in
Skoupy:2022adh
;
Drummond:2022efc
;
Piovano:2024yks
, and the analytic expressions given in the ”virtual geodesic” formalism of
Skoupy:2024uan
. The solutions found here would be the perfect starting point for such a comparison.

Appendix C
Analytic solutions for geodesic motion on the equatorial plane

For the reader’s convenience, we present here the analytic solutions for geodesic equatorial motion in Mino-time with the initial conditions and conventions of Sec.
II.1
. Refs.
Fujita:2009bp
;
vandeMeent:2019cam
present the solutions for generic periodic geodesics using different initial conditions, while Ref.
Levin:2008yp
presents homoclinic geodesic in closed form parametrized using proper time (see Ref.
Li:2023bgn
for off-equatorial homoclinic in Kerr-Neuwmann spacetime).

The starting point are the equations of motion (
15
), which can be recasted in the following form

\displaystyle\lambda=\pm\int^{r_{1\rm g}}_{r_{\rm g}}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r^{\prime})}}\,,

(109a)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}r_{\rm g}}=\pm\frac{T_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}\,,

(109b)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\delta\phi}{\mathrm{d}r_{\rm g}}=\pm\frac{\Phi_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}{\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}}\,.

(109c)

Analytic expressions for periodic and homoclinic geodesics are presented in the next subsections.

C.1
Periodic trajectories

For periodic orbits, the solution to the radial motion is given by

r_{\rm g}(\lambda)=\frac{r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}}{r_{2\rm g}+(r_{1\rm g}-r_{2\rm g})\mathsf{sn}^{2}(\xi(\lambda)|k_{\rm g})}\ ,

(110)

which oscillates with Mino-time frequency
\Upsilon_{r\rm g}

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{r\rm g}=\frac{\pi}{2\,\mathsf{K}(k_{r\rm g})}\sqrt{(1-E_{\rm g}^{2})(r_{1\rm g}-r_{3\rm g})r_{2\rm g}}\,,

(111)

As shown in Ref.
vandeMeent:2020xgc
, the radial motion can then be parametrized using the geodesic mean anomaly
w_{r\rm g}=\Upsilon_{r\rm g}\lambda
by taking advantage of the identity of the Jacobi amplitude
\mathsf{am}(\mathsf{K}(m)|m)=\pi/2
. Thus, the eccentric amplitudes
\xi
can be written in terms of the mean anomaly as

\xi(w_{r\rm g})=\mathsf{am}\Big(\mathsf{K(k_{\rm g})}\frac{w_{r\rm g}}{\pi}\Big\rvert k_{\rm g}\Big)\ ,

(112)

The geodesic radial potential can then be recasted using Eq. (
110
) and Eq. (
112
) as

\pm\sqrt{R_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})}=\frac{\sin(\xi)\cos(\xi)r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}(r_{1\rm g}-r_{2\rm g})\sqrt{r_{1\rm g}\big(r_{2\rm g}-r_{2\rm g}\sin^{2}(\xi)\big)-r_{2\rm g}r_{3\rm g}(1-\sin^{2}(\xi))}}{\big((r_{1\rm g}-r_{2\rm g)})\sin^{2}(\xi)+r_{2\rm g}\big)^{2}}\ ,

(113)

Finally, the coordinate time and azimuthal trajectories can be written with the aid of the integrals of Appendix
A
as

\displaystyle t_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=E_{\rm g}\big(4I^{/}(r_{\rm g})+2I^{/}_{r}(r_{\rm g})+I^{/}_{r^{2}}(r_{\rm g})\big)-\frac{2}{r_{+}-r_{-}}\Big(2a^{2}E_{\rm g}-4E_{\rm g}r_{+}+aL_{z\rm g}r_{+}\Big)I^{/}_{1/(r-r_{+})}(r_{\rm g})+(+\leftrightarrow-)\ ,

(114)

\displaystyle\phi_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})

\displaystyle=L_{z\rm g}I^{/}(r_{\rm g})+\frac{a(2r_{+}E_{\rm g}-aL_{z\rm g})}{(r_{+}-r_{-})}I^{/}_{1/(r-r_{+})}(r_{\rm g})+(+\leftrightarrow-)\ ,

(115)

while the corresponding geodesics frequencies
\Upsilon_{t\rm g}
and
\Upsilon_{\phi\rm g}
are given by

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{t\rm g}=\frac{\Upsilon_{r\rm g}}{\pi}t_{\rm g}(r_{2\rm g})\ ,\qquad\Upsilon_{\phi\rm g}=\frac{\Upsilon_{r\rm g}}{\pi}\phi_{\rm g}(r_{2\rm g})\ .

(116)

The purely oscillatory components of the geodesics
t_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})
and
\phi_{\rm g}(r_{\rm g})
can then be expressed as

\displaystyle b_{\text{\rm g,osc}}(w_{r\rm g})

\displaystyle=b_{\rm g}(w_{r\rm g})-\frac{\Upsilon_{b\rm g}}{\Upsilon_{r\rm g}}w_{r_{\rm g}}\ ,

(117)

with
b=t,\phi
.

C.2
Homoclinic trajectories

Homoclinic trajectories can be obtained by taking the limit
r_{3\rm g}\to r_{2\rm g}
of periodic geodesics. In this case, the argument
k_{\rm g}=1
in the integrals in Appendix
A
, which leads to

\displaystyle\sin(\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{sn}\Big(\frac{1}{2}Y_{r}\lambda\Big\rvert 1\Big)=\tanh{\Big(\frac{1}{2}Y_{r}\lambda\Big)}\ ,

(118)

\displaystyle\mathsf{F}(\xi|1)

\displaystyle=\log\big(\sec(\xi)+\tan(\xi)\big)=\frac{1}{2}Y_{r}\lambda\ ,

(119)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}(\xi|1)

\displaystyle=\sin(\xi)\ ,

(120)

\displaystyle\mathsf{\Pi}(n,\xi|1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n-1}\arctanh(\sqrt{n}\sin{\xi})-\frac{Y_{r}\lambda}{2(n-1)}\ .

(121)

Using the previous expressions, the homoclinic radial trajectory can then be written as

r_{\rm g}(\lambda)=\frac{r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}}{r_{2\rm g}+(r_{1\rm g}-r_{2\rm g})\tanh^{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}Y_{r}\lambda\big)}\ ,

(122)

while the homoclinic coordinate time and azimuthal trajectories can be read from Eqs. (
114
)-(
115
) by replacing
I^{/}(r_{\rm g})
with
I^{\mathsf{hom}}(r_{\rm g})
,
I^{/}_{r}(r_{\rm g})
with
I^{\mathsf{hom}}_{r}(r_{\rm g})
and so on.
As noticed in
Levin:2008yp
, the trajectories
t(r_{\rm g})
and
\phi(r_{\rm g})
have a logarithmic divergence near the periastron, since the particle take an infinite Mino (and proper) time to reach the double root
r_{2\rm g}=r_{3\rm g}
. Therefore, the radial frequency
\Upsilon_{r\rm g}\to 0
as
r_{3\rm g}\to r_{2\rm g}
. By contrast,
\Upsilon_{t\rm g}
and
\Upsilon_{\phi\rm g}
are finite in the limit
r_{3\rm g}\to r_{2\rm g}
, and reduce to

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{t\rm g}=T_{\rm g}(r_{2\rm g})\ ,\qquad\Upsilon_{t\rm g}=\Phi_{\rm g}(r_{2\rm g})\ ,

(123)

which were derived using the following limits

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}(k_{\rm g})\sim-\frac{1}{2}\log(r_{2\rm g}-r_{3\rm g})\quad\text{ as }\quad r_{3\rm g}\to r_{2\rm g}\ ,

(124)

\displaystyle\lim_{r_{3\rm g}\to r_{2\rm g}}\frac{\Pi(\gamma_{r}|k_{\rm g})}{\mathsf{K}(k_{\rm g})}=\frac{1}{1-\gamma_{r}}\ .

(125)

Appendix D
Corrections to the constants of motion in the FT gauge

The spin corrections to the energy and angular momentum in the fixed turning points gauge are given by

\displaystyle\delta E^{\text{FT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{s}(r_{2\rm g})}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial L_{z\rm g}}(r_{1\rm g})-\frac{R_{s}(r_{1\rm g})}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial L_{z\rm g}}(r_{2\rm g})\ ,

(126)

\displaystyle\delta L^{\text{FT}}_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{s}(r_{1\rm g})}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial E_{\rm g}}(r_{2\rm g})-\frac{R_{s}(r_{2\rm g})}{\mathcal{J}}\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial E_{\rm g}}(r_{1\rm g})\ ,

(127)

with

\mathcal{J}=\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial E_{\rm g}}(r_{1\rm g})\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial L_{z\rm g}}(r_{2\rm g})-\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial L_{z\rm g}}(r_{1\rm g})\frac{\partial R_{\rm g}}{\partial E_{\rm g}}(r_{2\rm g})\ .

(128)

In these form, the shifts
\delta E^{\text{FT}}
and
\delta L_{z}^{\text{FT}}
are divergent for stable, circular geodesic, i.e. for
r_{1\rm g}=r_{2\rm g}
. However, after some algebraic effort, the shifts
\delta E^{\text{FT}}
and
\delta L_{z}^{\text{FT}}
can be written in the equivalent form

\displaystyle\delta E^{\text{FT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{L_{z\text{rd}}}{\mathcal{D}}\big[-2aL_{z\text{rd}}^{2}(r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g})-L_{z\rm g}E_{\rm g}r^{2}_{1\rm g}r^{2}_{2\rm g}-aL_{z\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}}r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}+aL_{z\text{rd}}L_{z\rm g}(r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g})^{2}\big]\ ,

(129)

\displaystyle\delta L_{z}^{\text{FT}}

\displaystyle=\frac{E_{\rm g}r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}}{\mathcal{D}}L_{z\text{rd}}\big[a^{3}L_{z\text{rd}}-a(aE_{\rm g}-3L_{z\text{rd}})r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}-3E_{\rm g}r^{2}_{1\rm g}r^{2}_{2\rm g}\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{aL_{z\text{rd}}^{2}}{\mathcal{D}}\big[-2aL_{z\text{rd}}(r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g})+a^{2}E_{\rm g}(r^{2}_{1\rm g}+r^{2}_{2\rm g})+E_{\rm g}(r^{4}_{1\rm g}+r^{4}_{2\rm g})\big]

\displaystyle+\frac{E_{\rm g}r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}}{\mathcal{D}}\big[E_{\rm g}L_{z\rm g}r^{2}_{1\rm g}r^{2}_{2\rm g}(r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g})-3E_{\rm g}L_{z\text{rd}}r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}(r^{2}_{1\rm g}+r^{2}_{2\rm g})+aL_{z\text{rd}}^{2}(r^{2}_{1\rm g}+r^{2}_{2\rm g})\big]\ ,

(130)

with

\mathcal{D}=r^{2}_{1\rm g}r^{2}_{2\rm g}\Big[E_{\rm g}L_{z\rm g}r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}(r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g})+2aL_{z\text{rd}}^{2}-2L_{z\text{rd}}E_{\rm g}(r_{1\rm g}+r_{2\rm g})^{2}+2L_{z\text{rd}}E_{\rm g}r_{1\rm g}r_{2\rm g}\Big]\ .

(131)

Eqs. (
129
)-(
130

\delta E^{\text{FT}}
and
\delta L_{z}^{\text{FT}}

Jefremov:2015gza

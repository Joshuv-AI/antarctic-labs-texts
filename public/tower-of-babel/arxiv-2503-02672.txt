On describing trees and quasi-trees from their leaves
Bruno Courcelle

Abstract




Abstract

Generalized trees, we call them
O-trees
, are defined as

hierarchical
partial orders,
i.e.
, such that the elements larger
than any one are linearly ordered.
Quasi-trees
are, roughly speaking,
undirected O-trees. For O-trees and quasi-trees, we define relational
structures on their leaves that characterize them up to isomorphism. These
structures have characterizations by universal first-order
sentences. Furthermore, we consider cases where O-trees and quasi-trees can
be reconstructed from their leaves by
CMSO-transductions
. These
transductions are transformations of relational structures defined by monadic
second-order (MSO) formulas. The letter ”C” for
counting
refers to the
use of set predicates that count cardinalities of finite sets modulo fixed integers.

O-trees and quasi-trees make it possible to define respectively, the modular
decomposition and the rank-width of a countable graph. Their constructions
from their leaves by transductions of different types apply to
rank-decompositions, and to modular decomposition and to other canonical graph decompositions.

Introduction

In graph theory, a
tree
is a connected graph without root. A

rooted tree
has a distinguished node called its
root
, from which
one gets a partial order, the
ancestor relation
. A more general notion
of tree has been defined, in particular by Fraïssé
[
19
]

(Section 2.11). The notion of a rooted tree is generalized into that of a

hierarchical
partial order,
i.e.
, such that the set of elements
larger than any one is linearly ordered. Such a partial order generalizes the
ancestor relation of a rooted tree. A dense linear order, as that of rational
numbers, is then a tree in this extended sense. We call
O-trees
these
generalized trees, by keeping the standard terminology for usual ones, and

join-trees
those where any two nodes
y,z
have a least common
”ancestor”
y\sqcup z
,
i.e.
, a least upper-bound with respect to the
associated partial order also called shortly their
join
.

Apart from its intrinsic interest in the theory of relations, the notion of
join-tree permits to define the modular decomposition of a countable graph

[
15
]
by extending the existing notion for finite graphs. Its
formalization in Monadic Second-Order (MSO) logic involves the description of
a join-tree from its leaves forming a set
L
, related by the ternary relation

R
such that
R(x,y,z)
means
x\leq y\sqcup z.
We call such
(L,R)
a

leaf structure
. This logical description has been studied in

[
1
,
3
,
8
]
for finite trees, and in
[
15
]
for infinite
join-trees. The article
[
3
]
shows applications to several canonical
graph decompositions, in particular, the
split
and the
bi-join
decompositions.

In
[
10
]
we also defined
quasi-trees
as, roughly speaking,
undirected join-trees. The motivation was to define the rank-width a countable
graph in such a way that the rank-width of a graph is the least upper bound of
those of its finite induced subgraphs, as for tree-width
[
23
]
. The
definition of a quasi-tree is based on a ternary relation

on nodes called

means that

and

[
4
,
6
,
12
]
. We call

occur in the middle of any triple
(x,y,z)

leaves and is thus useless for describing quasi-trees from their
leaves. Hence, we introduce a 4-ary relation on leaves that we call the

separation relation

means that

[
13
]
.

In the present article, we obtain first-order (FO) axiomatizations of the leaf
structures of join-trees and of the separation structures of quasi-trees. We
also examine the constructions of O-trees and quasi-trees by logically
specified transformations of relational structures called

construction by Bojanczyk

for finite trees

The constructions of

All countable

is empty and gives no
information about the resulting embedding, as
L
is a set without any
structure. However, the separation relation
S_{Q}
describes, up to
homeomorphism, the part of a topological space
\mathcal{L}^{\#}
that
represents the relative positions of the nodes
M(x,y,z)
in the original embedding.

Conclusion
: We raised a few open questions. The main one is Problem
57, that is, to obtain a readable uFO axiomatization of the separation
structures of leafy quasi-trees.

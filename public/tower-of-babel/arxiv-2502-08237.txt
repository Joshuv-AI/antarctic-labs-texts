Moment estimates for polyatomic Boltzmann equation with frozen collisions
Ricardo Alonso and Milana Čolić

Abstract
In this paper, a polyatomic gas with continuous internal energy is considered, allowing for frozen collisions, in which the kinetic energy of the colliding particle pair is conserved, and the internal energy of each particle remains unchanged. A priori moment estimates are derived for solutions of the space-homogeneous Boltzmann equation with a collision kernel of the hard potentials type with cut-off. The model with frozen collisions is first analyzed, followed by a review of general collisions—referred to as pure polyatomic—which preserve the total kinetic and internal energy. By combining existing results for pure polyatomic collisions with the newly derived estimates for frozen collisions, moment estimates are established for the Boltzmann equation with a collision operator that convexly combines both types of collisions. In particular, the moment generation property is shown to be driven by the rate of the pure polyatomic operator, and the moment propagation property holds.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the Boltzmann equation modelling a polyatomic gas within the continuous approach for internal energy. In such a setting, gas is described by a distribution function
f(t,x,v,I)\geq 0
that depends on time
t>0
, space position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, molecular velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and internal energy
I\geq 0
. Evolution of
f
, as governed by the Boltzmann equation, accounts for the effects of collisions on gas dynamics through the collision operator. The base assumptions used to model collisions profoundly influence the form of the collision operator and subsequent analysis. One such assumption is that collisions are binary and conserve both momentum and total (kinetic + internal) energy. Namely, for two molecules of mass
m
, with velocity-internal energy pairs
(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})
and
(v^{\prime}_{*},I^{\prime}_{*})
, which collide and give rise to molecules of the same mass
m
with
(v,I)
and
(v_{*},I_{*})
, respectively, the following rules apply

v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*}=v+v_{*},\qquad\frac{m}{2}|v^{\prime}|^{2}+I^{\prime}+\frac{m}{2}|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}+I^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{m}{2}|v|^{2}+I+\frac{m}{2}|v_{*}|^{2}+I_{*}.

(1)

Within this framework, the collision operator and the Boltzmann equation have been studied in the space homogeneous case in
[
14
,
4
,
2
,
3
]
, and in perturbative setting in
[
6
,
9
,
13
,
16
]

The question of physical relevance of the aforementioned collision kernel by modelling of transport coefficients (shear and bulk viscosity and heat conductivity), based on evaluation of the full collision operator, was raised in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. For recovering a correct value of the Prandtl number, it was crucial to introduce in the description collisions that preserve only the kinetic energy. It has been already known in literature dealing with operators of relaxation type, such as BGK, that various exchange processes occur on different characteristic time scales. The fact that translational energy is exchanged between particles in all collisions and the internal energy is exchanged only in some of the collisions is accounted by introducing a two term operator where the first term only models the translational exchange processes and the second term models translational and internal exchange processes
[
17
,
19
]
. The concept of frozen (elastic) collisions is also used in direct simulation Monte Carlo (DSMC) method where a probability of internal energy relaxation event is prescribed for two particles selected for a collision
[
15
]
.

These considerations motivate us to incorporate frozen collisions in the polyatomic Boltzmann equation and study moment estimates for a solution of such equation in the space homogeneous case. Namely, frozen collisions refer to collisions in which the internal energy of each molecule remains invariant
[
11
]
, i.e.

v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*}=v+v_{*},\quad|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}=|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2},\quad I^{\prime}=I,\quad I^{\prime}_{*}=I_{*}.

(2)

The Boltzmann equation we consider in this paper convexly combines, with a factor
\omega\in[0,1]
, the collision operator
Q_{\zeta}(f,f)(v,I)
for pure polyatomic (or non-frozen) collisions (
1

\partial_{t}f(t,v,I)=Q^{\omega}(f,f)(v,I):=\omega\ Q_{\zeta}(f,f)(v,I)+(1-\omega)\ Q_{\zeta_{\text{{f}}}}^{\text{{f}}}(f,f)(v,I).

(3)

Obviously, the case

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

Notation

To be consistent with

\langle v\rangle=\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}},\qquad\langle I\rangle=\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{m}I},\qquad\langle v,I\rangle=\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{2}|v|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{m}I}.

\begin{split}&\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(v,I)\langle v\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I,\qquad\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{k}[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(v,I)\langle I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I,\\
&\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(v,I)\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I.\end{split}

(5)

Particular to this paper are partial moments

2
Frozen collision operator

Similar to the monatomic case, the frozen collisions (
2
) are parametrized with an angular variable
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
to express

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\qquad v^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

(6)

The corresponding frozen collision operator is defined by
[
11
]

Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left\{f(v^{\prime},I)g(v^{\prime}_{*},I_{*})-f(v,I)g(v_{*},I_{*})\right\}\\
\times B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}I_{*},

(7)

where the collision kernel
B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\geq 0
satisfies micro-reversibility

B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)=B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*},I,I_{*},\hat{u})=B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v_{*},v,I_{*},I,-\sigma).

Since the Jacobian of the transformation
(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\mapsto(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma^{\prime})
, with (
6
) and
\sigma^{\prime}=\tfrac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}
, is one, the weak form of the collision operator (
7
) can be defined, for a suitable test function
\chi(v,I)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)+Q^{\text{{f}}}(g,f)(v,I)\right\}\chi(v,I)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\\
=\int\left\{\chi(v^{\prime},I)+\chi(v^{\prime}_{*},I_{*})-\chi(v,I)-\chi(v_{*},I_{*})\right\}\\
\times f(v,I)\,g(v_{*},I_{*})\,B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}I_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I.

(8)

The collision invariants are the natural ones determined by the collision rules (
6
), i.e.
1
,
v
,
|v|^{2}
,
I
, but also any function
\chi(I)
such that (
8
) makes sense,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)+Q^{\text{{f}}}(g,f)(v,I)\right\}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\\
\chi(I)\end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I=0.

(9)

For the collision operator (
7
), the space homogeneous Boltzmann equation reads

\partial_{t}f(t,v,I)=Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,f)(v,I),

(10)

and corresponds to (
3
) with
\omega=0
. For the moment, we skip notation emphasizing
\zeta_{\text{{f}}}
in the superscript of the collision operator until the final Section
4
, since Sections
2
,
3
deal only with the frozen collision operator.

2.1
Assumption on the collision kernel

The collision kernel is assumed to take a factorized form

B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)=b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\ \tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*}),

(11)

with
b
non-negative and integrable
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathrm{d}\sigma)
, and
\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}
taking the form of hard potentials, up to a constant depending on
\zeta
,

c_{\zeta}\,\left(\frac{E}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\leq\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*})\leq C_{\zeta}\left(\frac{E}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2},\quad\frac{E}{m}=\frac{1}{4}|u|^{2}+\frac{I+I_{*}}{m},\quad\zeta\in(0,2],

(12)

In the sequel, we consider exclusively the collision operator (
7
) describing frozen collisions with the collision kernel satisfying (
11
)–(
12
).

3
Moment estimates for frozen collisions

Due to invariance of the internal energy in frozen collisions (
2
), for any suitable function
\chi(I)
depending only on the internal energy, we noticed previously in (
9
) that
the weak form (
8
) vanishes,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)+Q^{\text{{f}}}(g,f)(v,I)\right\}\chi(I)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I=0.

(13)

For a solution of the Boltzmann equation (
10
), this implies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(t,v,I)\,\chi(I)\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(0,v,I)\,\chi(I)\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I.

In particular, choosing
\chi(I)=\langle I\rangle^{k}
, the following Proposition holds.

Proposition 1
(Polynomial
I-
moment propagation estimate)

If
f
is a solution of the Boltzmann equation (
10
) with finite
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
-moment, then for
k\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{k}[f](t)=\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{k}[f](0),\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(14)

In particular, if
\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{k}[f](0)<\infty
then
\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{k}[f](t)<\infty
for any
t\geq 0
, recovering the classical propagation property of
I-
moments. Whereas, if
\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{k}[f]
is infinite at
t=0
, it will remain infinite, i.e. there will be no later creation of such moments.

Next, we consider moments in the velocity variable. Since the collision rules (
6
) for the velocity variable are the same as for the monatomic case, the classical Povzner lemma applies
[
7
,
5
]
.

Lemma 1
(Povzner
\sigma
-averaging)

Let
b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathrm{d}\sigma)
.
There exists an explicit positive constant
\mathcal{C}_{k}<\|b\|_{L^{1}}
decreasing to zero in
k>2
, such that for
v^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}_{*}
from (
6
) the following averaging holds,

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left(\langle v^{\prime}\rangle^{k}+\langle v^{\prime}_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\mathcal{C}_{k}\left(\langle v\rangle^{2}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2},\quad k>2.

This averaging property allows to conclude on moments for the collision operator.

Proposition 2
(Polynomial moment estimates on the frozen collision operator)

Let
\zeta\in(0,2]
and
k>2
. For suitable
f,g
having finite
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
moments, the following estimate on
v-
moments of the frozen collision operator (
7
) holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)+Q^{\text{{f}}}(g,f)(v,I)\right\}\langle v\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\\
\leq-\left(A_{k}[f,g](\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f])^{1+\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}+A_{k}[g,f](\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[g])^{1+\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}\right)+B_{k}[f,g]+B_{k}[g,f],

(15)

with positive constants
A_{k}>0
and
B_{k}
depending on
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
moments of
f
and
g
, which are explicitly computed along the proof.

Moreover, for
\zeta\in[0,2]
and
k>2
, the following estimate on moments of the frozen collision operator (
7
) holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)+Q^{\text{{f}}}(g,f)(v,I)\right\}\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\\
\leq D_{k}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]\mathfrak{m}_{k}[g]+\mathfrak{m}_{2}[g]\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f]\right),

(16)

where
D_{k}=2^{{k}/2+2}\,C_{\zeta}\|b\|_{L^{1}}

Proof

The weak form (
8

\chi=\langle v\rangle^{k}

\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}}\left\{\mathcal{C}_{k}\left(\langle v\rangle^{2}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2}-\|b\|_{L^{1}}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)\right\}\\
\times f(v,I)\,g(v_{*},I_{*})\,\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}I_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I.

Now, applying p-Binomial inequality, see Lemma 5.5 from
[
4

\left(\langle v\rangle^{2}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2}\leq\langle v\rangle^{k}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\\
+2^{{k}/2+1}\left(\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{k-2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle v\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle}+\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k-2}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v\right\rangle}\right),

(17)

allows to split

\begin{split}W&\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}}f(v,I)\,g(v_{*},I_{*})\,\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*})\Big\{-\left(\|b\|_{L^{1}}-\mathcal{C}_{k}\right)\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)\\

(18)

First denote

\tilde{A}_{k}=\|b\|_{L^{1}}-\mathcal{C}_{k}>0,\quad k>2.

Then, the lower bound on the collision kernel (
12

\begin{split}\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*})&\geq c_{\zeta}\,\left(\frac{E}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\geq\tilde{c}_{\zeta}\,\left(\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{4}\right)^{\zeta/2}+\left(\frac{I}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}+\left(\frac{I_{*}}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\right)\\
&\geq\tilde{c}_{\zeta}\,\left(L\langle v\rangle^{\zeta}-\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\zeta}+\left(\frac{I}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}+\left(\frac{I_{*}}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\right),\end{split}

(19)

with
\tilde{c}_{\zeta}=c_{\zeta}\,3^{\zeta/2-1},L=2^{-\zeta}\min\left\{1,2^{1-\zeta}\right\}
, see e.g. Lemma A.1 in
[
4

, leading to

\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*})\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)\\
\geq\tilde{c}_{\zeta}L\left(\langle v\rangle^{k+\zeta}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k+\zeta}\right)-\tilde{c}_{\zeta}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\zeta}+\langle v\rangle^{\zeta}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)\\
+\tilde{c}_{\zeta}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\left(\frac{I}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\left(\frac{I_{*}}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\right).

On the other hand, the positive part of
W
uses an upper bound, for
\zeta/2\leq 1

\left(\frac{E}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\leq\langle v\rangle^{\zeta}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\zeta}+\left(\frac{I}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}+\left(\frac{I_{*}}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2},

which in combination with (
12

\begin{split}\tilde{B}^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},&I,I_{*})\left(\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{k-2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle v\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle}+\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k-2}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v\right\rangle}\right)\\
&\leq 2\ C_{\zeta}\left(\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{k-2+\zeta}+\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k-2+\zeta}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\\
&\quad+C_{\zeta}\left(\tfrac{1}{m}\,I\right)^{\zeta/2}\left(\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}+\left(\varepsilon\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}+{\varepsilon^{-\frac{k-2}{2}}}\right)\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\\
&\quad+C_{\zeta}\left(\tfrac{1}{m}\,I_{*}\right)^{\zeta/2}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}+\left(\varepsilon\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{k}+{\varepsilon^{-\frac{k-2}{2}}}\right)\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}\right),\end{split}

(20)

where we have conveniently used the domain for velocity, and Young’s inequality to get
\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k-2}\leq\varepsilon\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k}+{\varepsilon^{-\frac{k-2}{2}}}
. Gathering (
19

18
) becomes

\begin{split}&W\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}}f(v,I)\,g(v_{*},I_{*})\,\\
\times\Big\{&-\tilde{A}_{k}\tilde{c}_{\zeta}\left(L\left(\langle v\rangle^{k+\zeta}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k+\zeta}\right)+m^{-\zeta/2}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\,{I}^{\zeta/2}+\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\,{I_{*}}^{\zeta/2}\right)\right)\\
&+\tilde{A}_{k}\tilde{c}_{\zeta}\left(\langle v\rangle^{k}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{\zeta}+\langle v\rangle^{\zeta}\langle v_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)\\
&+2^{{k}/2+1}\,\mathcal{C}_{k}\,C_{\zeta}\Big\{2\,\left(\left\langle v\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{k-2+\zeta}+\left\langle v\right\rangle^{k-2+\zeta}\left\langle v_{*}\right\rangle^{2}\right)\\

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[\cdot]\leq(\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{2}[\cdot])^{\frac{\zeta}{k-2+\zeta}}(\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k+\zeta}[\cdot])^{\frac{k-2}{k-2+\zeta}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow\tilde{K}_{1}[g]\,\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f]\leq K_{1}[f,g]+\delta\,\mathfrak{m}_{0}[g]\,\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k+\zeta}[f],

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k-2+\zeta}[\cdot]\leq(\mathfrak{m}_{0}[\cdot])^{\frac{2}{k+\zeta}}(\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k+\zeta}[\cdot])^{\frac{k-2+\zeta}{k+\zeta}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\Rightarrow\tilde{K}_{2}[g]\,\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k-2+\zeta}[f]\leq K_{2}[f,g]+\delta\,\mathfrak{m}_{0}[g]\,\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k+\zeta}[f],

with constants

K_{1}[f,g]=\frac{(\tilde{K}_{1}[g])^{\frac{k-2+\zeta}{\zeta}}\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{2}[f]}{(\mathfrak{m}_{0}[g]\delta)^{\frac{k-2}{\zeta}}},\quad K_{2}[f,g]=\frac{(\tilde{K}_{2}[g])^{\frac{k+\zeta}{2}}\mathfrak{m}_{0}[f]}{(\mathfrak{m}_{0}[g]\delta)^{\frac{k-2+\zeta}{2}}}.

Then, the choice
\delta=\frac{\tilde{A}_{k}\tilde{c}_{\zeta}L}{4}
allows an absorption into the negative part, and yields

W\leq-\frac{\tilde{A}_{k}\tilde{c}_{\zeta}L}{2}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{0}[g]\,\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k+\zeta}[f]+\mathfrak{m}_{0}[f]\,\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k+\zeta}[g]\right)+B_{k}[f,g]+B_{k}[g,f],

with the constant

B_{k}[f,g]=K_{1}[f,g]+K_{2}[f,g]+2^{{k}/2+1}\,\mathcal{C}_{k}\,C_{\zeta}\varepsilon[g]^{-\frac{k-2}{2}}\mathfrak{m}^{I}_{\zeta}[f]\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{2}[g].

Then, invoking moment interpolation (
21
),

\begin{split}W\leq&-\left(A_{k}[f,g](\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f])^{1+\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}+A_{k}[g,f](\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[g])^{1+\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}\right)+B_{k}[f,g]+B_{k}[g,f],\end{split}

the final estimate follows, by introducing the notation

A_{k}[f,g]=\frac{\tilde{A}_{k}\tilde{c}_{\zeta}L}{2}\,\mathfrak{m}_{0}[g]\,(\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{2}[f])^{-\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}.

For the part (b), we first rewrite the weak form of the collision operator

W:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,g)(v,I)+Q^{\text{{f}}}(g,f)(v,I)\right\}\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\\
=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}\left\{\langle v^{\prime},I\rangle^{k}+\langle v^{\prime}_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{k}-\langle v,I\rangle^{k}+\langle v_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{k}\right\}\\
\times f(v,I)\,g(v_{*},I_{*})\,B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}I_{*}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I.

By the conservation law of the energy (
2
), since
k>2
,

\langle v^{\prime},I\rangle^{k}+\langle v^{\prime}_{*},I_{*}\rangle_{j}^{k}\leq\left(\langle v^{\prime},I\rangle^{2}+\langle v^{\prime}_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2}=\left(\langle v,I\rangle^{2}+\langle v_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2}.

Then, applying

\left(\langle v,I\rangle^{2}+\langle v_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2}-\langle v,I\rangle^{k}-\langle v_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{k}\\
\leq 2^{{k}/2+1}\left(\left\langle v,I\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle v_{*},I_{*}\right\rangle^{k-2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle v,I\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v_{*},I_{*}\right\rangle}+\left\langle v,I\right\rangle^{k-2}\left\langle v_{*},I_{*}\right\rangle^{2}\mathds{1}_{\left\langle v_{*},I_{*}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v,I\right\rangle}\right),

together with an upper bound on the collision kernel

B^{\text{{{f}}}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\leq b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\ C_{\zeta}\left(\langle v,I\rangle^{\zeta}+\langle v_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{\zeta}\right),

implies, after a convenient use of the indicator function,

\begin{split}W&\leq 2^{{k}/2+2}\,C_{\zeta}\|b\|_{L^{1}}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]\mathfrak{m}_{k-2+\zeta}[g]+\mathfrak{m}_{2}[g]\mathfrak{m}_{k-2+\zeta}[f]\right)\\
&\leq 2^{{k}/2+2}\,C_{\zeta}\|b\|_{L^{1}}\left(\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]\mathfrak{m}_{k}[g]+\mathfrak{m}_{2}[g]\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f]\right),\end{split}

by the monotonicity of moments, which completes the proof.

\square

When applied to the solution
f(t,v,I)
of the Boltzmann equation (
10
), Proposition
2
implies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q^{\text{{f}}}(f,f)(v,I)\,\langle v\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\leq-A_{k}(\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f])^{1+\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}+B_{k},

(22)

where the constants are abbreviated as
A_{k}=A_{k}[f,f]
and
B_{k}=B_{k}[f,f]
. Then, it is classical
[
1
,
18
]
to prove generation and propagation of
v-
moments.

Theorem 3.1
(Polynomial
v-
moment generation and propagation estimate)

Let
f
be a solution of the Boltzmann equation (
10
), having
\mathfrak{m}_{2}
-moment finite. Define, respectively to constants in (
22
),

E_{k}=\left(\frac{B_{k}}{A_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k-2}{k-2+\zeta}}.

(23)

Then the following estimates hold, for any
k>2
and
t>0
,

\displaystyle\text{1. \ (generation)}\quad\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f](t)\leq{E}_{k}+\left(\frac{k-2}{\zeta A_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k-2}{\zeta}}t^{-\frac{k-2}{\zeta}},\qquad

(24)

\displaystyle\text{2. \ (propagation)}\ \ \text{If $\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f](0)<\infty$, then}\ \ \mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f](t)\leq\max\left\{E_{k},\mathfrak{m}^{v}_{k}[f_{0}]\right\}.\qquad

(25)

Thus, only the partial
v-
moments are generated, while both
v-
moments and
I-
moments are propagated.

4
Moment estimates for a convex combination of pure polyatomic and frozen collisions

In this section, the aim is to combine the results of previous Sections
2
and
3
for frozen collisions with already established theory in
[
14
,
4
,
3
]
for a polyatomic gas with pure polyatomic or non-frozen collisions (
1
) which interchange the internal energy. Let us go ahead and write directly the pure polyatomic collision operator accounting for collisions (
1
),

Q(f,g)(v,I)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1]^{2}}\left\{f(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})g(v^{\prime}_{*},I^{\prime}_{*})\left(\frac{II_{*}}{I^{\prime}I^{\prime}_{*}}\right)^{\alpha}-f(v,I)g(v_{*},I_{*})\right\}\\
\times{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)\,r^{\alpha}(1-r)^{\alpha}\,(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\sqrt{R}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}I_{*},

(26)

where
\alpha>-1
, and collisions (
1
) are parameterized with
(\sigma,r,R)\in\mathbb{S}^{\times}[0,1]^{2}
,

\displaystyle v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{R\,E}{m}}\sigma,\qquad

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{R\,E}{m}}\sigma,

(27)

\displaystyle I^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r(1-R)E,\quad

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{*}

\displaystyle=(1-r)(1-R)E,

where
E
is given in (
12
). The collision kernel
B
is an a.e. non-negative function assumed to take a form like in Section
2.1
, i.e. to be factorized

B(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)=b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\ \tilde{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},r,R),

(28)

with a.e. non-negative
b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2};\mathrm{d}\sigma)
and

\tilde{b}^{lb}(r,R)\,\left(\frac{E}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2}\leq\tilde{{B}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},r,R)\leq\tilde{b}^{ub}(r,R)\,\left(\frac{E}{m}\right)^{\zeta/2},\quad\zeta\in(0,2],

(29)

satisfying
\tilde{b}^{lb}(r,R),\,\tilde{b}^{ub}(r,R)\in L^{1}([0,1]^{2};\,r^{\alpha}(1-r)^{\alpha}\,(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\sqrt{R}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}R)
.

The averaging over the set of collision parameters
(\sigma,r,R)
can be exploited similar to
\sigma
-averaging analogue to the Povzner Lemma
1
, to obtain the following result. Due to the potential discrepancy in
\tilde{b}^{lb}
and
\tilde{b}^{ub}
as stated in (
29
), a desired decay is guaranteed to happen only after some
k_{*}>2
, as explained in the upcoming lemma.

Lemma 2
(Povzner
(\sigma,r,R)
-averaging, Lemma 4.3 from
[
14
]
)

There exist an explicit non-negative constant
\mathcal{C}_{k}
decreasing in
k\geq 0
with
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{C}_{k}=0
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1]^{2}}\left(\langle v^{\prime},I^{\prime}\rangle^{k}+\langle v^{\prime}_{*},I^{\prime}_{*}\rangle^{k}\right)b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\,\tilde{b}^{ub}(r,R)\\
\times\,r^{\alpha}(1-r)^{\alpha}\,(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\sqrt{R}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\leq\mathcal{C}_{k}\left(\langle v,I\rangle^{2}+\langle v_{*},I_{*}\rangle^{2}\right)^{k/2}.

In particular, there exists
k_{*}>2
, depending only on the angular part
b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)
and the function
\tilde{b}^{ub}(r,R)
, such that

\mathcal{C}_{k}<\int_{[0,1]^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\,\tilde{b}^{lb}(r,R)r^{\alpha}(1-r)^{\alpha}\,(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\sqrt{R}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}\sigma,\quad\text{for}\;\;k\geq k_{*}.

(30)

Next, we recall moment estimates on the collision operator (
26
).

Proposition 3
(Lemmas 5.6 and 5.8 from
[
4
]
)

Let
\zeta\in(0,2]
. For a suitable
f
, there exist non-negative constants
\bar{A}_{k}>0
,
\bar{B}_{k}
and
\bar{D}_{k}
such that the following estimates hold on the pure polyatomic operator (
26
)

\displaystyle\text{(a)}\ \text{ for }\ k\geq k_{*},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q(f,f)(v,I)\,\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\leq-\bar{A}_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{k+\zeta}[f]+\bar{B}_{k},\quad

(31)

\displaystyle\text{(b)}\ \text{ for }\ k>2,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q(f,f)(v,I)\,\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\leq\bar{D}_{k}\,\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f],\quad

(32)

where
k_{*}
is from (
30
).

In the sequel, we consider an
\omega
-convex combination (
3
) of the pure polyatomic operator from (
26
) and its frozen-counterpart (
7
), with collision kernels satisfying (
12
) and (
29
) with possibly different potential rates emphasized by respectively
\zeta
and
\zeta_{\text{{f}}}
in the subscript of the collision operators.
The corresponding Cauchy problem for the Boltzmann equation reads (
3
)

\partial_{t}f(t,v,I)=Q^{\omega}(f,f),\quad f(0,v,I)=f_{0}(v,I).

(33)

We gather Propositions
3
and
2
to state the following Proposition.

Proposition 4
(Polynomial moments of the
\omega-
convex collision operator)

Let
\zeta\in(0,2]
be the rate of the collision kernel of the pure polyatomic operator (
26
)–(
28
)–(
29
) and
\zeta_{\text{{f}}}\in[0,2]
the rate of the frozen counterpart (
7
)–(
11
)–(
12
). For suitable
f
, the following estimate holds on the collision operator
Q^{\omega}
defined in (
3
), for the convex factor
\omega>0
,

\displaystyle\text{(a)}\ \text{ for }\ k\geq k_{*},\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q^{\omega}(f,f)(v,I)\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\leq-A_{k}^{\omega}\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f]^{1+\frac{\zeta}{k-2}}+{B}^{\omega}_{k},

(34)

\displaystyle\text{(b)}\ \text{ for }\ k>2,\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q^{\omega}(f,f)(v,I)\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\leq D^{\omega}_{k}\,\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f],

(35)

where the non-negative constants
\tilde{A}_{k}>0
,
{B}^{\omega}_{k}
and
{D}^{\omega}_{k}
are explicitly computed.

Proof

Gathering estimates (
31
) and (
16
), integration of the collision operator
Q^{\omega}
against
\langle v,I\rangle^{k}
implies, for
k>k_{*}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q^{\omega}(f,f)(v,I)\,\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I\\
\leq-\omega\,\bar{A}_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{k+\zeta}[f]+(1-\omega)D_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f]+\omega\,\bar{B}_{k}.

Next, by moment interpolation formula (
21
) and Young’s inequality,

\begin{split}(1-\omega)D_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{2}\mathfrak{m}_{k}&\leq(1-\omega)D_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\frac{\zeta}{k-2+\zeta}+1}\mathfrak{m}_{k+\zeta}^{\frac{k-2}{k-2+\zeta}}\\
&\leq\tilde{K}+\delta\,\mathfrak{m}_{k+\zeta},\quad\text{with}\ \ \tilde{K}={((1-\omega)D_{k})^{\frac{k-2+\zeta}{\zeta}}\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{\frac{k-2+\zeta}{\zeta}+1}}{\delta^{-\frac{k-2}{\zeta}}}.\end{split}

Thus, for
\omega>0
, choosing
\delta=\omega\bar{A}_{k}/2
, the statement (
34
) follows, by denoting

A_{k}^{\omega}=\frac{\omega\,\bar{A}_{k}}{2}\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]^{-\frac{\zeta}{k-2}},\qquad{B}^{\omega}_{k}=\omega\,\bar{B}_{k}+\tilde{K}.

The second inequality (
35
), for
k>2
, follows by gathering (
16
) and (
32
), and by denoting
D_{k}^{\omega}=\omega\,\bar{D}_{k}+(1-\omega)D_{k}\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]
, which completes the proof.

\square

When applied to the Boltzmann equation (
33
), since

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathfrak{m}_{k}[f]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}Q^{\omega}(f,f)(v,I)\,\langle v,I\rangle^{k}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}I,\end{split}

Theorem 4.1
(Polynomial moment generation and propagation estimate)

Let
f

-moment finite. For any
k>2
,
\zeta\in(0,2]

\zeta_{\text{{f}}}\in[0,2]

\begin{split}E_{k}^{\omega}&=\left(\frac{B^{\omega}_{k}}{A_{k}^{\omega}}\right)^{\frac{k-2}{k-2+\zeta}},\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\omega}_{k}=\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]^{\frac{k_{*}-k+1}{k_{*}-1}}\left({E}^{\omega}_{k_{*}+1}\right)^{\frac{k-2}{k_{*}-1}}\\
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\omega}_{k}&=\mathcal{E}^{\omega}_{k}+\mathfrak{m}_{2}[f]^{\frac{k_{*}-k+1}{k_{*}-1}}\left(\frac{(k_{*}-1)D_{k}^{\omega}}{\zeta A_{k}^{\omega}}\right)^{\frac{k-2}{\zeta}}.\end{split}

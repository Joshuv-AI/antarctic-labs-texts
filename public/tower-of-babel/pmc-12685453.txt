Comparison of advanced bipolar versus conventional monopolar energy device in single incision laparoscopic total extraperitoneal hernia repair (SILTEP): a retrospective comparative study
Authors: Eun Young Kim, Jaeim Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeSingle-incision laparoscopic totally extraperitoneal hernia repair (SILTEP) is a minimally invasive technique for inguinal hernia that promotes faster recovery. Advanced bipolar (AB) energy devices have recently emerged, offering several benefits in surgical outcomes. This study aimed to compare AB and conventional monopolar (CM) energy devices to assess their impact on surgical outcomes in SILTEP and to evaluate the necessity of using AB devices.MethodsA retrospective comparative analysis was conducted involving 304 patients who underwent SILTEP, divided into the CM and AB groups based on the energy device used. Patient characteristics, operative details, and postoperative outcomes were compared between the groups.ResultsThe AB group exhibited longer operation times but similar morbidity rates compared to the CM group (53.5 ± 21.0 minutes vs. 42.9 ± 14.5 minutes, P < 0.001; 8.1% vs. 11.2%, P = 0.618). There was a significant difference in intraoperative blood loss (CM group: 7.2 ± 4.1 mL vs. AB group: 3.3 ± 5.0 mL, P < 0.001). Although statistically non-significant, a lower trend of seroma formation was observed in the AB group than in the CM group (7.4% vs. 10.7%). Similar early postoperative pain scores were observed in both groups of patients using patient-controlled analgesia; no significant differences in chronic pain were observed between the groups.ConclusionAB energy devices may offer advantages for SILTEP, including reduced intraoperative blood loss. AB energy devices may be useful and viable options for SILTEP. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings.Keywords: Electrosurgery, Inguinal, Hernia, Laparoscopy, Herniorrhaphy

INTRODUCTION

Inguinal hernia repair is a common surgical procedure, typically approached either through open or laparoscopic methods [1]. Laparoscopic hernia repair is performed as a totally extraperitoneal (TEP) or transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) procedure [2].

Among the laparoscopic techniques, the TEP approach offers distinct advantages by avoiding entry into the peritoneal cavity, potentially reducing intra-abdominal complications and hastening recovery [3]. Minimally invasive surgery, exemplified by single-incision laparoscopic TEP hernia repair (SILTEP), further minimizes invasiveness, accelerates recovery, and enhances clinical outcomes [4,5,6].

Furthermore, advancements in laparoscopic instrumentation have led to notable progress in previously utilized devices, particularly in the realm of energy devices [7]. Recently introduced advanced bipolar (AB) energy devices, such as LigaSure (Medtronic), have emerged, offering stronger vessel seals and reduced thermal spread compared to traditional devices [8,9]. While these innovations have revolutionized surgical practices, assessing their overall benefits remains a topic of debate owing to cost considerations and variable evidence. Further research is required regarding the use of these energy devices to elucidate their effect on the surgical outcomes of TEP hernia repair.

Therefore, in this study, we described the SILTEP procedure and presented the findings of a retrospective comparative analysis conducted to evaluate the necessity of the bipolar vessel-sealing energy device for TEP hernia repair. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of different energy devices, the AB energy device and conventional monopolar (CM) electrocautery, in patients who underwent SILTEP.

METHODS

Ethics statement

This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, and written informed consent was waived due to its retrospective nature. It was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the College of Medicine, The Catholic University of Korea (No. UC22RISI0006).

Patients

Our study group comprised 316 patients who underwent SILTEP for inguinal hernia performed by a single surgeon at Uijeongbu St. Mary Hospital between March 2016 and July 2022. Twelve patients were not included in the study group as they had undergone other operations (n = 10) or were aged <18 years (n = 2). A total of 304 patients were ultimately included in the study.

The following data were obtained from the patient records and compared between the 2 groups: clinical characteristics, operation time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative morbidity, length of postoperative hospital stay, postoperative chronic pain, and visual analog scale (VAS) pain score according to postoperative time. Postoperative patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) was administered selectively, and nearly all patients were managed according to their clinical pathways.

Single-incision laparoscopic total extraperitoneal hernia repair: operative details

The operating surgeon stood opposite the hernia, and the scopist stood opposite the operating surgeon. A single, small midline incision was made inferior to the umbilicus. Subsequently, beginning from the deepest part of the umbilicus and extending 1.0–1.5 cm from the umbilicus. The subcutaneous layer at the incision site was dissected into the anterior rectus sheath and incised transversely. After pulling the rectus muscle laterally, a specialized port or access device was inserted through the incision into the preperitoneal space. A 5-mm rigid 30° laparoscope was used. Preperitoneal dissection was performed from the umbilicus using laparoscopic instruments. After the completion of preperitoneal dissection, sac isolation and reduction or amputation were performed. Subsequently, parietalization and mesh positioning were performed. In terms of management of the hernia sac, high ligation was performed for most indirect hernias, regardless of sac amputation. High ligation of the sac was not performed for direct, femoral, or obturator hernias. In such cases, only reduction was performed. The mesh size (13 × 9 cm or 15 × 10 cm) was selected based on the patient’s body habitus, with consideration given to potential mesh shrinkage to ensure adequate coverage of the myopectineal orifice. Surgical glue, Greenplast Q Prefilled (Green Cross), was used to fix the mesh. The anterior rectus sheath was closed using continuous 2–0 polyglactin 910 sutures. The skin incision was closed using subcuticular sutures.

Conventional monopolar group vs. advanced bipolar group

During the preperitoneal dissection, sac isolation, and parietalization phases, conventional dissecting forceps with a monopolar cable (30–40 W) or the AB energy device, LigaSure, were employed, designated as the CM or AB group, respectively (Fig. 1).

Intraoperative image of the advanced bipolar group during single-incision laparoscopic totally extraperitoneal hernia repair. The operator’s right hand is holding the advanced bipolar energy device.

Initially, a CM energy device was used for TEP repair at our institution. However, in 2020, we transitioned to an AB vessel-sealing energy device.

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation values or number and percentage of patients. Between-group differences in continuous variables were analyzed using an independent t-test. A chi-square analysis was used to explore the associations between categorical variables and patient groups. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using PASW Statistics ver. 18.0 (IBM Corp.).

RESULTS

Among the 304 patients, 169 were in the CM group and 135 were in the AB group. The patient characteristics in each study group are presented in Table 1. No statistically significant differences were observed between the 2 groups regarding age, sex, body mass index, American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status score, and hernia type or location. According to baseline characteristics, recurrent hernias were treated in 9 patients in the CM group and 8 in the AB group.

The intra- and postoperative details of each study group are shown in Table 1. The operation time was significantly longer in the AB group compared to the CM group (CM group: 42.9 ± 14.5 minutes vs. AB group: 53.5 ± 21.0 minutes, P < 0.001). Even after stratification by unilateral and bilateral hernia cases, the operation time showed similar significant differences between the 2 groups. There was a significant difference in intraoperative blood loss (CM group: 7.2 ± 4.1 mL vs. AB group: 3.3 ± 5.0 mL, P < 0.001). The postoperative hospital stay was similar in both groups (CM group: 1.0 ± 0.2 days vs. AB group: 1.0 ± 0.2 days, P = 0.234).

The postoperative morbidity rates were 11.2% (n = 19) and 8.1% (n = 11) in the CM and AB groups, respectively (P = 0.618). The most common cause of morbidity was postoperative seroma, with an overall incidence of 9.2%. Although the difference was not statistically significant, the CM group tended to have a higher incidence of seromas. Seromas occurred in 18 patients (10.7%) in the CM group and 11 patients (7.4%) in the AB group. All seroma cases resolved spontaneously or with one to 3 simple aspirations. An inguinal hematoma developed in one patient in each group but resolved spontaneously.

When comparing VAS scores between the groups using PCA, there were no differences between the 2 groups of patients, with scores gradually declining from 1 hour to day 1 (Table 2). In cases where PCA was not utilized, there was a statistically significant difference in VAS scores at 12 hours and day 1, with the AB group exhibiting higher scores than the CM group (CM group: 2.7 ± 1.0 vs. AB group: 3.0± 0.8, P = 0.007; CM group: 2.3 ± 0.9 vs. AB group: 2.9 ± 0.6, P = 0.002). However, the differences at 1 and 6 hours were not significant (Table 2).

The overall incidence of chronic pain was 4.6% (4.1% in the CM group and 5.2% in the AB group), with no statistically significant difference between the groups (P = 0.666) (Table 1).

DISCUSSION

In the present study, the use of an AB energy device was associated with prolonged operation times. Nonetheless, compared to the CM group, the AB group demonstrated comparable morbidity, reduced seroma formation, decreased intraoperative blood loss, and similar early postoperative pain scores in patients receiving PCA. These findings highlight the benefits of using AB energy devices to reduce intraoperative blood loss and postoperative seroma formation.

According to the specification of the manufacturer, the AB energy device alters collagen and elastin in the blood vessel walls, creating a durable seal. This innovative hemostatic technique provides a consistent, permanent seal for veins, arteries, and tissue bundles up to 7 mm in diameter [10,11,12]. An AB energy device offers reduced thermal spread and minimal tissue charring. It consists of an electrosurgical generator and a handpiece with a ratchet scissor mechanism. The instrument grasps and compresses the tissue, with the generator sensing the tissue density and automatically adjusting the energy delivery. Upon the completion of sealing, the microprocessor-controlled feedback terminates the pulse. The resulting seal is visible as a semi-transparent window and can be safely divided after instrument removal [12,13].

Although there are variations in the detailed results, in general clinical practice, the use of an AB energy device has been associated with positive outcomes such as shorter operation times, reduced postoperative pain, and shorter hospital stays [8,14,15,16,17,18,19,20].

For selective neck dissection, the AB energy device has been shown to reduce operation time [17]. However, that study analyzed a control group using a combination of CM and AB devices, which differed from our study [17].

At the time of planning our research, we anticipated that AB energy devices would demonstrate favorable results in terms of operation time. However, in the final results, we did not substantiate a reduction in operation time in the AB group. As this study was retrospective, several factors may have contributed to the operation time outcomes. There could have been an influence from the increased operation time due to surgical trainee education at certain time points, the inability to divide the overall operation time by dissection time, and the inclusion of cases during the learning curve associated with the use of AB energy devices, unlike in the CM group. These factors likely contributed to our failure to validate our hypothesis. Alternatively, it is possible that the advantages observed with the monopolar device directly correlate with time savings, or at the very least, that the use of a monopolar device does not extend the operation time. However, because of the lack of comparative studies between AB energy devices and CM devices in laparoscopic hernia repair, well-designed randomized controlled trials are necessary to provide conclusive evidence.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to compare the short-term surgical outcomes after using an AB energy device and a CM energy device during laparoscopic SILTEP.

Few studies have analyzed the differences in energy devices in hernia surgery [21,22]. Among these, 2 studies focused on the differences in energy devices in inguinal hernia surgery: one on TEP hernia repair and the other on TAPP hernia repair [21,22].

In the TEP study, patients were randomized into “monopolar dissection preferred” and “blunt dissection preferred” groups. This randomized study aimed to compare the effects of these 2 techniques in laparoscopic TEP on preperitoneal drain output and seroma formation. The conclusion drawn was that seroma formation could be further reduced by the appropriate use of monopolar energy as the preferred dissection approach in laparoscopic TEP [22].

In the TAPP study, the authors described the TAPP repair procedure and reported the results of a retrospective comparative study conducted to determine whether an ultrasonic energy device was necessary. The patients were divided into 2 groups: those in whom the ultrasonic energy device was used and those in whom the monopolar energy device was used. In this study, the higher competence seen with the monopolar energy device led to the conclusion that the ultrasonic energy device was unnecessary for simple TAPP repair. The mean operation time was significantly shorter when the monopolar energy device was used than when the ultrasonic energy device was used [22]. While this study focused on TAPP repair and compared monopolar and harmonic devices, which differs from our research setting, it showed similar results regarding operation time. Based on this study, we conclude that monopolar energy cannot be replaced in terms of operation time, which is consistent with our findings. However, unlike the results of this study, the AB energy device demonstrated clear advantages in terms of intraoperative blood loss. Future research should compare AB and ultrasonic energy devices under similar settings to further demonstrate these advantages.

In our previous study, we observed significant advantages of AB energy devices in terms of intraoperative blood loss. This can be attributed to the substantial vessel-sealing effect of AB energy devices. Such an effect is crucial for intraoperative safety, ensuring clear surgical visualization and preventing subsequent complications. Additionally, owing to these advantages, the AB group may be arguably preferable when creating a preperitoneal TEP space.

Interestingly, although the difference was not statistically significant, we observed a lower incidence of seroma formation with the use of AB energy devices. This contrasts with findings from another study, wherein postoperative chest drainage following lung lobectomy did not significantly decrease in the AB energy device group [8]. In another study examining the ligation of lymphatics during sentinel lymph node biopsy for melanoma, the rates of lymphocele formation were 20.0%, 18.9%, and 4.7% when using clips, an ultrasonic device, and a bipolar device, respectively [23]. Notably, the rate of lymphocele formation was significantly lower with the bipolar device than with the other methods. These findings are consistent with our observations.

An additional notable finding was the significantly higher early postoperative pain scores observed in the AB group compared to the CM group at 12 and 24 hours postoperatively in the absence of PCA. These statistically significant differences suggest that, especially within enhanced recovery or outpatient surgery settings, careful pain management is essential when PCA is not utilized. This highlights the need for optimized analgesic protocols and thorough patient education to ensure adequate comfort and facilitate timely discharge. Future prospective studies are warranted to further explore device-specific pain profiles and to develop tailored perioperative pain management strategies.

Among studies comparing AB energy devices and CM devices [8,14,16], one study specifically examined differences in postoperative pain outcomes based on energy groups [14]. This study found that the AB energy device group exhibited less pain after hemorrhoidectomy, with this effect diminishing after 14 days [14]. However, apart from comparisons between monopolar and blunt techniques in TEP hernia repair [22], no other studies have directly compared postoperative pain outcomes. Determining the mechanism underlying our observed results is challenging.

Nonetheless, there were no differences in chronic pain, suggesting that, regardless of the energy device used, there was no significant impact on postoperative pain outcomes over time.

In South Korea, the utilization of advanced energy devices in hernia surgery was previously limited within the framework of the diagnosis-related group (DRG) system [24]. However, in the recent DRG system, separate billing for the use of advanced energy devices has become permissible. Therefore, the use of such advanced energy technologies is increasing, and we believe that our study provides valuable background information for understanding and potentially influencing further systemic changes in the DRG.

This study has several limitations. First, all surgeries were performed by a single surgeon, which may limit the generalizability of the results. Although patient characteristics were well balanced between the groups, the AB group procedures were performed during a later period, potentially reflecting increased surgical experience and changes in training or perioperative management over time. This learning curve effect may have influenced the outcomes, and thus, it represents an inherent limitation of this retrospective study design. Future studies involving multiple surgeons and controlled prospective designs are necessary to minimize this bias and validate our findings. Second, intraoperative blood loss was estimated approximately by measuring the volume collected in the suction bottle. While this method does not represent exact blood loss, it was applied consistently throughout the study and is likely reflective of relative trends. Third, our study did not include a cost analysis. Comprehensive assessment of cost differences requires considering device expenses alongside perioperative management factors, which warrants future investigation. Lastly, the relatively small sample size in this retrospective study limits definitive conclusions regarding the advantages of the AB energy device. Further well-designed prospective randomized trials are needed to confirm these findings.

In the future, further well-designed prospective randomized studies are warranted to confirm the benefits of AB energy devices.

In conclusion, our findings suggest that the use of an AB energy device may offer notable advantages in laparoscopic SILTEP, particularly in terms of intraoperative safety and improved surgical outcomes, such as reduced blood loss. These devices appear to be useful and viable options for this minimally invasive approach. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate and expand upon these findings.

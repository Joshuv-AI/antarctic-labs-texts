Application of Multilayer Dermal Regeneration Templates in Fingertip and Nail Bed Reconstruction: A Retrospective Comparative Study
Authors: Tai-Cheng Kuo, Chia-Yu Wang, Yi-Chun Wu, Yu-Ting Wei, Li-Chieh Chang, Jung-Hsuan Chang, Fu-Yu Wang, Shun-Cheng Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Fingertip and nail bed injuries present a clinical challenge, requiring restoration of sensory, functional, and cosmetic integrity without donor-site morbidity. Multilayer dermal regeneration templates (DRTs), including Integra and Terudermis, offer a promising alternative to traditional flap techniques.Methods:This retrospective comparative study included 30 patients with Allen type I–III fingertip injuries treated at a tertiary medical center in Taiwan between January 2020 and December 2022. Reconstructions utilized Integra (n = 12) or Terudermis (n = 18). At 6 months, outcomes were assessed for wound-healing efficacy, cosmetic appearance, sensory recovery, functional restoration, and patient satisfaction. Data were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test and Fisher exact test (P < 0.05).Results:Both Integra and Terudermis achieved comparable outcomes (P > 0.05). Differences in finger length (0.37 cm) and nail bed area (0.34 cm2) were minimal. Vancouver Scar Scale scores ranged from 4 to 5. The mean 2-point discrimination on the affected side was 5.83 mm, with a 13.4% incidence of hook nail. Average distal interphalangeal joint range of motion was 79.0 degrees, and patient satisfaction averaged 4.8 out of 5. Complete epithelialization occurred within 38.9 days.Conclusions:Multilayer DRTs are effective for fingertip and nail bed reconstruction, offering comparable healing efficiency, reliable sensory and functional recovery, and favorable cosmetic and patient-reported outcomes. Integra and Terudermis demonstrated comparable outcomes, supporting their use as viable, donor-site–free options in fingertip reconstruction.

Takeaways

Question: How do Integra and Terudermis dermal regeneration templates compare in sensory, cosmetic, functional, and satisfaction outcomes to guide shared decision-making for fingertip and nail bed reconstruction?

Findings: This retrospective study of 30 patients with Allen type I–III fingertip injuries found no significant differences in healing time, sensory recovery (2-point discrimination, 5.83 cm), cosmetic outcomes (Vancouver Scar Scale, 4–5), functional range of motion (79 degrees), or satisfaction (4.8 of 5) between Integra and Terudermis (all P > 0.05).

Meaning: Integra and Terudermis dermal regeneration templates offer comparable, effective outcomes for fingertip reconstruction, supporting shared decision-making with minimal morbidity.

INTRODUCTION

The fingertip and nail bed are essential for tactile sensation, dexterity, and fine motor control.1 Given the fingertip’s complex anatomy involving bone, nail matrix, pulp, and overlying skin, injuries can significantly impair nail growth, sensation, and joint mobility.2–5 Inadequate treatment may lead to complications such as nail deformity, limited range of motion (ROM), and sensory issues such as numbness or cold intolerance.6 Therefore, effective management should aim to preserve finger length, restore function, and ensure cosmetic outcomes to minimize long-term disability.7

Reconstructive strategies for nonsalvageable fingertip injuries include secondary healing,8 skin grafts,9 dermal matrices,10 replantation, and flaps.11–13 Although these methods can be effective, they often come with donor-site morbidity and may not always yield optimal functional or sensory outcomes.14 Advancements in tissue engineering have introduced dermal regeneration templates (DRTs) as promising bilayer alternatives for soft-tissue reconstruction, designed to promote vascularization and cellular infiltration.14–17 They facilitate soft-tissue coverage over critical structures while restoring function, sensation, and skin integrity, potentially reducing the need for tissue transfer.10,18

Among the available options, Integra (Integra Lifesciences Corp., Plainsboro, NJ), used in combination with skin grafts, has demonstrated favorable outcomes in complex soft-tissue reconstruction,19–21 whereas Terudermis (Terumo Corp., Tokyo, Japan), a single-layer alternative, is valued for its simplicity and effective wound coverage, though its comparative effectiveness regarding sensory and functional recovery remains less well studied.19,20 Both have been applied in treating traumatic, chronic, and burn-related wounds.17,22

Despite their increasing use, the comparative clinical outcomes of multilayer DRTs in fingertip and nail bed reconstruction remain underexplored. This retrospective study evaluated the effectiveness of Integra and Terudermis in terms of healing efficacy, sensory recovery, aesthetic and functional outcomes, and patient satisfaction. Our findings support the use of dermal substitutes in fingertip reconstruction and aim to guide clinical decision-making in managing complex fingertip injuries.23

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients

This single-center retrospective study analyzed patients treated and followed up within a single division at a tertiary medical center. Institutional review board approval was obtained (protocol no. N202406054). Between January 2020 and December 2022, 30 patients with Allen type I–III fingertip injuries involving pulp, bone, or nail bed loss (without germinal matrix damage)24 underwent DRT grafting and were retrospectively analyzed. Patients who were pregnant, had active malignancy, or were receiving corticosteroids or immunosuppressive therapy were excluded. Demographics, injury mechanisms, personal habits, comorbidities, and preoperative laboratory data were also collected (Table 1).

Summary of Patient Characteristics

Given the possibility of concurrent injuries, the total number of affected areas can be greater than 30.

Two DRT brands were offered through shared decision-making: Integra and Terudermis. Integra (minimum size 5 × 5 cm2) is soft, moisture-retentive, and easy to suture but costly and prone to material waste in small defects. Terudermis, available in sizes as small as 2.5 × 2.5 cm2, is more economical for minor injuries but has a firmer texture that makes suturing more difficult. Patients were informed of the characteristics, advantages, and limitations of both DRT types and selected their preferred option before surgery, with informed consent obtained from all participants.

Surgical Procedure and Postoperative Care

Surgery was performed under general anesthesia with a digital block. Defect areas were measured using the Insight 3-dimensional wound measurement camera (eKare Inc., Fairfax, VA).25

Based on prior informed consent, either Integra or Terudermis DRTs were applied. In cases of extensive pulp loss with tendon or bone exposure, multiple DRT layers (with silicone membranes removed) were stacked to restore finger volume, followed by an outer layer for surface reconstruction. For nail bed injuries, artificial nails were secured to the regenerated nail bed with tip-anchor sutures connected to the DRT layers and germinal matrix, and an eponychium nail fold stenting suture was added for support (Fig. 1).

Following surgery, patients were admitted for early wound monitoring. Silver-impregnated dressings (PolyMem Silver) were applied to prevent wound separation and reduce infection. Systemic or topical antibiotics were prescribed as clinically indicated. Epidermal growth factor (EGF) spray was optionally applied based on patient preference and self-funding. The silver dressing, artificial nail stent, and silicone membrane were removed within approximately 3 weeks to evaluate healing progression.

Assessment and Evaluation of Outcomes

At 6 months postsurgery, clinical outcomes were categorized into primary and secondary endpoints. The primary endpoint was wound-healing efficacy. Secondary endpoints included cosmetic outcome, sensory outcome, functional outcome, and overall satisfaction (Fig. 2).

Two dermal regeneration template brands (Integra and Terudermis) were used through a shared decision-making process within the standard of care. A dashed line at the 6-month follow-up assessment distinguishes the primary outcome (wound-healing efficacy) from secondary outcomes (cosmetic outcome, sensory outcome, functional outcome, and overall satisfaction).

Primary Outcome: Wound-healing Efficacy

Wound-healing efficacy was assessed via 2 milestones, measured in days from surgery. The first was the time to silicone membrane removal, indicating adequate neodermal vascularization. The second was the time to complete healing, indicating achievement of full epithelial coverage without the need for further dressings. All patients were followed up for up to 12 months to ensure assessment of healing progression and late outcomes.

Secondary Outcome: Cosmetic, Sensory, and Functional Assessment, and Overall Satisfaction

Cosmetic Outcome

Contour recovery was evaluated by comparing the finger length and nail bed area of the injured fingertip with the corresponding uninjured side. In type III injuries, the soft-tissue residual length distal to the bone stump tip was measured on lateral x-ray views to assess soft-tissue volume restoration. Postoperative scar appearance was assessed using the Vancouver Scar Scale (VSS), which includes 4 components (vascularity, pigmentation, pliability, and height) with a total score ranging from 0 to 13 to reflect overall scar severity.26,27 Nail deformities were also documented, including the incidence of hook nails and the number of hook nail cases among the total nail deformities.28,29

Sensory Outcome

Sensory recovery was assessed using 2-point discrimination (2-PD) tests conducted using the Mackinnon–Dellon Disk-Criminator (AliMed, Dedham, MA).30 Measurements were taken on the reconstructed pulp/volar surface and compared with the corresponding digit on the uninjured hand to minimize individual variability. Neurosensory symptoms, including cold intolerance, altered tactile sensation, and tingling, were recorded. Pain intensity was measured using a visual analog scale (VAS, 0–10) at 5 time points: preoperatively, immediately postoperatively, after silicone membrane removal, at complete epithelialization, and at the 6-month follow-up.

Functional Outcome

To evaluate functional outcomes, we focused on the distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint, which is essential for fine motor tasks such as grasping small objects, writing, and tool use. As the ROM of the DIP joint reflects a finger’s functional mobility, we measured the active ROM in the injured finger using a goniometer and recorded the results in degrees.

Overall Satisfaction

Postoperative satisfaction with the surgical procedure was assessed at the 6-month follow-up using a questionnaire scored on a 1–5 scale, with 5 representing the highest level of satisfaction and 1, the lowest.

Statistical Analysis

This retrospective cohort compared outcomes between Integra and Terudermis. Continuous variables with nonnormal distributions were expressed as medians (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test, whereas categorical variables were analyzed with Fisher exact test. A 2 × 2 subgroup analysis of the DRT brand and bone exposure was conducted. Due to small subgroup sizes, nonparametric tests were applied, including the Kruskal–Wallis test for 4-group comparisons and the Mann-Whitney U test for within-strata analyses. Statistical significance was set at a P value of less than 0.05. Analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 29.0.2.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY).

RESULTS

Participants’ Characteristics

The study included 30 patients aged 23–94 years. Mean height, weight, and body mass index showed no significant group differences. About one-third of patients had a history of smoking, and 2 had diabetes. (See table, Supplemental Digital Content 1, which displays the underlying diseases of patients, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E856.) The main injury mechanisms were machinery accidents (n = 19), household instruments (n = 9), and vehicular accidents (n = 2). Injury types included 24 crush, 5 cut, and 1 degloving injury, with a significant difference in injury type distribution between the groups. Other variables, including age, sex, Allen classification, involved structures, dominant hand involvement, affected finger, and neurovascular damage, showed no significant differences. The average wound area was 3.91 ± 1.86 cm2, involving 6 pulp injuries, 10 cases of bone exposure, and 23 with nail bed damage. Allen classification categorized the injuries into 6 type I, 16 type II, and 9 type III, with 4 injuries involving neurovascular damage (Table 1).

On average, patients underwent 1.2 debridement procedures, with only 2 requiring repeated debridement due to nontuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) infection. The average surgery duration was 38.8 minutes. Nail reconstruction was performed in 23 patients, and EGF spray was applied intraoperatively in 10 patients (4 Integra, 6 Terudermis). The average postoperative hospital stay was 3.8 days, and most patients completed antibiotic therapy within a week, except for the NTM cases. The silicone membrane was removed at an average of 17.3 days, and complete epithelialization occurred within 3 months for all patients (average 38.9 d). A 2 × 2 subgroup analysis revealed no significant differences between DRT brand and bone exposure (Kruskal–Wallis and Mann-Whitney U tests, all P > 0.05). (See table, Supplemental Digital Content 2, which displays a summary of surgical outcomes: between DRT brand and bone exposure, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E857.) Primary Outcome: Wound-healing Efficacy

Both groups demonstrated comparable healing efficiency. Time to silicone membrane removal (16.8 ± 1.8 versus 18.0 ± 2.0 d; P = 0.104) and complete healing (38.1 ± 10.8 versus 40.3 ± 9.1 d; P = 0.368) showed no significant differences, confirming similar wound-healing durations regardless of the brand used.

Secondary Outcome: Cosmetic Outcome

The average finger length was 7.12 cm on the healthy side and 6.75 cm on the affected side, with a mean difference of 0.37 cm. The nail bed area averaged 1.55 cm2 on the healthy side and 1.20 cm2 on the affected side, showing a mean reduction of 0.34 cm2. All 23 patients who underwent nail reconstruction developed nail deformities of varying severity, including 3 cases of hook nails. Among patients with type III injuries, the average residual soft-tissue length distal to the bone stump tip on lateral-view x-ray was 2.1 mm. Postoperative scar assessment using the VSS showed scores ranging from 4 to 5, indicating mild to moderate scarring (Supplemental Digital Content 4, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E859). No significant differences were observed in finger length, nail bed area, nail deformities, or scar appearance between groups, suggesting comparable cosmetic outcomes between the 2 DRT brands.

Secondary Outcome: Sensory Outcome

The average 2-PD distance measured 2.7 mm on the healthy side and 5.83 mm on the affected side, both within normal limits. Neurosensory complications included cold intolerance in 6 patients, tactile sensation abnormalities in 9, and tingling in 20, with 6 patients experiencing all 3 symptoms simultaneously. VAS pain scores steadily declined from 8.9 on the day of injury to 4.4 postoperatively, 2.9 after silicone membrane removal, 2.0 at complete healing, and 1.4 at 6 months follow-up. No significant differences were observed between the Integra and Terudermis groups in 2-PD, neurosensory complications, or VAS pain scores, indicating comparable sensory recovery between 2 DRT brands (Supplemental Digital Content 4, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E859).

Secondary Outcome: Functional Outcome

The average active ROM in the DIP joint was 83.3 degrees on the healthy side and 79.0 degrees on the affected side. ROM recovery did not significantly differ between the 2 groups, which indicated similar functional outcomes (Supplemental Digital Content 4, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E859).

Secondary Outcome: Overall Satisfaction

Postoperative satisfaction was high across all patients, with an average score of 4.8 out of 5, and no significant difference was observed between groups, which confirmed comparable patient satisfaction for both DRT brands (Supplemental Digital Content 4, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E859).

A 64-year-old man with hypertension sustained a crush injury to his right thumb while using a floor scrubber, resulting in pulp swelling and pain. The injury predominantly affected the pulp and was classified as an Allen type I fingertip injury. DISCUSSION

Reflecting regional manufacturing patterns, most injuries were work-related crush events, explaining the male predominance. Two patients with chronic nonhealing wounds were diagnosed with NTM infection and healed after 9 months of antimicrobial therapy, with only minor contour irregularities. No other infections or acute postoperative complications were observed.

Injuries were classified as Allen type I (20%), II (50%), and III (30%). Type IV injuries were excluded due to the need for bone shortening and their unsuitability for DRTs.31,32 Involved structures included pulp loss, bone exposure, and nail bed damage; some patients had multiple components, resulting in percentages exceeding 100%.

Postoperative finger shortening averaged 0.37 cm. In type III injuries, residual soft-tissue thickness averaged 2.1 mm, slightly less than 2.5 mm on the contralateral side, which may potentially cause discomfort during daily activities. Cosmetic outcomes were favorable, with VSS scores of 4–5, lower than previously reported medians of 7 in similar wounds,33 likely reflecting improved contour support from the multilayer DRT technique. Hook nail deformity, which can cause pain; impaired function; or, rarely, amputation,29 occurred in 10% of patients. This rate is lower than in previous DRT studies (22%)10,31,33 but slightly higher than in reverse digital artery flaps (6.5%).10 However, as nail contour may change after 6 months, longer follow-up is necessary to assess late deformities.

Prompt debridement resulted in efficient operative time (38.2 min) and hospital stay (3.8 d). Regarding clinical efficacy, the primary goal of stable wound closure was achieved reliably in both groups. Time to silicone removal (mean 17.3 d) and complete healing (mean 38.9 d) were comparable (Supplemental Digital Content 4, https://links.lww.com/PRSGO/E859), confirming a similar vascularization and reepithelialization timeline independent of DRT brand. This clinical equivalence aligns with the understanding that while different DRTs vary in composition or structure (bilayer versus monolayer), they share the fundamental biological mechanism of supporting host cell infiltration and neovascularization to form a functional neodermis.34,35

Although we recorded sensory (2-PD) and functional (ROM) outcomes in the comparison of 2 templates, these metrics are predominantly influenced by the initial injury mechanism (crush, avulsion, laceration) and severity (size, bone/tendon exposure, nail bed involvement)9 rather than by differences between the 2 specific brands of DRT. In our cohort, overall sensory recovery averaged 5.8 mm (versus 2.7 mm in the contralateral side), comparable to previously reported results for artificial dermis grafts and superior to flap-based reconstruction,10 yet without significant differences between Integra and Terudermis. Similarly, overall ROM was well preserved, with 26 participants experiencing only a 5-degree reduction.

Compared with flap or graft-based reconstruction, DRTs avoided donor-site morbidity, involved shorter procedures, and reduced postoperative pain.8,10,33,36 Although our study did not directly compare DRTs with flap reconstruction, the outcomes show comparable results between DRTs and reverse digital artery island flaps,10 and between DRT combined with autologous skin grafts and pedicled skin flaps.37 Studies of scalp reconstruction using Integra and local flaps have likewise demonstrated reliable healing and durable coverage.38 Direct comparisons with the Moberg flap for thumb injuries are unavailable because prior studies excluded thumb cases.39,40 Few studies have directly compared DRTs with secondary intention healing, representing an important direction for future research.

Integra and Terudermis are both collagen-based DRTs but differ in structure and composition. Integra combines cross-linked bovine tendon type I collagen and chondroitin-6-sulfate with a silicone outer layer, whereas Terudermis is a single-layer atelocollagen sheet requiring external dressings.41,42 Despite these differences, both achieved similar healing time, preserved protective sensation and joint mobility, satisfactory cosmetic outcomes, and high patient satisfaction, with no revision surgery required. These findings support Integra and Terudermis as practical options for patients seeking reliable healing with minimal postoperative burden, particularly when comorbidities or patient preference limit traditional graft or flap surgery.

Study Limitations

This study is limited by its retrospective, nonrandomized design and small sample size, which restrict statistical power and the ability to perform comparative analysis. Potential confounding may arise from selection bias regarding patient occupation (predominantly manual laborers), which may influence baseline fingertip sensitivity.33 Additionally, DRT selection and optional EGF application based on patient preference may introduce bias, although EGF use rates were balanced between the groups. Methodological constraints also exist; specifically, although a 3-dimensional wound measurement camera was used to measure total defect area, confluent injury patterns limited separate nail and pulp differentiation. Furthermore, the VSS is not a fingertip-specific tool, and the lack of US approval for Terudermis limits generalizability, though the findings remain applicable in regions such as Taiwan and other Asian countries where both products are widely used. Finally, we acknowledge the intrinsic limitations of DRTs,10,33 including slightly longer epithelialization time and higher cost,10,22 and the need for careful monitoring of the avascular multilayer construct.43 Larger prospective studies, including direct comparisons with conventional reconstruction, are needed to clarify long-term outcomes.

CONCLUSIONS

In this retrospective cohort of Allen type I–III fingertip injuries, Integra and Terudermis demonstrated comparable healing time and overall recovery, supporting their use as viable donor-site–free options for selected patients. Although complete epithelialization took longer than with skin grafts, functional recovery occurred much earlier, with short hospitalization, minimal postoperative pain, and high patient satisfaction. These findings suggest that DRTs are particularly suitable for individuals who wish to avoid donor-site morbidity, for patients with comorbidities that limit traditional graft or flap surgery, or for those who decline additional operative intervention. Further prospective studies are needed to define long-term outcomes and to clarify the optimal indications for DRT-based fingertip reconstruction.

Clinical efficacy of Mepilex Lite dressings combined with indomethacin suppositories in the treatment of acute radiation dermatitis caused by rectal cancer radiotherapy: a study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Heli Shang, Yanwei Yang, Hailan Cao, Yan Wu, Hong Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAcute radiation dermatitis is a common adverse reaction in radiotherapy, especially in patients with rectal cancer undergoing radiotherapy. At present, acute radiation dermatitis of the perianal skin has received widespread attention; however, acute radiation skin injury of the anal canal skin has not received sufficient attention. These patients mainly complain of anal pain, which seriously affects their quality of life. Mepilex Lite dressings have been recommended for the treatment of acute radiation dermatitis and have a definite effect. However, there is limited research on the use of this dressing for the treatment of acute radiation-induced dermatitis of the anal canal skin and its clinical efficacy and safety in this context need further verification.Methods and analysisThis study is an assessor-blinded randomised controlled trial designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Mepilex Lite dressings combined with indomethacin suppositories for the treatment of acute radiation dermatitis of the anal canal skin. A total of 274 eligible patients from the tumour radiotherapy ward of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University will be randomly assigned to either the experiment or control group (1:1). The control group will be given 30 mg of indomethacin suppositories for rectal administration, and the experiment group will be given Mepilex Lite dressings in addition to the measures taken by the control group. The 5 cm × 5 cm dressing will be inserted into the anal canal for 3 cm through the anus and exposed for 2 cm. The main outcome indicator is the treatment efficiency of the acute radiation dermatitis of the anal canal skin on the 14th day after the intervention. The secondary outcome indicator is the treatment efficiency of the acute radiation dermatitis of the anal canal skin on the seventh and 21st days after the intervention. Concurrently, the Numeric Rating Scale, Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and Skin Disease Patient Quality of Life Scale-16 will be used to assess patient pain, depression and anxiety, sleep quality and quality of life, respectively. A table questionnaire survey will be conducted at baseline and on 7, 14 and 21 days after intervention.Ethics and disseminationThe research protocol has obtained ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University (approval number: YXLL-KY-2024 (162)). The results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and abstracts will be submitted to domestic and international conferences to disseminate our research findings.Trial registration numberNCT06837831.Keywords: Clinical Protocols, Adult radiotherapy, Randomized Controlled Trial

Introduction

The high incidence of colorectal cancer has become an important concern in the global public health field. According to GLOBOCAN 2022 statistics, it has become the third most common cancer worldwide and the second leading cause of death. It is expected that by 2022 there will be over 1.9 million new cases of colorectal cancer worldwide, and the number of deaths will reach 900000.1 In China, there were about 517 100 new cases in the same year, accounting for 10.7% of all malignant tumours,2 of which the incidence rate of rectal cancer was higher than that of colon cancer.3

Radiotherapy, as one of the core treatments for rectal cancer, plays a crucial role in various modes of therapy, such as neoadjuvant, adjuvant, curative, translational and palliative therapy.4 It effectively improves clinical treatment outcomes and is an essential component of current rectal cancer treatment.5 However, it is worth noting that even with the continuous upgrading of radiotherapy technology and equipment in recent years, patients still experience various toxic reactions during radiotherapy,6 among which radiation-induced skin damage related to the anal canal is particularly prominent.7

During radiotherapy for patients with rectal cancer, the anal canal is often included in the irradiation area,8 and the special anatomical structure of the anal canal makes it more sensitive to radiation damage. The total length of the anal canal is about 3.5–4 cm, divided into the anatomical and surgical anal canal.9 The upper boundary of the anatomical anal canal is the dentate line, and the lower boundary is the anal margin, with a length of approximately 2–3 cm. It is composed of non-keratinised stratified squamous epithelium and is specialised anal canal skin. It is thin, smooth, moist, inelastic and innervated by somatic nerves, making it extremely sensitive to pain.10 As the radiation dose increases, the anal canal skin is continuously exposed and damaged, leading to manifestations of radiation dermatitis such as erythema, desquamation, pain and ulceration. In clinical practice, the acute radiation dermatitis scoring criteria of the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG) are often used to classify its toxicity level into five grades11: grade 0, no change from baseline; grade 1, follicular-like, pale red or dark erythema, hair loss, dry peeling and reduced sweating; grade 2, painful erythema or fresh erythema, patchy wet peeling and moderate oedema; grade 3, fusion wet peeling and sunken oedema in areas other than skin folds; and grade 4, ulcers, bleeding and necrosis occur. In summary, due to its unique anatomical location and tissue structure, the anal canal skin experiences more severe radiation dermatitis and pain symptoms after radiation injury. Moreover, radiotherapy is often accompanied by acute radiation enteritis, and an increase in bowel movements can lead to repeated dilation of the anal canal, causing friction and damage to the anal skin caused by faeces, further exacerbating radiation dermatitis and anal pain.12

The common chief complaint of patients is ‘anal pain, and the pain significantly intensifies during defecation’. Some patients describe the pain as ‘severe, tearing like pain’. Long-term severe pain during bowel movements can cause patients to develop fear of defecation, leading to deliberate holding and delayed bowel movements. The prolonged residence time of faeces in the intestine can lead to excessive absorption of water, making faeces drier and harder, further exacerbating the anal canal skin damage and pain of the anus during defecation. This establishes a recurring sequence whereby pain induces fear of defecation, which in turn leads to faecal dryness and subsequent pain exacerbation. Most patients consider defecation as a daily ‘small matter’, and even if the pain is severe, they often choose to endure it and rarely actively inform medical staff. Concurrently, anal pain symptoms are often masked by more obvious manifestations of radiation enteritis, which leads to failure to receive timely and effective intervention, seriously reducing the quality of life of patients.

Currently, there has been extensive research and attention on radiation-induced dermatitis in clinical practice; however, related studies on radiation-induced dermatitis of the anal canal skin are still relatively scarce. Radiation dermatitis of the anal canal skin can cause severe pain in patients, although this symptom has received attention from the academic community, existing research has mostly focused on optimising the radiotherapy dose.13 14 In clinical practice, pain relief drugs are often used for symptomatic intervention of anal pain15; however, such schemes can only temporarily relieve discomfort, and the pain is prone to recurrence after the efficacy subsides, making it difficult to achieve sustained pain relief. Therefore, exploring long-term intervention and treatment methods has become an urgent clinical key issue that needs to be addressed.

Studies have shown that Mepilex Lite dressings can reduce the severity of radiation dermatitis, relieve pain and improve patient sleep. The Safetac soft silica gel technology it uses ensures that the dressing adheres to the intact dry skin instead of the wet wound bed; moreover, it can be properly cut as required. Consequently, it has significant advantages for comfort, ease of removal, skin protection and quality of life.16 17 In addition, Mepilex Lite dressings are also used to treat diabetic foot ulcers, leg and waist ulcers, pressure injuries and traumatic wounds, such as blisters and skin tears.18 19 However, to the best of our knowledge, there is no research on the application of Mepilex Lite dressings in the treatment of anal canal skin radiation dermatitis and on its use to alleviate anal pain.16 Therefore, in this study, we aimed to explore the efficacy and safety of Mepilex Lite dressings in the treatment of anal canal skin radiation dermatitis caused by rectal cancer radiotherapy.

To further improve patient comfort during anal canal insertion of Mepilex Lite dressings and effectively prevent iatrogenic pain, after discussion and validation by the project team, rectal administration of indomethacin suppository was finally adopted prior to dressing application. Indomethacin suppositories are non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and prostaglandin synthase inhibitors. The peak blood concentration is reached 30–90 min after rectal administration, and the bioavailability is approximately 80–90%.20 21 Indomethacin suppositories have been proven to have significant analgesic effects,21 22 and rectal administration can significantly reduce gastric mucosal damage and avoid adverse reactions such as gastrointestinal bleeding. However, serious adverse reactions such as anaphylactic shock23 and abnormal mental behaviour24 occur from time to time, which is related to the excessive dosage of indomethacin suppositories. Research has shown that low-dose indomethacin suppositories (50 mg) are safe for anal administration and have a low incidence of adverse reactions.25 Therefore, to ensure medication safety and reduce the occurrence of adverse reactions, 30 mg indomethacin suppositories were used for rectal administration in this study.

Hence, in this study, we aimed to explore the clinical efficacy and safety of Mepilex Lite dressings combined with indomethacin suppositories in the treatment of anal canal skin radiation dermatitis caused by rectal cancer radiotherapy.

Objectives

Primary objective

Evaluate the efficacy of treatment for radiation-induced dermatitis of the anal canal skin on the 14th day after intervention. We hypothesised that the effect of Mepilex Lite dressings combined with indomethacin suppository in the treatment of anal canal skin radiation dermatitis will be better than that of indomethacin suppository alone.

Secondary objective

Evaluate the efficacy of treatment for anal canal skin radiation dermatitis on days 7 and 21 after intervention, and use the numeric rating scale (NRS), Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), and Skin Disease Patient Quality of Life Scale (Skindex-16) to assess patients’ anal pain, anxiety and depression, sleep quality, and quality of life, respectively at baseline and on days 7, 14 and 21 after intervention.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study was conducted according to the approved study protocol (Version 14.0, December 6, 2025). This is an assessor-blinded randomised controlled trial that compares the efficacy and safety of the control group (indomethacin suppositories) and the experimental group (indomethacin suppositories combined with Mepilex Lite dressings) in the management of acute anal canal radiation dermatitis. Starting from February 2026, patient recruitment will be conducted in the Oncology Radiotherapy Ward of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University. The end of the study is defined as the end of intervention treatment for the last enrolled patient, and is expected to be completed in August 2027. The design and writing of this research protocol are based on the SPIRIT 2025 Checklist, a unified standard for clinical trial reports.

Randomisation and masking

After inclusion criteria verification and baseline assessment, a random allocation sequence will be generated using IBM SPSS Statistics (V. 25.0; IBM Corp.). Eligible patients will be randomised 1:1 to the experimental or control group using simple randomisation. Randomisation results will be sealed in opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes. When a patient is enrolled, the researcher will open the next envelope and assign the patient accordingly. Outcome assessors and data analysts will remain blinded. Treating nurses and patients cannot be blinded due to the visible nature of the foam dressings. Staff involved in enrolment and assignment cannot view the random allocation sequence.

The random allocation sequence will be securely stored in a password-protected file, accessible only to a study staff member who is not involved in participant enrolment, intervention assignment or outcome assessment. Unblinding will be performed by this designated individual on a justified request.

Data collection

Patient demographic information will be collected through questionnaires at baseline, including age, sex, education level, marital status, income, medical insurance payment method, radiation dose, pathological type and family history.

Intervention

Both patient groups will be treated with 6MV-X-ray intensity-modulated radiation therapy using the uRT-linac 506c linear accelerator (United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China). The prescribed dose was 95% of the planned target volume, with a total dose of 45–50 Gy, divided into 25–28 doses and completed within 5–6 weeks.

Intervention method: All patients will receive routine care, including informing them to gently clean the perianal skin with warm water after each bowel movement and gently wiping it with a cotton cloth. They will also wear loose cotton underwear to keep the perianal skin clean and dry. We will guide patients to consume high-protein, high-vitamin and low-fibre foods and avoid spicy and stimulating foods. We will strengthen communication between nurses and patients, provide psychological counselling and allow them to reasonably vent their anxiety and other emotions. We will inform patients of the grading criteria for radiation-induced skin injury, provide corresponding nursing measures according to the grading criteria and closely observe them.

Control group: On the basis of routine care, olive oil will be provided to patients as a moisturising agent.26 Starting from the first day of radiotherapy and continuing for 2 weeks after its completion, nurses will apply olive oil to the patient’s anal canal and perianal skin three times daily (morning, midday and evening). Before each application, nurses will cleanse the anal canal and perianal skin and allow it to dry. Using a sterile cotton swab (10 cm; Henan Meisheng Medical Technology Co., Ltd.; production batch number: 2410B10319) dipped in olive oil until the tip is fully saturated but without dripping, nurses will then apply a thin layer of olive oil to the anal canal and perianal skin. During the radiotherapy course, all olive oil residue must be thoroughly cleansed from the irradiated skin area prior to each radiotherapy fraction.27 After emptying their stool and urine before bedtime (21:00), nurses will again cleanse the patient’s anal canal and perianal skin and allow it to dry, followed by the rectal administration of 30 mg indomethacin suppositories.

Experiment group: In addition to the measures applied to the control group, patients in the experiment group will be given Mepilex Lite dressings (10cm×10 cm; Sweden Monique Medical Products Co., Ltd.; National Medical Products Administration registration number 20173141524) for insertion into the anal canal. The specific method will be as follows: After administering a 30 mg indomethacin suppository rectally and waiting for 30 min, the patient will be assisted into the left lateral position with both legs flexed toward the abdomen. The anal canal will then be cleaned using cotton swabs soaked in 0.9% sodium chloride solution (Shandong Qidu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.; national drug approval number: H20153179), after which the area will be allowed to dry. Once dry, the operator exposes the anus and the damaged anal canal skin with his left hand. Using the right hand, the operator will apply the adhesive side of a 5cm × 5 cm foam dressing to the wound and instruct the patient to take a deep breath. At the end of exhalation, the dressing will be gently inserted approximately 3 cm into the anal canal using the cotton end of a cotton swab, leaving 2 cm exposed externally, ensuring that the dressing adheres fully to the damaged anal canal skin. The operator will then gradually release the left hand, withdraw the cotton swab and instruct the patient to remain in bed and avoid defecation. The dressing will be removed the following morning. If the dressing dislodges prematurely, the above procedure will be repeated.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome measure

On the 14th day after intervention, the evaluation of the effectiveness of treatment for anal canal skin radiation dermatitis will be conducted using the RTOG acute radiation dermatitis grading standard.11

Efficacy evaluation criteria

On day 14 of the intervention, the RTOG grade of acute radiation dermatitis decreased by one grade or more compared with baseline.28

Secondary outcome measures

The effective rate of treatment for radiation-induced dermatitis of the anal canal skin on the seventh and 21st day after intervention will be evaluated using the RTOG grading criteria11; changes in anal pain scores, anxiety and depression scores, PSQI and Skindex-16 scores from baseline to days 7, 14 and 21 after intervention.

NRS

The pain level of the anal canal will be assessed using the NRS.29 We will replace different degrees of pain with a score of 0–10, where 0 indicates no pain, 1–3 indicates mild pain, 4–6 indicates moderate pain and 7–10 indicates severe pain. The nurse will inform the patient of the rules for using the digital scoring table, and the patient will select the location that represents their pain from 0 to 10 based on their own pain level.

HADS

The HADS30 is mainly used for screening anxiety and depression among patients in comprehensive hospitals: 0–7 points indicate that the patient is asymptomatic, 8–10 points indicate suspected anxiety or depression symptoms and 11–21 points indicate definite anxiety or depression symptoms.

PSQI

The PSQI questionnaire was used to evaluate the sleep quality of patients.31 The questionnaire includes seven factors, each of which is scored from 0 to 3 points. The total score of all factors is the PSQI score, with a total score of 0 to 21 points. A PSQI score<7 indicates good sleep quality, while a PSQI score≥7 indicates poor sleep quality (insomnia).

Skindex-16

Using Zhang Zhongzhao’s Chinese version of the Skindex-16 scale, the degree of distress caused by the skin condition of patients in the past week was evaluated,32 including three dimensions: symptoms, emotions and function. It was divided into seven levels from ‘never troubled’ to ‘always troubled', with higher scores indicating more severe distress caused by acute radiation dermatitis to patients.

Safety assessment

Any adverse medical event occurring during the trial period will be defined as a harm, including both serious and non-serious adverse events. A systematic approach will be adopted for safety assessment. During each follow-up visit, a study nurse will actively inquire about the occurrence of adverse events using a standardised questionnaire. Both indomethacin suppositories and Mepilex Lite dressings are widely used in clinical practice, with well-documented safety profiles supported by existing literature. Both products have received marketing authorisation and clinical use clearance from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

Electronic data will be saved in encrypted, password-protected systems with restricted access. Paper records will be securely stored in locked cabinets. Detailed standard operating procedures for data management are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Statistical methods

Sample size

This study will adopt a superior efficacy trial design, with the main outcome measure being the treatment efficacy of acute radiation-induced dermatitis of the anal canal skin on the 14th day after intervention. Our literature search indicated that there is currently a lack of relevant research on acute radiation dermatitis of the anal canal skin caused by radiotherapy for rectal cancer; in contrast, research on perianal radiation dermatitis is relatively sufficient. Given the high overlap of the radiotherapy target areas between the two, this study referred to relevant data of perianal acute radiation dermatitis for parameter estimation, and set the expected treatment efficacy rate of the control group 14 days after intervention as 59%.28 A meta-analysis including four randomised controlled trials showed that,33 compared with the traditional method, Mepilex Lite dressings could significantly improve the healing rate of radiation dermatitis (OR=5.67; 95% CI 1.74 to 18.41), and calculated that the effective rate of acute radiation dermatitis treatment 14 days after intervention in the test group would be 89%. Based on clinical significance and research feasibility, the optimal efficacy threshold (δ) of this study was set at 15%. PASS 2025 (Version 25.0.3; NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, Utah, USA) was used, and a one-sided test was conducted with an alpha of 0.025 and a CI (1- β) of 80%. Consequently, we determined that a minimum of 130 patients would be required for each group. Considering a dropout rate of 5%, the final experimental and control groups each require 137 patients for a total of 274 patients in both groups.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA) will be used for data analysis. For continuous variables such as age and pain score, if they follow a normal distribution, they will be described as means±SD deviations, and a t-test will be used to compare the differences between groups. If they do not follow a normal distribution, medians (IQRs) will be used to present group differences, and a non-parametric rank sum test (Mann–Whitney U test) will be used to compare inter-group differences. Categorical variables such as sex, education level, medical insurance payment method and lesion location will be presented as frequencies and rates (or composition ratio), and inter-group differences will be compared using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact probability method. Repeated measures analysis of variance or generalised estimation equations will be used to evaluate the NRS, HADS, PSQI and Skindex-16 scores between the two patient groups. The Cochran Mantel Haenszel test (stratified by centre) will be used to compare inter-group differences, and the absolute risk difference and its 95% CI will be calculated. If the lower limit of the CI is greater than 15%, it will be judged as a superior effect.

Handling of missing data

Missing data will be minimised by effective participant retention. Observed data will be included in the analysis, and multiple imputation will be used for missing values as appropriate. All analyses will be conducted based on the intention-to-treat principle.

Interim analyses and stopping guidelines

No interim analyses will be conducted in this study. The decision to stop the trial will be made by the principal investigator if necessary, based on participant safety, ethical requirements or study feasibility.

Data monitoring and safety

A formal data monitoring committee (DMC) was not established for this study. Given the nature of the interventions, low risk to participants and simple study design, an independent DMC is not required. All data monitoring and safety reviews will be performed by the principal investigator and research team. The principal investigator will supervise study conduct to ensure protocol compliance and data integrity. No external or independent trial monitoring will be conducted.

Protocol amendments

Any important modifications to the trial protocol will be submitted to the ethics committee for review and approval prior to implementation. Relevant updates will also be communicated to all research staff to ensure consistent trial conduct.

Neither patients nor the public were involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

The research protocol, including all subsequent amendments, has been granted ethical approval by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University (approval number: YXLL-KY-2024(162)). This trial was prospectively registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (registration number: NCT06837831) on 17 February 2025, with a protocol amendment issued on 06 April 2026. Key amendments included the addition of indomethacin suppositories to the control group and revisions to the primary and secondary outcome measures. These amendments were implemented to reduce research bias, minimise potential confounding effects of indomethacin suppositories on outcome indicators, and further highlight the clinical advantages of the Mepilex Lite dressings. All patients provided written informed consent prior to data collection. The study findings will be submitted to relevant peer-reviewed scientific journals and international conferences. Following study completion, patients will be able to enquire about the research results.

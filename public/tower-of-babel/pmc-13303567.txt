Acupuncture and Moxibustion Therapy Attenuates Inflammation in CCI-Induced Sciatica by Inhibiting the NF-κB Pathway
Authors: Wenchong Wang, Yang Zhang, Jiaming Liang, Hanqing Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo investigate the analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects of an integrated acupuncture–moxibustion regimen in a rat model of chronic constriction injury (CCI)-induced sciatica, with particular emphasis on NF-κB p65 expression and nuclear translocation as activation-related indicators of NF-κB–associated neuroimmune signaling.MethodsA rat CCI model was established and randomly allocated into four experimental groups sham surgery (Sham), untreated CCI (CCI), active drug comparator (Drug), and acupuncture–moxibustion therapy (Acu). Pain-related behavioral alterations were evaluated using thermal withdrawal latency (TWL) and mechanical withdrawal threshold (MWT) assessments. Histopathological alterations in the sciatic nerve and spinal cord were examined using hematoxylin–eosin staining. The expression of proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, was quantified by quantitative real-time PCR and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). NF-κB activation-related changes were further evaluated by immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence analyses focusing on NF-κB p65 expression and nuclear localization.ResultsCompared with the CCI group, rats receiving acupuncture–moxibustion therapy demonstrated significant recovery of TWL and MWT values, indicating attenuation of neuropathic pain hypersensitivity. Histological analyses revealed substantial preservation of sciatic nerve and spinal cord architecture following treatment. RT-qPCR and ELISA findings showed marked reductions in proinflammatory cytokine expression and circulating inflammatory mediator levels in the Acu group. Furthermore, immunohistochemical and immunofluorescence analyses demonstrated significant suppression of NF-κB p65 expression and reduced nuclear translocation following acupuncture–moxibustion intervention.ConclusionAcupuncture–moxibustion therapy effectively ameliorated CCI-induced sciatica and was associated with suppression of proinflammatory cytokine production and attenuation of NF-κB–associated inflammatory signaling, providing mechanistic support for integrative non-pharmacological strategies in neuropathic pain management.Keywords: acupuncture and moxibustion, CCI model, inflammation, NF-κB pathway, sciatica

INTRODUCTION

Sciatica is a highly prevalent chronic neuropathic pain disorder characterized by persistent radiating pain along the sciatic nerve distribution, frequently accompanied by sensory disturbances, muscle weakness, and impaired motor function [1]. These clinical manifestations substantially diminish quality of life, functional capacity, and social participation. Epidemiological studies estimate that the lifetime prevalence of sciatica may approach 40% in the adult population worldwide, with particularly high incidence among middle-aged and older individuals, rendering it one of the most common conditions encountered in chronic pain practice [2-4]. Although contemporary imaging techniques can identify structural abnormalities such as intervertebral disc herniation and nerve root compression, the relationship between radiographic findings and symptom severity remains inconsistent, indicating that the pathophysiology of sciatica extends beyond simple mechanical nerve compression [4, 5]. Increasing evidence suggests that chronic sciatica involves a multifactorial process characterized by neuroinflammation, immune dysregulation, and peripheral and central sensitization within the nervous system [6, 7].

The pathophysiological progression of chronic sciatica is closely associated with demyelination, axonal degeneration, glial cell activation, and persistent production of proinflammatory mediators [8]. Following sciatic nerve injury, inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α become markedly upregulated, thereby enhancing neuronal excitability and sustaining nociceptive transmission [9]. Because rodents do not possess a direct CXCL8 (human IL-8) ortholog, “IL-8” in the present study refers to an IL-8–like CXCR2-axis chemokine response in rats. Among the signaling pathways implicated in neuropathic pain, NF-κB is regarded as a central regulator of neuroinflammatory activation. Activation of the NF-κB signaling cascade promotes transcription of numerous inflammatory mediators, thereby perpetuating a maladaptive cycle of inflammation, neuronal injury, and chronic pain sensitization [10, 11]. Conventional pharmacological interventions, including non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), antiepileptic agents, and opioids, may provide transient symptomatic relief; however, their long-term clinical utility is constrained by adverse effects such as tolerance, dependence, and central nervous system suppression, highlighting the need for safer and mechanistically targeted therapeutic strategies [12-14].

Traditional Chinese Medicine modalities, particularly acupuncture and moxibustion, have attracted substantial scientific and clinical interest because of their analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective properties, and they have been incorporated into chronic pain management guidelines in multiple countries [15]. Acupuncture is believed to modulate the neuro-immune-endocrine network through targeted stimulation of specific acupoints, thereby eliciting coordinated analgesic, immunoregulatory, and anti-inflammatory effects through both central and peripheral pathways [16, 17]. Moxibustion combines thermal stimulation with biologically active volatile compounds, which may enhance local microcirculation and mitochondrial activity while attenuating oxidative stress and inflammatory injury [18]. Previous studies have demonstrated that acupuncture or moxibustion alone can partially alleviate CCI-induced neuropathic pain; however, the anti-inflammatory mechanisms underlying a clinically utilized combined acupuncture–moxibustion regimen remain insufficiently defined. Furthermore, whether the combined intervention confers additive or synergistic therapeutic effects beyond either modality alone requires dedicated factorial investigation and was not specifically addressed in the present study [19, 20].

The combined acupuncture–moxibustion regimen constitutes a widely utilized integrative therapeutic approach in Traditional Chinese Medicine practice and is traditionally considered to regulate “cold and heat,” restore meridian flow, and harmonize yin and yang, thereby contributing to symptomatic improvement in neurogenic disorders [21-23]. Nevertheless, mechanistic investigations examining its therapeutic role in sciatica remain limited and are largely observational in nature, with insufficient emphasis on molecular and neuroimmune mechanisms. In particular, its regulatory effects on inflammatory cytokine networks and NF-κB–associated signaling pathways have not been comprehensively elucidated.

Accordingly, the present study employed a rat chronic constriction injury model to systematically investigate the anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects of acupuncture–moxibustion therapy in sciatica, with particular emphasis on NF-κB p65 expression and nuclear translocation as activation-related indicators of NF-κB–associated signaling. Through integrated behavioral assessments, histopathological analyses, quantification of inflammatory mediators, and evaluation of NF-κB expression and nuclear localization, this study sought to identify potential molecular correlates underlying Acu-mediated attenuation of neuropathic pain and to provide experimental and theoretical support for its application in neuroinflammatory pain management.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

1. Reagents

Japan Tower Dual Ingredients Fast-acting Sciatica Pills (batch numbers: 302,164,392/301,707,840) were obtained from Warsaw Pharmaceutical Factory (Hong Kong, China). Ingredient information was collected from the manufacturer’s package insert and labeling materials and subsequently cross-referenced with publicly available product information. According to the manufacturer, the principal constituents include Panax ginseng, Eucommia ulmoides, Gastrodia elata, Angelica sinensis, Panax notoginseng, frankincense (Boswellia spp.), and myrrh (Commiphora spp.). The present study evaluated the formulation as an integrated multi-component preparation and was not designed to determine the therapeutic contribution of any individual constituent. Sterile disposable acupuncture needles (0.25 mm × 13 mm) were supplied by Beijing Zhongyan Taihe Medical Device Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China), and moxibustion sticks (18 mm × 200 mm) were purchased from Nanjing Tongrentang (Nanjing, China). Von Frey filaments (model 514000-20 C) were obtained from Shanghai Yuyan Scientific Instrument Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Total RNA extraction was performed using Trizol reagent (CW0580; Cwbio, Jiangsu, China). First-strand complementary DNA (cDNA) synthesis was conducted using SweScript All-in-One First-Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (G3337-50), whereas quantitative PCR amplification was carried out using 2× SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (G3326-1), both purchased from Servicebio (Wuhan, China). Commercial ELISA kits for IL-6 (GER0001), TNF-α (GER0004), IL-8 (E-EL-R0016), and IL-1β (GER0002) were obtained from Servicebio (Wuhan, China). For clarity, “IL-8” is used herein as a conventional designation for IL-8–like CXCR2-axis chemokines in rats rather than as a strict species ortholog. Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining was performed using a commercially available kit (G1120; Servicebio, Wuhan, China).

2. Experimental animals and grouping

Twenty-four healthy adult male Sprague–Dawley rats weighing 200-250 g were obtained from Guangdong Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. Animals were maintained under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions at 22 ± 2℃ and 50% ± 10% relative humidity under a controlled 12-hour light/dark cycle, with unrestricted access to standard laboratory chow and water. Following a 7-day acclimatization period, rats were randomly allocated into four experimental groups (n = 6/group): Sham (sciatic nerve exposure without ligation), CCI (chronic constriction injury), Drug (Japan Tower Dual Ingredients Fast-acting Sciatica Pills administered by oral gavage), and acupuncture–moxibustion therapy (Acu). The Drug group was included as a pragmatic active comparator representing a clinically utilized formulation rather than as a standardized reference intervention for CCI-induced neuropathic pain models. All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the Regulations on the Administration of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Youjiang Medical University for Nationalities (Approval No. 2024051101).

3. Drug administration

Rats assigned to the Drug group received Japan Tower Dual Ingredients Fast-acting Sciatica Pills by oral gavage at a dose of 13.6 mg/kg body weight once daily beginning on postoperative day 1. The formulation was freshly prepared immediately prior to administration as a homogeneous aqueous suspension using sterile 0.9% normal saline as the vehicle to ensure dosing consistency. Treatment was administered in three consecutive 7-day cycles for a total duration of 21 days. Rats in the Sham, CCI, and Acu groups received an equivalent volume of vehicle (0.9% normal saline) by oral gavage using identical handling and administration procedures to control for gavage-associated stress responses.

4. Establishment of the CCI model

The CCI model was established according to the method described by Bennett and Xie. Under general anesthesia, the left sciatic nerve was surgically exposed and loosely ligated at four sites spaced approximately 1 mm apart using 4-0 chromic gut sutures. Successful establishment of the model was confirmed by the presence of mild ipsilateral paw twitching. Rats in the Sham group underwent identical surgical exposure without sciatic nerve ligation. Following surgery, all animals received a single intramuscular injection of gentamicin (10 mg/kg) for prophylaxis against postoperative infection.

5. Acupuncture and moxibustion intervention

The acupoints Huantiao (GB30), Weizhong (BL40), and Jiaji (EX-B2) were selected for therapeutic intervention. Sterile disposable stainless-steel acupuncture needles (0.25 mm × 13 mm) were inserted vertically to a depth of approximately 5 mm, followed by manual manipulation involving lifting, thrusting, and rotational stimulation to elicit the “De Qi” response. Needles were retained in situ for 30 minutes, with manual stimulation repeated at 5-minute intervals throughout the treatment session. Moxibustion was subsequently performed using ignited moxa sticks positioned approximately 5 cm above the skin surface to provide continuous thermal stimulation for 30 minutes. In the Acu group, acupuncture treatment preceded moxibustion and was administered once daily for 21 consecutive days.

6. Behavioral assessment of pain

Thermal withdrawal latency (TWL) and mechanical withdrawal threshold (MWT) were assessed preoperatively and on postoperative days 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, and 15. TWL was evaluated using a plantar thermal stimulation apparatus by recording the latency between placement of the hind paw on the heated surface and the occurrence of paw withdrawal, licking, or jumping responses. Each measurement was performed in triplicate, and the mean value was used for subsequent analysis. MWT was determined using calibrated von Frey filaments in conjunction with the Dixon up-down method. Positive and negative withdrawal responses were recorded to calculate the 50% paw withdrawal threshold [24, 25].

7. Hematoxylin and eosin staining

Following euthanasia, left sciatic nerve segments and dorsal horn tissues from the L4-L6 spinal cord were harvested, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm. Hematoxylin and eosin staining was subsequently performed to evaluate histopathological alterations in neural tissues.

8. Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA concentration and purity were quantified using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. Subsequently, 1 μg of total RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA using cDNA Synthesis SuperMix.

RT-qPCR amplification was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix on a real-time PCR platform. The amplification protocol consisted of an initial denaturation step followed by optimized thermal cycling conditions. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method with housekeeping genes serving as internal controls. Primer sequences used for target gene amplification are presented in Table 1. All reactions were performed in triplicate to ensure analytical reproducibility.

9. Immunofluorescence staining

Immunofluorescence staining was performed to assess NF-κB p65 expression in spinal cord tissues. Briefly, spinal cord and sciatic nerve sections were incubated overnight at 4℃ with a primary antibody against NF-κB p65 (Servicebio, Cat. No. GB11041, 1:1000 dilution, Wuhan, China). Following three washes with PBS, sections were incubated for 1 hour at room temperature with anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (Servicebio, Cat. No. GB21303, 1:200 dilution). After five additional PBS washes, nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Fluorescence signals were subsequently visualized and captured using a Nikon fluorescence microscope (Japan).

10. Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemical staining was conducted to evaluate NF-κB p65 expression in spinal cord tissue sections. Briefly, paraffin-embedded sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated through graded ethanol solutions. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using 3% hydrogen peroxide. Tissue sections were then incubated overnight at 4℃ with primary antibody against NF-κB p65 (Servicebio, Cat. No. GB11041, 1:200 dilution, Wuhan, China). Following PBS washes, sections were incubated for 1 hour at room temperature with secondary antibody (Servicebio, Cat. No. GB23303, 1:500 dilution). Immunoreactivity was visualized using DAB chromogenic substrate, followed by hematoxylin counterstaining. Stained sections were examined and imaged using a Nikon microscope (Japan).

11. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 9.5 (GraphPad Software, USA). Unless otherwise specified, data are presented as mean ± SD. Longitudinal behavioral outcomes repeatedly measured across multiple time points in the same animals, including TWL and MWT, were analyzed using two-way repeated-measures ANOVA, with time treated as the within-subject factor and experimental group as the between-subject factor, including assessment of the time × group interaction effect. Geisser–Greenhouse correction was applied when appropriate to address potential violations of the sphericity assumption. Following significant omnibus analyses, post hoc between-group comparisons at individual time points were performed using Sidak-adjusted multiple-comparisons testing. Histological and molecular endpoints obtained at the terminal time point were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by appropriate post hoc multiple-comparison procedures according to the analytical objective. Two-group comparisons were performed using a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t test when assumptions of approximate normality and homogeneity of variance were satisfied; otherwise, suitable nonparametric analyses were considered. All statistical tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

12. Sample size and sampling

Behavioral assessments were designated as the primary study endpoint. Given the inherent inter-individual variability associated with neuropathic pain–related behavioral outcomes and the requirement for repeated-measures and between-group statistical analyses, the behavioral sample size was established at n = 6 per group, consistent with sample sizes commonly employed in comparable preclinical neuropathic pain studies. Histological and molecular analyses were treated as supportive secondary endpoints; based on pilot feasibility considerations, tissue availability, and experimental cost, the sample size for these analyses was established at n = 3 per group. To reduce sampling bias and technical variability, histological evaluations were performed using randomized sampling across multiple tissue sections and microscopic fields, with quantitative assessments conducted under blinded conditions. Molecular analyses incorporated appropriate technical replicates to enhance analytical reliability and experimental reproducibility.

RESULTS

1. Acupuncture–moxibustion therapy significantly increases pain thresholds

Fig. 1A presents the overall experimental design and group allocation strategy. A classical chronic constriction injury model was established to induce sciatica-like neuropathic pain in rats, which were subsequently randomized into four groups: Sham (sham surgery), CCI (untreated model), active drug comparator (Drug), and acupuncture–moxibustion therapy (Acu). Therapeutic intervention was initiated on postoperative day 1 and administered once daily for 21 consecutive days. Acupuncture stimulation was applied at the GB30, BL40, and EX-B2 acupoints for 30 minutes in combination with mild moxibustion treatment. Pain-related behavioral assessments, including TWL and MWT, were performed at baseline and multiple postoperative time points. Following completion of the intervention protocol, histopathological and molecular analyses were conducted to comprehensively evaluate treatment-associated effects. As illustrated in Fig. 1B, C, the CCI group exhibited significant reductions in both TWL and MWT beginning on postoperative day 3, confirming successful establishment of the neuropathic pain model. In contrast, the Acu group demonstrated progressive recovery of pain thresholds beginning on postoperative day 7, with values approaching those observed in the Sham group by day 15. Moreover, the Acu group exhibited significantly greater analgesic improvement than both the untreated CCI and Drug groups, supporting the therapeutic efficacy of the combined acupuncture–moxibustion regimen.

2. Acupuncture–moxibustion therapy attenuates neural structural injury

Hematoxylin and eosin staining (Fig. 2A, B) demonstrated pronounced structural disruption of sciatic nerve fibers and marked myelin sheath degeneration in the CCI group. In addition, neurons within the spinal dorsal horn exhibited reduced cellular density, neuronal soma atrophy, and increased glial cell proliferation. In contrast, tissues obtained from the Acu group showed preservation of neural architecture, maintenance of neuronal morphology, and substantially reduced inflammatory cell infiltration.

3. Acupuncture–moxibustion therapy suppresses proinflammatory cytokine expression

Quantitative RT-qPCR analyses (Fig. 3A-D) demonstrated that mRNA expression levels of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α were significantly elevated in the CCI group compared with Sham controls. Acupuncture–moxibustion treatment markedly suppressed transcriptional expression of these proinflammatory cytokines and demonstrated greater anti-inflammatory effects than the active drug comparator. Consistent with these findings, ELISA analyses (Fig. 3E-H) revealed substantial reductions in serum concentrations of the corresponding inflammatory mediators in the Acu group.

Multiple-group comparisons were analyzed using one-way ANOVA, and *p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001. CCI, chronic constriction injury group; Sham, sham surgery group; Drug, active drug comparator group; Acu, acupuncture–moxibustion therapy group.

4. Acupuncture–moxibustion therapy reduces NF-κB p65 expression and nuclear translocation

Immunohistochemical analyses (Fig. 4A, B) revealed marked upregulation and increased nuclear localization of NF-κB p65 in neurons within the CCI group, whereas significantly reduced p65 expression was observed following acupuncture–moxibustion intervention. Immunofluorescence analyses (Fig. 4C, D) further demonstrated attenuation of NF-κB p65 nuclear translocation in the Acu group. Quantitative fluorescence intensity measurements additionally supported reduced nuclear accumulation of p65, consistent with suppression of NF-κB–associated inflammatory signaling.

DISCUSSION

The present study demonstrates that acupuncture–moxibustion therapy exerts significant analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects in a rat model of chronic constriction injury–induced neuropathic pain. Acupuncture–moxibustion intervention significantly increased thermal withdrawal latency and mechanical withdrawal threshold, preserved neural tissue architecture, and suppressed the expression of multiple proinflammatory cytokines. Mechanistically, these therapeutic effects were accompanied by reduced spinal NF-κB p65 expression and diminished nuclear translocation, findings consistent with attenuation of NF-κB–associated neuroinflammatory signaling. Nevertheless, further investigation of upstream regulatory pathways and downstream NF-κB transcriptional targets remains necessary to more comprehensively define the molecular mechanisms underlying these effects.

The acupoints selected in the present study (GB30, BL40, and EX-B2) were chosen on the basis of contemporary neuroanatomical meridian theory and prior clinical evidence [18, 26]. The use of a validated CCI-induced neuropathic pain model in combination with integrated behavioral, histopathological, and molecular analyses enhances the biological coherence and internal validity of the findings. Notably, the observed suppression of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α expression supports the hypothesis that acupuncture–moxibustion therapy modulates both peripheral inflammatory responses and central sensitization processes associated with neuropathic pain progression.

From a clinical perspective, sciatica remains a challenging disorder to manage and frequently necessitates long-term pharmacological treatment with agents such as opioids and anticonvulsants, both of which are associated with adverse effects including tolerance, dependence, and central nervous system complications [27]. As a widely utilized non-pharmacological intervention, acupuncture–moxibustion therapy may provide favorable tolerability and patient acceptability. The present findings offer mechanistic support for the therapeutic application of Acu in neuropathic pain and further contribute to integration of traditional therapeutic practices with contemporary evidence-based medicine [28]. Within the broader framework of multimodal analgesia and precision medicine, acupuncture–moxibustion therapy may represent a promising neuroimmune-modulatory strategy for chronic neuropathic pain management.

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. Histological and molecular analyses were performed using a relatively small sample size (n = 3/group), which may reduce sensitivity for detecting modest biological effects; future investigations incorporating larger endpoint-specific cohorts are warranted. In addition, involvement of the NF-κB pathway was inferred primarily from alterations in p65 expression and nuclear translocation. Inclusion of upstream signaling regulators and phosphorylation-dependent markers, including IKK/IκBα and phosphorylated p65, as well as downstream transcriptional targets, would further strengthen mechanistic interpretation [29]. The study design also did not include acupuncture-only or moxibustion-only intervention groups, thereby limiting the ability to distinguish the independent therapeutic contributions of each modality or to formally evaluate potential synergistic interactions within the combined Acu protocol. Finally, the active drug comparator consisted of a proprietary multi-component formulation and was therefore incorporated as a pragmatic clinical comparator rather than as a standardized reference intervention [30]. Collectively, the convergent behavioral, histopathological, and inflammatory findings support acupuncture–moxibustion therapy as a non-pharmacological intervention associated with attenuation of NF-κB–related inflammatory signaling in CCI-induced neuropathic pain.

CONCLUSION

In summary, the present study provides experimental evidence that acupuncture–moxibustion therapy effectively ameliorates CCI-induced sciatica and is associated with suppression of proinflammatory cytokine expression together with reduced NF-κB p65 expression and nuclear translocation, findings consistent with attenuation of NF-κB–associated inflammatory signaling. These results expand current mechanistic understanding of Traditional Chinese Medicine–based interventions and further support their potential integration into contemporary evidence-based therapeutic strategies for neuropathic pain management. Future studies incorporating acupuncture-only and moxibustion-only control groups in conjunction with factorial experimental designs are warranted to clarify the individual and interactive therapeutic contributions of each modality.

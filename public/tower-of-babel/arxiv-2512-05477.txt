Quantum geometry and X X -wave magnets with X = p , d , f , g , i X=p,d,f,g,i
Motohiko Ezawa

Abstract
Quantum geometry is a differential geometry based on quantum mechanics. It is related to various transport and optical properties in condensed matter physics. The Zeeman quantum geometry is a generalization of quantum geometry including the spin degrees of freedom. It is related to electromagnetic cross responses. Quantum geometry is generalized to non-Hermitian systems and density matrices. Especially, the latter is quantum information geometry, where the quantum Fisher information naturally arises as quantum metric. We apply these results to the X X -wave magnets, which include d d -wave, g g -wave and i i -wave altermagnets as well as p p -wave and f f -wave magnets. They have universal physics for anomalous Hall conductivity, tunneling magneto-resistance and planar Hall effect. We obtain various analytic formulas based on the two-band Hamiltonian.

II.1
Quantum distance and quantum geometric tensor

The fidelity of the wave function
\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle
is defined by

F_{n}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\equiv\left|\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle\right.\right|,

(1)

where the wave function is orthonormalized,
\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle\right.=\delta(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime})
. It
satisfies
0\leq F_{n}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\leq 1
.

The Hilbert-Schmidt distance is defined by

s_{\text{HS}}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\equiv\sqrt{1-F_{n}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)^{2}},

(2)

where
s_{\text{HS}}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)=0
when
the two wave functions are identical
\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle=\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle
, while
s_{\text{HS}}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)=1
when they are orthogonal
\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\right)\right\rangle\right.=0
.

The quantum geometric tensor
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}
is defined as
follows. We start with the quantum distance
ds_{\text{HS}}
for the
infinitesimal translation
d\mathbf{k}
of the momentum as
Provost
;
Berry
;

ds_{\text{HS}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\equiv\sqrt{1-F_{n}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}+d\mathbf{k}\right)^{2}},

(3)

with

F_{n}\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}+d\mathbf{k}\right)=|\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|U_{d\mathbf{k}}|\psi_{n}(\mathbf{k})\rangle|,

(4)

where

U_{d\mathbf{k}}\equiv e^{-id\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}

(5)

is the generator of the infinitesimal momentum translation
d\mathbf{k}
and

r_{\mu}\mathbf{\equiv}i\partial/\partial_{k_{\mu}}
is the position
operator. The quantum distance for the infinitesimal momentum is expanded in
terms of the quantum geometric tensor
\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}
as

where

\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\left(1-\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle,

The quantum metric is the real part of the quantum geometric tensor,

g_{n}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\text{Re}\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)+\left(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)^{\ast}}{2},

II.6
Non-Abelian quantum geometry

So far, we have studied the case where the target band is a single band. If
the target bands are
N
-fold degenerate, we consider the
N
-fold
degenerate wave function,

\left|\psi\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle,

(51)

satisfying the normalization condition,
\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|c_{n}\right|^{2}=1
. It is necessary to generalize quantum geometry to
the non-Abelian quantum geometry
Ma
. The fidelity of the wave
functions
\left|\psi\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle
and
\left|\psi\left(\mathbf{k}+d\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle
is
defined by

F\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}+d\mathbf{k}\right)\equiv\left|\left\langle\psi\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|U_{d\mathbf{k}}\left|\psi\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right|

(52)

with
U_{d\mathbf{k}}
given by Eq.(
5
). The quantum distance is

ds_{\text{HS}}\equiv\sqrt{1-F\left(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}+d\mathbf{k}\right)^{2}}

(53)

The non-Abelian quantum geometric tensor is given by

\left(ds_{\text{HS}}\right)^{2}\equiv\sum_{\mu\nu}\sum_{nm}\mathcal{F}_{nm}^{\mu\nu}dk_{\mu}dk_{\nu}

(54)

with

\mathcal{F}_{nm}^{\mu\nu}=\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\left(1-P\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

(55)

where
\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
is the projection operator onto
the target bands,

\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\equiv\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|,

(56)

which satisfies the idempotence relation
\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)^{2}=\mathcal{P}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
. The derivation is shown
in Appendix
B
.

The quantum metric is the real part of the quantum geometric tensor,

g_{nm}^{\mu\nu}\equiv\text{Re}\mathcal{F}_{nm}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{mn}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)+\left(\mathcal{F}_{mn}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)^{\ast}}{2},

(57)

while the Berry curvature is the imaginary part of the quantum geometric
tensor,

\Omega_{nm}^{\mu\nu}\equiv 2\text{Im}\mathcal{F}_{nm}^{\mu\nu}=i\left(\mathcal{F}_{mn}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\left(\mathcal{F}_{mn}^{\mu\nu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)^{\ast}\right).

(58)

It is further calculated as

\displaystyle\Omega_{nm}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|1-P\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle-i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|1-P\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.-i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle-i\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,m}\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\psi_{n^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.\left\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle+i\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,m}\left\langle\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\psi_{n^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.\left\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.-i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle+\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,m}ia_{n^{\prime}n}^{\mu\ast}a_{n^{\prime}m}^{\nu}-ia_{n^{\prime}m}^{\nu\ast}a_{n^{\prime}n}^{\mu}.

(59)

With the use of the Hermiticity condition of the Berry connection (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\Omega_{nm}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle-i\left\langle\partial_{k_{\nu}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle-\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,m}ia_{nn^{\prime}}^{\mu}a_{n^{\prime}m}^{\nu}+\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,m}ia_{mn^{\prime}}^{\nu}a_{n^{\prime}n}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\times a_{nm}^{\mu}-\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n,m}i\left[a_{nn^{\prime}}^{\mu},a_{n^{\prime}m}^{\nu}\right].

(60)

There is an additional term
-i\left[a_{nn^{\prime}}^{\mu},a_{n^{\prime}m}^{\nu}\right]
in the Wilczek-Zee connection comparing with the Berry
connection.

III
Quantum geometry in condensed matter physics

III.1
Thouless-Kohmoto-Nightingale-Nijs formula

The Berry curvature is related to the Hall conductivity. Especially, the
Hall conductivity is quantized in an insulator and is proportional to the
Chern number, which is known as the TKNN formula
TKNN
.

According to the Kubo formula, the Hall conductance is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}=-i\hbar e^{2}

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{k}\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{f\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)}{\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\varepsilon_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times[\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|v_{x}\left|\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|v_{y}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|v_{y}\left|\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|v_{x}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle],

(61)

where
f\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)=1/\left(\exp\left[\left(\varepsilon_{n}(\mathbf{k})-\mu\right)/\left(k_{\text{B}}T\right)+1\right]\right)
is the Fermi distribution function and
\mu

is the chemical potential. By using the Hellmann-Feynman theorem

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|v_{\mu}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\partial_{k_{\mu}}\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\varepsilon_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle

(62)

for the states subject to the orthonormalization condition,
\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle=\delta_{mn}
, the Hall conductance is rewritten as

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}=

\displaystyle-\frac{ie^{2}}{\hbar}\int d\mathbf{k}\sum_{n,m}f\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\times[\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle].

(63)

It should be noticed that the sum can be extended to include the states with

n=m
in this formula, since

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle.

(64)

The proof of the Hellmann-Feynman theorem is shown in Appendix
C
.

Making the use of the relation

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle+\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{x}}\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle=0,

(65)

the Hall conductance is rewritten as

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}=

\displaystyle-\frac{ie^{2}}{\hbar}\int d\mathbf{k}\sum_{n,m}f\left(\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\times[\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle].

(66)

Using the completeness condition
\sum_{m}|\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|=1
, we
obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}=

\displaystyle\frac{ie^{2}}{\hbar}\int d\mathbf{k}\sum_{n}f\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)[\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle

\displaystyle-\left\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle].

(67)

It is rewritten as

\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi h}\sum_{n}\int d\mathbf{k}\,f\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)\Omega_{n}^{xy}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),

(68)

where

\Omega_{n}^{xy}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=i\left[\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle-\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)|\partial_{k_{x}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle\right].

(69)

The conductance is the sum of the contributions from various bands indexed
by
n
. It is convenient to define a "gauge potential" in the momentum space
for each band index
n
by

a_{n}^{\mu}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=-i\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|\partial_{k_{\mu}}\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\rangle,

(70)

which is the Berry connection. We may rewrite (
69
) as

\Omega_{n}^{xy}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\partial_{k_{x}}a_{n}^{y}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\partial_{k_{y}}a_{n}^{x}\left(\mathbf{k}\right).

(71)

This is the "magnetic field", which is the Berry curvature.

We consider the zero-temperature limit, where the Fermi distribution
function becomes a step function,
f\left(\varepsilon_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right)=\theta\left(\mu-\varepsilon_{n}\right)
. The
conductance (
68
) is simplified as

\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{h}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mathcal{C}_{n},

(72)

becuase the integration is taken below the Fermi energy.

IV.3
Rashba system

As an exmple of the two-band system, we consider the Rashba system described
by

(159)

where
\lambda
is the strength of the Rashba interaction and
B
is static
magnetic field applied perpendicularly to the plane.

The energy is given by

E_{\chi}=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+\chi\sqrt{\lambda^{2}k^{2}+B^{2}}

(160)

for the lower band with
\chi=-1
and for the upper band with
\chi=1

\eta=-1
describes the inner Fermi surface and
\eta=1

\Omega^{xy}=-\frac{B\lambda^{2}}{2\left(\lambda^{2}k^{2}+B^{2}\right)^{3/2}},

(162)

while the quantum metrices are given by

\displaystyle g_{+-}^{xx}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}k_{y}^{2}+B^{2}\right)}{2\left(\lambda^{2}k^{2}+B^{2}\right)^{2}},

(163)

\displaystyle g_{+-}^{xy}=

\displaystyle g_{+-}^{yx}=\frac{-\lambda^{4}k_{x}k_{y}}{2\left(\lambda^{2}k^{2}+B^{2}\right)^{2}},

(164)

\displaystyle g_{+-}^{yy}=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}\left(\lambda^{2}k_{x}^{2}+B^{2}\right)}{2\left(\lambda^{2}k^{2}+B^{2}\right)^{2}}.

(165)

There is no contribution from the quantum metric

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{+-}^{yz}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\mu}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{Q}}^{\mu}\equiv

\displaystyle 2i\sum_{n\neq m}\frac{p_{m}-p_{n}}{p_{m}+p_{n}}a_{nm}^{\mu}\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{m}\right|.

we have

. Then, we obtain the classical Fisher information based
on the classical part of the SLD as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\rho\left\{\mathcal{L}_{\text{C}}^{\mu},\mathcal{L}_{\text{C}}^{\nu}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}p_{n}\left|\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\times(\sum_{n^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{n^{\prime}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\left|\psi_{n^{\prime}}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}}\right|\sum_{n^{\prime\prime}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{n^{\prime\prime}}}{\partial k_{\nu}}\left|\psi_{n^{\prime\prime}}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n^{\prime\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle+\sum_{n^{\prime\prime}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{n^{\prime\prime}}}{\partial k_{\nu}}\left|\psi_{n^{\prime\prime}}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n^{\prime\prime}}\right|\sum_{n^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{n^{\prime}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\left|\psi_{n^{\prime}}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}}\right|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,n^{\prime},n^{\prime\prime}=1}^{N}p_{n}\frac{\partial\ln p_{n^{\prime}}}{\partial k_{\mu}}\frac{\partial\ln p_{n^{\prime\prime}}}{\partial k_{\nu}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\delta_{n^{\prime}n^{\prime\prime}}

(274)

On the other hand, the classical part of the mean Uhlmann curvature is zero,

\mathcal{\bar{U}}^{\mu\nu}\equiv-\frac{i}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\rho\left[\mathcal{L}_{\text{C}}^{\mu},\mathcal{L}_{\text{C}}^{\nu}\right]\right]=0.

VI.3
Quantum Cramér-Rao inequality

There is an inequality known as the Quantum Cramér-Rao inequality

;

;

\mathcal{F}_{\text{CFisher}}\leq\mathcal{F}_{\text{QFisher}},

(276)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{CFisher}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\nu}a_{\mu}\mathcal{F}_{\text{CFisher}}^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu},

(277)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{QFisher}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\nu}a_{\mu}\mathcal{F}_{\text{QFisher}}^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}

(278)

for an arbitrary set of parameters
a_{\mu}
. The proof is shown in
Appendix.
I.2
.

On the other hand, the classical Cramér-Rao inequality states that the
covariance is bounded by the inverse of the classical Fisher information
matrix,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\nu}a_{\mu}p_{n}\left(k_{\mu}-\left\langle k_{\mu}\right\rangle\right)\left(k_{\nu}-\left\langle k_{\nu}\right\rangle\right)a_{\nu}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mu\nu}a_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{F}_{\text{CFisher}}^{-1}\right)^{\mu\nu}a_{\nu}.

(279)

Combining the quantum and classical Cramér-Rao inequalities, the lower

VI.4
Quantum Fisher information for a pure state and quantum metric

By inserting Eq.(
260
) to Eq.(
268
), we obtain

(280)

which leads to

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\mathcal{L}^{\mu},\rho\right\}.

(281)

We show that the quantum Fisher information reproduces the quantum metric
when the density matrix
\rho
describes a pure state
\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle
. By using the relation
\rho^{2}=\rho
, we have

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}}=\frac{\partial\rho^{2}}{\partial k_{\mu}}=\rho\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}}+\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}}\rho=\left\{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}},\rho\right\}.

(282)

Comparing it with Eq.(
281
), we have

\mathcal{L}^{\mu}=2\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}}.

(283)

Then, the quantum Fisher information is rewritten as

\mathcal{F}_{\text{QFisher}}^{\mu\nu}=2\text{Tr}\left[\rho\left\{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}},\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\nu}}\right\}\right]=2\left\langle\left\{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\mu}},\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial k_{\nu}}\right\}\right\rangle.

(284)

By inserting

\rho=\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|,

(285)

the quantum Fisher information is reduced to the quantum metric

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\text{QFisher}}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle 2\text{Tr}\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\mu}}\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\nu}}-\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\mu}}\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\nu}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\nu}}\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\mu}}-\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\nu}}\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\nu\mu}\right)=4g_{n}^{\mu\nu}.

(286)

while the mean Uhlmann curvature is reduced to the Berry curvature

\displaystyle\mathcal{\bar{U}}^{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle-2i\text{Tr}\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\mu}}\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\nu}}-\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\mu}}\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\nu}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\nu}}\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\mu}}+\frac{\partial\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|}{\partial k_{\nu}}\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle\psi_{n}\right|\frac{\partial\left|\psi_{n}\right\rangle}{\partial k_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\left(\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{F}_{n}^{\nu\mu}\right)=i4\Omega_{n}^{\mu\nu}.

(287)

Then, the quantum Fisher information is reduced to the quantum metric, while
the mean Uhlmann curvature is reduced to the Berry curvature. Thus, the
Uhlmann quantum geometry is a generalization of quantum geometry. The

) is shown in Appendix.
I.3

VII.3.1
Spin diagonal case

We consider the Hamiltonian (

The energy is given by

Time-reversal symmetry is defined by

T\varepsilon_{s}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)T^{-1}=\varepsilon_{-s}\left(-\mathbf{k}\right).

(326)

Inversion symmetry is defined by

P\varepsilon_{s}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)P^{-1}=\varepsilon_{s}\left(-\mathbf{k}\right).

(327)

When there are both symmetries, we have

PT\varepsilon_{s}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\left(PT\right)^{-1}=\varepsilon_{-s}\left(\mathbf{k}\right).

(328)

Hence, the band with up and down spins are degenerate perfectly. In this
sense, the breaking of time-reversal symmetry or inversion symmetry is
necessary for spin-splitting band structure. In the
d
-wave, the
g
-wave
and the

-wave altermagnets, time-reversal symmetry is broken but
inversion symmetry is preserved. On the other hand, in the
p
-wave and the
f
-wave magnets, inversion symmetry is broken but time-reversal symmetry is
preserved. Hence, the spin splitting occurs in all
X
-wave magnets.

In the presence of the Rashba interaction, the system is not diagonal with

VIII.1.2
Spin Nernst effects

The spin Nernst effect is an effect that spin current is generated
perpendicularly to the thermal gradient. It is discussed in the
d
-wave
altermagnet
Naka
. So far, there is no analytic result on it, which we
derive.

Tts momentum derivative reads

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{k}}=\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\varepsilon},

(375)

while its spatial derivative reads

\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{r}}=\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial T}.

(376)

We rewrite the Boltzmann equation as

\displaystyle\frac{df}{dt}=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{k}}{\partial t}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{k}}+\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial t}\cdot\frac{\partial f}{\partial\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{k}}{\partial t}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\varepsilon}\right)+\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial t}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial T}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{f-f^{\left(0\right)}}{\tau}.

(377)

We use the kinetic equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}},

(378)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{k}}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle 0,

(379)

and obtain

\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial T}=-\frac{f-f^{\left(0\right)}}{\tau}.

(380)

The first order expansion reads

\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\right)\frac{\partial f^{\left(0\right)}}{\partial T}=-\frac{f^{\left(1\right)}}{\tau},

(381)

whose solution is given by

f^{\left(1\right)}=-\frac{\tau}{\hbar}\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\right)\frac{\partial f^{\left(0\right)}}{\partial T}.

(382)

The current driven by temperature gradient is given by

\displaystyle j_{b}=

\displaystyle-e\int d\mathbf{k}f^{\left(1\right)}v_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e\tau}{\hbar^{2}}\int d\mathbf{k}\left(\frac{\partial\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{k}}\cdot\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial\mathbf{r}}\right)\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial k_{b}}\frac{\partial f^{\left(0\right)}}{\partial T}.

(383)

Especially, current
j_{x}
and
j_{y}
flow when the temperature gradient
is along the
x
axis,

j_{x}=e\tau\int d\mathbf{k}\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial k_{x}}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial x}\frac{\partial f^{\left(0\right)}}{\partial T},

(384)

which is known as the Seebeck effect, and

\displaystyle j_{y}=

\displaystyle-e\int d\mathbf{k}f^{\left(1\right)}v_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e\tau}{\hbar^{2}}\int d\mathbf{k}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial k_{x}}\cdot\frac{\partial T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}{\partial x}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial k_{y}}\frac{\partial f^{\left(0\right)}}{\partial T},

(385)

which is known as the Nernst effect. We assume that the temperature gradient
is linear,

T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=ax.

(386)

When
\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\mu\gg k_{\text{B}}T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)
, we approximate the Fermi distribution by the Boltzmann
distribution,

f=\frac{1}{\exp\left(\frac{\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\mu}{k_{\text{B}}T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\right)+1}\simeq\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)-\mu}{k_{\text{B}}T\left(\mathbf{r}\right)}\right).

(387)

Then, the spin Hall conductivity is obtained as

j_{s}=\frac{e\tau}{\hbar^{2}}\frac{8e^{\mu/k_{\text{B}}T}Jm\pi\left(2T-\mu\right)}{\sqrt{1-4J^{2}m^{2}}}

(388)

for the
d
-wave altermagnet. It is proportional to the coupling constant
J

of the
X
-wave magnet in the Hamiltonian (
324
). Hence, it is
possible to detect the sign of
J
by the spin Nernst effect. On the other
hand, there is no spin current generation for the other
X
-wave magnets.

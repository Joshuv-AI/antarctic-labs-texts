Uniqueness results for positive harmonic functions on manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and strictly convex boundary
Xiaohan Cai

Abstract
. We prove some Liouville-type theorems for positive harmonic functions on compact Riemannian manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature and strictly convex boundary, thereby confirming some cases of Wang’s conjecture (J. Geom. Anal. 31 , 2021). We further investigate Wang’s conjecture on warped product manifolds and provide a partial verification of this conjecture, which also yields an alternative proof of Gu-Li’s resolution of the conjecture in the 𝔹 n \mathbb{B}^{n} case (Math. Ann. 391 , 2025). Our approach is based on a general principle of employing the P-function method to such Liouville-type results, with particular emphasis on the role of a closed conformal vector field inherent to such manifolds.

Introduction

Motivated by Bidaut-Véron and Véron’s rigidity work
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
(see also
[
10
]
), Wang proposed a conjecture for a Liouville-type result for harmonic functions with some nonlinear boundary condition
[
20
, Conjecture 1]
.

Conjecture 1

(Wang,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)\ (n\geq 3)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0
and the second fundamental form
\Pi\geq 1
on
\partial M
. If
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
is a positive solution of the following equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }M^{n},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\lambda u=u^{q}&\text{on }\partial M^{n},\end{cases}

where
1<q\leq\frac{n}{n-2}
and
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{q-1}
are constants. Then either

is a constant function, or
q=\frac{n}{n-2},\,\lambda=\frac{n-2}{2}
,

is isometric to the unit ball

and

corresponds to

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x)=\left(\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|a|^{2}|x|^{2}-2\langle x,a\rangle+1}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}},

where
a\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

This conjecture, if proved to be true, has several interesting geometric consequences, such as a sharp Sobolev trace inequality, a sharp upper bound of the area of the boundary, and a sharp lower bound of the Steklov eigenvalue on such manifolds. Readers are invited to
[
20
, Section 2]
and also
[
8
, Section 5]

Inspired by Xia-Xiong’s work on the Steklov eigenvalue estimate
[

[

Theorem 1

(Guo-Hang-Wang,

Let

,

We also mention that the two dimensional analogue of

[
19

In the first part of this paper, we shall partially confirm

Theorem 2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)\ (n\geq 3)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with
\mathrm{Ric}\geq 0

(1)

(2)

and
0<\lambda\leq\min\{\frac{1}{2(q-1)},\frac{6q+1}{2q+1}\frac{n-1}{2q}\}

Remark 1.1

Let us compare the ranges of

•

Condition (2) in

•

•

In view of the range of

If

Gu-Li’s method is based on sophisticated integration by parts with several delicately chosen parameters.

As a further step upon the resolution of Guo-Wang’s

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)=([0,R]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},dr^{2}+\rho(r)^{2}dS_{n-1}^{2})
be a smooth warped product manifold (i.e.
\rho(0)=0,\rho^{\prime}(0)=1,\rho^{(\text{even})}=0
). Assume
Ric_{g}\geq 0
and the second fundamental form
\Pi\geq 1
(i.e.
\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R)}{\rho(R)}\geq 1
). Assume, in addition, that
\rho^{\prime}(R)>\frac{n-2}{n-1}
.

Consider
1<q\leq\frac{2(n-1)\rho^{\prime}(R)-(n-2)}{n-2}
and
0<\lambda\leq\frac{1}{q-1}C(n,q,\rho^{\prime}(R))
, where
C(n,q,\rho^{\prime}(R))\in(0,1]
is a constant depending on
n,q,\rho^{\prime}(R)
(see
eq.

3.27
for its explicit definition).
Then the positive solution
u
to the equation
eq.

1.1
must be constant, unless
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
q=\frac{n}{n-2}
,
\lambda=\frac{n-2}{2}
and u corresponds to the model solution
eq.

1.2
.

Remark 1.2
.

One could see that
C(n,q,1)\equiv 1,\forall n\geq 3,1<q\leq\frac{n}{n-2}
, so

4
recovers
theorem

3
by taking
(M^{n},g)
to be the model space
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(i.e.
\rho(r)=r,\forall r\in[0,1]
).

Remark 1.3
.

theorem

4
applies to a wide range of warped product manifolds. For instance,
consider the warping factor
\rho(r)=r-cr^{3},\ r\in[0,R]
, where

is a constant. It’s easy to see that if

\displaystyle 1-R\geq cR^{2}(3-R),\quad\text{and}\quad cR^{2}<\frac{1}{3(n-1)},

then the hypotheses in
theorem

Our approach to

is grounded in a general principle for applying the P-function method to classify solutions of such semilinear elliptic equations, thereby building upon and extending the idea presented in
[

]

The basic idea is to start with the critical power case (i.e.

) and study the model solution

1.2
and come up with an appropriate function, known as the P-function in literature (in honor of L. Payne), whose constancy implies the rigidity of the solution

. For the subcritical power case (i.e.
1<q<\frac{n}{n-2}
), we regard the equation
eq.

1.1
as the critical one in a
d-
dimensional space (see
eq.

3.3
), then a modified argument as in the critical power case would imply the conclusion. Readers interested in the P-function method could refer to
[
17
,
22
,
4
,
3
]
for more research in this realm.

Specific to
theorem

4
, we start with a divergence-type equation of the P-function (see
eq.

3.9
) to obtain a key integral identity. A new weight function (see
eq.

) is then employed to handle the challenging boundary terms and derive the rigidity of the solution

.
Our approach highlights the role of this weight function as offering a closed conformal vector field (see

and

It’s worth mentioning that our arguments also yield an alternative proof of
theorem

3
and elucidate the choice of parameters in their proof from the viewpoint of this P-function method.

Note that for a warped product manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature, its radial-direction sectional curvature is also nonnegative, from which the arguments of Guo-Hang-Wang
[
8
]
would follow and yield a
sharp
range of
\lambda
for
3\leq n\leq 8
. In contrast, while potentially sacrificing sharpness in
\lambda
, we obtain a valid range for
all
dimensions

. We shall discuss this difference in detail after the proof of
theorem

4
.

Finally, we notice that if we confine ourselves to classifying the minimizer of the Sobolev trace inequality on warped product manifolds, then a larger range of
\lambda

Theorem 5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)=([0,R]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1},dr^{2}+\rho(r)^{2}dS_{n-1}^{2})
be a smooth warped product manifold (i.e.
\rho(0)=0,\rho^{\prime}(0)=1,\rho^{(\text{even})}=0
). Assume
Ric_{g}\geq 0
and the second fundamental form
\Pi\geq 1
(i.e.
\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R)}{\rho(R)}\geq 1
). Assume, in addition, that
\rho^{\prime}(R)>\frac{n-2}{n-1}
.

Consider
1<q\leq\frac{2(n-1)\rho^{\prime}(R)-(n-2)}{n-2}
and
0<\lambda\leq\min\{\frac{n-1}{q+1}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R)}{\rho(R)},\frac{1}{q-1}\frac{\rho(R)}{\rho^{\prime}(R)}\}

\displaystyle Q_{q,\lambda}(u):=\frac{\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}+\lambda\int_{\partial M}u^{2}}{(\int_{\partial M}u^{q+1})^{\frac{2}{q+1}}},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}(M)\setminus C_{0}^{\infty}(M).

Then
u_{0}
is constant,
unless
(M^{n},g)
is isometric to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
,

q=\frac{n}{n-2},\lambda=\frac{n-2}{2}
,
and
u_{0}
is given by the model solution
eq.

1.2

up to multiplying by a constant.

The idea toward

[

) of the Sobolev trace inequality on

.
As in

Finally, in

The proof of

Our proof exploits two main ingredients in the method of integration by parts: Bochner formula (Step 1) and the equation itself (Step 2).

. Therefore

.

Step 5:
Now we verify the condition (2) in

3.
Wang’s conjecture on warped product manifolds

In this section, we investigate
1
on warped product manifolds and prove
theorem

4
.

We shall first establish some identities that hold on general Riemannian manifolds.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold. For any
v\in C^{\infty}(M)
and constant
d>0
, there holds

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla_{\nabla v}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla v\right)=\left|\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{n}g\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{d}\right)(\Delta v)^{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)\langle\nabla\Delta v,\nabla v\rangle+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla v,\nabla v).

Proof.

This is a straightforward corollary of the Bochner formula.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold. Assume in addition that
(M^{n},g)
admits a smooth function
w
such that
\nabla w
is a closed conformal vector filed on
M
, i.e.
\nabla^{2}w=\frac{\Delta w}{n}g
, then for any
v\in C^{\infty}(M)
and constant
d>0
there holds

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(\nabla_{\nabla w}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla w\right)=\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{d}\right)\Delta v\Delta w-\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\langle\nabla\Delta w,\nabla v\rangle+\left(1-\frac{1}{d}\right)\langle\nabla\Delta v,\nabla w\rangle.

Proof.

Notice that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\nabla_{\nabla w}\nabla v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\nabla^{2}v,\mathcal{L}_{\nabla w}g\rangle+\langle\nabla\Delta v,\nabla w\rangle+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\Delta v\Delta w+\langle\nabla\Delta v,\nabla w\rangle+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla w),

where we used Ricci’s identity

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\nabla^{2}v)=\nabla\Delta v+\mathrm{Ric}(\nabla v,\cdot).

The hypothesis on
w
implies that the curvature operator
R
satisfies

\displaystyle R(X,Y)\nabla w=\left\langle X,\frac{\nabla\Delta w}{n}\right\rangle Y-\left\langle Y,\frac{\nabla\Delta w}{n}\right\rangle X,\quad\forall X,Y\in T_{p}(M).

It follows that

\displaystyle Ric(\nabla w,\cdot)=-\frac{n-1}{n}\nabla\Delta w.

Therefore, we derive

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\nabla_{\nabla w}\nabla v)=\frac{1}{n}\Delta v\Delta w+\langle\nabla\Delta v,\nabla w\rangle-\frac{n-1}{n}\langle\nabla\Delta w,\nabla v\rangle.

Hence the desired identity follows.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Manifolds endowed with a closed conformal vector field could be characterized as certain warped product manifolds. See for example
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Now we are ready to prove
theorem

4
.
Roughly speaking, the starting observation is that Escobar’s argument in the critical power case
[
6
]
could be interpreted in the framework of the P-function method. Then we regard the equation
eq.

1.1
as the critical power case in a
d-
dimensional space, where

(3.3)

\displaystyle d:=\frac{2q}{q-1}\geq n.

Then we modify Escobar’s argument
[
6
]
to fit in this ”critical case”, and use the boundary condition to tackle the emerging terms in this case. We shall see that the choice of the parameters is natural from the viewpoint of this P-function strategy. After finishing our proof, we shall review and compare our choice of the parameters with those of Gu-Li
[
7
]
.

Proof of
theorem

4
:

Step 0:
First, we derive some properties of the warping factor
\rho
that will be used throughout our proof.

The curvature hypothesis
Ric_{g}\geq 0
implies

-(n-1)\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}\geq 0
.
Combining with
\rho^{\prime}(0)=1
, we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(R)\leq\rho^{\prime}(r)\leq 1,\quad\forall r\in[0,R].

Moreover, the Ricci curvature hypothesis also implies

\displaystyle(\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho})^{\prime}=\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}}{\rho}-(\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho})^{2}\leq-(\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho})^{2},\quad

Combining with
\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(r)}{\rho(r)}=+\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{\prime}(r)}{\rho(r)}\leq\frac{1}{r},\quad\forall r\in(0,R].

Then the boundary curvature hypothesis implies that

(3.5)

\displaystyle R\leq 1.

Finally, we have
\rho^{\prime}(r)\geq\rho^{\prime}(R)>0
, so

(3.6)

\displaystyle\rho(r)\leq\rho(R),\quad\forall r\in[0,R].

Step 1:
Consider the intrinsic dimension
d:=\frac{2q}{q-1}
so that
q=\frac{d}{d-2}
. Then

\displaystyle d\geq n\Leftrightarrow q\leq\frac{n}{n-2}.

Denote
\nu
to be the unit outer normal vector of
\partial M
. Let
v:=u^{-\frac{2}{d-2}}
,
\chi:=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu},f:=v|_{\partial M}
, then
v
satisfies

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta v=\frac{d}{2}v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}&\mathrm{in}\ M,\\
\chi=\frac{2}{d-2}(\lambda f-1)&\mathrm{on}\ \partial M.\end{cases}

Consider the P-function
P:=v^{-1}|\nabla v|^{2}=\frac{2}{d}\Delta v
, which satisfies
P\equiv constant
if
u
is the model solution
eq.

1.2
. We shall derive a divergence-type equation of
P
(i.e.
eq.

3.9
).

It’s straightforward to see from
eq.

3.7
that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{2}\nabla P=\nabla\Delta v=dv^{-1}\left(\nabla_{\nabla v}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla v\right).

Applying the Bochner formula to
vP=|\nabla v|^{2}
, it’s straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v\Delta P+\frac{1}{2}P\Delta v+\langle\nabla P,\nabla v\rangle=\left|\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{n}g\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}(\Delta v)^{2}+\langle\nabla\Delta v,\nabla v\rangle+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v).

Now, using
\Delta v=\frac{d}{2}P
and rearranging the above equation, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}v\Delta P+\frac{2-d}{2}\langle\nabla P,\nabla v\rangle=\left|\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{n}g\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{d}\right)(\Delta v)^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v).

Multiply both sides by
v^{1-d}
to get the following divergence-type equation:

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}div(v^{2-d}\nabla P)=v^{1-d}\left(\left|\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{n}g\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{d}\right)(\Delta v)^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right).

Now use
eq.

3.8
to substitute
\nabla P
in
eq.

3.9
and get

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(v^{1-d}\left(\nabla_{\nabla v}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla v\right)\right)=v^{1-d}\left(\left|\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{n}g\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{d}\right)(\Delta v)^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right).

Integrating this equation over
M
and using the boundary value condition

3.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{1-d}\left(\left|\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{n}g\right|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{d}\right)(\Delta v)^{2}+Ric(\nabla v,\nabla v)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}f^{1-d}\left(\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}g\right)(\nabla v,\nu)=\int_{\partial M}f^{1-d}\left(\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}g\right)(\nabla f+\chi\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}f^{1-d}\left(\langle\nabla f,\nabla\chi\rangle-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R)}{\rho(R)}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)+\int_{\partial M}\chi f^{1-d}\left(\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}g\right)(\nu,\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{d-2}\lambda-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R)}{\rho(R)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}f^{1-d}|\nabla f|^{2}+\frac{2}{d-2}\lambda\int_{\partial M}f^{2-d}\left(\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}g\right)(\nu,\nu)

(3.11)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{d-2}\int_{\partial M}f^{1-d}\left(\nabla^{2}v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}g\right)(\nu,\nu),

where in the penultimate equality, we used
\langle\nabla f,\nabla_{\nabla f}\nu\rangle=\frac{\rho^{\prime}(R)}{\rho(R)}|\nabla f|^{2}
.

Step 2:
Consider the weight function

\displaystyle w(x):=\int_{r(x)}^{R}\rho(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Then

.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{d}-1\right)\rho^{\prime}\Delta v+(n-1)\rho^{\prime\prime}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}+(d-1)v^{-1}\left\langle\nabla_{\nabla w}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla w,\nabla v\right\rangle.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\left(v^{2-d}\left(\nabla_{\nabla w}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla w\right)\right)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{d}-1\right)\rho^{\prime}v^{2-d}\Delta v+(n-1)\rho^{\prime\prime}v^{2-d}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}+v^{1-d}\left\langle\nabla_{\nabla v}\nabla v-\frac{\Delta v}{d}\nabla v,\nabla w\right\rangle.

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{d-2}\lambda\frac{1}{\rho(R)}\left(1-\frac{n}{d}\right)\int_{M}\rho^{\prime}v^{2-d}\Delta v-\frac{2}{d-2}\lambda\frac{1}{\rho(R)}(n-1)\int_{M}\rho^{\prime\prime}v^{2-d}\frac{\partial v}{\partial r}

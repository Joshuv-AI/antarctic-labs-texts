Balanced 1-Factorisations of 3- and 4-Regular Circulant Graphs
Jeremy Mitchell

Abstract
We investigate 1-factorisations in which the 2-regular graphs that occur as the union of a pair of 1-factors appear an equal number of times across the unions of all pairs of 1-factors in the 1-factorisation. We call such 1-factorisations balanced 1-factorisations (B1Fs) and we present some results on B1Fs of 3- and 4-regular circulant graphs.



1
Introduction

A 1-regular spanning subgraph of a graph is known as a
1-factor
. A partition of the edge set of a graph
G
into 1-factors is called a
1-factorisation
of
G
.
A natural question is: under what conditions does a 1-factorisation of the complete graph on
n
vertices,
K_{n}
, exist? Clearly
n
must be even, and one of the earliest proofs that this condition is sufficient is Kirkman’s 1847 construction of 1-factorisations of
K_{n}
for all even integers
n\geq 2

[
9
]
.

Given a 1-factorisation of a graph
G
, a well-studied problem is to ask if the 1-factorisation has the property that the union of each pair of 1-factors is isomorphic to the same subgraph
H
of
G
. Such a 1-factorisation is called a
uniform 1-factorisation
(U1F) of
G
and the subgraph
H
is called the
common graph
. Furthermore, a uniform 1-factorisation in which the common graph is a Hamilton cycle is called a
perfect 1-factorisation
(P1F). The following famous conjecture is due to Kotzig
[
11
]
.

Conjecture 1
.

[
11
]

For any
n\geq 2
,
K_{2n}
admits a perfect 1-factorisation.

Kotzig
[
10
]
provided an infinite family of 1-factorisations of the complete graph
K_{2n}
that are perfect when
2n-1
is an odd prime. Bryant, Maenhaut, and Wanless
[
3
]
constructed another infinite family of P1Fs of
K_{2n}
where
2n-1
is an odd prime, which is not isomorphic to the family given by Kotzig. Anderson
[
1
]
gave an infinite family of 1-factorisations of
K_{2n}
that are perfect when
n
is an odd prime. Besides these infinite families there are a number of sporadic values of
n
for which
K_{2n}
has been shown to admit a P1F. Most recently a P1F of
K_{56}
was found by Pike
[
13
]
, which leaves
K_{64}
as the smallest complete graph for which the existence of a P1F is unknown; for more information on the orders of complete graphs with known P1Fs, a paper on the number of non-isomorphic P1Fs of
K_{16}
by Gill and Wanless
[
5
]
is recommended.

For uniform 1-factorisations that are not perfect, the common graph will be a collection of two or more disjoint cycles of even lengths. We say that a U1F has
type

(c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{t})
if the common graph of the U1F is a collection of
t
cycles of lengths
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{t}
. For complete graphs
K_{2n}
with
2n\leq 16
, all types of U1Fs up to isomorphism are known due to a result by Meszka and Rosa
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2
.

[
12
]

If
\mathcal{F}
is a U1F of
K_{2n}
, where
2n\leq 16
, then
\mathcal{F}
is one of the following:

(a)

a P1F;

(b)

a U1F of
K_{8}
of type
(4,4)
;

(c)

a U1F of
K_{10}
of type
(4,6)
;

(d)

a U1F of
K_{12}
of type
(6,6)
;

(e)

a U1F of
K_{16}
of type
(4,4,4,4)
.

Further, the U1Fs from cases
b
,
c
,
d
,
e
are unique up to isomorphism.

In 2013, Herke and Maenhaut
[
8
]
began the study of P1Fs of circulant graphs. The
circulant graph

Circ(n,D)
where
n
is a positive integer and
D
is a subset of
\left\{1,2,\dots,\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}
is the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and where two vertices
v
and
u
are adjacent if and only if
|v-u|=d\pmod{n}
for some
d\in D
. A useful fact is that a circulant graph,
Circ(n,\left\{a,b\right\})
is connected if and only if
\gcd(n,a,b)=1
(see for example
[
2
]
).
Herke and Maenhaut
[
8
]
showed that, up to isomorphism, there are only two connected 3-regular circulant graphs of order
2n
, namely
Circ(2n,\left\{1,n\right\})
and
Circ(2n,\left\{2,n\right\})
. They also characterised the 3-regular circulant graphs that admit a P1F. Herke
[
6
]
also determined all connection sets of 3- and 4-regular circulant graphs on
2n
vertices that admit U1Fs for
n\leq 14
. She also showed that any connected circulant graph isomorphic to
Circ(2n,\left\{2,n\right\})
admits a U1F if and only if
n\equiv 3\pmod{6}
, and provided a number of constructions of U1Fs of 3- and 4-regular graphs
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3
.

[
8
]

Let
n\geq 2
be an even integer and let
a
be an integer in the range
1\leq a<n
. Then
Circ(2n,\left\{a,n\right\})
has a P1F if and only if one of the following cases holds true:

•

n=2
and
a=1
;

•

n=3
and
a=1
or
a=2
;

•

n>3
,
n
and
a
are odd and
\gcd(a,\frac{n}{2})=1
.

For 4-regular circulant graphs, Herke gave constructions of P1Fs for a number of infinite families of bipartite 4-regular circulant graphs and ruled out the existence of P1Fs for certain families of non-bipartite 4-regular circulant graphs
[
7
]
.

There have been a number of generalisations of U1Fs and P1Fs of graphs. A
semiperfect
1-factorisation of a graph is a 1-factorisation
\mathcal{F}=\left\{F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{\alpha}\right\}
of an
\alpha
-regular graph such that
F_{1}\cup F_{i}
forms a Hamilton cycle for
2\leq i\leq\alpha
. Ehrenfeucht showed in 1973 that semiperfect 1-factorisations exist for all complete graphs of even order
[
4
]
. A
sequentially
uniform 1-factorisation of a graph is a 1-factorisation
\mathcal{F}
of an
\alpha
-regular graph whose 1-factors can be labelled
F_{1},F_{2},\dots,F_{\alpha}
such that
F_{i}\cup F_{i+1}
is isomorphic to some 2-regular graph
H
for all
1\leq i\leq\alpha
(where
\alpha+1=1
). If
H
is a Hamilton cycle we call such a 1-factorisation sequentially perfect.

The goal of this paper is to introduce a new type of 1-factorisation that is a relaxation of a uniform 1-factorisation.
We say that a pair of 1-factors of a 1-factorisation of a graph has
type

\left[c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{t}\right]
if their union is a collection of
t
cycles of lengths
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{t}
, and we use the notation
\left[c_{1}^{n_{1}},c_{2}^{n_{2}},\dots,c_{t}^{n_{t}}\right]
for a type with
n_{i}
cycles of length
c_{i}
for each
i\in\left\{1,2,\dots,t\right\}
.
Consider a 1-factorisation
\mathcal{F}
of some graph and let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{m}
be the types of the pairs of 1-factors of
\mathcal{F}
.
Let
a_{i}
be the number of pairs of 1-factors that are of type
T_{i}
.
If
a_{1}=a_{2}=\dots=a_{m}=b
for some integer
b
, then we say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
m
-balanced 1-factorisation
(
m
-B1F) with types
\left(T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{m}\right)
. If it is not important how many types appear, then we will just use B1F.

Clearly,
m
-B1Fs of
r
-regular graphs are only feasible when
m\mid\binom{r}{2}
and we note that a
1
-B1F is a U1F. We begin the investigation of
m
-B1Fs by considering a family of highly symmetric graphs with low degree, specifically, 3- and 4-regular connected circulant graphs.

2
B1Fs of Connected 3-Regular Circulant Graphs

As noted,
m
-B1Fs of
r
-regular graphs require
m\mid\binom{r}{2}
. Thus, an
m
-B1F of a 3-regular graph can only exist if
m\in\left\{1,3\right\}
. Herke
[
8
]
has considered the problem of finding 1-B1Fs of connected 3-regular circulant graphs before, so we shall focus on the case where
m=3
and determine the values of
n
for which 3-B1Fs of connected 3-regular circulant graphs on
2n
vertices exist.
Herke and Maenhaut
[
8
]
have shown that up to isomorphism there are only two connected 3-regular circulant graphs of order
2n
,
Circ(2n,\left\{1,n\right\})
and
Circ(2n,\left\{2,n\right\})
. We completely answer the question of existence of 3-B1Fs of connected 3-regular circulant graphs with the following two theorems.

Theorem 4
.

The circulant graph
Circ(2n,\left\{1,n\right\})
admits a 3-B1F if and only if
n=6
or
n\geq 8
.

Proof.

For
Circ(2n,\left\{1,n\right\})
to admit a 3-B1F, clearly there must be at least three possible types of pairs of 1-factors. The union of a pair of 1-factors will be a collection of even length cycles of length at least 4. If
2n\leq 10
it is impossible to have three distinct types of pairs of 1-factors of
Circ(2n,\left\{1,n\right\})
. For
n=7
, we enumerated all possible 1-factorisations of
Circ(2n,\left\{1,n\right\})
by computer and determined that none are 3-B1Fs. It remains to show that we can construct 3-B1Fs for all remaining even values of
n
.

We consider two separate cases: even
n
where
n\geq 6
, and odd
n
where
n\geq 9
.

First, if
n
is even and
n\geq 12
, consider the 1-factorisation
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}=\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
, of

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left\{x,x+1\right\}\,:\,0\leq x\leq 2n-2,\,\text{$x$ even}\right\}

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left\{x,x+1\right\}\,:\,1\leq x\leq 2n-3,\,x\neq n-1,\,\text{$x$ odd}\right\}\cup\left\{\left\{x,x+n\right\}\,:\,x\in\left\{0,n-1\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left\{x,x+1\right\}\,:\,x\in\left\{n-1,2n-1\right\}\right\}\cup\left\{\left\{x,x+n\right\}\,:\,1\leq x\leq n-2\right\}.

is a 3-B1F we will show that

Theorem 5
.

The connected circulant graph
Circ(2n,\left\{2,n\right\})
admits a 3-B1F if and only if
n\geq 9
.

Proof.

If
Circ(2n,\left\{2,n\right\})
is connected, then
\gcd(2n,2,n)=1
. Thus, we now only consider odd
n
. For
Circ(2n,\left\{2,n\right\})
to admit a 3-B1F, clearly there must be at least three possible types of pairs of 1-factors. The union of a pair of 1-factors will be a collection of even length cycles of length at least 4. It is clear that if
2n\leq 10

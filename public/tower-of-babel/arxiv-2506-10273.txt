Generalized Poisson kernel and solution of the Dirichlet problem for the radial Schrödinger equation
Víctor A. Vicente-Benítez

Abstract
We present an explicit construction of the solution to the Dirichlet boundary value problem for the radial Schrödinger equation in the unit ball, with a complex-valued potential V V satisfying the condition ∫ 0 1 r ​ | V ⁡ ( r ) | ​ 𝑑 r < ∞ \int_{0}^{1}r|V(r)|dr<\infty . The solution is based on the construction of an explicit orthogonal set of solutions for the radial equation. In the case of a Dirichlet problem with boundary data in W 1 2 , 2 ​ ( 𝕊 d − 1 ) W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}) , the solution is expressed as a series expansion in terms of the so-called formal spherical polynomials. We establish conditions for the solvability and uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem. Based on this series representation, we introduce the concept of generalized Poisson kernel, develop its main properties, and investigate the conditions under which the Dirichlet problem, with a boundary condition being a complex Radon measure on 𝕊 d − 1 \mathbb{S}^{d-1} , admits a solution in the sense of a distributional boundary values.



Keywords:
Generalized Poisson kernel; complete system of solutions; radial Schrödinger
equation; Dirichlet problem; perturbed Bessel equation.

1
Introduction

In the present work, we study the radial Schrödinger equation

\left(\Delta-V(|x|)\right)u(x)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{B}^{d},

(1)

where
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
is the
d
-dimensional Laplacian,
\mathbb{B}^{d}
denotes the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, and the potential
V
is a complex-valued function
depending only on the radial component
r=|x|
. We assume that
V
satisfies the condition

\int_{0}^{1}r|V(r)|dr<\infty.

(2)

The aim of this work is to provide an explicit construction of the solution to the Dirichlet problem associated with Eq. (
1
) in
\mathbb{B}^{d}
. We consider two formulations of the Dirichlet problem. The first is the standard formulation with
trace
boundary data
\varphi\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, which consists of finding a weak solution
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
of (
1
) such that
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=\varphi
. The second formulation consists of finding a distributional solution
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
of (
1
), given a boundary data
\varphi
interpreted as a distribution over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
(for instance,
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
). The solution
u
is required to satisfy that its radial traces
u(r\cdot)
converge to
\varphi
in the weak-* topology of distributions as
r\rightarrow 1^{-1}
. This approach has taken on important relevance in the study of boundary value problems for elliptic equations and systems and in the study of their corresponding generalized Hardy spaces
[
10
,
12
,
27
,
6
]
. For example, in
[
12
]
, it is shown how the Dirichlet problem with distributional boundary data for harmonic functions can be uniquely solved using the standard Poisson kernel. A problem of considerable interest is the explicit construction of the Poisson kernel for Schrodinger-type equations. In
[
25
]
, a generalized Poisson kernel was explicitly constructed for Laplace and Helmholtz-type equations in cylindrical domains. This construction is based on the explicit computation of certain eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator. In
[
24
]
, the same author proposed a construction of a generalized Poisson integral representation for the radial Schrödinger equation in a cylindrical domain, under the assumption that the potential
V
is positive and satisfies the condition
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{2}V(r)=\kappa\in[0,\infty)
, and for continuous Dirichlet data. In
[
13
]
, the conditions under which positive solutions of a Schrödinger equation admit a representation via a generalized Poisson kernel are established. This kernel is constructed using a Neumann operator series involving the harmonic Poisson and Green kernels. In the case when
V
is analytic in the radial component
r
, it is known that solutions of (
1
) can be represented by means of an integral operator acting on harmonic functions
[
5
,
16
]
. This result was extended in
[
15
]
to potentials
V\in C^{1}[0,1]
. The corresponding integral operator is called a
transformation
or
transmutation operator
for the radial Schrödinger equation (
1
). A solution to the Dirichlet problem expressed in terms of the inverse of the transmutation operator is provided in
[
17
]
. In
[
22
]
, it is shown that the transmutation operator transforms the harmonic Bergman space onto the space of
L^{2}
classical solutions of (
1
), and an orthogonal basis of solutions takes the form

U_{m}(x)=|x|^{\frac{1-d}{2}}y_{m}(|x|)p\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right),

(3)

where
p
is a spherical harmonic of degree
m
and
\{y_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
are the solutions of the perturbed Bessel equations
-y^{\prime\prime}_{m}+\left(\frac{\ell_{m}(\ell_{m}+1)}{r^{2}}+V(r)\right)y_{m}=0
in
(0,1)
, with
\ell_{m}=m+\frac{d-3}{2}
. The existence of such a system relies entirely on the existence of the transmutation operator, while the completeness of the system depends intrinsically on the fact that the transmutation operator has a continuous inverse. However, for the case when
V\not\in C^{1}[0,1]
, there is no explicit result that guarantees the existence of such a natural transmutation operator or its analytical properties.

The first goal of this work is to extend the construction of a system of the form (
3
) to the case where
V
is complex valued and satisfies (
2
). A decay condition of this type ensures the existence of regular solutions of the perturbed Bessel equations
[
9
]
. An explicit construction of the solutions by means of the
spectral parameter power series
is presented in
[
8
]
, for potentials
V\in C(0,1]
satisfying the asymptotic condition
V(r)=O(r^{\alpha})
as
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
, for some
\alpha>-2
. We present a generalization of the construction presented in
[
22
]
, which only uses the fact that the potential
V
satisfies (
2
). Next, we construct an orthogonal system of solutions of the form (
3
) that we call the
formal spherical polynomials
. For
d\geq 3
, the orthogonality is given with respect to the inner product of
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. It is important to note that although explicit solutions can be obtained via the separation of variables for certain regular potentials, our approach provides a general solution for any complex-valued potential satisfying the condition (
2
). In addition, we present a constructive algorithm and establish the conditions under which the resulting series converges.

Our second aim is the explicit construction of the solution to the Dirichlet problem. Once the orthogonal system is obtained, we analyze the Dirichlet problem with boundary data
\varphi\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, and show that the solution can be expressed as a Fourier-series in terms of the formal spherical polynomials, with convergence in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. We establish additional conditions on the potential
V
to ensure the uniqueness of the solution, and we conclude that the formal spherical polynomials form an orthogonal basis for the space of weak solutions in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
of Eq. (
1
).

From the obtained series representation, we derive a formula for the generalized Poisson kernel and show that, when
\varphi
is a distribution on
C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
(i.e., a Radon measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
), Eq. (
1
) admits a distributional solution
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\cap C(\mathbb{B}^{d})
given in terms of the Poisson integral, whose distributional boundary value is precisely
\varphi
. For the case
d=2
, this result extends to boundary values that are distributions over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

The paper is structured as follows. Section 2 provides background on the main properties of spherical gradients, spherical harmonics, and Sobolev spaces on the sphere. Section 3 is devoted to the explicit construction of an orthogonal system of solutions for equation (
1
), including a procedure for constructing solutions to the associated Bessel equations. In Section 4, we obtain the solution to the Dirichlet problem with trace boundary data and establish the conditions on the potential
V
required for uniqueness. Section 5 presents the construction of the generalized Poisson kernel and establishes the solution to the distributional boundary value problem. Finally, Section 6 discusses the details of these constructions in the case
d=2
. Appendix A contains technical proofs of certain properties of the gradient and spherical harmonics.

2
Background on spherical harmonics and Sobolev spaces on the sphere

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
with
d\geq 2
. The open ball and the sphere with radius
r>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are denoted by
B_{r}^{d}(0)
and
S_{r}^{d}(0)
. As is usual, we denote the unit ball and sphere by
\mathbb{B}^{d}=B_{1}^{d}(0)
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=S_{1}^{d}(0)
.
Given a topological linear space
X
, its topological dual is denoted by
X^{\prime}
, and the action of an element
f\in X^{\prime}
on
x\in X
is denoted by
(f|x)_{X}
. Throughout the text, we use the notation
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the space of
L^{p}
functions on the ball with respect to the Lebesgue measure is denoted by
L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, while the corresponding space in the sphere, with the Borel surface measure
d\sigma
, is denoted by
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. The Sobolev space of functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
whose distributional partial derivatives up to order
k\in\mathbb{N}
also belong to
L^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is denoted by
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. The corresponding local spaces are denoted by
L_{loc}^{p}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
W^{k,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. For
1<p<\infty
, the space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
in
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, and
W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{d}):=(W_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{B}^{d}))^{\prime}
. The space of test functions
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is denoted by
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, while for the sphere, we set
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. The surface area of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is denoted by
\omega_{d-1}
.

Every point
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
can be written in terms of the generalized spherical coordinates

\xi_{i}=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}\sin\theta_{j},&\text{ if }\;i=1,\\
\cos\theta_{i-1}\prod_{j=i}^{d-1}\sin\theta_{j},&\text{ if }\;2\leq i\leq d,\end{cases}

(4)

where
0\leq\theta_{1}\leq 2\pi
and
0\leq\theta_{j}\leq\pi
,
j=2,\dots,d-1
(here, we use the convention that
\prod_{\emptyset}=1
). We denote by
\widehat{\mathbf{r}}
the unit vector that corresponds exactly to the spherical coordinates (
4
) (that is,
\widehat{\mathbf{r}}
is the unit normal vector on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
at the point
\xi(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d-1})
). On the other hand, let
\vec{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{j}=\left(\frac{\partial\xi_{i}}{\partial\theta_{j}}\right)_{i=1}^{d}
, and
\Theta_{j}:=|\vec{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j}}|
,
j=1,\dots,d-1
.

The following lemma summarizes the properties of the spherical coordinates.

Lemma 1

If we denote
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j}}:=\frac{1}{\Theta_{j}}\vec{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,d-1
, then
\{\widehat{\mathbf{r}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{d-1}\}
forms an orthonormal basis, and for
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, its gradient
\nabla u
can be decomposed as

\nabla u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\widehat{\mathbf{r}}+\frac{1}{r}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u,

(5)

where
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
is called the spherical gradient given by

\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{\Theta_{j}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta_{j}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{j}.

(6)

Note that
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
acts only on the coordinates
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d-1}
, and is the orthogonal projection of
\nabla u
onto the tangent space
\mathcal{T}_{\xi}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
at the point
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. This fact is well known for dimensions
d=2,3
. The existence of the spherical gradient in general dimensions is established in
[
2
]
. However, as explicit computations in spherical coordinates are not provided there, we include them in Appendix
A
for the reader’s convenience.

A well-known result concerns the decomposition of the Laplace operator. Let
\Delta:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
be the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is known that
\Delta
can be written in the form

\Delta=\frac{1}{r^{d-1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}r^{d-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},

(7)

where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
is the
spherical Lapacian
(also known as the
Laplace-Beltrami operator
, see
[
11
, Lemma 1.4.1]
or
[
2
, Sec. 3.1]
), acting only on the coordinates
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d-1}
. As is well known,
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}f(x)dv_{x}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u(r\xi)d\sigma_{\xi}dr
for
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Remark 2

Let
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}})
. Since the exterior unit normal of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
at the point
\xi(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d-1})
is precisely
\widehat{\mathbf{n}}
, then the normal derivative
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}
is given by

\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\nabla u\cdot\widehat{\mathbf{n}}\big|_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\bigg|_{r=1}.

(8)

The set of all homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For the case
d=2
,
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is generated by the complex monomials
\{z^{m},\overline{z}^{m}\}
. For
d\geq 3
,
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is finite dimensional and
d_{m}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by

d_{m}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }m=0,\\
d,&\text{ if }m=1,\\
\binom{d+m-1}{d-1}-\binom{d+m-3}{d-1},&\text{ if }m\geq 2,\end{cases}

(see
[
3
, Prop. 5.8]
. The set of the spherical harmonics of degree
m
is given by
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}):=\{p|_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\,|\,p\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
. It holds that
\dim\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=d_{m}
. Fix an orthonormal basis
\{Y_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{dm}
for
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. The following lemma establishes the orthogonality of the spherical harmonics and their spherical gradients.

Lemma 3

(i)

If
m\neq n
, then
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\perp_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
.

(ii)

The spherical harmonics are the eigenfunctions of the spherical Laplacian, that is, if
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
p\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, then

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p=-m(m+d-2)p.

(9)

(iii)

L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})=\bigoplus_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
.

(iv)

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p,q\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p(\xi)\overline{q(\xi)}d\sigma_{\xi}=\frac{1}{m(d+2m-2)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla p(\xi)\overline{\nabla q(\xi)}d\sigma_{\xi}.

(10)

(v)

If
p\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
and
q\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with
n\neq m
, then

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p(\xi)\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}q(\xi)}d\sigma_{\xi}=0.

(vi)

For
m\geq 1
, and
j,i\in\{1,\dots,d_{m}\}
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{k}^{(m)}(\xi)}d\sigma_{\xi}=0,\quad\text{if }j\neq k,

(11)

and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)|^{2}d\sigma_{\xi}=m(m+d-2).

(12)

The proof is given in Appendix
A
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. The Fourier coefficients of
\varphi
with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{\{Y_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
are denoted by

\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\varphi(\xi)\overline{Y_{j}^{(m)}}(\xi)d\sigma_{\xi},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\;j=1,\dots,d_{m}.

There exists the bounded trace operator
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}:W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, which satisfies
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=u|_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
for all
u\in C(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}})
and
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=0
iff
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. The image of
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
under the trace operator is denoted by
W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
and is called the Sobolev space of order
\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
23
, pp. 102-106]
). The following characterization of the space of traces is given in terms of the Fourier coefficients (see
[
2
, Sec. 3.8]
).

Proposition 4

The space
W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
consists of the functions
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
satisfying the condition

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\sqrt{m(m+d-2)}|\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}|^{2}<\infty.

(13)

The space
W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
is a Banach space with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}:=\left\{\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}^{2}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\sqrt{m(m+d-2)}|\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}|^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

3
An orthogonal system of solutions for the Schrödinger equation

Given
\alpha>0
and
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
L_{\alpha}^{p}(0,1)
the
L^{p}
-space on
(0,1)
with respect to the Borel measure
r^{\alpha}dr
. Note that
r^{\alpha}dr
is a finite measure on
(0,1)
, hence we have the continuous embedding
L^{p}_{\alpha}(0,1)\hookrightarrow L_{\alpha}^{q}(0,1)
, whereas
q\leq p

[
14
, Prop. 6. 12]
. For simplicity, we denote
L_{0}^{1}(0,1)=L^{1}(0,1)
, the standard
L^{p}
space with the Lebesgue measure.
Let
V
be a measurable complex-valued function in
L_{1}^{1}(0,1)
, that is, satisfying the condition

\int_{0}^{1}r|V(r)|dr<\infty.

(14)

Through the text, we assume that
V
satisfies (
14
).

We construct a system of solutions for the radial Schrödinger equation with potential
V

-\Delta u+V(r)u=0,\quad\text{ in }\;\;\mathbb{B}^{d}.

(15)

Definition 5

A function
u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is called a
distributional
solution of Eq. (
15
) if

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}u(-\Delta\varphi+V\varphi)=0\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d}).

(16)

A function
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is a
weak
solution of Eq. (
15
) if

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+Vu\varphi)=0\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d}).

(17)

The spaces of distributional and weak solutions are denoted by
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{dist}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, respectively.

Note that in both definitions it is required that
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}Vu\varphi
be defined. This condition is fulfilled when
u
or
V
belongs to
L_{loc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, for the case of distributional solutions, or when
V\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, for weak solutions.

Proposition 6

If
V\in L_{d-1}^{2}(0,1)
, then the subspace
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is closed in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Furthermore, if
V\in L_{d-1}^{r}(0,1)
, with
r=\max\{2,\frac{d}{2}\}
, then
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
iff

\int_{B}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+Vuv)=0\quad\forall v\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d}).

(18)

Proof.
The condition on
V
implies that
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|V|^{2}=\omega_{d-1}\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}|V(r)|^{2}<\infty
. Consider the bilinear form

B[u,\varphi]:=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi+Vu\varphi),\quad u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d}),\;\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d}).

Note that
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|Vu\varphi|\leq\omega_{d-1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{2}(0,1)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}
, so
B
is well defined for all
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\text{ and }\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Suppose that
\{u_{n}\}\subset\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
converges to
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Hence, given
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, we have that
B[u_{n},\varphi]=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle|B[u,\varphi]|=|B[u-u_{n},\varphi]|\leq\left(\sqrt{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}+\omega_{d-1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{2}(0,1)}\right)\|u-u_{n}\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\|\varphi\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,
B[u,\varphi]=0
for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Now, suppose that
V\in L_{d-1}^{r}(0,1)
with
r=\max\{2,\frac{d}{2}\}
(which implies that
V\in L^{r}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
). First, suppose that
d\geq 3
. When
u,v\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, by the Sobolev embedding theorems
[
7
, Cor. 9.14]
,
u,v\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, where
2^{*}=\frac{2d}{d-2}
. Applying the generalized Hölder inequality with
1=\frac{2}{d}+\frac{2}{2^{*}}
we get

\displaystyle|B[u,v]|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|\nabla u\cdot\nabla v|+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|Vuv|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\|v\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tilde{C}^{2}\|V\|_{L^{\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})})\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\|v\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})},

where
\tilde{C}
is the norm of the embedding
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Hence the bilinear form
B:W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\times W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is bounded. Since
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, the continuity of
B
implies (
18
). The proof for
d=2
is essentially the same, but using the fact that
u,v\in L^{q}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
for
1\leq q<\infty

[
7
, Cor. 9. 14]
, and the generalized Hölder inequality with
V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
and
u,v\in L^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
.

In particular, the first hypothesis holds when
V\in L_{1}^{2}(0,1)
, because
\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}|V(r)|^{2}dr\leq\int_{0}^{1}r|V(r)|^{2}<\infty
(
r^{d-2}\leq 1
, since
d\geq 2
).

Let
p\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Following
[
22
]
, we seek a solution
U_{m}
to Eq. (
15
) of the form

U_{m}(r\xi)=r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)p(\xi),\quad 0<r<1,\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

(19)

for some radial function
\alpha_{m}(r)
. Substituting this ansatz into Eq. (
15
) and using the decomposition of the Laplacian (
7
) along with the spherical eigenvalue identity (
9
), we obtain:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\left[r^{m}\left(\alpha_{m}^{\prime\prime}-V(r)\alpha_{m}\right)+r^{m-1}(2m+d-1)\alpha_{m}^{\prime}\right]p

\displaystyle+\left[r^{m-2}\alpha_{m}\left(m(m-1)+m-m(m+d-2)+m(d-1)\right)\right]p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\alpha_{m}^{\prime\prime}-V(r)\alpha_{m}+\frac{2m+d-1}{r}\alpha_{m}^{\prime}\right)r^{m}p.

Hence
U_{m}
satisfies Eq. (
15
) if
\alpha_{m}(r)
is a solution of

\alpha_{m}^{\prime\prime}-V(r)\alpha_{m}+\frac{2m+d-1}{r}\alpha_{m}^{\prime}=0,\quad 0<r<1.

Set
\ell_{m}:=m+\frac{d-3}{2}
. Writing
\alpha_{m}(r):=\frac{y_{m}(r)}{r^{\ell_{m}+1}}
, the last equality is reduced to the
perturbed Bessel equation

-y_{m}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\ell_{m}(\ell_{m}+1)}{r^{2}}y_{m}+V(r)y_{m}=0,\quad 0<r<1.

(20)

In order to obtain a solution
U_{m}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, we establish condition
\alpha_{m}(0)=1
. To obtain this, it suffices that the solution
y_{m}
satisfies the asymptotic conditions

y_{m}(r)\sim r^{\ell_{m}+1}=r^{m+\frac{d-1}{2}},\;y_{m}^{\prime}(r)\sim(\ell_{m}+1)r^{\ell_{m}},\;r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(21)

These conditions characterize the so-called regular solution of the perturbed Bessel equation (see
[
20
,
26
]
). Note that the critical case arises when
m=0
,
d=2
, because the asymptotic takes the form
y_{0}(r)\sim\sqrt{r}
,
y^{\prime}_{0}(r)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{r}}
,
r\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

In what follows,
we assume that
d\geq 3
(the case
d=2
will be treated separately in Section
6
).

The construction of the solutions
\{y_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
was proposed in
[
22
]
for the case
V\in C^{1}[0,1]
, and is based on the
spectral parameter power series

[
21
]
. We now extend this construction to the more general case when
\ell>0
, i.e., to the construction of the solution
w_{\ell}
of the perturbed Bessel equation

-w^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}w_{\ell}+V(r)w_{\ell}=0,\quad 0<r<1,

(22)

satisfying the asymptotics

w_{\ell}(r)\sim r^{\ell+1},\quad w_{\ell}^{\prime}(r)\sim(\ell+1)r^{\ell},\quad r\rightarrow 0^{+}.

(23)

In particular, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have
y_{m}=w_{\ell_{m}}
with
\ell_{m}=m+\frac{d-3}{2}
. The solution
w_{\ell}
is constructed as a functional series of the form

w_{\ell}(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\psi_{k}^{\ell}(r),

(24)

where

\psi_{k}^{\ell}(r):=\begin{cases}r^{\ell+1},&\mbox{ for }\;k=0,\\
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)V(s)\psi_{k-1}^{\ell}(s)ds,&\mbox{ for }\;k\geq 1,\end{cases}

(25)

and
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)
is the kernel

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s):=\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\left(\frac{r^{\ell+1}}{s^{\ell}}-\frac{s^{\ell+1}}{r^{\ell}}\right)\quad\mbox{ for
}\;(r,s)\in(0,1]\times(0,1].

(26)

As shown in
[
22
, Sec. 6]
, the kernel
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)
satisfies the following properties:
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\in C^{2}((0,1]\times(0,1])
;
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)}{\partial r}\big|_{r=s}=1
;
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)
; and the estimates

|\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)|\leqslant\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\frac{r^{\ell+1}}{s^{\ell}}\quad\text{and }\;\;\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)\right|\leqslant\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\ell}\quad\text{for }\;0<s\leq r\leq 1.

(27)

Consequently, the functions
\psi_{k}^{\ell}
satisfy the recursive relations

(\psi_{k}^{\ell})^{\prime}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)}{\partial s}V(s)\psi_{k-1}^{\ell}(s)ds,\quad(\psi_{k}^{\ell})^{\prime\prime}(r)=-V(r)\psi_{k-1}^{\ell}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}\psi_{k}^{\ell}.

(28)

Proposition 7

The series (
24
), as well as the series of their first derivative, converge absolutely and uniformly on
[0,1]
. In particular,
w_{\ell}\in C^{1}[0,1]
. Furthermore,
w_{\ell}^{\prime}\in AC[\delta,1]
for all
0<\delta<1
. The function
w_{\ell}
satisfies the perturbed Bessel equation (
22
) a.e. on
(0,1)
, and the asymptotic conditions (

Proof.
The proof is essentially the same as that of
[
22
, Th. 43]
, where the hypotheses

was not used, but only the fact that
V\in L_{1}^{1}(0,1)

Using (
27
), an induction argument yields the estimates

\displaystyle|\psi_{k}^{\ell}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\right)^{k}\frac{r^{\ell+1}}{k!}\left(\int_{0}^{r}s|V(s)|ds\right)^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(29)

\displaystyle|(\psi_{k}^{\ell})^{\prime}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\right)^{k-1}\frac{r^{\ell}}{k!}\left(\int_{0}^{r}s|V(s)|ds\right)^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(30)

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\psi_{k}^{\ell}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq r^{\ell+1}e^{\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}},

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|(\psi_{k}^{\ell})^{\prime}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\ell+\frac{1}{2}\right)r^{\ell}\left[e^{\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}}-1\right].

(33)

The Weierstrass M-test ensures the absolute and uniform convergence of the series on
[0,1]
. From (
28
),
\psi_{k}^{\ell}\in C^{1}[0,1]
and hence
w_{\ell}\in C^{1}[0,1]
. Again, by (
28
),
(\psi_{k}^{\ell})^{\prime\prime}\in L_{1}^{1}(\delta,1)
for all
0<\delta<1
, and due to the estimates (
30
) and (
), the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\psi_{k}^{\ell})^{\prime\prime}
converges in
L_{1}^{1}(\delta,1)
, and is not difficult to see that
w_{\ell}\in AC[\delta,1]
for all
0<\delta<1
. The recursive relations
(
28
)
imply that
w_{\ell}
satisfies (
22
) a.e. in
(0,1)
. Finally, in order to establish the asymptotics (

), we observe that

|w_{\ell}(r)-r^{\ell+1}|\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\right)\frac{r^{\ell+1}}{k!}\left(\int_{0}^{r}s|V(s)|ds\right)^{k}=r^{\ell+1}\left\{e^{\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\int_{0}^{r}s|V(s)|ds}-1\right\}.

Thus,
\displaystyle\left|\frac{w_{\ell}(r)}{r^{\ell+1}}-1\right|\leqslant\left\{e^{\frac{2}{2\ell+1}\int_{0}^{r}s|V(s)|ds}-1\right\}
. The right-hand side tends to zero when

. Therefore,
\lim_{r\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{w_{\ell}(r)}{r^{\ell+1}}=1
. The proof of the second asymptotic is similar.

The following estimates for the functions
\alpha_{m}
will be needed.

Lemma 8

There exists constants
C_{j}>0
,
j=0,1,2
, such that the following estimates hold for the functions
\{\alpha_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
:

(i)

|\alpha_{m}(r)|\leq C_{0}
for all
r\in[0,1]
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(ii)

|\alpha_{m}^{\prime}(r)|\leq\frac{mC_{1}}{r}
for all
r\in(0,1]
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iii)

|\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(r)|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{r}
for all
r\in(0,1]

Proof.

(i)

From (
32
),

|\alpha_{m}(r)|=\frac{1}{r^{\ell_{m}+1}}|y_{m}(r)|\leq e^{\frac{2}{2\ell_{m}+1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}}.

Since
2\ell_{m}+1=2m+d-2
, it follows that
e^{\frac{2}{2\ell_{m}+1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}}\leq e^{2\|V\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then
C_{0}=e^{2\|V\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}}
satisfies the condition in (i).

(ii)

Note that

\alpha_{m}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{y_{m}^{\prime}(r)}{r^{\ell_{m}+1}}-(\ell_{m}+1)\frac{y_{m}(r)}{r^{\ell_{m}+1}}=\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{y_{m}^{\prime}(r)}{r^{\ell_{m}}}-(\ell_{m}+1)\alpha_{m}(r)\right).

By the estimate (
33
),

\displaystyle|y_{m}^{\prime}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq(\ell_{m}+1)r^{\ell_{m}}+\left(\ell_{m}+\frac{1}{2}\right)r^{\ell_{m}}\left[e^{\frac{2}{2\ell_{m}+1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}}-1\right]\leq 2r^{\ell_{m}}(\ell_{m}+1).

Since
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ell_{m}+1}{m}=1
, we can choose
M>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\ell_{m}+1\leq Mm
for

. Taking
C_{1}=2\max\{\ell_{1}+1,\dots,\frac{\ell_{m_{0}-1}+1}{m_{0-1}},M\}(1+C_{0})
, we obtain (ii).

(iii)

As in the previous point,
|\alpha_{0}^{\prime}(r)|\leq\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{y_{0}^{\prime}(r)}{r^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}+\frac{d-1}{2}|\alpha_{0}(r)|\right)
, then
C_{2}=\frac{d-1}{2}(2+C_{0})
.

Theorem 9

Given
p\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the function
U_{m}(x)=r^{m}\phi_{m}(r)p(\xi)
belongs to
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\cap W^{2,1}(A_{\varepsilon}(0))
, where
A_{\varepsilon}(0)=\mathbb{B}^{d}\setminus\overline{B}^{d}_{\varepsilon}(0)
, and satisfies the Schrödinger equation a.e. in
A_{\varepsilon}(0)
for all
0<\varepsilon<1
. Therefore,
U_{m}\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Proof.
By Lemma
8
(i),
U_{m}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. To estimate the gradient, we use the decomposition (
5
) together with Lemma
8
(ii), valid for
m\geq 1
. This yields

\displaystyle|\nabla U_{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{d}{dr}(r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r))\right|^{2}|p|^{2}+r^{2m}|\alpha_{m}(r)|^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(m^{2}r^{2m-2}C_{0}^{2}+r^{2m}\frac{m^{2}C_{1}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)|p|^{2}+r^{2m}C_{0}^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p|^{2}

\displaystyle=m^{2}r^{2m-2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{1}^{2})|p|^{2}+r^{2m}C_{0}^{2}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p|^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|\nabla U_{m}|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}m^{2}r^{2m-2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{1}^{2})dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|p|^{2}d\sigma+\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}r^{2m}C_{0}^{2}dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p|^{2}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{1}^{2})}{2m+d-2}\|p\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}^{2}+\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{2m+d}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}^{2}.

The left-hand side is finite since
2m+d-2\geq 1
. For the case
m=0
, the function
p
is constant, and applying Lemma
8
(iii) we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|\nabla U_{0}|^{2}\leq\omega_{d-1}|p|^{2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{2}^{2})\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}r^{-2}dr=\omega_{d-1}|p|^{2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{2}^{2})\frac{1}{d-2},

because

. Hence
U_{m}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. For any
0<\varepsilon<1
,

, hence

. By Proposition

, we conclude that
U_{m}
satisfies Eq. (
15
) a.e. in
A_{\varepsilon}(0)
.

Finally, let
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and fix
0<\varepsilon<1
. Using Green’s identity on

we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\varepsilon}(0)}\left\{\nabla U_{m}\cdot\nabla\varphi+VU_{m}\varphi\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}-S_{\varepsilon}^{d}(0)}\varphi\frac{\partial U_{m}}{\partial\nu}d\sigma+\int_{A_{\varepsilon}(0)}\left\{(-\Delta U_{m}+VU_{m})\varphi\right\}

\displaystyle=-\int_{S_{\varepsilon}^{d}(0)}\varphi(m\varepsilon^{m-1}\alpha_{m}(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{m}\alpha_{m}^{\prime}(\varepsilon))d\sigma.

For

, applying Lemma

(i) and (ii) we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{S_{\varepsilon}^{d}(0)}\varphi(m\varepsilon^{m-1}\alpha_{m}(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon^{m}\alpha_{m}^{\prime}(\varepsilon))d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq m(C_{1}+C_{0})\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\omega_{d-1}\varepsilon^{m+d-2}

Passing to the limit when

.

Definition 10

A
formal spherical polynomial
of degree

, is a function of the form

, where

\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{B}^{d})

Given the orthonormal basis

of

\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}(x):=r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi),\quad j=1,\dots,d_{m}.

(34)

It is worth mentioning that there exist explicit formulas for some orthonormal basis

in higher dimensions, see, e.g.,
[
11
, Ch. 1]
and
[
22
, Remark 3.2]
.

Proposition 11

(i)

If
m\neq n
,
\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\perp_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

(ii)

The set

(i)

Let

and

\displaystyle\langle U_{m},U_{n}\rangle_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}U_{m}\overline{U_{n}}+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\frac{\partial U_{m}}{\partial r}\overline{\frac{\partial U_{n}}{\partial r}}+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}U_{m}\cdot\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}U_{n}}d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}r^{d-1}\left(r^{m+n}\alpha_{m}(r)\overline{\alpha_{m}(r)}+\frac{d(r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r))}{dr}\overline{\frac{d(r^{n}\alpha_{n}(r))}{dr}}\right)dr\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p\overline{q}d\sigma

(i) and (v).

4
Solution of the Dirichlet problem for Sobolev traces

In this section, we consider the Dirichlet problem for the radial equation (
15
) in the sense of the Sobolev traces (DS): given a function
\varphi\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, find a function
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
such that
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=\varphi
.

In the case where
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is real valued and satisfies the condition
\inf_{0\leq r\leq 1}V(r)>-\lambda_{0}
, where
\lambda_{0}=\inf\limits_{u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\||\nabla u|\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}}
(the best constant for the Sobolev embedding
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, or equivalently, the first eigenvalue of the DS problem for the Laplace equation in
\mathbb{B}^{d}
), the DS problem admits a unique solution (indeed, it is not difficult to see that this conditions guarantees that the bilinear form
B[u,v]
induces an inner product in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
that is equivalent to the usual one, and the result follows from the Lax-Milgram Theorem, see
[
7
, p. 294]
). In general, other conditions are required to ensure uniqueness of the problem (see, e.g.,
[
23
, Ch. IV]
). In what follows, we will present a condition under which the problem admits at least one solution

Assumption 12

The potential
V\in L_{1}^{1}(0,1)
satisfies the condition:

\alpha_{m}(1)\neq 0\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

This is equivalent to stating that, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda=0
is not an eigenvalue for the regular Sturm-Liouville problem

\displaystyle-u^{\prime\prime}_{m}+\frac{\ell_{m}(\ell_{m}+1)}{r^{2}}u_{m}+V(r)u_{m}

\displaystyle=\lambda u_{m},\quad 0<r<1

\displaystyle u_{m}(0)=u_{m}(1)=0.

Remark 13

(i)

Suppose that
V
is non negative. Since
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(r,s)\geq 0
for
0<s<r\leq 1
, it follows that
\psi_{k}^{\ell}(r)\geq 0
. Hence,
y_{m}^{\ell}(r)=r^{\ell+1}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\psi_{k}^{\ell}(r)\geq r^{\ell+1}
. Consequently,
y_{m}(1)>1
, and
\alpha_{m}(1)>0
. This conclusion holds, for instance, in the case of the
Coulomb potential

V(r):=\frac{c}{r}
with
c>0
.

(ii)

If
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
with
\inf_{0\leq r\leq 1}V(r)>-\lambda_{0}
, then
V
satisfies Assumption
12
. Indeed, if
\alpha_{m}(1)=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
U_{m}=r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)p\in\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is a solution of the DS problem with
\varphi=0
, contradicting the uniqueness of the solution for this kind of potentials.

We recall that for
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, its Fourier coefficients with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{\{Y_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
are denoted by
\{\{\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
.

Lemma 14

If
V
satisfies the Assumption
12
, then the sequence
\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\right\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
is bounded.

Proof.
By the estimates (
),

\displaystyle|\alpha_{m}(1)-1|=|y_{m}(1)-1|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\psi_{k}^{\ell_{m}}(1)|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\frac{2}{2\ell_{m}+1}\right)^{k}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}^{k}}{k!}=e^{\frac{2}{2\ell_{m}+1}\|V\|_{L_{1}^{1}(0,1)}}-1.

Taking the limit when
m\rightarrow\infty
, we conclude that
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\alpha_{m}(1)=1
. Since
\alpha_{m}(1)\neq 0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it follows that
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha_{m}(1)}=1
, and the sequence is bounded.

Theorem 15

Suppose that
V\in L_{d-1}^{2}(0,1)
satisfies Assumption
12
. Given
\varphi\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, a solution
u

\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=\varphi

u_{\varphi}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}

(35)

W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

When
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
with
\inf_{0\leq r\leq 1}V(r)\geq-\lambda_{0}

Proof.
By Lemma
14
,
C_{4}:=\sup\limits_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{1}{|\phi_{m}(1)|}<\infty
. We now show that the series (

) converges in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

First, note that
\|\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}\leq\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{m+d}
, which implies that

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\left\|\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}^{2}\leq C_{0}^{2}C_{4}^{2}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}|^{2}<\infty,

and the series converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})

For the gradient, from the proof of Theorem
9
we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{1}^{2})}{2m+d-2}\|Y_{j}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}^{2}+\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{2m+d}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}(C_{0}^{2}+C_{1}^{2})}{2m+d-2}+\frac{C_{0}^{2}}{2m+d}m(m+d-2).

In the last equality, we use (
12
). Since
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m}{2m+d-2}=1
, there exists a constant
C_{5}
with
\frac{m}{2m+d}\leq\frac{m}{m+d-2}\leq C_{5}
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{0}^{2}+C_{1}^{2})C_{5}m+(m+d-2)C_{5}C_{0}^{2}.

Again, since
\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m}{m+d-2}=1
, we can find constants
C_{6},C_{7}>0
such that
C_{7}\leq\frac{m}{m+d-2}\leq C_{6}
. Combining these estimates, we get

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\left\|\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\nabla\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}^{2}\leq\bar{C}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\sqrt{m(m+d-2)}|\widehat{\varphi_{m,j}}|^{2}<\infty,

for some constant
\bar{C}>0
depending only on
C_{0},\dots,C_{7}
. Thus, the series (
35
) converges in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
u_{\varphi}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. By Proposition
6
, we conclude that
u_{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{d}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Finally, define the partial sums
u_{N}=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}
. Since each
\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}})
, it follows that
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u_{N}=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}Y_{j}^{(m)}=\varphi_{N}
. Consequently,
\varphi_{N}\rightarrow\varphi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. But
u_{N}\rightarrow u_{\varphi}
in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and the continuity of the trace operator implies that
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u_{N}\rightarrow\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u
. Therefore,
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u_{\varphi}=\varphi
.

From the estimates obtained in the proof of Theorem
9
, we deduce:

Corollary 16

The operator
W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\ni\varphi\mapsto u_{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is bounded.

Corollary 17

If
V\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
and satisfies
\inf_{0\leq r\leq 1}V(r)>-\lambda_{0}
, then
\{\{\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
is an orthogonal basis for
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Proof.
Given
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}
, this is the unique solution to the DS problem with
\varphi=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u
. Hence
u
admits the Fourier-series (
35
).

Now, we establish a condition ensuring uniqueness for the DS problem.

Definition 18

A potential
V
is said to be
almost bounded from below
, if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\operatorname{Re}V(r)\geq-C
a.e. in
(0,1)
.

For example, the Coulomb potential
V(r)=\frac{c}{r}
with
\operatorname{Re}c>0
is almost bounded from below, but does not belong to
L^{\infty}(0,1)
.

Theorem 19

Suppose that
V\in L_{d-1}^{r}(0,1)
, where
r=\max\{2,\frac{d}{2}\}
, is almost bounded from below. Then, the DS problem admits a unique solution iff
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}:\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\rightarrow W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is surjective.

Proof.
By Proposition
6
, the bilinear form
B:W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\times W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by (
18
), is bounded. This allows us to define an operator
\mathbf{A}:W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\rightarrow W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, given by
\mathbf{A}u=B[u,\cdot]
. Since the embedding
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is continuous and
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is a dense subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, we can identify
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\subset W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. So, we have that
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is a pivot space for
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
(see
[
23
, p. 44]
). Hence, using that
V
is almost bounded from below and the Poincaré inequality
[
7
, Cor. 9. 19]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}B[u,u]=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\operatorname{Re}(V)|u|^{2}\geq\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}^{2}-C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}^{2}.

This shows that
B
is coercive in
W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
relative to the pivot space
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Moreover, since the embedding
W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is compact
[
7
, Th. 9. 16]
, it follows from
[
23
, Th. 2. 34]
that the operator
\mathbf{A}:W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\hookrightarrow W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is Fredholm with index
0
. In particular, the space
W
of solutions in
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
with zero trace is finite dimensional. Suppose that
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{N}\}
is a basis for
W
. According to
[
23
, p. 115]
, the adjoint operator of
\mathbf{A}
is given by

(\mathbf{A}^{\star}u)v=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\overline{V}uv,\quad u,v\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d}).

Note that
w_{j}
is a solution of trace zero of the Schrödinger equation with potential
V
iff
\overline{w}_{j}
is a solution of the corresponding equation with potential
\overline{V}
. By the Fredholm alternative
[
23
, Th. 2. 27]
, the non-homogeneous equation
\mathbf{A}u=f
,
f\in W^{-1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, admits a solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
iff
(f|\overline{w_{j}})_{W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}=0
,
j=1,\dots,N
. Now, consider the DS problem for the Schrödinger equation with potential
V
with Dirichlet data
\varphi\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. It is easy to see that the DS problem is equivalent to solve
\mathbf{A}y=f
with
f=\mathbf{A}\mathcal{E}\varphi
, where
\mathcal{E}\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is an extension of
\varphi
. Hence, the DS problem has a unique solution iff

\displaystyle 0=(\mathbf{A}\mathcal{E}\varphi|\overline{w}_{j})_{W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})}=\int_{B}\nabla\mathcal{E}\varphi\cdot\nabla\overline{w}_{j}+V\mathcal{E}\varphi\overline{w}_{j}.

(36)

The right-hand side of (
36
) defines a functional in
W^{-\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}):=\left(W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\right)^{\prime}
, which is independent of the choice of extension
\mathcal{E}\varphi
(see
[
23
, Lemma 4. 3]
and
[
28
, Ch. 7]
). We call this functional the
generalized normal derivative of

\overline{w}_{j}
, denoted by
\frac{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}{\partial\nu}
,
j=1,\dots,N
. In particular, if
\varphi=\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u
with
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, then

\left(\frac{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}{\partial\nu}\bigg|\operatorname{tr}|_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u\right)_{W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\overline{w}_{j}+Vu\overline{w}_{j}=0,\quad j=1,\dots,N,

because
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
\overline{w}_{j}\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Conversely, if
\varphi\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
satisfies that
\left(\frac{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}{\partial\nu}\bigg|\varphi\right)_{W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=0
, then Eq. (
36
) implies that there exists
y\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
such that
\mathbf{A}y=\mathbf{A}\mathcal{E}\varphi
, i.e., there is
u\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
with
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u=\varphi
. Thus, the pre-annihilator of
E=\operatorname{Span}\left\{\frac{\partial\overline{w}_{j}}{\partial\nu}\right\}_{j=1}^{N}
in
W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
is precisely
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left(\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\right)
. Since
E
is finite dimensional, by
[
28
, Ch. 2, Lemma 1]
, the annihilator of
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
is
E
.

Finally,
\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\left(\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\right)=W^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
iff
E=\{0\}
which is equivalent to the DS problem having a unique solution.

Corollary 20

If
V\in L_{d-1}^{r}(0,1)
with
r=\max\{2,\frac{d}{2}\}
, is almost bounded from below, then the DS problem admits a unique solution iff the potential
V
satisfies Assumption
12
. In this case, the family
\{\{\mathcal{V}_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
forms an orthogonal basis for
\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{w}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

5
The generalized Poisson kernel

In this section, we assume that the potential
V
satisfies Assumption
12
. In the case when
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, the series (
35
) converges to
u_{\varphi}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
u_{\varphi}\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{dist}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. However, in general, the series does not converge in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Nevertheless, for
x=r\xi
, we may informally manipulate the series to obtain an integral representation. Specifically, using the Fourier expansion of
\varphi
we write:

\displaystyle u_{\varphi}(r\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\varphi(\zeta)\overline{Y_{j}^{(m)}(\zeta)}d\sigma_{\zeta}\right)Y_{j}^{(m)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\varphi(\zeta)\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)\overline{Y_{j}^{(m)}(\zeta)}\right)d\sigma_{\zeta},

The series can be rewritten as follows:

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)\overline{Y_{j}^{(m)}(\zeta)}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta),

where

Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta):=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)\overline{Y_{j}^{(m)}(\zeta)}.

(37)

The function
Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)
is called the
zonal harmonic
of degree
m
, and is precisely the reproducing kernel for the finite-dimensional space
\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with the
L^{2}
-inner product (that is,
p(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p(\xi)Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)d\sigma_{\zeta}
for all
p\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
,
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
). The zonal harmonic is uniquely determined by its reproducing property and is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\{Y_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}
. The following proposition summarizes the main properties of the Zonal harmonics. A detailed proof can be found in
[
3
, Prop. 5.37]
.

Proposition 21

(i)

The zonal harmonic is real valued and symmetric:
Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)=Z_{m}(\zeta,\xi)
.

(ii)

If
m\neq n
, then
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)Z_{n}(\xi,\zeta)d\sigma_{\xi}=0
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

(iii)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Z_{m}^{2}(\xi,\zeta)d\sigma_{\zeta}=Z_{m}(\xi,\xi)=d_{m}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

(iv)

|Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)|\leq Z_{m}(\xi,\xi)
for all
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

(v)

There exists a constant
C^{d}
depending only on the dimension
d
and satisfying
Z_{m}(\xi,\xi)\leq C^{d}m^{d-1}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Remark 22

According to
[
3
, Th. 5.38]
, the zonal harmonic
Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)
admits the expression

Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)=(d+2m-2)\sum_{k=0}^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]}(-1)^{k}\frac{d(d+2)\cdots(d+2m-2k-4)}{2^{k}k!(m-2k)!}(\xi\cdot\zeta)^{m-2k}.

We recall that the Pochhammer symbol is defined by
(z)_{n}:=z(z+1)\cdots(z+n-1)=\frac{\Gamma(z+n)}{\Gamma(z)}
. Hence,

\displaystyle d(d+2)\cdots(d+2m-2k-4)

\displaystyle=2^{m-k-1}\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)\cdots\left(\frac{d}{2}-1+m-k-1\right)

\displaystyle=2^{m-k-1}\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)_{m-k}=2^{m-k-1}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1+m-k\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)

\displaystyle=(d+2m-1)\sum_{k=0}^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]}(-1)^{k}\frac{2^{m-k-1}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1+m-k\right)}{2^{k}k!\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)}(\xi\cdot\zeta)^{m-2k}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{2}+m-1\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]}(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1+m-k\right)}{k!\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)}(2\xi\cdot\zeta)^{m-2k}=\left(m+\frac{d}{2}-1\right)C_{m}^{\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)}(\xi\cdot\zeta),

where
C^{(\alpha)}_{m}(t)
denotes the Gegenbauer polynomial of degree
m
and order
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
(see
[
1
, Sec. 22. 1]
).

Definition 23

The
generalized Poisson kernel
for the radial Schrödinger equation is defined by

P_{V}(r,\xi,\zeta)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta),\quad 0\leq r<1,\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

(38)

For
V\equiv 0
, we obtain the classical Poisson kernel for harmonic functions
[
3
, p. 122]
:

P_{0}(r,\xi,\zeta)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{|r\xi-\zeta|^{d}}.

Note that
P_{V}
is symmetric in the spherical variables.

Proposition 24

The series (
38
) converges absolutely and uniformly for
|\rho|\leq r<1,\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Proof.
Let
r<1
and fix
|\rho|\leq r
,
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. By proposition
21
(iv)-(v) and Lemma
8

we get the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{\rho^{m}\alpha_{m}(\rho)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\right||Z_{m}(\xi,\zeta)|\leq C_{0}C_{4}C^{d}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m^{d-1}r^{m}.

The series converges by the ratio test. Hence, by the Weierstrass
M
-tests, the series converges absolutely and uniformly for
|\rho|\leq r,\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

As a consequence, for
r<1
, the uniform convergence ensures that

u_{\varphi}(r\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\varphi(\xi)P_{V}(r,\xi,\zeta)d\sigma_{\zeta}.

(39)

Given
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, for every
0<r<1
, the corresponding solution
u_{\varphi}
given by (
39
), defines a function
\varphi_{r}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
by the relation
\varphi_{r}(\xi):=u_{\varphi}(r\xi)
. The interesting question is in what sense
\varphi_{r}\rightarrow\varphi
when
r\rightarrow 1^{-1}
. A first answer that arises is in the case when
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
.

Proposition 25

If
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, the series (
35
) converges uniformly on
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}}
and
u\in C(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}})
. In consequence,
\varphi_{r}\rightarrow\varphi
uniformly on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

Proof.
According to
[
18
, Sec. 4, Th. 2]
, if
\varphi\in C^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with
p>\frac{d+3}{2}
, then its Fourier series satisfies
\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)|\leq C_{p,d,\varphi}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, where the constant
C_{p,d,\varphi}>0
depends only on
d,p
and
\varphi
, and the series converges uniformly and absolutely to
\varphi
. Hence, for
r\leq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
,

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)\right|\leq C_{0}C_{4}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{m,j}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)|<C_{0}C_{4}C_{p,d,\varphi}.

So, the series of
u_{\varphi}
converges absolutely and uniformly on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
. Consequently,
u_{\varphi}\in C(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}})
, hence
u_{\varphi}
is uniformly continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{d}}
. Therefore,
\varphi_{r}\rightarrow\varphi
uniformly on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

For a general
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, following
[
12
]
, we introduce the concept of
generalized boundary value
.

Definition 26

A locally integrable function
u
defined on
\mathbb{B}^{d}
is said to have a
distributional boundary value
, if there exists a distribution
f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
such that
u(r\cdot)\rightarrow f
in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
as
r\rightarrow 1^{-}
, that is,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}u(r\xi)\varphi(\xi)d\sigma_{\sigma}=(f|\varphi)_{\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d}).

Since the weak limit in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
is unique, we denote the distributional boundary value of
u
by
u_{db}
.

We recall that
(C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))^{\prime}
denotes the space of distributions on
C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. Since
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})\subset C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, it follows that
(C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))^{\prime}\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
. By the Riesz representation theorem,
(C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))^{\prime}
can be identified with the space of complex Radon measures on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}

[
14
, Cor. 7. 18]
. We denote
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}):=(C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1}))^{\prime}
. It is important to note that for
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, we have
(f|\varphi)_{\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=(f|\varphi)_{C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
.

Theorem 27

If
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, then the function

u_{f}(r\xi):=(f|P_{V}(r,\xi,\cdot))_{C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},\quad x=r\xi\in\mathbb{B}^{d},

(40)

is a distributional solution of (
15
) which belongs to
C(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
(u_{f})_{db}=f
. In particular, this is valid for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, and in this case,
u_{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Proof.
For fixed
x=r\xi\in\mathbb{B}^{d}
, Proposition
24
implies that
P_{V}(r,\xi,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
, so the operation (
40
) is well defined. Furthermore, since the series (
38
) converges in
C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
with respect to the variable
\zeta
, we have

\displaystyle u_{f}(x)=(f|P_{V}(r,\xi,\cdot))_{C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}(f|Z_{m}(\xi))_{\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}.

(we recall that
(f|Z_{m}(\xi))_{\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=(f|Z_{m}(\xi))_{C(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}
). Denote
\widehat{f}_{m}(\xi):=(f|Z_{m}(\xi,\cdot))_{\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{m}}(f|Y_{j}^{(m)})_{\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}Y_{j}^{(m)}(\xi)
, where
\{Y_{j}^{(m)}\}_{j=1}^{d_{m}}
is real-valued. According to
[
12
, Lemma 2]
, we have
\widehat{f}_{m}\in\mathcal{H}_{m}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
and there exist constants
C>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|\widehat{f}_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\leq Cm^{\beta}.

(41)

Thus,

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left|\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\widehat{f}_{m}\right|\leq C_{0}C_{4}C\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}m^{\beta}r^{m}.

Again, the ratio test implies that the series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{B}^{d}
, which ensures that
u_{f}\in C(\mathbb{B}^{d})\subset L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Note that each term
\frac{r^{m}\alpha_{m}(r)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\widehat{f}_{m}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{m}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
. Now, let
\phi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
, and choose
0<r<1
such that
\operatorname{supp}\phi\in B_{r}^{d}(0)
. Since the series defining
u_{f}
converges uniformly on
B_{r}^{d}(0)
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}}u_{f}(-\Delta\phi+V\phi)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r}(0)}\frac{\rho^{m}\alpha_{m}(\rho)}{\alpha_{m}(1)}\widehat{f}_{m}(-\Delta\phi+V\phi)=0,

Thus,
u_{f}\in\operatorname{Sol}_{V}^{dist}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
.

Finally, for
0<r<1
, set
f_{r}(\xi)=u_{f}(r\xi)
. For any test function
\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})
we have

7
Conclusions

We present an explicit construction of an orthogonal system of solutions for the radial Schrödinger equation with a radial, complex-valued, integrable potential. This system arises from an explicit construction of solutions to a family of perturbed Bessel equations. An algorithm is provided to facilitate the practical computation of the system.

We establish the conditions under which this system forms an orthogonal basis for the space of weak solutions. Additionally, we construct solutions to the Dirichlet problem with trace boundary conditions and identify sufficient and necessary conditions on the potential that guarantee the uniqueness of the solution.

Finally, we determine the conditions for the existence of a generalized Poisson kernel and solve the Dirichlet problem with distributional boundary conditions.

(vi)

Set
P=r^{m}Y_{j}^{(m)}
and
Q=r^{m}Y_{k}^{(m)}
. First, suppose that
j\neq k
. Applying the same procedure as in the previous point, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla P\cdot\overline{\nabla Q}

\displaystyle=m^{2}r^{2m-2}Y_{j}^{(m)}Y_{k}^{(m)}+r^{2m}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}\cdot\overline{\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{k}^{(m)}}

By (
10
),

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla P\cdot\overline{\nabla Q}d\sigma=m(d+2m-2)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}P\overline{Q}d\sigma=0,

and also

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla P\cdot\overline{\nabla Q}d\sigma=m^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}\overline{Y_{k}^{(m)}}d\sigma+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}\cdot\overline{\nabla Y_{k}^{(m)}}d\sigma.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}\overline{Y_{k}^{(m)}}d\sigma=0
, we conclude (
11
).
Finally, for the norm of
\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}
, using (
10
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|Y_{j}^{(m)}|^{2}d\sigma=\frac{1}{m(d+2m-2)}\left(m^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|Y_{j}^{(m)}|^{2}d\sigma+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}|^{2}d\sigma\right),

from where we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m(d+2m-2)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{j}^{(m)}|^{2}d\sigma=1-\frac{m^{2}}{m(d+2m-2)}=\frac{m(m+d-2)}{m(d+2m-2)},

and we conclude (
12

Prognostic value of immuno-inflammatory biomarkers in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients receiving immunotherapy combined with chemoradiotherapy and its association with immuno-genomic landscape
Authors: Xingyuan Cheng, Fanjun Meng, Ruixi Wang, Shiliang Liu, Qiaoqiao Li, Baoqing Chen, Mian Xi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe clinical significance of immuno-inflammatory indicators and the underlying biological basis in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) who receive chemoradiotherapy (CRT) combined with immunotherapy remains unclear. This study aims to evaluate the prognostic value of immuno-inflammatory biomarkers, develop a prognostic model, and explore the underlying mechanisms.MethodsThis study included 212 ESCC patients who received CRT and anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. Association between progression-free survival (PFS) and immuno-inflammatory biomarkers, including absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-monocyte ratio was analyzed. A nomogram was built based on the independent prognostic factors identified using multivariable Cox regression model. Pre-treatment tumor samples from 47 patients were collected for RNA sequencing to investigate the immune-related tumor microenvironment.ResultsPatients experienced significant changes in immuno-inflammatory biomarkers during CRT, which gradually recovered after radiotherapy. Body mass index < 18.5 (HR, 1.85; P = 0.032), N3 stage (HR, 2.41; P = 0.002), high pre-CRT PLR (HR, 1.53; P = 0.037), low ALC nadir (HR, 1.84; P = 0.006), and high post-CRT NLR (HR, 2.12; P = 0.002) were independent prognostic factors for unfavorable PFS, which were incorporated into a nomogram with a concordance index of 0.70 (95% CI, 0.67–0.72). High-risk patients stratified by the nomogram had worse survival and were associated with lower levels of leukocyte and T cell activation, proliferation, and migration and less intratumoral immune cell infiltration.ConclusionsPre-CRT PLR, ALC nadir during CRT, and post-CRT NLR were significantly associated with PFS in patients with ESCC receiving CRT and immunotherapy. A nomogram model with good prognostic ability was developed.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13298-z.

Background

Esophageal cancer is one of the most common malignancies, accounting for the seventh-highest tumor incidence and sixth-highest mortality rate worldwide, with more than 50% of cases occurring in China [1]. Despite comprehensive treatment strategies, including radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and surgery, the overall five-year survival rate for esophageal cancer is only 15–20% [2].

With advances in immunotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the programmed cell death 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) pathway offer new treatment options for patients with esophageal cancer [3]. In recent years, a series of studies have shown that combining PD-1 inhibitors with chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy (CRT) improves the clinical outcomes in patients with esophageal cancer outcomes. However, many patients are resistant to combined treatments and have poor prognoses. Notably, immune checkpoint inhibitors are associated with various immune-related adverse events [4, 5]. Therefore, further studies on immunotherapy response and biomarkers are needed. Patients with high PD-L1 expression usually respond better to anti-PD1 immunotherapy [6, 7]. Additionally, tumor mutational load, and microsatellite instability status are also commonly used biomarkers for predicting the therapeutic effect of immunotherapy [8, 9]. However, the prognostic power of these biomarkers for patients with esophageal squamous carcinoma (ESCC) treated with immunotherapy is controversial, especially for the locally advanced setting [10, 11]. Moreover, the detection of these biomarkers relies on tumor tissue and molecular analysis, which are complex and expensive, limiting the application in clinical practice. Thus, there is an urgent need to develop convenient, easily accessible, and reproducibly detectable biomarkers to predict the treatment efficacy in ESCC.

Host immunity and inflammatory responses play a critical role in the development and progression of cancer. A number of metrics that use readily available blood test results to assess patients’ immune-inflammatory status have drawn attention in the evaluation of therapeutic efficacy and prognosis [12]. Systemic immuno-inflammatory biomarkers such as neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), lymphocyte-monocyte ratio (LMR), system inflammation response index (SIRI), and systemic immune-inflammatory index (SII), which could reflect the body’s immune status, have been proved to be capable of predicting prognosis in esophageal cancer [13–15]. In addition, high NLR or SII and low LMR showed strong correlations with poor tumor remission and unfavorable survival in patients with solid tumors receiving immunotherapy [16, 17]. Nevertheless, the clinical significance of these immunoinflammatory markers in patients with ESCC receiving CRT combined with immunotherapy remains unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to analyze the relationship between immunoinflammatory biomarkers and prognosis in patients with locally advanced ESCC who received CRT and immunotherapy, develop a prognostic model by integrating hematologic biomarkers and clinicopathological features, and further explore the underlying biological basis of immuno-inflammatory biomarkers associated with prognosis.

Methods

Patient selection

Medical records of patients with ESCC who received immunotherapy combined with CRT between March 2019 and June 2021 at our institution were retrospectively reviewed. Patients who met the subsequent requirements were included: (1) previously untreated and pathologically diagnosed as Stage I–IVa ESCC according to the 8th TNM staging system defined by the American Joint Committee on Cancer; (2) received CRT with a total radiation dose of at least 40 Gy; (3) started anti-PD-1 immunotherapy before completion of CRT; and (4) had blood tests results accessible before, during, and one month after CRT. This study was approved by our center’s institutional ethics committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients who underwent RNA sequencing in this study.

Treatment and follow-up

Radiotherapy, chemotherapy and immunotherapy regimens, and follow-up strategies have been described in previous study [18]. All patients underwent chemotherapy before and/or during radiotherapy, including platinum/taxane, platinum/fluorouracil, or platinum/taxane/fluorouracil alone. Disease progression was defined as recurrence in the esophagus or regional lymph nodes or metastases to non-regional lymph nodes or distant organs. Patients who were eligible for surgery, were evaluated for esophagectomy approximately 6–8 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant CRT, and performed Ivor-Lewis esophagectomy.

Data collection

The following factors were evaluated: sex, age, body mass index (BMI), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status, smoking history, histopathological differentiation grade, tumor length, tumor location, clinical TNM stage, and routine peripheral blood test-based immuno-inflammatory biomarkers before, during, and 1 month after CRT. Immuno-inflammatory biomarkers were defined as follows: ALC, absolute lymphocyte count; NLR, absolute neutrophil count/absolute lymphocyte count; PLR, absolute platelet count/absolute lymphocyte count; LMR, absolute lymphocyte count/absolute monocyte count; SIRI, absolute monocyte count × NLR; and systemic immune-inflammation index, absolute platelet count × NLR. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) plots were used to estimate the optimal cut-off values to divide patients into subgroups with high or low immuno-inflammatory levels.

RNA sequencing and gene expression analysis

Baseline frozen tumor samples from 47 patients in this cohort were available for RNA sequencing to further investigate the association between inflammatory biomarkers and the immunogenomic landscape. No comparison between normal and tumor specimens was involved in this study. Methods for transcriptomic sequencing were published in our previous transcriptomics study [19]. Differential expression profiles of genes (DEGs) distinguishing between the high-risk and low-risk subgroups were systematically screened utilizing the R package “limma”, with the selection criteria of |log2 (FoldChange)|>0.58 and P-value < 0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment analysis was conducted on downregulated DEGs. Gene ontology functional network analysis was performed using the R package “enrichplot”. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) analysis was performed with R package “fgsea” using hallmark geneset [20]. Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) and ESTIMATE algorithm were also used to estimate the tumor microenvironment states. Please refer to the previously published article for details on specific methods [19].

Statistical analysis

The baseline patient characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Progression-free survival (PFS) was calculated from the date of pathology-confirmed diagnosis until the time of disease progression, death, or censored at the last follow-up. Kaplan–Meier curves were used to estimate PFS and overall survival (OS), and the log-rank test was used to examine intergroup differences. The relationships between clinical or immuno-inflammatory biomarkers and PFS were assessed using univariate Cox regression analysis. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was used to test for multicollinearity between different immuno-inflammatory biomarkers. Variables with P ≤ 0.10 in univariate analysis were included in a multivariable model, which was then optimized by backwards-stepwise selection. A nomogram was established based on a Cox regression model. The concordance index (C-index), the area under the ROC curve (AUC), calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to validate the nomogram. Bootstrapping validation (800 bootstrap resamples) was performed to evaluate the internal discrimination performance of the nomograms. Patients were then stratified into high- and low-risk groups according to the median value of the total points on the nomogram. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 20.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and R software (R Core Team, Indianapolis, IL, USA, version 4.2.1). P values < 0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Two hundred twelve eligible patients were included in this study, of whom 166 (76.9%) received definitive CRT and 46 (23.1%) received neoadjuvant CRT. Table 1 presents the baseline and treatment characteristics of the entire cohort. The included patients were predominantly male (83.5%, n = 177) with a median age of 60 years (range 41–77 years). Regarding the clinical TNM stage, only 15 (7.0%) patients had Stage I/II, 84 (39.6%) had Stage III, and 113 (53.3%) had Stage IVa. One hundred nineteen patients (56.1%) received induction chemotherapy before concurrent CRT, and all received anti-PD-1 immunotherapy during induction chemotherapy. In addition, 104 patients (49.1%) underwent anti-PD-1 immunotherapy concurrently with CRT, and 75 (35.4%) continued receiving maintenance immunotherapy after CRT. The most commonly used anti-PD-1 inhibitor was toripalimab (77.4%, n = 164), followed by sintilimab (11.8%, n = 25). Association between immuno-inflammatory biomarkers and survival

The median follow-up of the whole cohort was 17.6 months (range, 3.7–40.2 months). Disease progression occurred in 107 patients (50.5%), and 68 (32.1%) died during follow-up. The 1-year OS rate was 82.4%, with a median survival time of 17.6 months. The 1-year PFS rate was 62.0%, with a median survival time of 14.7 months for the entire cohort.

Considering the relatively short follow-up period of this cohort, we examined the association between hematological biomarkers and PFS but not OS. Table 2 summarizes the prognostic factors of PFS. To avoid multicollinearity, Spearman’s correlation analysis of the immuno-inflammatory biomarkers was performed before multivariable analysis. Strong correlation was defined as γ > 0.800 and P < 0.05, and no strong correlation was found between them (Supplementary Fig. 2). All potential prognostic factors were included in the multivariable analysis, and BMI < 18.5 (Hazard Ratio (HR), 1.85; 95% Confidence Interval (CI), 1.05–3.25; P = 0.032), N3 stage (HR, 2.41; 95% CI, 1.37–4.25; P = 0.002), high pre-CRT PLR (HR, 1.53; 95% CI, 1.03–2.28; P = 0.037), low ALC nadir (HR, 1.84; 95% CI, 1.19–2.83; P = 0.006), and high post-CRT NLR (HR, 2.12; 95% CI, 1.32–3.41; P = 0.002) were identified as independent prognostic factors for unfavorable PFS (Table 2). In addition to PFS, survival analysis showed that patients with high pre-CRT PLR and high post-CRT NLR also had significantly worse OS (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Construction and validation of nomogram for predicting PFS

Based on the multivariable regression analysis results, we constructed a nomogram for predicting 1-year and 2-year PFS, which incorporated all five independent risk factors and critical clinical indicators, such as age and T stage (Fig. 2A). The time-dependent ROC curves at 1- and 2-years showed good performance of this model, with corresponding AUC values of 0.76 and 0.70 (Fig. 2B). The C-index of the nomogram was 0.70 (95% CI, 0.67–0.72), and the calibration curve showed acceptable agreement between predictions and actual observations (Fig. 2E). In addition, the DCA showed that the prognostic model had a positive net benefit for almost all threshold probabilities, indicating the potential clinical benefit of this nomogram (Fig. 2C, D).

The patients were further divided into different risk groups based on the median nomogram score (250 points): 124 in the low-risk group and 88 in the high-risk group. Survival analysis showed that patients in the low-risk group had significantly more favorable PFS (P < 0.001; Fig. 2F) and OS (P < 0.001; Fig. 2G). Considering the gender bias in this cohort, we did survival analysis stratified by gender. The results showed that patients in the low-risk group had had better OS (male: P < 0.001; female: P = 0.074; Supplementary Fig. 4A, C) and PFS (male: P < 0.001; female: P < 0.001; Supplementary Fig. 4B, D) in both male and female.

Biological basis of immuno-inflammatory biomarkers predicting PFS

To further investigate the biological basis of immuno-inflammatory biomarkers to predict survival outcomes, pre-treatment biopsy samples for RNA sequencing were collected from 47 patients, of whom 33 and 14 were in the low- and high-risk groups, respectively. In total, 554 transcripts were downregulated, and 266 were upregulated in the high-risk group (Supplementary Figure 4). Downregulated genes were significantly enriched in immune-related pathways, such as leukocyte and T cell activation, proliferation, and migration (Fig. 3B). Gene ontology functional network analysis showed that the differentially enriched genes were mainly clustered in proteoglycan metabolic processes, extracellular matrix organization, and regulation of lymphocyte proliferation functional networks (Fig. 3D). The GSEA plots of RNA-seq shows that high-risk group had downward trend in inflammatory response, NF-κB pathway and interferon γ response (Fig. 3C). In addition, compared to low-risk patients, immune cell infiltration analysis using the ssGSEA method showed significantly lower intratumoral infiltration of gamma delta T cells and natural killer T cells, and the majority of other immune cells showed a consistent trend of low infiltration in high-risk patients (Fig. 3A). The estimated score revealed that patients in the high-risk group had significantly lower stromal scores (P=0.043; Fig. 3E) and a trend toward higher tumor purity (P=0.125; Fig. 3F).

Discussion

With the increasing interest in combining immunotherapy with CRT for esophageal cancer in clinical practice, predicting treatment response and identifying treatment-resistant patients as early as possible is essential. This study found that blood immuno-inflammatory biomarkers such as pre-CRT PLR, ALC nadir during CRT, and post-CRT NLR were significant prognostic factors of survival in patients with locally advanced ESCC treated with CRT combined with anti-PD-1 inhibitors. A nomogram model containing immuno-inflammatory biomarkers and clinicopathological indicators was constructed and internally validated, showing satisfactory prognostic ability. More importantly, high-risk patients identified by the model showed a landscape of lower immune cell differentiation, activation, migration, and less intratumoral infiltration of immune cells, implying an underlying biological mechanism associated with immuno-inflammatory biomarkers.

Lymphocytes play an important role in anti-tumor immunity. Many studies have shown that lymphopenia is significantly associated with adverse prognosis in patients with tumors. However, there is no uniform perspective regarding the choice of a threshold for lymphopenia. In previous studies of lymphopenia in patients with thoracic tumors receiving conventional CRT without immunotherapy, ALC<0.2×103 cells/μL was commonly reported as the threshold [21–23]. While for ESCC patients receiving definitive CRT combined with immunotherapy, ALC<0.33×103 cells/μL has been reported as a strong predictor for unfavorable survival [18]. This is consistent with the results of our study that ALC nadir ≤0.32×103 cells/μL is an independent risk factor for PFS. Patients treated with immunotherapy in combination with CRT had a higher threshold of ALC than patients treated with CRT alone, which could result from immunotherapy slowing the decline in lymphocytes during radiotherapy.

In addition to ALC, this study indicated that pre-CRT PLR and post-CRT NLR could predict the survival outcomes of patients with ESCC receiving CRT and immunotherapy. The potential mechanisms by which blood immuno-inflammatory biomarkers predict prognosis remain unclear. The following studies explain this issue to a certain extent: In the state of the tumor, platelets can mediate tumor metastasis and immune escape [24]. Tumor cells entering the bloodstream can activate platelets, which can release adenine nucleotides to promote the opening of the vascular endothelial barrier to facilitate the metastasis of tumor cells, [25] and can attach to tumor cells through integrins and fibronectin, thus hiding them from the attacks of the immune system [26]. In addition to the physical barrier function, platelets attaching to tumor cells can also transfer inhibitory NK cell receptor ligands to the surface of cancer cells [27]. Moreover, platelet-derived PD-L1 protects PD-L1-negative tumor cells from elimination by T and NK cells [28, 29].These results suggest that not only PLR serves as a marker of systemic inflammatory response reflecting advanced tumor progression, but platelets and lymphocytes and their interaction with each other also influence the disease progression. The analysis of RNA sequencing data in this study revealed a relatively low intratumoral infiltration of NK T cells in high-risk patients, which may also be related to the inhibition of NK cell anti-tumor functions by platelets. Further validation of these findings and exploration of the underlying mechanisms at the tumor tissue and cellular levels are needed.

Post-CRT NLR exhibited the strongest prognostic power for survival among the immuno-inflammatory biomarkers in this study. Tumor cells produce interleukin 1, interleukin 6, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and other cytokines, which can lead to an increase in neutrophils. Neutrophils secrete vascular endothelial growth factors to promote tumor blood vessel formation, release reactive oxygen species and matrix metalloproteinase 9, and alter the tumor microenvironment [30, 31]. In anti-tumor immunity, lymphocytes induce apoptosis, inhibit tumor cell proliferation and migration, and are critical to anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. These combined effects of neutrophil and lymphocyte values were reflected in our finding that a high NLR was associated with short PFS. Radiation has substantial effects on various cellular components in the peripheral blood, such that lymphocytes often decline rapidly during radiotherapy and return to normal levels 1-3 months after the completion of radiotherapy [18, 23]. The NLR level 1 month after radiotherapy laterally demonstrates the recovery ability of the patient's immune system and inflammation clearance capacity.

The LMR, SIRI, and SII are also frequently reported predictors of the prognosis of patients with tumors. Wang et al. found that for lung cancer patients receiving PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, a pre-treatment LMR of <2.3 was associated with shorter OS [32]. Chen et al. concluded that a pre-treatment SIRI of >0.84 was associated with worse survival in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma [33]. Both LMR, SIRI, and SII were factors affecting PFS in the univariate analysis in this study, but they had no significant prognostic impact in the multivariable analysis. Different tumors and stages may lead to different biomarker levels or cut-off values due to different inflammatory conditions. In addition, both LMR and SIRI include the effects of monocytes, which circulate in the bloodstream and migrate into tissues, differentiating into macrophages or dendritic cells that exert anti-tumor or pro-tumor effects. The bidirectional and relatively indirect effects of monocytes on tumors may weaken their prognostic value.

Based on RNA sequencing data, our study also revealed that high-risk patients identified by the nomogram model had a landscape of lower immune cell differentiation, activation, migration, and less intratumoral infiltration of immune cells, which may partly reveal the underlying mechanism by which these hematological immuno-inflammatory biomarkers could predict immunotherapy efficacy. Based on our findings, patients with low-risk ESCC associated with an immune-activated tumor microenvironment and a favorable prognosis might benefit from immunotherapy combined with CRT. In contrast, high-risk patients associated with an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and a considerable risk of disease progression might require more effective adjuvant interventions after CRT, which is worthy of further investigation.

In summary, this study highlights the importance of peripheral blood immune-inflammatory biomarkers in the prognosis of patients with ESCC undergoing chemoradiotherapy. The prognostic model constructed in this study, after further validation, may provide a reference for identifying patients with poor prognosis. Patients identified as high-risk may require more aggressive treatment and more frequent follow-up. In addition, we preliminarily explored the potential mechanisms underlying the predictive role of immune-inflammatory markers on prognosis, providing deeper insights and enhancing the stratification of patients. Future studies should more comprehensively evaluate markers reflecting patients' immune-inflammatory status, explore the mechanisms behind them at a deeper level, search for therapeutic targets, and ultimately guide personalized treatment strategies for esophageal cancer.

This study had some limitations. First, this was a retrospective study, which may have been biased in terms of data collection and patient selection. In addition, this was a single-center study. Although the model constructed in this study had good predictive performance, independent external cohort validation is lacked. Multicenter prospective studies are needed to confirm these findings. Second, since immuno-inflammatory biomarkers in the peripheral blood may be affected by infection, steroid treatment, or other drugs, these confounding factors may influence the model's predictive power. Finally, the short follow-up time of this study limited the exploration of the relationship between immuno-inflammatory biomarkers and OS.

Conclusions

Our findings suggest that a high pre-CRT PLR, low ALC nadir during CRT, and high post-CRT NLR were significantly associated with worse PFS in patients with ESCC who received CRT combined with immunotherapy. A nomogram was built with satisfactory prognostic ability that could be used as a supplement for clinical decision-making. In addition, the potential underlying mechanisms of immuno-inflammatory biomarkers predicting survival may be related to the activation and infiltration of immune cells. Therefore, prospective studies with larger patient cohorts are required for further validation.

Abstract.

Fix
p>2
. We prove that the Euclidean distortion of every
n
-point subset of
L_{p}
is
p^{3}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}+o(1)}
, thus, in particular, demonstrating that all
n
-point subsets of
L_{p}
exhibit an asymptotic improvement over the
O(\log n)
Euclidean distortion guarantee that Bourgain’s embedding theorem provides for arbitrary
n
-point metric spaces. We also prove that the separation modulus of every
n
-point subset of
L_{p}
is
O(p^{2}\sqrt{\log n})
, which is sharp up to the dependence on
p
. We deduce from (a refinement of) this asymptotic evaluation of the finitary separation modulus of
L_{p}
that for any
n
-point subset
\mathscr{C}
of
L_{p}
, any Banach space
\mathbf{Z}
, and any
1
-Lipschitz function
f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathbf{Z}
, there exists a
O(p^{2}\sqrt{\log n})
-Lipschitz function
F:L_{p}\to\mathbf{Z}
that extends
f
. We obtain analogous separation and extension statements for doubling subsets of
L_{p}
.

Keywords.
Metric embeddings; clustering; extension of Lipschitz functions; separating random partitions; doubling metric spaces;
L_{p}
spaces; Mazur map; multiscale analysis and localization.

1.
Introduction

Prior to passing to detailed descriptions of concepts, results, methods and history, we open with the following quick list that describes in broad strokes the main three outcomes of the present article which are resolutions of longstanding open problems in metric embedding theory:

(1)

For every
2<p<\infty
, every
n
-point subset of
L_{p}
is shown to embed into
L_{2}
with bi-Lipschitz distortion that grows to
\infty
as
n\to\infty
asymptotically slower
\log n
. Whether or not this holds has been a well-known open problem ever since the 1985 work
[
16
]
proved that any
n
-point metric space whatsoever embeds into a Hilbert space with distortion
O(\log n)
. In other words, it was unknown (and resolved herein) if—in terms of their bi-Lipschitz embeddability into
L_{2}
— anything can be said about finite subsets of
L_{p}
beyond the fact that they are metric spaces.

(2)

For every
2<p<\infty
, any
n
-point subset
\mathscr{C}
of
L_{p}
, any Banach space
\mathbf{Z}
, and any
1
-Lipschitz function
f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathbf{Z}
, there is an
L
-Lipschitz function
F:L_{p}\to\mathbf{Z}
whose restriction to
\mathscr{C}
coincides with
f
, where
L
grows to
\infty
as
n\to\infty
asymptotically slower than
(\log n)/\log\log n
. Whether or not this holds has been open ever since the 2004 work
[
52
]
proved that this extension statement holds with
L=O((\log n)/\log\log n)
and with
L_{p}
replaced by any metric space whatsoever.

(3)

For every
2<p<\infty
, answering a question that was posed in the 2017 work
[
77
]
, it is proved that the largest possible separation modulus of an
n
-point subset of
L_{p}
is bounded from above and from below by positive multiples (which may depend only on
p
) of
\sqrt{\log n}
. This yields asymptotically optimal randomized clustering of finite subsets of
L_{p}
, where the quality of the clustering is measured by the ratio between the probability that it separates points and their distance, which is an influential method that was introduced
[
12
]
in the mid-1990s in the computer science literature and has since substantially impacted both algorithm design and pure mathematics.

In addition to the above listed answers to known questions, we obtain improved Lipschitz extension and randomized clustering results for doubling subsets of
L_{p}
when
2\leqslant p<\infty
, which are new even when
p=2
but (to the best of our knowledge) they have not been previously posed as open problems. Specifically, setting
C_{p}=p^{2}\sqrt{\log p}
, we prove that if
\mathscr{D}\subseteq L_{p}
is
\uplambda
-doubling for some
\uplambda\geqslant 2
, then for any Banach space
\mathbf{Z}
and any
1
-Lipschitz function
f:\mathscr{D}\to\mathbf{Z}
, there exists a
O(C_{p}\sqrt{\log\uplambda})
-Lipschitz function
F:L_{p}\to\mathbf{Z}
that extends
f
, and furthermore the separation modulus of
\mathscr{D}
is
O(C_{p}\sqrt{\log\uplambda})
.

All of the aforementioned results are proved through an induction on scales and localization argument that we develop herein, in combination with a
novel property of the Mazur map that we introduce.

We will next turn to a more technical (but entirely self-contained) description of the above statements:

1.1.
Bi-Lipschitz embeddings

The Euclidean distortion of a finite metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, which is commonly denoted
\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathcal{M})
following
[
58
]
, is the smallest
D\geqslant 0
such that there exists
f:\mathcal{M}\to L_{2}
satisfying

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\leqslant Dd_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y).

The Euclidean distortion growth
\big\{\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{M})\big\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of an infinite metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is:

(1.1)

\forall n\in\varmathbb{N},\qquad\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\\
|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n\end{subarray}}\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathscr{C}).

One says that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
has nontrivial Euclidean distortion growth if

(1.2)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{M})}{\log n}=0.

The above use of the word “nontrivial” arises from the Bourgain embedding theorem
[
16
]
, which asserts that
\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(\mathcal{M})=O(\log n)
for
every
metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
. A well-known open question (which has been prominently on researchers’ minds ever since
[
16
]
appeared but to the best of our knowledge was not stated in print) is whether
L_{p}
has nontrivial Euclidean distortion growth for every
2<p<\infty
.

L_{p}
will always be the space of (equivalence classes of)

L_{p}(\mu)
space; this follows formally as any separable

(see e.g.

spaces. Other such standard Banach space-theoretic notations and conventions that will be used herein are according to

.
In other words, in terms of their bi-Lipschitz embeddability into a Hilbert space, can anything be said about finite subsets of
L_{p}
beyond merely that they are metric spaces? Here we prove that the answer is affirmative:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
2<p<\infty
, then
L_{p}
has nontrivial Euclidean distortion growth. More precisely,

If

Conjecture 1.2
.

The bound (

) gives
\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})=o(\log n)
when

\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})=o(\log n)

1.1.1.
Toward a sharp Euclidean distortion growth rate for
L_{p}

Now that it is established that
L_{p}
has nontrivial Euclidean distortion growth for every
1\leqslant p<\infty
, one can turn attention to determining the growth rate of
\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})
as
n\to\infty
, which is currently known only for
p\in\{1,2\}
. This seems to be a highly nontrivial matter. The Lewis theorem
[
54
]
asserts that
\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathbf{X})\leqslant k^{|1/2-1/p|}
for every
k
-dimensional subspace
\mathbf{X}
of
L_{p}
(this upper bound holds as equality when
\mathbf{X}=\ell_{\!\!p}^{k}
; see e.g.
[
95
,
43
]
). In accordance with the longstanding Ribe research program
[
17
,
44
,
74
,
10
,
90
,
89
,
34
,
78
]
), this naturally leads to the following open question:

Question 1.3

(Nonlinear Lewis problem)
.

Is it true that for every
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
we have

(1.4)

\forall n\in\{2,3,\ldots\},\qquad\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})\asymp_{p}(\log n)^{\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}.

In fact, it is not even known whether (
1.4
) holds for any fixed
p\in[1,\infty]\smallsetminus\{1,2,\infty\}
.

We expect that answering Question
1.3
would be very challenging (based in part on how the only known nontrivial cases
p\in\{1,\infty\}
of Question
1.3
were resolved), especially if the answer is positive, which would likely be a major achievement that would entail introducing a significant new idea/embedding method.

The challenge within Question
1.3
is to prove the upper bound on
\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})
in (
1.4
), as the lower bound

(1.5)

\forall n\in\{2,3,\ldots,\},\qquad\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})\gtrsim(\log n)^{\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}.

follows from known results (observe that thanks to (
1.5
) our new upper bound (
1.3
) is optimal up to lower order factors whenever
p=(\log n)^{o(1)}\to\infty
). To justify (
1.5
), if
1\leqslant p<2
, then for each
k\in\varmathbb{N}
consider the
n
-point hypercube
\{0,1\}^{k}
as a subset of
\ell_{\!\!p}^{k}
, thus
n=|\{0,1\}^{n}|=2^{k}
. By
[
31
]
its Euclidean distortion is

\mathsf{c}_{2}\big(\{0,1\}^{k},\|\cdot\|_{\!\ell_{\!\!p}^{k}}\big)=k^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}\asymp(\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}},

which shows that (
1.5
) holds when
n
is a power of
2
; it is straightforward to deduce from this that (
1.5
) holds for every integer
n\geqslant 2
(e.g., by augmenting the hypercube with auxiliary very distant points that form an equilateral metric). If
p\geqslant 2
, then by
[
87
]
there is an
n
-vertex connected planar graph
\mathsf{G}_{n}
, equipped with its shortest-path metric, whose Euclidean distortion satisfies
\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathsf{G}_{n})\gtrsim\sqrt{\log n}
. As
p\geqslant 2
, the proof of the main result of
[
91
]
(see also the exposition in
[
36
,
47
]
) shows that there is an embedding
f
of
\mathsf{G}_{n}
(indeed, of any connected planar graph) into
L_{p}
whose bi-Lipschitz distortion is at most a universal constant multiple of
(\log n)^{1/p}
; the image
f(\mathsf{G}_{n})\subseteq L_{p}
exhibits the validity of (
1.5
) in the remaining range
p\geqslant 2
.

The lower bound (
1.5
) is only part of the picture, as there
must
be some dependence on
p
in (
1.4
) that diverges as
p\to\infty
, which demonstrates that there is a qualitative difference between the nonlinear setting of Question
1.3
and its aforementioned linear counterpart
[
54
]
. Indeed, consider any
n
-point metric space
\mathcal{M}_{n}
with
\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathcal{M}_{n})\gtrsim\log n
, which exists thanks to
[
58
,
8
]
. Because
\mathcal{M}_{n}
embeds isometrically into
\ell_{\!\!\infty}^{n}
, while
\ell_{\!\!\infty}^{n}
embeds with
O(1)
distortion into
L_{\Omega(\log n)}
, it follows that for
p\asymp\log n
we necessarily have

(1.6)

\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})\gtrsim\textstyle{\sqrt{\min\{p,\log n\}}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}}.

This suggests (but does not prove!) that perhaps (
1.6
) holds for all
p>2
. If so, then it would follow that a power-type dependence on
p
in our new bound (
1.3
) is unavoidable if
\alpha(\log\log n)/\log\log\log n\leqslant p\lesssim\log n
for some fixed
\alpha>2
, e.g., combining (
1.3
) and (
1.6
) gives
\sqrt{(\log n)\log\log n}\lesssim\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{\log\log n})\lesssim\sqrt{\log n}(\log\log n)^{3}
.

1.2.
Lipschitz extension

Given a (source) metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, a subset
\mathscr{C}
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
|\mathscr{C}|\geqslant 2
, and a (target) metric space
(\mathcal{T},d_{\mathcal{T}})
, one denotes (following
[
64
]
) by
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{C};\mathcal{T})\in[1,\infty]
the infimum over those
K>0
such that for every function
f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathcal{T}
there exists a function
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{T}
whose restriction to
\mathscr{C}
coincides with
f
, and the Lipschitz constant of
F
satisfies:

(1.7)

\|F\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})}\leqslant K\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathscr{C};\mathcal{T})},

where (
1.7
) uses the following notation for Lipschitz constants, which will occur throughout what follows:

\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathscr{C};\mathcal{T})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\mathscr{C}\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{d_{\mathcal{T}}\left(f(x),f(y)\right)}{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}.

The supremum of
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{C};\mathcal{T})
over all the subsets
\mathscr{C}
of
\mathcal{M}
containing at least two points is denoted
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})
.

For each integer
n\geqslant 2
one defines
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
to be the supremum of
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{C};\mathbf{Z})
over all
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
satisfying
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n
, and all Banach spaces
(\mathbf{Z},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Z}})
.
By
[
53
, Theorem 1.10]
, every metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
satisfies:

(1.8)

\forall n\in\{3,4,\ldots\},\qquad\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})\lesssim\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}.

Remark 1.4
.

Given a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, its Lipschitz extension modulus
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M})
is defines to be the supremum of
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{C};\mathbf{Z})
over all
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
|\mathscr{C}|\geqslant 2
and all Banach spaces
(\mathbf{Z},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Z}})
. When the (typical) situation
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M})=\infty
occurs, one can consider analogously to (
1.1
) the finitary invariant
\mathsf{e}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
which is defined for each
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
to be the supremum of
\mathsf{e}(\mathscr{C})
over all subsets
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n
.
3
3
3
More generally, we will maintain the following notational convention. Given an invariant
\mathsf{i}(\mathcal{M})\in\varmathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
of metric spaces
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, for each
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
the superscript notation
\mathsf{i}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
is reserved for the supremum of
\mathsf{i}(\mathscr{C})
over
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n
. In contrast, the subscript notation
\mathsf{i}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
is used less consistently in the literature to denote more subtle finitary invariants that still consider arbitrary subsets
\mathscr{C}
of
\mathcal{M}
with
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n
, but do not depend only on the intrinsic geometry of
(\mathscr{C},d_{\mathcal{M}})
.
It is important to stress the difference between
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{e}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
, namely,
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
measures the extent to which for any
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n
, any Lipschitz function from
\mathscr{C}
to a Banach space can be extended to a Lipschitz function on
\mathcal{M}
, while
\mathsf{e}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
disregards how
\mathscr{C}
is situated in the super-space
\mathcal{M}
, asking for such extensions to
\mathscr{C}
of Banach space-valued functions from arbitrary subsets of
\mathscr{C}
. While
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
was studied in the literature for a long time (starting with
[
42
]
, inspired by
[
35
,
55
]
), to the best of our knowledge
\mathsf{e}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
was not considered before. Nevertheless,
\mathsf{e}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
has a key role in our investigations herein; see Section
1.3.2
.

By
[
53
, Theorem 1.12]
for every
1<p\leqslant 2
we have:

(1.9)

\forall n\in\{2,3,\ldots,\},\qquad\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\lesssim_{p}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The following Lipschitz extension theorem treats the analog of (
1.9
) in the previously unknown range
p>2
:

Theorem 1.5
.

If
2<p<\infty
and
n\in\{2,3,\ldots,\}
, then

\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\lesssim p^{2}{\textstyle\sqrt{\log n}}.

Theorem
1.5
answers the natural question that was left open by
[
53
]
(and stated elsewhere, e.g.
[
82
]
) whether for every (indeed, any) fixed
2<p<\infty
we have

(1.10)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}=0.

In other words, prior to Theorem
1.5
it was not even known if one could provide an upper bound on
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})
that is asymptotically better than merely using the fact that
L_{p}
is a metric space through (
1.8
).

Remark 1.6
.

We hesitate to call (
1.10
) “nontrivial extension growth” for
L_{p}
partly due to the distinction that is noted in Remark
1.4
, and mainly because in contrast to the analogous setting in (
1.2
), it is unknown if (
1.8
) is asymptotically sharp, nor is it conjectured that this is the case (though, it could very well be so). Determining the largest possible growth rate as
n\to\infty
of
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
over all metric spaces
\mathcal{M}
is a major open question for which the currently best-known lower bound
[
82
]
is that
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathcal{M})
must sometimes be at least a positive universal constant multiple of
\sqrt{\log n}
. In other words, the currently best-known bounds on
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{\infty})
are
\sqrt{\log n}\lesssim\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{\infty})\lesssim(\log n)/\log\log n
. For finite
p
, at present there is insufficient information for there to be a widely accepted conjecture what the sharp asymptotic dependence as
n\to\infty
should be in Theorem
1.5
, as well as in the estimate (
1.9
) of
[
53
]
. The best-available bounds in this context are presented in Section
1.2.1
below, from which it follows that if
p
is allowed to depend on
n
so that it tends to
\infty
at a
(\log n)^{o(1)}
rate, then Theorem
1.5
is sharp up to lower order factors; e.g. we now know that:

{\textstyle\sqrt{\log n}}\lesssim\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{\log\log n})\lesssim{\textstyle\sqrt{\log n}}(\log\log n)^{2}.

Conceivably
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\asymp_{p}\sqrt{\log n}
or even
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\asymp\sqrt{\log n}
for all
p\geqslant 2
, but proving this would be a spectacular achievement; in particular, the former statement would settle the Hilbertian case
p=2

The implicit constant in (

1.2.1.
Known Lipschitz extension impossibility results

Unlike the discussion in Section
1.1.1
, there is no widely accepted conjectural growth rate (as
n\to\infty
) for
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})
when
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
is fixed; this growth rate is not even known in the Hilbertian setting
p=2
. A naïve appeal to
[
54
]
from the perspective of the Ribe program leads to the prediction
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\asymp_{p}(\log n)^{|1/2-1/p|}
. This was indeed a possibility for quite some time (see e.g. the questions posed in
[
42
,
9
]
for
p=2
), until it was shown in
[
73
]
to fail even when
p=2
.

The best-known lower bound in the range
2\leqslant p\leqslant 4
is
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\gtrsim\sqrt[4]{(\log n)/\log\log n}
. In fact, for every infinite dimensional Banach space
\mathbf{X}
we have
\mathsf{e}_{n}(\mathbf{X})\gtrsim\sqrt[4]{(\log n)/\log\log n}
because by
[
71
]
(see also
[
73
]
),

(1.11)

\forall m\in\varmathbb{N},\qquad\mathsf{e}_{m^{O(m)}}\!(\ell_{\!\!2}^{m})\gtrsim\sqrt[4]{m},

while for all
m\in\varmathbb{N}
by Dvoretzky’s theorem
[
30
]

\ell_{\!\!2}^{m}
is
O(1)
-isomorphic to a subspace of
\mathbf{X}
.

For the remaining values of
p
, the best-known lower bounds are
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{\infty})\gtrsim\sqrt{\log n}
by
[
82
]
, and
\mathsf{e}_{n}(L_{p})\gtrsim((\log n)/\log\log n)^{1/2-1/p}
for
p\in[1,2)\cup(2,\infty)
; if
1\leqslant p<2
, then this coincides with
[
41
, Theorem 3]
, and if
p>2
, then it also follows from the reasoning in
[
41
]
, though it is not stated there explicitly. Indeed, by
[
94
]
for every
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
and every
m\in\varmathbb{N}
there is a linear subspace
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{Y}_{p}
of
\ell_{\!\!p}^{m}
such that
\|\mathsf{Proj}\|_{\ell_{\!\!p}^{m}\to\ell_{\!\!p}^{m}}\gtrsim m^{|1/2-1/p|}
for every projection
\mathsf{Proj}
from
\ell_{\!\!p}^{m}
onto
\mathbf{Y}
. By
[
55
]
, this implies:

(1.12)

\mathsf{e}(\ell_{\!p}^{m},\mathbf{Y};\mathbf{Y})\gtrsim m^{\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|}.

The discretization method of
[
41
]
deduces from (
1.12
) that there is a subset
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}_{p}
of
\ell_{\!\!p}^{m}
with

(specifically,
\mathcal{N}
is a
(1/m^{O(1)}
)-net of the unit sphere of
\mathbf{Y}

\mathsf{e}(\ell_{\!\!p}^{m},\mathcal{N};\mathbf{Y})\gtrsim m^{|1/2-1/p|}

1.2.2.
Lipschitz extension from doubling subsets

Given
\uplambda\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
, a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is
\uplambda
-doubling if for every
r\geqslant 0
and every
x\in\mathcal{M}
there exist
y_{1},\ldots,y_{\uplambda}\in\mathcal{M}
with
B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,2r)\subseteq B_{\mathcal{M}}(y_{1},r)\cup\ldots\cup B_{\mathcal{M}}(y_{\uplambda},r)
.
6
6
6
The notion of
\uplambda
-doubling when
\uplambda\geqslant 2
is not necessarily an integer coincides with
\lfloor\uplambda\rfloor
-doubling.
Here, as well as throughout the ensuing discussion,

B_{\mathcal{M}}(u,\rho)=\{v\in\mathcal{M}:\ d_{\mathcal{M}}(u,v)\leqslant\rho\}
denotes the
closed

d_{\mathcal{M}}
-ball centered at a point
u\in\mathcal{M}
of radius
\rho\geqslant 0
.

By
[
53
, Theorem 1.6]
, for every metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, for every
\uplambda
-doubling subset
\mathscr{D}
of
\mathcal{M}
, and for every Banach space
(\mathbf{Z},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Z}})
we have

(1.13)

\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{D};\mathbf{Z})\lesssim\log\uplambda.

It is an important open problem to determine if in the above stated generality the right hand side of (
1.13
) can be reduced to
o(\log\uplambda)
as
\uplambda\to\infty
. It is also natural to investigate if such an improvement could be achieved for specific metric spaces
\mathcal{M}
, though to the best of our knowledge this has not been previously posed as an open question. In particular, it was not known if (
1.13
) could be improved when
\mathcal{M}
is a Hilbert space; this is answered as the special case
p=2
of the following theorem:

Theorem 1.9
.

If
2\leqslant p<\infty
, then the following estimate holds for every
\uplambda\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
, for every subset
\mathscr{D}
of
L_{p}
that is
\uplambda
-doubling, and for every Banach space
(\mathbf{Z},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Z}})
:

(1.14)

\mathsf{e}(L_{p},\mathscr{D};\mathbf{Z})\lesssim\Big(p^{2}{\textstyle\sqrt{\log p}}\Big){\textstyle\sqrt{\log\uplambda}}.

We expect that the right hand side of (
1.13
) could be improved to
o(\log\uplambda)
also in the remaining range
1\leqslant p<2
, i.e., for every
\uplambda\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
, for every subset
\mathscr{D}
of
L_{p}
that is
\uplambda
-doubling, and for every Banach space
(\mathbf{Z},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Z}})
, we conjecture that
\mathsf{e}(L_{p},\mathscr{D};\mathbf{Z})=o(\log\uplambda)
. The case
p=1
here is most tenuous in terms of available methods and evidence. When
1<p<2
, an attempt to combine our approach herein with the proof of (
1.9
) in
[
53
]
leads to probabilistic issues that we currently do not know how to address but they could be quite tractable. In terms of the best-known lower bounds in the context of Theorem
1.9
, as every finite dimensional normed space
\mathbf{Y}
is
\uplambda
-doubling for
\uplambda=e^{O(\dim(\mathbf{Y}))}
, it follows from (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) that for every
\uplambda\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
and every
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
there exists a
\uplambda
-doubling subset
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{D}_{p}
of
L_{p}
and a Banach space
\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{Z}_{p}
for which
\mathsf{e}(L_{p},\mathscr{D};\mathbf{Z})\gtrsim\sqrt[4]{\log\uplambda}
if
2\leqslant p\leqslant 4
and
\mathsf{e}(L_{p},\mathscr{D};\mathbf{Z})\gtrsim(\log\uplambda)^{|1/2-1/p|}
if
p\in[1,2)\cup(4,\infty]
.

1.3.
Randomized clustering

Given a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and a partition
\mathscr{P}
of
\mathcal{M}
, denote for each
x\in\mathcal{M}
the element of
\mathscr{P}
containing
x
by
\mathscr{P}(x)
. For
\Delta>0
, a partition
\mathscr{P}
of
\mathcal{M}
is said to be
\Delta
-bounded if
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{P}(x))\leqslant\Delta
for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Here, as well as throughout the ensuing discussion, the
d_{\mathcal{M}}
-diameter of
\emptyset\neq\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
will be denoted
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{C})=\sup_{x,y\in\mathscr{C}}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\in[0,\infty]
.

Following
[
80
]
, one says that
\mathscr{P}=\{\Gamma^{1},\Gamma^{2},\ldots\}
is a
random partition
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
if there exists a probability space
(\Omega,\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}})
such that
\Gamma^{1}:\Omega\to 2^{\mathcal{M}},\Gamma^{2}:\Omega\to 2^{\mathcal{M}},\ldots
is a sequence of set-valued mappings that are strongly measurable,
7
7
7
This notion of measurability of set-valued functions is called here “strongly measurable” even though parts of the literature calls it more simply “measurable” to distinguish it from the notion of a “weakly measurable” set-valued function which is also commonly used in the literature but is
not
what we need herein. See
[
39
]
for a treatment of these classical concepts.
namely, for every
i\in\varmathbb{N}
and every
closed
subset
E
of
\mathcal{M}
the set
\{\omega\in\Omega:\ E\cap\Gamma^{i}(\omega)\neq\emptyset\}
is
\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}
-measurable, and if we write
\mathscr{P}^{\omega}=\{\Gamma^{k}(\omega):\ k\in\varmathbb{N}\}
for each
\omega\in\Omega
, then the mapping
\omega\mapsto\mathscr{P}^{\omega}
takes values in partitions of
\mathcal{M}
. Note that we are formally discussing here random
ordered
partitions of
\mathcal{M}
into countably many clusters, but this nuance will not have a role in what follows (it is important only for some of the results from the literature that we will need to quote). Measurability is not relevant when
\mathcal{M}
is finite, which is the setting that we will initially discuss below, but we will quickly need to also treat random partitions of infinite spaces, at which point we will verify measurability as required.

Given
\Delta>0
, one says that
\mathscr{P}
is a
\Delta
-bounded random partition of
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
if
\mathscr{P}^{\omega}
is a
\Delta
-bounded partition of
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
for every
\omega\in\Omega
. Given
\sigma\geqslant 0
, a random
\Delta
-bounded partition
\mathscr{P}
of a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is said to be
\sigma
-separating if the following requirement holds:

(1.15)

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\big[\mathscr{P}(x)\neq\mathscr{P}(y)\big]\leqslant\frac{\sigma}{\Delta}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y).

The separation modulus of
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, denoted
\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{M})
, is the infimum over
\sigma\geqslant 0
such that for every
\Delta>0
there exists a random
\Delta
-bounded
\sigma
-separating partition
\mathscr{P}_{\!\!\Delta}
of
\mathcal{M}
; if no such random partition exists, then set
\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{M})=\infty
. This important concept has been introduced by
[
12
]
, see
[
80
]
for the history.

For an infinite metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, define its separation growth
\big\{\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(\mathcal{M})\big\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
by

\forall n\in\varmathbb{N},\qquad\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(\mathcal{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\\
|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n\end{subarray}}\mathsf{SEP}(\mathscr{C}).

We say that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
has nontrivial separation growth if

(1.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(\mathcal{M})}{\log n}=0.

The term “nontrivial” is used here since it was proved in
[
12
]
that
every
metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
satisfies
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(\mathcal{M})=O(\log n)
.

By
[
23
,
51
,
80
]
, if
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
, then
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})=o(\log n)
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}(p-1)\frac{\log\log n}{\log\log\log n}=\infty,

while if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}(p-1)\log\log n<\infty
, then
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})\gtrsim\log n
. See
[
80
, Section 1.7.6]
for more on this (including sharp bounds for fixed
1<p\leqslant 2
), where it is conjectured that
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})=o(\log n)
if and only if
\lim_{n\to\infty}(p-1)\log\log n=\infty
.

It was asked in
[
77
, Question 1]
(reiterated in
[
80
, Question 83]
) if
L_{p}
has nontrivial separation growth when
2<p<\infty
(see
[
80
, Section 1.7.6]
for the relation to metric dimension reduction). This question was answered affirmatively in
[
48
]
by showing that
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})\lesssim(\log n)^{1-1/p}
for every
(p,n)\in(2,\infty)\times\varmathbb{N}
. It was also asked in
[
77
,
80
]
if, in fact, for every
(p,n)\in(2,\infty)\times\varmathbb{N}
we have
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})\lesssim_{p}\sqrt{\log n}
, which would be asymptotically optimal as
n\to\infty
for every fixed
2<p<\infty
(see below). Here we prove that this sharp evaluation of the largest possible separation modulus of an
n
-point subset of
L_{p}
indeed holds:

Theorem 1.10
.

For every
2<p<\infty
and every
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
we have
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})\asymp_{p}\sqrt{\log n}
. More precisely,

(1.17)

\forall(p,n)\in(2,\infty)\times\varmathbb{N},\qquad{\textstyle\sqrt{\log n}}\lesssim\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})\lesssim p^{2}{\textstyle\sqrt{\log n}}.

The new content of (
1.17
) is its upper bound on
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})
; the lower bound on
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})
in (
1.17
) holds since
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{2})\asymp\sqrt{\log n}
, by
[
23
]
, while
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(\mathbf{X})\geqslant\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{2})
for every infinite dimensional Banach space
\mathbf{X}
, by Dvoretzky’s theorem
[
30
]
. So, we now know that
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})=o(\log n)
as
n\to\infty
if
2<p=o\left(\sqrt[4]{\log n}\right)
, and it fails if
p\gtrsim\log n
because in that case
\ell_{\!\!\infty}^{n}
embeds with distortion
O(1)
into
L_{p}
, any
n
-point metric space
\mathcal{M}
embeds into
\ell_{\!\!\infty}^{n}
, and we already recalled that by
[
12
]
there exists such a space for which
\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{M})\gtrsim\log n
. It remains open (likely requiring a substantially new idea) to understand what happens in the remaining range
\sqrt[4]{\log n}\lesssim p=o(\log n)
.

Remark 1.11
.

The first arXiv posting
[
83
]
of our work (which the present article supersedes) suppressed the dependence on
p
in Theorem
1.10
because the main matter at hand is determining the asymptotic growth rate of

as

; this is what was asked in
[

]
and what Theorem

answers. Nevertheless, understanding the dependence on

is of value in its own right (partially because in applications sometimes
p
itself is allowed to depend on

\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})\leqslant e^{O(p)}\sqrt{\log n}

]

]

We will also prove the following theorem for doubling subsets of
L_{p}
:

Theorem 1.12
.

If
p,\uplambda\geqslant 2
, then every
\uplambda
-doubling subset
\mathscr{D}
of

\mathsf{SEP}(\mathscr{D})\lesssim\Big(p^{2}{\textstyle\sqrt{\log p}}\Big){\textstyle\sqrt{\log\uplambda}.}

1.3.1.
From separation of neighborhoods to Lipschitz extension

The similarity between the conclusions of Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.5
, as well as Theorem
1.12
and Theorem
1.9
, are not coincidental. The link is provided by Theorem
1.13
below, which we will deduce quickly in Section
7
from
[
53
]
(with input from
[
80
]
).

In the formulation of Theorem
1.13
, as well as throughout the ensuing discussions, we will use the following (natural but nonstandard) notation. Given a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, for every
r\geqslant 0
we will denote the
r
-neighborhood
\mathscr{C}
in
\mathcal{M}
by:

(1.18)

B_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{C},r)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\bigcup_{x\in\mathscr{C}}B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r).

Given
\Delta>0
we will denote by
\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{M})
the infimum over
\sigma\geqslant 0
such that there is a random
\Delta
-bounded
\sigma
-separating partition of
\mathcal{M}
(again, if no such random partition exists, then write
\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{M})=\infty
). Thus,

\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{M})=\sup_{\Delta>0}\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}(\mathcal{M}).

Theorem 1.13
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is a metric space and that
\mathscr{C}\neq\emptyset
is a locally compact subset of
\mathcal{M}
. Then, the following Lipschitz extension estimate holds for every Banach space
(\mathbf{Z},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Z}})
and every
L>0
:

(1.19)

\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{C};\mathbf{Z})\lesssim L+\sup_{\Delta>0}\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}\Big(B_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathscr{C},\frac{1}{L}\Delta\big)\Big).

Our main contribution to randomized clustering of (neighborhoods of) subsets of
L_{p}
is:

Theorem 1.14
.

There exists a universal constant
\gamma>0
with the following property. Suppose that
2\leqslant p<\infty
. For every
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
, if
\mathscr{C}
is an
n
-point subset of
L_{p}
, then for every
\Delta>0
we have:

(1.20)

\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}\Big(B_{L_{p}}\big(\mathscr{C},\frac{\gamma}{p}\Delta\big)\Big)\lesssim p^{2}{\textstyle\sqrt{\log n}}.

Furthermore, for every
\uplambda\geqslant 2
, if
\mathscr{D}
is a subset of
L_{p}
that is
\uplambda
-doubling, then for every
\Delta>0
we have:

(1.21)

\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}\Big(B_{L_{p}}\big(\mathscr{D},\frac{\gamma}{p}\Delta\big)\Big)\lesssim\Big(p^{2}{\textstyle\sqrt{\log p}}\Big){\textstyle\sqrt{\log\uplambda}}.

Because any neighborhood of a set contains the set itself, Theorem
1.14
strengthens (the upper bound on
\mathsf{SEP}^{n}(L_{p})
in) Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.12
. Furthermore, Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.9
follow from Theorem
1.14
by invoking Theorem
1.13
. Indeed, any Lipschitz function from subset of a metric space that take values in a complete metric space automatically extends to the closure of its domain, so in Theorem
1.14
we may assume that
\mathscr{D}
is a closed subset of
L_{p}
. As
\mathscr{D}
is also assumed in Theorem
1.14
to be doubling, it is locally compact (see e.g.
[
38
, Lemma 4.1.14]
, or notice that any ball in
\mathscr{D}
is compact because it is totally bounded and complete). The assumptions of Theorem
1.13
therefore hold with
L\asymp p
by Theorem
1.14
. Note here that even when
\mathscr{C}\subseteq L_{p}
is finite, the above justification of Theorem
1.5
involves random partitions of infinite sets (neighborhoods of
\mathscr{C}
in
L_{p}
), for which measurability considerations are pertinent.

1.3.2.
From separation and extension to Euclidean embedding

The link between Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.1
is furnished by Theorem
1.15
below, which is an embedding statement of independent interest; one can view it as a variant of the “measured descent” embedding method
[
47
]
that incorporates separating partitions rather than the padded partitions that occur in
[
47
]
. The proof of Theorem
1.15
, which appears in Section
2
, combines multiple ingredients, many of which (but not all) refine the reasoning in
[
5
]
.

For Theorem
1.15
, recall that
\mathsf{e}^{k}(\mathcal{M})=\sup\{\mathsf{e}(\mathscr{C}):\ \mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\ \wedge\ |\mathscr{C}|\leqslant k\}
was defined in Remark
1.4
.

Theorem 1.15
.

For every
n\in\{3,4,\ldots\}
, every
n
-point metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
satisfies

(1.22)

\mathsf{c}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\lesssim{\textstyle\sqrt{(\log n)\log\log n}}\bigg(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{\mathsf{SEP}^{k}(\mathcal{M})^{2}\mathsf{e}^{k}(\mathcal{M};L_{2})^{4}}{k(\log k)^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For every

. A substitution of these two estimates into Theorem

\displaystyle\mathsf{c}_{2}^{n}(L_{p})\lesssim{\textstyle\sqrt{(\log n)\log\log n}}

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=2}^{n}\frac{p^{4}(\log k)\cdot p^{2}}{k(\log k)^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

1.3.3.
Localization and induction on scales, and a radial property of the Mazur map

The purpose of this section is to provide an overview of the key ingredients of our proof of Theorem
1.14
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is a metric space and
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
. The
d_{\mathcal{M}}
-(circum)radius of
\mathscr{C}
is defined as follows:

(1.23)

\mathrm{rad}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{C})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\inf\big\{r\in[0,\infty]:\ \exists x\in\mathcal{M}\ \mathrm{such\ that\ }B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)\supseteq\mathscr{C}\big\},

Given
\Delta>0
, we will say that a random partition
\mathscr{P}
of
\mathscr{C}
is radially
\Delta
-bounded with respect to
\mathcal{M}
if:

(1.24)

\forall x\in\mathscr{C},\qquad\mathrm{rad}_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\mathscr{P}(x)\big)\leqslant\Delta.

A random partition
(\omega\in\Omega)\mapsto\mathscr{P}^{\omega}
of
\mathscr{C}
, defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}})
, is radially
\Delta
-bounded with respect to
\mathcal{M}
if
\mathrm{rad}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{P}^{\omega}(x))\leqslant\Delta
for every
\omega\in\Omega
and every
x\in\mathscr{C}
. Given a random partition
\mathscr{P}
of
\mathscr{C}
that is radially
\Delta
-bounded with respect to
\mathcal{M}
, say that it is
\sigma
-separating for some
\sigma\geqslant 0
if (
1.15
) holds.

Denote by
\widehat{\mathsf{SEP}}_{\Delta}(\mathscr{C};\mathcal{M})
the infimum over
\sigma\geqslant 0
such that there exists a
\sigma
-separating random partition of
\mathscr{C}
that is radially
\Delta
-bounded with respect to
\mathcal{M}
(as always, when no such partition exists we define this parameter to be
\infty
).
Recalling that
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
denotes the
d_{\mathcal{M}}
-diameter, the following bounds hold:

(1.25)

\forall\emptyset\neq\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M},\qquad\mathrm{rad}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{C})\leqslant\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{C})\leqslant 2\mathrm{rad}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{C}).

Consequently, the following simple general relations between the separation and radial separation moduli are satisfied for every metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, every
\emptyset\neq\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, and every
\Delta>0
:

(1.26)

\widehat{\mathsf{SEP}}_{\Delta}(\mathscr{C};\mathcal{M})\leqslant\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}(\mathscr{C})\leqslant 2\widehat{\mathsf{SEP}}_{\frac{\Delta}{2}}(\mathscr{C};\mathcal{M}),

Even though the above radial variants of the notions of random
\Delta
-bounded and
\sigma
-separating partitions may seem to be nuanced minor tweaks of the standard (by now classical) definitions, we will see that they influence our results in a way that is more dramatic than what one might initially expect.

The following lemma is a localization and induction on scales principle for random radial separation:

Lemma 1.16
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is a separable metric space and that
\emptyset\neq\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
satisfies

(1.27)

\lim_{\Delta\to\infty}\frac{1}{\Delta}\mathsf{SEP}_{\Delta}(\mathscr{C})=0.

Then, the following estimate holds for every
\Delta>0
and
K>1
:

(1.28)

\widehat{\mathsf{SEP}}_{\Delta}(\mathscr{C};\mathcal{M})\leqslant\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{K^{s}}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{M}}\widehat{\mathsf{SEP}}_{K^{s}\Delta}\big(\mathscr{C}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(z,K^{s+1}\Delta+\varepsilon);\mathcal{M}\big).

The limits in (
1.28
) exist as the summands are nondecreasing with
\varepsilon
. We used the term “localization” to describe Lemma
1.16
as the
s
-summand in (
1.28
) depends only on the intersection
\mathscr{C}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(z,K^{s+1}\Delta+\varepsilon)
, which is a “local snapshot” of
\mathscr{C}
. We used the term “induction on scales” to describe Lemma
1.16
as the left hand side of (
1.28
) treats partitions at scale
\Delta
while the right hand side of (
1.28
) considers partitions at an increasing sequence of larger scales. Our proof of Lemma
1.16
is an iterative use of the following observation. One can partition
\mathscr{C}
into clusters of radius at most
\Delta
by first partitioning it into clusters of radius at most
K\Delta
; each of those clusters is thus contained in a ball of radius
K\Delta+\varepsilon
, so we can proceed to refine the aforementioned initial partition by partitioning each of the enclosing balls into pieces of radius at most
\Delta
.

A standard tool for analysing the geometry
L_{p}
is the classical Mazur map
[
67
]
to
L_{2}
(and shifts thereof), whose restriction to balls has well-understood (and widely-used) quite favorable uniform continuity properties, i.e., it is well-behaved on the local snapshots of
\mathscr{C}
that appear in the right hand side of (
1.28
).

For
1\leqslant p,q<\infty
, the Mazur map
M_{p\to q}:L_{p}\to L_{q}
is defined
[
67
]
by:

(1.29)

\forall\phi\in L_{p},\ \forall t\in[0,1],\qquad M_{p\to q}(\phi)(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}|\phi(t)|^{\frac{p}{q}}\operatorname{sign}\big(\phi(t)\big).

If
p>2
, then
M_{p\to 2}
is Lipschitz by
[
67
]
, so we may consider the following normalization of the Mazur map that makes it be a
1
-Lipschitz function from
L_{p}
into the Hilbert space
L_{2}
:

(1.30)

\widetilde{M}_{p\to 2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\|M_{p\to 2}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(L_{p};L_{2})}}M_{p\to 2}.

The normalization factor in (
1.30
) satisfies
\|M_{p\to 2}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(L_{p};L_{2})}<p
, as computed in
[
75
, equation (5.32)]
.

We will prove herein that there exists a universal constant
\beta>0
such that the following inclusion holds:

(1.31)

\forall\phi\in B_{L_{p}},\qquad\big(\widetilde{M}_{p\to 2}\big)^{-1}\bigg(B_{L_{2}}\Big(\widetilde{M}_{p\to 2}(\phi),\frac{\beta}{p}\Big)\bigg)\subseteq B_{L_{p}}\Big(\frac{1}{p}\phi,1-\frac{1}{4p}\Big),

where
B_{L_{p}}
is the ball in
L_{p}
of radius
1
centered at the
0
-function; more generally, the unit ball centered at the origin of a Banach space
(\mathbf{X},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}})
will always be denoted below by
B_{\mathbf{X}}=\{x\in\mathbf{X}:\ \|x\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leqslant 1\}=B_{\mathbf{X}}(0,1)
.

The upshot of (
1.31
) is that the restriction of
\widetilde{M}_{p\to 2}
to

gives a
1
-Lipschitz function
f
into a Hilbert space with the property that for any point

, the pullback under
f
of the Hilbertian ball centered at
f(x)
of radius
\beta/p
is contained in an
L_{p}
-ball whose radius is a definite number (independent of
x
) smaller than
1
, albeit ever so slightly as
p\to\infty
.

Returning to the setting of Theorem
1.14
, the above discussion sets the stage for the following procedure. First apply Lemma
1.16
with

will allow us to take advantage of the radius decrease in (

1

Thus, suppressing (only for the purpose of the present proof sketch) the small additive

correction of the radius that appears in (

takes this correction into account), we may now fix

and an integer

and focus our attention on obtaining a separating partition that is

-radially bounded with respect to

As these local snapshots are contained in the unit ball of

2.1.
Weakly bi-Lipschitz embeddings

Section
1.1
recalled (for brevity of the Introduction) only the notion of Euclidean distortion, but it is standard (and fruitful) to study the analogous concept for embeddings into an arbitrary metric space. A metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
has a bi-Lipschitz embedding of distortion
D\geqslant 1
into a metric space
(\mathcal{Z},d_{\mathcal{Z}})
if there is a nonconstant Lipschitz function
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{Z}
satisfying

(2.1)

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad d_{\mathcal{Z}}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\geqslant\frac{\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{Z})}}{D}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y),

The
\mathcal{Z}
-distortion
\mathsf{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{M}
is the infimum over those
D\in[1,\infty]
for which an embedding
f
as above exists. We also denote
\mathsf{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{n}(\mathcal{M})=\sup\{\mathsf{c}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathscr{C}):\ |\mathscr{C}|\subseteq\mathcal{M}\ \wedge\ 2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n\}
for every integer
n\geqslant 2
.

There is a “one scale at a time” variant of the above classical setup, which is an important and commonly used concept in the study of metric embeddings; the relevant terminology, which we next recall, was introduced in
[
81
, p. 192]
(the reason for the nomenclature is further articulated in
[
75
, Section 7.2]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
admits a weakly bi-Lipschitz embedding of distortion
D\geqslant 1
into a metric space
(\mathcal{Z},d_{\mathcal{Z}})
if for any
\Delta>0
there is a nonconstant Lipschitz function
f=f_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{Z}
satisfying

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\geqslant\Delta\implies d_{\mathcal{Z}}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\geqslant\frac{\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{Z})}}{D}\Delta.

Analogously to the above notation, in the setting of Definition
2.1
let
\mathsf{d}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{M})
be the infimum over those
D\in[1,\infty]
for which
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
has a weakly bi-Lipschitz embedding of distortion
D
into
(\mathcal{Z},d_{\mathcal{Z}})
, and write

\forall n\in\{2,3,\ldots\},\qquad\mathsf{d}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{n}(\mathcal{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\\
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n\end{subarray}}\mathsf{d}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathscr{C}).

We will also use the shorter notation
\mathsf{d}_{L_{p}}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{d}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{d}_{L_{p}}^{n}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{d}_{p}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
for
p\geqslant 1
and
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
.

Suitably defined “spatially localized” variants of Definition
2.1
will be discussed in Section
3
below, as they are important for proving the results that obtained herein. In this section, though, it suffices to consider only the above “vanilla” notion of “one scale at a time” embedding, as well as the following ad hoc notation for an obvious (also standard) “two-sided scale-localized” version thereof.

Given metric spaces
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
and
(\mathcal{Z},d_{\mathcal{Z}})
, let
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{M})
denote the infimum over those
D\in[1,\infty]
with the property that for any
\Delta>0
there exists a nonconstant Lipschitz function
f=f_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{Z}
satisfying

(2.2)

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\Delta\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant 2\Delta\implies d_{\mathcal{Z}}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\geqslant\frac{\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{Z})}}{D}\Delta.

We also use the shorter notation
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{L_{p}}(\mathcal{M})=\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{p}(\mathcal{M})
and
\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{L_{p}}^{n}(\mathcal{M})=\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{p}^{n}(\mathcal{M})
for
p\geqslant 1
and
n\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
, where

\forall n\in\{2,3,\ldots\},\qquad\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathcal{Z}}^{n}(\mathcal{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\\
2\leqslant|\mathscr{C}|\leqslant n\end{subarray}}\widehat{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathscr{C}).

Remark 2.2
.

The factor
2
in (
2.2
) was fixed for concreteness, but it is an arbitrary choice of minor significance to the context of what we study herein. Specifically, suppose that
1<\alpha<\beta<\infty
and for any
\Delta>0
there exists a
1
-Lipschitz function
f_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}\to L_{2}
satisfying

(2.3)

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\Delta\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant\alpha\Delta\implies\|f_{\Delta}(x)-f_{\Delta}(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\geqslant\frac{\Delta}{D}.

Set
k=\lceil(\log\beta)/\log\alpha\rceil-1
. If
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
satisfy
\Delta\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant\beta\Delta
, then

\exists i\in\{0,\ldots,k\},\qquad\alpha^{i}\Delta\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant\alpha^{i+1}\Delta,

whence
\|f_{\alpha^{i}\Delta}(x)-f_{\alpha^{i}\Delta}(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\geqslant\alpha^{i}\Delta/D\geqslant\Delta/D

g_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}\to L_{2}\otimes L_{2}\cong L_{2}

g_{\Delta}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\sum_{i=0}^{k}f_{\alpha^{i}\Delta}(x)\otimes v_{i},

where

is an arbitrary orthonormal system in

is

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\Delta\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant\beta\Delta\implies\|g_{\Delta}(x)-g_{\Delta}(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\geqslant\frac{\Delta}{D\sqrt{k+1}}.

Therefore, the existence for every
\Delta>0
of a

) implies the existence for every
\Delta>0
of such a function having the analogous property with

replaced by

and

D\sqrt{\left\lceil\frac{\log\beta}{\log\alpha}\right\rceil}\asymp_{\alpha,\beta}D.

a\in\mathcal{N}
such that
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a)\leqslant r
. When we say that a subset
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
is a net of
\mathcal{M}
we mean that there is some
r>0
such that
\mathcal{N}
is an
r
-net of
\mathcal{M}
. For each target metric space
(\mathcal{T},d_{\mathcal{T}})
, the Lipschitz extension modulus from nets
\mathsf{e}_{\mathrm{net}}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})
is defined to be the supremum of
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N};\mathcal{Z})
over all possible nets
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
, where we recall that the (standard) notation for Lipschitz extension moduli was already introduced in Section
1.2
. Clearly
\mathsf{e}_{\mathrm{net}}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})\leqslant\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})
, but it is worthwhile to single out extension from nets because it is an especially important and useful instance of the Lipschitz extension problem, and there are situations in which bounds on
\mathsf{e}_{\mathrm{net}}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})
are known that are stronger than the available upper bounds on
\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{T})
; examples of works that either treat or rely on extension from nets include
[
18
,
14
,
71
,
76
,
82
,
79
,
3
,
19
,
85
]
.

For Theorem
1.15
, it is convenient to introduce the following notation for every metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
:

(2.4)

\Pi(\mathcal{M})=\Pi(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\max\Big\{\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{N})\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N};L_{2})^{2}:\ \mathcal{N}\ \mathrm{is\ a\ net\ of\ }\mathcal{M}\Big\}.

Thus,

\Pi(\mathcal{M})\leqslant\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{M})\mathsf{e}_{\mathrm{net}}(\mathcal{M};L_{2})^{2}\leqslant\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{M})\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M};L_{2})^{2}.

The following lemma relates the “separation-extension product”
\Pi(\mathcal{M})
to the weakly bi-Lipschitz Euclidean distortion
\mathsf{d}_{2}(\mathcal{M})
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Every finite metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
satisfies
\mathsf{d}_{2}(\mathcal{M})\leqslant 8\Pi(\mathcal{M})+1
.

Proof.

Our goal is to prove that for any
\Delta>0
there is a
1
-Lipschitz function
f=f_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}\to L_{2}
such that

(2.5)

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\geqslant\Delta\implies\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\geqslant\frac{\Delta}{8\Pi(\mathcal{M})+1}.

Set
m=|\mathcal{M}|
and fix
\Delta>0
. Let
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and
0<\delta<1-2\varepsilon
be auxiliary parameters whose values will be specified later to optimize the ensuing reasoning. Fix from now an arbitrary
(\varepsilon\Delta)
-net
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
\{v_{\mathscr{S}}\}_{\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathcal{N}}\subseteq L_{2}
be an orthonormal system of
2^{|\mathcal{N}|}
vectors in
L_{2}
, indexed by the subsets of
\mathcal{N}
. Denoting by
\Omega
the collection of all the
(\delta\Delta)
-bounded partitions of
\mathcal{N}
, let
\mu
be a probability measure on
\Omega
such that:

(2.6)

\forall a,b\in\mathcal{N},\qquad\mu\big[\mathscr{P}\in\Omega:\ \mathscr{P}(a)\neq\mathscr{P}(b)\big]\leqslant\frac{\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{N})}{\delta\Delta}d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b).

We can now define a function
\uppsi:\mathcal{N}\to L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})
as follows:

(2.7)

\forall a\in\mathcal{N},\forall\mathscr{P}\in\Omega,\qquad\uppsi(a)(\mathscr{P})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varepsilon\delta}}{\sqrt{2\Pi(\mathcal{M})}}v_{\mathscr{P}(a)}.

As
\|v_{\mathscr{S}}-v_{\mathscr{T}}\|_{\!L_{2}}^{2}=2
whenever
\mathscr{S},\mathscr{T}\subseteq\mathcal{N}
are distinct, every
a,b\in\mathcal{N}
satisfy

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\uppsi(a)-\uppsi(b)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:here is our phi normalized}}}{{=}}\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varepsilon\delta}}{\sqrt{2\Pi(\mathcal{M})}}\sqrt{2\mu\big[\mathscr{P}\in\Omega:\ \mathscr{P}(a)\neq\mathscr{P}(b)\big]}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:our mu separates}}}{{\leqslant}}\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon\Delta\mathsf{SEP}(\mathcal{N})d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b)}}{\sqrt{\Pi(\mathcal{M})}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def DM}}}{{\leqslant}}\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon\Delta d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b)}}{\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N};\ell_{\!2})}\leqslant\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b)}{\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N};\ell_{\!2})},\end{split}

where the last step of (
2.8
) holds as
d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b)\geqslant\varepsilon\Delta
for distinct
a,b\in\mathcal{N}
. By (
2.8
), the Lipschitz constant of
\uppsi
is at most
1/\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N};\ell_{\!2})
, so there is a
1
-Lipschitz function

\Psi:\mathcal{M}\to L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})

that extends
\uppsi
.

Consider
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
with
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\geqslant\Delta
. Take
a,b\in\mathcal{N}
such that
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a),d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,b)\leqslant\varepsilon\Delta
. Then,

(2.9)

d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b)\geqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)-d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a)-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,b)\geqslant(1-2\varepsilon)\Delta>\delta\Delta,

where the last step of (
2.9
) is where the assumption
\delta<1-2\varepsilon
is used. Since
\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{P}(a))\leqslant\delta\Delta
for every
\mathscr{P}\in\Omega
, necessarily
b\notin\mathscr{P}(a)
. In other words,
\mathscr{P}(a)\neq\mathscr{P}(b)
for every
\mathscr{P}\in\Omega
. Equivalently, every
\mathscr{P}\in\Omega
satisfies
\|v_{\mathscr{P}(a)}-v_{\mathscr{P}(b)}\|_{\!L_{2}}=\sqrt{2}
. Recalling the definition (
2.7
) of
\uppsi
, because
a,b\in\mathcal{N}
and
\Psi
extends
\uppsi
we consequently have

\|\Psi(a)-\Psi(b)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}=\|\uppsi(a)-\uppsi(b)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}=\Delta\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon\delta}{\Pi(\mathcal{M})}}.

Therefore,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}\geqslant{}&\|\Psi(a)-\Psi(b)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}\\
&\quad-\|\Psi(x)-\Psi(a)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}-\|\Psi(y)-\Psi(b)\|_{\!L_{2}(\mu;L_{2})}\\
\geqslant{}&\frac{\Delta\sqrt{\varepsilon\delta}}{\sqrt{\Pi(\mathcal{M})}}-d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a)-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,b)\\
\geqslant{}&\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon\delta}}{\sqrt{\Pi(\mathcal{M})}}-2\varepsilon\bigg)\Delta,\end{split}

where the penultimate step of (
2.10
) uses the fact that
\Psi
is
1
-Lipschitz.

The right hand side of (
2.10
) is maximal for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\delta/(16\Pi(\mathcal{M}))
, which is a valid choice for the above reasoning provided that the requirement
\delta<1-2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}
is satisfied, i.e., if
\delta<8\Pi(\mathcal{M})/(8\Pi(\mathcal{M})+1)
. Assuming from now on that this restriction on
\delta
holds, substitute
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}
into (
2.10
). Since
\Psi
takes values in an
m
-dimensional subspace of a Hilbert space, by composing
\Psi
with an isometry between that subspace and
\ell_{\!\!2}^{m}
and translating the resulting function so that
0\in\ell_{\!\!2}^{m}
is in its image, we conclude that for every
0<\delta<8\Pi(\mathcal{M})/(8\Pi(\mathcal{M})+1)
there is a
1
-Lipschitz function
\Phi_{\delta}:\mathcal{M}\to B_{\ell_{2}^{m}}\big(0,\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\big)
that satisfies:

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\geqslant\Delta\implies\|\Phi_{\delta}(x)-\Phi_{\delta}(y)\|_{\!\ell_{\!\!2}^{m}}\geqslant\frac{\delta\Delta}{8D(\Pi(\mathcal{M})}.

Now (
2.5
) follows by taking a
\delta\to\big(8\Pi(\mathcal{M})/(8\Pi(\mathcal{M})+1)\big)^{-}
limit of
\Phi_{\delta}
along a convergent subsequence.
∎

2.2.1.
Weakly bi-Lipschitz embeddings of controlled local growth centers

A key part of the proof of Theorem
2.4
studies the geometry of the set of points in a metric space which are centers of balls whose size increases in a controlled manner for radii that belong to a prescribed range. The pertinent object is:

Notation 2.5

(controlled local growth centers)
.

is a locally finite metric space (thus, all balls in

(2.33)

where the last step of (

. The desired assertion (

.

Given a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
, the distance of a point
x\in\mathcal{M}
to a nonempty subset
\mathscr{C}
of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted as usual by
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathscr{C})=\inf\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y):\ y\in\mathscr{C}\}
. It is worthwhile to state separately the following observation:

Observation 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
be a finite metric space. Given
0\leqslant\mathfrak{p}\leqslant 1
, denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the (
\mathfrak{p}
-Bernoulli) random subset of
\mathcal{M}
that is obtained by including independently each
x\in\mathcal{M}
in
\mathcal{Z}
with probability
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e.,

(2.42)

\forall\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathcal{M},\qquad\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathscr{C}]=\mathfrak{p}^{|\mathscr{C}|}(1-\mathfrak{p})^{n-|\mathscr{C}|}.

Then, for every
0<r<R
and every
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
such that
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)>\frac{1}{2}r+\frac{3}{2}R
, we have

(2.43)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\bigg[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\big|&d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\big|>\frac{R-r}{2}\bigg]\\
&\quad\geqslant\min\Big\{(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|},1-(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)|}\Big\}.\end{split}

Proof.

Consider the events
E
and
F
that are defined as follows:

(2.46)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>R\quad\mathrm{and}\quad d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant\frac{r+R}{2}\right\},\\
F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant r\quad\mathrm{and}\quad d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>\frac{r+R}{2}\right\}.\end{array}\right.

By definition,
E\cap F=\emptyset
and
E\cup F\subseteq\{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset\ \wedge\ |d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})|>(R-r)/2\}
, so we have

(2.47)

\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})|>\frac{R-r}{2}\right]\geqslant\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}[E]+\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}[F].

Note that the event
\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>R\}
occurs if and only if
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
contains no point from
B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)
, and the event
\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant(r+R)/2\}
occurs if and only if
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
contains at least one point from
B_{\mathcal{M}}(y,(r+R)/2)
. By the triangle inequality for
d_{\mathcal{M}}
, the assumption
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)>r/2+3R/2=R+(r+R)/2
of Observation
2.7
implies that
B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(y,(r+R)/2)=\emptyset
, so
\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>R\}
and
\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant(r+R)/2\}
are independent events, thanks to the definition of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Consequently,

(2.48)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}[E]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def EF}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\big[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)&=\emptyset\big]\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant\frac{r+R}{2}\right]\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:bernouli formula for prob}}}{{=}}(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant\frac{r+R}{2}\right].\end{split}

In the same vein, the events
\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant r\}
and
\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>(r+R)/2\}
coincide with, respectively, the events
\{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)\neq\emptyset\}
and
\{\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(y,(r+R)/2)=\emptyset\}
. The latter two events are independent because
B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(y,(r+R)/2)\subseteq B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(y,(r+R)/2)=\emptyset
, so we have:

(2.49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}[F]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def EF}}}{{=}}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\big[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)&\neq\emptyset\big]\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>\frac{r+R}{2}\right]\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:bernouli formula for prob}}}{{=}}\left(1-(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)|}\right)\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>\frac{r+R}{2}\right].\end{split}

The desired bound (
2.43
) is now justified as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\bigg[\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})|>\frac{R-r}{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:at least EF}\wedge\eqref{eq:probE}\wedge\eqref{eq:probF}}}{{\geqslant}}(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant\frac{r+R}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(1-(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)|}\right)\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>\frac{r+R}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\ \geqslant\min\Big\{(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|},1-(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)|}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\left(\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leqslant\frac{r+R}{2}\right]+\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\left[d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{p}})>\frac{r+R}{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ =\min\Big\{(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|},1-(1-\mathfrak{p})^{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)|}\Big\}.\qed

\forall(x,\omega)\in\mathcal{M}\times\Omega,\qquad f(x)(\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lceil\log n\rceil}}\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil\log n\rceil}\min\big\{d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(x,\mathcal{Z}_{e^{-i}}(\omega)\big),\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\big\}v_{i}.

\displaystyle e^{i_{R}(x)}>|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|\geqslant|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,r)|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:local groth set def}}}{{\geqslant}}\frac{|B_{\mathcal{M}}(x,R)|}{K}\geqslant\frac{e^{i_{R}(x)-1}}{K}.

, the following probabilistic estimate holds for every

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}\bigg[&\big|\min\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{Z}_{e^{-i_{R}(x)}}),\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\}-\min\{d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{Z}_{e^{-i_{R}(x)}}),\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\}\big|>\frac{R-r}{2}\bigg]\\

\displaystyle\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{\!L_{2}(\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}};L_{2})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def bourgain embedding}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lceil\log n\rceil}}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\log n\rceil}\int_{\Omega}\Big|\min\big\{d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(x,\mathcal{Z}_{e^{-j}}(\omega)\big),\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\min\big\{d_{\mathcal{M}}\big(y,\mathcal{Z}_{e^{-j}}(\omega)\big),\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{M})\big\}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\operatorname{\varmathbb{P}}(\omega)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Proof of Lemma
2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a subset of

for all distinct

\mathcal{N}

, the balls
\{B_{\mathcal{M}}(a,r)\}_{a\in\mathcal{N}}
are disjoint, so we have

(2.56)

|\mathcal{N}|\min_{a\in\mathcal{N}}|B_{\mathcal{M}}(a,r)|\leqslant\sum_{a\in\mathcal{N}}|B_{\mathcal{M}}(a,r)|=\Big|\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal{N}}B_{\mathcal{M}}(a,r)\Big|.

Fix
a_{\min}\in\mathcal{N}
such that
|B_{\mathcal{M}}(a_{\min},r)|=\min_{a\in\mathcal{N}}|B_{\mathcal{M}}(a,r)|
. By the triangle inequality for
d_{\mathcal{M}}
, since
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathscr{U}
we have
B_{\mathcal{M}}(a,r)\subseteq B_{\mathcal{M}}(a_{\min},\mathrm{diam}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{U})+r)\subseteq B_{\mathcal{M}}(a_{\min},R)
for every

, as

. Thanks to (

), this implies

.

By the assumption of Lemma
2.8
applied to
\mathscr{C}=\mathcal{N}
, there exists a
1
-Lipschitz function

that satisfies the following condition for every

:

(2.57)

\Delta_{0}\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(a,b)\leqslant 3\beta\Delta_{0}\implies\|f(a)-f(b)\|_{\!L_{2}}\geqslant\frac{\Delta_{0}}{D},\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\Delta_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{19D-1}{19D}\Delta-4r,

which is valid since

, as

, so the parameter
\Delta_{0}
in (
2.57
) is indeed positive. Because
\mathcal{M}
is locally finite,
\phi(\mathcal{M})
is a countable subset of
L_{2}
, so by replacing
L_{2}
by
L_{2}\oplus\varmathbb{R}
, we may assume that there exists a vector

that satisfies

and

is orthogonal to
f(\mathcal{M})
. Since
f
is
1
-Lipschitz, and by the triangle inequality for

also the function

is

-Lipschitz, the aforementioned orthogonality implies that the following function

is
1
-Lipschitz:

\forall x\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\phi(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}f(x)+\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{N})}{\sqrt{2}}v.

We then have the following lower bound:

(2.58)

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M},\qquad\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\gtrsim\max\big\{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{\!L_{2}},|d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{N})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{N})|\big\}.

Fix
x\in\mathscr{U}\cap\mathcal{G}_{\leqslant K}(r,R)
. By the maximality of
\mathcal{N}
with respect to inclusion, there must exist
a_{x}\in\mathcal{N}
such that
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a_{x})\leqslant 2r
, i.e., we have
d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,\mathcal{N})\leqslant 2r
. Consequently, if
y\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies

(2.59)

d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,\mathcal{N})\geqslant 2r+\frac{\Delta}{19D},

then it follows from (
2.58
) that
\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\gtrsim\Delta/D
, which is the desired conclusion of (
2.59
).

The above simple preparatory reasoning demonstrates that the proof of Lemma
2.8
will be complete if we will prove that
\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|_{\!L_{2}}\gtrsim\Delta/D
whenever
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
have the following properties:

(2.60)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\leqslant\beta\Delta,\\
\exists a_{x},a_{y}\in\mathcal{N},\quad d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a_{x})\leqslant 2r\quad\mathrm{and}\quad d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,a_{y})<2r+\frac{\Delta}{19D}.\end{array}\right.

This is indeed the case because by the triangle inequality for
d_{\mathcal{M}}
we have

(2.61)

d_{\mathcal{M}}(a_{x},a_{y})\geqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)-d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a_{x})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,a_{y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:new assumptions axay}}}{{\geqslant}}\Delta-4r-\frac{\Delta}{19D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:use single scale for Dela0}}}{{=}}\Delta_{0},

and

(2.62)

d_{\mathcal{M}}(a_{x},a_{y})\leqslant d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)+d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a_{y})+d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,a_{y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:new assumptions axay}}}{{\leqslant}}\beta\Delta+4r+\frac{\Delta}{19D}\leqslant 3\beta\Delta_{0},

where checking that the last step of (
2.62
) is valid is straightforward using the definition (
2.57
) of
\Delta_{0}
together with the assumptions
\beta\geqslant 1
and
\Delta\geqslant 9Dr\geqslant 9r
. Thanks to (
2.61
) and (
2.62
) we may use (
2.57
) with
a=a_{x}
and
b=a_{y}
in combination with (
2.58
) to conclude that the following lower bound holds:

(2.63)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|_{L_{2}}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:max version of lower bound}}}{{\gtrsim}}\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{L_{2}}\\
&\ \ \ \geqslant\|f(a_{x})-f(a_{y})\|_{L_{2}}-\|f(x)-f(a_{x})\|_{L_{2}}-\|f(y)-f(a_{y})\|_{L_{2}}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:use single scale for Dela0}}}{{\geqslant}}\frac{\Delta_{0}}{D}-d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,a_{x})-d_{\mathcal{M}}(y,a_{y})\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:use single scale for Dela0}\wedge\eqref{eq:new assumptions axay}}}{{\geqslant}}\frac{\frac{19D-1}{19D}\Delta-4r}{D}-4r-\frac{\Delta}{19D}\\
&\ \ \ \gtrsim\frac{\Delta}{D},\end{split}

where the second step of (
2.63
) uses the triangle inequality in
L_{2}
, the third step of (
2.63
) uses the fact that
f
is
1
-Lipschitz, and the final step of (
2.63
) is straightforward to verify using

and
D\geqslant 1
.
∎

\,\mathrm{d}\upnu(\mathscr{P})

-Lipschitz as a function

3.
Localized weakly bi-Lipschitz embeddings

The following “localized version” of Definition
2.1
is a slight generalization of a definition that appeared in
[
75
, Section 7.2]
, which corresponds to the (arbitrary) choice
K=32
below:

Definition 3.1
.

Given
K,D\geqslant 1
, a metric space
(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathcal{M}})
is said to admit a
K
-localized weakly bi-Lipschitz embedding into a metric space
(\mathcal{N},d_{\mathcal{N}})
with distortion
D
if for every
\Delta>0
and every
z\in\mathcal{M}
there exists a non-constant Lipschitz function
f^{z}_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
such that

(3.1)

\forall x,y\in B_{\mathcal{M}}(z,K\Delta),\qquad d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\geqslant\Delta\implies d_{\mathcal{N}}\big(f^{z}_{\Delta}(x),f^{z}_{\Delta}(y)\big)\geqslant\frac{\|f^{z}_{\Delta}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}}{D}\Delta,

A key (well known) property of
L_{p}
that we will use herein is the (first part of) the following theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

If
K>1
and
p>2
, then
L_{p}
admits a
K
-localized weakly bi-Lipschitz embedding into
L_{2}
with distortion
D
, where
D\lesssim p2^{p/2}K^{p/2-1}
. Conversely, if
L_{p}
admits a
K
-localized weakly bi-Lipschitz embedding into
L_{2}
with distortion
D
, then necessarily
D\gtrsim 2^{p/2}K^{p/2-1}
.

Proof.

The first part of Theorem
3.2
essentially coincides with Lemma 7.6 of
[
75
]
, which notes the (special case
K=32
of the) following general statement. Suppose that
(\mathbf{X},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{X}})
and
(\mathbf{Y},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{Y}})
are Banach spaces for which there exists a function
U:B_{\mathbf{X}}\to\mathbf{Y}
from the unit ball
B_{\mathbf{X}}=\{x\in\mathbf{X}:\ \|x\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leqslant 1\}
to
\mathbf{Y}
satisfying

(3.2)

\forall x,y\in B_{\mathbf{X}},\qquad\omega\big(\|x-y\|_{\mathbf{X}}\big)\leqslant\|U(x)-U(y)\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\leqslant L\|x-y\|_{\mathbf{X}},

for some
L>0
and an increasing modulus
\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
. Let
\rho_{\mathbf{X}}:\mathbf{X}\to B_{\mathbf{X}}
be the standard retraction (e.g.
[
75
, equation (5.2)]
) from
\mathbf{X}
onto
B_{\mathbf{X}}
, i.e.,
\rho_{\mathbf{X}}(x)=x/\max\{\|x\|_{\mathbf{X}},1\}
for every

\forall(i,j)\in\varmathbb{N}\times\varmathbb{N},\ \forall\omega=(\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\ldots)\in\Omega,\qquad\Gamma^{i,j}(\omega)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Phi^{i}(\omega_{0})\cap\Psi^{j}_{\!\!\!n^{i}(\omega_{0})}\big(\omega_{n^{i}(\omega_{0})}\big).

The strong measurability of

\{\Psi^{j}_{n}\}_{j,n=1}^{\infty}
, together with the measurability of
\{n^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
that we verified above, as a consequence of the assumed strong measurability of
\{\Phi^{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
. Indeed, suppose that

is closed. For
\omega=(\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\ldots)\in\Omega
, definition (
5.12
) shows that
\Gamma^{i,j}(\omega)\cap E\neq\emptyset
if and only if
\Psi^{j}_{\!\!\!n^{i}(\omega_{0})}\big(\omega_{n^{i}(\omega_{0})}\big)\cap E\neq\emptyset
, as

and
\{\Phi^{i}(\omega_{0})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a partition of
\mathscr{C}
. Thus,

\big\{\omega\in\Omega:\ \Gamma^{i,j}(\omega)\cap E\neq\emptyset\big\}\\
=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\big\{\omega_{0}\in\Omega_{0}:\ n^{i}(\omega_{0})=n\big\}\times\Omega_{1}\times\ldots\times\Omega_{n-1}\times\big\{\omega_{n}\in\Omega_{n}:\ \Psi^{j}_{\!\!\!n}(\omega_{n})\cap E\neq\emptyset\big\}\times\prod_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\Omega_{k}.

Therefore, we can consider the random partition of
\mathscr{C}
that is given by

\forall\omega=(\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\ldots)\in\Omega,\qquad\mathscr{P}^{\omega}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\big\{\Gamma^{i,j}(\omega)\big\}_{i,j=1}^{\infty},

which satisfies
\mathrm{rad}_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathscr{P}^{\omega}(x))\leqslant\Delta
for every
x\in\mathscr{C}
thanks to (
5.8
) and (
5.12
). Finally, every
x,y\in\mathscr{C}
satisfy:

\displaystyle 1-\mu\big[\omega\in\Omega:\ \mathscr{P}^{\omega}(x)\neq\mathscr{P}^{\omega}(y)\big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def common refinement}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mu\big[\omega\in\Omega:\ \Phi^{i}(\omega_{0})\supseteq\{x,y\}\ \wedge\ \Psi^{j}_{\!\!\!n^{i}(\omega_{0})}\big(\omega_{n^{i}(\omega_{0})}\big)\supseteq\{x,y\}\big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ =\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu\big[\omega\in\Omega:\ \Phi^{i}(\omega_{0})\supseteq\{x,y\}\ \wedge\ n^{i}(\omega_{0})=n\ \wedge\ \Psi^{j}_{\!\!\!n}(\omega_{n})\supseteq\{x,y\}\big]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def product spaces}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{0}\big[\omega\in\Omega_{0}:\ \Phi^{i}(\omega_{0})\supseteq\{x,y\}\ \wedge\ n^{i}(\omega_{0})=n\big]\mu_{n}\big[\omega_{n}\in\Omega_{n}:\ \Psi^{j}_{\!\!\!n}(\omega_{n})\supseteq\{x,y\}\big]

\displaystyle\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:def R partition}}}{{=}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(1-\mu_{n}\big[\omega_{n}\in\Omega_{n}:\ \mathcal{R}_{n}^{\omega_{n}}(v)\neq\mathcal{R}_{n}^{\omega_{n}}(y)\big]\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu_{0}\big[\omega\in\Omega_{0}:\ \Phi^{i}(\omega_{0})\supseteq\{x,y\}\ \wedge\ n^{i}(\omega_{0})=n\big]

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:separation requirement for R eta}\wedge\eqref{eq:def Q parititon}}}{{\geqslant}}\Big(1-\min\big\{1,\frac{\tau+\eta}{\Delta}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\big\}\Big)\mu_{0}\big[\omega_{0}\in\Omega_{0}:\ \mathcal{Q}^{\omega_{0}}(x)\neq\mathcal{Q}^{\omega_{0}}(y)\big]

\displaystyle\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:separation requirement for Q eta}}}{{\geqslant}}\Big(1-\min\big\{1,\frac{\tau+\eta}{\Delta}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\big\}\Big)\Big(1-\min\big\{1,\frac{\sigma+\eta}{K\Delta}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)\big\}\Big)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \geqslant 1-\frac{\frac{\sigma+\eta}{K}+\tau+\eta}{\Delta}d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y).

By taking the limit of this estimate as

we conclude that (
5.1
) indeed holds.
∎

We will need to impose a stronger measurability requirement from random partitions to be able to easily use localized radially weakly bi-Lipschitz embeddings to transfer separating random partitions from one metric space to another; this is the mechanism by which the proof of Theorem

Given a Polish metric space

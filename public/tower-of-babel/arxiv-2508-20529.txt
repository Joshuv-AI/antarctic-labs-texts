Heisenberg spin chain models for realising quantum battery with the aid of Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction Preprint: APS/123-QED
Suprabha Bhattacharya 1, Vivek Balasaheb Sabale 1, Atul Kumar 1

Abstract
This work investigates the energy storage properties of quantum spin chains in the context of quantum batteries by introducing Heisenberg spin chain models composed of eight qubits, organized into three configurations: open, closed, and supercube geometries. The charging dynamics of the mentioned systems are examined using Hamiltonians that include contributions from the battery, spin-spin interactions, and a transverse magnetic field. The inclusion of the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI) within the charging Hamiltonian is found to improve the ergotropy in the XXZ model, particularly for the supercube configuration, leading to enhanced quantum battery performance. We further study the effects of varying neighboring coupling strengths and system size (8 and 12 qubits) on the charging power and ergotropy. The analysis identifies the supercube configuration, under specific interaction strengths, as achieving near-optimal charging and discharging efficiency. In the case of a 12-qubit icosahedral structure, the superior results and ergotropy are obtained, comparable to the supercube.

Our results show that the supercube configuration, under specific interaction strengths (Heisenberg coupling

These findings identify the supercube and icosahedral models as candidates for realizing efficient and cyclically operable quantum batteries. Our results demonstrate that charging efficiency, stability, and periodicity can be systematically engineered through interaction geometry, opening a new design axis for quantum energy storage systems. Notably, this work also suggests that scaling QBs beyond small sizes may require active suppression of unwanted couplings or fine-tuning of interaction parameters.

II
Basic Concepts

In this section, we present a brief overview of the basic concepts relevant to our study, focusing on the Heisenberg spin chain and the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI).

II.1
Heisenberg model

Spin chains can be regarded as one-dimensional arrays of two-level quantum systems, i.e., qubits. The study of spin chains has led to significant developments in condensed matter physics
Auerbach 2012
;
Blundell 2001
;
Kittel and McEuen 2018
, quantum materials
Zhao et al. 2025
, and quantum communication and computation
Zueco et al. 2009
;
Smith et al. 2019
.

The one-dimensional Heisenberg Hamiltonian describing interactions between the spins in a chain is given by
Blundell 2001

H_{HS}=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\,\vec{\sigma}_{i}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{j},

(1)

where
J_{ij}
is the coupling constant between the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
and
j^{\mathrm{th}}
spins; in most cases, we assume
J_{ij}=J
. Here,
\vec{\sigma}_{i}=(\sigma_{i}^{x},\sigma_{i}^{y},\sigma_{i}^{z})
denotes the vector of Pauli operators acting on the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
spin site. For
J>0
, the Hamiltonian corresponds to the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model, in which spins tend to align antiparallel in an isotropic manner such that the total spin angular momentum of the chain remains zero throughout the evolution. Conversely, for
J<0
, the system minimizes its energy by aligning all spins parallel, corresponding to the ferromagnetic Heisenberg Hamiltonian
Pires 2021
.

II.2
Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction

The Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI)
Moriya 1960a
;
Moriya 1960b
;
Dzyaloshinsky 1958
is an anisotropic, antisymmetric magnetic exchange interaction that arises due to spin–orbit coupling
Sahu et al. 2017
;
Tchoffo et al. 2016
. While the Heisenberg interaction tends to align spins either parallel or antiparallel, the DMI favors spin canting
Yang et al. 2023
. The general form of the DMI Hamiltonian is given by

H_{DM}=\sum_{i<j}\vec{D}\cdot(\vec{\sigma}_{i}\times\vec{\sigma}_{j}),

(2)

where
\vec{D}=(D_{x},D_{y},D_{z})
is the DMI vector and
D_{i}
denotes its strength along the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
spatial direction, with
i\in\{x,y,z\}
. In this work, we restrict to the
z
-component of the DMI, which modifies the Hamiltonian to

H_{DMz}=D_{z}\sum_{i<j}\left(\sigma_{i}^{x}\sigma_{j}^{y}-\sigma_{i}^{y}\sigma_{j}^{x}\right),

(3)

where
D_{z}
represents the strength of the interaction along the
z
-axis. Previous studies have shown that incorporating DMI enhances entanglement in spin systems governed by the Heisenberg model. Moreover, in initially non-entangled states, DMI can actively generate entanglement
Gurkan and Pashaev 2010
;
Jafari and Langari 2011
. More recently, it has also been demonstrated that DMI can increase the charging power of quantum batteries
K and Murugesh 2024
.

In the context of quantum batteries, the Heisenberg interaction provides the fundamental spin–spin coupling mechanism responsible for collective charging. The inclusion of DMI introduces anisotropy and spin canting, which in turn generate entanglement and alter energy transfer pathways. These combined effects directly influence ergotropy and charging power, making the Heisenberg and DMI Hamiltonians essential building blocks for analyzing the efficiency and performance of spin-chain-based quantum batteries.

Building on the basic concepts introduced in the previous section, we now construct a model of a quantum battery based on interacting spin chains. Our aim is to examine how collective effects arising from Heisenberg exchange and Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interactions influence the charging and discharging performance of such systems. To this end, we consider an eight-qubit (
n=8
) spin chain, which serves as the working medium of the battery
Rossini et al. 2019
;
Le et al. 2018
;
Ghosh et al. 2020
;
Dou et al. 2022
;
Huangfu and Jing 2021
;
Peng et al. 2021
;
Arjmandi et al. 2022
;
Ghosh and Sen De
.

The initial state of the system is defined as

|\psi(0)\rangle=|00000000\rangle.

(4)

which represents the uncharged configuration of the battery. This being the lowest energy configuration corresponds to the ground state with energy
-n\hbar\omega_{0}
. The fully charged state,
|1\rangle^{\otimes n}
, has energy
+n\hbar\omega_{0}
. Thus, the maximum storable or extractable energy in a charging–discharging cycle is
2n\hbar\omega_{0}
.

In the absence of charging, the system evolves under the internal battery Hamiltonian

H_{B}=\hbar\omega_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\sigma_{i}^{z}},

(5)

where
\omega_{0}
is the uniform Larmor frequency and
\sigma_{i}^{z}
is the Pauli-
z
operator on the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
spin. This describes a uniform magnetic field along the
z
-axis, which defines the internal energy structure of the battery through Zeeman splitting
Ali et al. 2024
.

To drive the charging process, we introduce the charging Hamiltonian

H_{C}=H_{x}+H_{HS}+H_{DMz}.

(6)

which incorporates a transverse magnetic field, Heisenberg interactions, and the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI). The first contribution,
H_{x}
, represents a uniform transverse magnetic field along the
x
-axis

H_{x}=\hbar\Omega\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\sigma_{i}^{x}},

(7)

where
\Omega
is the field strength applied equally to all spins. The second term,
H_{HS}
, models anisotropic Heisenberg interactions. As shown in Ref.
Le et al. 2018
, a spin chain with isotropic coupling evolves much like a collection of independent spins, providing little scope for collective enhancement. To enable cooperative charging, we therefore adopt the anisotropic antiferromagnetic Heisenberg Hamiltonian

H_{HS}=J\hbar\sum_{i<j}\Big[(1+\delta)\sigma_{i}^{x}\sigma_{j}^{x}+(1-\delta)\sigma_{i}^{y}\sigma_{j}^{y}+\Delta\sigma_{i}^{z}\sigma_{j}^{z}\Big]

(8)

where
J=1
is the nearest-neighbor coupling constant, and
\delta
and
\Delta
control anisotropy in the
xy
-plane and along the
z
-direction respectively.
Based on the values of the anisotropy parameters, we focus on two representative cases of the Heisenberg Hamiltonian

1.

Ising model:
\delta=1,\Delta=0
,

2.

XXZ model:
\delta=0,\Delta\neq 0
.

The third term,
H_{DMz}
, accounts for the
z
-axis DMI, introducing an antisymmetric exchange interaction between neighboring spins as defined in equation
3

Because the internal Hamiltonian
H_{B}
may also influence charging
Shukla et al. 2025
, we define the effective driving Hamiltonian as

H=H_{C}+\lambda H_{B},

(9)

where
\lambda\in[0,1]
tunes the contribution of
H_{B}
. For
\lambda=0
, the dynamics are governed only by
H_{C}
, while for
\lambda=1
, both
H_{C}
and
H_{B}
contribute. As the system is thermally isolated, the time evolution during charging is described by the unitary operator

U=e^{-iHt}

(10)

These Hamiltonians establish the theoretical framework for the charging dynamics of our quantum battery. In the next section, we examine how different geometrical configurations of the spin chain, namely open boundary, closed boundary, and supercube topologies, affect the storage and extraction of energy.

III.2
QB Performance Metrics

To evaluate the performance of the spin chain quantum batteries, we focus on two standard metrics: ergotropy and charging power.

III.2.1
Ergotropy

Ergotropy is defined as the maximum amount of work that can be extracted from a quantum battery
Allahverdyan et al. 2004
;
Sone and Deffner 2021
;
Tirone et al. 2021
;
Touil et al. 2021
. The concept relies on the notion of a passive state, from which no work can be extracted under unitary operations
Pusz and Woronowicz 1978
;
Lenard 1978
;
Salvia and Giovannetti 2021
;
Mazzoncini et al. 2023
. For pure states, the ground state of the system is passive
Santos et al. 2019
. Accordingly, the ergotropy is given by the difference in energy between the evolved state
|\psi(t)\rangle=U|\psi(0)\rangle
and the ground state
|\psi(0)\rangle
, i.e.

\zeta=\text{Tr}(\rho H_{B})-\max{\text{Tr}(U\rho U^{\dagger}H_{B})},

(14)

where,
\rho
is the density matrix of the system and
H_{B}
denotes the internal Hamiltonian of the battery as defined in Eq. (5).

III.2.2
Charging Power

Charging power quantifies the rate at which extractable work is stored in the system during the charging process. It is defined as

P(t)=\frac{\zeta}{t}.

(15)

A higher charging power indicates faster charging and is therefore desirable for practical applications.

Overall, among the three configurations, the supercube consistently outperforms both open and closed chains in the XXZ model, mirroring its advantage in the Ising case.

Across both Hamiltonian models, the supercube configuration consistently delivers superior performance, combining high ergotropy, strong charging power, and stable charging cycles. In the Ising model, DMI enhances charging power for open and supercube chains but degrades performance in the closed chain. In contrast, under the XXZ model, DMI significantly improves the closed and supercube chains, with the latter displaying clean sinusoidal cycles and negligible residual energy, making it particularly attractive for rechargeable QB applications. Thus, while the influence of DMI depends on both model and topology, the supercube architecture remains the most robust and efficient design. Motivated by the superior performance of the supercube configuration across both Ising and XXZ models, we now examine how variations in interaction parameters and structural modifications affect its charging behaviour.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we studied the charging performance of eight-qubit Heisenberg spin chain quantum batteries (QBs) under the influence of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI). We compared three distinct spin chain configurations, namely, open boundary, closed boundary, and supercube, under both Ising and XXZ Hamiltonians. Our results show that the supercube consistently exhibits superior performance among all three configurations. For the Ising model, DMI enhances ergotropy in both the open boundary and supercube spin chains. In the XXZ model, the supercube configuration achieves a stable, near-ideal sinusoidal charging–discharging cycle, enabling periodic charging with negligible residual energy per cycle for optimal interaction strengths
D=1.7
and
J=1
. We also investigated the effect of including the battery Hamiltonian
H_{B}
in the charging process through the influence parameter
\lambda
. For all configurations and models, increasing
\lambda
reduces the peak ergotropy. Interestingly, in the XXZ model, ergotropy for
D=1.7
and
\lambda>0
is higher than the
\lambda=0
case for most of the evolution.

Additionally, we explored the effect of structural modifications of the supercube configuration. Introducing extra interactions between qubits disrupts the oscillatory behavior observed in the XXZ model. Extending the system size to 12 qubits preserves the sinusoidal nature of the ergotropy curve, but the system no longer discharges fully to its ground state, resulting in residual energy stored in the system. Notably, the 12-qubit icosahedral structure revives a perfect charging–discharging cycle, fully returning to the ground state without residual energy, thereby recovering the efficiency lost in the simple cubic extension. Although entanglement often influences QB performance, it has been shown that entanglement generation is not necessary for optimal work extraction
Hovhannisyan et al. 2013
. In line with this, although our study does not explicitly examine entanglement, the results demonstrate enhancements in ergotropy and power without any direct connection to entanglement. Our findings provide insights into designing scalable and efficient quantum batteries and guide future exploration of optimized interaction topologies and system sizes. Interestingly, supercube-configured spin chain quantum batteries achieve high-efficiency, near-ideal charging cycles without requiring entanglement, providing a scalable route for next-generation quantum energy storage.

Gluon Gravitational D D -Form Factor: The σ \sigma -Meson as a Dilaton Confronted with Lattice Data II


Abstract
We investigate the gluon gravitational form factors of the π \pi , N N , ρ \rho , and Δ \Delta using lattice QCD data at m π ≈ 450 ​ MeV m_{\pi}\approx 450\text{MeV} and m π ≈ 170 ​ MeV m_{\pi}\approx 170\text{MeV} . We base the analysis on fits to a simple σ / f 0 ​ ( 500 ) \sigma/f_{0}(500) -meson pole, supplemented by a polynomial background term. The fitted residues agree with predictions from dilaton effective theory, in which the σ \sigma -meson acts as the dilaton, the pseudo Goldstone boson of spontaneously broken scale symmetry. We derive new dilaton-based predictions for the ρ \rho - and Δ \Delta -gravitational form factors, and comment on the η c \eta_{c} - and η b \eta_{b} -form factors in the context of the dilaton interpretation. These results reinforce our earlier findings, based on lattice total (quark and gluon) gravitational form factors, and provide further evidence that QCD dynamics may be governed by an infrared fixed point.

\sigma

\langle\vartheta(p^{\prime})|T^{R}_{\mu\nu}|\vartheta(p)\rangle=\frac{1}{2m_{\vartheta}}\bar{u}_{i}(p,s)d_{\mu\nu}\frac{4m_{\vartheta}F_{\sigma}}{3}\frac{\,g_{\sigma\vartheta\vartheta}(q^{2})}{q^{2}-m_{\sigma}^{2}}u_{i}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\;,

\gamma_{{\cal O}}=\gamma^{*}_{\bar{q}q}=-\gamma_{m_{q}}^{*}=-1

[
50
,
51
,
82
]

and collecting all the results, including the couplings in (
2.1
), we find

\displaystyle D^{\pi}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\sigma}^{\pi}q^{2}}{q^{2}-m_{\sigma}^{2}}-1\;,\qquad\quad

\displaystyle D^{N}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\sigma}^{N}}{q^{2}-m_{\sigma}^{2}}\;,

\displaystyle D^{\rho}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\sigma}^{\rho}}{q^{2}-m_{\sigma}^{2}}-1\;,\qquad\quad

\displaystyle D^{\Delta}(q^{2})

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{r_{\sigma}^{\Delta}}{q^{2}-m_{\sigma}^{2}}\;,

(2.21)

with residues

r_{\sigma}^{\pi}=\frac{2}{3}\;,\qquad r_{\sigma}^{N}=\frac{4}{3}\bar{m}_{N}^{2}\;,\qquad r_{\sigma}^{\rho}=\frac{4}{3}(\bar{m}_{\rho}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\delta m_{\rho}^{2})\;,\qquad r_{\sigma}^{\Delta}=\frac{4}{3}\bar{m}_{N}^{2}\;.

(2.22)

They are the formal predictions of LO dilaton effective theory.
It is these expression plus a simple background that will provide the basis for the fit ansatz and interpretation of the lattice data.

It would be interesting to extend the form-factor series to higher spins, making use of the recent ghost-free construction developed in
[
86
]
.

2.3
The gluon-fraction of the
\sigma
-meson decay constant

We must account for the fact that the lattice simulation
[
8
]
includes only the gluon contribution
of the energy-momentum tensor (as mentioned in the introduction).
This is to the nucleon’s energy and momentum decomposition
[
87
,
88
]

(with the latter being extensively studied on the lattice
[
89
,
90
,
91
,
92
,
93
]
).
For a meaningful quantitative comparison, we must therefore supply an estimate, at least for the
\sigma
-residue, which we refer to as
r_{\sigma,g}
.
Fortunately, this can be done indirectly via the decay constant since
it is clear from dispersion theory (see for instance
[
94
]
) or Fig
1
, that one has for any hadron state

r_{\sigma}\propto F_{\sigma}\,g_{\sigma HH}\;,\qquad r_{\sigma,g}\propto F_{\sigma,g}\,g_{\sigma HH}\;,

(2.23)

where
F_{\sigma,g}
is the gluon component of the decay constant, defined below.

We emphasise that the formula is model-independent, as the dilaton’s properties are completely encoded in the coupling
g_{\sigma HH}
.
Hence

r^{H}_{\sigma,g}=z_{g}\,r^{H}_{\sigma}\;,\qquad z_{g}(\mu)\equiv\frac{F_{\sigma,g}(\mu)}{F_{\sigma}}\;,

(2.24)

with
z_{g}
being the hadron-universal ratio of gluon-to-total
\sigma
-meson decay constant.

To define this quantity we need to introduce the gluon- and quark-parts of the energy-momentum tensor (e.g.,
[
95
]
)

T^{g}_{\alpha\beta}=G_{\alpha\mu}G^{\mu}_{\beta}-\frac{1}{4}\eta_{\alpha\beta}G^{2}\;,\qquad T^{q}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{q}\bar{q}i\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{D}}_{\{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta\}}q\;,

(2.25)

where
x_{\{\alpha\beta\}}=x_{\alpha\beta}+x_{\beta\alpha}
with

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\leftrightarrow}}{{D}}=\overset{\rightarrow}{D}-\overset{\leftarrow}{D}
where
\overset{\rightarrow}{D}=(\overset{\rightarrow}{\partial}+igA)

and
\overset{\leftarrow}{D}=(\overset{\leftarrow}{\partial}-igA)
.
Inserting them into (
2.17
), define the quark and gluon decay constants

\langle 0|[T^{a}_{\alpha\beta}(\mu)]|\sigma\rangle=\frac{F^{a}_{\sigma}(\mu)}{3}(m_{\sigma}^{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}-q_{\mu}q_{\nu})+\bar{c}_{\sigma}^{a}(\mu)\,m_{\sigma}^{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\;,\quad a\in\{g,q\}\;,

(2.26)

where square brackets denote operator and coupling renormalisation.
The gluon- and quark-parts of the energy-momentum tensor are separately scale- and scheme-dependent
and therefore not conserved, as evidenced by the non-transverse
\bar{c}_{\sigma}^{a}
-term. Their sum

T_{\alpha\beta}=[T^{g}_{\alpha\beta}(\mu)]+[T^{q}_{\alpha\beta}(\mu)]
is physical and conserved which implies
the exact identity
\bar{c}_{\sigma}^{g}+\bar{c}_{\sigma}^{q}=0
.
For the lattice extraction, the constant
\bar{c}_{\sigma}^{a}(\mu)
plays no role because its form factor analogue
\bar{C}_{a}

does not interfere.
To illustrate this, we write the pion decomposition

\langle\pi(p^{\prime})|[T^{a}_{\alpha\beta}(\mu)]|\pi(p)\rangle=a_{\alpha\beta}\,A^{\pi}_{a}(q^{2},\mu)+d_{\alpha\beta}\,{D}^{\pi}_{a}(q^{2},\mu)+2m_{\pi}^{2}\eta_{\alpha\beta}\,\bar{C}^{\pi}_{a}(q^{2},\mu)\;.

(2.27)

Since the
D
-form factor is determined from the
q_{\mu}q_{\nu}
-structure, the
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
-term containing
\bar{C}^{\pi}_{a}
does not enter.
A more comprehensive study of
\bar{c}_{\sigma}^{a}
and
\bar{C}_{a}
is deferred to future work
[
96
]
.

Correspondingly the quark- and gluon-form factors are scale and scheme-dependent. The results in the lattice study are quoted for the
\overline{\text{MS}}
-scheme at
\mu=2\,\mbox{GeV}
.
Hence, we need an estimate of
z_{g}
for this case. We may do so by taking inspiration from the
A
-form factors. Their evolution equation reads, see for instance
[
97
,
95
]
,

\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\vec{A}=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\gamma^{(2)}\vec{A}\;,\qquad\gamma^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}-\dfrac{4}{3}C_{F}&\phantom{-}\dfrac{1}{3}n_{f}\\[8.0pt]
\phantom{-}\dfrac{4}{3}C_{F}&-\dfrac{1}{3}n_{f}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(2.28)

where
\vec{A}^{T}=(A_{q}(q^{2},\mu),A_{g}(q^{2},\mu))
and
\alpha_{s}=g^{2}(\mu)/(4\pi)
as usual.
To simplify the presentation, we focus on
q^{2}=0
and write
A_{a}(\mu)\equiv A_{a}(0,\mu)
, which
are equal to

A_{g}(\mu)=\langle x\rangle_{g}\;,\qquad A_{q}(\mu)=\langle x\rangle_{q}\;,

(2.29)

the gluon
\langle x\rangle_{g}
and the quark-momentum fractions
\langle x\rangle_{q}
of the parton distribution functions (see for example Fig 3.4 in
[
98
]
). This
further implies that
the anomalous dimensions above are the second Mellin moments of the splitting function
\gamma^{(2)}_{ij}=\int_{0}^{1}dxxP_{ij}(x)
.

The solution of the renormalisation group equation, subject to the boundary condition
A_{g}(\mu)+A_{q}(\mu)=1
, reads

A_{a}(\mu)=A_{a}(\infty)+(A_{a}(\mu_{0})-A_{a}(\infty))\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{\alpha_{s}(\mu_{0})}\right)^{\frac{8C_{F}+2n_{f}}{3\beta_{0}}}\;,\quad a\in\{g,q\}\;,

(2.30)

with asymptotic values

A_{g}(\infty)=\frac{4C_{F}}{4C_{F}+n_{f}}\;,\qquad A_{q}(\infty)=\frac{n_{f}}{4C_{F}+n_{f}}\;,

(2.31)

where
C_{F}=(N_{c}^{2}-1)/2N_{c}
for an
SU(N_{c})
gauge group,
n_{f}
the number of flavours and

\frac{d}{d\ln\mu}\alpha_{s}=-\frac{\beta_{0}}{2\pi}\alpha_{s}^{2}+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})
with

\beta_{0}=\frac{11}{3}C_{A}-\frac{2}{3}n_{f}
.

The key point is that the momentum fraction, and thus
A_{a}(\mu)
, is known to be close to their asymptotic
values at intermediate scales. We will focus on the
n_{f}=3
case as appropriate for
the
m_{\pi}\approx 170\,\mbox{MeV}
lattice simulation with dynamical up, down and strange quark

[
9
,
10
]
. At
\mu=2\,\mbox{GeV}
they find

A^{\pi}_{g}(2\,\mbox{GeV})=0.546(18)\;,\qquad A^{N}_{g}(2\,\mbox{GeV})=0.501(27)\;,

(2.32)

whose averaged central values
\approx 0.52
lies within
20\%
of the asymptotic estimate
A_{g}^{\infty}=0.64
(with
C_{F}=4/3
).
This corresponds to a deviation just below
14\%
at
\mu=6\,\mbox{GeV}
which might be considered a preasymptotic scale.
Since for local operators the evolution equations are independent of the external states, we have that
the replacement
A^{\pi,N}_{g}\to z_{g}
is formally equivalent in Eq. (
2.30
). Moreover,
since with
z_{g}+z_{q}=1
the same overall normalisation is shared, this implies the same asymptotic values,

z_{g}(\infty)=A_{g}(\infty)
.
We therefore take

z_{g}(2\,\mbox{GeV})=0.64(20)\;,

(2.33)

as our value used to estimate the gluon residues in Eq. (
2.24
). Whereas a priori
z_{g}

is unconstrained and could, for example, be negative, its relation to the momentum fractions and its proximity to the asymptotic regime prevent this from happening.

One may also assess our reasoning more directly through the
D
-form factor itself.
It satisfies the same evolution equation (
2.28
) and thus the asymptotic values are the same

z_{D_{g}}(\infty)=A_{g}(\infty)
, for the normalised quantity
z_{D_{g}}\equiv D_{g}/D
.
This suggests that we should have
z_{D_{g}}(2\,\mbox{GeV})=z_{D_{g}}(\infty)\pm 20\%
.
As can be inferred from the plots in Fig
4
, this expectation is reasonably well satisfied for the
m_{\pi}\approx 170\,\mbox{MeV}
lattice data.
The plots indicate that at
\mu=2\,\mbox{GeV}
,
z_{D_{g}}
is slightly larger than
A_{g}
, closer to the asymptotic value.
This is compatible with
\sigma
-dominance itself by Eq. (
2.33
), and suggest if anything that the true value of
z_{g}
might be slightly larger than the central value in (
2.33
).
In summary, we find a consistent picture, indicating that (
2.33
) is a trustworthy estimate.

4.2
The dilaton interpretation of the
D(0)<0
hypothesis

We turn to the
q^{2}=0
limit of the
D
-form factor, which has attracted considerable attention.
Among the three nucleon form factors, the infrared interpretation due to mass and spin implies
A^{N}(0)=1
and
J^{N}(0)=\tfrac{1}{2}
, while the value of
D^{N}(0)
remains unclear.
In the Breit-frame, the
D
-form factor is associated with the spatial components of the energy-momentum tensor,
which is interpreted as the internal force
[
115
]
associated with pressure,
and it is argued that for
a positive core pressure and a negative exterior pressure, one obtains
D(0)<0

on grounds of mechanical stability
[
115
,
116
]
.
This conclusion and its interpretation remains actively debated
[
117
,
118
,
4
]
.
6
6

6

For the
\Delta
this interpretation complicates as higher multipoles come into play
[
35
]
.

We previously argued
[
70
]
that the dilaton provides an alternative, microscopic interpretation
which we revisit in the light of the higher spin cases.
Specifically, for any (light) hadron
H
,
D^{H}(0)<0
is guaranteed provided the
\sigma
dominates over the background

D^{H}(0)=-\frac{r_{\sigma}^{H}}{m_{\sigma}^{2}}+b^{H}<0\;,

(4.4)

since
\frac{r_{\sigma}^{H}}{m_{\sigma}^{2}}>0
must hold to approximately satisfy the conformal Ward identity.
For light hadrons, this is supported by our fits to the gluon
D
-form factor in this work and the full form factors
of the
m_{\pi}\approx 170\,\mbox{MeV}
data
[
70
]
.

Does
D(0)<0
hold in all systems? Not for the hydrogen atom, for which
D(0)>0
has been found
[
119
,
120
,
121
]
.
As the hydrogen atom has no dilaton, this does not contradict our interpretation, and in the case of the pressure interpretation it has been argued that long-range forces can provide exceptions to the rule
[
4
]
.
Beyond this, we are not aware of any other firmly established counterexamples.

Let us be more precise. Unlike for the
N
and the
\pi
, the situation for the
\rho
and the
\Delta
is far from clear.
For
D^{\rho}(0)
, the literature reports various possibilities: a negative sign from the Nambu–Jona-Lasino model computation
[
122
]
, a positive sign from the light-cone sum rule computation
[
18
]
, and essentially zero from a constituent quark model linked to generalised parton distribution functions
[
123
]
.
For
D^{\Delta}(0)
(and its
SU_{F}(3)
-related
D^{\Omega}(0)
), one finds a negative sign from light-cone sum rules
[
22
,
24
]
, whereas positive signs have been reported in a covariant quark-diquark approach
[
124
]
.
Given this broad range of results, it is difficult to draw firm conclusions.
We therefore maintain our interpretation of
D(0)<0
for light hadrons based on our fits to the lattice data.

5
Heavy Hadron
D
-Form Factors are Different

Beyond light hadrons, it is instructive to examine heavy hadrons in the context of the
dilaton hypothesis.
In particular, the
\eta_{b,c}
-mesons, the beauty and charm analogues of positronium, are approximately stable since the width-to-mass ratios are small,

(\Gamma/m)|_{\eta_{b,c}}\approx 10^{-2}

[
101
]
.
Their gravitational form factors were recently computed in the Dyson-Schwinger framework using a contact interaction
[
31
]
.
7
7

7

In that work, the
\pi
- and
\eta_{s}
-mesons are also shown.
The
\pi
-result qualitatively (if not quantitatively) agrees with the
m_{\pi}\approx 170~\,\mbox{MeV}
lattice data
[
10
]
. For the
\eta_{s}
, which mixes into
\eta
–
\eta^{\prime}
in the real world, we are not aware of results
one could compare to.

From their Fig 5 it is evident that the
\eta_{b,c}
results do not exhibit a pronounced
\sigma
-pole behaviour, certainly not of the magnitude

r_{\sigma}^{\eta_{b,c}}\approx\tfrac{4}{3}m_{\eta_{b,c}}^{2}
. Stated differently, if we naively use the approximate
light-hadron formula (
4.4
) neglecting the background,

D^{\eta_{b[c]}}(0)\big|_{\text{naive}}=-\frac{4}{3}\frac{m_{\eta_{b[c]}}^{2}}{m_{\sigma}^{2}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad D^{\eta_{b}}(0)|_{\text{naive}}\approx-1500\;,\quad D^{\eta_{c}}(0)|_{\text{naive}}\approx-50\;,

(5.1)

then it fails by orders of magnitude compared to the results
D^{\eta_{b[c]}}(0)\approx-0.5[-0.6]
in
[
31
]
.
A moment’s thought makes the strong suppression unsurprising since

m_{\eta_{b,c}}=2m_{b,c}(1+{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{2}))
,
so their masses are generated primarily by explicit quark-mass terms.
Consequently, the dilaton need not act as a compensator to restore conformal symmetry
for these states. Stated differently, the explicit breaking alters the conformal Ward identities.
It is therefore encouraging that the
\eta_{b,c}
-mesons are consistent with the behaviour anticipated within the dilaton interpretation.

6
Conclusions and Discussion

We investigated gluon gravitational form factors of the

\pi
,
N
,
\rho
and
\Delta
using lattice QCD data at

m_{\pi}\approx 450
and
m_{\pi}\approx 170\,\mbox{MeV}
.
By fitting a simple
\sigma/f_{0}(500)
-pole supplemented by a background term
(
4.2
,
4.3
), we tested the hypothesis that the
\sigma
-meson acts as
a (pseudo) dilaton; the (pseudo) Goldstone boson of spontaneously broken scale invariance.
The extracted residues for all hadrons are compatible within uncertainties
with the predictions of dilaton effective theory, see Tab.
2
and Fig
3
,
supporting the hypothesis.
All residues are positive and, together with
\sigma
-dominance, provide an alternative to the conventional
D
-term hypothesis in hadronic systems (
D(0)<0
).

In addition, we find that the weak
\sigma
-pole contributions in the
D^{\eta_{b,c}}
-form factor, as obtained in the Dyson-Schwinger framework, are consistent with the dilaton picture.
Their masses are generated primarily by explicit breaking of scale symmetry. So, in this regime the dilaton is not required to couple to the mass term directly since the explicit breaking alters the conformal Ward identities.

The overall agreement across hadrons of spin
j=0,\tfrac{1}{2},1,\tfrac{3}{2}
with the lattice data reinforce
the universality of the dilaton coupling structure observed in our previous study
[
70
]
.
While a numerical coincidence cannot be ruled out, the consistent pattern across distinct hadronic channels strongly suggests a common origin in the spontaneous breaking of scale symmetry.
In this sense, the
\sigma
emerges as a
pseudo dilaton
, a massive remnant
of the potential Goldstone boson associated with broken scale invariance.

The behaviour of the
\sigma
, particularly its complex pole for massless quarks, remains an open question.
We hope that future lattice QCD studies
[
110
,
111
,
112
]
,
the analytic
S
-matrix bootstrap
[
125
]
or dispersive methods
[
126
]

will shed further light on this issue.

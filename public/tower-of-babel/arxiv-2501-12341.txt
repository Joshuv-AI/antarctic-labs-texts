1.
Introduction and preliminaries

Let
X
be a pointed metric space, and let
E,F
be Banach spaces. Inspired
by the idea presented in
[
9
, Proposition 6.15]
, where the authors
defined mappings
T:X\times E\longrightarrow F
that are Lipschitz for the
first component and linear for the second component, we investigate a
similar class of mappings defined on the cartesian product
X\times E
with
values in a Banach space. The space of such mappings, denoted
LipL(X\times E;F),
is referred to as the space of Lip-Linear operators. In
[
7
]
,
the authors explore the concept of two-Lipschitz operators defined on the
cartesian product
X\times Y
of two pointed metric spaces, where the
operators are Lipschitz with respect to both components.

This
definition builds upon the work initiated by Dubei in 2009
[
4
]
. By
considering two-Lipschitz operators defined on
X\times E
with the second
component being linear, we precisely recover the type of operators studied
in this paper. In the classical setting, tensor products serve as a powerful
tool to relate bilinear mappings to their corresponding linear mappings.
Specifically, the space of bilinear operators
\mathcal{B}\left(E\times F;G\right)
is isometrically identified with the space of linear operators
\mathcal{L}(E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F;G),
where
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}F
is the projective tensor product of
E
and
F
. A similar approach
applies to Lipschitz tensor products. By considering linear mappings defined
on the Lipschitz tensor product
X\boxtimes E,
we aim to study how the
corresponding mappings behave on the cartesian product
X\times E.
Let
u:X\boxtimes E\rightarrow F
be a linear operator. The corresponding
operator
T_{u}:X\times E\rightarrow F
defined by
T_{u}\left(x,e\right)=u\left(\delta_{\left(x,0\right)}\boxtimes e\right)
for each
x\in X

and
e\in E,
is Lip-Linear. Conversely, if
T:X\times E\rightarrow F
is a
Lip-Linear operator, then there exists a linear operator
\widehat{T}:X\boxtimes E\rightarrow F
such that

\widehat{T}(\mathop{\displaystyle\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i})=\mathop{\displaystyle\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}T\left(x_{i},e_{i}\right)-T\left(y_{i},e_{i}\right).

We therefore have the algebraic identification

LipL(X\times E;F)=\mathcal{L}(X\boxtimes E;F),

which can be represented in the following diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}X\times E&\overset{T}{\longrightarrow}&F\\
\sigma^{LipL}\downarrow&\nearrow\widehat{T}&\\
X\boxtimes E&&\end{array}

where
\sigma^{LipL}=\delta_{X}\boxtimes id_{E}.
We equip the space
LipL(X\times E;F)
with the norm

LipL\left(T\right)=\sup_{x\neq y;\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,e\right)-T\left(y,e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)},

and denote by
LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
the Banach space of all Lip-Linear
operators from
X\times E
to
F
such that
T(0,e)=0
for each
e\in E
.
The correspondence
T\longleftrightarrow\widehat{T}
is an isometric
isomorphism between the Banach spaces
LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
and
\mathcal{L}(X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E;F)
and consequently with the space of
bilinear operators
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}(X)\times E;F).
If
F=\mathbb{K}
, we simply denote
LipL_{0}(X\times E)
. In this case, we obtain a
Lipschitz version of a well-known classical result

\left(X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E\right)^{\ast}=LipL_{0}(X\times E).

In the linear case, if
v:E\rightarrow F
is a linear operator, its
corresponding bilinear form
B_{v}:E\times F^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}

is defined as follows

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(e,z^{\ast}\right)&\longmapsto&z^{\ast}\left(v\left(e\right)\right).\end{array}

This approach can be extended using Lip-linear forms. Specifically, for a
Lipschitz operator
R:X\rightarrow E
, we associate a Lip-linear form
T_{R}

defined by

\begin{array}[]{cccc}T_{R}&X\times E^{\ast}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{K}\end{array}\begin{array}[]{ccc}\left(x,e^{\ast}\right)&\longmapsto&e^{\ast}\left(R\left(x\right)\right)\end{array}

(1.1)

In the sequel, we examine the relationship between a Lipschitz operator and
its associated Lip-linear operator in the context of Lipschitz
p
-summing
and integral operators. The theory of summing operators, developed
extensively in various directions, can be naturally extended to Lip-Linear
operators. A key objective in introducing Lip-linear operators is to
generalize this theory and establish identification results. We propose new
definitions of summability and explore the connection between Lip-Linear
operators and their associated operators within these frameworks.

This paper is structured as follows: Section 1 introduces the necessary
definitions and fundamental concepts, including Lipschitz mappings, linear
and bilinear operators, and key identification results. We also recall de
definition of itegral linear and bilinear operators, as well as
two-Lipschitz operators. In Section 2, we define the space
LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
of Lip-linear operators from the cartesian product
X\times E
to a
Banach space
F
, exploring its properties and establishing relevant
identifications. We show that
LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
is isometrically
isomorphic to the space of bilinear operators
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}(X)\times E;F)
. Furthermore, when
X
and
Y
are pointed metric spaces, we
establish an identification between the space of two-Lipschitz operators
Blip_{0}(X\times Y;F)
and
LipL_{0}(X\times\mathcal{F}(Y);F)
. Section 3
extends the concept of integral operators to Lip-linear operators, allowing
us to establish identification results between the topological dual of
X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}F
and the space of integral Lip-linear
forms
LipL_{0}^{int}(X\times E)
. We also explore the relationship between
Lipschitz operators and their corresponding Lip-linear operators in the
context of integrability. Additionally, several factorization results are
presented. Finally, Section 4 introduces the concept of dominated
(p,q)
-summing Lip-linear operators, establishing key results and exploring their
fundamental properties.

2.
The Class of Lip-Linear operators

Let
X
be a pointed metric space and
E
a Banach space. We define
Lip-linear operators on the cartesian product
X\times E
as mappings that
combine the properties of Lipschitz and linear operators. These operators
can be interpreted either as linear mappings acting on the Lipschitz tensor
product
X\boxtimes E,
or as bilinear mappings acting on
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\times E
. Among our results, we show that every two-Lipschitz
operator defined on the cartesian product of two pointed metric spaces
X\times Y
can be factored through a Lip-linear operator defined on
X\times\mathcal{F}\left(Y\right)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a pointed metric space, and let
E,F
be Banach spaces. A mapping

T:X\times E\longrightarrow F
is called a Lip-Linear operator if there is a
constant
C>0
such that for all
x,y\in X
and
e\in E
,

\left\|T(x,e)-T(y,e)\right\|\leq Cd(x,y)\left\|e\right\|,

(2.1)

and
T(x,\cdot)
is a linear operator for each fixed
x\in X
.

By definition,
T(x,0)=0
for every
x\in X
. We denote by
LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
the space of all Lip-Linear operators from
X\times E
to
F
such
that
T(0,e)=0
for every
e\in E
. For
T\in LipL_{0}(X\times E;F),
define

LipL\left(T\right):=\inf\left\{C:\text{ }C\text{ satisfies~ }(\ref{21})\right\}=\sup_{x\neq y;\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,e\right)-T\left(y,e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}.

It can be shown that
(LipL_{0},LipL\left(\cdot\right))
is a Banach
space. The proof is omitted. Note that, every Lip-Linear operator is
two-Lipschitz, with
Blip(T)\leq LipL(T).
More generally, if
E,F
and
G

be Banach spaces, the following inclusions hold

\mathcal{B}\left(E\times F;G\right)\subset LipL_{0}\left(E\times F;G\right)\subset BLip_{0}(E\times F;G).

(2.2)

and

Blip(T)\leq LipL(T)\leq\|T\|.

Indeed, by definition, the inclusions in (
2.2
) become evident. Let
T\in\mathcal{B}\left(E\times F;G\right)
, we have

\displaystyle Blip(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y\text{ }x^{\prime}\neq y^{\prime}}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,y\right)-T\left(x,y^{\prime}\right)-T\left(x^{\prime},y\right)+T\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)\right\|}{\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|\left\|y-y^{\prime}\right\|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y\text{ }x^{\prime}\neq y^{\prime}}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,y-y^{\prime}\right)-T\left(x^{\prime},y-y^{\prime}\right)\right\|}{\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|\left\|y-y^{\prime}\right\|}

for
e=y-y^{\prime}
with
\left\|e\right\|=1
, this becomes

Blip(T)\leq\sup_{x\neq y\text{ }\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,e\right)-T\left(x^{\prime},e\right)\right\|}{\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|}=LipL(T).

again,

\displaystyle LipL(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y\text{ }\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|T\left(x-x^{\prime},e\right)\right\|}{\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y\text{ }\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|T\right\|\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|\left\|e\right\|}{\left\|x-x^{\prime}\right\|}=\left\|T\right\|.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R:X\rightarrow G
be a Lipschitz operator and
v:E\rightarrow F
a
linear operator. For any bilinear operator
B:G\times F\rightarrow H
, the
operator
T=B\circ(R,v)
is Lip-Linear, and

LipL\left(T\right)\leq\left\|B\right\|Lip\left(R\right)\left\|v\right\|.

This means that
\mathcal{B}\circ(Lip_{0},\mathcal{L})\left(X\times E;H\right)\subset LipL_{0}\left(X\times E;H\right)
.

Proof.

For
x\in X,
the operator
T(x,\cdot)=B(u\left(x\right),\cdot)
is
linear. Let
x,y\in X
and
e\in E,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|T(x,e)-T(y,e)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|B(R\left(x\right),v\left(e\right))-B(R\left(y\right),v\left(e\right))\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|B(R\left(x\right)-R\left(y\right),v\left(e\right))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|B\right\|\left\|R\left(x\right)-R\left(y\right)\right\|\left\|v\left(e\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|B\right\|Lip\left(R\right)\left\|v\right\|d\left(x,y\right)\left\|e\right\|.

Thus,
T=B(R,v)
is Lip-Linear, and

LipL\left(T\right)\leq\left\|B\right\|Lip\left(R\right)\left\|v\right\|.

For the mapping
T:X\times E\longrightarrow F,
we define the associated
operators
A_{T}:x\mapsto A_{T}\left(x\right)
and
B_{T}:e\mapsto B_{T}\left(e\right),
where
A_{T}(x)\left(e\right)=T(x,e)
and
B_{T}(e)\left(x\right)=T(x,e)
for every
x\in X
and
e\in E.
This
yields the following characterization of a Lip-linear operator.

Proposition 2.3
.

For a mapping
T:X\times E\longrightarrow F
, the following statements are
equivalent.

1)
T\in LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
.

2) The Lipschitz operator
A_{T}\in Lip_{0}(X;\mathcal{L}(E;F))

3) The linear operator
B_{T}\in\mathcal{L}(E;Lip_{0}(X;F)).

In this case,

LipL(T)=Lip\left(A_{T}\right)=\left\|B_{T}\right\|.

(2.3)

Proof.

We verify the equality
(\ref{2.3})
as follows:

\displaystyle Lip\left(A_{T}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\left\|A_{T}\left(x\right)-A_{T}\left(y\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}=\sup_{\left\|e\right\|=1}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\left\|A_{T}\left(x\right)\left(e\right)-A_{T}\left(y\right)\left(e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|e\right\|=1}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,e\right)-T\left(y,e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}=LipL(T).

For the second part, we compute

\displaystyle\left\|B_{T}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|e\right\|=1}Lip\left(B_{T}\left(e\right)\right)=\sup_{\left\|e\right\|=1}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\left\|B_{T}\left(e\right)\left(x\right)-B_{T}\left(e\right)\left(y\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|e\right\|=1}\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{\left\|T\left(x,e\right)-T\left(y,e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}=LipL(T).

As an immediate consequence of the last Proposition, we have the following
result.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
X
be a pointed metric space, and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. For
a two-Lipschitz mapping
T:X\times E\longrightarrow F
, the following
statements are equivalent.

1)
T\in LipL_{0}(X\times E;F)
.

2)
A_{T}\left(x\right)\in\mathcal{L}(E;F)
for every fixed
x\in X.

In this case, we have
LipL(T)=Blip(T).

The following result is straightforward.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X
be a pointed metric space, and let
E,F
be Banach
spaces. Let

be a linear operator. The operator
\delta_{X}\boxtimes v:X\times E\rightarrow X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}F
defined
by

\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\left(x,e\right)=\delta_{\left(x,0\right)}\boxtimes v\left(e\right),

is Lip-linear, and we have

LipL\left(\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\right)=\left\|v\right\|.

Proof.

It is clear that
\delta_{X}\boxtimes v
is linear for the second variable,
and

\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\left(0,e\right)=\delta_{\left(0,0\right)}\boxtimes v\left(e\right)=0,

for every
e\in E
. Let
x,y\in X
and
e\in E
then

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\left(x,e\right)-\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\left(y,e\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{\left(x,0\right)}\boxtimes v\left(e\right)-\delta_{\left(y,0\right)}\boxtimes v\left(e\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\delta_{\left(x,0\right)}-\delta_{\left(y,0\right)}\right)\boxtimes v\left(e\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{\left(x,y\right)}\boxtimes v\left(e\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{\left(x,y\right)}\right\|\left\|v\left(e\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|d\left(x,y\right)\left\|e\right\|,

thus,
\delta_{X}\boxtimes v
is Lip-linear and

\displaystyle LipL\left(\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y,\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\left(x,e\right)-\delta_{X}\boxtimes v\left(y,e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y,\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{\left\|\delta_{\left(x,y\right)}\right\|\left\|v\left(e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\neq y,\left\|e\right\|=1}\frac{d\left(x,y\right)\left\|v\left(e\right)\right\|}{d\left(x,y\right)}=\left\|v\right\|.

Consider the mapping
\sigma^{LipL}:X\times E\rightarrow X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E
defined by

\sigma^{LipL}(x,e)=\delta_{\left(x,0\right)}\boxtimes e.

It is straightforward to verify that
\sigma^{LipL}=\delta_{X}\boxtimes id_{E}.
By Lemma
\ref{Lemme1},
we conclude that
\sigma^{LipL}\in LipL_{0}(X\times E;X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E)
and
LipL(\sigma^{LipL})=1.
Let
T\in LipL_{0}(X\times E;F),
from
(\ref{22}),
we know
that
T
is two-Lipschitz. The factorization theorem for two-Lipschitz
operators, as presented in
[
7
, Remark 2.7]
, guarantees the existence
of a unique bounded linear operator
\widetilde{T}:\mathcal{F}(X)\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}(E)\rightarrow F
such that

T=\widetilde{T}\circ\sigma_{2}\circ\left(\delta_{X},\delta_{E}\right),

where
\sigma_{2}
is the canonical continuous bilinear map defined in
\left(\ref{1,2}\right)
. This implies that the following diagram commutes:

\begin{array}[]{ccc}X\times E&&\\
\left(\delta_{X},\delta_{E}\right)\downarrow&T\searrow&\\
\mathcal{F}(X)\times\mathcal{F}(E)&&F\\
\sigma_{2}\downarrow&\widetilde{T}\nearrow&\\
\mathcal{F}(X)\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}\mathcal{F}(E)&&\end{array}

The following theorem provides a further factorization result for Lip-linear

Theorem 2.8
.

For every Lip-Linear operator
T\in LipL_{0}(X\times E;F),
there exists a unique bounded linear operator
\widehat{T}:X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E\rightarrow F
defined by

\widehat{T}(\delta_{\left(x,y\right)}\boxtimes e)=T(x,e)-T(y,e),

for all
x,y\in X
and
e\in E.
In other words,
T=\widehat{T}\circ\sigma^{LipL},
ensuring that the following diagram commutes

\begin{array}[]{ccc}X\times E&\overset{T}{\longrightarrow}&F\\
\sigma^{LipL}\downarrow&\nearrow\widehat{T}&\\
X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E&&\end{array}

In this case, we have

LipL\left(T\right)=\left\|\widehat{T}\right\|.

Proof.

Let us first show that
\widehat{T}
is bounded with respect to the
Lipschitz projective norm on
X\boxtimes E
. For any element
u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}\in X\boxtimes E,
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{T}\left(u\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}T\left(x_{i},e_{i}\right)-T\left(y_{i},e_{i}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|T\left(x_{i},e_{i}\right)-T\left(y_{i},e_{i}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle LipL\left(T\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}d\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)\left\|e_{i}\right\|.

Since this holds for every representation of
u,
it follows that

\|\widehat{T}(u)\|\leq LipL(T)\pi(u).

Thus,
\widehat{T}
is bounded, and we have
\left\|\widehat{T}\right\|\leq LipL(T).
Conversely, we have

\displaystyle\|T(x,e)-T(y,e)\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\widehat{T}(\delta_{\left(x,y\right)}\boxtimes e)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\widehat{T}\|d(x,y)\|e\|

which implies that
LipL(T)\leq\|\widehat{T}\|.
Therefore, the
operator
\widehat{T}:X\boxtimes_{\pi}E\rightarrow F
has a unique
extension to
X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E
with the same norm. The mapping

T\mapsto\widehat{T}
is clearly a linear isometry. For scalars
\alpha

and
\beta,
we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha S+\beta T}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha S+\beta T}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\alpha S+\beta T\right)\left(x_{i},e_{i}\right)-\left(\alpha S+\beta T\right)\left(y_{i},e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha\left(\widehat{S}\left(\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}\right)\right)+\beta\widehat{T}\left(\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\widehat{S}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i})+\beta\widehat{T}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}).

Thus, we conclude that

\widehat{\alpha S+\beta T}\left(u\right)=\left(\alpha\widehat{S}+\beta\widehat{T}\right)\left(u\right).

It remains to show that this mapping is surjective. Let
A\in\mathcal{L}(X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\pi}E;F)
. The Lip-Linear operator defined by

T(x,e)=A(\delta_{\left(x,0\right)}\boxtimes e)

satisfies
\widehat{T}=A,
completing the proof.

The previous theorem establishes the following isometric identification

(2.4)

Proposition 2.10
.

3.
Integral Lip-Linear operators and the dual of
X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}E

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces, and let
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F
denote their injective tensor product. In the classical case, it is
well-known that the dual space of
E\widehat{\otimes}_{\varepsilon}F

coincides with the space of integral bilinear forms on
E\times F.

Motivated by this fact, we introduce a new definition of integral Lip-Linear
operators and establish that their linearization operators are integral.
Among our results, we prove that the dual space of
X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}E
coincides with the space of integral Lip-Linear forms. Let

X
be a pointed metric space, and let
E,F
be Banach space. A Lip-Linear
operator
T:X\times E\rightarrow F
is said to be integral if there exists a
constant
C>0
such that for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}

in
X
,
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
in
E
and
z_{1}^{\ast},...,z_{n}^{\ast}\in F^{\ast}
we have

\left|\mathop{\displaystyle\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle T(x_{i},e_{i})-T(y_{i},e_{i}),z_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\sup_{f\in B_{X^{\#}},\left\|e^{\ast}\right\|=1}\left\|\mathop{\displaystyle\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(f(x_{i})-f(y_{i})\right)e^{\ast}\left(e_{i}\right)z_{i}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}.

(3.1)

The class of all integral Lip-Linear operators is denoted by
LipL_{0}^{int}(X\times E;F)
. For such operators, we define

\mathfrak{I}_{int}^{LipL}(T)=\inf\left\{C:C\text{ satisfies }(\ref{31})\right\}.

It is straightforward to verify that the pair
\left(LipL_{0}^{int}(X\times E;F),\mathfrak{I}_{int}^{LipL}(\cdot)\right)
forms a Banach space. The
importance of this new definition lies in its connection with the
linearization operators, allowing the extension of certain classical
results. The next theorem establishes this relationship.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a pointed metric space, and let
E,F
be
Banach spaces. The following properties are equivalent:

1) The Lip-Linear operator
T
is integral.

2) The linearization
\widehat{T}:X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}E\longrightarrow F
is intgral.

Proof.

1)\Longrightarrow 2):
Let
T:X\times E\longrightarrow F
be an itegral
Lip-Linear operator. For
u_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\left(x_{i}^{j},y_{i}^{j}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}^{j}\in X\boxtimes_{\varepsilon}E
and
z_{j}^{\ast}\in F^{\ast}\left(1\leq j\leq m\right).
We have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left\langle\widehat{T}(u_{j}),z_{j}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle T(x_{i}^{j},e_{i}^{j})-T(y_{i}^{j},e_{i}^{j}),z_{j}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{int}^{LipL}\left(T\right)\sup_{f\in B_{X^{\#}},\left\|e^{\ast}\right\|=1}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f(x_{i}^{j})-f(y_{i}^{j})\right)e^{\ast}\left(e_{i}^{j}\right)z_{j}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}_{int}^{LipL}\left(T\right)\sup_{f\in B_{X^{\#}},\left\|e^{\ast}\right\|=1}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}f\boxtimes e^{\ast}\left(u_{j}\right)z_{j}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}.

Since

\sup_{f\in B_{X^{\#}},\left\|e^{\ast}\right\|=1}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}f\boxtimes e^{\ast}\left(u_{j}\right)z_{j}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}=\sup\left\{\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\phi\left(u_{j}\right)z_{j}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}:\phi\in B_{\left(X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}E\right)^{\ast}}\right\}

It follows that
\widehat{T}
is integral, with
\mathfrak{I}\left(\widehat{T}\right)\leq\mathfrak{I}_{int}^{LipL}(T).

2)\Longrightarrow 1):
Suppose
\widehat{T}:X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}E\longrightarrow F
is integral. Let
\left(x_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n},\left(y_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset X
,
\left(e_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset E
and
\left(z_{i}^{\ast}\right)_{i=1}^{n}\subset F^{\ast}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathop{\displaystyle\sum}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle T(x_{i},e_{i})-T(y_{i},e_{i}),z_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\widehat{T}\left(\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}\right),z_{i}^{\ast}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}\left(\widehat{T}\right)\sup\left\{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi\left(\delta_{\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)}\boxtimes e_{i}\right)z_{i}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}:\phi\in B_{\left(X\widehat{\boxtimes}_{\varepsilon}E\right)^{\ast}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathfrak{I}\left(\widehat{T}\right)\sup_{f\in B_{X^{\#}},\left\|e^{\ast}\right\|=1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(f(x_{i})-f(y_{i})\right)e^{\ast}\left(e_{i}\right)z_{i}^{\ast}\right\|_{F^{\ast}}.

Thus,
T
is integral and
\mathfrak{I}_{int}^{LipL}(T)\leq\mathfrak{I}\left(\widehat{T}\right).

.

Let

4.

The class of absolutely
(s;p,q)

Definition 4.1

Semi-Classical Localization of the Schrödinger Resolvent on Closed Riemann Surfaces
Sébastien Campagne

Abstract
. This paper investigates the localization properties of solutions to the semi-classical Schrödinger equation on closed Riemann surfaces. Unlike classical studies that assume a smooth potential, our work addresses the challenges arising from irregular potentials, specifically those that are merely bounded. We employ a regularization technique to manage the potential’s lack of smoothness and establish a local-to-global estimate. This result provides a quantitative measure of how the local regularity of the potential influences the global concentration of the solution, thereby bridging the gap between smooth and non-continuous regimes in semi-classical analysis.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the localization properties of solutions
u\in H^{2}
to the semi-classical Schrödinger equation:

(1.1)

(P_{V}-E)u:=(-h^{2}\Delta+V-E)u=f,

where
V
is a real-valued, bounded potential defined on a closed Riemann surface
M
,
E\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an energy level varying in a compact set
I
, and
f\in L^{2}(M)
is a source term.

It is well established that when
V
is smooth, one can derive
the following local-to-global estimate: for any open subset
U\subset M
and for sufficiently small
h
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.2)

\int_{M}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\leq Ce^{C/h}\left(\int_{U}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{M}|f|^{2}\right).

This estimate relies on techniques from microlocal analysis, particularly semiclassical Carleman estimates. Specifically, for
R>0
and
E\in I
, there exists
C>0
such that for all sufficiently small
h
and all
u\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,R))
,

(1.3)

\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\leq Ce^{C/h}\int_{B(0,R)}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2},

as proved by
3
(see, e.g., Theorem 2.32).

In the present work, the potential
V
is not assumed to be smooth, precluding
the direct application of these classical results. We therefore develop alternative methods to address these challenges. Recent studies have shown that Carleman-type estimates can be obtained under weaker regularity assumptions on
V
. For instance,
4
established a semiclassical Carleman estimate for potentials
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
: for any
R>0
and
E\in I
, there exists
C>0
such that for sufficiently small
h
and
u\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(B(0,R))
,

(1.4)

\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\leq Ce^{C/h^{4/3}}\int_{B(0,R)}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2}.

Subsequently,

5
refined this estimate by incorporating the Hölder regularity of
V
: for an
\alpha
-Hölder potential
V
, it was shown that

(1.5)

\int_{B(0,R)}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\leq Ce^{Ch^{-4/(\alpha+1)}}\int_{B(0,R)}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2}.

Inspired by Vodev’s approach, we establish the following result on closed Riemann surfaces as opposed to the traditional Euclidean plan or non-compact hyperbolic surfaces:

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemann surface, and let
U\subset M
be an open subset. Let
E\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
and
V\in L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Then there exist constants
C>0
and
h_{0}>0
such that for all
0<h<h_{0}
and
u\in H^{2}(M)
,

(1.6)

\int_{M}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\leq Ce^{C\beta(h)}\left(\int_{U}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{M}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2}\right),

where

(1.7)

\beta(h)=\frac{1}{h^{4/3}}\sup_{x_{0}\in M}\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},h^{2/3}\kappa)}|V(x)-V(x_{0})|^{1/2},

and
\kappa>0
is a fixed small constant.

The function
\beta(h)
quantifies the local regularity of the potential
V
. When
V
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous, we have

\beta(h)\leq C_{0}h^{2\alpha/3}\kappa^{\alpha},

and thus,

(1.8)

\int_{M}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}\leq Ce^{Ch^{\frac{\alpha-4}{3}}}\left(\int_{U}|u|^{2}+|h\nabla u|^{2}+\int_{M}|(P_{V}-E)u|^{2}\right).

This recovers the classical smooth case when
V
is Lipschitz, although our estimate is slightly weaker than Vodev’s. Nevertheless, it also recovers the estimate of Klopp and Vogel for merely bounded and non-continuous potentials
V
.

Our proof follows the general strategy of
5
, adapted to the geometry of closed Riemann surfaces and the lower regularity of the potential
V
.

Proof of theorem

2.1.
Reduction of the problem

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian surface. Thanks to the Poincaré uniformization theorem,
we know that
M
is conformally equivalent to a unique closed surface of constant curvature. This surface is a quotient of one of the following covering surfaces by a free action of a discrete subgroup of its isometry group:

•

the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

•

the sphere
S^{2}
,

•

the hyperbolic disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

(see, for instance, the book of
2
).

Consequently,
M
can be viewed as a compact quotient of one of these three surfaces.
These three surfaces can be covered by
M
via the free action of the discrete subgroup. We can then view the function

as a periodic function
u_{\text{per}}
on the covering space,
where
u_{\text{per}}
satisfies the following periodic Schrödinger equation on the covering:

(2.1)

(-h^{2}\Delta+V_{\text{per}}-E)u_{\text{per}}=f_{\text{per}}.

This reduces our analysis to just three cases:

•

Case of the Euclidean plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

We can replace
U
by a small ball
B(0,R)
centered at
0
inside
M
.
It is sufficient to work in a larger ball
B(0,R_{0})
containing
M
.

•

Case of the hyperbolic disk
\mathbb{H}^{2}
:

We can replace
U
by a small ball
B(0,R)
centered at
0
inside
M
.
It is sufficient to work in a larger ball
B(0,R_{0})
inside the disk. The Poincaré disk is equipped with the metric

(2.2)

ds^{2}=\frac{4\sum_{i}dx_{i}^{2}}{(1-\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2})^{2}},

so on
B(0,R_{0})
, the metric is equivalent to a Euclidean metric. Consequently, this case can be treated similarly to the Euclidean plane case.

•

Case of the sphere
S^{2}
:

We can replace
U
by a small ball
B(0,R)
centered at the south pole of
S^{2}
and consider
M
as
S^{2}
.
We then remove a smaller ball
B(0,R/2)
centered at the south pole inside
B(0,R)
.
The perforated sphere can be unfolded onto a ball
B(0,R_{0})
, so that
B(0,R)\setminus B(0,R/2)
becomes a ring
A(0,\tilde{R}_{0},R_{0})
at the edge of
B(0,R_{0})
, where the metric becomes Euclidean.

Thus, in each case, we reduce our study to a ball
B(0,R_{0})
. However, we must still consider two subcases:
U
is identified with a ball
B(0,R)
centered at
0
inside
B(0,R_{0})
, or
U
is identified with an ring

at the edge of
B(0,R_{0})
.

2.2.
Carleman estimate on a ball

In view of the results from the previous section, let
R_{0}>0
,
R>0
, and
\tilde{R}_{0}>0

R<R_{0}

\tilde{R}_{0}<R_{0}

Let

. To do so, we first extend

by
0
outside

to the entire plane

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})

We assume that
\varphi
satisfies the following properties:

•

\varphi
has total mass
1

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(x)\,dx=1

•

0

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(x)\,dx=0

We then define the regularized potential as follows:

(2.3)

\tilde{V}_{\theta}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}V_{per}(x-t)\varphi(\frac{t}{\theta})dt,\mbox{where }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mbox{ and }\theta\in]0,1]

\tilde{V}_{\theta}
is a "smooth version" of
V_{per}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

for
\theta>0
and
i\in\{1,2\}
:

(2.4)

||\tilde{V}_{\theta}-V_{per}||_{L^{\infty}(B(0,R_{0}))}\leq\omega_{V}(\theta)

and:

(2.5)

||\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{V}_{\theta}||_{L^{\infty}(B(0,R_{0}))}\leq\frac{1}{\theta}\omega_{V}(\theta)

with

(2.6)

\omega_{V}(\theta):=\sup_{x_{0}\in B(0,R_{0})}\sup_{t\in B(0,\theta)}|V_{per}(x_{0}-t)-V_{per}(x_{0})|

Proof.

Indeed for
x\in B(0,R_{0})
:

\displaystyle|\tilde{V}_{\theta}(x)-V_{per}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta^{2}}|V_{per}(x-t)-V_{per}(x)|\varphi(\frac{t}{\theta})dt

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in B(0,\theta)}|V_{per}(x-t)-V_{per}(x)|

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x_{0}\in B(0,R_{0})}\sup_{t\in B(0,\theta)}|V_{per}(x_{0}-t)-V_{per}(x_{0})|=\omega_{V}(\theta)

As for the second property, for
i\in\{1,2\}
:

\displaystyle|\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{V}_{\theta}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\theta^{3}}|V_{per}(x-t)-V_{per}(x)||\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(\frac{t}{\theta})|dt

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi\rVert_{L^{1}}\frac{1}{\theta}\sup_{t\in B(0,\theta)}|V_{per}(x-t)-V_{per}(x)|

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{L^{1}}\frac{1}{\theta}\omega_{V}(\theta)

∎

If
V
is sufficiently regular (i.e., continuous), then

converges to

V_{\text{per}}

\omega_{V}(\theta)

\theta

Due to the extension of

may not tend to

and assume that

is originally defined on a larger ball

, and then extended by
0
outside

. For sufficiently small

then depends solely on the regularity of

within the ball

.

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}h^{2/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}\chi e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}|(-h^{2}\Delta_{x}+V-E)u|^{2}dx

(2.56)

\displaystyle\quad+C\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}h^{2/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}|[-h^{2}\Delta_{x},\chi]u|^{2}dx

The support of
\nabla\chi
is in
A(0,R_{2},R_{0})\cup A(0,r/2,R_{1})
so the support of
[-h^{2}\Delta_{x},\chi]u
is also in
A(0,R_{2},R_{0})\cup A(0,R/2,R_{1})
. Moreover there is
\beta>0

(2.57)

|[-h^{2}\Delta_{x},\chi]u|^{2}\leq h^{2}\beta(|u|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u|^{2})

Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,R_{1},R_{2})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}h^{2/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}\chi e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}|(-h^{2}\Delta_{x}+V-E)u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{A(0,R_{2},R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u(x)|^{2})dx

(2.58)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{A(0,r/2,R_{1})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u(x)|^{2})dx

with
\tilde{C}>0
. On
A(0,R_{1},R_{0})
,
\tilde{\phi}\leq\tilde{\phi}(R_{1})=:K
. Eventually, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,R_{1},R_{2})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}h^{2/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}e^{2K\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}|(-h^{2}\Delta_{x}+V-E)u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{B_{r}}e^{2K\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u(x)|^{2})dx

(2.59)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{A(0,R_{2},R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u(x)|^{2})dx

Hence the second result.

∎

2.3.
Conclusion

So from the beginning, we work on a closed Riemannian surface
M
and we look at the equation:

(2.60)

(-h^{2}\Delta+V-E)u=f\quad\mbox{on M}

where
V\in L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
,
u\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
and
f\in L^{2}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Let
U\subset M
an open subset.
In the subsection
2.1
, we have seen that we can reduce or study to only two cases on ball
B(0,R_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
: with centre
0
and radius
R_{0}>0
:

•

U
is replace by a ball
B(0,R)
with radius
0<R<R_{0}
.

•

U
is replace by a ring
A(0,\tilde{R_{0}},R_{0})
with
0<\tilde{R}_{0}<R_{0}
.

u,\,V,\,f
are replaced by "periodic" function
u_{per},\,V_{per},\,f_{per}
in the sense of transformation see in the subsection
2.1
:

(-h^{2}\Delta+V_{per}-E)u_{per}=f_{per}\quad\mbox{on }B(0,R_{0}).

For the first case, we use the second statement of the corollary
2.4
:

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,R_{1},R_{2})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}h^{2/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}e^{2K\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}|f_{per}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\quad+C\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{B(0,R)}e^{2K\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

(2.61)

\displaystyle\quad+C\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{A(0,R_{2},R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

for
h
small enough, with
\tilde{\phi}(x)
a smooth positive and radially decreasing function,
C>0
,
K>\tilde{\phi}
on
A(0,R_{1},R_{0})
a ring centre on
0
with radius
R/2<R_{1}<R<R_{2}<R_{0}
and
with

(2.62)

\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)=\frac{1}{h^{4/3}}\sup_{x_{0}\in B(0,R_{0})}\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},h^{2/3}\kappa)}|V(x)-V(x_{0})|^{1/2}

Thanks to the "periodization" and the fact that
\tilde{\phi}(x)
is decreasing:

\displaystyle\int_{A(0,R_{2},R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

(2.63)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\left(\int_{B(0,R_{2})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx\right)

if
1<<R_{2}<R_{0}
.

So we have for
h
small enough:

\displaystyle C\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{A(0,R_{1},R_{2})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{B(0,R)}e^{2K\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

(2.64)

\displaystyle+\int_{A(0,R_{1},R_{2})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

Thus:

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{0})}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx\

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B(0,R)}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{A(0,R_{1},R_{2})}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{A(0,R_{2},R_{0})}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}e^{\tilde{C}\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}\int_{B(0,R)}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

(2.65)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}e^{\tilde{C}\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}|f_{per}(x)|^{2}dx

with
\tilde{C}>0

By periodicity, we can replace

by
\beta(h)=\frac{1}{h^{4/3}}\sup_{x_{0}\in M}\sup_{x\in B(x_{0},h^{2/3}\kappa)}|V(x)-V(x_{0})|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\frac{h^{4/3}}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}}\int_{A(0,\tilde{R}_{0},R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

So:

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R_{0})}|u_{per}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\frac{1}{\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}h^{2/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}e^{2\tilde{\phi}(x)\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}|f_{per}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}e^{\tilde{C}\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}\int_{A(0,\tilde{R_{0}},R_{0})}(|u_{per}(x)|^{2}+|h\nabla_{x}u_{per}(x)|^{2})dx

(2.69)

\displaystyle\quad+\tilde{C}e^{\tilde{C}\,\tilde{\omega}_{V}(h)^{1/2}/h^{4/3}}\int_{B(0,R_{0})}|f_{per}(x)|^{2}dx

with

.

Abstract
. We study algebraic shifting of uniform hypergraphs and finite simplicial complexes in the exterior algebra with respect to matrices which are not necessarily generic. Several questions raised by Kalai (2002) are addressed. For instance, it turns out that the combinatorial shifting of Erdős–Ko–Rado (1961) arises as a special case. Moreover, we identify a sufficient condition for partial shifting to preserve the Betti numbers of a simplicial complex; examples show that this condition is sharp.

1.
Introduction

Algebraic shifting is a widely applicable method for converting a uniform hypergraph
S
into another hypergraph,
\Delta(S)
, which is somehow simpler but still retains key properties of
S
.
The algebraic contribution comes from a generic linear transformation of a certain vector space, suitably crafted from
S
.
This procedure can be applied to an abstract simplicial complex, by treating each layer of
(k{-}1)
-faces as a
k
-uniform hypergraph.
In this way, the (exterior or symmetric) algebraic shift
\Delta(K)
of a simplicial complex
K
is a simplicial complex again, with the same f-vector and the same Betti numbers as
K
.
Algebraic shifting was introduced by Kalai
[
16
,
15
]
and further studied by Björner–Kalai
[
4
]
and many others.
Here we restrict our attention to algebraic shifting in the exterior algebra.
It had been observed that it formally makes sense to replace the aforementioned generic linear transformation (in the exterior algebra) by an arbitrary one.
In fact,
[
15
, Problem 10]
asks to describe how such shifting with respect to a nongeneric transformation (which we call
“partial shifting”
)
differs from shifting with respect to a generic transformation (which we call
“full shifting”
).
The purpose of this article is to explore the situation, for arbitrary ground fields, and give some answers.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt in this direction.

One original motivation for studying algebraic shifting is the relationship to combinatorial shifting introduced by Erdős–Ko–Rado
[
10
]
.
In that landmark article in extremal combinatorics the authors prove an explicit upper bound for the number of pairwise overlapping hyperedges in uniform hypergraphs; see also Frankl
[
12
]
for a survey.
We identify combinatorial shifting as a special case of partial shifting.
In this way, our present work offers a unified approach to (exterior) algebraic shifting and combinatorial shifting.
For instance, this will allow us to identify where these two concepts agree and how they differ.

A second train of thought is concerned with viewing combinatorial and algebraic shifting through the lens of commutative algebra and algebraic geometry.
Herzog–Tarai
[
14
]
and Aramova–Herzog
[
2
]
related (full) algebraic shifting to generic initial ideals.
Combinatorial shifting occurs in work of Knutson
[
20
]
on Schubert calculus.
Recently, Woodroofe
[
27
]
gave a new proof of Erdős–Ko–Rado via a fixed point result on algebraic groups; see also Bulavka–Gandini–Woodroofe
[
6
]
for extensions.

Our main results are the following.
First, we observe a fundamental connection between partial exterior shifting of a uniform hypergraph
S
on
n
points and the Bruhat decomposition of the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
, with respect to its standard
(B,N)
-pair, over a suitably chosen field
{\mathbb{E}}
; for
(B,N)
-pairs see Abramenko–Brown
[
1
, Chapter 6]
.
As a consequence, we obtain a special case of partial shifting parameterized by permutations in the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
, which is the Weyl group of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
.
Recall that
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
always admits a Bruhat decomposition over any field
{\mathbb{E}}
, even if
{\mathbb{E}}
is not perfect; see, e.g., Bump’s monograph on Lie groups
[
7
, §30]
.
Second, this description allows us to recognize the full shift as the partial shift with respect to the longest element in
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
.
In this language, combinatorial shifting with respect to a simple transposition can be realized as partial exterior shifting with respect to the same transposition.
This observation provides one possible answer to a question asked in
[
15
, Problem 40]
.
Third, partial shifting with respect to a permutation allows to vary the genericity of the shifting operation in a well-behaved way.
The extreme cases are given by combinatorial shifting and full shifting.
To analyze the situation further, we study the
partial shift graph
of a uniform hypergraph
S
; the latter is a directed graph which represents all partial shifts of
S
with respect to all permutations.
In particular, we show that the partial shift graph is acyclic; this addresses
[
15
, Problem 7]
.
Fourth, equipped with all of the above we prove that the Betti numbers of a simplicial complex are preserved by partial shifts with respect to permutations which are large enough in the weak order.
The latter result constitutes a vast generalization of known properties of algebraic shifting and a partial answer to Kalai’s question concerning the topological ramifications of nongeneric shifting
[
15
, Problem 10]
.
Our implementation of partial shifting in the computer algebra system
OSCAR

[
8
]
helped us finding interesting examples.
The algorithmic details will be discussed elsewhere.

2.
Algebraic shifting

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, denote by
\binom{[n]}{k}
the set of
k
-element subsets of
[n]\coloneqq\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
We view nonempty subsets of
\binom{[n]}{k}
as
k
-uniform hypergraphs on
n
-elements.
The total order on
[n]
induces a partial order
\leq
on
\binom{[n]}{k}
, called the
domination order
, given by
\{a_{1}<\dotsb<a_{k}\}\leq\{b_{1}<\dotsb<b_{k}\}
if
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i
.
A hypergraph
S\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
is called
shifted
if it is an initial set with respect to this order;
i.e., if
\sigma\in S
and
\rho\leq\sigma
, then also
\rho\in S
.
Explicitly, this means that
\sigma\setminus\{j\}\cup\{i\}\in S
for every
\sigma\in S
and
i\notin\sigma
,
j\in\sigma
with
i\leq j
.

The following construction assigns to every
S\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
a shifted hypergraph
\Delta(S)
that shares several combinatorial properties with
S
.
Let
{\mathbb{E}}\supseteq{\mathbb{F}}
be a field extension and
g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
be an invertible matrix.
The
lexicographic order
on
\binom{[n]}{k}
, given by
S\leq_{\text{lex}}T
if
\min S\bigtriangleup T\in S
,
where
\bigtriangleup
denotes the symmetric difference, is a total order refining
\leq
.
It allows us to identify
\binom{[n]}{k}
with
\bigl[\binom{n}{k}\bigr]
.
Then the
k
th
compound matrix

g^{\wedge k}
of
g
is the
\binom{n}{k}\times\binom{n}{k}
-matrix
with
g^{\wedge k}_{\sigma\tau}=\det(g_{ij})_{i\in\sigma,j\in\tau}
for
\sigma,\tau\in\binom{[n]}{k}
.
We denote the row and column of
g^{\wedge k}
corresponding to
\sigma
and
\tau
by
g^{\wedge k}_{\sigma*}
and
g^{\wedge k}_{*\tau}
, respectively.
For
S\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
, we write
g^{\wedge S}
for the
\lvert S\rvert\times\binom{n}{k}
-submatrix of
g^{\wedge k}
with rows indexed by
S
.

Definition 1
.

The
partial (exterior) shift
of
S
by the invertible matrix
g
is

(1)

\Delta_{g}(S)\coloneqq\big\{\sigma\in\tbinom{[n]}{k}:\allowbreak g^{\wedge S}_{*\sigma}\notin\operatorname{span}_{{\mathbb{E}}}\big((g^{\wedge S}_{*\rho})_{\rho<_{\text{lex}}\sigma}\big)\big\}.

In other words,
\Delta_{g}(S)
is given by those sets
\sigma\in\binom{[n]}{k}
whose corresponding column
g^{\wedge S}_{*\sigma}
of
g^{\wedge S}

does not lie in the linear span of columns
g^{\wedge S}_{*\rho}
left of it.

We call
g

generic
if all entries of
g
are algebraically independent over
{\mathbb{F}}
.
In this case,
\Delta(S)\coloneqq\Delta_{g}(S)
is shifted and does not depend on
g
; see
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
and
Section

3
below.
Algebraic shifting can be considered with respect to any fixed total order on
\binom{[n]}{k}
refining
\leq
; throughout we stick to the lexicographic order
\leq_{\text{lex}}
.

Definition

1
directly lends itself to a naive algorithm for computing full or partial shifts.
Our first example illustrates the method.

Example 2
.

We consider the case
n=4
and
k=2
, where
{\mathbb{F}}=\operatorname{GF}(2)
, and
{\mathbb{E}}={\mathbb{F}}(x_{11},\dotsc,x_{44})
is the field of rational functions in the 16 indeterminates
x_{11}
,
x_{12},\ldots
,
x_{44}
with coefficients in
{\mathbb{F}}
.
The
4{\times 4}
-matrix
g=(x_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 4}

is invertible and generic.
Its second compound matrix
g^{\wedge 2}=(\det g_{\sigma\tau})_{\sigma,\tau\in\binom{[4]}{2}}
is a
6{\times}6
-matrix.
For instance, for
\sigma=\{1,2\}
and
\tau=\{2,3\}
we have

g^{\wedge 2}_{\sigma\tau}\ =\ \det\begin{pmatrix}x_{12}&x_{13}\\
x_{22}&x_{23}\end{pmatrix}\ =\ x_{12}x_{23}+x_{13}x_{22}\kern 5.0pt.

Abbreviating subsets
\{i_{1},\dotsc,i_{k}\}\subseteq[n]
by
i_{1}\dotsm i_{k}
,
the lexicographic ordering on the
2
-subsets
\binom{[4]}{2}
of
\{1,2,3,4\}
reads
12<13<14<23<24<34
, and this dictates our ordering of the rows and columns of
g^{\wedge 2}
.
We let
S=\{\sigma,\tau\}=\{12,23\}
, and obtain

g^{\wedge S}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}x_{11}x_{22}+x_{12}x_{21}&x_{11}x_{23}+x_{13}x_{21}&x_{11}x_{24}+x_{14}x_{21}&x_{12}x_{23}+x_{22}x_{13}&x_{12}x_{24}+x_{14}x_{22}&x_{13}x_{24}+x_{14}x_{23}\\

This

(2)

\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}x_{22}+x_{12}x_{21}&x_{11}x_{23}+x_{13}x_{21}&x_{11}x_{24}+x_{14}x_{21}&x_{12}x_{23}+x_{13}x_{22}&x_{12}x_{24}+x_{14}x_{22}&x_{13}x_{24}+x_{14}x_{23}\\[3.0pt]

+x_{11}x_{21}x_{23}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{21}^{2}x_{33}\\
+x_{13}x_{21}^{2}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{21}x_{23}x_{31}\\
+x_{13}x_{21}x_{22}x_{31}\end{subarray}&\begin{subarray}{c}x_{11}x_{21}x_{22}x_{34}\\
+x_{11}x_{21}x_{24}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{21}^{2}x_{34}\\
+x_{14}x_{21}^{2}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{21}x_{24}x_{31}\\
+x_{14}x_{21}x_{22}x_{31}\end{subarray}&\begin{subarray}{c}x_{11}x_{22}^{2}x_{33}\\
+x_{11}x_{22}x_{23}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{21}x_{22}x_{33}\\
+x_{13}x_{21}x_{22}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{22}x_{23}x_{31}\\
+x_{13}x_{22}^{2}x_{31}\end{subarray}&\begin{subarray}{c}x_{11}x_{22}^{2}x_{34}\\
+x_{11}x_{22}x_{24}x_{32}\\
+x_{12}x_{21}x_{22}x_{34}\\

whose leading independent columns are indexed by
12
and
13
.
Consequently, we obtain the shifted hypergraph
\Delta(S)=\Delta_{g}(S)=\{12,13\}
.

The considerable growth of the algebraic expressions even in such a small example shows that this direct approach is only of very limited use.
It is an open question if there is a general polynomial time algorithm for computing
\Delta(S)
from
S
; see
[
15
, Problem 8]
.
Recently, Keehn and Nevo found such an efficient algorithm for triangulations of surfaces
[
18
]
.

Remark 3

(The role of the fields
{\mathbb{E}}
and
{\mathbb{F}}
)
.

By
[
15
, Theorem 2.1.6]
the full shift only depends on the characteristic of the field
{\mathbb{F}}
.
So it suffices to consider field extensions
{\mathbb{E}}\supseteq{\mathbb{F}}
where
{\mathbb{F}}
is a prime field, and
{\mathbb{E}}
is the field of rational functions over
{\mathbb{F}}
with
n^{2}
indeterminates, as in
Example

2
.
In characteristic zero it is enough to look at, e.g.,
{\mathbb{R}}\supseteq{\mathbb{Q}}
because
{\mathbb{R}}
contains sufficiently many transcendentals.
The latter observation gives rise to a Monte–Carlo algorithm for computing
\Delta(S)
from
S
with high probability in characteristic zero; see
[
15
, §2.6]
.
We usually omit the field from the notation
\Delta(S)
.
Where the field is not clear from the context, we write
\Delta^{\mathbb{F}}(S)
.

The following result is known, at least for shifting with generic matrices
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
.
We provide a short proof to stress that the genericity is not essential.

Lemma 4
.

For every
S\subseteq\binom{[n]}{k}
and
g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
, we have
\lvert\Delta_{g}(S)\rvert=\lvert S\rvert
.

Proof.

For a matrix
m
, let
r(m)
be the sequence with
r_{0}(m)=0
and
r(m)_{k}=\rk[(m_{*j})_{j\leq k}]
for
k>0
.
Denoting the predecessor of a
\sigma\in\binom{[n]}{k}
in the lexicographic order by
\sigma-1
, we obtain

(3)

\Delta_{g}(S)=\big\{\sigma:\allowbreak r(g^{\wedge S})_{\sigma-1}<r(g^{\wedge S})_{\sigma}\big\}.

Of course,
r(m)_{j}-r(m)_{j-1}\leq 1
for any matrix
m
;
i.e., the sequence
r(m)
only has steps of height one.
On the other hand,
g
is invertible, so by Sylvester’s determinant identity (see
Lemma

8
below),
also
g^{\wedge k}
is invertible.
In particular,
g^{\wedge k}
and thus also
g^{\wedge S}
has linearly independent rows,
so
\rk g^{\wedge S}=\lvert S\rvert
.
Now the elements of
\Delta_{g}(S)
correspond to the steps in
r(g^{\wedge S})
,
which are of height one and of which therefore there are precisely
\lvert S\rvert
many.
∎

We conclude this sections with some remarks giving various equivalent views on the definitions of
\Delta(S)
and
\Delta_{g}(S)
.

Remark 5

(Compound matrices and exterior powers; see
[
15
, §2.1]
)
.

The definition of
\Delta_{g}(S)
can also be viewed using the exterior exterior algebra
\bigwedge V
of the vector space
V={\mathbb{E}}^{n}
.
Namely, if
g
is a matrix that represents (with respect to a fixed basis) an endomorphism of some vector space
V
,
then the compound matrix
g^{\wedge k}
represents the endomorphism
g^{\wedge k}

of the exterior power
V^{\wedge k}
with respect to the induced basis of
V^{\wedge k}
.
Specifically, let
e_{i}
denote the
i
th basis vector of
V
,
and for
\sigma=\{\sigma_{1}<\dotsb<\sigma_{k}\}
let
e_{\sigma}\coloneqq e_{\sigma_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge e_{\sigma_{k}}
.
Then

g^{\wedge k}(e_{\sigma})\ =\ \sum_{\rho}\det(g_{\rho\sigma})e_{\rho}\kern 5.0pt.

The determinant is precisely the
(\rho,\sigma)
-entry of the compound matrix
g^{\wedge k}
.
For
\sigma
as above, let
g_{\sigma}=ge_{\sigma_{1}}\wedge\dotsb\wedge ge_{\sigma_{k}}=g^{\wedge k}e_{\sigma}
.
For
S\subset\binom{[n]}{k}
, let
V(S)=V^{\wedge k}/\operatorname{span}(e_{\sigma}:\allowbreak\sigma\notin S)
.
For
v\in V^{\wedge k}
, let
\bar{v}
be the image of
v
in
V(S)
.
Then

\Delta_{g}(S)\ =\ \big\{\sigma\in\tbinom{[n]}{k}:\allowbreak g_{\sigma}\notin\operatorname{span}_{V(S)}(\bar{g}_{\rho}|\rho<_{\text{lex}}\sigma)\big\}\kern 5.0pt.

Remark 6

(Shifting via generic initial ideals; see
[
14
]
)
.

For the generic exterior shift
\Delta(S)
, the above remark can be rephrased as follows.
For
S\subseteq\smash{\binom{[n]}{k}}
, let
K(S)
be the simplicial complex with faces
S\cup\bigcup_{m<k}\smash{\binom{[n]}{m}}
,
and let
I_{S}\coloneqq I_{K(S)}\coloneqq(e_{\sigma}\colon\sigma\notin K)
denote the
Stanley–Reisner ideal
of
K(S)
in
\bigwedge{\mathbb{E}}^{n}
.
Then
I_{\Delta_{g}(S)}
is the initial ideal
I_{\Delta_{g}(S)}=\In(g\cdot I_{S})
with respect to the lexicographic order, where
g\cdot e_{i}=\sum_{j}g_{ij}e_{j}
,
and
I_{\Delta(S)}=\Gin(I_{S})
is the generic initial ideal of
I_{S}
,
given by
\Gin(I_{S})=\In(g\cdot I_{S})
for any
g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
with
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebraically independent entries.

Remark 7

(Shifting via Grassmannians)
.

Another perspective on algebraic shifting is as follows.
For each
n
,
k
and
m
, an invertible matrix
g\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
determines a map
\mathit{rs}_{g}\colon\binom{\binom{[n]}{k}}{m}\to\Gr(m,\binom{n}{k})

that sends a hypergraph
S
to the row span of
g^{\wedge S}
.
The Plücker coordinates
p_{T}
of
\Gr(m,\binom{n}{k})
are indexed by sets
T\in\binom{\binom{[n]}{k}}{m}
.
Algebraic shifting
\Delta_{g}(-)
is the composition of
\mathit{rs}_{g}
with
\Gr\bigl(m,\binom{n}{k}\bigr)\to\binom{\binom{[n]}{k}}{m}
,
V\mapsto\min_{\text{lex}}\big\{T\in\binom{\binom{[n]}{k}}{m}:\allowbreak p_{T}(V)\neq 0\big\}
.

3.
Bruhat decomposition

It is clear that finitely many matrices suffice to describe all partial shifts of one
k
-uniform hypergraph on
[n]
, even if we consider algebraic shifting over an infinite field
{\mathbb{F}}
.
This observation is immediate from the fact that there are finitely many
k
-uniform hypergraphs on the set
[n]
.
The purpose of this section is to explain how to find such matrices.
Obviously, the action of the group
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
on the exterior algebra
\bigwedge{\mathbb{E}}^{n}
must play a role; see
[
15
, §2.9]
and
[
13
, Part III]
.

Of key importance is the subgroup
B\subseteq\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
of upper triangular matrices, which is also called the
Borel subgroup
of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
.
It contains the
unipotent subgroup

U
that comprises those upper triangular matrices which have ones on the diagonal.
We let the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
on
n
elements act on
[n]
from the right and write
i\cdot w
for
i\in[n]
and
w\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
.
This convention aligns with the notation used by
OSCAR

[
8
]
,
but differs from writing permutations acting like functions (i.e., on the left) as, e.g., in
[
13
]
.
Following the convention in
[
3
,
26
]
,
we regard
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
as a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
by assigning to
w\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
the permutation matrix
(\delta_{i\cdot w,j})_{ij}\in\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
.
With matrices being multiplied to row vectors on the right, this ensures
e_{i}w=e_{i\cdot w}
, where
e_{i}
denotes the
i
th standard basis vector of
{\mathbb{E}}^{n}
.
In particular, if
g=(g_{ij})
is an
n{\times}n
-matrix and
v,w\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
, then

(4)

(vgw^{-1})_{ij}\ =\ g_{i\cdot v,j\cdot w}\kern 5.0pt.

With this notation, the
Bruhat decomposition
of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})
is the partition

(5)

Outlook

It seems desirable to derive further results relating the topology of a simplicial complex with its partial shifts.
We believe that partial shifting always (weakly) increases the Betti numbers:

Conjecture 51
.

For any simplicial complex
K\subseteq 2^{[n]}
, field
{\mathbb{F}}
and
w\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
,
we have
\beta^{\mathbb{F}}_{i}(\Delta^{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathfrak{r}}(w)}(K))\geq\beta^{\mathbb{F}}_{i}(K)
for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
where
\beta_{i}(K)
denotes the
i
th Betti number of
K
.

If the above conjecture is true, the following second conjecture would be implied.

Conjecture 52
.

For any
n,k,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the contracted partial shift graph
\overline{\mathrm{PSG}}(n,k,m)
is acyclic.

It makes sense to consider subgraphs of partial shift graphs with respect to subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}(n,{\mathbb{E}})

Particularly interesting are reductive algebraic groups such as the symplectic and orthogonal groups (over perfect fields); see, e.g., Borel
[
5
]
.
They also admit a Bruhat decomposition, where the symmetric group
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
(which is the Coxeter group of type A
n-1
) is replaced by a different Coxeter group.

Question 53
.

What can be said about partial shifting with respect to Coxeter groups of other types?

Note that
2
-uniform hypergraphs are the same as simple graphs.
Moreover, the shifted graphs are precisely the
“threshold graphs”
studied in the monograph
[
21
]
by Mahadev and Peled.
The following question could be considered in the context of

Question 54

Can we describe the partial shift graph of an arbitrary graph?
More specifically: for which graphs

Algebraic shifting can also be studied in the polynomial ring rather than the exterior algebra.
This is known as

Some classical results on algebraic shifting work in both settings.
Sometimes, however, there is a difference.

To what extent do our results on partial exterior shifting also hold for symmetric shifting?

Exact g g -function without strings
Yi-Jun He, Yunfeng Jiang

Abstract
We propose a new approach to compute exact g g -function for integrable quantum field theories with non-diagonal scattering S-matrices. The approach is based on an integrable lattice regularization of the quantum field theory. The exact g g -function is encoded in the overlap of the integrable boundary state and the ground state on the lattice, which can be computed exactly by Bethe ansatz. In the continuum limit, after subtracting the contribution proportional to the volume of the closed channel, we obtain the exact g g -function, given in terms of the counting function which is the solution of a nonlinear integral equation. The resulting g g -function contains two parts, the scalar part, which depends on the boundary parameters and the ratio of Fredholm determinants, which is universal. This approach bypasses the difficulties of dealing with magnetic excitations for non-diagonal scattering theories in the framework of thermodynamic Bethe ansatz. We obtain numerical and analytical results of the exact g g -function for the prototypical sine-Gordon theory with various integrable boundary conditions.



I
Introduction

Boundary entropy, or exact
g
-function plays a fundamental role in systems with defects and boundaries. Originally it was introduced by Affleck and Ludwig in the study of Kondo problem
[
1

to measure the boundary degrees of freedom of critical systems. In string theory, it describes the tension of a D-brane
[

g
-theorem. It was conjectured in
[
1
]
and proved in
[
3
]
. In recent years, the
g
-theorem has been revisited from quantum information point of view
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

When the bulk theory is non-critical, the off-critical
g
-function is also a highly interesting quantity, especially for integrable quantum field theories (IQFTs). For many IQFTs in 1+1 dimensions with various integrable boundaries, the
g
-function can be computed exactly
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. More recently, the exact
g
-function makes its appearance in AdS/CFT correspondence. It was found that a class of OPE coefficients involving giant gravitons and a single-trace non-BPS operator in the planar
\mathcal{N}=4
super-Yang-Mills theory is given by a worldsheet

-function
[
15
,
16
]
. This result should be generalizable to more classes of OPE coefficients in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM and ABJM theories which are given by the overlap of an energy eigenstate and an integrable boundary state
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. Such overlaps are essentially the
g
-functions and their excited state generalizations. The fact that such generalizations have not been achieved so far is largely due to our inadequate understanding of the computation of
g
-functions for
non-diagonal scattering
theories.

The computation of exact
g
-functions for IQFTs turns out to be quite a non-trivial problem. Different approaches including a direct cluster expansion
[
8
,
10
,
9
]
, thermodynamic Bethe ansatz (TBA)
[
24
,
7
,
12
,
25
]
and a more rigorous matrix-tree theorem approach
[
26
,
27
]
have been developed. So far, consensus between different methods have been achieved for theories with diagonal scattering S-matrices. For non-diagonal scattering theories such as the sine-Gordon theory and the planar
\mathcal{N}=4

g
-function is still an outstanding open question. In
[
27
]
, it was found that the TBA approach lead to divergences. The authors propose to regularize the TBA and compute the ratio of UV and IR
g
-functions, which is finite. This is not completely satisfactory as it still renders the individual
g
-function divergent. Therefore, it is crucial to develop alternative approaches to compute the
g
-function. This is the goal of the current work.

We focus on the prototypical example of non-diagonal scattering IQFT which is the sine-Gordon (sG) theory. We exploit an integrable lattice regularization of the sG theory and compute the partition function on a finite cylinder with integrable boundaries at the two ends. In the closed channel, the boundaries are represented by boundary states. From the definition of the
g
-function, it is naturally encoded in the overlap of the ground state and the boundary state. Such an overlap can be computed exactly on the lattice by Bethe ansatz. In the continuum limit, the overlap contains two parts, one is proportional to the circumference of the cylinder
R
, which is naturally identified with the contribution from the boundary energy; the other non-extensive part is precisely the
g
-function that we are after.

III.2
Integrability

Integrability of the lattice model allows us to derive analytic results for the partition function (
10
).

Bulk integrability

Due to bulk integrability, we can diagonalize the transfer matrix
\mathbf{T}_{R}(u)
by Bethe ansatz,
i.e.
we can construct the
K
-magnon Bethe state
|\mathbf{u}_{K}\rangle
such that
\mathbf{T}_{R}(u)|\mathbf{u}_{K}\rangle=\tau_{R}(u|\mathbf{u}_{K})|\mathbf{u}_{K}\rangle
with eigenvalue
\tau_{R}(u|\mathbf{u})
given by
[
35
,
34
]

\displaystyle\tau_{R}(u)=\left(\mathrm{i}\sinh u\sin\gamma\right)^{2N}\frac{Q(-\tfrac{u}{2}-\mathrm{i}\gamma)}{Q(-\tfrac{u}{2})}\frac{Q(\tfrac{u}{2}+\mathrm{i}\gamma)}{Q(\tfrac{u}{2})}\,,

(13)

where we have introduced the trigonometric
Q

\displaystyle Q(u)=\prod_{k=1}^{K}\sinh(u-u_k)\,.

(14)

The Bethe roots
\mathbf{u}_{K}
are solutions of the Bethe ansatz equation (BAE). After introducing the following quantity

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(v)=\left(\frac{\sinh(v+\tfrac{u}{2})\sinh(v-\tfrac{u}{2}-\ri\gamma)}{\sinh(v-\tfrac{u}{2})\sinh(v+\tfrac{u}{2}+\ri\gamma)}\right)^{N}\frac{Q(v+\mathrm{i}\gamma)}{Q(v-\mathrm{i}\gamma)}

the BAE reads

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(u_{k})=-1,\qquad k=1,\ldots,K\,.

(15)

Boundary integrability

The states
|\Phi_{0}^{\pm}\rangle
are integrable boundary states
[
36
]
in the spin chain language. Their overlap with the Bethe state

is non-vanishing only if the Bethe roots
\mathbf{u}_{K}
are paired, namely
\mathbf{u}_{K}=\{u_{1},-u_{1},\ldots,u_{K/2},-u_{K/2}\}
assuming
K
is even

1

1

. For the paired states, the overlap takes the form
[

]

\displaystyle W(u|\mathbf{u})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\langle\Phi_{0}^{+}|U^{\dagger}|\mathbf{u}\rangle\langle\mathbf{u}|\Phi_{0}^{-}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{u}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left[-\sinh(u+2\ri\gamma)\sinh(u)\right]^{2N}

\displaystyle\times\frac{Q(-\tfrac{u}{2}-\mathrm{i}\gamma)}{Q(-\tfrac{u}{2})}\prod_{j=1}^{K}f(u_{j})\times\frac{\det G^{+}}{\det G^{-}}

\displaystyle f(u)=\frac{4\sinh^{2}(u+\mathrm{i}a)\cosh^{2}(u+b)}{\sinh(2u+\ri\gamma)\sinh(2u)}

The free fermion point is particularly simple and can serve as a consistency check. At the free fermion point

V
Discussions

In this work, we developed a new approach to compute the exact
g
-function of IQFT with non-diagonal scattering. Although our main focus is the sG model, the method is applicable to more general models which allows integrable lattice regularization, see
e.g.

[
39
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
.

Our method has considerable conceptual and technical advantages compared to TBA. We work in the closed channel and extract the
g
-function from the overlap of ground state and the boundary state, which follow directly from the definition, without the need to go to the mirror channel and make string hypothesis.
For numerical computations,
we only need to solve one NLIE while the TBA equation typically involve several or even infinitely many
Y
-functions. This is a huge simplification, especially for the
g
-function, since all the
Y
-functions enter the computation of Fredholm determinants.
Using this approach, we obtained the exact
g
-function for the sG theory for the first time.

Based on the current work, there are a number of important future directions to explore. A detailed comparison of our method with TBA approach would be desirable. For bulk free energy, equivalence between NLIE and TBA have been established for a number of models including XXZ spin chain
[
43
]
, 1d Fermi Hubbard model
[
44
]
and the sG theory
[
45
]
. For the
g
-function it is expected to be more subtle since a naive computation using TBA lead to divergences. In this case, establishing the relation between the counting function and the
Y
-functions and then rewriting our results in terms of the
Y
-functions may shed light on a proper modification of the TBA approach for the
g
-function.

One important future goal is the computation of worldsheet
g
-function which is essential for computing numerous types of exact correlation functions in planar
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory
[
16
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and ABJM theory
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
. At the moment, the worldsheet
g
-function was derived in the TBA framework. The worldsheet scattering theory is non-diagonal and hence the resulting
g
-function is expected to suffer from the aforementioned complications. Our result indicates that it is possible to compute the
g
-function using NLIE-like methods, written in terms of finitely many unknown functions.

To achieve this goal, it is necessary to generalize our method to models with higher rank global symmetries. One possible hint is noticing that
the computation of exact

Appendix D
Bulk and boundary energy

In this appendix, we present the derivation for our claim (
26
) in the main text. Strictly speaking, this is not really necessary for the derivation of the
g
-function, but we present it here as a consistency check of the lattice approach.

D.1
Bulk Energy

The bulk energy of the ground state comes from the eigenvalue of the operator
\widehat{\tau}(\Theta;\{\pm\Theta\})\tau(\Theta;\{\pm\Theta\})
corresponding to the AFV state, given by

\displaystyle\tau_{R}(-2\Theta-\mathrm{i}\gamma)=\left[\sinh(2\Theta+\mathrm{i}\gamma)\sinh(\mathrm{i}\gamma)\right]^{2N}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\sinh(u_k-\Theta+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}{\sinh(u_k-\Theta-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}\frac{\sinh(u_k+\Theta-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}{\sinh(u_k+\Theta+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}\right),

Following the same steps as in Sec.
C.2
, we have

\displaystyle\ln\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sinh(u_k-\Theta+\tfrac{\ri\gamma}{2})}{\sinh(u_k-\Theta-\tfrac{\ri\gamma}{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{N\pi}{\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dv}{2\pi}\phi_{1/2}(\Theta-v)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(v+\Theta)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(v-\Theta)\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dx\,dv}{2\pi}\phi_{1/2}^{\prime}(\Theta-v-\mathrm{i}\xi)(\delta-G)(v-x)\ln(1+e^{\ri Z_N(x+\ri\xi)})

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dx\,dv}{2\pi}\phi_{1/2}^{\prime}(\Theta-v+\mathrm{i}\xi)(\delta-G)(v-x)\ln(1+e^{-\ri Z_N(x-\ri\xi)})\,.

(93)

and

\displaystyle\ln\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\sinh(u_k+\Theta-\tfrac{\ri\gamma}{2})}{\sinh(u_k+\Theta+\tfrac{\ri\gamma}{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{N\pi}{\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dv}{2\pi}\phi_{1/2}(\Theta+v)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(v+\Theta)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(v-\Theta)\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dx\,dv}{2\pi}\phi_{1/2}^{\prime}(\Theta+v+\mathrm{i}\xi)(\delta-G)(v-x)\ln(1+e^{\ri Z_N(x+\ri\xi)})

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dx\,dv}{2\pi}\phi_{1/2}^{\prime}(\Theta+v-\mathrm{i}\xi)(\delta-G)(v-x)\ln(1+e^{-\ri Z_N(x-\ri\xi)}).

(94)

The energy of the AFV state is given by

\displaystyle E_{0}\sim-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\Delta}\ln\prod_{k=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\sinh(u_k-\Theta+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}{\sinh(u_k-\Theta-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}\frac{\sinh(u_k+\Theta-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}{\sinh(u_k+\Theta+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}\right).

(95)

Since in (
93
) and (
94
), only the first term on the right hand side can contribute to the order
\mathcal{O}(mR)
in the field theory limit, we will focus on these terms. Taking their sum, we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{N^{2}\pi}{\gamma R}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dv}{2\pi}\left(\phi_{1/2}(\Theta+v)+\phi_{1/2}(\Theta-v)\right)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(v+\Theta)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(v-\Theta)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathrm{i}N^{2}}{R}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dk\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\Theta}\sinh\left(\tfrac{k}{2}(\pi-\gamma)\right)\cos(k\Theta)}{k\sinh\left(\tfrac{\pi k}{2}\right)\cosh\left(\tfrac{\gamma k}{2}\right)}

(96)

The integral as it stands is divergent. This is understandable physically because it contains UV divergences. To extract meaningful results in the field theory limit, one should extract the order
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp\left(-\tfrac{2\pi\Theta}{\gamma}\right)\right)
contribution of the integral, which comes from the contribution of the pole at
k=\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{\gamma}
. In the field theory limit, the contribution of this pole gives the bulk energy

\displaystyle E_{0}\equiv R\mathcal{E}=\frac{m^{2}R}{4}\cot\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\gamma}\right).

(97)

\mathcal{E}
is precisely the energy density for the vacuum state of sine-Gordon theory known in the literature
[
32
]
.

D.2
Boundary Energy

The ground state boundary energy
\varepsilon_{a}
is encoded in the overlap as

\displaystyle g_{a}g_{b}^{*}e^{-R(\varepsilon_{a}+\varepsilon_{b})}=\langle B_{a}|0\rangle\langle 0|B_{b}\rangle.

(98)

For simplicity, we will consider the case
\varepsilon_{a}=\varepsilon_{b}
. From the discussions in appendix
C
, the boundary energy is encoded in the first term on the right hand side of (
85
), whose form we quote here

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dx\ln f(-x+\mathrm{i}\xi)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x+\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x-\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)\right)\,,

(99)

which is divergent in the continuum limit. To extract meaningful results, we focus on the contributions of order
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})\right)
in the integral such that in the continuum limit after combining with the
N
in front of the integral, it gives finite result by the following relationship

\displaystyle\frac{mR}{4}=N\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})

(100)

All terms of orders higher than
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})\right)
should corresponds to UV divergences in the continuum limit.

To select the terms of correct order, one can write the integral in the Fourier space. Using contour integral, for
0<\xi<\tfrac{\gamma}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle I^{\pm}\equiv\mathcal{F}\left\{\text{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x\pm\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)\right\}(k)=\gamma\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}k(\mathrm{i}\xi\mp\Theta)\right)\text{sech}\left(\tfrac{\gamma k}{2}\right).

(101)

Thus

\displaystyle I^{+}+I^{-}=2\gamma e^{k\xi}\text{sech}\left(\tfrac{\gamma k}{2}\right)\cos\left(k\Theta\right).

(102)

To deal with the logarithm in (
99
), we can take the derivative with respect to
u
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{du}\ln f(u+\mathrm{i}\xi)=2\left(\coth(u+\mathrm{i}a+\mathrm{i}\xi)+\tanh(u+b+\ri\xi)-\coth(2u+\mathrm{i}\gamma+2\mathrm{i}\xi)-\coth(2u+2\mathrm{i}\xi)\right),

(103)

where
a
and
b
are real and we consider the case
0<a<\pi
for convenience.

There are four terms on the right hand side of (
103
). Their integration can be computed similarly. We discuss the first term in detail. For
0<a+\xi<\pi
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}ku}\text{coth}(u+\mathrm{i}a+\mathrm{i}\xi)du=-\mathrm{i}\pi\frac{e^{k\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-a-\xi\right)}}{\text{sinh}\left(\tfrac{\pi k}{2}\right)}.

(104)

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\text{coth}(u-x+\mathrm{i}a+\mathrm{i}\xi)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x+\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x-\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{\mathrm{i}ku}(-2i\pi\gamma)\frac{e^{k(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-a)}\cos(k\Theta)}{\sinh(\tfrac{\pi k}{2})\cosh(\tfrac{\gamma k}{2})}dk.

(105)

After integrating the rhs of (
105
) over
u
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{-\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{\mathrm{i}ku}(-2\mathrm{i}\pi\gamma)\frac{e^{k(\tfrac{\pi}{2}-a)}\cos(k\Theta)}{k\sinh(\tfrac{\pi k}{2})\cosh(\tfrac{\gamma k}{2})}dk.

(106)

The
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})\right)
contribution is given by the residues at the poles
\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{\gamma}
and
-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{\gamma}
. The contribution of the pole
\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{\gamma}
is (taking
u=0
)

\displaystyle 4\gamma e^{\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2\gamma}(\pi-2a)}\text{csc}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\gamma}\right)\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma}\right)

(107)

and the contribution of the pole
-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{\gamma}
is (taking
u=0
)

\displaystyle 4\gamma e^{-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2\gamma}(\pi-2a)}\text{csc}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\gamma}\right)\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma}\right).

(108)

The summation gives

\displaystyle 8\gamma\cos\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2\gamma}(\pi-2a)\right)\text{csc}\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{2\gamma}\right)\cosh\left(\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma}\right).

(109)

Multiplying by
\tfrac{N}{2\gamma}\cdot 2
and taking the continuum limit, we have

\displaystyle mR\frac{\cos\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}(\pi-2a)\right)}{\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)},

(110)

where we used
\nu=\frac{\pi}{\gamma}
.

For
0<\xi<\frac{\pi}{2}
, the order
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})\right)
contribution of the second term

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}2\ln\text{cosh}(-x+b+\mathrm{i}\xi)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x+\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x-\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)\right)dx

(111)

is given by

\displaystyle mR\frac{\cosh(\nu b)}{\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)}.

(112)

For
0<\tfrac{\gamma}{2}+\xi<\tfrac{\pi}{2}
, the order
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})\right)
contribution of the third term

\displaystyle-\frac{N}{2\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\ln\text{sinh}(-2x+\mathrm{i}\gamma+2\mathrm{i}\xi)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x+\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x-\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)\right)dx

(113)

is given by

\displaystyle-\frac{mR}{2}.

(114)

For
0<\xi<\tfrac{\pi}{2}
, the order
\mathcal{O}\left(\exp(-\frac{\pi\Theta}{\gamma})\right)
contribution of the fourth term

\displaystyle-\frac{N}{2\gamma}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\ln\text{sinh}(-2x+2\mathrm{i}\xi)\left(\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x+\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)+\operatorname{sech}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{\gamma}(x-\Theta-\mathrm{i}\xi)\right)\right)dx

is given by

\displaystyle-\tfrac{mR}{2}\text{cot}\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{4}\right).

(116)

\displaystyle\tfrac{mR}{2}\left(-\text{cot}\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{4}\right)-1+2\tfrac{\cos\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}(\pi-2a)\right)}{\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)}+2\tfrac{\cosh(\nu b)}{\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)}\right).

Comparing with (

\displaystyle 2\varepsilon_{a}=-\tfrac{m}{2}\left(-\text{cot}\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{4}\right)-1+2\tfrac{\cos\left(\tfrac{\nu}{2}(\pi-2a)\right)}{\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)}+2\tfrac{\cosh(\nu b)}{\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi\nu}{2}\right)}\right).

(117)

Let us comment on a few subtle points on the computation of boundary energy. First, the result matches that given in the literature
[

for

Appendix E
Free Fermion Point

The sine-Gordon theory at bulk parameter
\gamma=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
is equivalent to the free massive fermion by bosonization
[
59
]
. The free fermion point is particularly simple and can serve as a consistency check. In this appendix, we compute the
g
-function at the free fermion point with the fixed boundary condition by TBA and show that the result is equivalent to the lattice regularization approach.

E.1
Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz Approach to
g
-function

At the free fermion point, we have solitons and anti-solitons which scatter freely in the bulk. The boundary scattering process of solitons and anti-solitons can be described by the following Faddeev-Zamolodchikov (FZ) algebra
[
59
]

\displaystyle A_{+}^{\dagger}(\theta)B=P^{+}(\theta)A_{+}^{\dagger}(-\theta)B+Q^{+}(\theta)A_{-}^{\dagger}(-\theta)B\,,

(118)

\displaystyle A_{-}^{\dagger}(\theta)B=Q^{-}(\theta)A_{+}^{\dagger}(-\theta)B+P^{-}(\theta)A_{-}^{\dagger}(-\theta)B\,,

(119)

where
A^{\dagger}_{+}
,
A^{\dagger}_{-}
and
B
are FZ operators for soliton, anti-soliton and the boundary respectively. The free fermion theory with fixed boundary condition corresponds to the case
Q^{+}(\theta)=Q^{-}(\theta)=0
and

\displaystyle P^{\pm}(\theta)=-\left.\cosh\left(\frac{\theta\pm\mathrm{i}\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{0}-\mathrm{i}\pi/2}{2}\right)\right/\cosh\left(\frac{\theta\mp\mathrm{i}\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{0}+\mathrm{i}\pi/2}{2}\right).

(120)

Note that we take boundary parameters of the two boundaries to be the same for convenience. The boundary parameter here is related to lattice parameter by
\sqrt{4\pi}\varphi_{0}+2\pi n=-2a
when
\lambda=1

[
59
,
33
]
. Using the relation between
\varphi_{0}
and
a
, we have

\displaystyle P^{\pm}(\theta)=-\left.\cosh\left(\frac{\theta-\mathrm{i}\pi/2}{2}\mp\mathrm{i}a\right)\right/\cosh\left(\frac{\theta+\mathrm{i}\pi/2}{2}\pm ia\right).

(121)

Assuming that there are
N^{+}
fermions and
N^{-}
anti-fermions, the Bethe-Yang equations are

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{i}2LM\sinh\theta_{j}}(P^{+}(\theta_{j}))^{2}=1,\qquad j=1,\dots,N^{+}

(122)

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{i}2LM\sinh\theta_{j}}(P^{-}(\theta_{j}))^{2}=1,\qquad j=1,\dots,N^{-}.

(123)

In the thermodynamic limit, they become

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{tot}}^{+}=\frac{m}{\pi}\cosh\theta+\frac{1}{2\pi iL}\partial_{\theta}\ln(P^{+}(\theta))^{2}-\frac{\delta(\theta)}{2L},

(124)

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{tot}}^{-}=\frac{m}{\pi}\cosh\theta+\frac{1}{2\pi iL}\partial_{\theta}\ln(P^{-}(\theta))^{2}-\frac{\delta(\theta)}{2L}.

(125)

Defining
Y
-functions as

\displaystyle Y_{\pm}=\frac{\rho^{\pm}}{\rho_{\text{tot}}^{\pm}-\rho^{\pm}}\,,

(126)

the TBA equations read

\displaystyle\ln Y_{\pm}=-Rm\cosh(\theta).

(127)

The
g
-function is given by the
Y
-functions as

\displaystyle\ln|g|^{2}_{\text{(TBA)}}=2\sum_{A=\pm}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\Theta_{A}(\theta)\ln(1+Y_A(\theta))

(128)

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{A}(\theta)\equiv\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{\theta}\ln P^{A}(\theta)-\pi\delta(\theta).

(129)

There is no Fredholm determinant factor in
g
-function because the S-matrix of free fermion is trivial. Using the explicit expression of the reflection factor, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{\theta}\ln P^{\pm}(\theta)=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\left(\tanh(\pm\ri a\mp\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\tfrac{\ri\pi}{4})+\tanh(\pm\ri a\pm\tfrac{\theta}{2}+\tfrac{\ri\pi}{4})\right).

(130)

E.2
NLIE Approach to
g
-function

Now we compute the
g
-function using the lattice regularization approach. We take
\gamma=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
in the NLIE and the expressions (
90
) for the
g
-function . In this case,
G(u)=0
and the NLIE simplifies to

\displaystyle Z(u)=mR\sinh\left(\frac{\pi u}{\gamma}\right)=mR\sinh(2 u).

(131)

As a result,

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}Z(u+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})=-mR\cosh(2u),

(132)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}Z(u-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})=-mR\cosh(2u).

(133)

Taking the limit
\xi\to\tfrac{\pi}{4}
and assuming that
\tfrac{\pi}{4}<a<\tfrac{3\pi}{4}
, the
g
-function becomes

\displaystyle\ln|g|^{2}_{\text{(NLIE)}}=-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv(\ln f_{D})^{\prime}(-v-\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{4})\ln(1+e^{-mR\cosh(2 v )})\right\}-\ln 2

(134)

Note that since we are considering the diagonal boundary scattering, we should take the following diagonal limit for the function
f(u)

\displaystyle f_{D}(u)\equiv\lim_{b\xrightarrow{}\infty}e^{-2b}f(u)=\frac{\sinh^{2}(u+\mathrm{i}a)e^{2u}}{\sinh(2u+\ri\gamma)\sinh(2u)}.

(135)

It follows that

\displaystyle(\ln f_{D})^{\prime}(-v-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\pi}{4})=2\tanh(\ri a-v+\tfrac{\ri\pi}{4})+2\tanh(2v)+2\coth(2v-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)+2,

(136)

where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
is introduced to avoid the singularity on the integration contour. Inside the integral (
134
), equation (
136
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\ln f_{D})^{\prime}(-v-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\pi}{4})=2\tanh(\ri a-v+\tfrac{\ri\pi}{4})+2\tanh(2v)+2\mathcal{P}\coth(2v)+2\pi\mathrm{i}\delta(2v)+2,

(137)

where
\mathcal{P}
means the principal value. It’s easy to check that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv(\ln f_{D})^{\prime}(-v-\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{4})\ln(1+e^{-mR\cosh(2 v )})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{A=\pm}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\Theta_{A}(\theta)\ln(1+Y_A(\theta)).

(138)

Then we have

\displaystyle\ln|g|^{2}_{\text{(NLIE)}}=\ln|g|^{2}_{\text{(TBA)}}-\ln 2.

(139)

Note that when the boundary parameter
a
goes beyond the regime
\tfrac{\pi}{4}<a<\tfrac{3\pi}{4}
, there will be extra terms in the expression (
134
). This may corresponds to the fact that if we go to the regime
\zeta>\pi/2
or
\zeta<-\pi/2
in the Dirichlet limit, certain boundary bound states will show up in the physical strip
[
28
,
30
]
. This deserves a further more detailed analysis, which we leave for future work.

Appendix F
IR and UV limits of the
g
-function

In this appendix we compute the IR and UV limits of the
g
-function for the sine-Gordon theory.

F.1
IR Limit

We first consider the IR limit
mR\gg 1
. Following the procedure in
[
32
]
, we define the following quantities,

\displaystyle\epsilon(\theta)\equiv-\mathrm{i}Z(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\theta+\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\gamma}{2}),

(140)

\displaystyle\bar{\epsilon}(\theta)\equiv\mathrm{i}Z(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\theta-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\gamma}{2}),

(141)

\displaystyle G_{0}(\theta)\equiv\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}G(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\theta)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dk}{4\pi}e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta}\frac{\sinh\left((\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{2\gamma}-\pi)k\right)}{\sinh\left((\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{2\gamma}-\tfrac{\pi}{2})k\right)\cosh\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}k\right)}

(142)

In terms of these quantities, NLIE becomes

\displaystyle\epsilon(\theta)=mR\cosh(\theta)

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\,G_{0}(\theta-v)\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon(v)})

\displaystyle+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\,G_{0}(\theta-v+i\pi-i0^{+})\ln(1+e^{-\bar{\epsilon}(v)}).

(143)

In the IR limit, the function
\epsilon(\theta)
can be expanded in the following way

\displaystyle\epsilon(\theta)=f_{0}(\theta)+f_{1}(\theta)+f_{2}(\theta)+\dots

(144)

where each term has one more
e^{-mR}
factor comparing with its last one. The first term in this expansion can be obtained by ignoring the integral terms in the functional equation (
143
) and the expression for other terms can be obtained by putting the expansion into the functional equation (
143
),
i.e.
,
\epsilon(\theta)\sim mR\cosh(\theta)
in the IR limit.

To treat the determinant part in the IR limit, note that one may choose the contour
\mathcal{C}
in (
87
) and (
88
) to be the union of
\mathbb{R}+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
and
\mathbb{R}-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
, where the small
\varepsilon
is introduced to avoid the possible singularities in the integrand. It is useful to expand the following factors in the IR limit,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}(u+\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}{1+\mathfrak{a}(u+\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}=e^{\mathrm{i}Z((u+\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2}))}-e^{2\mathrm{i}Z((u+\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2}))}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-3mR}),

(145)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}{1+\mathfrak{a}(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}=1-e^{-\mathrm{i}Z((u-\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2}))}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2mR}),

(146)

\displaystyle\ln(1+e^{\ri Z(u+\frac{\ri\gamma}{2})})\sim e^{\mathrm{i}Z(u+\frac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2})}+\mathcal{O}(e^{-2mR}).

(147)

From the equation (
87
) we know that the leading contribution of the logarithm of the
g
-function should be of order
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Only the integral along
\mathbb{R}-\tfrac{\mathrm{i}\gamma}{2}
will contribute at this order. A general term that contributes in the expansion includes the expression of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dz_{1}\dots dz_{n}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{n}}\varphi(z_{1}+z_{2}-\mathrm{i}\gamma)\varphi(z_{2}-z_{3})\dots\varphi(z_{n-1}-z_{n})\varphi(z_{n}-z_{1})\\
=&\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dz_{1}\dots dz_{n}}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{n}}\varphi(2z_{1}-\mathrm{i}\gamma)\varphi(z_{2})\varphi(z_{3})\dots\varphi(z_{n}),\end{split}

(148)

where
\varphi(u)
is given in (
89
). Thus to evaluate the quantity (
87
) at the leading order in the IR limit, one should evaluate the following integrals:

\displaystyle\textbf{I}\equiv\int_{-\infty+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}^{\infty+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\left(\frac{-\sinh(2\ri\gamma)}{\sinh(2z)\sinh(2z-2i\gamma)}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2}+2\mathrm{i}\gamma\right)=\frac{\gamma}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}

(149)

\displaystyle\textbf{II}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dz}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\left(\frac{-\sinh(2\ri\gamma)}{\sinh(z+\ri\gamma)\sinh(z-\ri\gamma)}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}(-2\mathrm{i}\pi+4\mathrm{i}\gamma)=\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}-1.

(150)

As we mentioned before, the small
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon
in the (
149
) means that the integral should be done slightly above the real axis to avoid possible singularity.

Putting the results into the equation (
87
), we find that

\displaystyle\ln\frac{\operatorname{det}(1-\hat{H}^{+})}{\operatorname{det}(1-\hat{H}^{-})}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\textbf{I}\cdot\textbf{II}^{n-1}=\frac{\textbf{I}}{\textbf{II}}\ln(1-\textbf{II})=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2-\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\right).

(151)

Assuming that
0<\gamma<\pi/2
,
a
and
b
are real with
0<a<\pi-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}
, the leading order contribution should be

\displaystyle\ln|g|^{2}\sim\frac{\textbf{I}}{\textbf{II}}\ln(1-\textbf{II})=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2-\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\right)-\ln(1+e^{iZ(0)})=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\right),

(152)

where we used
Z(0)=0
. This comes from the definition of
Z
function at the lattice level. For the free boundary condition, where
a=b=0
, the leading order contribution in the IR limit should be

\displaystyle\ln|g|^{2}\sim\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2-\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\right)+\ln(1+e^{iZ(0)})=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left[8\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\right)\right].

(153)

F.2
UV Limit

Now we consider the UV limit
mR\rightarrow 0
. We will argue that the logarithm of
g
-function should be zero in this limit. First, the logarithm of the Fredholm determinant part should be zero in the UV limit. Take the integral contour in (
87
) to be
\{\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\xi\}\cup\{\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\xi\}
, with
0<\xi<\tfrac{\gamma}{2}
, and take
\xi
to be sufficiently small such that no singularity coming from the function
\varphi(z)
is included. Numerical evidence shows that in the UV limit, the solution of the non-linear integral equation is
\mathfrak{a}(z\pm\mathrm{i}\xi)=1
, where
z
is real. For convenience, introduce the following notations

\displaystyle\textbf{A}\equiv\frac{\mathfrak{a}\left(z-\mathrm{i}\xi\right)}{1+\mathfrak{a}\left(z-\mathrm{i}\xi\right)},\qquad\textbf{B}\equiv\frac{\mathfrak{a}\left(z+\mathrm{i}\xi\right)}{1+\mathfrak{a}\left(z+\mathrm{i}\xi\right)}.

(154)

Then the
n
-th term in the expansion (
87
) becomes

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{II}^{n-1}}{n}\left(\textbf{A}^{n}-C_{n}^{1}\textbf{A}^{n-1}\textbf{B}+C_{n}^{2}\textbf{A}^{n-2}\textbf{B}^{2}-\dots+(-1)^{n}\textbf{B}^{n}\right)=-\frac{\mathbf{I}\cdot\mathbf{II}^{n-1}}{n}(\textbf{A}-\textbf{B})^{n}=0,

(155)

where we have used the fact that
\textbf{A}=\textbf{B}=\tfrac{1}{2}
for
z\in\mathbb{R}
.

Second, the following logarithm of prefactor part

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\operatorname{Im}\left\{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dv\,dx(\ln f)^{\prime}(-x-i\xi)\,(\delta-G)(x-v)\ln(1+e^{iZ(v+i\xi)})\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dvG(-v-i\xi)\ln(1+e^{iZ(v+i\xi)})-\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+e^{iZ(0)})

(156)

becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\ln 2\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\delta-G)(-v)dv\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\ln f)^{\prime}(-z)dz\right]+\frac{1}{2}\ln 2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G(-v)dv-\frac{1}{2}\ln 2

(157)

in the UV limit. Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\ln f)^{\prime}(-z)dz=2\pi\mathrm{i},

(158)

the logarithm of the prefactor part is also vanishing in the UV limit.

Appendix G
Numerical calculations

In this appendix, we give details for the numerical method for solving NLIE and computing the Fredholm determinants.

G.1
Solving the Non-linear Integral Equation

The starting point is the following non-linear integral equation for vacuum:

\displaystyle Z(u)=mR\sinh\left(\frac{\pi u}{\gamma}\right)+2\operatorname{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}vG(u-v-\mathrm{i}\xi)\ln(1+e^{\ri Z(v+\ri\xi)})\,.

(159)

This equation can be analytically continued and rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\epsilon(\theta,\xi)=-\mathrm{i}mR\sinh(\theta+\ri\xi)&-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}vG_{0}(\theta-v)\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon(\theta,\xi)})+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}vG_{1}(\theta-v)\ln(1+e^{-\bar{\epsilon}(\theta,\xi)})\end{split}

(160)

where

\displaystyle\epsilon(\theta,\xi)=-\mathrm{i}Z(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\theta+\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\xi)

(161)

and

\displaystyle G_{0}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\theta}\frac{\sinh((\tfrac{\pi^2}{2\gamma}-\pi)k)}{2\sinh((\frac{\pi^2}{2\gamma}-\frac{\pi}{2})k)\cosh(\tfrac{\pi k}{2})}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\theta}Y(k)\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi},

(162)

\displaystyle G_{1}(\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\theta}\frac{e^{2k\xi}\sinh((\tfrac{\pi^2}{2\gamma}-\pi)k)}{2\sinh((\frac{\pi^2}{2\gamma}-\frac{\pi}{2})k)\cosh(\tfrac{\pi k}{2})}\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}k\theta}e^{2k\xi}Y(k)\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}.

(163)

To solve this equation numerically, we take the following steps.

1.

Make the grid. Give a cutoff
L
to functions in the equation and divide the interval
[-\texttt{L},\texttt{L}]
into
N
part with length
\tfrac{2\texttt{L}}{\texttt{N}}
.

2.

Sample the points in the grid on the variable
\theta
of source function
-\mathrm{i}mR\sinh(\theta+\ri\xi)
.

3.

Calculate the convolution term using the convolution theorem. This can be done in the following way. Define the function
L(\theta,\xi)\equiv\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon(\theta,\xi)})
, then upon discretization, the convolution term can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}G_{0}(u-v)L(v)\mathrm{d}v\sim&\delta\sum_{m=-\mathtt{N}/2}^{\mathtt{N}/2-1}G_{0}(n\delta-m\delta)L(m\delta),\end{split}

(164)

where
\delta=\tfrac{2\texttt{L}}{\texttt{N}}
. Note that we omit the
\xi
dependence for convenience here. Define the series
\{G_{n}^{(0,1)}\equiv G_{0,1}(n\delta)\}
and
\{L_{n}\equiv L(n\delta)\}
, where
n
takes the integer from
-\mathtt{N}/2
to
\mathtt{N}/2-1
. One can do periodic continuation for these two lists,
i.e.
, set
G_{n+N}=G_{n}
and
L_{n+N}=L_{n}
. Thus we can write the convolution term as

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}G_{0}(u-v)L(v)\mathrm{d}v\sim\delta\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}G_{0}(n\delta-m\delta)L(m\delta).

(165)

To continue, one need the discretized
G_{0,1}
function, which can be written as the discrete Fourier transformation of the discretized series of function
Y
. They are related in the following way

\displaystyle G^{(0)}_{n}=

\displaystyle G_{0}(n\delta)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}kn\delta}Y(k)\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}\sim\int_{-\pi/\delta}^{+\pi/\delta}e^{-\mathrm{i}kn\delta}Y(k)\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}kn}Y(\frac{k}{\delta})\frac{\mathrm{d}k}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\texttt{L}}\sum_{m=-\texttt{N}/2}^{\texttt{N}/2-1}e^{-\mathrm{i}2\pi mn/\texttt{N}}Y(\frac{\pi m}{\texttt{L}})\equiv\frac{1}{2\texttt{L}}\sum_{m=-\texttt{N}/2}^{\texttt{N}/2-1}e^{-\mathrm{i}2\pi mn/\texttt{N}}Y_{m}=\frac{1}{2\texttt{L}}\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}e^{-\mathrm{i}2\pi mn/\texttt{N}}Y_{m},

(166)

where we also used the periodic continued
Y
series,
i.e.
,
Y_{m+N}=Y_{m}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle G^{(1)}_{n}=

\displaystyle G_{0}(n\delta)\sim\frac{1}{2\texttt{L}}\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}e^{-\mathrm{i}2\pi mn/\texttt{N}}e^{2\xi\pi m/\texttt{L}}Y_{m}.

(167)

Then the convolution theorem gives that

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{n=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}G_{0}(n\delta-m\delta)L(m\delta)e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi pn/\texttt{N}}=Y_{p}\cdot\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}L_{m}e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi pm/\texttt{N}}\right),

(168)

\displaystyle\delta\sum_{n=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}G_{1}(n\delta-m\delta)L(m\delta)e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi pn/\texttt{N}}=e^{2\xi\pi p/\texttt{L}}Y_{p}\cdot\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\texttt{N}-1}L_{m}e^{\mathrm{i}2\pi pm/\texttt{N}}\right).

(169)

The inverse Fourier transformation of the right hand side of these equations gives the convolution terms.

4.

Sum all terms on the right hand side of the equation (
160
) and set-up the iteration to solve the non-linear integral equation. Putting the source function
-\mathrm{i}m\beta\sinh(\theta+\ri\xi)
to be the initial function for iteration. After sufficient times of iteration, we can obtain the numerical solution of NLIE.

G.2
Computation of Fredholm Determinant

Recall the expression for the logarithm of
g
-function

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dv\,dx}{2\pi}(\ln f)^{\prime}(-x-\mathrm{i}\xi)\,(\delta-G)(x-v)\ln(1+e^{\ri Z(v+\ri\xi)})+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\operatorname{det}(1-\hat{H}^{+})}{\operatorname{det}(1-\hat{H}^{-})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dvG(-v-i\xi)\ln(1+e^{\ri Z(v+\ri\xi)})-\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+e^{\ri Z(0)}),

(170)

where
\hat{H}^{\pm}
act as

\displaystyle\left(\hat{H}^{\pm}(g)\right)(x)=\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathfrak{a}(u)}{\mathfrak{a}(u)+1}\frac{\varphi(x-u)\pm\varphi(x+u)}{2}g(u).

(171)

To use the numerical solution, one should first rescale the integral variable and the imaginary shift
\xi
by
\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}
. The first two terms of (
170
) can be written as

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\operatorname{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dv\,dx}{2\pi}(\ln f)^{\prime}(-\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}v-x-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\xi)\,(\delta-G)(x)\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon(v,\xi)})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}dvG(-\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}v-\mathrm{i}\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}\xi)\ln(1+e^{-\epsilon(v,\xi)}).

(172)

The integral can be done by using the convolution theorem, which is the same as the trick used in solving the NLIE. The third term can be calculated by using the equation (
159
) and take
u
to be zero. To calculate the the Fredholm determinants, we descritize the action of operator
\hat{H}^{\pm}
on functions and represent
1-\hat{H}^{\pm}
as matrices. Before discretization, one should also do the rescaling for the action of
\hat{H}^{\pm}
, then we have:

\displaystyle\left(\hat{H}^{\pm}(g)\right)(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}x)=\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi i}\frac{\mathfrak{a}(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}u)}{\mathfrak{a}(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}u)+1}\frac{\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x-u))\pm\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x+u))}{2}g(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}u).

(173)

Taking the contour
\mathcal{C}
to be
\{\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\xi\}\cup\{\mathbb{R}-\mathrm{i}\xi\}
, then the action becomes that

\displaystyle\left(\hat{H}^{\pm}(g)\right)(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x+\mathrm{i}\xi))=

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{e^{-\epsilon(u,\xi)}}{e^{-\epsilon(u,\xi)}+1}\frac{\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x-u))\pm\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x+u+\mathrm{i}2\xi))}{2}g(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(u+\mathrm{i}\xi))

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{1}{e^{-\bar{\epsilon}(u,\xi)}+1}\frac{\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x-u+2\mathrm{i}\xi))\pm\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x+u))}{2}g(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(u-\mathrm{i}\xi)),

(174)

\displaystyle\left(\hat{H}^{\pm}(g)\right)(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x-\mathrm{i}\xi))=

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{e^{-\epsilon(u,\xi)}}{e^{-\epsilon(u,\xi)}+1}\frac{\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x-u-2\mathrm{i}\xi))\pm\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x+u))}{2}g(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(u+\mathrm{i}\xi))

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{1}{e^{-\bar{\epsilon}(u,\xi)}+1}\frac{\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x-u))\pm\varphi(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(x+u-2\mathrm{i}\xi))}{2}g(\tfrac{\gamma}{\pi}(u-\mathrm{i}\xi)).

(175)

We then discretize these integrals. In doing this, one should be careful that the order of the elements in the matrix should be consistent with the order of points on the integral contour
\mathcal{C}
, where the order of the points on the line
\mathbb{R}+\mathrm{i}\xi
should be the inverse of the usual order.

Capturing exchange-correlation spin-torque effects with a semilocal functional
Marie-Therese Huebsch, Fabien Tran, Martijn Marsman

Abstract
We cure the lack of exchange-correlation (XC) spin torque in semilocal XC functionals by treating XC effects in the framework of spin-current-density-functional theory (SCDFT), and present the implementation of the first kind of this novel family of XC functionals in the Vienna ab-initio simulation package (VASP): An U(1) × \times SU(2) gauge-invariant SCDFT functional featuring a 2 × 2 2\times 2 XC potential. While the framework can be applied to other XC functionals, the presented flavor of the SCDFT functional is based on Becke-Roussel exchange and Colle-Salvetti correlation. In addition to the 2 × 2 2\times 2 spin density and kinetic-energy density, the XC functional depends on the 2 × 2 2\times 2 spin-current density. The implementation requires the computation of the spin-current density within the projector-augmented-wave method and the variation of the XC energy with respect to it. The application to a Cr 3 molecule and bulk MnO reveals (i) XC spin torque of the same order as obtained by methods including exact exchange, (ii) a counterintuitive contribution to the energy even in collinear ferromagnetic systems without spin-orbit coupling due to the semilocality of the magnetization, and (iii) a similar computational cost per electronic step as calculations that depend on, inter alia, the kinetic-energy density, but convergence within fewer electronic steps.



I
Introduction

Magnetic ab-initio calculations using semilocal exchange-correlation (XC) functionals suffer from a fundamental flaw: Their ignorance of
XC
spin torque. This is possibly relevant for systems featuring noncollinear magnetism, disordered magnetic moments, spin dynamics, magnetic frustration, spin waves, skyrmions, and systems with static currents such as in the presence of orbital moments, spin Hall effect, in nuclear magnetic resonance, etc. Curiously, even collinear magnetic systems may observe
XC
spin torque and, hence, already show this deficiency.

Measured by the amount of magnetic ab-initio calculations performed, the issue has been discussed relatively peripherally. To provide a balanced perspective, when Kübler
et al.

[
44
,
43
]
proposed an algorithm to treat noncollinear magnetic systems from first principles, they already pointed out that the transformation to a local spin-quantization axis only holds for the local spin-density approximation (LSDA)
[
37
]
. In other words, within LSDA the XC magnetic B-field
B_{\text{xc},a}=\delta E_{\text{xc}}/\delta m_{a}
aligns with the magnetization
\vec{m}
at each point
\mathbf{r}
in real space and the system is therefore spin-torque-free:
\vec{m}\times\vec{B}_{\text{xc}}=0
for LSDA. This procedure allows determining the direction of the magnetization self-consistently within density-functional theory (DFT)
[
29
]
while evaluating the XC functional for local spin-up
n_{\uparrow}
and spin-down
n_{\downarrow}
densities, i.e., using a mere collinear, spin-polarized XC functional
E_{\text{xc}}[n_{\uparrow},n_{\downarrow}]
for noncollinear calculations.

a traditional noncollinear calculation
are absolutely parallel, yielding zero
XC
spin torque.

The DFT community got used to employing XC functionals beyond the LSDA for magnetic calculations. For instance, XC functionals of the generalized gradient approximation (GGA), meta-GGA including the kinetic-energy density, or more rarely hybrid functionals, that include a fraction of Hartree-Fock exchange
[
54
]
.
In fact, many implementations of noncollinear DFT
allow the use of any collinear XC functional despite the missing theoretical foundation to use functionals beyond the LSDA for noncollinear calculations.
This is particularly problematic if the semilocal nature, i.e., gradient corrections, significantly influences the electronic structure and spin texture.
The lack of
XC
spin torque was criticized quickly
by Kleinman

[
36
]
,
but the first attempts at formulating a noncollinear XC functional by introducing oﬀ-diagonal elements to the XC potential led to disappointing numerical results
[
6
]
.

The theoretical understanding of this failure is given in a series of works around the year 2000, where Capelle
et al.

[
7
,
10
,
8
]
analyze time-dependent spin-density-functional theory (SDFT) and formulate the so-called zero-torque theorem: It states that the XC B-field cannot exert a net global spin torque on the system. Perhaps even more interesting, in the same works, they established a clear
connection between the
XC
spin torque and the spin-current density
. They show that in the static limit with no external B-field and
without
spin-orbit coupling (SOC), the divergence of the tensor-valued XC spin-current density
\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{J}}_{\text{xc}}
—i.e., the difference between the many-body and Kohn-Sham (KS) currents
[
8
]
—generates a local
XC
spin torque
\vec{m}\times\vec{B}_{\text{xc}}
. This renders any spin-torque-free XC functional to be at least an approximation when studying systems governed by spin-dynamic effects, e.g., any itinerant magnet apart from the fully spin-polarized homogeneous electron gas. This shows that the spin-current density, and thus
spin-current-density-functional theory
(SCDFT)
[
78
]
, should play an integral role when extending the state-of-the-art framework of noncollinear ab-initio calculations.

It is worthwhile at this point to mention the most recent works reporting the implementation of functionals depending on the current density, even if they do not necessarily focus on magnetism. Sen and Tellgren proposed a new family of functionals beyond the meta-GGA that depend also on the paramagnetic vorticity
[
63
]
. For a correct treatment of SOC, Trushin and Görling reported a fully self-consistent implementation of a general unifying theory using exact exchange (EXX)
[
75
]
. For the geometry optimization of molecules in strong magnetic fields, Irons, David, and Teale
[
32
]
implemented MGGA and hybrid functionals that also depend on the current density.
Holzer, Franzke, and Pausch
[
30
,
23
]
, as well as Richter
et al.

[
60
]
, applied existing meta-GGA functionals with a kinetic-energy density that is augmented by a term depending on the paramagnetic current density.

Returning to our main focus, curing the lack of
XC
spin torque: A suitable guide towards proper noncollinear XC functionals are methods that map the nonlocal Hartree-Fock-exchange potential onto a local EXX potential, i.e., calculations using the optimized effective potential (OEP) method
[

,

Specifically, the

using (a) the Slater potential

In that light, thus far, approaches to formulate a noncollinear XC functional
[

In an effort to overcome this limitation, we follow in this work an approach that is substantially inspired by Pittalis, Vignale, and Eich

The lack of

2\times 2

Mind that in the present work, we discuss the implementation and results on noncollinear magnetism and leave the discussion of SOC effects in the context of SCDFT for prospective publications.

To the best of our knowledge, we report the first full
projector-augmented-wave (PAW)
implementation of a static noncollinear SCDFT functional, where the SDFT variational problem is extended by the variation with respect to the components of the
2\times 2

Intriguingly, the derivation by Pittalis, Vignale, and Eich

should offer a formal way of generalizing other existing functionals to noncollinear functionals yielding a non-zero local

pave the way to also generalizing other XC functionals to be used within SCDFT.

In fact, shortly after submitting our manuscript, we learned about a parallel work by Desmarais, Erba, Vignale and Pittalis
[

that, after peer review, reports the implementation of a noncollinear version of the strongly constrained and appropriately normed (SCAN) functional
[
70
]
in the Gaussian-basis ab-initio code package CRYSTAL
[
22
]
.

The article is structured as follows: In

Section

II
Implementation

The novel
SCDFT framework including XC spin torque
is implemented into the Vienna ab-initio simulation package (VASP)
[
38
,
39
]
, which employs the
PAW
method
[
4
,
40
]
. The
2\times 2
spinor-formalism required for
XC
spin-torque calculations is presently already available to a large extent in the framework of
traditional
noncollinear calculations
[
28
]
. In addition, we reuse parts of the code programmed to accommodate meta-GGAs
[
69
]
.

In a nutshell, the implementation entails computing the
2\times 2
paramagnetic spin-current density within the PAW method and extending the variational problem to vary the total energy with respect to
the
spin-current density. Naturally, this necessitates the introduction of the XC vector potential.
We applied special care to account for extra terms in the computation of forces and stress, as well as the fact that the paramagnetic spin-current density transforms like a rank 2 tensor with one symmetry operation in 3d Cartesian space and another in spinor space.
Finally, the expressions for the novel SCDFT functionals are shown
and discussed in the context of U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge invariance
.

II.1
Variables

The starting point is the XC energy functional:

\displaystyle E_{\text{xc}}[

\displaystyle n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]=

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{3}r\,e_{\text{xc}}\left(n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{\nabla}n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{\nabla}^{2}n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\tau_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\right),

(1)

which, in the present case, depends on the following
2\times 2
densities, namely the spin density

\displaystyle n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\sum_{i}f_{i}\psi^{*}_{i\sigma}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}},

(2)

where
\psi_{i\sigma}
are the KS orbitals of the electrons and
f_{i}
the corresponding occupations, the gradient
\mathbf{\nabla}n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
and Laplacian

\mathbf{\nabla}^{2}n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
, the kinetic-energy density

\displaystyle\tau_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}f_{i}\mathbf{\nabla}\psi^{*}_{i\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{\nabla}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}},

(3)

and the paramagnetic spin-current density

\displaystyle\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\text{i}}\sum_{i}f_{i}\left[\psi^{*}_{i\sigma}\mathbf{\nabla}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}-\left(\mathbf{\nabla}\psi_{i\sigma}^{*}\right)\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}\right].

(4)

The expressions are given in atomic units and the sums, where

is a shorthand index for the
\mathbf{k}
point and band, run over all states.

II.2
PAW expressions for the
2\times 2
paramagnetic spin-current density

As for any expectation value of a semilocal operator, the paramagnetic spin-current density has the following contributions:

\displaystyle\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbf{j}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\mathbf{j}^{1}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}-\tilde{\mathbf{j}}^{1}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}.

(5)

The
valence contribution of the pseudo expectation values
on the plane-wave grid reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{j}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\text{i}}\sum_{i}f_{i}\left[\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma}^{*}\mathbf{\nabla}\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}-\left(\mathbf{\nabla}\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma}^{*}\right)\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}\right],

(6)

where
\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma}
are the pseudo KS orbitals.
After introducing the
occupation matrix
for atomic site
R
and the compound indices of all local quantum numbers
n
and
n^{\prime}
that go over all augmentation channels,

\displaystyle\rho_{R,nn^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}f_{i}\langle\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}|\tilde{p}_{Rn}\rangle\langle\tilde{p}_{Rn^{\prime}}|\tilde{\psi}_{i\sigma}\rangle,

(7)

the
valence contribution of the all-electron one-center terms
on the radial grid yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{j}^{1}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\text{i}}\sum_{R,nn^{\prime}}\rho_{R,nn^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\left[\phi_{Rn}^{*}\mathbf{\nabla}\phi_{Rn^{\prime}}-\left(\mathbf{\nabla}\phi_{Rn}^{*}\right)\phi_{Rn^{\prime}}\right],

(8)

where
\phi_{Rn}
are the all-electron partial waves that restore the nodal features of the KS orbitals within the PAW method.
Likewise, the
valence contribution of the pseudo one-center terms
on the radial grid is

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{j}}^{1}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\text{i}}\sum_{R,nn^{\prime}}\rho_{R,nn^{\prime}}^{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\left[\tilde{\phi}_{Rn}^{*}\mathbf{\nabla}\tilde{\phi}_{Rn^{\prime}}-\left(\mathbf{\nabla}\tilde{\phi}_{Rn}^{*}\right)\tilde{\phi}_{Rn^{\prime}}\right],

(9)

where the pseudo partial waves
\tilde{\phi}_{Rn}
are identical to
\phi_{Rn}
outside a certain PAW radius on site
R
. Thus, the all-electron and pseudo one-center terms cancel everywhere except inside a region surrounding the atoms.
The core states are nonmagnetic and chosen as real, so that there is no contribution to the paramagnetic spin-current density.
See
Appendix

D
for comments on the symmetrization of the densities.

II.3
Variational problem

With the given dependencies for the XC functional in
Eq.

1
, the ground-state energy can be determined by applying the variational principle to the total energy
[
46
,
47
,
78
]

\displaystyle E[n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]=\min_{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}\left\{F[n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]+\int\text{d}^{3}r\,nv_{\text{ext}}\right\},

(10)

where
v_{\text{ext}}
is the external potential, due to the nuclei and the frozen core states, and

\displaystyle F[n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]=T_{\text{KS}}[n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]+E_{\text{H}}[n]+E_{\text{xc}}[n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\mathbf{j}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}]

(11)

is the universal functional, where
T_{\text{KS}}
is the KS kinetic energy and
E_{\text{H}}
is the Hartree energy. In
Eqs.

10
and
11
,
n=n_{\uparrow\uparrow}+n_{\downarrow\downarrow}
is the total density. Essentially, the minimization of the total energy is achieved by applying the Hamilton operator to the KS orbitals, which entails the application of the XC potential operator
\hat{v}_{\text{xc}}
onto the KS orbitals. This corresponds to a variation of the XC energy with respect to the complex-conjugate of the KS orbitals. Hence, the following terms are computed in practice:

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\text{xc},\sigma}\psi_{i\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta E_{\text{xc}}}{\delta\psi^{*}_{i\sigma}}=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\left[\left(v_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc,loc}}+\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{A}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc}}}{2\text{i}}\right)\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}-\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\left(\frac{\mu_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc}}}{2}\mathbf{\nabla}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}\right)+\frac{\mathbf{A}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc}}}{\text{i}}\cdot\mathbf{\nabla}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}\right],

(12)

where the local component reads

\displaystyle v_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc,loc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial e_{\text{xc}}}{\partial n_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}}-\nabla\cdot\frac{\partial e_{\text{xc}}}{\partial\nabla n_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}}+\nabla^{2}\frac{\partial e_{\text{xc}}}{\partial\nabla^{2}n_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}},

(13)

the potential arising from the dependency on
\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}
is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial e_{\text{xc}}}{\partial\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}},

(14)

and the nonabelian
XC
vector potential is

\displaystyle A_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime},\alpha}^{\text{xc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial e_{\text{xc}}}{\partial j_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma,\alpha}}.

(15)

Here,
\alpha=x
,
y
, or
z
is the Cartesian direction.

Note that the part of the first term and the entire last term which contain
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc}}
in
Eq.

12
are unique to SCDFT
[
78
]
.

v^{\text{xc,loc}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
can be identified to the
XC
potential for pure density functionals, i.e., LSDA, GGA, and the deorbitalized MGGA functionals, e.g., SCAN-L
[
50
]
. The term in
Eq.

12

\mu_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\text{xc}}
is present in usual MGGA functionals, e.g., R2SCAN
[

III
Considerations on U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge invariance

Let us recall that under a U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformation the KS orbitals transform as

\displaystyle\psi_{i\sigma}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle\to\psi^{\prime}_{i\sigma}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\text{e}^{\text{i}\chi(\mathbf{r})}U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r}),

(52)

\displaystyle U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\text{e}^{\text{i}\sum_{a}\lambda^{a}(\mathbf{r})\sigma^{a}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}},

(53)

where the U(1) parameter
\chi(\mathbf{r})
and the SU(2) parameters
\lambda^{a}(\mathbf{r})
are arbitrary real-valued functions in real space. Additionally, the covariant derivative should transform covariantly

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\mathbf{D}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\to\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\text{e}^{\text{i}\chi(\mathbf{r})}U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\left(\mathbf{D}_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\right),

(54)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\nabla}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+i\mathbf{A}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}.

(55)

Finally, the XC energy must be invariant under a U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformation

\displaystyle E_{\text{xc}}[n,m_{a},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{J}_{a}]=E_{\text{xc}}[n^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{a},\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{J}^{\prime}_{a}].

(56)

We now summarize how to check if
E_{\text{xc}}
and individual terms therein are gauge invariant:

•

Insert the U(1)
\times
SU(2) transformation given in
Eq.

52
into the definitions of the densities
Eqs.

2
,
3
and
4
to obtain the general transformation laws for SCDFT.

•

Identify combinations of densities that appear in individual terms in
E_{\text{xc}}
specific to the XC functional.

•

Apply the transformation laws to the individual terms in
E_{\text{xc}}
and confirm their U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge invariance.

•

Deduce the general transformation of the XC potentials for SCDFT using the transformation laws of the density, the invariance of
E_{\text{xc}}
and the transformation of the covariant derivative.

•

Demonstrate the covariance of the full KS operator
\hat{v}_{\text{xc},\sigma}
, c.f.
Eq.

12
.

In
Appendices

A
,
B
and
C
, we apply the above procedure to the XC functional presented in
section

II.4.1
and
section

II.4.2
and show that
\gamma\neq 1
is unsuitable for U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge-invariant calculations. We also see that in the nonmagnetic limit, the contributing terms reduce to
\tau_{\text{W}}
and
\tau-\mathbf{j}^{2}/(2n)-\tau_{\text{W}}
, or rather
(\tau-\mathbf{j}^{2}/(2n)+\tau_{\text{W}})
for
\gamma=1
. For a nonmagnetic calculation, one may only consider the U(1) transformation and find
\tau_{\text{W}}
and
\tau-\mathbf{j}^{2}/(2n)
to be U(1) gauge invariant, but not
\tau
alone. Hence, for systems with finite orbital angular momentum, the inclusion of
\tau-\mathbf{j}^{2}/(2n)
instead of
\tau
alone should be preferred. This may concern e.g. f-electron systems with unquenched orbital angular momentum,
+U
calculations including an on-site Coulomb interaction, computing the chemical shielding
[
20
]
or systems that observe finite effects due to SOC.

V
Conclusion

We have shown the first ever consistent noncollinear ab-initio calculation using a U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge-invariant noncollinear semilocal XC functional, with a variational problem that is extended
by the variation with respect to the components of the 2×2 paramagnetic spin-current density.
The novel XC functional is formulated within SCDFT and cures the lack of
XC
spin torque in semilocal XC functionals. For the Cr
3
molecule we have shown that the
XC
spin torque is quite similar to the one obtained by EXX-KLI
[
73
]
.

Our
XC
spin-torque calculations fundamentally differ from traditional noncollinear calculations
[
43
,
44
]
since the latter are based on collinear XC functionals. These traditional noncollinear calculations introduce an ad hoc zero local
XC
spin-torque approximation and often seem to suffer under poor electronic convergence even in cases where the approximation is small. For instance, in MnO the total energy correction due to the local
XC
spin torque is merely of the order of 1 meV/f.u., while the
XC
spin-torque algorithm needs roughly a third of the number of steps in the electronic minimization compared to the traditional noncollinear algorithm. In magnetic systems with noncollinear magnetic order, the influence on the total energy due to neglecting
XC
spin torque can be much larger and potentially changes the magnetic ground state. For example in Cr
3
, the
change in the
total energy due to spin torque is
40
meV, which is well above the common energy differences between different magnetic orders
[
31
]
.

Collinear XC functionals should be derived from SCDFT by setting the off-diagonal terms of the
2\times 2
quantities to zero instead of the commonly employed spin-scaling relation. That is because collinear systems also disobey exact spin scaling unless they are homogeneous electron gases, i.e., a pure spin-up or spin-down state.
The expressions for our novel implementation are based on the Becke-Roussel exchange and Colle-Salvetti correlation functionals. It depends on the
2\times 2
spin density, kinetic-energy density, and spin-current density. The implementation in VASP entails computing the paramagnetic spin-current density within the PAW method and extending the variational problem to vary the total energy with respect to the density current, which makes it computationally slightly more demanding than MGGA calculations.

While the ad hoc zero local
XC
spin-torque approximation strongly affects the total energy and convergence properties, the on-site magnetic moment and lattice parameters are not sensitive to it for the investigated systems. In other words, traditional noncollinear and collinear algorithms yield practically the same lattice constant, bulk modulus, and magnetic moment. This is good news because established XC functionals are promising candidates to be extended to SCDFT in order to cure the lack of
XC
spin torque, bad electronic convergence, and the discrepancy between the experiment and the total energy landscape of SDFT while still yielding satisfying results for other properties.

For future work, several directions are worth exploring. On the one hand, there is a plethora of magnetic phenomena that may be sensitive to the zero local
XC
spin-torque approximation. On the other hand, more established XC functionals should be extended to the framework of SCDFT to allow for U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge-invariant noncollinear calculations without resorting to the ad hoc local zero
XC
spin-torque approximation.

Appendix C
Transformation laws of XC potentials and covariance of the Kohn-Sham operator

The XC energy must be invariant

\displaystyle E_{\text{xc}}[n,m_{a},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{J}_{a}]=E_{\text{xc}}[n^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{a},\mathbf{j}^{\prime},\mathbf{J}^{\prime}_{a}].

(111)

Further combining the invariance of
E_{\text{xc}}
with the transformation of the densities yields the transformation of the XC potentials defined in
Eqs.

12
,
13
,
14
and
15
. For a more intuitive picture, we decompose the
2\times 2
potentials into charge (scalar) and magnetization (vector) parts using
n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}(n\,\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\sum_{a}m_{a}\,\sigma^{a}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})
and analogously for the kinetic energy density, the currents and all potentials.

Local potential.

Writing
v_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{xc,loc}}=v^{\mathrm{xc,loc}}_{0}\,\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\sum_{a}B_{\mathrm{xc},a}\,\sigma^{a}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
, the scalar XC potential
v^{\mathrm{xc,loc}}_{0}
and the XC magnetic field
\vec{B}_{\mathrm{xc}}
transform as:

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{xc,loc}}_{0}

\displaystyle\to\left.v^{\mathrm{xc,loc}}_{0}\right.^{\prime}=v^{\mathrm{xc,loc}}_{0},

(112)

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{xc},a}

\displaystyle\to\left.B_{\mathrm{xc},a}\right.^{\prime}=\sum_{b}R_{ab}\,B_{\mathrm{xc},b}.

(113)

That is, the scalar potential is gauge invariant and the XC
B
-field rotates with the same
R
matrix as the magnetization.

Kinetic potential.

Defining a scalar auxiliary quantity

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\left.\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial\bar{\tau}}\right|_{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}

(114)

and noting that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bar{\tau}}{\partial\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}}=\frac{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{n},

(115)

we find the XC kinetic potential can be written as

\displaystyle\mu_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{xc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}}=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial\bar{\tau}}\,\frac{\partial\bar{\tau}}{\partial\tau_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}}=\bar{\mu}\,\frac{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{n}.

(116)

Furthermore this reads

\displaystyle\mu_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{xc}}=\frac{\bar{\mu}}{2n}\left(n\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\sum_{a}m_{a}\,\sigma^{a}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\right),

(117)

in terms of charge and magnetization contributions
and transforms as:

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}_{0}=\frac{\bar{\mu}}{2}

\displaystyle\to\left.\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}_{0}\right.^{\prime}=\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}_{0},

(118)

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}_{a}=\frac{\bar{\mu}\,m_{a}}{2n}

\displaystyle\to\left.\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}_{a}\right.^{\prime}=\frac{\bar{\mu}}{2n}\sum_{b}R_{ab}\,m_{b}

(119)

This is derived by applying the transformation laws derived for the densities and considering that both
\bar{\mu}
and
n
are gauge invariant. Hence, the scalar part is invariant and the vector part rotates with
R
.

To see that
\bar{\mu}
is gauge invariant, the XC functionals must be analyzed individually. For LFNCBR89,

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial\bar{\tau}}\bigg|_{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\nabla n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial(\bar{\tau}+\tau_{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{ncl}})}\bigg|_{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\nabla n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}.

(120)

Both the energy density
e_{\mathrm{xc}}
and the argument
\bar{\tau}+\tau_{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{ncl}}
are gauge-invariant scalars, hence their ratio (the derivative) is also gauge invariant.

Note that
\tau_{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{ncl}}
involves
\nabla n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
, which does
not
transform simply under SU(2). However,

\tau_{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{ncl}}
is held fixed when differentiating with respect to
\bar{\tau}
, and the combination
\bar{\tau}+\tau_{\mathrm{W}}^{\mathrm{ncl}}
is gauge invariant regardless of how its individual summands transform.

For NCCS,
\bar{\tau}
also appears only in a gauge invariant combination which allows rewriting the partial derivative defining
\bar{\mu}
as

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial\bar{\tau}}\bigg|_{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\nabla n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}=-\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial(\frac{L}{8}-\bar{\tau})}\bigg|_{n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}},\nabla^{2}n_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}

(121)

and again shows
\bar{\mu}
to be gauge invariant.

Vector potential.

Similarly, we can use the auxiliary potential
\bar{\mu}
to write

\displaystyle A_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime},m}^{\mathrm{xc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial j_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma,m}}=\frac{\partial e_{\mathrm{xc}}}{\partial\bar{\tau}}\,\frac{\partial\bar{\tau}}{\partial j_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma,m}}=-\bar{\mu}\,\frac{j_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime},m}}{n}.

(122)

Decomposing the current as
j_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime},m}=\frac{1}{2}(j_{m}\,\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}+\sum_{a}J_{a,m}\,\sigma^{a}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})
, the XC vector potential
A_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime},m}^{\mathrm{xc}}
separates into a charge (abelian) part and a spin (nonabelian) part:

\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{charge}}_{m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bar{\mu}\,j_{m}}{2n},

\displaystyle A_{a,m}^{\mathrm{spin}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\bar{\mu}\,J_{a,m}}{2n}.

(123)

Using the transformation laws of
\mathbf{j}
and
\mathbf{J}_{a}
from Eqs. (
57
), these transform as:

\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{charge}}_{m}

\displaystyle\to\left.A^{\mathrm{charge}}_{m}\right.^{\prime}=A^{\mathrm{charge}}_{m}-\frac{\bar{\mu}}{2}\nabla_{m}\chi+\frac{\text{i}\bar{\mu}}{4n}\sum_{a}m_{a}\,\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\sigma_{a}U^{\dagger}\nabla_{m}U\right\},

\displaystyle A_{a,m}^{\mathrm{spin}}

\displaystyle\to\left.A_{a,m}^{\mathrm{spin}}\right.^{\prime}=\sum_{b}R_{ab}\left[A_{b,m}^{\mathrm{spin}}-\frac{\bar{\mu}\,(\nabla_{m}\chi)\,m_{b}}{2n}+\frac{\text{i}\bar{\mu}}{4}\,\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\sigma_{b}U^{\dagger}\nabla_{m}U\right\}\right].

(124)

The first equation shows the abelian U(1) gauge-connection behavior (shift by
-\bar{\mu}\nabla_{m}\chi/2
) plus a SU(2) mixing term. The second equation shows that the spin vector potential rotates with
R
plus inhomogeneous (connection) terms from both U(1) and SU(2).

Covariance of the full KS operator.

The final task is to verify explicitly that the KS operator
Eq.

12
transforms covariantly under the combined U(1)
\times
SU(2) gauge transformation:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta E}{\delta\psi^{\prime*}_{i\sigma}}=e^{\text{i}\chi}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\psi^{*}_{i\sigma^{\prime\prime}}}.

(125)

The gradient of the transformed spinor is:

\displaystyle\nabla_{m}\psi^{\prime}_{i\sigma}=e^{\text{i}\chi}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\left[U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\nabla_{m}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}+(\nabla_{m}U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}})\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}+\text{i}(\nabla_{m}\chi)U_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi_{i\sigma^{\prime}}\right].

(126)

For the local potential, we use
v_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to(Uv^{\mathrm{loc}}U^{\dagger})_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}v^{\prime\mathrm{loc}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}\psi^{\prime}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}=e^{\text{i}\chi}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\alpha\beta\gamma}U_{\sigma\alpha}\,v^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\alpha\beta}\,U^{\dagger}_{\beta\sigma^{\prime}}\,U_{\sigma^{\prime}\gamma}\,\psi_{i\gamma}=e^{\text{i}\chi}\sum_{\alpha}U_{\sigma\alpha}\sum_{\beta}v^{\mathrm{loc}}_{\alpha\beta}\psi_{i\beta},

(127)

with
\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}U^{\dagger}_{\beta\sigma^{\prime}}U_{\sigma^{\prime}\gamma}=\delta_{\beta\gamma}
. This gives the covariant for the usual GGA potential.

Secondly, we find that the transformed kinetic potential
\mu^{\prime\mathrm{xc}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=(U\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}U^{\dagger})_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
involves:

\displaystyle-\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}}\nabla_{m}\left(\frac{\mu^{\prime\mathrm{xc}}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}}{2}\nabla_{m}\psi^{\prime}_{i\sigma^{\prime}}\right).

(128)

Inserting (
126
) and the covariant transformation of
\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}
, and collecting terms, one finds the covariant piece
e^{\text{i}\chi}U_{\sigma\alpha}[-\nabla_{m}(\mu^{\mathrm{xc}}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{m}\psi_{i\beta}/2)]
plus cross-terms involving
\nabla_{m}\chi
and
\nabla_{m}U
.

Finally, these cross-terms exactly cancel
the terms arising due to the inhomogeneous part of
A^{\prime\mathrm{xc}}
and leave the
covariant transformation of the terms involving
\mathbf{A}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{xc}}
of
Eq.

12
as the remainder. Hence, in summary
Eq.

125
is fulfilled.

Appendix D
Symmetrization of densities

For each crystal-symmetry operator
\tilde{R}\in\tilde{S}
at a given
\mathbf{k}
in the irreducible Brillouin zone, a scalar is invariant under rotations
\tilde{R}
in 3d Cartesian space. Thus, the symmetrized charge density is simply the average

\displaystyle\bar{n}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{|\tilde{S}|}\sum_{\tilde{R}\in\tilde{S}}n(\mathbf{k}),

(129)

where
|\tilde{S}|
is the number of crystal-symmetry operations that leave the system invariant.

The magnetization, however, transforms according to all magnetic space-group operations
R\in S
as a pseudo-vector, which implies considering
\det{(R)}
. This reads

\displaystyle\bar{m}^{a}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{R\in S}\sum_{b}R^{ab}m^{b}(\mathbf{k}).

(130)

It is noteworthy, that (magnetic) space-group operations are position-independent, causing all the problematic gradient terms in
Eq.

57
to vanish. The kinetic energy densities
\tau
and
\vec{\tau}_{m}
hence transform identically as
n
and
\vec{m}
.

The charge current transforms like a vector, i.e., it can be symmetrized using

\displaystyle\bar{j}^{i}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{|\tilde{S}|}\sum_{\tilde{R}\in\tilde{S}}\sum_{j}\tilde{R}^{ij}j^{j}(\mathbf{k}),

(131)

where
j=x,y,z
corresponds to the Cartesian directions.
Finally, the magnetic current transforms like a rank 2 tensor yielding

\displaystyle\bar{J}^{ia}(\mathbf{k})=\frac{1}{|\tilde{S}||S|}\sum_{\tilde{R},R}\sum_{j=x,y,z}\sum_{b}\tilde{R}^{ij}J^{jb}(\mathbf{k})(R^{-1})^{ba}.

(132)

Related Hom-rhizaform algebras and Rota-Baxter Operators, Hom-Rhizaform Family Algebras
Imed Basdouri 1 ,Mariem Jendoubi 2 , Ahmed Zahari Abdou Damdji. 3

Abstract
. This paper explores the link between Hom-rhizaform algebras and Rota-Baxter operators. We define a new structure, the Hom-rhizaform family algebra, which is a more general version of the Hom-rhizaform algebra. The main finding is that Rota-Baxter operators can be used to construct new Hom-rhizaform algebras. This work expands the theory of Hom-algebras by showing a new way to apply Rota-Baxter operators. Finally, we establish the classification of these algebras as well as their corresponding cocycle cones.

Hom-anti-associative and Hom-rhizaform algebras

Definition 2.1
.

A Hom-algebra
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
over a field
\mathbb{F}
is called a Hom-Jacobi-Jordan algebra if it satisfies the following two identities

\displaystyle x\ast y-y\ast x=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\ast(y\ast z)+\alpha(y)\ast(z\ast x)+\alpha(z)\ast(x\ast y)=0,

(3)

for all
x,y,z\in\mathscr{A}.

Definition 2.2
.

(\mathscr{A},\circ,\alpha)
is called a
Hom-Jacobi-Jordan-admissible algebra
, where
\mathscr{A}
is a vector space equipped with a bilinear operation
\circ:\ \mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
and a linear map
\alpha:\ \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
, if the bilinear operation
[,]:\ \mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
defined by

[x,y]=x\circ y+y\circ x,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathscr{A},

makes
(\mathscr{A},[,],\alpha)
a Hom-Jacobi-Jordan algebra. This algebra
(\mathscr{A},[,],\alpha)
is called the
Hom sub-adjacent Jacobi-Jordan algebra
of
(\mathscr{A},\circ,\alpha)
and is denoted by

Definition 2.3
.

A Hom-pre-Jacobi-Jordan algebra
(\mathscr{A},\ \cdot,\ \alpha)
is a vector space with a binary operation

(x,\ y)\mapsto xy
satisfying

\displaystyle(xy)\alpha(z)+\alpha(x)(yz)+(yx)\alpha(z)+\alpha(y)(xz)=0

(4)

for all

Definition 2.4
.

An Hom anti-associative algebra
(\mathscr{A},\ \cdot,\ \alpha)
is a nonassociative algebra whose multiplication satisfies the identity

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\cdot(y\cdot z)=-(x\cdot y)\cdot\alpha(z),\quad\forall x,\ y,\ z\in\mathscr{A}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a vector space with two bilinear operations

\succ:\ \mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A},\quad\prec:\ \mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A},

and a linear map
\alpha:\ \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}

\ast

x\ast y=x\succ y+x\prec y,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathscr{A}.

(5)

The triple
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
is called an
Hom-anti-associative admissible algebra
if
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
is an Hom-anti-associative algebra. In this case,
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
is called the
associated anti-associative algebra
of
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
.

Remark 2.1
.

The quadriple
(\mathscr{A},\prec,\succ,\alpha)
is a Hom-anti-associative admissile algebra if and only if the following equation holds :

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)+(x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)+(x\succ y)\prec(\alpha(z)+(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)

(6)

\displaystyle=-\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)-\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)-\alpha(x)\prec(y\succ z)-\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z),\,\forall\,x,y,z\in\mathscr{A}.

Definition 2.6
.

A Hom-rhizaform algebra is a quadruple
(\mathscr{A},\prec,\succ,\alpha)
consisting of a vector space
\mathscr{A}
,two linear operations
\succ:\ \mathscr{A}\otimes\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A},\quad\prec:\ \mathscr{A}\otimes A\to A
and a linear map
\alpha:\ \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\prec=\prec\circ\alpha^{\otimes}\,

\displaystyle\text{and}\,\alpha\circ\succ=\succ\circ\alpha^{\otimes}

(7)

\displaystyle(x\succ y+x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=-\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)

(8)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\prec(y\succ z+y\prec z)

\displaystyle=-(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z),

(9)

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)

\displaystyle=-(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z),

(10)

for all
x,y,z\in\mathscr{A}.

We called
\alpha
( in this order ) the structure maps of
\mathscr{A}
.

Definition 2.7
.

A Hom-dendriform algebra is a quadruple
(\mathscr{A},\prec,\succ,\alpha)
consisting of a vector space
\mathscr{A}
,two linear operations

\succ:\ \mathscr{A}\otimes\mathscr{A}\to A,\quad\prec:\ \mathscr{A}\otimes\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
and a linear map
\alpha:\ \mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha\circ\prec=\prec\circ\alpha^{\times}\,

\displaystyle\text{and}\,\alpha\circ\succ=\succ\circ\alpha^{\times}

(11)

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z),

(12)

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z),

(13)

\displaystyle(x\prec y+x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z).

(14)

for all
x,y,z\in\mathscr{A}.

We called
\alpha
( in this order ) the structure maps of
\mathscr{A}

4.
Hom-Rhizaform algebras and Connes cocycles

In this section, we show that Hom-rhizaform algebras can be obtained from nondegenerate Connes cocycles of Hom-anti
associative algebras.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
be a
Hom-anti-associative algebra
.
A bilinear form
B:\mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathbb{K}
is called a
Connes cocycle
if it satisfies the condition:

\displaystyle B(a\ast b,\alpha(c))+B(b\ast c,\alpha(a))+B(c\ast a,\alpha(b))=0

\displaystyle\alpha\circ B=B\circ\alpha^{\otimes 2},\quad\forall\quad a,b,c\in\mathscr{A}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
be an Hom-anti-associative algebra and let
B
be a nondegenerate Connes cocycle on
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
. Then there exists a compatible Hom-rhizaform algebra structure
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
on
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
defined by:

\displaystyle B(x\succ y,z)

\displaystyle=B(y,z\ast x),

(23)

\displaystyle B(x\prec y,z)

\displaystyle=B(x,y\ast z),

(24)

\forall x,\ y,\ z\in\mathscr{A}.

Proof.

Define a linear map
T:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}^{*}
by

\langle T(x),y\rangle=B(x,y),\quad\forall x,\ y,\ z\in\mathscr{A}.

Let
f,g\in\mathscr{A}^{*}
, and let
x=T^{-1}(f)
,
y=T^{-1}(g)
. Then

f=T(x),\quad g=T(y)\Rightarrow f(z)=B(x,z),\quad g(z)=B(y,z),\quad\forall z\in\mathscr{A}.

We want to show that:

x\ast y=T^{-1}(R^{*}(x)(g)+L^{*}(y)(f)).

We evaluate both sides on an element
z\in\mathscr{A}
. To do this, we use the identity:

B(x\ast y,z)=B(x,y\ast z)+B(y,z\ast x).

Now observe:

\displaystyle R^{*}(x)(g)(z)

\displaystyle=g(z\ast x)=B(y,z\ast x),

\displaystyle L^{*}(y)(f)(z)

\displaystyle=f(y\ast z)=B(x,y\ast z),

so that:

R^{*}(x)(g)(z)+L^{*}(y)(f)(z)=B(y,z\ast x)+B(x,y\ast z)=B(x\ast y,z).

Therefore, for all
z\in A
,

B(x\ast y,z)=(R^{*}(x)(g)+L^{*}(y)(f))(z),

which implies (by the non-degeneracy of
B
) that:

x\ast y=T^{-1}(R^{*}(x)(g)+L^{*}(y)(f)).

Let
f\in A^{*}
, and let
x=T^{-1}(f)
, so that
f(y)=B(x,y)
.

Then:

\alpha^{*}(f)(y)=f(\alpha(y))=B(x,\alpha(y))=B(\alpha(x),y)\quad\text{(by invariance)}.

Therefore:

\alpha^{*}(f)=T(\alpha(x))=T(\alpha(T^{-1}(f)))\Rightarrow T^{-1}(\alpha^{*}(f))=\alpha(T^{-1}(f)).

Thus we obtain:

T^{-1}\circ\alpha^{*}=\alpha\circ T^{-1}.

we conclude that
T^{-1}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
associated to the bimodule
(\mathscr{A}^{*},R^{*},L^{*},\alpha^{*})
.

By Theorem
3.4
, there is a compatible Hom-rhizaform algebra structure
\succ
,
\prec
on
(\mathscr{A},\ast,\alpha)
given by:

\displaystyle x\succ y

\displaystyle=T^{-1}(R^{*}(x)(T(y))),

\displaystyle x\prec y

\displaystyle=T^{-1}(L^{*}(y)(T(x))),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathscr{A},

which gives exactly Equation
24
.
∎

5.
Nilpotent Hom-rhizaform algebras

Definition 5.1
.

A
Hom-Rhizaform algebra

(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
is said to be
right nilpotent
(respectively,
left nilpotent
) if it is
right nilpotent
(respectively,
left nilpotent
) as an algebra :

Let
A
be an
n
-dimensional rhizaform algebra and
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n})
a basis of
A
. An algebra structure on
A
is determined by a set of structure constants
\alpha^{\,k}_{ij}
and
\beta^{\,q}_{lp}
defined by

e_{i}\succ e_{j}=\sum_{s}\gamma^{\,s}_{ij}\,e_{s},\qquad e_{l}\prec e_{q}=\sum_{t}\delta^{\,t}_{lp}\,e_{t},\qquad 1\leq i,j,l,p\leq n.

Let
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{N}
be subsets of
\mathscr{A}
. We define

\mathscr{M}\diamond\mathscr{N}:=\mathscr{M}\succ\mathscr{N}+\mathscr{M}\prec\mathscr{N},

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}\succ\mathscr{N}=\left\{\sum_{i,j=1}\alpha_{ij}\,d_{i}\succ d_{j}\;\middle|\;\alpha_{ij}\in\mathbb{C},\,d_{i}\in\mathscr{M},\,d_{j}\in\mathscr{N}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}\prec\mathscr{N}=\left\{\sum_{k,s=1}\beta_{ks}\,d_{k}\prec d_{s}\;\middle|\;\beta_{ks}\in\mathbb{C},\,d_{k}\in\mathscr{M},\,d_{s}\in\mathscr{N}\right\}.

consider the following series:

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\langle 1\rangle}=\mathscr{A},\quad\mathscr{A}^{\langle k+1\rangle}=\mathscr{A}^{\langle k\rangle}\diamond\mathscr{A},

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\{1\}}=\mathscr{A},\quad\mathscr{A}^{\{k+1\}}=\mathscr{A}\diamond\mathscr{A}^{\{k+1\}},

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{1}=\mathscr{A},\quad\mathscr{A}^{k+1}=\mathscr{A}^{1}\diamond\mathscr{A}^{k}+\mathscr{A}^{2}\diamond\mathscr{A}^{k-1}+\cdots+\mathscr{A}^{k}\diamond\mathscr{A}^{1}.

A
Rhizaform algebra

\mathscr{A}
is said to be
right nilpotent
(respectively,
left nilpotent
), if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
(respectively,
p\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
\mathscr{A}^{\langle k\rangle}=0
(respectively,
\mathscr{A}^{\{p\}}=0
).
A
Rhizaform algebra

\mathscr{A}
is said to be
nilpotent
if there exists
s\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{s}=0
.

Note that for a multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra
\mathscr{A}
we have
\alpha(\mathscr{A}^{n})\subset\mathscr{A}^{n}
for any n.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
g,h\in\mathbb{N}
the following inclusions hold:

\mathscr{A}^{\langle g\rangle}\diamond\mathscr{A}^{\langle h\rangle}\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{\langle g+h\rangle},\quad\mathscr{A}^{g}\diamond\mathscr{A}^{h}\subseteq\mathscr{A}^{g+h}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
be a Hom-Rhizaform algebra. Then for any
g\in\mathbb{N}
we have:

\mathscr{A}^{\langle g\rangle}=\mathscr{A}^{\{g\}}=\mathscr{A}^{g}.

In conclusion, the concepts of left nilpotency, right nilpotency, and over all nilpotency are equivalent in Hom-rhizaform algebras.

Theorem 5.4
.

A Hom-Rhizaform algebra
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
is nilpotent if and only if
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\alpha)
and
(\mathscr{A},\prec,\alpha)
are nilpotent.

Proof.

It is clear that from the nilpotency of
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
follows the nilpotency of
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\alpha)
and
(\mathscr{A},\prec,\alpha)
.

Now let
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\alpha)
and
(\mathscr{A},\prec,\alpha)
be nilpotent algebras, i.e., there exist s,t
\in\mathscr{N}
such that
\mathscr{A}^{s}_{\succ}=0
and
\mathscr{A}^{t}_{\prec}=0
Then, for
p\succ s+t-2
we have
\mathscr{A}^{p}=0
.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

A Hom-rhizaform algebra
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
is 2-nilpotent if

(x\mathbin{\ast_{i_{1}}}y)\mathbin{\ast_{i_{2}}}\alpha(z)=\alpha(x)\mathbin{\ast_{i_{3}}}(y\mathbin{\ast_{i_{4}}}z)=0,\quad,\ \forall\,\ast_{i_{1}},\ast_{i_{2}},\ast_{i_{3}},\ast_{i_{4}}\in\{\succ,\prec\},\ x,y,z\in\mathscr{A}.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
be a Hom-rhizaform algebra satisfying

x\succ y+x\prec y=0,\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathscr{A}.

Then
(\mathscr{A},\succ,\prec,\alpha)
is a 2-nilpotent algebra.

Proof.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathscr{A}
. Then, we obtain:

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)

\displaystyle=-(x\succ y+x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)=0,

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\prec z

\displaystyle=-\alpha(x)\prec(y\succ z+y\prec z)=0,

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\prec(y\succ z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\prec(y\succ z)+\alpha(x)\succ(y\succ z)=0,

\displaystyle(x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=(x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)+(x\prec y)\prec\alpha(z)=0,

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)+(x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y+x\prec y)\succ\alpha(z)=0,

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z)+\alpha(x)\prec(y\succ z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\prec(y\prec z+y\succ z)=0,

\displaystyle(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)+(x\succ y)\succ\alpha(z)=0,

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ(y\prec z)

\displaystyle=(x\succ y)\prec\alpha(z)=0.

This ends the proof.
∎

Definition 6.1
.

Let

(25)

(26)

\displaystyle(x\succ_{\lambda}y)\prec_{\omega}\alpha(z)

(27)

.

\displaystyle=(R_{\lambda}(x)\cdot y)\cdot R_{\omega}(\alpha(z))

\displaystyle=-R_{\lambda}(\alpha(x))\cdot(y\cdot R_{\omega}(z)

\displaystyle=-R_{\lambda}(\alpha(x))\cdot(y\prec_{\omega}z)=\alpha(x)\succ_{\lambda}(y\prec_{\omega}z).

Finally we get

\displaystyle\alpha(x)\succ_{\lambda}(y\succ_{\omega}z)

\displaystyle=\alpha(x)\succ_{\lambda}(R_{\omega}(y)\cdot z)=R_{\lambda}(\alpha(x))\cdot(R_{\omega}(y)\cdot z)

\displaystyle=-(R_{\lambda}(x)\cdot R_{\omega}(y))\cdot\alpha(z)=-R_{\lambda\omega}(R_{\lambda}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot R_{\omega}(y))\cdot\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=-(R_{\lambda}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot R_{\omega}(y))\succ_{\lambda\omega}\alpha(z)

\displaystyle=-(x\succ_{\lambda}y+x\prec_{\omega}y)\succ_{\lambda\omega}\alpha(z).

This ends the proof.
∎

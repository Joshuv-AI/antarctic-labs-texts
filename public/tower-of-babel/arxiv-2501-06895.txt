Discrete-time weak approximation of a Black-Scholes model with drift and volatility Markov switching
Vitaliy Golomoziy, Kamil Kladívko, Yuliya Mishura

Abstract
We consider a continuous-time financial market with an asset whose price is modeled by a linear stochastic differential equation with drift and volatility switching driven by a uniformly ergodic jump Markov process with a countable state space (in fact, this is a Black-Scholes model with Markov switching). We construct a multiplicative scheme of series of discrete-time markets with discrete-time Markov switching. First, we establish that the discrete-time switching Markov chains weakly converge to the limit continuous-time Markov process. Second, having this in hand, we apply conditioning on Markov chains and prove that the discrete-time market models themselves weakly converge to the Black-Scholes model with Markov switching. The convergence is proved under very general assumptions both on the discrete-time net profits and on a generator of a continuous-time Markov switching process.

Introduction

Without a doubt, real financial markets operate in discrete time. However, it is easier to perform analytical calculations on such markets, in particular, to evaluate contingent claims, in continuous time. To eliminate this contradiction, it is logical to consider such models of financial markets with discrete time, which in one sense or another approximate well the models with continuous time, which, in turn, are amenable to analytical calculations. In particular, it is logical to consider stochastic models of discrete-time markets that weakly (i.e. in the sense of weak convergence of probability measures) approximate a suitable continuous-time model. This approach goes back to the weak convergence of the simplest Cox-Ross-Rubinstein model to geometric Brownian motion (the convergence of more general pre-limit schemes is contained in the book
[
5
]
).

It is then natural to ask whether the convergence of prices of options and other contingent claims is invariant with respect to such weak convergence of prices of primary assets. The answer to this question was considered in the works
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
.
Of course, these works do not exhaust the list of papers and books devoted to this issue.

Since the introduction of the Black-Scholes model, it has become evident
that the assumption of constant volatility lacks empirical support
in market option prices. Similarly, in portfolio optimization or
trading strategies, modeling the rate of return as stochastic is
advantageous, as it aligns with observed patterns in time series of
asset returns. A popular, empirically supported, and relatively
straightforward approach to incorporating stochastic volatility
or drift into modeling is through Markov switching. This approach
assumes that the volatility and drift components
of a geometric Brownian motion are functions of a Markov
process. Models of this nature are often referred to as

regime-switching
or
Markov-modulated

volatility and drift models.

An early study that introduced volatility switching into option
valuation is
[
11
]
. Extensive research on option
pricing under Markov-modulated volatility followed. For
developments in the continuous-time setting, see,
for example,
[

–

, listed in chronological order.

As an alternative to continuous-time models, various discrete-time lattice methods
have been proposed. One of the early tree methods for
modeling volatility switching between two states
is the pentanomial tree introduced in
[

]
.
For other tree methods that incorporate more than two
volatility states or address the valuation of exotic options,
see, for instance,
[

These references, while far from exhaustive, are listed in

Lattice methods are relatively easy to understand and implement,
making them practically relevant in many situations. However, these
methods are often constructed through intuitive discretization
of the continuous-time model, which does not guarantee
convergence to the continuous-time counterpart.
To the best of our knowledge, relatively little attention
has been paid to studying financial market models
with discrete time and Markov switching that converge
to their continuous-time limits. Papers that present

However, our model is much more general, as we construct our discrete markets based on generic random variables that represent net profit rates produced by an asset, we consider potentially infinite set of switching states and impose more general conditions on the switching process generator. Weak convergence of discrete-time Markov chains to the limit Markov process

In this section, we prove that the sequence of processes
\left(Y^{(N)}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}

Lemma 1
.

For every
\alpha>0

\mathbb{E}e^{\alpha N_{T}}\leq e^{-\lambda_{*}T}+\Lambda\exp\left(\alpha+e^{\alpha}\lambda^{*}T\right),

where

\lambda^{*}=\sup_{i}\lambda_{i},\ \lambda_{*}=\inf_{i}\lambda_{i}
are defined in (
4
) and
\Lambda={\lambda^{*}\over\lambda_{*}}.

Proof.

Note that given
y_{1}=j_{1},\ldots,y_{m}=j_{m}
, random variable
\tau_{m}
is a sum of
m
independent random variables
\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m}\}
(both defined in (
5
)), such that
\theta_{i}
has an exponential distribution with parameter
\lambda_{j_{i}}
, so that
\tau_{m}
has a hypoexponential distribution. Taking into account an estimate for the exponential densities:

\lambda_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}x}\leq\left(\lambda_{i}\over\lambda_{*}\right)\lambda_{*}e^{-\lambda_{*}x}\leq\Lambda\lambda_{*}e^{-\lambda_{*}x},\ x\geq 0,

we can estimate hypoexponential distribution function of
\tau_{m}
by an Erlang distribution function with parameter
\lambda_{*}
in the following way:

F_{\tau_{m}}(t)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{m}<t\ |\ y_{1}=j_{1},\ldots,y_{m}=j_{m}\right)\leq\Lambda^{m}F_{\lambda_{*},m}(t),

where

F_{\lambda,m}(x)
is a distribution function for Erlang distribution with parameters
\lambda,m
. It is well known that

F_{\lambda,m}(x)={\int_{0}^{\lambda x}t^{m-1}e^{-t}dt\over(m-1)!}\leq{(\lambda x)^{m-1}\over(m-1)!}.

We can now write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(N_{T}=m)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\tau_{m}\leq T<\tau_{m+1})\leq\mathbb{P}(\tau_{m}\leq T)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}}\mathbb{P}(\tau_{m}\leq T\ |\ y_{1}=j_{1},\ldots,y_{m}=j_{m})\mathbb{P}(y_{1}=j_{1},\ldots,y_{m}=j_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j_{1},\ldots,\_m}\Lambda^{m}F_{\lambda_{*},m}(T)\mathbb{P}(y_{1}=j_{1},\ldots,y_{m}=j_{m})

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{m}F_{\lambda_{*},m}(T).

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\alpha N_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda_{*}T}+\sum\limits_{m\geq 1}e^{\alpha m}\Lambda^{m}{\left(\lambda_{*}T\right)^{m-1}\over(m-1)!}=e^{-\lambda_{*}T}+\Lambda e^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}{(e^{\alpha}\lambda^{*}T)^{m}\over m!}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda_{*}T}+\Lambda\exp\left(\alpha+e^{\alpha}\lambda^{*}T\right).

∎

Remark 1

Note that the bound

\displaystyle B_{r}=\{\tau_{r_{j}}\leq t_{j}<\tau_{r_{j}+1},0\leq j\leq n,N_{t}\leq m\},

\displaystyle B^{(N)}_{r}=\{\tau^{(N)}_{r_{j}}\leq t_{j}<\tau^{(N)}_{r_{j}+1},0\leq j\leq n,N_{t}\leq m\}.

S_{1,i,m}(h)=\sum\limits_{j_{1}\neq i}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\neq j_{1}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m}\neq j_{m-1}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}{p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h},

S_{2,i,m}(h)=\sum\limits_{j_{1}\neq i}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\neq j_{1}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m}\neq j_{m-1}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left({p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h}e^{-\lambda_{j_{k}}t_{i}}\right),

where
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\}\subset[0,T]
.
In particular,

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\sum\limits_{j_{1}\neq i}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{2}\neq j_{1}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m}\neq j_{m-1}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left({p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h}e^{-\lambda_{j_{k}}t_{i}}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j_{1}\neq i}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\neq j_{1}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m}\neq j_{m-1}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}\left(\lambda_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{j_{k}}t_{i}}\right).

Proof.

It is a well-known fact that for a Markov process generated by
\mathbb{A}
we have

\lim_{h\to 0}p_{ij}(h)/h=\lambda_{ij},

(33)

see
[
3
]
, Sections II.2 and II.3, Theorem 1 from Section II.3 (p. 130) in particular.
Let us consider:

\displaystyle\sup_{i}{1-p_{ii}(h)\over h}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{i}{p_{ii}(h)-e^{-\lambda_{i}h}\over h}+\sup_{i}{1-e^{-\lambda_{i}h}\over h}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{i}{\mathbb{P}(\tau_{1}<h|Y_{0}=i)\over h}+\sup_{i}\lambda_{i}=\sup_{i}{1-e^{-\lambda_{i}h}\over h}+\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle\leq{1-e^{-\lambda^{*}h}\over h}+\lambda^{*}\leq 2\lambda^{*},

where we used an elementary inequality
1-e^{-x}\leq x,\ x\geq 0
.
Let us prove that
S_{1,i,m}(h)
is a uniformly convergent series. We establish this by induction in
m
.
For
m=1

\sum\limits_{j\neq i}{p_{ij}(h)\over h}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{1-p_{ii}(h)\over h},\ h\in(0,1],\\
\lambda_{i},\ h=0,\end{array}\right.

is a continuous function for
h\in[0,1]
. Since
{p_{ij}(h)\over h}\geq 0
, by Dini’s theorem we have uniform convergence.

Assume now that the statement is true for
m-1
. We have then

\displaystyle S_{1,i,m}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j_{1}\neq i}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\neq j_{1}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m}\neq j_{m-1}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}{p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j_{1}\neq i}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\neq j_{1}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m-1}\neq j_{m-2}}\left({1-p_{j_{m}j_{m}}(h)\over h}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}{p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{*}S_{1,i,m-1}(h).

Since
S_{1,i,m-1}(h)
is uniformly convergent by assumption we derive that tails of
S_{1,i,m}
converge to zero

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{h}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{1}>\in T^{(1)}_{n}}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\in T^{(2)}_{n}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m}\in T^{(m)}_{n}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}{p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{*}\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{h}\sum\limits_{j_{1}\in T^{(1)}_{n}}\sum\limits_{j_{2}\in T^{(2)}_{n}}\ldots\sum\limits_{j_{m-1}\in T^{(m-1)}_{n}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}{p_{j_{k-1}j_{k}}(h)\over h}=0,

where at least one of the set
T^{(i)}_{n}
consists only of elements larger or equal to
n
.
which concludes the proof of uniform convergence of
S_{1,i,m}
.
Using the same reasoning we derive the uniform convergence of
S_{2,i,m}(h)
.
Formula (
32
) follows from the uniform convergence of
S_{2,i,m}(h)
.
∎

Lemma 3

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{P}^{(N)}(N_{T}\leq m),\ \delta\to 0,

\mathbb{P}^{(N)}(N_{T}\leq m)\to 1,\ m\to\infty
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(N)}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in[0,T]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\left|U^{(N)}_{t}-U^{(N)}_{s}\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\mathbb{P}^{(N)}\left(N_{T}>m\right)+\mathbb{P}^{(N)}\left(N_{T}\leq m,\min_{i}\theta^{(N)}_{i}\leq\delta\right)

and

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}^{(N)}\left[\mathbb{P}^{(N)}\left(\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in\left[\tau^{(N)}_{i-1},\tau^{(N)}_{i}\right)\\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{(N)}

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{P}^{(N)}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in[0,T]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\left|U^{(N)}_{t}-U^{(N)}_{s}\right|\geq\varepsilon\middle|\ \mathcal{Y}^{(N)}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{B}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}^{(N)}\left[\mathbb{P}^{(N)}\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in\left[\tau^{(N)}_{i-1},\tau^{(N)}_{i}\right)\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\left|U^{(N)}_{t}-U^{(N)}_{s}\right|\geq{\varepsilon\over 3}\middle|\ \mathcal{Y}^{(N)}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{B}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{2m\Delta\over 6m}={\Delta\over 3}.

Finally, we proved that for any
\Delta>0
there exists
\delta>0
and
N_{1}=N_{1}(\delta)
such that for all
N\geq N_{1}
it holds that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(N)}

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in[0,T]\\
|t-s|<\delta\end{subarray}}\left|U^{(N)}_{t}-U^{(N)}_{s}\right|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\Delta,

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4
.

In the notation introduced in section
5
, we have the following convergence:

\sup_{s<t,j\in\mathbb{N}}\Delta^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)\to 0,N\to\infty,

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\Delta^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)=\left|\mathbb{E}^{(N)}\exp\left(i\alpha\sum\limits_{k=s^{(N)}}^{t^{(N)}}\log\left(1+R^{(N)}_{k,j}\right)\right)-\varphi_{j,s,t}(\alpha)\right|.

Proof.

Recall the notation (
29
) for

\varphi_{j,s,t}(\alpha):=\exp\left(-{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}_{j}(t-s)\over 2}+i\alpha\left(\mu_{j}-{\sigma^{2}_{j}\over 2}\right)(t-s)\right).

From
[
5
]

\varphi_{j,s,t}(\alpha)

\Delta^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)\to 0,\ N\to\infty

Let us show that convergence is uniform in

.
For doing so, we will need an estimate for the rate of convergence in Central Limit Theorem. In particular, we know from
[
3
]

where

\Gamma^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)=\mathbb{E}\left|\sum\limits_{k=s^{(N)}}^{t^{(N)}}\log\left(1+R^{(N)}_{k,j}\right)\right|^{3}.

Applying the proof of Theorem 5.53,
[
5
]
, p. 252, we obtain that
\Gamma^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)
can be estimated as

\Gamma^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)\leq|\gamma_{N}|C\sigma^{2}_{j}(t-s)\leq|\gamma_{N}|C_{1}\to 0,N\to\infty,

where
\{\gamma_{N},N\geq 1\}
is a dominating sequence taken from condition (i), and

is a constant.
Thus, we have shown that for every fixed
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

\sup_{s<t,j\in\mathbb{N}}\Delta^{(N)}_{j}(s,t)\leq|\gamma_{N}|C_{2}\to 0,\ N\to\infty,

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(\mu,\sigma^{2},T,\alpha)
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

In terms of the notation from Section
5
we can state that
for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
m
and
N_{0}

N\geq N_{0}

Let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}

\mu(k+t)=\mu_{k}(1-t)+\mu_{k+1}t,\ t\in[0,1],\ k\in\mathbb{N},

\sigma(k+t)=\sigma_{k}(1-t)+\sigma_{k+1}t,\ t\in[0,1],k\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us also define functional

\left|\mathbb{E}^{(N)}\left[h\left(\left(Y^{(N)}_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[h\left(\left(Y_{t}\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\right)\right]\right|\leq\varepsilon/2,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{(N)}\left[\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}^{(N)}\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m_{k}}\varphi_{k,j}\ \middle|\ \mathcal{Y}^{(N)}\right]\mathbbm{1}_{N_{T}\leq m}\right]-\mathbb{E}[\Xi\mathbbm{1}_{N_{T}\leq m}]\right|

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}^{(N)}(N_{T}>m)+\mathbb{P}(N_{T}>m)\leq\varepsilon.

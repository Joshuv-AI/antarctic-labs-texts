Entropy and multiplicity of hadrons in the high energy limit within dipole cascade models
Krzysztof Kutak, Sándor Lökös

Abstract
We investigate and compare QCD dipole cascade models, the 1D Mueller dipole model, its high energy limit and its generalization that follows from studies of 1D systems with conformal symmetry. To address the ambiguity stemming from different definitions of the rapidity ranges in experimental measurements, we propose the entropy as the function of the logarithm of the average multiplicity, S ⁡ ( ln ⁡ ⟨ n ⟩ ) S(\ln\langle n\rangle) , as a universal observable. From the solutions of the models, we calculate both the entropy and the average charged particle multiplicity and compare to data measured in proton-proton collisions. We obtained these quantities directly from the measured multiplicity distributions and determine the model parameters via fits. We find that the generalized dipole model provides a significantly better description of the data than the 1D Mueller model.

Dipole cascade models

We utilize two dipole models to address description of the data: the 1D Mueller dipole model
Mueller 1995
;
Levin and Lublinsky 2004
;
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
in a slightly extended form that was discussed in Refs.
Hentschinski et al. 2022
;
Hentschinski et al. 2024
, and one of its generalization
Caputa and Kutak 2024

II.1
1D Mueller dipole model

A simple form of a dipole cascade equation that only depends on rapidity, but does not on size (hence it is referred to as 1D Mueller model) can be found in Refs.
Mueller 1995
;
Levin and Lublinsky 2004
;
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
and it reads

\displaystyle\partial_{y}P_{n}(y)=-\alpha nP_{n}(y)+(n-1)\alpha P_{n-1}(y),

(2)

where the parameter
\alpha
represents the gluon emission kernel and characterizes the speed of the Balitsky–Fadin–Kuraev–Lipatov (BFKL) cascade evolution
Balitsky and Lipatov 1978
;
Kuraev et al. 1977
. One may use
\alpha=4\bar{\alpha}_{s}\ln 2
, in the saddle point approximation. The equation describes the depletion of the probability to find
n
dipoles due to the splitting into
(n+1)
dipoles and the growth due to the splitting of
(n-1)
dipoles into
n
dipoles, it’s generalization to account for dipoles merging and transition to vacuum can be found in
Kutak and Praszałowicz 2025
. A solution of this equation
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
;
Mueller 1995
can be written as

\displaystyle P_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{C}e^{-\alpha y}\left(1-\frac{1}{C}e^{-\alpha y}\right)^{n-1},

(3)

where the parameter
C
, that slightly generalized the 1D Mueller solution, was introduced in Ref.
Hentschinski et al. 2022
. We note that the distribution given in Eq. (
3
) is the geometric distribution with
p=\frac{1}{C}e^{-\alpha y}
with its support starting at
n=1
,

\displaystyle P^{\rm geom}_{n}(y)=p\left(1-p\right)^{n-1}.

(4)

The
C
parameter can be interpreted as normalization but we keep it as a free parameter.
The mean multiplicity of dipoles, hence, can be expressed as the function of rapidity

\displaystyle\langle n\rangle=\frac{1}{p}=Ce^{\alpha y},

(5)

therefore the rapidity depends on the logarithm of the mean multiplicity,

\displaystyle y=\frac{1}{\alpha}\ln\left(\frac{\langle n\rangle}{C}\right).

(6)

By applying Eq. (
1
), a relation between the mean multiplicity and the entropy
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
can be obtained as:

\displaystyle S(y)=\langle n\rangle\ln\langle n\rangle-(\langle n\rangle-1)\ln(\langle n\rangle-1).

(7)

In the sub-asymptotic limit with
C=1
the Eq. (
6
) can be approximated by a so called universal formula
Hentschinski et al. 2023

\displaystyle S(\langle n\rangle)\approx\ln(\langle n\rangle)+1.

(8)

II.2
Generalized Mueller dipole model

A new cascade equation is

\displaystyle\partial_{y}P_{n}(y){=-}\alpha(n{+}2h)P_{n}(y){+}\alpha(n{-}1{+}2h)P_{n-1}(y)

(9)

which is a generalization of Eq. (
2
) with an extra parameter
h
. The equation has been obtained in the context of study of Krylov complexity
Nandy et al. 2025
;
Rabinovici et al. 2025
;
Baiguera et al. 2025
for the coherent states of the SL(2,R) group and mapped to QCD in Ref.
Caputa and Kutak 2024
. Here
h
, is a conformal weight of SL(2,R) and for BFKL case
h=1/2
.

In our setup, the parameter
h
measures the deviation from the 1D Mueller dipole model. While the Eq. (
9
) can be written in such form that after setting
h=1/2
it reduces to Eq. (
2
) in our studies, we use a version of this model first introduced in Ref.
Caputa and Kutak 2024
, since in this formulation one can also account for
p_{0}
, which effectively corresponds to the vacuum contribution. Such contributions were accounted for in recent studies
Hentschinski et al. 2024
;
Kutak and Praszałowicz 2025
and allowed for successful description of hadronic entropy data.

The solution of this generalized equation with the previously introduced normalization is given as

\displaystyle P_{n}(y)=\frac{\Gamma(2h+n)}{n!\Gamma(2h)}\left(\frac{1}{C}e^{-\alpha y}\right)^{2h}\left(1-\frac{1}{C}e^{-\alpha y}\right)^{n}.

(10)

One can notice once again that with
p=\frac{1}{C}e^{-\alpha y}
the solution can be identified with the negative binomial distribution (NBD), where
2h=k
is the shape or dispersion parameter of the distribution. An explicit form of the entropy of this distribution is not known (a closed form is given in Ref.
Cheraghchi 2017
), however, with this simple identification, one can explicitly express the mean multiplicity of dipoles or, in the context of Ref.
Caputa and Kutak 2024
, Krylov complexity (that is the measure of the spread of the underlying quantum state in Hilbert space), as the function of rapidity in the following form

\displaystyle\langle n\rangle=2h\left(Ce^{\alpha y}-1\right).

(11)

It is worth to emphasis that NBD is a widely applied parametrization of the measured charged particle multiplicity distributions and hitherto explained by phenomenological arguments, e.g., in Ref.
Giovannini et al. 1992
. Moreover, recently it has been shown that NBD distribution gives very good approximation of the dipole models that account for recombination effects and after introducing suitable normalization for transition to vacuum and saturation
Kutak and Praszałowicz 2025
.

III
Data and fit description

In this section we discuss fits that we obtained utilizing the previously described models. Our main observable is the
S_{h}(\ln\langle n\rangle)
that can be obtained from the models and compared to the data following the original conjecture by
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
that
S=S_{h}
.
In the following, we will assume that the mean multiplicity of hadrons can be approximated by multiplicity of dipoles and we will use the introduced models to describe hadronic entropy.
We analyzed published charged particle multiplicity distributions
P_{n}
at different energies and rapidities, the hadronic entropies
S_{h}
were directly obtained as the function of
\ln\langle n\rangle
. The 1D dipole model has already been compared to the data
Mueller 1995
;
Levin and Lublinsky 2004
;
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
;
Hentschinski et al. 2024
;
Hentschinski et al. 2022
;
Hentschinski et al. 2023
. Also, the NBD, that is the solution of the generalized model, is a common parametrization of the measured charged particle multiplicity distributions (see e.g. Refs.
Adam et al. 2017
;
Acharya et al. 2017
;
Acharya et al. 2023
).

The analyzed data sets are described in Tab.
1
. However, the psuedorapidity variable is not defined uniquely, by introducing the variable
\ln\langle n\rangle
, we are able to compare the obtained entropy among experiments. The details of the effect of the rapidity definition are discussed in Ref.
Lökös 2025
and in Appendix
A
. A comprehensive overview of multiplicity distributions and their shape can be found in Ref.
Grosse-Oetringhaus and Reygers 2010
.

We also obtain the uncertainties of
S_{h}
and
\ln\langle n\rangle
. The uncertainty of the entropy was estimated based on Refs.
Ricci et al. 2021
;
Basharin 1959
and the uncertainty of the
\ln\langle n\rangle
was propagated from the uncertainties of the multiplicity distributions. Both uncertainties have to be included into the definition of the
\chi^{2}
that is, therefore, given as

\displaystyle\chi^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{(y_{i}-f(x_{i}))^{2}}{\delta y_{i}^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial f(x_{i})}{\partial x}\delta x_{i}\right)^{2}},

(12)

where
y_{i}
is the obtained
S_{h}
at the
i^{\textmd{th}}

\ln\langle n\rangle
point,
f(x_{i})
is the model calculation evaluated at the
i^{\textmd{th}}

\ln\langle n\rangle
,
\delta x_{i}
and
\delta y_{i}
are the
i^{\textmd{th}}
uncertainties of the
\ln\langle n\rangle
and
S_{h}
, respectively. The derivative
\frac{\partial f(x_{i})}{\partial x}
is calculated numerically. The minimization was done by the CERN Minuit2 package.

Deep learning approach to energy consumption modeling in wastewater pumping systems
Authors: Jamshid Piri, Babak Masoudi, Mohammad Salkhordeh Haghighi, Ozgur Kisi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA novel deep learning approach combining ResNet, self-attention mechanisms, and Grey Wolf Optimizer is developed for wastewater pumping energy optimization. Compared to traditional PID control (baseline: 5–8% savings), our method achieves 10–30% energy savings, outperforming genetic algorithms (12–18%) and LSTM-based approaches (18–25%).The optimization framework employs β (ratio of static head to best efficiency point head) and α (pumping station sizing parameter) as key design variables. Performance evaluation using Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), newly developed Uncertainty-Aware Accuracy (UAPM), and Adaptive Complexity-Aware Errors (ACAE) revealed that higher β values (0.25 to 0.75) substantially improve energy efficiency while enhancing prediction capabilities (R²=0.9957 at β = 0.75). However, the model exhibits systematic underestimation of mean energy consumption by 40–50% across all configurations, potentially due to conservative regularization effects. Monte Carlo simulations quantified prediction uncertainty, improving operational robustness. Real-time energy optimization it contributes to sustainable wastewater infrastructure management by balancing efficiency with operational constraints, potentially reducing global energy consumption.Keywords: Deep learning, Wastewater pumping, Energy optimization, Variable speed pumps, Grey wolf optimizerSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

To contribute to the sustainability of the environment, resource management, and the economics of wastewater treatment, it is essential to incorporate an integrated approach that maximizes energy efficiency. It has been estimated that wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) in a country consume 1–3% of its total electricity, with pumping systems accounting for 15–70% of this consumption1,2. It has emerged as one of the most important research priorities within the wastewater industry to develop energy-efficient wastewater infrastructure as global energy demands intensify and environmental regulations become more stringent as the need for more energy increases. According to Torregrossa et al. (2018)., wastewater treatment facilities are often not as energy efficient as they should be, primarily because the pumping systems consume too much power to operate3. As a result of the increased sophistication of sensor networks today, WWTPs produce high-frequency operational data to assist with their operations, but comprehensive energy assessments are generally conducted only once every year. In order to process continuous data streams and optimize operations in real time, advanced algorithms are needed to handle a large amount of data that is available and energy-efficient strategies that can be implemented4–7.

In order to address this challenge, variable frequency drives (VFDs) can be used to adjust the pump parameters according to the changing flow conditions. Nevertheless, despite this, the full potential of a VFD is not developed if it is not installed with sophisticated control algorithms, which prevents it from reaching its full efficiency potential. Among the most significant expenditures attributed to water and wastewater infrastructure systems are the costs associated with pump operation scheduling, according to Lingom et al.(2023)8. Machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) could have a dramatic impact on wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs). Technology has advanced this field significantly. Using multimodal machine learning, greenhouse gas emissions can be reduced, and substantial energy savings can be achieved. Wang et al. (2024) found that advanced fuzzy control techniques reduced operational costs by 19.8%9. In addition, Mao et al. (2024) developed an artificial intelligence model that incorporated BP time-series analysis, particle swarm optimization (PSO), and Gaussian classification, resulting in a 30.9% decrease in airflow rates while maintaining high effluent quality prediction accuracy10(Table 1).

Furthermore, Ateunkeng Jean Gabain et al. (2024) propose an innovative bio-inspired machine learning control technique that reduces sludge production and aeration energy consumption while improving operational efficiency11. Based on these findings, substantial progress has been made toward improving WWTP operations’ efficiency and sustainability. A range of energy savings between 10% and 40% has been observed for these advanced control methods depending on the scale of the plant and the specific implementation12. The importance of AI-driven solutions in promoting responsible wastewater management can be seen in these innovations, which align with the Sustainable Development Goals of the United Nations. In addition to these pioneering efforts, reinforcement learning is being used to control advanced oxidation processes13 and data mining techniques are being used to optimize aeration systems14. Han et al. (2024) developed multi-objective control strategies that simultaneously addressed operational efficiency, effluent quality, and energy consumption using adaptive kernel function models and advanced optimization algorithms15. Internet of Things (IoT) technologies and intelligent water networks have made data-driven optimization methods possible. In one study, Moeinfar and Yazdi (2023) demonstrated that by simulating hydraulic networks and optimizing pump speeds, energy consumption was reduced by 6.9%16. Han et al. (2024) also developed predictive control methods that considered stochastic inflow variations and leveraged SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition) system data to target variable-speed wastewater pumps15.

Numerous studies have demonstrated measurable energy savings from algorithmic pump control. As a result of their work, Ormsbee and Lansey. (1994) developed optimal control algorithms that minimized energy costs while constraining the frequency at which pumps were switched on and off17. The results of Pulido-Calvo et al. (2011) are more dramatic: their variable-speed pumps were reduced by 92%, and their fixed-speed pumps were reduced by 50% due to their work18. An energy consumption reduction of 26.8% was achieved by Rajabpour et al. (2024) by applying genetic algorithms to sequential pumping stations19. According to Osman et al. (2024), advanced pump control strategies reduce mechanical wear, extend equipment lifespan, and require less maintenance beyond energy savings20. A dynamic model of pump stations in the Netherlands and Spain was used by De Keyser et al21., and a model for mixed-integer optimization was used by Fecarotta et al. (2018)22 for controlling pump sequencing and regulating pump speeds. According to Piri et al. (2021)23, hybrid genetic algorithms and neural network approaches perform better when variable flow conditions. In wastewater pumping applications, Gray Wolf Optimization (GWO) presents distinct advantages over other metaheuristic optimization algorithms. Mirjalili et al. (2014) developed the GWO model, miming gray wolf packs’ social hierarchy and hunting behavior24. It has conceptual simplicity, fewer tunable parameters, and robust convergence properties. It is particularly suitable for nonlinear optimization problems with complex characteristics intrinsic to pump system efficiency optimization due to its adaptive balancing of exploration and exploitation phases. The simplicity, adaptability, and effectiveness of GWO have made it successful in various engineering domains25. This goal has been achieved by implementing distributed generation and reorganizing electric distribution networks and capacitor banks26. Several civil engineering and Internet of Things (IoT) applications have also been demonstrated in the research27,28 using the algorithm. The versatility of GWO can be seen in its application to power systems, renewable energy, and manufacturing optimization25. A relatively unexplored area of GWO in hydraulic engineering remains wastewater pumping systems25,28.

This research proposes a new deep learning framework that combines the ResNet topology, self-attention, and Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) for optimizing the energy used by wastewater pumps in real time. The framework is built upon critical gaps in the literature and presents the following five contributions: (1) the first implementation of ResNet + self-attention + GWO for self-adaptive pumping system optimization, (2) real-time constraint adherence where the operational limitations are included in the training of the model, (3) Uncertainty-Aware Performance Metric (UAPM) and Adaptive Complexity-Aware Error (ACAE) for model evaluation are introduced as performance metrics, (4) evaluation of reliability in predictions using Monte Carlo methods for uncertainty quantification, and (5) multi-scale temporal modeling which integrates local pattern recognition and global dependency capturing. This framework is the first of its kind and is fully adaptive, data-driven, and capable of delivering real-time results (< 100ms inference). In addition, it was found that traditional methods are outperformed by this framework by 10–30%, effectively enabling energy-responsible management for wastewater systems. This work shifts the current paradigm for energy optimization in municipal water systems towards sustainable infrastructure management.

Monte Carlo validation

Synthetic data validation

It rigorously validated the synthesized minute-level flow data using log-normal distribution parameters (µ = 4.5490, σ = 0.3679) against 72 h of real minute-level measurements from a secondary pumping station. Statistical validation established distributional consistency through Kolmogorov-Smirnov (D = 0.048, p = 0.127), Anderson-Darling (A² = 0.821, p = 0.156), and chi-square (χ² = 12.4, p = 0.134) tests. All p-values were above 0.05, and thus the null hypothesis was accepted. Temporal correlation analysis revealed that the autocorrelation structure was maintained with lag-1 correlations matching within a difference of 1.0% (real: 0.847 vs. simulated: 0.839) and lag-5 correlations matching within a difference of 1.0% (real: 0.623 vs. simulated: 0.617). Parameter sensitivity analysis revealed that ± 10% deviations in µ and σ produced a change of < 3% in optimization results, affirming the model’s robustness. Uncertainty quantification was carried out via a 1,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation during inference for UAPM calculation. Although this validation ensures statistical consistency, we recognize that sub-hourly dynamics could be different from real minute-by-minute variability. Future validation against high-frequency measurement data is recommended when available.

Proposed method

Architectural justification

The selection of ResNet combined with self-attention mechanisms and GWO optimization is theoretically motivated by the specific characteristics of wastewater pumping systems:

ResNet Architecture: Chosen for its ability to handle vanishing gradient problems through residual connections, crucial for learning complex non-linear relationships between hydraulic parameters and energy consumption. The deep architecture enables extraction of hierarchical features from multi-dimensional operational data (flow rates, head variations, pump speeds).

Self-Attention Mechanism: Captures long-range temporal dependencies essential for understanding how flow patterns at different time scales influence energy consumption. Unlike traditional recurrent networks, self-attention allows the model to simultaneously consider relationships across the entire time sequence, critical for optimizing pump scheduling over extended periods.

Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO): Selected over alternative metaheuristic algorithms due to:

Conceptual simplicity with fewer tunable parameters.

Conceptual simplicity with fewer tunable parameters.

Robust convergence properties in non-linear optimization spaces.

Robust convergence properties in non-linear optimization spaces.

Proven effectiveness in hydraulic engineering applications.

Proven effectiveness in hydraulic engineering applications.

Adaptive balance between exploration and exploitation phases.

Synergistic Integration: ResNet extracts local operational patterns, self-attention captures global temporal relationships, and GWO provides robust optimization without local optima entrapment. This combination addresses the multi-scale, non-linear nature of wastewater pumping optimization more effectively than traditional PID control or simple genetic algorithms.

As shown in Fig. 5, we have developed this theoretically grounded deep learning model for real-time wastewater pumping optimization. This model cleverly combines ResNet and self-attention. Based on how the pumps work and how the water flows, ResNet analyzes the data and identifies important local patterns. Afterwards, self-attention determines the relationship between all those patterns, even subtle and long-range relationships. As these components work together, the model can learn and implement strategies that reduce the overall cost of running these vital systems while reducing energy consumption.

Novel accuracy introduction

Apart from conventional RMSE, we use two other measures for overall model assessment in dynamic wastewater systems:

Uncertainty-Aware Performance Metric (UAPM) takes into consideration prediction uncertainty estimated via Monte Carlo dropout at inference time (1000 forward passes with dropout on for estimating prediction variance). The lower the UAPM, the better the performance considering model uncertainty. Adaptive Complexity-Aware Error (ACAE) considers computational efficiency alongside prediction accuracy. Lower ACAE values indicate better performance considering computational constraints. These measures offer more complete assessment than conventional RMSE alone, especially vital for real-time wastewater pumping optimization where both predictive uncertainty and computational expediency are essential operational factors.

Limitations and future work

This study has several important limitations that should be acknowledged:

Synthetic Data Dependency: The use of Monte Carlo-generated minute-level data, while statistically validated, may not capture actual sub-hourly flow dynamics that could affect optimization performance.

Systematic Underestimation: The model consistently underestimates mean energy consumption by 40–50%. This bias may result from:

Single-Site Validation: Results are based on data from one wastewater treatment plant (Zahedan), limiting generalizability across different facility types and operational conditions.

Simulation-Only Environment: This study presents a simulation-based optimization framework. No real-time implementation or field deployment at the Zahedan WWTP was conducted. All validation was performed using historical operational data in a controlled simulation environment.

Computational Complexity: The proposed ResNet + self-attention architecture requires significant computational resources, potentially limiting real-time application in resource-constrained environments.

Conclusion

This study successfully demonstrates that the novel integration of ResNet, self-attention mechanisms, and Grey Wolf Optimizer achieves significant energy optimization in wastewater pumping systems, with variable-speed operations delivering 10–30% energy savings compared to constant-speed systems across diverse operational conditions. The proposed framework outperforms alternative approaches including traditional PID control (5–8% savings), genetic algorithms (12–18%), and LSTM-based methods (18–25%), while introducing three key methodological contributions: the first ResNet + self-attention architecture specifically designed for pumping optimization, original UAPM and ACAE performance metrics for uncertainty-aware evaluation, and a systematic bias correction framework that reduces energy underestimation from − 45% to −8%. With real-time inference capabilities (< 100ms), the approach enables practical SCADA integration and offers substantial economic benefits including $15,000–25,000 annual savings for medium-sized WWTPs and 8–12 tons CO₂ equivalent reduction annually. However, the study acknowledges important limitations including single-site validation that restricts generalizability, reliance on synthetic minute-level data that may not capture all operational dynamics, and computational requirements that could limit edge deployment applications. Future research should prioritize multi-site validation across diverse WWTP configurations, real-time deployment testing, transfer learning development for rapid facility adaptation, and further validation of the bias correction framework across different operational scenarios to enhance the practical applicability and robustness of this energy optimization approach in municipal wastewater infrastructure management.

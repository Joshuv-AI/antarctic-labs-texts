Clinicopathological analysis of 34 cases of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions
Authors: Hao Long, Lvfu He, Weimin He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions. A retrospective study was conducted on 34 patients with pathologically confirmed extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions who underwent ophthalmic surgery at West China Hospital of Sichuan University between January 2010 and December 2024. Clinical data, including demographic characteristics, clinical presentations, imaging features, pathological diagnoses, treatment regimens, and prognosis, were collected and systematically analyzed. Among the 34 patients, 18 were male and 16 were female, with ages ranging from 7 to 78 years (mean age: 48.2 ± 12.5 years). The main clinical manifestations included proptosis (70.6%, 24/34), decreased ocular motility (DOM) (32.4%, 11/34), and eyelid swelling (32.4%, 11/34). The predominant pathological types were inflammatory pseudotumor (38.2%, 13/34; including 5 sclerosing subtype and 8 mixed subtype), hemangioma (20.6%, 7/34), and mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma (17.6%, 6/34). The lateral rectus muscle was the most frequently involved site (38.2%, 13/34). The primary treatment modalities were surgery combined with glucocorticoids (47.1%, 16/34) or radiotherapy (29.4%, 10/34). Following treatment, 20.6% (7/34) of patients showed significant symptomatic improvement, 44.1% (15/34) achieved stable disease, and 26.5% (9/34) reached complete response. The main pathological types of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions are inflammatory pseudotumor (sclerosing and mixed subtypes), hemangioma, and MALT lymphoma, with the lateral rectus muscle being most commonly affected. Introduction

Extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions represent a group of highly heterogeneous disorders characterized by the proliferation of abnormal tissue within the extraocular muscles, with a complex and diverse etiology involving neoplastic, inflammatory, and autoimmune factors. Due to their non-specific clinical presentations, these lesions are prone to misdiagnosis and underdiagnosis in clinical practice. Most existing studies, both domestically and internationally, are limited to case reports or small-sample analyses, and a systematic investigation of their clinicopathological features is still lacking1–5. Advances in high-resolution imaging techniques and molecular pathological detection methods have significantly improved the diagnostic accuracy for these lesions and subsequently optimized treatment strategies. However, data regarding their clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis remain insufficient. This retrospective study analyzed the clinical data of 34 patients with pathologically confirmed extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions, aiming to systematically summarize the pathological types, imaging features, and prognostic outcomes, thereby providing an evidence-based foundation for clinical decision-making.

Materials and methods

Design and sample

A total of 34 patients with pathologically confirmed extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions, who underwent ophthalmic surgery at West China Hospital of Sichuan University between January 2010 and December 2024, were enrolled in this study. The inclusion criteria were as follows: definite histopathological diagnosis of an extraocular muscle space-occupying lesion; and availability of complete clinical data. The exclusion criteria were: concurrent involvement of other orbital tissues; and loss to follow-up. All cases were comprehensively diagnosed based on clinical presentations, imaging features, and histopathological examination results. This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of West China Hospital of Sichuan University (Approval No.: 2024 Review 841) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients or their legal guardians.

Surgical approaches

The surgical management approach—whether to perform a biopsy or proceed with complete resection—was determined intraoperatively based on the macroscopic appearance and intraoperative findings. Preoperative imaging (CT or MRI) was utilized to localize the involved extraocular muscle and to guide the surgical access route. Specifically, a superior conjunctival approach was used for three cases involving the superior rectus muscle; an inferior fornix conjunctival approach for nine cases of the inferior rectus; a medial conjunctival approach for nine cases of the medial rectus; a lateral orbitotomy approach for 13 cases of the lateral rectus; and a superomedial conjunctival approach for two cases of the superior oblique muscle.

The core surgical principle was to preserve normal extraocular muscle fibers to the greatest extent possible. Dissection of the mass or affected muscle was consistently performed along the direction of the muscle fibers to minimize functional disruption.

Data collection

Demographic data, clinical symptoms, imaging findings, pathological diagnoses, treatment regimens, and follow-up data were collected. For cases with recurrence, only the clinical data from the first recurrence event were analyzed. The follow-up deadline was August 31, 2025, with a mean follow-up duration of 28.8 months.

Treatment responses were defined as follows: Complete response was defined as the resolution of all clinical symptoms and signs to normal or near-normal levels, the normalization of related biomarkers, and the absence of detectable lesions on imaging. Stable disease was considered when a patient’s clinical symptoms, signs, biomarker levels, and imaging findings neither met the criteria for complete response nor showed evidence of progression. Improvement was characterized by the partial alleviation of symptoms and signs with mild to moderate residual effects, accompanied by ameliorated biomarker profiles and an enhanced quality of life. Disease progression was marked by significant negative changes in the patient’s symptoms, signs, laboratory parameters, or imaging results.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 24.0 (IBM Corp., USA). Normally distributed continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent samples t-test. Non-normally distributed data were presented as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were described as numbers (percentages) and compared using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographics characteristics

This study enrolled a total of 34 patients with extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions, comprising 18 males (52.9%) and 16 females (47.1%), with a mean age of 48.2 ± 12.5 years (range, 7–78). Clinical presentations

As summarized in Table 2, the clinical manifestations of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions were diverse. The most common presentation was proptosis (70.6%, 24/34), followed by decreased ocular motility (DOM) (32.4%, 11/34) and eyelid swelling (32.4%, 11/34). Notably, eyelid swelling was significantly more prevalent in patients with inflammatory pseudotumor or mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma (P < 0.05).

Imaging characteristics

All patients underwent orbital imaging, comprising computed tomography (CT) in 11 (32.4%), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in 25 (73.5%), and both modalities in 2 (5.9%). On CT, the majority of cases (8/11, 72.7%) presented as a well-defined, homogenous, isodense mass within the extraocular muscle (Fig. 1). Specific findings included diffuse muscle thickening with ill-defined margins in one case (9.1%) of sclerosing inflammatory pseudotumor, irregular muscle enlargement with indistinct borders in two cases (18.2%) of MALT lymphoma, and a well-circumscribed, round, homogeneous mass in one case (9.1%) of hemangioma (Table 3).

Comparative analysis of clinical features across histopathological groups revealed that patients with lymphoproliferative disorders (mean age, 56.1 years) and those with inflammatory diseases (mean age, 49.6 years) were significantly older than those with neoplastic diseases (mean age, 36.4 years) (P = 0.046 and P = 0.032, respectively). Inflammatory, lymphoproliferative, and structural lesions showed a male predominance, whereas neoplastic diseases were more common in females.

Treatment, response and outcomes

Table 7 outlines the treatment modalities and prognostic outcomes for the patients. During a mean follow-up period of 28.8 months (range, 8–60), tumor recurrence was observed in 3 patients (8.8%, Table 5). Among the cohort, surgical management consisted of biopsy in 13 patients (38.2%) and complete mass or muscle resection in 20 patients (58.8%). One patient (2.9%) underwent a second procedure for definitive resection following diagnostic confirmation via initial biopsy. The primary management for MALT lymphoma was surgery combined with radiotherapy (83.3%, n = 5), whereas inflammatory pseudotumor was primarily treated with surgery plus corticosteroids (61.5%, n = 8) or radiotherapy (38.5%, n = 5). Regarding prognosis, the overall complete response rate was 26.5% (n = 9), with 44.1% (n = 15) achieving stable disease, 20.6% (n = 7) showing improvement, and 5.9% (n = 2) experiencing disease progression. Outcomes varied significantly by pathology: all patients with hemangioma achieved complete response after surgical excision; patients with MALT lymphoma and most inflammatory pseudotumor (sclerosing and mixed types) had favorable outcomes, predominantly stable disease or improvement. In contrast, both patients with immunoglobulin G4-related ophthalmic disease (IgG4-ROD) experienced recurrence at 6 and 8 months post-treatment, respectively, despite combined corticosteroid therapy. The first case achieved stable disease after multidisciplinary consultation recommended adding the immunosuppressant mycophenolate mofetil to corticosteroids; the second case improved after re-excision of the entire affected muscle combined with corticosteroids. Furthermore, one patient with malignant schwannoma died from intracranial metastasis 3 years after treatment involving surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. Another patient with schwannoma, concurrently diagnosed with a retrobulbar meningioma, underwent biopsy-confirming schwannoma with simultaneous meningioma resection. This patient required a second surgery for schwannoma recurrence after 3 years, subsequently achieving stable disease.

Discussion

Extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions represent a group of rare clinical entities with diverse etiologies. Their clinical manifestations and imaging characteristics vary significantly according to the underlying pathology. This study, through a systematic analysis of 34 pathologically confirmed cases, delineates the clinicopathological features and therapeutic outcomes of these lesions.

Our investigation revealed a predominant unilateral involvement (94.1%), with a nearly equal gender distribution (male-to-female ratio: 1.13:1). Proptosis (70.6%) was the most common clinical presentation, which is consistent with the findings of He Yanjin et al.6 but differs from the study by Wang Yi et al.7 which reported DOM as the primary symptom. This discrepancy may be attributed to differences in sample selection criteria and case composition. Notably, patients with lymphoproliferative disorders were significantly older (mean age: 56.1 years) than those in other groups (P = 0.002) and had a longer disease duration (60.3 months vs. 19.1 months, P < 0.001), suggesting that age and symptom duration may serve as valuable references for differential diagnosis. Therefore, future studies should aim to develop and validate a clinical prediction model that incorporates key variables such as patient age, symptom duration, and distinctive imaging features to guide the decision for biopsy. Furtherly, whether the pathogenesis of lymphoproliferative disorders in this context is associated with an age-related decline in immune surveillance or persistent antigenic stimulation remains to be elucidated and warrants further investigation. In addition, while not statistically significant, inflammatory diseases showed a male predominance (62.5%), whereas neoplastic diseases were more common in females (57.1%). This trend warrants further validation with a larger sample size.

Our findings demonstrate that both CT and MRI provide valuable, complementary information for the preoperative evaluation of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions. A comparative analysis of the key imaging features across the three major pathological subtypes—inflammatory pseudotumor, hemangioma, and MALT lymphoma—reveals distinct patterns that can guide diagnostic considerations.

On CT, inflammatory pseudotumors are typically characterized by an irregular spindle shape, relatively homogeneous density, and relatively well-defined borders. Hemangiomas often present as irregularly rounded or oval masses with heterogeneous internal density yet well-defined margins. In contrast, MALT lymphomas mostly appear as irregular, mass-like lesions with homogeneous density but indistinct borders. These CT characteristics, particularly lesion morphology, density, and margin definition, offer a foundational diagnostic tendency prior to intervention.

MRI provides a more detailed soft-tissue assessment. Inflammatory pseudotumors commonly exhibit hypointense to intermediate T1 signal, intermediate to hyperintense T2 signal, and homogeneous post-contrast enhancement. Hemangiomas typically show intermediate T1 signal, markedly high T2 signal, and heterogeneous enhancement. MALT lymphomas frequently demonstrate hypointense to intermediate signals on both T1- and T2-weighted images, with homogeneous enhancement. The superior contrast resolution of MRI better delineates lesion boundaries and internal architecture, adding a functional dimension to the morphological assessment provided by CT.

In synthesis, while CT effectively outlines basic morphological and densitometric features that suggest certain pathological subtypes, MRI enhances diagnostic confidence by characterizing tissue composition, vascularity, and exact anatomical relationships. When clinically feasible, employing both modalities allows for a more comprehensive preoperative evaluation, integrating structural details from CT with the superior soft-tissue contrast of MRI to narrow the differential diagnosis and inform surgical planning.

Pathologically, inflammatory pseudotumor (38.2%; comprising 5 sclerosing and 8 mixed subtypes) was identified as the most frequent pathological type, followed by hemangioma (20.5%) and MALT lymphoma (17.6%). This distribution differs from the report by He et al.6, where hemangioma and schwannoma were cited as primary causes, a discrepancy likely related to differing inclusion criteria. The relatively high proportion of MALT lymphoma (17.6%) is consistent with findings from a Taiwanese study8, potentially indicating an association with environmental factors. The existing literature on extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions consists predominantly of isolated case reports. Documented examples include solitary fibrous tumors7, rectal cancer metastasis7, and rhabdomyosarcoma of the extraocular muscles7, cysticercosis of the inferior rectus muscle9, lipoma of the superior rectus muscle10, and bilateral superior rectus muscle enlargement secondary to metastatic breast cancer11.

In contrast to previous reports identifying the medial rectus as the most frequently involved muscle6, our study found that the lateral rectus was the most commonly affected site (38.2%). This discrepancy may be attributed to the predilection of orbital masses for the appendages of the lateral rectus muscle, or to the fact that the lateral rectus possesses the longest muscle belly among the extraocular muscles, thereby increasing its anatomical susceptibility to involvement. Further observation of this phenomenon in future studies is warranted to validate and elucidate its clinical relevance. Furthermore, as the sole abductor of the eye, even subtle involvement may lead to clinically noticeable abduction deficits, increasing its detection rate. Notably, the highest rate of bilateral involvement was observed in mixed-type inflammatory pseudotumor (15.4%), underscoring its systemic inflammatory nature.

The management and prognosis of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions vary significantly depending on their pathological nature. For non-neoplastic conditions such as inflammatory pseudotumor, IgG4-related ophthalmic disease, and reactive lymphoid hyperplasia, treatment primarily involves surgical intervention combined with glucocorticoid therapy, which in most cases leads to favorable outcomes. Benign tumors, including hemangioma, granular cell tumor, and benign schwannoma, are uniformly treated with surgical resection, resulting in complete remission and no recurrence. In cases of low-grade malignancies like MALT lymphoma, surgical excision combined with radiotherapy generally achieves disease stabilization or clinical improvement, aligns with previous studies8,12,13. By contrast, the patient with malignant schwannoma received multimodal therapy encompassing surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy but ultimately succumbed to intracranial metastasis three years later. Overall, prognostic outcomes are closely associated with pathological subtype, underscoring the importance of histology-guided clinical management.

Our cohort analysis confirms inflammatory pseudotumor (including sclerosing and mixed types) as the leading pathological diagnosis (38.2%), consistent with the overall epidemiological profile of orbital inflammatory pseudotumors14. It is noteworthy that while thyroid eye disease is the most common cause of extraocular muscle enlargement, inflammatory pseudotumor appears more prominent among cases requiring surgical intervention and pathological examination.

Among lymphoproliferative disorders, MALT lymphoma was the most prevalent (17.6%), matching the reported pathological distribution of ocular adnexal lymphomas15. Recent advances suggest an association between Chlamydia psittaci infection and the pathogenesis of ocular adnexal MALT lymphoma. Notably, antibiotic therapy targeting this pathogen has been shown to induce significant regression in seropositive patients16. Therefore, we recommend establishing standardized screening protocols for infectious agents in the clinical workflow. While detailed history-taking and systematic physical examination are invaluable for differential diagnosis, histopathological examination remains the gold standard for definitive diagnosis.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, the sample size is small, constrained by the rarity of these lesions. Secondly, its single-center, retrospective design may introduce selection bias. Finally, the lack of molecular biological or etiological testing (e.g., for C. psittaci) limits to verify or refute the role of this pathogen in MALT lymphoma of the ocular appendages in the Chinese population. Consequently, future multi-center, large-sample prospective studies incorporating molecular marker analysis are essential to optimize diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Despite these limitations, this study provides the first comprehensive summary of the clinicopathological characteristics of extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions.

Conclusion

Extraocular muscle space-occupying lesions are predominantly inflammatory pseudotumor (sclerosing and mixed types), hemangiomas, and MALT lymphomas, with the lateral rectus muscle being most frequently involved. Histopathological examination is crucial for definitive diagnosis, and treatment should be individualized based on the etiology. For instance, inflammatory lesions are typically managed with surgical excision combined with glucocorticoid therapy, whereas benign tumors generally require surgical resection alone. Malignant lesions, however, often demand multimodal and individualized treatment strategies. Future efforts should focus on expanding the sample size, prolonging follow-up duration, and integrating molecular biological detection to further validate and refine these conclusions, thereby providing more robust evidence for clinical practice.

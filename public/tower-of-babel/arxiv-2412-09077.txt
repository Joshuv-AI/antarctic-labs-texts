A Symplectic Discretization Based Proximal Point Algorithm for Convex Minimization Thanks: This work was partially supported by grant 12288201 from NSF of China.
Ya-xiang Yuan, Yi Zhang

Abstract
The proximal point algorithm plays a central role in non-smooth convex programming. The Augmented Lagrangian Method, one of the most famous optimization algorithms, has been found to be closely related to the proximal point algorithm. Due to its importance, accelerated variants of the proximal point algorithm have received considerable attention. In this paper, we first study an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) system, which provides valuable insights into proving the convergence rate of the desired algorithm. Using the Lyapunov function technique, we establish the convergence rate of the ODE system. Next, we apply the Symplectic Euler Method to discretize the ODE system to derive a new proximal point algorithm, called the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm (SPPA). By utilizing the proof techniques developed for the ODE system, we demonstrate the convergence rate of the SPPA. Additionally, it is shown that existing accelerated proximal point algorithm can be considered a special case of the SPPA in a specific manner. Furthermore, under several additional assumptions, we prove that the SPPA exhibits a finer convergence rate.

Introduction

With the growth of high-dimensional statistics and machine learning, many optimization problems without smooth derivative have come up. The Proximal Point Algorithm (PPA) is a powerful method for dealing with such non-smooth optimization challenges. Initially, PPA was used to the study of regularization of ill-posed problems

[

[

In 1983, Nesterov introduced the Nesterov’s accelerated gradient (NAG) method

1.1
Existing Results

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space, and let
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
denote the set of all closed, proper, convex functions defined on
\mathcal{H}
. The inner product on
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and the corresponding norm is
\|\cdot\|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
. Suppose that
L:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a positive definite operator. The inner product with respect to
L
is given by
\langle x,y\rangle_{L}=\langle Lx,y\rangle
, and the norm with respect to
L
is given by
\|x\|_{L}=\sqrt{\langle Lx,x\rangle}
.

Throughout this paper, we consider the following optimization problem:

\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}f(x),

(1)

where
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
. Here, we assume that the set of all minimizers of
f
, labeled as
\Omega
, is non-empty. The preconditioned proximal point algorithm (PPA) with respect to the preconditioner
L
is given by:

x_{k+1}=\mathop{\arg\min}_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{f(x)+\frac{1}{2\rho_{k}}\|x-x_{k}\|^{2}_{L}\right\},

(2)

where
\{\rho_{k}\}
is a sequence in
(0,\infty)
. If
L
is the identity operator
I
, then (
2
) reduces to the standard PPA.

In
[
15
]
, Güler proved that the convergence rate of the standard PPA is:

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leqslant\frac{\mathrm{dist}_{I}(x_{0},\Omega)^{2}}{2\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho_{i}},

where
f^{*}
is the minimum value of
f
, and
\mathrm{dist}_{L}(x_{0},\Omega)=\inf_{x\in\Omega}\|x_{0}-x\|_{L}
.

In
[
17
]
, Güler proposed the following accelerated PPA given as follows:

1.2
Our Contributions

In this paper, we aim to propose a new accelerated preconditioned PPA by employing the Symplectic Euler Method to discretize a first-order ODE. Owing to the usage of Symplectic Euler Method, we call this new PPA as
Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm (SPPA)
. The details of the development and theory of SPPA are provided as follows.

First, inspired by the ODE used to analyze the accelerated mirror descent method in
[
42
]
, we propose the following ODE:

\begin{split}Z&=b_{t}\dot{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X)+X,\\
\dot{Z}&=-a_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X),\\
Z(0)&=X(0)=x_{0},\end{split}

(4)

where
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
is continuously differentiable, and
a_{t},b_{t},
and
c_{t}
are functions defined on
[0,+\infty)
such that they are positive on
(0,+\infty)
and non-negative at
0
. Also, we assume that
a_{t}b_{t}
is differentiable respect to
t
. By applying the Lyapunov function technique, we first prove that the convergence rate of the solution trajectory to (
4
) is
O(1/a_{t}b_{t})
under the assumption that
0\leqslant\dfrac{d}{dt}(a_{t}b_{t})\leqslant a_{t}
. Additionally, we demonstrate that the convergence rate of the solution trajectory to (
4
) is
o(1/a_{t}b_{t})
under some additional assumptions.

Next, we apply the Symplectic Euler Method to (
4
) to obtain our SPPA. The motivation for applying the Symplectic Euler Method comes from the works in
[
36
,
35
]
. In these works, it was shown that the NAG method can be viewed as applying the symplectic method to the high-resolution ODEs formulated in phase space representation. Since the Symplectic Euler Method can preserve geometric structure of the ODEs and has a simple iteration rule, we use the Symplectic Euler Method to derive our algorithm.

First, we give a concise introduction to the Symplectic Euler Method. Consider the following Hamiltonian system:

Consider the following Hamiltonian system:

\begin{split}\dot{p}&=-\frac{\partial H(p,q)}{\partial q},\\
\dot{q}&=\frac{\partial H(p,q)}{\partial p}.\end{split}

(5)

The Symplectic Euler Method for solving (
5
) is given as follows:

\begin{split}p_{k+1}&=p_{k}-s\frac{\partial H(p_{k},q_{k+1})}{\partial q},\\
q_{k+1}&=q_{k}+s\frac{\partial H(p_{k},q_{k+1})}{\partial p},\end{split}\quad\text{or}\quad\begin{split}p_{k+1}&=p_{k}-s\frac{\partial H(p_{k+1},q_{k})}{\partial q},\\
q_{k+1}&=q_{k}+s\frac{\partial H(p_{k+1},q_{k})}{\partial p},\end{split}

(6)

where
s>0
is the step-size of Symplectic Euler Method. Because one variable takes an explicit step and the other variable takes an implicit step, this method is also referred to as the Semi-Implicit Euler Method. To acquire more acknowledge about symplectic methods, we refer to
[
14
]
and
[
20
]
. The introduction of Symplectic Euler Method can be found in I.1.2 in
[
14
]
.

Figure 1:
Comparison between some existing Euler methods. Here we apply the Explicit Euler Method, Symplectic Euler Method and Implicit Euler Method to the Hamiltonian system
\dot{p}=q
,
\dot{q}=-p
with initial
p(0)=0
and
q(0)=1
. The solution to such Hamiltonian system is
p=\sin t
,
q=\cos t
. As we can see from these three figures, the Symplectic Euler method preserve the structure of the solution.

Here we apply the Symplectic Euler Method to discretize (
4
), where
Z
takes the explicit step,
X
takes the implicit step, and
s=1
. Then, we obtain the following recursive rule:

\begin{split}z_{0}&=x_{0},\\
z_{k}&=b_{k}(x_{k+1}-x_{k})+c_{k}L^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k+1})+x_{k+1},\\
z_{k+1}&=z_{k}-a_{k}L^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k+1}),\end{split}

(7)

where the sequences
\{a_{k}\}
,
\{b_{k}\}
and
\{c_{k}\}
are positive if
k\geqslant 1
and non-negative if
k=0
. However, the convergence results of (
2
) do not require
f
to be differentiable. Therefore, we need to eliminate the usage of
\nabla f
from (
7
). By using the first-order characterization of the convex optimization problem, the update rule of
x_{k+1}
can be transformed into:

\displaystyle\tilde{x}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b_{k}+1}z_{k}+\frac{b_{k}}{b_{k}+1}x_{k},

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\arg\min}_{x}\left\{f(x)+\frac{b_{k}+1}{2c_{k}}\left\|x-\tilde{x}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{L}\right\}.

Additionally, using the relation
L^{-1}\nabla f(x_{k+1})=c_{k}^{-1}[b_{k}x_{k}+z_{k}-(b_{k}+1)x_{k+1}]
, the update rule for
z_{k+1}
can be rewritten as:

z_{k+1}=z_{k}+\frac{a_{k}(b_{k}+1)}{c_{k}}(x_{k+1}-\tilde{x}_{k+1}).

In conclusion, we obtain the following algorithm.

Algorithm 2

Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm

for

k=0,1,\cdots

do

Choose
a_{k}
,
b_{k}
and
c_{k}
such that all of them are positive if
k\geqslant 1
and non-negative for
k=0
;

\tilde{x}_{k+1}=\dfrac{1}{b_{k}+1}z_{k}+\dfrac{b_{k}}{b_{k}+1}x_{k}
;

x_{k+1}=\mathop{\arg\min}\limits_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\left\{f(x)+\dfrac{b_{k}+1}{2c_{k}}\left\|x-\tilde{x}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}_{L}\right\}
;

z_{k+1}=z_{k}+\dfrac{a_{k}(b_{k}+1)}{c_{k}}(x_{k+1}-\tilde{x}_{k+1})
;

Theoretically, by applying the Lyapunov function technique, we prove that the convergence rate of Algorithm
2
is
O(1/a_{k}b_{k})
under the assumptions that
0\leqslant a_{k+1}b_{k+1}-a_{k}b_{k}\leqslant a_{k}
and
c_{k}\geqslant\dfrac{c_{k}}{2}
. Under these conditions, we demonstrate that SPPA can be seen as a generalization of A-PPA. Moreover, the corresponding convergence rate of SPPA is shown to be faster than that of A-PPA. This enhanced convergence rate may be attributed to the structure-preserving property of the Symplectic Euler Method. Additionally, under some extra assumptions, we prove that the convergence rate of Algorithm
2
is
o(1/a_{k}b_{k})
.

Convergence Rate of ODE

In this section, we study the convergence rate of (
4
). For simplicity, when proving the theorems in this section, we use the notations

,
Z
,
\overset{\cdot}{X}
, and
\overset{\cdot}{Z}

\overset{\cdot}{X}(t)

First, we need to establish the

convergence rate. To achieve this, we propose the following Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{E}(t):=A_{t}[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\|Z(t)-x^{*}\|_{L}^{2},

(8)

where
x^{*}\in\Omega
. Here,
A_{t}[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]
represents the potential term of
\mathcal{E}(t)
, and
A_{t}
indicates the convergence rate of the solution trajectory to (
4
).
\dfrac{1}{2}\|Z(t)-x^{*}\|_{L}^{2}
is the mixed term. By analyzing (

Theorem 1
.

Let
(X(t),Z(t))
be the solution to (
4
). If

A_{t}=a_{t}b_{t},\qquad 0\leqslant\dot{A}_{t}\leqslant a_{t},

(9)

and

is continuous differentiable, then

is non-increasing. And we have

f(X(t))-f^{*}\leqslant\frac{A_{0}}{A_{t}}[f(x_{0})-f^{*}]+\frac{\mathrm{dist}_{L}(x_{0},\Omega)^{2}}{2A_{t}}.

(10)

Additionally, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(a_{t}-\dot{A}_{t})\left\langle\nabla f(X(t)),X(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\leqslant A_{0}[f(x_{0})-f^{*}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L},\ \forall x^{*}\in\Omega,

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{t}c_{t}\left\|\nabla f(X(t))\right\|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leqslant A_{0}[f(x_{0})-f^{*}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}_{L}(x_{0},\Omega)^{2}.

(12)

Proof.

Step 1: Estimating the upper bound of
\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)
. By calculating the derivative of (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\ \overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)=\dot{A}_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+A_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overset{\cdot}{Z},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \dot{A}_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+A_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle-a_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),b_{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X)+X-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \dot{A}_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]-a_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle-a_{t}c_{t}\left\|\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{L^{-1}}.

Since
f
is convex, we have

f(X)-f(x^{*})\leqslant\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Thus

\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leqslant(\dot{A}_{t}-a_{t})\left\langle\nabla f(X(t)),X(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle-a_{t}c_{t}\left\|\nabla f(X)\right\|_{L^{-1}}^{2}\leqslant 0.

Step 2: Deducing the convergence rates. Because
\mathcal{E}(t)
is non-increasing, we have

A_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(t)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0)=A_{0}[f(x_{0})-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}.

By dividing
A_{t}
on both sides of the above inequality and taking the infimum over to all
x^{*}\in\Omega
, we obtain (
10
).

Next, by integrating
\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)
from
0
to
\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(a_{t}-\dot{A}_{t})\left\langle\nabla f(X(t)),X(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t\leqslant-\int_{0}^{\infty}\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0),

(13)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{t}c_{t}\left\|\nabla f(X(t))\right\|_{L^{-1}}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\leqslant-\int_{0}^{\infty}\overset{\cdot}{\mathcal{E}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0).

(14)

It is obvious that (
13
) is exactly (
11
). Also, By taking the infimum over all
x^{*}\in\Omega
, we obtain (
12
).
∎

.

c_{t}

Lemma 1
.

Let
(X(t),Z(t))
be the solution to (

be an auxiliary function defined as

\mathcal{G}(t):=c_{t}[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\|X(t)-x^{*}\|^{2}_{L}-\langle X(t)-x^{*},Z(t)-x^{*}\rangle_{L}.

\left\|X(t)-x\right\|^{2}_{L}

Proof.

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{G}}(t)=

\displaystyle\ \dot{c}_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+c_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),\overset{\cdot}{X}\right\rangle+\left\langle\overset{\cdot}{X},X-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -b_{t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}\|^{2}_{L}+a_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle+\dot{c}_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})].

By previous calculations and the proof for Theorem

\displaystyle\ (\dot{A}_{t}+\alpha\dot{c}_{t})[f(X)-f(x^{*})]-(1-\alpha)a_{t}\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ [-(1-\alpha-d)a_{t}+\alpha\dot{c}_{t}][f(X)-f(x^{*})]-\alpha b_{t}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ 0.

Also, since

can be represented as sum of non-negative terms as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(t)=

\displaystyle\ (A_{t}+\alpha c_{t})[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|Z-x^{*}-\alpha(X-x^{*})\right\|^{2}.

which shows that

,

then we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}A_{t}[f(X(t))-f^{*}]=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
and Theorem
1
, we have that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{G}(t)
exists. Since
f(X(t))-f(x^{*})\leqslant O(1/A_{t})
and
c_{t}\leqslant\gamma a_{t}
for all
t\geqslant t_{0}
, then we have
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}c_{t}[f(X(t))-f(x^{*})]=0
. Thus

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left\|X(t)-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}-\left\langle X(t)-x^{*},Z(t)-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}\text{ exists.}

Next, we will show that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X(t)-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
exists. By calculating the derivative of
\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle\overset{\cdot}{X},X-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{b_{t}}\left\langle Z-X-c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{b_{t}}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}+\frac{1}{b_{t}}\left\langle X-x^{*},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}-\frac{c_{t}}{b_{t}}\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle

Then we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}+\frac{t}{\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\frac{t}{b_{t}\beta}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}+\frac{t}{b_{t}\beta}\left\langle X-x^{*},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}-\frac{c_{t}t}{b_{t}\beta}\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Since
c_{t}\leqslant\gamma a_{t}
for all
t\geqslant t_{0}
, then by using (
11
), we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{c_{t}t}{b_{t}\beta}\left\langle\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle=0.

Next, because
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\left\langle X-x^{*},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}
exists and
\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
is uniformly bounded, we have that

\displaystyle\ \lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{t}{b_{t}\beta}\right)\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}+\frac{t}{b_{t}\beta}\left\langle X-x^{*},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\left\langle X-x^{*},Z-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}.

Thus,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}+\dfrac{t}{\beta}\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
exists. Then by Lemma
4
,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
exists. Also, we have

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left\langle b_{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}\text{ exists.}

Finally, we can prove
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}A_{t}[f(X)-f^{*}]=0
. Owing to the existence of
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)
,
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|X-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}
, and
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\left\langle b_{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X),X-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}
, we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}A_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|b_{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{L}\text{ exists.}

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\frac{1}{2}\left\|b_{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{L}\leqslant b_{t}^{2}\|\overset{\cdot}{X}\|^{2}_{L}+c_{t}^{2}\left\|\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{L^{-1}}.

Then by (
11
), (
12
), (
16
), and the assumptions that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\dfrac{t}{b_{t}}\in(0,\infty)
and
c_{t}\leqslant\gamma a_{t}
, we have that

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}A_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]=\lim_{t\to\infty}A_{t}[f(X)-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|b_{t}\overset{\cdot}{X}+c_{t}L^{-1}\nabla f(X)\right\|^{2}_{L}=0.

∎

3
Convergence Rate of SPPA

In this section, we establish the convergence rate of Algorithm
2
. For notational convenience, we introduce the term
\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}):=\dfrac{(b_{k}+1)}{c_{k}}L(\tilde{x}_{k+1}-x_{k+1})
. By the first-order characterization of
x_{k+1}
, it follows that
\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\in\partial f(x_{k+1})
. Analogous to the discussion in Section
2
, our initial step is to demonstrate both the last-iterate and the convergence rate in a summation form of Algorithm
2
. To achieve this, we define the following discrete-time Lyapunov function:

\mathcal{E}(k):=A_{k}[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\|z_{k}-x^{*}\|_{L}^{2}.

(17)

This Lyapunov function serves as a discrete counterpart to (
8
). Through the analysis of (
17
), we derive the subsequent theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
,
\{\tilde{x}_{k+1}\}
, and
\{z_{k}\}
denote the sequences generated by Algorithm
2
. Assuming the conditions

A_{k}=a_{k}b_{k},\qquad 0\leqslant A_{k+1}-A_{k}\leqslant a_{k},\qquad c_{k}\geqslant\frac{a_{k}}{2},

(18)

and
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
, the sequence
\{\mathcal{E}(k)\}
is non-increasing. Consequently, the last-iterate convergence rate of Algorithm
2
is given by

f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leqslant\frac{A_{0}}{A_{k}}[f(x_{0})-f^{*}]+\frac{\mathrm{dist}_{L}(x_{0},\Omega)^{2}}{2A_{k}}.

(19)

Additionally, for any
x^{*}\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(a_{k}+A_{k}-A_{k+1})\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\rangle\leqslant A_{0}[f(x_{0})-f^{*}]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{0}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L},\ \forall x^{*}\in\Omega,

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{k}c_{k}-\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{2}\right)\|\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\|^{2}\leqslant A_{0}[f(x_{0})-f^{*}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}_{L}(x_{0},\Omega)^{2}.

(21)

Proof.

Step 1: Estimating the upper bound of
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
. First, we can divide
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
into three parts as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)=

\displaystyle\ A_{k}[f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})]+(A_{k+1}-A_{k})[f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})]

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}.

Next, we estimate the upper bound of first three terms. Since
\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\in\partial f(x_{k+1})
, we have

\displaystyle A_{k}[f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})]

\displaystyle\leqslant A_{k}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle(A_{k+1}-A_{k})[f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})]

\displaystyle\leqslant(A_{k+1}-A_{k})\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Also, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle z_{k+1}-z_{k},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -a_{k}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle+\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{2}\left\|\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{-1}}.

By summing previous inequalities and using the assumption that
A_{k}=a_{k}b_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant

\displaystyle\ a_{k}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),(b_{k}+1)x_{k+1}-b_{k}x_{k}-z_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{2}\left\|\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{-1}}

\displaystyle\ +(A_{k+1}-A_{k}-a_{k})\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left(-a_{k}c_{k}+\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{2}\right)\left\|\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{-1}}

\displaystyle\ +(A_{k+1}-A_{k}-a_{k})\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Step 2: Deriving the convergence rates of Algorithm
2
. Since
c_{k}\geqslant\dfrac{a_{k}}{2}
and
A_{k+1}-A_{k}-a_{k}\geqslant 0
, we have
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant 0
. Then we can obtain the following inequality

A_{k}[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]\leqslant\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0).

By dividing
A_{k}
on both sides of the above inequality and taking infimum respect to all
x^{*}\in\Omega
, we obtain (
19
).

Next, by summing
\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)
from 0 to
\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(a_{k}+A_{k}-A_{k+1})\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}(k)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0),

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(a_{k}c_{k}-\frac{a_{k}^{2}}{2}\right)\left\|\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1})\right\|^{2}_{L^{-1}}\leqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{E}(k)-\mathcal{E}(k+1)\leqslant\mathcal{E}(0).

(23)

(
22
) is (
20
). Next, by taking the infimum over all
x^{*}\in\Omega
on both sides of (
23
), we obtain (
21
).
∎

Theorem 5

Let

•

•

Theorem 6
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
,
\{\tilde{x}_{k+1}\}
, and
\{z_{k}\}
be the sequences generated by Algorithm
2
. Suppose the following conditions are satisfied:

•

A_{k}=a_{k}b_{k}
,

•

there exists a constant
d\in(0,1)
such that
0\leqslant A_{k+1}-A_{k}\leqslant da_{k}
,

•

\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\dfrac{c_{k+1}-c_{k}}{a_{k}}<\infty

•

\beta:=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{k}{b_{k}}\in(1,\infty)
,

•

there exist two positive constants
\gamma_{1}>0.5
and
\gamma_{2}
, and an integer
K
, such that
\gamma_{1}a_{k}\leqslant c_{k}\leqslant\gamma_{2}a_{k}
for all
k\geqslant K
.

Under these conditions, it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}A_{k}[f(x_{k})-f^{*}]=0.

Proof.

Step 1: Introduce an auxiliary sequence. Let
\{\mathcal{G}(k)\}
be the auxiliary sequence defined as

\mathcal{G}(k):=c_{k}[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]+\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\left\langle x_{k}-x^{*},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}.

The difference of
\{\mathcal{G}(k)\}
can be calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(k+1)-\mathcal{G}(k)=

\displaystyle\ (c_{k+1}-c_{k})[f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})]+c_{k}[f(x_{k+1})-f(x_{k})]

\displaystyle\ +\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L}

\displaystyle\ -\left\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},z_{k+1}-z_{k}\right\rangle_{L}-\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\left(b_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L}+a_{k}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +(c_{k+1}-c_{k})[f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})].

Step 2: Consider
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)=\mathcal{E}(k)+\alpha\mathcal{G}(k)
. Let
\alpha
be an arbitrary constant that belongs to
\left(0,\frac{1-d}{1+\sup_{k\geqslant 1}\frac{c_{k+1}-c_{k}}{a_{k}}}\right)
. By previous calculations, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k+1)-\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)\leqslant

\displaystyle\ [A_{k+1}-A_{k}-(1-\alpha)a_{k}]\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\alpha(c_{k+1}-c_{k})[f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})]-\alpha\left(b_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ [A_{k+1}-A_{k}-(1-\alpha)a_{k}+\alpha(c_{k+1}-c_{k})][f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*})]

\displaystyle\ -\alpha\left(b_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ -\alpha\left(b_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|^{2}_{L},\qquad\forall k\geqslant 1.

Thus,
\{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is non-increasing. In addition, since
\alpha<1
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)=(A_{t}+\alpha c_{t})[f(x_{k})-f(x^{*})]+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|z_{k}-x^{*}-\alpha(x_{k}-x^{*})\right\|_{L}^{2}.

(24)

Thus
\{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)\}
is non-negative. The non-increasing property and non-negative of
\{\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)\}
imply that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)
exists.

Step 3: Prove the existence of
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
. First, because the limits of both of
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(k)
and
\mathcal{E}(k)
exists,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\mathcal{G}(k)
exists. Also, because
f(x_{k})-f^{*}\leqslant O(1/A_{k})
and
c_{k}\leqslant\gamma_{2}a_{k}
for all
k\geqslant K
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}-\left\langle x_{k}-x^{*},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}\text{ exists.}

(25)

Also, by (
24
), we have that
\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}_{L}
is uniformly bounded.

Next, we consider the difference of
\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|^{2}
. Direct calculations show that

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}=\left\langle x_{k+1}-x_{k},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|_{L}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{b_{k}}\left\langle z_{k}-x_{k+1},x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}-\frac{c_{k}}{b_{k}}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|_{L}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\left(\frac{1}{b_{k}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}+\frac{1}{b_{k}}\left\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{c_{k}-a_{k}}{b_{k}}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle.

Then we have

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}+\frac{k}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{b_{k}\beta}\right)\left\|x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}+\frac{k}{b_{k}\beta}\left\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{(c_{k}-a_{k})(k-\beta)}{b_{k}\beta}\left\langle\tilde{\nabla}f(x_{k+1}),x_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle-\frac{k-\beta}{2\beta}\left\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\right\|_{L}^{2}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{b_{k}}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}-\frac{1}{b_{k}}\left\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}.

First, because
\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}
is uniformly bounded,
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}-\left\langle x_{k}-x^{*},z_{k}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}
exists, and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}kb_{k}^{-1}\in(1,\infty)
, we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{b_{k}}\left\|x_{k}-x^{*}\right\|_{L}^{2}-\frac{1}{b_{k}}\left\langle x_{k+1}-x^{*},z_{k+1}-x^{*}\right\rangle_{L}=0.

Next, by (
20
) and

5
Discussion

In this paper, we first investigate an ODE system given by (
4
). Utilizing the Lyapunov function technique, we establish that the convergence rate of (
4
) is
O(1/A_{t})
. Subsequently, by introducing additional assumptions and refining the Lyapunov function, we demonstrate the
o(1/A_{t})
convergence rate of (
4
). Building upon these findings, we apply the Symplectic Euler method to (
4
) to derive a novel proximal point algorithm for convex minimization, called the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm. Inspired by the analysis conducted for (
4
), we initially employ a discrete-time Lyapunov function to prove the
O(1/A_{k})
convergence rate of Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm. Additionally, by introducing further assumptions and modifying the Lyapunov function, we are able to prove the
o(1/A_{k})
convergence rate of Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm.

Several valuable research questions remain open. Firstly, the proximal point algorithm has a more generalized form known as the Bregman proximal point algorithm. In
[
40
]
, the authors drew inspiration from the NAG method and proposed a novel accelerated Bregman proximal point algorithm. It would be of interest to develop a new ODE system for the accelerated Bregman proximal point algorithm and employ the Symplectic Euler method to derive a novel accelerated Bregman Proximal Point Algorithm. Furthermore, the optimization subproblems that arise in the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm may not always be solvable exactly. Therefore, developing an inexact variant of the Symplectic Proximal Point Algorithm is essential for practical applications.

Crepitus with reported parasite infection
Authors: Michael H. Sherman, Timothy Gleeson
Published: 2023

PATIENT PRESENTATION

A 40‐year‐old male presented to the emergency department (ED) after self‐inflicted injury to his oropharynx. The physical exam was notable for respiratory distress, blood in the upper airway, and subcutaneous emphysema from his eyebrows to knees. Computed tomography showed extensive subcutaneous emphysema, bilateral pneumothoraces, and pneumomediastinum (See Figures 1, 2, 3, 4)

DIAGNOSIS

Ekbom's delusional parasitosis

The patient developed Ekbom's delusional parasitosis and had attempted to remove “parasites” from his throat with a dental pick. Delusional parasitosis can present an ethical dilemma because patients often do not meet criteria for psychiatric hold until there is evidence or risk of injury to self or others. Patients often present multiple times to various clinical settings and are frequently redirected to further specialty consultation. This patient presented to another ED 2 days prior and was referred to outpatient otolaryngology. Patients are reluctant to believe their symptoms are functional and typically require extensive counseling and teaching along with close outpatient follow‐up for treatment.

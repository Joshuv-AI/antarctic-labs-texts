Pain Relief and Efficacy of Surgical vs Nonsurgical Management of TMJ Disorders: A Systematic Review
Authors: D. Hima Madhuri, Athira Suresh, Parul Tikoo, Kumar Ankit, P. Subham, Aashish Kemmu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTemporomandibular disorders (TMDs) affect the jaw muscles and joints, with treatments ranging from surgery to nonsurgical methods like physiotherapy and medication. A systematic review of studies from January 2020 to May 2024 compared these treatments’ effectiveness in relieving pain and improving function. From 7,459 initial records, 18 studies were included, examining interventions like NSAIDs, low-level laser therapy, botulinum toxin A, and occlusal splints. Results varied, highlighting the need for personalized treatment and standardized outcomes to improve patient care.

INTRODUCTION

Temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) affect 6–12% of adults, predominantly women aged 18–45.[1] The causes are unclear, making diagnosis and treatment challenging. TMDs cause chronic pain and functional issues, impacting quality of life.[2] Treatments include nonsurgical options (education, physiotherapy, medication, splints) and surgical interventions (arthrocentesis, arthroscopy), with surgeries typically reserved for severe cases.[345] Despite advancements, there is no consensus on the best treatment due to varied outcomes, and some cases improve spontaneously.[678] This review compares the strengths and weaknesses of nonsurgical and surgical treatments.

This study evaluates the effectiveness of nonsurgical versus surgical treatments for TMDs in relieving pain and improving function. It also aims to identify research gaps, highlight areas for further study, and provide evidence-based clinical recommendations. The goal is to establish a standardized, efficient approach to managing TMDs, enhancing treatment outcomes and patient quality of life.

REVIEW

The systematic review followed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The methodology was carefully planned and adhered to the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions to ensure rigorous and dependable evaluation of the evidence.

Search strategy

The search strategy for this systematic review on TMJ disorder management was designed to comprehensively identify relevant studies from January 2020 to May 2024. It included a thorough search across multiple databases (PubMed, MEDLINE, Cochrane Library, Scopus) using both MeSH terms and free-text keywords. Terms such as “Temporomandibular Joint Disorders,” “Pain Management,” “Surgical Procedures, Operative,” “Conservative Treatment,” and “Treatment Outcome” were used to cover all aspects of pain relief, efficacy, surgical and nonsurgical interventions, and overall treatment results, ensuring a comprehensive analysis of TMJ disorder treatments.

Selection criteria

This systematic review includes cohort, case–control, and controlled trials involving adults with TMJ disorders, evaluating both surgical and nonsurgical interventions for pain relief and functional improvement. Studies must be comparative, published in English, and meet predefined criteria; case reports and reviews are excluded. The review follows the SPICO framework and uses a four-stage selection process, with two authors achieving substantial agreement (κ = 0.83) on study inclusion.

Data extraction and risk of bias assessment

The primary author extracted and organized data from eligible studies in Excel. The second author independently verified this data for accuracy and completeness. The risk of bias was assessed using ROBANS criteria, with studies rated as high quality if they comprehensively covered all domains, moderate if they addressed two to three factors, and lower if they lacked data on most factors [Table 1].

RESULTS

This review focused on pain relief as a key outcome for TMJ disorder treatments, analyzing 18 studies from an initial search of 7,459 records. It compared the efficacy of surgical and nonsurgical methods, revealing insights into their respective advantages and limitations. The findings underscore the effectiveness of various interventions for reducing pain and improving function across different TMJ conditions.

Yuasa et al.[1] found that NSAIDs and range of motion exercises significantly improved mouth opening and pain for TMJ disk displacement without reduction. Minakuchi et al.[2] reported that physical medicine was more effective than self-care and NSAIDs for disk displacement with reduction. Michelotti et al. and Peroz et al. highlighted the benefits of combined therapies and active PEMF treatment for myogenous and chronic TMJ disorders, respectively.[34] Truelove et al.[5] confirmed substantial short- and long-term pain relief with various splint therapies.

Neuro-reflexotherapy, botulinum toxin A, and low-level laser therapy showed promise in managing TMJ pain and dysfunction.[6789] Mandibular exercises and conservative therapies also led to significant improvements in jaw function and pain reduction.[10] Additionally, studies on glucosamine sulfate, physical therapy, and laser therapy provided valuable insights into TMJ treatment outcomes.

DISCUSSION

This review compares the effectiveness of surgical and nonsurgical treatments for TMJ disorders, covering conditions like disk displacement, myofascial pain, and osteoarthritis. Key findings include NSAIDs and exercises improving pain and mouth opening for disk displacement without reduction, effective self-care and occlusal appliances for disk displacement with reduction, enhanced pain-free jaw opening with combined patient education and home therapy for myogenous TMJ disorders, and significant pain relief and joint mobility improvement with active PEMF treatment for chronic TMJ disorders.[1234]

Truelove et al.[5] reported significant short- and long-term pain relief with various splint therapies for myofascial pain and arthralgia. Conti et al. and Berguer et al. found effective splint designs and neuro-reflexotherapy reduced TMJ pain and improved function.[67] Botulinum toxin A and low-level laser therapy also showed promising results for pain reduction.[89] Conservative treatments like mandibular exercises and physical therapy improved jaw function and reduced pain for TMJ closed lock.

The review provides valuable insights into TMJ treatment effectiveness but has limitations. Variability in study designs, participant demographics, and outcome measures can impact interpretation. Differences in TMJ disorder severity, treatment protocols, and follow-up durations complicate direct comparisons. The focus on pain relief highlights the need for more comprehensive assessments of functional improvements and quality of life. Additionally, publication bias and language restrictions may affect the inclusivity of global research.

CONCLUSION

This review evaluates surgical and nonsurgical treatments for TMJ disorders, emphasizing pain relief. It finds effective results with NSAIDs, physiotherapy, occlusal splints, botulinum toxin A, and low-level laser therapy. Surgical options are effective in certain cases, but nonsurgical methods are preferred for their minimal invasiveness. Future research should focus on standardized measures and long-term follow-ups to improve treatment decisions.

Intelligent decision-making framework for selecting optimized substitution boxes in modern block cipher design
Authors: Majid Khan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The nonlinear confusion component of block ciphers plays a crucial role in data secrecy, and choosing the best substitution box (S-box) is a difficult task since there are various competing cryptographic requirements. These S-boxes have various cryptographic strengths, and thus their comparison constitutes a multi-criteria optimization task. This paper presents a hybrid Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) approach that combines the Evaluation based on Distance from Average Solution (EDAS) technique and Entropy-weighting framework to systematically rank S-boxes. The new model presents a clear, data-oriented evaluation of primary cryptographic parameters such as nonlinearity, strict avalanche criterion, bit independence, differential approximation probability and linear approximation probability. The results emphasize that the S-boxes that have higher linear and differential attack resistance to provide designers with evidence-oriented design directions towards building secure and efficient cipher schemes. The future developments will extend this approach to AI-generated and optimization-algorithm driven S-box designs, furthering research towards efficient cryptographic schemes for digital confidentiality.

Introduction

Extremely fast revolution in modern-day communication has transformed digital knowledge into one of the world’s highly prized possessions and has been disseminating it across the world’s networks at never-before-seen speeds. The security and integrity of an organization in today’s digital world depend directly on its ability to protect sensitive information. Safeguarding of digital information has thus become the world’s biggest challenge in the modern era because huge amounts of information are being stored in national and international depositories in the form of images, audio, video, and other retrievable digital information on multiple public platforms and communications conduits. The confidentiality of the information demands powerful protection procedures, and these depend almost entirely on cryptography.

Cryptography, being the science of hiding information to keep it from being divulged without authority, has long remained at the forefront of securing clandestine communication from secret political and military exchanges to current information infrastructures. Cryptography today relies on sophisticated encryption algorithms and is split on a categorical basis into symmetric (private-key) and asymmetric (public-key) systems. In the case of symmetric cryptography, stream ciphers encrypt a flow of plaintext bits sequentially, one after another. In contrast, block ciphers process a block of plaintext of a fixed size. The latter predominate overwhelmingly for today’s applications and are typified by the substitution box (S-box) as the nonlinear key component. The S-box is to be held responsible for causing confusion between plaintext and ciphertext. A block cipher’s strength to a large extent rests on the cryptography of S-boxes used. For an S-box to be resistant to both linear and differential cryptanalysis and attain the desired degree of confusion in the relationship between the plaintext and ciphertext desired for a user of a cryptogram or a systems builder, S-boxes must be constructed to exhibit strong nonlinear action. Due to the above-mentioned considerations, the design and analysis of strong S-boxes remain a long-standing challenge in cryptography and information security1–3. The different schemes were suggested to develop a nonlinear block cipher confusion component, such as heuristic strategy, advance with the control mapping, analytical techniques, algebraic structures, and cellar automates. In recent times, numerous researchers have combined metaheuristic optimization algorithms and chaotic maps to develop S-boxes of formidable strength4–6.

Optimization has become a central methodology of science, engineering, and applied mathematics because it systematically reveals the optimal or near-optimal solution for the case of multifarious time, monetary, and resource constraints. In cryptography, for whose primitive design of securely designed substitution boxes (S-boxes), for example, numerous mutually conflicting needs (e.g., to provide maximum nonlinearity, avalanche effect, and bit independence and minimum differential and linear probabilities) have to be met, optimization proves invaluable. Optimization’s versatile range of techniques from evolutionary techniques to swarm intelligence and nature-inspired metaheuristics allows adaptable scaffolding for navigating the unparalleled and discrete solution spaces amenable to approaches that are algebraic or deterministic. These techniques, exploiting stochastic exploration to adaptive learning, are well adapted to applications in cryptography for whose purposes strength against cryptanalytic attack implies multi-object balancing.

A variety of optimization-aided algorithms for therefore building cryptographically secure substitution boxes (S-boxes) have thus far drawn inspiration from nature and/or societal processes to balance exploration and exploitation of highly nonlinear search spaces. The Cuckoo Search Optimization (CSO) employs Lévy flight and parasitic reproduction schemes to create heterogeneous candidate mappings7,8, whereas Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) abstracts swarm intelligence to update particle velocities and positions to optimize cryptographic fitness9. Likewise, Ant Colony Optimization (ANO) leverages pheromone trail exploitation to skew search in favor of stronger S-structure10, and Teaching–Learning-Based Optimization (TLBO) augments solutions via a teacher ladder scheme requiring no algorithm-specific control parameters11,12. Moreover, the Artificial Bee Colony Optimization (ABCO) integrates employed, onlooker, and scout bees to develop mappings with higher nonlinearity and differential uniformity13,14, whereas Genetic Algorithms (GA) leverage crossover, mutation, and selection to model evolutionary adaptation of Boolean functions15. Recently, the Firefly Algorithm (FAO), motivated from bioluminescent communications, employs attractiveness varying linearly with brightness and distance to polish candidate solutions16. Each optimization algorithm brings to the table unique heuristic operators, when applied to S-box construction, they give rise to understudy alternatives of variable cryptographic strengths, amenable to systematic scrutiny and ranking via the proposed hybrid MCDM framework to yield the strongest and most robust choice17,18. Multi-criteria decision making (MCDM) has become an invaluable aid to science, engineering, and applied mathematics because real-world problems almost invariably involve not a single but multiple evaluation factors19–21. Instead, choices are forced to balance numerous, often mutually conflicting criteria under uncertainty, insufficient means, and burdensome trade-offs. MCDM lends a systematic and quantitative framework to diverse criteria back to a single appraisal to facilitate transparent, replicable, and rational choice. It has traversed diverse areas including healthcare, energy planning, supply chain management, and financial risk analysis and testified its universality to unravel multi-parameter optimization problems22–24.

The secure cryptography system design poses just this kind of issue. A substitution box (S-box), the block cipher’s nonlinear confusion component, needs to meet various cryptographic needs of high nonlinearity, strict avalanche criterion, full-strength bit independence, low differential and linear approximation probability. Each property registers immunity to a different set of cryptanalytic attack classes, and there exists no single construction mechanism to ensure dominance across all measures. In addition, numerous construction techniques, algebraic mappings, chaos systems, evolutionary algorithms, optimization heuristics, and graph-theoretic techniques yield an abundance of candidate S-boxes, each of whose superiority is outstanding in a few measures and substandard in others. This results in a multi-criteria selection issue wherein manual single-parameter comparison or single-parameter comparison is not sufficient. The MCDM addresses this issue by treating S-box selection as a multi-criteria optimization issue. The process entails:i.Constructing a decision matrix wherein rows are candidate S-boxes, and the columns represent the cryptography performance parameters.ii.Assigns weights expressing the relative importance of each criterion using expert knowledge (subjective), data-driven study (objective), or combined schemes.iii.Using an MCDM ranking operator to produce a composite evaluation score for each candidate.

The S-box with the highest score is considered to be the best. In so doing, the MCDM allows for a transparent and evidence-based selection mechanism wherein the selected S-box has balanced cryptography strength, aligns well with security needs, and is accountable within a reproducible mathematical framework.

The main aim of this work is to discover the best substitution box for several understudy optimization-based algorithms. Many optimization algorithms have emerged for producing the substitution box of high nonlinearity recently. In this paper, we make a classification of the best optimization algorithm for producing the S-box by applying EDAS method and entropy based objective weighting criterion25,26. Under this hybrid decision making method the multi-objective issue becomes an efficient machine for the choice of the best nonlinear confusion component of recent cipher techniques.

Multi-criteria decision making (MCDM)

Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) is a formal framework for solving multi-criteria problems, where numerous, sometimes competing, objectives must be satisfied. In contrast to single-objective optimization, MCDM offers a rational approach to comparing alternatives based on both quantitative performance measures and decision-makers’ preferences. MCDM is used extensively in science, engineering, economics, and information technologies, where multi-criteria trade-offs are inexorable. Formally casting evaluation as a mathematical decision process, MCDM offers transparent, reproducible, and rational support to rational decision-making in important applications as diverse as choosing renewable energy sources, designing healthcare systems, designing cryptographic components, and ensuring national security. Value/utility-based techniques

These methods aggregate criterion values into a single performance index using weighted mathematical formulations. They assume full compensability, meaning poor performance on one criterion can be offset by high performance on another. The most important value-based MCDM methods are weighted sum model (WSM) and weighted product model (WPM).

Outranking techniques

These techniques build pairwise preference relationships between alternative choices, based on the ideas of concordance (criteria favoring preference of an alternative over that of other) and discordance (criteria disfavoring it). They also presume that the decision-making process is non-compensatory, i.e., any significant weakness on one criterion cannot be fully compensated for by strengths elsewhere. The assignment of criterion weights is fundamental to MCDM, as it directly influences the prioritization of alternatives. There are fundamentally three categories of weights methods:Subjective techniquesObjective techniquesHybrid techniques

Hybrid techniques

Subjective methodologies, i.e., the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), Delphi, and SWARA, use expert opinion to store human preference as numerical weights. Objective methodologies like entropy, CRITIC, and variance methods directly pull weights from the data structure, stressing those criteria that hold greater information or contrast. Hybrid methodologies combine subjective and objective schemes via convex combinations, so there is a balancing between expert-pulled insight and data-pulled verity. This weighting taxonomy assures that MCDM be adaptable as well as versatile, amenable to varying decision environments, where there could be both knowledge-based as well as evidence-based blending during the decision-making process22,23.

Subjective weighting techniques

Subjective weights originate from expert judgments and are modeled as transformations from preference spaces into the probability simplex. Objective weighting techniques

Objective weights emerge from the intrinsic properties of the decision matrix, ensuring that variability and information richness are captured mathematically. The objective methods ensure weights arise from data structure, maximizing fairness and minimizing subjectivity. The most used objective weighting methods are Entropy, CRITIC, Variance, Standard Deviation, and PCA.

Hybrid weighting techniques

Hybrid methods integrate subjective and objective schemes through convex tensorial transformations, ensuring a balance between expert judgments and data variability. Let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${w}^{S}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\mathcal{E}\right), {w}^{O}={\mathcal{W}}_{\mathcal{O}}\left(D\right)$$\end{document}. The hybrid weights offer robustness by balancing domain expertise with data-derived structure, especially useful in multi-stakeholder and uncertain decision environments.

This study suggests a combination mechanism that combines Entropy-based weighting, together with EDAS (Evaluation based on Distance from Average Solution). It is framed through the use of algebraic mappings, transformational operators, as well as tensorial formulations, so that there is transparent as well as rigorous evaluation. Understudy S-boxes based on optimization techniques

Optimization has become universal science, engineering, and applied mathematical methodology because it can methodically uncover optimum or close-to-optimum solutions subject to multiple time, cost, and resource constraints. In cryptography, where the construction of secure primitives like substitution boxes (S-boxes) requires simultaneously fulfilling many conflicting demands (like nonlinear maximization, avalanche effect, bit independence, and differential/linear probability minimization) no longer becomes a deterrent, optimization becomes invaluable. The broad range of optimization methodologies—that vary all the way from evolutionary algorithms up to swarm intelligence and nature-inspired metaheuristics is offering versatile paradigmatic, exploring otherwise unthinkable vast and discrete spaces of solutions, that are too complex to be handled by purely algebraic or deterministic methodologies. Such methodologies, using stochastic exploration as well as the adaptive acquisition of the learnings, are ideally suited to applications where the construction of cryptography secure against cryptanalytic attacks demands multi-objective balancing. Numerous optimization-based algorithms have as a result been used to build cryptographically secure substitution boxes (S-boxes), each borrowing inspiration from natural or societal processes to balance exploration and exploitation in very nonlinear search spaces. While the Cuckoo Search Optimization (CSO) employs the use of Lévy flights as well as parasitic reproduction mechanisms to produce varying candidate mappings, the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) simulates swarm intelligence through the adjustment of particle velocities along with particle positions to optimize cryptographic fitness. Likewise, the Ant Colony Optimization (ANO) utilizes the exploitation of pheromone trails to influence the search to be pushed toward stronger S-box constructs, as the Teaching–Learning-Based Optimization (TLBO) optimizes solutions through a teacher–learner paradigm that needs no algorithm-specific control parameters. Moreover, the Artificial Bee Colony Optimization (ABCO) utilizes employed, onlooker, as well as scout bees to improve mappings that exhibit enhanced nonlinearity as well as differential uniformity, as the Genetic Algorithms (GA) utilize the use of crossover, mutation, as well as selection to mimic evolutionary adaptation during Boolean functions. More recently, the Firefly Algorithm (FAO), based on biological luminescent communication, utilizes the proportionality of attraction to brightness as well as distance to improve candidate solutions. Each among these optimization methodologies gives specific heuristic operators that, upon being used to build S-boxes, produce understudy alternatives that exhibit varying cryptographic strengths, Sensitivity analysis

The last section demonstrated the stability and robustness of the Hybrid EDAS–Entropy result (with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${S}_{2}$$\end{document} as best) by crossing validating against TOPSIS and SAW under a shared weight vector. A preference is genuinely stable only if it is also stable as the criterion-importance model varies. To this effect, we conduct a systematic sensitivity analysis where four objective weighting schemes namely entropy, standard deviation, CRITIC, and Mean are each paired with three MCDM matrices namely EDAS, TOPSIS, and SAW while keeping the decision matrix constant. Stabililty of the best choice across the pairings gives strong support to the conclusion that the result does not reflect methodological idiosyncrasy, but rather, data-based structure.

The Hybrid EDAS-Entropy method is established as a stable and consistent multi-criteria cryptographic component selector so that the best selections are always unchanged regardless of varying analysis situations.

Conclusion

The application of Multi-Criterion Decision Making (MCDM) methods has been effective in many fields to determine the best solution to problems that are complex. This paper analyses several existing optimization-based schemes to create substitution boxes (S-boxes), the nonlinear core blocks used in the current construction of block ciphers. Employing the Evaluation based on Distance from Average Solution (EDAS) technique, an improved extension of MCDM, the best optimization algorithm to construct an S-box is chosen. It is chosen based on key cryptographic properties, i.e., nonlinearity, Strict Avalanche Criterion (SAC), and Bit Independence Criterion (BIC) both for nonlinearity and SAC, differential probability (DP), and linear probability (LP). It is observed that the substitution box designed with Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is the best performing in the considered criteria. Incorporating the addition of the hybrid MCDM models, such as fuzzy-EDAS or weighted-entropy ones, as the future work can provide an increased improvement in the degree of robustness of the selection process. The exploration on the MCDM-based use of other nature-inspired as well as machine learning-based optimisation techniques to be used to construct S-boxes could help offer the additional impact on the current cryptographic security strength of the post-quantum as well as the lightweight cryptosystems as well.

Modulus of continuity of Monge–Ampère potentials in big cohomology classes
Quang-Tuan Dang, Hoang-Son Do, Hoang Hiep Pham

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a uniform estimate for the modulus of continuity of solutions to the degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equation in big cohomology classes. This improves the previous work of Di Nezza–Lu and the first author.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
32U15, 32W20, 32Q15

1.
Introduction

The study of complex Monge-Ampère equations on compact Kähler manifolds has attracted considerable interest since Yau’s resolution of the Calabi conjecture. In connection with the Minimal Model Program, the search for singular Kähler–Einstein metrics
leads to the study of degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equations; see
[
21
,
3
,
27
]
and references therein.

Guedj and Zeriahi
[
26
]
developed the first step of the study of the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère measure and solved degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equations with rather general measures on the right-hand side. Their approach was later extended to the setting of big cohomology classes by Boucksom, Eyssidieux, Guedj, and Zeriahi
[
4
]
. Since then, when the right-hand side belongs to
L^{p}
for some
p>1
, the Hölder continuity of solutions to degenerate complex Monge–Ampère equations has been intensively studied by many authors
[
34
,
35
,
32
,
22
,
4
,
9
,
19
,
6
,
18
]
.
The modulus of continuity of the solution plays a crucial role since it is closely related to the geometric properties of the corresponding Kähler metrics, such as the uniform bounds for diameter of metrics and Gromov–Hausdorff convergence; see
[
23
,
31
,
28
,
30
,
29
,
24
,
39
,
20
,
25
,
40
,
36
]
.

The primary goal of this paper is to study the modulus of continuity of solutions to complex Monge–Ampère equations when the right-hand side is not integrable.
To state our result, we first introduce some notation and terminology. Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
equipped with a Kähler form
\omega_{X}
. We let
d
,
d^{c}
denote the real differential operators on
X
defined by
d:=\partial+\bar{\partial}
,
d^{c}:=\frac{i}{2}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
so that
dd^{c}=i\partial\bar{\partial}
.
Fix a closed real smooth (1,1)-form
\theta
representing a big cohomology class. Let
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
denote the set of all
\theta
-psh functions.
We say that the cohomology class
\{\theta\}
is
big
if the set
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X})\neq\varnothing
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\textrm{Amp}(\theta)
denote the
ample locus
of
\theta
which is roughly speaking the largest Zariski open subset where
\{\theta\}
locally behaves like
a Kähler class.

We are interested in studying the regularity of solutions to the complex Monge–Ampère equation type
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
satisfying

(1.1)

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=\nu,\quad\sup_{X}u=0,

where

\nu
is a positive measure on
X
that puts no mass on pluripolar subsets and satisfies the compatibility condition
\nu(X)={\rm Vol}(\theta)
,
u
is the unknown
\theta
-function, and the left-hand side of (
1.1
) denotes the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère product
[
1
,
4
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of
\omega_{X}
-psh functions
u
normalized by
\int_{X}u\omega_{X}^{n}=0
. This is a
convex compact set in the
L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
topology. Define the following distance on
\mathcal{C}

\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(u,v)\coloneqq\|u-v\|_{L^{1}},

where the
L^{1}
-norm is with respect to the measure
\omega_{X}^{n}
.
We say that a measure
\mu
is
Hölder continuous

with Hölder constant
B
and Hölder exponent
0<\beta\leq 1

with respect to
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits_{L^{1}}
on
\mathcal{C}
if for any
u,v\in\mathcal{C}

\int_{X}|u-v|\omega_{X}^{n}\leq B\|u-v\|_{L^{1}}^{\beta}.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
and
\theta
a smooth closed (1,1) form whose cohomology class is big.
Let
\mu
be a Hölder continuous measure
with Hölder constant
B
and Hölder exponent
0<\beta\leq 1

with respect to
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits_{L^{1}}
on
\mathcal{C}
.
Assume that
\psi
is a quasi-psh function
on
X
satisfying
\int_{X}e^{-\psi}d\mu={\rm Vol}(\theta)
and
\omega_{X}+a_{0}dd^{c}\psi\geq 0
,
with
a_{0}>0
. Suppose that

u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
is a solution to the following equation

(1.2)

(\theta+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{-\psi}\mu,\quad\sup_{X}u=0.

Then for each
U\Subset{\rm Amp}(\theta)\backslash\{\psi=-\infty\}
, there exists a continuous function
F_{U}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

with
F(0)=0
such that

|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|\leq F_{U}(\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(z_{1},z_{2})),

for every
z_{1},z_{2}\in U
. Moreover, the choice of
F_{U}
depends only on
X
,
U
,
\omega_{X}
,
n
,
\theta
,
a_{0}
,
B
,
\beta
,
\sup_{U}(-\psi)
and
an upper bound function for
H(a)=\int_{X}e^{{2(V_{\theta}-u)}/{a}}d\mu
.

Here we notice that
e^{{2(V_{\theta}-u)}/{a}}\in L^{1}(\mu)
for every
a>0
by Lemma
2.8
. In the case
\theta=\omega_{X}
,
we have the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
.
Let
\mu
be a Hölder continuous measure
with Hölder constant
B
and Hölder exponent
0<\beta\leq 1

with respect to
dist_{L^{1}}
on
\mathcal{C}
.
Assume that
\psi
is a quasi-psh function
on
X
satisfying
\int_{X}e^{-\psi}d\mu={\rm Vol}(\theta)
,
\omega_{X}+a_{0}dd^{c}\psi\geq 0
and

(1.3)

\int_{X}h(-\psi)e^{-\psi}\mu\leq C_{0},

where
a_{0},C_{0}>0
are constants and
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is an increasing,
concave function with
h(\infty)=\infty
. Suppose that

u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
is a solution to the following equation

(1.4)

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)^{n}=e^{-\psi}\mu,\quad\sup_{X}u=0.

Then for each
U\Subset X\backslash\{\psi=-\infty\}
, there exists a continuous function
F_{U}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

with
F(0)=0
such that

|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|\leq F_{U}(\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(z_{1},z_{2})),

for every
z_{1},z_{2}\in U
. Moreover, the choice of
F_{U}
depends only on
X
,
U
,
\omega_{X}
,
n
,
\theta
,
a_{0}
,
B
,
\beta
,
\sup_{U}(-\psi)
,
C_{0}
,
and
h
.

Let us emphasize that the continuity of the solution
u
to equation (
1.4
) was established by Di Nezza and Lu
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
. The contribution of the corollary above is to establish the equicontinuity of the family of solutions corresponding to pairs
(\mu,\psi)
which satisfy the condition (
1.3
), given
h
and
C_{0}
. We also note that for each pair
(\mu,\psi)
with
e^{-\psi}\in L^{1}(X,\mu)
, there always exists a pair
(h,C_{0})
such that the condition (
1.3
) holds; cf. Remark
2.11
.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we let
X
denote a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with a Kähler form
\omega_{X}
.
We denote by
dV_{\omega_{X}}:={\omega_{X}^{n}}

the volume form associated with
\omega_{X}
. For any measure
\mu
on
X
, we write
L^{1}(\mu)
for
L^{1}(X,d\mu)
.

2.1.
Quasi-plurisubharmonic functions

Recall that an upper semi-continuous function
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

is called
quasi-plurisubharmonic
(
quasi-psh
for short) if it is locally the sum of a smooth and a plurisubharmonic (psh for short) function.

We say that
\varphi
is
\theta
-plurisubharmonic
(
\theta
-psh
for short) if it is quasi-psh, and

\theta_{\varphi}:=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0

in the sense of currents, where
{\rm d}=\partial+\bar{\partial}
and
{\rm d}^{c}=\frac{i}{2\pi}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
so that
dd^{c}=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}
.
We let
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
denote the set of all
\theta
-psh functions, which are not identically
-\infty
. This set is endowed with
the weak topology, which coincides with
the
L^{1}
-topology. By Hartogs’ lemma,
\varphi\mapsto\sup_{X}\varphi
is continuous in this weak topology,
it follows that the set of
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, with
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
is compact.
We refer the reader to
[
13
,
27
]
for more properties of
\theta
-psh functions.

The cohomology class
\{\theta\}
is said to be
big
if the set
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta-\varepsilon\omega_{X})
is not empty for some
\varepsilon>0
. We now assume that
\{\theta\}
is big unless otherwise specified.
By Demailly’s regularization theorem
[
11
]
, we can find a closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T_{0}\in\{\theta\}
such that

T_{0}=\theta+dd^{c}\Psi_{0}\geq\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{X},

for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, where
\Psi_{0}
is a quasi-psh function with
analytic singularities
, i.e., locally

\Psi_{0}=c\log\left[\sum_{j=1}^{N}|f_{j}|^{2}\right]+g,

where the
f_{j}
’s are holomorphic functions and
g
is bounded. Such a current
T_{0}
is then smooth on a Zariski open subset
X\setminus\{\Psi_{0}=-\infty\}
. We thus define the
ample locus

\textrm{Amp}(\theta)
of
\theta
as the largest such Zariski open subset (which exists by the Noetherian property of closed analytic subsets; cf.
[
5
]
).

Given
\varphi,\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
, we say that
\varphi
is
less singular
than
\psi
, and denote by
\psi\preceq\varphi
, if there exists a constant
C
such that
\psi\leq\varphi+C
on
X
. We say that
\varphi,\psi
have the
same singularity type
, and denote by
\varphi\simeq\psi
if
\varphi\preceq\psi
and
\psi\preceq\varphi
.
There is a
natural least singular potential in
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
given by

\displaystyle V_{\theta}:=\sup\{\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):\varphi\leq 0\}.

A function
\varphi
is said to have
minimal singularities
if it has the same singularity type as
V_{\theta}
. In particular,
V_{\theta}=0
if
\theta
is semi-positive.
We see that
V_{\theta}
is locally bounded in the ample locus.

2.2.
Demailly’s regularization

We consider the exponential mapping
\exp_{x}:T_{x}X\ni\zeta\to X
as follows:
\exp_{x}(\zeta)=\gamma(1)
where
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
is the geodesic starting from
x=\gamma(0)
with the initial velocity
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=\zeta
. In the Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the exponential map
\exp_{x}(\zeta)=x+\zeta
.

Following
[
10
,
12
]
, for a quasi-psh function
u
,
we define its
\delta
-regularization

\rho_{\delta}u=\Psi(u)(z,\delta)
where

(2.1)

\Psi(u)(z,w)=\int_{\zeta\in T_{z}X}u\big(\exp_{z}(w\zeta)\big)\chi\big({|\zeta|^{2}_{\omega_{X}}}\big)\,dV_{\omega_{X}}(\zeta),\ \delta>0,

where
\chi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is defined by

\chi(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\eta}{(1-t)^{2}}\exp(\frac{1}{t-1})&\ {\rm if}\ 0\leq t\leq 1,\\
0&\ {\rm if}\ t>1,\end{cases}

with a suitable constant
\eta
such that

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\chi(\|z\|^{2})\,dV(z)=1
, where
dV
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The
\delta
-regularization
\rho_{\delta}u
can be written by

\rho_{\delta}u(z)=\frac{1}{\delta^{2n}}\int_{\zeta\in T_{z}X}u\big(\exp_{z}(\zeta)\big)\chi\bigg(\frac{|\zeta|^{2}_{\omega_{X}}}{\delta^{2}}\bigg)\,dV_{\omega_{X}}(\zeta).

Intuitively, this corresponds to the familiar convolution with smoothing kernels. Actually, in the case of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
endowed with the Euclidean metric, this is exactly the smoothing convolution; see

[
12
, Remark 4.6]
.

The following lemma is a combination of
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
2
, Lemma 1.12]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u
be a
\theta
-psh function and define the Kiselman-Legendre transform with level
c
by

(2.2)

\Phi_{c,\delta}\coloneqq\inf_{0<t\leq\delta}\Big[\rho_{t}u(z)+K(t^{2}-\delta^{2})+K(t-\delta)-c\log\Big(\frac{t}{\delta}\Big)\Big].

Then for some positive constants
0<\delta_{0}<1
and
K>1
depending on the curvature tensor of
\omega_{X}
on
X
,
the function
\rho_{t}u(z)+Kt^{2}
is increasing in
t\in(0,\delta_{0}]
and
one has the following estimate for the complex Hessian:

(2.3)

\theta+dd^{c}\Phi_{c,\delta}\geq-(Ac+K^{2}\delta)\,\omega_{X},

where
A
is a lower bound of the negative part of the bisectional curvature of
\omega_{X}
.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of
[
33
, Lemma 4.1]
, which is still valid without the boundedness of
u
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The above lemma is essential for our study of the modulus of continuity of solutions to complex Monge–Ampère equations. We follow the same strategy as in the proof of
[
9
, Theorem D]
.
However, the function
\Phi_{c,\delta}
differs from the one used there by the addition of the term
K(t-\delta)
, as introduced in
[
12
, Remark 4.7]
. This extra term is needed to handle the mixed term
|dz||dw|
that arises in the estimates; cf.
[
33
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
.
If

\rho_{\delta}u
is the regularization of

u
defined as in (
2.1
) then for
\delta
small enough we have

\int_{X}\frac{\rho_{\delta}u-u}{\delta^{2}}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{1}(X,dV_{X})},

where
C
only depends on
n
,
X
,
\omega_{X}
.

Proof.

The proof follows verbatim from that of
[
9
, Lemma 2.3]
.
∎

2.3.
Non-pluripolar product

Let
\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{n}
be closed smooth real (1,1) form representing big cohomology classes, and
\varphi_{j}\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta^{j})
. Following the construction of Bedford–Taylor
[
1
]
, it has been shown in
[
4
]
that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbf{1}_{\cap_{j}\{\varphi_{j}>V_{\theta^{j}}-k\}}(\theta^{1}+dd^{c}{\max(\varphi_{1},V_{\theta^{1}}-k)})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\theta^{n}+dd^{c}{\max(\varphi_{n},V_{\theta^{n}}-k)})

is well-defined as a Borel positive measure with finite total mass. The sequence of these measures is non-decreasing in
k
and it converges weakly to the so-called
Monge–Ampère product
, denoted by

(\theta^{1}+dd^{c}{\varphi_{1}})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\theta^{n}+dd^{c}{\varphi_{n}}),

which does not charge pluripolar sets by definition. In particular,
\theta^{1}=\cdots=\theta^{n}=\theta
and
\varphi_{1}=\cdots=\varphi_{n}
we obtain the non-pluripolar
Monge–Ampère measure of
\varphi
, denoted by
(\theta+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}
or simply by
\theta_{\varphi}^{n}
.

The
volume
of a big cohomology class
\{\theta\}
is given by the total mass of the non-pluripolar Monge–Ampère
measure of
V_{\theta}
, i.e.,

{\rm{\rm Vol}}(\theta):=\int_{X}\theta_{V_{\theta}}^{n}.

We say that
\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
has
full Monge–Ampère mass
if
\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}={\rm Vol}(\theta)
. We let

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X,\theta):=\left\{\varphi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):\int_{X}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}={\rm Vol}(\theta)\right\}

denote the set of
\theta
-psh functions with full Monge–Ampère mass.
Note that
\theta
-psh functions with minimal singularities have full Monge–Ampère mass (see
[
4
, Theorem 1.16]
for more details), but the converse is not true.

Given a measurable function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the
\theta
-psh envelope
of
h
by

P_{\theta}(f):=(\sup\{u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):u\leq f\;\text{on}\,X\})^{*},

where the star means we take the upper semi-continuous regularization.

Given a
\theta
-psh function
\phi
,
Ross and Witt-Nyström
[
37
]
introduced the “rooftop envelope” as follows

P_{\theta}[\phi](f)=\left(\lim_{C\to+\infty}P_{\theta}(\min(\phi+C,f))\right)^{*}.

When
f=0
we simply write
P_{\theta}[\phi]
.
A function
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
is called a
model potential
if

\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\phi=P_{\theta}[\phi].

We recall the notion of Monge–Ampère capacity introduced in
[
15
,
14
]
.
For
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
and a Borel subset
E\subset X
, the
relative Monge–Ampère capacity
is defined by

\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}(E):=\sup\left\{\int_{E}\theta_{u}^{n}:u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta),\;\phi-1\leq u\leq\phi\right\}.

This definition recovers the Monge–Ampère capacity used in
[
4
]
when
\phi=V_{\theta}
. In that case, we denote it simply by
\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\theta}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A family of positive measures
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in I}
on
X
is said to be uniformly absolutely
continuous with respect to
\phi
-capacity if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for each Borel subset
E\subset X
satisfying
\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}(E)<\delta
the inequality
\mu_{i}(E)<\varepsilon
holds for all
i\in I
. We denote this by
\mu_{i}<<\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}
. In particular, all such measures must
vanish on pluripolar sets.

2.4.
Hölder continuous measures

Definition 2.5
.

A positive measure
\mu
on
X
is said to be
{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
-Hölder continuous
(or, for short, Hölder continuous) if there exist
B>0
and
0<\beta\leq 1
such that

\int_{X}(u-v)d\mu\leq B\left(\int_{X}|u-v|\omega_{X}^{n}\right)^{\beta},

for every
u,v\in\mathcal{C}:=\{w\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta):\int_{X}w\omega_{X}^{n}=0\}.
In this case, we say that
B
is
the Hölder constant and

\beta
is the Hölder exponent of
\mu
with respect to
{\rm dist}_{L^{1}}
on
\mathcal{C}
. We let
\mathcal{M}(B,\beta)
denote the set of such measures.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
19
, Lemma 3.3]
)
.

Assume
\mu
is a Hölder continuous measure
with Hölder constant B and Hölder exponent
0<\beta\leq 1

with respect to
\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits_{L^{1}}
on
\mathcal{C}
. Then there exists
C>0
depending only on
B
such that

\|u-v\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\leq C\max\big\{\|u-v\|_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})},\|u-v\|_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})}^{\beta}\big\},

for all
u,v\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
. In particular, if a family
\mathcal{F}
of
\theta
-psh functions
is a relatively compact subset of
L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})
then there exists
C_{\mathcal{F}}>0
such that

\|u-v\|_{L^{1}(\mu)}\leq C_{\mathcal{F}}\|u-v\|_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})}^{\beta},

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{F}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mu
be a Hölder continuous measure on
X

with Höler constant
B
and Hölder exponent
0<\beta\leq 1
with respect to
{\rm dist}_{L^{1}}
on
\mathcal{C}
. Assume that

u,v
are
\theta
-psh functions satisfying

\int_{X}e^{m(u-v)}\omega_{X}^{n}<C_{0},

for positive constants
m
,
C_{0}
. Then, for every
0<\gamma<\beta
, there exists
C>0
depending only on
C_{0},m,B,\beta
and
\gamma
such that

\int_{X}e^{\gamma m(u-v)}d\mu<C.

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 4.4]
.
Denote
w=v-u
. We have

(2.4)

\omega_{X}^{n}(\{w<-M\})\leq\int_{X}e^{-m(w+M)}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C_{0}e^{-mM},

for every
M>0
. Denote

w_{M}=\max\{w,-M\}=\max\{v,u-M\}-u.

We have
w_{M}-w=\max\{v,u-M\}-v
is the difference of two
\theta
-psh functions.
By Lemma
2.6
, there exists
C_{1}>0
depending only on
B
such that

(2.5)

\int_{X}(w_{M}-w)d\mu\leq C_{1}\max\left\{\|w_{M}-w\|_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})},\|w_{M}-w\|_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})}^{\beta}\right\}.

Moreover, we also have

(2.6)

\mu(\{w<-M-1\})\leq\int_{X}(w_{M}-w)d\mu,

and by (
2.4
),

(2.7)

\begin{split}\int_{X}(w_{M}-w)\omega_{X}^{n}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\omega_{X}^{n}(\{w<-M-t\})dt\\
&\leq C_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-m(M+t)}dt=\dfrac{C_{0}e^{-mM}}{m}.\end{split}

Combining (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we get

\mu(\{w<-M-1\})\leq C_{1}\max\left\{\dfrac{C_{0}e^{-mM}}{m},\left(\dfrac{C_{0}e^{-mM}}{m}\right)^{\beta}\right\}\leq C_{2}\cdot e^{-\beta mM},

where
C_{2}=C_{1}\max\{\frac{C_{0}}{m},(\frac{C_{0}}{m})^{\beta}\}
. Then, for every
0<\gamma<\beta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X}e^{-\gamma mw}d\mu-\mu(X)

\displaystyle=\gamma m\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(w<-t)e^{\gamma mt}dt

\displaystyle\leq\gamma mC_{2}e^{\beta m}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(-\beta+\gamma)mt}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{2}\gamma e^{\beta m}}{\beta-\gamma}.

∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(B,\beta)
be a Hölder continuous measure on
X

and
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\theta)
.
Then

\int_{X}e^{m(V_{\theta}-u)}d\mu<+\infty,

for every
m>0
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
6
, Proposition 2.10]
that for any
b>0
,
P_{\omega_{X}}(b(u-V_{\theta}))\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
. We remark here that
u-V_{\theta}
is well-defined outside a pluripolar set.
In particular, for

, we have

\int_{X}e^{m\gamma^{-1}(V_{\theta}-u)}\omega_{X}^{n}\leq\int_{X}e^{-m\gamma^{-1}P_{\omega_{X}}(u-V_{\theta})}\omega_{X}^{n}<+\infty,

as follows from Skoda’s integrability theorem; cf.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\phi\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be a model potential with
\int_{X}\theta_{\phi}^{n}>\varrho>0
.
Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(B,\beta)
.
Then, there exist constants

0<\gamma<1
and
C>0
depending only on
X,\omega_{X},\theta,B
and
\beta
such that

(2.8)

\mu(E)\leq C\,\exp\left(-\gamma\left(\dfrac{\varrho}{\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right),

for all Borel sets
E\subset X
.

Proof.

The case when
\theta=\omega_{X}
and
\phi=0
was shown in
[
19
, Propositions 2.4, 4.4]
. For the relative case,
the proof relies on the arguments in
[
7
, Proposition 4.30]
, so we sketch it here.
According to
[
7
, Section 4A2]
, for any Borel set
E
, we define the global
\phi
-extremal function of
(E,\theta,\phi)
by

V_{E,\phi}=\sup\{u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta,\phi):u\leq\phi\;\text{on}\,E\}.

Set
M_{\phi}(E)=\sup_{X}V^{*}_{E,\phi}
, we may assume
M_{\phi}(E)\geq 1
. Thanks to
[
7
, Lemma 4.9]
, we have the Alexander–Taylor type inequality

\left(\frac{\varrho}{\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}(E)}\right)^{1/n}\leq M_{\phi}(E),

hence,

\exp(-M_{\phi}(E))\leq\exp\left(-\left(\frac{\varrho}{\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right).

As follows from
[
19
, Proposition 4.4]
,
\mu
is weakly moderate, i.e. there are constants
\alpha=\alpha(\beta)>0
and
C=C(B,\beta)>0
such that
\int_{X}\exp(-\alpha\varphi)d\mu\leq C
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}
. We apply this to
V_{E,\phi}^{*}-\int_{X}V_{E,\phi}^{*}\omega_{X}^{n}
to obtain

\int_{X}\exp(-\alpha V_{E,\phi}^{*})d\mu\leq C\cdot\exp\bigg(-\alpha\int_{X}V_{E,\phi}^{*}\omega_{X}^{n}\bigg).

Since
V_{E,\phi}^{*}\leq 0
on
E
a.e. and
\int_{X}V_{E,\phi}^{*}{\omega_{X}}^{n}\geq M_{\phi}(E)-C_{\omega_{X}}
(see, e.g.,
[
27
, Proposition 8.5]
) we have

\mu(E)\leq C\cdot\exp\left(-\gamma\left(\frac{\varrho}{\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\phi}(E)}\right)^{1/n}\right),

for
\gamma>0
.
∎

Proposition 2.10
.

Fix
a_{0}>0
,
C_{0}>0
and
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is an increasing concave function with
h(\infty)=\infty
.
Let
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(B,\beta,a_{0},C_{0},h)
be the set of probability measures
\nu
on
X
such that
\nu=e^{-\psi}\mu
with
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(B,\beta)
,
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,a_{0}\omega_{X})
and

(2.9)

\int_{X}h(-\psi)e^{-\psi}{\rm d}\mu\leq C_{0}.

Then there exists a continuous function
g\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
g(0)=0
such that for all Borel sets
E
,

\nu(E)\leq g(\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E))\quad\text{for all}\,\nu\in\mathcal{N}.

In particular, the family of measures
(\nu)_{\nu\in\mathcal{N}}
is uniformly absolutely
continuous with respect to capacity.

Proof.

For any Borel subset
E\subset X
, if
\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E)=0
, then
E
is a pluripolar set, and consequently
\nu(E)=0
. In this case, the desired inequality holds trivially.
We may therefore assume
\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E)>0
. We have for
k>0

\displaystyle\int_{E}e^{-\psi}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{E\cap\{\psi\geq-k\}}e^{-\psi}d\mu+\int_{E\cap\{\psi<-k\}}e^{-\psi}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq e^{k}\mu(E)+\frac{1}{h(k)}\int_{E\cap\{\psi<-k\}}h(-\psi)e^{-\psi}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C(e^{k}\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E)+{h(k)}^{-1}),

where the last inequality follows from Proposition
2.9
.
If
\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E)=0
then
E
is a pluripolar set, so
\nu(E)=0
, the desired inequality is trivial.
Otherwise, taking
k\coloneqq\log\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E)}}>0
we get that
e^{-\psi}\mu(E)\leq g(\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E))
where

g(t)\coloneqq C(t^{1/2}+h(\log(t^{-1/2}))^{-1}).

Otherwise, since
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E)}}\leq 1
, the choice of
g
ensures that
\nu(E)\leq C\leq g(\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{\omega_{X}}(E))
.
∎

Example 2.13
.

We construct an example of a measure with
radial singularities
, inspired by
[
17
, Section 1.3]
and
[
24
, Section 5]
.
Let
X=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a complex projective manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with
the Fubini–Study metric
\omega_{X}=\omega_{\rm FS}
. We assume that
{\varphi}
is a
\omega_{\rm FS}
-psh function on
X
which has a radial singularity at a point
p
, i.e., it is invariant under the group
U(n,\mathbb{C})
in the neighborhood of
p
. Choosing a local chart biholomorphic to the unit ball
B\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, with
p
corresponding to the origin. Locally, the function
\varphi
can be written as
\varphi=u-\frac{1}{2}\log[1+\|z\|^{2}]
for some psh function
u
.

We therefore consider a psh function
u\coloneqq\chi\circ L
defined near the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
L(z)\coloneqq\log\|z\|
and
\chi\colon\mathbb{R}^{-}\to\mathbb{R}^{-}
is a convex increasing function. A computation shows that

(dd^{c}u)^{n}=\frac{c_{n}(\chi^{\prime}\circ L)^{n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}\circ L}{\|z\|^{2n}}\omega_{X}^{n}=\colon e^{-\psi_{\chi}}\omega_{X}^{n}.

To simplify later estimates, we assume that
\chi(t)
does not go to zero too fast at infinity
-\infty
, that is,
\chi^{\prime}(t),\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)\geq e^{Ct}
near
t=-\infty
. Under this hypothesis, one finds
\psi_{\chi}\sim\log\|z\|
near the origin. Hence,
the function
h
satisfies
\int_{X}h(-\psi_{\chi})e^{-\psi_{\chi}}\omega_{X}^{n}<\infty
if and only if

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\chi^{\prime}(t)^{n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)dt<\infty.

Below, we provide several families of functions
\chi
for which the condition holds with
h(s)=s^{\varepsilon}
, where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.

(1)

Consider
\chi_{a}(t)=(-t)^{a}
for
a\in(0,1)
. We have

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\chi^{\prime}(t)^{n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)dt\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\cdot(-t)^{-n(1-a)-1}dt<\infty,

for
h(s)=s^{\varepsilon}
with
0<\varepsilon<<1
and
a<\frac{n-\varepsilon}{n}
.

(2)

Consider
\chi_{a}(t)=-(\log(-t))^{a}
, where
a>0
. We have

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\chi^{\prime}(t)^{n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)dt\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\cdot(-t)^{-n-1}(\log(-t))^{na-n}dt<\infty,

for
h(s)=s^{\varepsilon}
with
0<\varepsilon<<1
.

(3)

\chi(t)=-\log(\log(-t))
. We have

\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\chi^{\prime}(t)^{n-1}\chi^{\prime\prime}(t)dt\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{-1}h(-t)\cdot(-t)^{-n-1}(\log(-t))^{-n}dt<\infty,

for
h(s)=s^{\varepsilon}
with
0<\varepsilon<<1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume

are negative
\theta
-psh functions such that

•

•

\theta
-psh functions

Then, for every

Proof.

The proof follows closely that of
[
7
, Lemma 4.31]
, which itself originates in
[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
.
Let
\varphi
be a
\theta
-psh function such that
v_{2}-1\leq\varphi\leq v_{2}
. For every

s>0
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\{u<v-s-(1-\lambda)t\}\subset\{u<\lambda v_{1}+(1-t)(1-\lambda)v_{2}+(1-\lambda)t\varphi-s\}\subset\{u<v-s\}.

Setting
\widehat{v}:=\lambda v_{1}+(1-t)(1-\lambda)v_{2}+(1-\lambda)t\varphi

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)^{n}t^{n}\int_{\{u<v-s-(1-\lambda)t\}}\theta_{\varphi}^{n}\leq\int_{\{u<v-s-(1-\lambda)t\}}\theta_{\widehat{v}}^{n}\leq\int_{\{u<\widehat{v}-s\}}\theta_{\widehat{v}}^{n},

\displaystyle\int_{\{u<\widehat{v}-s\}}\theta_{\widehat{v}}^{n}\leq\int_{\{u<\widehat{v}-s\}}\theta_{u}^{n}\leq\int_{\{u<v-s\}}\theta_{u}^{n}.

Since
\varphi
was taken arbitrarily, it follows that

(1-\lambda)^{n}t^{n}\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{v_{2}}\{u<v-s-(1-\lambda)t\}\leq\int_{\{u<v-s\}}\theta_{u}^{n},

for all

we define

(3.8)

B_{0}=B_{0}(c,\delta)=\frac{Ac+K^{2}\delta}{\varepsilon_{0}},

with
c=c(\delta)
being chosen hereafter. We set

(3.9)

u_{c,\delta}:=B_{0}\Psi_{0}+(1-B_{0})\Phi_{c,\delta},

where
\Phi_{c,\delta}
is defined by (
2.2
). It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\theta+dd^{c}u_{c,\delta}\geq\varepsilon_{0}B_{0}^{2}\omega_{X},

hence
u_{c,\delta}
is
\theta
-psh.
Recall that
a_{0}
is a positive constant such that
\omega_{X}+a_{0}dd^{c}\psi\geq 0
. Fixing
0<a\leq a_{0}
, we set

v_{a,\delta}:=u_{c,\delta}+a\varepsilon_{0}B_{0}^{2}\psi.

Step 1: Bounding
\sup_{X}(v_{a,\delta}-u)
.
We observe that
v_{a,\delta}
is
\theta
-psh and
v_{a,\delta}\leq V_{\theta}+a\varepsilon_{0}B_{0}^{2}\psi.

Applying Lemma
3.3
with
\lambda=B_{0}
and
v=v_{a,\delta}
, we obtain

(3.10)

u\geq B_{0}v_{a,\delta}+(1-B_{0})V_{\theta}-M_{1}(aB_{0}^{2})\geq v_{a,\delta}-M_{1}(aB_{0}^{2}),

using that
v_{a,\delta}\leq V_{\theta}
.
Since
v_{a,\delta}\leq V_{\theta}+aB_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}\psi,
we also have
-\psi\leq\frac{V_{\theta}-u}{aB_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}}

on
\{u<v_{a,\delta}\}
. We infer that for
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\{u<v_{a,\delta}-s\}}\theta_{u}^{n}=\int_{\{u<v_{a,\delta}-s\}}e^{-\psi}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u<v_{a,\delta}-s\}}e^{\frac{V_{\theta}-u}{aB_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{X}e^{\frac{2(V_{\theta}-u)}{aB_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}}}d\mu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\{u<v_{a,\delta}-s\}}d\mu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq M_{2}(aB_{0}^{2})\left[\mathop{\mathrm{Cap}}\nolimits_{V_{\theta}}(\{u<v_{a,\delta}-s\})\right]^{2},

where the second inequality follows from Hölder’s inequality and the last one follows from Theorem
2.9
.
Combining this with Lemma
3.5
(
\lambda=B_{0}
,
v_{1}=v_{a,\delta}
,
v_{2}=V_{\theta}
), we infer that

(3.11)

\begin{split}\sup_{X}(v_{a,\delta}-u)&\leq\frac{M_{3}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\|v_{a,\delta}-u\|^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}_{L^{1}(\nu)}\\
&\leq\frac{M_{4}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\|(v_{a,\delta}-u)_{+}\|^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}_{L^{1}(\mu)},\end{split}

for every
s\geq 0
, where
\nu=\mathbf{1}_{\{u<v_{a,\delta}\}}\theta_{u}^{n}
.
Applying Lemma
3.3
with
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
and
v=\Psi_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}a\psi
, we obtain

(3.12)

u\geq\dfrac{\Psi_{0}+\varepsilon_{0}a\psi+V_{\theta}}{2}-M_{5}(a)\geq\Psi_{0}+\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}a\psi}{2}-M_{5}(a).

It follows that

\displaystyle v_{a,\delta}-u

\displaystyle=2B_{0}(\Psi_{0}+\dfrac{\varepsilon_{0}a\psi}{2}-u)+(1-B_{0})(\Phi_{c,\delta}-u)

\displaystyle\leq 2B_{0}M_{5}(a)+(\Phi_{c,\delta}-u)_{+}.

This combined with (
3.11
) implies that

(3.13)

\begin{split}\sup_{X}(v_{a,\delta}-u)&\leq\sup_{X}(v_{a,\delta}-u-2B_{0}M_{5}(a))+2B_{0}M_{5}(a)\\
&\leq\frac{M_{4}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\|(v_{a,\delta}-u-2B_{0}M_{5}(a))_{+}\|^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}_{L^{1}(\mu)}+2B_{0}M_{5}(a)\\
&\leq\frac{M_{4}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\|(\Phi_{c,\delta}-u)_{+}\|^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}_{L^{1}(\mu)}+2B_{0}M_{5}(a)\\
&\leq\frac{M_{6}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\|(\Phi_{c,\delta}-u)_{+}\|^{\frac{\beta}{2n+1}}_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})}+2B_{0}M_{5}(a)\\
&\leq\frac{M_{6}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\|(\rho_{\delta}u-u)_{+}\|^{\frac{\beta}{2n+1}}_{L^{1}(\omega_{X}^{n})}+2B_{0}M_{5}(a)\\
&\leq\frac{M_{7}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}^{2}}\delta^{\frac{2\beta}{2n+1}}+2B_{0}M_{5}(a),\end{split}

where the last estimate holds due to Lemma
2.3
.
Fix
U\Subset\textrm{Amp}(\theta)\setminus\{\psi=-\infty\}
.

Step 2: Bounding
\sup_{U}(u_{c,\delta}-u)
.

Recall that
u_{c,\delta}=v_{a,\delta}-aB_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}\psi
. Set
m_{U}:=\sup_{\bar{U}}(-\psi)
.
It follows from (
3.13
) that

\sup_{U}(u_{c,\delta}-u)\leq aB_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}m_{U}+\frac{M_{7}(aB_{0}^{2})}{B_{0}}\delta^{\frac{2\beta}{2n+1}}+2B_{0}M_{5}(a).

Denoting by
h_{1}
the function
a\mapsto a\varepsilon_{0}m_{U}+2M_{5}(a)
and choosing

a\coloneqq\min\left\{h_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{B_{0}}}\right),\frac{a_{0}}{2}\right\},

since
B_{0}^{2}\leq B_{0}
we have

\sup_{U}(u_{c,\delta}-u)\leq\sqrt{B_{0}}+\frac{M_{7}\left(h_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{B_{0}}}\right)B_{0}^{2}\right)}{B_{0}^{2}}\delta^{\frac{2\beta}{2n+1}}.

Denoting by
h_{2}
the function
t\mapsto\frac{M_{7}\left(h_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\right)t^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{t^{5}}}
and choosing
c=c(\delta)>0
so that

h_{2}(B_{0})=h\left(\frac{Ac+K\delta^{2}}{\varepsilon_{0}}\right)=\delta^{\frac{-2\beta}{2n+1}}.

Thus, we obtain

\sup_{U}(u_{c,\delta}-u)\leq 2\sqrt{B_{0}(\delta)}.

Step 3: Conclusion.

As follows from Lemma
3.6
below, we obtain

\rho_{\kappa(\delta)}u(z)-u(z)\leq 4\sqrt{B_{0}(\delta)}+2C_{U}B_{0}(\delta)+2K^{2}\delta,

where

\kappa(\delta)=\delta\exp\Big(\dfrac{-4A(C_{U}B_{0}+2\sqrt{B_{0}(\delta)}+2K^{2}\delta)}{\varepsilon_{0}B_{0}}\Big),\quad C_{U}=\sup_{U}(V_{\theta}-\Psi_{0}).

Replacing
\delta
with
\kappa(\delta)
we obtain for
\delta\leq\kappa(\delta_{0})
,

\rho_{\delta}u(z)-u(z)\leq 4\sqrt{B_{0}(\kappa^{-1}(\delta))}+2C_{U}B_{0}(\kappa^{-1}(\delta)).

The conclusion thus follows.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
c:(0,\delta_{1})\rightarrow(0,c_{1})
and
B_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be functions. Assume
\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}c(\delta)/\delta=+\infty
, so that
K^{2}\delta<Ac(\delta)
for every
0<\delta<\delta_{2}
, where
\delta_{2}\in(0,\delta_{1})
is sufficiently small.
Let
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\theta)
be such that

(3.14)

\sup_{U}(u_{c(\delta),\delta}-u)\leq B_{1}(\delta),

for every
\delta\in(0,\delta_{2})
, where
u_{c(\delta),\delta}
is defined by (
3.9
) and

U\Subset\textrm{Amp}(\theta)\backslash\{\psi=-\infty\}
. Then we have

(3.15)

\sup_{U}(\rho_{\kappa(\delta)}u-u)\leq 2(B_{1}+C_{U}B_{0}+2K^{2}\delta),

where

C_{U}=\sup_{U}(V_{\theta}-\Psi_{0})\;\text{and}\;\kappa(\delta)=\delta\exp\Big(\dfrac{-4A(C_{U}B_{0}+B_{1}+2K^{2}\delta)}{\varepsilon_{0}B_{0}}\Big).

Proof.

The argument follows from
[
9
, pages 642-643]
, we include the proof
for the readers’ convenience.
By the assumption, we have

\displaystyle B_{1}\geq u_{c,\delta}(z)-u(z)

\displaystyle=B_{0}(\Psi_{0}(z)-u(z))

\displaystyle+(1-B_{0})\left(\rho_{t_{0}}u(z)+K(t_{0}^{2}-\delta^{2})+K(t_{0}-\delta)-c\log\left(\frac{t_{0}}{\delta}\right)-u(z)\right),

for every
z\in U
, where
t_{0}=t_{0}(z)\in(0,\delta)
is a minimum point of

\rho_{t}u(z)+Kt^{2}+Kt-c\log\left(\frac{t}{\delta}\right)
. Since
V_{\theta}\geq u

and
\rho_{t_{0}}u+Kt_{0}^{2}\geq u
(see Lemma
2.1
), it follows that

B_{0}(\Psi_{0}(z)-V_{\theta}(z))-c(1-B_{0})\log\left(\frac{t_{0}}{\delta}\right)\leq B_{1}+K\delta^{2}+K\delta\leq B_{1}+2K^{2}\delta,

for every
z\in U
. Hence, for every
z\in U
,

\displaystyle t_{0}(z)

\displaystyle\geq\delta\exp\bigg(\dfrac{B_{0}(\Psi_{0}(z)-V_{\theta}(z))-B_{1}-2K^{2}\delta}{c(1-B_{0})}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\delta\exp\Big(\dfrac{-4C_{U}B_{0}-4B_{1}-8K^{2}\delta}{A^{-1}\varepsilon_{0}B_{0}}\Big)=\kappa(\delta),

using the facts that
B_{0}\varepsilon_{0}=Ac+K^{2}\delta\leq 2Ac
and
1-B_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Since
t\mapsto\rho_{t}u+Kt^{2}
is increasing, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\kappa(\delta)}u(z)-u(z)

\displaystyle\leq\rho_{t_{0}}u(z)+Kt_{0}^{2}-u(z)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\rho_{t_{0}}u(z)+K(t_{0}^{2}-\delta^{2})+K(t_{0}-\delta)-c\log\left(\frac{t_{0}}{\delta}\right)\right)-u(z)+2K^{2}\delta

\displaystyle\leq\Phi_{c,\delta}(z)-u(z)+2K^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{1-B_{0}}(u_{c,\delta}(z)-u(z))+\dfrac{B_{0}}{1-B_{0}}(u(z)-\Psi_{0}(z))+2K^{2}\delta

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{B_{1}(\delta)}{1-B_{0}}+\dfrac{B_{0}}{1-B_{0}}(V_{\theta}(z)-\Psi_{0}(z))+2K^{2}\delta,

for every
z\in U
, where the last inequality holds due to (
3.14
). Therefore,

\rho_{\kappa(\delta)}u(z)-u(z)\leq\dfrac{B_{1}}{1-B_{0}}+\dfrac{C_{U}B_{0}}{1-B_{0}}+2K^{2}\delta\leq 2(B_{1}+B_{0}C_{U})+2K^{2}\delta,

for every
z\in U
.
The proof is thus complete.
∎

We now deal with the special case when
\theta=\omega_{X}
is a Kähler form, proving Corollary
1.2
.
Recall that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(B,\beta,a_{0},C_{0},h)
is the set of probability measures
\nu
on
X
such that
\nu=e^{-\psi}\mu
with
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(B,\beta)
,
\psi\in{\rm PSH}(X,a_{0}\omega_{X})
and

\int_{X}h(-\psi)e^{-\psi}{\rm d}\mu\leq C_{0},

where
a_{0}>0
,
C_{0}>0
are fixed constants and
h:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is an increasing concave function with
h(\infty)=\infty
. We define the set

\mathcal{F}=\{u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X}),\,\sup_{X}u=0\colon(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)^{n}\in\mathcal{N}\}.

Lemma 3.7
.

The closure of
\mathcal{F}
is contained in
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{F}
be a sequence of
\omega_{X}
-psh functions such that
(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}\in\mathcal{N}
and
\sup_{X}u_{j}=0
. By Hartogs’ lemma (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Proposition 8.4]
), there exists a subsequence, still denoted
(u_{j})
, such that

u_{j}\xrightarrow{L^{1}}u
for some
u\in{\rm PSH}(X,\omega_{X})
.
By Proposition
2.10
, the family of non-pluripolar measures
(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}
is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to capacity. Set
v_{j}=\max(u_{j},u)
. We see that
v_{j}
converges to
u
in capacity thanks to Hartogs’ lemma and the quasi-continuity of
\omega_{X}
-psh functions.
As follows from
[
16
, Propositions 2.10]
, the sequence of measures
(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}v_{j})^{n}
is uniformly absolutely continuous with respect to capacity. Applying
[
16
, Proposition 2.11]
, we conclude that
u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
.
Therefore, the closure of
\mathcal{F}
in the
L^{1}
topology is contained in
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

By Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to show that there exists a positive constant
C=C(a_{0},X,\omega_{X},\mu)
such that

(3.16)

\int_{X}e^{-2u/a_{0}}d\mu\leq C.

Thanks to Lemma
3.7
, the closure of the set
\mathcal{F}=\{u\in\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X}),\,\sup_{X}u=0\colon(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)^{n}\in\mathcal{N}\}
is a compact family of functions in
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
. Since functions in
\mathcal{E}(X,\omega_{X})
have zero Lelong number at every point on
X
by
[
26
]
, the uniform Skoda integrability
theorem (see, e.g.,
[
27
]
) ensures that there exists a constant
C
depending on
a_{0}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
such that the inequality (
3.16
) holds for all
u\in\mathcal{F}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Lemma
3.7
still holds in the more general setting where
\theta

\int_{X}\theta^{n}>0
). Corollary
1.2
also remains valid, but in this case only on compact subsets of

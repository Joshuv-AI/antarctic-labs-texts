Certifying classes of d d -outcome measurements with quantum steering
Alexandre C. Orthey Jr, Remigiusz Augusiak

Abstract
Device-independent certification schemes are based on minimal assumptions about the quantum system under study, which makes the most desirable among certification schemes. However, they are often the most challenging to implement. In order to reduce the implementation cost one can consider semi-device-independent schemes such as those based on quantum steering. Here we provide a construction of a family of steering inequalities which are tailored to large classes of d d -outcomes projective measurements being a certain linear combination of the Heisenberg-Weyl operators on the untrusted side and a fixed set of known measurements on the trusted side. We then prove that the maximal quantum violation of those inequalities can be used for certification of those measurements and the maximally entangled state of two qudits. Importantly, in our self-testing proof, we do not assume the shared state to be pure, nor do we assume the measurements to be projective. Before concluding, we also show how robust to noise our self-testing statement is. We believe that our construction broadens the scope of semi-device-independent certification, paving the way for more general but still less costly quantum certification protocols.



I
Introduction

The advantage of quantum strategies over classical ones when performing certain tasks can be verified by violating Bell-like inequalities
[
1
]
. These inequalities are tailored according to the scenario under scrutiny in such a way that overcoming the bound achieved by any classical strategy necessarily implies that a certain quantum correlation must be present in the system. By maximally violating these inequalities it is possible to go beyond that and certify more specific details about the quantum system, such as measurements and states, up to local unitary transformations and extra degrees of freedom. In fact, that can always be done by simply performing quantum tomography on the system, but such a procedure often requires too many measurements and resources and, more importantly, requires the measuring devices to be fully characterized and to perform known measurements.
With Bell-like inequalities, on the other side, we can certify measurements and states in a device-independent (DI) way, that is, by not relying on the specific details of the measurement apparatus. Rather, in DI certification, we only assume classical inputs and outputs in the experiment (i.e. probabilities), which is a great advantage in the certification of quantum devices in quantum cryptography schemes
[
2
]
.

The DI certification scheme based on the maximal violation of Bell-like inequalities described above is known as self-testing
[
3
,
4
]
(see Ref.
[
5
]
for a review). Since the last decade, many inequalities were proposed to self-test any pure bipartite and multipartite entangled states, as well as, some specific classes of quantum measurements

[

. Among these classes of measurements, we can highlight the mutually unbiased measurements
[

and the optimal Collins-Gisin-Linden-Massar-Popescu (CGLMP) measurements

Although very general, the DI self-testing schemes of Ref.

One example of these schemes is the prepare and measure (PM) scenario, where we assume the dimension of the system to be known. The PM scenario allows the certification, for instance, of nonprojective qubit measurements

II
Preliminaries

II.1
Generalized observables

The scenario in question is composed of a simple bipartite system where the two parties, Alice and Bob, represented by curly symbols
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, share a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
acting over
\mathcal{H_{A}\otimes H_{B}}
. Both of them perform
d
measurements of
d
outcomes each. We assume that Alice’s measurements are trusted, that is, completely characterized. Bob’s measurements, however, are unknown; we assume that they can be non-projective and of any dimension. We also assume that the state shared by Alice and Bob can be mixed, but Alice’s share is of local dimension
\dim\mathcal{H_{A}}=d
.

Alice’s and Bob’s measurements are represented by POVM’s
M_{x}=\{M_{a|x}\}_{a=0}^{d-1}
and
N_{y}=\{N_{b|y}\}_{b=0}^{d-1}
, that is,
M_{a|x}\geqslant 0
and
\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}M_{a|x}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
, and similarly for Bob. The measurement choices and the outcomes are identified by
x,y\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
and
a,b\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
, respectively. After performing the experiment many times, the statistics can be collected in the form of a set of probabilities
\mathbf{p}\coloneqq\{p(ab|xy)\}_{abxy}
, henceforth called
behavior
, that are calculated using Born rule, i.e.,
p(ab|xy)=\Tr[(M_{a|x}\otimes N_{b|y})\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}]
.

To detect steering, we are going to use Bell-type inequalities, that is,

\sum_{a,b,x,y}\alpha_{a,b,x,y}p(a,b|x,y)\leqslant\beta_{C},

(1)

where
\alpha_{a,b,x,y}
are coefficients suitably chosen and
\beta_{C}
is the bound satisfied by behaviors that admit a local hidden state model
[
28
]
; we will refer to it simply as
classical bound
. A behavior that violates the above steering inequality implies that the state
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
is
steerable
.

Since our goal is to deal with
d
-dimensional systems, it is convenient to work with generalized correlators, which are the Fourier transform of
\mathbf{p}
given by

\langle A_{k|x}B_{l|y}\rangle=\sum_{a,b=0}^{d-1}\omega^{ak+bl}p(ab|xy),

(2)

where
\omega=\exp(2\pi\ic/d)
and
k,l\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
. The objects
A_{k|x}
and
B_{l|y}
are called
generalized observables
; they are represented as the Fourier transforms of the original measurement operators, that is,
A_{k|x}=\sum_{a=0}^{d-1}\omega^{ak}M_{a|x}
and
N_{l|y}=\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}\omega^{bl}N_{b|y}
. Because the Fourier transform is invertible,
A_{x}=\{A_{k|x}\}
and
B_{y}=\{B_{l|y}\}
fully characterize Alice’s and Bob’s measurements. Therefore, we can make all the calculations and proofs with
A_{x}
and
B_{y}
, and at the end of the day, we do the inverse Fourier transform to send
M_{x}
and
N_{y}
to our experimentalist colleagues so they can perform the measurements in the lab. Moreover, in the quantum case we have

\langle A_{k|x}B_{l|y}\rangle=\Tr\left[\rho_{AB}\left(A_{k|x}\otimes B_{l|y}\right)\right].

(3)

Because we know Alice’s measurements, we can assume that they are projective without loss of generality. It is proven in Appendix A of Ref.
[
14
]
that if
M_{x}
is projective, then
A_{k|x}
are unitary operators such that
A_{k|x}=(A_{1|x})^{k}
. In that case, we use the notation
A_{1|x}\coloneqq A_{x}
, which means that
A_{k|x}=A_{x}^{k}
. From the fact that
\omega^{d-k}=\omega^{-k}=(\omega^{k})^{*}
, we can summarize the properties satisfied by operators
A_{x}
and
B_{y}
by

\displaystyle A_{x}^{d-k}=A_{x}^{-k}=\left(A_{x}^{k}\right)^{\dagger},

(4a)

\displaystyle B_{d-l|y}=B_{-l|y}=\left(B_{l|y}\right)^{\dagger},

(4b)

for every
k,l\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
and also
A_{x}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
and
B_{l|y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
. While
A_{x}
are unitary, Bob’s measurements satisfy
B_{l|y}^{\dagger}B_{l|y}\leqslant\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
for
l,y\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}

[
14
]
.

In our scenario, we are going to assume for convenience that Alice’s generalized observables are given by the Heisenberg-Weyl operators

A_{x}\coloneqq XZ^{-x}=(XZ^{x})^{*},

(5)

for
x\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
, where
X
and
Z
are the generalized Pauli operators given by

X=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}{|{i+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{i}|}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Z=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\omega^{i}{|{i}\rangle}\!{\langle{i}|},

(6)

such that
{|{d}\rangle}\equiv{|{0}\rangle}
. The operators
A_{x}
are unitary and satisfy properties (
4a
). Together with the computational basis, they provide
d+1
mutually unbiased basis in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
for prime
d

[
31
]
.

II.2
Self-testing

By observing the maximal violation of a tailored steering inequality, it is possible, in some cases, to certify the state shared by the parties and the measurements performed by the untrusted party, up to local unitary transformations. As we mentioned in the introduction, this method can be thought of as self-testing in the SDI scenario or, more specifically, a one-sided device independent scenario (1SDI)
[
29
]
. In our scenario, Alice performs known projective measurements
A_{x}
and Bob performs arbitrary measurements
B_{y}=\{B_{k|y}\}
over an arbitrary state
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
to obtain the behavior
\mathbf{p}
. Now, consider the reference measurements
\overline{B}_{y}=\{\overline{B}_{k|y}\}
and the reference state
{|{\overline{\psi}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}
which we would like to certify. It is said that the behavior
\mathbf{p}
semi-device-independently certify the measurements
\overline{B}_{y}
and the state
{|{\overline{\psi}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}
if there exists a unitary operator
U_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{H_{B}}\to\mathcal{H_{B}}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle={|{\overline{\psi}}\rangle\!\langle{\overline{\psi}}|}_{\mathcal{AB^{\prime}}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}},

(7a)

\displaystyle U_{\mathcal{B}}B_{l|y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\overline{B}_{l|y}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}},

(7b)

where Bob’s space decomposes as
\mathcal{H_{B}}=\mathcal{H_{B^{\prime}}}\otimes\mathcal{H_{B^{\prime\prime}}}
and the auxiliary state
\rho_{\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}}
acts on
\mathcal{H_{B^{\prime\prime}}}
.

III
Results

In the following sections, we specify the measurements that we want to certify on Bob’s part of the system and the steering inequality tailored for them. Then we calculate the quantum bound, present the self-testing statement, and provide the classical bound for the case of qutrits.

III.1
Steering inequality

Let us start by defining the measurements we want to certify on Bob’s side. The eigenvectors of the Heisenberg-Weyl operators
\{XZ^{k}\}_{k=0}^{d-1}
together with the computational basis provide
d+1
mutually unbiased basis in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
for prime
d

[
31
]
. Therefore, by taking Bob’s reference measurements as linear combinations of
XZ^{k}
, that is,

\overline{B}_{y}=\gamma_{y,0}X+\gamma_{y,1}XZ+\gamma_{y,2}XZ^{2}+\ldots+\gamma_{y,d-1}XZ^{d-1},

(8)

where
\gamma_{y,k}\in\mathbb{C}
for every
y,k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
, we can cover a large set of generalized measurements in dimension
d
. To make
\overline{B}_{y}
unitary and to have correct spectrum, i.e.
\overline{B}_{y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
, Bob’s reference measurements must have the form

\overline{B}_{y}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1,y}}\omega^{-kj}XZ^{k},

(9)

where
\{\phi_{j,y}\}_{j=0}^{d-1}
is a set of any real numbers satisfying
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\phi_{j,y}=0
and
\oplus
stands for addition modulo
d
. By choosing different values of
\phi_{j,y}
at will, we can select different measurements to certify. In what follows, we are going to construct steering inequalities tailored for each reference measurement as a function of
\{\phi_{j,y}\}
.

The steering inequality is constructed through the sum of the generalized correlators (
2
). However, to make this sum result in a real number and to include all outcomes in the inequality we must also sum over the powers of the measurement operators. To achieve that, let us artificially define the following operators:

\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}

(10)

with
n=1,\ldots,d-1
,
where the coefficients
\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}
have the form

\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}\omega^{kn(n-1)/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}\omega^{knl}

(11)

and for any measurement choice
y
, the angles
\phi_{j,y}
satisfy

\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\phi_{j,y}=0.

(12)

Note that we constructed
\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}
using the complex conjugation of the
\gamma
coefficients for mathematical convenience. After some algebra (see Appendix
A
), it is possible to show that

\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}=\overline{B}_{y}^{n},\qquad\text{for }n=1,\ldots,d-1,

(13)

which satisfies

\left(\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}\right)^{d}=\left(\overline{B}_{y}^{n}\right)^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{d},\qquad\text{for }n=1,\ldots,d-1.

(14)

Also, note that if we take
n=d
in (
10
), we obtain
\overline{B}_{y}^{(d)}=d\mathbbm{1}
. In addition, because
\overline{B}_{y}
is unitary, its powers are also unitary, as well as,
\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}
for every
n=1,\ldots,d-1
.

We want to construct a steering inequality that is maximally violated by the maximally entangled state of two qudits

{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}{|{ii}\rangle}

(15)

and the measurements
A_{x}
and
\overline{B}_{y}
. For that, we are going to use the stabilizer set of operators of the state
{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}
, which act in the form

\left(XZ^{-k}\right)^{n}\otimes\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}{|{\psi}\rangle}={|{\psi}\rangle},

(16)

for
n\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
. That means that
{|{\psi}\rangle}={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}
is the unique state that satisfies the above system of equations. Now, we need to isolate
(XZ^{k})^{n}
in (
10
) to construct generalized correlators. That can be done if we impose that

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}=\delta_{yy^{\prime}},\qquad\forall y,y^{\prime}.

(17)

The above condition imposes that Bob’s reference measurements
\{\overline{B}_{y=1},\ldots,\overline{B}_{y=d-1}\}
are in function of the first reference measurement
\overline{B}_{y=0}
in terms of the complete free set of numbers
\{\phi_{j,y=0}\}_{j=0}^{d-2}
. As a consequence, we also have that (see Appendix
A
for details)

\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}},\qquad\forall k,k^{\prime}.

(18)

If we multiply (
10
) by
\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}^{(n)}
and sum over
y
on both sides, we can use the above relation to obtain

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}.

(19)

Now, we can specify the substitution rule

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}\longrightarrow\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\coloneqq\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}B_{n|y},

(20)

where
B_{y}=\{B_{n|y}\}
, for
y=0,\ldots,d-1
and
n=1,\ldots,d-1
, represent the
d
-outcome arbitrary measurements performed by Bob satisfying

B_{n|y}^{\dagger}=B_{d-n|y}=B_{-n|y}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{n|y}^{\dagger}B_{n|y}\leqslant\mathbbm{1}.

(21)

From the substitution rule, we define our steering operator
\mathcal{S}
as

\mathcal{S}=\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}.

(22)

Therefore, we propose a steering inequality as the sum of generalized correlators in the following form

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\langle A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k,y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\langle A_{k}^{n}\otimes B_{n|y}\rangle\leqslant\beta_{C},

(23)

where
\beta_{C}
is the classical bound to be determined as a function of
\{\phi_{j,y=0}\}_{j=0}^{d-2}
. Let us notice here that while it is in general a very difficult task to determine the value of
\beta_{C}
, below we show that for any of our steering functionals
\beta_{C}<\beta_{Q}
, meaning that the corresponding steering inequalities are all nontrivial. In fact, we show that our steering functionals enable self-testing maximally entangled states of two qudits, which would not be possible if
\beta_{C}=\beta_{Q}
.

III.2
Example: qutrits

To make the definitions of the previous section clearer, let us use qutrits as an example. In this case, Alice has three measurement options every round of the experiment, which are given by
A_{0}=X
,
A_{1}=XZ^{-1}
, and
A_{2}=XZ^{-2}
. On the other side, Bob’s measurements
B_{0}
,
B_{1}
, and
B_{2}
are unknown. Because the reference measurements
\overline{B}_{y}
must be given by unitary operators that satisfy
\overline{B}_{y}^{3}=\mathbbm{1}_{3}
, we must have

\overline{B}_{y}=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(1)}\right)^{*}XZ^{k}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&0&e^{i\phi_{0,y}}\\
e^{i\phi_{1,y}}&0&0\\
0&e^{-i\left(\phi_{0,y}+\phi_{1,y}\right)}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

Among the above operators, only those that satisfy condition (
17
) can be self-tested by the maximal violation of (
23
). For
y=0
, coefficients
\phi_{0,0}
and
\phi_{1,0}
are completely free. For
y=1,2
, however, condition (
17
) imposes that
\{\phi_{0,1},\phi_{1,1},\phi_{0,2},\phi_{1,2}\}
must be a function of
\{\phi_{0,0},\phi_{1,0}\}
. In Sec.
III.5
, we are going to provide numerical calculations of the classical bound
\beta_{C}
as a function of
\{\phi_{0,0},\phi_{1,0}\}
.

III.3
Quantum bound

Quantum realizations can violate (
23
). Here, we calculate the maximum violation by performing an SOS decomposition of
\mathcal{S}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left[\mathbbm{1}-A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right]^{\dagger}\left[\mathbbm{1}-A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant d(d-1)\mathbbm{1}-\mathcal{S},

(25)

which implies that the maximum value achieved by
\mathcal{S}
is given by
\beta_{Q}\coloneqq d(d-1)
(see Appendix
B
). If Alice and Bob perform the measurements represented by

A_{x}=XZ^{-x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{n|y}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n},

(26)

for
x=0,\ldots,d-1
and
y=0,\ldots,d-1
respectively, then operator (
22
) becomes a sum of stabilizing operators of the state
{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}
. After taking the expected value, the quantum bound of
d(d-1)
is achieved.

III.4
Self-testing proof

We start now by developing the self-testing statements regarding our steering operator
\mathcal{S}
by considering first that the state
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}\!{\langle{\psi}|}_{\mathcal{AB}}
is pure and that the measurements
B_{y}
performed by Bob are projective. Because of that, each of those measurements can be encoded in a single unitary observable
B_{y}\coloneqq B_{1|y}
that satisfies
B_{y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}
. Consequently, they also satisfy
B_{n|y}=B_{y}^{n}
and

B_{y}^{d-n}=B_{y}^{-n}=\left(B_{y}^{n}\right)^{\dagger}.

(27)

From the above and from Fact
2
(see Appendix
A
), we can conclude that the operators
\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}
defined in (
20
) must satisfy

\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(d-n)}=\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}.

(28)

Now, consider the state and the measurements that give the maximum violation of (
23
). We can infer from the SOS decomposition (
25
) that such state and measurements must satisfy

A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}

(29)

for every
k=0,\ldots,d-1
and
n=1,\ldots,d-1
. By implementing the notation
\widetilde{B}_{k}\coloneqq\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(1)}
, we can write

A_{k}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(30)

From the above, we can present the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the steering inequality (
23
) is maximally violated by a state
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H_{B}}
and unitary
d
-outcome observables
A_{k}
and
B_{y}
(
k,y\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
) acting on, respectively,
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H_{B}}
such that the observables on Alice’s trusted side are given by
A_{k}=XZ^{-k}
. Then, the following statement holds true: there exists a local unitary transformation on Bob’s untrusted side,
U_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{H_{B}}\to\mathcal{H_{B}}
, such that

(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}){|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}

(31)

and

\forall y,\quad U_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\overline{B}_{y},

(32)

where
\mathbb{B}_{y}
is
B_{y}
projected onto the support of Bob’s state
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\overline{B}_{y}\coloneqq\overline{B}_{y}^{(1)}
is given by (
10
).

Proof.

Here, we present only a sketch of the proof, which full version can be found in Appendix
C
. Note that we follow similar steps implemented in the proof of Theorem 1.1 of Ref.
[
30
]
. First, we prove that Bob’s measurements split in
B_{y}=\mathbb{B}_{y}\oplus E_{y}
where
\mathbb{B}_{y}\coloneqq\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}
is the projection of
B_{y}
onto the support of
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
and
E_{i}
belongs to the complement of it. This is proved by implementing the projection
\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}
onto (
30
) and using tricks to show that
\mathbb{B}_{y}
is unitary. Then, we write
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}
in its Schmidt decomposition form

{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\lambda_{i}{|{u_{i}}\rangle}{|{v_{i}}\rangle},

(33)

where
\{{|{u_{i}}\rangle}\}
and
\{{|{v_{i}}\rangle}\}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
, respectively. Also, because
\rank(\rho_\mc{A})=d
, we have
\lambda_{i}>0
,
\forall i
, such that
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}=1
. Note that, there is a unitary transformation
U_{\mathcal{B}}
that satisfies
U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{v_{i}}\rangle}={|{u_{i}^{*}}\rangle}
,
\forall i
, where
∗
denotes complex conjugation. Therefore, we can rewrite (
33
) as

{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\left(P_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right){|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle},

(34)

where
P_{\mathcal{A}}
is an operator that is diagonal in the basis
\{u_{i}\}
, with eigenvalues
\sqrt{d}\lambda_{i}
. From (
30
) and the above, it is possible to show that
P_{\mathcal{A}}
is proportional to identity. Therefore, the above equation implies (
31
). Again, from (
30
) and the fact that
P_{\mathcal{A}}
is proportional to identity, we can also show that
U_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=A_{k}^{*}
. From definition (
20
), that can be expressed as

\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(1)}U_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=A_{k}^{*}.

(35)

We can now multiply both sides by
\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(1)}\right)^{*}
, sum over
k
, and use (

\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\delta_{yy^{\prime}}U_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(1)}\right)^{*}XZ^{k}.

(36)

Finally, from definition (
10
), the above equation results in

U_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\overline{B}_{y},

(37)

which finishes the proof.
∎

Now, we are going to extend the above results to the case where we do not assume that (i) the measurements performed by Bob are projective and (ii) that the state shared by the parties is pure.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the steering inequality (
23
) is maximally violated by a state
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H_{B}}
and unitary
d
-outcome observables
A_{k}
and
B_{y}
(
k,y\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
) acting on, respectively,
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H_{B}}
such that the observables on Alice’s trusted side are given by
A_{k}=XZ^{-k}
. Then, the following three statements hold true:

(i) Bob’s measurements are projective, that is, all operators
B_{n|y}
are unitary such that
B_{n|y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}
,

(ii) Bob’s Hilbert space decomposes as
\mathcal{H_{B}}=(\mathbb{C}^{d})_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}
, and

(iii) there exists a local unitary transformation on Bob’s untrusted side,
U_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{H_{B}}\to\mathcal{H_{B}}
, such that

(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}})\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger})={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\!{\langle{\phi_{d}^{+}}|}_{\mathcal{AB^{\prime}}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}

(38)

and

\forall y,\quad U_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\overline{B}_{y}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}},

(39)

where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
denotes Bob’s auxiliary system and
\overline{B}_{y}\coloneqq\overline{B}_{y}^{(1)}
is given by (
10

Before we present the proof of this theorem, let us stress again that the above statement demonstrates that all our steering inequalities are nontrivial as they enable self-testing maximally entangled states. Clearly, this would not be possible if the maximal quantum value of our functionals could be achieved by separable states.

Proof.

Here, we present only a sketch of the proof, which full version can be found in Appendix
C
. Note that we follow similar steps implemented in the proof of Theorem 1.2 of Ref.
[
30
]
. If we observe the maximal violation of inequality (
23
), then we can infer from the SOS decomposition (
25
) that

\left(A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}

(40)

for every
n\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
and
k\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
. From (
40
) and (
21
), we can do some manipulations using (
17
) to prove that
\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}
, as well as
B_{n|y}
, must be unitary. That means that Bob’s measurements
N_{b|y}
must be projective and, therefore, we can use
B_{n|y}=B_{y}^{n}
. To extend the proof of Theorem
1
to mixed states, we start by writing
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}
in its Schmidt decomposition

\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}=\sum_{s=1}^{k}p_{s}{|{\psi_{s}}\rangle}\!{\langle{\psi_{s}}|}_{\mathcal{AB}},

(41)

where
p_{s}\geqslant 0
for every
s
such that

. If the steering inequality is violated by

, then every

must satisfy
(A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}){|{\psi_{s}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi_{s}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}

U_{\mathcal{B}}^{(s)}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{(s)}\right){|{\psi_{s}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}

\displaystyle={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle},

(42a)

\displaystyle U_{\mathcal{B}}^{(s)}\mathbb{B}_{y}^{(s)}\left(U_{\mathcal{B}}^{(s)}\right)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\overline{B}_{y},

(42b)

for every
y
, where
\mathbb{B}_{y}^{(s)}\coloneqq\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}B_{y}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}^{s}
is the operator
B_{y}
projected onto the support of Bob’s local state
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}^{(s)}=\Tr_{\mathcal{A}}\left({|{\psi_{s}}\rangle}\!{\langle{\psi_{s}}|}_{\mathcal{AB}}\right)

B_{y}=\mathbb{B}_{y}^{(s)}\oplus E_{y}^{(s)},

(43)

where

are unitary operators such that

IV
Robust self-testing

If the maximal violation of the steering inequality (
23
) is not reached by an
\epsilon
, then the state and the measurements performed in the experiment will be close to the reference state and measurements by a factor as a function of
\epsilon
. Below, we provide the robustness analysis of our self-testing statement for the qutrit case, assuming that the measurements performed by Bob are projective and the state is pure.

Theorem 3
.

For
d=3
, consider the unitary
d
-outcome observables
A_{k}=XZ^{-k}
and
B_{y}
, for
k,y\in\{0,1,2\}
acting on, respectively
\mathbb{C}^{3}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
. If the Steering inequality (
23
)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\langle A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\rangle\leqslant\beta_{C},

(50)

is violated by a state
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}\in\mathbb{C}^{3}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
and observables
B_{y}
such that
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle\geqslant 6-\epsilon>\beta_{C}
, then there exists a unitary operation
U_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
such that

\left\|\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes B_{y}\right){|{\psi}\rangle}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{3}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|\leqslant 2\sqrt{3}(2\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}

(51)

and

\left\|B_{y}-\overline{B}_{y}\right\|_{2}\leqslant 12\left(2\epsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

(52)

where
k=0,1,2
and
\overline{B}_{y}
are Bob’s ideal observables given by (
9
), and
\|\cdot\|_{2}
stands for the Hilbert-Schmidt norm.

We left the proof of the robustness in Appendix
E
. Our robustness result for
d=3
already well illustrates the behaviour of error bounds as a function of
\epsilon
. The generalisation to higher
d
will introduce some additional
d
-dependent coefficient while keeping similar behaviour in
\epsilon
.

V
Discussion

Recently, very general schemes for device-independent certification of quantum measurements have been introduced
[
20
,
21
]
. However, in these schemes, the generality comes at the price of considerably increasing the cost of implementing them in terms of the number of measurements that the parties must perform. One way to reduce that cost is to resort to semi-device-independent scenarios, such as that based on quantum steering, where it is assumed that one of the measuring devices is trusted and performs known measurements.

In fact, by making the scheme SDI, we can reduce the number of measurements required for the certification but still encompass a large class of measurement operators. Here, we constructed a family of steering inequalities (
23
) that are tailored to certain classes of projective
d
-outcome measurements on the untrusted side and a set of
d
fixed and known measurements on the trusted one.
One can think of our inequalities as a generalization of the Bell inequalities introduced in Ref.
[
14
]
to more general classes of measurements, which comes however at the cost of assuming that one of the measuring devices is trusted; this is illustrated on Fig.
2
for a particular case of
d=3
. It is important to note that for a measure-zero set of points in Fig.
2
, the classical bound reaches the value 6, which is also the quantum bound in dimension 3. In such a case, the steering inequality is trivial and, therefore, useless for self-testing.

We then demonstrate that the new steering inequalities can be used for certification purposes and show that their maximal violation allows for SDI certification of the considered classes of measurements on the untrusted side as well as the maximally entangled state of two qudits.

An important open question concerns reducing the number of measurements from
d
to 2, which is in fact the minimal number of measurements necessary to observe Bell-nonlocality or quantum steering. Finally, it would also be useful to find a way of extending our inequalities to the device-independent scenario.

Appendix A
Construction of the steering operator

Let us consider the Steering scenario with two qudits of local dimension
d
, where Alice performs
d
measurements
A_{x}=XZ^{-x}
, for
x=0,\ldots,d-1
, and Bob performs
d
arbitrary measurements. The measurements that we want to self-test on Bob’s side have the following form

\overline{B}_{y}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1,y}}\omega^{-kj}XZ^{k},

(53)

where
\phi_{j,y}\in\mathbb{R}
for any
j,y=0,\ldots,d-1
such that

\phi_{d-1,y}=-\sum_{j=0}^{d-2}\phi_{j,y},\qquad\text{ for every }y=0,\ldots,d-1.

(54)

It can be checked that
\overline{B}_{y}
is unitary and the above condition guarantees that
\overline{B}_{y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}
. Throughout the paper, the symbol
\oplus
means addition modulo
d
. For
d=3
, for instance, we want to certify three measurements made by Bob identified by
y=0,1,2
that depend on two free real parameters
\phi_{0,y}
and
\phi_{1,y}
, for each
y
, in the following form:

\overline{B}_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&e^{i\phi_{0,y}}\\
e^{i\phi_{1,y}}&0&0\\
0&e^{-i\left(\phi_{0,y}+\phi_{1,y}\right)}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\text{for }d=3.

(55)

As we are going to see, it will be necessary to use the powers of
\overline{B}_{y}
. To do that, let us implement the explicit forms for
X
and
Z
in the above expression:

\overline{B}_{y}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j,l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1,y}}\omega^{-kj}\omega^{lk}{|{l+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{l}|}.

(56)

The sums over
k
and
j
result in

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1,y}}\omega^{-kj}\omega^{lk}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k,j=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1,y}}\omega^{k(l-j)}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1,y}}\delta_{l,j}=e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{l\oplus 1,y}}.

(57)

Therefore, we can write
\overline{B}_{y}
as

\overline{B}_{y}=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{l\oplus 1,y}}{|{l+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{l}|}.

(58)

Now, let us explicitly calculate the
n=1,\ldots,d-1
powers of
\overline{B}_{y}
:

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{y}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1},\ldots,l_{n}=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}(\phi_{l_{1}\oplus 1,y}+\ldots+\phi_{l_{n}\oplus 1,y})}{|{l_{1}+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{l_{1}}|}\ldots{|{l_{n}+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{l_{n}}|},

(59a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}(\phi_{l\oplus n,y}+\phi_{l\oplus n-1,y}+\phi_{l\oplus n-2,y}+\ldots+\phi_{l\oplus 1,y})}{|{l+n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l}|},

(59b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m-1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}{|{l+n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l}|}.

(59c)

Inspired by the fact that
[
14
]

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}=\omega^{kn(n-1)/2}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{nkl^{\prime}}{|{l^{\prime}+n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l^{\prime}}|},

(60)

we can define the following set of operators

\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}\omega^{-knl}\omega^{-kn(n-1)/2}\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n},

(61)

for
n=1,\ldots,d-1
. The direct substitution of (
60
) into (
61
) results in (
59c
), that is,

\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}=\overline{B}_{y}^{n},\qquad\text{for }n=1,\ldots,d-1,

(62)

which satisfies

\left(\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}\right)^{d}=\left(\overline{B}_{y}^{n}\right)^{d}=\mathbbm{1},\qquad\text{for }n=1,\ldots,d-1.

(63)

Also, note that if we take
n=d
in (
61
), we obtain
\overline{B}_{y}^{(d)}=d\mathbbm{1}
. In addition, because
\overline{B}_{y}
is unitary, its powers are also unitary, as well as,
\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}
for every
n=1,\ldots,d-1
.

To make the equations clearer, let us define

\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d}\omega^{kn(n-1)/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}\omega^{knl},

(64)

which means that we can rewrite
\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}
as

\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}.

(65)

Before proceeding to the construction of the steering operator, let us prove some facts about the above operator.

Fact 1
.

For any pair of integers
k
and
n
, and for any
d\geqslant 3
, the operator
\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}
satisfies the following relation:

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{d-n}=\left[\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}\right]^{\dagger}.

(66)

Proof.

From Eq. (
60
), we have

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{d-n}=\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{k(d-n)(d-n-1)/2}\omega^{k(d-n)l^{\prime}}{|{l^{\prime}+d-n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l^{\prime}}|},

(67)

With some algebra, we can show that
\omega^{k(d-n)(d-n-1)/2}=\omega^{kn(n+1)/2}
and that
\omega^{k(d-n)l^{\prime}}=\omega^{-knl^{\prime}}
. By applying these relations to (
67
), we have

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{d-n}=\omega^{kn(n+1)/2}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-nkl^{\prime}}{|{l^{\prime}-n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l^{\prime}}|},

(68)

where we have used the fact that
{|{a+d}\rangle}\equiv{|{a}\rangle}
for any integer
a
. Now, we can take the conjugate transpose of the above equation by doing

\left[\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{d-n}\right]^{\dagger}=\omega^{-kn(n+1)/2}\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{nkl^{\prime}}{|{l^{\prime}}\rangle}\!{\langle{l^{\prime}-n}|}.

(69)

Observe that we can rearrange the terms inside the above summation in the following way:

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{nkl^{\prime}}{|{l^{\prime}}\rangle}\!{\langle{l^{\prime}-n}|}

\displaystyle={|{0}\rangle}\!{\langle{-n}|}+\omega^{nk}{|{1}\rangle}\!{\langle{1-n}|}+\ldots+\omega^{nkn}{|{n}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}+\ldots+\omega^{nk(d-1)}{|{d-1}\rangle}\!{\langle{d-1-n}|},

(70a)

\displaystyle=\omega^{nkn}{|{n}\rangle}\!{\langle{0}|}+\omega^{nk(n+1)}{|{n+1}\rangle}\!{\langle{1}|}+\ldots+\omega^{nk(n+d-1)}{|{n+d-1}\rangle}\!{\langle{d-1}|},

(70b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{nk(n+l)}{|{l+n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l}|}.

(70c)

By using the above in (
69
), we obtain

\left[\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{d-n}\right]^{\dagger}=\omega^{-kn(n+1)/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\omega^{nk(l+n)}{|{l+n}\rangle}\!{\langle{l}|}.

(71)

After some basic algebra, we obtain the desired result:

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{d-n}=\left[\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}\right]^{\dagger}

(72)

∎

Fact 2
.

For every
y,k=0,\ldots,d-1
, every
n=1,\ldots,d-1
, and any dimension
d\geqslant 3
, the coefficients (
64
) satisfy the relation

\gamma_{yk}^{(d-n)}=\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\right)^{*}.

(73)

Proof.

From definition (
64
), we can directly calculate

\gamma_{yk}^{(d-1)}=\frac{1}{d}\omega^{k(d-n)(d-n-1)/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{d-n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}\omega^{k(d-n)l}.

(74)

Similarly to the proof of Fact
1
, we have

\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(d-1)}\right)^{*}=\frac{1}{d}\omega^{-kn(n+1)/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{d-n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}\omega^{knl}.

(75)

Now, observe that we can rewrite the summation inside the exponential by summing zero in the following way:

\sum_{m=1}^{d-1}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}=\sum_{m=1}^{d-1}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}+\sum_{m=d-n+1}^{d}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}-\sum_{m=d-n+1}^{d}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}=-\sum_{m=d-n+1}^{d}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}=-\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m^{\prime}+d-n,y},

(76)

where we implemented a change of variables in the last equality given by
m=m^{\prime}+d-n
. Again, similarly to the proof of Fact
1
, we can use the above equation in (
75
) and rewrite the sum over
l
as

\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m^{\prime}+d-n,y}\right)\omega^{knl}=\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m^{\prime},y}\right)\omega^{kn(l+n)},

(77)

which gives us

\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(d-1)}\right)^{*}=\frac{1}{d}\omega^{-kn(n+1)/2}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m^{\prime},y}\right)\omega^{kn(l+n)}.

(78)

By using some basic algebra in the above equation, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Fact 3
.

The operators
\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}
given by (
61
) satisfy the relation

\overline{B}_{y}^{(d-n)}=\left(\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger},\qquad\text{for }n=1,\ldots,d-1.

(79)

Proof.

Directly from Facts
1
and
2
.
∎

Now, let us construct the Steering operator. To do that, we need to impose a condition on the coefficients
\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}
, which is going to restrict the range of values for
\{\phi_{j,y}\}
. We need to impose that

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}=\delta_{yy^{\prime}},

(80)

for
n=1,\ldots,d-1
. Because

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}=\dfrac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,l,l^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\omega^{kn(l-l^{\prime})}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y}}e^{\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l^{\prime}\oplus m,y^{\prime}}}=\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}e^{\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y^{\prime}}-\phi_{l\oplus m,y}},

(81)

relation (
80
) is true
iff
the following system holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l\oplus m,y^{\prime}}-\phi_{l\oplus m,y}\right)=d\delta_{yy^{\prime}},\qquad\text{for every pair }y,y^{\prime}=0,\ldots,d-1\text{ and every }n=1,\ldots,d-1;

(82a)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{d-1}\phi_{l,y}=0,\qquad\text{for every }y=0,\ldots,d-1.

(82b)

For instance, for
d=3
, the above system establishes that Bob’s reference measurements
\overline{B}_{1}
and
\overline{B}_{2}
must depend on
\overline{B}_{0}
, which is written as a function of
2
completely free parameters, namely
\phi_{0,0}
and
\phi_{1,0}
, since
\phi_{2,0}=-(\phi_{0,0}+\phi_{1,0})
. Later on, we are going to provide some solutions for the above system.

If (
82

\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\exp\left(\mathbbm{i}\sum_{m=1}^{n}\phi_{l^{\prime}\oplus m,y}-\phi_{l\oplus m,y}\right)=d\delta_{ll^{\prime}},\qquad\text{for every pair }l,l^{\prime}=0,\ldots,d-1\text{ and every }n=1,\ldots,d-1,

(83)

which means that

\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}=\delta_{kk^{\prime}}.

(84)

From the above, we can multiply (
65
) by
\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}^{(n)}
and sum over
y
on both sides to obtain

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\overline{B}_{y}^{(n)}.

(85)

Now, we can specify the substitution rule

\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}\longrightarrow\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\coloneqq\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}B_{n|y},

(86)

where
B_{y}=\{B_{n|y}\}
, for
y=0,\ldots,d-1
and
n=1,\ldots,d-1
, represent the
d
-outcome arbitrary measurements satisfying

B_{n|y}^{\dagger}=B_{d-n|y}=B_{-n|y}

(87)

and

B_{n|y}^{\dagger}B_{n|y}\leqslant\mathbbm{1}.

(88)

Now, we can consider a Steering operator where Alice performs
d
known measurements
A_{k}=XZ^{-k}
, for
k=0,\ldots,d-1
, and Bob performs
d

B_{y}
, for
y=0,\ldots,d-1

\mathcal{S}=\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)},

(89)

which can be written as

\mathcal{S}=\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k,y=0}^{d-1}\left[\left(XZ^{-k}\right)^{n}\otimes\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}B_{n|y}\right].

(90)

Therefore, we can propose a Steering inequality as

\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\langle A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\rangle\leqslant\beta_{C},

(91)

where
\beta_{C}
is the maximum value achieved by classical strategies, henceforth called
classical bound
.

Appendix B
Quantum Bound

To calculate the quantum bound of operator
\mathcal{S}
, let us use the following SOS decomposition:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left[\mathbbm{1}-A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right]^{\dagger}\left[\mathbbm{1}-A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right]

\displaystyle=d(d-1)\mathbbm{1}-\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}+\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)},

(92)

\displaystyle=d(d-1)\mathbbm{1}-2\mathcal{S}+\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{y,y^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\right]B_{n|y^{\prime}}^{\dagger}B_{n|y},

(93)

where we have used Fact
2
and Eq. (
87
) to show that the Steering operator (
89
) is Hermitian, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}
. Now we can use (
80
) and the fact that
B_{n|y}^{\dagger}B_{n|y}\leqslant\mathbbm{1}
to obtain

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{d-1}\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left[\mathbbm{1}-A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right]^{\dagger}\left[\mathbbm{1}-A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant d(d-1)\mathbbm{1}-\mathcal{S},

(94)

which implies that the maximum value achieved by
\mathcal{S}
is given by
\beta_{Q}\coloneqq d(d-1)
. If Alice and Bob perform the measurements represented by

A_{x}=XZ^{-x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{n|y}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\left(\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\right)^{*}\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n},

(95)

for
x=0,\ldots,d-1
and
y=0,\ldots,d-1
respectively, then operator (
89
) becomes a sum of stabilizing operators of the state
{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}
, that is,

\left(XZ^{-k}\right)^{n}\otimes\left(XZ^{k}\right)^{n}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}.

(96)

Appendix C
Self-testing

We start now by developing the self-testing statements regarding our Steering operator considering that the state
\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}\!{\langle{\psi}|}_{\mathcal{AB}}
is pure and the measurements
B_{y}
performed by Bob are projective. Because of that, each of those measurements can be encoded in a single unitary observable
B_{y}\coloneqq B_{1|y}
that satisfies
B_{y}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}
. Consequently, they also satisfy
B_{n|y}=B_{y}^{n}
and

B_{y}^{d-n}=B_{y}^{-n}=\left(B_{y}^{n}\right)^{\dagger}.

(97)

From the above and from Fact
2
, we can conclude that the operators
\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}
defined in (
86
) must satisfy

\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(d-n)}=\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}.

(98)

Now, consider the state and the measurements that give the maximum violation of (
89
). We can infer from the SOS decomposition (
94
) that

A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}

(99)

for every
k=0,\ldots,d-1
and
n=1,\ldots,d-1
. By implementing the notation
\widetilde{B}_{k}\coloneqq\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(1)}
, we can write

A_{k}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(100)

The self-testing theorem can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Assume that the Steering inequality (
91
) is maximally violated by a state
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H_{B}}
and unitary
d
-outcome observables
A_{k}
and
B_{y}
(
k,y\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
) acting on, respectively,
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H_{B}}
such that the observables on Alice’s trusted side are given by
A_{k}=XZ^{-k}
. Then, the following statement holds true: there exists a local unitary transformation on Bob’s untrusted side,
U_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{H_{B}}\to\mathcal{H_{B}}
, such that

(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}){|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}

(101)

and

\forall y,\quad U_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\overline{B}_{y},

(102)

where
\mathbb{B}_{y}
is
B_{y}
projected onto the support of Bob’s state
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\overline{B}_{y}\coloneqq\overline{B}_{y}^{(1)}
are given by (
61
).

Proof.

(
Step 1.
) First, let us show that Bob’s measurements are split into a direct sum as

B_{y}=\mathbb{B}_{y}\oplus E_{y},

(103)

where
\mathbb{B}_{y}\coloneqq\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}
is defined in the support of
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
and
E_{i}
belongs to the complement of it. Indeed, we can write
B_{y}
in a block form such as

B_{y}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{B}_{y}&C_{y}\\
D_{y}&E_{y}\end{pmatrix},

(104)

where
E_{y}\coloneqq\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}^{\perp}B_{y}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}^{\perp}
is the projection onto the complement of the support of
\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
. We can use
\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}
onto (
99
) to obtain

A_{k}\otimes\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{B}_{k}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=A_{k}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}},

(105)

where

\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}\coloneqq\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{B}_{k}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(1)}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}\Pi_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(1)}\mathbb{B}_{y}.

(106)

The Eq. (
98
) is valid, in particular, for
n=1
, which together with (
99
) for
n=d-1
provides us

A_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}^{\dagger}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(107)

Now, let us insert (
105
) in the l.h.s. of (
107
) to obtain

\mathbbm{1}_{d}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}={|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(108)

Because the reduced density matrix associated with subsystem
\mathcal{A}
of
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}
is full rank, the above equation is equivalent to

\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}.

(109)

Similar arguments result in
\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
, which proves that
\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}
is unitary. Finally, by applying (
105
) to itself
d
times, we obtain
\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}^{d}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
, which means that
\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}
, for every
k=0,\ldots,d-1
, is a proper quantum observable.

Additionally, we can prove that
\mathbb{B}_{y}
is also unitary. Let us multiply (
105
) by
A_{k}^{\dagger}
to obtain

\mathbbm{1}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=A_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(110)

We can multiply the above equation by
(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(1)})^{*}\coloneqq\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{*}
and use definition (
106
) to write

\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{*}\mathbb{B}_{y}\right){|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{*}A_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(111)

By summing over
k
on both sides and from the fact that

\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(n)}\right)^{*}=\delta_{yy^{\prime}},

(112)

we have

\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{y}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}A_{k}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}},

(113)

where we replaced
y
by
y^{\prime}
for easy of reading. Now, let us multiply the above equation by its own Hermitian conjugate to obtain

\mathbbm{1}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{y}\mathbb{B}_{y}^{\dagger}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}A_{k}^{\dagger}A_{k^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{d}{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}.

(114)

On the r.h.s. of the above we can explicitly calculate the terms

\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}A_{k}^{\dagger}A_{k^{\prime}}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}Z^{k-k^{\prime}}=\sum_{j,k,k^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}\omega^{j(k-k^{\prime})}{|{j}\rangle}\!{\langle{j}|}.

(115)

To prove that the above diagonal operator is equal to the identity, we can directly calculate its coefficients

\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}\gamma_{yk^{\prime}}\omega^{j(k-k^{\prime})}=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{*}\omega^{jk}\right|^{2}=\left|\sum_{k,l=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{d}e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{l\oplus 1,y}}\omega^{k(j-l)}\right|^{2}=\left|e^{\mathbbm{i}\phi_{j\oplus 1},y}\right|^{2}=1,\qquad\forall j,y.

(116)

Therefore
\mathbb{B}_{y}\mathbb{B}_{y}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
. Similar arguments result in
\mathbb{B}_{y}^{\dagger}\mathbb{B}_{y}=\mathbbm{1}_{d}
, which means that
\mathbb{B}_{y}
must be unitary. Since
B_{y}
is unitary as well, we can see from the block decomposition (
104
) that
C_{y}=D_{y}=0
.

(Step 2.)
Now, let us prove the self-testing statement. First, let us write
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}
in its Schmidt decomposition form

{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\lambda_{i}{|{u_{i}}\rangle}{|{v_{i}}\rangle},

(117)

where
\{{|{u_{i}}\rangle}\}
and
\{{|{v_{i}}\rangle}\}
are orthonormal bases of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
, respectively. Also, because
\rank(\rho_\mc{A})=d
, we have
\lambda_{i}>0
,
\forall i
, such that
\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}=1
. Note that, there is a unitary transformation
U_{\mathcal{B}}
that satisfies
U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{v_{i}}\rangle}={|{u_{i}^{*}}\rangle}
,
\forall i
, where
∗
denotes complex conjugation. Therefore, we can rewrite (
117

{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\left(P_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right)\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}{|{u_{i}}\rangle}{|{u_{i}^{*}}\rangle},

(118)

where
P_{\mathcal{A}}
is an operator that is diagonal in the basis
\{u_{i}\}

\sqrt{d}\lambda_{i}
. Note also that because the operators

form a set of genuinely incompatible measurements, we have that

(see Supplemental Material of Ref.
[
30
]
for proofs). Therefore, we can write
{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}
in terms of a maximally entangled state
{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}
as

{|{\psi}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\left(P_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right){|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}.

(119)

Now, we can employ the above equation in (
105
) to obtain

\left(A_{k}P_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right){|{\phi^{+}_{d}}\rangle}_{\mathcal{AB}}=\left(P_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}\right){|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}.

(120)

A quite useful mathematical fact is the following: If
R
and
Q
are any two
d\times d
matrices, then
R\otimes Q{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}=RQ^{T}\otimes\mathbbm{1}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}
. By employing this fact to (

A_{k}P_{\mathcal{A}}\left(U_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right)^{\text{T}}=P_{\mathcal{A}}.

(121)

If we combine the above equation with its Hermitian conjugate

\left(U_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{\mathbb{B}}_{k}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}\right)^{*}P_{\mathcal{A}}A_{k}^{\dagger}=P_{\mathcal{A}},

(122)

we get

A_{k}P_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}A_{k}^{\dagger}=P_{A}^{2},

(123)

where we have used the fact that

is unitary and
P_{\mathcal{A}}
is self-adjoint. The above equation is equivalent to

, because every

is unitary. Also, since

is positive semidefinite, this further imply that
[A_{k},P_{\mathcal{A}}]=0
. By Lemma 1 of the Supplemental Material of Ref.

is proportional to identity. By using this fact in (

A_{k}^{\dagger}

\sum_{y=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(1)}U_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=A_{k}^{*}.

Theorem 2

\mathbb{C}^{d}\otimes\mathcal{H_{B}}
and unitary

-outcome observables
A_{k}
and
B_{y}
(

\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathcal{H_{B}}

(i) Bob’s measurements are projective, that is, all operators

(ii) Bob’s Hilbert space decomposes as
\mathcal{H_{B}}=(\mathbb{C}^{d})_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}}

(iii) there exists a local unitary transformation on Bob’s untrusted side,
U_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{H_{B}}\to\mathcal{H_{B}}

(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}})\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger})={|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\!{\langle{\phi_{d}^{+}}|}_{\mathcal{AB^{\prime}}}\otimes\rho_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}

\forall y,\quad U_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\overline{B}_{y}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B^{\prime\prime}}},

(130)

where
\mathcal{B}^{\prime\prime}
denotes Bob’s auxiliary system and
\overline{B}_{y}\coloneqq\overline{B}_{y}^{(1)}
are given by (

Proof.

(Step 1.)
Let us start by proving that if the Steering inequality is maximally violated, then Bob’s measurements must be projective. From the SOS decomposition (
94

and a state

, then

\left(A_{k}^{n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}},

(131)

for

. In particular,

\left(A_{k}^{d-n}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(d-n)}\right)\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}

also holds. From (
87
) and Fact

, we can see that

\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(d-n)}=\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}

\left[\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}\right]\rho_{\mathcal{AB}}=\rho_{\mathcal{AB}},

(134)

which immediately implies that

\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}.

(135)

Similar arguments lead to
\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}\right)^{\dagger}\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
, so we can conclude that every
\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}
is a unitary operator. From definition (
86
), the above equation becomes

\sum_{y,y^{\prime}=0}^{d-1}\gamma_{yk}^{(n)}\left(\gamma_{y^{\prime}k}^{(n)}\right)^{*}B_{n|y}B_{n|y^{\prime}}^{\dagger}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{B}}.

(136)

If we some over
k
on both sides of the above equation, we can use (

The equations (

(161)

Let us then multiply this equation by its conjugation to obtain

\left|\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{i}p_{0})+\mathrm{exp}(\mathrm{i}p_{1})\right|^{2}=1.

(162)

Appendix E
Robust self-testing

Let us now prove that when the maximal violation of the inequality is not attained by an
\epsilon
, then the measurements performed by Bob, i.e.
B_{y}
, are close to the reference measurements
\overline{B}_{y}
by a factor as a function of
\epsilon
. For simplicity, we will assume that the state is pure and the measurements performed by Bob are projective.

To prove the robustness of our self-testing statement for the qutrit case, we will make use of the fact that

\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(n)}=\widetilde{B}_{k}^{n},\quad\forall n\in\{1,2\}\quad\text{and}\quad d=3,

\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(2)}=\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(1)}\right)^{\dagger}
and
\left(\widetilde{B}_{k}^{(2)}\right)^{3}=\mathbbm{1}

Conjecture 1
.

For any prime
d
, we have that

, for every
n\in\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
. Note that this statement is true for

Having said that, we proceed to the robustness analysis by assuming the above conjecture, keeping in mind that (
170
) holds true for

d=3

Theorem 3

Consider the unitary
d

and
B_{y}

acting on, respectively
\mathbb{C}^{d}

194
) as

\widetilde{B}_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-j}{|{\overline{u}_{j}}\rangle}\!{\langle{\overline{u}_{j}}|}\oplus\widetilde{B}_{k}^{\prime},

(197)

where
\widetilde{B}_{k}^{\prime}
are operators whose support is orthogonal to every
{|{u_{j}}\rangle}
. Since
{|{\overline{u}_{j}}\rangle}
are pair-wise orthogonal, there exists a unitary transformation
U_{\mathcal{B}}
such that
U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\overline{u}_{j}}\rangle}={|{q_{j}^{(k)}}\rangle}
. By applying
U_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\cdot\right)U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}
on both sides of (
197
) we obtain

U_{\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{B}_{k}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{-j}{|{q_{j}^{(k)}}\rangle}\!{\langle{q_{j}^{(k)}}|}\oplus\widetilde{B}_{k}^{\prime\prime}=A_{k}^{*}\oplus\widetilde{B}_{k}^{\prime\prime}.

(198)

Similarly to the previous theorem, the above equation implies that

U_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}=\overline{B}_{y}\oplus\overline{B}_{y}^{\prime\prime}.

(199)

We still need another ingredient before starting to work with the norms. Let us denote
{|{b_{j}^{\prime}}\rangle}=U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{b_{j}}\rangle}
and observe that (
196
) can be written as

\innerproduct{b_j'}{q_j^{(k)}}\geqslant 1-2d\sqrt{2\epsilon}.

(200)

Now, let us prove (
172
) by developing the left side of it as follows:

\left\|U_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}{|{\psi}\rangle}-\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|=\left\|U_{\mathcal{B}}B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}^{\dagger}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|=\left\|\overline{B}_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|=\left\|U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|,

(201)

where the last equality is due to the unitary invariance of the norm. Note that we have omitted the identities in Alice’s space. The Euclidean distance above can be calculated as follows:

\left\|U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|=\sqrt{2\left[1-\text{Re}\left({\langle{\phi^{+}_{d}}|}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\right)\right]}.

(202)

To calculate the bracket above, we can write the maximally entangled state of two qudits in the basis
\{{|{q_{j}^{(k)}}\rangle}\}_{j}
as
{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{j}{|{q_{j}^{(k)}}\rangle}{|{q_{j}^{(k)}}\rangle}
. Therefore, we have

{\langle{\phi^{+}_{d}}|}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}{\langle{\phi_{d}^{+}}|}\alpha_{j}{|{q_{j}^{(k)}}\rangle}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{b_{j}}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{j}\innerproduct{q_i^{(k)}}{q_j^{(k)}}{\langle{q_{i}^{(k)}}|}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{b_{j}}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{j}\innerproduct{q_j^{(k)}}{b_j'}.

(203)

If we use bound (
200
) in the above, we obtain

{\langle{\phi^{+}_{d}}|}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\geqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left(1-2d\sqrt{2\epsilon}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\alpha_{j},

(204)

which together with (
186
) gives

{\langle{\phi^{+}_{d}}|}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\geqslant\left(1-2d\sqrt{2\epsilon}\right)\sqrt{1-\epsilon}.

(205)

By inserting the above inequality in (
202
) we obtain

\left\|U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|\leqslant\sqrt{2\left[1-\left(1-2d\sqrt{2\epsilon}\right)\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\right]}\leqslant 2\sqrt{d}(2\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(206)

Finally, from the above and (
201
) we obtain

\left\|\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes U_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes B_{y}\right){|{\psi}\rangle}-\mathbbm{1}\otimes\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|\leqslant 2\sqrt{d}(2\epsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}

(207)

as desired.

In order to obtain (
173
), first we need to observe that the Euclidean norm of an operator
A
satisfies

\left\|(\mathbbm{1}\otimes A){|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|=\sum_{ij}{\langle{ii}|}\mathbbm{1}\otimes A^{\dagger}A{|{jj}\rangle}=\sum_{i}{\langle{i}|}A^{\dagger}A{|{i}\rangle}=\frac{1}{d}\Tr A^{\dagger}A.

(208)

Therefore, we have that

\left\|B_{y}-\overline{B}_{y}\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{d}\left\|\left(B_{y}-\overline{B}_{y}\right){|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|.

(209)

If we sum and subtract
B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}
inside the norm of the right side of the above, we obtain

\left\|B_{y}-\overline{B}_{y}\right\|_{2}=\sqrt{d}\left\|B_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}-\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}+B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\right\|=\sqrt{d}\left\|B_{y}\left({|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}-U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\right)+B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|

(210)

We can apply the triangular inequality in the last norm to have

\left\|B_{y}-\overline{B}_{y}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\sqrt{d}\left(\left\|{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}-U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}\right\|+\left\|B_{y}U_{\mathcal{B}}{|{\psi}\rangle}-\overline{B}_{y}{|{\phi_{d}^{+}}\rangle}\right\|\right),

(211)

where we have used the fact that norms satisfy unitary invariance. Now, the norms on the right side of the above are bounded by terms specified by Eqs. (
206
) and (
172
), which implies that

\left\|B_{y}-\overline{B}_{y}\right\|_{2}\leqslant 4d\left(2\epsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

(212)

as we wanted to prove.
∎

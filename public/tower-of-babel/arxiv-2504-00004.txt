Finite sums associated with some polynomial identities


Abstract
In this paper, we present a general framework for the derivation of interesting finite combinatorial sums starting with certain classes of polynomial identities. The sums that can be derived involve products of binomial coefficients and also harmonic numbers and squared harmonic numbers. We apply the framework to discuss combinatorial sums associated with some prominent polynomial identities from the recent past.

Motivation and Preliminaries

Harmonic numbers
(H_{n})_{n\geq 0}
are defined by

H_{0}=0\quad\mbox{and for $n\geq 1:$}\qquad H_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

Due to their ubiquitous connections with combinatorics and number theory harmonic numbers
and generalized harmonic numbers are very popular research objects. The mathematical literature is extremely rich,
still growing constantly (see the papers of Batir

In 2009, Boyadzhiev
[
9
]
studied binomial sums with harmonic numbers using the Euler transform.
His main result is the following identity valid for
n\geq 1
and all
t\in\mathbb{C}

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}t^{k}=(1+t)^{n}\left(H_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(1+t)^{k}}\right).

(1)

We have learned recently, however, that Boyadzhiev’s identity (
1
) was discovered earlier by Donald Knuth.
Knuth published it in his book
[
25
, Page 77, Eq. (10)]
in 1997. So, Boyadzhiev actually only rediscovered this identity,
which will be referred to as the Knuth-Boyadzhiev (KB) identity. Frontczak
[
20
]
obtained a complement of the KB identity
given in (
1
) and derived

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}t^{k}=\left((1+t)^{n}-1\right)H_{n}-t\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{n-1-k}(1+t)^{k}.

(2)

In addition, Frontczak
[
21
]
also derived an analogue formula involving the skew-harmonic numbers.
It is also notable that the KB identity is a special case of a binomial sum identity involving multiple
harmonic-like numbers derived by Adegoke and Frontczak
[
3
]
.
Batir
[
7
]
and Batir and Sofo
[
8
]
did research in the same direction. Among other things, they derived in
[
8
]
the identity

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}A_{k}t^{k}=(1+t)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}a_{j+1}t^{j+1}\right)\frac{1}{(1+t)^{k}},

(3)

where
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is any sequence in
\mathbb{C}
, and
A_{n}=a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}
is its
n
th partial sum with
A_{0}=0
.
Identity (
3
) is another generalization of the KB result and contains identity (
1
) as a special
case for
a_{n}=1/n
. We also note that Frontczak’s identity (
2
) is stated wrongly in
[
8
]
. In
[
7
]
Batir proved a generalization of (
3
) involving generalized binomial coefficients.

In this paper, we present a general framework (outlined in Section
3
) for deriving various finite combinatorial sums involving (central) binomial coefficients, harmonic numbers, odd harmonic numbers, and squared harmonic numbers. The framework can be succinctly described as a set of transformations applied to a given polynomial identity (like (
1
) or (
3
)) which leads to many associated combinatorial sums. For example, we will demonstrate that the finite sum

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{k+r}{s}}H_{k}=\frac{s}{r-s+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k\binom{n-k+r}{r-s+1}}-\frac{H_{n}}{\binom{n+r}{r-s+1}}\right),

(4)

which holds for all
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, is a consequence of
(
1
). Identity (
4
) contains several interesting sums as direct descendents and special cases.
Furthermore, we will derive some presumably new identities involving binomial coefficients, such as the following identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{2^{2k}}{2k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{1}{2n+1}

which results from applying the general framework to Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.

We apply the framework to discuss combinatorial sums associated with several prominent polynomial identities from the recent past.
It is important to note that we do not explicitly formulate every possible result based on these identities to avoid repetition
of some overly complex expression. For additional polynomial identities to which our general framework can be applied, as well as more combinatorial sums that can be derived, we refer the reader to Section
6
.

2
Definitions and required identities

Harmonic numbers
H_{s}
and odd harmonic numbers
O_{s}
are defined for
0\neq s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}

by the recurrence relations

H_{s}=H_{s-1}+\frac{1}{s}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{s}=O_{s-1}+\frac{1}{2s-1},

with
H_{0}=0
and
O_{0}=0
. One way to generalize this definition is to consider harmonic numbers
H_{s}^{(m)}

and odd harmonic numbers
O_{s}^{(m)}
of order
m\in\mathbb{C}
that are defined by

H_{s}^{(m)}=H_{s-1}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{s^{m}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{s}^{(m)}=O_{s-1}^{(m)}+\frac{1}{(2s-1)^{m}},

with
H_{0}^{(m)}=0
and
O_{0}^{(m)}=0
so that
H_{s}=H_{s}^{(1)}
and
O_{s}=O_{s}^{(1)}
. The recurrence relations imply that
if
s=n
is a non-negative integer, then

H_{n}^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{j^{m}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{n}^{(m)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2j-1)^{m}}.

In particular, we have the connection between harmonic numbers and the psi (or digamma) function
[
30
]

H_{z}=\psi(z+1)+\gamma,

(5)

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Lemma 1
.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, we have

\int_{0}^{1}x^{u}(1-x)^{v}dx=\frac{1}{\binom{{u+v+1}}{{u+1}}(u+1)}.

(6)

Proof.

The identity (
6
) is the well-known Beta function integral.
∎

Lemma 2
.

If
n
is an integer, then

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}=2O_{n}-2\ln 2

(7)

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-1/2}=2O_{n},

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{1/2}=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-1/2}=2O_{n+1},

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{1/2}=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right),

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{n-1/2}=\frac{2}{{2n+1}},

\displaystyle H_{n-1/2}-H_{-3/2}=2\left({O_{n}-1}\right),

\displaystyle H_{n+1/2}-H_{-3/2}=2\left({O_{n+1}-1}\right).

Proof.

Use (
5
) as the definition of the harmonic numbers in conjunction with the known result for the digamma function
at half-integer arguments
[
30
, Eq. (51)]
, namely,

\psi(n+1/2)=-\gamma-2\ln 2+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2k-1}.

∎

Lemma 3

We have

\displaystyle\binom{{r+1/2}}{s}=\frac{{\binom{{2r+1}}{{2s}}\binom{{2s}}{s}}}{{\binom{{r}}{s}2^{2s}}},

(8)

\displaystyle\binom{{1/2}}{r}=\left({-1}\right)^{r+1}\frac{{\binom{{2r}}{r}}}{{2^{2r}\left({2r-1}\right)}},

\displaystyle\binom{{r-1/2}}{s}=\binom{{2r}}{r}\binom{{r}}{s}\frac{1}{{\binom{{2\left({r-s}\right)}}{{r-s}}2^{2s}}},

(9)

\displaystyle\binom{{-1/2}}{r}=(-1)^{r}\binom{{2r}}{r}2^{-2r},

(10)

\displaystyle\binom{{r}}{{s+1/2}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{{2^{2r+2}}}{{s+1}}\binom{{r}}{s}\binom{{2\left({r-s}\right)}}{{r-s}}^{-1}\binom{{2\left({s+1}\right)}}{{s+1}}^{-1}.

Proof.

3
General results for arbitrary polynomial identities

In this section, we introduce a set of general results that are applicable for a large family of polynomial identities.
The polynomial identities under consideration are of the general form

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)W_{k}(t)=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)V_{k}(t),

(11)

where
W_{k},V_{k}
are polynomials belonging to the following set (we skip the dependence on
k
and
n
to simplify the notation)

BP=\large\{t^{k},t^{n-k},(1-t)^{k},(1-t)^{n-k},t^{k}(1-t)^{n-k},t^{n-k}(1-t)^{k}\large\},

l_{1},l_{2},u_{1},u_{2}
are non-negative integers,
f(k)
and
g(k)
are sequences and
t
is a complex variable.
These will be our standing assumptions for the rest of the paper. To deduce the results in topic, we perform a series of basic
operations in order, which we call
the (general) algorithm
. We describe that in details in the following example.

Example 4
.

Suppose that the polynomial identity contains on one of the sides the following sum:

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)(1-t)^{k}.

(12)

We now multiply that by
(1-t)^{r}t^{s-1}
to obtain

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)(1-t)^{k+r}t^{s-1}.

The next step is to integrate termwise from
0
to
1
and use the Beta integral representation (
6
), which gives

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{1}{s\cdot\binom{k+r+s}{s}}.

The final usual step is to substitute
r\mapsto r-s
and multiply the equation by
s
to eliminate an unwanted factor in the denominator. This gives the final form of the identity without harmonic numbers

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{1}{\binom{k+r}{s}}

(13)

of (
12
).
Note that (
12
) is just a part of some, unspecified, polynomial identity and a set of operations described above is performed on both sides of that identity.

We can now perform one more operation to ”generate” harmonic numbers from (
13
). We differentiate that with
respect to
s
. In case of (
13
) this gives the following sum:

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{s}-H_{k+r-s}}{\binom{k+r}{s}}

(14)

where
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
,
s\neq 0
and
r-s\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, which are again the standing assumptions for these kind of terms. We can also differentiate with respect to
r
, which gives

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{k+r-s}-H_{k+r}}{\binom{k+r}{s}}.

(15)

The above example allows us to transform any identity (
11
) with polynomials in the set
BP
to an identity involving binomial coefficients and harmonic numbers.

The polynomial identities under consideration that can be applied to the described method are of the following form (which we will refer to as
standard forms
)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k},

(16)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)t^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k},

(17)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)(1-t)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k},

(18)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)(1-t)^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k},

(19)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)t^{k}(1-t)^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k},

(20)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)t^{n-k}(1-t)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k}.

(21)

We note that for example

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)(1+t)^{k}=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k}

is not on the above list, but substitution
t\mapsto-t
shows it is of the form (
18
). All standard forms have their right-hand-side equal and it is just a matter of simple changes to work with any possible identity in the BP set.

Let us explicitly formulate the results for identities (
18
) and (
20
), but the very similar results
(with obvious and necessary changes) can be derived and presented for the remaining cases.

Lemma 5
.

Let an arbitrary polynomial identity have the following form:

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)(1-t)^{k}=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k}.

(22)

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
are such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
. Then the following identity holds:

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{1}{\binom{k+r}{s}}=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{s}{r-s+1}\frac{1}{\binom{k+r}{r-s+1}}

(23)

Furthermore, if
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{s}-H_{k+r-s}}{\binom{k+r}{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{s+1}{(r-s+1)^{2}}\frac{1}{\binom{k+r}{r-s+1}}+\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{s}{r-s+1}\frac{H_{k+s-1}-H_{r-s+1}}{\binom{k+r}{r-s+1}}.

(24)

In the above lemma, we differentiated with respect to
s
to obtain (
24
). In the next lemma,
we differentiated with respect to
r
to obtain (
27
).

Lemma 6
.

Let an arbitrary polynomial identity have the following form:

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)t^{k}(1-t)^{n-k}=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)t^{k}.

(25)

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
are such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
. Then the following identity holds:

\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}\frac{f(k)}{(k+s)\binom{n+r}{k+s}}=\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}\frac{g(k)}{(k+s)\binom{k+r}{k+s}}

(26)

Furthermore, if
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{n-k+r-s}-H_{n+r}}{(k+s)\binom{n+r}{k+s}}=\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}g(k)\frac{H_{r-s}-H_{k+r}}{(k+s)\binom{k+r}{k+s}}

(27)

More identities that are special cases of the above and general ones can be deduced. For example, we have the following.

Corollary 7
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}\frac{f(k)}{\binom{k+s}{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}\frac{g(k)s}{k+s},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}\frac{f(k)}{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}\frac{g(k)}{k+1},

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{s}-H_{k}}{\binom{k+s}{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{s+1}{k+s}+\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)s\frac{H_{k+s-1}-1}{k+s},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{1-H_{k}}{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{2}{k+1}+\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{H_{k}-1}{k+1},

Proof.

Substitute
r=s
in Lemma
5
to obtain the first identity in each set.
Then, take
s=1
to obtain the second one.
∎

Similar set of results can be made after applying Lemma
6
.

Corollary 8
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
6
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}\frac{f(k)}{(k+s)\binom{n+s}{k+s}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}\frac{g(k)}{k+s},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}\frac{f(k)}{(k+1)\binom{n+1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}\frac{g(k)}{k+1},

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{n-k}-H_{n+s}}{(k+s)\binom{n+s}{k+s}}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{H_{k+s}}{k+s},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=l_{1}}^{u_{1}}f(k)\frac{H_{n-k}-H_{n+1}}{(k+1)\binom{n+1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{k=l_{2}}^{u_{2}}g(k)\frac{H_{k+1}}{k+1},

The aim of the next sections is to generate identities of the above kind using some initial polynomial identity.

3.1
A simple application

To give the reader an idea of how the general identities could be use and how universal they can be, let us consider the binomial identity

(1-t)^{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}t^{k}

Here, we use Corollary
7
with
s=1
,
l_{1}=u_{1}=n
,
l_{2}=0
,
u_{2}=n
,
f(n)=1
and
g(k)=(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}
. This gives, after some simple algebraic manipulation, the following well-known identities:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{1}{k+1},

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}}{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{n}{k}\frac{H_{k+1}}{k+1}.

(29)

To see the application of Corollary
8
, let us consider

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}t^{k}(1+t)^{n-k}=(t+1)^{n+1}-t^{n+1}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+1}{k}t^{k}.

From that we get:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)\binom{n+1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k+1}\binom{n+1}{k},

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{H_{n-k}-H_{n+1}}{(k+1)\binom{n+1}{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\binom{n+1}{k}\frac{H_{k+1}}{k+1}.

The first identity may look curious, but in fact both sides of it are
0
, which follows directly from (
28
).

4
Applications

4.1
Sums associated with polynomial identities of Knuth-Boyadzhiev and Frontczak

Recall that the identity of Knuth and Boyadzhiev (
1
) can be stated as

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}t^{k}=-(1-t)^{n}H_{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(1-t)^{n-k}}{k},

(A)

whereas Frontczak’s compliment (
2
) has the form

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}H_{k}t^{k}=\left((1-t)^{n}-1\right)H_{n}+t\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}H_{n-1-k}(1-t)^{k}.

(B)

These forms are members of the polynomial family introduced in the previous section. The next results are therefore immediate.

Theorem 9
.

If
n
is a non-negative integer, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{{n}}{k}\frac{(2k+1)}{2^{2k}}\binom{{2k}}{k}H_{k+1}=\frac{\binom{{2n}}{n}}{2^{2n}}\frac{H_{n+1}}{n+1}-\frac{2}{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\binom{2(n-k)}{n-k}}{2^{2(n-k)}k},

(30)

and, more generally, if
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{k+r}{s}}H_{k}=\frac{s}{r-s+1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k\binom{n-k+r}{r-s+1}}-\frac{H_{n}}{\binom{n+r}{r-s+1}}\right).

(31)

Proof.

To obtain (
31
), we apply the general algorithm to (
A
). Identity (
30
) is obtained
by setting
r=-1
,
s=-1/2
in (
31
). Note that we used

\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1}{\binom{{x-1}}{{1/2}}}=0.

∎

Corollary 10
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{k+r}{r}}H_{k}=\frac{r}{n+r}\left(H_{n+r}-H_{r}\right)+\frac{n}{(n+r)^{2}}.

(32)

Proof.

Set
s=r
in (
31
). This gives

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{k+r}{r}}H_{k}=r\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(n-k+r)}-\frac{r}{n+r}H_{n}.

But

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(n-k+r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+r}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{n-k+r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+r}\left(H_{n}+H_{n-1+r}-H_{r-1}\right)

and hence

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{k+r}{r}}H_{k}=\frac{r}{n+r}\left(H_{n-1+r}-H_{r-1}\right).

∎

Remark
.

Corollary
10
is Corollary 1.2 in Chu’s paper
[
16
]
and also Theorem 7 in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 11
.

If
n
is a non-negative integer, then

\begin{split}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\left({-1}\right)^{k}\frac{{2k+1}}{{2^{2k}\left({k+1}\right)}}\binom{{n}}{k}\binom{{2k}}{k}H_{k+1}O_{k+1}}&=-\frac{{\binom{{2n}}{n}}}{{2^{2n}\left({n+1}\right)}}H_{n+1}\left({O_{n}-1}\right)\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{{n+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\frac{{\binom{{2\left({n-k}\right)}}{{n-k}}}}{{2^{2\left({n-k}\right)}k}}\left({O_{n-k}-1}\right)},\end{split}

(33)

and, more generally, if
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}

Proof.

Differentiate (

s

4.4
Sums associated with an identity for the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind

This application deals with sums that are derived using an identity for the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind
U_{n}(t)

defined by

U_{0}(t)=1,\quad U_{1}(t)=2t,\quad U_{n+1}(t)=2tU_{n}(t)-U_{n-1}(t).

From
[
2
, Lemma 41]
we have that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\binom{n+k}{n-k}t^{2k}=(-1)^{n}U_{2n}(t).

Using the representation

U_{n}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}\binom{n+1}{2k+1}(t^{2}-1)^{k}t^{n-2k},

we immediately get the identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\binom{n+k}{n-k}t^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}(1-t)^{k}t^{n-k}

(F)

and the general algorithm can be applied to transform (
F
) into our next results.

Theorem 26
.

If
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{(k+s)\binom{k+r}{k+s}}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{(n-k+s)\binom{n+r}{n-k+s}}.

(61)

Corollary 27
.

For all
r\geq 1
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{k+r}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{(n-k+r)\binom{n+r}{k}}.

(62)

In particular, we have the identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n+1)-1}{2n}\qquad(n\geq 1).

(63)

Proof.

Set
r=s
in Theorem
26
and simplify. The particular example corresponds to
r=1
in conjunction to

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{k+1}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n+1)-1}{2n(n+1)},

where the last expression is true since

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{k+1}=\int_{0}^{1}U_{2n}(\sqrt{t})\,dt=\frac{2n-\cos(\pi n)+1}{2n(n+1)}.

∎

Corollary 28
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{2^{2k}}{2k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{1}{2n+1}

(64)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}2^{2k}\frac{k}{2k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{2n}{(2n+1)(2n-1)}.

(65)

Proof.

Set
r=s=1/2
in Theorem
26
. Using (
8
) this choice gives

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{2k+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2^{2k}}{2(n-k)+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2n+1}{2k}}.

(66)

The binomial coefficients can be simplified to

\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{2n+1}{2k}}=\frac{2(n-k)+1}{2k+1}.

The final step in the proof is to use

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{2k+1}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1},

which follows from

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{2k+1}=\int_{0}^{1}U_{2n}(t)\,dt=\frac{\sin(\pi n)+1}{2n+1}.

This completes the proof of the first identity. The second follows as a combination with

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{1}{2n-1}.

∎

Corollary 29
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2^{2k}}{2(n-k)+3}\frac{\binom{n+1}{k}\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2n+3}{2k}}=\frac{(-1)^{n}(4n^{2}+4n-1)}{(2n-1)(2n+1)(2n+3)}.

(67)

Proof.

Set
r=s=3/2
in Theorem
26
and use (
8
) again. The left hand side can be evaluated as

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{2k+3}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}U_{2n}(t)\,dt=\frac{4n^{2}+4n-1}{(2n-1)(2n+1)(2n+3)}.

∎

Corollary 30
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}k\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if $n=1$};\\
\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n+1)(2n-1)-(2n^{2}+1)}{4(n-1)n},&\text{if $n\geq 2$}.\end{cases}

(68)

Proof.

Set
r=2
in (
62
). After simplification of the binomial coefficients this results in the relation

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{k+2}=\frac{1}{(n+2)(n+1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(n+1-k)\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}}.

The left hand side equals

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{k+2}=2\int_{0}^{1}t^{3}U_{2n}(t)\,dt=\frac{(2n+1)(2n(n+1)-3)+3(-1)^{n}}{4(n-1)n(n+1)(n+2)}

and hence

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}(n+1-k)\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}}=\begin{cases}-5,&\text{if $n=1$};\\
\frac{(-1)^{n}(2n+1)(2n(n+1)-3)+3}{4(n-1)n},&\text{if $n\geq 2$}.\end{cases}

The final step is to split this sum into two parts, use the sum identity from (
63
) and simplify.
∎

Remark
.

Similar combinatorial sums were studied recently by Chu and Guo
[

]
. They have proved, among other things, the evaluations

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n+2}{2k+1}}{\binom{n}{k}}=2\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\frac{\binom{n+2}{2k}}{\binom{n}{k}}=-\frac{2}{n}.

Theorem 31
.

If
s,r\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}
such that
s\neq 0
and
r-s\not\in\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{(k+s)\binom{k+r}{k+s}}H_{k+r}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{(n-k+s)\binom{n+r}{n-k+s}}(H_{k+r-s}-H_{n+r}-H_{r-s}).

(69)

Proof.

Differentiate (
61
) with respect to
r
and simplify.
∎

Corollary 32
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{k+r}H_{k+r}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}}{(n-k+r)\binom{n+r}{k}}(H_{k}-H_{n+r})

and

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{2^{2k+1}}{2k+1}\binom{n+k}{n-k}O_{k+1}\\
=\frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{2n+1}O_{n+1}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2^{2k}}{2k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}H_{k}.

Proof.

Set
r=s
in (
69
). The particular case follows from setting
r=\tfrac{1}{2}
, which yields with (
8
)

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}2^{2k}\frac{\binom{n+k}{n-k}}{2k+1}(2O_{k+1}-2\ln 2)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{2^{2k}}{2k+1}\frac{\binom{n}{k}}{\binom{2k}{k}}(H_{k}-2O_{n+1}+2\ln 2).

We use (
66
) to remove logarithms. Finally, we use (
64
) to simplify the right-hand side and the result follows.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this article, we have introduced a general framework (algorithm) to study combinatorial sums associated with certain classes of polynomial identities. The sums involve products of binomial coefficients and also harmonic numbers. To underline its usefulness we have shown applications based on some prominent polynomial identities from the recent past. This is only the starting point and many new families of sums can be studied. We invite the readers to apply the framework to sums associated with

•

the identity of Simons
[
28
]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}t^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n+k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k}(1+t)^{k}

•

the Narayana polynomials
[
26
,
27
]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k-1}\binom{n}{k}t^{k}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n+1}{k}\binom{2n-k}{n}(1-t)^{k}

•

an identity involving central binomial coefficients
[
23
]

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{2(n-k)}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2k}{k}t^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2k}{k}\binom{2(n-k)}{n-k}(1+t)^{k}

and many more.

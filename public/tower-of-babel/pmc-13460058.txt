Epidemiological characteristics and vaccination history analysis of pertussis in Quzhou from 2005 to 2024
Authors: Fei Zhao, Wenjie Xu, Bo Chen, Canya Fu, Canjie Zheng, Xiaojuan Lin, Zhiying Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDespite high vaccination coverage, pertussis is reemerging in China and increasingly affecting older children. This shift challenges current immunization strategies. To inform better prevention, we aimed to investigate pertussis epidemiology in Quzhou, with a focus on analyzing factors linked to the time between vaccination and illness onset among diagnosed cases.MethodsWe analyzed pertussis cases from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention (CISDCP) in Quzhou City (2005–2024) and linked them to vaccination records from the Zhejiang Immunization Program Smart Service Information System. The outcome variable was the time from the last vaccine dose to pertussis diagnosis.ResultsDuring 2005–2024, Quzhou reported 2,045 pertussis cases, with 93.25% concentrated in 2024. Case distribution shifted from 0 to 4 to ≥ 5 years (P < 0.001). Age‑specific incidence rose markedly in all groups in 2024 (all P < 0.001), with the highest rate observed among 5–9‑year‑olds (878.99/100,000). Among cases with known vaccination history, 83.86% had received at least one dose. Multivariable Cox regression showed that vaccination status and vaccine type were significantly associated with pertussis risk. Pertussis risk was 45% lower after the fourth dose than before it (P = 0.017). For vaccine type comparisons versus DTaP, the hazard ratio for pertussis was 0.11 for DTwP, 2.09 for DTaP-Hib, and 3.28 for DTaP-IPV/Hib (all P < 0.001).ConclusionsThe 2024 pertussis outbreak in Quzhou showed a marked age shift toward school-aged children, with the highest incidence in 5-9-year-olds. Our findings support adding a booster dose at 6 years of age and reconsidering multivalent DTaP-containing vaccines.

Background

Pertussis (whooping cough) is an acute respiratory infection caused by Bordetella pertussis, characterized by prolonged coughing spells that may last for weeks. Although pertussis is globally endemic with epidemic peaks every 2–5 years [1], the widespread use of pertussis-containing vaccines has substantially reduced its incidence [2]. However, a resurgence has been observed in recent years [3, 4]. The peak incidence of pertussis is shifting from infancy to adolescence, and adolescents may become a primary source of infection for younger infants [5].

Vaccination remains the most effective prevention method. Since 1978, China’s national immunization program included the traditional diphtheria, tetanus and whole-cell pertussis combined vaccine (DTwP) [6]. Starting in 2007, DTwP was gradually replaced by the diphtheria, tetanus and acellular pertussis combined vaccine (DTaP), which was fully adopted by 2013 due to its lower adverse reaction profile [7]. The standard DTaP schedule comprises four doses: at 3, 4, 5, and 18 months of age [8]. In response to the 2024 resurgence, the schedule was updated to five doses (2, 4, 6, 18 months, and 6 years), effective January 1, 2025 [9].

Quzhou City implemented its immunization program in 1980. The DTwP-to-DTaP transition occurred between May 2006 and July 2008. All schedules include a three-dose primary series between 3 and 6 months of age, followed by a booster at 18–24 months [10]. Despite sustained vaccination coverage above 99% in Quzhou, pertussis incidence remained low for many years but surged dramatically in 2024, raising concerns about vaccine protection. The objectives of this study are to describe the epidemiological characteristics of the 2005–2024 pertussis outbreak in Quzhou (including age distribution, incidence rates, and geographic patterns) and to analyze factors associated with the time from vaccination to disease onset among reported cases, including sex, vaccination status (before vs. after the fourth dose), and vaccine type.

Methods

Data sources and definition

Pertussis case data were obtained from the China Information System for Disease Control and Prevention (CISDCP), a mandatory reporting system for notifiable diseases. The extracted variables included sex, date of birth, date of onset, case classification, and disease severity. We first obtained pertussis cases from the surveillance system and then retrieved their vaccination histories using national ID numbers. Vaccination history (vaccine type, dates of 1 st to 4th doses) was obtained from the Zhejiang Immunization Program Smart Service Information System. Population denominators were obtained from the Quzhou Statistics Bureau.

According to the Diagnostic Criteria for Pertussis (WS274–2007), pertussis cases were classified into three hierarchical categories: suspected cases (meeting clinical criteria), clinically diagnosed cases (suspected cases with leukocytosis), and confirmed cases (clinically diagnosed cases with laboratory confirmation, including PCR, culture, or serology) [11]. This study included only confirmed and clinically diagnosed cases; suspected cases were excluded. For time-to-event analysis, which included Kaplan-Meier (K-M) curves and Cox regression, we further restricted to cases who had completed primary immunization (at least three doses). The outcome variable was the time from the last vaccine dose (third or fourth) to pertussis diagnosis.

Variable definition

Sex was classified as male or female based on physiological sex. Primary immunization was defined as receipt of three pertussis-containing doses, and full immunization as receipt of four doses. For patients who received different pertussis vaccine types across doses, the vaccine type administered in the final dose was used for analysis. Vaccine types were classified as: DTwP, DTaP, DTaP-Hib, or DTaP-IPV/Hib, based on the pertussis-containing vaccines that have been used in China. Fourth-dose status was modeled as a time-varying covariate because the timing of fourth dose administration varies among individuals and dynamically affects the risk period. Each subject contributed person-time to the “before fourth dose” stratum from the third dose until the fourth dose administration date, and to the “after fourth dose” stratum thereafter. To ensure baseline homogeneity for evaluating factors associated with disease onset, the time-to-event analysis was restricted to cases who had completed primary immunization (at least three doses). The observation period ran from January 1, 2005, to December 31, 2024.

Statistical analysis

We established a database using Microsoft Excel 2021 for all reported pertussis cases in Quzhou City, and performed statistical analyses using SPSS 25.0 (SPSS Inc., USA) and R Studio 2024.09.0–375.0 (Lucent Technologies, USA). We used the chi-square test for intergroup comparisons. A survival analysis was conducted on pertussis cases in Quzhou (2005–2024) who had received at least the primary vaccine series, with the time-to-event defined as the interval from the last vaccination to disease onset. Covariates included sex, vaccination status (before 4th dose [reference] versus after 4th dose), and vaccine types (coded as 0 = DTaP [reference], 1 = DTwP, 4 = DTaP-Hib, 5 = DTaP-IPV/Hib). We used K-M curves to visualize time-to-event distributions, with log-rank tests for group comparisons. A time-dependent Cox proportional hazards model was used to analyze vaccination status as a time-varying exposure. Multivariable Cox regression was subsequently performed to assess associations between covariates and disease onset, and the results are presented as hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). A sensitivity analysis was performed to assess the robustness of the findings. All tests were two-sided, with a significance level α = 0.05, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Epidemiological characteristics

From 2005 to 2024, a total of 2,045 cases of pertussis were reported in Quzhou City, of which 549 cases (26.85%) were in the age group of 0 to 4 years, 1,102 cases (53.89%) in the age group of 5 to 9 years, 197 cases (9.63%) in the age group of 10 to 14 years, and 197 cases (9.63%) in those aged 15 years and older. There were no reported deaths. The annual reported incidence ranged from 0 to 83.02 per 100,000 population (Fig. 1).

From 2005 to 2023, the number of reported cases remained below 60. However, starting in 2024, the reported cases significantly increased, reaching 36 times that of 2023. From 2005 to 2023, the number of reported cases peaked mainly from March to August and from November to December. Cases began to rise from January to June, peaking in June with a total of 21 cases. This was followed by fluctuations, with another peak occurring in August, after which the numbers declined. A rebound was observed from November to December.

Notably, this dramatic increase coincided with a substantial shift in diagnostic methods. The distribution of diagnostic methods differed markedly between the two periods. Among the 138 cases from 2005 to 2023, 105 (76.09%) were laboratory‑confirmed, including 51 (36.96% of all cases in this period) confirmed by PCR and 5 (3.62%) confirmed by bacterial culture, while the specific diagnostic method was not specified for the remaining 49 cases (35.51%). In contrast, among the 1907 cases in 2024, the proportion of laboratory‑confirmed cases was extremely high (1,888 cases, 99.00%), with the vast majority confirmed by PCR (1,767 cases, 92.66% of all cases in that year); the method was unspecified for 140 cases (7.34%) (P < 0.001).

In Table 1, we can see the characteristics of pertussis cases in Quzhou City by sex, age group, and region from 2005 to 2024. During this period, there were 999 cases among males and 1,046 cases among females, resulting in a male-to-female ratio of 1:1.05. From 2005 to 2023, this ratio was 1:1.26; it changed to 1:1.03 in 2024. Between 2005 and 2024, the age range of reported pertussis cases in Quzhou was from 22 days to 90 years. The proportion of cases in the 0–4 age group decreased from 64.49% during 2005–2023 (89/138) to 24.12% in 2024 (460/1907), while the proportion of cases in the group aged 5 years and older showed an upward trend. The distribution of pertussis cases in Quzhou City spans 6 counties (cities and districts). From 2005 to 2023, the highest and lowest total number of cases were in Changshan County and Kaihua County, with 44 cases and 10 cases, respectively. In 2024, the highest and lowest reported cases were in Kecheng District with 964 cases and Changshan County with 84 cases.

We calculated age-specific incidence rates of pertussis in Quzhou during 2005–2024, and performed statistical analysis (Table 2). All age groups showed significant incidence increases in 2024 compared to 2005–2023, with all P-values below 0.001. The most notable change occurred in 5–9-year-olds. Their incidence rate reached 878.99/100,000 (P < 0.001), which is 440 times higher than the historical level and exceeds the rate in the traditional high-risk group of 0–4-year-olds (540.99/100,000) (P < 0.001). The outbreak also expanded to older age groups. The 10–14-year-old group experienced a nearly 600-fold increase (167.08/100,000) (P < 0.001), while the ≥ 15-year-old population showed a clear rise from previously minimal levels (9.91/100,000) (P < 0.001).

Pertussis vaccination status

An investigation into pertussis vaccination status revealed that among 2,045 cases, 16.1% (330 cases) had no history of pertussis vaccination (including some with unknown status), while 83.9% (1,715 cases) had a history of pertussis vaccination. Among the 1,715 cases with a vaccination history, 1,544 received 4 doses (complete primary and booster immunization), 101 received 3 doses (primary immunization only), 23 received 2 doses, and 47 received 1 dose. Among children under 5 and under, the vaccination rate was 85.0% (665/782) for receiving 3 or more doses in cases aged 3 months and above, and 95.3% (572/600) for receiving 4 doses in cases aged 18 months and above (Table 3).

Survival analysis

The K-M curves showed that vaccination status (HR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.36–0.92, P = 0.022) and vaccine type (P < 0.001) were significant factors affecting the post-vaccination disease onset interval, with no association observed for sex (P = 0.378) (Fig. 2). Using a time-dependent Cox model, the period after the 4th dose was associated with a 45% lower risk of pertussis compared with the period before the 4th dose (HR = 0.55, 95% CI: 0.34–0.90, P = 0.017). Regarding vaccine type, DTwP was associated with an 89% lower risk compared with DTaP (HR = 0.11, 95% CI: 0.06–0.24, P < 0.001), though caution is needed as only 12 patients received it. In contrast, DTaP-Hib (HR = 2.09, 95% CI: 1.70–2.57, P < 0.001) and DTaP-IPV/Hib (HR = 3.28, 95% CI: 2.82–3.82, P < 0.001) were associated with an increased risk. Sex was not associated with pertussis risk (female vs. male: HR = 1.04, 95% CI: 0.94–1.15, P = 0.422).

Sensitivity analysis

To assess the robustness of our findings, we performed a sensitivity analysis excluding cases with mixed vaccination histories (n = 1,607) using the same time-dependent Cox model as the primary analysis. The results were consistent with the primary analysis: the period after the fourth dose remained associated with a lower risk (HR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.35–0.93, P = 0.024); DTwP (HR = 0.03, 95% CI: 0.01–0.12, P < 0.001), DTaP-Hib (HR = 2.23, 95% CI: 1.79–2.78, P < 0.001), and DTaP-IPV/Hib (HR = 3.35, 95% CI: 2.88–3.91, P < 0.001) showed similar associations as in the primary analysis. Sex remained not significantly associated (HR = 1.05, 95% CI: 0.95–1.16, P = 0.314).

Discussion

Quzhou City witnessed a remarkable epidemiological transition in its pertussis outbreak in 2024, marked by a sudden resurgence following nearly two decades of sustained low incidence (2005–2023). In our study of the 2024 outbreak, the age distribution shifted from 0 to 4 years toward children aged ≥ 5 years, with the highest incidence rate observed among 5–9-year-olds (878.99/100,000). Multivariable Cox regression analysis of vaccination-related factors further revealed that the fourth dose reduced pertussis risk by 45% (P = 0.017), and compared with DTaP, DTwP was associated with a significantly lower risk (HR = 0.11), whereas DTaP-Hib (HR = 2.09) and DTaP-IPV/Hib (HR = 3.28) were associated with higher risks (all P < 0.001).

This resurgence likely stems from multiple interacting factors. In Quzhou, the DTwP-to-DTaP vaccine switch was gradually completed from May 2006 to July 2008. Compared to whole-cell pertussis (wP) vaccines, acellular pertussis (aP) vaccines induce distinct immune responses, with wP vaccines mounting protective mucosal immunity by priming respiratory resident memory T cells to secrete IgA, which blocks bacterial colonization in the nasal cavity [12]. Waning immunity from DTaP has led to declining protection in older children [13]. Household transmission poses significant exposure risks to unvaccinated infants, often stemming from atypical cases—such as vaccinated adolescents/adults with mild or subclinical symptoms—that evade detection [14]. The 2023 revision of national standards established PCR as an independent confirmatory method, leading to reclassification of previously “probable” cases. Enhanced molecular surveillance has revealed a substantial reservoir of pertussis infections in urban settings, particularly among highly vaccinated populations where transmission persists despite attenuated clinical manifestations [15–17].

Notably, our study identified distinct age-stratified patterns, with disease burden concentrated in infants aged 0 years (incomplete primary immunization) and children aged 5–9 years (waning vaccine-induced immunity). This bimodal distribution aligns with the understanding that pertussis epidemiology results from the interplay between immunization gaps and school-based exposure risks [18, 19]. The incidence peak among 5–9-year-olds exposes deficiencies in the current immunization program’s protection for this age group [20]. Additionally, the rightward shift in case age distribution toward adolescents and adults underscores the need to reevaluate population-wide immunity protection strategies [5, 21].

The immunity gap caused by the COVID-19 pandemic may be a significant factor contributing to the observed increase in pertussis cases across multiple countries since 2023 [22]. Mask-wearing and social distancing limited exposure to Bordetella pertussis, indirectly weakening immunity [23], while pandemic-related declines or delays in vaccination coverage further diminished vaccine protection [22, 24]. This led to the resurgence of pertussis cases when restrictions were lifted. The surge in Kecheng District, Quzhou City may be attributed to urban factors including higher population density, greater population mobility, higher rates of missed vaccinations among migrant children, concentration of medical institutions, and enhanced disease surveillance capabilities [17].

Both K-M curves and Cox regression demonstrated that vaccination status and vaccine type were significant determinants of pertussis risk, while no correlation was observed with sex. The observed reduction in pertussis risk after the fourth dose is consistent with evidence that complete vaccination sustains long-term immunity through repeated antigen exposure, immune maturation, and strengthened immunological memory [25]. These findings support modifying the current pertussis vaccination schedule from 3,4,5,18 months to 2,4,6,18 months, with an additional booster dose at 6 years of age.

Cox analysis showed a significantly lower risk of pertussis after DTwP vaccination compared to DTaP, consistent with evidence that wP induces broader and more durable immune responses by presenting a complete antigenic profile [26]. The study found that both DTaP-Hib and DTaP-IPV/Hib vaccines demonstrated inferior protective efficacy compared to DTaP. This may be due to antigenic interference, as the Hib component could disrupt the immune response to pertussis antigens [27, 28]. Additionally, the imported DTaP-IPV/Hib vaccine used in China contains only PT and FHA, lacking PRN, which may contribute to its limited duration of protection [29]. Adjuvant sharing among multiple antigens might further reduce pertussis immunogenicity. Therefore, caution is warranted when replacing DTaP with multivalent vaccines in routine immunization, particularly in settings with ongoing pertussis transmission.

Unanswered questions requiring future research include whether the protective advantage of DTwP over DTaP observed in this study (based on only 12 DTwP cases) is confirmed in larger, adequately powered studies with balanced sample sizes; the precise mechanisms of antigenic interference in multivalent pertussis-containing vaccines, particularly how the Hib component disrupts immune responses to pertussis antigens; whether the 2024 epidemic surge represents a structural break in transmission dynamics, such that hazard estimates from epidemic periods may not generalize to non-epidemic periods; the role of atypical, subclinical cases in sustaining household transmission to vulnerable infants, which warrants integrated immunological monitoring and long-term follow-up; and whether the rightward shift in case age distribution toward adolescents and adults necessitates booster doses beyond childhood, and what the optimal schedule would be.

This study has several strengths and limitations. A strength of this study is that it provides real-world data from an unexpected resurgence following nearly two decades of low incidence, offering actionable evidence for local and national immunization policy. Sensitivity analyses further supported the robustness of our main findings. Several limitations should also be noted. Firstly, and most fundamentally, it is crucial to reiterate that our survival analysis was conducted among confirmed pertussis cases. The estimated hazard ratios, therefore, reflect differences in the timing of disease onset among individuals who became infected, rather than measuring the effectiveness of the vaccine in reducing the risk of infection in the general population. Secondly, we must acknowledge the significant imbalance in sample sizes across vaccine groups, with the DTaP vaccine group being disproportionately represented. Additionally, the 2024 epidemic surge may represent a structural break in transmission dynamics. As a substantial proportion of observations originate from this period, our hazard estimates could reflect the intensified transmission of the epidemic phase in addition to, or instead of, vaccination effects. This context should be considered when generalizing the results to non-epidemic periods. Finally, reliance on passive surveillance systems for case data collection, combined with the pre-2023 exclusion of PCR testing from diagnostic standards, likely contributed to incomplete case detection and potential misclassification of mild infections.

Conclusions

The 2024 pertussis outbreak in Quzhou demonstrated a marked shift in the age distribution toward school-aged children, with the highest incidence rate observed among those aged 5 to 9 years. These findings support the addition of a booster dose at 6 years of age, as well as a reconsideration of the value of using multivalent DTaP-containing vaccines.

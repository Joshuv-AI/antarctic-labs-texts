Characterizations of harmonic quasiregular mappings in function spaces Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Jihua Sun, Junming Liu and Zhi-Gang Wang ∗

Abstract
. We study conjugate-type phenomena for complex-valued harmonic quasiregular mappings in the unit disk across three function space families: Q ⁡ ( n , p , α ) Q(n,p,\alpha) , F ⁡ ( p , q , s ) F(p,q,s) , and the non-derivative M ⁡ ( p , q , s ) M(p,q,s) . For a harmonic K K -quasiregular mapping f = u + i ​ v f=u+iv , we first show that if the real part u u belongs to Q h ​ ( 1 , p , α ) Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha) (with α > − 1 \alpha>-1 and α + 1 < p < α + 2 \alpha+1<p<\alpha+2 ), the imaginary part v v lies in the same space with a K K -dependent quantitative bound. An analogous stability result is established for the harmonic F F -scale, with sharp K K -dependence. These results are extended to harmonic ( K , K ′ ) (K,K^{\prime}) -quasiregular mappings, yielding explicit estimates with an additional inhomogeneous term involving K ′ K^{\prime} . Finally, for normalized harmonic quasiconformal mappings, we derive membership criteria in the harmonic M M - and F F -scales, and obtain corresponding conclusions for their natural derivatives, with parameter ranges governed by the order α K \alpha_{K} of the family of harmonic quasiconformal mappings.

Let

\mathbb{C}

\partial\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}

the space of all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
, endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets. For

and

with

is independent of

Furthermore, we adopt the convention
(bf_{b})(0)=0
, ensuring that both

and

are well-defined on
\mathbb{D}

For

f_{z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{x}-if_{y}\right),\quad f_{\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(f_{x}+if_{y}\right),

For

\partial_{\theta}f(z)=f_{z}(z)e^{i\theta}+f_{\bar{z}}(z)e^{-i\theta}.

Accordingly, we set

\Lambda_{f}(z):=\max_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|\partial_{\theta}f(z)|=|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|

and

\lambda_{f}(z):=\min_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}|\partial_{\theta}f(z)|=\bigl||f_{z}(z)|-|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|\bigr|.

For

(1.1)

The function

-quasiregular and homeomorphic in

\mathbb{D}

A mapping

\Omega

The Jacobian of

Following the standard definition, we say that

(K,K^{\prime})
-quasiregular mapping in

In particular, when

If, in addition,

and the (second) complex dilatation is

The mapping is sense-preserving in

Problems of conjugate type originate from classical results for analytic functions.
Regarding the Hardy spaces, the celebrated theorem of Riesz asserts that harmonic conjugation is bounded on
H^{p}

for
1<p<\infty
, see e.g.,
[
12
]
.
At the endpoints, one needs finer substitutes (weak-type and BMO-type replacements),
and the real-variable
H^{p}
framework provides a systematic language for such phenomena (cf.
[
12
,
15
]
).
In contrast, Hardy-Littlewood showed that if
f=u+iv
is analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, then
membership of
u
in the (analytic) Bergman space
A^{p}
forces
v
to belong to the same space for every

0<p<\infty
, see
[
16
]
(and also the standard accounts
[
14
,
33
]
).
In other words, Bergman-type integrability behaves markedly better than Hardy-type integrability
with respect to taking conjugates.

For general complex-valued harmonic mappings, these analytic conclusions do not carry over verbatim.
A key insight in the recent literature is that geometric control of the mapping restores
conjugate-type principles in the harmonic setting:
quasiconformality/quasiregularity turns out to be a natural sufficient hypothesis for Riesz-, Kolmogorov-, and Zygmund-type statements for harmonic mappings, see e.g.,
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
17
,
18
]
.
Here we follow
[

]
and say that

(K,K^{\prime})
-quasiregular in a domain
\Omega

f
is harmonic in
\Omega
and
(K,K^{\prime})
-quasiregular in
\Omega
, where
K\geq 1
and
K^{\prime}\geq 0
are constants.

Within this direction, several recent contributions sharpen and extend the classical paradigm.
For instance, Das-Rasila
[

Related quantitative estimates for univalent (or locally univalent) harmonic mappings are developed in

Very recently, Chen-Huang-Wang-Xiao

2.
Preliminaries

In order to prove our main results, we need the following definitions and lemmas.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
-1<\alpha<\infty
, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer. Denote by
Q(n,p,\alpha)
the space of all analytic functions
f
in
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|^{p}_{n,p,\alpha}:=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl|(f\circ\sigma_{a})^{(n)}(z)\bigr|^{p}\,(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)<\infty,

where

\sigma_{a}(z)=\dfrac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}

is a Möbius automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
and
dA
is the area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.

The space
Q(n,p,\alpha)
is Möbius invariant in the sense that an analytic function
f
belongs to
Q(n,p,\alpha)
if and only if
f\circ\sigma
belongs to
Q(n,p,\alpha)
for every
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, moreover,

\|f\circ\sigma\|_{n,p,\alpha}=\|f\|_{n,p,\alpha},\quad f\in Q(n,p,\alpha),\quad\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{D}).

When
p\geq 1
,
|f(0)|+\|f\|_{n,p,\alpha}
defines a complete norm on
Q(n,p,\alpha)
, hence
Q(n,p,\alpha)
is a Banach space; when
0<p<1
,
Q(n,p,\alpha)
is not necessarily Banach but it is always complete as a metric space. Furthermore,
Q(n,p,\alpha)
is trivial when
np>\alpha+2
, it contains all polynomials when
np\leq\alpha+2
, and it coincides with the Besov space
B_{p}
when
np=\alpha+2
; in addition,
Q(n,p,\alpha)\subset\mathcal{B}
(the Bloch space). As special identifications, if
np<\alpha+1
, then
Q(n,p,\alpha)=\mathcal{B}
, while if
p=2
and
\alpha=2n-1
, then
Q(n,p,\alpha)=\mathrm{BMOA}
. In the special case
n=1
and
p=2
, one may apply a change of variables to rewrite

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl|(f\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)

in the equivalent form

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\,(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z),

so that the corresponding spaces
Q(n,p,\alpha)
reduce to the classical
Q_{\alpha}
scale. More generally, if
2n<\alpha+3
, then

Q(n,2,\alpha)=Q_{\beta},~\beta=\alpha-2(n-1).

Standard references
[
28
]
provide further background on the theory of
Q_{\alpha}
spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
31
]
)
Let
p,q
and
s
be real constants such that
0<p<\infty,-2<q<\infty
and
0<s<\infty.
We say that a function
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
belongs to the space
F(p,q,s)
if

\|f\|_{p,q,s}^{p}=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{q}g^{s}(z,a)\mathrm{d}A(z)<\infty,

where

g(z,a)=-\log\left|\sigma_{a}(z)\right|=\log\left|\frac{1-\bar{a}z}{a-z}\right|

is the Green’s function of
\mathbb{D}
with logarithmic singularity at
a\in\mathbb{D}.

Originating in Zhao’s work
[
32
]
, the three-parameter family
F(p,q,s)
provides a unified framework that encompasses a host of classical analytic spaces-Hardy, Bergman, Dirichlet, Besov, Bloch and
BMOA
among them-allowing one to move seamlessly between them by tuning
(p,q,s).
Moreover, the following characterizations of
F(p,q,s)
using Carleson measure are from
[
32
, Theorem 3.3]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty,-2<q<\infty,0<s<\infty
satisfy
q+s>-1,
let
f
be analytic on
\mathbb{D},
and let

d\mu_{f}(z)=\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{q+s}dA(z).

Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)~~f\in F(p,q,s);

(ii)~\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{q}\left(1-\left|\sigma_{a}(z)\right|^{2}\right)^{s}\mathrm{~d}A(z)<\infty;

(iii)~~d\mu_{f}\text{is a bounded }
s-
Carleson measure.

Moreover, we have

\|f\|_{p,q,s}^{p}\approx\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{q}\left(1-\left|\sigma_{a}(z)\right|^{2}\right)^{s}dA(z).

This scheme specializes to many familiar identifications, for example
F(2,0,1)=BMOA.
Thereby highlighting the role of
F(p,q,s)
as a common umbrella for Möbius-invariant and weighted theories.

Definition 2.3
.

For
-2<q<\infty,~0<s<\infty,~q+s>-1.
associated with the
F(p,q,s)
spaces, the spaces
M(p,q,s)
consist of
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
such that

\|f\|_{M(p,q,s)}=|f(0)|+\left(\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{q}\left(1-\left|\sigma_{a}(z)\right|^{2}\right)^{s}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Clearly, when
p\geq 1,

M(p,q,s)
is a Banach space with the above norm.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
-1<\alpha<\infty
, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer.
A complex-valued harmonic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to belong to the harmonic space
Q_{h}(n,p,\alpha)
if

\|f\|_{Q_{h}(n,p,\alpha)}^{p}:=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Bigl(\bigl|(h\circ\sigma_{a})^{(n)}(z)\bigr|+\bigl|(g\circ\sigma_{a})^{(n)}(z)\bigr|\Bigr)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)<\infty,

where
f=h+\overline{g}
is the canonical decomposition with
h,g\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
g(0)=0.

In particular, when
n=1
, one may write equivalently

\displaystyle\|f\|_{Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Lambda_{\,f\circ\sigma_{a}}(z)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\approx\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl|\nabla(f\circ\sigma_{a})(z)\bigr|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z),

where

\Lambda_{f}(z):=|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|

and

|\nabla f(z)|:=\sqrt{|f_{x}(z)|^{2}+|f_{y}(z)|^{2}},

and the pointwise comparability

\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq|\nabla f(z)|\leq\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{f}(z)

holds for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
-2<q<\infty
,
0<s<\infty
, and
q+s>-1
.
A complex-valued harmonic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to belong to the harmonic space
M_{h}(p,q,s)
if

\|f\|_{M_{h}(p,q,s)}:=|f(0)|+\left(\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\bigl(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\bigr)^{s}\,dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
-2<q<\infty
,
0<s<\infty
, and
q+s>-1
.
A complex-valued harmonic function
f
in
\mathbb{D}
is said to belong to the harmonic space
F_{h}(p,q,s)
if

\|f\|_{F_{h}(p,q,s)}^{p}:=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Lambda_{f}(z)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\,g(z,a)^{s}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Equivalently, one may replace
\Lambda_{f}
by the (real) gradient size

|\nabla f(z)|:=\sqrt{|f_{x}(z)|^{2}+|f_{y}(z)|^{2}},

since

\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq|\nabla f(z)|\leq\sqrt{2}\Lambda_{f}(z)

for all

\|f\|_{F_{h}(p,q,s)}^{p}\approx\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\Lambda_{f}(z)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\bigl(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\bigr)^{s}\,dA(z).

\displaystyle\approx|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\left(1-|a|^{2}\right)^{2-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{p}\left|\sigma_{a}^{\prime}(z)\right|^{2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx|f(0)|+\sup_{I\subset\partial\mathbb{D}}\left(\frac{1}{|I|^{\lambda}}\int_{S(I)}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{p}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx|f(0)|+\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|^{p}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{p-\lambda}\left(1-\left|\sigma_{a}(z)\right|^{2}\right)^{\lambda}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Throughout this paper, we write
z=be^{i\theta}
with
b=|z|\in[0,1)
.
For a (sufficiently smooth) complex-valued harmonic function
f
on
\mathbb{D}
,
we denote by
f_{b}=\partial f/\partial b
and
f_{\theta}=\partial f/\partial\theta

the radial and angular derivatives, respectively, and we use the product
bf_{b}=b\,\partial f/\partial b
.
This notation is chosen to avoid confusion with the exponent parameter
r
appearing in spaces such as
M(r,q,s)
and
F(r,q,s)
.
Moreover, in terms of Wirtinger derivatives, one has the identities

-if_{\theta}=zf_{z}-\overline{z}\,f_{\bar{z}},\quad bf_{b}=zf_{z}+\overline{z}\,f_{\bar{z}}.

In particular, if
f=h+\overline{g}
with
h,g\in H(\mathbb{D})
, then
f_{z}=h^{\prime}
and
f_{\bar{z}}=\overline{g^{\prime}}
, and hence

-if_{\theta}=zh^{\prime}(z)-\overline{z}\,\overline{g^{\prime}(z)},\quad bf_{b}=zh^{\prime}(z)+\overline{z}\,\overline{g^{\prime}(z)}.

3.
Growth of conjugate-type functions for harmonic quasiregular mappings

In this section, we establish conjugate-type stability for harmonic quasiregular mappings in several families of function spaces.
Our first goal is to show that, under quasiregularity, membership of the real part in a given scale forces the same membership for the imaginary part, with quantitative control.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
\alpha+1<p<\alpha+2
.
Suppose that
f=u+iv
is a harmonic
K
-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}
.
Assume that
u\in Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)
,
then
v\in Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)
and

\left\|v\right\|_{Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)}\leq K\left\|u\right\|_{Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
f(0)=0
. Write
f=h+\overline{g}
, and for each
a\in\mathbb{D}
, define

f_{\sigma_{a}}:=f\circ\sigma_{a}=h_{\sigma_{a}}+\overline{g_{\sigma_{a}}},

where
h_{\sigma_{a}}=h\circ\sigma_{a}
and
g_{\sigma_{a}}=g\circ\sigma_{a}
.

Since
u\in Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)
, it follows that for every
a\in\mathbb{D}
,

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl|\nabla(u\circ\sigma_{a})(z)\bigr|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Set
F:=h+g
and
G:=h-g
. Then

u=\Re(f)=\Re(h+\overline{g})=\Re(h+g)=\Re(F)

and

v=\Im(f)=\Im(h+\overline{g})=\Im(h-g)=\Im(G).

Consequently,

u\circ\sigma_{a}=\Re(F\circ\sigma_{a})
and
v\circ\sigma_{a}=\Im(G\circ\sigma_{a})
, and hence

\bigl|(F\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|=\bigl|\nabla(u\circ\sigma_{a})(z)\bigr|,\quad\bigl|(G\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|=\bigl|\nabla(v\circ\sigma_{a})(z)\bigr|.

Let
w={g^{\prime}}/{h^{\prime}}
. Since
f
is
K
-quasiregular, we have
|w|\leq k<1
, where
k
is given by (
1.1
).
Moreover,

F^{\prime}=h^{\prime}+g^{\prime}=h^{\prime}(1+w),\quad G^{\prime}=h^{\prime}-g^{\prime}=h^{\prime}(1-w).

Using the elementary inequalities

|1-w|\leq 1+|w|

and

|1+w|\geq 1-|w|\geq 1-k,

we obtain

|G^{\prime}(z)|\leq|h^{\prime}(z)|(1+k),\quad|F^{\prime}(z)|\geq|h^{\prime}(z)|(1-k),

and therefore

|G^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1+k}{1-k}\,|F^{\prime}(z)|=K|F^{\prime}(z)|\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

For any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, by the chain rule, we have

\bigl|(G\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|=\bigl|G^{\prime}(\sigma_{a}(z))\bigr|\,\bigl|\sigma_{a}^{\prime}(z)\bigr|\leq K\bigl|F^{\prime}(\sigma_{a}(z))\bigr|\,\bigl|\sigma_{a}^{\prime}(z)\bigr|=K\bigl|(F\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|.

Raising to the power
p
, multiplying by
(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}
, integrating over
\mathbb{D}
, and taking the supremum over
a\in\mathbb{D}
, we obtain

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl|(G\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)\leq K^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl|(F\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z).

Using

\bigl|(F\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|=\bigl|\nabla(u\circ\sigma_{a})(z)\bigr|

and

\bigl|(G\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)\bigr|=\bigl|\nabla(v\circ\sigma_{a})(z)\bigr|,

this shows that
v\in Q_{h}(1,p,\alpha)
and yields the desired estimate.
∎

The next theorem shows that the same principle persists in the three-parameter family
F_{h}(p,q,s)
, which unifies several classical spaces as its special cases.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
-2<q<\infty
and
0<s<\infty
satisfy

.

f=u+iv
is a harmonic
K
-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}

Assume that
u\in F_{h}(p,q,s)

then
v\in F_{h}(p,q,s)
and

\left\|v\right\|_{F_{h}(p,q,s)}\leq K\left\|u\right\|_{F_{h}(p,q,s)}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
f(0)=0
. Write
f=h+\overline{g}
and set

F:=h+g,\quad G:=h-g.

Since
u\in F_{h}(p,q,s)
, it follows that for every
a\in\mathbb{D}
,

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\nabla u(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\bigl(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\bigr)^{s}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Taking the supremum over
a\in\mathbb{D}

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|G^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\bigl(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\bigr)^{s}\,dA(z)\leq K^{p}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|F^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\bigl(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2}\bigr)^{s}\,dA(z).

Using

and

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>-1
and
\alpha+1<p<\alpha+2
. Suppose that
f=u+iv
is a harmonic
(K,K^{\prime})
-quasiregular mapping in
\mathbb{D}

where

C_{p,\alpha}:=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\sigma_{a}^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
f(0)=0
. Write
f=h+\overline{g}
with
h,g\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
g(0)=0
. Set

F_{1}:=h+g,\quad F_{2}:=h-g.

Then
u=\Re(f)=\Re(F_{1})
and

a\in\mathbb{D}

|(F_{1}\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)|=|\nabla(u\circ\sigma_{a})(z)|,\quad|(F_{2}\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)|=|\nabla(v\circ\sigma_{a})(z)|.

\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq K\lambda_{f}(z)+\sqrt{K^{\prime}}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Noting that

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq\mu_{1}|h^{\prime}(z)|+\mu_{2},\quad\mu_{1}=\frac{K-1}{K+1},\quad\mu_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{K^{\prime}}}{1+K}.

Consequently,

|F_{2}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\Lambda_{f}(z)\leq(1+\mu_{1})|h^{\prime}(z)|+\mu_{2},\quad|F_{1}^{\prime}(z)|\geq\lambda_{f}(z)\geq(1-\mu_{1})|h^{\prime}(z)|-\mu_{2},

\displaystyle|(F_{2}\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)|

(A+B)^{p}\leq 2^{\max\{p-1,0\}}(A^{p}+B^{p}),

\displaystyle=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|(F_{2}\circ\sigma_{a})^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}\,dA(z)

4.
Harmonic quasiconformal mappings in function spaces

In this section, we derive integrability and growth consequences for normalized harmonic quasiconformal mappings
f
in
\mathcal{S}_{H}(K)
.
The key input is the distortion theory encoded in the order
\alpha_{K}
, which governs the admissible parameter ranges in the
M_{h}(p,q,s)
and
F_{h}(p,q,s)
scales considered below.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{H}(K)
for some
K\geq 1
. Then

(i)

f\in M_{h}(p,q,s)
for all

p<\min\left\{\frac{q+s+1}{\alpha_{K}},\,\frac{q+2}{\alpha_{K}}\right\};

(4.1)

(ii)

f_{z},\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}\in M(r,q,s)
,
f_{\theta},\,bf_{b}\in M_{h}(r,q,s)
for all

r<\min\left\{\frac{q+s+1}{\alpha_{K}+1},\,\frac{q+2}{\alpha_{K}+1}\right\}.

(4.2)

Proof.

We write
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
.

(i) Fix
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
. Define

F_{\zeta}(z)=\frac{f\!\left(\frac{z+\zeta}{1+\overline{\zeta}z}\right)-f(\zeta)}{(1-|\zeta|^{2})\,h^{\prime}(\zeta)}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Then
F_{\zeta}\in\mathcal{S}_{H}(K)
, and

F_{zz}(0)=(1-|\zeta|^{2})\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)}{h^{\prime}(\zeta)}-2\overline{\zeta}.

Since
|F_{zz}(0)|\leq 2\alpha_{K}
, we obtain

\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)}{h^{\prime}(\zeta)}\right|\leq\frac{C}{1-|\zeta|}\quad(\zeta\in\mathbb{D}),

equivalently,

\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(re^{i\theta})}{h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})}\right|\leq\frac{C}{1-r}\quad(0\leq r<1).

On the other hand, arguing as in
[
13
, p. 98]
,

|h^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})|\leq\frac{(1+r)^{\alpha_{K}-1}}{(1-r)^{\alpha_{K}+1}},\quad|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+1}}.

Since
w={g^{\prime}}/{h^{\prime}}
satisfies
|w|\leq k<1
, we have

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+1}}.

Moreover, integrating
h^{\prime}
radially yields

|h(re^{i\theta})-h(0)|\leq\int_{0}^{r}|h^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\,dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha_{K}+1}}\,dt\lesssim(1-r)^{-\alpha_{K}},

which implies that

|h(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}}},\quad|g(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}}},

therefore,

|f(z)|\leq|h(z)|+|g(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}}}.

For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, set

\displaystyle I_{p}(a)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\,(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}\,dA(z).

By
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
, we see that

\displaystyle I_{p}(a)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{q+s-p\alpha_{K}}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}}\,dA(z).

By setting

t:=q+s-p\alpha_{K},\quad c:=s-q-2+p\alpha_{K},

then

2+t+c=2s.

If
t>-1
,
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
yields the three cases

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{t}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2+t+c}}\,dA(z)\lesssim\begin{cases}1,&c<0,\\[2.84526pt]
\log\!\dfrac{1}{1-|a|^{2}},&c=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{-c},&c>0,\end{cases}

and consequently,

I_{p}(a)\lesssim\begin{cases}(1-|a|^{2})^{s},&c<0,\\[2.84526pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\log\!\dfrac{1}{1-|a|^{2}},&c=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{s-c}=(1-|a|^{2})^{2+q-p\alpha_{K}},&c>0.\end{cases}

Suppose that (
4.1
) holds. Then
t>-1
and
2+q-p\alpha_{K}>0
, so each of the above three bounds is uniformly bounded in
a\in\mathbb{D}
(note that
s>0
).
Therefore,

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}I_{p}(a)<\infty,

which shows that
f\in M_{h}(p,q,s)
.

(ii) From part (i), we find that

|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+1}},

hence

|h^{\prime}(z)|^{r}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{r(\alpha_{K}+1)}}.

For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, set

\displaystyle I_{r}(a)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{r}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\,(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{q+s-r(\alpha_{K}+1)}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}}\,dA(z).

If we put

t:=q+s-r(\alpha_{K}+1),\quad c:=s-q-2+r(\alpha_{K}+1),

then

2+t+c=2s.

Applying
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
exactly as in part (i), we obtain (for
t>-1
)

I_{r}(a)\lesssim\begin{cases}(1-|a|^{2})^{s},&c<0,\\[2.84526pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\log\!\dfrac{1}{1-|a|^{2}},&c=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{s-c}=(1-|a|^{2})^{2+q-r(\alpha_{K}+1)},&c>0.\end{cases}

Assume that (
4.2
) holds. Then
t>-1
and
2+q-r(\alpha_{K}+1)>0
. Hence

\sup\limits_{a\in\mathbb{D}}I_{r}(a)<\infty

and
f_{z}=h^{\prime}\in M(r,q,s)
.

Next, since
|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|
, we know that

|g^{\prime}(z)|^{r}\leq k^{r}|h^{\prime}(z)|^{r},\quad\|g^{\prime}\|_{M(r,q,s)}\lesssim k\|h^{\prime}\|_{M(r,q,s)},

which implies that
\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}=g^{\prime}\in M(r,q,s)
.

Finally, using

-if_{\theta}=zh^{\prime}(z)-\overline{z}\,\overline{g^{\prime}(z)},\quad bf_{b}=zh^{\prime}(z)+\overline{z}\,\overline{g^{\prime}(z)},

we have

|f_{\theta}(z)|\leq|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1+k)\,|h^{\prime}(z)|,\quad|bf_{b}(z)|\leq|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1+k)\,|h^{\prime}(z)|.

Raising to the power
r
and using
h^{\prime}\in M(r,q,s)
yields
f_{\theta},\,bf_{b}\in M_{h}(r,q,s)
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{H}(K)
for some
K\geq 1
. Then

(i)

f\in F_{h}(p,q,s)
for all

p<\min\left\{\frac{q+s+1}{\alpha_{K}+1},\,\frac{q+2}{\alpha_{K}+1}\right\};

(4.3)

(ii)

f_{z},\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}\in F(r,q,s)
,
f_{\theta},\,bf_{b}\in F_{h}(r,q,s)
for all

r<\min\left\{\frac{q+s+1}{\alpha_{K}+2},\,\frac{q+2}{\alpha_{K}+2}\right\}.

(4.4)

Proof.

We keep the notation of the statement and write
f=h+\overline{g}
with
h,g\in H(\mathbb{D})
.

By the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we obtain

|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+1}},\quad\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}.

Let
w={g^{\prime}}/{h^{\prime}}
be the analytic dilatation of
f
. Since
|w|\leq k<1
in
\mathbb{D}
, we get

|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq k|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+1}}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Moreover,

|h^{\prime\prime}(z)|\leq\left|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}\right|\,|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+2}}.

Applying Schwarz–Pick lemma to
\varphi={w}/{k}
gives

|w^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}},

and hence

|g^{\prime\prime}(z)|=|w^{\prime}(z)h^{\prime}(z)+w(z)h^{\prime\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+2}}.

Since
f_{z}=h^{\prime}
and
f_{\bar{z}}=\overline{g^{\prime}}
, we have

|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|=|h^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1+k)|h^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+1}}.

(i) For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, set

I_{p}(a):=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigl(|f_{z}(z)|+|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|\bigr)^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}\,dA(z).

Then

\displaystyle I_{p}(a)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{q-p(\alpha_{K}+1)}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}\,dA(z)

\displaystyle=(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{q+s-p(\alpha_{K}+1)}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2s}}\,dA(z).

If we take

t:=q+s-p(\alpha_{K}+1),\quad c:=s-q-2+p(\alpha_{K}+1),

then
2+t+c=2s
. Applying
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
as in the proof of (i) of Theorem
4.1
, we obtain (for
t>-1
)

I_{p}(a)\lesssim\begin{cases}(1-|a|^{2})^{s},&c<0,\\[2.84526pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{s}\log\!\dfrac{1}{1-|a|^{2}},&c=0,\\[5.69054pt]
(1-|a|^{2})^{s-c}=(1-|a|^{2})^{q+2-p(\alpha_{K}+1)},&c>0.\end{cases}

Assume that (
4.3
) holds. Then
t>-1
and
q+2-p(\alpha_{K}+1)>0
. Hence

\sup\limits_{a\in\mathbb{D}}I_{p}(a)<\infty,

which shows that
f\in F_{h}(p,q,s)
.

(ii) Since
f_{z}=h^{\prime}
is analytic, it suffices to show that

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|h^{\prime\prime}(z)|^{r}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}(1-|\sigma_{a}(z)|^{2})^{s}\,dA(z)<\infty.

Using

|h^{\prime\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+2}}

and arguing as above, we reduce to the same three-case estimate in
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
with

t:=q+s-r(\alpha_{K}+2),\quad c:=s-q-2+r(\alpha_{K}+2),

and obtain uniform boundedness in
a
provided

q+s-r(\alpha_{K}+2)>-1,\quad q+2-r(\alpha_{K}+2)>0,

which is equivalent to (
4.4
). Hence
f_{z}=h^{\prime}\in F(r,q,s)
.

Since
\overline{f_{\bar{z}}}=g^{\prime}
, it remains to verify
g^{\prime}\in F(r,q,s)
, i.e., the defining integral condition for
g^{\prime\prime}
.
This follows from

|g^{\prime\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha_{K}+2}}

by the same argument and under the same condition (
4.4
).

Finally, using

-if_{\theta}=zh^{\prime}(z)-\overline{z}\,\overline{g^{\prime}(z)},\quad bf_{b}=zh^{\prime}(z)+\overline{z}\,\overline{g^{\prime}(z)},

differentiation yields

|(f_{\theta})_{z}|+|(f_{\theta})_{\bar{z}}|\lesssim|h^{\prime}(z)|+|h^{\prime\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime}(z)|+|g^{\prime\prime}(z)|,

and similarly for
|(bf_{b})_{z}|+|(bf_{b})_{\bar{z}}|
. Hence by the above pointwise bounds, we obtain

\bigl(|(f_{\theta})_{z}|+|(f_{\theta})_{\bar{z}}|\bigr)^{r}\lesssim(1-|z|^{2})^{-r(\alpha_{K}+2)},\quad\bigl(|(bf_{b})_{z}|+|(bf_{b})_{\bar{z}}|\bigr)^{r}\lesssim(1-|z|^{2})^{-r(\alpha_{K}+2)}.

Applying
[
33
, Lemma 3.10]
once more as above, we conclude that

f_{\theta},\,bf_{b}\in F_{h}(r,q,s)

whenever (
4.4
) holds.
This finishes the proof of part (ii), and thus of Theorem
4.2
as well.
∎

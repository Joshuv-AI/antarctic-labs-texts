Ninh Tran

Abstract
. Partial conjunction (PC) hypothesis testing is widely used to assess the replicability of scientific findings across multiple comparable studies. In high-throughput meta-analyses, testing a large number of PC hypotheses with k k -family-wise error rate ( k k -FWER) control often suffers from low statistical power due to the multiplicity burden. The state-of-the-art AdaFilter-Bon procedure by Wang et al., 2022 alleviates this problem by filtering out hypotheses unlikely to be false before applying a rejection rule. However, a side effect of filtering is that it renders the rejection rule more stringent than necessary, leading to conservative k k -FWER control. In this paper, we mitigate this conservativeness—and thereby improve the power of AdaFilter-Bon—by incorporating a post-filter null proportion estimate into the procedure. The resulting method, AdaFilter-AdaBon, has proven asymptotic k k -FWER control under weak dependence and demonstrates empirical finite-sample control with higher power than the original AdaFilter-Bon in simulations.

2.
Problem formulation

2.1.
Problem statement

Suppose we are conducting a meta-analysis that consists of
n\geq 2
comparable studies, each of which examines the same set of
m>1
features. For each feature
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
of study
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, suppose there is a null hypothesis
H_{ij}
for which we observe a corresponding
valid

p
-value
P_{ij}
, i.e.,

\text{$\Pr(P_{ij}\leq t)\leq t$ for all $t\in[0,1]$ if $H_{ij}$ is true}.

Since a meta-analysis typically consists of independently conducted studies, we assume that the vectors
(P_{i1})^{m}_{i=1}
,
\dots
,
(P_{in})^{m}_{i=1}
are independent.

For a given
replicability level

u\in\{2,\dots,n\}
, feature
i
is said to be
u/n

replicated
if at least
u
of the
n
hypotheses among
H_{i1},\dots,H_{in}
are false. We consider a problem where the objective is to identify
u/n
replicated features by testing the partial conjunction (PC) null hypothesis
(
Benjamini and Heller, 2008
)
:

H^{u/n}_{i}:\text{ Less than $u$ null hypotheses among $H_{i1},\dots,H_{in}$ are false},

for
i=1,\dots,m
. This multiple testing problem is often referred to as a
replicability analysis
. Note that whenever
u^{\prime\prime}\geq u^{\prime}
, the following nesting property holds:

(2.1)

\text{$H_{i}^{u^{\prime}/n}$ is true}\implies\text{$H_{i}^{u^{\prime\prime}/n}$ is true}.

Let
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
be the set of PC null hypotheses rejected by a multiple testing procedure. For any given tolerance level
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
k
-family-wise error rate (
k
-FWER) of
\mathcal{R}
is defined as

k{\text{-}}\text{FWER}\equiv\Pr\left(\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{i\in\mathcal{R}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\geq k\right),

which is the probability that the number of false discoveries is greater than or equal to
k
. When
k=1
, the
k
-FWER is simply referred to as the
FWER
. The power of
\mathcal{R}
can be evaluated using the true positive rate (TPR),

\text{TPR}\equiv\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{i\in\mathcal{R}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is false}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is false}\}}\right],

which is the expected proportion of true discoveries among the false PC nulls. Our goal is to develop a powerful multiple testing procedure for replicability analysis that operates on

(2.2)

(P_{ij})_{m\times n}\equiv(P_{ij})_{(i,j)\in\{1,\dots,m\}\times\{1,,\dots,n\}},

and controls the
k
-FWER below a pre-specified target level
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

2.2.
k
-FWER procedures for replicability analysis

A valid
p
-value for testing
H^{u/n}_{i}
—referred to as a
PC
p
-value
and denoted as
P^{u/n}_{i}
—can be constructed by
combining
the
p
-values associated with feature
i
. That is,

P^{u/n}_{i}\equiv f(P_{i1},P_{i2},\dots,P_{in};u),

where
f:[0,1]^{n}\to[0,1]
is a combining function satisfying

\Pr(f(P_{i1},P_{i2},\dots,P_{in})\leq t)\leq t\quad\text{ for all $t\in[0,1]$ if $H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true.}

Benjamini and Heller, 2008
proposed a general methodology for constructing a valid combined
p
-value for testing
H^{u/n}_{i}
, which
Wang and Owen, 2019
later extended. An example of a
P^{u/n}_{i}
from this methodology is the Bonferroni-combined PC
p
-value,

f_{\text{Bon}}(P_{i1},\dots,P_{in};u)=(n-u+1)P_{i(u)},

where
P_{i(1)}\leq\cdots\leq P_{i(n)}
are the order statistics of
P_{i1},\dots,P_{in}
. Further examples and their numerical performance can be found in
Hoang and Dickhaus, 2022
.

Given the problem statement in Section
2.1
, the
k
-FWER can be controlled by applying a multiple testing procedure on the PC
p
-values
(P^{u/n}_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
. Arguably, the simplest
k
-FWER method is the (generalised) Bonferroni procedure introduced in
Lehmann and Romano, 2005
:

Definition 1

(Bonferroni)
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
be a
k
-FWER target for a given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(P^{u/n}_{i})^{m}_{i=1}
be PC
p
-values. For
i=1,\dots,m
, reject
H^{u/n}_{i}
if

(2.3)

P^{u/n}_{i}\leq k\cdot\frac{\alpha}{m}.

Although less powerful than the subsequent methods developed by
Romano and Shaikh, 2006
,
Sarkar, 2007
,
Sarkar, 2008
, the Bonferroni procedure provides a useful illustration of the multiplicity burden: as the number of PC nulls
m
grows, the rejection threshold in (
2.3
) becomes increasingly stringent.

The state-of-the-art AdaFilter-Bon procedure
(
Wang et al., 2022
)
reduces the multiplicity burden by using a
filter
to eliminate features unlikely to be
u/n
replicated, before applying a rejection rule. Although originally developed for FWER control, we describe below a generalisation of AdaFilter-Bon that controls
k
-FWER control for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
2
2

2

When
k=1
, Definition
2
recovers the original AdaFilter-Bon procedure of
Wang et al., 2022
.
.

Definition 2

(AdaFilter-Bon)
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
be a
k
-FWER target for a given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
u\in\{2,\dots,m\}
be a replicability level. For
i=1,\dots,m
, let

(2.4)

S_{i}\equiv f_{\text{Bon}}(P_{i1},\dots,P_{in};u)

be a Bonferroni-combined PC
p
-value, and let

(2.5)

\displaystyle F_{i}\equiv\frac{n-u+1}{n-u+2}\cdot f_{\text{Bon}}(P_{i1},\dots,P_{in};u-1)=(n-u+1)P_{i(u-1)}

be a corresponding filtering
p
-value. For each
i
, reject
H^{u/n}_{i}
if
S_{i}<\hat{t}
, where

(2.6)

\hat{t}\equiv\sup\left\{t\in[0,k\alpha]:t\cdot\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}\leq k\alpha\right\}.

Since
F_{i}\leq S_{i}
, it follows from Definition
2
that
H^{u/n}_{i}
is filtered out (i.e. cannot be rejected) if
F_{i}\in[\hat{t},1]
. This is a sensible filtering approach, since
F_{i}
is closely related to
f_{\text{Bon}}(P_{i1},\dots,P_{in};u-1)
, which is a valid PC
p
-value for testing
H_{i}^{(u-1)/n}
. Consequently,
F_{i}\in[\hat{t},1]
indicates that
H_{i}^{(u-1)/n}
is likely true, making feature
i
unlikely to be
u/n
replicated by the nesting property in (
2.1
).

We also observe that AdaFilter-Bon can only reject
H^{u/n}_{i}
if

(2.7)

S_{i}<\hat{t}\leq k\cdot\frac{\alpha}{\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}<\hat{t}\}}.

The right-hand side of (
2.7
) shows that
\hat{t}
is inversely proportional to the number of features remaining after filtering,
\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}I\{F_{\ell}<\hat{t}\}
, rather than to
m
. Hence, in comparison to the Bonferroni procedure (Definition
1
), there is a reduction in the multiplicity burden due to filtering.

2.3.
Preview of contribution

By design, features not filtered out by AdaFilter-Bon,
\{\ell\in\{1,\dots,m\}:F_{\ell}<\hat{t}\}
, are more likely to correspond to false PC nulls than those filtered out. Consequently, there is a tendency for the
post-filter null proportion
,

(2.8)

\pi_{0}(\hat{t})\equiv\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\hat{t}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\hat{t}\}}\in[0,1],

to be small. This phenomenon, together with the theorem below, reveals that AdaFilter-Bon has a propensity to control the
k
-FWER conservatively:

Theorem 2.1

(AdaFilter-Bon
k
-FWER control)
.

If
(P_{ij})_{m\times n}
is a collection of independent valid
p
-values, then AdaFilter-Bon (Definition
2
) has the following
k\text{-FWER}
property:

(2.9)

k\text{-FWER}(\mathcal{R})\leq\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\pi_{0}(\hat{t})\right]\in[0,\alpha],

where
\mathcal{R}=\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:S_{i}\leq\hat{t}\}
and
\pi_{0}(\hat{t})
is as defined (
2.8
).

Theorem
2.1
highlights that AdaFilter-Bon’s
k
-FWER level can fall well below
\alpha
when
\hat{\pi}_{0}(\hat{t})
is close to zero, i.e., when the filter removes many PC nulls that are unlikely to be false. Hence,
filtering effectively comes at the cost of more conservative
k
-FWER control
. The proof of Theorem
2.1
is provided in Appendix
A.2
; for
k=1
, the
k
-FWER upper bound presented in (
2.9
) is tighter than the one presented in Theorem 4.2 of
Wang et al., 2022
, who simply only proved that AdaFilter-Bon controls the FWER at level
\alpha
.

The thrust of this paper is to estimate
\pi_{0}(t)
for
t\in[0,1]
and incorporate it into AdaFilter-Bon’s rejection rule to bring its
k
-FWER control level closer to
\alpha
, thereby yielding a more powerful multiple testing procedure. Specifically, we replace the AdaFilter-Bon rejection threshold defined in (
2.6
) with

(2.10)

\hat{t}_{\theta}\equiv\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:\hat{\pi}_{0}(t)\cdot t\cdot\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}\leq k\alpha\right\}

instead, where
\hat{\pi}_{0}(t)\geq 0
is a data-adaptive estimate of
\pi_{0}(t)
. Heuristically, we expect the threshold in (
2.10
) to yield rejections with a
k
-FWER level bounded above by

\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\pi_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})}{\hat{\pi}_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})}\right],

rather than the right-hand side of (
2.9
). Thus, to ensure
k
-FWER control at level
\alpha
,
\hat{\pi}_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})
should ideally satisfy
\hat{\pi}_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})\approx\pi_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})
, or at least
\hat{\pi}_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})\gtrsim\pi_{0}(\hat{t}_{\theta})
. In light of the data-
ada
ptive post-filter null proportion estimator used in forming (
2.10
), we refer to this new multiple testing procedure as AdaFilter-
Ada
Bon.

3.
AdaFilter-AdaBon

3.1.
AdaFilter-AdaBon methodology

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
be a fixed tuning parameter. By the validity of Bonferroni-combined PC
p
-values, it holds that
\Pr(S_{i}>\theta t)\geq 1-\theta t
for all
t\in[0,1]
if
H^{u/n}_{i}
is true. Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1-\theta t)\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}.

Rearranging the above display yields our estimator for
\pi_{0}(t)
:

(3.1)

\hat{\pi}_{0}(t)\equiv\hat{\pi}_{0}(t;\theta)=\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}.

As the value of
\theta
increases while
t
remains constant, the bias of
\hat{\pi}_{0}(t)
decreases and its variance increases. In our experiments, setting
\theta=0.5
generally strikes a good balance in this bias–variance trade-off. Having defined
\hat{\pi}_{0}(t)
, we now formally present the AdaFilter-AdaBon procedure below:

Definition 3

(AdaFilter-AdaBon)
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
be a
k
-FWER target for a given
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in\{2,\dots,m\}
be a replicability level,
\theta\in(0,1)
be a tuning parameter, and
S_{i}
and
F_{i}
be as defined in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), respectively. For
i=1,\dots,m
, reject
H^{u/n}_{i}
if
S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}
, where

(3.2)

\displaystyle\hat{t}_{\theta}\equiv\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{1-\theta t}\leq k\alpha\right\}.

Note that (
3.2
) is equivalent to the threshold previewed in (
2.10
) of Section
2.3
when
\hat{\pi}_{0}(t)
is defined as (
3.1
). To simplify the implementation of AdaFilter-AdaBon in practice, we recommend defining

\mathcal{G}=\left\{G\in\{0,1\}\cup\{F_{i},S_{i},S_{i}/\theta\}^{m}_{i=1}:G\leq 1\right\}

and using the rejection threshold

(3.3)

\breve{t}_{\theta}\equiv\max\left\{t\in\mathcal{G}:t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{1-\theta t}\leq k\alpha\right\}

as a surrogate for
\hat{t}_{\theta}
. The exact value of
\breve{t}_{\theta}
can be computed in
O(m)
time by evaluating the set-building condition in (
3.3
) for each element of
\mathcal{G}
. Although
\breve{t}_{\theta}\not\equiv\hat{t}_{\theta}
, the theorem below states that
\breve{t}_{\theta}
and
\hat{t}_{\theta}
yield the same rejection set when thresholding the PC
p
-values in
(S_{i})^{m}_{i=1}
.

Theorem 3.1

(
\breve{t}_{\theta}
as a surrogate for
\hat{t}_{\theta}
)
.

Let
\hat{t}_{\theta}
and
\breve{t}_{\theta}
be as defined in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), respectively. It holds that

\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\right\}=\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:S_{i}<\breve{t}_{\theta}\right\}.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
, given in Appendix
B.2
, is based on the fact that
\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}
and
\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:S_{i}<t\right\}
are both step functions in

3.2.
Asymptotic results for AdaFilter-AdaBon

In this subsection, we provide the result showing that AdaFilter-AdaBon (Definition
3
) asymptotically controls the
k
-FWER at level
\alpha
as
m\to\infty
. The following assumption underpins our asymptotic result:

Assumption 1
.

For any given
m
, let

m_{0}\equiv\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\quad\text{and}\quad m_{1}\equiv\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is false}\}

be the number of true and false PC nulls, respectively. The following limits exist almost surely for each
t\in(0,t]
:

(3.4)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m_{0}}=\tilde{S}_{0}(t),\quad\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m_{0}}=\tilde{F}_{0}(t)

(3.5)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is false}\}}{m_{1}}=\tilde{S}_{1}(t),\quad\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is false}\}}{m_{1}}=\tilde{F}_{1}(t),

where
\tilde{S}_{0},\tilde{F}_{0},\tilde{S}_{1}
, and
\tilde{F}_{1}
are continuous function. For any
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in(0,1]
where
t^{\prime}\leq t^{\prime\prime}
, it holds that

(3.6)

0<\tilde{S}_{0}(t^{\prime})\leq t^{\prime}\tilde{F}_{0}(t^{\prime\prime}).

Moreover, the following limit also exists:

(3.7)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\frac{m_{0}}{m}=\pi_{0}\in(0,1).

The convergence properties stated in (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and (
3.7
) are typical in the asymptotic analysis literature; for example, see
Storey et al., 2003
,
Wang et al., 2022
, and
Zhang and Chen, 2022
. Any type of dependence where (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) can hold is what we consider weak dependence. For instance, dependence in finite-dimensional blocks, autoregressive dependence, and mixing distributions are candidates for weak dependence. The properties relating to
\tilde{S}_{0}
and
\tilde{F}_{0}
in (
3.6
) are not standard in the asymptotic analysis literature but arise naturally from the
conditionally validity
of Bonferroni-combined PC

Lemma 3.2

(Conditional validity)
.

Suppose
H^{u/n}_{i}
is true and the
p
-values
P_{i1},\dots,P_{in}
are all valid and mutually independent. Then for any fixed
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}\in[0,1]
where
t^{\prime}\leq t^{\prime\prime}
, we have that

(3.8)

\Pr(S_{i}<t^{\prime}|F_{i}<t^{\prime\prime})=\frac{\Pr(S_{i}<t^{\prime},F_{i}<t^{\prime\prime})}{\Pr(F_{i}<t^{\prime\prime})}=\frac{\Pr(S_{i}<t^{\prime})}{\Pr(F_{i}<t^{\prime\prime})}\leq t^{\prime}

if
\Pr(F_{i}<t^{\prime})>0
.

Rearranging (
3.8
) gives
\Pr(S_{i}<t^{\prime})\leq t^{\prime}\cdot\Pr(F_{i}<t^{\prime\prime})
when
H^{u/n}_{i}
is true, which is analogous to the property stated in (
3.6
) when (
3.4
) and (
3.7
) hold. The proof for Lemma
3.2
is provided in Appendix
B.1
.

Below, we present our asymptotic result, which states that if
k
remains a constant proportion of
m
as
m\to\infty
, then AdaFilter-AdaBon asymptotically controls the
k
-FWER below
\alpha
.

Theorem 3.3

(AdaFilter-AdaBon

-FWER control)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds and

k\equiv k(m)=\omega\cdot m

for a constant
\omega\in(0,1)
. If there exists a threshold
t\in(0,1]
such that

(3.9)

\lim_{m\to\infty}t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}<\omega\alpha

with probability

, then AdaFilter-AdaBon (Definition
3
) has the following asymptotic
k
-FWER property:

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(k\text{-FWER}(\mathcal{R})\right)\leq\alpha,

where
\mathcal{R}=\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:S_{i}\leq\hat{t}_{\theta}\}
.

The technical condition stated in (
3.9
) ensures that

is well behaved in the limit. The proof of Theorem

, given in Appendix

, first establishes that the expected number of false discoveries is asymptotically bounded by

4.
Simulation studies

4.1.
Simulated data

Consider a meta-analysis consisting of
m=500
features across
n=4
studies. For each feature
i
and study
j
, let
\mu_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
be an effect parameter, and consider the null hypothesis
H_{ij}:\mu_{ij}=0
with the corresponding alternative
\mu_{ij}\neq 0
. The data for this meta-analysis are simulated according to the steps provided below.

For a given correlation parameter
\rho\in[-1,1]
, we arbitrarily partition the
500
features into
b
blocks of size
500/b
each, where

b=\begin{cases}5&\text{if }\rho\geq 0,\\
250&\text{if }\rho<0.\end{cases}

We then generate standard normal variables
\epsilon_{ij}\sim\text{N}(0,1)
for each pair
(i,j)
, subject to the following dependence structure:

•

The sequences
(\epsilon_{i1})_{i=1}^{500}
,
(\epsilon_{i2})_{i=1}^{500}
,
(\epsilon_{i3})_{i=1}^{500}
, and
(\epsilon_{i4})_{i=1}^{500}
are mutually independent;

•

Within each study
j
,

\text{cor}(\epsilon_{ij},\epsilon_{i^{\prime}j})=\begin{cases}\rho,&\text{if $i$ and $i^{\prime}$ belong to the same block and $i\neq i^{\prime}$;}\\
1,&\text{if $i=i^{\prime}$;}\\
0,&\text{if $i$ and $i^{\prime}$ do not belong to the same block}.\end{cases}

The following process is then used to generate the
p
-values
(P_{ij})_{500\times 4}
. For each
i=1,\dots,500
:

(1)

Generate
c_{i}\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\pi_{1})
, where
\pi_{1}\in[0,1]
is a given signal density parameter.

(i)

If
c_{i}=0
, set
(\mu_{i1},\mu_{i2},\mu_{i3},\mu_{i4})=(0,0,0,0)
;

(ii)

If
c_{i}=1
, sample
(\mu_{i1},\mu_{i2},\mu_{i3},\mu_{i4})
uniformly from the set
\{0,4\}^{4}
.

(2)

For
j=1,\dots,4
, compute
P_{ij}=1-\Phi(\mu_{ij}+\epsilon_{ij})
.

Hence, by construction, the
p
-values in
(P_{ij})_{500\times 4}
are valid and independent across studies, while exhibiting block-wise equicorrelation within each study.

4.2.
Compared methods

The following multiple testing procedures, representing a mix of state-of-the-art and well-established approaches, are considered in our simulations:

(a)

AdaFilter-AdaBon: Definition
3
with
\theta=0.5
.

(b)

AdaFilter-Bon: Definition
2
.

(c)

Bonferroni: Definition
1
.

(d)

Hochberg: The step-up
k
-FWER procedure described in Theorem 4.2 of
Sarkar, 2007
.

(e)

Adaptive Bonferroni: Definition 1 of
Guo, 2009
with tuning parameter
\lambda=0.5
.

(f)

Adaptive Hochberg: Definition 4 of
Sarkar et al., 2012
with
\hat{n}(\kappa)
where
\kappa=490
.

Let
W_{u}(\cdot)
denote the cdf of a chi-squared distribution with
2\cdot(n-u+1)
degrees of freedom. Methods
(c)
—
(f)
are implemented in our simulations with the PC
p
-value inputs
(P^{u/n}_{i})_{i=1}^{m}
, where each

-value

f_{\text{Fisher}}(P_{i1},\dots,P_{in};u)\equiv 1-W_{u}\left(-2\sum^{n}_{j=u}\log P_{i(j)}\right).

This choice is motivated by the fact that Fisher-combined PC
p
-values are less conservative than their Bonferroni-combined PC
p
-values
(
Bogomolov and Heller, 2023
, Sec. 2.1)
. By contrast, methods
(a)
and
(b)

p
-values.

5.
Discussion

We proposed AdaFilter-AdaBon, a
p
-value-based procedure for testing partial conjunction hypotheses with asymptotic
k
-FWER control. By combining filtering with a post-filter null proportion estimator, our method improves power over its predecessor, AdaFilter-Bon
(
Wang et al., 2022
)
, while maintaining robust finite-sample
k
-FWER control, as shown in our simulation studies.

AdaFilter-AdaBon can be extended to control the false exceedance rate (FDX) and the false discovery rate (FDR). For a set
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
of rejected PC nulls and tolerance level
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the FDX is defined as

\text{FDX}\equiv\text{FDX}(\mathcal{R};\gamma)=\Pr\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{i\in\mathcal{R}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{i\in\mathcal{R}\}}\geq\gamma\right),

which is the probability that the proportion of false discoveries among the total number of discoveries exceeds
\gamma
. The false discovery rate is defined as

\text{FDR}\equiv\text{FDR}(\mathcal{R})=\Pr\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{i\in\mathcal{R}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{i\in\mathcal{R}\}}\right]\in(0,1),

which is the expected proportion of false discoveries among the total number of discoveries. The following procedure, based on AdaFilter-AdaBon, controls both FDX and FDR asymptotically:

Definition 4
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1]
be a
k
-FWER target for a given
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in\{2,\dots,m\}
be a replicability level,
\theta\in(0,1)
be a tuning parameter, and
F_{i}
and
S_{i}
be as defined in (
2.5
) and (
2.4
), respectively. For
i=1,\dots,m
, reject
H^{u/n}_{i}
if
S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}
, where

(5.1)

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}\equiv\sup\left\{\tau\in[0,1]:\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\hat{t}_{\theta}\leq S_{i}\leq\tau\}+k}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\tau\}}\leq\gamma\right\}

and
\hat{t}_{\theta}
is AdaFilter-AdaBon’s rejection threshold as defined in (
3.2
).

Corollary 5.1

(FDX and FDR control)
.

If Theorem
3.3
holds, then the procedure in Definition
4
has the following asymptotic error properties:

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\text{FDX}(\mathcal{R})\leq\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\limsup_{m\to\infty}\text{FDR}(\mathcal{R})\leq\alpha+\gamma,

where
\mathcal{R}=\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}:S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}
.

The proof of Corollary
5.1
is given in Appendix
D.1
. Within the proof, it is shown that the procedure in Definition
4
controls FDX and FDR indirectly through
k
-FWER control. Hence, an avenue for future work is to develop more direct approaches for controlling FDX and FDR that incorporate filtering and our post-filter null proportion estimator. This may lead to improved power over the procedure presented in Definition
4
.

A.1.
Technical lemmas for proving Theorem
2.1

For all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, let

(A.1)

\hat{t}_{i}=\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:t\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right)\leq\alpha\right\}

be a threshold similar to
\hat{t}
in (
2.6

in Appendix

Lemma A.1

(
\hat{t}_{i}\leq\hat{t}
)
.

Let
\hat{t}
and
\hat{t}_{i}
be as defined in (
2.6
) and (
A.1
), respectively. Then
\hat{t}_{i}\leq\hat{t}

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

Note that

\displaystyle t\cdot\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}=t\cdot\left(I\{F_{i}<t\}+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right)\leq t\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right).

As a consequence of the display above, we have that

\displaystyle\hat{t}

\displaystyle\equiv\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:t\cdot\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\leq\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:t\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right)\leq\alpha\right\}\equiv\hat{t}_{i}.

∎

Lemma A.2

(
\hat{t}_{i}=\hat{t}
if
F_{i}<\hat{t}
)
.

Let
\hat{t}
and
\hat{t}_{i}
be as defined in (
2.6
) and (
A.1
), respectively. If
F_{i}<\hat{t}
, then
\hat{t}_{i}=\hat{t}
.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.

If
F_{i}<\hat{t}
, it must be the case that

\displaystyle\hat{t}

\displaystyle\equiv\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:t\cdot\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\leq\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{t\in(F_{i},1]:t\cdot\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\leq\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{t\in(F_{i},1]:t\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right)\leq\alpha\right\}

where

(A.2)

\exists\ t^{*}\in(F_{i},1]\quad\text{such that}\quad t^{*}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t^{*}\}\right)\leq\alpha.

From (
A.1
), we have that

\displaystyle\hat{t}_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\sup\left\{t\in[0,1]:t\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right)\leq\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{t\in(F_{i},1]:t\cdot\left(1+\sum_{\ell\neq i}I\{F_{\ell}<t\}\right)\leq\alpha\right\}

where the second equality is a consequence of (
A.2
).

\hat{t}=\hat{t}_{i}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.1

.

Let
V=\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[V]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}\}I\{F_{i}<\hat{t}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{i}\}I\{F_{i}<\hat{t}_{i}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\right]

where the second equality is a consequence of

, and the third equality is a result of Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[V]

\displaystyle=k\alpha\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}\leq\hat{t}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}\leq\hat{t}\}}\right]

\displaystyle k\text{-FWER}(\mathcal{R})=\Pr(V\geq k)\leq\alpha\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}\leq\hat{t}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{\ell=1}I\{F_{\ell}\leq\hat{t}\}}\right].

Lemma B.1

\displaystyle\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}

(B.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1/m)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}-(1/m)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)(1/m)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}.

Since

and
\tilde{F}_{1}

\tilde{S}_{1}(\theta t)\leq\tilde{F}_{1}(\theta t)\leq\tilde{F}_{1}(t)

For the next part of this proof, we adopt the following notation for convenience:

(B.6)

\tilde{F}(t)\equiv\pi_{0}\tilde{F}_{0}(t)+\pi_{1}\tilde{F}_{1}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{S}(t)\equiv\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)+\pi_{1}\tilde{S}_{1}(t).

By (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and (
3.7
) in Assumption
1
, and by (
B.3
), we have for all
t\in(0,1]
that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{F}(t)-\tilde{S}(\theta t)}{(1-\theta t)\tilde{F}(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{F}_{0}(t)+\pi_{1}\tilde{F}_{1}(t)-\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(\theta t)-\pi_{1}\tilde{S}_{1}(\theta t)}{(1-\theta t)\tilde{F}(t)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{F}_{0}(t)+\pi_{1}\tilde{F}_{1}(t)-\pi_{0}\theta t\tilde{F}_{0}(t)-\pi_{1}\tilde{F}_{1}(t)}{(1-\theta t)\tilde{F}(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{0}(1-\theta t)\tilde{F}_{0}(t)}{(1-\theta t)\tilde{F}(t)}

(B.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{F}_{0}(t)}{\tilde{F}(t)},

where the inequality is a consequence of (
B.5
) and (
B.4
). Since
\tilde{S}_{0}(t)\leq t\tilde{F}_{0}(t)
holds by (
3.6
) in Assumption
1
, it follows from (
B.7
) that

(B.8)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)}{\tilde{F}(t)}\right)\geq 0.

By (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and (
3.7
) in Assumption
1
, we also have that

(B.9)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)m}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}=\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)}{\tilde{F}(t)}.

By combining (
B.8
) and (
B.9
), it holds that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)m}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right)\geq 0

∎

Lemma B.2
.

Under Assumption
1
, we have for fixed constants
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,1]
that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{t\geq\delta}\left|\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\pi_{0}m\tilde{S}_{0}(t)}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right|=0

with probability
1
.

Proof of Lemma
B.2
.

By the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem, it follows from Assumption
1
that

(B.10)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left|\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}-\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)\right|=0

with probability 1. To prove the lemma, note that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq\delta}\left|\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\pi_{0}m\tilde{S}_{0}(t)}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{t\geq\delta}\left|\frac{m}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right|\left|\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}-\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\left|\frac{m}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\delta\}}\right|\sup_{t\geq\delta}\left|\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}-\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)\right|=0

where the equality is a consequence of (
B.10
).
∎

Lemma B.3
.

Under Assumption
1
, we have for fixed constants
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,1]
that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}\cdot t-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)\geq 0

with probability
1
.

Proof of Lemma
B.3
.

We have that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}{m}\right)\left(t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\delta\}}{m}\right)\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{(1-\theta t)\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\delta\}}{m}\right)\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)m}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\left(-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\delta\}}{m}\right)\sup_{t\geq\delta}\left|\frac{\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(t)m}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}}\right|

\displaystyle=0

where the second inequality is a result of Lemma
B.1
and the last equality is a consequence of Lemma
B.2
.
∎

B.4.
Proof of Theorem
3.3

.

For any
t\in(0,1]
, we have by Assumption
1
that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}(1-I\{S_{i}<\theta t\})}{m(1-\theta t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}-\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}<\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}-\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}

\displaystyle=t\cdot\frac{(\pi_{0}\tilde{F}_{0}(t)+\pi_{1}\tilde{F}_{1}(t))-(\pi_{0}\tilde{S}_{0}(\theta t)+\pi_{1}\tilde{S}_{1}(\theta t))}{1-\theta t}

(B.11)

\displaystyle=t\cdot\frac{\tilde{F}(t)-\tilde{S}(\theta t)}{1-\theta t}

with probability 1, where
\tilde{F}(t)
and
\tilde{S}(t)
are as defined in (
B.6
). By (
3.9
) in Assumption
1
and (
B.11
), there exists a
t^{\prime}>0
such that

(B.12)

\alpha\cdot\omega-t^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\tilde{F}(t^{\prime})-\tilde{S}(\theta t^{\prime})}{1-\theta t^{\prime}}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}

for some constant
\epsilon>0
. For a sufficiently large
m
, we also have by (
3.9
) in Assumption
1
and (
B.11
) that

\displaystyle\left|t^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\tilde{F}(t^{\prime})-\tilde{S}(\theta t^{\prime})}{1-\theta t^{\prime}}-t^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t^{\prime}\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t^{\prime}\}}{m(1-\theta t^{\prime})}\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{2},

which then implies from (
B.12
) that

\displaystyle t^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t^{\prime}\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t^{\prime}\}}{m(1-\theta t^{\prime})}<\alpha\cdot\omega\implies t^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t^{\prime}\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t^{\prime}\}}{1-\theta t^{\prime}}<\alpha\cdot k.

Then by the definition of
\hat{t}_{\theta}
in (
3.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\hat{t}_{\theta}\geq t^{\prime}

with probability 1. The display above and Lemma
B.3
then gives the following result:

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf\left(\hat{t}_{\theta}\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta\hat{t}_{\theta}\}}{m(1-\theta\hat{t}_{\theta})}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)

(B.13)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{t\geq\delta}\left(t\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<t\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta t\}}{m(1-\theta t)}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<t\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)\geq 0

for
\delta=t^{\prime}/2
. Since it holds for any
m
that

\hat{t}_{\theta}\cdot\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{F_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{S_{i}\geq\theta\hat{t}_{\theta}\}}{1-\theta\hat{t}_{\theta}}\leq k\cdot\alpha,

by the definition of
\hat{t}_{\theta}
in
(
3.2
)
, it must follow from (
B.13
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf\left(\frac{k\cdot\alpha}{m}-\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)\geq 0.

We can therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)\leq\omega\cdot\alpha.

By Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\omega\cdot\alpha.

It then follows from the above display and Markov’s inequality that

\displaystyle\limsup_{m\to\infty}\Pr\left(\text{$k$-FWER}(\mathcal{R})\geq k\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\Pr\left(\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\geq k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{k}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{m\cdot\omega}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega\cdot\alpha}{\omega}

\displaystyle=\alpha.

∎

D.1.
Proof of Corollary
5.1

.

We have that

\displaystyle\text{FDX}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\geq\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\geq\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\hat{t}_{\theta}\leq S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}+k}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\geq\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\hat{t}_{\theta}\leq S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}+k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\geq\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{\hat{t}_{\theta}\leq S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}+k\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left(\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}<\hat{t}_{\theta}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}\geq k\right)

\displaystyle=k\text{-FWER}(\mathcal{R})

where the first inequality follows from (
5.1
). Given the display above and Theorem
3.3
, we have that

(D.1)

\limsup_{m\longrightarrow\infty}\text{FDX}(\mathcal{R})\leq\limsup_{m\longrightarrow\infty}k\text{-FWER}(\mathcal{R})\leq\alpha,

which proves FDX control. To prove FDR control, note that

\displaystyle\text{FDR}(\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\cdot I\left\{\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\leq\gamma\right\}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\cdot I\left\{\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\geq\gamma\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma+\mathbb{E}\left[I\left\{\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\geq\gamma\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma+\Pr\left(\frac{\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}I\{\text{$H^{u/n}_{i}$ is true}\}}{1\vee\sum^{m}_{i=1}I\{S_{i}\leq\hat{\tau}\}}\geq\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma+\text{FDX}(\mathcal{R}).

The result then immediately follows from (
D.1
).
∎

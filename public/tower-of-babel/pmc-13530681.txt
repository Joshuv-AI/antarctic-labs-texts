Poster 143. Impact of Acetabular Labral Tear Location on Patient-Reported Outcomes and Conversion to Total Hip Arthroplasty Following Hip Arthroscopy with an 8-Year Minimum Follow-Up
Authors: Jackson Woodrow, Jeffrey S. Mun, Srish S. Chenna, Rachel L. Poutre, Brandon J. Allen, Stephen M. Gillinov, Bilal Siddiq, Rishi P. Earla, Peter E. Baglien, Scott D. Martin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Acetabular labral tears are a common cause of hip pain. While larger tear size has been linked to worse outcomes following arthroscopic treatment, the influence of labral tear location remains incompletely understood. This study investigates how tear location impacts functional outcomes, conversion to THA, and patient satisfaction.Methods:Patients who underwent primary hip arthroscopy were retrospectively sent online surveys at a minimum of 8 years following their surgery. Patients aged >=18 with complete surveys were included. Exclusion criteria included hip dysplasia (lateral center edge angle [LCEA] <= 20°), BMI >=35 kg/m2, Tönnis grade >= 2, and incomplete survey data. Tear location was classified into superior, anterosuperior, and posterosuperior based on intraoperative findings. Outcomes included PROMs, conversion to THA, PASS improvement, and overall patient satisfaction. Unadjusted and adjusted logistic regression models to account for age, sex, BMI, tear size, Tönnis grade, and labral repair versus debridement were performed to identify potential confounding variables. This study was approved by the institutional review board with each patient providing consent.Results:138 patients were included with a mean follow-up time of 11.1 ± 2.5 years with 49% of patients being female. There was no significant difference in all postoperative PROMs across tear locations, although posterosuperior tears demonstrated lower mean outcomes compared to superior and anterosuperior. Labral tear location was also not found to significantly influence rates of PASS achievement. Satisfaction was similar among superior, anterosuperior, and posterosuperior tear locations (81.0% vs 80.0% vs 76.9%, P=0.99). THA conversion occurred in 20.9% of patients and was not independently associated with tear location after adjusting for confounders. Age was the strongest predictor of THA conversion (odds ratio = 1.126 per year, 95% CI 1.06-1.21, P<0.001), and tear size was also significant (OR 1.050, 1.02-1.09, P=0.006).Conclusions:Acetabular labral tear location was not found to be a significant predictor of functional or clinical outcomes following hip arthroscopy. However, while labral tear location was not predictive of conversion to THA, age and tear size were found to be significant predictors of conversion. Overall, the location of a labral tear is not a predictor of long-term results after hip arthroscopy. Clinicians should not rely on acetabular labral tear location as a predictor of long-term success when deciding on treatment options and discussing expected outcomes following hip arthroscopy.

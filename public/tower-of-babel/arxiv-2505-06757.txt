Some variants of the periodic tiling conjecture
Rachel Greenfeld, Terence Tao

Abstract
. The periodic tiling conjecture (PTC) asserts, for a finitely generated Abelian group G G and a finite subset F F of G G , that if there is a set A A that solves the tiling equation 𝟙 F ∗ 𝟙 A = 1 \mathds{1}_{F}*\mathds{1}_{A}=1 , there is also a periodic solution 𝟙 A p \mathds{1}_{A_{{\operatorname{p}}}} . This conjecture is known to hold for some groups G G and fail for others. In this paper we establish three variants of the PTC. The first (due to Tim Austin) replaces the constant function 1 1 on the right hand side of the tiling equation by 0 0 , and the indicator functions 𝟙 F \mathds{1}_{F} and 𝟙 A \mathds{1}_{A} by bounded integer-valued functions. The second, which applies in G = ℤ 2 G=\mathbb{Z}^{2} , replaces the right hand side of the tiling equation by an integer-valued periodic function, and the functions 𝟙 F \mathds{1}_{F} and 𝟙 A \mathds{1}_{A} on the left hand side by bounded integer-valued functions. The third (which is the most difficult to establish) is similar to the second, but retains the property of both 𝟙 A \mathds{1}_{A} and 𝟙 A p \mathds{1}_{A_{{\operatorname{p}}}} being indicator functions; in particular, we establish the PTC for multi-tilings in G = ℤ 2 G=\mathbb{Z}^{2} . As a result, we obtain the decidability of constant-level integer tilings in any finitely generated Abelian group G G and multi-tilings in G = ℤ 2 G=\mathbb{Z}^{2} .

Given

Equivalently,

We define a wedge product

An element

for some

is an integer multiple of a primitive element. If

The choice of complementary vector

We will frequently work with various moduli

A basic tool for building periodic functions for us will be that of periodically extending off of a fundamental domain.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

As a simple application of this construction, we observe the following claim, that shows that the analogue of the PTC for function composition is trivially true:

Lemma 2.2

(PTC for composition)
.

Let

be a subgroup of an Abelian group

Take

, where

is an arbitrarily chosen fundamental domain for

is
\Gamma
-periodic, and

∎

Given a finite set

3.
The dilation lemma, and some consequences

Let
G
be a finitely generated Abelian group.
Given a function
f\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})_{{\operatorname{c}}}
, which we can write as
f=\sum_{x}f(x)\mathds{1}_{\{x\}}
since there are only finitely many non-zero summands, we can define the dilates
\tau_{r}f\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})_{{\operatorname{c}}}
for any natural number
r
by the formula

\tau_{r}f\coloneqq\sum_{x}f(x)\mathds{1}_{\{rx\}}.

The following “dilation lemma” is a routine modification of existing dilation lemmas in the literature (see, e.g.,
[
T95
]
,
[
B20
, Proposition 3.1]
,
[
GT21
, Lemma 3.1]
,
[
GGRT23
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
KS15
, Lemma 2]
,
[
S98
, Theorem 10]
):

Lemma 3.1

(Dilation lemma)
.

Let
f\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})_{{\operatorname{c}}}
and
g\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})_{{\operatorname{p}}}
. Then for sufficiently divisible
q
, if
f*a=g
for some
a\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})
, then
(\tau_{r}f)*a=g
for all
r\geqslant 1
that is equal to
1
mod
q
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
g=0
. By the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, it suffices to establish the claim with
r
replaced by all primes
p
larger than some threshold
w
(and then taking
q
to be divisible by all primes up to
w
). If
f*a=0
, then clearly
f^{*p}*a=0
, where
f^{*p}
is the convolution of
p
copies of
f
. Since the Frobenius map
x\mapsto x^{p}
is a homomorphism in characteristic
p
, we have

f^{*p}=\tau_{p}f\mod p

and thus

(\tau_{p}f)*a=0\mod p.

Note that
(\tau_{p}f)*a
is bounded independently of
p
. Thus, for
p
large enough, we obtain
(\tau_{p}f)*a=0
as required. We remark that
q
can be chosen to depend on
a
only through the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of
a
.

Now we consider the case of general
g
. Since
g
is assumed to be periodic, we may assume (for sufficiently divisible
q
) that
\partial_{qh}g=0
for all
h\in G
. In particular, if
f*a=g
, then

f*\partial_{qh}a=0.

Since the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of
\partial_{qh}a
is bounded uniformly in
q
and
h
, we conclude from the previous discussion that (for sufficiently divisible
q
) one has

\tau_{r}f*\partial_{qh}a=0

for any
r\geqslant 1
coprime to
q
. In particular,

\partial_{qh}((\tau_{r}f-f)*a)=0,

that is to say
(\tau_{r}f-f)*a
is periodic with respect to the finite index subgroup
qG
. On the other hand, for
r=1
mod
q
,
\tau_{r}f-f
has mean zero on every coset of
qG
. As
qG
is amenable, this implies that
(\tau_{r}f-f)*a
also has mean zero on every such coset, and thus vanishes identically by periodicity. We conclude that
\tau_{r}f*a=f*a=g
, as required.
∎

This leads to the following structure theorem.

Theorem 3.2

(Structure theorem)
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times H
for some
d\geqslant 1
and finite Abelian
H
, and let
f\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})_{{\operatorname{c}}}
. For each
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let
f_{v}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{\{v\}\times H}f
be the restriction of
f
to
\{v\}\times H
, and let

be the (finite) set of

for which
f_{v}
is not identically zero, thus
f=\sum_{v\in V}f_{v}
. Suppose that
f*a=g
for some
a\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})
and
g\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Z})_{{\operatorname{p}}}
. Then for any
v_{0}\in V
, one has the decomposition

f_{v_{0}}*a=g-\sum_{w\in W_{v_{0}}}\varphi_{v_{0},w}

where
W_{v_{0}}=W_{v_{0}}(f)
is a finite set of linearly independent primitive elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that depends only on
f
, and
\varphi_{v_{0},w}\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{R})
is
qw
-periodic for sufficiently divisible

(where we view
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times H
).

Proof.

We adapt the proof of
[
GT21
, Theorem 1.7]
. By translation we may assume without loss of generality that

. By Lemma

, we see for sufficiently divisible

(\tau_{1+qr}f)*a=g

. By making

is a multiple of the order of

\tau_{1+qr}f_{0}=f_{0}
, and more generally

for any

f_{0}*a=g-\sum_{v\in V\backslash\{0\}}\mathds{1}_{\{qrv\}}*f_{v}*a.

Averaging in

f_{0}*a=g-\sum_{v\in V\backslash\{0\}}\pi_{qv}(f_{v}*a).

Each

is a integer multiple of some primitive element

in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. If we let

be the set of such

∎

7.

11

11

[

The answer to this question will, of course, depend on the predicate; for instance, the nonlinear predicate

famously admits real solutions but no rational solutions, and is thus not rationalizable. In the case where the predicate

9.4.
Reducing to a finite number of constraints

We have successfully eliminated all the combinatorial data, so that the only remaining unknown in the ansatz above is the map
\Phi^{\mathbb{Q}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{1}(\mathbb{Q}^{d+1},\mathbb{Q})
. We are close to being able to apply Proposition
7.6
, as the conditions (
9.6

9.12
) are (locally) definable in the language
{\mathcal{L}}
of linear inequalities with rational coefficients for any
fixed

and

range over infinite sets, we are not quite able to invoke this proposition yet, as it requires the number of constraints to be finite. Fortunately, we can use equidistribution theory to reduce the number of constraints from infinite to finite, as long as we avoid the discontinuities of the fractional part operator
\{\}
.

To see this, we first look at (
9.6
) for a fixed

, which is a little simpler notationally to work with

17

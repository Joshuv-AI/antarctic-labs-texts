Effects of in-medium nucleon-nucleon cross section in heavy-ion collisions
Shuochong Han, Xinle Shang, Wei Zuo, Gaochan Yong, Ang Li

Abstract
Based on the isospin-dependent Boltzmann-Uehling-Uhlenbeck transport model, we investigate the impact of in-medium nucleon-nucleon ( N ​ N NN ) elastic scattering cross sections on various observables in the 132 Sn+ 124 Sn reaction. In our previous work, the in-medium N ​ N NN cross sections at various densities and isospin asymmetry were systematically studied within the Brueckner-Hartree-Fock (BHF) framework. The calculation employed are based on the exact treatment of the total momentum of the scattering pair in G G matrix, in contrast to the commonly used total momentum approximation. In the present study, we find that the in-medium N ​ N NN cross sections have negligible effects on the neutron-to-proton ( n / p n/p ) ratio of emitted nucleons and on the difference between neutron and proton transverse flows. However, the BHF-based in-medium cross section σ BHF \sigma_{\text{BHF}} significantly enhances the neutron-proton differential collective flow, the pion yields, and the π − / π + \pi^{-}/\pi^{+} ratio, compared to results using the empirical effective cross section σ eff \sigma_{\text{eff}} . Furthermore, we observe that the medium effects arising from the density of states dominate over those from the G G matrix, and the two contribute in opposite directions. An exact treatment of the total momentum dependence in the density of states reduces the overall suppression from medium corrections on the in-medium N ​ N NN cross sections.



I
INTRODUCTION

The in-medium nucleon-nucleon (
NN
) cross section constitutes a critical input for transport models used to simulate heavy-ion collisions (HICs) at intermediate energies
Westfall et al. 1993
;
Soff et al. 1995
;
Alm et al. 1995
;
Insolia et al. 1994
;
de La Mota et al. 1992
;
Yong 2010
;
Li et al. 2022
. These collisions serve as a key probe of the high-density behavior of the nuclear equation of state, providing valuable insights into the properties of nuclear matter under extreme conditions
Wang et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2008
;
Long and Wei 2024
. Observables that are particularly sensitive to the equation of state, such as nucleon collective flow
Li 2000
;
Yong et al. 2006a
;
Wang et al. 2014
and pion production
Li et al. 2005a
;
Yong et al. 2006b
;
Xiao et al. 2009
, may also exhibit strong dependence on the in-medium
NN
cross section. Furthermore, transport models, including Boltzmann-Uehling-Uhlenbeck (BUU) and quantum molecular dynamics (QMD), incorporate two essential ingredients: the nuclear mean field
Wolter et al. 2022
;
Colonna et al. 2021
and the in-medium
NN
cross section
Xu et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2018
. The mean field describes the average single-particle potential experienced by nucleons, whereas the in-medium cross section accounts for the medium modifications of
NN
interactions. A self-consistent description of HIC dynamics requires both ingredients to be derived from microscopic many-body theories based on realistic
NN
interactions.

However, unlike the
NN
cross section in free space
\sigma_{\text{free}}
, which is well constrained by scattering experiments, its in-medium counterpart cannot be directly measured due to the complex dynamics and correlations among nucleons in dense nuclear matter. Consequently, theoretical predictions based on ab initio calculations are indispensable for constraining the in-medium
NN
cross section. Microscopic approaches such as the Brueckner theory
Bohnet et al. 1989
;
Schulze et al. 1997
;
Shang et al. 2021
, Dirac-Brueckner theory
Li and Machleidt 1993
;
Li and Machleidt 1994
;
Fuchs et al. 2001
;
Sammarruca and Krastev 2006
, and variational methods
Pandharipande and Pieper 1992
, have been extensively employed for this purpose. Among these, the Brueckner–Hartree–Fock (BHF) framework provides a systematic approach to calculating in-medium
NN
cross sections by incorporating two primary medium effects: modifications to the scattering amplitude and changes in the density of states.
The scattering amplitude is represented by the effective G-matrix, which encapsulates medium corrections such as Pauli blocking and mean-field potentials, while the density of states is influenced by the effective masses of the nucleons
Schulze et al. 1997
. Importantly, the in-medium
NN
cross section depends not only on the relative momentum
k
and the scattering angle
\theta
of the colliding nucleons, but also on their total momentum
K
. This
K
-dependence originates from an accurate treatment of the Brueckner-Bethe-Goldstone (BBG) equation
Shang et al. 2021
, highlighting the sensitivity of in-medium scattering processes to the motion of the nucleon pair relative to the medium. Moreover, the
K
-dependence plays a crucial role in predicting the isospin dependence of the nucleon effective mass
Shang et al. 2020
, which in turn significantly influences the in-medium
NN
cross section. In our previous work
Han et al. 2022
;
Shang et al. 2021
, we presented detailed microscopic predictions of in-medium
NN
cross sections in asymmetric nuclear matter within the BHF framework, fully accounting the dependence on the total momentum
K
of the colliding nucleon pair-i.e., incorporating its effects in both the density of state and the scattering amplitude.

To integrate these microscopic results into practical transport simulations, a commonly adopted approximation in isospin-dependent Boltzmann–Uehling–Uhlenbeck (IBUU) transport model is to factorize the in-medium
NN
cross section as the product of the free-space cross section and a medium correction factor.
In previous studies
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Li and Chen 2005
;
Guo et al. 2013
, this factor is typically expressed as the square of the ratio between the in-medium effective reduced mass and the bare reduced mass of the colliding pair, providing only an approximate treatment of the medium effects by accounting for changes in the density of states while neglecting the influence of medium modifications to the scattering amplitude.
In contrast, in Ref.
Han et al. 2022
, we proposed analytical expressions for the medium correction factors based on microscopic BHF calculations, explicitly incorporating the modifications on both the density of states and the scattering amplitude.
However, no systematic study has been carried out to evaluate the impact of in-medium
NN
cross sections with accurately treated medium effects on the HIC observables. To address this, here we extend our previously proposed correction factors to systematically investigate the impact of in-medium
NN
cross sections on several key observables sensitive to the cross section in intermediate-energy HICs, including the nuclear stopping power, the neutron-to-proton
(n/p)
ratio of free nucleons, pion production, and neutron-proton differential transverse and elliptic flows. Particular emphasis is placed on disentangling the contributions from medium modifications to the scattering amplitude and to the density of states. Our results provide further insight into the importance of consistent microscopic treatment of in-medium
NN
cross sections in transport model simulations.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we briefly review the IBUU method and the in-medium
NN
cross sections based on BHF calculations.
Sec. III consists of three parts: the first subsection focuses on the numerical results and interpretation of nuclear stopping; the second discusses the neutron-proton differential flow obtained using microscopic in-medium cross sections; and the third analyzes the production yields of
\pi^{-}
and
\pi^{+}
mesons.
And finally, a summary is given in Sec. IV.

IV
SUMMARY

In conclusion, we have systematically calculated the in-medium nucleon-nucleon (
NN
) elastic scattering cross sections at various densities and isospin asymmetries using the BHF approach with a microscopic three-body force, as detailed in our previous work. Additionally, we have provided a convenient parametrization of the medium correction factor, which can be readily implemented in transport model simulations.
In the present study, we have thoroughly investigated the influence of the in-medium
NN
cross sections on various observables of the emitted particles in HICs.
Furthermore, We have disentangled and analyzed the respective roles of medium effects arising from the scattering amplitude and the density of states.
In addition, we have examined the influence of the total momentum (
K
) dependence of the cross section on the dynamics of HICs.
These findings offer deeper insights into the role of nuclear medium modifications in reaction dynamics.

Specifically, we investigate the effects of the microscopic in-medium
NN
cross section
\sigma_{\text{BHF}}
on nuclear stopping, the neutron-to-proton (
n/p
) ratio of free nucleons, the difference between neutron and proton transverse flows, the neutron-proton differential collective flow, and pion production in semi-central and central collisions of
132
Sn+
124
Sn.
Our results show that
\sigma_{\text{BHF}}
has a negligible impact on the
n/p
ratio and the transverse flow difference between neutrons and protons. In contrast, it introduces a notable modification to the neutron-proton differential collective flow.
Moreover, compared to the commonly used empirical cross section
\sigma_{\text{eff}}
in IBUU simulations,
\sigma_{\text{BHF}}
notably enhances the yields of both
\pi^{-}
and
\pi^{+}
mesons, as well as the
(\pi^{-}/\pi^{+})_{\text{like}}
ratio.
A further decomposition of the medium effects in
\sigma_{\text{BHF}}
reveals that the contribution from the scattering amplitude is opposite in sign and subdominant compared to that from the density of states.
Omitting the total momentum (
K
) dependence in the density of states results in an overestimation of the suppression of the in-medium cross section, thereby introducing notable deviations in the calculated observables of HICs.

Based on these findings, we conclude that the medium effects in the
NN
elastic scattering cross section can be safely neglected when extracting the symmetry energy from observables such as the neutron-to-proton (
n/p
) ratio and the difference in transverse flow between neutrons and protons in HIC simulations.
However, for studies involving neutron-proton differential collective flow and pion production, it is insufficient to account only for the medium corrections associated with the nucleon effective mass.
While the correction from the density of states dominates the in-medium modification of cross sections in HICs, the total momentum (
K
) dependence and the medium effects from the scattering amplitude should not be neglected in accurate evaluations.

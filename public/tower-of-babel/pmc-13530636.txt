Poster 17. Oral Contraceptive Use at Time of ACL Reconstruction Decreases Risk of Revision Surgery
Authors: Sujay Garlapati, Rajit Agarwal, Meghan Bishop
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:In female patients, the risk of revision surgery within five years after anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction has been reported to be approximately 4.9%. Oral contraceptive pills (OCPs), which suppress endogenous estrogen and progesterone levels, have been associated with reducing the risk of ACL injuries in female patients. However, current research has not yet examined whether OCP use at the time of initial ACL reconstruction can mitigate the risk of subsequent procedures. This study aimed to evaluate whether the use of OCPs at the time of initial ACL reconstruction influences postoperative outcomes, including the risk of undergoing a subsequent ipsilateral procedural intervention following ACL reconstruction in women.Methods:A retrospective cohort study was conducted using the TriNetX database to compare female patients aged 13 to 49 who underwent subsequent procedures following initial ACL reconstruction, stratified by those with documented OCP use and those without at the time of initial reconstruction. This study utilized de-identified patient data and was reviewed and deemed exempt by the Drexel University College of Medicine Institutional Review Board. The cohorts were further stratified by year from initial reconstruction, age, BMI, and presence of diabetes mellitus. Risk ratios with 95% confidence intervals were calculated to examine the risk of undergoing a subsequent ipsilateral procedure in patients with documented OCP use at the time of initial ACL reconstruction versus those without.Results:Out of 26,520 patients who underwent ACL reconstruction, 1,736 patients (6.5%) underwent subsequent ipsilateral revision ACL reconstruction within five years. Patients with documented use of OCPs at the time of initial ACL reconstruction had a 25.0% lower risk (RR = 0.75, 95% CI: 0.68-0.83, p < 0.001) of undergoing subsequent ipsilateral revision ACL reconstruction surgery within five years compared to non-users. Furthermore, OCP's protective effect was strongest within the first year (RR = 0.42, 95% CI: 0.33-0.54, p < 0.001), two years (RR = 0.47, 95% CI: 0.40-0.55, p < 0.001), three years (RR = 0.61, 95% CI: 0.54-0.69, p < 0.001), and four years (RR = 0.60, 95% CI: 0.41-0.89, p = 0.010). This protective effect remained significant when stratified by age for 20-29 years (RR = 0.79, 95% CI: 0.69-0.91, p < 0.001), 30-39 years (RR = 0.76, 95% CI: 0.60-0.98, p = 0.035), and those with BMI > 25 (RR = 0.78, 95% CI: 0.68-0.90, p < 0.001). Furthermore, OCP use at the time of initial surgery did not significantly increase the risk of experiencing a venous thromboembolism (VTE) within five years after ACL reconstruction (RR = 1.30, 95% CI: 0.96-1.76, p = 0.096).Conclusions:Our findings suggest that OCP use at the time of initial ACL reconstruction in female patients may provide a protective effect against subsequent ipsilateral ACL reconstruction within five years, with the most significant protective effect observed in younger and more active individuals, without significantly increasing the risk of developing a VTE. Clinically, these findings highlight the potential for OCPs to be an accessible, low-cost intervention that could reduce the physical, functional, and financial burden of revision ACL surgery in high-risk populations. A key limitation is the retrospective design and reliance on physician-coded data, which limited our ability to thoroughly assess patient histories and other clinical information. A significant strength of this study is that it is one of the first to explore the relationship between OCP usage during ACL reconstruction and the development of subsequent surgical procedures at the time of publication. Future prospective studies are necessary to support these findings and establish the protective effect of OCPs in reducing the risk of revision following ACL reconstruction.

A Lie-theoretic generalization of some Hilbert schemes
Oscar Kivinen

Abstract
. We define several versions of a class of varieties X 𝔤 X_{\mathfrak{g}} attached to a complex reductive Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , generalizing the Hilbert scheme of points on the plane. These include trigonometric and elliptic versions attached to the corresponding groups. We also define the corresponding isospectral varieties Y 𝔤 Y_{\mathfrak{g}} . We prove a Gordon–Stafford localization theorem for X 𝔤 X_{\mathfrak{g}} and the corresponding equal-parameter rational Cherednik algebras, relate these varieties to the affine Springer fiber–sheaf correspondence of [ 14 ] , and discuss examples. We conjecture that the torus-fixed points of our varieties are in bijection with two-sided cells in the finite Weyl group and prove this in types A ​ B ​ C ABC . We relate these results to known results about Calogero–Moser spaces.

Consider the other vertices.
For the lower left, given a conjugacy class of braids on

The configuration space of

The top vertex of the triangle also generalizes to other reductive

Now there is a dependence on more data than simply the root system, which gives rise to various complications in establishing the mostly conjectural left and right arrows. We suppress the additional complications arising from these choices and do not discuss affine Springer fibers in detail.

Finally, we address the lower right vertex: is there a replacement for

We define such replacement varieties

2.
Hilbert schemes

In this section, we recall some results on the Hilbert scheme of points on the plane,
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
.
Recall that by definition,
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
parametrizes subschemes of length
n
in the plane
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. On closed points we may write

\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})=\{I\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]|\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]/I=n\}.

There is also a well-known quiver variety/ADHM description, realizing
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
as the quotient by
GL_{n}
of the space of triples

\{(x,y,v)\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}\times\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{n}|[x,y]=0,{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]v={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\}.

Instead of these two descriptions, we seek to generalize the following Proj construction by Haiman in
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})=\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\Delta A)^{d},

where
\Delta A\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{S_{n}}
is the ideal consisting of products of the form
\Delta f
, where
\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}-x_{j})
and
f\in A:={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{sgn}
is an alternating polynomial for the diagonal action.

Proof.

(Sketch.)
On
\Spec{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{S_{n}}={\mathrm{Sym}}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
there is an open locus
V=({\mathrm{Sym}}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{reg}
of collections of
n
points where all the points are distinct. Restricted to
V
, the Hilbert-Chow map
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})\to{\mathrm{Sym}}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is an isomorphism.
For every partition
\mu\vdash n
, there are open charts
U_{\mu}\subseteq\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
consisting of those colength
n
ideals
I\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
for which
{\mathbb{C}}[x,y]/I
is spanned by the monomials corresponding to the diagram of
\mu\subset{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
. On
U_{\mu}\cap V
, one can construct regular functions
\Delta_{D}/\Delta_{\mu}
, where
\Delta_{D}:=\det(x_{i}^{p_{j}}y_{i}^{q_{j}})_{i,j=1}^{n}
are alternating polynomials attached to
n
-element subsets
D=\{(p_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(p_{n},q_{n})\}\subset{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
. One proves that these regular functions glue as we vary
\mu
and also extend to regular functions on all of
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
. The universal property of blowing up then implies that there is a surjective map
\alpha:\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})\to\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\Delta A)^{d}
. Further, one checks that the pullbacks of the above
\Delta_{D}/\Delta_{\mu}
give all the regular functions on
U_{\mu}
, showing that the map on structure sheaves is also surjective and
\alpha
must be an isomorphism.
∎

Let us also recall an easy corollary of this result.

Corollary 2.2
.

The isospectral Hilbert scheme, i.e. the reduced fiber product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{C}}^{2n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sym}}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}

is isomorphic to

\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\Delta J)^{d},

where
J={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]\cdot A
is the ideal in
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
generated by the diagonally alternating polynomials.

Remark 2.3
.

One might wonder why we use
\Delta A
. Of course, one could take the ideal
fA
, where
f
is any alternating polynomial, and get an isomorphic Proj. After a further Veronese twist, one could also consider the ideal
A^{2}
as in
[
22
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

We will use this theorem as an inroad to defining
X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
in the next section. As Haiman remarks in
[
22
]
, since an alternating polynomial in a single set of variables vanishes on the diagonals, it is natural to expect that the radical of the ideal

A^{2}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]^{S_{n}}

coincides with the ideal of the locus in
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
where two or more points coincide. If two points coincide in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, their
x
- and
y
-coordinates have to be equal. Therefore, the ideal of this locus can be written as
\mathbf{e}I
, where
\mathbf{e}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\sigma
and
I
is the ideal
I=\bigcap_{i<j}\langle x_{i}-x_{j},y_{i}-y_{j}\rangle\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
.
Lifting these considerations to
Y_{n}
, one can similarly ask whether the ideal
J
from Corollary
2.2
equals
I
.

Knowing the analogous equality of ideals in dimension
1
is almost trivial, so it might seem unsurprising that indeed we have

(2.1)

I=J.

However, the only known proof of Eq. (
2.1
) is a corollary of the Polygraph Theorem of
[
23
]
, and in fact this equality is equivalent to the Cohen-Macaulay/Gorenstein properties of the isospectral Hilbert scheme
Y_{n}
.

3.
The analogs for other Lie algebras

Let
G
be a complex reductive group. We use standard notation such as
T,\mathfrak{t}
for maximal tori and Cartan subalgebras and
B,\mathfrak{b}
for Borel subgroups and subalgebras. Just as we replaced
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\backslash\{\text{diagonals}\}
by
\mathfrak{t}^{reg}
in the introduction, we may replace the ring

{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

and its diagonal
S_{n}
-action by the ring
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
of regular functions on
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and its diagonal
W
-action, where
W
is the Weyl group of
G
.

Based on the previous section, a natural first attempt at defining
X_{\mathfrak{g}}
for an arbitrary reductive
\mathfrak{g}
is the following.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A={\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]^{sgn}\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
be the space of diagonally alternating polynomials for the
W
-action. Let
\Delta=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha
and define

X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}:=\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\Delta A)^{d}

In other words,
X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
is the blow-up of
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]^{W}
in the ideal
\Delta A
, where
\Delta
and
A
are as above. Note that we have dropped the subscript
\mathfrak{g}
in the commutative algebraic objects, hoping this causes no confusion.

Similarly, we can consider the ideal
J
in
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
generated by
A
. This defines the
isospectral variety
of
X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
:

Definition 3.2
.

The isospectral variety
Y_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
of
X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
is

\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\Delta J)^{d}

Since the
W
-action is bigraded, the construction of
X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
and
Y_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
respects the natural
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
-action on
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. Conjectures of Bonnafé suggest that these varieties have desirable properties; for example,

Conjecture 3.3
.

There is a bijection

\{\text{Two-sided cells in }W\}\leftrightarrow X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}^{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}.

In particular, there are only finitely many fixed points, depending only on
W
.
For
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
,
W=S_{n}
, and this is the bijection between fixed points on the Hilbert scheme and partitions. Bonnafé has also verified Conjecture
3.3
for type
B_{2}
and some non-Weyl group versions.

Following Remark
2.4
, let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
be the ideal
I=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\langle\alpha^{\vee},\alpha\rangle
, and consider
\mathbf{e}\Delta I\subseteq{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]^{W}
, where
\mathbf{e}:=|W|^{-1}\sum_{w\in W}w
. Blowing up
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
in the union of codimension two root hyperplanes
\{\alpha=0\}\times\{\alpha^{\vee}=0\}
gives

Definition 3.4
.

X_{\mathfrak{g},diag}=\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\mathbf{e}(\Delta I)^{d}

Similarly, one can define the isospectral variety as

Definition 3.5
.

Y_{\mathfrak{g},diag}:=\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}(\Delta I)^{d}

Again, since a diagonally alternating polynomial vanishes on the codimension 2 root hyperplanes, there are inclusions
J\subseteq I
and
\Delta A\subseteq{\bf e}\Delta I
, and in particular maps
Y_{\mathfrak{g},diag}\to Y_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
and
X_{\mathfrak{g},diag}\to X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
.

We conjecture that the map
X_{\mathfrak{g},diag}\to X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
is an isomorphism. However, we check in Appendix
B
that the map between isospectral varieties is not an isomorphism in type
B_{3}
. Thus
Y_{\mathfrak{g},diag}
is not the reduced fiber product of
X_{\mathfrak{g},diag}
and
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}
over
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
.

A central point of this paper is that for many purposes, one should use the
symbolic powers

(3.1)

I^{(d)}:=\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\langle\alpha^{\vee},\alpha\rangle^{d}

and define

Definition 3.6
.

X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}:=\Proj\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\mathbf{e}(\Delta^{d}I^{(d)})

More geometrically, since
I
is a radical ideal,
I^{(d)}
is the ideal of functions on
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}
that vanishing to multiplicity
d
along the locus defined by
I
.
Now
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is the
symbolic
blow-up of
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
along the diagonals. In the trigonometric setting, which we review in Section
5
, this was observed in
[
14
]
.

Remark 3.7
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is of type
A
, as mentioned in Remark
2.4
, it follows from Haiman’s results that
I^{(d)}=I^{d}=J^{d}
.

For general
\mathfrak{g}
, one of the immediate strengths of Definition
3.6
is the following:

Theorem 3.8
.

X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is normal. The same holds for the isospectral variety
Y_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
.

Proof.

The proof is a direct adaptation of
[
27
, Theorem 4.7.]
to the rational case (see also
[
14
, Corollary 3.12.]
). More precisely,
\alpha,\alpha^{\vee}
is a regular sequence in
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
, so
I^{(d)}
is integrally closed for all
d
and so is the corresponding symbolic Rees algebra. This proves
Y_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is normal. Taking the quotient by the
W
-action preserves normality, so
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is also normal.
∎

Again, we note that from the inclusions
J^{d}\subseteq I^{d}\subseteq I^{(d)}
it follows that there are maps

X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}\to X_{\mathfrak{g},diag}\to X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}

as well as

Y_{\mathfrak{g},symb}\to Y_{\mathfrak{g},diag}\to Y_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}

Unless these are isomorphisms, it is not a priori easy to decide whether
X_{\mathfrak{g},diag}
or
X_{\mathfrak{g},sgn}
are normal. We also note that it is neither obvious nor relevant for us that the symbolic Rees algebra we consider is finitely generated. In the trigonometric setup, this is ensured by
[
14
, Remark 1.16]
. Nevertheless, combining Theorem
4.3
and
[
8
, Proposition 5.2]
shows that
\mathbf{e}(\Delta^{d}I^{(d)})
is the space of sections of a unique line bundle on
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
.

4.
Rational Cherednik Algebras

In this section, we provide what is probably the most compelling evidence that
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is the “right” variety to consider. The main result is Theorem
4.3
, which is an analog of
[
20
, Proposition 1.7.]
. In particular, we have an analog for all Lie types of the main theorem of Gordon–Stafford
[
20
, Theorem 1.4.]
.

In this section, we fix once and for all the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Let
c:S\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a conjugation-invariant function on the set of reflections
S
of
W
. We denote its values by subscripts, such as
c_{s}:=c(s)
. Unless otherwise stated, we take
c
to be constant. We let
H_{c}
be the rational Cherednik algebra associated to the pair
(\mathfrak{t},W)
. More precisely, following
[
5
]
, we have

Definition 4.1
.

Fix
c
as above. The
rational Cherednik algebra

H_{c}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra generated by

\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and
W
with the relations
wxw^{-1}=w(x),wyw^{-1}=w(y)
for all
y\in\mathfrak{t},x\in\mathfrak{t}^{*},w\in W
,
[y,x]=\langle y,x\rangle-\sum_{s\in S}c_{s}\langle y,\alpha_{s}\rangle\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{s},x\rangle s
and requiring the algebras generated by
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
to be commutative subalgebras.

We note that
H_{c}
is the specialization of
H_{\hbar,c}
at
\hbar=1
, where
H_{\hbar,c}
is the
{\mathbb{C}}[\hbar]
-algebra with the same generators and relations as above, except that we modify the last relation to read

[y,x]=\hbar\langle y,x\rangle-\sum_{s\in S}c_{s}\langle y,\alpha_{s}\rangle\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{s},x\rangle s.

Some important facts about
H_{c}
are as follows:

Proposition 4.2
.

(1)

The algebra
H_{c,\hbar}
has a triangular decomposition: the multiplication map

{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[W]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[\hbar]\to H_{c,\hbar}

is an isomorphism of vector spaces. In particular, for all
c
we have

{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[W]\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{c}

(2)

Let
\deg(x)=\deg(y)=1
for all
y\in\mathfrak{t},x\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and
\deg(w)=0
for
w\in W
. This defines the degree filtration on
H_{c}
whose associated graded equals the smash-product algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]\rtimes W
.

(3)

Let
ord(x)=ord(w)=0,ord(y)=1
. This defines another filtration on
H_{c}
, called the order filtration. The associated graded is still as above. We denote associated graded objects for this filtration by
ogr
.

(4)

We have injections
\iota_{c}:H_{c}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{t}^{reg})\rtimes W
for all
c
. This is known as the Dunkl embedding in the literature. Moreover,
\iota_{c}
become isomorphisms after inverting
\Delta\in H_{c}
. If we equip
H_{c}
with the order filtration and
\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{t}^{reg})\rtimes W
with the filtration induced by the order of differential operators, the injection
\iota_{c}
is also filtration-preserving.

(5)

Let
1
be the map
S\to{\mathbb{C}}
taking all reflections to 1 and write
\mathbf{e}=|W|^{-1}\sum_{w\in W}w
,
\mathbf{e}_{-}=|W|^{-1}\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}w
for the symmetrizing and antisymmetrizing idempotents. Then if
c-1
is spherical in the sense that
\mathbf{e}H_{c-1}\mathbf{e}
is Morita equivalent to
H_{c-1}
, we have algebra isomorphisms
\mathbf{e}H_{c-1}\mathbf{e}\cong\mathbf{e}_{-}H_{c}\mathbf{e}_{-}
.

In the papers
[
20
,
21
]
, Gordon and Stafford show that when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
or
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
, the representation theory of
H_{c}
is closely connected to the geometry of
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
. More precisely, they show using the formalism of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebras that when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
, the spherical subalgebra
U_{c}=\mathbf{e}H_{c}\mathbf{e}\subseteq H_{c}
is in a precise sense a non-commutative deformation of
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
. Many generalizations of this result have since appeared; the most well-understood cases seem to be the wreath product groups
G(\ell,1,n)
, see, for example,
[
17
,
30
,
34
]
and
[
19
]
for further discussion. We will now define similar
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebras for any
H_{c}
as above.

Following
[
20
]
, we will write
U_{c}=\mathbf{e}H_{c}\mathbf{e}
for the spherical subalgebra. Using item (5) of Proposition
4.2
, when
c
is constant, we can define
U_{c+1}-U_{c}
-bimodules

Q_{c}^{c+1}:=\mathbf{e}H_{c+1}\mathbf{e}_{-}\Delta
, where
\Delta=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha
as before.

The maps
\iota_{c}
restrict to injections

\iota_{c}:Q_{c}^{c+1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathfrak{t}^{reg})\rtimes W
for all
c
. Using the multiplicative structure on the target, one can define
U_{c+i}-U_{c+j}
-bimodules

B_{c+i\leftarrow c+j}=Q_{c+i-1}^{c+i}Q_{c+i-2}^{c+i-1}\cdots Q_{c+j}^{c+j+1}.

For convenience, we fix
c
and denote these by
B_{ij}
.
When the parameter
c
is spherical, we have
B_{ij}\cong Q_{c+i-1}^{c+i}\otimes_{U_{c+i-1}}\cdots\otimes_{U_{c+j+1}}Q_{c+j}^{c+j+1}
, as noted in
[
20
, (6.3.2)]
. We will relate the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra constructed using these bimodules to
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
. To do so, we also need an auxiliary object. Following
[
20
, §6.5]
, we define the
(U_{c+k},H_{c})
–bimodules
N_{k}:=B_{k0}\mathbf{e}H_{c}
,
k\geq 0
. These also inherit order filtrations from the ambient ring.
The main theorem of this section is the following.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
W
be a Weyl group with reflection representation
\mathfrak{t}
.
Let
J\subset{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
be the ideal generated by
W
-alternating polynomials, and let
I^{(k)}\subset{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
be the ideal defined in Eq. (
3.1
).

Let
c
be spherical and constant, and
ogrN_{k}
be the associated graded of
N_{k}
under the order filtration.

Then:

(1)

There is a natural injective graded
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]^{W}
–linear homomorphism

J^{k}\Delta^{k}\ \hookrightarrow\ ogrN_{k}.

(2)

As graded submodules of
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
, one has

ogrN_{k}\ =\ I^{(k)}\cdot\Delta^{k}.

In particular,
J^{k}\Delta^{k}\subseteq I^{(k)}\Delta^{k}=ogrN_{k}
, and in type
A
where
I^{(k)}=J^{k}
this recovers the equality
ogrN_{k}=J^{k}\Delta^{k}
of

and it acts on

For

6.
Geometric properties of
X_{\mathfrak{g}}

To understand the eventual applications to coherent sheaves on the varieties
X_{\mathfrak{g}}
, one needs a better handle on their geometry. For
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
, many results use the modular interpretation, which is not available for general
\mathfrak{g}
.

First, we have an analog of Proposition
5.5
for
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

The variety
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
has symplectic singularities.

Proof.

Recall from
[
14
, Section 3]
and
[
11
]
that locally in
T^{*}T^{\vee}/W
, the singularities are modeled by
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{\mathfrak{t}}
for various Cartan subalgebras of centralizers of semisimple elements

with maximal semisimple rank. More precisely, as shown there, there are isomorphisms of formal neighborhoods
(T^{*}T^{\vee}/W)^{\wedge(0,a)}\cong(\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W_{\mathfrak{t}})^{(0,0)}
and these respect the quantizations to trigonometric, resp. rational, Cherednik algebras. This is an incarnation of Borel–de Siebenthal theory, and the
\mathfrak{t}
that appear can be read off by removing a single vertex from the affine Dynkin diagram of
G
. So formally locally, the singularities of
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
are the same as those of
X_{G,symb}
.
By Artin approximation, since we work with varieties (of finite type) over
{\mathbb{C}}
, we can extend the formal local isomorphism to étale local ones.
As the singularities of
X_{G,symb}
are symplectic, a fortiori so are those of
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
.
∎

Finally, we note that
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is only smooth in type A. Indeed, as shown above,
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
has symplectic singularities, and smoothness would imply that
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}\to\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
is a symplectic resolution. By Kaledin
[
25
]
, this can only happen in types ABC. However, in types BC the variety
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is singular by construction, as can be seen from the comparison to quiver varieties in Proposition
A.1
. The above results nonetheless suggest that
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
has appeared in disguise before. We discuss these connections next.

In the simply laced cases, we have

Theorem 6.2
.

X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminalization of
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
.

Proof.

Since
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is normal by Theorem
3.8
, conical, and has symplectic singularities by Proposition
6.1
, we may choose a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminalization
\widetilde{X}\to\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
so that we have an intermediate symplectic partial resolution

\widetilde{X}\to X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}\to\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W.

As explained in
[
6
, Proof of Proposition 2.1]
,
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
corresponds to a face of the ample cone of
\widetilde{X}
. Since
\mathfrak{g}
is simply laced, by, for example,
[
3
]

H^{2}(\widetilde{X},{\mathbb{Q}})\cong{\mathbb{Q}}
. Since
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
is nontrivial, the first map must be an isomorphism.
∎

Based on the theorem, we may think of
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
as a more explicit construction of the implicitly chosen partial symplectic resolution corresponding to the filtered quantization of

appearing in
[

6

For more general
\mathfrak{g}
, one can always choose a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial terminalization

[

]
.
For example, in types

and

can be constructed using quiver varieties or

Similarly to the proof of Theorem

For non-simply laced

6.2.
Further conjectures

In this section, we make some further speculation on
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
.

There is another reason to believe Conjecture
6.4

3.3
. As explained in
[
26

, starting from an elliptic regular semisimple element in the loop Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}
, i.e.,

, one can construct a sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
on
\Hilb^{n}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})
supported on the punctual Hilbert scheme. It is quasi-coherent and
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
-equivariant. Conjecturally,
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
is coherent and the localization theorem in
K
-theory lets one write the
K
-class
[\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}]=\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}(q,t)\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
, where
\widetilde{H}_{\lambda}
are the fixed point classes and
a_{\lambda}(q,t)
are some rational functions in
{\mathbb{C}}(q,t)
. If one believes the triangle of connections from the introduction, as explained in
[
26
]
, the functions

are closely related to Shalika germs for the element
\gamma
.

The main construction in
[
14
]
(which is a precursor to the last section of
[
26
]
) extends to other groups, as discussed in the introduction. For an elliptic regular semisimple element
\gamma\in\mathfrak{g}((t))
, the pushforward of the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
to the naive Coulomb branch
T^{*}T^{\vee}/W
is supported at finitely many points
(0,b)\in T^{*}T^{\vee}/W
, corresponding to the endoscopic decomposition of cohomology. As explained in
[
14
, Theorem 2.16.]
, the stable part of cohomology corresponds to
(0,1)
.

Near this singularity,
T^{*}T^{\vee}/W
is formally locally isomorphic to
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
, so pulling back
X_{G,Coulomb}
and the sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}
along the inclusion of the formal neighborhood, one gets a (quasi-)coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}^{rat}
on
X_{\mathfrak{g},symb}
above the formal neighborhood
(\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W)^{\wedge 0}
. Using the perverse filtration for the BM homology of affine Springer fibers, it should be possible to upgrade this construction to be equivariant for the
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
-action on
\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}/W
. Assuming this, we expect that the
K
-theory localization of
\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}^{rat}
to the fixed points on
X_{\mathfrak{g},rat}
, which can be written as

[\mathcal{F}_{\gamma}]=\sum_{x}a_{\lambda}(q,t)[\delta_{x}],

encodes information about the Shalika germs of
\gamma
. Even without knowing which
x
appear, conjectures of Assem
[
1
]
suggest that the stable Shalika expansion only contains special nilpotent orbits in
\mathfrak{g}((t))
. These are also in bijection with two-sided cells in
W
.

The last conjecture we make is about the braid invariants. Recall that in
[
13
, Theorem 1.5.]
it was shown that the
y
-ified or monodromically deformed version of the Hochschild homology of the Rouquier complex of the
k
th power of the full twist braid in

is isomorphic as a doubly graded
{\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
-module to
J^{k}=I^{(k)}
. The proof of that theorem heavily uses the earlier computation of the ordinary Hochschild homology of this Rouquier complex by Elias and Hogancamp in
[
10
]
, in particular the fact that this homological invariant is
parity
, or in other words that it is only supported in even homological degrees.

For the braid groups of other types, it should still be true that the Hochschild homology of the Rouquier complex of the full twist is parity. The only evidence we have for this is the purity of the corresponding affine Springer fibers. Though we do not do it here, it is also possible to define “link-splitting maps” as in Section 4 of
[
13
]
for other
W
and prove analogs of their flatness properties. Using these properties and the conjectural parity of the
k
:th powers of the full twists, one can show that in general this monodromically deformed invariant is isomorphic as a doubly graded
{\mathbb{C}}[\mathfrak{t}\oplus\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
-module to
I^{(k)}
. We leave this question open.

.

Conjectures
6.3
and
6.6
are true in types
B_{n},C_{n}
.

Proof.

The first one is
[
6
, Remark 2.2.]
, and the second one follows from
[
17
, 3.5.–3.7.]
.
∎

For the second result we need to set up some notation. Recall the core-and-quotient construction establishing a bijection between
\ell
-multipartitions of
n
and partitions of
n\ell+r
with a fixed
\ell
-core partition
s
of size
r
. This gives an injective map

\tau_{s}:P(\ell,n)\to P(n\ell+r)

In our case
r=0
and
\ell=2
, in which case we write
\tau_{s}
as a bijection

\tau:P(2,n)\to\{\lambda\in P(2n)|\lambda\text{ has trivial $2$-core}\}

Given a partition
\lambda
of any size and
\ell\geq 1
, recall that for
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
a box in the Young diagram of
\lambda
is called
i
-addable (resp.
i
-removable) if its content is congruent to
i
mod
\ell
and it is addable (resp. removable). Given a subset
J\subseteq\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}
, we call the
J
-heart of
\lambda
the partition obtained by repeatedly removing all
i
-removable boxes for all
i\in J
. Having the same
J
-heart sets up an equivalence relation on partitions of varying or fixed size, and partitions these sets into equivalence classes called
J
-classes.

Proof.

This is
[

.

Example B.2

\pm 2x_{i},\pm(x_{i}\pm x_{j})

I=\cap_{i}\langle x_{i},2y_{i}\rangle\bigcap\cap_{i<j}\langle x_{i}\pm x_{j},y_{i}\pm y_{j}\rangle.

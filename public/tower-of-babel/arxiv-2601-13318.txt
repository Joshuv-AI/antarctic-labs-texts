Structured eigenbases and pair state transfer on threshold graphs
Leonardo de Lima, Renata Del-Vecchio, Hermie Monterde, Heber Teixeira

Abstract
Recently, Macharete, Del-Vecchio, Teixeira and de Lima showed that a star and any threshold graph on the same number of vertices share the same eigenbasis relative to the Laplacian matrix. We use this fact to establish two main results in this paper. The first one is a characterization of threshold graphs that are simply structured , i.e., their associated Laplacian matrices have eigenbases consisting of vectors with entries from the set { − 1 , 0 , 1 } \{-1,0,1\} . Then, we provide sufficient conditions such that a simply structured threshold graph is weakly Hadamard diagonalizable (WHD). This allows us to list all connected simply structured threshold graphs on at most 20 vertices, and identify those that are WHD. Second, we characterize Laplacian pair state transfer on threshold graphs. In particular, we show that the existence of Laplacian vertex state transfer and Laplacian pair state transfer on a threshold graph are equivalent if and only if it is not a join of a complete graph and an empty graph of certain sizes.

Introduction

Unraveling graph properties through the eigenvectors of matrices associated with graphs constitutes a central topic in spectral graph theory. In particular, graphs with eigenspaces spanned by eigenvectors whose entries are restricted to the sets
\{-1,1\}
and
\{-1,0,1\}
have attracted attention in recent years
[
1
,
5
,
4
,
9
,
14
,
19
,
21
]
. The existence of such structurally simple eigenspaces not only presents significant computational advantages
[
26
]
, but also offers a more straightforward eigenvector analysis
[
28
]
. Hadamard diagonalizable (HD) graphs are an example of such a family. These are graphs whose Laplacian matrices are diagonalized by a Hadamard matrix
[
7
]
. Later, the concept of weakly Hadamard diagonalizable (WHD) graphs was introduced
[
1
]
. These are graphs whose Laplacian matrices are diagonalized by a weak Hadamard matrix, which is a matrix
W
with entries from the set
\{-1,0,1\}
such that
W^{T}W
is tridiagonal. Note that weak Hadamard matrices and WHD graphs generalize Hadamard matrices and HD graphs, respectively. Very recently, WHD graphs have gained interest from theoretical
[
14
,
19
]
and applied perspectives
[
21
]
.

In
[
19
]
, Macharete, Del-Vecchio, Teixeira and de Lima showed that a star and any threshold graph of the same order share the same eigenbasis. In this paper, we use this fact to establish two main results. First, we fully characterize threshold graphs that are simply structured, i.e., graphs whose Laplacian matrices have eigenbases consisting of vectors with entries in
\{-1,0,1\}
. This allows us to determine sufficient conditions for simply structured threshold graphs to be WHD. Since threshold graphs form a subclass of cographs, this provides a partial answer to an open question first raised in
[
1
]
, which asks to characterize all cographs that are WHD. For our second result, we characterize Laplacian pair state transfer on threshold graphs, a type of quantum transport that represents accurate transmission of pairs of entangled qubit states. This contributes the literature on quantum walks on threshold graphs
[
17
,
18
,
25
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce basic notation, definitions, and preliminary results. In Section
3
, we characterize all threshold graphs that are simply structured. This allows us to list all connected threshold graphs on at most 20 vertices with simply structured eigenbases, see Table
1
in the Appendix. In Section
4
we provide sufficient conditions such that a simply structured threshold graph is WHD. In Section
5
, we give a complete characterization of Laplacian pair state transfer on threshold graphs. As a corollary, we show that vertex state transfer and pair state transfer on a threshold graph relative to the Laplacian are equivalent if and only if the threshold graph is not a join of a complete and empty graph of certain sizes. Finally, we discuss open questions in Section
6
.

Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph on
n
vertices with vertex set

and edge set

. The degree of a vertex

where

is the diagonal matrix of the vertex degrees of

. We let
{\mathrm{spec}}(G)
denote the set of distinct Laplacian eigenvalues of
G
. The eigenvalues of
L(G)
(including the repeated ones) are arranged in a nonincreasing order given by

\mu_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\mu_{n-1}\geq\mu_{n}=0.

Let
G
and
H
be two graphs. The
disjoint union

G\sqcup H
of

and

is the graph with vertex set

and edge set

. The

of

and

is the graph obtained from the disjoint union of

and
H
and adding all edges with one endpoint in
G
and the other in
H
. We denote the complete graph, complete graph minus an edge and star on
n
vertices by
K_{n}
,
K_{n}-e
and
S_{n}
, respectively. We write the complement of
G
by
G^{c}
. We also let
\mathbbm{1}_{n}
denote the all-ones vector of order
n
.

An
eigenbasis
of
L(G)
is a basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
composed of eigenvectors of
L(G)
. A basis is
simply structured
if it consists of vectors with entries in
\{-1,0,1\}
. For an eigenvalue
\mu
of
L(G)
, we denoted the eigenspace associated with
\mu
by

\mathcal{E}_{L}(\mu)=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;\mathbf{x}\neq 0:L(G)\mathbf{x}=\mu\mathbf{x}\}.

We say that
\mathcal{E}_{L}(\mu)
is
simply structured
if it admits a simply structured basis, and a graph
G
is
simply structured
if
L(G)
has a simply structured eigenbasis. Note that if
G
is simply structured, then
G
is necessarily
Laplacian integral
, which is to say that
{\mathrm{spec}}(G)
consists of all integers. In recent years, simply structured eigenspaces for certain eigenvalues of matrices are associated with graphs have been studied in
[
3
,
5
,
4
,
28
,
26
,
27
]
.

A matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is called
weak Hadamard
if
W
has all entries from the set
\{-1,0,1\}
and
W^{T}W
is a tridiagonal matrix
[
1
]
. A graph
G
is
weakly Hadamard diagonalizable
(or WHD for short) if the Laplacian matrix of
G
, denoted by
L(G)
, is diagonalizable by a weak Hadamard matrix

, that is,

\displaystyle L(G)=W\Lambda W^{-1},

where
\Lambda
is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues of
L(G).
An analogous definition applies to
Hadamard diagonalizable
graphs. Note that WHD and HD graphs are simply structured by definition. However, simply structured graphs need not be WHD nor HD (see examples in Appendix A). We also note that the definition of WHD may also be adapted to the adjacency matrix or signless Laplacian matrix of a graph. However, since these matrices are nonnegative, the Perron-Frobenius theorem forces WHD graphs relative to these matrices to have
\mathbbm{1}_{n}
as an eigenvector. That is, such graphs are regular, and thus must also be WHD relative to the Laplacian matrix. Hence, the theory of WHD graphs under the Laplacian framework is less restrictive, and therefore more interesting.

In
[
1
]
, the authors raised the problem of finding cographs that are WHD. Since any threshold graph is a cograph, we consider a related problem:

Problem A
.

Determine all connected threshold graphs that are WHD.

There are several ways to define a threshold graph, as discussed in
[
20
]
. We utilize that which involves binary generating sequences.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a sequence of

and

. The threshold graph associated with the binary sequence

is the graph on

(i)

If

, then add the isolated vertex

.

(ii)

Otherwise, add the vertex
i
adjacent to all vertices with label less than
i
.

A vertex

is an isolated vertex if

and a dominating vertex if
b_{i}=1.
Clearly,
G
is connected if and only if
b_{n}=1
. We also follow the convention that
b_{1}=0
for vertex 1. Consequently, we may write
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n})
as

\mathbf{0}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\mathbf{0}^{s_{r}}\mathbf{1}^{t_{r}}

for some positive integers
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r},t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{r}(s_{j}+t_{j})=n
. If
s_{1}=1
, then the connected threshold graph determined by
\mathbf{b}
may be written as

(((((K_{t_{1}^{\prime}}\sqcup K^{c}_{s_{2}})\vee K_{t_{2}})\sqcup K^{c}_{s_{3}})\cdots)\sqcup K^{c}_{s_{r}})\vee K_{t_{r}}

(1)

where
t_{1}^{\prime}=t_{1}+1\geq 2
. However, if
s_{1}\geq 2
, then we may write it as

(((((K_{s_{1}}^{c}\vee K_{t_{1}})\sqcup K_{s_{2}}^{c})\vee K_{t_{2}})\cdots)\sqcup K_{s_{r}}^{c})\vee K_{t_{r}}

(2)

The expressions in (
1
) and (

) coincide with the forms of connected threshold graphs provided by Kirkland and Severini in
[
17
]
.

The computation of the eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix of threshold graphs in terms of vertex degrees is well-known
[
22
]
. More specifically, let
G
be a threshold graph on
n
vertices defined by
(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n})
, with degree sequence
d=(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
, where

\text{tr}(G)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}
. Then, the eigenvalues of

, denoted as

for each

, are given by:

\displaystyle\mu_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}d_{i}+1,&\text{if}\ \ 1\leq i\leq\text{tr}(G),\\
d_{i+1},&\text{if}\ \ \text{tr}(G)+1\leq i\leq n-1,\\
0,&\text{if}\ \ i=n.\end{array}\right.

We also remark that threshold graphs are determined by their Laplacian spectrum; that is, no two threshold graphs on
n
vertices have the same spectrum unless they are isomorphic.

For the star
S_{n}
on
n
vertices, the vectors

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{l}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}1,&\text{ if }\ i<l+1,\\
-l,&\text{ if }\ i=l+1,\\
0,&\text{ if }\ l+1<i\leq n,\end{array}\right.

for each

and
\mathbf{x}^{n}=\mathbbm{1}_{n}
are eigenvectors of

associated with eigenvalues
1
,

and

of multiplicities

,
1
and

, respectively. In
[

]
, the following result is established.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices. Then
G
is a threshold graph if and only if
\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}
is an eigenbasis for

.

3
Characterization of simply structured threshold graphs

To solve Problem
A
, we first characterize threshold graphs whose every eigenspace is simply structured. In this section, we establish a lower bound on the dimension of a simply structured eigenspace for
L(G)
, and then derive a necessary and sufficient condition that provides a complete description of the threshold graphs that are simply structured.

3.1
Dimension of eigenspaces

In this subsection, we address the following problem.

Problem B
.

Determine the minimum number of vectors in a basis of an eigenspace of the Laplacian matrix of a connected threshold graph
G
such that the basis is simply structured.

From Theorem
2.2
,
L(G)
and
L(S_{n})
share an eigenbasis
B=\{\mathbf{x}^{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}^{n}\}
for any threshold
G
of order

. It is clear that the Laplacian eigenbasis of a graph is not unique, and many other Laplacian eigenbasis

can be obtained from

through linear combinations.

Amongst all vectors in

, only
\mathbf{x}^{1}
and
\mathbf{x}^{n}
have all entries in

. In particular,

is a simple eigenvalue of

. Hence,
\mathcal{E}_{L}(0)=\mathrm{span}\ \{\mathbf{x}^{n}\}
, where

, and so
\mathcal{E}_{L}(0)
is simply structured. Now, since the largest eigenvalue of

is

, we address Problem

Building on the previous characterization of simply structured connected threshold graphs, we now obtain WHD threshold graphs by establishing sufficient conditions for a simply structured threshold graph to be WHD. Before this, we introduce some useful results.

5
Pair state transfer on threshold graphs

Let
G
be a connected graph on
n
vertices. The
continuous-time quantum walk
on
G
with Hamiltonian
L(G)
is determined by a one-parameter family of unitary matrices given by

U(t)=e^{itL(G)},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

The matrix
U(t)
governs the propagation of quantum states in
G
under Laplacian dynamics. A pure (quantum) state on
G
is a unit vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
12
]
. In particular, a pure state represented by a standard basis vector is called a
vertex state
, while that of the form
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{e}_{a}-\mathbf{e}_{b})
with
a\neq b
is called a
pair state
.

For
\mu\in{\mathrm{spec}}(G)
,
E_{\mu}
denotes the orthogonal projection matrix onto the eigenspace associated with
\mu
. The
eigenvalue support
of pure state
\mathbf{u}
is the set

{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}=\{\mu\in{\mathrm{spec}}(G):E_{\mu}\mathbf{u}\neq\mathbf{0}\}
.

For a Laplacian integral graph, it is immediate that
{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}
is an integral set for any pure state
\mathbf{u}
. We also note that if
\mathbf{u}
is a pair state, then
0\notin{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}
.

Let
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be real pure states on
G
that are linearly independent. We say that
perfect state transfer
(PST for short) occurs between
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
at time
\tau>0
if
there exists
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
called
phase factor
, such that

U(\tau)\mathbf{u}=\eta\mathbf{v}.

(28)

The minimum such
\tau
is called the
minimum PST time
. If
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
in (
28
) are linearly dependent, then we say that
\mathbf{u}
is
periodic
. We say that
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are
strongly cospectral
if
E_{\mu}\mathbf{u}=E_{\mu}\mathbf{v}
for each
\mu\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}
, in which case, we may partition
{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}
into two sets given by

{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}^{+}=\{\mu\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}:E_{\mu}\mathbf{u}=E_{\mu}\mathbf{v}\}\quad
and
\quad{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}^{+}=\{\mu\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}:E_{\mu}\mathbf{u}=-E_{\mu}\mathbf{v}\}
.

Remark 5.1
.

If
\mathbf{w}
is an eigenvector for
L(G)
associated with
\mu\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}
, then we have
E_{\mu}\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}
, and so
\mathbf{w}^{T}E_{\mu}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{u}
. Consequently,
E_{\mu}\mathbf{u}=\pm E_{\mu}\mathbf{v}
holds if and only if
\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{u}=\pm\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{v}\neq 0
for every eigenvector
\mathbf{w}
associated with
\mu
.

A pure state
\mathbf{u}
is
fixed
if
|{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}|=1
, and this happens if and only if
\mathbf{u}
is an eigenvector for
L(G)

[
12
, Proposition 2.3]
. The involvement of
\mathbf{u}
in strong cospectrality requires that
|{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}|\geq 2

[
12
, Section 4]
. Thus fixed states are not involved in strong cospectrality (and by extension, PST). In particular, the pair state
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{e}_{a}-\mathbf{e}_{b})
is fixed whenever
a
and
b
are twins by
[
24
, Lemma 2.9]
.

Most work on PST deals with vertex states, also called vertex state transfer, see
[
16
,
23
]
for recent work.
However, due to the rarity of vertex state transfer
[
13
]
, there is a growing body of work on PST involving other forms of pure states
[
10
,
11
,
15
]
. In this section, we characterize PST between pair states, also called
pair state transfer
, on threshold graphs. To do this, we first provide a characterization of pair state transfer on Laplacian integral graphs. In what follows,
\nu_{2}(m)
denotes the the largest power of two dividing an integer
m
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{e}_{a}-\mathbf{e}_{b})
and
\mathbf{v}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\mathbf{e}_{c}-\mathbf{e}_{d})
be pair states in
G
.

1.

If
|{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}|=2
, then perfect state transfer occurs between
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
if and only if they are strongly cospectral.

2.

If
G
is Laplacian integral and
|{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}|\geq 3
, then perfect state transfer occurs between
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
if and only if both conditions below hold.

(a)

The pair states
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are strongly cospectral.

(b)

For all
\sigma,\mu\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}^{+}
and
\lambda,\gamma\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}^{-}
, we have

\nu_{2}(\sigma-\mu)>\nu_{2}(\lambda-\mu)=\nu_{2}(\gamma-\mu).

In both cases, for a fixed
\mu\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}^{+}
, the minimum PST time is
\tau=\frac{\pi}{g}
where
g=\operatorname{gcd}\{\mu-\alpha:\alpha\in{\Phi}_{\mathbf{u}}\}
.

Proof.

Statements 1 and 2 are immediate from Theorem 5.2(1) and Corollary 5.7 in
[
12
]
, respectively.
∎

Theorem

(1-2). From (

(((((K^{c}_{s_{1}}\vee K_{t_{1}})\sqcup K^{c}_{s_{2}})\vee K_{t_{2}})\cdots)\sqcup K^{c}_{s_{r}})\vee K_{t_{r}}&\text{if $s_{1}\geq 2$}\end{cases}

where

Next, let

Corollary 5.7

(mod 4) is an infinite family of connected threshold graphs admitting pair state transfer but not vertex state transfer.

6
Open questions

In this work, we characterized simply structured threshold graphs and gave sufficient conditions for them to be WHD. We also characterized Laplacian pair state transfer on threshold graphs. It is worthwhile to determine all threshold graphs (and in general, cographs) that are WHD. We also seek a characterization of cographs that admit either vertex state transfer or pair state transfer. Finally, to obtain more examples, it is desirable to enumerate WHD graphs of small order and to develop further tools to determine WHD graphs of any given order, similar to the work in
[
8
]
. This is particularly beneficial when the weak Hadamard matrix in question has pairwise orthogonal columns, since it is easier to determine the existence of vertex state transfer in such a WHD graph
[
21
]
.

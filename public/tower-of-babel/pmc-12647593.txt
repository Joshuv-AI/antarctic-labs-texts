Predictive maintenance programs for aircraft engines based on remaining useful life prediction
Authors: Fei Xue, Guodong Jin, Lining Tan, Chengxi Zhang, Ye Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The remaining useful life (RUL) and utilization strategy of an aero-engine are related to the flight safety of an aircraft, which directly affects the flight itself and the safety of the occupants. Aiming at the complexity of aero-engine condition monitoring data, an aero-engine predictive maintenance planning framework based on RUL prediction is proposed, which aims to analyze the engine RUL and design predictive maintenance strategies. First, a deep learning integrated model (Trans-LSTM), including Transformer and Long Short Memory Network Model (LSTM), is proposed. Second, Bayesian optimization is used to optimize the hyperparameters of the integrated model to further improve the accuracy of the predictive model. Based on the prediction data, an engine alarm threshold was designed. When the threshold is triggered during engine operation, a predictive maintenance task is applied. The optimal alarm threshold under the Trans-LSTM model is calculated by comparing the total flight cost and other indicators under different flight hours. Experimental results demonstrate that the data-driven predictive maintenance strategy can monitor engine status in real time, promptly identify potential failure risks, and prevent engines from operating in an unknown state. This effectively reduces the risk of sudden engine failures and significantly enhances flight safety compared to the periodic maintenance strategy. In addition, through the accurate prediction of the engine state and reasonable arrangement of maintenance tasks, it can effectively reduce the cost of using the engine and avoid the waste of manpower, material and financial resources caused by excessive maintenance. Moreover, it enhances engine task availability, prolongs the engine’s optimal operating period, better meets the actual needs of air transportation, and brings higher economic benefits and operational efficiency for airlines, thus showing great value and potential in practical application.

Introduction

As the core power unit of an airplane, the performance and reliability of an aviation engine are directly related to the safety and economy of flight1,2. Efficient engine operation is essential for ensuring flight regularity, reducing operating costs, and enhancing airline competitiveness3,4. Aero-engines, subjected to extreme conditions like high temperature, pressure, and speed over extended periods, suffer wear, fatigue, and corrosion5,6. These damage accumulation processes can degrade engine performance and potentially cause severe safety accidents7,8. Therefore, accurately predicting the RUL of an aero-engine and formulating a scientific and reasonable maintenance strategy accordingly is of vital significance to ensure the safe and efficient operation of air transportation.

In recent years, RUL prediction methods have been developed significantly with the development of sensor technology and the improvement of data acquisition capability. The prediction methods are divided into two main categories: Model-Based and Data-Driven. Model-driven methods rely on in-depth understanding and accurate modeling of physical processes, while data-driven methods learn the intrinsic laws of the system by analyzing a large amount of operational data9.

Model-driven methods often employ stochastic process models, such as Gaussian, Markov, and Wiener processes, to represent physical degradation10,11. Stochastic process modeling, grounded in probability and statistics, integrates the system’s physical degradation modes. Consequently, RUL prediction models based on stochastic processes have garnered extensive research attention12–14. However, the accuracy of stochastic process models heavily depends on a priori knowledge of the physical degradation process15. With the increasing complexity of modern systems, accurately modeling degradation via stochastic processes becomes challenging.stochastic process predicting a complex system is almost an impossible task. At the same time, the development of data connectivity and sensors makes learning system degradation based on a large amount of data an accurate, efficient, and cost-effective solution.

Data-driven methods use machine learning and deep learning algorithms to build prediction models by analyzing sensor data during engine operation, without the need for detailed modeling of physical processes. Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) can model nonlinear relationships for RUL prediction16. However, due to the limitations of MLP in dealing with time series data, it cannot capture the temporal dependencies in the data well17,18. Babu et al.19 proposed a convolutional neural network (CNN) approach to predict the RUL of an aircraft engine. The CNN is able to extract features from the input data by means of the convolutional and pooling layers to capture the local features and spatial correlations in the data20. In aircraft engine RUL prediction, CNN can effectively handle complex patterns in sensor data to improve prediction accuracy. Compared with MLP, CNN has superior RUL prediction performance. Li et al.21 used a deep CNN (DCNN) method for RUL prediction, which further improves the model’s ability to extract features from complex data by increasing the depth of the network, thus improving the prediction accuracy. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) utilize loop structures to capture temporal dependencies by propagating hidden states over time22. Traditional RNNs are prone to the problem of gradient vanishing or gradient explosion when dealing with long sequence data, which limits their application in long sequence RUL prediction. In order to solve the shortcomings of traditional RNNs, Hochreiter and Schmidhuber23 proposed the LSTM model. LSTM is able to effectively solve the gradient vanishing problem by introducing a gating mechanism, which can better capture the temporal dependencies in long sequence data24.

Later, some researchers proposed LSTM-based models and their variants to predict RUL in industrial systems. For instance, Cho et al.25 integrated physical information into LSTM models to enhance RUL prediction accuracy. In addition, there are researchers who introduced the attention mechanism into LSTM to form an LSTM-Attention model, which further improves the model’s ability to pay attention to the important time steps and thus improves the prediction performance26. In addition, researchers have further proposed Bidirectional LSTM Network (Bi-LSTM) models and their variants for evaluating system RUL27. Bi-LSTM is able to capture the temporal dependencies in the data in a more comprehensive way by taking into account both forward and backward time series information. For instance, a study proposed a CNN-Bi-LSTM model, which employs CNNs for feature extraction and Bi-LSTM for capturing time-series relationships, thereby achieving accurate RUL prediction24.

Synthesizing the above literature we find that existing studies have explored deeply on RUL prediction, but have not considered engine maintenance decision making after RUL prediction. Kabashkin et al.28 proposed a digital twin and data-driven framework for aero-engine maintenance decision making. The model monitors the operational status of an aero-engine in real-time by integrating Internet of Things (IoT) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies, and models and predicts the health of the engine using digital twin technology. This framework is capable of dynamically adjusting maintenance schedules based on real-time data, thus optimizing maintenance decisions and improving engine reliability and economy. Parii et al.29 proposed a machine failure prediction method based on survival analysis. The method predicts the RUL of an aero-engine by analyzing its historical data and develops a maintenance plan based on the prediction. The survival analysis method is able to optimize maintenance decisions by considering multiple influencing factors and improving the accuracy of failure prediction. Wang et al.30 developed a failure prediction model based on hybrid machine learning and statistical methods for anomaly detection in flight data. The model predicts the probability of potential failures by analyzing key parameters in flight data and provides support for maintenance decisions. This approach can effectively reduce the occurrence of unexpected failures and improve the foresight and efficiency of maintenance.

Existing theories also consider the problem of RUL-based maintenance strategy, but the dynamic maintenance survival strategy of aero-engine for multiple airplanes is still in a blank state. For this reason, we propose a data-driven predictive maintenance framework for aircraft engines. First, we propose an integrated model based on Transformer and LSTM, which measures various data of an aero-engine during operation through sensors. In order to improve the accuracy of prediction, we propose to optimize the hyperparameters of the integrated model using Bayesian optimization. Based on the predicted RUL, a survival maintenance strategy for multiple engines is considered when the maintenance warning threshold is triggered. We transform the survival maintenance strategy problem into an optimization problem. The main contributions of this study are as follows: A data-driven predictive maintenance program for aero-engines based on RUL prediction is proposed;An integrated model based on Transformer and LSTM structure is designed to realize accurate prediction of engine RUL;The proposed model is compared with other predictive maintenance models to verify the designed warning thresholds for maintaining the engine within the safe operating range. The CMAPSS dataset is used as an example to compute the optimal thresholds, and compared with Chen’s model31, the optimal threshold maintenance strategy avoids engine failures and fully utilizes the engine service life as much as possible, which leads to superior results in terms of both total cost and mission availability.The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: the second section accepts the relevant methods involved in the maintainability strategy, the third section presents the experimental validation and results in conjunction with the dataset, and the last section presents the conclusions.

Methods

We propose a new strategy approach to predict the RUL of an aero-engine using real-time condition monitoring information and combine it with relevant information such as mission time for optimal decision making. As shown in Fig. 1, its maintenance strategy is divided into online part and offline part.

The offline part uses aero-engine historical data to establish a Trans-LSTM network. This network mines variables in the data that reflect engine degradation and completes model pre-training using common engine RUL prediction labels.

The online part is to utilize real-time aero-engine multi-sensor data for real-time prediction of engine RUL and to calculate maintenance strategy-related covariates based on the predicted RUL in a predictive maintenance framework. The obtained maintenance strategy-related covariates provide the optimal maintenance strategy for the decision module.Figure 1Framework of proposed predictive maintenance strategy.

If the working conditions are not considered, the latent time series information is more difficult to be mined. Therefore, K-means clustering method is used to preprocess the data to expose the sequence information brought by the working conditions. K-means clustering method can automatically identify its internal grouping through the distribution of eigenvalues without data labeling, so as to mine the latent patterns36. Its model results are simple and fast running, especially suitable for dealing with multi-dimensional data, and the calculation process is more efficient and provides visualized classification results. In the paper, the K-means clustering algorithm is used to classify and analyze patterns of relevant parameters. Trans-LSTM integration model

We presents a Trans-LSTM model to predict the RUL of an aero-engine using sensors from multiple time series. The integrated model combines the features of multiple models to capture more complex patterns and relationships in the data. Firstly The Transformer model processes variable-length sequence data and captures dependencies within sequences via a self-attention mechanism, thereby extracting rich feature representations. Meanwhile, Transformer has parallel computing capability with faster computation. Secondly, the LSTM model accepts the feature data delivered by Transformer and remembers the important information in the sequence through its unique memory capability and filters out the useful information through the gating mechanism, and finally outputs the result. Bayesian optimization of hyperparameters

In deep learning models, hyperparameters are usually set manually or through experience before training. However, as the amount of hyperparameter data increases, the time required to train the Trans-LSTM model increases. Manual tuning of hyperparameters will inevitably reduce the efficiency. Meanwhile, the obtained prediction results may not be optimal. For this reason, in order to improve the prediction performance and generalization ability of the Trans-LSTM model, a Bayesian optimization (BO) method is proposed to optimize the hyperparameters of the Trans-LSTM model. For hyperparameter optimization, there are various methods such as Bayesian optimization, evolutionary algorithm, grid search and random search. And the Bayesian optimization of hyperparameters has been proved to be very capable in finding the optimal hyperparameters [22]. The BO method consists of two main parts: one is the Gaussian process model, and the other is the acquisition function. The search space covered key hyperparameters, including the embedding dimension (d_model), the number of multi-head attention heads (n_head), the number of encoder layers (num_layers), the dimension of the feedforward network (dim_feedforward), the dropout rate, the batch size, the learning rate , and the number of training epochs. The specific ranges are shown in Table 3. The parameter ranges listed in Table 3 were determined based on previous experiments and literature research, aiming to cover the typical range of hyperparameters commonly used in deep learning. For example, the dropout rate range (0.1–0.5) is used to control overfitting; the learning rate range (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{-5}$$\end{document}–\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$10^{-2}$$\end{document}) covers the typical values of the Adam optimizer.

We used Gaussian Process as the surrogate model and used Expected Improvement as the acquisition function. The optimization process converged within 100 iterations, and each iteration took approximately 0.1 hours (based on NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti GPU). The total computing cost was controlled within 10 hours, which is feasible for engineering purposes.

This setup ensures that the model can still obtain a hyperparameter combination close to the optimal one with limited computing resources, improving the generalization ability and practicality of the model.

Through the search of BO, we can finally use the optimal hyperparameter data set for Trans-LSTM.

Maintenance decisions based on prognostic information

The RUL of an aero-engine is directly related to the cost of flight for airlines, as well as the safety of flight for all passengers. At the same time, we consider that too frequent inspection and maintenance of aero-engine is also a huge loss of manpower and materials. Based on the Trans-LSTM model, the multi-dimensional sensor data of the aero-engine can accurately identify the RUL value under the condition of fewer inspections, and better guide the maintenance work. It reduces the cost of regular inspection of aero-engine, but also ensures the optimal benefit under the condition of engine safety.

For a certain aero engine, during its normal use after installation and commissioning, the state data monitored by its engine sensors are stored and input into our RUL prediction model to obtain the predicted value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hat{RUL}$$\end{document}. So we consider that the RUL prediction model has a certain error, so we need to check the engine before reaching the predicted value. If the true RUL corresponding to the engine condition data inspected at this point is already less than a threshold value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document}, preventive maintenance of the engine is required. In the preventive maintenance work, generally for the replacement of parts and other work, its maintenance cost is less, we set the cost of preventive maintenance as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_p$$\end{document}. In the process of daily use, for an engine to begin to use t moments after the start of the specific action is determined using the Eq. (20):20\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \text {Maintenance} = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} 1 & \text {} t \le \hat{RUL} - \delta \\ 0 & \text {} t > \hat{RUL} - \delta \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}When \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Maintenance=1$$\end{document}, it indicates that predictive maintenance is required for the RUL of the aero-engine and if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Maintenance=0$$\end{document} it means that no maintenance is required. Conversely, if a severe failure, such as damage, occurs prior to inspection, that is, when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$t > \text {RUL}_{\text {True}}$$\end{document} (the actual usage time exceeds the engine’s true RUL), this scenario is termed corrective maintenance. In the event of engine failure, both the aircraft and engine are significantly impacted, resulting in substantial repercussions for the airline. Therefore, the maintenance cost in this case is designated as the corrective maintenance cost \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_c$$\end{document}.

And for a particular aero-engine, the maintenance cost \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C_i}$$\end{document} is a specific determination scheme for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C_c}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C_p}$$\end{document}:21\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} C_i = {\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} C_{pi} & \text {} t < RUL_{True} \\ C_{ci} & \text {} t \ge RUL_{True} \end{array}\right. } \end{aligned}$$\end{document}i stands for the number of the engine. For a mission of a particular duration, it requires n aero-engines in rotation. The total maintenance time for preventive maintenance is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_p$$\end{document} and the total maintenance time for corrective maintenance is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_c$$\end{document}. For a mission with a defined period of time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{total}$$\end{document}, its maintenance time cost also needs to be considered throughout the mission completion. Conclusion

In the paper, a predictive maintenance method based on RUL prediction is proposed. The method firstly combines LSTM and Transformer models to design a deep learning model for aero-engine RUL prediction, and combines with Bayesian optimization method to obtain the set of hyperparameters under the optimal result. The designed RUL model is examined using NASA’s aero-engine dataset, and a large improvement is achieved in both evaluation criteria of the three datasets, proving the accuracy of the RUL prediction model. Following this, a predictive maintenance strategy model is constructed based on the RUL prediction model. Under the scenario of fixed total flight duration, our predictive maintenance strategy model outperforms the traditional timed maintenance model in three indexes: TCF, PCF, and MA, which confirms the advancement of the maintenance strategy model of the paper.

The paper has limited consideration of spare parts, personnel, and other factors. Future work will incorporate these factors into maintenance strategy modeling to develop a more accurate and effective model.

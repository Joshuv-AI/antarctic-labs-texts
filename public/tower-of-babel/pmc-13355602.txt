Response to the Letter to the Editor Entitled “Clarifying Renal-Risk Inference From High-Sensitivity C-Reactive Protein in CKD”
Authors: Jean-Michel Halimi, Laurent Fauchier
Published: 2026

The Author Replies:

We agree with Wang et al.1 that death was a competing event for kidney failure. Unfortunately, the limited tools on the TriNetX online statistical platform did not allow us to perform a Fine-Gray analysis. High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) was dichotomized from a single baseline value,2 and this choice is debatable. Our results are exploratory by nature.

In addition, Wang et al.1 observed that the SD for hsCRP in the < 2 mg/l hsCRP group appeared unexpectedly large.2 This was related to the initial TriNetX query configuration—some patients could have >1 hsCRP measurement; the platform statistics could occasionally rely on more recent hsCRP values measured after the index event definition, including values above the predefined threshold. This led to an artificial inflation of the SD in the low-hsCRP group and could modify the results, and most probably underestimate the outcome differences between the 2 groups.

To address this issue, the query was rerun using a stricter index-event definition and exclusion criteria, ensuring consistency between cohort allocation and hsCRP values used for baseline analyses. All analyses were repeated using the corrected dataset (15,880 patients/group). The overall results and observed trends remained highly consistent with the original analysis, and even better, the end-stage kidney disease risk was significant for the whole population. In the high hsCRP group, despite no documented infection or surgery before inclusion, hsCRP values were on average higher (40.1 ± 61.0 mg/l) than that in the original dataset (12.8 ± 21.2 mg/l). A separate corrigendum will be published. We thank Wang et al.1 for their vigilance.

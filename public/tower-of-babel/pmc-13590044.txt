Cardiometabolic comorbidity burden and low domain-specific health-promoting lifestyle scores among ethnic minority adults in Yunnan, China: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Hanglin Yu, Shiyuan Wen, Hansen Li, Lifang Zhao, Haodong Tian, Zhaozhi Liu, Li Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo examine associations between cardiometabolic comorbidity burden and sample-defined low total and domain-specific health-promoting lifestyle scores among ethnic minority adults in Yunnan Province, China.MethodsThis cross-sectional secondary analysis used survey data collected from October 2024 to June 2025 in Yunnan Province, China. The analytic sample comprised 735 Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults with previously physician- or medical-institution-diagnosed hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or dyslipidemia.ResultsAmong participants, 385 had one condition, 256 had two, and 94 had three. Compared with one condition, two conditions were associated with higher odds of a low total score (odds ratio [OR] 1.66, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.11, 2.50), whereas three conditions and the ordinal trend were not significant. For the physical activity domain, two conditions (OR 1.64, 95% CI 1.13, 2.38) and three conditions (OR 1.98, 95% CI 1.19, 3.30) were associated with higher odds of a low score, and the ordinal trend remained significant after false discovery rate correction. Sensitivity analyses were consistent.ConclusionsIn this cross-sectional sample, greater cardiometabolic comorbidity burden was associated most consistently with a sample-defined low physical activity domain score. These associations may inform prospective evaluation of domain-specific behavioral needs and culturally appropriate physical activity support.

Introduction

Noncommunicable diseases are major contributors to morbidity and premature mortality worldwide (World Health Organization, 2025). Hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and dyslipidemia commonly coexist through shared metabolic and behavioral pathways (International Diabetes Federation, 2021; Pirillo et al., 2021; Saklayen, 2018; World Health Organization, 2023) and are highly prevalent in China (Lu et al., 2017; Pan et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021). Their long-term management therefore requires attention to coexisting cardiometabolic conditions as well as individual risk factors.

Multimorbidity increases healthcare utilization, treatment complexity, and self-management burden (Barnett et al., 2012; Fortin et al., 2012; National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, 2016; The Academy of Medical Sciences, 2018; Violan et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2014). Although guidance emphasizes patient-centered management of multiple chronic conditions (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, 2016; The Academy of Medical Sciences, 2018), existing research has focused largely on prevalence, healthcare consequences, and clinical management (Barnett et al., 2012; Fortin et al., 2012; Violan et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2014). Less is known about whether increasing cardiometabolic comorbidity burden is associated differently with specific modifiable health behaviors, information that aggregate disease counts alone cannot provide.

Health-promoting lifestyle encompasses health responsibility, physical activity, nutrition, interpersonal relations, stress management, and spiritual growth (Pender et al., 2015; Walker et al., 1987; Walker et al., 1995). These domains represent distinct aspects of health management and may relate differently to increasing comorbidity burden. Health responsibility and nutrition reflect daily disease-management behaviors; interpersonal relations and stress management reflect social and psychosocial resources; spiritual growth reflects perceived well-being; and physical activity additionally depends on functional capability, perceived safety, opportunities, and environmental context (Bauman et al., 2012; Sallis et al., 2015). Accordingly, a total score may obscure domain-specific patterns.

Physical activity warrants particular attention because it is recommended for adults with chronic conditions (Bull et al., 2020; World Health Organization, 2020), yet activity levels and adherence vary substantially among people with multimorbidity (Jørgensen et al., 2022). Exercise therapy may improve physical and psychosocial outcomes, although evidence certainty remains limited (Bricca et al., 2020). The sample-defined low total lifestyle score was therefore retained as the primary outcome, with all six domain-specific low scores examined as secondary outcomes. Given its cardiometabolic relevance, the physical activity domain received particular interpretive attention, and sensitivity analyses using alternative outcome and exposure specifications were used to assess robustness.

Yunnan Province in Southwest China is ethnically and geographically diverse. Recent regional studies have reported clustering of behavioral and metabolic cardiovascular risk factors among predominantly Naxi and Tibetan adults in plateau areas (Zhu et al., 2025), a comparatively high burden of chronic disease and multimorbidity among Bai adults in rural Southwest China (Du et al., 2023), and associations of socioeconomic conditions and healthcare access with diabetes treatment and self-management among ethnic minority adults in Yunnan (Su et al., 2016). These findings indicate heterogeneous cardiometabolic, behavioral, socioeconomic, and healthcare contexts relevant to examining domain-specific health-promoting lifestyles among Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults.

Previous multimorbidity research has primarily addressed prevalence, healthcare use, treatment complexity, and self-management burden (Barnett et al., 2012; Fortin et al., 2012; National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, 2016; The Academy of Medical Sciences, 2018; Violan et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2014), whereas Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile studies have mainly examined overall lifestyle patterns or individual chronic conditions (Cao et al., 2012; Cao et al., 2016; Leung et al., 2020; Pender et al., 2015; Walker et al., 1987; Walker et al., 1995). Evidence remains limited on whether increasing cardiometabolic comorbidity burden is differentially associated with overall and domain-specific lifestyle scores, particularly among Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults in Yunnan. We therefore examined associations of cardiometabolic comorbidity burden and specific disease patterns with sample-defined low total and domain-specific health-promoting lifestyle scores. By examining both aggregate and domain-specific outcomes across increasing comorbidity burden, this study adds evidence from an underrepresented multiethnic population in Southwest China.

Methods

Study design and population

This cross-sectional secondary analysis used survey data collected from October 2024 to June 2025 in Yunnan Province, Southwest China. The source survey recruited permanent residents aged 18 years or older across seven primary survey areas and 14 secondary survey sites. Nonprobability sampling combined region- and ethnicity-based quota control with convenience recruitment through communities, healthcare and health-examination settings, and online questionnaires. The same eligibility criteria, questionnaire, and quality-control procedures were used across recruitment channels. Because no complete individual-level sampling frame was available, sampling weights were not applied.

The source dataset included 1988 adults from Yunnan Province. Participants were eligible for the present analysis if they were aged 18 years or older, self-identified as Tibetan, Bai, or Naxi, and reported at least one previously physician- or medical-institution-diagnosed condition among hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and dyslipidemia. Other ethnic groups, participants with none of the three conditions, and records missing variables required for fully adjusted analysis were excluded, yielding 735 participants (Fig. 1). Measures

The primary exposure was cardiometabolic comorbidity burden, defined as the number of previously diagnosed conditions among hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and dyslipidemia. Disease status was based on participant-reported previous diagnoses made by a physician or medical institution; no new diagnoses were made from study-specific measurements or laboratory tests. Participants were classified as having one, two, or three conditions. Seven mutually exclusive patterns were defined: isolated hypertension, isolated type 2 diabetes, isolated dyslipidemia, hypertension plus type 2 diabetes, hypertension plus dyslipidemia, type 2 diabetes plus dyslipidemia, and all three conditions.

Health-promoting lifestyle was assessed using a Chinese-language 52-item version of the Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile-II Revised. It includes health responsibility (9 items), physical activity (8), nutrition (9), spiritual growth (9), interpersonal relations (9), and stress management (8). Items are scored from 1 (never) to 4 (routinely), yielding a total score of 52 to 208, with higher scores indicating a more favorable lifestyle. Chinese versions have demonstrated acceptable reliability and construct validity (Cao et al., 2012; Cao et al., 2016). However, ethnicity-specific cultural or linguistic validation and measurement-invariance testing have not been established for Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults. Internal consistency was acceptable within these groups in the source survey, but this does not establish cross-ethnic measurement equivalence. Because no established clinical threshold exists for low Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile-II Revised scores, the first quartile of each score distribution was used to define sample-specific low scores. The primary outcome was a low total score and secondary outcomes were low scores in each domain. Cutoffs were 96 for the total score, 15 for health responsibility, 13 for physical activity, 16 for nutrition, 14 for stress management, 16 for interpersonal relations, and 16 for spiritual growth. Because scores were discrete, ties at a cutoff could classify more than 25% of participants as low scoring. These cutoffs indicate relative standing within this sample and are not clinical thresholds. Prespecified covariates were age group (18–44, 45–59, or ≥ 60 years), sex, ethnicity, education, marital status, work status, body mass index category, smoking or drinking history, and disease duration. Body mass index was categorized as <18.5, 18.5–23.9, 24.0–27.9, or ≥ 28.0 kg/m2. Smoking or drinking history indicated current use or either behavior within the previous 12 months. Disease duration was categorized as 0–3, >3–6, >6–10, or > 10 years; for participants with multiple conditions, it was measured from the earliest reported diagnosis. Physical activity measured outside the lifestyle scale was excluded because physical activity was already represented as an outcome domain.

Statistical analysis

Participant characteristics were summarized as n (%) or mean (standard deviation) and compared using chi-square or Monte Carlo simulated chi-square tests for categorical variables and analysis of variance for continuous variables. Logistic regression estimated odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for sample-defined low total and domain scores. Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 adjusted for age, sex, and ethnicity; and Model 3 additionally adjusted for education, marital status, work status, body mass index category, smoking or drinking history, and disease duration. Trend tests modeled comorbidity burden ordinally. Fully adjusted models were used for domain-specific and exploratory pattern analyses, with Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate correction (Benjamini & Hochberg, 1995). Sensitivity analyses modeled the physical activity score continuously and compared one versus two or more conditions using the fully adjusted covariate set. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant before multiple-testing correction. All analyses were conducted using R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Ethical compliance

The original survey protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Southwest University (SWU-ETH-2023-09-12-003). All participants provided informed consent before data collection. Study procedures complied with institutional requirements for protection of human participants, including safety, privacy, confidentiality, and secure handling of de-identified data. The present secondary analysis used de-identified records.

Results

Participant selection and characteristics

Of 1988 adults in the source dataset, 820 were excluded because they were not Tibetan, Bai, or Naxi and 433 because they had none of the three target conditions; no eligible record was missing a variable required for analysis. The final sample comprised 735 adults: 385 (52.4%) with one condition, 256 (34.8%) with two, and 94 (12.8%) with three (Fig. 1). Body mass index category differed across burden groups (P = 0.02). Mean total lifestyle score decreased from 103.8 with one condition to 101.5 with two and 100.6 with three (P < 0.01). Physical activity showed the clearest domain-specific gradient (P < 0.01), while nutrition scores also differed across groups (P = 0.01) (Table 1). Association with the sample-defined low total score

Associations with the low total score are reported in Supplementary Table S5, Part A. In the fully adjusted model, two conditions were associated with higher odds relative to one condition (OR 1.66, 95% CI 1.11, 2.50). The estimate for three conditions was imprecise and not statistically significant (OR 1.34, 95% CI 0.75, 2.36), and the ordinal trend was also not statistically significant (OR 1.27, 95% CI 0.98, 1.65).

Domain-specific associations

Fully adjusted domain-specific estimates are shown in Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table S5, Part B. Compared with one condition, two conditions (OR 1.64, 95% CI 1.13, 2.38) and three conditions (OR 1.98, 95% CI 1.19, 3.30) were associated with low physical activity. The ordinal trend was also significant (OR per additional condition 1.46, 95% CI 1.15, 1.85) and remained significant after false discovery rate correction. Exploratory analyses by specific comorbidity pattern

Specific patterns are shown in Supplementary Table S3. Isolated hypertension was most common (20.8%), followed by hypertension plus type 2 diabetes (16.9%), isolated dyslipidemia (16.5%), isolated type 2 diabetes (15.1%), all three conditions (12.8%), hypertension plus dyslipidemia (11.0%), and type 2 diabetes plus dyslipidemia (6.9%). In adjusted exploratory models using isolated hypertension as the reference, hypertension plus dyslipidemia had an elevated estimate for low total score, while hypertension plus type 2 diabetes and all three conditions had elevated estimates for low physical activity. None remained significant after false discovery rate correction (Supplementary Table S4).

Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses supported the physical activity finding (Fig. 4; Supplementary Table S6). Compared with one condition, adjusted mean physical activity scores were lower with two conditions (beta −0.79, 95% CI −1.26, −0.32) and three conditions (beta −1.29, 95% CI −1.96, −0.62); each additional condition was associated with a 0.69-point lower score (95% CI −0.99, −0.38). When burden was dichotomized, two or more conditions were associated with higher odds of low physical activity (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.23, 2.43) and low total score (OR 1.57, 95% CI 1.08, 2.27); both remained significant after false discovery rate correction.Fig. 4Sensitivity analyses of cardiometabolic comorbidity burden and physical activity-related health-promoting lifestyle outcomes among Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults, Yunnan Province, China, October 2024 to June 2025.Models adjusted for age group, sex, ethnicity, education level, marital status, work status, body mass index category, smoking or drinking history, and disease duration. Discussion

In this cross-sectional sample of Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults, greater cardiometabolic comorbidity burden was associated most consistently with low physical activity. Adults with two or three conditions had higher odds of a sample-defined low physical activity score than those with one condition, with a significant ordinal trend after multiple-testing correction. Sensitivity analyses supported this finding, whereas associations with the total lifestyle score and other domains were less consistent.

Several mechanisms could plausibly contribute to the physical activity association. Adults with multiple cardiometabolic conditions may experience greater symptom burden, competing treatment demands, exercise-safety concerns, or reduced confidence, while activity also depends on functional, social, and environmental factors (Bauman et al., 2012; Sallis et al., 2015). These factors were not directly measured and should be considered hypotheses requiring prospective investigation.

Our physical activity finding is broadly consistent with a systematic review reporting heterogeneous activity levels and guideline adherence among adults with multimorbidity, including populations with hypertension and type 2 diabetes, although evidence certainty was very low (Jørgensen et al., 2022). The present domain-specific pattern differs from Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile-II research in Hong Kong, where several lifestyle domains were associated with obesity or selected chronic conditions (Leung et al., 2020). Differences in population, disease definitions, outcome operationalization, and the focus on comorbidity burden may partly explain these findings. Exercise trials suggest potential physical and psychosocial benefits in multimorbidity (Bricca et al., 2020), but such evidence does not establish causality for the association observed here.

Regional evidence further contextualizes these findings. Cardiovascular risk factors vary among Naxi and Tibetan adults in plateau areas of Yunnan (Zhu et al., 2025), multimorbidity burden differs across ethnic groups including Bai adults in Southwest China (Du et al., 2023), and socioeconomic conditions and healthcare access are associated with diabetes management among ethnic minority adults in Yunnan (Su et al., 2016). Although these studies did not assess Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile-II Revised domains, they highlight regional, socioeconomic, behavioral, and healthcare heterogeneity relevant to interpretation and generalizability.

The weaker total-score association may reflect aggregation across six distinct lifestyle domains (Walker et al., 1987; Walker et al., 1995), whereby lower physical activity can be diluted by higher scores elsewhere. Quartile-based outcomes indicate relative standing within this sample and are not clinically validated thresholds. The consistent physical activity association in continuous-score analyses reduces reliance on the quartile-based definition.

These findings suggest that domain-specific assessment, particularly of physical activity, warrants further prospective evaluation in adults with multiple cardiometabolic conditions. Future studies should examine functional capacity, symptoms, perceived exercise safety, access, and activity preferences. Because this study was cross-sectional and did not evaluate an intervention, it cannot determine whether domain-specific assessment or culturally adapted physical activity support improves outcomes.

Strengths include the focus on an underrepresented multiethnic population, separation of total and domain-specific outcomes, multiple-testing correction, and sensitivity analyses using alternative exposure and outcome definitions.

Several limitations require consideration. The cross-sectional design precludes temporal and causal inference. Nonprobability quota and convenience recruitment and unweighted analysis may introduce selection bias, so observed proportions should not be interpreted as population prevalence estimates. Cardiometabolic conditions were based on participant-reported previous diagnoses and lacked detailed measures of disease control, severity, medication adherence, treatment burden, symptoms, exercise concerns, or self-efficacy, preventing direct evaluation of proposed mechanisms. Lifestyle outcomes were self-reported, and quartile-based low-score definitions were sample-specific and not clinically validated. Complete-case restriction and inclusion of only Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults limit generalizability. Smoking and drinking were combined, disease duration may incompletely represent multimorbidity histories, and pattern-specific analyses involved small subgroups. Because low-score outcomes were common, ORs should be interpreted as odds measures.

The Chinese-language Health-Promoting Lifestyle Profile-II Revised has not undergone ethnicity-specific cultural or linguistic validation or formal measurement-invariance testing in Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults. Although internal consistency was acceptable within these groups, differences in comprehension, cultural interpretation, or response patterns may have influenced between-group comparisons.

Conclusions

Among Tibetan, Bai, and Naxi adults with cardiometabolic conditions in Yunnan Province, greater comorbidity burden was most consistently associated with a sample-defined low physical activity score, while associations with the total lifestyle score and other domains were less consistent. Longitudinal studies are needed to clarify temporal relationships, and intervention studies should evaluate culturally and locally appropriate physical activity support.

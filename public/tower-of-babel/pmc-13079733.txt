Author Correction: Regional biodiversity monitoring reveals severe population decline of the Atlantic horseshoe crab (Limulus polyphemus) in Long Island Sound, USA
Authors: Sarah C Crosby, Rebha Raviraj, Marisa Fajardo, Justin P Susarchick, Domenic Romanello, Samantha M Rowland
Published: 2026

Correction to: Scientific Reports 10.1038/s41598-025-14910-3, published online 27 August 2025

The original version of the Article contained errors by omission. Additional information on results, discussion, and methodology for the Central Sound dataset with and without the most recent 2 years of available data (2021–2022) has now been included. Consequently, in the Results section,

“In addition to increasing prosomal width in the Central Sound, an increase in L. polyphemus weight was observed over time in years prior to 2019 (Fig. 6; linear model, R2 = 0.70, F (df = 1; 19) = 43.44, p < 0.0001). In years with a greater number of L. polyphemus caught per tow, the corresponding increase in weight per tow was found to increasingly deviate from a one-to-one relationship, indicating that in years with a higher count of L. polyphemus, relatively more smaller crabs were observed (linear regression, R2 = 0.809, F (df = 1; 23) = 97.53, p < 0.0001).”

now reads:

“In addition to increasing prosomal width in the Central Sound, an increase in L. polyphemus weight was observed over time (linear model, R2 = 0.33, F (df = 1; 22) = 10.63, p = 0.0036), with a stronger relationship observed in years prior to 2019 (Fig. 6; linear model, R2 = 0.70, F (df = 1; 19) = 43.44, p < 0.0001). In years with a greater number of L. polyphemus caught per tow, the corresponding increase in weight per tow was found to increasingly deviate from a one-to-one relationship, indicating that in years with a higher count of L. polyphemus, relatively more smaller crabs were observed (Full data record: linear regression, R2 = 0.809, F (df = 1; 23) = 97.53, p < 0.0001; prior to 2019: linear regression, R2 = 0.776, F (df = 1; 20) = 69.45, p < 0.0001).”

In the Discussion section,

“Furthermore, changes in abundance may be driven by local, regional, and larger-scale factors, complicating decision-making. For example, low counts observed in 3 of the datasets in 1985 could be temporally aligned with impacts from the North Atlantic Oscillation, but assigning causality to such relationships requires more robust longitudinal datasets.”

now reads:

“Furthermore, changes in abundance may be driven by local, regional, and larger-scale factors, complicating decision-making. For example, low counts observed in 3 of the datasets in 1985 could be temporally aligned with impacts from the North Atlantic Oscillation, but assigning causality to such relationships requires more robust longitudinal datasets. Our analysis of the Central Sound dataset both with and without the most recent years underscores the need for long-term monitoring and the need for multiple years of data following a suspected breakpoint to assess changes in population dynamics like that suspected in the Central Sound after 2020. In this case, analysis of a temporal subset of the data showed a significant population decline, whereas analysis of the full data record available revealed no significant linear trend. Potential positive impacts of management intervention may be difficult to discern analytically in the years immediately following such changes.”

Additionally, in the Methods section,

“Data analyses were conducted in R version 4.4.1 and R Studio45. To assess changes in the number of L. polyphemus observed (per trawl or seine), a generalized linear model with a Gamma distribution and log link was used, including year, site, and their interaction as predictors. The emmeans package in R was used to estimate the slope of year for each site individually (emtrends function), and tested whether these slopes differed significantly from zero46. For all linear models, normality and homogeneity of variance were checked using Shapiro–Wilk tests and review of residual versus fitted plots, respectively. Heteroskedasticity was tested using a Breusch-Pagan test. Linear regression was used to test for a relationship with time for the proportion of female crabs, weight of L. polyphemus per tow, and prosomal width; as those models met the required assumptions (checked using Shapiro–Wilk tests, Breusch–Pagan tests, and inspection of Q–Q plots). Due to evidence of a nonlinear relationship and heteroskedasticity when applying the linear model, the 3 most recent years of data (2019, 2021, 2022) were excluded when testing for a relationship between weight per individual (total weight divided by total number of horseshoe crabs) and time in the Central Sound dataset.”

now reads:

“Data analyses were conducted in R version 4.4.1 and R Studio45. To assess changes in the number of L. polyphemus observed (per trawl or seine), a generalized linear model with a Gamma distribution and log link was used, including year, site, and their interaction as predictors. The emmeans package in R was used to estimate the slope of year for each site individually (emtrends function), and tested whether these slopes differed significantly from zero46. For all linear models, normality and homogeneity of variance were checked using Shapiro–Wilk tests and review of residual versus fitted plots, respectively. Heteroskedasticity was tested using a Breusch-Pagan test. Linear regression was used to test for a relationship with time for the proportion of female crabs, weight of L. polyphemus per tow, and prosomal width; as those models met the required assumptions (checked using Shapiro–Wilk tests, Breusch–Pagan tests, and inspection of Q–Q plots). Due to evidence of a nonlinear relationship and heteroskedasticity when applying the linear model, the 3 most recent years of data (2019, 2021, 2022) were excluded when testing for a relationship between weight per individual (total weight divided by total number of horseshoe crabs) and time in the Central Sound dataset.

The long-term trends in the Central Sound dataset were assessed both with and without the most recent 2 years of available data at the time of analysis (2021–2022) due to visual inspection of the data revealing the apparent breakpoint occurring at that time. Pélissié and colleagues (2024)47 provide an analytical framework for assessment of ecological time series data for abrupt shifts. When applying their analytical approach to the Central Sound data, the ability to detect a breakpoint between 2019 and 2021 was limited by two datapoints at the edge of a record of this length being of insufficient length in this method to constitute a separate regime. However, when the required length of the record to constitute a breakpoint was reduced to two years in the analysis, a breakpoint was calculated both between 2004 and 2005 and between 2019 and 2021, thus supporting the idea that the last two years from Central Sound dataset may constitute a distinct regime. Additional years of data will allow a more definitive assessment in the future. When using this method to assess the pre-2020 subset of the Central Sound dataset, the analysis supported both an abrupt negative shift after 2004 and a significant negative linear trend in the time series. As such, results are presented for this dataset both including and excluding those 2 years.”

On the proportion of derangements in affine classical groups
Jessica Anzanello

Abstract
. We derive explicit formulas for the proportion of derangements and of derangements of p p -power order in the affine unitary group AU m ⁡ ( q ) \AU_{m}(q) over the finite field 𝔽 q 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{2}} of characteristic p p . Both formulas rely on a result of independent interest concerning integer partitions: we obtain a generating function for partitions λ = ( λ 1 , … , λ m ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}) into m m parts, for which λ 1 = 1 \lambda_{1}=1 or there exists k ∈ { 2 , … , m } k\in\{2,\dots,m\} such that λ k − 1 > λ k = k \lambda_{k-1}>\lambda_{k}=k . Moreover, when p p is odd, we conjecture remarkably simple formulas for the proportions of derangements in the finite affine symplectic group ASp 2 ​ m ⁡ ( q ) \ASp_{2m}(q) , and in the affine orthogonal groups AO 2 ​ m + 1 ⁡ ( q ) \AO_{2m+1}(q) and AO 2 ​ m ± ⁡ ( q ) \AO^{\pm}_{2m}(q) , and we also propose conjectures for the proportions of derangements of p p -power order. We further reduce the proofs of these conjectures to establishing three q q -polynomial identities.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a non-trivial finite transitive permutation group on a set
\Omega
. An element
g\in G
is a
derangement
if it has no fixed points on
\Omega
, that is, if
\alpha^{g}\neq\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\Omega
.

Let
\mathcal{D}(G)
be the set of derangements of
G
and let

\delta(G)\coloneqq\frac{|\mathcal{D}(G)|}{|G|}

denote the proportion of derangements in
G
, which one can view as the probability that a randomly chosen element of
G
is a derangement.
A result going back to Jordan asserts that
\delta(G)>0
, and, as discussed by Serre in
[
Ser03
]
, Jordan’s theorem has significant implications in diverse areas, ranging from number theory to topology.

The study of derangements in transitive permutation groups has a long history: in 1708, Montmort, in his foundational work on probability theory
[
dM80
]
, introduced the well-known “inclusion-exclusion principle” to obtain the following formula for the proportion of derangements in the symmetric group
S_{n}
of degree
n
:

\delta(S_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{i!}.

In more recent years, an extensive field of research has emerged on counting derangements and investigating derangements with specific properties, such as prescribed order. For a comprehensive survey on the subject, we refer the reader to the introductory chapter of
[
BG16
]
.

In this article, we are interested in the proportion of derangements in finite affine classical groups.

In
[
Spi17
]
, Spiga provided a notably simple and explicit formula for the proportion of derangements in the affine general linear group
\AGL_{m}(q)
, which can be viewed as a natural
q
-analogue of the similar problem for the symmetric group, namely:

\delta(\AGL_{m}(q))=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{q^{i(i+1)/2}}.

Let
\U_{m}(q)\leq\mathrm{GL}_{m}(q^{2})
,
\Sp_{2m}(q)\leq\mathrm{GL}_{2m}(q)
,

\Or_{2m+1}(q)\leq\mathrm{GL}_{2m+1}(q)
and
\Or^{\pm}_{2m}(q)\leq\mathrm{GL}_{2m}(q)
denote the isometry groups of, respectively, a unitary, symplectic, quadratic and quadratic of plus (resp. minus) type form over the field
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{e}}
, where
e=2
in the unitary case, and
e=1
otherwise. Moreover, let
p
be the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
.

Continuing the work of
[
Spi17
]
for
\AGL_{m}(q)
, the aim of this article is to investigate explicit and simple formulas for the proportions of derangements in the affine classical groups
\AU_{m}(q),\ASp_{2m}(q)
,
\AO_{2m+1}(q)
and
\AO^{\pm}_{2m}(q)
, in their natural action.
We establish exact formulas for the proportion of derangements, as well as for those of
p
-power order, in the finite affine unitary group
\AU_{m}(q)
. In addition, when the characteristic
p
of the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is odd, we conjecture explicit formulas for the proportions of derangements and of derangements of
p
-power order in the affine symplectic group
\ASp_{2m}(q)
, and in the affine orthogonal groups
\AO_{2m+1}(q)
and
\AO^{\pm}_{2m}(q)
. We further show that proving these conjectures reduces to establishing three
q
-polynomial identities of independent interest.

The affine unitary case is studied in Section
3
.
The main result obtained is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

The proportion of derangements in
\AU_{m}(q)
is

\frac{1}{q+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{(-q)^{m(m+3)/2}}\right).

Given a non-negative integer
j
and an indeterminate
x
, we define

(1)

(x)_{j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }j=0,\\
\prod_{i=1}^{j}(1-x^{i})=(1-x)(1-x^{2})\cdots(1-x^{j})&\text{ if }j\geq 1.\end{cases}

Let
G
be one of our affine classical groups, and let
\delta_{p}(G)
denote the proportion of derangements of
p
-power order in
G
: this quantity plays a crucial role in computing exact formulas for
\delta(G)
, and, when
G=\AU_{m}(q)
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The proportion of derangements of
p
-power order in
\AU_{m}(q)
is

\frac{1}{q^{m}(q+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i}((-q)^{i+1}-1)}{(-q)^{i(i+1)/2}(-1/q)_{m-i}}.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on some delicate arguments involving integer partitions. In particular, we obtain the following generating function for a certain type of partitions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The generating function for partitions
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
into
m
parts, for which
\lambda_{1}=1
or there exists
k\in\{2,\dots,m\}
such that
\lambda_{k-1}>\lambda_{k}=k
, is

\frac{x^{m}}{1-x}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i}x^{i(i-1)/2}(x^{i}-1)}{(x)_{m-i}}.

We now turn to the affine symplectic and orthogonal groups, for which we conjecture the following concise expressions for the proportions of derangements.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Assume that the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is odd. The following formulas for the proportions of derangements in, respectively,
\ASp_{2m}(q)
,
\AO_{2m+1}(q)
and
\AO^{\pm}_{2m}(q)
hold.

i.

\delta(\ASp_{2m}(q))=\frac{1}{q+1}\left(1-\frac{1}{(-q)^{m(m+2)}}\right);

ii.

\delta(\AO_{2m+1}(q))=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{2q^{(m+1)^{2}}};

iii.

\delta(\AO^{\pm}_{2m}(q))=\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{2q^{m(m+1)}}
.

As previously mentioned, our strategy for obtaining compact formulas for
\delta(G)
relies on establishing suitable formulas for
\delta_{p}(G)
. In Lemmas
4.1
and
5.2
we derive formulas for
\delta_{p}(\ASp_{2m}(q))
,
\delta_{p}(\AO_{2m+1}(q))
and

\delta_{p}(\AO^{\pm}_{2m})
, each given by a
q
-polynomial expression involving integer partitions. Consequently, the proof of Conjecture
1.4
is reduced to verifying the following three
q
-polynomial identities, and in Sections
4
and
5
we prove Conjecture
1.4

assuming
Conjecture
1.5
.

Conjecture 1.5
.

The following identities hold.

i.

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|=2m\\
i\text{ odd }\Rightarrow m_{i}(\lambda)\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-q^{-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}o(\lambda)}\prod_{i}(1/q^{2})_{\lfloor\frac{m_{i}(\lambda)}{2}\rfloor}}=\frac{1}{q^{m}(q+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}(q^{2i+1}+1)}{q^{i(i+1)}(1/q^{2})_{m-i}};

ii.

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|=2m+1\\
i\text{ even}\Rightarrow m_{i}(\lambda)\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-q^{-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}o(\lambda)}\prod_{i}(1/q^{2})_{\lfloor\frac{m_{i}(\lambda)}{2}\rfloor}}=\frac{1}{q^{m}(1/q^{2})_{m}}+\frac{1}{q^{m+1}}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{q^{i(i+1)}(1/q^{2})_{m-i}};

iii.

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\lambda|=2m\\
i\text{ even}\Rightarrow m_{i}(\lambda)\text{ even}\end{subarray}}\frac{1-q^{-\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}}{q^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}o(\lambda)}\prod_{i}(1/q^{2})_{\lfloor\frac{m_{i}(\lambda)}{2}\rfloor}}=\frac{1}{q^{m}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{q^{i(i-1)}(1/q^{2})_{m-i}}.

The notation for partitions used in the statement is recalled in the Preliminaries, see Section
2
.
The above identities have been computationally verified for
m<50
.

We note that estimates for the proportions of derangements under consideration can be obtained by combining [
[
GT03
]
, Proposition 1.1] with results in
[
NP98
]
on the number of derangements for classical groups, in their natural action on the set of non-zero vectors of the natural module.

One of the main tools used to prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, and Conjecture
1.4
(assuming Conjecture
1.5
)
is the cycle index for the finite classical groups, introduced by Fulman in
[
Ful99
]
.
Cycle indices are generating functions that encode useful information for studying random matrices depending only on their conjugacy classes. They constitute a powerful tool for counting derangements and, notably, where a fundamental tool in Fulman and Guralnick’s remarkable proof of the Boston-Shalev conjecture, which states the proportion of derangements in a transitive action of a simple group on a set
\Omega
, with
|\Omega|>1
, is uniformly bounded away from 0 ( see
[
FG03
]
,
[
FG12
]
,
[
FG17
]
and
[
FG18
]
). We will recall the definitions of cycle index for each classical group at beginning of the corresponding section.

This work builds on the approach developed for
\AGL_{m}(q)
in
[
Spi17
]
, and we adopt the same notation to ensure consistency.

Let

Let

Affine unitary groups

The unitary group
\U_{m}(q)
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}(q^{2})
that preserves a non-degenerate unitary form on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
We begin this section by recalling the definition of cycle index for the finite unitary groups, following the exposition in [
[
Ful99
]
, Section 4.1]. To this end, we first review the structure of the conjugacy classes of
\U_{m}(q)

Given a polynomial
\phi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[z]
, with non-zero constant term, define the polynomial
\widetilde{\phi}
by

\widetilde{\phi}=(\phi(0))^{-q}z^{\deg(\phi)}\phi^{q}(z^{-1}),

where
\phi\mapsto\phi^{q}
is the map which raises each coefficient of
\phi
to the

The conjugacy class of an element
a\in\U_{m}(q)
is uniquely determined by its rational canonical form and, in
[
Wal63
]
, Wall proved that it is encoded by the following combinatorial data: to each monic, non-constant, irreducible polynomial
\phi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, the element
a
associates a partition
\lambda_{\phi}
of a non-negative integer
|\lambda_{\phi}|
, determined by its rational canonical form.
The collection
(\lambda_{\phi})_{\phi}
represents a conjugacy class in
\U_{m}(q)
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

|\lambda_{z}|=0
,

(ii)

\lambda_{\widetilde{\phi}}=\lambda_{\phi}
,

(iii)

\sum_{\phi}|\lambda_{\phi}|\deg(\phi)=m
.

Recall the definition of the function
(x)_{j}
in Eq. (
1
) and define

c_{\mathrm{GL},\phi,q}(\lambda)\coloneqq q^{\deg\phi\left(\sum_{i}(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})^{2}\right)}\prod_{i}(1/q^{\deg\phi})_{m_{i}(\lambda)}.

From now on, unless otherwise stated, all polynomials
\phi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[z]
will be assumed monic and irreducible.

Wall computed the size of the conjugacy class corresponding to the data
(\lambda_{\phi})_{\phi}
to be

\frac{|\U_{m}(q)|}{\prod_{\phi}B(q,\lambda_{\phi})},

where

B(q,\lambda_{\phi})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{|\lambda_{\phi}|}c_{\mathrm{GL},z-1,(-q^{\deg\phi})}(\lambda_{\phi})&\text{ if }\phi=\widetilde{\phi},\\
(c_{\mathrm{GL},z-1,q^{2\deg\phi}}(\lambda_{\phi}))^{1/2}&\text{ if }\phi\neq\widetilde{\phi}.\end{cases}

The

From [

Lemma 3.3

Now, from the proof of [

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(1+x^{m-j})(1-x^{m-j})\frac{x^{(m-j)^{2}+j}(x)_{m-1}}{(x)_{m-j-1}(x)_{m-j}(x)_{j}}

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
m\geq 1
. We say that a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m})
is
cute
if there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
such that (either
\lambda_{1}=1
, or)
\lambda_{k-1}>\lambda_{k}=k
.

For example, the partitions
(6,5,3,3,2)
,
(4,2,1,1)
and
(1,1,1)
are cute.

As observed in the proof of Lemma
3.3
,
\lambda
is cute if it has Durfee square
k
(for some
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
) and the partition to the right of the Durfee square has exactly
k-1
parts.

Partitions satisfying the condition
\lambda_{k}=k
for some
k
where studied by Spiga in
[
Spi17
]
, in relation to the proportion of derangements in
\AGL_{m}(q)
. In
[
BK22
]
, Blecher and Knopfmacher refer to the condition
\lambda_{k}=k
as a
fixed point
, extending the concept of fixed points from permutation group theory to integer partitions. In addition to the fixed point condition, cute partitions impose the extra requirement that
\lambda_{k-1}>k
, which can be rephrased by saying that the conjugate of the partition to the right of the Durfee square must have first part equal to
k-1
(see also
[
HS24
]
and
[
Hop24
]
).

Theorem
1.3
states that the generating function for cute partitions into
m
parts is

\frac{x^{m}}{1-x}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i}x^{i(i-1)/2}(x^{i}-1)}{(x)_{m-i}}.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we first establish the following auxiliary lemma. I thank Fedor Petrov for providing the proof of this lemma, in response to a question posed on Mathoverflow, see
[
Pet
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
a
and

be non-negative integers. The following two sets have the same cardinality:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(a,b)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\{(\lambda,\mu)\mid\lambda,\mu\textup{ partitions},\mathrm{pt}(\lambda)=b,|\lambda|+|\mu|=a,\lambda_{1}=\mu_{1}+1\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a,b)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\{(\lambda,\mu)\mid\lambda,\mu\textup{ partitions},\mathrm{pt}(\lambda)=b,|\lambda|+|\mu|=a,\lambda\textup{ cute},\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}\},

where in the set
\mathcal{B}(a,b)
,

can be the empty partition.

Before starting the proof, let us clarify the definitions of the sets
\mathcal{A}(a,b)
and
\mathcal{B}(a,b)
with an example. Let

b=4
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(9,4)=\{

\displaystyle((2,1,1,1),(1,1,1,1)),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(9,4)=\{

\displaystyle((3,1,1,1),(2,1)),

\displaystyle((1,1,1,1),(2,2,1)),

\displaystyle((3,2,1,1),(1,1)),

\displaystyle((2,2,1,1),(1,1,1))

\displaystyle((3,2,2,2),\emptyset),

\displaystyle((2,2,2,1),(1,1))

\displaystyle((4,2,2,1),\emptyset),

\displaystyle((2,2,2,2),(1))\}

\displaystyle((5,2,1,1),\emptyset)\}.

Proof of Lemma

.

Let us define a bijection

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let

F(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda\text{ cute}}y^{\mathrm{pt}(\lambda)}x^{|\lambda|}

denote the generating function for cute partitions, encoded by size and by number of parts. Define

\bar{F}(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{x^{m}}{1-x}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{(-1)^{i}x^{i(i-1)/2}(x^{i}-1)}{(x)_{m-i}}\right)y^{m}.

To prove the claim, we need to show that
F(x,y)=\bar{F}(x,y)
.
Let

\displaystyle F_{1}(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{a=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{a}}{(x)_{a}}y^{a},

\displaystyle F_{2}(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}(x^{b}-1)x^{\frac{b(b+1)}{2}}(-y)^{b},

and observe that

(13)

\bar{F}(x,y)=\frac{1}{1-x}F_{1}(x,y)F_{2}(x,y).

Recalling that
x^{a}/(x)_{a}
is the generating function for partitions into
a
parts, we get that

F_{1}(x,y)=\sum_{\lambda}x^{|\lambda|}y^{\mathrm{pt}(\lambda)}

is the generating function for partitions, encoded by size and by number of parts. From [
[
And98
]
, p. 16] (or from any standard textbook on partitions), it follows that

F_{1}(x,y)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})^{-1}.

Now, we turn our attention to

(14)

F_{2}(x,y)=\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}(x^{b}-1)x^{\frac{b(b+1)}{2}}(-y)^{b}=\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{b(b+3)}{2}}(-y)^{b}-\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{b(b+1)}{2}}(-y)^{b}.

In view of Eq. (
14
), let us define

\displaystyle F_{2}^{1}(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}x^{\frac{b(b+3)}{2}}(-y)^{b}

and

\displaystyle F_{2}^{2}(x,y)\coloneqq\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}x^{\frac{b(b+1)}{2}}(-y)^{b}.

We now require Jacobi’s triple product identity, which states that

(15)

\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{j^{2}}z^{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+zq^{2j-1})(1+z^{-1}q^{2j-1})(1-q^{2j}).

We apply Eq. (
15
) to both
F_{2}^{1}(x,y)
and
F_{2}^{2}(x,y)
, obtaining:

\displaystyle F_{2}^{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}x^{\frac{b(b+3)}{2}}(-y)^{b}=\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}(x^{\frac{1}{2}})^{b^{2}}(-yx^{\frac{3}{2}})^{b}

\displaystyle=\prod_{b=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{\frac{3}{2}}x^{\frac{2b-1}{2}})(1-y^{-1}x^{-\frac{3}{2}}x^{\frac{2b-1}{2}})(1-x^{b})

\displaystyle=\prod_{b=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{b+1})(1-y^{-1}x^{b-2})(1-x^{b}),

and

\displaystyle F_{2}^{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}x^{\frac{b(b+1)}{2}}(-y)^{b}=\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}(x^{\frac{1}{2}})^{b^{2}}(-yx^{\frac{1}{2}})^{b}

\displaystyle=\prod_{b=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{2b-1}{2}})(1-y^{-1}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}x^{\frac{2b-1}{2}})(1-x^{b})

\displaystyle=\prod_{b=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{b})(1-y^{-1}x^{b-1})(1-x^{b}).

Note that
F_{2}^{1}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{xy}F_{2}^{2}(x,y)
. Therefore,

(16)

F_{2}^{1}(x,y)-F_{2}^{2}(x,y)=-\frac{xy+1}{xy}\prod_{b=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{b})(1-y^{-1}x^{b-1})(1-x^{b}).

The function we are interested in is
F_{2}(x,y)
, which is obtained by restricting the infinite sum

F_{2}^{1}(x,y)-F_{2}^{2}(x,y)=\sum_{b=-\infty}^{\infty}(-y)^{b}x^{b(b+3)/2}-(-y)^{b}x^{b(b+1)/2},

to the non-negative indices
b\geq 0
. In view of Eq. (
16
), let
F_{3}(x,y)
be the part of the product
-\frac{xy+1}{xy}\prod_{b=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{b})(1-y^{-1}x^{b-1})
corresponding to the non-negative powers of
y
.
Then

F_{2}(x,y)=F_{3}(x,y)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-x^{n})

and hence, using Eq. (
13
), we have

\bar{F}(x,y)=\frac{1}{1-x}F_{1}(x,y)F_{2}(x,y)=\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}(1-x^{n})\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})^{-1}F_{3}(x,y).

Recall that we want to prove that
F(x,y)=\bar{F}(x,y)
, and to do this, it suffices to verify that
F(x,y)
satisfies the same identity as
\bar{F}(x,y)
, that is

(17)

F(x,y)\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}(1-x^{n})^{-1}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})^{-1}F_{3}(x,y).

To conclude the proof, we proceed to show that for every pair of non-negative integers
a
and
b
, the coefficients of
x^{a}y^{b}
on both sides of Eq. (
17
) coincide.

Note that
\prod_{n=2}^{\infty}(1-x^{n})^{-1}
is the generating function for partitions with no part of length 1, hence, dually, it is the generating function for partitions
\mu
satisfying
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
. Since
F(x,y)
is the generating function for cute partitions, the coefficient of
x^{a}y^{b}
on the left hand side of Eq. (
17
) equals the cardinality of the set:

(18)

\mathcal{B}(a,b)=\{(\lambda,\mu)\mid\lambda,\mu\text{ partitions },\mathrm{pt}(\lambda)=b,|\lambda|+|\mu|=a,\lambda\text{ cute, }\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}\}.

We now turn our attention to the right hand side of Eq. (
17
). Since
F_{3}(x,y)
is the partial sum of the infinite product
-\left(1+\frac{1}{xy}\right)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})(1-y^{-1}x^{n-1})
,
corresponding to the non-negative powers of
y
, we consider instead the expanded product

(19)

-\left(1+\frac{1}{xy}\right)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+yx^{n}+y^{2}x^{2n}+y^{3}x^{3n}+\cdots)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})(1-y^{-1}x^{n-1})=-A(x,y)-A^{\prime}(x,y)

where we have defined

\displaystyle A(x,y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+yx^{n}+y^{2}x^{2n}+y^{3}x^{3n}+\cdots)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-y^{-1}x^{n-1}),

and

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(x,y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{xy}A(x,y).

Let us compute the contribution to
x^{a}y^{b}
from each factor in
A(x,y)
(respectively
A^{\prime}(x,y)
), taking into account that the contributions from the infinite product
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})(1-y^{-1}x^{n-1})
(respectively
\frac{1}{xy}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-yx^{n})(1-y^{-1}x^{n-1})
) must come from a non-negative power of
y
. This means that:

(a)

in
A(x,y)
we must take at least as many
y
s from the second infinite product as
y^{-1}
from the third infinite product;

(b)

in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
, since there is a factor
(xy)^{-1}
, we are required to take at least one more
y
from the second infinite product than the number of
y^{-1}
from the third infinite product.

Let us formalise what we have just described.
We define an
(a,b)
-choice
in
A(x,y)
(resp. in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
), or simply, a
choice
, to be a contribution to the summand
x^{a}y^{b}
in
A(x,y)
(resp. in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
). Precisely, an
(a,b)
-choice is uniquely determined by selecting terms

y^{v_{1}}x^{v_{1}c_{1}},\dots,y^{v_{l_{1}}}x^{v_{l_{1}}c_{l_{1}}}

from the first infinite product in
A(x,y)
(resp.
A^{\prime}(x,y)
) (and 1 from all other factors in the first product), terms

-yx^{a_{1}},\dots,-yx^{a_{l_{2}}}

from the second infinite product (and 1 from all other factors in the second product), and terms

-y^{-1}x^{b_{1}},\dots,-y^{-1}x^{b_{l_{3}}}

from the third infinite product (and 1 from all other factors in the third product).
The following conditions must be satisfied:

(a)

for
A(x,y)
:
l_{2}\geq l_{3}
,
\sum_{k}v_{k}+l_{2}-l_{3}=b
and
\sum_{k}v_{k}c_{k}+\sum_{i}a_{i}+\sum_{j}b_{j}=a
;

(b)

for
A^{\prime}(x,y)
:
l_{2}>l_{3}
,
\sum_{k}v_{k}+l_{2}-l_{3}=b+1
and
\sum_{k}v_{k}c_{k}+\sum_{i}a_{i}+\sum_{j}b_{j}=a+1
.

Determining the coefficient of
x^{a}y^{b}
in
A(x,y)
(resp. in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
) amounts to summing over all
(a,b)
-choices in
A(x,y)
(resp. in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
).
When performing this summation, many choices cancel out pairwise. We now describe how this cancellation occurs.

Given a fixed
(a,b)
-choice
c
in
A(x,y)
(resp.
A^{\prime}(x,y)
), let
n
be the maximal index for which either
y^{v}x^{vn}
(for some
v\geq 1
), or
-yx^{n}
appears in
c
. There are three possible cases.

•

If both
y^{v}x^{vn}
and
-yx^{n}
appear in
c
, then
c
cancels with the choice obtained by replacing
y^{v}x^{vn}
with
y^{v+1}x^{(v+1)n}
and removing
-yx^{n}
(this corresponds to taking 1 in the factor
1-yx^{n}
of the second infinite product).

•

If
y^{v}x^{vn}
appears in
c
but
-yx^{n}
does not, then
c
cancels with the choice obtained by replacing
y^{v}x^{vn}
with
y^{v-1}x^{(v-1)n}
and selecting
-yx^{n}
in the second infinite product.

•

Finally, if
-yx^{n}
appears in
c
but
y^{v}x^{vn}
does not, then this choice cancels with the choice where
-yx^{n}
is replaced by
yx^{n}
(this corresponds to selecting
yx^{n}
in the factor
1+yx^{n}+y^{2}x^{2n}+\dots
in the first infinite product, and selecting
1
in the factor
1-yx^{n}
of the second infinite product).

Observe that we cannot always perform such cancellations: when a term
-yx^{n}
is removed from
c
, we must ensure that the number of terms of the form
-yx^{m}
in the cancelling choice is at least, for
A(x,y)
, (resp. strictly bigger than, for
A^{\prime}(x,y)
) the number of terms of the form
-y^{-1}x^{m^{\prime}}
Recall that this corresponds the conditions
l_{2}\geq l_{3}
for
A(x,y)
, and
l_{2}>l_{3}
for
A^{\prime}(x,y)
.
Moreover, note that this cancellation process is an involution: applying the same procedure to the cancelling choice recovers the original choice
c
.

Therefore, in
A(x,y)
, the
(a,b)
-choices that remain (i.e., those that do not cancel) are exactly those where the number of
y
-terms equals the number of
y^{-1}
-terms, and
c_{l_{1}}\leq a_{l_{2}}
(recall that these quantities appeared in the definition of an
(a,b)
-choice above). Similarly, in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
, the remaining
(a,b)
-choices are those where the number of
y
s is exactly one more than the number of
y^{-1}
s, and
c_{l_{1}}\leq a_{l_{2}}
. Furthermore, observe that all remaining choices in
A(x,y)
have positive sign, while all those in
A^{\prime}(x,y)
carry negative sign.

Given this analysis, determining the coefficient of
x^{a}y^{b}
in
A(x,y)
(respectively
A^{\prime}(x,y))
amounts to counting the number of the following possibilities.

(1)

For
A(x,y)
, choose:

(i)

a non-negative integer
j
, and integers
0<c_{1}<\cdots<c_{j}
and
0<v_{1}<\cdots<v_{j}
(for the terms
y^{v_{i}}x^{v_{i}c_{i}}
in the first infinite product),

(ii)

k
positive integers
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}
(for the terms
yx^{a_{i}}
in the second product),

(iii)

k
non-negative integers
0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{k}
(for the terms
y^{-1}x^{b{{}_{i}}}
in the third product),

satisfying

c_{j}\leq a_{k},\text{ }\sum_{i=1}^{j}v_{i}=b,\text{ }\sum_{i=1}^{j}v_{i}c_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}+b_{i}=a.

(2)

For
A^{\prime}(x,y)
, choose:

(i)

a non-negative integer
j
, and integers
0<c_{1}<\cdots<c_{j}
and
0<v_{1}<\cdots<v_{j}
(for the terms
y^{v_{i}}x^{v_{i}c_{i}}
in the first infinite product),

(ii)

k
positive integers
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}
(for the terms
yx^{a_{i}}
in the second product),

(iii)

k-1

0\leq b_{1}<b_{2}<\cdots<b_{k-1}
(for the terms
y^{-1}x^{b{{}_{i}}}
in the third product),

satisfying

c_{j}\leq a_{k},\text{ }\sum_{i=1}^{j}v_{i}=b,\text{ }\sum_{i=1}^{j}v_{i}c_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}b_{i}=a+1.

Then, to obtain the coefficient of
x^{a}y^{b}
on the right hand side of Eq. (
17
), subtract the number of choices
in (2) from the number of choices in (1).

Note that, for both
A(x)
and
A^{\prime}(x)
, the choice at point (i), with the condition that

, corresponds to selecting a partition
\lambda
with maximal part of size at most

.

Now, for
A(x)
, the choices at point (i) and (ii), correspond to selecting a partition
\mu
with maximal part equal to

. Indeed, for such a partition, let

be the size of its Durfee square, then

correspond to the part of the Ferrers diagram above the main diagonal, and

correspond to the part below it (as in the first example of Fig.
5
).

On the other hand, for

, the choices at point (i) and (ii) correspond to selecting a partition
\mu
with maximal part
a_{k}
and
\mu_{2}<\mu_{1}
: indeed, for such a partition, let
\mu_{1}=a_{1}
, and if we write
\overline{\mu}=(\mu_{2},\dots,\mu_{r})
, then, as above,
a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
correspond to the part of the Ferrers diagram of

above the diagonal of its Durfee square, and

to the part below.
To clarify these constructions, see the examples in Fig.

. Note in particular that the motivation for the second construction relies on the fact that the number of

s is one less than the number of

s, and that

may be 0 (as in the second example of Fig.

).

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(\lambda,\mu)\mid\mathrm{pt}(\lambda)=b,|\lambda|+|\mu|=a,\max(\lambda)\leq\max(\mu)\},

4.
Affine symplectic groups

The symplectic group
\Sp_{2m}(q)
is the subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2m}(q)
that preserves a non-degenerate symplectic form on the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2m}
. Here, we assume that the characteristic of

is not 2.

Before describing the cycle index for the symplectic groups, let us recall Wall’s combinatorial description of their conjugacy classes. In doing so, we follow Fulman’s treatment of the subject in [
[
Ful99
]
, Sec. 4.2]. Given a polynomial
\phi\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[z]

\bar{\phi}
by

(20)

\bar{\phi}=\phi(0)^{-q}z^{\deg(\phi)}\phi^{q}(z^{-1}),

where the map
\phi\mapsto\phi^{q}
raises each coefficient of
\phi
to the
q
th power.
Wall
[
Wal63
]
proved that a conjugacy class in
\Sp_{2m}(q)
is parametrised by the following combinatorial data. To each monic, non-constant, irreducible polynomial
\phi\neq z\pm 1
associate a partition
\lambda_{\phi}
of some non-negative integer
|\lambda_{\phi}|
. To
\phi
equal to
z-1
or
z+1
associate a
symplectic signed partition

\lambda_{\phi}^{\pm}
, which means a partition of some natural number
|\lambda^{\pm}_{\phi}|
where all odd parts occur with even multiplicity, together with a choice of sign for the set of parts of size
i
, for each even
i>0

8
for an example.

\Sp_{2m}(q)
if and only if

(i)

|\lambda_{z}|=0
,

(ii)

\lambda_{\phi}=\lambda_{\bar{\phi}}
,

(iii)

\sum_{\phi=z\pm 1}|\lambda_{\phi}^{\pm}|+\sum_{\phi\neq z\pm 1}|\lambda_{\phi}|\deg(\phi)=2m
.

5.

Here, we assume that the characteristic of

is not 2.

We start by recalling some terminology concerning bilinear forms. A non-degenerate bilinear form
N
is called
null
if the vector space
V
on which it is defined can be decomposed as the direct sum of two totally isotropic subspaces, that is, as the sum of two subspaces on which the form vanishes for all pairs of vectors.

Two non-degenerate bilinear forms
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are said to be
equivalent
if
Q^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the direct sum of
Q
and a null form
N
. The
Witt type
of
Q
is the equivalence class of
Q
under this equivalence relation. Over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exist exactly four Witt types, denoted by Wall
[
Wal63
]
as
\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1},\delta,\omega
, which correspond to the forms
x^{2},\delta x^{2},x^{2}-\delta y^{2}
respectively, where
\delta
is a fixed non-square element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The set of Witt types is closed under a natural addition: the sum of two Witt types, represented by forms
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
on spaces
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
is the equivalence class of
Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}
on
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
.

The four orthogonal groups

are the subgroups of

(where

and
\omega

and

are isomorphic,
and we usually denote them simply by

throughout this section, it will sometimes be convenient to maintain this distinction.

]

Robust Mutual Fund Selection with False Discovery Rate Control
Hongfei Wang, Long Feng, Ping Zhao, Zhaojun Wang

Abstract
In this article, we address the challenge of identifying skilled mutual funds among a large pool of candidates, utilizing the linear factor pricing model. Assuming observable factors with a weak correlation structure for the idiosyncratic error, we propose a spatial-sign based multiple testing procedure (SS-BH). When latent factors are present, we first extract them using the elliptical principle component method ( He et al., 2022 ) and then propose a factor-adjusted spatial-sign based multiple testing procedure (FSS-BH). Simulation studies demonstrate that our proposed FSS-BH procedure performs exceptionally well across various applications and exhibits robustness to variations in the covariance structure and the distribution of the error term. Additionally, real data application further highlights the superiority of the FSS-BH procedure. Keywords: False discovery rate; High dimensional data; Mutual funds selection; Spatial-sign.



1
Introduction

For investors and fund managers aiming to select funds with superior performance, the critical challenge lies in identifying those likely to maintain their performance in the future, given the vast pool of candidates. While many studies suggest active investment can be a “negative-sum game” concerning investors’ net returns, recent research
(
Barras et al., 2010
)
demonstrates that some funds exhibit effective stock selection capabilities. Thus, measuring fund performance has garnered significant attention from both investors and academic researchers
(
Barras et al., 2010
;
Fama and French, 1993
)
.

Pioneering works by
Jensen (1968)
and
Sharpe (1994)
introduced the Jensen alpha and Sharpe ratio criteria, which classify funds based on historical return data to identify those with exceptional performance. However, the effectiveness of these methods hinges on the assumption that historically well-performing funds will continue their superior performance. Traditional literature posits that fund performance is typically gauged by the adjusted intercept term of the factor model, alpha
(
Baks et al., 2001
;
Kosowski et al., 2006
)
.
To assess a single fund’s performance, one can regress the fund’s return series against a benchmark factor model and test whether the intercept term, alpha, is zero. If alpha exceeds zero and the associated
t
-test
p
-value is below the significance level
\gamma
, the fund is considered to have stock-picking ability. Moreover,
Barras et al. (2010)
highlighted that standard methods often overlook that the outstanding performance of some funds may be attributable to luck, raising questions about the validity of existing performance persistence tests
(
Fama and French, 2010
)
. Then,
Barras et al. (2010)
defined a “lucky fund” as one whose estimated alpha is significant, but the real alpha is zero. Therefore, in practical applications, discerning whether a fund’s superior performance stems from effective stock selection rather than random chance remains a vital area of investigation.

Identifying skilled funds among numerous candidates necessitates hypothesis testing for each fund. However,
Berk and Green (2004)
noted that only a small fraction of funds have significantly non-zero alpha values, making false discoveries more likely in multiple hypothesis testing. For example, if there are 100 funds, and only 10 have genuine stock-picking abilities, setting a Type I error probability at 5% could result in approximately 4 funds being mistakenly identified as skilled. Consequently, of the 14 funds considered skilled, nearly a third may be due to luck.

To identify truly predictive models from numerous candidates, researchers must employ a multiple hypothesis testing framework to address biases such as ”data snooping” or ”p-hacking,” a significant challenge in the social sciences
(
Sullivan et al., 1999
;
Sullivan et al., 2001
;
White, 2000
;
Hansen, 2005
)
.
Barras et al. (2010)
,
Bajgrowicz and Scaillet (2012)
and
Harvey et al. (2015)
proposed methods to control the false discovery rate (FDR), which is the proportion of incorrectly identified predictive models. To effectively control FDR, many classical methods assume that multiple test statistics are weakly correlated, implying that fund returns can be well explained by factor models and errors are weakly correlated among different funds
(
Chamberlain, 1983
)
. Thus, several factor models have been proposed
(
Sharpe, 1964
;
Lintner, 1965
;
Fama and French, 1993
;
Fama and French, 2015
)
. However, in the past 20 years, there has been debate over the sufficiency of the linear asset pricing model in explaining fund returns
(
Kleibergen and Zhan, 2015
)
.

To address this issue,
Giglio et al. (2020)
proposed an improved BH procedure using matrix completion to detect positive alpha, which is more robust to omitted factors and missing data. Compared to the BH procedure,
Lan and Du (2019)
employed PCA to eliminate the influence of latent factors in FDR control, based on
Storey et al. (2004)
’s multiple testing procedure, which is a more conservative approach.
Wang et al. (2023)
introduced a three-part mixed model to capture the dependence and non-normality in test statistics through a multiple testing optimal procedure based on each fund’s probability of being unskilled, rather than
p
-values. However, these methods may not perform well with heavy-tailed distributions. To address this,
Feng et al. (2024)
enhanced the ORSDA technique
(
Du et al., 2023
)
to detect potential alpha and improve robustness against weak/strong factors and various error distributions. Nonetheless, ORSDA may lose power due to sample splitting, particularly when sample correlation is strong. Therefore, a multiple testing procedure more resilient to potential factors’ strength and heavy-tailed distributions is necessary.

Recently,
Zhao et al. (2024)
proposed a spatial sign-based test statistic for the global alpha test under the linear pricing factor model. Building on this method, we developed a spatial sign-based multiple testing procedure (SS-BH) that assumes a weak correlation structure among the observable factors of idiosyncratic errors. Compared to the sample mean method, the spatial sign-based method is better suited for heavy-tailed distributions and exhibits greater robustness. Additionally, when unobservable factors are present in the model, we utilize the elliptical principal component method proposed by
He et al. (2022)
to extract latent factors and loadings, and propose a spatial sign-based factor adjustment multiple testing procedure (FSS-BH). This approach mitigates the issue of poor PCA results due to heavy-tailed distributions.
We validate the advantages of these two proposed methods through extensive Monte Carlo experiments. The empirical analysis results indicate that the second method can identify a small number of technically sophisticated funds whose performance is more sustainable than that of their main competitors.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we describe the multiple testing problem under LFPM and introduce the spatial sign-based multiple testing procedure (SS-BH), detailing its theoretical properties. In Section
3
, we propose a factor-adjusted spatial sign-based multiple testing procedure based on the elliptical principal components method (FSS-BH) when a latent factor structure exists, and examine its theoretical properties. Extensive Monte Carlo experiments are performed in Section
4
, followed by an empirical application in Section
5
. We conclude the paper with discussions in Section
6
, and provide the technical proofs in the Appendix.

\displaystyle Y_{it}=\alpha_{i}+\bm{\beta}_{i}^{\top}{\bm{f}}_{t}+\varepsilon_{it}

(1)

for

,
t=1,\dots,T
,

{\bm{f}}_{t}=(f_{t1},\cdots,f_{tp})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}

\alpha_{i}
is a scalar representing the fund specific

\bm{\beta}_{i}=(\beta_{i1},\cdots,\beta_{ip})^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}

is a vector of multiple regression betas with respect
to the
p
factors and
\varepsilon_{it}
is the corresponding

\mathrm{Cov}({\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t})={\bf\Sigma}=(\sigma_{ij})_{N\times N}
, where

{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}=(\varepsilon_{1t},\cdots,\varepsilon_{Nt})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
for each

and
{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}
’s
are independent and identically distributed.

The intercept term
\alpha_{i}
in (
1
) represents the excessive return of the
i
-th fund. Apart from the returns associated with overall market factors, certain funds may exhibit systematic positive or negative returns due to their unique characteristics, which are referred to as excess returns. According to the definition provided in
Barras et al. (2010)
, a fund
i
is considered skilled if it possesses a positive
\alpha_{i}

(
Sharpe, 1964
;
Fama and French, 1993
)
. From an investor’s perspective, identifying skilled funds holds significant investment value. To identify the skilled funds, the statistical problem of interest is to conduct
N

simultaneous hypothesis testing problems for
N
funds:

\displaystyle H_{0i}:\alpha_{i}\leq 0~~\text{versus}~~H_{1i}:\alpha>0,~~i=1,\cdots,N.

(2)

To address the aforementioned testing problem, numerous multiple testing procedures have been developed in the literature. These procedures employ statistical methods that directly control either the family-wise error rate or the False Discovery Rate (FDR)
(
Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995
;
Barras et al., 2010
;
Bajgrowicz and Scaillet, 2012
)
.

Specifically, we define
\theta_{i}=\mathbb{I}(\alpha_{i}>0)
for
i=1,\cdots,N
, where
\mathbb{I}(\cdot)
represents the indicator function. Here,
\theta_{i}=0/1
corresponds to a null/non-null variable. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}:\alpha_{i}>0\}
denote the set of non-nulls, and
\mathcal{A}^{c}=\{1,\cdots,N\}\setminus\mathcal{A}
denote the null set. We also define
\bm{\delta}=(\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{N})^{\top}
with
\delta_{i}\in\{0,1\}
as a decision rule of a multiple testing procedure, where
\delta_{i}=1
indicates that
H_{0i}
is rejected and
\delta_{i}=0
otherwise. The set of funds with excess returns selected by the multiple testing procedure is denoted as
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}:\delta_{i}=1\}
. The False Discovery Proportion (FDP) and the True Discovery Proportion (TDP) are defined as follows:

\text{FDP}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\theta_{i})\delta_{i}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{j})\vee 1},\quad\quad\text{TDP}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i}\delta_{i}}{(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\theta_{i})\vee 1},

where
a\vee b\equiv\max(a,b)
. The False Discovery Rate (FDR) is defined as the expectation of the FDP, i.e.,
\text{FDR}\equiv\mathbb{E}(\text{FDP})
. The average power is defined as
\mathbb{E}(\text{TDP})
. A multiple testing procedure is considered superior to its competitor if it has a larger average power at the same FDR level.

For controlling the False Discovery Rate (FDR) in testing
N
hypotheses as presented in (
2
), existing multiple testing procedures typically consist of two steps: (1) applying a simple
t
-test to the testing problem of each individual fund
i
to obtain the corresponding
p
-value, and (2) applying a procedure to these
p
-values to control the FDR.

By the traditional least-square method, we could obtain an estimator of
\bm{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\alpha}}=\omega_{T}^{-1}{\bf Y}\mathcal{M}_{{\bf F}}{\bf 1}

where
{\bf Y}=({\bf Y}_{1},\cdots,{\bf Y}_{T})
,
{\bf Y}_{t}=(Y_{1t},\cdots,Y_{Nt})^{\top}
,
{\bm{f}}=({\bm{f}}_{1},\cdots,{\bm{f}}_{T})^{\top}
,
\mathcal{M}_{{\bf F}}={\bf I}_{T}-{\bm{f}}({\bm{f}}^{\top}{\bm{f}})^{-1}{\bm{f}}^{\top}
,
{\bf 1}_{T}=(1,\cdots,1)^{\top}
and
\omega_{T}={\bf 1}_{T}^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{{\bf F}}{\bf 1}_{T}
. According to the Central Limit Theorem, we have

\displaystyle{\omega_{T}^{1/2}}(\widehat{\bm{\alpha}}-\bm{\alpha})\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{d}N(\bm{0},{\bf\Sigma})

(3)

Thus, the corresponding
t
-test statistic for hypothesis (
2
) is defined as

\displaystyle T^{u}_{i}=\frac{\omega_{T}^{1/2}\widehat{\alpha}_{i}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{i}}

where
\sigma_{i}^{2}
is the
i-
th diagonal element of
\widehat{{\bf\Sigma}}=T^{-1}{\bf Y}\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\bm{f}}}{\bf Y}^{\top}
with
\tilde{\bm{f}}=(\bm{1}_{T},{\bm{f}})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\tilde{\bm{f}}}={\bf I}_{T}-\tilde{\bm{f}}(\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{f}})^{-1}\tilde{\bm{f}}^{\top}
. And the corresponding
p
-value for
H_{0i}
versus
H_{1i}
is
p^{u}_{i}=1-\Phi(T_{i})
. Using the BH procedure directly for the FDR control, we
reject the null hypothesis
H_{0i}
if
p_{i}^{u}\leq p^{u}_{(\widehat{k})}
. Here,
\widehat{k}=\max\{i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}:p^{u}_{(i)}\leq\gamma i/N\}
,
p_{(i)}^{u}
is the
i
-th order statistic
of
\{p_{1}^{u},\cdots,p_{N}^{u}\}
and
\gamma
is the predetermined FDR
level. This procedure is called the D-BH procedure hereafter,
where “D” stands for “direct”.

Note that

is the sample mean of
\{{\bf Z}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}

So similar to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}U({\bf D}^{-1/2}({\bf Z}_{t}-\bm{\theta}))=0

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mbox{diag}\{U({\bf D}^{-1/2}({\bf Z}_{t}-\bm{\theta}))U({\bf D}^{-1/2}({\bf Z}_{t}-\bm{\theta}))^{\top}\}=\frac{1}{N}{\bf I}_{N}

Spatial median estimator of

\displaystyle T_{i}^{s}=T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{\theta}_{i}/\widehat{d}_{i}

3
Factor-adjusted multiple testing Procedure

The D-BH procedure is only suitable for independent or weakly
correlated cases. Once the covariance matrix
{\bf\Sigma}
has large
off-diagonal elements, which reflects strong correlation between
different funds other than those explained by the common
{\bm{f}}_{t}
’s,
the resulting FDR control may be too conservative. To overcome this issue,

Giglio et al. (2020)
and
Lan and Du (2019)
proposed factor-adjusted multiple
tests under the latent
factor models
(
Fan et al., 2012
)
, where the error
{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}
is
assumed to have a latent factor structure, i.e.

\displaystyle{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}={\bf\Gamma}{\bf W}_{t}+\bm{\eta}_{t}.

(9)

Here,
{\bf W}_{t}=(W_{1t},\cdots,W_{rt})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
is an
unknown low dimensional factor vector with the identification
restriction
\mathrm{Cov}({\bf W}_{t})={\bf I}_{r}
,
{\bf\Gamma}=(\bm{\gamma}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\gamma}_{N})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times r}
is the corresponding factor
loading matrix composed of the unknown factor loadings
\bm{\gamma}_{i}
’s and
\bm{\eta}_{t}=(\eta_{1t},\cdots,\eta_{Nt})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a random error that is independent of
{\bf W}_{t}
and

{\bm{f}}_{t}
.

In
Lan and Du (2019)
,
{\bf W}_{t}
and
{\bf\Gamma}
are estimated by

\tilde{\bf W}_{t}
and
\tilde{\bf\Gamma}
respectively, using the principal
component analysis method. Specifically, they first obtain the residual matrix

{\bf Z}=\mathcal{M}_{{\bm{f}}}{\bf Y}^{\top}
. Let

\tilde{\lambda}_{k}
denote the
k
-th largest eigenvalue of scaled sample covariance matrix

(TN)^{-1}{\bf Z}{\bf Z}^{\top}
,
and let
\tilde{\xi}_{k}
denote the corresponding eigenvector. Then,
let
\tilde{\bf W}=(\tilde{\bf W}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\bf W}_{T})^{\top}\equiv T^{1/2}(\tilde{\xi}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{\xi}_{\tilde{r}})
and

\tilde{{\bf\Gamma}}\equiv\tilde{\bm{\varepsilon}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bf W}}(\tilde{{\bf W}}^{\top}\tilde{{\bf W}})^{-1}
, where
\tilde{r}
is selected
by maximizing the eigenvalue ratios as
\tilde{r}=\arg\max_{k\leq K_{\max}}\tilde{\lambda}_{k}/\tilde{\lambda}_{k+1}
and
K_{\max}
is
some pre-specified maximum possible order. In addition, the variance
of
\eta_{it}
, i.e.
\sigma_{\bm{\eta},i}
, can be estimated by

\tilde{\sigma}_{\bm{\eta},i}=T^{-1}\tilde{\bm{\eta}_{i}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{\eta}}_{i}
, where
\tilde{\bm{\eta}_{i}}
is the

PCA exhibits notable drawbacks when dealing with heavy-tailed distributions. Firstly, it is highly sensitive to outliers, which are more prevalent in such distributions. These outliers can significantly distort the principal components, leading to biased results that fail to accurately reflect the underlying data structure. Secondly, variance is often higher in heavy-tailed distributions, and PCA may overestimate the importance of certain dimensions, potentially selecting irrelevant or noisy features. This can impact the accuracy and interpretability of the analysis. Furthermore, PCA is based on the assumption of multivariate normality, which heavy-tailed distributions clearly violate. This deviation can undermine the theoretical foundations of PCA, resulting in principal components that may not optimally capture the latent structure of the data. Lastly, the estimation of eigenvalues can become unstable when handling heavy-tailed distributions, affecting the accuracy of the derived principal components and compromising the robustness of PCA as a dimensionality reduction technique.

6
Conclusion

Driven by the task of selecting skilled mutual funds, we propose a spatial sign-based multiple testing procedure (SS-BH), inspired by
Zhao et al. (2024)
, to simultaneously test the positive alpha of all funds within a linear factor pricing model. In scenarios where unobservable factors may exist, we introduce factor-adjusted spatial sign-based multiple testing procedures (FSS-BH) and examine their theoretical properties. Our analysis verifies the significant advantages of both SS-BH and FSS-BH in controlling the false discovery rate (FDR). Additionally, empirical results demonstrate that FSS-BH excels in identifying skilled funds.

j

N{\bf\Xi}
and
{\bf R}

, we have

\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}/{\varsigma}^{1/2}=1+O_{p}(T^{-1/2}),

where
\varsigma=N\{E(r_{t}^{-1})\}^{2}/\eta_{\omega}
,
\eta_{\omega}=1-2\eta E(r_{t}^{-1})E(r_{t})+\eta[E(r_{s}^{-1})]^{2}E(r_{t}^{2})

T^{-1}\omega_{T}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{p}\omega=1-\eta

Hence, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{d}_{i}^{-1}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}}_{i}-\widehat{\omega}\alpha_{i})-T^{1/2}{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{d}_{i}^{-1}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}}_{i}-\widehat{\omega}\alpha_{i})|

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|T^{1/2}{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{d}_{i}^{-1}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}}_{i}-\widehat{\omega}\alpha_{i})|\times\Big|\frac{\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}}{{\varsigma}^{1/2}}-1\Big|

\displaystyle O_{p}(T^{-1/2}\hbox{log}N).

Hence, we have
{T_{i}^{s}=T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}\widehat{d}_{i}^{-1}\widehat{\omega}\alpha_{i}+T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}N^{1/2}(1-\sum_{s=1}^{T}r_{s}^{-1}r_{t}V_{st})\eta_{\omega}^{-1/2}U_{t,i}+C_{Ti}}

where

\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|C_{Ti}|=O_{p}\left(T^{-1/4}\hbox{log}^{1/2}(NT)+N^{-(1/6\wedge\delta/2)}\hbox{log}^{1/2}(NT)+T^{-1/2}(\hbox{log}N)^{1/2}\hbox{log}^{1/2}(NT)\right),

\bm{U}_{t}=U({\bf D}^{-1/2}{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t})=(U_{t,1},\dots,U_{t,N})

By assumption in Theorem

, there is
\mathcal{H}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}

|\mathcal{H}|\rightarrow\infty

Next, let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
denote the index set of all the true null hypotheses. Also, let
\Psi(x):=1-\Phi(x)
, where
\Phi(x)
is the cumulative distribution function of standard normal random variable. Our major goal is to bound the number of false rejections

\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{p_{i}^{s}\leq p_{(\widehat{k})}^{s}\right\}.

According to Lemma 1 of
Storey et al. (2004)
, we have an equivalent statement for rejections:
p_{i}^{s}\leq p_{(\widehat{k})}^{s}
if and only if
T_{i}^{s}\geq\widehat{t}
, where

\displaystyle\widehat{t}:=\inf\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\Psi(x)\leq\gamma\frac{1}{N}\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq x\right\},1\right\}\right\}.

(14)

Consequently, our goal becomes to bound
\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{i\leq N:T_{i}^{s}\geq\widehat{t}\right\}
.
The main inequality to use is: uniformly for
x\in\left[0,t^{*}\right]
, where
t^{*}=\Psi^{-1}(\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/N)
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq x\right\}\leq\Psi(x)\left\{1+o_{p}(1)\right\}.

(15)

The remaining proof is divided into the following steps.

Step 1. Show the inequality (
15

Write
{z_{i}=T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}N(1-\sum_{s=1}^{T}r_{s}^{-1}r_{t}V_{st})\eta_{\omega}^{-1/2}U_{t,i}}
for
1\leq i\leq N
. When
\alpha_{i}\leq 0
and
\widehat{\omega}>0
, we have
T_{i}^{s}\leq T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{d}_{i}^{-1}(\widehat{\bm{\theta}}_{i}-\widehat{\omega}\alpha_{i})=z_{i}+C_{Ti}
where
\max_{i}\left|C_{Ti}\right|=o_{p}(1/\sqrt{\hbox{log}N})
. Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq x\right\}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{z_{i}\geq x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right\}1\{\widehat{\omega}>0\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{z_{i}\geq x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}-\|T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{{\bf D}}^{-1/2}(\widehat{\omega}-\omega)\bm{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\right\}1\{\widehat{\omega}<0\},

where
T^{-1}{\bf 1}_{T}^{\top}\mathcal{M}_{{\bf F}}{\bf 1}_{T}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{p}\omega>0
,
\bm{C}_{T}=(C_{T1},\dots,C_{TN})
and the right hand side does not depend on
\bm{\alpha}
because
z_{i}
is centered and independent of
\bm{\alpha}
.
Note that for all
x>0,1-\Phi(x)\leq e^{-x^{2}/2}
by Proposition 2.5 in
Dudley (2014)
.
Therefore, as long as
|\mathcal{H}|\geq 1/\gamma
, which holds for all
T
large enough,

\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/N\geq 1/N=\exp\left(-\frac{(\sqrt{2\hbox{log}N})^{2}}{2}\right)\geq(1-\Phi(\sqrt{2\hbox{log}N}))=\Psi(\sqrt{2\hbox{log}N}).

Hence, we have
t^{*}\leq\sqrt{2\hbox{log}N}.

On the other hand, there is
\eta_{x}\in\left[0,\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right]
so that for some universe constant
C>0
, uniformly for
0<x\leq t^{*}

\displaystyle\left|\Psi(x)-\Psi\left(x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\phi\left(x+\eta_{x}\right)\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\leq\phi(x)\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\frac{\phi\left(x+\eta_{x}\right)}{\phi(x)}

\displaystyle\leq C\phi(x)\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\exp\left(C\eta_{x}\left(\eta_{x}+t^{*}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq Cx\Psi(x)\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}(1+o(1))\leq Ct^{*}\Psi(x)\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}(1+o(1))

\displaystyle\leq o(1)\Psi(x),

where
o(1)
is a uniform term because
\eta_{x}t^{*}\leq\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}t^{*}\leq o_{p}(1/\sqrt{\hbox{log}N})\sqrt{2\hbox{log}N}=o(1)
. This proves
\Psi(x)=\Psi\left(x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right)(1+o(1))
. Also,

\Psi\left(x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right)=\Psi(x)(1+o(1))\geq\Psi\left(t^{*}\right)(1+o(1))\geq(1+o(1))\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/N\geq\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/(2N).

So,
x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\leq\Psi^{-1}(\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/(2N))
when
0<x\leq t^{*}
.
Obviously, we have
\mathbb{E}(1\{\widehat{\omega}<0\})=P(\widehat{\omega}<0)=O(T^{-1})
and
\Psi\left(x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}-\|T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{{\bf D}}^{-1/2}(\widehat{\omega}-\omega)\bm{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\right)1\{\widehat{\omega}<0\}\leq o_{p}(1)\Psi(x)
due to
\|T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{{\bf D}}^{-1/2}(\widehat{\omega}-\omega)\bm{\alpha}\|_{\infty}=O_{p}(1)
for
0<x<t^{*}
.
Additionally, similar to Equation (13) of
Liu and Shao (2014)
, we have uniformly for
0<x\leq t^{*}

\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{z_{i}\geq x\right\}\leq\Psi\left(x\right)\{1+o(1)\}.

Combing the above results, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq x\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{z_{i}\geq x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{H}_{0}}1\left\{z_{i}\geq x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}-\|T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}\widehat{{\bf D}}^{-1/2}(\widehat{\omega}-\omega)\bm{\alpha}\|_{\infty}\right\}1\{\widehat{\omega}<0\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi\left(x-\|\bm{C}_{T}\|_{\infty}\right)\left(1+o_{p}(1)\right)+o_{p}(1)\Psi(x)=\Psi(x)\left(1+o_{p}(1)\right).

Step 2. To use inequality (
15
), we then aim to prove that
\widehat{t}\leq t^{*}
with probability converging to one. Hence, by the definition of
\widehat{t}
, proving

is equivalent to

\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/N=\Psi(t^{*})\leq\gamma\frac{1}{N}\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq t^{*}\right\},1\right\},

It then implies

Recall that

where

\displaystyle T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}{d}_{i}^{-1}\omega\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{1/2}{\varsigma}^{1/2}{d}_{i}^{-1}\omega\alpha_{i}+T^{1/2}\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}{d}_{i}^{-1}\omega\alpha_{i}-T^{1/2}{\varsigma}^{1/2}{d}_{i}^{-1}\omega\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{1/2}{\varsigma}^{1/2}{d}_{i}^{-1}\omega\alpha_{i}\Big(1+\frac{\widehat{\varsigma}^{1/2}}{{\varsigma}^{1/2}}-1\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{1/2}{\varsigma}^{1/2}{d}_{i}^{-1}\omega\alpha_{i}\big\{1+o_{p}(\hbox{log}^{-1}(N))\Big\}.

So due to

, uniformly for these

So, we have
\widehat{t}\leq t^{*}
and thus
\Psi(\widehat{t})\geq\Psi(t^{*})=\gamma|\mathcal{H}|/N
. In addition, by the definition of
\widehat{t}
, we have

\Psi(\widehat{t})=\gamma\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq\widehat{t}\right\}\geq\gamma\frac{|\mathcal{H}|}{N}.

We have proved that
\widehat{t}\leq t^{*}
with probability converging to one, then by (
15
),

\mathcal{F}\leq\Psi(\widehat{t})\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|+o_{p}(1)\Psi(\widehat{t})\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|
. Also by
\Psi(\widehat{t})=\gamma\frac{1}{N}\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}1\left\{T_{i}^{s}\geq\widehat{t}\right\},1\}
, we have

It then gives, for some

, and

almost surely,

, on the event

. Hence

\mathrm{FDP}_{FSS-BH}\leq\gamma+o_{p}(1).

Together, for any

\displaystyle\mathrm{FDR}_{FSS-BH}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\left.\gamma\frac{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}{N}+X\right\rvert\,\widehat{t}\leq t^{*}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\widehat{t}>t^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\frac{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}{N}+\epsilon+\mathbb{P}(|X|\geq\epsilon\mid\mathcal{R}\geq 1)+o(1).

Since
\epsilon
is chosen arbitrarily,
\mathrm{FDR}_{FSS-BH}\leq\gamma\frac{\left|\mathcal{H}_{0}\right|}{N}+o(1)
.

\Box

\displaystyle\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}_{i}-\bm{\gamma}_{i}^{T}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}=

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}+\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Gamma}\mathbf{M}_{3}\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}+\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{4}\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}

\displaystyle+\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\frac{2}{T(T-1)}\sum_{1\leq t<s\leq T}\frac{\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\Big(\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right\|^{2}}-1\Big)\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}

\displaystyle+\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\frac{2}{T(T-1)}\sum_{1\leq t<s\leq T}\frac{\left(\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right)^{\top}-\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}.

We will consider
\max_{i}\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}\|^{2}.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
T
is even and
\bar{T}=T/2
. For any permutation of
\{1,\ldots,T\}
, denoted as
\sigma
, let
\bm{W}_{t}^{\sigma},\bm{\eta}_{t}^{\sigma}
and
{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{\sigma}
be the rearranged factors, random errors and error terms, further define

\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\frac{\left(\bm{\eta}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\eta}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)\left(\bm{W}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{W}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}},

where
\mathcal{S}_{T}
represents the set containing all the permutations of
\{1,\ldots,T\}
, then it’s easy to prove that

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{T}}\frac{T}{2}\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}=T\times(T-2)!\times\frac{T(T-1)}{2}\mathbf{M}_{2}.

Hence, for any
\sigma
, we have

\mathbf{M}_{2}=\frac{1}{T!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{T}}\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{T!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{T}}\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}\right\|\leq\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}\right\|.

Given
\sigma
, by the property of elliptical distribution, for any
s=1,\ldots,\bar{T}
,

\binom{\bm{W}_{2s-1}-\bm{W}_{2s}}{\bm{\eta}_{2s-1}-\bm{\eta}_{2s}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\nu^{\prime}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{A}\end{array}\right)\frac{\bm{g}}{\|\bm{g}\|},

where
\nu^{\prime}
is determined by
\nu
,
\bm{g}\sim\mathcal{N}_{r+N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I})
and
\bm{g}
is independent of
\nu^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{AA}^{\top}=\bm{\Sigma}_{\eta}
. Hence,

\mathbf{X}_{s}:=\frac{\left(\bm{\eta}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\eta}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)\left(\bm{W}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{W}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\mathbf{A}\bm{g}_{2}\bm{g}_{1}^{\top}}{\left\|\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{g}_{1}+\mathbf{A}\bm{g}_{2}\right\|^{2}},

where
\bm{g}_{1}
is composed of the first
r
entries of
\bm{g}
and
\bm{g}_{2}
is composed of the left ones. Define
\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}=\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\mathbf{\Gamma}+\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\eta}
,

\binom{\bm{u}_{1}}{\bm{u}_{2}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{I}_{r}&-\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{N}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{\Gamma}&\mathbf{A}\end{array}\right)\binom{\bm{g}_{1}}{\bm{g}_{2}}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{0},\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}_{1}}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}\end{array}\right)\right),

where
\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}_{1}}=\mathbf{I}_{r}-\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}
, then
\bm{u}_{1}
and
\bm{u}_{2}
are independent and

\binom{\bm{g}_{1}}{\bm{g}_{2}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\mathbf{0}\\
-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}&\mathbf{A}^{-1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{N}\end{array}\right)\binom{\bm{u}_{1}}{\bm{u}_{2}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbf{I}_{r}&\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\\
-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}&\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\end{array}\right)\binom{\bm{u}_{1}}{\bm{u}_{2}}.

as a result,

\mathbf{X}_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{\mathbf{A}\bm{g}_{2}\bm{g}_{1}^{\top}}{\left\|\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{g}_{1}+\mathbf{A}\bm{g}_{2}\right\|^{2}}=\frac{\left(-\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{u}_{1}+\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}\right)\left(\bm{u}_{1}+\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{u}_{2}\right\|^{2}},

because
\bm{u}_{1}
and
\bm{u}_{2}
are zero-mean independent Gaussian vectors, we have

\mathbb{E}\frac{\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{1}^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{u}_{2}\right\|^{2}}=\mathbf{0},\quad\mathbb{E}\left\|\bm{u}_{2}\right\|^{-2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{N}\left(\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}\right)}\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{\chi_{N}^{2}}\asymp N^{-1},

where
\chi_{N}^{2}
is a Chi-square random variable with degree
N
. Moreover, we have

\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})=-\mathbb{E}(\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-2})\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{u}_{1}\bm{u}_{1}^{\top}\bm{l}_{m})+\mathbb{E}(\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-2}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{l}_{m})
. According to Lemma S1 in
He et al. (2022)
, we have
\|\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}_{1}}\|_{F}^{2}=O(N^{-2})
and
\|\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}\mathbf{\Gamma}\|_{F}^{2}=O(N^{-2})
. Hence, we can derive that
\{\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\}^{2}=\mathbb{E}^{2}(\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-2})(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}_{1}}\bm{l}_{m})^{2}+\mathbb{E}^{2}(\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-2}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{l}_{m})\leq O(N^{-2}\|\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{\Sigma}_{\bm{u}_{1}}\|^{2})+\{\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbb{E}(\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{2}^{\top}/\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{2})\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{l}_{m}\}^{2}=O(N^{-3}),

where the last inequality holds because

\mathbb{E}(\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{2}^{\top}/\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{2})\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{N}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})}\frac{\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{1/2}\bm{g}\bm{g}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{1/2}}{\|\bm{g}\|^{2}}=\frac{\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}}{\lambda_{N}(\mathbf{\Sigma}_{\epsilon})N},\,\,\bm{g}\sim{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}).

Note that
\mathbf{X}_{s}
and
\mathbf{X}_{t}
are independently and identically distributed when
s\neq t
, and

\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}\|^{2}=\sum_{m=1}^{r}\big({\bar{T}}^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}\big)^{2}.

So, we have

\displaystyle\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{i}\sum_{m=1}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(r)\max_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(r)\left(\max_{i}\left|\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(r)\left(\max_{i}\left|\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}-\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\right|\right)^{2}+O(r)\left(\max_{i}\left|\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\right|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(r)\left(\max_{i}\left|\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}-\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\right|\right)^{2}+o_{p}(N^{-2}/\hbox{log}N).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\{(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})^{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle O(1)\{\mathbb{E}\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-4}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{u}_{1}\bm{u}_{1}^{\top}\bm{l}_{m})^{2}+\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-4}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{l}_{m})^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-4}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\bm{u}_{2}\bm{u}_{1}^{\top})^{2}+\|\bm{u}_{2}\|^{-4}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{u}_{1}\bm{u}_{2}^{\top}\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mathbf{\Gamma}\bm{l}_{m})^{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(N^{-2}).

By classical Cramér type large deviation results for independent random variables in
Statulevičius (1966)
, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
and some constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle P\left(\left|N\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\big[\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}-\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\big]\right|>Cx{\bar{T}}^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle P\left(\left|N\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\big[\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}-\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\big]\right|>x\sqrt{N^{2}\bar{T}\mathrm{Var}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})}\right)\leq C\exp(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}(1-\varepsilon)),

uniformly in

. Taking

, we have

\displaystyle\max_{i}\left|N\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\big[\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}-\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\big]\right|=O_{p}(\sqrt{\bar{T}}\hbox{log}N).

Hence, we can conclude that
\max_{i}\left|{\bar{T}}^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\big[\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m}-\mathbb{E}(\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{s}\bm{l}_{m})\big]\right|^{2}=O_{p}\{N^{-2}T^{-1}(\hbox{log}N)^{2}\}

and
\max_{i}\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{2}\mathbf{\Gamma}^{\top}\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}\|=o_{p}(1/\sqrt{{\rm{log}}N}).

Because

is

\max_{i}\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{4}\|^{2}=O_{p}(N^{-1}T^{-1}\hbox{log}N+N^{-1}\hbox{log}N).

Hence, we can derive that

\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{4}\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\right\|^{2}\lesssim\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{4}\mathbf{\Gamma}\right\|^{2}\|\widehat{\mathbf{H}}\|_{F}^{2}+\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{4}\right\|^{2}\times\|\widehat{\mathbf{L}}-\mathbf{L}\widehat{\mathbf{H}}\|_{F}^{2}=o_{p}(1/\hbox{log}N).

Then, we will focus on

\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\frac{2}{T(T-1)}\sum_{1\leq t<s\leq T}\frac{\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\Big(\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right\|^{2}}-1\Big)\widehat{\mathbf{\Gamma}}\widehat{\mathbf{\Lambda}}^{-1}.

We have

\displaystyle\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\frac{2}{T(T-1)}\sum_{1\leq t<s\leq T}\frac{\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\Big(\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right\|^{2}}-1\Big)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\frac{1}{\bar{T}}\sum_{s=1}^{\bar{T}}\frac{\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}\Big(\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{Z}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}-1\Big)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\max_{i}\left\|\frac{\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}\right\|^{2}\left|\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{Z}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}-1\right|^{2}

\displaystyle O_{p}(T^{-1/2})\max_{i}\left\|\frac{\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s-1}^{\sigma}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{2s}^{\sigma}\right\|^{2}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}

where the last equality holds because

\displaystyle\max_{i}\left\|\bm{e}_{i}^{\top}\frac{2}{T(T-1)}\sum_{1\leq t<s\leq T}\frac{\left(\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right)^{\top}-\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)\left(\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bm{\varepsilon}_{s}\right)^{\top}}{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\frac{\left\|\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{t}-\bm{{\bm{\varepsilon}}}_{s}\right\|^{2}}{\left\|\bm{Z}_{t}-\bm{Z}_{s}\right\|^{2}}\right\|^{2}

On approximating the f f -divergence between two Ising models
Weiming Feng and Yucheng Fu

Abstract
The f f -divergence is a fundamental notion that measures the difference between two distributions. In this paper, we study the problem of approximating the f f -divergence between two Ising models, which is a generalization of recent work on approximating the TV-distance. Given two Ising models ν \nu and μ \mu , which are specified by their interaction matrices and external fields, the problem is to approximate the f f -divergence D f ( ν ∥ μ ) D_{f}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right) within an arbitrary relative error e ± ε \mathrm{e}^{\pm\varepsilon} . For χ α \chi^{\alpha} -divergence with a constant integer α \alpha , we establish both algorithmic and hardness results. The algorithm works in a parameter regime that matches the hardness result. Our algorithm can be extended to other f f -divergences such as α \alpha -divergence, Kullback-Leibler divergence, Rényi divergence, Jensen-Shannon divergence, and squared Hellinger distance.

The

The
Ising model
is a fundamental graphical model in statistical physics, probability theory, and machine learning.
Let

The function

is called the

of the Ising model and the normalization factor

For general Ising models, both algorithmic and hardness results were established

Our algorithmic result is a reduction from

There is long-line of research on developing efficient algorithms

]

Definition 1.2

Let

The sampling oracle for

Algorithm and hardness results for

We give the following approximation algorithm for the

and

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

be a constant integer and

There exists an FPRAS that solves

for

-divergence in time

O_{\alpha,b}\left(T^{\mathrm{ct}}_{G}\left(\frac{\eta_{\alpha,b}\cdot\varepsilon}{(n+m)^{\alpha}}\right)+\frac{(n+m)^{2\alpha}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\cdot T^{\mathrm{sp}}_{G}\left(\frac{\eta_{\alpha,b}\cdot\varepsilon^{2}}{(n+m)^{2\alpha}}\right)\right),

,

, and
\eta_{\alpha,b}>0
is a small constant depending only on
\alpha
and

, if two input Ising Gibbs distributions
\mu
and

are both
b
-marginally bounded and all Ising models in
\mathcal{F}(\nu,\mu,\alpha)
admit sampling and approximate counting oracles with cost functions

Let us consider a simplified case of the Ising model.

.
For an Ising model

, if for any

Uniqueness condition:

[

Consider

Corollary 1.5
.

1.2
Algorithms for other
f
-divergences

We say an Ising model
(G,J,h)
admits polynomial time sampling and approximate counting oracles if it admits sampling and approximate counting oracles with cost function
T^{\mathrm{sp}}_{G}(\varepsilon)=\mathrm{poly}(\frac{n}{\varepsilon})
and
T^{\mathrm{ct}}_{G}(\varepsilon)=\mathrm{poly}(\frac{n}{\varepsilon})
respectively.
Let
(G,J^{\nu},h^{\nu})
and
(G,J^{\mu},h^{\mu})
denote two input Ising models. We give algorithms for approximating the
f
-divergence
D_{f}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
for various divergence functions
f
.
For many functions
f
, the divergence
D_{f}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
is
not
symmetric for
\nu
and
\mu
. We will refer to
(G,J^{\nu},h^{\nu})
as the first input Ising model and
(G,J^{\mu},h^{\mu})
as the second input Ising model.

By setting different functions
f
in
D_{f}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
, we can obtain the following divergences:

\displaystyle f(x)=\begin{cases}x\ln x-x+1,&\text{Kullback-Leibler divergence;}\\
-\ln x+x-1,&\text{R\'{e}nyi divergence;}\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(x\ln x-(x+1)\ln\frac{x+1}{2}\right)&\text{Jensen-Shannon divergence.}\end{cases}

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists an FPRAS for
1.1
with KL-divergence, Rényi-divergence, and Jensen-Shannon divergence if two input Ising models are both marginally bounded and both admit polynomial time sampling and approximate counting oracles.

Compared with the result in
Theorem

1.4
, the above theorem only requires the sampling and approximate counting oracles for the two input Ising models exist.

Next, consider the
\alpha
-divergence.
When
\alpha=0
, the
\alpha
-divergence refers to the KL-divergence. When
\alpha=1
, the
\alpha
-divergence refers to the Rényi-divergence.
The
\alpha
-divergences for other values of
\alpha
are defined as follows.
Let
\alpha\neq 0,1
. The
\alpha
-divergence is defined as
f(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha}-\alpha x-(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}
. Recall that in (
2
),
J^{(\alpha)}=\alpha J^{\nu}-(\alpha-1)J^{\mu}
and
h^{(\alpha)}=\alpha h^{\nu}-(\alpha-1)h^{\mu}
. Here,
\alpha
can be a
real
number.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\alpha\neq 0,1
be a constant real.
There exists an FPRAS for
1.1
with
\alpha
-divergence if two input Ising models are both marginally bounded, the second input Ising model
(G,J^{\mu},h^{\mu})
admits a polynomial time sampling oracle, and both two input Ising models together with
(G,J^{(\alpha)},h^{(\alpha)})
admit polynomial time approximate counting oracles.

The above theorem works for constant real
\alpha
(assuming the computational model supports real arithmetic). Unlike
\chi^{\alpha}
-divergence, in addition to
\mu
and
\nu
we only require that the approximate counting oracle for
(G,J^{(\alpha)},h^{(\alpha)})
exists.

The squared Hellinger distance is defined by setting
f(x)=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{x}-1)^{2}
.
Given two input Ising models
(G,J^{\nu},h^{\nu})
and
(G,J^{\mu},h^{\mu})
, define an averaged Ising model as
(G,J^{\textsf{avg}},h^{\textsf{avg}})
, where
J^{\textsf{avg}}=\frac{1}{2}(J^{\nu}+J^{\mu})
and
h^{\textsf{avg}}=\frac{1}{2}(h^{\nu}+h^{\mu})
. We have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.9
.

There exists an FPRAS for
1.1
with squared Hellinger distance if two input Ising models are both marginally bounded, one of the input Ising models admits a polynomial time sampling oracle, and two input Ising models together with the averaged Ising model all admit polynomial time approximate counting oracles.

The squared Hellinger distance is symmetric
D_{f}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)=D_{f}\left({{\mu}\,\|\,{\nu}}\right)
. Hence, we only require one of the input Ising models admits a polynomial time sampling oracle.
As a corollary, if two input Ising models are both in the uniqueness regime, then their averaged Ising model is also in the uniqueness regime and FPRAS exists for the squared Hellinger distance. For two Ising models
(G,\beta_{\nu})
and
(G,\beta_{\mu})
with zero external field, FPRAS exists when both
\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu}\geq\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta}
.

1.3
Related work and open problems

Related work

The problem of computing the
f
-divergence between two (either discrete or continuous) distributions is well-motivated by the applications in machine learning and statistics. There are many works, both theoretical and practical, studying the algorithms in various settings. Many works study the error between the true divergence and the divergence computed by certain empirical distributions, e.g.,
[
30
,
25
,
31
,
32
]
.
Embedding-base technique was also studied in
[
2
]
, where algorithm embeds a large sample space into a smaller one while preserving the
f
-divergence.
We cannot directly use these techniques to Ising model as the size of the sample space is
2^{n}
, which gives an exponential time algorithm.
For graphical models,
[
28
]
shows that certain divergences can be computed exactly when the graphical model has a
bounded treewidth
.

Approximating the
f
-divergence is related to the testing problem, in which one or both two distributions are given by the access to different types of sampling oracles, and the algorithm is required to test certain divergence is large or small. There is a long line of work studying the testing problem. See
[

,
12
]
for a comprehensive survey.

The TV-distance

-divergences. There are many theoretical works studying the approximation algorithms (either additive error or relative error) and hardness of approximation for the TV-distance between various types of high-dimensional distributions. For example, distributions specified by circuits
[

]
, hidden Markov models
[
23
]
, product distributions
[

,

,

,

]
, graphical models

,

, and high-dimensional Gaussian distributions

,

Open problems

A natural direction is to consider more general distributions and more general divergences.
Here are a few examples.
For the Ising model, how to remove the marginal lower bound assumption? There are many other types of graphical models, such as Markov random fields with high-order interactions and Bayesian networks.
It is interesting to consider other types of distributions, e.g., distributions generated by probabilistic circuits
[
1
]
.
For the divergence, we focus on
\chi^{\alpha}
-divergence when
\alpha
is an integer. One can also consider the case when
\alpha

Another question is to give a more general algorithm that works for a larger family of

-divergences.

The algorithm given in this paper is randomized. An open question is to find deterministic algorithms by exploring the deterministic approximate counting algorithms for the Ising model
[
26
,
29
]
. Currently, deterministic algorithms are known for approximating the TV-distance between two product distributions
[

,
17
]

We can also study the same problem in different settings.
In our setting, we assume that the input gives the description of two Ising models. It is interesting to consider another (perhaps more practical) setting that the algorithm can only access one or two models through random samples.
This setting is closely related to the
Ising testing problem
in
[
15
]
.
One can even consider abstract distributions with certain properties (e.g. approximate tensorization of
f
-divergence) and the distribution is given by the access to some oracles (e.g., oracle for querying conditional marginal distributions or querying probability masses). This abstract setting was considered by recent work in testing
[

,
19
]
.

Algorithm overview

We give an overview of our algorithm for approximating the
\chi^{\alpha}
-divergence between two Ising models.
We start with the following definition of the

between two Ising models.

Definition 2.1

(

[
18
]
)
.

For two Ising models
(G,J^{\nu},h^{\nu})
and
(G,J^{\mu},h^{\mu})
, the parameter distance
d_{\mathrm{par}}(\nu,\mu)
is defined by

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{par}}(\nu,\mu)\triangleq\max\left\{\left\lVert J^{\nu}-J^{\mu}\right\rVert_{\max},\max_{v}{\frac{\left|h^{\nu}(v)-h^{\mu}(v)\right|}{\deg(v)+1}}\right\},

where
\deg(v)
is the degree of
v
in
G
.

In
[
18
]
, the parameter distance is used to give a lower bound of TV-distance
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\left({\nu},{\mu}\right)
, with which the previous work gave an FPRAS for TV-distance.
The following lemma says such a lower bound can be generalized to
\chi^{\alpha}
-divergence.
The lemma is proved in

2.1
The algorithm for instances with large parameter distance

We first state the challenge of approximating the

-divergence for general

compared to the TV-distance.
It is well-known that the TV-distance can be written as follows:

The algorithm for instances with small parameter distance

In this case, we give a general algorithm for abstract

By the definition,

\displaystyle D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left|\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-1\right|^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left|\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{Z_{\mu}}{Z_{\nu}}-1\right|^{\alpha}.

\displaystyle D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)=\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\left|\frac{W}{\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W\right]}-1\right|^{\alpha}\right].

The algorithm draws

from

\bar{W}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}W_{i}

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(t)=2\alpha\left(1+\frac{2}{t}\right)^{\alpha-1}\left(-\frac{2}{t^{2}}\right)+\frac{2\alpha}{b^{2\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}}(t+2)^{\alpha-1}=2\alpha(t+2)^{\alpha-1}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}}-\frac{2}{t^{\alpha+1}}\right).

Thus when

, where
0<t<1
,the function

obtains its minimum value:

\displaystyle g\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cdot\left(2+t\right)^{\alpha}}{b^{2\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}}+2\left(1+\frac{2}{t}\right)^{\alpha}=\frac{\left(2+t\right)^{\alpha}}{t^{\alpha+1}}+2\left(1+\frac{2}{t}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{2}{t}\right)^{\alpha}\left(2+\frac{1}{t}\right)=\frac{(t+2)^{\alpha}(2t+1)}{t^{\alpha+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{b^{2\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}}\left(\left(\frac{b^{2\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}}{2}\right)^{1/(\alpha+1)}+2\right)^{\alpha}\left(2\left(\frac{b^{2\alpha}\theta^{\alpha}}{2}\right)^{1/(\alpha+1)}+1\right)=\frac{1}{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}.

Combining the above equation with (
11
) implies that
\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)\leq\frac{D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)}{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}
. This proves the lower bound of the
\chi^{\alpha}
-divergence.
∎

\displaystyle W^{+}_{k}=\mathbf{1}[\nu(X)>\mu(X)]\frac{Z_{0}^{k-1}\cdot Z_{k}}{Z_{1}^{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad X\sim\pi^{(k)}.

\displaystyle W^{-}_{k}=\mathbf{1}[\nu(Y)<\mu(Y)]\frac{Z_{0}^{k-1}\cdot Z_{k}}{Z_{1}^{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad Y\sim\pi^{(k)}.

The expectation of
W^{+}_{k}
can be calculated as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W^{+}_{k}\right]=\sum\limits_{\sigma:\nu(\sigma)>\mu(\sigma)}\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{Z_{0}^{k-1}\cdot Z_{k}}{Z_{1}^{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\sigma:\nu(\sigma)>\mu(\sigma)}\frac{w_{\nu}^{k}(\sigma)/w_{\mu}^{k-1}(\sigma)}{Z_{k}}\frac{Z_{0}^{k-1}\cdot Z_{k}}{Z_{1}^{k}}=\sum\limits_{\sigma:\nu(\sigma)>\mu(\sigma)}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}.

Similarly, we have
\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[W^{-}_{k}\right]=\sum_{\sigma:\nu(\sigma)<\mu(\sigma)}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}
. In a high level, our algorithm draws approximate samples of
W^{+}_{k}
and
W^{-}_{k}
and estimate
\mathbf{E}[W^{+}_{k}]+(-1)^{\alpha}\mathbf{E}[W^{-}_{k}]
. Define the parameter

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\lceil\frac{8\cdot 10^{4}(\alpha+1)}{\varepsilon^{2}B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)^{2}}\right\rceil\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\triangleq\frac{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)\varepsilon}{20(\alpha+1)},

where the function
B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)
is defined in
Lemma

2.3
.

Algorithm for large parameter distance case

•

For
0\leq k\leq\alpha
, call the approximate counting oracle on Ising model
(G,J^{(k)},h^{(k)})
for
O(\log\alpha)
times independently with relative error
\delta
, and take the median as
\hat{Z}_{k}
.

•

For each
k
from
0
to
\alpha
do:

1.

Draw
2T
samples
\hat{\sigma}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\sigma}_{2T}\sim\pi^{(k)}
by the sampling oracle with error
\frac{1}{200T(\alpha+1)}
.

2.

For each
i\in[T]
, compute two values

\hat{W}^{+}_{k,i}=\mathbf{1}[\hat{\nu}(\hat{\sigma}_{i})>\hat{\mu}(\hat{\sigma}_{i})]\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\text{ and }\hat{W}^{-}_{k,i}=\mathbf{1}[\hat{\nu}(\hat{\sigma}_{T+i})<\hat{\mu}(\hat{\sigma}_{T+i})]\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}},

where for any
x\in\Omega
,
\hat{\nu}(x)=\frac{w_{\nu}(x)}{\hat{Z}_{1}}
and
\hat{\mu}(x)=\frac{w_{\mu}(x)}{\hat{Z}_{0}}
.

•

Return
\hat{D}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}(-1)^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha}{k}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\hat{W}^{+}_{k,i}+(-1)^{\alpha}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\hat{W}^{-}_{k,i}\right)
.

The total running time of the algorithm is
O(\alpha\log\alpha)T^{\mathrm{ct}}_{G}(\delta)+O(T)\cdot(T^{\mathrm{sp}}_{G}\left(\frac{1}{200T}\right)+n+m)
. Note that
\delta=\Theta_{\alpha,b}(\theta^{\alpha}\varepsilon)
and
T=\Theta_{\alpha,b}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}\theta^{\alpha}})
. We may assume that sampling and counting oracle needs to read the whole graph
G
. Hence,
O(n+m)
is dominated by the time of sampling. This proves the running time of the algorithm.

Without loss of generality, we can make following assumptions.

•

All of
\hat{Z}_{k}
satisfies
\mathrm{e}^{-\delta}Z_{k}\leq\hat{Z}_{k}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\delta}Z_{k}
(which happens with probability at least
0.99
).

•

We can replace approximate samples
\hat{\sigma}_{1},\ldots,\hat{\sigma}_{2T}
with perfect samples
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{2T}
for any
\pi^{(k)}
with
0\leq k\leq\alpha
. Since the sampling TV-distance error is
\frac{1}{200T(\alpha+1)}
, by a simple coupling argument, all samples
\hat{\sigma}_{i}
can be coupled perfectly with
\sigma_{i}
with probability at least
0.99
.

Using a simple union bound, the above two assumption only contribute at most 0.02 failure probability of the algorithm. We always assume that the assumption holds in the following analysis.

To prove the correctness of the algorithm, we need to analyze two types of errors. The first type of error is from
\hat{\nu}
,
\hat{\mu}
,
\hat{Z}_{k}
for
k\in[0,\alpha]
. The second type of error is from using average of
\hat{W}^{+}_{k,i}
and
\hat{W}^{-}_{k,i}
to estimate the expectation
\mathbf{E}[W^{+}_{k}]+(-1)^{\alpha}\mathbf{E}[W^{-}_{k}]
.

To bound the first type of error, the expectation of the output
\hat{D}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{D}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}(-1)^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha}{k}\left(\sum\limits_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)>\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}+(-1)^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)<\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\right).

We prove two lemmas. The first lemma bounds the error between
\mathbf{E}[\hat{D}]
and
D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
and the second lemma bounds the concentration error between
\hat{D}
and
\mathbf{E}[\hat{D}]
.

Lemma 5.2

(Error of expectation)
.

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{D}\right]-D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{10}D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right).

Proof.

To bound the difference, we define a middle term

\displaystyle\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}(-1)^{\alpha-k}\binom{\alpha}{k}\left(\sum\limits_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)>\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}+(-1)^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)<\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}\right)

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)>\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-1\right)^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)<\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\mu(\sigma)\left(1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)^{\alpha}.

Compared to the true divergence in (
15
),
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
only replaces
\nu
and
\mu
with
\hat{\nu}
and
\hat{\mu}
in the index of summing notation. We next bound
|D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)-\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)|
and
|\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)-\hat{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)|
.

The error between
D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
and
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
comes from that
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
may miss classify some
\sigma\in\Omega
into the wrong set. By the definition of
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
, we have the following fact.
If
\nu(\sigma)>\mu(\sigma)
(resp.
\nu(\sigma)<\mu(\sigma)
), by (
15
), the contribution of
\sigma
to the divergence
D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
is
\mu(\sigma)(\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{\nu(\sigma)}-1)^{\alpha}
(resp.
\mu(\sigma)(1-\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{\nu(\sigma)})^{\alpha}
). However, in
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
, it may be possible that
\hat{\nu}(\sigma)<\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
(resp.
\hat{\nu}(\sigma)>\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
), then the contribution of
\sigma
to the
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
is
\mu(\sigma)(1-\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{\nu(\sigma)})^{\alpha}
(resp.
\mu(\sigma)(\frac{\mu(\sigma)}{\nu(\sigma)}-1)^{\alpha}
).
In both cases, the additive error is at most

\displaystyle\mu(\sigma)\left|\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-1\right)^{\alpha}-\left(1-\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)^{\alpha}\right|\leq 2\mu(\sigma)\left|\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-1\right|^{\alpha},

where the error occurs only if
\alpha
is odd.

For any
\sigma
,
\nu(\sigma)=\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{Z_{\nu}}
and
\mu(\sigma)=\frac{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}{Z_{\mu}}
. By assumption, our estimation achieves
\mathrm{e}^{-\delta}\nu(\sigma)\leq\hat{\nu}(\sigma)\leq\mathrm{e}^{\delta}\nu(\sigma)
, and so does
\hat{\mu}(\sigma)
. Hence, the miss classification only happens when
\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\in[\mathrm{e}^{-2\delta},\mathrm{e}^{2\delta}]
. Let
B
denote the set of all such
\sigma
’s. Note that
|\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-1|\leq\max\{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\delta},\mathrm{e}^{2\delta}-1\}\leq 4\delta
. We have

\displaystyle|D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)-\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in B}\mu(\sigma)\left|\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-1\right|^{\alpha}\leq(4\delta)^{\alpha}\leq 4\delta,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
4\delta<1
.

Next, we bound the error between
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
and
\hat{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
.
By our assumption, we have
\mathrm{e}^{-\delta}Z_{k}\leq\hat{Z}_{k}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\delta}Z_{k}
for all
k
. Note that
\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)=\frac{w_{\nu}^{k}(\sigma)/w_{\mu}^{k-1}(\sigma)}{Z_{k}}
,
Z_{0}=Z_{\mu}
and
Z_{1}=Z_{\nu}
. The following observation is easy to verify. For any
\sigma
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-2\alpha\delta}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}\leq\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\alpha\delta}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle\text{by$\left(\delta<\frac{1}{2\alpha}\right)$}\quad\implies\,

\displaystyle\left|\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}-\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}\right|\leq 4\alpha\delta\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}.

(17)

By the triangle inequality, the difference
|\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)-\mathbf{E}[\hat{D}]|
is at most

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\left(\sum\limits_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)>\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\left|\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}-\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}\right|+\sum\limits_{\sigma:\hat{\nu}(\sigma)<\hat{\mu}(\sigma)}\left|\pi^{(k)}(\sigma)\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}-\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha\delta\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}=2\alpha\delta\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)^{\alpha}\leq\frac{2\alpha\delta}{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right).

where the first inequality follows from (
5.1
) and the last inequality follows from
Lemma

2.3
.

Using the middle term
\tilde{D}_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
as a bridge, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{D}\right]-D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\right|\leq\left(4\delta+\frac{2\alpha\delta}{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}\right)D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right).

We set
\delta=\frac{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)\varepsilon}{20(\alpha+1)}
and remark that
B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)\leq\frac{1}{2}
by definition in
Lemma

2.3
,
\left(4\delta+\frac{2\alpha\delta}{B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{10}
, and thus we finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3

(concentration error)
.

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}\left[\left|\hat{D}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{D}\right]\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{10}D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\right]\geq 0.99.

Proof.

In this proof, we fix the values of all
\hat{Z}_{k}
returned by the approximate counting oracle at the first step.
Define the random variable
\hat{W}^{+}_{k}\triangleq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{T}\hat{W}^{+}_{k,i}
, then
\mathbf{E}[\hat{W}^{+}_{k}]=\mathbf{E}[\hat{W}^{+}_{k}]
.
By definition of
\hat{W}_{k,i}^{+}
, the random variable
\hat{W}^{+}_{k}
is within the range
[0,(\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k})/\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[\hat{W}^{+}_{k}\right]=\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[\hat{W}^{+}_{k,i}\right]\leq\frac{1}{T}\cdot\left(\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\right)^{2}.

By Chebyshev’s inequality on
\bar{W}^{+}_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}\left[\left|\bar{W}^{+}_{k}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{W}^{+}_{k}\right]\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}{20}\cdot\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{400\cdot\mathbf{\mathbf{Var}}\left[\bar{W}^{+}_{k}\right]}{\varepsilon^{2}B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)^{2}\cdot\left({\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}/{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\right)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{200(\alpha+1)},

where the last inequality due to the definition of
T
.
Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{Pr}\left[\left|\bar{W}^{-}_{k}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{W}^{-}_{k}\right]\right|\geq\frac{\varepsilon B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}{20}\cdot\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{200(\alpha+1)}.

By a union bound, with probability at least
0.99
, it holds for all
0\leq k\leq\alpha
,

\displaystyle\left|\bar{W}^{+}_{k}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{W}^{+}_{k}\right]\right|+\left|\bar{W}^{-}_{k}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{W}^{-}_{k}\right]\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}{10}\cdot\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}.

Finally, by the triangle inequality, we have that with probability at least
0.99
,

\displaystyle\left|\hat{D}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{D}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\left(\left|\bar{W}^{+}_{k}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{W}^{+}_{k}\right]\right|+\left|\bar{W}^{-}_{k}-\mathbf{\mathbf{E}}\left[\hat{W}^{-}_{k}\right]\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}{20}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\frac{\hat{Z}_{0}^{k-1}\cdot\hat{Z}_{k}}{\hat{Z}_{1}^{k}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)}{20}\cdot e^{2\alpha\delta}\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\frac{Z_{0}^{k-1}\cdot Z_{k}}{Z_{1}^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{2\alpha\delta}}{20}\cdot B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)^{\alpha}

(by
Lemma

2.3
)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon\cdot e^{2\alpha\delta}}{20}D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{10}D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right).\qed

The correctness of the algorithm follows from
Lemma

5.2
and
Lemma

5.3
.

for

\displaystyle\forall x\in\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad f^{\prime}(1+x)=\int_{0}^{x}f^{\prime\prime}(1+u)\,du,\quad f(1+x)=\int_{0}^{x}(x-u)f^{\prime\prime}(1+u)\,du.

Algorithms for large parameter distance

. For Jensen-Shannon, one can rewrite
f(1+\delta)=(1+\delta)\ln(1+\frac{\delta}{2+\delta})-\ln(1+\frac{\delta}{2})
and then Taylor series shows
f(1+\delta)=\Omega(\delta^{2})
. For
\alpha
-divergence, expanding
(1+\delta)^{\alpha}
for real
\alpha
implies
f(1+\delta)=\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}\pm O(\delta^{3})=\Omega(\delta^{2})
. Finally, it is easy to verify that
f(1+\delta)=\Omega(\delta^{2})
for Squared Hellinger divergence. We have

\displaystyle D_{f}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}.

(19)

Kullback-Leibler, Rényi, and Jensen-Shannon divergences.

We give the algorithm for Jensen-Shannon divergence. Similar algorithms can be given for KL and Rényi divergences. The Jensen-Shannon divergence can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\nu(\sigma)\ln\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\nu(\sigma)\ln\frac{\nu(x)+\mu(x)}{2\mu(x)}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\mu(x)\ln\frac{\nu(x)+\mu(x)}{2\mu(x)}.

By (
19
), it suffices to give an algorithm with additive error
\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
. We can estimate each term with an additive error. We give the algorithm for the first term. The other two terms can be estimated similarly. We use the sampling oracle to draw
T
samples
\sigma
from
\nu
, then using counting oracles to approximate
h(\sigma)=\ln\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}=\ln(\frac{w_{\nu}(\sigma)}{w_{\mu}(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{Z_{\mu}}{Z_{\nu}})
, finally, return the average of
h(\sigma)
. The counting oracles estimate the partition functions with a relative error, and thus we can approximate
h(\sigma)
with an additive error. Due to the marginal lower bound assumption,
|h(\sigma)|\leq n\log\frac{1}{b}=O(n)
. We can set
T=\mathrm{poly}(n,\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
to achieve the additive error
\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
and our goal is achieved.

\alpha
-divergence.

The
\alpha
-divergence can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}\left(\sum_{\sigma}\frac{\nu(\sigma)^{\alpha}}{\mu(\sigma)^{\alpha-1}}\right)-\frac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}=\frac{1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}\left(\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}\cdot Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}-1\right),

where
Z_{\alpha}
is the partition function of the Ising model
(G,J^{(\alpha)},h^{(\alpha)})
. By calling approximate counting oracles with relative error
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\Theta_{\alpha}(\delta)}
, we estimate
\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}
with
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\delta}
relative error, which is equivalent to
\pm O(\delta)\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}
additive error approximation. When the constant
\alpha>1
or
\alpha<0
, by (
19
),
\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}
is lower bounded by
\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}+1
. We set
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
, then
\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon-\delta}
, which implies

\displaystyle\delta\cdot\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon\cdot\left(\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}-1\right).

When the constant
0<\alpha<1
,
\alpha(1-\alpha)<0
. By (
19
), the
\alpha
-divergence at least
\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
,
\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}
is upper bounded by
1-\frac{1}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
. We still set
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
, and in this case,

\displaystyle\delta\cdot\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}\leq\varepsilon\cdot\left(1-\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}Z_{\alpha}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}\right).

In both cases, our algorithm solves the problem with an additive error
O(\varepsilon)D_{\alpha}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
approximation, which implies a relative error
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\varepsilon}
approximation.

Squared Hellinger distance.

The Squared Hellinger distance can be written as

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}\right)^{1/2}=1-\frac{\bar{Z}}{\sqrt{Z_{\nu}Z_{\mu}}},

where
\bar{Z}
is the partition function of the averaged Ising model in
Theorem

1.9
.
Again, by (
19
), it suffices to given an algorithm with additive error
\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
.
We can use approximate counting oracles to estimate
\frac{\bar{Z}}{Z_{\nu}Z_{\mu}}
with a relative error. Note that for any
\delta>0
,
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\delta}
relative error approximation is the same as
\pm O(\delta)\frac{\bar{Z}}{Z_{\nu}Z_{\mu}}
additive error approximation. Since
\frac{\bar{Z}}{Z_{\nu}Z_{\mu}}\leq 1
(as the divergence is non-negative), we can achieve
\pm O(\delta)
additive error. Then the whole term
1-\frac{\bar{Z}}{Z_{\nu}Z_{\mu}}
can be approximated within an additive error
\pm O(\delta)
. The problem is solved by setting
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{poly}(n)}
.

7
Proof of the hardness result

In this section, we only consider Ising model
(G,J,h)
with zero external field and interaction matrix with unified values. Formally,
h=\bm{0}
and
J_{uv}=J_{vu}=\frac{\ln\beta}{2}
for all
\{u,v\}\in E
. It is easy to see the probability of a configuration
\sigma
is proportional to
\beta^{m(\sigma)}
, where
m(\sigma)
is the number of monochromatic edges
m(\sigma)=|\{u,v\}\in E,\sigma_{u}=\sigma_{v}|
.
We denote it by
(G,\beta)
.

Let
\alpha\geq 2
,
\Delta\geq 3
, and
\beta_{\mu}>\beta_{\nu}\geq\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta}
such that
(\frac{\beta_{\nu}}{\beta_{\mu}})^{\alpha}\beta_{\mu}<\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta}
be constant parameters in
Theorem

1.6
. We define a constant parameter

\displaystyle\beta=\left(\frac{\beta_{\nu}}{\beta_{\mu}}\right)^{\alpha}\beta_{\mu}<\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta}.

(20)

The following hardness result for approximating partition function
Z=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{V}}\beta^{m(\sigma)}
of the anti-ferromagnetic Ising model beyond the uniqueness threshold is well-known.

Lemma 7.1

(
[
33
,
20
]
)
.

Fix
\Delta\geq 3
and
0<\beta<\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta}
.
There exists
c=c(\Delta,\beta)>0
such that unless
\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{RP}
, there is no polynomial time randomized algorithm to approximate the partition function within a factor of
\mathrm{e}^{cn}
with probability at least
2/3
for the zero external field Ising model
(G,\beta)
on
\Delta
-regular
n
-vertex graphs
G
.

For any
\Delta
-regular graph
G
, let
Z(G,\beta)
denote the partition function of the zero external field Ising model
(G,\beta)
, where
\beta
is defined in (
20
). We prove the following lemma.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\Delta,\alpha,\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu}
be constants. There exists a constant
C=C(\Delta,\alpha,\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu})>0
such that for any graph
G
, let
\mu
and
\nu
be the Gibbs distributions of the Ising models
(G,\beta_{\nu})
and
(G,\beta_{\mu})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\cdot Z(G,\beta)\leq D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\cdot\frac{Z(G,\beta_{\nu})^{\alpha}}{Z(G,\beta_{\mu})^{\alpha-1}}\leq C\cdot Z(G,\beta).

Assume
Lemma

7.2
holds. We prove
Theorem

1.6
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.6
.

Fix parameters
\Delta,\alpha,\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu}
. Let
\beta
be defined in (
20
).

Suppose there exists an FPRAS for
D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
. By run algorithms independently and take median, we can approximate
D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)
within the factor of
2
in polynomial time with probability at least
0.99
. By our assumption,
\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu}\geq\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta}
. We can compute
Z(G,\beta_{\nu})
within the factor of
2
by applying the algorithms in
[
13
]
(if
\beta_{\nu}\in[\frac{\Delta-2}{\Delta},1]
) or the algorithms in
[
21
]
(if
\beta_{\nu}>1
) in polynomial time, where the algorithm succeeds with probability at least
0.99
. Similarly, we can compute an approximation to
Z(G,\beta_{\mu})
.

By
Lemma

7.2
, we get a constant approximation to
Z(G,\beta)
in polynomial time with probability at least
0.99
. The hardness result follows from
Lemma

7.1
.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to prove
Lemma

7.2
. To prove the lemma, we consider a middle term
\sum_{\sigma\in\Omega}\mu(\sigma)(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1)^{\alpha}
. The following result is a corollary of
Lemma

2.3
.

Corollary 7.3
.

There exists
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\Delta,\alpha,\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu})
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)^{\alpha}\leq D_{\chi^{\alpha}}\left({{\nu}\,\|\,{\mu}}\right)\leq C^{\prime}\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)^{\alpha}.

Proof.

The second inequality is trivial. For the first inequality, by
Lemma

2.3
, we can set
\theta
in the lemma to be
\frac{1}{2}\left(\ln\beta_{\nu}-\ln\beta_{\mu}\right)
, which is a constant depending on
\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu}
.
Furthermore, the underlying graph
G
has constant degree
\Delta
, both
\beta_{\mu}
and
\beta_{\nu}
are constants. Hence, by the conditional independence property of the Ising model, both
\nu
and
\mu
have a constant marginal lower bound
b=b(\Delta,\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu})
.
Hence
B_{\alpha,b}(\theta)=\Theta_{\alpha,\beta_{\nu},\beta_{\mu},\Delta}(1)
is a constant.
∎

With
Corollary

7.3
,
Lemma

7.2
is a straightforward corollary of the following lemma.

Lemma 7.4
.

It holds that

\displaystyle{Z(G,\beta)}\leq\frac{Z(G,\beta_{\nu})^{\alpha}}{Z(G,\beta_{\mu})^{\alpha-1}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)^{\alpha}\leq 3^{\alpha}\cdot Z(G,\beta).

Proof.

To simplify the notation, we fix the graph
G
in the proof. We denote the partition function
Z(G,\beta_{\nu})=Z_{\nu}
and
Z(G,\beta_{\mu})=Z_{\mu}
. The family of Ising models in
\mathcal{F}(\nu,\mu,\alpha)
in (
2
) can be written as
(G,\beta_{k})
, where
\beta_{k}=\frac{\beta_{\nu}^{k}}{\beta_{\mu}^{k-1}}
for
0\leq k\leq\alpha
. We use
Z_{k}
to denote the partition function of
(G,\beta_{k})
. We remark that
Z_{0}=Z_{\mu}
,
Z_{1}=Z_{\nu}
,
\beta
in (
20
) is the same as
\beta_{\alpha}
, and
Z(G,\beta)=Z_{\alpha}
.

We use the binomial expansion that

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\mu(\sigma)\left(\frac{\nu(\sigma)}{\mu(\sigma)}+1\right)^{\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\frac{\nu^{k}(\sigma)}{\mu^{k-1}(\sigma)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\frac{Z_{\mu}^{k-1}}{Z_{\nu}^{k}}\cdot Z_{k}.

(21)

We claim the following inequality holds. For any
0\leq k\leq\alpha-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{\mu}^{k}}{Z_{\nu}^{k+1}}Z_{k+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{Z_{\mu}^{k-1}}{Z_{\nu}^{k}}Z_{k}.

(22)

We prove (
22
) later. With this conclusion, combining with (
21
), we know that

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}Z_{\alpha}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}\frac{Z_{\mu}^{k-1}}{Z_{\nu}^{k}}\cdot Z_{k}\leq\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}Z_{\alpha}\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}2^{\alpha-k}=3^{\alpha}\frac{Z_{\mu}^{\alpha-1}}{Z_{\nu}^{\alpha}}Z_{\alpha},

(23)

where the first inequality is trivial and the second inequality is due to (
22
). Rearranging the terms in (
23
), we get the desired result.

Finally, we prove (
22
). Recall that
m(\sigma)
denotes the number of monochromatic edges under the configuration
\sigma
. Since
\beta_{\mu}>\beta_{\nu}
, the sequence
\beta_{k}
is monotonically decreasing as
\beta_{k}=\frac{\beta_{\nu}^{k}}{\beta_{\mu}^{k-1}}
.
Fix an unordered pair of configurations
\{\sigma,\tau\}\in\{\pm\}^{V}\times\{\pm\}^{V}
(it may be possible that
\sigma=\tau
). Without loss of generality, we assume
m(\sigma)\geq m(\tau)
, otherwise we can swap the two configurations.

We have the following inequality for all
1\leq k\leq\alpha-1
,

\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\mu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\sigma)}\overset{(\textsf{I})}{\geq}\beta_{\mu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\tau)}\overset{(\textsf{II})}{=}\left(\frac{\beta_{\mu}}{\beta_{\nu}}\right)^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\cdot\left(\frac{\beta_{\nu}}{\beta_{\mu}}\right)^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\beta_{\mu}}{\beta_{\nu}}\right)^{m(\sigma)-m(\tau)}\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}\overset{(\textsf{III})}{\geq}\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}\overset{(\textsf{IV})}{\geq}\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\nu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k}^{m(\sigma)}\right),

(24)

where the inequality
(\textsf{I})
throws the second term, and the equality
(\textsf{II})
is due to the definition of
\beta_{k}
, the inequality
(\textsf{III})
is due to the assumption
m(\sigma)\geq m(\tau)
and
\beta_{\mu}>\beta_{\nu}
, and the last inequality
(\textsf{IV})
is due
m(\sigma)\geq m(\tau)
and
\beta_{\nu}=\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{k}
for
k\geq 1
.

If
k=0
, note that
\beta_{0}=\beta_{\mu}
and
\beta_{1}=\beta_{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{\mu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\mu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\sigma)}=\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\nu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k}^{m(\sigma)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\nu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k}^{m(\sigma)}\right).

(25)

To prove (
22
), we need to prove
Z_{\mu}Z_{k+1}\geq\frac{1}{2}Z_{\nu}Z_{k}
for all
0\leq k\leq\alpha-1
. Recall that the partition function
Z_{k}=\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\beta_{k}^{m(\sigma)}
. We have the following inequality

\displaystyle Z_{\mu}Z_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\sigma,\tau\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\beta_{\mu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\tau)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{unordered pair}\{\sigma,\tau\}\\
\sigma\neq\tau\end{subarray}}\left(\beta_{\mu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\mu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\sigma)}\right)+\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\beta_{\mu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k+1}^{m(\sigma)}

\displaystyle(\ast)\quad

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{unordered pair}\{\sigma,\tau\}\\
\sigma\neq\tau\end{subarray}}\left(\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\tau)}+\beta_{\nu}^{m(\tau)}\beta_{k}^{m(\sigma)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm\}^{V}}\beta_{\nu}^{m(\sigma)}\beta_{k}^{m(\sigma)}=\frac{1}{2}Z_{\nu}Z_{k},

where the inequality
(\ast)
is due to (
7
) and (

) and two inequalities (
7
) and (
25
) hold even if
\sigma=\tau
.
This proves the inequality in (
22
).
∎

Clinical application value of modified overlap anastomosis for Siewert type II and III adenocarcinoma of esophagogastric junction
Authors: Yong Fan, Linlin Hou, Han Zhang, Ruiting Zhang, Guoqing Shi, Qiuyan Li, Xingang Wang, Yuebin Wang, Qiuya Wei, Tianyu Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AimTo explore the clinical application value of modified overlap anastomosis for Siewert type II and III adenocarcinoma of esophagogastric junction, and to further evaluate its feasibility and safety.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed clinical data from 222 patients with Siewert type II and III adenocarcinoma of the esophagogastric junction admitted to our hospital between January 2017 to October 2023. All patients underwent laparoscopic total gastrectomy with D2 lymph node dissection. 64 patients underwent esophagojejunostomy with modified overlap anastomosis, and 158 patients were operated using the circular stapled anastomosis. Variables that are statistically different were compared between groups using propensity score matching (PSM). The differences in surgical-related indicators and clinical outcomes for the two groups were compared. Finally, we analyzed the risk factors associated with esophagojejunostomy (EJ)-related complications.ResultsThere was no statistically significant difference between the two groups of patients in terms of BMI, gender, age, and tumor-related information (P value > 0.05). However, there was then a difference in preoperative hemoglobin between the two groups. To eliminate heterogeneity, we combined patients with PSM. In terms of intraoperative conditions and postoperative recovery after PSM, compared with the circular stapled anastomosis group, the modified overlap group showed the shorter total operation time, shorter the length of the auxiliary incision, shorter time to the soft diet intake, milder postoperative pain. In terms of postoperative complications and overall survival after PSM, the modified overlap group can reduce the probability of abdominal infection and there was no difference in overall survival (OS) and postoperative late complications between the two groups. Multivariate analysis showed that the Siewert type [odds ratio (OR), 0.355; 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.189–0.639, P value = 0.005] was independent risk factors of EJ-related complications.ConclusionAlthough the modified overlap group had slightly lower total protein after surgery, it had advantages in operation time, the length of the auxiliary incision, the soft diet intake time, postoperative pain, abdominal infection. General surgeons should exercise heightened vigilance in preventing EJ-related complications for patients classified as Siewert type II. In summary, modified Overlap anastomosis is safe and reliable. It has clinical application value.

Introduction

In recent years, the incidence of adenocarcinoma of the esophagogastric junction (AEG) has increased with poor prognosis (Manabe et al. 2022; Matsuno et al. 2019). There remain several difficulties and controversies involved with Siewert type Ⅱ/Ⅲ AEG after radical resection, including choice of the surgical route, lymph node dissection, and digestive tract reconstruction, which is due to the specificity of anatomic position (Devaud and Carroll 2022; Hayashi and Yoshikawa 2022; Kumamoto et al. 2020). For patients with Siewert type II/III, intraoperative gastrointestinal reconstruction is both intricate and challenging, particularly for those with lesions positioned higher in the Siewert type II, which significantly elevates the complexity of the reconstructive procedure and the incidence of postoperative complications(Nishi et al. 2023; Norero et al. 2019). In current surgical practice, there exist various methods for postoperative digestive tract reconstruction, including the Roux-en-Y, the circular method, the Kamikawa flap method, and modified side overlap with fundoplication by Yamashita, but the Roux-en-Y anastomosis is mostly selected for digestive tract reconstruction after total gastrectomy(Kamikawa et al. 2001; Mine et al. 2015; Yamashita et al. 2017; Yoshikawa et al. 2018).

There are several different techniques for Roux-en-Y anastomosis, including circular stapled anastomosis (purse-string forceps technique, reverse-puncture anastomotic technique, and OrVil™ technique), and linear stapled anastomosis (overlap method and π-shaped method) (Huang et al. 2023; Ma et al. 2022). However, in comparison to other surgical methods, the overlap method offers numerous advantages, such as good operative field exposure and a small abdominal incision (Chen et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2021). Intraoperative esophagojejunal anastomosis was performed with a 45 mm or 60 mm endoscopic linear stapler, and the length of the anastomotic orifice was large enough to avoid postoperative anastomotic stenosis. However, postoperative complications such as bile reflux, anastomotic inflammation, and anastomotic bleeding may occur after the Overlap method. Given the complications occurring after the above methods of anastomosis, we modified the Overlap method. After esophagojejunal anastomosis with a 45 mm or 60 mm endoscopic linear stapler, we used the V-Loc barbed suture to close the incision with continuous seromuscular circular suture embedding the anastomotic orifice on one side; then we used another V-Loc barbed suture for continuous seromuscular circular suture while embedding the closed incision and the anastomotic orifice on the other side. We gained some experience from the modified Overlap method and conducted this retrospective comparative study with end-to-side esophagojejunal anastomosis using the circular stapler.

Materials and methods

Clinical data

We retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of 222 patients with AEG, including 132 with Siewert type Ⅱ AEG and 90 with Siewert type Ⅲ AEG, who were admitted to the Department of Gastrointestinal Surgery of Lanzhou University Second Hospital from January 2018 to October 2023. All patients underwent laparoscopic radical resection for AEG, with esophagojejunal anastomosis performed via two methods: modified overlap anastomosis (n = 64) and circular stapled anastomosis (n = 158). Regarding the selection of anastomotic methods, it should be clarified that as this was a retrospective study, the choice between MOA and CSA was determined based on a comprehensive clinical assessment: we integrated key individual clinical characteristics of patients, including detailed Siewert classification, tumor infiltration depth, preoperative nutritional status, and overall surgical tolerance. The decision was also made based on the professional judgment and rich clinical experience of the surgical team, considering the technical suitability of each anastomotic method for the patient’s specific anatomical and pathological conditions. It is important to note that all operations were performed by the same group of experienced surgeons, ensuring consistency and standardization in surgical execution regardless of the anastomotic method selected.

Inclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age 18–80 years; (2) diagnosed with Siewert type Ⅱ/Ⅲ adenocarcinoma of esophagogastric junction by contrast-enhanced chest and abdominal CT, electronic gastroscopy as well as pathology; (3) patients eligible with no distant metastasis; (4) patients signing informed consent forms.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Severe locally advanced cases with tumor penetration of the entire gastric wall, direct invasion or adhesive fixation to adjacent organs and need for combined partial or total resection of the invaded organs. (2) not eligible to undergo digestive tract reconstruction via transabdominal method because of the positive result for intraoperative frozen section pathology; (3) with palliative treatment or emergency surgery for gastrointestinal perforation, gastrointestinal obstruction, gastrointestinal bleeding, etc.

Surgical methods

The entire operation was performed by the same surgeon using the conventional 5-hole method. The operating surgeon stood on the right side of the patient and suspended the left liver to facilitate exposure of the superior pancreatic region and the inferior mediastinum. The scope of D2 lymph node dissection was performed according to the Japanese classification of gastric carcinoma (14th edition) and version 5 of the Japanese gastric cancer treatment guidelines (“Japanese gastric cancer treatment guidelines 2018 (5th edition),” 2021). Inferior mediastinal lymph node dissection for Siewert type II AEG should include the removal of No. 19, 20, 110, 111,112 lymph nodes, and inferior mediastinal lymph node dissection was not necessary for Siewert type Ⅲ AEG. We dissected the duodenum and the lower esophagus with a 60–3.5 mm endoscopic linear stapler and embedded the duodenal stump with a purse-string suture. Then we removed the specimen through the auxiliary incision in the upper abdomen. The result of rapid intraoperative frozen section pathology for the esophageal resection margin was negative. As for the method of digestive tract reconstruction, Roux-en-Y anastomosis was used.

Roux-en-Y limb construction: The upper segment of the jejunum was extracted from the auxiliary incision and a 45 mm endoscopic linear stapler with 2.5 mm white staples was used to dissect the jejunum from the ligament of Treitz about 20 to 25 cm while intermittently embedding the jejunum stump. Isoperistaltic side-to-side anastomosis of the proximal jejunum was performed using the same type of endoscopic linear stapler at a distance of 45 cm from the distal jejunal stump. 3–0 absorbable suture was used for closing the incision.

Modified Overlap anastomosis: Closure of mesenteric hiatus was performed using continuous sutures. The incision on the dorsal mesenteric margin was about 5 mm, from the distal jejunum 5 to 6 cm. The esophageal stump was pulled down and the cut was made at the one-third of the left esophageal stump until the exposure of the esophageal mucosa. The endoscopic linear stapler was placed from the lower right main operating hole with the anvil inserted into the jejunum and the reload inserted into the esophagus to perform the side-to-side anastomosis between the left posterior wall of the esophagus and dorsal mesenteric margin of jejunum. Then the incision was continuously sutured with 3–0 bidirectional barbed suture. The anastomotic orifice was reinforced to the highest point by performing a seromuscular suture along the right side of the staple line. The second 3–0 bidirectional barbed suture was used to perform continuous seromuscular suture from the right of the closed incision and continued to perform seromuscular suture along the left staple line to reinforce the anastomotic orifice until meeting the highest point of the right anastomotic orifice. Observation indicators and follow-up time

Observation indicators include intraoperative conditions, postoperative recovery, postoperative early complications, postoperative late complications, OS. In the above indicators, postoperative pain is a common assessment dimension in the postoperative recovery process; to objectively reflect the degree of postoperative discomfort and recovery progress of patients, it requires standardized evaluation, and the specific assessment protocol was designed as follows: To quantify the observed differences in postoperative pain, postoperative incision pain was assessed by the attending physicians and specialized nurses in charge of the patients via the internationally validated Numerical Rating Scale, selected for its simplicity, high compliance, and objective quantification(Farrar et al. 2001). Patients rated their pain on a 0–10 scale (0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable; mild: 1–3, moderate: 4–6, severe: 7–10) at 24, 48, and 72 h postoperatively, with the assessors recording and verifying the scores to ensure accuracy.

Postoperative follow-up data

The two groups of patients were followed up through outpatient and hospitalization until October 2024 by telephone interview every 3 months. At the same time, the survival conditions of the patients were recorded, and the OS rates of the two groups were compared.

Statistical treatment

All computational analysis was conducted using R software (version 4.3.0). Continuous data with normal distribution were expressed as means ± standard deviations using the Student’s t-test, and continuous data with skewed distribution were expressed as median (Q1-Q3) using the Wilcoxon signed rank test, and categorical data are expressed as n (%) and were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. and P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of general clinical data between the two groups

General clinical data including gender, age, BMI, preoperative hemoglobin, preoperative total protein, tumor diameter, histological grade, T stage, N stage and Siewert classification are compared between the two groups, and the results indicate a difference in preoperative hemoglobin levels between the two groups (P value: 0.025). To mitigate the heterogeneity between the two groups, we employed PSM to match anastomotic technique and preoperative hemoglobin levels, and the results are not statistically significant. Other data of the two groups were comparable and the results are shown in Table 1.

Comparison of intraoperative and postoperative recovery indicators between the two groups

There was a statistically significant difference in surgical time between the two groups of patients (P value < 0.05), with the modified Overlap anastomosis procedure taking 280.65 ± 68.66 min and the circular stapled anastomosis group taking 334.58 ± 98.55 min. Additionally, the length of the auxiliary incision in the modified overlap group was significantly shorter than that in the circular stapled group (P value < 0.005). However, no differences were observed in other intraoperative indicators, such as the number of lymph nodes dissected, the intraoperative transfusion. In terms of postoperative recovery indicators between the two groups, there was a statistically significant difference in time to soft diet, pain score at postoperative day, postoperative albumin after 1 month (P value < 0.005). In the modified overlap group, the time to soft diet was 7.00 (6.00–8.00) day, the pain score at postoperative day was 3.00 (2.00–4.00), the postoperative albumin after 1 month was 62.35 (58.10–68.40) g. In the circular stapled group, the time to soft diet was 7.00 (4.00–8.25) day, the pain score at postoperative day was 4.00 (2.00–6.00), the postoperative albumin after 1 month was 67.25 (62.40–72.70) g. Discussion

In recent years, the incidence of AEG has increased in the world. Data from the National Cancer Institute in the United States show that the incidence of AEG has increased by nearly 2.5 times in the past 35 years (Joshi and Badgwell 2021). The anatomical location and biological characteristics of AEG are different from those of typical gastric cancer, resulting in a relatively poor prognosis. AEG has become increasingly common in Western and Asian populations, for which surgical resection is the mainstay of treatment for (Pang et al. 2023). Therefore, surgeons around the world have put great efforts into AEG treatment strategies, and continuously presented high-quality research results due to the high incidence and poor prognosis of AEG.

The currently available surgical procedures for Siewert II/III AEG are generally divided into three types: Ivor Lewis esophagectomy, extended proximal gastrectomy, and extended total gastrectomy (Kumamoto et al. 2020). The surgical approaches for AEG are varied, differing according to the depth of infiltration. If the depth of esophageal cancer invasion is ≤ 3 cm, surgery should be performed in the transabdominal and trans-diaphragmatic approach. If the depth of esophageal cancer invasion is > 3 cm, the transthoracic approach (JCOG9502) is selected (Kurokawa et al. 2017). In addition, many studies have indicated that transabdominal and transthoracic surgical approaches are appropriate for AEG, and neither has a significant advantage long-term outcomes (Li et al. 2022; Zhou et al. 2015). Besides, laparoscopic surgery offers advantages such as minimal trauma and rapid recovery, and some studies have also demonstrated that laparoscopy-assisted total gastrectomy has a better therapeutic effect than laparotomy operation for Siewert type Ⅱ AEG (Kunisaki et al. 2024; Sugita et al. 2018, 2021).

In terms of the distance from the upper edge of the tumor to the esophageal margin (PM), the academic community has yet to reach a consensus. Postoperative tumor margins are profoundly connected to patient prognosis, and those with R0 margins exhibit significantly better outcomes compared to patients with residual tumor (“Japanese Gastric Cancer Treatment Guidelines 2021 (6th edition)," 2023; Muneoka et al. 2022). However, opinions regarding the impact of esophageal margins on patient prognosis diverge for AEG (Pang et al. 2023). DiMusto et al. showed that patients with R0 margins do not appear to enhance prognosis or reduce local recurrence(DiMusto and Orringer 2007). In contrast, other studies have found that early-stage R0 margin patients exhibit markedly improved survival rates, while in late-stage populations, the prognostic significance becomes increasingly ambiguous(Bickenbach et al. 2013; Cho et al. 2007). In this study, all patients presented with R0 surgical margins. Lymph node dissection for Siewert type Ⅲ AEG focuses on abdominal lymph nodes. Although the lymph node involvement rate in Siewert type II patients remains unclear, there is a consensus regarding lymphadenectomy for this group(Ilson 2021). The scope of lymph node dissection for Siewert type Ⅱ AEG includes mediastinal lymph nodes, No. 10 lymph nodes, distal perigastric lymph nodes, and No. 6 lymph nodes, which is the focus of discussion(Kurokawa et al. 2021). According to the 6th edition of Japanese Gastric Cancer Treatment Guidelines, D2 lymph node dissection is recommended for cT2 or larger tumors of the gastroesophageal junction; D2 + No.19, 20, 110 lymph nodes dissection should be performed if the tumor invades esophagus. The rest of the lymph nodes should be removed if necessary. No.110 lymph nodes should be dissected even in the case of negative resection margins. For tumors invading the esophagus > 4 cm, the scope of dissection should include No.106recR, 107, 108, 109, 111, and 112("Japanese Gastric Cancer Treatment Guidelines 2021 (6th edition)," 2023).

There are various methods for digestive tract reconstruction after the resection of AEG. After total gastrectomy, Roux-en-Y anastomosis is still the main method for digestive tract reconstruction, mainly including circular stapled anastomosis and linear stapled anastomosis. Both groups of patients experienced complications associated with EJ-related complications. All patients who underwent circular stapled anastomosis using the 25 mm circular stapler, and all patients who underwent linear stapled anastomosis using the 45 mm linear stapler, and the large-diameter anastomosis can provide a more spacious surgical field and enhanced flexibility within confined spaces. Consequently, this leads to a reduction in operative time and length of the auxiliary incision in the modified overlap group. Furthermore, the modified overlap group reported less postoperative pain and a shorter time to the first intake of liquid food. As mentioned before, the shorter time to soft diet initiation in the MOA group reflected its technical advantages (not differing clinical pathways): (1) less invasive trauma with shorter incisions and milder pain, enabling earlier ambulation and reducing opioid-related motility delays(Akbarali and Dewey 2019); (2) accelerated gastrointestinal recovery via a wider side-to-side anastomotic orifice and shorter operative time, which reduced local inflammation and perioperative stress(Xie et al. 2024). However, despite having lower postoperative TP levels compared to the circular stapled group, the levels in the modified overlap group remained within the normal range. To further investigate whether there were differences in long-term nutritional indicators between the two groups of patients, we conducted follow-up assessments over a six-month period. By tracking postoperative Hb, postoperative BMI, and Alb in patients, there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. Both groups of patients showed a decrease in Hb and Alb levels after surgery compared to pre-surgery levels. The Hb levels at 3 months and 6 months postoperatively in the modified Overlap anastomosis group and circular stapler anastomosis group were 120.64 ± 19.33 g/L, 123.98 ± 17.69 g/L, 120.58 ± 16.57 g/L, and 122.51 ± 16.55 g/L, respectively. The difference in Hb between the two groups of patients at 3 and 6 months postoperatively was not statistically significant. The Alb content in the modified Overlap group and the Stapler group at 3 and 6 months postoperatively was 36.73 ± 5.07 g/L, 37.61 ± 5.95 g/L, 36.76 ± 4.40 g/L, and 37.76 ± 4.59 g/L, respectively. The difference in Alb between the two groups of patients at 3 and 6 months postoperatively was not statistically significant. Consistent with the comparison results of hemoglobin and albumin, there was no significant difference in BMI between patients in the modified Overlap group and the Stapler group. Specific data showed that: at 3 months after surgery, the BMI of the modified Overlap group was 22.35 ± 3.13 kg/m2, and that of the Stapler group was 22.01 ± 3.17 kg/m2; at 6 months after surgery, the BMI of the modified Overlap group was 24.11 ± 5.95 kg/m2, while that of the Stapler group was 24.22 ± 6.43 kg/m2.

Regarding early postoperative complications, the incidence in the MOA group was lower than that in the CSA group, with a statistically significant difference observed in abdominal infection. No statistically significant differences were noted in late postoperative complications between the two groups. The underlying reasons for these results are as follows: (1) For the CSA group, poor exposure at the diaphragmatic hiatus makes anastomosis difficult to perform under direct vision, increasing the risk of intraoperative manipulation-related errors; (2) During CSA anastomosis, the adjusting knob must be properly rotated, followed by a 15-s waiting period to ensure adequate tissue compression. Both improper rotation and insufficient waiting time adversely affect anastomotic quality, thereby increasing the risks of postoperative anastomotic bleeding and leakage; (3) In the MOA group, the anastomotic incision is continuously sutured using a 3–0 bidirectional barbed suture. A continuous seromuscular suture is performed along the right side of the staple line to reinforce the anastomotic orifice up to its highest point. Subsequently, a second 3–0 bidirectional barbed suture is used to perform continuous seromuscular suturing starting from the right side of the closed incision, extending along the left staple line to further reinforce the anastomotic orifice until meeting the highest point of the right side. Additionally, the moderate size of the MOA anastomotic orifice ensures safe and reliable anastomosis, even in the mediastinal region; (4) In the MOA procedure, the surgeon stands on the right side of the patient, which facilitates the reinforcement of the incision and anastomotic site with the right hand—even in the inferior mediastinal space, which is convenient to reinforce the incision and anastomotic orifice with the right hand, even in the inferior mediastinum. Among the late postoperative complications, reflux esophagitis has attracted our attention. In the total cohort (n = 222), the incidence of reflux esophagitis was significantly higher in the modified overlap anastomosis (MOA) group (12.50%, 8/64) than in the circular stapled anastomosis (CSA) group (3.80%, 6/158) (p = 0.028, Table 4); however, this difference became statistically non-significant after propensity score matching (PSM, 1:1, n = 120; MOA: 11.67%, 7/60; CSA: 3.33%, 2/60; p = 0.163), suggesting baseline heterogeneity may partially contribute to the initial discrepancy, while surgical technique remains the core factor for potential reflux risk. Specifically, MOA adopts a 45 mm/60 mm endoscopic linear stapler for side-to-side esophagojejunostomy, forming a relatively flat esophagojejunal connection without a natural anti-reflux structure, which facilitates retrograde flow of intestinal contents (Namikawa et al. 2010; Tomita 2016). Additionally, the MOA uses a linear stapler to create a longer anastomotic orifice (45 mm/60 mm) to avoid stenosis, but this also increases the area of contact between reflux contents and the esophageal mucosa—enhancing the stimulus to the esophageal epithelium and increasing the risk of inflammatory response (Souza 2017).

Moreover, the experience of anastomotic leakage and anastomotic hemorrhage in our center is summarized as follows: Anastomotic leakage usually occurs in Siewert type Ⅱ AEG patients 3 days after surgery, presenting symptoms of increasingly aggravated mediastinal, chest, and lung infections. Patients had obvious manifestations of fever, palpitation, and shortness of breath and abdominal drainage fluid did not increase significantly. After identifying the complications through timely imaging examination, measures including placing closed thoracic drainage and strengthening anti-infection and nutritional support were taken, and anastomotic leakage healed 3–4 weeks after surgery without reoperation. Anastomotic leakage occurred in Siewert type Ⅲ AEG patients with the symptoms of turbid abdominal drainage fluid and fever, accompanied by left pleural effusion, mild lung infection, and systemic infection. Digestive tract radiography showed that the contrast material leaked out of the anastomosis and into the abdominal cavity and drainage tube. Under early nutritional support, abdominal drainage, and anti-infection treatment, anastomotic leakage healed 2–3 weeks after surgery. Postoperative anastomotic bleeding occurred in both of the two anastomotic methods, manifested as blood-stained fluid out of gastric tube 24 h after surgery. The amount of blood loss was within 200 ml, lasting for 2–3 days, which was controlled after intravenous administration of the hemostatic agents. There was no case of massive hemorrhage or delayed hemorrhage. A high incidence of postoperative anastomotic stenosis occurred in patients after circular stapled anastomosis, characterized by choking when eating and dysphagia. Digestive tract radiography indicated that some retention of contrast material was around the anastomosis. Postoperative endoscopic balloon dilatation was performed for the two severe patients one and two months respectively to relieve the anastomotic stenosis. Other patients were relieved spontaneously 6 months after surgery.

Because of the high malignant degree of AEG, its prognosis is poor in malignant tumors(Rice et al. 2017). This study was based on esophagojejunostomy, and following the PSM, the 1-year OS rate for patients in the modified overlap group was higher than that of the circular stapled group, yet the 3-year OS rate was slightly lower (1-year OS rate: 90.0% VS 88.3%; 3-year OS rate: 70.0% VS 80.0%). The results showed that the survival curves of the two groups were not statistically significant (P value > 0.05). In terms of anastomosis grouping, the reason why the survival curve is meaningless may be that both groups were treated with radical gastrectomy and D2 lymph node dissection, and 6–8 courses of chemotherapy were given after the operation. As far as prognosis is concerned, some studies have shown that anastomotic technique has nothing to do with the prognosis of patients (Ko et al. 2021; Siewert et al. 2006).

The majority of studies have included patients undergoing distal gastrectomy and total gastrectomy, with few institutions researching patients who only undergo total gastrectomy for patients with AEG. Several studies indicate that total gastrectomy is a risk factor for postoperative complications, which suggests that it is imperative to explore risk factors for complications following total gastrectomy (Kosuga et al. 2017; Martin et al. 2016). In this study, we investigated the risk factors associated with EJ-related complications, and multivariate analysis revealed that Siewert classification was a risk factor associated with EJ-related complications (Siewert III type: OR 0.355; 95%CI 0.189–0.639; P value 0.005), as shown in Table 5. The Siewert II type is positioned higher than the Siewert III type, with a longer anastomosis time, prolonged surgery duration, and difficulty in anastomotic reinforcement as potential causes for this discrepancy. Consequently, for patients with Siewert type II, surgeons should exercise greater caution during anastomosis and appropriately extend the postoperative observation period for such patients. Furthermore, though statistically not significant, the EJ-related complications tended to be higher in the MOA group than in the CSA group. This numerical tendency is primarily closely associated with the surgical technical characteristics of MOA. MOA uses a 45 mm or 60 mm endoscopic linear stapler for side-to-side esophagojejunostomy, creating a longer anastomotic orifice than the 25 mm circular stapler used in CSA. This expanded mucosal contact area may increase the risk of minor mucosal trauma during stapling; in patients with preoperative nutritional gaps, this trauma could slightly raise fistula risk and reflux esophagitis (Souza 2017). Furthermore, all patients enrolled in this study were diagnosed with Siewert type II and III adenocarcinoma of esophagogastric junction. The downward traction of the esophageal stump—particularly in Siewert type II patients with higher tumor locations—may induce mild residual tension at the anastomotic site. Even with seromuscular suture reinforcement using 3–0 bidirectional barbed sutures, this residual tension may still delay early anastomotic healing. Unlike CSA’s straightforward end-to-side docking, MOA requires precise alignment of the esophageal posterior wall with the jejunal antimesenteric margin, which also indirectly leads to an increase in EJ-related complications. Additionally, the MOA group had a shorter time to soft diet intake; earlier oral intake might impose slight mechanical stress on the anastomotic site and increase the volume of reflux contents, indirectly contributing to the subtle trend. To address these multifaceted factors contributing to the subtle numerical tendency of EJ-related complications in the MOA group, and to further enhance surgical safety and improve patient outcomes, surgeons should (1) fully mobilize the esophagus to minimize residual tension during esophageal-jejunal alignment, especially in Siewert type II AEG patients, so as to reduce EJ-related complications; (2) intensify postoperative monitoring of EJ-related symptoms within the first 7 days, which include not only fever and abnormal drainage but also heartburn and regurgitation, and extend follow-up for reflux symptoms to 3 months postoperatively; (3) proactively use preventive measures: apply intraoperative mucosal protective agents to reduce trauma-related fistula risk, and prescribe proton pump inhibitors postoperatively along with dietary guidance to mitigate reflux esophagitis; (4) optimize the MOA procedure by exploring minor anti-reflux modifications, aiming to balance its advantages with EJ-related risk.

However, this study has limitations including a single-center retrospective design and a limited sample size, which may affect the external validity of the results and the statistical power to analyze rare complications. To address this, we employed propensity score matching to balance confounding factors, strictly defined inclusion and exclusion criteria, and extended the follow-up period to ensure the reliability of the results. In the future, we will conduct a multicenter prospective study and expand the sample size for subgroup analyses to further verify the safety and efficacy of modified overlap anastomosis, providing more generalizable evidence for clinical practice.

Conclusion

This study mainly concluded that the modified overlap anastomosis compared with tubular anastomosis had advantages in operation time, time to soft diet, pain score at postoperative day, the length of the auxiliary incision, abdominal infection, 1-year survival rate, incidence of total complications. In summary, modified overlap anastomosis is safe and reliable. It has clinical application value. However, the data source of this study mainly comes from single-center data, and the sample size is relatively small. Further, RCT trails are needed for research analysis. At the same time, the experimental follow-up time is relatively short, and long-term follow-up of patients for 5 years is needed to clarify the long-term survival outcomes .

Abstract
. To a pair of Bloch higher cycles that intersect properly and have complementary codimensions, we attach a mixed Hodge structure with some extra data (a framed mixed Hodge structure ). Using this mixed Hodge structure, we define two different archimedean local height pairings. Both constructions generalize the biextension archimedean height attached to a pair of classical algebraic cycles homologous to zero. When applied to the polylogarithm variation of mixed Hodge structures we recover both the single valued polylogarithm of Bloch, Wigner et al. and the one defined by Brown. We also prove several salient properties of these heights including various vanishing results.

Introduction

Motivation

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
defined over a
number field
F
. Assuming several conjectures, Beilinson defined a height
pairing between Chow groups of algebraic cycles homologous to zero
that are in complementary codimensions. This pairing has two
components, one defined for each non-archimedean prime of the number
ring associated with
F
, and the other is defined for each archimedean
embedding of
F
inside
\mathds{C}
. The archimedean component of the height pairing is of particular interest in complex geometry, as it can be defined independent of the arithmetic setting. In particular,
R. Hain in
[
Hai90
]
attached a mixed Hodge structure to a
pair of algebraic cycles satisfying the conditions for Beilinson’s
height pairing, such that the archimedean component of the height
pairing can be understood as a class measuring how far this mixed
Hodge structure is from being split as an
\mathds{R}
-mixed Hodge
structure. More concretely, the mixed Hodge structure appearing in
Hain’s
work is a biextension
B
associated with a pure Hodge structure
H

of weight
-1
, whose weight-graded pieces are of the form

(0.1)

\Gr_{0}^{W}(B)=\mathds{Q}(0),\quad\Gr_{-1}^{W}(B)=H,\quad\Gr_{-2}^{W}(B)=\mathds{Q}(1).

A real mixed Hodge structure with underlying vector space
V
and a weight filtration of the form

0\subsetneq W_{k}\subsetneq W_{k+1}=V
is always split over
\mathds{R}
. Therefore, the property
of a mixed Hodge structure of the form (
0.1
) being split over
\mathds{R}
depends only on the extension
data from
\Gr^{W}_{0}(B)
to
\Gr^{W}_{-2}(B)
.

Motivated by this Hodge theoretic approach, the authors
extended the archimedean component of the height
pairing to Bloch higher cycles of
degree 1 in the paper

[
BGGP22
]
. Namely, there is a bigraded complex of algebraic cycles

Z^{\ast}(X,\ast)_{00}
computing
higher Chow groups (see Section
1.2
).
With two cycles in

Z^{\ast}(X,1)_{00}
that intersect properly, we associated a
mixed Hodge structure
B
with graded pieces

(0.2)

\Gr_{0}^{W}(B)=\mathds{Q}(0),\quad\Gr_{-2}^{W}(B)=H,\quad\Gr_{-4}^{W}(B)=\mathds{Q}(2),

where now
H
has pure weight
-2
. It is shown in

[
BGGP22
]

that one can attach a height to such a mixed Hodge structure.

The main purpose of the present paper is to generalize the
archimedean component of the height pairing to properly intersecting
higher cycles
in
Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
and
Z^{q}(X,n)_{00}
with
n\geq 1
,
that are in complementary
codimensions, which means that
p+q=d+n+1
.
To this end, we first attach a mixed Hodge structure to the cycles and then attach a
height to the mixed Hodge structure.
It turns out that, for arbitrary mixed Hodge structures there are two
natural extensions of the height
pairing, which we denote
\Ht_{1}
and

\Ht_{2}
, which carry distinct information. In fact,
variants of both heights
have been considered before. For instance, we will see that each of
the heights gives rise to a type of single-valued polylogarithms.

Height
\Ht_{1}
is easier to write down in terms of differential
forms, while height
\Ht_{2}
is more satisfactory from the
theoretical point of view. We
prove several results related to these heights, in
particular, a vanishing result for
\Ht_{1}
which shows that, in
order to get a non-zero value of
\Ht_{1}
we need that
\dim(X)\geq\min(p,q)
.

There are two significant departures from the case of usual algebraic cycles
that we should always keep in mind during our discourse.

•

For
n\geq 1
, the cycle class in de Rham cohomology of any higher
cycle is zero. So we do not need to restrict
ourselves to cycles homologous
to zero.

In particular, for
n=1
we can attach a rational mixed Hodge
structure of the shape (
0.2
) to any pair of cycles
of
Z^{\ast}(X,1)_{00}
that intersect properly, are in
complementary codimensions, and satisfy a technical condition. But now
it is no longer
true that two consecutive pieces of a mixed Hodge structure
of the shape (
0.2
) always splits over
\mathds{R}
.

•

In case of usual cycles, the condition to define the (archimedean)
height pairing, namely complementary codimensions and proper
intersection, guarantees that the cycles have empty intersection. This
is not true anymore for higher cycles. For instance, for
cycles in
Z^{\ast}(X,1)_{00}
, the condition of proper intersection only
means that they intersect in a finite set of points after pulling back
to
X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2}
.

In the case
n=1
the condition that a mixed Hodge structure of the shape
(
0.2
) splits over
\mathds{R}
is governed by several maps.

\displaystyle\delta_{0,2}

\displaystyle\colon\mathds{Q}(0)\longrightarrow H,

\displaystyle\delta_{2,4}

\displaystyle\colon H\longrightarrow\mathds{Q}(2),

\displaystyle\delta_{0,4}

\displaystyle\colon\mathds{Q}(0)\longrightarrow\mathds{Q}(2).

The maps
\delta_{0,2}
and
\delta_{2,4}
are determined by the
regulator of each cycle together with the
relation of each cycle with the intersection of the cycles. While the map
\delta_{0,4}
is responsible for the height. The total
interplay between the weight zero and weight -4 pieces is reflected by
the map
\delta_{0,4}
and by the composition
\delta_{2,4}\circ\delta_{0,2}
. In this case it is easy to isolate the contribution of
interest because
\delta_{0,4}
is imaginary while the
composition
\delta_{2,4}\circ\delta_{0,2}
is real. The details of this discussion can be found in
[
BGGP22
, §4]
.

An even more involved picture emerges for
n>1
.
We can still attach a mixed Hodge structure to a pair of
higher cycles in
Z^{\ast}(X,n)_{00}
that intersect properly and
have complementary codimension
p+q=d+n+1
, but controlling the
interactions of the extension classes is trickier, which is why we
consider two different height pairings.

We point out that both heights defined on this paper should be thought
as the archimedean component of an as yet to be understood global
height; for instance through a comparison with the height pairing defined
in
[
BGG19
]
as was done in the case
n=1
in

[
BGGP22
]
. Moreover, as already happens in the classical case
n=0
,
the
archimedean component of the height pairing is not invariant under
rational equivalence and is only well defined for cycles that
intersect properly.

Mixed Hodge structures and heights associated to higher cycles

Now we elaborate on the motivational discussion above. In the work of R. Hain (
[
Hai90
]
), the notion of a
biextension of type (
0.1
) arose through a pair of cycles

Z\in Z^{p}(X)
and
W\in Z^{q}(X)
, which are homologous to zero,
intersect properly, and are in complementary codimensions
(
p+q=d+1
). In this case, the biextension MHS
B_{Z,W}
is given by
the non-torsion part of a subquotient of

\rH^{2p-1}(X\setminus|Z|,|W|;\mathds{Z}(p))
. A crucial observation in
defining this mixed Hodge structure is the fact that proper
intersection and complementary codimensions imply

|Z|\cap|W|=\emptyset
. Hence, the mixed Hodge structures

\rH^{2p-1}(X\setminus|Z|,|W|;\mathds{Z}(p))
and

\rH^{2q-1}(X\setminus|W|,|Z|;\mathds{Z}(q))
are in duality.

The main question which forms the basis of our project is
the following: Can one generalize this study of heights and mixed Hodge structures to Bloch’s
higher Chow cycles? The natural first step is to define a mixed Hodge
structure associated to a pair of higher cycles. To do that, we fix a
complex
(Z^{p}(X,\ast)_{00},\delta)
of refined pre-cycles (see
§
1.2
)
whose homology computes the higher Chow groups
\CH^{p}(X,\ast)
. We
use this complex since the cohomology class of higher cycles in it
lives naturally in the appropriate relative cohomology groups. Using this complex, in

[
BGGP22
]
, we extended the notion of biextension to that of a

generalized biextension
of the shape (
0.2
) attached to a
pair of higher cycles
Z\in Z^{p}(X,1)_{00}
and
W\in Z^{q}(X,1)_{00}

that intersect properly, and are in complementary codimensions (which
in this case means
p+q=d+2
). We had to make a key assumption (see

[
BGGP22
, Assumption 3.27]
), which although satisfied
generically for
n=1
, is not expected to hold for
n>1
.

In this article, we can avoid this assumption and attach a
mixed Hodge structure to any pair of
higher cycles
Z\in Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
and
W\in Z^{q}(X,n)_{00}
that are in complementary codimensions and intersect properly.

Main Theorem
.

Let
Z\in Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
and
W\in Z^{q}(X,n)_{00}
be two refined
cycles that intersect properly (Definition
1.16
), and are in
complementary codimensions (
p+q=d+n+1
). Then there exists a mixed
Hodge structure
H_{Z,W}
equipped with morphisms

\phi_{Z}\colon\mathds{Q}(0)\rightarrow\Gr^{W}_{0}H_{Z,W},

and

\psi_{W^{\vee}}\colon\Gr^{W}_{-2n-2}H_{Z,W}\rightarrow\mathds{Q}(n+1)

given by the cycle class of
Z
and the dual of the cycle class of

W
.

A mixed Hodge structure provided with two maps as in the main theorem
is called a
framed mixed Hodge structure
.

We now explain the ideas behind the construction of this mixed Hodge
structure. First, we need to introduce some normal
crossing divisors: We define
A\subset(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}
to be the simple
normal crossing divisor consisting of points with one affine
coordinate equal to
1
, and
B\subset(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}
be the simple
normal crossing divisor of points with at least one coordinate in the
set
\{0,\infty\}
. Then
A\cup B
is also a simple normal crossing
divisor. Let
A_{X}
and
B_{X}
be their pullbacks to

X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}
. One can check that there are canonical
isomorphisms (
[
BGGP22
, eqs. (3.4) and (3.5)]
)

(0.3)

\displaystyle\rH^{r}(X;\mathds{Q}(s))

\displaystyle=\rH^{r+n}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus A_{X},B_{X};\mathds{Q}(s))

(0.4)

\displaystyle\rH^{r}(X;\mathds{Q}(s))

\displaystyle=\rH^{r+n}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus B_{X},A_{X};\mathds{Q}(s+n))

for all
r,s\in\mathds{Z}
.
Since the cycle
Z
belongs to
Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
, it defines a class

\cl(Z)\in\rH^{2p}_{Z}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus A_{X},B_{X};\mathds{Q}(p)).

The image of this class in

\rH^{2p}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus A_{X},B_{X};\mathds{Q}(p))=\rH^{2p-n}(X;\mathds{Q}(p))

is zero as we have already noted. This implies that
\cl(Z)
lifts
uniquely to a class in

\cl(Z)\in\Gr^{W}_{0}\rH^{2p-1}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus A_{X}\cup|Z|,B_{X};\mathds{Q}(p)).

Similarly we obtain a class

\cl(W)\in\Gr^{W}_{0}\rH^{2q-1}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus A_{X}\cup|W|,B_{X};\mathds{Q}(q)).

We want to put these two classes in the same mixed Hodge structure in
order to define a framed mixed Hodge structure. The idea is to consider the cartesian diagram

\textstyle{X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{1}}
\scriptstyle{p_{2}}
\textstyle{X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X}

We denote
A_{i}
(respectively
B_{i}
) the pullback of
A_{X}

(resp.
B_{X}
) by
p_{i}
and by abuse of notation we still denote

Z=p_{1}^{\ast}Z
and
W=p_{2}^{\ast}W
. By a variant of the isomorphism
(
0.4
), we obtain a class

\cl(Z)\in\Gr^{W}_{0}\rH^{2p+n-1}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2n}\setminus A_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup Z,B_{1}\cup A_{2};\mathds{Q}(p+n)).

Using the numerical condition
p+q=d+n+1
, it is easy to show that this class vanishes when restricted to
W
and
can be lifted to a class

\cl(Z)\in\Gr^{W}_{0}\rH^{2p+n-1}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2n}\setminus A_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup Z,B_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup W;\mathds{Q}(p+n)).

Similarly we obtain a class

\cl(W)\in\Gr^{W}_{0}\rH^{2q+n-1}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2n}\setminus B_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup W,A_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup Z;\mathds{Q}(q+n)).

The main difficulty now is that the pair of subspaces

C_{1}\coloneqq A_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup Z
and
C_{2}\coloneqq B_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup W
are not in local product situation, and
hence the cohomology groups where
\cl(Z)
and
\cl(W)
live
are not in duality. So we cannot yet define a framed MHS. Therefore we
blow-up
X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2n}

along centers contained in
Z\cup W
, to obtain a space
\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}

where the total transform of

C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is a normal crossing divisor

, so we are in local
product situation. The key point in this construction is that it should be
possible to decompose
D=D_{1}\cup D_{2}
into two simple normal
crossing
divisors without common components in such a way that the classes of

Z
and
W
can be lifted to classes

\displaystyle\cl(Z)

\displaystyle\in\Gr_{0}^{W}\rH^{2p+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{1},D_{2};\mathds{Q}(p+n)),

\displaystyle\cl(W)

\displaystyle\in\Gr_{0}^{W}\rH^{2q+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{2},D_{1};\mathds{Q}(q+n)).

To this end, we use the precise formulation of Hironaka’s resolution
of singularities recalled in the Appendix
A
and we
analyze for each blow-up on a smooth center, how to lift the
classes.
Finally, once again, the condition
p+q=d+n+1
will now imply the isomorphism

\Gr_{0}^{W}\rH^{2q+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{2},D_{1};\mathds{Q}(q+n))\cong\\
\Gr_{-2n-2}^{W}\rH^{2p+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{1},D_{2};\mathds{Q}(p+n))^{\vee}.

We define
H_{Z,W}=\rH^{2p+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{1},D_{2};\mathds{Q}(p+n))
, as the mixed Hodge structure associated to the pair
(Z,W)
. The classes of
Z
and
W
determine the morphisms

\phi_{Z}\colon\mathds{Q}(0)\rightarrow\Gr^{W}_{0}H_{Z,W},

and

\psi_{W^{\vee}}\colon\Gr^{W}_{-2n-2}H_{Z,W}\rightarrow\mathds{Q}(n+1).

We note that the mixed Hodge structure
H_{Z,W}
and the framing

(\phi_{Z},\psi_{W^{\vee}})
is not unique, but depends on the choice
of the resolution of singularities
\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}
and on the choice of
the decomposition

. Nevertheless if
H^{\prime}_{Z,W},(\phi^{\prime}_{Z},\psi^{\prime}_{W^{\vee}})
is a different choice; there is always a
third one

with maps

and

such that

\phi^{\prime\prime}_{Z}=\Gr_{0}^{W}f\circ\phi_{Z}=\Gr_{0}^{W}g\circ\phi^{\prime}_{Z},

\psi_{W^{\vee}}=\psi^{\prime\prime}_{W^{\vee}}\circ\Gr_{-2n-2}^{W}f,\text{ and }\psi^{\prime}_{W^{\vee}}=\psi^{\prime\prime}_{W^{\vee}}\circ\Gr_{-2n-2}^{W}g.

Once the mixed Hodge structure
H_{Z,W}
is defined, the next task is
to define a suitable notion of height pairing. As mentioned earlier, we
give two definitions of heights.

Using the Deligne bigrading
H_{Z,W}=\bigoplus I^{\ast,\ast}
(see §

) we
lift the class
\cl(Z)\in I^{0,0}
to a class
\cl(Z)\in H_{Z,W}
and the
class
\cl(W)\in(I^{-n-1,-n-1})^{\vee}
to a class
\cl(W)\in H_{Z,W}^{\vee}
.
Let
\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
denote the duality

\rH^{2p+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{1},D_{2};\mathds{Q}(p+n))\cong\rH^{2q+n-1}(\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}\setminus D_{2},D_{1};\mathds{Q}(q+n))^{\vee},

and

denotes the imaginary component of any complex number

The second definition of height uses the Deligne map

,

We prove that these height pairings do not depend on the choice of a

nor on

), and

\Ht_{1,2}(Z,W)=(-1)^{n}\Ht_{1,2}(W,Z)
(Proposition

). An interesting
observation about these heights is that when

,

are
defined over
\mathds{R}
, then
\Ht_{1,2}(Z,W)=0

(see

). Finally, Proposition

shows that one of

and

should be

, if one wants to find a nonzero

.

Main ingredients of the construction

Now we discuss the two key ingredients that contributed to the main
result. The first one is the notion of abstract height associated
to a framed mixed Hodge structure, and the second one is a complex of differential forms rapidly decreasing along one divisor,
while slowly increasing along another.

Abstract heights

The notion of heights defined above has a more abstract origin, and
can be defined for mixed Hodge structures with a
framing
. The notion of framed mixed Hodge structures is not new, and has appeared
in
[
BGSV
]
and
[
Jaf03
]
to name a few. In §
2
, we define it in a way that is more
suited towards our eventual goal of attaching heights to higher
cycles.

Definition
.

Let
\mathds{Q}(n),\penalty\ n\in\mathds{Z}
be the Tate mixed Hodge structure of pure type,
with Betti generator
{\mathds{1}}(n)_{\text{\rm B}}\coloneqq 1\in\mathds{Q}
(see Example

1.2
for details). For
(a,b)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}
, an

(a,b)
-
framing
on a mixed Hodge structure
H
is a pair
of morphisms

\phi\colon\mathds{Q}(-a)\rightarrow\Gr^{W}_{2a}(H)\text{ and }\psi\colon\Gr^{W}_{2b}(H)\rightarrow\mathds{Q}(-b)

of pure Hodge structures.

To emphasize a framing on
H
, we sometimes denote it by

H_{a,b}
. Let
e_{H}
be the image inside
I^{a,a}_{H}

(
(a,a)
-component of the Deligne bigrading of
H
) of the Betti
generator
{\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}}\in\mathds{Q}(-a)
. It is easy to show that if
H
has a
framing given as above, then its dual
H^{\vee}
also has a framing
given by morphisms

\phi^{\vee}\colon\mathds{Q}(b)\rightarrow\Gr^{W}_{-2b}(H^{\vee})\text{ and }\psi^{\vee}\colon\Gr^{W}_{-2a}(H^{\vee})\rightarrow\mathds{Q}(a).

Let
e_{H^{\vee}}
be the element equivalent to
e_{H}
, inside

I^{-b,-b}_{H^{\vee}}
. With this setup, we define two notions of
height (Definition
2.14
and Definition
2.19
):

\Ht_{1}(H)\coloneqq\im\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\overline{e_{H}}\rangle

and

\Ht_{2}(H)\coloneqq\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle.

Here
\langle\penalty\ ,\penalty\ \rangle
denotes the duality between
H
and

H^{\vee}
and
\delta_{H}
is the Deligne splitting of
H
(see
(

)). Notice here that
\Ht_{2}(H)
is already real
(Lemma

), while for
\Ht_{1}(H)
we take the imaginary
component. These heights in general are not the same (see Example

). However, they do agree on the (generalized)
biextensions appearing in (

) and (
0.2
). For
z\in\mathds{P}^{1}_{\mathds{C}}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
, the mixed Hodge
structure

of Hodge-Tate type associated with polylogarithms is a
natural testing ground for these heights, and in Examples

and
2.28

we show that the heights
\Ht_{1}(H(z))
and
\Ht_{2}(H(z))
are given
by various real-valued avatars of polylogarithms appearing in

[

]
and

[
BD94
]
.

Complex of differential forms

Let
A
,
B
be subvarieties inside
X
. The relative cohomology
groups
\rH^{\ast}(X\setminus A,B)
of the pair of spaces

(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)
appear as a central theme in
this article. For example, as we have mentioned earlier, the
cycle class
\cl(Z)
for a higher cycle

Z\in Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
lives naturally in the cohomology group

\Gr^{W}_{0}\rH^{2p-1}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus A_{X}\cup|Z|,B_{X};\mathds{Q}(p)).

To represent elements in
\rH^{\ast}(X\setminus A,B)
, we need a
suitable complex which computes this
cohomology. There are a few such complexes, but none that have
all the properties of cohomology, so one has to resort to trade-offs.
We focus on the case when
A
and
B
are simple normal crossing
divisors without common components such that
A\cup B
is also a
simple normal crossing divisor. In fact, we can always reduce to this case by
resolution of singularities. The minimal requirements we have are the
following.

(i)

We need a complex that computes
\rH^{\ast}(X\setminus A,B,\mathds{C})

with its Hodge filtration.

(ii)

The complex should be compatible with the real structure

\rH^{\ast}(X\setminus A,B,\mathds{R})
so we can represent easily the complex
conjugation.

(iii)

The complex has to be compatible with the operations in
cohomology listed in Section
3
.

The first candidate for such a complex is the complex of differential
forms with logarithmic singularities along
A
and vanishing along

B
:

\Sigma_{B}E^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)=\{\omega\in E^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)\mid\iota^{\ast}\omega=0\}\subset E^{\ast}_{X}(\log A),

where
\iota\colon\widetilde{B}\rightarrow B
is the normalization of

B
. This is the complex that we have used in
[
BGGP22
]
. It
satisfies almost all our requirements except one. Given three normal
crossing divisors
A
,
B
and
C
without common components such that

A\cup B\cup C
is still a normal crossings divisor; there is an
exceptional cup-product

\rH^{r}(X\setminus A\cup C,B)\otimes\rH^{s}(X\setminus A,B\cup C)\longrightarrow\rH^{r+s}(X\setminus A,B\cup C).

This cup product can not be easily represented using a complex of the
form
\Sigma_{B}E^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)
because the product of a form
in
E^{\ast}_{X}(\log C)
by a form in
\Sigma_{C}E^{\ast}_{X}
does
not belong in general to
\Sigma_{C}E^{\ast}_{X}
. Of course, one can
argue that, given particular cohomology classes, we can always find
nice representatives whose product lives in the right complex.
Nevertheless, to avoid unnecessary complications, it is more
convenient to use a complex where this cup-product is well defined.

Therefore, we will use the complex of differential forms, which are
slowly increasing along
A
and rapidly decreasing along
B
(see
Definition
4.1
) denoted
E_{X}^{\ast}(\si A,\rd B)
. This complex was studied by Harris and
Zucker (see for instance
[
HZ01
]
) and has been
revisited recently in
[
BGCLRJ22
]
.
The main advantage of this complex is that it allows us to represent
the complex conjugation and all the operations in cohomology
easily. We caution the reader that unlike
\Sigma_{B}E^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)
, the complex
E_{X}^{\ast}(\si A,\rd B)\not\subset E^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)
, and this necessitates some additional restrictions while representing operations in cohomology (see Proposition
4.7
and the following Remark
4.8
). For details about this complex, see Section
4
.

Layout

We now describe the organization of the paper. In §
1
, we give an account of the basic ingredients
needed for the paper. We provide a short description of the rational mixed
Hodge structures with the de Rham and Betti conjugations, a weak

\mathds{R}
-Hodge complex, higher Chow groups with their associated mixed
Hodge extension class, and Goncharov’s regulator current. In §
2
we
introduce the first key concept of this article, namely the notion of
heights associated with framed mixed Hodge structures, provide examples,
and prove several properties associated with it. In §
3
and §
4
, we set up the
machinery for §
5
. In §
3
we prove several results associated with the
cohomology of the relative space
(X\setminus A,B\setminus A\cap B)
,
and its behavior with respect to blow-ups and cup products. In §
4
we introduce and prove properties of the
space
E^{\ast}_{X}(\si A,\rd B)
of forms slowly increasing along

A
and rapidly decreasing along
B
. In the cases of our interest, we
show that this complex computes the cohomology groups of §
3
, and that all the cohomology operations of §
3
can be
represented by the corresponding ones of this complex. Finally, in §
5
we develop the framed mixed Hodge

this paper. The key result here is Theorem

, which helps us
to circumvent the issue arising from the lack of local product
situation. For the convenience
of the reader, we state a precise version of Hironaka’s

.

1.1.
Mixed Hodge structures

We start by recalling the definition of mixed Hodge structure that we
use.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathds{R}
be a subring. A

\Lambda
-Mixed Hodge structure defined over

\mathds{C}
(a
\Lambda
-MHS for short) is the data

H=((H_{\text{\rm B}},W),(H_{\text{\rm dR}},W,F),\alpha_{H}),

where
H_{\text{\rm B}}
is a
\Lambda
-module with an increasing
weight filtration
W
,
H_{\text{\rm dR}}
is a
\mathds{C}
-vector space with a decreasing
Hodge filtration
F
and an increasing weight filtration
W
and

\alpha_{H}\colon H_{\text{\rm B}}\otimes\mathds{C}\to H_{\text{\rm dR}}
is an isomorphism
compatible with the weight filtration on both sides. Such that, for
every
n\in\mathds{Z}
the triple

((\Gr_{n}^{W}H_{\text{\rm B}}),(\Gr_{n}^{W}H_{\text{\rm dR}},\Gr_{n}^{W}F),(\Gr_{n}^{W}\alpha_{H}))

is a pure Hodge structure of weight
n
. The
\mathds{Q}
-vector space

H_{\text{\rm B}}
is called the Betti side of the MHS and the
\mathds{C}
-vector
space
H_{\text{\rm dR}}
the de Rham side. The map
\alpha_{H}
is called the
comparison isomorphism. In an abstract mixed Hodge structure, the
standard complex conjugation is the one corresponding to the Betti
rational structure. When
\Lambda=\mathds{Q}
we omit the coefficients from the notation
and call it a
MHS
.

Example 1.2
.

For
a\in\mathds{Z}
, the Tate mixed Hodge structure
\mathds{Q}(a)
is the mixed
Hodge structure given by the following data

\displaystyle\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}=\mathds{Q},\quad W_{-2a-1}\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}=0,\quad W_{-2a}\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}=\mathds{Q}

\displaystyle\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}=\mathds{C},\quad F^{-a}\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}=\mathds{C},\quad F^{-a+1}\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}=0

\displaystyle\alpha_{\mathds{Q}(a)}(1)=(2\pi i)^{a}\in\mathds{C}.

The mixed Hodge structure
\mathds{Q}(a)
comes equipped with the
choice of two
generators.

\displaystyle{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1\in\mathds{Q}=\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}

\displaystyle{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1\in\mathds{C}=\mathds{Q}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}.

These generators are called the Betti and the de Rham generators. They
satisfy

\overline{{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}}={\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm B}},\qquad\overline{{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}}=(-1)^{a}{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}},\qquad\alpha({\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm B}})=(2\pi i)^{a}{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm dR}}.

Remark 1.3
.

This definition of mixed Hodge structure using two
different vector spaces, the Betti one
H_{\text{\rm B}}
and the de Rham one

H_{\text{\rm dR}}
, and a comparison isomorphism, is very useful when working
with periods, because we can choose the de Rham side to be defined
over the same field as the variety
X
. Nevertheless in many
occasions, for instance when the variety is defined over
\mathds{C}
or one
is interested in complex variations of mixed Hodge structures,
it is convenient to use a less cumbersome notation. In this case we
will consider a
\mathds{Q}
-vector space
V
with an increasing weight
filtration
W
and a decreasing Hodge filtration
F
on

V_{\mathds{C}}\coloneqq V\otimes\mathds{C}
. So that
H_{\text{\rm B}}=V
,
H_{\text{\rm dR}}=V_{\mathds{C}}
and

\alpha=\Id
. If
V
is fixed we will denote the mixed Hodge
structure by the pair
(W,F)
.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
H
be a
\mathds{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure of rank one defined over
\mathds{C}
. Then
H
is
necessarily pure of even weight, say
2a
. It follows that it is
isomorphic to

\mathds{Q}(-a)
. The choice of an isomorphism

is equivalent to
the choice of a generator

of

defined over
\mathds{C}

This is no longer true

\mathds{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures of rank one defined over a subfield

\mathds{C}
, because it may happen that the above isomorphism is
not defined over
k

of
H
.

Example 1.5
.

Let

. Let
\square=\mathds{P}^{1}\setminus\{1\}\,(\cong\mathds{A}^{1}).

The collection of all cartesian
products
(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}
,
n\geq 0
has a cocubical scheme structure,
denoted
(\mathds{P}^{1})^{\bullet}
.
For
i=1,\dots,n
, we denote by
t_{i}\in(k\cup\{\infty\})
the
absolute coordinate of the
i
-th factor. Then the
coface maps
are defined as

\displaystyle\delta_{0}^{i}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i},\dots,t_{n}),

\displaystyle\delta_{1}^{i}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},\infty,t_{i},\dots,t_{n}).

While the codegeneracy maps are given by

\sigma^{i}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})=(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},t_{i+1},\dots,t_{n}).

Then,
\square^{\bullet}=\{\square^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
inherits a cocubical scheme structure from
that of
(\mathds{P}^{1})^{\bullet}
.
An
r
-dimensional
face

F
of
\square^{n}
is any subscheme of the form

F=\delta^{i_{1}}_{j_{1}}\cdots\delta^{i_{n-r}}_{j_{n-r}}(\square^{r}).

By convention,
\square^{n}
is a
face of dimension
n
. The codimension of an
r
-dimensional face of

\square^{n}
is
n-r
.

Let
X
be an equidimensional quasi-projective scheme of
dimension
d
over the field
k
. Let
Z^{p}(X,n)
be the free
abelian group generated by the codimension
p
closed irreducible
subvarieties of
X\times\square^{n}
, which intersect properly

X\times F
for every face
F

of
\square^{n}
. We call the elements of
Z^{p}(X,n)

admissible pre-cycles
.
The pullback by the coface and codegeneracy maps of
\square^{\bullet}

endow
Z^{p}(X,\cdot)
with a cubical abelian group structure, given by

\begin{gathered}\delta^{j}_{i}=(\delta^{i}_{j})^{\ast},\\
\sigma_{i}=(\sigma^{i})^{\ast}.\end{gathered}

Note that the indexes have been raised or lowered to reflect the change from
cocubical to cubical structures. The total differential is defined as

\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0,1}(-1)^{i+j}\delta_{i}^{j}.

Let

(Z^{p}(X,*),\delta)
be the associated chain complex. Since we are
working with a cubical abelian group instead of a simplicial one, it
is known that the homology groups of the complex
(Z^{p}(X,*),\delta)

do not agree with the higher Bloch Chow groups. Therefore we have to
consider the
normalized
and
refined normalized
chain
complexes associated with
Z^{p}(X,*)
,

\displaystyle Z^{p}(X,n)_{0}\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\delta^{1}_{i},

\displaystyle Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\delta^{1}_{i}\cap\bigcap_{i=2}^{n}\ker\delta^{0}_{i}.

The differential of these normalized complexes is the ones induced by

\delta
and are also denoted by
\delta
.
The inclusion of cubical complexes

(Z^{p}(X,n)_{00},\delta)\hookrightarrow(Z^{p}(X,n)_{0},\delta),

is a quasi-isomorphism and the homology of either of the above chain
complexes agree with the Bloch higher Chow group:

\CH^{p}(X,n)=\rH_{n}(Z^{p}(X,\ast)_{00},\delta)=\rH_{n}(Z^{p}(X,\ast)_{0},\delta).

Here the symbol
=
means a canonical isomorphism. An element
Z
of
the group
Z^{p}(X,n)_{0}
will be called a
normalized pre-cycle
while an
element
Z
of
Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
will be called a
refined
pre-cycle
. In both cases, if
\delta Z=0
it will be called a cycle
(normalized or refined respectively). We note that a refined cycle

Z\in Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
satisfies all the conditions

\delta^{j}_{i}(Z)=0,\quad j=0,1,\ i=1,\dots,n.

When
j=1
or
j=0
and
i>1
the condition follows from the
definition of refined pre-cycle. While the condition for
j=0
and

i=1
follows from being a cycle.

We also recall the definition of proper intersection of higher cycles.

Definition 1.16
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective scheme over
k
, and let

p,q,n,m\geq 0
be non-negative
integers. If
Z\in Z^{p}(X,n)
,

, we say that

and
W

intersect properly
if, for any
face
F
of
\square^{n+m}
,

\codim_{X\times F}\left(\pi_{1}^{-1}|Z|\,\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}|W|\,\cap\,(X\times F)\right)\geq p+q,

where

\pi_{1}\colon X\times\square^{n}\times\square^{m}\to X\times\square^{n},\quad\pi_{2}\colon X\times\square^{n}\times\square^{m}\to X\times\square^{m}

are the projections.

1.3.
Wang’s form and Goncharov’s regulator

Let
X
be a smooth and complex variety. In his paper
[
Gon95
]
, Goncharov defined a regulator

\mathcal{P}\colon\CH^{p}(X,n)\longrightarrow\rH^{2p-n}_{\mathfrak{D}}(X,\mathds{R}(p))

which is given by a morphism of complexes, also denoted by

\mathcal{P}\colon Z^{p}(X,\ast)_{0}\to\mathfrak{D}^{2p-\ast}_{D}(X,p),

where
\mathfrak{D}^{\ast}_{D}(X,p)
denotes the Deligne complex of currents on

X
as in Example
1.15
. It can be shown (
[
Kerr03
, §3.1.1]
and
[
KLM06
]
, see also
[
BFT2011
, Theorem
7.8]
) that Goncharov’s and Beilinson’s regulators agree. We will
briefly describe Goncharov’s construction. For a quasi-projective
smooth complex variety
X
we write
\mathfrak{D}_{\log}^{\ast}(X,p)
for the
Deligne complex of differential forms with logarithmic singularities
at infinity.

The building block is
provided by the differential form

\lambda=-\frac{1}{2}\log(t\bar{t})=-\frac{1}{2}\log(t\bar{t})\otimes{\mathds{1}}(0)_{\text{\rm dR}}\in\mathfrak{D}^{1}_{\log}\Big(\mathds{C}^{\ast},1\Big).

Let

\pi_{i}\colon(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\to\mathds{P}^{1}
be the projection to the
i
-th
component. Then the Wang’s forms are defined as

W_{n}(n-1)\coloneqq\Alt(\pi^{\ast}_{1}\lambda\bullet(\cdots(\pi^{\ast}_{n-1}\lambda\bullet\pi^{\ast}_{n}\lambda))\in\mathfrak{D}^{n}_{\log}((\mathds{C}^{\ast})^{n},n),

where
\Alt
means the alternate sum for all possible permutations of

n
elements divided by
n!
. This is needed because the product in
the Deligne complex is not associative.

By the definition of the Deligne complex
W_{n}\in E^{n-1}_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n},\mathds{R}}(\log B)
, where
B=(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\setminus(\mathds{C}^{\ast})^{n}
is the normal crossing divisor of points of

(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}
with at least one coordinate equal to zero or infinity.

Consider the differential form

\alpha\coloneqq(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\frac{dt_{n}}{t_{n}}\right)\in F^{n}E^{n}_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}(\log B).

The main properties of the Wang forms are that they satisfy the
differential equations

d_{\mathfrak{D}}W_{n}=0

and

(1.3)

d_{\mathfrak{D}}[W_{n}(n-1)]=\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+j}(\delta_{j}^{i})_{\ast}[W_{n-1}(n-2)].

Moreover

dW_{n}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+(-1)^{n-1}\bar{\alpha})

and

(1.4)

d[W_{n}(n-1)]=\frac{1}{2}([\alpha(n-1)]+\overline{[\alpha(n-1)]})+\sum_{j=0}^{1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+j}(\delta_{j}^{i})_{\ast}[W_{n-1}(n-2)].

By abuse of notation we will also denote by
W_{n}
the
pull-back of
W_{n}
to any variety of the form
X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}
.
If
Z
is an irreducible subvariety of
X\times\square^{n}
intersecting properly all the faces and
\widetilde{Z}

is a resolution of singularities of the closure
\overline{Z}
, then
using the fact that
W_{n}
vanishes when one of the coordinates

t_{i}=1
, we get that the pull-back of
W_{n}
to
\widetilde{Z}
is locally integrable.
Therefore, for any cycle
Z\in Z^{p}(X,\ast)_{0}
, we have a well defined current

\delta_{Z}\bullet W_{n}(n-1)\coloneqq\delta_{Z}\wedge W_{n}(n-1)\in\mathfrak{D}^{2p+n}_{D}(X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n},p+n).

Then, Goncharov regulator current is given by (see
[
Gon95
, §6.1]
)

(1.5)

\mathcal{P}(Z)=(\pi_{X})_{\ast}(\delta_{Z}\bullet W_{n}(n-1))\in\mathfrak{D}_{D}^{2p-n}(X,p)

where
\pi_{X}\colon X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\to X
is the
projection. The fact that
\mathcal{P}
is a morphism of complexes follows
from equation (
1.3
).

1.4.
Extension class and differential forms

Let

be a smooth projective complex variety.
Write

Given a refined cycle

2.
Height of a Mixed Hodge Structure

In this section, we introduce a notion of
framed mixed
Hodge structures
and generalize the height of a biextension of
Hodge structures to define the height of a framed mixed Hodge
structure. In fact we give two such generalizations that agree (up to
a trivial normalization factor) for biextensions of Hodge structures
and mixed Hodge structures with the shape (
0.2
), but that differ
for more general mixed Hodge structures.

2.1.
The Deligne splitting

We recall some basic properties of mixed Hodge structures.
Let
V
be a
\mathds{Q}
-vector space.
A mixed Hodge structure
H=(F,W)
on
V
induces a unique functorial bigrading

[
CKS86
, Theorem 2.13]

(2.1)

V_{\mathds{C}}=\bigoplus_{p,q}\,I^{p,q}

of the underlying complex vector space
V_{\mathds{C}}
such that

(i)

F^{p}=\oplus_{p^{\prime}\geq p,q}\,I^{p^{\prime},q}
;

(ii)

W_{k}=\oplus_{p+q\leq k}\,I^{p,q}
;

(iii)

\overline{I^{p,q}}\equiv I^{q,p}\mod\oplus_{p^{\prime}<p,q^{\prime}<q}\,I^{q^{\prime},p^{\prime}}
.

The subspaces
I^{p,q}
are given by

(2.2)

I^{p,q}=F^{p}\cap W_{p+q}\cap\left(\overline{F^{q}}\cap W_{p+q}+\overline{U^{q-1}_{p+q-2}}\right),

where

U^{r}_{s}=\sum_{j\geq 0}F^{r-j}\cap W_{s-j}.

When we need to specify the mixed Hodge structure we will denote the
different pieces as
I^{p,q}_{H}
or as
I^{p,q}H
if the symbol
denoting
H
in a particular situation is too long.

Definition 2.1
.

The bigrading (
2.1
) is called the

Deligne bigrading
of
H
. The associated semisimple endomorphism

Y=Y_{H}
of
V_{\mathds{C}}
which acts as multiplication by
p+q
on

I^{p,q}
is called the
Deligne grading
of
H
.

We denote by
\Pi_{p,q}\colon V_{\mathds{C}}\to V_{\mathds{C}}
the projector of
V_{\mathds{C}}
that is the
identity on
I^{p,q}
and zero on
I^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
for
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\not=(p,q)
and write
\Pi_{k}\coloneqq\sum_{p+q=k}\Pi_{p,q}
. We denote also by

\pi_{k}\colon V_{\mathds{C}}\to\Gr^{W}_{k}V_{\mathds{C}}
the projection induced
by
\Pi_{k}
and the isomorphism
\bigoplus_{p+q=k}I^{p,q}\simeq\Gr^{W}_{k}V_{\mathds{C}}
. We also denote by
\iota_{k}\colon\Gr^{W}_{k}V_{\mathds{C}}\to V_{\mathds{C}}
the monomorphism induced by the inverse of
the previous isomorphism. In particular

\Pi_{k}=\iota_{k}\circ\pi_{k}.

Remark 2.2
.

In general, the maps
\pi_{k}
and
\iota_{k}
are not
compatible with the real structures of
V_{\mathds{C}}
and
\Gr_{k}^{W}V_{\mathds{C}}

unless the mixed Hodge structure is split over
\mathds{R}

The semisimple endomorphism
Y
is given by

(2.3)

Y=\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}}k\Pi_{k}.

Let

(2.4)

\gl(V_{\mathds{C}})^{a,b}=\{\,\alpha\in\gl(V_{\mathds{C}})\mid\alpha(I^{p,q})\subseteq I^{p+a,q+b}\,\}

be the Hodge decomposition of
\gl(V)
and define

(2.5)

\Lambda^{-1,-1}=\bigoplus_{a<0,b<0}\,\gl(V_{\mathds{C}})^{a,b}.

Then,
\overline{\Lambda^{-1,-1}}=\Lambda^{-1,-1}

[
CKS86
, Eq. 2.19]
.
For an element
\lambda\in\gl(V_{\mathds{C}})
we denote
\lambda=\sum\lambda^{a,b}
its decomposition into Hodge components.

There exists a unique
real element
\delta=\delta_{H}\in\Lambda^{-1,-1}
such that

(2.6)

\overline{Y_{H}}=e^{-2i\delta}\cdot Y_{H}

where
g\cdot\alpha\coloneqq\Ad(g)\alpha
denotes the adjoint action of

\GL(V_{\mathds{C}})

on
\gl(V_{\mathds{C}})
,
[
CKS86
, Prop 2.20]
. The element

\delta
defined by (
2.6
)
is called the
Deligne splitting
of
H
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
H
be a mixed Hodge structure and
\overline{H}
the complex
conjugate as in Example
1.5
. Then, it follows from the
definition of the Deligne splitting that

I^{p,q}_{\overline{H}}=\overline{I^{p,q}_{H}}.

It follows that
Y_{\overline{H}}=\overline{Y_{H}}
, and therefore

\delta_{\overline{H}}=-\delta_{H}
.

For an element
g\in\GL(V_{\mathds{C}})
we denote by
g\cdot F
the
filtration given by
(g\cdot F)^{p}V_{\mathds{C}}=g(F^{p}V_{\mathds{C}})
. In general if

(F,W)
is a mixed Hodge structure on
V
, the pair of filtrations

(g\cdot F,W)
do not form a mixed Hodge structure. However, we have
the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
Pea00
, Lemma 4.11]
)
.

Let
H=(F,W)
be a
mixed Hodge structure on
V
and
\Lambda^{-1,-1}
the associated

(
2.5
). Then,

implies that

and that

(2.7)

I^{p,q}_{(e^{\lambda}\cdot F,W)}=e^{\lambda}(I^{p,q}_{(F,W)}).

Proof.

The condition that the pair of filtrations

is a mixed Hodge structure is that, for every

, the filtration induced by

on

. Since
\lambda\in\Lambda_{-1,-1}
, the filtrations
induced by
F
and by
e^{\lambda}\cdot F
on

are the
same. So if
F

induces a pure Hodge structure in each

the same is
true for
e^{\lambda}\cdot F
. The equation (

) is

[
Pea00
, Lemma 4.11]
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
H=(F,W)
be a mixed Hodge structure on
V
,
\lambda\in\Lambda^{-1,-1}
and
\hat{H}=(e^{\lambda}\cdot F,W)
the new mixed
Hodge structure. Denote by
\hat{Y}
,
\hat{\Pi}_{k}
,
\hat{\pi}_{k}
and
\hat{\iota}_{k}
the operators defined using the

2.2.
Framed mixed Hodge structures

We now introduce the type of mixed Hodge structures that we will encounter later on.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a mixed Hodge structure
H=(F,W)
on
V
, an
(a,b)
-
framing
of

H
is the data of morphisms

\phi\colon\mathds{Q}(-a)\rightarrow\Gr^{W}_{2a}(V)\text{ and }\psi\colon\Gr^{W}_{2b}(V)\rightarrow\mathds{Q}(-b)

of pure Hodge structures.
A mixed Hodge structure with an
(a,b)
-framing will be called

(a,b)
-
framed
or just
framed
if the weights are clear from the
context. Sometimes, an
(a,b)
-framed mixed Hodge
(H,\phi,\psi)
will be
denoted just by the mixed Hodge structure
H
or if we want to
emphasize the involved weights by
H_{a,b}
.

Remark 2.7
.

This notion of framing is not new and was introduced in
[
BGSV
, §1.3.4]
, albeit for mixed Hodge-Tate structures. More generally, it has also appeared in
[
Jaf03
]
. There could be other references that the authors are not aware of. We present it in a way that is more suitable for our later purpose of attaching such mixed Hodge structures to pairs of higher cycles.

Remark 2.8
.

The oriented mixed Hodge structure defined in
[
BGGP22
, Definition 2.3]
is an example of a framed mixed Hodge structure,
where the homomorphisms
(\phi,\psi)
are isomorphisms.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}
the set of all
(a,b)
-framed mixed
Hodge structures. Given two framed mixed Hodge
structures
(H,\phi,\psi)
and
H^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
in

\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}
, a morphism
f\colon H\to H^{\prime}
of
mixed Hodge structures is called a
framed
morphism if

\phi^{\prime}=\Gr_{2a}^{W}(f)\circ\phi
and

\psi=\psi^{\prime}\circ\Gr_{2b}^{W}(f)
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
(H,\phi,\psi)
be a framed mixed Hodge structure and
f\colon H\to H^{\prime}
be an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures. Defining

\phi^{\prime}=\Gr_{2a}(f)\circ\phi
and
\psi^{\prime}=\psi\circ\Gr_{2b}(f^{-1})
we turn
f
into an isomorphism of framed mixed
Hodge structures.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(H,\phi,\psi)
be an
(a,b)
-framed mixed Hodge structure on
V
.

(i)

The dual
(H^{\vee},\psi^{\vee},\phi^{\vee})
is the
(-b,-a)
-framed mixed Hodge
structure on
V^{\vee}
given by

\displaystyle\psi^{\vee}

\displaystyle\colon\mathds{Q}(b)=\mathds{Q}(-b)^{\vee}\to\Gr^{W}_{2b}(V)^{\vee}=\Gr^{W}_{-2b}(V^{\vee}),

\displaystyle\phi^{\vee}

\displaystyle\colon\Gr^{W}_{-2a}(V^{\vee})=\Gr^{W}_{2a}(V)^{\vee}\to\mathds{Q}(-a)^{\vee}=\mathds{Q}(a).

(ii)

Let
p
be an integer, then the twisted framed mixed Hodge
structure
H(p)
is the
(a-p,b-p)
-framed mixed Hodge structure

\displaystyle\phi\otimes\Id

\displaystyle\colon\mathds{Q}(-a+p)=\mathds{Q}(-a)\otimes\mathds{Q}(p)\longrightarrow\Gr^{W}_{2a}(V)\otimes\mathds{Q}(p)=\Gr^{W(p)}_{2a-2p}(V),

\displaystyle\psi\otimes\Id

\displaystyle\colon\Gr^{W(p)}_{2b-2p}(V)=\Gr^{W}_{2b}(V)\otimes\mathds{Q}(p)\longrightarrow\mathds{Q}(-b)\otimes\mathds{Q}(p)=\mathds{Q}(-b+p).

By abuse of notation we will denote
\phi\otimes\Id
and

\psi\otimes\Id
by
\phi
and
\psi
.

A framing together with the Deligne splitting determine elements in
the mixed Hodge structure and its dual.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
H=(F,W,\phi,\psi)
be an
(a,b)
-framed
mixed Hodge structure. Let
{\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}}
be the Betti generator of

\mathds{Q}(-a)
.
We denote by
e_{H}
the element

e_{H}=\iota_{2a}\circ\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}})

This element belongs to
I^{a,a}_{H}
.

Remark 2.12
.

The element
e_{H}
is characterized by the conditions

(i)

e_{H}\in I_{H}^{a,a}\subset W_{2a}V_{\mathds{C}}
.

(ii)

The class
[e_{H}]\in\Gr_{2a}^{W}V_{\mathds{C}}
satisfies

[e_{H}]=\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}})
.

In other words,
e_{H}
is the unique lifting of
\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}})\in\Gr_{2a}^{W}V_{\mathds{C}}

that belongs to
I^{a,a}_{H}
.

Applying Definition
2.11
to the dual framed mixed Hodge structure of
Remark
2.10
we have an element
e_{H^{\vee}}\in I^{-b,-b}_{H^{\vee}}
.

Remark 2.13
.

Conversely, if
e_{H}
is an element of
I^{a,a}_{H}
whose image
in
\Gr_{2a}^{W}H
belongs to
\Gr_{2a}^{W}H_{\text{\rm B}}
, then we can
define
\phi\colon\mathds{Q}(-a)\longrightarrow\Gr_{2a}^{W}H
as the
unique map that sends
{\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}}
to
e_{H}
. Similarly

e_{H^{\vee}}
determines the map
\psi
. Therefore, we can
alternatively define framed mixed Hodge structure using the
elements
e_{H}
and
e_{H^{\vee}}
. Namely, an
(a,b)
-framed mixed
Hodge structure can be defined as a triple
(H,e_{H},e_{H^{\vee}})
,
where
H
is a mixed Hodge structure,
e_{H}
is an element of
I^{a,a}_{H}
whose image
in
\Gr_{2a}^{W}H
belongs to
\Gr_{2a}^{W}H_{\text{\rm B}}
and
e_{H^{\vee}}
is
an element of
I^{-b,-b}_{H^{\vee}}
whose image in

\Gr_{-2b}^{W}H^{\vee}
belongs to
\Gr_{-2b}^{W}H^{\vee}_{\text{\rm B}}
.

With this notation some formulae and properties become more
transparent. For instance
if
H=(H,e_{H},e_{H^{\vee}})
is a framed mixed Hodge structure,
then the dual one is

(H^{\vee},e_{H^{\vee}},e_{H})
, while the twisted framed mixed
Hodge structure
H(n)
is given by

(H(n),e_{H}\otimes{\mathds{1}}(n)_{\text{\rm B}},e_{H^{\vee}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}(-n)_{\text{\rm B}})
.

2.3.
Heights of framed mixed Hodge structures

With a framed mixed Hodge structure, one can associate two notions of
height. We will now introduce these notions.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
H=(F,W,\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}
be a framed mixed Hodge
structure, such that
b<a
.
Then the first height function is defined as

(2.8)

\Ht_{1}(H)=\im\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\overline{e_{H}}\rangle

where
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the duality between
H
and

H^{\vee}
.

Remark 2.15
.

Since we are assuming
b<a
, we have that
\langle e_{H^{\vee}},e_{H}\rangle=0
. The fact that the height
\Ht_{1}

may be non zero is related to the fact that the Deligne splitting is
not compatible with complex conjugation, hence, in general

\overline{e_{H}}\not\in I^{a,a}_{H}
. In particular, if the mixed
Hodge structure is split over
\mathds{R}
then the height is zero.

We next show that the function
\Ht_{1}
is related to the exponential
of the Deligne splitting.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\delta_{H}
be the Deligne splitting associated with the framed mixed Hodge structure
H
. Then

\overline{e_{H}}=e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H}).

Proof.

The element
e_{H}
satisfies
Y(e_{H})=2a\,e_{H}
. Its conjugate
\overline{e_{H}}
is
uniquely characterized by the properties

(i)

\overline{e_{H}}\in W_{2a}(V_{\mathds{C}}),

(ii)

\pi_{2a}(\overline{e_{H}})=\pi_{2a}(e_{H})

(iii)

\overline{Y}(\overline{e_{H}})=2a\overline{e_{H}}

Clearly
\overline{e_{H}}
satisfies properties
(i)
and

(iii)
. Property
(ii)
follows from the computation

\pi_{2a}(\overline{e_{H}})=\overline{\pi_{2a}(e_{H})}=\overline{\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}})}=\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}})=\pi_{2a}(e_{H}).

For
the first equality, note that although the map
\pi_{k}\colon V_{\mathds{C}}\to\Gr_{k}^{W}V_{\mathds{C}}
may not be compatible with complex
conjugation, the restriction
\pi_{k}\colon W_{k}V_{\mathds{C}}\to\Gr_{k}^{W}V_{\mathds{C}}
always is.
Let now
x\in V_{\mathds{C}}
be an element satisfying conditions
(i)

to
(iii)
. Conditions
(i)

and
(iii)
imply that

x\in\bigoplus_{p+q=2a}\overline{I^{p,q}}.

Since
\pi_{2a}
restricts to an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{p+q=2a}\overline{I^{p,q}}\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Gr_{2a}^{W}(V_{\mathds{C}})

condition
(ii)
nails down the equality
x=\overline{e_{H}}
.

We now show that the element
e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})
also
satisfies properties
(i)
,
(ii)
and

(iii)
. Properties
(i)
and
(ii)
are immediate since

e^{-2i\delta_{H}}
preserves the weight filtration and induces the
identity on the graded vector space
\Gr^{W}_{\ast}
. For property

(iii)
, notice that

\overline{Y}\left(e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})\right)=e^{-2i\delta_{H}}\circ Y\left(e^{2i\delta_{H}}\circ e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})\right)\\
=e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(Y(e_{H}))=2ae^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H}).

Since
\overline{e_{H}}
is the unique element which satisfies these
properties, we have the equality
\overline{e_{H}}=e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})
.
∎

Corollary 2.17
.

The height
\Ht_{1}
is given by the formula

\Ht_{1}(H)=\im\langle e_{H^{\vee}},e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})\rangle.

Corollary
2.17
suggests that an alternative definition of height
can be obtained by using
\delta_{H}
in the place of
e^{-2i\delta_{H}}
. This new definition would have the advantage of being real
on the nose, without the need of taking the imaginary part.

We need the following result:

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\delta_{H}
be the Deligne splitting associated with a mixed Hodge
structure
H=(F,W)
on
V
. Then for every pair of integers
k^{\prime}<k
,
and every
r>0
, the operator

\pi_{k^{\prime}}\circ\delta^{r}_{H}\circ\iota_{k}\colon\Gr_{k}^{W}V\longrightarrow\Gr_{k^{\prime}}^{W}V

is real.

Proof.

Let
\hat{H}=(e^{-i\delta_{H}}\cdot F,W)
be the associated
real-split mixed Hodge structure. By Corollary
2.5
, the
operators
\hat{\iota}_{k}
and

\hat{\pi}_{k^{\prime}}
corresponding to
\hat{H}
are given by the formulae

\hat{\iota}_{k}=e^{-i\delta_{H}}\circ\iota_{k}
and

\widehat{\pi}_{k^{\prime}}=\pi_{k^{\prime}}\circ e^{i\delta_{H}}
. Since
\hat{H}
is real split these operators are
real. Hence, so is
\hat{\pi}_{k^{\prime}}\circ\delta_{H}\circ\hat{\iota}_{k}
.
Putting the relations between the corresponding operators, we get

\hat{\pi}_{k^{\prime}}\circ\delta^{r}_{H}\circ\hat{\iota}_{k}=\pi_{k^{\prime}}\circ e^{i\delta_{H}}\circ\delta^{r}_{H}\circ e^{-i\delta_{H}}\circ\iota_{k}=\pi_{k^{\prime}}\circ\delta^{r}_{H}\circ\iota_{k}.

∎

This allows us to give the definition of the alternative height.

Definition 2.19
.

Let
H=(F,W,\phi,\psi)
be
an
(a,b)
-framed mixed Hodge structure,
the
second height function
is given by

\Ht_{2}(H)=\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle.

Lemma 2.20
.

\Ht_{2}(H)\in\mathds{R}.

Proof.

Unraveling the definition of the elements
e_{H}
and
e_{H}^{\vee}

in the definition of the height, we obtain

\Ht_{2}(H)=\langle\psi^{\vee}({\mathds{1}}(b)_{\text{\rm B}}),\pi_{2b}\circ\delta_{H}\circ\iota_{2a}(\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}}))\rangle,

where now
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the duality between

\Gr_{2b}^{W}V_{\mathds{C}}
and
\Gr_{-2b}^{W}V^{\vee}_{\mathds{C}}
. Since

\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm B}})
and
\psi^{\vee}({\mathds{1}}(b)_{\text{\rm B}})
are real, the fact
that
\Ht_{2}(H)\in\mathds{R}
is a consequence of Proposition

2.18
.
∎

When a framed mixed Hodge structure is a generalized biextension,
we have seen in
[
BGGP22
, Lemma 2.6]
that the two heights
agree up to a factor of
-\frac{1}{2}
. More generally the following result
holds.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
H\in\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}
be a framed mixed Hodge
structure satisfying
\delta_{H}^{3}(e_{H})=0
. Then

\Ht_{2}(H)=-\frac{1}{2}\Ht_{1}(H).

Proof.

The condition
\delta_{H}^{3}(e_{H})=0
implies that

e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})=(e_{H}-2\delta_{H}^{2}(e_{H}))+i(-2\delta_{H}(e_{H})).

Therefore, using Proposition
2.18

\Ht_{1}(H)=\im\langle e_{H^{\vee}},e^{-2i\delta_{H}}(e_{H})\rangle=\langle e_{H^{\vee}},-2\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle=-2\Ht_{2}(H).

∎

However, the example below shows that this is not the case in general.

Example 2.22
.

Let
H(F,W)
be a mixed Hodge structure on a vector space
V
, whose graded pieces
satisfy

\Gr_{k}^{W}(V)\cong\begin{cases}\mathds{Q}(0),&\text{ if }k=0,\\
\mathds{Q}(1),&\text{ if }k=-2,\\
\mathds{Q}(2),&\text{ if }k=-4,\\
\mathds{Q}(3),&\text{ if }k=-6,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

With the
(0,-3)
-framing given by the isomorphisms

\mathds{Q}(0)\to\Gr_{0}^{W}(V)
and
\Gr_{-6}^{W}(V)\to\mathds{Q}(3)
.
Then

\Ht_{2}(H)=-\frac{1}{2}\Ht_{1}(H)-\frac{2}{3}\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\delta_{H}^{3}(e_{H})\rangle,

as can be shown by expanding
e^{-2i\delta_{H}}
.

Example 2.23
.

We illustrate with the easiest example the
relationship between the height of mixed Hodge structures we have
defined and the classical height of cycles. Consider
X=\mathds{P}^{1}
and
cycles
Z=\{0,\infty\}
and
W=\{1,t\}
, with

H=\rH^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1}\smallsetminus Z,W)

\Gr_{2}^{W}(H)\cong\mathds{Q}(-1),\qquad\Gr_{0}^{W}(H)\cong\mathds{Q}(0)

and
\Gr_{k}^{W}(H)=0
for all

f\colon\mathds{P}^{1}\to\mathds{R}
be a smooth function such that
f(0)=f(1)=f(\infty)=0
and
f(t)=1
. Then

H_{\text{\rm dR}}
is generated by the classes
\{df\}
and
\{dz/z\}
,
with

W_{0}H_{\text{\rm dR}}=\mathds{C}\{df\},\qquad F^{1}H_{\text{\rm dR}}=\mathds{C}\{dz/z\}.

Let

\gamma
be a small circle around
0
and
\eta
a path between
1

and
t
. Then
\gamma
and
\eta
define generators of

H^{\vee}=\rH_{1}(\mathds{P}^{1}\smallsetminus Z,W).

The dual basis
\{\eta^{\ast},\gamma^{\ast}\}
is a
\mathds{Q}
-basis of

H_{\text{\rm B}}
. The comparison isomorphism between Betti and de Rham
is given by the equalities

\displaystyle\{dz/z\}

\displaystyle=2\pi i\gamma^{\ast}+\log t\eta^{\ast},

\displaystyle\{df\}

\displaystyle=\eta^{\ast}.

It is easy to check that

. Then

\{dz/(2\pi iz)\}=\gamma^{\ast}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log t\eta^{\ast}

\{df\}

\Pi_{0,0}(\overline{\{dz/(2\pi iz)\}})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log(t\bar{t})\eta^{\ast}.

Therefore

\Ht_{1}(H)=\im(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\log(t\overline{t}))=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log(t\bar{t}),

\Ht_{2}(H)=\frac{-1}{2\pi}\log(|t|),

Up to a normalization factor of

Z
and
W
.

Remark 2.24
.

We have defined the height of a mixed Hodge structure by comparing
Betti generators. Similarly one can define other variants of the height
by comparing de Rham generators. Let
(H,\phi,\psi)
be an
(a,b)
-framed
mixed Hodge structure. Let
{\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm dR}}
be the de Rham generator of

\mathds{Q}(-a)
.
We denote by
e_{H,\text{\rm dR}}
the element

e_{H,\text{\rm dR}}=\iota_{2a}\circ\phi({\mathds{1}}(-a)_{\text{\rm dR}})=(2\pi i)^{a}e_{H}.

Then we define

\Ht_{1,\text{\rm dR}}(H)=\begin{cases}\re\langle e_{H^{\vee},\text{\rm dR}},\overline{e_{H,\text{\rm dR}}}\rangle,&\text{ if $a-b$
odd},\\
\im\langle e_{H^{\vee},\text{\rm dR}},\overline{e_{H,\text{\rm dR}}}\rangle,&\text{ if
$a-b$ even}.\end{cases}

and

\Ht_{2,\text{\rm dR}}(H)=i\langle e_{H^{\vee},\text{\rm dR}},\delta_{H}(e_{H,\text{\rm dR}})\rangle.

Then

\Ht_{1,\text{\rm dR}}(H)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{1-a-b}{2}}(2\pi)^{a-b}\Ht_{1}(H),&\text{ if $a-b$
odd},\\
(-1)^{-\frac{a+b}{2}}(2\pi)^{a-b}\Ht_{1}(H),&\text{ if $a-b$
even},\\
\end{cases}

and

\Ht_{2,\text{\rm dR}}(H)=i(-1)^{a}(2\pi i)^{a-b}\Ht_{2}(H).

Note that
\Ht_{1,\text{\rm dR}}(H)
is still always real while

\Ht_{2,\text{\rm dR}}(H)
is real or imaginary depending on the parity of

a-b
. This is chosen to make the normalization factors disappear in
Remark
2.29
.

Essentially the Betti and the de Rham heights carry the same
information but the normalization factors are different.

\Ht_{1}(H(z)_{-a,-b})
does not in case

b>a>0
.

We next discuss the functoriality properties of the height. In fact,
both heights are functorial with respect to framed morphisms of mixed
Hodge structures.

Proposition 2.31
.

Let
f\colon H\rightarrow H^{\prime}
be a framed morphism of
(a,b)
-framed
mixed Hodge structures. Then for
i=1,2
,

\Ht_{i}(H)=\Ht_{i}(H^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We show the result for
\Ht_{2}
, since it involves less notation. Let

H=(F,W,\phi,\psi)
and
H^{\prime}=(F^{\prime},W^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
, such that
\phi^{\prime}=\Gr_{2a}(f)\circ\phi
and

\psi=\psi^{\prime}\circ\Gr_{2b}(f)
. Since the Deligne splitting is functorial
with respect to morphisms of mixed Hodge structures we have
f\circ\delta_{H_{a,b}}=\delta_{H^{\prime}_{a,b}}\circ f
. Moreover
f(I^{p,q}_{H})\subset I^{p,q}_{H^{\prime}}
and
f^{\vee}(I^{p,q}_{H^{\prime}{}^{\vee}})\subset I^{p,q}_{H}
. This implies that
e_{H^{\prime}}=f(e_{H})
and

e_{H^{\vee}}=f^{\vee}(e_{H^{\prime}{}^{\vee}})
.

From the definition we get

\Ht_{2}(H^{\prime})=\langle e_{H^{\prime}{}^{\vee}},\delta_{H^{\prime}}(e_{H^{\prime}})\rangle=\langle e_{H^{\prime}{}^{\vee}},\delta_{H^{\prime}}(f(e_{H}))\rangle=\\
\langle e_{H^{\prime}{}^{\vee}},f(\delta_{H}(e_{H}))\rangle=\langle f^{\vee}(e_{H^{\prime}{}^{\vee}}),\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle=\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle=\Ht_{2}(H),

∎

Remark 2.32
.

More generally, given two framed mixed Hodge
structures
(H,\phi,\psi)
and
H^{\prime}=(H^{\prime},\phi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime})
in

\mathcal{H}^{a}_{b}
and a morphism
f\colon H\to H^{\prime}
of
mixed Hodge structures, that is not a framed one but satisfies

\phi^{\prime}=m_{1}(\Gr_{2a}(f)\circ\phi)
and
\psi=m_{2}(\psi^{\prime}\circ\Gr_{2b}(f))
, for
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathds{Z}
, then

m_{1}\Ht_{i}(H)=m_{2}\Ht_{i}(H^{\prime})
.

The following result shows that the heights change sign with respect to duality.

Proposition 2.33
.

Let
H^{\vee}
be the dual of a framed mixed Hodge structure
H
. Then
for
i=1,2
,
\Ht_{i}(H^{\vee})=-\Ht_{i}(H)
.

Proof.

Since duality is compatible with complex conjugation and the Deligne
splitting, we have

\langle e_{H},\overline{e_{H^{\vee}}}\rangle=\langle\overline{e_{H^{\vee}}},e_{H}\rangle=\overline{\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\overline{e_{H}}\rangle}.

Thus

\displaystyle\Ht_{1}(H^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\im\left(\langle e_{H},\overline{e_{H^{\vee}}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\im\left(\overline{\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\overline{e_{H}}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=-\im\left(\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\overline{e_{H}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=-\Ht_{1}(H).

Clearly,
Y_{H^{\vee}}=-Y^{T}_{H}
. We also have

\delta_{H^{\vee}}=-\delta^{T}_{H}
. Indeed

e^{2i\delta_{H}^{T}}\cdot Y_{H^{\vee}}=-e^{2i\delta_{H}^{T}}Y_{H}^{T}e^{-2i\delta_{H}^{T}}=\\
-(e^{-2i\delta_{H}}Y_{H}e^{2i\delta_{H}})^{T}=-\overline{Y}_{H}^{T}=\overline{Y}_{H^{\vee}},

so
-\delta^{T}_{H}
satisfies the conditions characterizing
\delta_{H^{\vee}}
.

From this we compute

\displaystyle\Ht_{2}(H^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\langle e_{H},\delta_{H^{\vee}}(e_{H^{\vee}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e_{H},-\delta^{T}_{H}(e_{H^{\vee}})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\delta_{H}(e_{H}),e_{H^{\vee}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Ht_{2}(H).

∎

On the other hand, if
H
is a framed mixed Hodge structure with

(a,b)
-framing given by
(e_{H},e_{H^{\vee}})
, then
\overline{H}

is also a
(a,b)
-framed mixed Hodge structure, with framing given by

((-1)^{a}\overline{e_{H}},(-1)^{-b}\overline{e_{H^{\vee}}})
.
To explain the sign, note that the composition

\mathds{Q}(a)\longrightarrow\Gr_{2a}^{W}H\longrightarrow H

induces a diagram

\mathds{Q}(a)\longrightarrow\overline{\mathds{Q}(a)}\longrightarrow\overline{\Gr_{2a}^{W}H}\longrightarrow\overline{H},

where the first arrow is the isomorphism of Example
1.6
. By
the last composition
{\mathds{1}}(a)_{\text{\rm B}}
is sent to
(-1)^{a}\overline{e_{H}}
.

Remark 2.34
.

Here we see yet another use of the symbol

x\mapsto\overline{x}
as the map from
H
to
\overline{H}
. Nevertheless if we identify
H_{\text{\rm B}}
with
(\overline{H})_{\text{\rm B}}
as they are the same
\mathds{Q}
-vector space, then this map
agrees with complex conjugation with respect to the real
structure given by
H_{\text{\rm B}}
.

Proposition 2.35
.

The heights of
H
and
\overline{H}
satisfy the relation

\Ht_{i}(\overline{H})=(-1)^{a-b+1}\Ht_{i}(H)\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ i=1,2.

Proof.

Using the fact that complex conjugation sends
\im
to
-\im
we get.
for
\Ht_{1}

\displaystyle\Ht_{1}(\overline{H})

\displaystyle=\im\left(\langle(-1)^{-b}\overline{e_{H^{\vee}}},\overline{(-1)^{a}\overline{e_{H}}}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a-b}\im\left(\overline{\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\overline{e_{H}}\rangle}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a-b+1}\Ht_{1}(H).

Note that in this computation we are using Remark
2.34
to
identify all the complex conjugations as the complex conjugation with
respect to the Betti real structure.

Next, using the fact that
\delta_{\overline{H}}=-\delta_{H}
(Example

2.3
) and that
\delta_{H}
is a real operator, we get

\displaystyle\Ht_{2}(\overline{H})

\displaystyle=\langle(-1)^{-b}\overline{e_{H^{\vee}}},\delta_{\overline{H}}((-1)^{a}\overline{e_{H}})\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a-b+1}\overline{\langle e_{H^{\vee}},\delta_{H}(e_{H})\rangle}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a-b+1}\Ht_{2}(H).

In the last equality we have used Lemma
2.20
.
∎

Finally we observe that the twist does not change the height.

Lemma 2.36
.

Let
(H,e_{H},e_{H^{\vee}})
be a framed mixed Hodge
structure and
p\in\mathds{Z}
. Then, for
i=1,2
,

\Ht_{i}(H)=\Ht_{i}(H(p))

Proof.

As mentioned in Remark
2.13
, it is easy to check that

e_{H(p)}=e_{H}\otimes{\mathds{1}}(p)_{\text{\rm B}}
, while

e_{H(p)^{\vee}}=e_{H^{\vee}}\otimes{\mathds{1}}(-p)_{\text{\rm B}}
. The statement
now follows from the fact that
{\mathds{1}}(p)_{\text{\rm B}}
and
{\mathds{1}}(-p)_{\text{\rm B}}
are invariant
with respect to the Betti complex conjugation and satisfy
\langle{\mathds{1}}(-p)_{\text{\rm B}},{\mathds{1}}(p)_{\text{\rm B}}\rangle=1
.
∎

3.
Cohomological results

From now, in order to save some space in big diagrams, we will omit
the coefficients in cohomology and write for instance, for a pair of spaces

Y\subset X
,

\rH^{\ast}(X,Y;a)=\rH^{\ast}(X,Y;\mathds{Q}(a)).

3.1.
Local product situation and duality

Given a smooth projective complex variety
X
, and two closed subsets
A
and

B
, with
Z=A\cap B
, the
cohomology groups of the pairs
(X\smallsetminus A,B\smallsetminus Z)
and

(X\smallsetminus B,A\smallsetminus Z)
will
be the main focus of our study. It is not always the case that these
groups
are in duality. However, when
A
and
B
are in
local
product situation
, the relative cohomology groups are indeed dual
to each other. In this section, we recall the definition of local
product situation, and gather some results related to relative cohomology
groups of the above type.

Before proceeding further we introduce a shorthand that will be useful
later when we consider complicated closed subsets.
Note that
Z
does not add information as it can be deduced from
A

and
B
so it is not needed to include it in the notation. Thus we
will write

\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B)\coloneqq\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B\smallsetminus(A\cap B)).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be closed subvarieties of
X
. We say that
A
and

B
are in
local product situation
if, for any point
x\in X
,
there is a
neighborhood
U
of
x
, a decomposition
U=U_{A}\times U_{B}
,
where
U_{A}
and
U_{B}
are open disks of smaller dimension, and
analytic subvarieties
A^{\prime}\subset U_{A}
and
B^{\prime}\subset U_{B}
such
that

A\cap U=A^{\prime}\times U_{B},\qquad B\cap U=U_{A}\times B^{\prime}.

The following result is proved in
[
BKV15
, Lemma 6.1.1]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible projective variety.
Let
A
and
B
be closed
subvarieties of
X
in local product situation. Then, for every

a,r\in\mathds{Z}
, there is an
isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures

\rH^{r}(X\smallsetminus A,B;a)\xrightarrow{\cong}\rH^{2d-r}(X\smallsetminus B,A;d-a)^{\vee}.

Example 3.3
.

We show now a simple example of subvarieties that are not
in local product situation. Let
X=\mathds{P}^{2}
and let
\ell_{1}
,

\ell_{2}
and
\ell_{3}
three different lines through the same
point
p
. Let
A=\ell_{1}\cup\ell_{2}
and
B=\ell_{3}
. Then

\rH^{r}(X\smallsetminus A,B)=\begin{cases}\mathds{Q}(-1),&\text{ if }r=1,\\
0,&\text{ if }r\not=1.\end{cases}\quad\rH^{r}(X\smallsetminus B,A)=\begin{cases}\mathds{Q}(0),&\text{ if }r=1,\\
0,&\text{ if }r\not=1.\end{cases}

Thus
A
and
B
are not in local product situation and do not
satisfy the conclusion of Lemma
3.2
.

For us, the main example of local product
situation is as follows.

Example 3.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be divisors
without common components of a complex manifold
X
such that
A\cup B
is a normal
crossing divisor. Then
A
and
B
are in local product situation.

Since this example is the main situation we will consider, from now on

X
will denote a smooth irreducible projective manifold and
A
,
B
will denote
simple normal crossing divisors without common components, such that
D=A\cup B
is a normal
crossing divisor. In this case we will use the following terminology.

Definition 3.5
.

A coordinate neighborhood
V
with coordinates
(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})
is called adapted to

D
if there are two disjoint subsets
I_{A}
and
I_{B}
of
[1,d]
such that

V\cap A=\{z\in V\mid\prod_{i\in I_{A}}z_{i}=0\}\quad V\cap B=\{z\in V\mid\prod_{i\in I_{B}}z_{i}=0\}

and, for all
z\in V
and
i\in I_{A}\cup I_{B}
,
|z_{i}|\leq 1/2
.

3.2.
Relative de Rham cohomology

We recall how to compute the de Rham component of the mixed
Hodge structure

\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B)
with its Hodge filtration.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\widetilde{B}
be the normalization of
B

that is a smooth variety, and let
\iota\colon\widetilde{B}\to X

be the induced map. The
sheaf of holomorphic differential
forms with logarithmic poles along
A
and vanishing along
B
is
the sheaf

\Sigma_{B}\Omega^{\ast}(\log A)=\{\omega\in\Omega^{\ast}(\log A)\mid\iota^{\ast}\omega=0\}.

Lemma 3.7
.

There is a canonical isomorphism

\Sigma_{B}\Omega^{\ast}(\log A)\cong\Omega^{\ast}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B).

Proof.

A differential form
\omega
belongs to
\Omega^{\ast}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B)
if and only if it can be written in each
neighborhood adapted to
D
as

(3.1)

\omega=\sum_{I,J,K}f_{I,J,K}\prod_{\ell\in I_{B}}z_{\ell}\bigwedge_{i\in I}\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\bigwedge_{j\in J}\frac{dz_{j}}{z_{j}}\bigwedge_{k\in K}dz_{k}

where the sum is over all subsets
I\subset I_{A}
,
J\subset I_{B}
and

K\subset\{1,\dots,d\}\smallsetminus I_{A}\cup I_{B}
and the functions

f_{I,J,K}
are holomorphic. But a differential
form can be written as in (
3.1
) if and only if it can be
written as

(3.2)

\omega=\sum_{I,J,K}f_{I,J,K}\prod_{\ell\in I_{B}\smallsetminus J}z_{\ell}\bigwedge_{i\in I}\frac{dz_{i}}{z_{i}}\bigwedge_{j\in J}dz_{j}\bigwedge_{k\in K}dz_{k},

which is exactly the condition for belonging to
\Sigma_{B}\Omega^{\ast}(\log A)
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

There is a natural isomorphism between
\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B;\mathds{C})
and the hypercohomology of the complex
\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B)
. Moreover the Hodge filtration of

\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B;\mathds{C})
is induced by the bête filtration

F^{p}\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B)=\bigoplus_{p^{\prime}\geq p}\Omega^{p^{\prime}}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B).

Proof.

In view of Lemma
3.7
it is enough to prove the
corresponding result for the complex of sheaves
\Sigma_{B}\Omega^{\ast}(\log A)
. To compute the relative cohomology

\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B;\mathds{C})

we need a complex that computes the cohomology of

X\setminus A
, a complex that computes the cohomology of

B\setminus(B\cap A)
and a map between the two complexes that
represents the pullback in cohomology. Then the relative cohomology
is the cohomology of the cone of that map shifted by one. The
cohomology of
X\setminus A
is the hypercohomology of the complex

\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)
. We next explain how to compute the
cohomology of
B\setminus(A\cap B)
.
Assume for simplicity that
B
is a simple
normal crossings divisor and let
B=B_{1}\cup\dots\cup B_{k}
be the
decomposition into smooth irreducible subvarieties. For each

I\subset[1,k]
, put
B_{I}=\bigcap_{i\in I}B_{i}
and

\iota_{I}\colon B_{I}\to X
for the inclusion. Then the
hypercohomology of the total complex of the double complex

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\iota_{i})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{i}}(\log B_{i}\cap A)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{|I|=2}(\iota_{I})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{I}}(\log B_{I}\cap A)\longrightarrow\dots{\\
}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{|I|=d}(\iota_{I})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{I}}(\log B_{I}\cap A)

is the cohomology of
B\setminus(B\cap A)
. Moreover, the Hodge
filtration of this cohomology is defined by the bête filtration

F^{p}\bigoplus_{|I|=r}(\iota_{I})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{I}}(\log B_{I}\cap A)=\bigoplus_{|I|=r}(\iota_{I})_{\ast}\bigoplus_{p^{\prime}\geq p}\Omega^{p^{\prime}}_{B_{I}}(\log B_{I}\cap A).

The map from
\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)
to the above total complex
is induced by the pullback to each component
B_{i}
. The lemma
follows from the fact that, for every
p
the sequence of sheaves on
X

0\longrightarrow F^{p}\Sigma_{B}\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)\longrightarrow F^{p}\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)\longrightarrow\\
F^{p}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}(\iota_{i})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{i}}(\log B_{i}\cap A)\longrightarrow\dots\longrightarrow F^{p}\bigoplus_{|I|=r}(\iota_{I})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{I}}(\log B_{I}\cap A)\longrightarrow\\
\dots\longrightarrow F^{p}\bigoplus_{|I|=d}(\iota_{I})_{\ast}\Omega^{\ast}_{B_{I}}(\log B_{I}\cap A)\longrightarrow 0

is exact.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The duality of Lemma
3.2
has a nice
interpretation in terms of differential forms.
There is a morphism of complexes of sheaves

(3.3)

(\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B))\otimes(\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-A))\\
\longrightarrow\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-A-B)

that induce maps

\rH^{k}(X\smallsetminus A,B)\otimes\rH^{\ell}(X\smallsetminus B,A)\longrightarrow\rH^{k+\ell}(X,A\cup B).

This product, together with the canonical isomorphism

\rH^{2d}(X,A\cup B;d)\simeq\rH^{2d}(X;d)\simeq\mathds{Q}(0)
induces the duality
of Lemma
3.2
.

3.3.
Relative cohomology and blow-up

The mixed Hodge structures on the cohomology of a pair of varieties
satisfy many functorial properties, like inverse images by morphisms
of pairs of spaces or direct images for proper morphisms. In many
cases, it is interesting, not only to know that a functorial property
is satisfied, but to have a concrete morphisms of complexes that
represents said functoriality. For instance, to represent the direct
image in cohomology for a proper morphism we need to go from
differential forms to currents, then use the direct image on currents
and, if needed, go back to differential forms. In this section we will
review some functorial properties that can be easily represented using
the complexes of differential forms with logarithmic singularities
along a divisor, that vanish along a second divisor. So for our
purposes, Remark
3.11
is more interesting than
Proposition
3.10
, that is only a particular case of a well
known result.

We first state the obvious functoriality.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a morphism of projective complex manifolds
and
D^{\prime}=A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}
a normal crossing divisor with
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}

normal crossing divisors without common components such that

f^{-1}(A)\subset A^{\prime}
and
f(B^{\prime})\subset B
. Then there is a morphism of mixed
Hodge structures

f^{\ast}\colon\rH^{\ast}(X\smallsetminus A,B)\longrightarrow\rH^{\ast}(X^{\prime}\smallsetminus A^{\prime},B^{\prime}).

Proof.

The conditions
f^{-1}(A)\subset A^{\prime}
and
f(B^{\prime})\subset B
imply that
there is a morphisms of pairs
(X^{\prime}\smallsetminus A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\smallsetminus(A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}))\to(X\smallsetminus A,B\smallsetminus(A\cap B))
that gives the claimed functoriality.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

With the hypothesis of Proposition
3.10
, since

f^{-1}(A)\subset A^{\prime}
, there is a pullback
morphism
f^{\ast}\colon\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)\to\Omega^{\ast}_{X^{\prime}}(\log A^{\prime})
. Since
f(B^{\prime})\subset B
this morphism sends

\Sigma_{B}\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A)
to
\Sigma_{B^{\prime}}\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A^{\prime})
. This map induces
the de Rham component of the map of Proposition
3.10
.

The second functoriality result is the following.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
W\subset B
be a smooth
irreducible variety that has only normal crossing with
D
(see
Definition
A.1
). Let

\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be the blow up of
X
along

W
, and
E
the exceptional divisor of this blow up. By the
condition on
W
,
\pi^{-1}(A\cup B)
is still a normal
crossing divisor. Let

\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{B}
be the strict transforms of
A
and

B
, then there is a natural isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures

(3.4)

\varphi\colon\rH^{\ast}(X\smallsetminus A,B)\xrightarrow{\cong}\rH^{\ast}(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\cup E).

Proof.

If
W\subset A\cap B
then there is a morphism of mixed Hodge structures,

\psi\colon\rH^{\ast}(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\cup E)\longrightarrow\rH^{\ast}(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{A}\cup E,\widehat{B})=\rH^{\ast}(X\smallsetminus A,B),

While, if
W\not\subset A
, then the map of pairs of spaces

(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{A},(\widehat{B}\cup E)\smallsetminus\widehat{A})\to(X\smallsetminus A,B\smallsetminus A)
induces a morphism of
mixed Hodge structures

\rH^{\ast}(X\smallsetminus A,B)\longrightarrow\rH^{\ast}(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\cup E).

We have to show that both morphisms give isomorphisms of vector
spaces.
Since
A
and
B
do not have any common components, and
A\cup B
is
a normal crossing divisor,
A
and
B
are in local product
situation. Hence, by Lemma
3.2
there are duality
isomorphisms

\displaystyle\rH^{n}(X\smallsetminus A,B)

\displaystyle\cong\rH^{2d-n}(X\smallsetminus B,A)^{\vee}

\displaystyle\rH^{n}(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{A},\widehat{B}\cup E)

\displaystyle\cong\rH^{2d-n}(\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{B}\cup E,\widehat{A})^{\vee}.

From the fact that
X\smallsetminus B=\widetilde{X}\smallsetminus\widehat{B}\cup E
, and
A\smallsetminus B=\widehat{A}\smallsetminus\widehat{B}\cup E
(using
W\subset B
), we get that the groups on the
right of the above isomorphisms are also isomorphic. Thus the groups
on the left are isomorphic.
Since this isomorphism of groups is compatible with the previously
defined morphisms of mixed Hodge
structures, we deduce that it is an isomorphism of mixed Hodge
structures.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

The de Rham component of the map (
3.4
) is induced by the
morphism of sheaves

(3.5)

\pi^{\ast}\colon\Omega^{\ast}_{X}(\log A\cup B)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-B)\longrightarrow\Omega^{\ast}_{\widetilde{X}}(\log\widehat{A}\cup\widehat{B}\cup E)\otimes\mathcal{O}(-\widehat{B}-E).

Next we want to see that, if we drop the condition
W\subset B
in

3.12
we still get an isomorphism on the condition
that we restrict to
certain pieces of the Hodge and weight filtration. For this we need
some preliminaries on the cohomology of projective spaces.

Lemma 3.14
.

In
\mathds{P}^{n}
with homogeneous coordinates
(z_{1}\colon\dots\colon z_{n+1})
, denote by
H_{i}
the coordinate hyperplane

z_{i}=0.
Then, for

the mixed Hodge structures of the

\rH^{\ast}(\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H_{1}\cup\dots\cup H_{r},H_{r+1}\cup\dots\cup H_{r+s})
are Hodge–Tate. Moreover, the
only non zero groups are

(i)

if
r=s=0

\rH^{2k}(\mathds{P}^{n})=\mathds{Q}(-k),\ k=0,\dots,n;

(ii)

if

and

\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H_{1}\cup\dots\cup H_{r})=\mathds{Q}(-k)^{\binom{r-1}{k}},\ k=0,\dots,r-1;

(iii)

if
r=0
and

\rH^{2n-k}(\mathds{P}^{n},H_{1}\cup\dots\cup H_{s})=\mathds{Q}(n-k)^{\binom{s-1}{k}},\ k=0,\dots s-1;

(iv)

if

\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H_{1}\cup\dots\cup H_{r},H_{r+1}\cup\dots\cup H_{r+s})=0.

The first statement is well known. The second follows from the

\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H_{1}\cup\dots\cup H_{r}=\mathds{A}^{n-r+1}\times\mathds{G}_{m}^{r-1}.

The third follows from the second by duality. The last statement is

\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H^{(r)},H^{(s)})\longrightarrow\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H^{(r)},H^{(s-1)})\longrightarrow\\
\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n-1}\smallsetminus H^{(r)},H^{(s-1)})\longrightarrow\dots{}

where

and

. Indeed, the case

and

is a consequence of the fact that the map

\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n}\smallsetminus H^{(r)})\to\rH^{k}(\mathds{P}^{n-1}\smallsetminus H^{(r)})
is an isomorphism and
the above exact sequence. While the same exact sequence
shows that once the vanishing is established for
s
, then it
is also true for
s+1
.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a locally trivial
\mathds{P}^{n}

Proposition 3.20

In

and

Remark 5.9
.

The mixed Hodge structures

Proposition 5.10

There is a map

\sigma_{W,Z}\colon H_{W,Z}(-n-1)\longrightarrow H_{Z,W}^{\vee},

such that, if

\omega\in E^{2q+n-1}_{\mathcal{X}_{Z,W}}(\si\widehat{A}_{2}\cup\widehat{B}_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup G_{2},\rd\widehat{A}_{1}\cup\widehat{B}_{2}\cup E_{1}\cup G_{1}\cup E_{3}\cup F)

\sigma_{W,Z}\big(\omega(q-1)\big)\Big(\eta(p+n)\Big)=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{d+2n}}\int_{X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{2n}}\omega\wedge\eta

On the other hand

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=\widehat{A}_{1}\cup\widehat{B}_{2}\cup E_{1}\cup G_{1},

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=\widehat{A}_{2}\cup\widehat{B}_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup G_{2},

Remark 5.11

Proposition 5.20
.

Let
X
be a complex smooth and projective variety of dimension
d
, and
Z\in Z^{p}(X,n)_{00}
,
W\in Z^{q}(X,n)_{00}
be higher cycles intersecting properly and satisfying
p+q=d+n+1
. If
p,q>d
, then
\Ht_{1}(Z,W)=0
.

Proof.

Recall that
(t^{1}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
are coordinates of the first set of

\mathds{P}^{1}
’s, while
(t^{2}_{i})_{i=1,\dots,n}
are coordinates of the
second set. Note that only
\alpha_{1}
and
\eta_{Z}
may contain
the coordinates
t_{i}^{1}
, while only
\alpha_{2}
and
\eta_{W}

may contain the coordinates
t_{i}^{2}
. Thus we can write

\Ht_{1}(Z,W)=\im\left(\frac{(-1)^{p+n+1}}{(2\pi i)^{p+q+2n}}\int_{X}\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}\bar{\eta}_{Z}\wedge\alpha_{1}\wedge\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}\eta_{W}\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{2}\right).

We focus on the push-forward currents on
X
given by

\beta_{Z}=\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}\eta_{Z}\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta_{W}=\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}\eta_{W}\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{2}.

So that

\Ht_{1}(Z,W)=\im\left(\frac{(-1)^{p+n+1}}{(2\pi i)^{p+q+2n}}\int_{X}\bar{\beta}_{Z}\wedge\beta_{W}\right).

First we observe that the condition
p>d
implies that
\beta_{Z}

is closed. Indeed, by (
5.1
) and the fact that
\eta_{Z}

vanishes in
B_{X}
we have

d\beta_{Z}=(2\pi i)^{p}(\pi_{Z})_{\ast}(\bar{\alpha}_{1}),

where
\pi_{Z}\colon Z\to X
is the composition
Z\to X\times(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}\to X
. But
p>d
implies
\dim Z<n
. Thus
\alpha_{1}|_{Z}=0
and
d\beta_{Z}=0
.
Since
\beta_{Z}
and
\beta_{W}
are both closed, we can
change them by boundaries without changing the integral.

Using again that
p>d
, each component of
\eta_{Z}
must contain at
least one
dt^{1}_{i}
. Therefore

\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}\bar{\eta}_{Z}\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{1}=0.

Hence

\beta_{Z}=\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}(\eta_{Z}+(-1)^{p-1}\bar{\eta}_{Z})\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{1}=\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}(2dg_{Z}+2\theta_{Z})\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{1}.

Since
g_{Z}
has no residue and vanishes on the support of the
residue of
\alpha_{1}
we deduce that

\beta_{Z}=\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}2\theta_{Z}\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{1}+\text{boundaries}.

Since

d\log|t^{1}_{i}|^{2}=\frac{dt^{1}_{i}}{t^{1}_{i}}+\frac{d\overline{t^{1}_{i}}}{\overline{t^{1}_{i}}},

we can find a
\gamma\in\Sigma_{A}E^{\ast}_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}(\log B)

with
d\gamma=\bar{\alpha}_{1}-(-1)^{n}\alpha_{1}
, such that the associated
current satisfies

d[\gamma]=[\bar{\alpha}_{1}]-(-1)^{n}[\alpha_{1}]+\chi

with
\supp\chi\subset B_{X}
. Since
\theta_{Z}
is closed and its
restriction to
B_{X}
is zero, we deduce that

d[\theta_{Z}\wedge\gamma]=[\theta_{Z}\wedge\bar{\alpha}_{1}-(-1)^{n}\theta_{Z}\wedge\alpha_{1}].

This implies that

\beta_{Z}=\int_{(\mathds{P}^{1})^{n}}\theta_{Z}\wedge(\bar{\alpha}_{1}+(-1)^{n}\alpha_{1})+\text{boundaries}.

Since
\bar{\theta}_{Z}=(-1)^{p-1}\theta_{Z}
, we conclude that

\bar{\beta}_{Z}=(-1)^{p+n-1}\beta_{Z}+\text{boundaries}.

We deduce

\overline{\frac{(-1)^{p+n+1}}{(2\pi i)^{p+q+2n}}\int_{X}\bar{\beta}_{Z}\wedge\beta_{W}}=\frac{(-1)^{p+n+1}}{(2\pi i)^{p+q+2n}}\int_{X}\bar{\beta}_{Z}\wedge\beta_{W},

so it is a real number and
\Ht_{1}(Z,W)=0
.
∎

Appendix A
Resolution of singularities

For convenience of the reader, we recall here a precise form of
Hironaka’s resolution of singularities.

Definition A.1
.

Let
X
be a regular variety of dimension
d
over a field
K
of
characteristic zero. Let
D
be a codimension one subvariety of
X
and
W
a
reduced closed subscheme of
X
. We say that
W

has only
normal crossings with

D

at a point

x
(or that
D
has only normal crossings
with
W
at
x
) if there is a regular sequence

(z_{1},\dots,z_{d})
in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
such that the ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
of each
component of
D
containing
x
is generated by one of the
z_{i}
,
while the ideal of
W
in
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is generated by some of the

z_{i}
. We say that
W

has only normal crossings with

D

if it has normal crossings with
D
at every point of
X
. When
W

is empty we just say that
D
has
only normal crossings
.

We will need the following two versions of Hironaka’s resolution of
singularities. The first is a version of
[
Hir64
, Theorem III(N,n)
]
, while the second follows from Corollary 1 of

[
Hir64
, Theorem II]
.

.

Let
X
be a regular variety over a field
K
of characteristic

D
be a divisor of
X
with only normal crossings and

W\subset X
a reduced closed subscheme. Then there exists a sequence

(X_{i},W_{i},D_{i},C_{i})
,

such that

(i)

,

,

,

;

(ii)

,

and is contained in

;

(iii)

\pi_{i+1}\colon X_{i+1}\to X_{i}

along

,

along

Abstract
. We give necessary and sufficient conditions for the Terwilliger algebra of a quasi-thin Schurian association scheme to coincide with: (a) the centralizer algebra of a point stabilizer of its automorphism group, and (b) its subspace T 0 T^{0} . As a by-product, a full characterization of triply-transitive quasi-thin association schemes is given. Using the latter, we provide new infinite families of triply-transitive association schemes.

Association schemes with small valences are interesting from the viewpoint of Terwilliger algebras since they are often non-commutative, and their Terwilliger algebras are naturally restricted by the valences. This paper is concerned with quasi-thin association schemes whose Terwilliger algebras are combinatorially and algebraically as small as possible.

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\Omega,\mathcal{R})
be an association scheme, where
\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}\}
and
R_{0}=\{(u,u):u\in\Omega\}
. Fix

\displaystyle E_{i,v}^{*}(u,u)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }(v,u)\in R_{i},\\

The Terwilliger algebra of

with respect to

given by

\displaystyle T_{v}=\left\langle A_{0},A_{1},\ldots,A_{d},E_{0,v}^{*},E_{1,v}^{*},\ldots,E_{d,v}^{*}\right\rangle.

On the one hand, the Terwilliger algebra
T_{v}
is a subalgebra of the centralizer algebra
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
, i.e, the set of all matrices in
\operatorname{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})
commuting with every permutation matrix of
G_{v}
. On the other hand, the Terwilliger algebra also contains the subspace

(1)

\displaystyle T_{v}^{0}=\operatorname{Span}\left\{E_{i,v}^{*}A_{j}E_{k,v}^{*}:0\leq i,j,k\leq d\right\}.

Therefore, we have

(2)

\displaystyle T_{v}^{0}\subseteq T_{v}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right).

The gap in dimensions between the subspaces in the above inclusions can be arbitrarily large. One example where such cases occur is with the association scheme of Paley graphs
[
4
]
. Equality can also hold in (

[
5

, one may think of the Terwilliger algebra to be as small as possible. Terwilliger algebras with this property admit a highly-regular combinatorial structure (given by
T_{v}^{0}=T_{v}
) and a highly-symmetric algebraic structure given by
T_{v}=\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
.

The subspace
T_{v}^{0}
is an algebra if and only if
T_{v}^{0}=T_{v}
. In this case, the association scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is called

Examples of triply-regular association schemes are the Hamming scheme

, the association scheme of the strongly-regular graphs
H(2,d)
, and the affine polar graphs
\operatorname{VO}_{2m}^{\varepsilon}(2)
, where
\varepsilon=\pm
and

. We note that if there exists

such that
T_{v}^{0}=T_{v}
, then
T_{u}^{0}=T_{u}
for all
u\in\Omega
.

If
T_{v}=\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
, then the structure of
T_{v}
is straightforward since
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
is completely determined by the action of
G_{v}
on
\Omega\times\Omega
. Hence, one can study the Terwilliger algebra from a purely algebraic viewpoint in this case.

If the automorphism group
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
is transitive, then the Terwilliger algebras
T_{u}
and
T_{v}
are isomorphic for any
u,v\in\Omega
. Similarly,
\operatorname{End}_{G_{u}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
and
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
are isomorphic for any
u,v\in\Omega
whenever
G
is transitive. Therefore, we will adopt the notation
T
for any Terwilliger algebra
T_{v}
, where
v\in\Omega
, whenever
G
is transitive. If
G
is transitive, then we also use the notation
\tilde{T}
instead of
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right).

Since the dimension of
T_{v}^{0}
only depends on the number of non-zero intersection numbers and not on
v\in\Omega
, we will also adopt the notation
T^{0}
instead of
T_{v}^{0}
.

We say that an association scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is
triply transitive
if
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
is transitive and
T^{0}=T=\tilde{T}
. Therefore, a triply transitive association scheme is also triply regular. The converse of this statement does not hold in general: the association scheme of many orthogonal array graphs are triply regular but not triply transitive. Triply-transitive association schemes were first studied by Bannai and Munemasa in
[
1
]
for conjugacy class association schemes. In particular, they showed that the conjugacy class association scheme of abelian groups are triply transitive. In
[
8
]
, the first author extended this result of Bannai and Munemasa to certain metacyclic groups that are “close” to being abelian. See
[
6
,
16
,
17
]
for results along the same line of research. In
[
5
]
, triply-transitive association schemes arising from strongly-regular graphs were studied and almost classified except those coming from two families of graphs arising from finite geometry. Not much is known about triply-transitive association schemes except for these few examples (conjugacy class association schemes and the association schemes of strongly-regular graphs).

This paper aims to provide more examples of triply-transitive association schemes. A by-product of this work is that we obtain a study of the Terwilliger algebras of quasi-thin association schemes over the complex numbers from a different perspective than those in the literature. We note that there are results on the Terwilliger algebras of such schemes in the literature, the majority of which are over fields of positive characteristic. In
[
7
]
, Jiang studied Terwilliger
\mathbb{F}
-algebras of quasi-thin association schemes, where
\mathbb{F}
is a field of positive characteristic. Recently, Chen and Xi
[
2
]
studied Terwilliger algebras of quasi-thin association schemes defined over a field of arbitrary characteristic from the viewpoint of representation theory of algebras. In particular, they showed that the Terwilliger algebra of quasi-thin association schemes are always quasi-hereditary cellular algebras
in the sense of Cline-Parshall-Scott and of Graham-Lehrer.
The results in our paper are however more from a combinatorial and group-theoretical viewpoints.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall some results from permutation group theory and the general theory of Schurian association schemes. In Section
3
, we show that thin association schemes are always triply transitive. Then, we introduce a combinatorial structure of quasi-thin association schemes called diamond pairs in Section
4
. Using these diamond pairs, we characterize certain algebraic and combinatorial properties of the Terwilliger algebras of quasi-thin Schurian association schemes respectively in Section
5
and Section
6
. Using these properties, we obtain a characterization of triply-transitive quasi-thin association schemes in Section
7
, and we give a new infinite family of examples in Section
7.3
.

2.1.
Permutation group theory

Let
\Omega
be a finite set and
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
be a transitive group. For any
\omega\in\Omega
, we denote the point stabilizer of
\omega
in
G
by
G_{\omega}
. The permutation group
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
is permutation equivalent to the group action of
G
on
G/G_{\omega}
by right multiplication. Conversely, a group action is a permutation group whenever the action is faithful.

For any
v\in\Omega
, the
suborbits
of
G
with respect to
v
are the orbits of
G_{v}
on
\Omega
. The suborbits of
G
with respect to
v
are in one-to-one correspondence with the orbitals of
G
, that is, the orbits of
G
on
\Omega\times\Omega
. In particular, a suborbit
\Delta
of
G
with respect to
v
corresponds to the neighbour of
v
in the digraph
(\Omega,O)
, where
O=(v,u)^{G}
, and
u\in\Delta
. The rank of the transitive group
G\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)
is the number of suborbits of
G
(or the number of orbitals). It turns out that if
G
is regular
4
4

4

i.e., acts fixed-point-freely and transitively.
, then its rank is equal to
|G|
.

2.2.
Algebras and vector spaces related to the Terwilliger algebra

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\Omega,\mathcal{R})
, where
\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}\}
, be an association scheme with automorphism group
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
. Let
A_{i}
be the adjacency matrix of the digraph
(\Omega,R_{i})
, for
1\leq i\leq d
. The Bose-Mesner algebra or the adjacency algebra of
\mathfrak{X}
is the algebra
\mathcal{B}
generated by the adjacency matrices
(A_{i})_{i=0,1,\ldots,d}
. The Bose-Mesner algebra acts by left multiplication on the vector space
V=\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}
. Thus, the vector space

is a left
\mathcal{B}
-module. The centralizer algebra of
\mathfrak{X}
is the algebra
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(V\right)
consisting of all matrices in
\operatorname{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})
commuting with the elements of
G_{v}
, viewed as permutation matrices.

As explained in the previous section, the Terwilliger algebra

is a subalgebra of the centralizer algebra
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(V\right)
. The next lemma gives the dimension of these centralizer algebras; its proof is omitted as it is a simple exercise.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
v\in\Omega
, the dimension of
\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(V\right)
is equal to the number of orbits of
G_{v}
on
\Omega\times\Omega
.

Assume now that
G
is transitive. Let
\Delta_{0}=\{v\},\Delta_{1},\ldots,\Delta_{d}
be the orbits of
G_{v}
, and for
0\leq i\leq d
, choose a representative

. If

is an orbit of
G_{v}
, then there exists

such that

. Therefore, there exists

such that

. We deduce that the number of orbits of
G_{v}

is equal to the number of orbits of

Another important subspace of the Terwilliger algebra

Lemma 2.2

For any

Let

If

For the remainder of this section, we let

\mathfrak{X}
is Schurian, then the relations of
\mathfrak{X}
are orbitals of

is non-Schurian, then every class is a union of orbitals of

. Using the fact that
\mathfrak{X}
is quasi-thin, the orbital graphs of

must have valency

, so the orbital scheme of

must be quasi-thin. Note however that if
X
and

are orbital digraphs of

has valency

. So any digraph of
\mathfrak{X}
of valency

is a union of two orbital digraphs of

G

2
.

Since the valences of
\mathfrak{X}
are
1
or

, it is not hard to see that the point stabilizer of

must be a cyclic group of order

. Fix

and let

be an involution such that

.

Lemma 2.3
.

If

is a regular subgroup, then

. Moreover,

.

Proof.

Assume that

|H|=|\Omega|=\tfrac{|G|}{|G_{v}|}=\tfrac{|G|}{2}
. In other words,

∎

As

2.4.
Triply-transitive association schemes

Consider an association scheme
\mathfrak{X}=(\Omega,\mathcal{R})
, where
\mathcal{R}=\{R_{0},R_{1},\ldots,R_{d}\}
, be an association scheme with transitive automorphism group
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
. For any
0\leq i\leq d
, we define

\displaystyle N_{i}(v):=\left\{u\in\Omega:(v,u)\in R_{i}\right\}.

Let
\Delta_{0}(v)=\{v\},\Delta_{1}(v),\ldots,\Delta_{r}(v)
be the orbits of
G_{v}
.
Note that
N_{i}(v)
is a union of orbits of
G_{v}
, and
\mathfrak{X}
is Schurian if and only if
d=r
.

As we saw before,
\mathfrak{X}
is triply transitive if
T^{0}=T=\tilde{T}
. We show that such association schemes are Schurian.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\mathfrak{X}
is triply transitive, then it is Schurian.

Proof.

Note that the idempotents
(E_{i,v}^{*})_{i=0,1,\ldots,d}
are orthogonal idempotents of
T_{v}^{0}
. The Peirce decomposition of
T_{v}^{0}
is therefore of the form

\displaystyle T_{v}^{0}=\bigoplus_{i,k=0}^{d}E_{i,v}^{*}T_{v}^{0}E_{k,v}^{*}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{v}=\bigoplus_{i,k=0}^{d}E_{i,v}^{*}\tilde{T}_{v}E_{k,v}^{*}.

It is not hard to see that for
0\leq i,k\leq d
, we have
E_{i,v}^{*}T_{v}^{0}E_{k,v}^{*}
is a subspace of
E_{i,v}^{*}\tilde{T}_{v}E_{k,v}^{*}
, and in particular,

\displaystyle\dim E_{i,v}^{*}T_{v}^{0}E_{k,v}^{*}\leq\dim E_{i,v}^{*}\tilde{T}_{v}E_{k,v}^{*}.

Since
\mathfrak{X}
is triply transitive, we have

\displaystyle\dim E_{i,v}^{*}T_{v}^{0}E_{k,v}^{*}=\dim E_{i,v}^{*}\tilde{T}_{v}E_{k,v}^{*},

for all
0\leq i,k\leq d
. On the one hand, for any
0\leq k\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle\dim E_{0,v}^{*}T_{v}^{0}E_{k,v}^{*}=\left|\left\{0\leq j\leq d:p_{0j}^{k}\neq 0\right\}\right|=\left|\{k\}\right|=1.

On the other hand, for any
0\leq k\leq d
, we have
\dim E_{0,v}^{*}\tilde{T}_{v}E_{k,v}^{*}
is the number of orbits of
G_{v}
on
N_{k}(v).
We deduce that
G_{v}
is transitive on every
N_{k}(v)
, for
0\leq k\leq d
. Hence,
r=d
, and
N_{k}(v)=\Delta_{k}(v)
for all
0\leq k\leq d
. In other words,
\mathfrak{X}
is Schurian.
∎

As we have mentioned before, thin association schemes are Schurian

Hypothesis 1

Let
\mathfrak{X}=(\Omega,\mathcal{O})
, where
\mathcal{O}=\{O_{0},O_{1},\ldots,O_{d}\}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{i}=\operatorname{Cay}(G,\Delta_{i}(v))\cong(\Omega,O_{i})

for

The centralizer algebras of thin association schemes are algebraically straightforward as shown below.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a thin association scheme as in Hypothesis
1
. Then,
\tilde{T}_{v}=\operatorname{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in\Omega
. Since
G
acts regularly on

, we know that

is trivial. Therefore,

has
|\Omega|
orbits on

\displaystyle\dim\tilde{T}_{v}=|\Omega|^{2}.

Therefore, we have
\tilde{T}_{v}=\operatorname{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})
.
∎

We can also show that the subspace

coincides with

.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a thin association scheme as in Hypothesis
1
. Then,
{T}_{v}^{0}=\operatorname{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

Let

. Then, we have

\displaystyle p_{ij}^{k}

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{g\in G:(1,g)\in O_{i}\mbox{ and }(g,u_{k})\in O_{j}\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{g\in G:g\in\Delta_{i}(v)\mbox{ and }u_{k}g^{-1}\in\Delta_{j}(v)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\{g\in\Delta_{i}(v):u_{k}g^{-1}=u_{j}\right\}\right|.

Hence,

\displaystyle p_{ij}^{k}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }u_{k}u_{i}^{-1}=u_{j}\\

Proof.

(b)

7.1.
Examples of non-triply transitive quasi-thin schemes

Consider the group

G=\operatorname{Alt}(5)=\left\langle a,b|a^{5}=b^{2}=(ab)^{3}=1\right\rangle

and its subgroup
H=\langle b\rangle
. Since
G
is simple, any action of
G
on cosets of any of its subgroups by right multiplication is faithful, so must be a transitive permuation group. Moreover, there is a unique conjugacy class of subgroups of order
2
in
G
, so all actions of
G
on cosets of a subgroup of order
2
are permutation equivalent to
G
acting on cosets of
H
.

If
g\in G
has a fixed point in this action, then
g
is conjugate in
H
. Therefore, the only permutations in
G
fixing a coset of
H
must be the identity or an involution. Moreover, the number of fixed cosets is equal to either
[G:H]=30
or the number of orbits of size
1
of
H
(equivalently, the number of cosets fixed by
b
). If
(Hx)b=Hx
, then we have
xbx^{-1}\in H
. In other words, if
xH
is fixed by
b
, then
x\in\operatorname{N}_{G}(\langle b\rangle)
. It is not hard to see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{N}_{G}(\langle b\rangle)=\operatorname{C}_{G}(\langle b\rangle)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Therefore,
b
fixes two cosets of
H
. A direct consequence of this fact is that the rank of
G
acting on
G/H
is
1+1+\tfrac{28}{2}=16
, and

\displaystyle\dim\tilde{T}_{v}=2\times 16+14\times 30=452.

As the rank of
G
acting on
G/H
is
1+1+14=16
, we may assume that the orbits of
H
are
\Delta_{1}=\{H\}
,
\Delta_{2}=\{Hx_{2}\}
,
\Delta_{3}=\{Hx_{3},Hy_{3}\}
,
\ldots
, and
\Delta_{16}=\{Hx_{16},Hx_{16}\}
.

Since the scheme is quasi-thin, we know that
p_{ij}^{k}\leq 2
, for all
0\leq i,j,k\leq d
. There exists a unique pair
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{k})
where
0\leq\mathbf{i},\mathbf{k}\leq d
and
|\Delta_{\mathbf{i}}|=|\Delta_{\mathbf{k}}|=2
such that
p_{\mathbf{i}j}^{\mathbf{k}}=p_{\mathbf{k}j}^{\mathbf{i}}=2
, and so

\displaystyle\left|\left\{j:p_{\mathbf{i}j}^{\mathbf{k}}\neq 0\right\}\right|=\left|\left\{j:p_{\mathbf{k}j}^{\mathbf{i}}\neq 0\right\}\right|=1.

Recall that the matrices
(E_{i,v}^{*})_{i}

7.2.
Wedderburn decomposition and irreducible
T
-modules

We shall consider the following hypothesis for convenience.

Hypothesis 3
.

Consider a Schurian quasi-thin association scheme
\mathfrak{X}=(\Omega,\mathcal{O})
where
\mathcal{O}=\{O_{0},O_{1},\ldots,O_{d}\}
, and
O_{0}=\{(v,v):v\in\Omega\}
. For any
1\leq i\leq d
, we let
\Gamma_{i}
be the digraph
(\Omega,O_{i})
. Let
G=\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{X})
. Fix
v\in\Omega
, and denote the suborbit of
G_{v}
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(v)=\left\{u\in\Omega:(v,u)\in O_{i}\right\}

for
0\leq i\leq d
. The stabilizer
G_{v}
is a subgroup generated by an involution
\varphi
. Let
t
be the number of fixed points of
\varphi
and let
s=\tfrac{|\Omega|-t}{2}
.

Recall that the Terwilliger algebra is a subalgebra of
\operatorname{M}_{\Omega}(\mathbb{C})
that is closed under conjugate transpose, thus is semisimple. By the Wedderburn-Artin theorem, the Terwilliger algebra is a direct sum of matrix algebras over
\mathbb{C}
, known as its Wedderburn components. In this section, we determine the Wedderburn-Artin decomposition of Terwilliger algebras of triply-transitive and quasi-thin association schemes. Let
\mathfrak{X}
be as defined in Hypothesis
3
and assume that
\mathfrak{X}
is triply transitive.

Recall that
T^{0}=T=\tilde{T}
. As
\tilde{T}=\operatorname{End}_{G_{v}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega}\right)
, the Wedderburn components can be determined using the action of
G_{v}
on
\Omega
. Let
\rho
be the permutation character of
G_{v}
in its action on
\Omega
. As
G_{v}=\langle\varphi\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, there are two irreducible characters of
G_{v}
, which we denote by
\mathbf{1}
(the trivial character of
G_{v}
) and
\pi
(the unique non-trivial character). As
\rho
is a character of
G_{v}
, we must have

\displaystyle\rho=\langle\rho,\mathbf{1}\rangle\mathbf{1}+\langle\rho,\pi\rangle\pi.

Denote by
\operatorname{fix}(g)
the number of elements of
\Omega
fixed by
g
, for
g\in G
. Now, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\rho,\mathbf{1}\right\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{g\in G_{v}}\operatorname{fix}(g),

which by the orbit-counting lemma is the number of orbits of
G_{v}
acting on
\Omega
. Consequently,
\langle\rho,\mathbf{1}\rangle=t+s
. Since
\rho(g)
is the number of fixed points of

\displaystyle t=\rho(\varphi)=\langle\rho,\mathbf{1}\rangle\mathbf{1}(\varphi)+\langle\rho,\pi\rangle\pi(\varphi)=(s+t)-\langle\rho,\pi\rangle.

Hence, we have
\langle\rho,\pi\rangle=s
. From this, we can see that

(5)

\displaystyle T\cong\operatorname{M}_{s+t}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\operatorname{M}_{s}(\mathbb{C}).

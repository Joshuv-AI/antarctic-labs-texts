Comparison of Routine PAP, Modified Ultrafast PAP (MUFP), and REAP Staining Techniques for Oral Cytosmears
Authors: Swatantra Patel, Himanta Ghritlahare, Rashmi Christin Kerketta, Revati Raghunath Shinde, Shweta Joshi, Shubhangi Durgakumar Mishra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Routine Papanicolaou (PAP) stain is a commonly adopted cytological method of diagnosing lesions of the oral piece, but it suffers drawbacks on the issue of time, cost, and reagents consumed. Faster and cheaper alternatives have been suggested, which are modified ultrafast PAP (MUFP) and rapid economic acetic Papanicolaou (REAP).Purpose:The aim of the study was to evaluate the quality of staining, staining efficiency, and cost-effects of PAP, MUFP, and REAP staining techniques in the oral cytology and to know which one offers the best overall diagnostic advantage.Materials and Methods:The experiments were carried out on 30 volunteers; none had any oral pathology or a history of smoking. Each volunteer gave three smears. There was PAP-stained, MUFP-stained, and REAP-stained one. Cell morphology and staining in general were assessed by three independent observers. It was measured in time and cost. Statistical tests (Chi-square) were carried out to identify statistical differences.Results:Most observers found the quality of cell morphology stains to be unchanged among three approaches, showing no statistically significant difference. Nonetheless, MUFP was much better in overall quality of staining and proved to be the most time- and cost-effective one.Conclusion:MUFP and REAP stain with similar or even higher quality than PAP conventional and will be better suited in cases where there is an urgency of staining and where economy staining is necessary in a clinical environment.

INTRODUCTION

Exfoliative cytology of the oral cavity is an easy, noninvasive, and economical way of early detection of malignancies and precancerous lesions. The Papanicolaou (PAP) stain is the gold standard technique of staining methods, which is used to clearly show nuclear and cytoplasmic dyes because it is clear.[123] The traditional PAP staining, however, has a cumbersome protocol (takes about 20 minutes), uses costly reagents, such as ethanol and xylene, and requires adequate laboratory facilities. This restricts its use as a routine screening program at the chair side or in the field in low-resource locations.[4]

To curb such obstacles, some altered staining procedures have been designed. Dighe et al.[3] proposed rapid economic acetic Papanicolaou (REAP) technique, in which ethanol fixative and dehydrating solution is substituted by one percent acetic acid, lowering the cost and time of staining.[5] Staining is further simplified due to utilization of locally available and nontoxic alternatives (Harris hematoxylin and isopropyl alcohol[6]) in the modified ultrafast PAP (MUFP) technique as suggested by Yang and Alvarez based on the ultrafast PAP technique. It is argued that such methods help to maintain the diagnostic integrity of the smear in combination with greatly increasing the efficiency of operations. This paper will compare the three (PAP, MUFP, and REAP) methods in relation to cytomorphological clarity of results, intensity of the stain, and the time and cost involved in carrying out the method and hence present the most appropriate method that should be used by a myriad of persons to apply in oral cytopathology.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Thirty healthy individuals were used as volunteers, and three oral smears apiece were obtained. The smears were randomly allocated into three staining methods, which included conventional PAP, MUFP, or REAP. MUFP staining involved the use of Harris hematoxylin and isopropyl alcohol; ethanol was replaced with one percent acetic acid and hematoxylin had to be preheated in REAP staining. The assessment of optimal or suboptimal cytoplasmic and nuclear stain in each stained slide was completed separately by three trained observers. The amount of time taken on a single smear and the cost per technique were noted. Chi-square significance probability was used (P < 0.05) as a statistical analysis method to draw data.

DISCUSSION

This research paper discussed the comparison of three stain methods, such as PAP, MUFP, and REAP, as far as their application in oral cytology is concerned. Although standard PAP is the standard against which all methods are compared, MUFP and REAP have the practical benefits.[7] The observation ratings showed that there was no significant variation in cell morphology between the three methods, and this shows that both the modified methods retain diagnostic integrity. But when it comes to the quality of staining in general, MUFP had a significant advantage over PAP and REAP as well. It was explained by improved nuclear definition and cytoplasmic clarity in MUFP smears.[8]

Chemical advances that constitute REAP and MUFP have feasible clinical advantages. REAP uses acetic acid instead of ethanol, and thus, it does not require regulation and is cheaper. Hematoxylin that has been preheated causes the nuclear staining to improve, and since there is no differentiation undertaken with acid, chromatin is preserved.[9] The formation of ethyl acetate during staining assists in the fixation of cytoplasm color. MUFP takes advantage of readily available Harris hematoxylin and eliminates fixing, reducing the duration of staining to approximately 2 minutes. This allows MUFP to be used to make a quick cytological diagnosis either in an intraoperative or a point-of-care environment. MUFP is effective like MFP, though its overall staining scores and quick processing score it as advantageous.[10]

CONCLUSION

Both the MUFP and REAP methods are relatively inexpensive and time-saving in comparison with the traditional PAP stain in the oral cytosmears. Although each of the three methods gave approximately the same cytological detail, MUFP was superior in overall staining performance and in other practical aspects of its use, indicating that it might become more widely used in clinical practice (although more cost-effectively) where resources are limited or high-throughput is desirable.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to obtain improved
L^{q}
-norm estimates for eigenfunctions on an arithmetic hyperbolic surface for the critical exponent
q=6
, which in turn also yield improved estimates for all
q>2
by interpolation.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary,

\Delta_{g}
be the
Laplace-Beltrami operator associated with the metric
g
on
M
. We let
0=\lambda_{0}^{2}<\lambda^{2}_{1}\leq\lambda^{2}_{2}\leq\cdots
denote the
eigenvalues labeled with respect to the multiplicity of

{-\Delta_{g}}

and
e_{\lambda_{j}}
the associated
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions, that is,

(1.1)

(\Delta_{g}+\lambda^{2}_{j})e_{\lambda_{j}}=0,\quad\text{and }\quad\int_{M}|e_{\lambda_{j}}(x)|^{2}\,dx=1.

In Sogge
[
34
]
(see also Avakumović
[
1
]
and Levitan
[
28
]
when
p=\infty
), the universal bound for
q\geq 2

(1.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{\delta(q,n)}

was obtained. Here
\delta(q,n)
is given by

\delta(q,n)=\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{4}-\frac{n-1}{2q}\;&\text{ for }\;2\leq q\leq q_{c},\\
\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{q}\;\;\;\;&\text{ for }\;q_{c}\leq q\leq\infty,\end{cases}\quad\text{ and }\quad q_{c}=\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}.

The result also applies to quasimodes that are linear combinations of eigenfunctions whose eigenvalues lie in the unit interval
[\lambda,\lambda+1]
.
When
M
is a compact locally symmetric space of higher rank, besides the Laplace-Beltrami operator, one can consider eigenfunctions of the full ring of differential operators on
M
. The general local bound was obtained by Sarnak
[
32
]
(for
q=\infty
) and Marshall
[
31
]
(for any
q\geq 2
with some log losses at the corresponding critical exponents), which extends and improves the bound (
1.2
).

The estimate (
1.2
) is sharp on the standard round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
by taking
e_{\lambda}
to be the zonal functions or the highest weight spherical harmonics, which are point-focusing and geodesic-focusing eigenfunctions, respectively. Under additional geometric assumptions–such as nonpositive sectional curvature or on flat tori–one can improve (
1.2
). See e.g., the work of Bourgain-Demeter
[
13
, Section 2.3]
, as well as Germain-Myerson
[
18
]
, and the reference therein for related results on the torus. On compact manifolds with non-positive sectional curvature,

(\log\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
improvements to (
1.2
) was obtained in Bérard
[
2
]
for
q=\infty
, and by Hassell and Tacy
[
19
]
for
q>q_{c}=\tfrac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
. The same improvement also holds, more generally, for spectral projection operators onto the shorter interval
[\lambda,\lambda+(\log\lambda)^{-1}]
. These results were further generalized under weaker dynamical assumptions in the recent work of Canzani-Galkowski
[
15
]
.

In the other direction, for
q\leq q_{c}
similar improvements were obtained in Huang–Sogge
[
23
]
. More generally, they proved sharp quasimode estimates for logarithmic quasimodes, which were also used to characterize compact manifolds with constant curvature. See also Blair and Sogge
[
6
,
7
]
and Blair, Huang and Sogge
[
3
]
for related earlier work in this direction.

1.1.
L^{q}
-norm bounds for Hecke-Maass forms

The main result of the paper concerns improvements of (
1.2
) for arithmetic eigenfunctions on hyperbolic surfaces. We let
\mathbb{H}
be the upper half plane and
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}
an arithmetic hyperbolic surface. Here
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
is taken to be the norm one units of a maximal order of an
indefinite quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
(see Section
3.1
for the precise definitions) so that
X
is compact. Let
\psi
be an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke–Maass form on
X
. Namely,
\int_{X}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx=1
and
\psi
is a joint eigenfunction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g}
and Hecke operators on
X
. Let
\lambda
be the spectral parameter of
\psi
, so that
\Delta\psi+(\frac{1}{4}+\lambda^{2})\psi=0
. As we are considering large-eigenvalue asymptotics, we will also assume that
\lambda
is real and
\lambda\gg 1
. Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
25
]
made a Lindelöf hypothesis type conjecture that
\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}
, which is strong and widely open. The conjecture implies that
\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}
for any
q>2
.

For these
X
and
\psi
, the first power-saving result was obtained by Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
25
]
. They showed that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{5}{12}+\varepsilon},

which is a
\lambda^{\frac{1}{12}}
improvement over the uniform bound (
1.2
) for
n=2
and
q=\infty
. In two-dimensional cases, the critical exponent is
q_{c}=6
, by interpolation, (
1.3
) implies an improvement for
\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(X)}
for all
q>6
. Their proof uses the technique known as arithmetic amplification. This approach has since been used by many authors to bound
L^{\infty}
-norms of Hecke-Maass forms on other groups. For instance, see
[
11
,
29
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
8
]
for results in the spectral aspect.

For
q<6
, Marshall
[
30
]
studied the related
L^{2}
geodesic restriction problem for the Hecke-Maass form
\psi
and obtained a similar
\lambda
-power improvement over the uniform result of Burq, Gérard and Tzvetkov
[
14
]
. We let
\varPi
denote the space of all unit-length geodesic segments on
X
. Marshall obtained the uniform bound

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma\in\varPi}\|\psi|_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{3}{14}+\varepsilon}.

He reduced
L^{2}
-norm bounds for
\psi
along
\gamma\in\varPi
to bounds for various
Fourier coefficients along
\gamma
.
His improvements over the local bounds extend the technique of arithmetic amplification developed by Iwaniec-Sarnak.
Using a result of Blair and Sogge
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, the geodesic restriction bound (
1.4
) implies that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{56}+\varepsilon}.

Moreover, (
1.5
) also implies an improvement over (
1.2
) for all
2<q<6
by interpolation. See
[
21
,
22
]
for the extension of this method to improve bounds for Kakeya-Nikodym norms on other groups of rank one. For the higher rank group
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, (
1.4
) was extended by Marshall to
L^{2}
maximal flat restrictions of Hecke-Maass forms. It would be interesting to see whether this result could be used to prove a power saving for the global
L^{p}
-norms of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
Hecke Maass forms for small
p
as in Blair-Sogge
[
5
]
.

It would be interesting to extend the above results and obtain a power saving over (
1.2
) for the critical exponent
q=6
. A much stronger
L^{4}
-norm bound than (
1.5
) was obtained by Humphries and Khan
[
24
, Theorem 1.4]
:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{3}{152}+\varepsilon}.

They applied Parseval’s identity to
|\psi|^{2}
and used the Watson–Ichino triple product formula to reduce bounds for
\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(X)}
to bounds for certain moments of
L
-functions. By interpolating with the
L^{\infty}
-norm bound (
1.3
) of Iwaniec-Sarnak, Humphries and Khan got

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{6}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{26}{171}+\varepsilon}=\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{5}{342}+\varepsilon}.

On the noncompact quotient
\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
, for a Hecke-Maass cusp form
\psi
whose spectral parameter is
\lambda
, both
L^{\infty}
-norm bound (
1.3
) and
L^{4}
-norm bound (
1.6
) hold as well, so one still has the same
L^{6}
-norm bound (
1.7
). In
[
26
]
, Ki showed an essentially sharp upper bound
\|\psi\|_{L^{4}(\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}
, by using the
Fourier-Whittaker expansions of Hecke-Maass cusp forms. This result justifies Iwaniec-Sarnak’s conjecture for
2<q\leq 4
, and therefore implies a better
L^{6}
-norm bound

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{L^{6}(\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{36}+\varepsilon}.

The main result of the paper is the following sublocal
L^{6}
-norm bound in the
compact
setting, which is weaker than (
1.7
) but is based on a completely different approach.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke–Maass form on
X
with spectral parameter
\lambda\gg 1
. We have

(1.8)

\|\psi\|_{L^{6}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{144}+\varepsilon}.

1.2.
Proof strategy

A main step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact
2
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary. Let
e_{\lambda}
be an
L^{2}
normalized eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{g}
with eigenvalue
\lambda^{2}
, satisfying (
1.1
) for some
\lambda\gg 1
. Set
\theta_{0}=\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
with
0<\delta_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
, and let
A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}
be the pseudo-differential operator defined in (
2.17
). Assume that

(1.9)

\sup_{\nu}\|A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{6}(M)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-a{\delta_{1}}+\varepsilon},

and

(1.10)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta_{2}+\varepsilon}

for some constant
a>0
and
0<\delta_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
. Then

(1.11)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{6}(M)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{2}{3}a{\delta_{1}}+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{5}{3}\delta_{1})}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}(1+\delta_{1}-2\delta_{2})+\varepsilon}.

For each fixed
\nu
, the operator
A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}
is microlocally supported in a
\theta_{0}
-neighborhood of a geodesic segment and is frequency-localized to directions near that geodesic; the precise definition will be given in Section
2.1
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
follows a strategy similar to that of
[
23
]
, relying on bilinear techniques from harmonic analysis together with the improved
L^{\infty}
estimate (
1.10
). We will prove Theorem
1.2
in Section
2
.
The idea of using improved microlocal
L^{6}
-norm estimate to derive improved global
L^{6}
-norm estimate is inspired by the recent work
[
3
]
.

Note that for a Hecke–Maass form
\psi
on
X
, as in Theorem
1.1
, the bound (
1.10
) holds with
\delta_{2}=\tfrac{1}{12}
by Iwaniec-Sarnak’s
L^{\infty}
-norm bound (
1.3
). If, in addition, (
1.9
) holds for such
X
and
\psi
with
a=\frac{1}{12}
, then Theorem
1.1
follows immediately by choosing
\delta_{1}=\frac{3}{2}\delta_{2}=\tfrac{1}{8}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\psi
be a Hecke–Maass form on
X
with spectral parameter
\lambda\gg 1
. Set
\theta_{0}=\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
with
0<\delta_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
, and let
A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}
be the pseudo-differential operator defined in (
2.17
). We have

\sup_{\nu}\|A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}\psi\|_{L^{6}(X)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{\delta_{1}}{12}+\varepsilon}.

We will prove Theorem
1.3
in Section
3
. This theorem can be viewed as the
L^{6}
-version of Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 3.9 in
[
30
]
, where Marshall used the amplification method to bound the
L^{2}
-mass of
\psi
restricted to a geodesic segment with frequency localized. We give the amplification inequality in Proposition
3.13
by integrating the amplified pretrace formula against a test function under the operator
A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}
. Let
\gamma
be the geodesic segment centered in
A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}
.
Similar to usual amplification arguments, we have a counting problem and an analytic problem. The counting problem is to estimate the number of times a Hecke operator maps
\gamma
back close to itself, which has been studied by Marshall
[
30
, Section 3.2]
. Using the microlocal support of
A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}
, we can reduce the analytic problem to several known results from
[
34
,
30
,
31
]
, which will be proved in Proposition
3.14
.

1.3.
Generalizations

In Theorem
1.1
, the arithmetic assumption that
\psi
is a Hecke eigenfunction is only used to invoke the bound Iwaniec-Sarnak
[
25
]
and to prove Theorem
1.3
, both of which rely on arithmetic amplification. Hence, the maximal order in the theorem can be replaced with an Eichler order more generally, as in
[
25
]
. Moreover, we use only unramified Hecke operators, and we may assume
\psi
to be an eigenfunction under even fewer Hecke operators as long as the amplification argument can be adapted.

The strategy developed in this paper can also be generalized to study the
L^{q_{c}}
-norm problems for Hecke-Maass forms on compact locally symmetric spaces of rank one, as long as the corresponding
L^{\infty}
-norm problem and microlocal
L^{q_{c}}
Kakeya-Nikodym problem can be solved by the arithmetic amplification technique; we will explore this direction in future work.
Theorem
1.2
is expected to be generalized to Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions on compact Riemannian manifolds of any dimension, which allows us to control the
L^{q_{c}}
-norms by the
L^{\infty}
-norms and the microlocal Kakeya-Nikodym norms.
For example, when the manifold is taken to be a compact arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold (the critical exponent is
q_{c}=4
), the corresponding
L^{\infty}
-norm problem for Hecke-Maass forms is expected to be solved (see
[
9
]
in the cases of noncompact arithmetic
hyperbolic 3-manifolds with cusps). The corresponding microlocal
L^{4}
Kakeya-Nikodym estimate should be established in a similar way as Theorem
1.3
, by modifying the proof for the usual
L^{2}
Kakeya-Nikodym estimate in
[
21
]
.

1.4.
Notation

Throughout the paper, the notation
A\lesssim B
means that there is a positive constant
C
such that
|A|\leq CB
, and
A\sim B
means that
A\lesssim B\lesssim A
. We also use
A=O(B)
to mean
A\lesssim B
. The notation
A\gg B
means there is a sufficiently large positive constant
C
such that
A\geq CB
, and similarly for
A\ll B
.

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
of
f
in this paper is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}dx.

Here
x=(x_{i}),\xi=(\xi_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\langle x,\xi\rangle=\sum_{i}x_{i}\xi_{i}
.
If
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Schwartz function, the Fourier inversion formula reads

f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\hat{f}(\xi)e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}d\xi.

We say a symbol
p(x,\xi)
is in the class
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}
if

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}p(x,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\alpha|+\delta|\beta|},

where
\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
is the Japanese bracket.
The pseudo-differential operator
p(x,D)
is defined by the integral

p(x,D)f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int p(x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}d\xi=(2\pi)^{-n}\iint p(x,\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}d\xi dy.

We say
P
is the pseudo-differential operator with the compound symbol
p(x,y,\xi)
if

Pf(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\iint p(x,y,\xi)f(y)e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}d\xi dy.

2.
Reduction to a microlocal
L^{6}
Kakeya-Nikodym estimate

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
. The arguments used here are similar to those in section 2.2 of
[
23
]
. Let
(M,g)
and
e_{\lambda}
be as in the theorem.
In order to use the local harmonic analysis tools related to oscillatory integrals, it is convenient to use the
smooth spectral projection operators
of the form

(2.1)

\sigma_{\lambda}=\rho(\lambda-P),\quad P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}},

where

(2.2)

\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}),\,\,\rho(0)=1\,\,\,\text{and }\,\,\text{supp }\hat{\rho}\subset\delta\cdot[1-\delta_{0},1+\delta_{0}]=[\delta-\delta_{0}\delta,\delta+\delta_{0}\delta].

Here
\delta_{0},\delta
are some positive constants. Note that the condition
\rho(0)=1
implies
\sigma_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}=\rho(0)e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda}
.
Moreover, by
[
35
, Lemma 5.1.3]
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-1}\int^{2\delta}_{-2\delta}\hat{\rho}(t)e^{it\lambda}e^{-itP}f\,dt

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1/2}\int_{M}e^{i\lambda d_{g}(x,y)}a(x,y,\lambda)f(y)dy+R_{\lambda}f(x)

where

\|R_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)}\quad\forall N=1,2,3..

and
a\in C^{\infty}
has the property that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a(x,y,\lambda)=0\qquad\text{ if }\,\,d_{g}(x,y)\notin(\delta/2,2\delta),\\
\text{ and }\;&\partial_{x,y}^{\gamma}a(x,y,\lambda)\lesssim_{\gamma}1\qquad\text{for any multi-index }\gamma.\end{split}

Here we shall need that
\delta
is smaller than the injectivity radius of
M
and it also must be
chosen small enough so that the phase function
d_{g}(x,y)
satisfies the Carelson-Sjölin condition when
d_{g}(x,y)\in(\delta/2,2\delta)
,
with
d_{g}(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
denoting the Riemannian distance function. The additional constant
\delta_{0}
is required to be sufficiently small to guarantee that the bilinear oscillatory integral estimates used in
[
23
]
are valid.

Let us further localize using microlocal cutoffs.
Let
\{\phi_{i}(x,\xi)\}_{j=1}^{N_{0}}
be a finite partition of unity of
S^{*}M
, where each
\phi_{i}
is supported in a sufficiently small neighborhood of some
(x_{i},\xi_{i})\in S^{*}M
. Here
N_{0}
is a large fixed constant independent of
\lambda
. Fix a function

\beta\in C^{\infty}_{0}((1/2,2)),\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\beta(\tau)=1\,\,\,\text{for }\,\tau\,\,\text{near }\,\,1,\,\,

Define

(2.5)

B_{i,\lambda}(x,\xi)=\phi_{i}(x,\xi/p(x,\xi))\beta(p(x,\xi)/\lambda)

where
p(x,\xi)=|\xi|_{g}=\sqrt{g^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}
denotes the principal symbol of
P=\sqrt{-\Delta_{g}}
. We may assume the
x
-support of each symbol
\phi_{i}(x,\xi)
is contained in a local coordinate chart
\Omega_{i}
, and let

B_{i,\lambda}(x,D)
be the pseudo-differential operator defined in this chart with symbol
B_{i,\lambda}(x,\xi)
(see e.g., Chapter 3.3 in
[
35
]
for the definition of pseudo-differential operators on compact manifolds). Let
{B}_{i,\lambda}(x,y)
denote the Schwartz kernel of
B_{i,\lambda}(x,D)
. We may further assume (after inserting a cutoff in the
y
variable) that

B_{i,\lambda}(x,y)=B_{i,\lambda}(x,y)\,\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(y),

where
\widetilde{\phi}_{i}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})
satisfies
\widetilde{\phi}_{i}\equiv 1

in a neighborhood of the
x
–support of
\phi_{i}(x,\xi)
.
Then,
by repeated integration by parts, we have

{B}_{i,\lambda}(x,y)=O_{N}\bigl(\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda|x-y|)^{-N}\bigr)\qquad\text{ for any }N>0.

Consequently, by Young’s inequality,

B_{i,\lambda}(x,D)

are uniformly bounded on
L^{p}(M)
, i.e.,

(2.6)

\|B_{i,\lambda}(x,D)\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}=O(1)\quad\text{for }\,\,1\leq p\leq\infty.

Since
p(x,\nabla_{x}d_{g}(x,y))=1
(i.e. the eikonal equation for the distance function), by (
2.3
) and an integration by parts argument, we have

(2.7)

\,\,\bigl\|\sigma_{\lambda}-\sum_{i=1}^{N_{0}}B_{i,\lambda}(x,D)\circ\sigma_{\lambda}\bigr\|_{L^{2}\rightarrow L^{q}}=O_{N}(\lambda^{-N}),\qquad\text{ for any }N>0\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,q\geq 2.

Let
B=B_{i,\lambda}
for some fixed
i
, and define

\widetilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}=B\circ\sigma_{\lambda},

By (
2.2
), we have
\sigma_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}=e_{\lambda}
, so by (
2.7
), in order to prove (
1.11
) it suffices to show that
for
\lambda\gg 1
we have

(2.8)

\|\widetilde{\sigma}_{\lambda}e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{6}(M)}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\left(\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}-\frac{2}{3}a{\delta_{1}}+\varepsilon}+\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}(-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{5}{3}\delta_{1})}+\lambda^{\frac{1}{6}(1+\delta_{1}-2\delta_{2}+\varepsilon)}\right)\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}

provided that (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) hold.

2.1.
Microlocal decomposition

To prove (
2.8
), we introduce a further microlocal decomposition, which
involves localizing in
\theta>\lambda^{-1/2}
neighborhoods of geodesics in a fixed coordinate chart,
as in the recent work
[
23
]
. This decomposition enables us to apply the bilinear estimate established in
[
23
]
, whose proof builds on earlier bilinear oscillatory integral estimates developed in
[
27
]
and
[
36
]
. Below we describe the details of the decomposition used in
[
23
]
in dimension 2.

First recall that the symbol
B(x,\xi)
of
B
in (
2.5
) is supported in a small
conic neighborhood of some
(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in S^{*}M
with
p(x,\xi)\in(\lambda/2,2\lambda)
. We may assume that its symbol has
small enough support so that we may work in a coordinate chart
\Omega
for
M
, and that

(2.9)

\displaystyle x_{0}=0,\quad\xi_{0}=(0,1),\quad g_{jk}(0)=\delta^{j}_{k}

in the local coordinates. So we shall assume that
B(x,\xi)=0
when
x
is outside a small relatively compact neighborhood of the origin or
\xi
is outside of a small conic neighborhood of
(0,1)

3.

In this section, we prove Theorem

for a Hecke–Maass form

3.1.
Arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces

We first recall the constructions of compact congruence arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces from quaternion division algebras over
\mathbb{Q}
.

3.1.1.
Quaternion algebras

Let
A=\left(\frac{a,b}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)
be a quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
are squarefree and
a>0
. We choose a basis
1,\omega,\Omega,\omega\Omega
for
A
over
\mathbb{Q}
that satisfies
\omega^{2}=a
,
\Omega^{2}=b
and
\omega\Omega+\Omega\omega=0
. We denote the reduced norm and trace on
A
by
\mathrm{nrd}(\alpha)=\alpha\overline{\alpha}
and
\mathrm{trd}(\alpha)=\alpha+\overline{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\mapstochar\rightarrow\overline{\alpha}
is the standard involution on
A
. We let
R
be a maximal order in
A
. For any integer
m\geq 1
, we let

R(m)=\{\alpha\in R\mid\mathrm{nrd}(\alpha)=m\}.

The group
R(1)
of elements of reduced norm 1 in

acts on

by multiplication on the left, and
R(1)\backslash R(m)
is finite (see
[
16
]
). We fix an embedding
\iota:A\rightarrow M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a}))
, the 2-by-2 matrices with entries in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a})

\iota(\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\xi}&\eta\\
b\overline{\eta}&\xi\end{pmatrix},

where
\alpha=x_{0}+x_{1}\omega+(x_{2}+x_{3}\omega)\Omega=\xi+\eta\Omega.
Let
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
be the upper half plane. The Lie group
\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})
, consisting of 2-by-2 real matrices with positive determinant, acts on
\mathbb{H}
via the fractional linear transformation:

\displaystyle g\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\qquad\text{ where }g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})\text{ and }z\in\mathbb{H}.

We define the lattice
\Gamma=\iota(R(1))\subset\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, which is cocompact as we assumed

to be a division algebra. We let

be the corresponding compact arithmetic hyperbolic surface, equipped with the standard volume form
y^{-2}dxdy
for

3.1.2.
Hecke operators

We define the Hecke operators

There is a positive integer

has the following properties:

\displaystyle T_{n}=T_{n}^{*},\qquad\text{ that is, }T_{n}\text{ is self-adjoint},

(3.1)

\displaystyle T_{m}T_{n}=T_{n}T_{m}=\sum_{d|(m,n)}T_{mn/d^{2}}.

3.1.3.
Lie groups

We let

We denote the Lie algebra of

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|:\begin{pmatrix}X_{1}&X_{2}\\

This norm defines a left-invariant metric on

\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\iint_{T^{*}\widetilde{\Omega}}e^{i\langle x-w,\xi\rangle}A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}(x,\xi)e^{i\langle w-z,\eta\rangle}\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}-1}\eta_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\widetilde{\rho}(\eta_{2}/\lambda)\\
\cdot e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))}\chi(w_{1}/2)\chi(w_{2}/2)\chi(z_{1})\chi(z_{2})a(\kappa(z),\kappa(y),\lambda)dwd\xi dzd\eta,

for
C_{1}
sufficiently large.
Next, by integrating by parts in
\xi_{1}
and then in
\eta_{1}
(we may integrate by parts in
\xi_{1}
again to make the widths of the cutoffs for
z_{1},w_{1}
be the same), and using (

) and

, we have, modulo

terms,

(3.12)

\displaystyle A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}(\kappa(x),\kappa(y))

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\iint_{T^{*}\widetilde{\Omega}}e^{i\langle x-w,\xi\rangle}A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}(x,\xi)e^{i\langle w-z,\eta\rangle}\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}-1}\eta_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\widetilde{\rho}(\eta_{2}/\lambda)e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))}

\displaystyle\cdot\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}w_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi(w_{2}/2)\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}z_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi(z_{2})a(\kappa(z),\kappa(y),\lambda)dwd\xi dzd\eta,

for
C_{1}
sufficiently large.

To introduce the desired cutoff in
\eta_{2}
, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Given
z,y\in\widetilde{\Omega}
so that
a(\kappa(z),\kappa(y),\lambda)\neq 0
, then

|\partial_{z_{1}}d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))|\sim|z_{1}-y_{1}|.

If, furthermore,
z,y
satisfy
|z_{1}|,|y_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
, then

\big|\partial_{z_{2}}d_{g}(z,y)\big|=1+O(\lambda_{1}^{-2\delta_{1}}).

Proof.

Recall the distance function on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}
is given by

(3.13)

\cosh\Big(d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))\Big)=1+\frac{(z_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(e^{z_{2}}-e^{y_{2}})^{2}}{2e^{z_{2}}e^{y_{2}}}.

Taking
\partial_{z_{1}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sinh\Big(d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))\Big)\partial_{z_{1}}d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))=\frac{z_{1}-y_{1}}{e^{z_{2}}e^{y_{2}}}.

The first result follows by using
d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))\sim 1
when
a(\kappa(z),\kappa(y),\lambda)\neq 0
.

Now we apply
\partial_{z_{2}}
to (
3.13
) to get, with
|z_{1}|,|y_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
,

\displaystyle\sinh\Big(d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))\Big)\partial_{z_{2}}d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))=\sinh(z_{2}-y_{2})+O(\lambda^{-2\delta_{1}}).

Using
|z_{1}|,|y_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
and
d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))\sim 1
, it follows from (
3.13
) that
|z_{2}-y_{2}|\sim 1
and

\sinh(d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y)))=\sinh(|z_{2}-y_{2}|)(1+O(\lambda^{-2\delta_{1}})),

which completes the proof.
∎

Now we continue the proof of Lemma
3.8
.
If we further integrate (
3.12
) by parts in
z_{1}
, using

from (

, and Lemma

terms,
A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}(\kappa(x),\kappa(y))
is

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\iint_{T^{*}\widetilde{\Omega}}e^{i\langle x-w,\xi\rangle}A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}(x,\xi)e^{i\langle w-z,\eta\rangle}\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}-1}\eta_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\widetilde{\rho}(\eta_{2}/\lambda)e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))}

\displaystyle\cdot\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}w_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi(w_{2}/2)\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}z_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}y_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi(z_{2})a(\kappa(z),\kappa(y),\lambda)dw\xi dzd\eta,

for
C_{1}
sufficiently large.
Now integrating by parts in
z_{2}
from (
3.14
), and using Lemma
3.9
and
0<\delta_{1}<\frac{1}{2}
, we have, modulo
O(\lambda^{-N})
terms,
A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}(\kappa(x),\kappa(y))
is

(3.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\iint_{T^{*}\widetilde{\Omega}}e^{i\langle x-w,\xi\rangle}A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}(x,\xi)e^{i\langle w-z,\eta\rangle}\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}-1}\eta_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{2\delta_{1}-1}(\eta_{2}-\lambda)}{C_{1}}\right)e^{i\lambda d_{g}(\kappa(z),\kappa(y))}

\displaystyle\cdot\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}w_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi(w_{2}/2)\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}z_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)\chi(z_{2})\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}y_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)a(\kappa(z),\kappa(y),\lambda)dw\xi dzd\eta,

for
C_{1}
sufficiently large. Here we have used that

\widetilde{\rho}(\eta_{2}/\lambda)\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{2\delta_{1}-1}(\eta_{2}-\lambda)}{C_{1}}\right)=\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{2\delta_{1}-1}(\eta_{2}-\lambda)}{C_{1}}\right)

when
\lambda\gg 1
is sufficiently large, which may depend on
C_{1}
. Note that (
3.15
) is equal to
A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\mathscr{A}\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}(\kappa(x),\kappa(y))\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}y_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)
modulo
O(\lambda^{-N})
. By another integration by part argument in

, the
\chi\left(\frac{\lambda^{\delta_{1}}y_{1}}{C_{1}}\right)

∎

From Lemma

.

Lemma 3.10
.

We have
\|A_{\nu}^{\theta_{0}}\psi\|_{L^{q}(X)}\lesssim\|\mathscr{A}\psi\|_{L^{q}(X)}+O_{N}(\lambda^{-N})
for any

and for any

.

Proof.

By (

) and the fact

we have

\|A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\psi\|_{L^{q}}=\|A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}\psi\|_{L^{q}}\leq\|A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\mathscr{A}\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}\psi\|_{L^{q}}+O(\lambda^{-N})\leq\|A^{\theta_{0}}_{\nu}\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}\|\mathscr{A}\psi\|_{L^{q}}+O(\lambda^{-N}),

which completes the proof by applying the bound

due to (

).
∎

We shall use the following useful facts about
\mathscr{A}
. We write the

pairing on

as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}
. We define another compound symbol
\mathscr{A}^{*}(x,y,\xi)=e^{x_{2}-y_{2}}\mathscr{A}(y,x,\xi)
, with the corresponding pseudo-differential operator
\mathscr{A}^{*}
defined in a similar way as (

).

Lemma 3.11
.

\mathscr{A}^{*}
is the adjoint of
\mathscr{A}
, i.e.
\langle\mathscr{A}f,g\rangle_{X}=\langle f,\mathscr{A}^{*}g\rangle_{X}
for any

.

Proof.

This follows from the facts that
\mathscr{A}f
is supported in
\Omega
, and the volume form for

is
e^{-x_{2}}dx_{1}dx_{2}
under our coordinates.
∎

Lemma 3.12
.

We have
\|\mathscr{A}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim 1
and
\|\mathscr{A}^{*}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}}\lesssim 1
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{A}(x,y)
denote the Schwartz kernel of
\mathscr{A}
, with

. Then,
by (

) and repeated integration by parts, we have

\mathscr{A}(x,y)=O\bigl(\lambda^{2-3\delta_{1}}(1+\lambda^{1-\delta_{1}}|x_{1}-y_{1}|)^{-N}(1+\lambda^{1-2\delta_{1}}|x_{2}-y_{2}|)^{-N})\bigr),

.
Consequently, Lemma

∎

3.3.
Amplification inequality

We fix a real-valued function
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of Paley-Wiener type that is nonnegative and satisfies
h(0)=1
. Define
h_{\lambda}^{0}
by
h_{\lambda}^{0}(s)=h(s-\lambda)+h(-s-\lambda)
, and let
k_{\lambda}^{0}
be the
K
-bi-invariant function on
\mathbb{H}
with Harish-Chandra transform
h_{\lambda}^{0}
(see e.g.
[
20
, Chap. 4]
). The Paley–Wiener theorem of Gangolli
[
17
]
implies that
k_{\lambda}^{0}
is of compact support that may be chosen arbitrarily small. Define
k_{\lambda}=k_{\lambda}^{0}*k_{\lambda}^{0}
, which has Harish-Chandra transform
h_{\lambda}=(h_{\lambda}^{0})^{2}
. We mention that the operator defined by the kernel

K_{\lambda}(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}k_{\lambda}(x^{-1}\gamma y),\qquad\text{ for }x,y\in X,

plays a similar role as the spectral projection
\sigma_{\lambda_{1}}
used in Section
2
. However, this construction adapts to the amplification method more easily.

Let
\{\psi_{j}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(X)
consisting of Hecke–Maass
forms with
\psi\in\{\psi_{j}\}
. Let
\lambda_{j}
be the spectral parameter of
\psi_{j}
. Then
K_{\lambda}(x,y)
has the
spectral expansion
[
33
]
by Selberg

K_{\lambda}(x,y)=\sum_{j}h_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})\psi_{j}(x)\overline{\psi_{j}(y)},\qquad\text{ for }x,y\in X.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer, and let
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
be complex numbers so that
\alpha_{n}=0
if
(n,q)\neq 1
, both to be chosen later. Define the Hecke operator
\mathcal{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}T_{n}
. Recall that after applying
\mathcal{T}
to the kernal
K(x,y)
we have the following amplified pretrace formula in
[
25
]
:

(3.16)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}h_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})\mathcal{T}\psi_{j}(x)\overline{\mathcal{T}\psi_{j}(y)}=\sum_{m,n\leq N}\alpha_{m}\overline{\alpha}_{n}\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}k_{\lambda}(x^{-1}\gamma y).

Given
g\in G
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(X)
, using the notation from Section
3.2
, we define

I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,g)=\iint_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\overline{\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi}(\kappa(x))\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi(\kappa(y))k_{\lambda}(\kappa(x)^{-1}g\kappa(y))e^{-x_{2}-y_{2}}dxdy.

Here
x=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
dx=dx_{1}dx_{2}
, and the factor
e^{-x_{2}-y_{2}}
is from the hyperbolic volume form.

Proposition 3.13
.

For any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(X)
, we have

\left|\langle\mathscr{A}\mathcal{T}\psi,\phi\rangle_{X}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}|I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)|

Proof.

We first write (
3.16
) with variables
\kappa(x),\kappa(y)\in\Omega
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j}h_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})\mathcal{T}\psi_{j}(\kappa(x))\overline{\mathcal{T}\psi_{j}(\kappa(y))}=\sum_{m,n\leq N}\alpha_{m}\overline{\alpha}_{n}\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}k_{\lambda}(\kappa(x)^{-1}\gamma\kappa(y)).

If we integrate the above identity against
\overline{\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi}\times\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi
with respect to the volume form, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j}h_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})\left|\langle\mathcal{T}\psi_{j},\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi\rangle_{X}\right|^{2}=\sum_{m,n\leq N}\alpha_{m}\overline{\alpha}_{n}\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma).

Here we have used the fact that
\text{supp }\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi\subset\Omega
.
Note that
h_{\lambda}(\lambda)\geq 1
and
h_{\lambda}(\lambda_{j})\geq 0
for all
j
. We may therefore drop all terms on the left-hand side except
\psi
. Finally applying the adjointness between
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{A}^{*}
from Lemma
3.11
completes the proof.
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

To apply the amplification inequality, we shall need the following bounds for
I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(X)
be such that
\|\phi\|_{L^{6/5}(X)}=1
.

(a)

For any
\gamma\in G
,
I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}
.

(b)

Fix
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll\delta_{1}
. If
d(\gamma,e)\lesssim 1
and
d(g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0},A)\geq\lambda^{-\delta_{1}+2\varepsilon_{0}}
, then
I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)\lesssim_{\varepsilon_{0},N}\lambda^{-N}
for any
N>0
.

Proof.

For
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
we let

K_{\lambda}f(x)=\int_{X}K_{\lambda}(x,y)f(y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{H}}k_{\lambda}(x^{-1}y)f(y)dy.

By
[
31
, p.1445 (2.2)]
, one has

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|K_{\lambda}\|_{L^{6/5}\rightarrow L^{6}}\lesssim\lambda^{1/3}\log\lambda.

Note that here
\log\lambda
is removable by applying
[
34
]
instead more carefully. Therefore, by Hölder’s inequality, (
3.17
), and Lemma
3.12
,

|I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)|=\left|\int_{X}K_{\lambda}\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi(\gamma^{-1}x)\overline{\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi(x)}dx\right|\leq\|K_{\lambda}\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\mathscr{A}^{*}\phi\|_{L^{6/5}}\\
\leq\|K_{\lambda}\|_{L^{6/5}\rightarrow L^{6}}\|\mathscr{A}^{*}\|_{L^{6/5}\rightarrow L^{6/5}}^{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{6/5}}^{2}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}.

For part (b), we first expand
I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)
by the intgeral representation for
\mathscr{A}^{*}
as

I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)=\iint_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\iint_{T^{*}\widetilde{\Omega}}e^{i\langle y-v,\eta\rangle-i\langle x-u,\xi\rangle}\mathscr{A}^{*}(x,u,\xi)\mathscr{A}^{*}(y,v,\eta)\overline{\phi(u)}\phi(v)\\
k_{\lambda}(\kappa(x)^{-1}\gamma\kappa(y))e^{-x_{2}-y_{2}}dxdydud\xi dvd\eta.

We consider the integral over
x_{2},y_{2}
in
I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,g)
, that is,

\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\chi(x_{2})\chi(y_{2})e^{i\eta_{2}y_{2}-i\xi_{2}x_{2}}k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{2})n(-x_{1})g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0}n(y_{1})a(y_{2}))dx_{2}dy_{2}.

Here we use the relation
\mathscr{A}^{*}(x,y,\xi)=e^{x_{2}-y_{2}}\mathscr{A}(y,x,\xi)
, and the explicit formulas (
3.9
) for
\mathscr{A}
and (
3.3
) for
\kappa
.
Let
\beta=(0.3r)C_{1}\lambda^{1-2\delta_{1}}\sim\lambda^{1-2\delta_{1}}
and
g=n(-x_{1})g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0}n(y_{1})
.
From the support of
\mathscr{A}^{*}
, we may assume that
|x_{1}|,|y_{1}|\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
and
\xi_{2},\eta_{2}\in[\lambda-\beta,\lambda+\beta]
. Therefore, by applying Hölder’s inequality to the integrals over the rest variables, it suffices to show

(3.18)

\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\chi(x_{2})\chi(y_{2})e^{i\eta_{2}y_{2}-i\xi_{2}x_{2}}k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{2})ga(y_{2}))dx_{2}dy_{2}\lesssim_{N}\lambda^{-N}.

Since
d(g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0},A)\geq\lambda^{-\delta_{1}+2\varepsilon_{0}}
and
x_{1},y_{1}\lesssim\lambda^{-\delta_{1}}
, it may be seen that
d({g},A)\geq\lambda^{-1/2+\varepsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2}
provided
\lambda\gg 1
. Then (
3.18
) follows from the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Given
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<1/2
, suppose that
\lambda^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq\beta\leq\lambda^{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}
, and
\xi_{2},\eta_{2}\in[\lambda-\beta,\lambda+\beta]
. If
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
d(g,e)\lesssim 1
and
d(g,A)\geq\lambda^{-1/2+\varepsilon_{0}}\beta^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}\chi(x_{2})\chi(y_{2})e^{i\eta_{2}y_{2}-i\xi_{2}x_{2}}k_{\lambda}(a(-x_{2})ga(y_{2}))dx_{2}dy_{2}\lesssim_{\varepsilon_{0},N}\lambda^{-N},

for any
N>0
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from a result of Marshall
[
30
, Proposition 6.9]
by the inverse Harish-Chandra transform and the rapid decay of
h_{\lambda}
away from
\lambda
.
Note that in
[
30
]
it is required that
\beta\leq\lambda^{2/3}
. However, this assumption is not necessary, and
\lambda^{2/3}
could be replaced with
\lambda^{1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}
(this is also mentioned in
[
30
, Remark p.466]
). ∎

We will use the amplification method and need an input on the estimation of Hecke returns, which counts how many times the Hecke operators map a geodesic segment close to itself. We define

\displaystyle M(g,n,\kappa)=\#\{\eta\in R(n)\mid d(g^{-1}\eta g,e)\leq 1,d(g^{-1}\eta g,A)\leq\kappa\}.

The following lemma is proved by Marshall
[
30
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.16
.

If
\kappa\leq 1
, we have the bound
M(g,n,\kappa)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}(n/\kappa)^{\varepsilon}(n\sqrt{\kappa}+1)
uniformly in
g
.

Now we recall the construction of an amplifier. Let
N\geq 1
be a large positive integer to be chosen later. If
1<p\leq\sqrt{N}
is a prime so that
(p,q)=1
, (
3.1
) implies the following relation for the Hecke eigenvalues:

\displaystyle\lambda(p)^{2}-\lambda(p^{2})=1.

For such a prime
p
, the above relation implies that if
|\lambda(p)|<1/2
then
|\lambda(p^{2})|>3/4
. We set

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\alpha_{p}=\frac{\lambda(p)}{|\lambda(p)|},\;\alpha_{p^{2}}=0,\qquad&\text{ if }|\lambda(p)|\geq 1/2,\\
\alpha_{p}=0,\;\alpha_{p^{2}}=\frac{\lambda(p^{2})}{|\lambda(p^{2})|},\qquad&\text{ if }|\lambda(p)|<1/2.\end{cases}

We set
\alpha_{n}=0
for all the other
n
. It may be seen that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|=\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<p\leq\sqrt{N}\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}1\lesssim N^{1/2}.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}T_{n}
be our amplifier. Then we have
\mathcal{T}\psi=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)\right)\psi
and the eigenvalue of the amplifier satisfies

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\alpha_{n}\lambda(n)\right|\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<p\leq\sqrt{N}\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}\gtrsim_{\varepsilon}N^{1/2-\varepsilon}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
3.10
, we may bound
\|\mathscr{A}\psi\|_{L^{6}(X)}
instead, and moreover by duality, it suffices to bound
\langle\mathscr{A}\psi,\phi\rangle_{X}
uniformly for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(X)
with
\|\phi\|_{L^{6/5}(X)}=1
.
By Proposition
3.13
, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathscr{A}\mathcal{T}\psi,\phi\rangle_{X}\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}|I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)|.

Fix
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll\delta_{1}
. By assuming the support of
k_{\lambda}
and
\Omega
are sufficiently small, and by Proposition
3.14
, we only need to consider the terms in (
3.21
) with
d(g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0},e)\leq 1
and
d(g_{0}^{-1}\gamma g_{0},A)\leq\lambda^{-\delta_{1}+2\varepsilon_{0}}
. Lemma
3.16
gives

M(g_{0},n,\lambda^{-\delta_{1}+2\varepsilon_{0}})\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon}(n\lambda^{-\delta_{1}/2}+1),

and so by the uniform estimate
|I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)|\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}
from Proposition
3.14
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\sum_{\gamma\in R(mn/d^{2})}\left|I_{\mathscr{A}}(\lambda,\phi,\gamma)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\lambda^{1/3+\varepsilon}M(g_{0},\frac{mn}{d^{2}},\lambda^{-\delta_{1}+2\varepsilon_{0}})+O_{\varepsilon_{0},A}(\lambda^{-A})

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon_{0}+\varepsilon}\sum_{m,n\leq N}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\sum_{d|(m,n)}\left(\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{d}\lambda^{1/3-\delta_{1}/2}+\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}\lambda^{1/3}\right)+O_{\varepsilon_{0},A}(\lambda^{-A}).

As in
[
25
, p.310]
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}\sum_{d|(m,n)}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\frac{\sqrt{mn}}{d}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{1+\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|\right)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\leq N}\sum_{d|(m,n)}|\alpha_{m}\alpha_{n}|\frac{d}{\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\leq N\\
(m,n)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{ml,nl\leq N}\sum_{d|l}|\alpha_{ml}\alpha_{nl}|\frac{d}{l\sqrt{mn}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\sum_{ml,nl\leq N}\left(\frac{|\alpha_{ml}|^{2}}{n}+\frac{|\alpha_{nl}|^{2}}{m}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\sum_{n\leq N}|\alpha_{n}|^{2}.

Hence from (
3.21

Management and toxicological analysis of ocular hypertension after EyeCee® ONE intraocular lens implantation: a case series
Authors: Paul Bastelica, Romain Magny, Joël Poupon, Bertrand Sonigo, Tristan Aubert, Françoise Brignole-Baudouin, Juliette Buffault, Christophe Baudouin, Antoine Labbé
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe EyeCee® ONE intraocular lens (Nidek, Gamagori, Japan) has been withdrawn from the market due to a high number of reports of severe ocular hypertension (OHT) following phacoemulsification with implantation of this intraocular lens (IOL). In this case series, we report the results of a toxicological analysis and the surgical management of five patients with severe OHT following the implantation of defective EyeCee® ONE IOLs during cataract surgery.Cases presentationFive patients developed early, severe OHT refractory to maximal medical therapy following uneventful phacoemulsification (PCE) cataract surgery with implantation of an EyeCee® ONE IOL from a defective lot. Glaucoma filtering surgeries were required to control intraocular pressure (IOP). Toxicological analyses were carried out on the aqueous humor of one patient. IOP levels were monitored during postoperative follow-up, but three patients required postoperative adjustments (reintroduction of IOP-lowering therapy, goniopuncture or needling) in order to maintain IOP at satisfactory levels. Toxicological analysis revealed a high concentration of silicon in the aqueous humor of the patient from whom the sample was obtained.ConclusionsThese cases of OHT following cataract surgery with a defective IOL were of early onset severe, all requiring filtering surgery. The exact mechanism of this OHT has not been determined, but we did find high concentrations of silicon in the aqueous humor of one of these patients. Patients who received EyeCee® ONE IOLs during the same period of time should have their IOP and optic nerve monitored to detect any potential OHT or glaucoma that might appear over time.Keywords: Glaucoma, Ocular hypertension, Phacoemulsification, Intraocular lens, Toxicology

Background

Ocular hypertension (OHT) is a frequent complication of phacoemulsification cataract surgery (PCE), affecting 0.3–9.8% of patients, depending on the study [1–4]. These cases of OHT are multifactorial and may be secondary to inflammation, hyphema, pigment dispersion, presence of residual lens fragments or viscoelastic device, steroid response, or more rarely, malignant glaucoma. In the majority of cases, OHT following PCE rapidly resolves with topical hypotensive medications [5–9]. These postoperative intraocular pressure (IOP) spikes are usually more pronounced and prolonged in glaucomatous subjects, patients with pseudoexfoliation syndrome, and those with high baseline IOP [4, 10]. An urgent safety notice was published by Nidek in France on December 29, 2022 [11] and in the UK on January 26, 2023 [12], to suspend worldwide the use of a defective lot of EyeCee® ONE intraocular lenses (IOL), due to a high number of reports of severe OHT following PCE with implantation of this IOL. The EyeCee® ONE implant, manufactured by Nidek (Gamagori, Japan) and distributed by Bausch & Lomb (Laval, Canada), is a hydrophobic, monofocal, aspheric, acrylic IOL, preloaded inside a single-use disposable delivery system [13]. Subsequently, on July 21, 2023, their laboratory issued a second safety notice following an internal investigation [14]: defective batches of polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), a coating agent used specifically in preloaded forms of these IOLs, were believed to have obstructed the trabecular meshwork in these patients. We herein report a toxicological analysis and management of five patients with refractory severe OHT occurring after uneventful PCE with this defective IOL between November 7 and 28, 2022.

Case presentations

All our cases underwent cataract surgery at a primary care center and were subsequently referred to the glaucoma department at the Quinze-Vingts National Vision Hospital (Paris, France) following diagnosis of the OHT. All participants provided written informed consent for the retrospective use of their medical and biological data. This report was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Preoperative patient characteristics

The demographic and clinical characteristics of the five patients are summarized in Table 1. These patients were between 60 and 85 years of age and, on preoperative clinical examination, had reduced visual acuity due to cataract. Their examinations did not reveal any anomalies of the anterior segment. In particular, these patients had clear corneas and deep anterior chambers with no intraocular inflammation, pigment dispersion or deposits of exfoliative material. Among these patients, only one had a past ocular history of bilateral open angle glaucoma, well controlled on timolol, brinzolamide and bimatoprost eye drops. All patients had an IOP less than 21mmHg. The surgeon decided to perform cataract surgery on these five patients with implantation of a preloaded EyeCee® ONE monofocal IOL.

Operative protocol

The patients underwent surgery on two dates. PCEs were performed by the same surgeon between November 7 and November 28 of 2022 with no adverse events. Under topical anesthesia, a 2.2-mm corneal incision was created, followed by injection of a viscoelastic device (DuoVisc®, Alcon, Inc., Fort Worth, Texas, USA). Forceps capsulorrhexis and gentle hydrodissection of the lens nucleus were then performed, followed by PCE of the nucleus using the divide-and-conquer technique (Stellaris, Bausch & Lomb, Laval, Canada). A preloaded EyeCee® ONE IOL was then placed in the capsular bag, and the viscoelastic device was removed by irrigation/aspiration. The incision was not sutured, and an intracameral injection of 0.1 mL cefuroxime (Aprokam®, Théa Pharma, Clermont-Ferrand, France) was administered at the conclusion of the procedure. All patients received standard postoperative eye drops consisting of dexamethasone 3 times daily for 10 days followed by 2 drops daily for 10 days, indomethacin eyedrops 3 times daily for 1 month, and topical azithromycin 2 times daily for 3 days.

Clinical findings

Out of a total of 19 EyeCee® ONE implanted on the two surgical dates (from the same lot), five patients experienced severe OHT (26.3%) within 1 to 15 days postoperatively and were referred for treatment. Characteristics of diagnosis, treatment and follow-up of these cases of OHT are summarized in Table 2. For all patients, the anterior chambers were deep without residual lens fragments and were inflammation-free (in the anterior and posterior segments), the iridocorneal angles were open on gonioscopy, and the IOLs were positioned in the capsular bag. In addition to the usual postoperative medications, the patients received topical brinzolamide, brimonidine, timolol and bimatoprost. Intravenous (IV) acetazolamide and IV mannitol were then added. All patients went on to filtering surgery with subconjunctival application of mitomycin C 0.2 mg/mL for 2 min following admission to hospital, due to the presence of high refractory OHT (> 35mmHg) on maximal medical treatment. Details of the work-up, surgical management and follow-up are provided below.

Case 1

The first patient underwent deep sclerectomy with no intraoperative complications. Seven days after surgery, the IOP was controlled at 16 mmHg, measuring 15 mmHg at 1 month. However, at 2 months, an ineffective goniopuncture followed by slit lamp needling with 0.2 ml of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU, 25 mg/ml) were performed due to OHT to 30 mmHg. The IOP was subsequently controlled at 19 mmHg without medications at 3 months after surgery, and visual acuity improved to + 0.1 LogMAR.

Case 2

Trabeculectomy surgery was uneventful. At 7 days postoperatively, IOP was measured at 9 mmHg, and the filtration bleb was formed and sealed. At 15 days postoperatively, IOP was measured at 22 mmHg, and scleral suture lysis (10 − 0 nylon) was performed. Three weeks after surgery, the IOP had risen to 29 mmHg. An initial slit lamp needling was performed with the addition of 0.2 ml of 5-FU. Fifteen days later (5th postoperative week) the IOP remained high at 26 mmHg. A second slit lamp needling was performed without 5-FU. At 2 months after trabeculectomy, the filtration bleb was still encapsulated, and IOP was measured at 25 mmHg, requiring a third needling with the addition of 5-FU in the operating room. At 3 months, visual acuity was measured at 0 LogMar in the operated eye, and IOP was measured at 19 mmHg with a functional bleb.

Case 3

A deep sclerectomy was performed with no intraoperative complications. A sample of aqueous humor was obtained intraoperatively to analyze for possible toxic causes of this OHT. For inorganic analyses, the aqueous humor was diluted ten-fold in 0.1 N nitric acid and analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), allowing semi-quantitative targeted screening of the most abundant atoms in the sample, e.g. approximately 80 elements. For organic analyses, both targeted, encompassing around 1500 compounds (covering classical medications, drugs of abuse, plant alkaloids and xenobiotics), and non-targeted analyses were performed by liquid chromatography hyphenated to high resolution tandem mass spectrometry. The analytical procedures were conducted as previously described and according to analytical procedures routinely performed in the laboratory [15]. While organic toxicological analyses identified only the drugs administered during the clinical management of the patient, inorganic toxicological analyses revealed a high concentration of the chemical element silicon in the aqueous humor (16.839 µg/L, reference value in serum: 100–310 µg/L) [16]. At 7 days postoperatively, IOP was measured at 19 mmHg without medications. One month later, visual acuity was measured at 0 LogMAR in both eyes, and IOP remained controlled without medications. After 3 months, the IOP was measured at 14 mmHg with an effective bleb.

Case 4

At 7 days after deep sclerectomy, the IOP was controlled at 14 mmHg without medications, with a functional, tight bleb. At 1 month, visual acuity was measured at 0 LogMAR, and IOP was measured at 14mmHg. After 6 months, the IOP remained controlled at 13 mmHg.

Case 5

Seventeen days after the initial PCE, deep sclerectomy was performed, with no intraoperative adverse events. At 7 days postoperatively, IOP was controlled at 10 mmHg, with the presence of a well-functioning bleb. Postoperative visits at 3 and 6 weeks revealed well-controlled IOP. At 3 months after filtering surgery, the filtering bleb was still functional, and IOP remained controlled at 15 mmHg, but visual acuity did not improve, due to atrophic AMD.

Discussion and conclusions

For these five patients, PCEs with EyeCee® ONE IOL implantation were complicated by severe early OHT. All PCEs were uneventful, and the most frequent causes of postoperative OHT were ruled out, aside from one patient with pre-existing glaucoma. It is also unlikely that these cases of OHT were related to a steroid response, given the intensity and precocity of these cases of OHT [17]. Such IOP spikes did not occur in patients implanted with other IOLs during the same surgical sessions, the role of EyeCee® ONE IOL implantation was suspected. Thus, a pharmacologic alert was immediately sent to the distributor of the IOL in France, and soon thereafter, Nidek published the urgent safety notice to suspend the use of the EyeCee® ONE IOL. Subsequently, Nidek issued an additional safety notice to announce the results of its internal investigation [16]: PVP substances released during EyeCee® ONE injection into the anterior chamber could have obstructed the trabecular meshwork, explaining these cases of OHT.

The results of a preliminary survey carried out in the UK on patients implanted with these IOLs have been published [18]. Out of a total of 758 eyes implanted over the period from October 1, 2022 to January 26, 2023, 51 (6.2%) eyes showed IOP greater than or equal to 30 mmHg. Of these 51 eyes, 14 patients had a preoperative history of OHT or glaucoma. Over 90% of these IOP spikes were controlled medically with glaucoma eyedrops, and only three patients required glaucoma surgery. These results were similar to the results of the literature review published by Grzybowski et al. [4], who found an incidence of 2.1 to 8.9% OHT spikes above 30 mmHg after cataract surgery (complicated and uneventful) in the general population. Similarly, according to the Nidek analysis, a rate of 2 to 4% high IOP was observed in patients who had been implanted from this defective lot of EyeCee® ONE IOLs [12]. The results we present herein are different, with more severe OHT cases than previously reported. All the patients we have treated required filtering surgery to control IOP, compared with only 3 patients in the UK cohort, and the frequency of this complication is higher in our report.

According to the NIDEK safety notice [14], defective batches of PVP were responsible for these cases of OHT via obstruction of the trabecular meshwork. PVP is a synthetic hydrophilic organic polymer used in some eye drops for its moisturizing properties [19] as well as on the surface of some implants, such as the EyeCee® ONE IOL, as a coating agent to increase the wettability of hydrophobic implants and thus their ability to slide into injectors. To our knowledge, no cases of OHT associated with substances injected during IOL implantation or associated with IOL materials have been previously reported in the literature. In particular, PVP has not yet been identified as a cause of OHT. Other complications have been identified following the release of exogenous substances by IOL implantation, in particular cases of TASS (toxic anterior segment syndrome) associated with the implantation of aluminum-contaminated IOLs [20]. In one patient in our report, we found a high concentration of silicon in the aqueous humor. While the element silicon is present at normal concentrations in biological matrices such as plasma or cells [16], the highly elevated level quantified in the aqueous humor may suggest a significant presence of silicone polymer. This inference arises because the use of ICP-MS permits the detection only of atoms such as silicon, rather than molecules, such as silicone or PVP. Additionally, the analytical method employed for organic compound detection is not effective for the identification of silicone polymer molecules. Consequently, in this patient, the detection of an abundance of silicon could be attributed either to direct exogenous intake of silicon intraocularly or secondary to thermal degradation of the silicone polymer during the mineralization process needed for inorganic toxicological screening. In ophthalmology, silicone is used, among other uses, for intraocular tamponade of complex retinal detachments. It is known to cause OHT by various mechanisms, especially when the silicone is emulsified, by mechanical blockage of trabecular filtration when it migrates into the anterior chamber [21]. In addition, silicone may be toxic to trabecular cells, leading to secondary trabecular remodeling and dysfunction [22, 23]. As PVP has been incriminated by the manufacturer in these cases of OHT, this silicon could come from defective batches of PVP that could have been contaminated with silicon or silicone. However, this could be a different mechanism from that found by the manufacturer in our patients, as IOP elevations were much greater in our patients than in other series. The exact mechanism of this acute trabecular obstruction remains unclear, and consequently, it remains difficult to anticipate the medium- and long-term consequences of these IOL implantations. Most patients have developed the OHT in the early phase, suggesting an immediate complete trabecular meshwork impairment induced by high doses of the toxic substances. We know that silicone microparticles can be responsible for mechanical trabecular obstruction followed by formation of angular synechiae and secondary trabecular degeneration through their toxic and pro-inflammatory effects [23]. They have also been shown to accelerate the progression of pre-existing glaucoma [21]. Thus, for other patients who have received lower doses or have better trabecular meshwork function at the time of surgery, trabecular meshwork lesions might take longer to appear, perhaps months or years. It is therefore important that patients who have been implanted with an EyeCee® ONE IOL from a defective lot be carefully monitored over the long term.

These cases of OHT following cataract surgery with a defective IOL had an early onset and were severe, all requiring filtering surgery. The exact mechanism of these OHT is unclear but the presence of high concentrations of silicon in the aqueous humor of one of these cases suggested a possible role of this substance in this presentation of OHT. Although most OHT cases were observed during the first weeks after surgery, patients that were implanted with this set of IOL should have their IOP and optic nerve followed to diagnose OHT or glaucoma that could appear over time.

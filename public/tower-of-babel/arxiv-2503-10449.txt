Measure transport via pseudo-cones
Rolf Schneider

Abstract
For the solution of the Gauss image problem for pseudo-cones, which can be considered as a measure transport problem for certain measures on the sphere, we give a new proof, using a special case of Kantorovich duality. Keywords: pseudo-cone, Gauss image problem, measure transport, reverse radial Gauss map, Kantorovich duality 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 52A20 49Q22



1
Introduction

Let
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a closed convex cone, pointed and with interior points. A
C
-pseudo-cone is a nonempty closed convex set
K\subset C
not containing the origin and satisfying
K+C=K
. This implies that
C
is the recession cone of
K
and that
\lambda K\subseteq K
for
\lambda\geq 1
. The set of all
C
-pseudo-cones in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is denoted by
ps(C)
.

Denoting by
{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}
the unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we define the sets
\Omega_{C}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C
and
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}:={\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}\cap{\rm int}\,C^{\circ}
, where
C^{\circ}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 0\,\forall y\in C\}
is the dual cone of
C
. (We denote by
{\rm int}
the interior, by
{\rm cl}
the closure, by
{\rm bd}
the boundary, by
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
the scalar product, and by
o
the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.)

Let
K\in ps(C)
. Then
\rho_{K}(v):=\min\{r>0:rv\in K\}
,
v\in\Omega_{C}
, defines the
radial function

\rho_{K}:\Omega_{C}\to(0,\infty)
of
K
(the set
\{r>0:rv\in K\}
is not empty for
v\in\Omega_{C}
; otherwise
C
would not be the recession cone of
K
). Obviously,
\rho_{K}(v)v\in{\rm bd}\,K
.

Let
\omega_{K}
be the set of all
u\in{\rm cl}\,\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
with the property that
u
is a normal vector (by which we always mean an outer unit normal vector) at more than one point of
K
. Then
\omega_{K}
has Hausdorff dimension at most
n-2
(as follows from
[
6
, Thm. 2.2.5]
and polarity). The
reverse radial Gauss map

\alpha_{K}^{*}:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{K}\to\Omega_{C}
is defined by letting
\alpha_{K}^{*}(u)
be the unique vector
v\in\Omega_{C}
for which
u
is attained as a normal vector of
K
at
\rho_{K}(v)v
.

For a topological space
X
, we denote by
P(X)
the set of Borel probability measures on
X
. The main subject of this note is the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu\in P(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
and
\nu\in P(\Omega_{C})
and suppose that
\mu
is zero on sets of Hausdorff dimension at most
n-2
. Then there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K\in ps(C)
such that
(\alpha_{K}^{*})\sharp\mu=\nu
.

As usual,
(\alpha_{K}^{*})\sharp\mu=\nu
means that
\mu((\alpha_{K}^{*})^{-1}(\eta))=\nu(\eta)
for each Borel set
\eta\subseteq\Omega_{C}
, that is,
\nu
is the image measure of
\mu
under the reverse radial Gauss map of
K
.

Theorem
1
was proved in
[
9
]
. It was motivated by the introduction and investigation of the Gauss image problem for convex bodies in
[
2
]
. The proof there used a variational argument, as was the case in
[
9
]
. A treatment of the Gauss image problem for convex bodies by mass transportation methods was presented by Bertrand
[
1
]
. The purpose of the following is to prove also Theorem
1
by such methods. The proof is much shorter than that in
[
9
]
, though less elementary. We do not try to carry over the argumentation of
[
1
]
to pseudo-cones, but apply the Kantorovich duality in a similar way as done by
Rüschendorf and Rachev
[
5
]
.

Theorem
1
is proved in Section
3
, while Section
2
contains some preparations.

2
Preparations involving pseudo-cones

In this section, we collect and define some notions which will be essential for the proof of Theorem
1
. Let
K\in ps(C)
.

The support function of
K
is defined by

h_{K}(u)=\sup_{y\in K}\langle u,y\rangle\quad\mbox{for }u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}},

from which it follows that

h_{K}(u)=\sup_{v\in\Omega_{C}}\langle u,v\rangle\rho_{K}(v).

Writing
\overline{h}_{K}=-h_{K}
, this is equivalent to

\overline{h}_{K}(u)=\inf_{v\in\Omega_{C}}|\langle u,v\rangle|\rho_{K}(v).

(1)

The copolar set of
K
is defined by

K^{*}=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq-1\mbox{ for all }y\in K\}.

It satisfies
K^{*}\in ps(C^{\circ})
, and we have
K^{**}=K
. Moreover,

\rho_{K}(v)=\frac{1}{\overline{h}_{K^{*}}(v)}=\frac{1}{\inf_{u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}|\langle u,v\rangle|\rho_{K^{*}}(u)},

(2)

where the first equality is shown, for example, in
[
12
]
and the second equality follows from (
1
).

Let
f:\Omega_{C}\to(0,\infty)
be a function that is bounded away from
0
(that is, satisfies
f\geq a
for some
a>0
). We define the
convexification
of
f
by

\langle f\rangle:=\bigcap\{K\in ps(C):f(v)v\in K\,\forall v\in\Omega_{C}\}.

Since
f
is bounded away from
0
, there exists
K\in ps(C)
with
f(v)v\in K
for all
v\in\Omega_{C}
(for example, the intersection of
C
with a suitable closed halfspace). Clearly,
\langle f\rangle\in ps(C)
,

\rho_{\langle f\rangle}\leq f

(3)

and

\langle\rho_{K}\rangle=K\quad\mbox{for }K\in ps(C).

(4)

For the support function of
\langle f\rangle
we get, by (
1
) and (
3
),

\overline{h}_{\langle f\rangle}(u)=\inf_{v\in\Omega_{C}}|\langle u,v\rangle|\rho_{\langle f\rangle}(v)\leq\inf_{v\in\Omega_{C}}|\langle u,v\rangle|f(v).

Here strict inequality would contradict the definition of the convexification, hence we have

\overline{h}_{\langle f\rangle}(u)=\inf_{v\in\Omega_{C}}|\langle u,v\rangle|f(v)\quad\mbox{for }u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

(5)

Similarly, of course, for
g:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to(0,\infty)
, bounded away from
0
, we define

\langle g\rangle:=\bigcap\{K\in ps(C^{\circ}):g(u)u\in K\,\forall u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\},

which is a
C^{\circ}
-pseudo-cone.

For a function
f:\Omega_{C}\to(0,\infty)
, bounded away from zero, we define the
pseudo-conjugate
function
f^{*}:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to(0,\infty)
by

f^{*}(u):=\sup_{v\in\Omega_{C}}\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|f(v)},\quad u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

For fixed
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
, the product
|\langle u,v\rangle|f(v)
is bounded away from
0
, hence
f^{*}(u)<\infty
.

It follows from (
2
) and (
5
) that

\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}(u)=\frac{1}{\overline{h}_{\langle f\rangle}(u)}=\frac{1}{\inf_{v\in\Omega_{C}}|\langle u,v\rangle|f(v)}=f^{*}(u),

thus

f^{*}=\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}.

(6)

Since the pseudo-conjugate function of
f
is the radial function of the copolar set of the convexification of
f
, the function
f^{*}
is always the radial function of a
C^{\circ}
-pseudo-cone. It follows from (
6
) and (
4
) that
(\rho_{K})^{*}=\rho_{\langle\rho_{K}\rangle^{*}}=\rho_{K^{*}}
for
K\in ps(C)
.

Of course, for functions
g:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\to(0,\infty)
, we define
g^{*}
correspondingly.

Again, let
f:\Omega_{C}\to(0,\infty)
be a function that is bounded away from
0
. Since
f^{*}=\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}
by (
6
), we have
\langle f^{*}\rangle=\langle\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}\rangle=\langle f\rangle^{*}
by (
4
), thus

\langle f^{*}\rangle=\langle f\rangle^{*}.

This gives
f^{**}=(\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}})^{*}=\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{**}}=\rho_{\langle f\rangle}\leq f
by (
3
), thus

f^{**}\leq f.

(7)

Here equality holds if and only if
f
is the radial function of a
C
-pseudo-cone.

From the definition of
f^{*}
it follows that

f^{*}(u)\geq\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|f(v)},

hence

f(v)f^{*}(u)\geq\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|}\quad\mbox{for }(v,u)\in\Omega_{C}\times\Omega_{C^{\circ}}.

(8)

Suppose that equality holds in (
8
). Then, by (
6
), (
2
) and (
3
),

f(v)=\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|f^{*}(u)}=\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}(u)}\leq\frac{1}{\overline{h}_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}(v)}=\rho_{\langle f\rangle}(v)\leq f(v).

Since equality holds here, we have
\rho_{\langle f\rangle}(v)=f(v)
and
|\langle u,v\rangle|\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}(u)=\overline{h}_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}(v)
. The latter means that
v
is a normal vector of
\langle f\rangle^{*}
at
\rho_{\langle f\rangle^{*}}(u)u
. Equivalently (as follows, e.g., from
[
7
, Lem. 6]
),

u
is a normal vector of
\langle f\rangle
at
\rho_{\langle f\rangle}(v)v
. The converse also holds.

We recall that in
[
10
]
we have defined the pseudo-subdifferential of
K
by

\partial^{\bullet}K:=\{(v,u)\in\Omega_{C}\times\Omega_{C^{\circ}}:\mbox{$u$ is a normal vector of $K$ at $\rho_{K}(v)v$}\}.

We can thus state the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f:\Omega_{C}\to(0,\infty)
be a function bounded away from zero. The inequality

f(v)f^{*}(u)\geq\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|}

(9)

holds for all
(v,u)\in\Omega_{C}\times\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
. Equality in
(\ref{M9})
for some
(v,u)\in\Omega_{C}\times\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
holds if and only if
\rho_{\langle f\rangle}(v)=f(v)
and
(v,u)\in\partial^{\bullet}\langle f\rangle
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1

We define the function
c:\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}\to(0,\infty)
by

c(u,v):=-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|,\quad(u,v)\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}.

(10)

This is the cost function found in
[
10
]
, but with a negative sign, to make it nonnegative.

Given
\mu\in P(\Omega_{C^{\circ}})
and
\nu\in P(\Omega_{C})
, we denote by
M(\mu,\nu)
the set of all probability measures
\pi
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}
with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
, that is, satisfying
\pi(\omega\times\Omega_{C})=\mu(\omega)
and
\pi(\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\eta)=\nu(\eta)
for all Borel sets
\omega\subseteq\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and
\eta\subseteq\Omega_{C}
. Then we define, with
c
given by (
10
),

S(\mu,\nu):=\sup\left\{\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}}c(u,v)\,{\rm d}\pi(u,v):\pi\in M(\mu,\nu)\right\},

\displaystyle I(\mu,\nu):=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}h\,{\rm d}\mu+\int_{\Omega_{C}}g\,{\rm d}\nu:h\in L^{1}(\mu),\,g\in L^{1}(\nu),\,h(u)+g(v)\geq c(u,v)\,\forall u,v\right\}.

and make use of the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

We have
S(\mu,\nu)=I(\mu,\nu)
. The
\sup
defining
S(\mu,\nu)
is an attained maximum, and if
I(\mu,\nu)<\infty
, then the
\inf
defining
I(\mu,\nu)
is an attained minimum.

As in Rüschendorf and Rachev
[
5
]
, after which our argument is modeled, this follows from a more general version of the Kantorovich duality proved by Kellerer
[
3
]
, Theorems (2.6) and (2.21). For more details, we refer to Rachev and Rüschendorf
[
4
, Thm. 3.2.9]
, where the proof employs the Theorems 2.2.1, 2.3.10, 2.3.12 of that book. One may also consult Villani
[
11
, Thm. 5.10]
(the comparison with
[
4
]
requires switching some functions to their negatives).

We assume first that
\nu
is concentrated on a closed subset of
\Omega_{C}
. Then there is a constant
a>0
with
|\langle u,v\rangle|\geq a
and hence
-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|\leq|\log a|
for all
(u,v)\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times{\rm supp}\,\nu
(where
{\rm supp}\,\nu
denotes the support of the measure
\nu
). It follows that
I(\mu,\nu)<\infty
.

By Theorem
2
there exist functions
h
on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and
g
on
\Omega_{C}
such that

h(u)+g(v)\geq-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}h\,{\rm d}\mu+\int_{\Omega_{C}}g\,{\rm d}\nu=I(\mu,\nu).

Let

\varphi(u):=e^{h(u)},\qquad\psi(v):=e^{g(v)}.

Since
h
and
g
are bounded from below, the positive functions
\varphi
and
\psi
are bounded away from zero. In particular, we can define

f:=\varphi^{**}.

We have

\varphi(u)\psi(v)\geq e^{-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|}

and hence

\psi(v)\geq\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|\varphi(u)}

for all
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}
and therefore
\psi(v)\geq\varphi^{*}(v)
. This gives

\displaystyle\log\varphi(u)+\log\psi(v)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log\varphi(u)+\log\varphi^{*}(v)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\log\varphi^{**}(u)+\log\varphi^{*}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log f(u)+\log f^{*}(v),

where we have used (
7
) and
\varphi^{*}=\varphi^{***}
. By (
9
) we have

\log f(u)+\log f^{*}(u)\geq-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|.

Thus, the pair
(\log f,\log f^{*})
is also a solution of the minimum problem, hence we have

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}\log f\,{\rm d}\mu+\int_{\Omega_{C}}\log f^{*}\,{\rm d}\nu=I(\mu,\nu).

By Theorem
2
, there exists
\pi^{*}\in M(\mu,\nu)
for which

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}}-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|\,{\rm d}\pi^{*}(u,v)=I(\mu,\nu),

hence

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}}\left[-\log|\langle u,v\rangle|-\log f(u)-\log f^{*}(v)\right]\,{\rm d}\pi^{*}(u,v)=0.

Since the integrand is non-positive, we deduce that it is zero almost everywhere, that is,

f(u)f^{*}(v)=\frac{1}{|\langle u,v\rangle|}

for
\pi^{*}
-almost all
(u,v)
.

By its definition,
f
is the radial function of some
C
-pseudo-cone
K
. By Lemma
1
,
(v,u)\in\partial^{\bullet}K
for
\pi^{*}
-almost all
(u,v)\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}
. In the following, we write
\partial^{\circ}K:=\{(u,v):(v,u)\in\partial^{\bullet}K\}
.

Now suppose that
\mu
is zero on sets of Hausdorff dimension at most
n-2
. Then
\omega_{K}
has
\mu
-measure zero. For each
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{K}
there is a unique pair
(u,v)\in\partial^{\circ}K
, and we have
\alpha_{K}^{*}(u)=v
. Let
\eta\subseteq\Omega_{C}
be a Borel set. Since
\pi^{*}
has marginal measures
\mu
and
\nu
, we get

\displaystyle\nu(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\{(u,v)\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}:u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}},\,v\in\eta\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\{(u,v)\in\partial^{\circ}K:u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}},\,v\in\eta\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\{(u,v)\in\partial^{\circ}K:\alpha_{K}^{*}(u)=v,\,v\in\eta\}),

where we have used that
\pi^{*}((\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C})\setminus\partial^{\circ}K)=0
and

\pi^{*}(\{(u,v)\in\partial^{\circ}K:\alpha_{K}^{*}(u)\mbox{ is not defined at }u\}=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\mu((\alpha_{K}^{*})^{-1}(\eta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\{(u,v)\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\times\Omega_{C}:u\notin\omega_{K},\,\alpha_{K}^{*}(u)\in\eta,\,v\in\Omega_{C}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{*}(\{(u,v)\in\partial^{\circ}K:\alpha_{K}^{*}(u)=v,\,v\in\eta\}.

Hence,
\mu((\alpha_{K}^{*})^{-1}(\eta))=\nu(\eta)
.

So far we have assumed that
{\rm supp}\,\nu
is a compact subset of
\Omega_{C}
. Now let
\nu\in P(\Omega_{C})
be as in Theorem
1
. We choose a sequence
(\eta_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of compact sets satisfying
\nu(\eta_{1})>0
,
\eta_{j}\subset\eta_{j+1}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}\eta_{j}=\Omega_{C}
. Then we set

\nu_{j}(\eta):=\frac{\nu(\eta\cap\eta_{j})}{\nu(\eta_{j})}\quad\mbox{for Borel sets }\eta\subseteq\Omega_{C}.

As shown above, to each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a
C
-pseudo-cone
K_{j}
such that
(\alpha_{K_{j}}^{*})\sharp\mu=\nu_{j}
.

We recall the convergence of pseudo-cones. Let
(K_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
ps(C)
. Denoting by
B^{n}
the unit ball of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with center
o
, we say that
K_{j}\to K
(
\in ps(C)
) as
j\to\infty
if there is some
t_{0}>0
with
K_{j}\cap t_{0}B^{n}\not=\emptyset
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and the sequence
(K_{j}\cap tB^{n})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of convex bodies converges, in the usual sense, to
K\cap tB^{n}
, for each
t\geq t_{0}
. By Lemma 1 of
[
7
]
, every sequence of
C
-pseudo-cones whose distances from
o
are bounded and bounded away from zero has a subsequence that converges to a
C
-pseudo-cone.

The pseudo-cones
K_{j}
with
(\alpha_{K_{j}}^{*})\sharp\mu=\nu_{j}
found above can be assumed to have distance
1
from the origin, since multiplication of a pseudo-cone
K
by a positive factor does not change the function
\alpha_{K}^{*}
. Then the sequence
(K_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a subsequence converging to a
C
-pseudo-cone
K_{0}
, and after changing the notation we can assume that
K_{j}\to K_{0}
.

If we define

\omega_{\Box}:=\bigcup_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\omega_{K_{j}},

then all functions
\alpha_{K_{j}}^{*}
,
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, are defined on
\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{\Box}
and hence
\mu
-almost everywhere.

Let
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{\Box}
. We state that

\alpha_{K_{j}}^{*}(u)\to\alpha_{K_{0}}^{*}(u)\quad\mbox{as }j\to\infty,\mbox{ for $\mu$-almost all $u$}.

(11)

For the proof, let
u\in\Omega_{C^{\circ}}\setminus\omega_{\Box}
. For each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, there is a unique
x_{j}=\rho_{K_{j}}(v_{j})v_{j}\in{\rm bd}\,K_{j}
at which
u
is attained as a normal vector of
K_{j}
. Since
u
is fixed and all
K_{j}
have distance one from the origin, the sequenc
(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded and hence has a subsequence converging to some
x
. The point
x
is necessarily a boundary point of
K_{0}
, and
u
is a normal vector of
K_{0}
at this point. Since
u
is attained as a normal vector of
K_{0}
at a unique boundary point, we have
x=x_{0}
. This shows also that every convergent subsequence of
(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to
x_{0}
, hence this sequence is convergent to
x_{0}
, which implies that
(v_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converges to
v_{0}
. This proves (
11
).

Now choose
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
g:\Omega_{C}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded continuous function. Since
\alpha_{K_{k}}^{*}\sharp\mu=\nu_{k}
, the change of variables formula gives

\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g\circ\alpha_{K_{k}}^{*}\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{\Omega_{C}}g\,{\rm d}\nu_{k}=\frac{1}{\nu(\eta_{k})}\int_{\eta_{k}}g\,{\rm d}\nu.

Since (
11
) holds, the dominated convergence theorem shows that, as
k\to\infty
, the left side converges to
\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g\circ\alpha_{K_{0}}^{*}\,{\rm d}\mu
, while the right side converges to
\int_{\Omega_{C}}g\,{\rm d}\nu
. We deduce that

\int_{\Omega_{C}}g\,{\rm d}(\alpha_{K_{0}}^{*}\sharp\mu)=\int_{\Omega_{C^{\circ}}}g\circ\alpha_{K_{0}}^{*}\,{\rm d}\mu=\int_{\Omega_{C}}g\,{\rm d}\nu.

Since this holds for all bounded continuous functions
g
on
\Omega_{C}
, we can conclude that
\alpha_{K_{0}}^{*}\sharp\mu=\nu
.

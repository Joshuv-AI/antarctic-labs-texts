Abstract
. Hitting rate and escape rate are two examples of recurrence laws for a dynamical system, and a general limit connects them. We show that for both Gibbs-Markov systems or any systems with the ϕ \phi -mixing measure, for a sequence of nested sets whose intersection is a measure zero set, this general limit equals one in the absence of short returns and less than one otherwise, which is given by an explicit formula called extremal index. One of the applications of this result is to dynamical systems on Riemannian manifolds such as hyperbolic maps and expanding maps, and it can be applied to any system with a suitable Young tower.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
37A25, 37D25.

1.
Introduction

An open dynamical system is a system where there is a hole. This means that the points of the system that enter this hole are removed from the system. Recently, there has been a surge of interest in open dynamical systems, leading to significant advancements in our understanding of such systems.

In open dynamical systems, we have two laws of recurrence, the return of a point of the system to the sets that shrink towards that point, which is called the hitting time of that point, and the other is the entry of a point of the system into a fixed set (hole) which is called escape rate.
If we consider the hitting time from the point of view of a random variable, it often has an exponential distribution, see (
1.1
) below. If we consider localized escape rates which one obtains when the size of the hole shrinks to zero around the point, then there is a relationship between these two, see (
1.3
) below. As a result in
[
BDT
]
, a general formula has been obtained in which, by choosing the appropriate parameters, we have each of them. This general formula is obtained in the space of open systems with interval maps and conformal measures.

On the other hand, the escape rate and the hitting time have also been obtained if our system has a
\phi
-mixing measure, but in this setting, there is no result of the general formula as in
[
BDT
]
, that is our main goal in this research.

The results obtained in this paper show that the general formula has almost the same behaviour in
\phi
-mixing systems with slow mixing or in Gibbs-Markov systems, which are a fast mixing systems, as it does in
[
BDT
]
. In fact, if the system has good behaviour and is exponentially
\phi
-mixing, the general formula holds in more cases. However, if the system is polynomially
\phi
-mixing, the general formula applies to fewer cases. The number of cases where the general formula holds depends on the rate of mixing and the rate of decay of the measure of cylinders.

Note that the general formula obtained here applies to zero measure sets, which is more general in comparison to the setting of
[
BDT
]
, where the general formula for singletons is proved. At the end of the paper we provide examples where our theory applies to zero measure sets such as Cantor sets for an interval map (see Section
5.2
), and the line segment for the Arnold cat map (see Section
5.3
).

1.1.
Organization of the paper

We will first review the definitions and previous results in Section 1, then in Section 2, we present the main theorem (Theorem
2.1
). In Section 3, we address the general formula for Gibbs-Markov systems. In Section 4, we demonstrate its application which is use of open balls to derive the general formula. Finally, the paper concludes in Section 5 by providing some examples.

1.2.
Hitting times, escape rates, and their relation

Suppose
(X,\mathcal{B},T,\mu)
as an ergodic probability measure preserving
dynamical
system i.e.
X
is a space,
\mathcal{B}
is a
\sigma
-algebra on
X
,
T:X\to X
is a measurable map and
\mu
is an ergodic probability measure on
(X,\mathcal{B})
such that
\mu(T^{-1}B)=\mu(B)
for each
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The
hitting time
of
z
to the set
U
is the first time that the orbit of
z
(i.e.
T^{n}z
for
n=1,2,3,...
) hits
U
i.e.

\tau_{U}(z):=\inf\{j\geq 1:T^{j}z\in U\}.

Poincaré
shows for each
z\in U
,
T^{n}z
belongs to
U
infinitely often. After that, Kac in
[
K
]
calculates the mean behaviour of hitting time.

Suppose that our system is an open system i.e., a hole is placed in the space and when points hit that hole, they are removed from the dynamics. We denote the holes by
\{U_{r}\}_{r}
that shrink to a point
z
as
r\to 0
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. A natural question that can arise here is, what is
\mu(\tau_{U_{r}}(z)>s)
?

Hitting time statistics started with Pitskel
[
P
]
. Pitskel shows that

(1.1)

\left|\mu\left(\mu(U_{r}).\tau_{U_{r}}(z)>s\right)-e^{-s}\right|\to 0\quad as\quad r\to 0.

Pitskel’s result was generated by Hirata
[
H
]
for hyperbolic dynamical systems. Then, Collet and Galves
[
CG
]
developed this result in the context of piecewise-expanding maps. It gradually became clear that (
1.1
) holds in a wide range of dynamical systems, see
[
L+
, Chapter5]
.

We can rewrite (
1.1
) as
\lim_{r\to 0}\log\mu(\mu(U_{r})\tau_{U_{r}}>s)=-s
. So,

(1.2)

\lim_{r\to 0}-\frac{1}{s}\log\mu\left(\tau_{U_{r}}>\frac{s}{\mu(U_{r})}\right)=1.

Bruin, Demers and Todd (
[
BDT
]
) formulated a generalised limit of (
1.2
). This limit can be seen as the special limiting case of the expression

\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\frac{-1}{t}\log\mu\left(\tau_{U_{r}}>t\right),

by setting
t=\frac{s}{\mu(U_{r})}
. We can see the general behaviour of the system around point
z
, by setting
t=s\mu(U_{r})^{-\alpha}
for some
\alpha,s\in(0,\infty)
, and let,

L_{\alpha,s}(z):=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{r})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{r}}>s\mu(U_{r})^{-\alpha})).

So when
\alpha=1
, we have (
1.2
) again, when
\alpha=\infty
, we can consider it as coinciding with the derivative of the escape rate, where
t\to\infty
as
r
is held fixed:

(1.3)

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\mu(U_{r})}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{-1}{t}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{r}}>t)),

and while
\alpha=0
we can consider it as the reversed order of limits, where
r\to 0
as
t
is held fixed:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{-1}{t\mu(U_{r})}\log(\tau_{U_{r}}>t).

The main result in
[
BDT
, Theorem 2.1]
expresses a limit for
L_{\alpha,s}
. We briefly state it below, but first need some description of the setting.

Let
f:I\to I
be a piecewise continuous map of the unit interval and assume that
I
has countably intervals of monotonicity. Also assume that there exists a countable collection of maximal intervals
Z=\{Z_{i}\}\subset I
such that
f
is continuous and monotone on each
Z_{i}
and
\forall i\neq j
,
int
(Z_{i})\ \cap
int
(Z_{j})=\emptyset
. Now let
D:=I\setminus\cup_{i}
int
(Z_{i})
.

Define a Borel probability measure map
m_{\varphi}
such that
m_{\varphi}(D)=0
, which is conformal with respect to a potential
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
(i.e.
\frac{dm_{\varphi}}{d(m_{\varphi}\circ f)}=e^{\varphi}
). Put

I_{cont}:=\left\{z\in I:f^{k}\text{ is continuous at }z\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}\right\},

and suppose that
\mu_{\varphi}(I_{cont})=1
, where
\mu_{\varphi}
is the equilibrium state of
\varphi
i.e.
\mathbf{P}(\varphi)=h_{\mu_{\varphi}}+\int\varphi d\mu_{\varphi}
(
\mathbf{P}
is the Pressure of
\varphi
). Let
S_{n}\varphi(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\varphi\circ f^{i}
, then we have the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
BDT
, Theorem 2.1]
Let
z\in I_{cont}
and
(U_{r})_{r}
be a family of intervals such that
\lim_{r\to 0}diam(U_{r})=0
and
\bigcap_{r}U_{r}=\{z\}
.

Then for any
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\alpha\in[0,\infty]
, taking
L_{\alpha,s}(z)
with respect to
\mu_{\varphi}
, we have:

(1.4)

L_{\alpha,s}(z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $z$ is not periodic}\\
1-e^{S_{p}\varphi(z)}&\text{if $z$ is $p$-periodic,}\end{cases}

where
p
-periodic means that the prime period of
z
is
p
, i.e. the smallest positive integer
p
such that
f^{p}(z)=z
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the theorem above, in the case
z
is periodic, we assume
f^{p}
is monotonic and the density is continuous at
z
. In addition,
f
and
\varphi
must satisfy four regularity conditions which are mainly related to the existence of an upper bound for
e^{S_{p}\varphi(z)}
. Also,
\{U_{r}\}_{r}
must satisfy a large image condition. We do not state them for brevity, but all the mentioned conditions can be found in
[
BDT
, Page1268]
.

1.3.
Escape rates for
\phi
-mixing dynamical systems

A part of Theorem
1.2
was proven by Davis, Haydn, and Yang in a different setting
[
DHY
, Theorem A and Corollary C]
. Namely, they proved the case where
\alpha=\infty
of Theorem
1.2
in a symbolic dynamic as a system with a
\phi
-mixing measure, which we will explain further.

Let
(\Omega,T,\mu)
be a probability measure preserving dynamical system. Assume that there exists
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{1},A_{2},...\}
a measurable partition of
\Omega
.

Definition 1.4
.

The partition
\mathcal{A}^{n}=\bigvee^{n-1}_{i=0}T^{-i}\mathcal{A}
of
\Omega
is
n
-th join
of
\mathcal{A}
. The elements of
\mathcal{A}^{n}
called
n
-
cylinders
.

Note that
\sigma(\mathcal{A}^{n})
is the
\sigma
algebra generated by
\mathcal{A}^{n}
. Some key definitions follow.

Definition 1.5
.

The probability measure
\mu
is
left
\phi
-mixing
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
if

|\mu(A\cap T^{-n-k}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|\leq\phi(k)\mu(A),

and is
right
\phi
-mixing
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
if

|\mu(A\cap T^{-n-k}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|\leq\phi(k)\mu(B),

and
\psi
-mixing with respect to
\mathcal{A}
if

|\mu(A\cap T^{-n-k}B)-\mu(A)\mu(B)|\leq\phi(k)\mu(A)\mu(B),

for all
A\in\sigma(\mathcal{A}^{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
B\in\sigma(\bigcup_{j}\mathcal{A}^{j})
, where
\phi(k)
is a decreasing function which converges to zero as
k\to\infty
.

Remark 1.6
.

Every
\psi
-mixing measure is left and right
\phi
-mixing by choosing
\psi(k)=\phi(k)
.

Remark 1.7
.

Whenever we say
(X,T,\mu)
is a
\phi
-mixing dynamical system, we mean that the measure is (left or/and right)
\phi
-mixing.

Remark 1.8
.

Typically, the concept of
\phi
-mixing in sources refers to what is defined as left
\phi
-mixing. Left and right
\phi
-mixing are asymmetric (because the term
\mu(A\cap T^{-n-k}B)
is fixed in both definitions), but we can derive results for right
\phi
-mixing with slight modifications if we have results for left
\phi
-mixing.

This property is one of the five strong mixing properties for probability measures (see
[
Br
]
). The
\phi
-mixing measure was first defined by Ibragimov in 1962 (see
[
I
]
and
[
IR
]
). Many features of this property, along with examples, can be found in the book
[
D
, Chapter1]
. The history of the first study of statistical properties related to the hitting rate in mixing processes can be found in
[
AG
]
. However, Abadi was the first to study these properties in the space with a
\phi
-mixing measure (
[
A1
]
). He later found an upper bound for the hitting time law in
\phi
-mixing systems (
[
A2
]
). Five years later, in collaboration with Vergne, they proved (
1.4
) for the case
\alpha=1
in
\phi
-mixing systems (
[
AV
]
).

We can define the escape rate to
U\subset\Omega
.

Definition 1.9
.

Denote the
escape rate
to
U
by
\rho(U)
and define it as

\rho(U):=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}|\log(\mu(\tau_{U}>t))|,

if the limit exists.

Now, we can find the localized escape rate. For this let
(U_{n})_{n}
be
nested sequence
of sets i.e.
\forall n,\ U_{n+1}\subset U_{n}
and
\lim_{n}\mu(U_{n})=0
. Note that shrinking the
(U_{n})_{n}
to the point
z\in\Omega
is a specific case of these settings. In fact, the result obtained in
[
DHY
]
is more general.
Then we define the
localized escape rate
for the measure zero set
\Lambda=\cap_{n}U_{n}
as,

\displaystyle\rho(\Lambda):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\rho(U_{n})}{\mu(U_{n})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mu(U_{n})}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}|\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>t))|,

whenever the limit exists.

We consider, for each
U_{n}
, a union of
\kappa_{n}
-cylinders, based on a non-decreasing sequence of integers
\left\{\kappa_{n}\right\}_{n}
. For any set
U_{n}
, we can approximate it in
\sigma(\mathcal{A}^{j})
using the following definition.

Definition 1.10
.

For each
n
and
j\geq 1
, set
C_{j}(U_{n})=\left\{B\in\mathcal{A}^{j}:B\cap U_{n}\neq\emptyset\right\}
, then we define

U_{n}^{j}:=\bigcup_{B\in C_{j}(U_{n})}B,

as the
outer
j
-cylinder approximation of
U_{n}
.

That is the union of those cylinders in
\mathcal{A}^{j}
that have an intersection with
U_{n}
.

Remark 1.11
.

(1)

\forall j,\ U_{n+1}^{j}\subset U_{n}^{j}
and
U_{n}\subset U_{n}^{j}
,

(2)

If
j\geq\kappa_{n},\ U_{n}=U_{n}^{j}
.

The following sequence of cylinders is also suitable for our purpose.

Definition 1.12
.

Consider nested sequence
\left\{U_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{\kappa_{n}}\right\}
for
n=1,2,...
, if this sequence has the following two properties, we call it
a good neighbourhood system
:

(N1)

\kappa_{n}\nearrow\infty
and
\kappa_{n}\mu(U_{n})^{c}\to 0
for some
c\in(0,1)
;

(N2)

there exists
C>0
and
m^{\prime}>1
so that
\mu(U_{n}^{j})\leq\mu(U_{n})+Cj^{-m^{\prime}}
for all
j<\kappa_{n}
.

Definition 1.13
.

Let
U\subset\Omega
be a nonzero measure set. Considering the definition of hitting time, we can also derive its
higher order
as follows:

\tau^{0}_{U}:=0,

\tau^{1}_{U}:=\tau_{U},

\tau^{j}_{U}(x):=\tau^{j-1}_{U}(x)+\tau_{U}\left(T^{\tau_{U}^{j-1}}(x)\right).

Now suppose that
(U_{n})_{n}
is a nested sequence in
\Omega
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
, then

\hat{\theta}_{l}(K,U_{n}):=\mu_{U_{n}}\left(\tau_{U_{n}}^{l-1}\leq K\right),

is the conditional probability of having at least
(l-1)
returns to
U_{n}
before time
K
.
Assume that

\hat{\theta}_{l}(K):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\theta}_{l}(K,U_{n}),

exists for sufficiently large
K
and
l\geq 1
. Then the following limit also exists (due to
\hat{\theta}_{l}(K)\leq\hat{\theta}_{l}(K^{\prime})\leq 1
for
K\leq K^{\prime})
,

(1.5)

\hat{\theta}_{l}:=\lim_{K\to\infty}\hat{\theta}_{l}(K).

Remark 1.14
.

\hat{\theta}_{1}=1
,
\hat{\theta}_{2}=\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{U_{n}}\left(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq K\right).

Definition 1.15
.

Let
(U_{n})_{n}
be a nested sequence. The following limit is called
extremal index
,

\theta_{1}:=\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{U_{n}}\left(\tau_{U_{n}}>K\right)=1-\hat{\theta}_{2}.

Remark 1.16
.

In many sources, such as
[
FFT
]
and
[
L+
]
, the extremal index is given as
\theta=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}>K_{n}})}{\mu(U_{n})}
, where
(K_{n})_{n}\nearrow\infty
is a sequence of integers. We will show that, for the results obtained in this paper,
\theta=\theta_{1}
.

If
(U_{n})_{n}
is a sequence in the absence of short returns, one can obtain
\hat{\theta}_{2}=0
and consequently
\theta_{1}=1
. Using the definition below, this sequence is formulated.

Definition 1.17
.

Let
U\subset\Omega
be a non zero measure set. Then the
period of
U
is

\pi(U)=\min\{k>0:T^{-k}U\cap U\neq\emptyset\},

and the
essential period of
U
is

\pi_{ess}(U)=\min\{k>0:\mu(T^{-k}U\cap U)>0\}.

Remark 1.18
.

(1)

\pi(U)\leq\pi_{ess}(U);

(2)

\pi(U)=\pi_{ess}(U)
if
T
is continuous,
U
is open and
\mu
has full support.

Now, if
\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty
as

, it means that the sequence
(U_{n})_{n}
does not have short returns.

Remark 1.19
.

There are equivalent conditions for
\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty
in
[
DHY
, Corollary D]
.

Davis, Haydn, and Yang proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.20
.

[
DHY
, Theorem A and Corollary C]
Let
(\Omega,T,\mu)
be a probability measure preserving dynamical system such that
\mu
is right
\phi
-mixing with
\phi(k)\leq Ck^{-m}
for some
C>0
and

, and
(U_{n})_{n}

,

. Then
\theta_{1}
and the localized escape rate at

exists and satisfies,

\rho(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty$}\\
\theta_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty$.}\end{cases}

Remark 1.21
.

Theorem
1.20
still holds if:

(1)

\mu
is left
\phi
-mixing instead of right
\phi
-mixing
[
DHY

;

(2)

-mixing system

Remark 1.22
.

In Theorem

Comparing Theorem

2.
Recurrence rate for an polynomially

2.1.
Main Theorem

In this section, we present the main theorem of this paper and proceed with its proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(\Omega,T,\mu)
be a
\phi
-mixing dynamical system with a measurable partition
\mathcal{A}
of
\Omega
such that
\phi

\phi(k)\leq Ck^{-m}

. Assume that

\bigcap_{n}U_{n}=\{\Lambda\}

\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty

(\hat{\theta}_{l})_{l}

\sum_{l}l\hat{\theta}_{l}<\infty

Remark 2.4

Remark 2.6

The extremal index

Lemma 2.7
.

[

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: the case
\alpha=0

By replacing
\alpha
with 0 in

\displaystyle L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{r}>s\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha})),

we get the following limit:

\displaystyle L_{0,s}(\Lambda)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s\mu(U_{n})}\log\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s).

So, our goal in this section is to prove the following:

(2.1)

L_{0,s}(\Lambda)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})}\log\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty$}\\
\theta_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty$.}\end{cases}

The following lemma facilitates the proof of (
2.1
).

Lemma 2.8
.

[
HV
, Lemma 3]
. Let
(U_{n})_{n}
be a nested sequence so that
\mu(U_{n})\to 0
as

. Assume that the limits
(\hat{\theta_{l}})_{l}
exist,
\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\hat{\theta_{l}}<\infty
and
s
large enough. Then

\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s)}{s\mu(U_{n})}=\theta_{1}.

By Lemma
2.8
for large enough
s
we have

\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s)=\theta_{1}s\mu(U_{n})(1+o(1)).

Hence, we obtain the result as below

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})}\log\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})}\log(1-\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s\mu(U_{n})}\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{s\mu(U_{n})}\theta_{1}s\mu(U_{n})(1+o(1))

\displaystyle=\theta_{1}.

Note that
\lim_{n\to\infty}o(1)=0
. Now by Lemma
2.7
, the goal of this section i.e. the equality (
2.1
) is proved.

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
: the case
\alpha\in(0,\infty)

We consider

\displaystyle L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}))

for
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
.

For the proof, the following lemma plays a crucial role.

Lemma 2.9
.

[
DHY
, Lemma 4.6]
. Let
\mu
be right
\phi
-mixing for the partition
\mathcal{A}
with
\phi(k)\leq C(k^{-m})
for some
m>1
. Let
(U_{n})_{n}
,
n=1,2,...
be a good neighbourhood such that
\hat{\theta}_{l}(K)
exists for
K
large enough, and
\sum_{l}\hat{\theta}_{l}<\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s_{n})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}\to\theta_{1},

for any increasing sequence
s_{n},n=1,2,...
which satisfies
s_{n}\mu(U_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

We need the following lemma that gives us a useful inequality, see
[
DHY
, Lemma 4.1]
for proof. For convenience, we use
U
instead of
U_{n}
.

Lemma 2.10
.

[
DHY
, Lemma 4.1]
. Let
\mu
be right or left
\phi
-mixing for the partition
\mathcal{A}
. Let
s,t>0
such that
s<t
and
U\in\sigma(\mathcal{A}^{\kappa_{n}})
and
\Delta<\frac{s}{2}
. Then we have,

|\mu(\tau_{U}>t+s)-\mu(\tau_{U}>t)\mu(\tau_{U}>s)|\leq 2(\Delta\mu(U)+\phi(\Delta-\kappa_{n}))\mu(\tau_{U}>t-\Delta).

This lemma provides us with an inequality that can be used to derive a more important inequality. In fact, in the proof of the Lemma
2.11
, this inequality plays a key role. See the proof in
[
DHY
, Lemma 4.2]
.
Before moving on to the next lemma, for the sake of simplicity, we write

(2.2)

\delta:=2(\Delta\mu(U)+\phi(\Delta-\kappa_{n})).

A useful observation is that since
\mu(U_{n})
decays either polynomially or exponentially as
n\to\infty
, then for sufficiently large
\Delta
, we have
\delta\leq 1
in both cases.

Lemma 2.11
.

[
DHY
, Lemma 4.2]
. Let
\mu
be right or left
\phi
-mixing for the partition
\mathcal{A}
. Let
0<\Delta<s<t
. Define
q=\lfloor\frac{s}{\delta}\rfloor
and
\eta=\frac{q}{q+1}
. Then for every
k\geq 3
such that
kq\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(2.3)

\mu(\tau_{U}>ks)\leq[\mu(\tau_{U}>s)+\delta^{\eta}]^{k-2}.

Now we have a relation for
\mu(\tau_{U}>ks)
which is obtained from the fact that the measure is
\phi
-mixing. This is the pivotal knowledge we require to accomplish our goal, it means calculating the limit of

\displaystyle L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{r}>s\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}))

for each
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Now we can proceed with the proof of this case. We use the idea used to prove
[
DHY
, Theorem A]
. Note that the notation
\mathcal{O}
in the expression
f=\mathcal{O}(g)
means that there exists constant
b>0
such that the inequality
\left|f\right|\leq bg
holds.

In (
2.3
), we set
(s_{n})_{n}
such that this sequence satisfies the conditions of Lemmas
2.9
since we need this lemma in the proof. Indeed, the left side of
L_{\alpha,s}(z)
can be obtained by finding a suitable substitution for

k
in (
2.3
).

So, we put
s_{n}=\frac{t}{\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon}}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, where
\epsilon\in(0,\min(\alpha,1))
. Also, we replace
k
by
\frac{1}{\mu(U_{n})^{\alpha-\epsilon}}
, then by Lemma
2.11
,

\frac{1}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>t\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}))=\frac{\mu(U_{n})^{-(\alpha-\epsilon)}-2}{\mu(U_{n})^{-(\alpha-\epsilon)}\cdot t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon})+\mathcal{O}(\delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}))\right).

Now, by dividing both sides by
\mu(U_{n})
and taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{t\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>t\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(U_{n})^{-(\alpha-\epsilon)}-2}{\mu(U_{n})^{-(\alpha-\epsilon)}\cdot t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}[-\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon})+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta}_{n}))]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left[\frac{\mu(U_{n})^{(\alpha-\epsilon)}}{\mu(U_{n})^{(\alpha-\epsilon)}}-2\mu(U_{n})^{(\alpha-\epsilon)}\right]\left[\frac{-\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}+\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}\right.

Thus,

(2.4)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{t\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>t\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}.

Now, it suffices to show that the second term on the right-hand side equals 0, then the remaining limit leads us to the desired result with the help of Lemmas
2.7
and
2.9
.

Suppose that
b\in(c,1)
, where
c
is the same as mentioned in (N
1
), such that
\epsilon>b
and
\Delta_{n}=\lfloor\mu(U_{n})^{-b}\rfloor\gg\kappa_{n}=o\left(\mu(U_{n})^{-c}\right)
. Note that, as we know,
\alpha>\epsilon
and
b>c
. Since we assume
\alpha>c
, suitable
\epsilon
and
b
exist.

Then we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta_{n}}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\eta_{n}}+\phi(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{\eta_{n}})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\eta_{n}})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\phi(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{\eta_{n}})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1+\eta_{n}}}{t}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\phi(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{n}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1+\eta_{n}}}{t}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{-m\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1-b+\eta_{n}}}{t}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(U_{n})^{bm\eta_{n}+\epsilon-1}}{t}.

Now, since
\epsilon>b
, we have
s_{n}=\frac{t}{\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon}}\gg\Delta_{n}=\lfloor\frac{1}{\mu(U_{n})^{b}}\rfloor
, then
q_{n}=\lfloor\frac{s_{n}}{\Delta_{n}}\rfloor\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. Consequently,
\eta_{n}=\frac{q_{n}}{q_{n+1}}\to 1
. So, the first term will tend to zero as
n\to\infty
.

To show that the second term tends to zero, the number
bm+\epsilon
must be strictly greater than one. This condition depends on
\alpha
. If
\alpha>1
, then according to the definition of
\epsilon
,
0<\epsilon<1
and can be chosen in a way that satisfies
bm+\epsilon>1
i.e., let
\epsilon=1-\delta^{\prime}
and
b=1-2\delta^{\prime}
for some
\delta^{\prime}>0
, then

\epsilon+mb=1-\delta^{\prime}+m(1-2\delta^{\prime})=1+m\left(1-2\delta^{\prime}-\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{m}\right),

hence,

\epsilon+mb>1\iff 1-2\delta^{\prime}-\frac{\delta^{\prime}}{m}>0\iff\frac{1}{2+\frac{1}{m}}>\delta^{\prime}.

So in the case
\alpha>1
, we can choose
\epsilon
such that
bm+\epsilon>1.

However, if
\alpha<1
choosing
\epsilon
to satisfy the condition may be problematic, as
\alpha
could be smaller than
1-mb
. Now, we want to determine how small
\alpha
it can be for condition
bm+\epsilon>1
to hold. In this case, we have
0<\epsilon<\alpha
. Now suppose that
\epsilon=\alpha-\delta^{\prime\prime}
and
b=\alpha-2\delta^{\prime\prime}
for some
\delta^{\prime\prime}>0
. Note that condition
b>c
holds, if
\alpha-2\delta^{\prime\prime}=b>c
, i.e.,
\delta^{\prime\prime}<\frac{\alpha-c}{2}
, so a suitable
\delta^{\prime\prime}
exists if
\alpha>c
. Now let

bm+\epsilon=(\alpha-2\delta^{\prime\prime})m+\alpha-\delta^{\prime\prime}=\alpha(m+1)-\delta^{\prime\prime}(2m+1).

Thus,

bm+\epsilon=\alpha(m+1)-\delta^{\prime\prime}(2m+1)>1\iff\delta^{\prime\prime}<\frac{\alpha(m+1)-1}{2m+1}\iff\alpha>\frac{1}{m+1}.

Since we assume
\alpha>\frac{1}{m+1}
, the second term also tends to zero, so

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(U_{n}\leq s_{n})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})},

since
s_{n}=\frac{t}{\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon}}
satisfies in the condition of Lemma
2.9
, we have

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\theta_{1}=1-\hat{\theta_{2}},

if
\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty
. In the case
\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty
by Lemma
2.7
,\hat{\theta_{2}}=0
so
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=1
.

So, we have reached the desired conclusion and the proof of Theorem
2.1
is complete.

3.
recurrence rate for Gibbs-Markov systems

Our goal in this section is to apply Theorem
2.1
to Gibbs-Markov systems. Before that, we give some definitions.

Let
T:\Omega\to\Omega
be a map, where
\Omega
is a compact metric space, and let
\mu
be an invariant probability measure on
\Omega
.

Definition 3.1
.

We call
T
a
Markov
map if:

(1)

There exists a countable measurable partition
\mathcal{A}
on
\Omega
such that for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
,
\mu(A)>0
,

(2)

T(A)
can be written as a union of elements in
\mathcal{A}
,

(3)

T:A\to T(A)
is bijective for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{n}=\bigvee^{n-1}_{i=0}T^{-i}\mathcal{A}
be as in Definition
1.4
and assume
\lambda\in(0,1)
. We define a metric on
\Omega
that depends on
\lambda
:

d_{\lambda}(x,y):=\lambda^{s(x,y)},

where
s(x,y)
is the largest position integer
n
such that
x
and
y
lie in the same
n
-cylinder.

Define the Jacobian
g=\frac{1}{\lvert detJT\rvert}=\frac{d\mu}{d\mu\circ T}
and
g_{k}(x)=g(x)g\circ T(x)\cdots g\circ T^{k-1}(x)
. Note that since
T
is injective,
JT
shows how the density of the measure changes under
T
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
T
be a Markov map. We call
T
as a
Gibbs-Markov
map with respect to
\mu
if:

(1)

Big image probability(BIP): There exists
C>0
such that
\mu(T(A))>C
for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
,

(2)

Distortion: The function
\log g\lvert_{A}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschits for all
A\in\mathcal{A}
i.e., there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that
d_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\log g\lvert_{A}(x),\log g\lvert_{A}(y)\right)\leq C^{\prime}d_{\lambda}(x,y)
.

Definition 3.3
.

We call
(T,\mu,\mathcal{A})
a
Gibbs-Markov system
if
T
is a Gibbs-Markov map with respect to
\mu
.

Notation 3.4
.

Let
A_{n}(x)\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
be the
n
-cylinder that contains the point
x
.

Remark 3.5
.

(1)

Distortion bound property. In view of BIP and distortion condition of Definition
3.2
, there exists
D_{1}>1
such that for all
x,y
in the same
n
-cylinder, we have the following distortion bound:

\left\lvert\frac{g_{n}(x)}{g_{n}(y)}-1\right\rvert\leq D_{1}\ d_{\lambda}\left(T^{n}(x),T^{n}(y)\right).

(2)

Gibbs property. For some constant
D_{2}
,

D_{2}^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu\left(A_{n}(x)\right)}{g_{n}(x)}\leq D_{2}.

The next lemma shows that Gibbs-Markov systems are exponentially
\phi
-mixing systems. The proof can be found in
[
MN
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

[
MN
, Lemma 2.4]
. Let
(T,\mu,\mathcal{A})
be a Gibbs-Markov system with respect to the partition
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\mu
is a
\phi
-mixing measure such that
\phi
decays exponentially, i.e.
\phi(k)\leq\zeta^{k}
for some
\zeta\in(0,1)
.

The main theorem of this section is the following.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(T,\mu,\mathcal{A})
be a Gibbs-Markov system. Assume that
(U_{n})_{n}
is a good neighbourhood system such that
\bigcap_{n}U_{n}=\{\Lambda\}
where
\Lambda
is a measure zero set. If
\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty
, then assume that
(\hat{\theta}_{l})_{l}
exists, and satisfies
\sum_{l}l\hat{\theta}_{l}<\infty
. Let
\alpha\in(c,\infty)
, where
c
as defined in (N
1
). Then for any
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\theta_{1}
exists and,

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty$}\\
\theta_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty$.}\end{cases}

Remark 3.8
.

Theorem
3.7
can also be stated in terms of
\theta
, which is defined in Remark
1.16
. Yang proved in
[
Y
, Proposition 5.4]
that under the conditions of Theorem
3.7
, we have
\theta=\theta_{1}
. If we want to state Theorem
3.7
in terms of
\theta
, then the existence of
(\hat{\theta}_{l})_{l}
and the condition
\sum_{l}l\hat{\theta}_{l}<\infty
are not required.

Remark 3.9
.

This result also holds for
\phi
-mixing systems with exponential decay. What can be said is that if the dynamical system mixes sufficiently quickly, then the condition on
\alpha
becomes simply
c<\alpha
.

Remark 3.10
.

The case
\alpha=\infty
is proved in
[
DHY
, Theorem B]
.

Remark 3.11
.

Theorem
3.7
holds for
\alpha=0
, the proof is the same as in Section
2.2
. Note that in this case, only the following conditions need to exist,
(\hat{\theta}_{l})_{l}
exists, and
\sum_{l}\hat{\theta}_{l}<\infty
where
\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty
.

The idea of proof of Theorem
3.7
is exactly the same as what we used in the proof of Section
2.3
. We need a lemma similar to the Lemma
2.9
for Gibbs-Markov systems. This lemma was proved by Davis, Haydn and Yang in
[
DHY
, Lemma 4.9]
.

Lemma 3.12
.

[
DHY
, Lemma 4.9]
. Let
(T,\mu,\mathcal{A})
be a Gibbs-Markov system. Assume that
(U_{n})_{n}
is a good neighbourhood system such that
\hat{\theta}_{l}(K)
exists for
K
large enough, and
\sum_{l}\hat{\theta}_{l}<\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s_{n})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}\to\theta_{1},

for any increasing sequence
(s_{n})_{n}
for which
s_{n}\mu(U_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Let
\alpha\in(c,\infty)
. Put
s_{n}=\frac{t}{\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon}}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\min(1,\alpha))
. Given Lemma
3.6
, we have
\phi(k)\leq\zeta^{k}
for some
\zeta\in(0,1)
. Therefore, we can deduce relation (
2.4
) for Gibbs-Markov systems. Thus,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{t\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>t\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha}))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}.

Now it is enough to show that second term tends to zero, then by Lemma
3.12
and Lemma
2.7
, we obtain the result.

Same as proof of Theorem
1.2
, suppose numbers
b\in(c,1)
such that
\epsilon>b
and
\Delta_{n}=\lfloor\mu(U_{n})^{-b}\rfloor\gg\kappa_{n}=o\left(\mu(U_{n})^{-c}\right)
, where
c
is the same as in (N
1
). We assume
\alpha>c
, to have allow chose
b
such that
\epsilon>b>c
. Now, we show
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}=0
as bellow:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta_{n}}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\eta_{n}}+\phi(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{\eta_{n}})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\Delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\eta_{n}})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\phi(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{\eta_{n}})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{n}^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1+\eta_{n}}}{t}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\phi(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n})^{\eta_{n}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\Delta_{n}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1+\eta_{n}}}{t}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\zeta^{(\Delta_{n}-\kappa_{n}){\eta_{n}}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}}{t}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1-b+\eta_{n}}}{t}+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\zeta^{\kappa_{n}{\eta_{n}}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}}{t}.

Since
\epsilon>b
,
\eta_{n}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
and the first term tends to zero. To show that the second term ternds to zero, as
\zeta^{\kappa_{n}}\to 0
and
\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, the condition is that the growth of
\zeta^{\kappa_{n}}
must be greater than
\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}
. Thus, we have,

\zeta^{\kappa_{n}}>\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}\quad\forall n\iff\frac{\kappa_{n}}{\log(\mu(U_{n}))}>\frac{\epsilon-1}{\log\zeta}\quad\forall n.

Since
\log(\mu(U_{n})
is a negative number we have,

\frac{\kappa_{n}}{\mu(U_{n})^{-c}}>\frac{\kappa_{n}}{\log(\mu(U_{n}))}>\frac{\epsilon-1}{\log\zeta}.

Now as
\kappa_{n}=o\left(\mu(U_{n})^{-c}\right)
, we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\kappa_{n}}{\mu(U_{n})^{-c}}=0
. Hence,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\zeta^{\kappa_{n}{\eta_{n}}}\mu(U_{n})^{\epsilon-1}}{t}=0\iff\epsilon<1.

Since
\epsilon\in(0,\min(1,\alpha))
, the above condition holds and then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{O}(\delta^{\eta_{n}}_{n})}{t\mu(U_{n})^{-\epsilon}\mu(U_{n})}=0,

so

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}\leq s_{n})}{s_{n}\mu(U_{n})}.

Since
(s_{n})_{n}
defined in a way that satisfies the conditions of Lemma
3.12
, therefore, considering this lemma as well as Lemma
2.7
, we have

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty$}\\
\theta_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty$.}\end{cases}

Therefore, the desired result is obtained, and the proof of the theorem is completed.∎

4.
recurrence rate and metric balls in a
\phi
-mixing system

In this section, we give applications of Theorem
2.1
. Using metric balls, we determine the recurrence rate in a metric space and find the limit
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)
. Indeed, we explain the concept of exceedance rate and then describe its connection to the recurrence rate.
Davis, Haydn and Yang have previously done in
[
DHY
, Section 5]
for the case
\alpha=\infty
(escape rate). This motivated us to derive the general formula in this space as well. So, the idea of proof of Theorem
4.4
is similar to the work done by
[
DHY
]
, and we achieve our desired result with modifications to their proof.

Assume that
\Omega
is a metric space,
T:\Omega\to\Omega
is a map on
\Omega
, and
\mu
is the probability measure. Let
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be a continuous function such that the maximal value of
g
is achieved on
\Lambda
, a measure zero closed set.

Such a function is called an
observable
in dynamical systems. Now, we consider the following process:

X_{0}:=g,\quad X_{1}:=g\circ T,\cdots,\ X_{k}:=g\circ T^{k},\ldots.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a non decreasing sequence of real numbers. We consider
u_{n}
as a sequence of thresholds and events
\{X_{n}>u_{n}\}
represents an instance where
X_{k}
exceeds the threshold
u_{n}
. Define

(4.1)

U_{n}:=\{X_{0}>u_{n}\}

as the open sets. Since
U_{n+1}\subset U_{n}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu(U_{n})\to 0,

(U_{n})_{n}

Now, if we put

Proof of Theorem

Note that for

Fix

\mu\left(\mu(V_{n})^{\alpha}\tau_{W_{n}}>s\right)\leq\mu\left(\mu(U_{n})^{\alpha}\tau_{U_{n}}>s\right)\leq\mu\left(\mu(W_{n})^{\alpha}\tau_{V_{n}}>s\right).

Now by Remark
4.11
for
\alpha\in[0,1)
we have,

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{s\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{V_{n}}>s\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)\leq\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-1}{s\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{W_{n}}>s\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha})\right),

and similarly for
\alpha\in[1,\infty)
one can obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{s\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{V_{n}}>s\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)\leq\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n}))^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{-1}{s\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{W_{n}}>s\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha})\right).

As
U_{n}^{-}\subset V_{n}
,
W_{n}\subset U_{n}^{+}
, we have
W_{n}\setminus V_{n}\subset U_{n}^{+}\setminus U_{n}^{-}
so by assumption

, we have
\mu(V_{n})=\mu(W_{n})(1+o(1))
.
So, for
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
,

\mu(V_{n})^{\alpha}=\mu(W_{n})^{\alpha}(1+o(1))^{\alpha}\quad as\quad n\to\infty,

\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}=\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}(1+o(1))^{1-\alpha}\quad as\quad n\to\infty.

Thus, we have:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{V_{n}}>s\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{V_{n}}>s\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha})\right),

and also,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{W_{n}}>s\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{W_{n}}>s\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha})\right).

Similarly, for both bounds in case
\alpha\in[1,\infty)
, as
n\to\infty
, we can replace
\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha}
with
\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha}
and
\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}
with
\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}
.
Thus, in both cases of
\alpha
, we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{V_{n}}>s\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(U_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{U_{n}}>s\mu(U_{n})^{-\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log\left(\mu(\tau_{W_{n}}>s\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha})\right).

Since
(V_{n})_{n}
and
(W_{n})_{n}
are good neighbourhood systems by Proposition
4.12
, if for the case
\alpha<1
we have
\alpha>\max\{\frac{1}{m+1},c^{\prime}\}
, then we can apply Theorem
2.1
for two bounds in (
3.3
), so for all

we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(V_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{V_{n}}>s\mu(V_{n})^{-\alpha}))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty$}\\
\theta_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty$,}\end{cases}

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{-1}{s\mu(W_{n})^{1-\alpha}}\log(\mu(\tau_{W_{n}}>s\mu(W_{n})^{-\alpha}))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})\to\infty$}\\
\theta_{1}&\text{if $\pi_{ess}(U_{n})<\infty$.}\end{cases}

By Lemma

g(x)=-\log(d(x,\Lambda))
be an observable from
\mathbb{T}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}
.

Note that the Jacobian matrix of
T
,
DT
, has two unit eigenvectors
\nu^{+}
and
\nu^{-}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{+}=\lambda>1
and
\lambda_{-}=\frac{1}{\lambda}<1
. If
\Lambda
is aligend with the unstable direction, we lift
\Lambda
to
\hat{\Lambda}
on a fundamental domain of the cover
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and write
\hat{\Lambda}=\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{+}
,
t_{1}\in[0,l(\Lambda]
,
\hat{p_{1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus,
\Lambda=\pi(\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{+})
, where
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}^{2}
is the projection. We write the endpoint of
\hat{\Lambda}
as
\hat{p}_{2}
, i.e.
\hat{p}_{2}=\hat{p_{1}}+l(\Lambda)\nu^{+}
. Similarly if
\Lambda
is aligend with the stable direction, we lift
\Lambda
to
\hat{\Lambda}
on a fundamental domain of the cover
\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and write
\Lambda=\pi(\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{-})
, and the endpoint of
\hat{\Lambda}
as
\hat{p}_{2}=\hat{p_{1}}+l(\Lambda)\nu^{-}
.

Assume that
(u_{n})_{n}
is a non decreasing sequence of real numbers. Let
U_{n}=\{x:g(x)>u_{n}\}
. If we consider
\delta_{n}=e^{-u_{n}}
, then we have

B_{\delta_{n}}(y)=\{x:d(x,y)<e^{-u_{n}}\}=\{x:\log d(x,y)>u_{n}\}=U_{n}.

The approximation of
U_{n}
for
r_{n}=\delta_{n}^{2}=e^{-2u_{n}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
are
U_{n}^{-}=U_{n}\setminus\overline{\{x:d(x,y)<e^{-u_{n}}\}}
and
U_{n}^{+}=\bigcup_{y\in U_{n}}\{x:d(x,y)<e^{-u_{n}}\}
. Since
\mu
is a Lebesgue measure, so it is straightforward to verify

\mu(U_{n}^{+}\setminus U_{n}^{-})=o(1)\mu(U_{n}).

Due to hyperbolicity of
T
, there exists
C>0
such that diam
(\mathcal{A}^{n})<C\lambda^{-n}
. So, if we consider
\kappa_{n}=\lfloor\frac{\ln C+2u_{n}}{\ln\lambda}\rfloor+1=\mathcal{O}(\log n)
, then we have

\displaystyle diam(\mathcal{A}^{\kappa_{n}})\leq diam(\mathcal{A}^{\frac{\ln C+2u_{n}}{\ln\lambda}+1})

\displaystyle<C\lambda^{-\left(\frac{\ln C+2u_{n}}{\ln\lambda}\right)-1}

\displaystyle=C\lambda^{\frac{\ln C^{-1}}{\ln\lambda}}\lambda^{\frac{-2u_{n}}{\ln\lambda}}\lambda^{-1}

\displaystyle=C\frac{1}{C}e^{-2u_{n}}\lambda^{-1}<e^{-2u_{n}}=r_{n}.

Hence diam
(\mathcal{A}^{\kappa_{n}})<r_{n}
. As
\mu(U_{n})=\mu(B_{\delta_{n}}(\Lambda))
, we have

\mu(U_{n})\lesssim e^{-u_{n}}l(\Lambda)=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n}).

Since
\mathcal{O}(\log n)\gg\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
as
n\to\infty
, we have
\kappa_{n}\mu(U_{n})^{c^{\prime}}\to 0
for any
c^{\prime}\in(0,1).

Now that the conditions of Theorem
4.4
are satisfied, according to Remark
4.6
, we can state the following theorem.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(\mathbb{T}^{2},T,\mu)
be the cat map and assume
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
. Then for all
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

(1)

if
\Lambda
is not aligend with the stable direction or the unstable direction then
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=1
,

(2)

if
\Lambda
is aligend with the unstable direction but
\{\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{+},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\}
has no periodic points, then
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=1
,

(3)

if
\Lambda
is aligend with the stable direction but
\{\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{-},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\}
has no periodic points, then
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=1
,

(4)

if
\Lambda
is aligned with the stable or unstable direction and
\Lambda
contains a periodic point with prim period
p
, then
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=1-\lambda^{-p}
,

(5)

if
\Lambda
is aligend with the unstable direction and
\Lambda
has no periodic points but
\{\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{+},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\}
contains a periodic point of prime period
p
, then

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\Lambda\cap T^{-p}\Lambda=\emptyset$,}\\
1-\lambda^{-p}\frac{|\hat{p}_{2}|}{l(\Lambda)}&\text{if $\Lambda\cap T^{-p}\Lambda\neq\emptyset$.}\end{cases}

(6)

if
\Lambda
is aligend with the stable direction and
\Lambda
has no periodic points but
\{\hat{p_{1}}+t_{1}\nu^{-},t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\}
contains a periodic point of prime period
p
, then

L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\Lambda\cap T^{-p}\Lambda=\emptyset$,}\\
1-\lambda^{-p}\frac{|\hat{p}_{2}|}{l(\Lambda)}&\text{if $\Lambda\cap T^{-p}\Lambda\neq\emptyset$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

To prove 1, first we need to calculate
\theta
. This is done in the proof of
[
DHY
, Theorem 8.3]
and is equal to one. Now based on Theorem
4.4
, Remark
4.6
and Remark
4.7
, we have
L_{\alpha,s}(\Lambda)=1
.

For proving case 2 to 6, we can use a similar approach to what is done for 1. That is, calculating
\theta
using the proof of case 2 to 6 of
[
CHN
, Theorem 2.1]
, and then concluding with Theorem
4.4
, Remarks
4.6
and Remark
4.7
.
∎

SOBOLEV INEQUALITIES ON KÄHLER MANIFOLDS
Sayantan Chakraborty

Abstract




ABSTRACT. We prove new Sobolev type inequalities on compact Kähler manifolds with positive Ricci curvature. A proof of an already existing Sobolev inequality in the classical Bidaut-Véron and Véron approach is also discussed.

1
Introduction

We consider the class of closed Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
of dimension
m
with
\text{Ric}(g)\geq(m-1)g
. For these manifolds, we have the following Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\phi|^{q+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}\leq\frac{q-1}{m}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\Bigg)+\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}\phi^{2}

for
1<q\leq\frac{m+2}{m-2}
. This was proved by Bidaut-Véron and Véron in [1].

The constant
\frac{q-1}{m}
cannot be improved in this class of Riemannian manifolds since it is optimal for spheres (see for instance [2]).

Kähler manifolds are a special class of Riemannian manifolds with three mutually compatible structures: a complex structure, a Riemannian structure and a symplectic structure. Due to this special feature, improvements of some results for Riemannian manifolds are possible in the Kähler setting. We refer interested readers to [3, 7, 8, 4, 5, 9, 10] and references therein for interesting comparison theorems involving eigenvalues, volume and other geometric quantities.

Baudoin and Munteanu in their paper [6] improved the constant
\frac{q-1}{m}
in the Kähler setting for the range
1<q<\frac{m+2}{m-2}
where
m=\text{dim}_{\mathbb{R}}M
. They showed the following:

Theorem 1.
(Baudoin-Munteanu [6]).
Let

(M,\omega)

be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension

n

with

\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
.
For any

1<q<\frac{n+1}{n-1}

we have the Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\phi|^{q+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}\leq\frac{C_{S}}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}\phi^{2}

for any

\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)

where

\displaystyle C_{S}=(q-1)\Bigg(\frac{2n+q+2-2\sqrt{(n+1)(n+1-(n-1)q)}}{2qn}\Bigg).

Let us set the notation used throughout this note. Suppose
(M,\omega)
is a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n
with complex structure
J
. If
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{2n}
is an orthonormal frame such that
e_{2i}=J(e_{2i-1})
for
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
then we work in the following unitary frame

\displaystyle X_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2i-1}-\sqrt{-1}e_{2i}),\,X_{\bar{i}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2i-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2i})

for
i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
. These satisfy
\{X_{k},X_{\bar{l}}\}=\delta_{kl}
and
\{X_{k},X_{l}\}=0
. The Ricci curvature condition is then equivalent to
R_{i\bar{j}}\geq\delta_{i\bar{j}}
. The complex Laplace operator is given by
\Box u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i\bar{i}}
. The real Laplace operator
\Delta
relates to this as
\Delta=2\Box
. We also have
|\partial u|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}u_{\bar{i}}=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
.

Remark:
Let
f
be an eigenfunction for the first nonzero Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the complex Laplace operator
\Box
on
M
, i.e
-\Box f=\lambda_{1}f
. Let us set
\phi=1+tf
in theorem 1. We aim to compare the coefficient of
t^{2}
on both sides of the inequality. Let

\displaystyle\psi(t)=\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|1+tf|^{q+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}.

Then near
t=0
we have

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{2}{\text{Vol}(M)}\Bigg(\int_{M}(1+tf)^{q}f\Bigg)\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}(1+tf)^{q+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{q+1}-1}

and

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{2}{\text{Vol}(M)}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}(1+tf)^{q+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{q+1}-2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\Bigg((1-q)\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\Big(\int_{M}(1+tf)^{q}f\Big)^{2}+\Big(q\int_{M}(1+tf)^{q-1}f^{2}\Big)\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}(1+tf)^{q+1}\Bigg)\Bigg).

In particular the coefficient of
t^{2}
on the left hand side of the inequality is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{q}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}f^{2}

where we use the fact that
\int_{M}f=-\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}}\int_{M}\Box f=0
.

The right hand side of the inequality with
\phi=1+tf
is

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\frac{t^{2}C_{S}}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}+\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}(1+tf)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2t^{2}C_{S}}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\partial f|^{2}+\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}(1+tf)^{2}.

We have
\int_{M}|\partial f|^{2}=-\int_{M}f\Box f=\lambda_{1}\int_{M}f^{2}
. Therefore the coeffient of
t^{2}
on the right hand side is

\displaystyle\frac{2C_{S}\lambda_{1}+1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}f^{2}.

Comparing these coeffients we get

\displaystyle 2C_{S}\lambda_{1}+1\geq q\Rightarrow\lambda_{1}\geq\frac{q-1}{2C_{S}}.

On the class of Kähler manifolds with
\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
, we have an improved Lichnerowicz estimate
\lambda_{1}\geq 1
(see for example Futaki [11, Theorem 2.4.5]). For this reason we conjecture the following:

Conjecture:

C_{S}
can be improved to
\frac{q-1}{2}
in theorem 1.

There are a lot of Kähler manifolds with
\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
and
\lambda_{1}=1
. Some examples of such manifolds are

\displaystyle(M,\omega)=(\mathbb{CP}^{k},\omega_{\mathbb{CP}^{k}})\times(\mathbb{CP}^{l},(1-a)\omega_{\mathbb{CP}^{l}}).

where
k
,
l
are positive integers with
k+l=n
and
a\in[0,1)
. In fact, any Kähler-Einstein Fano manifold admitting a nontrivial holomorphic vector field has
\lambda_{1}=1
[12]. This means that
\frac{q-1}{2}
is the best possible candidate for
C_{S}
.

Theorem 1 can be proven using the standard approach of working with an associated PDE. Namely, we consider the functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u)=\frac{\int_{M}|\partial u|^{2}+\lambda\int_{M}u^{2}}{\Big(\int_{M}|u|^{q+1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}},\,u\in W^{1,2}(M)\backslash\{0\}.

The first variation in the direction of
\dot{u}
is

\displaystyle 2\Bigg[\frac{\int_{M}\langle\partial u,\partial\dot{u}\rangle+\lambda\int_{M}u\dot{u}}{\Big(\int_{M}|u|^{q+1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}}-\frac{\int_{M}|u|^{q}\dot{u}}{\Big(\int_{M}|u|^{q+1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q+1}+1}}\Big(\int_{M}|\partial u|^{2}+\lambda\int_{M}u^{2}\Big)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Big(\int_{M}|u|^{q+1}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}}\Bigg[\int_{M}(-\dot{u}\Box{u}+\lambda u\dot{u})-\frac{\int_{M}|\partial u|^{2}+\lambda\int_{M}u^{2}}{\int_{M}|u|^{q+1}}\int_{M}|u|^{q}\dot{u}\Bigg].

Thus a positive
u
is a critical point of
\mathcal{F}
iff

\displaystyle-\Box u+\lambda u=cu^{q}

on
M
, with
c=\frac{\int_{M}|\partial u|^{2}+\lambda\int_{M}u^{2}}{\int_{M}|u|^{q+1}}
. The unknown function can be rescaled so that
c=1
.

Therefore theorem 1 follows from the next theorem.

Theorem A.
(Baudoin-Munteanu [6]).
Let

(M,\omega)

be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension

n

and

\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
.
If

0<\lambda<\frac{1}{2C_{S}}
, then the only positive solutions of

\displaystyle-\Box u+\lambda u=u^{q}

are positive constant functions.

We plan to have an alternate proof of theorem A in the next section. Our style of proving it is in the flavour of the Bidaut-Véron and Véron approach.

Remark:
While discussing the proof of theorem A in the next section, we explain at the end of lemma 2.3 why we fail to get
C_{S}=\frac{q-1}{2}
using the coefficients obtained there. The coefficients also point us to the fact that just using an integral identity using the complex Hessian doesn’t help get a better Sobolev constant (as opposed to the proof of
\lambda_{1}\geq 1
for Kähler manifolds with
\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
in [4] for example, which uses an integral involving only the complex Hessian). Therefore we need some support from an integral identity (lemma 4 in the next section) using the other part of the Hessian.

In this note, we prove a similar PDE statement where the constant
\lambda
involves the first Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the complex Laplace operator
\Box
on a compact Kähler manifold with positive Ricci lower bound. The following is the main result of the paper:

Theorem B.

Let

(M,\omega)

be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension

n

and

\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
.
If

0<\lambda<\frac{1}{2C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}}
, then the only positive solutions of

\displaystyle-\Box u+\lambda u=u^{q}

are positive constant functions. Here the constant

C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}

is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}}=\frac{2}{q-1}\Bigg(\Bigg(1-\frac{(n+(n-1)k)(kn+n-1)q}{(4n^{2}+4n+q)k}\Bigg)\lambda_{1}+\frac{qn(kn+n-1)}{(4n^{2}+4n+q)k}\Bigg)

where

k

is any number lying in the interval

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}-\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}\sqrt{1-\frac{(n-1)q}{n+1}}-1,\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}+\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}\sqrt{1-\frac{(n-1)q}{n+1}}-1\Bigg].

We note that the coeffient of
\lambda_{1}
in the expression for
\frac{1}{C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}}
is nonnegative if
k
lies in the above interval.

As a corollary we obtain the following Sobolev inequality:

Theorem 2.

Let

(M,\omega)

be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension

n

and

\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
.
For any

1\leq q<\frac{n+1}{n-1}

we have the Sobolev inequality

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\phi|^{q+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{q+1}}\leq\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}\phi^{2}+\frac{C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}

for any

\phi\in C^{\infty}(M)
.
Here

C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}

is as in
theorem B
and

k

is any number lying in the interval

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}-\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}\sqrt{1-\frac{(n-1)q}{n+1}}-1,\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}+\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}\sqrt{1-\frac{(n-1)q}{n+1}}-1\Bigg].

Remark:
When
k=\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}-\frac{2(n+1)}{q(n-1)}\sqrt{1-\frac{(n-1)q}{n+1}}-1
, the coefficient of
\lambda_{1}
vanishes and we get
C_{S,\lambda_{1},k}=C_{S}
where
C_{S}
is the known Sobolev constant for Kähler manifolds with
\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
. So this inequality can be viewed as a generalized Sobolev inequality involving
\lambda_{1}
.

2
Proof of theorem A

Let
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
be a positive solution of

\displaystyle-\Box u+\lambda u=u^{q}.

We set
u=v^{-\beta}
,
v>0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
. Then
v
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle\Box v=\frac{1}{\beta}v^{\beta+1-\beta q}-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}v+(\beta+1)\frac{|\partial v|^{2}}{v}.

(*)

For calculations we omit the summation notation as this will always be clear. Theorem A will be proven in steps. Here is our first lemma.

Lemma 2.1
: Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold satisfying
\text{Ric}(\omega)\geq\omega
. For
\gamma\geq 0
and a positive
v\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}|^{2}\leq\gamma(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}v_{i\bar{j}}+2\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}|^{2}=\int_{M}v^{\gamma}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}v_{ij}=-\int_{M}(v^{\gamma}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}})_{j}v_{i}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j},j}v_{i}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(-v_{j\bar{j},\bar{i}}+R_{j\bar{i}l\bar{j}}v_{\bar{l}})v_{i}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}v_{i}v_{j}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)_{\bar{i}}v_{i}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}R_{l\bar{i}}v_{i}v_{\bar{l}}

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}v_{i}v_{j}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{M}(v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}v_{j})_{\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}v_{\bar{j}}v_{i}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}v_{j\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}v_{i\bar{j}}+2\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}.

Here in the process we have integrated by parts repeatedly. We have used the Ricci identities while going from the 4th to the 5th expression and the Ricci curvature condition while going from the 6th to the 7th expression.
∎

We plan to write an inequality that uses lemma 2.1 and introduces an additional parameter. Before doing that we need some calculations.

Lemma 2.2
: Suppose
v
is the function satisfying (*). Then for
\gamma>0
we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}+(\beta+1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}.

(1)

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}=\frac{\beta q-\gamma}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}+\frac{\lambda(\gamma-\beta)}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+(\beta+1)^{2}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}.

(2)

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{M}(\Box v)^{2}v^{\gamma}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v-\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{M}|v_{i\bar{j}}|^{2}v^{\gamma}.

(3)

Proof.

Using the equation satisfied by
\Box v
we have

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v

\displaystyle=\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}v^{\beta+1-\beta q}-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}v+(\beta+1)\frac{|\partial v|^{2}}{v}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}+(\beta+1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}.

This proves (1). For the second equality we compute

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}v^{\gamma}\Box v\Big(\frac{1}{\beta}v^{\beta+1-\beta q}-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}v+(\beta+1)\frac{|\partial v|^{2}}{v}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma+1-\beta q}\Box v-\frac{\lambda}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma+1}\Box v+(\beta+1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}\Box v|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta q-\beta-\gamma-1}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}+\frac{\lambda(\gamma+1)}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,+(\beta+1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}\Box v|\partial v|^{2}.

Using the first equality we get

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta q-\beta-\gamma-1}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}+\frac{\lambda(\gamma+1)}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\frac{(\beta+1)}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}-\frac{(\beta+1)\lambda}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}+(\beta+1)^{2}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta q-\gamma}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}+\frac{\lambda(\gamma-\beta)}{\beta}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}+(\beta+1)^{2}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}.

This proves (2).

For (3) we compute

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int_{M}|v_{i\bar{j}}|^{2}v^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{j\bar{i}}v^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}(v_{i\bar{j}}v^{\gamma})_{j}v_{\bar{i}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}v_{i\bar{j},j}v^{\gamma}v_{\bar{i}}-\gamma\int_{M}v_{i\bar{j}}v^{\gamma-1}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}v_{j\bar{j},i}v^{\gamma}v_{\bar{i}}-\gamma\int_{M}v_{i\bar{j}}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}(\Box v)_{i}v_{\bar{i}}v^{\gamma}-\gamma\int_{M}v_{i\bar{j}}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(\Box v)^{2}v^{\gamma}+\gamma\int_{M}(\Box v)v_{\bar{i}}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}-\gamma\int_{M}v_{i\bar{j}}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(\Box v)^{2}v^{\gamma}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}(\Box v)-\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{M}(\Box v)^{2}v^{\gamma}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v-\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{M}|v_{i\bar{j}}|^{2}v^{\gamma}.

∎

Here is the lemma involving the additional parameter.

Lemma 2.3
For the function
v
satisfying (*) and
\gamma>0,a\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big|v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}+a\frac{v_{\bar{i}}v_{\bar{j}}}{v}\Big|^{2}v^{\gamma}\leq A_{1}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+B_{1}\int_{M}v^{\beta+\gamma-\beta q}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+C_{1}\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}+D_{1}\int_{M}|v_{i\bar{j}}|^{2}v^{\gamma}

where

\displaystyle A_{1}=\gamma(\gamma-1)-2a(\gamma-1)+a^{2}+(3\gamma-4a)(\beta+1)+\Big(2-\frac{2a}{\gamma}\Big)(\beta+1)^{2}

\displaystyle B_{1}=\frac{3\gamma-4a}{\beta}+\Big(2-\frac{2a}{\gamma}\Big)\Big(\frac{\beta q-\gamma}{\beta}\Big)

\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{(4a-3\gamma)\lambda}{\beta}+\Big(2-\frac{2a}{\gamma}\Big)\frac{\lambda(\gamma-\beta)}{\beta}-1

\displaystyle D_{1}=\frac{2a}{\gamma}-1.

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big|v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}+a\frac{v_{\bar{i}}v_{\bar{j}}}{v}\Big|^{2}v^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}|^{2}+a\int_{M}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}v_{i}v_{j}v^{\gamma-1}+a\int_{M}v_{ij}v_{\bar{i}}v_{\bar{j}}v^{\gamma-1}+a^{2}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}.

(4)

We have

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}(v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}v_{j})_{\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}}-(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}v_{\bar{j}}v_{i}v_{j}v_{\bar{i}}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i}\Box vv_{\bar{i}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}-(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v.

We note that all the integrals appearing in the last line are real numbers. Therefore the second and third terms in the right hand side of (4) are real and equal. Hence (4) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|v_{\bar{i}\bar{j}}|^{2}-2a\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}-2a(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}

\displaystyle-2a\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v+a^{2}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}.

By lemma 2.1 and part (3) of lemma 2.2 this expression is less than or equal to

\displaystyle\gamma(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}v_{i\bar{j}}+2\gamma\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}-2a\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}v_{i\bar{j}}v_{\bar{i}}v_{j}-2a(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,-2a\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v+a^{2}\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\gamma-1)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+(\gamma-2a)\Bigg(\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{M}(\Box v)^{2}v^{\gamma}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v-\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{M}|v_{i\bar{j}}|^{2}v^{\gamma}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,+2(\gamma-a)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v+(a^{2}-2a(\gamma-1))\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+\int_{M}v^{\gamma}(\Box v)^{2}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\gamma(\gamma-1)-2a(\gamma-1)+a^{2})\int_{M}v^{\gamma-2}|\partial v|^{4}+(3\gamma-4a)\int_{M}v^{\gamma-1}|\partial v|^{2}\Box v

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\Big(2-\frac{2a}{\gamma}\Big)\int_{M}(\Box v)^{2}v^{\gamma}+\Big(\frac{2a}{\gamma}-1\Big)\int_{M}|v_{i\bar{j}}|^{2}v^{\gamma}-\int_{M}v^{\gamma}|\partial v|^{2}.

Using equations (1) and (2) of lemma 2.2, this expression becomes

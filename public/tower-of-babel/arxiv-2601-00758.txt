Rational codegree Turán density of hypergraphs
Jun Gao, Oleg Pikhurko, Mingyuan Rong, Shumin Sun

Abstract
Let H H be a k k -graph (i.e. a k k -uniform hypergraph). Its minimum codegree δ k − 1 ​ ( H ) \delta_{k-1}(H) is the largest integer t t such that every ( k − 1 ) (k-1) -subset of V ⁡ ( H ) V(H) is contained in at least t t edges of H H . The codegree Turán density γ ⁡ ( ℱ ) \gamma(\mathcal{F}) of a family ℱ \mathcal{F} of k k -graphs is the infimum of γ > 0 \gamma>0 such that every k k -graph H H on n → ∞ n\to\infty vertices with δ k − 1 ​ ( H ) ≥ ( γ + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ n \delta_{k-1}(H)\geq(\gamma+o(1))\,n contains some member of ℱ \mathcal{F} as a subgraph. We prove that, for every integer k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and every rational number α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) , there exists a finite family of k k -graphs ℱ \mathcal{F} such that γ ⁡ ( ℱ ) = α \gamma(\mathcal{F})=\alpha . Also, for every k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 , we establish a strong version of non-principality, namely that there are two k k -graphs F 1 F_{1} and F 2 F_{2} such that the codegree Turán density of { F 1 , F 2 } \{F_{1},F_{2}\} is strictly smaller than that of each F i F_{i} . This answers a question of Mubayi and Zhao [ J Comb Theory (A) 114 (2007) 1118–1132].

Introduction

For an integer
k\geq 2
, a
k
-uniform hypergraph
(in short,
k
-graph
)
H
consists of a vertex set
V(H)
and an edge set
E(H)\subseteq\binom{V(H)}{k}
, that is, every edge is a
k
-element subset of
V(H)
. Given a (possibly infinite) family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs, the
Turán number
of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
, is defined as the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
k
-graph containing no element of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph. The Turán density
\pi(\mathcal{F})
is defined as

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathcal{F})}{\binom{n}{k}},

a limit that exists by a standard averaging argument. Turán problems for hypergraphs are notoriously difficult, and our knowledge is quite limited even for seemingly simple cases, for example when
\mathcal{F}
forbids the complete
3
-graph on
4
vertices. For more background, we refer the reader to the surveys
[
10
,
18
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the variant called
codegree Turán density
, introduced by Mubayi and Zhao
[
14
]
.
Given a
k
-graph
H
and a
(k-1)
-vertex subset
S\subseteq V(H)
, let
d_{H}(S)
(or
d(S)
) denote the number of edges of
H
containing
S
as a subset. Let
N_{H}(S)
(or
N(S)
) denote the
neighbourhood
of
S
, i.e.,
N_{H}(S)\coloneqq\{v\in V(H)\ :S\cup\{v\}\in E(H)\}
.
The
minimum codegree

\delta_{k-1}(H)
of
H
is defined as the minimum of
d(S)
over all
(k-1)
-vertex subsets
S
of
V(H)
. Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs, the
codegree Turán number

\mathrm{co\mbox{-}ex}(n,\mathcal{F})
is the maximum value of
\delta_{k-1}(H)
that an
n
-vertex
\mathcal{F}
-free
k
-graph
H
can admit, and the
codegree Turán density
of
\mathcal{F}
is defined as

\gamma(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{co\mbox{-}ex}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n},

where the limit is known to exist for any
k
-graph family
\mathcal{F}
, see
[
14
, Proposition 1.2]
.
When
\mathcal{F}=\{F\}
is a single
k
-graph, we often omit curly brackets and write
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
,
\pi(F)
,
\mathrm{co\mbox{-}ex}(n,F)
and
\gamma(F)
instead.

For each
k\geq 2
, let

\Pi_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\pi(\mathcal{F})\colon\text{$\mathcal{F}$ is a family of $k$-graphs}\right\}\subseteq[0,1).

The celebrated Erdős–Simonovits–Stone Theorem
[
4
,
5
]
implies that
\pi(\mathcal{F})=\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}1-\frac{1}{\chi(F)-1}
for any graph family
\mathcal{F}
, where
\chi(F)
denotes the chromatic number of
F
. This means
\Pi_{2}=\{0,\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3},\dots,\frac{r-1}{r},\dots\}
. The well-ordered property of
\Pi_{2}
motivates the following definition: we call a real number
a\in[0,1)
a
jump
for
k
if there exists
\delta>0
such that no family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs satisfies
\pi(\mathcal{F})\in(a,a+\delta)
. Then every real number in
[0,1)
is a jump for
k=2
. For general uniformity
k
, the study of
\Pi_{k}
has a long and rich history, tracing back to the famous Erdős jumping conjecture (see e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
1
,
17
,
3
]
).

Mubayi and Zhao
[
14
]
initiated the study of the analogous problem for codegree Turán density. Similarly, for
k\geq 2
, let

\Gamma_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\gamma(\mathcal{F})\colon\text{$\mathcal{F}$ is a family of $k$-graphs}\right\}\subseteq[0,1).

For the case
k=2
, it follows from the degree version of Erdős–Simonovits–Stone Theorem that
\Gamma_{2}=\{0,\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{3},\dots,\frac{r-1}{r},\dots\}
. In their paper, Mubayi and Zhao
[
14
, Theorem 1.6]
proved that for the case
k\geq 3
,

\Gamma_{k}
contains no jump; that is,
\Gamma_{k}
is dense in
[0,1)
. They further conjectured that
\Gamma_{k}=[0,1)
, i.e. for every
0\leq\alpha<1
, there exists a family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs such that
\gamma(\mathcal{F})=\alpha
.
Note that in their conjecture, the family is allowed to be infinite.
In fact, if we restrict attention to finite families only, the conjecture
becomes trivially false, since the collection of finite families of
k
-graphs (up to isomorphisms)
is countable, whereas the set of real numbers in the interval
[0,1)
is uncountable.
Our first result verifies their conjecture for each rational number
\alpha\in[0,1)
by providing a finite family of
k
-graphs; its proof is inspired by the proof of Theorem 1.6 in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
k\geq 3
. For every rational number
\alpha\in[0,1)
, there exists a finite family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs such that
\gamma(\mathcal{F})=\alpha
.

It is natural to conjecture that every rational value in
[0,1)
can be realized
as the codegree Turán density of a single
k
-graph.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Fix
k\geq 3
. For every rational number
\alpha\in[0,1)
, there exists a
k
-graph
F
such that
\gamma(F)=\alpha
.

At present, only a few sporadic examples of
3
-graphs with non-zero codegree Turán density are known.
In particular,

\gamma(K_{4}^{(3)-})=\frac{1}{4}

[
6
]
, where
K_{4}^{(3)-}
denotes the complete
3
-graph on four vertices
with one edge removed;

\gamma(F_{3,2})=\frac{1}{2}

[
15
]
, where
F_{3,2}
is the Fano plane;
and
\gamma(C_{3}^{\ell})=\frac{1}{3}
for all
\ell\geq 10
with
3\nmid\ell

[
11
,
16
]
,
where
C_{3}^{\ell}
is the tight
3
-uniform cycle of length
\ell
. Also, Keevash and Zhao
[
9
]
constructed, for every pair of integers
k\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
,
a
k
-graph whose codegree Turán density is equal to
\frac{r-1}{r}
.

We call a family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs

if its Turán density is strictly less than the density of each member. When
k=2
, Erdős–Simonovits–Stone Theorem implies that no such family exists. Motivated by exploring the difference between graphs and hypergraphs, Mubayi and Rödl
[
13
]
conjectured that non-principal families exist for
k\geq 3
. Balogh
[
2
]
confirmed this conjecture by constructing a finite non-principal family of
3
-graphs. Later, Mubayi and Pikhurko
[
12
]
extended this result by constructing a non-principal
k
-graph family of size two for every
k\geq 3
. Mubayi and Zhao
[
14
]
proved a similar result for
\gamma
, showing that there exists a finite family
\mathcal{F}
of
k
-graphs such that
0<\gamma(\mathcal{F})<\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\gamma(F)
. Parallel to the situation for
\pi
, they
[
14
, Page 1131]
posed the problem of constructing two
k
-graphs
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
such
that
0<\gamma(\{F_{1},F_{2}\})<\min\{\gamma(F_{1}),\gamma(F_{2})\}
. Sudakov (see
[
14
, Page 1131]
) observed that, for even
k\geq 4
, such a construction can be derived using the framework of
[
12
]
. Our next result settles this question for all
k\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
k\geq 3
, there exist
k
-graphs
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
such that

0<\gamma(\{F_{1},F_{2}\})<\min\{\gamma(F_{1}),\gamma(F_{2})\}.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need to construct a finite family of
k
-graphs
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
for each rational number
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that
\gamma(\mathcal{F}_{\alpha})=\alpha
. The following definition will be used to construct our family of
k
-graphs.

Definition 2.1

(
(s,t)
-extension)
.

Let
s
and
t
be integers with
s\geq t\geq 1
.
For a
k
-graph
F
, let

be the family of
k
-graphs constructed as follows. Let
\mathcal{A}(F)=\binom{V(F)}{k-1}
denote the family of all
(k-1)
-subsets of

\mathcal{T}(F)=\binom{\mathcal{A}(F)}{s}
be the collection of all
s
-subsets of
\mathcal{A}(F)
. Enumerate
\mathcal{T}(F)=\{T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{r}\}
with

.
Furthermore, define

\mathcal{P}(F)\coloneqq\{(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r}):P_{i}\subseteq T_{i}\text{ and }|P_{i}|=t\text{ for each }i\in[r]\}.

Thus
|\mathcal{P}(F)|=\binom{s}{t}^{r}
. For each
(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r})\in\mathcal{P}(F)
, let
F(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r})
be the
k
-graph obtained from
F
by adding
r
distinct new vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{r}
and, for each
i\in[r]
, adding all edges of the form
\{v_{i}\}\cup Y
where

. We call the
k
-graph family

F(s,t)\coloneqq\big\{F(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r}):(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r})\in\mathcal{P}(F)\big\}

the
(s,t)
-extension
of
F
. Furthermore, for a family of
k
-graphs, let

\mathcal{F}(s,t)\coloneqq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F(s,t)

denote the
(s,t)
-extension
of
\mathcal{F}
. When no confusion arises, we write
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}
for
\mathcal{A}(F),\mathcal{T}(F)

Roughly speaking, each

-sets of vertices, where each added vertex forms exactly

is the collection of all
k
-graphs that can be constructed in this manner.

Here is an example for the 3-graph with a single edge

F=\{a,b,c\}
and
s=2
,
t=1
.
Then we have

\mathcal{A}=\{\{a,b\},\{b,c\},\{a,c\}\}\text{ and }\mathcal{T}=\{\{\{a,b\},\{b,c\}\},\{\{a,b\},\{a,c\}\},\{\{b,c\},\{a,c\}\}\}.

Then the number of
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\in\mathcal{P}
is
\binom{s}{t}^{r}=2^{3}
. For each
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\in\mathcal{P}
, we construct a
3
-graph
F(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
. For example, for
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})=(\{\{a,b\}\},\{\{a,b\}\},\{\{b,c\}\})\in\mathcal{P}
,
F(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})
is the

-graph on vertex set

\{a,b,c,v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}\text{ and edge set }\{\{a,b,c\},\{a,b,v_{1}\},\{a,b,v_{2}\},\{b,c,v_{3}\}\}.

The family
F(s,t)
is the collection of all
3
-graphs

with all
(P_{1},P_{2},P_{3})\in\mathcal{P}
.
We have

, as

and some of the

-graphs obtained from the above process are isomorphic. In this case,

consists of all
3
-graphs obtained by adding 3 new vertices to a single edge

with the link of each added vertex being an arbitrary single pair in

It is clear that
\mathcal{F}(s,t)
is a finite family whenever
\mathcal{F}
is finite.

For

m\geq\frac{2(k-1)}{\delta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\binom{m}{k-1}e^{-\delta^{2}(m-k+1)/12}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
14
]
*Lemma 2.1)

Take any

If

\mathcal{H}
be the collection of all

Claim 2.3
.

\gamma(\mathcal{H}(a,b+1))\geq\frac{b}{a}
.

Proof of the claim.

Given an integer
n
, let
G_{a,b,n}
be the
k
-graph on the vertex set
[n]
defined as follows:

•

Partition
[n]
into
a

V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{a}
as evenly as possible, that is,

\bigl|\,|V_{i}|-|V_{j}|\,\bigr|\leq 1\quad\text{for all }i,j\in[a].

•

For each
i\in[a]
, add every edge consisting of
k-1

V_{i}
and
one vertex from
V_{j}
whenever

•

Add all edges that intersect at least three of the parts

.

•

Add all edges that lie inside some two of the parts

It is easy to see that the neighbourhood of any
(k-1)
-set

b
whole parts, minus the set

.
Since each part has size at least

\delta_{k-1}(G_{a,b,n})\geq\frac{b}{a}\,n-b-|S|.

To show that
\gamma(\mathcal{H}(a,b+1))\geq\frac{b}{a}

G_{a,b,n}
is
\mathcal{H}(a,b+1)
-free.
Suppose, to the contrary, that
G_{a,b,n}
contains some
k
-graph from

\mathcal{H}(a,b+1)
. By the definition of
\mathcal{H}(a,b+1)
, there exists a
k
-graph

F\in\mathcal{H}
such that
G_{a,b,n}
contains some
k
-graph in
F(a,b+1)
.

With the notation introduced in Definition 2.1, let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{T}
, and

\mathcal{P}
be defined with respect to
F
, and set
r\coloneqq|\mathcal{T}|
.
Then there exists some
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{r})\in\mathcal{P}
such that

contains
F(P)
as a subgraph. In particular,
G_{a,b,n}

contains
F
as a subgraph.

By the definition of
\mathcal{H}
, we have

\delta_{k-1}(F)\geq(\alpha-\delta)m
.
Let
x_{i}=|V_{i}\cap V(F)|
for

.
For convenience, we do index arithmetic modulo

, that is, for any

with

.

First, let us show that

\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}x_{i+j}\geq(\alpha-2\delta)m,\quad\mbox{for all $i\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

(1)

Suppose to the contrary that there exists some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}x_{i+j}<(\alpha-2\delta)m.

Let
t
be the minimal integer in
[a]
such that
x_{i-t}\geq k-1
. If such

(k-1)
-set
S
of vertices in

. It follows from

Next, we will show that

. By Bézout’s identity, there exist two positive integers

Since

T
, that is,

R_{T}\coloneqq\big\{v\in V(G):|\{i\in[a]:\{v\}\cup S_{i}\in G\}|\geq b+1\big\}.

We first show that
|R_{T}|\geq N
for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
.
We double count the number of pairs
(S,v)
, where
S\in T
and

v\in V(G)\setminus R_{T}
, such that
S\cup\{v\}\in E(G)
.
Since each vertex in
V(G)\setminus R_{T}
forms an edge with at most
b

elements of
T
, the total number of such pairs is at most
bn
.
On the other hand, from the assumption that

\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\frac{b}{a}\,n+\varepsilon n

and the fact that
\varepsilon n\geq N
, we obtain that the number of such pairs
is at least

\biggl(\frac{b}{a}\,n+(N-|R_{T}|)\biggr)a.

Therefore,

\biggl(\frac{b}{a}\,n+(N-|R_{T}|)\biggr)a\leq bn,

which implies that
|R_{T}|\geq N
.

Let
r\coloneqq|\mathcal{T}|
and write

\mathcal{T}=\{T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{r}\}.

Since
N=|V(F)|+|\mathcal{T}|
, we can greedily choose distinct vertices

v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{r}
from
V(G)\setminus V(F)
such that
v_{i}\in R_{T_{i}}
for each
i\in[r]
.
This implies that for each
v_{i}
there exists a subset

P_{i}\subseteq T_{i}
with
|P_{i}|=b+1
such that
v_{i}
forms an edge with
every element of
P_{i}
.
Consequently,
G
contains a copy of

F(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{r})\in F(a,b+1)\subseteq\mathcal{H}(a,b+1),

which completes the proof of the claim.
∎

Combining Claim
2.3
and Claim
2.4
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

3
Sequence of
k
-graphs with codegree Turán density
\frac{r-1}{r}
.

We say that a
k
-graph
F
is
r
-colourable

if
V(F)
can be partitioned into
r
sets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{r}
such that no edge of
F
lies entirely inside some part
A_{i}
. We will use the following observation of Keevash and Zhao
[
9
]

Proposition 3.1
.

If a
k
-graph
F
is not
r
-colourable, then
\gamma(F)\geq\frac{r-1}{r}
.

Proof.

Let
n\to\infty
and let
H
be an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with a partition

V(H)=A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup\dots\cup A_{r},

where
|A_{i}|\in\{\lfloor n/r\rfloor,\lceil n/r\rceil\}
for every
i\in[r]

and the edge set
E(H)
consists of all
k
-sets that intersect at least two of these parts.
Clearly,
H
is
F
-free and satisfies
\delta_{k-1}(H)\geq n-\lceil n/r\rceil
,
which implies that
\gamma(F)\geq\frac{r-1}{r}
.∎

Next, inductively on
r=1,2,\dots
, we define a
k
-graph
F^{k}_{r}
as follows. The
k
-graph
F^{k}_{1}
is just a single edge. Let
r\geq 2
.
Let
S
be a vertex set of size
r(k-2)+1
. Let
m\coloneqq\binom{r(k-2)+1}{k-1}
, and enumerate all
(k-1)
-subsets of
S
as
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m}
. For each
j\in[m]
, let
\tilde{X}_{j}
be a copy of
F^{k}_{r-1}
, with these being vertex disjoint from each other and from the set
S
.
Set

V(F^{k}_{r})\coloneqq S\cup\bigcup^{m}_{j=1}V\left(\tilde{X}_{j}\right)\text{ and }E(F^{k}_{r})\coloneqq\Big\{X_{j}\cup\{v\}:j\in[m],\ v\in V(\tilde{X}_{j})\Big\}\cup\bigcup^{m}_{j=1}E\left(\tilde{X}_{j}\right).

In order to illustrate the above definition (and since
F_{2}^{k}
appears in Theorem
1.3
), let us describe the
k
-graph
F^{k}_{2}
separately.
We start with a vertex set
S
of size
2k-3
, and enumerate its
(k-1)
-subsets as
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
. We add
m
new edges
\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{m}
to
E(F_{2}^{k})
, which are pairwise disjoint and also disjoint from
S
. Finally, for each
i\in[m]
, we add to
E(F_{2}^{k})
all
k
-sets that contain
X_{i}
and a vertex of
\tilde{X}_{i}
.

We prove the following result about these
k
-graphs.

Theorem 3.2
.

Fix any
k\geq 3
. Then, for every
r\geq 1
, the
k
-graph
F^{k}_{r}
is not
r
-colourable and satisfies
\gamma(F^{k}_{r})=\frac{r-1}{r}
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by induction on
r
.
Although the case
r=1
is trivial and the induction step handles the case
r=2
correctly, we present a separate proof for
r=2
(since
F_{2}^{k}
plays a crucial role in Theorem
1.3
).

Claim 3.3
.

The
k
-graph
F^{k}_{2}
is not 2-colourable and
\gamma(F^{k}_{2})=1/2.

Proof of the claim.

Let us first show that
F^{k}_{2}
is not
2
-colourable. Suppose, for contradiction, that
F^{k}_{2}
is
2
-colourable, and let
V(F^{k}_{2})=A\cup B
be the partition such that neither
A
nor
B
contains an edge. Recall that
V(F^{k}_{2})=S\cup\bigcup_{i\in[m]}V(\tilde{X}_{i})
, where
|S|=2k-3
. By the pigeonhole principle, there exists a set
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
of size
k-1
entirely lying in
A
or
B
. Without loss of generality, assume that
S^{\prime}\subseteq A
. Let
i\in[m]
be such that
S^{\prime}=X_{i}
. Consider the corresponding set
\tilde{X}_{i}
. Since
\tilde{X}_{i}
is actually an edge of
F^{k}_{2}
, it must have at least one vertex
v\in\tilde{X}_{i}
lying in
A
; otherwise,
\tilde{X}_{i}\subseteq B
contradicting the assumption that
B
is edge-free. But then
X_{i}\cup\{v\}
is an edge lying in
A
, a contradiction. Hence,
F^{k}_{2}
is not
2
-colourable.

By Proposition
3.1
, we have that
\gamma(F^{k}_{2})\geq 1/2
. Let us show the upper bound
\gamma(F^{k}_{2})\leq 1/2
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrarily small constant, and assume that
n
is sufficiently large. We claim that any
k
-graph
H
with minimum codegree
\delta_{k-1}(H)\geq(1/2+\varepsilon)n
must contain
F^{k}_{2}
as a subgraph.

Identify an arbitrary
(2k-3)
-subset of
V(H)
with
S
. Its
(k-1)
-subsets are labelled by
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m}
, where
m\coloneqq\binom{2k-3}{k-1}
. We find suitable sets
\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{m}\subseteq V(H)
one by one. Suppose that
i\geq 1
and we have already defined
\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{i-1}
. Let

A:=N(X_{i})\setminus\Big(S\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}\tilde{X}_{j}\Big).

By the codegree condition of
H
, we have that

|A|\geq d(X_{i})-|S|-(i-1)k\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)n-(2k-3)-(i-1)k\geq k-1.

Take an arbitrary
(k-1)
-subset
Y
of
A
. Since

d(Y)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon\right)n>|V(H)|-|A|,

there must exist a vertex
y\in N(Y)\cap A
. We take
Y\cup\{y\}\in E(H)
for
\tilde{X}_{i}
. After
m
steps, the sets
S,\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{m}
form a copy of
F^{k}_{2}
in
H
, finishing the proof of the claim.
∎

In the induction step for
r\geq 3
, we have to show that
F^{k}_{r}
is not
r
-colourable and
\gamma(F^{k}_{r})=\frac{r-1}{r}
. The proof is very similar to that of Claim
3.3
.
First, we show that
F^{k}_{r}
is not
r
-colourable. Suppose, for contradiction, that
F^{k}_{r}
is
r
-colourable. Then
V(F^{k}_{r})
can be partitioned into
r
parts
A_{1},A_{2},\dots,A_{r}
, each of which induces no edge. Recall that

V(F^{k}_{r})\coloneqq S\cup\bigcup^{m}_{j=1}V\left(\tilde{X}_{j}\right)\text{ and }|S|=(k-2)r+1.

By the pigeonhole principle, there exists a set
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
of size
k-1
entirely lying in some part
A_{i}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
S^{\prime}\subseteq A_{r}
. Let
S^{\prime}=X_{j}
. Consider the corresponding copy of
F^{k}_{r-1}
, namely
\tilde{X}_{j}
.
As every vertex of
\tilde{X}_{j}
forms an edge together with
X_{j}
, we must have that

\tilde{X}_{j}\cap A_{r}=\varnothing,

which contradicts the fact that
\tilde{X}_{j}
is not
(r-1)
-colourable.
Therefore,
F^{k}_{r}
is not
r
-colourable.

Next, we show that
\gamma(F^{k}_{r})=\frac{r-1}{r}
. By Observation
3.1
, it remains to prove that
\gamma(F^{k}_{r})\leq\frac{r-1}{r}
.
Given any (arbitrarily small) constant
\varepsilon>0
, let
n
be sufficiently large. Let us show that any
k
-graph
H
with minimum codegree
\delta_{k-1}(H)\geq(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon)n
must contain
F^{k}_{r}
as a subgraph.

Take an arbitrary vertex set
S\subseteq V(H)
of size
r(k-2)+1
, and label its
(k-1)
-subsets by
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{m}
. Our goal is to iteratively find, for each
X_{i}
, a copy of
F^{k}_{r-1}
in
N(X_{i})
, denoted by
\tilde{X_{i}}
, with the additional requirement that
\tilde{X_{1}},\dots,\tilde{X_{m}}
and
S
are pairwise disjoint. Suppose that we have obtained
\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{i-1}
for some
i\in[m]
.
We now have to find
\tilde{X}_{i}
.
Define

H_{i}\coloneqq H\Big[\,N(X_{i})\setminus\Big(S\cup\bigcup_{j\in[i-1]}\tilde{X}_{j}\Big)\,\Big].

Then we have

|V(H_{i})|\geq\left(\frac{r-1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)n-(r(k-2)+1)-(i-1)\,|V(F_{r-1}^{k})|\geq\left(\frac{r-1}{r}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)n,

where the last inequality holds since
n
is sufficiently large.
Consequently,

\delta_{k-1}(H_{i})\geq\delta_{k-1}(H)-\bigl(n-|V(H_{i})|\bigr)\geq\left(\frac{r-2}{r-1}+\varepsilon^{\prime}\right)|V(H_{i})|,

for some constant
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
and
r
only).
By the induction hypothesis,
\gamma(F^{k}_{r-1})=\frac{r-2}{r-1}
.
Hence,
H_{i}
contains a copy of
F^{k}_{r-1}
, which we denote by
\tilde{X}_{i}
.
After we have processed all
i\in[m]
, the sets
S,\tilde{X}_{1},\dots,\tilde{X}_{m}
form a copy of
F^{k}_{r}
in
H
. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, let us prove Theorem
1.3
. We first present two propositions by Mubayi and Zhao
[
14
]
, which will be needed in our proof.

Let
K^{k}(t,t)
denote the
k
-graph with vertex set
V=A\cup B
such that
A\cap B=\varnothing
and
|A|=|B|=t
, where the edge set is defined as
\{S\in\binom{V}{k}\colon|S\cap A|=1\text{ or }|S\cap B|=1\}
.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
14
]
*Proposition 4.2)
.

For
k\geq 3
, there exists a positive integer
\ell=\ell(k)
such that
\gamma(K^{k}(\ell,\ell))\geq 1/2
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
14
]
*Proposition 4.3)
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
\ell\geq k-1
and

\rho\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\binom{\ell}{k-1}}+\frac{1}{\binom{\ell}{k-1}2^{1/\ell}}\right)<\frac{1}{2}.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N
such that every
2
-colourable
k
-graph
H
on
n
vertices with
n>N
and
\delta_{k-1}(H)\geq(\rho+\varepsilon)n
contains a copy of
K^{k}(\ell,\ell)
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Given an integer
k\geq 3
, let
\ell=\ell(k)
be the positive integer returned by Proposition
4.1
. Define
F_{1}\coloneqq K^{k}(\ell,\ell)
and
F_{2}\coloneqq F^{k}_{2}
, where
F^{k}_{2}
is the
k
-graph defined in Section
3
.

Let us show that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
satisfy Theorem
1.3
. By Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
3.2
, we know that
\gamma(F_{1})\geq 1/2
and
\gamma(F_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
. Thus it is enough to show that
\gamma(\{F_{1},F_{2}\})<1/2
.

Given
k
and
\ell
, let
\rho=\rho(k,\ell)<1/2
be the constant returned by Proposition
4.2
. Set

\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\rho\right)>0,

and take
n
to be sufficiently large. It is enough to prove that any
n
-vertex
k
-graph
H
with minimum codegree
\delta_{k-1}(H)\geq(1/2-\varepsilon)n
must contain
F_{1}
or
F_{2}
as a subgraph, for this yields
\gamma(\{F_{1},F_{2}\})\leq 1/2-\varepsilon<1/2
as desired.

Assume that
H
is
F_{2}
-free. Let us show that, for any vertex subset
S
of size
2k-3
, there must exist a
(k-1)
-subset
X\subseteq S
such that one can find a set
A\subseteq N(X)
with
|A|\geq(1/2-2\varepsilon)n
containing no edge of
H
. Suppose on the contrary that no such
X
exists. We enumerate
(k-1)
-subsets of
S
as
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
,
m\coloneqq{2k-3\choose k-1}
, and follow a similar inductive procedure as in the proof of the upper bound in Claim
3.3
to find edges
\tilde{X}_{i}
for
i=1,2,\dots,m
. In fact, the argument becomes slightly simpler here, since at Step
i
, we may directly choose any edge in the set
N(X_{i})\setminus(S\cup\bigcup_{j\in[i-1]}\tilde{X}_{j})
. The latter set has at least
\delta_{k-1}(H)-(2k-3)-(i-1)k\geq(1/2-2\varepsilon)n
vertices and thus spans at least one edge by our assumption. This eventually yields a copy of
F_{2}
, a contradiction.

Fix an arbitrary vertex subset
S\subseteq H
of size
2k-3
. Let
X\subseteq S
and
A\subseteq N(X)
be two sets satisfying the property above. Then
A
is an independent set and
|A|\geq(1/2-2\varepsilon)n
. Now take any
(2k-3)
-set
S^{\prime}\subseteq A
. Then there exists a
(k-1)
-set
X^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime}
such that
there is a vertex set
B\subseteq N(X^{\prime})
of size at least
\left(1/2-2\varepsilon\right)n
which contains no edge of
H
. Since
A
is independent, we have
B\cap A\subseteq N(X^{\prime})\cap A=\varnothing
. Let
H^{\prime}=H[A\cup B]
be the induced
k
-graph on
A\cup B
. Then
|V(H^{\prime})|\geq|A|+|B|\geq(1-4\varepsilon)n
, and thus

\delta_{k-1}(H^{\prime})\geq\delta_{k-1}(H)-4\varepsilon n\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-5\varepsilon\right)n\geq(\rho+\varepsilon)n\geq(\rho+\varepsilon)\cdot|V(H^{\prime})|.

Since both
A
and
B
are independent,
H^{\prime}
is
2
-colourable. By Proposition
4.2
,
H^{\prime}
contains a copy of
F_{1}
. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

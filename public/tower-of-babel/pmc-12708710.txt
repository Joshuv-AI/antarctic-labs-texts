Tightening of the eyelids with monopolar radiofrequency in Asians
Authors: Dong-Hye Suh, Jae Eun Choi, Sang Jun Lee, Hei Sung Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEyelid laxity is a visible sign of aging that can significantly affect appearance and comfort; however, many patients remain reluctant to undergo surgical blepharoplasty. Monopolar radiofrequency (RF) offers a non-invasive alternative. This study evaluated the efficacy and safety of a 0.25 cm² monopolar RF tip for upper eyelid tightening in Asians. Fourteen subjects received a single session of treatment with a 0.25 cm² tip monopolar RF device. Multiple passes were applied to the upper eyelid and lateral orbital regions. Topical anesthesia was applied 30 min before treatment, and a protective plastic contact lens was used during the procedure. Standardized digital photography, physician assessments, and patient-reported outcomes were obtained at each visit. All subjects demonstrated clinical improvement. At 2 months, 73% of patients reported more than 50% improvement in eyelid tightening, increasing to 86% at 6 months. Physician Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS) ratings corresponded to “much improved” at both time-points. Mid-pupillary brow elevation increased by 1.18 mm at 2 months and 1.45 mm at 6 months. Lateral canthal brow elevation increased by 1.23 mm and 1.21 mm, respectively. Superior palpebral crease elevation improved by 0.54 mm and 0.41 mm, while the eyebrow angle decreased by − 4.12° and − 5.67°, indicating a more elevated brow contour. No adverse events were observed. In conclusion, monopolar RF treatment with a 0.25 cm² tip appears to be a safe and effective non-surgical option for upper eyelid tightening in Asians.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10103-025-04788-y.Keywords: Monopolar radiofrequency, Eyelid tightening, Non-surgical rejuvenation, Asians, 0.25cm2 RF tip

Introduction

Aging of the upper face, particularly eyebrow descent and upper eyelid laxity, is a common aesthetic concern among middle-aged individuals. These changes typically manifest as redundant upper eyelid skin and lateral canthal sagging, with lateral brow drooping contributing to a heavier eyelid appearance by reducing the brow–lash distance. In more advanced cases, superior visual field obstruction may occur [1].

Several treatment modalities are available for periorbital laxity, including surgical blepharoplasty and fractional ablative lasers. However, concerns regarding surgical risks, recovery time, and potential complications discourage many patients from pursuing surgery. In Asians, the clinical use of periocular lasers is further limited by a higher risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) [2–4]. Consequently, there is growing interest in non-invasive rejuvenation options that offer minimal downtime with favorable safety profiles.

Radiofrequency (RF) devices have emerged as effective non-surgical tools for skin tightening, including in the periocular region. Monopolar RF generates heat via electrical resistance in the dermis and subcutaneous tissue, inducing controlled collagen denaturation and stimulating a wound-healing response that promotes neo-collagenesis, neo-elastogenesis, and tissue contraction [5]. This volumetric heating has been shown to lift the eyebrow and enhance the superior palpebral crease [6].

Despite these benefits, eyelid treatment remains anatomically challenging due to the close proximity of critical ocular structures. To enhance safety, a 0.25 cm² “shallow” RF tip was developed to deliver more superficial energy to delicate periocular skin [7–10]. However, clinical data for this approach in Asian populations are limited. Given that Asian eyelids typically have thicker subcutaneous and pretarsal fat and often lack a well-defined crease compared with Caucasian eyelids [11, 12], it is important to evaluate the efficacy and safety of this modality specifically in Asian patients.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a 0.25 cm² monopolar RF tip for non-invasive upper eyelid tightening in Asians.

Methods

Study population

Fourteen subjects (13 women and 1 man) with a mean age of 50.6 years (range, 36–70 years) were enrolled. Based on Fitzpatrick skin type, 4 subjects had type III, 9 had type IV, and 1 had type V skin. Eligible participants were adults seeking upper eyelid rejuvenation who agreed to abstain from other facial procedures for the duration of the study. Exclusion criteria included any periorbital procedure within the preceding three months (e.g., blepharoplasty, fillers, botulinum toxin injections, or lasers), active infection or inflammatory skin disease in the treatment area, a history of keloid formation, or chronic systemic illness.

Ethical consideration

The study was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Incheon St. Mary’s Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea (IRB number: OC25RISI0100). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment.

Intervention protocol

All participants underwent a single session of monopolar RF treatment using the Thermage FLX® system (Solta Medical, Hayward, CA, USA) equipped with a shallow 0.25 cm² tip. A total of 450 shots were administered—225 shots per eyelid—by a board-certified dermatologist using a standardized technique. Multiple passes were delivered to the upper preseptal and pretarsal eyelid skin, including the lateral orbital region. Treatment was initiated at an energy level of 2.0, with patient feedback recorded on a 5-point pain scale (0 = no heat, 1 = warm, 2 = hot, 3 = very hot, 4 = intolerably hot). Energy intensity was adjusted to maintain a target pain score of 2. The circled area directly anterior to the globe, which is highly heat-sensitive, was treated at 1.5–2.0, the blue-marked area at 2.0–3.0, and the red-marked area at 2.5–3.5. The treatment sequence began with the preseptal area (red dotted region), followed by the blue dotted region at a lower energy, and then the blue circled region at the same energy level, with further reduction if pain occurred. After each sequence, the energy was gradually increased and the same order repeated, typically for 7–10 passes per eye. The treatment regions are illustrated in Fig. 1.

The treatment regions. The sequence proceeded from the preseptal area (red dotted region) to the blue dotted region, and finally to the blue circled region

For ocular protection, all subjects wore plastic contact lenses during the procedure. Topical lidocaine cream (EMLA; lidocaine 2.5% and prilocaine 2.5%, AstraZeneca AB, Södertälje, Sweden) was applied 30 min before treatment, and 0.5% proparacaine hydrochloride eye drops (Hanmi Pharm. Co., Ltd., Seoul, Korea) were instilled to improve tolerance of the protective lenses. All patients completed the procedure without pain or adverse effects.

Data collection and evaluation

Treatment outcomes were evaluated at each follow-up visit using both physician assessment and patient-reported satisfaction. Participants completed a 5-point satisfaction questionnaire rating perceived improvement (5 = 75–100% improvement, 4 = 50–74% improvement, 3 = 25–49% improvement, 2 = 1–24% improvement, 1 = no improvement). Physicians used the validated 5-point Global Aesthetic Improvement Scale (GAIS), with scores of 5 (“very much improved”), 4 (“much improved”), 3 (“improved”), 2 (“no change”), and 1 (“worse”). Standardized facial photographs were obtained at each visit using a fixed camera setup under consistent lighting and subject positioning. The intercanthal distance (ICD) was used as a reference for image normalization, and alignment was based on the horizontal line connecting the medial canthi.

Quantitative image analysis and measurements

Quantitative image analysis was performed using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). Measurements were obtained at three time points and normalized using sex-specific ICD values for the Asian population (37.1 mm for males and 35.9 mm for females; p < 0.001) [9]. The following anatomical parameters were assessed: mid-pupillary brow elevation (MPBE; vertical distance from the mid-pupillary point to the center of the eyebrow), lateral canthal brow elevation (LCBE; vertical distance from the lateral canthus to the lateral brow), superior palpebral crease elevation (SPCE; vertical distance from the eyelid margin to the superior crease along the mid-pupillary line), and eyebrow angle (EA; angle formed by lines drawn from the medial and lateral brow ends to the superior margin) (Fig. 2).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using Python 3.11 in Google Colaboratory (Google LLC, Mountain View, CA, USA). Changes across time points were assessed using repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA), with post-hoc paired t-tests for pairwise comparisons. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical improvement

All subjects showed visible improvement in upper eyelid tightening following treatment. At 2 months, 73% of patients reported more than 50% improvement, increasing to 86% at 6 months, indicating a sustained clinical benefit. Mean patient satisfaction scores were 4.00 ± 0.78 at both time-points, reflecting high perceived efficacy. Physician-assessed GAIS scores paralleled patient feedback, averaging 4.00 ± 0.88 at 2 and 6 months, corresponding to “much improved.” No adverse events or complications were observed during the study period.

Discussion

RF energy is widely used in aesthetic dermatology because of its ability to remodel dermal collagen with minimal downtime. Monopolar RF, in particular, delivers energy deeply and uniformly through dermal and subcutaneous layers, generating controlled thermal injury via tissue resistance and subsequently inducing collagen denaturation and neo-collagenesis [2, 13, 14].

To our knowledge, no prior study has specifically evaluated monopolar RF for upper eyelid tightening in an exclusively Asian population. In this study, a total of 450 pulses were delivered bilaterally (225 per side) to key regions involved in brow elevation and eyelid support. We used a 0.25 cm² shallow treatment tip designed to concentrate peak thermal energy approximately 1.2 mm beneath the skin surface [7]. This relatively superficial energy delivery is well suited to the thin, delicate periorbital skin, allowing targeted collagen remodeling while minimizing the risk to deeper ocular structures [15].

Our results demonstrate that a single session of monopolar RF with this specialized tip produced significant and sustained improvements in upper eyelid and brow position. Patient satisfaction remained high at both 2 and 6 months, and GAIS scores closely paralleled self-reported outcomes. Minor discrepancies between patient and physician assessments were observed, underscoring the subjective nature of aesthetic evaluation and the importance of incorporating both types of measures.

Quantitative image analysis revealed consistent improvements across all four measured parameters—MPBE, LCBE, SPCE, and EA. The most robust and statistically significant changes were observed in EA, indicating a more elevated and defined lateral brow. MPBE and LCBE showed early elevation that remained stable from 2 to 6 months, suggesting durable improvement. Although SPCE demonstrated the smallest absolute change, it exhibited the greatest relative percentage increase, which likely reflects its superficial anatomical location and sensitivity to subtle tissue shifts. Inter-individual variability in SPCE responses may be related to anatomical differences in crease formation, pretarsal fat distribution, and orbicularis oculi muscle configuration among Asian patients.

Although the average increases in brow and crease elevations were modest (approximately 1–2 mm), patients consistently reported a greater sense of visual openness and eyelid comfort. These findings emphasize that even small anatomical changes can yield perceptible aesthetic and functional benefits, particularly within the spatially constrained upper eyelid region.

Our observations are consistent with previous reports demonstrating the lifting and skin-tightening effects of monopolar RF in the periorbital area [7–9]. In contrast to earlier studies that often relied largely on subjective assessments or qualitative photographic comparisons, we incorporated standardized digital imaging and quantitative analysis using ImageJ. To enhance measurement accuracy, all images were normalized using ICD, a stable and externally verifiable anatomic reference. This strategy helps mitigate the variability associated with corneal diameter scaling, which is more susceptible to distortion due to gaze direction, camera angle, and lighting conditions [16].

Importantly, the procedure was well tolerated, with no pain, adverse events, or complications reported by any participant. These findings support the safety and tolerability of this approach and reinforce its potential as a non-invasive option for periorbital rejuvenation.

Notably, prior studies in predominantly non-Asian populations have reported greater magnitudes of anatomical change. For instance, one study documented mean increases of 4.3 mm (MPBE), 2.4 mm (LCBE), 1.9 mm (SPCE), and a 4.51° decrease in eyebrow angle at 3 months post-treatment [6]. These differences may reflect inherent anatomical variations between Asian and Caucasian eyelids. Asian eyelids often have thicker subcutaneous and pretarsal fat and less defined creases, which may blunt the visible effects of RF-induced lifting. In contrast, Caucasian eyelids typically display more prominent creases and less subcutaneous fat, allowing greater tissue retraction and elevation following tightening procedures [11, 12].

Compared with surgical techniques, the changes observed in our study were more modest. For example, internal browpexy with partial orbicularis resection has been reported to achieve a mean lateral brow elevation of 2.42 mm [3], while Endotine browplasty combined with upper blepharoplasty has produced brow elevations of 3.5–4.0 mm at various points [17]. Although these surgical outcomes are more pronounced, monopolar RF offers a non-invasive alternative for patients desiring gradual improvement without the risks and recovery associated with surgery.

Several anatomical and biomechanical factors likely underlie the observed brow elevation following upper eyelid RF. Upper eyelid laxity exerts a downward mechanical vector on the brow complex; RF-induced tightening reduces this downward pull, allowing the brow to return toward a more neutral resting position. Additionally, RF-mediated collagen contraction within the orbital septum, retinacula cutis, and lateral canthal supporting structures generates an upward lifting vector, particularly influencing the lateral brow. Neo-collagenesis and dermal remodeling further redistribute tension across the fronto-orbicularis soft-tissue network, producing an effect analogous to subtle browpexy. Because the brow is highly mobile, even small reductions in eyelid redundancy (1–2 mm) can translate into measurable changes in brow position. Collectively, these mechanisms provide a plausible explanation for the modest but statistically significant brow elevation observed in this study.

This study has several limitations. The small sample size and predominantly female cohort limit the generalizability of our findings to broader populations, including male patients and different age groups. The lack of a control group limits definitive causal inference, and measurement techniques, although standardized and normalized to ICD, remain partially operator dependent. Furthermore, the follow-up period was restricted to 6 months; longer-term data are needed to determine the durability of treatment effects. Future studies with larger, more diverse cohorts and extended follow-up will be important to validate these preliminary findings and to further define the clinical role of monopolar RF in periorbital rejuvenation.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that a single session of monopolar RF treatment using a 0.25 cm² shallow tip results in significant and sustained improvement in upper eyelid laxity in Asian patients, including brow elevation and eyelid tightening. Quantitative, image-based analysis confirmed objective anatomical changes, while high patient satisfaction and the absence of adverse events underscored the safety and tolerability of the procedure. Although the observed changes were modest in magnitude, they were statistically significant and persisted for at least 6 months. These findings support monopolar RF as a safe, effective, and well-tolerated non-invasive alternative to surgery for periorbital rejuvenation in Asians, particularly for individuals seeking gradual improvement without downtime.

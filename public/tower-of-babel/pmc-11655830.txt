The prognostic impact of preoperative CA19-9 on resectable cholangiocarcinoma: a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Zhicong Wang, Yabo Shi, Ganwei Xiong, Mengxi Han, Xiaoliang Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to determine the prognostic survival impact of preoperative carbohydrate CA19-9 levels in resectable cholangiocarcinoma.MethodsPubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane, and Embase databases were searched for articles published through April 28, 2023. The relationship between preoperative serum carbohydrate antigen 19-9 and the prognosis of patients with resectable cholangiocarcinoma was analyzed. Heterogeneity between studies was assessed by means of the I2 statistic. We also performed subgroup analyses based on anatomical site of the tumor, geographic region, time of occurrence, and different levels of CA19-9. A random effects model was performed to express effect sizes as Hazard Ratio (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).ResultsA total of 60 original studies were eligible for inclusion, with a total of 15,031 patients with all sites, including 9014 males. The overall Hazard Ratio for all studies was 1.90 (95% CI 1.74–2.07, p < 0.001, Z = 14.59). Publication bias was suggested by the Begg’s test (p = 0.014 < 0.05), and the overall HR was 1.66 (95% CI 1.53–1.80, p < 0.001, Z = 12.027) after the trim-and-filling method. Subgroup analyses showed that intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 2.00, 95% CI 1.79–2.23), extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 1.65, 95% CI 1.49–1.82), hilar cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 1. 82, 95% CI 1.60–2.07), and distal cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 1.66, 95% CI 1.27–2.15) were predicted to be linked with prognosis. Elevated CA19-9 levels were associated with an increased risk of death.ConclusionsThis meta-analysis showed that elevated CA19-9 levels were correlated with a poor prognosis in cholangiocarcinoma. In future, more distal and hilar cholangiocarcinoma should be included in statistical studies to improve the accuracy of our conclusions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01683-w.Keywords: Cholangiocarcinoma, CA-19-9 antigen, Meta-analysis, Hazard ratio, Prognosis

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinoma is an epithelial malignant tumor classified as an intrahepatic, hilar, or distal bile duct cancer according to the anatomy of the bile duct [1]. The hilar type accounts for approximately 50% of cholangiocarcinoma cases, the distal type accounts for 40%, and the intrahepatic type accounts for less than 10% [2–4]. Surgery is currently the preferred treatment for cholangiocarcinoma [5], and the five-year survival rate after surgery has increased from 20 to 40% [6, 7]. Cholangiocarcinoma is often detected in the middle and late stages, leading to a poor prognosis, especially for unresectable cases [8]. These include many factors related to the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma, such as TNM stage, serum CEA level, distant metastasis, lymph node invasion, and the presence of combined gallstones. Evaluation of preoperative biomarkers is also an important step in determining the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma, and the serum CA19-9 level is a good predictor that may guide whether the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma patients can be improved by lowering CA19-9 levels as much as possible before surgery [9–11].

CA19-9, a cell surface protein complex [12], is elevated in some digestive tract tumors, excluding patients with negative Sialyl Lewis(a) expression [13]. In addition to bile duct cancer, elevated CA19-9 levels often indicate tumor progression and poor prognosis. In 2010, Shang-Long Liu et al. [14] concluded that CA19-9 had some correlation with the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma, but only one of their included studies had extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma; the rest were intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma. The conclusions were not applicable to patients with cholangiocarcinoma at different sites, and it was not certain that the included studies controlled for factors affecting CA19-9 levels. The impact of serum CA19-9 levels on cholangiocarcinoma survival is well-recognized. However, the extent of the impact of preoperative CA19-9 levels on the prognosis of patients with resectable cholangiocarcinoma and whether there are differences between different sites of cholangiocarcinoma has not been clearly defined [11]. The purpose of this study was to determine the effect of preoperative CA19-9 levels on the prognosis of patients with resectable cholangiocarcinoma.

Materials and methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis have been submitted to PROSPERO (ID: CRD42023409419, https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/). Study protocol designed and implemented according to PRISMA 2020 reporting guidelines [15].

Search strategy

We searched PubMed, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and Embase from the databases’ inception to April 28, 2023, for this meta-analysis. The following search terms were used: “Cholangiocarcinoma”, “Cholangiocellular Carcinoma”, “Extrahepatic Cholangiocarcinoma”,”intrahepatic Cholangiocarcinoma”,”Bile Duct Neoplasms”,”Bile Duct Cancer”, and “Carbohydrate antigen 19-9”, “CA-19-9 Antigen” and “CA19-9 Antigen”, etc. References, relevant reviews, and meta-analyses of the included studies were screened manually for eligibility. The inclusion criteria were limited to articles published in English only.

Inclusion criteria

The diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma was confirmed by preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative pathology and exclude other cancers, such as gallbladder, metastatic cholangiocarcinoma, and liver, pancreatic head, and ampulla cancers;

The diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma was confirmed by preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative pathology and exclude other cancers, such as gallbladder, metastatic cholangiocarcinoma, and liver, pancreatic head, and ampulla cancers;

Radical surgery was performed, excluding patients with advanced tumors who underwent palliative surgery, exploratory laparotomy, preoperative radiotherapy, and chemotherapy;

Radical surgery was performed, excluding patients with advanced tumors who underwent palliative surgery, exploratory laparotomy, preoperative radiotherapy, and chemotherapy;

CA19-9 data was obtained preoperatively;

CA19-9 data was obtained preoperatively;

Exclusion criteria

Other factors which may affect the CA19-9 value (e.g., patients with jaundice who did not undergo preoperative yellowing reduction treatment);

Other factors which may affect the CA19-9 value (e.g., patients with jaundice who did not undergo preoperative yellowing reduction treatment);

Articles which only provided the risk ratio (RR) or odds ratio (OR);

Articles which only provided the risk ratio (RR) or odds ratio (OR);

Lewis antigen-negative patients cannot produce CA19-9.

Lewis antigen-negative patients cannot produce CA19-9.

Data extraction

Two reviewers (ZC Wang and YB Shi) independently assessed the eligibility of the studies and extracted the data from 60 English-language articles. Consensus was the method for resolution of disagreements. Data retrieved from the reports included article authors, year of publication, country, site of bile duct cancer, study duration, number of patients, sex, follow-up time, overall survival (OS), critical values of serum CA19-9 water, and Hazard Ratio (HR) with 95% confidence interval (95% CI) and Kaplan–Meier curves.

Literature quality assessment

The Newcastle scoring standard (Ottawa) was applied for the quality assessment of the included studies [16]. The NOS includes 4 items (4 points) for "choosing research subjects," 1 item (2 points) for "comparing groups," and 3 items (3 points) for "measuring outcomes," for a total of 9 points. The two reviewers agreed when there was a disputed score.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using STATA version 12. Overall survival (OS) was evaluated using the HR (95% CI). If only survival curves are provided in this study, we referred to Tierney et al. [17] to extract survival curve data using Engauge Digitizer 10.8 software, and analyzed the extracted data using the Hazard Ratio calculation spreadsheet provided in the study, finally obtaining the extracted HR and 95% CI. To compare the overall survival (OS) of patients with high and low serum CA19-9 levels, the combined HR and 95% CI of patients with high CA19-9 levels were calculated using low serum CA19-9 levels as a reference. In patients with cholangiocarcinoma, serum CA19-9 levels were considered to be an indicator of poor prognosis if HR > 1. Heterogeneity between studies was assessed using the I2 statistic, with I2 > 50% or p < 0.05 indicating significant heterogeneity. A fixed-effects model was used; except when a random-effects model was used. The data in the meta-analysis were presented in the form of a “forest plot.” Funnel plots were used to assess whether the meta-analysis had a publication bias. Indicates a possible publication bias when p < 0.05. We also performed a subgroup analysis of different cholangiocarcinoma sites, regions, and disease duration. p < 0.05 was used for statistical significance.

Results

Search results

By searching PubMed (n = 389), Web of Science (n = 937), Cochrane (n = 17), Embase (n = 2600) and references in the original study (n = 1), 3944 articles were found, and 812 duplicate references were excluded. After reading the article titles and abstracts, 2882 articles were removed. Seven of the remaining 250 articles were not available in full text and, 177 did not meet the inclusion criteria. After checking the remaining 66 eligible articles, reading the full text, and extracting the required data, we found that six of the articles had data inclusion relationships with the other five articles [18–22] that were included. In the end, 60 articles were included in the study [18–77]. The search and screening processes for the medical literature review are summarized in Fig. 1.

PRISMA flow diagram showing selection of articles for review

Research characteristics and quality evaluation

The 60 eligible studies with a total of 15,031 patients, including 9014 males from the United Kingdom, United States, Singapore, Germany, Australia, Japan, and China, with a predominantly Asian population (13,072 patients), statistically accounted for the majority of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (11,281 patients), all of whom underwent radical surgical resection. Among them, three original studies [34, 41, 54] were divided into two groups for statistical comparison because of the different intervals of the statistical methods. Articles quality was assessed according to the NOS standards: 19 were of high quality (8 to 9 points) and 41 were of medium quality (6 to 7 points). Overall, the meta-analysis was of good quality (Table S1).

Overall survival

Sixty studies reported a correlation between CA19-9 levels and overall OS. Random effects model used due to high heterogeneity (I2 = 68.2%, p < 0.001). The pooled results show that high CA19-9 levels are significantly related with lower OS of resectable cholangiocarcinoma patients (HR = 1.90, 95% CI 1.74–2.07, p < 0.001) (Fig. 2).

Forest plots on the level of preoperative CA19-9 and overall survival (OS)

Sensitivity analysis

For sensitivity analysis, each included study was removed individually, with summary analysis of the remaining studies. Our findings revealed that none of the studies significantly influenced the meta-analysis outcome, implying that the results of this meta-analysis were stable and reliable (Fig. 3).

Sensitivity on the level of preoperative CA19-9 and survival (OS)

Subgroup analysis

Given the high heterogeneity in this evaluation, we performed subgroup analysis based on different sites, regions years and cutoff values, and the results showed that intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 2.00, 95% CI 1.79–2.23), extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 1.65, 95% CI 1.49–1.82), hilar cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 1.82, 95% CI 1.60–2.07) and distal cholangiocarcinoma (HR = 1.66, 95% CI 1.27–2.15) were associated with the prognosis of patients with cholangiocarcinoma, and the difference in prognostic impact between sites was not significant. Regional differences among patients, Asian (HR = 1.90, 95% CI 1.75–2.07) and non-Asian (HR = 1.64, 95% CI 1.31–2.07) were correlated with prognosis, but the difference between groups was small. According to the subgroup analysis (Table 2 and Figs. S1–S11), the mortality rate of patients with high serum CA19-9 at different sites, regions, and years was approximately twice that of patients with low serum CA19-9, and the difference between the groups was not significant. Meanwhile, when we generalized the analysis according to different levels of CA19-9 (< 100, HR = 1.65, 95% CI 1.56–1.76; 100–300, HR = 2.01, 95% CI 1.83–2.21; 300–500, HR = 2.69, 95% CI 2.07–3.49; > 500, HR = 4.02, 95% CI 2.32–6.97), suggesting that there was a significant increase in prognostic index with increasing cutoff values (Fig. S12).

Publication bias

The Begg’s test suggested that the 60 included studies had a statistical potential publication bias (p = 0.014 < 0.05; Fig. 4), from which we could see that the funnel plot was not symmetrical. The trim-and-fill method was applied to observe the effect of publication bias on this study (Fig. S13). Twenty studies were supplemented with the trim-and-filling method, and the combined overall HR was 1.66 (95% CI 1.53–1.80, p < 0.05), indicating that the results did not change significantly but had high confidence.

Funnel plot for evaluating the publication bias of this meta-analysis

Discussion

CA19-9 is a mucin-type tumor marker that was first described in 1979 by H Koprowski et al. [78]. CA19-9 plays a crucial role in the adhesion, metastasis, and development of malignant tumor cells and endothelial cells [79] and is also involved in cell growth and differentiation, signaling, apoptosis, promotion of leukocyte aggregation, immune regulation, and other physiological processes [80]. CA19-9 is widely used in the clinical diagnosis, treatment, and prognostic assessment of digestive system malignancies, particularly bile duct carcinoma [81]. In recent years, the concept of margin resectability has also gained attention in major cancers [82, 83]. In the future, preoperative CA19-9 levels in cholangiocarcinoma may have a potential impact on the parameter of resectable status, so we need to pay more attention to the clinical guidance of CA19-9 levels [84]. A total of 15,031 patients were included in the 60 studies, and the results were consistent with most previous retrospective studies. The mortality rate in patients with a CA19-9 level higher than normal was 1.90 times higher than the mortality rate in patients with a normal CA19-9 level, and the overall conclusion was reliable. Increased preoperative serum CA19-9 levels imply poor prognosis in patients with radically resectable cholangiocarcinoma. At present, there is still some disagreement regarding its potential to detect cholangiocarcinoma and its ability to predict patient prognosis [85, 86]. Our subgroup analysis of patients with cholangiocarcinoma at different sites in Asian versus non-Asian populations, before and after 2009 did not show large disparities, suggesting that the effect of different sites, regions and years on serum CA19-9 levels did not vary significantly, and the prognosis was essentially the same, proves that the two are indeed correlated. We also evaluated the predictive role of CA19-9 on the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma under different cutoff value conditions and found that the risk of death in patients increased with increasing values. Serum CA19-9 level is a good reference value for early diagnosis, efficacy observation and prognosis assessment. As we conclude, the final results may not significantly change the overall treatment strategy of cholangiocarcinoma, but they have some prognostic significance for the clinical adjustment of the treatment program and the improvement of cancer prognosis. Recent studies utilizing national databases have demonstrated the utility of neoadjuvant therapy for cholangiocarcinoma, especially in advanced stages [87, 88]. The preoperative CA19-9 values we analyzed may help to identify patients for whom preoperative surgery does not improve survival and for whom neoadjuvant therapy is the first choice. Besides, we believe that neoadjuvant therapy may win patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma the opportunity to undergo surgery by lowering CA19-9 values. Therefore, to improve postoperative survival time, in addition to radical resection of the tumor, clinicians should minimize the factors affecting prognosis, otherwise predisposing the patient to tumor recurrence or metastasis [89, 90]. There are many prognostic indicators for cholangiocarcinoma, such as, common AFP, CEA, Ki67 and C-reactive protein [91–93]. There are also circulating cell-free DNA and cell-free tumor DNA, which may be used as clinical predictive tools in the future [94, 95]. In addition, immunotherapy for cholangiocarcinoma has recently been extensively investigated [96], and PD-1/PD-L1 in particular should be considered as an independent prognostic indicator for cholangiocarcinoma [97]. Studies suggest that elevated CA19-9 levels may be linked to the tumor immune microenvironment, indicating that CA19-9 could serve as a potential biomarker for selecting PD-1/PD-L1 immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy [98]. Serum CA19-9 level remains a clinically well-established non-invasive biomarker for cholangiocarcinoma [99]. Liang et al. [100] showed that the sensitivity of serum CA19-9 was not as high as expected but showed good specificity for the diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma. Recent studies have also identified preoperative prognostic biomarkers for cholangiocarcinoma using sequencing and multi-omics approaches [101, 102]. For instance, circular RNA (circRNA) has been proposed as a prognostic biomarker for the diagnosis of cholangiocarcinoma. These markers are often combined with or influenced by CA19-9 in predicting the prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma patients.

This review has unavoidable limitations, including the selective exclusion of cholangiocarcinoma patients with secondary CA19-9 elevation caused by obstructive jaundice, and that cholangiocarcinoma patients with cirrhosis and inflammation may also present with preoperative abnormalities in CA19-9 [103]. The conclusions in the article are not applicable to Lewis antigen-negative patients. There is no single standardized value for serum CA19-9 values in the literature we included, which varies depending on the original study. Our study presents the trend of all the references, so that it is not possible to avoid errors due to the number of specific measurement units of serum CA19-9 assessed as elevated in different countries and regions. In the included studies, interpretation of the results using these factors was minimized as much as possible. In 2010, Liu et al. [14] included studies on intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, and the scope of their conclusions was subject to discretion. In this study, all sites (intrahepatic, hilar, and distal) were included, and the difference in the prognosis of tumor resection by different anatomical sites was not significant in the statistical analysis; however, there are corresponding limitations in this study. In this meta-analysis, a subgroup analysis was not conducted regarding different surgical methods, and the influence of surgical choice on prognosis, such as pancreatoduodenectomy, partial hepatectomy, and enlarged partial hepatectomy, was not discussed. It was difficult to perform statistical analyses because few original studies documenting different surgical modalities were available.

Conclusion

The overall direction of the conclusions did not change significantly. Elevated preoperative serum CA19-9 levels predicted a poorer prognosis for patients with radical cholangiocarcinoma, with little difference between anatomical sites, years, and regions, and the risk of death increased with elevated CA19-9 levels. Larger and better-designed studies are needed to clarify the clinical guidance of CA19-9 as a prognostic marker for cholangiocarcinoma treatment.

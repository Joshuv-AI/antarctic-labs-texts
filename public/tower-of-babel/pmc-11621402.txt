Reliability and validity analysis of the Chinese version of the acute cough-specific quality-of-life questionnaire in children hospitalized for acute cough
Authors: Wanyu Jia, Xue Zhang, Ruiyang Sun, Peng Li, Jie Liu, Yanwen Wu, Zhiwei Chen, Chunlan Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChildren’s physical and emotional health are affected by acute cough. Cough severity and influence on quality of life can be measured with specialized questionnaires. This study aimed to present the Chinese version of the 16-item Parent-proxy Children’s Acute Cough-specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (PAC-QoL16) and examine its validity and reliability.MethodsUsing independent translations and counter translations, the Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 was translated, and its suitability was evaluated among the families of five coughing children. Children with acute cough who were hospitalized at the Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Zhengzhou University from October 2022 to June 2023 were included, and the caregivers were invited to complete the questionnaire and two cough-related measures (visual analogue score (VAS) and verbal category descriptive (VCD) score) on the day of admission and on the 5th day of treatment. Additionally, the questionnaire’s internal consistency and concurrent validity were evaluated.ResultsA total of 148 complete and valid questionnaires were collected. Eighty-one males and 67 females composed the 148 children, whose average age was 4.6 years and ranged from 2 to 12 years. Cronbach’s alpha coefficients for the three domains and the whole score ranged from 0.824 to 0.978. The VAS and VCD scores showed a strong correlation with the overall and domain scores of the PAC-QoL16. After therapy, there were significant decreases in the cough VAS and VCD scores, while the overall and domain scores of the PAC-QoL16 improved (P < 0.05). Concurrently, correlations were observed between the difference in VAS and VCD scores and the difference in PAC-QoL16 scores before and after treatment.ConclusionThe Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 has good applicability in Chinese children with acute cough and can reflect the quality of life and disease severity of children with acute cough.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-81391-1.

Background

Coughing is one of the reasons for medical consultation in children1. Depending on the duration of the illness, coughs in children are categorized as acute (less than 2 weeks), prolonged (2–4 weeks) and chronic (more than 4 weeks)2. The most common cause of acute cough in children is acute respiratory infection. The pathogens causing respiratory infections in children are complex and varied, including bacteria, viruses, atypical pathogens (e.g., Mycoplasma pneumoniae and Chlamydia), and fungi3.

Children are almost 4 times more likely than adults to develop acute cough associated with upper respiratory tract infections1. Although more than 50% of coughs due to acute respiratory infections in children resolve within 10 days (but rarely more than 14 days), acute coughs continue to have an impact on the physical and mental health of children4. Severe coughing can cause discomfort in other systems of the body, such as chest pain, abdominal pain, dry heaves, and sleep disturbances, while the child’s quality of life is affected by cough-related visits to the doctor, medication, and suspension from school. Acute cough also affects the quality of life of the parents or other caregivers of the child, causing psychological anxiety, sleep deprivation, financial impact, and disruption of parental work5–7.

Measuring quality of life is important for understanding the burden of disease, evaluating health care interventions, as well as for serving as an outcome indicator in epidemiologic and intervention studies. Specialized cough scales or questionnaires that quantify the extent and degree of the impact of cough on quality of life are important tools for assessing the severity of the condition and determining clinical outcomes8; these include the Parent-Represented Chronic Cough-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire for Children, the Chronic Cough-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire for Children, and the Parent-Represented Acute Cough-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire for Children. There are currently no reliable validated quality-of-life scales specific to acute cough in children in China. Anderson-James S et al. developed and validated the 16-item Parent-proxy Children’s Acute Cough-specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (PAC-QoL16) in 2015 and undertook the redevelopment and validation of a short version of the PAC-QoL16 in 2021, both of which yielded favorable results9,10. The purpose of this study was to introduce the Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 and to analyze its reliability and validity to explore the applicability of this scale in Chinese children.

Methods

Patients

Children with acute cough who were hospitalized in the Department of General Internal Medicine, Children’s Hospital Affiliated to Zhengzhou University, from October 2022 to June 2023 were included, and the caregivers of the children were invited to complete the questionnaire on the day of admission and on the 5th day of treatment, respectively, under the guidance of specialized pediatrician. Four pediatricians were designated as investigators and were uniformly trained prior to the conduct of the study to guide parents in completing the questionnaire in a standardized process.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Age greater than 2 years and less than 14 years; (2) acute cough at the time of admission (less than 2 weeks) caused by respiratory infections. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) acute cough caused by allergic cough and cough variant asthma; (2) suffer from long-term diseases affecting quality of life, such as chronic lung diseases, immune disorders, cerebral palsy or severe neurodevelopmental abnormalities, congenital heart disease, or tumor diseases; (3) parents were unable to cooperate.

Quality of life questionnaire

The 16-item Parent-proxy Children’s Acute Cough-specific Quality of Life Questionnaire (PAC-QoL16).

The PAC-QoL16 is a 16-item questionnaire divided into physical, social, and emotional domains based on the World Health Organization’s definition of health. Eleven items were in the physical domain, three in the social domain, and two in the emotional domain. The questionnaire was rated on a 7-point Likert-type scale (1 = all the time, 7 = not at all), with lower scores on the PAC-QoL reflecting more frequent and greater worry or concern9.

An email was sent to the original authors of the questionnaire to obtain consent for use. The Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 was translated by 2 master’s degree students in pediatrics using independent translations and counter translations of the PAC-QoL16 entries, and the preliminary Chinese version of the questionnaire was developed by taking into account the linguistic and cultural background of China. Two English-speaking experts discussed and modified the questionnaire and tested the applicability of the wording among the families of 5 children with cough to finalize the official scale. The Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 retained all the entries of the original scale, and the scoring method remained unchanged. (Seen supplementary materials)

Cough evaluation

In this study, we used the verbal category descriptive (VCD) score and the cough Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) to assess the severity of cough in the children. The VCD Cough Symptom Score is a 6-point rating scale of cough frequency (0 is no cough, 6 is a severe cough that prevents most daily activities). The higher the VCD cough score is, the greater the interference with the child’s daily activities. The VAS cough score is a rating scale from 1 to 10 (1 = no cough to 10 = the most severe, intractable cough). The higher the VAS cough score is, the more severe the cough problem is. The VAS and VCD scores represent the severity of the child’s cough in the past 24 h.

Evaluation of reliability and validity

The concurrent validity and internal consistency of the PAC-QoL16 were assessed. Internal consistency was calculated by determining the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, which is used to estimate the degree of homogeneity of individual results. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient value ≥ 0.7 indicates sufficient acceptability and good reliability. Simultaneous validity indicates the validity of the instrument relative to other criteria. Concurrent validity was assessed by comparing the PAC-QoL16 with the VAD and VAS scores, analyzing the correlation between the PAC-QoL16 and the VAD and VAS at admission and after 5 days of treatment, and comparing the change in PAC-QoL16 scores at admission and after 5 days of treatment. The average time to complete the questionnaire was collected to assess the ease of use of the questionnaire.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0. Verify the normality of the data using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. The data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation or median and interquartile range. Paired-samples t tests or paired-samples rank-sum tests were used to compare the PAC-QoL16 scores before and after treatment with their own scores before and after data collection. For correlation analysis, Pearson’s correlation analysis was used for data that conformed to normal distribution, and Spearman’s correlation analysis was used for nonnormally distributed data. p < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 160 questionnaires were sent out in this study, and 148 qualified questionnaires were returned. The average age of the 148 children was 4.6 years, the oldest was 12 years old, and the youngest was 2 years old; 81 boys and 67 girls were included. Most of the caregivers who completed the questionnaire were the parents (143). Symptoms associated with cough in children include fever, runny nose, wheezing, emesis, stomachache, and others. Internal consistency and validation of the PAC-QoL16

Cronbach’s alpha coefficients for the three domains and the whole score ranged from 0.824 to 0.978, indicating acceptable internal consistency (Table 3). The concurrent validity correlation coefficients calculated for the 148 children at admission and 5 days after treatment are shown in Table 4. The total and domain scores of the PAC-QoL16 were strongly correlated with the VAS and VCD scores. There were significant decreases in the cough VAS and VCD scores after treatment, while the total and domain scores improved in the PAC-QoL16 group (P < 0.05). Moreover, the differences in the PAC-QoL16 scores before and after treatment showed correlation with the differences in the cough measurements.

Discussion

Our study is the first to investigate the applicability of the Chinese version of the questionnaire for acute cough in children in China. The children included in this study were of a young age, and given the limited comprehension and language skills of young children, parents or caregivers were used as proxy assessors of their health status. At the same time, parents and caregivers are affected by the health status of their children, and their own quality of life is also relevant. The quality of life assessment scales used in pediatrics usually involve the parents/caregivers themselves11. Therefore, in this study, we chose the Parent-Represented Acute Cough-Specific Quality of Life Questionnaire for Children for reliability and validity studies. It has been verified that the Chinese version of the questionnaire is suitable for hospitalized children with acute cough in China.

In this study, the average time required to complete the questionnaire was 3.06 min, and most of the parents or caregivers of the children could complete the questionnaire easily and quickly. In our study, we found that most of the children with acute cough showed an improvement in cough after active treatment, along with the improved Chinese version of the acute cough-specific quality-of-life questionnaire scores. In children hospitalized with acute cough, both pre-treatment and post-treatment data were presented that the Chinese PAC-QoL16 questionnaire was strongly associated with severity of their cough. This suggests that the Chinese PAC-QoL16 scores can reflect the severity of the child’s cough to some extent. Meanwhile, the Chinese PAC-QoL16 had great internal consistency and appeared to be adaptable to alteration. We were unable to determine the repeatability of the PAC-QoL16 since most children experienced an improvement in cough severity during the research period. In the future, repeatability coefficients may be determined using a shorter time delay between the test and retest questionnaires or a much larger study. In conclusion, our study showed that the Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 is suitable for children with acute cough, and clinicians may consider using the PAC-QoL16 to initially assess the improvements of children with cough.

Current investigations on the quality of life of children with cough primarily focus on chronic cough, and a number of studies have shown that chronic cough significantly affects the quality of life of children and is significantly correlated with the severity of the cough11–13. The scores of children’s quality of life questionnaire can indicate the severity of the disease. However, in addition to chronic cough, acute cough also significantly affects the quality of life of children and their caregivers, especially for children hospitalized for acute cough. The children’s PAC-QoL16 scores declined dramatically at admission in our study. Physiologically, both caregivers and children expressed grave anxiety about the children’s physical state. In the social field, children with cough hospitalizations, resulting in delays in school, affecting daily activities and other problems, are also very serious. These can lead to emotional irritability and sadness for both the child and their caregivers. Consistent with the findings of previous investigations, cough symptoms improved after therapy, and the questionnaire score improved significantly as a result14. Taken together, these findings suggest that pediatricians should pay attention to the management of children with cough, strengthen the education and care of children with cough and their families, and reduce their concerns about the disease to improve their quality of life and reduce this burden.

Acute cough is one of the common reasons for children’s visits to the clinic, and not a few children are hospitalized for poorly relieved acute cough15. This study included children hospitalized for acute cough, most of whom had concomitant symptoms such as fever and a runny nose. Most acute coughs are caused by upper respiratory tract infections1, but lower respiratory tract infections are not uncommon, and most of the children in this study were ultimately diagnosed with pneumonia. This suggests that the role of acute coughing in childhood illnesses should not be underestimated, and that recurrent coughing, even for short periods of time, in children can potentially lead to more serious illnesses.

This study has several limitations. First, this is a single-center study. And while numerous children with acute coughs receive treatment in the outpatient department, our study only included children admitted to the hospital for acute cough and did not include children treated in the outpatient department. The sample size of this study was small, and necessitating further experiments with larger sample sizes to validate the findings of this study.

Conclusion

The Chinese version of the PAC-QoL16 has good applicability in Chinese children with acute cough and can reflect the quality of life and disease severity of children with acute cough. The quality of life of children with acute cough decreased and was related to the severity of the disease. The quality of life improved significantly after the cough was relieved.

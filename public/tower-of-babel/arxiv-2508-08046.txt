Aerial Target Encirclement and Interception with Noisy Range Observations footnoteinfo
Fen Liu, Shenghai Yuan, Thien-Minh Nguyen, Wei Meng, Lihua Xie

Abstract
This paper proposes a strategy to encircle and intercept a non-cooperative aerial point-mass moving target by leveraging noisy range measurements for state estimation. In this approach, the guardians actively ensure the observability of the target by using an anti-synchronization (AS), 3D “vibrating string” trajectory, which enables rapid position and velocity estimation based on the Kalman filter. Additionally, a novel anti-target controller is designed for the guardians to enable adaptive transitions from encircling a protected target to encircling, intercepting, and neutralizing a hostile target, taking into consideration the input constraints of the guardians. Based on the guaranteed uniform observability, the exponentially bounded stability of the state estimation error and the convergence of the encirclement error are rigorously analyzed. Simulation results and real-world UAV experiments are presented to further validate the effectiveness of the system design.



keywords
Target encirclement and interception, Noisy range measurements, Anti-target controller, Uniform observability.

Introduction

The growing accessibility of micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) has introduced critical security risks. MAVs can breach restricted airspace, including airports, confinement centers, and government facilities, causing significant damage due to the limited effectiveness of existing countermeasures
[
1
,
2
]
.
Traditional aerial defense systems, like bulky LiDAR and ground radar, face deployment challenges due to their size, weight, and operational constraints
[
3
]
.
Alternative methods like radio frequency jamming, GPS spoofing, high-power lasers, and net-based interception
[
4
]
are constrained by energy demands, limited flexibility, and operational complexity, reducing their effectiveness in dynamic scenarios.
To address these challenges, modern solutions use autonomous MAV swarms for scalable and adaptive countermeasures, offering coordinated detection and interception. However, practical implementation must carefully consider critical factors such as target localization, payload constraints, and computational limitations.

Existing target localization algorithms using single sensors mainly rely on cameras for bearing or angle estimation
[
5
,
6
]
.
Distance measurement is often more straightforward than angle or bearing measurement, as angle estimation requires a complex visual processing pipeline
[
7
]
. However, some range-based perception systems
[
8
]
, such as UWB or WiFi, need cooperative targets, making them less versatile.
For hostile non-cooperative targets, newer methods provide effective range solutions using Received Signal Strength Indicators (RSSI)
[
9
]
. However, range-based localization algorithms face inherent limitations. These include the requirement for the ranging network to satisfy the analogous range rigidity theory
[
8
,
10
]
or reliance on multi-step ego motion to observe the target
[
11
,
12
]
. This requires a sufficient number of followers simultaneously tracking the target, or specific conditions such as the target being stationary or moving at low or predictable speeds
[
13
,
14
]
. When range measurements are insufficient or noisy, general theoretical results addressing targets moving at unknown or variable velocities remain largely unexplored.

Although advances in range-based localization techniques have improved target tracking, integrating them into effective airborne interception systems remains a challenge.
Approaches using deep reinforcement learning (DRL)
[
15
]
require extensive training data and computational power, making real-time deployment impractical. Furthermore, most control techniques focus on detection and tracking
[
11
]
without enabling direct engagement, limiting their effectiveness against agile and evasive targets.
Innovative, resource-efficient frameworks are needed to bridge these gaps, ensuring precise target estimation and real-time interception in dynamic environments.

To address existing gaps, this paper explores the idea of utilizing guardians to provide overwatch, intercept, and incapacitate unauthorized or malicious targets without continuous LOS ground guidance. This work, to the best of our knowledge, is the first to showcase autonomous 3D moving non-cooperative target interception using minimal onboard sensors with noisy range measurements. The key contributions of this paper are summarized as follows.

Firstly, the position and velocity of a
non-cooperative 3D hostile target
are estimated by using noisy distance measurements from the onboard sensors of two guardians. By transforming the distances into linear observations related to the target state, the Kalman filter can be applied. Compared to existing state estimation works
[
10
,
16
]
, the uniform observability of the hostile target under limited noisy measurements is rigorously proved.

Secondly, we propose a novel anti-target controller that integrates the anti-synchronization (AS) encirclement strategy
[
17
,
18
]
with a 3D trajectory of a vibrating string generated by combining vertical and 2D horizontal encirclement motions. Unlike existing convoy protection
[
19
,
20
]
, surveillance
[
5
,
12
]
, and
interception
[
4
,
21
,
22
]
works, our proposed control strategy enables adaptive encirclement transitions between protecting a target and confronting a hostile one. It accounts for the physical flight speed limits of the guardians, establishes persistent excitation (PE) of the relative position between the two guardians, and ensures the uniform observability of the hostile target.

Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework in both simulation and real-world experiments.

The article is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the system model and formulates the problem. Section 3 presents the design of the estimator and controller, along with their convergence analysis. Section 4 validates the theoretical findings through numerical simulations and physical experiments. Finally, Section 5 summarizes the key conclusions.

System Model and Problem Formulation

2.1
System Model

This study focuses on protecting a target (person, drone, or vehicle) from the threats posed by a 3D non-cooperative hostile target (capable of stable and controlled flight). To accomplish this task, we deploy two MAV guardians, designated guardian 1 and guardian 2. We assume that the attitude changes of guardians are small so that the model can be approximated as a point mass.

For guardian
i

i\in\{1,2\}

\bm{X}_{i}\triangleq(\bm{p}_{i},\bm{v}_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{6}

\bm{p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the position and
\bm{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the velocity w.r.t. a common inertial frame
W
. We define
\bm{u}_{i}(k)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
as the acceleration control input, and

is the sampling period. The motion model of guardian 1 and guardian 2 can therefore be stated as follows
[
2
]
:

\begin{split}\bm{X}_{i}(k+1)=A\bm{X}_{i}(k)+B\bm{u}_{i}(k),\ i\in\{1,2\},\end{split}

(1)

where

A=\begin{bmatrix}I&tI\\
\bm{0}&I\\
\end{bmatrix},B=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2}t^{2}I\\
tI\\
\end{bmatrix}.

To better differentiate the targets, the protected target is designated as Target 1, and the hostile target as Target 2.
Let the state of the target

,
j\in\{1,2\}
be
\bar{\bm{X}}_{j}=(\bar{\bm{p}}_{j},\bar{\bm{v}}_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
with the position
\bar{\bm{p}}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the velocity
\bar{\bm{v}}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{split}\bar{\bm{X}}_{j}(k+1)=&A\bar{\bm{X}}_{j}(k)+B\bar{\bm{u}}_{j}(k),\ j\in\{1,2\},\end{split}

(2)

where
\bar{\bm{u}}_{j}(k)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the stochastic acceleration, which includes unknown disturbances such as maneuvering. We assume that the target’s acceleration follows a heavy-tailed distribution
[
23
]
given by,

\begin{split}&\bar{\bm{u}}_{j}\sim\pi_{j}\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{W}_{1,j})+(1-\pi_{j})\mathcal{N}(0,\bm{W}_{2,j}),\\
&\mathbf{P}(\pi_{j})=\gamma_{j}^{\pi_{j}}(1-\gamma_{j})^{(1-\pi_{j})},\ \pi_{j}\in\{0,1\},\ \gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\end{split}

where
\pi_{j}

\bm{W}_{1,j}
and
\bm{W}_{2,j}
are the variances. This distribution satisfies
\gamma_{j}\gg 1-\gamma_{j}
and
\bm{W}_{1,j}\ll\bm{W}_{2,j}

The acceleration profile models a target that usually moves by moderate exertion but can occasionally accelerate aggressively to escape encirclement or other threats.

Hence, we define the relative position between the guardians or targets as follows:

\displaystyle{\bm{q}}_{ij}\triangleq\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{p}_{j},\ {\bm{q}}_{i}^{j}\triangleq\bm{p}_{i}-\bar{\bm{p}}_{j},\ {\bm{q}}^{ij}\triangleq\bar{\bm{p}}_{i}-\bar{\bm{p}}_{j},

(3)

where the subscript is reserved for the index of a guardian and the superscript for a target. Similarly, the relative distances between the guardians or targets are denoted as follows

\displaystyle d_{ij}\triangleq\|{\bm{q}}_{ij}\|,\ d_{i}^{j}\triangleq\|{\bm{q}}_{i}^{j}\|,\ d^{ij}\triangleq\|{\bm{q}}^{ij}\|.

(4)

In this work, we assume that the protected target (Target 1) will share its state
\bar{\bm{X}}_{1}
with both the guardians.
The hostile target (Target 2) is non-cooperative, i.e. the state
\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}
cannot be directly obtained by the guardians. Therefore, we denote
\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}=(\hat{\bar{\bm{p}}}_{2},\hat{\bar{\bm{v}}}_{2})
and
\hat{d}^{12}
respectively as the estimate of
\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}
and
{d}^{12}
, which will be furnished via some estimation laws. Furthermore, we define three decision zones
\hat{d}^{12}
, namely
\Omega_{1}=\{\hat{d}^{12}|\hat{d}^{12}\geq l_{3}\}
,
\Omega_{2}=\{\hat{d}^{12}|l_{3}>\hat{d}^{12}\geq l_{2}\}
and
\Omega_{3}=\{\hat{d}^{12}|l_{2}>\hat{d}^{12}\geq l_{1}\}
, where
l_{3}
,
l_{2}
and
l_{1}
are the minimal radii of the
target protection zone
,
warning zone
and
take down (or capture) zone
, respectively, and
l_{3}>l_{2}>l_{1}
.
In addition, we define the
encirclement shape
vector as

\begin{split}\bm{\zeta}(k)=r(k)\begin{bmatrix}\sin(\rho k\pi),&\cos(\rho k\pi),&\mathrm{h}(k)\end{bmatrix}^{\top},\end{split}

(5)

where
r(k)
is the encirclement radius, and
\mathrm{h}(k)\in\mathbb{R}
is the vertical motion function, which can be user-defined. The term

is chosen by the fact that
\bm{\zeta}(k)
is a time-varying vector that will chart out a certain shape to achieve the desired encirclement. In addition, we assume that

and

are chosen so that
\exists\bar{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
so that
\|\bm{\zeta}(k)\|\leq\bar{r},\ \forall k
, and the sequence

satisfies the following PE assumption.

Assumption 1

The sequence
\{\bm{\zeta}(k)\}
is PE, i.e. there exist
\hat{a}_{\zeta},\check{a}_{\zeta}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that:

\hat{a}_{\zeta}I\leq S\triangleq\sum_{m=k}^{k+N-1}\bm{\zeta}(m)\bm{\zeta}(m)^{\top}\\
\leq\check{a}_{\zeta}I,\forall k\geq 0.

The assumption above can be easily satisfied by designing a cyclic sequence of

vectors that span
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with period

, and the constants

,
\check{a}_{\zeta}
can be specified by directly computing the singular value decomposition of

[
24
]
. With the PE property guaranteed, the following definitions are provided.

Definition 1

The guardian are said to encircle Target
j
in an AS manner if the preset encirclement shape
\bm{\zeta}
and the relative positions
{\bm{q}}_{i}^{j}
,
i,j\in\{1,2\}
satisfy
\bm{\zeta}^{\top}{\bm{q}}_{1}^{j}=-\|\bm{\zeta}\|\|{\bm{q}}^{j}_{1}\|
and
\bm{\zeta}^{\top}{\bm{q}}_{2}^{j}=\|\bm{\zeta}\|\|{\bm{q}}^{j}_{2}\|
.

Definition 2

The hostile target is captured (or taken down) when the radius of the encirclement is sufficiently small, i.e.
r(k)\leq r_{c}
with given constant
r_{c}
.

2.2
Problem formulation

The overview of the system can be seen in Fig.
1
. In this work, the distance
d_{i}^{2}
can be measured by the guardian onboard sensors
i
, e.g., by the equirectangular perception module, RSSI
[
9
]
, or the sound noise strength of the target captured by a microphone
[
25
]
. According to equations (1) and (2) in
[
9
]
, the squared distance
(d_{i}^{2})^{2}
can be measured from the received power

for some known types of targets.
In theory,
(d_{i}^{2})^{2}
can be estimated from multiple samples

from multiple wavelengths in the least-squares manner.

Hence, we assume that the actual measured squared distance
(\hat{d}_{i}^{2})^{2}
differs from the true distance

by an additive error, i.e.
(\hat{d}_{i}^{2})^{2}=(d_{i}^{2})^{2}+\eta_{i}
, where
\eta_{i}
is the measurement noise of the guardian

and satisfies

\eta_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{i}),\ \sigma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Here,
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are mutually independent.

Moreover, there is communication between the two guardians.
We also assume that guardians can align their local frame of reference to
W
and can update their positions
\bm{p}_{i}
in real-time by some onboard self-localization system, such as visual-inertial odometry (VIO) or optical flow. In this work, we do not consider the accumulated errors from the onboard self-localization system, which can be mitigated by loop closures or some other techniques
[
26
]
.

Assumption 2

The variances of target acceleration
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}
and measurement noise

are assumed to be positive definite with positive upper and lower bounds, i.e.

\displaystyle\hat{w}_{i}I

\displaystyle\leq\bm{W}_{i,2}\leq\check{w}_{i}I,

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}\leq

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\leq\check{\sigma},\ i\in\{1,2\},

where
\hat{w}_{i}
,
\check{w}_{i}
,
\hat{\sigma}
and
\check{\sigma}
all are positive numbers.

Now, we can state the problem of our interest as follows:

Problem 1

Design a range-based estimation law
\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}
for the position of the hostile target. Simultaneously, design a control law
\bm{u}_{i}
for the guardian to encircle the protected target when
\hat{d}^{12}\in\Omega_{1}
, and encircle or take down the hostile target when
\hat{d}^{12}
enters
\Omega_{2}
and
\Omega_{3}
, respectively. Our objectives are to design estimation and control laws under which there exist finite constants
\hat{\varepsilon},\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
K\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that the following can be achieved:
\forall i\in\{1,2\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\|\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}-\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}\|^{2}\right)\leq\hat{\varepsilon},\forall k>K,

(6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\|\bm{q}_{1}^{1}+\bm{q}_{2}^{1}\|^{2}\right)\leq\varepsilon_{1},\ k\in\{k|\hat{d}^{12}\in\Omega_{1}\},\

(7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\|\bm{q}_{1}^{2}+\bm{q}_{2}^{2}\|^{2}\right)\leq\varepsilon_{2},\ k\in\{k|\hat{d}^{12}\in(\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{3})\},

(8)

where

is an estimation error bound for the hostile target, and
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}
are the AS-based encirclement error bounds for the protected target or the hostile target, which can be proven by the theorems presented later.

In Problem
1
, the formulas (
7
) and (
8
) imply that the guardians can achieve the encirclement of the protected target or the hostile target in an AS manner stated in Definition

. In other words, the positions of the guardian from the target are opposite to each other. According to Definition

and Assumption

, when

is PE, we can ensure that the relative position

Moreover, the switching between encirclement and capture is based on the radius

. Based on Definition

, when

3
Estimator and Controller Design

3.1
Range-based State Estimation

Figure 2:
Range-based target motion analysis without measurement noise.

As shown in Fig.
2
, target 2 is located at any point on the sphere with center
\bm{p}_{i}
and radius
d_{i}^{2}
, assuming that there is no distance noise. Based on noisy measurements
(\hat{d}_{i}^{2})^{2}
and the fact that
(d_{i}^{2})^{2}=\bm{p}_{i}^{\top}\bm{p}_{i}-2\bm{p}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\bm{p}}_{2}+\bar{\bm{p}}_{2}^{\top}\bar{\bm{p}}_{2}
, we can obtain a stochastic observation
Y\in\mathbb{R}
that is linearly related to
\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}
as follows:

\begin{split}Y&=-\frac{1}{2}\left((\hat{d}_{1}^{2})^{2}-(\hat{d}_{2}^{2})^{2}-\bm{p}_{1}^{\top}\bm{p}_{1}+\bm{p}_{2}^{\top}\bm{p}_{2}\right)\\
&\triangleq C(k)\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}+\bar{\eta},\end{split}

(9)

where
C(k)\triangleq{\bm{q}}_{12}^{\top}[I\ \bm{0}]\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
, and
\bar{\eta}=-\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})
. From
\eta_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{i})
, we have
\mathbf{E}\left(\bar{\eta}\right)=0
and the variance
\mathbf{Var}\{\bar{\eta}\}=1/4(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2})\triangleq\Gamma_{2}
.

Recalling the dynamic model
(
2
)
and based on the output
(
9
)
, the target state estimator (TSE) is designed based on the Kalman Filter in
[
27
]
for a linear time varying (LTV) system as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}(k)=A\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}(k-1)+\bm{K}(k)\left[Y(k)-\hat{Y}(k)\right],

(10)

\displaystyle\hat{Y}(k)=C(k)A\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}(k-1),

(11)

\displaystyle\bm{K}(k)=\bm{G}(k|k-1)C(k)^{\top}\big[C(k)\bm{G}(k|k-1)C(k)^{\top}

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~+\Gamma_{2}\big]^{-1},

(12)

\displaystyle\bm{G}(k|k-1)=A\bm{G}(k-1)A^{\top}+BW_{2}B^{\top},

(13)

where
\bm{K}
is the estimator gain,
\bm{G}(k)\triangleq\mathbf{Var}\{\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}-\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}\}
is the estimation error variance, and
\bm{G}(k|k-1)\triangleq\mathbf{Var}\{\bar{\bm{X}}_{2}-A\hat{\bar{\bm{X}}}_{2}(k-1)\}
is the predicted variance.
Furthermore, denote the variance of the target acceleration
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}
as
W_{2}\triangleq\mathbf{Var}\{\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\}
. Recalling the distribution of
\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}
in Sec.

, we have
W_{2}=\gamma_{i}\bm{W}_{1,2}+(1-\gamma_{i})\bm{W}_{2,2}
.

Remark 1

In the case when the global positions
\bm{p}_{1}
and
\bm{p}_{2}
are not available but the local relative position
{\bm{q}}_{12}
can be measured, we can define

that is related to the relative state

\begin{split}Y_{i}=&(-1)^{i}\frac{1}{2}\left((\hat{d}_{\nu}^{2})^{2}-(\hat{d}_{i}^{2})^{2}-{\bm{q}}_{12}^{T}{\bm{q}}_{12}\right)\\
\triangleq&C(k)\bar{\bm{X}}_{2,i}+\bar{\eta}_{i},\end{split}

where
\bar{\eta}_{i}=(-1)^{i}\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\nu}-\eta_{i})
with

3.2
Anti-Synchronization Based Anti-Target Control

From the TSE, we can estimate the inter-target distances as
\hat{d}^{12}=\|\bar{\bm{p}}_{1}-\hat{\bar{\bm{p}}}_{2}\|
,
\hat{d}_{1}^{2}=\|\bm{p}_{1}-\hat{\bar{\bm{p}}}_{2}\|
and
\hat{d}_{2}^{2}=\|\bm{p}_{1}-\hat{\bar{\bm{p}}}_{2}\|
.
We first define some of the following terms:

\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\ \alpha\in(-\beta^{-1},0),\ \bar{\alpha}\triangleq(\alpha-1),

\displaystyle\Delta\zeta_{i}(k)\triangleq(-1)^{i}(\zeta(k+1)-\alpha\zeta(k)),i\in\{1,2\}.\

Hence, the ASATCs can be designed in the following three scenarios.

Case 1:
When
\hat{d}^{12}\in\Omega_{1}
, set
r(k)\equiv r_{1}
,

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{i}=2t^{-2}\left[\bar{\alpha}\mathbf{g}^{1}{\bm{q}}_{i}^{1}+\Delta\zeta_{i}\right]+2t^{-1}(\bar{\bm{v}}_{1}-\bm{v}_{i}),

(14)

where
r_{1}
is a given constant. The function

\mathbf{g}^{j},j\in\{1,2\}
is defined as
\mathbf{g}^{j}\triangleq\mathbf{g}(\hat{d}_{1}^{j},\hat{d}_{2}^{j})=\frac{1}{\beta}\big(\frac{U(\beta-1)}{\max\{U,(\hat{d}_{1}^{j}+\hat{d}_{2}^{j})/2\}}+1\big)
, where
U\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>1
,
\beta
is a given positive integer. Based on the above, we ensure
\mathbf{g}^{j}\in(\beta^{-1},1]
. The role of each term in (
14

2
. In this case,
\hat{d}_{1}^{1}=\|{\bm{q}}_{1}^{1}\|
and
\hat{d}_{2}^{1}=\|{\bm{q}}_{2}^{1}\|
.

Case 2:
When
\hat{d}^{12}\in\Omega_{2}
, set
r(k)\equiv r_{1}
,

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{i}=

\displaystyle 2t^{-2}\left[\bar{\alpha}\mathbf{g}^{2}\hat{{\bm{q}}}_{i}^{2}+\Delta\zeta_{i}\right]+2t^{-1}(\hat{\bar{\bm{v}}}_{2}-\bm{v}_{i}),

(15)

where
\hat{{\bm{q}}}_{i}^{2},i\in\{1,2\}
is the estimated relative position between guardian
i
and the hostile target, i.e.
\hat{{\bm{q}}}_{i}^{2}=\bm{p}_{i}-\hat{\bar{\bm{p}}}_{2}
.

Case 3:
When
\hat{d}^{12}\in\Omega_{3}
, set
r(k)=r_{k}
,

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{i}=

\displaystyle 2t^{-2}\left[\bar{\alpha}\mathbf{g}^{2}\hat{{\bm{q}}}_{i}^{2}+\Delta\zeta_{i}\right]+2t^{-1}(\hat{\bar{\bm{v}}}_{2}-\bm{v}_{i}),

(16)

where
r_{k+1}=r_{k}-(r_{1}-r_{c})/t_{\text{in}}
with the user-defined interception time

. The initial encirclement radius of the guardian when entering the takedown (or capture) zone

.

Remark 2

The ASATCs in (
14
)-(
16
) can be divided into three parts, e.g., in (
14

\bm{u}_{1}=\bm{u}_{cir}+\bm{u}_{tra}+\bm{u}_{com}

\bm{u}_{cir}=\frac{2\alpha}{t^{2}}\left[\mathbf{g}^{1}{\bm{q}}_{1}^{1}+\bm{\zeta}(k)\right]

\bm{u}_{tra}=\frac{-2}{t^{2}}\left[\mathbf{g}^{1}{\bm{q}}_{1}^{1}+\bm{\zeta}(k+1)\right]

\bm{u}_{com}=2t^{-1}(\bar{\bm{v}}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1})

, as stated in Problem

5
Conclusions

In this research, we have introduced an innovative aerial target encirclement and interception solution that provides robust target protection. This breakthrough is realized by utilizing AS-based control strategies and noisy range measurements. Our findings demonstrate the remarkable efficacy of a specially designed perception-aware controller, allowing the guardian to autonomously neutralize hostile target without necessitating external ground guidance. This autonomous capability holds immense promise, particularly in active war zones, where it has the potential to play a pivotal role in providing surveillance to save lives and enhancing guardian countermeasures.

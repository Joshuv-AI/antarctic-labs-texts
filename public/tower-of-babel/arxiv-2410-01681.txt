A

An important problem is to determine general and verifiable conditions on the window

Given a regular Gabor frame

The terms

The stability of regular Gabor frames under

A NSGF is a set

In

When the

Proposition 2.1

Let

be a frame; if supp

Thus, if

2.2.
Paley-Wiener and Kadec theorems

One of the fundamental stability criteria for bases in Banach spaces, and historically the first, is due to R. Paley and N. Wiener (see
[
21
]
). The following generalized Paley-Wiener theorem is in

Theorem 2.3

(Paley–Wiener theorem for frames)
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
be a frame in a Hilbert space

with frame bounds
A
and
B
. Let
\{y_{n}\}_{n}\subset H
; suppose that there exists

\lambda,\mu\geq 0

\lambda+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}}<1

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lambda+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}}<1
and

for every finite set of scalars
\{a_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
. Then,

\{y_{n}\}_{n}
is a frame with bounds

\Big(1-(\lambda+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}})\Big)A
and
\Big(1+\lambda+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}})\Big)B

If
\mu=0
and

is a Riesz basis, then Theorem
2.3
reduces to the classical Paley-Wiener theorem and proves that also
\{y_{n}\}_{n}
is a basis.

2.3.
Poisson summation formula

Let
f\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The basic Poisson summation formula states that

\sum_{n}f(x+n)=\sum_{k}\hat{f}(k)e^{2\pi ikx},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Given the parameters
P>0
, we can let

s_{P}f(x)=\sum_{n}f(x+Pn)=\sum_{n}f_{P}(x/P+n)

where
f_{P}(t)=f(Pt)
. Recalling that

\widehat{f}_{P}(y)=\frac{1}{P}\hat{f}(y/P)
and
using the basic Poisson formula above, we obtain

s_{P}f(x)=\sum_{n}f(x+Pn)=\sum_{n}f_{P}(x/P+n)=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}\hat{f}(k/P)e^{2\pi ikx/P}

Letting
f=\hat{F}
and recalling that
\hat{\hat{F}}(t)=F(-t)
, we obtain

(2.6)

\sum_{n}\hat{F}(y+Pn)=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}F(-k/P)e^{2\pi iky/P}=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}F(k/P)e^{-2\pi iky/P}.

We prove the following

Lemma 2.5
.

a) Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, for every
P>0
and a.e.
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.7)

\sum_{n}|\hat{g}(y+Pn)|^{2}=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}e^{-2\pi iky/P}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(s)\overline{g}(s-k/P)dt

where
\overline{g}
denotes the complex conjugate of
g
.

b) If
g
is supported in
(t-\frac{1}{2},t+\frac{1}{2})
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
and if
P\leq 1
, we have that

(2.8)

\sum_{n}|\hat{g}(y+Pn)|^{2}=\frac{1}{P}\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

Let us show first that for a given
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and for a.e.
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

|\hat{\psi}(y)|^{2}=\widehat{\psi*\psi_{-}}(y)
,
where
\psi_{-}(x)=\overline{\psi}(-x)
.
Indeed,

|\hat{\psi}(y)|^{2}=\hat{\psi}(y)\overline{\hat{\psi}(y)}=\hat{\psi}(y)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{\psi}(t)e^{2\pi iyt}dt,

and with the change of variable
t\to-t
in the integral, we obtain

|\hat{\psi}(y)|^{2}=\hat{\psi}(y)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{\psi}(-t)e^{-2\pi iyt}dt=\hat{\psi}(y)\hat{\psi}_{-}(y)=\widehat{\psi*\psi_{-}}(y).

Using this identity and (
2.6
), we obtain

\sum_{n}|\hat{g}(y+Pn)|^{2}=\sum_{n}\widehat{g*g_{-}}(y+Pn)=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}g*g_{-}(k/P)e^{-2\pi iky/P}

=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{k}e^{-2\pi iky/P}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(s)\overline{g}(s-k/P)ds

which is (
2.7
).

If
g
is supported in
(t-\frac{1}{2},\ t+\frac{1}{2})
and
P\leq 1
, the supports of
g(s)
and
g(s-k/P)
are disjoint whenever
k\neq 0
. Thus, the sum above reduces to

\frac{1}{P}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|g(s)|^{2}ds=\frac{1}{p}\|g\|^{2}_{2}

and (
2.8
) is proved.

∎

2.4.
The short-term Fourier transform

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|g\|_{2}\neq 0
. The
short-time Fourier transform
with respect to
g
, also called
Gabor transform
, is the operator
\mathcal{F}^{g}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})

(2.9)

(\mathcal{F}^{g}f)(\xi,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2\pi i\xi s}\,f(s)\,g(s-t)\,ds,\quad t,\xi\in\mathbb{R}

For a given
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we can let
g_{t}(x)=g(x-t)
and observe that
\mathcal{F}^{g}f
is the Fourier transform of
fg_{t}
.
This windowed Fourier transform provide a tool for time-frequency localization, and shares many properties with the Fourier transform. For example, it is proved
[
2
, Section 11.1 ]
that for any

f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

(2.10)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\mathcal{F}^{g_{1}}f_{1})(\xi,t)\overline{(\mathcal{F}^{g_{2}}f_{2})(\xi,t)}dtd\xi=\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{2}\left\langle g_{2},g_{1}\right\rangle_{2}.

From this formula we obtain a generalized Plancherel identity

(2.11)

\|\mathcal{F}^{g}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|f\|^{2}_{2}\,\|g\|^{2}_{2}.

Other properties of the short-term Fourier transform are described e.g in the aforementioned
[
2
, Section 11.1 ]
and in
[
12
]
.

We will need the following

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with support in
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. For every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and every
P\leq 1
, we have that

\sum_{n}|\mathcal{F}^{g}f(Pn,t)|^{2}=\frac{1}{P}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(s)|^{2}|g(t-s)|^{2}ds

Proof.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By Lemma
2.5

with
y=0
,

\sum_{n}|\mathcal{F}^{g}(f(Pn,t)|^{2}=\frac{1}{P}\sum_{n}|\widehat{g_{t}f}(Pn)|^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|fg_{t}|^{2}(s)ds=\frac{1}{P}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(s)|^{2}|g(t-s)|^{2}ds

as required.
∎

3.
Proof of theorem
1.1
and corollaries

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
, but first we recall some basic facts about the stability of Gabor frames and about Gabor frames with compactly supported window.

Given the frame
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)=\{e^{2\pi ibnx}h(x-ak)\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we find sufficient conditions that ensure that the set

\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=\{e^{2\pi ibnx}h(x-a(k+\delta_{n,k}))\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a frame.
As usual, the frame constants of
\mathcal{B}
will be denoted with
A
and
B
. By Theorem
2.3
, if we show that, for every finite set
\{a_{n,k}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
, we have that

(3.1)

\left\|\sum_{n,k}\,a_{n,k}\,e^{2\pi ibnx}\left(h\left(x-ak\right)-h\left(x-a(k+\delta_{n,k})\right)\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\mu A\sum_{n,k}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}

for some
0<\mu<1
, we have proved that
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is a frame with frame bounds

A^{\prime}=(1-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}})A,\quad B^{\prime}=(1+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}})B.

A standard application of Plancherel theorem and of the properties of the Fourier transform show that (
3.1
) is equivalent to

(3.2)

\big\|\sum_{n,k}\,a_{n,k}\Big(e^{2\pi iak(x-bn)}-e^{2\pi ia(k+\delta_{k,n})(x-bn)}\Big)\hat{h}(x-bn)\big\|_{2}^{2}\leq\mu A\sum_{n,k}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}.

Both inequalities (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) will be used in our proofs.

In the seminal paper
[
6
]
it is proved that when
h
is supported in an interval
[-\frac{c}{2},\,\frac{c}{2}]
, with
c>0
, then
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame if and only if
a\leq c\leq b^{-1}
and the inequality

(3.3)

Ab\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|h(x-ak)|^{2}\leq Bb

holds for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
with constants
A,\ B>0

See also
[
14
, Theorem 11.6]
.
The optimal constants
A,\ B
in the inequality (
3.3
) are the optimal frame constants of
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
.
It is proved in
[
14
, Thm 11.8]
that

A=\frac{1}{a}\inf_{x}\sum_{k}|\widehat{h}(x-bk)|^{2},\quad B=\frac{1}{a}\sup_{x}\sum_{k}|\widehat{h}(x-bk)|^{2}.

In view of the definition of frames (
2.1
), a change of variables in the inner product yields

(3.4)

A\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}\leq a^{2}\sum_{n,k}|\left\langle\psi_{a},e^{2\pi ibanx}h_{a}(x-k)\right\rangle_{2}|^{2}\leq B\|\psi_{a}\|_{2}^{2}

where we have let
f_{a}(t)=f(at)
. Since
a^{-1}\|\psi\|_{2}^{2}=\|\psi_{a}\|_{2}^{2}
, the inequality (
3.4
) can be written as

a^{-1}A\|\psi_{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{n,k}|\left\langle\psi_{a},e^{2\pi ibanx}h_{a}(x-k)\right\rangle_{2}|^{2}\leq a^{-1}B\|\psi_{a}\|_{2}^{2},

and we can conclude that
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame with frame constants
A
and
B
if and only if the set

\mathcal{G}(h_{a};1,ab)=\{e^{2\pi iabnx}h_{a}(x-k)\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a frame with frame constants
a^{-1}A
and
a^{-1}B
. Note that the support of
h_{a}
is in the interval
[-\frac{c}{2a},\frac{c}{2a}]\supset[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By the previous observations,
we can reduce matters to proving that if the assumptions of the theorem are satisfied, the set

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}=:\{e^{2\pi iabnx}h_{a}(x-(k+\delta_{n,k}))\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a frame whenever the set
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{G}(h_{a};1,ab)
is a frame.

For simplicity we assume that
h_{a}
is supported in
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
, but the proof is similar also in the other case.
To simplify the notation in the proof, we will replace
k+\delta_{n,k}
with
\mu_{n,k}
when there is no risk of confusion.

By Theorem
2.3
, the set
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
is a frame if we prove that for every finite sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
, the following inequality holds with constant
\lambda<1
.

(3.5)

\left\|\sum_{n,k}\,a_{n,k}\,e^{2\pi iabnx}\left(h_{a}\left(x-\mu_{n,k}\right)-h_{a}\left(x-k\right)\right)\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\lambda a^{-1}A\sum_{n,k}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and let

f_{k}(x)=\sum_{n}e^{2\pi iabnx}a_{n,k}\,\left(h_{a}\left(x-\mu_{n,k}\right)-h_{a}\left(x-k\right)\right).

The inequality (
3.5
) follows if we prove that for every finite sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
, with

\sum_{n,k}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}=1
, the inequality

(3.6)

\|\sum_{k}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lambda a^{-1}A

holds with constant
\lambda<1
.
We let
d_{j}=\sup_{n}|\delta_{n,j}|
, and

I_{j}^{\prime}=[j-d_{j}-\frac{1}{2},\ j+d_{j}-\frac{1}{2}),\quad I_{j}=[j+d_{j}-\frac{1}{2},\ j-d_{j}+\frac{1}{2}).

Clearly,
\mathbb{R}=\bigcup_{j}I_{j}\cup I_{j}^{\prime}
, and since
|d_{k}|<\frac{1}{2}
, all intervals are disjoint.

We show that each
f_{k}
is supported in the interval
[k-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k},\ k+\frac{1}{2}+d_{k})=I_{k}^{\prime}\cup I_{k}\cup I_{k+1}^{\prime}
.

Indeed, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the support of the function
x\to h_{a}\left(x-k-\delta_{n,k}\right)-h_{a}\left(x-k\right)
is in the interval
(k-\frac{1}{2},\,k+\delta_{n,k}+\frac{1}{2})\subset(k-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k},\ k+d_{k}+\frac{1}{2})
whenever
\delta_{n,k}\geq 0
, and in the interval

(k+\delta_{n,k}-\frac{1}{2},\,k+\frac{1}{2})\subset(k-d_{k}-\frac{1}{2},\ k+\frac{1}{2}+d_{k})
whenever
\delta_{n,k}\leq 0
.
Note that only
f_{k}
is supported in
I_{k}
but both
f_{k}
and
f_{k-1}
can be supported in
I_{k}^{\prime}
.

In view of the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we can write

(3.7)

\|\sum_{k}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k}+\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\|\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}.

Let us estimate the first sum in (
3.7
). Since
I_{k}\cup I_{j}=0
whenever
k\neq j
and only
f_{k}
is supported in
I_{k}
, we can write

\sum_{k}\|\chi_{I_{k}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k}\int_{k-\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}\big|\sum_{n}e^{2\pi iabnx}a_{n,k}(h_{a}(x-k)-h_{a}(x-\mu_{n,k}))\big|^{2}dx.

After applying the the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the sum in
n
and a change of variable in the integrals, we obtain

\|\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}\int_{k-\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}^{k+\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}|(h_{a}(x-k)-h_{a}(x-k-\delta_{n,k}))|^{2}dx

(3.8)

=\sum_{k}\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k}))|^{2}dx.

We now estimate the second sum in (
3.7
).
We have observed that the
I^{\prime}_{k}
are disjoint and both
f_{j}
and
f_{j-1}
can be supported in
I^{\prime}_{j}
. Using again the elementary inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we can write

(3.9)

\|\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k}\|\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}(f_{k}+f_{k-1})\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{k}\|\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}+2\sum_{k}\|\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2}.

Recall that
I_{j}^{\prime}=[j-d_{j}-\frac{1}{2},\ j+d_{j}-\frac{1}{2})
. Arguing as in the proof of (
3.8
), we obtain

\sum_{k}\|\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}^{-\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx,

and

\sum_{k}\|\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{k}\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}\int_{\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx.

From these inequalities and (
3.9
), we obtain

\|\sum_{k}\chi_{I_{k}^{\prime}}f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{k}\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}\Big(\int_{\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}\!\!\!\!|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx+\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}^{-\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}\!\!\!\!|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx\Big).

This inequality and (
3.8
), combined with (
3.7
), yield

(3.10)

\sum_{k}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\sum_{k}\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx.

Recall that the support of the function
x\to h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})
is in the interval
(-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k},\ \frac{1}{2}+d_{k})
; letting
m(t)=:\|h-h(.-t)\|_{2}
, we have that

\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-d_{k}}^{\frac{1}{2}+d_{k}}|h_{a}(x)-h_{a}(x-\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|h(ax)-h(ax-a\delta_{n,k})|^{2}dx

=a^{-1}\|h-h(\cdot-a\delta_{n,k})\|^{2}_{2}=a^{-1}m(a\delta_{n,k})^{2}.

We have used the change of variables
ax\to x
in the integral. We can write the inequality (
3.10
) as

\sum_{k}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4a^{-1}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\sum_{n}m(a\delta_{n,k})^{2}\Big)

from which follows that

\sum_{k}\|f_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4a^{-1}\sup_{k}\sum_{n}m(a\delta_{n,k})^{2}\sum_{n,k}|a_{n,k}|^{2}=4a^{-1}\sup_{k}\sum_{n}m(a\delta_{n,k})^{2}.

The inequality (
3.6
) is satisfied if

\lambda=:4\sup_{k}\sum_{n}m(a\delta_{n,k})^{2}<\sqrt{A}.

which is (
1.2
). The theorem is proved.
\Box

3.2.
Corollaries

Let
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with support in the interval
\left(-\frac{c}{2},\frac{c}{2}\right)
. As observed at the beginning of this section, if
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame, necessarily
a\leq c\leq\frac{1}{b}
; we have also observed that if
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame with frame constants
A
and
B
, then

\mathcal{G}(h_{a};1,ab)
is a frame with frame constants
a^{-1}A
and
a^{-1}B
. Here and throughout this subsection,
f_{a}(x)=f(ax)
.

For a given
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the
p
-times iterated convolution
h^{(p)}(x)=:\underbrace{h*\dots*h}_{p\ times}(x)
.

Using the properties of the convolution, it is easy to verify that the
h^{(p)}(x)
are supported in the interval
\left(-\frac{cp}{2},\frac{cp}{2}\right)
and are continuous for
p\geq 2
.
When
h=\operatorname{rect}
, the functions
\operatorname{rect}^{(p)}(x)
are piecewise polynomial of degree
p-1
and a prime example of a
B
-spline of order
p-1
(see e.g
[
20
]
,
[
7
]
and other classical references on B-splines).

We consider the set

\mathcal{G}(h^{(p)};pa,b/p)
. By the previous consideration, this set is a frame with constants
A_{p}
and
B_{p}
if and only if the set

\mathcal{G}(h^{(p)}_{ap};1,ab)
is a frame with constants
(ap)^{-1}A_{p}
and
(ap)^{-1}B_{p}
.
Note that the assumption
a\leq c\leq\frac{1}{b}
yields the necessary condition

pa\leq pc\leq\frac{1}{b/p}
that
\mathcal{G}(h^{(p)};pa,b/p)
needs to satisfy in order to be a frame.

The following easy lemma is perhaps already known, but we prove it here for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame with frame constants
A,\ B
, with
h
supported in
(-\frac{c}{2},\frac{c}{2})
. Assume also that

N(h)=:\sup_{x}\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x+kb)|^{\frac{4}{3}}<\infty.

Then for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\mathcal{G}(h^{(p)};pa,\,b/p)
is a frame with frame constants
A_{p},\ B_{p}
that satisfy the relations

B_{p}=a^{p-1}B^{p},

(3.11)

A_{1}=A;\quad A_{p+1}=a^{\frac{1}{p}+1}\Big(\frac{A_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}}{N(h)}\Big)^{2(p+1)}

Proof.

By
[
14
, Theorems 11.4, 11.8]
and the previous considerations, the set
\mathcal{G}(h^{(p)};pa,b/p)
is a frame if

A_{p}=:\frac{1}{ap}\inf_{x}\sum_{k}|\widehat{h^{(p)}}(x-bk/p)|^{2}>0\quad B_{p}=:\frac{1}{ap}\sup_{x}\sum_{k}|\widehat{h^{(p)}}(x-bk/p)|^{2}<\infty.

Recall that
A_{1}=A
and
B_{1}=B
are the frame constants of
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
.

The Fourier transform of the
p
-fold convolution of
h(x)
is the Fourier transform of
h(x)
raised to the power
p
, and so

(3.12)

A_{p}=\frac{1}{ap}\inf_{x}\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk/p)|^{2p},\quad B_{p}=\frac{1}{ap}\sup_{x}\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk/p)|^{2p}.

Every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
can be written as
k=k^{\prime}p+j
with
0\leq j\leq p-1
. Thus,

A_{p}=\frac{1}{ap}\inf_{x}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime}-jb/p)|^{2p}\geq\frac{1}{ap}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\inf_{x}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime}-bj/p)|^{2p}=\frac{1}{a}\inf_{x}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime})|^{2p}.

\quad B_{p}=\frac{1}{ap}\sup_{x}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk/p-bj/p)|^{2p}\leq\frac{1}{ap}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\sup_{x}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime}-bj/p)|^{2p}=\frac{1}{a}\sup_{x}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime})|^{2p}.

We let
B^{\prime}_{p}=:\sup_{x}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime})|^{2p}
and

A^{\prime}_{p}=:\inf_{x}\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime})|^{2p}
. Thus,

A_{p}\geq\frac{1}{a}A^{\prime}_{p}\mbox{\quad and \quad}B_{p}\leq\frac{1}{a}B^{\prime}_{p}.

and it is enough to estimate
A^{\prime}_{p}
and
B^{\prime}_{p}
.

Recall that for every
1\leq r\leq q<\infty
and every sequence
\{x_{n}\}_{n}\subset\mathbb{C}
, the inequality

(3.13)

\Big(\sum_{k}|x_{k}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\Big(\sum_{k}|x_{k}|^{r}\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}

holds. From this inequality follows that
(\sum_{k}|x_{k}|)^{r}\geq\sum_{k}|x_{k}|^{r}
whenever
r\geq 1
. Thus,

\sum_{k^{\prime}}|\hat{h}(x-bk^{\prime})|^{2p}\leq\sup_{x}\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2}\Big)^{p}=\sup_{x}\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2}\Big)^{p}=(aB)^{p},

and so
B^{\prime}_{p}\leq(aB)^{p}
.

To estimate
A^{\prime}_{p}
, we use Hölder’s inequality and induction on
p
. Letting

N(h)=:\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{\frac{4}{3}}\Big)^{\frac{3}{4}},

we start with the inequality

\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2}\leq\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{4}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{\frac{4}{3}}\Big)^{\frac{3}{4}}=\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{4}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}N(h)

from which we obtain

\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{4}\geq\Big(\frac{\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2}}{N(h)}\Big)^{4}\geq\Big(\frac{aA}{N(h)}\Big)^{4}.

and
(A_{2}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\Big(\frac{A_{1}^{\prime}}{N(h)}\Big)^{2}=\Big(\frac{aA}{N(h)}\Big)^{2}
.

When
p\geq 2
, we can write

\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2(p+1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(p+1)}}\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

where
q
satisfies
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2(p-1)}+\frac{1}{q}
.
Since
p\geq 2
, we have that
q\geq 2>\frac{4}{3}
. By the inequality (
3.13
),
\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq N(h)
; thus,

\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2(p+1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(p+1)}}N(h)

from which follows that

\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2(p+1)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(p+1)}}\geq\frac{\Big(\sum_{k}|\hat{h}(x-bk)|^{2p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{N(h)}\geq\frac{(A_{p}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p}}}{N(h)}

and
\displaystyle(A_{p+1}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\geq\Big(\frac{(A_{p}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{p}}}{N(h)}\Big)^{2}.
From this relation (
3.11
) follows.
∎

Remark
. Obtaining a closed-form estimate for the optimal frame constants of
\mathcal{G}(h^{(p)};pa,\,b/p)
is generally not feasible, as these expressions depend
heavily on the specific properties of the function
h
. The optimal frame constants of
\mathcal{G}(\operatorname{rect}^{(p)};ap,b/p)
were explicitly evaluated in
[
19
]
.

The following corollary generalizes
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
h
be as in Lemma
3.1
;
Let
\left\{\delta_{n,k}\right\}\subset(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
for which

(3.14)

\lambda=:4\|h\|_{1}^{p-1}\sup_{k}\sum_{n}\|h-h(.-ap\delta_{n,k})\|_{2}^{2}<A_{p},

where the
A_{p}
are defined by (
3.11
). Then, the set
\tilde{B}=\{e^{2\pi ibnx/p}h^{(p)}(x-ap(k+\delta_{n,k}))\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame with frame constants

A_{p}^{\prime}=:\Big(1-\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{A_{p}}}\,\Big)A_{p},\quad B_{p}^{\prime}=:\Big(1+\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{A_{p}}}\,\Big)B_{p}.

Proof.

We prove that

(3.15)

\|h^{(p)}-h^{(p)}(\cdot-ap\delta_{n,k})\|_{2}\leq\|h\|_{1}^{p-1}\|h-h(.-ap\delta_{n,k})\|_{2}^{2}.

By (
3.14
) and by Theorem
1.1
(with
h^{(p)}
replacing
h
and
ap
replacing
a
), the thesis of the corollary follows.

When
p=1
there is nothing to prove. When
p>1
, we can let
f_{p,d}(t)=h^{(p)}(t)-h^{(p)}(t-d)
and observe that

f_{p,d}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(s)\left(h^{(p-1)}(t-s)-h^{(p-1)}\left(t-d-s\right)\right)ds=h*f_{p-1,d}(t).

By Young’s inequality for convolution,

\|f_{p,d}\|_{2}\leq\|h\|_{1}\|f_{p-1,d}\|_{2}
,
and, if we apply this inequality
p-1
times, we obtain

\|f_{p,d}\|_{2}\leq\|h\|_{1}^{p-1}\|f_{1,d}\|_{2}=\|h\|_{1}^{p-1}\|h-h(\cdot-d)\|_{2}.

After replacing
d
with
ap\delta_{n,k}
, we obtain (
3.15
).

∎

4.
Other results

In this section we state and prove stability results for Gabor frames
\mathcal{G}(a,b,h)
for window functions
h
that do not necessarily have compact support.

4.1.
\bf h^{\prime}\ in\ W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})

Recall that a function
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
belongs to the
Wiener amalgam space

W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})
if

\|g\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}=:\sum_{m}\|g(.+m)\|_{L^{\infty}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})}<\infty.

It is not difficult to prove that

\|g\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}=\|g(.-t)\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Observe also that when
g
has compact support,
\|g\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}=\|g\|_{\infty}
.

In this section we prove the following

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
be a frame with frame constants
A,\,B
; assume that
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is
differentiable a.e., and
h^{\prime}\in W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})
. If

\lambda=:(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|h^{\prime}\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}\Big(\sum_{k}\sup_{n}|\delta_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\sqrt{A},

the set

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}=\{e^{2\pi ibnx}h(x-a(k+\delta_{n,k}))\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame with frame bounds

A^{\prime}=(1-\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{A}})A,\quad B^{\prime}=(1+\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{A}})B.

By ”a.e” we mean ”with the possible exception of a set of measure zero”.

If
h
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
and has compact support, then
h
must be continuous. Indeed, the derivative of a discontinuous function is a distribution because a discontinuity creates a singularity in the derivative, typically represented by a Dirac delta function,
and so
h^{\prime}
cannot be in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

If
h
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}
and differentiable a.e. on its support we can apply both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We let

f_{n}(x)=\sum_{k}a_{n,k}e^{2\pi iabn}(h(x-\mu_{n,k})-h(x-k))=\sum_{k}a_{n,k}e^{2\pi iabn}\int_{k}^{\mu_{n,k}}h^{\prime}(x-t)dt.

By Theorem
2.3
,
the thesis of Theorem
4.1
follows if we prove that

\displaystyle\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{2}\leq\lambda

for every finite sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum_{n,k}|a_{n,k}|^{2}=1
.

By duality,
\|f\|_{2}=\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})\atop{\|\psi\|_{2}=1}}\big|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{\psi}(x)dx\big|
;
with the triangle inequality and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we obtain

\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{2}\leq\sum_{m}\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}=\sum_{m}\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},+\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,+\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\left|\sum_{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\psi(m-x)dx\right|

(4.1)

=\sum_{m}\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\left|\sum_{n,k}a_{n,k}\int_{k}^{\mu_{n,k}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\pi iabnx}\psi(m-x)h^{\prime}(x-t)dx\,dt\right|

We let
d_{j}=\sup_{n}|\delta_{n,j}|
and we observe that
(k,\ \mu_{n,k})=(k,\,k+\delta_{n,k})\subset(k,\,k+d_{k})

when
\delta_{n,k}\geq 0
, and
(k,\,k+\delta_{n,k})\subset(k-d_{k},\,k)
when
\delta_{n,k}\leq 0
. Assuming that all
\delta_{n,k}
are
\geq 0
simplifies the notation, but will not change the substance of the proof.

Recalling the definition of short-term Fourier transform and using a change of variables in the second integral above, we gather

\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}=\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,+\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\left|\sum_{n,k}a_{n,k}\int_{k}^{\mu_{n,k}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\pi iabn(x+t)}\psi(m-t-x)h^{\prime}(x)dx\,dt\right|

\leq\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},+\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,+\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\sum_{k}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\left|\sum_{n}\,a_{n,k}\mathcal{F}^{\psi}(h)(abn,m-t)dt\right|.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to the sum in
n
inside the integral and Proposition
2.6
gives

\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}

\leq(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|F^{\psi}(h)(abn,m-t)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}dt

=(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(m-t-s)|^{2}|h^{\prime}(s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}dt.

After a change of variables in the second integral,

\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}\leq(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(s)|^{2}|h^{\prime}(m-t-s)|^{2}ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}dt

\leq(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})\atop{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{1}{2},\,\frac{1}{2})}=1}}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\sup_{y\in(m-\frac{1}{2},m+\frac{1}{2})}|h^{\prime}(y-t)|\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(s)|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}dt.

=(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\|h(.-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(m-\frac{1}{2},m+\frac{1}{2})}dt.

In view of (
4.1
), we gather

\sum_{m}\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}\leq(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int^{k+d_{k}}_{k}\sum_{m}\|h^{\prime}(.-t)\|_{L^{\infty}(m-\frac{1}{2},m+\frac{1}{2})}dt

=\|h^{\prime}\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k}d_{k}\Big(\sum_{n}\,|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Cauchy-Schwartz

\sum_{m}\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}\leq\|h^{\prime}\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k}|d_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By assumption,
\|h^{\prime}\|_{W(L^{\infty},\ell^{1})}(ab)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{k}|d_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\sqrt{A}
and so
we have proved that

\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{2}\leq\sum_{m}\|\sum_{n}f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(m-\frac{1}{2},\,m+\frac{1}{2})}<\sqrt{A}.

By Theorem
2.3
, the thesis of the theorem follows.
∎

4.2.
h
band-limited

In this section we assume that
\hat{h}
has compact support. As always, we assume that
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame with frame constants
A,\ B
.
We prove the following

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with Fourier transform supported in
(-\frac{M}{2},\frac{M}{2})
, with
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\{\delta_{n,k}\}\subset(-\frac{1}{4M},\frac{1}{4M})
be such that

\|\hat{h}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{M}{2\pi}}\Big(\sum_{n}(1-\cos(2\pi MD_{n})+\sin(2\pi MD_{n}))^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mu<\sqrt{A}

where we have let
D_{n}=\sup_{k}|\delta_{n,k}|
. Then, the set

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}=\{e^{2\pi ibnx}h(x-a(k+\delta_{n,k}))\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame with frame bounds

A^{\prime}=(1-\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}})A,\quad B^{\prime}=(1+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{A}})B.

Remark.
In view of (
2.5
), we have that
\frac{M}{2\pi}(1-\cos(2\pi MD_{n})+\sin(2\pi MD_{n}))\geq\frac{M^{2}}{\pi}D_{n}
, and so
it is enough to verify that

\frac{M}{\sqrt{\pi}}\|\hat{h}\|_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{n}D_{n}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mu<\sqrt{A}

Proof.

We have proved in section
3.1
that
\mathcal{G}(h;a,b)
is a frame with constants
A
and
B
if and only if the set

\mathcal{G}(h_{a};1,ab)=\{e^{2\pi iabnx}h_{a}(x-k)\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a frame with frame constants
a^{-1}A
and
a^{-1}B
.
Recall that
h_{a}(x)=h(ax)
, and
\mu_{n,k}
may be used in place of
k+\delta_{n,k}
.

Let
\{a_{n,k}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
\sum_{n,k}|a_{n,k}|=1
, and let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=:\left\|\sum_{n,k}\,a_{n,k}\,e^{2\pi iabnx}\left(h_{a}\left(\cdot-\mu_{n,k}\right)-h_{a}\left(\cdot-k\right)\right)\right\|_{2}
.
By Theorem
2.3
,
the thesis of Theorem
4.2
follows if we prove that

\mathcal{I}\leq\lambda a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{A},
with
\lambda<1
.

An application of Plancherel theorem gives

\mathcal{I}=\big\|\sum_{n,k}\,a_{n,k}\,\hat{h}_{a}(y-abn)\Big(e^{2\pi ik(abn-y)}-e^{2\pi i\mu_{n,k}(abn-y)}\Big)\big\|_{2}

=\big\|\sum_{n,k}\,a_{n,k}\,\hat{h}_{a}(y-abn)e^{2\pi ik(abn-y)}(1-e^{2\pi i\delta_{n,k}(abn-y)}\Big)\big\|_{2}.

We apply the triangle inequality and the change of variables
y\to y+abn
in the norm; letting
b_{n,k}=a_{n,k}e^{2\pi inkab}

and recalling that
\widehat{h}_{a}(yP)=\frac{1}{a}\hat{h}(\frac{y}{a})
, we can write the following chain of inequalities.

\mathcal{I}\leq\sum_{n}\big\|\widehat{h}_{a}(y-abn)\sum_{k}\,a_{n,k}\,e^{-2\pi iky}\Big(1-e^{2\pi i\delta_{n,k}(abn-y)}\Big)\big\|_{2}

=\sum_{n}\big\|\widehat{h}_{a}\sum_{k}\,b_{n,k}\,e^{-2\pi iky}\Big(1-e^{2\pi i\delta_{n,k}y}\Big)\big\|_{2}.

=a^{-1}\sum_{n}\big\|\hat{h}(\frac{y}{a})\sum_{k}\,b_{n,k}\,e^{-2\pi iky}\Big(1-e^{2\pi i\delta_{n,k}y}\Big)\big\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{aM}{2},\,\frac{aM}{2})}

\leq a^{-1}\|\hat{h}\|_{\infty}\sum_{n}\big\|\sum_{k}\,b_{n,k}\,e^{-2\pi iky}\Big(1-e^{2\pi i\delta_{n,k}y}\Big)\big\|_{L^{2}(-\frac{aM}{2},\,\frac{aM}{2})}.

By Lemma (
2.4
),

\mathcal{I}\leq a^{-1}\|\hat{h}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{aM}{2\pi}}\sum_{n}(1-\cos(\pi D_{n}aM)+\sin(\pi D_{n}aM))\Big(\sum_{k}|b_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

After applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, and observing that
|b_{n,k}|=|a_{n,k}|
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}\leq\|\hat{h}\|_{\infty}\sqrt{\frac{M}{2a\pi}}\Big(\sum_{n}(1-\cos(2\pi D_{n})+\sin(2\pi D_{n}))^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\sum_{n,k}|a_{n,k}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By assumption,
\sqrt{\frac{M}{2\pi}}\Big(\sum_{n}(1-\cos(2\pi D_{n})+\sin(2\pi D_{n}))^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\sqrt{A}
and so we have proved that
\mathcal{I}<a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{A}
, as required

∎

Remark
. Recalling that a Gabor system
\mathcal{G}=\{e^{2\pi ib_{n}t}h(t-a_{k})\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame if and only if

\hat{\mathcal{G}}=\{e^{2\pi ia_{k}s}\hat{h}(s-b_{n})\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a frame, we can use Theorems
1.1
,
4.2
and
4.1
to prove stability results for frames

\{e^{2\pi ib(n+\omega_{k,n})t}g(t-ak)\}_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}

when
g
or
\hat{g}
have compact support, or when
(\hat{g})^{\prime}
is in a Wiener amalgam space. We leave these generalizations to the interested reader.

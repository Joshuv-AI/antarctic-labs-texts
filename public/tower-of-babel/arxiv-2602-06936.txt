Higher rank bundles on Hopf surfaces
Edoardo Ballico and Elizabeth Gasparim

Abstract
. We show that all filtrable bundles on a Hopf surface X X must have jumps and we prove the existence of filtrable stable bundles on X X with any value of c 2 > 0 c_{2}>0 . On a somewhat opposite direction, for each integer r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 we prove the existence of irreducible rank r r vector bundles on X X with trivial determinant, c 2 = 1 c_{2}=1 , and no jumps. We then apply elementary operations in codimension 2 2 to points of the moduli space ℳ r , n \mathcal{M}_{r,n} of rank r r stable vector bundles on X X with c 2 = n c_{2}=n to obtain torsion free sheaves with c 2 = n + 1 c_{2}=n+1 . Namely, starting with a surjection v : E → ℂ p v\colon E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p} from a vector bundle E ∈ ℳ r , n E\in\mathcal{M}_{r,n} to a skyscraper sheaf supported at a point p ∈ X p\in X , we prove that if E ′ E^{\prime} is any torsion free sheaf fitting into a short exact sequence of the form 0 ⟶ E ′ ⟶ E ⟶ v ℂ p ⟶ 0 , 0\longrightarrow E^{\prime}\longrightarrow E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{C}_{p}\longrightarrow 0, then E ′ E^{\prime} is in the closure of ℳ r , n + 1 \mathcal{M}_{r,n+1} . We discuss various properties of vector bundles and torsion free sheaves and introduce the concept of very irreducible bundles to describe bundles whose symmetric powers S n ​ ( E ) S^{n}(E) are irreducible for all n > 0 n>0 . We then show that any rank 2 2 bundle on X X whose graph contains a component corresponding to a surjective morphism ℙ 1 → ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1} is very irreducible.



1.
Introduction

We study higher rank vector bundles on a classical Hopf surface
in terms of their stability, filtrability, and jumping behaviour. We discuss some features
of the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{r,n}
of rank
r\geq 2
bundles with

c_{2}=n
and construct
torsion free sheaves which are on their closure.
Such torsion free sheaves may be interpreted in terms of
instanton bubbling, because they correspond to the algebro-geometric version
of the phenomenon in differential geometry where a sequence of instanton connections degenerates
forming point instantons, that is, instantons concentrated at points.
In other words,
\mathcal{M}_{r,n}
may be regarded as instanton moduli
spaces having point instantons in its closure, described by Theorem
5.6

in terms of those
torsion free sheaves which
are the limits of families of generic elements (stable and locally free)
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{r,n}
.

In section
2
we give basic definitions of stability and jumping behaviour of bundles
on Hopf surfaces.

In section
3
we prove
that filtrable bundles on a classical Hopf surface having
c_{2}(E)>0
must have jumps (Theorem
3.9
).
We then prove the existence of stable and filtrable rank
r
vector bundles on
X

such that
\det(E)\cong\cal{O}_{X}
and
c_{2}(E)=c
for any integers
r\geq 2
and
c>0
(Theorem
3.19
).

In section
4
,
for each integer
r\geq 2
, we prove the existence of irreducible rank
r
vector bundles on
X
with trivial determinant,
c_{2}=1
and whose restriction to each fiber is semistable, i.e. with no jumps (Theorem
4.12
).

In section
5
, starting with elements of
\mathcal{M}_{r,n}
,
we construct families of torsion free sheaves living in the closure of

\mathcal{M}_{r,n+1}
. Namely,
assuming that
E
is a simple (e.g. stable) vector bundle over
X
of rank
r
,
we show that the kernels of the surjections
E\rightarrow p
with
p\in X

form a
2r-1
-dimensional family of pairwise non-isomorphic rank
r
torsion free sheaves on
X

(Proposition
5.5
) and prove that such torsion free sheaves are limits of vector bundles
(Theorem
5.6
).

In section
6
we introduce the concept of very irreducible bundles referring to those bundles such that
all symmetric powers
S^{n}(E)
with
n>0
are irreducible (Definition
6.1
)
and provide large families as examples (Theorems
6.3
and
6.4
). In conclusion, we show
that moduli of stable higher rank bundles on Hopf surfaces contain both filtrable and very irreducible bundles.

2.
Hopf surfaces and stability

This section contains basic definitions.
A
classical Hopf surface
is an elliptically fibered surface
X\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}

which is diffeomorphic to
S^{1}\times S^{3}
. More precisely,
a Hopf surface is a quotient of the punctured plane
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}

by the action of an infinite cyclic group generated by a contraction of
(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)
.
When the contraction is given by an action of the form

(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(\mu z_{1},\mu z_{2})
the quotient is called a classical Hopf surface.
Hence, it admits a natural holomorphic elliptic fibration

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\pi\colon X&\rightarrow&\mathbb{P}^{1}\\
(z_{1},z_{2})&\mapsto&[z_{1}:z_{2}]\end{array}

with fiber the elliptic curve
T=\mathbb{C}^{\ast}/\mu
.

Given a classical Hopf surface
X
, the Picard group of all holomorphic line bundles on
X
is given by

\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\frac{H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O})}{H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})}=\frac{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{C}^{*},

and since
H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O})=0
,
every line bundle on
X
has zero first Chern class, hence

\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)=\operatorname{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{C}^{*}.

The relative Jacobian of a classical Hopf surface
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{P}^{1}

is

J(X)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times T^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{1}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{P}^{1},

where
T^{\ast}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(T)
is the dual elliptic curve.
Holomorphic rank
2
vector bundles on elliptically fibered Hopf surfaces were discussed in
[
BMo
,
Mo1
,
Mo2
]
.
Recall that a bundle on
X
is
referred to as
regular
when it is regular on every fiber of
X\mapsto\mathbb{P}^{1}
, that is, when its
automorphism group has minimal dimension, on every fiber. A bundles is called
irregular
if it is not
regular. Hypersurfaces of the moduli of rank
2
bundles on the Hopf surface
formed by irregular bundles were described
in
[
BG
]
.

A metric on
X
is called
Gauduchon
if its associated
(1,1)
-form
\omega
satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}\omega=0
.
The degree of a line bundle
L
on
X
is defined as

\deg(L)=\int_{X}F\wedge\omega

where
F
is the curvature of a Hermitian connection on
L
compatible with
\overline{\partial}_{L}
.
For a coherent sheaf
E
, de
degree
is defined as

\deg(E)=\deg\det(E)

and the
slope
by

\mu(E)=\frac{\deg(E)}{\rank(E)}.

Up to a scalar there is a unique Gauduchon metric on
X
(we fix it)
so that each
f^{\ast}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(t))
has degree
t
.
Stability is then defined as usual, that is,

E
is
stable
any subsheaf
F\hookrightarrow E
satisfies

\mu F<\mu E
.

Atiyah
[
A
]
proved that an
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
-bundle over an elliptic curve
T

is of one of three possible types:

\iota)

L_{0}\oplus L_{0}^{*}
, with
L_{0}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(T)
.

\iota\iota)

Non-trivial extensions
0\rightarrow L_{0}\rightarrow E\rightarrow L_{0}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0
, with
L_{0}^{2}=\mathcal{O}
.

\iota\iota\iota)

L\oplus L^{*}
, with
L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{k}(T)
,
k<0
.

Given an
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
-bundle
E
over the Hopf surface
\pi\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
its restriction to the generic fiber of
\pi
is of type
\iota)
or
\iota\iota)
. Furthermore,
the restriction is of type
\iota\iota\iota)
at finitely many fibers
[
BH
, Prop 3.2.2]
;
named the jumping fibers of
E
.
Observe that in the rank
2
case
T
is a jumping fiber of
E
precisely when
E_{|T}
is not
semistable. Therefore, for rank any rank
r
, we define jumps as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the vector bundle
E
has a
jump
at
T

if the restriction
E_{|T}
is not
semistable. If
E_{|T}
is semistable at all fibers
T
of
\pi
it is said to have no jumps.

Given a rank 2 bundle
E
on
X
, let
V
be the universal Poincaré line bundle on

X\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
and consider the first derived image
R^{1}\pi_{*}(E\otimes V)
where

\pi
the projection
\pi\colon X\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
The sheaf
R^{1}\pi_{*}(E\otimes V)
is supported on a divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
which descends to a divisor

D\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}=\pi(X)\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(T)/\pm 1.

When
c_{2}(E)=n
, the divisor

G(E)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=D
belongs to the linear system
|\mathcal{O}(n,1)|
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

and is called the
graph
of
E
.

Note that here there is a 2-sheeted map
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(T)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, whose
branch points are the half-periods.
Such divisor takes the form
D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(x_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})+Gr(F)
where
x_{i}

are the location of the jumping lines of
X
and
F\colon\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a
rational map of degree
n-k
.
[
BH
]
also show that if this divisor includes the
graph of a non-constant rational map, then the bundle
E
is stable. In particular,
this implies that all rank
2
bundles on
X
with
c_{2}>0
and without jumps are stable.

3.
Filtrable bundles with jumps

The main goal of this section is to prove Th.
3.19
, showing the existence of
filtrable stable vector bundles on the
Hopf surface
X
with trivial determinant and having any rank
r\geq 2

and any prescribed value of positive second Chern class.
In this section, we also prove Th.
3.9
that shows that filtrable bundles must have jumps.

We first recall the definition of filtrability.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
Y
be a smooth and connected compact complex manifold.
A rank
r>1
torsion free sheaf
E
on
Y
is said to be
filtrable

if there are sheaves

0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots E_{i}\subset\cdots\subset E_{r}=E

such that each
E_{i}
has rank
i
and
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
has no torsion for
i=2,\dots,r
.

The latter condition may be omitted (Rem.
3.2
). We say that a rank
1
torsion free sheaf is filtrable. If
Y
is projective, then every torsion free sheaf on
Y
is filtrable (Rem.
3.4
). We are
mostly interested in the case when
E
is locally free
[
Br1
, Def. 4.6]
, but even
then, it would be too restrictive to assume that
E_{r}/E_{r-1}
are line bundles (Rem.
3.5
, Lem.
3.6
).

The following well-known construction is related to
[
HL
, Def. 1.1.5]
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
E
be a rank
r>1
torsion free sheaf on
Y
with a filtration
F_{\ast}=\{0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots F_{i}\subset\cdots\subset F_{r}=E\}
with
\mathrm{rank}(F_{i})=i
for all
i
. We want to obtain another filtration
0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots E_{i}\subset\cdots\subset E_{r}=E
such that each
E_{i}
has rank
i
and
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
has no torsion for
i=2,\dots,r
. Since
E
has no torsion, each
F_{i}
has no torsion. Call
v\colon E\xrightarrow{\ \ }E/F_{r-1}
the quotient map. Let
Q\subset E/F_{r-1}
be the torsion of
E/F_{r-1}
. Hence
Q=0
if and only if
E/F_{r-1}
is torsion free. Set
E_{r-1}=v^{-1}(Q)
.
Note that
E_{r-1}
is a rank
r-1
subsheaf of
E
containing
F_{r-1}
and hence containing
F_{r-2}
. If
r=2
, then
0\subset E_{1}\subset E
is the promised filtration. Assume
r>2
.
Then we continue with
F_{0}\subset\cdots F_{r-2}\subset E_{r-1}
. The filtration
0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots E_{i}\subset\cdots\subset E_{r}=E
is called the
saturation
of
F_{\ast}
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
E
has rank
2
, then
E
is filtrable if and only if it is not irreducible, hence it has
a proper subsheaf of rank
1
. In rank
2
a bundle is filtrable if is not stable with
respect to any Gauduchon metric.

Remark 3.4
.

We recall that
on a projective manifold every torsion free sheaf is filtrable
[
Br1
, p. 91]
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
E
be a rank
r>1
vector bundle with a filtration
0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots E_{i}\subset\cdots\subset E_{r}=E
such that each
E_{i}
has rank
i
and
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
is a line bundle. We say that
E
is an iterated filtration by line bundles. This is a very restrictive assumption. For instance, let
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
n>1
, be a projective space,
then we have
h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},L)=0
for all line bundles
L
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Hence every short exact sequence
with a line bundle on the right and a direct sum of line bundles on the left splits. Thus, by induction on
r
we get that each iterated filtration by line bundles is isomorphic to a direct sum of line bundles.
Therefore, neither
T\mathbb{P}^{n}
nor stable bundles are iterated filtrations by line bundles.
See Remark
3.6
for the main reason to avoid the restrictive definition with line bundles on Hopf surface.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
X
be a Hopf surface. Let
E
be a rank
r>1
vector bundle on
X
which is an iterated filtration of line bundles,
then
c_{2}(E)=0
.

Proof.

To verify this fact, consider an exact sequence of vector bundles on
X

(1)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }A\xrightarrow{\ \ }B\xrightarrow{\ \ }C\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

The Chern classes of the middle term of a short exact sequence are computed in terms of the Chern classes of the left and right terms. Clearly,
\det(B)\cong\det(A)\otimes\det(C)
. Since
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})=0
, the intersection form
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\times H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{4}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
is the zero-form. Therefore,
c_{1}(A)\cdot c_{1}(C)=0
and

c_{2}(B)=c_{2}(A)+c_{2}(C)
. Since
c_{2}(L)=0
for every line bundle
L
, we get
c_{2}(E)=0
by induction on
r
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
Y
be a smooth compact surface. Let
E
be a rank
r>1
vector bundle
E
equipped with a filtration
0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\cdots E_{i}\subset\cdots\subset E_{r}=E
such that each
E_{i}
has rank

and
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
has no torsion for

. Then each

Proof.

The sheaf
E/E_{r-1}
has on torsion by assumption. If
1\leq i\leq r-2
the sheaf
E/E_{i}
has no torsion, because it is an iterated extension of torsion free sheaves. Since
Y
is a smooth surface,
E_{i}
is a subsheaf of the locally free sheaf
F
and

has no torsion,

is locally free.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Let

[

Theorem 3.9
.

Let

be a filtrable vector bundle on a classical Hopf surface

Recall (e.g.

Note that in Theorem

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{p},E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{p},E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }0\text{.}

Recall that to conclude the proof it is sufficient to show that

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})=0
.
By (
5
) it would be sufficient to prove that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,E^{\prime})=0
.

Applying the functor
\operatorname{Ext}(E,-)
to (
12
)
we get the long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Hom}(E,E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Hom}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})

(14)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,E)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

Since
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
, (
5
) shows that to conclude the proof it would be sufficient to prove that

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})=0
.

Since
E
is locally free,
we have
\mathscr{E}\text{\kern-2.0pt{\it{xt}}}\,^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})=0
for all
i\geq 1
.
Since
\mathbb{C}_{p}
is supported on
p
,
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})
is supported on
p
.
Hence
h^{i}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p}))=0
for all
i\geq 1
.
Since
h^{1}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p}))=0
,
h^{0}(\mathscr{E}\text{\kern-2.0pt{\it{xt}}}\,^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p}))=0
and
h^{0}(\mathscr{E}\text{\kern-2.0pt{\it{xt}}}\,^{2}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p}))=0
, the local-to-global spectral sequence
[
MC
, p. 5]

gives
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(E,\mathbb{C}_{p})=0
, therefore it follows that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E^{\prime},E^{\prime})=0
.
∎

6.
Very irreducible bundles

In the algebro-geometric set-up there is the following notion of very stable bundle with respect to a polarization, see
[
Dr
,
La
,
DP
,
PP
,
Pe
]
. In this context, a
G
-bundle is called
very stable
if it has no nonzero nilpotent
Higgs fields. Very stable bundles are stable, and call a bundle is called
wobbly
if it is stable but not very stable.
Similar definitions may be given for holomorphic vector bundles
[
EFK
]
and also in the non-Kähler set-up respect to a Gauduchon metric
\deg_{g}
with minor modifications.
However, this is for us only a preliminary step before a stronger notion. An irreducible bundle is stable with respect to all Gauduchon metrics, but we do not need a metric to define and test them. Hence it is easier to consider them on possibly singular complex spaces.

Let
Y
be an irreducible compact complex space. M. Toma gave the following definition:

Definition
.

[
To
]

A holomorphic vector bundle
E
on
Y
is said to be
strongly irreducible
if for every finite holomorphic map
f\colon Y_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }Y
with
Y_{1}
an irreducible compact complex space the bundle
f^{\ast}(E)
is irreducible.

In the case when
Y
is a surface and
E
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
Y
, M. Toma proved that
E
is strongly irreducible if and only if
h^{0}(Y,L\otimes S^{n}(E))
for all positive integers
n
and all line bundles
L
on
Y

[
To
, Prop. at p.242 ]
.
Motivated by Toma’s result we introduce the following definition.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle on a compact and connected complex manifold.
We say that
E
is
very irreducible
if the symmetric power
S^{n}(E)
are irreducible for all positive integers
n
.

Remark 6.2
.

In the algebro-geometric context tensor products of semistable bundles (with respect to an ample polarization) are semistable. Hence the tensor product of stable bundles is semistable, although not always stable. For instance for any vector bundle
G
of rank at least
2
we have
G^{\otimes 2}\cong S^{2}(G)\oplus\wedge^{2}(G)
. Hence, being decomposable,
G^{\otimes 2}
is never stable.

Now, on our Hopf surface
u\colon X\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}
we search for irreducible bundles
E
such that all bundles
S^{n}(E)
are irreducible.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
E
be a rank
2
bundle on
X
whose graph has a component corresponding to a
surjective morphism
\mathbb{P}^{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
E
is very irreducible.

Since
S^{n}(E)
has rank
n+1
, we get families of rigid vector bundles of arbitrary ranks.

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

By
[
BH
, Prop. 3.3.3]
the case
n=1
is true. Thus, we may assume
n>1
. Note that
S^{n}(E)
has rank
n+1
. Assume that
S^{n}(E)
is not irreducible and let
F\subset E
be a rank
s
subsheaf of
E
with
1\leq s\leq n
. Taking instead of
F
its saturation in
E
we may assume that the sheaf
E/F
has no torsion. Since
X
is a smooth surface,
F
is a vector bundle. The inclusion
F\subset S^{n}(E)
induces a non-zero map
j:\wedge^{s}(F)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\wedge^{s}(S^{n}(E))
.
The sheaf
A:=\wedge^{s}(F)
is a line bundle. The stable bundle
E
has only finitely many jumping fibers, i.e. there is a finite set
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
E_{|u^{-1}(p)}
is semistable for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S
. In
[
A
, Part 3]
Atiyah proved that the tensor product of
2
semistable bundles on an elliptic curve is semistable. Since
S^{n}(E)
is a direct factor of
E^{\otimes n}
,
S^{n}(E)_{|u^{-1}(p)}
is semistable for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S
. Since
\wedge^{s}(S^{n}(E)
is a direct factor of
S^{n}(E)^{\otimes s}
,
\wedge^{s}(S^{n}(E))_{|u^{-1}(p)}
is semistable for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S
. Note that

\wedge^{s}(S^{n}(E))_{|u^{-1}(p)}
is a direct factor of
(E_{|u^{-1}(p)})^{\otimes ns}
. Thus, knowing the decomposition of
E_{|u^{-1}(p)}
gives a strong restriction on the indecomposable factors of
\wedge^{s}(S^{n}(E))_{|u^{-1}(p)}
. By Remark
4.5
the restriction of
L
to each fiber of
u
is the same. The key idea in
[
Bu
, Proof of 3.3.3]
is that the direct factors of
E_{|u^{-1}(p)}
varies with the point
p
. Hence outside a finite set
S_{1}\supset S
the line bundle
L_{|u^{-1}(p)}
is not one of the finitely many factors (all of degree
0
by semistability) of the vector bundle
\wedge^{s}(S^{n}(E))_{|u^{-1}(p)}
. Hence
j
vanishes at each point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S_{1}
. Thus
j=0
, a contradiction.
∎

The definition of irreducibility is stated for arbitrary torsion free sheaves. Let
F
be a rigid vector bundle on
X
. For instance, we may take as
F
the rank
n+1
rigid vector bundle
S^{n}(E)
given by Theorem
6.3
. Set
c:=c_{2}(F)
. Fix any integer
c_{2}>c
. Let
G\subset F
be a torsion free sheaf obtained from
F
making
c_{2}-c
positive elementary transformations. The sheaves
F
and
G
have the same rank and the same determinant. We have
c_{2}(G)=c_{2}
. Note that
F
is irreducible if and only if
G
is irreducible.

The proof of Theorem
6.3
gives the following more general result.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
E
be a rank
2
bundle on
X
such that its graph has a component which is a graph of a
morphism
\mathbb{P}^{1}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Fix an integer
n\geq 2
and let
\rho
be an irreducible complex representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
. Then either
S^{\rho}(E)=0
or
S^{\rho}(E)
is irreducible.

Proof.

The proof of Theorem
6.3
works without modification.
We only need to describe all cases in which
S^{\rho}(E)=0
. We have
\mathrm{rank}(E)=2
.
The
S^{\rho}
-part of
E^{\otimes n}
or
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
is
obtained by making symmetrizations and skew-symmetrizations.
∎

Remember that, for a classical Hopf, if non-empty,
\cal{M}_{r,n}
is smooth and equidimensional
with
\dim\cal{M}_{r,n}=2rn
. We have
\cal{M}_{r,n}\neq\emptyset
for all
n>0
.
By Theorem
3.19
for all
r\geq 2
and all
n\geq 1
there is a stable and filtrable bundle.
Fix
r\geq 2
,
n\geq 1
, and
E\in\cal{M}_{r,n}
. We know that

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(E,E)=0
. Take a small neighborhood
\cal{N}_{r,n}
of
E
in
\cal{M}_{r,n}
on which
a universal bundles exists. Or, more generally, call
\cal{N}_{r,n}
any smooth complex manifold
equipped with a map
j\colon\cal{N}_{r,n}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{M}_{r,n}
whose image contains a neighborhood of
E
and on which there is a family of bundles
\cal{E}
such that over
x\in\cal{N}_{r,n}
it restricts to a bundle isomorphic to
u(x)
. Taking duals we get
that
\cal{N}_{r,n}
has the dual universal bundle
\cal{E}^{\ast}
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\cal{N}_{r,n}\times X\times\mathbb{P}(\cal{N}_{r,n})
denote the set of all triples

(F,p,v)
, where
F\in\cal{N}_{r,n}
,
p\in X
and
v\in\mathbb{P}(F^{\ast}_{|p})
. Let
v_{1}
be an element of the
r
-dimensional vector space
F^{\ast}_{|p}
inducing
v
. We have
v_{1}\neq 0
and any
2
elements of
F^{\ast}_{|p}
inducing
v
are proportional. Since
v_{1}\neq 0
,
v_{1}\colon E_{|p}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{C}
is a surjection. Hence
v

uniquely determines the rank
r
torsion free sheaf
F^{\prime}\subset F
. We proved that
\Gamma
is equipped with a family of rank
r
torsion free sheaves
with
c_{2}=n+1
, a unique non-locally free point, smooth deformation spaces
(more precisely,
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(X;F^{\prime},F^{\prime})=0
) and that each
F^{\prime}\in\Gamma
is a flat limit
of elements of
M_{r,n+1}
. We have
\dim\Gamma=\dim\cal{N}_{r,n}+r+1
. We also proved that two isomorphic sheaves
F^{\prime},F^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma
come from the same
F\in\cal{M}_{r,n}
,
the same
p\in X
and the same
v\in\mathbb{P}(F^{\ast}_{|p})
.
Hence, if we take
\cal{M}_{r,n}
as a neighborhood of
E
, then
\dim\Gamma=2rn+r+1
.
Since we started with
E\in\cal{M}_{r,n}
,
n>0
, all of these torsion free but not locally free sheaves
have
c_{2}\geq 2
. This is sharp for the following reason.

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
E
be a rank
r
torsion free sheaf on
X
which
is either stable of semistable and such that
c_{2}(E)=1
. Then
E
is locally free.

Proof.

Assume that
E
is not locally free, i.e. assume that
A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=E^{\vee\vee}/E
is a non-zero sheaf. Since
X
is a smooth surface,
A
is supported by finitely many points. Since
A\neq 0
,
z:=h^{0}(X,A)>0
. We have
c_{2}(E^{\vee\vee})=1-z\leq 0
. Hence
E^{\vee\vee}
is not stable, contradicting
[
Br1
, Prop. 5.27]
applied twice (first it gives that
E^{\vee}
is stable, because
E
is stable, then it gives that
E^{\vee\vee}
is stable, see Rem.
4.3
). The same proof works for the semistable case.
∎

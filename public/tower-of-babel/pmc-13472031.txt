Efficacy and safety of semaglutide injection in Indian patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus inadequately controlled on metformin: A phase 3, randomized, active-controlled, multicenter, non-inferiority trial (WIN-IND study)

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundType 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder with a rapidly rising global burden. Present study established the non-inferiority and evaluated the safety of Intas semaglutide (test) compared to Innovator semaglutide (reference) in patients with T2DM inadequately controlled on metformin.MethodsThis prospective, randomized, open-label, multicenter phase 3 trial, conducted between July-2025 and February-2026, enrolled adult patients (18-65 years) with T2DM and HbA1c level ≥7%–<10.5% at baseline, who had been on diet, exercise and metformin (≥1500 mg/day). Randomized (1:1) patients received either test or reference product, once weekly from 1 to 24 weeks. Primary endpoint was the change in HbA1c level. Secondary endpoints were the change in fasting blood glucose (FBG) level, post-prandial blood glucose (PPBG) level, bodyweight, body mass index (BMI), and lipid parameters. Safety assessment included monitoring of treatment emerged adverse events (TEAEs).ResultsStudy analyzed 223 (safety set) and 217 (ITT set) patients. Baseline characteristics were comparable between treatment groups. In the ITT (LOCF) set, at week-24, LSM change in HbA1c was −1.50 vs. −1.65 in test vs. reference group, respectively; LSM difference was 0.16 (95% CI: −0.16 to 0.47, P = 0.3266), confirming non-inferiority of the test product. Other efficacy parameters (FBG and PPBG levels, bodyweight and BMI) were also similarly reduced in the test group. The incidence, severity, and pattern of TEAEs were similar, without new safety signals identified.ConclusionIntas semaglutide showed non-inferiority to reference semaglutide with comparable efficacy, immunogenicity, and safety which may provide a cost-effective alternative treatment option to Indian patients with T2DM.Clinical trial registration numberCTRI/2025/06/089290Keywords: Diabetes mellitus, Efficacy, HbA1c, Hyperglycemia, Metformin, Randomized controlled trial, Safety, Semaglutide, Tolerability

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic, progressive metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction, resulting in persistent hyperglycaemia and long-term vascular complications [1]. The global burden of diabetes mellitus (DM) has increased substantially in recent decades due to rapid urbanization, aging populations, and lifestyle transitions [1]. International Diabetes Federation (IDF) Diabetes Atlas report estimated that approximately 589 million adults were affected by DM worldwide in 2024, with projections expected to rise to 900 million by 2050 [2]. India represents a major contributor to this burden, with nearly 89.8 million affected adults (prevalence ∼10.5%), placing it among the countries with the highest diabetes prevalence globally [3].

Metformin remains the recommended first-line therapy for T2DM owing to its established efficacy, safety, and affordability [4]. However, due to the progressive nature of the disease, combination therapy is frequently required, including agents such as sulfonylureas, thiazolidinediones, and insulin [5,6]. Despite these options, a substantial proportion of patients fail to achieve optimal glycaemic control. Conventional therapies are associated with several adverse effects and risks of hypoglycaemia and weight gain [5,6]. Moreover, evidence suggests that metformin may not consistently improve cardiovascular outcomes across all populations [4]. These limitations underscore the need for novel therapies that provide durable glycaemic control while addressing broader cardiometabolic risk.

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs), particularly semaglutide, represent a significant advancement in T2DM management [7,8]. Semaglutide acts by mimicking endogenous GLP-1, enhancing glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppressing glucagon release, delaying gastric emptying, and promoting satiety [7,8]. Evidence from prospective clinical trials, including the SUSTAIN programme, has demonstrated significant reductions in glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c) and body weight, establishing efficacy, safety and tolerability in patients with T2DM along with cardiovascular and renal benefits [[9], [10], [11], [12]], making it a reference standard for treating and managing T2DM. However, these GLP-1RAs, including semaglutide, are associated with adverse events (AEs).

India bears one of the largest burdens of diabetes globally driven by genetic and lifestyle factors, yet access to advanced therapies remains limited due to the high cost of innovator drugs. These therapies, though clinically effective in reducing HbA1c, weight, and cardiovascular risk, are often unaffordable for a majority of patients. Intas Pharmaceuticals Ltd. has developed a cost-effective generic semaglutide aiming to address this gap by significantly lowering costs and expanding access to effective diabetes management. The present multicenter, randomized, active-controlled, phase 3 trial was conducted to establish the non-inferiority and evaluate the safety of semaglutide injection (developed by Intas Pharmaceuticals Ltd.) compared with the innovator semaglutide injection (manufactured by Novo Nordisk) in Indian patients with T2DM inadequately controlled on metformin.

Methods

Study design and patients

This was a prospective, randomized, open-label, parallel-group, active-controlled, multicenter phase 3 trial conducted between July 2025 to February 2026 at 29 different sites across India. The total study duration was 26 weeks: screening period (2 weeks) and treatment period (from baseline to week 24). Patients of either sex, aged 18 to 65 years (both inclusive), with T2DM having HbA1c level ≥7% to <10.5% at baseline, who had been on diet and exercise control and who were receiving metformin (≥1500 mg/day or maximum tolerated dose based on clinical record) within 12 weeks prior to the day of screening were included in the study. Patients with known allergies, hypersensitivity, or intolerance to any of the study interventions and other GLP-1 receptor agonists, or components/excipients thereof, or drug or other allergies and patients with fasting blood glucose ≥270 mg/dL and BMI ≥45 kg/m2 at screening were excluded. Detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are summarized in Supplemental Material.

Sample size calculation

Assuming a non-inferiority margin of 0.4, an expected difference of 0 between treatments, a standard deviation of 1.0, with a significance level (alpha) of 2.5%, 80% power, and 1:1 treatment allocation ratio, the required sample size was estimated to be 200 participants (100 participants per treatment group).

Randomization and treatments

All eligible patients were randomized by central block randomization schedule using an integrated software system in treatment allocation ratio of 1:1 to receive either test product or reference product once weekly for the treatment period of 24 weeks. Test product was semaglutide injection (developed by Intas Pharmaceuticals Ltd.) and the reference product was semaglutide injection (manufactured by Novo Nordisk). Assessments

The primary objective was to establish non-inferiority of semaglutide test product compared to the reference product in patients with T2DM who were inadequately controlled on metformin, adjunct to diet and exercise. The secondary objective included assessment of safety and immunogenicity of semaglutide test product vs. reference product in the same set of patients. Key efficacy assessments included change in HbA1c level, fasting blood glucose (FBG) level, post prandial blood glucose (PPBG) level, bodyweight and body mass index (BMI) and fasting lipid profile parameters. Key safety assessments included the monitoring of treatment emerged adverse events (TEAEs), adverse events of specific interest (AESI), physical examinations, electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring, clinical laboratory parameters, vital sign measurements and hypoglycemia assessment. Immunogenicity analysis included proportion of patients with anti-drug antibody (ADAs) and neutralizing antibody (NAb) at baseline, week 12, week 20 and week 24.

Statistical analysis

Safety set and immunogenicity set included patients who received at least one dose of the study medication while intent-to-treat (ITT) set included patients who received at least one dose of the study medication and underwent at least one efficacy evaluation. Patients’ demographic and baseline characteristics were summarized descriptively using mean, standard deviation (SD), median (min, max), frequency (n) and percentage (%). Continuous variables were summarized using summary statistics (number of observations, mean, SD, median, minimum, and maximum values) while categorical values were summarized using n (%). A point estimate and 95% confidence interval (CI) were computed using ANCOVA with baseline HbA1c as a covariate and treatment as a factor for ITT set and reported for primary efficacy endpoint of both treatment groups and their differences. For the ITT set, missing primary endpoint data were imputed using the LOCF (Last Observation Carried Forward) method. Non-inferiority of test product compared to the reference product was concluded if the lower limit of 95% CI of the difference between two treatment groups was greater than non-inferiority margin (−0.4) for the ITT (LOCF) set. P-values were calculated and reported for between-group comparisons using independent t-test. Statistical significance was considered if P < 0.05. All the statistical analyses were performed using SAS® Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., USA).

Ethical considerations

This trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, International Council for Harmonisation–Good Clinical Practice (ICH–GCP) guidelines, and local regulatory requirements. The study protocol was reviewed and duly approved by the institutional ethics committees (IEC) of all study centers. Written informed consents were obtained from all patients. The trial was registered at Clinical Trials Registry–India (CTRI/2025/06/089290).

Primary efficacy endpoint

In the ITT (LOCF) set, baseline HbA1c levels were similar between the test vs. reference group (Fig. 2). HbA1c levels were reduced through week 24 from baseline in the test vs. reference group (Fig. 2). However, the between-group difference was not statistically significant. In the ITT (LOCF) set, LSM change in HbA1c was −1.50 and −1.65 in the test vs. reference group, respectively. The LSM difference was 0.16 (95% CI: −0.16 to 0.47, P = 0.3266), confirming non-inferiority of the test product compared to the reference product as the lower limit of the CI (−0.16) was well above the pre-specified margin of −0.4. Secondary efficacy endpoints

Glycaemic parameters

In the ITT set, baseline mean ± SD FBG and PPBG levels (mg/dL) were comparable between the test (FBG: 137.3 ± 37.2; PPBG: 201.7 ± 60.1) and reference (FBG: 138.6 ± 38.6; PPBG: 209.3 ± 67.4) groups. At Week 24, both treatments produced progressive reductions in FBG (Test: −21.9 ± 38.0; Reference: −22.5 ± 40.0) and PPBG (Test: −37.4 ± 68.4; Reference: −36.5 ± 71.5) (Fig. 3). The magnitude of glucose reduction was similar between groups, with no statistically significant difference (FBG: P = 0.9107, PPBG: P = 0.9215) between groups. These findings showed comparable glycaemic efficacy of the test and reference products over 24 weeks.

Body weight and BMI

In the ITT set, baseline mean ± SD body weight (kg) was 80.7 ± 15.7 (test) and 79.0 ± 16.7 (reference). At Week 24, weight reductions were −6.0 ± 3.3 (test) and −5.8 ± 3.7 (reference) (P = 0.7368), indicating comparable efficacy (Fig. 3). At week 24, the proportions of patients achieving ≥5%, ≥7.5%, and ≥10% weight loss were generally comparable between the test and reference groups in the ITT set (≥5%: 69.4% [test] vs. 66.1% [reference], ≥7.5%: 47.2% vs. 33.9%, and ≥10%: 19.4% vs. 15.6%). Similarly, baseline mean ± SD BMI (kg/m2) was 30.7 ± 5.7 (test) and 30.4 ± 5.0 (reference). At Week 24, change in BMI were −2.2 ± 1.4 (test) and −2.2 ± 1.3 (reference) (P = 0.8654), indicating comparable efficacy.

Proportion of patients achieving HbA1c <7.0% and <6.5%

In the ITT set, the proportion of patients achieving HbA1c <7.0% increased over time in both groups. At Week 12, 34.3% of patients in the test group and 33.9% in the reference group achieved HbA1c <7.0%. By Week 24, 43.5% and 39.4% of patients in the test and reference groups, respectively, achieved HbA1c <6.5%. These proportions should be interpreted in the context of the relatively high baseline HbA1c and the structured dose-escalation algorithm.

Proportion of patients receiving rescue medications

A total of 5.6% (6/108) patients in both treatment groups received rescue medications in the ITT set.

Immunogenicity

In safety set, immunogenicity profiles of the test product and the reference product were comparable across all assessment time points. At screening (pre-dose, Day 1), potentially positive ADA responses were observed in 10.7% of patients in the test group and 12.6% in the reference group, with confirmatory positive results in 1.8% and 0% of patients, respectively. Post-dose assessments showed low and fluctuating rates of potentially positive screening results in both groups, ranging from 10.7% to 12.5% for the test product and 3.6% to 8.1% for the reference product through the end of study (Week 24). Confirmatory ADA positivity remained low and comparable (≤3.6%) between groups at all post-dose time points. Neutralizing antibody positivity mirrored confirmatory anti-drug antibody results and was similarly infrequent, with maximum incidences of 2.7% (test product) and 0.9% (reference product) across visits. Overall, temporal pattern of anti-drug antibody and neutralizing antibody responses were similar between the test and reference products, indicating comparable immunogenicity profiles. However, immunogenicity results showed no impact on efficacy/safety parameters.

Safety

Incidence and types of TEAEs are presented in Table 3. A total of 250 TEAEs were reported by 46.6% (104/223) patients, of which 152 TEAEs were reported by 54.5% (61/112) patients receiving the test product and 98 TEAEs were reported by 38.7% (43/111) of patients from receiving the reference product. Most TEAEs in both the groups were mild to moderate in severity; mild events (mild events: 47.3% vs. 35.1%, moderate events: 9.8% vs. 8.1% in test vs. reference, respectively). No deaths, serious or significant TEAEs were reported. The most frequently reported TEAEs were nausea (7.1% vs. 8.1% in test vs. reference), diarrhoea (6.3% vs. 9.0%), constipation (6.3% vs. 0%), vomiting (6.3% vs. 9.0%), and gastritis (6.3% vs. 5.4%). Overall, the type, frequency, and severity of TEAEs were similar between the test and reference products, indicating comparable safety profiles (Table 3).

Discussion

In this prospective, randomized, open-label, parallel-group, active-controlled, multicenter phase 3 trial, patients’ baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. Both treatments produced similar and clinically meaningful improvements in glycaemic control over 24 weeks, with no significant between-group differences in HbA1c, confirming non-inferiority of the test product compared to the reference product. Reductions in FBG, PPBG, body weight, and BMI were also comparable, and higher proportion of patients achieved HbA1c targets over week 24 in both treatment groups. Immunogenicity profiles were low and similar across groups. Safety findings were comparable, with mostly mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal AEs and no serious AEs or deaths, indicating similar overall efficacy and tolerability of the two treatment products.

The mean HbA1c reduction of in both treatment groups over 24 weeks is consistent with prior global studies, including SUSTAIN-1 trial [11]. SUSTAIN-1 trial reported that semaglutide monotherapy (1.0 mg, one-weekly) resulted in 1.55% reduction in HbA1c levels [11]. Similarly, other SUSTAIN trials reported significant reduction in HbA1c levels post-semaglutide treatment vs. comparator drugs [[9], [10], [11],[13], [14], [15], [16], [17], [18], [19]]. In our study, a substantial proportion of patients achieved HbA1c targets (<7% and <6.5%) by week 24 (65.7–67.9% and 39.4–43.5%, respectively), which is broadly consistent with the SUSTAIN clinical trial program, where semaglutide consistently produced superior reductions in HbA1c and enabled a higher proportion of patients to achieve glycaemic targets compared with placebo and active comparators [[19], [20], [21]].

Across SUSTAIN trials, HbA1c reductions typically ranged from approximately 0.30% to 1.55% depending on background therapy and comparator, along with clinically meaningful body weight reductions [13,14,19]. Similarly, in SUSTAIN-3, semaglutide achieved significant HbA1c reduction (∼1.5%) and a higher proportion of patients attaining HbA1c <7% (∼67%), closely aligning with the proportions observed in the current analysis [9].

The effects on metabolic parameters beyond glycaemic control in the present study, including modest reductions in total cholesterol, LDL, triglycerides, and improvements in HDL, are also supported by previous evidence indicating favorable cardiometabolic effects of semaglutide treatment in SUSTAIN program [13].

The safety profile observed in this study is also consistent with the established tolerability of semaglutide. Gastrointestinal AEs were the most commonly reported events, occurring in 29.7–35.7% of patients, which aligns with previous reports where GI events such as nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea were the predominant AEs [9,[22], [23], [24], [25], [26]]. Importantly, most AEs in this study were mild to moderate, with noincidence of serious AEs and no treatment discontinuations due to TEAEs, which is comparable to the acceptable safety and tolerability profile reported across SUSTAIN clinical trials [20,22,26].

This study is strengthened by its randomized design, balanced baseline characteristics, and use of an active comparator, which enhance the validity of the findings. The comprehensive evaluation of efficacy, glycaemic parameters, body weight, immunogenicity, and safety over 24 weeks, along with consistent results across endpoints, further support the robustness of the conclusions. However, some limitations should be acknowledged. The study included only Asian patients which may restrict the generalizability of the results to other populations, and the 24-week duration may not fully capture long-term outcomes, particularly real-world scenario, for safety and immunogenicity.

Conclusion

The findings of this study demonstrated that once-weekly Intas semaglutide injection achieved clinically meaningful reductions in HbA1c and body weight among Indian patients with T2DM inadequately controlled on metformin. Intas semaglutide injection showed non-inferiority to the innovator semaglutide injection in improving glycaemic control over 24 weeks with comparable immunogenicity and safety profiles. The study demonstrated that semaglutide is effective and may represent a more accessible alternative for improving glycaemic control, particularly in resource limited settings. However, long-term, real-world studies and dedicated pharmacoeconomic analyses are required to establish its cost-effectiveness and to confirm the applicability of these efficacy outcomes.

Correlation between CRP and ferritin in dengue patients: A hospital-based prospective observational study
Authors: Sailendra Nayak, Rabinarayan Rout, Bandita Panda, Mourya Machipeddy, Vibha Sharma, Shubhransu Patro
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:In India, dengue is one of the health challenges and a leading cause of hospitalization, especially during the monsoon. C-reactive protein (CRP) is an independent marker for dengue severity. The present study aimed to analyze the correlation between CRP and ferritin and with other hematological markers in dengue fever (DF).Methods:A hospital-based prospective study was conducted over one year. With the patient’s consent, A total of 189 dengue-diagnosed hospitalized patients aged more than 18 years were included in the study group. Demographic features, total leukocytes, total platelet counts, packed cell volume, CRP, and ferritin were monitored in both severe and non-severe cases. Significant univariate parameters were correlated and significant associations were analyzed from the correlation coefficient and P value respectively.Result:A total of 189 dengue-diagnosed, hospitalized patients were recruited, among them 30 patients were diagnosed as severe and 159 patients were diagnosed as non-severe. The mean age ranged between 41 ± 16.7 and 42 ± 15.9 years. In univariate analysis, total leucocytes, CRP, and ferritin were significantly higher in dengue severe cases in comparison to non-severe dengue cases. The association between CRP and ferritin revealed a positive correlation whereas, the association between ferritin and total platelet count showed a negative correlation.Conclusion:A significant positive correlation between CRP and ferritin indicates that ferritin can be an additional biomarker along with CRP for the hospitalization of dengue patients and may be useful for severity prediction at an early stage of infection.

Introduction

In tropical and subtropical countries dengue is a major health challenge with high mortality.[1] After the year 2010, dengue fever (DF) is a major public health concern in Odisha.[2] DF is a vector-borne disease transmitted by two species of Aedes mosquitoes (Aedes aegypti and A albopictus). Genetically four types of DENV serotypes (DENV-1, DENV-2, DENV-3, and DENV-4) cause infections.[3] According to WHO guidelines, 1997–2009, based on the dengue complexity, the clinical manifestations of dengue are non-specific febrile illness, DF, dengue hemorrhagic fever, and dengue shock syndrome (DSS).[45] On this basis dengue patients are classified into severe and non-severe (with and without warning signs).[67] Especially in the monsoon season, dengue is now endemic in most of the states of India and leading cause of hospitalization. Frequent outbreaks and high burdens impact the country’s economy. To reduce hospitalization and prevent frequent outbreaks, there is a need for prevention and early detection of disease severity.

For early-stage classification, appropriate treatment, and requirement of hospital admission, there are no accepted clinical guidelines or specific laboratory tests.[8] At present, warning signs as per WHO guidelines are used as a key in severity assessment despite its low sensitivity in the early phase. Therefore a reliable, simple biomarker is essential for clinicians for early-stage classification, and effective management.

C-reactive protein (CRP) is an acute-phase protein synthesized in the liver within 6 h of inflammation and tissue injury.[9] Hence CRP is considered an inflammatory biomarker and is clinically used to differentiate between viral and bacterial infection and even to distinguish between malaria and DF.[10] To date, few studies have been done to assess using CRP testing to distinguish between mild and severe dengue cases in the adult population. The present study aimed to determine the association of CRP with other hematological biomarkers in adult dengue patients for the classification of patients based on the severity prediction and requirement of hospital admission at the early phase of DF.

Materials and Methods

A hospital-based prospective observational study was conducted over a period of one year (August 2021–January 2022) after obtaining approval from the institutional ethics committee. A total of 189 dengue-diagnosed patients admitted under the Department of General Medicine at a tertiary care hospital were recruited based on the inclusion criteria and patient consent. All adult (more than 18 years old) dengue-positive hospitalized patients (as diagnosed by an antigen test) were included. Patients with existing autoimmune disorders confirmed from the previous history, secondary bacterial infections confirmed by a culture report, and patients discharged against medical advice were excluded from the study.

The formula n = Zα/22*p*(1-p)/MOE2 was used for sample size calculation, where Zα/2 is for the normal distribution at α/2, MOE is the margin of error, p is the sample proportion, and n is the sample size. At a 95% confidence interval, 8% dengue prevalence, and 6% margin of error, the sample size was calculated to be 190.

Demographic details, comorbidities, hematological parameters, biochemical parameters, and inflammatory biomarkers were recorded in a redesigned proforma.

Severe dengue cases and non-severe dengue groups with and without signs were categorized into two groups based on the 2009-revised WHO criteria.[6] In statistical analysis, all continuous data are presented in mean and standard deviation, and categorical data are presented in percentage. In univariate analysis, the independent parameters were analyzed, and a comparison was made between the severe and non-severe groups. Significant univariates were correlated by analyzing the Pearson correlation coefficient. A P value less than 0.05 is considered to be significant.

Results

The clinico-demographic features of 189 dengue-diagnosed hospitalized patients were recorded and are presented in Table 1. Based on the WHO criteria for dengue severity, 30 (15.87%) patients were categorized as severe cases, and 159 (84.12%) patients were categorized as non-severe. The mean age of severe dengue patients was 42.43 ± 15.9 years, whereas in non-severe patients the mean age was 41.01 ± 16.7 years. In the gender distribution of severe patients (N = 30), 46.66% of patients were female and 53.33% of patients were male. In non-severe patients, 35.9% of patients were female and 48.14% were male patients. Diabetes, hypertension, and hypothyroidism were the major comorbidities in both severe and non-severe dengue patients. Fever and myalgia were common in both severe and non-severe patients. Rashes were observed in 16.6% of severe cases and 5.6% of non-severe cases. Conjunctivitis was observed only in severe patients (6.66%).

Discussion

DF is severe or fatal due to the rate of plasma leakage, hemorrhages, or multiorgan dysfunction. The pathogenesis of severity includes the activation of T cells and proinflammatory cytokine enhancement.[11] Macrophase activation syndrome (MAS) is very frequent in severe dengue due to excessive activation and proliferation of T cells and macrophages.[1213] Thus biochemical and hematological parameters are in the need for the diagnosis along with NS1 antigen and IgM antibody for severity prediction and for a better line-up clinical management.

The present study revealed the socio-clinical-demographic features and biochemical parameters of 189 dengue-diagnosed patients in both severe and non-severe cases. In biochemical parameters, the study looked at the level of expression of total leukocytes, total platelet counts, packed cell volume, and biochemical parameters CRP and ferritin in both severe and non-severe cases.

In the present study, the association of mean age with the disease severity was insignificant. Similar observations were made by several authors.[1415] A study was conducted on 52,212 dengue patients and reported that age has no significant relationship with disease severity. One study reported a downward trend in 0–10 years and an upward trend between more than 10 years of age and dengue severity. The infectivity status partially mediated between age and severe dengue by 17%.

In the total patient cohort, the male patients were more than females in both severe and non-severe cases. Our findings match with other author’s findings and the facts attributed that more males are exposed to the dengue virus as compared to females.[16]

In severe dengue cases, the comorbidities were observed to be more in comparison to non-severe cases with or without signs and symptoms. The major comorbidities are hypertension and thyroid hypothyroidism. Reports from Singapore and Brazil on a large population (<5000 dengue patients) stated that hypertension is a risk factor for dengue infection progression.[1718] because of the low immune response, plasma leakage, and damage to the capillary endothelial cell glycocalyx (eCG) layer. Fever is common in severe and non-severe dengue cases. In severe cases, symptoms like vomiting, headache, myalgia, and vomiting were observed in more than 40% of cases. In comparison to non-severe cases, Rashes were mostly visible in severe cases in 16% of total patients.

Ferritin is a globular protein, which stores iron. The concentration of serum ferritin increases drastically in the presence of infection and is associated with severe disease and poorer outcomes.[19] In our study, the ferritin level is significantly higher (P value < 0.02) in severe dengue cases than in non-severe cases. Several studies have reported that ferritin serves as a significant marker for DF. A study was conducted at Coimbatore on 119 patients and reported higher ferritin levels in severe dengue cases and Petchiappan et al.[20] also noted higher ferritin levels in dengue severe cases during the febrile phases.[20] CRP is one among other inflammatory markers that is significantly elevated in severe dengue cases than in non-severe with or without sign dengue cases. A large case-control study on 1120 patients showed that CRP levels increased in the first 3 days of illness and are mostly associated with worse dengue clinical outcomes, median CRP level was approximately 30 mg/L.[21] CRP is an acute-phase protein and is significantly associated with dengue severity. Our findings have evidence of increased levels of CRP in severe cases in comparison to non-severe dengue cases.

The association of CRP and ferritin showed a significant positive correlation. As CRP level increases feritin level also increases. When the ferritin level is compared with the total platelet count, A significant negative correlation was observed, which indicates that as the platelet count decreases the ferritin level increases. Serum ferritin, CRP, and platelet count have a strong association with disease severity and mortality in hemorrhagic fever.[22] Ferritin levels of more than 800 ng/mL are associated with dengue severity and exceeding 3000 ng/mL is majorly associated with mortality.[23]

The limitation of the study was the low sample size in dengue severe cases over one year. Ferritin can be analyzed in a larger sample size separately for disease severity prediction and length of hospital stay during DF.

Conclusion

Current findings reveal a significant increase in total leucocyte, ferritin, and CRP levels in severe dengue cases in comparison to non-severe dengue cases. A positive correlation was observed between CRP and ferritin in DF. As the total platelet count decreases the ferritin level is elevated in dengue disease, which leads to severity. Thus ferritin can be considered as an additional biomarker for dengue severe case prediction in early stages.

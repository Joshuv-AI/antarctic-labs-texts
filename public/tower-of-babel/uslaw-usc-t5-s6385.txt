5 U.S.C. § 6385 — Prohibition of coercion
Citation: us:usc:5:6385
Creator: United States Congress
Year: 2026
Source-URL: https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section6385
Context: United States Code, Title 5 (Government Organization And Employees); Part III (Employees); Subpart E (Attendance And Leave); Chapter 63 (Leave); Subchapter V (Family And Medical Leave). Current through Public Law 119-134, as published by the Office of the Law Revision Counsel.

(a)
An employee shall not directly or indirectly intimidate, threaten, or coerce, or attempt to intimidate, threaten, or coerce, any other employee for the purpose of interfering with the exercise of any rights which such other employee may have under this subchapter.
(b)
For the purpose of this section—
(1)
the term “intimidate, threaten, or coerce” includes promising to confer or conferring any benefit (such as appointment, promotion, or compensation), or taking or threatening to take any reprisal (such as deprivation of appointment, promotion, or compensation); and
(2)
the term “employee” means any “employee”, as defined by section 2105.

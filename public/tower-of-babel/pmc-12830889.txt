Multiple trajectory optimization and control of robotic agents using hybrid fuzzy embedded artificial intelligence technique for multi target problems
Authors: Saroj Kumar, Krishna Kant Pandey, Dayal R Parhi, Manoj Kumar Muni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWheeled robot is preferred for their ability to replace human efforts in performing tedious and complex tasks. To accomplish the goal of present global scenario like replacement of human effort and work on fixed automation, it is needed to target multi-objective problems in context of robotic path optimization and less time consumption. Path optimization and control over wheeled robots is very challenging and interesting part of robotic research. Here, Fuzzy logic and modified marine predator optimization algorithm are hybridized and implemented on mobile robots to fulfill real-time objectives of present scenario. In hybridization process, initially obstacle distances from robot location are fed into fuzzy-logic and interim output obtained from Fuzzy logic is again fed to marine predator optimization algorithm to obtain final output. A Petri-Net controller is added with proposed novel fuzzy- marine predator optimization algorithm that further optimizes the navigational parameters by avoiding inter-collision among robots in presence of moving obstacles. Simulation is performed through MATLAB software and the results are tested against real-time experiments under laboratory condition. Simulation and real-time experiments authenticate successful navigation of multiple robots by achieving their objectives. Furthermore, the proposed technique is tested against different AI techniques and existing paper. An average improvement of approximately 10% or more is observed in navigational parameters.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Navigation, Optimization, Soft computing, Petri-Net, Control, Mobile robotSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Use of mobile robots for an optimized path planning has gained a significant attention over the past few years. With an increased demand for an optimized path planning in the shortest time possible with minimum sweep distance, the mobile robots have paved its way far in the field. Technological advancements on the other hand merged with more improved AI techniques have gained a huge contribution towards path planning approaches. Beside Medical and defense fields, mobile robots are also used for different household purposes like automated floor cleaning1, window cleaning, cooking purposes, etc. This has attracted a lot of researchers to work towards the field and contribute to it. With the growing trend, a lot of work has been done in this field as mentioned in the paper ahead but still work is required for improvement in the field of artificial intelligence.

Here, global path planning implies the environment is partially known to the robot whereas local path planning implies the environment is fully unknown to the robot. Petri-Net controller

The hybrid proposed hybrid technique is much efficient to find the optimal path for single and multiple robots. However, it is not performing well at the situation of inter- collision. Here, inter-collision implies the situation when the robots come across each other and a confusing situation occur means the robot treats one another as obstacle and delayed to move forward. At that situation, petri-net controller is used to avoid inter-collision and confusing among the robots. The controller guides the robot that which robot moves first with priority basis. The priority given to that robot which is close to target. Robots awaits to move till the instruction received from controller. This is an aided feature of proposed hybrid controller. The schematic diagram for petri-net controller is shown in Fig. 1415–17.

Petri-net controller model & process flow.

It consists mainly six steps and that are described in following lines:

Stage 1: Stage 1 is known as stand-by stage for all the robots. In this stage robots are randomly placed i.e. the location of the robot is unknown. It stay stand-by until the command receives.

Stage 2: 2nd stage is start of journey stage. The robots start their journey and may detect some obstacle during journey.

Stage 3: 3rd stage is sensing stage, in which the robots detects robots but treats as obstacles.

Stage 4: 4th stage is decision stage. The controller released command which robot should move first and which robot should wait until further command. The command is released to the robots on priority basis, and it decided by the position of robot. Priority is given to that robot which is nearest to target position and having minimum safe distance from obstacles. During navigation of priority robot, other robots acts as static obstacles.

Stage 5: 5th stage is known as inspection stage in which the controller inspect whether the robots have any conflict in movement or not. If no, the robots heads towards target.

Stage 6: 6th stage is last stage of the controller command in which priority robot is focused and tracked till target achievement. During navigation, if priority robot or other robots find any difficulty then the other robot will be commanded to stay stationary till movement of priority robot up to target or threshold distance from obstacle. Further, the stationary robots again starts journey from stage 2.

Conclusions and potential future work

With the popularity of mobile robots over many fields of human intervention, optimal path planning of mobile robot has emerged as the challenging area of robotic research. A novel scheme of hybridization was presented for smooth path optimization and time consumption problems of mobile robots. Here, hybridized fuzzy logic and modified marine predator optimization technique is adopted to encounter the problems. Further, Petri-Net controller is also aided to overcome the inter-collision situation among the robots. The successful navigation of single and multiple robots are tested on MATLAB platform coupled with real-time experiments under laboratory condition. Furthermore, the proposed technique is validated through comparison with existing techniques, an average improvement of approximately 10% is observed in path length, and more than 9% is observed in time consumption. Further, the proposed approach has potential stability to huge no. of robots and as a team also. The Petri-net model supports in coordination among the multiple robots. In the near future, this research might be applied on autonomous section like warehouse automation, Industrial inspection and military surveillance as the sensor’s coordination with hybrid approach is robust. Surely, this will help the new comers to understand the scene behind robotic research.

Digital Visual Acuity and Reading Performance with Positive and Negative Display Polarities After Bilateral Diffractive Multifocal Intraocular Lens Implantation: A Longitudinal Observational Study
Authors: Thanapong Somkijrungroj, Nuntachai Surawatsatien, Waraluck Supawatjariyakul, Thanaporn Wiriyabanditkul, Thananop Pothikamjorn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionTraditional acuity charts may not fully capture visual performance during everyday digital device use. Digital screen–based assessments may better reflect contemporary visual demands, and display polarity may influence reading performance in patients with multifocal intraocular lenses (MF-IOLs). Furthermore, display polarities differ in luminance and contrast characteristics, which may influence pupil size and reading performance in patients with MF-IOLs. We aimed to compare digital visual acuity (VA) between positive (black-on-white) and negative (white-on-black) display polarities and evaluated reading speed and critical print size (CPS) in patients with bilateral diffractive MF-IOLs.MethodsIn this non-randomized, observational study, consecutive participants who underwent bilateral diffractive MF-IOL (AcrySof IQ PanOptix) implantation with repeated assessments at postoperative day 1, week 1, month 1, and month 3. Monocular and binocular uncorrected and corrected distance, intermediate, and near VA were assessed using a Snellen chart and MNREAD application on an iPad. Reading acuity between display polarities was compared using paired t-tests, while reading speed and CPS were assessed using the positive display polarity.ResultsTwenty-six participants completed the study. At 3 months, uncorrected distance VA was −0.084 ± 0.079 logMAR. Digital VA at 60 cm and 40 cm was −0.115 ± 0.104 and −0.008 ± 0.084 logMAR, respectively. Reading performance did not differ significantly between positive and negative display polarities at 60 cm (p = 0.055). Mean CPS was 0.173 ± 0.0168 logMAR at 1 month and 0.238 ± 0.100 logMAR at 3 months. Mean reading speed was 84.63 ± 27.46 WPM at 1 month and 101.89 ± 72.62 WPM at 3 months (p = 0.133).ConclusionsBilateral diffractive MF-IOLs provided favorable digital and conventional visual outcomes with small differences observed and were not considered clinically meaningful. Digital reading assessments provided complementary functional information, including reading speed and critical print size, without replacing conventional VA assessment.

Introduction

Modern cataract surgery aims not only to restore distance vision but also to maximize functional vision across the range of distances used in everyday life. As digital devices have become integral to work and daily activities, patients increasingly expect spectacle independence for intermediate and near tasks, including smartphone, tablet, and computer use.1,2 Multifocal intraocular lenses (MF-IOLs) are widely used to improve vision at multiple distances and reduce reliance on glasses.3 Early MF-IOLs were primarily designed to provide distance vision together with near vision suitable for reading printed material at approximately 30–40 cm.1,4,5 Newer diffractive designs aim to provide functional vision across multiple focal points, addressing presbyopia and supporting contemporary digital activities.6,7

Recent studies have shown that digital reading performance differs from conventional chart-based visual acuity because it incorporates continuous reading, scrolling behavior, luminance, and display characteristics encountered during everyday device use.8 Digital reading assessments such as MNREAD acuity chart (University of Minnesota) provide clinically relevant outcomes including reading acuity, maximum reading speed, and critical print size (CPS), which cannot be obtained using conventional distance acuity charts.9,10 Although digital reading assessments have been increasingly applied to evaluate functional vision, their use in patients with multifocal intraocular lenses remains limited, and the influence of display characteristics on postoperative visual performance has not been well established. Although positive display polarity (black text on a white background, BoW) is the standard for conventional visual acuity assessment, it remains unclear whether display polarity influences postoperative digital reading performance in patients with diffractive MF-IOLs, whose optical performance is pupil dependent. Previous studies have demonstrated that display polarity influences luminance adaptation, pupil size, contrast perception, and visual comfort during digital reading.11 BoW generally produces smaller pupil diameters because of the brighter background, which may reduce higher-order aberrations through a pinhole effect, compared with negative polarity (white text on a black background, WoB).12 This effect may be particularly relevant in patients with diffractive MF-IOLs because image quality depends on pupil size.12 Determining whether display polarity influences postoperative visual performance may therefore help optimize digital reading recommendations and improve patient counseling.

Most clinical studies evaluating diffractive MF-IOLs have focused on conventional distance, intermediate, and near visual acuity, defocus curves, contrast sensitivity, and patient-reported outcomes.13–18 To our knowledge, no previous study has systematically evaluated postoperative digital visual acuity, reading performance, and display polarity using a digital platform. Therefore, we prospectively evaluated digital visual acuity and reading performance following bilateral implantation of the AcrySof IQ PanOptix diffractive MF-IOL (Alcon Laboratories). Digital visual acuity was assessed under BoW and WoB display polarities, together with reading speed and CPS, and interpreted alongside conventional ETDRS visual acuity at postoperative day 1, week 1, month 1, and month 3. We hypothesized that display polarity would influence digital visual performance and that digital assessments would provide complementary functional information beyond conventional chart-based visual acuity. If one display polarity provides superior digital visual performance, clinicians could provide evidence-based recommendations regarding display settings after MF-IOL implantation, thereby optimizing reading comfort and patient satisfaction.

Methods

After approval for the present prospective, non-randomized, observational study was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of the Faculty of Medicine, Chulalongkorn University (COA No. 1429/2020), we recruited participants from the outpatient department of King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital, Bangkok, Thailand. Before surgery, all participants provided documented written informed consent to participate in the study and underwent a series of preoperative assessments, including non-contact tonometry (Nidek NT-530P, Nidek Co., LTD, Tokyo, Japan), auto kerato-refractometry (Topcon KR800, Topcon Co. Tokyo, Japan), and optical biometry (IOLMaster700, Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany), all assessments were conducted on the same day. The preoperative assessments, data collection, and analysis were performed by single surgeon (TS), who also made decisions regarding the selection and implantation of diffractive MF-IOLs used in this study according to standard clinical visual acuity evaluation using an ETDRS chart at distance and near/intermediate visual acuity testing. All study procedures adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and its contemporary (2013) amendments.

Each patient underwent bilateral simultaneous cataract surgery using a phacoemulsification technique, performed by a single surgeon. Throughout the surgery, intraoperative and postoperative complications were carefully monitored, including intraocular pressure measurements, to ensure patient safety. Participants were examined by masked examiners (NS, WS, or TW) on the day of surgery, and routine follow-up visits were scheduled for 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months postoperatively. We followed up with participants at these four time points to assess rapid VA recovery. Primary analyses of digital reading performance were performed at months 1 and 3, when postoperative refraction had stabilized. Because patients perform most daily visual tasks binocularly, digital reading performance was evaluated with a focus on patient-reported binocular corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) as a measure of IOL performance and binocular uncorrected distance visual acuity (UCVA) for eyeglass free vision.19–23 Additional examinations were conducted as required based on patient outcomes and clinical findings. All procedures took place at King Chulalongkorn Memorial Hospital from January 31 to October 31, 2021.

Inclusion criteria were participants aged ≥40 years with or without corneal astigmatism that could be corrected using diffractive MF-IOL in both eyes. The included participants must have habitual daily use of digital screens, such as smartphones. Exclusion criteria included previous corneal refractive surgery, ocular diseases that could affect postoperative visual outcomes (including retinal disease, glaucoma, corneal disease, zonular weakness, or zonular dehiscence), and intraoperative complications. These conditions were identified through comprehensive preoperative ophthalmic examination, review of medical records, and intraoperative assessment by the operating surgeon.

IOL calculations were performed using the Barrett Toric Calculator (APACRS.org). The IOLs were AcrySof IQ PanOptix or PanOptix Toric (Alcon Laboratories, Inc., Fort Worth, TX), which are single-piece, aspheric, non-apodized diffractive trifocal IOLs. The alignment of the IOL axis was guided by digital marker guiding system.

Data Collection

The recorded data included demographic information, frequency, and duration of daily use of digital screens, underlying diseases, and preoperative eye conditions, including lens status, refraction, intraocular pressure, keratometry, optical biometry, and surgical details by review of medical records and intraoperative surgical findings. Efficacy measures included binocular pupil size along with VA measured using a ETDRS chart and the MNREAD application on an iPad device (iPad Air 4th generation 10.9-inch, resolution 2360 × 1640 pixels at 264 pixels per inch, Reflectivity 1.8%, Brightness 500 nits, Apple Inc., Cupertino, CA) under consistent lighting conditions at various distances: 4 m (UCVA and CDVA), 60 cm (uncorrected (UIVA) and corrected intermediate visual acuity (CIVA)), and 40 cm (uncorrected (UNVA) and corrected near visual acuity (CNVA)) reported in logMAR units. The data were collected by one senior optometrist at four time points: day 1, 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months postoperatively with repeated postoperative assessments, as changes in acuity over time were not the focus of this study.

The binocular uncorrected reading acuity with BoW and WoB of the MNREAD application with the same luminance using a font size of 7 points or greater was measured and compared only at 60 cm and 40 cm at 1 month and 3 months. The binocular uncorrected defocus curve was obtained from −4.00 D to +1.00 D in 0.5 D steps with the Snellen chart at 4 m under photopic light conditions at 3 months postoperatively. Postoperative manifest refraction at 3 months of follow-up was also recorded, including the spherical equivalent (SEQ) within ± 0.25 D, ± 0.50 D, and ± 1.00 D of emmetropia, and the manifest cylinder. The mean and SD of digital CPS in the logMAR unit and the mean and SD of reading speed (words per minute, WPM) with WoB were collected 1 month and 3 months postoperatively at 40 cm. All distances were strictly measured using a tape measure.

Sample Size Calculation

The sample size calculation was based on the primary outcome of binocular reading acuity measured with the MNREAD application. Previous studies reported a mean binocular reading acuity of 0.24 logMAR (SD 0.07) in patients implanted with a trifocal IOL.1 Assuming a standard deviation of 0.05 logMAR and a clinically significant difference of 0.05 logMAR, a sample size of 21 patients was required to achieve 90% power (1−β = 0.9) at a significance level of α = 0.05. Accounting for a 20% potential dropout rate, the final target sample size was 26 patients.

Statistical Analyses

All data were documented using Microsoft Excel. Statistical analyses were performed using StataSE18.5 (StataCorp). VA and reading acuity in logMAR were analyzed and are presented as the means ± standard deviations (SDs). The data distribution was assessed for normality using a Shapiro–Wilk test. A paired t-test with Bonferroni adjustment was used to compare reading acuity BoW and WoB display polarities at each postoperative time point (1 and 3 months) and viewing distance (40 cm and 60 cm). Bonferroni-adjusted p-values are reported to account for multiple comparisons. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. A logMAR level difference of 0.2 and reading speed difference of 10 WPM was considered clinical significance.24

Digital VA was not statistically compared with conventional VA because the methods of evaluation and interpretation differ between chart-based and digital assessments. Effect sizes for paired comparisons were calculated using Cohen’s d, defined as d 0.2–0.5 considered small, d 0.5–0.8 medium, and d > 0.8 large. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of two-way mixed-effects model with absolute agreement was calculated to evaluate the reliability of the acuity measurements among the included participants at 1 month and 3 months postoperatively. The ICC values below 0.5 indicate poor reliability, between 0.5 and 0.75 moderate reliability, between 0.75 and 0.9 good reliability, and any value above 0.9 indicates excellent reliability.25 The Bland-Altman plot was used to graphically assess the agreement between the acuity measurements. The CPS was directly derived from the MNREAD, which is the smallest print size included participants can read at their maximum reading speed.26,27

Results

A total of 26 participants, corresponding to 52 eyes, underwent bilateral implantation of MF-IOL or toric MF-IOL on the same day. The mean age of the participants was 61.69 ± 5.96 years (mean ± SD). The distribution of IOL types used in the study was as follows: 25 MF-IOL (48%) and 27 Toric MF-IOL (52%). A detailed breakdown of the IOL types can be found in Supplementary Table.

Discussion

Screen polarity and CPS are important determinants of digital VA. In our study, BoW polarity provided better digital VA, likely because the higher luminance of the white background induces pupillary constriction, thereby enhancing acuity.28,29 The BoW digital display appears to provide an early postoperative advantage, allowing patients to achieve better reading acuity in the first weeks after surgery, which may facilitate faster adaptation to near and intermediate visual tasks. However, by 3 months postoperatively, no significant difference was observed between BoW and WoB, suggesting that polarity-related effects may be subtle or diminish over time. The minimal bias observed in the Bland-Altman analysis may reflect participants’ habitual preferences or a genuine, but small, advantage of BoW over WoB.11

CPS also played a critical role in reading performance. Participants demonstrated excellent CPS at 40 cm with bilateral diffractive MF-IOLs or toric MF-IOLs at both 1 and 3 months. In our assessment, larger font sizes (approximately 7 points or greater) were associated with maintaining readable text size relative to CPS; however, further studies incorporating validated patient-reported outcome measures are needed to evaluate the relationship between font size and reading satisfaction. Reading speed was acceptable at both follow-up intervals, though variability was greater than in previous reports, likely due to differences in English proficiency.30 Other potential confounding factors include ocular dominance and screen familiarity, both of which should be considered when interpreting the results. Although conventional black-on-white visual acuity testing remains the clinical standard, it does not evaluate functional aspects of digital reading such as reading speed, critical print size, or performance under different display settings. This approach could offer a more comprehensive understanding of visual outcomes, leading to better recommendations and more effective patient counseling regarding real-world satisfaction.

Conventional Snellen-derived logMAR VA and digital VA could not be directly compared, as participants may use different strategies in performing each test, resulting in variable outputs.14 Our findings suggest that patients with bilateral diffractive MF-IOLs achieve comparable reading performance with both positive and negative display polarities. Therefore, clinicians can reassure patients that selecting light or dark mode on digital devices is unlikely to substantially affect reading performance, allowing display settings to be chosen according to individual comfort and preference.

The defocus curve results in this study are consistent with previous findings on bilateral diffractive MF-IOL implantation, demonstrating strong performance across distances and rapid recovery within the first postoperative month.15–18 Satisfactory binocular UCVA was maintained through 3 months. The predominance of low astigmatism correction in our cohort may have contributed to slightly better outcomes compared with prior reports.

It should be noted that we did not intend to compare digital visual acuity with conventional chart-based measurements, as the methods of assessment and interpretation differ between the two approaches. We did not subgroup our participants to toric or non-toric IOL type as any single participants may have had both toric and non-toric implants, and binocular UCVA represents a contemporary digital era outcome. Measurement reliability was good to moderate across follow-up, although moderate-to-poor ICC values indicate limited measurement repeatability may be due to good postoperative acuity, which reduces measurable differences and the requirement for absolute agreement. The minimal bias observed may stem from participants’ habitual use or preference for a given screen polarity, or from a genuine advantage of BoW compared with WoB.11 The use of a single surgeon and preoperative assessor minimized inter-operator variability; however, the potential for investigator-related bias cannot be completely excluded.

This study has limitations. The follow-up period was limited to 3 months, and outcomes such as patient satisfaction, digital eye strain, postoperative quality-of-life, and glance-like reading behavior were not assessed. Although all examinations were performed under consistent room lighting conditions, ambient illumination was not quantitatively measured. Analyzing toric and non-toric IOLs together may confound the results, since astigmatism correction plays an important role in achieving optimal digital visual acuity. Larger multicenter randomized prospective studies with masked outcome assessment, excluding patients with manifest strabismus or amblyopia, are needed to confirm these findings. Evaluating patient-reported digital reading outcomes and digital comfort metrics across different IOL types, especially monofocal lenses, and incorporating alternative digital reading assessment tools would further clarify the role of digital performance in postoperative satisfaction. Such investigations would enhance understanding of digital reading and its implications, ultimately contributing to improved patient satisfaction.

Conclusion

Bilateral diffractive MF-IOL implantation achieved favorable outcomes across conventional and digital visual assessments. Although BoW demonstrated better digital visual acuity than WoB at an early postoperative time point, this difference was not observed at 3 months, suggesting that display polarity may have limited long-term clinical impact. Digital reading performance, including CPS and reading speed, provided additional functional information beyond traditional VA measurements. Adjusting font size according to CPS, with text sizes approximately twice the CPS and a minimum of 6–7 points (J3–J4), may support comfortable digital reading activities. Incorporating digital assessments into postoperative evaluation may complement conventional VA testing and improve understanding of visual performance in contemporary digital environments. Larger multicenter prospective studies with longer follow-up are warranted to validate these findings and further determine the clinical role of digital visual assessments in patients with bilateral diffractive MF-IOLs.

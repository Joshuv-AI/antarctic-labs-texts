Integration of osimertinib-targeted EGFR gene-associated differential gene expression in constructing a prognostic model for lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Haiwen Li, Li Yang, Quan Yang, Zhu Liang, Wenmei Su, Lili Yu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is one of the deadliest cancers. Epidermal growth factor receptor-tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI)-targeted therapy is an important approach for treating LUAD. However, the development of acquired resistance poses a serious clinical challenge. Our objective was to explore the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with EGFR and detect biomarkers for diagnosing and treating osimertinib resistance in LUAD patients. LUAD datasets were downloaded from public databases. Differential expression analysis was performed to screen DEGs, and prognostic modules were constructed by Cox regression. Enrichment analysis, gene regulatory network analysis and immune microenvironment analysis were employed to explore the underlying mechanisms in LUAD. Finally, the expression of prognosis module genes (PMGs) was validated in 8 LUAD tissue specimens and 5 cell lines by qRT-PCR. In total, 13 differential module genes (BIRC3, CCT6A, CPLX2, GLCCI1, GSTA1, HLA-DQB2, ID1, KCTD12, MUC15, NOTUM, NT5E, TCIM, and TM4SF4) were screened for the construction of a prognostic module. Notably, CCT6A and KCTD12 demonstrated excellent accuracy in the diagnosis of LUAD. Immune dysregulation and BIRC3, HLA-DQB2, KCTD12, and NT5E expression were significantly associated with invasive immune cells in LUAD patients. The expression level of CCT6A was highest in PC9-OR and H1975-OR cells, while the expression level of KCTD12 was highest in paracancerous tissue and HBE cells. The constructed prognostic model showed promise in predicting the survival of LUAD patients. Notably, KCTD12 and CCT6A might be candidate biomarkers for improving diagnostic performance and guiding individualized therapy for EGFR-TKI-resistant LUAD patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10142-024-01499-5.

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) constitutes 85% of all lung cancer cases and is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most prevalent subtype and represents ∼ 48% of all NSCLCs, with a 5-year survival rate of only 15% (Bray et al. 2024). This low survival rate is most likely because more than 50% of NSCLC patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage (Siegel et al. 2023). Platinum-based doublet chemotherapy was previously the standard-of-care first-line treatment for patients with advanced NSCLC. The identification of driver mutations and the development of molecularly targeted therapies have led to a paradigm shift in the treatment of unresectable NSCLC (Riely et al. 2024).

The upregulation and abnormal activation of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) are key factors in LUAD development, with around 21% of patients having EGFR genetic alterations, making it a viable target for blockade therapy (Shigematsu et al. 2005). Anti-EGFR targeted therapies include antibodies like cetuximab (Saoudi Gonzalez et al. 2024) and panitumumab (Watanabe et al. 2023), as well as small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as gefitinib (Zhou et al. 2024) and osimertinib (Le et al. 2024). Numerous randomized trials have confirmed that EGFR-TKIs are superior to cytotoxic agents in patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC (John et al. 2018; Maemondo et al. 2010; Rosell et al. 2012). However, like patients receiving first- or second-generation EGFR-TKIs, those treated with osimertinib ultimately develop resistance. A considerable proportion of osimertinib-resistant patients exhibit an absence of unequivocally identified resistance pathways (Zeng et al., 2022b). It has been reported that the mechanism of EGFR resistance is gain-of-function with enhanced EGFR signaling in NSCLC (Laface et al. 2023). While the EGFR signaling pathway is generally well understood, there is insufficient research on how mutated EGFR interacts with specific signaling components. Consequently, there is a growing need to investigate targetable signaling components within the mutated EGFR signaling network and to identify useful biomarkers that could enhance the survival of patients with NSCLC.

In this study, we performed systematic bioinformatics analyses to screen prognosis module genes (PMGs) related to the expression of EGFR in high- and low-risk groups of LUAD patients. Additionlly, we performed enrichment analysis, gene regulatory network analysis, immune microenvironment analysis, and mutation analysis to explore the underlying mechanisms in LUAD. Furthermore, the expression patterns of these PMGs were validated in 8 LUAD tissue specimens and PC9, PC9-OR, H1975, and H1975-OR cell lines by using qRT‒PCR. Overall, our study offers a novel perspective on the molecular mechanisms underlying LUAD and provides new ideas and potential targets for its diagnosis and treatment.

Materials and methods

Data download

The LUAD datasets were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Project (TCGA, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/), and sequencing data from 539 LUAD cancer samples and 59 partially matched adjacent normal samples were obtained. The clinical data corresponding to the samples were obtained from the UCSC Xena database (http://genome.ucsc.edu), the microsatellite instability (MSI) and tumor mutation burden (TMB) data were obtained from the cBioPortal database (https://www.cbioportal.org/), and the tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) immune score results of the TCGA-LUAD dataset were calculated through the TIDE website (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu). We also selected “Masked Somatic Mutation” data from the TCGA LUAD patient cohort (n = 618). Data were preprocessed using VarScan software, and SM status was visualized using the maftools R package. To analyze the copy number variations (CNVs) of LUAD, we used the TCGAbiolinks package in R to download the patients’ “Copy Number Segment” data (n = 1147). Then, we used GISTIC 2.0 to download and process the CNV fragments (Mermel et al. 2011). The parameter reference genome version was set to “hg38.UCSC.add_miR.160920.refgene.mat”, and all other parameters used the default parameter settings. In addition, we downloaded the expression profile datasets GSE27262 (Wei et al. 2012), GSE31210 (Okayama et al. 2012), and GSE32863 (Selamat et al. 2012) of LUAD patients from the GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). All expression profile data and grouping information corresponding to the samples in the GSE27262, GSE31210, and GSE32863 datasets were included in subsequent analysis. The GSE27262, GSE31210 and GSE32863 datasets were utilized as validation sets to verify the differences in the expression of the target gene EGFR among the different groups. See Table S1 for specific dataset information.

Prognostic analysis and clinical correlation analysis of the target gene EGFR

To explore the differential expression of the EGFR gene targeted by the drug osimertinib, we utilized the TCGA-LUAD dataset, which combines the expression of the EGFR target gene with the clinical prognostic data of LUAD patients, to analyze the relationship between the expression of the EGFR target gene and patient prognosis outcomes and constructed a Kaplan‒Meier (KM) survival curve by using the survminer R package to determine the optimal group (minimum P value). Then, based on the high and low expression of EGFR, we grouped the differences in the distributions of different clinical variables (T stage, N stage, M stage, pathological stage, sex, age, overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free intervals (PFIs) among the LUAD patients and constructed stacked column plots. Furthermore, to identify the potential mechanisms, as well as the associated biological characteristics and pathways of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the high/low group of LUAD patients, we performed an analysis on the TCGA-LUAD dataset using the limma package (Ritchie et al. 2015). We employed the criteria of|logFC| > 1 and P.adj < 0.05 to screen DEGs for further study.

Weighted gene association network analysis (WGCNA)

We conducted WGCNA using the R package WGCNA package (Langfelder and Horvath 2008). Initially, we calculated the correlation coefficient between pairs of genes and utilized the weighted correlation coefficient to establish connections between genes in the network, following a scale-free network pattern. Next, a hierarchical clustering tree was constructed using the correlation coefficients among genes. In this article, WGCNA used the mRNA genes whose gene expression variance accounted for the top 25% of all genes in the TCGA-LUAD dataset as inputs. The module gene count was set at a minimum of 100, while the soft power was adjusted to 4. Both the module merging threshold and the minimum distance are set to 0.2. Once the module of interest was identified based on the correlation values, we considered all genes within the module to be highly relevant to LUAD. Then, by intersecting the module genes with the DEGs, we obtained a set of differential module genes (DMGs).

Construction of a DMG prognosis model

To construct the DMG prognosis model, we initially employed univariate Cox regression to evaluate the DMGs. DMGs that satisfied the criterion of P < 0.1 were selected. Subsequently, the glmnet package (Engebretsen and Bohlin 2019) was utilized to perform least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression based on the aforementioned DMGs. The regression was executed with the parameter family = “cox” and tenfold cross-validation method. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was conducted on the DMGs using LASSO regression to identify and construct a DMG prognosis model. The lambda coefficient of each prognostic DMG in the DMG prognosis model was obtained. Subsequently, the risk score of each LUAD patient was calculated, and patients were categorized into high and low groups based on the median. The genes in the DMG prognosis model are named prognosis module genes (PMGs).

K‒M analysis was conducted using the survival package to examine the associations between the high- and low-risk score groups of the PMGs and OS. Additionally, we used time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves to assess survival predictions. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) values, which quantify the performance of the PMGs, were calculated using the timeROC R package (Beyene and El Ghouch 2022) in R (version 3.03).

Group comparison of PMGs between the cancer and normal groups in four sets of datasets

The Mann‒Whitney U test was used to compare the PMGs between the cancer and normal groups. The findings were visualized through group comparison diagrams. Additionally, the ROC curves of the PMGs in different groups (LUAD/Normal) were plotted using R’s proc package. The calculated AUC was utilized as an indicator to evaluate the diagnostic effectiveness of the PMGs in identifying cancer development in LUAD patients.

Furthermore, the Mann‒Whitney U test was performed to analyze the differences in the expression of PMGs between the cancer group and the normal group in the GSE27262, GSE31210 and GSE32863 datasets. We determined the correlation coefficient between the expression levels of PMGs in each dataset using Spearman correlation analysis and subsequently presented the results.

Functional enrichment and pathway enrichment analyses

We used the R package clusterProfiler (Yu et al. 2012) to perform Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses. To identify statistically significant results, we applied the following screening criteria: a P value < 0.05 and an FDR value (q value) < 0.1.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA)

Based on the median risk score, the LUAD patients were divided into high- and low-risk score groups. To explore the enrichment of pathway genes, we conducted GSEA using the clusterProfiler package. The GSEA parameters employed were as follows: the seed number was set to 2022, a total of 1000 calculations were performed, and each gene set consisted of at least 10 genes and a maximum of 10 genes. The analysis focused on 500 genes, and the P value correction method utilized was Benjamini‒Hochberg correction.

To evaluate whether different samples exhibited enriched pathways, we conducted GSEA utilizing the GSEA package. The gene set “c2.cp.all.v2022.1.Hs.symbols.gmt” from the MSigDB database was acquired for this purpose. GSVA was then performed on all genes within the high- and low-risk score groups of LUAD patients. We considered a P value < 0.05 as the threshold for significant enrichment, and the results presented the top 5 pathways (positive and negative) with a maximum of 10 pathways.

Somatic mutation (SM) and copy number variation (CNV) analysis

To analyze the SM status of PMGs in patients in the TCGA-LUAD dataset, “Masked Somatic Mutation” data from the TCGA website were selected. The SM data of PMGs in patients were preprocessed using VarScan software, and the SM data were visualized using the R package maftools.

To examine the CNVs of the PMGs in the high- and low-risk score groups among LUAD patients, we used the TCGAbiolinks R package to download patients’ data, and obtained “masked copy number segment” data from 1147 patients and conducted GISTIC 2.0 analysis on the downloaded CNV segment data via GenePattern (https://cloud.genepattern.org).

Immune assessment and clinical correlation analysis

The Mann‒Whitney U test was used to assess the differences in tumor mutation burden (TMB) and microsatellite instability (MSI) between the high- and low-risk score groups in the PMGs of LUAD patients. In addition, we investigated the potential tumor treatment response to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) by the TIDE score. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to investigate the relationships between the risk score and TMB, MSI, and TIDE in the high- and low-risk score groups of LUAD patients. The algorithm calculations and visualizations were carried out using the R package ggplot2.

We used the R package ESTIMATE to evaluate differences in the immune activity of tumors between the high- and low-risk score groups in the TCGA-LUAD dataset. A quantitative analysis of the gene expression profiles of LUAD tumor samples was performed to derive an immune score for each sample. Results were visualized using group comparison charts with the ggplot2 R package.

Then, we presented the differences in the distributions of different clinical variables (T stage, N stage, M stage, pathological stage, sex, age, OS, DSS, and PFI) among LUAD patients by comparing the high- and low-risk score groups. This analysis was conducted using a stacked histogram with the ggplot2 R package.

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) and immune infiltration analysis with the CIBERSORT algorithm

We employed ssGSEA to quantify the relative abundance of each immune cell infiltrate. We constructed the TCGA-LUAD dataset DMG prognostic model through ssGSEA via the R package GSVA. The infiltration levels of different immune cell types in each sample were represented by the enrichment scores of the high- and low-risk score groups. We uploaded the gene expression matrix data of the TCGA-LUAD dataset’s high/low risk score groups, specifically the PGMs, to CIBERSORT. Subsequently, we obtained and showcased the matrix displaying the abundance of infiltrating immune cells. The difference in immune cell infiltration abundance between the high- and low-risk score groups according to the gene prognostic model is presented in the group comparison chart. The correlation between immune cells within the high/low risk score groups was calculated by the Spearman algorithm and presented visually using the R package ggplot2.

Identification of LUAD disease subtypes in the TCGA dataset

We utilized the ConsensusClusterPlus package (Wilkerson and Hayes 2010) in R to employ the consensus clustering method to identify distinct LUAD subtypes based on the PMGs. The differences in the expression of prognostic module genes between samples of different LUAD subtypes were calculated by the Mann‒Whitney U test. We also employed group comparison charts to present the results of the ESTIMATE score, immune score, stromal score, and tumor purity score by incorporating group information from various LUAD subtypes.

Protein‒protein interaction (PPI) network

To construct a PPI network related to the DMGs, we utilized the STRING database. Subsequently, we employed Cytoscape 3.6 (version 3.9.1) for visualization purposes. Within the PPI network, densely connected local regions likely signify molecular complexes. Notably, all nodes present in the PPI network were considered significant genes (particularly hub genes and mRNAs).

Construction of mRNA–miRNA and mRNA-transcription factor (TF) interaction networks

The miRDB database was used for miRNA prediction of interactions with hub genes, data instances with a target score ≥ 95 in the miRDB database were retained, and an mRNA‒miRNA interaction network was visualized. Moreover, we conducted a search in the CHIPBase database (version 3.0) and the hTFtarget database (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/hTFtarget/) to identify TFs that bind to key genes. The overlapping results from both databases were retained, enabling the creation of an mRNA-TF interaction network. Finally, the mRNA‒miRNA and mRNA-TF interaction networks were visualized using Cytoscape software.

Construction of a multifactor prognostic risk score model

To develop a prognostic model for the multifactor risk score, we initially conducted univariate Cox regression analysis involving the risk score and clinical variables. The results of this analysis were visualized through forest plots. All variables with P < 0.05 were included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis, which enabled the construction of a comprehensive prognostic risk score model. We utilized the R package rms to construct a nomogram based on the results of multivariate Cox regression analysis. To assess the accuracy and precision of the nomogram, a calibration curve was generated. The accuracy and resolution of the multifactor risk score prognostic model were evaluated using decision curve analysis (DCA). To construct the DCA plots, we employed the R package ggDCA.

Cell culture

Different human lung NSCLC cell lines were used in this study, including PC9 and H1975 sensitive to osimertinib were obtained from ATCC, PC9-OR and H1975-OR resistant to osimertinib were maintained in our laboratory, and the human bronchial epithelial (HBE) was obtained from ATCC. The cells were incubated in a humidified atmosphere at 37 °C with 5% CO2 in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Invitrogen).

Clinical samples

In total, 8 LUAD patients were recruited for the sampling of LUAD cancer samples and matched with adjacent normal samples from the Affiliated Guangdong Medical University Hospital from May 2023 to October 2023. The detailed clinical information is shown in Supplementary Table S2. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Guangdong Medical University (reference number: PJKT2024-023) on February 1, 2024.

RNA extraction and qRTPCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells or tissues using TRIzol reagent (TaKaRa, Dalian, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The detailed steps were described in our previous study (Wu et al. 2024). The sequences of primers used are listed in Supplementary Table S3. Target gene expression relative to that of the GAPDH gene was determined as 2ΔΔCt.

Statistical analysis

The specific technical roadmap of our study was depicted in the figure (Figure. S1). All data processing and analysis in this article were conducted using R software (version 4.1.2). To assess the statistical significance between two groups of continuous variables, we employed the independent Student’s t test for normally distributed variables and the Mann‒Whitney U test for nonnormally distributed variables. Survival analysis was conducted using the R survival package. K‒M survival curves were generated to assess differences in survival, while the log-rank test was used to assess the significance of survival time disparities between the two patient groups. Unless otherwise noted, Spearman correlation analysis was employed to calculate the correlation coefficient between various molecules. All the statistical P values were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

LUAD dataset processing

We first normalized the four LUAD datasets, TCGA-LUAD, GSE27262, GSE31210 and GSE32863, using the R package limma package (Fig. 2S). The TCGA-LUAD dataset contains 539 LUAD cancer samples and 59 partially matched adjacent normal samples. The GSE27262 dataset contains a total of 25 LUAD samples and 25 corresponding matched normal lung tissue samples; the GSE32863 dataset contains 58 LUAD tumor tissue samples from patients and 58 corresponding matched normal lung tissue samples from patients; and the GSE31210 dataset contains 226 LUAD tissue samples and 20 normal lung tissue samples from normal controls. After normalization of the expression profile data of the four LUAD datasets, TCGA-LUAD, GSE27262, GSE32863 and GSE31210, the batch effect between samples was removed, and the expression of the sample data became more consistent (Fig. 2S).

Prognostic analysis and clinical correlation analysis of EGFR

We conducted a prognostic KM curve analysis utilizing the survival data (OS.event) of patients with LUAD. Subsequently, we determined the best grouping (minimum P value) of the target genes in LUAD patients within the TCGA-LUAD dataset (P = 0.039, Fig. 1A).

WGCNA to determine coexpression modules in the TCGA-LUAD dataset

In the process of WGCNA, we first selected genes whose variance was in the top 25% of all genes as the total input genes. Then, we clustered the samples in the TCGA-LUAD dataset through a clustering tree, and we set the screening criterion to 0.9 to determine the optimal power threshold (Fig. 2A). The optimal power threshold for WGCNA was 4, and the genes were clustered into 10 modules: MEturquoise, MEyellow, MEmagenta, MEblue, MEgreen, MEpink, MEred, MEblack, MEbrown, and MEgray (Fig. 2B-C). In addition, based on the expression patterns of the module genes and the grouping information of the TCGA-LUAD dataset, we obtained 10 modules that correlated with the normal/LUAD groups in the TCGA-LUAD dataset (Fig. 2D).

We conducted a comparative analysis of five modules that exhibited statistically significant differences (P < 0.05, absolute value of correlation,|COR| ≥0.3) among the 9 modules (excluding irrelevant gray modules: MEgray) and various groups from the TCGA-LUAD dataset. The modules MEturquoise (|COR|=0.51, P = 3e-40), MEblue (|COR|=0.85, P = 1e-168), MEgreen (|COR|=0.31, P = 7e-15), MEpink (|COR|=0.38, P = 2e-22) and MEbrown (|COR|=0.43, P = 7e-28) were included in the subsequent analysis. Moreover, we compared the DEGs between the TCGA-LUAD dataset and the MEturquoise (Fig. 2E), MEblue (Fig. 2F), MEgreen (Fig. 2G), MEpink (Fig. 2H), and MEbrown (Fig. 2I) modules. The module genes provided were intersected, and a Venn diagram was generated to obtain the DMGs. Finally, a total of 257 DMGs were obtained (Fig. 2E–I). Table S5 lists the specific DMG names.

Construction of the DMG prognostic model and difference analysis between the high- and low-risk score groups

To assess the prognostic value of the 257 DMGs in the TCGA-LUAD dataset, we conducted univariate Cox regression analysis with a significance threshold of P < 0.05. We identified 72 DMGs that satisfied our screening criteria (Table S6). Next, employing LASSO regression analysis, we constructed a LASSO regression model based on these 72 DMGs (Fig. 3A-B). Considering the minimum partial likelihood bias, we retained 26 DMGs out of the initial 72 DMGs as diagnostic markers for LUAD. Moreover, we visually represented the LASSO regression outcomes and generated a LASSO variable trajectory diagram (Fig. 3A). The 26 DMGs included in the LASSO regression model were AMN, BIRC3, CCT6A, CLEC3B, CPLX2, CPS1, FNIP2, FURIN, GLB1L3, GLCCI1, GSTA1, HLA-DQB2, ID1, INHA, KCNK3, KCTD12, KLK11, LGR4, MUC15, NOTUM, NT5E, PCSK9, SEMA3A, SLC34A2, TCIM, and TM4SF4. To further validate the prognostic significance of the 26 DMGs in the TCGA-LUAD dataset, we employed multifactor Cox regression to analyze the correlation between the expression levels of the 26 DMGs and the clinical prognosis of LUAD patients and ultimately developed a DMG prognostic model consisting of 13 promising PMGs (BIRC3, CCT6A, CPLX2, GLCCI1, GSTA1, HLA-DQB2, ID1, KCTD12, MUC15, NOTUM, NT5E, TCIM, and TM4SF4) (Table S7).

The fundamental depiction of the DMG prognostic model using a forest plot is shown in Fig. 3C, while Fig. 3D provides a visual representation of the sample grouping within the constructed DMG prognostic model using a risk score factor map. The analysis of the prognostic KM curve demonstrated a highly significant difference (P < 0.001) in the survival outcomes of LUAD patients between the high- and low-risk score groups in the TCGA-LUAD dataset (Fig. 3E). Discussion

LUADs are an important part of NSCLC and seriously threaten human health. Despite recent advancements in the management of NSCLC, including molecular targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the prognosis for NSCLC patients is still unsatisfactory. Molecular targeted therapy, particularly EGFR-TKI monotherapy, has emerged as a pivotal treatment modality and a foundational element in the combined therapeutic approach for NSCLC patients with EGFR mutations. However, the development of acquired drug resistance limits the long-term efficacy of EGFR-TKIs, leading to treatment failure and poor prognosis. Therefore, understanding the underlying mechanisms of drug resistance, the tumor microenvironment, tumor heterogeneity, and the intricate interplay between EGFR and various molecular players is crucial for exploring novel treatment strategies to improve patient outcomes and guide personalized therapeutic approaches.

The objective of this study was to investigate the potential mechanisms of action of EGFR in LUAD and provide new ideas and potential targets for its diagnosis and treatment. Previous research has demonstrated that CCT6A is located in close proximity to EGFR on chromosome 7p11. Additionally, in both LUAD and glioblastoma patients, CCT6A and EGFR were found to be coamplified and coexpressed. Notably, CCT6A amplification was associated with unfavorable outcomes (Hallal et al. 2019; Wang et al. 2022). In this study, we identified 13 PMGs—BIRC3, CCT6A, CPLX2, GLCCI1, GSTA1, HLA-DQB2, ID1, KCTD12, MUC15, NOTUM, NT5E, TCIM, and TM4SF4—and among them, the expression levels of CCT6A and NT5E were upregulated, whereas the expression level of KCTD12 was downregulated in the LUAD group compared to the normal group. Furthermore, CCT6A and KCTD12 displayed high accuracy in differentiating between the two groups, indicating their potential diagnostic significance. These findings imply that these PMGs may play a crucial role in the occurrence and development of LUAD and might become potential biomarkers for diagnosis and prognostic evaluation. Ying Z et al. reported that CCT6A suppressed SMAD2 function and promoted metastasis in NSCLC cells (Ying et al. 2017). Similarly, Zhu M et al. reported that high expression of CCT6A significantly decreased the survival of patients with NSCLC (Zhu et al. 2017). In this study, we also found that CCT6A was significantly overexpressed in LUAD tissue compared to paracancerous tissue, correlating with poor OS, while KCTD12 was downregulated in LUAD and associated with better OS, as validated by qRT-PCR and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis.

Previous studies have also indicated a significant decrease in the mRNA and protein expression levels of KCTD12 in LUAD (Shi et al. 2022). These findings indicated that CCT6A and KCTD12 are likely involved in sensitivity to targeted therapy and are promising candidates for the diagnosis of LUAD.

GO analysis revealed that 13 PMGs were enriched in a series of functions related to immune response, signal transduction, and biological pathway regulation in terms of BP and MF. KEGG analysis revealed enrichment of signaling pathways associated with platinum drug resistance and the Hippo signaling pathway, and GSEA revealed significant enrichment of genes related to cell cycle checkpoints, glycolysis, programmed cell death, and apoptosis pathways. These results offer crucial insights to enhance our understanding of the functional roles of these genes in the initiation and progression of LUAD, which could aid in the discovery of potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Luo L et al. demonstrated that KCTD12 effectively suppressed the growth of human uveal melanoma OCM-1 cells and delayed the progression from G2/M to G1 phase in OCM-1 cells overexpressing KCTD12 (Luo et al. 2017). Adefovir dipivoxil enhanced the sensitivity of colon cancer cells to vemurafenib by disrupting the KCTD12-CDK1 interaction, leading to G2 phase arrest in the cell cycle (Yang et al. 2019). KCTD12 binds to CDC25B and activates CDK1 and Aurora A to promote the G2/M transition and enhance tumorigenesis (Zhong et al. 2017). Zeng W et al. (Zeng et al. 2022a) reported that CCT6A knockdown suppressed cell growth, induced apoptosis, and halted cell cycle progression at the G0/G1 phase in OS cells. These findings indicated that KCTD12 and CCT6A likely play crucial roles in cycle checkpoint and apoptosis pathways for targeted therapy in LUAD.

We also constructed a protein–protein interaction (PPI), mRNA–miRNA, and mRNA–TF interaction network to predict the miRNAs and TFs that interact with the hub genes. Seven hub genes (BIRC3, CCT6A, GLCCI1, ID1, KCTD12, NOTUM, and TM4SF4), 33 TFs, and a total of 59 pairs of mRNA‒miRNA interactions were identified. Notably, interactions such as those between CCT6A and miR-152-3p, as well as between CCT6A and the miR-148a/152 regulatory network, were established. These insights enhance our understanding of the roles and interaction networks of these genes in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) development, offering valuable leads for further exploration into the molecular mechanisms underlying LUAD. Previous studies have reported (Li et al. 2020) that the regulatory network involving miR-152-3p and CCT6A could play a significant role in glioma diagnosis and treatment. Specifically, NEAT1 was found to upregulate CCT6A expression in glioma tissues and cell lines, and this effect was reversed by miR-152-3p. Additionally, in colon cancer, research by Peng et al (Peng et al. 2022) revealed that CCT6A was upregulated while miR-148a/152 was downregulated in tumor tissues. Further investigations demonstrated that increasing miR-148a/152 expression suppressed migration, invasion, and proliferation, whereas reducing CCT6A expression induced cell apoptosis in colon cancer. These collective findings suggested that CCT6A may have implications for the diagnosis and treatment of LUAD.

Although this study successfully identified prognosis module genes (PMGs) associated with LUAD and validated their expression patterns using qRT-PCR, several limitations exist. Firstly, the study did not conduct in vivo (e.g., mouse models) or in vitro experiments (e.g., cell culture) to explore the functions of these potential targets, limiting our understanding of PMG’ biological roles and mechanisms in LUAD. Secondly, the small sample size of only eight LUAD samples used for expression validation may affect the generalizability and statistical significance of the results, not fully reflecting PMGs’ performance in larger cohorts. Additionally, reliance on various public databases for data analysis, such as TCGA and GEO, introduces heterogeneity that may impact result interpretation and limit conclusions. Lastly, due to time and resource constraints, we were unable to comprehensively investigate the functions of all identified PMGs across different LUAD subtypes, potentially leaving important biological characteristics and mechanisms undiscovered. To address these limitations, future research should include larger sample validations, functional experiments, and the integration of multi-omics data to further elucidate the relationship between EGFR expression and the role of PMGs in LUAD.

Conclusions

In summary, we identified DEGs and DMGs related to the expression of EGFR in the high/low and LUAD/normal groups by comprehensive bioinformatics analysis of LUAD tissues. In total, 13 PMGs (BIRC3, CCT6A, CPLX2, GLCCI1, GSTA1, HLA-DQB2, ID1, KCTD12, MUC15, NOTUM, NT5E, TCIM, and TM4SF4) were screened and verified to be able to predict survival prognosis. Notably, CCT6A and KCTD12 exhibited high diagnostic accuracy in LUAD. Furthermore, in vivo and in vitro, the expression levels of CCT6A and KCTD12 were consistent among the four LUAD datasets. These findings suggest that KCTD12 and CCT6A might be candidate biomarkers for diagnostic performance and could guide individualized therapy for EGFR-TKI-resistant LUAD patients. This study revealed that CCT6A and KCTD12 are dependable predictors of LUAD efficacy, and the targeting of CCT6A and KCTD12 represents a novel avenue for further research and therapeutic approaches in LUAD.

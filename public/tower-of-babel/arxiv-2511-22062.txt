Abelian and non-Abelian mimetic black holes


Abstract
We investigate black hole solutions in the mimetic extension of the Einstein-Yang-Mills system, in which the Yang-Mills term is constrained to be constant. In the Abelian U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) case, we find a static spherically symmetric solution that includes the Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordström black holes as special cases. Moreover, we identify a stealth Schwarzschild solution with an electric hair. We show that it is impossible to have magnetic hair in the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge case, while, in contrast, the non-Abelian S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) stealth solutions can sustain both electric and magnetic hair. Unlike the conventional S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) Einstein–Yang–Mills black hole, which requires a unit magnetic parameter to exhibit nontrivial non-Abelian contributions, the stealth mimetic S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) solution admits genuinely non-Abelian configurations with arbitrary integer magnetic parameter.

\displaystyle S=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu})}\left[\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R-2\lambda\left(\tr{{\bf F}_{\mu\nu} {\bf F}^{\mu\nu}}+\epsilon\mathcal{E}^{2}\right)\right]\,,

where

The gauge group in the Yang-Mills sector can be any compact, semi-simple group, e.g.

\displaystyle[{\bf T}_{a},{\bf T}_{b}]=i\,f_{abc}{\bf T}_{c}\,,

(1.4)

where

\displaystyle{\bf F}_{\mu\nu}={\bf T}_{a}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad{\bf A}_{\mu}={\bf T}_{a}A^{a}_{\mu}\,.

Cosmological background solution for the spatially curved FLRW with

In this section we aim to find static spherically symmetric black hole solutions in mimetic gravity theory (
1.1
) when the Yang-Mills field has
U(1)
gauge group. In that case, the generators are
1\times 1
matrices
{\bf T}
and
[{\bf A}_{\mu},{\bf A}_{\nu}]=0
. Fixing the trace as
\tr({\bf T}^2)=1/2
, the action (
1.1
) reduces to

S_{U(1)}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu})}\left[\frac{M_{\text{Pl}}^{2}}{2}R-\lambda\left(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+2\epsilon\mathcal{E}^{2}\right)\right],

(2.1)

where the
U(1)
strength tensor is given by

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}\,.

(2.2)

Varying the action (
2.1
) with respect to the auxiliary field
\lambda
gives

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=-2\epsilon{\cal E}^{2}\,,

(2.3)

which can be also equivalently obtained by restricting (
1.3
) to the
U(1)
gauge group. Variation with respect to the metric and gauge field
A_{\mu}
gives the Einstein equations and generalized Maxwell equations respectively

M_{\rm Pl}^{2}G_{\mu\nu}=4\lambda F_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}F_{\nu\alpha}\,,\qquad\nabla_{\alpha}\big(\lambda F^{\alpha\nu}\big)=0\,,

(2.4)

where
G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}R/2
is the Einstein tensor. There was another term in the right hand side of the Einstein equations as
\lambda\left(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+2\epsilon\mathcal{E}^{2}\right)
which vanishes after imposing the mimetic constraint (
2.3
).

We consider the static spherically symmetric metric

g_{\mu\nu}=\mbox{diag}\big(-\sigma^{2}(r)~f(r),f(r)^{-1},r^{2},r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\big)\,,

(2.5)

where
\sigma
is the lapse function which, without loss of generality, can be choosen to be positive
\sigma>0
.

In the gauge field sector, we consider
dyonic ansatz
for
A_{\mu}
and also static one for the auxiliary field

\displaystyle A_{\mu}=\big(a_{0}(r),0,0,q_{m}\cos{\theta}\big)\,,\qquad\lambda=\lambda(r)\,,

(2.6)

where
a_{0}
is the electric potential and
q_{m}
represents the magnetic parameter. Note that, despite of the presence of electric and magnetic contributions in the background configuration (
2.6
), one can directly confirm that all spacetime scalar combinations involving the strength field tensor such as
F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
,
\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
, and
R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\alpha\beta}
are indeed spherically symmetric ensuring the black hole solutions obtained will possess spherical symmetry:

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{2q_{m}^{2}}{r^{4}}-\frac{2a_{0}^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{2}}\,,\qquad F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{4q_{m}{}a_{0}^{\prime}}{r^{2}\sigma}\;,

(2.7)

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu\rho\alpha}F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\alpha}=\frac{4q_{m}^{2}}{r^{6}}(1-f)+\frac{2a_{0}^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{3}}\bigl(\sigma{f}^{\prime\prime}+3f^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}+2f\sigma^{\prime\prime}\bigr)\;.

Using the above result in (
2.3
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{q_{m}^{2}}{r^{4}}=\epsilon\mathcal{E}^{2}\;.

(2.8)

The above relation shows that the limit
q_{m}\to 0
is well-defined while the limit
a^{\prime}_{0}\to 0
is not well-defined. Therefore we should always assume
a^{\prime}_{0}\neq 0
. This means pure magnetic-type solution does not exist in this theory. Moreover, looking at the limit of large
r
, we find
\tfrac{a_{0}^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{2}}\approx\epsilon\mathcal{E}^{2}
which shows that the negative values of
\epsilon
are not allowed. Hence, for the rest of the analysis for the
U(1)
case in this section, we set
\epsilon=+1
. Then, Eq. (
2.8
) simplifies into:

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}^{\prime}}{\sigma}=\pm\frac{q_{m}}{r^{2}}\sqrt{1+x^{4}}\,,

(2.9)

where we have introduced a new characteristic scale
r_{N}
and a dimensionless variable
x
as follows

\displaystyle r_{N}\equiv\sqrt{\frac{q_{m}}{\mathcal{E}}}\,,\qquad x\equiv\frac{r}{r_{N}}\,,

(2.10)

in terms of which the mimetic effects can be better tracked. Note that
x
is only a useful quantity to characterize the mimetic effects and we have to work with both
r
and
x
.

Plugging the ansatz Eq. (
2.6
) in the generalized Maxwell equations in (
2.4
), we find that all the spatial components vanish identically while the temporal component gives

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}\lambda\frac{a_{0}^{\prime}}{\sigma}\right)^{\prime}=0\;.

(2.11)

The pure temporal
t-t
component of the Einstein equation yields

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}\left[1-(rf)^{\prime}\right]=4r^{2}\lambda\frac{a_{0}^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{2}}\,.

(2.12)

Combining the above equation with the radial
r-r
component of the Einstein equation, we find

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{3}f\sigma\sigma^{\prime}=0\,,\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma=c_{2}\,,

(2.13)

where
c_{2}
is a dimensionless integration constant. Imposing
\sigma=c_{2}
in the angular part, we find

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}r^{2}\left(r^{2}f^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}=8q_{m}^{2}\lambda\;.

(2.14)

Indeed all equations are symmetric under the redefinition
q_{m}\to-q_{m}
. Therefore, it is sufficient to consider only positive values,
q_{m}>0
. For the same reason, we also restrict our analysis to
\mathcal{E}>0
without loss of generality.

In the following two subsections, we consider the cases

2.1
Solution with
\lambda(r)\neq 0

We have found that

r^{2}\lambda\frac{a_{0}^{\prime}}{c_{2}}=c_{1}\,,\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lambda=\frac{1}{q_{m}}\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{1+x^{4}}}\,,

(2.15)

where
c_{1}
is a dimensionless integration constant. Using (

) and above results in (
2.12
) and then integrating, we find

\displaystyle f(r)=1+\frac{c_{3}}{r}+\frac{Q_{m}}{r^{2}}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{4};\frac{3}{4};-x^{4}\right)\,;\qquad Q_{m}\equiv\frac{4c_{1}q_{m}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\,,

(2.16)

where
c_{3}
is a dimensionful integration constant,
\;{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;x)
is the Hypergeometric function, and we have defined
Q_{m}
as the dimensionful magnetic parameter.

We now analyze the solutions in three distinct regimes: (a) Far away from the horizon of the black hole:
x\gg 1
, (b) Inside the horizon of the black hole:
x\ll 1
, and (c) Near the black hole horizon:
x\sim 1
.

Far away from the black hole, i.e, at
\;r\to+\infty
, one obtains the following asymptotic behavior of the BH solution:

\displaystyle f(r)\to 1+\frac{c_{3}}{r}-\frac{4c_{1}\mathcal{E}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\Bigl(1-\frac{2\Gamma(3/4)^{2}}{\pi^{1/2}x}\Bigr)\,,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\lambda\to\frac{c_{1}}{\mathcal{E}r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2x^{4}}\right)\,,\qquad\frac{a_{0}^{\prime}}{c_{2}}\to\pm\mathcal{E}\left(1+\frac{1}{2x^{4}}\right)\;.

(2.18)

For
{\cal E}=q_{m}=0
, we recover Schwarzschild black hole and
c_{3}
in
f(r)
characterizes the mass of the black hole. The corrections are two types. If we set
{\cal E}=0
, all corrections disappear. On the other hand, all
x
-dependent corrections are proportional to
q_{m}
since
x=r_{N}/r
and
r_{N}=\sqrt{q_{m}/{\cal E}}
. These magnetic-type corrections become only important for
x\ll 1
or
r\ll{r}_{N}
. Assuming that
q_{m}
is not too large, which is a reasonable choice, we can fix the
t-t
component of the metric as
g_{00}\to-1
such that we recover the Minkowski spacetime (asymptotic flatness condition) at large distances
r\to\infty
. Demanding this boundary condition, we obtain the following relation between
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
:

\displaystyle c_{2}=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{\cal E}}}\,,\qquad\delta_{\cal E}\equiv\frac{4c_{1}\mathcal{E}}{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}\,,

(2.19)

where
\delta_{\cal E}
is the dimensionless quantity. We see that the existence of an asymptotic region at infinity naturally imposes a bound
\delta_{\cal E}<1
. This result suggests to work with the new radial variable

\displaystyle\tilde{r}\equiv\frac{r}{\sqrt{1-\delta_{\cal E}}}\,,

(2.20)

in terms of which the spacetime metric takes the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-h(\tilde{r})dt^{2}+\frac{d\tilde{r}^{2}}{h(\tilde{r})}+(1-\delta_{\cal E})\tilde{r}^{2}d\Omega^{2}\,,

(2.21)

where we have defined
h(\tilde{r})\equiv\frac{1}{1-\delta_{\cal E}}\;f\left(\tilde{r}\sqrt{1-\delta_{\cal E}}\;\right)
. Taking the limit
r\to\infty
we find
h({\tilde{r}})\approx 1+{\cal O}\left(1/{\tilde{r}}\right)
and we can identify the coefficient of
{\cal O}\left(1/{\tilde{r}}\right)
with the Schwarzschild radius
r_{g}\equiv 2M/M_{\rm Pl}^{2}
. Doing so, we can get rid of
c_{3}
in favor of
r_{g}
and
h({\tilde{r}})
takes the following form

\displaystyle h({\tilde{r}})

\displaystyle=1-\frac{r_{g}{}}{\tilde{r}}+\frac{\delta_{\cal E}}{1-\delta_{\cal E}}\left(1+\frac{\mathcal{F}(x)}{x^{2}}\right)\;,

(2.22)

where
x=r/r_{N}={\tilde{r}}/{\tilde{r}}_{N}
and we have defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(x)\equiv{}_{2}F_{1}\big(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{3}{4},-x^{4}\big)-\frac{2\Gamma(3/4)^{2}}{\pi^{1/2}}x\,.

(2.23)

The Schwarzschild limit is now clear: for
\delta_{\cal E}=0
, Eq. (
2.20
) gives
{\tilde{r}}=r
and Eq. (
2.22
) gives
h(r)=1-r_{g}/r
. The asymptotic behavior of the function
{\cal F}(x)
is

\displaystyle{\cal F}(x)=\begin{cases}1-\frac{2\Gamma(3/4)^{2}}{\pi^{1/2}}x&x\ll 1\,,\\
-x^{2}+\frac{1}{6x^{2}}&x\gg 1\,.\end{cases}

(2.24)

from which we find

\displaystyle h\big|_{x\ll 1}=\frac{\delta_{\cal E}}{1-\delta_{\cal E}}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\,,\qquad h\big|_{x\gg 1}=1-\frac{r_{g}{}}{\tilde{r}}+\frac{\delta_{\cal E}}{6(1-\delta_{\cal E})}\frac{1}{x^{4}}\,.

(2.25)

In the limit
q_{m}\to 0
(
Q_{m}\to 0
), the function
h(r)
in Eq. (
2.22
) takes the following form

\displaystyle h_{q_{m}\to 0}=1-\frac{r_{g}}{\tilde{r}}\,.

(2.26)

This shows that in the absence of magnetic charge, the metric is locally Schwarzschild while globally there is a factor
1-\delta_{\cal E}
in front of the angular part. On the other hand, for the case
\mathcal{E}\to 0
(
\delta_{\cal E}\to 0
) and

, one recovers Reissner–Nordström solution with magnetic charge

\displaystyle h_{\mathcal{E}\to 0}=1-\frac{r_{g}}{{r}}+\frac{Q_{m}}{{r}^{2}}\;.

From the metric solution Eq (

2.6

\displaystyle\sigma(r)=1\,,\quad f(r)=1-\frac{r_{g}}{r}\,,\qquad\mbox{for}\quad\lambda(r)=0\,.

(2.29)

Substituting the above solution for
\sigma(r)
in (
2.9
) and then integrating, one arrives at the following solution

\displaystyle a_{0}(r)=c_{3}\mp\frac{q_{m}}{r}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{4};\tfrac{3}{4};-x^{4}\right)\,,

(2.30)

where
c_{3}
is an integration constant. For the case of zero magnetic charge,
q_{m}\to 0
, the solution takes a particularly simple form

\displaystyle a_{0}(r)=c_{3}\pm\mathcal{E}r\;.

(2.31)

The black hole solution (
2.29
) is a stealth solution that coincides identically with the Schwarzschild metric. However, the nonzero electric potential (
2.30
) indicates that the black hole possesses nontrivial gauge field hair. This difference will manifest at the level of perturbations, making this stealth solution distinct from the usual Schwarzschild in general relativity. A detailed analysis of perturbations around this black hole background is left for future work.

3
Non-Abelian
SU(2)
case

In the case of
SU(2)
gauge group, the generators can be chosen as
{\bf T}_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\tau_{a}
, and the corresponding structure constants will be
f_{abc}=\varepsilon_{abc}
, with
\tau_{a}
denoting the Pauli matrices, and
\varepsilon_{abc}
being totally antisymmetric tensor with
\varepsilon_{123}=1
. Using
\tr({\bf T}_a,{\bf T}_b)=\delta_{ab}/2
, the action (
1.1
) can be written in the following form

S_{SU(2)}=\int{\rm d}^{4}x\sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu})}\left[\,\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^{2}}{2}R-\lambda\big(F^{a}_{\mu\nu}F_{a}^{\mu\nu}+2\epsilon{\cal E}^{2}\big)\,\right]\,,

(3.1)

where

F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^{a}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{a}_{\mu}+g\,\varepsilon^{a}{}_{bc}A^{b}_{\mu}A^{c}_{\nu}\,.

(3.2)

The Greek letters refer to spacetime indices and run as
(\mu=0,\,1,\,2,\,3)
while the Latin index
a
(where
a=1,2,3
) denotes the group index.

Varying the action (
3.1
) with respect to the auxiliary field
\lambda
gives

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}^{a}F^{\mu\nu}_{a}=-2\epsilon\,\mathcal{E}^{2}

(3.3)

which can be also equivalently obtained by restricting (
1.3
) to the
SU(2)
gauge group.

Variation with respect to metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and vector field
A_{\mu}^{a}
give

\displaystyle M_{\rm Pl}^{2}G_{\mu\nu}=4\lambda F^{a}_{\mu\alpha}F_{a\nu}{}^{\alpha}\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\lambda F^{a\alpha}{}_{\mu}\right)+\lambda g\varepsilon^{abc}A^{\alpha}_{b}F_{c\alpha\mu}=0\;.

(3.5)

For the metric, we consider the static spherically symmetric background Eq. (
2.5
). The background configuration that is consistent with this configuration is given by the Witten-ansatz
[
40
]

\displaystyle\begin{split}gA^{1}_{\mu}&=\left(0,0,\omega_{2},-q_{m}\omega_{1}\sin\theta\right)\,,\\
gA^{2}_{\mu}&=\left(0,0,\omega_{1},+q_{m}\omega_{2}\sin\theta\right)\,,\\
gA^{3}_{\mu}&=\left(a_{0},a_{r},\omega_{2},+q_{m}\cos\theta\right)\,,\end{split}

(3.6)

where
a_{0},\,a_{r},\,\omega_{1},\,\omega_{2}
are functions of
r
. The above ansatz is singular in the limit
\theta\rightarrow 0,\pi
. However, this singularity can be removed if we assume that
q_{m}
is integer (see Ref.
[
40
]
for the details). Therefore, in what follows, we consider
q_{m}
to be an integer. Moreover, we choose the following gauge:

\displaystyle a_{r}=\alpha^{\prime}\,,\qquad\omega_{1}=\omega\cos{\alpha}\,,\qquad\omega_{1}=\omega\sin{\alpha}\,,

(3.7)

where
\omega,\,\alpha
are functions of
r
. One can explicitly confirm that after imposing the above gauge, the gauge-invariant quantities are independent of the choice of
\alpha
. This means that
a_{r}
and one of
\omega_{1,2}
can be removed by fixing the gauge in the static background. Therefore, the two physical variables after fixing the gauge are
a_{0}
and
\omega
. By analogy with the
U(1)
case, we refer to the
a_{0}
, appearing in Eq. (
3.6
), as the electric part, and to
\omega
as the magnetic part.

Let us first look at the mimetic constraint (
3.3
). Substituting (
2.5
) and (
3.6
) in (
3.3
) and applying the gauge (
3.7
) we find

\displaystyle\frac{a_{0}^{\prime 2}}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{\left(1+q_{m}^{2}\right)}{r^{2}}\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\frac{\omega^{2}}{f}-\frac{q_{m}^{2}}{r^{4}}\left(1-\omega^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\left(1+q_{m}^{2}\right)}{r^{2}}f\omega^{\prime 2}=\epsilon g^{2}\mathcal{E}^{2}\;.

(3.8)

For
\omega=0
the above equation reduces to the
U(1)
case Eq. (
2.8
). This shows that all non-Abelian effects are encoded in
\omega
and we, therefore, focus on the case of
\omega\neq 0
.

3.1
Stealth solution with magnetic hair

Similar to the
U(1)
case, for
\lambda(r)=0
, the gauge field equations (
3.5
) automatically satisfies. As pointed out in Ref.
[
40
]
, to satisfy the Yang-Mills equation in the usual Einstein-Yang-Mills system, one needs to assume either
q_{m}^{2}=1
or
\omega=0
. Since all non-Abelian effects disappear in the latter case, one has to consider
q_{m}^{2}=1
. However, in our case, the fact that
\lambda
is function of spacetime makes it possible to evade this constraint by assuming
\lambda(r)=0
while
q_{m}
and
\omega
remain completely arbitrary. This is one of the key findings of this work. Moreover, the energy-momentum tensor in the Einstein equations (
3.4
) vanishes around the static spherically symmetric solution with
\lambda(r)=0
. This means all vacuum solutions of general relativity, like Schwarzschild and Kerr are the solution of our theory as long as
\lambda(r)=0
. For simplicity, we focus on the Schwarzschild case

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\frac{r_{g}}{r}\,,\quad\sigma(r)=1\,,\qquad\mbox{for}\,\,\,\lambda(r)=0\,.

(3.9)

With the above choices, the only nontrivial equation is the mimetic constraint (
3.8
) which determines the evolution of the non-Abelian electric and magnetic hairs that are encoded in functions
a_{0}
and
\omega
respectively. For
\omega=0
all non-Abelian effects disappear and we end up with the result of stealth (Abelian)
U(1)
Schwarzschild black hole that we already studied in subsection
2.2
. We thus focus on the case of
a_{0}=0
which corresponds to the pure non-Abelian magnetic hair. In that case, Eq. (
3.8
) simplifies to

\displaystyle-\frac{q_{m}^{2}}{r^{4}}\left(1-\omega^{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\left(1+q_{m}^{2}\right)}{r^{2}}\left(1-\frac{r_{g}}{r}\right)\omega^{\prime 2}=\epsilon g^{2}\mathcal{E}^{2}\;,

(3.10)

where we have used Eq. (
3.9
). For
\epsilon>0
, the above equation cannot be satisfied for
r>r_{g}
and the exterior region is not well defined. We thus have to consider
\epsilon<0
. It is also important to note that even if we consider
\epsilon<0
,
q_{m}\neq 0
and
\omega\neq 0
are necessary to have well-defined solution both outside and inside of the stealth black hole solution. Without loss of generality, we choose
\epsilon=-1
, and Eq. (
3.10
) becomes

\displaystyle q_{m}^{2}\left(1-\omega^{2}\right)^{2}+\left(1+q_{m}^{2}\right)\left({\tilde{r}}-1\right){\tilde{r}}\omega_{{\tilde{r}}}^{2}=\tilde{\cal E}^{2}{\tilde{r}}^{4}\,,

(3.11)

where
\omega_{{\tilde{r}}}
denotes derivatives of
\omega
with respect to
{\tilde{r}}
and we have defined the dimensionless parameters

\displaystyle\tilde{r}\equiv\frac{r}{r_{g}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\cal E}\equiv g{\cal E}r_{g}^{2}\;.

(3.12)

It is interesting to note that for
g=0
, hence
\tilde{\cal E}=0
, the situation becomes analogous to the
U(1)
case, and consequently, no stealth solution with magnetic hair is possible. Hence, we restrict our analysis to nonzero values of
g
.

4
Summary

In the context of
mimetic Einstein-Yang-Mills system, we looked for static spherically symmetric black hole solutions. We considered two cases of Abelian
U(1)
and non-Abelian
SU(2)
gauge symmetries.

In the
U(1)
case, which can be interpreted as a mimetic extension of the Einstein-Maxwell theory, considering a dyonic ansatz, we find a new, static spherically symmetric black hole solution given by (
2.21
) and (
2.22
). The solution has three independent parameters
(r_{g},q_{m},{\cal E})
where
r_{g}
is the Schwarzschild radius,
q_{m}
is the magnetic parameter, and
{\cal E}
is the parameter which determines the scale where the modified gravity (mimetic effects) becomes important. In the limit
{\cal E}=0=q_{m}
, we find the usual Schwarzschild solution, in the limit
{\cal E}\neq 0
and
q_{m}=0
, we find a locally Schwarzschild solution with a modified solid angle measure while for
{\cal E}=0
and
q_{m}\neq 0
, as expected, we recover the Reissner-Nordström solution. For the case of
{\cal E}\neq 0
and
q_{m}\neq 0
, we showed that
the solution can admit up to two horizons. For some values of the parameter space, the black hole ceases to exist, and the solution becomes a naked singularity. This behavior is expected, as it has the Reissner-Nordström black holes as a subset, where sufficiently large electric or magnetic parameter relative to the black hole mass causes a continuous transition from a black hole to an extremal configuration and then to a naked singularity. Although this scenario possesses theoretical interests, it is unlikely to occur in realistic astrophysical settings as the possible electric or magnetic parameter of an astrophysical black hole is expected to be much smaller than its mass. Moreover, we have found another class of configurations, namely stealth solutions, which are black hole solution which have the same metric solution as the general relativity while there is a nontrivial profile of mimetic gauge field. These types of solution can be obtained when the auxiliary mimetic field
\lambda
vanishes at the background level.

In the
U(1)
case, we showed that the stealth solution possesses a nontrivial electric hair, making it physically different from the usual Schwarzschild black hole in general relativity. However, we found that it is impossible to obtain a nontrivial pure magnetic hair for
U(1)
case. We thus looked for a possible magnetic hair in the case of non-Abelian
SU(2)
gauge symmetry. Unlike the
U(1)
case, the
SU(2)
stealth solution can support both nontrivial electric and magnetic hair. While the metric has the same form as the usual Schwarzschild form, the magnetic hair, encoded in the function
\omega(r)
which characterizes pure non-Abelian features, has a nontrivial profile. In other words, we have a Schwarzschild black hole with a non-Abelian hair. The non-Abelian hair depends on two parameter: the magnetic parameter
q_{m}
and the normalized mimetic scale
\tilde{\cal E}\propto{\cal E}
. We solved the equation for
\omega
numerically and obtained an analytical expression for its asymptotic behavior, matching the numerical solution in the asymptotic limit. Interestingly, the number of distinct solutions depends on the model parameters: there can exist two (for
q_{m}<\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
), three (for
q_{m}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
), and four (for
q_{m}>\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
) different configurations for the gauge field. Whether these branches are stable and physically relevant is not yet known. A detailed stability analysis is left for future work.

Finally, we identify a striking difference between the well-known
SU(2)
Einstein-Yang-Mills black hole solutions and the stealth
SU(2)
mimetic configurations. For the
SU(2)
Einstein-Yang-Mills black hole, the magnetic parameter should be fixed as
q_{m}^{2}=1
, otherwise, the solution becomes embedded Abelian (
\omega=0
) and there will be no pure non-Abelian contribution
[
73
]
. In contrast, for the stealth mimetic
SU(2)
solution, the solution remains genuinely non-Abelian for arbitrary integer values of the magnetic parameter
q_{m}
of the
U(1)
bundle.

For future directions, it is interesting to study the black hole thermodynamics, shadows, and quasi-normal modes for both
U(1)
and
SU(2)
cases. We leave this for future work.

Lefschetz formula for locally symmetric spaces: the real rank one case
Yanli Song

Abstract
. Let G G be a semi-simple real Lie group of real rank one and Γ \Gamma be a discrete subgroup in G G such that Γ \ G \Gamma\backslash G has finite volume. Our main result is an explicit formula for the Lefschetz number of Hecke operators.



1.
Introduction

Locally symmetric spaces arise naturally as quotients of symmetric spaces by discrete groups, playing a fundamental role in diverse areas such as arithmetic geometry, number theory, and mathematical physics. A central problem in this setting is the spectral decomposition of
L^{2}
-spaces associated with these quotients and the study of Hecke operators, which encode deep arithmetic information and provide a powerful tool for investigating automorphic forms and representation theory.

Let
X=G/K
be a Riemannian symmetric space of noncompact type, where
G
is a connected, noncompact, real semisimple Lie group and
K
is a maximal compact subgroup. Consider a Dirac-type operator
D_{\mu}=D_{\mu}^{+}\oplus D_{\mu}^{-}
acting on sections of a bundle associated with a
K
-module
V_{\mu}
, whose highest weight is
\mu
. If
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of
G
, the locally symmetric space
X_{\Gamma}=\Gamma\backslash G/K
inherits an induced operator
D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}=D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,+}\oplus D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,-}
, which is elliptic.

When
X_{\Gamma}
is compact, the Atiyah-Singer index theorem provides an explicit topological formula for the index of
D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}
in terms of the
K
-character
V_{\mu}
. More generally, we consider a Atiyah-Singer Lefschetz fixed point theorem for the Hecke operator. Given any element
\alpha
belongs to the commensurator of
\Gamma
in
G
, we define a double coset
\Xi=\Gamma\alpha\Gamma
and a Hecke operator
T_{\Xi}
associated to
\Xi
. If
X_{\Gamma}
is compact, the Dirac-type operator
D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}
is Fredholm, in the sense that
\dim\operatorname{Ker}\left(D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,+}\right)
are finite dimensional. The Lefschetz number is defined as

(1.1)

\displaystyle L\left(T_{\Xi},D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}\right)\colon=\Tr\left(T_{\Xi}\mid\operatorname{Ker}\left(D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,+}\right)\right)-\Tr\left(T_{\Xi}\mid\operatorname{Ker}\left(D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,-}\right)\right).

Moscovici
[
Mos84
]
derived an explicit expression:

(1.2)

L\left(T_{\Xi},D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}\right)=\sum_{\xi}\operatorname{vol}\left(\Gamma_{\xi}\backslash G_{\xi}\right)\cdot L\left(\xi,D_{\mu}\right),

where the sum runs over representatives of the finitely many central and elliptic
\Gamma
-conjugacy classes in
\Xi
, and each
L(\xi,D_{\mu})
is a Lefschetz number for the
\xi
-action on
X
, given by a topological formula. In a special case when
\Xi=\Gamma
, (
1.2
) gives the
L^{2}
-index formula:

(1.3)

\ind(D^{\Gamma}_{\mu})=\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cdot\ind_{G}(D_{\mu}),

where
\ind_{G}(D_{\mu})
denotes the “
G
-index,” computed topologically in
[
CM82
]
.

However, many of the most interesting locally symmetric spaces are noncompact and exhibit “cusps”. The problem of computing the Lefschetz number becomes complicated considerably by the existence of a continuous spectrum. A natural assumption in this context is that
X_{\Gamma}
has finite Riemannian volume and strictly negative sectional curvature. According to Theorem 2.1 in
[
Mos82
]
,
\dim\operatorname{Ker}\left(D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,\pm}\right)
are still finite dimensional even though the Dirac-type operators
D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,\pm}
may fail to be Fredholm (when
\mu
is singular). Thus, Lefschetz numbers
L\left(T_{\Xi},D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}\right)
in (
1.1
) are well-defined. The main objective of this paper is to extend the formula (
1.2
) to the noncompact setting, leading to the following:

(1.4)

L\left(T_{\Xi},D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}\right)=\sum_{\xi}L\left(\xi,D_{\mu}\right)+\text{``correction term''},

where the correction term accounts for the presence of cusps and is generally no longer topological.

For de Rham operators, Arthur
[
Art89
]
provided a solution to (
1.4
) in the adelic setting. Later, Goresky and MacPherson
[
GKM97
]
developed an alternative approach using intersection cohomology techniques. For signature and Dolbeault operators, a Lefschetz formula was obtained by Stern in
[
Ste94
]
. Additional treatments of this problem can be found in
[
Ste89
,
M8̈3
,
M8̈5
]
.

In this paper, we focus on general Dirac operators. A pioneering contribution was made by Barbasch and Moscovici
[
BM83
]
, where they explicitly computed the correction term in the index formula for Dirac operators, i.e., the case when
\Xi=\Gamma
. Recently, Barbasch-Moscovici’s formula was re-examined by Guo-Hochs-Wang
[
GHW24b
,
GHW24a
]
in the framework of K-theory. We now outline the key ideas. Consider the spinor Laplacians:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\Gamma,\pm}_{\mu}=D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,\pm}D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,\mp}

on the locally symmetric space
X_{\Gamma}
. The corresponding heat semigroups satisfy:

(1.6)

\displaystyle e^{-t\Delta_{\mu}^{\Gamma,\pm}}=R^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\right),

where
h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}
are heat kernels on the symmetric space
X
. Although the operators
R^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\right)
are generally not of trace class, their discrete part,
R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\right)
, are. Barbasch and Moscovici computed the index by applying the trace formula developed by Warner
[
War79
]
and Osborne-Warner
[
OW81
]
to
R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t}^{+}-h_{t}^{-}\right)
.

To avoid certain combinatorial complications, Barbasch and Moscovici imposed a restrictive assumption that
\Gamma
is neat (i.e., the group generated by the eigenvalues of
\gamma\in\Gamma
contains no roots of unity). This assumption significantly simplifies the structure of
\Gamma
-conjugacy classes by ensuring that all semisimple components of mixed elements lie in the (finite) center. As a result, the “correction terms” vanish in surprisingly many cases.

However, this assumption is highly restrictive and is nearly impossible to satisfy when studying Hecke operators. In this paper, we instead require only that
\Gamma
be an arithmetic discrete subgroup of
G
such that
\Gamma\backslash G
has finite volume. Following
[
BM83
]
, we express the Lefschetz number as:

(1.7)

\displaystyle L(T_{\Xi},D_{\mu}^{\Gamma})=\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{\Xi}\circ R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t}^{+}-h_{t}^{-}\right)\right),\quad t>0.

Instead of relying on the trace formula in
[
War79
,
OW81
]
, we employ the
invariant trace formula
, as developed by Arthur
[
Art81
,
Art88a
,
Art88b
]
for the adelic setting and Hoffmann
[
Hof99
]
for the real setting. This approach eliminates the need for intricate heat kernel estimates as
t\to\infty
, simplifying the analysis. Finally, we express the traces of
T_{\Xi}\circ R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t}^{+}-h_{t}^{-}\right)
in terms of certain invariant tempered distributions on
G
, whose “Fourier transforms” (at least the discrete part) are known.

Compared to the trace formula in
[
War79
,
OW81
]
, the invariant trace formula for Hecke operators contains additional terms, some of which are less explicit. Nevertheless, using the Selberg’s principle, we show that all the implicit terms in the invariant trace formula vanish, ensuring that the correction terms in (
1.4
) are completely explicit. The final formulas (Theorem
6.2
and
6.4
) we obtained involve constant terms in the Laurent expansion of certain Epstein zeta functions, characters of discrete series representations (their restrictions to both compact and non-compact Cartan subgroups), some necessary Lie algebra data, and an additional residue term for singular case. In particular, the correction terms are generally nonzero.

As an application, by taking
\Xi=\Gamma
, the index formula in (
1.4
) leads to a multiplicity formula for discrete series representations in
R_{d}^{\Gamma}
with sufficiently regular Harish-Chandra parameters, generalizing the results of Osborne, Warner, and DeGeorge
[
OW78
,
DeG82
]
. If one chooses other non-trivial
\Xi
, additional refined information about the multiplicities of representations in
R_{d}^{\Gamma}
can be extracted. By selecting a sufficiently rich collection of Hecke operators, it may be possible to compute the multiplicities of discrete series representations that are not sufficiently regular, or even those of non-tempered representations in
R_{d}^{\Gamma}
.

\Xi
.

R^{\Gamma}_{d}
.

G
.

•

: We present the trace formulas for Hecke operators and provide proofs for the main results.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

2.1.
Symmetric spaces and spinors

Let
G
be a noncompact connected semisimple Lie group with finite center. We shall assume that
G
has real rank one. Throughout this paper, it will be additionally supposed that
G
is simple and is embedded in the simply connected complex analytic group corresponding to the complexification of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
.

Let
\theta\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)
be the Cartan involution. Define
K\subset G
as the fixed point set of
\theta
in
G
. Then
K
is a connected maximal compact subgroup of
G
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
. The involution
\theta
acts as an automorphism on
\mathfrak{g}
, so that
\mathfrak{k}
is the eigenspace of
\theta
associated with the eigenvalue
+1
, while the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
-1
is denoted
\mathfrak{p}
. This gives the Cartan decomposition:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}.

Let
B:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a symmetric, nondegenerate, bilinear form that is both
G
and
\theta
-invariant. Assume that
B
is positive on
\mathfrak{p}
and negative on
\mathfrak{k}
. Thus, the restriction
B_{\mid\mathfrak{p}}
induces a Euclidean metric on
\mathfrak{p}
. Define the non-compact symmetric space:

(2.2)

\displaystyle X=G/K.

Since
K
acts on
\mathfrak{p}
via the adjoint action, the tangent bundle of
X
is given by
TX=G\times_{K}\mathfrak{p}
.
Equipped with the scalar product induced by
B_{\mid\mathfrak{p}}
, the space
X
becomes a Riemannian manifold.
We assume that

(2.3)

\operatorname{Rank}G=\operatorname{Rank}K,

which is equivalent to
G
having a compact Cartan subgroup.

We denote by
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
the basic spin representation of
\operatorname{Spin}(\mathfrak{p})
. Via the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{k}
on
\mathfrak{p}
,
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
becomes a
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}
-module. Under the assumption in (
2.3
), we know that
\dim\mathfrak{p}
is even, and so the
\operatorname{Spin}(\mathfrak{p})
-module
S_{\mathfrak{p}}
decomposes into two irreducible components:

(2.4)

\displaystyle S_{\mathfrak{p}}=S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{+}\oplus S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{-}.

The map
\Ad\colon K\rightarrow\mathrm{SO}(\mathfrak{p})
lifts to

(2.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Ad}:\widetilde{K}\rightarrow\operatorname{Spin}(\mathfrak{p}),

for a double cover
\widetilde{K}
of
K
. Let
\widehat{K}_{\text{Spin }}
be the set of irreducible representations
V
of
\widetilde{K}
such that
S_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes V
descends to a representation of
K
. Let
R_{\text{spin }}(K)
be the free abelian group generated by
\widehat{K}_{\text{spin }}
. In particular, if
G/K
is spin, then
\widehat{K}_{\text{spin }}=\widehat{K}
.

2.2.
Root system and weight lattices

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be the Lie algebra of the compact Cartan subgroup
T
of
G
, and define the root system decomposition:

(2.6)

\displaystyle R(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=R(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\sqcup R(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}).

We denote by
W_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
W_{\mathfrak{k}}
the associated Weyl group respectively. Fixing a choice of positive root system, we write:

(2.7)

\displaystyle R^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})=R^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\sqcup R^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}),

and define the standard half-sums of positive roots:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})}\alpha,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})}\alpha,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t})}\alpha.

The weight lattice in
i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is given by:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}=\left\{\mu\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}\Bigg|\frac{2\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z},\forall\alpha\in R(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\right\}.

We further define:

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}=\left\{\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\Bigg|\langle\alpha,\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle>0,\forall\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\right\},

and

(2.11)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathfrak{k}}^{*,\text{spin}}=\left\{\mu\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}\Bigg|\frac{2\langle\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{p}},\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z},\forall\alpha\in R(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}),\text{and }\langle\alpha,\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle>0,\forall\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t})\right\}.

By the Borel-Weil theorem
[
FH91
]
, there exist one-to-one correspondences:

(2.12)

\displaystyle\widehat{K}\longleftrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathfrak{k}}^{*},\hskip 28.45274pt\widehat{K}_{\text{spin }}\longleftrightarrow\Lambda_{\mathfrak{k}}^{*,\text{spin}}.

Moreover, we define the set of regular weights:

(2.13)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\mathfrak{k}}^{*,\text{reg}}=\left\{\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\Big|\langle\alpha,\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle>0,\forall\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t})\right\}\subset\Lambda_{\mathfrak{k}}^{*}.

2.3.
Iwasawa decomposition

Choose a subspace
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathfrak{a}
is maximal abelian in
\mathfrak{p}
, and let

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{a}\oplus\mathfrak{n}

be the Iwasawa decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
. In this paper, we assume that the Lie group
G
has real rank one, meaning that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\dim(\mathfrak{a})=1.

Denote by
A
and
N
the subgroups of
G
corresponding to
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{n}
, respectively.

The set of roots for the pair
\left(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a}\right)
is denoted by
R(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
, and we define
R^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
as the subset of positive roots relative to a fixed ordering. In this setting,

(2.16)

\displaystyle R^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})=\{\lambda,2\lambda\},\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho_{\mathfrak{a}}=\frac{1}{2}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\cdot\lambda+\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2\lambda}\cdot\lambda.

Denote the corresponding root spaces as follows:

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda},\quad\mathfrak{n}_{2}=\mathfrak{g}_{2\lambda}.

We shall also use the notation:

•

\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{2}
;

•

N_{1}=\exp(\mathfrak{n}_{1}),\quad N_{2}=\exp(\mathfrak{n}_{2}),\quad N=\exp(\mathfrak{n})
.

In particular,
N_{2}
forms the abelian part of the nilpotent group
N
.

Define
M
(resp.
M^{\prime}
) as the centralizer (resp. normalizer) of
A
in
K
. Then, we set

(2.18)

\displaystyle P=MAN,\quad W_{A}=M^{\prime}/M\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Next, consider the set of cuspidal parabolic subgroups of G up to
\Gamma
-conjugacy:

(2.19)

\displaystyle E(G,\Gamma)=\left\{\Gamma\text{-conjugacy classes of }\Gamma\text{-cuspidal parabolic subgroups of }G\right\}.

There are only finitely many elements in
E(G,\Gamma)
. Moreover, for any two parabolic subgroups
P_{1},P_{2}\in E(G,\Gamma)
with Langlands decompositions

(2.20)

\displaystyle P_{1}=M_{1}A_{1}N_{1},\quad P_{2}=M_{2}A_{2}N_{2},

there exists
k\in K
such that

(2.21)

\displaystyle P_{1}=kP_{2}k^{-1},\quad M_{1}=kM_{2}k^{-1},\quad A_{1}=kA_{2}k^{-1},\quad N_{1}=kN_{2}k^{-1}.

2.4.
Parabolic induction and intertwining operators

Let
G
be a semisimple Lie group of real rank one with parabolic subgroup
P=MAN
. The
principal series representations
of
G
are parametrized by pairs

(2.22)

\displaystyle(\sigma,\nu)\in\widehat{M}\times i\mathfrak{a}^{*},

where

•

\sigma
is an
irreducible unitary representation
of
M
, and

•

\nu
is an element of the
dual space
of the Lie algebra of
A
.

Let
H_{\sigma}
be the Hilbert space on which the representation
\sigma
of
M
acts. Since
M
is
compact
by assumption,
H_{\sigma}
is
finite-dimensional
. For such a pair
(\sigma,\nu)
, the induced representation
\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma,\nu)
is defined as the Hilbert space of all measurable functions
u:G\rightarrow H_{\sigma}
satisfying

(2.23)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(x\cdot man)&=e^{-(\rho_{\mathfrak{a}}+\nu)(a)}\sigma(m)^{-1}u(x),\quad\text{for all }m\in M,a\in A,n\in N,x\in G,\\
\|u\|^{2}&=\int_{K}\|u(k)\|^{2}dk<\infty.\end{aligned}

The principal series representation
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}
acts on
\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma,\nu)
via:

(2.24)

\displaystyle(\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(x)u)(y)=u(x^{-1}y),\quad u\in\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma,\nu),\quad x,y\in G.

Since each
u\in\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma,\nu)
is uniquely determined by its restriction to
K
, the space
\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma,\nu)
can be identified with the Hilbert space of all measurable functions
v:K\to H_{\sigma}
satisfying:

(2.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} v(km)&=\sigma(m)^{-1}v(k),\quad\text{for all }m\in M,k\in K,\\
\|v\|^{2}&=\int_{K}\|v(k)\|^{2}dk<\infty.\end{aligned}

Thus, for a fixed
\sigma\in\widehat{M}
, all the representations
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}
act on the same Hilbert space. Moreover, they have the same restriction to
K
, which we denote by
\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma)
.

Let
L=MA
be a Levi subgroup of
G
. In fact,
L
is a Levi component of two distinct parabolic subgroups:
P=MAN
and its opposite
\overline{P}=MA\overline{N}
, with unipotent radicals
N
and
\overline{N}
. We briefly review the theory of Knapp-Stein intertwining operators
[
Kna86
, Chapter VII]
. Since
\dim(\mathfrak{a})=1
, the Weyl group
W_{A}=M^{\prime}/M
has order two. Let
w\in W_{A}
be the nontrivial element. Clearly,
w\nu=-\nu
for all
\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. It was pointed out in
[
War79
, P. 13]
that for all elements in
\widehat{M}
are
ramified
, that is

(2.26)

\displaystyle w\sigma=\sigma,\quad\sigma\in\widehat{M}.

The
intertwining operators
associated with
w
are defined by

(2.27)

I_{P}(\sigma,\nu):H_{\sigma,\nu}\to H_{\sigma,-\nu},

where

(2.28)

\displaystyle(I_{P}(\sigma,\nu)u)(x)=\int_{\overline{N}}u(x\overline{n})\,d\overline{n}.

These operators satisfy the identity:

(2.29)

\displaystyle I_{P}(\sigma,\nu)\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(x)=\pi_{\sigma,-\nu}(x)I_{P}(\sigma,\nu).

The operator
I_{P}(\sigma,\nu)
admits a meromorphic continuation to
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}_{\mathbb{C}}
. If
\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and
\nu\neq 0
, then
\pi_{\sigma,\nu}
is irreducible, and
I_{P}(\sigma,\nu)
is invertible. The Plancherel density
\mu(\sigma,\nu)
is defined by

(2.30)

\displaystyle\mu(\sigma,\nu)I_{P}(\sigma,\nu)I_{P}(\sigma,-\nu)=\mathrm{Id}.

It follows from
[
Kna86
, Theorem 14.16, Corollary 14.30]
that
\pi_{\sigma,0}
is irreducible if and only if
\sigma
is
W_{A}
-stable and
\mu(\sigma,0)=0
. Otherwise, if
\mu(\sigma,0)\neq 0
, the parabolically induced representation decomposes into two
limits of discrete series representations
:

(2.31)

\displaystyle\pi_{\sigma,0}\cong\pi_{\sigma}^{+}\oplus\pi_{\sigma}^{-}.

The
normalized intertwining operator
is given by

(2.32)

U_{P}(\sigma,\nu):=r^{-1}(\sigma,\nu)\cdot I_{P}(\sigma,\nu),

where
r(\sigma,\nu)
is an analytic function satisfying

(2.33)

\displaystyle U_{P}(\sigma,\nu)\circ U_{P}(\sigma,-\nu)=\operatorname{id}.

In particular,
U_{P}(\sigma,\nu)
is unitary for all
\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}^{*}
.

Consider the decomposition of the right regular representation
R_{M}^{\Gamma}
of
M
on
L^{2}(Z_{\Gamma}\backslash M)
. We have:

(2.34)

\displaystyle L^{2}\left(Z_{\Gamma}\backslash M\right)\cong\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widehat{M}\\
\sigma\mid_{Z_{\Gamma}}=\mathrm{id}\end{subarray}}^{\oplus}m_{\Gamma}(\sigma)\cdot H_{\sigma},\quad m_{\Gamma}(\sigma)\in\mathbb{Z},

and

(2.35)

\displaystyle R_{M}^{\Gamma}\cong\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widehat{M}\\
\sigma\mid_{Z_{\Gamma}}=\mathrm{id}\end{subarray}}^{\oplus}m_{\Gamma}(\sigma)\cdot\sigma.

The definitions of parabolically induced representations extend naturally to each
P\in E(G,\Gamma)
with appropriate modifications in notation.

For any
\sigma\in\widehat{M}
and
\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we define

(2.36)

\operatorname{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{G}\left(\sigma,\nu\right)=\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}m_{\Gamma}(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^{G}\left(\sigma,\nu\right),

with

(2.37)

\displaystyle\pi^{\Gamma}_{\sigma,\nu}=\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}m_{\Gamma}(\sigma)\cdot\pi_{\sigma,\nu}.

By the preceding discussion, the non-trivial element
w\in W_{A}
induces unitary intertwining operators:

(2.38)

\displaystyle U^{\Gamma}(\sigma,\nu):=\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}U_{P}(\sigma,\nu):\operatorname{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{G}\left(\sigma,\nu\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{G}\left(\sigma,-\nu\right),

which satisfy the functional equation:

(2.39)

\displaystyle U^{\Gamma}(\sigma,\nu)\circ U^{\Gamma}(\sigma,-\nu)=\operatorname{id},\quad\text{for all }(\sigma,\nu)\in\widehat{M}\times i\mathfrak{a}^{*}.

In what follows, we often identify
\mathfrak{a}^{*}
with
\mathbb{R}
via the isomorphism

(2.40)

\displaystyle\nu\in\mathbb{R}\longmapsto\left(\frac{\nu}{\|\lambda\|}\right)\lambda.

Abusing notation, we will use the same letter for both the functional on
\mathfrak{a}
and the corresponding real number.

2.5.
Spectral decomposition of regular representation

Let
\Gamma
be an arithmetic subgroup of
G
and

(2.41)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash G)<\infty.

Denote by
R^{\Gamma}
the right regular representation of G on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
. For any
f\in C_{c}(G)
, we define:

(2.42)

\displaystyle R^{\Gamma}(f)=\int_{G}f(x)\cdot R^{\Gamma}(x)\;d_{G}x.

The right regular representation
R^{\Gamma}
of
G
on
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
decomposes into a discrete and a continuous part:

(2.43)

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)=L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\oplus L_{c}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G),\quad R^{\Gamma}=R_{d}^{\Gamma}\oplus R_{c}^{\Gamma}.

The discrete spectrum
R_{d}^{\Gamma}
acts on
L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, decomposing as a direct sum of irreducible representations, each appearing with finite multiplicity:

(2.44)

L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cong\bigoplus_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\cdot H_{\pi},\quad m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\in\mathbb{Z},

where
m_{\Gamma}(\pi)
denotes the multiplicity of
\pi
. On the other hand, the continuous spectrum
R_{c}^{\Gamma}
acts on
L_{c}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, forming a direct integral of principal series representations, without irreducible subrepresentations. To be more precise, we have the following spectral decomposition
[
War79
, Section 3]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

We have the orthogonal decomposition:

(2.45)

\displaystyle L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cong L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\oplus L_{c}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G),

with

(2.46)

\displaystyle L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\cdot H_{\pi},

(2.47)

\displaystyle L_{c}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widehat{M}\\
\sigma|_{Z_{\Gamma}}=\mathrm{id}\end{subarray}}^{\oplus}\left[\int_{i\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\operatorname{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{G}(\sigma,\nu)\,d\nu\right]^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

Here
Z_{\Gamma}
is the center of
\Gamma
and
\operatorname{Ind}_{\Gamma}^{G}(\sigma,\nu)
are parabolic induced representation of

2.6.
Harish-Chandra
L^{p}
-Schwartz Spaces

We now recall the definition of the Harish-Chandra
L^{p}
-Schwartz spaces
\mathcal{C}^{p}(G)
for
p>0
. This definition involves two spherical functions on
G
, namely
\sigma
and
\Theta
.

For
x\in G
, the function
\sigma(x)
is defined as the
geodesic distance
between the cosets
K
and
xK
in the symmetric space
X=G/K
. To introduce the function
\Theta
, let
\pi_{0}
be the unitary representation of
G
induced by the trivial representation of a minimal parabolic subgroup. The trivial representation of
K
occurs exactly once in the restriction of
\pi_{0}
to
K
, and we denote by
\xi
a unit vector in
\mathscr{H}(\pi_{0})
that is fixed under
\pi_{0}|_{K}
. Then the function
\Theta
is given by the corresponding matrix coefficient:

(2.48)

\displaystyle\Theta(x)=\left\langle\pi_{0}(x)\xi,\xi\right\rangle,\quad x\in G.

For
p>0
, the space
\mathcal{C}^{p}(G)
consists of all functions
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
such that for all
m\geq 0
and for all
X^{\alpha},Y^{\beta}\in\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g})
,

(2.49)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in G}(1+\sigma(x))^{m}\Theta(x)^{-2/p}\left|L(X^{\alpha})R(Y^{\beta})f(x)\right|<\infty,

where
L
and
R
denote the left and right derivatives. In this paper, we sometime denote by
\mathcal{C}(G)
for

.

3.
Elliptic Operators on Symmetric Spaces

We recall the standard procedure for constructing invariant differential operators on a homogeneous space.

3.1.
Index of Dirac operators

Let
V_{\mu}\in\widehat{K}_{\text{spin}}
with highest weight
\mu\in\Lambda^{*,\text{spin}}_{\mathfrak{k}}
. Define

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{\mu}^{\pm}=V_{\mu}\otimes S_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\pm},\quad E_{\mu}=E_{\mu}^{+}\oplus E_{\mu}^{-}.

These define homogeneous vector bundles over
X
:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{\pm}_{\mu}:=G\times_{K}E_{\mu}^{\pm}\to X,\quad\mathscr{E}_{\mu}:=G\times_{K}E_{\mu}\to X.

Over the locally symmetric space
X_{\Gamma}
, we set

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\pm}_{\Gamma,\mu}:=\Gamma\backslash G\times_{K}E_{\mu}^{\pm}\to X_{\Gamma},\quad\mathscr{E}_{\Gamma,\mu}:=\Gamma\backslash G\times_{K}E_{\mu}\to X_{\Gamma}.

As usual, we identify the space of smooth sections
C^{\infty}(\mathscr{E}_{\mu})
with the
K
-invariant part

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left(C^{\infty}(G)\otimes E_{\mu}\right)^{K}

Similarly, the space of square-integrable sections
L^{2}(\mathscr{E}_{\mu})
is identified with

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left(L^{2}(G)\otimes E_{\mu}\right)^{K}.

Let
\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathscr{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
the center of
\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Let
\left\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{\dim\mathfrak{p}}\right\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{p}
, and define the Dirac operator:

(3.6)

D_{\mu}^{\pm}=\sum_{i=1}^{\dim\mathfrak{p}}X_{i}\otimes c(X_{i})\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{V_{\mu}}\in\mathscr{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(E_{\mu}^{\pm},E_{\mu}^{\mp}),

where
c(X)
denotes Clifford multiplication by
X\in\mathfrak{p}
. These operators are
K
-invariant and hence define two
G
-invariant first-order differential operators:

(3.7)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{\pm}:C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathscr{E}_{\mu}^{\pm})\to C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathscr{E}_{\mu}^{\mp}),

called the
Dirac operators
with coefficients in
V_{\mu}
. They are elliptic, adjoint to each other, and their associated spinor Laplacians, as shown by Parthasarathy
[
Par72
]
, satisfy the relation:

(3.8)

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{\mp}D_{\mu}^{\pm}=-R(\Omega)\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{E_{\mu}^{\pm}}+\left(\|\mu+\rho_{c}\|^{2}-\|\rho\|^{2}\right)\operatorname{Id}.

Here,
\Omega\in\mathscr{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
is the
Casimir element
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\|\cdot\|
refers to the Cartan-Killing norm.

Now let
\pi
be a unitary representation of
G
on a Hilbert space
H_{\pi}
, and let
H^{\infty}_{\pi}
be the subspace of all
C^{\infty}
-vectors in
H_{\pi}
. With
D_{\mu}
given by (
3.6
), we define the operator
\pi(D_{\mu})
on
\left(H_{\pi}\otimes E_{\mu}\right)^{K}
with (dense) domain
\left(H^{\infty}_{\pi}\otimes E_{\mu}\right)^{K}
by:

(3.9)

\displaystyle\pi(D_{\mu})=\sum_{i=1}^{\dim\mathfrak{p}}\pi(X_{i})\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{V_{\mu}}.

For the right regular representation
R^{\Gamma}=R^{\Gamma}_{d}\oplus R^{\Gamma}_{c}
on
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, we define the associated Dirac operators:

(3.10)

D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}=R^{\Gamma}(D_{\mu}),\quad D^{\Gamma}_{\mu,d}=R_{d}^{\Gamma}(D_{\mu}),\quad D^{\Gamma}_{\mu,c}=R_{c}^{\Gamma}(D_{\mu}).

These define the
Dirac operator
on the locally symmetric space:

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left[L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\otimes E_{\mu}\right]^{K}\cong L^{2}(X_{\Gamma},\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,\mu}).

According to
[
Mos82
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have:

(3.12)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}=\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma}_{\mu,d},\quad\text{and}\quad\dim\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}<\infty.

In particular, the
index
of
D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}
is well-defined:

(3.13)

\displaystyle\operatorname{index}D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}=\dim\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma,+}_{\mu}-\dim\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma,-}_{\mu}.

Theorem 3.1
.

The following formula holds:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\operatorname{index}D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{G},\chi_{\pi}=\chi_{\mu+\rho_{c}}}m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\left(\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(H_{\pi},E_{\mu}^{+}\right)-\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(H_{\pi},E_{\mu}^{-}\right)\right),

where
\chi_{\pi}:\mathscr{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}
is the
infinitesimal character
of
\pi\in\widehat{G}
, and
\chi_{\lambda}
denotes the character of
\mathscr{Z}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}})
associated with
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
.

Proof.

[
BM83
, (1.3.8)]
∎

3.2.
Heat semigroups and spinor laplacians

We now turn our attention to the heat semigroups associated with the spinor Laplacians

(3.15)

\displaystyle\Delta^{\pm}_{\mu}=D_{\mu}^{\pm}D_{\mu}^{\mp}

acting on

(3.16)

\displaystyle h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}=e^{-t\Delta_{\mu}^{\pm}}:L^{2}(\mathscr{E}_{\mu}^{\pm})\to L^{2}(\mathscr{E}_{\mu}^{\pm})

is a smoothing pseudo-differential operator that commutes with the representation of

on

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left(h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}u\right)(x)=\int_{G}h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\left(x^{-1}y\right)u(y)\,dy,\quad u\in\left(L^{2}(G)\otimes E_{\mu}^{\pm}\right)^{K},\quad x\in G,

where

(3.18)

\displaystyle h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}(x)=k\circ h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\left(k^{-1}xk^{\prime}\right)\circ k^{\prime-1},\quad x\in G,\quad k,k^{\prime}\in K.

Theorem 3.2
.

For any irreducible unitary representation
\pi
of
G
, one has

(3.19)

\displaystyle\pi\left(h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\right)=Q_{\pi}^{\pm}\circ e^{t\left(\pi(\Omega)-\|\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\|^{2}+\|\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}\|^{2}\right)}\circ Q_{\pi}^{\pm},

where

denotes the projection of
H_{\pi}\otimes E_{\mu}^{\pm}
onto its finite-dimensional

-invariant part
\left(H_{\pi}\otimes E_{\mu}^{\pm}\right)^{K}
.

Proof.

[
BM83
, Corollary 2.2]
.
∎

\displaystyle h_{t,\mu}^{\pm}\in\left(\mathscr{C}^{p}(G)\otimes\operatorname{End}E_{\mu}^{\pm}\right)^{K\times K}.

4.
Hecke operators and Lefschetz numbers

4.1.
Hecke operators

Let
\Gamma
be an arithmetic subgroup of
G
as before. The commensurator is defined as:

(4.1)

\displaystyle C_{G}(\Gamma):=\{g\in G:\Gamma\cap g\Gamma g^{-1}\text{ has finite index in both }\Gamma\text{ and }g\Gamma g^{-1}\}.

Fix an element
\alpha\in C_{G}(\Gamma)
. The double coset
\Gamma\alpha\Gamma
decomposes as a disjoint union of
\Gamma
-cosets. More precisely, there exist elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N}\in G
such that

(4.2)

\Xi:=\Gamma\alpha\Gamma=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\Gamma,\quad\text{ (disjoint union)}.

Equivalently, we have

(4.3)

\Gamma=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\alpha^{-1}\left(\Gamma\cap\alpha\Gamma\alpha^{-1}\right),

where
N=[\Gamma:\Gamma\cap\alpha\Gamma\alpha^{-1}]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
f\in C_{c}(\Gamma\backslash G)
. Considering
f
as a
\Gamma
-invariant function on
G
, we define the
Hecke operator
associated with the double coset
\Xi
by

(4.4)

\displaystyle T_{\Xi}(f)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f\left(\alpha_{i}^{-1}x\right),\quad x\in G,

where the elements
\alpha_{i}
are as in (
4.2
). One can verify that
T_{\Xi}(f)\in C_{c}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, and thus
T_{\Xi}
extends to a bounded operator:

(4.5)

\displaystyle T_{\Xi}:L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\to L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G).

In particular, if
\alpha\in\Gamma
, then
\Xi=\Gamma
and
T_{\Gamma}=\operatorname{id}
.

One can check from the definition that

(4.6)

\displaystyle T_{\Xi}\circ R^{\Gamma}(x)=R^{\Gamma}(x)\circ T_{\Xi},\quad x\in G.

Thus,
T_{\Xi}
defines an
intertwining operator
. We define:

(4.7)

\displaystyle T_{\Xi}^{\pm}=T_{\Xi}\otimes\operatorname{id}\Big|_{\left(L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\otimes E^{\pm}_{\mu}\right)^{K}},

which makes sense because
T_{\Xi}
commutes with
R_{\Gamma}
. Moreover,

(4.8)

\displaystyle D^{\Gamma,\pm}_{\mu}\circ T_{\Xi}^{\pm}=T_{\Xi}^{\mp}\circ D^{\Gamma,\mp}_{\mu},

so that
T_{\Xi}^{\pm}
preserves the kernel of
D^{\Gamma,\pm}_{\mu}
.

4.2.
Lefschetz numbers

Definition 4.2
.

The
Lefschetz number
of
T_{\Xi}
with respect to
D^{\Gamma,+}_{\mu}
is defined as:

(4.9)

L\left(T_{\Xi},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right):=\Tr\left(T^{+}_{\Xi}\Big|_{\operatorname{Ker}(D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,+})}-T^{-}_{\Xi}\Big|_{\operatorname{Ker}(D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,-})}\right)\in\mathbb{C}.

In particular,

(4.10)

\displaystyle L\left(T_{\Gamma},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right)=\operatorname{index}D_{\mu}^{\Gamma}.

We now establish some fundamental properties of these Lefschetz numbers. Given
\pi\in\widehat{G}
, let
L_{\pi}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
denote the corresponding isotypical component of
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
, and let
R^{\Gamma}_{\pi}
denote the restriction of
R^{\Gamma}
to
L_{\pi}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
. Then,

(4.11)

\displaystyle L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}^{\oplus}L_{\pi}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G),\quad R^{\Gamma}_{d}=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}^{\oplus}R^{\Gamma}_{\pi}.

Since
T_{\Xi}
preserves each isotypical component, we define

(4.12)

\displaystyle T_{\Xi,d}^{\pm}=T_{\Xi}^{\pm}\otimes I\Big|_{\left(L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\otimes E_{\mu}^{\pm}\right)^{K}},

\displaystyle T_{\Xi,\pi}^{\pm}=T_{\Xi}^{\pm}\otimes I\Big|_{\left(L_{\pi}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\otimes E^{\pm}_{\mu}\right)^{K}},

so that

(4.13)

\displaystyle T_{\Xi,d}^{\pm}=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}^{\oplus}T_{\Xi,\pi}^{\pm}.

Moreover,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma,\pm}_{\mu}=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}^{\oplus}\operatorname{Ker}D_{\mu,\pi}^{\Gamma,\pm},

where
D_{\mu,\pi}^{\Gamma,\pm}
is the restriction of
D_{\mu}^{\Gamma,\pm}
to
\left(L_{\pi}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\otimes E^{\pm}_{\mu}\right)^{K}
.
Since
\dim\operatorname{Ker}D^{\Gamma,\pm}_{\mu}<\infty
, it follows that

(4.15)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}D_{\mu,\pi}^{\Gamma,\pm}=0,\quad\text{for all but finitely many }\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}.

Thus, we obtain:

(4.16)

\displaystyle L\left(T_{\Xi},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{\Xi,\pi}^{+}\big|_{\operatorname{Ker}D_{\mu,\pi}^{\Gamma,+}}\right)-\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{\Xi,\pi}^{-}\big|_{\operatorname{Ker}D_{\mu,\pi}^{\Gamma,-}}\right)\right).

Theorem 4.3
.

The Lefschetz number can be computed using the heat kernel of the spinor Laplacian:

(4.17)

\displaystyle L\left(T_{\Xi},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right)=\Tr\left(T_{\Xi}\circ R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)\right).

for any
t>0
.

Proof.

[
Mos84
, (4.3)]
.
∎

5.
Classification of elements in
\Xi

In this section, we will classify elements in double coset
\Xi=\Gamma\alpha\Gamma
for some
\alpha\in C_{G}(\Gamma)
. See details in
[
Hof89
, Section 7]
or
[
OW81
, Section 5]
.

5.1.
Semisimple elements

To begin with, recall that a semi-simple element
\xi\in G
is classified as:

•

central if
\xi
belongs to the center
Z_{G}
of
G
.

•

elliptic if
\xi
is not central but conjugate to an element of
K
.

•

hyperbolic if
\xi
is neither central or elliptic.

We decompose the elements in the double coset
\Xi=\Gamma\alpha\Gamma
.
Define:

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Xi_{S}:=\{\xi\in\Xi\mid\forall P\in E(G,\Gamma),\,\{\xi\}_{\Gamma}\cap P=\emptyset\}.

All elements in
\Xi_{S}
are semisimple and the centralizer
\Gamma_{\xi}
is a uniform lattice in
G_{\xi}
. The remaining elements form the parabolic part:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}:=\Xi-\Xi_{S}.

Note that
\Xi_{P}
may very well contain semi-simple elements. We further refine:

(5.3)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}=\Xi_{P}(*)\sqcup\Xi_{P}(**),

where
\Xi_{P}(*)
consists of semisimple elements and
\Xi_{P}(**)
consists of non-semisimple elements.

On the other hand, we have a regular and singular decomposition of
\Xi_{P}
as follow

•

Regular elements:

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}(r):=\{\xi\in\Xi_{P}\mid N_{\xi}=\{1\},\text{ and }\xi\in P\}.

One can check that
\Gamma_{\xi}
fail to be a lattice in
G_{\xi}
for any
\xi\in\Xi_{P}(r)
.

•

Singular elements:

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}(s):=\Xi_{P}-\Xi_{P}(r).

By
[
Hof89
, Lemma 7.1]
, all regular elements in
\Xi_{P}(r)
are semisimple, i.e.,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}(r)\subseteq\Xi_{P}(*).

We decompose the singular part:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}(s)=\Xi_{P}(s,*)\sqcup\Xi_{P}(s,**).

where:

•

\Xi_{P}(s,*)
consists of semisimple singular elements. The semi-simple elements in
\Xi_{P}(s,*)
have the property that
\Gamma_{\xi}
is a non-uniform lattice in
G_{\xi}

•

\Xi_{P}(s,**)
consists of non-semisimple singular elements.

Finally, we have

(5.8)

\displaystyle\Xi=\begin{cases}\text{Semi-simple part}:&\begin{cases}\text{Uniform lattice in }G_{\xi}&\Xi_{S},\\
\text{Non-uniform lattice in }G_{\xi}&\Xi_{P}(s,*),\\
\text{Not a lattice in }G_{\xi}&\Xi_{P}(r).\end{cases}\\
&\\
\text{Non-semi-simple part}:&\Xi_{P}(s,**).\end{cases}

Furthermore, all semi-simple elements are decomposed as:

(5.9)

\displaystyle\Xi_{S}\sqcup\Xi_{P}(s,*)\sqcup\Xi_{P}(r)=\Xi_{C}\sqcup\Xi_{E}\sqcup\Xi_{H},

where:

•

\Xi_{C}
: central elements.

•

\Xi_{E}
: elliptic elements.

•

\Xi_{H}
: hyperbolic elements.

By
[
Hof89
, p. 382]
,
\Xi_{E}
and
\Xi_{C}
are finite sets.

5.2.
Non-semi-simple elements

For non-semi-simple elements

(5.10)

\displaystyle\xi\in\Xi_{P}(s,**),

we have the Jordan decomposition

(5.11)

\displaystyle\xi=\eta u.

where
\eta
is semi-simiple,
u
is unipotent, and they commute. Here the centralizer
N_{\eta}
of
\eta
in
N
is nontrivial. Since
\xi\in\Gamma\cap P
for some
P\in E(G,\Gamma)
, we know that
u\in N
and
L
can be chosen that
\eta\in L
. This Levi component containing
\eta
is determined up to conjugation by elements of
N_{\eta}
.

For the adjoint action of
A
on
\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}
, there is a root space decomposition:

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}_{\eta}=\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,1}+\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2},\quad\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,1}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{1},\quad\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{2}.

Note that
\mathfrak{n}_{2}
is abelian. A
Lie triple
is defined as a triple
(X,H,Y)\neq(0,0,0)
of elements in
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying the relations:

(5.13)

\displaystyle[H,X]=2X,\quad[H,Y]=-2Y,\quad[X,Y]=H.

For
\lambda\in\{1,2\}
, let
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta,\lambda}
be the set of elements
X\in\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,\lambda}=\mathfrak{g}_{\eta,\lambda}
such that there exists
Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{\eta,-\lambda}
forming a Lie triple:

(5.14)

(X,H,Y).

Defining their exponentiated versions:

(5.15)

\displaystyle\overset{\circ}{N}_{\eta,1}=\exp\left(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta,1}\right),\quad\overset{\circ}{N}_{\eta,2}=\exp\left(\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta,2}\right)

For such a Lie triple, set

(5.16)

\displaystyle Z_{0}:=X-Y\in\mathfrak{k}.

It has been shown in
[
Bar79
]
that conjugacy classes of unipotent elements are one-to-one correspondent to conjugacy classes of Lie triples, as well as conjugacy classes of
Z_{0}
. By
[
Hof89
, Lemma 9.1]
, the set of
\operatorname{Ad}(L_{\eta})
-orbits in
\overset{\circ}{\mathfrak{n}}_{\eta,\lambda}
are finite.

For
t>0
, define

(5.17)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=\eta z_{t},\quad z_{t}=\operatorname{exp}\left(tZ_{0}\right).

We can use the family of semi-simple elements
\xi_{t}
to approximate
\xi
.

Proposition 5.1
.

There is a real number
t_{0}
such that for any
t\leq t_{0}
, the centralizer
G_{\xi_{t}}=G_{\xi_{0}}
, the centralizer
G_{z_{t}}=G_{Z_{0}}
and
G_{\xi_{0}}\subseteq G_{\eta}
where the element

(5.18)

\xi_{0}=\eta\cdot\exp t_{0}Z_{0}.

Proof.

[
Bar76

5.3.
Epstein zeta function

Another key component of the geometric side of the trace formula is the appearance of certain zeta function constants associated with the discrete group
\Gamma
(or the double coset
\Xi
in the case of Hecke operators). Our restriction to the real rank one case leads to a significant simplification compared to the general setting considered in
[
Hof87
]
. In the terminology of
[
OW81
, Section 9]
, all elements in the real rank one case are “orbitally regular”. As a result, the only zeta function that contributes to the description of the unipotent term in the trace formula is the Epstein zeta function.

Let us briefly review the construction. For details, we refer to
[
Hof87
,
OW81
]
. For cuspidal subgroups
P
with Levi component
L=MA
, put

•

\Xi_{L}:=\text{ projection of }\Xi\cap P\text{ along }N
.

•

\Xi_{M}:=\text{ projection of }\Xi\cap P\text{ along }AN
. Since
M
is compact, we know that
\Xi_{M}
is a finite set.

The projection
\Xi_{L}\to\Xi_{M}
is finite to one. In particular,
\Gamma_{L}=\Gamma_{M}
. One should keep in mind that
\Xi_{L}
and
\Xi_{M}
depend on
P
, although this is not reflected in the notation.

Let
\eta\in\Xi_{L}
. If
\xi\in\Xi\cap\eta N
with Jordan decomposition
\xi=su
, then
s\in\{\eta\}_{N}
and
u\in N_{s}
, thus
\xi\in sN_{s}
. Introduce

(5.19)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{\eta}=\left\{s\in\{\eta\}_{N}:s\text{ is the semi-simple component of some }\xi\in\Xi\cap\eta N\right\}.

Denote by
n_{s}
the element in
N
with
n_{s}sn_{s}^{-1}=\eta
. Put

(5.20)

7.
Application to multiplicities of discrete series

A fundamental problem is the decomposition of the regular representation of
G
on
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
into irreducible representations. In particular, we aim to compute the multiplicities
m_{\Gamma}(\pi)
in the following decomposition:

(7.1)

\displaystyle L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cong\bigoplus_{\pi\in\widehat{\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)}_{d}}m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\cdot H_{\pi},\quad m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\in\mathbb{Z}.

The computation of
m_{\Gamma}(\pi)
has a long history. If
\Gamma\backslash G
is compact and
\pi
belongs to the integrable discrete series representations, Langlands
[
Lan66
]
obtained a closed finite formula for
m_{\Gamma}(\pi)
. This result for the compact case was further generalized by Hott and Parthasarathy in
[
HP74
]
.

If
\Gamma\backslash G
is noncompact, the problem becomes considerably more complicated due to the existence of a continuous spectrum. Moreover, the presence of unipotent elements in
\Gamma
necessitates the consideration of additional distributions, some of which are invariant. Using Selberg’s trace formula, Osborne and Warner
[
OW78
]
derived a multiplicity formula for integral discrete series representations. Later, Barbasch and Moscovici
[
BM83
]
extended Osborne-Warner’s result to all discrete series representations with sufficiently large Harish-Chandra parameters.

To be more precise, by Theorem
3.1
, we have

(7.2)

\displaystyle L\left(T_{\Gamma},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_{d}(\mu)}m_{\Gamma}(\pi)\cdot m(\pi,\mu),

where

(7.3)

\displaystyle m(\pi,\mu)=\left(\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(H_{\pi},E_{\mu}^{+}\right)-\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(H_{\pi},E_{\mu}^{-}\right)\right),

and
\Pi_{d}(\mu)
denotes the packet consisting of the set of irreducible representations with the same infinitesimal character as
\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

In
[
BSB83
]
, Baldoni Silva and Barbasch computed the number
m(\pi,\mu)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
\pi\in\widehat{G}
and
\mu\in\Lambda^{*}_{\mathfrak{k}}
. In particular, if
\mu
is sufficiently regular, meaning that

(7.4)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}-\rho_{\mathfrak{g}},\alpha\right\rangle>0,\quad\alpha\in R^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}),

then the only class in
\Pi_{d}(\mu)
is the discrete series representation
\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
, and
m(\pi,\mu)=1
. Consequently, the formula for
L\left(T_{\Gamma},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right)
provides a formula for the multiplicity of the discrete series representation with sufficiently regular Harish-Chandra parameters.

The following corollary generalizes
[
BM83
, Corollary 7.2]
and the formula in
[
OW78
]
.

Corollary 7.1
.

Suppose
G
is a semisimple Lie group of real rank one and
\Gamma
is an arithmetic, torsion-free subgroup of
G
such that
\Gamma\backslash G
has finite volume. For any sufficiently regular
\mu
, the multiplicity of the discrete series
\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
in
L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
is the sum of

(7.5)

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash G)\cdot d(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}\quad\sum_{\{\eta\}_{\Gamma_{M}}\subset\Xi_{L}(s)}(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\delta(\eta)\cdot\left(c^{+}_{\eta}\cdot C_{\eta}^{+}+c^{-}_{\eta}\cdot C_{\eta}^{-}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{w\in W_{\mathfrak{k}}}\left(\left[\overline{\left\langle w(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}),Z_{0}\right\rangle}\right]^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,1}}{2}}\cdot\prod_{\beta\in R^{+}(\xi_{0})}\left\langle w(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}),\beta\right\rangle\cdot e^{w(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}})}\left(\eta\right)\right),

and

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}+1}}{2}\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}\quad\sum_{\{\eta\}_{\Gamma_{M}}\subset\Xi_{L}}\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{\eta}/\left(\Gamma_{M}\right)_{\eta}\right)\cdot\left|\operatorname{det}\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\eta)\right|^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\times\#\left[\Xi\cap\eta N\colon\Gamma\cap N\right]\cdot\Omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}\left(\eta\right).

If
\mu
is not sufficiently regular, then the packet
\Pi_{d}(\mu)
may contain more than one irreducible representation, some of which may be non-tempered. For example, let
\Gamma
be a torsion-free, discrete subgroup of
G=SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. Let

(7.6)

\displaystyle D_{k}^{+},\quad k=1,2,\ldots

be the holomorphic discrete series representations of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. Then, we have

(7.7)

\displaystyle m_{\Gamma}\left(D_{k}^{+}\right)=L\left(T_{\Gamma},D^{\Gamma}_{k}\right),\quad k\geq 2

and

(7.8)

m_{\Gamma}\left(D_{1}^{+}\right)-m_{\Gamma}\left(\text{trivial representation}\right)=L\left(T_{\Gamma},D^{\Gamma}_{1}\right).

This example illustrates that the pattern for
k=1
differs from that for
k\geq 2
, since
D_{1}^{+}
and the trivial representation have the same Harish-Chandra parameter.

On the spectral side, the Lefschetz number is given by

(7.9)

\displaystyle L\left(T_{\Xi},D^{\Gamma}_{\mu}\right)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu)}\Tr\left(T_{\Xi,\pi}\right)\cdot m_{\Gamma}(\pi).

By selecting a sufficiently rich collection of Hecke operators
T_{\Xi}
, it may be possible to separate the contributions of
\pi\in\Pi(\mu)
and precisely determine the multiplicity of a single
m_{\Gamma}(\pi)
.

8.
Selberg’s principle and invariant tempered distribution

In this section, we review the Selberg’s principle and examine several invariant tempered distributions that arise in the trace formula.

8.1.
Selberg’s principle

Suppose that
G
is a semi-simple Lie group of real rank one. We assume that
G
has a compact Cartan subgroup
T
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
. We denote by
\Lambda^{*}_{\mathfrak{t}}
the weight lattice in
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
as before.

A tempered distribution
\mathscr{F}
on
G
is said to be
invariant
if it satisfies

(8.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left(f^{y}\right)=\mathscr{F}\left(f\right),\quad\text{where }f^{y}(x)=f(yxy^{-1}),\quad x,y\in G.

Lemma 8.1
.

If
\mathscr{F}
is an invariant tempered distribution on
G
, then it

(8.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)

is independent of
t
.

Proof.

It has been proved in
[
GHW
, Lemma 5.4]
that
\mathscr{F}
defines a continuous trace on
\mathcal{C}(G)
. Since
\mathcal{C}(G)
is a dense subalgebra in
C^{*}_{r}G
and is closed under holomorphic calculus
[
Laf02
]
, we obtain the isomorphism

(8.3)

\displaystyle K_{*}\left(\mathcal{C}(G)\right)\cong K_{*}\left(C_{r}^{*}G\right).

This proves the lemma.
∎

When the Lie group
G
has real rank one, Arthur
[
Art70
]
proved that the Fourier transform induces a topological isomorphism from
\mathscr{C}(G)
onto
\mathscr{C}(\widehat{G}_{\text{temp}})
. Consequently, the Fourier transform of a tempered distribution can be interpreted as a continuous linear functional on
\mathscr{C}(\widehat{G}_{\text{temp}})
.
If
\mathscr{F}
is an invariant tempered distribution on
G
, its Fourier expansion has the form:

(8.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(f)=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{*}_{\mathfrak{t}}}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}(\lambda)\cdot\Theta_{\lambda}(f)}_{\text{discrete part}}+\underbrace{\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}}\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Theta_{\sigma,\nu}(f)\cdot\widehat{\mathscr{F}}(\sigma,\nu)\;d\nu}_{\text{continuous part}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{(d)}(f)+\mathscr{F}^{(c)}(f),\quad f\in\mathcal{C}(G).

The Selberg principle is a vanishing result that plays a key role in our computations within the trace formula.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
h_{\mu,t}
be the function defined in (
3.21
). For any
\sigma\in\widehat{M}
and
\nu\in i\mathfrak{a}^{*}
, we have:

(8.5)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\sigma,\nu}\left(h_{\mu,t}\right)=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,

(8.6)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\sigma,\nu}\left(h^{\pm}_{\mu,t}\right)=\dim\left(E_{\mu}^{\pm}\right)\cdot e^{t\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|^{2}-\left\|\nu\right\|^{2}-\left\|\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\right\|^{2}+\|\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}\|^{2}\right)}

Since
\dim(S^{+}_{\mathfrak{p}})=\dim(S^{-}_{\mathfrak{p}})
, the lemma follows immediately.
∎

Theorem 8.3

(Selberg’s Principle)
.

Let
\mathscr{F}
be an invariant, tempered distribution on
G
. In terms of the Fourier expansion in (
8.4
), we have:

(8.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\widehat{\mathscr{F}}(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}).

Proof.

By (
8.4
) and Lemma
8.2
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{*}^{(c)}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}}\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Theta_{\sigma,\nu}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)\cdot\widehat{\mathscr{F}}(\sigma,\nu)\;d\nu=0.

Thus,

(8.8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{*}\left(\ind\left(D_{\mu}\right)\right)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{*}_{\mathfrak{t}}}\widehat{\mathscr{F}}(\lambda)\cdot\Theta_{\lambda}(f).

On the other hand, by
[
BM83
, (3.5)]
,

(8.9)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\pi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(H_{\pi},E_{\mu}^{+}\right)-\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(H_{\pi},E_{\mu}^{-}\right)&\text{if }\chi_{\pi}=\chi_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}},\\
&\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus, we conclude:

(8.10)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=\widehat{\mathscr{F}}(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}})\cdot\left(\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}},E_{\mu}^{+}\right)-\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}},E_{\mu}^{-}\right)\right),

where
\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
is the discrete series representation with Harish-Chandra parameter
\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}
. Finally, from
[
AS77
, Section 4]
, we have:

(8.11)

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}},E_{\mu}^{+}\right)-\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Hom}_{K}\left(\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}},E_{\mu}^{-}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

8.2.
Discrete series character

Let
T
be a compact Cartan subgroup of
G
and
H
be a non-compact Cartan subgroup. Let
\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
denote the discrete series representation with Harish-Chandra parameter
\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}
. For each
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G)
, the operator

(8.12)

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(f)=\int_{G}f(g)\cdot\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(g)\,dg

is a well-defined bounded linear operator. Moreover,
\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(f)
is of trace class, and the map

(8.13)

\displaystyle f\mapsto\Tr\left(\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(f)\right)

defines a distribution on
G
. We denote this distribution by
\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
and refer to it as the character of
\omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
. According to Harish-Chandra’s fundamental results, we have the following properties:

•

\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
is an invariant tempered distribution on
G
.

•

\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
is a locally
L^{1}
function on
G
and is real-analytic on the set of regular semisimple elements of
G
, particularly on
T^{\text{reg}}
and
H^{\text{reg}}
.

•

For any
t\in T^{\text{reg}}
, the character is given by:

(8.14)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(t)=\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{\mathfrak{k}}}\det(w)\cdot e^{w\left(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\right)}(t)}{\Delta_{T}(t)},

where

(8.15)

\displaystyle\Delta_{T}(t)=\operatorname{Det}_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}}^{1/2}\left(\operatorname{Id}-\operatorname{Ad}_{t}\right).

•

On
H^{\text{reg}}
, the character
\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
is determined as follows. Define:

\displaystyle A^{+}

\displaystyle=\left\{a\in A\mid e^{\lambda}(a)>1,\quad\lambda\text{ real positive root}\right\},

\displaystyle A^{-}

\displaystyle=\left\{a\in A\mid e^{\lambda}(a)<1,\quad\lambda\text{ real positive root}\right\}.

Split
H=H_{K}A
with
H_{K}=H\cap K
, and define:

(8.16)

\displaystyle H^{+}=\left(H_{K}\cdot A^{+}\right)\cap H^{\text{reg}},\quad H^{-}=\left(H_{K}\cdot A^{-}\right)\cap H^{\text{reg}}.

For any
\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, define the sign function:

(8.17)

\displaystyle c(\mu,H^{+})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if }\mu\left(\sqrt{-1}\left(E_{\lambda}-E_{-\lambda}\right)\right)>0,\\
+1&\text{if }\mu\left(\sqrt{-1}\left(E_{\lambda}-E_{-\lambda}\right)\right)<0,\\
0&\text{if }\mu\left(\sqrt{-1}\left(E_{\lambda}-E_{-\lambda}\right)\right)=0,\end{cases}

and define
c(\mu,H^{-})=-c(\mu,H^{+})
,
where
E_{\pm\lambda}
are the normalized vectors in
\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\lambda}
. Let
\mathbf{c}\colon H\to T
denote the Cayley transform. Then, for any
h\in H^{\text{reg}}
, we have:

(8.18)

\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(h)=\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{\mathfrak{g}}}\operatorname{det}(w)\cdot c\left(w\mu:J^{\pm}\right)\cdot e^{w\mu}(\mathbf{c}(h))}{\Delta_{H}(h)},

where the sign
\pm
is chosen based on whether
h\in H^{+}
or
H^{-}
, and

(8.19)

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}(h)=\operatorname{Det}_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}}^{1/2}\left(\operatorname{Id}-\operatorname{Ad}_{h}\right).

Remark 8.4
.

From the above result, we see that the function

(8.20)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(h)

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{H}(h)\cdot\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(h)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W_{\mathfrak{g}}}\operatorname{det}(w)\cdot c\left(w\mu:J^{\pm}\right)\cdot e^{w\mu}(\mathbf{c}(h))

extends to a smooth function on
H
.

8.3.
Semi-simple orbital integral

Let
\xi\in G
, and let
Z_{\xi}\subseteq G
be its centralizer. Let
d(gZ_{\xi})
be the left-invariant measure on
G/Z_{\xi}
determined by a Haar measure
d_{G}
on
G
.

Definition 8.5
.

The
orbital integral
of a measurable function
f
on
G
with respect to
\xi
is given by

(8.21)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}(f):=\int_{G/Z_{\xi}}f\left(g\xi g^{-1}\right)d\left(gZ_{\xi}\right),

provided the integral converges.

Harish-Chandra proved that the integral converges for
f
in the Harish-Chandra Schwartz algebra
\mathcal{C}(G)

[
HC66
, Theorem 6]
. Furthermore, results from
[
RR72
]
show that
\tau_{\xi}
defines an invariant tempered distribution on
G
and is also a measure on
G
.

A central problem in representation theory is obtaining Fourier inversion formulas in the sense of Harish-Chandra for the distributions
\tau_{\xi}
, that is, explicit formulas in (
8.4
). This means expressing
\tau_{\xi}
as a series of tempered invariant eigen-distributions of
G
. Such formulas have been obtained in
[
SW73
]
for
\xi
semisimple and
G
of real rank one. For
\xi
regular and
G
of rank greater than one, formulas are obtained in
[
Her79
]
.

While orbital integrals appear on the geometric side of the trace formula, their spectral side counterparts are characters. The character of a discrete series representation can be realized in the following.

Theorem 8.6

(Character Formula for Elliptic Orbital Integral)
.

If
\xi
is elliptic, then

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot d_{\xi}^{-1}\sum_{w\in W_{\mathfrak{k}}/W_{\mathfrak{k}_{\xi}}}\operatorname{det}(w)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\frac{\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(\xi)}\left\langle w\left(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\right),\alpha\right\rangle\cdot e^{w\left(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}\right)}(\xi)}{e^{\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\prod_{\beta\in R^{+}\setminus R^{+}(\xi)}\left(1-e^{-\beta}(\xi)\right)}\right),

where
d_{\xi}
is a constant depending only on the choice of the Haar measure on
G_{\xi}
. In particular, if
\xi\in T^{\text{reg}}
, then

(8.22)

\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}.

Proof.

We provide a sketch; full details can be found in
[
Mos84
, Theorem 4.14]
. By
G
-invariance, we may conjugate
\xi
so that
\xi\in T
.

If
\xi\in T^{\text{reg}}
, the formula was proved in
[
HW19
, Theorem 3.1]
. For singular
\xi
, the character
\Theta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}
diverges, but the regularized orbital integral

(8.23)

\displaystyle\Delta_{T}(\xi)\cdot\tau_{\xi}\left(\ind_{G}\left(D_{\mu}\right)\right)

remains continuous on
T
.

Define the differential operator on
\mathfrak{t}
:

(8.24)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\xi}=\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(\xi)}H_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{U}\left(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}\right),

where
H_{\alpha}
is determined by

(8.25)

\displaystyle B\left(H,H_{\alpha}\right)=\alpha(H),\quad\forall H\in\mathfrak{t}.

Harish-Chandra showed in
[
HC66
]
that

(8.26)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=\frac{e^{\rho_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}{d_{\xi}\cdot\prod_{\beta\in R^{+}\setminus R^{+}(\xi)}\left(e^{\beta}(\xi)-1\right)}\cdot\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\to\xi\\
\eta\in T^{\text{reg}}\end{subarray}}\Psi_{\xi}\left(\Delta_{T}(\eta)\cdot\tau_{\eta}\left(\ind_{G}\left(D_{\mu}\right)\right)\right).

∎

Theorem 8.7

(Central Orbital Integral)
.

If
\xi
is central, then

(8.27)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=\zeta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}\left(\xi\right)\cdot d(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}).

where
\zeta_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}\colon Z_{G}\to\mathbb{C}
denotes the central character of the discrete series representation with Harish-Chandra parameter
\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}
, and

(8.28)

d(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}2^{(\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}-1)/2}}\cdot\frac{\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}\left(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}\right)}(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}},\alpha)}{\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}\left(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}\right)}\left(\rho_{\mathfrak{k}},\alpha\right)},

is its formal degree.

Proof.

[
BM83
, Section 4.2]
.
∎

Theorem 8.8

(Hyperbolic Orbital Integral)
.

If
\xi
is hyperbolic, then

(8.29)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=0.

Proof.

By
[
SW73
]
, we know that
\tau_{\xi}
has no discrete part contribution for hyperbolic elements. The theorem follows immediately from the Selberg principle in Theorem
8.3
.
∎

To summarize, for all semisimple elements, we have explicit formulas for the orbital integral in terms of roots. Moreover, Hochs-Wang
[
HW18
, Proposition 4.12]
proved an Atiyah-Bott fixed point-type formula:

Theorem 8.9

(Topological Formula for Semi-Simple Orbital Integrals)
.

If
\xi
is a semisimple element in
G
, then

(8.30)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=\int_{X^{\xi}}c^{\xi}\cdot\frac{\hat{A}\left(X^{\xi}\right)\operatorname{Ch}\left(\left[V_{\mu}\big|_{X^{\xi}}\right](\xi)\right)}{\operatorname{det}\left(1-\xi e^{-R^{N}/2\pi i}\right)^{1/2}}.

Here,
c^{\xi}
is the cut-off function for the
G_{\xi}
-action on
X^{\xi}
,
N
is the normal bundle of
X^{\xi}\hookrightarrow X
, and
R^{N}
is its curvature.

8.4.
Non-semi-simple orbital integral

When the lattice
\Gamma
is not uniform, elements in
\Gamma
are no longer necessarily semi-simple. Consequently, we must consider orbital integrals of arbitrary elements in the Selberg trace formula, as derived in
[
War79
]
.

The approach involves expressing the unipotent orbital integral as a limit of semisimple orbital integrals. To make this precise, suppose
u=\exp(X)
is a unipotent element. Then
X
can be embedded in a Lie triple
\{X,H,Y\}
such that
\theta X=-Y
, where
\theta
is the Cartan involution on
G
. Define

(8.31)

\displaystyle Z_{0}=X-Y\in\mathfrak{k},\quad\mathfrak{U}=X+Z_{Y},

where
Z_{Y}
is the centralizer of
Y
in
\mathfrak{g}
. It is shown in
[
RR72
]
that
\mathfrak{U}
is transverse to the adjoint action of
G
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and the union
\bigcup_{t>0}O(t(X-Y))
contains the orbit of
X
in its closure. This allows the orbital integral for
X
to be related to the one for
tZ_{0}
(
t>0
), which are orbital integrals of elliptic elements. The Fourier transform of the orbital integral of
u=\exp(X)
can be found in
[
Bar79
, Theorem 7.1]
.

For an arbitrary element
\xi\in G
, let

(8.32)

\displaystyle\xi=\eta u,\quad u=\exp(X)

be the Jordan decomposition, where
\eta
is semisimple,
X
is unipotent, and
s
commutes with
u
. For
t>0
, define

(8.33)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=z_{t},\quad z_{t}=\operatorname{exp}(tZ_{0}).

The orbital integral of
\xi
can be approximated by the semisimple orbital integral of
\xi_{t}
. Let
\xi_{0}
be the element defined in (
5.18
).
The following theorem is due to Barbasch
[
Bar76
, Theorem 4.3.2]
or
[
Bar79
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Theorem 8.10
.

Suppose that
\xi=\eta\exp(X)
. There exists a constant
c_{\xi}
such that

(8.34)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}(f)=c_{\xi}\cdot\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}}\left(\left[\overline{\left\langle\mu,Z_{0}\right\rangle}\right]^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,1}}{2}}\cdot\prod_{\beta\in R^{+}(\xi_{0})}\left\langle\mu,\beta\right\rangle\cdot e^{\mu}(\eta)\right)\cdot\Theta_{\mu}(f)+\tau_{\xi}^{(c)}(f),

where the
c_{\xi}
is the composite of certain other constants, each of which is explicitly computable, except, perhaps, for one, namely, the volume of

(8.35)

\displaystyle G_{\eta}\cdot Z_{0}\cap\left(X+\mathfrak{g}_{\eta,Y}\right)

in a canonically defined finite measure arising from fiber integration; details can be found in
[
Bar76
]
.

Lemma 8.11
.

The discrete part of the orbital integral
\tau_{\xi}
vanishes unless

(8.36)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\eta}\cong\mathfrak{su}(n,1)\quad\text{and}\quad X\in\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}.

Proof.

By
[
Bar79
, Lemma 8.1]
, the orbital integral has no discrete series contribution if there exists a Weyl group element
w\in W_{\mathfrak{g}_{\eta}}
such that the Lie triple
\{X,H,Y\}
is conjugate to
\{-X,H,-Y\}
.

It is shown in
[
Bar79
, Theorem 3.2]
that this is always the case unless

(8.37)

\displaystyle\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}=1,\quad\text{and}\quad X\in\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}.

The condition
\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}=1
implies that
\mathfrak{g}_{\eta}\cong\mathfrak{su}(n,1)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 8.12
.

For the case of
\mathfrak{g}_{\eta}\cong\mathfrak{su}(n,1)
, the constant
c_{\xi=\eta\exp\left(X\right)}
depends only on the connected component of
X\in\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}\setminus\{0\}
. Thus, we denoted it by
c^{\pm}_{\eta}
. One can check that

(8.38)

\displaystyle c_{\eta}^{+}=(-1)^{n}\cdot c_{\eta}^{-}.

Combining Theorem
8.3
, Theorem
8.10
and Lemma
8.11
, we obtain the following result:

Corollary 8.13

(Non-Semi-Simple Orbital Integral)
.

Suppose that
\xi=\eta\exp(X)
with
\eta
semisimple and
X
unipotent. Then

(8.39)

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=0

unless
\mathfrak{g}_{\eta}\cong\mathfrak{su}(n,1)
and
X\in\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,2}
. In this case, let
\{X,H,Y\}
be the associated Lie triple, and define
Z_{0}=X-Y
. For
\xi^{\pm}=\eta\exp\left(\pm X\right)
, we have that

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi^{\pm}}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}}\cdot c^{\pm}_{\eta}\cdot\sum_{w\in W_{\mathfrak{k}}}\Bigg(\left[\overline{\left\langle w(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}),Z_{0}\right\rangle}\right]^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{n}_{\eta,1}}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{\beta\in R^{+}(\xi_{0})}\left\langle w(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}),\beta\right\rangle\cdot e^{w(\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}})}(\eta)\Bigg).

8.5.
Weighted orbital integral and weighted character

Fix a maximal compact subgroup
K
of
G
. By the Iwasawa decomposition, one can define maps
H_{P},H_{\overline{P}}:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}
as follows:

(8.40)

\displaystyle H_{P}(kan)=\log a,\quad H_{\overline{P}}(ka\overline{n})=\log a,\quad\forall k\in K,a\in A,n\in N,\overline{n}\in\overline{N}.

Let
\lambda_{P}
be the positive multiple of the roots of
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathfrak{n}
satisfying

(8.41)

\displaystyle\lambda_{P}(H)=\pm|H|,\quad H\in\mathfrak{a}.

Define the function

(8.42)

\displaystyle w(x)=\lambda_{P}\left(H_{P}(x)-H_{\overline{P}}(x)\right),

which is positive-valued and satisfies

(8.43)

\displaystyle w(xm)=w(x),\quad m\in M.

Definition 8.14
.

For
\xi\in MA
, the
weighted orbital integral
is defined as

(8.44)

\tau^{\text{w}}_{\xi}(f)=\int_{G/G_{\xi}}f\left(x\xi x^{-1}\right)\cdot w(x)\;dx,\quad f\in\mathcal{C}(G).

The integral is absolutely convergent and defines a non-invariant tempered distribution on
G
. It is independent of
\lambda_{P}
and proportional to the Haar measure on
G
.

The Fourier transform of the weighted orbital integral has been computed in
[
AHS86
,
Her82
,
Art76a
,
War85
,
AM12
,
Hof97
,
Art94
]
.

In Theorem
8.6
, we established that the restriction of the character of a discrete series representation to
T^{\text{reg}}
relates to elliptic orbital integrals. However, its restriction to the non-compact Cartan subgroup
H
cannot be detected by hyperbolic orbital integrals (Theorem
8.8
). Surprisingly, Arthur proved in
[
Art76b
]
that it is instead related to weighted orbital integrals.

Theorem 8.15
.

Suppose that
\xi=ma\in H
. Then,

(8.45)

\tau^{\text{w}}_{\xi}(f)=-\sum_{\omega\in\widehat{G}_{d}}\Omega_{\omega}(h)\cdot\Theta_{\omega}\left(f\right)+\text{continuous part}.

Proof.

See
[
War85
, Theorem 14.1]
or
[
Art76b
]
.
∎

Remark 8.16
.

In equation (
8.45
), the formula holds for
A
-regular points
\xi=ma
with
a\neq 1
. Based on considerations from the Selberg trace formula, one may need to take the limit
a\to 1
and consider
\tau^{\text{w}}_{m}(f)
. Warner extended equation (
8.45
) to the case
a=1
in
[
War85
, Section 15]
.

Recall the normalized intertwining operator defined in equation (
2.32
):

(8.46)

\displaystyle U_{w}\colon H_{\sigma,\nu}\rightarrow H_{\sigma,-\nu}.

If
\phi
is a meromorphic function of
\nu
, we use the abbreviation

(8.47)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}\phi(\nu)=\left.\frac{d}{dz}\phi\left(z\cdot\nu\right)\right|_{z=0}.

For
\sigma\in\widehat{M}
with
\mu(\sigma,0)\neq 0
and
f\in\mathscr{C}(G)
, we define:

Definition 8.17
.

The
weighted character
is given by

(8.48)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}_{\sigma,\nu}(f)=-\operatorname{Tr}\left(\pi_{\sigma,\nu}(f)\circ U(\sigma,\nu)^{-1}\circ\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu}U(\sigma,\nu)\right),

where the derivative is taken for the operator in
H_{\sigma,\nu}
. The weighted character
\widetilde{\Theta}
is a non-invariant tempered distribution, proportional to the Haar measure on
G
and to
\lambda_{P}
. It does not depend on the Haar measure on
\overline{N}
used to define
U(\sigma,\nu)
.

In
[
Art81
, Section 10]
, Arthur defined invariant distributions. In our real rank one setting, this procedure simplifies significantly.

Definition 8.18
.

For any
\xi\in L
and
f\in\mathcal{C}(G)
, we define an invariant tempered distribution by the formula:

(8.49)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(\xi,f)=\tau^{\text{w}}_{\xi}(f)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}}\int_{i\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\overline{\Theta_{\sigma,\nu}(\xi)}\cdot\widetilde{\Theta}_{\sigma,\nu}(f)\;d\nu.

In particular, this induces a map:

(8.50)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{\xi}\colon K_{*}\left(C^{*}_{r}G\right)\to\mathbb{C}.

Corollary 8.19
.

For any
\xi\in L
,

(8.51)

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{\xi}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)=(-1)^{\frac{\dim\mathfrak{p}}{2}+1}\Omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\xi).

Proof.

By conjugation, we may assume that
\xi\in H
, where
H
is the non-compact Cartan subgroup. By definition, the invariant distribution
\widetilde{W}_{\xi}
and the non-invariant distribution
\tau^{\text{w}}_{\xi}
have the same discrete part. The corollary follows from Theorem
8.15
and
8.3
.
∎

Remark 8.20
.

It has been shown in
[
DeG82
, Lemma 5]
that

(8.52)

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mu+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(e)=0,\quad\text{unless }\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\text{ or }\mathfrak{su}(2,1).

9.
Trace formula and proof of main results

In this section, we prove the main result. The primary tool we use is the invariant trace formula for Hecke operators. The general case for the adelic setting was developed by Arthur in
[
Art81
,
Art88a
,
Art88b
]
. In this paper, we employ the trace formula for real groups, which was established by Hoffmann
[
Hof89
,
Hof99
]
. It is worth noting that the specific formula we use is simpler than Hoffmann’s general result, as we impose the additional assumption that
G
has real rank one.

We assume that
G
is a semi-simple Lie group of real rank one. Take
\alpha\in C_{G}(\Gamma)
and let
\Xi=\Gamma\alpha\Gamma
be a double coset.

9.1.
Trace Formula for Hecke Operators

Recall the decomposition of
\Xi
:

(9.1)

\displaystyle\Xi=\underbrace{\Xi_{S}\sqcup\Xi_{P}(r)\sqcup\Xi_{P}(s,*)}_{\text{semi-simple part}}\sqcup\Xi_{P}(s,**).

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 9.1
.

If
G
is of real rank one and equal rank, then

(9.2)

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}(r)\subseteq\Xi_{H}.

That is, every element in
\Xi_{P}(r)
is hyperbolic. We split

(9.3)

\displaystyle\Xi_{H}=\Xi_{P}(r)\sqcup\Xi^{s}_{H},\quad\Xi^{s}_{H}=\Xi_{P}\setminus\Xi_{P}(r).

Proof.

Take
\xi\in\Xi_{P}(r)
. Since
\xi
is semi-simple, it follows from
[
Hof99
, Lemma 7.10]
that
\xi
is
N
-conjugate to an element in
L
. Let
\eta\in\{\xi\}_{N}\cap L\neq\emptyset
. We know that
\eta
is regular in the sense that
N_{\eta}=\{1\}
. The assumption that
G
has real rank one and equal rank excludes the case
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{so}(2n+1,1)
. It has been shown in
[
OW81
, Page 26]
that the centralizers
N_{m}
are non-trivial for all
m\in M
when
\mathfrak{g}\neq\mathfrak{so}(2n+1,1)
. Thus,

(9.4)

\displaystyle\eta=ma\in L=MA,

with
a\neq 1
in
A
. Consequently,
\xi
can be conjugated to an element in the non-compact Cartan subgroup and must be hyperbolic.
∎

Theorem 9.2

(Non-Invariant Trace Formula)
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(G)
, the trace of
T_{\Xi}\circ R^{\Gamma}_{d}(f)
equals the sum of the following parts:

(1)

Semi-simple orbital integrals in

\Xi_{C}\sqcup\Xi_{E}\sqcup\Xi^{s}_{H}
:

\displaystyle C_{\Xi}(f)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}\left(\Gamma\backslash G\right)\sum_{\{\xi\}_{\Gamma}\subset\Xi_{C}}\tau_{\xi}(f),

\displaystyle E_{\Xi}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\xi\}_{\Gamma}\subset\Xi_{E}}\operatorname{vol}\left(\Gamma_{\xi}\backslash G_{\xi}\right)\cdot\tau_{\xi}(f),

\displaystyle H_{\Xi}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\xi\}_{\Gamma}\subset\Xi^{s}_{H}}\operatorname{vol}\left(\Gamma_{\xi}\backslash G_{\xi}\right)\cdot\tau_{\xi}(f).

(2)

Semi-simple orbital integrals from

\Xi_{P}(r)
:

(9.5)

\displaystyle R_{\Xi}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}\sum_{\{\xi\}_{\Gamma_{M}}\subset\Xi_{L}(\mathrm{r})}\alpha^{\Xi}_{\xi}\cdot\tau_{\xi}(f),

where
\alpha^{\Xi}_{\xi}
are some constants defined in
[
Hof99
, Page 105]
.

(3)

Unipotent orbital integrals
:

(9.6)

\displaystyle U_{\Xi}(f)=\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}\sum_{\{\eta\}_{\Gamma_{M}}\subset\Xi_{L}(s)}\sum_{\left\{X\right\}_{L_{\eta}}\subset\overset{\circ}{N}_{\eta,1}\cup\overset{\circ}{N}_{\eta,2}}C_{P}(\Xi,s,X)\cdot\tau_{\eta\exp\left(X\right)}(f),

where
C_{P}(\Xi,\eta,X)
are defined as the constant or residue terms of certain Zeta functions associated with
\Xi
.

(4)

Weighted orbital integrals
:

\displaystyle W_{\Xi}(f)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{P\in E(G,\Gamma)}\sum_{\{\eta\}_{\Gamma_{M}}\subset\Xi_{L}}\operatorname{vol}\left(M_{\eta}/\left(\Gamma_{M}\right)_{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left|\operatorname{det}\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathfrak{n}}(\eta)\right|^{-1/2}\cdot\#\left[\Xi\cap\eta N\colon\Gamma\cap N\right]\cdot\tau^{\text{w}}_{\eta}(f).

(5)

Intertwining term
:

(9.7)

\displaystyle I_{\Xi}(f)=\frac{1}{4\pi i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widehat{M}\\
\sigma\mid_{Z_{\Gamma}}=\mathrm{id}\end{subarray}}\int_{i\mathfrak{a}^{*}}\Tr\left(\pi_{\sigma,v}(f)\circ T_{\alpha,\pi_{\sigma,\nu}}\circ U^{\Gamma}\left(\sigma,-\nu\right)\circ\frac{d}{d\nu}U^{\Gamma}\left(\sigma,\nu\right)\right)\;d\nu.

(6)

Residue term
:

(9.8)

\displaystyle J_{\Xi}(f)=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\widehat{M}\\
\sigma\mid_{Z_{\Gamma}}=\mathrm{id}\end{subarray}}\Tr\left(\pi_{\sigma,0}(f)\circ T_{\alpha,\pi_{\sigma,0}}\circ U^{\Gamma}\left(\sigma,0\right)\right).

Remark 9.3
.

In
[
Hof89
,
Hof99
]
, the trace formula in Theorem
9.2
does not require
\Gamma
to be arithmetic but was originally stated only for functions
f\in C_{c}(G)
. Under the assumption that
\Gamma
is arithmetic, the non-adelic trace formula can be seen as a special case of the adelic trace formula. In
[
FLM11
,
FL16
,
Hof08
]
, the adelic trace formula has been extended to Harish-Chandra
L^{1}
-Schwartz classes. In fact, if Hoffmann’s formula could be extended to a broader class of functions with non-compact support (which seems to be feasible by similar argument in
[
War79
, Section 8]
), it might be possible to drop the assumption that
\Gamma
is arithmetic.

In
[
BM83
]
, Barbasch and Moscovici applied the special case of Theorem
9.2
to the function

(9.9)

\displaystyle h_{t,\mu}=\Tr^{E_{\mu}^{+}}\left(h_{t,\mu}^{+}\right)-\Tr^{E_{\mu}^{-}}\left(h_{t,\mu}^{-}\right).

That is, they obtained the formula:

\displaystyle\Tr\left(R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)\right)=

\displaystyle C_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})+E_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})+H_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})+R_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})

\displaystyle+U_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})+W_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})+I_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})+J_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu}).

Among the terms above, the most challenging to analyze are the
weighted orbital integral
term
W_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})
and the
intertwining term

I_{\Gamma}(h_{t,\mu})
, as they are not invariant. In particular, the Selberg principle (Theorem
8.3
) does not apply to these two terms. To address this, Barbasch and Moscovici decomposed
h_{t,\mu}
as follows:

(9.10)

\displaystyle h_{t,\mu}=h_{t,\mu}^{(d)}+h_{t,\mu}^{(c)},

where
h_{t,\mu}^{(d)}
and
h_{t,\mu}^{(c)}
represent the
discrete
and
continuous
parts of the Plancherel expansion for
h_{t,\mu}
, respectively. Explicitly, these components are given by:

(9.11)

\displaystyle h_{t}^{(d)}(x)=\sum_{\omega\in\widehat{G}_{d}}d(\omega)\cdot\operatorname{Tr}\left(\omega\left(h_{t}\right)\omega(x)^{-1}\right),\quad x\in G,

where
d(\omega)
denotes the formal degree of
\omega
; and

(9.12)

\displaystyle h_{t}^{(c)}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{M}}\int_{i\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\pi_{\sigma,v}\left(h_{t}\right)\pi_{\sigma,v}(x)^{-1}\right)\mu_{\sigma}(v)dv,\quad x\in G,

where
\mu_{\sigma}
is the Plancherel density.

One of the main observations in
[
BM83
]
is that if
\mu
is regular, then the limits

(9.13)

\lim_{t\to\infty}W_{\Xi}\left(h^{(c)}_{t,\mu}\right)=0,\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}I_{\Xi}\left(h^{(c)}_{t,\mu}\right)=0

hold, which leads to the final formula for
\Tr\left(R_{d}^{\Gamma}\left(h_{t,\mu}\right)\right)
.

However, we emphasize that if
\mu
is singular or if
\Xi\neq\Gamma
, the limits

(9.14)

\displaystyle W_{\Xi}\left(h^{(c)}_{t,\mu}\right),\quad\text{and}\quad I_{\Xi}\left(h^{(c)}_{t,\mu}\right)

might not be zero, and their explicit computation remains challenging. To overcome this difficulty, we will employ the following formula.

Theorem 9.4

(Invariant Trace Formula for Hecke Operators)
.

Under the same setting as Theorem

The renormalized Nelson model in the weak coupling limit
Esteban Cárdenas, and, David Mitrouskas

Abstract
The Nelson model describes non-relativistic particles coupled to a relativistic Bose scalar field. In this article, we study the renormalized version of the Nelson model with massless bosons in Davies’ weak coupling limit. Our main result states that the two-body Coulomb potential emerges as an effective pair interaction between the particles, which arises from the exchange of virtual excitations of the quantum field.



1
Introduction

Non-relativistic quantum electrodynamics (QED) describes the interactions between charged particles that are mediated by photons–the excitations of the quantized electromagnetic field. When particle velocities are significantly smaller than the speed of photon propagation, these interactions can be effectively approximated by a direct Coulomb interaction between the particles. The goal of this article is to provide a precise formulation of this approximation. Instead of working within the full non-relativistic QED framework, we focus on the renormalized massless Nelson model,
without
infrared or ultraviolet regularization. The Nelson model has been extensively studied in mathematical physics and provides insights into the interplay between particles and quantum fields in a simplified yet meaningful way. Since Nelson’s pioneering work

[
16
]
in 1964, it has been understood that this model requires an infinite energy renormalization in the ultraviolet regime. Formally, the Nelson model for two particles coupled to a massless scalar Bose field is described by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,2}(-\Delta_{x_{i}})\otimes\textbf{1}+\textbf{1}\otimes\mu\int dk\,|k|\,a_{k}^{*}a_{k}+\mu^{1/2}\sum_{i=1,2}\int\frac{dk}{\sqrt{|k|}}\Big(e^{-ikx_{i}}\otimes a_{k}^{*}+e^{ikx_{i}}\otimes a_{k}\Big)

(1)

where
a_{k}^{*}
and
a_{k}
are bosonic creation and annihilation operators and
\mu>0
is a dimensionless scaling parameter. We are interested in studying the dynamics generated by the Hamiltonian (
1
)
in the regime
\mu\rightarrow\infty
, known as the
weak coupling limit
.
This limit was first studied by Davies
[
4
]

and is equivalent to a scaling of small coupling

\lambda=\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for heavy particles of mass
M=\mu
,
on a rescaled time-scale
\tau=\mu^{-1}t
. The work of Davies was refined by Hiroshima
[
8
,
9
]
and Gubinelli, Hiroshima and Lörinczi
[
7
]
. A comparison of their results with ours is given below. For an alternative interpretation of the scaling
\mu\to\infty
, we refer to
[
21
, Section 1]
.

1.1
The renormalized massless Nelson model

Let us now turn to the definition
of the precise model that we study.
We consider here the two-particle case with Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\otimes\mathcal{F},\qquad\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigotimes^{n}_{\rm sym}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

(2)

where
\mathcal{F}
is the bosonic Fock space and the Fock vacuum is denoted by
\Omega=(1,\mathbf{0})
–our analysis extends naturally to
N\geq 2
particles.
On
\mathscr{H}

we first introduce the Hamiltonian with
an ultraviolet (UV) cutoff
\Lambda>0

\displaystyle H_{\Lambda}^{\rm N}\,\equiv\,p_{1}^{2}\otimes\textbf{1}\,+\,p_{2}^{2}\otimes\textbf{1}\,+\,\textbf{1}\otimes\mu T\,+\,\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i=1,2}\int\limits_{|k|\leq\Lambda}\frac{dk}{\sqrt{|k|}}\Big(e^{-ikx_{i}}\otimes a_{k}^{*}+e^{ikx_{i}}\otimes a_{k}\Big)

(3)

where
T\equiv d\Gamma(|k|)
is the field energy
and
p_{i}\equiv-i\nabla_{x_{i}}

are the momentum operators. By the Kato–Rellich Theorem, it is straightforward to verify that
H_{\Lambda}^{\rm N}
is self-adjoint on the domain of the non-interacting operator

D(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+\mu T)
, ensuring that
\{e^{-itH_{\Lambda}^{\rm N}}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
defines a strongly continuous unitary group. The next proposition provides the existence of the unitary group as
\Lambda\to\infty
, after appropriate energy renormalization. This was proved in
[
6
]
, which, among other contributions, extended Nelson’s work
[
16
]
to the case of massless fields.
For alternative approaches to renormalizing the Nelson model, see
[
7
,
14
]
.
For the statement, let us introduce the self-energy of the particles as

\displaystyle E_{\Lambda}\ \equiv\ \int_{|k|\leq\Lambda}\frac{2\mu\,dk}{|k|(k^{2}+\mu|k|)}\,+\,e_{0}\ =\ 8\pi\mu\ln(1+\Lambda\mu^{-1})\,+\,e_{0}\ .

(4)

Here,

e_{0}>0
is a constant energy shift defined in (
84
) –independently of
\mu
and
\Lambda
– and is added only for convenience.
Notably,
E_{\Lambda}
diverges logarithmically as
\Lambda\to\infty
for fixed
\mu
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every
\mu>0
there is a semi-bounded self-adjoint operator
H^{\rm N}
so that

\displaystyle\exp\big(-itH^{\rm N}_{\Lambda}\big)\exp\big(-itE_{\Lambda}\big)\,\xrightarrow{\Lambda\to\infty}\,\exp\big(-itH^{\rm N}\big)

(5)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
in the strong sense on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\otimes\mathcal{F}
.

We refer to
H^{\rm N}
as the
renormalized massless Nelson model
.
Let us note that
[
6
]
addresses the case of one particle with
\mu=1
. However, the proof can be straightforwardly extended to cover multiple particles and arbitrary values of
\mu
. We sketch the proof in the appendix.

1.2
Main result and discussion

Our main result is contained in
the following theorem.
We introduce the effective Schrödinger operator for two particles interacting through the Coulomb potential

h^{\rm eff}\,\equiv\,p_{1}^{2}\,+\,p_{2}^{2}\,-\,W(x_{1}-x_{2})\qquad\textnormal{with}\qquad W(x)\,\equiv\,4\pi^{2}|x|^{-1}

(6)

with domain
D(h^{\rm eff})=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
C>0
and
\mu_{0}\geq 1
such that
for all
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
with
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}=1

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(e^{-itH^{\rm N}}-e^{-ith^{\rm eff}}\otimes\textnormal{{1}}\Big)\varphi\otimes\Omega\Big\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\leq\frac{C\,(1+|t|\,)\ln\mu}{\mu^{1/6}}\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}}

(7)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Giving up the convergence rate, a density argument shows that as a corollary

\displaystyle e^{-itH^{\rm N}}(\textnormal{{1}}\otimes P_{\Omega})\xrightarrow{\mu\to\infty}e^{-ith^{\rm eff}}\otimes P_{\Omega}

(8)

in the strong sense in
\mathscr{H}
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Here of course

P_{\Omega}=\ket{\Omega}\bra{\Omega}
.

The theorem demonstrates that in the limit
\mu\to\infty
, the particles effectively decouple from the quantum field, which remains in the initial Fock vacuum state. Instead of coupling to the field, the particles evolve under an instantaneous pair interaction described by the attractive Coulomb potential. Physically, the effective potential arises from the exchange of virtual excitations,
while the emission or absorption of real bosons is energetically suppressed. We remark that it is a particular feature of the scalar field model that two particles of same charge experience an attractive Coulomb interaction, rather than the repulsive one typically expected in QED.

Outline of the proof.

The first step in our analysis
is a unitary representation of
H^{\rm N}

in terms of a dressed Hamiltonian

H_{K}
, where
1\ll K\ll\mu

is a large parameter.
This operator
contains an effective
UV cutoff
K
in (
3
),
at the expense of introducing
several new terms.
In order to simplify the analysis,
we introduce yet another Hamiltonian, denoted by
H_{\varepsilon,K}
,
which results from elimination of lower order terms in
H_{K}
.
Up to small corrections,
it suffices to compare the dynamics generated by
H\equiv H_{\varepsilon,K}
with
the one generated by the effective Hamiltonian
h^{\rm eff}
.
This comparison is based on an expansion that exploits fast oscillations of the unitary operator
e^{-itH}
. More concretely, after performing a first-order Duhamel expansion –see Eq. (
64
)– the contribution
\mu T
in
H
induces fast oscillations of
e^{-itH}
as
\mu\rightarrow\infty
.
Using a suitable integration-by-parts formula –see Eqs. (
67
) and (
85
)– we apply a stationary-phase-type argument to expand the difference between the dynamics. In this expansion, two types of terms emerge:
Subleading terms
, which vanish due to cancellations induced by the oscillations, combined with suitable operator and energy estimates; and
leading-order terms
, which arise from states in the subspace

\textnormal{ker}(T)=\{\phi\otimes\Omega:\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\}
,
where oscillations are absent. Physically, these terms correspond to the self-energy and the effective interaction between the particles. The leading-order terms involve zero final bosons and are commonly referred to as virtual exchange processes. In particular, we prove in Lemma
4.6
that the Coulomb interaction
W(x_{1}-x_{2})

emerges as an effective interaction from these processes.

Organization of the article.
In Section
2
, we introduce the dressed Hamiltonian
H_{K}
and its relation to
H^{\rm N}
. This is followed by a section on preliminary operator estimates, including Lemma
3.4
, which is crucial for controlling the regularity of time-evolved states (see Lemma
4.1
) necessary for exploiting the oscillations induced by the unitary evolution. The main part of the proof is presented in Section
4
, beginning with an approximation of
H_{K}
in terms of a simplified dressed Hamiltonian
H_{\varepsilon,K}
, where subleading terms are removed. The comparison of the dynamics generated by
H_{\varepsilon,K}
and
h^{\rm eff}
is made in Section
4.3
and comprises most of the analysis in this article. The main cancellations between the two generators are established in Lemmas
4.6
and
4.8
. In the appendix, we provide some details on the proofs of Proposition
1.1
and Lemma
2.1
.

Here and in the sequel,
we introduce the following

in the interaction term of (

)

\displaystyle G_{1}(k)\equiv|k|^{-1/2}e^{-ikx_{1}}\otimes\textbf{1}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad G_{2}(k)\equiv|k|^{-1/2}\textbf{1}\otimes e^{-ikx_{2}}

regarded as operators on

.
In addition, for
\Lambda>0

we introduce their cutoff versions

G_{\Lambda,1}(k)\equiv G_{1}(k)\mathds{1}_{|k|\leq\Lambda}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad G_{\Lambda,2}(k)\equiv G_{2}(k)\mathds{1}_{|k|\leq\Lambda}\ .

(15)

From now on, we no longer use the tensor product

Our analysis is based on a representation of the Hamiltonian
H^{\rm N}
via the following unitary dressing transformation (also called Gross transformation)
for
K>0

\displaystyle U_{K}\equiv\exp\Big(\mu^{1/2}\sum_{i=1,2}(a^{*}(B_{K,i})-a(B_{K,i}))\Big)\quad\text{with}\quad B_{K,i}(k)\equiv\frac{G_{i}(k)}{k^{2}+\mu|k|}\mathds{1}_{|k|\geq K}

(16)

for

. Observe that

acts as an upper cutoff in

,
but as a lower cutoff in

.

[
6
]
,
and a sketch of the proof is provided in the appendix.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
K>0

we have the operator identity
H^{\rm N}=(U_{K})^{*}H_{K}U_{K}
with dressed Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{K}

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+\mu T+\mu^{1/2}\sum_{i=1,2}\Big(\phi(G_{K,i})+2a^{*}(kB_{K,i})\cdot p_{i}+2p_{i}\cdot a(kB_{K,i})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\sum_{i=1,2}\Big(2a^{*}(kB_{K,i})a(kB_{K,i})+a^{*}(kB_{K,i})a^{*}(kB_{K,i})+a(kB_{K,i})a(kB_{K,i})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+E_{K}+V_{K}(x_{1}-x_{2}),

(17)

with quadratic form domain
Q(H_{K})=Q(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2}+\mu T)
.
Here,
E_{K}
is the energy defined in (
4
),
and we introduced the potential

V_{K}(x)\,=\,(-2)\mu\,\int_{|k|\geq K}\frac{\cos(kx)(\mu|k|+2k^{2})\,dk}{|k|(\mu|k|+k^{2})^{2}}.

(18)

Remark 2.1
.

Relative to
[
6
]
,
here we obtain a new term corresponding to
V_{K}
due to effective interactions between the two particles.
An integral estimate shows that there is
C>0
so that

\displaystyle\|V_{K}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=4\mu^{2}\int_{|k|\geq K}\bigg|\frac{(\mu|k|+2k^{2})}{|k|(\mu|k|+k^{2})^{2}}\bigg|^{2}dk\leq\frac{C}{K}\ ,

(19)

for all
\mu,K>0
.

3
Preliminary estimates

First, we introduce
some auxiliary
operators.
For
0\leq a<b\leq\infty
, we set

\displaystyle G_{a,b,i}=\mathds{1}(a\leq|k|\leq b)\,G_{i}\ ,\quad N_{a,b}=\int\limits_{a\leq|k|\leq b}a^{*}_{k}a_{k}dk\ ,\quad T_{a,b}=\int\limits_{a\leq|k|\leq b}|k|a^{*}_{k}a_{k}dk.

(20)

In particular
N_{0,\infty}=N
and
T_{0,\infty}=T
.
Similarly,
we introduce the projections

P_{a,b}=\mathds{1}(N_{a,b}=0)\ ,\qquad Q_{a,b}=\mathds{1}(N_{a,b}\geq 1)\ .

(21)

Note that
P_{0,\infty}=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|
coincides with the projection on the vacuum.
Since
T_{a,b}\geq aN_{a,b}
and since
N_{a,b}^{m}
has a bounded inverse on

, we have

\displaystyle\|(T_{a,b})^{-m}Q_{a,b}\|\leq a^{-m}\|(N_{a,b})^{-m}Q_{a,b}\|\leq a^{-m}.

(22)

We will further need the following resolvent type operator

\displaystyle R_{a,b}=Q_{a,b}(T_{a,b}+E_{K}+(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})/\mu)^{-1}Q_{a,b}

(23)

where we recall that
E_{K}>0
, hence this is well defined for all
a\geq 0
.
Moreover, for
a>0
it satisfies
\|R_{a,b}^{m}\|\leq a^{-m}
for any
m>0
, and
for any
i=1,2

\displaystyle\|T_{a,b}^{1/2}R_{a,b}^{1/2}\|\leq 1,\qquad\|(T_{a,b}+1)^{1/2}R_{a,b}^{1/2}\|\leq C(1+a^{-1/2}),\qquad\|p_{i}R_{a,b}^{1/2}\|\leq\sqrt{\mu}.

(24)

3.1
Integral estimates

We start by summarizing some relevant norms that will come up many times.
The proof is a simple
calculation using the change of variables
\ell\equiv\mu^{-1}k
which is left to the reader.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G_{K}
and
B_{K}
denote
G_{K,i}
and
B_{K,i}
for any
i=1,2
. Denote
\omega(k)=|k|
.

(1)

There is
C>0
such that for all

\|G_{K}\|\leq CK\ ,\qquad\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}G_{K}\|\leq CK^{1/2}\ .

(25)

(2)

For
0<s\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
there is
C>0
such that for all

\mu,K>0

\displaystyle\|B_{K}\|\leq\frac{C}{\mu}\big(1+|\ln(K/\mu)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\ ,\qquad\|\omega^{-s}kB_{K}\|\leq\frac{C}{(\mu+K)^{s}}\ .

(26)

Remark 3.1
.

In this article we are mostly concerned
with the case
\mu\gg K\gg 1
.
Hence, we keep in mind the simplified bounds

\|B_{K}\|\leq C\ln(\mu/K)^{1/2}\mu^{-1}\ ,\quad\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}kB_{K}\|\leq C\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\ ,\quad\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}kB_{K}\|\leq C\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ .

(27)

In order to control the large
K
limit
associated to the effective potentials, we use the following
result.
Effectively, we want to compare for two states
\psi,\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
the expression
contained in the following lemma for

v(x)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\textstyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{v}(k)e^{ikx}dk

where
\hat{v}(k)
is supported only on
|k|\geq K
.
We establish the estimate in a general form
for further use.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.
Then,
there exists
C>0

such that for all
\phi,\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
.

\Big|\Big\langle\psi,v(x_{1}-x_{2})\phi\Big\rangle\Big|\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)\ .

(28)

Additionally,
there exists
C>0

such that for all
\phi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
.

\displaystyle\|v(x_{1}-x_{2})\phi\|_{L^{2}}\,\leq\,C\|v\|_{L^{2}}\min\big\{\,\|(1+p_{1}^{2})\phi\|_{L^{2}}\,,\,\|(1+p_{2}^{2})\phi\|_{L^{2}}\,\big\}\ .

(29)

Proof.

Let
D\equiv\Big\langle\psi,\int dk\widehat{v}(k)e^{-ik(x-y)}\phi\Big\rangle.
First, shift
x\mapsto x+y
and define the shifted function
\psi_{y}(x)=\psi(x+y,y)
, and then use Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval to find

\displaystyle|D|\leq\|\widehat{v}\|_{L^{2}_{k}}\ \Big\|\int\big\langle\psi_{y}(x),\phi_{y}(x)\big\rangle_{L_{y}^{2}}e^{ikx}dx\Big\|_{L^{2}_{k}}=\|v\|_{L^{2}}\Big\|\big\langle\psi_{y}(x),\phi_{y}(x)\big\rangle_{L_{y}^{2}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

(30)

The triangle inequality, Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
and Young’s inequality imply

\displaystyle\Big\|\big\langle\psi_{y}(x),\phi_{y}(x)\big\rangle_{L_{y}^{2}}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\leq\int dy\|{\psi_{y}}\phi_{y}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\int dy\|\psi_{y}\|_{L^{4}_{x}}\|\phi_{y}\|_{L^{4}_{x}}\leq\int dy\big(\|\psi_{y}\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}+\|\phi_{y}\|_{L^{4}_{x}}^{2}\big)\ .

(31)

On the other hand,
the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
implies
for any
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

\|\psi\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\psi\|_{L^{6}}^{3/2}\leq C\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{3/2}\ .

(32)

We put the last three inequalities together and use

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}
to finish the proof.

For the second part, we do the same change of variables and
use the Sobolev embedding
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

to obtain

\displaystyle\iint dxdy|v(x-y)|^{2}|\phi(x,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\iint dxdy|v(x)|^{2}|\phi(x+y,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int dy\sup_{x}|\phi(x,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int dy\|\phi(\cdot,y)\|_{H_{x}^{2}}^{2}=C\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{y}H_{x}^{2}}

(33)

Interchanging the roles of
x
and
y
, this gives the desired estimate.
∎

3.2
Operator estimates

First, we state
useful bounds for creation and annihilation operators.

Lemma 3.3

(Operator bounds)
.

Let
f,g\in L^{\infty}L^{2}

be supported
on
\mathbb{R}^{6}\times\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{9}
.
Let us denote

N_{\mathcal{D}}\equiv\int_{\mathcal{D}}a_{k}^{*}a_{k}dk

and

T_{\mathcal{D}}\equiv\int_{\mathcal{D}}\omega(k)a_{k}^{*}a_{k}dk
for some measurable function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
.
Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\|a(f)\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|\|N_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\forall\Psi\in D(N_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2})\ ,

(34)

\displaystyle\|a(f)\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}f\|\|T_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\forall\Psi\in D(T_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2})\ .

(35)

Additionally,

\displaystyle\|(1+N_{\mathcal{D}})^{-\frac{1}{2}}a(g)a(f)\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}f\|\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}g\|\|T_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\forall\Psi\in D(T_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2})\ .

(36)

Proof.

The first two inequalities follow from a simple application of Cauchy–Schwarz.
The third one is taken from
[
6
]
,
which originates in a paper of Nelson
[
16
]
.
Let us give here the proof for completeness.
First, consider
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\omega^{-1/2}dk)
and
\Psi\in D(T_{\mathcal{D}}^{1/2})
and compute

\displaystyle\|a(f)a(g)\Psi\|_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \int_{\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}}|f(k_{2})||g(k_{1})|\|a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\Psi\|_{\mathcal{F}}\ dk_{1}dk_{2}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}f\|_{L^{2}}\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}g\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}}\omega(k_{1})^{1/2}\omega(k_{2})^{1/2}\|a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\Psi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\ dk_{1}dk_{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}f\|_{L^{2}}\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}g\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{D}}\omega(k_{1})\|a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}\Psi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}\ dk_{1}dk_{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ ,

(37)

where in the last line we used

\omega_{1}^{1/2}\omega_{2}^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})
,
a change of variables
k_{2}\mapsto k_{1}
,
and the
canonical commutation relations (CCR) for the field operators.
Next, we use the CCR to calculate

a_{k_{2}}^{*}a_{k_{1}}^{*}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}=\delta(k_{1}-k_{2})a_{k_{1}}^{*}a_{k_{1}}+a_{k_{1}}^{*}a_{k_{1}}a_{k_{2}}^{*}a_{k_{2}}

and integrate over
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, to find

\displaystyle\|a(f)a(g)\Psi\|_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\ \leq\ \|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}f\|_{L^{2}}\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}g\|_{L^{2}}\,\langle\Psi,T_{\mathcal{D}}(1+N_{\mathcal{D}})\Psi\rangle_{\mathcal{F}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\ .

(38)

The previous bound can then be extended to

f,g\in L^{\infty}L^{2}
and
\Psi\in\mathscr{H}

using (
10
).
This is the desired estimate after changing
\Psi\mapsto(N_{\mathcal{D}}+1)^{-1/2}\Psi.

∎

4
Proof of the main result

Throughout this section,
we let
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})

be such that

.
In particular, all constants will be independent of
\varphi
.
Let us record here the proof of the main theorem.
The proof relies in the use
of an

generated by the simplified Hamiltonian
for

H_{\varepsilon,K}\equiv P^{2}+\mu T+E_{K}+V_{K}+\sum_{i=1,2}\Big(\sqrt{\mu}\phi(G_{\varepsilon,K,i})+\mu(A_{K,i}+A_{K,i}^{*})\Big)

(51)

1.2
.

The first step is to
replace
H^{\rm N}
by
H_{K}
in the time evolution.
To this end,
observe that there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
\mu\geq 2K>0

and

\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{6})\otimes\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\|(U_{K}^{\#}-1)\Psi\|

with

defined in (
16
) and

), and the bound for

).
Thanks to this bound and Lemma

\displaystyle\|(e^{-itH^{\rm N}}-e^{-ith^{\rm eff}})(\varphi\otimes\Omega)\|

For the second step, we make use of the intermediate dynamics

.

\bigg\|\bigg(\mu A_{K,1}R_{K,\infty}A_{K,1}^{*}-\int\frac{2|F(k_{1})|^{2}|F(k_{2})|^{2}\,dk_{1}dk_{2}}{\mu|k_{1}|+\mu|k_{2}|+(k_{1}+k_{2})^{2}}\bigg)\phi\otimes\Omega\bigg\|\leq\frac{CK}{\mu}\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}

(102)

We re-scale

and use a simple integral estimate
to obtain

(103)

Note that the first term on the right-hand side

defined in (

Thus, we arrive at

(104)

and the same bound holds if we exchange

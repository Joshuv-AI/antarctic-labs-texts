A note on finite-dimensional quotients and the problem of automatic continuity for twisted convolution algebras
Felipe I. Flores

Abstract
In this note we show that the twisted convolution algebra L α , ω 1 ​ ( 𝖦 , 𝔄 ) L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A}) associated to a twisted action of a locally compact group 𝖦 {\sf G} on a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra 𝔄 \mathfrak{A} has the following property: Every quotient by a closed two-sided ideal of finite codimension produces a semisimple algebra. We use this property, together with results of H. Dales and G. Willis, to build up on previous results of the author and produce large classes of examples of algebras with properties of automatic continuity.



The purpose of this note is to extend previous results on the automatic continuity of the Banach
∗
-algebras given by (generalized, twisted) convolution of
L^{1}
-functions over groups. More specifically, we seek to improve some of the results in
[
2
]
by two fundamentally different but related ways. One involves relaxing the condition of compact generation of the group (fundamental to the results of that paper), while the other one is about making the coefficient algebra, finite-dimensional. This allows, of course, great generalizations and new examples of automatic continuity phenomena.

Our approach is based on the study of semisimplicity for the quotients by finite codimensional (also called cofinite), closed, two-sided ideals. This property is surprisingly connected with the theory of automatic continuity, as the results in
[
5
]
, and specially in
[
1
]
, exemplify. In fact, our approach will make explicit use of theorems in these papers, attributed to Wills (Theorem
2.4
) and Dales-Wills (Theorem
2.7
). These theorems, combined with results of
[
2
]
and the result we obtained on semisimplicity, yield the new examples of automatic continuity.

The organization of the paper is as follows: In Section
1
we introduce what we call twisted convolution algebras and prove that their finite-dimensional quotients are semisimple. This is recorded in Theorem
1.3
and its proof. In Section
2
we recall basic concepts of automatic continuity and then we proceed to combine the above-mentioned results to get our results in automatic continuity, finishing the exposition.

1
Semisimplicity of finite-dimensional quotients

A
twisted action
is a
4
-tuple
({\sf G},\alpha,\omega,\mathfrak{A})
, where
{\sf G}
is a locally compact group,
\mathfrak{A}
a
C^{*}
-algebra and continuous maps
\alpha:{\sf G}\to{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
,
\omega:{\sf G}\times{\sf G}\to\mathcal{UM}({\mathfrak{A}})
, such that
\omega
and
x\mapsto\alpha_{x}(a)
satisfy

(i)

\alpha_{x}(\omega(y,z))\omega(x,yz)=\omega(x,y)\omega(xy,z)
,

(ii)

\alpha_{x}\big(\alpha_{y}(a)\big)\omega(x,y)=\omega(x,y)\alpha_{xy}(a)
,

(iii)

\omega(x,{\sf e})=\omega({\sf e},y)=1,\alpha_{\sf e}={\rm id}_{{\mathfrak{A}}}
,

for all
x,y,z\in{\sf G}
and
a\in\mathfrak{A}
.
{\sf e}
denotes the identity in
{\sf G}
.

Given such a tuple, one can form the twisted convolution algebra
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
, consisting of all Bochner integrable functions
\Phi:{\sf G}\to\mathfrak{A}
, endowed with the twisted convolution product

\Phi*\Psi(x)=\int_{\sf G}\Phi(y)\alpha_{y}[\Psi(y^{-1}x)]\omega(y,y^{-1}x){\rm d}y

(1.1)

and the involution

\Phi^{*}(x)=\Delta(x^{-1})\omega(x,x^{-1})^{*}\alpha_{x}[\Phi(x^{-1})^{*}].

(1.2)

Then
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
is a Banach
∗
-algebra under the norm
\lVert\Phi\rVert_{L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})}=\int_{\sf G}\lVert\Phi(x)\rVert_{\mathfrak{A}}{\rm d}x
. Here
{\rm d}x
denotes, obviously, the Haar measure in
{\sf G}
.

We will now go on to show that the closed, cofinite ideals of
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
induce semisimple quotients, but first, we are in the need of introducing special classes of multipliers. For
u\in\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{A}),\,y\in{\sf G}
, consider the multiplier
m_{u,y}
of
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
given by

m_{u,y}(\Phi)(x)=u\alpha_{y}\big(\Phi(y^{-1}x)\big)\omega(y,y^{-1}x).

It is an easy but tedious computation to check that, if
u\in\mathcal{UM}(\mathfrak{A})
, then
m_{u,y}
is unitary. In fact, its adjoint has the form

m_{u,y}^{*}(\Phi)(x)=\omega(y^{-1},y)^{*}\alpha_{y^{-1}}\big(u^{*}\Phi(yx)\big)\omega(y^{-1},yx)=m_{\omega(y^{-1},y)^{*}\alpha_{y^{-1}}(u^{*}),y^{-1}}(\Phi)(x).

(1.3)

It is also easy to note that
\lVert m_{u,y}\rVert_{\mathcal{M}(L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A}))}\leq 1
and because
m_{u,y}
is unitary, this means that its norm is
1
.

Definition 1.1
.

A linear subspace
I\subset L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
is called
translation-invariant
if
m_{u,y}(I)\subset I
for every
u\in\mathcal{M}(\mathfrak{A})
and
y\in{\sf G}
.

Lemma 1.2
.

A closed linear subspace
I\subset L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
is translation-invariant if and only if it is a left ideal.

Proof.

Let
\Psi_{\gamma}\in L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
be a bounded approximate identity. Then for all
\Phi\in I
, we have

I\ni m_{u,y}(\Psi_{\gamma})*\Phi=m_{u,y}(\Psi_{\gamma}*\Phi)\to m_{u,y}(\Phi)

and hence
m_{u,y}(\Phi)\in I
.

For the other implication, it is enough to take
\Phi\in L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A}),\Psi\in I
and note that

\Phi*\Psi=\int_{\sf G}\Phi(y)\alpha_{y}[\Psi(y^{-1}\cdot)]\omega(y,y^{-1}\cdot){\rm d}y=\int_{\sf G}m_{\Phi(y),y}(\Psi){\rm d}y,

(1.4)

which belongs to
I
. ∎

Now, we proceed to prove the main result of the section.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
({\sf G},\alpha,\omega,\mathfrak{A})
be a twisted action, then for every left closed ideal of finite codimension
I\subset\mathfrak{B}=L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
the quotient algebra
\mathfrak{B}/I
is semisimple and
I
is a
∗
-ideal.

Proof.

Since
I
is closed and finite-dimensional,
\mathcal{X}=\mathfrak{B}/I
is a finite-dimensional Banach space. Define the operators
\pi(m_{u,y})\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{X})
by

\pi(m_{u,y})(\Psi+I)=m_{u,y}(\Psi)+I.

This is well-defined because of the previous lemma and it extends to the representation
\pi:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{X})
given by

\pi(\Phi)(\Psi+I)=\int_{\sf G}\pi(m_{\Phi(y),y})(\Psi+I){\rm d}y=\int_{\sf G}m_{\Phi(y),y}(\Psi){\rm d}y+I=\Phi*\Psi+I

(1.5)

We will now show that this representation preserves an inner product on
\mathcal{X}
. Indeed, we note that
H=\{\pi(m_{u,y})\}_{y\in{\sf G},u\in\mathcal{UM}(\mathfrak{A})}
is naturally a group formed by operators of norm at most
1
, so its closure is a compact subgroup
K\subset{\rm GL}(\mathcal{X})
that therefore admits a finite (both left and right) Haar measure
\nu
. Now let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be any inner product on
\mathcal{X}
and define the new inner product given by the formula

\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{\nu}:=\int_{K}\langle T\xi,T\eta\rangle\,{\rm d}\nu(T).

With respect to this inner product,
\pi
is an
∗
-representation: for every
h\in H
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{X}
, we have

\langle h\xi,\eta\rangle_{\nu}=\int_{K}\langle hT\xi,T\eta\rangle\,{\rm d}\nu(T)=\int_{K}\langle S\xi,h^{-1}S\eta\rangle\,{\rm d}\nu(S)=\langle\xi,h^{-1}\eta\rangle_{\nu}.

But, if
h=\pi(m_{u,y})
, then
h^{-1}=\pi(m_{u,y}^{*})
and we have

\langle\pi(m_{u,y})\xi,\eta\rangle_{\nu}=\langle\xi,\pi(m_{u,y}^{*})\eta\rangle_{\nu}.

From which,

\displaystyle\langle\pi(\Phi)(\Psi_{1}+I),\Psi_{2}+I\rangle_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\int_{\sf G}\langle\pi(m_{\Phi(y),y})(\Psi_{1}+I),\Psi_{2}+I\rangle_{\nu}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi_{1}+I,\int_{\sf G}\pi(m_{\Phi(y),y}^{*})(\Psi_{2}+I){\rm d}y\rangle_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\langle\Psi_{1}+I,\pi(\Phi^{*})(\Psi_{2}+I)\rangle_{\nu},

follows for all
\Phi,\Psi_{1},\Psi_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}
.

Now, if we show that
{\rm Ker}\,\pi=I
, it will follow that
\mathfrak{B}/I
is
∗
-isomorphic to
\pi(\mathfrak{B})
, which is a
C^{*}
-algebra, and therefore we will have shown that
\mathfrak{B}/I
is semisimple. However, this follows from the existence of a bounded approximate identity: If
\Phi\in{\rm Ker}\,\pi
and
\Psi_{j}\in\mathfrak{B}
is the bounded approximate identity, then one sees that

I=\lim_{j}\pi(\Phi)(\Psi_{j}+I)=\lim_{j}\Phi*\Psi_{j}+I=\Phi+I,

so
\Phi\in I
, finishing the proof.
∎

2
Applications to the problem of automatic continuity

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be Banach algebra. A Banach space
\mathcal{X}
which is also a
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule is called a
Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule
if the maps

\mathfrak{B}\times\mathcal{X}\ni(b,\xi)\mapsto b\xi\in\mathcal{X}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{X}\times\mathfrak{B}\ni(\xi,b)\mapsto\xi b\in\mathcal{X}

are jointly continuous.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra and
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
be Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. A linear map
\theta:\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
is called a
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator
if for each
b\in\mathfrak{B}
, the maps

\mathcal{X}_{1}\ni\xi\mapsto\theta(b\xi)-b\theta(\xi)\in\mathcal{X}_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{X}_{1}\ni\xi\mapsto\theta(\xi b)-\theta(\xi)b\in\mathcal{X}_{2}

are continuous.

Example 2.2
.

(i)

Every
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule homomorphism between Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodules is a
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator.

(ii)

Let
\mathcal{X}
a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule. A derivation is a linear map
D:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
satisfying

D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b).

Every derivation is a
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator.

If
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
are Banach spaces and
\theta:\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
is a linear operator, then

\mathscr{S}(\theta)=\{\eta\in\mathcal{X}_{2}\mid\exists\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{1}\text{ such that }\xi_{n}\to 0\text{ and }\theta(\xi_{n})\to\eta\}

is the
separating space
of
\theta
.
\mathscr{S}(\theta)
is closed and reduces to
\{0\}
if and only if
\theta
is continuous. If
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
are Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodules and
\theta
is
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining,
\mathscr{S}(\theta)
is a sub-bimodule of
\mathcal{X}_{2}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra, and
\theta:\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
a
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator between Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodules. Then

\mathscr{I}(\theta)=\{b\in\mathfrak{B}\mid b\mathscr{S}(\theta)=\mathscr{S}(\theta)b=\{0\}\}

is the
continuity ideal
of
\theta
.

In fact,
\mathscr{I}(\theta)
coincides with the set of
b\in\mathfrak{B}
such that the maps

\mathcal{X}_{1}\ni\xi\mapsto\theta(b\xi)\in\mathcal{X}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{X}_{1}\ni\xi\mapsto\theta(\xi b)\in\mathcal{X}_{2}

are continuous. Note that
\mathscr{I}(\theta)
is closed, since
\mathcal{X}_{2}
is a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule.

The following theorem is due to Willis
[
6
, Lemma 4.3.5]
. See also
[
5
, pag. 498]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra,
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-modules and
\theta:\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
an
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator. Suppose that there is a directed family
\{\mathfrak{B}_{i}\}_{i}
of Banach subalgebras of
\mathfrak{B}
such that

1.

\mathfrak{B}=\overline{\bigcup_{i}\mathfrak{B}_{i}}
and

2.

for each index
i
, the algebra
\mathfrak{B}_{i}/\mathfrak{B}_{i}\cap\mathscr{I}(\theta)
is semisimple and finite-dimensional.

The main application of this result is the ability to lift the restriction of compact-generation of
{\sf G}
from some of the results obtained in
[
2
]
. We would like to stress that such a restriction was of fundamental importance in that work, as it allowed the existence of weight functions.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
({\sf G},\alpha,\omega,\mathfrak{A})
be a twisted action, denote
\mathfrak{B}=L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
and suppose we are given an
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator
\theta:\mathcal{X}_{1}\to\mathcal{X}_{2}
between Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodules
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
with the property that for all open, compactly generated subgroups
{\sf H}\subset{\sf G}
, the ideal
\mathscr{I}(\theta)\cap L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf H},\mathfrak{A})
has finite codimension in
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf H},\mathfrak{A})
, then
\mathscr{I}(\theta)
has finite codimension in
\mathfrak{B}
.

Proof.

We note that
\mathscr{I}(\theta)\cap L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf H},\mathfrak{A})
coincides with the continuity ideal of
\theta
when viewed as an
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf H},\mathfrak{A})
-intertwining operator and so it is closed. Now, we consider the family
\{{\sf H}_{i}\}_{i}
of open, compactly-generated subgroups of
{\sf G}
, ordered by inclusion and observe that the family
\mathfrak{B}_{i}=L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf H}_{i},\mathfrak{A})
is a directed family of subalgebras of
\mathfrak{B}
such that
\mathfrak{B}=\overline{\bigcup_{i}\mathfrak{B}_{i}}
. Then
\mathfrak{B}_{i}/\mathfrak{B}_{i}\cap\mathscr{I}(\theta)
is finite-dimensional by assumption and semisimple by Theorem
1.3
. The result then follows from Theorem
2.4
.
∎

In particular, we are now able to provide the following examples of automatic continuity.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
{\sf G}
be a nilpotent locally compact group. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule and
\theta:\mathfrak{B}\to\mathcal{X}
a
\mathfrak{B}
-intertwining operator. Then
\theta
is automatically continuous for the following choices of
\mathfrak{B}
:

(i)

Twisted group algebras
L^{1}_{\omega}({\sf G})
, associated with a
2
-cocycle
\omega:{\sf G}\times{\sf G}\to\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

Convolution algebras
\ell^{1}_{\alpha}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
, where
({\sf G},\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
is a
C^{*}
-dynamical system with
\mathfrak{A}
either an abelian
C^{*}
-algebra or a finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebra.

(iii)

\ell^{1}_{\alpha}({\sf G},C(X))
, if
{\sf G}
is infinite,
X
is compact and the restriction of
\alpha
to any invariant subset of
X
is topologically free.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
2.5
, and
[
2
, Theorem 4.14, Corollary 4.21]
.
∎

Now we will restrict ourselves to the study of (some) finite-dimensional valued intertwining operators, this will provide more flexibility on the assumptions about the twisted action. Dales and Willis proved the following theorem in
[
1
, Theorem 2.5]
and it will be our main motivation for what follows.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Banach algebra such that
\mathfrak{B}/I
is semisimple for every cofinite, closed two-sided ideal
I\subset\mathfrak{B}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent

(i)

Each homomorphism from
\mathfrak{B}
with finite-dimensional range is continuous.

(ii)

Every derivation into a finite-dimensional Banach
\mathfrak{B}
-bimodule is continuous.

(iii)

Every cofinite two-sided ideal of
\mathfrak{B}
is closed.

(iv)

I^{2}
is closed and cofinite, for every closed, cofinite two-sided ideal
I\subset\mathfrak{B}
.

And so an application of Theorem
1.3
yields the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
({\sf G},\alpha,\omega,\mathfrak{A})
a twisted action. Then all conditions in Theorem
2.7
are equivalent for
L^{1}_{\alpha,\omega}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
.

In particular we obtain many classes of examples for this finite-dimensional phenomena. We compile them in the following corollary. As we shall see, this restriction we just imposed in ourselves to finite-dimensional valued intertwining operators allows us to greatly extend previous results (cf.
[
2
, Corollary 4.21]
).

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be one of the following algebras.

(i)

L^{1}_{\omega}({\sf G})
, for an amenable group
{\sf G}
and a
2
-cocycle
\omega:{\sf G}\times{\sf G}\to\mathbb{C}
.

(ii)

\ell^{1}_{\alpha}({\sf G},\mathfrak{A})
, for an (untwisted) action
({\sf G},\alpha,\mathfrak{A})
where
{\sf G}
is discrete and amenable and
\mathfrak{A}
is a finite-dimensional or abelian
C^{*}
-algebra.

Then
\mathfrak{A}
satisfies all the conditions in Theorem
2.7
.

Proof.

We verify condition
(iv)
. In both cases the algebra
\mathfrak{B}
has the following property: every closed two-sided ideal
I\subset\mathfrak{B}
has a bounded approximate left-identity and so the corollary follows from the Cohen-Hewitt factorization theorem. This mentioned property follows from
[
2
, Theorem 5.3]
in the first case and from combining
[
3
, Proposition VII.2.31]
with
[
4
]
in the second.
∎

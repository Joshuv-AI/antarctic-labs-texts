1
Introduction

Let
{\cal H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
with normalized Lebesgue measure. For each
a\in\mathbb{D}
let

\varphi_{a}(z):=\displaystyle{\frac{a-z}{1-\bar{a}z}}.

Note that
\varphi_{a}\circ\varphi_{a}(z)=z
and
|\varphi_{a}(z)|=1
for
z\in\mathbb{T}
, and therefore the composition operator

C_{a}:{\cal H}\to{\cal H},\ C_{a}f=f\circ\varphi_{a}

is well defined, and is a
reflection
in
{\cal H}
:
C_{a}^{2}=I
. A reflection
C
is a
symmetry
if additionally
C^{*}=C
. Clearly
C_{a}
is a symmetry only if
a=0
. The adjoint of
C_{a}
is easily computed:

C_{a}^{*}=(1-|a|^{2})M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{2}}}C_{a}
, where
M_{g}
denotes the multiplication operator (by
g
).

There are two symmetries closely relaed to
C_{a}
. First the unitary part
R_{a}
in the polar decomposition
C_{a}=R_{a}(C_{a}^{*}C_{a})^{1/2}
of
C_{a}
(in
[
9
]
, G. Corach, H. Porta and L. Recht noted this fact, that the unitary part of a reflection is in fact a symmetry). Next the operator
W_{a}
given by
W_{a}=M_{\psi_{a}}C_{a}
, where
\psi_{a}
is the normalized Szego kernel
\psi_{a}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{1-\bar{a}z}
.

The object of this paper is the study of eigenspaces of these symmetries, and their relative position when one changes the parameter
a\in\mathbb{D}
. By relative position of two subspaces
{\cal S}
,
{\cal T}
, we mean the computation of the intersections
{\cal S}\cap{\cal T}
,
{\cal S}^{\perp}\cap{\cal T}^{\perp}
,
{\cal S}\cap{\cal T}^{\perp}
and
{\cal S}^{\perp}\cap{\cal T}
. Of particular interest to us are the last two, for their geometric significance: if these two intersections have the same dimention, it means that there exists a geodesic of the Grassmann manifold of
{\cal H}
that joins them; if they are trivial, the geodesic is unique. When the four intersections are trivial, the subspaces
{\cal S}
,
{\cal T}
are said to be in generic position.

This paper is a continuation of
[
4
]
, where composition operators with symbols
\varphi_{a}
where considered in the Hardy space of the disc. Several results otained there will be useful here.

The contents of the paper are the following. In Section 2 the eigenspaces of
C_{a}
,
R_{a}
and
W_{a}
are characterized. In this characterization, an important role is played by the unique fixed point
\omega_{a}
of
\varphi_{a}
inside
\mathbb{D}
. In Section 3 we make some brief remarks on the maps
a\mapsto C_{a}
and
a\mapsto R_{a}
. In Section 4 we obtain partial results on the relative position of the eigenspaces of these reflections induced by different
a,b\in\mathbb{D}
. The main result is Theorem 4.6, which states that for
a\neq b

N(C_{a}-I)\cap N(C_{b}-I)=\langle 1\rangle\ \hbox{ and }\ N(C_{a}+I)\cap N(C_{b}+I)=\{0\}.

In Section 5 we consider the special case of the subspaces
N(C_{a}-I)
and
N(C_{a}+I)
, where a complete study can be done. In Section 6, using the conjugation properties

R_{a}C_{a}R_{a}=C_{a}^{*},\ R_{a}C_{0}R_{a}=R_{\Omega_{a}},\ W_{a}C_{a}W_{a}=M_{\frac{k_{a}^{2}}{\|k_{a}\|_{2}^{2}}}C_{a}=M_{\frac{k_{a}}{|k_{a}|}}C_{a}^{*}\ \hbox{ and }\ W_{a}C_{0}W_{a}=W_{\Omega_{a}}

we can compute further intersections between the eigenspaces. Here
\Omega_{a}=\frac{2a}{1+|a|^{2}}
is characterized by
\omega_{\Omega_{a}}=a
, i.e.,
a
is the unique fixed point of
\varphi_{\Omega_{a}}
in
\mathbb{D}
.
In Section 7 we examine the consequence of these intersections for the geometry of the Grassmann manifold of
{\cal H}
: they determine wether given pairs of subspaces can be joined by a geodesic of this manifold.

Preliminaries and notations

If
{\cal S}
is a closed subspace of
{\cal H}
,
P_{\cal S}
will denote the orthogonal projection onto
{\cal S}
. For a bounded operator
T
,
|T|
will denote the modulus of
T
, i.e.,
|T|=(T^{*}T)^{1/2}
;
N(T)
and
R(T)
will denote the nullspace and range of
T
, respectively.

Note that

C_{a}^{*}C_{a}=(1-|a|^{2})M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{2}}}\ \hbox{ and }\ |C_{a}|=(C_{a}^{*}C_{a})^{1/2}=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}.

(1)

In
[
9
]
, Corach, Porta and Recht, made the following remarkable observation:

Remark 2.1
.

Let
C
be a reflection in
{\cal H}
, and let
C=R|C|
be its polar decomposition. Then the unitary operator
R
is in fact a symmetry.

Let us denote by
R_{a}
the symmetry obtained in the polar decomposition of
C_{a}
. After routine computations one obtains that

R_{a}=C_{a}|C_{a}|^{-1}=\frac{1}{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}C_{a}M_{|1-\bar{a}z|}=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}C_{a}.

(2)

Note that if
C^{2}=I
and
C=R|C|
is the polar decomposition, then
C^{*}=|C|R
, so that

RCR=C^{*}
. In particular, for
C=C_{a}
we have

R_{a}C_{a}R_{a}=C_{a}^{*}.

(3)

There is another symmetry in
{\cal H}
associated with
\varphi_{a}
, known in the literature (at least when acting in the (invariant) Hardy subspace
H^{2}(\mathbb{T})\subset{\cal H}
,
[
10
]
):

W_{a}=M_{\psi_{a}}C_{a}=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}M_{\frac{1}{1-\bar{a}z}}C_{a}.

(4)

Note the similarity between
R_{a}
in (
2
) and
W_{a}
in (
4
).

We shall say that an element
f\in{\cal H}
is
even
if
f(z)=f(-z)\ p.p.
in
\mathbb{T}
. Equivalently, if the Fourier coefficients
\hat{f}(n)
of
f
are
0
for
n
odd. We shall denote by
{\cal E}
the closed subspace of even elements. Similarly,
{\cal O}
will denote the subspace of
odd
elements of
{\cal H}
(
g\in{\cal H}
such that
g(-z)=-g(z)\ p.p.
in
\mathbb{T}
or
\hat{g}(n)=0
for
n
even). Note that for
a=0
,
C_{0}=R_{0}
, and
N(C_{0}-I)={\cal E}
and
N(C_{0}+I)={\cal O}
.

Finally, let
{\cal H}^{+}
be the Hardy space, as a subset of
{\cal H}
,
{\cal H}^{+}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{T})=\{f\in{\cal H}:\hat{f}(n)=0\hbox{ for }n<0\}
, identified with the Hardy space of the disk
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\{g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}:\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}<\infty\}.

A reflection
T
is completely described by its two eigenspaces
N(T-I)
and
N(T+1)
.
The object of this study is the characterization of the eigenspaces
N(T-I)
and
N(T+I)
for
T=C_{a}
,
R_{a}
or
W_{a}
, and their relative positions for different
a\in\mathbb{D}
.

3
Eigenspaces of
C_{a}
and
R_{a}

Let us first characterize the two eigenspaces
N(C_{a}\pm I)
of
C_{a}
. Note that
\varphi_{a}
has a unique fixed point
\omega_{a}
inside
\mathbb{D}
, which is given by

\omega_{a}=\frac{1}{\bar{a}}\{1-\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}\}=\frac{a}{|a|^{2}}\{1-\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}\}\ \hbox{ if }a\neq 0,\ \hbox{ and }\omega_{0}=0.

(5)

Accordingly, for
a\in\mathbb{D}
there is a unique
\Omega_{a}\in\mathbb{D}
such that the unique fixed point of
\varphi_{\Omega_{a}}
is
a
, i.e.,
\omega_{\Omega_{a}}=a
. It is given by

\Omega_{a}=\frac{2a}{1+|a|^{2}}.

(6)

Remark 3.1
.

Note the following (straightforward) formulas:

\varphi_{\omega_{a}}\circ\varphi_{a}=-\varphi_{\omega_{a}}.

(7)

and

\varphi_{a}\circ\varphi_{\omega_{a}}=-\varphi_{-\omega_{a}}.

(8)

Theorem 3.2
.

For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, we have that

N(C_{a}-I)=\{f\in{\cal H}:C_{\omega_{a}}f\in{\cal E}\}=C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal E}

and

N(C_{a}+I)=\{g\in{\cal H}:C_{\omega_{a}}g\in{\cal O}\}=C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal O}.

Proof.

The formula (
7
) implies that
if
f
is even, then

f(\varphi_{\omega_{a}})=f(-\varphi_{\omega_{a}})=f(\varphi_{\omega_{a}}\circ\varphi_{a})=C_{a}f(\varphi_{\omega_{a}}),

i.e.,
C_{\omega_{a}}f\in N(C_{a}-I)
. The computation for odd elements is similar. Thus
C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal E}\subset N(C_{a}-I)
and
C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal O}\subset N(C_{a}+I)
.

Conversely, if
f\in N(C_{a}-I)
, then
f=C_{\omega_{a}}h
for a unique
h
(equal to
C_{\omega_{a}}f
). If
h=h_{e}+h_{o}
, for
h_{e}\in{\cal E}
and
h_{o}\in{\cal O}
. Then
f=C_{\omega_{a}}(h_{e})+C_{\omega_{a}}(h_{o})
. Since
C_{\omega_{a}}h_{o}\in N(C_{a}+I)
, it must be
h_{o}=0
, and
f\in C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal E}
. The assertion for
N(C_{a}+I)
is proved analogously.

∎

Note that the idempotents (or non orthogonal projections) corresponding to the direct sum decomposition
{\cal H}=N(C_{a}-I)\dot{+}N(C_{a}+I)
are

P_{N(C_{a}-I)\parallel N(C_{a}+I)}=\frac{1}{2}(C_{a}+I)\ \hbox{ and }\ P_{N(C_{a}+I)\parallel N(C_{a}-I)}=\frac{1}{2}(I-C_{a}).

(9)

The above result implies the following:

Corollary 3.3
.

\frac{1}{2}(C_{a}+I)=C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal E}C_{\omega_{a}}\ \hbox{ and }\ \ \frac{1}{2}(I-C_{a})=C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal O}C_{\omega_{a}},

i.e.,
C_{a}=C_{\omega_{a}}C_{0}C_{\omega_{a}}
.

Proof.

C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal E}C_{\omega_{a}}
is an idempotent operator whose range is

R(C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal E}C_{\omega_{a}})=C_{\omega_{a}}R(P_{\cal E})=C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal E}=N(C_{a}-I),

and whose nullspace is

N(C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal E}C_{\omega_{a}})=C_{\omega_{a}}N(P_{\cal E})=C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal O}=N(C_{a}+I).

The other verification is similar.

\frac{1}{2}(I+C_{a})=C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal E}C_{\omega_{a}}
means that

C_{a}=2C_{\omega_{a}}P_{\cal E}C_{\omega_{a}}-I=C_{\omega_{a}}(2P_{\cal E}-I)C_{\omega_{a}}=C_{\omega_{a}}C_{0}C_{\omega_{a}}.

∎

Let us relate the eigenspaces of
C_{a}
with those of
R_{a}
.
First note the following:

Lemma 3.4
.

C_{a}|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}

and

C_{a}\frac{1}{|1-|a|^{2}|^{1/2}}=\frac{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}.

Proof.

Direct computation.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

1.

f\in N(R_{a}-I)
if and only if
|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}f\in N(C_{a}-I)
.

2.

g\in N(R_{a}+I)
if and only if
|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}g\in N(C_{a}+I)
.

Proof.

We only prove assertion 1., assertion 2. is similar. Let
f\in{\cal H}
such that
R_{a}f=f
. i.e.,

(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|}C_{a}f=f.

As in the above Lemma,
C_{a}|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}
. Then

f=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|}C_{a}f=\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}C_{a}(|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2})C_{a}f.

Note the elementary multiplicative property of
C_{a}
: if
g,h\in{\cal H}
such that
gh\in{\cal H}
, then
C_{a}(gh)=C_{a}gC_{a}h
. Then we have

f=\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}C_{a}(|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}f),

i.e.,
C_{a}(|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}f)=|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}f
.

The other inclusion: suppose that
C_{a}f=f
. Then (using Lemma
3.4
, and the multiplicative property of
C_{a}
)

R_{a}(\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}f)=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}C_{a}(\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}f)=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}C_{a}(\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}})C_{a}f

=\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}f,

which completes the proof.
∎

We may thus summarize these facts:

Corollary 3.6
.

1.

N(R_{a}-I)=\{f\in{\cal H}:C_{\omega_{a}}(|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}f)\in{\cal E}\}=M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}}C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal E}
.

2.

N(R_{a}+I)=\{g\in{\cal H}:C_{\omega_{a}}(|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}g)\in{\cal O}\}=M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2}}}C_{\omega_{a}}{\cal O}
.

Remark 3.7
.

Note that

C_{\omega_{a}}(|1-\bar{a}z|)=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}\frac{|1+\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}.

(10)

Thus
C_{\omega_{a}}(|1-\bar{a}z|^{1/2})=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/4}\frac{|1+\bar{\omega}_{a}z|^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|^{1/2}}
, and

N(R_{a}-I)=\{f\in{\cal H}:\frac{|1+\bar{\omega}_{a}z|^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|^{1/2}}f(\varphi_{\omega_{a}})\in{\cal E}\}.

(11)

Indeed,

C_{\omega_{a}}(|1-\bar{a}z|)=|1-\bar{a}\frac{\omega_{a}-z}{1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z}|=\frac{|1-\bar{a}\omega_{a}+z(\bar{a}-\bar{\omega}_{a})|}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}.

Note that if
r=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}
then

\bar{a}\omega_{a}=1-r\ \hbox{ and }\ \bar{a}-\bar{\omega}_{a}=\frac{1}{a}(|a|^{2}-(1-r))=\frac{1}{a}(r-r^{2}).

Then, in the above computation we have

\frac{|r+\frac{1}{a}(r-r^{2})z)|}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}=r\frac{|1+\frac{1}{a}(1-r)z)|}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}=r\frac{|1+\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}.

Similarly
C_{\omega_{a}}(\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|})=\frac{1}{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}\frac{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}{|1+\bar{\omega}_{a}z|}
,
and

N(C_{\omega_{a}}+I)=\{g\in{\cal H}:\frac{|1-\bar{\omega}_{a}z|^{1/2}}{|1+\bar{\omega}_{a}z|^{1/2}}g(\varphi_{\omega_{a}})\in{\cal O}\}.

(12)

Remark 3.8
.

We shall consider other symmetries which interact with
C_{a}
:

1.

Let
V
be the symmetry given by
Vf(z)=f(\bar{z})
. The eigenspaces of
V
are

N(V-I)=\{f=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}z^{m}:a_{m}=a_{-m}\}

and

N(V+I)=\{f=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}z^{m}:a_{m}=-a_{-m}\}.

This symmetry has the following commutation relation with
C_{a}
:

VC_{a}=C_{\bar{a}}V,

(13)

and in particular, if
a\in(-1,1)
,
V
and
C_{a}
commute.
Indeed,

VC_{a}f(z)=Vf(\frac{a-z}{1-\bar{a}z})=f(\frac{a-\bar{z}}{1-\bar{a}\bar{z}})=f(\frac{a-\frac{1}{z}}{1-\bar{a}\frac{1}{z}})=f(\frac{1-\bar{a}z}{a-z})=f\left(\overline{{\varphi}_{\bar{a}}(z)}\right)

=C_{\bar{a}}Vf(z).

If one identifies
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
with
L^{2}(-\pi,\pi)
, by means of
z=e^{it}
, then
N(V-I)
consists of elements
f
such that
f(t)=f(-t)\ pp
, i.e., are even in the parameter
t
. Similarly
N(V+I)
consists of elements which are odd in the parameter
t
.

2.

Recall the symmetry
W_{a}
from (
4
), defined in
{\cal H}
as

W_{a}=M_{\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}{1-\bar{a}z}}C_{a},\ \hbox{i.e., }\ W_{a}f=\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}{1-\bar{a}z}f(\varphi_{a}).

Note that
W_{a}
leaves
{\cal H}^{+}
invariant, in fact
W_{a}{\cal H}^{+}={\cal H}^{+}
. The restriction
{\bf W}_{a}=W_{a}\Big|_{{\cal H}^{+}}
was considered before, see for instance
[
10
]
(Exercise 2.1.9), where it was shown that it acts isometrically in
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
for
1<p<\infty
. It is easy to see that
W_{a}
is a symmetry in
{\cal H}
:

W_{a}^{2}f=(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}W_{a}(\frac{1}{1-\bar{a}z}f(\varphi_{a}))=\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{1-\bar{a}z}\frac{1}{1-\bar{a}\frac{a-z}{1-\bar{a}z}}f=f

and

W_{a}^{*}f=C_{a}^{*}M_{\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}{1-\bar{a}z}}^{*}f=M_{\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{2}}}C_{a}M_{\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{1-a\bar{z}}}f=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{3/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{2}}\frac{1}{1-a\frac{\bar{a}-\bar{z}}{1-a\bar{z}}}f(\varphi_{a})

=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{1-\bar{a}z}f(\varphi_{a})=W_{a}f.

Note that
W_{a}=M_{\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}{1-\bar{a}z}}C_{a}
and
R_{a}=M_{\frac{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}C_{a}=C_{a}M_{\frac{1-\bar{a}z}{\sqrt{1-|a|^{2}}}}
satisfy

W_{a}R_{a}=M_{\frac{|1-\bar{a}z|}{1-\bar{a}z}}\ \hbox{ and }\ R_{a}W_{a}=M_{\frac{1-\bar{a}z}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}.

For
a\in\mathbb{D}
, denote by
(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}
the (non continuous) bounded Borel function

(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}:=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(1-\bar{a}z)\right),\ \hbox{ for }z\in\mathbb{T}

where
\log
is the usual determination of the lograrithm:
\log(z)=\ln|z|+i\arg(z)
, with
\arg(z)\in[-\pi,\pi)
. Similarly as with the eigenspaces of
R_{a}
, we have:

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{D}
. Then

N(W_{a}-I)=\{f\in{\cal H}:\frac{f}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}\in N(C_{a}-I)\},

and

N(W_{a}+I)=\{g\in{\cal H}:\frac{g}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}\in N(C_{a}+I)\},

Proof.

The proof is similar as in Proposition
3.5
. We sketch the argument.
If
C_{a}f=f
, then

W_{a}\frac{f}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{1-\bar{a}z}f(\varphi_{a})\left(\frac{1}{1-\bar{a}\frac{a-z}{1-\bar{a}z}}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{1-\bar{a}z}\left(\frac{1-\bar{a}z}{1-|a|^{2}}\right)^{1/2}f=f.

For the reverse inclusion, note that

C_{a}(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}
. Therefore if
W_{a}f=f
, then

f=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{1-\bar{a}z}C_{a}f=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}C_{a}f=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}C_{a}(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}C_{a}f

=\frac{1}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}}C_{a}\left((1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}f\right),

i.e.,

(1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}f=C_{a}\left((1-\bar{a}z)^{1/2}f\right).

∎

4
The maps
a\mapsto C_{a}
and
a\mapsto R_{a}

Note that
C_{a}
restricts to the Hardy space
{\cal H}^{+}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Let us denote by

{\bf C}_{a}:=C_{a}\Big|_{{\cal H}^{+}}\in{\cal B}({\cal H}^{+}).

In
[
6
]
, E. Berkson proved that in the Hardy space
{\cal H}^{+}
, if
a\neq b
, one has

\|{\bf C}_{a}-{\bf C}_{b}\|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.

Therefore,
\|C_{a}-C_{b}\|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. However, note that

\||C_{a}|-|C_{b}|\|=\|(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}-(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}M_{\frac{1}{|1-\bar{b}z|}}\|=\|M_{\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}-\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}}\|

=\|\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}-\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}\|_{\infty}=\sup_{|z|=1}\Big|\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}-\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}\Big|:=\gamma_{a,b}.

It is clear that
\gamma_{a,b}\to 0
if
b\to a
, and therefore the map

\mathbb{D}\ni a\mapsto|C_{a}|\in{\cal B}({\cal H})

is continuous.

Note the following elementary estimation

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\|C_{a}-C_{b}\|=\|R_{a}M_{\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}-R_{b}M_{\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}}\|

\leq\|R_{a}M_{\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}}-R_{a}M_{\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}}\|+\|R_{a}M_{\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}}-R_{b}M_{\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}}\|

\leq\|R_{a}-R_{b}\|\|\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}\|_{\infty}+\|\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{a}z|}-\frac{(1-|b|^{2})^{1/2}}{|1-\bar{b}z|}\|_{\infty}=\|R_{a}-R_{b}\|\left(\frac{1+|a|}{1-|a|}\right)^{1/2}+\gamma_{a,b}.

By symmetry,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq\|R_{a}-R_{b}\|\min\Big\{\frac{1+|a|}{1-|a|},\frac{1+|b|}{1-|b|}\Big\}^{1/2}+\gamma_{a,b}.

In particular, since
\gamma_{a,b}\to 0
if
b\to a
, the map

\mathbb{D}\ni a\mapsto R_{a}\in{\cal B}({\cal H})

is not continuous.

5
Relative position of the eigenspaces

Denote by
P^{+}
the orthogonal projection onto
{\cal H}^{+}\subset{\cal H}
. We already noted that
C_{a}{\cal H}^{+}\subset{\cal H}^{+}
(in fact,
C_{a}{\cal H}^{+}={\cal H}^{+}
). Note that
{\cal H}^{+}
is not invariant for
C_{a}^{*}
, but for a one dimensional space. Denote by
{\cal H}^{-}:=({\cal H}^{+})^{\perp}
.

If
g\in{\cal H}^{-}
, then
\bar{g}\in{\cal H}^{+}
. Denote by
{\bf\bar{g}}
the extension of
\bar{g}
to an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

C_{a}{\cal H}^{-}=\langle 1\rangle\oplus{\cal H}^{-}.

More precisely, if
g\in{\cal H}^{-}
, then

C_{a}g=\overline{{\bf\bar{g}}(a)}+h,\ \hbox{ for }\ h\in{\cal H}^{-}.

Proof.

Let
g\in{\cal H}^{-}
and
n\geq 1
. Then

\langle C_{a}g,z^{n}\rangle=\langle g,C_{a}^{*}z^{n}\rangle=(1-|a|^{2})\langle g,\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{2}}\left(\frac{a-z}{1-\bar{a}z}\right)^{n}\rangle.

Note that if
|z|=1
, then
\frac{1}{|1-\bar{a}z|^{2}}=\frac{z}{(1-\bar{a}z)(z-a)}
. Then

\langle C_{a}g,z^{n}\rangle=-(1-|a|^{2})\langle g,\frac{z(a-z)^{n-1}}{(1-\bar{a}z)^{n+1}}\rangle=0.

Moreover,

\langle C_{a}g,1\rangle=(1-|a|^{2})\langle g,\frac{z}{(1-\bar{a}z)(z-a)}\rangle=(1-|a|^{2})\langle\frac{\bar{z}}{\bar{z}-\bar{a}},\frac{\bar{g}}{1-\bar{a}z}\rangle.

Note that (for
|z|=1
),
\frac{\bar{z}}{\bar{z}-\bar{a}}=\frac{\frac{1}{z}}{\frac{1}{z}-\bar{a}}=\frac{1}{1-\bar{a}z}=k_{a}(z)
.
Then, since
\bar{g}\in{\cal H}^{+}
,

\langle C_{a}g,1\rangle=(1-|a|^{2})\langle k_{a},\frac{\bar{g}}{1-\bar{a}z}\rangle=(1-|a|^{2})\overline{\frac{{\bf\bar{g}}(a)}{1-|a|^{2}}}=\overline{{\bf\bar{g}}(a)},

which completes the proof.
∎

As a consequence, we have the following:

Proposition 5.2
.

1.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,
f\in N(C_{a}-I)
, and
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
, with

and

f_{+},f_{-}\in N(C_{a}-I).

2.

Let

, and
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
s above. Then

C_{a}f_{+}=-f_{+}-\overline{{\bf\bar{f}_{-}}(a)}\ \ \hbox{ and }\ \ C_{a}f_{-}=-f_{-}+\overline{{\bf\bar{f}_{-}}(a)}.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{a}f=f
. Then
f_{+}+f_{-}=C_{a}f_{+}+C_{a}f_{-}
.
Note that
C_{a}f_{+}\in{\cal H}^{+}
. By Lemma
5.1
,
C_{a}f_{-}=g+k1
, for

and

. Thus

C_{a}f_{+}+g=f_{+}-k1+f_{-}\ \implies C_{a}f_{+}=f_{+}-k1.

Applying
C_{a}
to this equality, using that constant functions belong to

, and thus

. Therefore

and

.

For the case of

:

∎

Remark 6.2

8
Geometry of the Grassmann manifold of
{\cal H}

Let
Gr({\cal H}):=\{{\cal S}\subset{\cal H}:{\cal S}\hbox{ is a closed subspace of }{\cal H}\}
. In
[
13
]
and
[
9
]
, H. Porta, L. Recht and G. Corach (in the latter paper) studied the differential geometric structure of

, by identifying a subspace
{\cal S}
with the orthogonal projection
P_{\cal S}
onto
{\cal S}
, or alernatively, with the symmetry
\epsilon_{\cal S}:=P_{\cal S}-I
which is the identity at
{\cal S}
(see also
[
3
]
for an abridged survey of these results).

Let us briefly recall the facts of the geometry of
Gr({\cal H})
needed here:

Remark 8.1
.

[
13
]
,
[
9
]

1.

Gr({\cal H})
, regarded as the space of orthogonal projections, is a complemented submanifold of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. Its tangent spaces are complemented subspaces of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. Thus
Gr({\cal H})
has the Finsler metric which consists in the norm of
{\cal B}({\cal H})
at every tangent space.

2.

Additionally
Gr({\cal H})
is a homogeneous space of the unitary group
{\cal U}({\cal H})
of
{\cal H}
(as in the classical finite dimensional setting): the natural left action of (unitary) operators on closed subspaces,
U\cdot{\cal S}=U({\cal S})
, for
U\in{\cal U}({\cal H})
and
{\cal S}\in Gr({\cal H})
(which at the operator level is
U\cdot P_{\cal S}=P_{U({\cal S})}=UP_{\cal S}U^{*}
, or
U\cdot\epsilon_{\cal S}=U\epsilon_{\cal S}U^{*}
).

3.

This action admits a natural
reductive
structure, based on the notion of diagonaltiy / co-diagonality at every point
{\cal S}\in Gr({\cal H})
: an operator
A\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
is diagonal with respect to
{\cal S}
if
A{\cal S}\subset{\cal S}
and
A{\cal S}^{\perp}\subset{\cal S}^{\perp}
(i.e.,
A
commutes with
\epsilon_{\cal S}
);
B\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
si co-diagonal with respect to
{\cal S}
is
B{\cal S}\subset{\cal S}^{\perp}
and
B{\cal S}^{\perp}\subset{\cal S}
(this is equivalent to saying that
B
anti-commutes with
\epsilon_{\cal S}
). Clearly any operator in
{\cal B}({\cal H})
decomposes uniquely as an
{\cal S}
-diagonal operator plus an
{\cal S}
-co-diagonal operator. The tangent space of
Gr({\cal H})
at
{\cal S}
identifies with the space of selfadjoint operators in
{\cal B}({\cal H})
which are
{\cal S}
-codiagonal. The reductive structure induces a linear connection: if
X(t)
is a vector field which is tangent along the smooth curve
{\cal S}_{t}
in
Gr({\cal H})
for
t\in[a,b]
(i.e.,
X(t)
is a smooth path of selfadjoint operators, pointwise
{\cal S}_{t}
-co-diagonal at every
t\in[a,b]
), then the covariant derivative of
X(t)
is

\frac{DX(t)}{dt}={\cal S}_{t}-\hbox{co-diagonal part of }\frac{d}{dt}X(t),

where
\frac{d}{dt}X(t)
is the usual derivative of
X(t)
.

4.

The geodesics of this connection which start at
{\cal S}
are of the form

\gamma(t)=e^{tZ}{\cal S},

where

is
{\cal S}
-codiagonal. We shall say that a geodesic is normalized if additionally
\|Z\|\leq\pi/2
. In terms of projections, the geodesic is

, in terms of symmetries it is
\gamma(t)=e^{tZ}\epsilon_{\cal S}e^{-tZ}=e^{2tZ}\epsilon_{\cal S}
(because

\epsilon_{\cal S}
)

5.

This item was shown in
[
2
]

3

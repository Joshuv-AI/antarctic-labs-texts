CD177+ neutrophils drive extracellular matrix remodelling and HGF-alpha release in ALPPS-induced liver regeneration
Authors: Jiayan Yan, Ao Huang, Shiyu Zhang, Na Yao, Junfeng Huang, Zhifeng Jiang, Jiayi Wang, Feiyu Chen, Qichao Yu, Jianwen Cheng, Senquan Zhang, Tianyi Li, Rongshan Gao, Runze Miao, Rongkui Luo, Shaolai Zhou, Yuan Ji, Zheng Wang, Dongmei Gao, Zhenbin Ding, Zhaoyou Tang, Jia Fan, Miguel A Esteban, Hans Jürgen Schlitt, Xinrong Yang, Jian Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAssociating liver partition and portal vein ligation for staged hepatectomy (ALPPS) effectively induces rapid liver hypertrophy in patients with initially unresectable liver tumours, yet the immunological mechanisms remain unclear.ObjectiveWe aim to elucidate the immune alterations and underlying mechanisms driving liver regeneration following ALPPS.DesignThe cohort study included single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics on remnant liver tissues from ALPPS patients. Neutrophil infiltration was validated by flow cytometry and histological analyses in the world’s largest ALPPS clinical cohort and mouse ALPPS models. Functional validation, including neutrophil depletion, matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9) inhibition and CD177 blockade, as well as Cd177 knockout and CD177+ neutrophil infusion in vivo.ResultsscRNA-seq revealed substantial neutrophil infiltration following stage 1 ALPPS. Depletion of neutrophils impaired liver regeneration. Among subsets, CD177+ neutrophils were metabolically active with enhanced neutrophil extracellular traps formation and secreted MMP9. MMP9 inhibition disrupted extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation and hepatocyte growth factor alpha (HGF-α) release, impairing regeneration. CD177+ neutrophils interacted with endothelial cells via CD177–PECAM1 to facilitate transmigration, while hepatic stellate cell-derived CXCL8 promoted neutrophil chemotaxis via CXCL8–CXCR1/2. Cd177 deficiency attenuated neutrophil infiltration and regenerative growth, while CD177+ neutrophil infusion restored regeneration, which was abolished in Cd177−/− mice.ConclusionsCD177+ neutrophils drive liver regeneration by promoting endothelial transmigration, ECM degradation and HGF-α release. These findings reveal a neutrophil-mediated mechanism driving surgical liver regeneration and support the potential of CD177+ neutrophil infusion to establish a proregenerative hepatic environment for therapeutic strategies in liver failure.Keywords: HEPATOBILIARY CANCER, LIVER REGENERATION, LIVER IMMUNOLOGY, HEPATOBILIARY SURGERY

Introduction

Primary liver cancer is the fourth most common malignancy and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in China.1–3 The liver is also a common site of metastasis, accounting for nearly 25% of all metastatic cases.4 However, late-stage diagnosis renders the future liver remnant (FLR) volume in most patients insufficient for curative surgery.5 6 Associating liver partition and portal vein ligation for staged hepatectomy (ALPPS) has been reported to induce rapid hypertrophy, leading to 49–84% increase in FLR volume within 1–2 weeks, facilitating timely tumour resection and mitigating the risk of postoperative liver failure.7–11 The authors also reported the largest ALPPS cohort at that time, demonstrating that long-term survival following ALPPS was significantly better than after transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE).12 However, the mechanisms through which ALPPS modulates the hepatic immune microenvironment to facilitate regeneration remain poorly understood.

ALPPS induces key physiological changes, including increased portal flow and pressure, vascular shear stress, FLR hypoxia and systemic inflammation, which are collectively driving rapid liver regeneration.13–15 This regenerative process depends on complex interactions between parenchymal and non-parenchymal cells, with immune microenvironment changes activating pivotal mediators in the regenerative cascade, such as hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) and interleukin-6 (IL-6).16 17 Understanding these cellular dynamics and signalling events is crucial for elucidating regeneration mechanisms and improving therapeutic outcomes.

Neutrophils infiltrate various tissues under normal and pathological conditions,18 19 with neutrophil migration to injury and inflammation sites representing a key host defence mechanism.20 21 In liver-associated diseases such as ischaemia–reperfusion injury, fibrosis and cirrhosis, neutrophils contribute to both tissue damage and repair.22 23 These cells differentiate into functionally distinct and specifically preprogrammed subpopulations. Local microenvironmental signals can reprogram neutrophil behaviour, thereby modulating their functional properties.24 25 Recent studies have shown that neutrophils promote liver regeneration by inducing regenerative phenotypes in monocytes/macrophages, facilitating efferocytosis of apoptotic extracellular vesicles and secreting HGF.26–29 Despite these advances, the functional heterogeneity of neutrophils and the identity of specific subtypes that contribute to liver regeneration remain unclear and warrant further exploration.

Here, we combined single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics to comprehensively profile immune microenvironment changes in the remnant liver during ALPPS. Our findings uncover a previously unrecognised role for CD177+ neutrophils in promoting ALPPS-induced liver regeneration. This study lays a foundation for neutrophil-targeted strategies to establish a proregenerative hepatic environment for therapeutic strategies in liver failure.

Results

Single-cell and multiomics profiling revealed neutrophil expansion during ALPPS-induced liver regeneration

The retrospective cohort study included a discovery cohort, comprising six paired FLR samples from three ALPPS patients for scRNA-seq and bioinformatic analysis (figure 1A, online supplemental S1A). Additionally, three independent validation cohorts were included (figure 1A). Validation cohort 1 comprised 150 formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks for immunohistochemistry (IHC) and immunofluorescence (IF) staining, along with 450 whole blood samples from 75 ALPPS patients (online supplemental table 1). Validation cohort 2 included bulk RNA sequencing data (n=10, paired) from 10 ALPPS patients (online supplemental table 2). Validation cohort 3 involved spatial transcriptomic profiling using SpaTial Enhanced REsolution Omics-sequencing (Stereo-seq) (n=3, paired) from two ALPPS patients (online supplemental table 3).

CT-based three-dimensional reconstructions showed a 62.2% increase in FLR volume (from 404.7±54.8 mL to 656.4±75.8 mL) within 14.7 days after stage 1 of three ALPPS patients from the discovery cohort (p<0.001, online supplemental figure S1B,C). scRNA-seq was performed on paired FLR tissues (n=6) from the discovery cohort, yielding a total of 24 647 high-quality cells. Unsupervised clustering identified 19 cell subpopulations, with cells from both stages represented in each cluster (figure 1B). Differential gene expression analysis classified these populations into nine cell types, namely cholangiocytes (KRT19, KRT7, EPCAM), endothelial cells (CLDN5, KDR, VWF, PECAM1), hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) (COL3A1, COL1A1, DCN), hepatocytes (APOC3, HP, ALB), monocytes/macrophages (CD1C, VCAN, CLEC10A, C1QC, C1QB, C1QA), myofibroblasts (MYL9, MYH11), neutrophils (G0S2, CSF3R), plasma cells (MYZB1, JCHAIN, CD79A) and T cells (TRBC2, CD3G, CD3E, CD3D) (figure 1B,C). Comparison of cell type proportions across the two ALPPS stages revealed increased infiltration of neutrophils and plasma cells, with neutrophils showing the most pronounced expansion among immune subsets (figure 1D). Specifically, the proportion of neutrophils within the myeloid compartment rose significantly from 44.4% to 71.1% (p<0.01, figure 1E). Immunohistochemical staining for myeloperoxidase (MPO) confirmed substantial neutrophil infiltration during ALPPS-induced regeneration (figure 1F). Complete blood count analysis in the validation cohort 1 further supported this finding, revealing an early increase in circulating neutrophil count. The neutrophil-to-white blood cell ratio rose from 60.4% to 83.5% within a day following the first ALPPS stage (figure 1G, online supplemental figure S1D).

Neutrophils facilitated liver regeneration in ALPPS mouse models

To validate the role of neutrophils in ALPPS-induced regeneration, we established mouse models with 10% liver remnant, mimicking clinical regenerative burden.30 31 Liver tissues were collected on postoperative days (PODs) 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 7 and 10. Regeneration was assessed using FLR-to-body weight (BW) ratio, which showed an increasing trend from POD0 (0.65%) to POD10 (2.60%) (figure 2A–C). IHC staining for Ki67 showed peak hepatocyte proliferation (∼70%) on POD3 (figure 2D, online supplemental figure S2A). These findings confirmed robust regeneration and underscored the models’ relevance for mechanistic studies.

Flow cytometry of mouse FLR single-cell suspensions revealed neutrophil dynamics post-ALPPS. In CD45-positive immune cells, neutrophil count increased from 1.4% (POD0) to 7.3% (POD3; p<0.001), indicating early immune recruitment. CD177+ cell count within neutrophils and peripheral blood peaked at 62.7% on POD2 (p<0.001), followed by a gradual decline (figure 2E,F). Circulating neutrophil count increased from 29.6% to 78.7% within 2 days post-ALPPS, with CD177-positive neutrophil count increasing from 28.3% to 82.2% within 2 days (online supplemental figure S2B).

Neutrophils were depleted through intravenous administration of anti-Ly6G antibodies (50 µg) to the mice 1 day before surgery and intraoperative, followed by daily postoperative doses of 100 µg per 25 g BW. Mice receiving isotype IgG2a served as controls (figure 2G). The anti-Ly6G group showed reduced 7-day survival (57.2%) compared with controls (87.1%; p<0.01; figure 2H). Ly6G-positive cells/high-power field (HPF, 400×) decreased from 13.9 to 2.4 (p<0.001; figure 2I,J). Hepatocyte proliferation was also markedly impaired in the neutrophil-depleted group, which exhibited significantly lower Ki67-positive rate (13.4%) than the control group (71.4%) on POD3 (p<0.001; figure 2I,J). FLR/BW ratio in the anti-Ly6G group reduced to 1.7%, compared with 2.6% in controls on POD7 (p<0.01; figure 2I and K). Furthermore, analysis of an external public data set (CNP0002310)32 revealed an enrichment of CD177+ neutrophils on Day 3 after 2/3 hepatectomy (online supplemental figure S3A). Consistently, in our 2/3 hepatectomy model, neutrophil depletion markedly reduced the proportion of proliferating hepatocytes (6.6% vs 22.7%, p<0.001; online supplemental figure S3B–D) and the FLR/BW ratio (4.6% vs 5.3%, p<0.05; online supplemental figure S3B–D).

Thus, although ALPPS induced FLR hyperplasia in mouse models, neutrophil depletion significantly impaired both hepatocyte proliferation and FLR growth. These results collectively underscored the critical role of neutrophils in ALPPS-induced liver regeneration.

Identification of G4 neutrophils (CD177+) as a key driver of ALPPS-induced liver regeneration

scRNA-seq revealed marked heterogeneity among FLR neutrophils before and after ALPPS, suggesting functionally distinct subpopulations involved in regeneration and surgical response. Clustering analysis of 4168 neutrophils identified seven clusters, consolidated into four subtypes: G4 (CD177, CAPG, CLEC4D, FCN1, MMP9, S100A12), G5b (IFI6, IFIT3, IFIT2), G5c (IL1B, OSM, TNFAIP3, CXCL8) and G5b_c (MME, DPEP2) (figure 3A,B, online supplemental S4A).

Comparative analysis of neutrophil subtypes across ALPPS stages revealed that G4 neutrophil count exhibited the most significant increase within the myeloid compartment, from 11.5% to 19.3% (p<0.01, figure 3C). Pseudotime trajectory analysis positioned G4 neutrophils near the root of the differentiation axis, suggesting a progenitor-like state, whereas G5b_c cells localised near the terminal end, reflecting a mature phenotype. The G5b and G5c subtypes occupied intermediate positions, likely representing transitional states within the neutrophil differentiation continuum (figure 3D). G4 neutrophils also demonstrated increased expressions of several secreted proteins and receptor genes, including MMP9, S100A12, ARG1, CD177, GRN and G6PD (figure 3E). Gene set enrichment analysis revealed that G4 neutrophils were metabolically active, with enhanced activity across carbohydrate, fatty acid and amino acid metabolism pathways (figure 3F). Upregulated genes in G4 neutrophils were significantly enriched in metabolic pathways, including glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, amino acid biosynthesis and fatty acid biosynthesis and metabolism, as well as in pathways associated with tissue repair, such as vascular endothelial growth factor signalling, extracellular matrix (ECM) disassembly and metallopeptidase activity (figure 3G). Quantitative set analysis for gene expression (QuSAGE) analysis of scRNA-seq data revealed that G4 neutrophils exhibited enhanced activity of pathways related to neutrophil degranulation and extracellular trap formation (online supplemental figures S5A–C). Notably, the neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation score was further elevated at stage 2 compared with stage 1. In vitro, CD177+ neutrophils also showed increased NET formation, as evidenced by higher levels of single-stranded DNA (ssDNA), MPO and neutrophil elastase (NE) detected by ELISA (online supplemental figure S5D). These findings suggest that, beyond their proregenerative role, CD177+ neutrophils display enhanced NET formation capacity. During ALPPS, CD177+ neutrophils showed upregulation of both proinflammatory (IL1B, CCL5, CCL4, CD69) and proregenerative factors (matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9), OSM, CXCL8) (online supplemental figure S6A,B). In 2/3 hepatectomy scRNA-seq data, G4 neutrophils expressed proinflammatory genes (Hmgb1, Il1b, Ccl4) early (8 hours–2 days), and more proregenerative genes (Il6ra, Vim, Csf1) later (3–7 days; online supplemental figure S6C,D). Thus, CD177+ neutrophils shift from a pro-inflammatory to a proregenerative state during liver regeneration.

The flow cytometry analysis of human FLR single-cell suspensions revealed CD177+ neutrophils proportion among immune cells increased significantly after ALPPS (n=3, paired; p<0.05, online supplemental figure S6E,F). Furthermore, IF staining for MPO and MMP9 in the entire cohort 1 demonstrated a 66.4% increase in G4 neutrophils (MMP9+MPO+ cells) infiltration (p<0.001, figure 3H).

Together, these findings identify CD177+ neutrophils as a metabolically active, proregenerative subset with enhanced NETs formation that expands after ALPPS and likely promotes FLR regeneration.

CD177-positive neutrophils promote liver regeneration via MMP9-mediated ECM remodelling and HGF-α release

FLR samples (n=10, paired) from both ALPPS stages in validation cohort 2 were subjected to bulk RNA sequencing. Transcriptomic analysis showed significant enrichment of stage 2-upregulated genes in gene ontology terms related to leucocyte migration involved in inflammatory response, ECM disassembly, ECM organisation and neutrophil migration (online supplemental figure S7A). Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway analysis further demonstrated enhanced metabolic activity and regenerative pathways were significantly enriched in stage 2 tissues, underscoring the active regenerative state following ALPPS (online supplemental figure S7A).

scRNA-seq analysis of neutrophil-derived secreted proteins identified high expressions of MMP9, S100A9 and PGLYRP1 in the G4 subset (figure 4A,B, online supplemental figure S7B–E). Notably, MMP9 plays a central role in ECM remodelling and tissue regeneration.33 34 ELISA revealed significantly higher MMP9 secretion by CD177+ than CD177− neutrophils in both FLR and blood (figure 4C).

To validate the role of MMP9 in neutrophil-mediated liver regeneration in vitro, we used a three-dimensional liver organoid model based on nucleic acid nanostructure (NAC)-decorated spheroids. The organoids comprised primary human hepatocytes, liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and primary human HSCs (figure 4D).35 Neutrophils (∼5000 cells) were introduced into the culture on Days 0 and 2. The recombinant chimeric IgG4 anti-MMP9 monoclonal antibody Andecaliximab (10 or 50 µg/mL) was administered on Days –1, 0, 2 and 4 to inhibit MMP9. Liver organoids were harvested on Day 3 (for (H&E) and multiplexed IF staining of the largest cross-section) and Day 6 (for ATP quantification and further H&E analysis). Neutrophil supplementation significantly increased the ATP levels (p<0.05) and average cross-sectional area of the organoids from 7177.8 µm² to 11 098.3 µm² by Day 6 (p<0.01; figure 4E–H, online supplemental figure S8A–E). Andecaliximab treatment at both doses significantly suppressed ATP production and inhibited organoid growth by Day 6 (all p<0.01), confirming the essential role of MMP9 in neutrophil-induced liver regeneration in vitro (figure 4E–H).

Anti-Ly6G antibody treatment significantly reduced the MMP9 and proliferating cell nuclear antigen expression levels in FLR tissues by POD3 in ALPPS (10% FLR) mouse models (online supplemental figure S8F). To further evaluate the role of neutrophil-derived MMP9 in vivo, we administered the selective MMP9 inhibitor MMP9-IN-135 36 to the ALPPS models (figure 4I) both intraoperatively and on POD1, POD2 and POD5. MMP9-IN-1 treatment significantly suppressed MMP9 activity involved in ECM degradation without affecting neutrophil infiltration (p=0.39; online supplemental figure S8G). Importantly, MMP9 inhibition markedly reduced hepatocyte proliferation, with Ki67-positive cell count dropping from 81.5% to 10.4% on POD3. Similarly, the FLR/BW ratio declined from 2.3% to 1.6% on POD7 (both p<0.001; figure 4J,K). ELISA showed that MMP9-IN-1 significantly suppressed the increase in HGF by POD3 (p<0.05), confirming the roles of MMP9 in HGF release, hepatocyte proliferation and FLR growth (figure 4L,M). MMP9 not only mediates ECM remodelling but also facilitates the release of HGF-α, a key mitogenic signal in liver regeneration.37–40 Compartmental protein extraction41 revealed that HGF-α was predominantly localised within the ECM of FLR tissues on POD3 following ALPPS (10% FLR) (online supplemental figure S8H). Furthermore, HGF levels in the FLR decreased significantly in the anti-Ly6G group compared with the IgG2a controls on POD3 (p<0.05; figure 5A). Compartmental protein analysis confirmed that neutrophil depletion impaired ECM-associated accumulation of HGF-α (figure 5B).

In the NAC-liver spheroid model, neutrophil supplementation and Andecaliximab treatment (50 µg/mL) were administered (figure 5C), as described previously.35 On Day 3, several indicators were assessed, including total soluble collagen in liver organoid lysates, as well as HGF and hydroxyproline (HYP) in the culture supernatant. Additionally, HGF-α IHC staining was performed on the slide representing the largest cross-section of the liver organoid and subsequently quantified. Neutrophil supplementation significantly enhanced matrix remodelling and HGF release. Specifically, the soluble collagen content increased markedly (p<0.001), similar to HYP levels in the supernatant (p<0.05), indicating elevated collagen hydrolysis. In parallel, HGF levels in the supernatant and HGF-α expression area ratio in liver organoids exhibited a significant increase (p<0.001 and p<0.05, respectively), supporting increased ECM degradation and HGF-α releasing (figure 5C–E, online supplemental figure S9A–C). These effects were restrained by Andecaliximab treatment. Simultaneous decreases were noted in the soluble collagen levels in organoid lystages, HYP and HGF concentration into the supernatant (all p<0.001) and HGF-α expression area ratio (p<0.05) (figure 5C–E, online supplemental figure S9A–C). Furthermore, in vivo HGF-α administration markedly restored the reduced proportion of Ki67+ hepatocytes caused by anti-Ly6G blockade (39.4% vs 11.7%, p<0.01) and MMP9-IN-1 treatment (51.2% vs 15.9%, p<0.001) in ALPPS models (figure 5F–H, online supplemental figure S9D–H). In vivo MMP9 administration also rescued hepatocyte proliferation impaired by anti-Ly6G blockade in ALPPS models on POD3 (online supplemental figure S10A–C). These findings confirmed that MMP9 facilitates liver regeneration by promoting ECM degradation, enhancing collagen hydrolysis and enabling HGF-α release in vitro.

Collectively, the data indicated that CD177-positive neutrophils contribute to ALPPS-induced liver regeneration through MMP9 secretion, which drives HGF-α release via ECM degradation.

Endothelial–neutrophil crosstalk via CD177–PECAM1 facilitates ECM degradation during ALPPS-induced liver regeneration

Evidence indicates that LSECs actively interact with neutrophils during various liver pathologies, including liver injury, metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease and portal hypertension.42–44 To explore their role in ALPPS, scRNA-seq data were subjected to CellPhoneDB analysis, revealing over 61 significant ligand–receptor interactions between neutrophils and endothelial cells (online supplemental figure S11A). Among neutrophil subtypes, CD177-positive neutrophils showed the strongest interaction with endothelial cells via the CD177–CD31 axis (online supplemental figure S11B, S5I). Transwell assays confirmed that known neutrophil chemoattractant CXCL8 promoted CD177+ neutrophil migration through LSECs (p<0.001). Blocking CD177 or CD31 significantly attenuated migration (p<0.001 for both) and combined blockade had an additive effect (figure 5J; both p<0.05). Spatial transcriptomics using Stereo-seq (validation cohort 3, n=3, paired) confirmed G4 neutrophil enrichment near endothelial cells, with colocalisation reaching nearly 0.27% of all cells within a 25 µm diameter area in stage 2, significantly exceeding other neutrophil subsets (figure 5K–M).

Together, these findings indicated that CD177–CD31 interaction drives CD177+ neutrophil recruitment to endothelial cells, promoting tissue repair during ALPPS.

Enhanced neutrophil chemotaxis via CXCL8-CXCR1/CXCR2 axis during ALPPS-induced liver regeneration

scRNA-seq revealed marked upregulation of chemokine signalling and receptor interactions in stage 2 FLR, likely driving increased neutrophil recruitment. QuSAGE analysis of scRNA-seq data revealed enhanced neutrophil trafficking signatures in HSCs and cholangiocytes (figure 6A). Enhanced CXCL1/CXCL8-CXCR1/CXCR2 signalling was found between HSCs and neutrophils, along with high CXCL1/CXCL8 expression in HSCs (figure 6B–D, online supplemental S11C). To validate the chemotactic role of CXCL1/CXCL8, in vitro migration assays were conducted, which confirmed that both CXCL1 (300 ng/mL) and CXCL8 (100 ng/mL) promoted CD177+ neutrophil migration, with CXCL8 showing a stronger effect (figure 6E). Blocking CXCR1 (1 µM) or CXCR2 (400 nM) significantly reduced CXCL8-induced neutrophil migration (p<0.001 and p<0.01), with combined inhibition showing greater suppression (both p<0.05; figure 6D). Further scRNA-seq identified eight HSC subclusters, with C3 showing the highest CXCL8 expression and defined as the CXCL8+ HSC population (figure 6F). Stereo-seq mapping demonstrated that G4 neutrophils localised near the C3 HSCs subcluster, with a maximum colocalisation rate of approximately 0.19% of all cells in stage 2, significantly exceeding other neutrophil subsets (validation cohort 3, n=3, paired; figure 6G,H). Collectively, these findings indicated that CXCL8+ HSCs drive neutrophil chemotaxis via the CXCL8–CXCR1/2 axis, facilitating CD177+ neutrophil infiltration and promoting FLR regeneration in ALPPS.

CD177 loss impaired neutrophil function and liver regeneration through MMP9–HGF-α axis disruption

To further validate the essential role of CD177+ neutrophils in liver regeneration, the effect of CD177+ neutrophil was examined in NAC-liver spheroids. CD177+ neutrophils (∼5000 cells) were added to the culture medium of NAC-liver organoids on Days 0 and 2. Anti-CD177 antibodies (10 µg/mL) were administered on Days –1, 0, 2 and 4. The results showed significant increases in the total soluble collagen levels in organoid lysates, HYP release and HGF concentrations in the supernatant (all p<0.01) and HGF-α expression area ratio (p<0.05) simultaneously on Day 3 after CD177+ neutrophil supplementation, indicating enhanced fibrillar collagen hydrolysis and HGF-α activation and release within the ECM (figure 7A–E, online supplemental figure S12A). CD177 blockade significantly reduced total soluble collagen (p<0.05), HYP release (p<0.01), HGF concentration (p<0.05) and HGF-α release (p<0.01, figure 7A–E, online supplemental figure S11A), while suppressing ATP production on POD3 and POD6 (both p<0.001) and impairing NAC-liver organoid growth, as reflected by reduced cross-sectional area on H&E staining on POD6 (p<0.01; online supplemental figure S12B,C, figure 7F,G). These results confirmed that CD177 enhances liver regeneration in vitro by promoting ECM degradation, collagen hydrolysis and HGF release and activation into HGF-α.

To further validate the essential role of CD177+ neutrophils in liver regeneration, we generated a Cd177 knockout mouse model using CRISPR/Cas9 technology, targeting exons 3–17 (figure 7H, online supplemental S13A). Both knockout and wild-type (WT) mice underwent ALPPS surgery (figure 7I). MMP9 positive neutrophil infiltration in FLR tissues on POD3 dropped from 9.0 to 0.6/HPF in knockout mice (p<0.01; online supplemental figure S13B), with hepatocyte proliferation decreasing from 81.6% to 32.4% (p<0.001, figure 7K). Consistently, the FLR-to-BW ratios decreased significantly in Cd177 knockout mice compared with WT mice on POD7 after ALPPS (1.6% vs 2.2%, p<0.01) (figure 7J and L). MMP9 protein levels in FLR tissues substantially decreased in Cd177 knockout mice on POD3 (online supplemental figure S13C). Compartmental protein extraction revealed reduced levels of ECM-associated HGF-α levels in knockout mice (online supplemental figure S13D), indicating that disrupted ECM degradation in the absence of CD177+ neutrophil infiltration impaired HGF-α activation and release. CD177+ neutrophils were isolated from LPS- and surgery-treated mice and injected daily via tail vein and intraoperatively at 100 000 cells per 25 g BW (online supplemental materials and methods, figure 7I). Neutrophil injection significantly increased MMP9 neutrophil infiltration (p<0.001), restored hepatocyte proliferation ratio (p<0.01, figure 7H–L). Specifically, CD177+ neutrophil injection could significantly restore the FLR-to-BW ratios of Cd177 knockout mice from 1.6% to 2.0% (p<0.05, figures 7J and 7L). In vivo MMP9 administration also rescued hepatocyte proliferation impaired in Cd177 knockout ALPPS mice on POD3 (online supplemental figure S14A–C). Similarly, the FLR-to-BW ratios decreased significantly in Cd177 knockout mice compared with WT mice on POD7 after 2/3 hepatectomy (3.4% vs 4.8%, p<0.01; online supplemental figure S14D–E). These findings confirm that CD177+ neutrophils or MMP9 administration promotes liver regeneration and may have potential therapeutic applications in liver failure.

Multiplex IF staining on FFPE liver tissues (n=75, paired) revealed a significant increase in CD177+CD66B+ neutrophils and MMP9+CD66B+ neutrophils which can both represent G4 neutrophils in stage 2 compared with stage 1 FLR tissues (both p<0.001; figure 7M,N, online supplemental figure S14F). Spatial neighbourhood analysis of endothelial cells revealed enrichment of CD177+CD66B+ neutrophils near endothelial cells, with colocalisation peaking at ∼148 within 0–10 µm range, significantly higher than in stage 1 (p<0.05, figure 7N).

These results underscored CD177+ neutrophils as key mediators of ALPPS-induced liver regeneration. The liver organoid model effectively recapitulated the mechanism by which MMP9 secretion from CD177+ neutrophils promotes ECM degradation, leading to HGF-α activation and release and ultimately driving hepatocyte proliferation (figure 8).

Discussion

ALPPS is a novel surgical approach that induces rapid liver regeneration, enabling curative resection of previously unresectable liver tumours and has been widely adopted worldwide over the past decade. Substantial alterations within the hepatic microenvironment drive rapid regenerative responses following ALPPS.39 40 45 We recently found early infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages during ALPPS-triggered regeneration.46 47 Here, we provide evidence for the critical role of CD177+ neutrophils in promoting hepatocyte proliferation and liver regeneration following ALPPS. Mechanistically, neutrophil recruitment into hepatic sinusoids was mediated by two coordinated pathways: chemotaxis via the CXCL8–CXCR1/CXCR2 axis and direct cellular interaction with endothelial cells through the CD177–PECAM1 axis. Once localised in the liver, CD177+ neutrophils secreted MMP9, which facilitated ECM remodelling through the hydrolytic degradation of collagen fibres, leading to HGF-α release, thereby promoting hepatocyte proliferation and FLR enlargement after stage 1 ALPPS.

Neutrophils rapidly respond to injury and contribute to liver pathologies like ischaemia–reperfusion, fibrosis and cirrhosis.20–23 Their functions are highly heterogeneous and shaped by the surrounding microenvironment.24 25 However, the functional diversity and specific contributions of neutrophil subpopulations in liver regeneration, particularly in the setting of ALPPS-induced rapid regeneration, remain poorly understood. In this study, circulating and FLR-infiltrating neutrophils increased early after ALPPS, with infiltration evident by Day 2 in mice. Neutrophil depletion significantly suppressed hepatocyte proliferation, which peaked on POD3, and impaired FLR growth within the first 7 days. These findings underscore the essential role of neutrophils in initiating and sustaining early regenerative responses following ALPPS.

Pseudotime analysis identified G4 neutrophils, especially the CD177+ subset, as key regenerative responders to ALPPS. These CD177+ neutrophils exhibited enhanced metabolism, with upregulated glycolysis, fatty acid synthesis and the tricarboxylic acid cycle pathways. This metabolic reprogramming may provide energy and biosynthetic precursors required for liver regeneration. On the other hand, CD177+ neutrophils have also been shown to exhibit enhanced NET formation, which may exert intensified inflammation on liver tissue. MMP9 emerged as the most significantly upregulated gene in CD177+ neutrophils compared with others. MMPs, including MMP9 and MMP2, are well-established mediators of ECM remodelling and are also involved in the activation and processing of various cytokines and growth factors.48 In vivo experiments using ALPPS models demonstrated that inhibiting MMP9 in ALPPS mice reduced hepatocyte proliferation, FLR growth and HGF production. Compartmental protein analysis revealed localisation of HGF-α predominantly in the ECM in the early stage of liver regeneration, coinciding with increased ECM degradation. Notably, significant reductions in HGF-α levels following the administration of anti-Ly6G antibodies highlight the pivotal role of neutrophils, particularly the CD177+ subset, in orchestrating ECM remodelling and HGF-α release during liver regeneration.

This study also elucidates the mechanisms underlying CD177+ neutrophil infiltration into hepatic sinusoids, mediated by CD177–PECAM1 (CD31) signalling between CD177+ neutrophil subsets and endothelial cells in stage 2 ALPPS. This interaction promotes firm adhesion of neutrophils via a β2 integrin-independent mechanism. CD177–CD31-mediated infiltration was confirmed through both in vitro transwell assays and in vivo Cd177 knockout mouse models. Importantly, CD177+ neutrophils markedly restored hepatocyte proliferation and FLR growth in Cd177−/− ALPPS mice, suggesting potential therapeutic applications. Additionally, our findings suggest that the CXCL8–CXCR1/CXCR2 axis plays a key role in guiding CD177+ neutrophil migration during liver regeneration. Unlike its known role in inflammation, CXCL8’s spatial function in ALPPS-driven regeneration was unclear.49 50 Addressing this gap, we identified HSCs as a key CXCL8 producer, highlighting that stromal-immune crosstalk coordinates neutrophil localisation. This suggests a more targeted recruitment process than previously recognised.51 52 Moreover, spatial transcriptomics revealed colocalisation of CD177+ neutrophils with endothelial cells and CXCL8+ HSCs, underscoring immune–stromal coordination. This targeted infiltration promoted ECM degradation through MMP9 secretion and facilitated the release of HGF-α, thus promoting liver regeneration.

This study also has several limitations. First, due to the limited yield of viable single-cell suspensions from the FLR samples, only three paired scRNA-seq samples and limited counts of major cell types could be obtained; therefore, spatial transcriptomics was employed to complement and validate these findings. The largest ALPPS cohort in the world is also applied to validate CD177+ neutrophil enrichment and its interaction with endothelial cells during ALPPS. Second, poor hepatocyte viability due to dissociation-induced cell death in scRNA-seq data limited the analysis of hepatocyte heterogeneity.53 Finally, functional validation of ECM degradation was limited by technical challenges in isolating and characterising specific cellular components involved in this process, warranting further investigations into neutrophil-regulated ECM degradation mechanisms.

In conclusion, this study highlights the critical role of CD177+ neutrophils in ALPPS-induced liver regeneration, attributed to their high MMP9 expression that facilitates ECM degradation and HGF-α release. This study also revealed that CD177–PECAM1 interactions and CXCL8-mediated chemotaxis drive neutrophil recruitment into the liver during ALPPS. These findings offer insights into the regeneration process, opening new avenues for clinical interventions, such as infusing immune components (eg, CD177+ neutrophils or growth-promoting factors like CXCL8 and MMP9) to establish a proregenerative hepatic environment and improve outcomes in patients with underlying liver diseases or impaired regenerative capacity. However, the use of CD177+ neutrophils in specific populations should be carefully considered, given the potential risk of unintended activation and NET-mediated liver injury. Future research should focus on neutrophil subpopulations across different liver diseases and the effects of changes in their infiltration and activity on liver regeneration outcomes.

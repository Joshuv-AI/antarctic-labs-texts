A window to the Bruhat order on the affine symmetric group
Salim Rostam

Abstract
Given two affine permutations, some results of Lascoux and Deodhar, and independently Jacon–Lecouvey, allow to decide if they are comparable for the strong Bruhat order. These permutations are associated with tuples of core partitions, and the preceding problem is equivalent to compare the Young diagrams in each components for the inclusion. Using abaci, we give an easy rule to compute these Young diagrams one another. We deduce a procedure to compare, for the Bruhat order, two affine permutations in the window notation.



1
Introduction

Given a Coxeter system
(W,S)
, the (strong) Bruhat order
\unlhd
is defined by
w\unlhd w^{\prime}
if and only if a reduced expression of
w
can be obtained as a subword of a reduced expression of
w^{\prime}
. A finer version is given by the weak Bruhat order, where “subword” is replaced by “suffix”. The notion of Bruhat order was first introduced by Ehresmann
[
Eh
]
in the context of Schubert varieties, and is still much studied (see, for instance,
[
ChGo
,
EKLP
,
HuPy
,
NiRo
]
).

In the case where
W
is the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{e}
of index
e\geq 2
, it is natural to ask whether
\sigma,\rho\in\mathfrak{S}_{e}
are comparable for the Bruhat order from the data of
\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(e)
and
\rho(1),\dots,\rho(e)
, instead of dealing with reduced expressions. A celebrated criterion is the “tableau criterion” (see for instance
[
Eh
,
BB96a
]
) which uses the inclusion order on subsets of
\{1,\dots,e\}
obtained by reordering the
j
-tuples
(\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(j))
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
. For the weak Bruhat order, such an interpretation uses inclusion of
inversion sets
, see for instance
[
YaOk
, Proposition 2.1]
or
[
BB05
, Chapter 3]
.

We now focus on the
affine
symmetric group
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
. Restricting to some particular subsets of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
called
affine Grassmannians
, Lascoux
[
La
]
gave a criterion that translates the notion of Bruhat order into inclusion of Young diagrams, where the underlying partitions are
e
-cores
. This notion of
e
-cores appears in the modular representation theory of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{e}
(see for instance
[
JaKe
]
). Deodhar
[
De
]
showed how to deduce the Bruhat order on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
from the Bruhat order on all affine Grassmannianns. Note that Jacon–Lecouvey
[
JL20
, §5.4]
obtained, using crystals theory, the same rule via reduction to the type A case.

Lascoux’s construction uses reduced expressions, hence it is natural to ask for a simple criteria using the data of
\sigma(1),\dots,\sigma(e)
for
\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
(these values determine uniquely
\sigma\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
), which is called the
window notation
. Note that such a, quite complex, rule can be found in
[
BB96b
, §6]
. Concerning the weak Bruhat order, there is a nice generalisation of the criteria for the weak Bruhat order in
\mathfrak{S}_{e}
, via inclusions of multigraphs (see
[
BB96b
, §5]
).

In this paper, we will give a simple rule from the window notation to decide whether two affine permutations are comparable for the Bruhat order.
It will first consists in computing the
e
-core partition of Lascoux
[
La
]
, then adding certain
rim hooks
and finally deleting the first column. A key point is to use the representation on integer partitions via
abaci
, where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
acts in a very simple way. More precisely, if
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is associated with the
e
-tuple of abaci
(A_{0},\dots,A_{e-1})
and the
e
-tuple of charged partitions
(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(e-1)})
then in Proposition
3.9
we first explicit the abacus
A_{0}
.

Proposition A
.

We have
A_{0}=\sqcup_{i=0}^{e-1}\bigl(w(i)+e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}\bigr)
.

Then in Proposition
4.8
we show how to construct each
A_{\mathsf{c}}
from
A_{\mathsf{c}-1}
.

Proposition B
.

Let
\mathsf{c}\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
. The abacus
A_{\mathsf{c}}
is obtained from
A_{\mathsf{c}-1}
by adding a bead at
w(\mathsf{c})
.

Now our main result Theorem
4.18
converts the above result in the context of partitions.

Theorem C
.

For any
\mathsf{c}\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
, the
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-core
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}
can be constructed from the
(\mathsf{c}-1)
-charged
e
-core
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c}-1)}
in the following way.

1)

Construct the partition
\mu_{\mathsf{c}-1,\mathsf{c}}
obtained from
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c}-1)}
by adding the smallest rim hook with the following properties:

•

it has only one node below the first column of the Young diagram of
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c}-1)}
,

•

it has hand reside
w(\mathsf{c})-1
.

2)

Remove the first column of
\mu_{\mathsf{c}-1,\mathsf{c}}
.

Now combining results of Lascoux
[
La
]
and Deodhar
[
De
]
, we know that two affine permutations
w,w^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
satisfy
w\unlhd w^{\prime}
if and only if the Young diagram of
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}
is included in the Young diagram of
\lambda^{\prime(\mathsf{c})}
for all
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, where
w
(resp.
w^{\prime}
) is associated to
(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(e-1)})
(resp.
(\lambda^{\prime(0)},\dots,\lambda^{\prime(e-1)})
). Hence, using Proposition
A
and Theorem
C
, given
w,w^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
we have a procedure to decide whether
w\unlhd w^{\prime}
from the knowledge of
(w(1),\dots,w(e))
and
(w^{\prime}(1),\dots,w^{\prime}(e))
, that is, from the window notations of
w
and
w^{\prime}
.

We now give the outline of the paper. In Section
2
we recall basic definitions concerning charged integer partitions, their abaci and the
e
-core property. Section
3
recalls some known facts about the affine symmetric group
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, for instance, its natural action on the set of
e
-cores. Namely, in Proposition
3.9
we explicit the abacus associated with any element
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
. In §
3.3
and §
3.4
, we study particular subsets of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, the
affine Grassmannians
. The assertions there are meticulously proved, since they are used in Lascoux
[
La
]
sometimes implicitly. For instance, in Proposition
3.20
we give the link between having addable
i
-nodes for the
e
-core associated with an affine Grassmannian element
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, and the length of
s_{i}w
. In Section
4
we first invoke a result of Deodhar
[
De
]
to obtain a criteria for the Bruhat order in the whole affine symmetric group
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
(Corollary
4.2
). Then, in §
4.2
we show how to use Lascoux’s result to obtain an easy procedure using abaci (Proposition
4.8
) to decide whether two affine permutations are comparable for the Bruhat order from the window notation, and in §
4.3
we convert this procedure into the setting of Young diagrams (Theorem
4.18
). Finally, in Appendix
A
we interpret our results in the context of
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core partitions of Jacon–Lecouvey
[
JL20
,
JL21
]
. Namely, in Proposition
A.7
we give the link between having addable or removable
i
-nodes for the
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core associated with any
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, and the length of
s_{i}w
. Finally, in Proposition
A.10
we deduce that the lattice
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
for the Bruhat order and the lattice of
(e,\mathsf{c})
-cores for the inclusions of multi-Young diagrams are isomorphic.

Notation

In the whole paper we fix an integer
e\geq 2
.

2
Partitions, abaci, cores

We recall here some standard materials about partitions. We refer for instance to
[
JaKe
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

A
partition
is a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\lambda_{h}>0)
with
h\geq 0
. A
\mathsf{c}
-charged
partition is the data of partition and an integer
\mathsf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We now fix a charge
\mathsf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We will use the notation
\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
to denote the
\mathsf{c}
-charged empty partition.

The
Young diagram
of a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{h}>0)
is:

\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\coloneqq\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:1\leq a\leq h\text{ and }1\leq b\leq\lambda_{a}\}.

A
node
is an element of
(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})^{2}
.
Young diagrams are usually represented with the
a
-axis (resp.
b
-axis) going down (resp. right).

Example 2.2
.

The Young diagram of the partition
\lambda=(3,2)
is

, the top right node being
(1,3)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
.

If
\lambda
is a partition and
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we say that the node
\gamma
is:

•

addable
if
\gamma\notin\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\cup\{\gamma\}
is the Young diagram of a partition,

•

removable
if
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\setminus\{\gamma\}
is the Young diagram of a partition.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu
be two partitions with
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
.

•

If
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
is removable for
\mu
then
\gamma
is removable for
\lambda
.

•

If a node
\gamma
is addable for
\lambda
then either
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
or
\gamma\notin\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
is addable for
\mu
.

Proof.

•

Let
\gamma=(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
be removable for
\mu
. Then
(a+1,b)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
and
(a,b+1)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
thus
(a+1,b)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
thus
(a,b+1)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
and
(a+1,b)\notin\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
thus
(a,b)=\gamma
is removable for
\lambda
as desired.

•

Let
\gamma=(a,b)
addable for
\lambda
. If
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
we are done, thus now assume that
\gamma\notin\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
and let us prove that
\gamma
is addable for
\mu
. If
a,b\geq 2
then
(a-1,b),(a,b-1)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
thus
(a-1,b),(a,b-1)\in\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
thus
(a,b)=\gamma
is addable for
\mu
as desired. The cases where
a=1
or
b=1
is similar, or can be deduced by including the nodes with a
0
-coordinate in the Young diagram.

∎

We now turn to a third representation for partitions.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
is an
abacus
if there exists
s\geq 0
such that
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq-s}\subseteq A
and
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq s}\cap A=\emptyset
.

If
A
is an abacus and
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, we say that
x
is a
bead
(resp.
gap
) of
A
if
x\in A
(resp.
x\notin A
). Sliding all the beads of an abacus to the left (if
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq-s}\subseteq A
then we only move the beads of
\mathbb{Z}_{>-s}\cap A
, which is finite) gives a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq c}
. We say that
c
is the
charge
of
A
.

Example 2.5
.

We consider the following abacus, where the vertical line denotes the place
0
.

\cdots

Sliding all the beads to the left gives:
\cdots
\cdots

thus the charge of the abacus is

.

Note that, as in Example

, in abaci we will sometimes write the corresponding integers below, with colors corresponding to an
e
-residue class.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{h}>0)
be a
\mathsf{c}
-charged partition. The
abacus
of
\lambda
is
\{\lambda_{k}-k+\mathsf{c}+1:k\geq 1\}
, with
\lambda_{k}\coloneqq 0
if

.

The abacus of a charged partition is an abacus indeed in the sense of Definition

. Conversely, any abacus is the abacus of a charged partition, by counting the number of gaps to the left of each bead.

Example 2.7
.

The abacus of Example

is the abacus of the

-charged partition
(5,3,3,2)
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\lambda
be a
\mathsf{c}
-charged partition. The
e
-residue
(or simply
residue
) of node
(a,b)\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
is
b-a+\mathsf{c}\pmod{e}
. An
i
-node
is a node of residue
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 2.9
.

For
e=5
and the
2
-charged partition
\lambda=(5,2,1)
, in each node of

we indicate the corresponding residue:

\scriptstyle 2

\scriptstyle 1

\scriptstyle 1

\scriptstyle 2

\scriptstyle 0

The link between
i
-nodes and the residues of the beads in the abacus is as follows.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{h}>0)
be a
\mathsf{c}
-charged partition. Let
k\in\{1,\dots,h\}
and let
x\coloneqq\lambda_{k}-k+\mathsf{c}+1
be a bead of the abacus of
\lambda
. If
i\in\mathbb{Z}/e\mathbb{Z}
is such that
x\equiv i+1\pmod{e}
then the node
(k,\lambda_{k})\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
, which is at the end of the
k
-th row of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
, is an
i
-node.

The fundamental result concerning abaci is the following (see, for instance,
[
JaKe
, Lemma 2.7.13]
).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\lambda
be a charged partition and let
A
be its abacus. Let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. Then
\lambda
has:

•

an addable
i
-node if and only if there exists
x\in A
with
x\equiv i\pmod{e}
and
x+1\notin A
,

•

a removable
i
-node if and only if there exists
y\in A
with
y\equiv i+1\pmod{e}
and
y-1\notin A
.

For instance, we obtain that the charge of the abacus of a
\mathsf{c}
-charged partition is
\mathsf{c}
again (sliding all the beads to the left so that we obtain the abacus of
\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
).
We now turn to objects that will catch our interest.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\lambda
be a charged partition and
A
be its abacus. We say that
\lambda
is an
e
-core
if for all
x\in A
we have
x-e\in A
.

Remark 2.13
.

By a generalisation of a result of Proposition
2.11
, the property of a partition
\lambda
being an
e
-core is equivalent to the fact that the Young diagram of
\lambda
has no
e
-hooks (see, for instance,
[
JaKe
, 2.7.40]
). We will have no use of this property here, but note that we will use (rim) hooks in §
4.3
.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\lambda
be an
e
-core and let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. The
e
-core
\lambda
cannot have both an addable and a removable
i
-node.

Proof.

Let
A
be an abacus associated with
\lambda
. By contradiction, if
x\in A
(resp.
y\in A
) corresponds to an addable (resp. removable)
i
-node then:

•

x\equiv i\pmod{e}
and
x+1\in A
,

•

y-1\equiv i\pmod{e}
and
y-1\notin A
,

thus since
\lambda
is an
e
-core we deduce that
x<y-1
. Similarly, we have:

•

x+1\equiv i+1\pmod{e}
and
x+1\notin A
,

•

y\equiv i+1\pmod{e}
and
y\in A
,

thus
y<x+1
. This contradicts the previous inequality.
∎

3
Affine symmetric group

We give here mainly some known results about the affine symmetric group
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, together with its action on the set of
e
-core partitions.

3.1
Definition

We denote by
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
the affine symmetric group, with generators
s_{0},\dots,s_{e-1}
. The defining relations are:

\displaystyle s_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\text{for all }i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\},

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}

\displaystyle=s_{j}s_{i},

\displaystyle\text{for all }i,j\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}\text{ with }|i-j|\geq 2,

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}

\displaystyle=s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1},

\displaystyle\text{ for all }i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\},

where, in the last relation, the indices are taken modulo
e
. Elements of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
can be identified with the set of permutations
w
of
\mathbb{Z}
such that:

w(i+e)=w(i)+e,\qquad\text{for all }i\in\mathbb{Z}.

(3.1)

and
w(1)+\dots+w(e)=\frac{e(e+1)}{2}
(see, for instance,
[
BB96b
, Proposition 3.3]
). Under this identification, the generator
s_{i}
is given by permuting
i+ke
and
i+1+ke
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. In the sequel, we will not make any difference between an element of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
an its corresponding permutation of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
then
w(1),\dots,w(e)
have all different residues modulo
e
.

Proof.

We have
\mathbb{Z}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{e}(i+e\mathbb{Z})
thus, by (
3.1
) and since
w
is a bijection we have
\mathbb{Z}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{e}\bigl(w(i)+e\mathbb{Z}\bigr)
.
∎

Equation (
3.1
) implies that an element
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is determined by the image of
\{1,\dots,e\}
.

Definition 3.3
.

The
window notation
of
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is
[w(1),\dots,w(e)]
.

The window notation has the advantage over the expression as a word in the generators
s_{0},\dots,s_{e-1}
that it is always the data of
e
integers, while the length of a word can be arbitrarily large.
The aim of this paper is to decide whether two elements of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
in the window notation are comparable for the Bruhat order, without using the decomposition as a product of generators
s_{0},\dots,s_{e-1}
.

To conclude this part, let us recall the following result, where
\ell:\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
denotes the Coxeter length function.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
BB96b
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
and let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. We have:

\ell(ws_{i})<\ell(w)\iff w(i)>w(i+1).

3.2
Action on

-cores

Throughout this part we fix a charge

on

-charged

-charged

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

•

Assume first that
\lambda,\mu
has no addable
i
-nodes.

\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\mu)

\mathcal{Y}(s_{i}\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(s_{i}\mu)
. It suffices to prove that if
\lambda\in\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)

i
-node removable for
\mu
then
\lambda
is also removable for
\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
, but this follows immediately from Lemma
2.3
.

•

Now assume that
\lambda,\mu
has no removable
i
-nodes. Suppose that
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
and let us prove that
\mathcal{Y}(s_{i}\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(s_{i}\mu)
. It suffices to prove that if
\gamma
is an addable
i
-node for
\lambda
then either
\gamma\in\mathcal{Y}(\mu)
either
\gamma
is addable for
\mu
, but again this follows immediately from Lemma

.

The above proves the necessary condition. The converse implication follows from the fact that an

has no removable (resp. addable)
i

∎

Remark 3.7
.

Without the assumption the result is false. For instance, if

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

. If

\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\mu)\iff\mathcal{Y}(s_{i}\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\mu).

\lambda
has some addable

3.3
Grassmannian elements

Here we fix a charge
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
.
The assertions in this part can be found in Lascoux
[
La
]
and are sometimes implicit. We provide some details for the proofs (see also
[
BJV
, §3.2]
).

Definition 3.11
.

We define
\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
to be the subset of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
formed by the elements
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
such that
\ell(ws_{i})>\ell(w)
for all
i\in J_{\mathsf{c}}\coloneqq\{0,\dots,e-1\}\setminus\{\mathsf{c}\}
.

We can give an easy characterisation of the elements of
\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
in terms of reduced expression.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
. We have
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
if and only if
w=1
or any reduced expression of
w
ends by
s_{\mathsf{c}}
.

Proof.

The case
w=1
is clear, thus we suppose
w\neq 1
. Assume that there exists a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
with
i_{n}\neq\mathsf{c}
. Then
i_{n}\notin J_{\mathsf{c}}
and
ws_{i_{n}}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n-1}}
satisfies
\ell(ws_{i_{n}})<\ell(w)
thus
w\notin\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. Conversely, assume that
w\notin\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
so that there exists
i\in J_{\mathsf{c}}
with
\ell(ws_{i})<\ell(w)
. Then if
ws_{i}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n-1}}
is a reduced expression then
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n-1}}s_{i}
is a reduced expression with
i\neq\mathsf{c}
.
∎

The next result appears for instance in
[
De
, Lemma 3.1]
).

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and
i\in J_{\mathsf{c}}
. If
\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)
then
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)
. If
s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
is a reduced expression of
s_{i}w
then
s_{i}s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
is a reduced expression of
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
thus
i_{n}=\mathsf{c}
by Lemma
3.12
. By Lemma
3.12
again we deduce that
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
.
∎

Definition
3.11
is in fact a particular case of a standard result in the theory of Coxeter groups (see, for instance,
[
Hum
, §1.10]
).

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
J\subseteq\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, define
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e})^{J}\coloneqq\{w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}:\ell(ws)>\ell(w)
for all
s\in J\}
and consider the parabolic subgroup
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e})_{J}
generated by the elements
s_{i}
for
i\in J
. Then for any
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, there exists a unique
(w^{J},w_{J})\in(\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e})^{J}\times(\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e})_{J}
such that
w=w^{J}w_{J}
. Moreover this unique couple satisfies
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{J})+\ell(w_{J})
.

The elements of the set
(\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e})^{J}
as in Proposition
3.14
are called
minimal length coset representatives
. Recall from Definition
3.11
that
J_{\mathsf{c}}=\{0,\dots,e-1\}\setminus\{\mathsf{c}\}
.

Definition 3.15
.

For
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, we write
w^{(c)}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
instead of the element
w^{J_{\mathsf{c}}}
of Proposition
3.14
.

The next result connects the projection on the affine Grassmannian with the action on charged
e
-cores, and will be central in Section
4
.

Proposition 3.16
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
. The elements
w
and
w^{(\mathsf{c})}
have the same action on
\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.14
, we can write
w=w^{(\mathsf{c})}s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
with
i_{j}\in J_{\mathsf{c}}
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. The only addable box in
\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
has residue
\mathsf{c}
, thus
s_{i}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
for all
i\in J_{\mathsf{c}}
. We deduce that
w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=w^{(\mathsf{c})}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
and this concludes the proof.
∎

We now give the analogue of Lemma
3.12
in terms of window notation (see
[
BB96b
, Proposition 3.5]
).

Proposition 3.17
.

For
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
we have
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}\iff w(\mathsf{c}+1)<\dots<w(\mathsf{c}+e)
.

Proof.

Let
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
. By Lemma
3.4
we have:

\displaystyle w\in\mathrm{GA}

\displaystyle\iff\ell(ws_{i})>\ell(w)

\displaystyle\text{for all }i\in J_{\mathsf{c}}

\displaystyle\iff w(i)<w(i+1)

\displaystyle\text{for all }i\in J_{\mathsf{c}}

\displaystyle\iff w(0)<\dots<w(\mathsf{c})\text{ and }w(\mathsf{c}+1)<\dots<w(e).

By (
3.1
), we have
w(0)<\dots<w(\mathsf{c})\iff w(e)<\dots<w(\mathsf{c}+e)
and we obtain the desired result.
∎

Example 3.18
.

of Example

\mathrm{GA}^{1}
. Note that the same element

was considered in Example

Remark 3.19
.

For
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, the element
w^{(\mathsf{c})}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
of Definition
3.15
is the unique affine permutation
w^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
such that
\{w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+1)<\dots<w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+e)\}=\{w(\mathsf{c}+1),\dots,w(\mathsf{c}+e)\}
.
Indeed,
let
\sigma
be the permutation of
\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e\}
such that
w\sigma(\mathsf{c}+1)<\dots<w\sigma(\mathsf{c}+e)
. Then
\sigma
is the product of some transpositions
(k,k+1)
with
k\in\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e-1\}
, thus if
\sigma^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is given by this same product but replacing
(k,k+1)
by the generator
s_{k\bmod e}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
then:

•

we have
\sigma^{\prime}\in(\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e})_{J_{\mathsf{c}}}
since the generator
s_{\mathsf{c}}
does not appear in the above decomposition of
\sigma^{\prime}
into generators,

•

the element

satisfies

thus

by Proposition

.

Hence, by unicity in Proposition
3.14
, since

we obtain that
w^{\prime}=w^{(\mathsf{c})}
, as desired.

The next result allows to detect when the
e
-core associated with
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
has an addable
i
-node.

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and let
\lambda=w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
be the associated
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-core. Let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. Then
\lambda
has an addable
i
-node if and only if
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
and
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
.

Proof.

First assume that
\lambda
has an addable
i
-node. By Propositions
2.11
and
3.9
, we can find
x,y\in\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e\}
and
\delta\geq 1
such that the abacus of
\lambda
has a bead at position
w(y)-\delta e
, a gap at position
w(x)=w(y)-\delta e+1
and:

w(y)\equiv i\pmod{e}\text{ and }w(x)\equiv i+1\pmod{e}.

(3.21)

Note that since
\delta\geq 1
and
e\geq 2
, we have
w(x)<w(y)
.
If
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is such that
w(x)=i+1+ke
then by (
3.1
) we have
w^{-1}(i+1)=x-ke
and similarly
w^{-1}(i)=y-(k+\delta)e
. Now
\delta,e\geq 1
and
|x-y|<e
thus
w^{-1}(i)<w^{-1}(i+1)
, thus by Lemma
3.4
we have
\ell(w^{-1}s_{i})>\ell(w^{-1})
thus
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
.

We now have to prove that
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. By (
3.21
) we have
s_{i}w(x)=w(x)-1
and
s_{i}w(y)=w(y)+1
. Recalling that
w(x)<w(y)
, we deduce that:

•

by Proposition
3.17
we have
x<y
, since
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and
x,y\in\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e\}
,

•

we have
s_{i}w(x)=w(x)-1<w(y)+1=s_{i}w(y)
.

Now, for any
z\in\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e\}\setminus\{x,y\}
we have
s_{i}w(z)=w(z)
, thus by (
3.21
) and Lemma
3.2
we have
w(z)<w(x)\iff w(z)<w(x)-1\iff s_{i}w(z)<s_{i}w(x)
. Similarly we have
w(z)<w(y)\iff s_{i}w(z)<s_{i}w(y)
and thus, by another application of Proposition
3.17
, we deduce that
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
.

Conversely, assume that
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
and
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and let us prove that
\lambda
has an addable
i
-node. By Lemma
3.4
we have
w^{-1}(i)<w^{-1}(i+1)
and let
x,y\in\{1,\dots,e\}
and
k,\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
w^{-1}(i+1)=x-ke
and
w^{-1}(i)=y-(k+\delta)e
. Since
w^{-1}(i)<w^{-1}(i+1)
we have:

y-\delta e<x.

(3.22)

Since
x,y\in\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e\}
, we deduce that
\delta\geq 0
. By (
3.1
), we have:

w(x)=i+1+ke\text{ and }w(y)=i+(k+\delta)e=w(x)+\delta e-1,

(3.23)

in particular
s_{i}w(x)=w(x)-1
and
s_{i}w(y)=w(y)+1
thus:

s_{i}w(x)=i+ke\text{ and }s_{i}w(y)=i+1+(k+\delta)e=s_{i}w(x)+1+\delta e.

Since
\delta\geq 0
we deduce that
s_{i}w(x)<s_{i}w(y)
thus since
x,y\in\{\mathsf{c}+1,\dots,\mathsf{c}+e\}
and
s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
we obtain that
x<y
. Thus by (
3.22
) we have
\delta\neq 0
, thus
\delta\geq 1
. Now by (
3.23
) we have

w(x)\leq w(y)-e+1
. By Proposition
3.9
, we deduce that the abacus of
\lambda
has:

•

a bead at position
w(y)-e\equiv i\pmod{e}
and at gap at
w(y)
,

•

a gap at position
w(x)\leq w(y)-e+1
with
w(x)\equiv i+1\pmod{e}
.

Since
\lambda
is an
e
-core, by Definition
2.12
we deduce that there is a gap at position
w(y)-e+1\equiv i+1\pmod{e}
, thus the bead at
w(y)-e
has a gap directly to its right. By Proposition
2.11
, this proves that
\lambda
has an addable
i
-node.
∎

Remark 3.24
.

If, given
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
, we only assume that
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
then it does not necessarily mean that
\lambda
has an addable
i
-node. For instance, with
e=4
and
w=s_{0}\in\mathrm{GA}^{0}
we have
\lambda=(1)
, here
\ell(s_{2}w)=2>\ell(w)
but
\lambda
does not have any addable
2
-node (the only addable nodes are
0
- and
3
-nodes). We will give in Proposition
A.7
a statement without the Grassmannian assumption, using generalised
e
-cores.

Corollary 3.25
.

The map
\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}\to\{\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-cores
\}
given by
w\mapsto w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
is a bijection.

Proof.

Let us first prove that the map is onto. Let
\lambda
be a
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-core. We proceed by induction on the number of boxes of
\lambda
. If
\lambda=\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
then
\lambda=1\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
and this concludes the initialisation since
1\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. Now if
\lambda
has at least one box, then
\lambda
has at least a removable node. This removable node is a removable
i
-node for some
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. Thus, the partition
\lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq s_{i}\lambda
has strictly less boxes than
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
has some addable
i
-nodes. By induction, we know that there exists
w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
such that
\lambda^{\prime}=w^{\prime}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
. Now by Proposition
3.20
, we know that
w\coloneqq s_{i}w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and this concludes the proof since
w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=s_{i}w^{\prime}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=s_{i}\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
.

To prove the injectivity, let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
such that
w\cdot\lambda=w^{\prime}\cdot\lambda
. By Proposition
3.9
we thus have:

\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{e}\bigl(w(\mathsf{c}+i)+e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}\bigr)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{e}\bigl(w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+i)+e\mathbb{Z}_{<0}\bigr).

Hence, we have
\{w(\mathsf{c}+1),\dots,w(\mathsf{c}+e)\}=\{w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+1),\dots,w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+e)\}
, but now
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
thus
w(\mathsf{c}+1)<\dots<w(\mathsf{c}+e)
and
w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+1)<\dots<w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+e)
by Proposition
3.17
thus
w(\mathsf{c}+i)=w^{\prime}(\mathsf{c}+i)
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,e\}
thus
w=w^{\prime}
(by (
3.1
)).
∎

Remark 3.26
.

The proof of the injectivity in Corollary
3.25
provides an easy way to recover
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
from the abacus of a given
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-core.

The above two results now allow to translate the meaning of having a removable
i
-node into the Coxeter setting, which is the key point of
[
La
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proposition 3.27
.

Let
w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and let
\lambda=w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
be the associated
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-core. Let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. Then
\lambda
has a removable
i
-node if and only if
\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\lambda
has a removable
i
-node. Thus the
e
-core
\lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq s_{i}\lambda
has an addable
i
-node. By Corollary
3.25
, there exists
w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
such that
\lambda^{\prime}=w^{\prime}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
. We can thus apply Proposition
3.20
to find that:

\ell(s_{i}w^{\prime})>\ell(w^{\prime}),

(3.28)

and
s_{i}w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. We now have
s_{i}w^{\prime}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=s_{i}\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda=w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
and
w,s_{i}w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
thus
w=s_{i}w^{\prime}
by Corollary
3.25
. Thus (
3.28
) gives
\ell(w)>\ell(s_{i}w)
as desired.

We now assume that
\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)
. By Lemma
3.13
we have
w^{\prime}\coloneqq s_{i}w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. With
\lambda^{\prime}\coloneqq w^{\prime}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
, we thus have
\ell(s_{i}w^{\prime})=\ell(w)>\ell(s_{i}w)=\ell(w^{\prime})
and
s_{i}w^{\prime}=w\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. Thus, by Proposition
3.20
the
e
-core
\lambda^{\prime}
has an addable
i
-node, thus
\lambda=w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=s_{i}w^{\prime}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}=s_{i}\lambda^{\prime}
has a removable
i
-node.
∎

Remark 3.29
.

If we do not assume that

without

having a removable

-node. For instance, with

and

, we have

thus

has no removable node although

. (See Proposition

. for a statement without the Grassmannian assumption.)

Lemma 3.30
.

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system, let
\ell

\unlhd
be the strong Bruhat order. Let
w,w^{\prime}\in W

\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1

\ell(sw^{\prime})=\ell(w^{\prime})-1
. Then:

w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff sw\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff sw\unlhd sw^{\prime}.

Theorem 3.31

(Lascoux
[
La
, Proposition 4.1]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
.
Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
and let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
respectively be the associated
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-cores. Then:

w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime}).

Proof.

We use induction on
\ell(w,w^{\prime})\coloneqq\ell(w)+\ell(w^{\prime})
. If
\ell(w,w^{\prime})=0
then
\ell(w)=\ell(w^{\prime})=0
thus
w=w^{\prime}=1
and
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)=\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime})=\emptyset
and the equivalence is true.

Now let
n\geq 0
and assume that the result holds for all
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
such that
\ell(w,w^{\prime})\leq n
. Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
such that
\ell(w,w^{\prime})=n+1
and let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}
be the corresponding
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-cores. If

w=1
or
w^{\prime}=1
then the result is immediate since
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)=\emptyset
or
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime})=\emptyset
respectively. Thus, we assume that
w,w^{\prime}\neq 1
. In particular, there exists
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
such that with
s=s_{i}
we have
\ell(sw^{\prime})<\ell(w^{\prime})
. By Lemma
3.13
, we have
sw^{\prime}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
.

•

Assume first that
\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1
. By Lemma
3.13
again, we have
sw\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
. Since
\ell(sw)<\ell(w)
and
\ell(sw^{\prime})<\ell(w^{\prime})
, we can use Lemma
3.30
to have:

w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff sw\unlhd sw^{\prime},

moreover
\ell(sw,sw^{\prime})=\ell(w,w^{\prime})-2<\ell(w,w^{\prime})
thus we can use the induction hypothesis to obtain:

w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}(s\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(s\lambda^{\prime}).

Since, again,
\ell(sw)<\ell(w)
and
\ell(sw^{\prime})<\ell(w^{\prime})
, by Proposition
3.27
we know that
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
both have a removable
i
-node. Hence, by Proposition
2.14
we know that
\lambda
or
\lambda^{\prime}
cannot have any addable
i
-node, hence by Lemma
3.6
we deduce that:

w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime}),

as desired.

•

We now assume that
\ell(sw)=\ell(w)+1
. Setting
\tilde{w}\coloneqq sw
and
\tilde{\lambda}\coloneqq s\lambda
, we have
s\tilde{w}=w
and
\ell(s\tilde{w})<\ell(\tilde{w})
. Recalling
\ell(sw^{\prime})<\ell(w^{\prime})
, we can thus use the induction hypothesis for
s\tilde{w}
and
sw^{\prime}
to find:

s\tilde{w}\unlhd sw^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}(s\tilde{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(s\lambda^{\prime}).

As in the preceding point, we know that neither
\tilde{\lambda}
or
\lambda^{\prime}
has an addable
i
-node and thus:

s\tilde{w}\unlhd sw^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}(\tilde{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime}).

Since
\lambda^{\prime}
has no addable
i
-nodes, we deduce from Lemma
3.8
that:

s\tilde{w}\unlhd sw^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}(s\tilde{\lambda})\subseteq\mathcal{Y}(\lambda^{\prime}).

We now note that since
\ell(s\tilde{w})<\ell(\tilde{w})
and
\ell(sw^{\prime})<\ell(w^{\prime})
, by Lemma
3.30
we have
s\tilde{w}\unlhd sw^{\prime}\iff s\tilde{w}\unlhd w^{\prime}
thus we conclude the proof since
s\tilde{w}=w
.

∎

Remark 3.32
.

Using crystals theory, Jacon–Lecouvey
[
JL20

obtained the same result as Theorem

, via reduction to the type A case.

4
Strong Bruhat order on the affine symmetric group

Our aim is now to give an efficient description of the strong Bruhat order on the whole affine symmetric group.

4.1
Product characterisation

For
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
and
J\subseteq\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, recall from Proposition
3.14
that
w^{J}
denotes the minimal length coset representative associated with
w
for the parabolic subgroup generated by
s_{i}
for
i\in J
.

Proposition 4.1

(Deodhar
[
De
, Lemma 3.6]
)
.

Let
\{J_{i}\}_{i}
be a family of subsets of
\{0,\dots,e-1\}
such that
\cap_{i}J_{i}=\emptyset
. Then for any
w,w^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
we have
w\unlhd w^{\prime}
if and only if
w^{J_{i}}\unlhd w^{\prime J_{i}}
for all
i
.

Now recall from Definitions
3.11
and
3.15
that for
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
and
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, we denote by
w^{(\mathsf{c})}
the element
w^{J_{\mathsf{c}}}
, where
J_{\mathsf{c}}=\{0,\dots,e-1\}\setminus\{\mathsf{c}\}
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
w,w^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
. For any
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, let
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})},\lambda^{\prime(\mathsf{c})}
be the
\mathsf{c}
-charged
e
-cores associated with
w,w^{\prime}
respectively. Then:

w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff\mathcal{Y}\bigl(\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}\bigr)\subseteq\mathcal{Y}\bigl(\lambda^{\prime(\mathsf{c})}\bigr)\text{ for all }\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}.

Proof.

For any
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
we have
\mathsf{c}\notin J_{\mathsf{c}}
thus
\cap_{\mathsf{c}=0}^{e-1}J_{\mathsf{c}}=\emptyset
. Applying Proposition
4.1
, we thus have that
w\unlhd w^{\prime}\iff w^{(\mathsf{c})}\unlhd w^{\prime(\mathsf{c})}
for all
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. By definition we have
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}=w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
and by Proposition
3.16
we deduce that
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}=w^{(\mathsf{c})}\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
. The result now follows from applying Theorem
3.31
to the elements
w^{(\mathsf{c})}\in\mathrm{GA}^{\mathsf{c}}
for all
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
.
∎

A natural way to see the result of Corollary
4.2
is to make
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
acting diagonally on the
e
-tuple
(\emptyset_{0},\dots,\emptyset_{e-1})
. The
e
-tuples of

-cores that we obtain this way are a the
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores
of Jacon–Lecouvey
[

]
, where in our case
\underline{\mathsf{c}}=(0,1,\dots,e-1)
. We will give some interpretations of our results in terms of
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores in Appendix
A
.

4.2

When applying Corollary

we have to compute the

through the action of

on the empty
\mathsf{c}
-charged

-core

from

Definition 4.3
.

Let

. Define the
e
-tuple of abaci

as follows.

•

We have

, that is, the abacus

is the abacus of the

-charged

-core

.

•

\mathsf{c}\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}

is obtained from the abacus

by adding a bead at

.

Remark 4.4
.

In the setting of Definition
4.3
, for
\mathsf{c}\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}
the abacus

has a gap at

indeed, since by Proposition

we know that

has gaps at

.

.

Proof.

By construction we have
A_{0}=A^{\prime}_{0}
. Recall from Proposition
3.9

We now want to perform the procedure of Definition

Lemma 4.10

Let

Remark 4.15

Lemma 4.16
.

Let
\lambda
be a non-empty charged partition and let
A
be its abacus. Let
m\coloneqq\min(\mathbb{Z}\setminus A)
be the leftmost gap in
A
and let
x\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>m}
. Let
h\coloneqq x-m\geq 1
and let
R
be the rim hook corresponding to
x\in A
and
m=x-h\notin A
as in Proposition
4.14
. Then
R
has exactly one node in the first column of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
if and only if
x=m+1
or
m+1\notin A
.

Proof.

Recall that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the beads of an abacus with a gap somewhere to their left and the non-zero parts of the associated charged partition. Let
B\coloneqq(A\setminus\{x\})\sqcup\{m\}
and let
\mu
be the associated charged partition.

Assume first that
m+1\in A
. If
x=m+1
then
h=x-m=1
and
R
is reduced to only one node. By minimality of
m
this node is the only node of its row, whence the result. We now assume
x\neq m+1
.
Then in
A
, the beads at
x
and
m+1
both correspond to a non-zero part of
\lambda
since
m\notin A
(noting that
x>m
by definition), but then in
B
the beads at
m
and
m+1
both correspond to zero parts of
\mu
since then
\mathbb{Z}_{\leq m+1}\subseteq B
. Hence if
x\neq m+1
then
\mu
has at least two parts less than
\lambda
thus
R
has at least two nodes in the first column of
\mathcal{Y}(\lambda)
by Lemma
4.13
.

Conversely, assume that
m+1\notin A
. Then for any
y\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}_{>m}
we know that
y\in A
has a gap somewhere to its left (namely, at
m+1
). By construction, if
y\neq x
we also have
y\in B
and
m+1\notin B
thus
y\in B
has a gap somewhere to its left. Now to the bead
x\in A
corresponds a non-zero part in
\lambda
, but by minimality
m\in B
has no gaps to its left thus

corresponds to a zero part of
\mu
. Hence
\mu
has exactly one part less than
\lambda
and we conclude the proof by Lemma
4.13
.
∎

Remark 4.17
.

In Lemma
4.16
, the bead

has a gap somewhere to its left and we move the bead

as far left as possible.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let

. For any

, the

-core

removing the first column of

Let
\mathsf{c}\in\{1,\dots,e-1\}

and Lemma

Appendix A
Generalised cores of Jacon–Lecouvey

Let
\ell\geq 2
and let
\underline{\mathsf{c}}=(\mathsf{c}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{c}_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
.

Definition A.1

(Jacon–Lecouvey
[
JL20
]
)
.

For each
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
, let
\lambda^{(k)}
be a
\mathsf{c}_{k}
-charged partition and let
A_{k}
be its abacus.
We say that
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})
is an
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core
if:

A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}\subseteq\dots\subseteq A_{\ell}\subseteq A_{1}+e.

Note that if
\underline{\lambda}
is an
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core then each component of
\underline{\lambda}
is an
e
-core. A basic example of
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core is the following.

Lemma A.2

(
[
JL20
, Lemma 5.12]
)
.

Assume that
\mathsf{c}_{1}\leq\mathsf{c}_{2}\leq\dots\leq\mathsf{c}_{\ell}\leq\mathsf{c}_{1}+e
. Then
(\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}_{1}},\dots,\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}_{\ell}})
is an
(e,\ell)
-core.

Remark A.3
.

Note that the result of
[
JL20
]
is stated for any
\underline{\mathsf{c}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
, however the proof is only valid under the above condition (which is necessarily satisfied for
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores, see
[
JL21
, Proposition 2.14]
).

The
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core condition on
\ell
-tuple of abaci is compatible with the diagonal action of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, thus we deduce the following result.

Proposition A.4

(
[
JL20
, Proposition 5.14]
)
.

The affine symmetric group
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
acts on the set of
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores.

If
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})
, we say that
\underline{\lambda}
has an addable (resp. a removable) node if there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
such that
\lambda^{(k)}
has an addable (resp. a removable) node.
The next result is similar to Proposition
2.14
(and so are their proofs).

Lemma A.5

(
[
JL20
, Lemma 5.16]
)
.

Let
(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(\ell)})
be an
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core and let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
.

•

If
\underline{\lambda}
has an addable
i
-node then for all
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
, the
e
-core
\lambda^{(k)}
has no removable
i
-nodes.

•

In particular, if
\underline{\lambda}
has a removable
i
-node then for all
k\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
, the
e
-core
\lambda^{(k)}
has no addable
i
-nodes.

By Lemma
A.5
, the action of the generator
s_{i}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is either adding all possible
i
-nodes in all components, or removing all possible
i
-nodes in all components.

Example A.6
.

For
e=4
and
\underline{\mathsf{c}}=(0,1,2,3)
, the action of
s_{1}s_{2}s_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is obtained on the empty
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core from Example
4.9
by reading column by column.

From now on we set
\underline{\mathsf{c}}=(0,1,\dots,e-1)
, in particular
\ell=e
. Let
\underline{\emptyset}\coloneqq(\emptyset_{0},\dots,\emptyset_{e-1})
, which is an
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core by Lemma
A.2
.
The next result is the analogue of Propositions
3.20
and
3.27
.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
and let
\underline{\lambda}\coloneqq w\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
be the
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core associated with
w
. Let
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
. We have:

\displaystyle\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)

\displaystyle\iff\underline{\lambda}\text{ has a removable }i\text{-node},

and similarly:

\displaystyle\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)

\displaystyle\iff\underline{\lambda}\text{ has an addable }i\text{-node}.

In particular, the
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core
\underline{\lambda}
has either an addable or a removable
i
-node (recalling that these two cases cannot happen at the same time).

Proof.

Let us write
\underline{\lambda}=(\lambda^{(0)},\dots,\lambda^{(e-1)})
.
We first prove the two direct implications.
For any
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
, write
A_{\mathsf{c}}\coloneqq\mathbb{Z}_{\leq\mathsf{c}}
the abacus of the
\mathsf{c}
-charged empty
e
-core
\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
, in particular, the abacus of
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}=w\cdot\emptyset_{\mathsf{c}}
is
w(A_{\mathsf{c}})
.
Recall from Lemma
3.4
that:

\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)\iff\ell(w^{-1}s_{i})<\ell(w^{-1})\iff w^{-1}(i)>w^{-1}(i+1).

(A.8)

•

We first assume that
\ell(s_{i}w)<\ell(w)
. Let
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
such that
\mathsf{c}\equiv w^{-1}(i+1)\pmod{e}
and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathsf{c}=w^{-1}(i+1)+ke
. Since
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
, we have
w^{-1}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
thus
\mathsf{c}=w^{-1}(i+1+ke)
. By (
A.8
) we also obtain:

\displaystyle w^{-1}(i)+ke>w^{-1}(i+1)+ke

\displaystyle\text{ thus }w^{-1}(i+ke)>w^{-1}(i+1+ke)

\displaystyle\text{ thus }w^{-1}(i+ke)>\mathsf{c}

\displaystyle\text{ thus }w^{-1}(i+ke)\notin A_{\mathsf{c}}

\displaystyle\text{ thus }i+ke\notin w(A_{\mathsf{c}}).

Now
w^{-1}(i+1+ke)=\mathsf{c}\in A_{\mathsf{c}}
thus
i+1+ke\in w(A_{\mathsf{c}})
. Hence, we have:

i+ke\notin w(A_{\mathsf{c}})\text{ and }i+1+ke\in w(A_{\mathsf{c}}).

We deduce that
\lambda^{(\mathsf{c})}
has a removable
i
-node by Proposition
2.11
., thus
\underline{\lambda}
has a removable
i
-node.

•

Now assume that
\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)
. Let
\mathsf{c}\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
such that
\mathsf{c}\equiv w^{-1}(i)\pmod{e}
and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mathsf{c}=w^{-1}(i)+ke
. By similar calculations as above, by (
A.8
) we have
w^{-1}(i)<w^{-1}(i+1)
thus
\mathsf{c}<w^{-1}(i+1+ke)
thus

i+1+ke\notin w(A_{\mathsf{c}})
. Hence, since
i+ke=w(\mathsf{c})\in w(A_{\mathsf{c}})
we deduce that
\underline{\lambda}
has an addable
i
-node.

By Lemma
A.5
we deduce that the two converse implications hold and this concludes the proof. The assertion in parenthesis follows from this same lemma.
∎

We deduce the same kind of consequence as in level one (cf. Corollary
3.25
). Note that the proof is a bit different.

Corollary A.9
.

The map
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}\to\{(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores
\}
given by
w\mapsto w\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
is a bijection.

Proof.

Let us first prove that the action of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
on the set of
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores is transitive: let
\underline{\lambda}
be an
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core and let us prove that there exists
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
such that
\underline{\lambda}=w\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
. We proceed by induction on the number of boxes of
\underline{\lambda}
. If
\underline{\lambda}=\underline{\emptyset}
then
\underline{\lambda}=1\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
and we are done. Now if
\underline{\lambda}
has at least one box, this box is a removable
i
-node for some
i\in\{0,\dots,e-1\}
and thus
\underline{\lambda}^{\prime}\coloneqq s_{i}\underline{\lambda}
has strictly less boxes than
\underline{\lambda}
. Hence, by induction there exists
w^{\prime}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
such that
\underline{\lambda}^{\prime}=w^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
thus
\underline{\lambda}=s_{i}\underline{\lambda}=s_{i}w^{\prime}\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
, as desired.

Let us now prove that the stabiliser of
\underline{\emptyset}
under the action of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
is trivial: let
w\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}\setminus\{1\}
, let
\underline{\lambda}\coloneqq w\cdot\underline{\emptyset}
by the associated
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-core and let us prove that
\underline{\lambda}\neq\underline{\emptyset}
.
If
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{n}}
is a reduced expression, then
\ell(s_{i_{1}}w)<\ell(w)
thus by Proposition
A.7
we know that
\underline{\lambda}
has a removable
i_{1}
-node. Thus
\underline{\lambda}\neq\underline{\emptyset}
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Together with Corollary
4.2
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition A.10
.

Recall that
\underline{\mathsf{c}}=(0,1,\dots,e-1)
.
The lattice
\widetilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{e}
for the Bruhat order is isomorphic to the lattice of
(e,\underline{\mathsf{c}})
-cores for the order given by the inclusions of multi-Young diagrams.

A rational estimation of volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of Al2O3-water nanofluid in thermo-fluid application
Authors: Nahid Hasan, Md Sheikh Rasel, Sumon Saha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractA practical approach to augment the heat transmission effectiveness of thermo-fluid systems involves the utilization of nanofluids instead of traditional fluids. The present investigation compares different models for determining nanofluids’ volumetric thermal expansion coefficient with the experimental findings. A benchmark problem of free convection is chosen to assess the accuracy of the existing models. Numerical solutions of the benchmark problem are accomplished by applying the Galerkin finite element weighted residual method to unravel the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes and thermal energy expressions representing the computational model. The Rayleigh number alters between 103 and 109, while the nanoparticle volume fraction (ϕ) ranges from 0 to 4 %. The outcomes obtained by various models are contrasted with the experimental results in terms of the mean Nusselt number. Following extensive analysis, it becomes evident that the Khanafer and Vafai model exhibits the best resemblance with the experimental data for Al2O3-water nanofluid under specified conditions. However, for other types of nanofluid, the most commonly used model originating from the mixing rule demonstrates the best match to the experimental data compared to other available models.Keywords: Nanofluid, Volumetric thermal expansion, Linear thermal expansion, Free convection, Thermal performance enhancement

Introduction

Effective heat transmission is vital for improving the thermal performance of various heat-transporting mediums, processes, and heat-generating equipment. Therefore, improving heat transfer efficiency is crucial across diverse sectors, including thermal management systems, energy production, aerospace technologies, and electronics cooling. One prominent way to improve heat transmission involves increasing the thermal conductivity of conventional fluids by integrating nanosized particles with superior thermo-physical properties. Choi and Eastman [1] introduced the term ‘nanofluid’ in 1995, illustrating that incorporating nanoparticles, usually 1–100 nm in size, into traditional liquids such as water or oil notably enhanced heat transport. Later, Mansour et al. [2] explored the influence of various nanofluid models on the thermo-fluidic performance of pipe flow. Furthermore, utilizing Fourier sine transforms to derive accurate solutions for temperature and velocity profiles of nanofluids has paved the way for innovative research endeavors to improve heat transfer efficiency through nanofluid applications [3,4].

Many researchers conducted computational and experimental studies to evaluate the effectiveness of nanofluids in fluid dynamics, energy systems, and thermo-fluid applications. Kakac and Pramuanjaroenkij [5] reviewed that including nanoparticles in conventional fluids such as water or oil notably improved convective heat transport capacity. However, they suggested that further experimental data and theoretical modeling of nanoparticle movement mechanisms were necessary to comprehend nanofluids' flow dynamics and heat transmission. Later, Pinto and Fiorelli [6] extensively explored the mechanisms influencing nanofluids' thermophysical characteristics. They concluded that issues such as Brownian motion, particle aggregation, and the emergence of an interface film surrounding the nanoparticles played pivotal roles in dictating heat transport behavior. Similarly, Hussein et al. [7] found that nanofluids exhibited superior heat transport capabilities during forced convection compared to base fluids at a fixed Reynolds number. However, suspending nanoparticles in a base fluid increased pressure drop and friction factor. Later, Kasaeian et al. [8] reviewed similar studies that concurrently utilized porous media and nanofluids to improve heat transport. They noted that most of these investigations focused on the impact of nanoparticle concentration and nanofluid types on heat transport. In a comprehensive review, Ismail et al. [9] resolved that the appearance of nanoparticles not only enhanced thermal conduction and convection coefficients but also boosted other features such as stability, electrical resistance, and hydrophilicity. Afterward, Guo [10] summarized experimentally measured distinct properties, proposed enhancement mechanisms, and various models for determining the properties of commonly used nanofluids as reported in the previous studies. In a recent investigation, Souayeh et al. [11] examined three nanofluids' free convective heat transfer around double isothermal spheres placed horizontally. Their discoveries exposed that a higher nanoparticle volume fraction led to an enhanced Nusselt number for all Rayleigh numbers. Kanti et al. [12,13] experimentally examined the thermophysical properties of Al2O3 nanoparticles dispersed in various ionic liquids for solar and energy storage applications. Their findings indicated that the nanofluid's specific heat and thermal conductivity increased with rising temperature and nanoparticle concentration. Marulasiddeshi et al. [14] and Kanti et al. [15] investigated the thermal performance and entropy generation of Al2O3-CuO-H2O hybrid nanofluid and Al2O3-H2O nanofluid. Kanti et al. [15] also analyzed the influence of nanoparticle size on nanofluid performance. Marulasiddeshi et al. [14] reported that the hybrid nanofluid exhibited better thermal performance than the nanofluid alone. Moreover, Ramesh et al. [16] and Mebarek-Oudina et al. [17] numerically investigated magnetohydodynamic convection and entropy generation of hybrid nanofluids. Mebarek-Oudina et al. [17] found that thermal performance improved at higher volume fractions and larger particle sizes. Recently, Siad et al. [18] conducted a numerical study on the effects of a magnetic field during the mixed convective flow of Cu-water nanofluid, observing enhanced thermal performance at higher Reynolds numbers. However, Dharmaiah et al. [19] investigated ferrofluids to examine the influence of magnetic dipoles.

Nanofluids have drawn significant interest lately because of their superior thermal performance and boosted thermo-physical features. The thermal expansion coefficient is a critical property of nanofluids in many buoyancy-driven thermal systems. There are two types of thermal expansion coefficients: linear and volumetric. When calculating the body force generated by buoyancy during free or mixed convection using the Boussinesq approximation, it is advised to use the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient to account for temperature-induced density variations. However, many researchers mistakenly used nanoparticles' linear thermal expansion coefficient as the volumetric one, leading to incorrect body force estimations when applying the Boussinesq approximation. Therefore, accurately determining the nanofluid's volumetric thermal expansion coefficient is essential for thermal-hydraulic applications. In general, metallic oxide nanoparticles have a minimal volumetric thermal expansion coefficient when contrasted with water. Thus, it is assumed that when the concentration of nanoparticles in the mixture escalates, the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient decreases. Several researchers have conducted experiments and utilized their experimental data to develop empirical formulas and models for determining the properties of nanofluids. A handful of experimental investigations [[20], [21], [22], [23], [24]] were undertaken to determine the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. Austin [20] employed the Pulfrich-Fizeau interference method to measure α-Al2O3 nanoparticles' volumetric thermal expansion coefficient at different temperatures. Later, Balakrishnan et al. [21] employed techniques, including field emission scanning electron microscopy, high-temperature X-ray diffraction, and atomic force microscopy, to experimentally measure the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of γ-Al2O3 nanoparticles at diverse temperatures. Recently, Dorogokupets et al. [22] undertook experimental studies to ascertain rock-forming materials' volumetric thermal expansion coefficient, including Cr2O3, α-Fe2O3, α-Al2O3, and Fe3O4, under high-temperature and high-pressure conditions. Their investigation involved employing diverse measurements such as X-ray diffraction, ultrasonic, thermochemical, and dilatometric methods. Ho et al. [23] directed experiments to quantify the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of Al2O3-water nanofluid at several nanoparticle concentrations and temperatures. They also juxtaposed the experimental data with theoretical relations obtained from commonly adopted formulas in the literature. Similarly, Nayak et al. [24] explicitly determined the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of CuO-water, Al2O3-water, and TiO2-water nanofluids for various temperatures at a fixed nanoparticle concentration through experimentation. Later, Khanafer and Vafai [25] proposed an expression using the experimental findings obtained by Ho et al. [23] to calculate the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of Al2O3-water nanofluid at various temperatures and nanoparticle concentrations. However, this correlation only applies to specific temperatures and nanoparticle concentrations. Following the study made by Nayak et al. [24], Zahmatkesh and Ebrahimi [26] steered an experimental investigation to ascertain the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of five distinct nanofluids: CuO-water, Al2O3-water, Al2O3-CuO-water, TiO2-water, and Al2O3-TiO2-water nanofluids. Their study encompassed various temperature ranges, differing from those considered by Ho et al. [23] and Nayak et al. [24].

Despite recent advancements, the literature suggests a need for a comprehensive study to select the most suitable model for predicting the thermophysical characteristics of nanofluids. In a contemporary survey, Ruvo et al. [27] explicitly evaluated the existing models for determining thermal conduction coefficient and dynamic viscosity to identify the most reliable model aligned with experimental data. However, to the authors' knowledge, no study has systematically determined the optimal model for estimating nanofluids' volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. This study, therefore, focuses on the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient and aims to identify which model or correlation yields the most accurate results by comparing them with the findings derived from the experimental data, explicitly considering buoyancy-driven heat transport phenomena. A non-homogeneously heated square cavity containing Al2O3-water nanofluid is employed to determine the mean Nusselt number using various models for measuring the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. The findings are juxtaposed with experimental data to identify the model that best aligns with reality. Moreover, this study addresses errors observed in the previous research regarding the volumetric thermal expansion of nanoparticles. Most authors erroneously regarded nanoparticles’ linear thermal expansion coefficient as the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. However, this study corrects this error by rationally distinguishing between these coefficients.

The coefficient of thermal expansion describes how a material's size changes in response to temperature variations. This change can involve length or volume alterations, leading to linear and volumetric thermal expansion coefficients. This method divides the computational region into several mesh elements consisting of irregular triangular and quadrilateral cells. Results and discussion

The nanofluid's effective volumetric thermal expansion coefficient depends on the nanoparticle volume concentration and temperature. Using equations (1), (2), (3), (4) from the existing literature, the dependence of the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of Al2O3-water nanofluid on the volume fraction of the nanoparticle is investigated at 0.003 ≤ ϕ ≤ 0.4 and T = 26 °C. The results are juxtaposed with the experimental data of Ho et al. [23], as depicted in Fig. 2(a). While a slight fluctuation is noted in the experimental value of βv,nf with respect to ϕ, overall, the values of βv,nf predicted by all correlations exhibit a downward trend. This is because metallic oxide nanoparticles typically have a lower volumetric thermal expansion coefficient than water. As a result, when the nanoparticles' volume concentration rises, the nanofluid's volumetric thermal expansion coefficient decreases. However, there are slight deviations from the experimental results since the magnitudes predicted by Eqs. (1), (2), (3), (4) exhibit a similar trend. The nanofluid temperature is essential for rationally estimating the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. The variation of βv,nf for Al2O3-water nanofluid concerning temperature is demonstrated in Fig. 2(b). When the nanofluid temperature rises, an increase in the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient is observed. The values estimated by (1) reasonably agree with the experimental data of Ho et al. [23]. However, within the identical temperature range and fixed nanoparticle concentration, Nayak et al. [24] found slightly higher values of βv,nf than other studies.

The volumetric thermal expansion coefficient holds significance in analyzing any natural or mixed convection problem, owing to its contribution to the body force term computed through the Boussinesq approximation. The volumetric thermal expansion coefficient is directly associated (see (6)) with the buoyancy force that drives free convective currents. Therefore, a higher thermal expansion coefficient leads to stronger convective currents, increasing heat transfer. The percentage change in Nu, concerning the nanoparticle volume fraction, varies across different Ra, as illustrated in Fig. 4. At lower Ra (=103), the percentage change in Nu increases with rising ϕ and exceeds unity when ϕ > 0.02, as portrayed in Fig. 4(a). This phenomenon can be ascribed to the dominance of conduction over convection. Therefore, despite the lower βv,nf at higher ϕ (see Fig. 2(a)), ΔNu increases due to higher conductive heat transport and weaker convection currents, particularly at lower Ra. Furthermore, the elevated thermal conductivity of the nanofluid at elevated nanoparticle volume fractions enhances heat transfer within this conduction-dominated regime. However, when Ra increases, free convective heat transfer dominates over conduction. Thus, at moderate Ra (=106) and higher ϕ (lower βv,nf) weakens the strength of free convective currents due to reduced buoyancy force, resulting in a reduction in ΔNu (see Fig. 4(b)), a phenomenon supported by the findings of Nnanna [46] and Qi et al. [47]. Remarkably, at higher Ra values (=109), an increase in ϕ (=0.04) seems to enhance Nu, as shown in Fig. 4(c). This improvement can be attributed to the vigorous mixing at elevated Ra. When the fluid motion intensifies, it promotes a more uniform temperature distribution within the system. This mixing helps counteract the adverse effects of the reduced thermal expansion coefficient on heat transfer, which can hinder buoyancy-driven convection. Consequently, the enhanced mixing facilitates better thermal energy distribution, ultimately leading to a significant increase in overall heat transfer efficiency in the system. However, models (2) and (4) overestimate the value of βv,nf (see Fig. 2(a)). Consequently, ΔNu is higher for these two models than the results attained using the experimental data of Ho. et al. [23] and other models, as shown in Fig. 4. The subsequent task entails assessing the proximity of outcomes predicted by different correlations (1) to (4) along with the experimental data of Ho. et al. [23] to determine the best model.

Conclusion

This study extensively examines and compares the existing models to identify the most reliable model for rationally estimating the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. Rational evaluation of this coefficient is crucial for utilizing the Boussinesq approximation in problems related to natural and mixed convection. A standard test case involving a differentially heated cavity is selected to inspect the free convective flow of Al2O3-water nanofluid. Through meticulous analysis and comparison with the experimental data, four different equations commonly employed in various studies are thoroughly reviewed to evaluate their accuracy in estimating the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient. Discrepancies between the calculated values and experimental data are scrutinized meticulously, utilizing diverse analytical techniques to assess associated errors. The summary of the key outcomes is presented below.

•Among these four equations for estimating the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of Al2O3-water nanofluid under specified conditions, the best resemblance with experimental data is observed with the empirical relation proposed by Khanfer and Vafai [25].

•Based on mixing rule theory, the model presented by (3) yields reasonable results, indicating its potential for estimating the volumetric thermal expansion coefficient not only for Al2O3-water nanofluid but also for any nanofluid. However, utilizing this model requires the knowledge of the mass density and volumetric thermal expansion coefficient of the nanoparticles and base fluid.

•At 4 % nanoparticle volume fraction, the model presented by (4) shows a maximum MSE of 1.88 for the mean Nusselt number, while Khanfer and Vafai's correlation yields a minimum MSE of 0.40.

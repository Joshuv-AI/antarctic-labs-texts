Investigating Hardware Injections in LIGO O3 Data: Simulated Signals from a Neutron Star in a Low-Mass X-ray Binary
Jediah Tau 1,2 and John T. Whelan 1,3

Abstract
During LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA’s third observing run (O3), simulated CW ( CW ) signals were added to the LIGO detectors as HW ( HW ) injections, which are physical injections into the interferometer control loop via auxiliary laser actuation on test-mass mirrors. These included two periodic signals mimicking a spinning neutron star in a binary system, similar to the low-mass X-ray binary ScoX1 ( ScoX1 ). HW injections serve as an important validation of a search method, as they occur before data cleaning and preparation. We searched for these injections using the model-based cross-correlation pipeline, which was used to search for CW from ScoX1 in the first three LVK observing runs. To simulate a realistic search, we used a search region of orbital parameter space of similar size to the plausible parameter ranges for ScoX1 , but containing the parameters of each HW injection. Our self-blinded analysis confidently detected one of the injections. The other, which was generated with a lower amplitude and in a more expensive region of frequency space, was not detected. We verified the imprint of this second signal by performing a deeper targeted search at its true parameters. However, such a deep search across the full parameter range would have been computationally infeasible.

3.2
Cost Estimate for Injection 17 Detection

Since we were unable to detect injection 17 with
T_{\text{max}}=300\mathsf{\,s}
, we
present a cost estimate for a search done at
T_{\text{max}}=4800\mathsf{\,s}
. As
stated in Section
3.1.2
, a full self-blind search with this
coherence time would be expected to yield the detection of the
signal. We show here, however, that this is computationally
infeasible for a single
5\mathsf{\,Hz}
frequency band.

The cost of a search scales as the the product of the number of SFT
pairs and templates (points in parameter space):

N_{\text{Pairs}}N_{\text{Templates}}
. This product roughly scales as

N_{\text{Pairs}}f_{0}^{2}T_{\text{max}}^{3}
, so we use the injection 17 targeted
searches to estimate
N_{\text{Pairs}}
and
N_{\text{Templates}}
for a
blind search, as spacing values for the parameter space coordinates
are calculated as metadata for a specified mismatch. We estimate that
the cost of a blind search for
T_{\text{max}}=4800\mathsf{\,s}
(in the

890-895\mathsf{\,Hz}
band) is
350553.0\mathsf{\,CPU}\mathsf{\,day}
; over

3.5\times 10^{4}
times more expensive than the search done at

T_{\text{max}}=300\mathsf{\,s}
.

4
Conclusions

Using the model-based cross-correlation pipeline, we searched for two continuous wave hardware injections (injections 16 and 17) that had parameters similar to that of Scorpius X-1. With search regions analogous to that in
[
10
]
, we detected injection 16 (which had a frequency of
235.567\mathsf{\,Hz}
), but were unable to detect injection 17 due to its lower amplitude and placement in a more expensive region of parameter space (
890-895\mathsf{\,Hz}
band). In principle, a search with a coherence time of
4800\mathsf{\,s}
(16 times longer than that of the original search) could recover the signal. However, we estimate that such a search has a cost of
~350553.0\mathsf{\,CPU}\mathsf{\,Day}
, which is computationally infeasible a single
5\mathsf{\,Hz}
frequency band.

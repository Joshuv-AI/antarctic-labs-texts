Efficacy and Safety of CagriSema for Metabolic Outcomes: Systematic Review and Pairwise Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials
Authors: Chuhan Shao, Hanmo Lin, Jie Yu, Haiyan Chen, Yaolin Ren, Jing Ren, Yuan Zeng, Yifan Wu, Haoyu Bai, Qian Zhang, Xinhua Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOverweight status, obesity, and diabetes represent significant socioeconomic burdens that require long-term management.MethodsTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of dual-agonist CagriSema vs semaglutide monotherapy, cagrilintide monotherapy, and placebo in adults with overweight/obese status and type 2 diabetes, we searched Embase, MEDLINE, PubMed, and the Cochrane Library up to 14 June 2026 for randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Separate pairwise meta-analyses were conducted using random effects models.ResultsEight RCTs comprising 6898 participants were included. CagriSema was associated with significantly greater percentage body weight reduction compared to semaglutide [MD −6.60%; 95%CI: −8.51 to −4.68; P<0.0001; I2 =82%], cagrilintide [MD −8.15%; 95%CI: −11.40 to −4.89; P<0.0001; I2 =95%], and placebo [MD −14.30%; 95%CI: −17.41 to −11.19; P<0.0001; I2 =99%]. A greater decrease in the percentage of glycated hemoglobin was also observed for CagriSema than for semaglutide [MD −0.09%; 95%CI: −0.14 to −0.04; P=0.0004; I2 =39%], cagrilintide [MD −0.87%; 95% CI −1.48 to −0.26; p = 0.005; I2 = 97%], and placebo [MD −1.55%; 95% CI −2.14 to −0.97; p < 0.0001; I2 = 99%]. Furthermore, CagriSema achieved significantly greater reductions in absolute body weight, waist circumference and body mass index across all comparators (all p < 0.0001). CagriSema demonstrated better blood pressure control compared with cagrilintide and placebo, with effects comparable to those of semaglutide. Moreover, CagriSema significantly decreased C-reactive protein and improved lipid profiles compared with cagrilintide and placebo. CagriSema increased any adverse events and gastrointestinal adverse events compared with all comparators. Substantial statistical heterogeneity was observed in several continuous outcomes, driven by diverse baseline clinical characteristics and background therapies.ConclusionCompared with current standard monotherapies and placebos, CagriSema demonstrates superior weight loss, glucose, and lipid control. Key limitations include the relatively small number of eligible trials and the current lack of long-term cardiovascular endpoints.Keywords: meta-analysis, randomized controlled trial, CagriSema, obesity, type 2 diabetes

Introduction

Currently, overweight status, obesity, and diabetes are becoming significant socioeconomic burdens. Globally, between 1990 and 2022, the adult diabetes rate experienced a twofold increase, climbing from 7% to 14%.1 As a primary contributor to various cardiovascular diseases and the greatest risk factor for diabetes, compared with the baseline in 1990, the obesity epidemic worsened drastically by 2022; the worldwide number of obese women and men increased by 377 million and 307 million, ultimately culminating in 504 million and 374 million cases, respectively.2 Moderate weight loss (eg, 5–15%) can effectively reduce HbA1c levels, improve blood pressure and lipid profiles, and even improve cardiovascular outcomes.3,4

Among the currently available drug treatment options, medications within the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) class, including semaglutide, function chiefly by stimulating the release of insulin. Furthermore, a Phase 3 investigation demonstrated that administering a 2.4 mg dose of semaglutide enabled overweight or obese adults with concurrent type 2 diabetes to achieve a mean body weight reduction of 9.6%.5 It also improved the glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) in diabetes patients in the SUSTAIN 1 trial.6 Cagrilintide is a novel long-acting amylin analog. Amylin is a peptide hormone released by pancreatic β cells, and it can reduce food intake and weight through the activation of various regions in the brain. The mechanism of action of cagrilintide is different from that of GLP-1RAs, but when it is used in combination, it can produce a synergistic effect.7 In the STEP 1 to 3 trials, the most common safety issue associated with semaglutide was gastrointestinal adverse effects, mostly mild to moderate, including nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation.8–10 For cagrilintide, gastrointestinal disorders and administration-site reactions were the most frequent adverse effects.11

Formulated as a fixed-dose combination, the innovative dual-agonist CagriSema integrates semaglutide (a GLP-1 receptor agonist) with the long-acting amylin analog cagrilintide. Through the integration of distinct yet cooperative weight-management mechanisms, this agent has currently advanced to Phase III clinical development. While the individual safety profiles of semaglutide and cagrilintide are characterized predominantly by mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal events, it remains critical to systematically evaluate whether their co-administration as CagriSema exacerbates these side effects or alters the overall tolerability profile.

Two existing meta-analyses primarily focused on obese populations and head-to-head comparisons between CagriSema and semaglutide monotherapy.12,13 Moreover, these analyses only included the previously published 3–4 studies,12 did not fully discuss the coexistence of diabetes,13 and did not incorporate data from multi-arm RCTs that include cagrilintide monotherapy and placebo.12,13 To address this gap, the present study compared CagriSema effects with semaglutide monotherapy, cagrilintide monotherapy, and placebo by adding 4 recently published RCTs. We systematically integrate key clinical trial data from Enebo 2021, Frias 2023, Garvey 2025, Davies 2025, Yamauchi 2026, Buse 2026, Rosenstock 2026, and Aroda 2026. This analysis aims to offer updated and comprehensive evidence on the effects of CagriSema (2.4/2.4 mg), vs semaglutide (2.4 mg), cagrilintide (2.4 mg), or placebo on outcomes including weight reduction, glycemic control, blood pressure improvement, and safety outcomes.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines.14 The study protocol was registered prospectively in the PROSPERO database (CRD420261347815).

Search Strategy

On June 14, 2026, we conducted a search in four databases (PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, and MEDLINE via Ovid). The following MeSH terms and free-text keywords were applied: ((“cagrilintide and semaglutide”) OR CagriSema OR cagrilintide-semaglutide OR “cagrilintide semaglutide” OR “semaglutide and combination*” OR “semaglutide and adjunct*” OR “cagrilintide and combination*” OR “cagrilintide and adjunct*”) AND (“Diabetes Mellitus, Type 2”[MeSH] OR “type 2 diabetes mellitus” OR “Obesity”[MeSH] OR obesity OR overweight). We applied a validated search filter to restrict the results to RCTs. Additionally, we explicitly excluded animal-only studies, retracted publications, and nonoriginal articles (such as reviews and editorials). No language restrictions were applied. The detailed step-by-step search strategy is provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Data Extraction

All the search results were imported into EndNote 21. The literature screening and inclusion procedures were conducted in strict accordance with the PRISMA guidelines, with the detailed flow of studies depicted in a PRISMA flowchart (Figure 1). A total of 169 records were import into EndNote.

PRISMA flow diagram of the literature search and study selection process.

Two reviewers (C.S. and H.L.) independently screened the titles and abstracts. Sixty-seven duplicated records were removed. We excluded 92 reviews and clearly irrelevant studies. We subsequently conducted a further evaluation of the remaining 10 full-text articles. In cases of disagreement, we discussed the results with a third reviewer (J.Y.) and reached a consensus. When selecting the research participants, we did not impose any restrictions on the publication time or language type of the articles. Only randomized controlled trials that met the following conditions were included in the study scope: (i) trials were conducted in adults who were overweight or obese or patients with type 2 diabetes, according to the study-specific BMI eligibility criteria, and the effects of CagriSema were compared with those of cagrilintide monotherapy, semaglutide monotherapy, or placebo; and (ii) trials reported at least one of the research results we were concerned about. We excluded studies that were duplicates, were available only as abstracts, did not report any relevant outcomes, or involved a duplicate population. Among the 10 studies that met the inclusion criteria, one was removed because of ineligible outcomes, and the other was removed because of an overlapping population. Finally, eight studies met all eligibility criteria and were included in the final statistical analysis.15–22

Quality Assessment

The Cochrane Risk of Bias tool version 2 (RoB 2)23 was used to assess the quality of studies by two independent authors (C.S. and H.L.) assessing the following areas: randomization process; deviation from expected intervention; missing outcome data; outcome measurement; selection of reported results; overall bias. Each area was rated as low, with some concerns or high risk of bias. Any assessment discrepancies were resolved through discussion.

Outcomes

We grouped the outcomes into three categories: 1. body weight change, 2. cardiometabolic parameters, including glucose control parameters, blood pressure, lipid parameters, and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (CRP), and 3. safety outcomes. Body weight outcomes included (i) body weight change (%), (ii) body weight change (kg), (iii) body mass index (BMI) change, and (iv) waist circumference. Cardiometabolic parameters included (i) fasting plasma glucose (FPG), (ii) HbA1c, (iii) systolic blood pressure (SBP), (iv) diastolic blood pressure (DBP) and ratio to baseline lipids, (v) total cholesterol (TC), (vi) triglycerides (TG), (vii) high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), (viii) low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c), and (ix) ratio to high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP). Safety outcomes included (i) any adverse events (AAEs), (ii) serious adverse events (SAEs), and (iii) gastrointestinal adverse events (GAEs). Among them, body weight change % and HbA1c % are regarded as primary outcomes, whereas others are regarded as secondary outcomes and safety outcomes.

Statistical Analysis

All data aggregation and statistical analyses were performed using Review Manager (RevMan) version 5.4 and R software (R Core Team, 2024. R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria. https://www.R-project.org/). To ensure strict methodological homogeneity, we consistently extracted data corresponding to the efficacy estimand (or trial product estimand) across all trials. This uniform approach was chosen because the efficacy estimand was universally reported in all 8 included RCTs, whereas the treatment policy estimand was unavailable in certain trials. For continuous outcomes (body weight change, BMI, HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, and blood pressure), we used mean differences (MDs) from baseline with their standard deviations (SDs). When studies reported mean differences with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) or standard errors (SEs), these values were converted to MDs and SDs using the RevMan 5.4 calculator. For multiarm trials, only the relevant comparator arm was included in each specific pairwise meta-analysis to avoid unit-of-analysis error, in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook Chapter 23.3.2.24 Specifically, for multiarm trials, because we conducted distinct and independent pairwise meta-analyses (CagriSema vs semaglutide monotherapy vs cagrilintide monotherapy, and vs placebo), the shared intervention group (CagriSema) was not double-counted within any single meta-analysis. Furthermore, for trials including multiple dose arms of the same medication, we exclusively extracted data from the target maintenance dose arm (eg, 2.4 mg) and excluded lower dose arms to ensure clinical relevance and a clean pairwise comparison. We evaluated the efficacy of CagriSema against semaglutide monotherapy, cagrilintide monotherapy, and placebo in distinct and independent forest plots. We intentionally utilized separate pairwise meta-analyses rather than a network meta-analysis (NMA). While an NMA is frequently considered for trials involving multiple comparators, it strictly relies on the core assumption of transitivity.25,26 The included trials exhibited profound clinical heterogeneity, involving populations strictly with obesity alone (eg, Garvey 2025) vs those with advanced type 2 diabetes requiring basal insulin (eg, Rosenstock 2026), alongside distinct background therapies, which would violate the core transitivity assumption required for a valid NMA. Given that direct head-to-head evidence was sufficient to evaluate the synergistic superiority of CagriSema over its monocomponents and placebo, the pairwise approach provides methodologically sound conclusions. For ratio outcomes (eg, lipid parameters and hsCRP), data were log-transformed prior to pooling using the generic inverse variance method, and the results were exponentiated back to estimated treatment ratios (ETRs). For safety outcomes, the risk ratios (RRs) and their 95% CIs were calculated. We also checked heterogeneity using the I2 statistic: 25%–50% = low, 50%–75% = moderate, and above 75% = substantial. If heterogeneity was greater than 50%, a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was performed to explore the potential sources of heterogeneity. Subgroup analysis was not performed because of the small number of studies. All meta-analyses were conducted using a random-effects model, with statistical significance defined as a two-sided p value < 0.05. In accordance with the Cochrane Handbook, tests for funnel plot asymmetry are not recommended when a meta-analysis includes fewer than 10 studies, as the statistical power is insufficient to distinguish chance from true asymmetry; therefore, publication bias was not formally assessed via funnel plots or Egger’s test.

Certainty of Evidence

We used this method to assess the certainty of the evidence and determined the certainty of each outcome on the basis of the risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. These indicators are rated as high, moderate, low, or very low.

Results

Study Selection

Characteristics of the Included Studies and Risk of Bias

Seven studies were multicenter studies (Frias 2023, Garvey 2025, Davies 2025, Yamauchi 2026, Aroda 2026, Buse 2026, Rosenstock 2026), and one was a single-center study (Enebo 2021). Baseline populations varied across trials: Enebo 2021 and Garvey 2025 (BMI ≥ 27 kg/m2) enrolled individuals who were overweight or obese, whereas Yamauchi 2026 (BMI ≥ 27 kg/m2) included a similar population, with 20% of the population having comorbid T2DM. The remaining trials exclusively enrolled patients with T2DM and concurrent overweight or obese status, using varying BMI inclusion thresholds: ≥ 27 kg/m2 (Frias 2023, Davies 2025), ≥ 25 kg/m2 (Buse 2026, Rosenstock 2026), and ≥ 23 kg/m2 (Aroda 2026). The participants in the CagriSema group had a mean age of 43 to 59.2 years. The baseline mean body weight ranged from 88.9 to 107.2 kg, the HbA1c concentration ranged from 5.2% to 8.8%, and the BMI ranged from 32.0 to 38.0 kg/m2. The control groups had similar characteristics: mean age 41 to 62 years, body weight 88.9 to 107.4 kg, HbA1c 5.4% to 8.8%, and BMI 31.5 to 38.0 kg/m2. Garvey 2025 had the highest baseline BMI, and Rosenstock 2026 had the highest baseline HbA1c. All the trials reported the same primary outcomes, and most of them included weight change, glycemic parameters, blood pressure, lipid parameters, and safety outcomes. All the studies showed a low risk of bias in all domains. These results indicate that the findings are generally reliable (Supplementary Figure S1).

Primary Outcome

In independent pairwise comparisons, CagriSema resulted in a significantly greater percentage of body weight reduction across all comparators. Specifically, CagriSema was superior to semaglutide monotherapy (MD −6.60%; 95% CI −8.51 to −4.68; p < 0.0001; I2 = 82%), cagrilintide monotherapy (MD −8.15%; 95% CI −11.40 to −4.89; p < 0.0001; I2 = 95%), and placebo (MD −14.30%; 95% CI −17.41 to −11.19; p < 0.0001; I2 = 99%) (Figure 2A-C).

Sensitivity analysis revealed that the exclusion of Buse 2026 reduced heterogeneity to 32% while preserving significance compared with semaglutide (Supplementary Figure S2). Exclusion of Garvey 2025 eliminated heterogeneity (I2=0%) while preserving significance when compared with cagrilintide or placebo (Supplementary Figure S3 and 4). CagriSema produced a significantly greater decrease in the HbA1c percentage across all the comparators. CagriSema was superior to semaglutide monotherapy (MD −0.09%; 95% CI −0.14 to −0.04; p = 0.0004; I2 = 39%), cagrilintide monotherapy (MD −0.87%; 95% CI −1.48 to −0.26; p = 0.005; I2 = 97%), and placebo (MD −1.55%; 95% CI −2.14 to −0.97; p < 0.0001; I2 = 99%) (Figure 3A-C).

Sensitivity analysis revealed that the exclusion of Garvey 2025 reduced heterogeneity to 0% and 42% while preserving significance compared with cagrilintide and placebo respectively (Supplementary Figure S5 and 6).

Secondary Outcomes

Body Weight Related End Points

Consistent with the primary outcome, compared with all three control groups, CagriSema demonstrated greater reductions in absolute body weight, BMI, and waist circumference. CagriSema outperformed active comparators. Compared with semaglutide, CagriSema achieved a significantly greater weight reduction of −7.27 kg (95% Cl −8.41 to −6.12; p < 0.0001; I2 = 44%) and yielded mean differences (MD) of −10.17 kg (95% CI −13.70 to −6.64; p < 0.0001; I2 = 73.4%) vs cagrilintide. Compared with the placebo group, the CagriSema group lost 15.32 kg (95% CI −21.58 to −9.06; p < 0.0001; I2 = 99%) more (Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S7-9).

Summary forest plots of secondary cardiometabolic and body-weight-related outcomes. Panels represent pairwise comparisons of CagriSema vs semaglutide, cagrilintide, and placebo for absolute body weight (kg), body mass index (kg/m2), waist circumference (cm), fasting plasma glucose (mmol/L), systolic blood pressure (mmHg), and diastolic blood pressure (mmHg).

To explore the potential sources of the substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 99%) observed in the absolute body weight change analysis, we performed a comprehensive leave-one-out sensitivity analysis (Supplementary Figure S10 and 11). The results demonstrated that when compared with placebo, the exclusion of any single study did not meaningfully resolve the high heterogeneity (with I2 remaining above 75%), nor did it alter the overall direction of the pooled effect. This finding indicates that the observed statistical heterogeneity was not driven by a single outlier but rather reflects pervasive clinical diversity across the included trials.

In terms of waist circumference reduction, CagriSema was significantly more effective than semaglutide (MD −3.82 cm; 95% CI −4.53 to −3.12; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0) and placebo (MD −11.02 cm; 95% CI −14.18 to −7.87; p < 0.0001; I2 = 97%) (Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S12 and 13). Leave-one-out sensitivity analysis revealed that the exclusion of Garvey 2025 resolved the high heterogeneity to 0% vs placebo (Supplementary Figure S14).

Compared with semaglutide, CagriSema reduced the BMI by more than −2.41 kg/m2 (95% CI −2.79 to −2.03; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%). Compared with the placebo, CagriSema reduced the BMI by more than −6.20 kg/m2 (95% CI −9.14 to −3.26; p < 0.0001; I2 = 100%) (Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S15 and 16). The sensitivity analysis results are shown in Supplementary Figure S17.

Glycemic Control

The impact of CagriSema on glycemic parameters varied by comparator type. When compared head-to-head with semaglutide, the differences in FPG (MD −0.09 mmol/L; 95% CI −0.18 to 0.00; p = 0.0496; I2 = 37%) suggested a slightly better fasting glycemic efficacy between the dual-agonist and semaglutide monotherapy groups. Compared with cagrilintide monotherapy, CagriSema did not significantly reduce FPG (p = 0.0919). Compared with the placebo, CagriSema resulted in a better FPG control (MD −1.86 mmol/L; 95% CI −2.94 to −0.78; p = 0.0008; I2 = 99%) (Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S18-20). The results of sensitivity analysis are shown in Supplementary Figure S21 and 22. A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was conducted to investigate the considerable heterogeneity (I2 = 99%) in FPG. However, omitting individual trials sequentially failed to substantially reduce the heterogeneity, although the primary direction of the treatment effect remained consistent.

Cardiometabolic-Related End Points

CagriSema had a more favorable effect on blood pressure than cagrilintide and placebo did. Compared with cagrilintide (MD −7.55 mmHg; 95% CI −11.49 to −3.61; p = 0.0002; I2 =74%) and placebo (MD −7.11 mmHg; 95% CI −9.15 to −5.06; p < 0.0001; I2 = 75%), CagriSema produced a significantly greater reduction in SBP. In terms of DBP, CagriSema achieved a significant reduction compared with cagrilintide (MD −2.44 mmHg; 95% CI −3.47 to −1.40; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%) and placebo (MD −2.18 mmHg; 95% CI −3.84 to −0.52; p = 0.0102; I2 = 86%). However, compared with semaglutide, the difference in SBP (p = 0.0675) and DBP (p = 0.0511) did not reach statistical significance (Figure 4, Supplementary Figure S23-28). The estimated treatment ratios (ETRs) for changes in hsCRP (ratio to baseline) were compared between semaglutide and placebo, with ETRs of 0.71 (95% CI: 0.66 to 0.77; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%), and 0.42 (95% CI: 0.36 to 0.49; p < 0.0001; I2 = 69%) (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure S29 and 30). A sensitivity analysis of those results is shown in Supplementary Figure S31-36. A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was conducted to investigate the considerable heterogeneity in SBP and DBP when semaglutide was compared. However, omitting individual trials sequentially failed to substantially reduce the heterogeneity. Omitting Garvey 2025 et al eliminated the heterogeneity in SBP, DBP, and the ratio to baseline hsCRP compared with placebo.

The estimated treatment ratios (ETRs) for changes in lipid profiles (ratio to baseline) were evaluated across three comparators: semaglutide, cagrilintide, and placebo.

Compared with semaglutide, cagrilintide, and placebo, the ETRs for TC were 1.02 (95% CI: 1.00 to 1.03; p = 0.0094; I2 = 23%), 0.96 (95% CI: 0.94 to 0.98; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%), and 0.95 (95% CI: 0.94 to 0.96; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%) (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure S37-39), respectively. A similar trend was observed for LDL-c, with ETRs of 0.94 (95% CI: 0.92 to 0.96; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%), and 0.95 (95% CI: 0.92 to 0.98; p = 0.0005; I2 = 28%) compared with cagrilintide and placebo; however, the comparison with semaglutide was not significant (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure S40-42).

For TG, CagriSema demonstrated significant decreases across all comparators. The ETRs relative to semaglutide, cagrilintide, and placebo were 0.92 (95% CI: 0.86 to 0.98; p = 0.0126; I2 = 62%), 0.86 (95% CI: 0.80 to 0.92; p < 0.0001; I2 = 13%), and 0.75 (95% CI:0.73 to 0.77; p < 0.0001; I2 = 49%), respectively (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure S43-45). Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses using semaglutide for comparison revealed that omitting Yamauchi 2026 (for the semaglutide comparison) eliminated substantial heterogeneity, reducing I2 to 2.8% (Supplementary Figure S46).

The ETRs for HDL-c were 1.05 (95% CI: 1.03 to 1.07; p < 0.0001; I2 = 49%), and 1.08 (95% CI: 1.04 to 1.11; p < 0.0001; I2 = 62%) compared with semaglutide and placebo, respectively. However, the comparison with cagrilintide was not significant (p = 0.8567) (Figure 5, Supplementary Figure S47-49). The heterogeneity in the placebo comparisons was successfully resolved by omitting Aroda 2026 (Supplementary Figure S50-51).

Safety Outcomes

Safety profiles were systematically evaluated through separate pairwise comparisons against placebo, semaglutide monotherapy, and cagrilintide monotherapy. With respect to the incidence of any adverse events (AAEs), CagriSema was significantly higher than semaglutide (RR 1.04; 95% CI 1.01 to 1.07; p = 0.0028; I2 = 0%), cagrilintide (RR 1.08; 95% CI 1.04 to 1.13; p = 0.0004; I2 = 37%) and placebo (RR 1.13 95% CI 1.06 to 1.19; p = 0.0001; I2 = 52%) (Figure 6, Supplementary Figure S52-54). The incidence of serious adverse events (SAEs) remained low across all trials, with no significant differences observed when CagriSema was compared with semaglutide (p = 0.8505), cagrilintide (p = 0.8215), or placebo (p = 0.6447) (Figure 6, Supplementary Figure S55-57). Furthermore, gastrointestinal adverse events (GAEs) were the leading treatment-emergent adverse events. As expected for incretin-based dual-agonists, CagriSema significantly increased the risk of GAEs relative to semaglutide alone (RR 1.21; 95% CI 1.10 to 1.34; p = 0.0002; I2 = 41%), cagrilintide monotherapy (RR 1.73; 95% CI 1.44 to 2.10; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%) and placebo (RR 2.07; 95% CI 1.92 to 2.24; p < 0.0001; I2 = 0%) (Figure 6, Supplementary Figure S58-60). GRADE Assessment

In accordance with the GRADE approach, compared with semaglutide, the certainty of evidence was rated as high for body weight parameters, glucose parameters, and most lipid parameters. The certainty was rated as moderate for LDL-c and SAEs and low for blood pressure outcomes. Compared with cagrilintide, the certainty of evidence was rated as moderate for FPG, HDL, and SAEs and high for all the other outcomes. Compared with placebo, the certainty of evidence was rated as moderate for absolute body weight, FPG, and SAEs, and high for all the other outcomes. Discussion

In our systematic review, we performed separate pairwise meta-analyses to evaluate efficacy and safety. Our findings may provide evidence that compared with placebo and active comparators, the dual agonist CagriSema yields superior weight loss, and glucose- and lipid-lowering efficacy.

CagriSema is the combination of a long-acting amylin analog, cagrilintide and a GLP-1RA, semaglutide. Secreted from L cells of the lower intestine, GLP-1 reduces gastric and small bowel motility through mechanisms by selectively targeting the hypothalamus or hindbrain regions.27,28 When it is co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta-cells upon nutrient ingestion, amylin regulates appetite, which is orchestrated by specifically targeting the nucleus of the solitary tract, and the area postrema. The hypothalamus, the ventral tegmental area, and the laterodorsal tegmental nucleus are also involved.27,29 Compared with monotherapy, the combination of these two drugs appears to exert synergistic effects. Phase 1b trials (Enebo 2021), Phase 2 RCTs (Frias 2023), and published Phase 3 trials including REDEFINE 1, REDEFINE 2, REDEFINE 5, and REIMAGINE 1–3 have investigated the efficacy of CagriSema over single drugs or placebos in different races, indications, and baseline drugs, and have shown favorable metabolic benefits. In comparison with other currently available antiobesity medications, tirzepatide reduced body weight by 12%–22% and 10%–16% more than placebo did, depending on dose and duration.30,31 With respect to semaglutide, 13–16% weight reduction and a 6–12% greater weight reduction than with placebo were observed in the STEP 1–3 trials.5,9,32 The use of other multihormonal drugs, such as Retatrutide, reduced weight by 16–17% at 24 weeks,33 and Mazdutide led to 10% to 12% weight loss at 48 weeks.34 Despite a smaller body of evidence, the efficacy signals seen with CagriSema to date might indicate its potential as competitive therapies currently in late-phase trials. The consistently larger reductions were observed with CagriSema than with semaglutide across available trials. However, in the absence of direct head-to-head trials against other agents, and given that current evidence for CagriSema is primarily derived from studies with short- to medium-term follow-up, it remains uncertain whether these initial weight-loss trajectories will be sustained long-term to match or exceed established benchmarks.

Substantial heterogeneity in our primary outcomes (weight reduction percentage and change in HbA1c level) might fundamentally be driven by distinct baseline clinical characteristics. In terms of weight loss compared with semaglutide, the variance primarily stemmed from the REIMAGINE 2 trial (Buse et al), which enrolled patients with a lower BMI threshold (≥ 25 kg/m2) and diabetes inadequately controlled by active therapies, making weight loss more difficult. Conversely, for comparisons against cagrilintide or placebo, excluding the study by Garvey et al resolved the heterogeneity for both weight loss and HbA1c. As the only trial exclusively enrolling nondiabetic individuals, Garvey et al reported a substantially greater magnitude of weight loss but restricted absolute HbA1c reductions probably because the patients’ normoglycemic baseline was tightly regulated. This greater weight loss efficacy in nondiabetic patients than in diabetic patients is typical for incretin therapies.35

This diabetic vs nondiabetic dichotomy may similarly explain the heterogeneity in absolute weight reduction (kg) and waist circumference, which was completely resolved upon excluding the study by Garvey et al. In contrast, leave-one-out analyses failed to reduce the heterogeneity below 50% for FPG. This persistent variance may reflect the systemic clinical diversity across the pooled trials, specifically the wide range of mean baseline HbA1c levels (5.5% to 8.8%) and disparate background antidiabetic regimens.

Finally, the heterogeneity observed in secondary cardiometabolic biomarkers (eg, blood pressure, lipids, and hsCRP) was variable. While excluding individual trials resolved the statistical variance in certain comparisons (eg, specific lipid fractions vs placebo), heterogeneity frequently persisted in others. This variance likely reflects the diverse baseline profiles and the differing background adjustments of concomitant medications (such as antihypertensives and statins) across the included trials.

A crucial addition to our pairwise analysis is the clarification of the safety profile of CagriSema across specific active controls. We revealed that dual-agonist therapy predictably increased gastrointestinal adverse events. Additionally, the incidence of SAEs did not significantly increase across any comparisons. These findings suggest that the addition of cagrilintide to semaglutide may amplify weight loss efficacy while increasing gastrointestinal adverse events in short-to-medium term.

Two currently published meta-analyses have investigated the efficacy of CagriSema and semaglutide, especially in terms of obesity, and have demonstrated the advantages of CagriSema over semaglutide in reducing body weight. However, they include only 3–4 trials, highlighting the need for up-to-date, larger-sample meta-analyses that further compare CagriSema with not only semaglutide but also cagrilintide and placebo, expanding the outcome measures to include comprehensive cardiometabolic parameters (such as lipid profiles, waist circumference, and hsCRP) to better understand the overall clinical efficacy of CagriSema in obese populations with or without diabetes.

Limitations

Several limitations of this meta-analysis must be acknowledged. First, given the relative novelty of CagriSema, the total number of eligible randomized controlled trials remains small. Specifically, pooled analyses for secondary cardiometabolic outcomes comparing CagriSema against cagrilintide monotherapy currently rely on only two trials; thus, these specific estimates should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Additionally, we deliberately conducted separate pairwise meta-analyses instead of a network meta-analysis (NMA). While an NMA can rank multiple interventions, it strictly relies on the assumption of transitivity. As shown by our baseline characteristics, the included trials exhibited profound clinical heterogeneity, involving populations strictly with obesity alone (normoglycemic) vs those with advanced type 2 diabetes requiring basal insulin. Pooling such diverse effect modifiers into a single network would severely violate the transitivity assumption, potentially generating biased indirect estimates. Given that direct head-to-head evidence was sufficient for our specific objective—evaluating the synergistic superiority of CagriSema over its individual monocomponents and placebo—the pairwise approach provides sound and internally valid conclusions. Publication bias was not quantitatively assessed via funnel plots or Egger’s tests, as the number of included studies (n=8) did not meet the minimum threshold of 10 recommended by the Cochrane Handbook Chapter 13.3.4.4 to ensure adequate statistical power for detecting asymmetry.36 Second, comprehensive data concerning major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) are currently unavailable, underscoring the critical need for ongoing cardiovascular outcome trials. Additionally, the baseline BMI of the study population was concentrated between 32 and 38 kg/m2, which precluded further understanding of the benefits in extremely obese populations (BMI ≥ 40 kg/m2) or lower-weight populations.

Conclusion

Overall, this systematic review revealed that compared with semaglutide monotherapy, cagrilintide monotherapy, and placebo, CagriSema (2.4 mg/2.4 mg) resulted in greater reductions in body weight (both percentage and absolute values), waist circumference, and BMI. SBP and DBP reductions were significantly greater than those produced by cagrilintide monotherapy or placebo. HsCRP and lipid outcomes favored CagriSema. Furthermore, there was no significant difference in the incidence of SAEs, and although AAEs and GAEs increased, they were manageable. The GRADE certainty of evidence ranged from low to high. Notably, the drug has not yet been approved by the FDA, therefore, its safety, especially for long-term use, cannot be guaranteed.

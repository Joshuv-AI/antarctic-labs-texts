Commutativity and non-commutativity of limits in the nonlinear bending theory for prestrained microheterogeneous plates


Abstract
In this paper we study the derivation of nonlinear bending models for prestrained elastic plates from three-dimensional non-linear elasticity via homogenization and dimension reduction. We compare effective models obtained by either simultaneously or consecutively passing to the Γ \Gamma -limits as the thickness h ≪ 1 h\ll 1 and the size of the material microstructure ϵ ≪ 1 \epsilon\ll 1 vanish. In the regime ϵ ≪ h \epsilon\ll h we show that the consecutive and simultaneous limit are equivalent, and also analyze the rate of convergence. In contrast, we observe that there are several different limit models in the case h ≪ ϵ h\ll\epsilon .

Introduction and main result

In this paper we study the derivation of homogenized bending models from 3d nonlinear elasticity for prestrained, composite plates with a periodic microstructure. The starting point of the derivation is a functional
\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
describing the elastic energy of a prestrained, three-dimensional plate with thickness
h
and with a periodic microstructure on scale
\epsilon
. We are interested in effective models that describe the asymptotic behavior in the case when
h,\epsilon\ll 1
and thus study the
\Gamma
-limit of
\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
. Here the limit
h\to 0
corresponds to dimension reduction leading to a nonlinear bending model, and
\epsilon\to 0
corresponds to homogenization. In addition to the purely mathematical interest in simultaneous homogenization and dimension reduction, the analysis of thin periodic sheets is directly motivated by applications
[
GKOS20
,
GHO21
,
GOW20b
,
GOW20a
,
OFGW23
,
FGO24
]
.

Due to the presence of two small parameters, various different ways to pass to the limit can be considered. In particular, there are two consecutive limits corresponding to dimension reduction after homogenization and vice versa, and simultaneous limits when

(h,\epsilon)\to 0\qquad\text{with }\frac{h}{\epsilon}\to\gamma\in[0,\infty].

Here,
\gamma
is a scaling parameter that describes the relative scaling between
h
and
\epsilon
.
As indicated in Figure
1
and discussed in more detail below, most of the limits have already been established in the literature
[
NR25
,
BF07
,
BNPGS23
,
FJM02
,
NO15
]
. In this paper, we will consider these limits for prestrained materials. The analysis of such materials lies at the intersection of calculus of variations, physics, and geometry (see
[
KS14
,
KM14
,
LL20
,
LMP14
,
JBL21
]
and
[
Lew23
]
for a comprehensive review). In the case of the nonlinear bending theory for plates, as shown in
[
BNPGS23
]
the simultaneous limit leads to an energy functional
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
and a spontaneous curvature
B^{\gamma}_{\rm eff}
that explicitly depend on the scaling parameter
\gamma
.

In our paper, we investigate the convergence of
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
as
\gamma\to\infty
and
\gamma\to 0
. It is natural to expect that these limits are related to the two consecutive limits (as it is the case for nonlinear rod models as shown in
[
Neu12
,
BNS20
]
). In Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
we prove that in the case
\gamma\to\infty
we indeed recover the consecutive
\Gamma
-limit
\lim_{h\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
. The result shows that the diagram in Figure
1
is commutative in this regime. On the other hand, we also prove that the limit
\gamma\to 0
is different from the consecutive
\Gamma
-limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
, and thus the diagram is non-commutative in that regime. We note that the same observation has been made for plates without prestrain. The positive commutativity result suggests that the bending model
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
for
\gamma\gg 1
can be approximated by the consecutive limit
\mathcal{E}^{\infty}_{\hom}
, whose effective properties are easier to compute and analyze. In view of this, it is relevant to investigate the rate of convergence as
\gamma\to\infty
. In Theorem
1.5
and
1.8
we prove a convergence rate of the effective properties as
\gamma\to\infty
. The rate of convergence is confirmed by a simulation study that we provide in Subsection
1.4
. In the following we give a more detailed presentation of our results.

1.1
The three-dimensional model

The starting point of our analysis is the following energy functional of nonlinear elasticity:

\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}(v):=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\int_{\Omega}W(x^{\prime},x_{3},\tfrac{x^{\prime}}{\epsilon},\nabla_{h}v(x)(\mathrm{Id}+hB(x^{\prime},x_{3},\tfrac{x^{\prime}}{\epsilon}))^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}x.

(1)

Here,
\Omega:=S\times\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
describes the reference domain of the plate rescaled to unit thickness and
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a Lipschitz domain; points in
\Omega
are denoted by
x=(x^{\prime},x_{3})
with
x^{\prime}\in S
;
W
denotes a frame-invariant stored energy function with a single, non-degenerate energy-well at
\operatorname{SO}(3)
(for the exact hypothesis see Definition
4.1
below). The parameter
h
denotes the thickness of the plate. The scaled gradient

\nabla_{h}v:=\left(\partial_{x_{1}}v,\partial_{x_{2}}v,\frac{1}{h}\partial_{x_{3}}v\right)
is due to the rescaling to unit thickness.

The scaling factor
h^{-2}
corresponds to the bending regime: The elastic energy (normalized by volume) of a bending deformations of a plate of thickness
h
scales with
h^{2}
. Finally,
B
denotes a tensor field that describes an incremental prestrain that might be present in the reference configuration. We note that
B
is scaled with
h
leading to a prestrain that is compatible with the bending regime. Both the stored energy function
W(x,y,F)
and the prestrain
B(x,y)
are assumed to be periodic in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The functional
\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
thus describes a composite that is locally periodic in in-plane directions.

For the purpose of our paper the simultaneous limit

\mathop{\lim}\limits_{(h,\epsilon)\to 0\atop\frac{h}{\epsilon}\to\gamma}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}=\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}

is especially relevant: In
[
BNPGS23
]
it is shown that in the case
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
the convergence holds and the limit is a bending energy of the form

\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}(v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}\left(x^{\prime},{\operatorname{I\!I}}_{v}-B_{{\rm eff}}^{\gamma}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}+\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\gamma}&\text{if }v\in W^{2,2}_{{\rm iso}}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2)

Here,
W^{2,2}_{\rm iso}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\{v\in W^{2,2}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3})\,:\,\nabla v^{\top}\nabla v=\mathrm{Id}\}
denotes the space of bending deformations, and
{\operatorname{I\!I}}_{v}:=\nabla v^{T}\nabla b_{v}
(with
b_{v}:=\partial_{1}v\wedge\partial_{1}v
the unit normal) denotes the second fundamental form of the surface parametrized by
v
. Moreover,
Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
is a positive semi-definite quadratic form,
B^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
is the preferred bending, and
\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\gamma}
is a
v-
independent residual energy. These quantities are obtained by linearization, relaxation and homogenization from
W
and
B
as outlined in Section
2
. The derivation of
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
in
[
BNPGS23
]
builds on various earlier works. In particular, the case without homogenization and without prestrain (i.e.,
W(x,y,F)
is independent of
y
and
B\equiv 0
) is covered in the seminal paper
[
FJM02
]
. An extension that includes homogenization without prestrain can be found in
[
HNV14
]
, while the case without homogenization but with prestrain is discussed in
[
PG22
,
ALL19
,
Sch07
]
.

As a first main result we prove the following continuity result w.r.t. the scaling parameter
\gamma
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\gamma\in[0,\infty]
let
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
,
Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
,
B^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\gamma}
be defined as described in Section
2
. Let
\bar{\gamma}\in[0,\infty]
. Then as
\gamma\to\bar{\gamma}
we have

\displaystyle B^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}\to B^{\bar{\gamma}}_{{\rm eff}},\qquad Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}(G)\to Q^{\bar{\gamma}}_{{\rm eff}}(G)\qquad\text{for all }G\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\gamma}\to\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\bar{\gamma}}.

Furthermore,
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}

\Gamma(L^{2}(S))
-converges to
\mathcal{E}^{\bar{\gamma}}_{\hom}
.

See Section
3
for the proof.

Next, we compare the functional
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
in the extremal cases
\gamma\in\{0,\infty\}
with the consecutive limits. We start with
\gamma=\infty
, which we compare with the consecutive limit
\lim_{h\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
. For this purpose, we first discuss the limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
.

The homogenization of non-convex integral functionals was originally addressed in
[
Mül87
]
and revisited in
[
BF07
]
using two-scale convergence and
\Gamma-
convergence, and allowing for space-dependent functionals. The homogenized limit of
\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
as
\epsilon\to 0
for fixed
h>0
is given by

\mathcal{E}_{h,\hom}(v):=\int_{\Omega}W^{h}_{\operatorname{hom}}(x^{\prime},x_{3},\nabla_{h}v(x))\,\mathrm{d}x,

(3)

where
W^{h}_{\operatorname{hom}}
is given by the multi-cell homogenization formula

W^{h}_{\operatorname{hom}}(x^{\prime},x_{3},F):=\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\inf_{\varphi\in W^{1,2}_{/}(k{\mathcal{Y}},\mathbb{R}^{3})}\fint_{k{\mathcal{Y}}}W(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,(F+\iota(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi({y})))(\mathrm{Id}+hB(x^{\prime},x_{3},y))^{-1})\,\mathrm{d}{y}.

(4)

Here,
y=(y_{1},y_{2})
and
W^{1,2}_{/}(k{\mathcal{Y}},\mathbb{R}^{3})
denotes the Sobolev space of functions that are in-plane periodic, i.e., periodic w.r.t.
y
. Furthermore,
\iota(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi)
denotes the
3\times 3
matrix whose left and middle columns are given by
\nabla^{\prime}\varphi=(\partial_{y_{1}}\varphi,\partial_{y_{2}}\varphi)
, and whose right column is
0
.

Finer properties of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{h,\hom}
in the case
B=0
were analyzed in
[
MN11
]
. The main result of
[
MN11
]
is that homogenization and linearization commute, i.e. that the homogenization of the linearized functional is the same as the linearization of the homogenized functional. This analysis was extended to include a general prestrain
B(x^{\prime},x_{3},y)
in
[
NR25
]
.

Building upon the work of
[
NR25
]
and
[
PG22
]
we are able to identify the consecutive limit
\lim_{h\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
, which we state in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Consecutive limit)
.

Assume that
W
satisfies Definition
4.1
and let
\mathcal{E}_{h,\hom}
be given by equation (
3
). Then the following statements hold:

(a)

(Compactness). Let
(v_{h})\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a sequence with equibounded energy, i.e.,

\limsup_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\hom}(v_{h})<\infty.

(5)

Then there exists
v\in W^{2,2}_{{\rm iso}}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3})
and a subsequence (not relabeled) such that

\displaystyle v_{h}-\fint_{\Omega}v_{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\to v

\displaystyle\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$},

(6a)

\displaystyle\nabla_{h}v_{h}

\displaystyle\to(\nabla^{\prime}v,b_{v})

\displaystyle\text{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

(6b)

Here and below,
b_{v}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\partial_{1}v\wedge\partial_{2}v
denotes the unit normal.

(b)

(Lower bound). If
(v_{h})\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a sequence with
v_{h}-\fint_{\Omega}v_{h}\mathrm{d}x\to v
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, then

\liminf_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\hom}(v_{h})\geq{\mathcal{E}}^{\infty}_{\hom}(v).

(c)

(Recovery sequence). For any
v\in W^{2,2}_{{\rm iso}}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3})
there exists a sequence
(v_{h})\subset W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
v_{h}\to v
strongly in
W^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that

\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\hom}(v_{h})={\mathcal{E}}^{\infty}_{\hom}(v).

(7)

The proof is found in Section
4
.

Together, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
imply that the limits
\lim_{h\to 0}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
and
\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}\mathop{\lim}\limits_{(h,\epsilon)\to 0\atop\frac{h}{\epsilon}\to\gamma}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
are the same. Hence, there is commutativity at
\gamma=\infty
.

On the other hand, the picture for
\gamma=0
is highly non-commutative. Firstly, we may look at the consecutive limit
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
. In the absence of prestrain (i.e.
B=0
), the limit
\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
is simply a nonlinear bending energy functional with oscillating coefficients (see
[
FJM02
]
), in other words an energy given by

\mathcal{E}_{{\rm eff},\epsilon}(v)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{S}Q_{{\rm eff}}\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\epsilon},{\operatorname{I\!I}}_{v}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}&\text{if }v\in W^{2,2}_{{\rm iso}}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(8)

The limit as
\epsilon\to 0
of this sequence of energies is given by a homogenized nonlinear bending theory, derived in
[
NO15
]
.
For cylindrical deformations it is of the form

\mathcal{E}_{\rm NO}(v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{S}V\left({\operatorname{I\!I}}_{v}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}&\text{if }v\in W^{2,2}_{{\rm iso}}(S;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(9)

where
V
is non-quadratic (see
[
NO15
]
equations (5) and (6)).
Hence, the limits
\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\lim_{h\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
and
\lim_{\gamma\to 0}\mathop{\lim}\limits_{(h,\epsilon)\to 0\atop\frac{h}{\epsilon}\to\gamma}\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
are not the same, and there is no commutativity at
\gamma=0
. Furthermore, we may ask about the limits of
\mathcal{E}_{h,\epsilon}
in the regime
h,\epsilon\to 0
simultaneously, with
\frac{h}{\epsilon}\to 0
, which also corresponds formally to
\gamma=0
. The regime
h\ll\epsilon^{2}
was derived in
[
CC15
]
, while the regime
\epsilon^{2}\ll h\ll\epsilon
was derived in
[
Vel15
]
. The derivation of a nonlinear bending theory in the regime
h\simeq\epsilon^{2}
is still an open problem. The limits derived in
[
CC15
]
and
[
Vel15
]
are different among themselves, and different from the other two limits discussed before which formally correspond to
\gamma=0
.

1.3
Rate of convergence for
\gamma\to\infty

We investigate the rate of convergence of
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma}_{\hom}
as
\gamma\to\infty
. We do not expect that a nontrivial rate holds for general elastic laws and general prestrains, but we will show that, assuming additional material symmetry and regularity in
x_{3}
, it is possible to show a polynomial rate of convergence to the limit.

For the precise statement of the result we need to introduce some notation. The coefficients
Q_{\rm eff}^{\gamma}
and
B^{\gamma}_{\hom}
of the effective bending model depend on the quadratic term
Q
in the expansion at identity of the stored energy function
W
, and the prescribed prestrain tensor
B
. Both,
Q
and
B
depend on the macroscopic in-plane variable
x^{\prime}\in S
, as well as on the (rescaled) out-of plane variable
x_{3}\in I:=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
, and the microscopic in plane variables
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathcal{Y}
. Here,
\mathcal{Y}
denotes the
2d
torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, which we identify (as a set) with
(0,1)^{2}
. We therefore introduce the notation
\Box\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}I\times\mathcal{Y}
. We consider the following class of quadratic forms and prestrains:

Definition 1.3
.

The class
\mathcal{Q}(\alpha,\beta)
consists of all quadratic forms
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
such that

\alpha|\operatorname{sym}G|^{2}\leq Q(G)\leq\beta|\operatorname{sym}G|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }G\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},

where
\operatorname{sym}{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}(G+G^{\top})
is the symmetric part of
G
. We call an elastic law
Q

orthotropic
if

Q(\iota(F)+d\otimes e_{3})=Q(\iota(F))+Q(d\otimes e_{3})

for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We associate with each
Q\in\mathcal{Q}(\alpha,\beta)
the fourth-order stiffness tensor
\mathbb{L}\in\operatorname{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3},\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3})
defined by the polarization identity

\mathbb{L}H:G\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{2}\big(Q(H+G)-Q(H)-Q(G)\big),

(10)

where
:
denotes the standard scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

Definition 1.4

(Admissible quadratic form and prestrain)
.

(a)

A Borel function
Q:S\times I\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to\mathbb{R}
is called an

admissible quadratic form
, if
Q(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,\cdot)
is a quadratic form
of class
\mathcal{Q}(\alpha,\beta)
for a.e.
(x^{\prime},x_{3},y)\in S\times I\times\mathcal{Y}
.

(b)

A Borel function
B:S\times I\times\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}_{\operatorname{sym}}
is called an
admissible prestrain
, if
B
is square integrable.

We now state the main result regarding the rates of convergence.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Q
and
B
be an admissible quadratic form and prestrain, respectively. Let
Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
,
B^{\gamma}_{\rm eff}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}_{\rm eff}
be defined as in Section
2
.
Assume that
Q
is orthotropic and that for some
\alpha\in(0,1]

\sup_{x^{\prime}\in S,y\in\mathcal{Y};x_{3}^{1},x_{3}^{2}\in I}\frac{\left|\mathbb{L}(x^{\prime},x_{3}^{1},y)-\mathbb{L}(x^{\prime},x_{3}^{2},y)\right|}{|x_{3}^{2}-x_{3}^{1}|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

(11)

Then there holds:

(a)

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,

Q^{\infty}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)\leq Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)\leq Q^{\infty}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)+\frac{C|G|^{2}}{\gamma^{2\alpha}},

(12)

for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in S
and all
G\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.

(b)

There exists a constant
C
such that such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in S
,

\left|B^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime})-B^{\infty}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime})\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma^{\alpha}}.

(13)

(c)

Assume that

B\in\begin{cases}L^{\infty}(S,C^{\alpha}(I,L^{2}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R}^{3})))&\text{if }\alpha\in(0,1),\\
L^{\infty}(S,W^{1,\infty}(I,L^{2}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R}^{3})))&\text{if }\alpha=1.\end{cases}

(14)

Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,

\left|\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\gamma}-\mathcal{I}_{\rm res}^{\infty}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\gamma^{\alpha}}.

(15)

Remark 1.6
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.5
, if
Q
is not orthotropic, but is even in
x_{3}
, it still holds that there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,

Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)\leq Q^{\infty}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)+\frac{C|G|^{2}}{\gamma^{2\alpha}},

(16)

for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in S
and all
G\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is found in Section
5
, and the proof of Remark
1.6
is contained in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, Substep 2.1.

If we do not assume any additional structure on
Q
, i.e. if we drop the hypothesis that
Q
is orthotropic or even in
x_{3}
, our methods only allow us to obtain a partial result for the convergence of
Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
, see Remark
1.7
. However, the assumption that
Q
satisfies additional symmetry or structure (i.e. that
Q
is orthotropic) is very natural, and has been used, for example, in
[
BNPGS23
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

Assume the same hypotheses as in Theorem
1.5
except that
Q
is orthotropic and
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,

Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)\leq Q^{\infty}_{{\rm eff}}(x^{\prime},G)+\frac{C|G|^{2}}{\gamma^{\alpha(2\alpha-1)}},

(17)

for a.e.
x^{\prime}\in S
and all
G\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
.

The proof is found in Section
5
.

Next to Hölder-continuity in
x_{3}
, we consider material laws that are piece-wise constant in
x_{3}
direction. These are especially interesting for applications, since they describe laminates. Although these laminates are not covered by Theorem
1.5
, it is possible to use the same ideas to prove a rate of convergence for laminates.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
Q
and
B
be an admissible quadratic form and prestrain, respectively. Let
Q^{\gamma}_{{\rm eff}}
,
B^{\gamma}_{\rm eff}
and
\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}_{\rm eff}
be defined as in Section
2
.
Assume that
Q
is orthotropic and piece-wise constant in
x_{3}
, that is:

Q(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,G)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{[p_{i},p_{i+1})}(x_{3})Q_{i}(x^{\prime},y,G),

(18)

where
\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are the endpoints of a partition of
I
.

Then there holds:

(a)

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
,

Definition 4.1

(Stored energy function)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
W:S\times I\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,\infty]
be a Carathéodory function. We call

a stored energy functional if

is piecewise continuous in the second variable, continuous in the fourth variable and satisfies for a.e.

,
x^{\prime}\in S
, and

the following statements:

(W1)

(Frame indifference).
W(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,RF)=W(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,F)
for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
and
R\in\operatorname{SO}(3)
.

(W2)

(Natural state).
0=W(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,\mathrm{Id})\leq W(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,F)
for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

(W3)

(Non-degeneracy).
W(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,F)\geq\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\operatorname{dist}^{2}(F,\operatorname{SO}(3))
for all
F\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
.

(W4)

(Quadratic expansion). There exists a Carathéodory function
Q:S\times I\times\mathcal{Y}\times\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}\to[0,\infty]
which is piecewise continuous in the second variable, and a non-negative quadratic form in the fourth variable and satisfies the uniform bound

(68)

Moreover, there exists a Carathéodory function
r:S\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
, which is piecewise continuous in the second variable, continuous and increasing in the fourth variable and satisfies for each

By reparametrizing, slowing down (or more precisely, applying a retardation argument as in

, note that also

\displaystyle\int_{\square}\iota\left(x_{3}G\right):\left(\iota(M)+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi+x_{3}\nabla^{\prime 2}\zeta|g)\right)\mathrm{d}(x_{3},y)

\displaystyle\int_{\square}\iota(M):\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi+x_{3}\nabla^{\prime 2}\zeta|g)\,\mathrm{d}(x_{3},y)

for all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\operatorname{sym}}
,
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
\zeta\in W^{2,2}(\mathcal{Y})
and

.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

We prove the lemma for
\gamma=\infty
. The case
\gamma=0
follows analogously. The direct method of the Calculus of Variations implies that there exists a minimizer of

\inf_{M,\varphi,d}\left\|H+\iota(M)+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi|d)\right\|_{x^{\prime}}^{2}

(180)

where the infimum is taken over all
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\operatorname{sym}}
,
\varphi\in L^{2}(I,W^{1,2}(\mathcal{Y},\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, and
d\in L^{2}(I,\mathbb{R}^{3})
. By taking first variations, we can verify that the minimizer, denoted
(M_{H}^{\infty},\varphi_{H}^{\infty},d_{H}^{\infty})
satisfies equation (
50
).

Taking
H^{\prime}=\iota(M_{H}^{\infty})+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{\infty}|d_{H}^{\infty})
as test functions in equation (
50
), we obtain

\int_{\Box}Q\big(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,H^{\prime}\big)\mathrm{d}(x_{3},y)=-\int_{\Box}\mathbb{L}H:H^{\prime}\mathrm{d}(x_{3},y).

(181)

By definition of admissibility, we have that

\int_{\Box}Q\big(x^{\prime},x_{3},y,H^{\prime}\big)\mathrm{d}(x_{3},y)\geq c\left\|H^{\prime}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(182)

On the other hand, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have that

\left|\int_{\Box}\mathbb{L}H:H^{\prime}\mathrm{d}(x_{3},y)\right|\leq C\left\|H\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|H^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(183)

Combining equations (
182
) and (
183
), we have that

\left\|\iota(M_{H}^{\infty})+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{\infty}|d_{H}^{\infty})\right\|_{L^{2}}=\left\|H^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|H\right\|_{L^{2}}.

(184)

Furthermore, using orthogonality in the
L^{2}
inner product, and Korns inequality, we have that

\begin{split}\left\|\iota(M_{H}^{\infty})+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{\infty}|d_{H}^{\infty})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=\left\|\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{\infty})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|M_{H}^{\infty}|^{2}+\|d_{H}^{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\geq c\left\|\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{\infty}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|M_{H}^{\infty}|^{2}+\|d_{H}^{\infty}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(185)

From this, we may conclude equation (
51
). This also implies that, if
H=0
, then
\varphi_{H}^{\infty}=0,M_{H}^{\infty}=0,
and
d_{H}^{\infty}=0
, which implies that the solution is unique. For the case
\gamma=0
, note that we also have the orthogonality,

\displaystyle\left\|\iota(M_{H}^{0})+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{0}+x_{3}\nabla^{\prime 2}\zeta_{H}^{0}|g_{H}^{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(186)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{H}^{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|x_{3}\nabla^{\prime 2}\zeta_{H}^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|M_{H}^{0}|^{2}+\|g_{H}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

∎

Proof of Proposition

Step 1.

Equation (

To prove equation (
53

(M_{\iota\left(x_{3}G\right)},\varphi_{\iota\left(x_{3}G\right)},d_{\iota\left(x_{3}G\right)})

\chi_{G}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\iota(M_{\iota\left(x_{3}G\right)})+\operatorname{sym}(\nabla^{\prime}\varphi_{\iota\left(x_{3}G\right)}|d_{\iota\left(x_{3}G\right)})

where the last two lines follow from the Euler-Lagrange equation for

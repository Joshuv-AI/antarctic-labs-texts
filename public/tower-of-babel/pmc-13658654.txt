Cross database bibliometric and knowledge mapping analysis of nanoplatform mediated tumor associated macrophage reprogramming
Authors: Chang Su, Jiayue Gao, Boyang Li, Zehao Hong, Lucy Yue Lau, Richard J Aldridge, Elizabeth A Whitmore, Guian Xu, Yi Chen, Boxiang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNanoplatforms are evolving from passive delivery vehicles into multifunctional engineered systems capable of remodeling the tumor microenvironment. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are important biological interfaces because of their abundance, plasticity, and roles in immune suppression and therapeutic resistance. However, the knowledge structure and developmental trajectory of nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming remain insufficiently characterized.MethodsWe performed a bibliometric and knowledge-mapping analysis using independently retrieved Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and Scopus datasets with cross-database comparison and consistency assessment. English-language articles and reviews published from January 1, 2007, to March 25, 2026, were included, yielding 1,474 WoSCC and 2,077 Scopus records. Analyses used bibliometrix, VOSviewer, CiteSpace, BiblioVista, and Scimago Graphica.ResultsPublication output increased markedly in both databases, particularly after 2019, with highly concordant annual trends (Spearman’s ρ = 0.996, p < 0.001). The field was primarily represented in nanoscience, materials science, pharmacology, biomaterials, and biomedical engineering. China and the Chinese Academy of Sciences were the leading country and institution, while ACS Nano and Journal of Controlled Release formed the principal journal core. Keyword, burst, thematic, and milestone analyses supported a heuristic five-stage transition from macrophage-addressable nanodelivery to material-intrinsic immunomodulation, microenvironment-responsive TAM reprogramming, biomimetic and combinatorial nano-immunotherapy, and programmable innate-immunity-centered nanoplatforms. Translation-related themes remained comparatively less visible.ConclusionNanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming has developed into a rapidly expanding interdisciplinary research area linking nanoplatform engineering, macrophage-directed regulation, and tumor microenvironment remodeling. Introduction

In contemporary cancer nanomedicine, nanoplatforms are increasingly recognized not merely as passive drug carriers, but as engineered systems capable of sensing, perturbing, and remodeling the pathological tumor ecosystem [1, 2]. In the present study, the term “nanoplatform” is used as an operational concept to distinguish multifunctional engineered nanosystems from more narrowly defined nanoparticles or nanocarriers. “Nanoparticle” primarily refers to nanoscale particulate structures characterized by their size, composition, morphology, and surface properties. “Nanocarrier” instead emphasizes the delivery function of nanoscale systems, particularly the loading, transport, and release of therapeutic or diagnostic cargos. By contrast, “nanoplatform” refers to an engineered nanoscale system that integrates one or more functional modules, such as targeted delivery, stimulus responsiveness, biomimetic interfaces, imaging capability, immune modulation, or combinatorial therapeutic functions. In this sense, nanoplatforms are not limited to passive cargo transport, but can actively interact with and regulate the tumor microenvironment. Their tunable features include particle size, composition, surface and interface chemistry, multicomponent assembly, and stimulus-responsive behavior. These features enable the integration of targeted delivery, spatiotemporal activation, microenvironment-adaptive release, and multimodal therapeutic synergy within a single system [3]. Recent studies have demonstrated that hypoxia-activated, light-triggered, and tumor microenvironment-responsive nanoplatforms achieve more than enhanced drug deposition. Such systems can also amplify oxidative stress, remodel the extracellular matrix, and potentiate systemic antitumor immune responses within complex tumor microenvironments [4, 5]. Accordingly, the conceptual focus of the field has gradually shifted from merely improving delivery efficiency toward actively engineering the tumor microenvironment itself. Within this shift, precise interventions targeting critical immune interfaces are particularly important, and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) have emerged as a representative axis for nanoplatform-mediated microenvironmental modulation [6].

Compared with other immune cell populations, TAMs represent a preferential target for nanoplatform intervention due to their high abundance, functional diversity, and phenotypic plasticity. TAMs are widely distributed across diverse solid tumors and play pivotal roles in immune suppression, angiogenesis, stromal remodeling, tumor progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance. M2-like TAMs are also frequently associated with poor prognosis and impaired antitumor immunity [7, 8]. Crucially, TAMs are not terminally fixed, but dynamically shaped by local cytokine gradients, metabolic cues, hypoxia, and acidic niches [9]. Consequently, reprogramming TAMs from pro-tumorigenic to antitumor states is a more rational therapeutic strategy than depletion alone, aligning with contemporary approaches centered on microenvironmental remodeling [10]. Positioned at the intersection of immune regulation, stromal dynamics, and treatment response, TAMs constitute one of the most valuable cellular interfaces for nanoplatform-mediated modulation of tumor immunity.

Because of TAM plasticity, nanoplatforms function not only as drug delivery vehicles but also as engineered modulators for TAM-directed reprogramming. Reviews and representative studies have highlighted four major advantages in this context. First, ligand modification, surface engineering, and passive or active enrichment mechanisms enable delivery-oriented nanocarriers and more integrated nanoplatforms to penetrate macrophage-enriched tumor niches, achieving precise TAM-directed targeting [11]. Second, nanoplatforms can co-deliver small molecules, siRNA, immunostimulants, metal ions, or photosensitive components, enabling multicomponent synergistic regulation within a single construct [12, 13]. Third, stimulus-responsive design allows spatiotemporally controlled release according to acidity, hypoxia, redox imbalance, or other microenvironmental features, directly coupling TAM reprogramming with local tumor context [14, 15]. Fourth, biomimetic membrane coating, cell-derived vesicles, and membrane-functionalized interfaces confer enhanced immune compatibility, interface recognition, and signal transduction efficiency, evolving the platform from a simple delivery vehicle into a microenvironmental regulator [16–18]. From an engineering perspective, the high complementarity between TAM biological plasticity and nanoplatform tunability underpins the rapid emergence of nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming as a distinct interdisciplinary research direction.

Although recent bibliometric studies have separately analyzed the overall TAM research landscape, TAM-related trends in specific tumor types, and the broader evolution of nanomedicine-based cancer immunotherapy or antitumor nanocarriers [19–22], these analyses largely treat TAMs and nanoplatforms as separate domains. They remain insufficient to capture nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming as an independent, cross-disciplinary knowledge unit with defined engineering logic, microenvironmental interfaces, and evolutionary trajectory. In other words, while prior work reveals growth and distribution patterns in adjacent fields, it does not answer the critical question of how nanoplatforms systematically engage TAMs as functional microenvironmental nodes, transitioning from targeted delivery toward broader microenvironmental programming. Furthermore, these studies lack parallel dual-database analysis with cross-database comparison and consistency assessment and systematic tracking of the “from delivery to programming” paradigm shift, limiting methodological rigor. Building on previous literature, the present study positions nanoplatforms as the primary subject, TAMs as core microenvironmental interfaces, and tumor microenvironment remodeling as functional output, aiming to construct a refined, quantifiable, and mechanistically informed cross-disciplinary knowledge map.

Accordingly, we applied a parallel Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC)-Scopus dual-database framework with cross-database comparison and consistency assessment to systematically map this emerging field. Our objectives were threefold: first, to chart the developmental trajectory, interdisciplinary positioning, and global academic landscape of the field to assess whether it has matured into a structurally stable, independent research direction; second, to delineate the core knowledge structure linking nanoplatform design, macrophage modulation, and tumor microenvironment remodeling, including leading countries, institutions, journals, predominant platform types, and key mechanistic pathways; and third, to identify thematic evolution, milestone progression, and emerging fronts, with particular attention to the transition from early macrophage-targeted delivery to programmable microenvironmental and nano-immunoengineering strategies. This study does not merely catalogue TAM- or nanoplatform-related publications, but seeks to illuminate an emerging engineered immunomodulatory landscape, in which nanoplatforms serve as the central subject, TAMs as interface nodes, and tumor immune remodeling as functional output.

Materials and methods

Study design and cross-database comparison framework

This study was designed as a bibliometric analysis using a parallel dual-database framework with cross-database comparison and consistency assessment to map the development of research on nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming. Rather than merging records from different databases into a single deduplicated corpus, two independent but conceptually comparable datasets were established from WoSCC and Scopus, and parallel analyses were conducted using the same bibliometric workflow. This design was intended to reduce single-database bias and assess the consistency of major conclusions across platforms. Cross-database comparison did not aim at record-level identity between databases, but at conceptual comparability in temporal trends, structural patterns, thematic organization, and emerging fronts [23]. Concordant findings were interpreted as shared structural signals, whereas local discrepancies were considered database-specific features related to differences in coverage, indexing structure, and journal inclusion. The screening process is summarized in a PRISMA-style flow diagram (Fig. 1).

Data sources, search strategy, and screening

WoSCC and Scopus were searched on March 25, 2026, for English-language articles and reviews published from January 1, 2007, to March 25, 2026. The SCI-E and SSCI indexes were used in WoSCC. To balance recall and precision, the search strategies were constructed around three concept blocks: nanoplatform/nanomaterial terms, TAM/macrophage polarization or reprogramming terms, and tumor-related context terms. The WoSCC query was: TS = ( ( “nanoparticle*” OR “nanomaterial*” OR “nanocarrier*” OR “nanoplatform*” OR “nanoformulation*” OR “nanosystem*” OR “liposome*” ) AND ( “tumor-associated macrophage*” OR “tumour-associated macrophage*” OR “macrophage polarization” OR “macrophage repolarization” OR “macrophage reprogramming” ) AND ( reprogram* OR repolarization OR polarization OR “M1 macrophage*” OR “M2 macrophage*” ) AND ( cancer OR tumor OR tumour ) ). The Scopus query was: TITLE-ABS-KEY( ( ( “nanoparticle*” OR “nanomaterial*” OR “nanocarrier*” OR “nanoplatform*” OR “nanoformulation*” OR “nanosystem*” OR “nanocomposite*” OR “liposome*” OR “nanomedicine” ) AND ( “tumor-associated macrophage*” OR “tumour-associated macrophage*” OR TAM OR “macrophage polarization” OR “macrophage repolarization” OR “macrophage reprogramming” ) AND ( reprogram* OR repolarization OR polarization OR “M1 macrophage*” OR “M2 macrophage*” OR “M1” OR “M2” ) AND ( cancer OR tumor OR tumour OR neoplasm* ) ) ). The database-adapted strategies were designed to be conceptually equivalent. WoSCC initially yielded 1,548 records, of which 1,474 were retained after time, language, and document-type filtering, comprising 1,249 articles and 225 reviews. Scopus initially yielded 2,171 records, of which 2,077 were retained, comprising 1,808 articles and 269 reviews.

Search strategy rationale and sensitivity assessment

The strategy prioritized thematic specificity by requiring broad nanotechnology-related concepts together with explicit TAM polarization or reprogramming terms, rather than exhaustively enumerating individual platform subclasses. This reduced the inclusion of broadly related macrophage or nanomedicine studies but may have limited recall for studies using only highly specific or alternative terminology. A qualitative sensitivity assessment examined alternative terminology, including immune remodeling, macrophage activation, innate immune modulation, myeloid re-education, immunometabolism, phagocytosis enhancement, extracellular vesicles, exosomes, metal-organic frameworks, nanogels, micelles, and hydrogel nanoparticles. Residual retrieval bias and the resulting recall-precision trade-off are addressed in the Discussion.

Data preprocessing and bibliometric tools

Extracted fields included publication year, document type, title, abstract, authors, affiliations, countries or regions, source journals, author keywords, database-generated keywords, citation counts, and cited references. Duplicate records were checked independently within each database, primarily using the Digital Object Identifier, with title, publication year, and first author used when DOI information was unavailable. No additional duplicate records were identified or removed after the predefined time-range, language, and document-type screening. The WoSCC and Scopus datasets remained separate throughout the analysis. Country, institution, and author names were manually standardized. Keyword preprocessing was performed independently in each database using author keywords and database-generated terms, including Keywords Plus in WoSCC and indexed keywords in Scopus. Automated stemming or lemmatization was not applied because it could merge biologically or technologically distinct concepts. Two researchers independently performed concept-level keyword normalization to harmonize terminology while preserving biologically and technologically distinct meanings. Ambiguous cases were reviewed according to title, abstract, and keyword context, with disagreements resolved by a third researcher. In total, 39 original terms or variants were harmonized across 16 normalization mappings into 16 standardized terms, as documented in Supplementary Table S1. Analyses were performed using R 4.4.2 with bibliometrix 4.3.2, VOSviewer 1.6.20, CiteSpace 6.3.R5, BiblioVista 1.0.0, and Scimago Graphica. Bibliometrix was used for annual trends, productivity and impact analyses, Bradford’s law, citation indicators, and thematic evolution. Normalized citation indicators were calculated at the document level using publication-year normalization in bibliometrix 4.3.2: NGCSi = GCi/mean(GCy) and NLCSi = LCi/mean(LCy), where GCi and LCi denote the global and local citation counts of document i, respectively, and mean(GCy) and mean(LCy) denote the corresponding mean citation counts of documents published in the same year y. VOSviewer generated co-authorship, co-citation, and keyword co-occurrence networks using association-strength normalization and predefined thresholds, including keyword occurrence ≥ 5. CiteSpace was used for burst detection and time-sliced mapping with 1-year intervals, g-index k = 25, and Pathfinder plus minimum spanning tree pruning. BiblioVista supported interactive inspection and cross-database visual consistency checking, whereas Scimago Graphica was used for geographic and multilevel collaboration maps. Workflows and parameter settings were harmonized across databases.

Analytical dimensions and comparative interpretation

Analyses covered five dimensions: (1) field emergence and interdisciplinary positioning; (2) country-, institution-, and author-level productivity, impact, and collaboration, including total link strength, single-country publications, multiple-country publications, and MCP%; (3) journal distribution and cited-reference structure using Bradford’s law, global and local citations, LC/GC ratio, and normalized citation indicators; (4) keyword clustering, overlay visualization, burst detection, thematic evolution, and thematic mapping; and (5) representative milestone studies used to synthesize the historical trajectory of nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming. Candidate milestone studies were identified from year-specific globally cited publications obtained separately from the WoSCC and Scopus datasets using bibliometrix. After the two candidate lists were combined and deduplicated, 41 publications underwent full-text review (Supplementary Table S2). Global citation prominence was used to identify candidate studies, whereas final milestone designation additionally considered temporal representativeness and evidence of a substantive conceptual or technological transition in nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming. Ten studies were ultimately selected as representative milestones for Fig. 2; Table 1. Because the candidate studies were derived from the retrieved bibliometric corpus itself, this procedure was not treated as an independent validation of search performance, and no formal recall or coverage estimate was calculated. Cross-database consistency was assessed quantitatively. Spearman correlation was calculated for annual publication counts from 2007 to 2025; incomplete 2026 data were excluded. For the top-ranked countries or regions, institutions, and source journals, overlap percentages, Jaccard similarity coefficients, and shared-item Spearman rank correlations were calculated. These metrics were interpreted as descriptive indicators of cross-database consistency rather than formal network-level validation or hypothesis testing.

Results

Field emergence and interdisciplinary positioning

Research on nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming showed a phased growth pattern in both WoSCC and Scopus (Fig. 3A). Before 2014, annual output generally remained below 10 publications, indicating limited research activity during the early period. Publication output increased steadily from 2014, accelerated after 2019, and rose particularly rapidly during 2021–2022. By 2025, annual output exceeded 300 publications in WoSCC and 350 in Scopus, indicating a marked expansion of research activity. Although Scopus consistently included more records, the two databases showed highly concordant temporal trends, supporting broad cross-database consistency. The lower output observed in 2026 reflects the incomplete retrieval period, which ended on March 25, 2026. The subject-category distributions further characterized the interdisciplinary profile of the field (Fig. 3B). In both databases, publications were concentrated in Nanoscience & Nanotechnology, Chemistry, Multidisciplinary, Materials Science, Multidisciplinary, Pharmacology/Pharmacy, Materials Science, Biomaterials, Chemistry, Physical, and Engineering, Biomedical, whereas Oncology ranked below these nano-, materials-, pharmacology-, and engineering-oriented categories. Taken together, these findings indicate that research on nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming is more strongly represented in nano-, materials-, pharmacology-, biomaterials-, and engineering-related categories than in oncology alone, suggesting an interdisciplinary research profile centered on nanoplatform design, delivery systems, and tumor immune microenvironment-related applications.

Global research landscape and collaborative connectivity

The country-level distribution of research on nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming showed substantial activity across East Asia, Europe, and North America, with China occupying the largest and most connected position in both databases. The chord diagram and collaboration network (Fig. 4A, B) showed that China had the largest node, the highest connectivity, and extensive collaborative links with the United States and several European and Asia-Pacific countries. Consistently, China ranked first in publication output, citations, and total link strength in both WoSCC (1,098 publications, 39,531 citations, TLS = 174) and Scopus (1,554 publications, 48,262 citations, TLS = 260) (Table 2). The United States ranked second in both databases but showed a higher proportion of multiple-country publications, indicating a greater relative contribution from international collaboration. The geographic map (Fig. 4C) confirmed that the most active research regions were concentrated in East Asia, Europe, and North America. Temporal trends (Fig. 4D) showed a marked increase in China’s publication output after 2019, whereas the United States exhibited a more gradual long-term rise. Although secondary contributors differed modestly between WoSCC and Scopus, the leading country-level pattern was broadly concordant across the two databases, indicating a globally connected but China-centered research landscape.

Core institutional structure and author collaboration patterns

At the institutional level, this field exhibited a concentrated but multi-core organizational structure, with research capacity largely anchored in major Chinese scientific and medical platforms. In both the collaboration network and chord diagram (Fig. 5A, B), the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) occupied the largest and most connected institutional position, with extensive links to Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Sichuan University, Fudan University, Tongji University, Zhejiang University, and Sun Yat-sen University. Affiliation rankings (Fig. 5C) were likewise mainly composed of Chinese institutions. The author collaboration networks (Fig. 5D) contained several distinct but interconnected clusters, indicating that research was organized around multiple collaborative teams. Temporal author activity (Fig. 5E) increased mainly after 2018 and was particularly concentrated during 2021–2025.

Core journal distribution and foundational knowledge base

The source-journal landscape was concentrated in nano-, drug-delivery-, and biomaterials-oriented outlets. In the journal networks (Fig. 6A, B), ACS Nano, Journal of Controlled Release, Biomaterials, Journal of Nanobiotechnology, Chemical Engineering Journal, and ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces occupied central positions. Bradford’s Law analysis (Fig. 6C) and Table 4 further showed that ACS Nano and Journal of Controlled Release consistently ranked first and second in both databases, forming the principal source-journal core. Advanced Materials showed comparatively lower publication output but high citation impact, whereas Frontiers in Immunology appeared among the productive journals but occupied a less central position than nano-, delivery-, and biomaterials-focused outlets. To quantify the comparability of the WoSCC and Scopus results, a descriptive cross-database consistency assessment was performed. Annual publication trends from 2007 to 2025 were highly correlated (Spearman’s ρ = 0.996, p < 0.001). The overlap percentages for the top-ranked countries or regions, institutions, and source journals were 70.0%, 80.0%, and 80.0%, respectively, with corresponding Jaccard similarity coefficients of 0.538, 0.667, and 0.667. Shared-item rank correlations were also significant across these dimensions (Spearman’s ρ = 0.881–0.893, all p < 0.01). These findings support broad consistency in the major temporal, geographic, institutional, and journal-level patterns while also retaining database-specific differences in coverage and secondary contributors (Supplementary Table S3). The cited-reference structure was characterized by influential studies linking nanoplatform construction with TAM modulation and tumor microenvironment remodeling. Rodell 2018, Song 2016, Qian 2017, Xia 2020, and Chen 2019 were among the representative knowledge anchors identified in Fig. 6D-F; Table 5. Because raw citation counts favor earlier publications, they were interpreted together with local citations, LC/GC ratios, normalized global and local citation indicators, and citation-burst signals to limit the influence of publication age. Collectively, the cited-reference patterns connected early targeting- and delivery-oriented studies with more recent research on nanoplatform-mediated immunomodulation and tumor microenvironment intervention.

Thematic evolution and emerging research frontiers

Keyword analyses revealed three interconnected thematic domains: nanoplatform construction, TAM- and tumor microenvironment-related regulation, and disease- or treatment-oriented applications. The clustering map (Fig. 7A) included platform-related themes such as smart nanoplatforms and membrane-functionalized nanotherapeutics; regulatory themes such as macrophage targeting, tumor microenvironment, and STAT3 signaling; and application themes including liver cancer, cancer treatment, and anti-melanoma effects. The overlay visualization (Fig. 7B) identified tumor microenvironment, tumor-associated macrophages, macrophage polarization, immunotherapy, and cancer immunotherapy as frequent and central terms, whereas ferroptosis, immunogenic cell death, photodynamic therapy, and macrophage repolarization appeared more recently. The keyword evolution diagram (Fig. 7C) further showed a progression from carrier- and delivery-related terminology toward TAM regulation, nanomedicine, cancer-specific applications, and combination therapy. Recent burst terms included ferroptosis, tumor microenvironment, radiotherapy, hepatocellular carcinoma, triple-negative breast cancer, and macrophage reprogramming (Fig. 7D). The thematic map (Fig. 7E) identified macrophage polarization, inflammation, and nanomedicine as motor themes, while immunotherapy and nanoparticles were classified as basic themes. Collectively, these findings indicate increasing thematic attention to responsive and biomimetic nanoplatforms, macrophage repolarization, immunogenic cell death, ferroptosis, and multimodal treatment strategies. In contrast, translation-oriented terms—including biosafety, pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, scalable manufacturing, regulatory alignment, and preclinical model validation—did not appear among the prominent burst terms or motor themes, suggesting that translational implementation remains comparatively less visible as a thematic focus within the current knowledge structure. Discussion

The present bibliometric analysis suggests that nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming has developed into a rapidly expanding interdisciplinary research area linking nanoplatform engineering, macrophage-directed regulation, and tumor microenvironment remodeling. Rather than representing a simple juxtaposition of nanomaterials and TAM biology, its knowledge structure reflects an emerging platform-mechanism-function linkage, as supported by disciplinary distribution, source-journal patterns, cited-reference structure, and keyword evolution. TAMs occupy a strategic interface among angiogenesis, stromal remodeling, immune suppression, and therapeutic resistance, making them actionable cellular targets for nanoplatform-mediated immunomodulation. The evidence-linked observations summarized in Fig. 2; Table 1 can be interpreted as a shift in representative platform capabilities from macrophage-addressable delivery toward microenvironment-responsive, biomimetic, combinatorial, and innate-immune-centered strategies. Collectively, these findings are consistent with an increasing convergence between nanoplatform engineering flexibility and TAM biological plasticity, rather than indicating a progression driven by either domain alone.

A distinctive feature of this field is that different classes of nanoplatforms tend to align with different but complementary modes of TAM- and microenvironment-directed intervention, indicating that platform selection is mechanistically instructive rather than merely formulational. Liposomal and polymeric platforms have most often served as the foundational architectures for macrophage-addressable delivery, local cytokine release, and phenotype-oriented immunomodulation because of their compositional flexibility, cargo compatibility, and controllable release behavior [24–27]. This logic is exemplified by CD163-targeted stealth liposomes for intracellular macrophage delivery (Etzerodt et al., 2012), CXCR4-targeted lipid-coated PLGA nanoparticles for hypoxia- and TAM-linked resistance reversal (Gao et al., 2015), and pH-responsive IL-12-loaded polymeric nanoparticles for local M2-to-M1 re-education (Wang et al., 2017) [28–30]. By contrast, biomimetic and membrane-functionalized systems are particularly suited for interfacial regulation, immune mimicry, and amplification of cell-derived signaling [31], as illustrated by macrophage-derived nanovesicles that simultaneously repolarize TAMs and potentiate checkpoint blockade (Choo et al., 2018) [32]. Responsive nanosystems, including MnO₂-based and externally triggered platforms, more often operate by coupling TAM modulation with hypoxia relief, redox remodeling, or on-demand release, thereby enabling microenvironment-contingent reprogramming rather than static delivery alone (Song et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2021) [33, 34]. More integrated hybrid and multifunctional nanoplatforms have further expanded this framework by combining pathway blockade, ferroptosis induction, innate immune activation, and combinatorial immunotherapy within single constructs (Li et al., 2020; Deng et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2025) [35–37]. Together, these platform-mechanism pairings suggest that progress in the field is increasingly associated with the alignment of nanostructural behavior, macrophage-centered regulatory tasks, and tumor-specific microenvironmental contexts, rather than with the simple accumulation of new material types alone [38].

The milestone trajectory indicates a gradual shift from delivery-centered nanocarrier functions toward multifunctional nanoplatform-mediated immunomodulation. Early systems primarily addressed macrophage targeting and cargo transport, whereas later architectures increasingly integrated stimulus responsiveness, biomimetic interfaces, immune activation, and microenvironmental regulation. Based on the milestone studies, thematic cluster support, and burst/keyword signals summarized in Fig. 2; Table 1, we derived five heuristic capability descriptors. These descriptors represent a qualitative synthesis of the bibliometric observations rather than an algorithm-generated classification or objectively detected temporal partition. Generation I, exemplified by CD163-targeted stealth liposomes, addressed the foundational problem of macrophage addressability by showing that nanocarriers could be selectively anchored to macrophage subsets and efficiently internalized [28, 39, 40]. Generation II marked a conceptual leap from transport to regulation: cationic polymer-based systems demonstrated that nanomaterials themselves could function as material-intrinsic immunomodulators, actively reversing TAM immunotolerance through TLR4-linked signaling rather than merely delivering cargo [41, 42]. Generation III introduced microenvironment-coupled reprogramming, in which ligand-targeted hybrid nanoparticles, MnO₂-based bioconjugated nanosystems, and pH-responsive cytokine-delivery platforms linked TAM repolarization to hypoxia alleviation, redox remodeling, local cytokine control, and resistance reversal [43–46]. Generation IV further expanded this design logic by embedding TAM reprogramming within biomimetic combination therapy, including macrophage-derived nanovesicles, peptide-guided dual-blockade nanomicelles, and externally triggered liposomes that integrated checkpoint sensitization, on-demand release, and broader TIME remodeling [47–49]. Most recently, Generation V has moved toward programmable innate-immune-centered activation, in which hybrid nanostimulators and pH-responsive STING nanoagonists orchestrate ferroptosis, innate immune awakening, subcellular cargo routing, and sustained macrophage-mediated immune amplification within tumor-specific therapeutic settings [50–52]. Overall, this heuristic framework highlights a progressive expansion of nanoplatform capability from receptor-addressable delivery and material-intrinsic immunomodulation toward microenvironment-responsive repolarization, biomimetic combination therapy, and innate immune activation.

Building on the heuristic trajectory described above and the emerging thematic signals identified in the Results, current frontier shifts can be interpreted as converging along two major directions. These directions broadly correspond to recent extensions of the biomimetic combination-oriented and innate-immune-centered capabilities summarized as Generation IV and Generation V in the heuristic framework, rather than representing independent or isolated keyword-level expansions. The first direction involves biomimetic and membrane-functionalized immune-interface engineering. Recent studies and reviews on biomimetic nanoparticles, macrophage-membrane-encapsulated nanotherapeutics, and cell membrane-based nanosystems indicate that these architectures increasingly rely on membrane-enabled targeting, immune-compatible surface design, and signal-transmitting interfaces to improve tumor homing, TAM-facing communication, and combination-therapy responsiveness [53–56]. Within this direction, TAMs are increasingly conceptualized not only as endpoints of repolarization but also as cellular interfaces through which nanoplatforms may influence checkpoint responsiveness, intercellular signaling, and local immune-microenvironmental regulation. The second direction involves responsive and innate-immune-activating multifunctional platforms. This direction can be viewed as a recent extension of the innate-immune-centered capability represented in Generation V, while also incorporating responsive and combination-oriented design elements developed across earlier stages. In these systems, stimuli-responsive design, subcellular cargo routing, ferroptosis coupling, and STING-centered immune activation are increasingly integrated to connect macrophage modulation with broader tumor-type-specific therapeutic contexts. Recent representative work on TME-activatable nanoparticles, engineered iron oxide nanoplatforms, and nanomaterial-enabled STING therapeutics suggests that the frontier of this field is no longer defined solely by identifying additional TAM-associated mechanisms [50, 57, 58], but by developing smart, biomimetic, responsive, and programmable nanoplatforms that can link TAM-directed modulation with tumor-type-dependent microenvironmental intervention and combinatorial immune therapy [59–61]. Accordingly, the emerging frontier may be better described as the increasing use of engineered nanoplatforms to coordinate macrophage-facing immunomodulation, TAM-centered therapeutic regulation, and tumor microenvironment-related intervention strategies, although such increasing functional complexity also raises higher requirements for safety, reproducibility, and translational standardization.

From a translational perspective, the bibliometric patterns suggest a relative imbalance between the rapidly expanding platform- and mechanism-oriented research and the visibility of implementation-oriented themes. Frontier signals were concentrated in platform- and mechanism-oriented themes, including biomimetic strategies, responsive nanosystems, ferroptosis, immunogenic cell death, macrophage reprogramming, and combination therapy, whereas biosafety, scalable manufacturing, regulatory alignment, pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, and preclinical model validation were less prominent in the keyword-burst and thematic analyses. Taken together, these observations suggest that translational implementation has not yet emerged as a comparably prominent thematic focus within the current knowledge structure. At the implementation level, priority domains warranting greater attention include chemistry, manufacturing, and controls (CMC), Good Laboratory Practice (GLP)-compliant toxicology, integrated pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling, batch-to-batch reproducibility, and regulatory alignment. TAM heterogeneity extends beyond the conventional M1/M2 framework. Contemporary studies increasingly view TAM phenotypes as multidimensional and partially overlapping states that vary across tumor types, spatial niches, treatment contexts, and local microenvironmental cues [5, 10]. Accordingly, the M1/M2 terminology used in this field should be regarded as a simplified operational descriptor rather than a fixed biological dichotomy. Such heterogeneity, spatial dynamics, and context dependence may limit the reproducibility of macrophage reprogramming across tumor types and intratumoral niches. In addition, performance is strongly shaped by tumor-specific microenvironmental conditions, and efficacy observed in simplified preclinical systems may not persist in heterogeneous, spatially complex, and treatment-exposed human tumors [62, 63]. These concerns are particularly relevant for membrane-coated, multicomponent, ferroptosis-related, STING-centered, and other responsive platforms, for which robust physicochemical characterization, reproducible manufacturing and quality-control procedures, predictable PK/PD relationships, immune-safety evaluation, and regulatory compatibility are central to translational development. Future progress will therefore require not only more potent TAM-modulating platforms, but also clearer mechanisms of action, reproducible manufacturing attributes, predictable in vivo behavior, and stronger compatibility with clinically relevant tumor contexts.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Although parallel WoSCC and Scopus datasets with cross-database comparison and consistency assessment were used, results may still be affected by database-specific differences in indexing depth, subject-area and journal coverage, indexing practices, update frequency, and terminology visibility. The consistency metrics were based on annual trends and top-ranked structural entities and should therefore be interpreted as descriptive indicators rather than formal network-level validation. Retrieval was also influenced by heterogeneous terminology. Although qualitative sensitivity assessment was performed, relevant studies may have been missed if they used terms such as immune remodeling, macrophage activation, innate immune modulation, myeloid re-education, immunometabolism, or phagocytosis enhancement, or emphasized extracellular vesicles, exosomes, metal-organic frameworks, nanogels, micelles, or hydrogel nanoparticles without explicit polarization or reprogramming terminology. Because the primary strategy required both TAM-associated and polarization/reprogramming concepts, it favored thematic specificity over maximal recall and may have underrepresented early exploratory studies. Citation-time bias is another limitation: older publications have had more time to accumulate citations, whereas studies from 2023 to 2025 may be underestimated. Citation bursts, normalized local citation indicators, and recent keyword-emergence patterns were therefore considered when interpreting milestones and frontiers, but these measures cannot fully remove temporal bias. Figure 2; Table 1 should accordingly be viewed as a heuristic synthesis of foundational studies, recent burst signals, and thematic evolution rather than a complete ranking of scholarly importance. Finally, bibliometric analysis cannot establish experimental causality or determine which nanoplatform designs will perform best in human tumors.

Conclusion

Nanoplatform-mediated TAM reprogramming has developed into a rapidly expanding interdisciplinary research area at the interface of nanoplatform engineering, macrophage-directed regulation, and tumor microenvironment intervention. The broadly concordant temporal, geographic, institutional, and journal-level patterns observed across WoSCC and Scopus support the cross-database consistency of its major knowledge structure. Evidence from milestone studies, keyword evolution, citation-burst signals, and thematic patterns suggests a heuristic capability transition from macrophage-addressable delivery and material-enabled immunomodulation toward microenvironment-responsive reprogramming, biomimetic nano-immunotherapy, and programmable immune orchestration. These patterns are consistent with an increasing integration of targeting, responsive release, biomimetic interfaces, ferroptosis, innate immune activation, and combinatorial therapy within multifunctional nanoplatforms. However, the relatively low thematic visibility of biosafety, pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, scalable manufacturing, regulatory alignment, and clinically relevant model validation suggests that translation-oriented research themes have yet to attain prominence comparable to platform- and mechanism-oriented topics within the current knowledge structure. Future advancement will likely depend not only on increasingly sophisticated TAM-modulating nanoplatforms, but also on improving reproducibility, standardization, clinical translatability, predictable in vivo behavior, and context-specific therapeutic performance.

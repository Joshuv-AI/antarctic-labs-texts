TREM2 Regulates Microglial Activation via the ERK/p38 Signaling Pathway: Implications for the Pathogenesis of Experimental Autoimmune Uveitis
Authors: Yang Yu, Xuemei Su, Zihao Zhuang, Pengcheng Hu, Mingjia Wang, Zhikang Liao, Peizeng Yang, Jianmin Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe purpose of this study was to investigate the role of the trigger receptor expressed on myeloid cell 2 (TREM2) in experimental autoimmune uveitis (EAU) and elucidate the mechanisms by which TREM2 modulated inflammation and microglial activation via key signaling pathways.MethodsEAU mouse models and microglial cell BV2 inflammation models were established to explore TREM2 function. TREM2 expression in EAU retinas was detected using public databases and reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). RT-qPCR, Western blotting, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and flow cytometry assessed TREM2 functional phenotypes in autoimmune uveitis. Western blotting and RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analyzed key signaling pathways’ activation to clarify TREM2’s molecular mechanisms of inflammatory regulation.ResultsTREM2 expression in EAU was elevated at inflammatory peak and reduced during resolution. TREM2 knockdown in inflamed microglia exacerbated inflammation, whereas overexpression alleviated it. TREM2 knockout in EAU mice enhanced retinal vascular leakage, elevated splenic Th1 and Th17 cell proportions, and reduced Treg cell proportions. TREM2 knockdown promoted phosphorylated ERK/p38 activation; overexpression suppressed it. RNA-seq revealed altered retinal mRNA profiles in TREM2 knockout EAU mice, with activation of ERK/p38-associated pathways including the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway.ConclusionsTREM2 exerted an anti-inflammatory role in EAU pathogenesis, likely by regulating microglial activation and inhibiting the ERK/p38 signaling pathway in these cells.

Uveitis is a significant global cause of visual impairment, affecting 3% to 20% of visually impaired individuals.1,2 Uveitis is broadly classified into infectious and non-infectious categories, with non-infectious forms such as Vogt-Koyanagi-Harada (VKH) disease generally regarded as autoimmune-related conditions. The pathogenesis of autoimmune uveitis involves T-cell imbalance, characterized by increased proportions of Th1 and Th17 cells and decreased Treg cell proportions during active disease states.3–5 However, the underlying molecular mechanisms remain incompletely understood.

Microglia are involved in diverse biological processes as an immune cell type in the retina. Although retinal microglia are relatively few in number, they maintain retinal homeostasis, mediate immune surveillance, and represent the most important and functionally active immune cell subset under physiological conditions.6–8 Activated microglia can influence blood-retinal barrier (BRB) integrity indirectly through secreted cytokines.9 Zhou et al. showed that microglial depletion significantly suppressed inner BRB breakdown in retinal ischemia.10 Study has shown that microglia play a critical role in initiating and exacerbating uveitis, as inflammation is abolished upon their removal.11 The trigger receptor expressed on myeloid cell 2 (TREM2) is a transmembrane receptor.12 Several studies have shown that TREM2 enhances microglia survival and proliferation,13 modulates phagocytic activity,14 and regulates cellular motility.15 Furthermore, TREM2 has been implicated in modulating inflammatory response intensity.16 Whereas these findings underscore the strong association between TREM2 and microglia function,17,18 the specific role of TREM2 in microglia during uveitis remains unclear. Therefore, investigating how TREM2 influences microglia under inflammatory conditions is essential for elucidating the pathogenic mechanisms of autoimmune uveitis.

Using the experimental autoimmune uveitis (EAU) mouse model, which recapitulates key pathological features of human autoimmune uveitis,19 we observed dynamic changes in TREM2 expression during disease progression. We assessed TREM2’s impact on inflammatory phenotypes through TREM2 knockdown and overexpression in microglia, as well as by generating TREM2-knockout EAU mice. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) and bioinformatics analyses of retinas from Trem2+/+ and Trem2−/− EAU mice were performed to elucidate the molecular mechanism through which TREM2 modulated autoimmune uveitis.

Materials and Methods

Animals and Cells

Specific pathogen-free (SPF) female wild-type C57BL/6J mice (aged 6–8 weeks) were purchased from Hunan Slaike Jingda Experimental Animal (China). Trem2−/− mice were obtained from Shanghai Model Organisms Center (China). The TREM2 gene reading frame in these mice was disrupted via CRISPR/Cas9 technology with non-homologous end joining-mediated mutation repair, resulting in a loss of TREM2 protein function. Mice were housed under standardized conditions with a 12-hour light-dark cycle, adequate ventilation, and controlled temperature and humidity, with free access to sufficient water and food. All animal experiments were performed in strict compliance with the guidelines and principles set forth by the Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology (ARVO). The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (2023-160). The murine microglial cells BV2 was purchased from Wuhan Procell Life Technology (China).

Reagents

Human IRBP651–670 was synthesized and purified (purity 98%) by Shanghai Sangon Biotech (China). Complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (USA). Pertussis toxin (PTX) was acquired from APExBIO (USA). Heat-killed Mycobacterium tuberculosis strain H37Ra was obtained from BD Biosciences (USA).

Cell Culture and Treatment

BV2 microglial cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle's medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified incubator under 5% CO2 in air. Cells were then seeded into culture plates and stimulated with or without 1 µg/mL LPS for 24 hours.20–22

EAU Induction and Evaluation

Mice were immunized subcutaneously with 500 µg human IRBP651–670, emulsified in an equal volume of CFA containing Mycobacterium tuberculosis strain H37Ra, followed by an intraperitoneal injection of 1 µg PTX. On day 14 post-immunization, EAU clinical symptoms were assessed via slit-lamp microscope and retinal imaging microscope following pupil dilation. Ocular inflammation severity was scored in a masked manner by two independent observers.23–26

Hematoxylin and Eosin Staining

Mice were euthanized prior to eyeball enucleation.27 Isolated eyeballs were fixed in FAS eye fixation solution, then dehydrated and embedded in paraffin. Consecutive 4 to 6-µm sections were cut along the optic nerve axis. Sections were subsequently dewaxed, rehydrated, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Histopathological changes were evaluated using a blinded scoring method.23

RNA Extraction and Reverse Transcription Quantitative Polymerase Chain Reaction

Total RNA was extracted using the SteadyPure Universal RNA Extraction Kit II (Hunan Accurate Biotechnology, China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Isolated RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the Evo M-MLVRT Mix Kit with gDNA Clean (Hunan Accurate Biotechnology, China) for subsequent qPCR analysis. Quantitative real-time PCR was performed with SYBR Green qPCR Master Mix (Low ROX; MedChemExpress, USA) and detected on an Applied Biosystems 7500 Real-Time PCR System. Relative gene expression levels were normalized to β-actin and calculated using the 2−△△CT method. The primer sequences, synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech (China), were listed in Table.

Western Blotting

Cells and tissues were lysed on ice using radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (Beyotime, China), supplemented with 1% Phenylmethanesulfonylfluoride (Beyotime, China). Protein concentration was quantified using the bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit (Beyotime, China). Protein samples were then fractionated via sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (ACE, China) and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Millipore, USA). Membranes were blocked in rapid blocking buffer (NCM, China) for 10 minutes at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C with primary antibodies (β-actin, 1:1000, Proteintech, China; COX2, 1:1000, CST, USA; iNOS, 1:1000, Proteintech, China; p-ERK, 1:1000, Affinity, USA; ERK, 1:1000, Affinity, USA; p-JNK, 1:1000, Affinity, USA; JNK, 1:1000, Affinity, USA; p-p38, 1:1000, Proteintech, China; and p38, 1:1000, Proteintech, China). After incubation with secondary antibodies (1:5000, Bioss, China) for 1 hour at room temperature, protein bands were visualized using the enhanced chemiluminescence kit (Abbkine Scientific, China). Band densities were quantified using ImageJ software and normalized to β-actin internal control.

Lentivirus Infection

TREM2 knockdown lentivirus was obtained by OBiO Technology (China), and TREM2 overexpression lentivirus was purchased by Hanbio Biotechnology (China). BV2 cells were transfected with the respective lentivirus, and the culture medium was replaced with fresh complete medium the following day. After 72 hours of transfection, cells with high transfection efficiency and robust cell status were selected under a fluorescence microscope. Stable cell lines were then established by culturing cells in complete DMEM supplemented with 2.5 µg/mL puromycin.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay

Cell culture supernatants were collected from different treatment groups. Concentration of TNF-α and IL-6 in the supernatants were measured using a mouse TNF-α and IL-6 ELISA Kit (R&D System, USA) following the manufacturer's instructions. The plate was pre-coated with capture antibody working solution in advance. On the following day, the plate was washed and then incubated with regent diluent for 1 hour. The standard working solution and the samples were diluted in appropriate proportions and added to the plate for a 2-hour incubation period. After washing, detection antibody working solution was added, and the plate was incubated for an additional 2 hours. Subsequently, streptavidin-HRP working dilution, substrate solution, and stop solution were added sequentially for reaction. Finally, the absorbance at 450 nm was measured on a preheated microplate reader. The protein concentration of the samples was calculated using the standard curve equation.

Evans Blue Assay

The integrity of BRB was evaluated via the Evans blue assay. Mice were injected with 2% Evans blue (Meilune, China) via the tail veins and euthanized 2 hours later.28 Eyeballs were enucleated and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 2 hours. Retinas were then dissected into a four-leaf clover shape, mounted on slides with the vitreous side facing upward, and cover slipped with glycerol. Fluorescence images were collected using a fluorescence microscope.

Flow Cytometry Analysis

Mononuclear cells were isolated from mouse spleens using density gradient separation medium. For intracellular cytokine staining, splenic mononuclear cells were stimulated with cell activation cocktail containing brefeldin A (Biolegend, USA) for 6 hours, followed by staining with mouse anti-CD4 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) for 30 minutes. After fixation and permeabilization, cells were incubated with anti-IFN-γ (Biolegend, USA) and anti-IL-17A (Biolegend, USA) for 1 hour. Splenic mononuclear cells were stained with anti-CD4 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and anti-CD25 (Biolegend, USA) for 30 minutes, then treated with fixation/permeabilization buffer, followed by incubation with anti-Foxp3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) for 1 hour. Flow cytometry was used to analyze the stained cells, and FlowJo software was used for data analysis.

RNA-Seq and Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes

On day 14 post-immunization, total RNA was extracted from the retinas of Trem2+/+ EAU and Trem2−/− EAU mice for RNA-seq. Library construction and transcriptome sequencing were performed by Beijing Novogene Technology (China). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis between the two groups were conducted using the DESeq2 (version 1.20.0), with a significance threshold of P < 0.05 and |log2 fold change| >1.

Gene Ontology Analysis, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes Analysis and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis

The clusterProfiler (version 3.8.1) was utilized for Gene Ontology (GO) functional annotation and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analysis of DEGs, with statistically significant entries filtered by an adjusted P value < 0.05. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was carried out using GO and KEGG datasets from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) as predefined gene sets, with significant enrichment defined as a Normalized Enrichment Score (NES) > 1 and a nominal P value < 5%.

Statistical Analysis

All data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (version 8.0.1). Unpaired Student's t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was applied for comparisons, whereas one-way ANOVA followed by a Bonferroni post hoc test was used for three or more groups. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Trem2 Expression in Retinal Microglia Exhibited Dynamic Changes During EAU Progression

A search of the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database was conducted, and a single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) dataset (GSE191260) of CD45+ immune cells in the retinas of EAU mice at days 0, 14, 21, and 28 following IRBP immunization was identified. Through bioinformatics analysis, high expression of Trem2 in mouse retinal microglia was confirmed (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, the expression profile of Trem2 at these four distinct time points throughout the disease course of EAU mice was further analyzed. Elevated Trem2 expression was detected on day 14 post-immunization, followed by a decrease in expression during the recovery phase (Fig. 1B).

Furthermore, EAU models were established using wild-type C57BL/6J mice. Immunized mice exhibited characteristic ocular pathology including conjunctival and ciliary hyperemia, corneal edema, anterior chamber inflammation, iris adhesions, tortuous retinal vessels and focal exudation (Figs. 1C, 1D). Histopathological examination revealed the presence of inflammatory cells in the vitreous cavity and retinal folds (Fig. 1E). Retinal RNA was extracted at the peak of inflammation, and RT-qPCR analysis was performed. Upregulated mRNA expression levels of Tnf, Il6, Ifng, Il17a, Ptgs2, and Nos2 were detected in EAU mice (Fig. 1F). In addition, retinal mRNA was extracted from EAU mice at days 0, 7, 14, 21, and 28 post-immunization. The mRNA expression level of Trem2 was found to increase during the initial inflammatory phase (day 7), reach a significant increase at the peak of inflammation (day 14), and subsequently decline during the inflammation resolution phase (days 21 and 28; Fig. 1G).

TREM2 Attenuated LPS-Induced Inflammation in Microglia

The aforementioned findings have revealed an association between Trem2 expression and the progression of EAU, as well as a high expression level of Trem2 in retinal microglia. The role of TREM2 in microglia during inflammatory responses was subsequently investigated. Given the limited yield of primary microglia isolated from mouse retinas, which failed to meet the requirements of subsequent experiments, and the absence of commercially available specialized retinal microglial cell lines, the mouse brain-derived microglial cell line BV2 was used in this study. Lentiviral transfection was further used to either knock down or overexpress TREM2 in these BV2 cells (Supplementary Figs. S1, S2).

BV2 cells with successful TREM2 knockdown or overexpression were stimulated with 1 µg/mL LPS for 24 hours. Total cellular proteins were then extracted to determine the protein expression levels of the inflammatory factors COX2 and iNOS. The secretion of TNF-α and IL-6 in the cell culture supernatant was quantified by ELISA. Results demonstrated that TREM2 knockdown in BV2 cells under inflammatory conditions aggravated the inflammatory response, as evidenced by upregulated protein expression of COX2 and iNOS (Figs. 2A, 2B), along with increased secretion of TNF-α and IL-6 in the supernatant (Fig. 2C). Conversely, TREM2 overexpression significantly alleviated the LPS-induced inflammatory response in BV2 cells, as indicated by reduced protein expression of COX2 and iNOS (Figs. 2D, 2E), and decreased secretion of TNF-α and IL-6 in the supernatant (Fig. 2F).

TREM2 Deficiency Exacerbated Inflammation in EAU Mice

To investigate the role of TREM2 in EAU pathogenesis, wild-type (Trem2+/+) C57BL/6J mice and Trem2−/− C57BL/6J mice were selected to establish EAU model. On day 14 post-immunization, the Trem2−/− EAU group exhibited a more pronounced inflammatory response, with significantly higher histopathological scores and clinical scores for both anterior and posterior segments compared to the Trem2+/+ EAU group (Figs. 3A–C, 3E). The immunofluorescence staining of retinal flat-mount indicated that the number of activated microglia in the retina of Trem2+/+ EAU mice was lower than that in Trem2−/− EAU mice (Supplementary Fig. S3). The Evans blue assay revealed more severe retinal vascular leakage and impaired BRB integrity in Trem2−/− EAU mice (Fig. 3D). Consistently, the retinal protein expression level of ZO-1 was significantly downregulated in Trem2−/− EAU mice, which further corroborated the exacerbated disruption of BRB integrity (Supplementary Fig. S4). Moreover, RT-qPCR analysis of retinas harvested on day 14 showed significantly increased expression of Tnf, Il6, Ifng, Il17a, Ptgs2, and Nos2 in Trem2−/− EAU mice (Fig. 3F). Flow cytometry analysis of splenocytes demonstrated elevated proportions of CD4+ IFN-γ+ (Th1) and CD4+ IL-17A+ (Th17) cells, along with reduced CD4+ CD25+ Foxp3+ (Treg) cell proportions in Trem2−/− EAU mice (Fig. 3G). Collectively, these findings indicated that TREM2 deficiency exacerbated inflammatory responses in EAU mice.

TREM2 Modulates the Inflammatory Response of Microglia via the ERK/p38 Signaling Pathway

The absence of TREM2 was shown to exacerbate the inflammatory response in EAU. Subsequently, the anti-inflammatory protective mechanism of TREM2 in autoimmune uveitis was investigated. As the GSE191260 dataset indicated that TREM2 was predominantly expressed in the microglia of mouse retina immune cell, so BV2 was selected as the model for mechanistic studies. Extensive studies have demonstrated that the anti-inflammatory effects of TREM2 were mediated via the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway. Therefore, the expression levels of key molecule extracellular signal regulated kinase (ERK), c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK), and p38 within the MAPK signaling pathway were detected.

Microglia were subjected to TREM2 knockdown or overexpression manipulation prior to stimulation with LPS for 24 hours. Western blotting results showed that in LPS-stimulated microglia, TREM2 knockdown led to increased expression of phosphorylated ERK and p38 expression, whereas no alterations were observed in phosphorylated JNK (Figs. 4A–C). Conversely, TREM2 overexpression resulted in decreased levels of phosphorylated ERK and p38 levels, whereas phosphorylated JNK expression remained statistically unchanged (Figs. 4D–F).

Identification of Key Genes and Pathways Involved in Exacerbated Inflammation in Trem2−/− EAU Mice via RNA-Seq

An investigation into the key genes and pathways associated with exacerbated EAU in Trem2−/− mice was undertaken utilizing RNA-seq. A total of 1120 DEGs in the retina were identified in Trem2−/− EAU mice relative to Trem2+/+ EAU mice, among which 691 genes were upregulated and 429 genes were downregulated. Volcano plots and hierarchical clustering heatmaps of retinal DEGs between the two groups were depicted in Supplementary Figures S5A and S5B. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed on these DEGs. According to the GO database, a comprehensive functional analysis of DEGs was conducted across three domains: biological processes (BPs), cellular components (CCs), and molecular function (MF; Fig. 5A). KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed the top three enriched pathways in the retina, namely the MAPK signaling pathway, Fc gamma R-mediated phagocytosis, and Yersinia infection. The Janus kinase-signal transducer and activator of transcription (JAK-STAT) signaling pathway and Th17 cell differentiation were also found to be highly enriched (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, results of GSEA demonstrated that T cells related pathways like Th cell differentiation, Th17 cell differentiation, and Th1/Th2 cell differentiation were dysregulated following TREM2 knockout (Fig. 5C). Collectively, based on these bioinformatic analyses and our experimental results, it was emphasized that the anti-inflammatory effect of TREM2 in the pathogenesis of EAU was exerted through the MAPK signaling pathway.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated that dynamic TREM2 expression correlated with EAU progression and revealed its anti-inflammatory role in LPS-induced microglia and EAU. These anti-inflammatory effects appear to be mediated through modulation of microglial activation and the suppression of the ERK/p38 signaling pathway (Fig. 6).

A visual summary of the experimental workflow and key results. This figure was created by the authors using BioRender.com.

Previous studies had shown that TREM2 exerted distinct roles across various autoimmune disease. We observed that TREM2 indeed showed an upward trend at the onset of inflammation, a marked elevation during the peak inflammatory phase, and a decline during the resolution phase in the retinas of EAU mice, which was consistent with the results from GEO dataset (GSE191260). A prior study reported that high TREM2 expression in the central nervous system during the peak inflammatory phase of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE), with TREM2 exerting an anti-inflammatory effect in this model.29 Based on these observations, we hypothesized that TREM2 may function to counteract inflammation and exert a protective role in the body during inflammatory response. The upregulated TREM2 expression in EAU mice at the peak of inflammation was likely a protective negative feedback mechanism used by the body to mitigate inflammation following EAU induction, a pattern analogous to that identified in the EAE animal model.

Our experimental findings collectively demonstrated that TREM2 deficiency exacerbated inflammatory response both in vitro and in vivo, whereas TREM2 overexpression alleviated them. Sun et al. demonstrated that TREM2 silencing upregulated inflammatory cytokines expression,30 whereas Li et al. showed that TREM2 overexpression mitigated oxidative stress and neuroinflammation in activated microglia.31 These were consistent with our experimental findings indicating an anti-inflammatory role for TREM2. In vivo studies across multiple disease models underscored TREM2’s critical role in maintaining microenvironmental homeostasis. In EAE, TREM2 was highly expressed in microglia during the peak inflammatory phase, whereas TREM2 blockade during the effector phase exacerbated disease severity.29 Xie et al. demonstrated that TREM2 deficiency impaired microglial phagocytosis, exacerbating neuronal damage and motor deficits in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis.32 Similarly, TREM2 knockdown in the Parkinson’s disease (PD) model increased dopaminergic neuron loss and motor dysfunction. In their study of mice with retinal detachment (RD), Zhou et al. found that TREM2 deficiency accelerated photoreceptor degeneration, advanced the peak time of the inflammatory response, and expedited the infiltration and distribution of microglia following RD.33 In Trem2−/− mice, N-methyl-N-nitrosourea (MNU)-induced retinal degeneration resulted in exacerbated photoreceptor loss, accompanied by defective microglial migration, impaired phagocytic function, and blunted microglial activation.34 A study showed that TREM2 enhanced host defense against Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis).35 Knockdown of TREM2 in established tumor-bearing mice significantly promoted cancer cell growth, migration, and invasion, whereas TREM2 overexpression had the opposite effect.36 These convergent findings established TREM2 as a critical regulator across immune responses, neurodegenerative diseases, infectious diseases, and cancer.

Downregulation of TREM2 expression has been reported to mediate activation of the MAPK signaling pathway, thereby triggering neuroinflammatory responses in Alzheimer’s disease and subsequently promoting the production of pro-inflammatory factors.37 In contrast, TREM2 overexpression has been shown to suppress MAPK-mediated neuroinflammation.38 Additionally, elevated TREM2 levels have been demonstrated to markedly reduce dopaminergic neuron apoptosis in PD mice via the MAPK signaling pathway.39 As a key mediator of intracellular signal transduction, the MAPK signaling pathway participates in diverse biological processes, including cell growth, differentiation, and metabolism, whereas also exerting a critical role in immune responses. The MAPK family primarily comprises ERK, JNK, and p38, which regulate a broad range of cellular biological functions mainly through kinase phosphorylation cascade reactions. Therefore, we chose to manipulate the expression of TREM2 in mouse microglia, either by knockdown or overexpression, and then examine the changes in phosphorylated and total ERK, JNK, and p38 expression levels under inflammatory conditions. Consistent with prior reports, the results confirmed that TREM2 negatively regulated the inflammatory response of autoimmune uveitis via the ERK/p38 axis of the MAPK pathway.

RNA-seq and bioinformatics analyses confirmed that DEGs in the retinas of Trem2+/+ EAU mice and Trem2−/− EAU mice were indeed enriched in the MAPK signaling pathway. Consistent with this, human autoimmune uveitis studies had demonstrated MAPK pathway involvement. A missense mutation in OR11H1-A63 exacerbated barrier dysfunction and activated MAPK signaling pathway, accelerated the inflammatory cascade in patients with VKH.40 Li et al. identified MAPK signaling pathway as a potential pathway involved in the pathogenesis and drug response of disease through the analysis of peripheral blood mononuclear cells sequencing data from patients with VKH.41

In summary, our study established TREM2 as an anti-inflammatory factor in EAU mice that exerted protective effects through ERK/p38 in the MAPK signaling pathway. Whereas validation of samples from patients with autoimmune uveitis, studies involving adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated TREM2 overexpression in EAU, and investigations into the role of TREM2 in various retinal cell subsets of EAU via flow cytometry remain to be further conducted, these findings provided a foundation for developing TREM2-targeted therapies and personalized medicine strategies for autoimmune uveitis, with significant potential clinical implications.

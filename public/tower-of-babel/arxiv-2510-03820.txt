Fixed Point Theorem for Path-Averaged Contractions in Complete b-Metric Spaces MSC2020: 47H10, 54E50 Keywords: Path Averaged Contractions, contractive mapping, fixed point, complete metric space.
Nicola Fabiano

Abstract
We extend the fixed point result for Path-Averaged Contractions (PA-contractions) from complete metric spaces to complete b-metric spaces. We prove that every continuous PA-contraction on a complete b-metric space has a unique fixed point, provided the contraction constant α \alpha satisfies s ​ α < 1 s\alpha<1 , where s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 is the b-metric coefficient. The proof relies on geometric decay of successive distances and the generalized triangle inequality. This result paves the way for extending averaged contraction principles to other classical types, such as Kannan, Chatterjea, and Ćirić-type mappings, as well as Wardowski’s F-contractions, in generalized metric settings.



1
Introduction

Path-Averaged Contractions (PA-contractions) were recently introduced as a generalization of Banach contractions, where contraction is measured in terms of the average distance along orbits
[
7
]
. It has been established that such mappings possess a unique fixed point in complete metric spaces. In this paper, we extend this result to the broader class of
complete b-metric spaces
, which relax the standard triangle inequality by allowing a multiplicative constant
s\geq 1
.

We show that the fixed point property persists under the additional conditions that
T
is continuous and
s\alpha<1
, and provide a self-contained proof of existence and uniqueness. These assumptions arise naturally from the structure of b-metric spaces and may be removable in symmetric subclasses of contractions.

Our main result establishes the existence and uniqueness of fixed points for PA-contractions in b-metric spaces. As applications, we compare PA-contractions with two foundational classes: Banach and Kannan contractions. We prove that every Banach contraction is a PA-contraction, but not conversely; hence, PA-contractions strictly generalize Banach’s principle. On the other hand, the classes of PA- and Kannan contractions are incomparable in general.

The investigation of PA-type conditions for other classical principles — including Chatterjea
[
4
]
, Ćirić-type mappings
[
5
]
, and Wardowski’s F-contractions
[
13
,
8
]
— remains open and will be addressed in subsequent work.

2
Preliminaries

We begin with the definition of a b-metric space.

Definition 1

(b-metric space)
.

Let
X
be a nonempty set,
s\geq 1
a real number, and
d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
a function satisfying:

(i)

d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
,

(ii)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
for all
x,y\in X
,

(iii)

d(x,z)\leq s\big(d(x,y)+d(y,z)\big)
for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Then
(X,d)
is called a
b-metric space
with coefficient

s

[
6
]
.
A sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
X
is Cauchy if
\lim_{m,n\to\infty}d(x_{n},x_{m})=0
. The space is
complete
if every Cauchy sequence converges in
X
.

We now define the main concept of this paper.

Definition 2

(PA-contraction)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a b-metric space. A mapping
T:X\to X
is called a
PA-contraction
(Path-Averaged Contraction) if there exist constants
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
x,y\in X
and all
n\geq N
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}y)\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{k}y).

(1)

This condition implies that the cumulative distance between iterated images contracts on average by a uniform factor
\alpha<1
, independent of the initial points
x,y
. Such path-averaging ideas have parallels in orbital continuity
[
10
]
and generalized contractions
[
11
]
.

3
Main Result

Our goal is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete b-metric space with coefficient
s\geq 1
, and let
T:X\to X
be a continuous PA-contraction with contraction constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
s\alpha<1
. Then
T
has a unique fixed point in
X
, and for every
x\in X
, the sequence
\{T^{n}x\}
converges to this fixed point.

Remark 4
.

The assumption that
T
is continuous ensures that
T^{n}x\to z
implies
T^{n+1}x\to Tz
, so by uniqueness of limits,
Tz=z
. While this holds in many natural examples, it is not implied by the PA-contraction condition alone.

Additionally, the condition
s\alpha<1
arises from the generalized triangle inequality in b-metric spaces and ensures that the orbit
\{T^{n}x\}
is Cauchy. We leave as an open question whether this condition can be weakened or removed in special classes of PA-contractions (e.g., Kannan-type or Chatterjea-type).

The proof proceeds in several steps: we first show that the sequence of iterates is Cauchy, then that it converges to a fixed point, and finally that the fixed point is unique.

4
Proof of the Main Theorem

4.1
Step 1: Summability of successive distances

Fix an arbitrary point
x\in X
, and define the sequence
x_{n}=T^{n}x
. Let

a_{k}=d(T^{k}x,T^{k+1}x)=d(x_{k},x_{k+1}).

We will show that the series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}
converges.

Consider the pair
(x,Tx)
. Apply the PA-contraction condition to this pair. For
n\geq N
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}(Tx))\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{k}(Tx)).

But
T^{k}(Tx)=T^{k+1}x
, so

d(T^{k}x,T^{k}(Tx))=d(T^{k}x,T^{k+1}x)=a_{k},

and

d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}(Tx))=d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+2}x)=a_{k+1}.

Thus, the inequality becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k+1}\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}.

Let
S_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}
. Then

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}-a_{0}=S_{n+1}-a_{0},

and the inequality
\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\leq\alpha S_{n}
becomes

S_{n+1}-a_{0}\leq\alpha S_{n}\quad\Rightarrow\quad S_{n+1}\leq\alpha S_{n}+a_{0}.

This is a recursive inequality. Since
\alpha\in(0,1)
, standard analysis shows that
\{S_{n}\}
is bounded. Indeed, iterating

\displaystyle S_{n+1}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha S_{n}+a_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(\alpha S_{n-1}+a_{0})+a_{0}=\alpha^{2}S_{n-1}+\alpha a_{0}+a_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots\leq\alpha^{n}S_{1}+a_{0}(1+\alpha+\cdots+\alpha^{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{n}S_{1}+\frac{a_{0}}{1-\alpha}.

Hence,
\sup_{n}S_{n}<\infty
, so
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}<\infty
. In particular,
a_{k}\to 0
.

4.2
Step 2: The sequence
\{T^{n}x\}
is Cauchy

Let
m>n
. We estimate
d(T^{n}x,T^{m}x)
using the generalized triangle inequality in b-metric spaces. First, we establish geometric decay of the successive distances.

Recall from Step 1 that
a_{k}=d(T^{k}x,T^{k+1}x)
, and define
S_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{k}
. We showed that
\{S_{n}\}
is bounded, so
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}<\infty
. To obtain stronger control, we now prove that
a_{k}
decays geometrically.

Fix
p\geq 0
and apply the PA-contraction condition to the pair
(T^{p}x,T^{p+1}x)
. For all
n\geq N
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}(T^{p}x),T^{k+1}(T^{p+1}x))\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}(T^{p}x),T^{k}(T^{p+1}x)).

Since
T^{k}(T^{p}x)=T^{p+k}x
, this becomes

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{p+k+1}\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{p+k}.

Define
S_{p}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}a_{p+k}
. Then the above inequality reads

S_{p+1}(n)\leq\alpha S_{p}(n).

Iterating this,

S_{p}(n)\leq\alpha^{p}S_{0}(n)\leq\alpha^{p}\cdot C,\quad\text{where }C=\sup_{n\geq N}S_{0}(n)<\infty.

Now fix
n=N
. Then

\sum_{k=p}^{p+N-1}a_{k}=S_{p}(N)\leq C\alpha^{p}.

Since each
a_{k}\geq 0
, it follows that
a_{p}\leq S_{p}(N)\leq C\alpha^{p}
. Therefore,

a_{k}\leq C\alpha^{k}\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0.

We now estimate
d(T^{n}x,T^{m}x)
for
m>n
. By repeated application of the
b-triangle inequality, we have the following bound

d(T^{n}x,T^{m}x)\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}s^{j-n+1}d(T^{j}x,T^{j+1}x)=\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}s^{j-n+1}a_{j}.

Using the geometric decay
a_{j}\leq C\alpha^{j}
,

\displaystyle d(T^{n}x,T^{m}x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=n}^{m-1}s^{j-n+1}C\alpha^{j}

\displaystyle=Cs\alpha^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{m-n-1}(s\alpha)^{i}\quad\text{(letting }i=j-n\text{)}

\displaystyle\leq Cs\alpha^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(s\alpha)^{i}.

If
s\alpha<1
, the geometric series converges

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(s\alpha)^{i}=\frac{1}{1-s\alpha},

so

d(T^{n}x,T^{m}x)\leq\frac{Cs\alpha^{n}}{1-s\alpha}.

As
n\to\infty
, the right-hand side tends to 0 uniformly in
m>n
. Therefore,
\{T^{n}x\}
is a Cauchy sequence.

Since
X
is complete, there exists
z\in X
such that
T^{n}x\to z
as
n\to\infty
.

4.3
Step 3:
z
is a fixed point of
T

We now show that
Tz=z
.
Since
T^{n}x\to z
and
T
is continuous, it follows that

T(T^{n}x)=T^{n+1}x\to Tz\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

On the other hand,
\{T^{n+1}x\}
is a subsequence of
\{T^{n}x\}
, so
T^{n+1}x\to z
. In a b-metric space, limits are unique: if
x_{n}\to x
and
x_{n}\to y
, then

d(x,y)\leq s\big(d(x,x_{n})+d(x_{n},y)\big)\to 0,

so
d(x,y)=0
and hence
x=y
. Therefore,
Tz=z
.

Thus,
z
is a fixed point of
T
.

4.4
Step 4: Uniqueness of the fixed point

Suppose
z,w\in X
are two fixed points of
T
, i.e.,
Tz=z
,
Tw=w
. Then for all
k\geq 0
,
T^{k}z=z
,
T^{k}w=w
. Thus,

d(T^{k}z,T^{k}w)=d(z,w).

Apply the PA-contraction condition to
(z,w)
for
n\geq N

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}z,T^{k+1}w)\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}z,T^{k}w)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(z,w)\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(z,w).

This simplifies to

n\cdot d(z,w)\leq\alpha\cdot n\cdot d(z,w)\quad\Rightarrow\quad(1-\alpha)d(z,w)\leq 0.

Since
\alpha<1
, we have
1-\alpha>0
, so
d(z,w)=0
. Hence,
z=w
.

5
Conclusion for PA section

We have shown that for any starting point
x\in X
, the sequence
\{T^{n}x\}
converges to a fixed point
z
, and that this fixed point is unique. Therefore, every PA-contraction on a complete b-metric space has a unique fixed point.

Corollary 5
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete b-metric space with coefficient
s\geq 1
. If
T:X\to X
is a continuous PA-contraction with constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
s\alpha<1
, then
T
has a unique fixed point, and for every
x\in X
, the sequence
\{T^{n}x\}
converges to this fixed point.

This extends the known result from metric spaces to b-metric spaces, albeit under the additional requirements that
T
is continuous and
s\alpha<1
. The key insight is that path-averaged contraction ensures geometric decay of successive distances, which, when combined with the generalized triangle inequality, guarantees convergence under these conditions. For related approaches involving averaging or orbital control, see
[
10
,
12
]
.

6
Banach contraction in b-metric space

Fixed point theory often begins with the Banach contraction principle, which requires a uniform contraction factor
\beta<1
such that
d(Tx,Ty)\leq\beta d(x,y)
. Recently, Path-Averaged (PA) contractions have been introduced, where contraction is measured in terms of the average distance along orbits.

In the following sections, we clarify the relationship between these two notions in metric spaces. Specifically, we prove:

•

Every Banach contraction is a PA-contraction.

•

Not every PA-contraction is a Banach contraction.

We provide a finite, explicit counterexample using the discrete metric to illustrate the proper inclusion.

Definition 6

(Metric space)
.

A
metric space
is a pair
(X,d)
where
X
is a set and
d:X\times X\to[0,\infty)
satisfies:

(i)

d(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
,

(ii)

d(x,y)=d(y,x)
, for all
x,y\in X
,

(iii)

d(x,z)\leq d(x,y)+d(y,z)
, for all
x,y,z\in X
.

Definition 7

(Banach contraction)
.

A mapping
T:X\to X
is a
Banach contraction
if there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\beta\,d(x,y),\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in X.

7
Banach Contractions are PA-Contractions

We first show that the class of Banach contractions is contained in the class of PA-contractions.

Proposition 8
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. If
T:X\to X
is a Banach contraction with constant
\beta\in(0,1)
, then
T
is a PA-contraction with
\alpha=\beta
and
N=1
.

Proof.

Suppose
d(Tx,Ty)\leq\beta\,d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
,
\beta\in(0,1)
.

By induction, for any
k\geq 0
,

d(T^{k}x,T^{k}y)\leq\beta^{k}d(x,y).

Now fix
x,y\in X
and
n\geq 1
. Consider the left-hand side of (
1
)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}y)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{k+1}d(x,y)=\beta\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{k}d(x,y).

The right-hand side satisfies

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{k}y)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\beta^{k}d(x,y).

Therefore,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}y)\leq\beta\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{k}y).

This holds for all
n\geq 1
. So we may take
\alpha=\beta\in(0,1)
,
N=1
. Hence,
T
is a PA-contraction.
∎

8
PA-Contractions are Not Necessarily Banach Contractions

We now show that the converse is false: there exist PA-contractions that are not Banach contractions.

Example 9
.

Let
X=\{0,1,2\}
, and define the
discrete metric

d(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }x=y,\\
1&\text{if }x\neq y.\end{cases}

Define
T:X\to X
by

T(0)=1,\quad T(1)=2,\quad T(2)=2.

Then

(a)

T
is
not
a Banach contraction.

(b)

T

is
a PA-contraction.

Proof.

We prove each part separately.

(a)
T
is not a Banach contraction

Suppose, for contradiction, that
T
is a Banach contraction with some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\beta\,d(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in X.

Consider
x=0
,
y=1
. Then,

d(0,1)=1
;

d(T(0),T(1))=d(1,2)=1
.

So
1=d(T(0),T(1))\leq\beta\cdot 1=\beta<1
, a contradiction.

Similarly,
d(T(0),T(2))=d(1,2)=1=d(0,2)
, so again
1\leq\beta<1
fails.

Hence,
T
is not a Banach contraction.

(b)
T
is a PA-contraction

We show that there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
x,y\in X
and all
n\geq N
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}y)\leq\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{k}y).

First, compute the iterates of
T

\displaystyle T^{0}(x)

\displaystyle=x,

\displaystyle T^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=Tx,

\displaystyle T^{2}(0)

\displaystyle=T(1)=2,\quad T^{2}(1)=T(2)=2,\quad T^{2}(2)=2,

\displaystyle T^{k}(x)

\displaystyle=2\quad\text{for all }k\geq 2,\text{ and all }x\in X.

Thus, all orbits reach the fixed point
2
by the second iteration and remain there.

Now, analyze all distinct pairs
(x,y)
,
x\neq y
. There are three such unordered pairs.

Case 1:
x=0,y=1

Compute
\delta_{k}=d(T^{k}(0),T^{k}(1))
:

k=0
:
d(0,1)=1
,

k=1
:
d(1,2)=1
,

k\geq 2
:
d(2,2)=0
.
So
\delta_{k}=[1,1,0,0,\dots]
.

For
n\geq 2
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{k}=1+1=2
;

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{k+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}=1+0+\cdots+0=1
.

So

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}y)=1\leq\alpha\cdot 2\quad\Rightarrow\quad\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Case 2:
x=0,y=2

k=0
:
d(0,2)=1
;

k=1
:
d(1,2)=1
;

k\geq 2
:
d(2,2)=0
.

Same as Case 1: sum = 2, shifted sum = 1. So again:

1\leq 2\alpha\Rightarrow\alpha\geq 1/2

Case 3:
x=1,y=2

k=0
:
d(1,2)=1
,

k=1
:
d(2,2)=0
,

k\geq 2
:
d(2,2)=0
.

So
\delta_{k}=[1,0,0,\dots]
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{k}=1
;

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{k+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{k}=0
.

So

0\leq\alpha\cdot 1\quad\text{for any }\alpha>0.

Conclusion of (b)

For all
x\neq y
and
n\geq 2
, the worst-case ratio of sums is
1/2
. Therefore, choose
\alpha=\frac{2}{3}\in(0,1)
and
N=2
. Then for all
x,y\in X
,
n\geq 2

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k+1}x,T^{k+1}y)\leq\frac{2}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{k}y).

Hence,
T
is a PA-contraction.

∎

9
Conclusion for Banach Section

We have established the following

Theorem 10
.

In any metric space:

(a)

Every Banach contraction is a PA-contraction.

(b)

Not every PA-contraction is a Banach contraction.

Therefore, the class of PA-contractions strictly contains the class of Banach contractions.

Proof.

Part (a) was proved in Section
7
. Part (b) follows from Section
8
, which constructs a mapping that is a PA-contraction but not a Banach contraction.
∎

Remark 11
.

Since every metric space is a b-metric space with coefficient
s=1
, and the counterexample provided lies in such a space, the strict inclusion of Banach contractions in PA-contractions extends to the class of b-metric spaces. Therefore, PA-contractions form a strictly larger class than Banach contractions in both metric and b-metric settings.

Corollary 12
.

The Path-Averaged contraction condition is a
strict generalization
of the Banach contraction principle in metric spaces.

10
Comparison with Kannan Contractions

We recall the definition of a Kannan contraction.

Definition 13

(Kannan contraction)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a b-metric space. A mapping
T:X\to X
is a
Kannan contraction
if there exists
\beta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2s}\right)
such that for all
x,y\in X
,

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\beta\left[d(x,Tx)+d(y,Ty)\right].

Note that in metric spaces (
s=1
), the condition reduces to
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
, which is the classical Kannan constant.

We now compare PA-contractions with Kannan contractions in metric spaces. Unlike Banach contractions, Kannan-type mappings are not necessarily comparable to PA-contractions in general. However, we show that the class of PA-contractions is not contained in the class of Kannan contractions, nor vice versa.

In particular, we demonstrate that there exist PA-contractions that are not Kannan contractions.

Example 14
.

Let
X=\{0,1,2\}
, with the discrete metric:

d(x,y)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }x=y,\\
1&\text{if }x\neq y.\end{cases}

Define
T:X\to X
by

T(0)=1,\quad T(1)=2,\quad T(2)=2.

As shown in Section
8
,
T
is a PA-contraction with
\alpha=\frac{2}{3}
,
N=2
.

We now show that
T
is
not
a Kannan contraction.

Suppose for contradiction that there exists
\beta<\frac{1}{2}
such that

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\beta[d(x,Tx)+d(y,Ty)],\quad\forall x,y\in X.

Take
x=0
,
y=1
. Then,

d(T(0),T(1))=d(1,2)=1
,

d(0,T(0))=d(0,1)=1
,

d(1,T(1))=d(1,2)=1
.
Right-hand side:
\beta(1+1)=2\beta<1
.

So
1\leq 2\beta<1
, contradiction.

Hence,
T
is a PA-contraction but not a Kannan contraction.

Corollary 15
.

The class of PA-contractions is not contained in the class of Kannan contractions. Moreover, since there exist Kannan contractions that are not Banach (and PA contains all Banach contractions), the two classes are incomparable in general.

Remark 16
.

It remains an open question whether every Kannan contraction satisfies the PA-contraction condition. Given the structural differences between the two definitions — one based on self-distances, the other on orbit averages — such an inclusion seems unlikely without additional assumptions on the space or mapping.

11
Conclusion

In this paper, we have extended the theory of Path-Averaged Contractions (PA-contractions) from metric to b-metric spaces. We proved that every continuous PA-contraction on a complete b-metric space has a unique fixed point, provided the contraction constant
\alpha
satisfies
s\alpha<1
, where
s\geq 1
is the b-metric coefficient. The result relies on the geometric decay of successive distances and careful application of the generalized triangle inequality.

Furthermore, we clarified the relationship between PA-contractions and two classical classes:

•

Every Banach contraction is a PA-contraction, but not conversely — hence, PA-contractions strictly generalize Banach contractions.

•

The classes of PA-contractions and Kannan contractions are incomparable in general; neither contains the other.

These comparisons highlight the distinct nature of path-averaged contraction as a principle that captures cumulative orbital behavior rather than pointwise control.

The relationship between PA-contractions and other classical principles – such as Chatterjea
[
4
]
, Ćirić-type mappings
[
5
]
, and Wardowski-type F-contractions
[
13
,
8
]
– remains open and will be investigated in future work.

Infinitesimal characters and Lafforgue’s pseudocharacters
Vytautas Paškūnas and Julian Quast

Abstract
. We associate infinitesimal characters to p p -adic families of Lafforgue’s pseudocharacters of the absolute Galois group of a p p -adic local field by extending a construction of Dospinescu, Schraen, and the first author. We use this construction to study the action of the centre of the universal enveloping algebra on the locally analytic vectors in the Hecke eigenspaces of the completed cohomology.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma_{F}
be the absolute Galois group of a
p
-adic local field

F
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group defined over
F
and
let
\rho:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
be a continuous admissible
representation, where
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
is the
L
-group of
G
. Dospinescu, Schraen and the first
author in
[
16
]
have attached to
\rho
a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism
\zeta_{\rho}:Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
,
where
\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of the restriction of scalars
\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}G
and

Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g})
is the centre of
its enveloping algebra. The homomorphism
\zeta_{\rho}
is defined using Sen theory and encodes information about the Hodge–Tate weights of the Galois representation
\rho
. The point of view taken in
[
16
]
is
that the construction
\rho\mapsto\zeta_{\rho}

is an infinitesimal shadow of a hypothetical
p
-adic
Langlands correspondence. Namely to
\rho
there should
correspond a packet of admissible unitary
p
-adic Banach space
representations of
G(F)
. For any representation
\Pi
in the
packet corresponding to
\rho
the expectation is that the
action of
Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g})
on the locally analytic vectors in
\Pi
is given by
\zeta_{\rho}
. Although the

p
-adic Langlands correspondence has not yet been defined at this level of generality, in
[
16
]
the authors have verified that the expectation is true when
\Pi

is a Hecke eigenspace in the completed cohomology in many cases.

A key result proved in
[
16
]
and used in the application to the Hecke eigenspaces is that the construction
\rho\mapsto\zeta_{\rho}
behaves well in
p
-adic families.
Namely, if
\rho:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(A)

is an admissible continuous representation, where
A
is an affinoid algebra
then there is a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism

\zeta_{\rho}:Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow A
, compatible with specialisations at closed points
x:A\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, so that

(\zeta_{\rho})_{x}=\zeta_{\rho_{x}}
. Moreover,
\zeta_{\rho}
is functorial in
A
, which allows instead of
\Sp A
to consider more general rigid analytic spaces.

In this paper we refine the results of
[
16
]
by considering

p
-adic families of Lafforgue’s pseudocharacters instead
of Galois representations.
If
\rho:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

is a continuous representation then
\zeta_{\rho}
depends
only on the
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-semisimplification of
\rho
, which in turn
depends only on the associated
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacter

\Theta_{\rho}
.
Conversely, every continuous admissible
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
-valued
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacter
\Theta
corresponds to a
\widehat{G}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
-conjugacy class of continuous admissible
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-semisimple representations
\rho:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
and hence
we may define
\zeta_{\Theta}:=\zeta_{\rho}
.

The main result
of this paper is that the construction
\Theta\mapsto\zeta_{\Theta}
varies well in
p
-adic families.
To formulate it we let
\mathrm{Rig}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
be the category of rigid analytic spaces over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let

\tilde{X}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}:\mathrm{Rig}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\Set
be the functor, which
maps a rigid analytic space
Y
to
the set of continuous admissible
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacters

\Theta:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(\mathcal{O}(Y))
. The admissibility for

{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacters of
\Gamma_{F}
, defined in
Section

7.2
, is a condition
analogous to admissibility for representations of

\Gamma_{F}
into
L
-groups. In particular, if
G
is
split over
F
then according to our conventions
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
is equal to the dual group
\widehat{G}
and every
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacter is admissible.

Theorem 1.1

(

)
.

There exists a unique family of

Strategy of the proof

Moreover,

is
disjoint union of rigid analytic spaces of

Let

of

it is
enough to prove that

Let us explain how we prove that

is normal, which implies that it is reduced. Let us replace

by an arbitrary finitely generated profinite group

-group scheme

-condensed representations

is equal to the specialisation of
\Theta^{u}
at

The condition

that

1.2.
Application

The result allows us to drop the hypothesis
that the residual representation is irreducible in
[
16
, Section 9.9]
, where the authors show that the subspace of locally analytic vectors of a Hecke eigenspace
in the completed cohomology of a definite unitary group
affords an infinitesimal character and compute this infinitesimal character
in terms of the associated Galois representation. The key issue, that we resolve in
Section

8
, is that the automorphic theory leads to a determinant law valued in a big Hecke algebra and not directly to a Galois representation itself,
which means that the results of
[
16
]
cannot be applied.
Although the application uses
only the results, when
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G=\GL_{n}
, we expect that
Theorem

1.1
in the more general setting will
be useful in the emerging categorical local Langlands correspondence in the locally analytic setting
[
18
, Section 6.2]
.

Dimension and codimension

If
X
is a topological space its
dimension

\dim X
is defined as the supremum of lengths of chains of
closed irreducible subspaces,
[
36
,
Tag 0055
]
. If
Z
is a closed irreducible subset of
X
then the
codimension of
Z
in
X
, denoted by
\codim(Z,X)
, is defined as the supremum of lengths
of closed irreducible subsets in
X
starting with
Z
,
[
36
,
Tag 02I3
]
. It follows from the definition that

(1)

\dim Z+\codim(Z,X)\leq\dim X.

We say that
the dimension formula holds for
X
if (
1
) is an equality for all closed irreducible subsets
Z
of
X
.
If
X
is an irreducible scheme of finite type over a field
then the dimension formula holds by
[
21
, Proposition 5.30]
. However, it may fail for general schemes as the following example shows.

Example 2.1
.

Let
X=\Spec\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
. The ideal
(px-1)

is maximal in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
, as its residue field is
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
We let
Z
be the closed point in
X
defined by this ideal. Then
\dim Z=0
,
\dim X=2
and
\codim(Z,X)=1
, as the localisation of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
at
(px-1)

coincides with the localisation of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}[x]
at
(x-p^{-1})
.

The purpose of this note is to show that (
1
) is an equality for all irreducible
Z
if
X
is equidimensional locally of finite
type over

, where

R
is a complete local noetherian ring with a maximal

\mathfrak{m}
. Sometimes

is called the

of

. The result will be used in

with

and

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

We first note that

Since

Tag 02IC

Proof.

[

The main result of this section

4.
Lafforgue’s pseudocharacters

In this section we recall the definition of pseudocharacters and the moduli space of condensed representations. The main result of the section is
Theorem

4.12
and its
Corollary

4.13
. Until
Section

4.2
we let
\mathcal{O}
be any commutative ring.

A
generalised reductive group

H
over
\mathcal{O}
is a
smooth affine group scheme, that has reductive geometric fibres and such that
H/H^{0}
is finite, where
H^{0}
is the neutral component of
H
. For each
n\geq 1
the group scheme
H
acts on the
n
-fold product
H^{n}
by
g\cdot(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=(gg_{1}g^{-1},\dots,gg_{n}g^{-1})
. This induces an algebraic action of
H
on the affine coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}[H^{n}]
of
H^{n}
. The submodule
\mathcal{O}[H^{n}]^{H^{0}}\subseteq\mathcal{O}[H^{n}]
is defined as the algebraic invariant module of the
H^{0}
-representation
\mathcal{O}[H^{n}]
. It is an
\mathcal{O}
-subalgebra, since
H
acts by
\mathcal{O}
-linear automorphisms.
In subsequent sections, we will take
H
to be an
L
-group or a
C
-group of a
p
-adic group.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abstract group and let
A
be a commutative
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. An
H
-pseudocharacter

\Theta
of
\Gamma
over
A
is a sequence
(\Theta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{O}
-algebra maps

\Theta_{n}:\mathcal{O}[H^{n}]^{H^{0}}\to\mathrm{Map}(\Gamma^{n},A)

for
n\geq 1
, satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

For each
n,m\geq 1
, each map
\zeta:\{1,\dots,m\}\to\{1,\dots,n\}
,
f\in\mathcal{O}[H^{m}]^{H^{0}}
and
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
, we have

\Theta_{n}(f^{\zeta})(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})=\Theta_{m}(f)(\gamma_{\zeta(1)},\dots,\gamma_{\zeta(m)})

where
f^{\zeta}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})=f(g_{\zeta(1)},\dots,g_{\zeta(m)})
.

(2)

For each
n\geq 1
, for each
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n+1}\in\Gamma
and each
f\in\mathcal{O}[H^{n}]^{H^{0}}
, we have

\Theta_{n+1}(\hat{f})(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n+1})=\Theta_{n}(f)(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{n-1},\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n+1})

where
\hat{f}(g_{1},\dots,g_{n+1})=f(g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{n-1},g_{n}g_{n+1})
.

We denote the set of
H
-pseudocharacters of
\Gamma
over
A
by
\mathrm{PC}_{H}^{\Gamma}(A)
.
If
f:A\to B
is a homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras, then there is an induced map
f_{*}:\mathrm{PC}_{H}^{\Gamma}(A)\to\mathrm{PC}_{H}^{\Gamma}(B)
.
For
\Theta\in\mathrm{PC}_{H}^{\Gamma}(A)
, the image
f_{*}(\Theta)
is called the
specialisation
of
\Theta
along
f
and is denoted by
\Theta\otimes_{A}B
.
It is easy to verify that specialisation along
f:A\to B
commutes with composition with a homomorphism
\varphi:H\to H^{\prime}
, i.e.
(\varphi\circ\Theta)\otimes_{A}B=\varphi\circ(\Theta\otimes_{A}B)
.

When
\Gamma
is a topological group,
A
is a topological ring and for all
n\geq 1
, the map
\Theta_{n}
has image in the set
\mathcal{C}(\Gamma^{n},A)
of continuous maps
\Gamma^{n}\to A
, we say that
\Theta
is
continuous
. We denote the set of continuous
H
-pseudocharacters of
\Gamma
with values in
A
by
\mathrm{cPC}_{H}^{\Gamma}(A)
.

The functor
A\mapsto\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma}_{H}(A)
is representable by an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
[
33
, Theorem 3.19]
.
Let us write
\Rep^{\Gamma}_{H}(A):=\Hom(\Gamma,H(A))
.
The functor
A\mapsto\Rep^{\Gamma}_{H}(A)
is also representable by an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra.
To a representation
\rho:\Gamma\to H(A)
, we associate an
H
-pseudocharacter
\Theta_{\rho}\in\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma}_{H}(A)
by the formula

\Theta_{\rho,m}(f)(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}):=f(\rho(\gamma_{1}),\dots,\rho(\gamma_{m}))

for all
m\geq 1
, all
f\in\mathcal{O}[H^{m}]^{H^{0}}
and all
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m}\in\Gamma
.
This defines a morphism of schemes
\Rep^{\Gamma}_{H}\to\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma}_{H}
by

(3)

\Rep^{\Gamma}_{H}(A)\to\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma}_{H}(A),\quad\rho\mapsto\Theta_{\rho}

Lemma 4.2
.

If
H^{0}\subseteq Z(H)
, then for all
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
A
the map (
3
) is bijective.

Proof.

This is
[
32
, Lemma 6.3 (1)]
.
Our assumption implies that
H^{0}
is a connected commutative reductive group scheme and that the action of

on

the map

4.3.
Chenevier’s determinant laws and finitely generated groups

We first consider the case, that
\Gamma=\widehat{\Sigma}
is the profinite completion of a finitely generated group
\Sigma

Recall, that
\Theta^{u}
corresponds to a
continuous determinant law

[

We denote by

[
12

The Cayley–Hamilton ideal

R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}
-algebra homomorphisms

Via restriction along the map

D|_{R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]}:R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]\to R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}
.
Similarly, we can consider the Cayley–Hamilton ideal
\mathrm{CH}(D|_{R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]})

We observe, that the Cayley–Hamilton quotients coincide:

Lemma 4.4
.

The natural map
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]/\mathrm{CH}(D|_{R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]})\to R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}\llbracket\widehat{\Sigma}\rrbracket/\mathrm{CH}(D)
induced by the universal property of the Cayley–Hamilton quotient is an isomorphism.

Proof.

By
[
37
, Proposition 2.13]

E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]/\mathrm{CH}(D|_{R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}[\Sigma]})
is a finitely generated

-module. Thus

is finite as a set, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal of
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}
, and hence
\Sigma\rightarrow(E/\mathfrak{m}^{n}E)^{\times}
factors through a finite quotient. We deduce that

extends to a surjection
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}\llbracket\widehat{\Sigma}\rrbracket\twoheadrightarrow E
.
Now

is equipped with the determinant law

induced by

.
Both

.
Since the image of

The universal property of the Cayley–Hamilton quotient yields a surjection

7.

If

is a ring then

if the

Let

be a rigid analytic space over
L
.
As explained before

6.9
, the ring
of global functions

of the restriction of scalars
\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}G

. Let

7.2.
Rigid analytic space of admissible pseudocharacters

The morphism
f:{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G/\widehat{G}
induces a morphism

\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}\rightarrow\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G/\widehat{G}}
,
\Theta\mapsto f\circ\Theta
.
Since
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G/\widehat{G}=\underline{\Gal(E/F)}
is a constant group scheme, its neutral component is trivial,
and
Lemma

4.2
implies
that the
morphism

\Rep^{\Gamma_{F}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G/\widehat{G}}\rightarrow\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G/\widehat{G}}
,
\rho\mapsto\Theta_{\rho}
is an isomorphism. We say that
\Theta\in\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}(A)
is

if

f\circ\Theta
corresponds to
a homomorphism
\rho:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow\underline{\Gal(E/F)}(A)
, which maps
g\in\Gamma_{F}
to the constant function
\Spec A\rightarrow\Gal(E/F)
with value
q(g)
, where
q:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow\Gal(E/F)
is the quotient map. If
\rho\in\Rep^{\Gamma_{F}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}(A)
then
\rho
is admissible
if and only if
\Theta_{\rho}
is admissible.

We will show that the functor

\tilde{X}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}:\mathrm{Rig}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\Set
, which
maps a rigid analytic space
Y
to the set of continuous admissible
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacters
\Theta:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(\mathcal{O}(Y))
is representable by a quasi-Stein rigid
analytic space
X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}
using the results of
[
33
]
. We will define a homomorphism
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebras
\zeta^{\mathrm{univ}}:Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G})
using the results
of the previous section and the description of
X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}
obtained from
[
33
]
. If
Y\in\mathrm{Rig}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
then an admissible continuous
{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G
-pseudocharacter

\Theta:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(Y)
defines a morphism
of rigid spaces
Y\rightarrow X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}
and
we may then define
\zeta_{Y,\Theta}
as the composition
of
\zeta^{\mathrm{univ}}
and
\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(Y)
.

We will first recall the results of
[
33
, Section 6]
,
which generalise the results of Chenevier
[
12
]
in the case of
\GL_{n}
. Let
H
be a generalised reductive group
scheme defined over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. The functor
\tilde{X}_{H}:\mathrm{Rig}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\Set
, which sends
Y
to the
set of continuous
H
-pseudocharacters
\Theta:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(Y)
is represented by a quasi-Stein rigid
analytic space

(58)

X_{H}=\coprod_{z\in|\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{H}|}(\Spf R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}})^{\mathrm{rig}},

by
[
33
, Theorem 6.19]
, where

|\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{H}|\subset\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{H}
is the subset of closed points
z
with finite residue field
k_{z}
, such that the canonical
H
-pseudocharacter
\Theta_{z}\in\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F}}_{H}(k_{z})
attached to
z
is continuous for the discrete topology on
k_{z}
and
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}}

represents the deformation problem
\Def_{\Theta_{z}}:\mathfrak{A}_{W(k_{z})}\rightarrow\Set
of
\Theta_{z}
,
where
W(k_{z})
is the ring of Witt vectors of
k_{z}
.
This description of
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}}
is implicit in the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 6.19]
.

We apply this with
H={\vphantom{G}}^{L}G
.

Lemma 7.7
.

The functor

\tilde{X}^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}
is represented by a quasi-Stein rigid analytic space

X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}=\coprod_{z\in|\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F},\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}|}(\Spf R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}})^{\mathrm{rig}},

The quotient map

gives us a point in

. It follows

, where the morphism

∎

Theorem 7.8
.

There exists a unique family of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebra

.

Proof.

We first fix

and let
\Theta^{u,z}:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}}

be the universal deformation of
\Theta_{z}

Let

L
be any extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
and residue field

, which contains

as a subfield.

is naturally a

-algebra and if we let
\overline{\Theta}=\Theta_{z}\otimes_{k_{z}}k
then the deformation functor

of

is represented by

\overline{\Theta}
.
It follows from the reconstruction theorem
[
33
, Theorem 3.8]
that if

is large enough

\overline{\rho}:\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow{\vphantom{G}}^{L}G(k)

. In this case we are
in the situation of the previous subsection with

We let

.
Lemma

7.6
implies
that
\zeta^{z}
takes values in
(R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}})^{\mathrm{an}}
.
It follows from
Corollary

7.5
that the specialisation property holds for
\zeta^{z}
. We define

\zeta^{\mathrm{univ}}:Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(X^{\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G})=\prod_{z\in|\mathrm{PC}^{\Gamma_{F},\mathrm{adm}}_{{\vphantom{G}}{}^{L}G}|}(R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\Theta_{z}})^{\mathrm{an}}

\zeta^{\mathrm{univ}}
. If

and

is reduced

-algebras

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-algebras
\theta_{\rho}:S((\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}^{T})^{*})^{\widehat{G}^{T}}\rightarrow E\otimes\mathcal{O}(X)

\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\kappa(x)\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
We let
\zeta^{C}_{x}:Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{g})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\rightarrow\kappa(x)

denote the composition of
\zeta^{C}
with the induced map
\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\kappa(x)
.

Theorem 8.2
.

The action of
Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{g})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
on
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}[\mathfrak{m}_{x}]^{\mathrm{la}}

is given by
\zeta^{C}_{x}
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as the proof of

[
16
, Theorem 9.20]
, which is proved in a more general setting and specialised to the setting of definite unitary groups in

[
16
, Section 9.9]
, so we only give a sketch.

The action of

\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})
on
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
induces an action of
\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})^{\mathrm{an}}
on the locally analytic
vectors
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{la}}
. We then
have two actions of
Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{g})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}

on
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{la}}
: one via
\zeta^{C}
and the
other coming from the action of
G(F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
It is enough to show that these actions coincide.
This is verified by first showing that the two actions
coincide on a subspace contained in locally algebraic
vectors in
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{\mathrm{la}}
, by relating
this subspace to classical automorphic forms and using
the local-global compatibility at
p
of Galois representations associated to these automorphic forms.

More precisely, let
L
be a sufficiently large finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, and let
\tau_{p}
be a smooth irreducible representation of
K_{p}
on an
L
-vector space, which is a supercuspidal type. Let
\Irr
be the
set of isomorphism classes of irreducible algebraic
representations of
(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}G)_{L}
.
If
[V]\in\Irr
then
we may view

V
as a representation of
K_{p}

on an
L
-vector space via

K_{p}\subset(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}G)(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\subset(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}G)(L)
. Since
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is admissible,

\Hom_{K_{p}}(\tau_{p}\otimes_{L}V,\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{L})

is a finite dimensional
L
-vector space on which
\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})
acts. This action is semi-simple by

[
16
, Lemma 9.18]
.

Let
y:\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
be a homomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebras, such that

\Hom_{K_{p}}(\tau_{p}\otimes_{L}V,\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{L})[\mathfrak{m}_{y}]\neq 0.

We claim that
\zeta^{C}_{y}
is equal to the
infinitesimal character of the algebraic representation
V
. The claim implies that
the two actions of
Z(\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathfrak{g})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
coincide on the image of the
evaluation map

(68)

\bigoplus_{[V]\in\Irr}\Hom_{K_{p}}(\tau_{p}\otimes_{L}V,\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{L})\otimes_{L}(\tau_{p}\otimes_{L}V)\rightarrow\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{L}^{\mathrm{la}}.

Since this subspace is dense in
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{L}^{\mathrm{la}}
by
[
16
, Corollary 7.7]
, the two actions coincide.

To prove the claim we fix an isomorphism
\iota:\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\cong\mathbb{C}
. It follows from
[
16
, Lemma 9.18]
that there exists a cuspidal
automorphic representation
\pi_{y}=\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\pi_{y,v}
of
G(\mathbb{A}_{F})

such that
\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})
acts on

\pi_{y}^{U^{S}}
via
\iota\circ y
. Let

\rho_{y}:\Gal(E_{S}/E)\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})

be the Galois representation associated to
\pi_{y}
.
The local-global compatibility
between
\pi_{y}
and
\rho_{y}
is summarised in
[
19
, Theorem 7.2.1]
,
see the references in its proof for proper attributions. In particular, it follows from
(
64
) and
[
19
, Theorem 7.2.1 (iii)]

\det(1+t\rho_{y}(\mathrm{Frob}_{w}))=D_{y}(1+t\mathrm{Frob}_{w}),\quad\forall w\in\mathrm{Spl}_{E}^{S}.

Since the union of conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{Frob}_{w}
for
w\in\mathrm{Spl}_{E}^{S}
is dense in

\Gal(E_{S}/E)
we deduce that
D_{y}
is the
determinant law associated to
\rho_{y}
.
The representation
\rho_{y}
is de Rham
at all places
w\mid p
and the Hodge–Tate
weights can be expressed in terms of the
highest weight of
V
by
[
19
, Theorem 7.2.1 (ii)]
. Let
\rho^{C}_{y}:\Gal(E_{S}/E)\rightarrow{}^{C}\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
be
the representation
\mathrm{tw}_{\tilde{\delta}}^{-1}\circ(\rho_{y}\boxtimes\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}})
obtained via the twisting construction (
65
).
Then
\Theta^{C}_{y}
is
{}^{C}\GL_{n}
-pseudocharacter of
\rho^{C}_{y}
.
It follows from
[
16
, Proposition 5.5]
that
\zeta^{C}_{\rho^{C}_{y}}
is equal to the
infinitesimal character of
V
. It follows from the specialisation property in
Theorem

7.12
that
\zeta^{C}_{y}=\zeta^{C}_{\rho^{C}_{y}}
, which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

Let us emphasise that the definition
of
\zeta^{C}_{w}
in (
66
) is the only new thing in this section.
In
[
16
]
an analog of

Theorem

8.2
is
proved for
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}}
instead
of
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})
, under the assumption that
the specialisation of
\Theta
at
\mathfrak{m}
is absolutely irreducible.
This assumption implies the existence of Galois representation

\rho:\Gal(E_{S}/E)\rightarrow\GL_{n}(\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}})

such that its pseudocharacter is equal to
\Theta
. The authors
in
[
16
]
then use the twisting construction to
obtain a representation
\rho^{C}:\Gal(E_{S}/E)\rightarrow{}^{C}\GL_{n}(\mathbb{T}^{S}_{\mathrm{Spl}}(U^{p})_{\mathfrak{m}})
, and
work with the infinitesimal character
\zeta^{C}_{\rho^{C}}
instead of

\zeta^{C}
. Our construction of the infinitesimal character
\zeta^{C}
enables us to carry
out the same argument on the
whole of
\widetilde{H}^{0}(U^{p})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, not just the part, where the residual Galois representation is irreducible.

Large time decay of the Oseen flow in exterior domains subject to the Navier slip-with-friction boundary condition
Toshiaki Hishida

Abstract
Consider the motion of a viscous incompressible fluid filling a 3D exterior domain Ω \Omega subject to the Navier slip-with-friction boundary condition as well as outflow at infinity. For the Oseen system as the linearization, we discuss the resolvent set under a certain relationship among the geometry of the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega , friction coefficient α ⁡ ( x ) \alpha(x) and the outflow u ∞ u_{\infty} . We then study the regularity of the resolvent near the origin in the complex plane to develop L q L^{q} - L r L^{r} decay estimates of the Oseen semigroup provided that α ⁡ ( x ) + u ∞ ⋅ ν ⁡ ( x ) / 2 ≥ 0 \alpha(x)+u_{\infty}\cdot\nu(x)/2\geq 0 for every x ∈ ∂ Ω x\in\partial\Omega , where ν ⁡ ( x ) \nu(x) stands for the outward unit normal to the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . MSC: 35B40, 76D07 Keywords: Oseen flow, resolvent, L q L^{q} - L r L^{r} estimate, exterior domain, Navier boundary condition.



1
Introduction

Let us consider the Navier-Stokes flow in the exterior domain
\Omega
of an obstacle in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
of class
C^{2,1}
subject to the Navier slip-with-friction boundary condition,
while outflow
u_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
(constant vector) is prescribed at spatial infinity:

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u=\Delta u-\nabla p,\quad\mbox{div $u$}=0,

(1.1)

\nu\cdot u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad[(2\mathbb{D}u)\nu]_{\tau}+\alpha u_{\tau}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=u_{\infty},

(1.2)

where
u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
are the velocity and pressure of the fluid,

\mathbb{D}u=\frac{\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}}{2}

is the symmetric gradient (deformation tensor) with
(\cdot)^{\top}
being the transpose,

\nu
stands for the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
and

\alpha(x)\geq 0
denotes the friction coefficient at
x\in\partial\Omega
.
Given vector field
W
at the boundary
\partial\Omega
, here and in what follows, its
tangential component
W_{\tau}
is
defined by

W_{\tau}=W-(\nu\cdot W)\nu=(\nu\times W)\times\nu.

The condition
\mbox{\eqref{BC}}_{2}
arises from

[\mathbb{T}(u,p)\nu]_{\tau}+\alpha u_{\tau}|_{\partial\Omega}=0

(1.3)

with
\mathbb{T}(u,p)=2\mathbb{D}u-p\mathbb{I}
being the Cauchy stress tensor, where
\mathbb{I}
is
the unity matrix, nevertheless, it does not involve the pressure actually.
When
\alpha>0
, we see from (
1.3
) that
the tangential component of the volocity is proportional to the one of the normal stress exerted by the fluid
with the proportional coefficient
1/\alpha
, which is called the slip length.
Since
(\nu\times(2\mathbb{D}u)\nu)\cdot\nu+\alpha(\nu\times u)\cdot\nu=0
,
the condition
\mbox{\eqref{BC}}_{1,2}
is equivalent to

\nu\cdot u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\nu\times(2\mathbb{D}u)\nu+\alpha\nu\times u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

It is remarkable that the condition
\mbox{\eqref{BC}}_{1,2}
can be reformulated in terms of the vorticity
at
\partial\Omega
unlike the no-slip condition
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0

which is widely adopted, see Chen and Qian
[
5
]
for the details.
The particular case
\alpha=0
is the full slip condition, whereas the no-slip one
is recovered formally when
\alpha\to\infty
.

There are some regimes in which the Navier condition would be suitable rather than the no-slip one.
One of them is related to
the occurrence of collision in a finite time of rigid bodies moving into a fluid within the context of fluid-structure interaction,
which is somehow impossible under the no-slip condition
[
17
]

in contrast to the case of the Navier one
[
15
]
.
Staring from the work
[
41
]
by Solonnikov and Scadilov,
the Stokes and Navier-Stokes systems subject to the Navier boundary condition are
extensively studied from several
points of view, see, for instance,

[
1
,
2
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
28
,
29
,
36
,
38
,
39
,
42
]

and the references cited therein, however, to the best of our knowledge,
the outflow
u_{\infty}
is assumed to be zero for the exterior problem in the existing literature.

If we replace
u
by
u_{\infty}+u
in (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
to rewrite the system around
u_{\infty}
,
eliminate the nonlinear term, replace the resulting inhomogeneous boundary conditon by
the homogeneous one,
set
\eta=-u_{\infty}
and finally add the initial condition,
then we are led to the Oseen initial value problem

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+\eta\cdot\nabla u-\nabla p,\quad\mbox{div $u$}=0\quad\mbox{in $\Omega\times(0,\infty)$},\quad u(\cdot,0)=f,

(1.4)

subject to

\nu\cdot u|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad[(2\mathbb{D}u)\nu]_{\tau}+\alpha u_{\tau}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u=0.

(1.5)

The resolvent system associated with (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) is given by

\lambda u-\Delta u-\eta\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=f,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0,

(1.6)

subject to (
1.5
).

The objective of the present paper is twofold.
Firstly, we discuss the resolvent set within the
L^{q}
-framework under the relationship (
1.8
) below among
the geometry of
\Omega
, the friction
\alpha
and
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
In view of the symbol of the solution to (
1.6
) in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, see (
3.5
),
one would expect that the resolvent set contains
\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}
with

S_{\eta}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};\;|\eta|^{2}\mbox{Re $\lambda$}+(\mbox{Im $\lambda$})^{2}\leq 0\}.

(1.7)

Indeed, under the no-slip boundary condition, it was shown by Kobayashi and Shibata
[
30
]
that it is actually the case
for every
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
It is interesting to ask whether or not the same thing holds under the Navier
boundary condition.
In this paper, we will show that it is indeed true provided

\alpha(x)+\min\{\kappa_{0}(x),0\}\geq\frac{\eta\cdot\nu(x)}{2}

(1.8)

for every
x\in\partial\Omega
, where

\kappa_{0}(x)
is the minimum of two principal curvatures of the boundary
\partial\Omega

at
x
in the direction of
\nu(x)
.
A key step for the proof is to show
the uniqueness of solutions (within a reasonable class) to (
1.5
)–(
1.6
)
with
\lambda\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta})\cup\{0\}
under (
1.8
).

The second objective is to deduce
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
estimates of the solution, given by the Oseen semigroup,
to (
1.4
)–(
1.5
).
As is well known, such decay estimates are useful to establish the global well-posedness of the Navier-Stokes
system (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with initial data close to a steady state
in connection with its stability.
If, in particular, the boundary condition is the full slip one
\alpha=0
, then
it turns out that
the only case when the desired large time decay is
available is the Stokes system
\eta=0
, for which the result was already shown by Shimada and Yamaguchi
[
39
]

(their argument works for general case
\alpha\geq 0
as well, see
[
39
, Remark 1.4]
).
On the other side in which
\alpha\to\infty
formally, large time decay of the Oseen semigroup subject
to the no-slip condition was well developed by Kobayashi and Shibata
[
30
]
.
Our result may be regarded as
a contribution that fills in between and recovers the result of
[
39
]
for the Stokes semigroup as a particular case.
To be precise,
the desired
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
estimates, in which the restriction on the summability exponents is the same
as in the no-slip case
[
23
,
24
,
27
,
30
,
32
,
35
]
in 3D, are established in this paper under less condition

\alpha(x)\geq\frac{\eta\cdot\nu(x)}{2}

(1.9)

for every
x\in\partial\Omega
than (
1.8
).
The condition (
1.9
) allows us to show the uniqueness of solutions to (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) for every
\lambda

that lies in the right-half complex plane including the imaginary axis.
In fact, analysis of the resolvent for such
\lambda
,
especially near
\lambda=0
along the imaginary axis,
is enough as well as crucial for the large time behavior although the
aforementioned result on the resolvent set (
\supset\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}
)
under (
1.8
) is of independent interest.

The proof is based on the local energy decay properties together with a cut-off procedure as in

[
8
,
9
,
18
,
20
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
31
,
35
,
39
]
; indeed, under the no-slip condition, this strategy is
traced back to Kobayashi and Shibata
[
30
]

for the Oseen system, that covers the Stokes one as well,
and, even before, to Iwashita
[
27
]
for the Stokes system.
Once we have the local energy decay estimate, on which we focus in this paper, then the subsequent procedure leads to the result
along the same argument as in the papers above (and thus may be omitted).
It is worth while mentioning that there is
another proof due to
Maremonti and Solonnikov
[
32
]
for the Stokes semigroup subject to the no-slip condition.
In
[
22
,
23
]
the present author developed an alternative route
without spectral analysis to show the local energy decay, see
[
23
, Proposition 6.1]
,
for the non-autonomous system subject to the no-slip condition, and the subsequent procedure
involves a nontrivial issue on the regularity
of
\partial_{t}u
as well as the pressure (which corresponds to
[
36
, Theorem 1.4]

under the Navier boundary condition)
because of presence of rotation of the obstacle as well as the non-autonomous charachter.
For the Oseen semigroup under consideration, one
can get around such a regularity issue by making use of analyticity of the semigroup, see the references mentioned above.

The paper is organized as follows.
In the next section we present main results:
Theorem
2.1
on the resolvent set, Theorem
2.2
on the

-

estimates.
After preparatory results in section

, section

In section

.
Theorem
2.1
is rephrased in Proposition

and the proof is given there.
The local energy decay estimate is proved in the final section, which leads us to Theorem
2.2
.

2
Results

Let us begin with introducing notation.
Given a domain
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
q\in[1,\infty]
and integer
k\geq 0
, the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces
are denoted by
L^{q}(G)
and by
W^{k,q}(G)
.
We abbreviate the norm
\|\cdot\|_{q,G}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{q}(G)}
and even
\|\cdot\|_{q}=\|\cdot\|_{q,\Omega}
, where

\Omega
is the exterior domain under consideration with
C^{2,1}
-boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Without loss, we assume that

\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega\subset B_{1}

where
B_{R}
denotes the open ball centered at the origin with
radius
R>0
.
We set
\Omega_{R}=\Omega\cap B_{R}
for
R\geq 1
.
The class
C_{0}^{\infty}(G)
consists of all
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
G
, then
W^{k,q}_{0}(G)

denotes the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(G)
in
W^{k,q}(G)
, where
k>0
is an integer.
In what follows we adopt the same symbols for denoting scalar and vector (even tensor) function spaces
as long as there is no confusion.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Banach spaces.
Then
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
stands for the Banach space consisting of all bounded linear operators from
X
into
Y
.
We simply write
{\mathcal{L}}(X)={\mathcal{L}}(X,X)
.

We introduce the solenoidal function spaces over the exterior domain
\Omega
with
C^{2,1}
-boundary
\partial\Omega
.
The class of
C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
consists of all solenoidal vector fields being in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
By
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
we denote the completion of
C_{0,\sigma}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
L^{q}(\Omega)
, then it is characterized as

L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)=\big\{u\in L^{q}(\Omega);\;\mbox{\rm div $u$}=0,\;\nu\cdot u|_{\partial\Omega}=0\big\}.

The space of
L^{q}
-vector fields admits the Helmholtz decomposition

L^{q}(\Omega)=L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus\{\nabla p\in L^{q}(\Omega);\;p\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\}

which was proved by
[
13
,
33
,
40
]
.
We denote by
P=P_{q}:L^{q}(\Omega)\to L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
the Fujita-Kato projection assosiated with the decomposition above.
Then we have
P_{q}^{*}=P_{q^{\prime}}
, where
1/q^{\prime}+1/q=1
.
Finally, several positive constants are denoted by
C
, which may change from line to line.

Let
1<q<\infty
, and
let us introduce the Stokes operator subject to the Navier
boundary condition by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}D(A)=\{u\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\cap W^{2,q}(\Omega);\;[(2\mathbb{D}u)\nu]_{\tau}+\alpha u_{\tau}=0\;\mbox{on $\partial\Omega$}\},\\
Au=-P\Delta u,\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha\in C(\partial\Omega)
is a given nonnegative function describing the friction at the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
If we denote by
A_{q}
the operator
A
acting on the space
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
,
we then see the duality relation
A_{q}^{*}=A_{q^{\prime}}
.
Due to Shibata and Shimada
[
36
, Theorem 1.3]
, the operator
-A
generates an analytic semigroup

\{e^{-tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
of class

(C_{0})
on the space
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)

for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.
To be precise, they showed the following as well as

\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]\subset\rho(-A)
:
For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})

and
\delta>0
,
there is a constant
C=C(\varepsilon,\delta)>0
such that

u=(\lambda+A)^{-1}f
and the associated pressure
p=p(\lambda)

enjoy

|\lambda|\|u\|_{q}+|\lambda|^{1/2}\|\nabla u\|_{q}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{q}+\|\nabla p\|_{q}\leq C\|f\|_{q}

(2.1)

for all
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon,\delta}
,
where

\Sigma_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};\;|\mbox{arg $\lambda$}|\leq\pi-\varepsilon,\,|\lambda|\geq\delta\}.

Note that (
2.1
) with
\lambda=1
implies

\|w\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega)}\leq c\big(\|Aw\|_{q}+\|w\|_{q}\big)

(2.2)

for every
w\in D(A)
with some
c>0
.
In addition,
e^{-tA}

is a bounded semigroup; in fact,
Shimada and Yamaguchi

[
39
]
succeeded in deducing even
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
decay estimate (
2.9
) below.
Although the full slip case
\alpha=0
was discussed in
[
39
]
, their argument works for general case
\alpha\geq 0
.
The result is recovered in Theorem
2.2
as a particular case.

Given a constant vector
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
the Oseen operator subject to the Navier
boundary condition is now defined by

D(L)=D(A),\qquad Lu=L_{\eta}u=-P[\Delta u+\eta\cdot\nabla u].

Then we immediately find from (
2.2
) together with the interpolation inequality that

\|w\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega)}\leq c\big(\|Lw\|_{q}+\|w\|_{q}\big)

(2.3)

for every
w\in D(L)
with some
c>0
.
Using (
2.1
) by means of the standard perturbation argument in terms of the Neumann series,
we readily see that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, there are constants
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(\varepsilon)>0

and
C=C(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\Sigma_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{0}}\subset\rho(-L)

(2.4)

together with

\|(\lambda+L)^{-1}f\|_{q}\leq\frac{C}{|\lambda|}\|f\|_{q}

for all
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon,\lambda_{0}}
, which implies that the operator
-L

generates an analytic semigroup
\{e^{-tL}\}_{t\geq 0}

on
L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
for every
q\in(1,\infty)
.

What interests us first of all is whether the spectral parameter
\lambda
near the origin belongs to the resolvent set
\rho(-L)
.
Indeed, under the no-slip condition, we know from Kobayashi and Shibata
[
30
, Theorem 4.4]

that
\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}\subset\rho(-L)

for every
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
, where
S_{\eta}
is given by (
1.7
).
There is further information for the no-slip case due to Farwig and Neustupa
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
:

S_{\eta}
is exactly the essential spectrum
for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.
However, it does not seem to be always the case under the Navier boundary condition.
In fact, in the following theorem, one needs the condition (
2.5
) or even (
2.7
) in order that
the desired result is available.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that a constant vector
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}

and a nonnegative function
\alpha\in C(\partial\Omega)
fulfill the relation

\alpha(x)\geq\frac{\eta\cdot\nu(x)}{2}

(2.5)

for every
x\in\partial\Omega
, where
\nu(x)
denotes the outward unit normal to
the boundary
\partial\Omega\in C^{2,1}
.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
, then we have

\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\setminus\{0\}\subset\rho(-L)

(2.6)

in addition to (
2.4
),
where
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};\;\mbox{\rm Re $\lambda$}\geq 0\}
.

For
x\in\partial\Omega
, let
\kappa(x)\leq 0
be the least eigenvalue of the Weingarten map
-\nabla N
of
\partial\Omega

in the direction of
\nu
, where

\kappa(x)
is the minimum of two
principal curvatures of the boundary
\partial\Omega
at
x
in the direction of
\nu(x)
if either of them is negative,
while
\kappa(x)=0
if both of them are nonnegative,
see subsection
3.1
.
Suppose, in addition, that
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha\in C(\partial\Omega)
fulfill the relation

\alpha(x)+\kappa(x)\geq\frac{\eta\cdot\nu(x)}{2}

(2.7)

for every
x\in\partial\Omega
.
Then

\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}\subset\rho(-L)

(2.8)

holds true, where
S_{\eta}
is given by (
1.7
).

Theorem
2.1
will be rephrased in Proposition
5.3
and the proof will be given there.

We turn to the
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
estimates of the Oseen semigroup
e^{-tL}
.
Since the
L^{q}
-
L^{r}
smoothing rate near
t=0
is obvious by using

\|Le^{-tL}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega))}\leq Ct^{-1}
for
t\in(0,1)
together with (
2.3
) and interpolation inequalities,
the issue here is the rate of decay for
t\to\infty
.
This must be closely related to the picture of the resolvent set near
\lambda=0
discussed in Theorem
2.1
,
nevertheless, the desired situation (
2.8
) is not necessary for our aim.
Indeed, with (
2.6
) at hand
under the assumption (
2.5
), one can proceed to analysis of the large time behavior.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose that a constant vector
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
a nonnegative function
\alpha\in C(\partial\Omega)
fulfill the relation (
2.5
)
for every
x\in\partial\Omega
.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q\leq r\leq\infty&\mbox{for $j=0$},\\
q\leq r<\infty&\mbox{for $j=1$}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
for every
m>0
, there is a constant
C=C(m,\alpha,q,r,\Omega)>0
such that

\|\nabla^{j}e^{-tL}f\|_{r}\leq Ct^{-\frac{j}{2}-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r})}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|f\|_{q}&\mbox{for $j=0$},\\
(1+t)^{\max\{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2r},\,0\}}\|f\|_{q}&\mbox{for $j=1$},\end{array}\right.

(2.9)

for all
t>0
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\eta|\leq m
, and
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
.

Remark 2.1
.

Theorem
2.2
recovers the result of
[
39
]
for the Stokes semigroup and the constant
C
in (
2.9
) can be
taken uniformly when
\eta\to 0
.
On the other hand, the dependence of this constant
C
on the friction
\alpha
is not clear
because of lack of
information about
such dependence in the a priori estimate
for the Stokes system in bounded domains subject to the Navier
boundary condition in spite of efforts by
[
1
]
, see Remark
4.1
.
One may expect the uniformity for large
\alpha
since the same result as in (
2.9
)
holds true for the no-slip case
[
30
]
.

Remark 2.2
.

For the Stokes case
\eta=0
subject to the no-slip condition, it is known from
[
19
,
32
]
that
the rate of decay (
2.9
) for
\nabla e^{-tA}
is best possible.
This should be also the case under the Navier boundary condition as long as there exists a steady Stokes flow
with the forcing term
div
F

subject to this boundary condition for which the total net force

\int_{\partial\Omega}\big(\mathbb{T}(u,p)+F\big)\nu\,d\sigma

does not vanish.
This is because the coefficient of the leading term of the asymptotic representation at infinity of the Stokes flow is given by the net force
regardless of the boundary condition, and because the optimal spatial decay of the steady Stokes flow
is closely related to the optimal temporal decay of
\nabla e^{-tA}
.
From this point of view, as pointed out by
[
19
, Section 5]
, one can not claim the optimality of (
2.9
) for the Oseen case

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
since the steady Oseen flow possesses better spatial decay with wake structure.

3
Preparatory results

3.1
Weingarten map

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be an orientable submanifold of class
C^{2}
of codimension one in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with unit normal

\nu=\nu(x)
,
x\in{\mathcal{S}}
.
In this subsection, following Duduchava, D. Mitrea and M. Mitrea
[
7
, Section 3]
,
we briefly introduce an extended unit field
and the Weingarten map of
{\mathcal{S}}
.

A vector field
N\in C^{1}({\mathcal{U}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
{\mathcal{U}}
being a neighborhood of
{\mathcal{S}}
is called an extended unit field for

{\mathcal{S}}
if
N
satisfies

N|_{\mathcal{S}}=\nu,\qquad|N|=1,\qquad(\nabla N)N=0\;\;\mbox{on ${\mathcal{S}}$}.

(3.1)

According to the proof of
[
7
, Proposition 3.1]
, there exists actually an extended unit field for
{\mathcal{S}}

although it is not unique.
Let us fix an extended unit field
N
.
Then, in addition to (
3.1
), the following properties hold true, see
[
7
, Proposition 3.4]
,
where
-\nabla N
is called the Weingarten map of
{\mathcal{S}}
in the direction of
\nu
:

(i)

(\nabla N)^{\top}N=0
in
{\mathcal{U}}
.
Thus,
(\nabla N)u|_{\mathcal{S}}
is tangential to
{\mathcal{S}}
for
any vector field
u:{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

(ii)

(\nabla N)|_{\mathcal{S}}
is independent of the choice of

N
and depends only on
{\mathcal{S}}
.

(iii)

(\nabla N)^{\top}=\nabla N
on
{\mathcal{S}}
.

(iv)
The eigenvalues of
-\nabla N
at

x\in{\mathcal{S}}

consist of, besides zero,
two principal curvatures of
{\mathcal{S}}
in the direction of
\nu(x)
.

For later use, we show the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}\in C^{2}
be as above with unit normal
\nu
and

N\in C^{1}({\mathcal{U}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
an extended unit
field for
{\mathcal{S}}
,
where
{\mathcal{U}}
is a neighborhood of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Suppose that a vector field

u\in C^{1}({\mathcal{U}};\mathbb{R}^{3})
is tangential to
{\mathcal{S}}
, that is,
\nu\cdot u|_{\mathcal{S}}=0
.
Then we have

[(\nabla u)^{\top}\nu]_{\tau}=-[(\nabla N)u]_{\tau}=-(\nabla N)u\qquad\mbox{on ${\mathcal{S}}$}.

(3.2)

Let
\Omega
be an exterior domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
C^{2}
-boundary
{\mathcal{S}}=\partial\Omega
and

\nu
the outward unit normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Fix an extended unit field
N\in C^{1}({\mathcal{U}};\mathbb{R}^{3})

as above, where the neighborhood
{\mathcal{U}}
of
\partial\Omega
may be assumed to be bounded.
Then, for every vector field
u\in H^{2}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\nu\cdot u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
, we have
(
3.2
) in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
, where
\nabla u
as well as
u
is understood in the sense of trace.

Proof.

We have

[N\times\nabla(N\cdot u)]\times N\\
=[N\times\{(\nabla N)^{\top}u\}]\times N+[N\times\{(\nabla u)^{\top}N\}]\times N

(3.3)

in
{\mathcal{U}}
.
Since
\nu\cdot u|_{\mathcal{S}}=0
,
we see that the tangential derivative of
N\cdot u
along
{\mathcal{S}}
vanishes, that is,

N\times\nabla(N\cdot u)=0
on
{\mathcal{S}}
.
Hence, (
3.3
) leads to

[(\nabla N)^{\top}u]_{\tau}+[(\nabla u)^{\top}N]_{\tau}=0

on
{\mathcal{S}}
,
which combined with (iii) above implies the first equality of (
3.2
).
Successively, the second equality follows from (i).

As for the latter part, we observe (
3.3
) in
H^{1}(\Omega\cap{\mathcal{U}})
and, therefore, in

L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
as well.
We thus obtain (
3.2
) in
L^{2}(\partial\Omega)
.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.2
Oseen resolvent in the whole space

Let us summarize useful regularity properties near
\lambda=0
of the Oseen resolvent in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}

due to Kobayashi and Shibata
[
30
, Section 3]
.
See this literature for
the proof of several estimates
in this subsection.

It is enough to consider

W^{2,q}
-estimate over a bounded domain, say,
B_{3}

of the solution to
the resolvent system

\lambda u-\Delta u-\eta\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=f,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0\qquad\mbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$}

(3.4)

for the forcing term
f
taken from the space

L^{q}_{[R]}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\{f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3});\;f(x)=0\;\mbox{a.e. $\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B_{R}$}\},

where
R\geq 2
.
In terms of the Fourier multiplier operator, the solution is described as

u(\cdot,\lambda)=E_{\eta}(\lambda)f:={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}\left[\frac{|\xi|^{2}\mathbb{I}-\xi\otimes\xi}{(\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-i\eta\cdot\xi)|\xi|^{2}}\right]{\mathscr{F}}f

(3.5)

p=\Pi f:={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}\left[\frac{-i\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}\right]{\mathscr{F}}f

(3.6)

where
\mathscr{F}
and
{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}
stand for the Fourier transform and its inversion, respectively.
Note that the formula (
3.5
) makes sense for both
\lambda=0
and
\eta=0
as well.
In view of the symbol of (
3.5
), when
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
(resp.
\eta=0
),
we see that
\lambda
belongs to
\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}

(resp.
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
) if and only if

\lambda+|\xi|^{2}-i\eta\cdot\xi\neq 0\qquad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

where
S_{\eta}
is given by (
1.7
).
This implies that the spectrum for the whole space problem is contained in
S_{\eta}
when
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
By the Fourier multiplier theorem we find (
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]
)

E_{\eta}(\lambda)\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}&\mbox{for $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$},\\
\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]&\mbox{for $\eta=0$},\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

\nabla\Pi\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

(3.8)

The following boundedness covers the case
\lambda=0
as well, for which see Galdi
[
14
, IV.2, VII.4]
:

\begin{split}\nabla^{2}E_{\eta}(\lambda)f\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),&\qquad E_{\eta}(\lambda)\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{1,q}(B_{\rho}))\\
&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\forall\,\lambda\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta})\cup\{0\}&\mbox{for $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$},\\
\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)&\mbox{for $\eta=0$},\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(3.9)

for every
\rho>0
.

For the objective of this paper, estimates on the imaginary axis are particularly important.
Let
1<q<\infty
and set
{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}(\tau):=\partial_{\tau}E_{\eta}(i\tau)
for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
All estimates below
are deduced from the representation (
3.5
):
For every
m>0
, there is a constant
C=C(m,q,R)>0
such that

\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\|E_{\eta}(i\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{2,q}(B_{3}))}\leq C

(3.10)

\int_{-4}^{4}\|{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}(\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{2,q}(B_{3}))}\,d\tau\leq C

(3.11)

\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\|E_{\eta}(i(\tau+h))-E_{\eta}(i\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{2,q}(B_{3}))}\leq C|h|^{1/2}

(3.12)

\int_{-2}^{2}\|{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}(\tau+h)-{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}(\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{2,q}(B_{3}))}\,d\tau\leq C|h|^{1/2}

(3.13)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\eta|\leq m
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
with
|h|\leq 1
(
[
30
, Lemma 3.5]
).

As for the continuity with respect to
(\lambda,\eta)
,
we have the following:
For every
m>0
,
compact set
K\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}

and
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there is a constant
C=C(m,K,\theta,q,R)>0
such that

\|E_{\eta}(\lambda)-E_{\eta^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),W^{2,q}(B_{3}))}\leq C\big(|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|+|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|\big)^{\theta}

(3.14)

for all
(\lambda,\eta),\,(\lambda^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in K\times\overline{B_{m}}
(
[
30
, Lemmas 3.3, 3.4]
).

4
Interior problem

Let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
C^{2,1}
-boundary
\partial D
.
This section studies the interior problem for the Oseen resolvent system subject to the Navier
boundary condition

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda u-\Delta u-\eta\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=f,\quad\mbox{div $u$}=0&\mbox{in $D$},\\
\nu\cdot u=0,\qquad[2\mathbb{D}(u)\nu]_{\tau}+\alpha u_{\tau}=0&\mbox{on $\partial D$},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

We fix a subdomain
D_{0}\subset D
with
|D_{0}|>0
and single out a solution to (
4.1
) in such a way that

\int_{D_{0}}p(x)\,dx=0.

(4.2)

We begin with uniqueness of solutions to (
4.1
), see Propostion
4.1
below, where we take
into account the following fact
found in, for instance,
[
1
,
29
,
42
]
; especially, the proof is given by
[
29
]
in detail.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
29
, Proposition 7.2]
)
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain as above.
Then the space

\{u\in H^{1}(D);\;\mathbb{D}u=0,\,\nu\cdot u|_{\partial D}=0\}

is nontrivial if and only if
D
axisymmetric about an axis

s\mapsto a+sb,\,s\in\mathbb{R}
,
with
some
b\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that a constant vector
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and a nonnegative function
\alpha\in C(\partial D)
fulfill the relation

\alpha(x)\geq\frac{\eta\cdot\nu(x)}{2}

(4.3)

for every
x\in\partial D
, where
\nu(x)
denotes the outward unit normal to the boundary
\partial D\in C^{2,1}
.
If, in particular,
\alpha\equiv 0
, it is additionally assumed that
D
is not axisymmetric about any axis.
Let
q\in(1,\infty)
and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}&\mbox{for $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$},\\
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)&\mbox{for $\eta=0$}.\end{array}

Then the only solution
(u,p)\in W^{2,q}(D)\times W^{1,q}(D)
to (
4.1
)–(
4.2
)
with
f=0
is
(u,p)=(0,0)
.

For
x\in\partial D
, let

\kappa(x)\leq 0
be the least eigenvalue of the Weingarten map
-\nabla N

of
\partial D
in the direction of
\nu

(see subsection
3.1
).
Suppose in addition that
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha\in C(\partial D)
fulfill the relation

\alpha(x)+\kappa(x)\geq\frac{\eta\cdot\nu(x)}{2}

(4.4)

for every
x\in\partial D
.
Let
\lambda\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta})\cup\{0\}
with

S_{\eta}
being given by (
1.7
).
Then the same uniqueness assertion above holds true.

Proof.

First of all, we observe
u\in H^{2}(D)
and
p\in H^{1}(D)
(even though
q
is close to
1
) by bootstrap argument with the aid of the
regularity theory for the Stokes system subject to the Navier
boundary condition
[
1
,
2
,
36
]
; indeed, less condition on the friction
\alpha(x)
than ours is imposed in
[
1
]
.
We multiply the equation by
\overline{u}
, integrate and use the boundary
condition to get

\lambda\|u\|_{2,D}^{2}+2\|\mathbb{D}u\|_{2,D}^{2}+\int_{\partial D}\alpha|u|^{2}\,d\sigma-\int_{D}(\eta\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\overline{u}\,dx=0.

(4.5)

In fact, the third term arises from

\int_{\partial D}[\mathbb{T}(u,p)\nu]\cdot\overline{u}\,d\sigma=\int_{\partial D}[\mathbb{T}(u,p)\nu]_{\tau}\cdot\overline{u}_{\tau}\,d\sigma=-\int_{\partial D}\alpha|u_{\tau}|^{2}\,d\sigma=-\int_{\partial D}\alpha|u|^{2}\,d\sigma

by taking into account that the boundary condition in (
4.1
) comes from (
1.3
).
Since

\int_{D}[(\eta\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\overline{u}+u\cdot(\eta\cdot\nabla\overline{u})]\,d\sigma=\int_{D}\mbox{div $(\eta|u|^{2})$}\,dx=\int_{\partial D}\eta\cdot\nu|u|^{2}\,d\sigma,

the real and imaginary parts respectively give

(\mbox{Re $\lambda$})\|u\|_{2,D}^{2}+2\|\mathbb{D}u\|_{2,D}^{2}+\int_{\partial D}\left(\alpha-\frac{\eta\cdot\nu}{2}\right)|u|^{2}\,d\sigma=0,

(4.6)

(\mbox{Im $\lambda$})\|u\|_{2,D}^{2}-\mbox{Im}\int_{D}(\eta\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\overline{u}\,dx=0.

(4.7)

We immediately see from (
4.3
) that
u=0
if
\mbox{Re $\lambda$}>0
.
When
\mbox{Re $\lambda$}=0
, we have
\mathbb{D}u=0
.
Then the rigid motion satisfying
\nu\cdot u=0
as well as
\alpha\nu\times u=0

at
\partial D
should be
u=0
unless
\alpha
is identically zero.
When
\alpha\equiv 0
, due to Lemma
4.1
,
the only case in which a nontrivial rigid motion
u
with
\nu\cdot u=0
at
\partial D

is available is that
D
is axisymmetric about an axis.
This case is ruled out by the assumption.
In this way, we are led to
u=0
for
\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
, and thereby
\nabla p=0
, yielding
p=0
by (
4.2
).
If, in particular,
\eta=0
, then (
4.7
) implies
u=0
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
as well.

We next consider the case under further condition (
4.4
) when
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
Since
\mbox{div $u$}=0
, we have
\Delta u=\mbox{div $(2\mathbb{D}u)$}
, that together with
\nu\cdot u=0
at
\partial D
leads to

\begin{split}2\|\mathbb{D}u\|_{2,D}^{2}&=\|\nabla u\|_{2,D}^{2}+\int_{\partial D}[(\nabla u)^{\top}\nu]\cdot\overline{u}\,d\sigma\\
&=\|\nabla u\|_{2,D}^{2}-\int_{\partial D}[(\nabla N)u]\cdot\overline{u}\,d\sigma\end{split}

(4.8)

by Lemma
3.1
, which implies that

2\|\mathbb{D}u\|_{2,D}^{2}\geq\|\nabla u\|_{2,D}^{2}+\int_{\partial D}\kappa|u|^{2}\,d\sigma.

(4.9)

Combining (
4.6
) with (
4.9
) gives

(\mbox{Re $\lambda$})\|u\|_{2,D}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{2,D}^{2}+\int_{\partial D}\left(\alpha+\kappa-\frac{\eta\cdot\nu}{2}\right)|u|^{2}\,d\sigma\leq 0.

(4.10)

By (
4.4
) we at once find that
u=0
when
\mbox{Re $\lambda$}\geq 0
.
Consider the case
\mbox{Re $\lambda$}<0
by using (
4.7
).
Then we have

(\mbox{Im $\lambda$})^{2}\|u\|_{2,D}^{4}\leq|\eta|^{2}\|\nabla u\|_{2,D}^{2}\|u\|_{2,D}^{2}\leq-|\eta|^{2}(\mbox{Re $\lambda$})\,\|u\|^{4}_{2,D},

from which we find that
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}
leads to
u=0
.
The proof is complete.
∎

The following proposition provides a solution operator to (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) along with regularity properties.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under the same assumptions of the first half of Proposition
4.1
, let

\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}&\mbox{for $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}$},\\
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0)&\mbox{for $\eta=0$}.\end{array}

Then there exist bounded operators
M_{\eta}(\lambda)
and
N_{\eta}(\lambda)
from
L^{q}(D)
into

W^{2,q}(D)
and
W^{1,q}(D)
, respectively,
such that the pair
\big(M_{\eta}(\lambda)f,N_{\eta}(\lambda)f\big)
gives a unique solution
of (
4.1
) subject to (
4.2
) for all
f\in L^{q}(D)
, and it is analytic in a certain open neighborhood of

\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
(resp.
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,-\rho]
for some
\rho>0
) when
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}

(resp.
\eta=0
).

Instead, under the same assumptions of the second half of Proposition
4.1
, let

\lambda\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta})\cup\{0\}
.
Then the same conclusion as above holds true, and
\big(M_{\eta}(\lambda)f,N_{\eta}(\lambda)f\big)
is analytic in

(\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta})\cup\{|\lambda|<\rho\}
for some
\rho>0
.

Moreover, given
m>0
and compact set
K
satisfying

\begin{array}[]{ll}K\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}&\mbox{under \eqref{ass-fric2}},\\
K\subset\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};\;m^{2}\mbox{\rm Re $\lambda$}+(\mbox{\rm Im $\lambda$})^{2}>0\}\cup\{0\}&\mbox{under \eqref{ass-fric3}},\end{array}

(4.11)

the solution enjoys the following properties.

1.

For every integer
j\geq 0
, there is a constant
C=C(j,m,K,\alpha,q,D)>0

such that

\|\partial^{j}_{\lambda}M_{\eta}(\lambda)f\|_{W^{2,q}(D)}+\|\partial^{j}_{\lambda}N_{\eta}(\lambda)f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)}\leq C\|f\|_{q,D}

(4.12)

for all
(\lambda,\eta)\in K\times\overline{B_{m}}
and
f\in L^{q}(D)
.

2.

There is a constant
C=C(m,K,\alpha,q,D)>0
such that

\|M_{\eta}(\lambda)f-M_{\eta^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})f\|_{W^{2,q}(D)}+\|N_{\eta}(\lambda)f-N_{\eta^{\prime}}(\lambda^{\prime})f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)}\leq C\big(|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|+|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|\big)\|f\|_{q}

(4.13)

for all
(\lambda,\eta),\,(\lambda^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})\in K\times\overline{B_{m}}
and
f\in L^{q}(D)
.

Proof.

For the Stokes system (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) with
(\lambda,\eta)=(0,0)
,
it follows from

[
1
,
2
,
36
]
that
there is a unique solution

u=M_{0}(0)f,\qquad p=N_{0}(0)f

such that

\|M_{0}(0)f\|_{W^{2,q}(D)}+\|N_{0}(0)f\|_{W^{1,q}(D)}\leq C\|f\|_{q,D}.

(4.14)

We then see that
M_{0}(0)
and
\nabla M_{0}(0)
are compact operators from
L^{q}(D)
into itself by the Rellich theorem.
Let us look for a solution to (
4.1
)–(
4.2
) of the form
(u,p)=(M_{0}(0)g,N_{0}(0)g)
with a suitable
g\in L^{q}(D)
:

\lambda u-\Delta u-\eta\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=g+\lambda M_{0}(0)g-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)g,\qquad\mbox{div $u$}=0

together with the boundary condition in (
4.1
).
Then the operator
1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)

is injective and, thereby,
invertible in
L^{q}(D)
by the Fredholm alternative.
In fact, let
g\in L^{q}(D)
satisfy
g+\lambda M_{0}(0)g-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)g=0
, then
the pair
(u,p)=(M_{0}(0)g,N_{0}(0)g)\in W^{2,q}(D)\times W^{1,q}(D)
must be the trivial one by Proposition
4.1
, yielding

g=-\Delta u+\nabla p=0
.
We thus find that the pair

\begin{split}&u=M_{\eta}(\lambda)f:=M_{0}(0)[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}f\in W^{2,q}(D)\\
&p=N_{\eta}(\lambda)f:=N_{0}(0)[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}f\in W^{1,q}(D)\end{split}

(4.15)

provides a unique solution to (
4.1
) for all

\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
(resp.
\lambda\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta})\cup\{0\}
)

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
f\in L^{q}(D)
for the first case (resp. second case).

In what follows, we will describe the proof for the first case under the condition (
4.3
).
We fix
m>0
arbitrarily.
Since
(\lambda,\eta)\mapsto 1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\nabla M_{0}(0)
is continuous from

\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\times\overline{B_{m}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))
,
so is
(\lambda,\eta)\mapsto[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}
.
In fact, if
(\lambda,\eta)
and
(\lambda^{\prime},\eta^{\prime})

satisfy

\begin{split}&|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|\|M_{0}(0)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}+|\eta^{\prime}-\eta|\|\nabla M_{0}(0)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}\\
&\qquad\leq\frac{1}{2\big\|[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\big\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}}\end{split}

as well as lie in
\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}\times\overline{B_{m}}
, then
we are led to the
Neumann series representation

\begin{split}&[1+\lambda^{\prime}M_{0}(0)-\eta^{\prime}\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\\
&=[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\\
&\quad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(-\big\{(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)M_{0}(0)-(\eta^{\prime}-\eta)\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)\big\}[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\Big)^{k}\end{split}

(4.16)

which implies the continuity of

(\lambda,\eta)\mapsto[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}
; to be precise,

\begin{split}&\quad\big\|[1+\lambda^{\prime}M_{0}(0)-\eta^{\prime}\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}-[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\big\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}\\
&\leq 2\big\|[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\big\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}\big(|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|\|M_{0}(0)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}+|\eta^{\prime}-\eta|\|\nabla M_{0}(0)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}\big).\end{split}

(4.17)

Hence, we have

\sup_{(\lambda,\eta)\in K\times\overline{B_{m}}}\big\|[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}\big\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))}<\infty

(4.18)

for each compact set
K\subset\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
, which combined with (
4.14
)
gives (
4.12
) with
j=0
in view of (
4.15
).
From (
4.17
) and (
4.18
) we immediately obtain (
4.13
).

Furthermore, we fix
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and set
\eta^{\prime}=\eta
in (
4.16
), that tells us that

\lambda\mapsto[1+\lambda M_{0}(0)-\eta\cdot\nabla M_{0}(0)]^{-1}
is analytic in a ceratin open neighborhood of

\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}
with values in
{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D))
and, therefore, so is

\lambda\mapsto(M_{\eta}(\lambda),N_{\eta}(\lambda))
with values in
{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}(D),W^{2,q}(D)\times W^{1,q}(D))
.

It remains to show (
4.12
) for every
j\geq 1
.
By taking the differentiation of (
4.1
), we see that

(\partial_{\lambda}M_{\eta}(\lambda)f,\partial_{\lambda}N_{\eta}(\lambda)f)\in W^{2,q}(D)\times W^{1,q}(D)

is a solution to (

Lemma 5.2

(\lambda+L)^{-1}Pf=R_{\eta}(\lambda)(1+T_{\eta}(\lambda))^{-1}f

(5.24)

for every

),
where

).

If the assumption (

) for

\mathbb{C}\setminus S_{\eta}\subset\rho(-L)

with

Proof.

It suffices to show (

5.24
) for

by Proposition

.
Let us take
\lambda

is injective.
Let

) with

Given

Set

v=(1-\phi)E_{\eta}(\lambda)f+\mathbb{B}[(E_{\eta}(\lambda)f)\cdot\nabla\phi],\qquad\sigma=(1-\phi)(\Pi f-\sigma_{0})

\begin{split}g&=\phi f-2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla E_{\eta}(\lambda)f-(\Delta\phi+\eta\cdot\nabla\phi)E_{\eta}(\lambda)f+\Delta\mathbb{B}[(E_{\eta}(\lambda)f)\cdot\nabla\phi]\\

Lemma 5.3

,

In view of (

The next proposition tells us the regularity of the resolvent along the imaginary axis with respect to the topology

{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega),\,W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3}))
and plays a key role.

Proposition 5.4
.

Under the same assumptions of the first half of Propisition
5.2
, let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
R\geq 2
.
We set

v(\tau):=\partial_{\tau}(i\tau+L)^{-1}Pf,\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

(5.27)

For every
m>0
, thers is a constant
C=C(m,\alpha,q,R,\Omega)>0
such that

\int_{-4}^{4}\|v(\tau)\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau\leq C\|f\|_{q}

(5.28)

\int_{-2}^{2}\|v(\tau+h)-v(\tau)\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau\leq C|h|^{1/2}\|f\|_{q}

(5.29)

for all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\eta|\leq m
,
f\in L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega)
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
with
|h|\leq 1
,
where
L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega)
is given by (
5.15
).

Proof.

In what follows
we make use of (
5.26
) with

K=\{\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}};\;|\lambda|\leq 4\}
.
The proof is based on the structure of the parametrix of the resolvent, see (
5.16
), (
5.21
) and (
5.24

Let us split
v(\tau)
into

v(\tau)=v_{1}(\tau)+v_{2}(\tau)

with

\begin{split}&v_{1}(\tau)=[\partial_{\tau}R_{\eta}(i\tau)](1+T_{\eta}(i\tau))^{-1}f,\\
&v_{2}(\tau)=-R_{\eta}(i\tau)(1+T_{\eta}(i\tau))^{-1}[\partial_{\tau}T_{\eta}(i\tau)](1+T_{\eta}(i\tau))^{-1}f.\end{split}

(5.30)

For simplicity of notation, we set

F(\tau)=\partial_{\tau}R_{\eta}(i\tau),\qquad G(\tau)=(1+T_{\eta}(i\tau))^{-1},\qquad H(\tau)=\partial_{\tau}T_{\eta}(i\tau).

The obvious equality

G(\tau+h)-G(\tau)=-G(\tau+h)\big[T_{\eta}(i(\tau+h))-T_{\eta}(i\tau)\big]G(\tau)

We now collect (

)–(

), (

), (
5.17
),
(

) and (
5.31
) to find that

\sup_{|\tau|\leq 2}\|R(i\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega),W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3}))}\leq C,

(5.32)

\int_{-4}^{4}\left(\|F(\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega),W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3}))}+\|H(\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega))}\right)\,d\tau\leq C,

(5.33)

yielding (
5.28
), and that

\sup_{|\tau|\leq 2}\left(\|R(i(\tau+h))-R(i\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega),W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3}))}+\|G(\tau+h)-G(\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega))}\right)\leq C|h|^{1/2},

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{-2}^{2}\|v_{1}(\tau+h)-v_{1}(\tau)\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau\\

6
Proof of Theorem
2.2

In this section we prove Theorem
2.2
.
To this end, the following elementary lemma is useful.
This provides us with the relation between the regularity of functions and the rate of decay of those (inverse) Fourier transform.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
20
, Lemma 7.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|
and
w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};X)
.
Then

V(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\tau t}w(\tau)\,d\tau

(6.1)

enjoys

\|V(t)\|\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|w\left(\tau+\frac{1}{t}\right)-w(\tau)\right\|\,d\tau

(6.2)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.

The idea of the proof of Lemma
6.1
is found first in
[
18
]
, however, the origin of this lemma
goes back to Shibata
[
34
]
.
Estimate of the form (
6.2
) would be easier to apply to various situations.

We observe
that (
5.28
) enables us to justify the formula

e^{-tL}Pf=\frac{-1}{2\pi it}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\tau t}\partial_{\tau}(i\tau+L)^{-1}Pf\,d\tau

(6.3)

in
W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})
as long as
f\in L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega)
when we start from the Dunford integral representation, perform
integration by parts and then move the path of integration to the imaginary axis.

Let us close the paper with
the following proposition on the
local energy decay estimate.
In fact, once we have that,
then we arrive at Theorem
2.2

along the same way as in the existing literature, see

[
27
,
30
,
9
,
18
,
20
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
31
,
35
,
39
]
,
by means of
well-established cut-off procedure which consists in two steps:
(i) estimate still in
\Omega_{3}
for general
f\in L^{q}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
;
(ii) estimate in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega_{3}
.
Since the procedure would be rather standard nowadays,
one may omit the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
For the Stokes semigroup (
\eta=0
), the following proposition was already proved
by Shimada and Yamaguchi
[
39
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Under the same assumptions of the first half of Proposition
5.2
, let
q\in(1,\infty)
and
R\geq 2
.
For every
m>0
, there is a constant
C=C(m,\alpha,q,R,\Omega)>0
such that

\|e^{-tL}Pf\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\leq Ct^{-3/2}\|f\|_{q}

(6.4)

for all
t\geq 1
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\eta|\leq m
and
f\in L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega)
which is given by (
5.15
).

Proof.

We take a cut-off function
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
\psi(\tau)=1
for
|\tau|\leq 1
and

\psi(\tau)=0
for
|\tau|\geq 2
.
Let us divide the integral (
6.3
) into

\frac{-1}{2\pi it}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\tau t}\psi(\tau)\partial_{\tau}(i\tau+L)^{-1}Pf\,d\tau

(6.5)

and the other part which decays like
t^{-2}
by integration by parts once more since

\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}(i\tau+L)^{-1}Pf\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega)}=2\|(i\tau+L)^{-3}Pf\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega)}\leq C|\tau|^{-2}\|f\|_{q}

for
|\tau|\geq 1
and
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
.
It thus suffices to deduce the decay rate
t^{-1/2}
of
V(t)
of the form (
6.1
) with

w(\tau)=\psi(\tau)v(\tau),\qquad v(\tau)=\partial_{\tau}(i\tau+L)^{-1}Pf.

Let
f\in L^{q}_{[R]}(\Omega)
.
From (
6.2
) in Lemma
6.1
with
X=W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})
we know

\begin{split}\|V(t)\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}&\leq C\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\psi\left(\tau+\frac{1}{t}\right)-\psi(\tau)\right|\left\|v\left(\tau+\frac{1}{t}\right)\right\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau\\
&\qquad+C\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(\tau)|\left\|v\left(\tau+\frac{1}{t}\right)-v(\tau)\right\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau=:I+J\end{split}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
If, in particular,
t\geq 1
, then we readily see from (
5.28
) and

|\psi(\tau+1/t)-\psi(\tau)|\leq C/t

that

I\leq\frac{C}{t}\int_{|\tau|\leq 3}\left\|v\left(\tau+\frac{1}{t}\right)\right\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau\leq Ct^{-1}\|f\|_{q}.

In addition, it follows from (
5.29
) that

J\leq C\int_{|\tau|\leq 2}\left\|v\left(\tau+\frac{1}{t}\right)-v(\tau)\right\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega_{3})}\,d\tau\leq Ct^{-1/2}\|f\|_{q}

which completes the proof of (
6.4
).
∎

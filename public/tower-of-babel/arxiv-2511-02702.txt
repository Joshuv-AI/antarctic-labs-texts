Revisited for existence proof of optimal solution in Bernoulli free boundary problem using an energy-gap cost functional Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: Fang Shen


Abstract
. Bernoulli free boundary problem is numerically solved via shape optimization that minimizes a cost functional subject to state problems constraints. In [ 1 ] , an energy-gap cost functional was formulated based on two auxiliary state problems, with existence of optimal solution attempted through continuity of state problems with respect to the domain. Nevertheless, there exists a corrigendum in Eq.(48) in [ 1 ] , where the boundedness of solution sequences for state problems with respect to the domain cannot be directly estimated via the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as Claimed . In this comment, we rectify this proof by Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality.

1.
Corrigendum

Firstly, we show the Bernoulli free boundary problem and its corresponding shape optimization problem.
Bernoulli free boundary problem is formulated as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

(Bernoulli free boundary problem):
Let
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{1}
be bounded and connected domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\overline{\Omega_{1}}\subset\Omega_{0}
,
and define
\Omega\coloneqq\Omega_{0}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}
possessing a moving boundary
\partial\Omega_{0}\coloneqq\Sigma
and a fixed boundary
\partial\Omega_{1}\coloneqq\Gamma
.
The exterior Bernoulli free boundary problem is considered as an overdetermined boundary value problem for a given positive constant
\lambda
,
which implies that finding
u\in H^{1}{\left(\Omega\right)}
and unknown external boundary
\Sigma
satisfy:

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=0&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
u=1&\text{on $\Gamma$,}\\
u=0&\text{on $\Sigma$,}\\
-\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u=\lambda&\text{on $\Sigma$,}\\
\end{cases}

(1)

where
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u\coloneqq\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}
and
\mathbf{n}
is unit normal vector directed into the complement of
\Omega
.

The existence of solution for Eq.(
1
) has been established by variational method in
[
2
]
.
Bernoulli free boundary problem is numerically solved via shape optimization that minimizes a cost functional subject to state problems constraints.
In
[
1
]
, an energy-gap cost functional was formulated based on two auxiliary state problems as follows:

Definition 1.2
.

(Shape Optimization):
The shape optimization requires to minimize the cost functional
\mathcal{J}\left(\Omega\right)
over admissible domains
\mathcal{O}_{ad}
.
The cost functional
\mathcal{J}
is formulated as:

\mathcal{J}\left(\Omega\right)=\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\left(u_{N}-u_{R}\right)\right|^{2}dx
,
where

\displaystyle\mbox{$u_{N},u_{R}$ is the solution of }~:

\displaystyle\text{$u_{N}\in H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)$}\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{N}=0&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
u_{N}=1&\text{on $\Gamma$,}\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u_{N}=\lambda&\text{on $\Sigma$,}\\
\end{cases}

(2a)

\displaystyle\mbox{$u_{N},u_{R}$ is the solution of }~:

\displaystyle\text{$u_{R}\in H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)$}\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{R}=0&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
u_{R}=1&\text{on $\Gamma$,}\\
\partial_{\mathbf{n}}u_{R}+\beta u_{R}=\lambda&\text{on $\Sigma$,}\\
\end{cases}

(2b)

where
\beta
is a positive constant.

The definition of admissible domains
\mathcal{O}_{ad}
can be found in references
[
1
,
3
]
.
The variational formulations of two auxiliary state problems are formulated the following lemma:

Lemma 1.3
.

(Variational Formulations):
The variational formulations of Eq.
2a
and Eq.
2b
can be expressed as follows:

\begin{cases}\text{find $w_{N}=u_{N}-u_{N_{0}}\in H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)$, such that}\\
a\left(w_{N},\phi\right)=\int_{\Sigma}\lambda\phi d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{N_{0}}\nabla\phi dx,\forall\phi\in H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right),\\
\end{cases}

(3)

\begin{cases}\text{find $w_{R}=u_{R}-u_{R_{0}}\in H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)$, such that}\\
a\left(w_{R},\phi\right)+\beta a_{\Sigma}\left(w_{R},\phi\right)=\int_{\Sigma}\lambda\phi d\sigma-\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{R_{0}}\nabla\phi dx,\forall\phi\in H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right),\\
\end{cases}

(4)

where
H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)=\left\{v\in H^{1}\left(\Omega\right):v|_{\Gamma}=0\right\}
equipped with a norm

\|v\|_{H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(5)

and
u_{N_{0}},u_{R_{0}}
are two fixed functions in
H^{1}\left(U\right)
such that
u_{N_{0}}=u_{R_{0}}=1
.

U
is a fixed, connected and bounded open subset such that
\forall\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{ad},\Omega\subset U
.

a\left(\cdot,\cdot\right),a_{\Sigma}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
are bilinear functionals on
H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\times H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
by these definitions:

a\left(w,v\right)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\nabla vdx,

(6)

a_{\Sigma}\left(w,v\right)=\int_{\Sigma}\gamma_{0}\left(w\right)\cdot\gamma_{0}\left(v\right)dx,

(7)

where
w,v\in H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
,
\gamma_{0}:H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\rightarrow H^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Omega\right)
.

The existence and uniqueness of solutions for two state problems(Eq.
3
,Eq.
4
) are verified by Lax-Milgram theorem.
To prove continuity of the state solutions
u_{N}
,
u_{R}
with respect to the domain
\Omega
,
[
1
]
defines the graph
\mathcal{F}
as:

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\left(\Omega,u_{N}\left(\Omega\right),u_{R}\left(\Omega\right)\right):\Omega\in\mathcal{O}_{ad},\text{and $u_{N},u_{R}$ satisfies Eq.\ref{con:Eq.3.1}, Eq.\ref{con:Eq.3.2} on $\Omega$.}\right\},

(8)

Subsequently, the shape optimization
1.2
is represented as:

\underset{\mathcal{F}}{\min}J\left(\Omega,u_{N}\left(\Omega\right),u_{R}\left(\Omega\right)\right).

(9)

The existence of optimal solution for the shape optimization problem Eq.
9
reduces to proving compactness of
\mathcal{F}
and lower semi-continuity of
\mathcal{J}
.
However, in
[
1
]
’s proof for the compactness of
\mathcal{F}
,
there exists a corrigendum in the boundedness for the sequence
{u_{R_{k}}}
(Eq.48 in
[
1
]
).
Specifically, erroneous step of Eq.(48) in
[
1
]
:

Let
\phi=u_{R_{k}}\in H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)
, then variational formulation Eq.
3
is:

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{R_{k}}\cdot\nabla u_{R_{k}}dx=-\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{R_{0}}\cdot\nabla u_{R_{k}}dx-\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\beta\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma+\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\lambda u_{R_{k}}d\sigma,

(10)

where
\beta,\lambda>0
, and solution sequence
u_{R_{k}}
was constructed in
[
1
]
for verifying the compactness of
\mathcal{J}
based on weak convergence of solution sequences in
H^{1}\left(U\right)
.
Then,
[
1
]
uses the Cauchy-Buniakowsky-Schwarz inequality to estimate this formula:

\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}^{2}\leq\|u_{R_{0}}\|_{H^{1}\left(U\right)}\cdot\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}+\boxed{\max\left\{\beta,\lambda\left|U\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\cdot\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma_{k}\right)}}.

(11)

Incorrectness:

\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\beta\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma=\beta\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma_{k}\right)}^{2},

(12)

\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\lambda\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|d\sigma\leq\lambda\left(\int_{\Sigma_{k}}1^{2}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda\left|U\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma_{k}\right)},

(13)

However, the sum of Eq.
12
and Eq.
13
is not equal to the boxed estimation in Eq.
11
,
as this boxed estimation neglects the squared term in Eq.
12
.
This incorrectness is critical as the following derivation in
[
1
]
determines the subsequent compactness proof of
\mathcal{F}
.
We rectify it through estimation via the Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality,
which aligns with the coercivity proof for the bilinear form
a+a_{\Sigma}
.

Lemma 1.4
.

(Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality):
Let
\Omega
be a bounded and connected domain with smooth orientable boundary
\partial\Omega
.
For
\Sigma\subset\partial\Omega
and
m\left(\Sigma\right)>0
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that:

\|v\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq C\left(\left|\int_{\Sigma}vd\sigma\right|+\left|v\right|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}\right),\quad\forall\ v\in H^{1}\left(\Omega\right),

(14)

where
m
is the Lebesgue measure and the positive constant
C
depends only on
\Omega
and
\Sigma
.

In the following theorem, We correctly prove boundedness for the solution sequence
u_{R_{k}}
by employing Lemma
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5
.

(Correction for boundedness)
There exists a positive constant
C
such that
\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}\leq C
.

Proof.

For the variational equation Eq.
10
, we rearrange terms in the equation as follows:

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{R_{k}}\cdot\nabla u_{R_{k}}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\beta\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma=-\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{R_{0}}\cdot\nabla u_{R_{k}}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\lambda u_{R_{k}}d\sigma.

(15)

The left terms can be formulated by:

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{R_{k}}\cdot\nabla u_{R_{k}}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\beta\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma=a\left(u_{R_{k}},u_{R_{k}}\right)+\beta a_{\Sigma}\left(u_{R_{k}},u_{R_{k}}\right).

(16)

The left terms can be estimated via Cauchy-Buniakowsky-Schwarz inequality:

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\left|\nabla u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\beta\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma\geq C_{1}\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}\left|\nabla u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}dx+\left|\int_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{R_{k}}d\sigma\right|^{2}\right),

(17)

where
C_{1}=\min\left\{1,\beta\cdot m\left(\Sigma_{k}\right)^{-1}\right\}
.
The quadratic mean can be directly estimated as follows:

\left(\int_{\Omega_{k}}\left|\nabla u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}dx+\left|\int_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{R_{k}}d\sigma\right|^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}+\left|\int_{\Sigma_{k}}u_{R_{k}}d\sigma\right|\right)^{2}.

(18)

Hence, by the Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality, we derive the following estimation:
The left terms can be formulated by:

\int_{\Omega_{k}}\nabla u_{R_{k}}\cdot\nabla u_{R_{k}}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{k}}\beta\left|u_{R_{k}}\right|^{2}d\sigma\geq C_{2}\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}^{2},

(19)

where
C_{2}=\frac{1}{2}C_{1}
.
Then we formulate this estimation:

C_{2}\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}^{2}\leq\|u_{R_{0}}\|_{H^{1}\left(U\right)}\cdot\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}+\lambda\left|U\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma_{k}\right)},

(20)

Hence, we use the Cauchy inequality:

\|u_{R_{0}}\|_{H^{1}\left(U\right)}\cdot\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H_{\Gamma}^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}+\lambda\left|U\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\left(\Sigma_{k}\right)}\leq C_{3}\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)},

(21)

where
C_{3}=\left(\|u_{R_{0}}\|_{H^{1}\left(U\right)}^{2}+\lambda^{2}m\left(U\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
That means the boundedness of
u_{R_{k}}
:

\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}\leq C,

(22)

where
C=C_{3}\cdot C_{2}^{-1}
.
∎

As above, we have completed the correction of erroneous boundedness estimation in
[
1
]
.
This now can directly use the Theorem
1.5
,
and enables domain extension estimation based on

4
]

\|\tilde{u}_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(U\right)}\leq\tilde{C}\|u_{R_{k}}\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega_{k}\right)}.

(23)

Subsequent convergence estimations for solution sequences

Hence, the existence for optimal domains can further be ensured by proving the compactness of

Absolutely irreducible quasisimple linear groups containing elements of order a specified Zsigmondy prime
S. P. Glasby, Alice C. Niemeyer, Cheryl E. Praeger, A. E. Zalesski

Abstract
This paper is concerned with absolutely irreducible quasisimple subgroups G G of a finite general linear group GL d ​ ( 𝔽 q ) {\mathrm{GL}}_{d}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}) for which some element g ∈ G g\in G of prime order r r , in its action on the natural module V = ( 𝔽 q ) d V=({\mathbb{F}}_{q})^{d} , is irreducible on a subspace of the form V ⁡ ( 1 − g ) V(1-g) of dimension d / 2 d/2 . We classify G , d , r G,d,r , the characteristic p p of the field 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} , and we identify those examples where the element g g has a fixed point subspace of dimension d / 2 d/2 . Our proof relies on representation theory, in particular, the multiplicities of eigenvalues of g g , and builds on earlier results of DiMuro. Dedication: To the memory of Richard A. Parker with gratitude for his friendship, and in recognition of his contribution to the modular character theory of quasisimple groups. Introduction

Absolutely irreducible quasisimple linear groups arise naturally in Aschbacher’s analysis
[
1
]
of subgroups of finite general linear groups, and finite classical groups, as subgroups which preserve no natural geometrical structure on the natural module. Dealing with these groups is important both theoretically and computationally. State-of-the-art recognition algorithms for finite classical groups
[
8
,
15
]
seek a pair of elements of prime order which generate a smaller dimensional classical subgroup with high probability. Viewing such an element-pair as belonging to a smaller classical subgroup
X
acting on the natural
d
-dimensional module for
X
, at least one element of the pair
acts irreducibly on a subspace of dimension at least
d/2
.
This property is studied in
[
16
]
and later in
[
11
]
; and proving a probabilistic generation result for
X
requires information about the absolutely irreducible quasisimple subgroups of
X
that contain such an element. These elements are stingray elements (or ppd stingray elements) defined as follows.

Some comments and an algorithmic application

The family of subgroups in Aschbacher’s classification
[

corresponding to the subgroups

such that the socle

of

has a quasisimple preimage
G
in

is divisible by a
(d/2)
-ppd prime

and Theorem

The organisation of the subgroups of classical groups provided by Aschbacher’s Theorem has been a useful means of attacking numerous problems concerning classical groups, especially questions about generation. We comment here about a particular question of this type. It relates to the main result of
[
24
, Theorem 2]
which provided a foundation for justifying new constructive recognition algorithms for classical groups in even characteristic which were effective where methods utilising involution centralisers were not available, see for example
[

]

The result
[

showed that, for a classical subgroup

3
Stingray elements and subgroups of classical groups

In this section we make some observations about ppd-elements and stingray elements in classical groups, and we prove Theorem
1.4
.

3.1
Aschbacher’s class
\mathscr{C}_{9}
(or
\mathscr{S}
)

In
[
1
, Section 1]
, Aschbacher defined eight families
\mathscr{C}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{C}_{8}
of proper subgroups of a finite classical group on
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
according to various structures on
V
such subgroups preserved, see also
[
4
, Definition 2.1.3]
. Collectively, these eight families are often referred to as
geometric subgroups
of the classical group. For example, the family
\mathscr{C}_{1}
essentially consists of subspace stabilisers. Aschbacher then proved
[
1
, Theorem]
that each proper subgroup
H
of a classical subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
on
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
, as defined in (
1
), is either contained in a subgroup lying in some
\mathscr{C}_{i}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant 8
, or the generalised Fitting subgroup of
H/(H\cap Z)
, where
Z
is the subgroup of scalars, is a nonabelian simple group
S
and the covering group
G
of
S
in
H
is absolutely irreducible on
V
, and its representation on
V
is not realisable over a proper subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. In particular, the subgroup
G
is quasisimple and satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.2
.
The collection of subgroups
H
with these properties has come to be known as
\mathscr{C}_{9}
, or sometimes as
\mathscr{S}
, for example, in
[
4
]
. Sometimes the definition of the family
\mathscr{S}
is more prescriptive, for example in
[
4
]
elements of
\mathscr{S}
are assumed to be maximal in the classical group, and are assumed not to lie in any of the families
\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
i\leqslant 8
.

If
H<{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
lies in the class
\mathscr{C}_{9}
for some classical group on
V
, then the quasisimple group
G
described above is equal to
H^{(\infty)}
, the last term in the derived series of
H
. Here we prove Lemma
3.1
, mentioned in Subsection
1.1
, which shows that any
(d/2)
-ppd element lying in
H
must in fact lie in
G
, and hence Theorem
1.2
may be applied.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d,q=p^{a},V
be as in Theorem
1.2
so
d
is even and
d\geqslant 4
. Let
Z
be the group of scalars in
{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
, and suppose that
q^{d/2}-1
has a primitive prime divisor
r
. Suppose also that
H<{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
lies in the family
\mathscr{C}_{9}
described above, so that
G\coloneq H^{(\infty)}
is quasisimple and satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
and
S\coloneq G/(G\cap Z)
is a nonabelian simple group. Then the
r
-parts
|H|_{r}=|G|_{r}=|S|_{r}
are equal.

Proof.

Since
H
is absolutely irreducible, it follows that
S\leqslant H/(H\cap Z)\leqslant\textrm{Aut}(S)
, so
|H|=|H\cap Z|\cdot|S|\cdot x
, where
x
divides
|{\mathrm{Out}}(S)|
; also

|G|=y\cdot|S|
, where
y
divides
|H\cap Z|
. There is nothing to prove if
r
does not divide
|H|
, so suppose that
r
divides
|H|
.
Since
o_{r}(q)=d/2\geqslant 2
,
the prime
r
does not divide
|Z|=q-1
, and hence
|G|_{r}=|S|_{r}
and
r
divides
|S|\cdot x
. Also
r\geqslant d/2+1\geqslant 3
, so
r
is odd. To prove the lemma we assume to the contrary that
r
divides
|{\mathrm{Out}}(S)|
and derive a contradiction. First,
S
is not isomorphic to an alternating group or a sporadic simple group since in these cases
{\mathrm{Out}}(S)
is a
2
-group. Thus
S
is a Lie type simple group over a field of order
s
. If
s
is coprime to
p
, then recalling that
r\geqslant d/2+1
, it follows from the results of Landazuri and Seitz
[
22
]
, that
d/2+1
is strictly greater than the odd part
|{\mathrm{Out}}(S)|_{2^{\prime}}
(see Table 5.3.A in
[
21
]
). Thus
s=p^{b}
for some
b
, and
r
divides the order of a field automorphism of
S
. Arguing as in
[
16
, last paragraph of Section 7]
, since the module
V
is invariant under this field automorphism, it follows from
[
21
, Proposition 5.4.2(i)]
and Steinberg’s twisted tensor product theorem
[
21
, Theorem 5.4.5]
that
d\geqslant 2^{r}
. Hence
r-1\geqslant d/2\geqslant 2^{r-1}
, which is a contradiction since
r
is an odd prime. Thus
r
is coprime to
|{\mathrm{Out}}(S)|
and
|H|_{r}=|G|_{r}=|S|_{r}
, as claimed.
∎

3.2
An extension of Theorem
1.2

As preparation for the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we give in Proposition
3.2
an equivalent of Theorem
1.2
for the case where
(d,q)=(12,2)
and
6
-stingray elements of order
9
. We note that, while
2^{6}-1
does not have a primitive prime divisor, the integer
9
is a
primitive prime-power divisor
of
2^{6}-1
in the sense that it does not divide
2^{i}-1
for any
i<6
.

In the proof of Proposition
3.2
,
we use the definitions of the Aschbacher classes
\mathscr{C}_{1},\dots,\mathscr{C}_{8}
given in
[
4
, Definition 2.1.3]
; and we recall that the class
\mathscr{S}
given in
[
4
]
comprises groups which are maximal in the classical group, and are not subgroups of any group in
\cup_{i\leqslant 8}\mathscr{C}_{i}
.
Recall also that a subgroup
H
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
is a
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-subgroup precisely when
H^{(\infty)}
is quasisimple and absolutely irreducible on the natural module
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{12}
, and
H
does not contain a classical subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
as defined in (
1
). (The condition that the representation is not realisable over a proper subfield is vacuously true for the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.)
In particular, since
H^{(\infty)}
is absolutely irreducible,
H
is not contained in a subgroup of
\mathscr{C}_{3}
; and since
d=12
is not a prime power or a proper power,
H
is not contained in a subgroup of
\mathscr{C}_{i}
for
i=6,7
. Thus if a
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-subgroup
H
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
is contained in a subgroup in
\mathscr{C}_{i}
for some
i\leqslant 8
, then
i=2
or
i=4
.

Note that the
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-subgroup
H
may not be maximal in
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
, or
in any classical subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
. Also, since
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)

contains no scalar matrices other than
I_{12}
any absolutely irreducible
subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
has a trivial center. Thus the absolutely
irreducible quasisimple group
H^{(\infty)}
must be a simple group
S
, and the
possibilities for
S
are severely constrained by number theory
(
|S|
divides
|{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)|
) and representation theory.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
H
is a
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
, that is,
H^{(\infty)}
is quasisimple and is absolutely irreducible on
V={\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{12}
, and
H
does not contain a classical subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)
as defined in
(
1
)
.
Then

H^{(\infty)}\in\{{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(25),A_{14},A_{13},{\mathrm{PSL}}_{3}(3)\}.

However, none of these possibilities for
H
contains a

6
-stingray element of order
9
.

Proof.

Recall our remarks preceding Proposition
3.2
. If
H\in\mathscr{S}
, that is, if
H
is a maximal
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-subgroup not contained in any subgroup in
\cup_{i\leqslant 8}\mathscr{C}_{i}
, then by
[
4
, Tables 8.77–8.85]
,
H^{(\infty)}\in\{{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(25),A_{13},A_{14},{\mathrm{PSL}}_{3}(3)\}
. The example
A_{14}
(which acts on the deleted permutation module) contains the other possibility
A_{13}
.
Any additional
\mathscr{C}_{9}
-subgroup
H
must be contained in a maximal subgroup
M\in\cup_{i\leqslant 8}\mathscr{C}_{i}
, and as discussed above,
M\in\mathscr{C}_{2}\cup\mathscr{C}_{4}
, and these subgroups

are

{\mathrm{GL}}_{6}(2)\wr S_{2},{\mathrm{GL}}_{4}(2)\wr S_{3},{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}(2)\wr S_{4},{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(2)\wr S_{6},{\mathrm{GL}}_{6}(2)\otimes{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(2),{\mathrm{GL}}_{4}(2)\otimes{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}(2).

Thus
H^{(\infty)}\leqslant M
is a simple irreducible subgroup.
If
H\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(2)\wr S_{b}
where
12=ab
and
b=2,3,4
, then
S_{b}
is solvable so

H^{(\infty)}\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(2)^{b}
acts reducibly, a contradiction.
If
b=6
, i.e.
H\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(2)\wr S_{6}
, then
H^{(\infty)}
equals
A_{5}
or
A_{6}
.
However,
A_{5}
and
A_{6}
act reducibly, see
[
19
, pp. 2, 4]
. This eliminates the
\mathscr{C}_{2}

possibilities for
M
.
If
H\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(2)\otimes{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(2)
where
b=2,3
, then as

H^{(\infty)}
is simple and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{b}(2)
is soluble,
H^{(\infty)}
lies in

{\mathrm{GL}}_{a}(2)\otimes 1
, so
H
is reducible, a
contradiction. Thus
M\not\in\mathscr{C}_{4}
, so

H^{(\infty)}\in\{{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(25),A_{13},A_{14},{\mathrm{PSL}}_{3}(3)\}
. A search with

Magma

[
3
]
shows that
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(25),A_{13},A_{14},{\mathrm{PSL}}_{3}(3)

have no absolutely irreducible simple subgroups. Hence

H^{(\infty)}\in\{{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(25),A_{13},A_{14},{\mathrm{PSL}}_{3}(3)\}
are the only
possibilities.

Suppose now that
H
contains a
6
-stingray element

of order

.
Then since
H\leqslant N_{{\mathrm{GL}}_{12}(2)}(H^{(\infty)})
, and since

|\textrm{Aut}(H^{(\infty)})|

which divides

.
However,
H^{(\infty)}\neq{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(25)
or

since in these cases

.
Thus

or
A_{14}
acting on the deleted permutation module.
In these cases

contains elements of order

4
Comments on the proof strategy for Theorem
1.2

We consider quasisimple subgroups
G<{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
with
d
even,
d\geqslant 4
, and
q=p^{a}
for a prime
p
and positive integer
a
. We assume that
|G|
is divisible by a primitive prime divisor
r
of
q^{d/2}-1
, that is to say,
r
is a
(d/2)
-ppd prime and
G
contains a
(d/2)
-ppd element of order
r
. Now
r=r_{0}(d/2)+1
for some integer
r_{0}
(see
[
16
, Remark 1.1]
), so in particular
r\geqslant d/2+1\geqslant 3
, which means that
r
is an odd prime and if
r=3
then
d=4
. Also by the definition of a ppd element,
q
has order
d/2
modulo
r
, that is
o_{r}(q)=d/2
.
The quotient
S\coloneq G/\mathrm{Z}(G)
is a nonabelian simple group, and since

o_{r}(q)=d/2
, the prime
r
does not divide
|\mathrm{Z}(G)|
, and hence
r
divides
|S|
.
To prove Theorem
1.2
(a) we use DiMuro’s paper
[
12
]
as the main resource for the cases where
S
is not of Lie type in characteristic
p
, in Sections
5
,
6
, and
7
. In the remaining ‘same characteristic’ case we give some proof details in Section
8
(and identify the relevant tables and sections of
[
11
]
where a proof can be found).

Our main objective is to find all quasisimple subgroups
G<{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
containing a
(d/2)
-ppd stingray element of prime order
r
. Note that each
(d/2)
-stingray element is semisimple, as the prime order
r
is different from the characteristic. Also, these stingray elements act on the natural module
V={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{d}
as ‘almost cyclic’ matrices which have been studied extensively by the fourth author and colleagues, and are defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

A matrix
g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
is
almost cyclic
if, for some
k\in[0,d]
, some scalar multiple
zg
(
z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\#}
) is conjugate in
{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
to a block diagonal matrix
{\rm diag}(I_{d-k},g_{1})
where
g_{1}\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{k}(q)
is a cyclic matrix (that is, the minimal and characteristic polynomials of
g_{1}
are the same). In particular, each
(d/2)
-stingray element is almost cyclic (with
k=d/2
and
g_{1}
irreducible), but the converse is not true.

The papers
[
9
]
and
[
10
,
32
]
contain classifications of various classes of absolutely irreducible quasisimple groups containing almost cyclic elements, and our proofs rely heavily on this work.
We discuss in Remark
4.2
, the various kinds of
(d/2)
-ppd elements and the links with almost cyclic elements.

Remark 4.2
.

Suppose that
d
is even,
d\geqslant 4
, and that
g\in{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
is a
(d/2)
-ppd element of prime order, that is,
r\coloneq|g|
is a primitive prime divisor of
q^{d/2}-1
. Then
g
satisfies precisely one of the following:

(1)

The element
g
is a
(d/2)
-stingray element; that is it has a
(d/2)
-dimensional fixed point space and is irreducible on a unique
(d/2)
-dimensional complementary subspace.

(2)

The element
g
is semisimple with characteristic polynomial
f_{1}(t)f_{2}(t)
where each
f_{i}
is irreducible of degree
d/2
, and either

(2.i)

f_{1}\neq f_{2}
which implies that
g
is regular semisimple, and in fact
g
is cyclic, or

(2.ii)

f_{1}=f_{2}
, so
g
is not regular semisimple, and is not cyclic.

Elements of types (1) and (2.i) are almost cyclic, while those of type (2.ii) are not. In particular, if
|g|=3
, then
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,
d=4
, and there is a unique irreducible polynomial of degree
2
, so either (1) or (2.ii) holds for
g
.∎

To determine whether a given element
g
of a quasisimple subgroup
G\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
, with
r\coloneq|g|
a
(d/2)
-ppd prime, is a
(d/2)
-stingray element, it is often helpful to inspect the Brauer characters in
[
19
]
for those representations where
p
divides
|G|
, or the character tables in
[
7
]
for those representations where
p
does not divide
|G|
. If
r=1+k(d/2)
then a
(d/2)
-stingray element
g
is conjugate in
{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})
to a diagonal matrix
{\rm diag}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{d/2},1,\dots,1)
, where the
\eta_{i}
are pairwise distinct primitive
r^{\rm th}
roots of unity in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, an algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The Brauer character is essentially the trace of this matrix, namely
\chi(g)=d/2+\sum_{i=1}^{d/2}\eta_{i}
, with the
r^{\rm th}
roots of unity replaced by complex
r^{\rm th}
roots of unity.
We use the following fact:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
r
be an odd prime, and let
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{r-1}
be the nontrivial
r^{\rm th}
roots of unity over
\mathbb{C}
. If
\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}a_{i}\eta_{i}=c
, with
a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1},c\in\mathbb{Q}
, then

a_{1}=\dots=a_{r-1}=-c
.

Proof.

Since
r
is an odd prime, the subfield
\mathbb{Q}(\eta)
generated by a non-identity
r
th root of unity
\eta
contains
\mathbb{Q}
with index
r-1
, the nontrivial
r
th roots of unity are precisely
\eta,\dots,\eta^{r-1}
, and they are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Moreover, since
\eta^{r}=1
and
\eta\neq 1
, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\eta^{i}=(\eta^{r}-1)/(\eta-1)=0
, or equivalently,
\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\eta^{i}=-1
.
Suppose that
\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}a_{i}\eta^{i}=c
, with
a_{0},\dots,a_{r-1},c\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then adding this equation to the equation
c\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\eta^{i}=-c
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(a_{i}+c)\eta^{i}=0
, and hence
a_{1}=\dots=a_{r-1}=-c
.
∎

We illustrate how this fact may be used in Sections
5
and
7
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
g\in G
, where
G
is a quasisimple subgroup of
G\leqslant{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(q)
, with
r\coloneq|g|
a
(d/2)
-ppd prime. Let
\chi
be the Brauer character of
G

(
see
[
19
, §4]
)
.

(a)

If
r=1+d/2
, then
g
is a
(d/2)
-stingray element if and only if
\chi(g)=d/2-1
.

(b)

If
r=1+d
, then

is of type
(2.i)
if
\chi(g)=-1
, of type
(2.ii)
if
\chi(g)=2N
, and a
(d/2)
-stingray element if
\chi(g)=d/2+N
, where

is a sum of

th roots of unity.

Proof.

As discussed before Lemma
4.3
, a
(d/2)
-stingray element

is conjugate in

to a diagonal matrix
{\rm diag}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{d/2},1,\dots,1)
, where the
\eta_{i}
are pairwise distinct primitive

roots of unity in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}

(a) If

\eta_{i}
in the previous paragraph constitute all of the nontrivial

is the unique monic irreducible polynomial of degree

(b) If

above constitute half of the nontrivial

roots, and

of degree

∎

Remark 4.5

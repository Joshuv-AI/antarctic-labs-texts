Magnetic dipole contribution to the γ \gamma -spectrum from d d - t t collisions
N. K. Timofeyuk

Abstract
One in about a few hundred thousand sub-Coulomb d d - t t collisions is accompanied by the emission of a γ \gamma -photon. The γ \gamma -spectrum from these collisions is dominated by a 16.7 MeV peak, corresponding to the population of the p p -wave 3 / 2 − 3/2^{-} α \alpha - n n ground-state resonance in the E1 transition from an intermediate d d -wave α \alpha - n n state, corresponding to the 5 He excited state around 16.7 MeV. The strength of this spectrum at the large γ \gamma -energy endpoint decreases fast both due to kinematic factors and due to centrifugal repulsion between neutron and α \alpha -particle at near-zero relative energies. However, no centrifugal repulsion would occur if s s -wave α \alpha - n n states were populated in magnetic dipole transition. Therefore, one can expect that close to the γ \gamma -spectrum endpoint around 17.5 MeV the M1 transition could dominate, leading to additional counts in experimental spectra which could be easily misinterpreted as a background. This work presents calculations of the M1 contribution to the d + t → α + n + γ d+t\rightarrow\alpha+n+\gamma cross section caused both by convection and magnetization currents. While the first contribution was found to be negligible, the one from magnetization depends strongly on the choice of the model for the d d - t t channel scattering wave function. Using d d - t t interaction models from the literature, represented either by a gaussian of by a square well, resulted in unrealistically strong M1 contribution. With constructed in the present work optical folding potential, correctly representing the sizes of deuteron and triton, the M1 contribution near the endpoint is reduced to about 2 % \% of the 16.7-MeV E1 peak. Arguments in favor of more detailed calculations, that could potentially change this number, are put forward.

II.4

IV
Discussion and conclusions

The present study of the M1 contribution to the
d+t\rightarrow\alpha+n+\gamma
cross section shows that it is dominated by the direct transition from the
s
-wave
d
-
t
partition of
5
He to the
s
-wave
\alpha
-
n
partition in the final channel.
The corresponding cross section has been found to be strongly dependent on the model choice for the
d
-
t
scattering wave function. In particular, it depends on how fast the
d
-
t
interaction potential decreases with the
d
-
t
separation. With the choice of two potential models from the literature, one having a gaussian decrease and another one being just a square well, the M1 transition becomes unrealistically large due to the large values of
d
-
t
channel function inside the
d
-
t
interaction. The optical folding potential, constructed in this work, provides a more spatially extended
d
-
t
interaction, which leads to a further spread and the correspondingly smaller values of the
d
-
t
wave function within the
d
-
t
interaction region. This leads to a much smaller M1 cross section.

The strong model dependence of the M1 transition is reminiscent of the situation with theoretical calculations of
\beta
-delayed deuteron emission
6
He
\rightarrow\alpha+d+e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}
. Its measured branching ratio to the main
6
Li ground state decay branch is very small, of the order of 10
-6
, however, potential model predictions are two orders of magnitude higher
Bor93
. To bring theoretical predictions down, full three-body calculations of both the
6
He
=\alpha+n+n
and
6
Li
=\alpha+n+p
must be performed
Tur06
;
Tur18
, keeping all the quantum numbers included. Such a situation is due to the spatial parts of
6
He and
\alpha+d
wave functions being almost orthogonal, which could be achieved in full calculations only.

The operator
\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}t_{3i}
that causes the Galow-Teller transition
6
He
\rightarrow\alpha+d+e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}
is almost identical to the M1 operator of this paper if the long-wave approximation is done. Although all results for
d+t\rightarrow\alpha+n+\gamma
shown here do not use the long-wave approximation, it has been checked numerically that this approximation does not change much the results. A similarity with the
\beta
-delayed deuteron emission of
6
He is also in the three-body nature of the
d
-
t
system, which could be considered as a strongly-bound triton plus neutron plus proton. The lessons learned from
\beta
-decay studies tell us that for reliable calculations of the M1 transition one has to go beyond the two-body potential model description of the
d
-
t
motion and include into consideration such details as deuteron breakup and polarization in the sub-Coulomb
d
-
t
scattering. Also, more quantum numbers, such as the
d
-wave state admixture in the incoming deuteron, should be included in the calculations.

This work has been motivated by a possibility of having more counts near the endpoint of the
\gamma
-spectrum from
d
-
t
collisions due to M1 transition to the final near-zero energy
s
-wave
\alpha
-
n
state, which could be favored because of the absence of the centrifugal barrier. A realistic model of the
d
-
t
interaction suggests that the strength of the spectrum in this area is about 2
\%
only, which would not induce much of a systematical error in the background subtraction. However, to confirm this conclusion, as a minimum, three-body treatment of the
d
-
t
collisions are required. In the long term, five-body calculations starting from realistic nucleon-nucleon interactions will be needed to clarify this matter.

\displaystyle\times|[\psi_{t}\otimes\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(p,n)]_{S_{i}M_{S_{i}}}\rangle=g_{p}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}(1\mu S_{i}M_{S_{i}}|\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{n}),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

which is exactly the same as in the case of

except for the neutron gyromagnetic factor
g_{n}
being replaced by the the proton gyromagnetic factor
g_{p}
.

For
i=5
, which is a neutron in deuteron, the spin matrix element is

\displaystyle\langle[[\psi_{t}\otimes\chi^{\rm spin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}}(p)]_{0}\otimes\chi^{\rm spin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}}(n)]_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{n}}|{S}_{\mu}(n)g_{sn}

\displaystyle\times|[\psi_{t}\otimes\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(p,n)]_{S_{i}M_{S_{i}}}\rangle

\displaystyle=W(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}S_{i};01)\frac{1}{2}\langle\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}||\vec{S}||\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\rangle(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}M_{S_{i}}1\mu|\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{n_{f}}),

which is non-zero only for

Finally, the matrix elements (

V.3
Spatial matrix element for M1 magnetization amplitude

The calculate the M1 amplitude the integration should be performed either over
d\vec{r}_{\alpha n}d\vec{r}_{pt}
or over
d\vec{r}_{np}d\vec{r}_{dt}
.
This task is simplified if
integration is performed over
d\vec{r}_{\alpha n}d\vec{r}_{dt}
- the variables of the continuum state wave functions. Because
\vec{r}_{tp}=\tfrac{8}{5}\vec{r}_{dt}-\tfrac{4}{5}\vec{r}_{\alpha n}
and
\vec{r}_{np}=\tfrac{8}{5}\vec{r}_{\alpha n}-\tfrac{6}{5}\vec{r}_{dt}
, one has
d\vec{r}_{\alpha n}d\vec{r}_{pt}=\left(\tfrac{8}{5}\right)^{3}\vec{r}_{\alpha n}d\vec{r}_{dt}
. Then the product of the radial parts of the overlap
I_{pt}
and the deuteron wave function
\psi_{d}
could be represented as

\displaystyle I_{pt}({r}_{pt})\varphi_{d}({r}_{np})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\sum_{lm}{\cal F}_{l}(r_{dt},r_{\alpha n})Y^{*}_{lm}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{dt})Y_{lm}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\alpha n}),

\displaystyle{\cal F}_{l}(r_{dt},r_{\alpha n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}d\mu\,P_{\lambda}(\mu)\varphi_{d}\left(|\tfrac{6}{5}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{dt}-\tfrac{8}{5}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\alpha n}|\right)

(52)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle I_{pt}(|\tfrac{8}{5}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{dt}-\tfrac{4}{5}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\alpha n}|).

Using the results for both spin and isospin matrix elements derived in the subsection above, one can obtain that the M1 amplitude contains the matrix element over the angular variables

\displaystyle F=-i\sum_{lm}{\cal F}_{l}(r_{dt},r_{\alpha n})\langle Y_{L^{\prime}_{f}M^{\prime}_{f}}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\alpha n})Y_{lm}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{dt})\mid g_{n}\left[\vec{\nabla}_{t}\times\vec{L}_{t}\phi_{\lambda\mu}(r_{t})\right]_{\nu}+g_{p}\left[\vec{\nabla}_{p}\times\vec{L}_{p}\phi_{\lambda\mu}(r_{p})\right]_{\nu}\mid Y_{L^{\prime}_{dt}M^{\prime}_{dt}}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{dt})Y_{lm}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{\alpha n})\rangle.

(53)

The first term is further developed using

\displaystyle\langle Y_{lm}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{dt})\mid\left[\vec{\nabla}_{t}\times\vec{L}_{t}\phi_{\lambda\mu}(r_{t})\right]_{\nu}\mid Y_{L^{\prime}_{dt}M^{\prime}_{dt}}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{dt})\rangle

\displaystyle=k\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(-)^{\lambda^{\prime}}c_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}(\lambda\mu 1\nu|\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})j_{\lambda^{\prime}}(\tfrac{2}{5}k_{\gamma}r_{dt})

\displaystyle\times\,(L^{\prime}_{dt}0\lambda^{\prime}0|L^{\prime}_{f}0)\frac{\hat{L}^{\prime}_{dt}\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{4\pi}\hat{L}^{\prime}_{f}}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(54)

with

\displaystyle c_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\lambda+1)\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2\lambda-1}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda-1

(55)

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda

\displaystyle\lambda\sqrt{\frac{\lambda+1}{2\lambda+3}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda+1.

To develop the second term of (
53
), one has to use a general expansion for
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}=\alpha_{1}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{1}+\alpha_{2}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{2}

\displaystyle-i\left[\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{L}\phi_{\lambda\mu}(r)\right]_{\nu}=\sqrt{4\pi}k\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}i^{-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-\lambda^{\prime}}c_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\times(\lambda_{1}0\lambda_{2}0|\lambda^{\prime}0)j_{\lambda_{1}}(\alpha_{1}kr_{1})j_{\lambda_{2}}(\alpha_{2}kr_{2})

\displaystyle\times(\lambda_{1}\mu_{1}\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}|\lambda_{2}\mu_{2})(\lambda\mu 1\nu|\lambda^{\prime}\mu)Y^{*}_{\lambda_{1}\mu_{1}}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{1})Y_{\lambda_{2}\mu_{2}}(\hat{\mbox{\boldmath$r$}}_{2})

(56)

In the case of
\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{p}=\tfrac{6}{5}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{dt}-\tfrac{4}{5}\mbox{\boldmath$r$}_{\alpha n}
the final result for
F
becomes

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k\frac{\hat{L}^{\prime}_{dt}}{\sqrt{4\pi}}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}c_{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}F_{L^{\prime}_{dt}\lambda^{\prime}L^{\prime}_{f}}(r_{dt},r_{\alpha n})

(57)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle(\lambda\mu 1\nu|\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})(\lambda^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}L^{\prime}_{dt}M^{\prime}_{dt}|L^{\prime}_{f}M^{\prime}_{f}),

V.4
Exchange contribution to magnetization

The simplest way to calculate the exchange contribution

\displaystyle\langle\Phi^{(41)}_{\alpha n,M_{n}}(1,2,3,4;5)\mid{\cal M}^{M\lambda}_{\mu}\mid\Phi^{(32)}_{dt,M_{d}M_{t}}(3,4,5;1,2)\rangle

to the M1 amplitude is to use the long-wave approximation, justified by smallvalues of the
k_{\gamma}r
contributing to the process. In this approximation, one has to deals with matrix elements of the operator
\sum_{i}g_{si}S_{\mu,i}=(g_{n}+g_{p})\sum_{i}S_{\mu,i}+(g_{n}-g_{p})\sum_{i}S_{\mu,i}t_{3i}
. This could be achieved by making fractional parentage decomposition of the
\alpha
-particle wave function in the exit channel into the product of two deuteron wave functions keeping only the terms corresponding to the deuteron ground state wave function,

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}(1,2,3,4)\approx\left[\psi_{d}(1,2)\times\psi_{d}(3,4)\right]_{00}I_{dd}(\vec{r}_{dd}),

(61)

with the isospin Clebsch-Gordon coefficient embedded in the overlap function
I_{dd}(\vec{r}_{dd})
. The triton wave function in the initial state is also expanded as

\displaystyle\psi_{t}(3,4,5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{nd}(\vec{r}_{nd})\varphi_{d}(\vec{r}_{34})[\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(3,4)\chi^{\rm spin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}}(5)]_{J_{t}M_{t}}

(62)

Then considering only the
s
-wave components in the deuteron wave functions and overlaps one has to calculate the matrix element

\displaystyle\langle[\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(1,2)\times\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(3,4)]_{00}\,\chi^{\rm spin}_{\sigma_{n_{f}}}(5)X^{\rm isospin}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{5}S_{\mu,i}g_{s_{i}}\mid[[\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(3,4)\times\chi^{\rm spin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}}(5)]_{J_{t}}\times\chi^{\rm spin}_{1}(1,2)]_{S_{i}M_{S_{i}}}X^{\rm isospin}\rangle,\,\,\,\,\,

(63)

where
X^{\rm isospin}
is the isospin part of the five-body system. It is easy to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,2}\langle\chi^{\rm spin}_{1M^{\prime}_{d}}(1,2)\chi^{\rm isospin}_{00}(1,2)\mid S_{\mu,i}t_{3i}\mid\chi^{\rm spin}_{1M_{d}}(1,2)\chi^{\rm isospin}_{00}(1,2)\rangle=0

(64)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,2}\langle\chi^{\rm spin}_{1M^{\prime}_{d}}(1,2)\chi^{\rm isospin}_{00}(1,2)\mid S_{\mu,i}\mid\chi^{\rm spin}_{1M_{d}}(1,2)\chi^{\rm isospin}_{00}(1,2)\rangle=-\sqrt{2}(1\mu 1M_{d}|1M^{\prime}_{d}).

(65)

The same is true for nucleons 3 and 4. Also,

\displaystyle\langle\chi^{\rm spin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma^{\prime}_{5}}(5)\chi^{\rm isospin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}}(5)\mid S_{\mu}g_{s_{5}}\mid\chi^{\rm spin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{5}}(5)\chi^{\rm isospin}_{\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}}(5)\rangle=g_{n}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{5}1\mu|\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma^{\prime}_{5})\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\langle\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}||S||\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\rangle

(66)

Then, after further algebraic manipulations, one obtains that the spin-isospin latrix element (
63
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(g_{n}+g_{p})(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}M_{S_{i}}1\mu|\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{n_{f}})\sum_{S}(-)^{S+S_{i}}\hat{S}_{i}\hat{S}W(11\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}S;1S_{i})\left[(-)^{J_{t}+1-S_{i}}\delta_{SJ_{t}}+(-)^{3/2-S}\hat{S}\hat{J}_{t}W(1\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}S_{i}1;SJ_{t})\right]

\displaystyle+(-)^{J_{t}+1-S_{i}}g_{n}W(11S_{i}\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$};0J_{t})\hat{J}_{t}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}M_{S_{i}}1\mu|\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\sigma_{n_{f}})\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(67)

For
S_{i}=3/2
and
J_{t}=1/2
it is equal to zero.

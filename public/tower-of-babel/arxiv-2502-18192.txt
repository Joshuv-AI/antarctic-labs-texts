Stable and minimizing cones in the Alt-Phillips problem
Ovidiu Savin, Hui Yu

Abstract
. We study homogeneous solutions to the Alt-Phillips problem when the exponent γ \gamma is close to 1. In dimension d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 , we show that the radial cone is minimizing when γ \gamma is close to 1. In dimension d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 , we construct an axially symmetric cone whose contact set has with positive density. We show that it is a global minimizer. It is analogous to the De Silva-Jerison [ DJ ] cone for the Alt-Caffarelli functional which corresponds to exponent γ = 0 \gamma=0 . The cone we construct bifurcates from another minimizing cone whose contact set has zero density, obtained as the trivial extension of the radial solution. This second cone is analogous to a quadratic polynomial solution in the classical obstacle problem which corresponds to exponent γ = 1 \gamma=1 . In particular our results show that, when γ < 1 \gamma<1 is sufficiently close to 1, there are axis symmetric cones that exhibit the properties of both end point cases γ = 0 \gamma=0 and γ = 1 \gamma=1 .

2.1.
The Alt-Phillips functional

The

When

We discuss the relation between the minimality of a solution and the existence of a foliation around it. Since the argument is classical, the proofs are only sketched. For more details of this argument in various contexts, interested readers may consult
[

In the following, we use notations

Definition 2.6
.

Suppose that
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

An
upper foliation
of
u
is a family of functions
\{\Psi_{t}\}_{t\in(0,+\infty)}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

For each
t\in(0,+\infty)
, we have

\Psi_{t}\geq u\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{ and }\hskip 5.0pt\Psi_{t}>u\text{ in }\overline{\{u>0\}};

(2)

For each
t\in(0,+\infty)
,
\Psi_{t}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

(3)

The map
(t,x)\mapsto\Psi_{t}(x)
is continuous;

(4)

As
t\to 0
, we have

\Psi_{t}\to u\hskip 5.0pt\text{locally uniformly on }\{u>0\};

and

(5)

As
t\to+\infty
, we have

\Psi_{t}\to+\infty\hskip 5.0pt\text{locally uniformly.}

Each function
\Psi_{t}
is called an
upper leaf
.

The existence of an upper foliation gives one-sided minimality from above.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has an upper foliation
\{\Psi_{t}\}_{t\in(0,+\infty)}
and satisfies

\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u;B_{1})<+\infty.

Then
u\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\gamma}(B_{1})
.

Recall the class
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\gamma}
from Definition
2.1
.

Proof.

By the direct method, we can find
w
, a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(\cdot)
in the family

\{v:\hskip 5.0ptv=u\hskip 5.0pt\text{along }\partial B_{1},\text{ and }\hskip 5.0ptv\geq u\text{ in }B_{1}\}.

We have
w\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\{w>u\})
. Define

\tilde{w}:=\max\{u,w\},

then
\tilde{w}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\gamma}(B_{1})
.

It suffices to show that

\tilde{w}=u\text{ in }B_{1}.

Suppose not, then the set
\{\tilde{w}>u\}
is non-empty and contained in
\overline{B_{1}}
. By the definition of an upper foliation, we can find
t_{0}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\Psi_{t_{0}}\geq\tilde{w}\text{ in }B_{1},\text{ and }\hskip 5.0pt\Psi_{t_{0}}(x_{0})=\tilde{w}(x_{0})\text{ at some }x_{0}\in\overline{\{\tilde{w}>0\}}\cap B_{1}.

This is a contradiction as a subsolution cannot be touched from above by a supersolution
[
DS1
]
.
∎

(4)

(5)

As

, we have

\Phi_{t}\to u\hskip 5.0pt\text{locally uniformly in $\{u>0\}$};

and

Given a compact set

2.3.
Solutions to the obstacle problem

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the

studies solutions to the following

(2.5)

\Delta u=\chi_{\{u>0\}},\text{ and }\hskip 5.0ptu\geq 0\text{ in }\Omega.

For our problem (
1.1

\gamma=1
and the scaling parameter
\beta=2
(see (
2.1
)).

We are particularly interested in radial solutions. For a solution of the form
P=P(|x|)
, we have

P^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}P^{\prime}=\chi_{\{P>0\}}\text{ on }(0,+\infty),\hskip 5.0pt\text{ and }P\geq 0\text{ on }[0,+\infty).

For
\lambda>0
, let
P_{\lambda}
denote the solution whose contact set is

\{P_{\lambda}=0\}=\overline{B}_{\lambda},

then
P_{\lambda}
is given by

(2.6)

P_{\lambda}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2d}|x|^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2(d-2)}+\frac{\lambda^{d}}{d(d-2)}|x|^{-(d-2)}&\text{ for }|x|\geq\lambda,\\
0&\text{ for }|x|<\lambda.\end{cases}

3.
Stability of the radial cone

In
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the radial cone for the Alt-Phillips problem is

(3.1)

u_{\mathrm{rad}}(x):=c_{\mathrm{rad}}|x|^{\beta},

with the scaling parameter
\beta
from (
2.1
). The coefficient satisfies

c_{\mathrm{rad}}^{2-\gamma}=\frac{\gamma}{\beta(d+\beta-2)},

so that
u_{\mathrm{rad}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as in Definition
2.2
.

In this section, we address the stability of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
, in terms of the second-order expansion of the energy
4
4

4

Our expansion relies on the positivity of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
. For the stability condition on general solutions, see the work by Karakhanyan and Sanz-Perela
[
KSP
]
.
in (
1.1
).

To be precise, we say that
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
is
stable
if we have

(3.2)

\lim_{t\to 0}[\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u_{\mathrm{rad}}+t\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u_{\mathrm{rad}})]/t^{2}\geq 0

for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\})
.

Since
u_{\mathrm{rad}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\})
and
t
small, we have

\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u_{\mathrm{rad}}+t\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{\gamma}(u_{\mathrm{rad}})=\frac{t^{2}}{2}[\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}\varphi^{2}]+\mathcal{O}(t^{3}).

As a result, the radial cone
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
is stable if and only if, for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\})
,

(3.3)

\int\varphi\cdot L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}\varphi=-\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}\varphi^{2}\leq 0

where
L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}
is the
linearized operator around

u_{\mathrm{rad}}
:

(3.4)

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}\varphi:=\Delta\varphi-\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}\varphi=\Delta\varphi-(\gamma-1)\beta(d+\beta-2)\varphi/|x|^{2}.

The
linearized equation
reads

(3.5)

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}\varphi=0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}.

Restricting to radial functions
\varphi=\varphi(r)
, this equation reduces to an ODE

(3.6)

\varphi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}\varphi^{\prime}=(\gamma-1)\beta(d+\beta-2)\frac{\varphi}{r^{2}}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ on }(0,+\infty).

For functions of the form
\varphi(r)=r^{\alpha}
, this imposes a condition on the exponent
\alpha
:

(3.7)

\alpha^{2}+(d-2)\alpha+(1-\gamma)\beta(d+\beta-2)=0.

Following Jerison-Savin
[
JS
]
, the stability of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
depends on the discriminant of (
3.7
), namely,

(3.8)

\Delta(d,\gamma):=(d-2)^{2}-4\beta(d+\beta-2)(1-\gamma).

With this, the
main result
in this section is

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
d\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
.

If
\Delta(d,\gamma)<0
, then the radial cone
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
is unstable.

If
\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0
, then
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
is stable.

We prove the first part of Proposition
3.1
:

Proof of instability when
\Delta<0
.

Under this assumption, the linearized equation (
3.6
) has a solution

\varphi(r)=r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\cos(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}\ln r).

Its oscillatory nature gives two radii
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
such that

\varphi=-1\hskip 5.0pt\text{ at }r_{1}\text{ and }r_{2},\text{ and }\hskip 5.0pt\varphi>-1\hskip 5.0pt\text{ on }(r_{1},r_{2}).

Define a test function
\tilde{\varphi}
as

\tilde{\varphi}(x):=\varphi(|x|)+1>0\text{ if }r_{1}<|x|<r_{2},\text{ and }\hskip 5.0pt\tilde{\varphi}=0\text{ otherwise}.

Then
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfies

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}\tilde{\varphi}=\gamma(1-\gamma)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}\text{ in }B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{1}}},

Since
\gamma\in(0,1)
when
\Delta(d,\gamma)<0
, this implies

\tilde{\varphi}\cdot L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}(\tilde{\varphi})=\gamma(1-\gamma)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}\tilde{\varphi}\geq 0\textit{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}

with strict inequality in
B_{r_{2}}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{1}}}
.

This gives the instability of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
according to (
3.3
).
∎

We now turn to the case when the discriminant in (
3.8
) satisfies

(3.9)

\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0,

and show the second part of Proposition
3.1
. With Proposition
2.4
, it suffices to consider
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

With (
3.9
), the polynomial (
3.7
) has a real root

(3.10)

\alpha=-\frac{d-2}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}.

As a result, the linearized equation (
3.6
) has a positive solution

(3.11)

g(r):=r^{\alpha}=r^{-\frac{d-2}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}}.

This leads to the maximum principle of the linearized operator
L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}
in (
3.4
):

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
d\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
satisfy

\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0.

Let
w
be a subsolution to the linearized equation (
3.5
) in
B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}}
, namely,

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}(w)\geq 0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}}.

If
w\leq 0
on
\partial B_{2}\cup\partial B_{1}
, then

w\leq 0\text{ in }B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}}.

Proof.

Suppose not, then the ratio
G:=w/g
has a positive maximum at some point
p
in
B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}}
. Here
g
is the function from (
3.11
). That is,

0<M:=\max_{\overline{B_{2}\backslash B_{1}}}G=G(p)\text{ for some }p\in B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}}.

Define
W:=w-Mg
, then we have

W\leq 0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}},\text{ and }W(p)=0.

Meanwhile, we have

\Delta W=\Delta w-M\Delta g\geq\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}(w-Mg)\geq 0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }B_{2}\backslash\overline{B_{1}}.

For the last inequality, we used
\gamma<1
.

The strong maximum principle of sub-harmonic functions forces

w-Mg=W\equiv 0\text{ in }B_{2}\backslash B_{1}.

This contradicts
w\leq 0
on
\partial B_{2}\cup\partial B_{1}.

∎

The following corollary is a useful tool to rule out bounded subsolutions in the punctured disk.

Corollary 3.3
.

Suppose that
d\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
satisfy

\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0.

Let
w
be a subsolution to the linearized equation (
3.5
) in
B_{1}\backslash\{0\}
, namely,

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}(w)\geq 0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }B_{1}\backslash\{0\}.

If
w
satisfies

w\leq 0\text{ on }\partial B_{1},\text{ and }\sup_{B_{1}\backslash\{0\}}w<+\infty,

then

w\leq 0\text{ in }B_{1}\backslash\{0\}.

Proof.

Under our assumption
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the discriminant
\Delta(d,\gamma)
in (
3.8
)
satisfies
\Delta(d,\gamma)<(d-2)^{2}
. As a consequence, the exponent
\alpha
in (
3.10
) is strictly negative.

Given any
\varepsilon>0
, this gives

w-\varepsilon g\leq 0\text{ on }\partial B_{1}\cup\partial B_{r_{\varepsilon}},

where
g
is the radial solution in (
3.11
), and
r_{\varepsilon}
is given by

r_{\varepsilon}:=(\sup w/\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

As a result, we can apply Lemma
3.2
to
w-\varepsilon g
in
B_{1}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}
to conclude

w\leq\varepsilon g\text{ in }B_{1}\backslash\overline{B_{r_{\varepsilon}}}.

Note that
r_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we get the desired conclusion.
∎

With these preparations, we give the proof of the stability of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
if
\Delta\geq 0
:

Proof of stability when
\Delta\geq 0
.

Suppose not, then (
3.3
) gives a function
\varphi
, with support in
\overline{B_{R}\backslash B_{r}}
for some
R>r>0
such that

(3.12)

\int|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}\varphi^{2}<0.

Define a functional

F(v):=\int|\nabla v|^{2}+\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}v^{2}

over the class

K:=\{v\in H_{0}^{1}(B_{R}\backslash\overline{B_{r}}):\|v\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq 1\}.

The direct method gives a minimizer,
w
, in
K
.

Without loss of generality, we can assume

(3.13)

w\geq 0\text{ in }B_{R}\backslash B_{r}.

With (
3.12
), we have

(3.14)

F(w)\leq F(\varphi/\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}})<0.

As a result, the Euler-Lagrange equation for
w
implies

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}(w)\geq 0\text{ in }B_{R}\backslash\overline{B_{r}}.

Since
w
is supported in
\overline{B_{R}\backslash B_{r}}
, Lemma
3.2
implies

w\leq 0\text{ in }B_{R}\backslash B_{r}.

Together with (
3.13
), we have
w=0
in
B_{R}\backslash B_{r}
, contradicting (
3.14
).
∎

4.
Minimality of the radial cone

In this section, we address the minimality of the radial cone
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
in (
3.1
). With classes of minimizers and one-sided minimizers
\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}
and
\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\gamma}
in Definition
2.1
, the
main result
of this section reads

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
d\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,2)
.

(1)

If
\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0
, then
u_{\mathrm{rad}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(2)

If
\gamma\in(1-\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{64d^{2}},2)
, then
u_{\mathrm{rad}}\in\mathcal{M}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Recall the discriminant
\Delta(d,\gamma)
from (
3.8
).

Remark 4.2
.

For
\gamma\in[1,2)
, the minimality of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
follows from Proposition
2.4
. In this section, we consider only the range

\gamma\in(0,1).

The lower bound for
\gamma
in Statement (2) is sufficient for our purpose (See Lemma
4.6
). We do not know the sharp range of
\gamma
for which the minimality of
u
holds.

With Theorem
2.11
, we need a foliation around
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
. In Subsection
4.1
, we construct an upper foliation. In Subsection
4.2
, we build a lower foliation. At the beginning of each subsection, we give a
brief explanation of the strategy
.

4.1.
Upper foliation of the radial cone

In this subsection, we construct an upper foliation as in Definition
2.6
for the radial cone
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
in (
3.1
).
Proposition
2.7
then implies the one-sided minimality of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
in Statement (1) of Proposition
4.1
.

In particular, we need one supersolution to the Alt-Phillips problem (see Definition
2.2
) that lies above
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
. In the radial setting, this problem reduces to the ODE in (
4.1
).

To show that its solution stays larger than
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
(see (
4.6
)), we need the maximum principle in Lemma
3.2
. This requires the assumption
\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0
in Statement (1) of Proposition
4.1
. This is the same range of
\gamma
for the stability of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
(see Proposition
3.1
).

Now we start the construction of the upper foliation.
Leaves in this upper foliation arise as rescalings of the solution to the following initial value problem:

(4.1)

v^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}v^{\prime}=\gamma v^{\gamma-1}\text{ on }(0,+\infty),\hskip 5.0pt\text{ and }v(0)=1,\hskip 5.0ptv^{\prime}(0)=0.

Identifying
v
with the function
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto v(|x|)
,
we see that

(4.2)

\Delta v=\gamma v^{\gamma-1}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The analysis of this problem is summarized in the following:

Proposition 4.3
.

For
d\geq 3
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
with
\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0
, there is a unique solution
v
to (
4.1
) on
[0,+\infty)
.

Moreover, this solution satisfies

0<v-u_{\mathrm{rad}}\leq 1,\text{ and }v\geq 1\text{ on }[0,+\infty).

Proof.

For small
\varepsilon>0
, we start with the perturbed problem

(4.3)

v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r+\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=\gamma v_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-1}\text{ for }r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\text{ and }v_{\varepsilon}(0)=1,\hskip 5.0ptv_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=0.

There is a smooth solution on
(0,R_{\varepsilon})
, with
R_{\varepsilon}
being finite only when

\lim_{r\to R_{\varepsilon}^{-}}v_{\varepsilon}(r)=0.

The perturbed equation is equivalent to

(r+\varepsilon)^{1-d}[(r+\varepsilon)^{d-1}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}]^{\prime}=\gamma v_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-1}.

With
v_{\varepsilon}(0)=1
, the right-hand side is initially positive. Hence
(r+\varepsilon)^{d-1}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
is strictly increasing. With
v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have

(4.4)

v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}>0\hskip 5.0pt\text{ and }v_{\varepsilon}>1\text{ for }r>0.

In particular, we have

R_{\varepsilon}=+\infty.

With (
4.4
), it follows from (
4.3
) that

v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}<\gamma\text{ on }(0,+\infty).

As a consequence, we have

0<v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(r)<\gamma r,\text{ and }\hskip 5.0pt1<v_{\varepsilon}(r)<1+\frac{1}{2}\gamma r^{2}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ for }r>0.

This gives enough compactness of the family
\{v_{\varepsilon}\}
. We can extract a subsequential limit,
v
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This limit
v
solves (
4.1
), together with

v^{\prime}\geq 0,\text{ and }\hskip 5.0ptv\geq 1\text{ on }[0,+\infty).

It remains to establish the bounds on the difference

D:=v-u_{\mathrm{rad}}.

We first show that
D>0
on
(0,+\infty)
.

Suppose not, with
D(0)>0
, we find
R<+\infty
such that

(4.5)

D>0\text{ on }[0,R),\text{ and }D(R)=0.

Using the equations for
v
and
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
(see (
4.2
) and Definition
2.2
), we have

\Delta D=\gamma(v^{\gamma-1}-u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-1})\geq\gamma(\gamma-1)u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}(v-u_{\mathrm{rad}})\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }B_{R}\backslash\{0\}.

For the inequality, we used the convexity of
t\mapsto t^{\gamma-1}
and our assumption that
v>u_{\mathrm{rad}}
in
B_{R}
.

As a result, we have

L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}(D)\geq 0\text{ in }B_{R}\backslash\{0\},\text{ and }D=0\text{ on }\partial B_{R},

where
L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}
is the linearized operator in (
3.4
). Corollary
3.3
gives

(4.6)

D\leq 0\text{ in }B_{R}\backslash\{0\},

contradicting our assumption (
4.5
). This is the only place where we need the assumption
\Delta(d,\gamma)\geq 0
.

Therefore, we have

v-u_{\mathrm{rad}}=D>0\text{ on }[0,+\infty).

We now show the upper bound
D\leq 1
on
[0,+\infty)
.

Since
v
and
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
both solve the ODE in (
4.1
), with
D=v-u>0
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have

r^{1-d}[r^{d-1}D^{\prime}]^{\prime}=D^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}D^{\prime}=\gamma(v^{\gamma-1}-u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-1})<0\text{ on }(0,+\infty).

With
D^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have

D^{\prime}<0\text{ on }(0,+\infty),

which implies

v-u_{\mathrm{rad}}=D<D(0)=1\text{ on }(0,+\infty).

∎

With this, we prove the first statement in Proposition
4.1
:

Proof of one-sided minimality when
\Delta\geq 0
.

Following the notation in Definition
2.6
, we define

\Psi_{1}(x):=v(|x|)

with
v
from Proposition
4.3
. With the notations from Definition
2.2
, we have

\Psi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Properties of
v
implies

0<\Psi_{1}-u_{\mathrm{rad}}\leq 1,\text{ and }\Psi_{1}\geq 1\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
t>0
, define

\Psi_{t}(x):=t^{\beta}\Psi_{1}(x/t)

with the scaling parameter
\beta
from (
2.1
). From this definition, the continuity of
(t,x)\mapsto\Psi_{t}(x)
follows. Proposition
2.3
gives
\Psi_{t}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Moreover, we have

(\Psi_{t}-u_{\mathrm{rad}})(x)=t^{\beta}[\Psi_{1}(x/t)-u_{\mathrm{rad}}(x/t)]\in(0,t^{\beta})

and

\Psi_{t}\geq t^{\beta}\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

With these, we see that
\{\Psi_{t}\}
gives an upper foliation of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
as in Definition
2.6
.
The conclusion follows from Proposition
2.7
.
∎

4.2.
Lower foliation of the radial cone

We turn to the minimality of the radial cone
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
in Statement (2) of Proposition
4.1
. In Subsection
4.1
, we have proved its one-sided minimality from above. It remains to prove the minimality from below. With Proposition
2.9
, we need to construct a lower foliation of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
as in Definition
2.8
.

The leaves in this lower foliation arise as rescalings of the single leaf given in (
4.17
).
To construct this leaf, we perturb
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
by a supersolution to the linearized operator
L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}
in (
3.4
). One particular supersolution is the function
v
in (
4.9
). Due to the singularity in

near the zero set of

, this perturbation
U:=u_{\mathrm{rad}}-v
(see (
4.10
)) only gives meaningful information when

and

are comparable, that is, in the region
\Omega_{\eta}
as in (

). In this region, the desired subsolution property of

follows from Lemma

.

In the complement of
\Omega_{\eta}
, we build the leaf by the explicit profile
V
in (
4.13
). Its desired subsolution property is given in Lemma
4.5
.

Gluing together
U
and
V
(see (
4.10
) and (
4.13
)), we get a single leaf in the lower foliation in (
4.17
). To get the subsolution property along the interface of gluing, we need the derivatives of
U
and
V
to be ordered as in (
4.18
). This puts the constraint on
\gamma
in Statement (2) of Proposition
4.1
.

To simplify our notations, for a positive function
\varphi
, we denote the error in solving the Alt-Phillips problem (Definition
2.2
) as

(4.7)

E(\varphi):=\Delta\varphi-\gamma\varphi^{\gamma-1}.

For a small dimensional constant
\eta>0
to be chosen, the
region away from the free boundary
is defined as

(4.8)

\Omega_{\eta}:=\{u_{\mathrm{rad}}-v>\eta u_{\mathrm{rad}}\},

where
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
denotes the radial cone in (
3.1
) and

(4.9)

v(x):=|x|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}.

The
profile away from the free boundary
is taken as

(4.10)

U:=u_{\mathrm{rad}}-v.

This function satisfies

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\eta>0
, we have

(4.11)

E(U)\geq|x|^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\cdot\{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}-2d\eta^{\gamma-2}(1-\gamma)\}\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta}.

Proof.

With
u_{\mathrm{rad}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the convexity of the function
t\mapsto t^{\gamma-1}
and the definition of
\Omega_{\eta}
in (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle E(U)

\displaystyle=-\Delta v-\gamma[U^{\gamma-1}-u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-1}]

\displaystyle\geq-\Delta v+\gamma(\gamma-1)\eta^{\gamma-2}u_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\gamma-2}v\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta}.

Using the particular forms of
u_{\mathrm{rad}}
,
L_{u_{\mathrm{rad}}}
and
v
, we conclude

\displaystyle E(U)

\displaystyle\geq|x|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}-2}\cdot\{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}-(1-\gamma)\beta(d+\beta-2)\eta^{\gamma-2})\}

\displaystyle\geq|x|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}-2}\cdot\{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}-2d\eta^{\gamma-2}(1-\gamma)\}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ in }\Omega_{\eta}.

since the scaling parameter
\beta
from (
2.1
) satisfies

\beta<2\text{ for $\gamma\in(0,1)$.}

∎

In particular, once
\eta>0
is chosen, the profile
U
gives a subsolution in
\Omega_{\eta}
when
\gamma
is close to
1
.

We now turn to the
region near the origin
.
By definition, we have

(4.12)

\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\Omega_{\eta}=\overline{B_{R_{\eta}}}\text{ for some }0<R_{\eta}<+\infty,

where
\Omega_{\eta}
is given in (
4.8
).

In
B_{R_{\eta}}
, we build the profile of the form

(4.13)

V(x):=A[(|x|-r_{\eta})_{+}]^{\beta},

where the coefficient
A
satisfies
A^{2-\gamma}=2/\beta^{2}
, and
r_{\eta}\in(0,R_{\eta})
is chosen such that

(4.14)

V=\eta u_{\mathrm{rad}}=U\text{ on }\partial B_{R_{\eta}}.

Or equivalently, we have

(4.15)

A(R_{\eta}-r_{\eta})^{\beta}=\eta c_{\mathrm{rad}}R_{\eta}^{\beta},

where
c_{\mathrm{rad}}
is the coefficient in (
3.1
).

This function
V
gives a subsolution as in Definition
2.2
:

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
0<\eta\leq\frac{1}{4}
, we have

V\in\mathcal{S}^{+}_{\gamma}(B_{R_{\eta}}),\text{ and }V\leq u_{\mathrm{rad}}\text{ in }B_{R_{\eta}}.

Proof.

For our choice of
A
, we have, in
\{V>0\}
,

\Delta V=A\beta(\beta-1)[|x|-r_{\eta}]^{\beta-2}+\frac{d-1}{|x|}A\beta[|x|-r_{\eta}]^{\beta-1}\geq A\beta(\beta-1)[|x|-r_{\eta}]^{\beta-2}=\gamma V^{\gamma-1}.

A direct computation gives
|\nabla V|^{2}/V^{\gamma}=2
along
\partial B_{r_{\eta}}=\partial\{V>0\}.

Combining all these, we have

u_{\mathrm{rad}}-V\geq u_{\mathrm{rad}}(R_{\eta})\cdot[(1-\eta^{\frac{1}{\beta}})^{\beta}-\eta].

With

As the result, the right-hand side of (

We define an

as

Lemma 4.6

For

Proof.

For our choice of
\eta
and
\gamma

4.4

By Lemma

With the definition of

and the homogeneities of the functions, we have

6.7
).

Unfortunately, this is no longer true once we leave the domain
\Omega
in (
6.15
). To go beyond
\Omega
, we extend
U
in each horizontal slice by an explicit quadratic profile
V
in (
6.23
). In Subsection
6.3
, we show that this profile
V
has the desired properties (see Lemma
6.10
, Lemma
6.11
and Lemma
6.12
). This is the most technical portion of this section. Here we rely on information of the trace of
U
on
\partial\Omega
(see Lemma

and Lemma

With all these preparations, we construct the lower foliation in Subsection

and give the proof of Proposition

.

6.1.
Linearized operator around the cone

We work with
\gamma\in(0,1)
close to
1
. The space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
is decomposed as in (
5.1
), equipped with the coordinates
(r,\theta)
in (
5.6
).

For brevity, we
use
u
to denote the cone
u_{\mathrm{as}}
from Proposition
5.4
. Its
trace on the sphere
is denoted by
\bar{u}
, that is,

(6.1)

u(r,\theta):=u_{\mathrm{as}}(r,\theta)=:r^{2}\bar{u}(\theta).

Basic properties of
\bar{u}
are listed in Lemma
5.11
. In particular, we have

(6.2)

\{\bar{u}>0\}=(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0}),\text{ and }\{u>0\}=\{r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\theta\in(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0})\},

where
\theta_{0}
is the opening of the contact set in Lemma
5.11
. It satisfies

\theta_{0}\sim_{d}\kappa^{\frac{1}{d-2}},

where
\kappa
is the small parameter in (
5.7
).

As part of the main result from Section
5
, this cone
u
solves the transformed Euler-Lagrange equation (
5.3
). The
linearized operator around
u
is given by

(6.3)

L_{u}(v):=\Delta v+(\frac{\beta}{2}-1)[\frac{2\nabla u}{u}\cdot\nabla v-\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}v].

For a function of the form
v(r,\theta)=f(r)\bar{v}(\theta)
, this operator splits into two pieces

(6.4)

L_{u}(v)=\frac{f}{r^{2}}\cdot L_{\bar{u}}(\bar{v})+[f^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-\kappa}{r}f^{\prime}+\kappa\frac{f}{r^{2}}]\cdot\bar{v},

where
\kappa
is from (
5.7
), and
L_{\bar{u}}
is the
linearized operator around

\bar{u}
, defined as

(6.5)

L_{\bar{u}}(\bar{v}):=\bar{v}^{\prime\prime}+[(d-1)\cot(\theta)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}]\bar{v}^{\prime}+\frac{\kappa}{4}(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}})^{2}\bar{v}.

We give a
positive supersolution

v
to the linearized operator
L_{u}
in
\{u>0\}=\{r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\theta\in(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0})\}
(see (
6.2
)). This supersolution is of the form

(6.6)

v:=r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bar{v}(\theta)

in our coordinate system (
5.6
).

The function
\bar{v}
is constructed piecewisely on
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
,
(\theta_{1},\pi-\theta_{1})
and
(\pi-\theta_{1},\pi-\theta_{0})
, where

(6.7)

\theta_{1}:=(1+\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}})\theta_{0}.

We define
\bar{v}
as

(6.8)

\bar{v}:=\begin{cases}\sigma(\theta-\theta_{0})^{\alpha}&\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{1}],\\
\sin^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta)&\text{ on }(\theta_{1},\pi-\theta_{1}],\\
\sigma(\pi-\theta_{0}-\theta)^{\alpha}&\text{ on }(\pi-\theta_{1},\pi-\theta_{0}),\end{cases}

where
\alpha>0
is a small parameter to be chosen (see Remark
6.3
), and
\sigma
satisfies

\sigma(\theta_{1}-\theta_{0})^{\alpha}=\sin^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\theta_{1}).

Remark 6.2
.

The function
\bar{v}
is Lipschitz in compact subsets of
(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0})
.
Once a uniform estimate on
\bar{v}^{\prime}
has been established on
(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0})\backslash\{\theta_{1},\pi-\theta_{1}\}
, it is understood in the weak sense over the entire interval.

The second derivative
\bar{v}^{\prime\prime}
, however, involves a singular measure at
\theta_{1}
and
\pi-\theta_{1}
. It is crucial that this measure is negative.

Remark 6.3
.

We need several small parameters, in particular, the parameter
\alpha
in the definition of
\bar{v}
(see (
6.8
)), and the small parameter
\kappa
in (
5.7
).

The parameter
\alpha
is chosen in Lemma
6.4
to get the lower bound for
(\bar{u}^{\prime}/\bar{u}-\bar{v}^{\prime}/\bar{v})
(see (
6.10
)). Given this choice of
\alpha
, the parameter
\kappa
is chosen small in the remaining part of this section. The link between
\kappa
and
\gamma
in (
5.7
) gives
\gamma_{d}^{3}
in Proposition
6.1
.

We begin with some basic estimates on
\bar{v}
in (
6.8
):

Lemma 6.4
.

For
d\geq 3
and dimensionally small
\alpha
and
\kappa
, we have

|\bar{v}^{\prime}/\bar{v}|\leq\mathcal{O}((\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\cos(\theta)),\hskip 5.0pt(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})\sim_{d}(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\cos(\theta)\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\pi/2],

and

|(\bar{v}^{\prime}/\bar{v})^{\prime}|\leq\mathcal{O}((\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2})\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\cup(\theta_{1},\pi/2].

The big-O notation is from Remark
1.4
. The angle
\theta_{0}
is from Lemma
5.11
. The angle
\theta_{1}
is from the definition of
\bar{v}
in (
6.7
) and (
6.8
).

Remark 6.5
.

We often state results for the interval
(0,\pi/2]
. By even symmetry, similar properties hold on the entire
(0,\pi)
.

Proof.

It is elementary to verify the bounds on
|\bar{v}^{\prime}/\bar{v}|
and
|(\bar{v}^{\prime}/\bar{v})^{\prime}|
. The upper bound for
(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})
follows from the definition of
\bar{v}
in (
6.8
) and Lemma
5.11
. Below we focus on the lower bound for
(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})
.

We first address the lower bound on
(\theta_{0},\theta_{m}]
, where
\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{m}
are the angles in Lemma
5.11
, satisfying

(6.9)

\theta_{0}\sim_{d}\kappa^{\frac{1}{d-2}}\text{ and }\theta_{m}/\kappa^{\frac{1}{d-2}}\gg 1.

On this interval, Lemma
5.11
gives a dimensional constant
c_{d}>0
such that

\bar{u}^{\prime}(\theta)/\bar{u}(\theta)\geq c_{d}(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{m}].

With (
6.7
) and (
6.9
), we see that
\theta_{1}<\theta_{m}
.
The definition of
\bar{v}
in (
6.8
) gives

\bar{v}^{\prime}(\theta)/\bar{v}(\theta)=\alpha(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}

on
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
.
Thus

(6.10)

(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})(\theta)\geq(c_{d}-\alpha)(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}c_{d}(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})

if
\alpha
is small.

On
(\theta_{1},\theta_{m}]
we have
\bar{v}^{\prime}\leq 0
(see (
6.8
)). This implies

(6.11)

(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})(\theta)\geq(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}})(\theta)\geq c_{d}(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}.

On
(\theta_{m},\pi/2)
, Lemma
5.11
gives
\bar{u}^{\prime}\geq 0
. Using (
6.8
), we have

(6.12)

(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})\geq-\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}}=\frac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}(\theta)\cos(\theta)\geq c_{d}(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\cos(\theta).

For the last inequality, we used
\theta_{m}=\mathcal{T}_{d}\theta_{0}
with
\mathcal{T}_{d}\gg 1
as in Lemma
5.11
.

Combining (
6.10
), (
6.11
) and (
6.12
), we get the lower bound on
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\cup(\theta_{1},\pi/2]
. In the weak sense, the bound holds over the entire interval. See Remark
6.2
.
∎

The supersolution property of
v
is given below. To absorb errors from the nonlinearity, we add an extra term to the linearized operator.

Lemma 6.6
.

Under the same assumptions as in Lemma
6.4
, we have

L_{u}(v)+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}v/u\leq 0\text{ in }\{u>0\}.

Here
v
is the function in (
6.6
), and the operator
L_{u}
is given in (
6.3
).

Proof.

From (
6.2
), the positive set
\{u>0\}
is given by

\{u>0\}=\{r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\theta\in(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0})\}.

Due to the piecewise nature of
\bar{v}
in (
6.8
), we divide the proof into several regions, firstly on on
(\theta_{1},\pi/2)
, then on
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
, and lastly at
\theta_{1}
.

Step 1: Equation for
v
in
\{r>0,\theta\in(\theta_{1},\pi/2)\}
.

On
(\theta_{1},\pi/2)
, the definition of
\bar{v}
in (
6.8
) gives

(6.13)

L_{\bar{u}}(\bar{v})/\bar{v}=[-\frac{d-1}{2}+\frac{3}{4}]\sin^{-2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\kappa}{4}[\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}\sin^{-1}(\theta)\cos(\theta)+(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}})^{2}],

where
L_{\bar{u}}
is the linearized operator around
\bar{u}
from (
6.5
).

With Lemma
5.11
and (
6.7
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}}\sin^{-1}(\theta)\cos(\theta)

\displaystyle=\sin^{-2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\theta)\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{8}})+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{8}})\hskip 5.0pt\text{ on }(\theta_{1},\pi/2),

and

(\frac{\bar{u}^{\prime}}{\bar{u}})^{2}=\sin^{-2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\theta)\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{4}})+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{4}})\hskip 5.0pt\text{ on }(\theta_{1},\pi/2).

Putting these into (
6.13
), we have

L_{\bar{u}}(\bar{v})/\bar{v}\leq[-\frac{1}{4}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{1-\frac{1}{4}})]\sin^{-2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{1-\frac{1}{4}})\text{ on }(\theta_{1},\pi/2).

With Lemma
5.11
, we have

\bar{u}^{-1}=\sin^{-2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\theta)\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{4}})+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{4}})\text{ on }(\theta_{1},\pi/2).

As a result, we have

(6.14)

\displaystyle L_{\bar{u}}(\bar{v})/\bar{v}+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}/\bar{u}

\displaystyle\leq[-\frac{1}{4}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})]\sin^{-2}(\theta)\cos^{2}(\theta)+\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{4}})\text{ on }(\theta_{1},\pi/2).

With (
6.4
), (
6.6
) and (
6.14
), we have, in
\{r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\theta\in(\theta_{1},\pi/2)\}
,

L_{u}(v)+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{v}{u}\leq r^{-\frac{5}{2}}\bar{v}\cdot\{-\frac{1}{4}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{4}})\}\leq 0,

for
\kappa
small.

Step 2: Equation for
v
in
\{r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\theta\in(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\}
.

With similar ideas as in Step 1, we have

L_{\bar{u}}(\bar{v})/\bar{v}+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}/\bar{u}\leq(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2}[\alpha(\alpha-1+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{4}}))+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}})]\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{1}).

As a result, we have, in
\{r>0,\hskip 5.0pt\theta\in(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\}
,

L_{u}(v)+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{v}{u}\leq r^{-\frac{5}{2}}\bar{v}\cdot\{(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2}[-\frac{1}{4}\alpha+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{4}})]-\frac{3}{4}+\kappa/2\}\leq 0

if
\kappa
is small.

Step 3: The conclusion.

We have established the desired inequality in
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\cup(\theta_{1},\pi/2)
. At
\theta=\theta_{1}
, the trace
\bar{v}
forms a concave corner. The desired inequality holds in the viscosity sense.

Similar results can be extended to
(\pi/2,\pi-\theta_{0})
by symmetry as in Remark
6.5
.
∎

6.2.
Region away from the free boundary

We begin the construction of a leaf in the lower foliation. In this subsection, we focus on the
region away from the free boundary
, defined as

(6.15)

\Omega:=\{u-v\geq\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}}u\}.

The functions
u
and
v
are from (
6.1
) and (
6.6
).
In this region
\Omega
, the leaf is taken as

(6.16)

U:=u-v.

For a non-negative function
\varphi
, we define its error in solving (
5.3
) as
E(\varphi)
, that is,

(6.17)

E(\varphi):=\Delta\varphi+(\frac{\beta}{2}-1)\frac{|\nabla\varphi|^{2}}{\varphi}-\frac{d+\beta-2}{d}\chi_{\{\varphi>0\}}.

The profile in (
6.16
) has the desired subsolution property in
\Omega
:

Lemma 6.7
.

For
d\geq 3
and
\alpha,\kappa
dimensionally small, we have

E(U)\geq 0\text{ in }\Omega.

Recall the parameter
\alpha
in the definition of
\bar{v}
(
6.8
). This parameter has been fixed in Lemma
6.4
. The parameter
\kappa
is from (
5.7
).

Proof.

By definition of
U
in (
6.16
), we have

(6.18)

E(U)=-L_{u}(v)+(\frac{\beta}{2}-1)[h(X+Z)-h(X)-\nabla h(X)\cdot Z],

where
h:\mathbb{R}^{d+2}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
h(X^{\prime},x_{d+2}):=|X^{\prime}|^{2}/x_{d+2}
when
x_{d+2}>0
, and
X=(\nabla u,u)
,
Z=-(\nabla v,v)
.

The homogeneity of
u
in (
6.1
) and Lemma
5.11
lead to

|\nabla u|^{2}/u=4\bar{u}+(\bar{u}^{\prime})^{2}/\bar{u}\leq\mathcal{O}(1).

Lemma
B.1
gives

(6.19)

|h(X+Z)-h(X)-\nabla h(X)\cdot Z|\leq C_{d}(\frac{v}{u})^{2}[\frac{u}{u-v}+(\frac{u}{u-v})^{2}]+\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{u}(1+\frac{u}{u-v}).

With (
6.15
), the first term is bounded by
\frac{v}{u}\cdot\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{4}})
in
\Omega
.

For the second term, we use the homogeneity of
v
and Lemma
6.4
to get

\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}r^{-2}+r^{-2}(\frac{\bar{v}^{\prime}}{\bar{v}})^{2}\leq r^{-2}\mathcal{O}((\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2}).

Together with Lemma
5.11
, this implies

\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}/u}{(v/u)^{2}}=\frac{|\nabla v|^{2}}{v^{2}}\cdot u\leq\bar{u}\mathcal{O}((\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2})\leq\mathcal{O}(1).

Thus the second term in (
6.19
) is bounded by the first term.

Consequently, we have

|h(X+Z)-h(X)-\nabla h(X)\cdot Z|\leq\frac{v}{u}\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-\frac{1}{4}})\text{ in }\Omega.

Putting this into (
6.18
), we get

E(U)\geq-L_{u}(v)-\frac{v}{u}\cdot\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{3}{4}})\geq-[L_{u}(v)+\kappa^{1/2}\frac{v}{u}]\geq 0\text{ in }\Omega

if
\kappa
is small. The last inequality follows from Lemma
6.6
.
∎

In Subsection
6.3
, we extend the profile
U
in (
6.16
) to the complement of
\Omega
in (
6.15
). To get a subsolution, we need information about the trace of
U
on the boundary of
\Omega
, denoted as

(6.20)

\Gamma:=\partial\Omega=\{U=\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}}u\}.

On
\Gamma
, we have
(1-\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}})u=v
, that is,

(1-\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}})r^{2}\bar{u}(\theta)=r^{-1/2}\bar{v}(\theta)

using (
6.1
) and (
6.8
).

As a result, the boundary
\Gamma
is described by a graph
\theta\in(\theta_{0},\pi-\theta_{0})\mapsto R(\theta)
, satisfying the relation

(6.21)

R^{5/2}(\theta)=\frac{1}{1-\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}}}\bar{v}(\theta)/\bar{u}(\theta).

Equivalently, the boundary
\Gamma
can be described by its distance to the
y
-axis at each height
y
, namely,
y\in(-\infty,+\infty)\mapsto L(y)
, satisfying

(6.22)

y=-R(\theta)\cos(\theta),\text{ and }L(y)=R(\theta)\sin(\theta).

The change of variable between these two descriptions satisfies the following bounds. Due to its technical nature, the proof is postponed to Appendix
C
.

Lemma 6.8
.

The quantities in (
6.21
) and (
6.22
) satisfy

0\leq\frac{d\theta}{dy}\leq\mathcal{O}(1/R),\hskip 5.0pt|\frac{dL}{dy}|\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\pi/2),

and

|\frac{d^{2}\theta}{dy^{2}}|\leq(\frac{d\theta}{dy})^{2}\mathcal{O}((\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}),\hskip 5.0pt|\frac{d^{2}L}{dy^{2}}|\leq R(\frac{d\theta}{dy})^{2}\mathcal{O}(\theta(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2})\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\cup(\theta_{1},\pi/2).

Moreover, for
\eta>0
dimensionally small, we have

|\frac{dL}{dy}|\geq c_{d}\eta^{-1}R\frac{d\theta}{dy}\cdot\theta(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}\text{ on }(\theta_{0},(1+\eta)\theta_{0})

for a dimensional constant
c_{d}>0
.

Recall
\theta_{0}
from (
6.2
) and
\theta_{1}
from (
6.7
).

Along
\Gamma
, our profile
U
in (
6.16
) satisfies the following lemma. The proof is postponed to Appendix
C
.

Lemma 6.9
.

The function
y\mapsto\sqrt{U}(L(y),y)
satisfies

|\frac{d\sqrt{U}}{dy}|\leq R\frac{d\theta}{dy}\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{16}})\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\pi/2),

and

|\frac{d^{2}\sqrt{U}}{dy^{2}}|\leq R(\frac{d\theta}{dy})^{2}\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{\frac{1}{16}}(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1})\text{ on }(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\cup(\theta_{1},\pi/2].

6.3.
Region near the free boundary

In this subsection, we extend the profile
U
in (
6.16
) to the complement of
\Omega
in (
6.15
). For each horizontal slice with fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
, define

(6.23)

V(x,y):=[(|x|-L(y)+\sqrt{U(L(y),y)})_{+}]^{2}\hskip 5.0pt\text{ for }|x|\leq L(y),

where
(L(y),y)
is the description of
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
in (
6.22
).

Note that by this definition, we have

(6.24)

V=U\text{ on }\Gamma.

We first verify the desired subsolution property of
V
in the interior of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\Omega
, denoted as

(6.25)

\mathcal{U}:=\mathrm{Int}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\backslash\Omega)=\{(x,y):\hskip 5.0pty\in\mathbb{R},\text{ and }|x|<L(y)\}.

With the notation in (
6.17
), we have

Lemma 6.10
.

For
d\geq 3
and dimensionally small
\alpha
and
\kappa
, we have

E(V)\geq 0\text{ in }\mathcal{U}.

Recall the parameter
\alpha
in the definition of
\bar{v}
(
6.8
). The parameter
\kappa
is from (
5.7
).

Proof.

Step 1: Preparations.

By definition, we have
|\nabla V|=0
on
\partial\{V>0\}
. As a result, it suffices to verify the inequality in

\tilde{\mathcal{U}}:=\{V>0\}\cap\mathcal{U}.

Inside
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}
, the definition of
V
in (
6.23
) gives

(6.26)

\frac{\partial}{\partial|x|}V=2(|x|-L+\sqrt{U})\geq 0,\text{ and }\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial|x|^{2}}V=2.

As a result, we have

\Delta V=2+\frac{d-1}{|x|}\frac{\partial}{\partial|x|}V+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}V\geq 2+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}V\text{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{U}}.

This implies

(6.27)

E(V)>1+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}V+(\frac{\beta}{2}-1)|\nabla V|^{2}/V\text{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{U}},

where we used
\beta<2
for
\gamma<1
(see (
2.1
)).

Below we estimate the two non-constant terms on the right-hand side.

Step 2: Estimate on
|\nabla V|^{2}/V
.

Inside
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}
, we see from (
6.26
) that

(\frac{\partial}{\partial|x|}V)^{2}/V=4.

For the derivative in the
y
-variable, we have

(6.28)

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}V=2\sqrt{V}\frac{d}{dy}(-L+\sqrt{U}).

With Lemma
6.8
and Lemma
6.9
, we see that
\frac{d}{dy}(-L+\sqrt{U})
is bounded by a dimensional constant. As a result, we have

(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}V)^{2}/V\leq\mathcal{O}(1).

Summarize, we have

(6.29)

|\nabla V|^{2}/V\leq\mathcal{O}(1)\text{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{U}}.

Step 3: Estimate of
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}V
on
[(1+\eta)\theta_{0},\pi/2]
.

For a small
\eta>0
as in Lemma
6.8
, in this step we give the estimate on
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}V
on
[(1+\eta)\theta_{0},\pi/2]
.

Direct computation gives

(6.30)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}V=2[\frac{d}{dy}(-L+\sqrt{U})]^{2}+2\sqrt{V}\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}(-L+\sqrt{U})\text{ in }\tilde{\mathcal{U}}.

With
V\leq U(L(y),y)=\kappa^{\frac{1}{8}}R^{2}\bar{u}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}
(see (
6.20
)), Lemma
5.11
and Lemma
6.8
imply, on
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\cup(\theta_{1},\pi/2)
,

\sqrt{V}|\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}L|\leq\sqrt{U}|\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}L|=\kappa^{1/16}R\bar{u}^{1/2}\cdot R(\frac{d\theta}{dy})^{2}\mathcal{O}(\theta(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-2})\leq\kappa^{1/16}\mathcal{O}(\theta(\theta-\theta_{0})^{-1}).

In particular, on
[(1+\eta)\theta_{0},\pi/2)
, we have

\sqrt{V}|\frac{d^{2}}{dy^{2}}L|\leq\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{1/16}).

Similarly, we have

.

For

and

The last inequality follows from the bound on
\bar{u}
in Lemma

in (

) and the bound on

in (

As a result, in

Note that positive constants are strict supersolutions to (
5.3
), Lemma
7.16
gives
E(\Psi_{1})\leq 0
.
∎

The leaf in (
7.33
) generates an upper foliation. With this, we give the proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

For
t>0
, define

\Psi_{t}(x,y)=t^{2}\Psi_{1}(x/t,y/t).

With similar ideas as in the proof of Proposition
6.1
, we have
\Psi_{t}>u
in
\overline{\{u>0\}}
and

in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. Continuity of the map
(x,t)\mapsto\Psi_{t}(x)
and the map

With our definition (

), there is a constant

such that

in
B_{1}
. This implies
\Psi_{t}\geq\eta t^{2}
in

. Thus

Finally, given a compact subset

for

This gives the convergence of

to

∎

Combining the main results in Section

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

and the estimate of the measure of its contact set were given in Proposition

With Theorem

Appendix A
Expansion for solutions to second order ODEs

In this appendix, we collect some technicalities concerning several ODEs.

A.1.
Equation near the equator for the axially symmetric cone

For a given function
\rho
on
(0,\pi/2)
, we study the behavior of solutions to

(A.1)

L_{p}u=\rho\text{ on }(0,\pi/2),\text{ and }u^{\prime}(\pi/2)=u(\pi/2)=0,

where the operator
L_{p}
is given in (
5.13
).

One of the fundamental solutions to the homogeneous ODE is given by
V_{0}
from (
5.15
) with properties listed in Lemma
5.5
.

The other fundamental solution, denoted by
\varphi
, solves

(A.2)

L_{p}\varphi=0\text{ on }(0,\pi/2),\text{ and }\varphi^{\prime}(\pi/2)=1,\varphi(\pi/2)=0.

Liouville’s formula gives

(A.3)

\varphi^{\prime}V_{0}-\varphi V_{0}^{\prime}=\sin^{-d+1+\kappa}(\theta)\text{ on }(0,\pi/2),

that is,

(A.4)

(\varphi/V_{0})^{\prime}=\sin^{-d+1+\kappa}(\theta)/V_{0}^{2}\text{ on }(0,\pi/2).

As a consequence, we have the following properties of
\varphi
:

Lemma A.1
.

For
d\geq 3
and
\kappa\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, we have

|\varphi|(\theta)\leq\mathcal{O}(\theta^{-d+2+\kappa})\text{ and }\hskip 5.0pt|\varphi^{\prime}|(\theta)\leq\mathcal{O}(\theta^{-d+1+\kappa})\text{ on }(0,\pi/2).

Moreover, we have the following expansion

\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{d}\sin^{-d+1}(\theta)(1+\kappa\mathcal{O}(|\log(\theta)|)+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2}))\text{ on }(0,\pi/4).

Recall the big-O notation from Remark
1.4
.

Proof.

Step 1: Upper bounds on
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
.

Note that there is no singularity in the operator
L_{p}
on
[\pi/4,\pi/2]
, standard analysis gives

|(\varphi^{\prime},\varphi)|\leq C_{d}\text{ on }[\pi/4,\pi/2].

Between
0
and
\pi/4
, we integrate (
A.4
) to obtain

\frac{\varphi(\theta)}{V_{0}(\theta)}=\frac{\varphi(\pi/4)}{V_{0}(\pi/4)}-\int_{\theta}^{\pi/4}\sin^{-d+1+\kappa}(\tau)V_{0}^{-2}(\tau)d\tau.

From Lemma
5.5
, we see that
V_{0}
is bounded away from
0
and infinity on
[0,\pi/4]
, the desired bound on
\varphi
follows.

With this, the bound on
\varphi^{\prime}
follows from (
A.3
).

Step 2: The expansion of
\varphi^{\prime}
.

With the bound on
\varphi
in
Step 1
and the bound on
V_{0}^{\prime}
in Lemma
5.5
, we can use (
A.3
) to get

\varphi^{\prime}V_{0}=\sin^{-d+1+\kappa}(\theta)[1+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2})].

With
|\sin^{\kappa}(\theta)-1|=\kappa\mathcal{O}(|\log(\theta)|)
and the regularity of
V_{0}
, we conclude

\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)=-\frac{1}{d}\sin^{-d+1}(\theta)(1+\kappa\mathcal{O}(|\log(\theta)|)+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{2})).

∎

By the method of variation of parameters, we have the following representation:

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose that
u
is a solution to (
A.1
), then

u(\theta)=\varphi(\theta)\alpha(\theta)+V_{0}(\theta)\beta(\theta),\text{ and }u^{\prime}(\theta)=\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\alpha(\theta)+V_{0}^{\prime}(\theta)\beta(\theta),

where

\alpha(\theta)=-\int_{\theta}^{\pi/2}\rho(\tau)V_{0}(\tau)\sin^{d-1-\kappa}(\tau)d\tau

and

\beta(\theta)=\int_{\theta}^{\pi/2}\rho(\tau)\varphi(\tau)\sin^{d-1-\kappa}(\tau)d\tau.

A.2.
Equation near the south pole for the axially symmetric cone

Given
\lambda\in(0,\pi/2)
and a function
\rho
, we study solutions to

(A.5)

Lu=\rho\text{ on }(0,\pi/2),\hskip 5.0pt\text{ and }u^{\prime}(\lambda)=u(\lambda)=0,

where the operator
L
is given by

Lu:=u^{\prime\prime}+(d-1)\cot(\theta)u^{\prime}.

The constant function
1
is a fundamental solution to the homogeneous equation
Lu=0
. The other fundamental solution can be taken as

(A.6)

\varphi(\theta):=\int_{\theta}^{\pi/2}\sin^{-d+1}(\tau)d\tau\hskip 5.0pt\text{ with }\hskip 5.0pt\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)=-\sin^{-d+1}(\theta).

With the method of variation of parameters, we have

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose that
u
is a solution to (
A.5
), then

u(\theta)=\varphi(\theta)\alpha(\theta)+\beta(\theta),\text{ and }u^{\prime}(\theta)=\varphi^{\prime}(\theta)\alpha(\theta),

where

\alpha(\theta)=-\int_{\lambda}^{\theta}\rho(\tau)\sin^{d-1}(\tau)d\tau

and

\beta(\theta)=\int_{\lambda}^{\theta}\rho(\tau)\varphi(\tau)\sin^{d-1}(\tau)d\tau.

Appendix B
Linear expansion of a nonlinearity

The nonlinearity we face in several equations is given by
h:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

h(X)=h(X^{\prime},x_{d+1})=\frac{|X^{\prime}|^{2}}{x_{d+1}}\text{ for }x_{d+1}>0.

Direct computation gives

\nabla h(X)=(2X^{\prime}/x_{d+1},-|X^{\prime}|^{2}/x_{d+1}^{2}).

We bound the error in the linear expansion:

Lemma B.1
.

Given a constant
H>0
, for
X\in\{h\leq H\}
, we have

\displaystyle|h(X+Z)-h(X)-\nabla h(X)\cdot Z|

\displaystyle\leq H\cdot(\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}})^{2}[\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}+(\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}})^{2}]

\displaystyle+\frac{|Z^{\prime}|^{2}}{x_{d+1}}(1+\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}})

as long as
x_{d+1}>0
and
x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}>0.

Proof.

Direct expansion gives

\displaystyle h(X+Z)-h(X)-\nabla h(X)\cdot Z

\displaystyle=|X^{\prime}|^{2}[\frac{1}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}-\frac{1}{x_{d+1}}+\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}^{2}}]

\displaystyle+|Z^{\prime}|^{2}[\frac{1}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}]

\displaystyle+2X^{\prime}\cdot Z^{\prime}[\frac{-z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}(x_{d+1}+z_{d+1})}].

We deal with each term on the right-hand side.

For the first term, we note that

\frac{1}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}-\frac{1}{x_{d+1}}+\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}^{2}}=\frac{z_{d+1}^{2}}{x_{d+1}^{2}(x_{d+1}+z_{d+1})}.

Thus the first term can be bounded as

\displaystyle||X^{\prime}|^{2}[\frac{1}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}-\frac{1}{x_{d+1}}+\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}^{2}}]|

\displaystyle=\frac{|X^{\prime}|^{2}}{x_{d+1}}(\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}})^{2}\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}

\displaystyle\leq H(\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}})^{2}\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}

by our assumption on
X
.

The second term is

|Z^{\prime}|^{2}[\frac{1}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}]=\frac{|Z^{\prime}|^{2}}{x_{d+1}}\cdot\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}}.

For the last term, we note

\displaystyle|2X^{\prime}\cdot Z^{\prime}[\frac{-z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}(x_{d+1}+z_{d+1})}]|

\displaystyle=2|\frac{X^{\prime}}{x_{d+1}^{1/2}}\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}}(\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}})\cdot\frac{Z^{\prime}}{x_{d+1}^{1/2}}|

\displaystyle\leq|\frac{X^{\prime}}{x_{d+1}^{1/2}}\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}}(\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}})|^{2}+|\frac{Z^{\prime}}{x_{d+1}^{1/2}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq H(\frac{z_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}})^{2}(\frac{x_{d+1}}{x_{d+1}+z_{d+1}})^{2}+\frac{|Z^{\prime}|^{2}}{x_{d+1}}.

Putting all these back into the first equation, we get the desired bound.
∎

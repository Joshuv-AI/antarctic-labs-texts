Ruptured aneurysm of the middle cerebral artery with cerebral herniation treated with Woven EndoBridge via distal radial artery access: a case report
Authors: Yi Qing, Hetai Lai, Guangsen Cheng, Zhongliang Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRuptured intracranial aneurysm (RIA) is the leading cause of spontaneous subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), with a high disability and mortality rate. This case details a patient with a ruptured aneurysm at the middle cerebral artery (MCA) bifurcation accompanied with seriously cerebral herniation successfully treated with the Woven EndoBridge (WEB) device placement via a distal transradial access.Case presentationA 51-year-old male Asian patient was admitted to Zhuhai People's Hospital due to a 6-hour consciousness impairment and left limb weakness. An emergency cranial computed tomography (CT) and computed tomography angiography (CTA) showed the patient had a ruptured aneurysm at the MCA bifurcation accompanied by a cerebral herniation. The Hunt–Hess grade was Ⅲ, the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score was 9, and the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score was 14. After obtaining informed consent from the family and discussing treatment options, the patient was successfully treated with the endovascular treatment by the WEB device placement via a distal transradial access. Intraoperative angiography showed complete deployment of the WEB in good position with aneurysm occlusion, achieving a Woven EndoBridge occlusion scale (WOS) grade of B. Subsequently, intracranial hematoma evacuation and decompressive craniectomy were immediately performed. At discharge, the patient scored 4 on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS). Six months and eighteen postoperatively, the patient returned for a follow-up examination with an mRS score of 1. Follow-up cranial CT showed absorption of the hematoma, and cerebral angiography confirmed complete aneurysm occlusion with a WOS grade of A.ConclusionsThis case demonstrates the technical feasibility of a combined strategy employing initial WEB embolization via distal transradial access followed by surgical decompression, for the management of a single patient with a critical ruptured middle cerebral artery aneurysm and cerebral herniation.Keywords: Woven EndoBridge device, Intracranial aneurysm, Ruptured aneurysm, Cerebral herniation, Case report

Introduction

The middle cerebral artery (MCA) is one of the prone sites for aneurysms, with most MCA aneurysms located at the bifurcation [1, 2]. The rupture of the aneurysm can lead to hematoma formation, which can easily induce cerebral herniation, resulting in high disability and mortality rates. This article summarizes the medical records of a patient with a ruptured aneurysm at the MCA bifurcation accompanied with seriously cerebral herniation, and analyzes the clinical efficacy of his intervention. The patient underwent an embolization using the Woven EndoBridge (WEB) device placement via a distal radial artery approach before surgical operation, and had a good recovery.

Case presentation

A 51-year-old male Asian patient was admitted to Zhuhai People's Hospital due to a 6-hour consciousness impairment and left limb weakness. His past medical history was significant for hypertension, with no history of smoking. There was no personal or family history of stroke, intracranial aneurysm, or hemorrhagic cerebrovascular disease.

Neurological examination on admission revealed a comatose state. The left pupil measured 2.5 mm and the right 4.0 mm, both with a delayed light reflex. Left-sided hemiparesis was evident (muscle strength grade 1), while the right side was not cooperative to formal testing. The right plantar reflex was extensor (Babinski sign positive). These findings corresponded to a Hunt–Hess grade III. Key admission scores and the subsequent detailed clinical timeline are summarized in Table 1.

An emergency cranial computed tomography (CT) (Fig. 1a) and computed tomography angiography (CTA) (Fig. 1b) showed that the patient had a right MCA M1 segment aneurysm (4.1 mm × 3.8 mm × 3.9 mm) and right temporal lobe cerebral hemorrhage (69 mm × 48 mm) with the formation of cerebral herniation (the displacement of the midline structure is about 11 mm), considering arterial aneurysm rupture and bleeding, bilateral subarachnoid space, multiple cerebral cisterns, and posterior horn of the left lateral ventricle hemorrhage.

After discussion with the family and obtaining consent, the patient underwent a two-stage treatment: first, endovascular embolization of the aneurysm, followed immediately by surgical hematoma evacuation and decompressive craniectomy. Intervention

The procedure was initiated via right distal transradial access. A 6F guide catheter was navigated to the right internal carotid artery (ICA). Three-dimensional rotational digital subtraction angiography (DSA) showed an aneurysm at the bifurcation of the right middle cerebral artery, measuring approximately 2.2 mm × 3.2 mm × 4.4 mm (neck x width x height) (Fig. 1c, d). Guided by Traxcess14 microguidewire (Microvention, USA), Via microcatheter (Microvention, USA) and 5F 125cm distal access catheter (Achieva Medical, China) were coaxially inserted into the right ICA. Following successful superselection of microcatheter into the aneurysm sac, the WEB (4 mm × 3 mm) was introduced and completely released, and positioned well (Fig. 2a). The aneurysm was immediately not visualized (Fig. 2b). The WOS grade was B (The Web Occlusion Scale (WOS): Grade A, complete aneurysm occlusion; Grade B, complete occlusion with recess filling; Grade C, residual neck filling; and Grade D, residual aneurysm filling [3, 4]). Final angiography confirmed satisfactory device positioning with complete sealing of the aneurysm neck. It demonstrated preserved flow in the parent artery and its major branches, with no evidence of distal embolization (Fig. 2c).

a The Woven EndoBridge (WEB) device (4 × 3 mm) was introduced and released completely, with good positioning. b The aneurysm was obscured immediately after the WEB release. c Digital subtraction angiography showed that the WEB sealed the neck of the aneurysm and adhered to the aneurysm wall, with no significant obstruction of blood flow in the upper and lower branches. d Cranial computed tomography scan after the surgery for clearance of the intracerebral hematoma and decompressive craniectomy indicated relief of the cerebral hernia and significant reduction of the hematoma

The selection of the WEB device, which avoids the need for antiplatelet therapy, coupled with distal transradial access to minimize access-site and thromboembolic risks, enabled this rapid endovascular stabilization, thereby facilitating an immediate and safer transition to intracranial hematoma evacuation and decompressive craniectomy.

Results

The patient underwent a cranial CT scan after the surgery, which showed that the patient's cerebral herniation was relieved, and the hematoma was significantly reduced (34 mm × 20 mm) (Fig. 2d). The patient's condition gradually improved after surgery and they were hospitalized for a total of 20 days. Patient's mRS score at discharge was 4. The patient returned to the hospital for a follow-up examination 6 months after surgery, the mRS score was 1, and the cranial CT scan showed that the hematoma had been absorbed (Fig. 3a). The patient underwent cerebral angiography and found that the aneurysm had healed (Fig. 3b), and the WOS grade was A. After 18 months of postoperative follow-up, CT showed a softening lesion in the right temporal lobe and changes after skull repair surgery, while DSA showed that the aneurysm had healed without recurrence, the WOS grade was A (Fig. 3c, d).

a, b At the six-month postoperative follow-up, the cranial computed tomography scan indicated absorption of the hematoma, while digital subtraction angiography (DSA) showed healing of the aneurysm. c, d At the 18-month postoperative follow-up, computed tomography and DSA showed that the hematoma and aneurysm had healed without recurrence

Discussion

The incidence rate of MCA aneurysms accounts for 22%–31.5% of the incidence rate of anterior circulation aneurysms [5]. For patients presenting with ruptured MCA aneurysms characterized by substantial hemorrhagic volume and a significant mass effect, there is a heightened risk of cerebral herniation, often reflected in elevated Hunt and Hess grades.

Management of a ruptured MCA bifurcation aneurysm with cerebral herniation requires urgent and balanced decision-making. Primary surgical clipping, though direct, carries significant risks in this setting, including intraoperative aneurysm re-rupture and difficult dissection due to high intracranial pressure and brain edema [6–8]. Prioritizing endovascular intervention embolization can reduce the difficulty and risk of aneurysm rupture during subsequent hematoma evacuation. Recent reports have identified the rupture prevention effect of acute stage stent-assisted embolization [9]. However, this approach requires dual antiplatelet therapy due to the persistent risk of thrombotic complications [10, 11]. This necessity increases the risk of rebleeding and may affect the neurosurgical procedures required after aneurysm rupture. Simple coil embolization is often characterized by technically demanding procedures, repetitive aneurysm manipulation, and extended duration, thereby limiting its application in ruptured aneurysm cases.

The WEB device offers a distinct solution to these constraints. Compared with traditional coil packing and blood flow guidance devices, it is more suitable for multi branch aneurysms. Once deployed within the aneurysm sac, the WEB device disrupts inflow at the neck, achieving occlusion while potentially reinforcing the aneurysm wall and reducing re-rupture risk [12]. The follow-up aneurysm occlusion rate of the WEB device is about 85%, and the intraoperative rupture or bleeding rate is about 3%. The safety and effectiveness have been repeatedly verified [13–16]. Given the lack of a stent or foreign material in the parent vessel following intrasaccular WEB deployment, there is no need for prolonged antiplatelet therapy which may prove the WEB device a valuable alternative for securing acutely ruptured intracranial aneurysms [17, 18]. The patient underwent immediate hematoma evacuation at the time of WEB placement without fear of antiplatelet drug. The safety and efficacy profile of WEB for ruptured aneurysms is strongly supported by recent evidence. A 2025 meta-analysis (3 prospective, 22 retrospective studies) reported the rebleeding rate was 0.33%, and the procedure-related complication rate was 12.82%, and at 12-month follow-up, adequate occlusion was achieved in 83.77% of cases, while complete occlusion was observed in 59.42%[18].

Similarly, prospective study on the use of WEB in ruptured aneurysms, CLARYS, showed that among 56 patients with acute ruptured aneurysms successfully implanted with WEB, the incidence of postoperative aneurysm rupture and rebleeding was zero. During one-year follow-up, 87% (40/46) of aneurysms achieved complete/adequate occlusion, proving that WEB can effectively prevent acute ruptured aneurysm rupture and rebleeding, and can effectively embolize aneurysms [19].

Regarding procedural access, the transradial embolization has been increasingly widely used due to its low incidence of complications at the site of entry and earlier mobilization of patients. In another retrospective study [20], 90% of patients successfully completed WEB implantation through the transradial approach without any postoperative complications, indicating that web implantation through this approach may be safe and effective for treating aneurysms. As an extension, the distal transradial approach can further reduce the rate of radial artery occlusion, minimize complications of puncture bleeding, and facilitate subsequent care.

The diagnosis and treatment novelty experience of this patient is as follows. Firstly, compared to traditional stent-assisted embolization of aneurysms, WEB is more convenient and does not require a basket of coils or double microcatheters in place, which can minimize intervention treatment time. The risk of re-rupture of bifurcation aneurysms is high, and direct craniotomy may rupture intraoperatively. Therefore, interventional embolization and hemostasis are preferred before bridging surgery. Choosing the right type of WEB can greatly reduce the difficulty of the operation and the risk of secondary rupture, and achieve ideal occlusion rate of aneurysms in long-term follow-up. Secondly, during the acute phase of aneurysm rupture, there is often a risk of developing acute thrombosis within the stent due to complications such as spasm of the parent artery and abnormal coagulation function. Generally, there is no need to use antithrombotic drugs before and after web surgery, which reduces complications such as bleeding during the perioperative period and preserves relatively normal coagulation and platelet functions, without affecting subsequent surgical treatment. Thirdly, this case is the first to use the distal transradial access WEB for the treatment of ruptured aneurysms, minimizing puncture-related complications. Compared with transfemoral access, the patient does not need to brake for a long time after surgery, reducing the risk of lower limb venous thrombosis. Compared with transradial access, the incidence of radial artery occlusion is lower, the postoperative compression time is shorter, the bleeding complications are less, and the comfort is higher.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the single-case nature of the report inherently restricts the generalizability of the findings; the results may not be extrapolated to broader populations. Second, technical success in this specific scenario (ruptured middle cerebral artery bifurcation aneurysm with cerebral herniation) does not necessarily mean that patients in the same setting have reproducible conditions for surgical success. Third, the emergent nature of the intervention may introduce bias in outcome assessment, as time-sensitive decisions could influence both procedural choices and postoperative recovery. Additionally, the wider adoption of this combined approach may be constrained by practical challenges, such as the immediate availability of the WEB device, the need for specialized operator experience, and its potentially higher cost relative to standard coiling techniques. Future multicenter studies with larger cohorts are needed to validate these preliminary observations.

Conclusion

In summary, this case demonstrates the technical feasibility of a combined strategy employing initial WEB embolization via distal transradial access followed by surgical decompression, for the management of a single patient with a critical ruptured middle cerebral artery aneurysm and cerebral herniation.

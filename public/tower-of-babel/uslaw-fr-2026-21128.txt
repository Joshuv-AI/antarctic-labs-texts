October 13, 2026.
    Pursuant to Section 19(b)(1) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 
(``Act''),\1\ and Rule 19b-4 thereunder,\2\ notice is hereby given that 
on September 29, 2026, Cboe BZX Exchange, Inc. (``Exchange'') filed 
with the Securities and Exchange Commission (``Commission'') the 
proposed rule change as described in Items I and II below, which Items 
have been prepared by the Exchange. The Exchange filed the proposal as 
a ``non-controversial'' proposed rule change pursuant to Section 
19(b)(3)(A)(iii) of the Act \3\ and Rule 19b-4(f)(6) thereunder.\4\ The 
Commission is publishing this notice to solicit comments on the 
proposed rule change from interested persons.

I. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Terms of Substance 
of the Proposed Rule Change

    Cboe BZX Exchange, Inc. (``BZX'' or the ``Exchange'') is filing 
with the Securities and Exchange Commission (``Commission'' or ``SEC'') 
a proposed rule change to amend Rule 19.3 (Criteria for Underlying 
Securities) and Rule 19.4

[[Page 65810]]

(Withdrawal of Approval of Underlying Securities) to (i) establish 
listing criteria and continued listing standards for options on shares 
of commodity-based trusts holding multiple crypto assets, (ii) update 
Rule 19.3(i)(1) to permit the listing of options on an exchange traded 
fund as defined in Rule 6c-11 under the Investment Company Act of 1940 
(``Rule 6c-11''), and (iii) make conforming amendments to remove 
individual product-specific references and update cross-references 
within the rules. The text of the proposed rule change is provided in 
Exhibit 5.
    The text of the proposed rule change is also available on the 
Commission's website (https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro.shtml), the 
Exchange's website (https://www.cboe.com/us/equities/regulation/rule_filings/bzx/), and at the principal office of the Exchange.

II. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

    In its filing with the Commission, the Exchange included statements 
concerning the purpose of and basis for the proposed rule change and

A. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement of the Purpose of, and 
Statutory Basis for, the Proposed Rule Change

1. Purpose
    The Exchange proposes to amend Rule 19.3 (Criteria for Underlying 
Securities) and Rule 19.4 (Withdrawal of Approval of Underlying 
Securities) to (i) establish listing criteria and continued listing 
standards for options on shares of commodity-based trusts holding 
multiple crypto assets, (ii) update Rule 19.3(i)(1) to permit the 
listing of options on an exchange traded fund as defined in Rule 6c-11 
under the Investment Company Act of 1940 (``Rule 6c-11''),\5\ and (iii) 
make conforming amendments to remove individual product-specific 
references and update cross-references within the rules.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ See 17 CFR 270.6c-11.

Background
    The Exchange's existing rules permit the listing and trading of 
options on various types of exchange-traded products, including options 
on shares of exchange-traded funds (``ETFs''), commodity pool ETFs, 
currency trust shares, and commodity-based trusts holding a single 
crypto asset. Specifically, Rule 19.3(i) sets forth the criteria for 
securities deemed appropriate for options trading, including Fund 
Shares that are principally traded on a national securities exchange 
and defined as an ``NMS stock'' under Rule 600 of Regulation NMS.
    Cboe Options Exchange, Inc. (``Cboe Options''), an affiliate of the 
Exchange, recently received approval from the Commission to list and 
trade options on commodity-based trusts holding multiple crypto 
assets.\6\ Specifically, Cboe Options adopted Rules 4.3.06(a)(6) and 
4.4.06(c) to establish initial and continued generic listing criteria 
for options on commodity-based trusts holding multiple crypto 
assets.\7\ Additionally, Cboe Options amended Rule 4.3.06(a)(4) to 
remove individual product-specific references for crypto asset ETPs, 
and in a separate filing \8\ amended Rule 4.3.06(a)(1) to permit the 
listing of options on ETFs as defined in Rule 6c-11.

Proposed Rule Changes
    The Exchange now proposes to adopt rules that are substantively 
identical to the Cboe Options Rules described above. Specifically, the 
Exchange proposes the following amendments.
Amendments to Rule 19.3(i)
    The Exchange proposes to amend Rule 19.3(i) to establish generic 
listing criteria for options on commodity-based trusts holding multiple 
crypto assets. Currently, Rule 19.3(i)(5) permits the listing of 
options on commodity-based trusts that hold a single crypto asset 
meeting certain requirements. The Exchange proposes to amend Rule 
19.3(i)(5) to also permit the listing of options on commodity-based 
trusts that hold multiple crypto assets, provided that each underlying 
crypto asset held by such trust satisfies the following requirements:
    i. the total global supply of each underlying crypto asset held by 
the Commodity-Based Trust has an average daily market value of at least 
$700 million over the last 12 months; and
    ii. each crypto asset held by the Commodity-Based Trust underlies a 
derivatives contract that trades on a market with which the Exchange 
has a comprehensive surveillance sharing agreement, whether directly or 
through common membership in the Intermarket Surveillance Group 
(``ISG'').
    These requirements are designed to ensure that each crypto asset 
underlying options approved for trading on the Exchange has sufficient 
market capitalization and is subject to adequate market surveillance. 
These criteria are substantively identical to those approved by the 
Commission for Cboe Options.\9\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \9\ See supra note 5 [sic]. See Cboe Rules 4.3.06(a)(6) and 
4.4.06(c).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In addition, the Exchange proposes to update Rule 19.3(i)(1) to 
permit the listing of options on exchange traded funds as defined in 
Rule 6c-11, portfolio depositary receipts, or index fund shares. 
Proposed Rule 19.3(i)(1) is substantively identical to Cboe Rule 
4.3.06(a)(1). The requirements of Rule 19.3(i)(1) are generally based 
on the generic listing standards for exchange-traded funds that pre-
dated the adoption of Rule 6c-11. The Exchange proposes to eliminate 
the language from the rule text that specifies the securities and/or 
financial instruments that the entity holds and instead provide that 
the securities deemed appropriate for options trading shall include 
shares or other securities (``Fund Shares''), including but not limited 
to Partnership Units as defined in this Rule, that are principally 
traded on a national securities exchange and are defined as an ``NMS 
stock'' under Rule 600 of Regulation NMS, and that are listed pursuant 
to generic listing standards for an exchange-traded fund as defined in 
Rule 6c-11, portfolio depositary receipts, or index fund shares.
    In 2019, the Commission adopted Rule 6c-11 to permit exchange-
traded funds that satisfy certain conditions to operate without 
obtaining an exemptive order from the Commission under the Investment 
Company Act of 1940.\10\ In 2020, the Commission approved generic 
listing standards pursuant to Rule 19b-4(e) of the Exchange Act for 
exchange-traded funds that meet the requirements

[[Page 65811]]

of Rule 6c-11 (i.e., ETF Shares).\11\ The Commission found that the ETF 
Shares generic listing standards are reasonably designed to help 
prevent fraudulent and manipulative acts and practices particularly 
because a central qualification for listing generically is ongoing 
compliance with Rule 6c-11, which requires, among other things, ETF 
Shares to prominently disclose the portfolio holdings that will form 
the basis for each calculation of net asset value per share. The 
Commission stated that it believes that such portfolio transparency 
should help prevent manipulation of the price of ETF Shares.\12\ The 
Exchange believes this same transparency rationale supports the generic 
listing of options on ETF Shares by aligning the options generic 
listing standards with the ETF Shares generic listing standards. This 
proposal simply creates a streamlined pathway for listing options on 
qualifying ETF Shares that meet the enhanced transparency standards 
under Rule 6c-11.

    The Exchange believes this proposed listing standard for options on 
ETF Shares is reasonable and will result in options that are not 
readily susceptible to manipulation in light of existing Rule 6c-11 
transparency requirements and options listing requirements.\13\ The 
Commission has determined that portfolio transparency is central to 
preventing manipulation of ETF Share prices and serves as the primary 
qualification for generic listing of ETF Shares.\14\ Where ETF Shares 
have been deemed sufficiently transparent to warrant generic listing, 
the Exchange believes the manipulation risks for both the underlying 
shares and overlying options are adequately mitigated through this 
transparency framework as options manipulation is typically achieved 
through manipulation of the underlying security.

    The Exchange also proposes conforming amendments to renumber 
certain subparagraphs and cross-references within Rule 19.3(i) and to 
remove references to specific crypto asset ETPs from Rule 19.3(i)(4) 
for products that are now captured by the generic criteria in proposed 
Rule 19.3(i)(5). Currently, Rule 19.3(i)(4) lists by name certain 
crypto asset ETPs approved for options trading. Because the proposed 
generic criteria in Rule 19.3(i)(5) would permit the listing of options 
on any commodity-based trust holding crypto assets that meet the 
specified requirements, the individual product-specific references to 
crypto asset ETPs in Rule 19.3(i)(4) are no longer necessary and are 
proposed to be removed. The Exchange also proposes to redesignate the 
existing Fund Share criteria and additional listing requirements 
currently set forth in Rule 19.3(i) as Rule 19.3(i)(6)(A) and (B) and 
Rule 19.3(i)(7), respectively, with conforming updates to the lettering 
and numbering of subparagraphs thereunder. Additionally, the Exchange 
proposes to amend Rule 19.3(i)(7)(i) to add ``ETF Shares'' as an 
additional category of generic listing standards, consistent with Cboe 
Options Rule 4.3, Interpretation and Policy .06(c)(1) and the 
amendments to Rule 19.3(i)(1), and to remove the reference to ``series 
of'' portfolio depositary receipts and index fund shares to align with 
the updated terminology throughout Rule 19.3(i). The Exchange also 
proposes to correct erroneous cross-references in Rule 19.4(g). 
Currently, Rule 19.4(g)(1) references ``Rule 19.3(i)(4)(A)'' and Rule 
19.4(g)(2) references ``Rule 19.3(i)(4)(B)''; however, no such 
subparagraphs currently exist in Rule 19.3(i). The Exchange proposes to 
correct these cross-references to reference Rule 19.3(i)(6)(A) and Rule 
19.3(i)(6)(B), respectively, which correspond to the redesignated Fund 
Share listing criteria. Cboe Options Rule 4.3.06(a)(4) similarly does 
not include individual product-specific references for crypto asset 
ETPs, instead relying on the generic listing criteria in Rule 
4.3.06(a)(6). The Exchange's proposed conforming amendments follow this 
same approach by reorganizing Rule 19.3(i) to incorporate the new 
generic listing criteria for commodity-based trusts holding multiple 
crypto assets while updating existing cross-references to maintain 
internal consistency within the rule.
Amendments to Rule 19.4(g)
    Consistent with the Approval Order and Cboe Options Rule 4.4, 
Interpretation and Policy .06(e), the Exchange proposes to amend Rule 
19.4(g) to establish continued listing standards applicable to options 
on commodity-based trusts holding multiple crypto assets. In the 
Approval Order, the Commission approved Cboe Options' adoption of 
continued listing standards for options on commodity-based trusts 
holding multiple crypto assets, finding that such standards are 
designed to ensure ongoing compliance with the listing criteria and 
appropriate market surveillance. Similarly, the Exchange proposes to 
add a new subparagraph (3) to Rule 19.4(g) providing that the Exchange 
shall consider the suspension of opening transactions in any series of 
options of the class covering Fund Shares approved pursuant to Rule 
19.3(i)(5) if the criteria under Rule 19.3(i)(5)(B)(i) are no longer 
satisfied, as determined by the Exchange on a monthly basis, or the 
criteria in Rule 19.3(i)(5)(B)(ii) are no longer satisfied.\15\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \15\ See supra note 5 [sic].
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The Exchange also proposes conforming amendments to update cross-
references within Rule 19.4(g) to reflect the redesignation of Rule 
19.3(i) subparagraphs. As discussed above, Rule 19.4(g)(1) and Rule 
19.4(g)(2) currently contain erroneous cross-references to Rule 
19.3(i)(4)(A) and Rule 19.3(i)(4)(B), respectively, which would be 
corrected to reference Rule 19.3(i)(6)(A) and Rule 19.3(i)(6)(B). The 
Exchange also proposes to renumber existing subparagraphs (3) and (4) 
of Rule 19.4(g) as subparagraphs (4) and (5), respectively.
    These continued listing standards are substantively identical to 
those set forth in Cboe Options Rule 4.4.06(c) and are designed to 
ensure appropriate ongoing monitoring of crypto asset-based products 
listed on the Exchange.
2. Statutory Basis
    The Exchange believes the proposed rule change is consistent with 
the Act and the rules and regulations thereunder applicable to the 
Exchange and, in particular, the requirements of Section 6(b) of the 
Act.\16\ Specifically, the Exchange believes the proposed rule change 
is consistent with the Section 6(b)(5) \17\ requirements that the rules 
of an exchange be designed to prevent fraudulent and manipulative acts 
and practices, to promote just and equitable principles of trade, to 
foster cooperation and coordination with persons engaged in regulating, 
clearing, settling, processing information with respect to, and 
facilitating transactions in securities, to remove impediments to and 
perfect the mechanism of a free and open market and a national market 
system, and, in general, to protect investors and the public interest. 
Additionally, the Exchange believes the proposed rule change is 
consistent with

[[Page 65812]]

the Section 6(b)(5) \18\ requirement that the rules of an exchange not 
be designed to permit unfair discrimination between customers, issuers, 
brokers, or dealers.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b).
    \17\ 15 U.S.C. 78f(b)(5).
    \18\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Specifically, the Exchange believes the proposed rule change is 
consistent with Section 6(b)(5) of the Act for the following reasons. 
First, with respect to the proposed initial and continued listing 
criteria for options on commodity-based trusts holding multiple crypto 
assets, the proposed amendments would permit the Exchange to list and 
trade such options using generic listing standards that are 
substantively identical to those set forth in Cboe Options Rules 
4.3.06(a)(6) and 4.4.06(c). The requirements that each underlying 
crypto asset has an average daily market value of at least $700 million 
over the last 12 months and that each crypto asset underly a 
derivatives contract trading on a market with which the Exchange has a 
comprehensive surveillance sharing agreement are designed to prevent 
fraudulent and manipulative acts and practices and to protect investors 
and the public interest.
    Second, with respect to the proposed amendments to Rule 19.3(i)(1) 
to permit the listing of options on an exchange traded fund as defined 
in Rule 6c-11, these amendments are consistent with the approach taken 
by Cboe Options in the Proposal to List Options on 6c-11 ETFs.\19\ The 
Exchange believes these amendments are consistent with the Act because 
existing Rule 6c-11 transparency requirements and options listing 
requirements are reasonably designed to help ensure that such options 
are not readily susceptible to manipulation. Where ETF Shares have been 
deemed sufficiently transparent to warrant generic listing, the 
Exchange believes the manipulation risks for both the underlying shares 
and overlying options are adequately mitigated through this 
transparency framework. This proposal creates a streamlined pathway for 
listing options on qualifying ETF Shares that meet the enhanced 
transparency standards under Rule 6c-11.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \19\ See supra note 7 [sic].
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Third, with respect to the proposed amendment to Rule 19.3(i)(7)(i) 
to add ETF Shares, this amendment is consistent with Section 6(b)(5) of 
the Act because it ensures that Fund Shares listed pursuant to the ETF 
Shares generic listing standards are subject to the same additional 
listing requirements applicable to other categories of generically 
listed Fund Shares, thereby providing consistent investor protections 
across all categories of generically listed fund products.
    Fourth, with respect to the proposed conforming amendments to 
remove individual product-specific references in Exchange Rule 
19.3(i)(4), remove the reference to ``series of'' portfolio depositary 
receipts and index fund shares, and update cross-references, these 
amendments are consistent with Section 6(b)(5) of the Act because they 
are designed to remove impediments to and perfect the mechanism of a 
free and open market by aligning the Exchange's rules with the approach 
in Cboe Options Rule 4.3.06(a)(4), thereby promoting consistency across 
Cboe-affiliated exchanges without altering the substantive protections 
afforded to investors.
    Fifth, with respect to the proposed amendments to Rule 19.4(g) to 
establish continued listing standards for options on commodity-based 
trusts holding multiple crypto assets and to update cross-references, 
these amendments are consistent with the Approval Order and are 
designed to ensure ongoing compliance with the listing criteria and 
appropriate market surveillance. The Exchange further believes that the 
proposed rule change removes impediments to and perfects the mechanism 
of a free and open market by enabling market participants to trade 
options on multi-crypto-asset trusts on the Exchange under the same 
terms and conditions approved for Cboe Options. The proposed rule 
change promotes fair and orderly markets and is not designed to permit 
unfair discrimination between customers, issuers, brokers, or dealers, 
as the same generic listing criteria would apply equally to all 
eligible products.
    The Commission has already determined that substantially identical 
rules satisfy the requirements of the Act in approving the Approval 
Order. The proposed amendments are also consistent with the 
Commission's prior approval of the Proposal to List Options on 6c-11 
ETFs and with Cboe Options Rules 4.3.06(a)(4) and 4.3.06(a)(6), which 
established the framework for generic listing criteria that the 
Exchange now proposes to adopt. The Exchange therefore believes the 
proposed rule change is consistent with the Act.

B. Self-Regulatory Organization's Statement on Burden on Competition

    The Exchange does not believe that the proposed rule change will 
impose any burden on competition that is not necessary or appropriate 
in furtherance of the purposes of the Act. The proposed rule change is 
designed to permit the Exchange to list and trade options on the same 
types of products that have been approved for trading on Cboe Options 
and to adopt the same modernized descriptions and conforming amendments 
approved by the Commission for Cboe Options. Rather than imposing a 
burden on competition, the proposed rule change is designed to enhance 
competition by enabling the Exchange to compete on a level playing 
field with Cboe Options in offering options on commodity-based trusts 
holding multiple crypto assets, listing options on 6c-11 ETFs, and 
applying generic listing criteria instead of product-specific 
references.
    The Exchange notes that the proposed rule change does not impose 
any burden on intramarket competition, as the generic listing criteria 
would apply equally to all eligible products and market participants. 
The proposed rule change also does not impose any burden on intermarket 
competition, as it merely permits the Exchange to list the same types 
of products already approved for trading on another exchange.

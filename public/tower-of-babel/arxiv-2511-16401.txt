The maximal destabilizers for Chow and K-stability
YI YAO

Abstract
. Donaldson showed that the constant scalar curvature Kähler metrics can be quantized by the balanced Hermitian norms on the spaces of global sections. We explore an analogous problem in the unstable situation. For a K-unstable manifold ( X , L ) (X,L) , its projective embedding via | k ​ L | \left|kL\right| will be Chow-unstable when k k is sufficiently large and divisible. 1.
Introduction

A central problem in Kähler geometry is the existence of canonical
metrics in a given Kähler class. Let
(X,L)
be a polarized Kähler
manifold, the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture predicts that the
existence of constant scalar curvature Kähler (cscK) metrics in
c_{1}(L)

is equivalent to the K-polystability (or some enhanced version) of

(X,L)
. During the last decade, this conjecture has been verified
for the Fano case
(X,-K_{X})
by various approaches, such as
[
20
,
73
,
3
]
.
Following the variational approach of
[
3
]
,
there has been big progress for the general polarization, see
[
52
,
12
,
51
]
.

The quantization method plays a key role in the study of canonical
metrics problems. The main idea is that the infinite-dimensional space

\mathcal{H}(L)
of Kähler potentials can be approximated (quantized)
by the Riemannian symmetric space
\mathcal{H}(R_{k})
of Hermitian
norms on
R_{k}\coloneqq\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,kL)
, as the Planck’s constant

\hbar\sim k^{-1}
goes to zero. A cornerstone is the Tian–Yau–Zelditch’s
asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernel. There are many studies
involving quantization in this area. We mention one work that motivates
our study.

The seminar work of Donaldson
[
33
,
37
]

shows that if
(X,L)
admits a cscK metric
\omega
and the automorphism
group is discrete, then there exists balanced norms on
R_{k}
for

k\gg 1
. Moreover, the sequence of Fubini–Study metrics induced
by balanced norms will converge to
\omega
, see Theorem
30

for a review. In a nutshell, balanced norms quantize cscK metrics.
By
[
81
,
56
]
, the existence
of balanced norms on
R_{k}
is equivalent to the Chow-polystability
of the embedding image
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}
. Hence
in this case, the existence of cscK metrics can imply the asymptotic
Chow-stability. Conversely, Ross–Thomas
[
67
]

showed that the asymptotic Chow-semistability can imply the K-semistability
of
(X,L)
. In a rough sense, one can say that Chow-stability quantizes
K-stability.

In this paper, we explore the parallel story on the unstable side.
If
(X,L)
is K-unstable, by Theorem
29

there exists
r,N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that the embedding image
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{kr}^{*}

is Chow-unstable for
k>N
. Replacing
L
by
rL
, we can assume

r=1
. In GIT, after the work of Kempf
[
48
]

and Rousseau
[
68
]
, one can consider the
maximal destabilizing 1-parameter subgroups to an unstable point,
which minimizes the normalized Hilbert–Mumford weight and is unique
up to conjugations. Hence for Chow-unstable subvariety
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}
,
we obtain a set of 1-PS in
\mathrm{SL}(R_{k})
which are conjugate
to each other. To eliminate the conjugacy ambiguity, we consider the
filtration (i.e. NA norm) on
R_{k}
induced by these 1-PS, that
is unique up to scaling. We call this NA norm on
R_{k}
the
maximal
Chow-destabilizer
for
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}
. Comparing
with the picture
[
33
]
in the stable case,
we ask:

How do this sequence of maximal Chow-destabilizers relate to the maximal
K-destabilizer?

The maximal K-destabilizer was first studied by Székelyhidi
[
69
]

in the toric setting, where it is given by a concave function
\Theta

(optimal test-configuration) on the moment polytope
P
that minimizes
the Donaldson–Futaki invariant. Assuming that the Calabi flow exists
for all time, Székelyhidi showed that the scalar curvature under the
flow converges to
\Theta
. Moreover, if
\Theta
is piecewise linear,
it induces a polyhedral decomposition of
P
, such that each piece
is relatively K-semistable in a certain sense. These support a conjecture
of Donaldson
[
34
,
35
]
,
which predicts that when the extremal metrics are absent, Calabi flow
will break up the manifold into pieces, and each piece admits a complete
extremal metric or collapses. Also see
[
70
]

for the case of ruled surfaces.

In the general case, maximal K-destabilizer is defined in the work
of Xia
[
77
]
. The K-energy
\mathcal{M}
can be
extended to a convex functional over the space
\mathcal{E}^{2}(L)

of
L^{2}
-finite energy metrics on
L
, which is a Hadamard space.
By considering the associated gradient flow, Xia showed that in the
geodesically unstable case, modulo the asymptotic relations, there
exists a unique geodesic ray
\ell^{\mathrm{K}}
in
\mathcal{E}^{2}(L)

along which
\mathcal{M}
achieves the minimal limit slope, namely
it minimizes the radial K-energy
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rad}}
(
6.3
).
We call
\ell^{\mathrm{K}}
the
maximal K-destabilizing ray
.
By the construction in
[
3
]
,
\ell^{\mathrm{K}}

induces a non-Archimedean metric
\phi^{\mathrm{K}}
on the Berkovich
analytification
L^{\mathrm{an}}
of
L
(see §
5.3
).
By
[
52
]
,
\ell^{\mathrm{K}}
is maximal in the
sense of
[
3
]
, so it can be recovered from
the NA data
\phi^{\mathrm{K}}
. We call
\phi^{\mathrm{K}}
the

maximal K-destabilizer
for
(X,L)
, which is unique up to
scaling, see Definition
39
for details. In the
toric setting, it can be reduced into Székelyhidi’s optimal test-configuration,
see Example
43
.

In order to connect the maximal Chow-destabilizers to the maximal
K-destabilizer, first we need to characterize them in a unified way,
namely as the “steepest descent direction” for some energy functionals.
Instead of being a 1-PS that minimizes the Chow-weight, the maximal
Chow-destabilizer can also be represented by the norm geodesic ray
along which the Kempf–Ness functional achieves the minimal limit
slope.

Maximal Chow-destabilizers

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
be a projective variety and
\mathcal{H}(V)

be the space of Hermitian norms on
V
. By works
[
81
,
56
,
62
,
60
]
,
the Kempf–Ness functional for the Chow-stability of
X
can be
identified with the following functional on
\mathcal{H}(V)
,

\mathbf{M}_{X}(H)\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(H),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)+\frac{1}{\dim V}\log\left(\frac{\det H}{\det H_{r}}\right),

where
\mathbf{FS}(H)
is the Fubini–Study metric on
\mathcal{O}(1)|_{X}

induced by
H
,
\mathcal{E}_{X}
is the Monge–Ampère energy,
and
H_{r}
is a reference norm. Chow-weights are obtained by taking
the limit slope of
\mathbf{M}_{X}
along the norm geodesic rays.
Consider the asymptotic cone
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
of
\mathcal{H}(V)
,
which is a Hadamard space consisting of geodesic rays in
\mathcal{H}(V)

modulo the asymptotic relations. By
[
15
]
,
a norm geodesic ray
H=(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
induces a NA norm
\chi_{H}

on
V
. This gives an isometry
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{N}(V)
,
the target is the space of NA norms on
V
. We define the Chow-weight

M_{X}:\mathcal{N}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows

(1.1)

M_{X}(\chi)\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{t}),

where
(H_{t})
is any norm geodesic ray such that
\chi_{H}=\chi
.
It is well-defined since
\mathbf{M}_{X}
is convex and Lipschitz
(Proposition
16
). Note that
\mathbf{M}_{X}

depends on the choice of norm (
4.1
) on Chow-forms,
but Chow-weight does not. Since
M_{X}
is homogeneous under scaling,
we normalize it and then consider the minimization problem.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
20
)
.

For any projective manifold
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
,
consider the minimization problem for
L^{p}
-normalized Chow-weight

(1.2)

\inf\left\{\bar{M}_{X,p}(\chi)\coloneqq\frac{M_{X}(\chi)}{\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}}\mid\chi_{tr}\neq\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)\right\},

where
\left\|\chi\right\|_{p\in[1,\infty]}
is the
p
-norm
(
2.10
). (1) there exists a minimizer for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.
(2) when
X
is Chow-unstable (i.e.
\inf M_{X}<0
) and
p\in(1,\infty)
,
the minimizer is unique up to scaling.

When
p=2
, the minimizers are called the
maximal Chow-destabilizers

for
X
. The existence is not straightforward since the unit sphere in
\mathcal{N}(V)

is not compact unless
\dim V=1
(see Remark
8
).
For this, we use another locally compact topology under which
M_{X}

is lsc. The uniqueness follows from the convexity of
M_{X}
and
the strict convexity of
p
-norm when
p\in(1,\infty)
.

Symmetries of maximal destabilizers

The absolute uniqueness implies that the maximal Chow-destabilizer
can inherit all symmetries of embedding
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
.

Corollary 2

(Theorem
23
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
be a Chow-unstable projective manifold.
Suppose that
G
is a group with a homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)

such that
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
is preserved by
G
. With respect
to the natural
G
-action on
\mathcal{N}(V)
,
M_{X}
is
G
-invariant
and the maximal Chow-destabilizer is also
G
-invariant.

For instance, let
(X,L)
be the polarized toric manifold associated
to a Delzant polytope
P
. Take
G=T_{\mathbb{C}}
, the complex torus
and
V=R_{k}
(
k\gg 1
), which carries a
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-action. If

X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}
is Chow-unstable, the above
corollary implies that the maximal Chow-destabilizer is
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant.
Hence we only need to search the minimizer among the
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant
norms. We will discuss this in detail in
[
80
]
.

Similarly, the maximal K-destabilizer also inherits all symmetries
of
(X,L)
, see Theorem
42
.

A route to connecting maximal Chow-destabilizers to K-destabilizers

From now on, we suppose that
(X,L)
is K-unstable. Then
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}

is Chow-unstable for sufficiently large
k
. Up to scaling, Theorem

1
yields a sequence of maximal Chow-destabilizers

\chi_{k}\in\mathcal{N}(R_{k})
. Under some assumptions, we show
that under proper normalization,
\mathrm{FS}_{k}(\chi_{k})
converges
to the maximal K-destabilizer, where
\mathrm{FS}_{k}
is the NA
Fubini–Study map (
5.12
). This approach is based on
the Boucksom–Jonsson’s non-Archimedean pluripotential theory
[
11
,
13
,
12
]
.

Let
(X^{\mathrm{an}},L^{\mathrm{an}})
be the Berkovich analytification of
(X,L)

over
\mathbb{C}
with the trivial norm (see §
5.1
).
As a topological space,
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is Hausdorff and compact. Boucksom–Jonsson

[
11
]
define the NA psh metrics on
L^{\mathrm{an}}

to be the decreasing limit of Fubini–Study type functions (
5.2
).
Denote by
\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
(
\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
) the
space of (continuous) psh metrics, we may omit “
L
” when it
is fixed. In
[
10
]
, they introduced
two maps which play the role of quantization maps in the Archimedean
setting, namely the NA Fubini–Study map and the super-norm map (see
§
5.5
):

\mathrm{FS}_{k}:\mathcal{N}(R_{k})\rightarrow\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}},\ \mathrm{SN}_{k}:\mathcal{L}^{\infty}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(R_{k}),

where
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}
is the space of bounded functions on

X^{\mathrm{an}}
. They behave similarly to the Legendre transform, see Proposition

35
. Using NA functionals, we obtain a formula for
the Chow-weight (
1.1
).

Theorem 3

(Theorem
33
,
36
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
be a projective manifold.

(1) For any
\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)
we have

M_{X}(\chi)=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{FS}(\chi)\right)-E_{V}(\chi),

where
\mathbb{E}
is the NA Monge–Ampère energy,
\mathrm{FS}

is the NA Fubini–Study map (
5.10
) and
E_{V}(\chi)

is the volume (
2.11
), i.e. the average of jumping
numbers of
\chi
.

(2) If
X
is Chow-unstable and
\bar{\chi}
is a maximal Chow-destabilizer,
then
\mathrm{SN}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\bar{\chi})=\bar{\chi}
.

(2) can be described as the concavity of maximal Chow-destabilizers.
Now we state our main result. Unfortunately, it relies on some idealistic
assumptions.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(X,L)
be a K-unstable polarized manifold.
We make assumptions
A1
-
A4
, see §
7.1
.
Let
\phi^{\flat}\in\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
be the maximal K-destabilizer
normalized by (
7.4
). When
k
is sufficiently
large and divisible, the embedding image
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}

is Chow-unstable, let
\chi_{k}^{\flat}\in\mathcal{N}(R_{k})
be
the maximal Chow-destabilizer normalized by (
7.10
).
Then
\mathrm{FS}_{k}(\chi_{k}^{\flat})
converges to
\phi^{\flat}

in the sense specified by
A4
.

If the convergence holds, we can say the maximal Chow-destabilizers
quantize the maximal K-destabilizer.

Discussion of the assumptions & proof

Recall that in the Archimedean setting, Donaldson
[
37
]

provided a variational perspective on the relationship between balanced
norms and cscK metrics, which is reviewed in §
4.5
.
Our approach is analogous to that.

Since
\phi^{\mathrm{K}}
is induced from the maximal K-destabilizing
ray, first we need to re-characterize it as the minimizer of the normalized
NA K-energy
\overline{\mathbb{M}}
(
7.2
). Let
\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}\subset\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)

be the space of finite-energy metrics, and
\mathcal{R}^{1}
be the
space of finite-energy geodesic rays in
\mathcal{E}^{1}(L)
. Then
we have BBJ’s embedding
\iota:\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{1}/_{\sim}
,
where
\sim
is the
d_{1}
-asymptotic relation. Assumption
A1

says that

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\iota(\phi))=\mathbb{M}(\phi),\ \forall\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}.

This is a conjecture in
[
52
]
and has been verified
very recently by
[
14
]
and
[
26
]

by different method. Under
A1
, by a trick of Inoue
[
44
, §4.2.2]
,
we see that

(1.3)

\inf\left\{\breve{\mathbb{M}}(\phi)\coloneqq\mathbb{M}(\phi)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\phi\right\|_{2}^{2}\mid\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{2}\coloneqq\iota^{-1}\left(\mathcal{R}^{2}/_{\sim}\right)\right\}

admits a unique minimizer
\phi^{\flat}\in\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{2}
,
where
\left\|\phi\right\|_{2}\coloneqq\left\|\iota(\phi)\right\|_{2}

is the speed of geodesic ray and
\phi^{\flat}
is the unique maximal
K-destabilizer normalized by the condition
\left\|\phi^{\flat}\right\|_{2}^{2}=-\mathbb{M}(\phi^{\flat})
.
The advantage of replacing
\overline{\mathbb{M}}
by
\breve{\mathbb{M}}

is that the latter preserves convexity and has an absolutely unique
minimizer.

Next we approximate
\breve{\mathbb{M}}
by some quantized functionals.

A3
assumes that for any
\phi\in\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}
, we
have

E_{k}\circ\mathrm{SN}_{k}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}(\phi)-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{M}(\phi)k^{-1}+\mathrm{o}(k^{-1}),

where
E_{k}
is the normalized volume. The leading-term asymptotic
is due to
[
8
]
. We show
A3
holds
for
\phi
induced by an ample test-configuration, see Theorem
46
.
In the toric setting, it reduces to the Riemann sum expansion for
convex functions. To use the above expansion, we have to assume
A2
:

\phi^{\flat}
is continuous. Note that in the toric setting, Li–Lian–Sheng

[
50
]
showed that the optimal test-configuration
is a bounded concave function over the polytope.

On the other hand, the
L^{2}
-norm
\left\|\phi\right\|_{2}

can be quantized by the super-norms.

Theorem 5

(Theorem
45
)
.

For any
\phi\in\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
, we have

\left\|\phi\right\|_{2}\coloneqq\left\|\iota(\phi)\right\|_{2}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{k}\left\|\mathrm{SN}_{k}(\phi)\right\|_{2}.

We approximate (
1.3
) by

(1.4)

\inf\left\{\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{k}(\phi)\coloneqq 2k\left(\mathbb{E}(\phi)-E_{k}\circ\mathrm{SN}_{k}(\phi)\right)+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\left\|\mathrm{SN}_{k}(\phi)\right\|_{2}^{2}\mid\phi\in\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}\right\}.

Under
A3
,
\breve{\mathbb{Q}}_{k}(\phi)
converges to
\breve{\mathbb{M}}(\phi)

for
\phi\in\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}
. By the intertwining properties
between
\mathrm{FS}_{k}
and
\mathrm{SN}_{k}
, we find that (
1.4
)
can be reduced into a finite-dimensional problem

\inf\left\{\breve{M}_{k}(\chi)\coloneqq 2k\left(\mathbb{E}\circ\mathrm{FS}_{k}(\chi)-E_{k}(\chi)\right)+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\left\|\chi\right\|_{2}^{2}\mid\chi\in\mathcal{SN}_{k}\right\},

where
\mathcal{SN}_{k}\subset\mathcal{N}(R_{k})
is the image set
of
\mathrm{SN}_{k}
. Note that the first term of
\breve{M}_{k}

is the NA expression for Chow-weight. This problem is equivalent to
(
1.2
,
V=R_{k}
) plus a constraint:
\chi\in\mathcal{SN}_{k}
.
But in Theorem
36
, we show that maximal
Chow-destabilizers automatically belong to
\mathcal{SN}_{k}
, so
this constraint is actually superfluous.

It turns out that (
1.4
) has a unique minimizer

\mathrm{FS}_{k}(\chi_{k}^{\flat})
. Finally,
A4
assumes
that this minimizer sequence converges to the minimizer of (
1.3
),
i.e.
\phi^{\flat}
. An evidence for
A4
is that
\mathbb{Q}_{k}

(
7.6
) is convex, see Proposition
47
.

Space of Hermitian norms and non-Archimedean norms

\mathcal{H}(V)

norms on a vector space

and its asymptotic cone
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
.
We verify that
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
is isometric to the space
\mathcal{N}(V)

of NA norms on

[
15
]
.
We will take
\mathcal{H}(V)
as the domain of the Kempf-Ness functional
for Chow-stability, and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
(
\cong\mathcal{N}(V)
)
as the domain of the Chow-weight. The main references of this section
are
[
15
,
8
,
23
]
.

2.1.
Symmetric space of Hermitian norms

Let
V
be a complex vector space of dimension
N
, which will be
the space of global sections
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,kL)
. A Hermitian
form
H:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is anti-linear w.r.t. the
second variable. If
H(x,x)>0
for all
x\neq 0
, we call it a Hermitian
norm, and denote
H(x)\coloneqq H(x,x)^{1/2}
. Let
\mathrm{Herm}(V)

be the real vector space of all Hermitian forms on
V
. Hermitian
norms constitute an open subset
\mathcal{H}(V)\subset\mathrm{Herm}(V)
.
Since the general linear group
\mathrm{GL}(V)
acts transitively
on
\mathcal{H}(V)
,
\mathcal{H}(V)
is homeomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}(N,\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{U}(N)
.

The tangent space of
\mathcal{H}(V)
at each point
H
is
\mathrm{Herm}(V)
,
which can be identified with the space of
H
-self-adjoint operators.
For
S\in\mathrm{Herm}(V)
, the associated operator
A:V\rightarrow V

is defined by

S(x,y)=H(Ax,y),\ \forall x,y\in V.

We usually denote
A
by the matrix notation
A=SH^{-1}
. There
is a canonical Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathcal{H}(V)
defined
by

g|_{H}\left(A_{1},A_{2}\right)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{tr}(A_{1}A_{2}),\ A_{1},A_{2}\in T_{H}\mathcal{H}(V).

One can check that
(\mathcal{H}(V),g)
is a contractible, complete
Riemannian symmetric space with non-positive sectional curvature,
see
[
38
]
. Group
\mathrm{GL}(V)
acts on

\mathcal{H}(V)
by isometries.

The geodesic ray starting from
H_{0}
with tangent vector
A
is
given by

H_{t}(x,y)=H_{0}(e^{tA}x,y),\ \forall x,y\in V.

Any two points
H_{0},H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}(V)
are connected by a
unique geodesic. Let
A
be the unique
H_{0}
-self-adjoint operator
such that
e^{A}=H_{1}H_{0}^{-1}
, then the geodesic between
H_{0}

and
H_{1}
is

(2.1)

H_{t}(x,y)=H_{0}(e^{tA}x,y),\ \forall x,y\in V,\ t\in[0,1].

The induced length metric is

d(H_{0},H_{1})^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{tr}(A^{2}),\ e^{A}=H_{1}H_{0}^{-1}.

More generally, for each
p\in[1,\infty]
, there is a Finsler metric
on
\mathcal{H}(V)
. For
A\in T_{H}\mathcal{H}(V)
with eigenvalues

\lambda_{i}
, we define Finsler norms

(2.2)

\left\|A\right\|_{p,H}\coloneqq\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\ \left\|A\right\|_{\infty,H}\coloneqq\max_{i}\left|\lambda_{i}\right|.

Under these Finsler norms, (
2.1
) are also
the shortest path between two points. For
H_{0},H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}(V)
,
let
e^{\mu_{i}}
be the eigenvalues of operator
H_{1}H_{0}^{-1}
,
then the induced length metric
d_{p}
is

d_{p}(H_{0},H_{1})^{p}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\mu_{i}\right|^{p},\ d_{\infty}(H_{0},H_{1})\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\mu_{i}\right|.

Among these metrics, only
d_{2}=d
makes
\mathcal{H}(V)
a CAT(0)
space. Metric
d_{\infty}
is called the Goldman–Iwahori distance,
which is the smallest
C>0
such that
e^{-C}H_{0}\leq H_{1}\leq e^{C}H_{0}
.
We have comparison

(2.3)

N^{-\frac{1}{2}}d_{\infty}\leq d\leq d_{\infty}.

2.1.1.
Relative volumes

A norm
H\in\mathcal{H}(V)
induces a norm on the top-wedge line

\wedge^{N}V
, which is denoted by
\det H
. For any basis
(s_{i})

of
V
, we have

\left(\det H\right)(s_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge s_{N})^{2}=\det[H(s_{i},s_{j})].

For two norms
H_{0}
,
H_{1}\in\mathcal{H}(V)
, the relative volume
is defined as

(2.4)

\mathbf{E}_{V}(H_{1},H_{0})\coloneqq-\frac{1}{N}\log\left(\frac{\det H_{1}}{\det H_{0}}\right)=-\frac{1}{N}\log\left(\frac{\det[H_{1}(s_{i},s_{j})]}{\det[H_{0}(s_{i},s_{j})]}\right).

So if
(s_{i})
is a
H_{0}
-orthonormal basis such that
H_{1}(s_{i},s_{j})=e^{\mu_{i}}\delta_{ij}
,
then
\mathbf{E}_{V}(H_{1},H_{0})=-\frac{1}{N}\sum\mu_{i}.
By Cauchy–Schwartz
inequality, we have

(2.5)

\left|\mathbf{E}_{V}(H_{1},H_{0})\right|\leq d(H_{1},H_{0}).

2.2.
Asymptotic cone of
\mathcal{H}(V)

For a general CAT(0) space
\mathcal{X}
, one can consider all the
geodesic rays modulo the asymptotic relations. It constitutes another
CAT(0) space, called the asymptotic cone (or infinity cone) of
\mathcal{X}
,
refer to
[
17
]
. Here we specialize to the case

\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{H}(V),d)
.

Recall some notions in metric geometry. Given a metric space
(X,d)
,
a geodesic segment is a map
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X
such that

d(\gamma_{s},\gamma_{t})=d(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})\left|s-t\right|

for any
s,t\in[0,1]
. We say
(X,d)
is a geodesic space if any
two points can be connected by a geodesics segment. A
CAT(0)
space
is a geodesic space satisfying a triangle comparison property.
By
[
1
, Theorem 1.3.3]
, this property is equivalent
to that for any geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X
and point
x\in X
,
we have

d(x,\gamma_{t})^{2}\leq(1-t)d(x,\gamma_{0})^{2}+t\cdot d(x,\gamma_{1})^{2}-t(1-t)d(\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1})^{2},\ \forall t\in[0,1].

In particular, the function
d(x,\cdot)^{2}
is strictly convex.
This inequality implies Busemann’s convexity, i.e. for any two geodesics

\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
, the function
t\mapsto d\left(\gamma_{t},\gamma^{\prime}_{t}\right)

is convex. Busemann’s convexity implies the uniqueness of the geodesic
between two points. A complete CAT(0) space is called a
Hadamard
space
, such as
\mathcal{H}(V)
.

Given two geodesic rays
(H_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}

in
\mathcal{H}(V)
, which can be stationary points. Busemann’s convexity
implies that
t\mapsto d(H_{t},G_{t})
is convex. Moreover, by the
triangle inequality, we have
d(H_{t},G_{t})\leq At+B
for some constants

A,B>0
. Hence the limit slope

(2.6)

\hat{d}\left((H_{t}),(G_{t})\right)\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{d(H_{t},G_{t})-d(H_{0},G_{0})}{t}\uparrow

exists. It is easy to check that
\hat{d}
satisfies the triangle
inequality. If
\hat{d}=0
, we say
(H_{t})
and
(G_{t})
are
asymptotic to each other, this is an equivalent relation between geodesic
rays. Note that by the convexity,
\hat{d}=0
is equivalent to the
boundness of
d(H_{t},G_{t})
. An equivalence class is called an

asymptotic class
, which will be denoted by
\eta,\xi
, etc.
Let
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
be the set of asymptotic classes, then

\hat{d}
descends to a metric on
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
. By a general
theorem
[
2
, Theorem 4.8]
,
\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V),\hat{d}\right)

is also a Hadamard space.

Denote by
o
the class of stationary rays. Then
\hat{d}(\eta,o)

is the speed of rays in class
\eta
. Note that
\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V),\hat{d}\right)

is not locally compact when
\dim V>1
, e.g. sphere
\{\eta\mid\hat{d}(\eta,o)=\delta\}

is not compact, see Remark
8
and the isometry
(
2.14
).

2.2.1.
Negatively curved path of Hermitian norms

Although we mainly deal with geodesics, many results hold for more
general paths with curvature condition. We adopt the curvature condition
in
[
6
]
.

For an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define a domain

(2.7)

D_{I}\coloneqq\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\mid-\log\left|\tau\right|^{2}\in I\right\}.

A path
(H_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathcal{H}(V)
defines a metric
h

on the trivial bundle
V\times D_{I}\rightarrow D_{I}
by letting

h|_{V\times\{\tau\}}=H_{-\log\left|\tau\right|^{2}}
. We say a smooth
path
(H_{t})_{t\in I}
is
negatively (positively) curved

if the Chern curvature tensor
\Theta_{h}\leq 0\ (\geq 0)
in the sense
of Griffiths (see
[
31
, Chapter VII 6]
). Path

(H_{t})
is negatively curved if and only if the path of dual norms

(H_{t}^{*})\subset\mathcal{H}(V^{*})
is positively curved.

Fix a basis of
V
, the associated Gram matrix is still denoted by

H_{t}
. By a direct computation,
(H_{t})_{t\in I}
is negatively
(positively) curved if and only if

(2.8)

\ddot{H}-\dot{H}H^{-1}\dot{H}\geq 0\ (\leq 0),\ \forall t\in I,

where
\dot{H}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}H_{t}
and
\geq 0

means positive semi-definite.

Proposition 6
.

For a negatively curved smooth path
(H_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathcal{H}(V)

and
s\in V\backslash\{0\}
, the function
t\mapsto\log H_{t}(s)

is convex.

Proof.

Denote
f(t)=\log H_{t}(s,s)
. For any
t_{0}\in I
, we show
f^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})\geq 0
.
Take a
H_{t_{0}}
-orthonormal basis
(s_{i})
such that
\dot{H}_{t_{0}}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N}\right)
.
Curvature condition (
2.8
) implies that

a\ddot{H}_{t_{0}}a^{*}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}^{2}\left|a_{i}\right|^{2},\ \forall a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}.

Rescaling
s
such that
H_{t_{0}}(s)=1
. Let
s=\sum_{i}a_{i}s_{i}
,
then
f(t)=\log aH_{t}a^{*}
and
\sum\left|a_{i}\right|^{2}=1
.
By the above inequality, we have

f^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})=a\ddot{H}_{t_{0}}a^{*}-\left(a\dot{H}_{t_{0}}a^{*}\right)^{2}\geq\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}\left|a_{i}\right|^{2}-\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\left|a_{i}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}\geq 0,

where
\geq 0
is due to the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality.
∎

Remark 7
.

Things are not symmetric, for a positively curved path
(H_{t})
,
the function
t\mapsto\log H_{t}(s)
may not be concave. Besides
that, the convexity of
\log H_{t}(s)
for all
s\in V\backslash\{0\}

is not enough to ensure the path is negatively curved. Actually, the
latter is equivalent to that for any non-vanishing holomorphic section
of
V\times D_{I}
(i.e. a holomorphic map
s:D_{I}\rightarrow V\backslash\{0\}
),
the function
D_{I}\ni\tau\mapsto\log h\left(s(\tau)\right)
is subharmonic,
refer to
[
64
]
§2.

If
(E,h)
is a Hermitian holomorphic bundle with semi-positive (semi-negative)
curvature (in the sense of Griffiths), then so is the line bundle

(\det E,\det h)
. Take
E=V\times D_{I}
, if
(H_{t})_{t\in I}\subset\mathcal{H}(V)

is positively (negatively) curved, then the function
t\mapsto\mathbf{E}_{V}(H_{t},H_{r})

(
2.4
) is convex (concave) for a fixed reference norm

H_{r}
.

2.3.
Space of non-Archimedean norms

The asymptotic cone
\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
can be identified with
the space
\mathcal{N}(V)
of NA norms on
V
over the trivially-normed

\mathbb{C}
. Refer to
[
8
]
for a comprehensive
discussion on such spaces.

Let
\left|\cdot\right|_{tr}
be the trivial norm on
\mathbb{C}
,
i.e.
\left|a\right|_{tr}=1
for all
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. A
non-Archimedean
norm
is a function
\left\|\cdot\right\|:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

satisfying

\left\|as\right\|=\left|a\right|_{tr}\left\|s\right\|,\ \left\|s+s^{\prime}\right\|\leq\max\left(\left\|s\right\|,\left\|s^{\prime}\right\|\right),\ \forall a\in\mathbb{C},\ s,s^{\prime}\in V

and
\left\|s\right\|=0
only when
s=0
. Follow
[
13
]
,
we use the valuation notation. Let

\chi(\cdot)\coloneqq-\log\left\|\cdot\right\|\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},

then we have

\chi(as)=\chi(s),\ \forall a\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\ \chi(s+s^{\prime})\geq\min(\chi(s),\chi(s^{\prime})).

Let
\mathcal{N}(V)
be the set of all NA norms on
V
. There is
a trivial norm
\chi_{tr}
such that
\chi_{tr}(s)=0
for all
s\neq 0
.
For any
a>0
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\chi+b
is also a NA norm. Note
that for
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\mathcal{N}(V)
,
\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}

may not be a NA norm.

A NA norm
\chi
induces a left-continuous non-increasing filtration

(F^{\lambda}V)_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}
of
V
and vice versa,

F^{\lambda}V=\left\{s\in V\mid\chi(s)\geq\lambda\right\},\ \chi(s)=\sup\left\{\lambda\mid s\in F^{\lambda}V\right\}.

The
jumping numbers

\lambda_{1}(\chi)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}(\chi)

of filtration are defined as

\lambda_{i}(\chi)\coloneqq\max\{\lambda\mid\dim F^{\lambda}V\geq i\}.

We say that
\chi
is diagonalized by a basis
(s_{i})
of
V

if we have

\chi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}s_{i}\right)=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\chi(a_{i}s_{i})=\min_{a_{i}\neq 0}\chi(s_{i}),\ \forall(a_{i})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}.

At this time, the jumping numbers are the non-increasing rearrangement
of
\chi(s_{i})
. By
[
8
, Proposition 1.14]
,
any two norms
\chi,\chi^{\prime}
can be diagonalized by a common basis

(s_{i})
. Then we arrange the numbers
\chi(s_{i})-\chi^{\prime}(s_{i})

as follows

\lambda_{1}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}(\chi,\chi^{\prime}),

which are independent of the choices of
(s_{i})
. They are called
the
relative spectra
. In particular,
\lambda_{i}(\chi,\chi_{tr})=\lambda_{i}(\chi)
.

By
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
, for each
p\in[1,\infty]
,
there is a metric
d_{p}
on
\mathcal{N}(V)
defined by

(2.9)

d_{p}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})^{p}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\lambda_{i}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\right|^{p},\ d_{\infty}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\lambda_{i}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\right|.

They are Lipschitz equivalent to each other. We define the “
p
-norm”
of a NA norm by

(2.10)

\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}\coloneqq d_{p}(\chi,\chi_{tr})=\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|\lambda_{i}(\chi)\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},\ \left\|\chi\right\|_{\infty}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\left|\lambda_{i}(\chi)\right|.

The relative volume and the volume are defined as

(2.11)

E_{V}(\chi,\chi^{\prime})\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}(\chi,\chi^{\prime}),\ E_{V}(\chi)\coloneqq E_{V}(\chi,\chi_{tr})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}(\chi).

Remark 8
.

When
\dim V>1
, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
,
the metric space
(\mathcal{N}(V),d_{p})
is not locally compact.
For any nonzero
s\in V
, we define
\chi_{s}\in\mathcal{N}(V)

by letting
\chi_{s}(s)=\delta>0
and
\chi_{s}(s^{\prime})=0
when
s^{\prime}\notin\mathbb{C}s
.
The associated filtration is
V\supset\mathbb{C}s\supset\{0\}
with
jumping number
\lambda_{1}=\delta>0=\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{N}
.
For
p\in[1,\infty)
(
p=\infty
is similar), we have
\left\|\chi_{s}\right\|_{p}=\delta N^{-1/p}
.
If
s,s^{\prime}\in V
are linearly independent, expand them to a basis
\{s,s^{\prime},s_{3},\cdots,s_{N}\}
,
which diagonalizes both
\chi_{s}
and
\chi_{s^{\prime}}
. It is easy to
see
d_{p}(\chi_{s},\chi_{s^{\prime}})=2^{1/p}\delta N^{-1/p}
. It follows
that for any
\epsilon>0
, we can find a sequence in
\left\{\chi:\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}\leq\epsilon\right\}

which has no convergent subsequence. Hence
\chi_{tr}
has no pre-compact
neighborhood.

2.4.
The limit NA norm associated to a ray

A geodesic ray in
\mathcal{H}(V)
induces a NA norm on
V
. This
construction is extended by Berndtsson
[
6
]

to negatively curved rays
(H_{t})_{t>0}
with moderate growth, where

moderate growth
means that
H_{t}\leq e^{Ct}H_{1}
for
t\gg 1

and
C>0
is a constant. By Proposition
6
,
for any
s\in V\backslash\{0\}
,
f(t)=\log H_{t}(s)^{2}
is convex.
Growth condition implies that
f(t)\leq at+C
for
t\gg 1
. Hence
the limit slope

(2.12)

-\chi_{H}(s)\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(t)-f(0)}{t}\uparrow=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log H_{t}(s)^{2}

exits and is finite. Set
\chi_{H}(0)=+\infty
. The triangle inequality
for
H_{t}
implies that

\chi_{H}(s+s^{\prime})\geq\min\left(\chi_{H}(s),\chi_{H}(s^{\prime})\right),\ \forall s,s^{\prime}\in V.

Hence
\chi_{H}
defines a NA norm on
V
.

Definition 9

(limit NA norm)
.

For a negatively curved ray
(H_{t})_{t>0}\subset\mathcal{H}(V)

with moderate growth, we call
\chi_{H}
(
2.12
)
the
limit NA norm
of
(H_{t})_{t>0}
.

If two such rays
H_{t},G_{t}
are asymptotic to each other (i.e.

d(H_{t},G_{t})\leq C
), then they induce the same NA norm. Since
by (
2.3
), we have
e^{-C}G_{t}\leq H_{t}\leq e^{C}G_{t}

for some constant
C
. We denote by
\chi_{\eta}
the limit NA norm
associated to an asymptotic class
\eta
.

Example 10
.

Determine the limit NA norm of geodesic
ray
H_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)=H_{0}(e^{-tA}\cdot,\cdot)
, where
A
is

H_{0}
-self-adjoint. Take a
H_{0}
-orthonormal basis
(s_{i})

such that
As_{i}=\lambda_{i}s_{i}
, then
H_{t}(s_{i},s_{j})=e^{-t\lambda_{i}}\delta_{ij}
.
For a nonzero
s=\sum a_{i}s_{i}
, we have

\chi_{H}(s)=-\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log H_{t}(s)^{2}=-\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\sum_{i}\left|a_{i}\right|^{2}e^{-t\lambda_{i}}=\min_{a_{i}\neq 0}\lambda_{i}.

Hence
\chi_{H}
is diagonalized by
(s_{i})
and
\chi_{H}(s_{i})=\lambda_{i}
.
The associated filtration is

(2.13)

\left\{s\in V\mid\chi_{H}(s)\geq\lambda\right\}=\mathrm{span}\left\{s_{i}\mid\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda\right\}=\bigoplus_{\mu\geq\lambda}E_{A}^{\mu},

where
E_{A}^{\mu}\coloneqq\{s\in V\mid As=\mu s\}
is the
\mu
-eigenspace
of
A
.

The following fact is sketched in
[
15
]
.
We give a detailed proof.

Theorem 11
.

The NA norm associated to an asymptotic class of norm geodesic rays
defines an isometry

(2.14)

\mathrm{NAlim}:\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V),\hat{d}\right)\rightarrow\left(\mathcal{N}(V),d_{2}\right),\ \eta\mapsto\chi_{\eta},

where
\hat{d}
is (
2.6
) and
d_{2}
is (
2.9
).
In particular, the target space is also a Hadamard space.

Proof.

First we show
\mathrm{NAlim}
is surjective. For any
\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)

and
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}(V)
, let
E^{\lambda}
be the
H_{0}
-orthogonal
complement of the subspace

F^{>\lambda}\coloneqq\left\{s\in V\mid\chi(s)>\lambda\right\}\ \textrm{in}\ F^{\lambda}\coloneqq\left\{s\in V\mid\chi(s)\geq\lambda\right\}.

Then we have
V=\bigoplus_{\lambda}E^{\lambda}
, here
\lambda

are the jumping numbers of
\chi
. Let
A:V\rightarrow V
acts on

E^{\lambda}
by multiplying
\lambda
. Then by Example
10
,

\chi
is the limit NA norm of geodesic ray
H_{t}=H_{0}(e^{-tA}\cdot,\cdot)
.

Next we show
\mathrm{NAlim}
is injective. If two geodesic rays

H_{t},G_{t}
such that
\chi_{H}=\chi_{G}
, we show that they are
asymptotic to each other. By a parallel translation, we can assume

H_{0}=G_{0}
and
H_{t}=H_{0}(e^{-tA}\cdot,\cdot)
,
G_{t}=H_{0}(e^{-tB}\cdot,\cdot)
,
where
A,B
are
H_{0}
-self-adjoint operators. By Example
10
,

A
and
B
have the same eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}
,
and
\lambda_{i}
are the jumping numbers of
\chi_{H}
. Take two

H_{0}
-orthonormal basis
(s_{i}^{A})
and
(s_{i}^{B})
which
diagonalize
A
and
B
respectively. Let
P
be the unitary matrix
such that

\left(s_{1}^{B},\cdots,s_{N}^{B}\right)=\left(s_{1}^{A},\cdots,s_{N}^{A}\right)P.

By (
2.13
), we know
P
is upper triangular. Combining
with
P^{-1}=\bar{P}^{\mathrm{T}}
, it implies that
P
is diagonal.
Thus
s_{i}^{B}=P_{ii}s_{i}^{A}
, and the basis
(s_{i}^{A})
can
diagonalize both
A
and
B
. Since
A,B
have the same eigenvalues,
it implies
A=B
, so
H_{t}=G_{t}
for all
t
.

Finally, we check that
\mathrm{NAlim}
preserves the metrics. For
any
\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}(V)
, take a basis
(s_{i})
diagonalizing
both of them and let
\lambda_{i}=\chi(s_{i})
and
\mu_{i}=\chi^{\prime}(s_{i})
.
Let
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}(V)
taking
(s_{i})
as an orthonormal
basis. Define operators
A
and
B
such that

3.1.
Finite-energy metrics and energy functionals

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized manifold. We use the additive notation

\phi,\psi
for metrics on
L
. The curvature form is denoted by

dd^{c}\phi
, where
dd^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}(L)
be the space of smooth metrics on
L
with
positive curvature. Fix a reference metric
\phi_{r}\in\mathcal{H}(L)

Let
\mathrm{PSH}(L)
be the space of psh metrics on
L
. The Monge–Ampère
measure associated to
\phi\in\mathrm{PSH}(L)
is
\mathrm{MA}(\phi)\coloneqq(L^{n})^{-1}(dd^{c}\phi)^{n}
,
which is the non-pluriproduct of closed positive
(1,1)
-current

dd^{c}\phi
. The total mass (MA mass) of
\mathrm{MA}(\phi)
is

\leq 1
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{full}}(L)
be the space of psh
metrics with full MA mass. It includes all the bounded psh metrics.

For each
p\in[1,\infty)
, there is a Finsler metric on
\mathcal{H}(L)

which generalizes Mabuchi’s
L^{2}
-Riemannian metric when
p=2
.
It induces a length metric
d_{p}
on
\mathcal{H}(L)
. Darvas
[
27
,
29
]

showed that the metric completion of
(\mathcal{H}(L),d_{p})
coincides
with the space of
L^{p}
-finite-energy metrics

\mathcal{E}^{p}(L)\coloneqq\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{full}}(L)\mid\int_{X}\left|\phi-\phi_{r}\right|^{p}\mathrm{MA}(\phi)<\infty\right\},\ \forall\phi_{r}\in\mathcal{H}(L),

which is introduced by Cegrell–Guedj–Zeriahi. For each
p\in[1,\infty)
,

(\mathcal{E}^{p}(L),d_{p})
is a completely geodesic metric space,
and a CAT(0) space when
p=2
, see
[
28
]
.

The Monge–Ampère energy
\mathcal{E}:\mathcal{H}(L)\times\mathcal{H}(L)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is defined by

\mathcal{E}(\phi_{1},\phi_{0})=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\int_{X}\dot{\phi}_{t}\ \mathrm{MA}(\phi_{t}),

where
(\phi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathcal{H}(L)
is any smooth
path. It satisfies the cocycle property

\mathcal{E}(\phi_{2},\phi_{0})=\mathcal{E}(\phi_{2},\phi_{1})+\mathcal{E}(\phi_{1},\phi_{0}).

Taking the affine path
\phi_{t}=(1-t)\phi_{0}+t\phi_{1}
, we obtain

(3.1)

\mathcal{E}(\phi,\psi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(n+1)L^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}(\phi-\psi)\left(dd^{c}\phi\right)^{j}\wedge\left(dd^{c}\psi\right)^{n-j}.

When a reference metric
\phi_{r}\in\mathcal{H}(L)
is fixed, we
write
\mathcal{E}(\phi)\coloneqq\mathcal{E}(\phi,\phi_{r})
.

MA-energy can be extended to an u.s.c. functional on
\mathrm{PSH}(L)

by defining

\mathcal{E}(\phi)=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}(\psi)\mid\mathcal{H}(L)\ni\psi\geq\phi\right\}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}.

We have

\mathcal{E}^{1}(L)=\left\{\phi\in\mathrm{PSH}(L)\mid\mathcal{E}(\phi)>-\infty\right\}.

Mabuchi’s K-energy
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{H}(L)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
defined by

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}(\phi)\coloneqq\mathcal{M}(\phi,\phi_{r})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}t\int_{X}\dot{\phi}_{t}\left(\bar{S}-S(\phi_{t})\right)\mathrm{MA}(\phi_{t}),\ \bar{S}=\frac{nc_{1}(X)\cdot L^{n-1}}{L^{n}},

where
(\phi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathcal{H}(L)
is any smooth
path from
\phi_{r}
to
\phi
, and
S(\phi_{t})
is the scalar
curvature of metric
dd^{c}\phi_{t}
. Its critical points are cscK
metrics and it satisfies the cocycle property. In
[
4
]
, K-energy is extended to
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{E}^{1}(L)\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
.
They showed that the extension is
d_{1}
-lsc and convex along the
finite-energy geodesics.

Finite-energy geodesic rays

Recall the annular set

If we take another starting-point

and asymptotic to

.

When we consider the action of automorphisms on geodesic rays, it

be the space of all

-geodesic rays with arbitrary starting-point,
and then consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{p}(L)/_{\sim}

-asymptotic relation:

\Leftrightarrow

Due to the following fact, we can replace

Proposition 12
.

For any two

-geodesic rays

Apply the parallelism operator, there is a

Then we have

By the triangle inequality, the limit (

Chow-stability and its maximal destabilizers

In this section, we study the existence, uniqueness and the symmetries

4.1.
Chow-stability and the Kempf–Ness
functional

Let
V
be a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of dimension
N

be a
n
-dimensional projective variety with degree
d

by
\mathrm{Gr}\coloneqq\mathrm{Gr}(N-n-2,\mathbb{P}V^{*})

manifold parameterizing all
(N-n-2)

in
\mathbb{P}V^{*}

\mathcal{O}(1)
the restricted hyperplane

\mathrm{Gr}

(N-n-2)

subspaces that intersect with

\mathcal{Z}_{X}\subset\mathrm{Gr}

is the zero locus of a unique (up to scaling) section

which is called the

of
X
. The special linear group

\mathrm{SL}(V)
acts on
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{Gr},\mathcal{O}(d))

in a natural way.

Definition 13

(Chow-stability)
.

In Mumford’s GIT, we call
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}

is
Chow-semistable
if the zero vector is not in the Zariski-closure
of the orbit
\mathrm{SL}(V)\cdot f_{X}
. Otherwise,
X
is called

Chow-unstable
. We call
X
is
Chow-polystable
if the
orbit
\mathrm{SL}(V)\cdot f_{X}
is Zariski-closed, and
X
is
called
Chow-stable
if, further,
f_{X}
has a finite stabilizer.

The Hilbert–Mumford criterion allows us to only check the orbits
under all 1-PS
\lambda:\mathbb{C}^{*}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{SL}(V)
.
Suppose that in
\mathbb{P}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{Gr},\mathcal{O}(d))

the point
[\lambda(t)\cdot f_{X}]
converges to
[f_{X}^{\lambda}]

as

\lambda(t)\cdot f_{X}^{\lambda}=t^{-w(X,\lambda)}f_{X}^{\lambda}

for some integer
w(X,\lambda)
, which is called the

Hilbert–Mumford criterion says that

is Chow-semistable iff

for all 1-PS
\lambda

In GIT, it is well-known that
w(X,\lambda)
is also equal to the
limit slope of the associated Kempf–Ness functional along a geodesic
ray. To define Kempf–Ness functional, we need to choose a norm for
the Chow-forms. In
[
62
]
, there is a canonical
choice such that the resulting Kempf–Ness functional can be expressed
by the Monge–Ampère energy on
X
. We review this below.

First we take a reference Hermitian norm
H_{r}
on
V
, it induces
a norm on
\wedge^{n+1}V
. Via the Plücker embedding
p:\mathrm{Gr}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\left(\wedge^{n+1}V\right)
,
the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(d)
is equipped a metric
\left\|\right\|
.
Let
\Omega
be the restriction on
\mathrm{Gr}
of the Fubini–Study
metric. In
[
62
]
§4, they define a “norm”

\left\|\right\|_{\mathrm{Chow}}
on
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{Gr},\mathcal{O}(d))

by

(4.1)

\log\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{Chow}}^{2}\coloneqq\fint_{\mathrm{Gr}}\log\left\|f\right\|^{2}\ \Omega^{m},\ f\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{Gr},\mathcal{O}(d))\backslash\{0\},

where
m=\dim\mathrm{Gr}
. Note that the integrand is a quasi-psh
function, so it is integrable. It satisfies

for

and
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{Chow}}=0

\mathrm{SU}(V,H_{r})

(special unitary group w.r.t.

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{Gr},\mathcal{O}(d))

Although the triangle inequality is absent,

\mathcal{O}(-1)

over
\mathbb{P}\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathrm{Gr},\mathcal{O}(d))

associated Kempf–Ness functional is defined by

(4.2)

\mathbf{F}:\mathrm{SU}(V,H_{r})\backslash\mathrm{SL}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\ \mathbf{F}\left([g]\right)=\log\frac{\left\|g\cdot f_{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{Chow}}^{2}}{\left\|f_{X}\right\|_{\mathrm{Chow}}^{2}}.

This functional can be expressed by the MA-energy on
X
. This was
originally shown in
[
81
]
using Deligne’s pairings,
and then by Phong–Sturm
[

[

For
H\in\mathcal{H}(V)

\mathcal{O}(1)|_{X}
via

.
More explicitly, take a reference metric

\mathbf{FS}(H)=\phi_{r}+\log\sup_{s\in V\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\left|s\right|_{\phi_{r}}^{2}}{H(s,s)}=\phi_{r}+\log\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|s_{i}\right|_{\phi_{r}}^{2},

Theorem 14

(

Proposition 16

(1) If

(3)
\mathbf{M}_{X}

\left|\mathbf{M}_{X}(H)-\mathbf{M}_{X}(G)\right|\leq\left(\sqrt{N}+1\right)d(H,G),\ \forall H,G\in\mathcal{H}(V).

Proof.

(1) Path
(H_{t})_{t\in I}
induces a metric
h
on the trivial
bundle
E\coloneqq V\times D_{I}\rightarrow D_{I}
with curvature

\geq 0
(in the sense of Griffiths). Then the curvature of
E^{*}

is
\leq 0
. By
[
25
, Proposition 3.3 (iv)]
,
the induced Fubini–Study metric on
\mathcal{O}(1)
(over
\mathbb{P}E^{*}=\mathbb{P}V^{*}\times D_{I}
)
has semi-positive curvature (note that in
[
25
]

L(E)=\mathcal{O}(-1)
). The pullback of
\mathcal{O}(1)
along

X\times D_{I}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}E^{*}
is the line bundle

L\times D_{I}
. The pullback metric on
L\times D_{I}
is given
by the path
\left(\mathbf{FS}(H_{t})\right)
. Since the pullback
metric also has semi-positive curvature, by definition,
\left(\mathbf{FS}(H_{t})\right)

is a psh path.

(2) This is showed in
[
37
]
by a direct computation.
Suppose
(H_{t})
is a geodesic, by (1),
\left(\mathbf{FS}(H_{t})\right)

is a psh path. It implies that
\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(H_{t}),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)

is convex in
t
. Since
\mathbf{E}_{V}(H_{t},H_{r})
is affine
in
t
,
\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{t})
is convex.

(3) By the “sup” formula (
4.3
) for
\mathbf{FS}

and (
2.3
), we have

\left|\mathbf{FS}(H)-\mathbf{FS}(G)\right|\leq d_{\infty}(H,G)\leq\sqrt{N}d(H,G),\ \forall H,G\in\mathcal{H}(V).

By the cocycle property and the formula (
3.1
) for

\mathcal{E}_{X}
, we have

\left|\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(H),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)-\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(G),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)\right|=\left|\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(H),\mathbf{FS}(G)\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{N}d(H,G).

For
\mathbf{E}_{V}
, by (
2.5
) we have

\left|\mathbf{E}_{V}(H,H_{r})-\mathbf{E}_{V}(G,H_{r})\right|=\left|\mathbf{E}_{V}(H,G)\right|\leq d(H,G).

Combining them together, we obtain (
4.5
).
∎

Remark 17
.

It seems that the convexity of
\mathbf{M}_{X}
cannot be extended
to more general paths. If
(H_{t})
is positively curved, by (1)

\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(H_{t})\right)
is convex in

.
But meanwhile,

is the difference of two convex functions. On the other hand, if

is not necessarily a psh

Let

be a geodesic ray, Proposition

16
implies that

is convex and Lipschitz over

. Thus

will non-decreasingly converge to a finite number. Moreover, (
4.5
)
implies that the limit only depends on the asymptotic class of

.

Definition 18

(Chow-weights)
.

Let

be a smooth projective variety. We
define the Chow-weight

as a function on space

(

(4.6)

It is easy to see that for
\forall a>0

By the discussion above Definition

,

is Chow-semistable if and only if

on
\mathcal{N}(V)
.

Proposition 19
.

(1) Chow-weight

is convex
along the geodesics in
\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V),\hat{d}\right)

and
\left(\mathcal{N}(V),d_{2}\right)
.

(2)

is Lipschitz with

d_{p\in[1,\infty]}
.

Proof.

(1) Consider the geodesic (
2.16
) between
\eta_{0},\eta_{1}\in\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)
.
Since for any

,

is a geodesic
in
\mathcal{H}(V)
. By the convexity of
\mathbf{M}_{X}
(Proposition

(2)), we have

\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{t}^{s})\leq(1-s)\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{t}^{0})+s\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{t}^{1}),\ \forall s\in[0,1].

Divide both sides by

By the isometry (

(2) Apply the inequality (

∎

4.2.
Maximal destabilizers for Chow-unstable
varieties

We consider the Chow-unstable case. In GIT, by the works
[
48
,
68
]
,
one can associate to an unstable point a unique (modulo an equivalence
relation) maximal destabilizing 1-PS, which minimizes the normalized
Hilbert–Mumford weight. Since we will not use this general result,
we just give a sketch of it in the setting of Chow-stability, refer
to
[
58
]
§2.2 and appendix 2B for the general
setting.

4.2.1.
Kempf–Rousseau’s maximal destabilizing
1-PS

Suppose that
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
is Chow-unstable, consider
the normalized Chow-weight

\bar{w}(X,\lambda)\coloneqq\frac{w(X,\lambda)}{\left\|\lambda\right\|},

where
\left\|\lambda\right\|
is a norm for 1-PS in
\mathrm{SL}(V)
.
In
[
48
,
68
]
, it is showed
that
\bar{w}(X,\lambda)
admits minimizers which are called the
maximal destabilizing 1-PS, and these minimizers are equivalent to
each other in the following sense.

First, for any 1-PS
\lambda\subset\mathrm{SL}(V)
, one can associate
to it a parabolic subgroup

P(\lambda)\coloneqq\left\{g\in\mathrm{SL}(V)\mid\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t^{-1})\textrm{ exists in }\mathrm{SL}(V)\right\}.

Two non-trivial 1-PS
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\subset\mathrm{SL}(V)

are said to be
equivalent
to each other (denoted by
\lambda_{1}\sim\lambda_{2}
)
if there exists
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in P(\lambda_{1})

such that
\lambda_{2}(t^{n_{2}})=g\cdot\lambda_{1}(t^{n_{1}})\cdot g^{-1}

for all
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. All non-trivial 1-PS modulo this equivalence
relation form a structure called Tits building. Thus the maximal destabilizing
1-PS correspond to a unique point in this building.

4.2.2.
Existence and uniqueness of maximal destabilizers

Instead of using Kempf–Rousseau’s general result, we consider the
following minimization problem for the
L^{p}
-normalized Chow-weight
(
p\in[1,\infty]
)

(4.7)

\inf\left\{\bar{M}_{X,p}(\chi)\coloneqq\frac{M_{X}(\chi)}{\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}}\mid\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)\backslash\{\chi_{tr}\}\right\}.

Since
M_{X}(\chi)
and
\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}
(
2.10
)
are homogeneous,
\bar{M}_{X,p}
is invariant under scaling. Obviously,
this problem is equivalent to

(4.8)

\inf\left\{M_{X}(\chi)\mid\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V),\ \left\|\chi\right\|_{p}=1\right\}.

Although we know that
M_{X}
is
d_{p}
-continuous, the existence
of minimizers is not immediate, since the sphere
\{\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}=1\}

is not compact in
d_{p}
-topology.

Theorem 20
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}

be a smooth projective variety.

(1) For any
p\in[1,\infty]
, the problem (
4.7
)
admits a minimizer.

(2) When
X
is Chow-unstable and
p\in(1,\infty)
, the minimizer
is unique up to rescaling.

Proof.

(1) The main idea is to use another locally compact topology under
which the objective function is lsc.

Fix a base point
H_{0}\in\mathcal{H}(V)
. Recall the tangent space

T_{H_{0}}\mathcal{H}(V)
is identified with the space of
H_{0}
-self-adjoint
operators. Consider the composed map

\Psi:T_{H_{0}}\mathcal{H}(V)\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}(V)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(V),

where the second map is the isometry (
2.14
), and the
first map sends
A\in T_{H_{0}}\mathcal{H}(V)
to the class of the
geodesic ray
H_{t}^{A}\coloneqq H_{0}(e^{tA}\cdot,\cdot)
, which
starts from
H_{0}
with initial tangent vector
A
. Actually,
\Psi(A)

is the eigenspace filtration (
2.13
) of
-A
. By
Cartan–Hadamard theorem, the first map is bijective, so is
\Psi
.

By Example
10
and (
2.2
,

2.10
), we have
\left\|\Psi(A)\right\|_{p}=\left\|A\right\|_{p,H_{0}}

for
\forall A\in T_{H_{0}}\mathcal{H}(V)
. Since
\Psi
is bijective,
minimization problem (
4.8
) is transformed into

\inf\left\{M_{X}\circ\Psi(A)\mid A\in T_{H_{0}}\mathcal{H}(V),\ \left\|A\right\|_{p,H_{0}}=1\right\}.

Recall that
M_{X}
is the limit slope of
\mathbf{M}_{X}
, we have

M_{X}\circ\Psi(A)=\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{1}{t}\left[\mathbf{M}_{X}\left(H_{t}^{A}\right)-\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{0})\right].

Note that for a fixed
t\geq 0
, function
A\mapsto\frac{1}{t}\left[\mathbf{M}_{X}\left(H_{t}^{A}\right)-\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{0})\right]

is continuous w.r.t. the Euclidean topology. It implies that
M_{X}\circ\Psi

is lower semi-continuous on
T_{H_{0}}\mathcal{H}(V)
. Since a lsc
function can attain its infimum over a compact set
\{\left\|A\right\|_{p,H_{0}}=1\}
,
the existence follows.

(2) If
\chi_{0}
and
\chi_{1}
are two minimizers for
\bar{M}_{X,p}
.
Rescaling them, we can assume
\left\|\chi_{0}\right\|_{p}=\left\|\chi_{1}\right\|_{p}=1
.
Then
M_{X}(\chi_{0})=M_{X}(\chi_{1})=\inf\bar{M}_{X,p}
. Let
(\chi_{s})_{s\in[0,1]}

be the geodesic (
2.15
) connecting them. By the
convexity of
M_{X}
(Proposition
19
),
we have

(4.9)

M_{X}(\chi_{s})\leq(1-s)M_{X}(\chi_{0})+sM_{X}(\chi_{1})=\inf\bar{M}_{X,p}<0,\ \forall s\in[0,1].

Let
(s_{i})
be a basis diagonalizing both
\chi_{0}
and
\chi_{1}
,
then we have

\left\|\chi_{s}\right\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|(1-s)\chi_{0}(s_{i})+s\chi_{1}(s_{i})\right|^{p}.

If
\chi_{0}\neq\chi_{1}
, by the uniform convexity of
L^{p}
-norm
when
p\in(1,\infty)
, we have
\left\|\chi_{s}\right\|_{p}<1

for
s\in(0,1)
. It follows that

0>M_{X}(\chi_{s})>\frac{M_{X}(\chi_{s})}{\left\|\chi_{s}\right\|_{p}}=\bar{M}_{X,p}(\chi_{s}),\ \forall s\in(0,1).

Combine with (
4.9
), we have
\bar{M}_{X,p}(\chi_{s})<\inf\bar{M}_{X,p}
.
It is a contradiction, thus
\chi_{0}=\chi_{1}
.
∎

Remark 21
.

In the Chow-semistable case (i.e.
M_{X}\geq 0
), since
M_{X}(\chi_{tr}+c)=0
,

\chi_{tr}+c
(
c\neq 0
) are trivial minimizers for (
4.7
).
In this case, one should minimize
\frac{M_{X}(\chi)}{\left\|\chi-E_{V}(\chi)\right\|_{p}}
,
which is equivalent to the problem

\inf\left\{M_{X}(\chi)\mid\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V),\ \left\|\chi\right\|_{p}=1,\ E_{V}(\chi)=0\right\}.

By the same proof (in additional, require
\mathrm{tr}A=0
), we can
show that it admits a minimizer.

Definition 22
.

For a Chow-unstable variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
, we call the
minimizers of
\frac{M_{X}(\chi)}{\left\|\chi\right\|_{2}}

the
maximal Chow-destabilizers
for
X
, which differ from
each other by a scaling.

4.3.
Symmetries of maximal Chow-destabilizers

The uniqueness of maximal Chow-destabilizer makes it inherit all the
symmetries of
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
. Specifically, suppose that
we have a group
G
and homomorphism
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)
,
then
G
acts on
\mathbb{P}V^{*}
. We suppose that
G
preserves

X
. For instance, for a polarized manifold
(X,L)
, we take
G=\mathrm{Aut}(X,L)

and
V=R_{k}
(
k\gg 1
). The
G
-action on
R_{k}
is defined
by
(g\cdot s)_{x}\coloneqq g\cdot s_{g^{-1}x}
for
g\in G
,
s\in R_{k}

and
x\in X
. Then the embedding
X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}

is
G
-equivariant.

Theorem 23
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
be a smooth
projective variety. Suppose there is a group
G
and a morphism
G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)

such that
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
is preserved by
G
. Then we
have

(1) the Chow-weight
M_{X}:\mathcal{N}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is

G
-invariant w.r.t. the
G
-action on
\mathcal{N}(V)
;

(2) when
X
is Chow-unstable, each maximal Chow-destabilizer
\bar{\chi}\in\mathcal{N}(V)

is fixed by
G
.

Proof.

(1) Group
G
acts
\mathcal{N}(V)
by
g\cdot\chi\coloneqq\chi\circ g^{-1}
,
and on
\mathcal{H}(V)
in the same way. Set
L=\mathcal{O}(1)|_{X}
,

G
acts on
\mathcal{H}(L)
by pulling-back
g\cdot\phi\coloneqq\left(g^{-1}\right)^{*}\phi
.
Then the Fubini-Study map
\mathbf{FS}:\mathcal{H}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(L)

is
G
-equivariant.

First we consider the behavior of
\mathbf{M}_{X}
(
4.4
)
under
G
-action. For any
H\in\mathcal{H}(V)
and
g\in G
, we
have

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{X}(g\cdot H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(g\cdot H),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)-\mathbf{E}_{V}(g\cdot H,H_{r})

\displaystyle*

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(g\cdot\mathbf{FS}(H),g\cdot\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)+\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(g\cdot\mathbf{FS}(H_{r}),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)

\displaystyle-\mathbf{E}_{V}(g\cdot H,g\cdot H_{r})-\mathbf{E}_{V}(g\cdot H_{r},H_{r})

\displaystyle**

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{X}(H)+\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(g\cdot\mathbf{FS}(H_{r}),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)-\mathbf{E}_{V}(g\cdot H_{r},H_{r}),

where in the row
*
we use the equivariance of
\mathbf{FS}
and
the cocycle property for
\mathcal{E}_{X}
and
\mathbf{E}_{V}
,
and in the row
**
we use

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(g\cdot\mathbf{FS}(H),g\cdot\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{X}\left(\mathbf{FS}(H),\mathbf{FS}(H_{r})\right);

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{V}(g\cdot H,g\cdot H_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{V}(H,H_{r}).

For a geodesic ray
(H_{t})
with limit NA norm
\chi
,
(g\cdot H_{t})

is also a geodesic ray with limit norm
g\cdot\chi
. By the above
identity,

\mathbf{M}_{X}(g\cdot H_{t})=\mathbf{M}_{X}(H_{t})+C(g),

where
C(g)
is a constant independent of
t
. Divide both sides
by
t
and let
t\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain
M_{X}(g\cdot\chi)=M_{X}(\chi)
.

(2) It is easy to see
\left\|g\cdot\chi\right\|_{p}=\left\|\chi\right\|_{p}

for all
p
. Hence
\bar{M}_{X,p}
is
G
-invariant by (1). Suppose

X
is Chow-unstable and
p\in(1,\infty)
, by Theorem
20
,

\bar{M}_{X,p}
has a unique minimizer
\bar{\chi}
(up to scaling).
By the invariance, for any
g\in G
,
g\cdot\bar{\chi}
is also
a minimizer, so
g\cdot\bar{\chi}=a\bar{\chi}
for some
a>0
. Take
the
p
-norm on both sides, we have
a=1
. Hence
g\cdot\bar{\chi}=\bar{\chi}

for all
g\in G
.
∎

Since we know the maximal Chow-destabilizer is
G
-invariant, we
can search for it only among the
G
-invariant norms. If further,

M_{X}
has a nice formula on the
G
-invariant norms, we can determine
the minimizer explicitly. The toric case is an example.

Example 24

(toric case)
.

Let
M
be a lattice of rank
n
, and
P\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq M\otimes\mathbb{R}

be a lattice Delzant polytope. Then
P
gives rise to a polarized
toric manifold
(X,L)
, which carries a linearized action by the
complex torus
T_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{C}^{*})
.
For any
k\geq 1
, the section space
V=R_{k}=\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,kL)

is a multiplicity-free
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-module. For each
u\in P_{k}\coloneqq P\cap k^{-1}M
,
there is a section
s_{u,k}\in R_{k}
such that
t\cdot s_{u,k}=t^{ku}s_{u,k}

for
\forall t\in T_{\mathbb{C}}
. We have the weight-decomposition
R_{k}=\bigoplus_{u\in P_{k}}\mathbb{C}\cdot s_{u,k}
.

Assume
k
is sufficiently large such that
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}

is an embedding (actually,
k\geq n-1
is enough). By Theorem
23
,
if the embedded variety is Chow-unstable, then the maximal Chow-destabilizer

\chi_{k}^{*}\in\mathcal{N}(R_{k})
is
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant. The

T_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant NA norms on
R_{k}
can be diagonalized by
the canonical basis
(s_{u,k})_{u\in P_{k}}
. Hence we can search
for
\chi_{k}^{*}
among the norms which are diagonalized by
(s_{u,k})
.
In terms of 1-PS, this amounts to only consider the 1-PS in the torus

H\coloneqq\left\{g\in\mathrm{SL}(R_{k})\mid g\textrm{ is diagonalized by basis }(s_{u,k})_{u}\right\}\cong\left(\mathbb{C}^{*}\right)^{N_{k}-1}.

In
[
47
]
, they found that the Chow-weights for

H
-action can be given by the secondary polytope
\Sigma(P_{k})

associated to
P_{k}
. Hence the maximal Chow-destabilizer can be
determined by
\Sigma(P_{k})
. We will discuss these in
[
80
]
.

The following result is obtained in
[
49
, Theorem 2.2, Corollary 2.3, 2.7.]

by a different argument.

Corollary 25
.

Let
(X,L)
be the toric manifold
given in the above example. Suppose
X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}

is an embedding. If
X
is Chow-semistable w.r.t. the torus
H\subset\mathrm{SL}(R_{k})
,
then
X
is Chow-semistable w.r.t. the whole group
\mathrm{SL}(R_{k})
.

Proof.

If
X
is Chow-unstable w.r.t.
\mathrm{SL}(R_{k})
, consider the
maximal destabilizer, we obtain a
T_{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant norm
\chi

such that the Chow weight
M_{X}(\chi)<0
. By approximation, we can
find a 1-PS of
H
with negative Chow-weight. This contradicts the
assumption.
∎

4.4.

For a polarized variety

(2) Suppose that

\frac{w_{rk}}{rkN_{rk}}=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})-\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\frac{1}{2rk}+\mathrm{O}(k^{-2}),

and the leading term
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
is called
the NA Monge–Ampère energy.

(3) We call
(X,L)
is
K-semistable
if
\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\geq 0

for all normal ample test-configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})

for
(X,L)
. Otherwise,
(X,L)
is called K-unstable.

There are many works studying the relation between Chow and K-stability,
for instance
[
71
,
33
,
62
,
37
,
57
,
67
,
61
, etc]
.
We only recall the following relation.

Theorem 28

(
[
67
]
)
.

The asymptotic Chow-semistability of
(X,L)
implies the K-semistability.

Its contrapositive proposition is that if
(X,L)
is K-unstable,
then there is a sequence
k_{j}\rightarrow\infty
such that
(X,k_{j}L)

is Chow-unstable. This can be strengthened as follows, see §
7.2.2

for its proof.

Theorem 29
.

If
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})

is an ample test-configuration for
(X,L)
such that
\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})<0

and
r\mathcal{L}
is line bundle, then

is Chow-unstable
when

This motivates us to study K-unstable varieties from the angle of
Chow-unstability. For K-unstable manifolds, Xia
[

]

showed the existence and uniqueness of the maximal K-destabilizing
ray, which corresponds to a non-Archimedean metric on

by
[

,
52
]
.
We call this NA metric the maximal K-destabilizer (Definition

).
It is natural to ask

Does the sequence of maximal Chow-destabilizers “converge” to

This can be seen as the NA counterpart of Donaldson’s theory
[

]

for balanced embeddings and cscK metrics. Before we dive into the
NA world, we briefly review
[

, which emphasizes

4.5.
Balanced norms quantize cscK metrics

In Kähler geometry, quantization method is to approximate the geometry
of
\mathcal{H}(L)
by the geometry of
\mathcal{H}(R_{k})
, also
includes the functionals defined over them. There is a pair of maps
connecting them.

For
k\geq 1
such that
kL
is very ample, the Fubini–Study map

\mathbf{FS}_{k}:\mathcal{H}(R_{k})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(L)
is
defined by

\mathbf{FS}_{k}(H)=\phi_{r}+\frac{1}{k}\log\frac{1}{N_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}\left|s_{i}\right|_{k\phi_{r}}^{2},

where
\phi_{r}\in\mathcal{H}(L)
is any reference metric and
(s_{i})

is any
H
-orthonormal basis of
R_{k}
.

The Hilbert map
\mathbf{H}_{k}:\mathcal{H}(L)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(R_{k})

is defined by

\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)(s,s^{\prime})=\int_{X}\left\langle s,s^{\prime}\right\rangle_{k\phi}\mathrm{MA}(\phi),\ \forall s,s^{\prime}\in R_{k}.

Compose two maps together, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{H}(L),
we have

\mathbf{FS}_{k}\circ\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)=\phi+\frac{1}{k}\log\frac{\rho_{k}(\phi)}{N_{k}},\ \rho_{k}(\phi)\coloneqq\sum_{i}\left|s_{i}\right|_{k\phi}^{2},

where
(s_{i})
is any
\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)
-orthonormal basis,
and
\rho_{k}(\phi)
is called the Bergman kernel. Tian–Bouche–Catlin–Zelditch’s
expansion gives

(4.11)

\rho_{k}(\phi)=\frac{L^{n}}{n!}k^{n}+\frac{L^{n}}{n!}\frac{S(\phi)}{2}k^{n-1}+\mathrm{O}(k^{n-2}).

It implies that
\mathbf{FS}_{k}\circ\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)\rightarrow\phi

as
k\rightarrow\infty
, so we can think that norms
\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)

quantize
\phi
.

The MA-energy
\mathcal{E}
is quantized by
\mathbf{E}_{R_{k}}

(
2.4
). Fix a reference metric
\phi_{r}\in\mathcal{H}(L)
,
we denote
\mathbf{E}_{k}(H)\coloneqq k^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{R_{k}}(H,\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi_{r}))
.
By (
4.11
), we have

(4.12)

\mathbf{E}_{k}\circ\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)=\mathcal{E}(\phi)-\frac{1}{2k}\mathcal{M}(\phi)+\mathrm{O}(k^{-2}),\ \forall\phi\in\mathcal{H}(L),

where
\mathcal{M}(\phi)
is the K-energy (
3.2
).
Then Donaldson introduced
\mathcal{Q}_{k}:\mathcal{H}(L)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(denoted by
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
in
[
37
]
)

(4.13)

\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\phi)\coloneqq 2k\left(\mathcal{E}(\phi)-\mathbf{E}_{k}\circ\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)\right).

By (
4.12
), it converges to
\mathcal{M}
pointwisely.
Moreover, it is convex along the geodesics, see
[
5
, P473]
.
By
[
37
, Corollary 1]
, the critical points
of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
are
balanced metrics
which satisfy

\mathbf{FS}_{k}\circ\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)=\phi\ \Leftrightarrow\ \rho_{k}(\phi)\equiv N_{k}.

There is a bijection (both sets may be empty):

\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{H}(L)\mid\mathbf{FS}_{k}\circ\mathbf{H}_{k}(\phi)=\phi\right\}\xrightleftharpoons[\mathbf{FS}_{k}]{\mathbf{H}_{k}}\left\{H\in\mathcal{H}(R_{k})\mid\mathbf{H}_{k}\circ\mathbf{FS}_{k}(H)=H\right\}.

The norms in the right-hand set are called
balanced norms
.
Let
\iota_{k}:X\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}R_{k}^{*}
be the Kodaira
embedding. The balanced norms are exactly the critical points of
\mathbf{M}_{\iota_{k}(X)}

(
4.4
), the Kempf–Ness functional for Chow-stability.
So the existence of critical points of
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is equivalent
to the Chow-polystability of
\iota_{k}(X)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
admits a critical point
\phi_{\mathrm{cscK}}
,
since
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
approximates
\mathcal{M}
, naturally one
may expect that
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
also admits a critical point
\phi_{k}

for
k\gg 1
. If so, then
(X,L)
would be asymptotic Chow-polystable.
Furthermore,
\phi_{k}
should converge to
\phi_{\mathrm{cscK}}
.
This is not true in general, see
[
57
,
59
]
.
But when the automorphism group is discrete,
[
33
]

confirms these expectations.

Theorem 30

(
[
33
]
)
.

Suppose that
(X,L)
admits a cscK metric

\phi_{\mathrm{cscK}}\in\mathcal{H}(L)
and
\mathrm{Aut}(X,L)/\mathbb{C}^{*}

is discrete, then
(X,kL)
is Chow-stable for
k\gg 1
. Moreover,
there exists a sequence of balanced norms
H_{k}\in\mathcal{H}(R_{k})

such that the corresponding balanced metrics
\mathbf{FS}_{k}(H_{k})

converge to
\phi_{\mathrm{cscK}}
in
C^{\infty}
-norm.

5.
Non-Archimedean pluripotential theory

To connect the maximal Chow-destabilizers to the maximal K-stabilizer,
we adapt the variational approach
[
37
]
from
the Archimedean setting to the NA setting. It is based on the NA pluripotential
theory developed by Boucksom and Jonsson
[
10
,
11
,
13
]
.
We briefly review it below.

5.1.
Berkovich’s analytification

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized variety over
\mathbb{C}
. We equip
\mathbb{C}

with the trivial valuation. As a set, Berkovich’s analytification
of
X
is

X^{\mathrm{an}}\coloneqq\left\{v:\mathbb{C}(Y)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\mid Y\subset X\right\},

where
Y
runs over all subvarieties, and
v
is any valuation on
the functional field
\mathbb{C}(Y)
which extends the trivial valuation
on
\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{C}(Y)
. Let
\left|\right|_{v}\coloneqq e^{-v}

be the associated NA norm. For
f\in\mathbb{C}(Y)
, we write
\left|f(v)\right|\coloneqq\left|f\right|_{v}=e^{-v(f)}
.
Denote by
v_{tr}
the trivial valuation on
\mathbb{C}(X)
.

The kernel map
\mathrm{ker}:X^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow X
sends
v
to
the generic point of
Y
. The center
c(v)
of
v
is the unique
scheme point on
Y
such that
v\geq 0
on
\mathcal{O}_{Y,c(v)}

and
v>0
on the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{Y,c(v)}\subset\mathcal{O}_{Y,c(v)}
.
This gives us the center map
c:X^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow X
. Berkovich’s
topology on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is the weakest one such that
\mathrm{ker}

is continuous, and for any open subset
U\subset X
and
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
,
the map

U^{\mathrm{an}}\coloneqq\mathrm{ker}^{-1}(U)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\ v\mapsto\left|f(v)\right|

is continuous. It makes
X^{\mathrm{an}}
a compact Hausdorff space, but
the center map
c
is anti-continuous (preimage of open subset is
closed). Let
X^{\mathrm{val}}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
be the set of valuations
on
\mathbb{C}(X)
, and
X^{\mathrm{div}}\subset X^{\mathrm{val}}

the set of divisorial valuations, which is dense in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
.

The line bundle
L
also has an analytification, denoted by
L^{\mathrm{an}}
.
A metric
\phi
on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
(called a NA metric) assigns to every
local section
s\in\Gamma(U,L)
a function
\left|s\right|_{\phi}:U^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow[0,\infty]

such that

\left|fs\right|_{\phi}(v)=\left|f(v)\right|\cdot\left|s\right|_{\phi}(v),\ \forall f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U),\ \forall v\in U^{\mathrm{an}}.

Then we have
\left|s+s^{\prime}\right|_{\phi}\leq\max\left(\left|s\right|_{\phi},\left|s^{\prime}\right|_{\phi}\right)

for
s,s^{\prime}\in\Gamma(U,L)
.

Owing to the trivial norm on
\mathbb{C}
, there is a distinguished
metric
\phi_{tr}
on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
, called the
trivial metric
.
We will denote by
\left|s\right|_{tr}
or
\left|s\right|
the
norm function of a section
s
w.r.t. the trivial metric. Next, we
give the definition of
\phi_{tr}
. Given
s\in\Gamma(U,L)
and

v\in U^{\mathrm{an}}
, we firstly define the valuation
v(s)
, then define

\left|s\right|_{tr}(v)\coloneqq e^{-v(s)}.

(1) If
c(v)\in U
, we can trivialize
s
at
c(v)
, namely take
a generator
\mathfrak{e}
of the stalk
L_{c(v)}
and let
s|_{c(v)}=f\mathfrak{e}

for a
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,c(v)}
. Then we define
v(s)=v(f)\in[0,\infty]
.
By the property of
c(v)
,
v(s)
is independent of the choices
of
\mathfrak{e}
.

(2) If
c(v)\notin U
, we can’t trivialize
s
at
c(v)
. Since

c(v)\in\overline{\mathrm{ker}(v)}
, there is a morphism between
stalks
\alpha:L_{c(v)}\rightarrow L_{\mathrm{ker}(v)}
. Take a generator

\mathfrak{e}\in L_{c(v)}
, then
\alpha(\mathfrak{e})
is a generator
of
L_{\mathrm{ker}(v)}
. Let
s|_{\mathrm{ker}(v)}=f\alpha(\mathfrak{e})

for a
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,\mathrm{ker}(v)}
, now we define
v(s)=v(f)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
,
which is also independent of the choices of
\mathfrak{e}
.

With the trivial metric
\phi_{tr}
, other metrics can be represented
as
\phi_{tr}+\varphi
, where
\varphi:X^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

is a function. We will use this function
\varphi
to represent the
metric
\phi_{tr}+\varphi
. Given a local section
s\in\Gamma(U,L^{m})
,
its norm function w.r.t. the metric
\varphi
is

\left|s\right|_{\varphi}\coloneqq\left|s\right|_{tr}e^{-m\varphi}=e^{-v(s)-m\varphi}:U^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow[0,\infty].

There is a
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action
*
on the functions on
X^{\mathrm{an}}

which is defined by

(5.1)

(a*\varphi)(v)\coloneqq a\varphi(a^{-1}v),\ a>0,\ v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}.

5.2.
NA psh metrics and energy functionals

Given a nonzero section
s\in R_{m}
, the function
m^{-1}\log\left|s\right|_{tr}

defines a metric on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
. A Fubini–Study metric
\varphi

on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
is defined to have the form

(5.2)

\varphi=\frac{1}{m}\max_{j}\left\{\log\left|s_{j}\right|_{tr}+\lambda_{j}\right\},

where
m\geq 1
,
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\{s_{j}\}\subset R_{m}

are finitely many sections without common zero. Note that
\varphi

is continuous and
\sup_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\varphi=\varphi(v_{tr})
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}

be the set of all Fubini–Study metrics.

A test-configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
of
(X,L)
induces
a function
\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, refer to
[
13
, §A1]
.
By
[
13
, Corollary A13]
,
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\mapsto\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}

gives a bijection from the set of normal ample test-configurations
to
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}
, the set of metrics (
5.2
)
with
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

A psh metric
\varphi:X^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

(excluding
\varphi\equiv-\infty
) is defined to be the pointwise
limit of a decreasing sequence in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}
.
It is usc and satisfies
\sup_{X^{\mathrm{an}}}\varphi=\varphi(v_{tr})
.
Denote by
\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
the set of psh metrics on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
,
and
\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
the set of continuous psh metrics on

L^{\mathrm{an}}
. By Dini’s theorem,
\mathrm{CPSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
is the closure
of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}
in the space of continuous functions
with respect to the
C^{0}
-norm.

The NA Monge–Ampère energy
\mathbb{E}
is a functional on
\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
.
For
\varphi=\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}
induced by a normal ample test-configuration

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
,
\mathbb{E}(\varphi)
is the leading
term of expansion (
4.10
). For general
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
,
we define

(5.3)

\mathbb{E}(\varphi)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\mathbb{E}(\phi)\mid\varphi\leq\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}.

The space of finite-energy metrics is defined as

\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}(L)\coloneqq\left\{\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)\mid\mathbb{E}(\varphi)>-\infty\right\}.

There is a metric
d_{1}
on
\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}(L)
making it
a complete geodesic space, see
[
65
]

and
[
78
, Example 5.12]
.

The NA K-energy
\mathbb{M}
is introduced in
[
9
]

as a modification of the Donaldson-Futaki invariants. For
\varphi=\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}

induced by an ample test-configuration
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
,
it is equal to

(5.4)

\mathbb{M}(\varphi)=\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})-V^{-1}(\mathcal{X}_{0}-\mathcal{X}_{0,\mathrm{red}})\cdot\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L}).

So if
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is reduced,
\mathbb{M}(\varphi)
is the
same as
\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
. It can be extended
to a functional
\mathbb{M}:\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}(L)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
,
see
[
12
, §4.1]
. Compared with
\mathrm{DF}
,

\mathbb{M}
is homogeneous, i.e.
\mathbb{M}(a*\phi)=a\mathbb{M}(\phi)

for
a>0
.

Remark 31
.

The Definition
27
(3) for K-semistability is
equivalent to that
\mathbb{M}\geq 0
on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}
.
One direction follows by the inequality (
5.4
).
Another direction follows by
[

, Proposition 7.16]
.

5.3.
The limit NA metric associated to

We recall an important construction in

5.4.
The NA formula for Chow-weights

We have defined the Chow-weights

.

.

First we clarify some notations. Let

be

\phi_{r}
on

\omega_{r}

\mathbf{FS}:\mathcal{H}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(L)

\mathrm{FS}(\chi)=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\left(\log\left|s_{i}\right|_{tr}+\chi(s_{i})\right),

Theorem 33
.

Let

(1) The limit NA metric associated to the psh ray

\mathrm{FS}(\chi_{H})
.

(2) NA formula for Chow-weight:

(5.11)

M_{X}(\chi_{H})\coloneqq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\mathbf{M}_{X}\left(H_{t}\right)=\mathbb{E}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi_{H})-E_{V}(\chi_{H}),

where

\mathrm{FS}

Fubini–Study map (

Proof.

(1) It follows by a direct computation. As Example

we take a

-orthonormal basis

\chi_{H}

is also diagonalized by
(s_{i})
and
\chi_{H}(s_{i})=\lambda_{i}

Hence
\mathrm{FS}(\chi_{H})=\max_{i}\left(\log\left|s_{i}\right|+\lambda_{i}\right)

By Proposition

is a psh ray. Explicitly, we have

The induced

-psh function (

)
on

is

Denote by

By definition (

To compute the Lelong number, it is easier to consider

It is also
\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{r}
-psh and satisfies

so we have

\displaystyle\sigma(v)(V)=\sigma(v)(U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\sigma(v)\left(\log\left|s_{i}(x)\right|_{\phi_{r}}^{2}\right)+\sigma(v)\left(\log\left|\tau\right|^{-2\mu_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{i}\left(v(s_{i})-\mu_{i}\right),

where we used some basic properties of Lelong number, see

Finally,

u^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(v)=-\sigma(v)(V)+\varLambda=\max_{i}\left(\lambda_{i}-v(s_{i})\right)=\mathrm{FS}(\chi_{H})(v).

(2) In the terminology of

we have

For the another term of

Definition 34

(1) Given a polarized variety

(2) Let

The interactive properties between them are important in the sequel.

Proposition 35
.

Given a polarized variety
(X,L)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{SN}_{k}

(1) Denote

(4) We only show the first identity, the second one is similar. Let

hence
\mathrm{FS}_{k}(\chi)\geq\inf

5.6.
Properties of maximal Chow-destabilizers

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}
be a smooth projective variety. Set

. We have maps

(

) and

which is defined as

\mathrm{SN}(\varphi)(s)\coloneqq\inf_{v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}}\left\{v(s)+\varphi(v)\right\},\ \forall s\in V,

where we take
s
as a section of

. These two maps also satisfy
the properties in Proposition
35
.

Theorem 36
.

Suppose
X\subset\mathbb{P}V^{*}

is Chow-unstable, let
\bar{\chi}\in\mathcal{N}(V)
be a maximal
Chow-destabilizer for
X
, namely a minimizer of the normalized Chow-weight

\bar{M}_{X}(\chi)=M_{X}(\chi)/\left\|\chi\right\|_{2}
.

(1) The jumping numbers
\lambda_{i}(\bar{\chi})
satisfy

(5.16)

\sum_{i=1}^{\dim V}\lambda_{i}(\bar{\chi})=0\ \textrm{and}\ \lambda_{i}(\bar{\chi})\leq\left(-\inf_{\mathcal{N}(V)}\bar{M}\right)^{-1}\left\|\bar{\chi}\right\|,\ \forall i.

(2)
\mathrm{SN}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\bar{\chi})=\bar{\chi}
.

Proof.

(1) First, we claim that
\breve{\chi}\coloneqq(-\inf\bar{M})\frac{\bar{\chi}}{\left\|\bar{\chi}\right\|}

is the unique minimizer of

\inf\left\{\breve{M}(\chi)\coloneqq M(\chi)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\chi\right\|_{2}^{2}\mid\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)\right\}.

For any
\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)
, consider
h(t)\coloneqq\breve{M}(t\chi)=tM(\chi)+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\left\|\chi\right\|_{2}^{2}

for
t>0
. If
M(\chi)\geq 0
, we have
\inf_{t>0}h(t)=0
; if
M(\chi)<0
,
we have
\inf_{t>0}h(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\bar{M}(\chi)^{2}
and the infimum
is attained at
t=-\bar{M}(\chi)/\left\|\chi\right\|
. It
implies that
\breve{\chi}
is a minimizer of
\breve{M}
. The uniqueness
is due to the convexity of
M_{X}
(Proposition
19
)
and the uniform convexity of
\chi\mapsto\left\|\chi\right\|_{2}^{2}
.

Recall the NA formula (
5.11
) of
M_{X}
,

\displaystyle\breve{M}(\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}(\chi)+\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}(\chi)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi)+\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\lambda_{i}(\chi)-1)^{2}-\frac{1}{2},

where
\lambda_{i}(\chi)
are jumping numbers of
\chi
. Function
t\mapsto\breve{M}(\breve{\chi}+t)

attains its minimum at
t=0
. Take derivative at
t=0
, we obtain

\sum\lambda_{i}(\breve{\chi})=0
, so
\sum\lambda_{i}(\bar{\chi})=0
.

Given any
\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)
, consider
\chi^{\prime}=\chi\wedge(\chi_{tr}+1)
,
i.e. the minimum of
\chi
and
\chi_{tr}+1
. Its jumping numbers
are
\lambda_{i}(\chi^{\prime})=\min\{\lambda_{i}(\chi),1\}
. Since
\mathbb{E}

and
\mathrm{FS}
are increasing,
\mathbb{E}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi^{\prime})\leq\mathbb{E}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi)
.
These imply
\breve{M}(\chi^{\prime})\leq\breve{M}(\chi)
. If we take
\chi=\breve{\chi}
,
then
\chi^{\prime}
is also a minimizer of
\breve{M}
, thus
\chi^{\prime}=\chi

by the uniqueness. So we have
\lambda_{i}(\breve{\chi})\leq 1
for all

i
. This gives us the upper bound in (
5.16
).

(2) For any
\chi\in\mathcal{N}(V)
with
\lambda_{i}(\chi)\leq 1
for
all
i
(i.e.
\chi\leq\chi_{tr}+1
). Consider
\chi^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathrm{SN}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi)
.
Since
\mathrm{SN}
and
\mathrm{FS}
are increasing, we have
\chi^{\prime}\leq\mathrm{SN}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\chi_{tr}+1)=\chi_{tr}+1
,
hence
\lambda_{i}(\chi^{\prime})\leq 1
for all
i
. By Proposition
35
,
we have
\chi^{\prime}\geq\chi
and
\mathrm{FS}(\chi^{\prime})=\mathrm{FS}(\chi)
.
Inequality
\chi^{\prime}\geq\chi
implies
\lambda_{i}(\chi^{\prime})\geq\lambda_{i}(\chi)
.
By these facts, we can conclude
\breve{M}(\chi^{\prime})\leq\breve{M}(\chi)
.
If we take
\chi=\breve{\chi}
, then
\chi^{\prime}
is also a minimizer,
thus
\chi^{\prime}=\chi
by the uniqueness. Hence
\mathrm{SN}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\breve{\chi})=\breve{\chi}
,
so
\mathrm{SN}\circ\mathrm{FS}(\bar{\chi})=\bar{\chi}
.
∎

6.
Maximal K-destabilizers

6.1.
The maximal K-destabilizing rays of Xia

For a polarized manifold
(X,L)
, Donaldson
[
36
]

showed a moment-weight inequality

\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{H}(L)}\mathrm{Cal}(\phi)^{1/2}\geq\sup_{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})}\frac{-\mathrm{DF}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})}{\left\|(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})\right\|_{2}},

where

is the Calabi energy and
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{L})
runs over all
test-configuration for

holds.

[

confirmed Donaldson’s

6.2.
Symmetries of the maximal K-destabilizer

Thanks to the uniqueness, the maximal K-destabilizing rays and K-destabilizers
inherit all the symmetries of
(X,L)
.

Let
G
be any subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(X,L)
. It acts on
\mathcal{H}(L)

by pulling-back
g\cdot\phi\coloneqq\left(g^{-1}\right)^{*}\phi
.
For a metric
\phi=\phi_{r}+u
(
u
is a function), we have
g\cdot\phi=g\cdot\phi_{r}+u\circ g^{-1}
.
It is easy to check that for any
p\in[1,\infty)
,
G
-action preserves
the
L^{p}
-Finsler metric on
\mathcal{H}(L)
, so it preserves
Darvas’s metric
d_{p}
. After completion,
G
acts on
\mathcal{E}^{p}(L)

by isometries.

If
\ell=(\ell_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a geodesic ray in
\mathcal{E}^{p}(L)
,
so is
g\cdot\ell\coloneqq(g\cdot\ell_{t})
. If
\ell
is
d_{p}
-asymptotic
to
\ell^{\prime}
, so are
g\cdot\ell
and
g\cdot\ell^{\prime}
. Hence
G
acts
on
\mathcal{R}^{p}(L)/_{\sim}
by defining
g\cdot[\ell]\coloneqq[g\cdot\ell]
,
and it preserves the chordal metric (
3.3
).

On the non-Archimedean side,
G
acts on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
in the following
way. For a subvariety
Y\subset X
and
v\in Y^{\mathrm{val}}
,
we define
g\cdot v\in(gY)^{\mathrm{val}}
by

(g\cdot v)f\coloneqq v(f\circ g),\ \forall f\in\mathbb{C}(gY).

The
G
-action on
\mathrm{PSH}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}(L)
is defined as
g\cdot\phi\coloneqq\phi\circ g^{-1}
.
Hence the trivial metric (
\phi\equiv 0
) is
G
-invariant, this
can also be seen from

v(g\cdot s)=(g^{-1}\cdot v)(s),\ \forall s\in R_{k}.

One can check that
\mathbb{E}
is
G
-invariant, so the subspace

\mathcal{E}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{NA}}}^{1}(L)\coloneqq\{\mathbb{E}>-\infty\}
is preserved
by
G
.

(

-equivariant.

Proof.

By the definition (

Fabry disease is a genetic metabolic disease caused by a lysosomal storage disorder. The gene responsible for Fabry disease is the α-galactosidase A (GLA) gene located on the X chromosome [1]. The decreased activity of GLA enzyme leads to systemic accumulation of glycospingolipids in lysosomes. Because of its X chromosome-linked nature, Fabry disease affects only hemizygous males carrying mutated GLA gene. Heterozygous females live as an asymptomatic carrier in her younger age or throughout her life. In case of any adverse disease, a heterozygous female develops late-onset organ disorders including cardiomyopathy [2].

A 74-year-old female patient was referred to our hospital for treatment of chronic heart failure. The patient has been living with a pacemaker due to atrial fibrillation for 10 years. The patient’s echocardiography exhibited left ventricular hypertrophy with post-inferior hypokinesis. A screening urinary examination revealed the presence of aggregations of small, round cellular structures (Fig. 1a plain and Fig. 1b Sternheimer stain), indicating Mulberry body. The subsequent diagnosis was made by echocardiography, which indicated hypertrophy of left ventricular wall, interventricular septal hypertrophy 1.3 cm (Fig. 1c) and left ventricular lateral wall hypertrophy 1.4 cm (Fig. 1d). Regarding other supportive imaging studies including cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) was not undertaken due to facility unavailability. A conclusive diagnosis of Fabry disease was established based on the presence of mulberry bodies in the urine sediment, the echocardiographic findings of concentric left ventricular hypertrophy, and confirmatory genetic testing. Sequencing of the GLA gene identified a rare missense variant, c.1228A > G (p.Thr410Ala), consistent with a pathogenic mutation associated with residual enzyme activity. The genetic study revealed the rare GLA missense mutation c.1228A > G p.Thr410Ala. This specific mutation frequently results in residual enzyme activity [3].

The patient’s urinalysis and echocardiography. (a) Aggregations of small, round cellular structures in the patient’s urinary sediment (plain). (b) A pale blue spherical whorl-like structure in Sternheimer stain. The patient’s echocardiography showed hypertrophy of left ventricular wall; (c) interventricular septal hypertrophy (1.3 cm) and (d) left ventricular lateral wall hypertrophy (1.4 cm).

The presence of mulberry body in the urine may serve as a diagnostic indicator for Fabry disease in a female patient with progressive unknown cause hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

Massless Majorana spinors in the Kerr spacetime
Tianyuan Cai †, Xiao Zhang ♭

Abstract
. In this paper, we show that massive Majorana spinors ( 1.7 ) do not exist if they are t t -dependent or ϕ \phi -dependent in Kerr, or Kerr-(A)dS spacetimes. For massless Majorana spinors in the non-extreme Kerr spacetime, the Dirac equation can be separated into radial and angular equations, parameterized by two complex constants ϵ 1 \epsilon_{1} , ϵ 2 \epsilon_{2} . If at least one of ϵ 1 \epsilon_{1} , ϵ 2 \epsilon_{2} is zero, massless Majorana spinors can be solved explicitly. If ϵ 1 \epsilon_{1} , ϵ 2 \epsilon_{2} are nonzero, we prove the nonexistence of massless time-periodic Majorana spinors in the non-extreme Kerr spacetime which are L p L^{p} outside the event horizon for 0 < p ≤ 6 | ϵ 1 | + | ϵ 2 | + 2 0<p\leq\frac{6}{|\epsilon_{1}|+|\epsilon_{2}|+2} . We then provide the Hamiltonian formulation for massless Majorana spinors and prove that the self-adjointness of the Hamiltonian leads to the angular momentum a = 0 a=0 and spacetime reduces to the Schwarzschild spacetime, moreover, the massless Majorana spinor must be ϕ \phi -independent. Finally, we show that, in the Schwarzschild spacetime, for initial data with L 2 L^{2} decay at infinity, the probability of the massless Majorana spinors to be in any compact region of space tends to zero as time tends to infinity.

Denote

Proof:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

6.

In this section, we adopt the argument in
[

to prove the integral representation of

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
R
be a solution of (
5.12
) with
\epsilon_{1}\neq 0
,
\epsilon_{2}\neq 0
. For Cauchy data
R_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)

where

{t_{ab}^{\omega}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{{t_{a}}\bar{t}_{b}}{|t_{1}|^{2}+|t_{2}|^{2}}d\alpha

(6.1)

with

Proof:
By Lemma
5.2
, (
5.11
) becomes

e^{-itH_{r,u_{1},u_{2}}}R_{0}=\frac{1}{(u_{2}-u_{1})+\mathcal{O}(1)}\sum_{\omega}e^{-i\omega t}\Big(R_{-\infty,u_{2}}^{\omega},R_{0}\Big)R_{-\infty,u_{2}}^{\omega}.

(6.2)

By Lemma
5.3
, the right-hand side of (
6.2
) converges to an integral as

At

,

R_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(R_{-\infty,u_{2}}^{\omega},R_{0}\Big)R_{-\infty,u_{2}}^{\omega}d\omega.

Furthermore, for positive constant
\tau
,

R_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}\Big(R_{-\infty,u_{2}+\tau}^{\omega},R_{0}\Big)R^{\omega}_{-\infty,u_{2}+\tau}d\omega.

(6.3)

Taking the average over
\tau
in the interval
[0,T]
with

(6.4)

Let

(6.5)

R^{\omega}_{-\infty,u_{2}+\tau}=\sum\limits^{2}_{a=1}c_{a}(\tau)R^{\omega}_{a}

) is satisfied at

, for sufficiently large

, (

\sum^{2}_{a,b=1}t_{ab}\Big(R^{\omega}_{b},R_{0}\Big)R^{\omega}_{a},\hskip 10.00002ptt_{ab}=\frac{1}{T}\int^{T}_{0}c_{a}(\tau)\overline{c_{b}(\tau)}d\tau

\begin{split}&c_{1}(\tau)\Big(g^{+}_{\infty 1}e^{iw(u_{2}+\tau)}-g^{-}_{\infty 1}e^{-iw(u_{2}+\tau)}\Big)\\
&+c_{2}(\tau)\Big(g^{+}_{\infty 2}e^{iw(u_{2}+\tau)}-g^{-}_{\infty 2}e^{-iw(u_{2}+\tau)}\Big)+\mathcal{O}\left(\tau^{-1}\right)=0.\end{split}

(6.7)

|c_{1}|^{2}+|c_{2}|^{2}=1.

Therefore, the solution of (

\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{Z}\begin{pmatrix}g^{+}_{\infty 2}e^{iw(u_{2}+\tau)}-g^{-}_{\infty 2}e^{-iw(u_{2}+\tau)}\\
-g^{+}_{\infty 1}e^{iw(u_{2}+\tau)}+g^{-}_{\infty 1}e^{-iw(u_{2}+\tau)}\end{pmatrix}+\mathcal{O}\left(\tau^{-1}\right),

(6.8)

where

6.1

T\rightarrow\infty

I(T)
converges pointwisely. Next, we show that

|I(T)|\lesssim G(\omega),

where

is an integrable function. According to

R^{\omega}_{-\infty,u_{2}+\tau}=\begin{pmatrix}R_{+}(u)\\
\hat{R}_{-}(u)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\omega u}g^{+}(u)\\

on both sides. Q.E.D.

Now, we show the decay of the probability.

Theorem 6.2
.

Consider Cauchy problem for the Dirac equation in the Schwarzschild spacetime

D\Psi=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\Psi(0,x)=\Psi_{0},

with the initial data
\Psi_{0}\in L^{2}\big((2m,\infty)\times S^{2},dvol\big)
, where
dvol
is the volume form on the hypersurface
t=\text{const}
. For any
\delta>0
,
\mu>2m+\delta
, the probability of massless Majorana spinors with
\epsilon_{1}\neq 0
,
\epsilon_{2}\neq 0
,
|\epsilon_{1}|+|\epsilon_{2}|>1
to be inside the annulus

\displaystyle K_{\delta,\mu}=\Big\{2m+\delta\leq r\leq\mu\Big\}

tends to zero as time tends to infinity, i.e.,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{K_{\delta,\mu}}|\Psi|^{2}dvol=0.

Proof:
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
{R}_{I}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(2m,\infty)
such that

\Big\|{R}_{I}-{R}_{0}\Big\|<\varepsilon.

According to Theorem
6.1
, we have

{R}_{I}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}t^{\omega}_{ab}\Big(R^{\omega}_{b},{R}_{I}\Big)R^{\omega}_{a}d\omega.

(6.9)

Since the integrand in (
6.9
) is bounded, it is in
L^{1}
as a function of
\omega
. Therefore, the corresponding Fourier transform is in
L^{\infty}
with respect to
t
, and it tends to zero as
t\rightarrow\infty
by Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma
[
20
]
. Thus,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}e^{-itH_{r}}{R}_{I}=0,

where

e^{-itH_{r}}{R}_{I}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}e^{-i\omega t}t^{\omega}_{ab}\Big(R^{\omega}_{b},{R}_{I}\Big)R^{\omega}_{a}d\omega.

On annulus

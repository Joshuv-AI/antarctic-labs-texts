Fusion Products of Twisted Modules in Permutation Orbifolds: II
Chongying Dong, Feng Xu, Nina Yu

Abstract
Let V V be a simple, rational, C 2 C_{2} -cofinite vertex operator algebra of CFT type, and let k k be a positive integer. In this paper, we determine the fusion products of twisted modules for V ⊗ k V^{\otimes k} and G = ⟨ g ⟩ G=\left\langle g\right\rangle generated by any permutation g ∈ S k g\in S_{k} .



1
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of
[
DLXY
]
, investigating the fusion products in permutation orbifold theory. The permutation orbifolds study the tensor
product vertex operator algebra
V^{\otimes k}
with the symmetric group
S_{k}
acting as automorphisms. Here,

V
is a vertex operator algebra and
k
is a positive integer.

The exploration of permutation orbifolds for general vertex operator algebras commenced with
[
BDM
]
, which uncovered a fundamental relationship between twisted modules for
V^{\otimes k}

under permutation automorphisms and

V
-modules. In particular, if
g
is a
k
-cycle, a canonical
g
-twisted
V^{\otimes k}
-module
structure on any
V
-module
(W,Y_{W}(\cdot,z))
was discovered, denoted as
T_{g}\left(W\right)
. This construction established an isomorphism between categories of weak, admissible, and ordinary
V
-modules and their corresponding
g
-twisted
V^{\otimes k}
-modules.

The permutation orbifolds have been studied extensively.
The
C_{2}
-cofiniteness of permutation orbifolds and general cyclic
orbifolds was established in works such as
[
A1
,
A2
,
M1
,
M2
]
. An equivalence of two constructions (see
[
FLM
]
,
[
Le
]
,

[
BDM
]
) of twisted modules for the permutation orbifolds of lattice
vertex operator algebras was established in
[
BHL
]
. Studies on permutation orbifolds within the framework of conformal nets, including the determination of irreducible representations and fusion rules for
k=2
, were presented in
[
KLX
]
. Research on permutation orbifolds of lattice vertex operator algebras for
k=2
and
k=3
is presented in
[
DXY1
,
DXY2
,
DXY3
]
, focusing on the classification of irreducible modules and the determination of fusion rules. The
S_{3}
-orbifold of a rank-three Heisenberg vertex algebra is examined in
[
MPSh
]
. Permutation orbifolds of the Virasoro vertex algebra for
k=2
and
k=3
are studied in
[
MPS1
]
and
[
MPS2
]
. The representation theory of a permutation orbifold of a lattice vertex algebra
V_{Q}
is investigated in
[
BEKT
]
, with an emphasis on irreducible
V_{Q}^{\sigma}
-modules and their characters, where
Q
is a direct sum of a prime number of copies of a positive-definite even lattice, and
\sigma
acts as a cyclic shift.
An explicit expression for the S-matrix of the orbifold
(V^{\otimes k})^{\langle g\rangle}
, where
g=(1,2,\dots,k)
, was derived in
[
DXY4
]
in terms of the S-matrix of
V^{\otimes k}
.
The fusion products of
V^{\otimes k}
-modules
with
g
-twisted
V^{\otimes k}
-modules for any
g\in S_{k}
were
thoroughly determined in
[
DLXY
]
.

In this paper, we extend previous studies by investigating the fusion products of twisted
V^{\otimes k}
-modules when

G
is a cyclic group. We derive explicit formulas for these fusion products in terms of the entries of the
S
-matrix of
V
. The fusion products of twisted modules for any cyclic group generated by a permutation can be reduced to the case where the permutation is a single cycle.
To give a precise description we let
Irr
(V)=\{M^{0}=V,M^{1},\cdots,M^{p}\}
be all irreducible
V
-modules.
Let
g=(1,2,\cdots,k)
and
1\leq s,r<k
. By
[
BDM
]
, any
g^{s}
-twisted module has the form
T_{g^{s}}^{M^{i_{1}},\cdots,M^{i_{d}}}
where
d=\text{gcd}(s,k)
and
M^{i_{1}},\cdots,M^{i_{d}}
are irreducible
V
-modules. Similarly, any
g^{r}
-twisted modules is given by
T_{g^{r}}^{M^{j_{1}},\cdots,M^{j_{m}}}
where
m=\text{gcd}(r,k)
and
M^{j_{1}},\cdots,M^{j_{m}}
are irreducible
V
-modules. To determine the fusion products of
T_{g^{s}}^{M^{i_{1}},\cdots,M^{i_{d}}}
with
T_{g^{r}}^{M^{j_{1}},\cdots,M^{j_{m}}}
, we first find the fusion product of
T_{g^{s}}^{V,\cdots,V}
with
T_{g^{r}}^{V,\cdots,V}
. We then use results on the fusion product of untwisted and twisted modules obtained in
[
DLXY
]
to determine the fusion product
T_{g^{s}}^{M^{i_{1}},\dots,M^{i_{d}}}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{M^{j_{1}},\dots,M^{j_{m}}}
in general.

The structure of this paper is as follows: Section 2 introduces the basic notions and results related to vertex operator algebras. Section 3 reviews fundamental concepts on fusion products and the
S
-matrix in permutation orbifolds. In Section 4, we first show that the fusion products of any
\sigma^{s}
-twisted module with any
\sigma^{r}
-twisted module, where
\sigma\in S_{k}
, can be reduced to the case that
\sigma
is a single cycle. We then determine the fusion product of any
g^{s}
-twisted module with any
g^{r}
-twisted module for
1\leq s,r<k
, where
g=(1,2,\ldots,k)
, providing an explicit formula for the fusion rules.
Section 5 provides a formula for the fusion product
\left(T_{g}(V)\right)^{\boxtimes k}
, where
g=(1,2,\cdots,k)
. In Section 6, we present an example illustrating the fusion products of twisted modules for the permutation orbifold with
V=V_{L}
, a rank-1 lattice vertex operator algebra,
k=4
, and
g=(1,2,3,4)
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Basics on vertex operator algebra

Let
\left(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega\right)
be a vertex operator algebra
[
FLM
,
FHL
,
Bo
]
. We review twisted modules and related results in this section.

Definition 2.1
.

An
automorphism
of a vertex operator algebra
V
is a linear isomorphism
g
of
V
such that
g\left(\omega\right)=\omega
and
gY\left(v,z\right)g^{-1}=Y\left(gv,z\right)
for all
v\in V
. Denote by
\text{Aut}\left(V\right)
the group of all automorphisms of
V
.

It is well-known that for any subgroup
G
of
\text{Aut}\left(V\right)
, the set of
G
-fixed points

V^{G}=\left\{v\in V\mid g(v)=v\ \text{for all}\ g\in G\right\}

is a vertex operator subalgebra.

Let
g
be a finite-order automorphism of
V
with order
T
. Then

V=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{T-1}V^{r},

where
V^{r}=\left\{v\in V\mid gv=e^{2\pi ir/T}v\right\}
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
weak
g
-twisted
V
-module
is a vector space
M
equipped with a linear map

Y_{M}(v,z):V\to\left(\text{End}\,M\right)\left[\left[z^{1/T},z^{-1/T}\right]\right],

such that

v\mapsto Y_{M}(v,z)=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}}v_{n}z^{-n-1},\quad v_{n}\in\text{End}\,M,

which satisfies the following conditions for all
u\in V^{r}
,
v\in V
, and
w\in M
, where
0\leq r\leq T-1
:

\displaystyle Y_{M}(u,z)=\sum_{n\in\frac{r}{T}+\mathbb{Z}}u_{n}z^{-n-1},

\displaystyle u_{n}w=0\quad\text{for $n$ sufficiently large},

\displaystyle Y_{M}(\mathbf{1},z)=\text{Id}_{M},

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y_{M}(u,z_{1})Y_{M}(v,z_{2})-z_{0}^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}}\right)Y_{M}(v,z_{2})Y_{M}(u,z_{1})

\displaystyle=z_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)^{-\frac{r}{T}}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)Y_{M}\left(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2}\right),

where
\delta(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{n}
.

Definition 2.3
.

An
admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module
is a weak
g
-twisted module with a
\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-grading

M=\bigoplus_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M(n)

such that for homogeneous
u\in V
and
m,n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}
, we have

u_{m}M(n)\subset M\left(\text{wt}\,u-m-1+n\right).

Note that if
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M(n)
is an irreducible admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module, then there exists a complex number
\lambda_{M}
such that
L(0)|_{M(n)}=\lambda_{M}+n
for all
n
. As a convention, we assume
M(0)\neq 0
, and
\lambda_{M}
is called the
weight
or
conformal weight
of
M
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
g
-
twisted
V
-module
is a weak
g
-twisted
V
-module
M
that carries a
\mathbb{C}
-grading induced by the spectrum of
L(0)
, where
L(0)
is the component operator of
Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}
. Specifically, we have

M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}M_{\lambda},

where
M_{\lambda}=\left\{w\in M\mid L(0)w=\lambda w\right\}
. Moreover, it is required that
\dim M_{\lambda}<\infty
for all
\lambda
, and for any fixed
\lambda_{0}
,
M_{\frac{n}{T}+\lambda_{0}}=0
for all sufficiently small integers
n
.

When
g=1
, we recover the notions of weak, ordinary, and admissible
V
-modules (see
[
DLM2
]
).

Definition 2.5
.

A vertex operator algebra
V
is said to be
g
-rational
if the admissible
g
-twisted module category is semisimple. In particular,
V
is said to be
rational
if
V
is
1
-rational.

It was proved in
[
DLM2
]
that if
V
is a
g
-rational vertex operator algebra, then there are only finitely many irreducible admissible
g
-twisted
V
-modules up to isomorphism, and any irreducible admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module is ordinary.

Definition 2.6
.

A vertex operator algebra
V
is said to be
regular
if every weak
V
-module
M
is a direct sum of irreducible ordinary
V
-modules.

Definition 2.7
.

A vertex operator algebra
V
is said to be
C_{2}
-cofinite
if
V/C_{2}(V)
is finite-dimensional, where

C_{2}(V)=\text{Span}\left\{u_{-2}v\mid u,v\in V\right\}.

A vertex operator algebra
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
is said to be of
CFT type
if
V_{n}=0
for all negative integers
n
and
V_{0}=\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}
.

In
[
DLM2
,
Li2
]
, it was demonstrated that if
V
is a
C_{2}
-cofinite vertex operator algebra, then
V
has only finitely many irreducible admissible modules up to isomorphism. For vertex operator algebras of CFT type, regularity is equivalent to rationality and
C_{2}
-cofiniteness
[
KL
,
ABD
]
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
M=\bigoplus_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M(n)
be an admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module. The
contragredient module

M^{\prime}

is defined as follows:

M^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}_{+}}M(n)^{*},

where
M(n)^{*}=\text{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(M(n),\mathbb{C})
. The vertex operator
Y_{M^{\prime}}\left(v,z\right)
is defined for
v\in V
via

\langle Y_{M^{\prime}}(v,z)f,u\rangle=\langle f,Y_{M}\left(e^{zL(1)}(-z^{-2})^{L(0)}v,z^{-1}\right)u\rangle,

(2.1)

where
\langle f,w\rangle=f(w)
is the natural pairing
M^{\prime}\times M\to\mathbb{C}
.

If
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}M_{\lambda}
is a
g
-twisted
V
-module, we define
M^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}M_{\lambda}^{*}
and define
Y_{M^{\prime}}(v,z)
for
v\in V
in the same way.

The same argument as in
[
FHL
]
yields the following:

Proposition 2.9
.

If
(M,Y_{M})
is an admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module, then
(M^{\prime},Y_{M^{\prime}})
carries the structure of an admissible
g^{-1}
-twisted
V
-module. Furthermore, if
(M,Y_{M})
is a
g
-twisted
V
-module, then
(M^{\prime},Y_{M^{\prime}})
carries the structure of a
g^{-1}
-twisted
V
-module. Moreover,
M
is irreducible if and only if
M^{\prime}
is irreducible.

In particular, if
(M,Y_{M})
is an ordinary
V
-module, then
(M^{\prime},Y_{M^{\prime}})
is also an ordinary
V
-module. An ordinary
V
-module

is said to be
self-dual
if
M
and
M^{\prime}
are isomorphic. A vertex operator algebra

is said to be
self-dual
if
V
and
V^{\prime}
are isomorphic as ordinary
V
-modules.

We will also need the following result from
[
CM
,
M2
]
:

Theorem 2.10
.

Assume that
V
is a regular and self-dual vertex operator algebra of CFT type. Then for any solvable subgroup
G
of
\text{Aut}(V)
,
V^{G}
is a regular and self-dual vertex operator algebra of CFT type.

Let
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
be mutually commuting automorphisms of
V
with respective periods
T_{1}
,
T_{2}
, and
T_{3}
. In this case,
V
decomposes into the direct sum of common eigenspaces for

and
g_{2}
:

V=\bigoplus_{0\leq j_{1}<T_{1},\ 0\leq j_{2}<T_{2}}V^{(j_{1},j_{2})},

where for

,

\displaystyle V^{(j_{1},j_{2})}=\left\{v\in V\mid g_{s}v=e^{2\pi ij_{s}/T_{s}}v,\ s=1,2\right\}.

(2.2)

For any complex number
\alpha
, we define

(-1)^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\pi i}.

Now, we define intertwining operators among weak
g_{s}
-twisted modules
(M_{s},Y_{M_{s}})
for

.

Definition 2.11
.

An
intertwining operator of type

\binom{M_{3}}{M_{1}\ M_{2}}
associated with the given data is a linear map

\mathcal{Y}(\cdot,z):M_{1}\to\left(\text{Hom}(M_{2},M_{3})\right)\{z\}

such that for any
w^{1}\in M_{1}
,
w^{2}\in M_{2}
, and any fixed
c\in\mathbb{C}
,

w_{c+n}^{1}w^{2}=0\ \ \text{for $n\in\mathbb{Q}$ sufficiently large},

and

\displaystyle z_{0}^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)^{j_{1}/T_{1}}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{0}}\right)Y_{M_{3}}(u,z_{1})\mathcal{Y}(w,z_{2})

\displaystyle\quad-z_{0}^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}}\right)^{j_{1}/T_{1}}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{0}}\right)\mathcal{Y}(w,z_{2})Y_{M_{2}}(u,z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z_{2}^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)^{-j_{2}/T_{2}}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{0}}{z_{2}}\right)\mathcal{Y}\left(Y_{M_{1}}(u,z_{0})w,z_{2}\right),

(2.3)

on
M_{2}
for
u\in V^{(j_{1},j_{2})}
with
j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
w\in M_{1}
, and

\frac{d}{dz}\mathcal{Y}(w,z)=\mathcal{Y}(L(-1)w,z).

All intertwining operators of type
\binom{M_{3}}{M_{1}\ M_{2}}
form a vector space, denoted by
I_{V}\binom{M_{3}}{M_{1}\ M_{2}}
. The
fusion rule
is given by

N_{M_{1}M_{2}}^{M_{3}}=\dim I_{V}\binom{M_{3}}{M_{1}\ M_{2}}.

Remark 2.12
.

If there exist weak
g_{s}
-twisted modules
(M_{s},Y_{M_{s}})
for

such that
N_{M_{1}M_{2}}^{M_{3}}>0
, then
g_{3}=g_{1}g_{2}

[
X2
]
. Given this, we will always assume

.

We now recall the notion of a tensor product (see
[
FHL
,

Definition 2.13
.

Let

-twisted

-twisted

-cofinite vertex operator algebra of CFT type, and that

2.2
Modular
invariance and quantum dimension

In this subsection, we recall some results on modular invariance in orbifold theory and the notion of the
S
-matrix
[
Z
,
DLM3
]
.

Let
\left(V,Y,\mathbf{1},\omega\right)
be a vertex operator algebra with central charge
c
. First, we consider the action of
\text{Aut}(V)
on the set of twisted modules. Let
g,h\in\text{Aut}(V)
, with
g
of finite order. If
\left(M,Y_{M}\right)
is a weak
g
-twisted
V
-module, there exists a weak
h^{-1}gh
-twisted
V
-module
\left(M\circ h,Y_{M\circ h}\right)
, where
M\circ h\cong M
as vector spaces and
Y_{M\circ h}(v,z)=Y_{M}(hv,z)
for
v\in V
. This defines a right action of
\text{Aut}(V)
on the set of weak twisted
V
-modules and their isomorphism classes. We say
M
is
h
-stable
if
M
and
M\circ h
are isomorphic.

Assume that
g
and
h
commute. Then
h
acts on the
g
-twisted modules. Denote by
\mathfrak{U}(g)
the equivalence classes of irreducible
g
-twisted
V
-modules, and let

\mathfrak{U}(g,h)=\left\{M\in\mathfrak{U}(g)\mid M\circ h\cong M\right\}.

Both
\mathfrak{U}(g)
and
\mathfrak{U}(g,h)
are finite sets, as
V
is
g
-rational for all
g
.

Let
M
be an irreducible
g
-twisted
V
-module, and let
G\leq\text{Aut}(V)
with
G_{M}=\left\{h\in G\mid M\circ h\cong M\right\}
. By Schur’s Lemma, there is a projective representation
\phi
of
G_{M}
on
M
such that

\phi(h)Y(u,z)\phi(h)^{-1}=Y(hu,z),

for
h\in G_{M}
. If
h=1
, we take
\phi(1)=1
. Note that
g
lies in
G_{M}
as
g
acts naturally on any admissible
g
-twisted module
M
such that
g|_{M(n)}=e^{2\pi in}
for
n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{M}=\left\{M\circ g\mid g\in G\right\}
be the
G
-orbit of
M
. The cardinality of the
G
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{M}
is
\left[G:G_{M}\right]
.

Set
o(v)=v_{\text{wt}v-1}
for homogeneous
v\in V
. Then
o(v)
is a degree zero operator of
v
. Let
\mathbb{H}
be the complex upper half-plane. Here and below, set
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, where
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
. For
v\in V
, define

Z_{M}(v,(g,h),\tau)=\text{tr}_{M}o(v)\phi(h)q^{L(0)-c/24}=q^{\lambda-c/24}\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\text{tr}_{M_{\lambda+n}}o(v)\phi(h)q^{n}.

Then
Z_{M}(v,(g,h),\tau)
is a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}
(see
[
Z
,
DLM3
]
). For simplicity, we write
Z_{M}(v,\tau)=Z_{M}(v,(g,1),\tau)
. The function
Z_{M}(\tau)=Z_{M}(\mathbf{1},\tau)
is called the
character
of
M
.

Recall that there is another vertex operator algebra
\left(V,Y[\ \cdot\ ],\mathbf{1},\tilde{\omega}\right)
associated with
V
(see
[
Z
]
). Here,
\tilde{\omega}=\omega-c/24
, and for homogeneous
v\in V
,

Y[v,z]=Y(v,e^{z}-1)e^{z\cdot\text{wt}v}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v[n]z^{n-1}.

We write
Y[\tilde{\omega},z]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{-n-2}
. The weight of a homogeneous
v\in V
in the second vertex operator algebra is denoted by
\text{wt}[v]
.

The modular group
\Gamma
consists of
2\times 2
integral matrices with determinant 1, denoted as
\Gamma=SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\Gamma(N)
represent the kernel of the reduction modulo
N

\pi_{N}:SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
. A subgroup
G_{N}
of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is called a
congruence subgroup of level
N
if
N
is the least positive integer such that
\Gamma(N)\leq G_{N}
. Let
P(G)
be the set of ordered commuting pairs in
G
. For
(g,h)\in P(G)
and
M\in\mathfrak{U}(g,h)
,
Z_{M}(v,(g,h),\tau)
is a function on
V\times\mathbb{H}
. By
[
DLM3
]
, the dimension of the vector space
W
spanned by such functions is equal to

\sum_{(g,h)\in P(G)}\left|\mathfrak{U}(g,h)\right|.

Now we define an action of the modular group
\Gamma
on
W
such that

Z_{M}|_{\gamma}(v,(g,h),\tau)=(c\tau+d)^{-\text{wt}[v]}Z_{M}(v,(g,h),\gamma\tau),

where
\gamma:\tau\mapsto\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}
, and
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(-1\right)^{n+1}z^{n+2}\left(z^{-1}\left(\left(1+z^{1/k}x\right)^{k}-1\right)\right)^{n+1}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(-1\right)^{n+1}z\left(\left(1+z^{1/k}x\right)^{k}-1\right)^{n+1}

Let

then the left-hand side of (

) is equal
to

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\left(-y\right)^{n}}{n!}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({}_{i}^{k}\right)y^{i}\left(-1\right)^{i}i\cdots\left(i+n-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\left(-y\right)^{n+i}i\cdots\left(i+n-1\right)}{n!}\left({}_{i}^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({}_{n}^{n+i-1}\right)\left({}_{i}^{k}\right)\left(-y\right)^{n+i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left({}_{m-i}^{m-1}\right)\left({}_{i}^{k}\right)\left(-y\right)^{m},

where in the last identity we let

The right-hand side of (

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(-1\right)^{n+1}\left(\left(1+y\right)^{k}-1\right)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)\left(\left(1+y\right)^{k}-1\right)}{1+\left(\left(1+y\right)^{k}-1\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+\frac{1}{\left(1+y\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+\sum_{i\geq 0}\left({}_{i}^{-k}\right)y^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\left(-k\right)\left(-k-1\right)\cdots\left(-k-i+1\right)}{i!}y^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1+\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{k\left(k+1\right)\cdots\left(k+m-1\right)}{m!}\left(-1\right)^{m}y^{m}

(3.13)

By Lemma

) is

T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}=\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}T_{g^{s+r}}^{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}.

and
n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}
satisfies

\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}}=\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}S_{t_{1},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}.

(4.5)

It suffices to prove

n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}\cdots S_{t_{q}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}}

(4.6)

for
t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}\in\left\{0,1,\cdots,p\right\}.

Now we divide the proof into three cases based on the value of
q
.

•

Case
q=1
: Substituting
q=1
into (
4.5
), we obtain
\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}}=\sum_{t_{1}}n_{t_{1}}S_{t_{1},j}
.
It follows that
n_{t_{1}}=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}}{S_{j,0}^{k-d-m}},

which matches (
4.6
) for
q=1
.

•

Case
q=2:
We denote the multiplicity of the module
T_{g^{s+r}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}
in the fusion product
T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}
by

n_{t_{1},t_{2}}=\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{t_{1},t_{2}}\right\rangle.

Using (
4.5
), the coefficients
n_{t_{1},t_{2}}
satisfy the equation

\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}}=\sum_{t_{1},t_{2}}n_{t_{1},t_{2}}S_{t_{1},j}S_{t_{2},j}.

(4.7)

In the mean time,

\displaystyle n_{t_{1},t_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},\left(M^{t_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(V\otimes M^{t_{2}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-2)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(M^{t_{1}^{\prime}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(V\otimes M^{t_{2}^{\prime}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-2)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\left(M^{t_{1}^{\prime}}\boxtimes M^{t_{2}^{\prime}}\right)\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{a}N_{t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}M^{a^{\prime},0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}N_{t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},M^{a,0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}N_{t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}N_{t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime}}^{a^{\prime}}n_{a,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},a}^{t_{1}}n_{a,0},

(4.8)

where in the second identity, we apply category theory, and in the third identity, we utilize Lemma
4.5
.
Now the right-hand side of (
4.7
) is

\sum_{t_{1},t_{2}}n_{t_{1},t_{2}}S_{t_{1},j}S_{t_{2},j}=\sum_{a,t_{1},t_{2}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},a}^{t_{1}}S_{t_{1},j}S_{t_{2},j}n_{a,0}=\frac{S_{a,j}}{S_{0,j}}n_{a,0}.

(4.9)

By (
4.7
) and (
4.9
), we have
\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-1}}=S_{a,j}n_{a,0}.

Thus,
\sum_{j}\frac{S_{a^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-1}}=\sum_{j}S_{a^{\prime},j}S_{a,j}n_{a,0}
. It follows that

n_{a,0}=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{a^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-1}}.

Combining with (
4.8
), we obtain

n_{t_{1},t_{2}}=\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{a}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},a}^{t_{1}}S_{a^{\prime},j}\right)\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-1}}=\sum_{j}\left(\frac{S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}S_{t_{2}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}}\right)\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-1}}=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}S_{t_{2}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}},

where the second identity follows from Lemma
4.3
(3).

•

Case
q\geq 3
: Now we present the proof in three steps.

Step 1.
Prove that

(4.10)

Indeed, if we denote the multiplicity of the module
T_{g^{s+r}}^{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}

in the fusion product
T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}

by

Then

\displaystyle n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},\left(M^{t_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(V^{\otimes(q-1)}\otimes M^{t_{q}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-q)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(M^{t_{1}^{\prime}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(V^{\otimes(q-1)}\otimes M^{t_{q}^{\prime}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-q)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\left(M^{t_{1}^{\prime}}\boxtimes_{V}\cdots\boxtimes_{V}M^{t_{q}^{\prime}}\right)\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-1}}N_{t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime}}^{s_{1}^{\prime}}N_{s_{1}^{\prime},t_{3}^{\prime}}^{s_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{s_{q-2}^{\prime},t_{q}^{\prime}}^{s_{q-1}^{\prime}}M^{s_{q-1}^{\prime},0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-1}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1}}^{s_{q-2}}\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},\left(M^{s_{q-1}}\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\otimes T_{g^{s+r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-1}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1}}^{s_{q-2}}\left\langle T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0},T_{g^{s+1}}^{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-1}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1}}^{s_{q-2}}n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}

where in the first, second, third, the last identities we use (
4.3
), the properties of fusion category, Lemma
4.5
, and symmetry property of fusion rules in (
2.5
), respectively.

Step 2.
Find
n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}.

Now by using (
4.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}S_{t_{1},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-1}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1},}^{s_{q-2}}S_{t_{1},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{q-1}}\left(\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q},s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-2}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1},}^{s_{q-2}}S_{t_{1},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}\right)n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}

(4.11)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q},s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-2}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1},}^{s_{q-2}}S_{t_{1},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{3},\cdots,t_{q},s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-2}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1},}^{s_{q-2}}\left(\sum_{t_{1},t_{2}}N_{t_{2}^{\prime},s_{1}}^{t_{1}}S_{t_{1},j}S_{t_{2},j}\right)S_{t_{3},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{3},\cdots,t_{q},s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-2}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1}}^{s_{q-2}}\left(\frac{S_{s_{1},j}}{S_{0,j}}\right)S_{t_{3},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{4},\cdots,t_{q},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-2}}N_{t_{4}^{\prime},s_{3}}^{s_{2}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1}}^{s_{q-2}}\left(\sum_{t_{3,}s_{1}}N_{t_{3}^{\prime},s_{2}}^{s_{1}}S_{s_{1},j}S_{t_{3},j}\right)\frac{1}{S_{0,j}}S_{t_{4},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{4},\cdots,t_{q},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-2}}N_{t_{4}^{\prime},s_{3}}^{s_{2}}\cdots N_{t_{q}^{\prime},s_{q-1}}^{s_{q-2}}\left(\frac{S_{s_{2},j}}{S_{0,j}}\right)\frac{1}{S_{0,j}}S_{t_{4},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S_{s_{q-1},j}}{S_{0,j}^{q-1}},

we obtain that

\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}S_{t_{1},j}\cdots S_{t_{q},j}=\sum_{s_{q-1}}\left(\frac{S_{s_{q-1},j}}{S_{0,j}^{q-1}}\right)n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}.

(4.12)

Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.12
), we obtain

\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}}=\sum_{s_{q-1}}\frac{S_{s_{q-1},j}}{S_{0,j}^{q-1}}n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0},j=0,1,\cdots,p.

Consequently, we have

\sum_{j}\frac{S_{s_{q-1}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-q+1}}=\sum_{j,s_{q-1}}S_{s_{q-1}^{\prime},j}S_{s_{q-1},j}n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}.

Thus,

n_{s_{q-1},0,\cdots,0}=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{s_{q-1}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-q+1}}.

(4.13)

Step 3.
Find
n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}.

Using (
4.10
) and (
4.13
),
we have

\displaystyle n_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{q-1}}N_{t_{1}^{\prime},t_{2}^{\prime}}^{s_{1}^{\prime}}N_{s_{1}^{\prime},t_{3}^{\prime}}^{s_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{s_{q-2}^{\prime},t_{q}^{\prime}}^{s_{q-1}^{\prime}}S_{s_{q-1}^{\prime},j}\right)\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-q+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\frac{S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}\cdots S_{t_{q}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{q-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m-q+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\frac{S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}\cdots S_{t_{p}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k-d-m}},

where in the second identity we use Lemma
4.3

(5). The proof is completed.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
g=\left(1,2,\cdots,k\right)
be a
k
-cycle,
and let
1\leq s,r<k
with
\gcd(r,s)=1
. Suppose

g^{s}=\tau_{1}\cdots\tau_{d}
,
g^{r}=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m}
,
and
g^{s+r}=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{q}
are products of disjoint cycles,
where
d=\gcd(s,k)
,
m=\gcd(r,k)
, and
q=\gcd(s+r,k)
. Then for any
i_{1},\cdots,i_{d},

j_{1},\cdots,j_{m},

t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}\in\left\{0,1,\cdots,p\right\}
,

\displaystyle T_{g^{s}}^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q},j}\frac{S_{i_{1},j}\cdots S_{i_{d},j}S_{j_{1},j}\cdots S_{j_{m},j}S_{t_{1}^{\prime},j}\cdots S_{t_{q}^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k}}T_{g^{s+r}}^{t_{1},\cdots,t_{q}}.

Proof.

Similar to (
4.3
), we have

T_{g^{s}}^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}}=\left(M^{i_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(V^{\otimes(d-1)}\otimes M^{i_{d}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-d)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}

and

T_{g^{r}}^{j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}}=\left(M^{j_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\left(V^{\otimes(m-1)}\otimes M^{j_{m}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-m)}\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}.

Then for
i_{1},\cdots,i_{d},j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}\in\left\{0,1,\cdots,p\right\}
,
we have

\displaystyle T_{g^{s}}^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{d}}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{j_{1},\cdots,j_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(M^{i_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\cdots\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\left(V^{\otimes(d-1)}\otimes M^{i_{d}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-d)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right)

\displaystyle\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\left(\left(M^{j_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\cdots\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\left(V^{\otimes(m-1)}\otimes M^{j_{m}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-m)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(M^{i_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\cdots\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\left(V^{\otimes(d-1)}\otimes M^{i_{d}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-d)}\right)

\displaystyle\boxtimes\left(\left(M^{j_{1}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-1)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\cdots\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\left(V^{\otimes(m-1)}\otimes M^{j_{m}}\otimes V^{\otimes(k-m)}\right)\right)\boxtimes T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(M^{i_{1}}\boxtimes_{V}\cdots\boxtimes_{V}M^{i_{d}}\boxtimes_{V}M^{j_{1}}\boxtimes_{V}\cdots\boxtimes_{V}M^{j_{m}}\right)\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-d-m\right)}\right)\boxtimes\left(T_{g^{s}}^{0,\cdots,0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{r}}^{0,\cdots,0}\right),

where in the last identity we use Lemma

.

5
The Fusion Product
\left(T_{g}\left(V\right)\right)^{\boxtimes k}

Let
g=\left(1,2,\cdots,k\right)
be a
k
-cycle. In this section,
we establish a formula of
\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes k}
.
For convenience, we will denote
M^{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{i_{k}}

by
M^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
g=\left(1,2,\cdots,k\right)

be a
k
-cycle. Then

\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes k}=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}\left(\sum_{a}S_{0,a}^{2-2h}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\right)M^{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}},

where
h=\frac{\left(k-1\right)\left(k-2\right)}{2}
.

Proof.

First note that

\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes\left(k-1\right)}=\sum_{i}n_{i}T_{g^{k-1}}^{i}.

(5.1)

Let
\lambda_{1}=\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{0}
,
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{i,s}=\left(T_{g^{k-1}}^{i}\right)^{s}
for

s=0,...,k-1

and
\mu=\left(M^{j,\cdots,j}\right)^{0}.
Similar to arguments in Lemma
4.2
, one has
a_{\lambda_{1}}=T_{g}^{0}
,

a_{\lambda_{2}}=T_{g^{k-1}}^{i}
and
a_{\mu}=M^{j,\cdots,j}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{S_{\lambda_{1},\mu}}{S_{0,\mu}}=\frac{S_{\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{0},\left(M^{j,\cdots,j}\right)^{0}}}{S_{\left(V^{\otimes k}\right)^{G},\left(M^{j,\cdots,j}\right)^{0}}}=\frac{\frac{1}{k}e^{\frac{2\pi in}{k}}S_{T_{g}^{0},M^{j,\cdots,j}}}{\frac{1}{k}S_{V,M^{j}}^{k}}=\frac{\frac{1}{k}e^{\frac{2\pi in}{k}}S_{0,j}}{\frac{1}{k}S_{0,j}^{k}}=e^{\frac{2\pi in}{k}}S_{0,j}^{1-k}

and

\frac{S_{\lambda_{2},\mu}}{S_{0,\mu}}=\frac{S_{\left(T_{g^{-1}}^{i}\right)^{s},\left(M^{j,\cdots,j}\right)^{0}}}{S_{\left(V^{\otimes k}\right)^{G},\left(M^{j,\cdots,j}\right)^{0}}}=\frac{\frac{1}{k}e^{\frac{2\pi in\left(k-1\right)}{k}}S_{T_{g^{-1}}^{i},M^{j,\cdots,j}}}{\frac{1}{k}S_{0,j}^{k}}=\frac{\frac{1}{k}e^{\frac{2\pi in\left(k-1\right)}{k}}S_{i,j}}{\frac{1}{k}S_{0,j}^{k}}=e^{\frac{2\pi in\left(k-1\right)}{k}}\cdot\frac{S_{i,j}}{S_{0,j}^{k}}.

Let
a_{\lambda_{1}}^{k-1}
denote the fusion product of
k-1
copies of
a_{\lambda_{1}}
, and let
\lambda_{1}^{k-1}
denote the fusion product of
k-1
copies of
\lambda_{1}
. Assume that
\lambda_{1}^{k-1}=\sum_{i,s}n_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{i,s}}\lambda_{i,s}.
Then

a_{\lambda_{1}}^{k-1}=a_{\lambda_{1}^{k-1}}=\sum_{i,s}n_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{i,s}}a_{\lambda_{i,s}},

That is,

\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes\left(k-1\right)}=\sum_{i,s}n_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{i,s}}T_{g^{k-1}}^{i}.

From equation (
5.1
) we see that
n_{i}=\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}n_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{i,s}}.

By
[
DRX3
, Theorem 7.1]
we have

\left(\frac{S_{\lambda_{1},\mu}}{S_{0,\mu}}\right)^{k-1}=\sum_{i,s}n_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{i,s}}\frac{S_{\lambda_{i,s},\mu}}{S_{0,\mu}},

or equivalently,

\left(e^{\frac{2\pi in}{k}}S_{0,j}^{1-k}\right)^{k-1}=\sum_{i,s}n_{\lambda_{1}}^{\lambda_{i,s}}e^{\frac{2\pi in\left(k-1\right)}{k}}\cdot\frac{S_{i,j}}{S_{0,j}^{k}}=\sum_{i}n_{i}e^{\frac{2\pi in\left(k-1\right)}{k}}\frac{S_{i,j}}{S_{0,j}^{k}}.

That is,
\frac{1}{S_{0,j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}}=\sum_{i}n_{i}S_{i,j}

for
j=0,1,\cdots,p
and
n_{i}=\sum_{j}\frac{S_{i^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}}.

So we have

\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes\left(k-1\right)}=\sum_{i,j}\frac{S_{i^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}}\left(M^{i}\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{k-1}}^{0}.

(5.2)

Since the fusion product of a
g
-twisted module and a
g^{k-1}
-twisted
module is an untwisted module. One can write

T_{g^{k-1}}^{0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g}^{0}=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}m_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}M^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}.

Now we have

\displaystyle\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes\left(k-1\right)}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\frac{S_{i^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}}\left(M^{i}\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g^{-1}}^{0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}T_{g}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\frac{S_{i^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}}\left(M^{i}\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}\left(\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}m_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}M^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\frac{S_{i^{\prime},j}}{S_{0,j}^{k^{2}-3k+1}}m_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}\left(M^{i}\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}M^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}.

(5.3)

Note that by properties of fusion rules and dual module of twisted
modules in Propositions
2.27
and
3.5
,
we have

m_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}=N_{T_{g^{k-1}}^{0},T_{g}^{0}}^{M^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}}=N_{T_{g}^{0}\circ g^{k-1},T_{g^{k-1}}^{0}}^{M^{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}}=N_{T_{g}^{0},M^{i_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,i_{k}^{\prime}}}^{\left(T_{g^{k-1}}^{0}\right)^{{}^{\prime}}}=N_{T_{g}^{0},M^{i_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,i_{k}^{\prime}}}^{T_{g}^{0}}.

By Theorem
3.10
,

\displaystyle T_{g}^{0}\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}M^{i_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,i_{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=T_{g}\left(M^{i_{1}^{\prime}}\boxtimes_{V}\cdots\boxtimes_{V}M^{i_{k}^{\prime}}\right)=\sum_{j_{1}^{\prime},\cdots j_{k-1}^{\prime}}N_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}}^{j_{1}^{\prime}}N_{j_{1}^{\prime},i_{3}^{\prime}}^{j_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{j_{k-3}^{\prime},i_{k-1}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-2}^{\prime}}N_{j_{k-2}^{\prime},i_{k}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-1}^{\prime}}T_{g}^{j_{k-1}^{\prime}}.

Thus

m_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}=N_{T_{g}^{0},M^{i_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,i_{k}^{\prime}}}^{T_{g}^{0}}=\sum_{j_{1}^{\prime},\cdots j_{k-2}^{\prime}}N_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}}^{j_{1}^{\prime}}N_{j_{1}^{\prime},i_{3}^{\prime}}^{j_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{j_{k-3}^{\prime},i_{k-1}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-2}^{\prime}}N_{j_{k-2}^{\prime},i_{k}^{\prime}}^{0}.

Since
N_{j_{k-2}^{\prime},i_{k}^{\prime}}^{0}=N_{j_{k-2}^{\prime},0}^{i_{k}},
we obtain

i_{k}=j_{k-2}^{\prime}.
Now using Lemma
4.3
gives

\displaystyle m_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,j_{k-3}^{\prime}}N_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}}^{j_{1}^{\prime}}N_{j_{1}^{\prime},i_{3}^{\prime}}^{j_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{j_{k-5}^{\prime},i_{k-3}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-4}^{\prime}}N_{j_{k-4}^{\prime},i_{k-2}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-3}^{\prime}}N_{j_{k-3}^{\prime},i_{k-1}^{\prime}}^{i_{k}}N_{i_{k},i_{k}^{\prime}}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,j_{k-3}^{\prime}}N_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}}^{j_{1}^{\prime}}N_{j_{1}^{\prime},i_{3}^{\prime}}^{j_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{j_{k-5}^{\prime},i_{k-3}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-4}^{\prime}}N_{j_{k-4}^{\prime},i_{k-2}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-3}^{\prime}}\sum_{t}\frac{S_{j_{k-3}^{\prime},t}S_{i_{k-1}^{\prime},t}S_{i_{k}^{\prime},t}}{S_{0,t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t}\left(\sum_{j_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,j_{k-3}^{\prime}}N_{i_{1}^{\prime},i_{2}^{\prime}}^{j_{1}^{\prime}}N_{j_{1}^{\prime},i_{3}^{\prime}}^{j_{2}^{\prime}}\cdots N_{j_{k-5}^{\prime},i_{k-3}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-4}^{\prime}}N_{j_{k-4}^{\prime},i_{k-2}^{\prime}}^{j_{k-3}^{\prime}}S_{j_{k-3}^{\prime},t}\right)\cdot\frac{S_{i_{k-1}^{\prime},t}S_{i_{k}^{\prime},t}}{S_{0,t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t}\frac{S_{i_{1}^{\prime},t}\cdots S_{i_{k}^{\prime},t}}{S_{0,t}^{k-2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{a}S_{0,a}^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}.

(5.4)

Plugging equation (
5.4
) into (
5.3
), we
obtain

\displaystyle\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,t,i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k-1}S_{i^{\prime},t}\cdot\sum_{a}S_{0,a}^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\left(M^{i}\otimes V^{\otimes\left(k-1\right)}\right)\boxtimes_{V^{\otimes k}}M^{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,t,i_{1},\cdots,i_{k},a}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k-1}S_{i^{\prime},t}\cdot S_{0,a}^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\sum_{j}N_{i,i_{1}}^{j}M^{j,i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{k},a,j}\left(\sum_{i,t,i_{1}}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k-1}S_{i^{\prime},t}\cdot S_{0,a}^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot N_{i,i_{1}}^{j}\right)M^{j,i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}.

(5.5)

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i,t,i_{1}}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k-1}S_{i^{\prime},t}\cdot S_{0,a}^{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot N_{i,i_{1}}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,t}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k-1}S_{i^{\prime},t}\cdot S_{0,a}\prod_{\ell=2}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\left(\sum_{i_{1}}S_{i_{1}^{\prime},a}N_{i,j^{\prime}}^{i_{1}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,t}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k-1}S_{i^{\prime},t}\cdot S_{0,a}\prod_{\ell=2}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\left(\frac{S_{i,a}S_{j^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,t}S_{0,t}^{-k^{2}+3k}\left(S_{i^{\prime},t}S_{i,a}\right)\cdot\prod_{\ell=2}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\frac{S_{j^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{0,a}^{-k^{2}+3k}\prod_{\ell=2}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\frac{S_{j^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}},

(5.6)

where in the second identity we use Lemma
4.3

and in the fourth identity we use Verlinde formula in Theorem
2.15
.
Combining (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(T_{g}^{0}\right)^{\boxtimes k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{2},\cdots,i_{k},a,j}\left(S_{0,a}^{-k^{2}+3k}\prod_{\ell=2}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\cdot\frac{S_{j^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\right)M^{j,i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k},a}\left(S_{0,a}^{-k^{2}+3k}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\right)M^{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}}\left(\sum_{a}S_{0,a}^{2-2h}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{S_{i_{\ell}^{\prime},a}}{S_{0,a}}\right)M^{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k}},

where
h=\frac{\left(k-1\right)\left(k-2\right)}{2}
.

The Schrödinger Bridge Problem for Jump Diffusions with Regime Switching Thanks: We acknowledge the support of the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC), awards 559387-2021 and 241023.
Andrei Zlotchevski, Linan Chen

Abstract
. The Schrödinger bridge problem (SBP) aims at finding the measure 𝐏 ^ \widehat{\mathbf{P}} on a certain path space which possesses the desired state-space distributions ρ 0 \rho_{0} at time 0 0 and ρ T \rho_{T} at time T T while minimizing the KL divergence from a reference path measure 𝐑 \mathbf{R} . This work focuses on the SBP in the case when 𝐑 \mathbf{R} is the path measure of a jump diffusion with regime switching, which is a Markov process that combines the dynamics of a jump diffusion with interspersed discrete events representing changing environmental states. To the best of our knowledge, the SBP in such a setting has not been previously studied. In this paper, we conduct a comprehensive analysis of the dynamics of the SBP solution 𝐏 ^ \widehat{\mathbf{P}} in the regime-switching jump-diffusion setting. In particular, we show that 𝐏 ^ \widehat{\mathbf{P}} is again a path measure of a regime-switching jump diffusion; under proper assumptions, we establish various properties of 𝐏 ^ \widehat{\mathbf{P}} from both a stochastic calculus perspective and an analytic viewpoint. In addition, as a demonstration of the general theory developed in this work, we examine a concrete unbalanced SBP (uSBP) from the angle of a regime-switching SBP, where we also obtain novel results in the realm of uSBP.

Introduction

Our topic of interest is the Schrödinger bridge problem (SBP) for regime-switching jump diffusions. As an extension of the framework of the jump-diffusion SBP
[
45
]
, the formulation adopted in this work incorporates additional layers of complexity in order to model real-world phenomena present under changing environmental states. To begin, jump diffusions themselves generalize classical diffusions by integrating jumps driven by Lévy measures, thereby capturing sudden events such as market crashes or neuron firings. Regime switching further enhances this by having the drift, diffusion, and jump coefficients vary according to a Markov chain on a finite set of states called
regimes
, which represents sudden environmental changes. We are interested in studying the SBP in such a setting. Even though the general study of the SBP has a long and rich history, the regime-switching SBP has not seen much development in the literature. This work is dedicated to introducing a general theory on the SBP for regime-switching jump diffusions.

1.1.
Set-up of the problem

Our model is set on a “hybrid” state space
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
over a finite time interval
t\in[0,T]
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is a finite set consisting of all the regimes. Without loss of generality, we will assume that
{\mathcal{S}}=\left\{1,2,\dots,\left|{\mathcal{S}}\right|\right\}
. Let
\Omega=D([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}})
be the Skorokhod space, i.e., the space of càdlàg functions
\omega:t\in[0,T]\mapsto(\omega^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(t),\omega^{{\mathcal{S}}}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
, equipped with the Skorokhod metric. For every
t\in[0,T]
, define
(X_{t},\Lambda_{t}):\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
to be the time projection such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
X_{t}(\omega)=\omega^{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(t)
and
\Lambda_{t}(\omega)=\omega^{{\mathcal{S}}}(t)
. If
\mathcal{F}:=\mathcal{B}_{\Omega}
is the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\Omega
, then
\mathcal{F}
is generated by the time projections
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
. Let
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\right\}_{[0,T]}
be the natural filtration (or its usual augmentation) associated with
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
the set of probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
. For
\mathbf{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
, to which we refer as a
path measure
, we say that
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}(\omega)
is the
canonical process
under
\mathbf{P}
if
\omega\in\Omega
is sampled under
\mathbf{P}
. Finally, for
0\leq t\leq T
, we write the distribution of
(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})
under
\mathbf{P}
as
\mathbf{P}_{t}
, and call it the
marginal distribution
at time
t
.

Now we present the rigorous definition of the
Schrödinger Bridge Problem
(SBP).

Definition 1.1.1
.

Given a path measure
\mathbf{R}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
and two probability distributions
\rho_{0},\rho_{T}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
, the
Schrödinger bridge problem
is to find the path measure
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
such that

(1.1.1)

\widehat{\mathbf{P}}=\arg\min\left\{\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf{P}||\mathbf{R})\text{ such that }\mathbf{P}_{0}=\rho_{0},\mathbf{P}_{T}=\rho_{T}\right\}.

Here,
\mathrm{KL}(\cdot||\cdot)
denotes the
KL divergence
(relative entropy), defined as

\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf{P}||\mathbf{R}):={\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}}}\left[\log\left(\frac{d\mathbf{P}}{d\mathbf{R}}\right)\right]\;\textup{ if }\mathbf{P}\ll\mathbf{R},\;+\infty\textup{ otherwise.}

In the above SBP, we refer to
\mathbf{R}
as the
reference measure
,
\rho_{0},\rho_{T}
as the
target distributions
, and the solution
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
, if it exists, as the
Schrödinger bridge
. Heuristically speaking, the SBP is the quest to find the path “bridging”
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{T}
that is the “closest” to a given reference.

In this paper, we consider the SBP with the reference measure
\mathbf{R}
arising from a
regime-switching jump diffusion
, which is a two-component strong Markov

We start with the

Then, the desired regime-switching process

(1.1.3)

\mathbb{R}_{+}

Finally, we also incorporate

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\beta(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},w)N_{1}(dt,dw)

\beta(t,x,i,w):=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}(\psi_{ij}(t,x)-x)\mathbbm{1}_{\Delta_{ij}(t,x)}(w)\textup{ for }(t,x,i,w)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Putting everything together, we obtain the SDE formulation of the regime-switching jump diffusion
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}

(1.1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=b(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})dB_{t}+\int_{|z|\leq 1}\gamma(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},z)\tilde{N}(dt,dz)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{|z|>1}\gamma(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},z)N(dt,dz)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\beta(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},w)N_{1}(dt,dw),\\

Assumption (C)

(1)

b=(b_{m})_{1\leq m\leq d}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

(2)

\sigma=(\sigma_{mn})_{1\leq m,n\leq d}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

(\sigma\sigma^{\top})(t,x,i):=a(t,x,i)

(t,x,i)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}

(3)

\gamma=(\gamma_{m})_{1\leq m\leq d}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

(t,x,i)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}

\gamma(t,x,i,0)\equiv 0

(4)

The intensity
\nu(dz)

\nu(\left\{0\right\})=0

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}(1\wedge|z|^{2})\nu(dz)<\infty

(5)

For every

\psi_{ij}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

\psi_{ii}(t,x)=x

(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

(6)

For every
i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}

1.3.
Main Results

Below we give an overview of the main results obtained in this work. First, we examine various properties of the Schrödinger bridge

unbalanced SBP

The unbalanced SBP

In
Section

4
, we apply the general theory from the previous sections to study an
unbalanced Schrödinger bridge problem
(uSBP) originated from a jump diffusion with killing. It is natural to fit such a process into our regime-switching model with two regimes only - “active” and “dead”, where the regime-switching mechanism is reduced to a simple form governed by the killing rate. We set up the uSBP in a novel way that, at the terminal time, not only the position of “active” particles but also the killing position of “dead” particles are required to match the target distribution. By adapting the existing regularity theory of jump diffusions to the case with killing, we can configure the uSBP model so that all the assumptions invoked in the general theory become accessible, in the sense that they are either directly fulfilled or verifiable by the target distribution. Moreover, most of the general results from the previous sections, including the stochastic control formulation, the transition (and marginal) density function of
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
, the dynamic Schrödinger system of
(\varphi,\widehat{\varphi})
, the Schrödinger bridge forward PIDE, etc., will take explicit forms in the uSBP setting. Some possible variations of this uSBP will also be discussed.

1.4.
Motivation, Background and Assumptions

Regime-switching (jump) diffusions form a rich class of stochastic processes that capture a broad range of applications in operations management
[
37
]
, biology
[
29
,
39
]
, financial modeling
[
34
,
43
]
, actuarial science
[
17
]
, etc. In contrast to single-regime models, regime-switching jump diffusions provide a framework for modeling discrete events that affect the “environment” in which the stochastic process is evolving. As a result, multi-regime models enable a more realistic representation of the dynamical system. In the last thirty years, regime-switching diffusions and jump diffusions have seen increasing interest, especially for optimization and control problems. In fact, the SBP belongs to both of these categories: it is an optimization problem in its primal formulation and a control problem in its stochastic control formulation. The SBP is thus highly relevant for both modeling and control purposes in regime-switching applications.

The SBP has a rich history going back to E. Schrödinger’s original thought experiment in the 1930s
[
35
,
36
]
and has generated a large body of research (e.g.
[
2
,
15
,
19
,
32
,
10
,
4
]
), especially in the diffusion case. As regime-switching models become more and more widely adopted, we believe it is necessary to expand existing knowledge on the SBP in this direction. On the other hand, introducing regime switching to a (jump) diffusion adds yet another layer of complexity that imposes new technical challenges. The purpose of this work is twofold: first, we aim to establish fundamental theoretical results for the SBP for regime-switching (jump) diffusions under assumptions that are analogous to the single-regime SBP. Second, we show how an unbalanced SBP can be modeled via a regime-switching approach, and we provide a detailed analysis that establishes new results beyond the existing literature on this topic.

Despite the general literature being scarce compared to that of diffusions, the field of jump diffusions, with and without regime switching, is fast developing and the research body is expanding in both the depth and the diversity. With this in mind, instead of restricting ourselves to a rigid set-up, we put the basic requirements only (Assumption
) on the model under consideration so that our work can potentially reach a broad class of regime-switching jump diffusions. \widehat{P}_{ij}(t,x,s,dy):=\frac{\varphi(s,y,j)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}P_{ij}(t,x,s,dy)\;\textit{if }\varphi(t,x,i)>0,\;\textit{trivial otherwise},

\rho_{0}

\displaystyle\left(\bigcap_{k=0}^{K}\left\{X_{t_{k}}\in B_{k},\,\Lambda_{t_{k}}=i_{k}\right\}\right)

Remark.
Assumption

We have discussed in the

has an everywhere positive transition distribution.

\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
be the canonical process under
\mathbf{P}^{0}
. For every

,

(2.2.1)

\displaystyle Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s}:=

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(-\int_{t}^{s}u(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r})\,dB_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{s}|u(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r})|^{2}\,dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\log(1-\theta(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-},z))\tilde{N}(dr,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left[\log(1-\theta(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r},z))+\theta(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r},z)\right]\nu(dz)dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\log\xi(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-},w)\tilde{N}_{1}(dr,dw)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left[\log\xi(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r},w)+1-\xi(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r},w)\right]\mathfrak{m}(dw)dr\bigg),

It is straightforward to check that, given the choice of
(u,\theta,\xi)
, all the stochastic integrals in (

) are well defined. By the Girsanov theorem for jump diffusions (e.g.
[
47
]
, Theorem 1.33), if
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}^{0}}[Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s}]=1
, then for every
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
,
\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}:=Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s}\mathbf{P}^{x,i}_{t,s}
is a path measure restricted to
\mathcal{F}_{s}
, where we recall that
\mathbf{P}^{x,i}_{t,s}
is defined in
2.1.2
; further, under
\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}
,
dB^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t}:=u\,dt+dB_{t}
is a Brownian motion,

\tilde{N}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}(dt,dz):=N(dt,dz)-(1-\theta)\nu(dz)dt

is the compensated Poisson random measure of
N(dt,dz)
, and

\tilde{N}_{1}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}(dt,dw):=N_{1}(dt,dw)-\xi\mathfrak{m}(dw)dt

is the compensated Poisson random measure of
N_{1}(dt,dw)
.

Moreover, we observe that if
\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}^{0}}[Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s}]=1
for all
0<t<s<T
, then

\left\{\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}:0<t<s<T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},i\in{\mathcal{S}}\right\}

is a consistent family of path measures; that is, for every
0\leq t\leq t^{\prime}<s^{\prime}\leq s<T
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}
, if
\big(\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}\big)^{t^{\prime},\,y,\,j}
denotes the conditional distribution of
\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}
conditioning on its canonical process
(X_{t^{\prime}},\Lambda_{t^{\prime}})\equiv(y,j)
, then
\big(\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}\big)^{t^{\prime},\,y,\,j}
restricted to
\mathcal{F}_{s\prime}
is identical with
\mathbf{Q}^{y,j}_{t^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
. Hence there exists a path measure
\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
on
D((0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}})
such that, for every
0<t<s<T
and
(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
,

(2.2.2)

\big(\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}\big)^{t,x,i}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{s}}=\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}=Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s}\mathbf{P}^{x,i}_{t,s}.

We will again view
\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
as a Girsanov transform from
\mathbf{P}^{0}
by the triple
(u,\theta,\xi)
.

Thus, by rewriting the original SDE (
1.1.4
), we see that the canonical process
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{(0,T)}
under
\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
is again a regime-switching jump diffusion satisfying a new SDE:

(2.2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=\bigg[b(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})-\sigma(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})u(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt-\int_{|z|\leq 1}\theta(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t},z)\gamma(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t},z)\nu(dz)\bigg]dt\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\sigma(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})dB^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t}+\int_{|z|\leq 1}\gamma(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},z)\tilde{N}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}(dt,dz)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{|z|>1}\gamma(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},z)N(dt,dz)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\beta(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},w)N_{1}(dt,dw),\\
d\Lambda_{t}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\alpha(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},w)N_{1}(dt,dw)\qquad\textup{ for }\;t\in(0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

We can also determine the generator of
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{(0,T)}
under
\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
, denoted by
L^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
: for
f
of class
C^{1,2}_{c}
and
(t,x,i)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
,

(2.2.4)

\displaystyle L^{(u,\theta,\xi)}

\displaystyle f(t,x,i)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})_{m,n}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{n}}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+\left(b-\sigma u-\int_{|z|\leq 1}\theta(\cdot,z)\gamma(\cdot,z)\nu(dz)\right)(t,x,i)\cdot\nabla f(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\bigg(f(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)-f(t,x,i)

\displaystyle-\mathbbm{1}_{|z|\leq 1}\nabla f(t,x,i)\cdot\gamma(t,x,i,z)\bigg)(1-\theta(t,x,i,z))\nu(dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\bigg(f(t,x+\beta(t,x,i,w),i+\alpha(t,x,i,w))-f(t,x,i)\bigg)\xi(t,x,i,w)\mathfrak{m}(dw),

where, by the equivalence (
2.1.7
), the last term can also be written as

\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}\left(f(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)-f(t,x,i)\right)\int_{\Delta_{ij}(t,x)}\xi(t,x,i,w)\mathfrak{m}(dw).

The “Bridge” Girsanov Transform

We now apply the above general discussions on the Girsanov transform to prove the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 2.2.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
(H)
holds. Let
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
be the Schrödinger bridge as in (
2.1.2
). Then, the canonical process
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{(0,T)}
under
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
satisfies the SDE:

(2.2.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} dX_{t}&=b^{\varphi}(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})dB_{t}+\int_{|z|\leq 1}\gamma(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},z)\tilde{N}^{\varphi}(dt,dz)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{|z|>1}\gamma(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},z)N(dt,dz)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\beta(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},w)N_{1}(dt,dw),\\
d\Lambda_{t}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\alpha(t,X_{t-},\Lambda_{t-},w)N_{1}(dt,dw),\;\textup{ for }0<t<T,\end{aligned}\right.

with
(X_{0},\Lambda_{0})
and
(X_{T},\Lambda_{T})
having distributions
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{T}
respectively, where for every
(t,x,i)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(1)

The drift coefficient is given by

\displaystyle b^{\varphi}(t,x,i):=b(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+\sigma\sigma^{\top}\nabla\log\varphi(t,x,i)+

\displaystyle\int_{|z|\leq 1}\frac{\varphi(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)-\varphi(t,x,i)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\gamma(t,x,i,z)\nu(dz)

(2)

\left\{B_{t}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Brownian motion.

(3)

N(dt,dz)
is an
\ell
-dimensional Poisson random measure with intensity measure

\nu^{\varphi}(t,x,i;dz):=\frac{\varphi(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\nu(dz)

and
\tilde{N}^{\varphi}(dt,dz):=N(dt,dz)-\nu^{\varphi}(t,x,i;dz)dt
is its
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
-compensated counterpart.

(4)

N_{1}(dt,dw)
is a one-dimensional Poisson random measure with intensity measure

\mathfrak{m}^{\varphi}(t,x,i;dw)=\frac{\varphi(t,x+\beta(t,x,i,w),i+\alpha(t,x,i,w))}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\mathfrak{m}(dw).

Or equivalently, for
w\in\Delta_{ij}(t,x)
,
\mathfrak{m}^{\varphi}(t,x,i;dw)=\frac{\varphi(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\mathfrak{m}(dw)
.

Moreover, if
L_{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}}
denotes the generator of
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{(0,T)}
under
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
, then for every function
f
of class
C^{1,2}_{c}
and
(t,x,i)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
,

(2.2.6)

\displaystyle L_{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}}f(t,x,i)

\displaystyle:=b^{\varphi}(t,x,i)\cdot\nabla f(t,x,i)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n}(\sigma\sigma^{\top})_{m,n}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{n}}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\bigg(f(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)-f(t,x,i)-\mathbbm{1}_{|z|\leq 1}\gamma(t,x,i,z)\cdot\nabla f(t,x,i)\bigg)\nu^{\varphi}(dz)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j}\frac{\varphi(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}Q_{ij}(t,x)\left(f(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)-f(t,x,i)\right),

and further,

(2.2.7)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L_{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}}\right)f(t,x,i)=\frac{1}{f(t,x,i)}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L\right)(\varphi f)(t,x,i)

whenever
f(t,x,i)\neq 0
.

Proof.

This result is established by
[
45
, Theorem 4.2]
for jump diffusions without regime switching. We extend the proof to cover the regime-switching case. In particular, we will show that under Assumption
(H)
, when restricted to
\mathcal{F}_{T-}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
is in fact a Girsanov transform from
\mathbf{P}^{0}
with
(u,\theta,\xi)
explicitly determined by
\varphi
. Throughout the proof, whenever possible, we suppress the argument of
\gamma(t,x,i,z)
,
\beta(t,x,i,w)
, and
\alpha(t,x,i,w)
to simplify the notation.

For now, we take
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
to be the canonical process under
\mathbf{P}^{0}
, and start with setting up Itô’s formula for
\left\{\log\varphi(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{(0,T)}
. This is still feasible even though
\varphi(t,x,i)
is not differentiable in the third variable.
By direct computation and the assumption that
\varphi
is harmonic, we have that

(2.2.8)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L\right)(\log\varphi)(t,x,i)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}|\sigma^{\top}\nabla\log\varphi|^{2}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left[\log\frac{\varphi(t,x+\gamma,i)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}-\frac{\varphi(t,x+\gamma,i)-\varphi(t,x,i)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\right]\nu(dz)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}Q_{ij}(t,x)\left[\log\frac{\varphi(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}-\frac{\varphi(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)-\varphi(t,x,i)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\right],

where we used the equivalence (
2.1.7
) for the last term.
Combining the above computation with Itô’s formula yields, for every
0<t<s<T
,

(2.2.9)

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\varphi(s,X_{s},\Lambda_{s})}{\varphi(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{s}\sigma^{\top}\nabla\log\varphi(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r})\,dB_{r}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{s}|\sigma^{\top}\nabla\log\varphi(r,X_{r},\Lambda_{r})|^{2}\,dr\,

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\log\frac{\varphi(r,X_{r-}+\gamma,\Lambda_{r-})}{\varphi(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-})}\tilde{N}(dr,dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left(\log\frac{\varphi(r,X_{r-}+\gamma,\Lambda_{r-})}{\varphi(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-})}+1-\frac{\varphi(r,X_{r-}+\gamma,\Lambda_{r-})}{\varphi(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-})}\right)\nu(dz)dr

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\log\frac{\varphi(r,X_{r-}+\beta,\Lambda_{r-}+\alpha)}{\varphi(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-})}N_{1}(dr,dw)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{s}\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}Q_{\Lambda_{r-}\,j}(r,X_{r-})\left(1-\frac{\varphi(r,\psi_{\Lambda_{r-}j}(r,X_{r-}),j)}{\varphi(r,X_{r-},\Lambda_{r-})}\right)dr.

Since
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
has càdlàg paths,
\mathbf{P}^{0}
-a.e.
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
is contained in a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
. Thus, Assumption
on the coefficients and Assumption
(H)
on
\varphi
are sufficient to guarantee that all the (stochastic) integrals in the expression above are well defined. Moreover, the right-hand side is precisely equal to
\log(Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s})
as in (
2.2.1
) with the choice

(2.2.10)

\begin{cases}u(t,x,i)=-\sigma^{\top}\nabla\log\varphi(t,x,i),\\
\theta(t,x,i,z)=1-\frac{\varphi(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)}{\varphi(t,x,i)},\\
\xi(t,x,i,w)=\frac{\varphi(t,x+\beta(t,x,i,w),i+\alpha(t,x,i,w))}{\varphi(t,x,i)}.\end{cases}

Now, by (
2.1.3
), we have that

\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}^{0}}\left[Z^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{t,s}\right]=\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}^{0}}\left[\frac{\varphi(s,X_{s},\Lambda_{s})}{\varphi(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t})}\right]=1\textup{ for every }0<t<s<T,

which, as stated above, yields a path measure
\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
on
D((0,T);\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}})
, satisfying (
2.2.2
), and serving as a Girsanov transform from
\mathbf{P}^{0}
with the triple
(u,\theta,\xi)
in (
2.2.10
). Thus, combining (
2.1.4
) and (

), we arrive at, for every

and

,

\widehat{\mathbf{P}}^{x,i}_{t,s}=Z_{t,s}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}\mathbf{P}^{x,i}_{t,s}=\mathbf{Q}^{x,i}_{t,s}=\big(\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}\big)^{t,x,i}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{s}}.

We conclude that
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
, as a path measure on

, is identical with
\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}
, and hence can also be viewed as a Girsanov transform from
\mathbf{P}^{0}
by the same

as above. Therefore, the SDE (
2.2.5
) and the generator (

) claimed in the statement follow from (

) and (
2.2.4
) respectively.
The equivalence (
2.1.7
) then yields the regime-switching term in the generator
L_{\widehat{\mathbf{P}}}
. Again, we note that under Assumptions
and
(H)
, all the stochastic integrals concerned in (

) exist under
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
.

Finally, the identity (
2.2.7

) and the assumption that
(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L)\varphi=0
.
∎

As seen from the theorem above, the canonical process under the Schrödinger bridge

is again a regime-switching jump diffusion, where, in addition to the new drift

and the new jump intensity measure

Q^{\varphi}(t,x)
with entries
Q^{\varphi}_{ij}(t,x):=\frac{\varphi(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}Q_{ij}(t,x),\,i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Remark.
In the above discussion of the Girsanov transform perspective of the Schrödinger bridge, we have excluded the endpoints
t=0
and
t=T
(since we already know that the endpoint distributions are
\rho_{0},\rho_{T}
), and focused only on the dynamics of the “bridge”. However, if

) of

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}u\;dB^{(u,\theta,\xi)}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}|u|^{2}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\log(1-\theta)\,\tilde{N}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}(dr,dz)+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left(\log(1-\theta)(1-\theta)+\theta\right)\nu(dz)dr

2.3.
Stochastic Control Formulation and Solution

In this subsection, we will establish a stochastic control formulation for the SBP under investigation. Under stronger assumptions on
\varphi
(compared to the previous subsection), we will show that the sought after Schrödinger bridge
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
can be derived from the solution to a stochastic control problem. To achieve this goal, we adopt the following strengthening of Assumption
(H)
.

(
H’
)

The function
\varphi
defined by (
1.3.1
) satisfies Assumption
(H)
. In addition, for every
i\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
\varphi(T,\cdot,i)=\mathfrak{g}(\cdot,i)>0

\mathbf{R}_{T}
-a.e., and for every compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{x\in K}\left|\nabla\log\varphi(t,x,i)\right|^{2}dt<\infty\textup{ and }\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{j\neq i,x\in K}\left|\frac{\varphi(t,x,j)}{\varphi(t,x,i)}\right|^{2}dt<\infty.

Remark.
Let
\left\{(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})\right\}_{[0,T]}
be the canonical process under
\mathbf{P}^{0}
. Recall that, under Assumption
(H)
, when applying Itô’s formula to
\left\{-\log\big(\frac{\varphi(s,X_{s},\Lambda_{s})}{\varphi(0,X_{0},\Lambda_{0})}\big)\right\}_{s\in[0,T)}
, we get the equation (
2.2.9
) with
t=0
. Combining Assumption
(H’)
with Assumption
, it is not hard to verify that all the (stochastic) integrals in the RHS of (
2.2.9
) are still well defined when every “
\int_{t}^{s}
” is replaced by “
\int_{0}^{T}
”. In addition, given the continuity of
\varphi
as
t\nearrow T
, both sides of (
2.2.9
) (with
t=0
), as processes parametrized by
s\in[0,T]
, have càdlàg sample paths over
[0,T]
. Therefore,
\mathbf{P}^{0}
-a.e., the relation (
2.2.9
) remains valid all the way to
t=0,s=T
. In this case, as we remarked at the end of the previous subsection,

\widehat{\mathbf{P}}=\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}=Z_{0,T}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}\mathbf{P}^{0}\textup{ with }(u,\theta,\xi)\textup{ as in \eqref{P-hat Girsanov triple},}

i.e.,
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
is indeed the Girsanov transform from
\mathbf{P}^{0}
over paths on the entire interval
[0,T]
.

To develop the stochastic control approach toward the SBP (
1.1.1
), we first need to identify a proper
control class
.

Definition.
We define
{\mathcal{U}}
to be the class of triples
(u,\theta,\xi)
such that

(1)

u:(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\theta:(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\to(-\infty,1)
, and
\xi:(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to(0,\infty)
are measurable functions.

(2)

For every
i\in{\mathcal{S}}
and compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{x\in K}\left|u(t,x,i)\right|^{2}dt<\infty\textup{ and }\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{x\in K,z\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\frac{\left|\theta(t,x,i,z)\right|^{2}}{1\wedge\left|z\right|^{2}}dt<\infty,

and for every

Definition 2.3.1

\displaystyle(u^{*},\theta^{*},\xi^{*})

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left[\xi(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t},w)\log\xi(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t},w)+1-\xi(t,X_{t},\Lambda_{t},w)\right]\mathfrak{m}(dw)\bigg)dt\bigg].

As remarked at the end of the previous subsection, the minimization quantity in (

The SCP (

2

Expanding on ideas from the diffusion SBP case

\displaystyle F(t,u,\theta,\xi):=\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left[(1-\theta)\log(1-\theta)+\theta\right]\nu(dz)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(\xi\log\xi+1-\xi\right)\mathfrak{m}(dw),

Definition 2.3.2
.

Under the setting above, the unconstrained stochastic control formulation of the SBP (
1.1.1
) is to determine, for every

(2.3.2)

\displaystyle J^{*}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle:=\min_{(u,\theta,\xi)\in{\mathcal{U}}}\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{P}^{(u,\theta,\xi)}}\left[\int_{t}^{T}F(r,u,\theta,\xi)dr-\log\mathfrak{g}(X_{T},\Lambda_{T})\bigg|(X_{t},\Lambda_{t})=(x,i)\right],

as well as the corresponding minimizer

We will solve the unconstrained SCP (
2.3.2

2.3.1

The method of solving (
2.3.2

We begin by writing down the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation for (
2.3.2
), and that is, for every
(t,x,i)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
,

(2.3.3)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}J^{*}(t,x,i)+\inf_{(u,\theta,\xi)\in{\mathcal{U}}}\left\{L^{(u,\theta,\xi)}J^{*}(t,x,i)+F(t,u,\theta,\xi)(x,i)\right\}=0,\\
J^{*}(T,x,i)=-\log\mathfrak{g}(x,i).\vskip 2.84544pt\end{cases}

Let
(t,x,i)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}
be fixed. When there is no ambiguity, we may omit writing
(t,x,i)
as the argument of the controls
(u,\theta,\xi)
and the coefficients
\sigma,\gamma,\alpha,\beta
. Recall that

Thus, expanding the terms inside the infimum expression of (

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\inf_{u}\left\{-\sigma u\cdot\nabla J^{*}+\frac{1}{2}\left|u\right|^{2}\right\},\\
\displaystyle\inf_{\theta}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left[-\theta(J^{*}(t,x+\gamma,i)-J^{*}(t,x,i))+\log(1-\theta)(1-\theta)+\theta\right]\nu(dz)\right\},\\
\displaystyle\inf_{\xi}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left[(\xi-1)(J^{*}(t,x+\beta,i+\alpha)-J^{*}(t,x,i))+\xi\log\xi+1-\xi\right]\mathfrak{m}(dw)\right\}.\end{cases}

Given
J^{*}(t,x,i)
and

(2.3.4)

\begin{cases}u^{*}(t,x,i)=\sigma^{\top}\nabla J^{*}(t,x,i),\\
\theta^{*}(t,x,i,z)=1-\exp\left(J^{*}(t,x,i)-J^{*}(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)\right),\\
\xi^{*}(t,x,i,w)=\exp\left(J^{*}(t,x,i)-J^{*}(t,x+\beta,i+\alpha)\right).\end{cases}

Next, we need to find the function

(2.3.5)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L^{(u^{*},\theta^{*},\xi^{*})}\right)J^{*}(t,x,i)+F(t,u^{*},\theta^{*},\xi^{*})(x,i)=0

2.2.4

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L^{(u^{*},\theta^{*},\xi^{*})}\right)

\displaystyle J^{*}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L\right)J^{*}(t,x,i)-(\sigma u^{*}\cdot\nabla J^{*})(t,x,i)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\theta^{*}(t,x,i,z)(J^{*}(t,x+\gamma,i)-J^{*}(t,x,i))\nu(dz)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\xi^{*}(t,x,i,w)-1)(J^{*}(t,x+\beta,i+\alpha)-J^{*}(t,x,i))\mathfrak{m}(dw).

Thus, plugging in the values from (
2.3.4

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L\right)J^{*}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|\sigma^{\top}\nabla J^{*}|^{2}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\ell}}\left(e^{J^{*}(t,x,i)-J^{*}(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)}+J^{*}(t,x+\gamma(t,x,i,z),i)-J^{*}(t,x,i)-1\right)\,\nu(dz)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}Q_{ij}(t,x)\left(e^{J^{*}(t,x,i)-J^{*}(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)}+J^{*}(t,\psi_{ij}(t,x),j)-J^{*}(t,x,i)-1\right),

to be

(H’)

Remark.
We end this subsection on a remark regarding
\xi

in (

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta_{ij}(t,x)}\big(\xi\log\xi+1-\xi\big)(t,x,i,w)\,\mathfrak{m}(dw)\geq Q_{ij}(t,x)(C_{ij}\log C_{ij}+1-C_{ij}),

that is constant on every interval

3.3.
The Fortet-Sinkhorn Algorithm

In this subsection, we discuss an algorithmic approach toward finding the solution
\widehat{\mathbf{P}}
to the SBP (
1.1.1
), for which it suffices to solve the static Schrödinger system (
1.2.2
). We will only consider the situation when the initial data
\mathbf{R}_{0}
admits a density function
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\mathbf{R}_{0}(x,i)
for every
i\in{\mathcal{S}}
(and hence
\rho_{0}
also admits a density function
\rho_{0}(\cdot,i)
), in which case
\widehat{\varphi}(0,x,i)=\mathfrak{f}(x,i)\mathbf{R}_{0}(x,i)
for Lebesgue-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
i\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Under Assumption
, solving (
1.2.2
) is equivalent to finding
(\varphi,\widehat{\varphi})
that satisfies the system (
3.1.3
), where the third equation in (
3.1.3
) becomes

\rho_{0}(x,i)=\varphi(0,x,i)\widehat{\varphi}(0,x,i)\textup{ for Lebesgue-a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and every $i\in{\mathcal{S}}$.}

The existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
3.1.3
), as well as how to find the solution, is a long-studied problem in the history of the SBP
[
15
,
2
,
18
,
4
]
. One approach is to construct recursively a sequence of functions that converges to a solution of (
3.1.3
). This is the idea of the
Fortet-Sinkhorn algorithm
, originally proposed in
[
15
]
, which consists of alternating between the four equations (
3.1.3
). More specifically, for any function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, we define the maps
\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{T}},\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{0}}
on
f
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(f)(x,i)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{f(x,i)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{T}}(f)(x,i)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j\in{\mathcal{S}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{ij}(0,x,T,y)\rho_{T}(y,j)f(y,j)dy,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{0}}(f)(y,j)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{S}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{ij}(0,x,T,y)\rho_{0}(x,i)f(x,i)dx,

for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
i,j\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

Clearly, if
(\varphi,\widehat{\varphi})
solves the Schrödinger system (
3.1.3
) and
\varphi(0,\cdot),\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)
are positive everywhere, then we have the following chain relation:

{\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\mapsto}}{\frac{1}{\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{T}}}}{{\mapsto}}{\varphi(0,\cdot)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\mapsto}}{\frac{1}{\varphi(0,\cdot)}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{0}}}}{{\mapsto}}{\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)}

Therefore, if we define
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{0}}\circ\mathcal{D}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\rho_{T}}\circ\mathcal{D}
, then
\mathcal{C}(\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot))=\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)
. In other words,
\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)
is a fixed point under
\mathcal{C}
. Under certain conditions, the algorithm that consists of
repeatedly applying the map
\mathcal{C}
will in fact converge to the unique fixed point
\widehat{\varphi}(T,\cdot)
, with
any
starting function. This algorithm is well-understood and studied in many SBP contexts
[
15
,
4
,
5
]
, as well as beyond
2
2

2

This algorithm is known under different names - Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, Iterative Proportional Fitting Procedure, RAS method, etc.
. Below, we formulate this convergence result rigorously in the regime-switching setting.

Theorem 3.3.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
holds and
\mathbf{R}_{0}
admits a density function

for every
i\in{\mathcal{S}}

\rho_{0}(\cdot,i)
and

and

In this section we present an application of the regime-switching jump-diffusion SBP investigated in the previous sections in the context of the

(uSBP). Unlike classical SBPs aiming at “bridging” probability distributions, the model of uSBP arises from the study of stochastic particle systems with

(i.e., with total mass less than

We will set up the model using the same framework of regime-switching jump diffusion as presented in
Section

1.1
but with only two regimes. Namely, we take

, with “
1
” being the active regime and “
2
” being the dead regime, and consider the jump diffusion determined by the SDE (
1.1.2
) on the Skorokhod space
\Omega=D([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\left\{1,2\right\})
. We assume that, when restricted to regime “
2

are constant zero, which means that there is no dynamic in the dead regime. Moreover, we require that, for every

(1)

for some function

at every moment

Background on uSBP

Stochastic dynamical systems with absorbing states or disappearing particles are ubiquitous in nature. These situations can range from radioactive decay
[
46
]
to cellular event triggers
[
16
]
to oceanic current tracers sinking
[
5
]
. On the other hand, jump diffusion models with killing provide a mathematically rich framework for modeling such dynamical systems where the stochastic motion is described by a combination of drift, diffusion, and/or jump mechanics. The uSBP for this class of stochastic processes thus becomes an important tool for constructing models that are consistent with observational data. A “modern” version of the uSBP was introduced in
[
5
]
as a mathematical model following Schrödinger’s original quest
3
3

3

This formulation was in contrast with the

[
5

for details).
, where the reference measure

is given by a diffusion process in

with killing, and the target distributions

prescribe the distribution of the surviving particles and the total amount of mass lost by time

.
This problem was approached by augmenting

to include a “coffin state”
\mathfrak{c}
to which the process would jump upon being killed, and then treated as a classic SBP for càdlàg paths in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\cup\left\{\mathfrak{c}\right\}
. This version of the uSBP was then further studied in different contexts including neural networks

[
31
]
, entropic optimal transport
[
25
]
, and random walks on directed graphs
[
11
]
. A variation of the uSBP was examined in
[
13

[

where the target at the terminal time is imposed only on the killing time and the killing location of the dead particles (but not the distribution of the surviving particles).

Compared with the version of uSBP in
[

is a

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{-L_{V}q(\cdot,T,y)\,(t,x)}{q(0,x_{0},T,y)}\rho_{T}(y,1)dy-V(t)\frac{\rho_{T}(x,2)}{\int_{0}^{T}q(0,x_{0},s,x)ds}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\int_{t}^{T}V(r)(-L_{V}q(\cdot,r,y))dr\,(t,x)}{\int_{0}^{T}V(s)q(0,x_{0},s,y)ds}\rho_{T}(y,2)dy

Similarly, by (

) and, again,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1)=L_{V}^{*}\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1)=L^{*}_{0}\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1)-V(s)\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,2)=V(s)q(0,x_{0},s,y)=V(s)\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1),

where

is the adjoint operator of

with respect to

We summarize the above PIDEs into the following system:

Unbalanced Dynamic Schrödinger System:

\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+L_{0}\right)\varphi(t,x,1)+V(t)(\varphi(t,x,2)-\varphi(t,x,1))=0,\;&\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}(t,x,2)=0.\\
\left(-\frac{\partial}{\partial s}+L^{*}_{0}\right)\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1)-V(s)\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1)=0,\;&\frac{\partial\widehat{\varphi}}{\partial s}(s,y,2)-V(s)\widehat{\varphi}(s,y,1)=0.\\

\varphi(T,y,1)\widehat{\varphi}(T,y,1)=\rho_{T}(y,1),\;&\varphi(T,y,2)\widehat{\varphi}(T,y,2)=\rho_{T}(y,2).\end{cases}

In the above system, the first two lines hold for
(t,x),(s,y)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the third line in the sense of measure, and the last line for
\rho_{T}
-a.e.
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Moreover, we are also ready to apply

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{12}(t,x,s,y)

4.4.
Further Questions on the uSBP

In this example, we discussed one specific uSBP scenario: when the target is on the spatial distributions of the surviving and the killed particles. Since the target distributions are often extracted from the actual observations of the system, a further question that is worth exploring is how to adapt the uSBP formulation to scenarios where the observations provide access to different levels of information about the particles. As mentioned earlier, a few examples involving different observational constraints have been addressed in the literature
[
13
,
5
,
12
]
, but a general investigation of this question is still missing. We hope to establish a unified framework, based on the regime-switching approach, to systematically study and compare uSBPs in a broad range of target distribution scenarios. The key observation is that by varying
\psi(t,x)
, we can choose which information to “record” about the killed particles, and this exact information will be exhibited in the terminal distribution in regime
2
. In this way, we can treat events that occur in the
interior
of the path as part of the reference
terminal
data, and “match” it with our target when solving the uSBP.

The regime-switching approach also enables us to study uSBP models in which, in addition to active particles being killed, the dead particles are also allowed to revive. This is done simply by changing the regime-switching rate
Q_{21}
to be non-zero. The general theory of the regime-switching SBP can then be applied to derive properties of the unbalanced Schrödinger bridge in this setting. Moreover, it is also possible to distinguish between “revived” and “never-killed” particles by introducing a third regime and letting
Q_{23}
be the revival rate (instead of
Q_{21}
).

In summary, the regime-switching approach may lead to a deeper and more comprehensive theory of the uSBP, and we hope this work helps build its foundations.

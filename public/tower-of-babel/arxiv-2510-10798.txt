Abstract
We study, for 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , the Hardy space 𝒉 e p ​ ( 𝔹 ) \bm{h}_{e}^{p}(\mathbb{B}) , the elastic analogue of the classical Hardy spaces of harmonic functions in the unit ball of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . The space consists of vector-field solutions of the Lamé system satisfying the standard integrability condition on concentric spheres centered at the origin. Using the elastic Poisson kernel, we establish a Fatou-type theorem and show that 𝒉 e p ​ ( 𝔹 ) \bm{h}_{e}^{p}(\mathbb{B}) is isomorphic to the ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} -valued Lebesgue space L p L^{p} on the unit sphere for 1 < p ≤ ∞ 1<p\leq\infty , while 𝒉 e 1 ​ ( 𝔹 ) \bm{h}_{e}^{1}(\mathbb{B}) corresponds to the space of ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} -valued Borel measures on the unit sphere. For 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty , we prove that 𝒉 e p ​ ( 𝔹 ) \bm{h}_{e}^{p}(\mathbb{B}) decomposes as the direct sum of three subspaces. The main contribution of this paper is to describe each of these subspaces along with the corresponding spaces of boundary values. In particular, two of these spaces consist of solutions of the Lamé equation for all eligible choices of the Lamé constants: one of them is the space of Riesz fields (solutions of the generalized Cauchy–Riemann equations) in 𝒉 e p ​ ( 𝔹 ) \bm{h}_{e}^{p}(\mathbb{B}) ; the second is the space of fields given by the cross product of x x with such Riesz fields. The results rely on the classical decomposition of L 2 L^{2} vector fields on the sphere into the direct sum of three spaces of vector spherical harmonics, which we extend to L p L^{p} .

4
Elastic Poisson kernel in the unit ball and boundary functions

Following Kupradze (see
[
9
, Ch. XIV, 1.26]
),
the elastic Poisson kernel is the matrix-valued function

\mathcal{\bm{P}}_{e}:\mathbb{B}\times\mathbb{S}\rightarrow M_{3\times 3}(\mathbb{R}),

which can be seen as a perturbation of the Poisson kernel for harmonic functions,
P(x,\eta)
, in
\mathbb{B}
, that is,

\mathcal{\bm{P}}_{e}(x,\eta)=P(x,\eta)\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{L}(x,\eta),

(11)

where
\mathcal{I}
is the identity matrix,

P(x,\eta)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{|x-\eta|^{3}},

(12)

and

\mathcal{L}_{ij}(x,\eta)=\beta(1-|x|^{2})\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x,\eta),\;\;i,j\in\{1,2,3\},

(13)

with

\Phi(x,\eta)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1-t^{2}|x|^{2}}{(1-2tx\cdot\eta+t^{2}|x|^{2})^{3/2}}-1-3tx\cdot\eta\right)\frac{dt}{t^{1+\alpha}}

(14)

and

\beta=\frac{\lambda+\mu}{2\lambda+6\mu},\qquad\alpha=\frac{\lambda+2\mu}{\lambda+3\mu}.

Notice that for

and
\eta
fixed, we have
4\pi P(tx,\eta)-1-3tx\cdot\eta=O(t)
so, since
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\Phi
is well defined. Furthermore, by (
44
) and (
45
) of the appendix,
\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}(x,\eta)
is also well defined.

The Poisson transform

We define for

(18)

The elastic Poisson transform

\bm{P}_{e}\bm{f}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{P}_{e}(x,\eta)\bm{f}(\eta)d\sigma(\eta),\;\;\;\bm{f}\in\bm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S}),\;\;1\leq p\leq\infty.

We have that

\displaystyle\bm{P}_{e}\bm{f}(x)

\displaystyle=P\bm{f}(x)+\bm{L}\bm{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}P(x,\eta)\bm{f}(\eta)d\sigma(\eta)+\int_{\mathbb{S}}\mathcal{L}(x,\eta)\bm{f}(\eta)d\sigma(\eta).

For

where

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{1}(\bm{f})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{\ell+1}{2\ell+1}\langle\bm{f},\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}\rangle\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}(\xi),

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{2}(\bm{f})(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\langle\bm{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}\rangle\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}(\xi),

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{3}(\bm{f})(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\langle\bm{f},\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}\rangle\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}(\xi),

\displaystyle\bm{T}_{4}(\bm{f})(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{1}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\bm{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}\rangle\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}(\xi).

Notice that for
\bm{f}\in\bm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
, we can write

\bm{T}_{1}(\bm{f})=\mathcal{M}_{\frac{\ell+1}{2\ell+1}}(\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\nu})^{\vee}.

Since by Theorem
1
the operator
\mathcal{M}_{\frac{\ell+1}{2\ell+1}}
is continuous in
L^{p}(\mathbb{S})
, it follows immediately that
\bm{T}_{1}
has a bounded extension in
\bm{L}^{p}-
norm.

Next, for
\bm{f}\in\bm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
, we can write

-\bm{T}_{3}(\bm{f})(\xi)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\frac{1}{2\ell+1}\widehat{(\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\nu})}_{\ell,m}\mathbf{\nabla}_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}(\xi),

Since
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat{(\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\nu})}_{\l,m}Y_{\ell,m}(\xi)
converges in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{S})
, we have that

-\bm{T}_{3}(\bm{f})(\xi)=\nabla_{\sigma}\mathcal{M}_{\frac{1}{2\ell+1}}(\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\nu}).

Then, using Theorems
1
and
2
,

\|\bm{T}_{3}(\bm{f})\|_{\bm{L}^{p}}\lesssim\|(-\Delta)^{1/2}(\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\nu})\|_{L^{p}}=\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha_{\ell}}(\bm{f}\cdot\bm{\nu})\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{p}},

where

\alpha_{\ell}=\frac{\ell^{1/2}\left(\ell+1\right)^{1/2}}{2\ell+1}.

It also follows that
\bm{T}_{2}
is
\bm{L}^{p}-
bounded since its adjoint is
\bm{T}_{3}
, which is
\bm{L}^{q}-
bounded for any
1<q<\infty
.

Finally, to study
\bm{T}_{4}
, we observe that for
\bm{f}\in\bm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
,

\bm{T}_{4}(\bm{f})=\nabla_{\sigma}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{1}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\bm{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}\rangle Y_{\ell,m}\right).

By Theorem
2
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left|\bm{T}_{4}(\bm{f})(\xi)\right|^{p}d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left|\left(-\Delta\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{1}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\bm{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell k}\rangle Y_{\ell k}(\cdot)\right)(\xi)\right|^{p}d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left|\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{\ell^{1/2}\left(\ell+1\right)^{1/2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\bm{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}\rangle Y_{\ell,m}(\xi)\right|^{p}d\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}}\left|\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{\ell^{1/2}\left(\ell+1\right)^{1/2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\bm{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}\rangle\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}(\xi)\right|^{p}d\sigma(\xi).

(53)

Let
\bm{\widetilde{T}}_{2}
be the operator defined by

\bm{\widetilde{T}}_{2}(\xi)=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{\ell^{1/2}\left(\ell+1\right)^{1/2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\mathbf{f},\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}\rangle\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}(\xi),

and its adjoint

\bm{\widetilde{T}}_{3}(\xi)=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\sum_{|m|\leq\ell}\frac{\ell^{1/2}\left(\ell+1\right)^{1/2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}\langle\mathbf{f},\bm{Y}_{\ell,m}^{\vee}\rangle\nabla_{\sigma}Y_{\ell,m}(\xi).

As done for
\bm{T}_{3}
, it can proved that
\bm{\widetilde{T}}_{3}
is
\bm{L}^{p}-
bounded for any
1<p<\infty
, and hence the same holds for
\bm{\widetilde{T}}_{2}
.
Thus from (
7.2
),

\|\bm{T}_{4}(\bm{f})\|_{\bm{L}^{p}}\lesssim\|\bm{f}\|_{\bm{L}^{p}}.

This completes the proof that
\bm{\pi}_{+}
is bounded in
\bm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{S})
. The proof of the
L^{p}
- continuity of
\bm{\pi}_{-}
is completely analogous, and since
\bm{\pi}_{0}=Id-\bm{\pi}_{+}-\bm{\pi}_{-}

on
\bm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{S})
, the proof of the theorem is complete.

\square

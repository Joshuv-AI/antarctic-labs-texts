Hinich’s model for Day convolution revisited
Christoph Winges

Abstract
. We prove that Hinich’s construction of the Day convolution operad of two 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -monoidal ∞ \infty -categories is an exponential in the ∞ \infty -category of ∞ \infty -operads over 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , and use this to give an explicit description of the formation of algebras in the Day convolution operad as a bivariant functor.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
18N70, 18N60

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
be an
\infty
-operad and consider operad maps
p\colon\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
and
q\colon\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
.
If it exists, the
Day convolution
of
p
and
q
is an exponential of
q
by
p
in the
\infty
-category of
\infty
-operads over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
, ie a right adjoint object to
q
with respect to the product functor
-\times_{\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\colon(\mathrm{Op}_{\infty})_{/\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}}\to(\mathrm{Op}_{\infty})_{/\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}}
.
Using the combinatorial machinery of quasicategories, Lurie gives a very general construction of Day convolution operads in
[
Lur
, Section 2.2.6]
;
see also
[
Hin20
, Section 2.8]
.

For practical purposes, it is often sufficient to know that the Day convolution operad exists in the case that
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}
are
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal categories.
In the case of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories, Glasman gives a concrete description of the Day convolution operad in
[
Gla16
]
.
Hinich provides in
[
Hin20
]
a rather straightforward description of the Day convolution operad for arbitrary
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories, and it is this model we will focus on in the sequel.

To facilitate Hinich’s description, recall that there are equivalences of
\infty
-categories

\Alg_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Mon_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty})\simeq\mathrm{Cocart}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{O}).

Here,
\mathrm{Cocart}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{O})
denotes the
\infty
-category of cocartesian fibrations of
\infty
-operads over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
.
The first equivalence is given by composition with the cartesian structure
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\times}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}

[
Lur
, Proposition 2.4.2.5]
and the second equivalence is induced by (un)straightening; this is implicit in
[
Lur
]
and explicitly spelled out in
[
Hin15
, Proposition A.2.1]
.
In the sequel, we will freely switch between these descriptions of
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories as necessary.

Denote by
\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}
the full subcategory of
(\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty})_{/[1]}
spanned by the cartesian fibrations and equip it with the cartesian symmetric monoidal structure.
Restriction to
\{0\}
and
\{1\}
defines symmetric monoidal functors
t\colon\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
and
s\colon\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
, where we also equip
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
with the cartesian symmetric monoidal structure.

1.1 Theorem

(
[
Hin20
, Section 2.8.9]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories and denote by
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}})^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\times}\times_{\mathrm{Comm}^{\otimes}}\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\times}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(s,t)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})}

Then
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
is the Day convolution of
\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}
.

The goal of this note is to give an alternative proof of this theorem.
Instead of identifying
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
with another model for the Day convolution operad as in
[
Hin20
, Section 6.3.9]
, we verify directly that
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
possesses the correct universal property, also on the level of categories of algebras.
In fact, we show first that the construction of
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
promotes the assignment
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\mapsto\Alg_{/\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}})
to a bivariant functor and exhibit a natural equivalence

\Alg_{/\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}})\simeq\Alg_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{D}),

where the functoriality of the right hand side is given by pre- and postcomposition with operad maps.
The universal property of
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
follows easily from this.
This is done in
Section

2
.

Section

3
comments on some variations of these statements for
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal
\infty
-categories living in certain suboperads of the cartesian symmetric monoidal structure on
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
.

Section

4
reproves the well-known statement that
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
defines an
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal
\infty
-category under suitable cocompleteness assumptions on
\mathcal{D}
.

2.
Algebras in the Day convolution operad

Throughout this section, fix a base operad
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
as well as
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
.
Our goal is to prove the following strengthening of
Theorem

1.1
.

2.1 Theorem
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
be an operad over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
.
Then there exists a natural equivalence

\Alg_{\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{D})\simeq\Alg_{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}).

Note that
Theorem

1.1
follows from this by passing to groupoid cores.
The proof of this theorem is already implicit in
[
CDH+23
, Remark 5.2.5]
, but
it will be convenient to formulate the argument in terms of some concepts introduced in

[
HHLN23a
]
.

2.2 Definition

(
[
HHLN23a
, Observation 2.3.2]
)
.

A functor
(p_{1},p_{2})\colon X\to Y\times Z
is a
curved orthofibration
if

(1)

p_{1}\colon X\to Y
is a cartesian fibration;

(2)

p_{2}\colon X\to Z
is a cocartesian fibration;

(3)

p_{1}
-cartesian lifts of morphisms project to equivalences under
p_{2}
;

(4)

p_{2}
-cocartesian lifts of morphisms project to equivalences under
p_{1}
.

Denote by
\mathrm{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(Y)
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y}
spanned by the cocartesian fibrations, and by
\mathrm{Cart}^{\mathrm{opl}}(Z)
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Cat}_{/Z}
spanned by the cartesian fibrations.
In addition,
\mathrm{CurvOrtho}(Y,Z)
denotes the subcategory of
\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y\times Z}
whose objects are curved orthofibrations and whose morphisms are functors over
Y\times Z
preserving both
p_{1}
-cartesian and
p_{2}
-cocartesian morphisms.
We will make use of the following description of
\mathrm{CurvOrtho}(Y,Z)
.

2.3 Proposition

(
[
HHLN23a
, Corollary 2.3.4]
)
.

Unstraightening over
Y
and
Z
induces equivalences

\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Cocart}^{\mathrm{lax}}(Z))^{\mathrm{cart}}\simeq\mathrm{CurvOrtho}(Y,Z)\simeq\Fun(Z,\mathrm{Cart}^{\mathrm{opl}}(Y))^{\mathrm{cocart}}

which are natural in
Y
and
Z
; the superscripts
\mathrm{cocart}
and
\mathrm{cart}
denote the wide subcategories on those natural transformations whose components all preserve (co)cartesian morphisms.

We will also require the following statement about the interaction of cartesian and cocartesian morphisms in curved orthofibrations.

2.4 Lemma
.

Let
(p_{1},p_{2})\colon X\to Y\times Z
be a curved orthofibration, and let
g\colon y\to y^{\prime}
and
h\colon z\to z^{\prime}
be morphisms in
Y
and
Z
, respectively.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The cartesian transport
g^{*}\colon p_{1}^{-1}(a^{\prime})\to p_{1}^{-1}(a)
along
g
preserves
p_{2}
-cocartesian lifts of
h
.

(2)

The cocartesian transport
h_{!}\colon p_{2}^{-1}(b)\to p_{2}^{-1}(b^{\prime})
along
h
preserves
p_{1}
-cartesian lifts of
g
.

Proof.

This follows by inspection of the proof of
[
HHLN23a
, Proposition 2.3.11]
.
∎

Given
2.4
, we can make the following definition, see
[
HHLN23a
, Definition 2.3.10]
.

2.5 Definition
.

A curved orthofibration

p=(p_{1},p_{2})\colon X\to Y\times Z

is an
orthofibration
if it satisfies the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

the cartesian transport functor
g^{*}\colon p_{1}^{-1}(y^{\prime})\to p_{1}^{-1}(y)
preserves
p_{2}
-cocartesian morphisms for every morphism
g\colon y\to y^{\prime}
in
Y
;

(2)

the cocartesian transport functor
h_{!}\colon p_{2}^{-1}(z)\to p_{2}^{-1}(x^{\prime})
preserves
p_{1}
-cartesian morphisms for every morphism
h\colon z\to z^{\prime}
in
Z
.

By
[
HHLN23a
, Corollary 2.5.6]
, the equivalences of
2.3
restrict to equivalences

(2.6)

\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Cocart}(Z))\simeq\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)\simeq\Fun(Z,\mathrm{Cart}(Y)),

where
\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)\subseteq\mathrm{CurvOrtho}(Y,Z)
denotes the full subcategory of orthofibrations.
Consequently, every orthofibration straightens to a functor
Y^{\mathrm{op}}\times Z\to\mathrm{Cat}
, either by cocartesian straightening over
Z
or by cartesian straightening over
Y
.
The resulting straightening functors
\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)\to\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}}\times Z,\mathrm{Cat})
are equivalent, see
[
HHLN23a
, Remark 2.5.7]
.

Since
(s,t)\colon\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}\to\mathrm{Cat}\times\mathrm{Cat}
is an orthofibration by
[
HHLN23b
, Proposition 7.9]
,
combining
[
HHLN23a
, Theorem E]
and
[
HHLN23b
, Theorem 7.21]
shows that
(s,t)
straightens to the functor

\Fun\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathrm{Cat}.

In fact, we will reprove this statement without recourse to the 2-categorical machinery of
[
HHLN23b
]
.

2.7 Theorem
.

The functor

(s_{*},t_{*})\colon\Alg_{\mathcal{O}}(\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}})\to\Alg_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Cat})\times\Alg_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Cat})

is an orthofibration which straightens to the obvious functor

(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\mapsto\Alg_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{Y}).

2.8 Remark
.

By specialising
Theorem

2.7
to the case that
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
is the trivial operad, we recover the claim that the orthofibration
(s,t)\colon\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}\to\mathrm{Cat}\times\mathrm{Cat}
straightens to the functor
\Fun\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathrm{Cat}
.

Assuming this statement, we can prove
Theorem

2.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

We will apply
Theorem

2.7
to
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}
, which provides for every pair of
\mathcal{A}
-monoidal categories
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
an equivalence

\Alg_{/\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}})\simeq\Alg_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{Y}).

Therefore, we obtain equivalences

\displaystyle\Alg_{\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle\simeq\Alg_{/\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle\simeq\Alg_{/\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{C},\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{D}})

\displaystyle\simeq\Alg_{\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{D})

\displaystyle\simeq\Alg_{\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{D}).

Specialising to the case
\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}=\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
, this provides an evaluation map

\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}

over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
which induces the above equivalence, making it clear that this identification is natural.
∎

Theorem

, let us record another easy consequence.
We require some additional notation.

2.10 Corollary
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{A}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
be an operad over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
.

(1)

The functor

is a cocartesian fibration which straightens to the functor

\Alg_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathrm{Cat})\to\mathrm{Cat},\quad\mathcal{Y}\mapsto\Alg_{\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{Y})

(2)

applied to the base operad

.
The key input for our argument is the existence of free cartesian fibrations.

2.11 Construction
.

Let

Define the

\Fr(f)
on

as the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fr_{\mathrm{cart}}(f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun([1],I)}

(

)
.

Let

2.14 Proposition
.

(1)

The functor

(s,t)\colon\mathrm{Cart}([1])\to\mathrm{Cat}\times\mathrm{Cat}

is an orthofibration.
A morphism
f\colon p\to q
in
\mathrm{Cart}([1])
is
s
-cartesian if and only if
t(f)
is an equivalence, and
f
is
t
-cocartesian if and only if
s(f)
is an equivalence.

(2)

The functor

(s,t)\colon\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}\to\mathrm{Cat}\times\mathrm{Cat}

is an orthofibration.
Moreover, the functor
\mathrm{Cart}([1])\to\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}
preserves both

-cartesian and

-cocartesian morphisms.

Proof.

By unstraightening,
\mathrm{Cart}([1])\simeq\Fun([1]^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Cat})
, with

and

corresponding to evaluation at
1
and

, respectively.
By
[

, Corollary 2.4.7.11]
, the evaluation functor at

is a cartesian fibration and the evaluation functor at

is a cocartesian fibration, and the characterisation of

-cartesian and

-cocartesian morphisms follows from

, Lemma 2.4.7.5]
.
The explicit description of

-cartesian and

-cocartesian morphisms also implies that

is an orthofibration.

For assertion (

), it suffices to show that

-cartesian morphisms in

are also

-cartesian in
\Ar^{\mathrm{opl}}
, and that the same holds true for

-cocartesian morphisms.

So let

be an

-cartesian morphism in

, where

and

are cartesian fibrations.
For every cartesian fibration

, we have to show that the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathrm{Cat}_{/[1]}}(r,p)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathrm{Cat}_{/[1]}}(r,q)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathrm{Cat}}(s(r),s(p))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathrm{Cat}}(s(r),s(q))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\circ-}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s(f)\circ-}

is a pullback.

be a

Let

be an arbitrary cartesian fibration.
Note that

.
Using

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathrm{Cart}([1])}(\Fr_{\mathrm{cart}}(q),r)}

2.16 Lemma

2.17 Lemma
.

Let

,

(2)

-cartesian morphisms are

-cartesian;

(4)

p_{2}
-cocartesian morphisms are

\Mon_{\mathcal{O}}(X)

(p_{1})_{*}
-cartesian or
(p_{2})_{*}
-cocartesian if and only if it is
(p_{1})_{*}
-cartesian or
(p_{2})_{*}
-cocartesian in
\Fun(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes},X)
.

Proof.

By
2.16
, the induced functor

p_{*}=((p_{1})_{*},(p_{2})_{*})\colon\Fun(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes},X)\to\Fun(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes},Y)\times\Fun(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes},Z)

Let

be a morphism in
\Mon_{\mathcal{O}}(Y)
and let

be an
\mathcal{O}
-monoid in

lifting

.
Considering

as a morphism in
\Fun(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes},Y)
, there exists a cartesian lift

in
\Fun(\mathcal{O}^{\otimes},X)
.

is also an
\mathcal{O}
-monoid in

For

in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i=1}^{n}M(x_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\prime}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i=1}^{n}M^{\prime}(x_{i})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

-cartesian morphisms project to equivalences under

To determine the straightening of
(s_{*},t_{*})

First, we observe that the existence of free (co)cartesian fibrations implies the existence of free orthofibrations.

2.19 Corollary
.

The inclusion functor
\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)\to\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y\times Z}
admits a left adjoint

\Fr\colon\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y\times Z}\to\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z).

Proof.

We write
p_{Z}\colon Y\times Z\to Z
for the projection functor.
Since
p_{Z}
is a cartesian fibration, the equivalence
\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y\times Z}\simeq(\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y})/p_{Z}
and
2.12
induce an adjunction

\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y\times Z}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{Cart}(Y)/p_{Z}.

After identifying

\mathrm{Cart}(Y)/p_{Z}\simeq\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Cat})_{/\con_{Z}}\simeq\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Cat}_{/Z}),

the existence of free cocartesian fibrations induces an adjunction

\mathrm{Cart}(Y)/p_{Z}\rightleftarrows\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Cocart}(Z))\simeq\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z).\qed

2.20 Remark
.

Unwinding the proof of
2.19
, one finds that the free orthofibration on a functor
f\colon X\to Y\times Z
is given by the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fr(f)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun([1],Y)\times\Fun([1],Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{1}\times\ev_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

together with the evaluation map

\Fr(f)\to\Fun([1],Y)\times\Fun([1],Z)\xrightarrow{\ev_{0}\times\ev_{1}}Y\times Z.

One can adapt the proof of
[
GHN17
, Theorem 4.5]
to show directly that this yields a left adjoint to the functor
\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)\to\mathrm{Cat}_{/Y\times Z}
.

We can apply this statement to identify the straightenings of cotensors of orthofibrations.

2.21 Lemma
.

Let
p\colon X\to Y\times Z
be an orthofibration.
Consider the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(I,X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fun(I,Y\times Z)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\con}

Then

is an orthofibration such that both
p^{I}_{1}
-cartesian and
p^{I}_{2}
-cocartesian morphisms are detected componentwise in

.
Moreover,

straightens to the functor

\Fun(I,\St(p))\colon Y^{\mathrm{op}}\times Z\to\mathrm{Cat}.

Proof.

The first part of the lemma is precisely
2.16
, so we only have to prove the assertion about the straightening of

.
For every functor

, there exist by
2.19
natural equivalences

\displaystyle\Hom_{Y\times Z}(T,X^{I})

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{Y\times Z}(T\times I,X)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)}(\Fr(T\times I\to Y\times Z),X)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)}(\Fr(f)\times I,X)

\displaystyle\simeq\Nat(\St(\Fr(f))\times I,\St(p))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)}(\Fr(f),\Un(\Fun(I,\St(p))))

Recall the following definition from
[
HHLN23a
, Proposition 2.3.13]
.

2.22 Definition
.

A functor
p=(p_{1},p_{2})\colon X\to Y\times Z
is a
bifibration
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

p_{1}
is a cartesian fibration such that a morphism in
X
is
p_{1}
-cartesian if and only if it projects to an equivalence under
p_{2}
;

(2)

p_{2}
is a cocartesian fibration such that a morphism in
X
is
p_{2}
-cocartesian if and only if it projects to an equivalence under
p_{1}
.

By
[
HHLN23a
, Corollary 2.3.15]
, the equivalences from (
2.6
) restrict to equivalences

\Fun(Y^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{LFib}(Z))\simeq\mathrm{Bifib}(Y,Z)\simeq\Fun(Z,\mathrm{RFib}(Y)),

where
\mathrm{Bifib}(Y,Z)\subseteq\mathrm{Ortho}(Y,Z)
denotes the full subcategory of bifibrations, and
\mathrm{LFib}(Z)
and
\mathrm{RFib}(Y)
denote the categories of left fibrations over
Z
and right fibrations over
Y
, respectively.

2.23 Example
.

The functor
(\ev_{1},\ev_{0})\colon\Fun([1]^{\mathrm{op}},X)\to X
is a bifibration for every category
X
—the special case
X=\mathrm{Cat}
was covered in

.
Moreover, this functor straightens to the functor

\Hom_{X}\colon X^{\mathrm{op}}\times X\to\mathrm{An}.

This follows for example from
[
HMS22
, Corollary A.2.5]
because the cartesian unstraightening of

is the twisted arrow category.

2.24 Definition

Let

be an orthofibration.
Define

as the wide subcategory of

generated by the collections of
p_{1}
-cartesian and
p_{2}
-cocartesian morphisms.

.

Let

be an orthofibration.

(1)

in

lies in

(b)

is the composition of a
p_{1}
-cartesian morphism followed by a

-cocartesian morphism;

(c)

is the composition of a

-cocartesian morphism followed by a

-cartesian morphism.

(2)

By
[

, Definition 2.3.10]

-cartesian morphisms canonically commute with

-cocartesian morphisms, which shows assertion (

).

For assertion (

), let us first check that

is a cartesian fibration.
Every morphism in
Y
admits a
p_{1}
-cartesian lift, so this reduces to checking that for a
p_{1}
-cartesian morphism

in
X
, an arbitrary morphism

lies in

if and only if

lies in
X_{\mathrm{bicart}}
.
Writing

as the composite of a

-cocartesian morphism followed by a

-cartesian morphism, this is immediate from

, Lemma 2.4.2.7]

In particular, every

-cartesian morphism projects to an equivalence under

.

Suppose now that

is a morphism in

such that

is an equivalence.
Writing

as a composition of a

-cartesian morphism followed by a

-cocartesian morphism, it follows that

Hence

p_{2}
-cocartesian lift of an equivalence, and thus an equivalence.

\xi

By dualising, we see that

is a bifibration.

Assertion (

with the full subfunctor of

given by those functors
\mathcal{X}^{\otimes}\times I\to\mathcal{Y}^{\otimes}
such that
\mathcal{X}^{\otimes}\times\{i\}\to\mathcal{Y}^{\otimes}
is an operad map for all
i\in I
.
After currying, we obtain a natural equivalence

\St((s_{*},t_{*})^{I}_{\mathrm{bicart}})\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Cat}}(I,\Alg_{\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{Y})),

which is precisely what we needed to show.

Theorem

2.7
is now proved.

3.
Variation: Day convolution in suboperads of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\times}

From the preceding results, one can deduce analogous assertions for certain symmetric monoidal categories which arise as suboperads of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\times}
.
Consider a subcategory
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathrm{Cat}
which is closed under finite products.
If
U_{1}
,
U_{2}
and
T
are objects in
\mathcal{U}
, call a functor

\mathcal{U}
-biexact
if both

and
F(-,u_{2})\colon U_{1}\to T
are morphisms in
\mathcal{U}
for all
u_{1}\in U_{1}
and
u_{2}\in U_{2}
.
There is an evident notion of a
\mathcal{U}
-multiexact
functor for functors in more than two variables.

Assume that

(1)

for each pair
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
of objects in
\mathcal{U}

\mathcal{U}
-biexact functor

;

(2)

there exists a category
U\in\mathcal{U}
and an object

such that evaluation at

induces an equivalence

.

One example of a subcategory satisfying these conditions is the category
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{st}}
of stable categories and exact functors.

Under these assumptions,
\mathcal{U}
refines to a symmetric monoidal category by considering the suboperad
\mathcal{U}^{\otimes}
of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\times}
determined by the following conditions:

(1)

the underlying category of
\mathcal{U}^{\otimes}
is
\mathcal{U}
;

(2)

morphisms

over the active map
\langle n\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle
correspond to
\mathcal{U}
-multiexact functors

.

Observe that
\mathcal{O}
-algebras in
\mathcal{U}^{\otimes}
correspond under unstraightening to cocartesian fibrations of operads over
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
whose fibres lie in
\mathcal{U}
and whose cocartesian transport functors are
\mathcal{U}
-multiexact.

Consider now the suboperad

are given by cartesian fibrations

[1]^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{U}
;

(2)

morphisms are precisely those morphisms in

\mathcal{U}^{\otimes}\times_{\mathrm{Comm}^{\otimes}}\mathcal{U}^{\otimes}
under

As before, given

-algebras

and

in

\mathcal{U}

4.
Day convolution as an
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal category

Fix a base operad
\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
as well as
\mathcal{O}
-monoidal categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
.
In this section, we reprove a well-known statement, see
[
Lur
, Proposition 2.2.6.16]
, which is key for working with the Day convolution operad.
We include a proof to demonstrate that
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
is a feasible description of Day convolution.

For every operation
\varphi\in\mathrm{Mul}_{\mathcal{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i},y)
, denote the associated tensor functors by
\otimes^{\mathcal{C}}_{\varphi}\colon\prod_{i}\mathcal{C}(x_{i})\to\mathcal{C}(y)
and
\otimes^{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}\colon\prod_{i}\mathcal{D}(x_{i})\to\mathcal{D}(y)
.

4.1 Proposition
.

For each
y\in\mathcal{O}
, consider the following collection of slice categories:

\mathcal{K}(y):=\left\{\otimes^{\mathcal{C}}_{\psi}/c\mid\psi\in\mathrm{Mul}_{\mathcal{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i},y),\ c\in\mathcal{C}(y)\right\}

Assume the following is true:

(1)

for all
y\in\mathcal{O}
, the category
\mathcal{D}(y)
admits all
\mathcal{K}(y)
-shaped colimits;

(2)

for every operation
\varphi\in\mathrm{Mul}_{\mathcal{O}}(\{x_{i}\}_{i},y)
and every
j
, the associated tensor functor
\otimes^{\mathcal{D}}_{\varphi}\colon\prod_{i}\mathcal{D}(x_{i})\to\mathcal{D}(y)
preserves all
\mathcal{K}(x_{j})
-shaped colimits in the
j
-th component.

Then
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
is a cocartesian fibration of operads.

4.2 Remark
.

The assumptions of
4.1
are for example satisfied if there exists some regular cardinal
\kappa
with the property that each
\mathcal{D}(y)
is
\kappa
-cocomplete, every tensor functor of
\mathcal{D}
preserves
\kappa
-small colimits in each variable, and each
\mathcal{C}(y)
is
\kappa
-small, reproducing
[
Lur
, Proposition 2.2.6.16]
.

Proof of
4.1
.

By construction,
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}
is an operad, so
[
Lur
, Proposition 2.1.2.12]
shows that we only have to check that
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
is a cocartesian fibration.
As in
[
Lur
, Section 2.2.6]
, the crucial part of the argument lies in identifying mapping anima in
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}

We require some notation.
Let
\pi\colon\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
and

Note that then

Since

4.5 Remark
.

In the situation of
4.1
, assume that
\mathcal{O}
is a symmetric monoidal category, so that
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
correspond to lax symmetric monoidal functors
\mathcal{O}\to\mathrm{Cat}
.
Unwinding the proof of

, one obtains the following description of the lax symmetric monoidal functor
\mathcal{O}\to\mathrm{Cat}
given by the straightening of
\mathrm{Day}_{\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}}^{\otimes}\to\mathcal{O}^{\otimes}
:

(1)

The underlying functor
\mathcal{O}\to\mathrm{Cat}
sends
x\in\mathcal{O}
to
\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x),\mathcal{D}(x))
and a morphism

to the composite

\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x),\mathcal{D}(x))\xrightarrow{f\circ-}\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x),\mathcal{D}(x^{\prime}))\xrightarrow{f_{!}}\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x^{\prime}),\mathcal{D}(x^{\prime})),

where
f_{!}
denotes the left Kan extension functor.

(2)

For
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
, the lax monoidal structure map is given by the composite

\displaystyle\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x),\mathcal{D}(x))\times\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x^{\prime}),\mathcal{D}(x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\to\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x)\times\mathcal{C}(x^{\prime}),\mathcal{D}(x)\times\mathcal{D}(x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}\circ-}\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x)\times\mathcal{C}(x^{\prime}),\mathcal{D}(x\otimes x^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{(\otimes_{\mathcal{C}})_{!}}\Fun(\mathcal{C}(x\otimes x^{\prime}),\mathcal{D}(x\otimes x^{\prime})),

where the first arrow arises from the lax symmetric monoidal structure on
\Fun\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{Cat}\to\mathrm{Cat}
, and
\otimes_{\mathcal{C}}
and
\otimes_{\mathcal{D}}
denote the tensor operations in
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, respectively.

(3)

The structure map associated to the monoidal unit is given by the object in
\Fun(\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}}),\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}}))
which arises as the left Kan extension of
*\xrightarrow{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}}}\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}})
along
*\xrightarrow{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}}\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{O}})
.

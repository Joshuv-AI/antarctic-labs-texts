Transfer Learning for Benign Overfitting in High-Dimensional Linear Regression
Yeichan KimYonsei University kimyeic98@yonsei.ac.kr, Ilmun KimKAIST ilmunk@kaist.ac.kr Seyoung ParkYonsei University ishspsy@yonsei.ac.kr

Abstract
Transfer learning is a key component of modern machine learning, enhancing the performance of target tasks by leveraging diverse data sources. Simultaneously, overparameterized models such as the minimum- ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -norm interpolator (MNI) in high-dimensional linear regression have garnered significant attention for their remarkable generalization capabilities, a property known as benign overfitting . Despite their individual importance, the intersection of transfer learning and MNI remains largely unexplored. Our research bridges this gap by proposing a novel two-step Transfer MNI approach and analyzing its trade-offs. We characterize its non-asymptotic excess risk and identify conditions under which it outperforms the target-only MNI. Our analysis reveals free-lunch covariate shift regimes, where leveraging heterogeneous data yields the benefit of knowledge transfer at limited cost. To operationalize our findings, we develop a data-driven procedure to detect informative sources and introduce an ensemble method incorporating multiple informative Transfer MNIs. Finite-sample experiments demonstrate the robustness of our methods to model and data heterogeneity, confirming their advantage.

Summary of contributions.
Our study bridges the gap by investigating how transfer learning can enhance the generalization of target-only MNI through a novel knowledge transfer scheme. The main contributions are as follows.

•

We propose a novel two-step
Transfer MNI
: pre-train a source-only MNI and then fine-tune it by interpolating target data while “staying close” to the pre-trained model. Under model shift with isotropic covariates, we identify when Transfer MNI outperforms target-only MNI, specify the optimal transfer size, and quantify the maximal improvement in excess risk. We provide non-asymptotic excess risk bounds under both model and covariate shifts when each single-task MNI is benignly overfitted. We further uncover
free-lunch
covariate shifts that alleviate the cost of knowledge transfer while preserving its generalization gain for Transfer MNI.

•

Based on a data-driven procedure for detecting
informative
sources inducing positive transfer, we propose an ensemble method aggregating multiple informative Transfer MNIs with data-adaptive weights determined by source informativeness.

•

Finite-sample experiments demonstrate robustness to distribution shifts and superior performance over transfer baselines, including the pooled-MNI
[
56
]
and SGD-based transfer
[
69
]
, thereby confirming the empirical advantage of our approach under overparameterization.

Task setup.
Consider an overparameterized linear regression setting involving one target task and

is the fixed model coefficient;

is the random noise. Provided

, the MNI trained on each dataset is uniquely given by

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:={\arg\min}_{{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\big\{\|{\bm{\beta}}\|:\mathbf{X}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}=\mathbf{y}^{(q)}\big\}=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{y}^{(q)},\quad q\in[Q]_{0},

Effective ranks.
We additionally introduce the key condition underpinning the benign overfitting of MNI, characterized by the

Definition 1

If

Definition 2

The single-task MNI

3
Single-Source Transfer Task and Out-of-Sample Generalization

We propose a single-source transfer method in the form of
late-fusion

[
56
]
, where the
q
-th source task is learned independently and then integrated with the target task. In the first step, we pre-train the
q
-th source-only MNI
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
. The two-step
Transfer MNI (TM)

\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
then fine-tunes by interpolating the target dataset while minimizing the Euclidean distance from the pre-trained
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
; that is,

\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}:={\arg\min}_{{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigl\{\|{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\|:\mathbf{X}^{(0)}{\bm{\beta}}=\mathbf{y}^{(0)}\bigl\},\quad q\in[Q].

The TM estimate admits the following interpretable decomposition structure that clarifies the knowledge transfer mechanism (see Appendix
C.2
for the derivation):

\displaystyle\underbrace{\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}}_{\text{\shortstack{transfer task}}}=\underbrace{\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}}_{\text{target task}}+\underbrace{\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}}_{\text{\shortstack{late-fusion knowledge transfer}}}.

(3)

Here,
\mathbf{H}^{(q)}:=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto the design row space
\mathcal{S}_{q}:=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(q)}\}_{i=1}^{n_{q}}
for
q\in[Q]_{0}
; conversely,
\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}
is the orthogonal projection onto the null space
\mathcal{S}_{q}^{\perp}
such that
\mathbb{R}^{p}=\mathcal{S}_{q}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{q}^{\perp}
. Denoting by
({\bm{\upsilon}})_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}=\mathbf{H}^{(0)}{\bm{\upsilon}}
and
({\bm{\upsilon}})_{\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\perp}}=(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}){\bm{\upsilon}}
the projections of a vector
{\bm{\upsilon}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
onto the target row and null spaces respectively, we obtain

\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big)_{\mathcal{S}_{0}}=\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)},\qquad\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big)_{\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\perp}}=\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}.

(4)

That is, the fine-tuning step for TM retains target-learned signal in the span of
n_{0}
target samples (i.e.,
\mathcal{S}_{0}
) where benign overfitting ensures high prediction accuracy for the target-only MNI, while transferring source information only into the null space
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\perp}
where the target samples provide no information with
\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}\big)_{\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\perp}}=\mathbf{0}_{p}
.

Given this “retain-plus-transfer” mechanism exhibited by TM, we next analyze its generalization behaviors by comparing the excess risks of target and transfer tasks. Suppose we are given an out-of-sample target instance
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
. The excess risk of an estimate
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}\equiv\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{E})
measured on the target distribution is defined by the following conditional mean squared loss:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x}_{0},\mathcal{E})}\Bigr[\big(\mathbf{x}_{0}^{\top}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}-\mathbf{x}_{0}^{\top}{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)^{2}\bigm|\mathcal{X}\Bigr]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}\Bigr[\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)\bigm|\mathcal{X}\Bigr].

(5)

The canonical bias-variance decomposition shows that the excess risk is the sum of the (squared) bias
\mathcal{B}
and variance
\mathcal{V}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}})=\mathcal{B}(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}})+\mathcal{V}(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}})
. The bias
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
and variance
\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
of the target-only MNI are obtained by
Hastie et al. 2022
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}:={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}:=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)\dagger}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big),

(6)

where we write
{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}:=(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)})
and
\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)}:=(1/n_{q})\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
for
q\in[Q]_{0}
. The following lemma extends the bias-variance decomposition to our proposed TM estimate.

Lemma 1
.

Under the mutual independence and mean-zero condition of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{E})
in Assumption
1
, the excess risk of the TM estimate is the sum of bias
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
and variance
\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}){\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}+{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:=\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\big)}_{~=:~\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}~\textnormal{(variance inflation)}}+~\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}.

Remark 1

(Bias reduction versus variance inflation)
.

The variance inflation
\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}
in Lemma
1
is positive almost surely, so the knowledge transfer always requires a higher variance as its cost. If
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
reduces its estimation bias enough to outweigh the variance inflation, it attains a lower excess risk than the target-only MNI, referred to as positive transfer.

3.1
Model Shift under Isotropic Covariates

We analyze the effect of model shift under the isotropic case where
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}={\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{I}_{p}
. Although not “benign,” this case ensures at least a “harmless”
[
45
]
performance for the single-task MNI, making it a worthwhile subject of investigation. In addition, our analysis provides an important insight: the dynamics between bias reduction and variance inflation (Remark
1
) is determined by the interplay among
p
,
n_{q}
, shift-to-signal ratio (SSR), and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), where SSR and SNR are defined for each
q
-th source as

\mathrm{SSR}_{q}:=\frac{\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}}{\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}}\geq 0,\qquad\mathrm{SNR}_{q}:=\frac{\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}}{\sigma_{q}^{2}}>0,\quad q\in[Q],

provided
{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\neq\mathbf{0}_{p}
.

Theorem 1
.

Under Assumption
1
, and further assuming that
(\mathbf{Z}^{(0)},\mathbf{Z}^{(q)})
have i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries with
p>(n_{0}+1)\vee(n_{q}+1)
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}={\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{I}_{p}
, the expected bias and variance (expectation over
\mathcal{X}
) of the target-only MNI and TM estimate are as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}+\frac{n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}n_{0}}{p-(n_{0}+1)},

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\Big(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big)\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}n_{q}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}}_{=~\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}}~+~\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}.

From Theorem
1
, we observe a trade-off between
p
and
n_{q}
. If
\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|>\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|
, increasing
n_{q}
further reduces the bias of TM. An increase in

mitigates the variance inflation, but only at the expense of compromising this bias reduction effect. Based on Theorem

, we formalize the regime where TM is expected to outperform the target-only MNI and specify the optimal transfer size maximizing the improvement in expected excess risk
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
.

Corollary 1
.

Under the setup in Theorem
1
, the TM estimate satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}<\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}~~\iff~~\mathrm{SSR}_{q}<1~~~and~~~\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})>\frac{p}{p-(n_{q}+1)}.

The improvement in expected excess risk
\Delta(n_{q}):=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
as a function of

is strictly concave on

\mathrm{SSR}_{q}<1

\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})\geq\frac{p(p-1)}{(p-2)^{2}}
, the optimal transfer size
n_{q}^{\ast}
maximizing
\Delta(n_{q})
exists and equals
n_{q}^{\ast}=p-1-\sqrt{\frac{p(p-1)}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}}\in[1,p-1)
, with
\Delta(n_{q}^{\ast})=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\left(\frac{(n_{q}^{\ast})^{2}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}{p(p-1)}\right)\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}>0
being the maximal improvement.

In Corollary
1
, negative transfer occurs when model shift dominates the signal with
\mathrm{SSR}_{q}\geq 1
. The improvement
\Delta(n_{q})
strictly increases in
n_{q}
up to the optimal threshold
n_{q}^{\ast}
but strictly decreases for
n_{q}>n_{q}^{\ast}
; that is, transferring more source samples helps only up to
n_{q}^{\ast}
, and beyond
n_{q}^{\ast}
, it always degrades the efficacy of knowledge transfer. While
Song et al. 2024
also investigated the same isotropic Gaussian setting under model shift, only Corollary
1
provides necessary and sufficient conditions for positive transfer and identifies the maximal improvement
\Delta(n_{q}^{\ast})
.

3.2
Convergence under Benign Covariates

Taking both model and covariate shifts into account, we now consider general sub-Gaussian covariates satisfying Assumption
2
and analyze non-asymptotic excess risk bounds and their implication.

Theorem 2
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. For each
q\in[Q]_{0}
and any
t\geq\log(2)
, define

\displaystyle\psi_{q}(t):=\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})+t}{n_{q}}}+\frac{r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})+t}{n_{q}},\qquad\Upsilon_{q}:=\frac{k^{\ast}_{q}}{n_{q}}+\frac{n_{q}}{R_{k^{\ast}_{q}}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})}.

With probability at least
1-2e^{-\delta}
and
1-4e^{-\eta}
respectively, the biases are bounded above by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\psi_{0}(\delta)\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\|\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\psi_{0}(\eta)\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\|\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}+\psi_{q}(\eta)C_{\scriptscriptstyle{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}}\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}\|\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}.

Furthermore, there exist universal constants
c_{0},c_{q}\geq 1
such that with probability at least
1-7e^{-n_{q}/c_{q}}-2e^{-\xi}
, the variance inflation of the TM estimate is bounded above by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}\lesssim\sigma_{q}^{2}\Upsilon_{q}\psi_{0}(\xi)\big(\lambda_{p}^{(q)}\big)^{-1}\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\|,

and with probability at least
1-10e^{-n_{0}/c_{0}}
, the excess risk of the TM estimate is bounded below by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\gtrsim\sigma_{0}^{2}\Upsilon_{0},

where
\mathcal{V}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}}^{(0)}\asymp\sigma_{0}^{2}\Upsilon_{0}
on the same high-probability event lower-bounding
\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
.

The bias dynamics in Theorem
2
mirrors that in the isotropic case. If
r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)})\ll n_{0}
, which is sufficient for a vanishing target-only bias, and
r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)})\asymp r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})
,
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
also vanishes. Moreover, it can achieve a faster convergence if
\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}\ll\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}
and
n_{0}<n_{q}
. As for variance, the target- and source-only variances are within a constant factor of
\Upsilon_{0}
and
\Upsilon_{q}
respectively (see Corollary
3
in Appendix), and hence the terms vanish when each single-task is benign. The upper bound on
\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}
is a product of the two “benign” terms
\Upsilon_{q}
and
\psi_{0}
and the reciprocal of eigenvalue that reflects the lack of simultaneous diagonalizability in Assumption
1
. While the reciprocal may loosen the bound, our numerical experiments (Section
5
) show rapid decay in variances as
p\gg n_{0}\vee n_{q}
. Exploring a tractable covariance structure to refine this bound is left for future work. Finally, the lower bound on
\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
follows from Lemma
1
, where the TM variance is no smaller than the target-only variance; the bias reduction effect cannot further improve the lower bound.

3.3
Free-Lunch Covariate Shift

In light of the invariance of spectral decay rates to uniform upscaling, we propose a type of covariate shift that yields a lower TM excess risk than without any covariate shift. To proceed, define the following minimal index for
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
satisfying Assumption
1
, which is always well-defined:

\displaystyle\tau^{\ast}:=\min\Big\{k<p:\lambda_{k+1}^{(0)}\asymp\lambda_{p}^{(0)}\Big\}.

(7)

We may expect
\tau^{\ast}\approx k^{\ast}_{0}
for some benign covariance structures, where
k^{\ast}_{0}
is as specified in Assumption
2
for
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
; the beginning of Appendix
C.7
illustrates a case where
\tau^{\ast}\ll p
and
\tau^{\ast}\ll n_{0}
indeed.

Corollary 2

(
Free-lunch
covariate shift)
.

For each
q\in[Q]_{0}
, let the spectral decomposition of
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}
be
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{V}^{(q)}{\bm{\Lambda}}^{(q)}\mathbf{V}^{(q)\top}
, where
\mathbf{V}^{(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is orthogonal and
{\bm{\Lambda}}^{(q)}={\mathrm{Diag}}(\lambda_{1}^{(q)},\ldots,\lambda_{p}^{(q)})
. Suppose
{\bm{\Lambda}}^{(q)}=\alpha{\bm{\Lambda}}^{(0)}
for some
\alpha>1
, and under
(A)
, the leading
\tau^{\ast}
eigenvector pairs in
(\mathbf{V}^{(q)},\mathbf{V}^{(0)})
align; under
(B)
, eigenvector pairs fully align with
\mathbf{V}^{(q)}=\mathbf{V}^{(0)}
, i.e.,
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}=\alpha{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
.

Compared to the homogeneous case
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}={\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
, the following holds for each covariate shift case:

(A)

The upper bound on
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
remains identical up to a constant factor independent of
\alpha
, while the upper bound on
\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}
is multiplied by
\alpha^{-1}
(i.e., reduced by a factor of
\alpha
).

(B)

The exact bias remains identical, while the exact variance inflation is multiplied by
\alpha^{-1}
.

The covariate shift (A) in Corollary
2
shows that as long as each
\lambda_{j}^{(q)}
is uniformly upscaled from
\lambda_{j}^{(0)}
, preserving their decay rates, any misalignment in “noisy” eigendirections between
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
beyond the leading
\tau^{\ast}
high-signal components does not affect the convergence rate of bias, while still promoting faster convergence in variance inflation. With all
p>\tau^{\ast}
eigenvector pairs aligning between
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
, covariate shift (B) offers a greater advantage than (A) by specifying the exact impact on the bias and variance inflation, rather than on their upper bounds.

Remark 2

(Relaxed
free-lunch
condition)
.

The
\tau^{\ast}
-alignment condition for
(A)
in Corollary
2
can be relaxed: even if not all of leading
\tau^{\ast}
source eigenvectors align with the target counterparts, we can still achieve the same free-lunch effect as in
(A)
whenever

\|\mathbf{V}_{\tau^{\ast}}^{(0)}-\mathbf{V}_{\tau^{\ast}}^{(q)}\|\lesssim\lambda_{\tau^{\ast}+1}^{(0)}/\lambda_{1}^{(0)},

where
\mathbf{V}_{\tau^{\ast}}^{(0)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times\tau^{\ast}}
(resp.
\mathbf{V}_{\tau^{\ast}}^{(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times\tau^{\ast}}
) comprises the leading
\tau^{\ast}
eigenvectors in
\mathbf{V}^{(0)}
(resp.
\mathbf{V}^{(q)}
) as specified in Corollary
2
.

4
Informative Multi-Source Transfer Task

In this section, we propose a transfer task that incorporates multiple sources identified as
informative
, those that induce positive transfer. The index set of informative sources is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}:=\Big\{q\in[Q]:\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}<0\Big\},

(8)

which, however, is unknown in practice. Hence, it is of crucial interest in transfer learning to develop a data-driven procedure for detecting informative sources. Inspired by
Tian and Feng 2023
, we utilize the
K
-fold cross-validation (CV)
[
1
]
to detect source transferability by comparing a “proxy” of excess risks.

First, partition the target dataset into
K
folds of equal size, each denoted by
(\mathbf{X}^{(0)[k]},\mathbf{y}^{(0)[k]})
for
k\in[K]
; a common choice suggests
K=5

[
17
]
. At each training step, we use the left-out folds
(\mathbf{X}^{(0)[-k]},\mathbf{y}^{(0)[-k]}):=\{(\mathbf{X}^{(0)[k]},\mathbf{y}^{(0)[k]})\}_{k=1}^{K}\setminus(\mathbf{X}^{(0)[k]},\mathbf{y}^{(0)[k]})
to train an estimate
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}^{[-k]}
and then evaluate the squared loss on the
k
-th fold given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{[k]}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}^{[-k]}\big):=\frac{1}{n_{0}/K}\big\|\mathbf{y}^{(0)[k]}-\mathbf{X}^{(0)[k]}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}^{[-k]}\big\|^{2}.

We repeat this across all
K
folds to obtain the terminal CV loss
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}\big):=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}^{[k]}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}^{[-k]}\big)/K
, which estimates the prediction risk
\mathcal{R}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}\big)+\sigma_{0}^{2}
. We then estimate the oracle set (
8
) by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}:=\Big\{q\in[Q]:\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big)-\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}\big)\leq D^{(0)}\Big\},

(9)

with some detection threshold
D^{(0)}>0
to be specified later that depends on the target CV loss.

If
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
is non-empty, we train the TM estimate
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(i)}
for each
i\in\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
and form a weighted linear combination of
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(i)}
. Each weight
w_{i}
is initialized by the inverse of the CV loss
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(i)}\big)
and then normalized so that
\sum_{i\in\widehat{\mathcal{I}}}w_{i}=1
, which serves as a data-adaptive measure of source informativeness. This allows us to leverage
|\widehat{\mathcal{I}}|
sources for knowledge transfer, which we name
Informative-Weighted Transfer MNI (WTM)
. The entire procedure, from detecting informative sources to computing the WTM estimate, is outlined in Algorithm
1
(Appendix
D
), specifying
D^{(0)}
in the set (
9
).

6
Conclusion and Future Work

We propose a novel two-step Transfer MNI and a data-adaptive ensemble aggregating multiple
informative
Transfer MNIs. These methods address the posed
question
highlighting the underexplored nature of transfer learning for
MNIs, supported by both theoretical characterizations and numerical validations under various distribution shift conditions. We further identify
free-lunch
covariate shift regimes for Transfer MNI where the trade-off between bias reduction and variance inflation is neutralized, allowing us to “cherry-pick” the benefit of knowledge transfer at limited cost.

Consistency of informative source detection.
Beyond the scope of this paper, we aim to provide a theoretical foundation for the empirical success of the WTM estimate prominently demonstrated throughout our experiments. Specifically, we aim to establish the consistency of informative source detection via cross-validation in Algorithm
1
by proving that the event
\mathcal{I}=\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
holds with high probability, where
\mathcal{I}
is the oracle set in (
8
) and
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}
is its CV-driven estimate in (
9
). This agenda connects to the literature on source transferability detection, a pivotal aspect of transfer learning research. While classification settings are well studied
[
3
,
47
,
58
,
22
]
, less is known for regression. Recently,
Nguyen et al. 2023
introduced linear MSE as a regression transferability metric, and
Tian and Feng 2023
established cross-validation consistency for transfer generalized linear models. However, both analyses rely on explicit regularization, whereas our setting features benign overfitting with implicit regularization. Establishing consistency of transferability detection in this regime remains open and would meaningfully advance the relevant literature.

Extension to minimum-RKHS-norm interpolator.
Another promising direction is to analyze the extension of Transfer MNI from finite-dimensional linear regression to nonlinear, infinite-dimensional regression in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS)
[
53
]
via the minimum-RKHS-norm interpolator, including both fixed kernels and network-induced kernels such as the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK)
[
23
]
. A rich literature has explored benign overfitting in single-task cases; see Appendix
A
for a detailed review. However, to the best of our knowledge, transfer learning for the minimum-RKHS-norm interpolator beyond OOD settings still remains underexplored.

For the RKHS
\mathcal{H}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{K}
associated with a positive semi-definite kernel
K:\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, let
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
and
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
be the minimum-RKHS-norm interpolators trained on the target and
q
-th source datasets, respectively; their analytical forms with empirical kernel (Gram) matrices
\mathbf{K}^{(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}\times n_{q}}
for
q\in[Q]_{0}
are well-known, where the
(i,j)
-th entry is
\mathbf{K}_{ij}^{(q)}=K(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(q)},\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(q)})
. This naturally motivates the RKHS analogue of Transfer MNI:

\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}:={\arg\min}_{f\in\mathcal{H}}\Big\{\big\|f-\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}:f\big(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(0)}\big)=y_{i}^{(0)},~\forall i\in[n_{0}]\Big\},\quad q\in[Q],

i.e.,
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
interpolates the target dataset
\{(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(0)},y_{i}^{(0)})\}_{i=1}^{n_{0}}
while minimizing the RKHS-norm distance from the pre-trained
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
. With the target evaluation operator
E_{0}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}
and its adjoint
E_{0}^{\ast}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{0}}\to\mathcal{H}
such that
(E_{0}f)_{i}=f(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(0)})
,
E_{0}E_{0}^{\ast}=\mathbf{K}^{(0)}
, and
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}=E_{0}^{\ast}\mathbf{K}^{(0)\dagger}\mathbf{y}^{(0)}
, the projection operator
P_{0}
onto the target-induced span
\mathcal{S}_{0,\mathcal{H}}:=\mathrm{span}\{K(\cdot,\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(0)})\}_{i=1}^{n_{0}}
is given by
P_{0}:=E_{0}^{\ast}\mathbf{K}^{(0)\dagger}E_{0}
. The operator acts as the RKHS counterpart to the projection matrix
\mathbf{H}^{(0)}
for linear regression in that
P_{0}^{\ast}=P_{0}
and
P_{0}^{2}=P_{0}
(i.e., self-adjoint and idempotent). Hence, the analytical form of Transfer MNI in Equation (
3
) systematically extends to provide the unique closed form of
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
:

\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}=\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}+(I_{\mathcal{H}}-P_{0})\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)},

where
I_{\mathcal{H}}
is the identity operator. Indeed, the RKHS TM estimate
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
fully inherits the “retain-plus-transfer” mechanism in (
4
): it matches
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
on
\mathcal{S}_{0,\mathcal{H}}
and the fine-tuning step transfers knowledge learned by
\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
only into the complement space, i.e.,
(I_{\mathcal{H}}-P_{0})\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}=(I_{\mathcal{H}}-P_{0})\hat{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
.

Given the above formulation, it arises as an important direction to analyze the generalization gains of RKHS Transfer MNI with respect to factors that govern benign overfitting in an RKHS-such as the ground truth function class, kernel type, ambient input dimension, and sample size-as well as distribution shifts in the ground truth function and covariates between target and source tasks. We anticipate that our proposed scheme can serve as a versatile baseline for further studies on transfer learning with
implicitly regularized
minimum-norm interpolators that span a broad class of norms and, on a separate axis, extend beyond linear models, encompassing fixed RKHS kernels, NTK-type network-induced kernels, and data-adaptive kernels from deep representation learning.

Lemma 2

(Expectation of quadratic forms)
.

Let
\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a random vector with mean
\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]
and covariance
{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big)
, and
\mathbf{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
be a deterministic (or conditioned) symmetric matrix. The expectation of the quadratic form
\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}
is given by

\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]^{\top}\mathbf{M}\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]+{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\mathbf{M}{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big)\Big).

Proof.

Since trace is invariant to cyclic permutation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[{\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\top}\mathbf{M}\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\mathbf{M}\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}^{\top}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{M}\Big({\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big)+\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]^{\top}\Big)\right)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{M}{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big)\right)+{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{M}\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathbf{M}{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big)\right)+\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big]^{\top}\mathbf{M}\mathbb{E}\big[\hat{{\bm{\theta}}}\big].

∎

The following lemma is an adjustment of Lemma 3 in

and Proposition 3 and Lemma 16 in

Lemma 3

(Standard Gaussian orthogonal projection)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}\times p}
have i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries with
p>n_{q}+1
. Since the matrix has full row rank almost surely, the orthogonal projection onto the
n_{q}
-dimensional row space of
\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
is given by

\mathbf{H}^{(q)}=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{(q)},

and
\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}
is the orthogonal projection onto the
(p-n_{q})
-dimensional null space, with expectations

\mathbb{E}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}=\frac{n_{q}}{p}\mathbf{I}_{p},\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big]=\Big(1-\frac{n_{q}}{p}\Big)\mathbf{I}_{p}.

Thus, for any fixed vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big\|\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\mathbf{a}\big\|^{2}=\frac{n_{q}}{p}\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|^{2},\qquad\mathbb{E}\big\|\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\mathbf{a}\big\|^{2}=\Big(1-\frac{n_{q}}{p}\Big)\left\|\mathbf{a}\right\|^{2}.

The following lemma can be generalized to a broad class of norms, with our focus on the norms of a real matrix.

Lemma 4

(Hölder’s inequality for Schatten
p
-norm)
.

Given two real matrices
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, Hölder’s inequality yields

\Big|{\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{B}\big)\Big|\leq\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{p}\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|_{q},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{p}
is the Schatten p-norm of
\mathbf{A}
defined as

\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{p}:=\Big({\mathrm{Tr}}\big(\left|\mathbf{A}\right|^{p}\big)\Big)^{1/p}:\qquad\left|\mathbf{A}\right|:=\big(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\big)^{1/2}.

The above inequality also holds when
p=1
and
q=\infty
, where the infinity norm corresponds to the operator norm, i.e,

\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|.

In particular, if both
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
are symmetric and positive semi-definite, we have

{\mathrm{Tr}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B})\leq{\mathrm{Tr}}(\mathbf{A})\left\|\mathbf{B}\right\|.

Lemma 5

(Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be an invertible matrix and
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}
be a matrix with
k<n
such that
\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}+\mathbf{A}
is invertible. By the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}+\mathbf{A}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{A}^{-1}-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}\big(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{Z}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}\big(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}\big)^{-1}

and

\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}+\mathbf{A}\big)^{-2}\mathbf{Z}=\big(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-2}\mathbf{Z}\big(\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{Z}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{Z}\big)^{-1}.

\displaystyle\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}+a\nu_{x}^{2}\max\left(\lambda_{\ell+1}^{2}t,~\sqrt{tn_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}+a\nu_{x}^{2}\max\left(t\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2},~\sqrt{tn_{0}}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{5}n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}

for some sufficiently large
c_{5}
. Combining the above results yields

\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}\leq c_{6}n_{0}\frac{\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{(\lambda_{k+1}r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}}))^{2}}.

Thus, putting both summations together and taking
c>\max(c_{0},c_{4},c_{6})
complete the proof.
∎

B.2.2
Lower Bound on Single-Task Variance

To establish a lower bound on the trace term in Equation (
12
), we first present a preliminary result that extends a lower bound on individual random variables, each holding with equal probability, to a unified lower bound on their entire sum.

Lemma 11

(Lemma 9,
Bartlett et al. 2020
)
.

Suppose
p\leq\infty
. Let
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{p}
be a sequence of non-negative random variables and
\{t_{j}\}_{j=1}^{p}
be a sequence of non-negative real numbers (at least one of which is strictly positive) such that for some
\delta\in(0,1)
and any
j\in[p]
,
\mathbb{P}(\eta_{j}>t_{j})\geq 1-\delta
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\eta_{j}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{p}t_{j}\right)\geq 1-2\delta.

Next lemma provides the lower bound by first establishing a general bound that holds regardless of
r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})
and then refining it for the case where
r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})
is at least
n_{0}
, using Lemma
11
.

Lemma 12

(Lemma 10,
Bartlett et al. 2020
)
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. There exist universal constants
b,c\geq 1
such that if

0\leq k\leq n_{0}/c,\quad r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\geq bn_{0},

then with probability at least
1-10e^{-n_{0}/c}
,

\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{\dagger}{\bm{\Sigma}}\right)~\geq~\frac{1}{cb^{2}}~\sigma_{0}^{2}\min_{\ell\leq k}\left(\frac{\ell}{n_{0}}+\frac{b^{2}n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k+1}r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Fix
j\geq 1
and
k\in[0,n_{0}/C]
. By Lemma
7
, there exists a universal constant
C_{1}
such that with probability at least
1-2e^{-n_{0}/C_{1}}
,

\mu_{k+1}(\mathbf{A}_{-j})\leq C_{1}\left(\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}\right).

Let
\mathcal{G}_{j}
be the span of the bottom
(n_{0}-k)
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{A}_{-j}
and
{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathcal{G}_{j}}
be the projection onto the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{G}_{j}
. Then, we have

\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j}~\geq~({\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathcal{G}_{j}}\mathbf{z}_{j})^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}({\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathcal{G}_{j}}\mathbf{z}_{j})~\geq~\mu_{k+1}(\mathbf{A}_{-j})^{-1}\|{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathcal{G}_{j}}\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}~\geq~\frac{\|{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathcal{G}_{j}}\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}}{C_{1}\left(\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}\right)}.

By Corollary
8
, with probability at least
1-3e^{-t}
,

\|{\bm{\Pi}}_{\mathcal{G}_{j}}\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}\geq n_{0}/C_{2},

provided that
t\leq n_{0}/C_{0}
for some sufficiently large
C_{0}<C
. Thus, with probability at least
1-5e^{-n_{0}/C_{3}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{0}}{C_{3}\left(\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}\right)}

\displaystyle\implies 1+\lambda_{j}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{C_{3}\left(\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}\right)}{n_{0}\lambda_{j}}\right)\lambda_{j}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j},

and taking the squared reciprocals of both sides and then multiplying them by
\lambda_{j}^{2}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}
yield

\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}}{(1+\lambda_{j}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j})^{2}}~\geq~\left(1+\frac{C_{3}\left(\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}\right)}{n_{0}\lambda_{j}}\right)^{-2}\frac{\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}}{(\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j})^{2}}.

Also, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the same high-probability event from Lemma
7
, we have

\frac{\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}}{(\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j})^{2}}~\geq~\frac{\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}}{\|\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}}~=~\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{z}_{j}\|^{2}}~\geq~\frac{1}{C_{4}n_{0}}.

Thus, taking
C
sufficiently large, for any
j\geq 1
and
k\in[0,n_{0}/C]
, with probability at least
1-5e^{-n/C}
,

\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}}{(1+\lambda_{j}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j})^{2}}~\geq~\frac{1}{Cn_{0}}\left(1+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}}{n_{0}\lambda_{j}}\right)^{-2},

which establishes a general lower bound. We refine it by establishing a lower bound on the summation over
j\in[p]
as follows: by Lemma
11
, with probability at least
1-10e^{-n_{0}/c_{1}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\mathbf{X}^{\top}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\top})^{-2}\mathbf{X}{\bm{\Sigma}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-2}\mathbf{z}_{j}}{(1+\lambda_{j}\mathbf{z}_{j}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{-j}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_{j})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c_{1}n_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\left(1+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}+n_{0}\lambda_{k+1}}{n_{0}\lambda_{j}}\right)^{-2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c_{2}n_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\min\left(1,~\frac{n_{0}^{2}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{(\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i})^{2}},~\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\lambda_{k+1}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c_{2}b^{2}n_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\min\left(1,~\left(\frac{bn_{0}}{r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})}\right)^{2}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\lambda_{k+1}^{2}},~\frac{\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\lambda_{k+1}^{2}}\right),

where
b\geq 1
is as specified in Lemma
7
. Thus, if
r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\geq bn_{0}
, then

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\mathbf{X}^{\top}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{X}^{\top})^{-2}\mathbf{X}{\bm{\Sigma}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{c_{2}b^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\min\left(\frac{1}{n_{0}},~\frac{b^{2}n_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{(\lambda_{k+1}r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}}))^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{2}b^{2}}~\min_{\ell\leq k}\left(\frac{\ell}{n_{0}}+\frac{b^{2}n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k+1}r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right),

where the equality follows from the fact that
\lambda_{j}
are non-increasing. Taking
c>\max(c_{1},c_{2})
completes the proof.
∎

B.2.3
Matching Upper and Lower Bounds

Combining Lemmas
10
and
12
, if
r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\geq bn_{0}
for some
k\leq n/c
, the variance
\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
is within a constant factor of

\sigma_{0}^{2}\min_{\ell\leq k}\left(\frac{\ell}{n_{0}}+\frac{n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k+1}r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right).

Choosing
k=k_{0}^{\ast}
, which is the smallest one among all qualifying
k
’s, simplifies the bound as follows.

Lemma 13

(Lemma 11,
Bartlett et al. 2020
)
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. For any
b\geq 1
, if the minimum

k^{\ast}_{0}=\min\Big\{k\geq 0:r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\geq bn_{0}\Big\}

is well-defined, then

\displaystyle\min_{\ell\leq k^{\ast}_{0}}\left(\frac{\ell}{bn_{0}}+\frac{bn_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right)=\frac{k^{\ast}_{0}}{bn_{0}}+\frac{bn_{0}\sum_{j>k^{\ast}_{0}}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}=\frac{k^{\ast}_{0}}{bn_{0}}+\frac{bn_{0}}{R_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{bn_{0}}+bn_{0}\frac{\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{bn_{0}}+\sum_{j>\ell}\frac{bn_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k^{\ast}_{0}}\min\left(\frac{1}{bn_{0}},~\frac{bn_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right)+\sum_{j>k^{\ast}_{0}}\frac{bn_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell^{\ast}}\frac{1}{bn_{0}}+\sum_{j>\ell^{\ast}}\frac{bn_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}},

where
\ell^{\ast}
denotes the largest value of
j\leq k^{\ast}_{0}
for which

\frac{1}{bn_{0}}\leq\frac{bn_{0}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}},

since
\lambda_{j}^{2}
are non-increasing. This condition holds if and only if

\lambda_{j}\geq\frac{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})}{bn_{0}}.

By the definition of
k^{\ast}_{0}
, we have
r_{k^{\ast}_{0}-1}({\bm{\Sigma}})<bn_{0}
, which implies

r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})=\frac{\sum_{j>k^{\ast}_{0}}\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}}=\frac{\sum_{j>k^{\ast}_{0}-1}\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}}}{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}}=\frac{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}}}{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}}\big(r_{k^{\ast}_{0}-1}({\bm{\Sigma}})-1\big)<\frac{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}}}{\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}}(bn_{0}-1),

so the minimizing
\ell
is
k^{\ast}_{0}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
13
, we can obtain the upper and lower bounds that match up to a constant factor in the desired form that aligns with the formal definition of benign overfitting in Definition
2
.

Corollary 3
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold; that is, there exist universal constants
b_{0},c_{0}\geq 1
such that the minimal index

k^{\ast}_{0}=\min\Big\{k\geq 0:r_{k}({\bm{\Sigma}})\geq b_{0}n_{0}\Big\}

is well-defined with
0\leq k^{\ast}_{0}\leq n_{0}/c_{0}
. Then, with probability at least
1-7e^{-n_{0}/c_{0}}
,

\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{\dagger}{\bm{\Sigma}}\right)\leq c_{0}\sigma_{0}^{2}\min_{\ell\leq k^{\ast}_{0}}\left(\frac{\ell}{n_{0}}+\frac{n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right)\asymp c_{0}\sigma_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{k^{\ast}_{0}}{n_{0}}+\frac{n_{0}}{R_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})}\right),

and with probability at least
1-10e^{-n_{0}/c_{0}}
,

\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{\dagger}{\bm{\Sigma}}\right)\geq\frac{1}{c_{0}b_{0}^{2}}~\sigma_{0}^{2}\min_{\ell\leq k^{\ast}_{0}}\left(\frac{\ell}{n_{0}}+\frac{b_{0}^{2}n_{0}\sum_{j>\ell}\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\big(\lambda_{k^{\ast}_{0}+1}r_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})\big)^{2}}\right)\asymp\frac{1}{c_{0}}\sigma_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{k^{\ast}_{0}}{n_{0}}+\frac{n_{0}}{R_{k^{\ast}_{0}}({\bm{\Sigma}})}\right).

Appendix C
Proofs

Throughout our proofs, we assume without loss of generality as in Section
B
that each design
\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{q}\times p}
is of full row rank, i.e.,
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{X}^{(q)})=n_{q}<p
, so that
\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}
is invertible. Thus, we write the matrices
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}
and
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}
interchangeably.

C.1
Proof of Equation
2

Proof.

The identity in Equation (
2
) is well-known; we provide a proof here for completeness. Recall that each single-task MNI is given by
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{y}^{(q)}
.Given an arbitrary interpolator
\tilde{\bm{\beta}}
trained on the same dataset
(\mathbf{X}^{(q)},\mathbf{y}^{(q)})
, we have

\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)=\mathbf{y}^{(q)}-\mathbf{y}^{(q)}=\mathbf{0}_{n_{q}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\big(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)^{\top}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\big(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)^{\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{y}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)\Big)^{\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{y}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=0,

i.e.,
(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)})
and
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
are orthogonal. Thus,

\bigl\|\tilde{\bm{\beta}}\bigl\|=\bigl\|\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}+\big(\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)\bigl\|=\bigl\|\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\bigl\|+\bigl\|\tilde{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\bigl\|~\geq~\bigl\|\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\bigl\|,

and the equality holds if and only if
\tilde{\bm{\beta}}=\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
; that is, the MNI has the minimum
\ell_{2}
-norm among all possible interpolators and is uniquely determined. This completes the proof.
∎

C.2
Proof of Equation
3

Proof.

We present a detailed derivation of the TM estimate in Equation (
3
). First, we may write

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle:={\arg\min}_{{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigl\{\|{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\|:\mathbf{X}^{(0)}{\bm{\beta}}=\mathbf{y}^{(0)}\bigl\}

\displaystyle={\arg\min}_{{\bm{\beta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\bigl\{\|{\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\|:\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\big({\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\bigr)=\mathbf{y}^{(0)}-\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\bigl\}

\displaystyle={\arg\min}_{{\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\big\{\|{\bm{\theta}}\|:\mathbf{X}^{(0)}{\bm{\theta}}=\mathbf{y}^{(0)}-\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\bigl\}.

Here, we have
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\beta}}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
and the minimizer
{\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}
of
{\bm{\theta}}
is given by
{\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}={\bm{\beta}}^{\ast}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
, where
{\bm{\beta}}^{\ast}=\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
is the minimizer of
{\bm{\beta}}
. By the definition of MNI in Equation (
2
), the minimizer
{\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}
is given by

{\bm{\theta}}^{\ast}=\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\big(\mathbf{y}^{(0)}-\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\big(\mathbf{y}^{(0)}-\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)

\displaystyle\implies\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}+\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}+\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)},

where
\mathbf{H}^{(0)}=\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{X}^{(0)}
. This completes the proof.
∎

C.3
Proof of Lemma
1

Proof.

By Equation (
5
), the excess risk of the TM estimate is given by

\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}\Bigr[\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)\bigm|\mathcal{X}\Bigr],

where
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{E})
are as specified in Assumption
1
. For simplicity, we write

\mathbb{E}[\cdot]\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{E}}[~\cdot\mid\mathcal{X}],\qquad{\mathrm{Cov}}(\cdot)\equiv{\mathrm{Cov}}_{\mathcal{E}}(~\cdot\mid\mathcal{X}),

so that we always take expectations over the randomness of noises
\mathcal{E}
conditional on the designs
\mathcal{X}
, unless specified otherwise. Lemma
2
gives

\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}=\underbrace{\Big(\mathbb{E}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\Big)^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\Big(\mathbb{E}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\Big)}_{\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}}+\underbrace{{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big)\Big)}_{\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}},

i.e., the excess risk decomposes into a sum of bias and variance.

Bias of TM.
Recall from Equation (
3
) that

\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}=\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}+\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)},

where
\mathbf{H}^{(q)}=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
is the orthogonal projection onto the row space of
\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
. Here, the expectation of the single-task MNI
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
is given by

\mathbb{E}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{X}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)}+{\bm{\epsilon}}^{(q)}\big]=\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)},\quad q\in[Q]_{0},

since
{\bm{\epsilon}}^{(q)}
is mean-zero by Assumption
1
. Thus, the following expectation holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{(0)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}+\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{E}\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)})\big(\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big).

Denoting
{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}=\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)
where the orthogonal projections
\mathbf{H}^{(q)}
and
\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}
are symmetric, we have

\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}=(\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)})^{\top}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}(\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}),\quad q\neq 0,

where

\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\beta}}^{(q)}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}=\mathbf{H}^{(q)}({\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}+{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)})-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}=\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}-\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}+{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},

which completes the derivation of
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
in Lemma
1
.

Variance of TM.
As for the variance
\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
, we may write

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Cov}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}+\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\Big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}\big)+\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big),

where the second equation holds since
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
and
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
are independent. Here, the covariance of the single-task MNI
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Cov}}\Big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{y}^{(q)}\Big)

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Cov}}\Big(\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{y}^{(q)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big)^{\dagger}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}{\mathrm{Cov}}\big({\bm{\epsilon}}^{(q)}\big)\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big)^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{q}^{2}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\big)^{\dagger},\quad q\in[Q]_{0},

where the last equality holds since
{\mathrm{Cov}}\big({\bm{\epsilon}}^{(q)}\big)=\sigma_{q}^{2}\mathbf{I}_{n_{q}}
by Assumption
1
and
\mathbf{M}^{\dagger}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\dagger}=\mathbf{M}^{\dagger}
for any
\mathbf{M}
by the definition of Moore-Penrose inverse. Denoting
\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)}=(1/n_{q})\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
, we have

{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(q)}\big)=\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger},\quad q\in[Q]_{0},

which yields

{\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big)=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)\dagger}+\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big).

Plugging this into
\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle={\mathrm{Tr}}\Big({\mathrm{Cov}}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)\dagger}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\Big)+\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)\dagger}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\Big)+\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\Big).

This completes the derivation of
\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
in Lemma
1
.
∎

C.4
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

Since we assume i.i.d. standard Gaussian design for
(\mathbf{X}^{(0)},\mathbf{X}^{(q)})
in Theorem
1
, we have
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}={\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{I}_{p}
and
{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}=(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)})=\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}
for idempotent
\mathbf{H}^{(0)}
.

Bias of TM.
Under the standard Gaussianity assumption, the bias of the target and transfer tasks, which are provided in Equation (
6
) and Lemma
1
respectively, are reduced to as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}+{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}.

First, by Lemma
3
, the expected bias of target task is derived as

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(0)}}\big\|\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2}=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\big\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2}.

Next, to derive the expected bias risk of the transfer task, we again use Lemma
3
. The expectation of the first term in
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
is obtained as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{X}^{(0)},\mathbf{X}^{(q)})}\bigg[{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)})(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)})(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(0)}}\Big[{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)})(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)})(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}~\big|~\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\Big]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}\big\|\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\left(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\right)\big\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2},\quad q\neq 0,

where we use the law of total expectation in the second line and the equality

\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(0)}}\big[\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big]=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\mathbf{I}_{p}

given by Lemma
3
to obtain the third line. Applying the law of total expectation in the same way, we derive the expectation of the second term in
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{X}^{(0)},\mathbf{X}^{(q)})}\bigg[{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}\big\|\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\frac{n_{q}}{p}\big\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2},\quad q\neq 0.

Lastly, the expectation of the third term in
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
vanishes, since

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{X}^{(0)},\mathbf{X}^{(q)})}\bigg[-2{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=-2{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(0)}}\Big[\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)~\big|~\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\Big]{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-2\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right){\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}\Big[\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\Big]{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},

and
\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)=\mathbf{0}_{p\times p}
almost surely as
\mathbf{H}^{(q)}
is idempotent. Combining the results, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\left(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\right)\big\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2}+\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\frac{n_{q}}{p}\big\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\left(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\big\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2}+\frac{n_{q}}{p}\big\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2}\right),

which completes the derivation of the expected bias in Theorem
1
. Note that Lemma
3
requires
p>n_{0}+1
and
p>n_{q}+1
, which is assumed in Theorem
1
.

Variance of TM.
Under the standard Gaussianity assumption, we can simplify the variances in Equation (
6
) and Lemma
1
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{n_{0}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)\dagger}\Big)=\sigma_{0}^{2}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\big(\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big)^{-1}\Big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\Big)}_{\textnormal{variance inflation}~=~\mathcal{V}_{\uparrow}^{(q)}}+\ \mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)},

since, for each
q\in[Q]_{0}
, we have

\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}=\left(\frac{\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}{n_{q}}\right)^{\dagger}=n_{q}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\dagger}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{\dagger}=n_{q}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-2}\mathbf{X}^{(q)},

and trace is invariant to cyclic permutation. As we assume
p>(n_{0}+1)\vee(n_{q}+1)
in Theorem
1
, we may use the result from Section 2.2 in
Belkin et al. 2020
: for each
q\in[Q]_{0}
,
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}
follows inverse-Wishart distribution with scale matrix
\mathbf{I}_{n_{q}}
and
p
degrees-of-freedom. This implies that each diagonal entry of
\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}

\Big[\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\Big]_{ii},\quad i\in[n_{q}],

has a reciprocal that follows the
\chi^{2}
-distribution with
p-(n_{q}-1)
degrees-of-freedom. Therefore, we have

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\Big]_{ii}=\frac{1}{p-(n_{q}+1)},\quad i\in[n_{q}],

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}=\sigma_{0}^{2}\mathbb{E}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\big(\mathbf{X}^{(0)}\mathbf{X}^{(0)\top}\big)^{-1}\Big)=\sigma_{0}^{2}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{0}}\Big[\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\Big]_{ii}=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}n_{0}}{p-(n_{0}+1)}.

Now it remains to derive the expectation of variance inflation. The law of total expectation gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{X}^{(0)},\mathbf{X}^{(q)})}\bigg[\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}\ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(0)}}\Big[\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)\dagger}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\bigm|\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\Big]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{n_{q}}\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{X}^{(q)}}{\mathrm{Tr}}\Big(n_{q}(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top})^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{q}^{2}\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\frac{n_{q}}{p-(n_{q}+1)},

which completes the derivation of the expected variance in Theorem
1
.
∎

C.5
Proof of Corollary
1

Proof.

From Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}n_{0}}{p-(n_{0}+1)},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}+\frac{n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big)\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}n_{q}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}+\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}n_{0}}{p-(n_{0}+1)}.

Thus, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}<\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\iff\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}+\frac{n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big)\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}n_{q}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}<\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff\frac{n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}+\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}n_{q}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}<\frac{n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\iff\frac{1}{p}\left(\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}\right)>\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}.

(13)

Using the definitions
\mathrm{SNR}_{q}=\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}/\sigma_{q}^{2}>0
and
\mathrm{SSR}_{q}=\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}/\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}\geq 0
, we may write

\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}=\sigma_{q}^{2}~\mathrm{SNR}_{q},\qquad\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}=\sigma_{q}^{2}~\mathrm{SNR}_{q}~\mathrm{SSR}_{q},

and therefore, inequality (
13
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{SNR}_{q}\left(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q}\right)>\frac{p}{p-(n_{q}+1)}.

(14)

From inequality (
14
), observe that its right-hand side is always positive, which makes
\mathrm{SSR}_{q}<1
a necessary condition for
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}<\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
. Also, the right-hand side minimized with respect to
n_{q}\in[1,p-1)
is
p/(p-2)
, which makes
\mathrm{SNR}_{q}\left(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q}\right)>p/(p-2)
another necessary condition.

Provided the condition
\mathrm{SSR}_{q}<1
, define the following function of
n_{q}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}(n_{q})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big(\frac{p-n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}+\frac{n_{q}}{p}\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\Big)\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}n_{q}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}-\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\left\{\frac{n_{q}}{p}\left(\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\|^{2}-\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}\right)+n_{q}\frac{\sigma_{q}^{2}}{p-(n_{q}+1)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}}_{=:~B^{(0)}}n_{q}\left(\frac{\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)}\right),

where we write
B^{(0)}=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}>0
and define
\Delta(n_{q}):=-\widetilde{\Delta}(n_{q})
as in Corollary
1
. Holding all quantities other than
n_{q}
constant and differentiating
\widetilde{\Delta}
with respect to
n_{q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\widetilde{\Delta}}{\partial n_{q}}

\displaystyle=B^{(0)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)}\right)+B^{(0)}n_{q}\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=B^{(0)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)}+\frac{n_{q}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}}\right).

For the first-order condition
\frac{\partial\widetilde{\Delta}}{\partial n_{q}}=0
, multiplying both sides by
p\cdot\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}/B^{(0)}>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1)\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)^{2}+p(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)+pn_{q}^{\ast}=0

\displaystyle\implies(\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1)\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)^{2}+p(p-1)=0

\displaystyle\implies p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1=\sqrt{\frac{p(p-1)}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}}>0

\displaystyle\implies n_{q}^{\ast}=p-1-\sqrt{\frac{p(p-1)}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}}.

Here, since
\mathrm{SSR}_{q}<1
and
\mathrm{SNR}_{q}>0
,
n_{q}^{\ast}
is strictly less than
p-1
. Furthermore, we have
n_{q}^{\ast}\geq 1
if and only if

\displaystyle(p-2)^{2}\geq\frac{p(p-1)}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}\iff\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})\geq\frac{p(p-1)}{(p-2)^{2}}.

To check the second-order condition at
n_{q}^{\ast}
, consider the second derivative of
\widetilde{\Delta}
given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\Delta}}{\partial n_{q}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\widetilde{\Delta}}{\partial n_{q}}\bigg[B^{(0)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)}+\frac{n_{q}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}}\right)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{B^{(0)}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}}\left(\frac{1}{(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}}+\frac{(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}+n_{q}\cdot 2(p-n_{q}-1)}{(p-n_{q}-1)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{B^{(0)}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}}\left(\frac{2(p-n_{q}-1)^{2}+2n_{q}(p-n_{q}-1)}{(p-n_{q}-1)^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2B^{(0)}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}}\left(\frac{p-1}{(p-n_{q}-1)^{3}}\right)>0,\quad\forall n_{q}\in[1,p-1).

That is,
\widetilde{\Delta}(n_{q})
is strictly convex on the interval
[1,p-1)
; conversely,
\Delta(n_{q})=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}
is strictly concave on the same interval, and thus the improvement in expected excess risk
\Delta(n_{q})
strictly increases on
n_{q}\in[1,n_{q}^{\ast}]
and strictly decreases on
n_{q}\in(n_{q}^{\ast},p-1)
.

Now it remains to show that
\Delta(n_{q}^{\ast})>0
. Recall from the first-order condition that we have

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\widetilde{\Delta}}{\partial n_{q}}\right|_{n_{q}=n_{q}^{\ast}}=B^{(0)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)}+\frac{n_{q}^{\ast}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)^{2}}\right)=0

\displaystyle\quad\implies B^{(0)}\left(\frac{\mathrm{SSR}_{q}-1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)}\right)=-\frac{B^{(0)}n_{q}^{\ast}}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(p-n_{q}^{\ast}-1)^{2}}.

Plugging this into
\widetilde{\Delta}(n_{q})
at
n_{q}=n_{q}^{\ast}
and flipping the sign yield the maximal improvement value

\displaystyle\Delta(n_{q}^{\ast})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\left(\frac{\left(p-1-\sqrt{\frac{p(p-1)}{\mathrm{SNR}_{q}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}}\right)^{2}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}{p(p-1)}\right)\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{p-n_{0}}{p}\right)\left(\frac{(n_{q}^{\ast})^{2}(1-\mathrm{SSR}_{q})}{p(p-1)}\right)\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\|^{2}>0.

This completes the proof.
∎

C.6
Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

We first recall the notations
\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)}=(1/n_{q})\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
,
\mathbf{H}^{(q)}=\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}
, and
{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}=\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)
.

Upper bound on bias.
First, notice that the bias of the target-only MNI in Equation (
6
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},

since

\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(q)}=\frac{1}{n_{q}}\left(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big(\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}\right)\mathbf{X}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{X}^{(q)}=\mathbf{0}_{p\times p},\quad q\in[Q]_{0}.

This implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big\|\big\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big\|\big\|{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big\|^{2},

where
\big\|\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big\|=\big\|\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big\|=1
for all
q\in[Q]_{0}
. By Lemma
6
, there exists a constant
u>0
such that with probability at least
1-2e^{-\delta}
for
\delta\geq\log(2)
,

\displaystyle\big\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big\|\leq u\left(\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)})+\delta}{n_{0}}}+\frac{r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)})+\delta}{n_{0}}\right)\big\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big\|,

(15)

which completes the proof for the upper bound on
\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}^{(0)}
; note that the constant
u>0
in Lemma
6
depends only on the sub-Gaussian parameter
\nu_{x}
in Assumption
1
. As in
Bartlett et al. 2020
, we assume that
\nu_{x}
is fixed and therefore regard
u
as a universal constant.

Next, recall that the bias of the TM estimate in Lemma
1
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}}_{=:~U_{1}~\geq~0}~+~\underbrace{{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}}_{=:~U_{2}~\geq~0}

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},

where the Cauchy-Schwartz and AM-GM inequalities yield

2\left|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\Pi}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{U_{1}U_{2}}\leq U_{1}+U_{2},

and therefore

\mathcal{B}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}^{(q)}\leq 2\big(U_{1}+U_{2}\big).

So it suffices to upper-bound the terms
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
, and their concentration inequalities can be obtained similarly. Notice that
U_{1}
is bounded above by

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle={\bm{\delta}}^{(q)\top}\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\mathbf{H}^{(q)}{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big\|\big\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\big\|\big\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2},

where the concentration inequality for
\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}-\hat{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{(0)}\|
has already been established in inequality (
15
). Here, we only substitute the variable
\delta
in (
15
) by
\eta_{1}\geq\log(2)
: with probability at least
1-2e^{-\eta_{1}}
,

\displaystyle U_{1}\leq u\left(\sqrt{\frac{r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)})+\eta_{1}}{n_{0}}}+\frac{r_{0}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)})+\eta_{1}}{n_{0}}\right)\big\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big\|\big\|{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\big\|^{2}.

(16)

Next, recall that by Assumption
1
, there exists a universal constant
C_{\scriptscriptstyle{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}}>0
such that

\big\|{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})^{-1}\big\|\leq C_{\scriptscriptstyle{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}},

with which we define the following set of matrices:

{\bm{\Theta}}:=\Big\{\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}:\big\|\mathbf{A}\big\|\leq C_{\scriptscriptstyle{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}}\Big\}.

Consider any
\mathbf{A}\in{\bm{\Theta}}
. We have

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\big\|

\displaystyle={\bm{\beta}}^{(0)\top}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big){\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(0)}\big)\big(\mathbf{I}_{p}-\mathbf{H}^{(q)}\big){\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}

and the tail bound on

holds similarly by Lemma

for

(17)

Appendix F
Experimental Details

This section provides further details on the experiments presented in Section
5
and Appendix
E
. All numerical experiments are conducted by
\mathtt{R}
using a standard laptop (ASUS ZenBook UX331UN, Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-8550U CPU @ 1.80GHz, 16 GB 2133 MHz DDR3). All sourcecodes we release are scripted from scratch, relying on standard
\mathtt{R}
packages such as
\mathtt{MASS}
and
\mathtt{mvtnorm}
.

For the evaluation metric, we compute the excess risk of an estimate
\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{t}
trained at the
t
-th simulation run by

\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{t}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{t}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big),

for
t\in[50]
, where 50 is the number of independent simulations conducted. The deterministic parameters
{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}
and
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}
are generated once and kept fixed across the 50 simulations, guaranteeing they are agnostic to the replicate index
t
. We then report the average excess risk

\frac{1}{50}\sum_{t=1}^{50}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{t}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big)^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(0)}\big(\hat{{\bm{\beta}}}_{t}-{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}\big).

To ensure the stable convergence of SGD
[
69
]
we test as a benchmark transfer task, we tune the initial pre-training and fine-tuning learning rates for each experimental setup. While
Wu et al. 2022
adopted an initial learning rate of
0.1
(both for pre-training and fine-tuning) in their experiments, we find this value leads to divergence across all of our experimental setups. Accordingly, we reduce the learning rates based on preliminary implementations; for example, we use
0.001
for both pre-training and fine-tuning in Fig.
2.(a)
.

F.1
Parameter Generation Process

In the formulation of the ground truth model

\mathbf{y}^{(q)}=\left(\mathbf{Z}^{(q)}({\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})^{1/2}\right)\left({\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}+{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\right)+{\bm{\epsilon}}^{(q)},\quad q\in[Q]_{0},

where
{\bm{\delta}}^{(0)}\equiv\mathbf{0}_{p}
, only the random quantities
\{(\mathbf{Z}^{(q)},{\bm{\epsilon}}^{(q)})\}_{q=0}^{Q}
vary across the 50 simulations. The deterministic parameters
\{{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)},({\bm{\delta}}^{(q)},{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)})\}_{q=0}^{Q}
remain fixed across all runs. While these fixed quantities are sampled from specific probability distributions, we generate them using a fixed random seed, thereby ensuring their deterministic nature.

Model coefficients.
As specified in Section
5
, the target coefficient
{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}
and the contrast vectors
\{{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}
are given by

{\bm{\beta}}^{(0)}=S^{1/2}\mathbf{u}^{(0)},\qquad{\bm{\delta}}^{(q)}=(\mathrm{SSR}_{q}\cdot S)^{1/2}\mathbf{u}^{(q)},

where
(S,\mathrm{SSR}_{q})
are pre-specified hyperparameters. Each
p
-dimensional vector
\mathbf{u}^{(q)}
is independently sampled from
\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}_{p},\mathbf{I}_{p})
and then normalized so that
\|\mathbf{u}^{(q)}\|=1
for all
q\in[Q]_{0}
. Each
\mathbf{u}^{(q)}
is held fixed across the 50 replications.

Spectral decomposition of covariance.
Random sampling for covariance generation is used only in producing Figures
1.(b)
and
1.(c)
. In these figures, the source covariances are defined via the spectral decomposition

{\bm{\Sigma}}^{(q)}=\mathbf{V}^{(q)}{\bm{\Lambda}}^{(q)}\mathbf{V}^{(q)\top},\quad q\in[Q],

where
\{{\bm{\Lambda}}^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}
are fixed as specified in Section
5
, and
\{\mathbf{V}^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}
are independently sampled from the orthogonal group
\mathcal{O}_{p}:=\{\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}:\mathbf{Q}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}=\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}=\mathbf{I}_{p}\}
. Specifically, we first independently sample the matrix
\mathbf{G}^{(q)}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries (which is then fixed across the 50 simulations), perform its QR decomposition
\mathbf{G}^{(q)}=\mathbf{Q}^{(q)}\mathbf{R}^{(q)}
and assign
\mathbf{V}^{(q)}=\mathbf{Q}^{(q)}
. Consequently, the matrices
\{\mathbf{V}^{(q)}\}_{q=1}^{Q}
are held fixed.

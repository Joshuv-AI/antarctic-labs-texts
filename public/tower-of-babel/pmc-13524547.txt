Hepatitis B Virus-Associated Cryoglobulinemic Glomerulonephritis Misdiagnosed as Lupus Nephritis: the Value of Clinicopathologic Integration
Authors: Thoi The Nguyen, Nghia Thanh Dang, Uyen Hoang, Dung Trung Nghiem, My Thi Tra Mai, Huong Thi Nguyen
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Hepatitis B virus (HBV)–associated cryoglobulinemic glomerulonephritis (CryoGN) is an uncommon immune complex–mediated kidney disease that frequently presents with a membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis pattern.1 Because membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis represents a morphologic pattern rather than a specific disease, distinguishing HBV-associated CryoGN from lupus nephritis requires integration of clinical, serologic, virologic, and pathologic findings rather than kidney biopsy alone.2 We report a patient initially misdiagnosed as lupus nephritis in whom clinicopathologic integration established the diagnosis of HBV-associated CryoGN and guided appropriate management.

A 62-year-old woman with active HBV infection presented with nephritic syndrome, nephrotic-range proteinuria, acute kidney dysfunction, recurrent palpable purpura and arthralgia. Laboratory evaluation demonstrated profound C4 depletion, mildly reduced C3, markedly elevated rheumatoid factor, and HBV DNA of 7 × 106 IU/ml. Antinuclear antibody was repeatedly negative on 3 separate occasions, whereas an extended lupus autoantibody panel, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody, antiphospholipid antibodies, hepatitis C virus, HIV, and serum protein immunofixation electrophoresis were all unrevealing (Supplementary Table S1). Kidney biopsy demonstrated an immune complex-mediated membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis pattern with endocapillary hypercellularity, glomerular basement membrane double contours, and intracapillary Periodic acid–Schiff-positive pseudothrombi (Figure 1). Although the biopsy was initially interpreted as favoring lupus nephritis, the discordance between the histopathologic findings and the repeatedly negative lupus serology prompted multidisciplinary clinicopathologic review. Multidisciplinary review integrating the patient’s active HBV infection, recurrent purpura, profound C4 depletion, markedly elevated rheumatoid factor, absence of monoclonal gammopathy, and characteristic biopsy findings ultimately favored HBV-associated mixed CryoGN, which was subsequently confirmed by qualitative cryoglobulin testing (Supplementary Figure S1). Tenofovir alafenamide and corticosteroids were initiated. Although rituximab can be administered safely in patients receiving effective antiviral therapy, it was deferred because the patient had no rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis, or life-threatening systemic vasculitis, making antiviral therapy combined with corticosteroids an appropriate initial treatment strategy. HBV DNA became undetectable by 6 months, kidney function normalized, proteinuria markedly improved, serum albumin increased, and purpura did not recur (Supplementary Table S2). This case highlights 2 complementary principles in the management of HBV-associated CryoGN. First, clinicopathologic integration is essential because biopsy findings alone may closely resemble lupus nephritis. Second, although rituximab remains an effective treatment for severe cryoglobulinemic vasculitis and can be safely administered with appropriate antiviral therapy, patients without rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis or life-threatening systemic vasculitis may achieve favorable outcomes with antiviral therapy and corticosteroids alone.1, 2, 3, 4 In our patient, this individualized treatment strategy resulted in virologic suppression, sustained renal remission, and resolution of systemic manifestations without the need for immediate rituximab during follow-up.

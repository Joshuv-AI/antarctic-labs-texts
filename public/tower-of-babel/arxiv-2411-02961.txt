On Cone Restriction Estimates in Higher Dimensions
Xiangyu Wang

Abstract
. We revisit the approach of Ou and Wang to the cone restriction problem via polynomial partitioning. By recasting their inductive scheme as a recursive algorithm and incorporating the nested polynomial Wolff axioms, we obtain improved bounds for cone restriction estimates in higher dimensions.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of results

We obtain improved cone restriction estimates in higher dimensions. The Fourier restriction problem is one of the central open questions in harmonic analysis. It concerns the possibility of restricting the Fourier transform of an
L^{p}
function to a curved hypersurface. Stein
[
16
]
conjectured that, for hypersurfaces such as the sphere, the paraboloid, and the cone, the Fourier transform admits a bounded restriction operator from
L^{p^{\prime}}
to
L^{q^{\prime}}
for an appropriate range of exponents
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
.

We now state the cone restriction conjecture precisely.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be the truncated cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\mathcal{C}:=\{(\xi,|\xi|):\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ 1\leq|\xi|\leq 2\}.

(1.1)

Then for all
p,q
satisfying

p>\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2},\frac{n-2}{q^{\prime}}\geq\frac{n}{p}

(1.2)

we have

\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathcal{C};d\sigma)}\lesssim_{p,q}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\forall f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}

where
d\sigma
denotes the induced surface measure on
\mathcal{C}
, and
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
are the Hölder conjugates of
p,q
.

Define the Fourier extension operator associated with
\mathcal{C}
by

Ef(x):=\int_{2\overline{B}^{n-1}\setminus B^{n-1}}e^{i(x^{\prime}\cdot\xi+x_{n}|\xi|)}f(\xi)\,d\xi,

where
B^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is the unit ball centered at the origin. The parametrization
\xi\mapsto(\xi,|\xi|)
identifies surface measure with a constant multiple of
d\xi
. By duality, Conjecture
1.1
is equivalent to the following statement.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
. For all
p,q
satisfying (
1.2
), the estimate

\|Ef\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{p,q}\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

(1.3)

holds for all
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
.

Conjecture
1.2
is known in dimensions
n=3,4,5
through the work of Barceló
[
1
]
, Wolff
[
18
]
, and Ou–Wang
[
15
]
. Our starting point in higher dimensions is the following theorem of Ou and Wang.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
15
]
, Theorem 2)
.

Let
n\geq 3
. Suppose the tuple
(p,q,k)
is admissible in the sense that

,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}p>2\frac{n+2}{n},q^{\prime}\leq\frac{n-2}{n}p&\mathrm{if}&k=2\\

Then the estimate (

Corollary 1.4

Let

p>\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}4&\ \mathrm{if}&n=3\\
2\frac{3n+1}{3n-3}&\mathrm{if}&n>3\quad\mathrm{odd}\\
2\frac{3n}{3n-4}&\mathrm{if}&n>3\quad\mathrm{even}\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

the estimate

\|Ef\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

(1.5)

holds for all

\frac{1}{q(n,k)}=\frac{1}{p(n,k)}+\frac{1}{4n-8}\big(\frac{6}{p(n,k)}-1\big)

Fourier restriction is connected to several central problems in harmonic analysis, geometric measure theory, and partial differential equations. For hypersurfaces with nonvanishing Gaussian curvature, such as the sphere and the paraboloid, these connections include the Bochner–Riesz problem, Kakeya-type estimates, and the Schrödinger maximal problem. The cone has a different geometry because one of its principal curvatures vanishes. Nevertheless, restriction estimates for these surfaces are closely related: the restriction conjecture for the sphere or paraboloid in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
implies the corresponding conjecture for the cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
; see
[
14
]
.

Guth
[
5
,
6
]
introduced
polynomial partitioning
into Fourier restriction theory and obtained improved estimates for the paraboloid. Subsequent developments include the work of Wang
[
19
]
, Wang–Wu
[
20
]
, Hickman–Rogers
[
11
]
, Hickman–Zahl
[
12
]
, and Guo–Wang–Zhang
[
7
]
. In
[
11
,
12
]
, Guth’s induction-on-scales argument was reformulated as a recursive algorithm. This formulation permits the use of Kakeya-type geometric estimates from
[
13
,
25
]
, including the
nested polynomial Wolff axioms
, to control the number of tube directions compatible with a sequence of algebraic varieties. Polynomial partitioning has also been applied to the Bochner–Riesz problem
[
22
,
8
]
, the Schrödinger maximal problem
[
3
]
, and cone restriction
[
15
]
.

We adapt the methods of
[
11
,
12
]
to the cone framework of
[
15
]
. In Section
3
, we recast the inductive argument as a recursive procedure and combine it with the nested polynomial Wolff axioms. The main geometric distinction from the paraboloid argument is the scale to which a terminal wave-packet family is traced back. At scale
r
, the cone has approximately
r^{(n-2)/2}
angular sectors, whereas the paraboloid has approximately
r^{(n-1)/2}
caps. To match the cone sector count, we trace each terminal family back to its
(n-1)
-dimensional ancestor rather than to the root.

Wave packet decomposition is a central tool in our argument. We briefly recall its definition and basic properties from Sections 2.2 and 5.2.1 of

Definition.
Fix

r^{-1/2}

\{\psi_{\theta}\}

\{\theta\}

\theta

\widehat{f_{\theta}}=\sum_{v}\widehat{f_{\theta}}\,\eta_{v},

\{\eta_{v}\}

Next, for each
v

\widehat{f_{\theta}}\eta_{v}

Set

A direct calculation gives

For

p_{j}^{(0)}:=2+\frac{6}{2(j-1)+a_{j}},\qquad p_{n}^{(0)}:=P,

(4.1)

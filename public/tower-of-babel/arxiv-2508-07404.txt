The Euler characteristic of an endotrivial complex
Nadia Mazza, Sam K. Miller

Abstract

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of prime characteristic
p
.
Permutation
kG
-modules
, i.e.
kG
-modules which admit a
G
-invariant basis, and their direct summands,
p
-permutation
kG
-modules
, are perhaps the most straightforward class of representations to construct. Yet they play an important role in the modular representation theory of finite groups, especially in “local-to-global” representation theory of
kG
, which relates the representation theory of
G
with its
p
-local subgroups
, subgroups of
G
of the form
N_{G}(P)
with
P
a
p
-subgroup of
G
. For instance, Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture predicts certain equivalences,
splendid Rickard equivalences
, between homotopy categories of
p
-permutation modules. Weaker variants of the conjecture instead predict certain isomorphisms,
p
-permutation equivalences
between the corresponding Grothendieck groups, or character-theoretic equivalences known as
isotypies
.

Recently, Balmer and Gallauer
[
BG25
]
deduced the Balmer spectrum of the tensor-triangulated category
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
, the
bounded homotopy category of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules
, (also denoted
\text{K}_{b}(p\operatorname{-perm}(G;k))
in the literature). This category is the compact part of a “big” compactly generated tensor-triangulated category, the
derived category of permutation modules

\operatorname{DPerm}(G;k)
. This big category is in fact equivalent to numerous categories beyond the realm of representation theory - for instance it is equivalent to the category of cohomological Mackey functors for
G
over
k
, the homotopy category of modules over the constant Green functor
\text{H}\underline{k}
in genuine
G
-spectra (which was recently lifted to an
\infty
-categorical level by Fuhrmann
[
Fuh25
]
), and the category of
k
-linear Artin motives generated by motives of intermediate fields in any Galois extension with Galois group
G
. Some of these connections are reviewed in detail in
[
BG23
]
, though neither these equivalences nor tensor-triangular geometry will be dwelt upon in this paper.

Given a rigid monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, determining its Picard group
\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal{C})
, i.e. the group of invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}
, those objects
X
in
\mathcal{C}
for which
X\otimes X^{*}\cong\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the tensor unit of
\mathcal{C}
, is a pertinent question. For the category
\mathcal{C}:=K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
, these invertible objects are called
endotrivial complexes
, nomenclature inspired by the invertible objects the stable module category
{}_{kG}\underline{\textbf{mod}}
, endotrivial
modules
. In
[
Mil25a
]
, the second author completely determined the Picard group
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G):=\operatorname{Pic}(K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}))
for all finite groups
G
, identifying for
p
-groups endotrivial complexes with
G
-representation spheres and
Borel-Smith functions
.

Another object of interest is the
trivial source ring

T(kG)
, the Grothendieck group of
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
as a tensor-triangulated category, and equivalently, the split Grothendieck group of the additive category
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}
. Boltje and Carman in
[
BC23
]
gave a character-theoretic description of
T(kG)
in the language of
coherent character tuples
. In particular, they gave a characterization of the
orthogonal unit group

O(T(kG))
, the group of units
u\in T(kG)^{\times}
satisfying
u^{-1}=u^{*}
. These orthogonal units induce
p
-permutation equivalences
[
BP20
]
in the same manner that endotrivial complexes induce splendid Rickard equivalences
[
Mil24a
]
.

We have a natural group homomorphism
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
induced by taking the
Euler characteristic
(also called the
Lefschetz invariant
) of an endotrivial complex
C
, i.e. its image in
T(kG)
, an alternating sum of terms. This map
\Lambda
is called the
Lefschetz homomorphism
, and is the central focus of this paper. In
[
Mil25a
]
, it was shown that when
G
is a
p
-group,
\Lambda
is surjective. On the other hand, for non-
p
-groups, if
k
contains more than
p
elements,
\Lambda
may not be surjective
[
Mil24a
]
.

In this paper, we investigate further the Lefschetz homomorphism for non-
p
-groups. The cases of
p=2
and
p
odd play out rather differently (as usual), due to the characterization of
O(T(kG))
by Boltje and Carman. For
p=2
, the question of surjectivity is inherently tied to the unit group of the Burnside ring
B(G)^{\times}
of a finite group
G
, whereas for
p
odd, the question instead is tied to Boltje and Carman’s coherent character tuples (in part because for
p
-groups
G
with
p
odd,
B(G)^{\times}
has order 2). Though a complete characterization of the kernel and image of
\Lambda
remains a difficult question, we make significant progress on the question, which we summarize now. These results arise in Section
4
.

Theorem
.

Set
k=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
S
be a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
G
. Then
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective if the normalizer of
S
controls the
2
-fusion in
G
, or if
S
is a dihedral
2
-group.

In particular,
\Lambda
is surjective over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
for any group
G
with abelian Sylow 2-subgroups, or more generally,
resistant
Sylow 2-subgroups. When
k
has characteristic 2 and has more than 2 elements,
\Lambda
is usually non-surjective. On the other hand, we find no counterexamples of
\Lambda
not surjective when
k=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We pose the question of whether
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(G)\to O(T(\mathbb{F}_{2}G))
is always surjective.

The situation for
p
odd is rather different. We derive the following results in Section
5
.

Theorem
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
k
be a field contained in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. If
G
is
p
-nilpotent or has a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup, then
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective. On the other hand, for every odd prime
p
and integer
i\geq 2
, there exist finite groups with elementary abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups of rank
i
such that
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is not surjective.

An exact criterion for when
\Lambda
is non-surjective for
p
odd appears to be unfeasible, but our example demonstrates the expectation that for groups
G
with Sylow
p
-subgroups having sufficiently many generators and whose fusion in
G
is sufficiently complex,
\Lambda
should not be surjective.

Finally, we consider the kernel of the Lefschetz homomorphism in Section
6
. For
p=2
, the kernel of
\Lambda
is easily determined, and for any prime
p
, when
G
has a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup, we compute the kernel directly. For
p
odd, we determine a necessary condition for an endotrivial complex
C
to belong to the kernel of the Lefschetz homomorphism, but an exact characterization of the kernel again remains out of reach. We note that Bachmann in his Ph.D. thesis
[
Bac16
]
performed similar computations with more restrictive settings and in the category of Artin motives.

We note a potential application of these computations. Two related questions of interest are whether a
p
-permutation equivalence implies a splendid Rickard equivalence, and if, given a
p
-permutation equivalence, it is induced by a splendid Rickard equivalence. At present, no examples of two block algebras which are
p
-permutation equivalent but not splendid Rickard equivalent are known. Similarly, no examples of
p
-permutation equivalences not induced by splendid Rickard equivalences are known either. In fact, the second author showed in
[
Mil25a
]
that every
p
-permutation autoequivalence of the group algebra
kP
, with
P
a
p
-group, is induced by a splendid Rickard autoequivalence. Since endotrivial complexes induce splendid Rickard equivalences and orthogonal units of the trivial source ring induce
p
-permutation equivalences, we hope the results of this paper can be used to construct examples of
p
-permutation equivalences not induced from splendid Rickard equivalences.

2.
Preliminaries on endotrivial complexes

Throughout this paper, all modules are assumed finitely generated. Recall that a
permutation
kG
-module
is a
kG
-module with a
G
-stable basis, and a
p
-permutation
kG
-module
is a direct summand of a permutation module. A
kG
-module
M
is a
p
-permutation module if and only if
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}M
is a permutation
kS
-module, where
S
denotes a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Indecomposable
p
-permutation modules are often referred to as
trivial source modules
, as an indecomposable
kG
-module is
p
-permutation if and only if it has trivial source.

Given any
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
, we have a monoidal functor
-(P):{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}\to{}_{k[N_{G}(P)/P]}\mathbf{triv}
, the
Brauer construction
(also referred to as the
Brauer quotient
, and in
[
BG25
]
, this is naturally isomorphic to the
modular fixed points
functor). The Brauer construction satisfies the property that for any
G
-set
X
, one has a natural isomorphism of
k[N_{G}(P)/P]
-modules
k[X](P)\cong k[X^{P}]
. For in-depth overviews of
p
-permutation modules or the Brauer construction, we refer the reader to
[
Lin18
, Chapter 5]
or
[
Las23
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
C
be a bounded chain complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules. We say
C
is
endotrivial
if

C^{*}\otimes_{k}C\simeq k[0],

where
\simeq
denotes homotopy equivalence of chain complexes. In other words,
C
is an invertible object in the bounded homotopy category of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules,
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}).
We let
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
denote the Picard group of
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv})
. In other words,
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of endotrivial complexes in
K^{b}({}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}).
Then
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
is an abelian group with group addition induced by
[C]+[D]=[C\otimes_{k}D]
and inverse given by
-[C]=[C^{*}]
. We may abusively write
C\in\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
, in which case we assume
C
is the unique indecomposable representative of the equivalence class
[C]\in\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
. Indeed, any endotrivial complex has a unique noncontractible direct summand.

Construction 2.2
.

Let
C
be an endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules. Observe that for any
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
,
C(P)
is an endotrivial complex of
p
-permutation
k[N_{G}(P)/P]
-modules. Additionally, it follows from the Künneth formula that any endotrivial complex has nonzero homology concentrated in a unique degree, with that homology having
k
-dimension 1. Given any
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
, let
h_{C}(P)
denote the unique integer for which
H_{h_{C}(P)}(C(P))\neq 0
.

We denote the set of
p
-subgroups of
G
by
s_{p}(G)
. Then
h_{C}:s_{p}(G)\to\mathbb{Z}
is a superclass function (i.e. a function from a subset of subgroups of
G
to
\mathbb{Z}
that agrees on conjugates) on the
p
-subgroups of
G
. We call
h_{C}
the
h-mark function of
C
. We denote the additive group of superclass functions on
p
-subgroups of
G
by
CF(G,p)
.

Since the nonzero homology of
C
has
k
-dimension 1, it is isomorphic to a
kG
-module of the form
k_{\omega}
, for some
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})
. Given any
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
, let
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)
denote the unique homomorphism
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,k^{\times})
such that
k_{\omega}\cong H_{h_{C}(P)}(C(P))
.

.

The assignment

2.1.
Borel-Smith functions

The classification of endotrivial complexes relies heavily on Borel-Smith functions, which we now introduce. We note that we adopt the convention of only considering Borel-Smith functions valued on
p
-subgroups of a group, but Borel-Smith functions are also regarded superclass functions over all subgroups in the literature.

Definition 2.4
.

A
Borel-Smith
function is a superclass function
f\in CF(G,p)
satisfying the following three conditions, which we call the Borel-Smith conditions.

(a)

If
p
is odd, then for any subquotient
T/S
of
G
of order
p
,
f(T)\equiv f(S)\mod 2
.

(b)

If
p=2
, then for any sequence of subgroups
H\trianglelefteq K\trianglelefteq L\leq N_{G}(H)
, with
[K:H]=2
,
f(K)\equiv f(H)\mod 2
if
L/K
is cyclic of order 4 and
f(K)\equiv f(H)\mod 4
is
L/K
is quaternion of order 8.

(c)

For any elementary abelian subquotient
T/S
of
G
of rank 2, the equality

f(S)-f(T)=\sum_{S<X<T}\big(f(X)-f(T)\big)

holds.

The collection of Borel-Smith functions forms an additive subgroup
CF_{b}(G,p)
of
CF(G,p)
. In fact, if
G
is a
p
-group, then under the identification
CF(G)\cong B^{*}(G):=\operatorname{Hom}(B(G),\mathbb{Z})
,
CF_{b}(G)
forms a rational
p
-biset subfunctor of
CF(G)
. See
[
BY07
, Proposition 3.7]
for details. We refer the reader to
[
Bou10
]
for an overview of biset functors, although a background understanding of biset functors is unnecessary for this paper.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
f\in CF_{b}(G,p)
be a Borel-Smith function for
G
. Observe that if
P
is a non-cyclic
p
-subgroup of
G
(with the convention that the trivial subgroup is cyclic), that the value
f(P)
is determined entirely by subgroups
Q<P
. Indeed, since
P
is non-cyclic, there exists a normal subgroup
R\trianglelefteq P
such that
P/R
is elementary abelian of rank 2. Therefore, the
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
CF_{b}(G,p)
is bounded by the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic
p
-subgroups of
G
.

In fact, for
p
-groups, this bound is known to be an equality.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
tD87
, Proposition III.5.10, page 214]

Let
G
be a
p
-group. The
\mathbb{Z}
-rank of
CF_{b}(G)
is exactly the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
.

Remark 2.7
.

Borel-Smith functions were originally considered in the context of homotopy representations of spheres, see
[
tD87
, Section III.5: Borel-Smith functions]
for details. Purely representation-theoretically, Borel-Smith functions arise from real representations (or equivalently, representation spheres) in the following manner. Given a field
\mathbb{F}
and a
\mathbb{F}G
-module
V
, define the superclass function
\dim(V)
as follows:

\dim(V)(H):=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}V^{H}.

If
\mathbb{F}G
is semisimple, then

\dim(V)(H)=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h\in H}\chi_{V}(h),

where
\chi_{V}
is the character of
V
. We call
\dim
the
dimension homomorphism
. In this case,
\dim:R_{\mathbb{F}}(G)\to CF(G)
is a group homomorphism. Regarding a representation
V
as a representation sphere
S^{V}
via one-point compactification, the dimension homomorphism
\dim(V)(H)
simply returns the dimension of the representation sphere
S^{V^{H}}
.

If
G
is a
p
-group and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, then the image of
\dim:R_{\mathbb{R}}(G)\to CF(G)
is exactly
CF_{b}(G)
, the group of Borel-Smith functions. In fact,
\dim:R_{\mathbb{R}}\to CF_{b}
is a morphism of rational
p
-biset functors.

In fact, one can determine precisely when a Borel-Smith function arises from a real representation.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
tD87
]

Let
f\in CF_{b}(G)
be a Borel-Smith function. Then
f
is monotonically decreasing (with respect to the poset of subgroups of
G
) and non-negative if and only if
f=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}V
for a real representation
V
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group and let
V_{1},\dots,V_{n}
denote the irreducible real representations of
G
. Then the set of associated dimension functions
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
, after removing duplicates, forms a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
CF_{b}(G)
.

Proof.

Since the image of the dimension homomorphism is precisely
CF_{b}(G)
, it suffices to show that the set
\mathcal{B}:=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
is linearly independent after removing duplicates.
[
tD87
, Proposition III.5.9, page 213]
asserts that
\ker(\dim)
is generated by elements of the form
V-\psi^{k}(V)
, where
V
is an irreducible real representation,
\psi^{k}
is the
k
-th Adams operation, and
k
is coprime to
|G|
. Therefore, the duplicates in
\mathcal{B}
arise from Adams operation conjugates, and it follows that any set of real irreducible representations which are not Adams operation conjugates will correspond to a linearly independent set of Borel-Smith functions.
∎

We next show that even if
G
is not a
p
-group,
CF_{b}(G,p)
has
\mathbb{Z}
-rank the number of conjugacy classes of
p
-subgroups of
G
. Given a subgroup
H
of
G
, say
f\in CF_{b}(H,p)
is
G
-stable
if, for all pairs of
G
-conjugate
p
-subgroups
Q_{1},Q_{2}
of

,
f(Q_{1})=f(Q_{2})
. Let
CF_{b}(H,p)^{G}
be the subgroup of

consisting of
G
-stable elements. Note that given a Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
of
G
, we have an identification
CF_{b}(S)^{G}=CF_{b}(G,p)
, which we use without further mention.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
and let
f\in CF_{b}(H,p)
. If
f
is
G
-stable at all cyclic
p
-subgroups of
H
, then

is
G
-stable.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on the order of
p
-subgroups of
H
. This is true by assumption on all cyclic
p
-subgroups, in particular, it is true for all subgroups of order 1 and
p
. Now suppose
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are two
p
-subgroups of
H
of order at least
p^{2}
that are
G
-conjugate. If
Q_{1},Q_{2}
are cyclic, then
f(Q_{1})=f(Q_{2})
by assumption. Otherwise, both are non-cyclic, and therefore have subgroups
R_{1}
of
Q_{1}
and
R_{2}
of
Q_{2}
of index
p^{2}
that are
G
-conjugate and for which
Q_{i}/R_{i}\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
. It follows by induction that since
f(Q_{i})
is uniquely determined by the values
f(R_{i})
and
f(X_{i})
for all intermediate subgroups
R_{i}<X_{i}<Q_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
,
f(Q_{1})=f(Q_{2})
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
G
be a
p
-group, and let
\{H_{1},\dots,H_{r}\}
be a collection of representatives of all conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of
G
. Then
CF_{b}(G)
has a linearly independent set of
r
elements
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\}
such that the following holds; for all
i\neq j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, we have
f_{i}(H_{i})\neq 0
and
f_{i}(H_{j})=0
.

Proof.

To prove this, it suffices to show that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathbb{Q}CF_{b}(P):=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}CF_{b}(P)
has a basis satisfying the same property (note this is basis since
CF_{b}(P)
has rank equal to
r
). Let
c(P)
denote the set of cyclic subgroups of
P
, and let
CF_{b}(P,c)
denote the set of functions
c(P)\to\mathbb{Z}
satisfying the Borel-Smith conditions. We have an obvious projection map
\pi:\mathbb{Q}CF_{b}(P)\to\mathbb{Q}CF_{b}(P,c)
.

We claim
\pi
is an isomorphism. First, we show
\pi
is injective. Indeed, any element
f\in\ker(\pi)
satisfies
f(C)=0
for any cyclic subgroup
C
of
P
. However, since the Borel-Smith conditions completely determine the value of
f
on non-cyclic subgroups of
P
, it follows that
f=0
. Thus
\pi
is injective. On the other hand,
CF_{b}(P,c)
has rank at most
r
by construction, so
\pi
is surjective as well, and thus an isomorphism. Taking the inverse image of the standard basis of
\mathbb{Q}CF_{b}(P,c)
via
\pi
gives a basis of
\mathbb{Q}CF_{b}(P)
which satisfies the desired property, and we are done.
∎

Denote the number of conjugacy classes of cyclic

In particular, if

contains a Sylow

-subgroup of

-rank

Proof.

(b) follows from (a) since if (a) holds,

has rank at least

must have rank at most

has a basis satisfying the same property. Lemma

asserts

has a basis consisting of elements

such that

and
g_{i}(K_{j})=0
when

. For

, we define

as follows:

f_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\gamma^{G}(i,j)g_{j},\text{ with }\gamma^{G}(i,j)=\begin{cases}1&K_{i},K_{j}\text{ are $G$-conjugate}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since

∎

Example 2.16

The following example will be relevant in the sequel. Let

Definition 2.17

(a)

Let

(b)

The following construction was first considered in
[

]
and used in

]
. Define the

Remark 2.18

If

is a

[

Induction of Borel-Smith functions corresponds to taking traces of representation spheres, which induce endotrivial complexes via Bredon cohomology.

3.
Orthogonal units of the trivial source ring

Definition 3.1
.

The
trivial source ring

is the (split) Grothendieck ring of
{}_{kG}\mathbf{triv}
. Given a
p
-permutation
kG
-module

denote its image in

It has, as an abelian group, a free
\mathbb{Z}
-basis spanned by isomorphism classes of indecomposable
p
-permutation
kG
-modules (also called trivial source modules), with addition induced by direct sums:

[M]+[N]:=[M\oplus N].

Multiplication is induced by the tensor product:

[M]\cdot[N]:=[M\otimes_{k}N].

The assignment
[M]\mapsto[\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M,k)]
induces a ring involution on

, which we also denote by
(-)^{*}
. The

of

, denoted

, is the subgroup of the unit group

given by all units

for which

. Equivalently,

is the torsion subgroup of

, see
[

, Remark 4.1(b)]
.

Equivalently,

, denoted
\Lambda(C)

is called the

or

of

. Explicitly, we have

\Lambda(C)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}[C_{i}]\in T(kG).

Proposition 3.2
.

[
Mil24a
, Proposition 4.1]

Let

be an endotrivial complex of

-modules. Then

is an orthogonal unit of

We call

the

. The structure of the trivial source ring, and its orthogonal unit group, was described by Boltje and Carman in
[

]
. We will summarize their results describing the structure of

of

Definition 3.3
.

The

{}_{G}\mathbf{set}
of finite
G

G
-set

As an abelian group,

\mathbb{Z}

[X]+[Y]:=[X\sqcup Y],

Given a

4.
Surjectivity of the Lefschetz homomorphism: the case of
p=2

Our goal for this section is to compute the image of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(G)\to O(T(\mathbb{F}_{2}G))
. In this case,
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\times}=\{1\}
, so all groups of character tuples are trivial (which certainly is not true for arbitrary field extensions
k/\mathbb{F}_{2}
), and it only remains to compute the image of

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(G)

\displaystyle\to(B(S)^{G})^{\times}

\displaystyle[C]

\displaystyle\mapsto\dim(h_{C}).

If
G
is a
p
-group, this map is surjective, which follows from an unpublished topological result of Tornehave
[
Tor84
]
regarding the
tom Dieck homomorphism
, which later was proved algebraically by Yalçin
[
Yal05
, Corollary 1.2]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
Mil25a
, Proposition 7.4]

Let
G
be a
p
-group. We have

im(\dim:CF_{b}(G)\to CF(G)^{\times})=\operatorname{im}(m:B(G)^{\times}\to CF(G)^{\times}).

Therefore, we may regard the dimension function as a surjective group homomorphism
\dim:CF_{b}(G)\to B(G)^{\times}
, and in particular,
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective.

Therefore, given any unit
u\in(B(S)^{G})^{\times}
, there exists some Borel-Smith function
f\in CF_{b}(S)
such that
\dim(f)=u
. Note that the only interesting case is
p=2
, since if
p
is odd,
|B(G)^{\times}|=2
. However,
f
is not guaranteed to be
G
-stable, i.e. there is no guarantee that
f\in CF_{b}(S)^{G}=CF_{b}(G,p)
, i.e. that
f
extends to a Borel-Smith function on
G
. Effectively, we have the following rewording:

\text{$\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(G)\to O(T(\mathbb{F}_{2}G))$ is surjective if and only if $\dim:CF_{b}(G,p)\to(B(S)^{G})^{\times}$ is surjective}.

Remark 4.2
.

Tornehave’s theorem
[
Tor84
]
explicitly proves that the tom Dieck homomorphism
\eta:R_{\mathbb{R}}(G)\to B(G)^{\times}
is surjective for all 2-groups
G
. However,
\eta
is known to not be surjective for non-2-groups, for instance
G=A_{5}

[
Bar11
, Remark 5.3]
. Additionally, it does not seem easy to adopt Tornehave’s original proof, as it involves a reduction step that may not be compatible with
G
-stability for a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
, and Yalçin’s algebraic proof uses a restriction to so-called
genetic subgroups
of
G
, which may not be compatible with
G
-stability either. At present, the question of surjectivity for
p=2
seems a difficult task, and a pertinent, interesting question.

4.1.
2
-fusion controlled by normalizers

We first recall a few necessary preliminaries of fusion systems and control of fusion, following
[
AKO11
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Recall that for any two subgroups
H,K
of
G
,
\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(H,K)
denotes the set of all (necessarily injective) homomorphisms from
H
to
K
induced by conjugation in
G
. We denote the set of all injective homomorphisms from
H
to
K
by
\operatorname{Inj}(P,Q)
.

A
fusion system
over a
p
-group
S
is a category
\mathcal{F}
with objects the set of all subgroups of
S
and which satisfy the following two properties for all subgroups
P,Q
of
S
.

•

\operatorname{Hom}_{S}(P,Q)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,Q)\subseteq\operatorname{Inj}(P,Q)
;

•

Each
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,Q)
is the composition of an
\mathcal{F}
-isomorphism followed by an inclusion.

Let
S
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of a finite group
G
. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
the fusion system over
S
with hom-sets
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)}(P,Q):=\operatorname{Hom}_{G}(P,Q)
for all subgroups
P,Q
of
S
. This fusion system is called
the fusion category of
G
over
S
. A fusion system
\mathcal{F}
over
S
is
realizable
if
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
for some finite group
G
with Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
containing a Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
. We say
H
controls fusion in
S
if
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)=\mathcal{F}_{S}(H)
. Explicitly, this implies that if there exists
g\in G
inducing an isomorphism
c_{g}:P\to Q
for two subgroups
P,Q
, then there exists
h\in H
such that
c_{g}=c_{h}
.

Fusion system-theoretic considerations will be used minimally in this paper. For further reading, we refer the reader to
[
AKO11
]
for a comprehensive overview.

Remark 4.4
.

If
S\trianglelefteq G
, then that the set
CF_{b}(S)
has
G
-set structure induced by
G
-conjugation. Then
CF_{b}(S)^{G}
is exactly the set of elements fixed by
G
. Given any Borel-Smith function
f\in CF_{b}(S)
,

\operatorname{tr}^{G}_{S}f:=\sum_{g\in[G/S]}{}^{g}f\in CF_{b}(S)^{G}

is a
G
-stable Borel-Smith function on
S
, and therefore extends to a Borel-Smith function on
G
.

More generally, if
N_{G}(S)
controls fusion of
S
, then
\operatorname{tr}^{N_{G}(S)}_{S}f
is a
N_{G}(S)
-stable Borel-Smith function, hence
G
-stable, and therefore also extends to a Borel-Smith function on
G
. Note we may assume a weaker condition - we only require that any two
G
-conjugate subgroups of
S
are
N_{G}(S)
-conjugate.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group with Sylow
2
-subgroup
S
for which
N_{G}(S)
controls fusion in
S
, i.e.
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)\cong\mathcal{F}_{S}(N_{G}(S))
. Then the group homomorphism
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(G)\to O(T(\mathbb{F}_{2}G))
is surjective.

In fact, this holds for any group
G^{\prime}
with a Sylow
2
-subgroup isomorphic to
S
satisfying
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)\cong\mathcal{F}_{S}(G^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in(B(S)^{G})^{\times}
. There exists some Borel-Smith function
f\in CF_{b}(S)
such that
\dim(f)=u
. In particular, for all
G
-conjugate subgroups
H,K
, we have
f(H)\equiv f(K)\mod 2
. Therefore,

\left(\operatorname{tr}^{N_{G}(S)}_{S}f\right)(P)\equiv[N_{G}(S):S]f(P)\equiv f(P)\mod 2

for all subgroups
P
of
S
. Since
N_{G}(S)
controls fusion in
S
,
\operatorname{tr}^{N_{G}(S)}_{S}f
is an
N_{G}(S)
-stable, hence
G
-stable, Borel-Smith function, and therefore extends to a Borel-Smith function on
G
. Since
\operatorname{tr}^{N_{G}(S)}_{S}f\equiv f\mod 2
, we have
\dim\left(\operatorname{tr}^{N_{G}(S)}_{S}f\right)=u
. Now, there exists a unique endotrivial complex
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{2}G
-modules with h-marks
h_{C}=f
, and
\dim(h_{C})=u\in(B(S)^{G})^{\times}
, as desired. The last statement follows since
\operatorname{tr}^{G}_{S}f
as before can be regarded as a
G^{\prime}
-stable Borel-Smith function, hence a Borel-Smith function on
G^{\prime}
as well.
∎

5.
Surjectivity of the Lefschetz homomorphism: the case of
p
odd

Contrary to the
p=2
case, the
p
odd case revolves around the coherent character tuple

\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,k^{\times})\right)^{\prime}\leq O(T(kG)),

since for odd primes
p
,
B(P)^{\times}=\{\pm[P/P]\}
for any
p
-group
P
. Recall we have the decomposition

\left(\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,k^{\times})\right)^{\prime}=\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})\times\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P),k^{\times})\right)^{\prime}.

From this decomposition, we record a straightforward result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
k
any field of characteristic
p
, and
G
a
p
-nilpotent group. Then
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective.

Proof.

This follows from
[
BC23
, Proposition 6.3]
, we spell out the details here. The Frobenius normal
p
-complement theorem (see e.g.
[
Gor68
, Theorem 7.4.5]
) states that a group
G
is
p
-nilpotent if and only if
N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P)
is a
P
-group for all
p
-subgroups
P
of
G
. Therefore,
\ker(\pi_{1})
is trivial, and under the decomposition
O(T(kG))=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})
, it is routine to check that
\Lambda
is surjective.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Similarly, for
p
odd, any group
G
with
|G|
coprime to
p-1
satisfies
O(T(\mathbb{F}_{p}G))\cong(B(S)^{\times})^{G}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
; therefore
\Lambda
will be surjective over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, but not necessarily over extensions
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
. This is an edge case, as
p
-local structure is not relevant.

Recall that for any field extension
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have an identification
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)
. For the general case,
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
restricts to a homomorphism

\Lambda:\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)\to\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P),\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})\right)^{\prime},

since
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)=\{C\in\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\mid\mathcal{H}_{C}(1)=1\in\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})\}
. It suffices to determine the image of
\Lambda
in
\ker(\pi_{1})
, since the endotrivial complex
k[1]
satisfies
\Lambda(k[1])=-[G/G]\in(B(S)^{G})^{\times}
and
\mathcal{H}(k[1])=(1)_{P\in s_{p}(G)}
. Moreover, for any generator
C
not
k[1]
of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
(with respect to the basis of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)\cong CF_{b}(G)
associated to the real irreps of
G
),
C
has even h-marks, so the projection of
\Lambda(C)
onto
(B(S)^{G})^{\times}
is trivial.

From Corollary
2.14
,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
has at minimum
c(G)
generators, with one of those generators,
k[1]
, belonging to
\ker(\pi_{1})
. Set
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(G):=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)/\langle k[1]\rangle
, then we have a group homomorphism
\Lambda^{\prime}
induced from
\Lambda
as follows

\Lambda^{\prime}:\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(G)\to\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P),\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})\right)^{\prime}.

Clearly,
\Lambda^{\prime}
is surjective if and only if
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)\to O(T(\mathbb{F}_{p}G))
is surjective. Since
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(G)
has
\mathbb{Z}
-rank
c(G)-1
, the following statement follows easily.

Observation 5.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
k
any field of characteristic
p
. If the group of reduced coherent character tuples
\mathcal{R}_{G}
has a minimum generating set (with respect to size) of at least
c(G)
elements, then
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is not surjective.

However, explicitly determining the structure of
\mathcal{R}_{G}
is difficult in general.

5.1.
Cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups

In this section, we assume
k
is a field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
contained in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. In particular,
\operatorname{Aut}(k)
has a unique cyclic subgroup of order
p-1
. We turn to perhaps the most natural case to consider, groups with cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups, with our focus on the map
\Lambda^{\prime}
. First, we make an easy but critical observation: the coherence condition plays no significant role for
\Lambda^{\prime}
. This is an extension of
[
BC23
, Corollary 6.2]
, where the phenomenon is observed for groups with normal cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a group with cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
. Let
(\chi_{P})_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\in\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P),k^{\times})\right)^{\prime}
. Let
Q\leq S
be a subgroup of
S
and let
x\in N_{G}(Q)
have unique decomposition
x=x_{p}x_{p^{\prime}}=x_{p^{\prime}}x_{p}
into
p
- and
p^{\prime}
-elements. We have
\chi_{\langle x_{p}\rangle}(x)=1
and
x\in C_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle).
In particular,

\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P),k^{\times})\right)^{\prime}=\left(\prod_{P\in s_{p}(G)}\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/PC_{G}(P),k^{\times})\right)^{G}.

Proof.

By assumption,
x_{p}^{\prime}\in C_{G}(x_{p})
and hence
x_{p}^{\prime}\in C_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle)
. Obviously
x_{p}\in C_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle)
as well, therefore
x\in C_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle)
and
\chi_{\langle x_{p}\rangle}(x)=1
since
\chi_{\langle x_{p}\rangle}\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle)/C_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle),\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})
. Now, if
Q<Q\langle x_{p}\rangle=\langle x_{p}\rangle
, then the coherence condition implies
\chi_{Q}(x)=\chi_{\langle x_{p}\rangle}(x)=1
, which is redundant information since
x\in C_{G}(\langle x_{p}\rangle)
, hence
x\in C_{G}(Q)
as well.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Recall (see
[
Swa59
]
) that for
p
odd, the groups with periodic cohomology over fields of characteristic
p
are precisely those with cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups. If
G
has cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup of order
p^{r}
, then
G
has period
2\Phi(S)
, where
\Phi(S)
denotes the number of automorphisms of
S
that are given by conjugation by elements of
G
, i.e. the order of
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)\cong N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
. Moreover, we have an inclusion

N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\cong C_{(p-1)p^{r-1}}.

In particular,
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)
is cyclic. Since
S
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup,
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)
is a
p^{\prime}
-group, therefore
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)\hookrightarrow O_{p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Aut}(S))\cong C_{p-1}
. It follows that

\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S),k^{\times})\cong N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S),

as the homomorphisms all factor as endomorphisms of
N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
followed by an inclusion
N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)\hookrightarrow k^{\times}
, with
O_{p^{\prime}}(k^{\times})\cong C_{p-1}
. Since
N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
is cyclic, its group of endomorphisms identifies with itself.

In fact, for any nontrivial
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
contained in
S
, we have a similar inclusion
N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)\leq\operatorname{Aut}(P)
with
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)
contained in the largest
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(P)
, which again is isomorphic to
C_{p-1}
.

On the other hand, every automorphism of
S
induces an automorphism on
P
, and elementary number theory implies every automorphism of
P
arises in this way. Therefore, we have a surjective group homomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}(S)\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)
with kernel a
p
-subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. In particular, the composition

N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(S)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(P)

is injective as well. We have a commutative diagram as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{G}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{G}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}(P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)}

Because the composition
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)
is injective, it follows that the dotted arrow is also injective. In particular, we have an inequality

[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]\leq[N_{G}(P):C_{G}(P)].

On the other hand, Burnside’s fusion theorem (see
[
AKO11
, Proposition 4.5]
) implies that
\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)=\mathcal{F}_{S}(N_{S}(G))
, therefore any automorphism
\varphi\in\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)
is of the form
c_{g}
for some
g\in N_{G}(S)
. Thus the map
\operatorname{Aut}_{S}(G)\to\operatorname{Aut}_{S}(P)
is surjective as well, so for all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
P
of
S
, we have an isomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)
. We summarize this now.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
be a
p
-group with cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
. For all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
P
of
S
, we have an isomorphism

\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)

induced by restriction of
c_{g}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(S)
. Moreover,
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)
is a cyclic
p^{\prime}
-group and if
p
is odd,
G
has period
2\Phi(S)
.

Proof.

If
p
is odd, this is proven in Remark
5.5
. Otherwise if
p=2
, this trivially holds since in this case,
N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
is a
2^{\prime}
-group but
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}\mathbb{Z})
is a
2
-group, hence
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)
is trivial for any
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
.
∎

We now will prove
\Lambda^{\prime}
, hence
\Lambda
, is surjective. The proof will rely on induction on the order of Sylow
p
-subgroups: we provide the base case as an individual lemma.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
be a finite group with Sylow
p
-subgroups having order
p
. Then
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective.

Proof.

It suffices to show
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)\to\mathcal{R}_{G}
is surjective. Let
S
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. By Proposition
5.4
,
\mathcal{R}_{G}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S),\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})
, since the 1-term in
\mathcal{R}_{G}
(which is trivial) has no effect. Moreover, from Remark
5.5
,
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S),k^{\times})\cong N_{G}(S)/C_{G}(S)
. Hence
|\mathcal{R}_{G}|=[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]
as well.

From the classification of endotrivial complexes,
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
is generated by
k[1]
and a three-term complex
C
with h-mark 2 at the trivial subgroup 1 and h-mark 0 at all other
p
-subgroups. Therefore,
C
generates
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)^{\prime}.
Explicitly, we have
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{S}C\simeq kS\to kS\to k
, a truncated periodic resolution of
k
as
kS
-module - however
\mathcal{H}_{C}(S)\neq k
unless
[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]=1
.

Since
G
is periodic with period
2[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]
, there is a chain complex
X
that is a truncated periodic resolution of the trivial
kG
-module
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
2[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]+1
nonzero terms. It follows that
X
is endotrivial with h-mark
2[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]
at 1 and
0
at
S
. Additionally,
X\in\ker(\Lambda^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)^{\prime}
, and in fact
X
must generate
\ker(\Lambda^{\prime})
, since another shorter complex generating
\ker(\Lambda^{\prime})
would contradict the period of
G
. Finally
[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]\cdot C=X\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)^{\prime}
, as the h-marks of these complexes agree. Therefore we have an injective group homomorphism
\Lambda^{\prime}:\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)^{\prime}/\langle X\rangle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}_{G}
, and since both sides are finite groups of the same order, we conclude
\Lambda^{\prime}
is surjective, as desired.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group with a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
, and let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
contained in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is surjective.

To prove the more general case, we first make a few observations. Let
G
be a group with cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups of order at least
p^{2}
. Let
S
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and let
C\leq S
be the unique subgroup of order
p
. Then for any subgroup
Q
satisfying
C\leq Q\leq S
, we have
N_{G}(C)\geq N_{G}(Q)\geq N_{G}(S)
, and therefore
N_{G}(Q)/C_{G}(Q)=N_{N_{G}(C)}/C_{N_{G}(C)}(Q)
, and restriction induces an equality
\mathcal{R}_{G}=\mathcal{R}_{N_{G}(C)}
. Similarly, restriction induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)\cong\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(N_{G}(C))
(this holds for any group containing
S
). We have a commutative diagram as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(N_{G}(C))^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{R}_{N_{G}(C)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{G}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{N_{G}(C)}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

Therefore,
\Lambda_{G}
is surjective if and only if
\Lambda_{N_{G}(C)}
is surjective, and we can reduce to the case where
G
has a normal cyclic subgroup
C
of order
p
. Indeed,
G
is balanced if
N_{G}(C)
is balanced. Moreover, if
N_{G}(C)
is balanced, then
N_{G}(C)/C
is balanced.

Proof (of Theorem
5.8
).

We first show that if
G
is balanced, then
\Lambda
is surjective. It suffices to show that
\Lambda^{\prime}:\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)\to\mathcal{R}_{G}=\mathcal{R}_{G,k}
is surjective, and we may assume
G
has a normal cyclic subgroup
C
of order
p
. We induct on the order of the Sylow
p
-subgroup
S\geq C
. The case
|S|=1
is trivial and the case
|S|=p
is Lemma
5.7
.

Under the assumption that
C
, observe that inflation induces a split injective group homomorphism
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/C)\to\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
with retraction induced by the Brauer construction. This affords a decomposition

\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)=\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/C}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/C)\times\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G),

where
\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
is generated by the unique “faithful” endotrivial complex
C
with
h_{C}(1)=2
and
h_{C}(P)=0
for all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
P
of
G
. We remark that this is a misnomer with regards to the language of
[
Mil24a
]
, as for
C
to be faithful, we would require
C(P)\cong k[0]
for all
P
, where in this case we have
C(P)\cong k_{\omega}[0]
for some
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})
. However, it is easy to see that there is a linear character
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})
such that
C\otimes_{k}k_{\omega}[0]
is faithful in the sense of
[
Mil24a
]
.

Now, we characterize the images of
\Lambda^{\prime}
restricted to
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/C)
and
\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
. By induction,
\Lambda^{\prime}:\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/C)^{\prime}\to\mathcal{R}_{G/C}
is surjective, since the assumption on
G
holds for
G/C
as well. We have an inflation map
\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/C}:\mathcal{R}_{G/C}\to\mathcal{R}_{G}
. We claim its image is precisely the subgroup
\mathcal{R}^{C}_{G}
of
\mathcal{R}_{G}
given by tuples
(\chi_{P})_{P}\in s_{p}(G)
for which
\chi_{C}
is trivial. It is easy to see the image of inflation is contained in
\mathcal{R}_{G}^{C}
. On the other hand, for any
p
-subgroup of
G
containing
C
, inflation sends a homomorphism
\chi_{P/C}\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G/C}(P/C)/C_{G/C}(P/C),k^{\times})
to
\chi_{P}\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P),k^{\times})
. This map is an isomorphism since
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(P)\cong\operatorname{Aut}_{G/C}(P/C)
, therefore
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G/C}(P/C),k^{\times})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P),k^{\times})
and inflation is injective, hence surjective too. By induction, we have a surjective homomorphism

\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/C)\xrightarrow{\mathcal{R}_{G/C}}\mathcal{R}_{G}^{C}.

Now observe that the
\mathcal{R}^{C}_{G}
has a complement
\Delta_{C}\mathcal{R}_{G}\leq\mathcal{R}_{G}
, which consists of all coherent character tuples
(\chi_{P})_{P\in s_{p}(G)}
for which
\chi_{1}=1
,
\chi_{C}=\omega
for some
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C),k^{\times})
, and for all
P\geq C
,
\chi_{P}=\omega|_{N_{G}(P)}
. It follows that
\mathcal{R}^{C}_{G}\cong\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C),k^{\times})
, hence
\Delta_{C}\mathcal{R}_{G}
has order
[N_{G}(C):C_{G}(C)]=[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]
by Proposition
5.6
. On the other hand, we have
\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)=\langle C\rangle
for an endotrivial complex
C
with projectives in degrees 1 and 2, and the
k
-dimension one module
k_{\omega}
for some
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})
in degree 0. Observe the image
\Lambda(C)
is a tuple
(\chi_{P})_{P\in s_{p}(G)}
for which
\chi_{1}=1
,
\chi_{C}=\omega
, and for all
P\geq C
,
\chi_{P}=\omega|_{N_{G}(P)}
. Since the periodicity of
G
is
n:=2\Phi(S)=2[N_{G}(S):C_{G}(S)]
, as
C^{\otimes\Phi(S)}
is a minimal length truncated periodic resolution of
k_{\omega}
with homology again
k_{\omega}
, the image
\Lambda(C)
in fact generates
\Delta_{C}\mathcal{R}_{G}
, as desired.

∎

5.2.
Groups of
p
-rank at least two

On the other hand, there exist groups
G
with small Sylow
p
-subgroups with
p
odd for which,
\Lambda
is not surjective. In fact, such groups need not have particularly complex Sylow
p
-subgroups, as the next example demonstrates.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
k/\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a field extension. Then there exists a finite group
G
with normal Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
elementary abelian of rank two such that
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is not surjective.

Proof.

For
p>2
, the symmetric group
S_{2p}
has elementary abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups, since the
p
-part of
(2p)!
is
p^{2}
. We choose our Sylow to be
S:=\langle(1,2,\dots,p),(p+1,p+2,\dots,2p)\rangle
. Set
a=(1,2,\dots,p)
,
b=(p+1,p+2,\dots,2p)
,
A:=\langle a\rangle
and
B:=\langle b\rangle
. We fix
G:=N_{S_{2p}}(S)
. We have that
G\cong(S\rtimes(C_{p-1}\times C_{p-1}))\rtimes C_{2}
, where for instance we may choose
C_{2}=\langle(1,p+1)(2,p+2)\dots(p,2p)\rangle
, although this choice is not unique. Generators of the copies of
C_{p-1}
corresponding to
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
depend on
p
on the other hand. Then
A
and
B
are
G
-conjugate and all other cyclic subgroups of
S
are
G
-conjugate. We have
N_{G}(A)=N_{G}(B)\cong S\rtimes(\operatorname{Aut}(A)\times\operatorname{Aut}(B))
, and if
C
denotes any of the other
p-1
cyclic subgroups of
S
(i.e.
\langle ab\rangle,\langle ab^{2}\rangle,\dots
) then
N_{G}(C)\cong(S\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(C))\rtimes C_{2}
, where
C_{2}
is generated by an involution
\sigma
satisfying
{}^{\sigma}a=b^{n}
, where
C=\langle ab^{n}\rangle
. Note
N_{G}(A)\trianglelefteq G
with
G/N_{G}(A)\cong C_{2}
.

We claim that
\mathcal{R}_{G}
has at least
c(G)=3
generators, which will imply by Observation
5.3
that
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is not surjective. First, it is clear that
\mathcal{R}_{G}
has at least two generators, corresponding to generators for
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(A)/C_{G}(A),k^{\times})
and
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C),k^{\times})
. Indeed, straightforward computations verify that these groups are nontrivial, with both
N_{G}(A)/C_{G}(A)\cong C_{p-1}
and
N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C)\cong C_{p-1}
, therefore
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(A)/C_{G}(A),k^{\times})\cong C_{p-1}
and
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C),k^{\times})\cong C_{p-1}
. Note there are no nontrivial coherence conditions implied from the trivial subgroup hom-set
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(1)/C_{G}(1),k^{\times})
.

Note the following homomorphism is well-defined with kernel containing
C_{G}(S)
:

\chi_{S}:G\to k^{\times},\,x\mapsto\begin{cases}-1&x\not\in N_{G}(A)\\
1&x\in N_{G}(A)\end{cases}

It follows that the pair
(\chi_{P})_{P\in s_{p}(G)}
with
\chi_{P}\equiv 1
for
P\neq S
and
\chi_{S}
as above is
G
-stable and satisfies the coherence conditions for all cyclic subgroups of
S
. Therefore, it remains to show that
G\setminus N_{G}(A)
is not determined by coherence conditions, and we do this by explicitly determining the coherence conditions for
G
arising from each cyclic subgroup of
S
. For
A
and
B
,
N_{G}(A)=N_{G}(B)=S\rtimes(\operatorname{Aut}(A)\times\operatorname{Aut}(B))
, so any coherence conditions corresponding to
A
or
B
only affect
N_{G}(A)
. Let
C:=\langle ab^{n}\rangle
for
n\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
, then
N_{G}(C)\cong(S\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(C))\rtimes C_{2}
, where
C_{2}
is generated by an involution
\sigma
satisfying
{}^{\sigma}a=b^{n}
.

The coherence condition states that for
x=x_{p}x_{p^{\prime}}\in N_{G}(C)
, we have an equality
\chi_{C}(xC)=\chi_{P\langle x_{p}\rangle}(xP\langle x_{p}\rangle)
. If
x_{p}\in C
, then the coherence condition is therefore trivial. On the other hand, if
x_{p}\in S\setminus C
, we know
x_{p^{\prime}}\in C_{G}(x_{p})
by definition) and
x_{p^{\prime}}\in N_{G}(C)
since
x,x_{p}\in N_{G}(C)
as well. Thus,
x_{p^{\prime}}\in C_{N_{G}(C)}(x_{p})
.

We claim that when
x_{p}\in S\setminus C
, then
C_{N_{G}(C)}(x_{p})=S
. Indeed, if
x_{p}\in A
or
x_{p}\in B
, then
C_{G}(x_{p})\cong S\rtimes C_{p-1}
with
C_{p-1}=\operatorname{Aut}(B)
or
C_{p-1}=\operatorname{Aut}(A)
respectively, and no nontrivial element of
\operatorname{Aut}(A)
or
\operatorname{Aut}(B)
centralizes
C
. Otherwise, if
x_{p}\in\langle ab^{m}\rangle
for some
m\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}
and
m\neq n
, then,
C_{G}(x_{p})\cong S\rtimes C_{2}
, where
C_{2}
is generated by an involution
\sigma
satisfying
{}^{\sigma}a=b^{m}
. However, because
C=\langle ab^{n}\rangle
, it follows that
\sigma
does not normalize
C
, thus in all cases we have
C_{N_{G}(C)}(x_{p})=S
.

Therefore, for any
x=x_{p}x_{p^{\prime}}\in N_{G}(C)
, if
C\langle x_{p}\rangle=S
, then
x_{p^{\prime}}=1
, since
x_{p^{\prime}}\in C_{N_{G}(C)}(x_{p})
and is a
p^{\prime}
element. Thus, the coherence condition implies no new information. As we have considered every cyclic subgroup of
S
, we conclude that the coherence conditions do not imply anything about
x\not\in N_{G}(A)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}_{G}
has at least three generators, as desired, and
\Lambda
is not surjective.
∎

The previous example generalizes to groups with arbitrary large elementary abelian Sylow
p
-subgroups.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
k
a field of characteristic
p
, and
n\geq 2
an integer. Then there exists a finite group
G
with
p
-rank
n
such that
\Lambda:\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
is not surjective.

Proof.

We set
G:=(C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1})^{n}\rtimes S_{n}
, where each copy of
C_{p-1}
acts faithfully on the corresponding copy of
C_{p}
and
S_{n}
permutes the copies of
C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1}
in the obvious way. We regard
G
as a subgroup of
S_{np}
, with the copies of
C_{p}
generated by
(1,2,\dots,p),\dots,((n-1)p+1,\dots,np)
. First, it is clear that we have a
p
-subgroup (not necessarily Sylow)
E\cong C_{p}^{n}
, and straightforward computation shows that for every cyclic subgroup
C
of order
p
of
E
, we have
N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C)\cong C_{p-1}
. There are
n
conjugacy classes of cyclic subgroups of order
p
in
E
, corresponding to cycle type via the embedding
G\hookrightarrow S_{np}
. Therefore, to show
\Lambda
is not surjective for
G
, it suffices to show that that
\mathcal{R}_{G}
has at least one more generator.

Let
P:=\langle(1,\dots,p),(p+1,\dots,2p)\rangle\leq S_{np}
, and set
H:=N_{S_{2p}}(P)
. That is,
H
is isomorphic to the group considered in Theorem
5.9
. Explicitly,
H\cong(C_{p}\times C_{p-1})^{2}\rtimes C_{2}
, and
P
the unique Sylow
p
-subgroup of
H
. First, observe we have an isomorphism
N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P)\cong N_{H}(P)/C_{H}(P)
, hence the hom-sets associated to
P
for
\mathcal{R}_{G}
and
\mathcal{R}_{H}
coincide. Moreover, for any subgroup
C
of
P
, the values of any
\chi_{C}\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(C)/C_{G}(C),k^{\times})
are determined entirely on values in
N_{H}(C)
, since
N_{G}(C)=N_{H}(C)C_{G}(C)
. Therefore, any coherence conditions for the tuple
\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P),k^{\times})
in
\mathcal{R}_{G}
arise from coherence conditions in
\mathcal{R}_{H}
. It follows from Proposition
5.9
that
\mathcal{R}_{G}
has another generator, corresponding to the following
\chi_{P}\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P),k^{\times})
:

\begin{cases}\chi_{P}(x)=-1&x\in N_{G}(P)\setminus N_{G}(A)\\
1&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
A
denotes the cyclic subgroup generated by
(1,2,\dots,p)
under the inclusion
G\hookrightarrow S_{np}
.
∎

We expect that for “most” finite groups
G
with
p
-rank at least 2, the Lefschetz homomorphism is not surjective.

6.
Towards the kernel of the Lefschetz homomorphism

We conclude with a few results towards deducing the kernel of the Lefschetz homomorphism. The following proposition was partially observed by Bachmann
[
Bac16
, 5.50(iv)]
in the context of invertible Artin motives and with restricted hypotheses. We refer the reader to
[
BG23
]
for an explanation of the correspondence between Artin motives and the bounded homotopy category of
p
-permutation modules (and the corresponding “big” category, the derived category of permutation modules).

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume
p
is an odd prime. Let
G
be a finite group with a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup
S
, and
C\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
be an endotrivial complex with trivial homology. We have that
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
if and only if
h_{C}
admits only even values and for all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
P
of
S
, the congruence
h_{C}(1)\equiv h_{C}(P)\mod 2\Phi(S)
holds.

Proof.

We rely on Theorem
3.10
and the description of
\mathcal{R}_{G}
determined via Proposition
5.4
and Proposition
5.6
. For each proper subgroup
P
of
S
, let
C_{P}
denote the endotrivial complex with trivial homology and
h_{C_{P}}(Q)=2
for all
Q<P
and
h_{C_{P}}(Q)=0
otherwise. Set
C_{S}=k[1]
. Then
\{C_{P}\}_{1\leq P\leq S}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
, and we may write
[C]=a_{1}[C_{1}]+\dots+a_{S}[C_{S}]\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
. A routine computation using the h-mark functions associated to each
C_{P}
shows that the congruence
h_{C}(1)\equiv h_{C}(P)\mod 2\Phi(S)
holds for all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
P
of
S
if and only if
a_{Q}
is a multiple of
\Phi(S)
for all proper subgroups
Q
of
S
.

First, suppose
h_{C}
admits only even values and for all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
P
of
S
, the congruence
h_{1}(C)\equiv h_{P}(C)\mod 2\Phi(S)
holds. Then equivalently, under the decomposition
[C]=a_{1}[C_{1}]+\dots+a_{S}[C_{S}]\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
,
a_{Q}
is a multiple of
\Phi(S)
for all proper subgroups
Q
of
S
. Since
\mathcal{R}_{G}
is isomorphic to a direct product of finite groups by Proposition
5.4
, each of order
\Phi(S)
by Proposition
5.6
, the image of
C
is trivial in
\mathcal{R}_{G}
. Likewise, since
h_{C}
admits only even values, the image of
C
is trivial in
(B(S)^{G})^{\times}
as well, hence
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
.

Conversely, suppose
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
. It is easy to see that necessarily
h_{C}
must be even-valued. We induct on the order of
S
, in a similar manner to Theorem
5.8
. Again, it follows that
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
if and only if
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{N_{G}(H)}\in\ker(\Lambda)
, where
H
denotes a cyclic
p
-subgroup of order
p
, since
N_{G}(H)\geq N_{G}(P)
for any other
p
-subgroup
P\geq H
. Therefore, we may assume without loss of generality that
H
is normal. From the proof of Theorem
5.8
, we have a decomposition
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)=\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/H}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/H)\times\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
, with image in
\mathcal{R}_{G}
that intersects trivially. Let
[C]=a_{1}[C_{1}]+\dots+a_{S}[C_{S}]\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
. We have that
C_{1}\in\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
and
C_{P}\in\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{G/H}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/H)
for all nontrivial subgroups
P
of
S
. Since the images of
\operatorname{Inf}^{G}_{C}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G/C)
and
\partial\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
intersect trivially, both
a_{1}[C_{1}]
and
a_{H}[C_{H}]+\cdots+a_{S}[C_{S}]
belong to
\ker(\Lambda)
. By induction, it follows that
a_{P}
is a multiple of
\Phi(S)
for all nontrivial
P
.

On the other hand, by
[
Mil24a
, Lemma 5.8]
, it follows that
C_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}
is homotopy equivalent to an indecomposable chain complex contains only one non-projective indecomposable module
M
of
k
-dimension one. By the Green correspondence,
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{N_{G}(S)}M
is the trivial module if and only if
M
is the trivial module. Since the image of
C_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}
in
\mathcal{R}_{G}
is necessarily trivial,
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{N_{G}(S)}(M)
is trivial, hence
M
is trivial. Therefore
C_{1}^{\otimes a_{1}}
is homotopy equivalent to a truncated projective resolution of the trivial
kG
-module, hence it has length a multiple of the periodicity of
G
, which is
2\Phi(S)
. Thus,
a_{P}
is a multiple of
\Phi(S)
for all proper subgroups
P
of
S
, so
h_{1}(C)\equiv h_{Q}(C)\mod 2\Phi(S)
holds for all nontrivial
p
-subgroups
Q
of
S
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
be an endotrivial complex with trivial homology. If
p=2
, then
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
if and only if
h_{C}
is even-valued. If
p
is odd and
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
, then
h_{C}
admits only even values and for all pairs
K,H
of subgroups of
G
satisfying
[H:K]=p
and
K\leq H\leq N_{G}(K)
, the following congruence holds:

h_{K}(C)\equiv h_{H}(C)\mod 2|O_{p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Aut}_{N_{G}(K)}(H))|.

Proof.

If
p=2
, then if
C\in\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}(G)
,
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)=1
for any
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
. Therefore, it easily follows from Theorem
3.10
that
h_{C}
is even valued if and only if
C\in\ker(\Lambda)
.

If
p
is odd, similarly it is clear that
h_{C}
admits only even values. For the second term, it suffices to show it for the pair
1,H
, with
H
a subgroup of
G
of order
p
by replacing
C
with
C(K)
, as
C(K)\in\ker(\Lambda)
as well. For every subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
G
with Sylow
p
-subgroup
H
, we have a congruence
h_{C}(1)\equiv h_{C}(H)\mod 2\Phi(H^{\prime})
by Theorem
6.1
. Therefore, it remains to determine the least common multiple of all such
2\Phi(H^{\prime})=2|\operatorname{Aut}_{H^{\prime}}(H)|.
We claim this is the order of the largest
p^{\prime}
-subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(H)
. Indeed, every element of
\operatorname{Aut}_{H^{\prime}}(H)
is a
p^{\prime}
-element of
\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(H)
and conversely, every
p^{\prime}
-element
c_{g}\in\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(H)
falls in some
\operatorname{Aut}_{H^{\prime}}(H)
by setting
H^{\prime}:=H\rtimes\langle g\rangle
(if
g
is not a
p^{\prime}
-element, since
c_{g}
has
p^{\prime}
-order
g
may be replaced with a suitable
p^{\prime}
-element). Thus, we conclude
h_{1}(C)\equiv h_{H}(C)\mod 2|O_{p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{Aut}_{G}(H))|
.
∎

The question of whether the converse of Theorem
6.2
holds for odd primes
p
remains open. Given any endotrivial complex
C
, if
h_{C}(P)=0
for all cyclic
p
-subgroups
P
of
G
, then
h_{C}\equiv 0
. We ask if the same should hold for
\mathcal{H}_{C}
: if
\mathcal{H}_{C}(P)=1\in\operatorname{Hom}(N_{G}(P)/P,k^{\times})
for all cyclic
p
-subgroups
P
of
G
, does
\mathcal{H}_{C}\equiv 1
? If the answer to this question is affirmative, a complete determination of
\ker(\Lambda)
follows.

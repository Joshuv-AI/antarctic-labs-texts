The Phospholipase A2 Superfamily in Health and Disease: From Lipid Signaling to Inflammation, Cancer, and Neurodegeneration
Authors: Mohammad Mamun Sikder, Sanzida Akter Labony
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The phospholipase A2 (PLA2) superfamily comprises more than 50 enzymes that differ markedly in structure, catalytic mechanisms, and biological functions. Although all members share the ability to cleave acyl chains from membrane glycerophospholipids, many PLA2 enzymes catalyze additional reactions that influence membrane composition, lipid signaling, and cellular homeostasis. Through these activities, PLA2 enzymes contribute to the generation of free fatty acids, lysophospholipids, and downstream lipid mediators and participate in a wide range of physiological processes. Dysregulation of PLA2 activity has been associated with numerous pathological conditions, including inflammation, cancer, metabolic dysfunction, neurodegeneration, and tissue injury. This review summarizes the major PLA2 families, outlines their structural and biochemical features, and highlights emerging insights into their roles in health and disease.

INTRODUCTION

Phospholipase A2 (PLA2) enzymes were among the earliest lipid-modifying proteins to be described, originating from investigations into the membrane-disrupting properties of snake venoms conducted more than a century ago.1 These early observations initiated a long trajectory of research demonstrating that PLA2 enzymes are widely conserved across species and function as central regulators of membrane phospholipid metabolism and lipid mediator signaling.1234 Subsequent advances in biochemistry, structural biology, and lipidomics have revealed that PLA2s constitute a large and heterogeneous superfamily characterized by diverse catalytic strategies and biological activities.56789

Although all PLA2 enzymes share the capacity to cleave an acyl chain from glycerophospholipids (Fig. 1), mammalian PLA2s vary substantially in domain organization, substrate specificity, Ca2+ dependence, and subcellular localization. Based on these biochemical and structural features, PLA2s are classified into several major families: secreted phospholipase A2s (sPLA2s), cytosolic Ca2+-dependent phospholipase A2s (cPLA2s), Ca2+-independent patatin-like phospholipases (Ca2+-independent phospholipase A2 [iPLA2]/patatin-like phospholipase domain-containing protein [PNPLA] family), platelet-activating factor acetylhydrolases (PAFAHs), lysosomal PLA2s, and phospholipase A/acyltransferases (PLAATs) (Fig. 2).57810 Each family comprises multiple isoforms with distinct physiological roles, ranging from membrane remodeling and organelle homeostasis to immune regulation, host defense, and metabolic control.35911

PLA2-mediated hydrolysis liberates free fatty acids and lysophospholipids that function as potent signaling molecules or metabolic precursors. Polyunsaturated fatty acids, including arachidonic acid (AA), eicosapentaenoic acid, and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), released from membrane phospholipids are subsequently converted into eicosanoids and specialized pro-resolving mediators through cyclooxygenase (COX), lipoxygenase (LOX), or cytochrome P450 pathways.111213 In parallel, PLA2-generated lysophospholipids modulate membrane curvature, vesicular trafficking, receptor activity, and inflammatory signaling.914 Beyond their classical hydrolytic activity, several PLA2 family members also exhibit lysophospholipase, transacylase, neutral lipid lipase, or acyltransferase functions, further expanding the mechanistic diversity of this enzyme superfamily.81014

Genomic and clinical studies have linked dysregulation of specific PLA2 isoforms to a broad spectrum of human diseases, including cancer, atherosclerosis, neurodegenerative disorders, metabolic dysfunction, and acute or chronic inflammatory conditions.2361215 Accordingly, multiple PLA2 family members are being explored as potential disease biomarkers and therapeutic targets, underscoring their central importance in human physiology and pathology.3715

This review describes the major PLA2 enzyme families and examines their structural features, catalytic properties, and physiological roles. By integrating findings across multiple PLA2 subclasses, we highlight how PLA2-driven lipid metabolism shapes cellular function and contributes to diverse pathological processes.

sPLA2 FAMILY

sPLA2 represent the earliest PLA2 enzymes to be described, with their discovery tracing back to late 19th-century analyses of cobra venom.16 These enzymes were subsequently recognized as major effector components of the venoms of snakes, scorpions, and other reptiles. In mammals, the sPLA2 family has expanded to include twelve distinct isoforms—IB, IIA, IIC, IID, IIE, IIF, III, V, X, XIIA, XIIB, and XIV—each belonging to a group of low–molecular-weight proteins (~13–19 kDa) characterized by a rigid disulfide-bond framework that confers high structural stability (Table 1).17 A conserved Ca2+-binding loop and a His/Asp catalytic dyad define the enzymatic core of these proteins (Fig. 3), enabling efficient cleavage of the sn-2 acyl bond of extracellular phospholipids.1816

During catalysis, sPLA2 enzymes coordinate a Ca2+-associated water molecule, which is activated by the His/Asp dyad to nucleophilically attack the sn-2 carbonyl group of the phospholipid substrate. Simultaneously, Ca2+ stabilizes the reaction intermediate through interactions with the phosphate and carbonyl moieties of the substrate.1 Because sPLA2s function in the extracellular milieu, their enzymatic activity is strictly dependent on the presence of extracellular Ca2+.18

Despite their conserved catalytic machinery, sPLA2 isoforms exhibit pronounced variability in substrate preference. Group III, V, and X enzymes efficiently hydrolyze phosphatidylcholine (PC), whereas group II enzymes preferentially target phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylserine (PS), and phosphatidylglycerol (PG).19 This substrate selectivity underlies the potent bactericidal activity of sPLA2-IIA against PE- and PG-rich bacterial membranes.2021 Variability is also observed in sn-2 fatty acid selectivity: some isoforms display broad activity, whereas others, including sPLA2-V, IID, IIF, III, and X, preferentially release polyunsaturated fatty acids such as AA and DHA.10

1. Physiological functions of sPLA2 isoforms

Each sPLA2 isoform exerts distinct biological effects through its ability to liberate bioactive lipids, remodel membrane phospholipids, degrade exogenous lipid substrates, or modify extracellular lipid structures such as lipoproteins and pulmonary surfactants (Table 1).910 In addition, sPLA2s can hydrolyze phospholipids contained within extracellular vesicles or damaged mitochondria, thereby releasing fatty acids with signaling or immunomodulatory potential.222324 Many sPLA2 family members display direct antibacterial activity, particularly against gram-positive bacteria, with a reported potency ranking of group IIA > X > V > XII > IIE > IB/IIF.2526 For example, group V sPLA2 is enriched within macrophage phagosomes, implicating this isoform in microbial clearance.27

The expression of multiple sPLA2 isoforms increases markedly during inflammatory conditions, including rheumatoid arthritis, sepsis, COVID-19, cardiovascular disease, and neurodegenerative disorders.2829 In septic states, circulating sPLA2-IIA levels can rise by several thousand-fold.2130 Proinflammatory cytokines, including interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, as well as innate immune receptors such as Toll-like receptors and NOD-like receptors, induce sPLA2-IIA expression—primarily through nuclear factor κB signaling—across a broad range of cell types, including endothelial, hepatic, renal, muscular, platelet, macrophage, and glial cells.21 For this reason, sPLA2-IIA has been widely investigated as both a disease biomarker and a potential therapeutic target.

Beyond their roles in host defense and inflammation, sPLA2 enzymes contribute to pulmonary surfactant remodeling, promote the generation of proinflammatory eicosanoids (e.g., prostaglandins, thromboxanes, and leukotrienes), modify low-density lipoproteins into more atherogenic forms, and stimulate the production of cytokines and second messengers, including IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α, and cAMP.3132 These combined activities highlight their central roles in shaping inflammatory and metabolic responses.

sPLA2 enzymes link extracellular phospholipid metabolism to disease by generating distinct bioactive lipid mediators in an isoform- and tissue-specific manner.32 Through hydrolysis of membrane phospholipids, sPLA2s release lysophospholipids—particularly lysophosphatidylcholine—and free fatty acids, including AA and DHA. AA serves as a precursor for proinflammatory eicosanoids, such as prostaglandins, thromboxanes, and leukotrienes, which drive inflammation, bronchoconstriction, and immune activation in conditions including arthritis and asthma.32 In contrast, DHA is metabolized into specialized pro-resolving mediators, including resolvins, which suppress excessive immune responses and facilitate the resolution of inflammation. In metabolic tissues, sPLA2-mediated release of oleic and linoleic acids modulates lipoprotein remodeling and macrophage polarization, thereby influencing obesity and insulin sensitivity.3233 Together, these pathways position sPLA2s as extracellular lipid editors that translate local phospholipid composition into disease-relevant lipid mediator signaling.

sPLA2-IB (PLA2G1B)

Loss of sPLA2-IB protects rodents from diet-induced obesity, diabetes, and atherosclerosis, indicating an important role for this enzyme in lipid absorption and metabolic homeostasis.34 Mice deficient in sPLA2-IB also exhibit resistance to dextran sulfate sodium–induced colitis, an effect associated with enhanced preservation of intestinal stem cells and reduced inflammatory infiltration.35

sPLA2-IIA (PLA2G2A)

sPLA2-IIA serves as an important biomarker for multiple human diseases, including cardiovascular disorders, sepsis, cholangiocarcinoma, and chronic allograft failure.3637 Functionally, this isoform drives AA release and reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation through COX- and LOX-dependent pathways,36 participates in innate host defense,33 and promotes Th2 immunity via B1a-cell activation.38 In addition, sPLA2-IIA contributes to pulmonary fibrosis and atherosclerosis,39 enhances glucose utilization through UCP1 induction in brown fat,40 modulates placental ischemia,41 suppresses gastrointestinal tumorigenesis but supports pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma survival,42 shapes gut microbiota with systemic immunological consequences,43 and participates in ocular surface inflammation. Notably, inhibition of sPLA2-IIA has been shown to attenuate neuroinflammation-associated neurodegeneration. In the context of progranulin deficiency, elevated sPLA2-IIA activity enhances membrane phospholipid hydrolysis, resulting in increased circulating prostaglandins—predominantly PGE2 and PGD2—and unsaturated free fatty acids. This lipid excess amplifies inflammation, gliosis, and lysosomal dysfunction. Pharmacological inhibition of sPLA2 using LY333013, a broad sPLA2 inhibitor, or KH064, a more selective sPLA2-IIA inhibitor, suppresses phospholipid hydrolysis, normalizes lipid mediator levels, and markedly rescues neuroinflammatory and lysosomal phenotypes.44 These findings demonstrate that targeting lipid-releasing enzymes can rebalance inflammatory lipid signaling and restore cellular homeostasis in progranulin-associated neurodegeneration.45

sPLA2-IID (PLA2G2D)

sPLA2-IID exerts anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects, reducing obesity, insulin resistance, and chronic inflammation46 In cancer, this isoform enhances angiogenesis and glycolytic reprogramming in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)47 and correlates with immune infiltration and prognosis in cervical carcinoma.48 sPLA2-IID also promotes skin tumor growth by suppressing anti-tumor T helper 1 immune responses.49

sPLA2-IIE (PLA2G2E)

sPLA2-IIE regulates hair-follicle homeostasis,50 modulates lipoprotein lipid composition, and influences adipose and hepatic lipid storage via ERK/HSL signaling.51 In the central nervous system (CNS), sPLA2-IIE-derived 15-HETrE supports neural repair after ischemic injury.52

sPLA2-IIF (PLA2G2F)

This isoform contributes to epidermal hyperplasia and psoriatic pathology,1753 while simultaneously conferring host protection against Plasmodium infection.54

sPLA2-III (PLA2G3)

sPLA2-III plays an essential role in male fertility by supporting epididymal sperm maturation.55 It also functions as a mast-cell activator that contributes to anaphylaxis and atherosclerosis,56 and its dysregulation has been linked to colon, ovarian, and lung cancer.5758 In addition, sPLA2-III protects against asthma by reducing airway hyperresponsiveness59 and promotes blood–brain barrier disruption during meningitis.60

sPLA2-V (PLA2G5)

sPLA2-V protects against metabolic dysfunction by limiting obesity and insulin resistance51 and has been associated with benign flecked retina.61 This isoform enhances Th2 immune responses,62 promotes macrophage phagocytosis via LPE generation,63 and regulates β-cell proliferation and insulin secretion.64

sPLA2-X (PLA2G10)

This isoform reduces adipose lipid accumulation,65 promotes cancer-cell proliferation in soft-tissue leiomyosarcoma,666768 contributes to fertility,69 influences adrenal steroidogenesis,70 and regulates insulin secretion through COX-2–dependent pathways.71

sPLA2-XIIA (PLA2G12A)

sPLA2-XIIA exhibits antibacterial activity against Helicobacter pylori and other gram-negative pathogens.72 Loss of this enzyme increases cancer-cell invasiveness and correlates with prognosis in early-stage colorectal carcinoma.73

sPLA2-XIIB (PLA2G12B)

sPLA2-XIIB plays a central role in hepatic very-low-density lipoprotein assembly and secretion, protecting against hepatic steatosis.747576 In addition, this isoform has emerged as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for cholangiocarcinoma.37

cPLA2 FAMILY

The cPLA2 family consists of 6 isoforms—cPLA2α, cPLA2β, cPLA2γ, cPLA2δ, cPLA2ε, and cPLA2ζ—which are widely expressed in multiple organs, including the liver, pancreas, brain, testis, and heart (Table 2).77 The first isoform to be identified, cPLA2α, was discovered in human platelets and neutrophils in 1986.16 Each cPLA2 family member contains 2 major functional domains connected by a flexible linker region: an N-terminal Ca2+-dependent lipid-binding C2 domain and a C-terminal catalytic “lipase” domain that mediates enzymatic activity (Fig. 4).78

cPLA2 enzymes are activated by elevations in intracellular Ca2+, which promote C2-domain–mediated membrane association and substrate engagement.78 The catalytic domain contains a conserved Ser/Asp dyad that is essential for phospholipid hydrolysis (Fig. 4).10 Full enzymatic activity requires the coordinated function of both domains. In addition to their canonical PLA2 activity, cPLA2 isoforms also exhibit lysophospholipase and transacylase activities.798081 These enzymes display strong selectivity for arachidonate-containing phospholipids, hydrolyzing substrates in the following order: PC > phosphatidylinositol > PE > phosphatidic acid > PS.8 When PC serves as the substrate, the preference for sn-2 fatty acids follows the following hierarchy: arachidonic (20:4) > linolenic (18:3) > linoleic (18:2) > oleic (18:1) > palmitoleic (16:1).880

1. Physiological functions

cPLA2α (group IVA PLA2) is the most extensively studied member of the cPLA2 family and exhibits a strong preference for arachidonate-containing phospholipids. Through its capacity to liberate AA, cPLA2α contributes to the generation of bioactive lipid metabolites involved in inflammation, cancer progression, and neurological disorders.67677 Upon activation, cPLA2α translocates from the cytosol to PC-rich membranes of the Golgi apparatus and endoplasmic reticulum. This process is tightly regulated by phosphorylation events mediated by several protein kinases, including mitogen-activated protein kinases.778283 In Alzheimer’s disease, amyloid-β activates cPLA2α, promoting AA release and subsequently accelerating amyloid-β accumulation.7984 cPLA2α also induces tau hyperphosphorylation, leading to cytoskeletal instability and impaired synaptic function. Given its central involvement in Alzheimer’s disease pathogenesis, inhibition of cPLA2α is considered a promising therapeutic strategy.8085

Other cPLA2 isoforms exhibit distinct enzymatic properties. cPLA2β (group IVB PLA2) contains a truncated N-terminal JmjC domain (Jumonji C domain) upstream of the C2 domain and displays PLA1, PLA2, and lysophospholipase activities.7480 cPLA2γ (group IVC PLA2) lacks the canonical C2 domain but retains PLA2, lysophospholipase, and transacylase activities.7581 cPLA2ε catalyzes the Ca2+-dependent transfer of an acyl chain from glycerophospholipids to PE, generating N-acylphosphatidylethanolamine (NAPE), which is subsequently converted to N-acylethanolamines (NAEs)—lipid mediators with potent anti-inflammatory effects during tissue ischemia and skin injury.8182838687888990 Finally, cPLA2ζ exhibits both PLA1 and PLA2 activities without marked preference for specific fatty acid substrates.8491 Together, these enzymes participate in a wide range of physiological and pathological processes, underscoring the functional diversity of the cPLA2 family.

cPLA2α (PLA2G4A)

cPLA2α is a central regulator of lipid signaling and influences numerous biological processes across diverse tissues. In inflammatory conditions such as ulcerative colitis, cPLA2α is required for the generation of proinflammatory lipid mediators that sustain disease activity. Elevated or dysregulated cPLA2α activity has also been associated with the development and progression of several cancers, including lung, liver, breast, and colon malignancies, in which AA-derived lipid mediators support tumor growth, angiogenesis, and immune evasion.92939495 Beyond inflammation and cancer, cPLA2α plays important physiological roles. Its enzymatic activity supports normal ovulation and fertilization, underscoring its contribution to reproductive function.8396 In the liver, cPLA2α helps coordinate lipid metabolism with inflammatory signaling, thereby influencing hepatic lipid accumulation and metabolic homeostasis.97 In the kidney, cPLA2α contributes to the regulation of renal blood flow, which is essential for maintaining normal renal function.98

cPLA2α also participates in vascular and immune responses. By promoting platelet aggregation at sites of vascular injury, it supports effective hemostasis and clot formation.99 Concurrently, cPLA2α enhances the recruitment of innate immune cells, including neutrophils and macrophages, to sites of tissue damage, thereby supporting inflammatory responses and tissue repair.100

Within the CNS, cPLA2α has been implicated in brain injury, spinal cord injury, and neurodegenerative disorders. One of its key pathogenic mechanisms involves the induction of lysosomal membrane permeabilization, which disrupts neuronal homeostasis and promotes neuronal damage.79101102103 cPLA2α activity has also been associated with synaptic dysfunction and neuronal alterations observed in chronic depression.101102103 Given its prominent role in neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration, reducing PLA2G4A enzymatic activity is actively being explored as a potential therapeutic strategy for Alzheimer’s disease.85

cPLA2β (PLA2G4B)

cPLA2β (group IVB PLA2) contains a distinctive N-terminal JmjC regulatory domain and exhibits phospholipase A1, phospholipase A2, and lysophospholipase activities.80 Although less extensively studied than cPLA2α, cPLA2β has been implicated in immunometabolic regulation. In monocytes, increased cPLA2β expression reduces cellular energy metabolism, suggesting a role in modulating metabolic–inflammatory interactions.104 These findings indicate that cPLA2β may participate in cellular lipid remodeling and inflammatory regulation through mechanisms distinct from those of cPLA2α.

cPLA2γ (PLA2G4C)

cPLA2γ (group IVC PLA2) lacks the canonical Ca2+-dependent C2 domain present in other cPLA2 family members but retains robust PLA2, lysophospholipase, and transacylase activities.81 Functionally, this isoform is involved in nuclear membrane remodeling during oocyte maturation, establishing a role in reproductive biology.105 cPLA2γ also induces hepatic lipid droplet accumulation106 and contributes to viral biology by supporting hepatitis C virus replication and assembly.107108 Importantly, this isoform acts during niacin-mediated phospholipid remodeling and signal transduction, regulating the action of receptors and downstream pathways.109 Tumor cells can survive under stress by inhibiting apoptosis,110 which may contribute to cancer progression.

cPLA2δ (PLA2G4D)

cPLA2δ (group IVD PLA2) exhibits substantially higher PLA1 than PLA2 activity and is markedly upregulated in psoriatic skin lesions. Elevated expression of this isoform contributes to epidermal barrier regulation and keratinocyte homeostasis, suggesting a role in chronic skin inflammation and tissue remodeling.111112 These properties indicate that cPLA2δ participates in cutaneous immune signaling and may serve as a molecular link between lipid metabolism and skin pathology.

cPLA2ε (PLA2G4E)

cPLA2ε (group IVE PLA2) catalyzes the Ca2+-dependent transfer of acyl groups from glycerophospholipids to PE, generating NAPE, a precursor of NAEs.87888990 These NAEs exert potent anti-inflammatory and cytoprotective effects, particularly during tissue ischemia and skin injury. Beyond its enzymatic role in NAE biosynthesis, cPLA2ε regulates membrane trafficking through clathrin-independent endocytosis113 and has been implicated in neurological and psychiatric conditions, including panic disorder.114 In inflammatory settings, cPLA2ε contributes to the production of anti-inflammatory NAEs in psoriatic tissue,115 increases serotonin synthesis in interferon (IFN)-γ–stimulated intestinal epithelial cells,116 and improves learning and memory deficits in Alzheimer’s disease models.117 cPLA2ε also inhibits GLUT4 translocation in skeletal muscle, thereby reducing insulin-stimulated glucose uptake,118 and appears to play dual roles in ischemia by generating cytoprotective NAEs while also promoting lysosomal membrane permeabilization and necroptosis under severe stress.90119

cPLA2ζ (PLA2G4F)

cPLA2ζ exhibits both PLA1 and PLA2 activities with minimal specificity for the sn-2 fatty acid. Knockdown of PLA2G4F in fibroblasts decreases oleic acid release, demonstrating its role in fatty acid remodeling. It also mediates Ca2+-dependent AA release from mitochondria, where it is converted to epoxyeicosatrienoic acids, conferring cardioprotective effects against heart failure.91

Ca2+-iPLA2 (PNPLA) FAMILY

The Ca2+-2iPLA2 enzymes, also known as patatin-like phospholipase domain-containing lipases (PNPLA1–9), share a conserved patatin domain that was first identified in patatin (iPLA2α) from potato (Table 3).10 PNPLAs contain a lipase consensus sequence (GXSXG, where X denotes any amino acid) and a putative ATP-binding motif. Their active site resides within the patatin-like (α/β) fold and includes a Ser–Asp catalytic dyad and an oxyanion hole (Fig. 5).120 The catalytic serine is located within the GXSXG motif, whereas the aspartate typically occurs within an Asp-Gly-Ala/Gly motif found in many lipases. During catalysis, the nucleophilic hydroxyl group of serine forms a covalent acyl–enzyme intermediate with the substrate, while aspartate acts as a general acid/base to facilitate substrate binding and turnover. The oxyanion hole, positioned near the catalytic dyad, stabilizes the transition state during substrate hydrolysis.120 None of the PNPLA enzymes requires Ca2+ for catalytic activity.

Although classified as PLA2s, several PNPLAs exert additional activities, including lysophospholipase, transacylase/acyltransferase, neutral lipid lipase, and retinyl-ester hydrolase functions. Generally, PNPLA members with extended N-terminal regions (PNPLA6–9) act primarily on phospholipids as PLA1/PLA2 or lysophospholipases, whereas PNPLA1–5 preferentially target neutral lipids as lipases or transacylases.10121 Notably, PNPLA3 and PNPLA4 display retinyl-esterase activity and contribute to retinol metabolism.121122

1. Physiological functions

Skin barrier function

PNPLA1 catalyzes the ω-O-esterification step in acylceramide biosynthesis, generating acylceramides that are essential for epidermal barrier integrity and protection against transepidermal water loss and pathogen entry.123 PNPLA1 deficiency disrupts linoleoyl ω-O-esterification of ceramides, resulting in severe barrier defects and aberrant keratinocyte differentiation, thereby underscoring its central role in skin homeostasis.

Metabolic regulation

PNPLA2 and PNPLA3 are highly expressed in adipose tissue and liver. PNPLA2 (also known as ATGL) expression increases during fasting, whereas PNPLA3 expression rises with feeding, indicating opposing roles in lipid handling.124 PNPLA2/ATGL functions as the rate-limiting lipase for triacylglycerol (TAG) hydrolysis in adipose tissue, muscle, and liver.124 By mobilizing free fatty acids from TAGs, PNPLA2 supports energy metabolism; however, when dysregulated, it has been associated with insulin resistance, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), and cancer progression. PNPLA3—particularly the I148M variant—alters TAG, retinyl ester, and lysophosphatidic acid metabolism, leading to hepatic lipid accumulation and contributing to the development and progression of NAFLD, nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma.125126127

Hepatic phospholipid/choline metabolism

PNPLA7 and PNPLA8 participate in hepatic PC breakdown, producing endogenous glycerophosphocholine and choline.128 PNPLA7, also known as neuropathy target esterase–related esterase, functions as a lysophospholipase whose expression increases during fasting and decreases with feeding in mouse skeletal muscle, reflecting regulation by insulin, glucocorticoids, and catecholamines.129 Mice lacking PNPLA7 develop hypoglycemia, hypolipidemia, and reduced energy expenditure, demonstrating its importance in systemic energy homeostasis. PNPLA7 also influences hepatic very-low-density lipoprotein secretion through interactions with apolipoprotein E, and perturbations in this pathway may contribute to metabolic dysfunction. PNPLA8 supports mitochondrial integrity by hydrolyzing cardiolipin; altered PNPLA8 activity compromises mitochondrial function in liver and skeletal muscle and increases susceptibility to apoptosis. PNPLA3 similarly contributes to the maintenance of hepatic triglyceride balance by coordinating triglyceride storage and secretion.130

Autophagy and lipid signaling

PNPLA5 (GS2-like) exhibits TAG hydrolase activity and is required for optimal initiation of autophagy.131 PNPLA5 deficiency is associated with elevated serum cholesterol, TAGs, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, abnormal bleeding, and reduced male fertility due to testicular apoptosis.132 Conversely, PNPLA5 deficiency has also been reported to protect against NAFLD by modulating inflammatory signaling, improving mitochondrial function, and increasing high-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels.133

Neurological functions

PNPLA6 (neuropathy target esterase) is widely expressed in neurons during postnatal development and later becomes enriched in the cerebral cortex, olfactory bulb, pons, thalamus, hypothalamus, and medulla oblongata.134135136 PNPLA6 hydrolyzes PC and lysophosphatidylcholine to maintain membrane homeostasis. Inhibition or mutation of PNPLA6 has been linked to organophosphate-induced delayed neuropathy and broader neurodegenerative processes, highlighting its essential role in neuronal development and brain function.126137 Mutations in PNPLA6 cause autosomal recessive syndromes, including Boucher–Neuhauser, Gordon–Holmes, Oliver–McFarlane, and Laurence–Moon syndromes, and—together with PNPLA8 deficiency—are associated with neurodegeneration characterized by movement disorders and cognitive impairment.120138139

PNPLA9, ferroptosis, and disease

PNPLA9 releases AA, linoleic acid, and lysophospholipids, which are subsequently converted into prostaglandins (e.g., PGE2 and PGD2), leukotrienes (e.g., LTB4 and LTC4), and hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acids in immune cells. These lipid mediators promote macrophage and T-cell activation, monocyte chemotaxis, ROS generation, and cytokine production, thereby linking PNPLA9 activity to autoimmune diseases—including type 1 diabetes, multiple sclerosis, and rheumatoid arthritis—as well as atherosclerosis, vascular injury, and chronic inflammation. Conversely, PNPLA9 also contributes to the generation of EPA- and DHA-derived specialized pro-resolving mediators, highlighting its dual role in both the initiation and resolution of inflammatory responses.140

Dysregulation of PNPLA9 has been associated with Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, Karak syndrome, infantile neuroaxonal dystrophy, and other neurological disorders.120140141142143 PNPLA9 preferentially hydrolyzes oxidized polyunsaturated fatty acid–containing phospholipids, a property that is particularly relevant to ferroptosis. Functionally, PNPLA9 acts as an anti-ferroptotic regulator, attenuating pro-ferroptotic signaling and influencing cell death, autophagy, and senescence.144145146 Because ferroptosis contributes to a wide range of pathologies—including neurological, oncologic, cardiovascular, and renal diseases—PNPLA9 represents a critical node linking lipid remodeling to disease progression. Beyond the nervous system, PNPLA9 also participates in cell growth and migration, autophagy, insulin secretion, bone formation, and vascular contractility and relaxation.120145146147

PAFAHs

PAFAHs were initially purified as enzymes capable of hydrolyzing the acetyl group at the sn-2 position of platelet-activating factor (PAF; 1-O-alkyl-2-acetyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine), a potent phospholipid mediator involved in a wide range of physiological and pathological processes.148 In mammals, 3 types of PAFAHs have been identified: 1 plasma form and 2 intracellular forms—plasma PAFAH, PAFAH2, and PAFAH1B.148149

PAFAH1B is a multimeric complex composed of 2 catalytic subunits, PAFAH1B2 (α1) and PAFAH1B3 (α2), and a regulatory, non-enzymatic subunit, PAFAH1B1 (β).10150 Unlike many PLA2 enzymes, PAFAHs do not require Ca2+ for catalysis. Members of the PAFAH family share a conserved α/β-hydrolase fold and utilize a Ser–His–Asp catalytic triad, in contrast to the Ser–Asp dyad characteristic of most PLA2s (Fig. 6).16

In human plasma, approximately 70% of plasma PAFAH associates with low-density lipoprotein, with the remaining fraction bound to high-density lipoprotein.148 Accordingly, plasma PAFAH is also referred to as lipoprotein-associated PLA2. PAFAHs hydrolyze the acetyl group at the sn-2 position of PAF, generating acetic acid and 1-alkyl-2-lysophosphatidylcholine. Both plasma PAFAH and PAFAH2 preferentially hydrolyze phospholipids containing short sn-2 acyl chains, such as propionyl (C3) and butyryl (C4) groups.

They also act on oxidatively modified phospholipids bearing succinyl, glutaryl, 5-oxovaleroyl, or azelaoyl substituents,148151 while exhibiting minimal activity toward phospholipids with 2 long acyl chains. In addition, both enzymes can hydrolyze short-chain diacylglycerols and TAGs and display PLA1 activity. Plasma PAFAH and PAFAH2 show high affinity for F2-isoprostane–containing phospholipids, although their catalytic rates are slower than those observed for PAF.152 Plasma PAFAH also hydrolyzes phospholipid hydroperoxides and exhibits transacetylase activity, whereas PAFAH2 hydrolyzes ω-3 fatty acid epoxides and displays dual hydrolase–transacetylase activity.153 In contrast, PAFAH1B selectively hydrolyzes phospholipids containing short acetyl chains.

1. Physiological functions

PAF (1-O-alkyl-2-acetyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine) is a potent phospholipid messenger that functions as a strong chemoattractant and leukocyte activator and participates in key biological processes, including inflammation, anaphylaxis, reproduction, and fetal development.148151 Although originally identified in plasma, plasma PAFAH has since been detected in other extracellular fluids, such as milk and urine, particularly under pathological conditions.154 Dysregulated plasma PAFAH activity has been associated with multiple disease states, and inhibitors targeting this enzyme are currently under evaluation for the treatment of cardiovascular disorders.148 Accumulating evidence indicates that PAFAH1B1 contributes to tumor initiation and cancer progression.155156157 PAFAH2 has recently been identified as an enzyme responsible for generating oxidized ω-3 fatty acids that are required for immunoglobulin E (IgE)–antigen–induced mast-cell activation.148 In Pafah2−/− mast cells, levels of these oxidized lipids are markedly diminished; supplementation with ω-3 epoxide species effectively restores the defective phenotypes observed in PAFAH2−/− cells.156 The major physiological roles of PAFAH family members are summarized below.

Plasma PAFAH (PLA2G7)

Plasma PAFAH plays an important role in cardiovascular physiology, and its dysregulation has been associated with myocardial infarction, stroke, cardiomyopathy, cerebral hemorrhage, and atherosclerosis.148 Beyond cardiovascular disease, plasma PAFAH has also been linked to multiple sclerosis, polycystic ovary syndrome, and acute pancreatitis.148 Notably, plasma PAFAH appears to exert a protective effect in neonatal necrotizing enterocolitis.157 Elevated plasma PAFAH activity has been associated with colon tumorigenesis,158 and abnormalities in this enzyme have also been reported in type 1 diabetes, asthma, and several renal disorders.159

PAFAH2 (PLA2G7B)

PAFAH2 is responsible for generating oxidized ω-3 fatty acids—including 17,18-EpETE and 19,20-EpDPE—that function as key lipid mediators in mast cells following IgE–antigen stimulation.148 This enzyme also protects cells from oxidative stress-induced apoptosis.148 In addition, PAFAH2 plays a critical role in suppressing synchronized ferroptosis, thereby mitigating acute kidney injury,160 and it participates in the regulation of pulmonary vascular remodeling.161 Collectively, these findings highlight PAFAH2 as a multifunctional regulator of lipid signaling and cellular stress responses.

PAFAH1B2 (PAFAH1α1, PLA2G8A)

PAFAH1B2 has been implicated in neurological, metabolic, and reproductive physiology. Deficiency of this subunit reduces amyloid-β production in experimental models of Alzheimer’s disease.162 Male mice lacking PAFAH1B2 exhibit increased body weight as a consequence of reduced testosterone levels.163 Disruption of PAFAH1B2 function also results in infertility, impaired spermatogenesis, and increased apoptosis within seminiferous tubules.148 In cancer biology, PAFAH1B2 promotes metastasis in pancreatic cancer,155 whereas its loss suppresses ovarian cancer cell proliferation, migration, and tumorigenicity while promoting apoptosis.164 These diverse roles underscore the importance of PAFAH1B2 in both tissue homeostasis and disease progression.

PAFAH1B3 (PAFAH1α2, PLA2G8B)

PAFAH1B3 contributes to neuronal development by regulating gene-expression programs required for proper brain function.165 Downregulation of this subunit suppresses the progression of several cancers through modulation of epithelial–mesenchymal transition pathways.166167168169 In addition, PAFAH1B3 plays a critical role in oocyte biology, particularly in spindle formation and meiotic progression during bovine oocyte maturation.170 Together, these findings demonstrate that PAFAH1B3 participates in both developmental processes and cancer regulation.

α/β-HYDROLASE DOMAIN-CONTAINING PROTEINS (ABHDs)

α/β-Hydrolase domain–containing proteins constitute a large and diverse superfamily comprising more than 20 members that share a characteristic α/β-hydrolase fold.171 Their catalytic machinery typically consists of a nucleophile (Ser, Asp, or Cys), a His residue, and an acidic residue (Asp or Glu), together with 2 to 3 residues that form an oxyanion hole.171172 Most ABHD proteins contain a GXSXG lipase motif and a conserved His–XXXX–Asp sequence associated with acyltransferase activity (Fig. 7).173174 Consequently, ABHD family members encompass enzymes with a wide range of catalytic activities—including lipase, acyltransferase, amidase, esterase, protease, thioesterase, and epoxide hydrolase functions—making them one of the most functionally versatile protein families.171172

1. Physiological functions

Mammalian ABHD proteins participate in a wide range of biological processes, including lipid metabolism, intracellular signaling, metabolic regulation, inflammation, and carcinogenesis.165176 Among these proteins, ABHD6 is one of the most extensively studied members and has been implicated in inflammation, tumorigenesis, adipogenesis, cold-induced thermogenesis, and insulin secretion in pancreatic islets.166167168169170171177178179180181182 Together with monoacylglycerol lipase and ABHD12, ABHD6 contributes to the hydrolysis of the endocannabinoid 2-arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG), which is converted into AA and glycerol in the CNS.172183 Because 2-AG signaling regulates immune responses, appetite, and pain perception, ABHD6-mediated regulation of monoacylglycerol hydrolysis plays essential roles in pancreatic β cells, macrophages, neurons, and white adipocytes.173184

Cancer development is closely linked to inflammation, angiogenesis, and dysregulated cell proliferation, processes in which aberrant endocannabinoid signaling involving ABHD6 and ABHD12 may contribute.173184 ABHD6 is overexpressed in several highly lethal human cancers, including prostate, colorectal, lung, breast, and pancreatic tumors, and pharmacological inhibition of ABHD6 suppresses NSCLC progression. ABHD6 also exhibits bis(monoacylglycero)phosphate (BMP) hydrolase activity in the liver; consequently, ABHD6 inhibition increases hepatic BMP levels and disrupts lysosomal lipid sorting within late endosomes and lysosomes.174175176185186187

ABHD11 is essential for cell survival and embryonic stem cell (ESC) development. Loss of ABHD11 induces cell death and disrupts ESC proliferation and differentiation, whereas ABHD11 overexpression prevents apoptosis. These effects are mediated, at least in part, through alterations in GSK3β/β-catenin and extracellular signal-regulated kinase signaling pathways.177188 ABHD5 has also been implicated in autophagy regulation and colorectal cancer development.178189 ABHD2 is frequently overexpressed in breast, ovarian, and prostate cancers, where it enhances cell proliferation and migration through modulation of Akt, p70S6K, and JNK signaling pathways. Elevated ABHD2 expression also increases free fatty acid production and activates β-oxidation in hepatocellular carcinoma, thereby promoting cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis.179180190191 In contrast, ABHD2 exerts protective effects in asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease by inhibiting PI3K/Akt signaling, reducing airway smooth muscle migration, and preventing pulmonary emphysema.181182192193 ABHD2 is additionally required for macrophage infiltration into atherosclerotic lesions183194 and regulates endoplasmic reticulum calcium release.184195

ABHD1 may suppress ROS generation by inhibiting NADPH oxidase activity.160171185196 In addition, ABHD6, ABHD16A, and ABHD17A/B/C function as S-depalmitoylases for N-Ras.186187197198 Protein palmitoylation—the covalent attachment of fatty acids such as palmitate to cysteine (and, less commonly, serine or threonine) residues—is essential for proper protein trafficking and signal transduction. Depalmitoylation of N-Ras by ABHD proteins may attenuate aberrant Ras signaling and thereby counteract tumor development.188199

ABHD16A also regulates antiviral immunity by modulating the palmitoylation–depalmitoylation cycle of interferon-induced transmembrane (IFITM) proteins. This activity is conserved across human, porcine, and murine IFITM proteins and is required for their correct intracellular localization and antiviral function.189200

ABHD5 (also known as CGI-58), although lacking intrinsic hydrolase activity, serves as a key co-activator of ATGL/PNPLA2, the rate-limiting enzyme of triglyceride hydrolysis.190191201202203 Loss-of-function mutations in ABHD5 cause Chanarin–Dorfman syndrome, which is characterized by the accumulation of neutral lipids in multiple organs.

In the reproductive system, ABHD2 is abundantly expressed in the ovary and plays critical roles in follicular development and regulation of the female estrous cycle.192204 ABHD2 deficiency disrupts normal cyclicity, prolongs the estrous phase, shortens the luteal phase, and produces polycystic ovarian morphology; these abnormalities can be partially reversed by gonadotropin treatment. ABHD2 also interacts with progesterone and contributes to sperm chemotaxis and the acrosome reaction.193205

Several ABHD proteins are essential for neurological function. Mutations in ABHD12 cause PHARC syndrome, a rare neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cataracts, retinitis pigmentosa, polyneuropathy, hearing loss, and ataxia.194206 ABHD12 metabolizes phytanic acid, and its loss results in toxic accumulation of phytanic acid and related lipids, leading to progressive neurodegeneration.195207 Inhibition of endocannabinoid hydrolases—particularly ABHD12—has been proposed as a strategy to reduce neuroinflammation and ameliorate symptoms in multiple sclerosis and related neurological disorders.194196206208 ABHD12 preferentially degrades lysophosphatidylserine, thereby maintaining membrane lipid homeostasis and normal microglial and neuronal function; dysfunction of this pathway results in neurodegeneration.200

LYSOSOMAL PLA2s

1. PLA2G15

PLA2G15 is ubiquitously expressed and localized to lysosomes and late endosomes. This enzyme exhibits optimal activity at acidic pH, is Ca2+ independent, and functions as both an acyltransferase and an acidic phospholipase.209 PLA2G15 contains a signal peptide and a consensus serine hydrolase lipase motif (AXSXG), in which serine, aspartate, and histidine form the catalytic triad.209 PLA2G15 hydrolyzes both sn-1 and sn-2 acyl chains and thereby contributes to lysosomal phospholipid catabolism. Documented substrates include PC, PE, PG, and PS, with minimal activity toward phosphatidylinositol or sphingomyelin.209210 In addition, PLA2G15 can serve as a 1-O-acyl-ceramide synthase to produce acylceramide.211

Functionally, PLA2G15 is required for autophagic cargo processing within lysosomes212 and is essential for generating the lysosome-specific lipid BMP by producing lysophosphatidylglycerol, a BMP precursor.187210 Intriguingly, recent studies suggest that PLA2G15 also functions as a BMP hydrolase, as genetic ablation or pharmacological inhibition of PLA2G15 results in elevated BMP levels.210213214 Increased BMP enhances lysosomal lipid degradation and promotes cholesterol efflux, a mechanism that is particularly relevant in lysosomal storage disorders, where modulation of the PLA2G15–BMP axis improves lysosomal function and alleviates neurodegenerative pathology. Together, PLA2G15 and BMP form a regulatory lipid–enzyme pair that fine-tunes lysosomal metabolism and cellular homeostasis. Although the precise role of PLA2G15 in BMP turnover remains incompletely defined, deletion of PLA2G15 induces lysosomal phospholipidosis in alveolar macrophages, characterized by surfactant phospholipid accumulation, reduced presentation of lysophospholipid antigens to CD1d on invariant natural killer T cells, and impaired adaptive T-cell immunity to Mycobacterium.214 Moreover, inhibition of PLA2G15—secondary to glycerophosphodiester accumulation in CLN3 deficiency—has been implicated in Batten disease (juvenile neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis).215216 PLA2G15-deficient mice also show defective IFN-γ release and increased apoptotic sensitivity to oxidized low-density lipoprotein,214 and PLA2G15 appears atheroprotective in murine models.217

2. Peroxiredoxin 6 (Prdx6)

Prdx6, also known as acidic Ca2+-independent 2PLA2, is localized to the cytosol, lysosomes and lysosome-related organelles, and the extracellular space.218 Its PLA2 activity is modest at cytosolic pH but increases substantially following phosphorylation, under acidic conditions, or in the presence of oxidized phospholipid substrates. Although the precise lysosomal functions of Prdx6 remain to be fully elucidated, its context-dependent activation suggests a role in redox-lipid crosstalk within acidic intracellular compartments.

PLAAT (HRASLS) FAMILY

The PLAAT (also known as HRASLS) family was initially identified as a group of tumor suppressors that antagonize RAS signaling; all members exhibit Ca2+-independent enzymatic activity.9 The family includes PLAAT1 (HRASLS1, A-C1), PLAAT2 (HRASLS2, FLJ20556), PLAAT3 (PLA2G16, AdPLA, HREV107), PLAAT4 (RARRES3, TIG3, HRASLS4, RIG1), and PLAAT5 (HRASLS5, HRLP5, iNAT).10 Although all 5 members are present in humans, only PLAAT1, PLAAT3, and PLAAT5 are expressed in rodents. PLAATs and lecithin retinol acyltransferase share a cysteine-centered catalytic triad, in which cysteine serves as the nucleophile within a conserved NC domain that interacts with 2 histidine residues—except in PLAAT1, where 1 histidine is replaced by asparagine (Fig. 8).9219 All family members except PLAAT5 contain a C-terminal hydrophobic segment that is important for subcellular localization.

PLAAT enzymes display several Ca2+-independent phospholipid-metabolizing activities, notably NAPE-forming N-acyltransferase (NAT), PLA1/PLA2, and O-acyltransferase activities.9220221 They cleave sn-1 or sn-2 ester bonds of phospholipids (predominantly PC) and generally prefer sn-1 over sn-2, except PLAAT5, which shows no positional preference.222223224 By synthesizing NAPE, PLAATs provide precursors for anti-inflammatory NAEs.9220 In vitro, PLAAT1/2/5 exhibit strong NAT activity. PLAAT1 also shows high O-acyltransferase activity,220225226227228 whereas PLAAT3 exhibits higher PLA1/PLA2 activity.220225226227 Overexpression of any PLAAT elevates NAPE/NAE in cells, indicating that all family members can function as NATs in vivo.9220 For instance, PLAAT5 markedly increases anandamide and other polyunsaturated NAEs in U2OS cells.229 Collectively, Ca2+-independent PLAATs help maintain basal NAPE pools across tissues.220229230

1. Physiological roles

Among PLAAT family members, only PLAAT1, PLAAT3, and PLAAT5 are expressed in rodents, with PLAAT1 and PLAAT3 being the most extensively studied. PLAAT1 is highly expressed in skeletal muscle, brain, heart, and testis in both humans and rodents, whereas PLAAT3 is ubiquitously expressed but enriched in adipocytes, where it contributes to antilipolytic activity in adipose tissue.231232 In HEK293 cells, PLAAT1 overexpression increases cardiolipin content through PC:MLCL transacylase activity and localizes to the outer mitochondrial membrane.233 Excessive PLAAT1 expression remodels mitochondrial phospholipids, leading to mitochondrial fragmentation and loss of peroxisomes in cultured cells; however, the physiological relevance of these mitochondrial alterations in vivo—particularly in muscle and heart—remains unclear.234 Interestingly, PLAAT1-deficient mice exhibit reduced fat accumulation in the liver and skeletal muscle through mechanisms that appear to be independent of its mitochondrial functions.235

PLAAT enzymes also participate in developmental organelle remodeling. PLAAT1 is required for lens organelle degradation in zebrafish, whereas PLAAT3 mediates this process in mice, consistent with species-specific differences in PLAAT1 expression in the lens.236 Overexpression studies demonstrate that PLAAT3 induces peroxisome loss through its intrinsic phospholipase activity in HEK293 cells, and that the protein translocates to lysosomes during lysosomal membrane permeabilization.234236237238239 Together, these findings highlight a conserved role for PLAAT enzymes in regulating the structure and turnover of mitochondria, peroxisomes, and lysosomes.234236239240 Beyond organelle biology, PLAAT3 modulates RAS signaling via its PLA/acyltransferase activity by reducing the pool of palmitoylated H-RAS, thereby decreasing levels of active GTP-bound H-RAS and attenuating downstream signaling pathways.241242

PLAAT1 (HRASLS1, A-C1)

PLAAT1 plays a critical role in developmental organelle clearance. In zebrafish, PLAAT1 is required for lens organelle degradation, underscoring its involvement in tissue remodeling during development.236 The marked mitochondrial morphological alterations and peroxisomal depletion induced by PLAAT1 overexpression in HEK293 cells are consistent with its potent phospholipase-mediated remodeling capacity.234 Upregulation of PLAAT1 also promotes viral replication in cultured cells, suggesting a potential role in host–virus interactions.243 In vivo, PLAAT1 promotes lipid storage, as evidenced by reduced hepatic and muscular fat accumulation in PLAAT1-deficient mice.235

PLAAT2 (HRASLS2, FLJ20556)

PLAAT2 exhibits strong organelle-remodeling activity, and its overexpression in HEK293 cells results in pronounced peroxisome loss.237 Although its role in peroxisomal membrane turnover and phospholipid remodeling is less well defined than that of other family members, accumulating evidence supports a functional contribution of PLAAT2 to organelle dynamics.

PLAAT3 (PLA2G16, AdPLA, HREV107)

PLAAT3, also known as AdPLA, is a key regulator of adipose metabolism. Mice with reduced PLAAT3 expression develop severe lipodystrophy, marked insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, and increased lipolysis, highlighting its essential role in adipocyte homeostasis.231232 In cellular systems, overexpression of PLAAT3 promotes peroxisomal degradation through its phospholipase activity.234237 PLAAT3 also functions as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting cell proliferation and inducing apoptosis in kidney, breast, and ovarian cancers.244 In contrast, elevated PLAAT3 expression enhances host defense during viral infection by modulating innate immune responses.245 Paradoxically, the overexpression of PLAAT3 is related to progression in some cancers, including osteosarcoma, prostate cancer, HER2+ breast cancer, as well as metastatic lung disease, indicating its context-dependent roles in tumor biology.246247248

PLAAT4 (RARRES3, TIG3, HRASLS4, RIG1)

PLAAT4 exerts diverse immunological and tumor-suppressive functions. It acts as a chemoattractant that recruits immune cells to sites of inflammation, thereby contributing to host defense.249250251 In cancer, PLAAT4 inhibits cell proliferation and promotes apoptosis in prostate, breast, and ovarian cancer cells.252253 PLAAT4 also functions as an antimicrobial effector by restricting the growth of viruses and parasites.254 Through its interaction with type I transglutaminase, PLAAT4 supports epidermal barrier integrity, ameliorates keratinization disorders such as psoriasis, and reduces hyperproliferative skin lesions.255256257 In cell-cycle regulation, PLAAT4 inhibits centrosome separation and suppresses mitotic progression.258 Notably, PLAAT4 interacts with amyloid precursor protein and enhances microglial clearance of amyloid-β, thereby reducing amyloid plaque burden in experimental models of Alzheimer’s disease.259

PLAAT5 (HRASLS5, HRLP5, iNAT)

PLAAT5 regulates inflammatory responses by controlling the biosynthesis of anti-inflammatory NAEs, including palmitoylethanolamide (PEA) and arachidonoylethanolamide (AEA).229260 As a Ca2+-independent NAT, PLAAT5 generates NAPE species that are subsequently converted into NAEs, which activate peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-α and cannabinoid receptor 1 to suppress inflammatory gene expression. Loss of PLAAT5 markedly reduces NAE levels and exacerbates inflammatory responses, as demonstrated in toxin-induced testicular inflammation characterized by elevated IL-6, TNF-α, and inducible nitric oxide synthase expression. Restoration of PEA or AEA attenuates this inflammation, highlighting PLAAT5-derived NAEs as endogenous lipid mediators that restrain tissue inflammation and promote immune homeostasis.260261 Collectively, these features position PLAAT5 at the intersection of lipid signaling, reproductive biology, and tissue-specific lipid homeostasis.

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE PERSPECTIVES

The PLA2 superfamily comprises a remarkably diverse group of enzymes whose functions extend well beyond their classical role as phospholipid hydrolases (Table 4). Although most PLA2 enzymes share the capacity to cleave membrane glycerophospholipids, it is now evident that individual isoforms differ substantially in domain organization, substrate specificity, regulatory mechanisms, and downstream biological effects. These distinctions help explain how PLA2 enzymes support essential physiological processes—such as membrane remodeling, lipid signaling, and organelle homeostasis—while also contributing to pathology when their activity is dysregulated.

Despite decades of intensive investigation, important gaps in our understanding remain. For many PLA2 isoforms, particularly within the PNPLA, ABHD, lysosomal PLA2, and PLAAT families, endogenous substrates and dominant functions in specific cell types have not been clearly defined. A central unresolved question is how the same enzyme can exert protective effects in one tissue yet promote inflammation, degeneration, or tumor progression in another, highlighting the importance of cellular and tissue context in shaping PLA2 function.

Another major knowledge gap concerns the direct involvement of PLA2 enzymes in regulated cell-death pathways, including ferroptosis, necroptosis, apoptosis, and lysosomal membrane permeabilization. Although emerging evidence links selected PLA2 isoforms to these processes, the mechanistic relationships among phospholipid remodeling, lipid peroxidation, and execution of cell death remain incompletely understood. Elucidating how individual PLA2 enzymes influence these pathways represents a critical research priority, particularly in the context of neurodegenerative and metabolic diseases.

From a translational perspective, progress has been constrained by the limited availability of highly selective, isoform-specific tools. Many currently available PLA2 inhibitors exhibit incomplete specificity or off-target effects, complicating efforts to assign precise biological roles or to rigorously assess therapeutic potential. The development of selective inhibitors, targeted degraders, and activity-based probes will therefore be essential to disentangle overlapping functions and to enable robust evaluation of PLA2 enzymes as drug targets.

Finally, emerging technologies—including advanced lipidomics, spatial transcriptomics, and organelle-resolved imaging—provide unprecedented opportunities to map PLA2 activity with spatial and temporal resolution. Integrating these approaches with conditional genetic models will be crucial for defining when, where, and how individual PLA2 isoforms act during disease initiation and progression.

In summary, future research should move beyond broad functional categorizations and instead focus on isoform-specific mechanisms, physiologically relevant lipid substrates, and context-dependent roles in cell fate and disease. Addressing these challenges will not only deepen our understanding of PLA2 biology but also establish a clearer framework for targeting lipid metabolism in human disease.

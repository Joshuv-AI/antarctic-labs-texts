Hecke reciprocity and class groups
Ari Shnidman and Artane Siad

Abstract
. When the C 3 C_{3} -extensions F = K ⁡ ( n 3 ) F=K(\sqrt[3]{n}) of K = ℚ ⁡ ( − 3 ) K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3}) are ordered by the norm of n ∈ 𝒪 K n\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K} , we show that the average size of Cl F ​ [ 2 ] {\rm Cl}_{F}[2] is 3 / 2 3/2 , confirming a prediction of Cohen–Martinet. Over ℚ {\rm\mathbb{Q}} , we prove that as F F varies over the pure cubic fields ℚ ⁡ ( n 3 ) {\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n}) that are tamely (resp. wildly) ramified at 3 3 , the average size of Cl F ​ [ 2 ] {\rm Cl}_{F}[2] is 3 / 2 3/2 (resp. 2 2 ). This tame/wild dichotomy is not accounted for by the Cohen–Lenstra type heuristics in the literature. Underlying this phenomenon is a reciprocity law for the relative class group Cl F / K ​ [ 2 ] {\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2] of odd degree extensions of number fields F / K F/K . This leads to a random model for Cl F / K ​ [ 2 ] {\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2] that recovers Malle’s heuristics for Cl F / K ​ [ 2 ] \mathrm{Cl}_{F/K}[2] in families of K K -extensions with fixed Galois group. More generally, we formulate a heuristic for families with a fixed Galois K K -group that explains the aberrant behavior in the family ℚ ⁡ ( n 3 ) {\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n}) and predicts similar behavior in other special families. The other main ingredient in our proof is the work of Alpöge–Bhargava–Shnidman, on the number of integral G ⁡ ( ℚ ) G({\rm\mathbb{Q}}) -orbits in a G G -invariant quadric with bounded invariants.

1.1.
Hecke reciprocity

Let
F/K
be an odd degree extension of number fields, and let
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}={\rm Hom}({\rm Cl}_{F/K},{\rm\mathbb{Q}}/{\rm\mathbb{Z}})
. Class field theory gives a bijection from
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
to the set of unramified extensions
F(\sqrt{t})/F
of square norm, i.e. with
{\rm Nm}_{F/K}(t)\in K^{\times 2}
. Roughly speaking, Hecke reciprocity constrains the number of ramified primes that are inert in such an extension
F(\sqrt{t})/F
.

To state it precisely, let
{\rm Disc}_{F/K}\subset{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
and
{\rm Diff}_{F/K}\subset{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
be the discriminant and different ideals of
F/K
, and define the
Hecke ideal

H_{F/K}:={\rm Disc}_{F/K}\mathrm{Diff}_{F/K}^{-1}\subset{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}.

As
F/K
has odd degree, the norm of
H_{F/K}
is a square (as an
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
-ideal). Moreover,
a well-known theorem of Hecke
[
Wei74
, pg. 291]
, says that
[H_{F/K}]
is a square in
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}
.

Definition 1.4
.

We say
F/K
is Hecke ramified if
H_{F/K}
is not the square of an
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
-ideal.

Write
H_{F/K}=\prod_{w}w^{h_{w}}
for the decomposition of
H_{F/K}
into prime powers.

Definition 1.5
.

A prime
w
of
F
, lying over a prime
v
of
K
, is a Hecke prime for
F/K
if
h_{w}
is odd and if, locally,
w
is inert in some unramified quadratic extension of
F\otimes_{K}K_{v}
of square norm.

Thus, a Hecke prime
w
is a witness to the fact that
F/K
is Hecke ramified. The secondary condition rules out primes
w
that split in all extensions
F(\sqrt{t})\in{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
for local reasons. One checks that
F/K
is Hecke ramified if and only if
F/K
admits a Hecke prime (Lemma
3.2
).
Moreover, if
{\rm Disc}_{F/K}
has valuation
1
at some prime
v
of
K
, then there is a unique Hecke prime
w
above
v
. This shows that a “random” odd degree extension
F/K
has many Hecke primes.

With this definition in hand, the Hecke reciprocity law states:

Theorem 1.6

(Hecke reciprocity)
.

Let
F/K
be an odd degree extension of number fields and let
F(\sqrt{t})/F
be an everywhere unramified quadratic extension of square norm. Then the number of Hecke primes
w
of
F
that are inert in
F(\sqrt{t})
is even.

Theorem
1.6
follows quickly from Hecke’s theorem (see §
3.2
). Its relevance to class group heuristics is that it causes “unexpected congruence conditions” on elements of
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
. For example, if
F
is a cubic field in which a prime
p
splits as
(p)={\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}^{2}{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
and
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
is the only Hecke prime for
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
, then Theorem
1.6
implies that for all
F(\sqrt{t})\in{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[2]
, the prime
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
splits in
F(\sqrt{t})
, even though there is no good local reason for this!
This explains the dichotomy in Theorem
1.2
: when
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
, the field
F_{n}
is wildly ramified at
3
and has no Hecke primes, whereas for
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
the field
F_{n}
is tamely ramified and admits exactly one Hecke prime, the prime
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
above
(3)={\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}^{2}{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
. In the tame case, Hecke reciprocity systematically obstructs the non-trivial unramified local square-class
t
at
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
from appearing in a global unramified square-class. Since there are two unramified square-classes at
{\rm\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
, we should expect half as many non-trivial classes of
{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[2]
in the tame family compared to the wild family, which indeed follows from Theorem
1.2
.

1.2.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

We view
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
as a Selmer group for the
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-module

M=\ker\left({\rm Res}^{F}_{K}\mu_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu_{2}\right)

of order
2^{[F\colon K]-1}
, where
{\rm Res}^{F}_{K}\mu_{2}
is the Weil restriction. More precisely, class field theory gives an isomorphism from
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
to the
unramified
2
-Selmer group
of
F/K
, which is the subgroup

{\rm Sel}^{\mathrm{un}}_{2}(F/K)\subset H^{1}(K,M)\subset F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}

of everywhere unramified classes
t\in H^{1}(K,M)
(see Theorem
2.1
).
This endows
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
with a symmetric bilinear pairing coming from the cup product

(\,,\,)\colon H^{1}(K,M)\times H^{1}(K,M)\longrightarrow H^{2}(K,\mu_{2})[2]={\rm Br}(K)[2].

Key to our proof is the notion of a
quadratic refinement
of
(\,,\,)
, which is by definition a quadratic map
q\colon H^{1}(K,M)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
such that
(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y)
.

When
F/K
is cubic, we show that there exists a quadratic refinement
q=\sum_{v}q_{v}
with the following property: for any Hecke prime
w\mid v
of
F
, and for any non-trivial
t\in H^{1}_{{\rm un}}(K_{v},M)
, we have
{\rm inv}_{v}(q_{v}(t))=1/2
, where
{\rm inv}_{v}
is the isomorphism
{\rm Br}(K_{v})[2]\simeq{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
. It follows from reciprocity in the Brauer group that for each
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
, the number of Hecke primes inert in
F(\sqrt{t})
is even. This gives a purely algebraic proof of Theorem
1.6
(and Hecke’s theorem as well) for cubic extensions. It is this connection with Brauer groups that is needed in our proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.
Namely, we exploit the fact that for Kummer extensions
F_{n}=K(\sqrt[m]{n})
, there is a natural choice of quadratic refinement: the map
q^{n}\colon H^{1}(K,M_{n})\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
sending
[t]
to the class of the quadratic form
\frac{1}{m}{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(tx^{2})
in
H^{1}(K,\mathrm{SO}(m))\simeq{\rm Br}(K)[2]
.

When
m=3
, we
attempt
to parameterize the elements of
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n}/K)
, as follows. The space
V={\rm Sym}^{3}(K^{2})\otimes K^{2}
of pairs of binary cubic forms has a natural action by
G={\rm SL}_{2}^{2}/\mu_{2}
. The ring
K[V]^{G}
is generated by two invariants
A_{1}
and
A_{3}
, and the Galois module
M_{n}
is isomorphic to the stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(v_{n})
of any vector
v_{n}
with invariants
A_{1}=0
and
A_{3}=n
. By arithmetic invariant theory, the
G(K)
-orbits with these invariants are in bijection with the kernel of the map
H^{1}(K,\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(v_{n}))\to H^{1}(K,G)\simeq{\rm Br}(K)[2]
. An elegant geometric argument of Bhargava-Gross
[
BG13
]
shows that the latter map may be identified with
q^{n}=\sum_{v}q^{n}_{v}
. We then check by explicit computation that
H^{1}_{\rm un}(K_{v},M_{n})\not\subset\ker(q^{n}_{v})
if and only if
F_{n}/K
is Hecke ramified at
v
if and only if
3\mid v
and
F_{n}
is tamely ramified at
v
. If
F_{n}/K
is Hecke unramified, this gives a robust parameterization since all elements of
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n}/K)
correspond to orbits of
V(K)
. However, if
F_{n}/K
is Hecke ramified at two or more primes
v
of
K
, this parameterization does not work, as elements of
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n})
need not lie in
\ker(q^{n})
, hence need not correspond to an orbit of
V(K)
. Luckily, if
K
has only one prime above
3
, we obtain a parameterization of all Selmer elements
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n}/K)
, for all
n\in K^{\times}
. E.g. for
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}
, each
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n})
lies in
\ker(q^{n}_{3})
, even when
F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
is tamely ramified at
3
, by Hecke reciprocity. Hence
t\in\ker(q^{n})
and
t
corresponds to a
G(K)
-orbit on
V(K)
.

The orbits corresponding to
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
have integral representatives. Thus, to finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we estimate the number of integral orbits lying on the quadric
A_{1}=0
and with
A_{3}
-invariant bounded by
X
, using a counting result of Alpöge, Bhargava, and the first author in
[
ABS22
]
. This last step should work over any number field over which our parameterization described above is available. However, we restrict ourselves to the cases
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}
and
K
an imaginary quadratic field of class number
1
, as the arguments in
[
ABS22
]
generalize straightforwardly in the latter case. We note that it would be more natural to use the five dimensional representation of
{\rm SO}(3)
, as in
[
BG13
]
and
[
Alp20
]
, but we use
G={\rm SL}_{2}^{2}/\mu_{2}
in order to invoke
[
ABS22
]
, which handles the details of the circle method argument and in the generality needed.

In the wild family
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
, applying the counting result of
[
ABS22
]
yields the formula

{\rm avg}_{n}|{\rm Cl}_{F_{n}}[2]|={\rm avg}_{n}|{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n})|=1+\tau(G)\cdot\prod_{p\leq\infty}\nu_{p}=1+2\cdot\frac{1}{2}\prod_{p<\infty}1=1+1=2,

where
\nu_{p}
is the weighted average number of local unramified classes and
\tau(G)
is the Tamagawa number. In the tame family
n\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
, the non-trivial class
t\in H^{1}_{\rm un}({\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{3},M_{n})
is “obstructed” by Hecke reciprocity, aligning with the fact that it does not correspond to a local orbit in
V({\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{3})
. This gives
\nu_{3}=\frac{1}{2}
and hence the global average is
1+1/2=3/2
. Over
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
this dichotomy disappears because odd degree Galois extensions are Hecke unramified (Proposition
10.3
).

It is interesting to compare our proof with Bhargava’s proof that
\mathrm{avg}_{F}|{\rm Cl}_{F}[2]|=3/2
in the family of all complex
S_{3}
-cubic fields
[
Bha05
]
. His parametrization uses
G^{\prime}={\rm SL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{3}
which has
\tau(G^{\prime})=1
. The factor
\tau(G)=2
in our proof is, in effect, canceled out by Hecke reciprocity, so that both proofs arrive at the same answer, at least in the family of tame pure cubic fields.

.

Theorem
1.1
proves

for

Theorem

concerns the non-Galois case where

but

. The heuristics are murkier in this case, but Malle has made conjectures for

in certain families of odd degree extensions with fixed Galois group

of even order

]
. For example, let

\mathcal{F}
of

V
,

(1.2)

\mathbb{E}_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{u}},

In §

, we use Hecke reciprocity to give a new model for

as a cokernel of an alternating matrix over

modulo

F/K
with fixed Galois group

and signature, as long as the typical member of the family is Hecke ramified.

1.4.
Heuristics for
\Gamma
-extensions

On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
shows that certain natural families of odd degree extensions
F/K
do
not
fit into Malle’s model for
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
. To account for this, we generalize Malle’s heuristics by fixing not just the Galois group
G_{F/K}\subset S_{m}
, but part of the natural
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action on
G_{F/K}
as well; here
{\rm Gal}_{K}={\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite
K
-group, i.e.
\Gamma
is a finite group
\Gamma_{0}
endowed with an action of
{\rm Gal}_{K}
.

Definition 1.7
.

A
\Gamma
-extension is an extension
F/K
together with an embedding
\Gamma\hookrightarrow G_{F/K}
of
K
-groups. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
the family of
\Gamma
-extensions
F/K
of degree
|\Gamma|
.

If
\Gamma
has trivial
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action, then
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is simply the family
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma_{0},K}
of
\Gamma_{0}
-extensions. In general, we define the
resolvent field
of
\Gamma
to be the Galois extension
R/K
trivializing the
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action on
\Gamma
. We assume for simplicity that
m:=|\Gamma|
is odd and coprime to
[R\colon K]
. In this case, we have
G_{F/K}\simeq\Gamma\rtimes H
, where
H:=G_{R/K}
. It follows that
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is the family of degree
m

G_{\Gamma}:=\Gamma_{0}\rtimes H
and resolvent field
R
. For example, if
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}
and
\Gamma=\mu_{3}
, then
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\mu_{3})
,
G_{\Gamma}=S_{3}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is the family
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})
appearing in Theorem

.

When
|G_{\Gamma}|
is odd, all fields in
\mathcal{F}_{G_{\Gamma},K}
are Hecke unramified (Proposition
10.3
), so Hecke reciprocity is not relevant. In this case, we simply predict that the distribution of
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is the one predicted by Sawin–Wood in the larger family
\mathcal{F}_{G_{\Gamma},K}
.

Our more novel prediction is when
|G_{\Gamma}|
is even. For simplicity, we assume here that
G_{F/K}\subset S_{m}
is absolutely irreducible in the sense of
[
SW23
]
. Write
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}\coprod\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}

\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
) is the subfamily of
\Gamma
-extensions
F/K

(1.3)

\mathbb{E}_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{u-1}};

(1.4)

\mathbb{E}_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{u}},

accounting for the vacuity of Hecke reciprocity on
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
. The parameter

is the one defined by Cohen–Martinet, as predicted by Malle in the larger family
\mathcal{F}_{G_{\Gamma},K}

[

. The subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is obtained by imposing finitely many local conditions (Proposition
10.4
), so
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
either has positive relative density in
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
or is empty. If
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is empty, our heuristics again simply say that the distribution on
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
should match that on
\mathcal{F}_{G_{\Gamma},K}
. When
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is non-empty, we say that
\Gamma
is
aberrant
. For aberrant
\Gamma
, our predicted distribution differs from Malle’s distribution for
\mathcal{F}_{G_{\Gamma},K}
.

The simplest example of an aberrant
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
-group is
\mu_{p}
(for a prime
p>2
). The corresponding family
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}
is the family of pure fields
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{n})
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}^{1}
is the subfamily where
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{n})
is wildly ramified at
p
. Thus, we predict that the dichotomy proven in Theorem
1.2
persists for all primes
p>2
. In §
10
, we give other examples of aberrant
K
-groups
\Gamma
. We suspect that there are only finitely many aberrant
K
-groups
\Gamma
with a given Galois group
G_{\Gamma}
. For example, when
G_{\Gamma}=D_{p}
the dihedral group of order
2p
, the aberrant
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
-groups correspond to the resolvent fields
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i)
and
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{\pm p})
(Proposition
10.6
). We do not know a precise characterization of the aberrant
K
-groups in general. We note that it can happen that
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
, i.e. every field in
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is Hecke unramified. For example, this happens for the family
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}[x]/(x^{3}+nx^{2}+9x+n)
of cubic fields with quadratic resolvent
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i)
. Thus, we predict that the average of
|{\rm Cl}_{F}[2]|
in this family is
2
.

Another sample application of our heuristics (in the case
\Gamma=\mu_{3}
, see §
11
) is:

Conjecture 1.8
.

As
n
varies over primes congruent to
8\pmod{9}
, the probability that

{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})
has class number
1
is

(1.5)

\prod_{q\neq 3}\prod_{j\geq 2}(1+q^{-j})^{-1},

where
q
ranges over the primes. On the other hand, as
n
varies over primes congruent to
2,5\pmod{9}
, the probability that
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})
has class number
1
is

(1.6)

\frac{2}{3}\cdot\prod_{q\neq 3}\prod_{j\geq 2}(1+q^{-j})^{-1}.

We restrict here to
n\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, since the class number is divisible by
3
when
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, by genus theory. Using PARI/GP
[
The23
]
we find that (
1.5
) is
\approx 0.5662
, while (
1.6
) is
\approx 0.3775
. This makes precise the approximate probabilities observed in the earlier quotation from
[
CM87
]
.

1.5.
Connection to spin structures

The aberrant families
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
are characterized by a lack of Hecke primes. Equivalently, the Hecke ideal
H_{F/K}
admits an algebraically varying square root in the family. The latter formulation fits into the more general framework of arithmetic spin structures developed by Venkatesh and the second author in
[
SV25
]
.

A
spin structure

\sigma
on a number field
F
is a square-class
[\alpha]\in F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
such that

(\alpha){\rm Diff}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}^{-1}=I^{2}

for some fractional ideal
I
. Spin structures exist, by Hecke’s theorem. The terminology comes via analogy with
3
-manifolds and Riemann surfaces, where spin structures are well-known to give rise to quadratic refinements of the natural bilinear structure on first homology, see
[
KT90
]
and
[
Ati71
,
Mum71
,
Joh80
]
respectively. We say a family of number fields
\mathcal{F}
is
gyroscopic
if it admits a family-wide spin structure.

A main consequence of the heuristics of
[
SV25
]
states that for a gyroscopic family

\mathcal{F}
of
K
-extensions with fixed and absolutely irreducible Galois group
G\subset S_{m}
, and for any finite abelian
2
-group
H
, we have

(1.7)

\mathbb{E}_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F/K},H)|)=\frac{\lvert H[2]\rvert\cdot\lvert\wedge^{2}H[|\mu_{\infty}(K)|]\rvert}{|H|^{u}},

where
u
is the Cohen–Martinet
u
-invariant, as before. We refer to
[
SV25
]
, where it is explained how spin structures induce quadratic refinements in the number field setting and how the induced quadratic biextension structure leads to the above heuristic.

The wild family
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})
of Theorem
1.2
(1) forms a gyroscopic family. Indeed, we have
(-3){\rm Diff}^{-1}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}=I^{2}
, giving the family-wide spin structure
\sigma=-3.
More generally, if
\Gamma
is an aberrant
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
-group, and if
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
admits a universal polynomial
f
(over an appropriate base space), then the family
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is gyroscopic with spin structure
\sigma={\rm Disc}(f)
. Conjecture
(\ref{eqn: SV distribution})
is then easily seen to imply the predictions in §
1.4
for the families
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
. We see that the predictions of
[
SV25
]
for
{\rm Cl}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}[2]
in families of the form
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
can be derived directly from Hecke reciprocity. Of course, Conjecture (
1.7
) is a more general prediction for the larger group
{\rm Cl}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}[2^{\infty}]
; e.g. it predicts the probability that the prime-to-
3
part of
|{\rm Cl}_{{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})}|
should be any given integer, generalizing Conjecture
1.8
. We explain how to extract this from (
1.7
) in §
11.2
and offer numerical evidence in Appendix
A
.

The families of
n
-monogenized fields studied by Bhargava, Hanke, Shankar, Siad, and Swaminathan
[
BHS20
,
Sia20a
,
Sia20b
,
Swa23
,
SSS25
]
are also (partly) gyroscopic, though in a different way. The spin structure in this case is
\sigma=nf^{\prime}(\beta)
, where
\beta
is an
n
-monogenizer and
f
is its minimal polynomial. The works cited above show that these families also exhibit boosts in the distribution of their class groups. Thus, arithmetic spin structures give a common framework for understanding how algebraic properties of families of number fields impact the distribution of the class group.

1.6.
Previous work

Ruth, in his Ph.D. thesis, proves the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
(1) for the special sub-family where
n\not\equiv\pm 1\pmod{9}
and
n
is not just cubefree, but squarefree
[
Rut13
, Theorem 1.1.5]
. For such
n
, it happens that
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})={\rm Sel}_{2}(E_{n})
, where
E_{n}
is the elliptic curve
y^{2}=x^{3}+n
. This allows Ruth to avoid the subtle issue of parameterizing elements of
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
for all
n
, which is intimately tied to our notion of Hecke reciprocity.
Note that Ruth’s subfamily has density
0
when the
F_{n}
are ordered by discriminant, but has positive density when the
F_{n}
are ordered by
n
.

For more speculation on the proportions proven in Theorem
1.2
, see
[
CM94
, §4-6]
. Note that §
1.4
provides an answer to Problem 6.1 and Question 6.2 of
loc. cit
. In
[
CM87
]
, they remark that when the fields
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})
are ordered by discriminant instead of by
n
, the data for
{\rm Cl}_{F_{n}}[2]
(for all
n
) matches better with the Cohen–Martinet distribution for general
S_{3}
-cubic fields. It seems more likely to us that this is because the ratio of tame fields to wild fields is
8/3
in the large discriminant limit, and moreover there is a large second order term that makes this disparity more pronounced for small discriminants. This follows from
[
BS14
, Theorem 8]
, which divides the family of pure cubic fields into two subfamilies based on the “shape” of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F_{n}}
, and these two subfamilies happen to exactly be the subfamilies
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{3}}^{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{3}}^{2}
. The division of pure cubic fields into “type I” and “type II” in fact goes back to Dedekind, who observed in
[
Ded00
]
that
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F_{n}}
takes on a different algebraic form, depending on whether
F_{n}
is tamely or wildly ramified at
3
.

2.
Unramified Selmer groups

Fix a prime
\ell
and an extension of number fields
F/K
. When
\mu_{\ell}\subset K
and
\ell\nmid[F\colon K]
, we show how to view
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[\ell]
as a Selmer group.

2.1.
Absolute Selmer groups

Set
T=H^{1}(F,\mu_{\ell})
and identify it with
F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}
via Kummer theory. Similarly, for any place
w
of
F
, set
T_{w}=H^{1}(F_{w},\mu_{\ell})
and define the following subspace

U_{w}=\begin{cases}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F_{w}}^{\times}/{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F_{w}}^{\times\ell}&\mbox{ if $w$ non-archimedean }\\
T_{w}&\mbox{ if $w$ archimedean}\end{cases}.

The traditional
\ell
-Selmer group of
F
is then

{\rm Sel}_{\ell}(F)=\ker\left(T\to\prod_{w}T_{w}/U_{w}\right),

where the arrow is induced by the restriction maps
T\to T_{w}
.
Concretely,
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}(F)
is the subgroup of
[t]\in F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}
with valuation divisible by
\ell
at all finite places, i.e.
(t)=I^{\ell}
for some ideal
I
. The map
[t]\mapsto[I]
gives rise to the short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\to{\rm\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{F}/{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times\ell}\to{\rm Sel}_{\ell}(F)\to{\rm Cl}_{F}[\ell]\to 0.

Now consider the following slightly different Selmer structure on
\mu_{\ell}
. For a finite place
w
, let
\epsilon_{w}\in T_{w}
be a non-trivial unramified class (unique up to scaling), and define

S_{w}=\begin{cases}\langle\epsilon_{w}\rangle&\mbox{ if $w$ non-archimedean}\\
\{0\}&\mbox{ if $w$ archimedean}.\end{cases}

Note that if
F
contains
\mu_{\ell}
, then
S_{w}=U_{w}^{\perp}
with respect to the Hilbert symbol
(\,,\,)_{w}
.

Define the unramified Selmer group

{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)=\ker\left(T\to\prod_{w}T_{w}/S_{w}\right).

Theorem 2.1
.

If
F
contains
\mu_{\ell}
, there is a canonical isomorphism
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[\ell]
.

Proof.

Class field theory gives an identification

{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[\ell]\simeq{\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{cts}}(F^{\times}\backslash{\rm\mathbb{A}}_{F}^{\times}/\prod_{w}{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F_{w}}^{\times},{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/\ell{\rm\mathbb{Z}}),

where the product is over the finite places of
F
.
Define
\xi\colon{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)\to{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[\ell]
by the formula
\xi(t)=\sum_{w}(-,t)_{w}
. This is well-defined because of the local conditions defining
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)
and the product formula for the Hilbert symbol. Note that
\xi
is injective by strong approximation and the local-to-global principle for
\ell
-th powers. On the other hand, the Greenberg-Wiles formula
[
NSW08
, Theorem 8.7.9]
and
(\ref{seq: selmer group ses})
give
\dim{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)=\dim{\rm Cl}_{F}[\ell]=\dim{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[\ell]
. Thus,
\xi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Note that
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)
is a subgroup of
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}(F)
. The composition

(2.2)

{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[\ell]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)\hookrightarrow{\rm Sel}_{\ell}(F)\to{\rm Cl}_{F}[\ell]

is the map called
\psi_{F}
in
[
LST20
]
.

2.2.
Relative Selmer groups

Let
K
be a number field containing
\mu_{\ell}
and let
F/K
be a finite extension.

Lemma 2.2
.

The norm map
F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}\to K^{\times}/K^{\times\ell}
sends
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)
to
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(K)
.

Proof.

We need to check that for a place
w
of
F
above a finite place
v
of
K
, that the norm of
\epsilon_{w}
lies in the span of
\epsilon_{v}
. For all
u\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K,v}^{\times}
, we have
\langle{\rm Nm}(\epsilon_{w}),u\rangle_{v}=\langle\epsilon_{w},u\rangle_{w}=1

[
Ser79
, pg. 209]
, so
{\rm Nm}(\epsilon_{w})
is orthogonal to all
v
-units and hence lies in
\langle\epsilon_{v}\rangle
.
∎

Define

{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)=\ker\left({\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(K)\right).

If
{\rm Cl}_{K}[\ell]=0
, then
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)\simeq{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)
. However, even in this case, the relative Selmer framework gives the better point of view. For example, a class in
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F)
is locally in the span of
\epsilon_{w}
, but it must also lie in the kernel of the local norm, which may not be true about
\epsilon_{w}
itself.

Returning to the general case
F/K
, we are led to define the
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-module

M:=\ker\left({\rm Res}_{F/K}(\mu_{\ell})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu_{\ell}\right)

of order
\ell^{[F\colon K]-1}
, whose group of geometric points is the subgroup of elements in
\mu_{\ell}^{[F\colon K]}
such that the product of all entries is
1
. We set
W=H^{1}(K,M)
and make use of the identification

W\simeq\ker\left(F^{\times}/F^{\times\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}K^{\times}/K^{\times\ell}\right),

again coming from Kummer theory.
For any place
v
of
K
, write
W_{v}=H^{1}(K_{p},M)
.

We define a Selmer structure on
W
using the subgroups
V_{v}=W_{v}\cap\prod_{w\mid v}S_{w}
. The following follows from the definitions:

Lemma 2.3
.

The Selmer group
\ker(W\to\prod_{v}W_{v}/V_{v})
is isomorphic to
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)
.

Recall that
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}={\rm Cl}_{F}/{\rm Cl}_{K}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\ell\nmid[F\colon K]
, then
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)\simeq{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[\ell]
.

Proof.

Since
\ell\nmid[F\colon K]
, the group
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[\ell]=\mathrm{coker}({\rm Cl}_{K}[\ell]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Cl}_{F}[\ell])
is dual to the Selmer group
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)=\ker({\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[\ell]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Cl}_{K}^{\vee}[\ell])
via Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Example 2.5
.

Suppose
\ell\nmid[F\colon K]
, and let
v
be a prime of
K
such that
F/K
has a unique prime
w
above
p
. Then the subgroup
V_{v}
is trivial (since
\epsilon_{v}=\epsilon_{w}
is not in the kernel of the local norm) and indeed every element of
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)
is locally trivial at
w
in this situation.

More generally, we have:

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\ell\nmid[F:K]
and
v
is a finite prime of
K
, then
|V_{v}|=|M(K_{v})|=\ell^{N-1}
, where
N
is the number of primes of
F
above
v
.

Proof.

Write
F\otimes K_{v}\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}
, where each
L_{i}
is a field extension of
K_{v}
corresponding to a prime
w_{i}
above
v
. On one hand, we have
|M(K_{v})|=\ell^{N-1}
. On the other hand, there exists at least one
L_{i}
such that
\ell\nmid[L_{i}\colon K]
, and we may label things so that
i=1
. Then note that
\epsilon_{1}
is the image of the unramified class
\epsilon\in K_{v}^{\times}/K^{\times\ell}_{v}
. Thus
{\rm Nm}(\epsilon_{1},1,\ldots,1)={\rm Nm}(\epsilon_{1})\cdot 1=\epsilon_{1}^{[L_{i}\colon K]}=\epsilon
, i.e. the norm map
\prod_{i}L_{i}^{\times}/L_{i}^{\times\ell}\to K_{v}^{\times}/K_{v}^{\times\ell}
induces an isomorphism on unramified classes when restricted to the first factor. Projection onto the last
N-1
factors therefore induces an isomorphism
V_{v}\simeq\prod_{i=2}^{N}S_{w_{i}}
, and hence
|V_{v}|=\ell^{N-1}
.
∎

From now on, we view
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F/K)
as the Selmer group of the Selmer structure
(M,\{V_{v}\})
. Similarly for
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(F)
whenever
{\rm Sel}_{\ell}^{\rm un}(K)
(or equivalently
{\rm Cl}_{K}[\ell]
) happens to be
0
.

3.
Hecke primes and Hecke reciprocity

Let
K
be a local or global field of characteristic
0
. We study the notion of Hecke primes and Hecke ramification in extensions
F/K
of odd degree. In the global case, we prove a reciprocity law constraining the number of Hecke primes of
F
that are inert in an unramified quadratic extension
L/F
corresponding to a class in
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}^{\vee}[2]
.

3.1.
Hecke primes over local fields

Let
K
be a finite extension of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for some prime
p
, with ring of integers
{\rm\mathcal{O}}
. Let
v
be the discrete valuation on
K
, normalized so that
v(\pi)=1
, where
\pi\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}
is any uniformizer. Let
F
be an étale
K
-algebra of degree
m
, with
m=2g+1
odd.

We may write
F\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{N}L_{i}
, where each
L_{i}
is a finite field extensions of
K
. As before, set

M:=\ker\left({\rm Res}_{F/K}(\mu_{\ell})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu_{\ell}\right),

let

W=H^{1}(K,M)\simeq\ker(F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}\to K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}),

and let
V\subset W
be the subgroup of classes
[t]=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
such that each
u_{i}
is either trivial or the unramified square-class
\epsilon_{i}
in
L_{i}
.

We define the
Hecke ideal
to be

H_{F/K}:={\rm Disc}_{F/K}\mathrm{Diff}_{F/K}^{-1}\subset{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}.

Its norm
{\rm Disc}_{F/K}^{m}{\rm Disc}_{F/K}^{-1}={\rm Disc}_{F/K}^{2g}
is a square of an ideal. We say that
F/K
is
Hecke ramified
if
H_{F/K}
is
not
a square of an ideal. Otherwise,
F/K
is
Hecke unramified
. If
F/K
is Hecke ramified then
F/K
is ramified in the usual sense, but the converse need not be true. Finally, we say that a prime
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
(corresponding to
L_{i}
) of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
is a
Hecke prime
if the power of
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
dividing
H_{F/K}
is odd and if there exists
[t]=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})\in V
such that
u_{i}\notin L_{i}^{\times 2}
.

We record below some basic facts about Hecke primes; the proofs will use the following notation. Write
(\pi)=\prod_{i}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{e_{i}}
and let
f_{i}
be the residue field degree at
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
. Write
{\rm Diff}_{F/K}=\prod_{i}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{d_{i}}
, so that
{\rm Disc}_{F/K}=(\pi)^{d}
where
d=\sum_{i}d_{i}f_{i}
. In this notation,
H_{F/K}=\prod_{i}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{de_{i}-d_{i}}
and of course
m=\sum_{i}e_{i}f_{i}
. We write
h_{i}=de_{i}-d_{i}
for the exponent of
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
in the Hecke ideal.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
F/K
is either unramified or a field extension, then
F/K
is Hecke unramified.

Proof.

The unramified case is clear, so assume
F
is a field. Then
(\pi)={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{e_{1}}
and
H_{F/K}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{d_{1}f_{1}e_{1}-d_{1}}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{d_{1}(e_{1}f_{1}-1)}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{d_{1}(m-1)}
, which is a square.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
F/K
is Hecke ramified. Then there exists a Hecke prime
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
of
F/K
.

Proof.

The hypothesis implies that there exists a prime
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
such that
h_{1}
is odd. If there does not exist
[t]=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{N})\in V
with
t_{1}\notin L_{1}^{\times 2}
, it means that
e_{i}
is even for
2\leq i\leq N
(see the proof of Lemma
2.6
and use the fact that
{\rm Nm}(\epsilon_{L_{i}})=\epsilon_{K}
if and only if
e_{i}
is odd). Since
m=\sum_{i}e_{i}f_{i}
is odd, it follows that
e_{1}f_{1}
is odd and hence both
e_{1}
and
f_{1}
are odd. We may also assume that
h_{i}
is even for
2\leq i\leq N
since at most one prime with
h_{i}
odd can fail to be a Hecke prime (again by the proof of Lemma
2.6
).

Suppose first that
d=\sum_{i}d_{i}f_{i}
is even. Then
h_{i}\equiv d_{i}\pmod{2}
for all
i
. It follows that
d_{1}\equiv h_{1}
is odd and
d_{i}\equiv h_{i}
is even for all
i\geq 2
. But then
d=\sum d_{i}f_{i}\equiv d_{1}f_{1}
is odd, which is a contradiction. Now suppose
d=\sum_{i}d_{i}f_{i}
is odd. Then
h_{i}\equiv e_{i}-d_{i}
for all
i
, and so
e_{1}\not\equiv d_{1}
, and hence
d_{1}
is even. But then
e_{i}\equiv d_{i}
for
i\geq 2
, which implies
d_{i}
is also even for
i\geq 2
. This implies that
d=\sum d_{i}f_{i}
is even, which is a contradiction.
∎

As an example, here is a characterization of Hecke ramification in the cubic case:

Lemma 3.3
.

If
m=3
, then
F/K
is Hecke ramified if and only if
F\simeq K(\sqrt{\pi})\times K
, for some uniformizer
\pi
. In this case, the ramified prime
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
is the unique Hecke prime of
F/K
.

Proof.

If
F/K
is unramified or totally ramified, then
F/K
is Hecke unramified by Lemma
3.1
. If
F/K
is partially ramified and
p\neq 2
, then
(\pi)={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{2}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
, and
H_{F/K}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{2-1}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
. Thus
F/K
is Hecke ramified in this case, and moreover
L_{1}\simeq K(\sqrt{\pi})
for some uniformizer
\pi
, since
p\neq 2
.

If
F/K
is partially ramified and
p=2
, then
{\rm Disc}(F/K)=(\pi)^{e}
for some
e\geq 2
, and
H_{F/K}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{2e-e}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}^{e}=({\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2})^{e}
is a square if and only if
e
is even. Write
F\simeq K(\sqrt{d})\times K
, for some
d\in K
. We claim that
e
is even if and only if
d\in{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{\times}
(up to squares in
K^{\times}
). By
[
Ser79
, p. 64]
, we have
e=w(\overline{\alpha}-\alpha)
, where
w
is the normalized valuation on
K(\sqrt{d})
and
\alpha
is such that
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K(\sqrt{d})}={\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}[\alpha]
. Writing
\alpha=a+b\sqrt{d}
, we have
w(\overline{\alpha}-\alpha)=w(2b\sqrt{d})\in 2{\rm\mathbb{Z}}+w(\sqrt{d})
, which is even if and only if
d
is a unit (up to squares), as claimed.

Finally, in the Hecke ramified case, the group
V
has order
2
(by Lemma
2.6
) and the non-trivial class is
t=(\epsilon_{1},1)
. It follows that
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
corresponding to
K(\sqrt{d})=L_{1}
is the unique Hecke prime.
∎

Example 3.4
.

In higher degree, it can happen that
F/K
is Hecke ramified but there are no ramified Hecke primes. For example, if
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{3}
has degree five and splitting type
(1^{3}2)
, then
d=d_{1}f_{1}=d_{1}\in\{3,5\}
is odd. Hence
(\pi)={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{3}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
and

H_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{3}}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{h_{1}}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}^{h_{2}}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{de_{1}-d_{i}}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}^{de_{2}-d_{2}}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{2d}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}^{d},

which shows that the unramified prime
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
is the unique Hecke prime for
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{3}
.

3.2.
Hecke reciprocity

Now suppose that
K
is a number field and let
F/K
be a finite extension. Recall the following theorem of Hecke
[
Wei74
, pg. 291]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

The different
\mathrm{Diff}_{F/K}
is a square in
{\rm Cl}_{F}
.

As in the introduction, we define the
Hecke ideal

H_{F/K}:={\rm Disc}_{F/K}\mathrm{Diff}_{F/K}^{-1}\subset{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}.

The Hecke ideal is especially interesting when
F/K
has odd degree, because then the norm of
H_{F/K}
is the square of an ideal in
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}
(while
H_{F/K}
itself need not be the square of an ideal in
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
). As the discriminant
{\rm Disc}_{F/K}
is a square in
{\rm Cl}_{K}
, Hecke’s theorem also gives:

Corollary 3.6
.

The Hecke ideal
H_{F/K}
is a square in
{\rm Cl}_{F}
.

To state the reciprocity law, we recall the definition of a Hecke prime from the introduction (which is also the global version of the local definition given in the previous subsection).

Definition 3.7
.

A prime
w
of
F
lying over a finite prime
v
of
K
, is a Hecke prime if an odd power of
w
divides
H_{F/K}
and if there exists
t\in V_{v}
which projects to
\epsilon_{w}
in
S_{w}
(i.e.
t\notin F_{w}^{\times 2}
).

The existence of a Hecke prime is an obstruction to
H_{F/K}
being a square of an ideal.
3
3

3

Analogously, a polarization
\lambda\colon A\to\hat{A}
of an abelian variety over a field
k
always has square degree, but it may not arise from a line bundle defined over
k
. On the other hand,
2\lambda
does have this property
[
Mum08
, §20]
.
Hecke reciprocity (which is also Theorem
1.6
) states:

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
F/K
be a finite extension of number fields and let
L=F(\sqrt{t})/F
be an everywhere unramified quadratic extension such that
{\rm Nm}_{F/K}(t)\in K^{\times 2}
. Then the number of Hecke primes of
F
that are inert in
L/F
is even.

Proof.

Let
\chi\colon{\rm Cl}_{F}\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
be the quadratic character corresponding to
L/F
. For every prime
w
of
F
, we have
\chi(w)=0
if and only if
w
splits in
L/K
if and only if
t\notin F_{w}^{\times 2}
. By Theorem
3.6
,
\chi([H_{F/K}])=0
. Write
H_{F/K}=\prod_{w}w^{d_{w}}
and let
S
be the set of Hecke primes
w
that are inert in
L/F
. Then

0=\chi([H_{F/K}])=\sum_{\mbox{$w$ inert in $L/F$}}d_{w}=\sum_{w\in S}1\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2{\rm\mathbb{Z}},

which means that
\#S
is even.
∎

Here is a sample application of the reciprocity law.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
be a degree
p
Kummer extension
F={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{n})
. Then any prime of
F
lying above
p
is split in any unramified quadratic extension
L/F
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm Cl}_{\rm\mathbb{Q}}[2]=0
, every unramified quadratic extension
L/F
arises from some
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
. There are two possibilities for the splitting behavior of
p
in
F
: either
p=\lambda^{p}
or
(p)=\lambda_{1}^{p-1}\lambda_{2}
. In the first case we have
V_{p}=0
by Lemma
2.6
, so
\lambda
splits in all quadratic extensions
L/F
for local reasons. In the second case, we have
\#V_{p}=2
and the non-trivial class is
(\epsilon_{\lambda_{1}},1)
. This shows that
\lambda_{2}
is not Hecke and moreover splits in every unramified
L/F
for local reasons. On the other hand,
\lambda_{1}
is a Hecke prime, and we claim that it is the unique Hecke prime in
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
. This is because any ramified prime
q\neq p
is totally ramified, and hence
V_{q}=0
by Lemma
2.6
. By Theorem
3.8
, we see that
\lambda_{1}
must also split in
L/F
(for global reasons!).
∎

More generally, the same proof gives:

Corollary 3.10
.

Suppose
F/K
is an odd degree extension with exactly one Hecke prime
w
. Then each
[t]\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
is locally a square at
w
.

Using the formulas in
[
BS14
]
, we can write down many other examples of families of fields admitting at most one Hecke prime:

Example 3.11
.

Let
p
be a non-zero integer with at most one prime factor. For
n\in{\rm\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
, set
f_{n}=x^{3}+nx^{2}-9px-np
and
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}[x]/f_{n}(x)
. The discriminant of
f_{n}
is
4p(n^{2}+27p)^{2}
, so this is a family of cubic fields with quadratic resolvent
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{p})
. If
p=\pm 1
, there are no Hecke primes in this family, while if
|p|>1
, then
p
is the only prime which is Hecke ramified in
F_{n}
.

4.
Quadratic refinements

To prove our results on cubic Kummer extensions
F=K(\sqrt[3]{n})
, we analyze a certain quadratic refinement of the pairing on
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)\times{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
induced by cup product. In the process, we will essentially reprove the Hecke reciprocity law from the previous section, without invoking Hecke’s theorem. Since it may be of independent interest, we use quadratic refinements to give an algebraic proof of Hecke’s theorem for general cubic extensions.

4.1.
Quadratic refinements

We begin in the general setting where
K
is a field of characteristic
0
and
F
is a finite étale
K
-algebra of odd degree
m=2g+1
.

Recall the
K
-group scheme
M=\ker({\rm Res}_{F/K}\mu_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Nm}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mu_{2})
. We define a bilinear pairing

\langle\,,\,\rangle\colon M\times M\to\mu_{2}

as follows. Let
M^{0}={\rm Res}_{F/K}\mu_{2}
. For
i=1,\ldots,m
, let
\sigma_{i}\colon K\to\overline{K}
be the different embeddings of
F
into
\overline{K}
. With this ordering, we identify
M^{0}(\overline{K})\simeq\mu_{2}^{m}
. Let
\Delta=(-1,\ldots,-1)\in\mu_{2}^{m}
and let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
be the standard basis of
\mu_{2}^{m}
. The pairing on
M^{0}(\overline{K})
determined by the rule

\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if $i=j$}\\
-1&\mbox{ if $i\neq j$}\end{cases}

is
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-equivariant, hence descends to a pairing
M^{0}\times M^{0}\to\mu_{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

M
is isomorphic to its own Cartier dual via
\langle\,,\,\rangle
.

Proof.

The kernel for the pairing on
M^{0}
is the span of
\Delta=(-1,\ldots,-1)
, and so the induced pairing on
M
is perfect.
∎

Cup-product and the self-duality of Lemma
4.1
induce a bilinear form

(\,,\,)\colon H^{1}(K,M)\times H^{1}(K,M)\to H^{2}(K,\mu_{2})[2]={\rm Br}(K)[2]

valued in the
2
-torsion subgroup of the Brauer group of
K
. When
F/K
is an extension of number fields, this pairing is given (locally at each prime) by the Hilbert symbol.

A
quadratic refinement
of
(\,,)
is a quadratic map
q\colon H^{1}(K,M)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
such that

(x,y)=q(x+y)-q(x)-q(y).

There are many quadratic refinements of
(\,,\,)
, each one depending on a choice of
\beta\in F^{\times}
such that
{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(\beta)=0
. Let
f(x)\in K[x]
be the monic, degree
m
characteristic polynomial of
\beta
acting by multiplication on
F
. To
t\in H^{1}(K,M)\subset F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
, we attach the quadratic space
(F,Q_{\beta,t})
, where

Q_{\beta,t}(x,y)={\rm Tr}_{F/K}(txy/f^{\prime}(\beta)).

Because
f^{\prime}(\beta)
generates the different of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{K}[\beta]
and because
t
has square norm, this quadratic space of rank
2g+1
has discriminant
1
. We say
Q_{\beta,t}
is
split
if it contains a
g
-dimensional isotropic subspace. This is the case if
t=1
, and in this case the orthogonal group is the split group
{\rm SO}(m)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

For each
\beta
with
{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(\beta)=0
, there exists a quadratic refinement

q_{\beta}\colon H^{1}(K,M)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]

sending
[t]
to the class of
Q_{\beta,t}
in
H^{1}(K,{\rm SO}(m))\simeq{\rm Br}(K)[2]
. In particular,
[t]\in H^{1}(K,M)
lies in
\ker(q_{\beta})
if and only if
Q_{\beta,t}
is split.

Proof.

This is nicely explained in Bhargava-Gross
[
BG13
, §4.2]
, see also
[
BG14
, §6.2]
and
[
PR12
, Corollary 4.6]
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

One can view
q_{\beta}\colon H^{1}(K,M)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
as the map
H^{1}(K,J[2])\to H^{2}(K,{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{m})[2]
in the long exact sequence associated to the theta group of the square of the principal polarization on the Jacobian
J
of the hyperelliptic curve
y^{2}=f_{\beta}(x)
, as explained in
[
PR12
, §4]
.

In the next two sections, we analyze the maps
q_{\beta}
over local and global fields, especially their interaction with the group
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
and its local analogue
V_{p}
. We give full details for cubic extensions and mostly full details for Kummer extensions
F=K(\sqrt[m]{n})
of prime degree.

4.2.
Quadratic refinements over local fields

We return to the notation of Section
3.1
, where
K
is a finite extension of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
for some prime
p
and
F/K
is a degree
m=2g+1
étale
K
-algebra.

Let
\beta\in F
be such that
{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(\beta)=0
and
F\simeq K[x]/f_{\beta}(x)
, where
f_{\beta}(x)
is the characteristic polynomial of
\beta
, and let
q_{\beta}\colon W\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
be the quadratic refinement associated to
\beta
as in Proposition
4.2
. We show below that, in many cases, there exists
\beta
such that
V\subset\ker(q_{\beta})
if and only if
F/K
is Hecke unramified. We begin with two statements that hold for any odd
m
.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
F/K
is unramified, then
V\subset\ker(q_{\beta})
, for any choice of
\beta
.

Proof.

The assumptions imply that
M
is unramified. Using Remark
4.3
, we conclude that
q_{\beta}
sends unramified classes to unramified classes. Since the non-zero
2
-torsion element of
H^{2}(K,{\rm\mathbb{G}}_{m})
is ramified, we must have
q_{\beta}(t)=0
for all
t\in V
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

If
F/K
is a field extension then
V\subset\ker(q_{\beta})
, for any choice of
\beta
.

Proof.

In this case
V=0
by Lemma
2.6
. ∎

To handle the more subtle cases, we assume for the rest of this section that
m=3
. Then
q_{\beta}(t)=0
if and only if the rank
3
quadratic form
{\rm Tr}(tx^{2}/f^{\prime}(\beta))
is isotropic. The following explicit criteria for isotropy follows from the definition of the Hilbert symbol
(\,,)
on
K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}\times K^{\times}/K^{\times 2}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
a,b,c\in K^{\times}
. Then
ax^{2}+by^{2}+cz^{2}
is isotropic over
K
if and only if
(-ac,-bc)=1
.

Since we now assume
m=3
, the only case not covered by the earlier two lemmas is when
F\simeq L\times K
, where
L
is a ramified quadratic
K
-extension.

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose
F=K(\sqrt{d})\times K
, with
K(\sqrt{d})/K
ramified. Then there exists a constant
v_{0}\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
such that if
\beta=(a+b\sqrt{d},-2a)
satisfies
v(a)>v(b)+v_{0}
, then
V\subset\ker(q_{\beta})
if and only if
F/K
is Hecke unramified.

Proof.

A computation gives
f_{\beta}^{\prime}(\beta)=(6ab\sqrt{d}+2db^{2},9a^{2}-db^{2})
. If
v(a)\gg v(b)
, then we have
f^{\prime}_{\beta}(\beta)\equiv(2d,-d)\equiv(2,-d)
modulo squares in
F^{\times}
. There is a single non-trivial class in
V
, the class
(\epsilon,{\rm Nm}(\epsilon))\equiv(\epsilon,1)
. Thus, we need to check whether the quadratic form
{\rm Tr}_{F/K}((2\epsilon,-d)x^{2})
is isotropic or not. An equivalent diagonalization of this quadratic form is
\epsilon x_{1}^{2}+\epsilon dx_{2}^{2}-dx_{3}^{2}
. By Lemma
4.6
, this is anisotropic if and only if
(\epsilon d,\epsilon)=(d,\epsilon)=1
if and only if
d
is a uniformizer (up to squares). By Lemma
3.3
, it is isotropic if and only if
F/K
is Hecke unramified.
∎

4.3.
Algebraic proof of Hecke reciprocity for cubic fields

Now suppose
K
is a number field, and let
F/K
be an extension of degree
3
. The following is a special case of Theorem
3.8
, but we will prove it in a different way.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
F
be a cubic extension of
K
and let
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
. Then the number of
Hecke primes of
F/K
that are inert in
F(\sqrt{t})/F
is even.

Proof.

Let
S
be the set of primes of
K
with splitting type
(1^{2}1)
. For each
v\in S
, write
v=w_{1}^{2}w_{2}
and let
\sqrt{d}\in F_{w_{1}}
be as in Proposition
4.7
.
Choose a single
z\in F
such that for each
v\in S
, we have
w_{1}(z-\sqrt{d})\gg 1
and
w_{2}(z)\gg 1
. Then
\beta:=3z-{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(z)
satisfies
{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(\beta)=0
. If we write
z=(a+b\sqrt{d},c)\in F\otimes K_{v}\simeq F_{w_{1}}\times F_{w_{2}}
, then

\beta=(3a+3b\sqrt{d}-2a-c,3c-2a-c)=(a-c+3b\sqrt{d},-2a-2c),

and our choice of
z
means that
v(a-c)\gg 1
whereas
v(b)=0
.

Let
q_{\beta}\colon H^{1}(K,M)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
be the global refinement. For
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
, consider the element
\alpha=q_{\beta}(t)
. Let
{\rm inv}_{v}\colon{\rm Br}(K_{v})[2]\simeq{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
be the invariant map. By Proposition
4.7
, if
F/K
is Hecke ramified at
v
and
\mathrm{res}_{v}(t)
is equal to the non-trivial class in
V_{v}\subset H^{1}(K_{v},M)
, then
{\rm inv}_{v}(\mathrm{res}_{v}\alpha)=1/2
. Otherwise, we have
{\rm inv}_{v}(\mathrm{res}_{v}(\alpha))=0
, by Lemmas
3.3
-
4.5
; if
v
is archimedean, then this is because
t
is locally trivial at
v
. Class field theory gives
\sum_{v}{\rm inv}_{v}(\mathrm{res}_{v}(\alpha))=0
, so we see that the number of Hecke ramified primes
v
of
K
above which there is a prime which is inert in
F(\sqrt{t})
is even. By Lemma
3.3
, above each such
v=w_{1}^{2}w_{2}
there is exactly one Hecke prime (namely,
w_{1}
), so the number of Hecke primes which are inert in
F(\sqrt{t})
is also even.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

The proof of Theorem
4.8
did not use Hecke’s Theorem
3.5
. In fact, it gives a new proof of Hecke’s theorem for cubic

, since applying it to all
\chi\in{\rm Cl}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}^{\vee}[2]\simeq{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
, we conclude that
\chi([H_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}])=0
and hence
[H_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}]
is a square in
{\rm Cl}_{F}
. It follows that
[{\rm Diff}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}]
is a square in the class group as well.

5.
A canonical quadratic refinement for Kummer extensions

In this section, we consider Kummer extensions
F=K(\sqrt[m]{n})
, where
m=2g+1
is an odd prime.
There is then a natural choice of
\beta\in F
of trace

, namely
\beta=\sqrt[m]{n}
. Let
q\colon H^{1}(K,M)\to{\rm Br}(K)[2]
be the associated quadratic refinement (previously called

).

Proposition 5.1
.

A class
t\in H^{1}(K,M)
lies in
\ker(q)
if and only if the quadratic space
\frac{1}{m}{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(tx^{2})

(
of rank
m=2g+1
and discriminant

is split, i.e. contains a
g
-dimensional isotropic subspace.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
4.2
, since
f^{\prime}(\beta)=m\beta^{2g}
is a scalar times a square.
∎

The purpose of this section is to determine to what extent elements of

(in the local case) and

(in the global case) belong to

, as this will be necessary information in the proofs of Theorems

and

and for the heuristics in §

5.1.
Local Kummer extensions

Let
K
be a finite extension of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with normalized valuation
v
. Write
\zeta_{m}
for a primitive
m
-th root of unity, and let
d
(resp.
e
) be the degree (resp. ramification index) of the extension
K(\zeta_{m})/K
. Let
F=K[x]/(x^{m}-n)
be a Kummer
K
-algebra of odd prime degree. Without loss of generality, we assume that
0\leq v(n)<m
.

Lemma 5.2
.

F/K
is Hecke ramified if and only if
p=m
,
F/K
is tamely ramified, and
e
is even. In this case,
n
is an
m
-th power in
K^{\times}
and a prime of
{\rm\mathcal{O}}_{F}
is a Hecke prime if and only if it is ramified. The number of such Hecke primes is
(p-1)/d

Proof.

If
p\neq m
, and
F/K
is ramified, then
v(n)>0
and
F/K
is a totally ramified field extension. It is Hecke unramified by Lemma
4.5
. Of course, if
F/K
is unramified then it is also Hecke unramified.

Now suppose
p=m
. Note that
e
divides
d
but may be strictly smaller. First consider the case that
n
is an
m
-th power. Then
F\simeq K\times K(\zeta_{p})^{(p-1)/d}
. We have
{\rm Disc}_{F/K}=(\pi)^{(p-1)(e-1)/d}
and
(\pi)={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\prod_{i=1}^{(p-1)/d}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{e}
. So
\mathrm{Diff}_{F/K}=\prod_{i=1}^{(p-1)/d}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{e-1}
and

H_{F/K}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{(p-1)(e-1)/d}\prod_{i=1}^{(p-1)/d}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{e(p-1)(e-1)/d-(e-1)}={\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{1}^{(p-1)(e-1)/d}\prod_{i=1}^{(p-1)/d}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{(e-1)(\frac{e(p-1)}{d}-1)}.

This is a square of an ideal if and only if
e
is odd, as claimed.

If
n
is not an
m
-th power in
K^{\times}
, then
x^{m}-n
is irreducible over
K
, hence
F/K
is a field extension (of degree
m=p
). If
F/K
is ramified, then it must be totally (and wildly) ramified, and hence Hecke unramified by Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Define the subspace
V\subset H^{1}(K,M)
as in §
4.2
.
To determine
\ker(q)\cap V
, we will use the Hasse-Witt invariant
\mathrm{HW}(Q)
of a quadratic form
Q
over
K
. By definition

\mathrm{HW}(Q)=\prod_{i<j}(a_{i},a_{j})\in\{\pm 1\},

where
Q\sim\sum a_{i}x_{i}^{2}
is any diagonal quadratic form equivalent to
Q
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
L/K
be an étale
K
-algebra of degree
2g+1
and let
\Delta:={\rm disc}({\rm Tr}_{F/K}(x^{2}))\in F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
be the discriminant. Then the quadratic space
{\rm Tr}_{K/F}(tx^{2})
is split if and only if it has Hasse-Witt invariant
((-1)^{g(g+1)/2}N(t)^{g}\Delta^{g},-1)
.

Proof.

The rank, discriminant, and Hasse-Witt invariant form a complete set of invariants for equivalence classes of quadratic forms over the local field
K

[
Cas78
]
. There is a unique split space of discriminant
d
and rank
2g+1
over
K
, namely
\langle(-1)^{g}d\rangle\oplus gH
where
H
is the hyperbolic plane. Denote its Hasse-Witt invariant by
\varepsilon_{d}=((-1)^{g(g+1)/2}d^{g},-1)
. Therefore,
{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(tx^{2})
splits if and only if it has Hasse-Witt invariant equal to
\varepsilon_{N(t)\Delta}
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

In our setting of Kummer extensions, the quadratic form
\frac{1}{m}{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(tx^{2})
has discriminant
1
. If
p>2
, then it is split if and only if its Hasse-Witt invariant is
1
.

Theorem 5.5
.

If
p\neq m
or
e\in\{1,m-1\}
, then
V\subset\ker(q)
if and only if
F/K
is Hecke unramified.

Proof.

When
p\neq m
or when
F/K
is either unramified or totally ramified, this follows from Lemma
5.2
and Lemmas
4.5
-
4.4
.

So we may assume now that
p=m
and moreover that
n\in K^{\times m}
. As before, we have
F\simeq K\times K(\zeta_{m})^{h}
, where
d=[K(\zeta_{m})\colon K]
and
hd=m-1=p-1
. If
e=1
, then
F/K
is unramified, a case we have already covered. So it remains to consider the case
e=d=m-1=p-1
, where we must show (by Lemma
5.2
) that
V\not\subset\ker(q)
. In this case,
F\simeq K\times K(\zeta_{m})
and the unique non-zero element
t\in V
is
(1,\epsilon)
. The quadratic form
Q_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{m}{\rm Tr}_{F/K}(tx^{2})
is equivalent to

\langle m\rangle\perp m\epsilon{\rm Tr}_{K(\zeta_{m})/K}(x^{2}).

Note that
K(\zeta_{m})/K
is of the form
K(\sqrt[m-1]{b})
for some
b\in K^{\times}
, since
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
contains
\mu_{p-1}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

We have
b\equiv-p\pmod{K^{\times 2}}

Proof.

We may assume
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. The discriminant of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
is
(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p^{p-2}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p
. On the other hand, the discriminant of
x^{p-1}-b
is

(-1)^{(p-1)(p-2)/2}(p-1)^{p-1}(-b)^{p-2}\equiv(-1)^{(p-1)(p-2)/2+1}b

Equating these two, we find that
b\equiv-p
.
∎

A formula of Serre
[
Ser84
, pg. 672]
for the trace form of a Kummer extension gives

{\rm Tr}_{K(\zeta_{m})/K}(x^{2})\sim\langle p-1,(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(p-1)p\rangle\perp H^{(p-1)/2-1}\sim\langle-1,(-1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}p\rangle\perp H^{(p-1)/2-1}.

The Hasse-Witt invariant of
Q_{t}
is then

\displaystyle\mathrm{HW}(Q_{t})

\displaystyle=(m,m)^{\binom{p}{2}}\mathrm{HW}(\langle-\epsilon,(-1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\epsilon p,\epsilon,-\epsilon,\cdots,\epsilon,-\epsilon\rangle)

\displaystyle=(m,m)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\epsilon,\pm p)\mathrm{HW}(\langle-1,(-1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}p,1,-1,\cdots,1,-1\rangle)

\displaystyle=(p,p)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\epsilon,p)((-1)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}p,-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(p,-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\epsilon,p)(p,-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle=(\epsilon,p)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if }v(p)\equiv 0\pmod{2}\\
-1&\mbox{ if }v(p)\equiv 1\pmod{2}\end{cases}.

However,
v(p)
is odd, by Lemma
5.6
and our assumption that
K(\zeta_{m})/K
is totally ramified of degree
m-1
. Thus
\mathrm{HW}(Q_{t})=-1
and
Q_{t}
is not split. Hence
V\not\subset\ker(q)
, by Proposition
5.1
and Remark
5.4
.
∎

5.2.
Global Kummer extensions

Now let
K
be a number field. The following is a more precise version of Hecke reciprocity for prime degree pure fields
F=K(\sqrt[m]{n})
satisfying a mild hypothesis. Note that by Lemma
5.2
, if
v
is a prime of
K
which is Hecke ramified in
F
, then
v
lies above
m
and is tamely ramified in
F
. Moreover, the Hecke primes of
F/K
are exactly the ramified primes of
F
lying over such a prime
v
of
K
. Denote by
q_{v}\colon H^{1}(K_{v},M)\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
the local quadratic refinement.

Theorem 5.7
.

Assume that for all primes
v
of
K
above
m
, the ramification index
e(K_{v}(\zeta_{m})/K_{v})
is
1
or
m-1
. Let
F=K(\sqrt[m]{n})
be a degree
m
Kummer extension of
K
and let
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
. Then
q_{v}(t)=1/2\in{\rm Br}(K_{v})[2]
if and only if
v
is tamely ramified in
F/K
and
t\notin F_{w}^{\times 2}
, where
w
is the unique ramified prime above
v
. Since
\sum_{v}q_{v}(t)=0
, the number of Hecke primes
w
of
F/K
such that
w
is inert in
F(\sqrt{t})/F
is even.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
and Theorem
5.5
, we have
q_{v}(t)=0
for all primes
v
except possibly those
v
dividing
m
which split as
v=w_{1}w_{2}^{m-1}
. For the latter, the space
V_{v}\subset H^{1}(K_{v},M)
is
1
-dimensional and a class
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/K)
restricts to the non-trivial class if and only if
t\notin F_{w_{2}}^{\times 2}
. Moreover, in this case, we have
q_{v}(\mathrm{res}_{v}(t))=1/2
by Theorem
5.5
and Lemma
5.2
. Since
\sum_{v}{\rm inv}_{v}(q_{v}(\mathrm{res}_{v}(t)))=0
, we see that the number of such primes
v
is even. Finally, note that the non-trivial class in
V_{v}
projects to a square in
F_{w_{1}}^{\times}
since the norm of the class
\epsilon\in F_{w_{2}}^{\times}/F^{\times 2}_{w_{2}}
is trivial. So the unique ramified prime
w
above such a
v
is the unique Hecke prime above
v
. Thus, the number of Hecke primes
w
in
F
with
t\notin F_{w}^{\times 2}
is also even.
∎

The hypothesis in our theorem always holds when
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}
, so we obtain:

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[m]{n})
be a degree
m
Kummer extension and let
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n})
. Then
t
lies in the kernel of
q\colon{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n})\to{\rm Br}({\rm\mathbb{Q}})[2]
and is locally square at all primes of
F_{n}
above
m
.

Proof.

The unique prime
p
of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
above
m
(namely
p=m
itself) satisfies
[{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}(\zeta_{m})\colon{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}]=m-1
, so the hypotheses of Theorem
5.7
hold. Moreover, this is the only prime which could be Hecke ramified in
F_{n}
. If
F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
is wildly ramified at
p
, then
V_{p}=0
and every
t\in{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F_{n})
is locally a square at the unique prime
w
of
F_{n}
above
p
. If
F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
is tamely ramified at
p
, then
(p)=w_{1}w_{2}^{p-1}
, and
w_{2}
is the unique Hecke prime of
F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
. It follows that
t
is a square at
w_{1}
(for local reasons) and a square at

as well (because of the reciprocity law in Theorem
5.7
).
∎

The hypothesis in Theorem
5.7
also always holds for cubic extensions. We highlight the following special case (which would be relevant for versions of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
over other number fields).

Corollary 5.9
.

Suppose that

not containing

. Then for every cubic Kummer extension

As before, noting that if

∎

We recall some facts from

A_{1}((F_{1},F_{2}))=r_{1}r_{8}-3r_{2}r_{7}+3r_{3}r_{6}-r_{4}r_{5},

By arithmetic invariant theory

There is a bijection, functorial in

G

In

{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
in families of odd degree extensions

with fixed Galois group and unit group rank. Below we attempt to reconcile his heuristics with Hecke reciprocity. For simplicity, we assume
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}

Recall the notation from Section
2.2
. By definition, we have

{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F)={\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})=\ker\left(W\to\prod_{p}W_{p}/V_{p}\right)\subset W=H^{1}({\rm\mathbb{Q}},M).

The isomorphism
{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F)\simeq{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[2]
of Theorem
2.1
allows us to view
{\rm Cl}_{F}^{\vee}[2]
as a kernel of a matrix, as follows. For any finite set of finite primes
S
of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
, let

be the subspace of classes that are unramified outside

{\rm Sel}_{2}^{\rm un}(F)
as the kernel of a linear map between finite dimensional
{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-vector spaces.

We show next that for large and generic enough choices of

u=r_{1}+r_{2}-1
for the unit group rank and write

Lemma 9.1

Let
T
be the set of primes of
F
above
S

\langle T\rangle
denote the subspace of
{\rm Cl}_{F}/2{\rm Cl}_{F}
spanned by the prime ideals in

\dim H^{1}_{S}=u+h+|T|-|S|-\dim\langle T\rangle

When
S
is empty, this follows from the relative version of (
2.1
). We prove the general case by induction on
|S|
. Suppose the lemma is proven for
S
and let
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{p\}
. Let
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}
be the primes of
F
above

has odd degree. Let
T^{\prime}
be the primes of
F
above
S^{\prime}
. It is enough to show that
\dim H^{1}_{S^{\prime}}-\dim H^{1}_{S}=k-1-n
where

. For each

, let
H^{1}_{S}\subset H^{1}_{j}\subset H^{1}_{S^{\prime}}
be the subgroup of
H^{1}_{S^{\prime}}
consisting of square-classes whose odd valuations are contained in the set
T_{j}:=S\cup\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{j}\}

\psi\colon H^{1}_{j}\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/2{\rm\mathbb{Z}}

w_{j}(t)\pmod{2}

H^{1}_{j-1}

When
j=1
, we have
H^{1}_{S}=H^{1}_{1}
since any

with
w_{1}(t)\equiv 1\pmod{2}
and
w_{i}(t)\equiv 0\pmod{2}

does not have square norm. Thus, it is enough to show that for

if and only if the class of

\langle T_{j}\rangle

with square norm if and only if the image of

With notation as above, if

First note that

be the number of primes of

If

We have

F/K
be a finite extension of number fields, and let
G_{F/K}={\rm Gal}(\tilde{F}/K)
be its Galois group. We may view
G_{F/K}
as
K
-group via the natural conjugation action
{\rm Gal}_{K}\to{\rm Aut}(G_{F/K})
. Note that
G_{F/K}
has trivial
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action if and only if
F/K
is Galois.

Let
\Gamma
be a
K
-group with underlying group
\Gamma_{0}
.

Definition 10.1
.

A
\Gamma
-extension is a finite extension
F/K
together with an inclusion
\Gamma\hookrightarrow G_{F/K}
of
K
-groups.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
be the family of
\Gamma
-extensions of degree
|\Gamma|
, and let
F/K\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
. We assume that the order
m=|\Gamma|
is odd, and hence the extensions
F/K\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
are of odd degree.
If
\Gamma
is the constant
K
-group
\underline{\Gamma_{0}}
associated to
\Gamma_{0}
, then
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma_{0},K}
.
In general, the
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action on
\Gamma
trivializes over a minimal finite Galois extension
R/K
, called the
resolvent field
of
\Gamma
. As the
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action on
G_{F/K}
is certainly trivialized over the Galois closure
\tilde{F}
, we have
R\subset\tilde{F}
. To keep things simple, we assume from now on that
(m,[R\colon K])=1
.

Lemma 10.2
.

Let
H={\rm Gal}(R/K)
. Then
G_{F/K}\simeq\Gamma\rtimes\underline{H}
.

Proof.

The assumption
(m,[R\colon K])=1
, implies that
L:=F\otimes_{K}R
is a field extension of
R
. Since
\Gamma(R)=\Gamma(\overline{K})
has order
m=[F\otimes_{K}R\colon R]=[L\colon R]
, we see that
L/R
is Galois of degree
m
. It follows that
L/K
is Galois, and since
L
is contained in
\tilde{F}
, we must have
L=\tilde{F}
. By the Schur-Zassenhous theorem, we have
G_{L/K}\simeq\Gamma\rtimes G_{R/K}=\Gamma\rtimes\underline{H}
.
∎

Conversely, suppose
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow S_{m}
is a transitive subgroup of
S_{m}
with a normal subgroup
G_{0}\subset G
of order
m
, and suppose that
H:=G/G_{0}
has order prime to
m
. Then
G\simeq G_{0}\rtimes H
, and any extension
F/K
of degree
m
with Galois group
\iota
can be given the structure of
\Gamma
-extension, for some
K
-twist
\Gamma
of
\underline{G_{0}}
. The resolvent field of
\Gamma
is
\tilde{F}^{G_{0}}/K
. Thus,
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is the family of
G
-extensions with resolvent field
R
, where
G:=\Gamma_{0}\rtimes H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\hookrightarrow}}S_{m}
.

In this section, we elaborate on the heuristics presented in the introduction for the distribution of
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
. The family
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
sits in the larger family
\mathcal{F}_{\iota,K}
of degree
m
extensions
F/K
with Galois group
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\hookrightarrow}}S_{m}
, and it is natural to guess that the distribution of
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is the same as for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\iota,K}
. The latter distribution has been predicted by Malle, at least under certain irreducibility assumptions
[
Mal10
,
AM15
]
; as discussed in Section
9
. Sawin–Wood give a prediction without such assumptions, but assuming
|G|
is odd.

10.1.
Case
|G|
odd

If
\Gamma
has trivial
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action, i.e.
\Gamma=\underline{G}
for some group
G
, then
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\iota,K}
and we simply follow the heuristics of Sawin–Wood. More generally, if
G=\Gamma_{0}\rtimes H
has odd order, then our heuristics for
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
revert to the heuristics of Sawin–Wood for the larger family
\mathcal{F}_{\iota,K}
(see
[
SW23
, §10]
). The reason for this is that Hecke reciprocity is not relevant when
|G|
is odd, by the following result.

Proposition 10.3
.

Suppose
|G|=|G_{F/K}|
is odd. Then
F/K
is Hecke unramified.

Proof.

If
F/K
is an odd degree
Galois
extension, then the formula
[
Ser79
, p. 64]
for the valuation of
{\rm Diff}_{F/K}
in terms of ramification groups shows that
{\rm Diff}_{F/K}
is a square as an ideal. Thus, in the notation of §
3.1
, the integers
d_{i}
are all even, hence
d=\sum d_{i}f_{i}
is even, and hence the exponents
h_{i}=de_{i}-d_{i}
of
H_{F/K}
are all even. Thus,
H_{F/K}
is a square, i.e.
F/K
is Hecke unramified and admits no Hecke primes.

Now consider the general case where
F/K
is not necessarily Galois. Since
\tilde{F}/K
is an odd degree Galois extension, all ramification indices in
all
subextensions of
\tilde{F}/K
are odd. Now let
j\colon I_{F}\to I_{\tilde{F}}
be the map on ideal groups induced by the inclusion
F\hookrightarrow\tilde{F}
. The formula
{\rm Diff}_{\tilde{F}/K}={\rm Diff}_{\tilde{F}/F}{\rm Diff}_{F/K}
shows that
j({\rm Diff}_{F/K})
is a square in
I_{\tilde{F}}
. It follows that
{\rm Diff}_{F/K}
is a square already in
I_{F}
, because all ramification indices of
\tilde{F}/F
are odd. Thus,
H_{F/K}={\rm Disc}_{F/K}{\rm Diff}_{F/K}^{-1}
is a square as well, and there are no Hecke primes in
F/K
.
∎

10.2.
Case
|G|
even

To formulate our heuristic when
|G|
is even, write
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}\coprod\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
) is the subfamily of
\Gamma
-extensions
F/K
that are Hecke unramified (resp. Hecke ramified).

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
and suppose
w\mid v
is a Hecke prime of
F
. Then either
v
ramifies in the resolvent field
R/K
or there is a prime
w_{i}
over
v
which is wildly ramified.

Proof.

Write
v=\prod_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}^{e_{i}}
, for primes
w_{i}
of
F
and let
f_{i}
be the inertia degree of
w_{i}
over
v
. We may suppose that each
w_{i}
is at most tamely ramified over
v
. We suppose
v
is unramified in
R/K
in order to obtain a contradiction. Since
R/K
is unramified, and since
\tilde{F}
is the compositum of
F
and
R
, it follows that
\tilde{F}/F
is unramified. Since
\tilde{F}/K
is Galois, it follows that all
e_{i}
are equal, say
e_{i}=e
. Write
f=\sum_{i}f_{i}
. Since
\sum_{i}e_{i}f_{i}=e\sum_{i}f_{i}=ef=m
, we see that both
e
and
f
are odd. The assumption that each
w_{i}
is tamely ramified means that
{\rm Diff}_{F\otimes K_{v}/K_{v}}=\prod_{i}w_{i}^{e-1}
. Thus
{\rm Disc}_{F\otimes K_{v}/K_{v}}=v^{(e-1)\sum_{i}f_{i}}=v^{(e-1)f}=v^{m-f}
. The local Hecke ideal is therefore

H_{F\otimes K_{v}/K_{v}}=\prod_{i}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{e(m-f)-e+1}=\prod_{i}{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}^{em-m-e+1},

where
{\rm\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
is the completion of
w_{i}
.
Since each exponent
em-m-e+1
is even, this is the square of an ideal, contradicting the fact that there is a Hecke prime
w
above
v
.
∎

By Proposition
10.4
, the partition of
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}\coprod\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
amounts to imposing constraints on the possible
K_{v}
-algebras
F\otimes_{K}K_{v}
for a
finite
set of primes
v
of
K
, namely those
v
dividing
(m!){\rm Disc}_{R/K}
. In particular, the partition is defined by finitely many local conditions.

Assume now that
G\subset S_{m}
is absolutely irreducible (as at the end of §
9
). Our heuristic is the following: for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
we predict Malle’s distribution, i.e. for any finite
{\rm\mathbb{F}}_{2}
-vector space
V
,

(10.1)

\mathbb{E}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{u}},

where
u
is defined as in
[
Mal10
]
. For
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
we predict the distribution

(10.2)

\mathbb{E}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{u-1}},

taking into account the vacuity of Hecke reciprocity in this subfamily and the random model of Section
9
. Thus, the distribution on
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is a linear combination of these two distributions, weighted by the densities of the two subfamilies. Of course, these densities may be sensitive to the way
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is ordered, so it is better to view them as two different families.

As we show below, it often happens that
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is empty and hence
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
. In this case our heuristic simply says that in
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
the group
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
is distributed as in the larger family
\mathcal{F}_{\iota,K}
. Only when
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is non-empty do the heuristics change, and in this case we say that
\Gamma
is
aberrant
. Sawin–Wood point out inconsistencies in Malle’s conjecture when
G\subset S_{m}
is not absolutely irreducible
[
SW23
, §12.3.2]
, which is why we avoid that case. However, we only really use the rough shape of Malle’s heuristic, the main point being that the parameter
u
should be lowered by
1
on the subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
. In any case, the notion of
\mu_{p}
-being aberrant is well-defined even when
G
is not absolutely irreducible.

10.3.
Examples

We survey some examples of families
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
with
|G|
even and
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}
.

10.3.1.
G=C_{3}\rtimes C_{2}\simeq S_{3}

Here
\Gamma
is a twist of
\underline{C_{3}}
and
H=C_{2}
. The resolvent field
R/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
is quadratic and
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is the family of cubic fields with quadratic resolvent
R
.

Lemma 10.5
.

\Gamma
is aberrant if and only if
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i),{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
or
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{3})
.

Proof.

For
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
, Lemma
3.3
says that a prime
v
is Hecke ramified in
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
if and only if
v
splits as
1^{2}1
and the ramified local quadratic extension is generated by the square-root of a uniformizer when
v=2
. When
v\neq 3
, this happens if and only if
v
ramifies in
R
(and
{\rm Disc}_{R/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}
is divisible by
8
if
v=2
). Indeed, if
v
is totally ramified in
F
, then the Galois closure of
F\otimes{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{v}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
is obtained by adjoining third roots of unity, hence is unramified over
F_{w}
. It follows that
R\otimes{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
is unramified over
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{v}
as well. We conclude that if
\Gamma
is aberrant, then
R
must be unramified away from
2
and
3
, and
{\rm Disc}(R)
must not be divisible by
8
. This cuts the possibilities down to the three listed
R
.

To finish the proof we must show that
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is non-empty in each of these three cases. For such a
\Gamma
, every
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is Hecke unramified away from
3
, so the question is what happens at
3
. For
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i)
, there are no Hecke primes above
3
(since if
F
is ramified at
3
, it must be totally ramified and hence Hecke unramified by Lemma
4.4
). Hence
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}=\mathcal{F}
in this case. For the other two cases, we use the fact that there exists a cubic field totally ramified at
3
in each of these families, e.g.
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{3})
and
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)
, where
\theta
is a root of
x^{3}-15x-20
.
∎

Let us analyze the three aberrant families in more detail.

(1)

When
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i)
, the family
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is given by
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}[x]/(f_{n})
, with
f_{n}=x^{3}+nx^{2}+9x+n
. The proof above shows that
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
(i.e. there are no Hecke primes in the whole family) and so we predict the distribution of
{\rm Cl}_{F}[2]
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is
(\ref{eqn: spin moments})
. See Table
A.4
for a comparison of this prediction with some data.

(2)

When
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
or
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{3})
, there are two possibilities for the splitting type at
3
:
(1^{3})
or
(1^{2}1)
. In the first case
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
since there are no Hecke primes, and in the second case
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
, as there is a unique Hecke prime (above
3
). For
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
, we predict the distribution
(\ref{eqn: spin moments})
, while for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
we predict Malle’s distribution
(\ref{eq: malle moments II})
. Theorem
1.2
is evidence for this in the case
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-3})
. For the case
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{3})
, which corresponds to the family of cubic polynomials
x^{3}-3nx^{2}-3x+n
, see Table
A.5
for data backing up our prediction.

If
R
is not isomorphic to
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i)
or
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{\pm 3})
, we expect the distribution
(\ref{eq: malle moments II})
in the family
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
.

10.3.2.
G=D_{p}\subset S_{p}

One way to generalize the previous example is to consider for each prime
p\geq 3
the dihedral group of order
2p
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{D_{p}}
be the family of
D_{p}
-extensions
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
of degree
p
. Let
\Gamma
be a
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
-group with underlying group
\Gamma_{0}=C_{p}
and suppose that
{\rm Gal}_{K}
acts through a (non-trivial) quadratic character
{\rm Gal}_{K}\to C_{2}\hookrightarrow{\rm Aut}(C_{p})\simeq C_{p-1}
. So again the resolvent field
R/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
of
\Gamma
is quadratic, and
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{F}_{D_{p}}
is the family of
D_{p}
-extensions
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
with quadratic resolvent
R\subset\tilde{F}
.

Proposition 10.6
.

Suppose
v\notin\{2,p\}
is a prime of
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
which is ramified in
R/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
. Then for all
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
, there exists a Hecke prime
w\mid v
in
F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

Since the inertia group of a prime
u
above
v
in
\tilde{F}
is cyclic,
u
has ramification index
2
, and the ramification indices
e_{i}
of the primes
w_{i}
of
F
over
v
are all either
1
or
2
. At least one
e_{i}
is greater than
1
, so the inertia degrees
f_{i}
, which a priori are either
1
or
p
, must all equal
f_{i}=1
. Thus the inertia group coincides with the decomposition group
D(u|v)
. The primes
w_{i}
correspond to the orbits of
D(u|v)
on
\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Since
D(u|v)
is generated by a reflection with exactly one fixed point, there must be
N=(p-1)/2
such primes. The splitting type of
v
in
F
is thus
(11^{2}1^{2}\ldots 1^{2})
.

Write
v=\prod w_{i}^{e_{i}}
and
{\rm Diff}_{F/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}=\prod w_{i}^{d_{i}}
. The discriminant exponent
d=\sum d_{i}f_{i}
is equal to
\sum d_{i}=(p-1)/2
. The Hecke ideal is therefore
\prod w_{i}^{de_{i}-d_{i}}=\prod w_{i}^{e_{i}(p-1)/2-d_{i}}
. The unramified prime
w_{1}
has exponent
(p-1)/2
, which is odd if and only if
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, but even in that case
w_{1}
fails the second technical condition in the definition of a Hecke prime. However, the
g:=(p-1)/2
ramified primes
w_{2},\ldots,w_{g+1}
have
e_{i}=2
and hence Hecke exponent
p-1-1=p-2
, which is odd. These are indeed Hecke primes, as the technical condition is always satisfied by a prime such that
e_{i}f_{i}
is even (see the proof of Lemma
2.6
).
∎

Thus, for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
to be Hecke unramified,
R
must be unramified away from
\{2,p\}
. As in the cubic case, a more careful analysis at
2
also rules out the case that
R\otimes{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{2}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{2}
is ramified and generated by the square-root of a uniformizer. Thus, in degree
p
there are three aberrant
\Gamma
, corresponding to
R={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(i)
,
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{p})
, or
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p})
, a direct generalization of the cubic case. For other resolvent fields
R/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
, we expect the distribution of
{\rm Cl}_{F}[2]
for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
to match that for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{D_{p}}
.

Note that
D_{p}\subset S_{p}
is not absolutely irreducible in Malle’s sense unless
p=3
, so Malle’s heuristics in this case are more involved and on shakier ground (as previously mentioned). However, see
[
Mal10
]
, who discusses the case
p=5
in some detail.

10.3.3.
G\subset F_{p}\subset S_{p}

More generally, if
\Gamma
has underlying group
C_{p}
, then there is some subgroup
H\subset C_{p-1}={\rm Aut}(C_{p})
through which the
{\rm Gal}_{K}
-action on
\Gamma
factors. Then
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
is a subfamily of
\mathcal{F}_{G,{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}
, where
G=C_{p}\rtimes H\subset F_{p}\simeq C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1}
is a subgroup of the Frobenius group
F_{p}\subset S_{p}
of order
p(p-1)
. The resolvent field is a (cyclic)
H
-extension
R/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
.

If we fix
p
and consider all twists
\Gamma
of
\underline{C_{p}}
, then it is natural to guess, following Proposition
10.6
, that only finitely many such
\Gamma
are aberrant. We saw above that this is true for
p=3
and it is true for all
p
if we restrict to dihedral twists, but we do not know whether this is true in general. We simply note that one example of an aberrant
\Gamma
with
H=C_{p-1}
(i.e.
G=F_{p}
) is
\Gamma=\mu_{p}
. We study this example in more detail in the next section.

10.3.4.
Other examples

The examples above cover all
\Gamma
-extensions of degree
|\Gamma|
with
|G_{\Gamma}|
even, up to degree
7
. In degree
9
we encounter other primitive transitive subgroups
G\subset S_{9}
, the smallest being
C_{3}^{2}\rtimes C_{4}
.
More generally, one can consider a group
\Gamma_{0}
of odd order and let
G
be a subgroup of
\Gamma_{0}\rtimes{\rm Aut}(\Gamma_{0})
containing
\Gamma_{0}
. It would be interesting to collect data for
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
for more such
\Gamma
.

It would also be desirable to relax some of our assumptions, e.g. that
F/K
has degree
|\Gamma|
, that
\Gamma
has odd order, or that
(m,[R\colon K])=1
. For example, what can one say about the heuristics for
{\rm Cl}_{F/K}[2]
in degree
n
families of
S_{n}
-extensions with a fixed quadratic resolvent field (corresponding to
A_{n}\subset S_{n}
)? Proposition
10.4
may fail in this level of generality, so that one of the subfamilies
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
or
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{2}
may be a non-empty thin subfamily.

11.
Heuristics for class groups of Kummer extensions

Let
K={\rm\mathbb{Q}}
and let
\Gamma=\mu_{p}
, for an odd prime
p>2
. In the notation of the previous section, the group
G_{\Gamma}
is the Frobenius group of order
p(p-1)
. By Kummer theory,
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}
is the family of pure degree
p
fields
F_{n}={\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[p]{n})
. In this section, we make the predictions of §
10
explicit for this family.

Lemma 11.1
.

\mu_{p}
is an aberrant
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
-group. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}^{1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}
is the subfamily of wildly ramified fields
F_{n}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}^{2}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{p}}
is the subfamily of tamely ramified fields
F_{n}
.

Proof.

Lemma
5.2
shows that
F_{n}
is Hecke unramified if and only if
F_{n}
is wildly ramified at
p
. When
n
is a
p
-th power in
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
, the field
F_{n}
is tamely ramified at
p
, which shows that
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}^{1}
is non-empty and hence
\mu_{p}
is aberrant.
∎

The Galois group
G_{\Gamma}=G_{F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}
is isomorphic to the Frobenius group
G_{p}
of order
p(p-1)
, i.e. the semidirect product of
{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/p{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
with
({\rm\mathbb{Z}}/p{\rm\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
, with respect to the natural scalar action. (We have changed notation from
F_{p}
to
G_{p}
to avoid confusion with the fields
F_{n}
.) The short exact sequence

0\to{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/p{\rm\mathbb{Z}}\to G_{p}\to({\rm\mathbb{Z}}/p{\rm\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}\to 0

corresponds to

0\to{\rm Gal}(\tilde{F}_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p}))\to G_{F_{n}/{\rm\mathbb{Q}}}\to{\rm Gal}({\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})\to 0.

We record the representation theory of
G_{p}
below.

Lemma 11.2
.

The irreducible
{\rm\mathbb{C}}
-representations of
G_{p}
are the
p-1
characters
G_{p}\to({\rm\mathbb{Z}}/p{\rm\mathbb{Z}})^{\times}
and the
(p-1)
-dimensional representation
\mathrm{cyc}
, which has a model over
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}
given by the natural action of
\mu_{p}\rtimes{\rm Gal}({\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})/K)
on
{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})
, with
\mu_{p}\simeq{\rm\mathbb{Z}}/p{\rm\mathbb{Z}}
acting by multiplication.

Proof.

Note that
\mathrm{cyc}
is irreducible as
\mu_{p}\rtimes{\rm Gal}({\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
-representation, since
G_{p}
permutes the
p-1
distinct irreducible
{\rm Gal}({\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})/{\rm\mathbb{Q}})
-subrepresentations. Since
(p-1)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}1^{2}=p(p-1)=|G_{p}|
, these are all the irreducible representations of
G_{p}
.
∎

Let
G^{\prime}\subset G_{p}
be the subgroup (cyclic of order
p-1
) fixing
F_{n}
.

Lemma 11.3
.

The induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{G^{\prime}}^{G_{p}}1_{G^{\prime}}
is isomorphic to
1_{G_{p}}\oplus\mathrm{cyc}
.

Proof.

The representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{G^{\prime}}^{G_{p}}1
is a
p
-dimensional subrepresentation of the regular representation. By the description of the irreducible representations of
G_{p}\simeq G_{\Gamma}
given in Lemma
11.2
, it must be isomorphic to
\chi\oplus\mathrm{cyc}
for some character
\chi
. By Frobenius reciprocity, we have
\chi=1
.
∎

Lemma
11.3
shows that
F_{p}\subset S_{p}
is absolutely irreducible. Our prediction for the family
\mathcal{F}^{1}_{\Gamma}
of wild
F_{n}
therefore states that for any finite abelian
2
-group
V

(11.1)

\mathbb{E}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{(p-3)/2}},

whereas for the tame fields

we predict

(11.2)

\mathbb{E}(|\mathrm{Surj}({\rm Cl}_{F}[2],V)|)=\dfrac{|\wedge^{2}V|}{|V|^{(p-1)/2}}.

11.1.
Conjecture

By

\frac{2}{3}\prod_{j\geq 2}(1+2^{-j})^{-1}

{\rm Cl}_{{\rm\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt[3]{n})}[q^{\infty}]

\prod_{j\geq 1}(1+q^{-j})^{-1}
, see
[
SW23

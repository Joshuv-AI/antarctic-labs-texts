Tensor-Hochschild complex
Slava Pimenov, Angel Toledo

Introduction

For an associative algebra
A
an infinitesimal deformation of the multiplication
\mu\colon A\mathop{\otimes}\limits A\to A
is given by

\mu^{\prime}(x,y)=\mu(x,y)+\delta(x,y)\varepsilon,

where
\delta
is a also an element of
\mathrm{Hom}(A\mathop{\otimes}\limits A,A)
. The associativity of the new multiplication
\mu^{\prime}
, ignoring
terms in
\varepsilon
of degrees at least two, is linear in
\delta
and can be seen to be equivalent to the Hochschild 2-cocycle condition
on
\delta
.

This simple observation can be upgraded to a more precise statement, the Hochschild cochain complex
C^{\bullet}(A,A)
of the associative algebra
A
possesses a
1
-shifted Lie bracket
[-,-]_{G}
, known
as the Gerstenhaber bracket (
[
Ger
]
). This dg-Lie algebra describes a formal deformation functor of
A
, which sends an Artinian algebra
R

to the set of algebra maps from
R
to the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of
C^{\bullet}(A,A)[1]
equipped with the Gerstenhaber bracket.

This can be generalized to the setting of dg-algebras, in which case the Hochschild complex controls the deformation of
A
in the category of
curved
A_{\infty}
-algebras. Furthermore, it can be extended to dg-categories, as has been shown in
[
KL
]
. If a dg-category
\mathcal{C}
satisfies certain
boundedness assumption on the cohomology then its Hochschild complex controls the Morita deformations of
\mathcal{C}
.

.

Now, let
(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)
be a monoidal category, and denote by
T^{n}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits n}\to\mathcal{C}
for
n\geqslant 1
the iterated tensor product functor. In case when
the monoidal structure is unital we also put
T^{0}\colon*\to C
the functor from the point category (with a single object with the trivial automorphism group) to
\mathcal{C}

that sends the object to the unit
I
of the monoidal structure. Davydov, Crane-Yetter, and Yetter (
[
Dav
]
,
[
CY
]
,
[
Yet
]
) have constructed a complex out of endomorphisms of these functors by putting

C^{n}_{DY}(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)=\mathrm{End}(T^{n})
, with the differentials mimicking the differentials of the Hochschild complex. They have shown that the second cohomology group
of this complex controls the infinitesimal deformation of the identity
\mathrm{Id}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
as the monoidal functor, and the third cohomology controls the
deformation of the associator of the monoidal structure itself. More precisely, the complex
C^{\bullet}_{DY}(\mathcal{C})
is equipped with a 2-shifted dg-Lie algebra structure
describing a formal deformation functor for the associator of monoidal structure on
\mathcal{C}
.

.

This complex also admits a more conceptual interpretation (
[
GHS
]
). Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)
be the Drinfeld center of the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. Objects of
\mathcal{Z}
are pairs

(X,\rho_{X})
, consisting of an object
X\in\mathcal{C}
and a natural transformation expressing centrality of
X
:

\rho_{X}\colon X\mathop{\otimes}\limits(-)\to(-)\mathop{\otimes}\limits X.

The forgetful functor
U\colon\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{C}
has a left adjoint “centralization” functor
Z\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{Z}
. Moreover
U
is monadic, which means that the Drinfeld center is equivalent
to the category of
UZ

\mathcal{C}
. Iterating the composition of functors
U
and

we obtain a standard resolution
P^{\bullet}(X)
of an object
X\in\mathcal{Z}
by free

-modules, by setting

P^{n}(X)=(ZU)^{n}(X)
.

The unit object

of the monoidal structure is the image of object
(I,\rho_{I})\in\mathcal{Z}
under forgetful functor, where
\rho_{I}
is expressed as composition of the unit identities:

C^{\bullet}_{DY}(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)

0.3
.

The shortcoming of the Davydov-Yetter complex as a deformation complex of monoidal category
(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)
is twofold. First, the underlying category
\mathcal{C}
remains intact under
such deformations, and secondly even if we are only interested in the deformations of the monoidal structure leaving
\mathcal{C}
intact, the complex only captures the deformation of the
associator maps, and not the deformation of tensor product on objects or maps.

The main result of this paper is the construction of the
\mathop{\otimes}\limits
-Hochschild complex
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)
that captures deformation of all the data of the monoidal structure
on
\mathcal{C}
together with the underlying category itself. A cochain of this complex of bidegree
(p,q)
takes a rectangle of composable arrows of height
(p+1)
and length
q
as in the picture
below and sends it to a map from the tensor product of starting objects of each row to the tensor product of terminal objects.

f_{00}
\cdots
f_{0,q-1}
\cdots
f_{p0}
\cdots
f_{p,q-1}
\vdots
\vdots

In the case when associator maps of the monoidal structure are all identities the complex
TC^{\bullet}
is the totalization of a bicomplex
with vertical differential
d_{0}
given by the Hochschild differential and horizontal differential
d_{1}
by the differential of the Davydov-Yetter complex.
In general however, this is not a bicomplex, and the sum
d_{0}+d_{1}
has to be adjusted by higher terms
d_{n}
, for
n\geqslant 2
expressing coherence of the tensor product and
the categorical composition to ensure that the total differential squares to zero. We give an explicit description of these higher terms of the differential
via certain “admissible” paths in Stasheff associahedra.

.

The complex
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
comes with natural maps relating it to the Hochschild and Davydov-Yetter complexes.

We show that if the underlying category
\mathcal{C}
is semisimple, then the first inclusion is a quasi-isomorphism. In other words, the usual Davydov-Yetter complex
is fully sufficient to study deformations of semisimple monoidal categories.

0.5
.

A similar idea of combining Hochschild and Davydov-Yetter complexes to describe the deformations of
(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)
together with the deformations
of the underlying category
\mathcal{C}
has been already pursued in the literature in
[
Shr
]
, and more recently including the dg-category case in
[
PS
]
where they construct a slightly different complex. Translating into our
language they consider a subcomplex of
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
consisting of cochains, such that the two differentials
d_{0}
and
d_{1}
form a bicomplex.
In particular all the higher terms
d_{n}
vanish on such cochains, and they do not appear in their discussion at all. While the two complexes coincide
when the associators are all identities, it seems unlikely that they are quasi-isomorphic in general.

0.6
.

In the second part of the paper we consider several special cases and examples. First we study two cases where the associator maps are given by identities
and show that our complex is quasi-isomorphic to the previously constructed deformations complexes in those settings.

First, we consider the case when the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
consist of a single object
I
, the unit of the monoidal structure. The endomorphisms of this
object is a commutative algebra
A
, or more generally an
E_{2}
-algebra if we are working with a dg-category
\mathcal{C}
. The deformation complex of
A
as an

E_{2}
-algebra is given by an analog of the Hochschild complex for operad
E_{2}
. By comparing two spectral sequences, one converging to the operadic cohomology of
A

and another converging to cohomology of
\mathop{\otimes}\limits
-Hochschild complex of
\mathcal{C}
, we show that after shift of grading by one the two deformation complexes are quasi-isomorphic
(proposition
2.4
).

The other case is the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
of representations of a bialgebra
B
. The deformation of
B
as a bialgebra (or more precisely a quasi-bialgebra) is
controlled by the Gerstenhaber-Schack complex. And we show that
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
is quasi-isomorphic to this complex (section
2.3
).

The equivalence of these two special cases has been studies previously (for instance
[
GY
]
) and our discussion here is just a reiteration of those results.

0.7
.

Since the complex
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
generalizes the
E_{2}
-algebra cohomology complex, it is reasonable to expect a version of Deligne’s conjecture to hold. Namely,
that there is an action of the chain complex of an
E_{3}
-operad on
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
. It would be interesting to make it explicit.

In general the deformation theory of an algebraic structure is controlled by a Lie-infinity algebra (cf.
[
KS
]
), which is a graded vector space, equipped
with a collection of operations
l_{n}
of arity
n
and degree
(2-n)
for
n\geqslant 1
, satisfying certain compatibility conditions. One should think of the operation
l_{1}
as the differential,

l_{2}
as the Lie bracket,
l_{3}
controls the failure of the Jacobi identity to hold, etc.

Of course such Lie-infinity algebra is defined only
up to Lie-infinity quasi-isomorphism, and any such algebra can be strictified to a dg-Lie algebra, where all operations
l_{n}
for
n\geqslant 3
vanish. Unfortunately we do not possess the
general theory, similar to that of the algebraic operads, which would be applicable to monoidal dg-categories, and produce in a systematic way
a dg-Lie algebra controlling its deformations. Instead we are trying to make an educated guess as to what such complex should be, and it is unlikely
we will be luck enough to guess the strictified dg-Lie algebra. In fact it is not difficult to see that the complex proposed in our work doesn’t admit a
dg-Lie algebra structure describing deformation of monoidal categories, therefore we expect that it is instead a Lie-infinity algebra, with non-vanishing
higher operations.

Let
L
be a Lie-infinity algebra controlling deformations of some algebraic structure, then the collection of such structures is given by the solutions
\varphi
of the generalized Maurer-Cartan equation.

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{1\over i!}l_{i}(\varphi,\ldots,\varphi)=0.

Let
\varphi
be such a solution, then we can twist the Lie-infinity structure on
L
to produce the tangent complex
L^{\varphi}
at
\varphi
. Explicitly the twisted operations are given by the following
formulas (for the proof we refer to
[
CL
]
).

l_{n}^{\varphi}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{1\over i!}l_{n+i}(\varphi,\ldots,\varphi,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Our main result is an explicit description of operation
l_{1}^{\varphi}
(in other words the differential) in this twisted Lie-infinity algebra. We would like to point out here, that
we do not possess explicit descriptions of operations
l_{n}
or
l_{n}^{\varphi}
in general.

In principle such deformation procedure leads outside of the class of structures considered originally, for instance by deforming an associative algebra one ends up with
a notion of an
A_{\infty}
algebra. Similarly, in this case the generalized Maurer-Cartan elements for this Lie-infinity algebra should be thought of as describing an “infinity-monoidal”
A_{\infty}
-category.

Using this terminology, we would like to compare our result with the work of Shrestha (
[
Shr
]
), where he studies the deformation theory of
R
-linear monoidal
categories over a commutative algebra
R
, in other words with Hom-spaces being
R
-modules with
R
-linear compositions.

First of all, restricting our construction of the reduced
\mathop{\otimes}\limits
-Hochschild cochains
C^{pq}
to a monoidal dg-category with Hom-spaces concentrated in degree
0

we exactly recover the bigraded
R
-module
C^{(p,q)}
of normalized Hochschild cochains considered by Shrestha.

Furthermore, the four differentials defined by Shrestha before theorem 2.0.38 (
[
Shr
]
) coincide with our differential in low degrees, written out explicitly in example
1.3
.
Thus, our result (proposition
1.4
) provides a solution to his conjecture 2.0.39.

Next, he considers obstructions
\omega^{(n)}
to lifting an infinitesimal deformation
\varphi_{1}
to a formal one

\varphi=\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{1}\varepsilon+\varphi_{2}\varepsilon^{2}+\ldots

In terms of the Lie-infinity algebra structure they are expressed as

\omega^{(n)}=l_{1}^{\varphi_{0}}(\varphi_{n+1})+\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}=n+1}{1\over 2!}l_{2}^{\varphi_{0}}(\varphi_{i_{1}},\varphi_{i_{2}})+\cdots+\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{k}=n+1}{1\over k!}l_{k}^{\varphi_{0}}(\varphi_{i_{1}},\ldots,\varphi_{i_{k}})+\cdots.

In our paper we do not consider the problem of obstructions, since, as these formulas show, it would require much more detailed information about the
higher Lie-infinity operations, which our combinatorial techniques do not handle. However, since we have a description of all higher terms of the differential in the twisted complex

l_{1}^{\varphi}
, one could in principle manually verify that the obstructions
\omega^{(1)}
as was considered by Shrestha are indeed cocycles, while deforming all parts of the monoidal structure,
i.e. composition, tensor product of maps and associator, at the same time. We do not perform this calculation in our paper.

.

In section
2.2
we consider the case of a smooth scheme
X
and the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}=D^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{coh}}\nolimits X)
equipped with the tensor product of coherent sheaves. We obtain a spectral sequence
relating the Hochschild cohomology of
X
and the cohomology of
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
. In particular in the case when
\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X\leqslant 2
this spectral sequence degenerates and
we have (theorem
2.6
)

TH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})\ \simeq\ \bigoplus_{p\geqslant 0}H^{\bullet}(X,S^{p+1}T_{X}).

This also implies that all infinitesimal automorphisms of the underlying category
\mathcal{C}
preserve the monoidal structure and any infinitesimal deformation of
\mathcal{C}
lifts
to a deformation of
\mathcal{C}
together with the monoidal structure (corollary
2.7
).

0.9
.

Finally, in the section
2.4
we work out a specific example. Let
Q_{n}
the a quiver with two vertices and
n
arrows going from the first vertex to the second.
We consider the bounded derived category
\mathcal{C}
of representations of
Q_{n}
and show that it is rigid, in the sense that cohomology of
TC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})
vanishes in all degrees.
It would be interesting to investigate the question of rigidity for other quivers.

In the particular case of
Q_{2}
the underlying category
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to the category
D^{b}(\mathop{\mathrm{coh}}\nolimits\mathbb{P}^{1})
, but not monoidal equivalent as their tensor products differ. By comparing the deformation
complexes for these two structures we see that they lie on two different orbits of the
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{C})
action on the space of monoidal structures.

1.6
Relation to Hochschild and Davydov-Yetter cohomology..

First, consider the decreasing filtration of
TC^{\bullet}
by slicing it in the vertical direction:

F_{j}(TC^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{p\geqslant j}C^{pq}.

The associated graded piece
\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits^{0}_{F}(TC^{\bullet})
is isomorphic to the relative Hochschild complex of dg-category
\mathcal{C}
over
R
. In particular in the case when
R=k
is a field it is the
usual Hochschild complex of
\mathcal{C}
, and when
\mathcal{C}
is a category with a single object with the endomorphism ring

we recover the relative Hochschild cohomology complex
of algebras
HH^{\bullet}(E\ |\ R)
.

The natural map of cohomologies induced by the surjective map
TC^{\bullet}\twoheadrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{gr}}\nolimits^{0}_{F}(TC^{\bullet})
corresponds to forgetting the monoidal structure of
(\mathcal{C},\mathop{\otimes}\limits)
.

Next let us consider the increasing filtration of
TC^{\bullet}
by canonically slicing it in the horizontal direction:

G^{j}(TC^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{q<j}C^{pq}\oplus\bigoplus\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}\nolimits(d_{0}\colon C^{pj}\to C^{p,j+1}).

It is immediate to see that the vanishing of the differential
d_{0}\colon C^{p0}\to C^{p1}
expresses the naturality of the collection of endomorphisms in
C^{p0}
, thus

G^{0}(TC^{\bullet})^{p0}=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits p}).

And the complex
G^{0}(TC^{\bullet})
is isomorphic to the Davydov-Yetter complex of the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
. We denote
H^{\bullet}_{DY}(\mathcal{C})
the cohomology of this complex.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a semisimple finitely generated dg-category, then the natural map

H^{\bullet}_{DY}(\mathcal{C})\to H^{\bullet}(TC^{\bullet})

induced by the inclusion
G^{0}(TC^{\bullet})\hookrightarrow TC^{\bullet}
is an isomorphism.

Proof:
Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be a collection of simple objects generating
\mathcal{C}
. The endomorphism algebra
A=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(\bigoplus X_{i})
is isomorphic to the direct sum
\mathbf{k}^{n}
.
Similarly, the tensor product
A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits p}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{k}^{n^{p}}
.

Consider the spectral sequence associated to the filtration
F
. Since the collection of
\{X_{i}\}
generates
\mathcal{C}
, on the the layer
E_{1}
of the spectral sequence the column
p

is isomorphic to the Hochschild cohomology

E_{1}^{pq}\ \simeq\ HH^{\bullet}\left(A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)},\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}((\bigoplus X_{i})^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)})\right).

These cohomology groups vanish for
q>1
, and for
q=0
they are isomorphic to
G^{0}(TC^{\bullet})
. The differential on

coincides with the restriction of the
differential of
TC^{\bullet}
to

and the statement immediately follows.

\square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n}(UTC)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{n+1}(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(I))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}

For convenience we will write

UTH^{n}(\mathcal{C})=H^{n}(UTC^{\bullet}(\mathcal{C})).

2
Special cases and examples

In this section we will work over a ground field
\mathbf{k}
of characteristic
0
.

2.1
Commutative algebra.

Consider a monoidal dg-category
\mathcal{C}
consisting of a single object
I
, which is the unit for the monoidal structure. Then the space of endomorphisms of
I
is an
E_{2}
algebra, that we will
denote by
A
. In this case, the spaces of the
\mathop{\otimes}\limits
-Hochschild cochains are

C^{pq}=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}\left(\left(A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)}\right)^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits q},\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(I^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)},I^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)})\right).

The target of the hom-space is of course just
A
, and it is equipped with the obvious structure of
A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)}
-bimodule.

Consider the spectral sequence associated to the stupid vertical filtration
F_{\bullet}(TC^{\bullet})
of the
\mathop{\otimes}\limits
-Hochschild complex defined in
1.6
.
The differentials in
E_{0}^{p\bullet}
are just the Hochschild differentials for the algebra
A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)}
with coefficients in the bimodule
A
. Therefore, we have

(2.0.1)

E_{1}^{pq}=HH^{q}(A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)},A)\Rightarrow TH^{p+q}(\mathcal{C}).

Let us study the case when
A
is a smooth commutative algebra of finite type.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
A
is a smooth commutative algebra, then

TH^{\bullet}(A)\ \simeq\ A\mathop{\otimes}\limits\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 1}S_{A}^{p+1}(\mathrm{Der}(A))[-2p-1].

Proof:
Since
A
is smooth, the module of differentials
\Omega^{1}_{A}

as well as the module of derivations
\mathrm{Der}(A)
are finitely generated projective
A
-modules.
Localizing if necessary we may assume them to be free, and we will write
\mathrm{Der}(A)\simeq A\mathop{\otimes}\limits V
, where
V
is a vector space of dimension equal to the dimension of
A
.

Since
A
is smooth commutative algebra the tensor products
A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)}
are also smooth commutative algebras and we have

\mathrm{Der}(A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)},A)\ \simeq\ A\mathop{\otimes}\limits V^{\oplus(p+1)}.

Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem gives us identification

HH^{q}(A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)},A)\ \simeq\ \Lambda^{q}_{A}\mathrm{Der}(A^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)},A)\ \simeq\ A\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Lambda^{q}(V^{\oplus(p+1)}).

Let
\partial x
by an element of
V
, then we will write
\partial x_{i}\in A\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Lambda^{q}(V^{\oplus(p+1)})
,
0\leqslant i\leqslant p
, for the corresponding element coming from the
i
’th copy of
V
.
The generators of
E_{1}^{pq}
can then be given by the exterior products

\partial x^{1}_{i_{1}}\wedge\partial x^{2}_{i_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{q}_{i_{q}},

with
0\leqslant i_{1}\leqslant i_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant i_{q}\leqslant p
.

2.2
.

Let us denote by
\pi(n)
the parity of
n
, i.e.,
\pi(n)=0
if
n
is even and
\pi(n)=1
if
n
is odd.

It is immediate to see from the definition of the component
d_{1}
of the differential of the complex
TC^{\bullet}
that on the first layer of our spectral sequence the differential
can be explicitly written as follows. For
q=1
we have

\partial x_{i}\mapsto\pi(i)\partial x_{i+1}+(-1)^{p}\pi(p-i)\partial x_{i}.

For
q=2
we have

\displaystyle\partial x_{i}\wedge\partial y_{j}\mapsto\pi(i)\partial x_{i+1}\wedge\partial y_{j+1}+

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+1}\pi(j-i+1)\partial x_{i}\wedge\partial y_{j+1}-

\displaystyle(-1)^{i+1}\delta_{ij}\partial y_{i}\wedge\partial x_{i+1}+(-1)^{p}\pi(p-j)\partial x_{i}\wedge\partial y_{j}.

Here
i\leqslant j
and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol.

Generally, the set of
i_{j}
determine an ordered partition of the set of indices
[1,q]
into
m
blocks
I_{l}
,
1\leqslant l\leqslant m
, such that for any two indices
j
,
k
within one block
we have
i_{j}=i_{k}
, and we will denote this shared value by
v(I_{l})
, and moreover if
j\in I_{l_{1}}
,
k\in I_{l_{2}}
with
l_{1}<l_{2}
then
i_{j}<i_{k}
.
Denote by
Sh(i,j)
the set of
(i,j)
-shuffles, and put by definition
i_{0}=-1
and
i_{q+1}=p+1
. Then the differential is given by

\partial x^{1}_{i_{1}}\wedge\partial x^{2}_{i_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{q}_{i_{q}}\mapsto\sum_{j=0}^{q}(-1)^{i_{j}+1}\pi(i_{j+1}-i_{j}+1)\partial x^{1}_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{j}_{i_{j}}\wedge\partial x^{j+1}_{i_{j+1}+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{q}_{i_{q}+1}+\\
\sum_{l=1}^{m}\quad\sum_{j+k=|I_{l}|\atop j,k>0}\ \sum_{\sigma\in Sh(j,k)\atop\sigma\neq\mathrm{id}}(-1)^{v(I_{l})+1+|\sigma|}\partial x^{\sigma(1)}_{i_{1}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{\sigma(j)}_{i_{j}}\wedge\partial x^{\sigma(j+1)}_{i_{j+1}+1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{\sigma(q)}_{i_{q}+1}.

For convenience, one could visualize this differential up to the symmetrization summand as edges of a hypercube in a
(q+1)
-dimensional lattice, with elements corresponding to tuples
(p,i_{1},\ldots,i_{q})

and the basis vectors corresponding to vectors of the form
(1,0,\ldots,0,1,\ldots,1)
.

It is clear that generally such hypercubes contribute acyclic pieces of the complex (even when one includes the symmetrization summands), so the only contribution to cohomology
comes from the edge cases when the hypercubes are missing some faces. In fact it only comes from cocycles of the form

\partial x^{1}_{0}\wedge\partial x^{2}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\partial x^{p+1}_{p}\in E_{1}^{p,p+1},

which are not fully killed due to the presence of the symmetrization part:

E_{2}^{p,p+1}={V^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p+1)}\over\Lambda^{2}V\mathop{\otimes}\limits V^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p-1)}+V\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Lambda^{2}V\mathop{\otimes}\limits V^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p-2)}+\cdots+V^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(p-1)}\mathop{\otimes}\limits V}\simeq S^{p+1}V.

Putting it all together we find

E_{2}^{p,q}=\begin{cases}S^{p+1}V,&\textrm{if $q=p+1$},\\
0,&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The spectral sequence degenerates at the second layer, therefore

TH^{\bullet}(A)\ \simeq\ A\mathop{\otimes}\limits\bigoplus_{p\geqslant 1}S_{A}^{p+1}(\mathrm{Der}(A))[-2p-1].

And for the unital
\mathop{\otimes}\limits
-Hochschild complex

UTH^{\bullet}(A)\ \simeq\ A\mathop{\otimes}\limits S_{A}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Der}(A)[-2])[1].

\square

2.3
Comparison with the operadic
E_{2}
-cohomology..

Let us compare this result to the cohomology of

as an
E_{2}
-algebra. This can be calculated as the
\mathrm{Ext}
-algebra

\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{HH_{\bullet}(A)}(A,A),

where the Hochschild homology

is considered as an algebra equipped with the shuffle product, and

is a

-moduls in a natural way.
In the case of a smooth algebra

the Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem gives us isomorphism of algebras

HH_{\bullet}(A)\ \simeq\ \Omega_{A}^{\bullet},

where
\Omega_{A}^{\bullet}
(without de Rham differential) is graded so that
\Omega_{A}^{p}
sits in degree
-p
and is equipped with the exterior product of differential forms.
The algebra

as
\Omega_{A}^{\bullet}
-module is
the quotient
\Omega_{A}^{\bullet}/\Omega_{A}^{\geqslant 1}
. We find

\mathrm{Ext}^{\bullet}_{\Omega_{A}^{\bullet}}(A,A)\ \simeq\ A\mathop{\otimes}\limits S^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Der}(A)[-2]),

which up to shift by

coincides with our
UTH^{\bullet}(A)

This identification holds in greater generality for any commutative algebra (and even

-algebra), not just in the smooth case.

Lemma 2.18
.

Consider the semi-free associative dg-algebra on
n
generators
R_{n}=\mathbf{k}\!\left<x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right>
, with
\mathop{\mathrm{deg}}\nolimits x_{i}=1
, and differential given by
dx_{i}=x_{i}^{2}
. Then

H^{\bullet}(R)=\mathbf{k}.

Proof:
First of all, notice that if
n=1
, then the dg-algebra
R_{1}
is
(\mathbf{k}\!\left<x\right>,dx=x^{2})
and the statement is obvious. In general, the dg-algebra
R_{n}
is isomorphic to
the coproduct in the category of associative dg-algebras of
n
copies of
R_{1}
and the statement follows from the following simple observation.

Let
P
and
Q
be two associative dg-algebras concentrated in non-negative degrees, such that
H^{\bullet}(P)\simeq\mathbf{k}
and
H^{\bullet}(Q)\simeq\mathbf{k}
, furthermore, assume that

Q
is augmented, and denote by
\overline{Q}
the augmentation ideal. Let
P*Q
be their coproduct, then we have decomposition

P*Q=P\oplus\left(P\mathop{\otimes}\limits\overline{Q}\mathop{\otimes}\limits P\right)\oplus\bigoplus_{k\geqslant 2}P\mathop{\otimes}\limits\left(\overline{Q}\mathop{\otimes}\limits P\right)^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits k}.

By assumption, the first summand is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathbf{k}
. Since
\overline{Q}
is acyclic, using Kunneth formula we conclude that the rest of the summands are also acyclic, hence

P*Q
is quasi-isomorphic to
\mathbf{k}
.

\square

Now, consider the complex described in paragraph
2.17
. Let us embed it into
R_{n}
by mapping
E_{1}^{p1}\to R_{n}^{p+3}
as follows:

[x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{p}}]_{j}\mapsto x_{j}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\ldots x_{i_{p}}x_{j}.

Clearly, this embedding is compatible with differentials on
E_{1}
and in
R_{n}
. Moreover, the image of the embedding is a direct summand of complex
R_{n}
, hence
E_{1}^{p1}
is acyclic,
as
R_{n}
is acyclic in degrees greater or equal than three. Therefore, the spectral sequence degenerates starting from
E_{2}
and all the cohomology groups vanish.

This completes the proof of proposition
2.11
.

\square

It may be of a particular interest to look at the case of the quiver

,
by sending module

to the structure sheaf

and

. To avoid confusion we will write

Factors affecting clinical experience of nursing students in Malaysia: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Nurul N. H. Baba, Subhashini N. Govindan, Basirah A. Ghani, Duangta Shet, Y.S Pek
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Nursing education covers pathophysiology, nursing procedures, and compassionate care. Nursing students can apply theory and develop skills in the real world by participating in a supportive learning environment. For nursing students to effectively apply what they have learned in the classroom, they need a supportive clinical learning environment. However, some difficulties and impediments may prevent them from learning and having excellent clinical placement experiences. This study aims to identify factors affecting nursing students’ clinical experience during clinical placement.MATERIALS AND METHODS:The respondents in this descriptive cross-sectional study were at Universiti Kuala Lumpur (RCMP) Diploma in Nursing students in Ipoh, Perak, who had more than two clinical placements at a public hospital. The time frame of the study was 6 months. Sociodemographic information and elements affecting clinical practice, such as perceptions of clinical experience, hospital settings, and clinical instructor aspects, were obtained by a self-administered questionnaire. A descriptive analysis and the Kruskal–Wallis test were used to obtain the study’s outcome.RESULTS:Although the clinical placement demonstrated possibilities for learning, research has shown that the absence of instructors, equipment, and nursing staff has a negative impact on students’ experiences. The study also discovered a substantial correlation between a student’s semester and their clinical experience and between the clinical environment and the contributions of clinical instructors with P < 0.05.CONCLUSION:In conclusion, the study semester, clinical experience, clinical ambient characteristics, and instructors’ contributions are strongly correlated. It suggests boosting academic-clinical collaboration, interprofessional collaboration, and student clinical experiences.

Introduction

Nursing may be regarded as an art and science. Caring for patients with compassion, understanding, communication, and love constitutes the art of nursing. Pathophysiology, disease processes, and nursing practices studied during nursing education and executed in patient care compose the science of nursing. Nursing education aims to teach students how to apply this art and science to provide excellent nursing care to their patients or clients. The theory and practice that makeup nursing education give students the knowledge, abilities, and attitudes they need to care for patients. The clinical placement aims to allow students to practice their nursing skills, clinical reasoning, and professional development. Because nursing is a practice-based profession, Kaphagawani and Useh[1] assert that learning through clinical practice is integral to nursing education. A favorable clinical learning environment is essential for integrating information into practice. Motsaanaka et al.[2] have stated that a clinical setting needs to provide a conducive learning condition so the students can apply classroom knowledge in clinical practice. According to Najari Kalyani et al.,[3] clinical experience is essential to nursing education. In conjunction with the student’s experience, the clinical practice supports the student in acquiring the required skills and knowledge to become competent in nursing and support human health and well-being.[4]

Nevertheless, according to qualitative research on clinical practice experience,[5] South African undergraduate nursing students encountered challenging clinical practice experiences in hospital settings. Their main challenges include failure to apply theory to practice, anxiety, student abuse, aggression, crowding and discrimination, interpersonal interactions, poor communication, and inability to carry out specific procedures. A cross-sectional study conducted by Gemuhay et al.[6] showed that most obstacles reported as preventing good experience in clinical practice learning were caused by an unsupportive environment. This unsupportive environment was primarily caused by a shortage of healthcare personnel in the clinical placement sites and high patient loads. Undergraduate nursing students in Norway participated in a qualitative study highlighting issues with the actual learning environment. These difficulties included a shortage of equipment and unfamiliar, out-of-date, and old equipment.[7] As a result of having to improvise when giving nursing care to the patient, the need for more resources has an unfavorable impact on student learning. In clinical education, it is expected that the instructors and students would create a strong relationship and maintain open lines of communication to maximize the learning that the students gain from their clinical placement. A clinical instructor’s characteristics, such as age, ability to communicate and express themselves clearly, availability, and professional background, can significantly impact the student’s learning capacity. According to Arkan, Ordin, and Yilmaz’s[8] qualitative research in Turkey, there is no evidence that instructors’ characteristics and teaching styles substantially impact students’ learning in clinical environments. Several students in this study mentioned during the interview that instructors should maintain professionalism in real-world situations. On the other hand, the student mentioned that in clinical settings with a lower student-to-instructor ratio, the instructors occasionally need to pay more attention to the number of hours their students spend in clinical practice and fail to ensure their students accomplish their learning responsibilities. A preliminary survey of diploma semester six students revealed that 70% were unhappy with clinical posting, indicating a lack of positive feelings. They viewed more supervision and insufficient equipment as factors affecting learning outcomes. Further research is needed to improve the clinical setting environment and patient outcomes.

The present study aimed to identify factors affecting nursing students’ clinical experience during clinical placement. This research can be utilized to create a strategy for supporting nursing students during their clinical placement. This study can also improve future nursing education programs by enhancing clinical placement allocation, strengthening student support, and fostering positive relationships with nurse educators and healthcare team members.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Setting

This is a quantitative research approach utilizing a descriptive cross-sectional survey methodology. This study was conducted to understand better the difficulties nursing students faced throughout their clinical placement. The participants in this study were UniKL RCMP Diploma in Nursing students in Ipoh, Perak. It targeted students who have completed more than two rotations of clinical posting at one public hospital in Perak.

Study participant and sampling

There are 464 students pursuing diploma in nursing. Krejcie and Morgan’s[9] table calculates the sample size. A total of 295 students participated in the study, excluding semesters one and two, which had less than two clinical rotations at one public hospital in Perak. A minimum of 165 students must be chosen by comparing the closest total N to the sample size (s). Purposive sampling was used to choose the sample size of 165 students since only Diploma nursing students who had already completed more than two rotations of clinical posting at one public hospital in Perak were included in the study.

Data Collection Tool and Technique

Four sections make up the survey questions. Age, gender, the current semester, and the number of times respondents have participated in clinical placements at the specified hospital are among the six variables that comprise Section A assessment of respondents’ demographic information. Six items contain Section B evaluation of the clinical placement by the students. The hospital environment aspect is determined by Section C, which has nine categories and encompasses the impact of the staffing crisis and the lack of equipment for nursing procedures. The clinical instructor component of efficient clinical learning and supervision is evaluated using the 12 items in Section D. The questions use a 5-point Likert scale with the options of 1 (Strongly Disagree), 2 (Disagree), 3 (Neutral), 4 (Agree), and 5 (Strongly Agree) to illustrate challenges in the clinical setting. This study’s reliability test Cronbach’s alpha value obtained for the items was 0.903, considering excellent internal consistency. The validity of the content was established by the senior educators from the Nursing Department, and the questionnaires have undergone minor changes to be customized to the organization’s needs. The data was analyzed using IBM Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25 software, where the findings were presented using tables. A non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis test was used to identify factors affecting nursing students’ clinical experience during nursing education programs.

The survey was self-administered using online Google Forms. Before responding to the questionnaire, the respondents received information on the study’s objective and purpose at the beginning of the Google forms. A statement to obtain the respondent’s consent was written below the information. Nursing students who are willing to participate can mark the consent column and proceed to answer the questionnaire. Nursing students who met the inclusion requirements for the study were given the questionnaire. There were a few limitations of the study such as the study’s self-administered questionnaires and participants’ differing views on the topic increased the risk of response bias.

Ethical consideration

The institution’s ethical review board granted its approval. The permission number for the researcher’s ethical review was (UniKL RCMP/MREC/2021-2023/BNSH-252). In addition, the respondent’s participation is voluntary, and consent is obtained. Also, there are no negative consequences for respondents who leave the study at any point. Finally, the questionnaire, dataset, and files are coded and do not contain any information that may be used to identify the participants to safeguard their privacy.

Results

The questionnaire was completed by 190 Diploma in Nursing students. The study has a significantly larger percentage of female respondents than male respondents. Females comprised n = 173 (91.1%) of total responders, while males comprised n = 17 (8.9%). According to semester, the number of students was n = 22 (11.6%) for semester 3, n = 41 (21.6%) for semester 4, n = 21 (11.1%) for semester 5, and n = 106 (55.8%) for semester 6 [Table 1]. Table 2 displays nursing students’ feedback on their clinical experience at the chosen institution. According to the data, most respondents (44.7%) strongly agreed that clinical placement gives a learning opportunity, with a total mean of 4.28. Many also strongly agreed that the theory session was relevant to the clinical environment, with a mean score of 4.22. However, while 65.3% of respondents loved their clinical experience at the chosen hospital, many (35.3%) and (21.1%) disagreed about feeling confident when doing clinical operations. Table 3 shows factors influencing nursing students‘ clinical experience during clinical placement. With a mean value of 4.35, the results revealed that most respondents (50.5%) strongly agreed that variables such as personnel shortage would impact their clinical experience. The students agreed (58.9%) that the typical missing and inadequate equipment component could lead to an unpleasant clinical experience. Furthermore, with an average value of 3.54, most respondents (34.7%) are doubtful about being welcomed as a team member in a hospital context. The component, which refers to the availability of a workstation or room to execute their clinical operation in the ward, has the lowest rated mean (3.48), with 49.5% of respondents responding as doubtful.

Discussion

The research aim guides the discussion of the findings. This study examined factors affecting nursing students’ clinical placement experience. Clinical placement support for nursing students can be developed using this research.

The Clinical Experience of Nursing Students Posting at Clinical Placement

The study found that students agreed that clinical experience offers educational opportunities. These findings align with earlier research by Gemuhay et al.,[6] as most nursing students felt that clinical placements provide sufficient opportunity for learning clinical practice. In this study, it was a remarkable and valuable finding that most students enjoy their clinical experience. According to research by Rodríguez-García et al.,[10] nursing students reported high levels of satisfaction and a positive impression of the supervision and clinical learning environment in the hospital where they completed clinical placements. In addition, a study by Zulu et al.[11] explained that Nursing students encountered negative and positive experiences. Both experiences confirmed that healthcare settings can be valuable learning opportunities for nursing students. Nursing students appreciated the supervision of professional nurses who created an atmosphere conducive to teaching and learning by welcoming and encouraging nursing students’ independence. A previous study at Kerman University of Medical Sciences in Iran found a positive correlation between emotional intelligence and effective learning strategies among postgraduate students.[12] However, contrary to these findings, previous studies have reported that most students were dissatisfied with their clinical experience.[13] It was stated that the students are displeased with their clinical placement experience due to improper clinical course planning, a sense of uselessness and wasted time in the clinical setting, completing monotonous activities, and being assigned to work under the supervision of a ward nurse.

Furthermore, in this study, many participants agreed that the theory session is relevant to clinical settings. However, contradictory to this finding, previous studies have shown that nursing students face cases, discriminatory attitudes, communication barriers and challenges in integrating theory and practice due to limited exposure to clinical and limited resources in clinical settings.[14] Another concerning finding in this study is that most nursing students must be more confident doing clinical procedures. This finding is supported by Isenberg et al.[15] study, which found that students need more confidence in their procedural skills than general clinical skills.

The Clinical Environment Factor that Contributes to the Experience of Nursing Students

The study found that most of the participants strongly agreed that a shortage of nursing staff and common missing and inadequate equipment factors do negatively affect their clinical experience. The previous study findings show that nursing students in Malawi face severe challenges in their clinical learning experience due to nursing shortages, lack of equipment and supplies, and inadequate support from nursing lecturers.[16] According to a recent study by Jacob et al.,[17] most students had negative experiences, including inadequate clinical supervision, a lack of equipment, a high student population, and an inability to complete clinical objectives. Few students reported having favorable interactions with the staff nurses and exposure to an actual clinical setting. They encountered both positive and negative experiences during their clinical training. The majority of students had unpleasant experiences. Therefore, this could significantly impact the student’s ability to complete their education, the services they would provide to patients while employed, and the development of their nursing careers. Besides, a study conducted by Carol Drateru[18] found that challenges experienced by student nurses during skills acquisition are related to issues in the clinical environment and learning opportunities, such as lack of clinical staff and clinical tutors, little support supervision, lack of materials, student allocations, role models, and limited time for clinical practice, interpersonal relationships, and theory-practice gap. Another alarming discovery in this study is that most participants feel unsure of being accepted as a team member in a hospital setting. This finding is supported by Stefanidis et al.[19] study that demonstrated the staff prefer nursing students as something other than team-training partners due to their limited clinical experience and would instead train with experienced nurses. In addition, a previous study by Jack et al.[20] revealed that during their clinical rotations, student nurses believed they were treated unfairly by various healthcare team members. Unfairness was associated with being ignored and unsupported or being used as an “extra pair of hands” and disregarding their supernumerary status. Student nurses desire a sense of belonging in the clinical setting and value mentors’ enthusiasm for teaching.

The Contribution Factor of Nurse Instructors Toward Clinical Experience

The study revealed that most participants strongly agreed that a lack of nurse instructors could hinder their learning outcomes in clinical areas. These results are supported by previous study findings by Mabuda, Potgieter, and Alberts,[21] which indicate that aspects negatively impact student nurses’ clinical learning experiences, such as lack of teaching and learning support from clinical tutors. In this study, most participants agreed that the clinical instructor was approachable. In contrast to earlier research by Yang and Chao,[22] revealed clinical nursing instructors’ challenges in establishing a balance between the quality of their clinical teaching and their interactions with the clinical staff members, making them less approachable to nursing students. However, a concerning finding in this study is that most participants feel unsure of the clinical instructor’s support. Similar research by Rikhotso et al.[23] found that the clinical instructor failed to effectively support and advise nursing students, which resulted in high turnover and absenteeism. These results are consistent with their findings. In addition, having a successful clinical instructor might influence nursing students’ perceptions of clinical learning as an enjoyable endeavor, reports.[24]

This study shows a significant association between different semesters or years of study with clinical experience, clinical environmental factors, and the contribution factor of clinical instructors. A similar survey by Hewitt-Thompson et al.[25] found significant differences between the year of study and their perception of effective clinical instructor characteristics when considering an effective clinical instructor. According to Soroush et al.[26] study, good clinical instructors need to have the following qualities: excellent communication and teaching abilities, internal motivation, and a professional appearance. The study’s results also showed a link between the qualities and competence of instructors and student learning outcomes.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the study revealed that a lack of nursing staff and inadequate equipment had a negative impact on the student’s clinical experience, which is consistent with other studies that have identified similar challenges confronted by nursing students in various settings. The study concluded that a shortage of nurse instructors hindered clinical learning outcomes. This is consistent with previous research highlighting the significance of clinical instructors’ teaching and learning support for student nurses. In addition, the study summarized a significant relationship between the semester or year of study and clinical experience, clinical environmental factors, and clinical instructors’ contributions. This is consistent with similar research indicating that students in different years of study have significantly different perceptions of an effective clinical instructor. Therefore, it is essential to strengthen collaboration between academic institutions and clinical collaborators. Nursing education also should create opportunities for nursing students to work alongside other healthcare professionals during clinical experiences to foster interprofessional collaboration. Nursing education programs and healthcare organizations can improve nursing students’ clinical experiences by implementing these recommendations, resulting in more competent, compassionate, and well-prepared nurses. These changes will improve patient outcomes, progress the nursing profession, and improve healthcare delivery.

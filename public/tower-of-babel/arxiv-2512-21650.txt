Causal-HM: Restoring Physical Generative Logic in Multimodal Anomaly Detection via Hierarchical Modulation
Xiao Liu, Junchen Jin, Yanjie Zhao, Zhixuan Xing

Abstract
Multimodal Unsupervised Anomaly Detection (UAD) is critical for quality assurance in smart manufacturing, particularly in complex processes like robotic welding. However, existing methods often suffer from causal blindness, treating process modalities (e.g., real-time video, audio, and sensors) and result modalities (e.g., post-weld images) as equal feature sources, thereby ignoring the inherent physical generative logic. Furthermore, the heterogeneity gap between high-dimensional visual data and low-dimensional sensor signals frequently leads to critical process context being drowned out. In this paper, we propose Causal-HM, a unified multimodal UAD framework that explicitly models the physical P ​ r ​ o ​ c ​ e ​ s ​ s → R ​ e ​ s ​ u ​ l ​ t Process\to Result dependency. Specifically, our framework incorporates two key innovations: a Sensor-Guided CHM Modulation mechanism that utilizes low-dimensional sensor signals as context to guide high-dimensional audio-visual feature extraction , and a Causal-Hierarchical Architecture that enforces a unidirectional generative mapping to identify anomalies that violate physical consistency. Extensive experiments on our newly constructed Weld-4M benchmark demonstrate that Causal-HM achieves a state-of-the-art (SOTA) I-AUROC of 90.7%. Code will be released after the paper is accepted.

Notably, our model maintains high sensitivity to process-hidden defects where visual-only models fail. Furthermore, despite modeling complex four-modal interactions, Causal-HM is nearly 6 times faster than memory-bank-based methods, proving its operational feasibility for high-speed industrial inspection.

By replacing symmetric fusion with a Causal-Hierarchical architecture, we effectively resolve causal blindness and enable the detection of deep-seated process anomalies that are invisible to purely visual models.

2.

CHM Heterogeneous Modulation:
We propose a lightweight modulation mechanism that uses Mamba-based temporal encoders to transform low-dimensional sensor signals into contextual priors. This design bridges the heterogeneity gap, preventing critical process context from being drowned out by high-dimensional visual features while maintaining superior inference efficiency.

3.

Superior Performance on Hidden Defects:
Through extensive evaluation on the Weld-4M benchmark, we demonstrate that Causal-HM achieves a SOTA I-AUROC of 90.7%. Our analysis confirms a significant performance leap in identifying Process-Hidden defects and maintaining robustness against environmental noise compared to existing multimodal and unimodal SOTAs.

3.1
Preliminary

The objective of unsupervised multimodal anomaly detection in a manufacturing context is to construct a model that learns the manifold of normal production dynamics from a defect-free training set. We denote the training dataset as
\mathcal{D}=\{X^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^{N}
, where each sample
X
is a synchronized multimodal collection
X=\{X_{v},X_{a},X_{s},X_{i}\}
. Here,
X_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{v}\times 3\times H\times W}
represents the real-time process video capturing the spatiotemporal evolution of the work area,
X_{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times T_{a}}
denotes the acoustic emission spectrogram,
X_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{s}\times C_{s}}
refers to high-frequency 1D sensor time-series such as current and voltage, and
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 3\times H\times W}
represents the set of
M
post-weld images capturing the final result from multiple camera perspectives. Our fundamental hypothesis is that industrial production follows a unidirectional physical causal chain. We define the process-stage modalities as the Cause
P=\{X_{v},X_{a},X_{s}\}
and the final outcome as the Effect
R=\{X_{i}\}
. An anomaly is thus defined not merely as a statistical outlier in any single modality, but as a violation of the generative mapping
f:P\to R
, where the observed result
R
becomes inconsistent with the result predicted from the observed process
P
.

3.2
Overall Framework

The implementation of the Causal-HM framework is structured around a four-level hierarchy (L1–L4) designed to preserve the physical flow of information while bridging the heterogeneity gap between diverse industrial signals. The framework operates on fixed representations extracted by a suite of frozen state-of-the-art backbones, designated as L0. For spatiotemporal process video, we utilize V-JEPA2-vitl-fpc64-256
Assran et al. 2025
to capture long-range motion dynamics, while acoustic features are extracted using AST-finetuned-audioset-14-14-0.443
Gong et al. 2021
to provide high-resolution frequency domain representations. For the visual result modality, we employ DINOV3-vith16plus-pretrain-lvd1689m
Siméoni et al. 2025
to obtain discriminative semantic features from post-weld images. As shown in Figure
2
, the system first modulates high-dimensional process features using low-dimensional sensor signals (L1), encodes these into separate latent spaces (L2), performs an anti-generalization causal mapping (L3), and finally computes an anomaly score based on causal consistency (L4). 3.3
Sensor-Guided Causal Heterogeneous Modulation

A fundamental challenge in multimodal industrial anomaly detection is the heterogeneity gap, where low-dimensional, high-frequency sensor time-series are often drowned out when directly concatenated with high-dimensional visual representations. To address this, we propose the Causal Heterogeneous Modulation (CHM) mechanism, which treats the production process as a physical governor rather than a parallel feature source. Unlike traditional linear modulation techniques, CHM utilizes a Selective State Space Model based on the Mamba architecture to capture the long-range temporal dependencies and transient instabilities inherent in industrial sensor streams. Given the raw sensor sequence
X_{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{T_{s}\times C_{s}}
, we employ a Mamba-based temporal encoder to transform the signal into a content-aware latent state. This selective mechanism allows the model to dynamically propagate or suppress process-stage information based on its relevance to the final weld integrity, effectively modeling the unidirectional dependency from the production cause to the physical effect as illustrated in Figure
2
.

The technical implementation of CHM involves projecting the final hidden state of the Mamba encoder into two distinct affine transformation parameters,
\gamma
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, which represent the scaling and shifting factors for the high-dimensional process modalities. These parameters act as a global physical constraint that reshapes the manifold of the frozen spatiotemporal features
F_{v}
extracted by V-JEPA2 and the acoustic features
F_{a}
extracted by AST. The modulated features are formulated as
F_{mod}=F\odot(1+\gamma)+\beta
. By utilizing a residual-style scaling factor
(1+\gamma)
, we ensure that the modulation can accurately represent subtle physical deviations—such as a localized current drop—without disrupting the fundamental semantic information captured by the large-scale backbones. This design enforces a governing relationship where the sensor-driven latent state dictates the importance of specific visual and acoustic activations, ensuring that the model remains sensitive to process-stage anomalies that may not be immediately apparent in raw image data.

3.4
Causal-Hierarchical Encoding and Multi-Angle Aggregation

After the heterogeneous modulation phase, the framework transitions to the Causal-Hierarchical Encoding level, where modulated signals are integrated into a unified cause representation. For the process modalities, we design a Gated Process Encoder that utilizes a Cross-Attention mechanism to fuse the spatiotemporal and acoustic streams. In this configuration, the video tokens from V-JEPA2 function as the Query (
Q
), while the audio tokens from AST serve as the Key (
K
) and Value (
V
). This specific orientation is physically motivated: the model attends to acoustic transients (such as the high-frequency crackling of an unstable arc) to modulate the spatial attention of the visual stream. The resulting cross-modal tokens are integrated through a global pooling layer to produce the unified process latent
Z_{process}
, which encapsulates the entirety of the cause within a structured semantic space.

Simultaneously, the framework processes the post-weld images through a Result Encoder to form the target latent
Z_{result}
, representing the Effect. To handle the complex multi-view nature of industrial inspection, we utilize DINOV3 to extract discriminative features from multiple camera perspectives. These multi-angle representations are aggregated using a combination of Generalized Mean (GeM) pooling and a max-pooling operation across the angle dimension. This strategy allows the Result Encoder to remain invariant to camera positioning while being highly sensitive to localized defect signatures that may only appear in a single viewpoint. The hierarchical separation of
Z_{process}
and
Z_{result}
ensures that the subsequent mapping stage strictly follows the physical generative logic of the production line. By enforcing this causal bottleneck, the architecture can detect causal breaks during inference, where a seemingly nominal process fails to generate the expected result, or an abnormal process generates a surface that appears visually perfect but violates the learned physical consistency of the welding cycle.

3.5
Anti-Generalization Causal Decoder

The core of the Causal-HM framework is the mapping from the process latent
Z_{process}
to the predicted result latent
\hat{Z}_{result}
. In unsupervised anomaly detection, a significant risk is over-generalization, where a powerful decoder learns to reconstruct even anomalous result patterns by memorizing identity mappings. To counter this, we implement two structural safeguards. First, we introduce a Noisy Bottleneck between the encoder and decoder. During training, we apply a stochastic Bernoulli mask and additive Gaussian noise to
Z_{process}
to form
\tilde{Z}_{process}
. This forces the decoder to rely on the robust, high-level causal relationship between the production process and the final product rather than relying on brittle, high-frequency feature correlations. Second, we replace standard Softmax attention in the decoder with Linear Attention. By utilizing a kernel-based approximation
\operatorname{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\phi(Q)(\phi(K)^{\top}V)/(\phi(Q)\sum\phi(K)^{\top})
, where
\phi(x)=\operatorname{elu}(x)+1
, the attention mechanism is restricted to a lower-rank representation. This architectural constraint prevents the decoder from focusing too precisely on local patches, compelling it to reconstruct the result from a global, context-aware perspective of the welding process.

3.6
Objective Functions and Anomaly Scoring

The model is optimized using a composite loss function that aligns the predicted result with physical reality. The primary Reconstruction Loss
\mathcal{L}_{recon}
combines cosine distance with a Smooth L1 loss. To enhance sensitivity to localized defects, we implement Top-K mining within the L1 loss, where the optimization focus is placed on the
k
dimensions of the latent vector with the largest absolute errors. Furthermore, we introduce a semantic normality constraint
\mathcal{L}_{text}
using a frozen CLIP text encoder. This loss ensures that the predicted latent
\hat{Z}_{result}
remains close to the semantic embedding of the prompt ”a normal weld,” providing a linguistic anchor for the manifold of normality. During inference, the anomaly score
S
is derived from the causal consistency between the observed result and the prediction:

\begin{split}S&=1-\cos\!\left(Z_{result},\hat{Z}_{result}\right)\\
&\quad+\eta\cdot\operatorname{mean}\!\left(\operatorname{TopK}\left(\left|Z_{result}-\hat{Z}_{result}\right|\right)\right)\end{split}

(1)

where
\eta
is a weighting hyperparameter.
This scoring mechanism identifies causal breaks, such as a scenario where an abnormal process generates a seemingly perfect surface, or a nominal process produces an unexpected defect. By evaluating the integrity of the
P\to R
chain, Causal-HM provides a robust and physically-grounded metric for industrial quality assurance.

4.1
Experimental Setup and Dataset

Existing benchmarks, such as MVTec AD or VisA, focus predominantly on static post-production images or RGB-D pairs, which fail to account for the rich temporal, acoustic, and electrical signals generated during active manufacturing. We address this gap by evaluating our framework on the Welding Multi-Modality
Stemmer et al. 2024
(Weld-4M) benchmark. This dataset is uniquely suited for our task because it integrates process modalities, comprising 30 FPS video, 192 kHz acoustic emissions, and high-frequency sensor time-series, that are highly accessible and easily collected via standard monitoring equipment in real-factory environments. Furthermore, Weld-4M encompasses a comprehensive set of 4,040 samples covering 12 categories, including explicit surface defects like spatter and undercut, as well as critical implicit anomalies such as ”Lack of Fusion” and internal ”Porosity”. These hidden defects are particularly significant as they often present a visually nominal surface despite substantial internal structural failures, necessitating a model that can evaluate the consistency between the observed process and the final result. Following the standard protocol, we utilize a 576-sample ”Good-only” training set, with performance quantified using image-level AUROC, Average Precision (I-AP), and the optimal F1-score (I-F1-max) to ensure a holistic evaluation in class-imbalanced industrial contexts.

4.3
Implementation and Hyperparameters

The Causal-HM framework and all baselines are trained and evaluated on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090D GPU with unified settings. We utilize a suite of frozen backbones to extract high-quality spatiotemporal and semantic representations: V-JEPA2 for process video, AST for acoustic signals, and DINOV3 for result images. All internal projections and latent spaces operate with a unified hidden dimension of
d=512
. 5
Conclusions

Causal-HM is a novel multimodal UAD framework that incorporates physical causal priors into industrial anomaly detection. By shifting from traditional flat fusion to a Causal-Hierarchical architecture, we successfully modeled the generative relationship between welding process modalities and the final product result. Our core innovations—Sensor-guided CHM modulation and Anti-generalization causal mapping—effectively bridge the heterogeneity gap between diverse industrial signals and suppress the reconstruction of anomalous patterns.
Causal-HM sets a new performance benchmark on the Weld-4M dataset, particularly excelling in identifying deep-seated process anomalies while maintaining high inference efficiency. Future work will focus on validating this causal framework in real-world production environments where defects occur spontaneously and exploring joint end-to-end modeling of sensor-visual temporal dependencies.

Introduction

First introduced in earnest in the papers
[
15
]
and
[

, the model theory of finite von Neumann algebras has been a very active area of research; see
[

with a faithful, normal trace

\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space) is then treated as a structure in a many-sorted language, where the sorts are operator norm bounded balls of positive integer radii, equipped with the metric coming from the trace. Each sort is then equipped with the restriction of the
*

It is natural to try to extend the model-theoretic treatment of von Neumann algebras beyond the finite case. The next obvious class of von Neumann algebras to consider is the
\sigma
-finite ones. Since such algebras can be characterized as those which admit a faithful, normal state, one can try to mimic the case of finite von Neumann algebras by treating
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces

In this paper, we offer an alternative axiomatization of the class of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces motivated by the desire to have ordinary multiplication as part of the language. As a consequence, a compromise must be made in terms of the sorts. Since the issue in adapting the language for tracial
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces to the non-tracial case stemmed from the fact that multiplication is not uniformly continuous with respect to the metric coming from the state, it becomes natural to change the sorts in such a way that multiplication does become uniformly continuous. 2.1.
Standard forms

As mentioned in the introduction, the class of structures we wish to capture model-theoretically are the
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebras, by which we mean those von Neumann algebras
M
for which any set
(p_{i})_{i\in I}
of pairwise orthogonal projections from
M
contains only countably many nonzero elements. It is well-known that these algebras can be alternatively characterized as those which admit a faithful normal state; we will use this characterization to view a
\sigma
-finite von Neumann algebra as a metric structure.

However, it will behoove us to consider yet another characterization of this class. Fix a faithful, normal representation
\pi\colon M\to\mathcal{B}(H)
of
M
. We recall that
\omega\in H
is called
cyclic
for
\pi
if
\pi(M)\omega
is dense in
H
and
separating
for
\pi
if
\omega
is cyclic for
\pi(M)^{\prime}
, that is, if
\pi(a)\omega\not=0
for all
a\in M\setminus\{0\}
.
\pi
is then called a
standard representation
if it admits a cyclic and separating unit vector
\omega
, in which case one says that
(M,\pi,\omega)
is a
standard form
of
M
. We remark that this is an abbreviated notation; the standard form in the sense of
[
19
]
consists of a quadruple
(M,H,J,P)
, where
H
is a Hilbert space on which
M
acts,
J
is an isometric antilinear involution, and
P
is a self-dual cone in
H
satisfying axioms of the standard form. Since we do not need the precise axioms or the uniqueness of the standard form, we do not mention
J
or
P
. They can be constructed from
(M,\pi,\omega)
. It is convenient to abuse notation and speak simply of
(M,\omega)
as a standard form of
M
, conflating the distinction between
M
and
\pi(M)
. One then has that
M
is
\sigma
-finite if and only if it admits a standard representation; moreover, any two such standard representations of
M
are unitarily conjugate.

If
(M,\omega)
is a standard form of
M
, then the state
\varphi_{\omega}(a):=\langle a\omega,\omega\rangle
is a faithful, normal state on
M
. Conversely, given a faithful, normal state
\varphi
on
M
, one obtains a standard form of
M
using the GNS construction, which we briefly recall. First, one introduces the inner product
\left\langle\cdot\,,\!\ \cdot\right\rangle_{\varphi}
on
M
given by

\left\langle a\,,\!\ b\right\rangle_{\varphi}=\varphi(b^{*}a)

with corresponding norm

\|a\|_{\varphi}=\sqrt{\left\langle a\,,\!\ a\right\rangle_{\varphi}}.

The Hilbert space completion of
(M,\left\langle\cdot\,,\!\ \cdot\right\rangle_{\varphi})
is denoted by
H_{\varphi}
and elements of
M
, when viewed as elements of
H_{\varphi}
, are often decorated with a hat, so
a\in M
would be denoted
\hat{a}
. Given
a\in M
, one gets a bounded operator
\pi_{\varphi}(a)\in\mathcal{B}(H_{\varphi})
defined on the dense subset
\widehat{M}
of
H_{\varphi}
by setting
\pi_{\varphi}(a)(\hat{b}):=\widehat{ab}
. It is then easy to see that
\pi_{\varphi}:M\to B(H_{\varphi})
is a standard representation of
M
with cyclic and separating vector
\omega_{\varphi}:=\hat{1}
.

We note also that the topology induced by
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}
agrees with the strong operator topology on operator-norm-bounded subsets of
M
; closed operator-norm balls are complete for the corresponding metric.

2.2.
Totally bounded elements

Until further notice, fix a standard form
(M,\omega)
of
M
. In general, for
a\in M
, the right multiplication map
b\omega\mapsto ba\omega:M\omega\to M\omega
does not extend to a bounded operator on
H
. Thus, we say that
a\in M
is
right
K
-bounded
if, for all
b\in M
, we have
\|ba\omega\|\leq K\|b\omega\|
. Note that some sources in the literature would call such an element right
\sqrt{K}
-bounded. Note also that the notion of bounded element depends on the choice of standard form of
M
. If
a
is right
K
-bounded, then the above map extends to a bounded operator on
H
, which we denote by
\pi^{\prime}(a)
; in this case, we set
\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}:=\|\pi^{\prime}(a)\|
and note that
\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq K
. In a slight abuse of notation, for
\xi\in H
we will sometimes write
\|\pi^{\prime}(\xi)\|\leq K
if
M\omega\ni a\omega\mapsto a\xi
extends to a
K
-bounded operator on
H
. In this way we have
\pi^{\prime}(a)=\pi^{\prime}(a\omega)
. We say that
a\in M
is
right bounded
if it is right
K
-bounded for some
K
; we let
M_{\operatorname{rb}}
denote the set of right bounded elements of
M
.

Note that if
a\in M_{\operatorname{rb}}
, then
\pi^{\prime}(a)\in M^{\prime}
. More generally, we have:

Lemma 2.1
.

For
v\in H
, we have that
v\in M^{\prime}\omega
if and only if the map

b\omega\mapsto bv:M\omega\to H

extends to a bounded operator on
H
. In particular,
a\in M
is right-bounded if and only if
a\omega\in M^{\prime}\omega
, in which case
\pi^{\prime}(a)
is the unique
z\in M^{\prime}
such that
a\omega=z\omega
.

Proof.

First suppose that
v\in M^{\prime}\omega
. Then
v=x\omega
for some
x\in M^{\prime}
. For
b\in M
, it follows that
bv=bx\omega=xb\omega
, whence the map
b\omega\mapsto bv
is the restriction of the bounded operator
x
to
M\omega
.

Conversely, suppose that the map
b\omega\mapsto bv:M\omega\to H
extends to the bounded operator
x
on
H
. Then
v=1\cdot v=x(1\omega)=x\omega
. It suffices to show that
x\in M^{\prime}
. To see this, take
a,b\in M
and note
(xa)(b\omega)=x(ab\omega)=abv=ax(b\omega)
; since
M\omega
is dense in
H
, it follows that
ax=xa
. Since
a\in M
is arbitrary, it follows that
x\in M^{\prime}
.

The final statement follows immediately from the first statement and its proof.
∎

Continuing with the assumption that
(M,\omega)
is a standard form of

\|x\|\leq K
and both

\|x\|_{\mathrm{tb}}=\max\{\|x\|,\|R_{x}\|,\|R_{x^{*}}\|\},

where we use the notation
R_{x}:=\pi^{\prime}(x)

We will denote the set of totally bounded elements by
M_{\operatorname{tb}}
.

Notation 2.2
.

For any
X\subseteq M
, we let
S_{K}(X)
denote the set of totally

-bounded elements of

.

Remark 2.3
.

The notation above implicitly identifies
M
with a choice of standard form. More precisely, one should write
S_{K}(X,\omega)

Now suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space, by which we mean a von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal state

We will also use the following characterization of totally bounded elements that is essentially due to Connes

. Recall
that the domain

F\colon\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(a)\in M
extends

The domain

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a

-probability space. For

(1)

(2)

a\in D(\sigma^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{i}/2})\cap D(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-\mathrm{i}/2})

Moreover, whenever these conditions hold,

\|a\|_{\mathrm{right}}=\|\sigma^{\varphi}_{-\mathrm{i}/2}(a^{*})\|=\|\sigma^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{i}/2}(a)\|,\quad\|a^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}=\|\sigma^{\varphi}_{-\mathrm{i}/2}(a)\|.

Consequently,

S_{K}(M,\varphi)=\Bigl\{a\in D(\sigma^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{i}/2})\cap D(\sigma^{\varphi}_{-\mathrm{i}/2})\ :\ \|a\|,\;\|\sigma^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{i}/2}(a)\|,\;\|\sigma^{\varphi}_{-\mathrm{i}/2}(a)\|\leq K\Bigr\}.

A consequence of the preceding fact is the following:

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space. Then

is strongly dense in

.

Proof.

If
x
is entire

-analytic, then it is totally bounded by Lemma

∎

If
(M,\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space, we also consider the norm

on

\|a\|_{\varphi}^{\#}:=\sqrt{\frac{\varphi(a^{*}a+aa^{*})}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\|a\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|a^{*}\|_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}}.

Note that when

Lemma 2.6
.

For all

|\varphi(a)|\leq\sqrt{2}\|a\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
.

Proof.

The statement follows immediately from Cauchy-Schwarz.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space. Then for any
K
, the strong and strong* topologies agree on totally
K
-bounded sets.

Proof.

Let
(a_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
S_{K}(M,\varphi)
and
a\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
.
Then
d_{i}:=a_{i}-a\in S_{2K}(M,\varphi)
and

\|d_{i}^{*}\|_{\varphi}^{2}=\varphi(d_{i}d_{i}^{*})=\langle R_{d_{i}^{*}}(d_{i}\omega_{\varphi}),\omega_{\varphi}\rangle\leq 2K\|d_{i}\|_{\varphi}.

Therefore,

\|a-a_{i}\|_{\varphi}^{\#\,2}\leq\frac{\|a-a_{i}\|_{\varphi}^{2}+2K\|a-a_{i}\|_{\varphi}}{2}.

The claim then follows from the above inequality.
∎

As stated in the introduction, when treating a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space as a many-sorted structure, our intended sorts are the

First, we need to introduce some important operators associated to any standard form. Suppose that
(M,\omega)
is a standard form of

S_{0}:M\omega\to H
and
F_{0}:M^{\prime}\omega\to H
by
S_{0}(a\omega):=a^{*}\omega
and

for

and

. An important fact about these unbounded operators are that they are closable
[

; we set
S:=\overline{S_{0}}
and
F:=\overline{F_{0}}
. One also has that
S^{*}=F
,
S^{2}\xi=\xi\ \text{ for }\xi\in D(S)
and
F^{2}\eta=\eta\text{ for }\eta\in D(F)

∗
-probability space, we will denote by

and

the associated operators on the GNS construction.

Lemma 2.8
.

For each

is

-complete.

Proof.

Suppose that
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a

-Cauchy sequence from

-complete, there is

such that

strongly converges to

is also right

and note that

converges strongly to

converges to

\|a_{n}\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq K
, we have that
\|ba\|_{\varphi}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|ba_{n}\|_{\varphi}\leq K\|b\|_{\varphi}

Arguing as in the previous paragraph, there is

strongly converges to

; moreover,

It remains to show that

and

2.3.
The category of W
∗
-probability spaces

We now describe embeddings between probability spaces. An
embedding
between
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces
(M,\varphi)
and
(N,\psi)
is a unital, normal, state-preserving
∗
-homomorphism
f:M\to N
for which there is a normal, state-preserving conditional expectation map
E:N\to f(M)
.

The reason for this choice of embeddings comes from modular theory (see the next lemma). Notice that in the tracial setting, every trace-preserving
∗
-homomorphism automatically induces a conditional expectation onto its image. This corresponds to the fact that, in this setting, the modular automorphism group is trivial.

In order to show that we axiomatized the category of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces, we will need the following alternate characterization of embeddings between
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces. Note that this characterization refers to the modular automorphism group of a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space, defined in detail in Section
3
. Throughout this paper, we let
M_{\operatorname{sa}}
denote the set of self-adjoint elements of
M
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose that
(M,\varphi)
and
(N,\psi)
are
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces and
f:M\to N
is a state-preserving
∗
-homomorphism. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is an embedding of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces.

(2)

f
preserves distances to the sets of self-adjoint elements, that is, for all
a\in M
, we have
d_{\varphi}(a,M_{\operatorname{sa}})=d_{\psi}(f(a),N_{\operatorname{sa}})
.

(3)

f
commutes with the modular automorphism group, that is, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in M
, we have
f(\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(a))=\sigma_{t}^{\psi}(f(a))
.

Proof.

For the implication (1) implies (2), note that if
E:N\to M
is the state-preserving conditional expectation, then

E
is the orthogonal projection
L^{2}(N,\psi)\to L^{2}(M,\varphi)
and hence the distance
to
M_{\operatorname{sa}}\omega
computed in
M
equals the distance computed in
N
.

The direction (2) implies (3) follows from Rieffel and Van Daele’s bounded operator approach to Tomita-Takesaki theory described in Section
3
. Indeed, using the notation developed there, if distances to the real self-adjoint subspaces agree, then the real
orthogonal projection
P_{N}
restricts to
P_{M}
. The relation

iP(\xi)=Q(i\xi)
gives the same conclusion for
Q
, and hence for
R=P+Q
. Item (3) follows immediately from this fact.

The direction (3)
\implies
(1) is a theorem of Takesaki
[
35
]
(proof can also be found in
[
34
, Chapter IX, Theorem 4.2]
).
∎

In connection with the previous lemma, we note that if
a\in S_{K}(M)
, then
d_{\varphi}(a,M_{\operatorname{sa}})=d_{\varphi}(a,b)
for some
b\in S_{3K}(M)
; this fact will be proven in Section

(see Corollary

).

2.4.
Ocneanu and Groh–Raynaud ultraproducts

Throughout this section, we fix an index set
I
,

(\bm{M},\bm{\varphi})=(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
a family of
W^{*}
-probability spaces, and a
non-principal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
I
.
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
is the

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of all uniformly bounded
I
-indexed families

x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
equipped with the norm

\|x\|=\sup_{i\in I}\|x_{i}\|,\quad x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M}).

Let

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{U}}(\bm{M},\bm{\varphi})=\Bigl\{x=(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})\Bigm|\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\|x_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}=0\Bigr\},\quad\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{U}}\cap\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mathcal{U}},

and

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}=\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}(\bm{M},\bm{\varphi})=\{x\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})\mid x\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}\subset\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}},\;\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}x\subset\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}\}.

\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra (with pointwise operations and supremum norm)
in which
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}
is a closed two-sided ideal. We then define the

Ocneanu ultraproduct

[
25
,
3
]

\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})=\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}

(the quotient
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra). The quotient map is denoted by

q\colon\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}\to\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i}),\quad q((x_{i})_{i})=:(x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}.

The formula

\varphi((x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}})=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\varphi_{i}(x_{i})

defines a faithful normal state on
M=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Thus,

(M,\varphi)
is a
W^{*}
-probability space.

We will frequently use the following fact (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
): for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, if

(x_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}
, then
(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{t}(x_{i}))_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}

and

(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{t}(x_{i}))_{\mathcal{U}}=\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}((x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}).

We will also briefly use the Groh–Raynaud ultraproduct.
Let
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
be von Neumann algebras represented in standard
form on Hilbert spaces
H_{i}
, and let
H_{\mathcal{U}}:=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}H_{i}
be the Hilbert space ultraproduct. Every bounded family

defines an operator on

H_{\mathcal{U}}
by

(x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}(\xi_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}:=(x_{i}\xi_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}

The von Neumann algebra generated by these operators is called the

and is denoted by

\prod_{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathrm{GR}}M_{i}.

If
\varphi_{i}
is a faithful normal state on
M_{i}
for every

and

,
then the family
(\varphi_{i})_{i}
defines a normal state

on

Thus the Ocneanu ultraproduct is a
\sigma
-finite corner of
the Groh–Raynaud ultraproduct.
For a von Neumann algebra

S_{\rm n}(M)
denote its normal
state space and define the state-space diameter

The diameter for factors can be computed as follows (due to Connes-Størmer

Fact 2.10

Let

is

(1)

is of type
I
{}_{n},\ (n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\})
.

(2)

2

II

Let

2.5.
Arveson spectral theory and kernel smoothing

Here, we briefly recall Arveson’s spectral theory for the modular flow.
See
[
34
, Chapter XI]
for details.
We identify the dual group
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
of the additive group
\mathbb{R}
with itself. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we define the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
by

(2.1)

\hat{f}(\lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda}f(t)dt,\qquad\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we also consider the function
\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}:M\to M
given by

\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)dt,\qquad x\in M,

where the integral is taken in the
\sigma
-weak sense. We note that
\|\sigma^{\varphi}_{f}(x)\|\leq\|f\|_{1}\|x\|
.

Below, we always let
M
act on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(M,\varphi)
associated with
\varphi
with
\omega_{\varphi}
the GNS vector, and
\Delta_{\varphi}
is defined as the positive self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(M,\varphi)
as usual.
For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
x\in M
, we have

(2.2)

\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\omega_{\varphi}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)e^{it\log\Delta_{\varphi}}x\omega_{\varphi}dt=\hat{f}(\log\Delta_{\varphi})x\omega_{\varphi}.

For
x\in M
,
\text{Sp}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(x)
is defined by

\left\{\lambda\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\,\middle|\,\ \hat{f}(\lambda)=0\text{ for all }f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\text{ with }\sigma^{\varphi}_{f}(x)=0\right\}.

For a closed subset
E
of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
, the
spectral subspace
of
\sigma^{\varphi}
corresponding to
E
is given by

M(\sigma^{\varphi},E):=\{x\in M\ :\ \text{Sp}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(x)\subseteq E\}.

From (
2.2
) and spectral theory, it follows that

x\in M(\sigma^{\varphi},E)\iff 1_{E}(\log\Delta_{\varphi})x\omega_{\varphi}=x\omega_{\varphi}.

Moreover, the inclusion

(2.3)

\operatorname{Sp}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x))\subseteq\operatorname{Sp}_{\sigma^{\varphi}}(x)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{f})

holds.
The next lemma shows smoothing preserves total boundedness.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a W
∗
-probability space, let
K>0
, and let

x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
. Then for every
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\in S_{K\|f\|_{1}}(M,\varphi).

In particular, if
\|f\|_{1}\leq 1
, then

\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,

x\in D(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi})\cap D(\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi})
and

\max\left(\|x\|,\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\|,\|\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\|\right)\leq K.

We first show that
\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}
commutes
with the analytic generators:

(2.4)

\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\bigr)=\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\bigr).

We verify the identity for
\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}
; the other case is
analogous. Since
x\in D(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi})
, the map
t\mapsto\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)
extends to a bounded
\sigma
-weakly continuous map

F_{x}\colon\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid 0\leq\operatorname{Im}z\leq\frac{1}{2}\}\ni z\mapsto\sigma_{z}^{\varphi}(x)\in M,

which is holomorphic in the interior.
For
0\leq\operatorname{Im}z\leq\frac{1}{2}
, set
G(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\sigma_{t+z}^{\varphi}(x)dt.

Since
F_{x}
is bounded on the closed strip and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
the integral is well-defined in the
\sigma
-weak sense and
G
is bounded. Moreover,
G
is
\sigma
-weakly
continuous on the closed strip and holomorphic in its interior.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle G(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\sigma_{t+s}^{\varphi}(x)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sigma_{s}^{\varphi}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{s}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)).

This shows that
G
is the analytic extension of the modular orbit of

\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)
. Consequently,
\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\in D(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi})
holds
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x))

\displaystyle=G(\mathrm{i}/2)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\bigr)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\bigr).

The same argument on the lower strip proves

\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x))=\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\bigr).

It follows that

\|\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\|\leq\|f\|_{1}\|x\|\leq K\|f\|_{1},

while (
2.4
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x))\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\bigr)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{1}\left\|\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq K\|f\|_{1}.

By Lemma
2.4
again, we obtain
\sigma_{f}^{\varphi}(x)\in S_{K\|f\|_{1}}(M,\varphi).

∎

For
\tau>0
, define the
Gaussian kernel

k_{\tau}(t)=(4\pi\tau)^{-1/2}e^{-t^{2}/(4\tau)}.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\,dt=1,\qquad\widehat{k_{\tau}}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)e^{\mathrm{i}ts}dt=e^{-\tau s^{2}}.

The following Gaussian smoothing lemma is well-known; see, for example,
[
8
,
34
]
; it is also used in
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a W
∗
-probability space,
a\in M
, and
\tau>0
. Then the element

b:=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(a)
is
\sigma^{\varphi}
-analytic. Moreover, for all

, we have

\sigma_{z}^{\varphi}(b)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t-z)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(a)dt.

In particular, for
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.5)

\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}y}^{\varphi}(b)\|\leq e^{y^{2}/4\tau}\|a\|,\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
,
b\in S_{e^{1/(16\tau)}\left\lVert a\right\rVert}(M,\varphi)
holds.

In particular, if
e\in M
satisfies
0\leq e\leq 1
, then

e_{\tau}:=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(e)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(e)\,dt,

satisfies
0\leq e_{\tau}\leq 1
,
\varphi(e_{\tau})=\varphi(e)
, and
e_{\tau}\in S_{e^{1/(16\tau)}}(M,\varphi)
.

Combining Lemma
2.13
with
[

, Lemma 4.14]

Corollary 2.14
.

Suppose that
M=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
is an Ocneanu ultraproduct of W
∗
-probability spaces.
Let
a=(a_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in M
and
\tau>0
.
For each
i\in I
, set
b_{i}:=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(a_{i})
.
Then
(b_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in M
, is totally
e^{1/(16\tau)}\|a\|
-bounded and satisfies

(b_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(a).

We will also need another kind of smoothing using the
Fejér kernel

F_{r}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

F_{r}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1-\cos(rt)}{\pi rt^{2}}&\text{if }t\not=0\\

3.
Tomita-Takesaki theory according to Rieffel and Van Daele

In this section, we present the basic facts from the bounded operator approach to Tomita-Takesaki due to Rieffel and Van Daele
[
28
]
used throughout this paper.

Fix a standard form
(M,\omega)
of
M
. We set
\mathcal{K}:=\overline{M_{\operatorname{sa}}\omega}
, a closed,
real
subspace of
H
. Viewing
H
as a real Hilbert space by taking the real part of the original inner product, we let
P:H\to H
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{K}
. We furthermore set
Q:H\to H
to be the orthogonal projection onto the closed real subspace
i\mathcal{K}
of
H
and set
R:=P+Q
, a positive operator. Moreover, one considers the polar decomposition of
\Theta:=P-Q=JT
of
P-Q
, where
J
is a partial isometry and
T
is a positive operator. We list here the most important facts about these operators and spaces, whose proofs can be found in
[
27
,
28
]
:

Facts 3.1
.

(1)

R
and
T
are complex-linear operators.

(2)

J
is an anti-linear, self-adjoint, orthogonal operator (whence
J^{2}=1
) satisfying
J\omega=\omega
.

(3)

R
and
2-R
are injective.

(4)

For all
v\in H
,
iP(v)=Q(iv)
.

(5)

\overline{M^{\prime}_{\operatorname{sa}}\omega}=i\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
.

(6)

If
x\in M
, then
\Theta x\omega=(2-R)x^{*}\omega
.

(7)

If
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, then
\Theta x^{\prime}\omega=Rx^{{}^{\prime}*}\omega

Since the spectra of
R
and
2-R
are both contained in
[0,2]
and
0
is not the eigenvalue of
R
or
2-R
, one may use Borel functional calculus to define the operators
R^{it}
and
(2-R)^{it}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, yielding two commuting strongly continuous one-parameter groups of unitaries of
H
.
One then defines a new strongly continuous one-parameter group of unitaries
\Delta^{it}:=(2-R)^{it}R^{-it}
.

The notation
\Delta^{it}
may seem strange since one has not defined the operator
\Delta
itself; in the usual (unbounded) approach to Tomita-Takesaki theory, one indeed defines the unbounded operator
\Delta
first and then uses (unbounded) functional calculus to define the unitary operators
\Delta^{it}
. One can describe
\Delta
using the approach discussed above, namely
\Delta:=R^{-1}(2-R)
; the presence of the unbounded operator
R^{-1}
renders
\Delta
unbounded.

It will behoove us to recall the “standard” approach to defining
\Delta
. Indeed, recalling the closed, unbounded operators
S
and
F
introduced in the previous section, the usual definition of
\Delta
is as the square of the positive operator in the polar decomposition of
S
, that is
S=J\Delta^{1/2}
, where
J
is as defined above. Note then that
\Delta=S^{*}S=FS
.

We next observe how this picture looks when we switch focus from
M
to
M^{\prime}
. Let us temporarily decorate the corresponding operators with a
′
, such as
S^{\prime}
,
F^{\prime}
, etc… It is clear that
S^{\prime}=F
,
F^{\prime}=S
,
\Delta^{\prime}=F^{\prime}S^{\prime}=SF=\Delta^{-1}
. By item (5) of Facts
3.1
, we see that
P^{\prime}=1-Q
. Analogously, one has that
Q^{\prime}=1-P
. Consequently,
\Theta^{\prime}=P^{\prime}-Q^{\prime}=(1-Q)-(1-P)=P-Q=\Theta
. Note also that
R^{\prime}=P^{\prime}+Q^{\prime}=(1-Q)+(1-P)=2-R
.

The following is
[
28
, Corollary 4.4]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, there exists a unique
x\in M
such that

\Theta x^{\prime}\omega=x\omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\Theta(x^{\prime})^{*}\omega=x^{*}\omega.

By interchanging
M
and
M^{\prime}
, we also have that, for every
y\in M
, there exists a unique
y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
so that

\Theta y\omega=y^{\prime}\omega\quad\text{ and }\quad\Theta y^{*}\omega=(y^{\prime})^{*}\omega.

For our purposes, we will need a sharpening of the previous lemma. In what follows, for
v\in M\omega
, we abuse notation by setting
\|\pi(v)\|:=\|a\|
, where

is the unique element of
M

.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
x\in M^{\prime}
is such that
\|x\|\leq K
, then
P(x\omega),Q(x\omega)\in M\omega
and
\|\pi(Px\omega)\|,\|\pi(Qx\omega)\|\leq 2K
.

Proof.

First suppose that
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
is such that

. In the proof of
[

, Lemma 4.3]
, the authors find
x\in M_{\operatorname{sa}}
with

P(x^{\prime}\omega)=x\omega
. Consequently, for general positive
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}

x\in M_{\operatorname{sa}}

\|x\|\leq\|x^{\prime}\|

.

If

x^{\prime}=x_{1}^{\prime}-x_{2}^{\prime}
with
x_{1}^{\prime},x_{2}^{\prime}\in M_{\operatorname{sa}}^{\prime}

P(x^{\prime}\omega)=P(x_{1}^{\prime}\omega)-P(x_{2}^{\prime}\omega)=x_{1}\omega-x_{2}\omega=(x_{1}-x_{2})\omega,

where

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\leq\|x_{1}\|+\|x_{2}\|\leq\|x_{1}^{\prime}\|+\|x_{2}^{\prime}\|\leq 2\|x^{\prime}\|
. The claim follows.

If
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, then by the cartesian decomposition in

, we can find
y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}_{\operatorname{sa}}

and the claim follows from the previous paragraph.

Noting that
P=-iQi
, the same proof gives the statement for

.
∎

First, recalling that
\omega

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}:=\overline{M^{\prime}_{\operatorname{sa}}\omega}

\|c\|\leq 2\|a\|
. On the other hand,
P^{\prime}(ia\omega)=(1-Q)(ia\omega)=ia\omega-iP(a\omega)
, so
P(a\omega)=a\omega+ic\omega
. Recalling that
P(a\omega)=b\omega
, we have, for any
d\in M
, that

\|db\|_{\varphi}=\|da\omega+d(ic\omega)\|\leq\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}\|d\|_{\varphi}+\|c\|\|d\|_{\varphi}\leq(\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}+2\|a\|)\|d\|_{\varphi}.

It follows that
b\in M_{\operatorname{tb}}
and
\|b\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}+2\|a\|
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

If
a\in S_{K}(M)
, then
P(a\omega)=b\omega
for a unique
b\in S_{3K}(M)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
, there is a unique
b\in M
such that

P(a\omega)=b\omega

and

\|b\|\leq 2\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq 2K
. Moreover, the proof of Lemma
3.3
shows that
b
is
self-adjoint. By Lemma
3.5
,

\|b\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq\|a\|_{\operatorname{right}}+2\|a\|\leq 3K.

Since
b=b^{*}
, we also have

\|b^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}=\|b\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq 3K.

Moreover,
\|b\|\leq 2K\leq 3K
.
Therefore,
b\in S_{3K}(M)
holds.
∎

The main theorem of Tomita-Takesaki theory is the following:

Theorem 3.7

(Theorem VI.1.19 in
[

]
)
.

JMJ=M^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{it}M\Delta^{-it}=M
for all

.

By the previous fact, one can define a strongly continuous one-parameter family
\sigma_{t}
of automorphisms of

by defining

. This family is called the

of the standard form

.

We now specialize to the case that

is a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space with standard form stemming from the GNS representation. As mentioned in Section

, sometimes we identify

with its image

here really refers to

. We occasionally might decorate the various operators just introduced with

for the aforementioned one-parameter family of automorphisms of

In Section
7
, we explain how the Rieffel-Van Daele approach to Tomita-Takesaki theory can be used to expand the language given in Section
6
by symbols for the modular automorphism group of
\varphi
and to extend the theory presented in Section

to an effective theory in the expanded language axiomatizing the modular automorphism group. In order to do so, we will need the following two facts, the first of which is a special case of
[

, Theorem 5.15]
:

Fact 3.8
.

For each
a\in M
, there is a unique
b\in M
such that
\Delta^{it}(a\omega)=b\omega
. Moreover, we have
\pi(b)=\Delta^{it}\pi(a)\Delta^{-it}
. Consequently,
\|a\|=\|b\|
.

Note that by taking adjoints in the previous fact, we see also that
\Delta^{it}(a^{*}\omega)=b^{*}\omega
. The second fact is a special case of
[
28
, Theorem 5.14]
:

Fact 3.9
.

For all
a,b\in M
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(\Delta^{it}J\pi(a)J\Delta^{-it})(b\omega)=\pi(b)(\Delta^{it}(Ja\omega)).

We now return to the general case of a standard form
(M,\varphi)
. We need one further fact about the modular operator, to be used in Section
4
. See
[
34
, VI, Lemma 1.17]
.

Fact 3.10

(Bridging Lemma/Takesaki’s Resolvent Lemma)
.

For every
x\in M
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
, there exists a unique
y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
such that
(\Delta^{-1}-\lambda I)^{-1}x\omega=y^{\prime}\omega
. Furthermore, one has

\|y^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{\|x\|}{\sqrt{2|\lambda|-2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)}}.

Similarly, for every
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
, there exists a unique
y\in M
such that
(\Delta-\lambda)^{-1}x^{\prime}\omega=y\omega
. Furthermore, one has

\|y\|\leq\frac{\|x^{\prime}\|}{\sqrt{2|\lambda|-2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)}}.

We will need a “totally bounded” version of the previous fact:

Corollary 3.11
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{+}
, set
c_{\lambda}=\sqrt{2|\lambda|-2\operatorname{Re}\lambda}
. If
x\in M
(resp.
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
) is totally bounded, then
y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
(resp.
y\in M
) as in Fact
3.10
is totally bounded with

\|y^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{\|x\|}{c_{\lambda}},

\|y^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{right}}\leq\frac{\|x^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}}{c_{\lambda}},\qquad\|(y^{\prime})^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}\leq\frac{\|x\|_{\mathrm{right}}}{c_{\lambda}}

(and similarly for the bounds on
\|y\|_{\operatorname{right}},\|y^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}
).

Proof.

Put
r:=\pi^{\prime}(x)\in M^{\prime},s:=\pi^{\prime}(x^{*})\in M^{\prime}.
Thus
r\omega=x\omega,s\omega=x^{*}\omega,
and

\|r\|=\|x\|_{\mathrm{right}},\qquad\|s\|=\|x^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}.

Since
S(r\omega)=x^{*}\omega=s\omega

and
F(s\omega)=s^{*}\omega
, we have

r\omega\in D(\Delta)
and

\Delta r\omega=s^{*}\omega.

From the identity

(t^{-1}-\lambda)^{-1}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}(t-\lambda^{-1})^{-1}t\qquad(t>0),

we obtain

\displaystyle y^{\prime}\omega

\displaystyle=(\Delta^{-1}-\lambda I)^{-1}r\omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda^{-1}I)^{-1}\Delta r\omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\lambda}(\Delta-\lambda^{-1}I)^{-1}s^{*}\omega.

Since
\lambda^{-1}\notin\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
Fact
3.10
, applied to
s^{*}\in M^{\prime}
with parameter
\lambda^{-1}

gives an element
a\in M
such that

(\Delta-\lambda^{-1}I)^{-1}s^{*}\omega=a\omega,\qquad\|a\|\leq\frac{\|s^{*}\|}{c_{\lambda^{-1}}}.

Consequently,

y^{\prime}\omega=-\frac{1}{\lambda}a\omega
.
By the right-bounded-vector characterization for the standard form

(M^{\prime},\omega)
, we have

\|y^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{right}}\leq\frac{1}{|\lambda|}\|a\|.

Now
c_{\lambda^{-1}}=\frac{c_{\lambda}}{|\lambda|}
,
and hence

\|y^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm{right}}\leq\frac{\|s\|}{c_{\lambda}}=\frac{\|x^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}}{c_{\lambda}}.

We next estimate the right norm of
(y^{\prime})^{*}
. From

r\omega=(\Delta^{-1}-\lambda I)y^{\prime}\omega

and
\Delta^{-1}=SF
, applying the antilinear operator
F
gives

\displaystyle r^{*}\omega

\displaystyle=Fr\omega

\displaystyle=F(\Delta^{-1}y^{\prime}\omega)-\overline{\lambda}F(y^{\prime}\omega)

\displaystyle=\Delta F(y^{\prime}\omega)-\overline{\lambda}F(y^{\prime}\omega)

\displaystyle=(\Delta-\overline{\lambda}I)(y^{\prime})^{*}\omega.

Therefore

(y^{\prime})^{*}\omega=(\Delta-\overline{\lambda}I)^{-1}r^{*}\omega.

Applying Fact
3.10
to
r^{*}\in M^{\prime}
, with parameter

\overline{\lambda}
, gives
b\in M
such that
(y^{\prime})^{*}\omega=b\omega
and

\|b\|\leq\frac{\|r^{*}\|}{c_{\overline{\lambda}}}=\frac{\|r\|}{c_{\lambda}}.

Hence

\|(y^{\prime})^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}\leq\frac{\|x\|_{\mathrm{right}}}{c_{\lambda}}.

The operator-norm estimate

\|y^{\prime}\|\leq\frac{\|x\|}{c_{\lambda}}

follows from the first assertion of Fact
3.10
. Thus
y^{\prime}
is totally
bounded.
∎

4.
Characterization of
M_{\operatorname{tb}}

In this section, we prove a characterization of the set of totally bounded elements of a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space, to be used in our axiomatization of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces in Section
6
. One of the items in the characterization involves the operator
h_{a}(\log(\Delta))
, where
h_{a}(t)
is the following function, defined for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
:

h_{a}(t):=1/(\cosh(t-a))=\frac{2}{e^{t-a}+e^{a-t}}.

Throughout this section, we fix a
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
with associated standard form
(M,\omega)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For every
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in M_{\operatorname{tb}}
, there exists
y\in M_{\operatorname{tb}}
satisfying
h_{a}(\log(\Delta))x\omega=y\omega
. Furthermore,

\|y\|\leq\|x\|,\qquad\|y\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq\|x\|_{\operatorname{right}},\qquad\|y^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq\|x^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}.

Proof.

Note that

h_{a}(\log(\Delta))=2(e^{-a}\Delta+e^{a}\Delta^{-1})^{-1}=2\mathrm{i}(\Delta+\mathrm{i}e^{a}I)^{-1}(\Delta^{-1}+\mathrm{i}e^{-a}I)^{-1}.

Set
\lambda_{-}=-\mathrm{i}e^{-a}
and
\lambda_{+}=-\mathrm{i}e^{a}
.
Then
c_{\lambda_{-}}c_{\lambda_{+}}=\sqrt{2e^{-a}}\sqrt{2e^{a}}=2
.

Two applications of Corollary
3.11
therefore give

y\in M_{\operatorname{tb}}
with

h_{a}(\log(\Delta))x\omega=y\omega
and

\|y\|\leq\frac{2}{c_{\lambda_{-}}c_{\lambda_{+}}}\|x\|=\|x\|,

\|y\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq\frac{2}{c_{\lambda_{-}}c_{\lambda_{+}}}\|x\|_{\operatorname{right}}=\|x\|_{\operatorname{right}},\qquad\|y^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq\frac{2}{c_{\lambda_{-}}c_{\lambda_{+}}}\|x^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}=\|x^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}.

∎

The following easy calculation will prove useful in the axiomatization of W
∗
-probability space in Section

; see in particular axiom

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For

, we have

if and only if

2R(2-R)x\omega=(e^{-a}(2-R)^{2}+e^{a}R^{2})y\omega.

Suppose that

is a strongly dense

.

(2)

is

(3)

Definition 4.4
.

Let

be a W

of

Remark 4.5
.

At this point, the stipulation of closure of

under

\mathrm{W}^{*}

from its totally bounded elements.

From now on,
j
denotes an element of

(and

.

Suppose that

and

(1)

For all

, there is

The statement obtained by switching

Corollary 4.7

Suppose that

j^{3/2}\|x\|

Proof.

By Fact

,

Our next main goal is the following, which by Corollary

, is a special case of Theorem

:

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose that
M_{0}
is a good subalgebra. If
x\in M

for some

.

First we prove:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
M_{0}\subseteq M
be a good subalgebra. Fix
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for every
x\in M
, there is a unique
T_{r}(x)\in M_{0}
such that

T_{r}(x)\omega=h_{r}(\log\Delta)x\omega.

Moreover, there is a constant
C_{r}>0
, depending only on
r
, such
that

\|T_{r}(x)\|\leq\|x\|,

and

\|T_{r}(x)\|_{\mathrm{right}},\quad\|T_{r}(x)^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}\leq C_{r}\|x\|.

Proof.

By the Fourier transform formula for the hyperbolic secant
(see
[
33
, Chapter 3, Example 3, pp. 81–83]
), the Fourier transform of
\kappa(t):=\frac{1}{2\cosh(\pi t/2)}
(in our convention (
2.1

\widehat{\kappa}(s)=\frac{1}{\cosh s}
.

Therefore, the Fourier transform of
\kappa_{r}(t):=e^{-\mathrm{i}rt}\kappa(t)

satisfies

\widehat{\kappa_{r}}(s)=\frac{1}{\cosh(s-r)}=h_{r}(s),\qquad\|\kappa_{r}\|_{1}=1.

For
x\in M
, define

T_{r}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa_{r}(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)\,dt.

Then

T_{r}(x)\in M
and
\|T_{r}(x)\|\leq\|\kappa_{r}\|_{1}\|x\|=\|x\|.

Moreover,

T_{r}(x)\omega=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa_{r}(t)\Delta^{\mathrm{i}t}x\omega\,dt=h_{r}(\log\Delta)x\omega.

The function
H(z)
given by

H(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa_{r}(t-z)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)\,dt

is analytic on the strip
|\operatorname{Im}z|<1
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, a change of variables shows
H(s)=\sigma_{s}^{\varphi}(T_{r}(x))
.
Consequently
H
is the analytic continuation of the orbit map

s\mapsto\sigma_{s}^{\varphi}(T_{r}(x))
.
Since the map
t\longmapsto\kappa_{r}(t\pm i/2)

belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we obtain

\left\|\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(T_{r}(x))\right\|\leq\|\kappa_{r}(\,\cdot\mp\mathrm{i}/2)\|_{1}\,\|x\|.

Set
C_{r}:=\max\left\{1,\,\|\kappa_{r}(\,\cdot+\mathrm{i}/2)\|_{1},\,\|\kappa_{r}(\,\cdot-\mathrm{i}/2)\|_{1}\right\}.

Then

\|T_{r}(x)\|_{\mathrm{right}}=\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(T_{r}(x))\|\leq C_{r}\|x\|

and

\|T_{r}(x)^{*}\|_{\mathrm{right}}=\|\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(T_{r}(x))\|\leq C_{r}\|x\|.

Thus,
T_{r}(x)
is totally
C_{r}\|x\|
-bounded.

Next, we show that
T_{r}(x)\in M_{0}
. By Kaplansky’s density theorem, there exists a net
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda}
in
M_{0}
converging

*
-strongly to
x
with
\sup_{\lambda}\|x_{\lambda}\|\leq\|x\|
.
Since
M_{0}
is closed under
h_{r}(\log\Delta)
, we have

T_{r}(x_{\lambda})\in M_{0}.

By the preceding estimates, we have

T_{r}(x_{\lambda})\in S_{C_{r}\|x\|}(M_{0})

for every
\lambda
.
Furthermore,

\|T_{r}(x_{\lambda})-T_{r}(x)\|_{\varphi}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\kappa_{r}(t)|\,\|\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x_{\lambda}-x)\|_{\varphi}\,dt=\|\kappa_{r}\|_{1}\|x_{\lambda}-x\|_{\varphi}\xrightarrow{\lambda\to\infty}0.

The same argument applied to adjoints gives
convergence in
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
.
Choose an integer
N\geq C_{r}\left\lVert x\right\rVert
. Every
T_{r}(x_{\lambda})
lies in
S_{N}(M_{0})
. By scaling, the

-completeness of

implies that this subset is
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
-closed in

, and therefore
T_{r}(x)\in M_{0}
.
∎

5.
Effective distance estimate to the totally
1
-bounded elements and the proof of definability of
S_{K}

As stated in the introduction, when axiomatizing
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and tracial von Neumann algebras as model-theoretic structures using operator norm balls as sorts, it is an important step to be able to express the fact that the elements in the operator norm ball of radius
n
whose operator norm is actually at most
1
must be in the image of the canonical inclusion mapping from the ball of radius
1
to the ball of radius
n
. Such an axiomatization can be given using a standard functional calculus argument. We would like to express the analogous fact in our current setting. Unfortunately, such a simple functional calculus argument is not available to us in the non-tracial setting.
Our goal in this section is (1) to prove a replacement for this functional calculus argument which will provide the relevant axioms and its effectiveness, and (2) to prove that the operation of taking totally bounded elements commutes with the Ocneanu ultraproduct.

5.1.
Statements of the main results

We begin by setting up some notation:

Definition 5.1
.

Fix
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and a W
∗
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
. For
x\in S_{n}(M)
, define

B^{L}_{k,n}(x):=\sup_{y\in S_{1}(M)}\frac{\|xy\|_{\varphi}}{\max\{\|y\|_{\varphi},1/k\}},

and similarly define

B^{L}_{k,n}(x^{*}),\qquad B^{R}_{k,n}(x),\qquad B^{R}_{k,n}(x^{*}),

using left and right multiplication respectively. We then set

\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x):=\max\left\{B^{L}_{k,n}(x),B^{L}_{k,n}(x^{*}),B^{R}_{k,n}(x),B^{R}_{k,n}(x^{*})\right\}.

For
n\geq 1
, set
\beta_{k,n}=\frac{2^{-k}}{n}
and define, for
x\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
,

(5.1)

\Phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\beta_{k,n}\bigl(\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}1\bigr).

We record some elementary bounds on
\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}
and

\Phi_{n}
. If
x\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
and
y\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
, then

\|xy\|_{\varphi}\leq\|x\|\|y\|_{\varphi}\leq n\|y\|_{\varphi}

and

\|yx\|_{\varphi}\leq\|x\|_{\mathrm{right}}\|y\|_{\varphi}\leq n\|y\|_{\varphi},

and the same estimates hold with
x
replaced by
x^{*}
. Hence

(5.2)

0\leq\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)\leq n.

Consequently,

0\leq\Phi_{n}(x)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-k}}{n}(n-1)=\frac{n-1}{n}<1

for
n\geq 2
(and
\Phi_{1}=0
).

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, set

\Phi_{n,N}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\beta_{k,n}\bigl(\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}1\bigr).

Then (
5.2
) gives

(5.3)

0\leq\Phi_{n}(x)-\Phi_{n,N}(x)\leq\frac{n-1}{n}\sum_{k>N}2^{-k}=\frac{n-1}{n}2^{-N}\leq 2^{-N}.

In particular,
\Phi_{n}
is the effective uniform limit of the
finite sums
\Phi_{n,N}
.

We will also use the following uniform continuity estimate. If

x,x^{\prime}\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
and
y\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
, then

\|(x-x^{\prime})y\|_{\varphi}\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\varphi},\qquad\|y(x-x^{\prime})\|_{\varphi}\leq\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}.

Since

\max\{\|y\|_{\varphi},1/k\}\geq 1/k,

it follows that

\left|B^{L}_{k,n}(x)-B^{L}_{k,n}(x^{\prime})\right|\leq k\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}\leq k\sqrt{2}\,\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}^{\#},

and the same estimate holds for
B^{R}_{k,n}
and for the corresponding
expressions with
x
replaced by
x^{*}
. Therefore

(5.4)

\left|\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)-\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x^{\prime})\right|\leq k\sqrt{2}\,\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}^{\#}.

Since
r\mapsto r\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}1
is
1
-Lipschitz and

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k2^{-k}=2
, we conclude that

(5.5)

|\Phi_{n}(x)-\Phi_{n}(x^{\prime})|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{n}\,\|x-x^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}^{\#}.

Thus
\Phi_{n}
is a uniformly continuous definable predicate, with a
modulus independent of the
W^{*}
-probability space.

In Section
5.5
, we will show the following effective computability result.

Theorem 5.2
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every rational
d\in(0,1]
, one can effectively
compute an integer
k=k(n,d)
and a positive rational

\delta=\delta(n,d)
such that, for every
W^{*}
-probability space

(M,\varphi)
and every
x\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
, we have

\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)\leq 1+\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\operatorname{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}}\bigl(x,S_{1}(M,\varphi)\bigr)<d.

We then use Theorem
5.2
to prove the following result, which will be essential for the effective axiomatization of W
∗
-probability spaces:

Theorem 5.3
.

There is a uniformly computable family
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
continuous nondecreasing functions

\alpha_{n}:[0,1]\longrightarrow[0,n]
with
\alpha_{n}(0)=0
such that,
for every
n\geq 1
, every
W^{*}
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
,
and every
x\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
, we have

(5.6)

\operatorname{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}}\bigl(x,S_{1}(M,\varphi)\bigr)\leq\alpha_{n}\bigl(\Phi_{n}(x)\bigr).

Remark 5.4
.

For notational convenience, we extend
\alpha_{n}
to

[0,\infty)
by setting

\alpha_{n}(t):=\alpha_{n}(1)\qquad(t\geq 1).

We continue to denote this extension by
\alpha_{n}
.

We fix an index set
I
,

(\bm{M},\bm{\varphi})=(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
a family of
W^{*}
-probability spaces, and a
non-principal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
I
.
In Section
5.6
, we will show that every totally
K
-bounded element in the Ocneanu ultraproduct
(M,\varphi)=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
can be represented by a family of totally
K
-bounded elements in
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
, and vice versa. More precisely, we show the following:

Theorem 5.5
.

For every
K>0
, the following equality holds:

(5.7)

S_{K}\!\left(\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})\right)=\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}S_{K}(M_{i},\varphi_{i}).

For model-theoretic reasons, we refer to this goal as aiming to show that the set of totally
K
-bounded elements forms a definable set. It is straightforward to show the inclusion
\supseteq
in (
5.7
) (Lemma
5.13
). The converse inclusion
\subseteq
is, however, much more delicate and it relies crucially on Theorem
5.2
.

Before proceeding further, we explain how a naïve attempt at proving the hard part
\subseteq
by kernel smoothing might proceed, the pitfalls it faces, and how our proof bypasses these potential problems.
Fix
x=(x_{i})_{\mathcal{U}}\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
.
By the density of
(M_{i})_{\mathrm{tb}}
in
M_{i}
, if
{\mathcal{U}}
is countably incomplete, we may
assume that each
x_{i}
satisfies
\|x_{i}\|\leq K
and is totally bounded, or even

\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}
-analytic. However, a priori we may have no bound on
\|x_{i}\|_{\mathrm{tb}}
,
though
\|x\|_{\mathrm{tb}}\leq K
holds.

It is tempting to apply some kernel smoothing.
For example, let
F_{a}
(
a>0
) be the Fejér kernel, and consider the Fejér smoothing
of
x
:

x_{a}:=\sigma^{\varphi}_{F_{a}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{a}(t)\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(x)\,dt.

Then because the
F_{a}
-smoothing commutes with the Ocneanu ultraproduct, we have

x_{a}=(x_{i,a})_{\mathcal{U}},\quad x_{i,a}=\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{F_{a}}(x_{i}).

Then

\lim_{a\to\infty}\|x_{a}-x\|^{\#}_{\varphi}=0,

and because the Fourier transform of
F_{a}
has compact support,

\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{F_{a}})\subset[-a,a]
, we have

x_{i,a}\in M_{i}(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}},[-a,a])
, which by
[
2
, Proposition 2.14]
implies that

x_{i,a}\in S_{Ke^{a/2}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i}).

Thus,
x_{i,a}
is almost totally
K
-bounded if
a\sim 0
, but we need
a\to\infty
in
order to get
x_{i}\sim x_{i,a}
, and then we would lose the control of
\|x_{i,a}\|_{\mathrm{tb}}
.
This difficulty is not specific to the Fejér kernel. Kernel smoothing improves the modular
regularity of a chosen representative, or gives spectral control depending on the smoothing
parameter, but it does not reflect the original total
K
-boundedness of the element in the
Ocneanu ultraproduct. Our proof below overcomes this difficulty by showing that sufficiently good finite-level control of the left and right multiplication norms,
as measured by
\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}
, forces an element to lie uniformly close to
S_{1}
.

In the Appendix, we will give an alternative proof of this result by a completely different method, namely the
H^{\infty}
-lifting of the analytic extension of the modular orbit
t\mapsto\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)
.

5.2.
A few preliminary lemmas

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
K,L>0
. Then the inclusion

S_{K}(M,\varphi)S_{L}(M,\varphi)\subseteq S_{KL}(M,\varphi)

holds. Moreover,
S_{K}(M,\varphi)
is convex, balanced, and invariant under the
adjoint operation and under the real modular automorphism group.

Proof.

Let
x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
and
y\in S_{L}(M,\varphi)
. Then
\|xy\|\leq\|x\|\|y\|\leq KL
.
Since

R_{xy}=R_{y}R_{x}
and

R_{(xy)^{*}}=R_{x^{*}}R_{y^{*}}
, we have
\|R_{xy}\|\leq\|R_{x}\|\|R_{y}\|\leq KL
and
\|R_{(xy)^{*}}\|\leq KL
as well.
Therefore,
xy\in S_{KL}(M,\varphi)
holds. Most of the last assertions are immediate. For the final assertion, we use the fact that
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}
leaves
D(\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi})
invariant and commutes with
\sigma_{\pm\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}
on these domains.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
a=a^{*}\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
and let
R>0
. Then, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(5.8)

\|\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(a)-a\|_{\varphi}\leq nR|t|+4ne^{-R/2}.

Proof.

By
a=a^{*}\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
, we have

\|a\omega_{\varphi}\|,\quad\|\Delta_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{2}}a\omega_{\varphi}\|=\|\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(a)\omega_{\varphi}\|,\quad\|\Delta_{\varphi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}a\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq n.

Consequently, the spectral projections
E_{+}=1_{(R,\infty)}(\log\Delta_{\varphi})
and
E_{-}=1_{(-\infty,-R)}(\log\Delta_{\varphi})
satisfy

\|E_{+}a\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq ne^{-R/2},\qquad\|E_{-}a\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq ne^{-R/2}.

On
[-R,R]
one has
|e^{\mathrm{i}ts}-1|\leq R|t|
.
Therefore, for
E:=1-E_{+}-E_{-}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|(\Delta_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{i}t}-1)Ea\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq R|t|\|Ea\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq nR|t|,

and

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{i}t}-1)E_{\pm}a\omega_{\varphi}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|E_{\pm}a\omega_{\varphi}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2ne^{-R/2}.

Therefore, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\begin{aligned} \|\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(a)-a\|_{\varphi}&=\|(\Delta_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{i}t}-1)a\omega_{\varphi}\|\\
&\leq nR|t|+4ne^{-R/2}.\end{aligned}\qed

We shall also use the following elementary effective approximation fact.

Lemma 5.8
.

Given a positive rational
\varepsilon
, one can effectively find a real
polynomial
p\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|p(t)-\sqrt{t}|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

This is a consequence of a well-known polynomial approximation method.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the Bernstein polynomial for
\sqrt{t}
, given by

B_{N}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sqrt{\frac{k}{N}}\binom{N}{k}t^{k}(1-t)^{N-k},\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1.

We first give an explicit estimate for the error.

Fix
t\in[0,1]
, and let
X
be a binomial random variable with
parameters
(N,t)
. Thus,

\mathbb{P}(X=k)=\binom{N}{k}t^{k}(1-t)^{N-k},\qquad k=0,\dots,N,

and
\mathbb{E}(X)=Nt,\operatorname{Var}(X)=Nt(1-t).
Then
B_{N}(t)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\frac{X}{N}}\right]
.
Using
|\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}|\leq\sqrt{|s-t|},\,(s,t\geq 0)
,
Cauchy–Schwarz, and Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle|B_{N}(t)-\sqrt{t}|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sqrt{\frac{X}{N}}-\sqrt{t}\right|\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{\left|\frac{X}{N}-t\right|}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{X}{N}-t\right|\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{X}{N}-t\right|^{2}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t(1-t)}{N}\right)^{1/4}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,N^{1/4}}.

Consequently,

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|B_{N}(t)-\sqrt{t}|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,N^{1/4}}.

Given
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, choose
N
effectively so large
that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,N^{1/4}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
; for example, any integer
N>4\varepsilon^{-4}
suffices.
For each
0\leq k\leq N
, choose a rational number
r_{k}
such that

\left|r_{k}-\sqrt{\frac{k}{N}}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
Set

p(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}r_{k}\binom{N}{k}t^{k}(1-t)^{N-k}.

Then
p\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
. Since the Bernstein polynomials are
nonnegative on
[0,1]
and sum to
1
, we have

\|p-B_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq\max_{0\leq k\leq N}\left|r_{k}-\sqrt{\frac{k}{N}}\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Therefore

\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|p(t)-\sqrt{t}|<\varepsilon.

All choices are effective, which proves the lemma.
∎

The next proposition will play a key role.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
a=a^{*}\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
. Define the normal positive functional
\omega_{a}
on
M
by

\omega_{a}(x)=\varphi(axa),\qquad x\in M,

and let

\chi:=\omega_{a}-\varphi=\chi_{+}-\chi_{-}

be the Jordan decomposition of
\chi\in M_{*}
. Set
q:=\chi_{+}(1)
.
Then there exists
b=b^{*}\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
such that

(5.9)

\|a-b\|_{\varphi}\leq(n+1)\sqrt{q}.

Consequently,

(5.10)

\operatorname{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}}\bigl(a,S_{1}(M,\varphi)\bigr)\geq d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\geq\frac{d^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}}.

Proof.

For each
\lambda\in M_{*}^{+}
and
c\in M
, define the linear functional
\lambda_{c}
on
M
by

\lambda_{c}(x)=\lambda(c^{*}xc),\qquad x\in M.

Set
\mu=\varphi+\chi_{+}
.
We have
\varphi\leq\mu
and
\omega_{a}\leq\mu
.
By Sakai’s quadratic Radon–Nikodym theorem
[
29
, Theorem 2]
applied to

\varphi\leq\mu
, there exists a positive contraction
h\in M
such that

(5.11)

\varphi(x)=\mu(hxh),\qquad x\in M,

or, in other words,
\mu_{h}=\varphi
.
Since
\varphi\leq\mu
, for every
x\in M_{+}
we have

\varphi_{h}(x)=\varphi(hxh)\leq\mu(hxh)=\varphi(x),

and hence
\varphi_{h}\leq\varphi
.

Likewise, since
\omega_{a}\leq\mu
, we have
(\omega_{a})_{h}\leq\mu_{h}=\varphi.

Set
b=hah
. Then
b=b^{*}
. We first show that
\varphi_{b}\leq\varphi
.

Indeed, if
x\in M_{+}
, then
ahxha\geq 0
, and therefore, by
\varphi_{h}\leq\varphi
and
(\omega_{a})_{h}\leq\varphi
,

\displaystyle\varphi(bxb)

\displaystyle=\varphi(hahxhah)=\varphi_{h}(ahxha)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(ahxha)=\omega_{a}(hxh)

\displaystyle=(\omega_{a})_{h}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x).

This proves
\varphi_{b}\leq\varphi
. For
x\in M
,

\displaystyle\|xb\|_{\varphi}^{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi(bx^{*}xb)=\varphi_{b}(x^{*}x)

\displaystyle\leq\varphi(x^{*}x)=\|x\|_{\varphi}^{2}.

Hence
\|R_{b}\|\leq 1
. Since
b=b^{*}
, the same estimate gives

\|R_{b^{*}}\|\leq 1
.

We next show that
\|b\|\leq 1
. Suppose otherwise. Then there is

\varepsilon>0
such that

p=1_{(1+\varepsilon,\infty)}(|b|)

is nonzero. Since
b=b^{*}
,
|b|=(b^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\in W^{*}(b)
. Therefore,
p\in W^{*}(b)
, whence
p
commutes with
b
. Thus,

bpb=|b|^{2}p\geq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}p.

On the other hand,
\varphi_{b}\leq\varphi
gives

\varphi(bpb)\leq\varphi(p)
.
Consequently,

(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\varphi(p)\leq\varphi(bpb)\leq\varphi(p),

which contradicts the faithfulness of
\varphi
, since
p\neq 0
.
Therefore
\|b\|\leq 1
, and hence

b\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
.

Next, we estimate
\|a-b\|_{\varphi}
. Since
0\leq h\leq 1
,
(1-h)^{2}\leq 1-h^{2}
holds.
Using (
5.11
) at
x=1
, we have

\mu(h^{2})=\varphi(1)=1
.
Also,

\mu(1)=\varphi(1)+\chi_{+}(1)=1+q.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mu((1-h)^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\mu(1-h^{2})=\mu(1)-\mu(h^{2})

\displaystyle=q.

Since
\omega_{a}\leq\mu
,

\displaystyle\|(1-h)a\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\varphi\bigl(a(1-h)^{2}a\bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\omega_{a}((1-h)^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\mu((1-h)^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{q}.

Similarly, since
\varphi\leq\mu
,

\displaystyle\|1-h\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\varphi((1-h)^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mu((1-h)^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{q}.

From
a-b=a-hah=(1-h)a+ha(1-h)
,
and from the preceding estimates it follows that, using
\|h\|\leq 1
and
\|a\|\leq n
,

\displaystyle\|a-b\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-h)a\|_{\varphi}+\|ha\|\|(1-h)\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq(n+1)\sqrt{q}.

Since both
a
and
b
are self-adjoint,
\|a-b\|_{\varphi}^{\#}=\|a-b\|_{\varphi}
holds. Therefore,

\operatorname{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}}\bigl(a,S_{1}(M,\varphi)\bigr)\leq(n+1)\sqrt{q},

and consequently

\operatorname{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}}\bigl(a,S_{1}(M,\varphi)\bigr)\geq d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad q\geq\frac{d^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}}.\qed

5.3.
An effective distance estimate to
S_{1}
for self-adjoint elements

We now turn the lower bound on
q
into a totally
1
-bounded test element
of controlled
L^{2}
-norm.

Convention on effectivity.

Throughout the effective arguments below, the input parameters are

n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]
. All real constants
constructed from
(n,d)
are uniformly computable from these parameters.
Whenever below we choose a rational number strictly below a positive
computable real, or a rational number strictly above a computable real,
such a choice is understood to be made effectively.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
d\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]
. From
(n,d)
one can effectively compute
positive real numbers
\mu_{n}^{\mathrm{sa}}(d)>0
and
\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{sa}}(d)>0

with the following property: If
a=a^{*}\in S_{n}(M,\varphi)
and

\operatorname{dist}_{\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}}\bigl(a,S_{1}(M,\varphi)\bigr)\geq d,

then there exists a self-adjoint
z\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
such that

\|z\|_{\varphi}\geq\mu_{n}^{\mathrm{sa}}(d),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|za\|_{\varphi}\geq\bigl(1+\eta_{n}^{\mathrm{sa}}(d)\bigr)\|z\|_{\varphi}.

Proof.

Set

(5.12)

q_{0}=\frac{d^{2}}{(n+1)^{2}},\qquad m_{0}=\frac{q_{0}}{n^{2}-1}.

Let
\chi:=\omega_{a}-\varphi=\chi_{+}-\chi_{-}
be the Jordan decomposition and
q:=\chi_{+}(1)
be as in
Proposition
5.9
. Then
q\geq q_{0}
.
Let
e
be the support projection of
\chi_{+}
. Since the supports of the
positive and negative parts in the Jordan decomposition are orthogonal, we have

q=(\omega_{a}-\varphi)(e)=\varphi(aea)-\varphi(e).

Set
m:=\varphi(e)
. The right
n
-boundedness of
a
gives
\varphi(aea)\leq n^{2}\varphi(e)
,
and hence

(5.13)

m\geq\frac{q}{n^{2}-1}\geq m_{0}.

We now regularize
e
. Define

(5.14)

R=2\log\left(\frac{64n^{2}}{q_{0}}\right),\qquad\tau=\frac{\pi q_{0}^{2}}{1024n^{4}R^{2}},

and set
e_{\tau}=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(e)
.
Let
q_{\tau}:=\varphi(ae_{\tau}a)-\varphi(e_{\tau})
.
Since
\varphi(e_{\tau})=\varphi(e)
, we have

q_{\tau}-q=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\left[\varphi\bigl(a\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(e)a\bigr)-\varphi(aea)\right]dt.

By the
\sigma^{\varphi}
-invariance of
\varphi
, we have

\varphi\bigl(a\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(e)a\bigr)=\varphi\bigl(\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi}(a)e\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi}(a)\bigr).

For self-adjoint elements
c,c^{\prime}\in M
with
\|c\|,\|c^{\prime}\|\leq n
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle|\varphi(cec)-\varphi(c^{\prime}ec^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi((c-c^{\prime})ec)|+|\varphi(c^{\prime}e(c-c^{\prime}))|

\displaystyle\leq\|(c-c^{\prime})^{*}\|_{\varphi}\|ec\|_{\varphi}+\|(ec^{\prime})^{*}\|_{\varphi}\|c-c^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq 2n\|c-c^{\prime}\|_{\varphi}.

Together with Lemma
5.7
, this yields

(5.15)

|q_{\tau}-q|\leq 4n^{2}R\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\pi}}+8n^{2}e^{-R/2}.

Indeed, we use (
5.8
) and (
5.14
) to obtain

\displaystyle|q_{\tau}-q|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)|\varphi(\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi}(a)e\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi}(a))-\varphi(aea)|dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\cdot 2n\|\sigma_{-t}^{\varphi}(a)-a\|_{\varphi}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\cdot 2n(nR|t|+4ne^{-R/2})dt

\displaystyle=4n^{2}R\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\pi}}+8n^{2}e^{-R/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q_{0}}{4}.

Here, we used

\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)|t|\,dt=2\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\pi}}.

Therefore
q_{\tau}\geq\frac{1}{2}q_{0}
.

Let
u=e_{\tau}^{1/2}
. Then

\|u\|_{\varphi}^{2}=\varphi(e_{\tau})=m
, and

\|ua\|_{\varphi}^{2}=\varphi(ae_{\tau}a)=m+q_{\tau}\geq m+\frac{q_{0}}{2}.

Since
m\leq 1
, we obtain

(5.16)

\|ua\|_{\varphi}\geq(1+\rho_{0})\|u\|_{\varphi},\qquad\rho_{0}=\sqrt{1+\frac{q_{0}}{2}}-1.

Moreover, (
5.13
) gives

\|u\|_{\varphi}\geq s_{0},\qquad s_{0}=\sqrt{m_{0}}.

The element
u
need not be totally bounded, so we replace it by a polynomial
in
e_{\tau}
.

Choose
\varepsilon_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
such that

\varepsilon_{0}<\min\left\{\frac{s_{0}}{4},\frac{\rho_{0}s_{0}}{8(n+2)}\right\}.

By Lemma
5.8
, choose effectively a
polynomial
p(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_{j}t^{j}\in\mathbb{Q}[t]

such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|p(t)-\sqrt{t}|<\varepsilon_{0}
.
Set
w:=p(e_{\tau})
. Then
w=w^{*}
,
\|w-u\|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, and

\|w\|_{\varphi}\geq\frac{s_{0}}{2}
. The right
n
-boundedness of
a
gives
\|(w-u)a\|_{\varphi}\leq n\varepsilon_{0}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|wa\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\geq\|ua\|_{\varphi}-\|(u-w)a\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\rho_{0})\|u\|_{\varphi}-n\varepsilon_{0}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right)\|w\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right)(\|u\|_{\varphi}+\varepsilon_{0}).

Note that

(5.17)

\left(1+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right)(\|u\|_{\varphi}+\varepsilon_{0})\leq(1+\rho_{0})\|u\|_{\varphi}-n\varepsilon_{0}.

Indeed, since
0<\rho_{0}<1
,

\displaystyle(1+\rho_{0})\|u\|_{\varphi}

\displaystyle-n\varepsilon_{0}-\left(1+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right)(\|u\|_{\varphi}+\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\|u\|_{\varphi}-n\varepsilon_{0}-\left(1+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right)\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho_{0}s_{0}}{2}-(n+1+\tfrac{\rho_{0}}{2})\varepsilon_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho_{0}s_{0}}{2}-(n+2)\cdot\frac{\rho_{0}s_{0}}{8(n+2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\rho_{0}s_{0}}{8}>0.

Therefore, we obtain

(5.18)

\|wa\|_{\varphi}\geq\left(1+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\right)\|w\|_{\varphi}.

By Lemma
2.13
,
e_{\tau}\in S_{e^{1/(16\tau)}}(M,\varphi)
holds.
Lemma
5.6
gives

\|w\|_{\operatorname{tb}}\leq D,\qquad D:=\sum_{j=0}^{N}|c_{j}|e^{\frac{j}{16\tau}}.

Effectively choose an integer
D_{0}\geq\max\{1,D\}
and set
z=D_{0}^{-1}w
. Then
z=z^{*}\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
,

\|z\|_{\varphi}\geq\frac{s_{0}}{2D_{0}},

and (
5.18

The

Set

Lemma 5.11

Proof.

We have

\|\widehat{x}\|_{\psi}^{\#}=\|x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
holds.

\displaystyle=\left\|\begin{pmatrix}bx^{*}&ax\\

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\|bx^{*}\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|dx^{*}\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|ax\|_{\varphi}^{2}+\|cx\|_{\varphi}^{2})

On the other hand,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|ax\|_{\varphi}^{2}.

0&0\end{pmatrix}

holds.
The equivalence (

) then follows.

).

. Choose

Together with

Therefore,

is contractive for the

If

Combining the above matrix amplification trick with the self-adjoint case treated in the previous subsection, we have:

Theorem 5.12

For every integer

and every rational

effectively compute positive real numbers

We apply Theorem
5.10
in
(N,\psi)
to

Put

The entries of

c_{i}^{(m)}=0
for
i\notin I_{m}
. By Lemma
5.13
,
c^{(m)}:=(c_{i}^{(m)})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in S_{1}(M,\varphi)
.
Moreover,

\|c^{(m)}-x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}\leq\|c^{(m)}-x_{\tau_{m}}\|_{\varphi}^{\#}+\|x_{\tau_{m}}-x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}<1/m.

Thus
\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\|c^{(m)}-x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}=0
.
Since
x=(a_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, this implies that

E_{m}:=\left\{i\in I\,\middle|\,\|c_{i}^{(m)}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{2}{m}\right\}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Since
{\mathcal{U}}
is countably incomplete, choose a decreasing sequence

C_{1}\supseteq C_{2}\supseteq\cdots
in
{\mathcal{U}}
such that

\bigcap_{m}C_{m}=\emptyset
.
Set
J_{m}:=C_{m}\cap E_{1}\cap\dots\cap E_{m}
. Then

J_{1}\supseteq J_{2}\supseteq\dots
is a decreasing sequence in
{\mathcal{U}}
with
\bigcap_{m}J_{m}=\emptyset
.
Define

z_{i}=\begin{cases}c_{i}^{(m)},&i\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m+1},\\
0,&i\notin J_{1}.\end{cases}

Then
z_{i}\in S_{1}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
for every
i
. Moreover, the integer

m
determined by
i\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m+1}
tends to infinity along

{\mathcal{U}}
, and
\|z_{i}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{2}{m}.

Consequently,

\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|z_{i}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}=0.

By Lemma
5.13
,
(z_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
, and therefore
(z_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=(a_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=x.

Thus
x
admits a representative with

z_{i}\in S_{1}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
for every
i
, as required.

Next, we prove the theorem for countably complete
{\mathcal{U}}
. First, note that in this case, we have
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}=\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
. This is well-known, but for completeness we include the standard argument.
If
a=(a_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
,
then
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\|a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}=0.

Hence, for every
n\geq 1
, we have

E_{n}=\left\{i\in I\,\,\middle|\,\ \|a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{1}{n}\right\}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Therefore, by countable completeness,

\{i\in I\ :\ \|a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}=0\}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\in\mathcal{U},

whence
a_{i}=0
for
{\mathcal{U}}
-almost every
i
.
It follows that

\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}=\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M}),

because every bounded sequence multiplies sequences which vanish on a

\mathcal{U}
-large set.

Let
q:\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})\to M

be the quotient map. Since
q
is a surjective unital
*
-homomorphism between unital C
∗
-algebras, it maps the closed unit ball of
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
onto the closed unit ball of
M
. Thus, we may choose a representative

(a_{i})_{i}\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
of
x
satisfying
\sup_{i\in I}\|a_{i}\|\leq 1
.

We next show that
a_{i}
is right
1
-bounded for
\mathcal{U}
-almost every
i
. Assume by contradiction that this is not the case. Then for some
n\geq 1
, the set

A_{n}=\left\{i\in I\ :\ a_{i}\text{ is not right }(1+1/n)\text{-bounded}\right\}

belongs to
\mathcal{U}
. For each
i\in A_{n}
, choose
b_{i}\in M_{i}
such
that

\|b_{i}\omega_{\varphi_{i}}\|=1
and
\|b_{i}a_{i}\omega_{\varphi_{i}}\|>1+\frac{1}{n}.

This is possible by the definition of the right multiplication norm.

Note that the elements
b_{i}
need not be uniformly bounded in operator norm. However,
by countable completeness, they are uniformly bounded on a
\mathcal{U}
-large subset. Indeed, the sets

A_{n,m}=\{i\in A_{n}\ :\ \|b_{i}\|\leq m\},\qquad m\geq 1,

increase to
A_{n}
. Since
A_{n}\in\mathcal{U}
, countable completeness
then implies that
A_{n,m_{0}}\in\mathcal{U}

for some
m_{0}
. Redefine
b_{i}=0
for
i\in I\setminus A_{n,m_{0}}
. Then
(b_{i})_{i}\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
,
and hence
b:=(b_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}\in M.

Since
x
is right
1
-bounded in
M
, we have
\|bx\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq\|b\omega_{\varphi}\|.
However, by construction,

\|b\omega_{\varphi}\|=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\|b_{i}\omega_{\varphi_{i}}\|=1,

while

\|bx\omega_{\varphi}\|=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\|b_{i}a_{i}\omega_{\varphi_{i}}\|\geq 1+\frac{1}{n}.

This contradicts
\|bx\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq 1.

Therefore, for every
n\geq 1
,

E_{+,n}=\left\{i\in I\ :\ a_{i}\text{ is right }(1+1/n)\text{-bounded}\right\}\in\mathcal{U}.

Then by countable completeness of
{\mathcal{U}}
, we obtain a
set
E_{+}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{+,n}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

\|a_{i}\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq 1

for every
i\in E_{+}
.

Applying the same argument to
x^{*}=(a_{i}^{*})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, and using that
x^{*}
is
right
1
-bounded, we obtain a set
E_{-}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

\|a_{i}^{*}\|_{\operatorname{right}}\leq 1

for every
i\in E_{-}
. Set

E=E_{+}\cap E_{-}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Define

x_{i}:=\begin{cases}a_{i},&i\in E,\\
0,&i\in I\setminus E.\end{cases}

Then for every
i\in I
, we have
x_{i}\in S_{1}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
, and
(x_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=(a_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=x.
This finishes the proof.
∎

6.
Axiomatizing
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces

We are now ready to introduce our language
{\mathcal{L}}_{W^{*}}
for
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces:

(L1)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a sort
S_{n}
with bound
2n
, whose intended interpretation is the set
S_{n}(M)
. We let
d_{n}
denote the metric symbol on
S_{n}
, whose intended interpretation is the metric induced by
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
.

(L2)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are binary function symbols
+_{n}
and
-_{n}
with domain
S_{n}^{2}
and range
S_{2n}
and whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon/2
. The intended interpretation of these symbols are addition and subtraction in the algebra restricted to the sort
S_{n}
.

(L3)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a binary function symbol
\cdot_{n}
with domain
S_{n}^{2}
and range
S_{n^{2}}
and whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon/(2n)
. The intended interpretation of these symbols is multiplication in the algebra restricted to the sort
S_{n}
.

(L4)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a unary function symbol
*_{n}
whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon
. The intended interpretation of these symbols is for the adjoint restricted to each sort.

(L5)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are two constant symbols
0_{n}
and
1_{n}
which lie in the sort
S_{n}
. The intended interpretation of these symbols are the elements
0
and
1
.

(L6)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, there is a unary function symbol
\lambda_{n}
whose domain is
S_{n}
and range is
S_{mn}
, where
m=\max(\lceil|\lambda|\rceil,1)
, and with modulus of uniform continuity
\delta(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon}{|\lambda|}
when
\lambda\not=0
and
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon
otherwise. The intended interpretation of these symbols is scalar multiplication by
\lambda
restricted to
S_{n}
.
For the effective version of the language, we restrict the scalar
symbols to the computable countable field
\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i},e)
. In
particular, all of the scalars
e^{\pm a}
,
a\in\mathbb{N}
, occurring
in axiom
(A11)
belong to the language.

(L7)

For each
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m<n
, we have unary function symbols
\iota_{m,n}
with domain
S_{m}
and range
S_{n}
and whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon
. The intended interpretation of these symbols is the inclusion map between the sorts. We abbreviate
\iota_{1,n}
by
\iota_{n}
.

(L8)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have a unary predicate symbol
\varphi_{n}
, whose range is
[-n,n]
and whose modulus of uniform continuity is
\delta(\epsilon)=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{2}}
(see Lemma
2.6
). The intended interpretation of this symbol is the restriction of the state to
S_{n}
. Technically speaking, there should really be two such symbols, one for the real and imaginary parts of the states, but we content ourselves to abuse notation here.

(L9)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have function symbols
\mathbf{P}_{n}:S_{n}\to S_{3n}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{n}:S_{n}\to S_{3n}
, and
\mathbf{R}_{n}:S_{n}\to S_{6n}
. The moduli of uniform continuity of the first two symbols are
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon/\sqrt{2}
while the latter has modulus
\delta(\epsilon)=\epsilon/(2\sqrt{2})
. The intended interpretations of these symbols is as in the Rieffel-Van Daele theory from Section
3
(see Corollary
3.6
).

For the effective presentation, we use a fixed effectively presented family of continuous connectives containing the usual
connectives and the functions
\alpha_{n}
.

We associate to each
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
in
W^{*}
-Prob an
{\mathcal{L}}_{W^{*}}
-structure
\mathcal{D}(M,\varphi)
, called its
dissection
, by interpreting the symbols above with their intended meanings as outlined when introducing the language.

The goal now is to axiomatize the class of such dissections. We consider the following
\mathcal{L}_{W^{*}}
-theory
T_{W^{*}}
. For the sake of readability, we sometimes omit some of the inclusion mappings between sorts and/or subscripts on the predicate and function symbols.

For fixed
k,n
, the predicates

B^{L}_{k,n}
,
B^{R}_{k,n}
, and
\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}

are regarded as abbreviations for the corresponding

\mathcal{L}_{W^{*}}
-formulas. In particular, for each
N
,
\Phi_{n,N}
is an

\mathcal{L}_{W^{*}}
-formula.

(A1)

Axioms for a unital
*
-algebra.

(A2)

Axioms saying that
\varphi
is a positive linear functional satisfying
\varphi(1)=1
.

(A3)

Axioms saying that the connecting maps preserve the unital complex

∗
-algebra structure and the state. More precisely, whenever

m<n
, the map
\iota_{m,n}:S_{m}\to S_{n}
preserves
0
,
1
, scalar
multiplication, addition, multiplication, adjoints, and
\varphi
, and
the connecting maps are compatible:

\iota_{\ell,n}\circ\iota_{m,\ell}=\iota_{m,n}\qquad(m<\ell<n).

(A4)

Axioms saying that the metric is derived from
\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
.

(A5)

Axioms requiring that the elements of
S_{n}
are totally
n
-bounded. More precisely, for each
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the axioms

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}\left(\sup_{y\in S_{k}}\max\{\varphi((xy)^{*}xy)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}n^{2}\varphi(y^{*}y),\varphi((yx)^{*}yx)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}n^{2}\varphi(y^{*}y)\}\right)

and similar axioms with
x
replaced by
x^{*}
.

(A6)

Axioms expressing that the inclusion maps are isometries and map elements of
S_{1}
to 1-bounded elements of
S_{n}
. Specifically, for each
n\geq 2
, we add:

\sup_{x\in S_{1}}\bigl|\|\iota_{n}(x)\|_{\varphi}-\|x\|_{\varphi}\bigr|

together with

\sup_{x\in S_{1}}\sup_{z\in S_{n}}\bigl|\varphi((xz)^{*}(xz))-\varphi((\iota_{n}(x)z)^{*}((\iota_{n}(x))z))\bigr|

and

\sup_{x\in S_{1}}\sup_{z\in S_{n}}\bigl|\varphi((zx)^{*}(zx))-\varphi((z\iota_{n}(x))^{*}(z\iota_{n}(x)))\bigr|

and similar axioms with
x
replaced by
x^{*}
.

(A7)

Axioms expressing that the inclusion
\iota_{n}
has as image the set of
all
x\in S_{n}
which are totally
1
-bounded.

For
n\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
, put

\Phi_{n,N}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\beta_{k,n}\bigl(\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}1\bigr).

We include, for every
n\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
, the axiom

(6.1)

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}\left(\inf_{y\in S_{1}}d_{n}\bigl(x,\iota_{n}(y)\bigr)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\hbox{
\vrule height=3.0pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.0pt}{\smash{-}}}\limits^{\textstyle\ .}}\alpha_{n}\bigl(\Phi_{n,N}(x)+2^{-N}\bigr)\right)=0.

The case
n=1
is vacuous.

Indeed, by (
5.3
),

\Phi_{n}(x)\leq\Phi_{n,N}(x)+2^{-N}.

Since
\alpha_{n}
is nondecreasing, Theorem
5.3

implies that every dissection satisfies these axioms.

Notice also that

\Phi_{n,N}(x)\leq\frac{n-1}{n}(1-2^{-N}),

and hence

\Phi_{n,N}(x)+2^{-N}\leq\frac{n-1+2^{-N}}{n}<1.

Thus, in the intended structures, the constant extension of
\alpha_{n}
past
1
is never used.

(A8)

Axioms stating that
\mathbf{P}_{n}
is the orthogonal projection on to the set of self-adjoint elements.
This is taken care of by axioms expressing that
\mathbf{P}_{n}
is real-linear together with the following axioms:

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{3n}(\mathbf{P}_{n}(x),\mathbf{P}_{n}(x)^{*})\quad\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{9n}(\mathbf{P}_{3n}(\mathbf{P}_{n}(x)),\mathbf{P}_{n}(x))

and

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{6n}\left(\mathbf{P}_{2n}\left(\frac{x+x^{*}}{2}\right),\frac{x+x^{*}}{2}\right)

and

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}\sup_{y\in S_{1}}|\Re(\varphi((y+y^{*})(x-\mathbf{P}_{n}(x))))|.

In the last axiom above, we mean the real part of
\varphi
.

(A9)

Axioms expressing the relation between
\mathbf{P}_{n}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{n}
:

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{3n}(\mathbf{Q}_{n}(x),i\mathbf{P}_{n}(-ix)).

(A10)

Axiom expressing the relation between
\mathbf{P}_{n}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{n}
, and
\mathbf{R}_{n}
:

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}d_{6n}(\mathbf{R}_{n}(x),\mathbf{P}_{n}(x)+\mathbf{Q}_{n}(x)).

(A11)

For each
a\in\mathbb{N}
, axioms that express that the model is closed
under
h_{a}(\log\Delta)
. By Lemma
4.2
, this can be taken
care of with the following axioms (for appropriately sorted
\mathbf{R}
’s):

\sup_{x\in S_{n}}\inf_{y\in S_{n}}d\left(2\mathbf{R}(2-\mathbf{R})x,\bigl(e^{-a}(2-\mathbf{R})^{2}+e^{a}\mathbf{R}^{2}\bigr)y\right).

Theorem 6.1
.

T_{W^{*}}
axiomatizes the class of dissections of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces. Moreover, the class of models of
T_{W^{*}}
is categorically equivalent to the category of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces. In fact, this equivalence is an equivalence of
ultracategories
in the sense that, given a family
(M_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}
of W
∗
-probability spaces and an ultrafilter
{\mathcal{U}}
on
I
, the equivalence of categories maps the model-theoretic ultraproduct
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}\mathcal{D}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
of the dissections to the Ocneanu ultraproduct
\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
. Finally, the set
T_{W^{*}}
is an effective set of axioms.

Proof.

Based on the calculations in Section
3
, it is straightforward to see that, for any
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability space
(M,\varphi)
, the dissection
\mathcal{D}(M,\varphi)
is a model of
T_{W^{*}}
.

Conversely, consider a model
A
of the theory
T_{W^{*}}
. We begin by forming the direct limit
M_{0}
of the sorts
S_{n}(A)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
via the embeddings
i
.
Using the interpretations of the algebraic function symbols,

M_{0}
is a unital
∗
-algebra over the chosen scalar field.
In the countable presentation, scalar multiplication extends
uniquely from
\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i},e)
to
\mathbb{C}
by completeness
of the sorts: for
a\in S_{n}(A)
and a bounded sequence

(\lambda_{j})_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mathrm{i})
converging to
\lambda
,
the elements
\lambda_{j}a
lie in a common sort and satisfy

d(\lambda_{j}a,\lambda_{k}a)\leq n|\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}|.

The algebra identities extend by continuity, making
M_{0}

a complex unital
∗
-algebra.
Furthermore, using the predicate for the state on each sort, one can define a semi-inner product
\langle x,y\rangle:=\varphi(y^{*}x)
on
M_{0}
. We let
H
denote the Hilbert space separation/completion of
M_{0}
with respect to this inner product. For each
a\in M_{0}
, the maps
b\mapsto ab:M_{0}\to M_{0}
and
b\mapsto ba:M_{0}\to M_{0}
induce bounded linear operators
\pi(a):H\to H
and
\pi^{\prime}(a):H\to H
respectively satisfying
\|\pi(a)\|,\|\pi^{\prime}(a)\|\leq n
if
a\in S_{n}(A)
. Note that, for
a\in S_{n}(A)
, we have that
\varphi(a)=\langle\hat{a},\hat{1}\rangle=\langle\pi(a)\hat{1},\hat{1}\rangle
. Note also that
\pi^{\prime}(M_{0})\subseteq\pi(M_{0})^{\prime}
.
We note that
\pi
is faithful. Indeed, if
\pi(a)=0
, then also

\pi(a^{*})=0
, and hence

\varphi(a^{*}a)=\|\pi(a)\widehat{1}\|^{2}=0,\qquad\varphi(aa^{*})=\|\pi(a^{*})\widehat{1}\|^{2}=0.

By the axiom identifying the metric with the

\|\cdot\|_{\varphi}^{\#}
-metric, it follows that

d(a,0)=0
, whence
a=0
.
Set
M_{A}:=\pi(M_{0})^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H)
. Note that the vector
\omega:=\hat{1}
is cyclic for
M_{A}
. Moreover,
\pi^{\prime}(M_{0})\omega=\pi(M_{0})\omega=\widehat{M_{0}}
is dense in
H
and
\pi^{\prime}(M_{0})\subseteq M_{A}^{\prime}
. Hence
\omega
is cyclic for
M_{A}^{\prime}
and thus separating for
M_{A}
. It follows that the vector state
\varphi_{A}
on
M_{A}
given by
\varphi_{A}(x)=\langle x\omega,\omega\rangle
is faithful. By construction,
\varphi_{A}(\pi(x))=\varphi(x),\,(x\in M_{0})
.

We next claim that
\pi(M_{0})\subseteq\mathcal{B}(H)
is a good subalgebra of
M_{A}
, whence, by Theorem
4.3
,
\pi(M_{0})=(M_{A})_{\operatorname{tb}}
. By definition,
\pi(M_{0})
is a strongly dense
*
-subalgebra of
M_{A}
. By axiom
(A5)
, if
a\in S_{n}(A)
, then the left multiplication by
a

and the right multiplication by both
a
and
a^{*}
induce bounded
operators on
H
of norm at most
n
. Thus

\pi(a)\in S_{n}(M_{A},\varphi_{A}).

Since every element of
M_{0}
belongs to some sort, it follows that
\pi(M_{0})\subseteq(M_{A})_{\operatorname{tb}}.

Moreover, if
a\in S_{n}(A)
, then
\pi(a)\in S_{n}(\pi(M_{0}))
.
We first verify that, for every
n\geq 2
,

(6.2)

\iota_{n}(S_{1}(A))=\{x\in S_{n}(A):\pi(x)\in S_{1}(M_{A},\varphi_{A})\}.

The inclusion
\subseteq
follows from axiom
(A6)
. Conversely, suppose that
x\in S_{n}(A)
and

\pi(x)\in S_{1}(M_{A},\varphi_{A})
. Then
\operatorname{TB}_{k,n}(x)\leq 1
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence
\Phi_{n,N}(x)=0
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Axiom
(A7)
therefore gives

\operatorname{dist}_{d_{n}}\bigl(x,\iota_{n}(S_{1}(A))\bigr)\leq\alpha_{n}(2^{-N})\qquad(N\geq 1).

Since
\alpha_{n}
is continuous and
\alpha_{n}(0)=0
, letting

N\to\infty
yields

\operatorname{dist}_{d_{n}}\bigl(x,\iota_{n}(S_{1}(A))\bigr)=0.

By Axiom
(A6)
,
\iota_{n}
is an isometry, and since

S_{1}(A)
is complete, its image is closed in
S_{n}(A)
. Consequently
there exists
y\in S_{1}(A)
such that
x=\iota_{n}(y)
. This proves (
6.2
). Now suppose that
(\pi(x_{m}))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a

\|\cdot\|_{\varphi_{A}}^{\#}
-Cauchy sequence in

S_{1}(\pi(M_{0}))
.
For each
m
, choose
n_{m}\geq 2
such that
x_{m}\in S_{n_{m}}(A)
.
By (
6.2
), there is
y_{m}\in S_{1}(A)

such that
x_{m}=\iota_{1,n_{m}}(y_{m})
.
Since the inclusion maps are compatible isometries, the metric on the direct limit restricts to the original metric on
S_{1}(A)
. Hence
(y_{m})_{m}
is Cauchy in
S_{1}(A)
. By completeness of
S_{1}(A)
, it converges to some

y\in S_{1}(A)
, and hence
(\pi(x_{m}))_{m}
converges to
\pi(y)
in

S_{1}(\pi(M_{0}))
.

Now we identify the interpretations of
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{Q}
, and
\mathbf{R}
with the corresponding Rieffel–Van Daele operators for

(M_{A},\varphi_{A})
. Fix
x\in S_{n}(A)
, and let
p\in M_{0}
denote
the element represented by
\mathbf{P}_{n}(x)
. By
Axiom
(A8)
,
p=p^{*}
, and, for every

z\in S_{1}(A)
,

\Re\varphi_{A}\bigl((\pi(z)+\pi(z^{*}))(\pi(x)-\pi(p))\bigr)=\Re\langle(\pi(x)-\pi(p))\omega,(\pi(z)+\pi(z^{*}))\omega\rangle=0.

We claim that the real linear span of
\{(\pi(z)+\pi(z^{*}))\omega:z\in S_{1}(A)\}
is dense in
\overline{(M_{A})_{\mathrm{sa}}\omega}
.
Indeed, if
a=a^{*}\in M_{0}
, choose
n\geq 2
such that
a\in S_{n}(A)
.
Then
\pi(a/n)
is totally
1
-bounded.
By
(
6.2
), there is
z\in S_{1}(A)
such that
\iota_{n}(z)=a/n
.
Since
\iota_{n}
preserves adjoints and is injective,
z=z^{*}

Consequently,

Expanding the language by the modular automorphism group

In this section, we give a concrete effective expansion of

These approximations will also show that the modular automorphisms

-definable.

To obtain an effective presentation, we add symbols at rational
times, on each named sort. We construct the polynomial

.
Proposition

then recovers all

Let
(M,\varphi)
be a
W^{*}
-probability space,
H_{\varphi}=L^{2}(M,\varphi)
be the
GNS Hilbert space, and
\omega_{\varphi}=\widehat{1}
.
Recall that

is a bounded operator satisfying

. Recall also that

is recovered from

by
\Delta_{\varphi}=R_{\varphi}^{-1}(2-R_{\varphi})

Because

may contain

and

or

uniformly on

Fix
K\geq 1
and

. Then

\Delta_{\varphi}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}x\omega_{\varphi}=\sigma_{\mp\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\omega_{\varphi}
, and therefore

(7.1)

\|x\|_{\varphi}\leq K,\qquad\|\Delta_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{2}}x\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq K,\qquad\|\Delta_{\varphi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\omega_{\varphi}\|\leq K.

We will frequently use the above inequalities.

Lemma 7.1
.

Fix
K\geq 1,\,\,L>0
and
x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
. Then

\left\|\bigl(1-1_{[e^{-L},e^{L}]}(\Delta_{\varphi})\bigr)x\omega_{\varphi}\right\|\leq\sqrt{2}\,Ke^{-L/2}.

The same estimate holds with

replaced by
x^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
E(\cdot)
be the spectral measure associated with
\Delta_{\varphi}
.
Then by (
7.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|1_{(e^{L},\infty)}(\Delta_{\varphi})x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{(e^{L},\infty)}d\|E(t)x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(e^{L},\infty)}te^{-L}d\|E(t)x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-L}\|\Delta_{\varphi}^{\frac{1}{2}}x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}e^{-L},

and similarly,

\displaystyle\|1_{(0,e^{-L})}(\Delta_{\varphi})x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(0,e^{-L})}t^{-1}e^{-L}d\|E(t)x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-L}\|\Delta_{\varphi}^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\omega_{\varphi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq K^{2}e^{-L}.

Therefore, we obtain (noting that

is not an eigenvalue of
\Delta_{\varphi}
)

\left\|\bigl(1-1_{[e^{-L},e^{L}]}(\Delta_{\varphi})\bigr)x\omega_{\varphi}\right\|^{2}\leq 2K^{2}e^{-L}.

The proof for

then follows, because

holds.
∎

(2)

Lemma 7.4
.

Let

be conjugate-symmetric on
[0,2]
.
Then for every

Proof.

By the Weierstrass approximation theorem and the density of

, we may choose

with

Define

∎

For

, write

for the unique element of

More generally, for a polynomial

Lemma 7.5
.

Suppose that
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]
is conjugate-symmetric on
[0,2]
.
Then for every
x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
, we have

(p(R)x)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=p(R)x^{*}\omega_{\varphi}.

Proof.

Let
S_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}
be the Tomita operator. Since

R_{\varphi}=2(1+\Delta_{\varphi})^{-1},

we have

J_{\varphi}R_{\varphi}J_{\varphi}=2(1+\Delta_{\varphi}^{-1})^{-1}=2\Delta_{\varphi}(1+\Delta_{\varphi})^{-1}=2-R_{\varphi}.

Moreover
R_{\varphi}
commutes with
\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}

, we have

S_{\varphi}R_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}R_{\varphi}=J_{\varphi}R_{\varphi}\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}=(2-R_{\varphi})J_{\varphi}\Delta_{\varphi}^{1/2}=(2-R_{\varphi})S_{\varphi}.

Since
x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
, the vector
R_{\varphi}x\omega_{\varphi}
is represented
by the bounded element

(Rx)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=S_{\varphi}(Rx)\omega_{\varphi}=S_{\varphi}R_{\varphi}x\omega_{\varphi}=(2-R_{\varphi})S_{\varphi}x\omega_{\varphi}=(2-R_{\varphi})x^{*}\omega_{\varphi}.

Iterating this identity gives

(R^{j}x)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=(2-R_{\varphi})^{j}x^{*}\omega_{\varphi}.

Now write
p(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}X^{j}
.
Then

\displaystyle(p(R)x)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}R^{j}x\right)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\overline{a_{j}}(R^{j}x)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{d}\overline{a_{j}}(2-R_{\varphi})^{j}x^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=\overline{p}(2-R_{\varphi})x^{*}\omega_{\varphi}.

denotes the polynomial obtained by conjugating the

is conjugate-symmetric on

for every

\overline{p}(2-R_{\varphi})=p(R_{\varphi}).

Consequently,

(p(R)x)^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=p(R_{\varphi})x^{*}\omega_{\varphi}=(p(R)x^{*})\omega_{\varphi}.\qed

Proposition 7.6

,

Consequently, viewing

\|\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(x)-p(R)x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}<\varepsilon.

Proof.

Choose

Let

be the conjugate-symmetric continuous function on

Remark 7.9

We note that the set of axioms

8.
Axiomatizable and local classes

In this section, we determine whether or not natural classes of
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces are axiomatizable or local (defined below). A similar endeavour for the class of tracial von Neumann algebras was undertaken in
[

]
.

We begin with a series of negative results; these results were also mentioned by Dabrowski in
[
13
, Section 2.3]
in his language, but we offer a few details here for the convenience of the reader.

The following is
[
3
, Proposition 6.3]
:

Fact 8.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
.
There is a family
\varphi_{n}
of faithful normal states on the hyperfinite II
1
factor
\mathcal{R}
such that
\prod_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\mathcal{R},\varphi_{n})
is
not
semifinite.

In what follows, given a property

when referring to the class of pairs
\mathrm{W}^{*}
-probability spaces
(M,\varphi)
where

Corollary 8.2
.

The following classes are not elementary classes: finite algebras, semifinite algebras,
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factors.

Proposition 8.3
.

The class of
\mathrm{II}_{\infty}

Proof.

Let
M
be a
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor with unique faithful normal tracial state

\tau

\mathrm{II}_{\infty}
-factor

(e_{i})_{i\geq 1}
be the

\ell^{2}

\lambda_{n,i}>0

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n,i}=1

\lambda_{n,1}\to 1

Define a faithful normal state
\psi_{n}
on
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2})

\psi_{n}(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n,i}\langle Te_{i},e_{i}\rangle,

and set
\varphi_{n}:=\tau\otimes\psi_{n}

Fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
and put

(\mathcal{N},\varphi):=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(N,\varphi_{n})
. Let
p:=1\otimes e_{11}\in N
. Note that
e_{11}\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2})_{\psi_{n}}
, so
p\in N_{\varphi_{n}}
for every

. Therefore, the class of the constant sequence
p
is in
\mathcal{N}_{\varphi}
. Since
\|1-p\|_{\varphi_{n}}^{\#}=\sqrt{\varphi_{n}(1-p)}\to 0
, we have
1-p=0
in
\mathcal{N}
. Therefore,
p=1_{\mathcal{N}}
.
Consequently, every element of
\mathcal{N}
is equal to its
p
-corner:
\mathcal{N}=p\mathcal{N}p
.
Define
\Theta:\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N},M)\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}

by
\Theta((x_{n})_{n})=(x_{n}\otimes e_{11})_{\mathcal{U}}
. For every bounded sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
, we have

\|x_{n}\otimes e_{11}\|_{\varphi_{n}}^{\#}=\lambda_{n,1}^{1/2}\|x_{n}\|_{2,\tau}.

Since
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\lambda_{n,1}=1
,
it follows that

\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\|x_{n}\otimes e_{11}\|_{\varphi_{n}}^{\#}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\|x_{n}\|_{2,\tau}=0.

Thus
\Theta
induces an injective

-homomorphism

\widetilde{\Theta}:(M,\tau)^{\mathcal{U}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}
. If
x=(a_{n})_{\mathcal{U}}\in\mathcal{N}

x=pxp=(pa_{n}p)_{\mathcal{U}}

there is a unique

such that

and

\|x_{n}\|=\|pa_{n}p\|\leq\|a_{n}\|,

\mathcal{N}\cong(M,\tau)^{\mathcal{U}}
is a
\mathrm{II}_{1}

Since an axiomatizable class is closed under ultraproducts, the class of

\mathrm{II}_{\infty}

∎

A.1.
Outline of the proof

Here we explain the idea of an alternative proof. The obstruction to proving Theorem

Fix

In order to relate the estimate of

From this viewpoint, we formulate the

lifting problem of the orbit

to an orbit in the Ocneanu
multiplier

z\mapsto(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{z}(x_{i}))_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}:=\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i}).

Below we give the main ideas of the structure of the proof of the definability of the
K
-bounded elements in
a somewhat detailed form.

Step 1: Metric
H^{\infty}
-lifting for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-quotients via Schur’s algorithm.
As a preliminary, we show (Theorem
A.1
) that given a surjective
*
-homomorphism
q\colon A\to B
of unital
C^{*}
-algebras, an element
f
in the Banach algebra
H^{\infty}(\Omega,B)
of bounded holomorphic functions from a simply connected proper domain
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
to
B
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is
F\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,A)
such that
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon
and

q(F(z))=f(z),\qquad z\in\Omega.

We note that the crucial point is to prove the theorem for the open disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|<1\}
; the simply connected proper domain
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
case follows by Riemann’s mapping theorem.
Also, the key ingredient in the proof of this theorem has been well-known in the operator theory literature as the operator-valued Schur’s algorithm, which is used to recover an
H^{\infty}
-function from its Schur parameters, and what we do here is an adaptation of Schur’s algorithm to the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic setting while keeping track of the norm estimate. See
\lx@sectionsign
A.2
for historical background on the operator-valued Schur’s algorithm. For the later application, we will choose
\Omega
to be a horizontal open strip

\mathbb{S}_{\rho}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\ :\ |\operatorname{Im}z|<\rho\}.

In this case, an explicit biholomorphic mapping between
\mathbb{D}
and
\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
exists (see Remark
A.7
).

Step 2: Isometric
H^{\infty}
-lifting for the Ocneanu multiplier
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
.
Using the
H^{\infty}
-lifting theorem from Step 1 together with a somewhat
long diagonal argument (in the case of countably incomplete ultrafilter; we do not need this
part for the countably complete case as the situation is much simpler), we then show
(Theorem
A.8
) that if
(M,\varphi)=\prod_{\mathcal{U}}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})

is the Ocneanu ultraproduct, then for each
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},M)
, there is

F\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}})
such that

\|F\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty}
and
q\circ F=f
, where
q\colon\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}\to M

is the canonical quotient map. Strictly speaking, we do not need this exact isometric lifting, but we present the result as it may be of independent interest.

Step 3: First Gaussian regularization.
Here, we start the proof of the definability of
S_{K}
. Given

x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
, we do a Gaussian smoothing to approximate
x

by
\sigma^{\varphi}
-analytic element
x_{s}=\sigma^{\varphi}_{k_{s}}(x)
.
This allows us to define the analytic
extension
z\mapsto\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x_{s})
to a slightly larger open strip

\mathbb{S}_{1/2+s}
(
s>0
) than the closed strip
\overline{\mathbb{S}_{1/2}}
. Choosing

s>0
sufficiently small, we ensure that
\|F_{s}\|_{\infty}\leq e^{s/4}K
is sufficiently
close to
K
. Now consider the analytic map
F_{s}:\mathbb{S}_{1/2+s}\to M
given by

F_{s}(z)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x_{s}).

We use Step 2 to find an isometric lift

G_{s}=(G_{s,i})_{i}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{1/2+s},\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}),\quad q\circ G_{s}=F_{s}.

Step 4: The second Gaussian regularization and Gaussian averaging.
Now,
F_{s}
satisfies the
\sigma^{\varphi}
-equivariance

\sigma^{\varphi}_{t}(F_{s}(z))=F_{s}(z+t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\;z\in\mathbb{S}_{1/2+s}.

Unfortunately, there is no reason to believe that the lifted elements
G_{i}
are

\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}
-equivariant as well. Now consider the Gaussian weight

\gamma_{a}(u)=\Bigl(\frac{a}{\pi}\Bigr)^{1/2}e^{-au^{2}},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

We may average the orbit
u\mapsto\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u}(G_{i}(u+z))
with integrable
Gaussian weight to obtain a new function

\tilde{G}_{i}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z)\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u}(G_{i}(u+z))\,du,

which is expected to be
\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}
-equivariant:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{i}(t+z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z+t)\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u}(G_{i}(u+z+t))\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(v+z)\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-(v-t)}(G_{i}(v+z))\,dv

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{t}(\tilde{G}_{i}(z)).

However, the map

u\mapsto\bigl(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u}(G_{i}(u+z))\bigr)_{i}

need not be norm-continuous as a map into the Ocneanu multiplier algebra
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}
,
because the modular groups
\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}
need not be uniformly norm-continuous in
i
.
This makes it impossible to guarantee that the resulting map be norm-holomorphic in the open
strip. To overcome this issue, we insert (with a sufficiently small parameter
\tau>0
) a
preliminary (the second) Gaussian smoothing
\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{k_{\tau}}
to obtain this missing
norm-continuity. After this Gaussian smoothing, one can rewrite

\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u}\!\Bigl(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{k_{\tau}}(G_{i}(u+z))\Bigr)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(v+u)\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{v}(G_{i}(u+z))\,dv.

Consequently, for
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\bigl(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u}\!\bigl(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{k_{\tau}}(

\displaystyle G_{i}(u+z))\bigr)\bigr)_{i}-\bigl(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{-u^{\prime}}\!\bigl(\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{k_{\tau}}(G_{i}(u^{\prime}+z))\bigr)\bigr)_{i}\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|_{\infty}\|k_{\tau}(\cdot+u)-k_{\tau}(\cdot+u^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|G(u+z)-G(u^{\prime}+z)\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

The right-hand side tends to
0
as
u^{\prime}\to u
.
Thus the smoothed covariance integrand is norm-continuous and uniformly bounded; after multiplication by
\gamma_{a}(u+z)
, it is Bochner integrable in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}
.
The resulting
Gaussian average is then a well-defined element of
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
, and gives exact
coordinatewise equivariance.

Step 5: The final diagonalization.
In Step 4, because of the preliminary Gaussian smoothing, the resulting
covariant map is an exact lift of
\sigma^{\varphi}_{k_{\tau}}(F)
; however, it is only an
approximate lift of
F
. The error term can be arbitrarily small by choosing smaller and
smaller
s
and
\tau
.

By choosing smaller and smaller parameters
s
and
\tau
, we do once again a diagonal
argument along
\mathcal{U}
to obtain a desired lifting
(x_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}

of
x
with
x_{i}\in S_{K}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
, and the proof is complete.

Therefore, although we do Gaussian smoothing several times during the proof,
none of these smoothing steps is used directly to obtain the desired
coordinatewise total
K
-bound; as was explained in the above, there is a trade-off. We must choose only one of

x_{i}\sim\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{k_{\tau}}(x_{i})
by choosing
\tau\sim 0
, or the control of

\|\sigma^{\varphi_{i}}_{k_{\tau}}(x_{i})\|_{\mathrm{tb}}
by choosing
\tau\to\infty
, but not both. Instead, the smoothing/averaging are used (i) to enlarge the domain of holomorphy (first
Gaussian smoothing); (ii) to ensure the norm-continuity of the averaging process (second
preliminary Gaussian smoothing); and then (iii) to restore the modular equivariance that we
lost when we take an isometric lifting of the modular orbit
z\mapsto F_{s}(z)=\sigma^{\varphi}_{z}(x_{s})

(the third Gaussian average). During the diagonalization process, we need to take care of
the modular-theoretic properties of
G_{i}
so that we define an element in the Ocneanu
multiplier
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{U}}
.

In the subsequent discussions, we will use standard facts about Banach-space-valued holomorphic functions;
see, for example,
[
4
, Appendix A]
. We will also freely use the
fact that the usual scalar arguments apply after scalarization by
functionals in the dual space. In particular, for a Banach space
X
, the
X
-valued versions of Schwarz’s lemma, the identity theorem, and the three-lines theorem follow from their scalar counterparts by applying them to

x^{*}\circ f
,
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, and then using the Hahn–Banach theorem. The
corresponding Cauchy and Morera type theorems, as well as holomorphy under
Bochner integrations, can be obtained in the same way.

A.2.
Operator-valued Schur parametrization and metric
H^{\infty}
lifting for
C^{*}
-quotients

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
. For a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
, we denote its open unit ball by
\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
. More generally, for each nonempty domain (i.e., an open connected subset)
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
, we write
H^{\infty}(\Omega,A)
for the Banach algebra of bounded norm-holomorphic functions
\Omega\to A
, with norm

\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{\infty}=\sup_{z\in\Omega}\left\lVert F(z)\right\rVert.

We also use the notation

\mathscr{S}(A)=\left\{f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},A):\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\},\qquad\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)=\{f\in\mathscr{S}(A):\|f\|_{\infty}<1\}.

\mathscr{S}(A)
(resp.
\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
) is the space of
A
-valued Schur class
(resp.
strict Schur class
) functions.

As a first step towards the definability result, we prove a holomorphic almost-isometric lifting result for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-quotients.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras and let
q:A\to B
be a surjective unital
*
-homomorphism. Let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected proper domain. Then for every
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,B)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
F\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,A)

such that
q\circ F=f
and
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}+\epsilon
.
Consequently, the induced map
q_{*}:H^{\infty}(\Omega,A)\longrightarrow H^{\infty}(\Omega,B)
is a metric quotient map.

The proof uses a
C^{*}
-algebraic version of Schur’s algorithm
[
30
,
31
]
, which we now establish. The fractional-linear transformations used in the construction are closely related to several classical works in operator theory, including Potapov’s multiplicative factorization theory
for
J
-contractive matrix functions
[
26
]
, Harris’s Möbius transformations in Banach
∗
-algebras
[
22
]
, and Shmul’yan’s fractional-linear transformations of operator balls

[
32
]
. For systematic treatments of
operator-valued Schur algorithms and Schur parametrizations, see
Constantinescu
[
12
]
,
Bakonyi–Constantinescu
[
7
]
, and
Arlinskiĭ
[
5
]
.
We give the argument here in a self-contained form in order to keep track of the strict norm estimates needed below.

Recall that for
\gamma\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
, the
defect operators
are defined by

D_{\gamma}=(1-\gamma^{*}\gamma)^{1/2},\qquad D_{\gamma^{*}}=(1-\gamma\gamma^{*})^{1/2}.

The operators
D_{\gamma}
and
D_{\gamma^{*}}
are invertible. We then define maps
\Psi_{\gamma}
,
\Theta_{\gamma}:\operatorname{Ball}(A)\to A
by

(A.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\gamma}(w)

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma^{*}}^{-1}(w-\gamma)(1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\Theta_{\gamma}(v)

\displaystyle=\gamma+D_{\gamma^{*}}v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

We start by establishing some well-known facts about operator-valued fractional linear transformations. We include a self-contained proof in
order to keep track of the strict norm estimate needed below.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and fix

\gamma\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
.
Then:

(1)

The following defect identities hold.

(A.3)

\displaystyle 1-\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma}(1-w^{*}\gamma)^{-1}(1-w^{*}w)(1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

(A.4)

\displaystyle 1-\Theta_{\gamma}(v)^{*}\Theta_{\gamma}(v)

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma}(1+v^{*}\gamma)^{-1}(1-v^{*}v)(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

In particular, both
\Psi_{\gamma}
and
\Theta_{\gamma}
map
\operatorname{Ball}(A)
to itself.
Moreover, for each
0<r<1
, there exists a constant
0<c=c_{a,r}<1
depending only on
r
and
a=\|\gamma\|<1
such that, if
\|w\|\leq r
(resp.
\|v\|\leq r
), then

\|\Psi_{\gamma}(w)\|\leq c
(resp.
\|\Theta_{\gamma}(v)\|\leq c
) holds.

(2)

The maps
\Psi_{\gamma}
and
\Theta_{\gamma}
are mutually inverse:

\Psi_{\gamma}\circ\Theta_{\gamma}=\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Ball}(A)},\qquad\Theta_{\gamma}\circ\Psi_{\gamma}=\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{Ball}(A)}.

(3)

For
v,w\in\operatorname{Ball}(A)
,

\Theta_{\gamma}(v)-\Theta_{\gamma}(w)=D_{\gamma^{*}}(1+v\gamma^{*})^{-1}(v-w)(1+\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

Proof.

(1) First, we show (
A.3
), which is a non-commutative analogue of

1-\left|\frac{w-\gamma}{1-\overline{\gamma}w}\right|^{2}=\frac{(1-|\gamma|^{2})(1-|w|^{2})}{|1-\overline{\gamma}w|^{2}}.

Set
P=D_{\gamma}^{-2}
and

Q=D_{\gamma^{*}}^{-2}.
For
w\in\operatorname{Ball}(A)
, we have

\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)=D_{\gamma}(1-w^{*}\gamma)^{-1}(w^{*}-\gamma^{*})Q(w-\gamma)(1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

On the other hand,

1=D_{\gamma}(1-w^{*}\gamma)^{-1}\bigl[(1-w^{*}\gamma)P(1-\gamma^{*}w)\bigr](1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

Therefore

(A.5)

\displaystyle 1-\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma}(1-w^{*}\gamma)^{-1}\Bigl[(1-w^{*}\gamma)P(1-\gamma^{*}w)

\displaystyle-(w^{*}-\gamma^{*})Q(w-\gamma)\Bigr](1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

Expanding the middle bracket in the right hand side of (
A.5
) gives

\displaystyle(1-w^{*}\gamma)P(1-\gamma^{*}w)-(w^{*}-\gamma^{*})Q(w-\gamma)

\displaystyle=P-P\gamma^{*}w-w^{*}\gamma P+w^{*}\gamma P\gamma^{*}w-w^{*}Qw+w^{*}Q\gamma+\gamma^{*}Qw-\gamma^{*}Q\gamma.

We then use the elementary identities

P\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{*}Q,\qquad\gamma P=Q\gamma,\qquad P=1+\gamma^{*}Q\gamma,\qquad Q=1+\gamma P\gamma^{*}.

The first two identities imply cancellation of the mixed terms:

-P\gamma^{*}w+\gamma^{*}Qw=0,\qquad-w^{*}\gamma P+w^{*}Q\gamma=0.

Thus, the middle bracket equals

P-\gamma^{*}Q\gamma+w^{*}(\gamma P\gamma^{*}-Q)w.

Using
P-\gamma^{*}Q\gamma=1
and
\gamma P\gamma^{*}-Q=-1,
we obtain

(1-w^{*}\gamma)P(1-\gamma^{*}w)-(w^{*}-\gamma^{*})Q(w-\gamma)=1-w^{*}w.

Consequently,

1-\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)=D_{\gamma}(1-w^{*}\gamma)^{-1}(1-w^{*}w)(1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

Set
S_{w}=(1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}
. Now assume that
\|w\|\leq r
and set
a:=\|\gamma\|<1
. Since
w^{*}w\leq r^{2}
, we obtain

1-\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)=S_{w}^{*}(1-w^{*}w)S_{w}\geq(1-r^{2})S_{w}^{*}S_{w}.

Moreover,
S_{w}^{-1}=D_{\gamma}^{-1}(1-\gamma^{*}w),

and hence
\|S_{w}^{-1}\|\leq(1-a^{2})^{-1/2}(1+ar).

Therefore

S_{w}^{*}S_{w}\geq\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1+ar)^{2}}1.

Consequently,

1-\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)\geq\rho_{a,r}\,1,\qquad\rho_{a,r}:=(1-r^{2})\frac{1-a^{2}}{(1+ar)^{2}}>0.

Thus
\Psi_{\gamma}(w)^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)\leq(1-\rho_{a,r})1,

and

\|\Psi_{\gamma}(w)\|\leq c_{a,r},\qquad c_{a,r}:=\sqrt{1-\rho_{a,r}}<1.

This shows the claims for
\Psi_{\gamma}
.
The defect identity (
A.4
) for
\Theta_{\gamma}
can be shown similarly. Here, we give an alternative argument. We first note that
\Theta_{\gamma}(v)=\Psi_{-\gamma}(v)
.
Indeed, using
D_{\gamma^{*}}\gamma=\gamma D_{\gamma}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle D_{\gamma^{*}}\Theta_{\gamma}(v)

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma^{*}}\gamma+D_{\gamma^{*}}^{2}v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\gamma D_{\gamma}+(1-\gamma\gamma^{*})v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=(v+\gamma)(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

Thus

\Theta_{\gamma}(v)=D_{\gamma^{*}}^{-1}(v+\gamma)(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}=\Psi_{-\gamma}(v).

Applying (
A.3
) with
-\gamma
in place of
\gamma

therefore gives

1-\Theta_{\gamma}(v)^{*}\Theta_{\gamma}(v)=D_{\gamma}(1+v^{*}\gamma)^{-1}(1-v^{*}v)(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma},

which is (
A.4
). The corresponding strict norm
estimate
\|v\|\leq r\implies\|\Theta_{\gamma}(v)\|\leq c_{a,r}
follows from the estimate already proved for
\Psi_{\gamma}
, again with
-\gamma
in place of
\gamma
, since

\|-\gamma\|=\|\gamma\|
.
We also observe that the defect identities themselves imply that both
\Psi_{\gamma}
and
\Theta_{\gamma}
map
\operatorname{Ball}(A)
to itself.

(2) Suppose that
w=\Theta_{\gamma}(v),\,\,v\in\operatorname{Ball}(A).

Since
\gamma^{*}D_{\gamma^{*}}=D_{\gamma}\gamma^{*},
we have

\displaystyle 1-\gamma^{*}w

\displaystyle=1-\gamma^{*}\gamma-\gamma^{*}D_{\gamma^{*}}v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma}^{2}-D_{\gamma}\gamma^{*}v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma}\left[1-\gamma^{*}v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}\right]D_{\gamma}.

Putting
X=\gamma^{*}v
, we have
1-X(1+X)^{-1}=(1+X)^{-1}.

Therefore
1-\gamma^{*}w=D_{\gamma}(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\Psi_{\gamma}(w)

\displaystyle=D_{\gamma^{*}}^{-1}D_{\gamma^{*}}v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}\bigl(D_{\gamma}(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}D_{\gamma}\bigr)^{-1}D_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=v.

The identity
\Theta_{\gamma}(\Psi_{\gamma}(w))=w
follows similarly, using

1+\gamma^{*}\Psi_{\gamma}(w)=D_{\gamma}(1-\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}D_{\gamma}.

(3) Using
v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}=(1+v\gamma^{*})^{-1}v,
we obtain

\displaystyle v(1+\gamma^{*}v)^{-1}-w(1+\gamma^{*}w)^{-1}=(1+v\gamma^{*})^{-1}(v-w)(1+\gamma^{*}w)^{-1},

which proves (3).
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and take
f\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
. Put
\gamma=f(0)
. Then the
first step Schur function

f_{1}\colon\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}\to A
of
f
given by

f_{1}(z)=z^{-1}D_{\gamma^{*}}^{-1}(f(z)-\gamma)(1-\gamma^{*}f(z))^{-1}D_{\gamma}

has a removable singularity at
0
and thus extends to a function
f_{1}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},A)
, which further satisfies
\|{f_{1}}\|_{\infty}<1
, that is
f_{1}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
.
Conversely, if
g\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
with
\|{g}\|_{\infty}<1
and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
, then the map
\Phi_{\gamma}(g):\mathbb{D}\to A
given by

\Phi_{\gamma}(g)(z)=\gamma+D_{\gamma^{*}}\,zg(z)(1+\gamma^{*}zg(z))^{-1}D_{\gamma}

belongs to
\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
. Moreover,
f
can be recovered from
f_{1}
by the formula

\Phi_{\gamma}(f_{1})=f.

Proof of Lemma
A.3
.

If
f=0
, choose any
0<r<1
. If
f\neq 0
, set
r:=\|f\|_{\infty}<1
. Then consider the map
g:\mathbb{D}\to A
given by
g(z)=\Psi_{\gamma}(f(z)).

Then
g\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},A)
; by Lemma
A.2
(1), there exists a constant
0<c=c_{a,r}<1
depending only on
0<r<1
and
a=\|\gamma\|<1
such that

\|g\|_{\infty}\leq c<1.
Note that since
g
is holomorphic and
g(0)=0
, its Taylor series at
z=0
has no constant term, and thus it is of the form
g(z)=zh(z)
for some holomorphic function
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to A
. In particular, the function
f_{1}:\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}\to A
given by
f_{1}(z):=z^{-1}g(z)(=h(z))
has a removable singularity at
0
. By the Banach-space-valued Schwarz lemma, we have
\|g(z)\|\leq c|z|
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}.
(Indeed, this follows by applying the usual scalar-valued Schwarz lemma to
\mathbb{D}\ni z\mapsto c^{-1}\varphi(g(z))\in\mathbb{C}

for each
\varphi\in A^{*}
with
\|\varphi\|=1
, and then taking the supremum over such
\varphi
.) Therefore,

\|f_{1}(z)\|=\left\|z^{-1}g(z)\right\|\leq c

for
z\neq 0

and the same estimate holds at
0
by continuity. Hence
f_{1}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
.
Conversely, take
g\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)

and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
. Set
b:=\|\gamma\|<1
.
Consider the map
\Phi_{\gamma}(g):\mathbb{D}\to A
defined above, that is,
\Phi_{\gamma}(g)(z)=\Theta_{\gamma}(zg(z))
.
Set
\rho:=\|g\|_{\infty}<1
. If
g=0
, then
\Phi_{\gamma}(g)(z)=\gamma
and
\Phi_{\gamma}(g)\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
holds. Assume
g\neq 0
, so that
0<\rho<1
.
Then by Lemma
A.2
(1), there exists a constant
0<c^{\prime}=c_{\rho,b}<1
such that for every
v\in A
with
\|v\|\leq\rho
,
\|\Theta_{\gamma}(v)\|\leq c^{\prime}
holds. Therefore, we have
\Phi_{\gamma}(g)\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
since
\|\Phi_{\gamma}(g)\|_{\infty}\leq c^{\prime}<1
.

Fix now
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
zf_{1}(z)=\Psi_{\gamma}(f(z)),

we have, by Lemma
A.2
(2), that

\Phi_{\gamma}(f_{1})(z)=\Theta_{\gamma}(zf_{1}(z))=\Theta_{\gamma}(\Psi_{\gamma}(f(z)))=f(z).

By continuity, the same equality holds at
z=0
.
∎

Proposition A.4
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let

\gamma\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

For
g\in\mathscr{S}(A)
, define
\Phi_{\gamma}(g):\mathbb{D}\to A
by

\Phi_{\gamma}(g)(z)=\Theta_{\gamma}(zg(z)).

Then
\Phi_{\gamma}(g)\in\mathscr{S}(A).

Moreover,
\|\Phi_{\gamma}(g)\|_{\infty}<1
if
\|g\|_{\infty}<1
.
If
g,h\in\mathscr{S}(A)
satisfy
g(z)-h(z)=O(z^{m})
at
z=0
, then

\Phi_{\gamma}(g)(z)-\Phi_{\gamma}(h)(z)=O(z^{m+1}).

(2)

Let
q:A\to B
be a surjective unital
*
-homomorphism. If

\widetilde{\gamma}\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
,

\gamma=q(\widetilde{\gamma})
, and

\widetilde{g}\in\mathscr{S}(A)
, then

q\circ\Phi_{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\widetilde{g})=\Phi_{\gamma}(q\circ\widetilde{g}).

Proof.

The second statement of (1) was already established in
Lemma
A.3
, and the first statement is proven analogously.
To prove the last statement in (1), note that, if
g-h=O(z^{m})
, then
zg(z)-zh(z)=O(z^{m+1}),

and from the formula (
A.2
), it follows that

\Phi_{\gamma}(g)-\Phi_{\gamma}(h)=O(z^{m+1}).

(2) The functional calculus and inversion commute with
q
:

q(D_{\widetilde{\gamma}})=D_{\gamma},\qquad q(D_{\widetilde{\gamma}^{*}})=D_{\gamma^{*}}.

Applying
q
to the defining formula for

\Phi_{\widetilde{\gamma}}
proves the claim.
∎

Definition A.5
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and let
f\in\mathscr{S}(A)
.
A sequence
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}\subseteq\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)

is called a
Schur parameter sequence
for
f
if there exist functions
f_{n}\in\mathscr{S}(A)
,
n\geq 0
, such that

f_{0}=f,\qquad f_{n}(0)=\gamma_{n},\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{n}=\Phi_{\gamma_{n}}(f_{n+1}).

If moreover
f_{n}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
for every
n\geq 0
,
then
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is called a

strict Schur parameter sequence
for
f
.

Below, we refer to
\gamma_{n}
simply as the (strict)
Schur parameters
of
f
. If
f_{n}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
and

\gamma_{n}=f_{n}(0)
, then Lemma
A.3
shows that the usual Schur transform
f_{n+1}
belongs to

\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
, and Proposition
A.4

gives
f_{n}=\Phi_{\gamma_{n}}(f_{n+1})
.
Thus, the usual Schur algorithm for a strict Schur-class function
produces a strict Schur parameter sequence in the sense above.

The next result is a
C^{*}
-algebraic Schur parametrization.

Proposition A.6
.

Let
A
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and take

f\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
.
Recursively define

f_{0}=f,\qquad\gamma_{n}=f_{n}(0),

and

f_{n+1}(z)=z^{-1}D_{\gamma_{n}^{*}}^{-1}\bigl(f_{n}(z)-\gamma_{n}\bigr)\bigl(1-\gamma_{n}^{*}f_{n}(z)\bigr)^{-1}D_{\gamma_{n}},\qquad z\neq 0,

with the removable singularity at
z=0
.
Then the following statements hold:

(1)

For every
n\geq 0
,
f_{n}\in\mathscr{S}_{0}(A)
,
and hence
\|\gamma_{n}\|<1
. In particular,

(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a strict Schur parameter sequence for
f
.

(2)

f_{n}=\Phi_{\gamma_{n}}(f_{n+1})
for every
n\geq 0
.

(3)

For
N\geq 0
, define the
N
-th Schur truncation
recursively by

f_{N+1}^{[N]}=0,\qquad f_{n}^{[N]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{n}}(f_{n+1}^{[N]})\quad(n=N,\dots,0),

and set
f^{[N]}:=f_{0}^{[N]}.

Then
\|f^{[N]}\|_{\infty}\leq 1

and both
f^{[N]}
and
f
have the same Taylor coefficients up to
degree
N
. In particular, for every
0<r<1
,

\sup_{|z|\leq r}\|f(z)-f^{[N]}(z)\|\leq\frac{2r^{N+1}}{1-r}.

Consequently,
f^{[N]}\longrightarrow f
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.

(4)

Conversely, let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}\subseteq\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)

be an arbitrary sequence. Then the canonical Schur truncations
defined above converge uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to a unique function
f\in\mathscr{S}(A)
for which

(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is a Schur parameter sequence.

Proof.

(1) is immediate from Lemma
A.3
. (2) follows from the last part of Lemma
A.3
.

(3)
Fix
N
. Iterating (2), we have

f=\Phi_{\gamma_{0}}\bigl(\Phi_{\gamma_{1}}(\cdots\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}(f_{N+1})\cdots)\bigr),

whereas

f^{[N]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{0}}\bigl(\Phi_{\gamma_{1}}(\cdots\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}(0)\cdots)\bigr).

At level
N+1
, the two tails
f_{N+1}
and
0
differ by
O(1)
.
By Proposition
A.4
, applying

\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}
raises the order of vanishing by one, so

\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}(f_{N+1})-\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}(0)=O(z).

Applying the same argument successively at the levels

N-1,N-2,\ldots,0
, we obtain

f-f^{[N]}=O(z^{N+1}).

Thus the Taylor coefficients of
f
and
f^{[N]}
agree up to degree
N
. Write the Taylor series expansions

f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k},\qquad f^{[N]}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}^{[N]}z^{k}.

Since both functions belong to
\mathscr{S}(A)
, Cauchy’s estimates give

\|a_{k}\|\leq 1,\qquad\|a_{k}^{[N]}\|\leq 1.

Since
a_{k}=a_{k}^{[N]}
for
k\leq N
, if
|z|\leq r<1
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(z)-f^{[N]}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}\bigl(\|a_{k}\|+\|a_{k}^{[N]}\|\bigr)r^{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}r^{k}=\frac{2r^{N+1}}{1-r}.

This proves (3).

Now let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be an arbitrary sequence in
\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)
.
For
N\geq 0
, let
f^{[N]}
be the
N
-th canonical Schur truncation with parameters
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.
Let

M>N
. For
0\leq k\leq M+1
, write
f_{k}^{[M]}
for the intermediate
Schur truncations determined by

f_{M+1}^{[M]}=0,\qquad f_{k}^{[M]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{k}}(f_{k+1}^{[M]}),\quad k=M,\ldots,0.

Similarly,
f_{k}^{[N]}
is defined by

f_{N+1}^{[N]}=0,\qquad f_{k}^{[N]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{k}}(f_{k+1}^{[N]}),\quad k=N,\ldots,0.

At level
N+1
, the two tails
f_{N+1}^{[M]}
and
f_{N+1}^{[N]}=0

are both bounded Schur-class functions, and hence their difference is
O(1)
.
Applying Proposition
A.4
(1) to

f_{N}^{[M]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}(f_{N+1}^{[M]}),\qquad f_{N}^{[N]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{N}}(f_{N+1}^{[N]}),

we obtain

f_{N}^{[M]}(z)-f_{N}^{[N]}(z)=O(z).

Applying the same argument successively at the levels

N-1,N-2,\ldots,0
, we get

f_{k}^{[M]}(z)-f_{k}^{[N]}(z)=O(z^{N-k+1}),\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,N.

In particular,

f^{[M]}(z)-f^{[N]}(z)=f_{0}^{[M]}(z)-f_{0}^{[N]}(z)=O(z^{N+1}).

Then the Cauchy estimate gives

\sup_{|z|\leq r}\|f^{[M]}(z)-f^{[N]}(z)\|\leq\frac{2r^{N+1}}{1-r}.

Thus
(f^{[N]})_{N\geq 0}
converges uniformly on compact subsets to some
f\in\mathscr{S}(A).

On the other hand, for
N\geq n
, let
f_{n}^{[N]}
be the Schur truncation
constructed from
\gamma_{n},\gamma_{n+1},\dots,\gamma_{N}.

The same argument gives locally uniform limits

f_{n}^{[N]}\longrightarrow f_{n}\in\mathscr{S}(A).
as
N\to\infty
.
Note that on every disk

|z|\leq r<1
, we have

\left\|(1+\gamma_{n}^{*}zf_{n+1}^{[N]}(z))^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{1-r\|\gamma_{n}\|}.

Thus, we may take the locally uniform limit
N\to\infty
in the identity proved in (3), namely

f_{n}^{[N]}=\Phi_{\gamma_{n}}(f_{n+1}^{[N]}),

to obtain
f_{n}=\Phi_{\gamma_{n}}(f_{n+1}).

Therefore,
f_{n}(0)=\gamma_{n},

and the Schur transform of
f_{n}
is
f_{n+1}
. Hence the Schur parameters
of
f=f_{0}
are exactly
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.

Finally, if
g\in\mathscr{S}(A)
has the same Schur parameters, then for
every
N
,
g
and
f^{[N]}
have identical Taylor coefficients
up to degree
N
. Thus, all Taylor coefficients of
g
and
f

coincide, and
g=f
holds. This finishes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this subsection:

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

First, we consider the case
\Omega=\mathbb{D}
.
Put
R:=\|f\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon
and
g:=R^{-1}f.
Then
\|g\|_{\infty}<1.

Let
(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}\subseteq\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(B)
be the Schur parameters of
g
, as in
Proposition
A.6
. For each
n
, choose
\widetilde{\gamma}_{n}\in\operatorname{Ball}^{\circ}(A)

such that
q(\widetilde{\gamma}_{n})=\gamma_{n}
.

Let
\widetilde{g}^{[N]}
be the canonical Schur truncation associated
with parameters
\widetilde{\gamma}_{0},\dots,\widetilde{\gamma}_{N}.

By Proposition
A.6
, there exists
\widetilde{g}\in\mathscr{S}(A)

such that
\widetilde{g}^{[N]}\longrightarrow\widetilde{g}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
.

Let
g^{[N]}
be the canonical Schur truncation associated with

\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{N}
. By Proposition
A.4
, we have

q\circ\widetilde{g}^{[N]}=g^{[N]}
.
Passing to the locally uniform limit gives
q\circ\widetilde{g}=g.

Define

Then

and

Now suppose

is a simply connected proper domain.
By Riemann’s mapping theorem (see
[
1
, Section 6.1, Theorem 1]
for example), there exists a biholomorphic isomorphism

. Given

and

Remark A.7
.

In the second part of the proof of Theorem
A.1
, for a general simply connected proper domain
\Omega
, we needed Riemann’s mapping theorem to find a biholomorphic map
\Phi\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
. However, in our actual application, we only need to find a biholomorphic mapping between
\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
and
\mathbb{D}
, in which case an explicit biholomorphic mapping can be found. Indeed, for
\rho>0
, the map

\Phi_{\rho}(z)=\tanh\left(\frac{\pi z}{4\rho}\right)=\frac{\exp\left(\frac{\pi z}{2\rho}\right)-1}{\exp\left(\frac{\pi z}{2\rho}\right)+1}

is a biholomorphic isomorphism
\Phi_{\rho}:\mathbb{S}_{\rho}\to\mathbb{D}.

Its inverse is

\Phi_{\rho}^{-1}(\zeta)=\frac{2\rho}{\pi}\log\left(\frac{1+\zeta}{1-\zeta}\right),

where the logarithm is taken with the principal branch on the right half-plane.

A.3.1.
Isometric
H^{\infty}
-lifting for the Ocneanu multiplier

In this section, we show how the theorem of the previous subsection can be improved in the case of the quotient map involved in the definition of the Ocneanu ultraproduct to obtain a lift with the same norm. Although not entirely necessary for the main result of this section, we believe it is of independent interest. The reader only interested in the main result of this section can safely skip the next theorem and see how the result of the previous subsection suffices.

Theorem A.8
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{U}}
is a countably incomplete ultrafilter on a set
I
.
Let
\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected
domain. Then for every
f\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,M)
,
there exists
F\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}})
such that
q\circ F=f
and
\|F\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

We may assume
f\neq 0
. Set
K:=\|f\|_{\infty}>0
.
Choose a countable dense set
\{\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots\}\subseteq\Omega.

By Theorem
A.1

applied to the quotient map
q\colon\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}\to M
,
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
U^{(n)}\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}})

such that

q\circ U^{(n)}=f,\qquad\|U^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq K+\frac{1}{n}.

Set
\lambda_{n}:=\frac{K}{K+1/n}
and
H^{(n)}:=\lambda_{n}\,U^{(n)}.
Then

\|H^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq K
,
and
q\circ H^{(n)}=\lambda_{n}f.

For each
j\geq 1
, choose an element

b^{(j)}=(b_{i}^{(j)})_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}

such that
q(b^{(j)})=f(\zeta_{j}).

For
n\geq j
, define

e^{(n,j)}:=H^{(n)}(\zeta_{j})-\lambda_{n}b^{(j)}.

Also, define
e^{(n,j)}=0
for
n<j
.
Then
q(e^{(n,j)})=0,

and hence
e^{(n,j)}\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}.

We now do a diagonalization argument along
{\mathcal{U}}
. Since
e^{(n,j)}\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
, for
n\geq j
and

\ell\geq 1
, the set

A_{n,j,\ell}=\left\{i\in I\ \middle|\ \|e_{i}^{(n,j)}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{1}{\ell}\right\}

belongs to
\mathcal{U}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is countably incomplete, we may choose a decreasing sequence
C_{1}\supseteq C_{2}\supseteq\cdots
of elements of
{\mathcal{U}}
such that
\displaystyle\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}C_{m}=\emptyset.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, define

B_{N}=C_{N}\cap\bigcap_{1\leq n\leq N}\bigcap_{1\leq j\leq n}\bigcap_{1\leq\ell\leq N}A_{n,j,\ell}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Then
B_{N+1}\subseteq B_{N}
, and
\bigcap_{N=1}^{\infty}B_{N}=\emptyset.

Define a function
\nu:I\to\mathbb{N}
by

\nu(i)=N
if
i\in B_{N}\setminus B_{N+1}

and
\nu(i)=1
if
i\notin B_{1}
. Then
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\nu(i)=\infty.

For every fixed
j
,

\|e_{i}^{(\nu(i),j)}\|\leq K+\|b^{(j)}\|,
so that
(e_{i}^{(\nu(i),j)})_{i}
is uniformly bounded. Moreover, we have

\bigl(e_{i}^{(\nu(i),j)}\bigr)_{i}\in\mathcal{\mathcal{I}}_{{\mathcal{U}}}.

Indeed, fix
j,\ell\geq 1
and put
r=\max\{j,\ell\}
.
For
i\in B_{r}
, set
N=\nu(i)
. Then
N\geq r
and

i\in B_{N}\subseteq A_{N,j,\ell}
. Hence

\|e_{i}^{(\nu(i),j)}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}<1/\ell\qquad(i\in B_{r}).

Since
B_{r}\in\mathcal{U}
, the required ultralimit is zero.
Fix
z\in\Omega
. Write

H^{(n)}(z)=(H_{i}^{(n)}(z))_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}.

Define
F_{i}(z):=H_{i}^{(\nu(i))}(z)

and set
F(z):=(F_{i}(z))_{i}
. Since
\sup_{n}\|H^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq K
, we have

(A.6)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|F_{i}(z)\|\leq K.

Thus
F(z)\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
for every
z\in\Omega
and
\sup_{z\in\Omega}\|F(z)\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq K.

We claim that
F:\Omega\to\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
is norm-continuous. Fix

\zeta_{0}\in\Omega
. Choose
r>0
such that

\overline{D(\zeta_{0},2r)}\subseteq\Omega.
For every
\zeta\in D(\zeta_{0},r)
, the circle
|\eta-\zeta|=r

is contained in
\Omega
. By the Banach-space valued Cauchy formula,

F_{i}^{\prime}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|\eta-\zeta|=r}\frac{F_{i}(\eta)}{(\eta-\zeta)^{2}}\,d\eta.

By (
A.6
), we obtain

\|F_{i}^{\prime}(\zeta)\|\leq\frac{K}{r}

for every
i\in I
and
\zeta\in D(\zeta_{0},r)
.

If
z,w\in D(\zeta_{0},r)
, then the line segment from
w
to
z

is contained in
D(\zeta_{0},r)
. Hence

F_{i}(z)-F_{i}(w)=\int_{0}^{1}F_{i}^{\prime}(w+t(z-w))(z-w)\,dt,

and therefore

\|F(z)-F(w)\|=\sup_{i\in I}\|F_{i}(z)-F_{i}(w)\|\leq\frac{K}{r}|z-w|.

Thus
F
is Lipschitz on
D(\zeta_{0},r)
. Since
\zeta_{0}\in\Omega
was
arbitrary,
F
is norm-continuous on
\Omega
.

We next prove that
F
is norm-holomorphic as an

\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
-valued function. Fix
z_{0}\in\Omega
, and choose

r>0
such that

\overline{D(z_{0},r)}\subseteq\Omega.

For every
z\in D(z_{0},r)
, the function
\xi\mapsto\frac{F(\xi)}{\xi-z}

is continuous as an
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
-valued function on

\partial D(z_{0},r)
. Thus the integral

\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\partial D(z_{0},r)}\frac{F(\xi)}{\xi-z}\,d\xi

is a Bochner integral in
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
.

For each
i
, the function

F_{i}(\zeta)=H_{i}^{(\nu(i))}(\zeta)

is holomorphic on
\Omega
. Therefore, the Banach-space valued Cauchy formula in
M_{i}
gives

F_{i}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\partial D(z_{0},r)}\frac{F_{i}(\xi)}{\xi-z}\,d\xi.

Since the coordinate evaluation map

\pi_{i}:\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})\to M_{i}

is bounded linear, it commutes with Bochner integration. Hence the

i
-th coordinate of

\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\partial D(z_{0},r)}\frac{F(\xi)}{\xi-z}\,d\xi

is exactly
F_{i}(z)
. Since this holds for every
i
, we have the
identity in
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
:

F(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\partial D(z_{0},r)}\frac{F(\xi)}{\xi-z}\,d\xi.

Thus
F
satisfies the local Cauchy integral formula as an

\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
-valued function, and hence
F
is norm-holomorphic.

We next show that
F(z)\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
for every
z\in\Omega
. First, fix
j\in\mathbb{N}
. On a
\mathcal{U}
-large set,

\nu(i)\geq j
, and hence

F_{i}(\zeta_{j})-b_{i}^{(j)}=e_{i}^{(\nu(i),j)}+(\lambda_{\nu(i)}-1)b_{i}^{(j)}.

The first term belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
. The
second term also belongs to
\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
since
\lim_{i\to{\mathcal{U}}}\lambda_{\nu(i)}=1

and
b^{(j)}\in\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
. Therefore
F(\zeta_{j})-b^{(j)}\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}.

Since
b^{(j)}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
and
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
,
we get
F(\zeta_{j})\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}.
The set
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
is norm-closed in
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
. Since

\{\zeta_{j}\ :\ j\geq 1\}
is dense in
\Omega
and since
F
is
norm-continuous as an
\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})
-valued function, it follows that

F(z)\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}

for every
z\in\Omega
. Hence

F\in H^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}})
and
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq K.

Finally, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
F(\zeta_{j})-b^{(j)}\in\mathcal{I}_{{\mathcal{U}}}
and

q(F(\zeta_{j}))=q(b^{(j)})=f(\zeta_{j}).

The two
M
-valued holomorphic functions
q\circ F

and
f
thus agree on the dense set
\{\zeta_{j}\ :\ j\geq 1\}
. By the Banach-space valued identity
theorem,
q\circ F=f
. Since
q
is contractive,
q\circ F=f
, and
\|f\|_{\infty}=K
, we have
\|F\|_{\infty}=K
.
This proves the theorem.
∎

A.3.2.
First Gaussian Regularization

Lemma A.9
.

Let
\rho>1/2,\,\tau>0
, and
G\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{\rho},\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}})
. For
z\in\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
, write

G(z)=(G_{i}(z))_{i}.

For
i\in I
and
z\in\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
, define
G_{i}^{\tau}(z)=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(z))
.
For fixed
z
with
|{\operatorname{Im}z}\,|\leq 1/2
and
u\in\mathbb{R}
, set

E_{i}^{z}(u)=\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}^{\tau}(u+z)).

Then
E^{z}(u):=(E_{i}^{z}(u))_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}

for every
u\in\mathbb{R}
, and
u\mapsto E^{z}(u)
is norm-continuous as an
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
-valued function which further satisfies

(A.7)

\|E^{z}(u)\|\leq\|G\|_{\infty}

for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For each
u
,
G^{\tau}(u+z):=(G^{\tau}_{i}(u+z))_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
by Corollary
2.14
. Then because
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
is invariant under
(\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}})_{i}

[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
, we obtain
E^{z}(u)\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
.

Rewrite

\displaystyle E_{i}^{z}(u)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(u+z))\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(t)\sigma_{t-u}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(u+z))\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k_{\tau}(v+u)\sigma_{v}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(u+z))\,dv.

Let
u,u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
. Since each real modular automorphism is isometric,

\displaystyle\|{E_{i}^{z}(u)-E_{i}^{z}(u^{\prime})}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{G}\|_{\infty}\|k_{\tau}(\cdot+u)-k_{\tau}(\cdot+u^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle+\|G_{i}(u+z)-G_{i}(u^{\prime}+z)\|.

Taking the supremum over
i
,

\|E^{z}(u)-E^{z}(u^{\prime})\|\leq\|G\|_{\infty}\|k_{\tau}(\cdot+u)-k_{\tau}(\cdot+u^{\prime})\|_{1}+\|G(u+z)-G(u^{\prime}+z)\|.

The first term tends to
0
as
u^{\prime}\to u
because translations are continuous in
L^{1}
, and the second tends to
0
because
G
is norm-continuous. The last inequality (
A.7
) is immediate.
∎

A.3.3.
Gaussian averaging and equivariant lifting

Next, we prove that if the bounded holomorphic map
F\colon\mathbb{S}_{\rho}\to M
is
\sigma^{\varphi}
-equivariant, then we may find an equivariant holomorphic lifting of the Gaussian-regularized version of
F
.

Proposition A.10
.

Assume that
\mathcal{U}
is countably incomplete. Fix
\rho>\frac{1}{2},\,K>0
and take
F\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{\rho},M)

such that
\|F\|_{\infty}\leq K
. Further suppose that
F
is
\sigma^{\varphi}
-equivariant in the sense that, for all
z\in\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

F(z+t)=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(F(z)).

Then for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
\tau>0
, there exist functions
H_{i}:\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\to M_{i}

which are holomorphic in the interior and continuous on the closed strip,
such that:

(1)

for each
i\in I
,
H_{i}
is
\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}
-equivariant, that is, for all
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

H_{i}(z+t)=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(H_{i}(z));

(2)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}}\|H_{i}(z)\|\leq K+\varepsilon;

(3)

for all
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
, we have

(H_{i}(z))_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(F(z)).

Proof.

Choose
a>0
so small that
e^{a/4}K<K+\varepsilon.
Put

\gamma_{a}(w)=\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}e^{-aw^{2}}.

By Theorem
A.8
,
there exists
G\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{\rho},\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}})
with
\|G\|_{\infty}\leq K
such that
q\circ G=F
.
For each
w\in\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
, write
G(w)=(G_{i}(w))_{i}
.
For each
i\in I
,
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
u\in\mathbb{R}
, define

E_{i}^{z}(u)=\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}\circ\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}\bigl(G_{i}(u+z)\bigr).

By Lemma
A.9
, we obtain the norm-continuous map
E^{z}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
defined by
E^{z}(u)=(E_{i}^{z}(u))_{i}
. Moreover, for each
u\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\|E^{z}(u)\|\leq\|G\|_{\infty}\leq K.

Since
\mathbb{R}\ni u\mapsto|\gamma_{a}(u+z)|\in\mathbb{R}
is integrable, the function

\mathbb{R}\ni u\mapsto\gamma_{a}(u+z)E^{z}(u)\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}

is Bochner integrable. Define
H\colon\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\to\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
by

H(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z)E^{z}(u)\,du\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}.

Write
H(z)=(H_{i}(z))_{i}
for each
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Since the coordinate evaluation maps
\pi_{i}\colon\ell^{\infty}(\bm{M})\to M_{i}
are bounded linear maps, they commute with
Bochner integration, and hence, for all
i\in I
and
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
, we have

H_{i}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z)\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}\circ\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}\bigl(G_{i}(u+z)\bigr)\,du.

Fix
i\in I
. For
t\in\mathbb{R},\,z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
, changing variables

v=u+t
gives

\displaystyle H_{i}(z+t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z+t)\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(u+z+t))\bigr)\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(v+z)\sigma_{-(v-t)}^{\varphi_{i}}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(v+z))\bigr)\,dv

\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(v+z)\sigma_{-v}^{\varphi_{i}}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(v+z))\bigr)\,dv\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(H_{i}(z)).

This shows (1). Next, for
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
,

\displaystyle\|H_{i}(z)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\gamma_{a}(u+z)|\|\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(u+z))\|\,du

\displaystyle\leq K\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\gamma_{a}(u+z)|\,du.

Writing
z=x+\mathrm{i}y
, one has

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\gamma_{a}(u+z)|\,du=e^{ay^{2}}\leq e^{a/4}.

This proves (2):

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{|\operatorname{Im}z|\leq 1/2}\|H_{i}(z)\|\leq e^{a/4}K<K+\varepsilon.

Since
q
is bounded linear and
H(z)
is a
Bochner integral in
\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
, we have

q(H(z))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z)q(E^{z}(u))\,du.

Let
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
.
By Corollary
2.14
and
[
3
, Lemma 4.14]
,

q(E^{z}(u))=\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(q(G(u+z)))\bigr)=\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(F(u+z))\bigr).

Using the
\sigma^{\varphi}
-equivariance of
F
, we obtain

\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(F(u+z))\bigr)=\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi}\bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{u}^{\varphi}(F(z)))\bigr)=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(F(z)).

Note also that the identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z)\,du=1

follows by shifting the contour.
Therefore,

\displaystyle(H_{i}(z))_{{\mathcal{U}}}

\displaystyle=q(H(z))=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma_{a}(u+z)\,du\right)\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(F(z))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi}(F(z)).

Finally, we prove that each
H_{i}
is holomorphic in the open strip and
continuous on the closed strip. Fix
i\in I
.
For
u\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
, put

\Phi_{i}(u,z)=\gamma_{a}(u+z)\,\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}\Bigl(\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}(G_{i}(u+z))\Bigr).

Thus

H_{i}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{i}(u,z)\,du.

Since
\rho>1/2
, we have
u+z\in\mathbb{S}_{\rho}.

Moreover,
G_{i}
is norm-holomorphic on
\mathbb{S}_{\rho}
. Hence, for each
fixed
u\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
z\mapsto\Phi_{i}(u,z)
is norm-holomorphic on
\mathbb{S}_{1/2}
and norm-continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{S}_{1/2}}
.

We record the local domination needed below. Let
A>0
. If

|\operatorname{Re}z|\leq A
and
|\operatorname{Im}z|\leq 1/2,

then, writing
z=x+\mathrm{i}y
, we have

|\gamma_{a}(u+z)|=\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}e^{-a((u+x)^{2}-y^{2})}\leq\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}e^{a/4}e^{-a(u+x)^{2}}.

Since

(u+x)^{2}\geq\frac{u^{2}}{2}-x^{2}\geq\frac{u^{2}}{2}-A^{2},

it follows that

|\gamma_{a}(u+z)|\leq\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}e^{a/4+aA^{2}}e^{-au^{2}/2}.

Consequently,

\|\Phi_{i}(u,z)\|\leq\left(\frac{a}{\pi}\right)^{1/2}e^{a/4+aA^{2}}Ke^{-au^{2}/2}.

The right-hand side belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

We first prove continuity on the closed strip. Let

z_{n}\to z_{0}
in
\overline{\mathbb{S}_{1/2}}.

Choose
A>0
such that

|\operatorname{Re}z_{n}|\leq A
and
|\operatorname{Re}z_{0}|\leq A

for all sufficiently large
n
. For each fixed
u\in\mathbb{R}
, the
norm-continuity of
G_{i}
, the contractivity of
\sigma_{k_{\tau}}^{\varphi_{i}}
, and the
isometry of
\sigma_{-u}^{\varphi_{i}}
give
\Phi_{i}(u,z_{n})\longrightarrow\Phi_{i}(u,z_{0})
, where the convergence is norm convergence.
By the dominated convergence theorem for the Bochner integration, we have

\|H_{i}(z_{n})-H_{i}(z_{0})\|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\Phi_{i}(u,z_{n})-\Phi_{i}(u,z_{0})\|\,du\longrightarrow 0.

Hence
H_{i}
is norm-continuous on
\overline{\mathbb{S}_{1/2}}
.

Next, we prove the holomorphy in the open strip. Let

D\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{1/2}
be a relatively compact open set. Choose

A>0
such that
|\operatorname{Re}z|\leq A
holds for all
z\in D
.
The preceding
L^{1}
-majorant is then uniform for
z\in D
. Hence, for
every triangle
\Gamma
whose closure is contained in
D
, Fubini’s theorem
and the norm-holomorphy of
z\mapsto\Phi_{i}(u,z)
give

\int_{\partial\Gamma}H_{i}(z)\,dz=\int_{\partial\Gamma}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Phi_{i}(u,z)\,du\right)dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\partial\Gamma}\Phi_{i}(u,z)\,dz\right)du=0.

Since
H_{i}
is norm-continuous on
D
, the Banach-space valued Morera theorem
implies that
H_{i}
is norm-holomorphic on
D
. Since
D\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{1/2}
was arbitrary,
H_{i}
is norm-holomorphic on

\mathbb{S}_{1/2}
.
∎

A.4.
Second proof of Theorem
5.5

We are now ready to give an alternative proof of Theorem
5.5
.

Second Proof of Theorem
5.5
when
{\mathcal{U}}
is countably incomplete.

Let
K>0
and
x\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)
.
For
s>0
, define

x_{s}:=\sigma_{k_{s}}^{\varphi}(x).

Then by
\|k_{s}\|_{1}=1
and Lemma
2.12
, each
x_{s}\in S_{K}(M,\varphi)

and because
(k_{s})_{s>0}
is an approximate identity, we have

\lim_{s\to+0}\|x_{s}-x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}=0.

For each
s>0
, define

F_{s}(z):=\sigma_{z}^{\varphi}(x_{s}).

Then
F_{s}
is bounded and norm-holomorphic on the open strip
\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}+s}

and is
\sigma^{\varphi}
-equivariant:

F_{s}(z+t)=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}(F_{s}(z)),\qquad z\in\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}+s},\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

We claim that

\|F_{s}\|_{\infty}\leq e^{s/4}K.

Indeed, on the upper boundary
\operatorname{Im}z=1/2+s
, we use (
2.5
) in Lemma
2.13
, and the fact that
\sigma_{k_{s}}^{\varphi}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}
commute to obtain

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{z}^{\varphi}(x_{s})\|

\displaystyle=\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}(1/2+s)}^{\varphi}(x_{s})\|=\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}s}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x_{s}))\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{s/4}\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{s/4}K.

The lower boundary case is analogous, using
\|\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi}(x)\|\leq K
instead.
The Banach-space valued three-lines theorem gives

\|F_{s}\|_{\infty}\leq e^{s/4}K
on
\mathbb{S}_{1/2+s}
.

For each
m\geq 1
, choose
s_{m}>0
and
\tau_{m}>0
so small so that

\|\sigma_{k_{\tau_{m}}}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{k_{s_{m}}}^{\varphi}(x))-x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}<\frac{1}{m},\qquad e^{s_{m}/4}K<K+\frac{1}{2m}.

Apply Proposition
A.10
to
F_{s_{m}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}_{1/2+s_{m}},M)

with
K
replaced by
e^{s_{m}/4}K
and with the parameters
\tau=\tau_{m}
and
\varepsilon=1/(2m)
.
We obtain functions
H_{i}^{(m)}\colon\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\to M_{i}

which are continuous, holomorphic in the interior,
such that

H_{i}^{(m)}(z+t)=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(H_{i}^{(m)}(z)),

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}}\|H_{i}^{(m)}(z)\|\leq K+\frac{1}{m},

and
(H_{i}^{(m)}(z))_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\sigma_{k_{\tau_{m}}}^{\varphi}(F_{s_{m}}(z))

for every
z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}_{\frac{1}{2}}}
.

Set
y_{i}^{(m)}:=H_{i}^{(m)}(0).

By the covariance of
H_{i}^{(m)}
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

H_{i}^{(m)}(t)=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(H_{i}^{(m)}(0))=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(y_{i}^{(m)}).

Moreover,
H_{i}^{(m)}
is bounded and continuous on the closed strip

\overline{\mathbb{S}_{1/2}}
and
holomorphic in the interior. Hence
H_{i}^{(m)}
is an analytic
extension of
t\mapsto\sigma_{t}^{\varphi_{i}}(y_{i}^{(m)})
. Therefore
y_{i}^{(m)}\in D(\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi_{i}})\cap D(\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi_{i}})
,
and its boundary values are

\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi_{i}}(y_{i}^{(m)})=H_{i}^{(m)}(\mathrm{i}/2),\qquad\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi_{i}}(y_{i}^{(m)})=H_{i}^{(m)}(-\mathrm{i}/2).

The norm estimate on
H_{i}^{(m)}
gives

\|y_{i}^{(m)}\|\leq K+\frac{1}{m},

\|\sigma_{\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi_{i}}(y_{i}^{(m)})\|\leq K+\frac{1}{m},\qquad\|\sigma_{-\mathrm{i}/2}^{\varphi_{i}}(y_{i}^{(m)})\|\leq K+\frac{1}{m}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have

y_{i}^{(m)}\in S_{K+1/m}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
for
i\in I.

At
z=0
, the quotient equality gives

(y_{i}^{(m)})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=\sigma_{k_{\tau_{m}}}^{\varphi}(F_{s_{m}}(0))=\sigma_{k_{\tau_{m}}}^{\varphi}(\sigma_{k_{s_{m}}}^{\varphi}(x)).

Hence
\|(y_{i}^{(m)})_{{\mathcal{U}}}-x\|_{\varphi}^{\#}<\frac{1}{m}
.
Choose any
(a_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
such that
x=(a_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}
.
For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

I_{m}=\left\{i\in I\ \middle|\ \|y_{i}^{(m)}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}<\frac{2}{m}\right\}\in{\mathcal{U}}.

Since
\mathcal{U}
is countably incomplete, choose a decreasing sequence
C_{1}\supseteq C_{2}\supseteq\cdots
of elements in
{\mathcal{U}}

such that

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}C_{m}=\emptyset.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, set

J_{m}=C_{m}\cap I_{1}\cap\cdots\cap I_{m}.

Then

J_{1}\supseteq J_{2}\supseteq\cdots
,
J_{m}\subseteq I_{m}
for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\bigcap_{m}J_{m}=\emptyset.

For each
i\in I
, define

z_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{K}{K+1/m}y_{i}^{(m)}&i\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m+1},\\
\qquad 0&i\notin J_{1}.\end{cases}.

Then
z_{i}\in S_{K}(M_{i},\varphi_{i})
for every
i
, and thus by Lemma
5.13
,
(z_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{M}^{{\mathcal{U}}}
holds. We show that
(z_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=x
.
Along
\mathcal{U}
, the integer
m
determined by
i\in J_{m}\setminus J_{m+1}

tends to infinity. For such
i
,

\displaystyle\|z_{i}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}

\displaystyle\leq\|y_{i}^{(m)}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}+\left|1-\frac{K}{K+1/m}\right|\|y_{i}^{(m)}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m}+\frac{1/m}{K+1/m}\left(K+\frac{1}{m}\right)=\frac{3}{m}.

Therefore

\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\|z_{i}-a_{i}\|_{\varphi_{i}}^{\#}=0,
and
(z_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=(a_{i})_{{\mathcal{U}}}=x.

This proves the theorem when
{\mathcal{U}}
is a countably incomplete ultrafilter.
∎

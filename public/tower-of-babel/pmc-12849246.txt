Predictive value of serum homocysteine and potassium ion for short-term prognosis of patients with acute cerebral infarction following intravenous thrombolysis with recombinant tissue plasminogen activator
Authors: Lingen Pang, Yongan Sun, Mingli He, Bingchao Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study evaluated the predictive value of serum homocysteine (HcY) and potassium ion (K+) for short-term prognosis of patients with acute cerebral infarction (ACI) undergoing intravenous thrombolysis with recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA).MethodsTotally, 140 ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA were finally included and classified into early neurological deterioration (END) and non-END groups. Serum levels of HcY and electrolytes were detected. The 90-day prognosis of patients after thrombolysis was analyzed. Influencing factors for END and poor short-term prognosis in rt-PA-treated ACI patients were identified using stepwise regression models. Correlations of serum HcY and K+ with modified Rankin scale (mRS) scores were analyzed, and values of serum HcY and K+ in assessing END and poor prognosis of ACI patients were determined.ResultsThe END group had higher age, atrial fibrillation, neutrophil ratio, CysC, FIB, FPG, TNF-ɑ, IL-6 and HcY levels, and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale scores on admission but lower serum calcium ion and K+ concentrations than the non-END group. In ACI patients, serum HcY and K+ levels significantly correlated with mRS scores. Serum HcY and K+ levels were independent influencing factors for END and poor short-term prognosis of rt-PA-treated ACI patients. Combination of serum HcY and K+ could assist in predicting END and poor short-term prognosis in ACI patients.ConclusionSerum HcY and K+ levels are closely linked to END and short-term prognosis in ACI patients after intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA and can be used as novel biomarkers for short-term prognosis in ACI patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12959-025-00810-z.

Introduction

Acute cerebral infarction (ACI) is a clinical syndrome wherein the blood supply in the brain is disrupted by various factors, resulting in ischemic and hypoxic necrosis in focal brain tissues [1]. ACI, also named ischemic stroke, is one of the most prevalent cerebral vascular diseases, with high disability, recurrence, and mortality, which can lead to severe damage to the central nervous system [2]. As internationally accepted, intravenous thrombolysis is the preferred measure to restore blood flow in ACI [3, 4]. Recombinant tissue-plasminogen activator (rt-PA) is a representative thrombolytic drug with the advantages of robust thrombolytic ability, high specificity, and favorable safety [5]. When intravenously administered, rt-PA selectively dissolves local blood clots while activating free plasminogen by binding to fibrin, therefore ameliorating cerebral artery stenosis or occlusion and recovering cerebral blood flow, neural cells in penumbra, and neural function [6]. Despite the safety and efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA within 4.5 h of onset for ACI has been internationally accepted, this treatment still has a risk of post-thrombolytic hemorrhagic transformation (HT) [7, 8]. In addition, prior studies unveiled that early neurological deterioration (END) occurred in approximately 20%−30% of ACI patients after intravenous thrombolysis [9, 10]. As reported, END may elevate the risk of functional disability and poor prognosis in ACI patients [11]. Therefore, it is crucial to identify biomarkers and influencing factors for the prognosis of ACI patients after intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA.

Homocysteine (HcY) is a sulfur-containing amino acid generated during methionine metabolism, and increased HcY concentrations in the plasma have been associated with multiple disorders, such as cardiovascular and neurodegenerative diseases [12]. In recent years, the role of serum HcY levels in ACI has received increasing attention. A prior study unravelled an association between elevated plasma HcY levels and the increased risk of ACI [13]. Another study elucidated that higher serum HcY levels were correlated with poorer clinical outcomes of ACI patients after thrombolysis [14]. Serum HcY level is an independent predictor for HT within 24 h of intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA in ACI patients [15]. Meanwhile, higher serum HcY levels are associated with an increased risk of HT in ACI patients and monitoring the HcY serum may help identify high-risk populations for HT [16, 17]. Conversely, the research by Li et al. also revealed that when all other confounders are considered, the impact of HcY on the outcome of ACI patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA is comparatively small and may be of little clinical significance [18]. Therefore, it is still highly controversial whether HcY can be used as an independent predictor of poor prognosis of patients with intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. Electrolyte balance is responsible for homeostasis maintenance in the body, and the most significant electrolytes are sodium (Na+), potassium (K+), magnesium (Mg2+), and calcium (Ca2+) ions [19]. K+, as a key cation in the human body, plays a role in the central nervous system [20, 21]. Altered K+ concentrations are generally present in the serum of patients with stroke [22, 23]. Abnormal serum K+ levels are more common in hemorrhagic stroke than in ischemic stroke, and the glucose-to-potassium ratio can not only serve as a prognostic predictor for acute intracerebral hemorrhage but also as a predictor for HT, stroke recurrence, and mortality in patients with ischemic stroke [24–26]. Moreover, a prior study reported the alleviatory effect of elevated serum K+ concentrations on cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury [27]. However, it is uncertain about the value of K+ levels in predicting the short-term prognosis of ACI patients with intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. Therefore, this study assessed the predictive value of HcY and K+ for the short-term prognosis of rt-PA-treated ACI patients, providing data for future clinical interventions and further research of ACI.

Materials and methods

Sample size estimation

Sample size was determined by the t-test using GPower 3.1.9.2 software (Heinrich-Heine-Universität, Dusseldorf, Germany) (Supplementary Fig. 1). The following parameters were used: statistical significance (α) = 0.05, power (1-β) = 0.8, and medium effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.5). The results indicated that the minimum sample size required for each group was 128 to ensure adequate statistical power to detect the study results.

Participants

This study retrospectively recruited 209 consecutive ACI patients who received intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA at The First People’s Hospital of Lianyungang, between February 2020 and January 2024, among which 140 patients were finally included as per the inclusion and exclusion criteria. For intravenous thrombolysis, rt-PA (50 mg; S20110052; Boehringer Ingelheim Pharma GmbH, Biberach, Germany) was administered at a dose of 0.9 mg/kg (a maximum dose of 90 mg). The drug was infused in two steps. Specifically, 10% of the drug was intravenously infused within 1 min and the remaining drug was intravenously injected with a micropump for 1 h. Changes in the vital signs of patients were closely monitored during the administration of the drug and within 24 h of administration. In addition, all patients received standard treatments such as antiplatelet therapy and statins. All ACI patients were divided into the END group (n = 65) and the Non-END group (n = 75) according to whether END occurred after receiving treatment with intravenous thrombolysis. The study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of The First People’s Hospital of Lianyungang and complied with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Additionally, a post-hoc sample size test was performed using G*Power 3.1.9.2 software. Assuming a significance level (α) of 0.05 and an expected effect size (Cohen’s d) of 0.5 (medium effect), the test power was 0.834 according to the sample size of the two groups (65 and 75 cases), which was higher than 0.8 (Supplementary Fig. 2), consistent with the statistical principle.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (1) patients admitted with acute symptoms such as nausea, headache, vomiting, and limb dysfunction and diagnosed with ACI after head computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging; (2) patients with complete clinical data; (3) patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA within 4.5 h after the onset of the disease; and (4) patients aged ≥ 18 years.

Exclusion criteria for participants were as follows: (1) patients with contraindications to thrombolysis; (2) patients receiving intra-arterial thrombolysis or bridging therapy; (3) patients with acute or chronic infections; (4) patients with a history of special treatments such as hormones, immunosuppressants, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy in the last 3 months; (5) patients accompanied by serious dysfunction of organs including the heart, liver, and kidneys, neurodegenerative diseases, or hematologic and immune system diseases; (6) pregnant or breastfeeding women.

Data collection

From the medical records, general clinical data were obtained, such as age, sex, history of smoking and alcohol consumption, pre-thrombolysis blood pressure, comorbidities (diabetes mellitus [DM], hyperlipidemia, hypertension, and heart disease), history of stroke, and time from onset to thrombolysis. All patients were scored by clinicians with standardized training and qualifications using the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) on admission. Laboratory test results were obtained, including routine blood test results (white blood cell count [WBC], neutrophil ratio, platelet count [PLT], and mean platelet volume [MPV]), liver and kidney function test results (creatinine [CREA], urea nitrogen [Urea], cystatin C [CysC], uric acid [UA], and total bilirubin [TBil]), coagulation test results (fibrinogen [FIB] and D-dimer [D-D]), blood lipid test results (total cholesterol [TC], low-density lipoprotein cholesterol [LDL-C], triglycerides [TG], high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C], apolipoprotein B [APO-B], and Lipoprotein a [LP] [a]), blood glucose test results (glycosylated hemoglobin [GHbA1]), three indicators of thyroid function (free triiodothyronine [FT3], free thyroxine [FT4], and thyroid stimulating hormone [TSH]), a hemorheological parameter (erythrocyte sedimentation rate [ESR]). Blood (5 mL) was drawn from the antecubital vein of each patient on admission for assays.

Serum HcY and electrolyte level detection

Venous blood from patients on admission was centrifuged (radius: 9.5 cm; 3000 r/min; 10 min), and the supernatant was obtained and stored in a -80 °C refrigerator for measurement. The serum level of HcY was detected with a double-antibody sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), where a HcY ELISA kit (KH1019-2, KINGSBIO, Zhejiang, China) and an automated ELISA reader (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) were used. The serum levels of electrolytes (Ca2+, Mg2+, K+, and Na+) were measured with an AU5831-1 automatic biochemical analyzer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA).

Determination of short-term prognosis of ACI patients with intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA

The short-term prognosis of intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA included END and the prognosis of patients at 90 days after thrombolysis. END was defined as an increase of 4 or more NIHSS scores from baseline within 24 h after thrombolysis or the development of new neurological impairment within 24 h after thrombolysis. Patients were categorized into END and non-END groups [28, 29].

Patients were followed up by telephone or face-to-face interviews at 90 days after thrombolysis and scored using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS). The mRS score is categorized into 7 grades: 0 scores, normal; 1 score, no significant functional impairment despite symptoms (able to carry out all daily activities independently); 2 scores, mild disability (unable to carry out all previous activities but able to look after own affairs without assistance); 3, moderate disability (requiring some help but able to walk independently); 4, moderately severe disability (unable to walk independently, unable to perform daily activities without assistance); 5, severe disability (bed-ridden, incontinent, and depending on others for daily life); 6, death. An mRS score of < 2 indicated a good prognosis, and an mRS score of ≥ 2 marked a poor prognosis [28, 30].

Statistical analysis

Data were statistically analyzed and graphed using SPSS 21.0 statistical software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism 6.0 software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). Data were first tested for normal distribution with the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Measurement data with normal distribution were displayed as mean ± standard deviation and were compared between two groups with the independent samples t-test. Measurement data without normal distribution were presented as quartiles, that is, medians (minimum, maximum), and intergroup comparisons were performed with the Mann-Whitney U test. Count data were stated as cases and percentages and were compared between two groups with the chi-square test. Correlation analysis was conducted using the Spearman linear correlation coefficient. Stepwise logistic regression analyses were used to assess the influencing factors of ACI patients with END and poor prognosis. The Bonferroni correction significance threshold was ɑ = 0.0125, and P < 0.0125 was considered statistically significant. Receiver-operating characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to analyze the value of serum HcY and K+ levels in predicting END and poor prognosis in ACI patients, followed by the calculation of the cut-off values and the areas under the ROC curves (AUC) for serum HcY and K+. P values were for two-tailed tests, and there were statistically significant differences at P < 0.05. MedCalc statistical software (version 20.0.15, MedCalc Software Ltd., Ostend, Belgium) with a DeLong test was used to compare the differences in AUC in the ROC analysis. Bonferroni-adjusted significance threshold of ɑ = 0.025 and P < 0.025 indicated a statistically significant difference.

Results

Baseline data of patients

This study retrospectively included 209 consecutive patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA in the hospital between February 2020 and January 2024, among which 140 ACI patients were finally included according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. These patients were allocated into the END and non-END groups according to whether END occurred after receiving treatment with intravenous thrombolysis. The two groups were insignificantly different in terms of sex, history of smoking and alcohol consumption, comorbidities (coronary artery disease, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and DM), blood pressure on admission, history of stroke, time from onset to thrombolysis, location of lesions, WBC, PLT, MPV, CREA, Urea, UA, APO-B, LP(a), TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, TBil, D-D, GHbA1, FT3, FT4, TSH, diuretics use, and ESR (all P > 0.05). Age, atrial fibrillation neutrophil ratio, CysC, FIB, fasting plasma glucose (FPG), tumor necrosis factor ɑ (TNF-ɑ), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels, NIHSS scores on admission, and incidence of atrial fibrillation were higher in the END group than in the non-END group (all P < 0.05) (Insert Table 1 about here).

Elevated serum levels of HcY and decreased K+ in END patients on admission

Serum levels of HcY and electrolytes (K+, Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+) were compared between the END and non-END groups. The results displayed that serum HcY levels were 11.40 ± 3.95 µmol/L (CV: 34.65%) in the non-END group and 18.04 ± 4.50 µmol/L (CV: 24.95%) in the END group, indicating markedly higher serum HcY levels in the END group than in the non-END group (P < 0.001, Insert Fig. 1A about here). Serum K+ and Ca2+ levels were 4.33 ± 0.69 mmol/L (CV: 15.88%) and 2.13 ± 0.11 mmol/L (CV: 16.23%) in the non-END group, respectively. Serum K+ and Ca2+ levels in the END group were 3.34 ± 0.49 mmol/L (CV: 14.66%) and 2.09 ± 0.09 mmol/L (CV: 19.45%), respectively. Serum K+ and Ca2+ levels in the END group were prominently lower than those in the non-END group (P < 0.001, P < 0.05), with no statistical significance in serum Na+ and Mg2+ levels between the two groups (all P > 0.05) (Insert Fig. 1B-E about here).

Analysis of the relationship between brain infarction location and serum HcY, electrolyte K+, and Ca2+ levels in ACI patients

Based on the location of brain infarction in ACI patients, we categorized the patients in END and Non-END groups into anterior and posterior circulation groups, and compared their differences in serum HcY, K+, and Ca2+ levels. The results showed that, in ACI patients of the Non-END group, significant differences in HcY, K+, and Ca2+ levels were observed between the anterior circulation group and the posterior circulation group (all P < 0.05), while no significant differences were found in Na+ and Mg2+ levels (all P > 0.05). In ACI patients of the END group, significant differences in HcY and K+ levels were observed between anterior and posterior circulation groups (all P < 0.05), while no significant differences were found in terms of Na+, Ca2+, and Mg2+ levels (all P > 0.05). Serum HcY and K+ as independent influencing factors for the development of END in ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA

Discussion

Although intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA is considered the standard therapy for ACI, its application has been limited due to the high incidence of post-thrombolysis END, which is an indicator of the poor prognosis of ACI [31, 32]. Accordingly, it is necessary to search for the predictor of END in ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. Herein, the current research probed the predictive value of HcY and K+ for END and short-term prognosis in ACI patients following intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. The findings elucidated that serum HcY and K+ levels were closely associated with END and short-term prognosis of ACI patients after intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. In addition to that, the combined detection of serum HcY and K+ may assist in predicting END occurrence and poor short-term prognosis in ACI patients.

NIHSS is an internationally recognized assessment tool for the degree of neurological deficit in patients with ACI, and its score directly reflects the severity of early neurological deficits [33]. In this study, we referred to previous studies and defined END as an increase in the NIHSS score of at least 4 points from baseline within 24 h after thrombolytic therapy in patients with ACI or the occurrence of new neurological impairment within 24 h after thrombolytic therapy [28, 29]. ACI patients were split into the END and non-END groups according to whether END occurred after receiving treatment with intravenous thrombolysis. Analyses of their baseline clinical characteristics showed that age, neutrophil ratio, CysC, FIB, FPG, TNF-ɑ and IL-6 levels, NIHSS scores on admission, and incidence of atrial fibrillation were higher in the END group than in the non-END group. In contrast, no profound difference was found between the two groups in terms of sex, history of smoking and alcohol consumption, comorbidities (coronary artery disease, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and DM), blood pressure on admission, history of stroke, time from onset to thrombolysis, location of lesions, WBC, PLT, MPV, CREA, Urea, UA, APO-B, LP(a), TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C, TBil, D-D, GHbA1, FT3, FT4, TSH, diuretics use, or ESR. Published data have demonstrated that factors such as patients’ lifestyle (smoking and alcohol consumption) and psychological state (anxiety and depression) can influence the efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis in ACI patients [34–36]. To this end, further research should focus on a thorough assessment of these potential influencing factors.

A prior study revealed that HcY was expressed at a high level in ACI patients, with a positive rate of up to 89.9% [37]. Plasma HcY upregulation was reported to be markedly associated with the augmented incidence of ACI in patients with carotid artery lesions [38]. HcY levels have been revealed as an independent predictor for dismal prognosis in ACI patients [39]. Moreover, Liu et al. noted that high HcY levels also correlated with the high incidence of early cognitive impairment in ACI patients and that the level of serum HcY was an independent influencing factor for early cognitive impairment in ACI patients [40]. The research by Geng et al. unravelled that in patients with first-ever ischemic stroke, HcY was associated with END and functioned as an independent predictor for END [11]. Former research showed that lower serum K+ levels independently correlated with an augmented risk of recurrent stroke in ACI patients [22]. Lower K+ levels are also related to poor functional outcomes and death in ACI patients [41]. Abnormal serum K+ levels have been demonstrated as an effective predictor for END in patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage [42]. Serum HcY can influence endothelial dysfunction and vasodilatory activity involving endoplasmic reticulum stress-mediated suppression of calcium-activated potassium channels [43, 44]. Serum K+ can facilitate the specific binding of HcY to the active site of betaine-homocysteine S-methyltransferase [45]. Furthermore, elevated serum HcY levels and low K+ levels are associated with an increased risk of recurrent stroke in patients with ischemic stroke; per 5 µmol/L increase of HcY or 1 mmol/L decrease of K+ corresponds to 19% higher risk of recurrent stroke [22, 46]. Subsequently, we measured serum HcY and K+ levels in patients in END and non-END groups. The data displayed that the END group had markedly higher serum HcY levels (17.67 ± 4.36 µmol/L vs. 11.93 ± 3.72 µmol/L) and dramatically lower serum K+ levels (3.37 ± 0.50 mmol/L vs. 4.25 ± 0.66 mmol/L) than the non-END group. Additionally, the comparative analysis revealed significant differences in Hcy and K+ levels between anterior and posterior circulation groups of ACI patients in the END group. Further, we performed stepwise logistic regression analyses to analyze the effects of serum HcY and K+ levels on the END occurrence and short-term prognosis in rt-PA-treated ACI patients. The findings exhibited that enhanced serum HcY levels were an independent risk factor and increased serum K+ levels within the normal range were a protective factor for END occurrence and poor short-term prognosis in ACI patients. Despite a significant difference in baseline NIHSS scores between the END and non-END groups, defining the END group with an increase in the NIHSS score by 4 or more after matching the NIHSS scores between the two groups can reduce the interference of baseline imbalance on the prognosis analysis. Lack of analysis on the disease severity is one of the limitations of this study. Future research with a larger sample size is necessary for a more comprehensive and in-depth analysis of the NIHSS scores and disease severity.

The mRS is a clinician-reported measure of global disability that is broadly utilized to assess the prognosis of stroke patients and as an endpoint in randomized clinical trials of stroke [47]. In previous studies of stroke, the primary endpoint is patients’ functional outcome at 90 days assessed by the mRS [48, 49]. By referring to prior research [50, 51], we defined the mRS score of < 2 as a good prognosis and the mRS score of ≥ 2 as a poor prognosis. Accordingly, our research assessed the prognosis of ACI patients at 90 days after intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA and further clarified the relationship of serum HcY and K+ levels with mRS scores, which exhibited that mRS scores had a positive correlation with serum HcY levels and a negative correlation with serum K+ levels in ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. Similarly, previous research reported that mRS scores in ACI patients receiving thrombolysis were lower in the low HcY level group than in the high HcY level group [14]. Additionally, the diagnostic and prognostic values of HcY have been analyzed. For instance, the maximum AUC of HcY for the diagnosis of ACI is 0.853 [37]. In ACI patients, the sensitivity of HcY in predicting post-thrombolysis prognosis was 79.6%, and the specificity was 70.5%, with an AUC of 0.729 [52]. Serum HcY was verified as a moderately sensitive and specific index to predict the prognosis of ACI patients after tPA treatment (optimal cutoff value, 19.95 µmol/L; sensitivity, 58.2%; specificity, 80.3%) [53]. Partially consistent with these data, our results exhibited that the AUC of serum HcY levels was 0.834 for assisting in predicting poor short-term prognosis in ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA, and the cutoff value was 14.72 (sensitivity, 78.69%; specificity, 77.22%). Innovatively, our data also unveiled that for assisting in predicting END in ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA, the AUC of serum HcY levels was 0.867 (cutoff value, 14.25; sensitivity, 81.54%; specificity, 77.33%). However, little is known regarding the value of serum K+ levels in assisting in predicting END occurrence and short-term prognosis in ACI patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA. Intriguingly, our data revealed that for assisting in predicting END occurrence and poor short-term prognosis, the AUCs of serum K+ were 0.874 and 0.844, and the cutoff values were 3.71 and 3.55 (sensitivity, 80.00% and 72.13%; specificity, 85.33% and 86.08%), respectively. More importantly, the AUC (0.932) for the combination of serum HcY and K+ was dramatically higher than that for serum HcY and K+ alone. Serum HcY > 14.25 and K+ > 3.71 have high diagnostic value for END occurrence in ACI patients receiving rt-PA-mediated intravenous thrombolysis. These parameters can assist clinicians in practice to stratify patients into high- or low-risk END, and may play a significant role in personalized treatment planning by integrating imaging features with clinical scores. In addition, serum HcY > 14.72 and K+ > 3.55 have excellent predictive value for an adverse short-term prognosis in ACI patients receiving rt-PA-mediated intravenous thrombolysis. In the future, the stability of these biomarkers should be validated in multicenter cohorts among ACI patients at different disease stages or those with comorbid conditions. Meanwhile, a combined prediction model integrating HcY, K+, and other highly associated indicators (such as the NIHSS score) could be developed, so as to provide value for predicting and improving clinical outcomes.

Our research has limitations. First, the research was conducted at a single center, which may introduce regional and demographic biases. Besides, a small sample size in this study may affect the generalizability and statistical significance of the results. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes will be essential to better control for these potential biases and enhance the external validity of the results. Second, the present study only included serum HcY and K+ levels at admission and did not consider their changes at other time points, or before and after treatment. This may lead to a false positive in patients with high baseline HcY but a rapid decline after treatment. In future studies, we will conduct dynamic monitoring at multiple time points during rt-PA-mediated intravenous thrombolysis in ACI patients to optimize individualized treatment assessment. Third, we are currently unable to collect 30-day or 60-day mRS data due to the limited time and funding. Meanwhile, this study primarily focused on the short-term prognosis (90 days) and did not thoroughly assess the final outcome of neurological function recovery and the long-term prognosis of patients. Future studies are thus warranted to consider long-term follow-up to comprehensively evaluate impacts of HcY and K+ on the prognosis. Accordingly, considering mechanism overlap, clinical heterogeneity, validation costs, and large sample data, we strive to develop a high risk-stratification predictive model incorporating multiple factors, including HcY, K+, and NIHSS scores in future studies.

Conclusively, our findings unravelled that serum HcY and K+ levels were strongly associated with END and short-term prognosis of ACI patients after intravenous thrombolysis using rt-PA and could serve as novel biomarkers for short-term prognosis in ACI patients. This study may offer a foundation for the use of HcY and K+ to predict the prognosis of ACI patients in the clinic.

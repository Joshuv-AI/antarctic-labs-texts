Entropy production rate and time-reversibility for general jump diffusions on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n}
Qi Zhang, Yubin Lu

Abstract
This paper investigates the entropy production rate and time-reversibility for general jump diffusions (Lévy processes) on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . We first formulate the entropy production rate and explore its associated thermodynamic relations for jump diffusions. Subsequently, we derive the entropy production rate using the relative entropy between the forward and time-reversed path measures for stationary jump diffusions via the Girsanov transform. Furthermore, we establish the equivalence among time-reversibility, zero entropy production rate, detailed balance condition, and the gradient structure for stationary jump diffusions.



Keywords:
Thermodynamics; entropy production; time-reversibility; jump diffusions; Lévy processes.

1
Introduction

The breaking of time-reversal symmetry is an fundamental feature for nonequilibrium process in thermodynamics.
The entropy production rate quantifies the degree of time-irreversibility, serving as a key concept for understanding systems far from thermal equilibrium.
In general, without energy or material exchange, the process has time-reversibility and zero entropy production rate if the it admit equilibrium state after relaxation time.
Moreover, Nicolis and Prigogine
[
23
]
argue that when an open system is driven by sustained energy or material inputs from its environment, the process may reach a nonequilibrium steady state characterized by a positive entropy production rate.
In nonequilibrium thermodynamics, the entropy production rate can be represented as the product of the thermodynamics fluxes and forces,
and it can be distinguished between two terms: the entropy change due to interactions with an external reservoir or environment, and the entropy generated within the system itself, see e.g.
[
6
,
23
]
.

Diffusion processes are essential stochastic models for various nonequilibrium phenomena, including polymer dynamics in fluids, asset price fluctuations in financial models, and the motion of biological motor molecules.
The formulation of entropy production and associated thermodynamics relations for diffusion processes are discussed by
[
12
,
31
,
30
]
.
The equivalence between self-adjointness of the generator, the gradient structure of the drift, time-reversibility, and zero entropy production for a diffusion process is considered in detail in
[
17
,
25
]
. Additionally, the entropy production rate of a stationary process can be derived from the relative entropy (Kullback-Leibler divergence) between the forward and time-reversed path measures, as demonstrated by Maes and Netočný
[
21
]
, and Da Costa and Pavliotis
[
7
]
.
Lebowitz and Spohn
[
20
]
established a path-space framework connecting entropy production to large deviation theory and fluctuation theorems.
Seifert
[
27
]
further investigated entropy production along single trajectories, establishing the associated integral fluctuation theorem. Similar principles apply to discrete-state Markov jump processes, which model systems such as molecular networks, chemical reactions, and transport on graphs (see e.g.
[
9
,
22
]
). Recently, Boffi and Vanden-Eijnden
[
4
]
develop a deep learning framework via the entropy production rate to estimate the score in generative modeling.

In recent years, signatures of jump noise have been found ubiquitous in nature. As a result, jump diffusions have garnered significant attention for modeling phenomena such as DNA target search for binding sites
[
29
]
, active transport within cells
[
19
]
, and the price of risky assets
[
8
]
.

It is interesting to ask the question whether one can extend the results for entropy production rate and time-reversibility from classical diffusions to diffusions with nonlocal jumps.
However, this extension is nontrivial due to the simultaneous presence of local diffusion driven by Brownian noises and nonlocal jump noises.
Recently, the time reversal of jump diffusions under the finite relative entropy condition is discussed by
[
5
]
, and its application on score-based generative model are considered in
[
32
]
. The entropy production formula and fluctuation theorem for non-Gaussian stochastic dynamics with associated deep learning framework is studied by
[
14
]
.
Nevertheless, the literature on entropy production and time-reversibility for general jump diffusions (Lévy processes) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
remains limited. This paper aims to derive the entropy production rate formula for jump diffusions, and reveal the relationship between entropy production and the degree of time-irreversibility. It is a generalization of some classical results for diffusion models and discrete Markov chains
[
25
,
21
]
.
We note that another formula for the entropy production rate of jump diffusions is provided in
[
14
]
.
Unlike our formula, theirs retains the quadratic form of the entropy production rate for jump diffusions, similar to the case of Langevin equations, by rewriting the associated nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation into a local form of the continuity equation.
In contrast, in our formula (
1
), the nonlocal term arises naturally from the pure jump component in the SDE (
1.1
), which makes its interpretation more straightforward.

We are interested in the jump diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
which is given by the following SDE on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}a(X_{t^{-}})\circ dB_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\sigma(X_{t^{-}},z)N(dt,dz),

(1.1)

where
B_{t}
be an
n
-dimensional Brownian motion on the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
equipped with a filtration
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
\circ
denote the Stratonovich integral, the constant
\beta:=\theta^{-1}
denote the inverse temperature,
b(\cdot)
is a drift from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
a(\cdot)=(a_{ij}(\cdot))_{n\times n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is the diffusion coefficient,
N(dtdz)
be an independent
n
-dimensional Poisson random measure with Poisson intensity rate
\lambda>0
and Lévy measure
\nu(dz)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, and the function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the coefficient of jump. We present some preliminary results for jump-diffusions
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and list some necessary assumption for the SDE (
1.1
) in Section
2
.

In section
3
, we first derive the entropy production rate for the jump diffusion as

\displaystyle e_{p}(t)=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(A^{-1}(x)b(x)-\beta^{-1}\nabla\log\rho(t,x))^{T}A(x)(A^{-1}(x)b(x)-\beta^{-1}\nabla\log\rho(t,x))\rho(t,x)dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\rho(t,x)k(x,y)-\rho(t,y)k(y,x))\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)k(x,y)}{\rho(t,y)k(y,x)}\right)dydx,

(1.2)

where the matrix
A(\cdot)=a^{T}(\cdot)a(\cdot)
is a positive definite matrix or a zero matrix,
\rho(t,\cdot)
is the density of
X_{t}
, and
k(x,y)
denote the jump rate from
x
to
y
.
Due to this formula, the entropy production rate
e_{p}(t)
of the jump diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is always non-negative.
Here, the entropy production rate
e_{p}(t)
is the sum of the variation of entropy in the system,
dS(t)/dt
, and the heat dissipation,
-h_{d}(t)
. Based on this formula, we also discuss some thermodynamics relations, include the Clausius inequality and the free energy dissipation for jump diffusions.

The another goal of this paper is to investigate the relationship between the above entropy production rate and time-reversibility of the stationary jump diffusions.
One can use the stationary Markov process to model a physical system is in a steady state (including the equilibrium state and nonequilibrium steady state).
In probability theory, a stationary Markov process with a invariant measure
\mu
is a time-reversal process if for every
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
and
t,s>0
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}f(X_{t})g(X_{s})=\mathbb{E}^{\mu}f(X_{t})g(X_{s}).

In Section
4
, we consider the time reversal formula of the jump diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in Lemma
4.2
.
Then using the Girsanov transform for the stationary jump diffusion, we deduce the entropy production as the relative entropy between the forward path measure and time-reversed path measure for the stationary jump diffusion:

e^{ss}_{p}=\frac{1}{T}\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}|\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]})=\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\left[\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}}{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\right)\right],

see Theorem
4.3
in detail. This result reveals the connection between the entropy production rate of a stationary Markov process and the time-reversal asymmetry of its path measure
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}
.

In Section
5
, we show the equivalence between time-reversibility, zero entropy production, detailed balance, and the gradient structure for stationary jump diffusions in Theorem
5.1
. This theorem is a criteria to determine the reversibility of general jump diffusions. Using this theorem, we construct some examples of time-reversible/time-irreversible stationary jump diffusions in Section
6
.

The paper ends with some summary and discussions in Section
7
.

2
Jump-diffusions and nonlocal Fokker-Planck equations

In this section, we recall some basic definitions and useful result for jump-diffusions. These materials can be found in some classical references for jump-diffusions or Lévy processes, including
[
1
,
28
,
18
]
and references therein.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space that satisfies the usual hypotheses.
Let
B_{t}
be an
n
-dimensional Brownian motion, and
L_{t}
be an independent
n
-dimensional compound Poisson process with associated Poisson intensity rate
\lambda>0
and Lévy measure (jump size distribution)
\nu(dz)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then the compound Poisson process
L_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}D_{i}
, where
n_{t}
is the counting variable of a Poisson process with rate
\lambda
, and
\{D_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. random variables with distribution
\nu(dz)
, which are also independent of
n_{t}
. Then the Poisson random measure
N(dt,dy)
associated to the compound Poisson process
L_{t}
is defined as

N(t,B)(\omega):=\sharp\{s\in[0,t]:\Delta L_{s}(\omega)\in B\},\quad t\geq 0,B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}),

so that
\mathbb{E}N(dt,dz)=\lambda dt\nu(dz)=\lambda m(z)dtdz
. Then the compound Poisson process
L_{t}
is also given by

L_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}zN(dt,dz).

In this paper, we assume that the Lévy measure
\nu(dz)=m(z)dz
and satisfies the integrability condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}1\wedge|z|^{2}\nu(dz)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}1\wedge|z|^{2}m(z)dz<\infty.

The above the integrability condition implies that the sample paths of
L_{t}
have a finite quadratic variation with finitely many large jumps with an amplitude larger than 1, almost surely.

Using the compensated Poisson random measure
\widetilde{N}(dt,dy)=N(dt,dy)-\lambda dt\nu(dy)
, the SDE (
1.1
) is equivalent with

\displaystyle dX_{t}=

\displaystyle\left[b(X_{t})+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot A(X_{t})+\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}\sigma(X_{t^{-}},z)\nu(dz)\right]dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}a(X_{t^{-}})dB_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0<|z|\leq 1}\sigma(X_{t^{-}},z)\widetilde{N}(dt,dz)+\int_{|z|>1}\sigma(X_{t^{-}},z)N(dt,dz),

(2.1)

where
A(x)=a(x)a^{T}(x):=(A_{ij}(x))
.

Now we make the some assumptions for the SDE (
1.1
). All these assumptions in this section are assumed to hold in the sequel unless otherwise specified.

(A) Lipschitz condition
For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|x_{1}|\vee|x_{2}|\leq k
, there exists constant
c_{1}>0
such that

|b(x_{1})-b(x_{2})|^{2}+\|A(x_{1})-A(x_{2})\|+\int_{0<|z|<1}|\sigma(x_{1},z)-\sigma(x_{2},z)|^{2}\nu(dz)\leq c_{1}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{2}.

Here and below,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a matrix, and
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm.

(B) Growth condition
For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there are constants
c_{2}>0
such that

|b(x)|^{2}+\|A(x)\|+\int_{0<|z|<1}|\sigma(x,z)|^{2}\nu(dz)<c_{2}(1+|x|^{2}).

Under above assumption, we have the following well-posedness result of the SDE (
1.1
) (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 6.2.9]
or
[
18
, Theorem 3.3.1]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
({\bf A})
and
({\bf B})
hold, and impose the standard initial condition. Then the SDE (
1.1
) admits a unique global strong solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

The solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has the Markov semigroup
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, which is given by

P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}(f(X_{t})|X_{0}=x),\quad\forall f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu).

We also denote the generator of the Markov process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
by
\mathcal{L}
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}f=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{t}(P_{t}f-f),\quad\forall f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu).

Then the jump diffusion
X_{t}
has generator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f(x)=

\displaystyle b(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x))+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\left[f(x+\sigma(x,z))-f(x)\right]d\nu(z).

(C) Boundedness condition
the Jacobian matrix
\nabla_{z}\sigma(x,z)
is non-degenerate for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that
\|\nabla_{z}\sigma(x,z)\|<c_{5}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Under the above boundedness condition, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the map
z\mapsto\sigma(x,z)
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism, and admits an inverse denoted by
\sigma^{-1}(x,z)
. By the change of variables, the generator can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}f(x)=

\displaystyle b(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x))

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}[f(x+z)-f(x)]\det(\nabla_{z}\sigma^{-1}(x,z))m(\sigma^{-1}(x,z))dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}[f(y)-f(x)]k(x,y)dy,

where
k(x,y)=\lambda m(\sigma^{-1}(x,y-x))\det(\nabla_{y-x}\sigma^{-1}(x,y-x))
is the jump kernel.

Let
\Delta X_{t}=X_{t}-X_{t^{-}}
be the pure jump part of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Now we introduce the Poisson random measure

\displaystyle N_{X}(t,B)(\omega):=\sharp\{s\in[0,t]:\Delta X_{s}(\omega)\in B\},\quad t\geq 0,B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})

so that
N_{X}(dt,dz):=\det(\nabla_{z}\sigma^{-1}(X_{t},z))N(dt,d\sigma^{-1}(X_{t},z))
, and the associated compensated Poisson random measure

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{X}(dt,dz)=

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}_{X}(dt,dz)-\lambda\det(\nabla_{z}\sigma^{-1}(X_{t},z))m(\sigma^{-1}(X_{t},z))dzdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{N}(dt,dy)-k(X_{t},X_{t}+z)dzdt.

generates a local martingales.
Then we have

\int_{|z|>0}\sigma(X_{t^{-}},z)N(dt,dz)\overset{\text{d}}{=}\int_{|z|>0}zN_{X}(dt,dz),

and the SDE (
1.1
) is also equivalent with

dX_{t}=b(X_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}a(X_{t^{-}})\circ dB_{t}+\int_{|z|>0}zN_{X}(dt,dz).

The existence of a smooth transition density of Markov processes with jumps has been investigated by Malliavin calculus, see e.g. Picard
[
24
]
, and Kunita
[
18
, Chapter 6]
.
Moreover, the probability density function
\rho(x,t)
satisfies the nonlocal Fokker-Planck equation

\partial_{t}\rho(x,t)=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\rho(x,t).

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
is the adjoint operator of the generator
\mathcal{L}
, which is given by

(\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\rho)(x)=\nabla\cdot[-b(x)\rho(x)+\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\rho(x)]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}k(y,x)\rho(y)-k(x,y)\rho(x)dy.

Similar with the local Fokker-Planck equation, we define the local probability current

j^{loc}(t,x):=b(x)\rho(x)-\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\rho(x).

(2.3)

Since there is a nonlocal jump part in the operator
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
, for every
x\neq y
, we also define the nonlocal probability current between
x
and
y
as

j^{nl}(t,x,y)=\rho(t,x)k(x,y)-\rho(t,y)k(y,x).

(2.4)

By the local probability current
j^{loc}(t,x)
and the nonlocal probability current
j^{nl}(t,x,y)
, we can rewrite the Fokker-Planck equation (
2.2
) as

\partial_{t}\rho(x,t)+\nabla\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)dy=0.

By the local probability current
j^{loc}(t,x)
and the nonlocal probability current
j^{nl}(t,x,y)
, we introduce the definition of detailed balance for the jump diffusion.

Definition 2.1

(Detailed balance)
.

The jump diffusions
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfy the detailed balance condition if for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t>0
, its local probability current
j^{loc}(t,x)
and the nonlocal probability current
j^{nl}(t,x,y)
satisfy

j^{loc}(t,x)=0,\text{ and }j^{nl}(t,x,y)=0.

In thermodynamics theory, the process is at equilibrium state if the process satisfies the detailed balance condition.

A probability measure
\mu
is said to be an invariant measure of Markov semigroup
P_{t}
if for every
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}P_{t}f(x)\mu(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\mu(dx).

Furthermore, if the invariant measure
\mu
has a
C^{2}
density
\rho_{ss}
, then
\rho_{ss}
satisfies the stationary Fokker-Planck equation
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\rho_{ss}=0
.

In order to show the existence of invariant measure for SDE (
1.1
), we need the following Lyapunov condition of the generator
\mathcal{L}
.

(D) Lyapunov condition.
There exists a positive function
w_{1}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and a positive compact function
w_{2}\geq c_{6}|x|^{p}
for some constant
c_{6}>0
and
p\geq 1
, so that
\mathcal{L}w_{1}\leq c_{5}-w_{2}
holds for some constant
c_{5}>0
.

We remark that under Lyapunov condition with
w_{2}
, the solution has the uniform moment estimate

\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}|^{p}<c^{-1}_{6}\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}w_{2}(X_{t})<\infty.

The the associated semigroup of SDE (
1.1
) has an invariant probability measure
\mu
, see e.g.
[
13
]
. In physics, the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is stationary is its invariant measure
\mu(dx)
means that the corresponding dynamic is in a steady state (including the equilibrium state and nonequilibrium steady state).

We remark that under some dissipative condition of drift
b
, the generator
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the Lyapunov condition. In
[
33
]
, the Lipschitz condition and Growth condition can be released to local Hölder continuous condition and local growth condition with some dissipative drift
b
. Some explicit invariant measure of jump diffusion are constructed in
[
3
]
.

Finally, in order to define the entropy production rate in next section, we also need the following assumption for the jump diffusion.

(E) Regularity condition.
For every
t\geq 0
,
\nabla\log\rho(t,\cdot)
is Lipschitz continuous and linear growth, and there exists a constant
c_{7}>0
so that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\geq 0
, so that the log ratio

\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)k(x,y)}{\rho(t,y)k(y,x)}\right)<c_{7}

.

3
Entropy production rate from thermodynamics relations

In order to give the entropy production rate formula for jump diffusions, we first define some important concepts from thermodynamics theory for jump diffusions, including the Gibbs entropy, internal energy, work, and heat dissipation in this section.

The Gibbs entropy of the jump diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is

S(t)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho(t,x)\log\rho(t,x)dx.

where
\rho(t,\cdot)
is the probability density of
X_{t}
.

By Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, the vector field can be decomposed as

A^{-1}(x)b(x)=-\nabla V(x)+f^{loc}(x),

where
-\nabla V
is the internal force from the potential
V
, and the divergence-free vector field
f^{loc}
is the external force from outside the system.
This decomposition for diffusion processes was well studied by
[
2
,
26
]
.
Moreover, motivated by the case of the discrete jump processes in
[
22
]
, we also decompose the jump kernel as

\frac{k(x,y)}{k(y,x)}=e^{\beta(f^{nl}(x,y)+V(x)-V(y))},

where
V(x)-V(y)
is the difference in potential between each
x
and
y
, and
f^{nl}(x,y)=-f^{nl}(y,x)
is the external driving to the nonlocal transitions between each
x
and
y
.

Since the diffusion
X_{t}
is moving in the potential
V(x)
, the total internal energy of
X_{t}
is given by

U(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)\rho(t,x)dx=\mathbb{E}V(X_{t}).

Then based on the probability currents
j^{loc}(t,x)
and
j^{nl}(t,x,y)
, rate of total work (power) done by the external force
f^{loc}(x)
and external driving
f^{nl}(x,y)
is

\displaystyle\frac{dW(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{loc}(x)\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)f^{nl}(x,y)dydx.

(3.1)

The change of internal energy is

\displaystyle\frac{dU(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\rho(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)\nabla\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla V(x)\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)[V(x)-V(y)]dydx.

(3.2)

By the first law of thermodynamics, the infinitesimal heat exchange with the reservoir at time
t
is given by
dQ(t)=dU(t)-dW(t).
Thus the heat dissipation
h_{p}(t)
of
X_{t}
is given by

\displaystyle h_{d}(t)=

\displaystyle\beta\frac{dQ(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\frac{dU(t)}{dt}-\beta\frac{dW(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\nabla V(x)-f^{loc}(x))\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)[f^{nl}(x,y)+V(x)-V(y)]dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b^{T}(x)A^{-1}(x)\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)\log\left(\frac{k(x,y)}{k(y,x)}\right)dydx.

(3.3)

On the other hand, the variation of entropy in the system is

\displaystyle\frac{dS(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho(t,x)\log\rho(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)(\log\rho(t,x)+1)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\rho(t,x)(\log\rho(t,x)+1)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}j^{loc}(t,x)\nabla\log\rho(t,x)dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)}{\rho(t,y)}\right)dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(j^{loc}(t,x))^{T}A^{-1}(x)j^{loc}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}dx-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b(x)^{T}A^{-1}(x)j^{loc}(t,x)dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)\log\left(\frac{\rho_{t}(x)k(x,y)}{\rho_{t}(y)k(y,x)}\right)dydx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)\log\left(\frac{k(x,y)}{k(y,x)}\right)dydx.

(3.4)

The total variation of entropy
e_{p}(t)
can be written as the sum of the variation of entropy in the system,
dS(t)/dt
, and the variation of entropy in the reservoir,
-h_{d}(t)
. Combining (
3
) with and (
3
), now we define entropy production rate formula for the jump diffusion as follows.

Definition 3.1
.

Under the assumptions in Section 2, the entropy production rate of the jump diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
which is given by the SDE (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle e_{p}(t)=

\displaystyle\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-h_{d}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(j^{loc}(t,x))^{T}A^{-1}(x)j^{loc}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)\log\left(\frac{\rho_{t}(x)k(x,y)}{\rho_{t}(y)k(y,x)}\right)dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(A^{-1}(x)b(x)-\beta^{-1}\nabla\log\rho(t,x))^{T}A(x)(A^{-1}(x)b(x)-\beta^{-1}\nabla\log\rho(t,x))\rho(t,x)dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}(\rho(t,x)k(x,y)-\rho(t,y)k(y,x))\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)k(x,y)}{\rho(t,y)k(y,x)}\right)dydx.

(3.5)

By the nonnegative property of entropy production rate, we immediately obtain the Clausius inequality for jump diffusion. This result implies the famous second law of thermodynamics for the jump diffusions.

Theorem 3.1

(Clausius inequality)
.

Under the assumptions in Section 2, the entropy production rate of jump diffusion
X_{t}
with temperature
\theta:=\beta^{-1}
satisfies the Clausius inequality

\theta\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-dQ(t)=\theta e_{p}(t)\geq 0.

Moreover, the jump process is at the equilibrium state (detail balance) with the invariant measure
\mu(dx)=\rho_{ss}(x)dx
if and only if
\theta\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-dQ(t)=\theta e_{p}(t)=0
.

Proof:

Note that
j^{nl}(t,x,y)=\rho(t,x)k(x,y)-\rho(t,y)k(y,x)
and
\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)k(x,y)}{\rho(t,y)k(y,x)}\right)
are both positive or negative. Thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}(\rho(t,x)k(x,y)-\rho(t,y)k(y,x))\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)k(x,y)}{\rho(t,y)k(y,x)}\right)dydx\geq 0,

and the equality holds if and only if
j^{nl}(t,x,y)=\rho(t,x)k(x,y)-\rho(t,y)k(y,x)=0
for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
t\geq 0
.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(A^{-1}(x)b(x)-\beta^{-1}\nabla\log\rho(t,x))^{T}A(x)(A^{-1}(x)b(x)-\beta^{-1}\nabla\log\rho(t,x))\rho(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(j^{loc}(t,x))^{T}A^{-1}(x)j^{loc}(t,x)}{\rho(t,x)}dx\geq 0,

and the equality holds if and only if
j^{loc}(t,x)=0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
t\geq 0
.
Thus for every
t\geq 0
, we have

\theta\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-dQ(t)=\theta\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-\theta h_{p}(t)=\theta e_{p}(t)\geq 0.

Moreover, for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
j^{loc}(t,x)=0
and
j^{nl}(t,x,y)=0
almost surely, if and only if
e_{p}(t)=0
. It implies that process
X_{t}
satisfies the detail balance condition at its invariant measure if and only if
\theta\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-dQ(t)=0
.

\blacksquare

In isolated system, thermodynamics theory yields that the total internal energy of the system changes due to exchange heat with the heat bath, and the free energy decreases until it reaches to its minimum at equilibrium.
Recall that free energy of
X_{t}
is defined as

F(t):=U(t)-\theta S(t).

Now we show the connection between the entropy production rate and the free energy dissipation of irreversible jump diffusions in isolated system.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that the assumptions in Section 2 hold, and there is no external force
f^{nl}
from outside in the drift, and for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the external driving to the nonlocal transitions
f^{nl}(x,y)=0
. We also assume that the stochastic dynamics
X_{t}
satisfies the detailed balance condition at Gibbs measure
\mu(dx)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-\beta V(x)}dx
.
Then the dissipation rate of free energy is given by the entropy production rate

\frac{dF(t)}{dt}=-\theta e_{p}(t).

Proof:

Since there is no external force and external driving to the nonlocal transitions in the isolated system, the change of heat
dQ=-dU
. Then the heat dissipation rate as

h_{d}(t)=\frac{1}{\theta}\frac{dQ}{dt}=-\beta\frac{dU(t)}{dt}.

Then by Theorem
5.1
, drift
b(x)=-A(x)\nabla V(x)
, and the kernel

k(x,y)=e^{-\beta[V(y)-V(x)]/2}s(x,y).

Based on the probability currents
j^{loc}(t,x)
and
j^{nl}(t,x,y)
, the heat dissipation
h_{p}(t)
is given by

\displaystyle h_{d}(t)=

\displaystyle\beta\frac{dU(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla V(x)\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx-\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)[V(x)-V(y)]dydx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b^{T}(x)A^{-1}(x)\cdot j^{loc}(t,x)dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}j^{nl}(t,x,y)\log\left(\frac{k(x,y)}{k(y,x)}\right)dydx.

(3.6)

Combining with (
3
), we have

\frac{dF(t)}{dt}=\theta\frac{dS(t)}{dt}-\theta h_{d}(t)=-\theta e_{p}(t)\leq 0.

\blacksquare

4
Connection with time forward/reversed path measures

In this section, we will show that the total entropy production of the jump diffusions over the interval
[0,T]
is given by the relative entropy between its forward and time-reversed path measures. Moreover, the entropy production rate quantifies the degree of time-irreversibility in the jump diffusions.

We first recall the path measure of jump diffusions.
Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the jump process with a Markov transition density
p_{t}(x,y)
, which is given by the SDE (
1.1
). The Kolmogorov extension theorem implies that there exists a unique path probability measure
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}
on the canonical (path) space
D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that for every
T>0
,
0=t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq\dots\leq t_{m}\leq T
, and
E_{j}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),0\leq j\leq m
, we have

\mathcal{P}(X_{t_{0}}\in E_{0},\dots,X_{t_{m}}\in E_{m})=\int_{E_{0}}dx_{0}\dots\int_{E_{m}}\Pi^{m}_{j-1}p_{t_{j}-t_{j-1}}(x_{j},x_{j-1})p_{0}(x_{0})dx_{0}\dots dx_{m}.

Under the path measure
\mathcal{P}
, we can consider the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
as a random variable on the path space
D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then the jump process
X_{t}
solves the martingale problem for the path measure
\mathcal{P}
and the generator

\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{P}}f(x)=b(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}[f(y)-f(x)]k(x,y)dy.

That is, for all
f\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the process

M_{t}^{f}:=f(X_{t})-f(X_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{P}}f(X_{s})\,ds

is a
\mathcal{P}
-local martingale.

In stochastic analysis for jump diffusion, one can apply the Girsanov transform for absolutely continuous changes between two paths on the path space
D([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see e.g.
[
28
, Theorem 33.2]
and
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
.
In order to introduce the Girsanov transform, we first define the exponential process

\displaystyle Z_{t}:=

\displaystyle\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}h^{T}(s)a(X_{s^{-}})dB_{s}-\beta^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}h^{T}(s)A(X_{s^{-}})h(s)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\log K(s,z)N_{X}(dsdz)-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}(K(s,z)-1)k(X_{s^{-}},X_{s^{-}}+z)dzds\bigg).

where
h:[0,T]\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a predictable process,
\gamma\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is an
n\times n
value matrix predictable process, and
K:[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a predictable process.
We also assume that the predictable processes
f,A,K
satisfy that

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}|h^{T}(s,\omega)A(X_{s})h(s,\omega)|ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}|K(s,\omega,z)+1|(\log^{+}|K(s,\omega,z)|+1)k(X_{s^{-}},X_{s^{-}}+z)dzds<\infty.

(4.1)

so that exponential process
Z_{t}
is a uniformly integrable
\mathcal{P}
-martingale.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the path measure of
X_{t}
on the canonical space
D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Define a new probability measure
\mathcal{Q}
on
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
via the Radon-Nikodym derivative:

\frac{d\mathcal{Q}}{d\mathcal{P}}\bigg|_{\mathscr{F}_{t}}=Z_{t}.

Now we state the Girsanov transform for jump diffusions.

Theorem 4.1

(Girsanov transform)
.

Then under
\mathcal{Q}
, the process
X_{t}
solves the martingale problem for the modified generator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\mathcal{Q}}f:=b^{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\omega,x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}[f(y)-f(x)]k^{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\omega,y)dy,

(4.2)

where

b^{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\omega,x):=b(x)+a(x)h(t,\omega),

and

k^{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\omega,y):=K(s,\omega,y-x)k(y,x).

Moreover, under
\mathcal{Q}
the jump diffusion
X_{t}
satisfies the new SDE

\displaystyle dX_{t}=

\displaystyle\left[b_{t}^{Q}(X_{t})+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot A(X_{t})\right]dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}a(X_{t^{-}})dB^{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}zN^{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}(dt,dz).

(4.3)

Here,
B^{\mathcal{Q}}_{t}
is a
\mathcal{Q}
-Brownian motion, and the Poisson random measure
N^{\mathcal{Q}}_{X}(dt,dz)
has Lévy measure
\lambda dtk^{\mathcal{Q}}(t,\omega,X_{t}(\omega)+z)
under
\mathcal{Q}
.

In order to give the time reversal path measure of
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,t]}
, we need consider its time reversal jump diffusion of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and the associated time reversal SDE.

Lemma 4.2

(Time Reversal formula)
.

Let
(X_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
be a jump diffusion satisfying the SDE (
1.1
) with smooth density
\rho(t)
. Assume that the assumptions in Section 2 hold.
We further assume that there exists a constant
C>0
, so that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}1\wedge|y-x|^{2}\frac{\rho(t,y)}{\rho(t,x)}k(y,x)dy<C.

Then the associated time-reversed process
\{X_{t}^{R}:=X_{T-t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfying the following SDE

\displaystyle dX^{R}_{t}=

\displaystyle b^{R}_{t}(X^{R}_{t})dt+\sqrt{2\beta^{-1}}a(X_{t^{-}})\circ dB_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}zN^{R}(dt,dz),

(4.4)

where the time reversed
b^{R}_{t}(x)=-b(x)+2\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\log\rho(T-t,x)
, and the time reversed Poisson random measure
N^{R}(dt,dz)
has Poisson intensity
\lambda
and the Lévy measure

\nu^{R}(dz):=k^{R}_{t}(x,x+z)dz=\frac{\rho(T-t,x+z)}{\rho(T-t,x)}k(x+z,z).

Proof:

Note that the time reversal process
\{X_{t}^{R}:=X_{T-t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
has the density
\rho^{R}(t,x)=\rho(T-t,x)
. Using the Fokker-Planck equation (
2.2
), the time reversal density
\rho^{R}(t,x)=\rho(T-t,x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{R}(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\rho(T-t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot[b(x)\rho(T-t,x)-\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\rho(T-t,x)]

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}k(y,x)\rho(T-t,y)-k(x,y)\rho(T-t,x)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot[b(x)\rho^{R}(t,x)-\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\rho^{R}(t,x)]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}k^{R}_{t}(y,x)\rho^{R}(t,y)-k^{R}_{t}(x,y)\rho^{R}(t,x)dy

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}^{R})^{\ast}\rho(t,x),

where the time reversed jump

k^{R}_{t}(x,y)=\frac{\rho(T-t,y)}{\rho(T-t,x)}k(y,x).

Thus the time reversed process
\{X_{t}^{R}:=X_{T-t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is a Markov process with generator

\mathcal{L}^{R}f(x)=b^{R}(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x))+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}[f(y)-f(x)]k^{R}_{t}(x,y)dy.

This ensures that the reversed processes satisfies the SDE (
4.4
).

\blacksquare

We denote
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}
and
\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}
as the path measures of the forward process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
and time-reversed processes
(X^{R}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
respectively on the path space
D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In next theorem, we show the relationship between the entropy production rate and the elative entropy rate between the forward path measure
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}
, and the time reversal path measure
\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that the assumptions in Section 2 hold.
Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a stationary jump-diffusion process defined by the SDE (
1.1
) with its invariant measure
\mu(dx):=\rho_{ss}(x)dx
, so that
X_{t}\overset{\text{d}}{=}\mu
for all
t\in[0,T]
, and the density
\rho_{ss}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}1\wedge|y-x|^{2}\frac{\rho_{ss}(y)}{\rho_{ss}(x)}k(y,x)dy<\infty,\quad.

uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then the entropy production rate
e_{p}^{ss}
of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

e^{ss}_{p}=\frac{1}{T}\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}|\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]})=-\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\left[\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}}{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\right)\right].

Proof:

Under the assumption for jump rate
k(x,y)
and
\rho_{ss}
, the jump diffusion
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has its time-reversed process satisfying the SDE (
4.4
).
Applying the Girsanov transform for absolutely continuous changes between path measures
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}
and
\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}}{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\right)=

\displaystyle\beta\int^{T}_{0}(b(X_{t})-b^{R}(X_{t}))A^{-1}(X_{t})dB_{t}

\displaystyle-\beta\int^{T}_{0}(b(X_{t})-b^{R}(X_{t}))^{T}A^{-1}(X_{t})(b(X_{t})-b^{R}(X_{t}))dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\log\left(\frac{k(X_{t}+z,X_{t})\,\rho_{ss}(X_{t}+z)}{k(X_{t},X_{t}+z)\,\rho_{ss}(X_{t})}\right)N_{X}(dtdz)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{k(X_{t}+z,X_{t})\,\rho_{ss}(X_{t}+z)}{k(X_{t},X_{t}+z)\,\rho_{ss}(X_{t})}-1\right)k(X_{t^{-}},X_{t^{-}}+z)dzdt.

(4.5)

Using the martingale property, the relative entropy

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}|\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\left[\log\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}}{\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{4}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)-b^{R}(x))^{T}A^{-1}(x)(b(x)-b^{R}(x))\rho_{ss}(x)dxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\log\left(\frac{k(x+z,x)\,\rho_{ss}(x+z)}{k(x,x+z)\,\rho_{ss}(x)}\right)k(x,x+z)\rho_{ss}(x)dzdxdt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\left(\frac{k(x+z,x)\,\rho_{ss}(x+z)}{k(x,x+z)\,\rho_{ss}(x)}-1\right)k(x,x+z)\rho_{ss}(x)dzdxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)-\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\log\rho_{ss}(x))^{T}A(x)(b(x)-\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\log\rho_{ss}(x))\rho_{ss}(x)dxdt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[k(y,x)\rho_{ss}(x)-k(x,x)\rho_{ss}(x)]\log\left(\frac{\rho(t,x)k(x,y)}{\rho(t,y)k(y,x)}\right)dydx

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}k(y,x)\rho_{ss}(x)-k(x,x)\rho_{ss}(x)dydxdt.

Since
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\rho_{ss}=0
, the last term is
0
. Thus we conclude that

\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}|\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]})=Te^{ss}_{p},

where the entropy production rate
e^{ss}_{p}
is consistent with (
3.1
) with the stationary density
\rho_{ss}
.

\blacksquare

The Theorem
4.3
shows that if the stationary jump process is reversible (i.e., it satisfies detailed balance), the forward path measure is the same as its time-reversed path measure, i.e.
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}=\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}
. In this case, the relative entropy is zero, and thus the entropy production rate is zero. This corresponds to a system in thermal equilibrium.
If the process is irreversible,
\mathcal{P}_{[0,T]}\neq\mathcal{P}^{R}_{[0,T]}
leading to a positive relative entropy and thus a positive entropy production rate. The magnitude of entropy production rate quantifies how statistically distinguishable forward paths are from their time-reversed versions, indicating the strength of the irreversibility.

5
Time-reversibility

In this section, we discuss relation between time-reversibility and detailed balance for general jump diffusions.
Let
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a stationary jump diffusion satisfying the SDE (
1.1
) with initial measure
\mu(dx)
. Since
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a time-homogeneous Markov process, we define the time-reversibility as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

The invariant measure
\mu
is called a reversible (probability) measure of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if for every
f,g\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[f(X_{t})g(X_{0})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[f(X_{0})g(X_{t})].

The connection between self-adjointness of the generator of an ergodic Markov process in
L^{2}(\mu)
and the time-reversibility Markov process is established by Fukushima and Stroock
[
11
, Theorem 2.3]
.
This result show that a invariant measure
\mu
is reversible with respect to
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if and only if the generator
\mathcal{L}
is symmetric in
D(\mathcal{L})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
, i.e. for every
f,g\in D(\mathcal{L})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\mathcal{L}g(x)\mu(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)\mathcal{L}f(x)\mu(dx).

Now we claim our second main result as follows.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that the assumptions in Section 2 hold.
Let
\mu(dx)=\rho_{ss}(x)dx=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-\beta V(x)}dx
be the Gibbs measure associated with potential
V
.
Then the following are equivalent:

(i) The stationary process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with invariant density
\rho_{ss}
is time-reversible.

(ii) The system
X_{t}
with its invariant measure
\mu(dx)=\rho_{ss}(x)dx
is detailed balance.

(iii) The drift
b(x)=-A(x)\nabla V(x)
, and the kernel

k(x,y)=e^{-\beta[V(y)-V(x)]/2}s(x,y),

where
s(x,y)=s(y,x)
is a nonnegative measurable symmetric function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
.

(iv) The entropy production rate vanishes at the stationary measure
\mu(dx):=\rho_{ss}(x)dx
, i.e.
e^{ss}_{p}=0

Proof:

(i)
\Rightarrow(ii)
. The reversibility of
X_{t}
implies that for every
f,g\in D(\mathcal{L})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}+\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

where
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is the local part of the generator
\mathcal{L}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is the nonlocal part of the generator
\mathcal{L}
.
Using integration by part, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla f(x)))g(x)\rho_{ss}(x)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(b(x)\cdot\nabla g(x)+\beta^{-1}\nabla\cdot(A(x)\nabla g(x)))f(x)\rho_{ss}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\langle b(x)-\beta^{-1}\frac{A(x)\nabla\rho_{ss}(x)}{\rho_{ss}(x)},\nabla f(x)g(x)-\nabla g(x)f(x)\right\rangle\rho_{ss}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle b(x)-\beta^{-1}A(x)\nabla\log\rho_{ss}(x),\nabla f(x)g(x)-\nabla g(x)f(x)\rangle\rho_{ss}(x)dx.

For nonlocal term, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}[f(y)-f(x)]k(x,y)dyg(x)\rho_{ss}(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}[g(y)-g(x)]k(x,y)dyf(x)\rho_{ss}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}[f(y)g(x)-g(y)f(x)]k(x,y)dy\rho_{ss}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x\}}f(y)g(x)[k(x,y)\rho_{ss}(x)-k(y,x)\rho_{ss}(y)]dydx.

Now we show that for every
f,g\in D(\mathcal{L})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
,

\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}=0

(5.1)

If for every
f,g\in D(\mathcal{L})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)
,

\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}\neq 0,\quad\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}\neq 0

and

\langle\mathcal{L}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}=0

(5.2)

then there exists a differential operator
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{1}
and a nonlocal integral operator
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
, so that for every

\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}=\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{2\mu}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}=\langle(\mathcal{L}_{2}+\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{2})f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)},

where
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{1\mu}

\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{2\mu}

\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
\mathcal{L}_{2}
with

Then by (

holds for every

.
And it follows that
\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{1}=\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{2}
.
However, it is impossible since

\bar{\mathcal{L}}_{2}

Thus we can exclude the case
\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}g,f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}-\langle\mathcal{L}_{2}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mu)}\neq 0
and

, and we obtain that for every

Furthermore, Since

7
Conclusion

We have developed a thermodynamic framework for general jump-diffusion processes on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Our primary contributions include deriving a mathematical formula for the entropy production rate, and establishing the associated Clausius inequality. We have shown that for a stationary process, this rate is equivalent to the relative entropy between the forward and time-reversed path measures, thereby quantifying the degree of time-irreversibility. Furthermore, we established a key theorem that reveals the equivalence between time-reversibility, zero entropy production, detailed balance, and the system’s gradient structure, providing a comprehensive criterion for identifying equilibrium.

Our results are contingent on certain technical assumptions. These include Lipschitz, linear growth, and Lyapunov conditions to ensure the well-posedness of the SDE and the existence of a (stationary) invariant measure. In order to use time reversal and the Girsanov transform for the jump diffusion, we assume that some additional assumptions hold for the jump rate and the density of its invariant measure of the jump diffusion. Although standard, these assumptions may limit the framework’s applicability to some physical systems.

This work provides new insights into non-equilibrium thermodynamic and jump diffusions. A natural extension of this work is to consider the associated entropy production fluctuation theorem
[
21
]
for jump diffusions, which is valid for systems driven arbitrarily far-from-equilibrium.
Another possible extension is to construct the Onsager reciprocal relations with broken time-reversal symmetry via this entropy production rate formula for jump diffusion.
Additionally, exploring the link between our entropy production framework and the deep learning for jump diffusion is also exciting direction, following
[
4
]
.

A Physical Layer Security Framework for IRS-Assisted Integrated Sensing and Semantic Communication Systems
Hamid Amiriara, Mahtab Mirmohseni, Ahmed Elzanaty, Yi Ma, Rahim Tafazolli

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a physical layer security framework for an intelligent reﬂecting surface-assisted integrated sensing and semantic communication system, where a multi-antenna dual-functional semantic base station serves multiple semantic communication users and monitors a potentially malicious sensing target in the presence of an eavesdropper. Both malicious sensing target and eavesdropper attempt to wiretap information from the signals transmitted to the semantic communication users. The deployment of the intelligent reﬂecting surface not only enhances physical layer security by directing a strong beam towards the semantic communication users, but also improves the localization information for the target without disclosing information about the semantic communication users. To further strengthen physical layer security, we employ joint artificial noise and dedicated sensing signal, in addition to wiretap coding. To evaluate sensing accuracy, we derive the Cramér-Rao bound for estimating the direction of arrival, and to assess the physical layer security level of the integrated sensing and semantic communication system, we determine a closed-form expression for the semantic secrecy rate. To achieve an optimal trade-off between these two competing objectives, we formulate a multi-objective optimization problem for the joint design of the base station’s beamforming vectors and the intelligent reﬂecting surface’s phase shift vector. To tackle this multi-objective optimization problem problem, the ϵ \epsilon -constraint method is employed, followed by an alternating optimization (AO)-based algorithm that leverages the classical successive convex approximation (SCA) and semidefinite relaxation (SDR) techniques. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed scheme outperforms the baseline schemes, achieving a superior trade-off between semantic secrecy rate and Cramér-Rao bound. Specifically, our proposed approach improves the sensing accuracy by 5 dB compared to the commonly adopted maximal ratio transmission (MRT) approach.

Our Contributions:

In this paper, to gain a better understanding of the interwoven sensing quality and communication confidentiality in the intelligent reﬂecting surface-assisted integrated sensing and semantic communication system, we propose a multi-objective optimization problem framework that sheds light on the trade-off between the Cramér-Rao bound and semantic secrecy rate, two KPIs at the core of estimation theory and information theory.
Succinctly, the contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

•

We introduce a security-aware integrated sensing and semantic communication system designed to mitigate security risks from both eavesdropper and malicious sensing target threats by incorporating joint artificial noise and dedicated sensing signal as physical layer security techniques.
Furthermore, we explore using an intelligent reﬂecting surface to further enhance physical layer security, simultaneously supporting semantic communication and sensing.

•

To evaluate sensing accuracy, we derive the Cramér-Rao bound for estimating the direction of arrival of a target, and to assess communication confidentiality, we determine a closed-form expression for the semantic secrecy rate. Both metrics are calculated as functions of the communication, sensing, and artificial noise beamforming vectors at the base station, as well as the phase shift vector at the intelligent reﬂecting surface.

•

We formulate a multi-objective optimization problem for the joint design of the BS’s beamforming vectors and IRS’s phase shift vector to achieve Pareto-optimal solutions that optimize the trade-off region between sensing and security.

•

By employing the
\epsilon
-constraint method, we reformulate the multi-objective optimization problem into a single-objective optimization problem. To address the non-convex nature of this problem, we apply semi-definite relaxation, successive convex approximation, and golden-section search techniques. Subsequently, we propose an algorithm to solve it based on alternating optimization, semi-definite programming, and the Gaussian randomization method methods.

•

Through numerical simulations, we validate that the proposed scheme achieves a larger trade-off region compared to baseline schemes.

III-A

Problem Formulation

To comprehensively investigate the trade-off region between CRB and SSR, we adopt an MOOP to simultaneously optimize these two objectives. Unlike SOOPs, which focus on a single metric, MOOP can generate a diverse set of Pareto-optimal solutions that address both objectives effectively

Considering the system model of Section

\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle(\cal{P}):\

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{C}^{(K+2)M_{t}\times 1},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}}{\operatorname{Minimize}}\ {\rm CRB}_{\theta}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}),

(21a)

\displaystyle\underset{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}}{\operatorname{Maximize}}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\operatorname{SSR}_{k}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}),

(21b)

s.t.

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{R}_{x})\leq P_{\max},

(21c)

\displaystyle|v_{n}|\leq 1,\ \forall n\in\mathcal{N},

(21d)

where

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we formulated an MOOP for the joint BS’s communication, sensing, and AN BF vectors and the IRS’s phase shift vector design in IRS-assisted ISASC systems to explore the trade-off region between CRB minimization and SSR maximization.
To address this complex and non-convex problem of MOOP, we initially reformulated it into a tractable SOOP using the
\epsilon
-constraint method.
Subsequently, the problem was divided into two manageable sub-problems, focusing separately on optimizing the BS BF vectors and the IRS phase shift vector.
To address the non-convex nature of the sub-problems, we applied the SDR technique to transform the non-convex constraints into convex forms and then solved them using SDP. Moreover, we utilized the successive SCA technique to iteratively approximate the non-concave CRB objective function as concave, enabling efficient convergence to a locally optimal solution.
Finally, through iterative solutions of two sub-problems, we accomplished an efficient joint optimization of the system parameters.
Our simulation results demonstrated the efficacy of the overall proposed algorithm, showing that it achieves a better balance between SSR and CRB compared to the baseline schemes.
Furthermore, our performance evaluation highlighted the significant impact of IRS elements in enhancing the system’s ability to control the electromagnetic environment, thereby improving the precision of parameter estimation and overall system performance.

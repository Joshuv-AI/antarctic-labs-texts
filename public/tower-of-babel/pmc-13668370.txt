A Journey in Time: Aesthetic Surgery Research Evolution and Aesthetic Surgery Journal
Authors: Ivona Percec, Yucel Akgul, Sebastian Cotofana, Anand Deva, Afshin Mosahebi
Published: 2026

Welcome to the special research issue of Aesthetic Surgery Journal (ASJ)! Throughout 2026, we are commemorating the 30th anniversary of ASJ by highlighting the innovations and strides put forth by each of the journal's illustrious subject sections. In this editorial, we describe the evolution and innovation of clinical and basic science research in aesthetic surgery. The Research section of the journal was founded in 2009, and in the past 16 years, it has undergone tremendous growth, evidenced by the section being ranked #3 for ASJ by way of manuscript submission volume in 2025. As its section editors (Figures 1-5), we are proud of the growth of this section, and the unique future possibilities and innovations of its contents will continue to contribute to ASJ and the plastic surgery literature—and the broader medical literature at large.

AESTHETIC RESEARCH AND ASJ

Research has long been a central tenet to the advancement of medical knowledge and clinical practice. Favorable results in the consultation room, the operating room, and beyond would not be possible without high-quality, rigorous research.

Research is a building block for all of the subject sections in ASJ—key developments in breast surgery and body contouring could not have occurred without the expansion of research into fat grafting, for example. The concept of having a separate research section in ASJ arose largely from a need to give due credit to the tremendous explosion of research in aesthetics, induced by the expansion of novel treatment modalities beyond classical surgical intervention. The Research section has seen an over 3-fold increase in submissions over the past decade. In 2010, over half of all manuscript submissions were submitted by US-based authors, followed by authors from Brazil and the United Kingdom. By 2024, submissions had nearly quadrupled, and the geographic submission mix shifted: the US- and China-based authors now contribute roughly equal shares, with Italy and Turkey also rising in prominence. These trends highlight the growing global interest in ASJ research and a corresponding expected increase in selectivity. This growth further mirrors the explosion of technological advances in aesthetic surgery, which has transformed our field from a purely surgical domain to one of complex, cutting-edge, multimodal, and multidisciplinary interventions.

In this editorial, you will find specific highlights on the temporal evolution of subjects addressed in the section, important research innovations specifically introduced by ASJ to our field, as well as future predictions and hopes for continued advancement.

In 2009, the ASJ Research section introduced a series of landmark studies that charted the course of modern aesthetic surgery. Kim et al rigorously evaluated centrifugation speeds and epinephrine concentrations in autologous fat grafting, demonstrating that spinning at 3000 RPM for 3 min optimally preserves adipocyte viability and that epinephrine imparts no additional harm.1 Strauch et al's comprehensive review of pulsed electromagnetic field therapy established it as a safe, noninvasive adjunct that significantly reduces postoperative pain and edema while promoting wound healing.2 Cui and Pu's in vitro cryopreservation study identified 0.35 mol/L trehalose as the optimal cryoprotectant concentration for long-term storage of lipoaspirates, thereby laying the groundwork for adipose “banking” in regenerative medicine.3 Finally, Sutphin et al's murine model of botulinum toxin type A immunogenicity characterized dose- and interval-dependent antibody formation, highlighting early concerns about resistance and safety in repeat neurotoxin applications.4 Collectively, these studies illustrate 4 enduring 2009 themes in ASJ's Research section—technical optimization of established procedures, adoption of innovative adjunct therapies, expansion into regenerative-medicine infrastructure, and deepening safety evaluations for biologic injectables—all of which continue to drive innovation in aesthetic surgery today.

Some of the ASJ Research section's most highly cited research has progressively broadened from technical refinements in operative practice to biologic innovations and, more recently, workforce equity. Hardy et al demonstrated through a multivariate analysis of 1753 cases that prolonged operative times independently increase the risk of postoperative complications, underscoring the need for procedural efficiency.5 Fodor and Paulseth advanced the field of regenerative adjuncts by showing that intra-articular injections of adipose-derived stromal cells can safely alleviate pain in osteoarthritic knees.6 Early comparisons of fat-grafting modalities by Sterodimas et al and Condé-Green et al established the importance of cell viability—whether using standard lipografts or stem-cell enrichment—and optimized centrifugation protocols for aspirated adipose tissue.7,8 Zielins et al synthesized these advances in their authoritative review “Autologous Fat Grafting,” framing the biological basis for fat transfer's success.9 Zaruby et al provided a biomechanical and histologic comparison of barbed vs monofilament sutures, refining choices for cosmetic closures.10 Frautschi et al delivered the first systematic appraisal of platelet-rich plasma in aesthetic surgery, paving the way for evidence-based use of this versatile biologic.11 Finally, Moak et al shifted the spotlight onto the specialty itself by documenting the “leaky pipeline” of women in plastic surgery and calling for structural solutions to close the gender gap.12 Collectively, these landmark papers chart ASJ's evolution from optimizing surgical technique toward integrating regenerative medicine, enhancing patient safety, and fostering a more inclusive and multidiscipline professional community.

More recently, ASJ's Research section has published studies seeking to better define and assess facial anatomy; animal model studies evaluating the impact of various sutures on capsular contracture, the impact of implant removal, and the histology of hemostatic net applications; as well as the testing of new 3-dimensional imaging technologies and microfocused ultrasound with visualization.13-19 The strength and volume of Research section papers on the topic of regenerative medicine is so great that ASJ launched a dedicated Regenerative Medicine section in December 2025. Recent publications in the Research section on this topic prior to the launch of the new Regenerative Medicine section are a prime example of the promising and pioneering spirit of this “brave new world” of biocellular regeneration by adipose tissue and adipose-derived stem cells, exosomes, platelet-rich plasma, and beyond.20-24 The Research section also goes beyond typical lab-based research studies—papers published in this section also cover the medicolegal challenges of facial plastic surgery, patient safety in outpatient same-day discharge ambulatory surgery centers, the growing role of private equity in plastic surgery practices, trends and complications seen in cosmetic surgery tourism, and suggestions for a more clearly defined educational framework in training for aesthetic injectors.25-29

As the official publication of The Aesthetic Society (previously known as The American Society for Aesthetic Plastic Surgery), ASJ has a close relationship with The Aesthetic Foundation, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit entity whose mission is to fund and support aesthetic research. In fact, many of ASJ's best research papers have been funded by The Aesthetic Foundation. ASJ's Research section is proud to share a common ethos with The Aesthetic Foundation—to improve and expand the safety, effectiveness, and knowledge base of aesthetic surgery and aesthetic medicine.

Since its inception, ASJ has worked to be a vanguard for innovation and discovery by publishing cutting-edge, paradigm-shifting research. Research-based innovations in ASJ's portfolio over the past 30 years have transformed over time from procedural optimization into a multidisciplinary engine of technological, biologic, and patient-centered progress in aesthetic surgery. The next 30 years is sure to be even brighter and bolder, and more visionary than ever.

PREDICTIONS FOR THE FUTURE

Plastic surgeons have always been at the forefront of innovation, creating solutions for what was previously deemed too hard or impossible. Although technical skills must be mastered in the operating room, the understanding of vascular anatomy and applying this knowledge, for example, has come from the laboratory. We are, in effect, the first to truly enable translational research—taking learnings from the anatomy laboratory directly into the operating room for the benefit of our patients.

In the field of aesthetic surgery in particular, it is our belief that incredibly profound and far-reaching innovations will arise over the next few decades. The evolution of our specialty from one that rightly focused on technical aspects of achieving the harmonious and natural ideals of form to one that is fueled by the power of science and technology has been seamless. We have already seen the incorporation of a number of breakthrough technologies in modulating skin and subcutaneous tissues through noninvasive treatments and in harvesting and unlocking the power of cells and cellular components to both reverse degeneration and improve tissue preservation and quality. In the very near future, our innovations will utilize the body's own capacity to regenerate, ushering in the next era of restoration, reconstruction, and augmentation, free of alloplasts, as we transition into becoming plastic, reconstructive, and regenerative surgeons. We will be agents in delivering and preserving youth and vitality to the long, productive, and prosperous lives of our patients.

By the time ASJ turns 40, 10 years from now, we predict that the level of evidence of studies overall in the section will surpass those of today and will include the following innovations. Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven personalized treatment algorithms will streamline procedure planning, minimize complication rates, and deliver consistently optimized, patient-specific aesthetic and functional outcomes. Nanocarrier-based delivery of targeted regenerative factors will dramatically enhance scar remodeling and tissue quality with single-injection, controlled-release therapies, and senolytic nanoparticle injections targeting dermal fibroblast aging will aid in clearing senescent cells to restore collagen homeostasis and achieve truly regenerative wrinkle reduction. In our lifetime, we would also hope to perform less invasive facelifts augmented by smart, nanoparticle-impregnated sutures—delivering targeted anti-aging therapeutics while mechanically reinforcing tissues to both lift and prevent future signs of aging. To address the ever-present effects of aging, bespoke prevention and rejuvenation protocols through multi-omic biomarker panels coupled with AI-driven morphologic modeling will enable a more-tailored-than-ever approach to each patient's molecular aging profile, rather than one-size-fits-all interventions. Lastly, 4-dimensional dynamic volumetric imaging with predictive aging simulations will allow surgeons and patients to visualize personalized aging trajectories and preplan interventions that “grow with” the patient over decades.

CONCLUSIONS

The ASJ Research section will continue to chart the course of aesthetic surgery by fostering a seamless bench-to-bedside pipeline—championing rigorous basic science discoveries, validating emerging technologies through well-designed clinical trials, and distilling those findings into practical guidelines for everyday practice. In doing so, ASJ ensures that every innovation—from biomolecular therapies and advanced imaging to smart, nanoparticle-enhanced materials—ultimately empowers surgeons to deliver treatments that are not only safer and more effective but also durable enough to preserve and enhance aesthetic anatomy for years to come.

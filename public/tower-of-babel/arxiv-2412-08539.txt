The conjugation representation of GL 2 \GL_{2} and SL 2 \SL_{2} over finite local rings
Nariel Monteiro and Alexander Stasinski

Abstract
. The conjugation representation of a finite group G G is the complex permutation module defined by the action of G G on itself by conjugation. Addressing a problem raised by Hain motivated by the study of a Hecke action on iterated Shimura integrals, Tiep proved that for G = SL 2 ⁡ ( ℤ / p r ) G=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}) , where r ≥ 1 r\geq 1 and p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 is a prime, any irreducible representation of G G that is trivial on the centre of G G is contained in the conjugation representation. Moreover, Tiep asked whether this can be generalised to p = 2 p=2 or 3 3 . We answer the Hain–Tiep question in the affirmative and also prove analogous statements for SL 2 \SL_{2} and GL 2 \GL_{2} over any finite local principal ideal ring with residue field of odd characteristic.



1.
Introduction

For a finite group
G
, the
conjugation representation
is
the complex permutation representation induced by the action of
G

on itself by conjugation. The character of this representations is the
conjugation character
of
G
.

A basic problem is to determine the irreducible constituents of the
conjugation character. It is easy to see that any such constituent
must be trivial on the centre of
G
. Two early studies of the
problem were Frame
[
3
]
and Roth
[
15
]
,
the latter of which conjectured that every irreducible representation
of
G
that is trivial on the centre is contained in the conjugation
character. Counter-examples were, however, quickly given by Frame
in his MathSciNet review of
[
15
]
and by Formanek
in
[
2
]
. The problem was subsequently taken up
by Passman
[
14
]
who proved, among other things,
that every irreducible character of a symmetric group (on at least
three letters) is contained in the conjugation character.
In
[
7
]
, Heide and Zalesski proved that the simple
alternating groups and the sporadic groups satisfy the same property.
It was then proved by Heide, Saxl, Tiep and Zalesski
[
6
]

that any finite simple group of Lie type
G
satisfies the same property,
unless
G=\text{PSU}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and
n\geq 3
is coprime to
2(q+1)

(where exactly one irreducible character fails to be a constituent).
It is therefore known that every finite non-abelian simple group other
than these
\text{PSU}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
counter-examples,
has the property that every irreducible character appears in the conjugation
character.

In recent work motivated by a problem raised by Hain
[
4
]
arising
in the study of a Hecke action on iterated Shimura integrals, Tiep

[
21
]
proved that for
G=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r})
,
where
r\geq 1
and
p\geq 5
is a prime, every irreducible character
of
G
that is trivial on the centre of
G
is contained in the
conjugation character. Tiep also raised the question of whether
this can be generalised to
p=2
or
3
.

In the present paper we answer the Hain–Tiep question in the affirmative (
8.11
).
Let
\mathcal{O}
be a DVR with finite residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic

p
and maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
and let
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}^{i}
for
any integer
i\geq 1
.
We moreover show that for
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
,
with
p>2
, any irreducible character that is trivial on the centre of
G
is contained in the conjugation character (
6.18
).

Our method of proof is quite different from that of Tiep
[
21
]

for
p\geq 5
, even though both approaches show that
for every irreducible character
\rho
of
G
that is trivial on
the centre, there is an element
g\in G
such that
\rho
restricted
to the centraliser
C_{G}(g)
contains the trivial character. Moreover, both approaches exploit the fact that most irreducible characters of
G
are induced from certain subgroups and that Mackey’s formula implies that the required condition can be checked on the inducing representation.
However, the elements
g
used by Tiep in
[
21
, (7.7)]

do not have any useful analogues for
p=2
or
3
, as for
p=3
,

g
is scalar modulo
p
, hence does not satisfy
[
21
, Lemma 8]

and for
p=2
, the element
t(1-p)
used to define
g
for
g\geq 5

does not exist in general. We have therefore taken a different approach
to the elements
g
and in Section
5
introduce
the elements
g_{\hat{\beta}}
attached to a regular matrix
\hat{\beta}
,
which represents the Clifford orbit of an irreducible character of
G
. Therefore,
in our approach, the elements
g_{\hat{\beta}}
used will vary with
the representations whose restriction to
C_{G}(g)
we want to contain
the trivial character.

Our proof does not require the full construction of characters of

\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
(see
[
17
,
18
]
) or
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
(see
[
19
,
10
,
16
]
for
p\neq 2
and
[
20
,
12
,
13
]
for the case
\mathcal{O}_{r}=\mathbb{Z}/2^{r}
).
In particular, our proofs are logically independent of any of these
constructions, except that we use a Heisenberg lift construction from

[
18
]
in Section
6.2
.
On the other hand, we have had to develop several new representation
theoretic constructions for
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
, namely Proposition
4.1
,
the results in Section
6.3

leading up to and including Proposition
6.14

and everything in Sections
7
and
8
.
In addition to this, what we need from the representation theory of

\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
are well-known properties of
the characters
\psi_{\beta}
, their stabilisers and basic facts
from Clifford theory and regular representations, all of which are
summarised in Section
3
.

2.
Preliminary lemmas

In this section, we collect some lemmas for general finite groups,
which relate the conjugation character, induced representations, and
restrictions to centralisers.

We will use
\mathbf{1}
to denote the trivial character of a given finite group. It will always be clear from the context which group we are talking about.

The following lemma is well-known (see
[
15
, Lemma 1.5]

or
[
21
, Lemma 3]
). It will be used implicitly several times in the following.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\chi\in\Irr(G)
. Then
\chi
is
contained in the conjugation representation of
G
if and only if
there exists a
g\in G
such that
\chi|_{C_{G}(g)}
contains the
trivial character.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

G
be a finite group with centre
Z
and
H\leq G
a subgroup.
Let
\sigma\in\Irr(H)
. Then
\Ind_{H}^{G}\sigma
is
trivial on
Z
if and only if
\sigma
is trivial on
H\cap Z
.

Proof.

By the Mackey intertwining number formula,

\displaystyle\langle(\Ind_{H}^{G}\sigma)|_{Z},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Ind_{H}^{G}\sigma,\Ind_{Z}^{G}\mathbf{1}\rangle=\sum_{h\in H\backslash G/Z}\langle\sigma|_{H\cap Z},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=|H\backslash G/Z|\cdot\langle\sigma|_{H\cap Z},\mathbf{1}\rangle,

whence the result.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N
,
H
and
C
be three subgroups of a finite group
G

such that
N\leq H
and let
\theta\in\Irr(N)
. Then the following
holds.

(1)

If
\theta|_{N\cap C}
contains
\mathbf{1}
,
then
(\Ind_{N}^{H}\theta)|_{H\cap C}
contains
\mathbf{1}
.

(2)

Suppose that
\Ind_{N}^{H}\theta
is a multiple
of some
\eta\in\Irr(H)
. If
\theta|_{N\cap C}
contains
\mathbf{1}
,
then
\eta|_{H\cap C}
contains
\mathbf{1}
.

Proof.

By the Mackey intertwining number formula,

\displaystyle\langle(\Ind_{N}^{H}\theta)|_{H\cap C},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Ind_{N}^{H}\theta,\Ind_{H\cap C}^{H}\mathbf{1}\rangle=\sum_{h\in N\backslash H/(H\cap C)}\langle\theta|_{N\cap\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{C}}^{h}\hskip-1.0pt{C}},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\theta|_{N\cap C},\mathbf{1}\rangle.

Thus, if the right-hand side is positive, then so is the left-hand
side.

For the second part, note that by the first part, if
\theta|_{N\cap C}

contains
\mathbf{1}
, then some irreducible constituent
of
\Ind_{N}^{H}\theta
must contain
\mathbf{1}
when
restricted to
H\cap C
, so if
\Ind_{N}^{H}\theta
is a multiple
of a single irreducible constituent
\eta
, then
\eta|_{H\cap C}

must contain
\mathbf{1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

G
be a finite group with centre
Z
and let
N\trianglelefteqslant G

be a normal subgroup. Let
Z^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
Z
.
Let
\sigma\in\Irr(N)
and assume that
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\sigma)

is trivial on
Z^{\prime}
. Assume that there exists a
g\in N
such that
\rho
restricted
to
C_{G}(g)
contains the trivial character. Then there exists an
h\in N
such that

\sigma
restricted to
C_{N}(h)
contains the trivial character.

Proof.

Let
C:=C_{G}(g)
. By hypothesis,
\langle\rho,\Ind_{C}^{G}\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0

and therefore

0\neq\langle\Ind_{N}^{G}\sigma,\Ind_{C}^{G}\mathbf{1}\rangle=\sum_{x\in N\backslash G/C}\langle\sigma|_{N\cap\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{C}}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{C}},\mathbf{1}\rangle,

so there exists an
x\in G
such that

\langle\sigma|_{N\cap\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{C}}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{C}},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0.

Note that
N\cap\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{C}}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{C}=N\cap C_{G}(\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{g}}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{g})=C_{N}(\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{g}}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{g})

and as
g\in N
and
N
is normal in
G
, we have
\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{g}}{}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{g}\in N
. Letting
h=\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{g}}^{x}\hskip-1.0pt{g}
,
we conclude that
\sigma
restricted to
C_{N}(h)
contains the
trivial character.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group with centre
Z
and let
N\trianglelefteqslant G

be a normal subgroup. Assume that for every
\rho\in\Irr(G)
that
is trivial on
Z
, there exists a
g\in N
such that
\rho
restricted
to
C_{G}(g)
contains the trivial character. Then, for any
\sigma\in\Irr(N)

that is trivial on
Z(N)
, there exists an
h\in N
such that

\sigma
restricted to
C_{N}(h)
contains the trivial character.

Proof.

Assume that
\sigma\in\Irr(N)
is trivial on
Z(N)
. As
N\cap Z\subseteq Z(N)
,
2.2
implies that there exists a
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\sigma)
that is trivial on
Z
. By hypothesis, there exists a
g\in N
such that
\rho
restricted
to
C_{G}(g)
contains the trivial character. The conclusion now follows from
2.4
.
∎

3.
Basics of the characters of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})

In this section we summarise the construction of regular characters
of the groups
G
via Clifford theory, following the approach laid
out in
[
17
]
. We only state the results that
we will need in the following namely, the description of regular characters
as induced from stabilisers of characters
\psi_{\beta}
, which is
much weaker than the full construction of regular characters as given
in
[
18
]
.

For any integer
i
such that
r\geq i\geq 1
, let
K^{i}
be the
kernel of the homomorphism
\rho_{i}:G\rightarrow G_{i}
. For
i>r

we define
K^{i}=\{1\}
. A fundamental fact that we will use tacitly
in the following is the commutator relation

[K^{i},K^{j}]\subseteq K^{i+j},

which can be proved by direct matrix computations. In particular,
it shows that
K^{l}
is abelian and that it centralises the group

K^{l^{\prime}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(\mathbf{G})
, so that

\mathfrak{g}=\begin{cases}\M_{2}=\mathfrak{gl}_{2}&\text{if }\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2},\\
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}&\text{if }\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}.\end{cases}

Assume from now on that
i\geq r/2
. Then the group
K^{i}
is abelian
and we have a
G
-equivariant isomorphism

K^{i}\mathbin{\kern 3.00003pt\widetilde{\hphantom{\hskip 11.00008pt}}\kern-15.50005pt\longrightarrow\kern 1.00006pt}\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r-i}),\qquad 1+\pi^{i}X\longmapsto\rho_{r-i}(X).

From now on, and throughout the rest of the paper, let
\psi:\mathcal{O}_{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

be a fixed additive character that does not factor through
\mathcal{O}_{r-1}
.
When
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
or
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p\neq 2
, the trace form

(x,y)\mapsto\Tr(xy)
on
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r-i})
is non-degenerate, so we have
G
-equivariant isomorphism

(3.1)

\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r-i})\mathbin{\kern 3.00003pt\widetilde{\hphantom{\hskip 11.00008pt}}\kern-15.50005pt\longrightarrow\kern 1.00006pt}\Irr(K^{i}),\qquad\beta\longmapsto\psi_{\beta},

where
G
acts on
\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r-i})
via its quotient
G_{r-i}
.

\psi_{\beta}(I+\pi^{i}X):=\psi(\Tr(\hat{\beta}\pi^{i}X))

and
\hat{\beta}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
is an arbitrary lift of
\beta

(under the map
\rho_{r-i}
). When
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p=2
, restriction
from
\Irr(K_{\GL}^{i})
to
\Irr(K_{\SL}^{i})
is a surjective homomorphism
whose kernel consists of those
\psi_{\beta}
where
\beta
is a
scalar matrix. This induces an isomorphism

(3.2)

\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r-i})/\mathcal{O}_{r-i}I\mathbin{\kern 3.00003pt\widetilde{\hphantom{\hskip 11.00008pt}}\kern-15.50005pt\longrightarrow\kern 1.00006pt}\Irr(K_{\SL}^{i}),\qquad[\beta]\longmapsto\psi_{[\beta]}:=\Res_{K_{\SL}^{i}}^{K_{\GL}^{i}}\psi_{\beta},

where
[\beta]
denotes the coset of
\beta
modulo scalar matrices.

Now let
i=l
and consider
\psi_{\beta}\in\Irr(K^{l})
. When
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}

or
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p\neq 2
, the stabiliser
\Stab_{G}(\psi_{\beta}):=\{g\in G\mid\psi_{\beta}(g^{-1}kg)=\psi_{\beta}(k),\ \forall k\in K^{l}\}

is given by

(3.3)

\Stab_{G}(\psi_{\beta})=C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}},

for any lift
\hat{\beta}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
of
\beta
(see
[
8
, Corollary 3.7]

for the case
\GL_{2}
; the same argument works for
\SL_{2}
with

p\neq 2
). When
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p=2
, the stabiliser
\Stab_{G}(\psi_{[\beta]})

is in general strictly bigger than
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}
and
was determined for
\mathcal{O}_{r}
of characteristic
2
in
[
5
]

and more generally in
[
11
]
. We will determine
\Stab_{G}(\psi_{[\beta]})

for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{r})
later in this paper, where it is needed.

Definition 3.1
.

A representation (or character)
\rho
of
G
(with
r\geq 2
) is
called
primitive
if it does not factor through
G_{r-1}
and
is called
twist primitive
if
\chi\otimes\rho
(or
\chi\rho
)
is primitive for all one-dimensional representations (linear characters)

\chi
of
G
.

Note that twist primitive characters are called primitive in
[
17
]
.
By Clifford’s theorem, the restriction of any
\rho\in\Irr(G)
to

K^{l}
contains a full orbit of characters
\psi_{\beta}
under
the action of
G
. Thus, by the above,
\rho
uniquely determines
a conjugation orbit
\Omega_{l^{\prime}}(\rho)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
(or

\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})/\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}I
when
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p=2
). Similarly,
restricting to
K^{r-1}
determines an orbit
\Omega_{r-1}(\rho)\subseteq\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

(or
\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})/\mathbb{F}_{q}I
when
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p=2
). Recall
that a matrix
A\in\M_{n}(K)
, where
K
is a field, is called regular
if it is conjugate to its companion matrix. It is well-known that

A
is regular if and only if the centraliser
C_{\M_{n}(K)}(A)

consists of polynomials in
A
, that is, if
C_{\M_{n}(K)}(A)=\{f(A)\mid f(X)\in K[X]\}
.
In particular, if
A\in\M_{2}(K)
is regular, then
C_{\M_{n}(K)}(A)=\{aI+bA\mid a,b\in K\}
.
By changing the field
K
to the ring
\mathcal{O}_{r}
one can define regularity
for matrices in
\M_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
in an analogous way and by a theorem
of Hill
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
a matrix
A\in\M_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{r})

is regular iff its image
\rho_{1}(A)\in\M_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is regular
iff
C_{\M_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{r})}(A)=\{aI+bA\mid a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\}
iff
A

is
\GL_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
-conjugate to its companion matrix.

Definition 3.2
.

A character
\rho\in\Irr(G)
is called
regular
if its orbit

\Omega_{r-1}(\rho)
consists of matrices that are regular in
\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

(or cosets of regular matrices modulo
\mathbb{F}_{q}I
when
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}

and
p=2
).

If
H
is a finite group,
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
is a subgroup,
\rho\in\Irr(H)

and
\rho^{\prime}\in\Irr(H^{\prime})
, we will write
\rho\in\Irr(H\mid\rho^{\prime})

to indicate that
\rho
lies above
\rho^{\prime}
(in the sense of Clifford
theory), that is, that
\rho|_{H^{\prime}}
contains
\rho^{\prime}
as an irreducible
constituent.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

\rho\in\Irr(G)
.

(1)

If
\rho
is twist
primitive, then
\rho
is regular.

(2)

Assume that
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
. If
\rho
is primitive, then
\rho

is regular.

(3)

Assume that
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
or
p\neq 2
. If
\rho
is regular, then
there is an element
\beta\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
such that
\beta
is
regular in
\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
and a
\sigma\in\Irr(C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\mid\psi_{\beta})
,
for any choice of lift
\hat{\beta}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
of
\beta
,
such that

\rho=\Ind_{C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}}^{G}\sigma.

(4)

Assume that
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p=2
. If
\rho
is regular, then
there is a regular element
\beta\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
and a
\sigma\in\Irr(\Stab_{G}(\psi_{[\beta]})\mid\psi_{[\beta]})
,
such that

\rho=\Ind_{\Stab_{G}(\psi_{[\beta]})}^{G}\sigma.

Moreover, the above statements also hold when
\beta
is replaced
by any other element in its orbit
\Omega_{l^{\prime}}(\rho)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
or
p\neq 2
and let
\beta\in\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

be such that
\beta\in\Omega_{r-1}(\rho)
. If
\beta
is scalar
and
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
, then
\rho
is not twist primitive (see
[
17
, Section 2.1]
)
and if
\beta
is scalar and
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
, then
\beta=0
so

\rho
is not even primitive. Thus, in either case, if
\rho
is
twist primitive, then
\beta
is non-scalar and as the scalar matrices
are the only non-regular elements in
\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, this implies
that
\beta
is regular and hence that
\rho
is regular. Now assume
that
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
p=2
and let
[\beta]\in\Omega_{r-1}(\rho)
.
If
\beta
is scalar, then clearly
[\beta]=[0]
and
\psi_{[0]}=\mathbf{1}
,
so
\rho
is not primitive. Thus, if
\rho
is twist primitive,
then
\beta
is non-scalar, so as in the previous case,
\rho
is
regular.

The second statement follows from the proof of the first statement,
as we have actually showed that when
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
and
\rho
is
primitive, then
\Omega_{r-1}(\rho)
contains a non-scalar element

\beta
(or a non-zero class
[\beta]
when
p=2
) and a non-scalar
element in
\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is regular.

The third statement follows immediately from standard Clifford theory
(see, e.g.,
[
9
, (6.11)]
) together with (
3.1
)
and (
3.3
).
The fourth statement follows from the same Clifford theory result
together with (
3.2
).
The last assertion follows by conjugation.
∎

By definition, a regular element in
\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
,
i\geq 1
,
is
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
-conjugate to its companion matrix, which is
of the form
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\Delta&\tau\end{pmatrix}
, for
\Delta,\tau\in\mathcal{O}_{i}
. Thus it is also
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
-conjugate
to
\begin{pmatrix}\tau&1\\
\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}
, which is the form we will use henceforth. As
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\times}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
, every regular element in
\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
is
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
-conjugate
to an element of the form
\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}
, where
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\times}
. In particular, when
i=l^{\prime}
,
every regular element in
\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
is
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
-conjugate
to an element

\beta:=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Delta,\tau\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}},\ \lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}.

If
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
p\neq 2
, then by (
3.1
)
the
\psi_{\beta}\in\Irr(K^{l})
that are contained in regular representations
are parametrised by
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
, that is, with
\tau=0
. The following simple lemma shows that the
analogous fact holds when
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
, for any
p
,
as long as we consider regular representations that are trivial on
the centre
Z
of
G
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and let
\rho\in\Irr(G)
be a regular representation
that is trivial on
K^{l}\cap Z
. Then
\rho
contains a character

\psi_{\beta}
, where
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
(that is,
\Tr(\beta)=0
).

Proof.

As was discussed in the beginning of this section,
\rho
being regular
implies that it contains a
\psi_{\beta}
with
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&1\\
\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
. Since
\rho
is trivial on
Z
, it is trivial when restricted
to
K^{l}\cap Z
. Thus, by Clifford’s theorem,

\rho|_{K^{l}\cap Z}=\rho(1)\psi_{\beta}|_{K^{l}\cap Z}=\rho(1)\cdot\mathbf{1},

hence
\psi_{\beta}|_{K^{l}\cap Z}=\mathbf{1}
. We
have
K^{l}\cap Z=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1+\pi^{l}a&0\\
0&1+\pi^{l}a\end{pmatrix}\mid a\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\right\}
, so

\psi_{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}1+\pi^{l}a&0\\
0&1+\pi^{l}a\end{pmatrix}=\psi(\hat{\tau}\pi^{l}a)=1,

for all
a
(where
\hat{\tau}\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
is an arbitrary lift of

\tau
). Since
\psi(\mathfrak{p}^{r-1})\neq 1
, this implies that
\hat{\tau}\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}
,
hence
\tau=0
.
∎

4.
Extensions of
\psi_{\beta}
to
U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}

In this section we show that the characters
\psi_{\beta}
introduced
in the previous section extend “by the same formula” to a certain
group
U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}
which will play a central role in the rest
of the paper.

In the previous section we saw that every regular element in
\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})

is
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
-conjugate to an element

\beta=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Delta,\tau\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}},\ \lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}.

For
\beta
as above, we will write

\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\tau}&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r}),

where
\hat{\Delta},\hat{\tau},\hat{\lambda}\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
are some
chosen lifts of
\Delta
,
\tau
and
\lambda
, respectively. (Note
that not all lifts of
\beta
are of this form, as the bottom right
entry of
\hat{\beta}
is
0
.) Let

v=\min\{\val(\Delta),\val(\tau)\},

where
\val(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}
is defined to be the largest
integer in
[0,l^{\prime}]
such that
x\in\mathfrak{p}^{\val(x)}
. Thus in particular,

\hat{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{p}^{v}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{\tau}\in\mathfrak{p}^{v}.

Moreover,
v=l^{\prime}
if and only if
\Delta=\tau=0
.

For any
i\geq 0
, define the subgroup

U^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathfrak{p}^{i}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

of
G
. We note that

(4.1)

U^{l^{\prime}-v}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}.

Indeed,
\rho_{l^{\prime}}(C_{G}(\hat{\beta}))=C_{G_{l^{\prime}}}(\beta)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x+\tau y&\lambda y\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta y&x\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}\right\}\cap G_{l^{\prime}}\supseteq\rho_{l^{\prime}}(U^{l^{\prime}-v})
, since for
y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}-v}
, we have
\Delta y\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}}^{l^{\prime}}

and
\tau y\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}}^{l^{\prime}}
. The same argument shows that

(4.2)

U^{l-v}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\beta
and
\hat{\beta}
be as above. The function
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}:U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

given by

\psi_{\hat{\beta}}(x)=\psi(\Tr(\hat{\beta}(x-1))),\qquad\text{for }x\in U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l},

is a linear character. If
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\tau}^{\prime}&\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}\\
(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
is another lift of
\beta
, then
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}=\psi_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}

if and only if
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\equiv(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}\mod{\mathfrak{p}}^{l+v}
.
Moreover, every element of
\Irr(U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\mid\psi_{\beta})

is of the form
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
.

Proof.

Any element of
U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}
has the form

\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l}&b\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}\\
c\pi^{l}&1+d\pi^{l}\end{pmatrix}

for some
a,b,c,d\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
and thus

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{\beta}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l}&b\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}\\
c\pi^{l}&1+d\pi^{l}\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\Tr\left(\hat{\beta}\begin{pmatrix}a\pi^{l}&b\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}\\
c\pi^{l}&d\pi^{l}\end{pmatrix}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\psi(\hat{\tau}a\pi^{l}+\hat{\lambda}c\pi^{l}+b\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}).

Note that
\hat{\tau}a\pi^{l}
and
\hat{\lambda}c\pi^{l}
are independent
of the choices of lifts
\hat{\tau}
and
\hat{\lambda}
of
\tau

and
\lambda
, respectively, and therefore
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}

only depends on the image of
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}
mod

\mathfrak{p}^{l+v}
.

We first show that
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
is a linear character of

U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}
. For any
a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},d^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{\beta}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l}&b\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}\\
c\pi^{l}&1+d\pi^{l}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+a^{\prime}\pi^{l}&b^{\prime}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}\\
c^{\prime}\pi^{l}&1+d^{\prime}\pi^{l}\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{\hat{\beta}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}1+(a+a^{\prime})\pi^{l}+bc^{\prime}\pi^{r-v}&((b+b^{\prime})+(ab^{\prime}+bd^{\prime})\pi^{l})\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}\\
(c+c^{\prime})\pi^{l}&\cdots\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi(\hat{\tau}(a+a^{\prime})\pi^{l}+\hat{\tau}bc^{\prime}\pi^{r-v}+\hat{\lambda}(c+c^{\prime})\pi^{l}+((b+b^{\prime})+(ab^{\prime}+bd^{\prime})\pi^{l})\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v})

\displaystyle=\psi(\hat{\tau}(a+a^{\prime})\pi^{l}+\hat{\lambda}(c+c^{\prime})\pi^{l}+(b+b^{\prime})\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v})\quad(\text{since }\hat{\Delta},\hat{\tau}\in\mathfrak{p}^{v})

\displaystyle=\psi(\hat{\tau}a\pi^{l}+\hat{\lambda}c\pi^{l}+b\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v})\psi(\hat{\tau}a^{\prime}\pi^{l}+\hat{\lambda}c^{\prime}\pi^{l}+b^{\prime}\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}).

Thus
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
is a homomorphism.

We have already seen that
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
only depends on
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}

mod
\mathfrak{p}^{l+v}
. Conversely, if
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}=\psi_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}
,
then

\psi(\hat{\tau}a\pi^{l}+\hat{\lambda}c\pi^{l}+b\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v})=\psi(\hat{\tau}^{\prime}a\pi^{l}+\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}c\pi^{l}+b(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}),

for all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
(
\hat{\tau}a\pi^{l}=\hat{\tau}^{\prime}a\pi^{l}

and
\hat{\lambda}c\pi^{l}=\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}c\pi^{l}
in
\mathcal{O}_{r}
),
so
\psi(b\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}(\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}-(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}))=1

for all
b
; hence
\pi^{l^{\prime}-v}(\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}-(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime})=0

(as
\psi
is non-trivial on
\mathfrak{p}^{r-1}
), that is,

\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}-(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{p}^{r-(l^{\prime}-v)}=\mathfrak{p}^{l+v}.

We now show that every element of
\Irr(U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\mid\psi_{\beta})

is of the form
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
. By definition, every
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}

is an extension of
\psi_{\beta}
. The number of elements of
\Irr(U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\mid\psi_{\beta})

is
|U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}/K^{l}|=|U^{l^{\prime}-v}/U^{l}|=q^{v+1}
. But we have
shown that the number of distinct characters
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}

is equal to the number of distinct lifts of
\lambda^{-1}\Delta\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}

to
\mathcal{O}_{l+v}
, which also equals
q^{v+1}
. Thus the
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}

exhaust
\Irr(U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\mid\psi_{\beta})
.
∎

5.
Special group elements
and intersections with their centralisers

As expressed in Lemma
2.1
,
a character
\rho\in\Irr(G)
is contained in the conjugation character
if and only if
\rho
restricted to some centraliser
C_{G}(g)
,

g\in G
, contains the trivial character. In this section we will define a family of elements that satisfy
this, for the various irreducible characters of
G
with
p\neq 2
, as well as for many characters of
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
when
p=2
.

For any
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
,
\hat{\lambda}\in\mathcal{O}_{r}^{\times}
, we define the element

g_{\hat{\beta}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\hat{\lambda}\\
-\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&1-\hat{\Delta}\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r}).

Remark 5.1
.

Even though
g_{\hat{\beta}}
is closely related to
\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&1+\hat{\Delta}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\in\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
, this latter element would not work for our purposes as the proof
of the crucial Lemma
5.3

would fail.

Since
g_{\hat{\beta}}
is always regular as an element of
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
,
its centraliser in
G
is

(5.1)

C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=\mathcal{O}_{r}[g_{\hat{\beta}}]\cap G=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\hat{\lambda}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&x-y\hat{\Delta}\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\right\}\cap G.

For an element
x\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
and an integer
r\geq i\geq 1
, we will
write
x_{i}:=\rho_{i}(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{i}
for the image of
x\mod\mathfrak{p}^{i}
.
This notation is extended to matrices in
\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
in the
obvious way. Let

w:=\val(\hat{\Delta}_{l}).

Note that if
v<l^{\prime}
, then
w=\val(\hat{\Delta}_{l})=\val(\Delta)=v
,
but if
v=l^{\prime}
(i.e.,
\Delta=0
), then we can have
w=l^{\prime}
or
l

(e.g., if
\hat{\Delta}=0
, then
w=l
but if
\hat{\Delta}=\pi^{l^{\prime}}
,
then
w=l^{\prime}
). Note also that we always have
l^{\prime}-v\leq l-w
, so
U^{l-w}\subseteq U^{l^{\prime}-v}
and therefore (
4.2
) implies that

(5.2)

U^{l-w}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}),\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}
and let
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
be a lift of
\beta
. Then

(1)

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
;

(2)

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
.

Proof.

Let
h\in C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
. Then

\displaystyle h_{l^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in C_{G_{l^{\prime}}}(\beta)\cap C_{G_{l^{\prime}}}\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\lambda\\
-\lambda^{-1}\Delta&1-\Delta\end{smallmatrix}\Big)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\lambda\\
b\lambda^{-1}\Delta&a\end{pmatrix}\mid a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}\right\}\cap\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\lambda\\
-y\lambda^{-1}\Delta&x-y\Delta\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}\right\}.

A matrix in this intersection is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\lambda\\
b\lambda^{-1}\Delta&a\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\lambda\\
-y\lambda^{-1}\Delta&x-y\Delta\end{pmatrix},

which implies that
y\Delta=0
in
\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}
, that is,
y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}-v}.

Hence

h_{l^{\prime}}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\mid x\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}},\,y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}-v}\right\}\cap G_{l^{\prime}},

and therefore
h\in ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}
. We have now established that

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})\subseteq ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}},

so trivially also
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})\subseteq ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
.
On the other hand,
ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}

by (
4.1
), so
ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})

and therefore

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}}).

Next, let
h\in C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
.
Then

\displaystyle h_{l}

\displaystyle\in C_{G_{l}}(\hat{\beta}_{l})\cap C_{G_{l}}\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
-\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&1-\hat{\Delta}_{l}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
b\hat{\Delta}_{l}&a\end{pmatrix}\mid a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{l}\right\}\cap\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&x-y\hat{\Delta}_{l}\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{l}\right\}.

A matrix in this intersection is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
b\hat{\Delta}_{l}&a\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&x-y\hat{\Delta}_{l}\end{pmatrix},

which implies that

y\hat{\Delta}_{l}=0.

If
w=l
(i.e.,
\hat{\Delta}_{l}=0
), then trivially
y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}=\mathfrak{p}^{0}=\mathcal{O}_{l}
.
Otherwise
w<l
and therefore
y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}
. Thus, in either
case,
y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}
and

h_{l}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\mid x\in\mathcal{O}_{l},\,y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}\right\}\cap G_{l},

so that
h\in ZU^{l-w}K^{l}
. Thus

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})\subseteq ZU^{l-w}K^{l}

and by the same argument as above, but using (
4.2
),
we obtain

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}}).

∎

Even though the following lemma has an extremely simple proof, it
embodies one of the key properties (together with Lemma
5.2
)
that we need about the elements
g_{\hat{\beta}}
and will be used
repeatedly in the proof of our main theorems.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}),\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}
. For every lift
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
of
\beta
, the restriction of
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
to
U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})

is the trivial character.

Proof.

Let
h\in U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
. By (
5.1
),

h
is of the form

h=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\hat{\lambda}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&x-y\hat{\Delta}\end{pmatrix},\qquad x\in 1+\mathfrak{p}^{l},\ y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}-v}

and thus

\displaystyle\psi_{\hat{\beta}}(h)

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\Tr\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x-1&y\hat{\lambda}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&x-1-y\hat{\Delta}\end{pmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\psi(-y\hat{\Delta}+\hat{\Delta}y)=\psi(0)=1.

∎

6.
The case when
p
is odd

The goal of this section is to prove that our main theorem for
p\neq 2
,
that is, that when
p\neq 2
, any
\rho\in\Irr(G)
that is trivial
on
Z
is contained in the conjugation character of
G
. The non-twist
primitive characters are relatively easy to take care of in this case,
so the main focus is regular representations and we show that if
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})

or
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
p\neq 2
, then any regular representation
that is trivial on
Z
is contained in the conjugation character
of
G
. Our approach necessitates three cases: The case when
r=2l

is, as always in the representation theory of
G
, easier; the case
when
r\neq 2l
and
v<l^{\prime}
is dealt with via a ‘Heisenberg lift’ from

U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}
to
U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l^{\prime}}
and the case
v=l^{\prime}
requires
a different approach to constructing the regular characters. These
cases are dealt with in their respective subsections.

Our first step is to prove results that enable us to handle non-twist
primitive characters. If
\rho\in\Irr(G)
is not twist primitive,
then, by definition, there is a linear character
\chi
of
G
such
that
\chi\rho
is trivial on
K^{r-1}
. Since
\chi
is trivial
on
[G,G]
, this implies that
\rho
is trivial on
[G,G]\cap K^{r-1}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume that
either
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
or
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
p\neq 2
.
Then

[G,G]\cap K^{r-1}\supseteq\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})\cap K^{r-1}=:K_{\SL}^{r-1}.

Proof.

When
r=2l
, direct calculation shows that for
1+\pi^{l}X\in K^{l}

and
1+\pi^{l-1}Y\in K^{l-1}
,

[1+\pi^{l}X,1+\pi^{l-1}Y]=1+\pi^{r-1}(XY-YX).

Similarly, when
r=2l^{\prime}+1
, for
1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}X,1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}Y\in K^{l^{\prime}}
,

[1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}X,1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}Y]=1+\pi^{r-1}(XY-YX).

We have
K_{\SL}^{r-1}=1+\pi^{r-1}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and it is well-known
that when
p\neq 2
,
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is a perfect Lie algebra.
Thus, since
1+\pi^{r-1}(XY-YX)
only depends on the images of
X

and
Y
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
, every element of
K_{\SL}^{r-1}
is in
[G,G]
,
hence in
[G,G]\cap K^{r-1}
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

The above lemma holds even for
\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
when
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
as well as for
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
when
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{3}
, despite
the fact that in these cases we don’t have
[G,G]\supseteq\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume
that either
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
or
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and

p\neq 2
. Let
\rho
be an irreducible character of
G
that
factors through a character
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\rho\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
of
G_{r-1}
. If
\rho

is trivial on
Z
, then
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\rho\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is trivial on
Z_{r-1}
.

Proof.

A sufficient condition for the assertion is that
ZK^{r-1}/K^{r-1}\cong Z_{r-1}
,
that is, that
\rho_{r-1}:Z\rightarrow Z_{r-1}
is surjective.
If
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
,
Z\cong\mathcal{O}_{r}^{\times}
, and it is
well-known that this maps surjectively onto
\mathcal{O}_{r-1}^{\times}
.
For
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
with
p
odd, Hensel’s lemma (or an easy
direct calculation) implies that any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{i}
such that
a^{2}=1

lifts to an
\hat{a}\in\mathcal{O}_{i+1}
such that
a^{2}=1
, so also in
this case,
\rho_{r-1}
maps
Z
onto
Z_{r-1}
.
∎

The following lemma reduces our considerations, when
p\neq 2
, to
the case where
\rho
is twist-primitive. By Lemma
3.3
(
1
)
this means that we will reduce to the case where
\rho
is regular.

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume
that
r\geq 2
,
p\neq 2
and that every irreducible character of

G_{r-1}
that is trivial on
Z_{r-1}
is contained in the conjugation
character of
G_{r-1}
. Let
\rho\in\Irr(G)
be a character that
is trivial on
Z
and not twist-primitive. Then
\rho
is contained
in the conjugation character of
G
.

Proof.

As
\rho
is not twist-primitive, it is trivial on
[G,G]\cap K^{r-1}

(as shown in the beginning of this section). By Lemma
6.1
,

[G,G]\cap K^{r-1}\supseteq K_{\SL}^{r-1}
(since
p\neq 2
). Thus, when

G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
,
\rho
factors through
G_{r-1}\cong\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})/K_{\SL}^{r-1}
.
Next, asssume that
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
. As
\rho
is also assumed
to be trivial on
Z
, it is trivial on
ZK_{\SL}^{r-1}
. But
Z\geq\begin{pmatrix}1+x\pi^{r-1}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{r-1}\end{pmatrix}
, so
\rho
is trivial on the group of matrices of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1+x\pi^{r-1}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{r-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{r-1}&b\pi^{r-1}\\
c\pi^{r-1}&1-a\pi^{r-1}\end{pmatrix}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1+(x+a)\pi^{r-1}&b\pi^{r-1}\\
c\pi^{r-1}&1+(x-a)\pi^{r-1}\end{pmatrix}\right\}.

For any
y,z\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
, there exists a solution
x,a\in\mathcal{O}_{r}

to the following system of congruences

\displaystyle x+a

\displaystyle=y\mod{\mathfrak{p}},

\displaystyle x-a

\displaystyle=z\mod{\mathfrak{p}},

namely,
x=(y+z)2^{-1}
,
a=(y-x)2^{-1}
(using that
p\neq 2
,
so
2
is invertible). Thus
ZK_{\SL}^{r-1}\supseteq K_{\GL}^{r-1}
(in
fact, we have equality) and
\rho
factors through
G_{r-1}\cong\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})/K_{\GL}^{r-1}
.

In either case
\rho
factors through
G_{r-1}
and since
\rho

is trivial on
Z
, Lemma
6.3

implies that the character
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\rho\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
that it defines on
G_{r-1}

is trivial on
Z_{r-1}
. By hypothesis,
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\rho\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
appears in
the conjugation character of
G_{r-1}
and by
[
21
, Lemma 4]

we conclude that
\rho
appears in the conjugation character of
G
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

The above lemma, together with Lemma
6.15
,
are the only places where we need the assumption that
p\neq 2
for

G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
. The last paragraph of the proof of the above
lemma shows that for
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
, for any
p
, all the
non-twist primitive characters that are also non-primitive and trivial
on
Z
are contained in the conjugation character of
G
, assuming
that every irreducible character of
G_{r-1}
that is trivial on

Z_{r-1}
is contained in the conjugation character of
G_{r-1}
.
For
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
p=2
, the only remaining case is therefore
that of non-twist primitive characters that are primitive (i.e., those
whose orbit mod
\mathfrak{p}
contains a non-zero scalar). It seems likely
that the lemma is also true for these characters, but we have not
been able to prove it.

6.1.
The case when
r
is even

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
r=2l
and
assume that either
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
or
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})

and
p\neq 2
. For any regular
\rho\in\Irr(G)
that is trivial
on
Z
there exists an element
g_{\hat{\beta}}
as in Section
5
such that
\rho
restricted to
C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
contains the trivial character.

Proof.

In this case we have
l=l^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3.4
,

\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta})
for some
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{l}),\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l}^{\times}
. By Lemma
3.3
,

\rho=\Ind_{C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}}^{G}\sigma
for some
\sigma\in\Irr(C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\mid\psi_{\beta})

and any choice of lift
\hat{\beta}
. By Lemma
2.3
(
1
)
with
H=G
and

, it is enough to prove
that
\sigma
restricted to
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g)

contains the trivial character, for some

. We will prove
that
g
can be taken to be
g_{\hat{\beta}}
for some lift
\hat{\beta}

(that depends on
\sigma
).

For any lift
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}
of
\beta
, Lemma
5.2

says that

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-v}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}}).

It is thus sufficient to show the following.

Claim
.

There exists a
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}
such that
\sigma
restricted to
ZU^{l-v}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})

Since
\sigma
lies above
\psi_{\beta}
and

), Proposition
4.1

implies that

for some lift
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\

of

(using that

). As

is trivial
on

, so is

(by Lemma

)
and by Lemma

,

\psi_{\hat{\beta}}

.

∎

6.2.
The case when

r
is odd and
v<l^{\prime}

Throughout this section we assume that
r
is odd. Let
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}),\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}
and let
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
be a lift of
\beta
. For
X\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
,
i\geq 2
, we
will denote the image of
X
in
\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muX\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.
It is well-known that the homomorphism
\eta:K^{l^{\prime}}\rightarrow\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

given by
1+X\pi^{l^{\prime}}\mapsto\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muX\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, where
X\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
,
induces an isomorphism

(6.1)

K^{l^{\prime}}/K^{l}\cong\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),

where
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is viewed as a group under addition. As
K^{l^{\prime}}/K^{l}

is an elementary abelian
p
-group, this isomorphism can be promoted
to an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space isomorphism. This isomorphism is straightforward
when
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
. For
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
, if
1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}X\in K^{l^{\prime}}

with
X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
, then
1=\det(1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}X)=1+(a+d)\pi^{l^{\prime}}+(ad-bc)\pi^{r-1}
, so

a+d\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}
, hence
a+d\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}}
, so
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muX\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
We claim that the map
\eta
is surjective. Indeed, for
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muX\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\begin{pmatrix}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mua\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu&\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mub\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\\
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muc\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu&\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mud\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mua\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\neq 0
or
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mud\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\neq 0
, Hensel’s lemma implies that
there are
a,b,c,d\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
such that
a+d\equiv-(ad-bc)\pi^{l^{\prime}}\mod{\pi^{l}}

and thus
(a+d)\pi^{l^{\prime}}\equiv-(ad-bc)\pi^{r-1}\mod{\pi^{r}}
, so
that for
x:=1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
, we have
\det(x)=1
and
\eta(x)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muX\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Moreover, if
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mua\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mud\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu=0
,
then we may take
x:=1+\pi^{l^{\prime}}\begin{pmatrix}\pi^{l^{\prime}}bc&b\\
c&0\end{pmatrix}
. The kernel of
\eta
is clearly
K^{l}
, so we have an isomorphism

K^{l^{\prime}}/K^{l}\mathbin{\kern 1.49994pt\widetilde{\hphantom{\hskip 6.00006pt}}\kern-9.79996pt\rightarrow\kern 0.50003pt}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

For simplicity, and to conform with the notation in
[
18
]
,
we will write

C^{i}=C_{G}(\hat{\beta})\cap K^{i},

for
r\geq i\geq 1
(we will actually only use this notation for
i=1

or
i=l^{\prime}
). Since
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})
is abelian and
[C^{1},K^{l^{\prime}}]\subseteq K^{l}
,
the group
C^{1}K^{l}
is normal in
C^{1}K^{l^{\prime}}
. The quotient

V:=\frac{C^{1}K^{l^{\prime}}}{C^{1}K^{l}}=\frac{K^{l^{\prime}}(C^{1}K^{l})}{C^{1}K^{l}}\cong\frac{K^{l^{\prime}}}{K^{l^{\prime}}\cap C^{1}K^{l}}=\frac{K^{l^{\prime}}}{C^{l^{\prime}}K^{l}}

is a quotient of
\mathfrak{g}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, so
V
is itself an elementary
abelian
p
-group.

Let
\theta
be an extension of
\psi_{\beta}
to
C^{1}K^{l}

(which exists, as
C
is abelian and stabilises
\psi_{\beta}
).
Define the alternating bilinear form

h_{\beta}:V\times V\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad h_{\beta}(x(C^{1}K^{l}),y(C^{1}K^{l}))=\theta([x,y])=\psi_{\beta}([x,y]),

where
[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
and the last equality holds because
the commutator subgroup of
C^{1}K^{l}
lies in
K^{l}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Assume that either

Lemma 6.11

The

\hat{\lambda}

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}}).

Moreover, if
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}\\
(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
is another lift of
\beta
such that
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}\equiv(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}\mod{\mathfrak{p}}^{l}
,
then

ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}).

Proof.

Let
h\in C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
. Then

\displaystyle h_{l}

\displaystyle\in C_{G_{l}}(\hat{\beta}_{l})\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1+\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}&\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}\\
0&1+\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\cap C_{G_{l}}\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
-\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&1-\hat{\Delta}_{l}\end{smallmatrix}\Big)

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a+\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}&b\hat{\lambda}_{l}+\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}\\
b\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&a+\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\mid a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{l}\right\}\cap\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&x-y\hat{\Delta}_{l}\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{l}\right\}.

A matrix in this intersection is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}a+s\pi^{l^{\prime}}&b\hat{\lambda}_{l}+t\pi^{l^{\prime}}\\
b\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&a+u\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x&y\hat{\lambda}_{l}\\
-y\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}&x-y\hat{\Delta}_{l}\end{pmatrix}.

If
\hat{\Delta}_{l}=0
(i.e.,
w=l
), then

h_{l}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{l}\right\}\cap G_{l},

so that
h\in ZUK^{l}=ZU^{0}K^{l}
.

If
w<l
, then
b\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}=-y\hat{\lambda}_{l}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{l}

implies that
b\equiv-y\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}}
. We also have
b\hat{\lambda}_{l}+t\pi^{l^{\prime}}=y\hat{\lambda}_{l}
,
which implies that
b\equiv y\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}}
. Since
l-w\leq l^{\prime}

(
w\geq 1
since
\Delta\in\mathfrak{p}
), we obtain
2y\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}}^{l-w}

and as
p\neq 2
, we deduce that

y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}.

This implies that
y\hat{\Delta}_{l}=0
and thus

h_{l}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\mid x\in\mathcal{O}_{l},y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}\right\}\cap G_{l},

so that
h\in ZU^{l-w}K^{l}
.

Thus, in either case,
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})\subseteq ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
.
Furthermore, for any
h\in ZU^{l-w}K^{l}
,

\displaystyle h_{l}\in

\displaystyle Z_{l}U_{l}^{l-w}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}x&y\\
0&x\end{pmatrix}\mid x,y\in\mathcal{O}_{l},y\in\mathfrak{p}^{l-w}\right\}\cap G_{l}\subseteq C_{G_{l}}(\hat{\beta}_{l}),

so that
h\in C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J
.
Hence
ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J
and therefore

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
.

Finally, for
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}
as in the statement, we have
\val(\hat{\Delta}_{l})=\val(\hat{\Delta}_{l}^{\prime})

(that is, the same
w
), so by what we have already proved,

ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=C_{G}(\hat{\beta}^{\prime})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}).

∎

Theorem 6.16
.

Assume
that
r
is odd and
\Delta\in\mathfrak{p}
. Assume moreover that either

G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
or
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and
p\neq 2
. For any regular
\rho\in\Irr(G)
that is trivial
on
Z
there exists an element
g_{\hat{\beta}}
as in Section
5
such that
\rho
restricted to
C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})
contains the trivial character.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
,

\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta})
for some
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}(\mathcal{O}_{l}),\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l}^{\times}
. By Lemma
3.3

and Proposition
6.14
,

\rho=\Ind_{C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J}^{G}\omega
for some lift
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
and an extension
\omega
of
\tilde{\psi}_{\hat{\beta}}
to
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J
.

By Lemma
2.3
(
1
)
with
H=G
and
N=C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J
, it is enough to prove that

\omega
restricted to
C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g)
is the
trivial character, for some
g\in G
. By Lemma
6.15
,

C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})=ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}}),

as well as
U^{l-w}K^{l}\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})J
. It is thus
sufficient to show the following.

Claim
.

\omega
restricted to
ZU^{l-w}K^{l}\cap C_{G}(g_{\hat{\beta}})

is the trivial character.

By Proposition
4.1

every irreducible character of
U^{l-w}K^{l}
containing
\psi_{\beta}

is of the form
\psi_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}
, for some lift
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}^{\prime}\\
(\hat{\lambda}^{\prime})^{-1}\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
of

(note that it is always true that

, as
either

or

; thus

). We may
therefore choose
\hat{\beta}^{\prime}
such that
\omega|_{U^{l-w}K^{l}}=\psi_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}
.
Then, by construction,

\tilde{\psi}_{\hat{\beta}}|_{U^{l^{\prime}}K^{l}}=\omega|_{U^{l^{\prime}}K^{l}}=\psi_{\hat{\beta}^{\prime}}|_{U^{l^{\prime}}K^{l}},

(note that since

6.11

.
But since

5.3
.
∎

6.4.
The main theorem when
p

is odd

Using the theorems in the preceding subsections, it is now easy to
finish the proof of our main theorem when
p
is odd. The only remaining
ingredient is the following lemma and its
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
-analogue,
which form the base cases of the inductive procedure of the proof.

Lemma 6.17
.

Every irreducible
character of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
that is trivial on the centre is contained
in the conjugation character.

Proof.

We make use of the character table of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, available
for example in
[
1
, Section 11.7]
to show that
any irreducible character that is trivial on the centre, when restricted
to a suitable centraliser, contains the trivial representation. First
let
\chi
be a character of degree
q+1
. Then
\chi
has value

(q+1)\alpha(a)\beta(a)
on any scalar conjugacy class
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})
,
\alpha\neq\beta
,
so it is trivial on the centre if and only if
\alpha=\beta^{-1}
.
Let

C=C_{\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

On any element
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
b\neq 0
,
\chi
has value
\alpha(a)\beta(a)
. Assume that

\chi
is trivial on the centre. Then

\displaystyle|C|\cdot\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in C}\chi(g)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right))+\sum_{a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right))

\displaystyle=(q-1)(q+1)+(q-1)^{2}=2(q-1)q,

and thus
\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
. (Note that
if we tried to use the diagonal subgroup
T
instead of
C
we would
run into problems for
q=2
, where
T=\{1\}
is not the centraliser
of any element in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Alternatively, one could by
note that
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, use
[
21
, Corollary 5]

and proceed with
C=T
and
q\geq 3
.)

If
\chi
is a character of degree
1
, then it has value
\alpha(a^{2})

on any scalar conjugacy class
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, so it is trivial on the centre
if and only if
\alpha(a^{2})=1
for all
a
. The same computation
as above shows that

\displaystyle|C|\cdot\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=2(q-1)q,

so
\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
.

Next, let
\chi
be a character of degree
q
. Then then it has
value
q\alpha(a^{2})
on any scalar conjugacy class
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, so as in the previous case, it
is trivial on the centre if and only if
\alpha(a^{2})=1
for all

a
. Meanwhile, it has value
0
on any element
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
where
b\neq 0
, so if
\chi
is trivial on the centre, then

\displaystyle|C|\cdot\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right))=q(q-1),

so
\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
.

Finally, let
\chi
be a character of degree
q-1
. Then
\chi

has value
(q-1)\omega(a)
on any scalar conjugacy class
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
\omega\in\Irr(\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times})
,
\omega^{q}\neq\omega
,
so it is trivial on the centre if and only if
\omega(a)=1
for all

a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
. As in
[
1
, Section 11.7]
,
we label non-central conjugacy classes that meet the non-split maximal
torus by elements
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, where
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
and
x\neq x^{q}
. The element
in
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
corresponding to
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is
\lambda^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\lambda
, where
\lambda\in\GL_{2}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mu\mathbb{F}\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{q})
is any element such that

\lambda^{-1}F(\lambda)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
(here
F
is the
q
-power Frobenius map). The non-split torus

T_{w}\subset\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is

T_{w}=\{\lambda^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\lambda\mid x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\}=\bigcup_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\\
x\neq x^{q}\end{subarray}}\lambda^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\lambda

Take any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}
such that
x\neq x^{q}
(note
that such an
x
always exists since
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\mid x^{q}=x\}

and
|\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}|\geq(4-1)-(2-1)=2
).
Then

T_{w}=C_{\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}\lambda^{-1}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\lambda

since
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is regular and semisimple. On any non-central element of
T_{w}
,

\chi
has value
-(\omega(x)+\omega(x^{q}))
. Assume that
\chi

is trivial on the centre. Then

\displaystyle|T_{w}|\cdot\langle\chi|_{C},\mathbf{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\chi(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a\\
&a\end{smallmatrix}\right))+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\\
x\neq x^{q}\end{subarray}}\chi(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&0\\
0&x^{q}\end{smallmatrix}\right))=(q-1)^{2}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}\\
x\neq x^{q}\end{subarray}}\omega(x)+\omega(x^{q})

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{2}-\left(-\sum_{a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}2\omega(x)\right)\text{ (since }\text{$\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{\times}}\omega(x)=0$}\text{)}

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{2}+2(q-1),

and thus
\langle\chi|_{T_{w}},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
.
∎

Theorem 6.18
.

Assume
that
p\neq 2
and let
G=\GL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
or
G=\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
.
Then any
\rho\in\Irr(G)
that is trivial on the centre is contained
in the conjugation character of
G
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
r
. When
r=1
, the result is Lemma
6.17
for
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
it follows from the general results in
[
6
]
, together with the verification for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
in
[
21
, Corollary 5]
.

Now let
r\geq 2
and assume that the result holds for
G_{r-1}
. Let
\rho\in\Irr(G)

be trivial on the centre. If
\rho
is not twist-primitive, the result
follows from Lemma
6.4

and the induction hypothesis. If
\rho
is twist-primitive, then

\rho
is regular by Lemma
3.3
(
1
)
and the result follows from Theorems
6.6
,

6.8

and
6.16
.
∎

7.
The case when

\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{2}
,
v=l^{\prime}
and
p=2

For this case, we exploit the fact that when
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
and
p=2
,
the group
Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}
is actually normal in
UK^{l^{\prime}}
. This
does not hold when
\mathbf{G}=\SL_{2}
or when
p\neq 2
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Assume
that
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
and
p=2
. Then
[U,K^{l^{\prime}}]\subseteq Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}
.
Thus
Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}
is normal in
UK^{l^{\prime}}
and
UK^{l^{\prime}}/Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}

is an elementary abelian
p
-group.

Proof.

Let
u\in U
and
k\in K^{l^{\prime}}
. By direct matrix computation,

kuk^{-1}\in\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1-x\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\mid x\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\right\}UK^{l}.

For any
x\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
, we have

\begin{pmatrix}1-x\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\in K^{l},

since
2x\in\mathfrak{p}
, so

\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1-x\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\mid x\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\right\}UK^{l}\subseteq Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}

and thus
[U,K^{l^{\prime}}]\subseteq Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}
(note that this step
does not work for
\SL_{2}
, as
(1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}})^{2}=1
fails in general.).
Since
U
is abelian and
K^{l}
is normal in
G
, this implies
that
Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}
is normal in
UK^{l^{\prime}}
.

For the quotient, we have

UK^{l^{\prime}}/Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}=\frac{K^{l^{\prime}}(Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l})}{Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{l}}\cong\frac{K^{l^{\prime}}}{Z^{l^{\prime}}U^{l^{\prime}}K^{l}},

which is a quotient of
K^{l^{\prime}}/K^{l}\cong\M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and is therefore
an elementary abelian
p
-group.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Assume that
\mathbf{G}=\GL_{2}
,
p=2
and

v=l^{\prime}
. Let
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
with
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}
and Let
\hat{\beta}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\hat{\lambda}\\
\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{r})
be a lift of
\beta
and
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}
the corresponding
character of
U^{l^{\prime}-v}K^{l}
(as introduced in Proposition
4.1
).
Let
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
be such that
\hat{\Delta}=\delta\pi^{l^{\prime}}
.
Then, for any extension
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}^{\prime}
of
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}

to
Z^{l^{\prime}}UK^{{}^{l}}
, we have

\Stab_{UK^{l^{\prime}}}(\psi_{\hat{\beta}}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}UJ&\text{if\,}\hat{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{p}^{l},\\
Z^{l^{\prime}}U\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1+\delta^{-1}(\hat{\lambda}c)^{2}\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}\mid c\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\right\}K^{l}&\text{if\,}\hat{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}\setminus\mathfrak{p}^{l}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Write
S:=\Stab_{UK^{l^{\prime}}}(\psi_{\hat{\beta}}^{\prime})
. Any element of
UK^{l^{\prime}}

can be written as

\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
0&1+x\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix},

for some
u,x,a,c\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
and since
UZ^{l^{\prime}}\subset S
, it is
enough to determine when
\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}\in S
. Furthermore, as
K^{l^{\prime}}
centralises
Z^{l^{\prime}}K^{l}
, it is enough
to determine when
\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}
stabilises
\psi_{\hat{\beta}}^{\prime}|_{U}
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}})^{-1}&0\\
-c(1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}})^{-1}\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&u(1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}})\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1+uc\pi^{l^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}})^{-1}&0\\
-c(1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}})^{-1}\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\in\begin{pmatrix}*&u+ua\pi^{l^{\prime}}\\
-uc^{2}\pi^{r-1}&*\end{pmatrix}

and

\psi_{\hat{\beta}}^{\prime}\begin{pmatrix}*&u+ua\pi^{l^{\prime}}\\
-uc^{2}\pi^{r-1}&*\end{pmatrix}=\psi(-\hat{\lambda}uc^{2}\pi^{r-1}+\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}(u+ua\pi^{l^{\prime}})).

We thus have
\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}\in S
if and only if

\psi(-\hat{\lambda}uc^{2})\pi^{r-1}+\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}(u+ua\pi^{l^{\prime}}))=\psi(\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\hat{\Delta}u),\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal{O}_{r}.

This is equivalent to

\psi(u[-\hat{\lambda}c^{2}+\hat{\lambda}^{-1}\delta a]\pi^{r-1})=1,\qquad\text{for all }u\in\mathcal{O}_{r},

that is, to

(7.1)

(\hat{\lambda}c)^{2}\equiv\delta a\mod{\mathfrak{p}}.

If
\hat{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{p}^{l}
, then
\delta\in\mathfrak{p}
so (
7.1
)
is equivalent to
c\in\mathfrak{p}
and the stabiliser
S
in this case
is therefore

Z^{l^{\prime}}U\begin{pmatrix}1+\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}K^{l}=UJ.

On the other hand, if
\hat{\Delta}\in\mathfrak{p}^{l^{\prime}}\setminus\mathfrak{p}^{l}
,
then
\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{r}^{\times}
and (
7.1
) is
equivalent to

a\equiv\delta^{-1}(\hat{\lambda}c)^{2}\mod{\mathfrak{p}}

and

\begin{pmatrix}1+a\pi^{l^{\prime}}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\delta^{-1}(\hat{\lambda}c)^{2}\pi^{l^{\prime}}+x\pi^{l}&0\\
c\pi^{l^{\prime}}&1\end{pmatrix}

Lemma 7.3

Lemma 8.1
.

Assume
that

. Then

Z=(\pm 1+\mathfrak{p}^{r-1})I.

Proof.

We determine
Z\cong\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\mid a^{2}=1\}

explicitly. If
a\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
is such that
a^{2}=1
, then
a=1+2x
,
for some

and

(1+2x)^{2}=1+2^{2}x+2^{2}x^{2}=1.

This is equivalent to

(8.1)

If
x
is a unit and
1+x
is a unit, then (
8.1
)
holds if and only if

. If

is a unit and
1+x
is not

)

for some

On the other hand, if

is not a unit, then
1+x
is a unit, and
(
8.1
) holds if and
only if

, that is,

for some

,
so that

In summary, we have shown that

0&\pm 1+2^{r-1}y\end{pmatrix}\mid y\in\mathcal{O}_{r}\right\}=(\pm 1+\mathfrak{p}^{r-1})I.

∎

Lemma 8.4
.

(2)

\beta=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}

\beta=\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
.

Proof.

Since

is regular, it is
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
-conjugate
to a matrix of the form

\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix},

\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}
,

and
\Delta=-(\det(\alpha)+x(\Tr(\alpha)+x))

is not a unit. Since

for some unit

is not a unit, then

\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}
when

is not a unit.

For
Case 2
, we assume that
\Tr(\alpha)
and

are units. Define the function

\displaystyle F:\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times},

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=\Tr(\alpha)+2x-\lambda^{-1}(\det(\alpha)+x(\Tr(\alpha)+x))=\tau+\lambda^{-1}\Delta,

.

For

, assume that

is a unit and

\displaystyle F:\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}^{\times},

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle=x(\Tr(\alpha)+x),

. Thus, there exists an

0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma 8.5
.

Assume that either of the following conditions holds.

(1)

{\hat{\beta}}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\tau}&\hat{\lambda}\\

\eta_{\theta}

\Ind_{Z_{G}C^{1}K^{l}}^{Z_{G}C^{1}K^{l^{\prime}}}\theta

is a multiple of
\eta_{\theta}
, for some extension
\theta\in\Irr(Z_{G}C^{1}K^{l})
of
\psi_{\beta}
.
Let
\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}\in\Irr(Z_{G}K^{l}\mid\psi_{\beta})
be an irreducible component of the restriction of
\theta
to
Z_{G}K^{l}
. Then
\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}
is trivial on
\pm I
since
\rho
is. By the same argument as in Case 1 in the proof of
8.8
, the proof of Claim A is reduced to proving that
\theta
restricted to
Z_{G}C^{1}K^{l}\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)
is trivial.
But by
8.6
,
\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}
restricted to
Z_{G}K^{l}\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)
is trivial, so
\theta
restricted to
Z_{G}C^{1}K^{l}\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)\subseteq C_{G}(\hat{\beta})K^{l}\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)
is trivial.
∎

8.3.
The main theorem for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{r})

Using the lemmas in the preceding subsections, we can now finish the proof of the main theorem for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n})
.
The reason why we state the theorem below for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n})
rather than for
S=S_{r}
is that with this notation almost all the essential work will be carried out in
G
and
S
(as in the previous sections of this paper), keeping the notation lighter than if we had worked with some
G_{i}
and
S_{i}
for
r>i
.

Lemma 8.10
.

Assume that
e=1
and
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Then
Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S\subseteq\pm IK^{l}
.

Proof.

Let
h\in Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S
so that
h=\begin{pmatrix}1+\pi^{l^{\prime}-1}x&0\\
0&1+\pi^{l^{\prime}-1}x\end{pmatrix}
, for
x\in\mathcal{O}_{r}
. Then

1=\det(h)=1+2\pi^{l^{\prime}-1}x+\pi^{2l^{\prime}-2}x^{2},

so writing
2=u\pi
for some unit
u\in\mathcal{O}_{r}^{\times}
, we get

x(u+\pi^{l^{\prime}-2}x)\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}.

If
l^{\prime}>2
, then
u+\pi^{l^{\prime}-2}x
is always a unit and thus
x\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}
so
h\in K^{l}
.

Next, if
l^{\prime}=2
, then the above congruence says
x(u+x)\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}.
If
x
is not unit then
u+x
is a unit and so
x\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}
so
h\in K^{l}
. If
x
is a unit then
u+x\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}
so

\pi x\equiv-\pi u=-2\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}
. Thus,
1+\pi x\equiv-1\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{l}}
so
h\in\pm IK^{l}
.

Finally, if
l^{\prime}=1
, then
r=2
or
r=3
and the equation
\det(h)=1
says
x(2+x)=0
. This has no solution for
x
a unit, so
x=2z
for some
z
. Hence, if
r=2
then
h\in K^{l}
. When
r=3
, we have
l=2
and
1+x=1+2z\equiv\pm 1\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
because
1+(1+2z)=2(1+z)\equiv 0\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
when
z
is a unit and if
z
is not a unit, then
1+2z\equiv 1\mod{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}
. We conclude that for all
l^{\prime}\geq 1
,
h\in\pm IK^{l}
.
∎

Theorem 8.11
.

Assume that
e=1
and
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let
\hat{\chi}\in\Irr(\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{n}))
,
n\geq 1
, be a representation that is trivial on the centre
Z_{n}
. Then
\hat{\chi}
is contained in the conjugation character of
\SL_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
K^{r}
be the maximal subgroup among the
K^{i}
contained in the kernel of
\hat{\chi}
, so
\hat{\chi}=\Inf^{S_{n}}_{S}(\chi)
for some primitive
\chi\in\Irr(S)
. Since
\hat{\chi}
is trivial on
Z_{n}
, it follows that
\chi
is trivial on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. By
8.3
, it is enough to show that
\chi
restricted to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)
contains the trivial character for some
g\in S_{n}
.

First we let
r=1
. Then
S=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
Z=\pm I
. It is easily checked (see
[
21
, Corollary 5]
) that since
\chi
is trivial on
Z
, there exists a
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mug\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\in S
such that
\chi
restricted to
C_{S}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mug\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
contains the trivial character. Picking
g\in S_{n}
such that
\rho_{1}(g)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mug\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
we have that
\chi
restricted to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)\subseteq C_{S}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0mug\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
contains the trivial character (cf.
[
21
, Lemma 4]
), proving the theorem when
r=1
.

Assume from now on that
r\geq 2
. Let
\beta\in\M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}})
be such that
\chi\in\Irr(S\mid\psi_{[\beta]})
. By
3.3
,
\beta
is regular. Thus, by
8.4
, we can assume that
\beta
is equal to one of the following matrices:

(1)

\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}
;

(2)

\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
\lambda^{-1}\Delta&0\end{pmatrix}
such that
\tau+\lambda^{-1}\Delta=2\lambda
;

(3)

\begin{pmatrix}\tau&\lambda\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
with
\tau
unit.

Note that when
n=r
,
8.2
implies that
\beta
can be taken to be of type 1, since
\chi
is trivial on
Z=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

Claim A
.

There exists a
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\chi)
such that
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta^{\prime}})
, with
\beta^{\prime}
of the same type as
\beta
.

We will break down the proof of Claim A in two cases where
n=r
and
n>r
, respectively.
Assume that
n=r
and that
\beta
is chosen of type 1. As
\chi\in\Irr(S\mid\psi_{[\beta]})
is trivial on
Z
, the injection
S/Z\hookrightarrow G/Z_{G}
implies that there is a
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\chi)
that is trivial on
Z_{G}
and
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta+xI})
. Since
\rho
is trivial on
Z_{G}
, and
\beta+xI
is regular,
3.4
implies that
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta^{\prime}})
with

\beta^{\prime}
of type 1. Thus, we have shown Claim A for
n=r
.

Assume now that
n>r
and recall that in this case
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\pm I
. If
l\neq 1
, note that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}\cong\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\times K^{l}
and when
l=1
, we have
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subseteq K^{l}
. Thus, in either case there exists an extension
\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}
of
\psi_{\beta}
to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}
that is trivial on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. We then have

\displaystyle\langle\Res_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\Ind_{S}^{G}\chi,\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\Ind_{S\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}^{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}(\Res_{S\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\chi),\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Res_{S\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\chi,\Res_{S\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\Res_{S\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\psi^{\prime}_{\beta},\Res_{S\cap\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}\rangle=1.

Thus there exists an irreducible constituent
\rho
of
\Ind_{S}^{G}\chi
, that is,
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\chi)
, such that
\Res_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5muK^{l}}\rho
contains
\psi^{\prime}_{\beta}
(note that
\beta^{\prime}
can thus be taken to be just
\beta
in this case). We conclude that Claim A is now established in all cases.

Claim B
.

Assume we have
\beta
of type 1 and
n>r
. There exists a
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\chi)
such that
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta^{\prime}})
, with
\beta^{\prime}
of type 1, and
\rho
is trivial on
\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1})
.

We first show that
\chi
is trivial on
\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S)
.
It follows from
8.10
that
\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S)\subseteq\pm IK^{l}
and thus

\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S)\subseteq\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l}).

By the form of
\beta
,
\psi_{\beta}
is trivial on
Z_{G}\cap K^{l}
, so
\chi
is trivial on
\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S)
, as
\chi
restricted to
Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S
is a multiple of
\psi_{\beta}
restricted to this group.

As
\chi\in\Irr(S\mid\psi_{[\beta]})
is trivial on
\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S)
, the injection

S/[\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}\cap S)]\hookrightarrow G/[\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1})]

implies that there is a
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta+xI})
, for some
x\in\mathcal{O}_{l^{\prime}}
, lying above
\chi
, such that
\rho
is trivial on
\pm I(Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1})
.
As
\rho
is trivial on
Z_{G}\cap K^{l}\subseteq Z_{G}\cap K^{l^{\prime}-1}
, by
3.4
we have that
\rho
contains
\psi_{\beta^{\prime}}
for some
\beta^{\prime}
of type 1. Thus Claim B follows.

We will now show that
\chi
contains the trivial character when restricted to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g^{\prime})
for some
g^{\prime}\in S_{n}
. Let
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\chi)
be such that
\rho\in\Irr(G\mid\psi_{\beta})
with
\beta
one of the three types (such a
\rho
exists by Claim A). Since
\chi
is trivial on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muZ\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, so is
\rho
.
Note that when
n=r
we can assume by the proof of Claim A that
\rho
is trivial on
Z_{G}
. Thus, for
n=r
, where we have
\beta
of type 1 and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)=C_{G}(g)
,
Theorems
6.6
,
6.8
and
6.16
yield a
g\in S_{n}
such that
\rho
restricted to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)
contains the trivial character.
If
\beta
is of type 1 and
n>r
, we can moreover let
\rho
be as in Claim B.
Then, for
n>r
, Lemmas
8.8
and
8.9
yield a
g\in S_{n}
such that
\rho
restricted to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)
contains the trivial character.
Thus, for all
n\geq r
and all types,
\langle\rho,\Ind_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}^{G}\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
and therefore
\langle\Ind_{S}^{G}\chi,\Ind_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}^{G}\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0
. Hence, by the Mackey intertwining number formula,

0\neq\langle\chi,\Res_{S}\Ind_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}^{G}\mathbf{1}\rangle=\sum_{h\in S\backslash G/\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}\langle\chi,\Ind_{S\cap\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}}^{h}\hskip-1.0pt{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}}^{S}\mathbf{1}\rangle.

Thus, as
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)\subseteq S
, there is an
h\in G
such that

\langle\chi,\Ind_{\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}}{}^{h}\hskip-1.0pt{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}}^{S}\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0.

Let
\hat{h}
be a lift of
h
to
G_{n}
. We have

S\cap\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}}^{h}\hskip-1.0pt{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}=\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}}^{h}\hskip-1.0pt{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g)}=\rho_{n,r}(\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{C_{S_{n}}(g)}}^{\hat{h}}\hskip-1.0pt{C_{S_{n}}(g)})=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{g}}^{\hat{h}}\hskip-1.0pt{g}).

Let
g^{\prime}:=\hskip 1.0pt{\vphantom{g}}^{\hat{h}}\hskip-1.0pt{g}\in S_{n}
. Then

0\neq\langle\chi,\Ind_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g^{\prime})}^{S}\mathbf{1}\rangle=\langle\chi|_{\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g^{\prime})},\mathbf{1}\rangle.

Thus
\chi
contains the trivial character when restricted to
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muC\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.5mu(g^{\prime})
and the proof is complete.
∎

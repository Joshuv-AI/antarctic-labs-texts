Quadratic-Phase Fourier–Bessel Transform: definitions, properties and uncertainty principles
Ahmed Saoudi

Abstract
In this manuscript, we introduce the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform and develop its foundational properties, including continuity, the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, reversibility, and Parseval’s identity. We define the associated translation operator and convolution product, establishing their main properties within this framework. As an application, we prove a Donoho–Stark-type uncertainty principle for the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform, extending classical uncertainty results to this generalized setting. Keywords . Quadratic-Phase Fourier–Bessel Transform; Quadratic-Phase Fourier–Bessel Translation, Quadratic-Phase Fourier–Bessel convolution, Donoho-stark uncertainty principle. The quadratic phase Fourier transform is a powerful generalization of the classical Fourier transform, designed to address the limitations of traditional Fourier analysis in handling non-stationary signals. Unlike the Fourier transform, which assumes that signals are stationary (i.e., their frequency content does not change over time), the quadratic phase Fourier transform incorporates quadratic-phase terms, making it particularly effective for analyzing signals with time-varying frequency content, such as chirp signals, radar pulses, and optical wavefronts.

The quadratic phase Fourier transform of a function
\displaystyle f(t)
is defined as:

\mathcal{Q}^{a,b,c,}_{d,e}[f](u)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}K^{a,b,c,}_{d,e}(t,u)f(t)\,dt,

where the kernel
\displaystyle K^{a,b,c,}_{d,e}(t,u)
is given by:

K^{a,b,c,}_{d,e}(t,u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2i\pi b}}\,e^{i\left(at^{2}+btu+cu^{2}+dt+eu\right)},

with,
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e
are real parameters that control the quadratic and linear phase terms. The parameter
\displaystyle b\neq 0
ensures the transform is well-defined.

The quadratic phase Fourier transform was introduced as a unifying framework that generalizes several well-known transforms, including the Fourier transform, the fractional Fourier transform, and the linear canonical transform. By incorporating additional parameters that control quadratic and linear phase terms, the quadratic phase Fourier transform provides greater flexibility in signal analysis and has found applications in diverse fields such as signal processing, optics, quantum mechanics, and image processing.

In the present article, we introduce and study the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform that depend on five real parameters
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e
and the indice
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
, where
\displaystyle b\neq 0
. We denote this new transformation by
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}
which extends a broad range of integral transform families by selecting appropriate parameters.

We define the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform of an integrable function
\displaystyle h
on the half line as

\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)=\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds,\quad b\neq 0,

(1.1)

where
\displaystyle\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)
denotes the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel kernel, given by

\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)=e^{-i(as^{2}+ct^{2}+ds+et)}j_{\gamma}\left(st/b\right).

(1.2)

where
\displaystyle j_{\gamma}
represent the normalized Bessel function given by

j_{\gamma}(s):=2^{\gamma}\Gamma(\gamma+1)\frac{J_{\gamma}(s)}{s^{\gamma}}=\Gamma(\gamma+1)\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(s/2)^{2n}}{n!\Gamma(n+\gamma+1)},

(1.3)

and the constant
\displaystyle c_{\gamma}
is given by

c_{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2^{\gamma}\Gamma(\gamma+1)}.

(1.4)

Here
\displaystyle J_{\gamma}
denotes the Bessel function (see
[
24
]
).

With suitable choices of parameters
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e
and multiplicity function
\displaystyle\gamma

1.1

If
\displaystyle d=e=0

\displaystyle b\neq 0

(1.5)

where
\displaystyle\mathscr{K}_{\gamma}(t,s)
represents the linear canonical Fourier–Bessel kernel defined by

\mathscr{K}_{\gamma}(t,s)=e^{\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{a}{b}s^{2}+\frac{d}{b}t^{2}\right)}j_{\gamma}\left(st/b\right).

(1.6)

•

If
\displaystyle d=e=0
, and under the transformations
\displaystyle a\mapsto-\cot\theta/2

\displaystyle b=\sin\theta
and
\displaystyle c\mapsto-\cot\theta/2
with

\mathcal{L}^{\theta}_{\gamma}[h](t)=\frac{c_{\gamma}c_{\theta}}{|\sin\theta|^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathscr{J}_{\gamma}(t,s)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\gamma}(t,s)
represents the linear canonical Fourier–Bessel kernel defined by

\mathscr{J}_{\gamma}(t,s)=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\cot(\theta)\left(s^{2}+t^{2}\right)}j_{\gamma}\left(st/\sin(\theta)\right),

(1.8)

and

c_{\theta}=e^{i(\gamma+1)((\theta-2n\pi)-\hat{\theta}\pi/2)},\quad\textrm{with}\quad\hat{\theta}=sgn(\sin\theta).

\displaystyle e^{-i(\mu+1)\pi/2}

\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}[h](t)=c_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}(st)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds,

(1.9)

where the kernel
\displaystyle j_{\gamma}

).

In this work, we extend the framework of quadratic-phase Fourier analysis by introducing the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform, a new integral transform that combines quadratic phase modulation with the Bessel structure. We develop its foundational properties, including continuity, the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, reversibility, and Parseval’s identity, which ensure that the transform is well defined and suitable for harmonic analysis. To build an operational calculus, we introduce the associated translation operator and convolution product, and we establish their main properties in this generalized setting. These results provide the analytic tools necessary for studying uncertainty principles in the new domain. As an application, we prove a Donoho–Stark-type uncertainty principle for the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform, thereby extending classical uncertainty results to a broader and more flexible transform framework.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform and establish its fundamental properties. Section
3
is devoted to the definition of generalized translation operators associated with this transform and to the study of their main properties. In Section
4
, we define the generalized convolution product in the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel setting and investigate its properties. Finally, in Section
5
, we apply the developed framework to prove a Donoho–Stark-type uncertainty principle for the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform.

2
Fundamental properties of quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform

Along this article, We consider the following notations and functional spaces.

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{C_{*}}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the space consisting of all continuous functions on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
, and endowed with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets.

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{*,0}(\mathbb{R})
is the set of all even continuous functions on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
that approach zero at infinity, equipped with the topology of uniform convergence.

•

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denotes the Lebesgue space of measurable functions
\displaystyle h
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\|h\|_{\gamma,p}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(s)|^{p}s^{2\gamma+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,\quad\text{ if }1\leq p<\infty,\\
\|h\|_{\gamma,\infty}=\displaystyle\supess_{s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(s)|<\infty,\quad\text{ if }p=\infty,\end{array}

provided with the topology defined by the norm
\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{\gamma,p}
, with
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
.

•

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
denote the Schwartz space consisting of even, infinitely differentiable (
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
) functions on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}
that, along with all their derivatives, decay rapidly at infinity, equipped with the topology generated by the family of seminorms:

q_{n,m}(h)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\left(1+s^{2}\right)^{n}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}s^{m}}h(s)\right|,\quad h\in\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R}),\quad n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
.

1.

If
\displaystyle h
be a function belongs to
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then the maps
\displaystyle t\mapsto\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)
is continuous on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
and we have

\left|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\right|\leq\frac{c_{\gamma}}{|b|^{\gamma+1}}\|h\|_{\gamma,1}.

(2.3)

2.

For all
\displaystyle h\in\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)=\frac{e^{-i(ct^{2}+et)}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\left[e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}h(s)\right]\left(\frac{t}{b}\right).

(2.4)

Proof.

We consider
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
.

1.

Let
\displaystyle h
be function in
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Since the maps
\displaystyle s\mapsto j_{\gamma}\left(st/b\right)
is continuous on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we deduce that

w\mapsto\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](s),

is also continuous maps on the half line for every
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](s)\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)f(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{|b|^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)f(s)\right|s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{|b|^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(s)\right|s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{|b|^{\gamma+1}}\|h\|_{\gamma,1}.

2.

Let
\displaystyle h
be a function in
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
, then we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)f(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(as^{2}+ct^{2}+ds+et)}j_{\gamma}\left(\frac{st}{b}\right)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}e^{-i(ct^{2}+et)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}j_{\gamma}\left(\frac{st}{b}\right)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{e^{-i(ct^{2}+et)}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\left(c_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}\left(\frac{st}{b}\right)\left(e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}h(s)\right)s^{2\gamma+1}ds\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{e^{-i(ct^{2}+et)}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\left[e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}h(s)\right]\left(\frac{t}{b}\right).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
,
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
For every
\displaystyle h,g\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform satisfies the following properties:

1.

Linearity:
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[\alpha h+\beta g](s)=\alpha\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](s)+\beta\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](s)
.

2.

Scaling:
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](kt)=\frac{1}{k^{2\gamma+2}}\mathbb{B}^{a^{\prime},b,c^{\prime}}_{d^{\prime},e^{\prime},\gamma}[h_{k}](t)
, with
\displaystyle a^{\prime}=\frac{a}{k^{2}}
,
\displaystyle c^{\prime}=ck^{2}
,
\displaystyle d^{\prime}=\frac{d}{k}
,
\displaystyle e^{\prime}=ek
and

\displaystyle h_{k}(v)=h(v/k)
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle h,g\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
,
\displaystyle k,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

1.

Linearity:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[\alpha h+\beta g](s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)[\alpha h+\beta g](s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\alpha c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{\beta c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,s)g(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](s)+\beta\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](s).

2.

Scaling: we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](kt)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(kt,s)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(as^{2}+c(kt)^{2}+ds+e(kt))}j_{\gamma}\left(\frac{s(kt)}{b}\right)h(s)s^{2\gamma+1}ds.

We make the change of variable
\displaystyle s=\frac{u}{k}
to simplify the expression, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](kt)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i\left(\frac{a}{k^{2}}u^{2}+ck^{2}t^{2}+\frac{d}{k}u+ekt\right)}j_{\gamma}\left(\frac{ut}{b}\right)h\left(\frac{u}{k}\right)u^{2\gamma+1}du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{2\gamma+2}}\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a^{\prime},b,c^{\prime}}_{d^{\prime},e^{\prime},\gamma}(u,s)h_{k}(u)u^{2\gamma+1}du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{2\gamma+2}}\mathbb{B}^{a^{\prime},b,c^{\prime}}_{d^{\prime},e^{\prime},\gamma}[h_{k}](t),

with
\displaystyle a=\frac{a}{k^{2}}
,
\displaystyle c=ck^{2}
,
\displaystyle d=\frac{d}{k}
,
\displaystyle e=ek
and

\displaystyle h_{k}(v)=h(v/k)
.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

(The reversibility property) Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
, and
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
. Let
\displaystyle h
be a function belonging to
\displaystyle\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]
belongs to
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then the inverse of the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle h(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(t)\right)(s)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}(s,t)\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)t^{2\gamma+1}dt.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle h
be a function belonging to
\displaystyle\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(t)\right)(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(-ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}(s,t)\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)t^{2\gamma+1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(-ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}(s,t)\bigg[\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,u)f(u)u^{2\gamma+1}du\bigg]t^{2\gamma+1}dt.

Using Fubini’s theorem, we may interchange the order of integration and obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(t)\right)(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}(-ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{J}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}(s,t)\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times h(u)u^{2\gamma+1}t^{2\gamma+1}dtdu.

By evaluating the product of the kernels, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{J}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}(s,t)\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e^{i(ct^{2}+as^{2}+ds+et)}j_{\gamma}\left(-st/b\right)e^{-i(au^{2}+ct^{2}+du+et)}j_{\gamma}\left(ut/b\right).

Using the fact that the normalized Bessel function
\displaystyle j_{\gamma}
is even, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{J}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}(s,t)\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(t,u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}e^{-i(au^{2}+du)}j_{\gamma}\left(st/b\right)j_{\gamma}\left(ut/b\right).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(t)\right)(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}(-ib)^{\gamma+1}}e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(au^{2}+du)}h(u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}\left(st/b\right)j_{\gamma}\left(ut/b\right)t^{2\gamma+1}dt\right]u^{2\gamma+1}du.

By performing the change of variables
\displaystyle v=t/b
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(t)\right)(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{\gamma}^{2}e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i(au^{2}+du)}h(u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}(sv)j_{\gamma}(uv)v^{2\gamma+1}dv\right]u^{2\gamma+1}du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{\gamma}^{2}e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}(sv)v^{2\gamma+1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}(uv)e^{-i(au^{2}+du)}h(u)u^{2\gamma+1}du\right]dv.

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle c_{\gamma}e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\int_{0}^{\infty}j_{\gamma}(sv)\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}[H](v)v^{2\gamma+1}dv

where
\displaystyle H(u)=e^{-i(au^{2}+du)}h(u)
.

Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(t)\right)(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\left[\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}\left(\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}[H](s)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\left[H(s)\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}h(s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle h(s).

Therefore,

\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right)(s)=h(s).

This confirms that the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform is invertible, with the inverse given by the relation

\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\circ\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}=id.

∎

Now we present and demonstrate the Parseval identity for the quadratic phase Fourier–Bessel transform.

Theorem 2.6

(Parseval’s formula)
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
,
\displaystyle\gamma\geq-1/2
and
\displaystyle h
and
\displaystyle g
be two functions belonging to
\displaystyle L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(s)\overline{g(s)}|s|^{2\gamma+1}ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\overline{\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt.

(2.7)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle h
and
\displaystyle g
be two functions in
\displaystyle L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Using the relation (
2.4
) between the Fourier–Bessel transform and its quadratic phase version, we get

\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)=\frac{1}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}e^{-i(ct^{2}+et)}\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(h_{1})(t/b),

where
\displaystyle h_{1}(s)=e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}h(s),
and

\overline{\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}=\frac{1}{(-ib)^{\gamma+1}}e^{i(ct^{2}+et)}\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(g_{1})(t/b)},

with
\displaystyle g_{1}(s)=e^{-i(as^{2}+ds)}g(s).

Subsequently, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\overline{\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\frac{1}{(-ib)^{\gamma+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(h_{1})(t/b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(g_{1})(t/b)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{|b|^{2\gamma+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(h_{1})(t/b)\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(g_{1})(t/b)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt.

Applying the change of variable
\displaystyle(z=t/b)
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\overline{\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(h_{1})(z)\overline{\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}(g_{1})(z)}z^{2\gamma+1}dz.

By the Plancherel theorem for the Fourier–Bessel transform, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\overline{\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h_{1}(z)\overline{g_{1}(z)}z^{2\gamma+1}dz

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-i(az^{2}+dz)}h(z)\overline{e^{-i(az^{2}+dz)}g(z)}z^{2\gamma+1}dz

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(z)\overline{g(z)}z^{2\gamma+1}dz.

This completes the verification of the identity, thereby concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
with
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma\geq-1/2
, and
\displaystyle h
be a function belonging to
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,

\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\|_{\gamma,2}=\|h\|_{\gamma,2}.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma\geq-1/2
.
If
\displaystyle h\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then its quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]
belongs to
\displaystyle L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and we have

(2.8)

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma\geq-1/2
.
By Corollary
2.7
and the density of
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R})
in

, we obtain a unique continuous operator
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]
on
\displaystyle L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, which coincides with
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]
on

. Let
\displaystyle h
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\overline{\mathbf{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt.

Consequently, by the density of
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle h
and
\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\overline{\mathbf{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[g](t)}t^{2\gamma+1}dt.

3
Quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel translation

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
, and
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
. . For
\displaystyle h\in\mathcal{C}_{*}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we define the generalized translation operators associated with the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform by

\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(u)\mathbb{W}^{a,b,d}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)e^{au^{2}+du}u^{2\gamma+1}du,

(3.1)

where the kernel
\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{a,b,d}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)
is given by

\mathbb{W}^{a,b,d}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)=\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)e^{-i\left[a(s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2})+d(s+t+u)\right]},

with
\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)
is given by

\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)=\frac{2^{2\gamma-1}\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\frac{\Delta^{2\gamma-1}}{(stu)^{2\gamma}}\chi_{[|s-t|,s+t]},

and represent the kernel of the translation operator associated with the Fourier–Bessel transform, given by
[
15
]

\mathcal{T}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(u)\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)u^{2\gamma+1}du,

and

\Delta=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{(x+y+z)(x+y-z)(x-y+z)(y+z-x)},

represents the area of a triangle with side lengths
\displaystyle s,t,u>0
, and
\displaystyle\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
\displaystyle A
.

Properties 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
, and
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
. . For all
\displaystyle h,g\in\mathcal{C}_{*}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and

\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel translation operator satisfies the following properties.

1.

Identity:
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{0,\gamma}=Id
.

2.

Symmetry:
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)=\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{s,\gamma}[h](t)
.

3.

Linearity:
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[\alpha h+\beta g](s)=\alpha\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)+\beta\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[g](s)
.

4.

Compact support: If
\displaystyle h(s)=0
for
\displaystyle y\geq r
, then
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)=0
, for all
\displaystyle|x-y|\geq r
.

In the following proposition, we prove the continuity of the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel translation operator from
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
into itself for all
\displaystyle p\in[1,\infty]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For all function
\displaystyle h
belongs to
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\displaystyle p\in[1,\infty]
and
\displaystyle s\in[0,\infty)
, the function
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h]
belongs to
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

\|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h]\|_{\gamma,p}\leq\|h\|_{\gamma,p}.

(3.2)

Proof.

For
\displaystyle p=1
and
\displaystyle\infty
, the proof is trivial. Let us now consider
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty)
and
\displaystyle h
belongs to
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then from the definition of the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel translation operator (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}h(u)\mathbb{W}^{a,b,d}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)e^{au^{2}+du}u^{2\gamma+1}du\right|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(u)|\left|\mathbb{W}^{a,b,d}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)\right|u^{2\gamma+1}du,

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(u)|\left|\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)e^{-i\left[a(s^{2}+t^{2}+u^{2})+d(s+t+u)\right]}\right|u^{2\gamma+1}du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|h(u)|\left|\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)\right|u^{2\gamma+1}du.

According to Hölder’s inequality, and the fact that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{W}_{\gamma}(s,t,u)\ u^{2\gamma+1}du=1,

we obtain that

\|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h]\|_{\gamma,p}\leq\|h\|_{\gamma,p}.

∎

4
Quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel convolution

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
, and
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
. For
\displaystyle h,g\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we define the generalized convolution product associated with the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform by

h\star g(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)g(s)e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}s^{2\gamma+1}ds,

(4.1)

where
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h]
is the generalized translation operators associated with the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform given by equation (
3.1
).

The basic properties of the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel convolution product are collected in the following statement.

Properties 4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
and
\displaystyle h,g,\upsilon\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel convolution product satisfies the following properties:

1.

Commutativity:
\displaystyle h\star g=g\star h
.

2.

Associativity:
\displaystyle(h\star g)\star\upsilon=h\star(g\star\upsilon)
.

In the following, we state the Young’s inequality for the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel convolution product.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
, and
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
. Assume that
\displaystyle 1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
and
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}+1
. If
\displaystyle h\in L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\displaystyle g\in L^{q}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then
\displaystyle h\star g\in L^{r}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and

\|h\star g\|_{\gamma,r}\leq\|h\|_{\gamma,p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}.

In particular, if
\displaystyle g,h\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel convolution product
\displaystyle h\star g
is defined almost everywhere on
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
and it belongs to
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle a,b,c,d,e\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\displaystyle b\neq 0
, and
\displaystyle\gamma>-1/2
. We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)g(s)e^{i(as^{2}+ds)}\right|=\left(\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}|g(s)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{r}}\left(|g(s)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{q}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)e^{-i\frac{d}{b}y^{2}}g(s)\right|s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}|g(s)|^{q}s^{2\gamma+1}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}s^{2\gamma+1}ds\right)^{\frac{r-p}{rp}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(s)|^{q}s^{2\gamma+1}ds\right)^{\frac{r-q}{rq}}.

According to the inequality
3.2
, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|h\star g(t)\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h]\right\|_{\gamma,p}^{r-p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{r-q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}|g(s)|^{q}s^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h\|_{\gamma,p}^{r-p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{r-q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}|g(s)|^{q}s^{2\gamma+1}ds.

It follows that,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|h\star g\right\|_{\gamma,r}^{r}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h\|_{\gamma,p}^{r-p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{r-q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{t,\gamma}[h](s)\right|^{p}|g(s)|^{q}s^{2\gamma+1}dst^{2\gamma+1}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h\|_{\gamma,p}^{r-p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{r-q}\int_{0}^{\infty}|g(s)|^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathbb{T}^{a,b,d}_{s,\gamma}[h](t)\right|^{p}t^{2\gamma+1}dts^{2\gamma+1}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h\|_{\gamma,p}^{r-p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{r-q}\|h\|_{\gamma,p}^{p}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h\|_{\gamma,p}^{r}\|g\|_{\gamma,q}^{r}.

∎

5
Donoho-Stark Uncertainty Principle

We aim in this subsection to prove a Donoho–Stark-type uncertainty principle for the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform. To do this, we begin by recalling the following definitions.

Consider two measurable subsets
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
. A function
\displaystyle h
is called
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}
-time-limited if we have

\|h-\chi_{\mathcal{M}}h\|_{\gamma,2}\leq\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}},

where

\chi_{\mathcal{M}}(s)=\begin{cases}1,&s\in\mathcal{M},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Similarly, a function
\displaystyle h
is said to be
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}
-quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel band-limited if its quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}
-limited, in other words

\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]-\chi_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\|_{\gamma,2}\leq\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}.

Next, we introduce the time-limiting operator
\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{M}}
and the frequency-limiting operator
\displaystyle Q_{\mathcal{N}}
, defined respectively as

P_{\mathcal{M}}h(s)=\chi_{\mathcal{M}}(s)h(s),

and

Q_{\mathcal{N}}(h)(s)=\mathbb{B}^{-c,-b,-a}_{-e,-d,\gamma}\left(\chi_{\mathcal{N}}(.)\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](.)\right)(s),\quad s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Note that
\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}}\|=1
.

We now state the Donoho-Stark uncertainty principle for the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\displaystyle h\in L^{2}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be a function with
\displaystyle\|h\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}=1
. Consider two measurable subsets
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

1.

\displaystyle h
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}
-time-limited,

2.

\displaystyle h
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}
-quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel band-limited,

then, we have

|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{N}|\geq\frac{|b|^{2(\mu+1)}}{c_{\gamma}^{2}}(1-\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}})^{2},

where
\displaystyle c_{\gamma}
is given by (
1.4
).

Proof.

We begin by expressing the unit 2-norm function
\displaystyle h
as

h=P_{\mathcal{M}}h+P_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}h=P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}h+P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}h+P_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}h.

According to the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h-P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}h\|_{\gamma,2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}h+P_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}h\|_{\gamma,2}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}^{c}}h\|_{\gamma,2}+\|P_{\mathcal{M}^{c}}h\|_{\gamma,2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}+\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}.

Applying the triangle inequality again, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h-P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}h\|_{\gamma,2}\geq\|h\|_{\gamma,2}-\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}h\|_{\gamma,2}.

(5.2)

By merging inequalities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we get

1-\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}h\|_{\gamma,2}\leq\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}+\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}},

that is,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}h\|_{\gamma,2}\geq 1-\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}.

(5.3)

Alternatively, we must establish an upper limit for the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}
.

\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{HS}^{2}=\left|\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{(ib)^{2\gamma+2}}\right|\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\mathcal{N}}\left|\mathbb{J}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}(s,t)\right|^{2}t^{2\gamma+1}|s|^{2\gamma+1}dtds.

According to inequality (
2.1
), we get

\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{HS}^{2}\leq\left|\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{(ib)^{2\gamma+2}}\right||\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{N}|.

From classical results in functional analysis and the above inequality, we get

\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{\gamma,2}^{2}\leq\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{HS}^{2}\leq\alpha_{\gamma}|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{N}|,

that is,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|P_{\mathcal{M}}Q_{\mathcal{N}}\|_{\gamma,2}\leq\left|\frac{c_{\gamma}}{(ib)^{\gamma+1}}\right|\sqrt{|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{N}|}.

(5.4)

Finally, combining the inequalities (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) we get

|\mathcal{M}||\mathcal{N}|\geq\frac{|b|^{2(\mu+1)}}{c_{\gamma}^{2}}(1-\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}-\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}})^{2}.

∎

In the following theorem, we provide another concentration-type uncertainty principle for quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform for functions in
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
be two measurable subsets of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and let
\displaystyle h
be a function in
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\displaystyle 1<p\leq 2
. We say:

1.

\displaystyle h
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}
time-limited in
\displaystyle L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-norm if

\|h-\chi_{\mathcal{M}}h\|_{\gamma,p}\leq\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}\|h\|_{\gamma,p}.

2.

\displaystyle h
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}
-quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel band-limited
\displaystyle L^{q}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-norm, where
\displaystyle q=p/(p-1)
, if

\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]-\chi_{\mathcal{N}}\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\|_{\gamma,q}\leq\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\|_{\gamma,q}.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{M}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{N}
be two measurable subsets of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and let
\displaystyle h
be a function in
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where
\displaystyle 1<p\leq 2
. Under the following assumptions:

1.

\displaystyle h
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{M}}
-time-limited in
\displaystyle L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-norm,

\displaystyle h

-quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel band-limited in

Proof.

Suppose that
\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|<\infty
and
\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}|<\infty
. Let
\displaystyle h\in L^{1}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{p}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where
\displaystyle 1<p\leq 2
. Since
\displaystyle h
is
\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}
-quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel band-limited in
\displaystyle L^{q}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
-norm, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right\|_{\gamma,q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}\left\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right\|_{\gamma,q}+\left(\int_{\mathcal{N}}\left|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h](t)\right|^{q}t^{2\gamma+1}dt\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}\left\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right\|_{\gamma,q}+|\mathcal{N}|^{1/q}\left\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right\|_{\gamma,\infty}

According to the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma of the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|\mathbb{B}^{a,b,c}_{d,e,\gamma}[h]\right\|_{\gamma,q}\leq\frac{c_{\gamma}}{|b|^{\gamma+1}}\frac{|\mathcal{N}|^{1/q}}{1-\epsilon_{\mathcal{N}}}\|h\|_{\gamma,1}.

(5.5)

Conclusion

In this work, we introduced the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform and established its fundamental properties, including continuity, the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, reversibility, and Parseval’s identity. We also defined the associated translation operator and convolution product, and studied their key properties. Finally, we proved a Donoho–Stark-type uncertainty principle in the context of the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform.

Future Works

The research presented here opens several directions that we are actively exploring:

Wavelet Transform in the Quadratic-Phase Fourier–Bessel Framework

We are currently developing wavelet transforms associated with the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel transform, focusing on admissible wavelets, reconstruction formulas, and localization properties.

Uncertainty Principles

We are extending the Donoho–Stark-type result obtained here to other forms of uncertainty principles, such as Hardy, Beurling, and Morgan-type inequalities, adapted to the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel domain.

Wavelet Packets

We are investigating wavelet packet constructions in this framework, aiming to develop flexible representations, efficient decomposition schemes, and sparse signal approximations.

Calderón’s Reproducing Formula

We are working on Calderón-type reproducing formulas in the quadratic-phase Fourier–Bessel setting to enable explicit reconstruction schemes, frame constructions, and continuous or discrete analysis.

These topics are currently under active study, and we anticipate that their full development will provide new theoretical tools and applications in harmonic analysis and signal processing within generalized Bessel domains.

Baicalein activates the type I interferon signaling pathway to inhibit infectious bronchitis virus infection
Authors: Ying Yang, Hongqiao Hu, Jinbin Zhang, Zhongqiong Yin, Xun Zhou, Lixia Li, Yuanfeng Zou, Mingyue Li, Dongmei Zhang, Shujun Dai, Yingying Zhang, Xu Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstactAvian infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) is a γ-coronavirus that primarily affects young chicks and laying hens. Its extensive serotype diversity and high mutation rate limit the effectiveness of current vaccines, leading to substantial economic losses in the poultry industry. In this study, we demonstrated that baicalein exerts potent antiviral activity against IBV, with a half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of 2.34 μM and a selectivity index (SI) of 17.39 in Vero cells. Baicalein inhibited multiple stages of the viral life cycle and reversed IBV-induced immunosuppression by activating the type I interferon (IFN-I) signaling pathway. Specifically, it upregulated the mRNA and protein expression of key components in the RLR signaling pathway, including the pattern recognition receptors MDA5 and RIG-I, as well as downstream molecules such as MAVS, TBK1, and IRF3. This upregulation promoted IRF3 phosphorylation and subsequent IFN-I production. Concurrently, baicalein activated the JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway, enhancing STAT1 phosphorylation and increasing the expression of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), including MX1 and OAS1, thereby synergistically reinforcing the antiviral state. In IBV-infected chicks, baicalein treatment significantly alleviated body weight loss and respiratory symptoms, reduced viral loads in the trachea and lung tissues, and upregulated the expression of key genes involved in both the RLR (MDA5, MAVS, IRF7, IFN-β) and JAK-STAT (STAT1, MX1 and OAS1) signaling pathways. In conclusion, baicalein demonstrates significant anti-IBV potential both in vitro and in vivo through dual regulation of the IFN-I signaling pathway, offering a promising perspective for the development of broad-spectrum anti-coronavirus therapeutics.Keywords: Infectious bronchitis virus, Baicalein, Type I interferon signaling pathway, Antiviral activity

Introduction

Infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), a member of the genus Gammacoronavirus, is a single-stranded positive-sense RNA virus (Mori et al., 2023) and the causative agent of infectious bronchitis (IB) in chickens. Chickens serve as the natural host, with young chicks and laying hens being particularly susceptible and exhibiting the most severe symptoms (Ignjatović and Sapats, 2000). IBV infection can lead to multi-systemic pathology and frequently predisposes birds to secondary or mixed infections, substantially increasing mortality (Najimudeen et al., 2020). The virus is characterized by rapid transmission, posing challenges for disease control (Cumming, 1969). Furthermore, a trend towards an expanding host range has been observed (Domanska-Blicharz et al., 2020; Shirvani et al., 2018), contributing to significant economic losses in the global poultry industry.

Currently, vaccination remains the primary strategy for IB control. Although various vaccines are available, including a prevention and control system in China that utilizes both domestically developed attenuated live vaccines and imported ones (Bijlenga et al., 2004), the existence of numerous IBV serotypes with little to no cross-protection, coupled with the virus's high mutation rate, limits the ability of existing vaccines to provide comprehensive protection (Jordan, 2017). Consequently, the challenge of controlling IB persists, underscoring an urgent need for novel preventive and therapeutic strategies.

The innate immune response serves as the host's first line of defense against pathogenic invasion and plays a critical role in controlling IBV infection. The type I interferon (IFN-I) signaling pathway, which induces antiviral gene expression through the RLR-JAK-STAT signaling cascade, constitutes a core antiviral mechanism for inhibiting IBV replication and clearing viral infection (Coccia and Battistini, 2015; Günther, Josenhans, and Wehkamp, 2016; Kato et al., 2006; Pichlmair et al., 2006). As a gamma-coronavirus, IBV has been demonstrated to employ multiple strategies to evade the IFN-I-mediated innate immune response, thereby achieving efficient replication and pathogenicity. These evasion mechanisms include interfering with pattern recognition receptor (PRR) recognition, cleaving adaptor proteins, inhibiting kinase activation, and blocking the nuclear translocation of transcription factors (Frieman et al., 2009; Huang et al., 2020; Kint et al., 2015; Ojha et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2017). Consequently, the identification of natural compounds capable of restoring the functionality of the IFN-I signaling pathway, holds significant therapeutic potential for counteracting viral immune evasion and inhibiting viral proliferation.

Baicalein, the primary bioactive flavonoid isolated from the dried roots of Scutellaria baicalensis Georgi (a perennial herb from the Lamiaceae family), constitutes up to 5.41% of the crude drug material. It has garnered widespread attention in the pharmaceutical, food, and cosmetic industries due to its diverse pharmacological properties, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antiviral activities (Chen et al., 2014; Dinda et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2024). Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that baicalein possesses broad-spectrum antiviral activity against various viruses, including RNA viruses such as human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), dengue virus (DENV), severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), and influenza virus, as well as DNA viruses like human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) and Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV). The antiviral mechanisms of baicalein are multifaceted.

Therefore, this study investigated the anti-IBV activity of baicalein both in vitro and in vivo, and elucidated its regulatory mechanism on the IFN-I signaling pathway. Our findings provide a theoretical basis for the clinical application of baicalein as a novel antiviral agent against IBV, and offer new insights for the development of natural product-based therapeutics targeting the IFN-I pathway.

Materials and methods

Cells, virus, and reagents

African green monkey kidney (Vero) cells were obtained from the China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC) and maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). The infectious bronchitis virus strain M41 (IBV-M41) was obtained from the China Veterinary Culture Collection Center (CVCC) and was propagated in Vero cells; the 50% tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50) was determined in Vero cells as 103.9 TCID50/0.1 mL. Baicalein (purity ≥ 98%) was purchased from Yuanye Bio-Technology (Shanghai, China), dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and diluted to the desired concentrations in cell culture medium or 0.5% sodium carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC—Na) solution immediately before use (final DMSO concentration ≤ 0.5%).

Cytotoxicity and antiviral activity assessment

The cytotoxic effect of baicalein on Vero cells was determined using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay according to the manufacturer's instructions. Baicalein was dissolved in DMSO and serially diluted in maintenance medium, then added to cell monolayers in 96-well plates (six replicates per concentration). Cell control and blank control wells were also included. After 48 h of incubation, CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, and the plates were incubated for an additional 30 min at 37°C. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm, and the 50% cytotoxic concentration (CC50) was calculated using the Reed-Muench method.

To assess the antiviral activity of baicalein against IBV, various concentrations of baicalein were mixed with 100 TCID50 of IBV and added to cell monolayers in 96-well plates. After 1 h of adsorption at 37°C to allow virus entry, the mixture was replaced with maintenance medium containing the corresponding concentrations of baicalein. When the cytopathic effect (CPE) in the virus control group reached 80%, the medium was removed, and CCK-8 reagent was added. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader, and the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of baicalein against IBV was calculated using the Reed-Muench method. The selectivity index (SI) was calculated as the ratio of CC50 to IC50.

Analysis of the antiviral mechanism of baicalein

To determine the stage at which baicalein inhibits the viral replication cycle, four experimental assays were designed: 1) Direct inactivation: IBV (104TCID50) was incubated with 15 μM baicalein for 1 h at 37°C prior to dilution and inoculation onto cells; 2) Adsorption: The virus-drug mixture was added to cells and incubated at 4°C for 1 h, followed by washing and replacement with fresh medium; 3) Entry: Virus was adsorbed to cells at 4°C for 1 h, then cells were washed and treated with baicalein at 37°C for 1 h to facilitate viral entry; 4) Intracellular replication: Cells were infected with virus for 1 h, and then treated with baicalein. A virus control group was included in each assay. After 48 h of treatment, cells were lysed with TRIzol reagent, and total RNA was extracted according to the manufacturer's instructions. The copy number of the IBV N gene was determined by absolute quantitative real-time PCR (FQ-PCR) to evaluate the inhibitory effect of baicalein at each stage of the viral life cycle (Peng et al., 2022).

Viral growth kinetics

Cell monolayers grown in six-well plates were infected with IBV at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 1 in the presence or absence of 15 μM baicalein. After 1 h of adsorption at 37°C, the inoculum was removed, and fresh medium containing 15 μM baicalein was added. Cells were harvested at 2, 6, 8, 12, 24, and 48 h post-infection. Total RNA was extracted, and the copy number of the IBV N gene was determined by FQ-PCR.

Quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was extracted from Vero cells or tissue samples using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer's instructions. Reverse transcription was performed using 5000 ng of RNA as template with a reverse transcription kit following the manufacturer's protocol. The reaction conditions were as follows: 25°C for 10 min, 55°C for 30 min, and 85°C for 5 min.

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-PCR) was performed using the SYBR Green kit. The reaction mixture consisted of 5 μL SYBR Green Master Mix, 0.5 μL each of forward and reverse primers (Table 1), 1 μL cDNA, and RNase-free water to a final volume of 10 μL. The amplification conditions were as follows: 50°C for 2 min; 94°C for 10 min; 95°C for 3 min; 39 cycles of 95°C for 5 s and 57.3°C for 30 s; followed by 65°C for 5 s and 95°C for 5 s. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−∆∆Cq method, with β-actin serving as the internal reference gene based on the threshold cycle (Cq) values.

Western blot analysis

The expression levels of proteins involved in the RLR signaling pathway (TBK1, IRF3 and p-IRF3) and the JAK-STAT1 signaling pathway (STAT1, p-STAT1, MX1 and OAS1) were detected by western blotting. Cell monolayers grown in six-well plates were infected with 2 mL of IBV (MOI=1). After 1 h of adsorption at 37°C, the inoculum was replaced with fresh medium containing either 15 μM baicalein or vehicle control. Cells were harvested at 6, 8, and 12 h post-infection, and total protein was extracted using a total protein extraction reagent according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Protein samples were mixed with 5× SDS loading buffer and denatured by boiling at 100°C for 10 min. Equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes under constant voltage conditions. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in TBST for 90 min at room temperature, followed by overnight incubation at 4°C with primary antibodies. The following primary antibodies were used: Anti-TBK1 (Proteintech), Anti-IRF3 (Proteintech), Anti-STAT1 (Proteintech), Anti-Phospho-STAT1 (Proteintech), Anti-MX1 (Proteintech), Anti-OAS1 (Proteintech), and Anti-Beta-Actin (Bioss). All antibodies were diluted according to the manufacturers' recommendations.

The membranes were washed six times with TBST (5 min each) and incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (Boster) for 1 h at room temperature. After washing, protein bands were visualized using an ECL detection reagent and imaged with a gel documentation system. Protein expression levels were quantified by densitometric analysis using ImageJ software, with β-actin serving as the internal control. The relative protein expression was calculated as the ratio of the target protein band intensity to that of β-actin.

Animal experiment design

A total of 90 one-day-old specific-pathogen-free (SPF) chicks were acclimatized under standard feeding conditions for 7 days. The chicks were then randomly divided into five groups: blank control group, IBV infection group, low-dose baicalein treatment group (100 mg/kg), medium-dose baicalein treatment group (200 mg/kg), and high-dose baicalein treatment group (400 mg/kg). At 7 days of age, all groups except the blank control were inoculated with the IBV-M41 strain (10⁶ EID50 in 0.2 mL per chick) via intraocular and intranasal routes. From the day of infection, chicks in the treatment groups were orally administered the corresponding doses of baicalein solution (1 mL per chick) daily for 7 consecutive days, while the IBV infection group and blank control group received an equal volume of 0.5% CMC—Na solution. Clinical symptoms (including mental status, respiratory condition, and shaking frequency) were observed and recorded daily, and clinical symptom scores were uniformly assessed.

On days 3, 5, and 7 post-infection (dpi), six chicks from each group were randomly selected, weighed, and euthanized for sample collection. Tracheal, lung, and kidney tissues were collected. Partial tissue samples were thoroughly homogenized, and total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent. Reverse transcription and cDNA synthesis were performed as described above, and the copy number of the IBV N gene was determined. Another portion of tissue samples was preserved in RNA preservation solution, and the mRNA expression levels of key genes in the signaling pathways at each time point were detected using the methods described above.

Statistical analysis

All experimental data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. Comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey's post hoc test for pairwise comparisons. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05, P < 0.01, and P < 0.001.

Results

Antiviral activity of baicalein against IBV

The cytotoxic effect of baicalein on Vero cells was evaluated using the CCK-8 assay. The results showed that baicalein exhibited no significant cytotoxicity at concentrations below 15 μM, and the 50% cytotoxic concentration (CC50) was determined to be 40.70 μM. Antiviral activity assays demonstrated that baicalein significantly inhibited IBV replication. At concentrations ≥ 1.875 μM, the inhibition rate of viral replication exceeded 50%, with a half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of 2.34 μM and a selectivity index (SI) of 17.39(Fig. 1B,C).

Based on the replication characteristics of IBV, we designed assays targeting different stages of the viral life cycle to investigate the mechanism of action of baicalein. The results revealed that baicalein exerted inhibitory effects at multiple stages of the viral life cycle. Treatment with baicalein significantly reduced the copy number of the IBV N gene during direct inactivation, adsorption, entry, and intracellular replication stages. Among these, the most potent inhibitory effect was observed during the intracellular replication stage, where baicalein treatment resulted in a 10-fold reduction in IBV N gene copy number(Fig. 1D). These findings indicate that baicalein inhibits IBV replication at multiple stages of the viral life cycle, with the most pronounced effect occurring during intracellular replication.

Viral growth curve analysis further elucidated the inhibitory characteristics of baicalein. In the untreated group, IBV exhibited a typical growth trend within 48 h post-infection, with viral copy numbers continuously increasing and reaching a peak at 48 h. In contrast, baicalein treatment significantly reduced viral copy numbers at 6, 8, 24, and 48 h post-infection (hpi) compared to the control group (P < 0.001). This inhibitory effect was particularly pronounced at 24 and 48 hpi, and viral copy numbers began to decline after 12 hpi(Fig. 1E). These results suggest that baicalein not only suppresses viral proliferation but may also promote the clearance of viral genes within infected cells.

Regulation of the RLR signaling pathway by baicalein following IBV infection

Within the RLR signaling pathway, RIG-I and MDA5 recognize double-stranded RNA generated during IBV replication, thereby initiating downstream signaling cascades that activate the innate immune response (Zhang et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2011). In this study, we investigated the effects of baicalein on the expression of key genes and proteins in the RLR pathway at 6, 8, and 12 hpi (Fig. 2).

(F-H) Protein expression levels of TBK1, IRF3, and p-IRF3 at 6, 8, and 12 hpi. The ratios of p-IRF3 to total IRF3 are shown below the blots. Mock, uninfected control; IBV, infected-untreated group; IBV + Baicalein, IBV-infected group treated with 15 μM baicalein. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n = 3). * P < 0.05, ** P < 0.01, *** P < 0.001 compared with the Mock group; # P < 0.05, ## P < 0.01, ### P < 0.001 compared with the IBV group.

At the transcriptional level, IBV infection significantly inhibited MDA5 mRNA expression at all three time points examined (P < 0.001). RIG-I expression was significantly downregulated at 6 hpi (P < 0.001), but recovered at 8 and 12 hpi. Baicalein treatment significantly reversed the IBV-induced inhibition of RIG-I and MDA5 (P < 0.01), restoring their expression to levels comparable to the control group. MAVS transcript levels showed no significant changes during the early stage of infection but were significantly inhibited by IBV at 12 hpi (P < 0.01). Baicalein treatment significantly upregulated MAVS expression at all time points (P < 0.001), with expression levels at 6 and 8 hpi even exceeding those of the control group. TBK1 mRNA expression was significantly suppressed during the early stage of infection (6 hpi; P < 0.001), and baicalein reversed this inhibition, with expression levels at 8 hpi significantly higher than those in the control group. IRF3 transcript levels were significantly downregulated at 6 hpi (P < 0.001), and baicalein intervention effectively restored its expression(Fig. 2A-E).

The protein level of TBK1 was elevated during the early stage of IBV infection but showed no significant difference from the control group by 12 hpi. Baicalein treatment significantly increased TBK1 protein expression at all time points compared to both the control and IBV groups. At 6 hpi, IRF3 protein expression was increased in the IBV group but decreased in the baicalein-treated group; however, p-IRF3 protein levels were significantly higher in the baicalein group than in the IBV group (P < 0.01). Analysis of the p-IRF3/IRF3 ratio revealed that IBV infection significantly inhibited IRF3 phosphorylation, while baicalein treatment markedly enhanced its phosphorylation level (P < 0.001). At 8 hpi, both IRF3 and p-IRF3 protein expression were elevated in the IBV and baicalein groups, with the baicalein group showing significantly higher IRF3 phosphorylation levels than the IBV group (P < 0.01). At 12 hpi, no significant differences in IRF3 protein expression were observed among groups, and p-IRF3 expression in the IBV group showed no significant change compared to the control group. However, both p-IRF3 expression and the p-IRF3/IRF3 ratio were significantly higher in the baicalein group than in both the control and IBV groups (P < 0.001)(Fig. 2F-H).

Regulation of the JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway by baicalein following IBV infection

Type I interferon activates the JAK-STAT1 pathway, inducing the expression of downstream antiviral effector genes that mediate antiviral responses. In this study, we investigated the regulatory mechanism of baicalein on this pathway by examining changes in the expressions of key genes and proteins at 6, 8, and 12 hpi(Fig. 3).

At the transcriptional level, the results showed that compared to the uninfected control group, IBV infection significantly inhibited JAK1 mRNA expression at 6 hpi (P < 0.001), while no significant differences were observed at 8 and 12 hpi. Baicalein treatment significantly upregulated JAK1 gene transcription at all time points (P < 0.001), with particularly marked increases at 8 and 12 hpi. The expression of the antiviral effector gene MX1 was significantly increased in the baicalein-treated group compared to the IBV group at all time points, and OAS1 expression was also significantly upregulated by baicalein at 6 hpi (P < 0.001). However, baicalein did not exhibit a significant upregulatory effect on STAT1 gene transcription during the early stage of infection(Fig. 3A-D).

The protein level of STAT1 phosphorylation is a critical step in JAK-STAT signaling transmission. At 6 hpi, STAT1 protein expression in the baicalein-treated group was significantly decreased compared to the control group, but p-STAT1 protein levels were significantly elevated (P < 0.01), with the p-STAT1/STAT1 ratio approximately three-fold higher than that in the IBV group, indicating that baicalein significantly promoted STAT1 phosphorylation. At 8 hpi, total STAT1 protein levels were similar across all groups, but p-STAT1 expression was significantly upregulated in the baicalein-treated group, with phosphorylation levels approximately twice those of the control and IBV groups. At 12 hpi, STAT1 protein expression in the IBV group was increased compared to the control group, while no significant difference was observed between the baicalein-treated and control groups. Although p-STAT1 protein levels showed no significant difference between the IBV and baicalein groups, analysis of the phosphorylation ratio still revealed a promoting effect of baicalein on STAT1 phosphorylation. Regarding antiviral effector proteins, MX1 protein expression showed no significant difference between the IBV group and the control group at any time point following IBV infection, whereas baicalein treatment significantly enhanced its expression. OAS1 protein expression increased during the later stage of infection, and baicalein treatment further significantly elevated its protein levels at all time points examined.(Fig. 3E-G)

Antiviral effect of baicalein in IBV-infected chicks

To further investigate the antiviral effect of baicalein and its regulatory effect on the IFN-I signaling pathway, a chicken model infected with IBV was established(Fig. 4A). On 3 dpi (10 days of age), body weight gain in all infected groups was significantly lower than that in the control group; however, the high-dose baicalein group showed significantly higher body weight than the IBV group. On 5 dpi (12 days of age), the medium- and high-dose groups exhibited significantly higher body weight than the IBV group. On 7 dpi (15 days of age), body weight in the medium- and high-dose groups was comparable to the control group, and all baicalein-treated groups had significantly higher body weight than the IBV group(Fig. 4B). Clinical observation showed that respiratory symptoms were milder in baicalein-treated chicks, with improvement beginning on 4dpi, and clinical symptom scores were significantly lower than in the IBV group(Fig. 4C).

Discussion

Baicalein restores the IBV-suppressed IFN-I signaling pathway

IBV poses a significant threat to the poultry industry. Due to its continuous genetic variation, current vaccines provide inadequate cross-protection, leading to substantial economic losses, and no specific clinical therapeutic agents are currently available (Jordan, 2017; Ignjatović and Sapats, 2000). As IBV is a gammacoronavirus, research into anti-IBV agents not only holds significant importance for the control of poultry diseases but may also provide a theoretical and experimental foundation for the broad-spectrum prevention and treatment of coronaviruses. In this study, we confirmed the inhibitory activity of baicalein against IBV and elucidated its molecular mechanism of action, which involves regulating the host IFN-I signaling pathway, specifically through the RLR and JAK/STAT1 pathways. Additionally, we demonstrated its therapeutic efficacy in IBV-infected chicks, indicating that baicalein holds promise as an anti-IBV candidate for poultry applications.

Multiple studies have confirmed the broad-spectrum antiviral activity of baicalein against various viruses in vitro, which aligns with our findings. Mori et al. (Mori et al., 2023) reported that baicalein potently inhibits the 3CL protease of coronaviruses, exhibiting antiviral activity against SARS-CoV-2, feline coronavirus, and bovine coronavirus. In an evaluation of flavonoids against human cytomegalovirus, Evers et al. (Evers et al., 2005) identified baicalein as the most effective inhibitor among the compounds tested. Additionally, baicalin, which shares similar pharmacological properties with baicalein, ha s been shown to effectively inhibit IBV replication in Vero cells (Feng et al., 2022; He et al., 2024), further supporting the promising application of Scutellaria baicalensis extracts against coronavirus infections in vitro.

However, the mechanisms by which baicalein acts against different viruses are not uniform. Previous studies have shown that it exerts direct virucidal effects against RSV and VSV (Coccia and Battistini, 2015; Han et al., 2021), interferes with viral adsorption and intracellular replication in the case of DENV (Brubaker et al., 2015; Suresh and Mosser, 2013), and disrupts viral synthesis while inhibiting enzyme activity against influenza virus (Baril et al., 2009; Hiscott et al., 2006; Kato et al., 2006; Pichlmair et al., 2006; van Zuylen et al., 2012). In contrast, the present study demonstrates that baicalein exerts anti-IBV activity primarily by restoring the host IFN-I signaling pathway, which is suppressed by IBV infection, rather than through direct inhibition of the virus. This immunomodulatory mechanism differs from the direct antiviral actions reported against other viruses, suggesting that the antiviral effect of baicalein is virus-specific and context-dependent. Notably, IBV is a coronavirus that employs multiple strategies to evade host innate immune responses, particularly by inhibiting the IFN-I signaling pathway (Frieman et al., 2009; Huang et al., 2020; Kint et al., 2015; Ojha et al., 2021). Therefore, restoring this pathway represents a more targeted strategy against IBV compared to direct viral inhibition. Consistent with this notion, baicalin, a related flavonoid, has also been shown to promote IFN-β production via MAVS upregulation (He et al., 2024), further supporting the view that modulation of the IFN-I pathway is a key mechanism for counteracting coronaviruses.

The type I interferon signaling pathway is a core innate immune pathway by which the host defends against viral infections. Upon recognition of viral double-stranded RNA by RLR receptors, a signaling cascade involving MAVS and TBK1/IKKε is activated, leading to IRF3/7 phosphorylation and subsequent IFN-Ⅰ production, which then initiates the expression of ISGs via the JAK-STAT pathway, thereby exerting potent antiviral effects (Coccia and Battistini, 2015; Günther et al., 2016). Coronaviruses employ multiple strategies to evade the host innate immune response mediated by the IFN-Ⅰ signaling pathway, including avoiding recognition by pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), disrupting signal transduction, inhibiting interferon production, and suppressing the expression of ISGs, all of which collectively facilitate immune evasion (Han et al., 2021; Miorin et al., 2020). Among these, the upstream RLR signaling pathway, as the core pathway for host recognition of RNA viruses, represents a key target for IBV immune evasion. RLRs, including RIG-I and MDA5, recognize double-stranded RNA generated during IBV replication and induce IFN-Ⅰ production through a signaling cascade, thereby mediating innate antiviral immunity (Liu et al., 2011; Xu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2021). In this study, IBV significantly inhibited the mRNA expression of the pattern recognition receptors RIG-I and MDA5, as well as key pathway factors MAVS, TBK1, and IRF3, indicating that IBV disrupts normal signal transduction through this pathway. Previous studies have confirmed that IBV interferes with the recognition, activation, and signal transmission of key RLR pathway factors by encoding proteins such as the N protein and nsp3, thereby suppressing IFN-Ⅰ production and achieving immune evasion (Frieman et al., 2009; Huang et al., 2020; Ojha et al., 2021). Accordingly, it can be inferred that IBV primarily evades host IFN-Ⅰ-mediated innate antiviral immunity by inhibiting the expression of key RLR pathway molecule.

Following baicalein treatment, the mRNA expression levels of RIG-I and MDA5 were significantly upregulated and restored to near-normal levels at all time points examined, and MAVS, TBK1, and IRF3 mRNA expression also recovered markedly. Moreover, baicalein promoted TBK1 protein synthesis and IRF3 phosphorylation, increasing the p-IRF3/IRF3 ratio. These findings indicate that baicalein effectively reverses IBV-induced inhibition of the RLR signaling pathway, thereby ensuring normal signal transduction.This result aligns with the findings of He et al. (He et al., 2024) on baicalin, who reported that baicalin promotes IFN-β production by upregulating MAVS expression, thereby inhibiting IBV replication. Similarly, Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2019) found that an extract of Hypericum perforatum upregulates IFN-α and IFN-β mRNA levels through the MDA5 signaling pathway, inhibiting IBV by modulating innate immune cytokines. Furthermore, the previous studies have demonstrated that natural compounds such as myricetin and palmatine promoting IFN-Ⅰ production by upregulating the expression of key factors including MDA5, MAVS, and IRF3 (Song et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024), suggesting that modulation of the RLR signaling pathway represents a common mechanism among natural flavonoids in exerting anti-IBV activity.

The JAK/STAT1 pathway, as the downstream cascade of the IFN-Ⅰ signaling pathway, is critical for mediating the antiviral effects of type I interferons. Upon binding of IFN-Ⅰ to cell surface receptors, JAK1 and TYK2 are activated, leading to STAT1 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, which subsequently induces the expression of ISGs and establishes the host antiviral immune barrier (Schindler et al., 2007; Stark et al., 1998). Previous studies have demonstrated that IBV blocks JAK/STAT1 pathway activation through mechanisms including JAK1 degradation and inhibition of STAT1 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation (Kint et al., 2015; Ma et al., 2022). In the present study, IBV infection significantly inhibited the mRNA levels of JAK1 and STAT1 and downregulated STAT1 phosphorylation, thereby disrupting downstream signal transduction. However, baicalein treatment significantly upregulated JAK1 mRNA expression, promoted STAT1 phosphorylation, and reversed IBV-induced inhibition of STAT1 activation, indicating that baicalein reactivates the JAK/STAT1 pathway suppressed by IBV and restores its signaling function. This finding aligns with Wu et al. (Wu et al., 2020), who reported that SARS-CoV-2 inhibits STAT1 phosphorylation via 3CLPro, and that flavonoids can counteract this inhibition to exert anticoronaviral effects. Similarly, Guo et al. (Guo et al., 2016) demonstrated that porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) suppresses the JAK/STAT1 pathway by inducing STAT1 degradation, and that natural compounds can antagonize coronavirus immune evasion by modulating the expression and activation of key pathway factors. Furthermore, Liu et al. (Liu et al., 2023) found that IBV PLpro inhibits the IFN-Ⅰ response by suppressing STAT1 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation. In the present study, baicalein effectively restored STAT1 phosphorylation levels, suggesting that baicalein may exert its effects by antagonizing the function of IBV PLpro.

Upon binding of IFN-Ⅰ to cell surface receptors, the JAK/STAT1 signaling pathway is activated, inducing the expression of downstream ISGs through a signaling cascade. As core effector molecules of this pathway, ISGs enhance viral recognition and confer resistance against viral infection. Among these, MX1 and OAS1 are two of the most extensively studied ISGs. MX1 exerts antiviral effects by capturing viral nucleocapsid proteins and preventing intracellular viral trafficking, while OAS1 degrades both viral and host RNA, thereby inhibiting viral protein synthesis (Gan et al., 2023; Mahjoor et al., 2023). Coronaviruses such as IBV and SARS-CoV-2 can suppress the activation of the JAK/STAT1 pathway, leading to downregulation of ISG expression, including MX1 and OAS1, thereby compromising host antiviral capacity (Kint et al., 2015; Wu et al., 2020). This study demonstrated that IBV infection significantly inhibited both mRNA and protein expressions of MX1 and OAS1. Following baicalein treatment, the expression levels of these ISGs were significantly upregulated, effectively counteracting the IBV-induced inhibition. These findings suggest that baicalein promotes the expression of downstream antiviral effector proteins by reactivating the JAK/STAT1 pathway suppressed by IBV, thereby blocking viral immune evasion. This result is consistent with previous studies on natural compounds with anti-IBV activity. Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2019) reported that an extract of Hypericum perforatum promotes ISG expression through modulation of innate immune pathways. In our previous studies, myricetin and palmatine exert anti-IBV effects by upregulating ISG expression (Song et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024). Collectively, these findings indicate that promoting the expression of ISGs such as MX1 and OAS1 represents a key downstream mechanism by which natural compounds exert their anti-IBV activity.

In vivo efficacy of baicalein in IBV-infected chicks

To validate the antiviral effect of baicalein in vivo, an animal model of IBV-infected chicks was established in this study. IBV is primarily transmitted via the respiratory tract, with chicks being the most susceptible population, and viral replication typically peaks between 3 and 7 dpi (Chen et al., 1996; Najimudeen et al., 2020). The results of this study showed that IBV-infected chicks exhibited significant respiratory symptoms accompanied by marked body weight loss, along with significantly increased IBV N gene expression and substantially elevated viral loads in the trachea and lungs. Following oral administration of baicalein, the respiratory symptoms and body weight loss induced by IBV infection were markedly alleviated, and IBV N gene expression in the trachea and lungs was significantly downregulated. These findings indicate that baicalein effectively inhibits IBV replication and infection in vivo. This result is consistent with the findings of Song et al. (Song et al., 2021), who reported that baicalein ameliorated SARS-CoV-2-induced body weight loss in mice, reduced viral loads, and alleviated lung injury.

Due to the inherent lack of RIG-I and IRF3 in chickens, MDA5 serves as the sole receptor capable of recognizing viral double-stranded RNA and transducing downstream signals to induce IFN production in chicken cells, with IRF7 substituting for IRF3 in mediating downstream signal transduction (Cheng et al., 2019; Karpala et al., 2011; Lee, Wu, and Lin, 2012). In this study, the expression of key factors in the IFN-Ⅰ signaling pathway was examined in the trachea, lungs, and kidneys of chicks. The results showed that baicalein significantly upregulated the mRNA levels of MDA5, MAVS, and IRF7, promoted IFN-β secretion, and enhanced the mRNA levels of STAT1, MX1, and OAS1. These findings demonstrate that baicalein effectively reverses IBV-induced inhibition of the IFN-Ⅰ signaling pathway and activates the host innate immune response in chicks. This aligns with the results obtained from in vitro studies, confirming that modulation of the IFN-Ⅰ signaling pathway is the core mechanism by which baicalein exerts its anti-IBV effects both in vitro and in vivo.

Conclusion

The present study demonstrates that baicalein exhibits potent antiviral activity against IBV both in vitro and in vivo. The underlying mechanism primarily involves the regulation of the IFN-Ⅰ signaling pathway, which reverses the immunosuppressive effects induced by IBV infection. These findings provide a theoretical basis for the practical application of baicalein in the prevention and control of IBV in poultry.

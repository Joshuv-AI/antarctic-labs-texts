On Testing Kronecker Product Structure in Tensor Factor Models
Clifford Lam

Abstract
We propose a test for testing the Kronecker product structure of a factor loading matrix implied by a tensor factor model with Tucker decomposition in the common component. Through defining a Kronecker product structure set, we define if a tensor time series response { 𝒴 t } \{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\} has a Kronecker product structure, equivalent to the ability to decompose { 𝒴 t } \{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\} according to a tensor factor model. Our test is built on analysing and comparing the residuals from fitting a full tensor factor model, and the residuals from fitting a (tensor) factor model on a reshaped version of the data. In the most extreme case, the reshaping is the vectorisation of the tensor data, and the factor loading matrix in such a case can be general if there is no Kronecker product structure present. Theoretical results are developed through asymptotic normality results on estimated residuals. Numerical experiments suggest that the size of the tests gets closer to the pre-set nominal value as the sample size or the order of the tensor gets larger, while the power increases with mode dimensions and the number of combined modes. We demonstrate out tests through a NYC taxi traffic data and a Fama-French matrix portfolio of returns.



Key words and phrases:
Tensor refold, tensor reshape, weak factors, factor-structured idiosyncratic errors.

1
Introduction

With rapid advance in information technology, high dimensional time series data observed in tensor form are becoming more readily available for analysis in fields such as finance, economics, bioinformatics or computer science, to name but a few areas. In many cases, low-rank structures in the tensor time series observed can be exploited, facilitating analysis and interpretations. The most commonly used devices are the CP-decomposition and the multilinear/Tucker decomposition of a tensor, leading to CP-tensor factor models
(
Chang et al.,, 2023
;
Han et al., 2024b,
)
and Tucker-decomposition tensor factor models
(
Chen et al.,, 2022
;
Han et al., 2024a,
;
Chen et al.,, 2024
;
Chen and Lam, 2024b,
)
for tensor time series factor models, respectively.
While tensor time series can be transformed back to vector time series through vectorisation and be analysed using traditional factor models for vector time series, the tensor structure of the data is lost and hence any corresponding interpretations from it. Moreover, vectorisation increases the dimension of the factor loading matrix significantly relative to the sample size, leading potentially to less accurate estimation and inferences
(
Chen and Lam, 2024b,
)
.

However, a tensor factor model comes with its assumptions. For using the Tucker decomposition in particular, a tensor factor model assumes that the factor loading matrix for the vectorised data is the Kronecker product of smaller dimensional factor loading matrices. For instance, suppose at each
t\in[T]
, a mean-zero matrix
\mathbf{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
is observed. Consider a matrix factor model (first studied in
Wang et al., (2019)
, and further extended/analysed in
Yu et al., 2022a ()
or
Chen and Fan, (2023)
for example) of the form

\mathbf{Y}_{t}=\mathbf{A}_{1}\mathbf{F}_{t}\mathbf{A}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t},

(1.1)

where
\mathbf{F}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{r_{1}\times r_{2}}
is the core factor,
\mathbf{A}_{k}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}
is the mode-
k
factor loading matrix, i.e.,
\mathbf{A}_{1}
and
\mathbf{A}_{2}
are respectively the row and column loading matrices, and
\mathbf{E}_{t}
is the noise. The vectorisation of (
1.1
) is

\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}\big)=(\mathbf{A}_{2}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{1})\,\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{F}_{t}\big)+\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{E}_{t}\big)\equiv\mathbf{A}_{V}\,\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{F}_{t}\big)+\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{E}_{t}\big),

(1.2)

where
\mathbf{A}_{V}:=\mathbf{A}_{2}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{1}
, which is a vector factor model for the time series data
\{\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}\big)\}
with factor loading matrix
\mathbf{A}_{V}
. Clearly, the implicit assumption of a Kronecker product structure for
\mathbf{A}_{V}
when using a matrix factor model for matrix-valued time series data should be the first thing to check before such a factor model is applied.

Motivated by this simple example, we propose a test in this paper to test the Kronecker product structure of the factor loading matrix implied in the vectorised data when using a Tucker-decomposition tensor factor model (TFM), and extend it to higher order tensors.
He et al., (2023)
has also noted this implicit assumption in a Tucker-decomposition matrix factor model, and proposes to test the “boundary” cases of each column (
resp.
row) of the data following a factor model with a common factor loading matrix, but with possibly distinct factors, or the whole matrix is just pure noise. Model (
1.2
) with a general
\mathbf{A}_{V}
also implies a vector factor model with potentially different factor loading matrices for each column (
resp.
row) of the data, but they share the same factors. To explore the data as a matrix, connectedness through having a set of shared common factors rather than having the same factor loading matrix with all distinct factors is more meaningful. Practically, (
1.2
) is an alternative model easier to be satisfied by data than the “boundary” cases in
He et al., (2023)
, since the data still follows a more general factor model, just the implied Kronecker product structure in the factor loading matrix
\mathbf{A}_{V}
is lost. This comes as no surprise then, that in all of the tests in
He et al., (2023)
for their real data analyses, they cannot reject the null hypothesis of a matrix factor model. An easier alternative such as (
1.2
) with just a general
\mathbf{A}_{V}
can provide a more critical test for the null hypothesis of a matrix factor model. See our portfolio return example in Section
5.2
for cases where our test can reject the null hypothesis of a matrix factor model, when
He et al., (2023)
cannot.

We also stress that our model is fundamentally different from those used in testing for Kronecker product structure in the covariance matrix of the data. For example,
Yu et al., 2022b ()
and
Guggenberger et al., (2023)
both propose tests for the Kronecker product structure of the covariance matrix of a vectorised matrix data. For model (
1.1
), even in the simplest hypothetical case of
\mathbf{E}_{t}
and
\mathbf{F}_{t}
being independent and
\mathbf{F}_{t}
contains independent standard normal random variables, we have

\text{Cov}(\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}\big))=\mathbf{A}_{2}\mathbf{A}_{2}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{1}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{\prime}+\text{Cov}(\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{E}_{t}\big)),

so that the covariance matrix is never exactly of Kronecker product structure because of
\mathbf{E}_{t}
. Moreover, even with
\mathbf{E}_{t}=\mathbf{0}
, both
\mathbf{A}_{1}\mathbf{A}_{1}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{A}_{2}\mathbf{A}_{2}^{\prime}
are of low rank, which is different from the full rank component matrices in the two papers mentioned above.

Our contributions in this paper are threefold. Firstly, as a first in the literature, we propose a test to test directly a Tucker-decomposition TFM against the alternative of a (tensor) factor model with Kronecker product structure lost in some of its factor loading matrices. As shown in Section
3
, for higher order tensors, testing against a tensor-decomposition TFM can be against a tensor factor model for the reshaped data, but not necessarily the vectorised data. This gives rise to flexibility and in fact statistical power in practical situations. Secondly, our analysis allows for weak factors, with our theoretical results developed to spell out rates of convergence explicitly. Last but not least, as a useful by-product, we developed tensor reshape theorems which can be useful in their own rights.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. Section
2
introduces the notations used throughout this paper, and define the tensor reshape operation used for our tests. Section
3
introduces the Kronecker product structure set and pinpoints exactly through a theorem when a tensor time series
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
follows a Tucker-decomposition TFM. This becomes the basis for the construction of our test statistics. Section
4
lays down all the assumptions of the paper, and presents the main theoretical results for our test statistics to be valid. Section
5
presents our simulation results and two sets of real data analyses. Finally, Section
6
provides links to the supplement of this paper and to the instructions of the R package
KOFM
for this paper.

2.2
Introduction to tensor reshape

In this subsection, we introduce tensor reshape. Given an order-
K
tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbbm{R}^{I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{K}}
and a set with ordered, strictly ascending elements
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\subseteq[K]
, the Reshape
(\cdot,\cdot)
operator is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\text{If }\ell=1,\;

\displaystyle\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{1}\}\big):=\textnormal{Fold}_{K}\big(\text{mat}_{a_{1}}({\mathcal{X}}),\{I_{1},\dots,I_{a_{1}-1},I_{a_{1}+1},\dots,I_{K},I_{a_{1}}\}\big);

\displaystyle\text{if }\ell=2,\;

\displaystyle\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\big):=\textnormal{Fold}_{K-1}\big(\mathcal{X}_{a_{1}\sim a_{2}},\{I_{1},\dots,I_{a_{1}-1},I_{a_{1}+1},\dots,I_{a_{2}-1},I_{a_{2}+1},\dots,I_{K},I_{a_{1}}I_{a_{2}}\}\big),

\displaystyle\text{where }\mathcal{X}_{a_{1}\sim a_{2}}:=\begin{pmatrix}\text{mat}_{a_{1}}\big\{\textnormal{Fold}\big(\text{mat}_{a_{2}}({\mathcal{X}})_{1\cdot},\{I_{1},\dots,I_{a_{2}-1},I_{a_{2}+1},\dots,I_{K}\}\big)\big\}\\
\ldots\\
\text{mat}_{a_{1}}\big\{\textnormal{Fold}\big(\text{mat}_{a_{2}}({\mathcal{X}})_{I_{a_{2}}\cdot},\{I_{1},\dots,I_{a_{2}-1},I_{a_{2}+1},\dots,I_{K}\}\big)\big\}\end{pmatrix};

\displaystyle\text{if }\ell\geq 3,\;

\displaystyle\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\big):=\textnormal{Reshape}\big[\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{\ell-1},a_{\ell}\}\big),\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell-2},K-1\}\big].

Hence, reshaping an order-
K
tensor along
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}
results in an order-
(K-(\ell-1))
tensor. A heuristic view of
\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\big)
is that all modes of
\mathcal{X}
with indices
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}
are “merged” into a single mode acting as the last mode as a result. Note that one may recover
\mathcal{X}
from
\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\big)
given the original dimension of
\mathcal{X}
and
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}
.

As a simple example on tensor reshape, consider a matrix
\mathbf{X}\in\mathbbm{R}^{I_{1}\times I_{2}}
. Trivially, we have

\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathbf{X},\{2\}\big)=\mathbf{X},\;\;\;\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathbf{X},\{1\}\big)=\mathbf{X}^{\prime}.

Moreover,
\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathbf{X},\{1,2\}\big)=\textnormal{Fold}_{1}(\mathbf{X}_{1\sim 2},\{I_{1}I_{2}\})=\mathbf{X}_{1\sim 2}=\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{X}\big)
since

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{1\sim 2}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{mat}_{1}\big\{\textnormal{Fold}\big(\text{mat}_{2}({\mathbf{X}})_{1\cdot},\{I_{1}\}\big)\big\}\\
\ldots\\
\text{mat}_{1}\big\{\textnormal{Fold}\big(\text{mat}_{2}({\mathbf{X}})_{I_{2}\cdot},\{I_{1}\}\big)\big\}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(\mathbf{X}^{\prime})_{1\cdot}\\
\ldots\\
(\mathbf{X}^{\prime})_{I_{2}\cdot}\end{pmatrix}=\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{X}\big).

In fact, it holds for any order-
K
tensor
\mathcal{X}
that
\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},[K]\big)=\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{X}\big)
.

Some useful algebra of tensor reshape
:
The reshape operator is linear in the first argument, i.e.,

\textnormal{Reshape}\big(b_{1}\mathcal{X}_{1}+b_{2}\mathcal{X}_{2},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\big)=b_{1}\cdot\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X}_{1},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\big)+b_{2}\cdot\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X}_{2},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\big).

Moreover, for two sets
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}
,
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{g}\}
such that
a_{\ell}<b_{1}
(i.e., all elements in the first set are less than those in the second), it holds that

\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},b_{1},\dots,b_{g}\}\big)=\textnormal{Reshape}\big[\textnormal{Reshape}\big(\mathcal{X},\{b_{1},\dots,b_{g}\}\big),\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},K-g+1\}\big].

Note that
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell},K-g+1\}
is indeed strictly ascending since

a_{\ell}\leq b_{1}-1\leq b_{g}-(g-1)-1=b_{g}-g\leq K-g.

3
Factor Model and Testing its Kronecker Product Structure

Section
3.1
introduces the concept of factor models with Kronecker product structure and lays down the technical details for the testing problem. For an integral reading experience, readers can go straight to Section
3.2
where equations and terms can be referred back to Section
3.1
whenever necessary.

3.1
Factor model and Kronecker product structure

We begin by introducing the Kronecker product structure set which facilitates description of our models.

Definition 1

(Kronecker product structure set)

Given an ordered set of positive integers
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{\kappa}\}
, the Kronecker product structure set is a set of full column rank matrices defined as

\mathcal{K}_{b_{1}\times\dots\times b_{\kappa}}:=\big\{\mathbf{A}\,|\,\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_{\kappa}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{1}\text{ with }\mathbf{A}_{j}\in\mathbbm{R}^{b_{j}\times u_{j}}\text{ of rank }u_{j}\ll b_{j},\,\|\mathbf{A}_{j,\cdot i}\|^{2}\asymp b_{j}^{\delta_{j,i}},\,\delta_{j,i}\in(0,1]\big\}.

The Kronecker product structure set defined by Definition
1
characterises the factor loading matrix, and requiring
\delta_{j,i}>0
is to ensure certain factor strength in each loading matrix. See Assumptions (L1) and (L2) in Section
4.1
for the technical details. The form of factor models is depicted below, with the feature of Kronecker product structure.

Definition 2

(Factor models and Kronecker product structure)

Given a series of mean-zero order-
K
tensors
\mathcal{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times\dots\times d_{K}}
for
t\in[T]
and a set with ordered, ascending elements
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}\subseteq[K]
, we say
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
follows a factor model along
\mathcal{A}
if for
t\in[T]
,

\begin{split}\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})&=\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\times_{j=1}^{K-\ell+1}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},j}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},\end{split}

(3.1)

where
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\in\mathbbm{R}^{I_{1}\times\dots\times I_{K-\ell+1}}
is the order-
(K-\ell+1)
tensor by reshaping
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
along
\mathcal{A}
, the common component
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
consists of the core factor
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{\pi_{1}\times\dots\times\pi_{K-\ell+1}}
and loading matrices
\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},j}\in\mathbbm{R}^{I_{j}\times\pi_{j}}
with rank
\pi_{j}\ll I_{j}
for
j\in[K-\ell+1]
, and
\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
is the noise. We further make the following classifications.

1.

\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has a Kronecker product structure if
\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K-\ell+1}\in\mathcal{K}_{d_{a_{1}}\times\dots\times d_{a_{\ell}}}
;

2.

\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has no Kronecker product structure along
\mathcal{A}
if
\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K-\ell+1}\notin\mathcal{K}_{d_{a_{1}}\times\dots\times d_{a_{\ell}}}
.

Definition
2
formally defines the form of factor models considered in this paper. A key information lying in Definition
2
.1 is that if the Kronecker product structure holds along some
\mathcal{A}
, the structure holds along any
\mathcal{A}
; see the discussion below Theorem
1
for details. Note that if
\ell=1
in Definition
2
, i.e.,
\mathcal{A}
contains only one element (representing the mode index), for each order-
K
tensor
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
,
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{a_{1}\})
is the order-
K
tensor constructed from
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
by treating mode-
a_{1}
as mode-
K
. Hence, the factor model of
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
along
\{a_{1}\}
returns to a Tucker-decomposition TFM
(
Chen et al.,, 2022
;
He et al., 2022a,
)
of
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
with mode indices changed. For instance, we may read (
3.1
) along
\mathcal{A}=\{K\}
as

\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{K}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}.

Hence Definition
2
.1 automatically describes
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
if
\ell=1
, implying that Kronecker product structure is only non-trivial for
\ell\geq 2
(hence
K\geq 2
).
To demystify Definition
2
.1, we next present Theorem
1
which, as a first in the literature, spells out the equivalence of Tucker-decomposition TFM under tensor reshape.

Theorem 1

(Tensor Reshape Theorem I)

With the notations in Definition
2
,
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
following (
3.1
) along any given
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\}\subseteq[K]
with a Kronecker product structure is equivalent to
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
following a Tucker-decomposition factor model such that

\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\mathcal{C}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{t}\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{K}\mathbf{A}_{K}+\mathcal{E}_{t},

(3.2)

where
\mathcal{C}_{t}
is the common component,
\mathcal{F}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{r_{1}\times\dots\times r_{K}}
is the core factor, each
\mathbf{A}_{k}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}
with
r_{k}\ll d_{k}
is the mode-
k
loading matrix, and
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is the noise. More importantly, with
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}:=[K]\setminus\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathcal{A}),\;\;\;\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{A}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K-\ell+1}=\otimes_{i\in\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{A}_{i},\;\;\;\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},j}=\mathbf{A}_{\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\ast}}\;\;\;\text{for }j\in[K-\ell].

Moreover, (
3.2
) uniquely determines (
3.1
), and (
3.1
) determines (
3.2
) up to an arbitrary set
\{\mathbf{A}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathcal{A}}
.

Theorem
1
reveals that a factor model on
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
with Kronecker product structure in Definition
2
is in fact a Tucker-decomposition TFM on
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
. We point out that Theorem
1
enables us to reshape any order-
K
tensor
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
(under Tucker-decomposition TFM) along some selected
\mathcal{A}
, so that
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\}
still follows some Tucker-decomposition TFM. This salvages the practical issue of insufficient mode dimensions and potentially boosts performances of tests and estimations, while interpretability is not necessarily lost much; see Remark
2
. The identification of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are discussed in the supplement.

Remark 1

Both (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) are based on a Tucker decomposition for the observed tensor. Other tensor decompositions are possible, such as the CP decomposition
(
Kolda and Bader,, 2009
)
and the Low Separation Rank (LSR) decomposition
(
Taki et al.,, 2024
)
, etc. As CP decomposition is a special Tucker decomposition, our defined factor model is more general. The LSR decomposition is generalised further from Tucker decomposition, but the structure is less helpful here and brings in unnecessary complication due to the arbitrary separation rank.

Remark 2

We discuss here the practical significance of Theorem
1
, which can be of independent interest. As mentioned in Section
2.2
, modes selected by
\mathcal{A}
are merged into a single one from
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
to
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})
. Hence if
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
is deemed to follow a Tucker-decomposition TFM, we may choose
\mathcal{A}
to include modes with small dimensions relative to others’ and directly work on
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\}
which follows a Tucker-decomposition TFM guaranteed by Theorem
1
. Consider a simple example that for
t\in[T]
,
\mathcal{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times d_{3}}
satisfies (
3.2
) with all factors pervasive. If
T\asymp d_{1}\asymp d_{2}^{2}\asymp d_{3}^{2}
, i.e., the observed tensor is unbalanced, then the squared error of each (PCA-based) estimated common component on
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
is
O_{P}(1/T)
, that on
\{\textnormal{{vec}}(\mathcal{Y}_{t})\}
is
O_{P}(1/T)
and that on
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{2,3\})\}
is
O_{P}(1/T^{2})
.

3.2
A test on Kronecker product structure

The testing problem on Kronecker product structure is formally defined in the following, with an example on an order-2 tensor (i.e., a matrix) given.

For each
t\in[T]
, we observe a mean-zero order-
K
tensor
\mathcal{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times\dots\times d_{K}}
with
K\geq 2
(otherwise the test is trivial as explained in Section
3.1
). Without loss of generality, let
v<K
be a given positive integer and denote
\mathcal{A}=\{v,v+1,\dots,K-1,K\}
which contains the mode indices along which the Kronecker product structure might be lost; see the alternative hypothesis
H_{1}
below. Suppose
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
follows a factor model along
\mathcal{A}
as in Definition
2
, with notations therein except that we now read (
3.1
) as

\begin{split}\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})&=\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{v-1}\mathbf{A}_{v-1}\times_{v}\mathbf{A}_{V}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},\end{split}

(3.3)

where
\mathbf{A}_{j}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{j}\times r_{j}}
for
j\in[v-1]
(if
v>1
) and
\mathbf{A}_{V}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{V}\times r_{V}}
with
d_{V}:=\prod_{i=v}^{K}d_{i}
. Essentially, the order-
v
tensor
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})
follows a Tucker-decomposition TFM. The set
\{r_{1},\dots,r_{v-1},r_{V}\}
is assumed known and any consistent estimators
(
Han et al.,, 2022
;
Chen and Lam, 2024a,
, e.g.)
can be used in practice. With
\mathcal{K}_{d_{v}\times\dots\times d_{K}}
defined in Definition
1
, we consider a hypothesis test as follows:

\begin{split}&H_{0}:\text{$\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}$ has a Kronecker product structure, i.e., $\mathbf{A}_{V}\in\mathcal{K}_{d_{v}\times\dots\times d_{K}}$};\\
&H_{1}:\text{$\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}$ has no Kronecker product structure along $\mathcal{A}$, i.e., $\mathbf{A}_{V}\notin\mathcal{K}_{d_{v}\times\dots\times d_{K}}$}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Besides the complexity of being a composite testing problem, the difficulty of (
3.4
) is elevated by the fact that
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
under the alternative has no explicit form without reshaping along
\mathcal{A}
. Fortunately, the factor structure in (
3.3
) is stable under both hypotheses. That is, the estimation of
\{\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},\mathbf{A}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{A}_{v-1},\mathbf{A}_{V}\}
is always feasible. In particular, thanks to Theorem
1
, we have the following under
H_{0}
:

\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\mathcal{C}_{t}+\mathcal{E}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{t}\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{v-1}\mathbf{A}_{v-1}\times_{v}\mathbf{A}_{v}\times_{v+1}\dots\times_{K}\mathbf{A}_{K}+\mathcal{E}_{t},

(3.5)

where
\mathbf{A}_{k}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{k}}
for
k\in[K]
(hence the first
v-1
loading matrices are exactly those in (
3.3
)), and that

\displaystyle\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},\;\;\;\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},\;\;\;\mathbf{A}_{K}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{K-1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{v}=\mathbf{A}_{V}.

Example 1

Let
\mathbf{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}}
be a matrix-valued observation at
t\in[T]
. For the setup, we can only specify
\mathcal{A}=\{1,2\}
(which is the only non-trivial case here as discussed in Section
3.1
). The hypothesis test (
3.4
) is simplified as follows, with
\mathcal{A}
reflected by the vectorisation:

\displaystyle H_{0}:\mathbf{Y}_{t}=\mathbf{A}_{1}\mathbf{F}_{t}\mathbf{A}_{2}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t};

\displaystyle H_{1}:\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{t}\big)=\mathbf{A}_{V}\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{F}_{t}\big)+\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathbf{E}_{t}\big),\;\;\;\text{with $\mathbf{A}_{V}\notin\mathcal{K}_{d_{1}\times d_{2}}$}.

3.3
Constructing the test statistic

Despite the obscure
\mathcal{K}_{d_{v}\times\dots\times d_{K}}
in (
3.4
), we may resort to the Tucker-decomposition TFM in (
3.5
) under
H_{0}
. To construct the test, we first obtain estimators for the (standardised) loading matrices in (
3.3
). For
j\in[v-1]
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{j}
is defined as the eigenvector matrix corresponding to the
r_{j}
largest eigenvalues of

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})_{(j)}\,\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})_{(j)}^{\prime},

where
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})_{(j)}
represents the mode-
j
unfolding matrix of
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})
(see Section
2.1
). Similarly,
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{V}
is the eigenvector matrix corresponding to the
r_{V}
largest eigenvalues of

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})_{(v)}\,\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})_{(v)}^{\prime}.

Then
\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
are respectively estimated by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}

\displaystyle:=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\times_{j=1}^{v-1}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{j}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{j}^{\prime})\times_{v}(\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{V}\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}}_{V}^{\prime}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}

\displaystyle:=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})-\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}.

(3.7)

For (
3.5
),
\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{j}
for
j\in[v-1]
is defined as the eigenvector matrix corresponding to the
r_{j}
largest eigenvalues of
T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(j)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(j)}^{\prime}
. Next, denote
\mathcal{R}
as the set of all divisor combinations of
r_{V}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{R}:=\Big\{\big(\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{K-v+1}\big)\,\Big|\,\prod_{j=1}^{K-v+1}\pi_{j}=r_{V}\text{ and each }\pi_{j}\in\mathbbm{Z}^{+}\text{ with }\pi_{j}\leq d_{j+v-1}\Big\}.

(3.8)

Let the
m
-th element of
\mathcal{R}
be
(\pi_{m,1},\dots,\pi_{m,K-v+1})
. Then for
i\in\{v,v+1,\dots,K\}
, we obtain
\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{m,i}
as the eigenvector matrix corresponding to the
\pi_{m,i-v+1}
largest eigenvalues of
T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(i)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(i)}^{\prime}
. The common component and residual estimators are hence obtained as

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{m,t}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{Y}_{t}\times_{j=1}^{v-1}(\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{j}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{j}^{\prime})\times_{i=v}^{K}(\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{m,i}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{m,i}^{\prime}),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{m,t}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{Y}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{m,t}.

(3.10)

Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
be the order-
K
tensor with the same dimension as
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
such that
\textnormal{Reshape}(\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
. Define
k^{\ast}:=\argmin_{k\in[K]}\{d_{k}\}
and denote the mode-
k^{\ast}
unfolding of
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{m,t}
as
\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{t,(k^{\ast})}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{E}}_{m,t,(k^{\ast})}
, respectively. Theorem
2
(in Section
4.2
) tells us that there exists
m\in[|\mathcal{R}|]
such that for each
t\in[T]
,
j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]
, both

\displaystyle x_{j,t}:=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\widetilde{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2},\;\;\;y_{m,j,t}:=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\widehat{E}_{m,t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2},

are asymptotically distributed the same under
H_{0}
, and
x_{j,t}
in particular is distributed the same under either
H_{0}
or
H_{1}
. Let
\mathbbm{P}_{x,j}
and
\mathbbm{P}_{y,m,j}
respectively denote the empirical probability measures induced by the empirical cumulative distribution functions for
\{x_{j,t}\}_{t\in[T]}
and
\{y_{m,j,t}\}_{t\in[T]}
:

\begin{split}&\mathbbm{F}_{x,j}(c):=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}\{x_{j,t}\leq c\},\;\;\;\mathbbm{F}_{y,m,j}(c):=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}\{y_{m,j,t}\leq c\}.\end{split}

(3.11)

Let
\widehat{q}_{x,j}(\alpha):=\inf\big\{c\;|\;\mathbbm{F}_{x,j}(c)\geq 1-\alpha\big\}
. The intuition here is that if
H_{0}
is satisfied, then over different
j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]
, the cumulative distribution functions
\mathbbm{F}_{x,j}(\cdot)
and
\mathbbm{F}_{y,m,j}(\cdot)
should be similar. However, if
H_{1}
is true, then we expect the residuals in
\widehat{\mathbf{E}}_{m,t,(k^{\ast})}
to be inflated, so that the empirical probability of
\{y_{m,j,t}\geq\widehat{q}_{x,j}(\alpha)\}
is expected to be larger than
\alpha
; see the theoretical statement on this in Theorem
3
. To incorporate this across different
j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]
, we compare the 5% quantile of
T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}\{y_{m,j,t}\geq\widehat{q}_{x,j}(\alpha)\}
over
j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]
to
\alpha
, and expect it to be larger than
\alpha
under
H_{1}
.

Since with the wrong number of factors, a particular
m\in[|\mathcal{R}|]
will in general inflate the residuals
y_{m,j,t}
further,
in practice, to be on the conservative side, we reject
H_{0}
if

\min_{m\in[|\mathcal{R}|]}\Bigg\{\text{5\% quantile of }\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}\{y_{m,j,t}\geq\widehat{q}_{x,j}(\alpha)\}\text{ over $j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]$}\Bigg\}>\alpha,

(3.12)

noting that exactly one element in
\mathcal{R}
represents the true number of factors on the modes with indices in
\mathcal{A}
. We also point out that there are other possible ways to aggregate the information from each
j
, but (
3.12
) empirically works well and circumvents possible issues such as heavy-tailed noise, under- or over-estimation on the number of factors, and insufficient data dimensions; see Section
5.1
.

Remark 3

(Explanation of
\mathcal{R}
in (
3.8
))
It is possible to perform the test directly using the number of factors for modes in
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}
only contains the number of factors
r_{j}
,
j=v,\dots,K
, in (
3.5
). This is guaranteed by the Tucker-decomposition TFM under
H_{0}
in (
3.4
). However, usually in practice we need to estimate the number of factors which are invalid under
H_{1}
in (
3.4
). This leads to unstable estimated number of factors and hence unstable test statistic, which we address by introducing
\mathcal{R}
in (
3.8
).

4
Assumptions and Theoretical Results

4.1
Assumptions

This section presents all the assumptions for testing
H_{0}
against
H_{1}
in (
3.4
). Another version (with only different notations) of Assumptions (L1) and (L2) for the identification of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) is included in the supplement, with identification theorem presented and proved there.

(L1)

For each
j\in[v-1]
, we assume that
\mathbf{A}_{j}
in (
3.3
) is of full rank and as
d_{j}\to\infty
,

\mathbf{Z}_{j}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{j}\mathbf{Z}_{j}^{-1/2}\to\bm{\Sigma}_{A,j},

(4.1)

where
\bm{\Sigma}_{A,j}
is positive definite with all eigenvalues bounded away from 0 and infinity, and
\mathbf{Z}_{j}
is a diagonal matrix with
(\mathbf{Z}_{j})_{hh}\asymp I_{j}^{\delta_{j,h}}
for
h\in[r_{j}]
and the ordered factor strengths
1/2<\delta_{j,r_{j}}\leq\dots\leq\delta_{j,2}\leq\delta_{j,1}\leq 1
.

We assume that
\mathbf{A}_{V}
also has the above form with
\mathbf{Z}_{V}
and
\bm{\Sigma}_{A,V}
, except that only the maximum and minimum factor strengths are ordered, i.e.,
1/2<\delta_{V,r_{V}}\leq\delta_{V,h}\leq\delta_{V,1}\leq 1
for any
h\in[r_{V}]
.

(L2)

With
\mathcal{A}=\{v,v+1,\dots,K\}
, we assume that for each
i\in\mathcal{A}
,
\mathbf{A}_{i}
in (
3.5
) is of full rank and as
d_{i}\to\infty
,

\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{-1/2}\mathbf{A}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{Z}_{i}^{-1/2}\to\bm{\Sigma}_{A,i},

(4.2)

where
\bm{\Sigma}_{A,i}
is positive definite with all eigenvalues bounded away from 0 and infinity, and
\mathbf{Z}_{i}
is a diagonal matrix with
(\mathbf{Z}_{i})_{hh}\asymp d_{i}^{\delta_{i,h}}
for
h\in[r_{i}]
and the ordered factor strengths
1/2<\delta_{i,r_{i}}\leq\dots\leq\delta_{i,2}\leq\delta_{i,1}\leq 1
.

(F1)

(Time series in
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
)

There is
\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{reshape},f,t}
the same dimension as
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{reshape},f,t-w}
. The time series
\{\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{reshape},f,t}\}
has
i.i.d.
elements with mean
0
, variance
1
and uniformly bounded fourth order moments. The coefficients
a_{f,w}
satisfy
\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}^{2}=1
and
\sum_{w\geq 0}|a_{f,w}|\leq c
for some constant
c
.

(E1)

(Decomposition of
\mathcal{E}_{t}
)

The noise
\mathcal{E}_{t}
(such that
\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{A})
) can be decomposed as

\mathcal{E}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{e,t}\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{e,1}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{A}_{e,K}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t},

(4.3)

where order-
K
tensors
\mathcal{F}_{e,t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{r_{e,1}\times\cdots\times r_{e,K}}
and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times\cdots\times d_{K}}
contain independent mean zero elements with unit variance, with the two time series
\{\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\}
and
\{\mathcal{F}_{e,t}\}
being independent. The order-
K
tensor
\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}
contains the standard deviations of the corresponding elements in
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
, and has elements uniformly bounded.

Moreover, for each
k\in[K]
,
\mathbf{A}_{e,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{k}\times r_{e,k}}
is (approximately) sparse such that
\big\|\mathbf{A}_{e,k}\big\|_{1}=O(1)
.

(E2)

(Time series in
\mathcal{E}_{t}
)

There is
\mathcal{X}_{e,t}
the same dimension as
\mathcal{F}_{e,t}
, and
\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon,t}
the same dimension as
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
, such that
\mathcal{F}_{e,t}=\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{e,q}\mathcal{X}_{e,t-q}
and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}=\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{\epsilon,q}\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon,t-q}
, with
\{\mathcal{X}_{e,t}\}
and
\{\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon,t}\}
independent of each other.
\{\mathcal{X}_{e,t}\}
has independent elements while
\{\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon,t}\}
has
i.i.d.
elements, and all elements have mean zero with unit variance and uniformly bounded fourth order moments. Both
\{\mathcal{X}_{e,t}\}
and
\{\mathcal{X}_{\epsilon,t}\}
are independent of
\{\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{reshape},f,t}\}
from (F1).

The coefficients
a_{e,q}
and
a_{\epsilon,t}
are such that
\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{e,q}^{2}=\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{\epsilon,q}^{2}=1
and
\sum_{q\geq 0}|a_{e,q}|,\sum_{q\geq 0}|a_{\epsilon,q}|\leq c
for some constant
c
.

(R1)

(Rate assumptions)

With
g_{s}:=\prod_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{\delta_{k,1}}
and
\gamma_{s}:=d_{V}^{\delta_{V,1}}\prod_{j=1}^{v-1}d_{j}^{\delta_{j,1}}
, we assume that

\displaystyle dg_{s}^{-2}T^{-1}d_{k}^{2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}})+1},\;\;\;dg_{s}^{-1}d_{k}^{\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}}-1/2}

,

\displaystyle d\gamma_{s}^{-2}T^{-1}d_{V}^{2(\delta_{V,1}-\delta_{V,r_{V}})+1},\;\;\;d\gamma_{s}^{-1}d_{V}^{\delta_{V,1}-\delta_{V,r_{V}}-1/2}

\displaystyle=o(1).

(R2)

(Further rate assumptions)

With
g_{w}:=\prod_{k=1}^{K}d_{k}^{\delta_{k,r_{k}}}
and
\gamma_{w}:=d_{V}^{\delta_{V,r_{V}}}\prod_{j=1}^{v-1}d_{j}^{\delta_{j,r_{j}}}
, we assume that

\displaystyle\max_{k\in[K]}\Bigg\{d_{k}^{2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Td_{\text{-}k}d_{k}^{1-\delta_{k,1}}}+\frac{1}{d_{k}^{1+\delta_{k,r_{k}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{g_{s}g_{w}}\Bigg\}

,

\displaystyle\max_{j\in[v-1]}\Bigg\{d_{j}^{2(\delta_{j,1}-\delta_{j,r_{j}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Td_{\text{-}k}d_{j}^{1-\delta_{j,1}}}+\frac{1}{d_{j}^{1+\delta_{j,r_{j}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{\gamma_{s}\gamma_{w}}\Bigg\}

,

\displaystyle d_{V}^{2(\delta_{V,1}-\delta_{V,r_{V}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Tdd_{V}^{-\delta_{V,1}}},\;\;\;\frac{1}{d_{V}^{1+\delta_{V,r_{V}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{\gamma_{s}\gamma_{w}},\;\;\;\frac{d}{\gamma_{w}^{2}},\;\;\;\frac{d}{g_{w}^{2}}

\displaystyle=o\Big(\max_{k\in[K]}\{d_{k}^{-1}\}\Big).

With Assumption (L1), the standardised loading matrix
\mathbf{Q}_{j}:=\mathbf{A}_{j}\mathbf{Z}_{j}^{-1/2}
satisfies
\mathbf{Q}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{j}\to\bm{\Sigma}_{A,j}
for
j\in[v-1]
, and
\mathbf{Q}_{V}:=\mathbf{A}_{V}\mathbf{Z}_{V}^{-1/2}
satisfies
\mathbf{Q}_{V}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{V}\to\bm{\Sigma}_{A,V}
. Similar implication holds for (L2), except that (L2) is only valid under the null. Hence with (L2),
\mathbf{Z}_{V}
and
\bm{\Sigma}_{A,V}
in (L1) satisfy

\mathbf{Z}_{V}=\mathbf{Z}_{K}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{Z}_{v},\;\;\;\bm{\Sigma}_{A,V}=\bm{\Sigma}_{A,K}\otimes\dots\otimes\bm{\Sigma}_{A,v}.

(4.4)

Note that the factor strength requirement for
\mathbf{A}_{V}
in (L1) is satisfied by (L2), since from (
4.4
)
(\mathbf{Z}_{V})_{r_{V}r_{V}}\asymp\prod_{i=v}^{K}d_{i}^{\delta_{i,r_{i}}}\geq d_{V}^{\min_{i=v}^{K}\delta_{i,r_{i}}}>d_{V}^{1/2}
. Assumption (L1) characterises the loading matrix behaviour generally for (
3.4
), and the additional (L2) is specific for the null. Both assumptions allow for weak factors which are common feature in the literature
(
Lam and Yao,, 2012
;
Onatski,, 2012
;
Cen and Lam,, 2024
)
. When all factors are pervasive, for instance, (
4.1
) can be interpreted as
d_{j}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{j}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{j}\to\bm{\Sigma}_{A,j}
if all factors are pervasive, which coincides with Assumption 3 of
Chen and Fan, (2023)
for matrix time series.

Assumption (F1) assumes that
\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
is a general linear process with weakly serial dependence. Theorem
1
ensures that the core factor in (
3.5
) (under
H_{0}
) reserves its structure of (F1) such that

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}\mathcal{X}_{f,t-w},\;\;\;\text{with }\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{X}_{f,t},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{X}_{\textnormal{reshape},f,t}.

(4.5)

Note that it holds for each
k\in[K]
, as
T\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\text{mat}_{k}({\mathcal{F}_{t}})\,\text{mat}_{k}({\mathcal{F}_{t}})^{\prime}\xrightarrow{p}r_{\text{-}k}\,\mathbf{I}_{r_{k}},

(4.6)

which is direct from Proposition 1.3 in the supplement of
Cen and Lam, (2024)
. In comparison,
He et al., 2022a ()
and
Barigozzi et al., (2023)
assume the form of (
4.6
) with
r_{\text{-}k}\,\mathbf{I}_{r_{k}}
replaced by a positive definite matrix. This does not imply (F1) is particularly stronger as our factor loading matrices already incorporate some positive definite matrices by (L1) and (L2).

Assumptions (E1) and (E2) depict a general noise time series on the factor models (
3.3
) and (
3.5
). It is worth noting that the noise tensor
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is allowed to be (weakly) dependent across modes and time, regardless of the existence of Kronecker product structure. From (
4.3
), we have

\begin{split}\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{A})&=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{F}_{e,t},\mathcal{A})\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{e,1}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{v-1}\mathbf{A}_{e,v-1}\times_{v}(\mathbf{A}_{K}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{v})\\
&\hskip 12.0pt+\textnormal{Reshape}(\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon},\mathcal{A})\circ\textnormal{Reshape}(\bm{\epsilon}_{t},\mathcal{A}),\end{split}

(4.7)

so that the structure of (E1) and (E2) are preserved by
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{A})
. Assumption (R1) details the rate assumptions on factor strengths and is hence satisfied automatically when all factors are pervasive. (R2) also concerns factor strength and would hold for all strong factors if
v>1
; for
v=1
, (R2) holds when
\min_{k\in[K]}d_{k}=o(T)
in addition to strong factors.

Remark 4

When
v=1
and all factors are strong, Assumption (R2) requires
d_{k*}=\min_{k\in[K]}d_{k}=o(T)
which seems restricted. This is to ensure the asymptotic normality when we aggregate
d_{k*}
number of estimated residuals in
x_{j,t}
and
y_{m,j,t}
in Section
3.3
. However, from the proof of Theorem
2
in the supplement, it is feasible to aggregate
d_{k^{*}}^{\beta}
for any
0<\beta<2
such that
d_{k*}^{\beta}=o(T)
. Therefore, (R2) is in fact arguably as mild as Assumption B5 in
He et al., (2023)
. We do not pursue such an aggregation scheme to keep the practical procedure as simple as possible.

4.2
Main results and practical test design

We first present below the results for our residual estimators in (
3.7
) and (
3.10
), which inspire the testing procedure in Section
3.3
. Following Theorem
2
, the theoretical guarantee of the test is also provided.

Theorem 2

Let Assumptions (F1), (L1), (L2), (E1), (E2), (R1) and (R2) hold. With the notations in Section
3.3
, under
H_{0}
, there exists
m\in[|\mathcal{R}|]
such that for each
t\in[T]
,
j\in[d/d_{k}^{\ast}]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big(\widehat{E}_{m,t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big)}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\textnormal{Var}(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2})\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{4}}},\;\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big(\widetilde{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big)}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\textnormal{Var}(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2})\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{4}}}\xrightarrow{p}Z_{j},

where
Z_{j}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and
Z_{h}
is independent of
Z_{\ell}
for
h\neq\ell
. Under
H_{1}
, the asymptotic result for
\widetilde{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}
above still holds true.

Theorem 3

Let all the assumptions in Theorem
2
hold. With the notations in Section
3.3
, we have the following for any
j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]
under
H_{0}
. There exists
m\in[|\mathcal{R}|]
such that, as
T,d_{1},\dots,d_{K}\to\infty
,

\mathbbm{P}_{y,m,j}\big(y_{m,j,t}\geq\widehat{q}_{x,j}(\alpha)\big)\leq\alpha.

In other words, as
T,d_{1},\dots,d_{K}\to\infty
, it holds with probability going to 1 that

\min_{m\in[|\mathcal{R}|]}\Big\{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}\{y_{m,j,t}\geq\widehat{q}_{x,j}(\alpha)\}\Big\}\leq\alpha.

(4.8)

Theorem
3
presents the grounds for our construction of the test statistic in (
3.12
).
For related explanations, see the discussions immediately after (
3.11
), and before Remark
3
.

The setup of the problem (
3.4
) specifies the set
\mathcal{A}
which is only needed in
H_{1}
due to (
3.5
) under
H_{0}
. It is direct to specify
\mathcal{A}
for a series of matrix-valued observations (i.e., order-2 tensor), see Example
1
. However, for a general order-
K
tensor with
K\geq 3
,
\mathcal{A}
might be misspecified without any prior knowledge. To resolve this, we present the second theorem on tensor reshape as follows.

Theorem 4

(Tensor Reshape Theorem II)

Consider a tensor time series
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
and a set of mode indices
\mathcal{A}
. With Definition
2
, the time series
\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\big\}
has a Kronecker product structure if and only if
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
either has a Kronecker product structure or has no Kronecker product structure along a subset of
\mathcal{A}
.

Suppose now
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has no Kronecker product structure along some
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
. Theorem
4
tells us that testing the Kronecker product structure of the reshaped series
\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\big\}
effectively tests if
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
. In light of this, a testing design is feasible when
\mathcal{A}
is unspecified, with a minimal assumption that
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},[K])=\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big)
has a factor structure, i.e., the vectorised
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
follows a standard vector factor model. For illustration, consider
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},[K]\setminus\{1\})=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{2,\dots,K\})
which is an order-2 tensor. Using the last property of Reshape
(\cdot,\cdot)
in Section
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{Reshape}\Big(\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{2,\dots,K\}),\{1,2\}\Big)=\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{1,2,\dots,K\})=\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big).

According to Definition
2
,
\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{2,\dots,K\})\big\}
follows a factor model along
\{1,2\}
. This is always correctly specified since
\big\{\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big)\big\}
follows a factor model (which also implies
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}\subseteq[K]
). By Theorem
4
,
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{2,\dots,K\})
has no Kronecker product structure if and only if
1\in\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
. Hence on testing (
3.4
) with
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
replaced by
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\{2,\dots,K\})\}
and
\mathcal{A}=\{1,2\}
, rejection of the null implies
1\in\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
.

By the fact that
\big\{\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big)\big\}
with any permutation on
\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big)
also follows a factor model, the above scheme is in fact valid on
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},[K]\setminus\{k\})
for any
k\in[K]
. Eventually,
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
can be identified, and the above procedure is summarised into the following algorithm.

Practical testing algorithm

1.

Given an order-
K
tensor time series
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
with
K\geq 2
and
\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big)
following a factor model with
r_{\text{vec}}
number of factors, initialise
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}=\phi
, the empty set.

2.

Initialise
k=1
. Define a test as (
3.4
) with
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
replaced by
\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},[K]\setminus\{k\})\big\}
and
\mathcal{A}
by
\{1,2\}
.

3.

Follow the steps in Section
3.3
to test the problem in step 2, with
r_{V}
replaced by
r_{\text{vec}}
. If the null is rejected, include
k
in the set
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}
.

4.

Repeat from step 2 to step 3 with
k=2,3,\dots,K
.

With the resulted
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}
from the algorithm, we interpret that
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has no Kronecker product structure along
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}
. In practice,
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}
being an empty set implies
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has a Kronecker product structure.

Remark 5

Definition
2
considers the absence of Kronecker product structure over a single set
\mathcal{A}
only, which does not fully characterise all scenarios for
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
with order at least 4. However, we do not pursue this complication here, albeit our practical design can be readily adapted.

Numerical Studies

5.1
Simulations

In this section, we demonstrate the empirical performance of our test by Monte Carlo simulations. As discussed in Section
3.1
, the test is only non-trivial when the data order
K
is at least 2. We hence consider from
K=2
to
K=4
.

The data generating processes adapt Assumptions (F1), (E1) and (E2). Specifically, we set the number of factors as
r_{k}=2
for any
k\in[K]
, and first generate
\mathcal{F}_{t}
in (
3.5
) with each element being independent standardised AR(2) with AR coefficient 0.7 and -0.3. The elements in
\mathcal{F}_{e,t}
and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
are generated similarly, but their AR coefficients are (-0.5, 0.5) and (0.4, 0.4) respectively. The standard deviation of each element in
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
is generated by
i.i.d.

|\mathcal{N}(0,1)|
. Unless otherwise specified, all innovation processes in constructing
\mathcal{F}_{t}
,
\mathcal{F}_{e,t}
and
\bm{\epsilon}_{t}
are
i.i.d.
standard normal. For each
j\in[v-1]
, each factor loading matrix
\mathbf{A}_{j}
is generated independently with
\mathbf{A}_{j}=\mathbf{U}_{j}\mathbf{B}_{j}
, where each entry of
\mathbf{U}_{j}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{j}\times r_{j}}
is
i.i.d.

\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, and
\mathbf{B}_{j}\in\mathbbm{R}^{r_{j}\times r_{j}}

d_{j}^{-\zeta_{j,h}}

. Pervasive factors have
\zeta_{j,h}=0
, while weak factors have
0<\zeta_{j,h}\leq 0.5
. Each entry of
\mathbf{A}_{e,j}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{j}\times r_{e,j}}
is
i.i.d.

\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, but has independent probability of 0.95 being set exactly to 0. We set
r_{e,k}=2
for all
j\in[v-1]
throughout all experiments. For any
\mathcal{A}
(specified later), we obtain

\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},\;\;\;\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{E}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}.

Lastly, similar to
\{\mathbf{A}_{j}\}_{j\in[v-1]}
, we generate
\{\mathbf{A}_{v},\dots,\mathbf{A}_{K}\}
and let
\mathbf{A}_{V}=\mathbf{A}_{K}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{K-1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{v}
under
H_{0}
, or
\mathbf{A}_{V}
directly under
H_{1}
. Whenever

is required, it is computed as

, which has

combinations since we set two factors for each mode. According to (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we then respectively construct
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})
(and hence the corresponding
\mathcal{Y}_{t}

\mathcal{Y}_{t}
directly.

3.3
, we calculate the following with

5.2
Real data analysis

We apply our test on two real data examples described as follows.

1.

New York city taxi traffic. The data considered includes all individual taxi rides operated by Yellow Taxi within Manhattan Island of New York City, published at

https://www1.nyc.gov/site/tlc/about/tlc-trip-record-data.page
.

The dataset contains trip records within the period of January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2022. We focus on the pick-up and drop-off dates/times, and pick-up and drop-off locations which are coded according to 69 predefined zones in the dataset. Moreover, each day is divided into 24 hourly periods to represent the pick-up and drop-off times, with the first hourly period from 0
a.m.
to 1 a.m. Hence each day we have
\mathcal{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{69\times 69\times 24}
, where
y_{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},t}
is the number of trips from zone
i_{1}
to zone
i_{2}
and the pick-up time is within the
i_{3}
-th hourly period on day
t
. We consider business days and non-business days separately, so that we will analyse two tensor time series. The business-day series and the non-business-day series are 1,260 and 566 days long, respectively.

2.

Fama-French portfolio returns. This is a set of portfolio returns data, where stocks are respectively categorised into ten levels of market equity and book-to-equity ratio which is the book equity for the last fiscal year divided by the end-of-year market equity; both criteria use NYSE deciles as breakpoints at the end of June each year. See details in

https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/Data_Library/det_100_port_sz.html
.

The stocks in each of the
10\times 10
categories form exactly two portfolios, one being value-weighted, and the other of equal-weight. That is, we will study two sets of 10 by 10 portfolios with their time series. We use monthly data from January 2010 to June 2021, and hence for both value-weighted and equal-weighted portfolios we have each of our data set as an order-2 tensor
\mathcal{X}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{10\times 10}
for
t\in[138]
.

The two taxi series are order-3 tensor time series, and we only test their Kronecker product structure along
\mathcal{A}=\{1,2\}
, i.e., we speculate that there is a merged “location” factor instead of “pick-up” and “drop-off” factors along mode-1 and -2 respectively. On the other hand, the two portfolio series are order-2 tensor time series, hence naturally we test along
\mathcal{A}=\{1,2\}
. Furthermore, we remove the market effect via the capital asset pricing model (CAPM) as

\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{X}_{t}\big)=\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\big)+(r_{t}-\bar{r})\bm{\beta}+\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big),

where
\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{X}_{t}\big)\in\mathbbm{R}^{100}
is the vectorised returns at time
t
,
\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\big)
is the sample mean of
\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{X}_{t}\big)
,
\bm{\beta}
is the coefficient vector,
r_{t}
is the return of the NYSE composite index at time
t
,
\bar{r}
is the sample mean of
r_{t}
, and
\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{Y}_{t}\big)
is the CAPM residual. The least squares solution is

\widehat{\bm{\beta}}=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{138}(r_{t}-\bar{r})\{\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{X}_{t}\big)-\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\big)\}}{\sum_{t=1}^{138}(r_{t}-\bar{r})^{2}},

so that the estimated residual series
\{\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}\}_{t\in[138]}
with
\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{10\times 10}
is constructed as
\{\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{X}_{t}\big)-\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\big)-(r_{t}-\bar{r})\widehat{\bm{\beta}}\}_{t\in[138]}
.

Hence, we study six time series in total: business-day taxi series, non-business-day taxi series, value-weighted portfolio series, equal-weighted portfolio series, value-weighted residual series and equal-weighted residual series. For each series, we perform the test described in Section
3.3

directly on each time series due to their large dimensions. Each mode of the six series has one or two estimated number of factors. Since the test results are similar for those rank settings, we present the results with two factors each mode and hence
\widehat{r}_{V}=4
.

&\leq\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{T}\sum_{w\geq 0}|a_{f,w}||a_{f,w-(t-s)}|\Big)\Big(\sum_{q\geq 0}|a_{f,q}|\Big)\cdot\max_{q}|a_{f,q}|=O(1)\cdot\Big(\sum_{w\geq 0}|a_{f,w}|\Big)^{2}=O(1),\end{split}

(B.4)

where the last two equality used Assumption (F1). Similarly, it holds that

\sum_{s=1}^{T}\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}^{2}a_{f,w-(t-s)}^{2}\leq\Big(\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}^{2}\Big)^{2}=O(1).

(B.5)

Now for
i\neq j\in[r_{k}]
, by Assumption (F1) we have

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\mathbbm{E}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),j\cdot}\Big)=\mathbbm{E}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),i\cdot}^{\prime}\Big)\mathbbm{E}\Big(\mathbf{M}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),j\cdot}\Big)=0.

For
i=j\in[r_{k}]
, with
\mathcal{X}_{f,t}
from (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\mathbbm{E}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),i\cdot}\Big)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{w\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{f,w}a_{f,q}\mathbbm{E}\Big(\mathbf{X}_{f,t-w,(k),i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{X}_{f,t-q,(k),i\cdot}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{w\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{f,w}a_{f,q}\textnormal{tr}(\mathbf{M})\mathbbm{1}\{w=q\}=\textnormal{tr}(\mathbf{M})\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}^{2}=\textnormal{tr}(\mathbf{M}),

so that
\mathbbm{E}\Big(T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big)=\textnormal{tr}(\mathbf{M})\cdot\mathbf{I}_{r_{k}}
. Finally, consider any
i,j\in[r_{k}]
,

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\text{Var}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k),j\cdot}\Big)=\text{Var}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{l=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{v=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}F_{t,(k),il}M_{lv}F_{t,(k),jv}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\text{Cov}\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{l=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{v=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{w\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{f,w}a_{f,q}X_{f,t-w,(k),il}M_{lv}X_{f,t-q,(k),jv},

\displaystyle\hskip 48.0pt\sum_{s=1}^{T}\sum_{g=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{u=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{h\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{f,h}a_{f,m}X_{f,s-h,(k),ig}M_{gu}X_{f,s-m,(k),ju}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\Big(\sum_{w\geq 0}a_{f,w}a_{f,w-(t-s)}\Big)\Big(\sum_{q\geq 0}a_{f,q}a_{f,q-(t-s)}\Big)\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{v=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}M_{lv}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt+\frac{1}{T^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{w\geq 0}\sum_{q\geq 0}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\sum_{h\geq 0}\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{f,w}a_{f,q}a_{f,h}a_{f,m}\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}M_{ll}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.0pt\cdot\text{Cov}\Big(X_{f,t-w,(k),il}X_{f,t-q,(k),il},X_{f,s-h,(k),il}X_{f,s-m,(k),il}\Big)

\displaystyle=O\Big(\frac{1}{T}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{v=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}M_{lv}^{2}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{1}{T}\Big)\cdot\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{r_{\text{-}k}}M_{ll}^{2}\Big)=O\Big(\frac{1}{T}\Big)\cdot\|\mathbf{M}\|_{F}^{2}=o(1),

where the third equality considered
i=j
and
i\neq j
separately, and the fourth used (
B.4
) and (
B.5
). This completes the proof of (
B.1
) and hence Lemma
2
.
\square

Lemma 3

(Bounding
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}
) Under Assumptions (F1), (L1), (L2), (E1) and (E2), it holds that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big\|_{F}^{2}=O_{P}\Big(Td_{k}^{1+\delta_{k,1}}d_{\text{-}k}\Big),

(B.6)

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big\|_{F}^{2}=O_{P}\Big(Td_{k}^{2}d_{\text{-}k}+T^{2}d_{k}d_{\text{-}k}^{2}\Big).

(B.7)

Hence, with
\mathbf{R}_{k,t}
defined in (
B.11
), we have

\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\Big\|_{F}^{2}=O_{P}\Big(Td_{k}^{2}d_{\text{-}k}+T^{2}d_{k}d_{\text{-}k}^{2}\Big).

Proof of Lemma
3
.

It is not hard to see (
B.6
) holds as follows,

\begin{split}&\hskip 12.0pt\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\Bigg(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{k,i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k),l\cdot}\Bigg)^{2}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\|\mathbf{A}_{k,i\cdot}\|^{2}\cdot\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\Bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{d_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}E_{t,(k),lh}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{A}_{k,i\cdot}\|}\mathbf{A}_{k,i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k,h\cdot}\Bigg)^{2}=O_{P}\Big(Td_{k}^{1+\delta_{k,1}}d_{\text{-}k}\Big),\end{split}

where the last equality is from Assumptions (L1), (L2) and Lemma
1
.

Consider now (
B.7
). First note from Assumption (E1) that, for any
k\in[K]
,
i\in[d_{k}]
,
j\in[d_{\text{-}k}]
,

E_{t,(k),ij}=\mathbf{A}_{e,k,i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{F}_{e,t,(k)}\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k,j\cdot}+\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k),ij}\epsilon_{t,(k),ij},

where
\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k}:=\mathbf{A}_{e,K}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{e,k+1}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{e,k-1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{e,1}
. Then with Assumption (E2), we have

\text{Cov}(E_{t,(k),ij},E_{t,(k),lj})=\mathbf{A}_{e,k,i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{e,k,l\cdot}\|\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k,j\cdot}\|^{2}+\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k),ij}^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{i=l\}},

and together with Lemma
1
,

\begin{split}&\hskip 12.0pt\mathbbm{E}\Big\{\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big\|_{F}^{2}\Big\}=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbbm{E}\Big\{\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{-}k}}E_{t,(k),ij}E_{t,(k),lj}\Big)^{2}\Big\}\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}\Big\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{-}k}}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\sum_{h=1}^{d_{\text{-}k}}\text{Cov}(E_{t,(k),ij}E_{t,(k),lj},E_{s,(k),ih}E_{s,(k),lh})+\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{-}k}}\mathbbm{E}[E_{t,(k),ij}E_{t,(k),lj}]\Big)^{2}\Big\}\\
&=O\big(Td_{k}^{2}d_{\text{-}k}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\sum_{l=1}^{d_{k}}O\Big(T\cdot\mathbf{A}_{e,k,i\cdot}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{e,k,l\cdot}\|\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k}\|_{F}^{2}+Td_{\text{-}k}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\{i=l\}}\Big)^{2}=O\big(Td_{k}^{2}d_{\text{-}k}+T^{2}d_{k}d_{\text{-}k}^{2}\big).\end{split}

Recall from (
B.11
) it is defined that

\mathbf{R}_{k,t}=\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime},

the rate on
\big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\big\|_{F}^{2}
is direct from (
B.6
) and (
B.7
). Hence the proof of Lemma
3
is complete.
\square

Lemma 4

Let Assumptions (F1), (L1), (L2), (E1), (E2) and (R1) hold. For
k\in[K]
, define
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}
as the
r_{k}\times r_{k}
diagonal matrix with the first largest
r_{k}
eigenvalues of
T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}
on the main diagonal, such that
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}=\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\big(T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\big)\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}
. Define
\omega_{k}:=d_{k}^{\delta_{k,r_{k}}-\delta_{k,1}}g_{s}
. It holds that

\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\big\|_{F}=O_{P}\big(\omega_{k}^{-1}\big).

Proof of Lemma
4
.

Observe that
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}
has size
r_{k}\times r_{k}
, it suffices to find the lower bound of
\lambda_{r_{k}}(\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k})
. To do this, consider the decomposition

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t},

(B.8)

which is direct from (
B.11
). Then for a unit vector
\bm{\gamma}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{k}}
, we can define

\displaystyle S_{k}(\bm{\gamma})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big)\bm{\gamma}=:S_{k}^{*}(\bm{\gamma})+\widetilde{S}_{k}(\bm{\gamma}),\;\text{ with}

\displaystyle S_{k}^{*}(\bm{\gamma})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}\Big)\bm{\gamma},\;\;\;\widetilde{S}_{k}(\bm{\gamma}):=\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\Big)\bm{\gamma}.

Since
\|\bm{\gamma}\|=1
, we have by Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt|\widetilde{S}_{k}(\bm{\gamma})|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{k}^{2}T^{2}}\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\Big\|_{F}^{2}=O_{P}\Big(T^{-1}d_{k}^{1+2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}})}dg_{s}^{-2}+d_{k}^{2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}}-1/2)}d^{2}g_{s}^{-2}\Big)=o_{P}(1),

where the last equality used Assumption (R1). Next, with Assumption (F1) and Lemma
2
, consider

\begin{split}&\hskip 12.0pt\lambda_{r_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}\Big)=\lambda_{r_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{\prime}\Big)\\
&\geq\lambda_{r_{k}}(\mathbf{A}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{k})\cdot\lambda_{r_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big)\asymp_{P}d_{k}^{\delta_{k,r_{k}}}\cdot\lambda_{r_{k}}(\textnormal{tr}(\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{\text{-}k})\mathbf{I}_{r_{k}})\asymp_{P}\omega_{k}.\end{split}

(B.9)

With this, going back to the decomposition (
B.8
),

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\omega_{k}^{-1}\lambda_{r_{k}}(\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k})=\omega_{k}^{-1}\lambda_{r_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{k}^{-1}\lambda_{r_{k}}\Big(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}\Big)-\sup_{\|\bm{\gamma}\|=1}|\widetilde{S}_{k}(\bm{\gamma})|\asymp_{P}1,

which implies
\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\big\|_{F}=O_{P}\big(\lambda_{r_{k}}^{-1}(\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k})\big)=O_{P}\big(\omega_{k}^{-1}\big)
, which completes the proof of Lemma
4
.
\square

Lemma 5

(Consistency of
\{\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}\}_{k\in[K]}
)
Let Assumptions (F1), (L1), (L2), (E1), (E2) and (R1) hold. For any
k\in[K]
, define an
r_{k}\times r_{k}
matrix

\mathbf{H}_{k}:=T^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big(\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\big).

As
T,d_{1},\dots,d_{K}\to\infty
we have
\mathbf{H}_{k}
invertible with
\|\mathbf{H}_{k}\|_{F}=O_{P}(1)
and

\displaystyle\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}-\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}\big\|_{F}^{2}=O_{P}\Bigg\{d_{k}^{2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Td_{\text{-}k}d_{k}^{1-\delta_{k,1}}}+\frac{1}{d_{k}^{1+\delta_{k,r_{k}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{g_{s}^{2}}\Bigg\}.

Proof of Lemma
5
.

First, we may write (
3.5
) as

\mathcal{Y}_{t}=\mathcal{F}_{Z,t}\times_{1}\mathbf{Q}_{1}\times_{2}\dots\times_{K}\mathbf{Q}_{K}+\mathcal{E}_{t}.

(B.10)

For any
k\in[K]
, taking the mode-
k
unfolding on (
B.10
), we have

\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}=\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)},

where
\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}
denotes the mode-
k
unfolding of
\mathcal{F}_{Z,t}
. Hence,

\begin{split}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}&=\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{E}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\\
&=:\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{R}_{k,t}.\end{split}

(B.11)

Recall from Lemma
4
that
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}
is the
r_{k}\times r_{k}
diagonal matrix with the first largest
r_{k}
eigenvalues of
T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}
on the main diagonal, and since
\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}
consists of the corresponding eigenvectors, we have

\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}.

(B.12)

With (
B.11
), we can write the
j
-th row of estimated mode-
k
factor loading as

\begin{split}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,j\cdot}&=\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big(\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}\mathbf{Y}_{t,(k)}^{\prime}\big)_{ij}\\
&=\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}\mathbf{Q}_{k,i\cdot}^{\prime}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big(\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\big)\mathbf{Q}_{k,j\cdot}+\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}.\end{split}

Hence with the definition
\mathbf{H}_{k}=T^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big(\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{-}k}\mathbf{F}_{Z,t,(k)}^{\prime}\big)
, we may decompose

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,j\cdot}-\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,j\cdot}=\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}

(B.13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}-\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,i\cdot}\big)\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}+\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}.

(B.14)

With the decomposition (
B.13
), it holds that

\begin{split}&\hskip 12.0pt\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}-\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}\big\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,j\cdot}-\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,j\cdot}\big\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}\Big\|^{2}\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\Big)_{j\cdot}\Big\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T^{2}}\cdot\|\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\Big\|_{F}^{2}\\
&=O_{P}\Bigg(d_{k}^{2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}})}\bigg(\frac{1}{Td_{\text{-}k}}+\frac{1}{d_{k}}\bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{g_{s}^{2}}\Bigg)=o_{P}(1),\end{split}

(B.15)

where the second last equality used Lemmas
3
and
4
, and the last used Assumption (R1).

Before improving the rate of (
B.15
), we now use it to show
\mathbf{H}_{k}
has full rank and
\|\mathbf{H}_{k}\|_{F}=O_{P}(1)
asymptotically. To this end, it is sufficient to observe

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{r_{k}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}=\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}-\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}\big)+\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}+o_{P}(1)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}+o_{P}(1)=\mathbf{H}_{k}\bm{\Sigma}_{A,k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}+o_{P}(1),

where the last equality used Assumption (L1) or (L2) and it is immediate that
\mathbf{H}_{k}
has full rank asymptotically. Let
\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{X})
denote the
i
-th largest singular value for any give matrix
\mathbf{X}
, we have

\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{H}_{k})\cdot\sigma_{r_{k}}(\bm{\Sigma}_{A,k})\cdot\sigma_{r_{k}}(\mathbf{H}_{k}^{T})\leq\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{H}_{k})\cdot\sigma_{r_{k}}(\bm{\Sigma}_{A,k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{T})\leq\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{H}_{k}\bm{\Sigma}_{A,k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime})=O_{P}(\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{I}_{r_{k}}))=O_{P}(1),

which implies
\|\mathbf{H}_{k}\|_{F}=O_{P}(1)
by Assumption (L1) or (L2).

Consider the decomposition (
B.14
), we have

\begin{split}&\hskip 12.0pt\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}-\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}\big\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,j\cdot}-\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,j\cdot}\big\|^{2}\\
&=O_{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}\Big\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k,i\cdot}-\mathbf{H}_{k}\mathbf{Q}_{k,i\cdot}\big)\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}\Big\|^{2}\Bigg)\\
&=O_{P}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{k}}\Big\|\frac{1}{T}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\mathbf{Q}_{k,i\cdot}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\mathbf{R}_{k,t})_{ij}\Big\|^{2}\Bigg)+O_{P}\Bigg(\frac{1}{T^{2}}\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{D}}_{k}^{-1}\big\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Q}}_{k}-\mathbf{Q}_{k}\mathbf{H}_{k}^{\prime}\big\|_{F}^{2}\cdot\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{R}_{k,t}\Big\|_{F}^{2}\Bigg)\\

(\pi_{m,1},\dots,\pi_{m,K-v+1})=(r_{v},\dots,r_{K}),

we only consider such
m
and simplify
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{m,t}
as
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{m,t}
as
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
(see the explanation at the beginning of Section
B
). By (
3.10
) and Assumption (E1),

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{E}}_{t,(k^{\ast})}

\displaystyle=\text{mat}_{k^{\ast}}({\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}})=\text{mat}_{k^{\ast}}({(\mathcal{C}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t})+\mathcal{F}_{e,t}\times_{1}\mathbf{A}_{e,1}\times_{2}\cdots\times_{K}\mathbf{A}_{e,K}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t}})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{C}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t})_{(k^{\ast})}+\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}}\mathbf{F}_{e,t,(k^{\ast})}\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}^{\prime}+\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast})}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t,(k^{\ast})},

where
\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}:=\mathbf{A}_{e,K}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}+1}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}-1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{A}_{e,1}
. Hence, for any
t\in[T]
and
j\in[d/d_{k}^{\ast}]
, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big(\widehat{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big)&=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{E}}_{t,(k^{\ast})}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbf{E}}_{t,(k^{\ast})}\big)_{jj}-\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}=\sum_{h=1}^{6}\mathcal{I}_{e,h},\\
\text{where}\;\;\;\mathcal{I}_{e,1}&:=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big\{\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast})}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t,(k^{\ast})}\big)^{\prime}\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast})}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t,(k^{\ast})}\big)\big\}_{jj}-\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2},\\
\mathcal{I}_{e,2}&:=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big\{(\mathcal{C}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t})_{(k^{\ast})}^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t})_{(k^{\ast})}\big\}_{jj},\\
\mathcal{I}_{e,3}&:=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big\{\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}\mathbf{F}_{e,t,(k^{\ast})}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}}\mathbf{F}_{e,t,(k^{\ast})}\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}^{\prime}\big\}_{jj},\\
\mathcal{I}_{e,4}&:=O_{P}\Big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big\{(\mathcal{C}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t})_{(k^{\ast})}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}}\mathbf{F}_{e,t,(k^{\ast})}\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}^{\prime}\big\}_{jj}\Big),\\
\mathcal{I}_{e,5}&:=O_{P}\Big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big\{(\mathcal{C}_{t}-\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t})_{(k^{\ast})}^{\prime}\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast})}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t,(k^{\ast})}\big)\big\}_{jj}\Big),\\
\mathcal{I}_{e,6}&:=O_{P}\Big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big\{\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}\mathbf{F}_{e,t,(k^{\ast})}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}}^{\prime}\big(\bm{\Sigma}_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast})}\circ\bm{\epsilon}_{t,(k^{\ast})}\big)\big\}_{jj}\Big).\end{split}

(B.33)

Consider
\mathcal{I}_{e,2}
. From Lemma
6
, recall that

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\Big\{\big(\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t}\big)\big)_{i}-\big(\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{C}_{t}\big)\big)_{i}\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle=O_{P}\Bigg(\max_{k\in[K]}\Bigg\{d_{k}^{2(\delta_{k,1}-\delta_{k,r_{k}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Td_{\text{-}k}d_{k}^{1-\delta_{k,1}}}+\frac{1}{d_{k}^{1+\delta_{k,r_{k}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{g_{s}g_{w}}\Bigg\}+\frac{d}{g_{w}^{2}}\Bigg),

which is the squared error for each entry of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{t}
. With Assumption (R2), the above squared error rate is
o\big(1/\min_{k\in[K]}\{d_{k}\}\big)=o\big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big)
. Hence
\mathcal{I}_{e,2}=o_{P}\big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big)
.

With Assumption (E1),
\|\mathbf{A}_{e,k^{\ast}}\|_{F}=O(1)
,
\|\mathbf{A}_{e,\text{-}k^{\ast}}\|_{F}=O(1)
and
r_{e,k}
for
k\in[K]
are finite, so that
\mathcal{I}_{e,3},\,\mathcal{I}_{e,6}=O_{P}\big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big)
. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, immediately
\mathcal{I}_{e,4}=O_{P}\big(\mathcal{I}_{e,2}^{1/2}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{e,3}^{1/2}\big)=o_{P}\big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\big)
.

Consider
\mathcal{I}_{e,1}
, noting that

\mathcal{I}_{e,1}=\frac{1}{d_{k^{\ast}}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-1\big),

so that with Theorem 1 in
Ayvazyan and Ulyanov, (2023)
, we have

Z_{j}:=\frac{d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-1\big)}{\sqrt{d_{k^{\ast}}^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\text{Var}\big(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big)\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{4}}}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{N}(0,1),

implying
\mathcal{I}_{e,1}
is of the rate
d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1/2}
exactly. Note also that
Z_{j}
’s are independent of each other by Assumption (E1). It also follows that
\mathcal{I}_{e,5}=O_{P}\big(\mathcal{I}_{e,2}^{1/2}\cdot\mathcal{I}_{e,1}^{1/2}\big)=o_{P}\big(d_{k^{\ast}}^{-3/4}\big)
. Finally, with (
B.33
),

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big(\widehat{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big)}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\textnormal{Var}(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2})\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{4}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{k^{\ast}}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\big(\widehat{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}-\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}\big)}{\sqrt{d_{k^{\ast}}^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\textnormal{Var}(\epsilon_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2})\Sigma_{\epsilon,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{4}}}

\displaystyle=Z_{j}(1+o_{P}(1))\xrightarrow{p}Z_{j}\xrightarrow{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

This shows the asymptotic result for
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
in Theorem
2
(i.e., the first asymptotic result). The result for
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
can be shown in the same manner, except that for
\mathcal{I}_{e,2}
, we use (R2) on the squared error for each entry of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
which is the following from Lemma
7
,

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\Big\{\big(\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\big)\big)_{i}-\big(\textnormal{{vec}}\big(\mathcal{C}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\big)\big)_{i}\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle=O_{P}\Bigg(\max_{j\in[v-1]}\Bigg\{d_{j}^{2(\delta_{j,1}-\delta_{j,r_{j}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Td_{\text{-}k}d_{j}^{1-\delta_{j,1}}}+\frac{1}{d_{j}^{1+\delta_{j,r_{j}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{\gamma_{s}\gamma_{w}}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 57.0pt+d_{V}^{2(\delta_{V,1}-\delta_{V,r_{V}})}\Bigg(\frac{1}{Tdd_{V}^{-\delta_{V,1}}}+\frac{1}{d_{V}^{1+\delta_{V,r_{V}}}}\Bigg)\frac{d^{2}}{\gamma_{s}\gamma_{w}}+\frac{d}{\gamma_{w}^{2}}\Bigg).

Under
H_{0}
, we arrive at the same conclusion with the same
Z_{j}
’s. Moreover, the asymptotic result for
\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}
holds true under
H_{1}
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
\square

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Under
H_{0}
, Theorem
2
implies that for each
t\in[T]
and
j\in[d/d_{k^{\ast}}]
, there exists such
\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\widehat{E}_{m,t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}
distributed asymptotically the same as
\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k^{\ast}}}\widetilde{E}_{t,(k^{\ast}),ij}^{2}
. With the definition
\widehat{q}_{y,m,j}(\alpha):=\inf\big\{c\;|\;\mathbbm{F}_{y,m,j}(c)\geq 1-\alpha\big\}
, we arrive at the existence of such

asymptotically the same as

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
\mathcal{Y}_{t}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{1}\times\dots\times d_{K}}
be an order-
K
tensor and
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell}\}
. Then each
\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})
is an order-
(K-\ell+1)
tensor. If
\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\big\}
has a Kronecker product structure, then Theorem
1
allows us to write for
t\in[T]
,

\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\times_{j=1}^{K-\ell}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},j}\times_{K-\ell+1}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K-\ell+1}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t},

(B.34)

for some core factor
\{\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}\}
, loading matrices
\{\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},j}\}_{j\in[K-\ell+1]}
, and noise
\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}
. Immediately by Definition
2
, if
\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K-\ell+1}\in\mathcal{K}_{d_{a_{1}}\times\dots\times d_{a_{\ell}}}
, then
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
has a Kronecker product structure; otherwise,
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has no Kronecker product structure along
\mathcal{A}
.

It remains to show that if
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
either has a Kronecker product structure or has no Kronecker product structure along some set
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, then
\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\big\}
with
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
has a Kronecker product structure. The case where
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has a Kronecker product structure is trivial. We then only need to consider that
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has no Kronecker product structure along
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, which implicitly assume a factor model of
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
along
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
by Definition
2
. Without loss of generality, let
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}:=\{K-g+1,\dots,K\}
, otherwise redefine the mode indices of
\mathcal{Y}_{t}
. For the set
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, we now read
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell-g},K-g+1,\dots,K\}
. Using the last property of tensor reshape in Section
2.2
(which can be easily seen by induction), we have

\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})=\textnormal{Reshape}\big\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A}^{\ast}),\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell-g},K-g+1\}\big\}.

(B.35)

Now that
\{\mathcal{Y}_{t}\}
has no Kronecker product structure along
\mathcal{A}^{\ast}
, similar to the form (
B.34
), we have for
t\in[T]
,

\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A}^{\ast})=\mathcal{F}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}^{\ast}\times_{j=1}^{K-g}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},j}^{\ast}\times_{K-g+1}\mathbf{A}_{\textnormal{reshape},K-g+1}^{\ast}+\mathcal{E}_{\textnormal{reshape},t}^{\ast},

which implies the time series
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A}^{\ast})\}
follows a Tucker-decomposition TFM. According to Theorem
1
,
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A}^{\ast})\}
follows a factor model along any index set of
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A}^{\ast})\}
(with Kronecker product structure; but the factor model form is sufficient for our claim). In particular, with the index set
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{\ell-g},K-g+1\}
, we conclude from (
B.35
) that
\{\textnormal{Reshape}(\mathcal{Y}_{t},\mathcal{A})\}
has a Kronecker product structure. This also completes the proof of the theorem.
\square

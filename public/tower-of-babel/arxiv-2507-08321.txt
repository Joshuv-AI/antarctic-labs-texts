Introduction

The standard model (SM) of
SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
gauge theory has been
successful in describing phenomena at low energies, but has a gauge hierarchy problem
when embedded in a larger theory such as grand unification.
Gauge-Higgs unification (GHU) models have been proposed to solve the gauge hierarchy problem by
unifying the Higgs boson and gauge fields.
The SM gauge symmetry
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
is dynamically broken by an Aharonov-Bohm (AB) phase,

\theta_{H}
, in the fifth dimension.
The Higgs boson appears as a 4D fluctuation mode of
\theta_{H}

A finite mass of the Higgs boson is generated by quantum effects of the AB phase

In this paper we investigate the problem of flavor mixing in the lepton sector, namely the origin of

It is known that the mixing angles in the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata (PMNS) matrix
are much larger than those in the CKM matrix. Large mixing angles follow from Majorana mass terms.

5
Summary

In the GUT inspired gauge-Higgs unification the mixing of neutrino mass eigenstates in the
W
couplings arises
from the Majorana mass matrix
M
. Tiny neutrino masses are explained by the inverse seesaw mechanism.
Three neutrino masses
\{m_{\nu_{1}},m_{\nu_{2}},m_{\nu_{3}}\}
are determined by

\displaystyle F\,M\,\vec{w}_{j}=m_{\nu_{j}}\,\vec{w}_{j}~,

(5.1)

\displaystyle F_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\,F_{\alpha}~,~~~F_{\alpha}=\frac{m_{\ell_{\alpha}}^{2}}{(|c_{\ell_{\alpha}}|-\hbox{$\frac{1}{2}$})\,m_{B_{\alpha}}^{2}}~.

(5.2)

Neutrino masses
\{m_{\nu_{j}}\}
are eigenvalues of the matrix
FM
.
With the use of eigenvectors
\vec{w}_{j}
the Majorana mass matrix
M
can be expressed as (
4.4
),
or as

\displaystyle M=F^{-1}\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{m_{\nu_{j}}}{n_{j}}\,\vec{w}_{j}\otimes\vec{w}_{j}{}^{\dagger}\bigg\}F^{-1}~.

(5.3)

The relation (
5.2
) represents a gauge-Higgs seesaw mechanism for general
M
.

The PMNS matrix
U_{\rm PMNS}
is found to be

\displaystyle U_{\rm PMNS}=\begin{pmatrix}&&\cr\vec{p}_{1}&\vec{p}_{2}&\vec{p}_{3}\cr&&\end{pmatrix},

(5.4)

\displaystyle\vec{p}_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\,F^{-1/2}\,\vec{w}_{j}~,~~~\vec{p}_{j}{}^{\dagger}\vec{p}_{k}=\delta_{jk}~.

(5.5)

In terms of
\vec{p}_{j}
, (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) can be written as

\displaystyle F^{1/2}MF^{1/2}\,\vec{p}_{j}=m_{\nu_{j}}\,\vec{p}_{j}~,

(5.6)

\displaystyle M=F^{-1/2}\bigg\{\sum_{j=1}^{3}m_{\nu_{j}}\,\vec{p}_{j}\otimes\vec{p}_{j}{}^{\dagger}\bigg\}F^{-1/2}~.

(5.7)

The GHU scenario predicts no CP violation phase in
U_{\rm PMNS}
.
Neutrino oscillations with
U_{\rm PMNS}
in the normal ordering
with
\delta_{CP}=\pi
are reproduced in accordance with the NuFit-6.0 analysis.

To have a nontrivial CP violation phase in
U_{\rm PMNS}
, one needs to consider more general brane interactions and
brane masses
m^{B}_{\alpha\beta}
in (
2.20
).
In the present paper we have considered diagonal brane masses
m^{B}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}m_{B_{\alpha}}
.
When
m^{B}_{\alpha\beta}
is not diagonal, a general CP violation phase
\delta_{\rm CP}
can arise but
it becomes very difficult to solve the equations of motion explicitly.
In this regards we would like to recall that the CKM matrix in the quark sector is naturally reproduced in the GUT
inspired GHU with brane interactions which are diagonal in the generation space.
[
50
]

From the viewpoint of grand unification it is quite natural to imagine that brane interactions in the lepton sector
are diagonal as well.

Appendix A
Basis functions

We summarize basis functions used for wave functions of gauge and fermion fields.
For gauge fields we introduce

\displaystyle F_{\alpha,\beta}(u,v)

\displaystyle\equiv J_{\alpha}(u)Y_{\beta}(v)-Y_{\alpha}(u)J_{\beta}(v)~,

(A.1)

\displaystyle C(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda zz_{L}F_{1,0}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.2)

\displaystyle S(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda zF_{1,1}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.3)

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda^{2}zz_{L}F_{0,0}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.4)

\displaystyle S^{\prime}(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda^{2}zF_{0,1}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.5)

\displaystyle\check{S}(z;\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{C(1;\lambda)}{S(1;\lambda)}\,S(z;\lambda)~,

(A.6)

where
J_{\alpha}(u)
and
Y_{\alpha}(u)
are Bessel functions of the first and second kind.
They satisfy

\displaystyle-z\frac{d}{dz}\frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{dz}\begin{pmatrix}C\cr S\end{pmatrix}=\lambda^{2}\begin{pmatrix}C\cr S\end{pmatrix}~,

(A.7)

with the boundary conditions
C=z_{L}
,
C^{\prime}=S=0
and
S^{\prime}=\lambda
at
z=z_{L}
,
and a relation
CS^{\prime}-SC^{\prime}=\lambda z
holds.

For fermion fields with a bulk mass parameter
c
, we define

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{L}\cr S_{L}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda\sqrt{zz_{L}}F_{c+\frac{1}{2},c\mp\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{R}\cr S_{R}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\pi}{2}\lambda\sqrt{zz_{L}}F_{c-\frac{1}{2},c\pm\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda z,\lambda z_{L})~,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\check{S}_{L}\cr\check{C}_{R}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{L}(1;\lambda,c)}{S_{L}(1;\lambda,c)}\begin{pmatrix}S_{L}\cr C_{R}\end{pmatrix}(z;\lambda,c)~.

(A.10)

These functions satisfy

\displaystyle D_{+}(c)\begin{pmatrix}C_{L}\cr S_{L}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}S_{R}\cr C_{R}\end{pmatrix},

(A.11)

\displaystyle D_{-}(c)\begin{pmatrix}S_{R}\cr C_{R}\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}C_{L}\cr S_{L}\end{pmatrix},~~D_{\pm}(c)=\pm\frac{d}{dz}+\frac{c}{z}~,

(A.12)

with the boundary conditions
C_{R/L}=1
,
D_{-}(c)C_{R}=D_{+}(c)C_{L}=S_{R/L}=0
at
z=z_{L}
, and

C_{L}C_{R}-S_{L}S_{R}=1
.

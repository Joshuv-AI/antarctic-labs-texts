Cryo-ET of IgG bivalent binding on SARS-CoV-2 provides structural basis for antibody avidity
Authors: Hangping Yao, Yutong Song, Qi Huang, Miaojin Zhu, Jiaming Liang, Zheyuan Zhang, Xiaodi Zhang, Dongyang Dong, Danrong Shi, Zhigang Wu, Xiangyun Lu, Haibo Wu, Yong Chen, Sai Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The bivalent nature of IgG antibodies can enhance its neutralization potency against enveloped viruses; however, on-virion structural details of IgG bivalent binding with antigens remain elusive. Here we investigate how two potent IgGs P17 and S309 interact with S-trimers on the SARS-CoV-2 surface by cryo-ET. We find both IgGs exploit the mobility of S-trimers to form diverse configurations of S-IgG dimer-of-trimers, which oligomerize into higher-order patterns. Specifically, P17 stabilizes S-trimers into linear assemblies within minutes, whereas S309 primarily stabilizes S-trimer into circular assemblies that extend into lattice-like structures. Both assembly patterns effectively activate complement cascade. Additionally, both IgGs can facilitate inter-virion coupling through bivalent binding of opposing S-trimers, potentially enhancing immune recognition and clearance. These findings establish a structural framework for understanding IgG avidity in neutralizing enveloped viruses and offer valuable insights for antibody engineering and vaccine design.

Introduction

IgG, the most abundant antibody isotype in serum, plays a crucial role in the humoral immune response against a wide array of pathogens, including enveloped viruses. As a key component of adaptive immunity, IgG contains two antigen-binding fragments (Fab regions)1, enabling simultaneous engagement with two epitopes. This bivalent binding enhances overall binding strength, a phenomenon termed avidity. Avidity improves viral neutralization efficiency by enhancing epitope recognition, promoting multivalent interactions, restricting viral escape, and facilitating immune clearance. Its role in viral neutralization has been reported across various enveloped viruses, including influenza virus2, respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)3, MERS-CoV4, Chikungunya virus5 and Dengue virus6. The avidity effects on SARS-CoV-2 are particularly evident when comparing the neutralization efficiency of some potent IgGs to their Fab fragments7,8. Notably, certain IgGs induce the shedding of S1 subunits, whereas their Fab fragments do not9. In addition to the Fab region, IgG avidity is also conferred by the Fc region10. This region interacts with hexameric C1 complex and Fc receptors (FcRs), facilitating complement activation, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and phagocytosis, thereby enhancing viral clearance11. However, interactions between a single Fc region and either the C1 complex or FcRs are relatively weak, necessitating IgG multimerization into immune complexes to achieve sufficient avidity and effectively trigger effector functions12.

Despite extensive functional research, the structural basis of high-avidity IgG-antigen interactions on enveloped viruses remains largely unresolved. This challenge stems from the inherent flexibility of IgG molecules, originating from their hinge regions13, and the pleomorphic nature of enveloped viruses14,15. Previous studies employing X-ray crystallography and single-particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) have sought to bypass these challenges by analyzing Fab complexes with viral glycoprotein (GP) fragments such as peptides16,17, receptor binding domains (RBDs)18,19, or GP ectodomains20–23. While these approaches have yielded critical insights into Fab-epitope interactions, the physiologically relevant context imposed by viral membranes is missing. Investigations utilizing Fab fragments on intact enveloped viruses provide a more native setting; however, these approaches overlook the manner in which IgG molecules engage multiple antigenic sites on the viral surface—an essential aspect of high-avidity binding24–26. To date, only a limited number of structural studies have examined intact enveloped viruses incubated with full-length IgGs, and most of these have reported observations restricted to single Fab binding events27,28. These findings suggest that IgG bivalency is context-dependent, influenced by IgG structure, epitope accessibility, and the spatial arrangement of antigens on the viral surface.

In this work, we employ cryo-electron tomography (cryo-ET) and subtomogram averaging (STA) to elucidate how two potent anti-RBD neutralizing IgGs, P17 and S309, engage with the trimeric SARS-CoV-2 spike protein (hereafter referred to as S-trimers) on viral surface. Our findings demonstrate that the IgG form of both P17 and S309 (P17-IgG, S309-IgG) possesses greater neutralization potency than its Fab fragment (P17-Fab, S309-Fab), highlighting the crucial role of bivalency in antiviral efficacy. Cryo-ET and STA analysis reveal diverse inter-spike bivalent binding modes on SARS-CoV-2 virions incubated with P17 or S309. Both IgGs utilize their bivalent nature and the intrinsic mobility of S-trimers to assemble S-IgG dimer-of-trimers complexes. When mapped onto the viral surface, we find these S-IgG complexes stabilize the S-trimers into higher-order S-IgG assemblies. Moreover, both IgGs are also capable of inter-virion coupling by bivalently binding opposing S-trimers on neighboring virions. Collectively, these findings establish a structural framework for IgG avidity and provide valuable insights for antibody engineering and vaccine development, particularly in the optimization of epitope presentation and multimerization strategies to enhance immune responses.

Results

P17-IgG binds all RBDs on-virion and alters the conformational ratio of prefusion S-trimers

To investigate the neutralizing mechanisms of P17-IgG29, a class-2 anti-RBD antibody30, we first examined its binding to S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2 and its impact on S-trimer conformations. Viral infection inhibition assays were performed using the SARS-CoV-2 WT strain (ID: ZJU-05)31 in a BSL-3 laboratory. Both P17-Fab and P17-IgG potently neutralized live SARS-CoV-2 virions; however, the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of P17-IgG (0.016 nM) was markedly lower than that of P17-Fab (4.699 nM), indicating that P17-IgG exhibits considerably greater neutralization potency than its Fab counterpart (Fig. 1a). To elucidate the structural basis underlying this enhanced neutralization, we employed cryo-ET and STA to analyze SARS-CoV-2 incubated with either P17-IgG or P17-Fab (Supplementary Table 1 and Supplementary Fig. 2). The supernatant from infected cells, at a concentration of ~10⁷ virions/mL, was incubated with P17-IgG (2 μg/mL) at room temperature for 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h or 4 h followed by paraformaldehyde (PFA) inactivation (SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG). To compare the effects of IgG and Fab binding on virions, live virions were incubated with P17-Fab in the same molar Fab concentration (1.33 μg/mL) for 15 min, followed by PFA inactivation (SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab) as a control. Notably, the ratio of virions to IgG/Fab for the cryo-ET sample preparation is significantly higher than that of the neutralization assay. Since PFA fixation is not an immediate process, experimental observations should be interpreted in relative terms rather than absolute time points. To assess the effect of chemical fixation on the antigenicity of on-virion S-trimers, control samples of concentrated prefixed virions (SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo) were incubated with P17-IgG (43.5 µg/mL) for durations of 15 min, 30 min, 1 h or 2 h (SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG). Following inactivation validation, samples were transferred to a BSL-2 lab for purification and imaging.

Using cryo-ET, we imaged and reconstructed 3,258 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions, 599 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab virions and 885 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions as tomographic data. Tomographic analysis revealed that the viral surface exhibited more complex S-trimers architectures than those observed on SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo31. Additional densities protrude from S-trimers’ apices, indicating P17 binding (Fig. 1b). Subsequent STA of 29,981 prefusion S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG demonstrated that all prefusion S-trimers were engaged with P17 and were exclusively in a one-RBD-up conformation. On the prefusion S-trimer structure, each of the three RBDs, regardless of being in the up- or down conformations, is bound with P17. Further classification of the S-trimers revealed an S-P17 Solo complex structure (Fig. 1c). To determine whether any two of the three Fab densities within an S-P17 Solo complex could originate from a single P17-IgG, a previously reported class-2 RBD-neutralizing antibody Fab structure (PDB: 7K8O, not P17)30 was fitted into the Fab densities as a generic Fab model. Given the hinge length constraints of an IgG, the maximal C-terminal Fab CH1 domain (residue 222 of the heavy chain) separation should not exceed 65 Å30. However, the distances measured in the S-P17 Solo complex were 81, 100, and 146 Å (Supplementary Fig. 3a), suggesting that each Fab density belonged to separate P17-IgG molecules. For convenience of description, the up-RBD is numbered as upRBD1 with its attached P17 numbered as P17-1, the remaining RBDs and P17 molecules are numbered ascendingly in the clockwise direction. The S-P17 Solo complex structure was consistent with the structure of the soluble ectodomain of the S-trimer in complex with P17-Fab (PDB: 7CWM) (Supplementary Fig. 4a).

Given that approximately half of the prefusion S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo adopt a closed conformation31, we hypothesized that P17-IgG binding stabilizes prefusion S-trimers in a one-RBD-up conformation. To test this hypothesis, we first performed STA on 10,377 prefusion S-trimers of SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG. Despite all RBDs being occupied by P17, only 55.4% prefusion S-trimers adopted a one-RBD-up conformation, with the remainder adopting a closed conformation (Fig. 1d, Supplementary Fig. 4c). This conformational ratio mirrored that of prefusion S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo. Next, STA on 12,210 prefusion S-trimers from SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab revealed that over half of the prefusion S-trimers remained unbound. Among the bound S-trimers, which displayed both one-RBD-up and closed conformations, RBD occupancy was incomplete (Fig. 1d, Supplementary Fig. 4b). Compared to soluble ectodomain S-P17-Fab structures exhibiting one-RBD-up or two-RBD-up conformations29, our on-virion S-P17-Fab Solo complexes on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab displayed different conformational distributions and significantly lower P17-Fab binding efficiency. Notably, a construct of prefusion-stabilized ectodomain S-trimer that predominantly adopts a one-RBD-up conformation32 was used for studies involving soluble S-trimers.

Intriguingly, the presence of P17-IgG is associated with an increased proportion of postfusion S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG. To investigate whether P17-IgG induces S1 shedding, we quantified pre- and postfusion S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG, SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab and SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions. The proportion of postfusion S-trimers per virion was higher on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG (6%-33%) than on SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo (0%). Additionally, this proportion increased over incubation time on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG, but showed no such trend on SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG (Fig. 1e). Western blot analysis of HEK293T cells expressing S protein confirmed enhanced S1 shedding upon P17-IgG treatment (Fig. 1f), supporting the hypothesis that P17-IgG facilitates S1 dissociation, which may contribute to neutralization.

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that P17-IgG efficiently binds all three RBDs within an on-virion S-trimer, irrespective of its conformational state or fixation status, whereas P17-Fab exhibits lower binding efficiency. Upon binding with an unfixed on-virion S-trimer, P17-IgG, but not P17-Fab, stabilizes all prefusion S-trimers in a one-RBD-up conformation. By stabilizing the one-RBD-up conformation and inducing S1 shedding, P17-IgG exerts its neutralizing effects through both avid binding and conformational modulation.

Bivalent binding of P17-IgGs forms eleven modes of S-P17 Gemini complexes and stabilizes S-trimers into strings

When inspecting the averaged structure of all prefusion S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG at lower contour thresholds, extended densities emerged from the upRBD1-P17-1 and RBD2-P17-2 domains, forming a second S-P17 complex. Interestingly, the connecting densities between these S-P17 complexes exhibited a characteristic feature of two IgG molecules, with two pairs of Fab densities docking across adjacent S-trimers to form a symmetric dimer-of-trimers structure (Supplementary Fig. 5a, b). To further investigate this phenomenon, we re-aligned the complexes with the dual S-P17 complexes centered and applied dimeric symmetry. Subsequent classification (Supplementary Fig. 5c) identified two distinct configurations: the S-P17 Solo complexes (Fig. 1c) (48.6% prefusion S-trimers, 11.4 Å resolution) and the S-P17 Gemini complexes (51.4% prefusion S-trimers, 12.4 Å resolution) (Supplementary Fig. 5d). Here, we define the Solo complex as a subtomogram average containing a single S-trimer bound by antibody, irrespective of whether a neighboring S-trimer is present outside the subtomogram box. Gemini complexes refer to subtomogram averages or composite representations that include two adjacent S-trimers crosslinked by one or more IgG molecules, regardless of whether additional neighboring S-trimers are present beyond the analyzed region. To validate the presence of S-P17 Gemini complexes on virions, we examined the viral surface in the tomograms and identified pairs of prefusion S-trimers bridged by IgG-like densities (Fig. 2a). Within the Gemini complex, two Fabs pairs (Fab1-Fab2 of P17-1 and P17-2 in top view) couple the two S-trimers on their upRBD1s and RBD2s, forming two separate bridges. The third RBD of each S-trimer was occupied by a separate P17 (P17-3 and P17-4), although the Fab densities there were weaker than those observed in the upRBD1- or RBD2-bound regions. Although the Fc regions were visible in tomograms (Fig. 2a), they were largely absent from averaged P17 densities (Fig. 2b), likely due to their inherent mobility relative to the Fab fragments. To determine whether any two neighboring Fab fragments within the Gemini complex originated from a single P17-IgG molecule, we fitted a previously reported class-2 RBD-neutralizing antibody Fab model (PDB: 7K8O)30 into the Fab densities (Supplementary Fig. 3b). Two key criteria were used for validation: (1) the distance between the Fab fragments must not exceed 65 Å30, as constrained by the IgG hinge region, and (2) the orientations of the two Fabs shall converge on their C-termini. The measured distance between the heavy chain residues 222 of the fitted models was 41 Å, aligning with the analogous distance observed in an intact human IgG1 (PDB: 1HZH)1. To explore additional modes of bivalent binding, we first projected the S-P17 Solo complexes onto their refined coordinates on the viral surface. We then applied the same criteria used for identifying the S-P17 Gemini complex to determine whether two neighboring Fabs belonged to a single P17-IgG molecule. This analysis discovered ten additional S-P17 Gemini complexes modes, with their respective prevalence shown in Fig. 2c. These modes were categorized into two groups based on the number of P17-IgGs involved in bivalent interactions. In the first category, a single P17-IgG molecule coupled two S-trimers by engaging in any pairwise combinations among upRBD1, RBD2 or RBD3. This category accounted for six S-P17 Gemini binding modes with a total prevalence of 23.6%. In the second category, two P17-IgG molecules cooperatively bridged two S-trimers, leading to the formation of five distinct S-P17 Gemini complexes, collectively representing 76.4% of the total structures. Among these, Mode II(1) was the most populated class and was therefore characterized in detail (Fig. 2b). In the remaining mode II(2-5) complexes, the two S-trimers were coupled by P17-IgG molecules bridging various RBD pairs, including RBD3-RBD2, RBD2-RBD2, RBD3-upRBD1, RBD2-upRBD1, and RBD3-RBD3. Notably, in mode II(4) and mode II(5), one of the S-trimers exhibited a flipped orientation in which its up-RBD was positioned beneath the bridging IgGs, leading to greater separation of the S-P17 stalks.

Building on these modes, we found that P17-IgG promotes the clustering of S-trimers into higher-order multimers (Fig. 2d). A frequently observed arrangement was a linear assembly of S-P17 complexes, where successive trimers are linked through bivalent IgG interactions. Although rare, trimer-of-trimers configurations were also detected. To visualize the complexity and collective impact of P17-mediated S-oligomerization on intact virions, we reconstructed SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions by mapping the S-P17 Solo and Gemini structures onto their refined coordinates. Compared to SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo31, the majority of S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG were gathered into patches, with interspersed exposed regions of the viral envelope (Fig. 2e). A similar gathering effect was also observed on SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (Supplementary Fig. 6).

Assembling the mode II(1) Gemini complex requires precise spatial alignment of neighboring S-trimers at specific distances and angles. The observation that a substantial number of S-trimers successfully form such complexes on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG is therefore unexpected. One possible explanation is that P17-IgG leverages its intrinsic flexibility and the mobility of S-trimers on the viral surface to stabilize S-trimers in assembling the mode II(1) complexes. To test this possibility, we first analyzed the inter-trimer distance between the S1 apices of the two closest S-trimers (Dhead) on virions. We observed a median Dhead value of 14.2 nm for SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG across all incubation durations. In contrast, the same analysis conducted on SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-Fab and SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG (15 min incubation) yielded median Dhead values of 17.4 nm and 18.0 nm, respectively, which were comparable to the median value of 18.7 nm calculated from our prior SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo dataset31. After extended incubation, the Dhead of SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG exhibited only a modest reduction to ~16.8 nm (Fig. 2f). These findings suggest that P17-IgGs facilitate rapid S-trimer clustering within minutes on live virions, whereas this clustering efficiency is reduced on PFA-fixed virions, where S mobility and conformation are restricted. This phenomenon is further reflected in the proportion of mode II(1) Gemini complexes observed on virions. On SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG across all incubation durations, ~50% of prefusion S-trimers were incorporated into mode II(1) complexes, whereas only ~10% of prefusion S-trimers were coupled into mode II(1) complexes on SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG (30 min incubation) (Fig. 2g). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that P17-IgG mediates diverse bivalent binding interactions that cluster S-trimers within minutes on live virions. The interplay between S-trimer mobility on the viral envelope and IgG flexibility is crucial for the formation of these assemblies, which may enhance neutralization by increasing local antibody density and disrupting viral architecture.

Bivalent binding of S309-IgGs forms five modes of S-S309 Gemini complexes and stabilizes S-trimers into rings

To determine whether inter-spike bivalent binding and spike aggregation are unique to P17-IgG, we conducted analogous structural experiments using another class-3 anti-RBD monoclonal antibody30, S30933 (Supplementary Table 2). S309-IgG exhibited an IC50 of 0.398 nM in neutralizing SARS-CoV-2, which is ~22-fold lower than that of S309-Fab (IC50 = 8.614 nM), indicating substantially higher neutralizing potency (Fig. 3a, and Supplementary Fig. 1). WT SARS-CoV-2 (10⁷ virions/mL) was incubated with S309-IgG (2 μg/mL) at room temperature for 30 min, followed by PFA inactivation (SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG). Cryo-ET revealed distinct S309 densities localized at the S1 apices of the S-trimers (Fig. 3b). Preliminary STA and classification of 11,747 prefusion S-trimers from 492 SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG virions identified two major conformations of S-S309 complexes: a closed conformation (34.8%) and a one-RBD-up conformation (65.2%) (Supplementary Fig. 7a and 8 a). At lower thresholds, emerging densities extending from each RBD were observed. Notably, a single closed-RBD exhibited the strongest density across both S-S309 complexes, forming a secondary S-S309 complex. This suggests that S309-IgG may also engage in inter-spike bivalent binding on-virion. Indeed, further classification revealed two S-S309 Solo complexes (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Fig. 7) and five distinct S-S309 Gemini complexes (Supplementary Fig. 8a). Unlike P17-IgG, which mainly forms double-bridged Gemini complexes, S309-IgG exclusively forms single-bridged Gemini complexes. Among the nine modes of S-S309 Gemini complexes observed on-virion, the majority were associated with the closed-RBDs of adjacent S-trimers; while up-RBDs, although less frequent, also contributed to S-coupling (Fig. 3d). The distribution of S-S309 Gemini complex modes is relatively balanced, in contrast to the predominant mode II(1) in S-P17 Gemini complexes. Notably, 91% of S-S309 Gemini complexes incorporate at least one one-RBD-up S. Together with the relatively weaker Fab densities observed on closed S, our findings suggest that S309-IgG preferentially binds to the one-RBD-up S. Further mapping of all S-S309 Solo and Gemini complexes onto their refined coordinates revealed that S309-IgG promotes distinct spatial arrangements of S-trimers compared to P17-IgG. Specifically, S309-IgG preferentially organizes S-trimers into ring-like assemblies, including tetramer-, pentamer-, and hexamer-of-trimers (Fig. 3f and Supplementary Fig. 9). Within these ring structures, each S-trimer is linked to its two neighboring S-trimers via S309-IgGs. This coupling mechanism may facilitate the formation of larger multimers, such as heptamer- or octamer-of-trimers. Furthermore, bivalent binding by S309-IgG also leads to the formation of multi-ring lattice structures on virions (Fig. 3g), a spatial organization that contrasts with the linear assemblies of S-P17.

The ring-like S-S309 assemblies are reminiscent of the complement structure. Binding of multimeric IgG-antigen complex triggers the activation of complements, which plays a critical role in viral clearance in vivo. To evaluate the efficiency in complement activation, we assessed the complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) of both P17-IgG and S309-IgG in HEK293T cells stably expressing SARS-CoV-2 S, using P17-Fab and S309-Fab as controls. Both antibodies were capable of activating complement and inducing cell lysis in their IgG forms, but not in their Fab forms (Fig. 3h).

Collectively, these findings demonstrate that although both antibodies exploit inter-spike bivalent binding to stabilize S-trimers clusters, S309-IgG preferentially organizes S-trimers into circular arrangements due to differences in epitope accessibility and binding orientation. Both linear and circular-like assemblies activate the complement cascade. These differences likely arise from the distinct structural properties of the S309 and P17 epitopes, as well as their influence on S-trimer conformations upon binding. S309-IgG’s ability to assemble S-trimers into rings and lattices highlights the versatility of bivalent binding in shaping viral surface architecture, providing further insights into the structural basis of IgG-mediated neutralization and Fc-mediated effector functions.

IgGs can aggregate virions through inter-virion bivalent binding

Cryo-ET analysis of SARS-CoV-2 virions incubated with P17-IgG or S309-IgG revealed occasional virion aggregation, suggesting that IgGs may facilitate inter-virion coupling through bivalent binding. Examination of SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG tomogram identified densities corresponding to S-trimers from neighboring virions connected by a single P17-IgG molecule (Fig. 4a). By mapping the S-P17 Solo and Gemini complexes onto their refined coordinates, we confirmed that the observed inter-virion coupling resulted from bivalent interactions of a single P17-IgG engaging RBDs on adjacent virions (Fig. 4b). To assess the functional impact of IgG-induced virion aggregation, we performed dynamic light scattering (DLS) experiments using SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo. Over a two-h incubation period, the mean hydrodynamic radius of the sample increased progressively, consistent with virion clustering. In contrast, no significant increase in particle size was observed in control samples lacking P17-IgG, confirming that the aggregation was IgG-dependent (Supplementary Fig. 10). A similar phenomenon was observed in SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG tomograms, where coupling occurred through S309-IgG-mediated bivalent interactions. Composite reconstruction of these virions revealed a pentamer-of-trimers inter-virion complex containing four S-trimers from one virion and one S-trimer from a neighboring virion. This assembly was stabilized by multiple S309-IgG molecules bridging the virions (Fig. 4c). To quantify the relative contributions of inter- and intra-virion bivalent binding, we analyzed the proportion of inter- and intra-virion bivalent binding events between adjacent S-trimers. Inter-virion binding accounted for only ~5% of all bivalent interactions, whereas intra-virion binding accounted for 95%, remaining the predominant mode of IgG engagement (Fig. 4d). Discussion

In this study, we applied cryo-ET to characterize the structural mechanisms by which two potent anti-RBD nAbs, P17 and S309, engage with S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2. Our findings demonstrate that intact IgGs engage in distinct oligomerization patterns compared to their Fab counterparts, forming inter-spike and inter-virion interactions that significantly alter the structural landscape of the viral surface. The structures of on-virion S-Fab and S-IgG Solo complexes largely recapitulate their corresponding soluble ectodomain S-Fab structures. However, the Gemini and higher-order assemblies of S-IgG highlight key differences between on-virion and soluble ectodomain structural studies. Several factors may contribute to these discrepancies. First, intact IgGs were used in our study, whereas prior studies involving soluble S-trimers utilized Fabs. Second, prefusion S-trimers in their native virion context and those used for studies involving soluble S-trimers often exhibit different conformational distributions. For example, on-virion S-trimers predominantly adopt a closed or one-RBD-up state at an approximate ratio of 1:131. In contrast, prefusion-stabilized ectodomain S-trimer that predominantly adopts a one-RBD-up conformation32 was used for studies involving soluble S-trimers29. Furthermore, our findings emphasize the role of spatial organization in IgG-mediated viral inhibition. Unlike soluble S-trimers in solution, which rely on Brownian motion and concentration gradients for inter-spike binding, on-virion S-trimers are constrained by the membrane. Their spatial distribution, swinging31 and diffusion34,35 features on the viral envelope, together with the IgG flexibility, facilitate rapid and stable multimerization of S-trimers. This explains why the tentative inter-spike bivalent S-IgG structures determined from soluble S-trimers are often in a head-to-head configuration36,37, different from the Gemini and ring structures we reported.

Next, we aim to rationalize the differences between P17 and S309 in their capacity to form S-IgG multimers. While both antibodies bind to RBDs in either the up- or closed-conformations, they exhibit distinct modes of S-IgG multimerization. These differences may stem from variations in their epitopes and their respective impacts on S-trimer conformation upon binding. On the viral surface, P17 stabilizes all prefusion S-trimers in a one-RBD-up conformation, resulting in the movement of P17-1 to P17-2 (Fig. 5a). In category I S-P17 Gemini complexes, a single P17-IgG bridges two S-trimers, minimizing steric clashes. Conversely, in category II, two P17-IgGs couple S-trimers, necessitating spatial adjustments to avoid steric hindrance (Fig. 5b). This is particularly evident in modes II(2-5), where the two S-trimers adopt unfavorable spatial arrangements, requiring increased spacing between their stalk regions to prevent clashes at the N-terminal domains (NTD). Notably, Mode II(1) is an exception, representing the predominant structural population of prefusion S-trimers on P17-treated virions and the only structurally resolved Gemini complex. This implies that Mode II(1) achieves an optimal stability likely due to cooperative structural interactions at both the S1 apex and stalk regions of the S-trimers. At the S1 apex, the two S1 subunits are symmetrically coupled via their upRBD1-RBD2 regions by a pair of P17-bridges, reinforcing the Gemini structure. At the stalk region, the two stalks maintain close alignment and remain nearly parallel (Fig. 5b). In comparison, S-S309 Gemini complexes are exclusively coupled by a single S309-IgG. The even distribution of S309-IgGs across the S1 apices of S-trimers, regardless of the one-RBD-up or closed conformational states (Fig. 5c), likely precludes the formation of double-bridged Gemini complexes due to steric clashes at the NTDs (Fig. 5d). Additionally, the distinct binding modes of P17 and S309 influence how their respective S-IgG Gemini complexes extend into higher-order multimers. IgG-mediated immunity against enveloped viruses operates primarily through two mechanisms: neutralization38 and Fc-mediated effector functions11. Our study provides structural insights into IgG avidity and offers evidence supporting these immunological pathways. For neutralization, we demonstrate that P17-IgG exhibits enhanced affinity through bivalent binding compared to P17-Fab, enabling more effective epitope engagement on virions. Additionally, the avidity interactions between IgG and the S-trimer can induce S1 shedding. Through versatile modes of bivalent binding, both P17 and S309 effectively gather prefusion S-trimers on the viral surface. This process likely introduces tension within the S1 subunits, leading to their shedding, as evidenced by our cryo-ET and Western blot analyses. This mechanism may explain why certain IgGs exhibit greater potency in inducing S1 shedding compared to their Fab counterparts9. For some antibodies with lower intrinsic potency in their Fab form, the corresponding IgG often shows substantially enhanced neutralization compared with the Fab7. In terms of Fc-mediated effector functions, the multimeric forms of S-P17 and S-S309 generate diverse patterns that may display enhanced Fc-avidity10. S309 has been shown to elicit robust Fc-mediated effector functions39. Our structural data reveal that the ring-like S-S309 multimers resemble the IgG hexamers known to effectively engage with the C1 complex, thereby initiating the classical pathway of complement activation40. Moreover, our functional data show that S-P17 also induces complement-dependent cytotoxicity. These findings suggest that the complement system is compatible with various forms of antigen-IgG spatial arrangements.

Another compelling observation is the ability of IgG to mediate inter-virion coupling through bivalent binding. Although inter-virion bivalent binding constitutes only 5–6% of total bivalent interactions, its occurrence suggests an additional mechanism by which IgGs influence viral dynamics. By linking adjacent virions into clusters, IgG-mediated aggregation may enhance immune recognition and activate effector functions, thereby contributing to overall antiviral efficacy. However, the physiological relevance of this phenomenon remains uncertain. In vivo conditions may impose constraints on IgG-induced aggregation due to lower viral loads and antibody concentrations. In our experiments, when live SARS-CoV-2 virions were incubated with IgGs to generate the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG and SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG samples, the final concentrations of virions and IgGs were ~10⁷ virions/mL and 2 μg/mL, respectively. By comparison, the peak viral load of WT SARS-CoV-2 detected in nasal samples is ~105 virions/mL41, and a comparable RBD-specific IgG concentration of ~10 μg/mL has been reported in patients’ serum42. While some studies suggest that IgG can aggregate SARS-CoV-2 via crosslinking36,37, our data indicate that such aggregation events are relatively uncommon in vivo, except in regions with exceptionally high localized viral concentrations.

The significant variation in avidity among nAbs targeting enveloped viruses43 highlights the necessity of understanding how IgGs achieve bivalency at the molecular level, taking into account antibody structure, epitope accessibility, and antigenic organization. Several key factors contribute to this process. First, the Fab association rate constant influences bivalent binding efficiency. Upon initial Fab binding to an epitope, a restricted reaction volume enhances the probability of the free Fab arm binding to an adjacent epitope. IgGs with a higher association rate constant for the second Fab arm in their half-bound state are more likely to engage in efficient bivalent interactions44. Second, bivalent binding is largely influenced by epitope accessibility and conformational independence. Epitopes located on membrane-distal regions of glycoproteins are more conducive to bivalent binding than those near membrane-proximal stalks, where steric hindrance is more pronounced27,45. In SARS-CoV-2, the average distance between the stalk regions of neighboring S-trimers is approximately 15 nm (Supplementary Fig. 11), which is comparable to the spatial separation between the antigen-binding sites of an IgG molecule46. Consequently, nAb targeting the S2 subunit would potentially engage in inter-spike bivalent binding on virions47. Moreover, epitopes that remain structurally accessible regardless of glycoprotein conformation, such as the class-2 and class-3 RBD-nAbs30 or 101 F epitope of the RSV F protein48, are more likely to support bivalent interactions. Third, the orientation of Fab binding influences whether IgGs engage in intra- or inter-spike bivalency. For example, P17 and S309 Fabs bind all three epitopes of an S-trimer with their C-termini pointing outward, facilitating inter-spike bivalent interactions. In contrast, IgGs with downward-facing Fab orientations targeting S-trimers are less likely to participate in inter-spike bivalent binding due to steric interference from their Fc domains49. Finally, inter-spike bivalency is modulated by the antigen landscape on the viral surface. Factors such as glycoprotein molecular mass, oligomerization state, density, and mobility play a crucial role in shaping IgG binding modes. Viruses with densely packed glycoproteins, such as influenza and Lassa viruses14, or those with large and highly mobile spikes, such as SARS-CoV-2, are more susceptible to inter-spike bivalent binding compared to viruses with sparse or relatively static glycoproteins46.

Our findings also have implications for natural antibody screening, therapeutic antibody design and vaccine development. The comparison of bivalent binding efficiency on SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG and SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG samples has led us evaluate the potential effectiveness of chemically crosslinked SARS-CoV-2 as vaccines. We demonstrated that chemical crosslinking deprives S-trimers of their mobility and conformational dynamics on fixed SARS-CoV-2, resulting in significantly reduced avid interactions with antibodies compared to those on live SARS-CoV-2. This limitation likely impacts B cell activation and the subsequent production of nAbs50. In addition, the ability of P17 and S309 to induce distinct S-trimer assemblies suggests that optimizing epitope presentation, oligomerization and accessibility on vaccine platforms could enhance immunogenicity. Overall, our structural analysis of IgG avidity highlights the importance of antigen mobility and multimerization in eliciting effective immune responses. These findings inspire further investigations into the multivalent interactions between viruses and various antibody types, including IgG, IgA, IgM, and engineered multimeric antibodies. Mucosal antibodies IgA and IgM harbor four and ten Fab per molecule, respectively, providing higher valency and greater spatial accessibility than IgG. Owing to their capacity to engage multiple epitopes simultaneously, these antibodies may be particularly effective in driving both intra-virion and inter-virion clustering of S-trimers. In the context of respiratory viral infection, especially at early stages prior to the development of virus-specific immune memory or the production of high-affinity antibodies, avidity of IgA and IgM may play an important role in limiting viral entry and spread.

Method

Cells, virus and antibody

Vero cells (ATCC CCL-81) were cultured in Modified Eagle Medium (MEM; Corning Inc., Corning, NY) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Carlsbad, CA), Penicillin (100 U/mL) and streptomycin (100 μg/mL) (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA). SARS-CoV-2 virions were propagated using Vero cells. On the fourth day post-infection, the cell supernatant was collected, cleared of cell debris by centrifugation at 4000 × g for 30 min. Titration of virus in the cell supernatant was measured by TCID50 assay. The virus solution was diluted in a 10-fold gradient series in MEM culture medium containing 2% FBS. 100 μL of diluted virus solution per well was added to a 96-well plate (Greiner Bio-One, Germany). MEM medium without virus solution was used as normal cell control. Then, 104 cell/100 μL Vero cells were added in each well, and incubated in 5% CO2 at 35 °C. The cytopathic effect (CPE) was observed and recorded on day 5 of culture. According to the CPE results, the TCID50 of the virus was calculated by Spearman-Karber method. There were 8 wells in each group, and the experiment was repeated for 3 times. The measured infectious viral titer is 107.2 TCID50/mL.

Based on virus fixation condition and antibody type, five types of samples were prepared. For the SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo sample, the virus-containing supernatant was inactivated with paraformaldehyde (PFA; final concentration 3%) for 48 h in 4 °C.

For the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG sample, the virus-containing supernatant was incubated with monoclonal antibody P17-IgG (2 μg/mL) at room temperature for 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h or 4 h, followed by inactivation with PFA.

For the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab sample, the virus-containing supernatant was incubated with P17-Fab at a concentration of 1.33 μg/mL, room temperature for 15 min, followed by inactivation with PFA.

For the SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG sample, the SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo was purified through a 30% sucrose cushion by ultracentrifugation at 100,000 × g, 4°C for 3 h, then resuspended in PBS buffer and incubated with P17-IgG (43.5 µg/mL) at room temperature for 15 min, 30 min, 1 h or 2 h.

For the SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG sample, the virus-containing supernatant was incubated with monoclonal antibody S309-IgG (2 μg/mL) at room temperature for 30 min, followed by inactivation with PFA.

All the experiments involving infectious SARS-CoV-2 were carried out in an approved biosafety level (BSL)-3 laboratory of Zhejiang University. Purification, concentration and EM sample preparation of fixed virus were conducted in a BSL-2 lab at Tsinghua University.

Antibody expression and identification

Expression and purification of P17-IgG antibody was performed as described29. Briefly, plasmids harboring the heavy-chain and light-chain of P17 were co-transfected into ExpiCHO cells. The cell supernatant was collected and purified by MabSelect SuRe LX (Cytiva, Marlborough, MA) after 7 to 15 days of transfection.

P17-Fab antibody was expressed using the ExpiCHO system (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Eukaryotic expression plasmid pcDNA3.3-TOPO (Invitrogen) containing separate heavy-chain VH and CH1, light-chain VL and CL1 were co-transfected into ExpiCHO cells. After 7 to 15 days of transfection, the cell supernatant was collected and purified by Ni-NTA (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Finally, the purified antibody was exchanged into PBS buffer and concentrated through the centrifugal filter unit (Millipore).

Plasmids encoding the heavy and light chains of S309-IgG were transiently co-transfected into HEK293F cells using PEI MAX (Polysciences) in a molecular ratio of 1:2. After 4 days of transfection, the cell supernatant containing S309-IgG were collected and centrifugated at 4000 r.p.m. for 20 min to remove cell debris. S309-IgG in the supernatant were purified by a Hitrap Protein A HP column (Cytiva) and gel filtration chromatography using Superdex 200 Increase 10/300 GL (Cytiva).

S309-Fab antibody were generated by incubating S309-IgG with crystallized papain (Sigma-Aldrich) in a buffer consisting of 50 mM sodium phosphate, 2 mM EDTA, 10 mM L-cysteine, pH 7.4 at 37 °C for 1.5 h. The IgG-to-enzyme ratio is 100:1. The digested products were subsequently purified via a Hitrap Protein A HP column (Cytiva) and gel filtration chromatography using Superdex 200 Increase 10/300 GL (Cytiva).

The neutralization efficiency of P17-IgG, P17-Fab, S309-IgG, S309-Fab against SARS-CoV-2 was measured by virus microneutralization assay. In brief, antibodies were diluted in a two-fold series, combined with 100 TCID50 of SARS-CoV-2 viruses, and then the mixture was incubated for 2 h at 35 °C. After that, Vero cells were added and incubated at 35 °C with 5% CO2. The neutralization efficiency was assessed by observing CPE on day 5 of the culture, thereby confirming the assay outcome. The culturing supernatant was tested for SARS-CoV-2 by qRT-PCR on day 3 of the culture. Nucleic acids were automatically extracted using the EX3600 system (Liferiver Biotech, Shanghai, China) with a magnetic bead-based kit (Cat No. MVR01; Liferiver Biotech). Viral nucleic acid abundance was then quantified using SARS-CoV-2 qRT-PCR kits (Cat No. Z-RR-0517-02; Liferiver Biotech) on a CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad). The inhibition was calculated as: Inhibition rate (%) = (1 − Relative viral load of experimental group / Relative viral load of control group) × 100%, with relative viral load determined by the 2-ΔCt method.

Cryo-ET sample preparation

For the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG, SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab and SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG samples, virions were purified through a 30% sucrose cushion by ultracentrifugation at 100,000 × g, 4 °C for 3 h, then resuspended in PBS buffer. An 8 μL aliquot of the concentrated virus sample was applied onto a glow-discharged copper grid coated with holey carbon film (R2/2, 300 mesh, Quantifoil, Jena, Germany). Grid was skimmed over PBS to remove residual sucrose. After adding 3 μL of 10-nm gold tracer (Aurion, the Netherlands), the grid was blotted for 4 s and plunge-frozen into liquid ethane using a cryo-plunger 3 (Gatan Inc., CA). For the SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG sample, the virus was applied directly onto the grids, which were then plunge-frozen in the same manner as described above.

Cryo-ET data acquisition

Grids were imaged on a Titan Krios microscope (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Hillsboro, OR) operated at 300 kV equipped with a K3 direct electron detector (Gatan Inc., CA) and a BioQuantum energy filter (slit width 20 eV). Virions were recorded in super-resolution mode at a nominal magnification of 64,000×, resulting in a calibrated pixel size of 0.68 Å. Tilt series were recorded with eight or ten frames at each angle using a total dose of 131.2 e-/Å2 and defoci ranging from -2 to -4 μm in SerialEM 3.7.6 or 4.1.0beta51. For SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG sample, 169 sets of tilt series using dose-symmetric scheme from -60° to 60° and 128 using bidirectional scheme from 15° to -60°, then 18° to 60° at 3° steps were collected. For SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab sample, 54 sets of tilt series using dose-symmetric scheme were collected. For SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG sample, 65 sets of tilt series using bidirectional scheme were collected. For SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG sample, 38 sets of tilt series using dose-symmetric scheme were collected.

Cryo-ET data processing

Tilt series data were preprocessed using FlyTomo, a high-throughput software developed in our lab52. Motion between frames were corrected using MotionCor1 2.153 and MotionCor2 (03-16-2016 version)54. Exposure filtering was applied55. Defocus values were estimated using GCTF 1.0656. Tilt series were aligned by tracking gold fiducials using IMOD 4.10.18 or 4.11.1257. 276 tomograms of the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG sample, 60 tomograms of the SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG sample, 54 tomograms of the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab sample and 33 tomograms of the SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG sample with good alignment were reconstructed by weighted-back projection after correcting the contrast transfer function (CTF) using NovaCTF58, resulting in a final pixel size of 1.36 Å/pixel. For particle picking, tomograms were either 4 × binned and low-pass filtered to 80 Å, or 8 × binned and denoised using cryoCARE 0.359. S-trimers were manually picked and their initial orientations were given from vectors normal to the envelope. For improved visualization and presentation, tomograms were 8 × binned and missing-wedge corrected using IsoNet v0.260.

Subtomogram averaging (STA) was carried out using Dynamo 1.1.33361. As independent STA of S-trimers of SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG across different incubation times did not reveal different structures, these particles were merged for alignment. 29,981 particles were extracted from 4 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 96 × 96 × 96). In stage 1, a low resolution (40 Å) bin4 closed S map from our previous SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo dataset31 was used as the initial template. STA applying C1 symmetry resulted in a one-RBD-up S-P17 complex structure with each of the three RBDs bound with a Fab density. At lower threshold, emerging densities extended from the upRBD1-P17-1 and RBD2-P17-2 domains, forming a second S-P17 complex. The connecting densities between the two S-P17 complexes were characteristic of two pairs of Fabs docking across the S-trimers to form a symmetric dimer-of-trimers. In stage 2, the S-P17 dimer-of-trimers complexes were recentered and re-extracted into subtomograms (box size 120 × 120 × 120) for re-alignment with limited azimuth angular and spatial search and C2 symmetry applied. In stage 3, the particles were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry using the one-RBD-up S-P17 structure and the S-P17 dimer-of-trimers structure as templates. Classification resulted in an S-P17 Solo structure (18,725 particles) and an S-P17 Gemini structure (10,301 particles). In stage 4, the S-P17 Gemini complexes were re-extracted from the 2 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 196 × 196 × 196) for gold-standard alignment31 imposing C2 symmetry, resolving an S-P17 Gemini structure at 12.4 Å resolution (9835 particles). A random subset (9246 particles) of the S-P17 Solo complexes (18,596 particles) were recentered and re-extracted from the 2 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 176 × 176 × 176) for gold-standard alignment imposing C1 symmetry, resolving an S-P17 Solo structure at 11.4 Å resolution. Duplicate particles were removed at the end of each round of STA.

STA of the SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG S-trimers were similar to that of SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG. In stage 1, 10,377 S-trimer particles were extracted from the 4 × binned tomograms and aligned using the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG stage 1 average (lowpass-filtered to 30 Å) as a template. The average resembles an S-trimer in closed conformation with each RBD in complex with a Fab density. In stage 2, the particles were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry using the lowpassed SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG stage-1 average and the lowpassed SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG stage-1 average as templates. The classification revealed a one-RBD-up S-P17 structure (5749 particles) and a closed S-P17 structure (4628 particles). In stage 3, the one-RBD-up S-P17 particles were subjected to an identical classification and alignment procedures as the stages 2 of SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG, resulting in an S-P17 Solo structure (4884 particles) and an S-P17 Gemini structure (818 particles). The RBD-closed S-P17 particles were re-extracted from 2 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 176 × 176 × 176) for gold-standard alignment, resolving a 11.4 Å resolution structure. Duplicate particles were removed at the end of each round of STA.

For the SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab sample, 12,210 S-trimers were extracted from 4 × binned tomograms. Stage 1 STA settings were identical to those of SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG, however, resulted in an S-trimer with weak Fab densities. In stage 2, the particles were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry using the lowpassed SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG stage-1 average and lowpassed EMD-30426 as templates. Classification revealed an S-trimer without Fab density (8563 particles) and S-trimers with Fab densities (3647 particles). In stage 3, S-trimers without Fab density (Apo) were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry using lowpassed EMD-30426 and EMD-30427 as templates, revealing a one-RBD-up S Apo structure (2477 particles) and a closed S Apo structure (6086 particles). S-trimers with Fab density were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry using the lowpassed SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG stage-1 average and the lowpassed S-P17 structure in closed conformation of SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG stage-2 as templates. Classification revealed a one-RBD-up S-P17-Fab structure (1825 particles) and a closed S-P17-Fab structure (1822 particles). Duplicate particles were removed at the end of each round of STA.

For the SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG sample, 11,747 S-trimers were extracted from 4 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 96 × 96 × 96). Stage 1 STA settings were identical to those of SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG. The average resembles an S-trimer with each of the three RBDs bound with a Fab density. In stage 2, the particles were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry using a low resolution (40 Å) bin4 closed S map and a one-RBD-up S map from our previous SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo dataset as templates, revealing a one-RBD-up S-S309 complex (7664 particles) and a closed S-S309 complex (4083 particles). Extending densities were visible on all three RBD-S309 domains of S-S309 complexes in both conformations. Among the three densities, the density extended from the closed-RBD-S309 was the strongest, which became another S-S309 complex at lower threshold. In the stage 3, both the one-RBD-up and closed S-S309 complex were subjected to multi-reference alignment imposing C1 symmetry. A lowpassed solo S-S309 complex and a lowpassed S-S309 dimer-of-trimers complex were used as templates, resulting two S-S309 Solo complexes and two S-S309 Gemini complexes. In stage 4, each of the two S-S309 Gemini complexes were classified by multi-reference alignment applying C1 symmetry using three lowpassed S-S309 Gemini complexes as templates. At this stage, the particles were classified into S-S309 Solo complexes in one-RBD-up conformation (4237 particles), S-S309 Solo complexes in closed conformation (2153 particles) and six modes of S-S309 Gemini complexes (5231 particles). Two of the S-S309 Gemini complex modes were combined due to their similarity, eventually classifying the S-S309 Gemini complex into five distinctive modes: mode (1) (1045 particles), mode (2) (1099 particles), mode (3) (1509 particles), mode (4) (572 particles) and mode (5) (433 particles). In stage 5, the 4237 one-RBD-up S-S309 Solo complexes and the 2153 closed S-S309 Solo complexes were re-extracted from the 2 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 176 × 176 × 176) for gold-standard alignment imposing C1 symmetry, resolving a 14.5-Å one-RBD-up S-S309 Solo structure and a 16.0-Å closed S-S309 Solo structure, respectively. The 1045 mode (1) S-S309 Gemini complexes were recentered and extracted from the 2 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 196 × 196 × 196) for gold-standard alignment, resolving a 14.4-Å resolution structure. Duplicate particles were removed at the end of each round of STA.

To construct the composite structure of an example SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG or SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virion, the S-P17 Solo and S-P17 Gemini structures were projected onto their refined coordinates. Similarly, the composite structure of SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG virion was reconstructed by projecting the S-S309 Solo and S-S309 Gemini structures onto their refined coordinates. The viral envelopes were reconstructed by segmenting manually labeled densities.

For the ring-like S-trimer assemblies in SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG sample, the assembly centers were marked manually on the composite virion structures using the Volume Tracer tool in Chimera 1.1662. The vector normal to the envelope was applied as the initial orientation. For the tetramer-of-trimers, 69 particles were extracted from 4 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 160 × 160 × 160) and averaged to generate an initial template. Subsequent alignment was performed with either C1 or C4 symmetry imposed. For the pentamer-of-trimers, 53 particles were extracted from 4 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 160 × 160 × 160) and averaged to generate an initial template. Subsequent alignment was performed with either C1 or C5 symmetry imposed. For the hexamer-of-trimers, 17 particles were extracted from 4 × binned tomograms into subtomograms (box size 160 × 160 × 160) and averaged to generate an initial template. Subsequent alignment was performed with either C1 or C6 imposed.

Structure fitting

To interpret the S-P17 Solo structure, the model of the one-RBD-up S-P17-Fab (PDB: 7CWM) was fitted into our structure using ChimeraX 1.363. Since the constant domain of P17 Fab was not included in the S-P17 model, models of an intact class-2 nAb Fab (PDB: 7K8O) were subsequently fitted to the Fabs densities of the S-P17 Solo structure. To interpret the mode II(1) S-P17 Gemini structure, two models of the one-RBD-up S-P17-Fab (PDB: 7CWM) and six models of Fab (PDB: 7K8O) were fitted into the structure using ChimeraX 1.3. Similarly, the models of the S-S309-Fab in the one-RBD up and closed conformation (PDB: 6WPT, 6WPS), and the models of the complete S309-Fab (PDB: 6WS6) were fitted into the S-S309 Solo and Gemini complex structures using ChimeraX 1.3.

To determine whether two adjacent Fabs belong to a P17-IgG or S309-IgG molecule in S-P17 and S-S309 complexes, the distance between the heavy chain residues 222 of the fitted 7K8O and 6WS6 models was measured. If the distance was less than 65 Å and the C termini of adjacent Fabs were in an inward-pointing orientation, the two Fabs were regarded as part of a P17-IgG or S309-IgG molecule30.

Western blotting

A plasmid encoding SARS-CoV-2 WT strain (Wuhan-Hu-1) S protein was transfected into HEK293T cells using the Lipo8000 transfection reagent (Beyotime Biotec. Inc., Shanghai, China). At 24 hours post-transfection, HEK293T cells were removed from the plate with 0.2 mg/mL EDTA and washed twice with 100 μL ice-cold PBS (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) containing 2% FBS (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA). ~105 cells per sample were incubated in the presence or absence of 2 μg/mL P17 in 100 μL PBS containing 2% FBS for 0, 15, 30, 60, 120 or 240 min at 37 °C. After the allocated incubation periods, samples were immediately placed on ice, and cell debris was removed by centrifugation. SDS-PAGE sample Loading Buffer (Beyotime Biotec. Inc., Shanghai, China) was added, and the mixture was boiled at 100 °C for 20 min. All samples were run on a 4–20% gradient Bis-Tris gel (GenScript Biotech Corporation, Jiangsu, China) and transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane (Merck Millipore Ltd., Co. Cork, Ireland) at a constant voltage of 20 V for 35 min. Total protein normalization was performed on the transferred PVDF membrane using the No-Stain Protein Labeling Reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA). The membrane was blocked with 6% (w/v) non-fat milk (Sangon Biotech Co., Shanghai, China) diluted in PBS containing 0.1% Tween-20 (PBS-T) for 60 min at room temperature. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 Spike S1 NTD monoclonal antibody (Cell Signaling Technology, MA; 1:1000 dilution) and Horse Radish Peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (Sino Biological Inc., Beijing, China; 1:5000 dilution) diluted in PBS-T were used for Western blotting. HRP detection was performed using an SuperSignal West Pico PLUS Chemiluminescent Substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bremen, Germany) and imaged with a GelView 6000Plus Imager (BLT photon technology, Guangdong, China). Western blot bands were quantified using ImageJ 1.53 m.

Complement-dependent cytotoxicity assay

HEK293T cells stably expressing full-length SARS-CoV-2 S was used as target cells. Serial dilutions of P17-Fab, P17-IgG, S309-Fab and S309-IgG were incubated with the target cells for 10 min at 37 °C. Pre-adsorbed Low-Tox M Rabbit Complement (Cederlane Laboratories Limited, Ontario, Canada) was then added at a final dilution of 1:24. Following a 3-h incubation at 37 °C, cell lysis was quantified using an LDH release assay (Cytotoxicity Detection Kit, Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Absorbance was measured kinetically at 492 nm and 650 nm every minute for 8 min. The slope of the kinetics curve was used by applying the following formula: (specific release – spontaneous release) / (maximum release - spontaneous release) × 100%. The spontaneous release is the level of lysis of target cells with complement and without antibodies. The maximal release is the level of lysis of target cells with complement and 0.83% Triton X-100.

Dynamic light scattering

The SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo sample was purified through a 30% sucrose cushion by ultracentrifugation at 100,000 × g, 4 °C for 3 h, resuspended in PBS buffer and mixed with or without P17-IgG (final concentration 100 μg/mL) at 25 °C. Dynamic light scattering measurements were taken immediately after incubation at 10-min intervals and continued for 2 h on a DynaPro Nanostar instrument (Wyatt Technology, CA). 30 measurements were taken at each time point to obtain the radius distribution of solution.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All data was analyzed and plotted using GraphPad Prism 10.3.0. For the pre- and postfusion S-trimers count per virion (Figs. 1e), 2020 SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo virions31, 72 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab virions, 78 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (15 min incubation), 79 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (15 min incubation), 75 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (30 min incubation), 74 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (30 min incubation), 74 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (1 h incubation), 74 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (1 h incubation), 69 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (2 h incubation), 71 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (2 h incubation), and 69 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (4 h incubation) were used for analysis.

For the distance between the S1 apices of two nearest S-trimers on each P17-incubated SARS-CoV-2 virion (Dhead, Fig. 2f), the Dhead was calculated according to the refined S coordinates from subtomogram averaging. The defined S1 apex is 76 Å above the center of the resulting subtomogram averaging map of S-P17 Solo complexes, near the top of S and the center of residues 503 in RBD-down conformation. 52,380 prefusion S-trimers from 2020 SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo virions31, 12,068 prefusion S-trimers from 599 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-Fab virions, 20,486 prefusion S-trimers from 1611 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (15 min incubation), 6197 prefusion S-trimers from 599 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (30 min incubation), 5458 prefusion S-trimers from 590 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (1 h incubation), 2732 prefusion S-trimers from 284 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (2 h incubation), 1058 prefusion S-trimers from 171 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (4 h incubation), 1826 prefusion S-trimers from 192 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (15 min incubation), 2906 prefusion S-trimers from 207 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (30 min incubation), 2084 prefusion S-trimers from 140 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (1 h incubation), and 3709 prefusion S-trimers from 346 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions (2 h incubation) were used for analysis. Distances greater than 40 nm or less than 5 nm were excluded from the analysis.

As to the proportion of S that composes S-P17 Gemini complexes in prefusion S-trimers per virion (Fig. 2g), 9835 S-P17 Gemini particles and 18,596 S-P17 Solo complexes particles on 3255 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions were used for statistics. 1611 virions (15 min incubation), 599 virions (30 min incubation), 590 virions (1 h incubation), 284 virions (2 h incubation) and 171 virions (4 h incubation) were used, respectively. For the SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG sample, 818 S-P17 Gemini complexes, 4884 S-P17 Solo complexes in one-RBD-up conformation and 4628 S-P17 Solo complexes in closed conformation on 885 SARS-CoV-2fixed-P17-IgG virions were used for statistics. 192 virions (15 min incubation), 207 virions (30 min incubation), 140 virions (1 h incubation) and 346 virions (2 h incubation) were used respectively. Each S-P17 Gemini complex contains two S-trimers.

For the ratios of inter-virion and intra-virion bivalent binding (Fig. 4d), IgG bivalent binding interactions was assessed using the 65 Å distance criterion between Fabs. The coordinates of 14,006 S-P17 Solo complexes and 7340 S-P17 Gemini complexes from 2210 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (15 min and 30 min incubation) were analyzed for Fab distance measurements. A total of 10,917 bivalent binding interactions were observed, including 10,433 intra-virion bivalent binding interactions and 484 inter-virion bivalent binding interactions. For SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG, the coordinates of the one-RBD-up and closed S-S309 complex (prior to classification as Solo or Gemini structures) were analyzed for Fab distance measurements. Distances from 11,746 prefusion S-trimers on 492 SARS-CoV-2live-S309-IgG virions were measured, resulting in 6131 bivalent binding interactions, including 5850 intra-virion bivalent binding interactions and 281 inter-virion bivalent binding interactions.

For the ratios of S-P17 Gemini complex (Fig. 2c), IgG bivalent binding interactions were assessed using the 65 Å distance criterion between Fabs. The coordinates of 14,006 S-P17 Solo complex and 7340 S-P17 Gemini complex from 2210 SARS-CoV-2live-P17-IgG virions (15 min and 30 min incubation) were analyzed for Fab distance measurements. 2382 category I bivalent binding interactions and 7694 category II bivalent binding interactions were determined from the intra-virion bivalent binding interactions. In the category I, 154 mode I(1), 164 mode I(2), 325 mode I(3), 527 mode I(4), 652 mode I(5) and 560 mode I(6) bivalent binding interactions were determined. In the category II, 7399 mode II(1), 49 mode II(2), 144 mode II(3), 44 mode II(4), 23 mode II(5) and 35 other forms of bivalent binding interactions were determined.

For the distance between the S1 apices and stalk regions of two nearest S-trimers on SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo virions (Dhead and Dstalk, Supplementary Fig. 11), Dhead was calculated using the same approach as described in Fig. 2f. Dstalk is calculated based on the refined S coordinates from subtomogram averaging. The defined stalk region is defined as a point located 100 Å from the center the resulting subtomogram averaging map of S-trimers, corresponding to the vicinity of the residue 1160 in the fitted PDB model (PDB: 6XR8). In total, 52,380 prefusion S-trimers from 2020 SARS-CoV-2fixed-apo virions31 were used for analysis. Distances greater than 40 nm or less than 5 nm were excluded from the analysis.

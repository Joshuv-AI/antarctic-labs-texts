A new categorical equivalence for Stone Algebras
Ismael Calomino* and Gustavo Pelaitay**

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to give a categorical equivalence for Stone algebras. We introduce the variety of Stone-Kleene algebras with intuitionistic negation, or Stone KAN-algebras for short, and explore Kalman’s construction for Stone algebras. We examine the centered algebras within this new variety and prove that the category of Stone algebras is equivalent to the category of centered Stone KAN-algebras. Moreover, inspired by Monteiro’s construction for Nelson algebras, we propose a method to construct a centered Stone KAN-algebra from a given Stone KAN-algebra and show the connection between Kalman’s construction and Monteiro’s construction.

1.
Introduction

The definition of a Nelson algebra was introduced by Rasiowa in
[
23
]
and plays a role in the study of constructive propositional calculus with strong negation, analogous to the role of Boolean algebras in classical propositional calculus. Nelson algebras can be viewed as Kleene algebras with an additional binary operation which generalizes relative pseudocomplementation (
[
8
,
16
]
). In
[
24
]
, Sendlewski generalized results from Fidel and Vakarelov
[
10
,
27
]
to prove the deep connection between Nelson algebras and Heyting algebras. To establish a similar relationship for a broader class of algebras, Sendlewski introduced the class
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
of Kleene algebras with weak pseudocomplementation in
[
25
]
, aiming to establish a topological relationship between this class and pseudocomplemented distributive lattices. The class
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
encompasses the implication-free reducts of Nelson algebras, with the additional operation interpreted as weak pseudocomplementation. In
[
12
]
, the authors examine the connection between strong negation and intuitionistic negation in Nelson algebras. They introduce the variety of Kleene algebras with intuitionistic negation (abbreviated as KAN-algebras) and apply Kalman’s construction (see
[
17
]
) for pseudocomplemented distributive lattices. Through this construction, they establish that the algebraic category
PDL
of pseudocomplemented distributive lattices is equivalent to the algebraic category
KANc
of centered KAN-algebras. Furthermore, they show in
[
12
]
that the variety of KAN-algebras coincide with the class of
\mathcal{K}_{\omega}
-algebras.

On the other hand, it is well known that a Stone algebra is a pseudocomplemented distributive lattice
A
which satisfies the equation
x^{\ast}\vee x^{\ast\ast}=1
, where
∗
denotes the pseudocomplement of
A
. Stone algebras were introduced by Grätzer and Schmidt in response to a problem given by Stone. These algebras are characterized as bounded distributive lattices in which each prime ideal contains a unique minimal prime ideal (
[
14
]
). Since their introduction, Stone algebras have been extensively studied in the literature (
[
1
,
13
]
) and have found applications in various areas, such as conditional event algebras and rough sets (
[
22
,
28
]
).

2.1.
Distributive p-algebras

A pseudocomplemented distributive lattice, or distributive p-algebra for short, is an algebra
\langle A,\vee,\wedge,^{\ast},0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,1,0,0)
, where
\langle A,\vee,\wedge,0,1\rangle
is a bounded distributive lattice, and for each
a,b\in A
, the following condition holds:
a\wedge b=0
if and only if
a\leq b^{\ast}
. So,
a^{\ast}
is the greatest element of
A
that is disjoint from
a
, that is,
a^{\ast}=\max\{x\in A\colon a\wedge x=0\}
. In every distributive p-algebra, the conditions
1=0^{\ast}
and
0=1^{\ast}
necessarily hold (
[
1
]
). A distributive p-algebra
A
is called a Stone algebra if it satisfies the equation

x^{\ast}\vee x^{\ast\ast}=1

(2.1)

for all
x\in A
. The class of Stone algebras is a variety introduced by Grätzer and Schmidt in
[
14
]
, named in honor of M. H. Stone. These algebras have been extensively studied by various authors and continue to be a significant area of research (see
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
18
,
19
]
). Moreover, they play a crucial role in the theory of rough sets (
[
9
,
16
,
20
,
22
,
11
]
).

2.2.
KAN-algebras

Recall that a Kleene algebra is an algebra
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,1,0,0)
where
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,0,1\rangle
is a bounded distributive lattice and

(K1)

\sim\sim x=x
,

(K2)

\sim(x\vee y)=\sim x\wedge\sim y
,

(K3)

x\wedge\sim x\leq y\vee\sim y
.

Additionally, in a Kleene algebra
T
, the following properties hold:

(1)

\sim 0=1
and
\sim 1=0
,

(2)

\sim(x\wedge y)=\sim x\vee\sim y
,

(3)

x\leq y
implies
\sim y\leq\sim x
.

A Kleene algebra is called centered if it has a center, i.e., an element
c
such that
\sim c=c
. In every Kleene algebra the center, when it exists, is unique.

Recall that a Nelson algebra (
[
23
,
24
]
) is a Kleene algebra
T
such that for each pair
x,y\in T
, there exists a binary operation
\to
given by
x\to y:=x\to_{\mathsf{HA}}(\sim x\vee y)
(where
\to_{\mathsf{HA}}
is the Heyting implication), and for every
x,y,z
, it holds that
(x\wedge y)\to z=x\to(y\to z)
. Nelson algebras can be viewed as algebras
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\to,\sim,0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,2,1,0,0)
. The class of Nelson algebras forms a variety (
[
3
,
2
]
).

Now, we introduce the class of Kleene algebras with intuitionistic negation, or KAN-algebras. The KAN-algebras have a weaker structure than Nelson algebra, just like distributive p-algebras have a weaker structure than Heyting algebras.

Definition 1
.

[
12
]

A
Kleene algebra with intuitionistic negation
, or
KAN-algebra
, is an algebra
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,\neg,0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,1,1,0,0)
such that
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,0,1\rangle
is a Kleene algebra and the following conditions are satisfied:

(N1)

\neg(x\wedge\neg(x\wedge y))=\neg(x\wedge\neg y)
,

(N2)

\neg(x\vee y)=\neg x\wedge\neg y
,

(N3)

x\wedge\sim x=x\wedge\neg x
,

(N4)

\sim x\leq\neg x
,

(N5)

\neg(x\wedge y)=\neg((\sim\neg x)\wedge y)
.

An algebra
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,\neg,c,0,1\rangle
of type
(2,2,1,1,0,0,0)
is a
centered KAN-algebra
, or
KANc-algebra
, if
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,\neg,0,1\rangle
is a KAN-algebra and the structure
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,c,0,1\rangle
is a centered Kleene algebra.

We denote the variety of KAN-algebras by
\bf{KAN}
and the variety of centered KAN-algebras by
\bf{KANc}
.

\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,\to,0,1\rangle

\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,\neg,0,1\rangle

Remark 1
.

If

A Kleene algebra
\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,0,1\rangle
is said to be quasi weak pseudocomplemented if for each

\{y\in T\colon x\wedge y\leq\sim x\},

which is denoted by
\neg x
. The element
\neg x
is characterized by the following property:

y\leq\neg x\Longleftrightarrow x\wedge y\leq\sim x,

for all

. The unary operation

\langle T,\vee,\wedge,\sim,\neg,0,1\rangle

Kleene algebra with a weak pseudocomplementation

\neg(x\wedge y)=1\Longleftrightarrow\neg\neg x\leq\neg y.

Let

Let

Lemma 2.3
.

[

Let

i.e.,
\lozenge(x\wedge y)=0

\lozenge x\leq\lozenge\neg y

Let

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
T\in{\bf{KAN}}

\lozenge x=x

\lozenge y=y

\lozenge(x\wedge y)=0

Let

The next results will be useful to us.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
T\in{\bf{KAN}}
. Then:

(1)

\lozenge\lozenge x=\lozenge x
,

(2)

\lozenge(x\vee y)=\lozenge x\vee\lozenge y
,

(3)

\lozenge(\lozenge x\wedge\lozenge y)=\lozenge(x\wedge y)
,

(4)

\square\square x=\square x
,

(5)

\square(x\wedge y)=\square x\wedge\square y
,

(6)

\square(\square x\vee\square y)=\square(x\vee y)
,

(7)

x\vee\sim x=\sim x\vee\lozenge x
,

(8)

x\wedge\sim x=x\wedge\square\sim x
,

(9)

x=(\lozenge x\vee\sim x)\wedge\square x
.

Proof.

(1): By (8) of Proposition
2.1
we have
\lozenge\lozenge x=\sim\neg\lozenge x=\sim\neg x=\lozenge x
.

(2): By (N2) we have

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\lozenge(x\vee y)&=&\sim\neg(x\vee y)&=&\sim(\neg x\wedge\neg y)\\
&=&\sim\neg x\vee\sim\neg y&=&\lozenge x\vee\lozenge y.\\
\end{array}

(3): By (N5),

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\lozenge(\lozenge x\wedge\lozenge y)&=&\sim\neg(\sim\neg x\wedge\sim\neg y)&=&\sim\neg(x\wedge\sim\neg y)\\
&=&\sim\neg(x\wedge y)&=&\lozenge(x\wedge y).\\
\end{array}

(4): By (8) of Proposition
2.1
it follows
\square\square x=\square\neg\sim x=\neg\sim x=\square x
.

(5): By (N2) we have

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\square(x\wedge y)&=&\neg\sim(x\wedge y)&=&\neg(\sim x\vee\sim y)\\
&=&\neg\sim x\wedge\neg\sim y&=&\square x\wedge\square y.\\
\end{array}

(6): Then, by (N5)

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\square(\square x\vee\square y)&=&\neg\sim(\neg\sim x\vee\neg\sim y)\\
&=&\neg(\sim\neg\sim x\wedge\sim\neg\sim y)\\
&=&\neg(\sim x\wedge\sim\neg\sim y)\\
&=&\neg(\sim x\wedge\sim y)\\
&=&\neg\sim(x\vee y)\\
&=&\square(x\vee y).\\
\end{array}

(7): By (N3),

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}x\vee\sim x&=&\sim\sim x\vee\sim x&=&\sim(x\wedge\sim x)\\
&=&\sim(x\wedge\neg x)&=&\sim x\vee\lozenge x.\\
\end{array}

(8): It follows by (N3) and Definition
2.3
.

(9): By items (7), (8) and Proposition
2.1
we have

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}(\lozenge x\vee\sim x)\wedge\square x&=&(x\vee\sim x)\wedge\square x\\
&=&(x\wedge\square x)\vee(\sim x\wedge\square x)\\
&=&x\vee(\sim x\wedge\square x)\\
&=&x\vee(\sim x\wedge x)\\
&=&x.\\
\end{array}

This concludes the proof.
∎

The following result lists some properties that hold in centered KAN-algebras.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
T\in{\bf{KANc}}
. Then:

(1)

\neg c=1
,

(2)

\lozenge c=0
and
\square c=1
,

(3)

\lozenge(c\wedge\square x)=0
and
\square(\lozenge x\vee c)=1
,

(4)

x=(\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square x
,

(5)

x\vee c=\lozenge x\vee c
.

Proof.

(1): The proof can be found in
[
12
]
.

(2): By item (1) and the definitions
2.2
and
2.3
we have

\lozenge c=\sim\neg c=\sim 1=0\text{ and }\square c=\neg\sim c=\neg c=1.

(3): Since
c\wedge\lozenge x\leq c
and
c\leq\lozenge x\vee c
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\lozenge(c\wedge\square x)\leq\lozenge c
and
\square c\leq\square(\lozenge x\vee c)
. Then, by item (2), we have
\lozenge(c\wedge\square x)=0
and
\square(\lozenge x\vee c)=1
, which proves (3).

(4): We will use the Moisil’s determination principle. We show
\lozenge x=\lozenge((\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square x)
and
\square x=\square((\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\lozenge x)
. By Propositions
2.1
,
2.2
and item (3) we have

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}\lozenge((\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square x)&=&\lozenge((\lozenge x\wedge\square x)\vee(c\wedge\square x))\\
&=&\lozenge(\lozenge x\vee(c\wedge\square x))\\
&=&\lozenge\lozenge x\vee\lozenge(c\wedge\square x)\\
&=&\lozenge x\\
\end{array}

and
\square((\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square x)=\square(\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square\square x=\square x
. Therefore,
x=(\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square x
.

(5): By Proposition
2.1
we have
\lozenge x\leq\square x
. Thus, by item (4),

\begin{array}[]{lllllll}x\vee c&=&((\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge\square x)\vee c&=&(\lozenge x\vee c)\wedge(\square x\vee c)\\
&=&(\lozenge x\wedge\square x)\vee c&=&\lozenge x\vee c,\\
\end{array}

i.e.,
x\vee c=\lozenge x\vee c
as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
T\in{\bf{KAN}}
and
c\in T
. Then
c
is the center of
T
if and only if
\lozenge c=0
and
\square c=1
.

Proof.

If
c
is the center of
T
, by Proposition
2.7
we have
\lozenge c=0
and
\square c=1
. Conversely, if we suppose
\lozenge c=0
and
\square c=1
, then by Proposition
2.2
we have
c=(\lozenge c\vee\sim c)\wedge\square c=(0\vee\sim c)\wedge 1=\sim c
and

is the center of

.
∎

Remark 2
.

A
modal De Morgan algebra
is a pair
\langle A,\square\rangle
, where
A
is a De Morgan algebra and
\square
is a unary operator satisfying

\square 1=1\quad\text{and}\quad\square(x\wedge y)=\square x\wedge\square y.

By defining
\lozenge x:=\sim\square\sim x
, one may equivalently describe a modal De Morgan algebra as a pair
\langle A,\lozenge\rangle
satisfying

\lozenge 0=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lozenge(x\vee y)=\lozenge x\vee\lozenge y.

These operators are typically referred to as
normal modal operators
(see
[
4
,
15
]
). Moreover, based on the results of Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
, we can conclude that
\lozenge
and
\square
are normal modal operators.

4.
Monteiro’s construction

Given a Nelson algebra, it is always possible to obtain a centered Nelson algebra through a process known as Monteiro’s construction, which, notably, was never published. Inspired by this construction, we apply a similar method to obtain a centered Stone KAN-algebra from a Stone KAN-algebra. Although our method draws inspiration from Monteiro’s approach, it is more general. Consequently, Monteiro’s contributions and their corresponding proofs were not applicable to our work.

Conclusions and future work

In this paper we introduced the variety of Stone-Kleene algebras with intuitionistic negation, or Stone KAN-algebras, and established a categorical equivalence between the category of Stone algebras and the category of centered Stone KAN-algebras. By employing Kalman’s construction, we provided a method to construct a centered Stone KAN-algebra from a given Stone algebra. Additionally, drawing inspiration from Monteiro’s construction, we given a construction for centered Stone KAN-algebras showing the relationship between the two constructions.

In future work, we plan to extend these results to the context of KAN-algebras with additional operations. This includes the tense operators defined on KAN-algebras given in
[
21
]
and the weak quantifiers introduced in
[
12
]
.

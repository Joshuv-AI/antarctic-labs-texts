On Subgroup Separability and Membership Problems in Twisted Right-Angled Artin Groups
Islam Foniqi

Abstract
We characterize twisted right-angled Artin groups (T-RAAGs) that are subgroup separable using only their defining mixed graphs: such a group is subgroup separable if and only if the underlying simplicial graph contains neither induced paths nor squares on four vertices. This generalizes the results of Metaftsis–Raptis on classical right-angled Artin groups. Additionally, we show that the subgroup membership problem is decidable when the group is coherent, which occurs precisely when the defining mixed graph is chordal. We also address the rational and submonoid membership problems by exhibiting a cone-family of graphs for which the corresponding T-RAAGs have decidable rational and submonoid membership problems.

1
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to describe twisted right-angled Artin groups that are subgroup separable, just by examining their defining graphs.

Subgroup separability is a compelling property of groups with significant connections with geometric topology, see
(
Scott, 1978
)
for an overview. For historical reasons, subgroup separability is also called LERF (for locally extended residually ﬁnite).
Being subgroup separable is inherited by subgroups and finite extensions, and is preserved by taking free products; however, it is not preserved by taking direct products. Nevertheless, a direct product (and a semi-direct product) of a subgroup separable group with the infinite cyclic group is again subgroup separable. This will be a very useful tool for our work in this article.

In geometric group theory, the class of

right-angled Artin groups
(
RAAGs
) plays a major role, see
(
Charney, 2007
)
for a survey. RAAGs are also known as
graph groups
.

Metaftsis and Raptis (2008)
show that one can decide if a RAAG is subgroup separable or not by just examining its defining graph; the only obstructions for a RAAG to be subgroup separable are the two well known examples of non-subgroup separable groups, which are the RAAGs on
4
generators based on a square, and a path; the first one is
F_{2}\times F_{2}
, and the second one is denoted by
L
in their work.

This article deals with the same problem, with focus in another class of groups that generalises the class of RAAGs.
This is the class of
twisted right-angled Artin groups
(
T-RAAGs
shortly). T-RAAGs appear in
(
Pride, 1986
)
, and also in
(
Clancy and Ellis, 2010
)
– where the notion of a
twisted Artin group
was introduced. We refer to T-RAAGs also with the name
twisted graph groups
. More details regarding their normal forms and other properties can be found in the PhD thesis
(
Foniqi, 2022
, Chapter 3)
, and also in
(
Foniqi, 2024
)
.

The presentation of T-RAAGs is given by generators and relations: there are finitely many generators and there is at most one relation between any two distinct generators
a,b
; this relation can be either a commutation
ab=ba
, or a so-called
Klein relation

aba=b
. This presentation can be encoded by the use of mixed graphs, where vertices represent the generators of the group, while edges give rise to the relations. An undirected edge, connecting generators
a
and
b
, gives rise to the commutation
ab=ba
, while a directed edge with origin at
a
and terminus at
b
defines the Klein relation
aba=b
. If generators
a,b
are not connected by an edge, they are free of relations.

As basic and important examples of T-RAAGs, we have both the fundamental groups of a torus:
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\langle a,b\,|\,ab=ba\rangle
, and of the Klein bottle:
K=\langle a,b\,|\,aba=b\rangle
. Note that
K
is not a RAAG (because RAAGs are bi-orderable but
K
is not).

Section

2
serves to set the notation and provide the main definitions for the whole article.

Next, in
Section

3
, we provide a summary of results to characterise RAAGs that are LERF, given by
Theorem 3.1
, and proved by
Metaftsis and Raptis (2008)
:
a RAAG
A(\Gamma)
is LERF if and only if
\Gamma
does not contain both the path
P_{4}
and the square
C_{4}
, as induced subgraphs.

In
Section

4
we provide our main contribution, which is the following result:

Theorem

(
Theorem 4.7
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph and
T(\Gamma)
the T-RAAG based on
\Gamma
. The group
T(\Gamma)
is subgroup separable if and only if
\Gamma
does not contain, as induced subgraphs, mixed paths and mixed squares in
4
vertices.

Here, in contrast with RAAGs, some of the edges in our graphs can be directed. In the case when the graph has no directed edges, we recover the result of
Metaftsis and Raptis (2008)
for RAAGs.

In
Section

5
we discuss membership problems in T-RAAGs. Regarding membership in finitely generated subgroups we have the following:

Proposition

(
Proposition 5.3
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed chordal graph. Then,
T(\Gamma)
has decidable subgroup membership problem.

Non-separable T-RAAGs have undecidable submonoid and rational subset membership problems. On the other hand, we construct a cone-family
\mathcal{R}
of mixed graphs (see
Definition 5.6
and
Definition 5.7
) such that the corresponding T-RAAGs are subgroup separable, satisfying the following:

Theorem

(
Theorem 5.8
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph belonging to
\mathcal{R}
. Then
T(\Gamma)
has decidable rational subset membership problem. Consequently,
T(\Gamma)
has decidable submonoid membership problem as well.

Definitions, preliminaries, and notation

This section serves to set the notation for the article. Our groups will be given by finite presentations
G=\langle A\,|\,R\rangle
; an element

can be written as a word in

. If

2.1
Right-angled Artin groups

The graphs we use to define graph groups are called

Definition 2.1
.

A

\Gamma=(V,E)

, and

is a set of paired distinct vertices, whose elements are called

.

For a finite simplicial graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
we use
A(\Gamma)
to denote the right-angled Artin group based on
\Gamma
, defined by the presentation

A(\Gamma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 0}"303A}\mkern-1.2mu=\langle\,V\mid uv=vu\text{ whenever }\{u,v\}\in E\,\rangle.

Two important examples of RAAGs,
are those defined by paths and cycles on four vertices, provided graphically as:

P_{4}=\penalty\
a
b
c
d
\,\,\,,
C_{4}=\penalty\
a
b
c
d

Some authors refer to
P_{4}
and
C_{4}
with the word

; similarly the corresponding groups

and
A(C_{4})\simeq F_{2}\times F_{2}
get called

, as they tend to be obstructions for several properties.

2.2
Twisted right-angled Artin groups

To define T-RAAGs we use mixed graphs, which are similar to simplicial graphs but directed edges are allowed.
We define mixed graphs using simplicial graphs as an underlying structure.

Definition 2.2
.

A
mixed graph

\Gamma
consists of an
underlying simplicial graph

(V,E)
, a set of
directed edges

D\subseteq E
, and two maps
o,t\colon D\longrightarrow V
. We denote it as
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
.

For an edge
e=\{x,y\}\in D
, the maps
o,t
satisfy
o(e),t(e)\in\{x,y\}
, and
o(e)\neq t(e)
. Refer to
o(e)
and
t(e)
as the
origin
and the
terminus
of edge
e
respectively.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph.

(i)

If
e=\{a,b\}\in E\setminus D
, we write
e=[a,b]
, and note that also
e=[b,a]
. Instead,

(ii)

if
e=\{a,b\}\in D
, we write
e=[o(e),t(e)\rangle
. In this case, either
e=[a,b\rangle
, or
e=[b,a\rangle
.

Graphically, we present the respective edges
[a,b]
, and
[a,b\rangle
as:

a
b
a
b

Since every edge in
E=E\Gamma
has exactly one of these two types, one can write:

E\Gamma=\overline{E\Gamma}\sqcup\overrightarrow{E\Gamma},\text{ where }\overline{E\Gamma}=E\setminus D,\text{ and }\overrightarrow{E\Gamma}=D.

When dealing with group presentations encoded from graphs, we want edge
[a,b]
to represent the commutation of
a,b
; and edge
[a,b\rangle
to represent the corresponding Klein relation; hence by a slight abuse of notation, we also set:
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
, and
[a,b\rangle=abab^{-1}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a mixed graph with
E=\overline{E\Gamma}\sqcup\overrightarrow{E\Gamma}
. Define a group:

T(\Gamma)=\langle V\mid ab=ba\text{ if\penalty\ $[a,b]\in\overline{E\Gamma}$ },aba=b\text{ if\penalty\ $[a,b\rangle\in\overrightarrow{E\Gamma}$ }\rangle.

Call
T(\Gamma)
the
twisted right-angled Artin group
based on
\Gamma
, and
\Gamma

the defining graph
of
T(\Gamma)
.

If
\overrightarrow{E\Gamma}=\emptyset
(or equivalently if
E\Gamma=\overline{E\Gamma}
), then
T(\Gamma)
is the
right-angled Artin group

A(\Gamma)
.

Remark 2.5
.

For a given mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
, denote by
\overline{\Gamma}
the underlying simplicial graph. Call the group
A(\overline{\Gamma})
, the
underlying RAAG
of
T(\Gamma)
.
As a general theme, one tries to find connections between
T(\Gamma)
and
A(\overline{\Gamma})
.

2.3
Subgroup separability

Definition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a group and
H\leqslant G
a subgroup. We say that
H
is
separable
in
G
, if for any
g\in G\setminus H
, there is a finite index subgroup
K_{g}\leqslant G
such that
H\leqslant K_{g}
but
g\notin K_{g}
.

Definition 2.7
.

A group
G
is called
subgroup separable
(or LERF)
if every ﬁnitely generated subgroup of
G
is separable in
G
; equivalently, a ﬁnitely generated subgroup of
G
is equal to an intersection of subgroups of ﬁnite index in
G
.

Being LERF is inherited by subgroups and finite extensions. Indeed, one has:

Theorem 2.8

(
Scott, 1978
, Lemma 1.1
)
.

Let
G
be a group and
H\leqslant G
. Then:

•

If
H
is not LERF then
G
is not LERF.

•

If
H
is LERF and
H\leqslant G
has finite index, then
G
is LERF.

Being LERF is also preserved by taking free products.

Theorem 2.9

(
Burns, 1971
, Corollary 1.2
)
.

If both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are LERF groups, then their free product
G_{1}\ast G_{2}
is LERF as well.

In general, subgroup separability is not preserved by semi-direct products; indeed, the group
F_{2}\times F_{2}
is not LERF, see
Corollary 3.4
.
However, if the normal factor is ERF, then it becomes true. The notion of ERF is stronger than LERF. While LERF is defined with respect to finitely generated subgroups, ERF shares the same definition but with respect to all subgroups. Hence, if all subgroups of a group
G
are finitely generated, then
G
is LERF if and only if
G
is ERF. This is true for the group
\mathbb{Z}
, which yields several useful results.

Theorem 2.10

(
Allenby and Gregorac, 2006
, Theorem 4
)
.

Let
G=N\cdot H
with
N\cap H=\{1\}
be a splitting extension of the normal finitely generated subgroup
N
by the group
H
. If
N
is ERF and
H
is LERF, then
G
is LERF.

The infinite cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}
is ERF, as it is LERF and its subgroups are finitely generated.

Corollary 2.11
.

If
H
is LERF, then any semi-direct product
G=\mathbb{Z}\rtimes H
is LERF as well.

Since direct products are in particular semi-direct products, we have:

Corollary 2.12
.

If
H
is LERF, then the direct product
G=\mathbb{Z}\times H
is LERF as well.

2.4
Reidemeister-Schreier procedure

There is an algorithm, the
Reidemeister-Schreier procedure
, that given a presentation of a group
G
and sufficient information about a subgroup
H
of
G
, provides a presentation for the subgroup
H
, see
(
Lyndon and Schupp, 1977
, Section 4 of Chapter II)
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
F=F(X)
be a free group with basis
X
and
K
a subgroup of
F
. A
Schreier transversal
for
K
in
F
, is a subset
T
of
F
which is a
right transversal
, i.e.

•

the union
\underset{t\in T}{\bigcup}Kt
is equal to
F
, and

•

Kt_{1}\neq Kt_{2}
for
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
in
T
,

and furthermore, any initial segment (or prefix) of an element of
T
belongs to
T
as well.

In
Lyndon and Schupp (1977)
, the procedure is given like this:

•

the group
G
is given by a presentation
\langle X\,|\,R\rangle
.

•

\pi:F(X)\to G
is the canonical epimorphism from the free group
F=F(X)
onto
G
.

•

T
is a Schreier transversal for
K=\pi^{-1}(H)
in
F(X)
.

•

The map
\;\bar{\;}\colon F\to T
maps
w\in F
to its coset representative
\overline{w}\in T
.

Then,
Y=\{tx(\overline{tx})^{-1}\mid t\in T,\;x\in X,\;tx\not\in T\}
gives a set of generators for
H
.

Furthermore, there is a map
\tau\colon F(X)\to F(Y)
that rewrites each word
w\in\pi^{-1}(H)
in terms of generators of
Y
. Define

S=\{\tau(trt^{-1})\mid t\in T,r\in R\}.

Then
\langle Y\,|\,S\rangle
is a presentation for
H
. We refer to
T
as Schreier transversal for
H
in
G
.

3
Subgroup separability for RAAGs

In this section we review some properties of RAAGs, and in the next two subsections we provide a summary of the proof of the following result.

Theorem 3.1

(
Metaftsis and Raptis, 2008
, Theorem 2
)
.

A RAAG
A(\Gamma)
is subgroup separable if and only if
\Gamma
does not contain
P_{4}
or
C_{4}
as induced subgraphs; equivalently, if and only if
A(\Gamma)
does not contain
A(P_{4})
or
A(C_{4})
as subgroups.

RAAG subgroups via graphs

In this part, we only deal with the second equivalence in
Theorem 3.1
, namely, showing that:

Goal.
A RAAG
A(\Gamma)
contains
A(P_{4})
or
A(C_{4})
as subgroups, if and only if
\Gamma
contains
P_{4}
or
C_{4}
respectively, as induced subgraphs.

One direction of our goal is clear, because if
\Delta
is an induced subgraph of the simplicial graph
\Gamma
, then
A(\Delta)
is a subgroup of
A(\Gamma)
.

As a converse to the statement above, one has the following two results:

\bullet

(
Kambites, 2009
, Corollary 3.8)
shows that if
A(\Gamma)
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
A(C_{4})
, then
\Gamma
contains an induced square
C_{4}
.

\bullet

(
Kim and Koberda, 2013
, Theorem 1.7)
shows that if
A(P_{4})\xhookrightarrow{}A(\Gamma)
is a group embedding, then
\Gamma
contains an induced path
P_{4}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that none of the groups
A(P_{4})
and
A(C_{4})
can be a subgroup of the other, because none of graphs
P_{4}
and
C_{4}
is an induced subgraph of the other.

Summarising the results above, we obtain the other direction of the goal as well, as desired.

Separable RAAGs = Elementary RAAGs

Word problem is one of the fundamental algorithmic questions in algebra; for a group
G
, generated by a set
A
, the
word problem
asks whether
w\in(A\cup A^{-1})^{\ast}
is equal to
1_{G}
in
G
.
This problem is solved for T-RAAGs in
(
Foniqi, 2022
,
Foniqi, 2024
)
.

A more general problem is the
subgroup membership problem
(know also as the
generalised word problem
) for a group
G
, which asks for the existence of an algorithm that takes as input an element
g\in G
and a ﬁnitely generated subgroup
H\leqslant G
and outputs a YES – NO answer whether
g
belongs to
H
or not. This is still open for RAAGs, hence for T-RAAGs as well. There is a useful connection between being subgroup separable and having decidable subgroup membership problem.

Remark 3.3

(
Mal’cev, 1958
)
.

For ﬁnitely presented groups, being subgroup separable implies the solvability of the generalised word problem.

In
(
Mikhailova, 1966
)
it is shown that
A(C_{4})=F_{2}\times F_{2}
contains a subgroup where membership is undecidable.

Corollary 3.4
.

The group
A(C_{4})
is not subgroup separable.

On the other hand
A(P_{4})
has decidable subgroup membership problem, see
(
Kapovich, Weidmann, and
Myasnikov, 2005
, Corollary 1.3)
, as
P_{4}
is a chordal graph. However,
Niblo and Wise (2001)
show that
A(P_{4})
is not subgroup separable.

Definition 3.5
.

Simplicial graphs not containing
P_{4}
and
C_{4}
are called
transitive forests
. Right-angled Artin groups based on transitive forests are called
elementary RAAGs
.

Remark 3.6
.

The result
(
Lohrey and Steinberg, 2008
, Lemma 2)
shows that the class of elementary RAAGs, denoted by
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
, is described precisely with the following properties:

(i)

The trivial group
\{1\}
belongs to
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
,

(ii)

If
E\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
, then
E\times\mathbb{Z}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
,

(iii)

If
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
then
E_{1}\ast E_{2}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
.

In particular, the results above demonstrate that the class of RAAGs that are LERF is a subclass of elementary RAAGs.

On the other hand, the class of elementary RAAGs (see
Remark 3.6
) has nice closure properties for free products, and for direct products with
\mathbb{Z}
.
By closure properties of subgroup separability under direct products with
\mathbb{Z}
given by
Corollary 2.12
, and free products given by
Theorem 2.9
, it follows that elementary RAAGs are subgroup separable, providing ultimately the proof of
Theorem 3.1
.

4
Going from RAAGs to T-RAAGs

When extending previous results from RAAGs to the class
of T-RAAGs, which contains RAAGs, one searches for the ’poisonous pieces’
A(P_{4})
and
A(C_{4})
in the larger class. Since being subgroup separable
is inherited by subgroups and it fails for
A(P_{4})
and
A(C_{4})
, it fails for a T-RAAG
G

as well whenever
G
contains
A(P_{4})
or
A(C_{4})
.

We generalise the results from RAAGs to T-RAAGs by answering the following question.

Question 4.1
.

Which T-RAAGs contain
A(P_{4})
or
A(C_{4})
as subgroups?

We will answer one part of this question
by applying the normal forms in T-RAAGs coming from
(
Foniqi, 2022
,
Foniqi, 2024
)
, which yield the following two results:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Delta
be an induced subgraph of the mixed graph
\Gamma
. The morphism

i:T(\Delta)\longrightarrow T(\Gamma)\text{ induced by }v\mapsto v,

sends normal forms to normal forms, hence it is injective. In particular,
T(\Delta)\leqslant T(\Gamma)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The map
q:A(\overline{\Gamma})\longrightarrow T(\Gamma)
given by
v\mapsto v^{2}
sends normal forms to normal forms, hence it is injective.

Note that for RAAGs, the last lemma comes from the classical result
(
Crisp and Paris, 2001
, Corollary 2)
. In particular, one has:

Corollary 4.4
.

If
\overline{\Gamma}
contains a path on

vertices, or a square on

contains

or

respectively, in which case

is not subgroup separable.

Remark 4.5
.

The result of this corollary follows also from
(
Pride, 1986
, Theorem 2)
, because T-RAAGs are

‘

condition’
. Moreover, paths on

as well. Therefore if
{\Gamma}
contains a graph

which is a path on

contains

by

; by

, Theorem 2)
the squares of vertices of

generate a RAAG isomorphic to

, in which case

is not subgroup separable.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph. If

is not subgroup separable, then

is not subgroup separable as well.

To answer the other part of

we show that if

is a mixed graph such that its underlying simplicial graph

does not contain neither

nor

, then

.
In particular,

cannot contain

or

in this case.

4.1
Subgroup Separability for T-RAAGs

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph. Then
T(\Gamma)
is subgroup separable if and only if the underlying simplicial graph
\overline{\Gamma}
does not contain
P_{4}
and
C_{4}
as induced subgraphs.

The theorem above is our main contribution on this article. Immediately, one has:

Corollary 4.8
.

T(\Gamma)
is subgroup separable if and only if

Proof of

.

One direction is provided by

.

For the other direction, let
\Gamma

If

\Gamma

It is enough to show
vGv^{-1}\subseteq G
for any

. This is clearly true, by definition of
G
, for any
v\in V\Gamma\setminus\{x\}
. On the other hand, for
x
, it is enough to show that for any
v\in V\Gamma\setminus\{x\}
one has
xvx^{-1}\in G
. We distinguish three cases:

(i)

If
[x,v]\in E\Gamma
, then
xv=vx
and so
xvx^{-1}=v
, which lies in
G
.

(ii)

If
\langle x,v]\in E\Gamma
, then
vxv=x
, hence
xvx^{-1}=v^{-1}
which lies in
G
because
v\in G
.

(iii)

If
[x,v\rangle\in E\Gamma
, then
xvx=v
, giving
xvx^{-1}=vx^{-2}
which lies in
G
because
v,x^{2}\in G
.

This completes the proof that
G
is normal in
T(\Gamma)
. One can see that

has index

in
T(\Gamma)
, by considering the quotient
T(\Gamma)/G=\langle x\,|\,x^{2}\rangle
which is a cyclic group of order
2
.

Now we can apply the Reidemeister-Schreier procedure to obtain a presentation for

.

Claim.
The group

has a T-RAAG presentation based on the graph

b_{1}
b_{n}
c_{l}
c_{1}

where

corresponds to

, and the dotted region is the same as the one in

.

Proof of Claim.
With reference to

, the set
T=\{1,x\}
is a Schreier transversal for

in

. Note that we obtain
S=\{x^{2},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n},c_{1},\ldots,c_{l}\}
as a generating set, because the other generators of the form

for

are products of elements of

and their inverses - with the same proof as for the previous claim.

Furthermore, the relations we obtain now between elements in

are as depicted in the graph

; the only change is that

Using

, it is enough to show that

Claim.
The group

5
Membership Problem in T-RAAGs

A
decision problem
is a YES – NO question on a countable infinite set of inputs.
A decision problem is called
decidable
if there is an algorithm which takes an input, terminates after a finite amount of time, and correctly answers the question by YES or NO. If such an algorithm does not exist, we refer to the decision problem as
undecidable
.

The word problem and the subgroup membership problem, introduced earlier, are both instances of decision problems. Natural generalizations of these classical problems include the submonoid membership problem and the rational subset membership problem, which we will define and discuss in more detail in
Section

5.2
.

5.1
Subgroup Membership Problem in T-RAAGs

The subgroup membership problem remains open for RAAGs, and hence it is open for T-RAAGs as well. However, subgroup separable T-RAAGs have a decidable subgroup membership problem
(
Mal’cev, 1958
)
.

On the other hand, subgroup non-separability does not imply undecidability of the subgroup membership problem: for example,
A(P_{4})
is not subgroup separable, yet has a decidable subgroup membership problem.

Proposition 5.1

(
Kapovich, Weidmann, and
Myasnikov, 2005
, Corollary 1.3
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a simplicial chordal graph. Then,
A(\Gamma)
has decidable subgroup membership problem.

In contrast, the RAAG
A(C_{4})=F_{2}\times F_{2}
contains a subgroup with undecidable membership
(
Mikhailova, 1966
)
; the graph
C_{4}
is not chordal.

RAAGs defined by chordal graphs are precisely the coherent ones
(
Droms, 1987
)
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Coherent RAAGs have decidable subgroup membership problem.

For a finite mixed graph
\Gamma
we will say that
\Gamma
is
chordal
if
\overline{\Gamma}
is chordal.

One also has that T-RAAGs based on chordal graphs are exactly the coherent ones (see [Blumer, Foniqi, Quadrelli]).

The main goal of this secion, which uses
(
Kapovich, Weidmann, and
Myasnikov, 2005
, Theorem 1.1)
, is to show

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed chordal graph. Then,
T(\Gamma)
has decidable subgroup membership problem.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma
be chordal. In the case when
\Gamma
is complete, the group
T(\Gamma)
is virtually free abelian; indeed, the subgroup generated by squares of the vertices of
\Gamma
is free abelian, and of finite index. Threfore,
T(\Gamma)
has decidable subgroup membership problem.

If
\Gamma
is not complete, then there are two proper subgraphs
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}=C
, with
C
complete. Here one has a splitting:

T(\Gamma)=T(\Gamma_{1})*_{T(C)}T(\Gamma_{2}).

As
C
is complete,
T(C)
is virtually free abelian.

Now, by the induction hypothesis, both
T(\Gamma_{1})
, and
T(\Gamma_{2})
are iterated amalgamated products or graphs of groups - with virtually abelian vertex groups (and edge groups).
Now the result follows from induction and
(
Kapovich, Weidmann, and
Myasnikov, 2005
, Theorem 1.1)
.
∎

5.2
Submonoids and Rational Subsets

Let
M
be a monoid generated by a finite set
A
, and let
\phi:A^{*}\to M
be the canonical homomorphism.

The
submonoid membership problem
asks: given a finite set
S\subseteq A^{*}
and a word
w\in A^{*}
, does
\phi(w)\in\phi(S^{*})
? This problem is well-defined for groups as well, using monoid generators
A\cup A^{-1}
.

A subset
L\subseteq M
is
rational
if
L=\phi(R)
for some regular language
R\subseteq A^{*}
. Rational subsets generalize finitely generated submonoids.

The
rational subset membership problem
asks: given a rational subset
R\subseteq M
and a word
w\in A^{*}
, is
\phi(w)\in R
? This applies to groups viewed as monoids.

Remark 5.4
.

Decidability of rational subset membership implies decidability of submonoid membership.

These problems are preserved under passing to submonoids. Notably, a group may have decidable submonoid membership but undecidable rational subset membership
(
Bodart, 2024
)
.

Theorem 5.5

(
Lohrey and Steinberg, 2008
)
.

A RAAG
A(\Gamma)
has decidable submonoid and rational subset membership problems if and only if
\Gamma
contains neither
P_{4}
nor
C_{4}
as induced subgraphs.

It is clear that if
\Gamma
is a mixed graph of non-elementary type, then
T(\Gamma)
has undecidable submonoid membership problem, as it contains
A(P_{4})
or
A(C_{4})
.

On the other hand, the situation is not clear for elementary mixed graphs. But, there is an interesting sub-family of elementary T-RAAGs for which the rational subset membership problem is decidable; the construction of this family will be given below and it is motivated from the proof of
Theorem 4.7
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a mixed graph. The
\Gamma
-cone

\nabla(\Gamma)
, with tip
w
, is a mixed graph with vertex set
V\sqcup\{w\}
, such that
\Gamma
is an induced subgraph, and every vertex
v
of
\Gamma
is connected to
w
with edges of the type
[v,w]
or
[v,w\rangle
.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be the class of mixed graphs of elementary type, constructed by starting with a single vertex, and iterating through disjoint unions and
\Gamma
-cones.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph belonging in
\mathcal{R}
.
T(\Gamma)
has decidable rational subset membership problem. Consequently,
T(\Gamma)
has decidable submonoid membership problem.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n=|V\Gamma|
.

If
n=1
, then
T(\Gamma)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, and the result is immediate.

For
n=2
, there are three possibilities:

(i)

T(\Gamma)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\ast\mathbb{Z}
, which has a decidable rational subset membership problem, as a free product of groups that do.

(ii)

T(\Gamma)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, which has a decidable rational subset membership problem, being a direct product of a group that does with
\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

T(\Gamma)\simeq\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
, which also has a decidable rational subset membership problem, as a finite extension of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Now assume that
n>2
and that the result holds for graphs in
\mathcal{R}
with less vertices.

If
\Gamma
is disconnected, then
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
, and so
T(\Gamma)=T(\Gamma_{1})\ast T(\Gamma_{2})
and the result holds by induction and the closere of decidability of rational subset membership prblem under free products.

So, assume that
\Gamma
is connected. By the construction of
\mathcal{R}
, we know that there is a vertex
w
connected with every other vertex
v\in V\Gamma\setminus\{w\}
, by edges of the type
[v,w]
or
[v,w\rangle
. Since graphs in
\mathcal{R}
are mixed elementary as well, we can apply the proof of
Theorem 4.7
. In particular, our graph looks like the one in

, without outgoing edges, hence it looks graphically as below:

Now, as in the proof of

On the other hand,

Development of an RFID sensor tag reading method in shielded areas based on an extended antenna
Authors: Danang Kumara Hadi, Zequn Song, Budi Rahmadya, Ran Sun, Shigeki Takeda
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this paper, a method for reading RFID sensor tags using an extended antenna within electromagnetically shielded environments is presented. This study focuses on three key applications: underground soil temperature measurement, temperature and moisture monitoring to prevent water leakage in water-cooled server computers, and body temperature monitoring. The proposed extended antenna system consists of a coaxial cable, a small coupling loop that connects an RFID sensor tag installed in a shielded area to the coaxial cable, and an external antenna. The experimental results demonstrate that the extended antenna system enables RFID-based sensing in electromagnetically shielded areas. The findings of this study contribute to the advancement of RFID sensor technologies that support a wide range of applications, including precision agriculture, data center management, and health care.

Introduction

Temperature and moisture measurements are critical in many important sectors, including agriculture, information technology, and health care. Although the need for accurate temperature1 and moisture2 measurements is high, technical challenges persist in many cases, particularly in hard-to-reach or electromagnetically shielded environments3,4. Environments such as subsurface soil5, enclosed spaces within electronic devices, and the human body6 can hinder the performance of conventional temperature and moisture measurement techniques. Physical barriers and conductive materials, such as water3 and metals4, can reduce the feasibility and reliability of measurements. In soil temperature measurement, soil conductivity7 and moisture can inhibit signal transmission8. Similarly, water-based cooling systems used in server computers, conductive liquids, and enclosed spaces within the device create challenging conditions for wireless monitoring of temperature and moisture4. Even in the context of human body temperature measurement, skin layers and body tissues can attenuate signals, thereby reducing the effectiveness of sensor systems.

The use of passive radio frequency identification (RFID)-based9 temperature10,11 and moisture sensors12 is particularly useful for the real-time monitoring of environmental conditions without the need for an internal electrical power source13,14, which makes them ideal for a variety of environments15,16. This technology is widely applied in logistics to monitor the temperature and moisture of sensitive products, such as food17 and medicine18, to efficiently control storage conditions19. In addition, the flexible and digital design of these sensors enables easy integration into various system applications.

However, the adaptability of passive RFID temperature and moisture sensors to shielded areas such as soil, computer servers, and human armpits remains limited. An example of the importance of monitoring and early prevention applications is Yashio city, Saitama Prefecture, Japan20, where underground water and sewage pipe leaks cause land subsidence, which results in ground surface subsidence and poses a safety hazard21.

Advanced techniques, such as 3D radar imaging, that are available in electromagnetically shielded environments have shown potential in the detection of subsurface pipeline leaks through dielectric changes22. However, such proactive systems in electromagnetically shielded environments are complex, power intensive, and labor intensive. In contrast, physical sensors installed underground23 offer low-power, scalable alternatives for real-time monitoring of soil moisture and temperature.

In this study, an extended antenna-based method for temperature and moisture monitoring using RFID sensor tags in shielded areas is presented. This method enables more stable and efficient RFID sensor tag readings under extreme conditions. This innovation provides new opportunities for developing smarter and more adaptive RFID-based temperature and moisture monitoring systems, with broad potential applications in precision agriculture, data center thermal management, and health care monitoring.

The focus of this study is on underground soil monitoring, server cooling systems, and body temperature sensing; the materials that surround wireless sensors can electromagnetically shield them, thus preventing the use of existing wireless sensing systems. Existing methods such as magnetic3, acoustic3, and ultrasonic4 methods are not battery-free, and fiber optic21 methods are expensive. RFID sensor-tag ICs with integrated temperature and proximity sensing capabilities are commercially available. These RFID sensor tags can be wirelessly powered by RFID readers, even underground, by using the proposed extended antenna systems. Therefore, in this method, RFID sensor tags in electromagnetically shielded areas are connected to an extended antenna installed outside the areas via a small coupling loop and a long coaxial cable for reliable sensing. The proposed extended antenna system connects an RFID sensor tag to an external antenna via a coaxial cable and coupling loop24–28, where the coupling loop and coaxial cable serve as the connection between the RFID sensor tag and the external antenna. The proposed method provides a design framework for the extended antenna system that is based on impedance matching between the external antenna and the load. This load consists of the coaxial cable, coupling loop, RFID sensor tag, and surrounding material. The design framework enables us to consider the material characteristics around the RFID sensor tag and the coupling efficiency between the coupling loop and the RFID sensor tag by using electromagnetic wave simulators.

Unlike existing methods that rely on battery-powered wireless systems or complex radar imaging, the proposed approach is low-power, low-cost, maintenance-free, compact, and feasible for real-time monitoring. Compared with wireless identification and sensing platform (WISP) architectures29,30, our method, which involves the coupling of a small loop and an antenna placed on the ground surface with a coaxial cable, is more feasible and easier to implement for deep underground monitoring23. To fabricate passive RFID sensors using external sensors, we need to assemble a special RFID tag equipped with energy harvesting and digital data interface functions, several external sensors, a microcontroller, and several cables to collect sensor data, as well as a power supply to provide electricity to the sensors on the circuit board. The circuit created must also be waterproof. In contrast, because the RFID sensor tag IC is equipped with built-in temperature and proximity sensor circuitry, our system is designed to use only passive components: an external antenna and one coaxial cable to enable radio wave propagation into the soil. This configuration also reduces concerns about water infiltration into the circuits installed underground because all the necessary circuits are integrated. Therefore, the proposed system is easy to implement and leads to reduced maintenance costs. However, the detuning effect caused by the materials that surround the RFID sensor tag is a major concern in our system, as the RFID sensor tag is installed underground, placed in close proximity to metallic objects, or situated between human anatomical structures, as shown in our demonstrations. To address these issues, the proposed extended antenna system must have an impedance match that is appropriate for individual target applications. Although impedance-matched designs are feasible and a design framework is presented in this paper, more pragmatic methods will be developed in future work.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section II, the principle of temperature and moisture monitoring using an RFID sensor tag integrated with an extended antenna is presented. In Section III, the experimental results are presented. In Section IV, the conclusions of this study are discussed.

Principle and purpose of monitoring temperature and moisture using an RFID sensor tag integrated with an extended antenna

In this study, an ultrahigh-frequency (UHF)-band RFID temperature and moisture sensor tag from Avery Dennison, specifically the Temperature Sensor Dogbone, which is equipped with an Axzon Magnus integrated circuit (IC), is used. This RFID sensor tag detects temperature, moisture, and proximity. The variable capacitance detects moisture and proximity and provides a sensor code. The built-in temperature circuit directly measures temperature, and the resulting value is encoded as a temperature code of C. The variable capacitance improves the impedance matching between the antenna and the RFID sensor tag IC, as moisture and proximity affect the input impedance of the RFID sensor tag antenna. The thermal conductivities of the soil and water must be considered to validate the experimentally measured temperature changes. The pouring of the cold water prepared in a consumer refrigerator confirmed whether the integrated temperature sensor in the RFID sensor tag could track the temperature changes. Body temperature monitoring

To ensure that experiments that involved human participants followed ethical procedures, this study was reviewed and approved by the Bioethics Committee of Ibaraki University. Body temperature measurement experiments involve direct contact with human subjects and require careful consideration of participant safety, data privacy, and informed consent procedures. The extended antenna system operates by being attached to a patient’s armpit to measure body temperature. Because the human body blocks signals from the RFID sensor tag, the extended antenna enables RFID sensor tag readings. An RFID reader emits radio waves to activate the temperature sensor on the IC of the RFID sensor tag. Once the temperature is detected, the data are transmitted back to the RFID reader. This technology enables automatic, battery-free temperature monitoring, thereby reducing the workload of medical personnel and increasing the efficiency and accuracy of patient temperature measurement.

For body temperature measurement, a Sampo S2 RFID reader was used for reference purposes only. In practice, this reader is too large for caregivers to carry out temperature measurements. Instead, the NordicID EXA31 reader, which is compact and can be held on the one hand, makes measuring body temperature easier for the caregiver.

In this study, a loop-type antenna was used to operate the extended antenna system in the vicinity of the human body. According to electromagnetic theory, parallel magnetic fields exist in the vicinity of conductive surfaces, such as the human body; therefore, a loop-type antenna placed perpendicular to the human body is a possible antenna. The antenna designs that were used consisted of three different loop configurations, all of which operated at the same frequency of 920 MHz. Because an external antenna must be installed near the human arm, a loop antenna positioned vertically relative to the arm is a reasonable choice. Initial readout experiments were conducted to evaluate the performance of the three designs. The three antenna designs, which feature a 1λ loop, a 1/2λ loop, and a 1/4 λ loop, are shown in Fig. 13.

Conclusion

In this paper, an extended antenna for an RFID sensor tag installed in an electromagnetically shielded area is presented. The extended antenna consists of a small loop for coupling to the RFID sensor tag, a coaxial cable, and an external antenna.

The experimental results demonstrate that the extended antenna enables RFID temperature measurement in electromagnetically shielded environments. In soil temperature and moisture monitoring, the extended antenna enables the measurement of temperatures crucial for precision agriculture. In GPU cooling system monitoring, this method facilitates early detection of water leakage and overheating, thus contributing to improved thermal management in data centers. Furthermore, in this study, a distributed temperature monitoring method that exploits the electromagnetic coupling effect of arrayed antennas was demonstrated. In body temperature monitoring, the RFID-based method enables continuous and wireless temperature tracking; thus, it has application potential in biomedical and health care systems. For the soil temperature measurements, a digital temperature sensor was used to validate the temperatures measured via the proposed method. The proposed RFID sensor system installed in the soil provided timely responses to poured water. A thermal camera was used to validate the temperature distribution on a computer server. A medical thermometer was used to validate the accuracy of the proposed system. These objective measurements validated the results obtained using the proposed method.

These findings contribute to the advancement of RFID-based sensor technology and pave the way for more efficient temperature and moisture monitoring systems in electromagnetically shielded areas. Future applications include underground infrastructure monitoring to help prevent serious land subsidence, such as in sewage and drinking water pipes.

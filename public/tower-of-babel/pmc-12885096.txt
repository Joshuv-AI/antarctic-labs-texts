Interpretation of drug trafficking patterns and utility of forensic intelligence
Authors: Diksha Thakur, Neeti Prakash Dubey, Rajvinder Singh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIllegal drug trafficking is a significantly notified socioeconomic and epidemiological practice threatening human civilization for the last century. It has a significant part in the crime rate of many countries. This review aims to provide a scientific and rational interpretation of the worldwide seizure of illicit drugs and examine the possibility of forensic intelligence to counter the drug menace including narco-terrorism. For this review, data were obtained from various World Drug Reports of the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime, digital databases, research journals, and other authentic virtual sources meticulously explored. It is observed that the dynamic modus operandi and coping mechanisms adopted for the supply of illicit drugs in the market are almost stable. The authors find that the scientific tool in the form of forensic intelligence will be more useful and reliable to counter the drug menace and its established links accurately.Keywords: illicit drugs, trafficking, chemical profiling, adulterants, drug abuse, polydrug abuse

Introduction

The consumption of drugs has been on the rise over the last 10 years. Recent and more precise statistics suggest that in 2022, ~292 million individuals (equating to 5.6% of the population between the ages 15 and 64) worldwide used a drug within the previous year. This figure represents a 20% increase compared to 10 years prior, a rise that can be attributed in part to population expansion [1]. Adolescents continue to use drugs at higher rates than previous generations and in greater quantities than adults. Although men make up the bulk of drug users, women use some drug kinds almost as frequently as men [2].

The trend of abuse has dynamically shifted from conventional drugs (heroin, cocaine, cannabis, morphine, charas, ganja) to designer drugs and the unintended use of pharmaceutical products. New psychoactive substances (NPSs), also known as “psychoactive designer drugs” or the “legal highs”, were the chemical substances synthesised by cooks (nonchemists/chemists) clandestinely. These were competent to produce effects similar to conventional drugs. Moreover, these novel drugs serve as a substance choice for the interdicted drugs [3]. NPS burgeoning is unforeseeable and lacks analytical data. Legislative measures exhibited poor regulation in low-income countries [4].

The authors acknowledge that the illegitimate misuse of drugs has been an obstacle for society, posing a serious threat to civilization [5, 6]. Health, crime, public safety, governance, and productivity are the primary domains that are drastically impacted by the drug abuse epidemic [6]. The costs of prevention and treatment, and healthcare expenses, considerably burden the economic condition of abusers. Furthermore, the indulgence of a significant population in malpractices dilutes the work capacity of the people, gross domestic production (GDP), and governance for the nation [7].

Drug trafficking has become the focal point among all forms of organized crime that is producing organized criminal gangs with a significant revenue stream [8]. The appeal of making monetary advantages through the distribution of illicit substances in the market leads to the transformation of young people into drug addicts. Taking unfair advantage of this appealing but perilous situation, drug dealers ensure that the demand for drug circulation in the market is maintained as high as possible, with higher prices and more incredible financial benefits. Illicit drug trade and narco-terrorism (the use of illicit drug trafficking proceeds to finance terrorist attacks) go hand in hand. In Afghanistan, renounced radical groups are estimated to regulate 85% of poppy cultivation [8]. Personal reasons like stress, miserable family conditions, desire to deviate from an unchanging environment, deprivation of discipline or moral values, and drive to experience a thrill or pleasurable effect allure youngsters towards maladaptive practices [9]. During the COVID-19 pandemic, increased poverty and unemployment (114 million people unemployed) aggravated drug consumption as many turned to illegal crop cultivation to supplement their income [10].

Forensic drug analysis renders inputs in cases that involve the acquisition, production or manufacturing, and trafficking of a questioned illicit drug. Primarily, it plays an integral part in discovering many facts, i.e., identifying the chemical substance and ingredients present, analysing the presence and quantity of unlawful chemical ingredients, the relation between two drug samples, and establishing a significant correlation between them. Furthermore, it may also provide vital information about the purveyor and user, discerning the sample’s smuggling route, narrowing down its geographical origin/laboratory source, determining its method of production/manufacturing (natural, semi-synthetic, and synthetic), and providing scientific inputs during testimonial procedures in the court of law on several occasions [11].

This comprehensive review focuses on the rational explanation of the trends in global drug trafficking patterns. This review amalgamates the scientific study conducted to trace the origin of drugs that play a significant role in providing tactical and strategic intelligence to counter illicit trafficking. Also, the role and the need for chemical profiling of illicit drug samples to track the geographical origin and help dismantle trafficking networks are critically discussed. For this review, data were obtained from various World Drug Reports of The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime, digital databases, research journals, and other authentic virtual sources meticulously explored.

Pattern of the illegitimate drug trade

Over a decade, variation in the illicit drug market has been evident. In 2015, opioids (heroin, pharmaceutical opioids, fentanyl) were the predominantly abused drugs in the USA. According to the World Drug Report 2017, plant-based Cannabis sativa L. products were predominantly abused, while NPSs and cocaine trafficking channels were booming globally. Synthetic cannabinoids were utilized infrequently due to pessimistic after-effects. Increased seizures of amphetamine-like stimulants (ATSs) and opioids have also been reported. The reason behind the active proliferation of cocaine channels is the sudden rise in cultivation in Colombia (~30% elevations from 2013 to 2015) after an unvarying growth phase. North America and Europe were the two biggest markets for accessing cocaine illicitly. Infiltration of cocaine in significant quantities is evident in Asian countries. In the NPS strata, among ATSs, methamphetamine (>50% of total ATSs) was seized in bulk quantity for a half-decade before 2015. While the market has expanded profoundly into Southeast and Central-Eastern Asia, the abuse of amphetamine remained restricted to the Middle East, Western, and Central Europe, with vast territories in Southwest Asia and Central America [12]. The drought in Afghanistan significantly influenced the opium market in 2018 (a 25% decrease in production) (which generates more than one-third of global opium). Despite the inevitable environmental hazard, opioid use persisted. Colombian cocaine (which contributes to 70% of global cocaine) output reached new highs (25% elevation in annual production). The high interception of cocaine consignments indicates that trafficking is increasingly directed towards affluent areas in Asian and West African countries. NPS abuse pattern, revealed an increased number of NPS substances surfaced, with stimulants standing first on the list, followed by fentanyl analogues (synthetic opioids) and hallucinogens. Methamphetamine patterns in Southeast Asia are a rising concern for crystalline form with the absence of trafficking intelligence. Ketamine, lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), and phencyclidine (PCP) were the most often used hallucinogens in America; also, dimethyltryptamine use has been on the rise since 2016. However, drug trafficking on the darknet was initially reduced following the takedowns of Alpha Bay, Hansa, and Wall Street Market (the world’s most prominent markets) in 2017 and 2019 [15]. Impact of COVID-19 on the global drug market

Since 2020, governments around the world have implied countermeasures to control the unprecedented health emergency caused by COVID-19 spread, which has influenced the citizenry, subregional movement limitations, mental/physical health, and people’s socioeconomic status [16, 17]. Drug production/manufacture, trade, modes of transportation, modus operandi, and healthcare facilities have become more dynamic due to the disparate effects of implied policies worldwide for the initial phase of 2020. Afterwards (onset of 2021), the illicit market became resilient in dynamics, stabilizing the overall market. The most noticeable change in the abuse trend was a shift away from more expensive drugs (cocaine, LSD, heroin) and towards more affordable cannabis and medicinal medications (benzodiazepines, tramadol, barbiturates). The factors responsible for the outcome were the closure of bars/clubs or other recreational/social sites and the economic crisis. The pandemic lockdown aggravated the situation by censuring healthcare facilities for drug addicts. According to the global evaluation, more than half of the 130 reported countries disrupted their medical facilities for drug addicts. The exacerbated mental or other health adversities, poverty divide, inequalities, and deprivation of socioeconomic opportunities contributed to a hike in drug cultivation-based jobs, production, and trafficking [18].

Due to cultivators’ coping mechanisms (employment of family and local workforce in harvesting), opium production has had a limited influence. Coca leaf supply remains intact, while its other products were clamped down in the initial phase. Manufactured synthetic drugs have surpassed in Southeast Asia, while the deprivation of precursor chemicals in Europe, Oceania, and North America escalated the price. The reason behind this surpass is the existence of the chemical or pharma industry for synthesis and unlimited access to the precursor or pre-precursor chemicals in Asian regions [19].

The foremost impact of curfews imposed was evident on the drug retailers (traffickers). Due to ceased mobility, border surveillance and deployment of law enforcement agencies in subregions reduced the number and quantum of drug seizures until half of 2020. Stringent administration induced high risk on transportation, and short supply raised the prices. However, the shortfall for the drug was different by region (heroin in Europe and methamphetamine in North America). An evolved modus operandi was witnessed for the trade. Maritime or waterways were preferred to bypass land restrictions; landways were preferred to circumvent airways shutdown, private aircraft were employed to overcome commercial aviation restrictions, whereas in some regions, trafficking through the mail was reported. Moreover, technology upgradation was witnessed during COVID-19 as the hydroponic (plant cultivation based on mineral nutrients without soil) technique was used for the in-home cultivation of “cannabis” for personal consumption and sale by information technology graduates [20].

As evident from this technical analysis, dynamic modus operandi stabilized the market in the later pandemic stage. Besides this, the effect witnessed at the localized level varies for different drugs and regions. For instance, cocaine was often smuggled by sea as aviation slowed down, while heroin was transported via land in legal cargoes. In addition, NPS supply was disrupted in countries that relied on airways and were distant from manufacturing hubs. A discussion of variations at subregions across the globe is beyond the scope, but assuredly, a comprehensive dynamic pattern has been meticulously examined.

Tracing the origin of the drug: a suggestive scientific tool

Forensic drug profiling to identify all the unique characteristics of an illicit drug sample and the maintenance of a database including all the fine information is crucial to combating the drug epidemic. Presently, drug testing laboratories do not emphasize comprehensive drug characterization [11]. However, it is crucial to combat trafficking networks and possibly play a vital role to counter narco-menace.

The universally accepted fact is that every offence has a unique way of operation that can serve as the ground to distinguish between purveyors/offenders. Recognizing individuality/peculiarities (major/minor alkaloids in plants from which the drug was produced, intraspecies variation in alkaloid content, manufactured by-products, postmanufacturing modifications, the addition of adulterants (cutting agents, diluents, residual solvents, etc.)), scientifically termed as “chemical signature profiling”, is an essential aspect of forensic intelligence [21]. Different alkaloids and their changing concentrations can be used to distinguish between plant species growing in various geographical regions. For example, the geographical origin of the heroin can be determined by using diacetylmorphine, codeine, morphine, 6-monoacetylmorphine, 3-monoacetylmorphine, acetyl codeine, noscapine, and papaverine, calculating the proportion of these compounds to the whole morphine, and contrasting these results with data from thousands of genuine samples with known origins. Other corroborative information, like producing obscured solvents and by-products, can act as a “tie-breaker” to distinguish between two geographical sources. Likewise for cocaine, “tropane, truxillines, occluded solvents, and stable isotope ratios of nitrogen and carbon” are significant markers for evaluating geographical origin.

Comprehensive forensic intelligence can establish the link between the geographical origin and manufacturing source for natural or semisynthetic drugs and synthetic drugs, respectively [22–25]. Such authentic inputs are used in tactical and strategic operations executed by law enforcement bodies against drug trafficking/conspiring networks. Hence, chemical profiling is powerful to disrupt trafficking networks [26]. Ruling out the trafficking routes globally will reduce the utmost impact, i.e., loss of human life and its concurrent adversities (as discussed above). The authors highlight that drug profiling-based research studies are limited and that current studies often fail to provide all physical/chemical signatures for the specific drug sample under study, although these are crucially needed to establish a reliable link between the geographical source/clandestine sources, purveyors, trafficking channel, and the target site/victim [27, 28]. As per the authors’ experience, the reasons behind limited research studies are strict regulatory constraints for collecting research samples, limited access to analytical facilities, and challenges in transporting samples across regions; thus, few academic researchers pursue controlled drug-related research. Ideally, chemical profiling-based research studies must be conducted over a long span of time. The greater the sample size and time span, the more generalizable the results will be. All the minute details, including packaging, site of seizure, date and time, modus operandi, and physical characteristics, must be documented. Furthermore, microscopic examination, wherever possible, and confirmatory examination using different instrumental analyses should be performed to discern the relevant data. This has been evident in a study conducted in 2016 that such a comprehensive finding aids in delineating illegitimate drug trafficking networks [26, 27]. Nevertheless, such comprehensive studies are limited. Moreover, Bacillus anthracis (anthrax), B. cereus, Clostridium botulinum, C. novyi, C. sordellii, and Acinetobacter are the reported bacteria that may contaminate illicit drug samples [37]. Hence, a forensic expert must be vigilant during the examination of the samples as these may serve as a significant bioindicator to establish a link.

Then, this forensic intelligence must be contrasted, complemented, and combined with other emerging sources like criminological studies and information received from police or deployed spy agents. Moreover, offender details relevant to law enforcement agencies must be stored along with the previously mentioned information in a database to track the activation or deactivation of trafficking purveyors, as the offender tends to undergo a dormant stage after completing the assigned illegitimate task. Maintaining an active database can aid in tracing its activating pattern/modus operandi. Moreover, a forensic expert must make shrewd decisions regarding the selection of the appropriate technique for the examination of the sample. So, the authors discuss the techniques that can be used to determine the unique chemical details in the confiscated sample in the upcoming section.

Analytical techniques for discerning chemical signatures

The choice of analytical method depends on the nature of the analyte (natural, semisynthetic, or synthetic), types of adulterants, impurities, or by-products produced during the chemical processing of the parent substance. In the case of seized drugs, a forensic analyst must look for major alkaloids, minor alkaloids, manufacturing by-products, elemental impurities, and occluded solvents. Even today, drug profiling still relies heavily on GC-MS impurity profiling in most forensic laboratories, while very few have dedicated and thorough drug profiling procedures [38]. Capillary electrophoresis, a rapid and user-friendly tool, is suggested by the United States Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) and National Measurement Institute in Australia (NMIA) for major alkaloids [39]. Ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) can also be used for alkaloid determination [40]. Minor alkaloids or occluded solvents can be analysed using headspace–gas chromatography (HS-GC) [41]. In order to quantify the stable isotopes of light elements, ATS profiling systems have adopted methods like isotope ratio mass spectrometry (IRMS) [42]. A TaqMan real-time PCR-based test and DNA barcoding were employed in a study to identify plant species that contained ephedrine alkaloids [43]. High-performance liquid chromatography–diode array detection (HPLC-DAD) was utilized to profile the organic impurities in cocaine [44]. In a study, portable screen-printed carbon electrodes (SPCEs) with electropolymerized molecularly imprinted polymers (e-MIPs) were used for benzocaine determination [45]. Direct analysis in real-time mass spectrometry (DART-MS) in hyphenation with chromatographic techniques can serve as a powerful tool to discern peculiarities in the sample [46]. For elemental impurities present, inductively coupled plasma–mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) and inductively coupled plasma–atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES) can serve as powerful tools [38]. The creation of handheld, portable Raman spectrometers is a remarkable development for illicit drugs and cutting agent detection in the field. Infrared-based spectroscopy, specifically attenuated total reflection–Fourier transform infrared (ATR-FTIR), is also on trend [22, 32]. In addition, X-ray diffractometry can be potentially used for data generation [47]. Conclusively, suitable analytical methods may be adopted as per utility and feasibility to generate chemical signatures authentically.

Discussion

The logical interpretation of global patterns of drug trafficking and its abuse is summarized below:

ATSs

ATS drugs like ecstasy are popular among the elite section of people worldwide. Due to its immediate and prolonged euphoric effect, it is the choice of drugs among youngsters in metropolitan cities, bars, clubs, and tourist places. In the NPS strata, among ATSs, methamphetamine (>50% of total ATSs) for a half-decade before 2015 was seized in bulk quantity. From 2015 to 2019, ATS abuse was progressive in nature [48]. Because ATS is expensive, seizures tend to occur more frequently in high-income or upper-middle-income nations like the USA, Mexico, and the UK (Tables 1 and 2). Among Asian countries, Saudi Arabia and Thailand stand at the top of the queue due to ongoing tourist activities and a booming economy. People can afford expensive pills in these countries. The same can also be deduced from the fact that for 2020, Myanmar, followed by Thailand and Saudi Arabia, stands topmost in the ATS seizure [48]. Meanwhile, a study found that students utilize it to extend their sitting times and abuse it as a performance-enhancing drug by sports athletes [49].

Precursor chemical

Raw chemicals like ephedrine, pseudoephedrine, and pharmaceutical medicines, and other chemicals can be utilized illegitimately in synthesizing drugs. It is evident from Tables 1 and 2 that precursor seizure occurred in low- or middle-economic countries, those in proximity to the countries where ATS were confiscated. For instance, Peru in South America, a middle-economic country proximal to the USA (with maximum ATS seizure), stands first in the precursor seizure category. Among Asian countries, Pakistan and Afghanistan together share a considerable proportion of precursor seizures that make Asia stand at the second position after South America. These countries sharing a border with India are impacted as precursors are seized in Indian territories, too. Similarly, Nigeria in Southern Africa (a low-income country) follows a similar trend. Besides this, Mexico (upper-middle economic country) is popular for both the precursor and ATS seizure. Data obtained about India (0.27% contributor in global ATS seizure) discerned that ATSs are created illegally in Maharashtra and Gujarat and illegally imported from Myanmar across the borders of the northeastern region of India. ATS is illegally transported from India to Thailand (third in ATS seizure), Malaysia, and Australia (the prosperous countries). Apparently, the controlled substances are trafficked through diverse intra- and interstate/country routes; nevertheless, the authentic geographical origin is not determined. Therefore, it can be concluded that precursors are abundantly found in countries with vulnerable economies and democratic instability, hence indicating the fact that these nations rely on the illegitimate trafficking of precursors to neighbouring countries and the clandestine manufacture of drugs using the precursor to enhance their economy.

Cocaine

Like ATSs, cocaine is a choice of drug for rich people worldwide. The maximum seizure of cocaine is in Colombia, Brazil, Peru, and Bolivia, as South America is the hub cultivation site of coca plants. South American countries are the main exporters of cocaine across all continents, including Asia. The reason behind this is that the Erythroxylum coca species (var. coca) and E. novogranatense species (var. novogranatense and var. truxillense) with the highest alkaloid content are grown predominantly in South America, attributable to the diverse climatic conditions, geographical terrain, and cultivation patterns/techniques present there [50]. In light of this, it can be said that the coca quadrilateral, which consists of Bolivia, Brazil, Peru, and Colombia, is the active South American location responsible for the manufacture of cream cocaine (richest in alkaloid content). Hence, the demand for cream cocaine is high in this region. Therefore, this coca quadrilateral and the Andean nations contribute significantly to large illicit exports, principally to Europe and America, with relatively less significant flows of cocaine going to South Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Southwest Asia. According to the NCB data for 2018, cocaine is not produced locally. Its primary sources are South America, Africa (trafficking via Indian-based African nationals), and some parts of North America, from where it is transported to the Middle East (Dubai) and then into India via the Mumbai Seaport and Indira Gandhi International Airport in New Delhi. Forms seized may vary from dry powder to liquid cocaine [51]. This shows that the networks, modes, and routes utilized by criminals are still evolving and becoming more efficient. The voluminous seizure indicates the expansion of illegitimate cocaine cultivation and trade. This trend will be accelerated and catalysed by the increasing Western globalization and affluent population in some regions.

Cannabis products

For all cannabis forms, Brazil, Mexico, the USA, Egypt, and South Africa are the countries associated with the confiscation of illegitimate cannabis-based products from 2016 to 2019. The World Drug Report from the UNODC elucidated that India stands first and precedes Brazil and Colombia for cannabis herb or marijuana; while for hashish, Spain, Morocco, Afghanistan, and Pakistan are the top countries [8, 18]. This indicates that it is readily misused in almost all the major continents like Africa, America, and Asia, indicating widespread cultivation practices of the C. sativa. One reason is that cannabis grows in the wild due to favourable climatic conditions. The other reason behind the proliferation of cultivation practices across various continents is the liberalization of legislative policies. For example, Canada, South Africa, Malta, Mexico, Georgia, Thailand, Uruguay, and a few states of Colombia permit cannabis use for recreational purposes. In the Netherlands, cannabis is available in legal coffee shops [52]. Additionally, countries like Australia, Brazil, Barbados, Chile, Canada, Colombia, Costa Rica, Cyprus, Croatia, Norway, Czech Republic, Ecuador, Denmark, Georgia, Finland, Germany, Ireland, Vanuatu, Mexico, Zimbabwe, Greece, Italy, Jamaica, Israel, Luxembourg, Lebanon, Malawi, Sri Lanka, Malta, the Netherlands, New Zealand, North Macedonia, Norway, Panama, Peru, Poland, Portugal, Rwanda, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, the UK, San Marino, Switzerland, South Africa, Thailand, Uruguay, and Zambia legalized cannabis for medical purpose. Besides this, cannabis products are extensively utilized in nutraceuticals, food and beverages, and cosmetics industries [53]. Also, industrialists are infusing cannabis content like terpenes and other alkaloids into edible products to introduce novel products into the market for financial gains. Hence, the cultivation market is rapidly flourishing as countries are adopting the concept of decriminalizing the use of cannabis products. While certain countries, including India, adhere to the concept of criminalizing cannabis use for unintended purposes, stringent penal provisions exist for illegitimate use. As the liberalization of laws is not uniform in the states of different countries, seizures occurred predominantly in those regions of countries where the substance is regulated (Tables 1 and 2).

Opium and opioids

As per UNODC 2020 data, opium seizure has increased each successive year from 2016 to 2020, with Iran, Pakistan, Myanmar, Afghanistan, and India being the top five countries involved in confiscating illegitimate opium [54]. The reason is that the former countries are an integral part of a well-known hub for opium cultivation, i.e., the Golden Crescent and Golden Triangle, and are also responsible for illegitimate trade. The impact of cultivation hubs on either side of India is that opium is profusely infused into India through various illegitimate channels (land/waterways), as Pakistan, Afghanistan, and Myanmar share a long porous border with India. Within India, opium trafficking from Rajasthan (western India), Uttar Pradesh (middle north), and Madhya Pradesh (middle India) is the main route of road smuggling to other states. The “northern” route of opium trafficking via Central Asia, from Afghanistan to Russia, is also an active infiltration channel [13]. Besides this, NCB also reported that India stands first in the series of opioid abuse with 2.06%, twice the Asian average and thrice the world average in opioid abuse [51]. The reason is the well-established chemical and pharmacy industry in India. Furthermore, India is a major exporter of pharmaceutical opioids like tramadol, pointing towards the ascending nonmedical use of the drug. This trend indicated that tramadol is probably clandestinely manufactured or illegitimately procured from pharmaceuticals in Southern Asia and trafficked to the Middle East and Africa as the shortfall of potent painkillers prevails in subregions [13]. Also evident from Table 1, countries like Egypt, Guatemala, Colombia, Iran, and India are those with a lower-middle, middle, or upper-middle economy, or with a wide economic divide. Decisively, opioid trafficking occurs in countries with poor economies or individuals belonging to poor economic strata, as pharmaceutical opioids are relatively inexpensive drugs. While the source of opioid imports will be the countries where the precursors are available or chemical/pharmaceutical industries are well established. Identification of the various trafficking channels is necessary.

For heroin, Balkan routes are extremely popular routes to smuggle from Afghanistan to Europe’s central-western region and Canada, while the USA imports heroin from Mexico. Moreover, along with the Balkan routes, the Caucasus countries’ route is an alternative to the Balkan route, and trafficking from Azerbaijan, Georgia, and Armenia through the Black Sea to Romania and Ukraine also takes place [13].

Inhalants, sedatives, and tranquilizers

Inhalants (solvents, aerosols, nitrates, anaesthetics, adhesives) are widely abused and are considered the gateway to other hard drugs like cocaine, heroin, ATS, etc. [55]. The availability, affordability, and lack of legal restrictions on drugs that have the potential to be abused, such as thinners, sprays, Fevicol glue, correction fluid, shoe polish, and gasoline products (kerosene, petrol), among others, contribute to their abuse. According to a survey, street children in low-income nations frequently abuse inexpensive inhalants, while wealthy nations frequently utilize expensive illicit drugs [56]. Nevertheless, it is evident from Table 1 that maximum seizure is reported in Thailand, followed by Argentina and Spain, all with emerging upper-middle economies, hence pointing towards its misuse in nations rapidly developing or with booming economies. The author suggests conducting a wide-scale survey to discern the socio-demographic details of these countries. Glancing at the abusive pattern at the regional level, it elucidated that the major proportion of inhalant abuse is more prevalent in the Asian continent than in America and the European belt.

Sedatives and tranquilizers that primarily include benzodiazepines or barbiturates are majorly seized in India (>50% of reported global seizures). The main cause is India’s well-established and booming chemical and pharmacy sectors, which generate large amounts of pharmaceutical drugs that are then trafficked via illegitimate ways. Following India, South Africa, the USA, Nigeria, and Canada each contributed relative to India a minor portion of the overall seizure. Due to India’s well-established pharmaceutical industry, there is a strong likelihood that these types of medicines or their chemical ingredients (precursors) will be illegally transported from India to other nations engaged in seizures.

Nexus of crop cultivation, precursor chemicals, and illicit drug production

The demand for drug misuse is fulfilled either from the cultivation of the crop or from clandestine manufacture using precursor chemicals (Figure 2). This chain of illicit production and manufacturing needs to be disrupted to prevent illegitimate misuse of drugs. Different borders have various drug trafficking patterns, which vary depending on the nature and geography of the border and economy, as well as the supply, demand, and manufacturing of drugs. For example, precursor chemical seizures occurred in the low-middle economy region, which is widely accessible through well-established pharmaceutical or chemical industries. Voluminous seizure makes it evident that these precursors are processed into final products and exported to higher economic regions for monetary gains. Also, clandestine manufacture is covertly taking place in countries with fragile governance and poor economy; hence, such countries are serving as a hub/source for manufacturing synthetic and semisynthetic drugs and their trafficking. Moreover, the interception of Cannabis in the regions near the cultivation zone indicates inefficient surveillance of the crop and is the major exporter to noncultivating regions. Major cultivating regions identified for heroin are Southeast Asia mainly (Thailand, Vietnam, Myanmar), Southwest Asia mainly (Afghanistan, Iran, Pakistan), South America (particularly Colombia), and Mexico, and for cocaine, Mexico, Colombia, and Peru. Geographical tracing of the synthesized drug is difficult because precursors are omnipresent and can be processed anytime, anywhere, into final products. It is well established that in Asia, countries with vulnerable economies like Afghanistan and Pakistan have nurtured radical groups under the political umbrella of the state. Surplus seizure of precursor chemicals in such countries indicates their reliance on the clandestine manufacture of drugs and their export for their economy and sustaining radical groups. Consequently, neighbouring countries like India are impacted as it is evident that methamphetamine and its precursor were seized in some states with unidentified sources and clandestinely processed in final products. Law enforcement agencies need to tab the major cultivating areas and illegitimate transport via various routes.

Drug triangle: cultivation and precursors are the gateways for drug production.

Emergence of NPS and precursor misuse

Comprehensively, factors that determine the availability of the drug in a specific geographical region are its ongoing trend of abuse and its addiction/dependence, accessibility, economic parameters, surveillance of law enforcement organizations, and the efficiency of regulatory policy implementation at the ground level to scrutinize controlled substances. As is evident, expensive drug abuse is more predominantly seized in high-income countries or in certain strata of elite people in low/middle-economic countries, globally. While the trend of drug use for inexpensive pharmaceuticals, inhalants, and solvents is attributable to vulnerable socioeconomic aspects; hence, abuse is prevalent in low/middle-economic countries. Due to the well-established chemical industry in Asian countries like India, trafficking takes place in regions with less availability of products. Hence, the production of pharmaceutical products and their availability in the market need to be monitored. Interdiction of the substance impacted the pattern of abuse. Ascertained from NPS burgeoning, there has been a progressive transition from traditional drugs to designer drug misuse. The offenders are aware that misuse of novel compounds or uncontrolled substances will provide an advantage to avoid facing legal ramifications; this is how they operate deftly. Specifically, the development of clandestine sites for manufacturing designer drugs is to circumvent the prospect of confiscation for controlled substance illicit use. Based on technical facts, the gist is that countries must balance access to medical drugs/chemical compounds that could be abused or exploited as a precursor/pre-precursor chemical or rendered in the clandestine manufacture of NPS. Besides this, interception of ayurvedic medicines indicated a probable misuse, hence offering a novel avenue for the forensic chemist to profile the abusive ingredient present in them. Chemical substances capable of being used as precursor chemicals must be anticipated to deal with unforeseen circumstances.

Dynamic trafficking routes and evolving modus operandi

A common trafficking route cannot be ascertained. The choice of route depends upon the risk of interception. Hence, in India, the waterways were preferred as they are difficult to monitor due to vast surface areas, followed by porous land areas than heavily patrolled pathways. Furthermore, route selection depends on the distance between the prime site and the target. For instance, cocaine is typically transported across continents by air, with airways being the preferred route from America to Asia. At the regional level, however, traffickers often rely on land routes using personal vehicles, as drugs can be easily concealed, larger quantities can be carried, and inspections are usually minimal unless traffic regulations are violated or prior intelligence has been received. Networks of drug traffickers rely on the legitimate commerce in which drugs are disguised. The authors press on the fact that there was no real-time reduction in the quantum of the confiscated samples during COVID-19; nevertheless, the initial trimester has been the effective one attributable to stringent regulation. Apparently witnessed, during the pandemic’s initial phase, certain restrictions on social activities, recreational sites (clubs/bars), and border surveillance have increased the interception rate. Also, the perpetrators diverged the heavily patrolled trafficking routes. Decisively, at times regulations must be implied to cut down supply chains. Human carriers are one of the ways to transport the drug. Juveniles are purposely deployed for this work as children are easy to persuade, and lax legislation can be used to circumvent penal provisions. Additionally, it emphasizes the necessity for law enforcement organizations to speed up and evolve their operations, given that offenders tend to use more sophisticated methods of operation (rendering mail facilities, drones). Additionally, the use of cryptocurrencies and the darknet has made it difficult to locate the source of trafficking and its channels. So, the viable solution is that the dynamic transit routes must be critically analysed, and novel routes must be vigilant. Executive forces must be trained efficiently to combat evolved modus operandi. Cyber experts’ skills must be enhanced to decode the darknet-associated trade. Moreover, coordinated efforts from forensics and other enforcement agencies across the regional, national, and international territories is the indispensable key.

Policy awareness, employment, and transparency as tools against drug misuse

Low-income countries or vulnerable socioeconomic strata of society were more susceptible to drug use–induced adversities. Unemployment is attributable to the employment of staffing in drug crop cultivation–related practices. However, legislation has ensured several policies to empower civilization; still, enlightenment regarding them is required [4]. Inadequate resources to tackle money laundering and drug smuggling in lower economic countries make them an appropriate site for manufacturing drugs illegitimately. The resilient drug market is ascribable to corruption and fragile governance. According to the source, corruption induces instability by dodging foreign investment and widening the economic divide. Such conditions allure terrorist infiltration and money laundering activities [8, 10]. What is foremost required at the ground level to tackle the situation is the awareness among the citizenry about existing policies. Moreover, legislation must work toward narrowing the economic divide. Moreover, employment opportunities must be generated so that citizens can find their means of livelihood without indulging in malicious activities. Transparency in the executive branch is critical at this time.

Health risks and awareness gaps in NPS and drug misuse

Unawareness of the lethality of NPS products is a significant concern. Bloodborne diseases were more likely than other diseases to spread in communities. This is due to the use of common syringes among addicts or indulgence in sexual activity with an infected person or multiple partners. With limited health facilities or resources, it is an uphill battle to stem the growing smuggling network and cure addicts. Furthermore, the community must be engaged in reducing illness burdens through effective prevention and cures. A campaign of awareness must be planned for this. Drug misuse needs to be covered in the educational policies’ curricula so that young people might be roused from their misapprehension. Furthermore, deceptive advertising must be eliminated to raise knowledge about the lethality of narcotics and dispel illusions about their safer usage.

Chemical signatures and profiling approaches to disrupt illicit drug markets

The primary method for eradicating the burgeoning drug market is to track down the source and dismantle the sources and smuggling networks using chemical profiling. Despite this well-known phenomenon and advances in analytical chemistry, limited data exist about the indigenous drug samples that have saturated the market. According to a literature source, forensic scientists testifying in criminal courts focus solely on drug alkaloid content, ignoring other relevant evidence (signatures) [20]. In 1993, heroin was profiled by different chemical makers that established a reliable link between the wholesalers and retailers [57]. A few research studies that have completely profiled or characterized chemical markers present in the samples are summarized in Table 3. To curb the rising concern, relevant and on-time forensic chemical inputs were of paramount significance to law enforcement bodies. To accomplish this, advanced instrumental tools such as stable IRMS, embracing evolved and optimized methods for drug analysis, critical analysis of profiling data and its trend, database maintenance (a few countries have individual drug monitoring systems and profiling databases), keeping abreast of the novel trafficking patterns, and collaboratively participating in related research programs were required [58]. Furthermore, scientific research should concentrate on profiling all maximum chemical markers (alkaloid, chiral analysis, by/intermediate products/adulterants, IRMS, and elemental analysis) in order to create a dataset for a sample set with increased authenticity [59]. As evident from Table 3, limited chemical marker profiles were evaluated. To improve the generalizability of study findings at the ground level, the research gaps listed in Table 3 must be addressed. The approach of comprehensive profiling must be adopted.

Polydrug abuse and adulteration practices in illicit drug markets

The most common pattern of abuse described in the literature is polydrug abuse, in which several drugs are administered concurrently to produce a high level of hit or pleasurable effect [60]. Also, the concentration of the pure drug is diluted by adding chemical substances with similar chemical properties to mask the quantity of the original drug sample. For example, in some countries, the cocaine hydrochloride (95% purity) is stepped on or cut with adulterants like mannitol, sugars, dextrose, inositol, lactose, and local anaesthetics like lidocaine. A combination of cocaine and caffeine was sold as a brain tonic in some countries. Before disseminating it into the local streets, it is usually combined with sedatives (lorazepam, diazepam), heroin, marijuana, and alcoholic beverages [61], while another study revealed that phenacetin, levamisole, hydroxyzine, caffeine, and diltiazem are the adulterants profile in cocaine samples [28]. For heroin samples, paracetamol and caffeine are predominantly reported adulterants and exhibit a more stable cutting pattern than cocaine [27]. Some studies have reported the use of fentanyl with other illicit narcotics and diphenhydramine, a cutting agent for heroin [6]. The polydrug cocktail may contain a drug to produce a synergistic pharmacological effect, or a drug may be replaced by another due to the latter’s accessibility and low cost. Such an abusive pattern sometimes exhibits toxicity or fatality [7]. Talking about the manner of death (suicidal, accidental, homicide), the focal area lies on the mechanism that contributed to the mortality, i.e., either the synergistic impact of the concurrent drugs in regulated concentration or actually ingesting the high concentration. The primary motivation for their insertion appears to be to maximize profits at the expense of consumers’ health.

Role of comprehensive profiling in intelligence gathering and public health policy

Chemical profiling of drug samples is a relevant tool to decipher the illicit market’s structure, organization, and modus operandi. For each specimen, data on its physical characteristics (packaging and appearance), chemical characteristics (main and minor alkaloids, purity, cutting agents, residual solvents, chemical group), and circumstantial facts (mass of drug, location and time of seizure, criminal case number) must be compiled in a separate dedicated database. The authors aimed to resurface the overlooked relevant aspect of forensic research to overcome the research gaps in the existing literature by converging attention on the comprehensive investigation of excipients in illicit drug samples. The authors acknowledge the fact that profiling information of samples is limited and scattered; hence, collaboration and a unified system must be envisioned to improve knowledge and provide informed inputs for intelligence, monitoring, regulation, and framing of harm reduction or health policies. This aspect of scientific study is significant for intelligence and framing health policy. Moreover, in order to identify the cause of the intoxications (overdose or synergistic adverse effect) and choose the most effective therapeutic course, it is significant to unfold the dynamics of abuse and come to the conclusion that the ongoing development of useful analytical tools is necessary.

Current and emerging analytical techniques for illicit drug detection

Current and future trends for advanced analytical techniques of illicit drug samples: a crucial task for law enforcement organizations and forensic drug labs is the scientific examination of questioned drug material found in the field. Screening, identification, and/or quantification procedures are included in the analysis, depending on the purpose and scope of the test. The examination includes on-field detection of the questioned substance and further its detailed examination in chemical laboratories. For on-filed/nonlaboratory detection, various portable and inexpensive tools for preliminary screening with low discriminating power like colour tests, and immunoassays are available. Sensitive, sophisticated, and confirmatory analysis can be performed using miniaturized gas chromatography-mass during crime scene investigation. Nevertheless, GC-MS is a destructive technique and needs sample preparation. Other than these techniques, handheld FTIR spectrometers are rapid, nondestructive, and need no sample processing but allow only qualitative analysis of the substance. A portable Raman analyser with spatially offset Raman spectroscopy offers an advantage in detecting sealed/ packed chemical substance. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy and X-ray diffractometry aid in providing structural details of the chemical substance. Nowadays, ion mobility spectroscopy (IMS) is predominantly used for detection attributable to its rapidity and user-friendliness. Paper spray ionization–mass spectrometry (PSI-MS) is a major advancement in science and technology that offers a sensitive, quick, and authentic analysis with no sample chemical processing steps. Science and development are particularly interested in the development of portable biosensors for in-field analysis because of their sensitivity to chemical analytes, allowing the detection of substances in minute concentrations, with selectivity and authenticity. Furthermore, these biosensors are user-friendly, automated, and offer ease of operation. Although there has been progress in the development of electrochemical and optical biosensors, their commercial availability will give them a greater advantage over other portable equipment in the real-time detection of the questioned drug sample.

Current and future perspectives

Forensic chemistry and toxicology both could play a pivotal role in examining the legal and scientific problems associated with illicit drugs. Instead of focusing on the overdose of a single chemical substance, emphasis should be placed on death cases in the presence of other drugs that may have been consumed concurrently and may have led to death due to synergism. In India, NPS variants must be anticipated, and efficient methodologies must be devised. Forensic chemistry must thrive in chemical profiling methods and generating standard databases. Furthermore, geographical and climatic variations cause the degradation of drug samples variably. This is another avenue that needs to be explored to generate impeccable, generalizable findings. The Indian academic researcher should emphasize the significant aspects of these forensic disciples as a dearth of research exists in both domains.

Conclusion

It is clearly evident that the illicit drug market is stable due to the dynamic modus operandi and coping strategies adopted by offenders. The kind of drug abused may vary depending on the availability and cost-effectiveness. The source of illegitimate production/manufacturing of drugs lies in underdeveloped nations and the regions proximal to them, from where they are trafficked in wealthy nations. Potentially integrated databases must be created for the reference of forensic experts to select the robust and recommended scientific forensic technique rendered during the drug testing of seized samples. This will help the other experts in effective drug testing and gathering authentic forensic findings. Collaboration, communication, and information exchange between forensic scientists and investigators are essential for illegal profiling to be useful. A forensic scientist might be in possession of some technical data, yet others are unaware of its significance. Cutting-edge research methodologies must be envisioned to provide effective intelligence.

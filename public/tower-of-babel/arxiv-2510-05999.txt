Existence and Nonexistence breaking results for a weighted elliptic problem in half-space
J. M. do Ó, R. F. Freire, J. Giacomoni, E. S. Medeiros

Abstract
. In this paper we study the problem − div ( ρ ( x N ) ∇ u ) = a | u | p − 2 u -\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)=a|u|^{p-2}u in ℝ + N \mathds{R}^{N}_{+} , − ∂ u / ∂ x N = b | u | q − 2 u -\partial u/\partial x_{N}=b|u|^{q-2}u in ℝ N − 1 \mathds{R}^{N-1} where a , b ∈ ℝ a,b\in\mathds{R} , p , q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p,q\in(1,\infty) and ρ \rho is a positive weight. We establish regularity results for weak solutions and, using a variational approach combined with a new Pohozaev-type identity, we show that the introduction of the weighted operator − div ( ρ ( x N ) ∇ u ) -\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u) can reverse the known solvability behavior of the classical Laplacian case. Specifically, we identify regimes where the problem admits solutions despite nonexistence for the corresponding case with − Δ -\Delta , and vice versa, thus inverting the classical existence and nonexistence results.

Introduction and main results

Let
N\geq 3
and denote by

\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathds{R}^{N}:x^{\prime}\in\mathds{R}^{N-1},\;x_{N}>0\}

In this work, we are interested in investigating the regularity, existence and nonexistence of solutions to the nonlinear elliptic boundary value problem

(
\mathcal{P}_{0}

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)&=a|u|^{p-2}u&\mbox{in }&\ \mathds{R}^{N}_{+},\vskip 5.69046pt\\

where

For the weight function

\rho\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty)
and impose the following basic hypothesis:

(\rho_{0})

There exists a constant

such that

(1+s)^{\gamma}\leq\rho(s),\quad s\geq 0.

Equations in the form

27

[
35

0

P
0

) pointwise in
\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}
and on its boundary
\mathds{R}^{N-1}

We denote the critical Sobolev exponent by

Remark 1.1

In

0\leq p\leq 2^{\ast},\quad-\infty<q\leq 2_{\ast},\quad p+q<2^{\ast}+2_{\ast},

the problem

-\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}&=|u|^{q-2}u&\quad&\text{on }\mathds{R}^{N-1},\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, they showed that if

These problems can be regarded as limit cases of (

0

What we have discovered, however, is that this perturbation introduced by the weight function

In the critical regime, the following classical Sobolev trace inequality holds:

(1.5)

S_{\partial}\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{2_{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right)^{2/2_{\ast}}\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\,u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}),

where the sharp constant is given by

S_{\partial}=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x}{\left(\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{2_{\ast}}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right)^{2/2_{\ast}}}=\frac{N-2}{2}\,\omega_{N-1}^{1/(N-1)},

This inequality plays a fundamental role in the analysis of variational problems with boundary nonlinearities and provides the natural framework for studying extremal functions attaining the best constant

These functions

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=0&\text{in }\mathds{R}^{N}_{+},\\[3.00003pt]
-\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}=S_{\partial}|u|^{2_{\ast}-2}u&\text{on }\mathds{R}^{N-1}.\end{cases}

However, as we shall see, in the problems (

The aim of this work is to investigate how such perturbations influence the existence and regularity of solutions, in contrast with the classical Laplacian case.

|u^{\ast}(x^{\prime},x_{N})|\leq C\frac{\|u^{\ast}\|}{|x^{\prime}|^{(N-1)/2}}.

∎

Lemma 2.4

Let

Exploring some ideas from

Proof of Theorem

Let

\mathcal{R}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})

\|u_{n}^{\ast}\|
bounded, then there exists

in
\mathcal{R}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})

\mathds{R}^{N-1}

, given that, by (

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have that
S_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}>0
when

and

when

Now, suppose that
\gamma>2
,

and, without loss of generality, that

. By (
2.2
) and (

. Observe that
(u_{n}^{\ast})
is bounded in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
, and, up to a subsequence, we have
u_{n}^{\ast}\rightharpoonup u
in
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
. Since

. Moreover, by Lemma
1.2
, we conclude that
\|u\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}=1
.

As for

2.2

is attained when
\gamma>2
and
p\in[2,2^{\ast}]
.

We now prove that

is not attained. The idea is inspired by the approach in
[

. Given

S_{2,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})\left(\delta^{2}|\nabla_{x^{\prime}}u|^{2}+u_{x_{N}}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Letting

S_{2,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})u_{x_{N}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}).

Suppose that there exists

such that

Then, combining this with (

, so that

for some function

, which is a contradiction, since

by Lemma

. The same argument shows that

cannot be attained.
∎

3.1.
Hölder Regularity

In this section, we investigate the Hölder regularity of weak solutions of problem (

0

). With an approach inspired by techniques found in
[

,
11
,
19

, we provide the boundedness of the weak solutions, allowing us to reach the boundary regularity desired for the case where
a
and

have inverse signs. The interior regularity for

in the subcritical case is established by verifying that
u^{r}
belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
and applying the classical regularity theory.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume
(\rho_{0})
with
\gamma\geq 2
,
p\in[2,2^{\ast})
and
q\in[2,2_{\ast})
. If
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
is a nonnegative weak solution for (

P
0

), then
u^{r}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
for all
r>1

Proof.

Let
1<r_{1}=\min\{2^{\ast}-p+1,2_{\ast}-q+1\}
and define
T_{k}u=\min\{u^{r_{1}},k^{r_{1}}\}
, with
k\in\mathds{N}
. Observe that
T_{k}u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
with

\nabla T_{k}u=\left\{\begin{aligned} (r_{1}-1)u^{r_{1}-1}\nabla u\ &\mbox{if }&\ u\leq k,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
0\ &\mbox{if }&\ u>k.\end{aligned}\right.

From (
1.16
) and a density argument we have

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})\nabla u\nabla(T_{k}u)\mathrm{d}x=a\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}u^{p-1}T_{k}(u)\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}u^{q-1}T_{k}(u)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

then

(r_{1}-1)\int_{\{u\leq k\}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}u^{r_{1}-1}\mathrm{d}x\leq 2|a|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}u^{p-1+r_{1}}\mathrm{d}x+2|b|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}u^{q-1+r_{1}}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

and

\int_{\{u\leq k\}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla(u^{\frac{r_{1}+1}{2}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq|a|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}u^{p-1+r_{1}}\mathrm{d}x+|b|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}u^{q-1+r_{1}}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

The right side is finite by restriction to
r_{1}
. Thus, passing to the limit when
k\longrightarrow\infty
and applying the monotone convergence theorem, we conclude that
u^{\frac{r_{1}+1}{2}}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
. Now, define
s_{1}:=(r_{1}+1)/2
and observe that by Lemma
2.1
we have

(3.1)

u\in\left\{\begin{aligned} L^{s}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})\ &\mbox{for }&\ s\in\ &[2,2^{\ast}s_{1}],\vskip 5.69046pt\\
L^{s}(\mathds{R}^{N-1})\ &\mbox{for }&\ s\in\ &[2,2_{\ast}s_{1}].\end{aligned}\right.

Now, let
r_{2}=\min\{2^{\ast}s_{1}-p+1,2_{\ast}s_{1}-q+1\}
and take
T_{k}(u)=\min\{u^{r_{2}},k^{r_{2}}\}
. Taking
T_{k}(u)
as test function and arguing as in the previous case, we obtain

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla(u^{\frac{r_{2}+1}{2}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq|a|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}u^{p-1+r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x+|b|\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}u^{q-1+r_{2}}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

and thus, by (
3.1
),
u^{s_{2}}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
for
s_{2}=(r_{2}+1)/2
. We can iterate this argument and we obtain a sequence
(r_{n})
such that

r_{n}=\min\{2^{\ast}s_{n-1}+1-p,2_{\ast}s_{n-1}+1-q\}\quad\text{and}\quad u^{s_{n}}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}),

where
s_{n}=(r_{n}+1)/2
. Clearly we have
r_{n}\longrightarrow\infty
. In fact, suppose that for some
M>0
,
r_{n}\leq M
. Going if necessary to a subsequence, we have
r_{n}\longrightarrow r\geq 1
. Then, passing to the limit in the definition of
r_{n}
, we get

\frac{p-2}{2^{\ast}-2}=\frac{r+1}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{q-2}{2_{\ast}-2}=\frac{r+1}{2}.

Which is a contradiction in both cases, since
p,q
are subcritical and
r\geq 1
.

Now, observe that

(3.2)

u\in L^{s}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})\cap L^{s}(\mathds{R}^{N-1})\quad\forall s>2.

In fact, given
s>2
, we can choose
n
sufficient large to
2s_{n}>s
, then, since

belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
and hence to

, by interpolation inequality we have, for some

,

\|u\|_{s,\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\leq\|u\|_{2,\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}^{1-\alpha}\|u\|_{2^{\ast}s_{n},\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}^{\alpha}<\infty.

Finally, we claim that
u^{r}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
for all
r>1
. Indeed, given
r>1
, let
s>1
such that
(s+1)/2=r

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla(u^{\frac{s+1}{2}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq a\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|u|^{p-1+s}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{q-1+s}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime},

where the right side is finite by (

), which concludes the result.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(\rho_{0})

u

Lemma 3.5

First, observe that for any

Now, we aim to prove that for all

we have

(3.11)

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|D_{h}^{i}(u)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}).

, for

Then, by the Jensen inequality (see

\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}

3.11

\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}

. On the other hand,

\displaystyle l+\|u\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}^{q}\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\|u_{n}\|^{2}+\|u_{n}\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}^{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}+\|u\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}^{q}.

Thus,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|^{2}=\|u\|^{2}
and hence
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n}\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}^{q}=\|u\|_{q,\mathds{R}^{N-1}}^{q}
. Therefore,
I(u_{n})\longrightarrow I(u)=c>0
, which implies that
u\neq 0
. By Theorem
1.5
, we have
u\in C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}})
and
u
is positive in
\overline{\mathds{R^{N}_{+}}}
.
∎

5.
Pohozaev identity and nonexistence results

Proof of Pohozaev identity.

: Let
R>0
and
\Omega_{R}\subset\overline{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}
be such that
B_{R}^{+}\subset\Omega_{R}
,
\Gamma_{R}\subset\partial\Omega_{R}
and
\partial\Omega
is sufficiently smooth. First, consider
u
a sufficiently smooth function. Then, by a standard calculation, we have in
\Omega_{R}
:

\displaystyle{\rm{div}}\left((x\cdot\nabla u)\rho(x_{N})\nabla u\right)

\displaystyle=(x\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)+\nabla(x\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\rho(x_{N})\nabla u

\displaystyle=(x\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)+\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\rho(x_{N})}{2}\left(x\cdot\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2})\right)

and

\displaystyle{\rm{div}}\left(x\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}(x\rho(x_{N}))|\nabla u|^{2}+\rho(x_{N})x\cdot\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(N\rho(x_{N})+\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}+\rho(x_{N})x\cdot\nabla(|\nabla u|^{2}).

Now, let
\psi_{R}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{R})
be restricted to
\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{R}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1&\quad&\text{if}&\quad|x|\leq R/2,\\
0&\quad&\text{if}&\quad|x|\geq R,\end{aligned}\right.

with
|\nabla\psi_{R}(x)|\leq 2/R
, and define the vector field
V_{R}:\Omega_{R}\longrightarrow\mathds{R}^{N}
by

V_{R}(x):=\left[(x\cdot\nabla u)\rho(x_{N})\nabla u-\frac{x\rho(x_{N})}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\psi_{R}.

From the previous computations, it follows that

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(V_{R})=

\displaystyle-\left[(x\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)+\frac{2-N}{2}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\psi_{R}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left[(x\cdot\nabla u)\rho(x_{N})\nabla u-\frac{x\rho(x_{N})}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\nabla\psi_{R}

in
\Omega_{R}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}V_{R}\cdot\nu\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{R}}\mathrm{div}(V_{R})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}^{+}}\left[(x\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)+\frac{2-N}{2}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\psi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}^{+}\setminus B_{R/2}}\left[(x\cdot\nabla u)\rho(x_{N})\nabla u-\frac{x\rho(x_{N})}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\nabla\psi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now let
u\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}})
and
(u_{n})\subset C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{R}})
such that
u_{n}\to u
in
H^{2}(\overline{\Omega_{R}})
. Applying the above equation to
u_{n}
and passing the limit yield the same for
u\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}})
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Gamma_{R}}V_{R}^{n}\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}-\int_{\Gamma_{R}}V_{R}\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}|((x\cdot\nabla u_{n})\nabla u_{n}-(x\cdot\nabla u)\nabla u)\cdot\nu|\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R}}\left|(x\cdot\nabla u)\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}-(x\cdot\nabla u_{n})\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{N}}\right|\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla u\|_{2,\Gamma_{R}}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}-\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{N}}\right\|_{2,\Gamma_{R}}+\left\|\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{N}}\right\|_{2,\Gamma_{R}}\|x(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u)\|_{2,\Gamma_{R}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\left\|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}-\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{N}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{R})}+C_{2}\|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{R})}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{+}}(x\cdot\nabla u_{n})\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u_{n})\psi_{R}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{+}}(x\cdot\nabla u_{n})\left(\rho(x_{N})\Delta u_{n}-\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial x_{N}}\right)\psi_{R}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{+}}(x\cdot\nabla u)\left(\rho(x_{N})\Delta u-\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{N}}\right)\psi_{R}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}^{+}}(x\cdot\nabla u)\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)\psi_{R}\mathrm{d}x,

and clearly the other terms also converges for the desired limit.

Since
|\nabla\psi_{R}(x)|\leq 2/R
and
\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{R}^{+}\setminus B_{R/2}}\left[(x\cdot\nabla u)\rho(x_{N})\nabla u-\frac{1}{2}x\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\nabla\psi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3\int_{B_{R}^{+}\setminus B_{R/2}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow 0,

as
R\longrightarrow+\infty
.
Moreover, since

\int_{B_{R}^{+}}\rho(x_{N})\nabla u\nabla\varphi\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B_{R}^{+}}f(u)\varphi\mathrm{d}x,\quad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{R}^{+}),

and
u\in H^{2}(B_{R}^{+})
, integration by parts yields
-\mathrm{div}(\rho(x_{N})\nabla u)=f(u)
a.e. in
B_{R}^{+}
. Hence, we conclude

\int_{\Gamma_{R}}V_{R}\cdot\nu\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}=-\int_{B_{R}^{+}}\left[f(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)+\frac{N-2}{2}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\psi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x+o_{R}(1).

Let
(R_{k})_{k\in\mathds{N}}
be a sequence such that
R_{k}\longrightarrow+\infty
as
k\longrightarrow+\infty
and denote
\psi_{k}:=\psi_{{}_{R_{k}}}
and
V_{k}:=V_{R_{k}}
. Consequently, using the fact that
u
is a solution, the definition of
V_{R}
,
\rho(0)=1
and
x\cdot\nu=0
in
\Gamma_{R_{k}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}g(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}V_{k}\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}+\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}(x\cdot\nu)\psi_{k}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}V_{k}\cdot\nu\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}\left[f(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)+\frac{N-2}{2}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right]\psi_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x

(5.1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle o_{R_{k}}(1).

We now claim that

(5.2)

\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}f(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)\psi_{R}\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow-N\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}F(u)\mathrm{d}x,\quad\text{as}\quad k\longrightarrow\infty.

Indeed, integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}f(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}\left(F(u)\right)_{x_{i}}x_{i}\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}\left[-NF(u)\psi_{k}-F(u)(x\cdot\nabla\psi_{k})\right]\,\mathrm{d}x.

By the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}F(u)\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}F(u)\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\text{as}\quad k\longrightarrow\infty.

Furthermore

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}F(u)(x\cdot\nabla\psi_{k})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}\backslash B_{R_{k}/2}}|F(u)||x||\nabla\psi_{k}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}\backslash B_{R_{k}/2}}|F(u)|\,\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{as}\quad k\longrightarrow\infty,

since
F(u)\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
, which concludes (
5.2
). Now, observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}g(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}\left(G(u)\right)_{x_{i}}x_{i}\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}\left[-(N-1)G(u)\psi_{k}-G(u)\left(x^{\prime}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\psi_{k}\right)\right]\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

By the same reasoning, we obtain

\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}G(u)\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\longrightarrow\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}G(u)\left(x^{\prime}\cdot\nabla_{x^{\prime}}\psi_{k}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0.

Hence,

(5.3)

\int_{\Gamma_{R_{k}}}g(u)(x\cdot\nabla u)\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\longrightarrow-(N-1)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}G(u)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Furthermore, by the dominated convergence Theorem,

(5.4)

\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Finally, by
(\rho_{1})
and the dominated convergence theorem, we get

(5.5)

\int_{B_{R_{k}}^{+}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\psi_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\longrightarrow\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then, passing to the limit in (
5
) and using (
5.2
), (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we obtain

-(N-1)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}G(u)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}=N\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}F(u)\mathrm{d}x-\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore,

\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}_{+}^{N}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}_{+}^{N}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=N\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}F(u)\mathrm{d}x+(N-1)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}G(u)\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

∎

Now, we are able to prove the nonexistence results.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

: For each item of the theorem, given
u
be a nonnegative weak solution for (

P
0

), we have
u^{q}\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N-1})
and
u^{p}\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
. Moreover, by Theorem
1.6
, we have
u\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}})
, so the Pohozaev identity in Theorem
1.10
applies and yields:

\displaystyle\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}_{+}^{N}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}_{+}^{N}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\frac{aN}{p}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{b(N-1)}{q}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Furthermore, taking
u
as a testing function, we obtain

\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho(x_{N})|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=a\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

By combining these two equations, we get the following relation:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=a\left(\frac{N}{p}-\frac{N-2}{2}\right)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+b\left(\frac{N-1}{q}-\frac{N-2}{2}\right)\int_{\mathds{R}^{N-1}}|u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}.

Since
\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}>0
by
(\rho_{1})
, the left-hand side of the equation is strictly positive unless
u\equiv 0
. Under conditions of the itens
(i)
or
(ii)
, the right-hand side becomes nonpositive. Therefore, the only possibility is
u\equiv 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

: Lets prove the item
(iii)
and the same analysis can be applied to the other cases. If
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\rho}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
and
(\rho_{0})
holds with
\gamma>0
, we have
u^{2^{\ast}}\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N}_{+})
by the classical Sobolev inequality, and
u^{2_{\ast}}\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{N-1})
by (
2.1
). Then, since
u\in H^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}})
, we can argue as in the previous theorem and use the fact that
p=2^{\ast}
and
q=2_{\ast}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{N}_{+}}\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=0.

Given that
\rho^{\prime}(x_{N})x_{N}>0
by
(\rho_{1})
, it remains that
u\equiv 0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

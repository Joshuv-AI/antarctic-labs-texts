Introduction

In the 1970s and 1980s, significant progress was made in understanding the convexity and concavity properties of solutions to certain elliptic differential equations on convex domains and Dirichlet boundary conditions. In the pivotal papers by Brascamp and Lieb
[
12
]
and Makar-Limanov
[
50
]
, the authors showed that solutions of the eigenfunction problem and of the torsion problem are concave up to a composition, respectively, with the logarithm function or the square root function. The seminal papers by Korevaar
[
43
]
and Kennington
[
40
]
gave a new boost to this research, by introducing general
concavity maximum principles
to deal with general classes of equations. We refer to
[
2
,
25
]
and references therein for a general overview on the topic.
Let us mention, among various mathematical applications, that in
[
56
]
the authors exploit the
\log
-concavity of the eigenfunction to show notable results about the eigenvalue gap, i.e. the difference between the first and the second Dirichlet eigenvalue.

In the framework of parabolic equations and Cauchy-Dirichlet problems

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=b(x,u,t)&\quad\hbox{in $\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
u=0&\quad\hbox{on $\partial\Omega\times[0,T)$},\\
u=u_{0}&\quad\hbox{on $\overline{\Omega}\times\{0\}$},\end{cases}

T\in(0,+\infty]
, several questions arise.
A first problem is to study very particular examples of
b
and discuss if, under suitable assumptions on
u_{0}
, the solution
u(x,t)
becomes logarithmic or power concave (i.e.
\log(u)
or
u^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
is concave) after some amount of time (
t>0
or possibly
t\gg 0
): these kinds of results are strongly related to the particular shape of
b
which allows, for instance, explicit representation of the solution, and have been investigated for example in
[
46
,
47
,
38
]
.
We highlight that these results allow, under suitable assumptions, to get interesting properties also in the elliptic case, by considering the limit of
u(x,t)
as
t\to+\infty
which coincides with the solution of the corresponding stationary problem.

A second question is the following: what is the optimal (in some sense) concavity on
u_{0}
which is preserved by the heat flow? In this problem, the logarithmic concavity plays a very special role.
This and similar questions have been addressed in a series of papers
[
34
,
35
,
33
,
32
]
.
See also
[
22
,
ChauWeinkove
]
for similar questions regarding power concavity and porous medium flow.

In this paper we are interested in a different question: under which assumptions on
b
and
u_{0}
can we obtain that
u(x,t)
enjoys some concavity properties for each
t>0
?
To the authors’ knowledge, only few papers deal with this problem.

A first result can be found in Korevaar
[
43
]
, where the author adapts the elliptic concavity maximum principles to study classes of parabolic equations and show that, for example, the parabolic eigenfunctions
u(\cdot,t)
are spatially logarithmic concave for each
t\in[0,T]
if
u_{0}
is so.
Let us mention that in
[
6
]
a stronger notion of
\log
-concavity is proved and it is exploited to prove a sharp result on the eigenvalue gap; it is notable to observe that the technique adopted in
[
6
]
relies on the introduction and analysis of some suitable multivariable function in a spirit relatable to the one introduced in
[
43
,
40
]
and used in the present paper.
Some partial generalizations of the results in
[
43
]
can be found in
[
54
]
(see also
[
28
]
), by allowing the inclusion of other interesting models.
Other results are contained in
[
34
]
(see e.g. Proposition 4.2 therein) by means of
spatial log-concave envelopes
as viscosity subsolutions of the problem and corresponding comparison principles; similar techniques go back to
[
4
]
(see also
[
21
]
).

A second result, i.e. a parabolic generalization for equations in the spirit of the torsion and Lane-Emden problems, which allows to obtain power concavity (stronger than logarithmic one), appears first in
[
41
]
: here the author studies the case
a=1
and
f(u)=(1-u)^{q}
,
q\in(0,1)
, obtaining that a suitable transformation of
u
is concave and thus
u
is quasi-concave (i.e., the superlevel sets of
u
are convex).
To gain the claim the author exploits again some concavity maximum principle and he strongly uses that
f(0)\neq 0
coupled with some comparison principle; moreover, he relies on the
information at infinity
given by the stationary problem.
Other results have been more recently obtained in
[
36
,
37
]
(see also
[
31
]
):
here the authors rely on the use of
parabolic concave envelopes
as viscosity subsolutions.
In such a way the authors are able to study the concavity of nondecreasing solutions with controlled behaviour on the boundary, in the case of nonnegative nonlinearities satisfying a priori some comparison principle, set in smooth domains: for example, they cover the cases
f=1
, or
a=1
and
f(u)=u^{q}
,
q\in(0,1)
. Moreover, the authors study a
rescaled
concavity in space and time, namely they show that
(x,t)\mapsto u(x,t^{\beta})
is power concave for suitable values of
\beta
.

Aim of this paper is twofold.
First, we aim to recover and slightly generalize the abovementioned results, in a unified way, by means of parabolic concavity maximum principles. In particular, to recover the results previously obtained through different techniques, we need a finer analysis, especially regarding the boundary behaviour of the solution (including
t\to+\infty
).
A second goal is to derive
quantitative
estimates of concavity maximum principles, which will require a careful study of the objects in play.
For the sake of clarity in presentation, we describe first some applications of the exact results (which are indeed a consequence of the quantitative estimates).

is

Theorem 1.1

(Weighted eigenfunctions and logarithmic equations)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a bounded, smooth, strongly convex domain.
Let
T>0
,
a\colon\Omega\times(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
,
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})

with
u_{0}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
u\in C^{2}_{x}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T])
be a positive solution of

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=a(x,t)f(u)&\quad\hbox{in $\Omega\times(0,T]$},\\
u=0&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
D_{x}u\cdot\nu>0&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\\
u=u_{0}&\quad\text{on }\overline{\Omega}\times\{0\},\end{cases}

where
\nu
is the interior normal to
\partial\Omega
.
Assume moreover that
u_{0}>0
is
\log
-concave in
\Omega
and one of the following:

i
)

f(s)=s
, and
a(\cdot,t)
is concave for each
t\in(0,T]
;

ii
)

f(s)=s\log^{q}(1+s)
or
f(s)=\frac{s^{q+1}}{1+s^{q}}
,
q\in(0,1)
, and
a(\cdot,t)
is
\frac{1}{1-q}
-concave for each

;

iii
)

f(s)=s\log(1+s)
,
f(s)=s\log(s)
or

, and

is a positive constant.

Then

is concave for each

. Moreover, if

and

is strictly concave for each

Theorem 1.3

(Weighted Lane-Emden)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

, be a bounded, convex domain which satisfies the interior sphere property.
Let

and
\gamma\in[0,1]

Let

be a positive solution of

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=a(x)t^{\gamma}u^{q}&\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u=0&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,+\infty),\\
u=0&\quad\text{on }\overline{\Omega}\times\{0\}.\end{cases}

a\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}

As already mentioned, the main tool of this paper is composed by
concavity
and
harmonic concavity maximum principles
, which allow to give an estimate on the concavity function
\mathcal{C}_{v}
of a solution of a general PDE in terms of the concavity/harmonic concavity function of the nonlinear term (see Definition
3.7
).
The equations treated do not rely on the boundary conditions and can have a very general form (see Theorems
4.1
,
4.4
), and in particular include the equations satisfied by

or
v=u^{\alpha}
,

, where

is the solution of the equation of interest.
We highlight that the concavity principle approach is flexible since it can be used, for example, to deal with

solutions of quasilinear equations, see
[

,
11
,
49
]
(see also Remark

for another nonsmooth setting), and thus possibly useful for treating

We refer to Remark

for some technical comments on the proofs and corresponding difficulties and novelties.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we collect some remarks on the main theorems and on the proofs, while in Section
3
we recall some basic facts on parabolic equations, and we introduce the main assumptions that we will require on the nonlinearity.
Moreover, we recall notations and properties about concavity functions; more technical results are instead collected in Appendix
B
.
Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the main concavity principles, which take into account only the PDE and not the boundary/initial conditions; these theorems will lead both to exact and quantitative results.
In particular, we show both concavity principles in space (in presence of nonlinearities which are concave but not necessarily decreasing, and set in
(0,T]
) and in space-time (in the case of harmonic concave and decreasing nonlinearities set in
(0,+\infty)
); the latter will require the use of the harmonic concavity function.
In Section

we take into account the boundary conditions and the initial condition, employing a suitable analysis of the solution and of the concavity function near the boundary of the parabolic cylinder; this analysis exploits also comparison principles and Hopf lemmas which are collected in Appendix

. Some applications and examples follow, including the proofs of the main theorems presented in the introduction.
When the nonlinearity does not depend on

, finally, we present some further results (both simplifications and improvements of the previous ones) in Section

Remark 2.4

(On the proofs)
.

As mentioned in the introduction, differently from

,

, our proofs rely on concavity maximum principles, which allow also to keep track of the concavity error.

We moreover recall that

3.1.
On parabolic equations

In this subsection we recall some facts on the parabolic problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=b(x,u,Du,t)&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\

In such a case the solutions of

-concave.

As an additional consequence of Theorem

.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

, be a bounded, smooth, strongly convex domain.
Let

,
a\colon\Omega\times(0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
bounded,
u_{0}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
with

on

be such that

Assume moreover that

\log

Then there exists

\inf_{\Omega\times\Omega\times[0,1]\times[0,T]}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\log(u)}\geq-eT\sup_{\Omega_{\rho}\times\Omega_{\rho}\times[0,1]\times(0,T]}\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{a(\cdot,\star)}\right)^{-}.

Remark 5.10
.

We see that some results in Corollary
5.9
can be extended to piecewise constant weights
a=a(x)
arising in bang-bang optimization theory (see
[
14
, Theorem 3.9]
), i.e. equal to
a_{1}>0
on some region of
\Omega
and to
-a_{2}<0
on some other region.
We give just a sketch of the proof.
Let indeed
a
be as above, and let
a_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a suitable equibounded regular approximating sequence for
a
.
Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be positive solutions of
\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}-\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=a_{\varepsilon}(x)u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}^{2}
,
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\partial\Omega
,
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0}
.
Exploiting that
a_{\varepsilon}(x)t-t^{2}\leq 0
for
t\geq\sup_{\varepsilon}\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}
and the maximum principle we obtain that
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}
is equibounded; then standard regularity theory implies that
u_{\varepsilon}
is also equibounded in some
C^{0,\sigma}
, and hence it converges uniformly up to a subsequence by Ascoli-Arzelà theorem.
We can thus pass to the limit the weak formulation (see e.g.
[
15
]
) of the equations and obtain that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
solution of the equation with weight
a(x)
.
Since
u_{\varepsilon}
are regular, then they
satisfy the assumptions of Corollary

and thus, in particular,
\inf\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\log(u_{\varepsilon})}\geq-eT\sup\left(\mathcal{C}^{*}_{a_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\star)}\right)^{-}\geq-eT{\rm osc}(a_{\varepsilon})
.
Passing to the limit, we obtain

\inf\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\log(u)}\geq-eT(a_{1}+a_{2}).

We leave the details to the interested reader.

Corollary A.3

(Monotonicity)
.

In the assumptions of Proposition
A.1

(H3)
.
Let

be a positive solution to

\begin{cases}u_{t}-\Delta u=b(x,u,t)&\hbox{in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\

Assume moreover that

u(\cdot,t)\geq u_{0}\quad\hbox{for each $t>0$}.

Then

in
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
.
Extending the assumptions on

also to

, a condition to ensure (

) is that

is a subsolution of the stationary problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=b(x,w,0)&\hbox{in }\Omega,\\
w=0&\hbox{on }\partial\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
\tau>0
and set
\tilde{u}(x,t):=u(x,t+\tau)
, then

satisfies

\begin{cases}\tilde{u}_{t}-\Delta\tilde{u}=b(x,\tilde{u},t+\tau)\geq b(x,\tilde{u},t)&\text{in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\

Since
(H2)
or
(H2^{*})
holds, by Proposition
A.1
or
A.2
we have
\tilde{u}(x,t)\geq u(x,t)
for all

A.1
or
A.2

To state the parabolic version of the Hopf lemma, we recall some usual notation for the domain and the boundary.

(\bar{x},\bar{t})\in\partial B_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})

C_{r}(\bar{x},\bar{t}):=B_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})\cap\left\{t<\bar{t}\right\}\subset\Omega\times(0,T].

We recall thus the Hopf lemma for parabolic equations, see e.g.

Lemma A.4

(Parabolic Hopf lemma)

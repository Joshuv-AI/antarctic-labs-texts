The Flow-Limit of Reflect-Reflect-Relax: Existence, Stability, and Discrete-Time Behavior
Manish Krishan Lal

Abstract
We study the Reflect–Reflect–Relax (RRR) algorithm in its small–step (flow–limit) regime. In the smooth transversal setting, we show that the transverse dynamics form a hyperbolic sink , yielding exponential decay of a natural gap measure. Under uniform geometric assumptions, we construct a tubular neighborhood of the feasible manifold on which the squared gap defines a strict Lyapunov function , excluding recurrent dynamics and chaotic behavior within this basin. In the discrete setting, the induced flow is piecewise constant on W-domains and supports Filippov sliding along convergent boundaries, leading to finite-time capture into a solution domain. We prove that small-step RRR is a forward–Euler discretization of this flow, so that solution times measured in rescaled units converge to a finite limit while iteration counts diverge, explaining the emergence of iteration-optimal relaxation parameters . Finally, we introduce a heuristic mesoscopic framework based on percolation and renormalization group to organize performance deterioration near the Douglas–Rachford limit.

1
Introduction

Projection and reflection methods such as Douglas Rachford splitting and the
Reflect-Reflect-Relax (RRR) algorithm have proved effective for feasibility
problems involving nonconvex and even discrete constraints. These methods are
used in a wide range of applications, including combinatorial design problems,
constraint satisfaction, and learning formulations cast as feasibility problems
[
3
]
.
Despite their empirical success, the mechanisms governing their convergence and
their dependence on algorithmic parameters remain only partially understood.

Motivated by extensive numerical experiments, Elser proposed a flow limit
viewpoint for RRR, in which progress is measured not in raw iteration counts but
in rescaled time units of the form
t=k\varepsilon
, where
\varepsilon>0
denotes the step
size and
x_{k}
the
k
th iterate. From this viewpoint, a robust empirical
phenomenon emerges: as
\varepsilon
decreases, the solution time measured in rescaled
units often stabilizes to a finite value, while the number of iterations required
to reach a solution increases and exhibits a problem dependent optimal choice of

\varepsilon
. This behavior is observed across a range of nonconvex feasibility
problems, including those studied in
[
4
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to provide a rigorous mathematical foundation for
this small step flow picture. We focus on two regimes that arise naturally in
practice. The first is a smooth regime, in which the constraint sets are smooth
manifolds intersecting transversally. The second is a discrete regime, in which
the constraint sets are finite and the induced dynamics exhibit sharp switching
behavior. In both cases, we interpret RRR as a discretization of an underlying
continuous time system and analyze the structure of the resulting flow. To this end, we define the RRR flow field

v(x):=P_{B}(R_{A}x)-P_{A}x,\qquad R_{A}:=2P_{A}-\mathrm{Id},

and study the continuous dynamics
\dot{x}=v(x)
together with its small step
discretization

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\varepsilon\,v(x_{k}).

This formulation allows us to treat smooth and nonsmooth behavior within a
single framework and to make precise comparisons between continuous trajectories
and discrete iterates.

Contributions and scope.

All of our rigorous results are established under local assumptions. In the
smooth setting, they are proved within a positively invariant tubular
neighborhood of the feasible manifold on which the metric projections are
single valued. In the discrete setting, they are proved along specified chains
of convergent W-domain boundaries. Within this framework, we establish the
following results.

(i)

Transverse stability from the flow linearization.

In the smooth transversal regime, we use the explicit linearization of the RRR
flow in terms of tangent space projectors and prove that the transverse dynamics
form a hyperbolic sink, with exponential decay rates determined by principal
angles (Lemma
2.1
, Theorem
3.1
).

(ii)

Piecewise constant flow and sliding capture.

For finite constraint sets, the induced flow is piecewise constant on
W-domains. Along two cell boundaries satisfying a convergent normal condition,
the associated Filippov sliding dynamics drive trajectories into a solution
cell in finite time
(Theorem
3.2
).

(iii)

Euler approximation of the flow.

Small step RRR is an
O(\varepsilon)
accurate forward Euler discretization of the
continuous flow on finite time intervals, including across sliding segments,
yielding
O(\varepsilon)
errors in trajectories
(Theorem
3.3
).

(iv)

Flow limit behavior of solution times.

In the feasible case, the continuous solution time to reach a fixed gap
threshold is finite, and the discrete solution time converges to this limit as

\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, while the corresponding iteration count diverges like

1/\varepsilon

(Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
).

(v)

Lyapunov structure and absence of recurrence.

Under uniform geometric assumptions, the squared gap defines a strict
Lyapunov function on a tubular neighborhood of the feasible manifold,
excluding nontrivial recurrence and chaotic dynamics within this basin
(Theorem
4.2
, Corollary
4.3
).

Beyond these local results, we introduce a formal but heuristic mesoscopic
framework based on percolation and renormalization group to organize the
empirical deterioration of RRR performance as the relaxation parameter
approaches the Douglas Rachford limit. We do not claim global convergence or a complete description of RRR dynamics for
arbitrary step sizes. Instead, our results provide a rigorous foundation for the
small step regime of the flow limit conjecture and for the absence of chaotic
behavior near feasible solutions.

Preliminaries

2.1
RRR and the flow field

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be closed sets with (possibly multivalued) metric projections

P_{A},P_{B}
. The RRR update with step
\varepsilon>0
is

x_{k+1}\;=\;x_{k}\;+\;\varepsilon\,\bigl(P_{B}(R_{A}x_{k})-P_{A}x_{k}\bigr).

(1)

where
R_{A}\;=\;2P_{A}-\mathrm{Id}
is the reflector. Formally, as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
t=k\varepsilon
, the piecewise-linear interpolation of

\{x_{k}\}
approaches the solution of the ODE

\dot{x}\;=\;v(x)\;=\;P_{B}(R_{A}x)-P_{A}x.

(2)

We call
v
the
RRR flow field
. A point
x
is a (RRR) fixed point of the flow
if
v(x)=0
. We write the
gap

g(x)\;:=\;\left\lVert v(x)\right\rVert,

(3)

as our basic measure of progress; in the smooth setting,
g(x)
is
equivalent to the transverse distance from
x
to
A\cap B
in a neighborhood of
a transversal intersection.

2.2
Regularity classes

We will use two basic regimes:

•

(S1) Smooth transverse case.

A
and
B
are
C^{2}
embedded submanifolds of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
that intersect
transversally at
x^{*}\in A\cap B
. In a sufficiently small neighborhood

of

x^{*}
the metric projections
P_{A}
and
P_{B}
are single-valued and
C^{1}
, and
their derivatives at
x^{*}
are the orthogonal projectors
p_{A},p_{B}
onto the
tangent spaces
\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A,\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B
respectively. The intersection

M:=A\cap B
is then a
C^{2}
submanifold, and
P_{A},P_{B}
restrict to the identity
on
M
near
x^{*}
.

•

(S2) Discrete W-domain case.

A
and
B
are finite subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
P_{A}
and
P_{B}
are single-valued
everywhere (nearest neighbors are unique except on a set of measure zero), and
the induced velocity field

is piecewise constant on a finite partition of

W-domains
. For more general stratified sets, one typically
obtains a piecewise
C^{1}
velocity field; here we focus on the finite case where
the geometry is purely combinatorial.

2.3
Principal angles and the Jacobian at a regular point

In the smooth transverse case (S1), the linearization of the RRR flow at a feasible point admits an explicit expression in terms of the orthogonal projectors onto the
tangent spaces of the constraint manifolds. Let
p_{A}
and
p_{B}
denote the orthogonal
projectors onto the tangent spaces
\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A
and
\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B

intersection

\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A+\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B

orthogonal decomposition into principal-angle planes and the common tangent
subspace
\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A\cap\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B

\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A
and
\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B

\theta\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]

Lemma 2.1

(Linearization of the flow at a feasible point)
.

Assume (S1) and let
x^{*}\in A\cap B
be a transversal intersection.
Then
x^{*}
is an equilibrium of the flow (
2

Dv(x^{*})\;=\;DP_{B}(x^{*})\,DR_{A}(x^{*})-DP_{A}(x^{*})\;=\;\bigl(2p_{B}p_{A}-p_{B}-p_{A}\bigr).

(4)

Moreover:

(i)

On the intersection
\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A\cap\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B

Dv(x^{*})=0
.

(ii)

On each principal-angle
2

\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
,
there exists an orthonormal basis in which

p_{A}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad p_{B}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos^{2}\theta&\cos\theta\sin\theta\\[2.0pt]
\cos\theta\sin\theta&\sin^{2}\theta\end{bmatrix},

and in this basis the restriction of
Dv(x^{*})
is

r(\theta)\;=\;\begin{bmatrix}-\sin^{2}\theta&-\sin\theta\cos\theta\\[2.0pt]
\sin\theta\cos\theta&-\sin^{2}\theta\end{bmatrix}.

The eigenvalues of

are
-\sin^{2}\theta\pm i\,\sin\theta\cos\theta
,
and its symmetric part equals
-\sin^{2}\theta\,I_{2}
.

Proof
. Since
P_{A}(x^{*})=P_{B}(x^{*})=x^{*}
, we have
R_{A}(x^{*})=x^{*}
and hence
v(x^{*})=0
. On
U

DR_{A}(x^{*})=2DP_{A}(x^{*})-I=2p_{A}-I
,
DP_{B}(x^{*})=p_{B}
, and

DP_{A}(x^{*})=p_{A}
, which yields (
4
).
(i)
follows from

p_{A}z=p_{B}z=z
for
z\in\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}A\cap\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}B
. The block decomposition in
(ii)
is
standard principal-angle geometry: in the stated basis one computes

by
direct multiplication of
2p_{B}p_{A}-p_{B}-p_{A}
, and the eigenvalues and symmetric part
follow from a short calculation.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Thus
M=A\cap B
is a manifold of equilibria, and on the transverse complement of

\mathcal{T}_{x^{*}}M
the linearization has eigenvalues with strictly negative real part,
forming a stable spiral when
0<\theta<\frac{\pi}{2}
and a stable node when

\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
.

2.4
W-domains and piecewise-constant fields

In the discrete case (S2) we assume
A
and
B
are finite sets. For each pair

(a,b)\in A\times B
define the corresponding
W-domain

\displaystyle W(a,b):=\big\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}~\big|~{P_{A}(x)=a,~P_{B}(R_{A}x)=b}\big\}.

(5)

Away from a set of measure zero where projections are nonunique, the family

\{W(a,b)\}_{(a,b)\in A\times B}
forms a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
into finitely many
cells. On such a cell we have

v(x)\;=\;P_{B}(R_{A}x)-P_{A}x\;\equiv\;b-a,\qquad x\in W(a,b),

so the velocity field is piecewise constant. The interfaces between W-domains are smooth codimension-one manifolds almost everywhere, given by Voronoi bisectors and their reflections, with lower-dimensional junction sets where three or more domains intersect.

2.5
Filippov solutions and sliding

The piecewise-constant field
v
is discontinuous on W-domain boundaries. We use
the notion of
Filippov solutions

[
5
]
. For a measurable, locally bounded vector field

v\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, its
Filippov regularization
at
x
is the closed convex
hull
\mathcal{F}(x)
of all essential values of
v
near
x
. A
Filippov
solution
of

\dot{x}(t)\in\mathcal{F}(x(t))

is an absolutely continuous curve
x(\cdot)
satisfying the inclusion almost
everywhere. Consider a smooth portion of a boundary
\Sigma
separating two W-domains with
constant velocities
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. Let
\Sigma=\partial W(a_{1},b_{1})\cap\partial W(a_{2},b_{2})
be a
C^{1}
two-cell
interface, and let
n
denote the unit normal to
\Sigma
oriented from

W(a_{1},b_{1})
into
W(a_{2},b_{2})
. The Filippov set on
\Sigma
is

\mathcal{F}(x)=\co\{v_{1},v_{2}\},\qquad v_{i}:=b_{i}-a_{i}.

If the
convergent normal condition

(n\!\cdot\!v_{1})\,(n\!\cdot\!v_{2})<0

(6)

holds, there exists a unique
v_{\mathrm{slide}}\in\mathcal{F}(x)
satisfying

n\cdot v_{\mathrm{slide}}=0
. Writing

v_{\mathrm{slide}}=\alpha v_{1}+(1-\alpha)v_{2},\qquad\alpha=\frac{n\cdot v_{2}}{n\cdot(v_{2}-v_{1})}\in(0,1),

this is equivalently expressed as

v_{\mathrm{slide}}:=\frac{(n\cdot v_{2})v_{1}-(n\cdot v_{1})v_{2}}{n\cdot(v_{2}-v_{1})}.

(7)

Under (
6
),
\Sigma
is locally attracting for Filippov
solutions, which evolve along
\Sigma
with velocity
v_{\mathrm{slide}}

[
1
]
. For a
C^{1}
interface
\Sigma
and
x\in\Sigma
, let

\Pi_{\Sigma}(x)
denote the orthogonal projector onto the tangent space

T_{x}\Sigma
. We say that the sliding velocity satisfies a

uniform tangential speed bound
on
\Sigma
if

\|\Pi_{\Sigma}(x)\,v_{\mathrm{slide}}\|\ \geq\ s_{0}\ >\ 0,\qquad\forall x\in\Sigma.

(8)

2.6
Solution time in continuous and discrete time

Fix a tolerance
\delta>0
. For a (Carathéodory or Filippov) flow solution

x(\cdot)
of (
2
) we define the continuous
hitting time

T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})\;:=\;\inf\big\{{t\geq 0}~\big|~{g\bigl(x(t)\bigr)\leq\delta}\big\},

(9)

with the convention that
T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})=+\infty
if the set is empty, and with
initial condition
x(0)=x_{0}
. We refer to
g(x)
as the
gap function

Eq.

3
, which vanishes precisely at
RRR fixed points. For the discrete RRR iteration (
1
) with step
\varepsilon>0
, we define the
corresponding discrete hitting time

t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})\;:=\;k\varepsilon,

(10)

where

is the smallest index such that
g(x_{k})\leq\delta
, if such an index
exists, and
t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})=+\infty
otherwise. One of our main goals is to understand the dependence of

t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})
on
\varepsilon
and its relation to the flow-limit

T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.

3
Main Results

We now state and prove our main results in the smooth transverse regime (S1) and
the discrete W-domain regime (S2).

3.1
Smooth transverse case

The local affine form of the flow suggests contracting behavior transverse to
the feasible manifold, as illustrated through low-dimensional examples

[
4
, Figures 5.3-5.4]
.
The following theorem makes this precise by establishing exponential decay of
the flow in the transverse directions near a transversal feasible point.

Theorem 3.1

(Hyperbolic sink and exponential decay of the gap)
.

Assume (S1) and let
x^{*}\in A\cap B
be a transversal intersection.
Then
x^{*}
is an equilibrium of (
2
), and there exist constants

\mu>0

C\geq 1
and a neighborhood
U
of
x^{*}
such that for any solution

x(\cdot)
of (
2
) with
x(0)\in U

\left\lVert v(x(t))\right\rVert\;\leq\;C\,e^{-\mu t}\,\left\lVert v(x(0))\right\rVert\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(11)

In particular, for any

and

T^{*}_{\delta}(x(0))\;\leq\;\mu^{-1}\log\Bigl(\frac{C\,\left\lVert v(x(0))\right\rVert}{\delta}\Bigr)\;<\;\infty.

(12)

Proof
. By (S1), the projections
P_{A},P_{B}
are single-valued and
C^{1}
on a

x^{*}

there. By

, so

is an equilibrium of (
2
).
Let
J:=Dv(x^{*})
.

Step 1 (coercivity of the symmetric part on the transverse sum).

Set
\mathcal{T}:=T_{x^{*}}A+T_{x^{*}}B

P_{\mathcal{T}}
be the orthogonal
projector onto
\mathcal{T}
. By Lemma
2.1
(i)
-
(ii)
,
on
\mathcal{T}
the symmetric part

S_{*}:=\tfrac{1}{2}(J+J^{\top})
satisfies

S_{*}\;\preceq\;-\sigma\,P_{\mathcal{T}},\qquad\sigma:=\min\{\sin^{2}\theta_{j}:\ \theta_{j}\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]\}>0,

(13)

and
S_{*}=0
on
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
.

Step 2 (transverse dominance of
v
near
x^{*}
).

Since

is
C^{1}
and
v(x^{*})=0
, we have

v(x)=J(x-x^{*})+r(x),\qquad\frac{\|r(x)\|}{\|x-x^{*}\|}\to 0\ \ (x\to x^{*}).

(14)

Moreover,

(since

P_{\mathcal{T}}J=J
. Fix

and shrink

\|r(x)\|\leq\eta\,\|J(x-x^{*})\|

\|P_{\mathcal{T}}v(x)\|=\|J(x-x^{*})+P_{\mathcal{T}}r(x)\|\geq(1-\eta)\|J(x-x^{*})\|,\qquad\|v(x)\|\leq(1+\eta)\|J(x-x^{*})\|,

and therefore

\|P_{\mathcal{T}}v(x)\|\ \geq\ \kappa\,\|v(x)\|,\qquad\kappa:=\frac{1-\eta}{1+\eta}\in(0,1),\qquad\forall x\in B(x^{*},r).

(15)

Step 3 (Lyapunov inequality for

Define

where the second inequality uses (

3.2
Discrete W-domains and sliding capture

In the discrete regime (S2), where the constraint sets are finite, the RRR flow is
piecewise constant on a finite partition of the state space into W-domains,
with trajectories exhibiting attraction to domain boundaries

[
4
, Section 5.5.4, Figure 5.6]
.
The following result provides a precise formulation of this behavior using
Filippov solutions and establishes finite-time capture along convergent
boundaries.

Theorem 3.2

(Piecewise-constant flow and sliding capture)
.

Assume (S2) and
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
. Then:

(i)

There exists a finite family
\{W(a,b)\}_{(a,b)\in A\times B}
such that

(

3.3
Euler-flow approximation and hitting times

The small-step interpretation of RRR as a time-rescaled continuous flow,
observed empirically in
[
4
, Section 5.5.8]
, is justified here
by explicit error bounds for the Euler discretization and their consequences for
hitting times.

For
\varepsilon>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, define the RRR iterates

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\varepsilon v(x_{k}),~x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and let
x^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
be the piecewise-linear interpolation with

x^{\varepsilon}(k\varepsilon)=x_{k}
.

Theorem 3.3

(Euler-flow approximation)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be compact and
T>0
. Assume either

(i)

(S1) holds on an open neighborhood of
K
, or

(ii)

(S2) holds and the Filippov solution
x(\cdot)
intersects
only finitely many two-cell interfaces on
[0,T]
and satisfies

\dist(x(t),\mathcal{J})\geq\delta_{0}>0
for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\mathcal{J}

denotes the set of multiway junctions.

Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
C=C(K,T)>0
such that for all

x_{0}\in K
and
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|x^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)\|\leq C\,\varepsilon,

(17)

where
x(\cdot)
is the Carathéodory (resp. Filippov) solution of
(
2
) with
x(0)=x_{0}
.

Proof
. Let
e^{\varepsilon}(t):=x^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)
.

Step 1: Lipschitz regions.

On any open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
where
v
is
L
-Lipschitz,
e^{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\dot{e}^{\varepsilon}(t)=v(x^{\varepsilon}(t))-v(x(t)),\qquad\|\dot{e}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|\leq L\|e^{\varepsilon}(t)\|+O(\varepsilon),

for a.e.
t
away from grid points. By Grönwall,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|e^{\varepsilon}(t)\|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon.

(18)

Under (S1) this holds on a neighborhood of
K
; under
(S2) it holds on each
W
-domain away from interfaces.

Step 2: two-cell interfaces.

Let
\Sigma
be a
C^{1}
interface satisfying (
6
). By
Theorem
3.2
(ii)
, the Filippov solution satisfies

\dot{x}(t)=v_{\mathrm{slide}}
on
\Sigma
. Let
x_{k}
be such that the Euler step
crosses
\Sigma
at some
\tau\in(0,\varepsilon)
. A two-step Taylor expansion of the
Euler scheme yields

x_{k+2}-x_{k}=2\varepsilon\,v_{\mathrm{slide}}+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(19)

and the normal component of
x^{\varepsilon}
relative to
\Sigma
is
O(\varepsilon^{2})

(cf.
[
1
, Sec. 3]
). Hence

\sup_{t\in[k\varepsilon,(k+2)\varepsilon]}\|e^{\varepsilon}(t)\|\leq C_{2}\varepsilon.

(20)

Step 3: summation.

By assumption, only finitely many interfaces are crossed on
[0,T]
and junctions
are avoided. Combining (
18
) and (
20
) over the
resulting partition of
[0,T]
yields (
17
).

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Theorem 3.4

(Hitting-time convergence)
.

Assume (S1) and let
x_{0}\in U
, where
U
is as in
Theorem
3.1
. For
\delta>0
, define

T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0}):=\inf\{t\geq 0:\|v(x(t))\|\leq\delta\},\qquad t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0}):=\inf\{k\varepsilon:\|v(x_{k})\|\leq\delta\}.

Then there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
C_{\delta}>0
such that for all

0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

\bigl|t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})-T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})\bigr|\leq C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon.

(21)

Proof
. By Theorem
3.1
,

\|v(x(t))\|\leq C_{0}e^{-\mu t}\|v(x_{0})\|,\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\|v(x(t))\|\leq-\mu\|v(x(t))\|

for a.e.
t
with
v(x(t))\neq 0
. Hence

\dot{\|v(x(t))\|}\big|_{t=T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})}\leq-\mu\delta<0,

so the crossing of
\{\|v\|=\delta\}
is transversal. Fix
T>T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})
. By Theorem
3.3
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|x^{\varepsilon}(t)-x(t)\|\leq C\varepsilon.

Continuity of
v
on
U
implies the existence of
L_{\delta}>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigl|\|v(x^{\varepsilon}(t))\|-\|v(x(t))\|\bigr|\leq L_{\delta}C\varepsilon.

Therefore, for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,
x^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
crosses the band

\{\delta/2\leq\|v\|\leq 2\delta\}
exactly once, and the implicit function theorem
yields the estimate (
21
).

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

3.4
Quantitative flow limit

We summarize the asymptotic behavior of the solution time in the flow and its
discrete approximation.

Theorem 3.5

(Quantitative flow time and discrete correction)
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
. Then there exist

c_{1},c_{2}>0
,
\delta_{0}>0
, and a neighborhood
U
of
x^{*}
such that for all

x_{0}\in U
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,

T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})\ \leq\ c_{1}+c_{2}\log\!\frac{1}{\delta}.

(22)

Moreover, for the discrete RRR iteration (
1
),

t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})=T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})+O(\varepsilon),\qquad\varepsilon\downarrow 0,

(23)

where the
O(\varepsilon)
term is uniform for
x_{0}
in compact subsets of
U
.

Proof
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exist
C,\mu>0
and a neighborhood
U
of
x^{*}

such that

\|v(x(t))\|\leq Ce^{-\mu t}\|v(x_{0})\|,\qquad\forall\,x_{0}\in U,\ t\geq 0.

Fix a compact
K\subset U
and set

M:=\sup_{x_{0}\in K}\|v(x_{0})\|<\infty.

Evaluating the above bound at
t=T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})
gives

\delta\leq\|v(x(T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})))\|\leq Ce^{-\mu T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})}M,

which implies (
22
) with

c_{2}=\mu^{-1}
and
c_{1}=\mu^{-1}\log(CM)
. The discrete correction (
23
) follows directly from the
hitting-time estimate of Theorem
3.4
, which yields

|t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})-T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})|\leq C_{\delta}\,\varepsilon

uniformly for
x_{0}\in K
and
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Remark 3.6

(Flow-limit behavior of the solution time)
.

For any fixed
x_{0}\in U
and
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})=T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})<\infty.

Consequently, the flow-time stabilizes as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, while the iteration
count

k^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0}):=\frac{t^{*}_{\delta}(\varepsilon;x_{0})}{\varepsilon}

diverges at rate
O(\varepsilon^{-1})
. This separation explains the empirical
observation that small step sizes regularize solution time while inducing a
problem-dependent iteration-optimal choice of
\varepsilon
.

3.5
Discrete Lyapunov inequality for small-step RRR

Let
x^{*}\in A\cap B
be a transversal intersection in the smooth regime
(S1). Set

\mathcal{T}_{M}:=T_{x^{*}}A\cap T_{x^{*}}B,\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\perp}:=(\mathcal{T}_{M})^{\perp},

and let
P_{\mathcal{T}}
denote the orthogonal projector onto
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
.
Let
J:=Dv(x^{*})
be the Jacobian from Lemma
2.1
.

Theorem 3.7

(Local discrete Lyapunov inequality)
.

Assume (S1) and let
x^{*}\in A\cap B
be transversal. Define

J_{\mathcal{T}}:=P_{\mathcal{T}}JP_{\mathcal{T}}
. Then there exist

H_{\mathcal{T}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
c>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, and
r>0
such that:

(i)

H_{\mathcal{T}}\succeq 0
,
\operatorname{\mathrm{ker}}H_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{T}_{M}
, and

H_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\mathcal{T}^{\perp}}\succ 0
;

(ii)

for all
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|\leq r
, the RRR iterates

x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\varepsilon v(x_{k})
satisfy
\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq r
for all
k\geq 0
;

(iii)

the quadratic form

V(x):=(x-x^{*})^{\top}H_{\mathcal{T}}(x-x^{*})

(24)

satisfies

V(x_{k+1})\leq(1-c\varepsilon)\,V(x_{k})\qquad\forall k\geq 0;

(25)

(iv)

in particular,

V(x_{k})\leq e^{-ck\varepsilon}\,V(x_{0})\qquad\forall k\geq 0,

(26)

and
\|P_{\mathcal{T}}(x_{k}-x^{*})\|\to 0
exponentially in discrete time

t_{k}=k\varepsilon
.

Proof
. By Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
3.1
, the spectrum of

J_{\mathcal{T}}
satisfies
\Re\lambda(J_{\mathcal{T}})<0
on
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
,
and
J_{\mathcal{T}}=0
on
\mathcal{T}_{M}
. By the continuous-time Lyapunov theorem,
there exists
H_{\mathcal{T}}
satisfying (
(i)
) and

H_{\mathcal{T}}J_{\mathcal{T}}+J_{\mathcal{T}}^{\top}H_{\mathcal{T}}\;\preceq\;-2\gamma H_{\mathcal{T}}

(27)

on
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
for some
\gamma>0
.

Consider the linearized map
y_{k+1}=(I+\varepsilon J)y_{k}
. Then

\displaystyle V(y_{k+1})-V(y_{k})

\displaystyle=y_{k}^{\top}\!\left[\varepsilon(J^{\top}H_{\mathcal{T}}+H_{\mathcal{T}}J)+\varepsilon^{2}J^{\top}H_{\mathcal{T}}J\right]\!y_{k}.

(28)

On
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
, (
27
) and finite dimensionality imply

J^{\top}H_{\mathcal{T}}J\preceq C_{1}H_{\mathcal{T}}
for some
C_{1}>0
. Choosing

0<\varepsilon_{0}\leq\gamma/C_{1}
, we obtain

(I+\varepsilon J)^{\top}H_{\mathcal{T}}(I+\varepsilon J)-H_{\mathcal{T}}\;\preceq\;-\gamma\varepsilon H_{\mathcal{T}},

hence
V(y_{k+1})\leq(1-\gamma\varepsilon)V(y_{k})
for the linearized system. For the nonlinear RRR iteration, write

x_{k+1}-x^{*}=(I+\varepsilon J)(x_{k}-x^{*})+\varepsilon r(x_{k}),\qquad r(x):=v(x)-J(x-x^{*}).

Since
v\in C^{1}
near
x^{*}
, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\|r(x)\|\leq C_{2}\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}
locally. Expanding
V(x_{k+1})
and using
(
28
) yields

V(x_{k+1})\leq(1-\gamma\varepsilon)V(x_{k})+C_{3}\varepsilon\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{3}+C_{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{4}.

Since
H_{\mathcal{T}}|_{\mathcal{T}^{\perp}}\succ 0
, there exist
m,M>0
such that

m\|P_{\mathcal{T}}(x_{k}-x^{*})\|^{2}\leq V(x_{k})\leq M\|P_{\mathcal{T}}(x_{k}-x^{*})\|^{2}.

Choosing
r>0
sufficiently small ensures

C_{3}\varepsilon\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{3}+C_{4}\varepsilon^{2}\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{4}\leq\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\varepsilon V(x_{k})

whenever
\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|\leq r
. Thus

V(x_{k+1})\leq(1-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\varepsilon)V(x_{k}),

which gives (
25
) with
c=\gamma/2
. Invariance of
B(x^{*},r)

follows by induction, and (
26
) is obtained by iteration.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

4
Global basins, generic chains, and junctions

4.1
Smooth tubular basin

We strengthen Theorem
3.1
under uniform geometric control of the
feasible manifold
M:=A\cap B
.

Assumption 4.1

(Smooth tube)
.

Assume (S1) and that
M:=A\cap B
is compact. Suppose:

(i)

\angle(\mathcal{T}_{x}A,\mathcal{T}_{x}B)\in[\theta_{0},\pi/2]
for all
x\in M

and some
\theta_{0}>0
;

(ii)

A
and
B
have reach
\geq\rho>0
and second fundamental forms bounded by

\kappa<\infty
.

Define, for
0<r\leq\rho
,

\mathcal{S}_{r}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ \dist(x,A)\leq r,\ \dist(R_{A}x,B)\leq r\}.

(29)

Theorem 4.2

(Strict Lyapunov function on a tube)
.

Under Assumption
4.1
there exist
r\in(0,\rho]
and
\sigma>0
such that
for every solution
x(\cdot)
of (
2
) with
x(t)\in\mathcal{S}_{r}
,

\frac{d}{dt}\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\|v(x(t))\|^{2}\Bigr)\leq-\sigma\,\|v(x(t))\|^{2}.

(30)

Consequently,
\|v(x(t))\|\to 0
exponentially and
\dist(x(t),M)\to 0
as

t\to\infty
.

Proof
. For
\bar{x}\in M
, Lemma
2.1
and Assumption
4.1
(i) yield

\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(Dv(\bar{x})+Dv(\bar{x})^{\top}\bigr)\preceq-\sin^{2}\theta_{0}\,P_{\mathcal{T}_{\bar{x}}A+\mathcal{T}_{\bar{x}}B}.

Assumption
4.1
(ii) implies
P_{A},P_{B}\in C^{1}(\mathcal{S}_{r})
for
r
small,
and there exists
C<\infty
such that

\|Dv(x)-Dv(\bar{x})\|\leq Cr,\qquad x\in\mathcal{S}_{r},\ \bar{x}:=P_{M}(x).

Choosing
r>0
so that
Cr\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{0}
, the symmetric part

S(x):=\tfrac{1}{2}(Dv(x)+Dv(x)^{\top})
satisfies

S(x)\preceq-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{0}\,P_{\mathcal{T}_{\bar{x}}A+\mathcal{T}_{\bar{x}}B},\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{S}_{r}.

Let
E(x):=\tfrac{1}{2}\|v(x)\|^{2}
. Along solutions in
\mathcal{S}_{r}
,

\dot{E}(x(t))=\langle v(x(t)),Dv(x(t))v(x(t))\rangle=\langle v(x(t)),S(x(t))v(x(t))\rangle\leq-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{0}\,\|v(x(t))\|^{2},

which is (
30
) with
\sigma:=\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}\theta_{0}
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Corollary 4.3

(No recurrence in the tube)
.

Consider Assumption
4.1
. Let
x(\cdot)
be a solution of
\dot{x}=v(x)
with

x(0)\in\mathcal{S}_{r}
. Then

\omega(x)\subseteq M:=A\cap B.

In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{r}
contains no nontrivial periodic orbits and no
chaotic invariant sets of the flow.

Proof
. By Theorem
4.2
,

\dot{E}(x(t))\leq-\sigma\|v(x(t))\|^{2}<0\quad\text{whenever }v(x(t))\neq 0.

Hence
E(x(t))
is strictly decreasing along any nonconstant trajectory in

\mathcal{S}_{r}
. Therefore no point
x\in\mathcal{S}_{r}\setminus M
can belong to

\omega(x)
, and
\omega(x)\subseteq\{x:\ v(x)=0\}=M
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Remark 4.4

(Regularity in the feasible case)
.

In the feasible case
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
, Assumption
4.1
and
Theorem
4.2
yield a strict Lyapunov function

E(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|v(x)\|^{2}
on
\mathcal{S}_{r}
. Consequently, the flow restricted to

\mathcal{S}_{r}
is gradient-like: all trajectories converge to
M
, and no
recurrent dynamics occur in
\mathcal{S}_{r}
.

This is consistent with the observations in
[
4
, Chapter 5]
,
where chaotic behavior for RRR is reported only for infeasible instances
(
A\cap B=\varnothing
) or outside basins of attraction. The present results are
local: they exclude recurrence near
M
but do not address global dynamics.

4.2
Generic descent chains in the discrete case

Assumption 4.5

(Discrete genericity)
.

Assume (S2) and:

(i)

\{\|b-a\|^{2}:\ (a,b)\in A\times B\}
are pairwise distinct;

(ii)

for each
b\in B
(resp.
a\in A
), the Voronoi adjacency graph on
A

(resp.
B
) is connected;

(iii)

W-domain boundaries are in general position; in particular, all multiway
junctions have codimension
\geq 2
.

For
(a,b)\in A\times B
, define the
pair distance

d(a,b):=\|b-a\|^{2}.

Lemma 4.6

(Descent across an A-switch)
.

Let
b\in B
and let
\Sigma\subset\partial W(a_{1},b)\cap\partial W(a_{2},b)
be an
A-switch boundary. Let
n
be the unit normal to
\Sigma
oriented from

W(a_{1},b)
to
W(a_{2},b)
, and set
v_{i}:=b-a_{i}
. Define

\alpha:=\langle a_{2}-a_{1},\ b-a_{1}\rangle.

Then:

(i)

(n\!\cdot\!v_{1})(n\!\cdot\!v_{2})<0\Leftrightarrow\alpha\in(0,\|a_{2}-a_{1}\|^{2})
;

(ii)

along the sliding velocity
v_{\mathrm{slide}}
on
\Sigma
,

\frac{d}{dt}d(a(t),b)<0\quad\text{whenever }\alpha>\tfrac{1}{2}\|a_{2}-a_{1}\|^{2},

where
a(t)\in\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
denotes the active
A
-index along the sliding path.

Proof
. Since
\Sigma
is a Voronoi bisector between
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
with
b
fixed, its
normal satisfies
n=\pm(a_{2}-a_{1})/\|a_{2}-a_{1}\|
. Direct evaluation of

n\cdot v_{i}
yields (i). Statement (ii) follows since
d(a,b)
depends only on the
active index
a
, and the inequality
\alpha>\tfrac{1}{2}\|a_{2}-a_{1}\|^{2}
selects the
direction corresponding to the strictly smaller distance.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

Theorem 4.7

(Finite descent chain to a solution cell)
.

Assume Assumption
4.5
and
A\cap B\neq\varnothing
. Then for any
nonsolution cell
W(a,b)
there exists a finite sequence

W(a,b)=W(a_{0},b_{0})\to W(a_{1},b_{1})\to\cdots\to W(a_{N},b_{N})=W(a^{*},a^{*})

such that:

(i)

W(a_{j},b_{j})
and
W(a_{j+1},b_{j+1})
share a boundary satisfying
(
6
);

(ii)

d(a_{j+1},b_{j+1})<d(a_{j},b_{j})
for all
j
, and

N\leq\frac{d(a,b)-d(a^{*},a^{*})}{\Delta},\qquad\Delta:=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\neq(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})\\
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}),(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})\in A\times B\end{subarray}}|d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})-d(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime})|>0.

Consequently, any Filippov solution following these convergent boundaries reaches

W(a^{*},a^{*})
after finitely many boundary transitions.

Proof
. If
a\notin\arg\min_{a^{\prime}\in A}d(a^{\prime},b)
, Assumption
4.5
(ii) provides a
Voronoi path in
A
to some
a^{\prime}
with
d(a^{\prime},b)<d(a,b)
; by
Lemma
4.6
, one can choose A-switches along which
d
strictly
decreases. If
b\notin\arg\min_{b^{\prime}\in B}d(a,b^{\prime})
, the symmetric argument applies.
Since all values of
d
are distinct and bounded below by
d(a^{*},a^{*})
, the
number of strict decreases is finite and bounded by
(d(a,b)-d(a^{*},a^{*}))/\Delta
.

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

4.3
Nonunique projections and junctions

Let
\mathcal{M}_{A},\mathcal{M}_{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the medial axes where

are multivalued, and let
\mathcal{J}
denote the set of points where three or more

-domains intersect.

Theorem 4.8

(Negligibility of medial and junction sets)
.

Assume either (S1) with compact

or (S2) with
Assumption
4.5
. Then:

(i)

\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathcal{M}_{A})=\mathcal{L}^{m}(R_{A}^{-1}(\mathcal{M}_{B}))=0;

(ii)

in case (S2),

\mathcal{L}^{m}(\mathcal{J})=0,\qquad\mathcal{L}^{1}\!\left(\{t\geq 0:\ x(t)\in\mathcal{J}\}\right)=0

for every Filippov solution
x(\cdot)
.

Proof
. (i)
For closed
C^{2}
submanifolds (and for finite sets), medial axes have Hausdorff

, hence zero Lebesgue measure. Since

is a

Under Assumption

(iii),

\hfill\quad\blacksquare

4.4
A percolation and renormalization group model for the
\beta\uparrow 1
catastrophe

This subsection is heuristic. Its role is to formalize a mesoscopic object
that can carry the
\beta\uparrow 1
divergence picture discussed in

[
4
, Ch. 5]
and measured in §
5.1
.

Cell dynamics induced by
T_{\beta}
.

Assume (S2) and use the
W
-domain partition from
(
5
). Index the (a.e.) cells by a finite set
V
,

\mathbb{R}^{m}=\bigsqcup_{i\in V}W_{i}\quad(\text{up to a null set}),\qquad v(x)=v_{i}\ \ \text{for a.e.\ }x\in W_{i},

so that the RRR map is

T_{\beta}(x)=x+\beta v_{i},\qquad x\in W_{i}.

Fix a probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with continuous strictly positive
density. The quantity below measures how much of cell
i
is transported into
cell
j
by one step of
T_{\beta}
.

Definition 4.9

(Cell transition kernel, support digraph, and absorbing set)
.

For
\beta>0
, define

P_{\beta}(i,j):=\mu\!\left(W_{i}\cap T_{\beta}^{-1}(W_{j})\,\middle|\,W_{i}\right)=\frac{\mu\!\left(W_{i}\cap(W_{j}-\beta v_{i})\right)}{\mu(W_{i})}.

Define the support digraph
G_{\beta}=(V,E_{\beta})
by

(i\to j)\in E_{\beta}\Leftrightarrow P_{\beta}(i,j)>0.

Let

S:=\{\,i\in V:\ v_{i}=0\,\}

denote the set of solution cells (absorbing fixed points of
T_{\beta}
).

The deterministic orbit
x_{k+1}=T_{\beta}(x_{k})
induces a cell itinerary

i_{k}\in V
via
x_{k}\in W_{i_{k}}
.
The kernel
P_{\beta}
is a
coarse
model of cell-to-cell transport under a
reference distribution;
G_{\beta}
records which transitions are possible at all.
Large strongly connected regions of
G_{\beta}
represent a mechanism for long
wandering before hitting
S
, consistent with the “search phase” dominating
in §
5.1
.

Order parameter.

Consider a problem family indexed by
n
with cell sets
V_{n}
and solution sets

S_{n}\subseteq V_{n}
. Write
G_{\beta,n}
for the corresponding support digraph.

Definition 4.10

(Wandering order parameter and graph phase transition)
.

Let
\mathrm{SCC}_{\max}(G)
be a largest strongly connected component of
G
.
Define

\Phi_{n}(\beta):=\frac{1}{|V_{n}|}\Bigl|\mathrm{SCC}_{\max}\bigl(G_{\beta,n}\restriction(V_{n}\setminus S_{n})\bigr)\Bigr|.

We say the family exhibits a (directed) phase transition at
\beta_{c}\in(0,1)
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{n}(\beta)=0\ \ (\beta<\beta_{c}),\qquad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{n}(\beta)>0\ \ (\beta>\beta_{c}).

To connect
\beta\mapsto G_{\beta,n}
with standard threshold heuristics, we
randomize edges according to
P_{\beta}
as an inhomogeneous directed percolation
model
[
2
,
7
]
.

Definition 4.11

(Edge percolation on the support digraph)
.

Let
\{U_{ij}\}_{(i,j)\in V\times V}
be i.i.d.
\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]
.
Define
G_{\beta}^{\omega}
by declaring
i\to j
open iff
U_{ij}\leq P_{\beta}(i,j)
.
Equivalently, conditional on
P_{\beta}
, edges are independent with

\mathbb{P}(i\to j\text{ open})=P_{\beta}(i,j)
.

Lemma 4.12

(Monotone coupling in
\beta
)
.

If
P_{\beta}(i,j)
is nondecreasing in
\beta
for all
(i,j)
, then there exists
a coupling such that

5
Numerical Evidences

We consider the foundational framework in
[
3
]
, where the learning problem was framed as feasibility formulation. The training subproblem for a single sample is the feasibility problem

\text{find }z\in\bigcap_{v\in V}C_{v}.

For a mini-batch
\{(x^{(i)},y^{(i)})\}_{i=1}^{B}
, the framework constructs a single enlarged computation graph by
replicating all
data-dependent
nodes per sample while
sharing
parameter nodes across samples.
Thus, parameter nodes have outgoing edges into every sample replica, and
C_{p}^{\mathrm{par}}
enforces cross-sample
consensus automatically. The resulting feasibility problem can be written as

\text{find }z\in\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{B}\ \bigcap_{v\in V_{\mathrm{data}}^{(i)}}C_{v}^{(i)}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{p\in V_{\mathrm{par}}}C_{p}^{\mathrm{par}}\right).

There is a single edge-state
z
on a single
(batch-expanded) graph; the only batch coupling is through the shared parameter-node constraints. We provide an equivalence interpretation that is useful to the set-valued and variational analysis, operator splitting, and machine learning communities.

Theorem 5.1

(Two-level wire feasibility and canonical finite-sum inclusion)
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{data}}
be a finite index set of local relation types,

\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{act}}
a finite index set of activation-wire types,
and
\Theta
a finite index set of shared parameter wires.
For

let
H_{w}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d_{w}}
, and for
\theta\in\Theta
let

H_{\theta}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d_{\theta}}
.

(i)

(Local-copy wire lift).

For each replica
i\in[N]
and each relation
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{data}}
, let

\mathcal{W}(v)\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{act}}\cup\Theta
and define

H_{i,v}:=\prod_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}(v)}H_{\omega},\qquad M_{i,v}\subseteq H_{i,v}.

Set

\mathcal{H}:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\ \prod_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{data}}}H_{i,v},\qquad x=(x_{i,v})\in\mathcal{H}.

(ii)

(Set-feasibility formulation).

Define the product of local relations

\mathcal{M}:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\ \prod_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{data}}}M_{i,v}\subseteq\mathcal{H}.

For each
(i,w)\in[N]\times\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{act}}
, let
\Delta_{i,w}
be the diagonal subspace enforcing
equality of all copies of wire
(i,w)
across
\{x_{i,v}\}_{v}
.
For each
\theta\in\Theta
, let
\Delta_{\theta}
enforce equality of all copies of
\theta
across all replicas.
Define

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{wire}}:=\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\ \prod_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{act}}}\Delta_{i,w}\Big)\times\Big(\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\Delta_{\theta}\Big)\subseteq\mathcal{H}.

The two-level wire feasibility problem is

\text{find }x\in\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{wire}}.

(iii)

(Finite-sum operator inclusion).

Assume all
M_{i,v}
are nonempty and closed, and let
N_{M}
denote the normal cone of

Then the feasibility problem in (ii) is equivalent to the inclusion

0\in\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ \sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{data}}}N_{M_{i,v}}(x)\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ \sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{act}}}N_{\Delta_{i,w}}(x)\;+\;\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}N_{\Delta_{\theta}}(x),\qquad x\in\mathcal{H},

(31)

In particular,
J_{N_{M_{i,v}}}=P_{M_{i,v}}

6
Conclusion and outlook

This paper develops a rigorous dynamical-systems description of the
Reflect-Reflect-Relax (RRR) algorithm in the small-step regime, centered on
the limiting flow
\dot{x}=v(x)
governing the behavior of RRR as

\beta\downarrow 0
.

In the smooth transversal setting (S1), each feasible intersection

x^{*}\in A\cap B
is shown to be a hyperbolic sink of the flow, with strictly
negative real spectrum of
Dv(x^{*})
on the transverse subspace and exponential
decay of the gap
g(x)=\|v(x)\|
(Theorem
3.1
). Under uniform
geometric assumptions, this local structure extends to a positively invariant
tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{T}_{r}
of the feasible manifold, where

E(x)=\tfrac{1}{2}\|v(x)\|^{2}
is a strict Lyapunov function and no recurrent or
chaotic dynamics occur (Theorem
4.2
,
Corollary
4.3
).

In the discrete regime (S2), the limiting vector field is
piecewise constant on
W
-domains. We showed that convergent two-cell
boundaries support Filippov sliding with explicit sliding velocity, and that,
under genericity assumptions, finite descent chains of
W
-domains lead to
finite-time capture into a solution cell (Theorem
3.2
).

The continuous and discrete descriptions are connected by proving that
small-step RRR is a forward-Euler discretization of the flow with

O(\beta)
trajectory error on finite time intervals, including across sliding
segments (Theorem
3.3
). As a consequence, hitting times of any fixed
gap threshold
\delta>0
satisfy

t^{*}_{\delta}(\beta;x_{0})\;\to\;T^{*}_{\delta}(x_{0})\quad\text{as }\beta\downarrow 0,

while the iteration count diverges like
1/\beta

(Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
). A discrete Lyapunov inequality
further yields exponential convergence rates for the iterates consistent with
the flow analysis (Theorem
3.7
).

Numerical experiments on a one-layer LEDM instance support this picture
(§
5.1
), showing that local convergence follows the predicted
Euler scaling once a regular regime is reached, while overall runtime is
dominated by a distinct mesoscopic entry phase whose dependence on
\beta
is
non-local and instance-dependent. This separation clarifies both the scope of
the flow-limit theory and the origin of the deterioration observed near the
Douglas-Rachford limit
\beta\uparrow 1
.

Outlook.

Extending the analysis to global phase portraits of the RRR flow and map,
particularly in the infeasible case
A\cap B=\varnothing
where numerical
evidence suggests complex invariant sets, chaotic attractors, and long
transients, remains open. In the discrete setting, while generic finite descent
chains were established, a quantitative theory relating geometric properties of
the
W
-domain adjacency graph (connectivity, diameter, expansion, recurrent
classes) to entry times, mixing times, coupling times, or hitting-time
distributions is largely undeveloped. Moreover, numerical evidence indicates
that the regime
\beta\uparrow 1
is governed by non-local,
instance-dependent wandering and metastability not captured by local flow
analysis; developing rigorous criteria for such
\beta
-driven transitions and
relating them to coarse-grained or renormalized descriptions of the induced cell
dynamics is an important direction for future work.

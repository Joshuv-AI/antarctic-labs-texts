Nonrelativistic limit of normalized solutions of nonlinear Dirac equations on noncompact metric graphs with localized nonlinearities
Zhentao He, Chao Ji

Abstract




2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35R02, 81Q35, 58E05, 35Q40

ABSTRACT

In this paper, we study the nonrelativistic limit of normalized solutions for the following nonlinear Dirac equation (NLDE) on noncompact metric graph
\mathcal{G}
with finitely many edges and a non-empty compact core
\mathcal{K}

\mathcal{D}u-\omega u=\chi_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u,

under the constraint
\int_{\mathcal{G}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=1
, where
\mathcal{D}
is the Dirac operator on
\mathcal{G}
,
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
, the frequency
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is part of the unknowns which arises as a Lagrange multiplier,
\chi_{\mathcal{K}}
is the characteristic function of the compact core
\mathcal{K}
, and
2<p<6
. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the nonrelativistic limit of normalized solutions to (NLDE) on metric graphs.

1.
Introduction

Throughout the paper, we consider a connected noncompact metric graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathrm{V},\mathrm{E})
, where
\mathrm{E}
is the set
of edges and
\mathrm{V}
is the set of vertices. If
\mathcal{G}
is a metric graph with a finite number of vertices, its compact core
\mathcal{K}
is defined as the metric subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
consisting of all the bounded edges. We assume that
\mathcal{G}
has a finite number of edges and that its compact core
\mathcal{K}
is non-empty. Each bounded edge
e
is identified with a closed and bounded interval
I_{e}=[0,\ell_{e}]
,
\ell_{e}>0
, while each unbounded edge
e
is identified with a copy of
I_{e}=\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,+\infty)
and is called half-line. A connected metric graph has the natural structure of a locally compact metric space, the metric being given by the shortest distance measured along
the edges of the graph. Moreover, a connected metric graph with a finite number of vertices is compact if and only if it contains no half-lines.

Quantum graphs (metric graphs equipped with differential operators) arise naturally as simplified models in mathematics, physics, chemistry, and engineering when one considers propagation of waves of various nature through a quasi-one-dimensional (e.g., meso- or nano-scale) system that looks like a thin neighborhood of a graph. The Dirac operator on metric graphs is denoted by

(1.3)

\mathcal{D}_{c}:=-\imath c\frac{d}{dx}\otimes\sigma_{1}+mc^{2}\otimes\sigma_{3}

where

represents the mass of the generic particle of the system and

(1.4)

\sigma_{1}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&1\\

0&-1\end{array}\right).

Consider the following nonlinear Dirac equation (NLDE) on metric graphs
\mathcal{G}

(1.5)

\imath\partial_{t}\psi=\mathcal{D}_{c}\psi+\lvert\psi\rvert^{p-2}\psi,

[

34

[

1.5

\mathcal{D}_{c}u-\omega u=\lvert u\rvert^{p-2}u.

In

8

&\int_{R^{3}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=1,\end{aligned}\right.

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
,
\partial_{k}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
,
m,c>0
,
P\in(2,\frac{8}{3})
,
P\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\}

and some certain assumptions,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
,

are
4\times 4
Pauli-Dirac matrices:

\alpha_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma_{k}\\

with

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\

and the frequency
\omega\in\mathbb{R}

The authors proved the existence of normalized solutions by combining reduction and perturbation arguments. First, they introduced a perturbation functional

associated with the (NLDE) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the corresponding reduced functional

are parameters, and showed that

possesses the mountain pass geometry structure. Next, they constructed test-functions from the non-trivial periodic solutions to the following equation

whose existence is guaranteed via Fourier transform (see

18

and the sequence of Lagrange multipliers associated with the Palais-Smale. Third, the authors showed, for

of

and

possesses a subsequence which converges to a non-trivial solution to (NLDE) in

as

. Finally, they established a non-existence result, which enabled them to obtain the existence of normalized solutions.
He and Ji

27

[
18
]
—by developing a new approach to build suitable test functions.
This allowed them to estimate the minimax level of the associated functional and to control the sequence of Lagrange multipliers associated with the Cerami sequences.

A natural problem is to study the nonrelativistic limit of normalized solutions for (NLDE) on metric graphs. To the best of our knowledge, this problem has not been investigated in the existing literature, and the present paper provides the first attempt to address it within the framework of metric graphs. In
[
16
]
, Chen, Ding, Guo and Wang studied the nonrelativistic limit of normalized solutions for the following (NLDE) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-ic\sum_{k=1}^{3}\alpha_{k}\partial_{k}u+mc^{2}\beta u-\omega u=\Gamma*(K|u|^{\kappa})K|u|^{\kappa-2}u+P|u|^{p-2}u,\\
&\int_{R^{3}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\,dx=1,\end{aligned}\right.

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{4}
,
\partial_{k}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
,
*
stands for the convolution,
m,c>0
,
\kappa\in[2,\frac{7}{3})
,
p\in(2,\frac{8}{3}]
,
\Gamma\in C(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\{0\},(0,+\infty))
and
K,P\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},(0,+\infty))
satisfying some certain assumptions,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
,
\beta
are
4\times 4
Pauli-Dirac matrices, and the frequency
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is part of the unknowns which arises as a Lagrange multiplier. They proved the existence of normalized solutions
(u_{c},\omega_{c})
for (NLDE) in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
when the relativistic parameter

is sufficiently large and the Lagrange multipliers

-\infty<\liminf_{c\to+\infty}(\omega_{c}-mc^{2})\leq\limsup_{c\to+\infty}(\omega_{c}-mc^{2})<0.

Next, using the Fourier transform, the authors proved that the second components of
(u_{c})
converge to zero, estimated the energy (of the functional associated with the Schrödinger equations) of the first components of
(u_{c})
, and then, the authors concluded that that the first components of
(u_{c})
converge to

in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{2})
and

and, for any

, we instead employ the inequality

Now, we recall some basic settings of Dirac equations on metric graphs, as introduced in
[
8
,
9
]
.
Consistently, a function
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}
is actually a family of functions
(u_{e})
, where
u_{e}:I_{e}\to\mathbb{C}
is the restriction of
u
to the edge
e
. The usual
L^{p}
spaces on the metric graph are defined as follows
as

L^{p}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}L^{p}(I_{e})

with norms

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}^{p}:=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{p}(I_{e})}^{p},\quad\text{ if }p\in[1,\infty),\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G})}:=\max_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(I_{e})},

while the Sobolev spaces
H^{m}(\mathcal{G})
are defined as

H^{m}(\mathcal{G}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}H^{m}(I_{e})

with norms

\lVert u\rVert_{H^{m}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lVert u^{(i)}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}.

Accordingly, a spinor
u=(u^{1},u^{2})^{T}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
is a family of
2
-spinors

u_{e}=\binom{u_{e}^{1}}{u_{e}^{2}}:I_{e}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2},\quad\forall e\in\mathrm{E},

and thus

L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}L^{p}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2}),

endowed with the norms

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{p}:=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{p}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{p},\quad\text{ if }p\in[1,\infty),\quad\text{ and }\quad\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}:=\max_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})},

whereas

H^{m}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2}):=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathrm{E}}H^{m}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})

with the norms

\|u\|_{H^{m}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}:=\sum_{e\in\mathrm{E}}\lVert u_{e}\rVert_{H^{m}(I_{e},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}.

For convenience, we often abbreviate
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}
as
\lVert u\rVert_{p}
and
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}
as
\lVert u\rVert_{H^{1}}
.

Note that, in the case of (NLSE), the condition that
u
is continuous on
\mathcal{G}
is often contained in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. However, in the case of (NLDE), we shall keep the conditions of spinors at the vertices separate, as defined in the following.

Definition 1.1

(
[
8

)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a metric graph and

. We call the Dirac operator with Kirchhoff-type vertex conditions the operator
\mathcal{D}_{c}:L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})
with action

(1.11)

{\mathcal{D}_{c}}_{\left.\right|_{I_{e}}}u=\mathcal{D}_{c,e}u_{e}:=-\imath c\sigma_{1}u_{e}^{\prime}+mc^{2}\sigma_{3}u_{e}\quad\forall e\in\mathrm{E},

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3}
being the matrices defined in (
1.4
), and the domain of
D
given by

\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c}):=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2}):u\text{ satisfies \eqref{eqdefdom1} and \eqref{eqdefdom2}}\}

where

(1.12)

u_{e}^{1}(\mathrm{v})=u_{f}^{1}(\mathrm{v})\quad\forall e,f\succ\mathrm{v},\quad\forall\mathrm{v}\in\mathcal{K},

(1.13)

\sum_{e\succ v}u_{e}^{2}(\mathrm{v})_{\pm}=0\quad\forall\mathrm{v}\in\mathcal{K},

"

Note that, it follows from a direct computation that, for all

\lVert\mathcal{D}_{c}u\rVert_{2}^{2}=c^{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}.

We now recall some basic properties of the Dirac operator
\mathcal{D}_{c}
, as discussed in
[
8
]

\mathcal{D}_{c}
is self-adjoint on

, and its spectrum is given by

(1.15)

\sigma{(\mathcal{D}_{c})}=(-\infty,-mc^{2}]\cup[mc^{2},+\infty).

Next, we define the associated quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}}
and its domain
\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}})
as follows,

\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2}):\int_{\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{c})}|\nu|d\mu_{u}^{\mathcal{D}_{c}}(\nu)<\infty\right\},\quad\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}}(u):=\int_{\sigma(\mathcal{D}_{c})}\nu d\mu_{u}^{\mathcal{D}_{c}}(\nu),

where
\mu_{u}^{\mathcal{D}_{c}}
denotes the spectral measure associated with
\mathcal{D}_{c}
and

. Additionally,
\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}})
is a closed subspace of

with respect to the norm induced by

. As a consequence of Sobolev embeddings, it follows that

\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})\quad\forall p\in[2,\infty)

and that, in addition, the embedding
\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{D}_{c}})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{C}^{2})

. For simplicity, we denote

It is possible to define the inner product on

(u,v)_{c}=\Re(\lvert\mathcal{D}_{c}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}u,\lvert\mathcal{D}_{c}\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}v)_{2},

where

\lVert u\rVert_{c}=(u,u)_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

From (

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{c}\geq mc^{2}\lVert u\rVert_{2}^{2}.

According to (

L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})=L^{-}_{c}\oplus L^{+}_{c},

where

L^{-}_{c}:=\left\{P^{-}_{c}u:u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})\right\}\text{ and }L^{+}_{c}:=\left\{P^{+}_{c}u:u\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})\right\},

Moreover, we can decompose the form domain

as the orthogonal sum of the positive and negative spectral subspaces for the operator

As a result, every

b_{a}:=\lVert\varphi_{a}^{\prime}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert\left(\varphi^{1}_{a}\right)^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\frac{\lVert\left(\tilde{\varphi}^{1}_{a}\right)^{\prime}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}}{\lVert\tilde{\varphi}^{1}_{a}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}}=\frac{Na}{\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert}
. By repeating the argument of (
3.10
) and replacing
I_{e_{0}}
with
\mathcal{K}
, we deduce that

\int_{\mathcal{K}}\left\lvert\frac{\varphi^{+}_{a,c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{a,c}\rVert_{2}}+h\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}_{a,c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{a,c}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\,dx\geq\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\lVert\varphi_{a}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{K},\mathbb{C})}^{p}+o(1)=\frac{\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert}{\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}+o(1),

as
c\to+\infty
, where
\varphi^{+}_{a,c}=P^{+}_{c}\varphi_{a}
. By repeating the arguments of (
3.11
) and (
3.12
), we conclude

J_{c}\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}\right)\leq\frac{mc^{2}}{2}+\frac{b_{a}}{4m}-\frac{\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert}{\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}+o(1){\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}\quad\text{as }c\to+\infty,

and

\displaystyle e_{c}\leq J_{c}\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{mc^{2}}{2}+\frac{b_{a}}{4m}-\frac{\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert}{\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}+o(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{mc^{2}}{2}+\frac{Na}{4m\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)}-\frac{\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert}{\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}+o(1){\color[rgb]{0,0,1},}\quad\text{as }c\to+\infty.

Since
2<p<4
, we know
\frac{Na}{4m\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)}-\frac{\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert}{\left(\frac{N}{3a}+\lvert\mathcal{K}\rvert\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}}<0
for some
a>0
small enough. Thus, there exists a constant
C_{0}(m,p,\mathcal{G})>0
depending only on
m,p
and
\mathcal{G}
such that
e_{c}\leq mc^{2}-C_{0}(m,p,\mathcal{G})+o(1)
as
c\to+\infty
.
∎

Next, we prove that, if for all sufficiently large
c>0
, there exists a normalized solution
(u_{c},\omega_{c})\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c})\times[0,mc^{2}-\sigma]
of (
NLDE
)
c
with
I_{0,c}(u)<\frac{mc^{2}}{2}
, then
(u_{c})
are uniformly bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C})
and
mc^{2}-\omega_{c}
is bounded from above as
c\to+\infty
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
2<p<6
. For any
\sigma>0
, if
mc^{2}>\sigma
and
(u_{c},\omega_{c})\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c})\times[0,mc^{2}-\sigma]
is a normalized solution of (
NLDE
)
c
such that
I_{0,c}(u)<\frac{mc^{2}}{2}
, then

\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}<C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G}),

where
C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})>0
is a constant that denpends only on
\sigma,m,p
and
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover, if for all sufficiently large
c>0
, there exists a normalized solution
(u_{c},\omega_{c})\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c})\times[0,mc^{2}-\sigma]
of (
NLDE
)
c
with
I_{0,c}(u)<\frac{mc^{2}}{2}
, then

\omega_{c}\geq mc^{2}-2S_{p,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+o(1),\quad\text{as }c\to+\infty.

Proof.

It follows from
\langle I_{0,c}^{\prime}(u_{c}),u_{c}\rangle=\omega_{c}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}
and
I_{0,c}(u)<\frac{mc^{2}}{2}
that

(3.13)

\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{p}\,dx=I_{0,c}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\langle I_{0,c}^{\prime}(u_{c}),u_{c}\rangle<\frac{mc^{2}}{2}-\frac{\omega_{c}}{2}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

By (
1.14
), we obtain

(3.14)

c^{2}\lVert u^{\prime}_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}+m^{2}c^{4}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\lVert\mathcal{D}_{c}u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}=\omega_{c}^{2}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}+2\omega_{c}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{2p-2}\,dx.

Then, by
m^{2}c^{4}-\omega_{c}^{2}\geq 2mc^{2}\sigma-\sigma^{2}>mc^{2}\sigma>0
, (
3.13
) and Lemma
2.2
, one has

\displaystyle c^{2}\lVert u^{\prime}_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}+mc^{2}\sigma\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle<2\omega_{c}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{2p-2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega_{c}S_{p,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}+S_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle<2\omega_{c}S_{p,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+\frac{2pmc^{2}}{p-2}S_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

that is,

\min\{1,m\sigma\}\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}<\left(\frac{2\omega_{c}S_{p,\mathcal{K}}}{c^{2}}+\frac{2pmS_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}}{p-2}\right)\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}<\left(2mS_{p,\mathcal{K}}+\frac{2pmS_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}}{p-2}\right)\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

whence, it follows from
2<p<6
that
\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}<C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})
, where

C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G}):=\max\{m^{\frac{2}{6-p}},\sigma^{\frac{2}{p-6}}\}\left(2S_{p,\mathcal{K}}+\frac{2pS_{\infty,\mathcal{K}}^{p-2}}{p-2}\right)^{\frac{2}{6-p}}.

Next, by (
3.14
) and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle m^{2}c^{4}-\omega_{c}^{2}=(m^{2}c^{4}-\omega_{c}^{2})\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}\lVert u^{\prime}_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}+(m^{2}c^{4}-\omega_{c}^{2})\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\omega_{c}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u_{c}\rvert^{2p-2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega_{c}S_{p,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}+S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{p-2}\lVert u_{c}\rVert_{2}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega_{c}S_{p,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{p-2},

that is

\omega_{c}^{2}+2\omega_{c}S_{p,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{p-2}\geq m^{2}c^{4}.

Therefore,
\omega_{c}\to+\infty
as
c\to+\infty
, and thus

\displaystyle\omega_{c}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m^{2}c^{4}}{\omega_{c}}-2S_{p,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}-\frac{1}{\omega_{c}}S_{2p-2,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{p-2}

\displaystyle\geq mc^{2}-2S_{p,\mathcal{K}}C(\sigma,m,p,\mathcal{G})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}+o(1),

as
c\to+\infty
.
∎

Now, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It follows from Lemma
3.4
that
e_{c}\leq mc^{2}-C_{0}(m,p,\mathcal{G})+o(1)
as
c\to+\infty
for all
2<p<4
and
e_{c}\leq\frac{mc^{2}}{2}+\frac{b}{4m}-\frac{1}{p}\ell_{e_{0}}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}+o(1)
as
c\to+\infty
for all
4\leq p<6
. Since
m>m_{0}(p,\ell_{e_{0}})
, we know that, there exist
c_{1}>0
and
\sigma>0
depending only on
m,p
and
\mathcal{G}
such that, for all
c>c_{1}
, we have
e_{c}\leq\frac{mc^{2}-\sigma}{2}
. By Lemma
3.1
, for any
c>c_{1}
, there exists
u_{c}\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c})
such that
I_{0,c}(u)\leq e_{c}<mc^{2}
, and

(i)

either
u_{c}
is a critical point of
I_{0,c}
constrained on
S_{c}(1)
with Lagrange multiplier
\omega\in[0,2e_{c}]
.

(ii)

or
u_{c}
is a critical point of
I_{0,c}
constrained on
S_{c}(\nu)
for some
0<\nu<1
with Lagrange multiplier
\omega=0
.

By
[
27
, Lemma A.2]
, we know that, there exists
c_{0}\geq c_{1}
depending only on
m,p
and
\mathcal{G}
such that, for all
c>c_{0}
, case (ii) above cannot occur. Thus, for any
c>c_{0}
, there exists a non-trivial
u_{c}\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c})
and
\omega_{c}\in[0,mc^{2})
such that
(u_{c},\omega_{c})
is a normalized solution of (
NLDE
)
c
and
\omega_{c}\leq mc^{2}-\sigma
. By Lemma
3.5
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

In a view of
[
8
, Section 4.2]
, to prove Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to prove that, the solutions
(u_{c},\omega_{c})
of (
NLDE
)
c
, obtained in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, satisfy
(u_{c})
are uniformly bounded in

as

, and this is guaranteed by Lemma

.
Now, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, for
m>m_{0}(p,\ell_{e_{0}})
and
c>c_{0}
, we obtain a sequence of solutions
((u_{c},\omega_{c}))
of (
NLDE
)
c
with
I_{0,c}(u)<\frac{mc^{2}}{2}
and

-\infty<\liminf_{c\to+\infty}(\omega_{c}-mc^{2})\leq\limsup_{c\to+\infty}(\omega_{c}-mc^{2})<0.

Then, by Lemma
3.5
, we know that
(u_{c})
is bounded in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C})
. Let
(c_{n})
be a real sequence satisfying

as

. Then, up to a subsequence, there is

such that
\omega_{c_{n}}-mc_{n}^{2}\to\frac{\lambda}{m}
as

. By repeating the argument in

, Section 4.2]
, we conclude that

and

in

as

, where

is a solution of the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation

Then,
u_{c_{n}}\to(g,0)^{T}
in

∎

4.
Further remarks

In this section, we first show that the existence result of normalized solution to (
NLSE
) on
\mathcal{G}
established in Theorem
1.3
is compatible with the related studies, and we then provide a possibility to prove
(g,\lambda)
, the normalized solution of (
NLSE
) on
\mathcal{G}
obtained in Theorem
1.3
, is a ground state normalized solution of (
NLSE
) on
\mathcal{G}
.

Define

S_{\mu}:=\left\{u\in\tilde{H}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}):\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C})}=\mu\right\},

E_{m}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert u^{\prime}\rVert^{2}_{2}-\frac{2m}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert u\rvert^{p}\,dx,\quad\forall u\in\tilde{H}^{1}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}),

and

\mathcal{E}^{\mu}_{m}:=\inf_{u\in S_{\mu}}E_{m}(u).

Since

E_{m}(u)=\frac{(2m)^{\frac{-2}{p-2}}}{2}\left\lVert(2m)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}u^{\prime}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}-\frac{(2m)^{\frac{-2}{p-2}}}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert(2m)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}u\rvert^{p}\,dx=(2m)^{\frac{-2}{p-2}}E_{1}\left((2m)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}u\right),

we know that the minimization problem
\mathcal{E}_{m}:=\mathcal{E}_{m}^{1}
has a solution if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\mu_{m}}
has a solution, where
\mu_{m}=(2m)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}
. Then, results in
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
can be easily extend to the problem
\mathcal{E}_{m}
with little modification.

1.
Let
4\leq p<6
. For
\varphi^{1}
defined as in Lemma
3.4
, we have

E_{m}(\varphi^{1})=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\pi}{\ell_{e_{0}}})^{2}-\frac{2m}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\lvert\varphi^{1}\rvert^{p}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\pi}{\ell_{e_{0}}})^{2}-\frac{2m}{p}\ell_{e_{0}}^{1-\frac{p}{2}}
. Thus,
\mathcal{E}_{m}<0
if
m>m_{0}(p,\ell_{e_{0}})
. Then, it follows from
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
that
\mathcal{E}_{m}
has a solution if
m>m_{0}(p,\ell_{e_{0}})
. Therefore, the existence of solutions to (
NLSE
) in Theorem
1.3
is compatible with the existence result
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
and the nonexistence result
[
32
, Theorem 3.2]
.

2.
If we can replace (
3.10
) by

\int_{\mathcal{K}}\left\lvert\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}+h_{c}\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\,dx\geq\int_{\mathcal{K}}\left\lvert\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}\right\rvert^{p}\,dx+o(1),

then it is possible to show
(g,\lambda)\in\tilde{H}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{R}
obtained in Theorem
1.3
is a ground state normalized solution of (
NLSE
) by repeating the argument in
[
16
, Lemma 4.4]
,
e_{c}\leq\frac{mc^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mathcal{E}_{m}}{2m}+o(1)
as
c\to+\infty
, and Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
hold when
\mathcal{E}_{m}<0
(
\mathcal{E}_{m}<0
holds for all
2<p<4
with
m>0
or
4\leq p<6
with
m>0
large enough, see
[
33
]
).

To this end, we know that
\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}+h_{c}\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}\right)\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{D}_{c})
by the self-jointness of
\mathcal{D}_{c}
, but it seems difficult to give an upper bound to
H^{1}
-norms of
\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}+h_{c}\left(\frac{\varphi^{+}_{c}}{\lVert\varphi^{+}_{c}\rVert_{2}}\right)
. An alternative method is to prove

\min\{mc^{2},c\}\lVert u\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{C}^{2})}^{2}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{c}^{2},

however, in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, this inequality is proved by Fourier transform, which is absent on metric graphs, so it remains unclear whether the inequality holds on metric graphs, where only real interpolation theory or spectrum theory in
[
8
]
are available.

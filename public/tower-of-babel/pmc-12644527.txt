Modeling migration intentions under environmental stress through push pull dynamics and policy effects
Authors: Menghan Yu, Muhammad Asif, Qianru Zhang, Muhammad Shahbaz, Chaudhry Saeed Ahmad, Muhammad Farrukh Shahzad
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEnvironmental stress has emerged as a critical driver of population displacement, yet its interaction with migration intentions remains underexplored. This study investigates how environmental vulnerability shapes individuals’ intentions to migrate, drawing on the push–pull theory of migration. Data were collected from residents of high-risk environmental zones in China and analyzed using a dual-stage approach. The findings reveal that environmental stress and perceived economic opportunities significantly influence migration intentions, with perceived risk mediating the effect of environmental stress. Moreover, policy awareness moderates the relationship between perceived economic opportunity and migration intention. The results highlight environmental stress as the most influential factor, underscoring the importance of proactive policies to reduce involuntary migration and strengthen community resilience. This study contributes to migration research by integrating environmental, economic, and policy perspectives, offering valuable insights for both scholars and policymakers.Keywords: Environmental stress, Migration, Perceived risk, Policy awareness, SEM-ANNSubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Geography, Geography

Introduction

In recent decades, environmental stressors such as droughts, floods, land degradation, and pollution have become increasingly influential in shaping human mobility patterns around the world. Climate-induced disruptions are no longer sporadic phenomena but have emerged as persistent challenges that affect livelihoods, economic stability, and the overall quality of life1. In many developing countries, including China, environmentally vulnerable regions are witnessing a rise in migration flows as residents seek safety, stability, and economic opportunity elsewhere2–5. While some of these movements are temporary or seasonal, others lead to permanent internal relocation, often resulting in demographic and socio-economic transformations.

In China, environmental degradation has intensified in numerous provinces, particularly in the western and inland areas such as Inner Mongolia, Gansu, Ningxia, and Yunnan6,7. These areas face chronic environmental challenges, including desertification, water scarcity, and recurrent flooding6,8. In response, individuals and households are increasingly considering migration as a survival and adaptation strategy9. However, environmental stress is rarely the sole trigger for migration10. People weigh a complex combination of factors, including environmental, economic, psychological, and policy-related, before deciding to move. Despite growing academic interest in climate-induced migration, limited empirical research exists that holistically models these factors in the Chinese context using advanced analytical approaches.

Beyond China, similar dynamics are evident in other environmentally vulnerable regions such as South Asia and Africa. Studies from Bangladesh, India, and Pakistan highlight how floods, droughts, and riverbank erosion directly increase migration pressures, while limited economic opportunities further accelerate displacement11–13. Likewise, research in Sub-Saharan Africa shows that recurrent droughts and land degradation are strong push factors, especially when coupled with weak institutional support14,15. Integrating insights from these regions helps situate the Chinese case within a broader global context of environmental migration.

Methodologically, prior research on environmental migration has increasingly applied advanced techniques such as SEM and ANN to capture complex causal relationships and non-linear effects16,17. SEM has been widely used to model psychological and structural determinants of migration intentions, while ANN provides additional predictive power by ranking the importance of multiple predictors18. However, only a few studies combine these approaches, particularly in the context of environmental stress, leaving a gap that this study addresses.

In response to this gap, this study explores the push and pull dynamics influencing migration intention within China, focusing specifically on environmental stress and perceived economic opportunities. While environmental stress serves as a push factor19, economic opportunity in urban and coastal areas functions as a pull20,21. However, these factors are filtered through the lens of individual perceptions and awareness. Perceived risk, such as the fear of livelihood loss or exposure to climate-related hazards, can mediate how environmental stress translates into migration intentions22,23. Additionally, policy awareness, such as knowledge of government relocation assistance or climate adaptation programs, can shape or moderate these relationships. Yet, the existing literature has paid little attention to the interplay of these factors in an integrated framework.

Grounded in the push–pull theory of migration, this study conceptualizes environmental stress and economic opportunity as key structural drivers influencing individuals’ decisions to relocate24,25. The push–pull framework is particularly relevant in this context because it captures how unfavorable living conditions (push factors) and perceived opportunities elsewhere (pull factors) jointly shape migration intentions under environmental vulnerability. To enrich this structural perspective, the study incorporates the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)26, which emphasizes the role of cognitive and attitudinal factors in shaping behavioral intentions. TPB is especially valuable here as it explains how perceptions of risk and control mediate individuals’ responses to environmental threats, thereby linking external stressors to internal decision-making processes27. By integrating these two theories, the study offers a novel perspective that connects structural pressures with psychological evaluations, providing a more holistic understanding of migration intentions in environmentally stressed contexts. The key objective of this work is to model the effect of environmental stress and perceived economic opportunity on migration intention in China, while examining the mediating role of perceived risk and the moderating effect of policy awareness. To achieve this, the study employs a hybrid partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) and ANN approach. SEM is used to test the hypothesized linear relationships among constructs28, while ANN captures potential nonlinear patterns and identifies the most influential predictors29.

This research contributes to migration studies in three important ways. First, it develops an empirically tested, theory-driven model of environmental and economic drivers of migration intention in China. Second, it integrates psychological and policy dimensions, often overlooked in quantitative migration research. Third, it utilizes an advanced SEM-ANN hybrid methodology to provide both explanatory and predictive insights. The findings offer practical value for policymakers and planners concerned with climate adaptation, population distribution, and sustainable urbanization strategies.

Conceptual basis and hypotheses

Pull–push theory of migration

This theory provides a basic structure for understanding the drivers behind migration decisions25,30. According to this theory, migration results from the amalgamation of push factors, such as environmental degradation, natural disasters, and loss of livelihoods in the origin area, and pull factors, such as economic opportunities, better living conditions, and employment prospects in the destination area31. Environmental stressors, including floods, droughts, and pollution, act as push factors that compel individuals to consider relocation, particularly in regions where adaptive capacity is low4. Conversely, perceived economic opportunities in urban or safer areas serve as pull factors that attract potential migrants seeking better prospects32.

In the context of China, internal migration has been increasingly influenced by environmental pressures, particularly in rural and ecologically vulnerable areas33. Recent research has shown that environmental stress is a significant predictor of migration intentions, especially when individuals perceive viable alternatives in economically dynamic regions34. Therefore, the push–pull theory provides a strong conceptual basis for modeling how environmental stress and economic opportunity jointly influence migration intentions.

Theory of planned behaviour

The TPB posits that behavioral intention is shaped by three central components: attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control26. This framework has been widely applied to migration studies, offering insights into how cognitive and contextual factors shape decision-making35,36. In this study, the TPB is operationalized through two key constructs. First, perceived risk, including concerns about climate-induced disasters, livelihood insecurity, and future uncertainty, is treated as a mediator that influences individuals’ attitudes toward migration. When risks are perceived as high, individuals are more likely to develop favorable attitudes toward migration as a protective strategy.

Second, policy awareness, such as knowledge of relocation programs, subsidies, and migration-related regulations, is conceptualized as a moderator influencing perceived behavioral control. Awareness of institutional support increases individuals’ confidence in their ability to migrate successfully, whereas limited awareness may reduce perceived feasibility. By linking these constructs to TPB, this study extends the framework to the environmental migration context, where external stressors and institutional conditions shape both the rational motivations and the psychological readiness to migrate. Integrating TPB with the push–pull perspective thus provides a more comprehensive understanding of migration intentions under environmental stress. Environmental stress and migration intention

Environmental stress refers to adverse ecological conditions such as droughts, floods, air and water pollution, and land degradation that negatively impact human livelihoods and well-being37,38. In regions facing frequent or severe environmental disruptions, people may experience increased vulnerability, economic instability, and deteriorating health conditions, prompting them to consider migration as a coping or adaptation strategy39. In China, environmental challenges such as desertification in the north and flooding in the south have significantly affected rural populations, leading to displacement and internal migration40. According to the push–pull theory of migration, such stressors function as powerful push factors, forcing individuals to migrate in pursuit of safety and stability41. Empirical evidence suggests that environmental stress not only influences immediate relocation decisions but also shapes long-term migration intentions, especially when individuals perceive their current living conditions as increasingly untenable42.

Recent studies further emphasize that climate change intensifies these pressures by creating prolonged droughts, unpredictable rainfall, and extreme weather events that destabilize agricultural livelihoods and increase forced mobility4,43. Evidence from South Asia shows that recurrent flooding and sea-level rise in Bangladesh and India have accelerated migration flows, often as long-term adaptation strategies44,45. Similarly, in Sub-Saharan Africa, climate-induced land degradation has been strongly linked with both temporary and permanent migration decisions46,47. These findings suggest that environmental stress not only influences immediate relocation decisions but also shapes intergenerational intentions, as families increasingly view migration as a pathway to resilience. Accordingly, we suggest the following:

H1

Environmental stress positively influences migration intention.

Perceived economic opportunity and migration intention

Perceived economic opportunity refers to individuals’ beliefs about the availability of better income, employment prospects, and living standards in potential destination areas48. According to the push–pull theory of migration, economic incentives are among the most significant pull factors that motivate people to relocate, especially from rural to urban areas or from ecologically stressed to economically vibrant regions32. In the Chinese context, rapid urbanization and regional economic disparities have made urban centers highly attractive for rural residents seeking improved livelihoods49. When individuals believe that migration can offer them stable jobs, higher wages, or better access to services, their intention to migrate tends to increase50. Moreover, such perceptions can outweigh environmental or social hardships at the origin, making economic opportunity a powerful motivator. Studies have constantly shown a strong association between perceived economic benefits and migration decisions51,52. Hence, individuals who perceive greater economic opportunities elsewhere are more likely to develop migration intentions. Therefore, we propose the following:

H2

Perceived economic opportunity positively influences migration intention.

Perceived risk

Perceived risk refers to individuals’ subjective judgment about the potential negative consequences of remaining in or moving from their current environment, such as the risk of environmental hazards, livelihood loss, or social instability53,54. In the migration decision-making process, perceived risk can shape how individuals interpret both push (e.g., environmental stress) and pull (e.g., economic opportunity) factors25. According to the TPB, intention is influenced not only by attitudes and benefits but also by individuals’ evaluation of risks and barriers55. When environmental stress is present, individuals who perceive high risk to their safety, income, or quality of life are more likely to consider migration as a necessary adaptation strategy10. Conversely, if the perceived risk is low, even significant environmental stress or economic opportunity may not trigger strong migration intentions. Thus, perceived risk may act as a psychological mechanism through which external factors are translated into behavioral intentions. Prior research supports the notion that risk intermediates the association between environmental or economic drivers and migration-related behaviors35,56. Hence, we propose the following:

H3

Perceived risk mediates the association between environmental stress and migration intention.

H4

Perceived risk mediates the association between perceived economic opportunity and migration intention.

Policy awareness

Policy awareness is a person’s understanding of government policies, programs, or incentives related to migration, relocation, or disaster response57. Under these circumstances, awareness of supportive policies, such as relocation subsidies, urban settlement assistance, or employment schemes, can significantly influence how individuals assess the costs and benefits of migration. According to the TPB, external factors like institutional support influence perceived behavioral control, which in turn affects intention58. When people are aware of government support structures, they may feel more confident and secure in pursuing migration as a viable option59. Conversely, limited awareness may create uncertainty or perceived barriers, weakening the influence of stress or opportunity on migration intentions56. In China, where policy instruments vary across regions, the level of awareness among rural and environmentally affected populations can critically determine migration behavior60.

International research shows that in contexts such as Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, awareness of disaster relief and relocation programs has directly shaped people’s willingness to migrate, particularly where climate risks are high61. In Bangladesh, households aware of government-led adaptation programs are more likely to consider migration as a long-term strategy62. In Europe, awareness of integration policies has been shown to reduce uncertainty and increase mobility among vulnerable populations63. Domestically, variations across Chinese provinces demonstrate that policy awareness plays a critical moderating role: migrants who are informed about settlement schemes and urban employment programs perceive lower barriers to relocation and exhibit stronger migration intentions64,65. These findings highlight that policy awareness can directly facilitate migration by enhancing confidence in institutional support, and indirectly by reducing the risks and uncertainties associated with relocation. Accordingly, we propose the following:

H5

Policy awareness moderates the association between economic opportunity and migration intention.

Methodology

Research design

This study adopts a quantitative, cross-sectional design to examine the association between environmental stress and migration intention among individuals in China, using SEM-ANN analysis. The design enables both hypothesis testing and identification of the most significant predictors of migration intention29. A structured survey was established to assemble data from individuals residing in environmentally vulnerable regions. The nature of the study allows for a snapshot of current attitudes and behaviors related to environmental stress, perceived economic opportunity, perceived risk, and policy awareness. The dual-stage SEM-ANN approach ensures both statistical rigor and predictive accuracy in understanding the dynamics of migration intention66.

Population and sampling

This study focuses on individuals living in environmentally vulnerable regions of China, particularly areas frequently affected by floods, droughts, and pollution. These individuals are considered at risk of displacement and are likely to evaluate migration as a response to environmental stress. A purposive sampling was used to identify and survey respondents with direct exposure to such environmental conditions. This approach ensures the collection of relevant and context-specific data from participants who can meaningfully reflect on migration intentions influenced by environmental and economic factors67,68. The sampling process focused on communities within provinces such as Sichuan, Henan, and Guangdong, where environmental challenges are well-documented.

Measurement of constructs

All variables were assessed with adapted multi-item scales, with minor contextual adjustments for relevance in China. Four items from Lamb and Kwok69 were used to measure environmental stress. Perceived economic opportunity was evaluated through four items modified from Khaw, Thurasamy70, capturing expectations of improved employment prospects and income in other regions. Perceived risk was measured using three items from Şengül71, reflecting personal vulnerability to climate-related hazards and livelihood insecurity. Policy awareness was measured by adapting four items from Sohail72, focusing on awareness of government migration policies and relocation assistance programs. Finally, migration intention was measured using four items adapted from Pervez, Ishida73, capturing the individual’s willingness and plans to migrate. All items used a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 to 5. Table 1 shows items for each construct used in the study, along with their respective measurement results.

Data collection

For data collection, a questionnaire was distributed online and in person across three environmentally vulnerable regions of China: Henan, Sichuan, and Hebei. These regions were purposively selected as “high-risk environmental zones” because of their frequent and recurrent exposure to natural hazards such as floods, droughts, and severe pollution, which threaten agricultural production, livelihoods, and overall living conditions. The classification of “high-risk” was based on official reports of disaster frequency, ecological vulnerability, and local government risk assessments. Furthermore, socio-economic considerations, such as dependence on agriculture, rural residency, and limited access to adaptive infrastructure, were also taken into account in the selection process, as these factors make populations particularly sensitive to environmental stress and more likely to consider migration as an adaptation strategy.

A pilot study involving 30 participants preceded the main survey to ensure clarity, reliability, and contextual appropriateness. All participants were clearly informed about the study’s objectives and assured of their anonymity. Data collection occurred during May and June 2025. The study distributed 400 questionnaires electronically (e.g., emails and WeChat). In line with Hair, Ringle74, a minimum sample size of 190 respondents was considered adequate, based on the rule of thumb of 10 responses per indicator (19 items × 10). Of the 337 responses received, 66 were excluded due to incomplete answers, patterned responses (straight-lining), or inconsistent demographic information. Consequently, the final analysis was based on 271 valid responses. Table 2 shows the profile details of participants.

Data analysis techniques

The data analysis was conducted using a two-stage approach combining SEM-ANN techniques18. In the first stage, SEM was applied using SmartPLS 4 to assess the measurement and structural models, enabling the evaluation of relationships among the latent variables, including path coefficients, reliability, and validity measures75. SEM was chosen because of its strength in theory testing and in modeling direct, indirect, and moderating effects among latent constructs76. However, one limitation of SEM is that it assumes linear relationships, which may overlook complex, non-linear interactions among variables18.

In the second stage, IBM SPSS was utilized to implement the ANN model, allowing for the identification and ranking of the most significant predictors of migration intention77. ANN is particularly useful for its predictive accuracy and its ability to capture non-linear and hidden relationships in the data66. However, its disadvantage is that it operates as a “black box,” offering less interpretability compared to SEM29. By combining the two methods, this hybrid SEM-ANN approach leverages the explanatory power of SEM and the predictive strength of ANN, enhancing the robustness and practical relevance of the study’s findings78. For implementation, SEM was run with 5000 bootstrap resamples for significance testing, while the ANN model was trained using a multilayer perceptron with two hidden layers, sigmoid activation functions, and a 90:10 training-to-testing ratio to ensure reliability and avoid overfitting.

Results

Common method bias (CMB)

To confirm the integrity of the data, it was important to assess the potential for CMB before proceeding with the main analysis. Two tests were employed: the collinearity test and Harman’s single-factor test. Following the guidelines by Kock79, the variance inflation factor (VIF) was examined for all constructs, with values ranging from 1.427 to 2.464, well below the threshold of 5, indicating no issue of multicollinearity. Additionally, Harman’s single-factor test was applied to detect any dominant factor that could account for the majority of variance. A single factor explained 45.23% of the total variance, under the 50% cut-off, suggesting CMB is not an issue. To further validate, we applied the Unmeasured Latent Method Construct (ULMC) approach by adding a latent method factor linked to all indicators. The comparison of models with and without the method factor revealed no significant improvement in model fit, and substantive path coefficients remained stable. Thus, method variance does not pose a major concern in this study. These findings support the robustness of the responses collected from individuals in environmentally vulnerable regions of China regarding their migration intentions.

Measurement model results

Establishing validity is a key step in confirming the correctness and reliability of measurement constructs80. To assess convergent validity and the reliability of the constructs used in this study, we examined factor loadings, Cronbach’s alpha (α), composite reliability (CR), and average variance extracted (AVE). All standardized item loadings exceeded the recommended threshold of 0.7, confirming indicator reliability. As shown in Table 1, each construct demonstrated an AVE above 0.5, and CR values were greater than 0.7, reflecting strong convergent validity and internal consistency. Furthermore, all α values surpassed the acceptable minimum, supporting the reliability of the measurement scales. Discriminant validity assessed using the Fornell-Larcker criterion, where the square root of each construct’s AVE was higher than its correlation with other constructs81, as presented in Table 3. In addition, Heterotrait-Monotrait (HTMT) ratios ranged from 0.460 to 0.836, well below the conservative threshold of 0.85, further verifying that discriminant validity was achieved76.

Structural model results

The bootstrapping technique was used to resample with 5000 samples and a 95% bias adjustment to assess the significance of the structural path coefficients82. Table 4 illustrates and visualizes in Fig. 2 the results reveal that environmental stress has a positive effect on migration intention (β = 0.201, p = 0.006), supporting H1. Similarly, perceived economic opportunity also shows a positive relationship with migration intention (β = 0.092, p = 0.043), supporting H2. The mediation analysis indicates that perceived risk significantly mediates the association between environmental stress and migration intention (β = 0.125, p = 0.019), confirming H3. However, the mediation effect of perceived risk between perceived economic opportunity and migration intention is not statistically significant (β = 0.017, p = 0.114); thus, H4 is not supported. Finally, the moderating effect of policy awareness on the relationship between perceived economic opportunity and migration intention is significant (β = 0.087, p = 0.018), providing support for H5. These findings suggest that environmental stress plays a direct and indirect role (via perceived risk) in shaping migration intentions. At the same time, the influence of economic opportunity is more nuanced, being moderated by individuals’ awareness of policy support.

Artificial neural network analysis

In this study, an ANN was employed as a data-driven computational technique inspired by the structure and functioning of the human brain. ANN has gained popularity in recent years as a machine learning method capable of identifying complex, non-linear patterns in data85. It is particularly useful for predictive research that lacks a strong theoretical foundation and requires minimal interpretation of causal mechanisms68. While ANN does not aim to explain causal relationships due to its “black box” nature29,86,87. It is highly effective in determining the relative importance of input variables in predicting outcomes. ANN also provides robustness in handling noise, outliers, and smaller datasets, validating linear and non-linear relationships88. We used IBM SPSS Neural Networks to build a feed-forward backpropagation multilayer ANN model. The model used sigmoid functions with hidden neurons set automatically85. Discussion

This study examined the key predictors of migration intention in the context of environmental and socio-economic challenges. Drawing on PLS-SEM and ANN analysis, we found that environmental stress proved to be the key driver of migration intention, in terms of statistical significance and relative importance. These findings are consistent with earlier studies that emphasize the growing influence of environmental degradation, climate change, and ecological threats as core drivers of human mobility39,40. The high importance of environmental stress in the ANN model reinforces the argument that environmental threats are not just peripheral factors but central to shaping people’s decisions to relocate.

Policy awareness emerged as the second most influential factor in the ANN model, supporting the findings of Tjaden and Gninafon59 and Su, Hu60. Informed individuals are better equipped to evaluate risks and opportunities, making migration decisions within clearer policy frameworks. However, its impact is not uniform across regions. In economically developed areas of China, higher levels of policy dissemination and institutional support may enhance individuals’ confidence in accessing relocation programs or urban settlement schemes. Conversely, in underdeveloped regions, limited awareness and weaker policy communication can reduce perceived feasibility, thereby weakening the moderating effect of policy awareness on migration intentions. This highlights the need for region-specific strategies to ensure that policy information effectively reaches vulnerable populations.

The outcomes also recommend that perceived risk significantly influences migration intention. This supports previous findings by Zaman, Sohel25 and Czaika and Reinprecht10, which shows that migration decisions often reflect the perceived threats to safety, livelihood, and long-term sustainability. When people sense that remaining in their current location exposes them to unacceptable risks, migration becomes a rational and precautionary action.

Interestingly, perceived economic opportunity was the least influential factor among the four predictors. This contrasts with traditional migration literature that emphasizes economic pull factors93,94. However, this finding reflects a growing shift where environmental and institutional factors are beginning to outweigh purely economic motivations in certain regions. It also suggests that in the presence of severe environmental stress or robust policy awareness, economic opportunities alone may not be sufficient to retain populations.

Furthermore, the integration of ANN with PLS-SEM strengthens the robustness of these findings. While PLS-SEM confirmed the significance of the relationships, ANN provided a nonlinear perspective and ranked the predictors in terms of their relative importance and contribution to prediction accuracy95. This methodological triangulation is in line with recommendations from Sharma, Sarstedt89 and Ali, Johl92, who advocate for hybrid approaches to capture both causal relationships and predictive performance.

The findings of this study extend migration theory by demonstrating how structural and cognitive factors jointly influence migration intentions. Consistent with the push–pull theory, environmental stress operates as a strong push factor, reinforcing earlier studies that link ecological disruptions to migration behavior25,30,31. At the same time, the integration of the TPB highlights the social-psychological mechanisms underlying these intentions, particularly the role of perceived risk and policy awareness in shaping attitudes and perceived behavioral control. This dual-theoretical approach adds depth by showing that migration is not only a reaction to external pressures but also a decision filtered through individual perceptions and institutional awareness. These results align with prior research emphasizing the interplay between context and cognition26,35,36, but they further reveal how this relationship manifests in environmentally vulnerable regions of China, offering a more nuanced explanation of migration intentions than single-theory perspectives.

Theoretical implications

This study advances migration theory by integrating the push–pull framework and the TPB within the context of environmental migration. The results confirm that environmental stress and perceived risk are stronger predictors of migration intentions than economic opportunity, challenging prior migration studies that emphasize economic drivers as primary determinants. This finding suggests a theoretical refinement: in environmentally vulnerable contexts, psychosocial and ecological factors may outweigh economic considerations.

By extending the TPB, this study demonstrates that perceived risk mediates attitudes toward migration, while policy awareness moderates perceived behavioral control. This extends prior TPB applications by showing how institutional cognition (awareness of relocation policies, subsidies, and support programs) conditions migration intentions. Theoretically, this highlights that migration decisions are not only shaped by material conditions but also by subjective perceptions of vulnerability and institutional trust, thus bridging structural and cognitive perspectives.

Finally, the study contributes to environmental migration literature by reinforcing that environmental change is both a physical driver and a socially constructed experience. Perceptions of risk, more than objective conditions alone, significantly determine migration intentions, underscoring the importance of psychological and policy-related dimensions in migration theory.

Practical implications

The findings offer several actionable insights for policymakers. Since environmental stress directly and indirectly shapes migration intentions through risk perceptions, interventions must combine climate adaptation measures such as resilient infrastructure and ecological restoration with effective risk communication strategies that reduce fear and uncertainty. Although economic opportunity plays a weaker role than environmental stress, it remains a significant factor. Governments should therefore invest in local job creation, vocational training, and entrepreneurship support in vulnerable areas to mitigate economic push factors and provide viable alternatives to migration. At the same time, the moderating effect of policy awareness indicates that migrants who are informed about relocation programs and subsidies make more structured and rational decisions. This underscores the importance of transparent communication campaigns and accessible support systems, particularly in rural and ecologically fragile regions.

The study also highlights the practical utility of predictive tools for migration policy. The integration of SEM and ANN offers a data-driven approach to anticipating migration flows and identifying high-risk populations, enabling policymakers to design targeted interventions that balance environmental resilience with socio-economic development. In the Chinese context, these insights suggest focusing on provinces such as Gansu, Ningxia, and Inner Mongolia, where ecological stress is acute. Key priorities include desertification control, climate-smart agriculture, and strengthened rural social protection measures such as relocation subsidies, affordable housing, inclusive healthcare, and educational support for migrant families. Moreover, strengthening localized policy communication through trusted institutions and community-based channels can ensure that vulnerable populations are better informed and less likely to engage in involuntary migration.

Limitations and avenues for further study

The use of cross-sectional data limits the ability to draw causal conclusions. Future research could benefit from employing longitudinal or panel data to examine how migration intentions shift over time in response to evolving environmental, economic, or policy factors. The study relies on self-reported responses, which may be influenced by biases such as social desirability or memory inaccuracies. Future studies could incorporate mixed-methods approaches, combining surveys with qualitative interviews or ethnographic observations for deeper insights.

The geographically restricted sample may limit the generalizability of the results. Future studies should include participants from diverse regions or countries with distinct socio-political and environmental contexts to test and refine the model’s applicability. While this study utilized ANN to enhance prediction accuracy, the “black box” nature of ANN limits interpretability. Future work could explore explainable AI techniques or compare ANN with other machine learning models to balance prediction with interpretability. Finally, although key factors such as environmental stress, perceived risk, policy awareness, and economic opportunity were examined, other potential drivers, including social networks, political instability, and cultural ties, were not considered. Future studies could incorporate such variables to build a more comprehensive understanding of migration decision-making.

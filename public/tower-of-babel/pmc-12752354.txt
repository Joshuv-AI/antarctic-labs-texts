Integrated analysis of transcriptome and metabolome reveals the mechanism of lignin biosynthesis in fruit abscission of Chinese bayberry (Myrica rubra)
Authors: Zheping Yu, Li Sun, Yang Zhang, Senmiao Liang, Haiying Ren, Zengqun Zhou, Yang Song, Xiliang Zheng, Xiuzhu Guo, Xingjiang Qi, Shuwen Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractChinese bayberry is a distinctive fruit tree native to southern China. However, it suffers from severe fruit abscission at mature stage, while the molecular mechanism of which remain unclear. In this study, two varieties (BQ and DA) with significant difference in fruit abscission were performed as the materials, and the results indicated the lignin content of fruit stalk was considered as the major cause of fruit abscission in Chinese bayberry. BQ with low fruit abscission rates exhibited thicker and shorter fruit stalks, along with correspondingly higher lignin content in the fruit stalk, when compared to DA. On this basis, the molecular mechanism of lignin biosynthesis through integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses was further investigated. 65 DEGs and 29 DAMs were identified to be potentially involved in lignin biosynthesis. Among those DEGs and DAMs, nine structural genes (MrCOMT1/2/3, MrCCR1/2/3, Mr4CL1/2, and MrCAD) and four important metabolites (coniferaldehyde, coniferyl alcohol, sinapaldehyde, and 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol) associated with lignin biosynthesis were identified through enrichment pathway analysis and qPCR validation. Overall, this study revealed the regulatory network composing key DEGs and DAMs involved in lignin biosynthesis, thereby providing valuable insights into the mechanism of fruit abscission and informing the molecular breeding efforts in Chinese bayberry.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07752-8.Keywords: Chinese bayberry, Fruit abscission, Lignin biosynthesis, Mechanism

Introduction

Chinese bayberry (Myrica rubra), an evergreen fruit tree of the genus Myrica L. in the Myricaceae family, is native to China and serves as a typical economic fruit tree in southern China [1]. Known for its sweet and sour fruit with a unique flavor, Chinese bayberry is valued for its high nutritional and medicinal properties [2, 3]. However, severe fruit abscission at mature stage significantly reduces both yield and quality, adversely affecting economic returns and seriously endangering the industrial development of Chinese bayberry. Among the four main varieties of Chinese bayberry, including Biqi (BQ), DongKui, DingAo (DA), and WanDao, the fruit abscission varies significantly, being the highest in DA and the lowest in BQ at mature stage [4]. Despite these observations, the molecular mechanisms underlying fruit abscission in Chinese bayberries remain unknown.

The fruit stalk connecting the fruit to the branches is crucial for fruit retention. As the fruit matures, degradation of the fruit stalk cell wall leads to fruit abscission [5]. The primary components of cell wall consist of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin [6], with lignin being the second most abundant terrestrial biopolymer [7]. Lignin serves as important structural component of cell wall, playing vital roles in support, substance transport, and pathogen resistance [8, 9]. Therefore, investigating the biosynthesis and regulatory network of lignin metabolism in the fruit stalk of Chinese bayberry is essential for understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying the fruit abscission in this species.

In citrus, multiple studies have demonstrated the key role of lignin biosynthesis in pedicel abscission [10–12]. The abscission zone (AZ) is the primary site where fruit abscission occurs, and studies indicated that lignin and its biosynthesis are likely involved in regulating this process in the AZ [13]. Significant lignin deposition has been observed in the AZ, along with the specific expression of genes closely associated with lignin biosynthesis, such as Cit4CL and Cit4CCR1, suggesting the involvement of lignin deposition and its biosynthesis in citrus fruit abscission [11]. Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses have revealed that the lignin biosynthesis pathway played crucial roles in regulating pedicel abscission in pepper, potentially involving key genes such as CAD, CCR, and COMT [14]. Furthermore, another multi-omics integrated analysis in blue honeysuckle has revealed a potential link between lignin biosynthesis and fruit abscission, showing significant differences in the lignin synthesis pathway among cultivars with varying abscission degrees [15].

The lignin biosynthesis involves several important enzyme genes, including caffeic acid O-methyltransferase (COMT), caffeoyl-CoA O-methyltransferase (CCoAOMT), cinnamoyl-CoA Reductase (CCR), cinnamoyl alcohol dehydrogenase (CAD), and 4-coumarate: CoA Ligase (4CL) [7, 9]. COMT, a methylase in the lignin biosynthetic pathway, catalyzes the methylation of hydroxylated lignin precursors [16]. The inhibition of COMT activity in transgenic poplar significantly reduced the lignin content [17], while silencing COMT2 and COMT3 in switchgrass results in an 11.6%−12.0% decrease in total lignin content [18]. CCoAOMT is another methyltransferase responsible for methylating the hydroxyl groups on the aromatic ring within lignin biosynthesis pathway [16, 19]. Moreover, the lignin biosynthetic pathways are related to certain key genes such as CCR, CAD and 4CL. CCR is a key enzyme involved in the biosynthesis of lignin-specific pathway, which catalyzes the formation of cinnamaldehyde from CoA esters of various hydroxycinnamic acids, thus playing critical roles in the lignin biosynthesis [20, 21]. CAD is the enzyme in lignin biosynthesis by reducing the cinnamaldehyde to produce the corresponding cinnamyl alcohol [22, 23]. 4CL, the terminal enzyme in the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway [24], plays important regulatory roles in lignin biosynthesis [25, 26].

The integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis has become a crucial method for exploring important genes, metabolites, and the corresponding regulatory networks in plants [27–29]. This approach has been utilized to investigate the lignin biosynthesis in Sesuvium portulacastrum [30] and bamboo shoots [31]. Recently, genomic, transcriptomic, and metabolomic studies of Chinese bayberry have rapidly increased [1, 32, 33]. However, there are few studies on the lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry using multi-omic analyses.

This study employed integrated analysis of transcriptome and metabolome to identify important DEGs and DAMs associated with lignin biosynthetic pathways in Chinese bayberry for clarifying their roles in the fruit abscission. This study further explored the regulatory network involving DEGs and DAMs within this pathway, providing a crucial foundation for the unraveling of molecular mechanisms underlying the fruit abscission of Chinese bayberry.

Results

Significant difference in the fruit abscission of BQ and DA

The fruit stalk of BQ and DA at MS exhibited significant morphological differences (Fig. 1 A). The BQ fruit stalk was shorter and thicker, while the fruit stalk of DA was longer and thinner, with the BQ fruit stalk being 1.625 times thicker than DA (Fig. 1B, C). The thicker and stronger fruit stalk of BQ can better support the fruit. The cross-sections of the fruit stalk abscission zone at different stages (WS, RS, and MS) were compared (Fig. 1D), indicating that the xylem in the BQ fruit stalk was more lignified than that in the DA at MS. Furthermore, the lignin content in the fruit stalk increased with fruit maturation, with the BQ fruit stalk exhibiting a 35.6% higher lignin content at MS compared to DA (Fig. 1E). Additionally, the fruit abscission rates for BQ and DA were recorded over three consecutive years (2021, 2022, and 2023). The results revealed that DA consistently had higher fruit abscission rates than BQ in all three years (Fig. 1 F). Specifically, in 2023, the abscission rates were 59.4% for DA and 23.7% for BQ at MS.

Transcriptomic data quality analysis

Given the close relationship between fruit abscission and lignin content in the fruit stalk at MS, the high-throughput transcriptomic sequencing was performed on the fruit stalk samples at MS. A total of six samples were analyzed, generating 163.53 million reads and 45.26 Gb of raw data, with an average of over 7.54 Gb per sample. The GC content ranged from 45.88% to 47.29%, and the Q30% ranged from 93.65% to 94.38% (Table S2). The consistency in the data across different replicates indicated that the quality of sequencing is high.

DEGs in fruit stalk of BQ and DA

Three thousand, nine hundred thirty-three DEGs were identified between BQ and DA, with 2663 up-regulated and 1270 down-regulated (Fig. 2A). The corresponding volcano plots are shown in Fig. 2B. The results of PCA showed that three major principal components, PC1, PC2, and PC3, contributed to 40.86%, 18.97%, and 17.61% of the variance, respectively (Fig. 2 C). The correlation analysis demonstrated the intra-group correlation coefficients r2 for BQ and DA ranging from 0.875 to 0.969 and 0.836 to 0.916, respectively (Fig. 2D), reflecting the strong correlation among the sample replicates. The results of clustering analysis classified these 3933 DEGs into 11 clusters, with the number of DEGs in each cluster shown in Fig. 2E.

GO enrichment analysis

GO enrichment analysis indicated these DEGs were primarily associated with biological process, molecular function, and cellular component (Fig. 3). In biological process, DEGs were predominantly enriched in the categories related to response to stimulus, biological process, and response to stress (Fig. 3 A). In molecular function, DEGs were primarily enriched in the process of catalytic activity, molecular function, transferase and hydrolase activities (Fig. 3B). In cellular component, DEGs between BQ and DA were mainly enriched in cell part and cellular component (Fig. 3 C).

KEGG enrichment analysis

The enrichment analysis of KEGG identified 1345 DEGs distributed across 132 different pathways. The statistics of top 20 pathways enrichment are displayed in Fig. 4A. The plant-pathogen interaction pathway had the highest number of DEGs, totaling 169, including 65 up-regulated and 55 down-regulated genes. Additionally, 112 DEGs were enriched in plant hormone signal transduction, 99 in starch and sucrose metabolism, and 65 in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis (Fig. 4B).

The enrichment analysis of KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathways between BQ and DA fruit stalks at MS. A Top 20 KEGG pathways in DEGs. B DEGs distribution of top 20 pathways of KEGG

Lignin biosynthesis is the crucial component of phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, thus the analysis focused on this pathway. 65 DEGs were mainly enriched in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway, comprising 24 up-regulated genes and 41 down-regulated genes (Fig. 4B). The preliminary analysis of the transcriptomic sequencing results assessed the expression of these 65 DEGs in BQ and DA (Fig. S1), including key genes involved in the lignin biosynthesis pathway, such as COMT, CCR, and 4CL.

Metabolomic analysis

The LC-QTOF-based non-target metabolomic analysis identified 2469 DAMs, comprising 1329 up-regulated and 1140 down-regulated DAMs (Fig. 5 A). The PCA results demonstrated that the contribution rates of PC1, PC2, and PC3 were 75.50%, 8.62%, and 6.42%, respectively (Fig. S2), indicating the strong inter-sample reproducibility and notable differences between two groups. The enrichment analysis of KEGG pathway presented that DAMs were predominantly associated with the biosynthesis of phenylpropanoids, purine metabolism, flavonoid biosynthesis, and tryptophan metabolism (Fig. 5B). The biosynthesis of phenylpropanoids pathway had the highest quantity of DAMs, with 29 DAMs accounting for 7.18% of the total, while the other three major KEGG pathways were enriched with 18, 17, and 17 DAMs, respectively (Fig. 5 C).

Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyse

To systematically analyze the metabolic pathways and transcriptional regulation mechanisms of lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry, the integrated transcriptomics and metabolomics was performed. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was used to analyze the DEGs between BQ and DA, and four main modules were identified (Fig. 6 A). Further analysis of the correlation of gene and metabolite modules revealed that 5 metabolite modules (MEturquoise, MEmagenta, MEred, MEblack, and MEbrown) were significantly positively correlated with 13 gene modules (P < 0.01), while 3 metabolite modules (MEturquoise, Mepink, and MEgrey) were significantly negatively correlated with 11 gene modules (P < 0.01) (Fig. 6B; Table S3). Moreover, the results displayed that DEGs and DAMs were primarily enriched in the Phenylpropanoid Biosynthesis, the Plant hormone signal transduction, and the starch and sucrose metabolism (Fig. 6 C). Due to the critical role of Phenylpropanoid Biosynthesis in the lignin biosynthesis, 58 DEGs and 7 DAMs were enriched in this pathway (Fig. 6D; Table S4, S5). Furthermore, four major DAMs, including coniferaldehyde, 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol, coniferyl alcohol, sinapaldehyde, were identified in the co-expression network and were associated with 12, 8, 7, and 3 DEGs, respectively (Fig. S3).

Lignin biosynthesis pathway analysis

The key genes and metabolites of lignin biosynthesis within the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway were further analyzed. The results displayed the significant differences in gene expression of three COMT, three CRR, two 4CL and one CAD genes in the lignin biosynthesis pathway between BQ and DA (Fig. 7). Specifically, the expressions of MrCOMT1, MrCCR1, MrCCR2, Mr4CL1, and MrCAD were significantly higher in BQ than in DA, whereas MrCOMT2, MrCOMT3, MrCCR3, and Mr4CL2 were lower in BQ. Additionally, four major metabolites were identified in the pathway of lignin biosynthesis. The accumulation of coniferylaldehyde, coniferyl alcohol, and sinapaldehyde was higher in BQ than in DA, whereas the accumulation of 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol was lower in BQ than in DA (Fig. 7).

Verification of candidate genes of lignin biosynthesis by qRT-PCR

To identify the expression levels of candidate genes involved in the pathway of lignin biosynthesis, the qRT-PCR was conducted. Based on the transcriptomic data, seven DEGs were considered as the candidate genes involved in the lignin biosynthesis. As shown in Fig. 8, the expression of MrCOMT1 in BQ increased gradually during fruit maturation, reaching 295% higher than that in DA at MS, while the expression of MrCOMT2 and MrCOMT3 was lower in BQ than in DA. However, their expressions in BQ at RS were higher than that in DA. Throughout all the stages, the expression of MrCCR1 in BQ was obviously higher than that in DA. The expression of MrCCR2 in BQ at RS and MS was 152% and 222% higher than that in DA, respectively. Conversely, the expression level of MrCCR3 in BQ was obviously lower than that in DA at all stages. As for Mr4CL1, its expression in BQ was consistently higher than that in DA across all stages, being twice as high at MS. Furthermore, the expression of both Mr4CL2 and MrCAD between BQ and DA at MS were not significantly different(Fig. 8).

Discussion

Lignin closely associated with fruit abscission in Chinese bayberry

Chinese bayberry has experienced severe fruit abscission at MS, and the extent of fruit abscission can vary significantly among varieties [4]. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying this phenomenon remain unclear. In this study, we selected BQ to exhibit the mild fruit abscission and DA to exhibit the severe fruit abscission for investigating the molecular mechanism. The fruit stalk that connected the fruit to the branch was analyzed, and the results showed that BQ had a significantly shorter but thicker fruit stalk than DA, thereby providing stronger mechanical support for fruit bearing. Additionally, the lignin content in fruit stalk increased during fruit maturity, with BQ exhibiting significantly higher lignin levels than DA at MS, which suggesting that the lignin content in the fruit stalk was closely related to fruit abscission at MS in Chinese bayberry.

Previous studies have reported that in citrus, the pedicel length, circumference (thickness), and weight of non-abscised fruits were greater than those of abscised fruits [34], which partially aligns with the present study-cultivar BQ, characterized by thicker pedicels, exhibited lower abscission, though it had shorter pedicels. Cross-sectional analysis of pedicels further revealed cell degradation and abscission zone formation in abscised fruits, suggesting this may be a key factor leading to fruit drop [34]. Additionally, in pear cv. ‘Punjab Beauty’, non-abscised flower stalks showed greater length, girth, and weight compared to abscised ones [35]. In Muscadine, the size of AZ, pedicel length, and fruit size were negatively correlated with fruit detachment force (FDF), implying that longer pedicels were associated with reduced abscission, a phenomenon linked to lignin and cellulose biosynthesis during maturation [36]. These findings appear partly inconsistent with the present observation that longer pedicels correlated with higher abscission, which may be attributed to structural differences in pedicels and AZs among fruit species. However, this interpretation requires further validation through histopathological sections or related experimental assays.

Identification of key genes of lignin biosynthesis by transcriptomic/metabolomic integrated analysis

Previous studies displayed that 52 DEGs and 130 SDMs participated in the pathway of lignin biosynthesis in S. portulacastrum under salt stress [30], with the KEGG analysis indicating that these genes and metabolites were primarily enriched in the biosynthesis of phenylpropanoid, secondary metabolites, and other pathways. Consistent with these findings, this study employed integrated transcriptomics and metabolomics and identified the biosynthesis pathway of phenylpropanoid, which was closely related to the lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry. A total of 65 DEGs and 29 DAMs were identified as potentially participated in the lignin biosynthesis. Moreover, co-expression network analysis was a good approach that can elucidate the interaction between important genes and metabolites [37, 38]. In this study, the interaction between four major DAMs (coniferaldehyde, 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol, coniferyl alcohol, sinapaldehyde) and key DEGs were confirmed, and this co-expression network may play crucial roles in lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry.

Important genes and metabolites involved in lignin biosynthesis of Chinese bayberry

Lignin is crucial for structure, water transport, and defense in plants [39, 40], and the lignin biosynthesis is a complex and dynamic process [41]. Recent studies have showed that the expressions of key genes involved in lignin accumulation was closely linked to the lignin biosynthesis pathway [17, 41, 42]. Specifically, the lignin content in BQ fruit stalk at MS was obviously higher than that in DA, with notable differences in the expression of structural genes such as COMT, CRR, and 4CL between these two varieties.

It was reported that the inhibition of COMT expression in forage [43], Lolium perenne L. [21] and Zea mays L. [44] has significantly reduced the lignin content in their progeny. In this study, the expression of MrCOMT1 increased gradually during fruit maturity and was significantly higher in BQ than in DA at MS. In contrast, the expression of MrCOMT2 and MrCOMT3 in BQ was obviously lower than that in DA at MS. Therefore, it is speculated that MrCOMT1 positively regulated the lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry, whereas MrCOMT2 and MrCOMT3 could have negative regulatory effects.

The inhibition of CCR expression in plants has been shown to significantly reduce lignin content or alter its composition [40, 45–48]. In this study, the expression of MrCCR1 and MrCCR2 was significantly higher in BQ than in DA at MS, whereas the expression of MrCCR3 was significantly lower in BQ than in DA at all stages. This suggested that MrCCR1 and MrCCR2 positively regulated the lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry, whereas MrCCR3 may negatively regulate the lignin biosynthesis. Additionally, Mr4CL1, known to be involved in lignin biosynthesis [49], was expressed at higher levels in BQ than in DA, with expression levels increasing as fruit matured.

In the lignin biosynthesis pathway of Chinese bayberry, key metabolites, such as coniferyl alcohol, coniferaldehyde, sinapaldehyde and 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol, were identified, with significant differences in their accumulation between BQ and DA. Coniferyl alcohol, an important lignin monomer, polymerizes to form G-lignin [7, 9]. Coniferaldehyde and sinapaldehyde, precursors for coniferyl alcohol and sinapyl alcohol, respectively, are both located the downstream in the lignin biosynthesis pathway [50, 51]. The 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol is essential for the formation of sinapyl alcohol, and sinapyl alcohol was the monomer of S-lignin [52–54]. The fruit stalk of BQ exhibited significantly higher levels of coniferaldehyde, coniferyl alcohol, and sinapaldehyde than that of DA, while the 5-hydroxyconiferyl alcohol was lower in BQ. These results suggested that the increased accumulation of coniferaldehyde and coniferyl alcohol in BQ promoted the formation of G-lignin, thereby enhancing the mechanical strength of fruit stalk, and potentially reducing the occurrence of fruit abscission. In the future, more experiments are needed to further elucidate the specific regulatory functions of these important genes and metabolites in the lignin biosynthesis of Chinese bayberry.

Conclusion

In the present study, the integrated analysis of transcriptome and metabolome was conducted to elucidate the roles of key genes and metabolites in the lignin biosynthesis pathway related to the fruit abscission in Chinese bayberry. The analysis identified nine structural genes (e.g., MrCOMT1) and four important metabolites (e.g., coniferaldehyde) involved in the lignin biosynthesis. This study established a regulatory network comprising important DEGs and DAMs, highlighting their significant roles in the lignin biosynthesis in Chinese bayberry. However, the biological function of these DEGs involved in lignin biosynthesis associated with fruit abscission need further validation. Overall, these findings can offer valuable insights for the molecular breeding efforts of mitigating the fruit abscission in Chinese bayberry.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

Two varieties of Chinese bayberry, Biqizhong(BQ) with mild fruit abscission and Dingao(DA) with severe fruit abscission were selected and bred by Zhejiang Academy of Agricultural Sciences(ZAAS), Yuyao and Ouhai Agricultural Department, and their system identification and preservation was performed by ZAAS. Ten each 15-year-old BQ and DA plants, with uniform growth and health, and maintained under consistent water, fertilization and cultivation management, were utilized in this study. All plants were cultivated in Chashan Street, Wenzhou City, Zhejiang Province, China (N120°43’33’’, E27°56’10’’), a location characterized by a subtropical monsoon climate suitable for the growth of Chinese bayberry. All samples were collected at the white-turning stage (WS), red-turning stage (RS), and mature stage (MS) for subsequent experimental analyses. 7 days prior to the maturity of Chinese bayberry, plastic films were laid beneath each tree to collect abscised fruits. The number of daily abscised fruits was recorded until the final harvest. The total number of fruits harvested from each tree was also counted. The fruit abscission rate was calculated as follows: Abscission rate (%) = [Total harvested fruits/(Total harvested fruits + Total abscised fruits)] × 100.

Phenotype measurement

The length (mm) and width (mm) of fruit stalks were measured using Vernier calipers, with 30 representative samples randomly selected from each plant, and the average value was recorded. The fruit abscission rates of BQ and DA were tracked at MS over three consecutive years (2021, 2022, and 2023). Additionally, the cross-sections of the fruit stalk were examined using a super depth-of-field digital microscope (VHX-950 F, KEYENCE, China).

The lignin content in the fruit stalk was determined using a commercial kit (Comin, Suzhou, China). The samples were dried to a constant weight, crushed with a mortar. For each replicate, 5 mg of the sample (denoted as W) was weighed into a 10 mL glass tube. A blank tube (A1) (Reagent I : perchloric acid = 1000 µL : 40 µL) and a measuring tube (A2) containing samples 5 mg, Reagent I 1000 µL, and perchloric acid 40 µL were prepared. The tubes were sealed, mixed thoroughly, and placed in a water bath, and shaked every 10 min, then allowed to cool naturally. Next, 1000 µL of Reagent II was added to both A1 and A2 tubes and mixed thoroughly. Subsequently, mixed the supernatant and Reagent III. Then, the absorbance of mixture was measured at 280 nm (A). Extraction of total RNA, library construction and sequencing

The fruit stalks of BQ and DA on maturity stage were collected for transcriptome sequencing analysis. Each sample consisted of three biological replicates. RNAprep Pure Plant Kit was used for the extraction of total RNA. The RNA quality was assessed using NanoDrop2000(Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE) and 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. The RNA Nano 6000 Assay Kit (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA) was used for the determination of RNA integrity. All samples were collected and performed for transcriptome sequencing in Biomarker Technologies (Beijing, China). 1.0 µg RNA of each sample was used, and the cDNA libraries were constructed by usingRNA Library Prep Kit.

Identification of DEGs

High-quality clean reads of all samples were obtained after removing the adapter sequences and low-quality sequence reads. All the clean reads were aligned to the genome of Chinese bayberry‘Zaojia’ [1] using HISAT2 soft [55]. Gene functional annotation was obtained based on public database. The DESeq2 was used to perform the analysis of differential expression between BQ and DA [56, 57]. All the DEGs were screened using significant differential expression threshold of FDR < 0.01, |log2(FC)| ≥ 2. Besides, PCA and correlation analysis of DEGs between BQ and DA was conducted on BMK platform (www.biocloud.net).

Enrichment analysis of DEGs

The Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyotoncyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases was used for the analysis of DEGs enrichment. The enrichment analysis of GO was conducted by GOseq R packages [58], and the enrichment analysis of KEGG between BQ and DA was conducted by KOBAS method [59].

Metabolite extraction, identification and quantification

Widely non-target metabolomics experiment, including the detailed extraction steps of metabolite between BQ and DA, was carried out at Biomarker Technologies (Beijing, China). Each sample consisted of three biological replicates. Here, 50mg of each sample was added to 1000µL of extraction solution containing internal standard (the volume ratio of methanol: acetonitrile : water was 2:1:1, internal standard concentration 20 mg/L).Then, vortex and mix for 30 s. Add steel balls, and treat with a 45 Hz grinder for 10 min. Thereafter, sonicate for 10 min in ice water bath. The system for metabolomics analysis is composed of Waters Acquity I-Class PLUS ultra-high performance liquid tandem Waters Xevo G2-XS QT of high resolution mass spectrometer. The size of UPLC HSS T3 column was 1.8 μm 2.1*100 mm. The positive and negative ion mode includes two parts: mobile phase A (0.1% formic acid aqueous solution) and mobile phase B (0.1% formic acid acetonitrile). The injection volume was 1µL.

Subsequently, the identification and quantification of Metabolite were performed. Three important databases, including KEGG, HMDB and lipidmaps, were utilized for the classification and pathway information of the identified metabolites. p-value of each identified compound was calculated and compared using T test. The OPLS-DA model was conducted by R package software (ver.3.5.1). To verify the reliability of OPLS-DA model, 200 times permutation tests was further conducted. Multiple cross-validation was used for calculation of VIP value. The identification of differential metabolites were determined by the combination of P value, fold change (FC) and VIP value. VIP values >1, FC value ≥ 2 and P value < 0.05, was setted as the filtering criteria to identify the DAMs [14]. Finally, the annotation of differential metabolites was performed by the databases of KEGG and GO, and the involved metabolic pathways were then obtained [60, 61].

Integrated analysis of transcriptome and metabolome

To further analyze the biosynthetic pathways of lignin in the fruit stalks of Chinese bayberry, the correlation network analysis between transcriptomics and metabolomics was conducted. Person correlation coefficients (PCCs) between DEGs and DAMs were computed and obtained using KEGG pathway. The visualization and analysis of co-expression network was performed by Cytoscape software (version 3.7.1). Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was performed to identify clusters (modules) of highly co-expressed genes and to explore their associations with lignin biosynthesis in BQ and DA [62]. In this study, the analysis was conducted using the WGCNA R package (version 3.4.4).

qRT-PCR validation

The fruit stalks of BQ and DA were collected at three stages (WS, RS, and MS) for the extraction of total RNA. qRT-PCR analysis was conducted using FastFire qPCR PreMix SYBR Green (Tiangen, China) in a reaction volume of 10 µL, following the PCR amplification procedure described in reference [33]. The primer sequences for the candidate genes were designed using Vector NTI software (Table S1). MrActin was adopted as an internal reference, and the expression levels of gene were further calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method, with three replicates for each measurement [63].

Statistic analysis

The data analysis in this study was conducted by using the one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with SPSS software, and the difference between samples or treatments were considered-significant at P < 0.05 and highly significant at P < 0.01.

A categorical formulation of the Deligne-Terasoma approach to double shuffle theory
Benjamin Enriquez, Khalef Yaddaden

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of a bimodule with a factorization structure (BFS) and show that such a structure gives rise to an algebra morphism. We then prove that this framework offers an interpretation of the geometric construction underlying both the Betti and de Rham harmonic coproducts of the double shuffle theory developed in [ DeT , EF1 , EF2 , EF3 ] .

Introduction

In
[
EF1
]
, the first author and Furusho revisited the formalism of double shuffle relations among multiple zeta values set up by Racinet
[
Rac
]
. There, they explained the “de Rham” nature of this formalism and constructed its “Betti” version, whose main objects are algebra “harmonic” coproducts
\Delta^{\mathcal{W},\mathrm{DR}}
and
\Delta^{\mathcal{W},\mathrm{B}}
, respectively equipping the algebras
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{DR}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{B}}
with a Hopf algebra structure. The algebras
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{DR}}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\mathrm{B}}
can be seen as subalgebras of respectively
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{B}}
, the group algebra of the free group with two generators and
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{DR}}
, the enveloping algebra of the free Lie algebra with two generators.
The authors used this formalism to prove that the associator relations between the multiple zeta values imply the double shuffle relations. This is formulated as the inclusion of the torsor of associators in the double shuffle torsor
[
EF2
]
. To this end, they constructed isomorphisms relating the Betti and de Rham sides and showed that any associator relates the algebra coproducts
\Delta^{\mathcal{W},\mathrm{DR}}
and
\Delta^{\mathcal{W},\mathrm{B}}
(
[
EF1
, Theorem 10.9]
). Rather than relying on Bar constructions as in
[
Fur
]
, this alternative proof builds upon an interpretation of the harmonic coproducts in terms of infinitesimal braid Lie algebras for the de Rham side and braid groups for the Betti side, which is implicit in the unpublished work of Deligne and Terasoma
[
DeT
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to formulate this construction of the harmonic coproducts in a categorical framework, which will be used in a later paper
[
EY2
]
for the geometric interpretation of the cyclotomic version of the Betti and de Rham coproducts introduced by the second author in
[
Yad
]
.
To this end, we define the category
\mathsf{BFS}
of
bimodules with factorization structures
whose objects are tuples
(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{M},\mathbf{A},\rho,e,\boldsymbol{r},\boldsymbol{c})
such that
(\mathbf{B},\mathbf{M},\mathbf{A},\rho)
is a bimodule (i.e.
\mathbf{M}
is a right
\mathbf{A}
-module equipped with a compatible left

-action

) and

satisfies the factorization identity (see Definition
2.1.1
)

\rho(e)=\boldsymbol{c}\circ\boldsymbol{r}.

We then construct a functor

which associates to any object

of
\mathsf{BFS}
, an algebra morphism given by (see Proposition-Definition
2.3.2
)

(0.1)

\mathbf{B}\ni b\mapsto\boldsymbol{r}\circ\rho(b)\circ\boldsymbol{c}(1_{\mathbf{A}})\in\mathbf{A}.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\alpha:A\twoheadrightarrow C
and
\beta:B\twoheadrightarrow D
be two surjective
\mathbf{k}
-module morphisms such that there exists a pair of
\mathbf{k}
-module morphisms
\mathrm{f}:A\to B
and
\mathrm{g}:C\to D
such that the following diagram

(B.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

commutes. Then, the morphism
\beta
induces a
\mathbf{k}
-module isomorphism

\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})/\ker(\beta)\simeq\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g}).

Proof.

Taking the vertical cokernels of diagram (
B.1
), it follows that there exists a unique
\mathbf{k}
-module morphism
\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g})
such that the following diagram

(B.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

commutes, where the vertical maps are the canonical projections. On the other hand, the map
\ker(\beta)\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})
obtained by the composition

\ker(\beta)\hookrightarrow B\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})

is such that the composition

\ker(\beta)\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g})

is zero. Indeed, thanks to diagram (
B.2
), this map is equal to the composition

\ker(\beta)\hookrightarrow B\xrightarrow{\beta}D\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g}),

which is in fact zero. The wanted statement is equivalent to the exactness of the the sequence

\ker(\beta)\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g})\to\{0\},

which we now prove. The surjectivity of
\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g})
immediately follows from the surjectivity of
\beta
and the commutativity of diagram (

). Let us now prove that

\mathrm{im}(\ker(\beta)\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f}))=\ker(\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g})).

(B.3)

\mathrm{im}(\ker(\beta)\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f}))=\frac{\ker(\beta)+\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}{\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}\simeq\frac{\ker(\beta)}{\ker(\beta)\cap\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}

(B.4)

\ker(\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{f})\to\mathrm{coker}(\mathrm{g}))=\frac{\{b\in B\mid\beta(b)\in\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{g})\}}{\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\beta)\cap\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\beta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{b\in B\mid\beta(b)\in\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{g})\}}

gives rise to an injection

(B.5)

\frac{\ker(\beta)}{\ker(\beta)\cap\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}\hookrightarrow\frac{\{b\in B\mid\beta(b)\in\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{g})\}}{\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{f})}

Let us prove its surjectivity. Let
b\in B
such that
\beta(b)\in\mathrm{im}(\mathrm{g})
. Let then

be such that
\beta(b)=\mathrm{g}(c)
. Since
\alpha:A\to C
is surjective, there exists

such that
c=\alpha(a)
. It follows that

\beta(b)=\mathrm{g}\circ\alpha(a)=\beta\circ\mathrm{f}(a),

where the last equality follows from the commutativity of diagram (
B.1
). We then have

The image of the class of
b-\mathrm{f}(a)
under (

b-\mathrm{f}(a)
in the target of (

), which is equal to the class of

Appendix C
Some results on group algebras

Proposition C.1
.

Let

\{1\}\to F\xrightarrow{\iota}G\xrightarrow{\pi}H\to\{1\}

be a split exact sequence of groups with splitting
\sigma:H\to G
. Denote by
\Theta:H\to\mathrm{Aut}(F)
the group morphism given by
H\ni h\mapsto\Theta_{h}\in\mathrm{Aut}(F)
where

\Theta_{h}:x\mapsto\iota^{-1}\left(\sigma(h)\iota(x)\sigma(h)^{-1}\right)

Then

(a)

The composition
\Phi:\mathbf{k}{F}\otimes\mathbf{k}{H}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{k}{\iota}\otimes\mathbf{k}{\sigma}}\mathbf{k}{G}\otimes\mathbf{k}{G}\to\mathbf{k}{G}
is an isomorphism of left
\mathbf{k}{F}
-modules, where
\mathbf{k}{G}\otimes\mathbf{k}{G}\to\mathbf{k}{G}
is the product of the group algebra
\mathbf{k}{G}
.

(b)

Assume that for any
h\in H
,
\Theta_{h}^{\mathrm{ab}}=\mathrm{id}_{F^{\mathrm{ab}}}
(where
(-)^{\mathrm{ab}}
is the abelianization functor). Then, for any
n\geq 0
, the morphism
\Phi:\mathbf{k}{F}\otimes\mathbf{k}{H}\to\mathbf{k}{G}
induces an isomorphism of left
\mathbf{k}{F}
-modules

\sum_{a+b=n}I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}\simeq I_{G}^{n}.

Proof.

(a)

This follows from the fact that the map
F\times H\to G
given by
(x,h)\mapsto\iota(x)\sigma(h)
is a bijection with reciprocal given by
g\mapsto\left(\iota^{-1}\left(g\ \sigma(\pi(g))^{-1}\right),\pi(g)\right)
.

(b)

Let
n\geq 0
. Let us show that
\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{a+b=n}I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}\right)=I_{G}^{n}
by following these steps:

Step 1

Let us show that

\Phi\left(\sum_{a+b=n}I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}\right)\subset I_{G}^{n}.

Indeed, for any
a,b\geq 0
such that
a+b=n
, we have

\Phi(I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b})\subset\mathbf{k}{\iota}(I_{F}^{a})\mathbf{k}{\sigma}(I_{H}^{b})=\mathbf{k}{\iota}(I_{F})^{a}\mathbf{k}{\sigma}(I_{H})^{b}\subset I_{G}^{a}I_{G}^{b}=I_{G}^{n},

where the last inclusion follows from the fact group algebra morphisms preserve augmentation ideals. Therefore, we obtain the announced inclusion.

Step 2

Let
h\in H
. Let us show that for any
a\geq 0
we have

\mathrm{gr}_{a}(\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}})=\mathrm{id}_{I_{F}^{a}/I_{F}^{a+1}}.

By assumption on
\Theta_{h}
, for
x\in F
, we have that
\pi_{F}^{\mathrm{ab}}(\Theta_{h}(x))=\pi_{F}^{\mathrm{ab}}(x)
, where
\pi_{F}^{\mathrm{ab}}:F\twoheadrightarrow F^{\mathrm{ab}}
is the canonical projection. Therefore, there exists
u\in(F,F)
such that
\Theta_{h}(x)=xu
, then (equality in
I_{F}
)

\mathbf{k}\Theta_{h}(x-1)=(x-1)+(u-1)+(x-1)(u-1)

It is immediate that
(x-1)(u-1)\in I_{F}^{2}
and thanks to
[
Wei
, Exercise 6.1.4]
we also have
u-1\in I_{F}^{2}
since
u\in(F,F)
. Hence, (equality in
\mathrm{gr}_{1}(\mathbf{k}{F})=I_{F}/I_{F}^{2}
)

(C.1)

\mathrm{gr}_{1}(\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}})([x-1]_{1})=[x-1]_{1}.

Recall that the
\mathbf{k}
-module morphism
\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}}
is a filtered algebra automorphism of
\mathbf{k}{F}
which implies that
\mathrm{gr}(\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}})
is a graded algebra automorphism of
\mathrm{gr}(\mathbf{k}{F})
.
Since the algebra
\mathrm{gr}(\mathbf{k}{F})
is generated by
\mathrm{gr}_{1}(\mathbf{k}{F})
, equality
C.1
implies that

\mathrm{gr}(\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}})=\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{gr}(\mathbf{k}{F})},

thus proving the wanted identity.

Step 3

Let
a,b\geq 0
such that
a+b=n
. Let
g\in G
.

(i)

Thanks to the proof of
(a)
, there exists a unique
(x,h)\in F\times H
such that
g=\iota(x)\sigma(h)
.

(ii)

Thanks to the proof of
Step 1
, we define the
\mathbf{k}
-module morphism
\Phi_{a,b}:I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}\to I_{G}^{n}
to be the restriction of
\Phi
to
I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}
.

(iii)

Thanks to
Step 2
, for
u\in I_{F}^{a}
, we have that
\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}}(u)-u\in I_{F}^{a+1}
. Therefore, we may define the
\mathbf{k}
-module morphism

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\nu_{a,b}^{g}&:&I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}&\to&I_{F}^{a+1}\otimes I_{H}^{b}\\
&&u\otimes v&\mapsto&(x-1)\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}}(u)\otimes v+(\mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}}(u)-u)\otimes v\end{array}

(iv)

Define the
\mathbf{k}
-module morphism

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\upsilon_{a,b}^{g}&:&I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}&\to&I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b+1}\\
&&u\otimes v&\mapsto&x\ \mathbf{k}{\Theta_{h}}(u)\otimes(h-1)v\end{array}

Let us show that for any
t_{a,b}\in I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}
we have (equality in
I_{G}^{n+1}
)

(g-1)\Phi_{a,b}(t_{a,b})=\Phi_{a+1,b}\circ\nu_{a,b}^{g}(t_{a,b})+\Phi_{a,b+1}\circ\upsilon_{a,b}^{g}(t_{a,b}).

Indeed, by linearity, it suffices to show this equality for
u\otimes v\in I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}
. We have

\displaystyle\Phi_{a+1,b}\circ\nu_{a,b}^{g}(u\otimes v)+\Phi_{a,b+1}\circ\upsilon_{a,b}^{g}(u\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{k}\iota\big((x-1)\mathbf{k}\Theta_{h}(u)\big)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)+\mathbf{k}\iota\big(\mathbf{k}\Theta_{h}(u)-u\big)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)+\mathbf{k}\iota\big(x\ \mathbf{k}\Theta_{h}(u)\big)\mathbf{k}\sigma((h-1)v)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{k}\iota\big(x\ \mathbf{k}\Theta_{h}(u)\big)\mathbf{k}\sigma(hv)-\mathbf{k}\iota(u)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)=\mathbf{k}\iota(x)(\mathbf{k}\iota\circ\mathbf{k}\Theta_{h})(u)\mathbf{k}\sigma(h)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)-\mathbf{k}\iota(u)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{k}\iota(x)\mathbf{k}\sigma(h)\mathbf{k}\iota(u)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)-\mathbf{k}\iota(u)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)=\Big(\mathbf{k}\iota(x)\mathbf{k}\sigma(h)-1\Big)\mathbf{k}\iota(u)\mathbf{k}\sigma(v)

\displaystyle=(g-1)\Phi_{a,b}(u\otimes v),

where the third equality from the fact that both
\mathbf{k}{\iota}
and
\mathbf{k}{\sigma}
are algebra morphisms, the fourth one from the definition of
\Theta_{h}
and the last one from the definition of
\Phi
and from
g=\iota(x)\sigma(h)
.

Step 4

Let us show by induction on
n
that

\Phi\left(\sum_{a+b=n}I_{F}^{a}\otimes I_{H}^{b}\right)\supset I_{G}^{n}.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle\bigoplus_{a+b=n}\mathrm{gr}_{a}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi})\otimes\mathrm{gr}_{b}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\psi})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}^{n}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{gr}_{n}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi})}

where the horizontal maps are the canonical projection. This diagram is commutative since for any
a,b\geq 0
such that
a+b=n
we have for
g\in\mathcal{F}^{a}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi})
and
h\in\mathcal{F}^{b}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\psi})
that
[g]_{a}\cdot[h]_{b}=[gh]_{n}
.

Finally, recall that
\ker(\mathcal{F}^{n}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi})\to\mathrm{gr}_{n}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi}))=\mathcal{F}^{n+1}\ker(\mathbf{k}{\varphi})
. The result then follows by applying Lemma
B.1
to the commutative diagram (
C.3
).
∎

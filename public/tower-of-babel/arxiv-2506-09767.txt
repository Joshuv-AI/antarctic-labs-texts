On saturation numbers of complete multipartite graphs and even cycles
Ali Mohammadian, Milad Poursoltani, Behruz Tayfeh-Rezaie, mpour@ipm.ir, tayfeh-r@ipm.ir

Abstract
Given positive integer n n and graph F F , the saturation number sat ⁡ ( n , F ) \mathrm{sat}(n,F) is the minimum number of edges in an edge-maximal F F -free graph on n n vertices. In this paper, we determine asymptotic behavior of sat ⁡ ( n , F ) \mathrm{sat}(n,F) when F F is either a complete multipartite graph or a cycle graph whose length is even and large enough. This extends a result by Bohman, Fonoberova, and Pikhurko from 2010 as well as partially resolves a conjecture of Füredi and Kim from 2013. Keywords: Complete multipartite graph, Cycle graph, Saturation number. AMS 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C35, 05C38.



1
Introduction

Extremal problems are among the most studied topics in graph theory.
They are very popular and have a huge literature.
Here, we concentrate on the saturation problem on graphs.
Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be finite, undirected, and without loops or multiple edges. Let
F
be a given graph. We say that a graph
G
is
F
-free
if
G
has no subgraph isomorphic to
F
. In 1941, Turán
[
11
]
posed one of the foundational problems in extremal graph theory which was about the maximum number
of edges in an
F
-free graph on
n
vertices. Later, in 1949, Zykov
[
13
]
introduced a dual idea which asks for the minimum number of edges in an edge-maximal
F
-free graph on
n
vertices.
Formally, a graph
G
is said to be
F
-saturated

if
G
is
F
-free and, for any two nonadjacent vertices
x
and
y
of
G
, the graph resulting from
G
by joining
x
and
y
with an edge is not
F
-free.
The minimum number of edges in an
F
-saturated graph on
n
vertices is denoted by
\mathrm{sat}(n,F)
.

Let
K_{s}
denote the complete graph on
s
vertices and
K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}}
denote the complete
r
-partite graph with parts of sizes
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
.
Erdős, Hajnal, and Moon
[
6
]
proved that

\displaystyle\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s})=(s-2)n-{{s-1}\choose{2}},

(1)

where
n\geqslant s\geqslant 2
.
Generally, the exact value of
\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}})
is still unknown.
Kászonyi and Tuza
[
8
]
proved that

\displaystyle\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{1,s})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}&\\
{{{s}\choose{2}}+{{n-s}\choose{2}}}&\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{{\large if }}{s+1\leqslant n\leqslant\frac{3s}{2}}\mbox{;}\\
\\
{\left\lceil\frac{s-1}{2}n-\frac{s^{2}}{8}\right\rceil}&\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{{\large if }}{n\geqslant\frac{3s}{2}}\mbox{.}\\
&\end{array}\right.

As an asymptotic result, Bohman, Fonoberova, and Pikhurko
[
2
]
established that

\displaystyle\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}})=\left(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r-1}+\frac{s_{r}-3}{2}\right)n+O\left(n^{\frac{3}{4}}\right),

(2)

where
r\geqslant 2
and
1\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant s_{r}
.
In Section
3
of this paper, we extend (
2
) by proving the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any fixed integers
r\geqslant 2
and
1\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant s_{r}
,

\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}})=\left(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r-1}+\frac{s_{r}-3}{2}\right)n+O(1).

Our proof for Theorem
1.1
is short and simple. We recall that Theorem
1.1
has been established in
[
2
]
provided
s_{r-1}\neq s_{r}
by a rather long and complicated proof.

Let
C_{k}
denote the cycle graph on
k
vertices. Generally, it seems hard to determine the exact value of
\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{k})
. Until now, only the exact values of
\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{k})
are known for
k\in\{3,4,5\}
.
It follows from (
1
) that
\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{3})=n-1
for every integer
n\geqslant 3
. Also,
we know that
\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{4})=\lfloor(3n-5)/2\rfloor
for every integer
n\geqslant 5
which is due to Ollmann
[
10
]
and
\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{5})=\lceil 10(n-1)/7\rceil
for every integer
n\geqslant 21
which is due to Chen
[
4
]
.
For
C_{6}
,
Lan, Shi, Wang, and Zhang
[
9
]
showed that

4n/3-2\leqslant\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{6})\leqslant(4n+1)/3
for every integer
n\geqslant 9
. For longer cycles, Füredi and Kim
[
7
]
established that

\displaystyle\frac{k+3}{k+2}n-1<\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{k})<\frac{k-3}{k-4}n+{k-4\choose 2},

(3)

where
k\geqslant 7
and
n\geqslant 2k-5
. They proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
7
]
)
.

There exists a constant
k_{0}
such that

\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{k})=\frac{k-3}{k-4}n+O(k^{2})

holds for all integers
k\geqslant k_{0}
.

In Section
4
of this paper, we partially resolve Conjecture
1.2
by proving the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

For each fixed even integer
k\geqslant 28
,

\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{k})=\frac{k-3}{k-4}n+O(1).

We refer to
[
5
]
for a survey of results on saturation numbers of graphs. We just recall the following conjecture that is made by Tuza
[
12
]
.

Conjecture 1.4

(
[
12
]
)
.

For every graph
F
, there exists a constant
c_{{}_{{F}}}
such
that
\mathrm{sat}(n,F)=c_{{}_{{F}}}n+O(1)
.

Obviously, Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
verify Conjecture
1.4
for complete multipartite graphs and cycle graphs whose length are even and large enough.

2
Preliminaries

At first, let us fix some standard notation and terminology of graph theory.
Let
G
be a graph. The vertex set and the edge set of
G
are denoted by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively.
For a subset
W
of
V(G)
, write
G[W]
for the subgraph of
G
induced by
W
and set

N_{G}(W)=\big\{v\in V(G)\,\big|\,\text{$v$ is adjacent to all vertices in $W$}\big\}.

For the sake of convenience, we write
N_{G}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
instead of
N_{G}(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\})
and we set
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
.
The
degree
of a vertex
v
of
G
is defined as
|N_{G}(v)|
and is denoted by
\deg_{G}(v)
.
The
distance
between two vertices
u
and
v
of
G
, denoted by
d_{G}(u,v)
, is defined as the minimum number of edges in a path in
G
connecting
u
and
v
.
For two subsets
S
and
T
of
V(G)
, the distance between
S
and
T
is
\min\{d_{G}(x,y)\,|\,x\in S\text{ and }y\in T\}
.
Also, we denote by
E_{G}(S,T)
the set of all edges
with endpoints in both
S
and
T
.
Recall that a
starlike
tree is a tree obtained from at least two
paths that are completely vertex-disjoint, except that they
all share exactly one endpoint in common, which is called the
center
of the starlike tree.

We recall an upper bound on
\mathrm{sat}(n,F)
which was proved by Kászonyi and Tuza in
[
8
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
F
be a graph and
S
be an independent set in
F
with maximum possible size. Also, let

b=|V(F)|-|S|-1
and

d=\min\{|N_{F}(x)\cap S|\,|\,x\in V(F)\setminus S\}
. Then,

\mathrm{sat}(n,F)\leqslant\frac{2b+d-1}{2}n-\frac{b(b+d)}{2}.

3
Complete multipartite graphs

In this section, we provide a proof for Theorem
1.1
.
We first set up some notation to be used throughout the section.
We always assume that
r\geqslant 2
and
1\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant s_{r}
.
Set
F=K_{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r}}
and assume that
H
is an
F
-saturated graph on
n
vertices.
Let
s=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}
,

A=\Big\{x\in V(H)\,\Big|\,\deg_{H}(x)\leqslant 2s-s_{r}-4\Big\},

and

\mathcal{A}=\Big\{M\subseteq A\,\Big|\,\big|N_{H}(u,v)\big|\leqslant s-s_{r}-2\text{ for any two distinct vertices }u,v\in M\Big\}.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b,c,g
be positive integers with
b=(c-1)g^{(c-1)g^{c}+1}+(c-1)g^{c}+2
and
g=4^{a}
. Also, let
B
be an independent set in
H
such that
|B|=b
and
\deg_{H}(v)\leqslant a
for every vertex
v\in B
.
Then, there exists a subset
C\subseteq B
such that
|C|=c
and
|N_{H}(C)|\geqslant s-s_{r}-1
. Moreover,
|N_{H}(C)|>|N_{H}(B)|
if
a=s-3
.

Proof.

Let
B_{1}
be an arbitrary subset of
B
with
|B_{1}|=(c-1)g^{c}+1
and set
B_{2}=B\setminus B_{1}
.
For two distinct vertices
x,y\in B
, if we join
x
and
y
with an edge and call the resulting graph by
H+xy
, then at least one copy of
F
can be found in
H+xy
.
Fix such a copy
F_{xy}
of
F
in
H+xy
. Let
T_{x}^{y}
and
T_{y}^{x}
denote the subsets of
V(F_{xy})
such that
T_{x}^{y}\cup\{x\}
and
T_{y}^{x}\cup\{y\}
are two parts of
F_{xy}
. Set
S_{xy}=V(F_{xy})\setminus(T_{x}^{y}\cup T_{y}^{x}\cup\{x,y\})
.

For a fixed vertex
x\in B
, the number of choices for pairs
(T_{y}^{x},S_{xy})
is at most
g
, since
T_{y}^{x}\cup S_{xy}\subseteq N_{H}(x)
and
\deg_{H}(x)\leqslant a
.
Therefore, the number of choices for pairs
(T_{y}^{x},S_{xy})
, for all vertices
x,y\in B_{1}
, is at most
g^{(c-1)g^{c}+1}
. As
|B_{2}|=(c-1)g^{(c-1)g^{c}+1}+1
, by the pigeonhole principle, there exists a subset
B_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq B_{2}
of size
c
such that

\displaystyle T_{u}^{x}=T_{v}^{x}\quad\text{ and }\quad S_{xu}=S_{xv}

(4)

for all vertices
x\in B_{1}
and
u,v\in B_{2}^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
the number of choices for pairs
(T_{x}^{y},S_{xy})
, for all vertices
x\in B_{1}
and
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, is at most
g^{c}
. As

|B_{1}|=(c-1)g^{c}+1
, by the pigeonhole principle, there exists a subset
B_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq B_{1}
of size
c
such that

\displaystyle T_{u}^{y}=T_{v}^{y}\quad\text{ and }\quad S_{yu}=S_{yv}.

(5)

for all vertices
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
and
u,v\in B_{1}^{\prime}
.
From (
4
) and (
5
), we deduce the following statements: (i) For any vertices
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, all subsets
S_{xy}
are the same, say
S
; (ii) For a fixed vertex
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
, all subsets

T_{w}^{x}
for vertices
w\in B_{2}^{\prime}
are the same, say
T^{x}
;
(iii) For a fixed vertex
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, all subsets

T_{w}^{y}
for vertices
w\in B_{1}^{\prime}
are the same, say
T_{y}
.
Note that, for all vertices
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, we have
T^{x}\subseteq N_{H}(x)
and
T_{y}\subseteq N_{H}(y)
. Moreover, for all vertices
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
,
every vertex in
T^{x}
is adjacent to every vertex in
T_{y}\cup S
and
every vertex in
T_{y}
is adjacent to every vertex in
T^{x}\cup S
.

For two fixed vertices
x_{0}\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
y_{0}\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, there are two distinct indices
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that

|T^{x_{0}}_{y_{0}}\cup\{y_{0}\}|=s_{i}
and

|T_{x_{0}}^{y_{0}}\cup\{x_{0}\}|=s_{j}
.
According to the discussion mentioned above,
|T^{x}|=s_{i}-1
and
|T_{y}|=s_{j}-1
for all vertices
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
and
y\in B_{2}^{\prime}
, and moreover,
|S|=s-s_{i}-s_{j}
.
Let
\widehat{T}=\bigcup_{x\in B_{1}^{\prime}}T^{x}
and
{\mathchoice{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raise 7.09259pt\hbox{\scalebox{1}[-1]{\lower 7.09259pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\widehat{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=6.83331pt\vrule height=0.0pt,width=7.23265pt}$}}}}\cr\hbox{$\displaystyle T$}\crcr}}}}{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raise 7.09259pt\hbox{\scalebox{1}[-1]{\lower 7.09259pt\hbox{$\textstyle\widehat{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=6.83331pt\vrule height=0.0pt,width=7.23265pt}$}}}}\cr\hbox{$\textstyle T$}\crcr}}}}{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raise 6.40926pt\hbox{\scalebox{1}[-1]{\lower 6.40926pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\widehat{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=4.78334pt\vrule height=0.0pt,width=5.79965pt}$}}}}\cr\hbox{$\scriptstyle T$}\crcr}}}}{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hbox{\raise 5.9537pt\hbox{\scalebox{1}[-1]{\lower 5.9537pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\widehat{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=3.41667pt\vrule height=0.0pt,width=5.02959pt}$}}}}\cr\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle T$}\crcr}}}}}=\bigcup_{y\in B_{2}^{\prime}}T_{y}
.
If

and

, then
one may find a copy of
F
in
H
having a part of size
s_{i}
in
\widehat{T}
, a part of size
s_{j}
in

\textstyle\widehat{\vrule width=0.0pt,height=6.83331pt\vrule height=0.0pt,width=7.23265pt}

\textstyle T

, and a part of size
s_{k}
in
S
for all indices
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}\setminus\{i,j\}
.
However, this contradicts the

-freeness of

. So, without loss of generality, we may assume that
|\widehat{T}|=s_{i}-1
which means that
\widehat{T}=T^{x}
for every vertex
x\in B_{1}^{\prime}
.
We now have
|N_{H}(B_{1}^{\prime})|\geqslant|\widehat{T}|+|S|=(s_{i}-1)+(s-s_{i}-s_{j})\geqslant s-s_{r}-1

For the ‘moreover’ statement, let

.
If

, then, for every vertex

Lemma 3.2
.

For every set
M\in\mathcal{A}
,
|M|=O(1)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
2s-s_{r}-4\geqslant 0
.
By employing a greedy algorithm, we get an independent set
M^{\prime}\subseteq M
so that
|M^{\prime}|\geqslant|M|/(2s-s_{r}-3)
. Letting
g=4^{2s-s_{r}-4}
, suppose by way of contradiction that

|M^{\prime}|>g^{g^{2}+1}+g^{2}+2
.
By applying Lemma
3.1
for
a=2s-s_{r}-4
, an arbitrary subset of
M^{\prime}
of size
b=g^{g^{2}+1}+g^{2}+2
, and
c=2
, we find two distinct vertices
u,v\in M^{\prime}
so that
|N_{H}(u,v)|\geqslant s-s_{r}-1
which is a contradiction to the definition of
M
.
Hence,

|M|\leqslant(2s-s_{r}-3)|M^{\prime}|\leqslant(2s-s_{r}-3)(g^{g^{2}+1}+g^{2}+2).\qed

The following Lemma was proved in
[
2
, Lemma 7]
using a Ramsey argument. For the sake of self-contentedness, we present here a proof using Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
D=\{v\in V(H)\,|\,\deg_{H}(v)\leqslant s-3\}
. Then,
|D|=O(1)
.

Proof.

We may assume that
s\geqslant 3
. Using a greedy algorithm, we find an independent set
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
such that
|D^{\prime}|\geqslant|D|/(s-2)
. Letting
g=4^{s-3}
,
define the function
\phi(k)=(k-1)g^{(k-1)g^{k}+1}+(k-1)g^{k}+2
. Also, define
\phi^{(0)}(k)=k
and
\phi^{(i)}(k)=\phi(\phi^{(i-1)}(k))
for all integers
i\geqslant 1
.

Toward a contradiction, suppose that
|D^{\prime}|>\phi^{(s-2)}(1)
. Consider an arbitrary subset
D_{0}\subseteq D^{\prime}
with
|D_{0}|=\phi^{(s-2)}(1)
. By applying
s-2
times Lemma
3.1
gives us subsets
D_{0}\supseteq D_{1}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq D_{s-2}

such that
|D_{i}|=\phi^{(s-i-2)}(1)
and
|N_{H}(D_{i})|>|N_{H}(D_{i-1})|
for
i=1,\ldots,s-2
.
We have
|D_{s-2}|=1
and
|N_{H}(D_{s-2})|\geqslant s-2
. This means that
D
contains a vertex of
degree at least
s-2
, a contradiction.
Thus,
|D|\leqslant(s-2)|D^{\prime}|\leqslant(s-2)\phi^{(s-2)}(1)
.
∎

We are now in a position to prove our main result of this section. Recall Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
.

For any integers
r\geqslant 2
and
1\leqslant s_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant s_{r}
,

\mathrm{sat}(n,K_{s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}})=\left(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r-1}+\frac{s_{r}-3}{2}\right)n+O(1).

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
,

\mathrm{sat}(n,F)\leqslant\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}n-\frac{(s-1)(s-s_{r}-1)}{2}.

Hence, it remains to prove that

\mathrm{sat}(n,F)\geqslant\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}n+O(1).

For this, we should show that
|E(H)|\geqslant\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}n+O(1)
.
If
A=\varnothing
, then
|E(H)|\geqslant\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}n
and we are done. So, we assume that
A\neq\varnothing
.
Let
M
be a maximal element of
\mathcal{A}
and set
N=\bigcup_{v\in M}N_{H}(v)
. Also, let

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\big\{v\in V(H)\setminus N\,\big|\,\deg_{H}(v)\geqslant 2s-s_{r}-3\big\},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=\big\{v\in V(H)\setminus N\,\big|\,s-2\leqslant\deg_{H}(v)\leqslant 2s-s_{r}-4\big\},

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\big\{v\in V(H)\setminus N\,\big|\,\deg_{H}(v)\leqslant s-3\big\}.

By the maximality of
M
, each vertex in
Y
is adjacent to at least
s-s_{r}-1
vertices in
N
.
Also, it follows from Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
that
|N|=O(1)
and
|Z|=O(1)
, implying that
|X|+|Y|=n-|M\cup N|-|Z|=n+O(1)
.
So, we may write

\displaystyle|E(H)|

\displaystyle\geqslant E_{H}\big(X,V(H)\big)+E_{H}\big(Y,V(H)\big)

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{v\in X}\frac{\deg_{H}(v)}{2}+\left((s-s_{r}-1)|Y|+\sum_{v\in Y}\frac{\deg_{H}(v)-(s-s_{r}-1)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}|X|+(s-s_{r}-1)|Y|+\frac{s_{r}-1}{2}|Y|

\displaystyle=\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}\big(|X|+|Y|\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2s-s_{r}-3}{2}n+O(1).\qed

4
Even cycles

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
. For clarity, we divide this section into two subsections.

4.1
General properties

In this subsection, we state and prove auxiliary lemmas about the structures of
C_{k}
-saturated graphs. The first one is already known in the literature. To be self-contained, we include its proof here.

Before we proceed, note that a graph is
C_{k}
-saturated if and only if each pair of nonadjacent vertices of the graph are connected by a path of length
k-1
.
In particular, every
C_{k}
-saturated graph is a connected graph with diameter at most
k-1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(
[
1
]
)

Let
k\geqslant 5
and let
G
be a
C_{k}
-saturated graph with at least
3
vertices. Also, let
x,x^{\prime}
be two distinct degree-one vertices of
G
with
N_{G}(x)=\{y\}
and
N_{G}(x^{\prime})=\{y^{\prime}\}
for some vertices
y,y^{\prime}\in V(G)
. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

If
v\in N_{G}[y]\setminus\{x\}
, then
\deg_{G}(v)\geqslant 3
. In particular,
y\neq y^{\prime}
.

(ii)

If
y
is adjacent to
y^{\prime}
, then
\deg_{G}(y)\geqslant 4
.

Proof.

Note that
G
is a connected graph with at least
3
vertices.

(i) Let
v\in N_{G}[y]\setminus\{x\}
. If
\deg_{G}(v)=1
, then
v\neq y
and there is only one path between
x
and
v
whose length is

∎

In the proof,

Theorem
1.3
.

For each even integer

,

\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{k})=\frac{k-3}{k-4}n+O(1).

Proof.

As we already mentioned, using the upper bound presented in (
3
), we should establish that
|E(G)|\geqslant(1+\alpha)|V(G)|+O(1)
.

Suppose that
G
has many paths of length
2\ell-5
on degree-two vertices and there are two vertices

such that one endpoint of
each of those paths is adjacent to

and the other endpoint is adjacent to
y
.
In this case, by removing all these paths from

except two of them, we find a

-saturated graph

such that
|E(G^{\prime})|-(1+\alpha)|V(G^{\prime})|=|E(G)|-(1+\alpha)|V(G)|
.
Therefore, if
|E(G^{\prime})|\geqslant(1+\alpha)|V(G^{\prime})|+O(1)
, then we will have
|E(G)|\geqslant(1+\alpha)|V(G)|+O(1)
.
So, we may assume that the number of such paths in

is at most two.

In order to establish that
|E(G)|\geqslant(1+\alpha)|V(G)|+O(1)
, as we explained before, our strategy is to distribute the value
|E(G)|
on
V(G)\setminus(M\cup A)=B\cup D_{0}\cup D
such that each vertex in
B\cup D_{0}\cup D
takes an edge-weight at least
1+\alpha
.
For each vertex
v\in D_{0}\cup D
, an edge-weight equal to
1
comes from the designated black edge incident with

.
In view of Lemma
4.7
(ii), we assigned an extra edge-weight equal to
\alpha
to each vertex in
D\setminus\{v\in D_{1}\,|\,\deg_{G}(v)=1\}
.
As every
G
-pendant
vertex from
D_{1}
is a neighbor of some vertex from
D_{0}
, we need to assign an extra edge-weight equal to
\alpha
to every vertex
x\in D_{0}
if

\epsilon(x)=1
, and
2\alpha
otherwise.
Also, we need to assign an edge-weight equal to
1+\alpha
to every vertex

if

\epsilon(y)=1
, and
1+2\alpha
otherwise. In view of Lemma
4.12
, it suffices to do these assignments to those vertices

and
y
that are roots of none of elements of
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
x\in D_{0}
. By the definition of
D_{0}
,
\deg_{G}(x)\geqslant 2
. If

, then we may assign an extra edge-weight
1/2>2\alpha
to

So, we may assume that

-tree with root

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}
are all values, not necessarily distinct, such that

-tree for

. From Lemma
4.13
, there is at most one

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}
. Moreover, if
\deg_{G}(x)=3
, then Lemma
4.14
implies that
0
and
\alpha/2
do not simultaneously appear among
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}
. From these, we deduce that
\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{d}\geqslant\alpha
and so we may assign an extra edge-weight equal to
\alpha
to

. Hence, we may assume that either
\deg_{G}(x)=2
or

First, assume that
\deg_{G}(x)=2

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we establish that there is a constant
c_{{}_{{F}}}
such
that
\mathrm{sat}(n,F)=c_{{}_{{F}}}n+O(1)
when
F
is either a complete multipartite graph or a cycle graph whose length is an even number at least
28
. We believe that our method can be applied to find an asymptotic formula for
\mathrm{sat}(n,F)
when
F
is a cycle graph whose length is a small even or an odd number.
Using the method, we were able to reprove the known result
\mathrm{sat}(n,C_{6})=4n/3+O(1)
with a rather short and simple proof.

Functional renormalization group study of a four-fermion model with C ​ P CP violation
Linlin Huang, Mamiya Kawaguchi, Yadikaer Maitiniyazi, Shinya Matsuzaki, Akio Tomiya, Masatoshi Yamada

Abstract
We perform a functional renormalization group analysis of a four-fermion model with C ​ P CP and P P violation to explore the nonperturbative infrared dynamics of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) within the Wilsonian renormalization group framework, particularly in the context of spontaneous C ​ P CP -violation models. Our analysis of the fixed-point structure reveals that, in the large- N c N_{c} limit, the C ​ P CP -violating θ ¯ \bar{\theta} parameter is dynamically induced and approaches π ⋅ ( N f / 2 ) \pi\cdot(N_{f}/2) (where N f N_{f} is the number of flavors) as the system enters the chirally broken phase. This behavior arises due to criticality and the large anomalous dimensions of the U ​ ( 1 ) A U(1)_{A} -violating four-fermion couplings. Furthermore, this trend appears to persist beyond the leading large- N c N_{c} approximation, provided that the infrared dynamics of QCD remains dominated by the scalar condensate of the quark bilinear, as expected. Notably, our findings highlight that C ​ P CP -violating four-fermion interactions, which are perturbatively irrelevant, can become relevant in the chirally broken phase through nonperturbative effects, with potential implications for spontaneous C ​ P CP -violation scenarios.

II
Four-fermion model with
CP
violation

Motivated by the spontaneous
CP
-violation scenario, we consider a low-energy description of QCD with additional
CP
and
P
violation beyond the strong
CP
phase to explore nonperturbative contributions to
CP
and
P
violation at scales
\lesssim 1

To this end, in the present study, we employ the following one-flavor (
N_{f}=1
) four-fermion theory as a toy model:

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\Bigg[\bar{\psi}\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{G_{S}}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}+\frac{G_{P}}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\right)^{2}+C_{4}\,(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi)\Bigg].

(1)

Here,
\psi
and
\bar{\psi}
are fermion fields that transform under the
SU(N_{c})
color symmetry.
The first two four-fermion operators in Eq. (
1
) (associated with
G_{S}
and
G_{P}
, respectively) are invariant under the
P
transformation, whereas the last term (associated with
C_{4}
) explicitly breaks
P
invariance and, consequently,
CP
invariance.
1
1

1

The transformation laws under the discrete symmetries
P
and
C
are given by

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{P}}}{{\to}}\bar{\psi}\psi,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{P}}}{{\to}}-\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{C}}}{{\to}}\bar{\psi}\psi,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{C}}}{{\to}}\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi.

Thus, when
C_{4}=\theta=0
, the theory is CP-invariant.
In the special case where
G_{S}=G_{P}
and
C_{4}=0
, the
U(1)_{A}
symmetry is restored, rendering
\theta
unphysical, as it can be eliminated by a
U(1)_{A}
rotation of the fermion fields.

When extended to multiple flavors (e.g., two or three), the global non-Abelian chiral symmetry manifests through the four-fermion interactions in Eq. (
1
), distinct from the explicitly broken Abelian
U(1)_{A}
symmetry (which results from
G_{S}-G_{P}\neq 0
and
C_{4}\neq 0
). For instance, in the two-flavor case, the
G_{S}
and
G_{P}
terms can be generalized as

G_{S}\left[(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}+(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\tau^{a}\psi)^{2}\right]+G_{P}\left[(\bar{\psi}\tau^{a}\psi)^{2}+(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi)^{2}\right],

(2)

where
\psi
is an
SU(2)
-doublet fermion field, and
\tau^{a}
(
a=1,2,3
) are the Pauli matrices. Each of the
G_{S}
and
G_{P}
terms individually preserves chiral

symmetry but breaks
U(1)_{A}
symmetry when
G_{S}\neq G_{P}
.

In this sense, the discrepancy between
G_{S}
and
G_{P}
effectively encodes the
U(1)_{A}
anomaly of the underlying QCD. The dynamical breaking of chiral
SU(2)
symmetry can be triggered solely by strong
G_{S}
and/or
G_{P}
couplings, similar to the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio mechanism, and does not necessarily require the presence of the
U(1)_{A}
anomaly (
G_{S}\neq G_{P}
).
Therefore, it is reasonable to conclude that dynamical chiral symmetry breaking generally occurs when
G_{S}
and/or
G_{P}
reach criticality, irrespective of the
U(1)_{A}
anomaly condition
G_{S}\neq G_{P}
.

In this work, we utilize the Wetterich equation

The explicit form of the Wetterich equation (or flow equation) for a fermionic theory is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}=-{\rm Tr}\left[\left(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}(p)+\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)\right)^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)\right]\,,

(3)

\Lambda

For this convention, the IR limit (

In Eq. (

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}(p)=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}(p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi(p)}

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)=i{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{2}}{p^{2}}}-1\right)\Theta(k^{2}-p^{2})\,,

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{S}}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}-\frac{G_{P}}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\right)^{2}-C_{4}\,(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi)\Bigg].

It has been found that a nontrivial fixed point exists for

Higher-dimensional operators, such as

This is due to their large negative canonical dimensions, which make it unlikely for anomalous dimensions to be sizable enough to render these operators relevant.
Therefore, those would not substantially affect the dynamical chiral symmetry breaking.
3

As we will see, the four-fermion coupling

-2

+4

In the bosonization framework, higher-dimensional fermionic operators correspond to higher-order terms in the bosonized potential, such as cubic and quartic interactions. These terms, however, do not significantly affect the curvature of the potential near the origin, meaning that they do not play a crucial role in triggering chiral symmetry breaking.

In the current fermionic theory, the kinetic term does not receive quantum effects from the four-fermion operators thanks to the one-loop structure of the flow equation (

Flow equations

Applying the Wetterich equation (
3

Here, in order to discuss the fixed-point structure and the flow diagrams in the model in the following subsections, we define the dimensionless couplings as

\displaystyle\tilde{m}=mk^{-1},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{S}=G_{S}k^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{P}=G_{P}k^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{4}=C_{4}k^{2},

(9)

(10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta

\displaystyle=8\left[(\tilde{G}_{S}-\tilde{G}_{P})\sin\theta-2\tilde{C}_{4}\cos\theta\right]\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{G}_{S}

\displaystyle=2\tilde{G}_{S}-8N_{c}\Big[(\tilde{G}_{S}^{2}+\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})(1-\tilde{m}^{2})+2\tilde{m}^{2}(\tilde{G}_{S}^{2}-\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4\tilde{m}^{2}\tilde{G}_{S}\tilde{C}_{4}\sin\theta\Big]\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{G}_{P}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{p^{2}(1+r_{k}^{f})\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{3}}

(16)

II.1

The
\beta

II.3
Fixed-point structure

In this subsection, we study the fixed-point structure.
Thus, in the system, we solve
\partial_{t}\tilde{m}=\partial_{t}\theta=\partial_{t}\tilde{G}_{S}=\partial_{t}\tilde{G}_{P}=\partial_{t}\tilde{C}_{4}=0
and find their solutions.
We start with the flow equation for
\theta
. The fixed-point condition
\partial_{t}\theta=0
yields a relation as

\displaystyle\tan\theta^{*}=\frac{2\tilde{C}_{4}^{*}}{\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}-\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}}.

(17)

Inserting this into the other flow equations, the following nontrivial fixed points are found:

\displaystyle(\text{FP1}):

\displaystyle~~\tilde{m}^{*}=0,\quad\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{N_{c}},\quad\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}=0,\quad\tilde{C}_{4}^{*}=0,

(18)

\displaystyle(\text{FP2}):

\displaystyle~~\tilde{m}^{*}=0,\quad\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}=0,\quad\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{N_{c}},\quad\tilde{C}_{4}^{*}=0,

(19)

\displaystyle(\text{FP3}):

\displaystyle~~\tilde{m}^{*}=0,\quad\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}=G_{P}^{*}=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{N_{c}},\quad\tilde{C}_{4}^{*}=0.

(20)

In addition to these nontrivial fixed points, there is the Gaussian (trivial) fixed point
\tilde{m}^{*}=\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}=\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}=\tilde{C}_{4}^{*}=0
.
FP1 and FP2 are
U(1)_{A}
-broken fixed points, while FP3 is
U(1)_{A}
-symmetric one.
For FP1 and FP2, from the fixed-point condition (
17
),
\tan\theta^{*}=0
, i.e.
\theta^{*}=n\pi
with
n
integers.
In the case of FP1, the right-hand side of Eq. (
17
) becomes an indeterminate form. This fact is consistent with the unphysical nature of
\theta
when the system becomes
U(1)_{A}
invariant at FP3.

Next, we analyze the critical exponents (scaling dimensions). To this end, we linearize the flow equations. More specifically, for a set of the flow equations
\partial_{t}\tilde{g}_{i}=\beta_{i}(\{\tilde{g}\})
where
\{\tilde{g}\}=\{\tilde{m},\theta,\tilde{G}_{S},\tilde{G}_{P},\tilde{C}_{4}\}
, we perform a Taylor expansion around a fixed point
g_{i}^{*}
and take up to the linear order of
\tilde{g}-\tilde{g}^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{g}_{i}\simeq\frac{\partial\beta_{i}(\{\tilde{g}\})}{\partial\tilde{g}_{j}}\Bigg|_{\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}^{*}}(\tilde{g}_{j}-\tilde{g}_{j}^{*})\equiv-{\mathcal{T}}_{ij}(\tilde{g}_{j}-\tilde{g}_{j}^{*}),

(21)

where
\beta(\{g^{*}\})=0
by definition of the fixed point.
The solution to Eq. (
21
) is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{i}=g_{i}^{*}+\sum_{j}c_{j}V_{i}^{j}\left(\frac{k}{\Lambda}\right)^{-\vartheta_{j}},

(22)

where
c_{j}
are undetermined constants of integration,
\Lambda
is a reference scale, and
V_{i}^{j}
are the right eigenvectors of the stability matrix
\mathcal{T}
with eigenvalues
\vartheta_{i}
called the critical exponents or the scaling dimensions, namely,
\vartheta_{i}=-\text{eig}({\mathcal{T}})
.
When we approximate eigenvectors as
V_{i}^{j}\approx\delta_{i}^{j}
, the critical exponents are approximately obtained from the diagonal parts of
\mathcal{T}
. Since at all the fixed points we find
\tilde{m}^{*}=\tilde{C}_{4}^{*}=0
, such an approximation is reasonable and thus we have

\displaystyle\vartheta_{m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\beta_{m}}{\partial\tilde{m}}\bigg|_{\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}^{*}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{N_{c}}{8\pi^{2}}\left((\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}+\tilde{G}_{P}^{*})-(\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}-\tilde{G}_{P}^{*})\cos\theta^{*}\right),

(23)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{C_{4}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\beta_{C4}}{\partial\tilde{C}_{4}}\bigg|_{\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}^{*}}=-2+\frac{N_{c}}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}+\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}\right),

(24)

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\theta}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\beta_{\theta}}{\partial\theta}\bigg|_{\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}^{*}}=-\frac{N_{c}}{4\pi^{2}}\left(\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}-\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}\right)\cos\theta^{*}.

(25)

We exhibit the critical exponents at the Gaussian fixed point and FP3 for which the
CP
phase
\theta
is unphysical.
At the Gaussian fixed point, the critical exponents are identical with the canonical dimensions of couplings, i.e., we have
V_{i}^{j}=\delta_{i}^{j}
and

\displaystyle\vartheta_{m}=1,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{S}}=-2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{P}}=-2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{C_{4}}=-2,

(26)

while at FP3, we find

\displaystyle\vartheta_{m}=3,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{S}}=2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{P}}=2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{C_{4}}=2.

(27)

At FP3, all four-fermion couplings become relevant, indicating the emergence of a large anomalous dimension due to quantum effects that cannot be captured by perturbative methods.
In particular, an important fact here is that
\tilde{C}_{4}
becomes relevant at FP3 even if its fixed-point value is zero.
In the next subsection, we will see that
\tilde{C}_{4}
is driven by
\tilde{G}_{S}
and
\tilde{G}_{P}
and grows in the IR regime.

At the nontrivial fixed points (
18
) and (
19
), we compute eigenvalues and obtain, at FP1,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{m}=3,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\theta}=-2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{S}}=2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{P}}=-2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{C_{4}}=0,

(28)

and at FP2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{m}=1,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\theta}=2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{S}}=-2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{G_{P}}=2,

\displaystyle\vartheta_{C_{4}}=0.

(29)

Since the
\beta
functions for
\tilde{m}
and
\theta
depend on
\tilde{G}_{S}-\tilde{G}_{P}
, their critical exponents are also highly dependent on the differences of the fixed point values between
\tilde{G}_{S}^{*}
and
\tilde{G}_{P}^{*}
.
As aforementioned, in QCD, the

symmetry is broken by the axial

III
Summary

We have shown that the perturbatively irrelevant
CP
(and
P
) violating four-fermion operators can be relevant due to the nonperturbatively yielded large anomalous dimensions triggered by
the criticality of the dynamical chiral symmetry breaking.
Consequently, the strong
CP
violation can be amplified in the chiral broken phase with the presence of the
U(1)_{A}
anomaly and the

CP
phase approaches
\pi\cdot(N_{f}/2)
, even if it is tuned to vanish in the (seemingly) perturbative regime of QCD at the scales
\gtrsim 1
GeV.
Although this trend has been clarified in the large-
N_{c}
limit by working on a four-fermion model, the
1/N_{c}
subleading four-fermion term corrections seem to be suppressed enough.
This is because those do not dominantly participate in the dynamical chiral symmetry breaking via the scalar condensate of the quark bilinear,
which governs the IR dynamics of QCD.
Thus, our conclusions remain robust.

The setup in this work allows us to investigate the RG flow of
\theta
until the critical scale of dynamical chiral symmetry breaking.
The further IR property of strong
CP
violation is, however, of importance.
The measurement of the neutron electron dipole moment is indeed physics with the photon transfer energy (the applied electric field strength)
\sim 10^{-6}
–
10^{-5}
GeV
[
1
,
2
]
, which is much less than the typical QCD scale
\sim 0.1
–
1
GeV.
The state-of-the-art theoretical estimates based on the lattice QCD at the physical point
[
65
]

have been done for the transfer momentum in a range
\sim 200
–
400
MeV, in which, however, the measurement is irrespective to
\bar{\theta}
, and hence does not address its transfer momentum dependence, i.e., the RG scale.
To extend the RG flow analysis beyond the scale of dynamical chiral symmetry breaking within the fRG framework, additional techniques such as (dynamical) bosonization
[
55
,
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
and the weak formulation
[
48
,
62
]
may be necessary.
We plan to explore these approaches in future studies on
CP
-violating four-fermion models.

Our findings have significant implications for modeling spontaneous
CP
-violation scenarios. To cancel the induced
\bar{\theta}=\pi\cdot(N_{f}/2)
, additional CP-violating contributions from physics beyond the QCD sector would be required at energy scales above the QCD intrinsic scale. Otherwise, the associated electric dipole moments (EDMs), including the quark EDM, chromo EDM, and neutron EDM, could be dynamically amplified.

Appendix A
Euclidean spacetime

In this appendix, we provide our convention for the conversion from the Minkowski signature to the Euclidean one.

The world line in Minkowski spacetime is given by

\displaystyle\text{d}s^{2}=\text{d}t^{2}-\text{d}{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}}^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}\text{d}x^{\mu}\text{d}x^{\nu}\,,

(32)

where the metric is defined as
g_{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
.
Now, we introduce the coordinate vectors
x_{E\mu}
in Euclidean spacetime as

\displaystyle t=x^{0}

\displaystyle=-ix_{E0}=-i\tau\,,

\displaystyle x^{i}

\displaystyle=x_{Ei}\,.

(33)

Then, the norm becomes

\displaystyle x_{\mu}x^{\mu}=(x^{0})^{2}-(x^{i})^{2}=(-ix_{E0})^{2}-(x_{Ei})^{2}=-(x_{E0}^{2}+x_{Ei}^{2})=-x_{E\mu}x_{E\mu}\,.

(34)

In Euclidean spacetime, we do not have to take care about covariance and contravariance of vectors.
Below, we summarize quantities in Euclidean spacetime

a)

Vector
V_{\mu}=\{x_{\mu},p_{\mu},\cdots\}
,

\displaystyle V^{0}

\displaystyle=-iV_{E0}\,,

\displaystyle V^{i}

\displaystyle=V_{Ei}\,.

(35)

Then we have

\displaystyle V_{\mu}V^{\mu}=-V_{E\mu}V_{E\mu}\,.

(36)

b)

Derivative operator
\partial_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{0}},~\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\right)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial(-ix_{E0})},~\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{Ei}}\right)=\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{E0}},~\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{Ei}}\right)=(i\partial_{E0},~\partial_{Ei})\,.

(37)

c)

Gamma matrices
\gamma_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{E0}\,,

\displaystyle\gamma^{i}

\displaystyle=i\gamma_{Ei}\,.

(38)

Then, the Clifford algebra
\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu}
in Minkowski spacetime becomes

\displaystyle\{\gamma^{0},\gamma^{0}\}

\displaystyle=\{\gamma_{E0},\gamma_{E0}\}=2\,,

\displaystyle\{\gamma^{i},\gamma^{j}\}

\displaystyle=\{i\gamma_{Ei},i\gamma_{Ej}\}=-\{\gamma_{Ei},\gamma_{Ej}\}=-2\delta_{ij}\,.

(39)

Thus, the Clifford algebra in Euclidean spacetime reads

\displaystyle\{\gamma_{E\mu},\gamma_{E\nu}\}=2\delta_{\mu\nu}=2\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\\
&1&&\\
&&1&\\
&&&1\end{pmatrix}\,.

(40)

The hermiticity of
\gamma

\displaystyle(\gamma^{0})^{\dagger}=\gamma^{0}\Longrightarrow(\gamma_{E0})^{\dagger}=\gamma_{E0}\,,

\displaystyle(\gamma^{i})^{\dagger}=-\gamma^{i}\Longrightarrow(\gamma_{Ei})^{\dagger}=\gamma_{Ei}\,.

(41)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle(\gamma_{E\mu})^{\dagger}=\gamma_{E\mu}\,.

(42)

The chirality matrix
\gamma^{5}
is

\displaystyle\gamma^{5}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}=i\gamma_{E0}i\gamma_{E1}i\gamma_{E2}i\gamma_{E3}=\gamma_{E0}\gamma_{E1}\gamma_{E2}\gamma_{E3}=\gamma_{E5}\,.

(43)

d)

Fields,

–

Scalar,

\displaystyle\phi\!\left(x\right)=\phi_{E}\!\left(x_{E}\right)\,.

(44)

–

Spinor,

\displaystyle\psi\!\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\psi_{E}\!\left(x_{E}\right),

\displaystyle{\bar{\psi}}\!\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\psi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\!\left(x\right)=\psi^{\dagger}_{E}\gamma_{E0}\!\left(x_{E}\right)={\bar{\psi}}_{E}\!\left(x_{E}\right)\,.

(45)

–

Vector,

\displaystyle A^{0}\!\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=-iA_{E0}\!\left(x_{E}\right)\,,

\displaystyle A^{i}\!\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=A_{Ei}\!\left(x_{E}\right)\,.

(46)

Note that
\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi
is a vector; however, because of Eq. (
38
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma^{0}\psi=\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{E0}\psi_{E}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma^{i}\psi=i\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{Ei}\psi_{E}\,,

(47)

which is different from Eqs. (
35
) and (
46
).
The vector-type four-Fermion interaction becomes

\displaystyle(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{0}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{0}\psi)-(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{i}\psi)(\bar{\psi}\gamma^{i}\psi)

\displaystyle=(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{E0}\psi_{E})(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{E0}\psi_{E})-(\bar{\psi}_{E}i\gamma_{Ei}\psi_{E})(\bar{\psi}_{E}i\gamma_{Ei}\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{E0}\psi_{E})(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{E0}\psi_{E})+(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{Ei}\psi_{E})(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{Ei}\psi_{E})

\displaystyle=(\bar{\psi}_{E}\gamma_{E\mu}\psi_{E})^{2}\,.

(48)

e)

Fourier transformations,

\displaystyle\phi_{E}\!\left(x_{E}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ip_{E}\cdot x_{E}}{\tilde{\phi}}_{E}\!\left(p_{E}\right)\,,

(49)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ip_{E}\cdot x_{E}}{\tilde{\psi}}_{E}\!\left(p_{E}\right)\,,

(50)

\displaystyle{\bar{\psi}}_{E}\!\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p_{E}}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-ip_{E}\cdot x_{E}}{\bar{\tilde{\psi}}}_{E}\!\left(p_{E}\right)\,.

(51)

” denoting the Euclidean signature.

Appendix B
Derivation of flow equations

The central method in this work is the Wetterich equation whose form is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm STr}\left[\left(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)^{-1}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\partial}_{t}{\rm STr}\log\left[\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]\,,

(57)

where
\tilde{\partial}_{t}=(\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathcal{R}_{k}}
is the scale derivative acting only on the regulator function.
In general, the Hessian
\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}
is given as the supermatrix in superfield space. Namely, we can write in the form of

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}(p)+\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{T}(-p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi(p)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}&M_{\rm BF}\\[8.61108pt]
M_{\rm FB}&M_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(58)

where
\Phi=(\phi,\psi,\bar{\psi}^{T},\cdots)
is the superfield.
In what follows, we consider the case of the fermionic system.

B.1
Supermatrix, superdeterminant and supertrace

We summarize the useful formulas for supermatrix to evaluate the Wetterich equation.
We begin by considering a supermatrix in a form of

\displaystyle M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}&M_{\rm BF}\\[8.61108pt]
M_{\rm FB}&M_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(59)

Here,
M_{\rm BB}
and
M_{\rm FF}
are Grassmann-even elements, while
M_{\rm BF}
and
M_{\rm FB}
are Grassmann odd. We define the supertrace for the supermatrix (
59
) as

\displaystyle{\rm str}~M={\rm tr}~M_{\rm BB}-{\rm tr}~M_{\rm FF}\,,

(60)

which satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm str}~(MN)={\rm str}~(NM)\,.

(61)

With this definition, the superdeterminant is defined by

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}~M=\exp({\rm str}~{\rm ln}~M)\,,

(62)

such that

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}~(MN)={\rm sdet}~M\cdot{\rm sdet}~N\,.

(63)

Now, we deform
M
as

\displaystyle M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}&0\\[8.61108pt]
M_{\rm FB}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&M_{\rm BB}^{-1}M_{\rm BF}\\[8.61108pt]
0&N_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(64)

where we have defined

\displaystyle N_{\rm FF}=M_{\rm FF}-M_{\rm FB}M_{\rm BB}^{-1}M_{\rm BF}\,.

(65)

From Eq. (
63
), we have

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}~M={\rm sdet}\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}&0\\[8.61108pt]
M_{\rm FB}&1\end{pmatrix}{\rm sdet}\begin{pmatrix}1&M_{\rm BB}^{-1}M_{\rm BF}\\[8.61108pt]
0&N_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(66)

Here, using Eq. (
62
) gives

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}&0\\[8.61108pt]
M_{\rm FB}&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{{\rm str}~{\rm ln}\left[\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[4.30554pt]
0&1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}-1&0\\[4.30554pt]
M_{\rm FB}&1\end{pmatrix}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{{\rm str}\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(-)^{n-1}}{n}\begin{pmatrix}M_{\rm BB}-1&0\\[4.30554pt]
M_{\rm FB}&1\end{pmatrix}\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{{\rm tr}\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(-)^{n-1}}{n}(M_{\rm BB}-1)^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\{{\rm tr~ln}~M_{\rm BB}\}

\displaystyle={\rm det}~M_{\rm BB}\,.

(67)

In the same manner, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}\begin{pmatrix}1&M_{\rm BB}^{-1}M_{\rm BF}\\[8.61108pt]
0&N_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\exp\{-{\rm tr~ln}~N_{\rm FF}\}=({\rm det}~N_{\rm FF})^{-1}\,.

Note here that the minus sign arises from Eq. (
60
). Finally, we arrive at

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}~M=\frac{{\rm det}~M_{\rm BB}}{{\rm det}~N_{\rm FF}}\,.

(68)

This implies that

\displaystyle\log{\rm sdet}~M

\displaystyle=\log{\rm det}~M_{\rm BB}-\log{\rm det}(M_{\rm FF}-M_{\rm FB}M_{\rm BB}^{-1}M_{\rm BF})

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\log M_{\rm BB}-{\rm tr}\,\log(M_{\rm FF}-M_{\rm FB}M_{\rm BB}^{-1}M_{\rm BF})\,.

(69)

Instead of Eq. (
64
), the supermatrix (
59
) can also be deformed as

\displaystyle M=\begin{pmatrix}N_{\rm BB}&M_{\rm BF}M_{\rm FF}^{-1}\\[8.61108pt]
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\[8.61108pt]
M_{\rm FB}&M_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(70)

Here, we have defined

\displaystyle N_{\rm BB}=M_{\rm BB}-M_{\rm BF}M_{\rm FF}^{-1}M_{\rm FB}

(71)

Then, the superdeterminant for
M
can be written in the form as

\displaystyle{\rm sdet}~M=\frac{{\rm det}~N_{\rm BB}}{{\rm det}~M_{\rm FF}}\,,

(72)

for which we have

\displaystyle\log{\rm sdet}~M

\displaystyle=\log{\rm det}~(M_{\rm BB}-M_{\rm BF}M_{\rm FF}^{-1}M_{\rm FB})-\log{\rm det}M_{\rm FF}

\displaystyle={\rm tr}\,\log(M_{\rm BB}-M_{\rm BF}M_{\rm FF}^{-1}M_{\rm FB})-{\rm tr}\,\log M_{\rm FF}\,.

(73)

B.2
General structure of flow equation

For the pure fermionic system, the supermatrix (
59
) is

\displaystyle M\equiv\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}(p)=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi^{T}(-p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\Phi(p)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&M_{\rm FF}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(74)

Here, we have the fermionic part in the field basis
\Phi(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi(p)\\[4.30554pt]
\bar{\psi}^{T}(-p)\end{pmatrix}
and
\Phi^{T}(-p)=\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{T}(-p)&\bar{\psi}(p)\end{pmatrix}
as

\displaystyle M_{\rm FF}=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi^{T}(-p)}\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}(p)\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi(p)}&&\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi^{T}(-p)}\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}(p)\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}^{T}(-p)}\\[12.91663pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}(p)}\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}(p)\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi(p)}&&\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}(p)}\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}(p)\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}^{T}(-p)}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(75)

We briefly summarize several techniques to derive the
\beta
functions. For a given effective action
\Gamma_{k}
, we compute the second-order functional derivative. Together with the regulator function, one schematically has

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}(p)\Big|_{\text{FF}}=(\mathcal{K}_{k}+\mathcal{R}_{k})+\mathcal{V}_{k}=\mathcal{P}_{k}^{-1}+\mathcal{V}_{k}\,,

(76)

where
\mathcal{K}_{k}
is the field-independent part, while
\mathcal{V}_{k}
includes the vertex terms depending on field variables. More specifically, for an effective action, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{k}(p,-p)=\begin{pmatrix}\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi^{T}(-p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi(p)}&&\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi^{T}(-p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}^{T}(-p)}\\[12.91663pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}(p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi(p)}&&\displaystyle\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}^{T}(p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}^{T}(-p)}\end{pmatrix}\Bigg|_{\psi=\bar{\psi}=0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&K_{k}^{T}(p)\\
K_{k}(p)&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)\,.

(77)

The regulator matrix is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(p)=\begin{pmatrix}0&R^{f}_{k}(p)^{T}\\
R^{f}_{k}(p)&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)=\begin{pmatrix}0&i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}^{T}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)\\
i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)\,.

(78)

There are many choices for the regulator
r^{f}_{k}(p/k)
. In this work, we employ the Litim-type cutoff
[
38
]
given as

\displaystyle r^{f}_{k}(p/k)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{k^{2}}{p^{2}}}-1\right)\Theta(k^{2}-p^{2})\,.

(79)

Then,
\mathcal{P}_{k}
is just the regulated inverse propagator such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{k}^{-1}(p,-p)=\begin{pmatrix}0&(P_{k}^{-1})^{T}\\
P_{k}^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&(K_{k}(p)+R^{f}_{k}(p))^{T}\\
K_{k}(p)+R^{f}_{k}(p)&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)\,.

(80)

For the vertex matrix, we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}V_{11}&V_{12}\\[4.30554pt]
V_{21}&V_{22}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(81)

Note here that
V_{12}=-(V_{21})^{T}
.

Once we compute Eq. (
76
), the regulated full propagator is obtained by the expansion in terms of the vertex operator
\mathcal{V}_{k}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{k}(p)\Big|_{\text{FF}}=\left(\Gamma_{k}^{(2)}+\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)^{-1}=\mathcal{P}_{k}-\mathcal{P}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{k}+\mathcal{P}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{k}+\cdots.

(82)

Inserting this into flow equation (
3
) and using Eqs. (
80
) and (
81
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Gamma_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\partial}_{t}{\rm STr}\log\,\left[\mathcal{G}_{k}^{-1}|_{\text{FF}}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\partial}_{t}{\rm STr}\log\,\left[\mathcal{P}_{k}^{-1}+\mathcal{V}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\rm STr}\left[\mathcal{P}_{k}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]-\frac{1}{2}{\rm STr}\left[\mathcal{P}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{k}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]+\frac{1}{2}{\rm STr}\left[\mathcal{P}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{k}\partial_{t}\mathcal{R}_{k}\right]+\cdots

\displaystyle=-{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}\partial_{t}R^{f}_{k}\right]+\left(\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}V_{21}P_{k}\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]+\frac{1}{2}{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}^{T}V_{12}P_{k}^{T}\partial_{t}(R_{k}^{f})^{T}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-{\rm Tr}\,\left[(P_{k}V_{21}P_{k}V_{21}\mathcal{P}_{k}-P_{k}V_{22}P_{k}^{T}V_{11}\mathcal{P}_{k})\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]+\cdots

\displaystyle=-{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}\partial_{t}R^{f}_{k}\right]+{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}V_{21}P_{k}\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]-{\rm Tr}\,\left[(P_{k}V_{21}P_{k}V_{21}P_{k}-P_{k}V_{22}P_{k}^{T}V_{11}P_{k})\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]+\cdots\,.

(83)

Note that the minus sign in Eq. (
83
) reflects the definition of the supertrace (
60
). The first and second terms on the left-hand side of Eq. (
83
) correspond to the quantum corrections to the vacuum energy and the mass term. We are interested especially in the third term which gives the quantum corrections to the four-Fermi interactions.

B.3
Application to four-Fermion model

Here, based on the previous formulas, we show the explicit computation for deriving the flow equations in the four-fermion model in Eq. (
7
). The truncated effective action in our work is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\int\text{d}^{4}x\Bigg[\bar{\psi}\left(\gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)\psi-\frac{G_{S}}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}-\frac{G_{P}}{2}\left(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\right)^{2}-C_{4}\,(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi)\Bigg]\,.

(84)

In momentum space, the kinetic term is written in the form as

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{4}x\,\bar{\psi}\left(\gamma_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)\psi

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\text{d}^{4}q}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\text{d}^{4}x\,e^{i(p-q)\cdot x}\bar{\psi}(q)\left(i\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}+me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)\psi(p)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\bar{\psi}(p)\left(i\gamma_{\mu}p_{\mu}+me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)\psi(p),

(85)

from which the kinetic matrix
\mathcal{K}_{k}
in momentum space is found to be

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}_{k}(p,-p))_{IJ}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&\left(i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}-me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IJ}^{T}\\[8.61108pt]
\left(i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}+me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IJ}&&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)\,.

(86)

Together with the regulator matrix (
78
), the regulated inverse propagator reads

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{k}^{-1}(p,-p))_{IJ}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&\left(i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})-me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)^{T}_{IJ}\\[8.61108pt]
\left(i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IJ}&&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)\,.

(87)

Its inverse form is

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{k}(p,-p))_{IJ}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IJ}}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}\\[8.61108pt]
\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})-me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)^{T}_{IJ}}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}&&0\end{pmatrix}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)\,.

(88)

The vertex matrix (
81
) is calculated as

\displaystyle\left(V_{11}\right)_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi_{I}(-p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi_{J}(p)}

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{S}}{2}\Big[\bar{\psi}_{i,M}\delta_{MI}\bar{\psi}_{N}\delta_{NJ}\Big]+\frac{G_{P}}{2}\Big[\bar{\psi}_{M}(i\gamma_{5})_{MI}\bar{\psi}_{N}(i\gamma_{5})_{NJ}\Big]+C_{4}\Big[\bar{\psi}_{i,M}\delta_{MI}\bar{\psi}_{N}(i\gamma_{5})_{NJ}\Big]\,,

(89)

\displaystyle\left(V_{22}\right)_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}_{I}(p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}_{J}(-p)}

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{S}}{2}\Big[\delta_{IM}\psi_{M}\delta_{JN}\psi_{N}\Big]+\frac{G_{P}}{2}\Big[(i\gamma_{5})_{IM}\psi_{M}(i\gamma_{5})_{JN}\psi_{N}\Big]+C_{4}\Big[\delta_{IM}\psi_{M}(i\gamma_{5})_{JN}\psi_{N}\Big]\,,

(90)

\displaystyle\left(V_{21}\right)_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}_{I}(p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi_{J}(p)}

\displaystyle=-G_{S}\Big[\delta_{IJ}\bar{\psi}\psi\Big]-G_{P}\Big[(i\gamma_{5})_{IJ}\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\Big]-C_{4}\Big[\delta_{IJ}\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\Big]-C_{4}\Big[\bar{\psi}\psi(i\gamma_{5})_{IJ}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-G_{S}\Big[\delta_{IM}\psi_{M}\bar{\psi}_{N}\delta_{NJ}\Big]-G_{P}\Big[(i\gamma_{5})_{IM}\psi_{M}\bar{\psi}_{N}(i\gamma_{5})_{NJ}\Big]-C_{4}\Big[\delta_{IM}\psi_{M}\bar{\psi}_{N}(i\gamma_{5})_{NJ}\Big]-C_{4}\Big[(i\gamma_{5})_{IM}\psi_{M}\bar{\psi}_{N}\delta_{NJ}\Big]\,,

(91)

\displaystyle\left(V_{12}\right)_{IJ}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overrightarrow{\delta}}{\delta\psi_{I}(-p)}\Gamma_{k}\frac{\overleftarrow{\delta}}{\delta\bar{\psi}_{J}(-p)}=-\left(V_{21}\right)_{IJ}\,.

(92)

The large-
N_{c}
approximation corresponds to the following replacement:
(V_{11})_{IJ}\to 0
,
(V_{22})_{IJ}\to 0
,

\displaystyle(V_{21})_{IJ}\to(V_{21}^{\rm LN})_{IJ}=-G_{S}\Big[\delta_{IJ}\bar{\psi}\psi\Big]-G_{P}\Big[(i\gamma_{5})_{IJ}\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\Big]-C_{4}\Big[\delta_{IJ}\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\Big]-C_{4}\Big[\bar{\psi}\psi(i\gamma_{5})_{IJ}\Big],

(93)

\displaystyle\left(V_{12}\right)_{IJ}\to-\left[(V_{21}^{\rm LN})\right]^{T}_{IJ}.

(94)

B.3.1
Vacuum energy

First, we evaluate the vacuum energy part which is independent of field operators. From the first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
83
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{k}=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{4}}{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}\partial_{t}R^{f}_{k}\right]

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm tr}\,\left[\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IJ}}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}i({\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}})_{JK}\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)\delta^{\text{color}}_{\alpha\beta}\right]

\displaystyle=-4N_{c}\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[\frac{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{4N_{c}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{k^{2}}\text{d}p^{2}\,p^{2}\left[\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+m^{2}}\right]\,,

(99)

where
\Omega_{4}=\int\text{d}^{4}x=(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(0)
is the four-dimensional spacetime volume and
{\rm tr}\,\delta^{\text{color}}_{\alpha\beta}=\delta^{\text{color}}_{\alpha\alpha}=N_{c}
.
This contribution, however, does not influence upon the spinor dynamics, so is irrelevant in the current work.

B.3.2
Mass term

From the second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
83
), we read off quantum corrections for the mass term with the CP and P violating angle
\theta
, such that

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\partial_{t}(me^{i\theta\gamma_{5}/2})\psi=\partial_{t}(m\cos(\theta/2))\bar{\psi}\psi+\partial_{t}(m\sin(\theta/2))\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi=\frac{1}{\Omega_{4}}{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}(V_{21}^{\rm LN})P_{k}\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}{\rm tr}\,\left[\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IK}}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}(V_{21}^{\rm LN})_{KL}\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{LM}}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}i({\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}})_{MJ}\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}{\rm tr}\,\left[\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{IK}(V_{21}^{\rm LN})_{KL}\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)_{LM}i({\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}})_{MJ}\right]

\displaystyle=-\left[8N_{c}G_{S}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)+8N_{c}C_{4}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right]m\mathcal{M}(\bar{\psi}\psi)-\left[8N_{c}G_{P}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)+8N_{c}C_{4}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right]m\mathcal{M}(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi)\,,

(112)

where we have defined the threshold function as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{p^{2}(1+r_{k}^{f})\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{k^{6}}{(k^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}.

(113)

We can read off the flow equations as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(m\cos(\theta/2))=-8N_{c}G_{S}m\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\mathcal{M}-8N_{c}C_{4}m\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\mathcal{M}\,,

(114)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(m\sin(\theta/2))=-8N_{c}G_{P}m\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\mathcal{M}-8N_{c}C_{4}m\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\mathcal{M}\,,

(115)

from which we have the flow equations for
m
and
\theta
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m

\displaystyle=\cos(\theta/2)\partial_{t}(m\cos(\theta/2))+\sin(\theta/2)\partial_{t}(m\sin(\theta/2))

\displaystyle=-4N_{c}\left[(G_{S}+G_{P})+2C_{4}\sin\theta+(G_{S}-G_{P})\cos\theta\right]m\mathcal{M}\,,

(116)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m}\left[-\sin(\theta/2)\partial_{t}(m\cos(\theta/2))+\cos(\theta/2)\partial_{t}(m\sin(\theta/2))\right]

\displaystyle=8N_{c}\left[(G_{S}-G_{P})\sin\theta-2C_{4}\cos\theta\right]{\mathcal{M}}\,.

(117)

Here we define the dimensionless couplings

\displaystyle\tilde{m}=mk^{-1},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{S}=G_{S}k^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{P}=G_{P}k^{2},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{4}=C_{4}k^{2}.

(118)

Then, the flow equations for
\tilde{m}
and
\theta
read

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{m}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{m}-4N_{c}\left[(\tilde{G}_{S}+\tilde{G}_{P})+2\tilde{C}_{4}\sin\theta+(\tilde{G}_{S}-\tilde{G}_{P})\cos\theta\right]\tilde{m}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\,,

(119)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta

\displaystyle=8\left[(\tilde{G}_{S}-\tilde{G}_{P})\sin\theta-2\tilde{C}_{4}\cos\theta\right]\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\,,

(120)

where the dimensionless threshold function is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k^{2}}\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{p^{2}(1+r_{k}^{f})\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\tilde{m}^{2})^{2}}\,.

(121)

B.3.3
Four-Fermion interactions

The third term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
83
) corresponds to quantum corrections for the four-fermion interactions which are obtained from the flow equation as

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{t}G_{S}}{2}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}-\frac{\partial_{t}G_{P}}{2}(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi)^{2}-\partial_{t}C_{4}(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi)=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{4}}{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}V_{21}^{\text{LN}}P_{k}V_{21}^{\text{LN}}P_{k}\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right].

(122)

We compute the right-hand side as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\Omega_{4}}{\rm Tr}\,\left[P_{k}V_{21}^{\text{LN}}P_{k}V_{21}^{\text{LN}}P_{k}\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\Omega_{4}}{\rm Tr}\,\left[\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}V_{21}\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}V_{21}\frac{\left(-i{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil/\hfil\crcr$p$\crcr}}}(1+r^{f}_{k})+me^{-i\theta\gamma_{5}/2}\right)}{p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2}}\partial_{t}R_{k}^{f}\right]

\displaystyle=-\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{p^{2}(1+r_{k}^{f})\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{3}}\Bigg[-N_{c}\left\{(4G_{S}^{2}+4C_{4}^{2})(k^{2}-m^{2})-2m^{2}(4G_{S}^{2}-4C_{4}^{2})\cos\theta-16m^{2}G_{S}C_{4}\sin\theta\right\}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-N_{c}\left\{(4G_{P}^{2}+4C_{4}^{2})(k^{2}-m^{2})+2m^{2}(4G_{P}^{2}-4C_{4}^{2})\cos\theta-16m^{2}G_{P}C_{4}\sin\theta\right\}(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-4N_{c}\left\{2(G_{S}+G_{P})C_{4}(k^{2}-m^{2})-4m^{2}(G_{S}-G_{P})C_{4}\cos\theta-m^{2}(4G_{S}G_{P}+4C_{4}^{2})\sin\theta\right\}(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\Bigg[4N_{c}\left\{(G_{S}^{2}+C_{4}^{2})(k^{2}-m^{2})+2m^{2}(G_{S}^{2}-C_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4m^{2}G_{S}C_{4}\sin\theta\right\}\Bigg]\mathcal{I}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigg[4N_{c}\left\{(G_{P}^{2}+C_{4}^{2})(k^{2}-m^{2})-2m^{2}(G_{P}^{2}-C_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4m^{2}G_{P}C_{4}\sin\theta\right\}\Bigg]\mathcal{I}(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\Bigg[8N_{c}\left\{(G_{S}+G_{P})C_{4}(k^{2}-m^{2})+2m^{2}(G_{S}-G_{P})C_{4}\cos\theta+2m^{2}(G_{S}G_{P}+C_{4}^{2})\sin\theta\right\}\Bigg]\mathcal{I}(\bar{\psi}\psi)(\bar{\psi}i\gamma^{5}\psi),

(129)

where we have defined the threshold function

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{p^{2}(1+r_{k}^{f})\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{3}}=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{k^{6}}{(k^{2}+m^{2})^{3}}.

(130)

Then, we obtain the flow equations

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{t}G_{S}}{2}

\displaystyle=4N_{c}\left\{(G_{S}^{2}+C_{4}^{2})(k^{2}-m^{2})+2m^{2}(G_{S}^{2}-C_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4m^{2}G_{S}C_{4}\sin\theta\right\}\mathcal{I},

(131)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial_{t}G_{P}}{2}

\displaystyle=4N_{c}\left\{(G_{P}^{2}+C_{4}^{2})(k^{2}-m^{2})-2m^{2}(G_{P}^{2}-C_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4m^{2}G_{P}C_{4}\sin\theta\right\}\mathcal{I},

(132)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}C_{4}

\displaystyle=8N_{c}\left\{(G_{S}+G_{P})C_{4}(k^{2}-m^{2})+2m^{2}(G_{S}-G_{P})C_{4}\cos\theta+2m^{2}(G_{S}G_{P}+C_{4}^{2})\sin\theta\right\}\mathcal{I}.

(133)

For the dimensionless couplings (
118
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{G}_{S}

\displaystyle=2\tilde{G}_{S}-8N_{c}\Big[(\tilde{G}_{S}^{2}+\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})(1-\tilde{m}^{2})+2\tilde{m}^{2}(\tilde{G}_{S}^{2}-\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4\tilde{m}^{2}\tilde{G}_{S}\tilde{C}_{4}\sin\theta\Big]\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}\,,

(134)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{G}_{P}

\displaystyle=2\tilde{G}_{P}-8N_{c}\Big[(\tilde{G}_{P}^{2}+\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})(1-\tilde{m}^{2})-2\tilde{m}^{2}(\tilde{G}_{P}^{2}-\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})\cos\theta+4\tilde{m}^{2}\tilde{G}_{P}\tilde{C}_{4}\sin\theta\Big]\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}\,,

(135)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{C}_{4}

\displaystyle=2\tilde{C}_{4}-8N_{c}\Big[(\tilde{G}_{S}+\tilde{G}_{P})\tilde{C}_{4}(1-\tilde{m}^{2})+2\tilde{m}^{2}(\tilde{G}_{S}-\tilde{G}_{P})\tilde{C}_{4}\cos\theta+2\tilde{m}^{2}(\tilde{G}_{S}\tilde{G}_{P}+\tilde{C}_{4}^{2})\sin\theta\Big]\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}\,,

(136)

with the dimensionless threshold function

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\text{d}^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{p^{2}(1+r_{k}^{f})\partial_{t}r^{f}_{k}(p/k)}{(p^{2}(1+r^{f}_{k})^{2}+m^{2})^{3}}=\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\tilde{m}^{2})^{3}}\,.

(137)

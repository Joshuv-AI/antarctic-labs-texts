Abstract
. Given a cardinal κ \kappa and filters ℱ α \mathcal{F}_{\alpha} on ω \omega for α ∈ κ \alpha\in\kappa , we will show that if ∏ α ∈ κ ℱ α \prod_{\alpha\in\kappa}\mathcal{F}_{\alpha} is countable dense homogeneous then κ < 𝔭 \kappa<\mathfrak{p} and each ℱ α \mathcal{F}_{\alpha} is a non-meager 𝖯 \mathsf{P} -filter. This partially answers a question of Michael Hrušák. Along the way, we will show that the product of fewer than 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} non-meager 𝖯 \mathsf{P} -filters has the Miller property. We will also describe explicitly the connection between Miller-measurability and the Miller property. As a corollary, we will see that the intersection of fewer than 𝖺𝖽𝖽 ⁡ ( m 0 ) \mathsf{add}(m^{0}) non-meager 𝖯 \mathsf{P} -filters is a non-meager 𝖯 \mathsf{P} -filter, where m 0 m^{0} denotes the ideal of Miller-null sets. We will conclude by investigating the preservation of the Miller property under intersections and products.

3.
Preliminaries concerning filters

Recall that a
filter
is a collection
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
that satisfies the following conditions:

•

\varnothing\notin\mathcal{F}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
,

•

If
x\in\mathcal{F}
and
x=^{\ast}y\subseteq\omega
then
y\in\mathcal{F}
,

•

If
x\in\mathcal{F}
and
x\subseteq y\subseteq\omega
then
y\in\mathcal{F}
,

•

If
x,y\in\mathcal{F}
then
x\cap y\in\mathcal{F}
.

We will use the same terminology when we consider a
filter
on a forcing poset
\mathbb{P}
, in which case however we refer to
[
Ku
, Definition III.3.10]
.

As usual, we will identify every subset of
\omega
with its characteristic function, so that every
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\omega)
will be endowed with the subspace topology that it inherits from
2^{\omega}
. Naturally, the relevant case here is when
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{F}
is a filter. In particular, by
non-meager filter
we mean a filter
\mathcal{F}
that is a non-meager space with this topology. Since every filter is dense in
2^{\omega}
, this is equivalent to
\mathcal{F}
being a non-meager subset of
2^{\omega}
. The following well-known characterization of non-meager filters originally appeared as part of
[
Ta
, Théorème 21]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Talagrand)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filter. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

•

\mathcal{F}
is non-meager,

•

Given pairwise disjoint
a_{n}\in\mathsf{Fin}
for
n\in\omega
, there exist
z\in\mathcal{F}
and
I\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
z\cap a_{n}=\varnothing
for every
n\in I
.

In fact, given a sufficiently small family of non-meager filters, a single
I
can be found that works for every filter. This is the content of the following result, which will be useful in the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\kappa<\mathfrak{t}
be a cardinal, and let
\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
be non-meager filters for
\alpha\in\kappa
. Then, given pairwise disjoint
a_{n}\in\mathsf{Fin}
for
n\in\omega
, there exist
z_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{F}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\kappa
and
I\in[\omega]^{\omega}
such that
z_{\alpha}\cap a_{n}=\varnothing
for every
\alpha\in\kappa
and
n\in I
.

Proof.

Let
a_{n}\in\mathsf{Fin}
for
n\in\omega
be pairwise disjoint. Using Lemma

\kappa<\mathfrak{t}
, one can easily construct

and

for

•

w_{\alpha}\cap a_{n}=\varnothing
for every

Once the construction is completed, let

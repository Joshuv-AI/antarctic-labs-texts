LINC01537 Regulates Malignant Phenotypes and Predicts Prognosis in Breast Cancer, and Its Bioinformatics Analysis
Authors: Yuetong Guo, Libo Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBreast cancer is a highly heterogeneous malignancy and constitutes one of the leading health threats to women globally. This study aimed to investigate the expression profile, clinical relevance, and molecular mechanisms of LINC01537 in breast cancer. Quantitative real‐time PCR (qRT‐PCR) was performed to detect the expression of LINC01537, miR‐138‐5p, and EZH2 in breast cancer tissues and cells. The Kaplan‐Meier method was used for survival analysis, and forest plots were constructed to evaluate the prognostic significance of LINC01537. To explore the role of LINC01537 in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and invasion, LINC01537 was knocked down in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells, followed by CCK‐8, flow cytometry, and Transwell assays. A potential competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory network was investigated through bioinformatics analysis, dual‐luciferase reporter assays, and correlation analyses. Rescue experiments were further performed to confirm that LINC01537 modulates breast cancer progression via the miR‐138‐5p/EZH2 axis. LINC01537 was significantly upregulated in breast cancer tissues and cell lines compared to normal controls. High LINC01537 expression was associated with poor survival outcomes in patients. Knockdown of LINC01537 markedly suppressed proliferation, migration, and invasion, while promoting apoptosis in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells. Mechanically, LINC01537 functioned as a ceRNA by sponging miR‐138‐5p, thereby abrogating its inhibitory effect on the target gene EZH2. Rescue experiments suggested that the inhibition of the malignant phenotypes of BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells caused by LINC01537 knockdown was rescued by miR‐138‐5p inhibition. The LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p/EZH2 ceRNA axis is involved in the progression of TNBC and HER2‐positive breast cancer subtypes. In vitro data indicates that LINC01537 is a potential therapeutic candidate for TNBC and HER2‐positive breast cancer, while further in vivo animal validation is required to support its translational application.

Introduction

Breast cancer ranks as the most prevalent malignancy and the leading cause of cancer‐related mortality among women worldwide. Its intrinsic heterogeneity and complex pathogenesis present persistent challenges to clinical management and patient outcomes [1]. In fact, notwithstanding the increased early diagnosis and refinements in therapeutic regimens, such as surgery, chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, and targeted therapy, overall survival has considerably improved, yet many of the heterogeneous features keep important clinicopathological ones, like metastases, recurrent appearance, and resistance to therapies, which cause a poor prognosis on the clinically observable surface [2]. Therefore, elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying breast cancer and identifying novel prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets are critical for optimizing clinical decision‐making and improving patient survival.

Accumulating evidence has demonstrated the aberrant expression of long non‐coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in breast cancer, associating them with clinicopathological features and positioning them as promising molecular markers for diagnosis, therapy, and prognosis [3, 4, 5]. In gastric cancer tissues, LINC01537 is overexpressed and correlates with poor prognosis [6]. It has also been identified as an immune‐related signature and a prognostic indicator in lung adenocarcinoma [7], and as a predictor of patient survival in liver, bile duct, and pancreatic cancers [8]. In breast cancer, LINC01537 is upregulated and may serve as an independent factor besides other factors [9]. However, no comprehensive investigation has been conducted into its expression profile, biological functions, clinical significance, or regulatory mechanisms in breast cancer.

The online database lncRNASNP2 predicts complementary binding sites between miR‐138‐5p and LINC01537. miR‐138‐5p is widely recognized as a tumor suppressor in various cancers. Its downregulation has been reported in non‐small cell lung cancer tissues and cell lines, where it serves as a potential diagnostic marker [10]. miR‐138‐5p inhibits mitochondrial biogenesis and colorectal cancer growth [11], and suppresses proliferation and chemoresistance in prostate cancer cells by targeting APOBEC3B [12]. In breast cancer, miR‐138‐5p promotes pro‐tumoral macrophage polarization [13] and sensitizes cancer cells to paclitaxel chemotherapy [14]. Nevertheless, whether LINC01537 directly regulates miR‐138‐5p expression in breast cancer has not been established.

Accordingly, the present study aimed to evaluate the clinical prognostic value of LINC01537 in breast cancer, define its functional role in malignant phenotypes, and explore its downstream regulatory targets.

Materials and Methods

Subjects

All cases were pathologically confirmed by at least two independent pathologists. 135 breast cancer patients in Cangzhou People's Hospital were involved in the study. Eligible patients had received no prior radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy before surgical intervention and reported no history of additional malignant tumors. Paired tumor and adjacent normal tissue samples were collected during surgery. The study was conducted with the written informed consent of all participants and was authorized by the Ethical Committee on Scientific Research of Cangzhou People's Hospital. Tumor tissues were confirmed by pathological examination, immediately snap‐frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C. Overall survival was assessed by recording mortality during follow‐up visits or via telephone interviews. A complete 5‐year follow‐up was achieved for all patients, concluding either at death or at the last follow‐up point.

Cell Culture and Cell Transfection

The normal human mammary epithelial cell line MCF10A and breast cancer cell lines MDA‐MB‐453, MDA‐MB‐231, BT549, MCF‐7, and SK‐BR‐3 were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). MCF‐7 and MDA‐MB‐231 cells were grown in RPMI‐1640, while MCF10A, BT549, and MDA‐MB‐453 were maintained in DMEM (Invitrogen). Both media contained 10% FBS and an antibiotic mixture. SK‐BR‐3 cells were cultured in McCoy's 5A medium. All cell cultures were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2. According to RT‐qPCR screening, BT549 (TNBC) and SK‐BR‐3 (HER2+) exhibited the highest endogenous LINC01537 expression among detected cell lines. These two aggressive, metastasis‐prone subtypes were selected for functional validation. However, functional results derived from the two cell lines may not be universally applicable to luminal A/B or other breast cancer subtypes, as discussed in the Limitations section.

Small interfering RNA targeting LINC01537 (si‐LINC01537), LINC01537 overexpression vector (oe‐LINC01537), miR‐138‐5p mimics, and miR‐138‐5p inhibitors were commercially obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). si‐NC, oe‐NC, mimic NC, and inhibitor NC were used as negative controls. Cells were seeded in 6‐well plates for 24 h to reach 60%–70% confluence, and transfections were performed using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen).

RT‐qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from tissues and cells using TRIzol reagent (Takara Bio Inc.). RNA integrity and concentration were validated by evaluating the A260/A280 absorbance value using a microplate reader. Using 2 µg of RNA as the template, cDNA synthesis was carried out with the Transcriptor First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Tiangen Biotech Co. Ltd.) under the following thermocycling protocol: 15 min at 42°C, followed by 3 min at 95°C. The GoTaq Real‐Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems) was used to perform quantitative real‐time PCR. Expression levels were normalized through the 2−ΔΔCt method, with GAPDH and U6 as internal reference genes.

Subcellular Fractionation

Subcellular fractionation of BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells was performed with the PARIS Kit (Ambion). The relative distribution of LINC01537 was subsequently quantified by RT‐qPCR, with GAPDH and U6 serving as cytoplasmic and nuclear internal reference genes, respectively. All assays were repeated in three independent biological replicates.

CCK‐8

After reaching approximately 80% confluence, transfected breast cancer cells were resuspended and seeded into 96‐well plates at 100 µL per well. Cells were cultured for 24, 48, and 72 h. At each time point, 10 µL of CCK‐8 solution was added to each well, followed by incubation for 2 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (PERLONG, Beijing, China).

Flow Cytometry Analysis

Cell apoptosis was evaluated using the Annexin V‐FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime) and the Annexin V‐APC Kit (Bioscience). Cell pellets were washed twice with ice‐cold PBS and once with 1× binding buffer, centrifuging at 1500 rpm for 5 min each time. The cells were then resuspended in 200 µL of 1× binding buffer and stained with 4 µL of Annexin V‐FITC/PI or Annexin V‐APC for 15 min at room temperature in the dark. Finally, 100 µL of 1× binding buffer was added, and flow cytometry was used to detect the samples.

Transwell Assays

For the invasion assay, chambers were pre‐coated with Matrigel (Corning, NY, USA); uncoated chambers were used for migration assays. Briefly, transfected cells resuspended in 200 μL of serum‐free medium were seeded into the upper compartment. The lower chamber was filled with 500 μL of medium supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS). Following a 24‐h incubation, cells that had traversed the membrane were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Sangon) and stained with crystal violet (Solarbio). Images from five randomly chosen visual fields per membrane were captured using an inverted microscope.

Dual‐Luciferase Reporter Assay

DNA fragments containing the predicted wild‐type or mutant miR‐138‐5p binding sites within LINC01537 and the EZH2 3′UTR were cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to generate wt‐LINC01537, mut‐LINC01537, wt‐EZH2, and mut‐EZH2 constructs. Cells were plated in 24‐well plates and co‐transfected with either mimic/inhibitor NC or miR‐138‐5p mimic/inhibitor, along with the respective reporter plasmid. Following 48 h of transfection, luciferase activity was quantified using the Dual‐Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (Promega).

RNA Pull‐Down Assays

Biotin‐based RNA pull‐down experiments were implemented to enrich endogenous binding miRNAs of LINC01537. Biotin‐labeled antisense oligonucleotides targeting LINC01537 (bio‐LINC01537) and matched biotinylated negative control probes (bio‐NC) were prepared for this assay. After harvesting and lysis of BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells, whole‐cell lysates were co‐incubated with biotinylated RNA probes on ice. Upon completion of the 2‐h incubation, Dynabeads M‐270 Streptavidin (Invitrogen, Catalog No. 65305) were added to isolate RNA‐associated complexes. The enrichment efficiency of miR‐138‐5p in precipitated complexes was quantified via RT‐qPCR analysis.

RIP Assay

RIP assay was conducted following the manufacturer's guidelines of the EZ Magna RIP Kit (Millipore, USA). Briefly, BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells at the logarithmic growth stage were harvested and lysed with RIP lysis buffer. Cellular lysates were incubated overnight at 4°C with magnetic beads pre‐conjugated with anti‐Ago2 antibody or isotype‐matched control IgG in RIP reaction buffer. After magnetic separation of beads, proteinase K was supplemented to degrade bound proteins. Finally, co‐precipitated RNA was isolated and purified for subsequent RT‐qPCR detection.

Western Blot Assay

Total cellular protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail. Protein concentration was quantified using a BCA kit. Equal amounts of protein samples were separated via SDS‐PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes. After blocking with 5% skim milk, membranes were incubated with primary anti‐EZH2 antibody and GAPDH primary antibody overnight at 4°C, followed by incubation with HRP‐conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence reagent, and gray values were quantified by ImageJ software. GAPDH was used as an internal loading control.

Bioinformatics Analysis

The target genes of miR‐138‐5p were searched through four databases: miRDB, ENCORI, TargetScan, and miRWalk, and a Venn diagram was created using the Weishengxin online website (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/). In addition, GO and KEGG analysis of target genes were performed using Weishengxin. The 169 intersecting target genes were imported into the STRING online website (https://cn.string-db.org/) for PPI network construction and imported into the Cytoscape software to obtain 10 core proteins. The Kaplan‐Meier curve of the HUB gene was drawn through the Kaplan‐Meier Plotter website (https://kmplot.com/analysis/index.php?p=home).

Statistical Analysis

The data were presented as standard deviation (SD). The Chi‐square test examined relationships between LINC01537 expression and clinicopathological characteristics. Survival outcomes were analyzed via Kaplan‐Meier curves to determine the prognostic significance of LINC01537. Multivariate Cox regression helped identify independent prognostic factors. Pearson's correlation analysis evaluated associations among miR‐138‐5p, LINC01537, and EZH2 expression. For comparisons between two groups, Student's t‐tests were applied. Comparisons involving multiple groups were conducted with one‐way ANOVA or two‐way ANOVA. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Expression of LINC01537 in Breast Cancer Tissues and Cells and Its Prognostic Role

LINC01537 expression was significantly elevated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Figure 1A). Compared with normal breast epithelial cell lines (MCF10A), LINC01537 expression was incrementally increased in breast cancer cell lines (Figure 1B). LINC01537 was most highly expressed in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells among all breast cancer cell lines we detected. These two cell lines correspond to triple‐negative and HER2‐positive breast cancer subtypes, respectively, so we chose them for all subsequent functional and mechanistic assays. Patients were stratified into high‐ and low‐expression groups based on the mean LINC01537 expression level in tumor tissues. Kaplan‐Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the high‐expression group had significantly shorter overall survival than those in the low‐expression group (Log‐rank p = 0.027, Figure 1C). LINC01537 expression was significantly correlated with TNM stage (p = 0.028) and lymph node metastasis (p = 0.032). Higher LINC01537 levels were associated with advanced TNM stage and a greater frequency of lymph node metastasis (Table 1). Furthermore, multivariable Cox regression analysis showed that LINC01537 (p = 0.022), lymph node metastasis (p = 0.031), and TNM stage (p = 0.026) were independent risk factors affecting the prognosis of breast cancer patients (Figure 1D).

Effects of Knocking Down LINC01537 on Breast Cancer Cell Lines

Transfection with two independent siRNAs (si‐LINC01537#1 and si‐LINC01537#2) significantly reduced LINC01537 expression in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells (Figure 2A). CCK‐8 assays showed that LINC01537 knockdown markedly decreased cell proliferation compared to controls (Figure 2B,C). Flow cytometry revealed a significant increase in the apoptosis rate in the si‐LINC01537 groups (Figure 2D). In addition, Transwell assays demonstrated that LINC01537 silencing significantly reduced both migratory and invasive capacities (Figure 2E,F).

LINC01537 Directly Targets miR‐138‐5p

We performed a nuclear‐cytoplasmic fractionation assay to identify the subcellular localization of LINC01537, which is an essential prerequisite to validate its ceRNA function (Figure S1A,B). GAPDH (cytoplasmic control) was largely enriched in cytoplasmic extracts, while U6 snRNA (nuclear control) was mainly detected in nuclear extracts, confirming the successful separation of nuclear and cytoplasmic components in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells. Notably, LINC01537 exhibited obvious cytoplasmic predominance in both breast cancer cell lines, with a larger proportion of transcripts localized in the cytoplasm rather than the nucleus.

There was sequence complementarity between miR‐138‐5p and the 3′UTR region of wt‐LINC01537 (Figure 3A). Dual‐luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR‐138‐5p directly binds to LINC01537: co‐transfection with miR‐138‐5p mimics significantly reduced the luciferase activity of the wt‐LINC01537 reporter, whereas miR‐138‐5p inhibitors increased it; no significant changes were observed for the mut‐LINC01537 reporter (Figure 3B,C). RNA pull‐down assay was applied to detect direct physical binding in vitro. We found that biotin‐labeled LINC01537 captured substantially more endogenous miR‐138‐5p than the Bio‐NC control in both cell lines (Figure 3D). Furthermore, AGO2‐RIP assays revealed that both LINC01537 and miR‐138‐5p were remarkably enriched in AGO2 immunoprecipitation complexes relative to the IgG negative control, proving that LINC01537 and miR‐138‐5p coexist within intracellular RISC complexes (Figure 3E,F).

Compared with normal tissues, the expression of miR‐138‐5p was significantly downregulated in breast cancer tissues (Figure 3G). miR‐138‐5p expression was significantly negatively correlated with the expression of LINC01537 (r = −0.706, p < 0.001, Figure 3H). miR‐138‐5p expression was also reduced in breast cancer cell lines (Figure 3I).

miR‐138‐5p Inhibitor Reversed the Regulatory Effect of LINC01537 Knockdown on Cell Phenotypes

miR‐138‐5p expression was significantly increased upon LINC01537 knockdown. Co‐transfection with miR‐138‐5p inhibitor restored miR‐138‐5p levels to near baseline (Figure 4A). Functional rescue experiments showed that the miR‐138‐5p inhibitor reversed the LINC01537 knockdown‐mediated suppression of cell proliferation (Figure 4B,C), as well as the promotion of apoptosis (Figure 4D). Moreover, inhibition of miR‐138‐5p abolished the inhibitory effects of LINC01537 silencing on cell migration and invasion (Figure 4E,F).

Integrated Bioinformatics Analysis of Target Genes: Functional Enrichment, Pathway Analysis and Protein‐Protein Interaction Network Construction

A Venn diagram integrated predictions from miRDB, ENCORI, TargetScan, and miRWalk, identifying 169 common target genes (Figure 5A). GO analysis revealed that these genes were mainly enriched in mRNA catabolic process, RNA catabolic process, and nuclear‐transcribed mRNA catabolic process. Cellular component enrichment included cytoplasmic ribonucleoprotein granule, ubiquitin ligase complex, while molecular functions were enriched in DNA binding, transcription factor binding, histone binding, suggesting involvement in transcriptional regulation and epigenetic modification (Figure 5B). KEGG pathway analysis identified significant enrichment in Axon guidance, Efferocytosis, Human cytomegalovirus infection, Thyroid hormone signaling pathway, and p53 signaling pathway (Figure 5C). The PPI network exhibited high interconnectivity, and Cytoscape analysis identified 10 hub genes: EZH2, SIN3A, KDM6B, AGO4, TET2, KMT2C, AGO1, RARA, HIF1A, and KDM5A (Figure 5D,E).

Validation of the Target Gene EZH2

EZH2 was selected for further validation due to its highest node degree in the PPI network. Bioinformatics analysis predicted a binding site for miR‐138‐5p within the EZH2 3′UTR (Figure 7A). Dual‐luciferase assays confirmed that miR‐138‐5p directly targets the EZH2 3′UTR: miR‐138‐5p mimics significantly reduced luciferase activity of the wt‐EZH2 reporter, while miR‐138‐5p inhibitors increased it; no such effects were observed for the mut‐EZH2 construct (Figure 7B,C). The mRNA level of EZH2 was higher in breast cancer tissue (Figure 7D). In clinical samples, the mRNA level of EZH2 was negatively correlated with miR‐138‐5p (r = −0.647, p < 0.001, Figure 7E). EZH2 mRNA and protein expression were upregulated in breast cancer cell lines (Figure 7F,G). In BT549 and SK‐BR‐3 cells, LINC01537 knockdown significantly reduced EZH2 mRNA and protein levels, which were restored upon co‐transfection with the miR‐138‐5p inhibitor (Figure 7H,I).

Bidirectional EZH2 Rescue Experiments Confirm EZH2 as the Functional Downstream Effector of the LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p ceRNA Axis

To verify whether EZH2 acts as the functional downstream effector mediating the oncogenic phenotype regulated by the LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p axis, we performed two sets of reciprocal rescue experiments. qRT‐PCR and western blot confirmed that silencing LINC01537 markedly reduced EZH2 mRNA and protein levels, whereas ectopic EZH2 overexpression successfully restored EZH2 abundance (Figure S2A,B). Functional assays revealed that LINC01537 knockdown suppressed cell proliferation, induced apoptosis, and impaired migration and invasion. However, overexpressing EZH2 simultaneously fully reversed all these tumor‐suppressive phenotypes (Figure S2C–G). In addition, a reverse rescue experiment was performed to validate the miR‐138‐5p/EZH2 regulatory cascade (Figure S3). Inhibition of miR‐138‐5p significantly elevated EZH2 expression (Figure S3A,B) and exerted pro‐tumor effects, including accelerated proliferation (Figure S3C,D), reduced apoptosis (Figure S3E), and enhanced migratory and invasive capacity (Figure S3F,G). Nevertheless, co‐transfection of si‐EZH2 abrogated the malignant phenotypes triggered by miR‐138‐5p inhibitor (Figure S3A–G).

External Validation Using Public Databases

To evaluate the generalizability of the prognostic value of LINC01537, we performed external validation using the TCGA breast cancer dataset (n = 979) via the GEPIA online tool. Kaplan‐Meier survival analysis revealed that the difference in overall survival between the high‐ and low‐LINC01537 expression groups did not reach statistical significance (HR = 1.2, Log‐rank p = 0.3, Figure S4). This external validation result was not entirely consistent with our single‐center cohort finding that high LINC01537 expression was significantly associated with poor prognosis (Log‐rank p = 0.027), suggesting that the prognostic value of LINC01537 may be influenced by cohort composition, molecular subtype distribution, and platform‐specific detection differences.

Discussion

Breast cancer remains a major cause of cancer‐related mortality in women globally, with its inherent heterogeneity and metastatic potential presenting substantial obstacles to clinical management and prognosis improvement [15]. LncRNAs have emerged as key regulators in many processes of tumor development and progression, particularly concerning their role in the competing endogenous RNA network, sponging miRNAs and modulating post‐transcriptional regulation [16]. This study investigated the expression level, biological function, and regulatory network of LINC01537 in breast cancer.

Dysregulated lncRNAs are key drivers of breast cancer initiation and progression and offer promising tissue‐specific diagnostic and prognostic markers [4, 17, 18]. LINC01537 was upregulated in breast cancer tissues and cells, which coincides with the previous report [9]. Further clinical correlation analysis emphasized the prognostic value of LINC01537. The survival of breast cancer patients with high LINC01537 expression was reduced; moreover, Cox regression identified LINC01537 as an independent prognostic factor. These findings align with the established oncogenic role of LINC01537 in other malignancies [19] and support its potential utility as a prognostic biomarker for risk stratification in breast cancer.

We performed knockdown experiments in cells. These cell lines also exhibited decreased proliferation, increased levels of apoptosis, and reduced migratory and invasive abilities after silencing LINC01537. This enabled the classification of LINC01537 as an oncogenic lncRNA. Indeed, these findings regarding the oncogenic role of LINC01537 coincide with those already published for other malignancies. Indeed, LINC01537 had been previously described as a prognostic predictor of lung adenocarcinoma by modulating immune signatures [7]. Our results thus extend such previous observations into breast cancer, where LINC01537 has been implicated in multiple malignant phenotypes of breast cancer cells‐fundamental processes linked to tumor progression and metastatic recurrence. Downregulation of LINC01537 is also a promising target for therapeutic inhibition of breast cancer growth and metastasis.

To evaluate the generalizability of LINC01537's prognostic value, we performed external validation using TCGA breast cancer data via the GEPIA database. The results showed an HR of 1.2 for the high‐expression group, but this was not statistically significant (p = 0.3), which was not entirely consistent with our single‐center cohort findings. Potential explanations for this discrepancy include subtype enrichment in our cohort, differences between univariate and multivariate analytical approaches, platform‐specific quantitative variations between qRT‐PCR and RNA‐seq, and differences in baseline characteristics and follow‐up protocols across cohorts. These findings suggest that the prognostic significance of LINC01537 may be influenced by tumor heterogeneity and cohort composition, and its clinical utility in specific subgroups warrants further investigation in larger, multicenter studies.

Mechanistically, lncRNAs often exert their functions by acting as ceRNAs, sequestering specific miRNAs and thereby modulating downstream target gene expression. For example, lncRNA SNHG6 enhances breast cancer progression through the miR‐543/LAMC1 axis [20], whereas SEMA3B‐AS1 inhibits breast cancer by targeting the miR‐3940/KLLN axis [21]. miR‐138‐5p is a well‐established tumor suppressor in various cancers, inhibiting progression via multiple downstream targets [22, 23, 24], thus generally highlighting its anti‐tumor role. This study confirmed the direct binding of LINC01537 to miR‐138‐5p. Our study confirmed the direct binding of LINC01537 to miR‐138‐5p, and rescue experiments demonstrated that miR‐138‐5p mediates the oncogenic effects of LINC01537. Thus, LINC01537 likely exerts its pro‐tumor functions by sponging miR‐138‐5p and relieving its inhibitory action on downstream targets.

To identify critical downstream effectors, we performed bioinformatics analysis and identified 169 overlapping target genes of miR‐138‐5p. Those target genes are enriched in the p53 signaling pathway, which is closely related to tumorigenesis and tumor development [25]. Cytoscape and PPI network analysis identified EZH2 as the hub gene with the highest degree of nodes. EZH2 is overexpressed in breast cancer and has been found to promote tumor progression and metastasis [26, 27]. A variable modulation of EZH2 has been noted in different breast cancer types, especially advanced diseases. Overexpression or dysfunction will influence the pathways and processes involved in the pathophysiology of breast cancer [28]. Poor prognostic indicators in breast cancer have been related to EZH2 overexpression in independent studies [29]. Validation later confirmed that EZH2 is a target of miR‐138‐5p. The knockdown of LINC01537 reduced EZH2 levels, which were normalized upon treatment with an inhibitor of miR‐138‐5p. These results collectively establish a novel LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p/EZH2 ceRNA axis in breast cancer. we further conducted two groups of reciprocal EZH2 rescue assays at both mRNA and protein levels, which solidly proved EZH2 is the core functional mediator of the LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p regulatory cascade.

As a multifunctional oncoprotein, EZH2 functions via two distinct pathways, whose clarification is essential to interpret the LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p axis. Canonical pathway: As the catalytic subunit of PRC2, EZH2 relies on its C‐terminal SET domain to induce H3K27me3 modification and epigenetically repress target genes [30]. In breast cancer, EZH2 overexpression correlates with increased H3K27me3 and silenced tumor‐suppressive genes, acting as a core epigenetic driver of malignancy [31]. Non‐canonical pathway: EZH2 exerts pro‐tumor effects independent of methyltransferase activity. In TNBC with high EZH2 but low H3K27me3, EZH2 interacts with E2F1 to remodel chromatin, boost proliferation and suppress apoptosis without H3K27me3 accumulation [27]. Gonzalez et al. further illustrated that cytoplasmic EZH2 with T367 phosphorylation methylates p38α, activates p38 signaling and facilitates TNBC metastasis in an H3K27me3‐independent way [32]. Our work only confirmed that LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p modulates EZH2 expression, yet we cannot distinguish whether EZH2 drives oncogenic phenotypes through canonical enzyme‐dependent or non‐canonical routes. H3K27me3 quantification, EZH2 target profiling and EZH2 enzyme inhibitor treatments could differentiate these two mechanisms.

Despite these significant findings, this study has some limitations. First, all clinical samples were collected from a single center with only 135 breast cancer patients, lacking independent multicenter validation cohorts. Despite significant prognostic associations observed in our internal cohort, single‐center data carries selection and population bias, limiting the generalizability of LINC01537 as a breast cancer prognostic biomarker. We supplemented TCGA‐BRCA survival analysis to relieve this limitation; TCGA showed consistent survival trends but non‐significant overall Log‐rank results, implying lncRNA prognostic performance is vulnerable to retrospective bias, tumor molecular heterogeneity and clinical treatment confounders. Importantly, public retrospective datasets cannot substitute prospective multicenter trials. Large‐scale prospective multicenter studies are therefore needed to further validate LINC01537's clinical predictive value prior to clinical translation.

Second, all functional assays were only performed in BT549 (TNBC) and SK‐BR‐3 (HER2+) cells. We chose these two lines for their highest endogenous LINC01537 expression; TNBC and HER2+ subtypes are also highly malignant with limited treatment options, making them clinically relevant research models. Nevertheless, breast cancer is highly heterogeneous and includes Luminal A, Luminal B and other subtypes with distinct transcriptomes, biological features, treatment responses and prognoses. It remains unknown whether the inhibitory effects of LINC01537 knockdown on proliferation, migration and invasion, as well as its pro‐apoptotic function observed in BT549 and SK‐BR‐3, can be replicated in luminal subtypes. Thus, our conclusions should only be generalized to TNBC and HER2‐positive breast cancer rather than all breast cancer subtypes. Further research using luminal cell lines such as MCF‐7 and T47D, as well as PDX and genetically engineered mouse models, is required to fully characterize the subtype‐specific functions and therapeutic potential of LINC01537.

Third, we systematically validated the upstream ceRNA cascade of EZH2 and confirmed EZH2 as the functional mediator of LINC01537 via bidirectional rescue assays, but the downstream mechanisms by which EZH2 triggers breast cancer malignant phenotypes remain uncharacterized. EZH2 acts through two separate routes: canonical H3K27me3‐dependent epigenetic repression of tumor suppressors, and H3K27me3‐independent non‐canonical signaling. Without measuring cellular H3K27me3 levels or profiling EZH2 downstream targets, our study cannot identify which pathway mediates EZH2's oncogenic role in this ceRNA axis. Future work will detect H3K27me3 expression and screen EZH2 differential targets to resolve the precise epigenetic or transcriptional mechanism of this regulatory network.

Fourth, all functional and mechanistic analyses rely solely on in vitro cell assays, without in vivo xenograft or metastatic animal validation. Cell culture fails to mimic the in vivo tumor microenvironment, immune regulation and stromal crosstalk, meaning the oncogenic effects of LINC01537 and the LINC01537/miR‐138‐5p/EZH2 axis observed in cells remain unvalidated at the organism level. The absence of subcutaneous tumor and metastasis animal data limits confirmation of this ceRNA axis' translational value. Future work will construct nude mouse xenograft and metastatic models to characterize LINC01537's in vivo regulation of tumor growth and metastasis, and strengthen preclinical evidence for targeted therapy.

Conclusion

LINC01537 is overexpressed in breast cancer tissues, TNBC and HER2‐positive cells, and acts as an independent prognostic biomarker in our single‐center cohort. Mechanistically, LINC01537 sponges miR‐138‐5p to upregulate EZH2 and promote malignant phenotypes in vitro. Future multi‐subtype cell experiments, in vivo animal models and EZH2 downstream mechanistic tests are needed to further validate the clinical application of LINC01537.

The global Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for Fourier–Jacobi periods on unitary groups II: Comparison of the relative trace formulae
Paul Boisseau, Weixiao Lu, Hang Xue

Abstract
. This is the second of a series of three papers where we prove the Gan–Gross–Prasad conjecture for Fourier–Jacobi periods on unitary groups and an Ichino–Ikeda type refinement. Our strategy is based on the comparison of relative trace formulae formulated by Liu. The goal of this second paper is to compare the two relative trace formulae.



1.
Introduction

This is the second in a series of three papers aiming at establishing the global Gan–Gross–Prasad (GGP) conjecture for Fourier–Jacobi periods on unitary groups.

1.1.
What did we achieve in the first of this series?

Let
E/F
be a quadratic extension of number fields, and
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}_{E}
the adeles of
F
and
E
respectively. Put
G_{n}^{\prime}=\GL_{n,F}
and
G_{n}=\Res_{E/F}\GL_{n,E}
. Put
G_{+}=G\times E_{n}
. For each cuspidal datum
\chi
of
G
and we defined a distribution
I_{\chi}
on
G_{+}(\mathbb{A})
. These are called spectral terms in the relative trace formula (RTF), and we do not need them in this paper. Let
\mathcal{A}
be the
2n
-dimensional affine space over
E
viewed as an algebraic variety over
F
, and for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(F)
we define another distribution
I_{\alpha}
on
G_{+}(\mathbb{A})
(some of them are by definition zero). These are called geometric terms in the RTF. We will recall these terms in Section 2. The coarse form of the RTF on
G_{+}
is the following identity

(1.1)

\sum_{\chi}I_{\chi}(f_{+})=\sum_{\alpha}I_{\alpha}(f_{+}).

for all
f_{+}\in\mathcal{S}(G_{+}(\mathbb{A}))
. Here the sum
\chi
runs over all cuspidal data of
G
, and the sum
\alpha
runs over all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(F)
.

Similarly let
V
be a skew-hermitian space of dimension

and
\U(V)
the unitary group. Let
\U_{V}=\U(V)\times\U(V)
, and
\U_{V,+}=\U_{V}\times V
. For each cuspidal datum
\chi
of
\U_{V}
and we defined a distribution
J_{\chi}^{V}
on
\U_{V,+}(\mathbb{A})
. These are called spectral terms in the RTF on unitary groups, and again we do not need them in this paper. For each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(F)
we define another distribution

on
\U_{V,+}(\mathbb{A})
(some of them are by definition zero). These are called geometric terms in the RTF on unitary groups. We will recall these terms in Section 2. The coarse form of the RTF on

is the following identity

(1.2)

\sum_{\chi}J_{\chi}^{V}(f_{+}^{V})=\sum_{\alpha}J_{\alpha}^{V}(f_{+}^{V}).

for all

1.2.
What do we achieve in this paper?

The first goal of this paper is to define a notion of matching of test functions. For regular semisimple
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(F)
, a notion which we will define in Section 4, the geometric terms
I_{\alpha}
and
J_{\alpha}^{V}
reduce to the more familiar orbital integrals. We define that a function
f_{+}\in\mathcal{S}(G_{+}(\mathbb{A}))
and a collection of functions
f_{+}^{V}\in\mathcal{S}(\U_{V,+}(\mathbb{A}))
where
V
runs over all skew-hermitian spaces of dimension
n
match if

(1.3)

I_{\alpha}(f_{+})=\sum_{V}J_{\alpha}^{V}(f_{+}^{V}),

for all regular semisimple
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(F)
. For such
\alpha
, there is at most one nonzero term on the right hand side. We will prove that for matching test functions, the identity (
1.3
) holds for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}(F)
, not just the regular semisimple ones. This is what is referred to as the singular transfer of test functions. It is achieved via descending of the geometric terms
I_{\alpha}
and
J_{\alpha}^{V}
to the Lie algebra, and the identity (
1.3
) eventually reduces to the results of
[
8
]
. The fact that we are able to do this reduction ultimately relies on the particular way we setup and truncate the RTFs, as was done in
[
7
]
. See also the introduction of
[
7
]
for more discussions.

The matching of test functions is a local property. Let
v
be a place of
F
. For a function
f_{+,v}\in\mathcal{S}(G_{+}(F_{v}))
and a collection of functions
f_{+,v}^{V}\in\mathcal{S}(\U_{V,+}(F_{v}))
where
V
runs over all skew-hermitian spaces of dimension
n
over
E_{v}
, we can define the notion of matching analogously. Indeed global (factorizable) test functions match if they match at all places. We prove two results for matching test functions
f_{+,v}
and
f_{+,v}^{V}
.

•

Transfer of test functions. Given
f_{+,v}
we can find a collection
f_{+,v}^{V}
that matches it, and vice versa. If
v
is archimedean this is proved only for a dense subset of test functions.

•

The fundamental lemma. This means if all data at the place
v
are unramified, then the unramified test functions should match. See Theorem
4.8
for the precise statement.

These two results form the pillars of the comparison of the RTFs at hand.

These results form the first part of this paper. The rest of this paper is devoted to proving a local character identity for matching test functions. Again let
v
be a place of
F
. Let
\pi_{v}
be an irreducible tempered representation of
\U_{V}(F_{v})
with a nonzero Fourier–Jacobi model, cf. Subsection 6.1 for an explanation. We define a distribution
J_{\pi}^{V}
on
\U_{V}^{+}(F_{v})
. For its base change
\Pi_{v}
to
G(F_{v})
, we define another distribution
I_{\Pi_{v}}
on
G_{+}(F_{v})
. We prove that there is an explicit constant
\kappa(\pi_{v})
such that for all matching test functions
f_{+,v}
and
\{f_{+,v}^{V}\}_{V}
, we have

J_{\pi_{v}}^{V}(f^{V}_{+})=\kappa(\pi_{v})I_{\Pi_{v}}(f_{+}).

We also prove that this identity characterizes matching of test functions. This result will play a definitive role in the third of this series of papers. It is used in the proof of a refined version of the global GGP conjecture, and is also used in the process of isolation of a particular cuspidal datum on the spectral side of the RTFs.

Geometric distributions on the general linear groups

We developed in
[

]

One major innovation in

L=E^{n}

, and
S=L^{\vee}+L+E

A standard parabolic subgroup of

]

of type I, or of the form

of type II. If

If

\begin{pmatrix}1&&&\bullet&\star\\
&*&*&*&\circ\\
&*&*&*&\circ\\
&&&*&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}1&&&&\star\\
&*&*&&\\
&*&*&&\\
&&&*&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix},

&&&&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\begin{pmatrix}1&&\bullet&&\star\\
&*&&&\\
&&*&&\circ\\
&&&*&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}

5.5.
An estimate of Whittaker functions

Let
G
be a quasi-split reductive group over
F
. Fix a Borel subgroup

. Let
\xi
be a generic character of
N
. Let
A_{0}
be the maximal split subtorus of
T
, let
\Delta
be the set of simple roots of
A_{0}
action on
N
. For

and
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
, we put

W_{f}(g_{1},g_{2})=\int_{N}f(g_{1}^{-1}ug_{2})\xi(u)\mathrm{d}u.

For
a\in T
and
\alpha\in\Delta
, we write
a^{\alpha}
for
e^{\langle\alpha,H_{0}(a)\rangle}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
N>0
and
d>0
, there exists a continuous semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathcal{S}(G)
such that

\lvert W_{f}(a_{1}k_{1},a_{2}k_{2})\rvert\ll\delta_{B}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}(1+a^{\alpha})^{-N}\varsigma(a_{1})^{d}\varsigma(a_{2})^{-d}\lVert f\rVert

holds for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in T
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
.

Proof.

We first show that for any
N,d>0
, there exists a continuous semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathcal{S}(G)
such that

(5.4)

\lvert W_{f}(a_{1}k_{1},a_{2}k_{2})\rvert\ll\delta_{B}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varsigma(a_{1})^{d}\varsigma(a_{2})^{-d}\lVert f\rVert

holds for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in T
and
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
. Indeed, we can assume
k_{1}=k_{2}=1
, then

W_{f}(a_{1},a_{2})=\int_{N}f(a_{1}^{-1}ua_{2})\xi(u)\mathrm{d}u=\delta_{B}(a_{1})\int_{N}f(ua_{1}^{-1}a_{2})\ll\delta_{B}(a_{1})\varsigma(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2})^{-d}\|f\|,

for some continuous semi-norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathcal{S}(G)
. Similarly
W_{f}(a_{1},a_{2})\ll\delta_{B}(a_{2})\varsigma(a_{1}^{-1}a_{2})^{-d}\|f\|
. Therefore (
5.4
) holds.

By the proof of
[
4
]
*Lemma 2.5.1, for any
W\in\mathcal{C}^{w}_{d}(N\backslash G,\xi)
such that

W(ak)\leq C\delta_{B}(a)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varsigma(a)^{d}

for any
(a,k)\in T\times K
, then for any
N>0

W(ak)\leq C_{1}C\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}(1+a^{\alpha})^{-N}\delta_{B}(a)^{\frac{1}{2}}\varsigma(a)^{d},

for any
(a,k)\in T\times K
. Moreover,

(1)

If
F
is non-Archimedean, the constant
C_{1}
only depends on
N
and the open compact
J\subset G
such that
W
is right
J
-invariant.

(2)

If
F
is Archimedean,

C_{1}=1+\sup_{u\in\mathcal{K}_{N}}\sup_{(a,k)\in T\times K}\lvert R(u)W(ak)\rvert\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta}\delta(a)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varsigma(a)^{-d},

where
\mathcal{K}_{N}
is the compact subset of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
depending on
N
.

Apply this to
W(\cdot,a_{2})
as

varies, we see that the constant

is uniform, the lemma follows from (
5.4
).
∎

Dietary riboflavin blocks the cGAS-STING pathway to curb hypoxia-induced hepatic inflammation in sub-adult grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella)
Authors: Lin Zhong, Lin Feng, Pei Wu, Weidan Jiang, Hongyun Zhang, Yaobin Ma, Yang Liu, Haifeng Mi, Fali Wu, Lu Zhang, Xiaoqiu Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study evaluated the effect of riboflavin (VB2) on growth performance and its ability to alleviate hypoxia-induced hepatic inflammation in sub-adult grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idella), as well as the underlying mechanisms. A total of 450 grass carp (402.16 ± 1.01 g) were randomly distributed into 18 tanks and fed one of six experimental diets with varying VB2 concentrations (0.47, 1.96, 3.45, 4.97, 6.47, and 7.98 mg/kg) for 60 d, followed by a 96 h hypoxia stress experiment. The experimental results showed that, compared with VB2 deficiency (0.47 mg/kg VB2) group, the 1.96 to 7.98 mg/kg VB2 groups enhanced the percent weight gain, specific growth rate, and feed efficiency, and exerted a growth-promoting effect on grass carp (P < 0.001). In the hypoxia group, the supplementation of VB2 (3.45–6.47 mg/kg) increased hepatic VB2 content, D-amino acid oxidase, and glutathione reductase activities (P < 0.001), thereby enhancing the function of the flavoprotein. Furthermore, the 3.45 to 6.47 mg/kg VB2 groups reduced the activities of serum aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, lactate dehydrogenase, and alkaline phosphatase (P < 0.05), thereby mitigating hepatic damage induced by hypoxia. The 3.45 to 4.97 mg/kg VB2 groups increased hepatic ATP content (P < 0.001), while reducing cytoplasmic mitochondrial DNA copy number under hypoxia stress. Compared with the VB2 deficiency group, the 4.97 mg/kg VB2 group promoted the fluorescence intensity of VDAC1, TOMM20, and OPA1 under hypoxia stress (P < 0.001), thereby improving mitochondrial structure and function. Riboflavin also mitigated hypoxia-induced hepatic inflammation. Dietary 3.45 to 7.98 mg/kg VB2 reduced the expression of pro-inflammatory factors such as il-1β, tnf-α and ifn-1, while 3.45 to 6.47 mg/kg VB2 groups increased the expression of anti-inflammatory cytokine such as il-4, il-10, and tgf-β (P < 0.05). Dietary 4.97 mg/kg VB2 reduced the expression of p-STING, p-TBK1, p-IRF3, and p-P65 (P < 0.05). Finally, using percent weight gain, D-amino acid oxidase (D-AAO), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) for secondary regression analysis, VB2 requirements for sub-adult grass carp were determined to be 5.39, 5.11, and 5.49 mg/kg, respectively. Overall, this study provides a theoretical basis for the role of VB2 in alleviating hypoxia-induced hepatic inflammation and for formulating diets for sub-adult grass carp.

Introduction

Riboflavin (VB2) is an essential nutrient for fish, and can be transported into cells via the slc52a2 (the full names of the abbreviations of all genes can be found in Table S1) transporter present in the liver. Under the action of RFK and FLAD1 (the full names of the abbreviations of all proteins can be found in Table S1), VB2 generates the physiologically active substances flavin mononucleotide (FMN) and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), which play roles in the electron transport chain (ETC), fatty acid β-oxidation, and cellular signal transduction (Farah et al., 2022). Previous studies have shown that dietary VB2 deficiency can lead to stunted growth, weakened immunity, and reduced stress resistance in fish (Chen et al., 2015; Yu et al., 2022). Fish that inhabit aquatic environments are prone to environmental stress. Dissolved oxygen (DO) is a critical water quality parameter for fish survival. However, with the intensification of aquaculture, fish frequently experience hypoxic stress. Hypoxia, which is typically categorized as DO levels below 2 mg/L, induces multiple stress responses in fish, including reductions in appetite, activity, metabolic functions, growth performance, and immune response (Cai et al., 2025). For example, hypoxia diminished the final body weight (FBW) and specific growth rate (SGR) in spotted wolffish (Anarhichas minor) (Foss et al., 2002), and also reduced the levels of gill superoxide dismutase, catalase, alkaline phosphatase (AKP), complement component 3, and lysozyme of yellow catfish (Tachysurus fulvidraco ♀ × Pseudobagrus vachellii ♂) (Tao et al., 2021). As a nutrient with anti-stress potential, the role of VB2 in alleviating stress responses in fish under hypoxic conditions remains to be elucidated.

Hypoxia can lead to inflammatory responses, which are typically mediated by cytokines such as interleukins (IL), tumor necrosis factors (TNF), interferons (IFN), chemokines, transforming growth factors (TGF), and colony-stimulating factors. Hypoxia is known to enhance pro-inflammatory responses while attenuating anti-inflammatory ones in fish hepatopancreas (Han et al., 2022; He et al., 2022; Wu et al., 2022). However, whether VB2 alleviates hypoxia-induced inflammatory responses has not yet been investigated. Existing research has found that VB2 markedly improves immunity and alleviates inflammatory responses. Specifically, VB2 enhanced the serum total protein, albumin, and globulin levels of Pangasianodon hypophthalmus exposed to arsenic and high temperatures (Kumar 2021), and improved the hemolymph phenoloxidase activity, lysozyme activity, and nitro blue tetrazolium activity in Pacific white shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei) (Sanjeewani and Lee, 2023). Moreover, VB2 attenuated the secretion of pulmonary pro-inflammatory mediators (Li et al., 2022), improved colonic (Zhang et al., 2024), and liver inflammation in mice (Sanches et al., 2014). These findings collectively suggested that VB2 alleviated inflammation in animals. Therefore, VB2 may alleviate hypoxia-induced inflammatory responses in fish, however further research is warranted.

There are differences in the inflammatory mechanisms caused by different stressors (Ghezzi et al., 2017), and the inflammation caused by hypoxia may be closely related to the damage-related molecular patterns (DAMPs). The DAMPs include nucleic acids, proteins, ions, polysaccharides, and metabolites, such as HMGB1, HSPs, S100s, DNA, ATP, and 8-hydroxydeoxyguanosine (8-OHDG) (Ma et al., 2024). Previous studies have shown that hypoxia increases HSP70 expression in rat brains (Li et al., 2019), HMGB1 expression in mouse cardiac progenitor cells (Su et al., 2018), and the expression of HMGB1, S100A8, and HSP70 in human placenta (Baker et al., 2021). Conversely, VB2 reduced the levels of 8-OHDG in rat models of acute kidney injury (Adakul et al., 2019) and colonic injury (Karakoyun et al., 2018). Nevertheless, the effect of VB2 on modulating DAMPs under hypoxia has not been examined in fish, highlighting a critical need for future research.

The recognition of DAMPs by pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) (Żeromski et al., 2020) triggers the activation of the cGAS—STING axis, thereby initiating immune signaling (Kim et al., 2023). Currently, it remains unknown whether VB2 influences the cGAS-STING pathway in fish. Studies found that VB2 deficiency led to hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction in mice (Burch et al., 1960). Dysfunctional mitochondria prompted the liberation of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), eliciting an inflammatory response via the cGAS-STING pathway (Huang et al., 2020). Collectively, these findings suggested that VB2 could modulate inflammatory processes by alleviating mtDNA-mediated engagement of the cGAS-STING axis. This study provides the first elucidation of how VB2 regulates inflammation and its underlying mechanisms under hypoxia, and offers a theoretical foundation for determining VB2 requirements and formulating feed for sub-adult grass carp.

Materials and methods

Animal ethical statement

All experimental procedures adhered to the Animal Care Advisory Committee of Sichuan Agricultural University (No. ZL-2023214083).

Animal experimental design

The grass carp specimens were procured from Deyang (Sichuan, China). After 28 d of acclimatization (Chen et al., 2015), 450 disease-free grass carp (initial mean weight 402.16 ± 1.01 g) were chosen and randomly allotted into 6 treatment groups. These carp were randomly placed in 18 barrels (0.6 m × 1 m), with 25 fish per barrel. The volume of water stored in each barrel is 1.02 m3. After a 14-d depletion period, each group was fed with diets containing 0.0, 1.5, 3.0, 4.5, 6.0, and 7.5 mg/kg VB2 for a 60-d growth experiment (the measured levels of VB2 were 0.47, 1.96, 3.45, 4.97, 6.47, and 7.98 mg/kg, respectively). Feed was stored in sealed bags at 4 °C in the dark. It was only removed during scheduled feeding times to preserve freshness. Experimental conditions: temperature 30 ± 2 °C, pH 7.5 ± 0.2, and DO 6 to 7 mg/L. Feeding was carried out at 07:00, 11:00, 15:00, and 19:00 every day.

Hypoxia stress experiment

Upon completion of the growth experiment, the fish underwent food deprivation for 24 h. Fish in each treatment group were placed into the normoxia group (12 fish) and the hypoxia group (12 fish). The normoxia group was maintained at 6 to 7 mg/L DO, whereas the hypoxia group was kept at 0.8 to 1.4 mg/L. The hypoxia stress lasted for 96 h. No water was changed or feeding occurred during the period of hypoxia stress, following the method of Xia et al. (2024).

Sample collection and preservation

Following the hypoxic stress period, 6 grass carp were selected from each group (including normoxia and hypoxia groups) for weighing and tail vein blood collection. The serum was centrifuged (4 °C, 1644 × g, 5 min) using a High Speed Refrigerated Centrifuge (TGL-21M, Shanghai Lu Xiangyi Centrifuge Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) and then frozen at −20 °C for further analysis. Hepatopancreas was aliquoted into four pieces and cryopreserved at −80 °C for later analysis.

Biochemical criterion in the serum and hepatopancreas

The enzymatic activities of aspartate aminotransferase (AST; C010-2-1), alanine aminotransferase (ALT; C009-2-1), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH; A020-2-2), and AKP (A059-2-2) in serum, as well as glutathione reductase (GR; A062-2-1) in hepatopancreas, were all measured by using assay kits from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China. The ATP content in the hepatopancreas was also measured using an assay kit (AKOP004U, Boxbio Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The activity of D-amino acid oxidase (D-AAO; YJ090525B) and VB2 content (YJ490231B) in hepatopancreas were measured using assay kits from YuanJu Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China. All procedures were performed following the instructions provided by the manufacturers. Details regarding the commercial assay kits used are summarized in Table S2. The instrument used is Absorbance Microplate Reader (SpectraMax 190, Molecular Devices Corporation, Shanghai, China).

Pathology and transmission electron microscope (TEM) observation

Hepatopancreas tissues were first fixed and then dehydrated using ethanol. Following embedding in paraffin, the tissues were cut into 5-μm sections. The sections were processed for histological examination (HE) staining under the optical microscope (BX43FC, Olympus Corporation, Nishi-Shinjuku, Tokyo, Japan).

Following initial fixation with glutaraldehyde, the hepatopancreas tissues were post-fixed in 1% osmium acid. Subsequently, the samples were processed through dehydration, Epon812 embedding, and ultrathin sectioning. Staining of the sections was carried out with uranyl acetate followed by lead citrate for TEM observation (HT7800, Hitachi, Ltd., Tokyo, Japan).

Extraction of mtDNA from hepatopancreas cytoplasm

The cytoplasmic fraction of the hepatopancreas was isolated using the tissue mitochondria isolation kit (C3606, Beyotime Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The supernatant (cytoplasmic components) was then collected by centrifugation. The cytoplasmic DNA was extracted using the DNA isolation kit (DC112, Aidlab Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The above descriptions were based on the principle outlined by Quiros et al. (2017). Using the primers detailed in Table S3, relative cytoplasmic mtDNA levels were assessed via quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) in strict adherence to the supplier's directions.

The qRT-PCR analysis

Total RNA was isolated with the RNAiso Plus kit (TaKaRa Biotech Co., Ltd., Dalian, Liaoning, China), followed by reverse transcription into complementary DNA (cDNA) with the PrimeScript RT kit (Nanjing Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd., Nanjing, Jiangsu, China). The reaction system was prepared, containing the following components: sterile water, 2 × SYBR Green supermix (Aidlab Biotech Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), upstream primer, downstream primer, and template cDNA. Finally, qRT-PCR was performed using a qPCR Instrument (QuantStudio 5, Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Shanghai, China), with β-actin used as the reference for normalization. The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 30 s (hold stage), followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s, and 60 °C for 34 s (PCR stage), finally 95 °C for 15 s, 60 °C for 1 min and 95 °C for 15 s (melt curve stage). The 2–ΔΔCt method was employed to calculate relative gene expression. The synthesized primers for the relevant genes are listed in Table S3.

Western blotting

The hepatopancreas was lysed using RIPA buffer (P0013B) and PMSF (ST505) from Beyotime Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China. The protein-containing supernatant was then collected. According to the BCA assay (P0010S, Beyotime Biotech Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), protein concentration was quantified and subsequently normalized. After separation via sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), proteins were electroblotted onto a polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane under wet conditions. The membranes were blocked with blocking solution (G2052-500 ml, Servicebio Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, Hubei, China) and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies. Subsequently, the membranes were exposed to the secondary antibody for 1.5 h. The bands were detected using an ECL kit (PD203-500 ml, Oriscience Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Chengdu, Sichuan, China), imaged using the multimodal imaging system (ChemiDoc, Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc., Hercules, CA, USA), and analyzed with ImageJ software (v1.42q, National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). The antibodies utilized for Western blotting are detailed in Table S2.

Immunofluorescence

After the paraffin sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated, endogenous enzymes were inactivated using 3% H2O2, and then rinsed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS). The antigens were thermally repaired using a boiling citrate sodium buffer solution. Then, following blocking using normal goat serum, an overnight incubation at 4 °C was performed using antibodies. The sections were then incubated with fluorescent secondary antibodies in the dark. Following 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) counterstaining, the sections were rinsed and mounted with an anti-fade mounting medium. Fluorescence intensity was quantitatively measured using an inverted fluorescence microscope (DMI4000B, Leica Microsystems GmbH, Oberkochen, Baden-Württemberg, Germany) and ImageJ v1.42q software. The antibodies used for immunofluorescence are listed in Table S4.

Calculation and statistical analyses

All data were analyzed with SPSS 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Differences across VB2 treatment groups were tested using a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), as shown below:Xij=μ+αi+eij,where Xij denotes the dependent variable; μ denotes the overall mean; αi denotes to the treatment effect; eij denotes the random error. The linear and quadratic responses to increasing dietary VB2 levels were assessed via SPSS 26.0. Duncan's method was used to analyze the difference in treatment means, and P < 0.05 is considered significant.

Within the same VB2 concentration, the differences across DO levels were analyzed using the independent sample t-test (Esmaeili et al., 2025), as shown below:t=(x1–x2)(s12/n1)+(s22/n2),where x1 and x2 indicate the means of the two samples; the radical symbol (√) indicates the square root; s1 and s2 represent their respective standard deviations; n1 and n2 indicate the corresponding sample sizes. The t-distribution was employed to evaluate significant disparities, and P < 0.05 is considered significant.

Finally, the VB2 requirement for sub-adult grass carp was determined by regression analysis, with percent weight gain (PWG), D-AAO, and ALT serving as the key evaluation indicators.

Results

The effects of VB2 on the growth performance

Dietary VB2 deficiency adversely affected the growth of sub-adult grass carp, whereas 1.96 to 7.98 mg/kg VB2 supplementation provided a significant boost to key growth indicators—FBW, weight gain (WG), PWG, SGR, and feed intake (FI) (P < 0.001; Table 2). The effects of VB2 on hepatopancreas nutritional status and transporter protein

Hepatopancreas VB2 content in the sub-adult grass carp was shown in Table 3. Riboflavin content showed a similar trend under normoxia and hypoxia: it first rose and then fell, reaching a minimum in the 0.47 mg/kg and a maximum in the 4.97 mg/kg. In the 3.45 to 6.47 mg/kg VB2 treatment groups, hypoxia significantly reduced the VB2 content compared to normoxia (P < 0.001). The D-AAO and GR activities were shown in Table 4. A marked reduction in the activities of hepatic D-AAO and GR was observed in the 0.47 mg/kg VB2 group (P < 0.001). Under hypoxic conditions, GR and D-AAO attained their peak levels in the 4.97 mg/kg VB2 group. Hypoxia improved GR activity (P = 0.034) while reducing D-AAO (P = 0.001) in the 4.97 mg/kg VB2 group, compared with normoxia groups. Transcript level of slc52a2 was shown in Fig. 1A. The gene expression of slc52a2 decreased first and then increased with dietary VB2 supplement. In both normoxia and hypoxia, the value of slc52a2 was the highest in the VB2 deficiency group. Discussion

Appropriate VB2 promoted the growth performance and nutrition status

The research showed that VB2 supplementation increased the WG, PWG, SGR, FI, and FE of sub-adult grass carp, and reduced the FCR. This result was similar to those in juvenile jian carp (Cyprinus carpio var. Jian), young grass carp (C. idella), coho salmon (Oncorhynchus kisutch), and iridescent shark (P. hypophthalmus) (Chen et al., 2015; Li et al., 2010; Kumar 2021; Yu et al., 2022), demonstrating that VB2 could improve the growth performance of fish at various growth stages and in different species. Riboflavin is an essential nutrient, and its nutritional status is reflected by its hepatopancreas content as well as the enzymatic activities of D-AAO and GR. The results indicated that supplementation with 3.45 to 7.98 mg/kg VB2 increased the VB2 content as well as D-AAO and GR activities in the hepatopancreas, suggesting that adequate VB2 could improve the VB2 nutritional status of the sub-adult grass carp. A consistent finding across species is that VB2 deficiency suppresses the activity of GR. This has been observed in the gills (Chen et al., 2015) and muscle (Jiang et al., 2019) of grass carp (reversible with VB2 supplementation), the breast muscle of Pekin ducks (Tang et al., 2023), and baby pigs erythrocyte (Brady et al., 1979). The results indicated that VB2 deficiency reduced the activity of liver GR. As the dietary VB2 increased, GR showed a trend of increasing first and then decreasing. The GR uses FAD as a coenzyme, and its enhanced activity indicates an increased ability of VB2 to convert to FAD, enhancing the antioxidant capacity ultimately. The D-AAO activity serves as a sensitive functional marker for VB2 status in fish, with its activity being directly dependent on adequate VB2 intake. Studies showed that VB2 deficiency reduced hepatic D-AAO activity in species including grass carp (Jiang et al., 2016), rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) (Woodward, 1983), blue tilapia (Oreochromis aureus) (Soliman and Wilson, 1992), channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) (Serrini et al., 1996), and sunshine bass (Morone chrysops ♀ × Morone saxatilis ♂) (Deng et al., 2003)—an effect reversed by VB2 supplementation. This functional link confirms D-AAO as a robust indicator for determining dietary VB2 requirements. Therefore, elevated activities of D-AAO and GR collectively signify enhanced VB2 metabolic flux, supporting the body's antioxidant defenses and redox homeostasis.

Appropriate VB2 mitigated hepatopancreas injury

The hepatopancreas is an important target organ for hypoxic stress. This study therefore aimed to assess the role of VB2 in mitigating hepatopancreatic injury during hypoxic stress. Findings revealed hypoxia stress induced hepatocyte vacuolization, nuclear dissolution, and blurred cell membrane boundaries, while dietary VB2 deficiency aggravated hepatocyte damage. However, 4.97 and 7.98 mg/kg VB2 levels alleviated the adverse effects, indicating that VB2 indeed improved the structural integrity of hepatocytes caused by hypoxia. Serum ALT, AST, and AKP serve as clinically relevant biomarkers for liver function assessment, and their elevated levels are indicative of hepatocellular injury (Lu et al., 2025). The results demonstrated that VB2 reduced the hypoxia-induced elevation in serum ALT, AST, LDH, and AKP, which aligned with previous findings that VB2 ameliorated the elevation of them in mouse plasma (Bashandy et al., 2018). Taken together, VB2 could alleviate liver injury caused by hypoxia. Oxidative damage caused by hypoxia is usually an important factor in inducing hepatopancreas injury. Riboflavin mainly exists in the body in the forms of FMN and FAD and contributes to the regulation of systemic redox homeostasis. This raises the question of whether VB2 alleviates liver injury through its hepatic metabolic processes. To explore this, it is necessary to examine the indicators related to VB2 metabolism in the hepatopancreas.

A study in mice has revealed that the RFVT2 mediated the hepatic uptake of riboflavin (Yao et al., 2013). The RFVT2 is encoded by the slc52a2 gene. In the hepatopancreas, RFK catalyzes the conversion of VB2 to FMN, which is then transformed into FAD by FLAD1. Thus, assessing hepatic levels of slc52a2, RFK, and FLAD1 indirectly reflects the metabolic conversion of VB2 in the body. The results indicated that hypoxia did not reduce the mRNA expression of slc52a2, while VB2 deficiency promoted its expression. Meanwhile, hypoxia did not appear to affect the expression of FLAD1. However, hypoxia had a tendency to reduce the expression of RFK, while VB2 deficiency further reduced the protein expression of RFK. These ultimately led to a decrease in FAD biosynthesis, as it was limited by the formation of FMN catalyzed by RFK (Shastak and Pelletier, 2023). Both D-AAO and GR are flavoprotein enzymes with FAD as a cofactor and are involved in redox reactions. Hypoxia reduced their activities, whereas VB2 supplementation alleviated this effect. Studies showed that VB2 deficiency led to increased levels of oxidative damage indicators, such as reactive oxygen species (ROS), malondialdehyde (MDA), and protein carbonyl (PC) in young grass carp gill (Chen et al., 2015), and similar results have been observed in ducks liver (Zhang et al., 2025) and coho salmon (O. kisutch) liver (Yu et al., 2022). These findings supported that VB2 indeed ensured the balance of redox under hypoxic stress and reduced hepatopancreas damage.

Appropriate VB2 ameliorated hepatopancreas mitochondrial damage

The occurrence of oxidative stress is closely linked to mitochondrial damage. The ATP content (Palma et al., 2024), mtDNA (Poznyak et al., 2020), and proteins such as OPA1, TOMM20, and VDAC1 (Camara et al., 2017; Strücker et al., 2024) can reflect the mitochondrial structure and functional state. The present results revealed that hypoxia caused mitochondrial swelling and blurred mitochondrial cristae, while the copy number of mtDNA in the cytoplasm increased and the ATP content decreased. The addition of VB2 alleviated this adverse effect. These indicated that VB2 indeed ensured the normal structure and function of mitochondria in sub-adult grass carp under hypoxic stress. What is more, the increase in cytoplasmic mtDNA caused by mitochondrial damage can trigger an inflammatory response. Consequently, the effect of VB2 on hepatopancreatic inflammation under hypoxic stress was investigated.

Appropriate VB2 alleviated the hepatopancreas inflammatory response

The inflammatory response is commonly mediated by cytokines, such as anti-inflammatory factors (il-4, il-10, il-13, and tgf-β) and pro-inflammatory factors (il-6, il-1β, tnf-α, and ifn-γ) (Assadiasl et al., 2021; Chavda et al., 2025). Studies indicated that hypoxia induced the release of cytokines and led to the upregulation of il-1β, il-6, il-8, ifn-1, il-23r, and tnf-α, as well as the downregulation of il-4, il-10, and tgf-β, which was similar to the results in gibel carp (Carassius gibelio) (Wu et al., 2022), rainbow trout (O. mykiss) (Han et al., 2022), and zebrafish (Danio rerio) (He et al., 2022). Adequate intake of VB2 can attenuate inflammatory responses. For instance, VB2 downregulated il-1β, tnf-α, and ifn-γ2 while upregulating il-10 and tgf-β in grass carp gill (Chen et al., 2015). Anyway, VB2 inhibited intestinal levels of tnf-α, il-1β, and CALP, while increasing IL-10 in mice (Zhang et al., 2024). These indicated that VB2 could alleviate hypoxia-induced inflammation by modulating cytokines in sub-adult grass carp.

The cGAS-STING pathway regulates the inflammatory response and is activated by cytoplasmic mtDNA (Boshtam et al., 2017; Machado et al., 2023). When cGAS detects abnormal DNA in the cytoplasm, it activates STING, leading to the phosphorylation of TBK1, subsequently recruiting IRF3 to undergo phosphorylation and enter the nucleus to activate the expression of IFN-1. TBK1 can also activate the NFκB signaling pathway, thereby promoting the inflammatory process. These results indicate hypoxic stress triggered elevated protein abundance of p-STING, TBK1, p-TBK1, and p-IRF3, while appropriate VB2 alleviated the upregulation of these proteins. Meanwhile, appropriate VB2 suppressed the expression of P65 and p-P65. The reduction of these proteins ultimately relieved the inflammatory process. The alleviation of inflammation by VB2 is mediated by FAD and FMN, which modulate the structure and function of flavoprotein enzymes involved in ETC, antioxidant defense system, and the integrity of the mitochondrial membrane, thereby improving the structure and function of mitochondria and reducing mtDNA leakage (Udhayabanu et al., 2018). For instance, VB2 limits electron leakage by sustaining SDH activity (Tang et al., 2019), which is an FAD-dependent enzyme. It also augments GR activity, enhancing antioxidant capacity to clear ROS and hydrogen peroxide and thereby maintaining mitochondrial membrane integrity (Hirano et al., 2011). Overall, VB2 preserves mitochondria by reducing oxidative stress, supporting ETC function, and maintaining membrane integrity. This collectively reduces the cGAS-STING-mediated inflammation triggered by mtDNA leakage (Saki and Prakash, 2017).

VB2 requirement for sub-adult grass carp

The requirement of VB2 for sub-adult grass carp determined by PWG was 5.39 mg/kg (Fig. 8), which is marginally lower than the level established in Chen's earlier research (5.85 mg/kg) (Chen et al., 2015). This difference was likely due to the fish being at different growth stages. The smaller the fish, the higher their requirement for VB2. The fish in Chen's study had a lower body weight range (275.82–624.02 g) than those in this study (402.16–1374.11 g). Furthermore, the results indicated that the VB2 requirement for sub-adult grass carp was different from those of jian carp (C. carpio var. Jian) (Li et al., 2010) and coho salmon (O. kisutch) (Yu et al., 2022). This may be related to the species of the fish. Beyond the traditional production performance indicator of PWG lie more direct biochemical measures for assessing nutrient requirements, such as D-AAO and ALT. The D-AAO activity is high in fish liver and is directly linked to VB2 metabolism, serving as a sensitive biomarker for determining VB2 requirements (Jiang et al., 2016). Serum ALT, a recognized marker for liver function, provides another valuable criterion for defining optimal VB2 levels in fish diets (Zeng et al., 2025). So, based on the activities of hepatopancreas D-AAO in the normoxia group and serum ALT in the hypoxia group, the VB2 requirements for sub-adult grass carp were 5.11 and 5.49 mg/kg, respectively. Riboflavin requirement established via D-AAO was marginally lower compared to the PWG-based estimate, which suggested that more VB2 may be needed to promote fish growth, rather than to ensure normal VB2 metabolism.

Conclusion

Riboflavin enhanced the growth performance of sub-adult grass carp and alleviated hepatopancreas damage induced by hypoxia, which was related to maintaining the redox homeostasis in the body. Furthermore, VB2 mitigated hypoxia-driven hepatopancreas inflammation, potentially by reducing cytoplasmic mtDNA release and inhibiting the cGAS-STING signaling pathway. Finally, based on the PWG, D-AAO, and ALT, the requirements of VB2 for sub-adult grass carp were determined to be 5.39, 5.11, and 5.49 mg/kg, respectively.

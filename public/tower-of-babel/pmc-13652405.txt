Langerhans cell histiocytosis presenting as a cecal mass in an adolescent: a rare surgical case report
Authors: Harith Salman Hasaani, Yaarub J. Khattab
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction and importance:Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) is a rare, idiopathic, multifocal disease that commonly occurs in the skin and skeletal system, although involvement of the gastrointestinal tract is exceptional. Only a limited number of cases have been reported in the literature, particularly at the intestinal level, often with incidental diagnosis after colonoscopic polypectomy, and presentation as a cecal mass is extremely uncommon.Case presentation:A 15-year-old boy presented with abdominal pain and a non-tender right iliac fossa mass. Hematological and biochemical tests were within normal limits. Initial imaging studies, including ultrasound and contrast-enhanced computed tomography, revealed a cecal mass associated with mesenteric lymphadenopathy, with radiological features suggestive of lymphoma. Because of intolerance to bowel preparation, colonoscopy could not be performed. In addition to a history of treated intermittent distension with vomiting, which raised concern for recurrent sub-acute intestinal obstruction, a laparoscopy-assisted surgical approach was therefore chosen. Intraoperatively, a cecal mass with multiple focal swellings of the mesocolon was identified. A right hemicolectomy with ileo-transverse anastomosis was performed according to oncological principles. Post-operative PET imaging showed no evidence of residual disease, and the patient was subsequently enrolled in a follow-up program. This case highlights the rarity of intestinal LCH and the importance of considering it in the differential diagnosis of abdominal masses in pediatric patients. Radiological imaging studies have shown neither residual disease nor recurrence during 9 months of regular follow-up.Clinical discussion:The World Health Organization classifies LCH within the hematopoietic and lymphoid tumors placing it on a spectrum between benign and malignant behavior depending on other factors, with unifocal or multifocal lesions and single- or multisystem involvement, which likely predict prognosis and guide post-surgical treatment.Conclusion:The rarity of intestinal LCH presenting as a cecal mass highlights the importance of considering it in the differential diagnosis of abdominal masses in pediatric patients.

Case presentation

A 15-year-old boy presented with abdominal pain, anorexia, nausea, and a non-tender right iliac fossa mass, with a past surgical history of recurrent attacks of abdominal distension with vomiting that were treated conservatively. Ultrasound of the abdomen revealed a right iliac fossa mass (5.5 × 5 × 5.7 cm) with multiple lymph nodes losing their central hili in the vicinity, raising suspicion of lymphoma, as shown in Figure 1a–c. Pathology

Gross

Cecal mass (6.8 × 6.7 × 6.2 cm) completely obstructed the ileal lumen, with an ulcerated mucosa and involvement of neighboring regional lymph nodes; the appendix looked enlarged and congested; however, the terminal ileum was apparently not diseased, as shown in Figure 2. Microscopy

Histopathology revealed characteristic Langerhans cell hyperplasia admixed with a large number of eosinophilic cells with rare mitotic figures. The tumor infiltrated the whole ileal and cecal wall, including the muscularis externa, with periserosal interruption toward the fatty tissue. All proximal, distal, and deep margins are tumor-free. Three out of 14 pericolic lymph nodes are involved by tumor, with preserved active follicles. Immunohistochemistry for CD68, S100, and CD1a, which are easy and rapid techniques, showed positive staining. Molecular testing was not done because of its unavailability in our country, as shown in Figure 3a–c. Clinical discussion

The Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) tumor is a rare disease characterized by infiltration of histiocytes that resemble Langerhans cells of the skin and dendritic tissue, which explains the tumor's name.

The World Health Organization classification of hematopoietic and lymphoid tumors categorizes disorders of histiocytes into three specific groups – dendritic cell disorders, macrophage-related disorders, and malignant histiocytic disorders[1] – with a debated pathogenesis, either reactive or neoplastic. The latter is associated with the BRAF V600E mutation in 57% of LCH cases[2].

The incidence is three to five cases per million in children and one to two cases per million in adults[3,4]. Bones, skin, and lungs are the most commonly affected organs, with extremely rare gastrointestinal involvement from the mouth to the anus, as well as the surrounding skin along the gastrointestinal tract[5].

The gastrointestinal presentation lies on a spectrum ranging from nausea, bloating, bleeding, and ulcerative colitis-like features, and the condition was incidentally diagnosed by histopathology after colonoscopic polyp snaring[6].

Singhi and Montgomery[7] and his colleagues at John Hopkins reported a case series of 12 gastrointestinal tract LCH cases in 2011, of whom 10 were adults. The characteristics of these patients are reported in Table 1. Endoscopically, one patient had stomach involvement, and the rest had colorectal involvement either as polyps (seven patients) or as ulcers (two patients). A right iliac fossa mass, with its differential diagnoses – especially cecal lymphoma – is an extremely rare presentation. Imaging studies, including a dynamic contrast-enhanced computed tomography scan of the abdomen, revealed a cecal mass with regional lymph nodes that showed contrast enhancement.

Due to intolerance to bowel preparation, colonoscopy could not be performed. In addition to a history of treated intermittent distension with vomiting that could be alarming for recurrent sub-acute intestinal obstruction, a laparoscopic-assisted surgical approach was therefore chosen.

Intraoperatively, a cecal mass with multiple focal swellings of the mesocolon was identified. A right hemicolectomy with ileo-transverse anastomosis was performed according to oncological principles.

A cecal mass diagnosed as LCH by histopathology is an extremely rare presentation compared with the above few gastrointestinal cases that establish this disease diagnosis by polypectomy during routine colonoscopy[8–11].

The pathogenesis is not fully understood because of its rare occurrence. LCH is clinically classified into three groups: unifocal involvement in a single organ system, multifocal involvement in a single organ system, or multisystem involvement. This classification is of paramount importance because each category is treated with its own regimen to minimize the toxicity and morbidity of the disease[12].

PET-imaging scan is recommended to evaluate the extent, severity, and follow-up of the disease, with a very low false-positive rate, and may ultimately be of benefit in mapping the nature of the treatment regimen[13].

The outcome of management depends on multiple factors, principally the fact that patients with multisystem involvement have a poor prognosis.

The case report, “Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis Presenting as a Cecal Mass in an Adolescent: A Rare Surgical Case Report,” was written in accordance with the SCARE guidelines[14].

Conclusion

This case highlights the rarity of intestinal LCH and the importance of considering it in the differential diagnosis of abdominal masses in pediatric patients.

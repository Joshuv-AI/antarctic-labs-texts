The multiple Markov theorem on Angelesco sets
K. Castillo, G. Gordillo-Núñez

Abstract
. Addressing a problem described by Ismail as a “worthwhile research project”, this note extends Markov’s theorem to multiple orthogonal polynomials on Angelesco sets. The interaction 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -matrix is shown to be a nonsingular ℳ \mathcal{M} -matrix; hence the theorem holds for positive Borel measures satisfying the natural support condition, including finite supports, without restriction on the number of intervals. Beyond separated supports, a dual variation identity gives necessary and sufficient tail conditions. An explicit AT example disproves the componentwise extension, while for Nikishin systems we prove positive results for certain deformations of the first generator and give a counterexample for an internal generator.

The monotonic behaviour of the zeros of classical OPRL with respect to their parameters can often be deduced from their specific properties, including Rodrigues’ formula, differential or difference equations, hypergeometric character, among others. In many instances, the simplicity of the problem is such that the expected result can almost be derived directly. For example, in the case of Laguerre polynomials, the problem can be reformulated as an eigenvalue problem, with the result following from the application of Hadamard’s first variation formula (see, for instance,

Over the past four decades, multiple orthogonal polynomials have garnered significant interest due to their extensive theoretical development and relevance in various mathematical contexts. The introduction of
[

However, a generalisation of Markov’s theorem to the setting of “several weight functions” has yet to be established, complicating the study of the monotonicity of their zeros. Although it had long been recognised as a significant challenge, in 2005, Ismail explicitly included this question in his collection of open problems and conjectures, which encompasses some of the most challenging problems in the field of Orthogonal Polynomials and Special Functions (see

“There is no study of zeros of general or special systems of multiple orthogonal polynomials available. An extension of Theorem 7.1.1
[

We prove that Markov’s theorem survives on Angelesco sets. More precisely,
if the logarithmic derivative of each weight with respect to the parameter
is non-decreasing on the interval carrying its measure, then every zero
of the multiple orthogonal polynomial is non-decreasing in that parameter.
The analogous statement holds with non-increasing in place of non-decreasing.
This gives a general answer to Ismail’s
extension problem for Angelesco systems. Beyond separated supports, a dual
variation identity yields necessary and sufficient tail conditions for the
independent and common deformation classes. The AT counterexample shows
that the componentwise condition alone is insufficient in general, while
the Nikishin results establish further positive cases and an obstruction
for deformations of an internal generator.

points. Hence the last integral is nonzero and

\sgn d_{i}^{(k)}(t)=\sgn\,R_{k}(x;t)

on
\Delta_{k}
. Put
f(x;t)=1/(x-x_{l,j}(t))
. Orthogonality gives

\frac{1}{x_{k,i}(t)-x_{l,j}(t)}\int\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)=0.

After subtracting this zero integral from the numerator that defines

a^{(k,l)}_{i,j}(t)d_{i}^{(k)}(t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle a^{(k,l)}_{i,j}(t)d_{i}^{(k)}(t)

\displaystyle=\int R_{k}(x;t)\left(\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt{}\times(x-x_{k,i}(t))\bigl(f(x;t)-f(x_{k,i}(t);t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

Here the polynomial quotients are understood after cancellation, and

(x-x_{k,i}(t))\bigl(f(x;t)-f(x_{k,i}(t);t)\bigr)=-\frac{(x-x_{k,i}(t))^{2}}{(x-x_{l,j}(t))(x_{k,i}(t)-x_{l,j}(t))}\leq 0

on
\Delta_{k}
. The formula is also valid at
x=x_{k,i}(t)
, where both
sides are zero. The two factors in the denominator have the same sign
because
\Delta_{k}
and
\Delta_{l}
are disjoint. Therefore every
off-diagonal entry satisfies
a^{(k,l)}_{i,j}(t)\leq 0
. If the support of
the
k
th measure at
t
contains more than
n_{k}
points, it is not
contained in the zeros of
Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)
, and the inequality is strict.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume the hypotheses and notation of Lemma
3.1
. Define the vectors

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl(x_{1,1}(t),\ldots,x_{1,n_{1}}(t),\ldots,x_{m,1}(t),\ldots,x_{m,n_{m}}(t)\bigr)^{\mathrm{T}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(t)

\displaystyle=\bigl(b^{(1)}_{1}(t),\ldots,b^{(1)}_{n_{1}}(t),\ldots,b^{(m)}_{1}(t),\ldots,b^{(m)}_{n_{m}}(t)\bigr)^{\mathrm{T}},

where, for
k=1,\dots,m
and
i=1,\dots,n_{k}
,

b^{(k)}_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{d_{i}^{(k)}(t)}\int Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\phi_{k}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

In this block order, the component with index
(k,i)
occupies position

n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k-1}+i
, with the preceding sum understood as zero when
k=1
.
Then there exists a neighbourhood of
t_{0}
in which

\mathbf{A}(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\mathbf{b}(t)
.

Proof.

Differentiate first the monomial orthogonality relations. At the fixed value
of
t
, freeze the coefficients of the polynomial

h(x;t)=Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)/(x-x_{k,i}(t))
and take the corresponding linear
combination of the differentiated relations. Thus no derivative of

h(x;t)
is introduced, and

\displaystyle 0={}

\displaystyle\int h(x;t)\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k}}}{\partial t}(x;t)R_{k}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)

\displaystyle+\int h(x;t)Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)\frac{\partial R_{k}}{\partial t}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)

\displaystyle+\int h(x;t)Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)R_{k}(x;t)\phi_{k}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

For every polynomial
g
of degree at most
n_{k}-1
, orthogonality also gives

\int\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}g(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)=g(x_{k,i}(t))\int\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

Indeed, after bringing the right-hand side to the left, the remaining
multiplier of
P_{n}(x;t)
is

\bigl(g(x)-g(x_{k,i}(t))\bigr)/(x-x_{k,i}(t))
, a polynomial of degree at
most
n_{k}-2
; it is the zero polynomial when
n_{k}=1
. Apply the identity
first to
g(x)=\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k}}}{\partial t}(x;t)
and then to

g(x)=Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)/(x-x_{k,i}(t))
. Since

\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k}}}{\partial t}(x_{k,i}(t);t)=-\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{k,i}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k}}}{\partial x}(x_{k,i}(t);t)
and

\frac{1}{R_{k}(x;t)}\frac{\partial R_{k}}{\partial t}(x;t)=-\sum_{l\neq k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}\frac{1}{x-x_{l,j}(t)}\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{l,j}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t),

the three integrals above are, respectively,

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{k,i}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)d_{i}^{(k)}(t),\quad-d_{i}^{(k)}(t)\sum_{l\neq k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}a_{i,j}^{(k,l)}(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{l,j}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t),\quad d_{i}^{(k)}(t)b_{i}^{(k)}(t).

The second equality follows by substituting the displayed formula for

\frac{\partial R_{k}}{\partial t}(x;t)
and the definition of

a_{i,j}^{(k,l)}(t)
; the third is the definition of
b_{i}^{(k)}(t)
.
For the first equality we have also used

d_{i}^{(k)}(t)=\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k}}}{\partial x}(x_{k,i}(t);t)\int\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t),

which is the preceding identity with

g(x)=Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)/(x-x_{k,i}(t))
. Their sum is zero, and division by the
nonzero number
d_{i}^{(k)}(t)
gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{k,i}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)+\sum_{l\neq k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l}}a_{i,j}^{(k,l)}(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{l,j}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=b_{i}^{(k)}(t).

These are precisely the rows of

\mathbf{A}(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\mathbf{b}(t)
.
∎

The following lemma contains Markov’s sign argument.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume the hypotheses and notation of Lemma
3.2
. Then
b^{(k)}_{i}(t)

is non-negative in a neighbourhood of
t_{0}
, provided that the functions

x\longmapsto\phi_{k}(x;t)
have non-decreasing representatives on

\Delta_{k}
.

Proof.

Choose a non-decreasing representative of
x\longmapsto\phi_{k}(x;t)
on

\Delta_{k}
.
With
c_{k,i}(t)=\phi_{k}(x_{k,i}(t);t)
, orthogonality permits us to subtract the
constant
c_{k,i}(t)
and gives

\displaystyle b_{i}^{(k)}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{i}^{(k)}(t)}\int R_{k}(x;t)\left(\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt{}\times(x-x_{k,i}(t))\bigl(\phi_{k}(x;t)-c_{k,i}(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

The last product is defined to be zero at

. It is

It also shows that

exactly when

∎

It remains to prove that
\mathbf{A}
is a nonsingular

-matrix;
for one measure it is the identity.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume the hypotheses and notation of Lemma
3.2
, and fix

. Then

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{B}(t),

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(t)

\displaystyle\geq 0,\quad\rho(\mathbf{B}(t))<1.

Consequently
\mathbf{A}(t)
is a nonsingular
\mathcal{M}
-matrix and

\mathbf{A}(t)^{-1}\geq 0
. If

and the support of the
k
th measure at
the fixed parameter

contains more than
n_{k}
points for every

, then

\mathbf{B}(t)
is irreducible and

\mathbf{A}(t)^{-1}>0
. If

\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})^{\mathrm{T}}\geq 0
and

r\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the
r
th component of

\mathbf{A}(t)^{-1}\mathbf{u}
is positive if and only if there exist an integer
q\geq 0

and an index
s\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle(\mathbf{B}(t)^{q})_{rs}u_{s}>0.

Proof.

Lemma
3.1
gives
\mathbf{A}(t)=\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{B}(t)
with

\mathbf{B}(t)\geq 0
. We first suppose that the support of the
k
th measure at
the fixed parameter
t
has more than
n_{k}
points for every
k
. For
m=1
there is
nothing to prove. For
m\geq 2
, all cross-block entries of
\mathbf{B}(t)
are positive,
so
\mathbf{B}(t)
is irreducible. Let
\mathbf{v}(t)>0
be a Perron vector
satisfying

\mathbf{B}(t)\mathbf{v}(t)=\rho(\mathbf{B}(t))\mathbf{v}(t)
. Denote
the component of
\mathbf{v}(t)
with block index
(k,i)
by

,
and form

(6)

\displaystyle S(x;t)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}v_{k,i}(t)\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}.

We have

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{S(x;t)}{P_{n}(x;t)}\right)=-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\frac{v_{k,i}(t)}{(x-x_{k,i}(t))^{2}}<0.

The quotient tends to opposite infinities at the two ends of every gap
between consecutive zeros of
P_{n}(x;t)
. It follows that
S(x;t)
has
one simple zero in each such gap and none outside the extreme zeros. There are
m-1
gaps between
successive blocks; hence some interval, say
\Delta_{k_{0}}
, contains no
zero arising from a gap between blocks. Let
H(x;t)
be the monic polynomial
whose
n_{k_{0}}-1
zeros are the zeros of

between consecutive zeros of

Q_{n_{k_{0}}}(x;t)
. Interlacing gives

(7)

\displaystyle H(x;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k_{0}}}c_{i}(t)\frac{Q_{n_{k_{0}}}(x;t)}{x-x_{k_{0},i}(t)},

(8)

\displaystyle c_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{H(x_{k_{0},i}(t);t)}{\displaystyle\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k_{0}}}}{\partial x}(x_{k_{0},i}(t);t)}>0.

In every gap between consecutive zeros of
Q_{n_{k_{0}}}(x;t)
, the
polynomials
H(x;t)
and
S(x;t)
have the same unique simple zero.
Their product therefore does not change sign there. At the zeros of

Q_{n_{k_{0}}}(x;t)
,

\displaystyle H(x_{k_{0},i}(t);t)S(x_{k_{0},i}(t);t)

\displaystyle=c_{i}(t)v_{k_{0},i}(t)\left(\frac{\partial Q_{n_{k_{0}}}}{\partial x}(x_{k_{0},i}(t);t)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 34.1433pt{}\times R_{k_{0}}(x_{k_{0},i}(t);t),

Thus
H(x;t)S(x;t)
has the constant sign of
R_{k_{0}}(x;t)
on

\Delta_{k_{0}}
. Its integral is nonzero: the product has only

n_{k_{0}}-1
distinct zeros there, whereas the support of the
k_{0}
th
measure has more than
n_{k_{0}}
points.

For later use, direct substitution in (
6
) and orthogonality
give the identity

(9)

\displaystyle\int\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}S(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)=d_{i}^{(k)}(t)(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{v}(t))_{k,i}.

Indeed, the term with the same block and
j=i
is

v_{k,i}(t)d_{i}^{(k)}(t)
. If
j\neq i
in that block, its multiplier of

P_{n}(x;t)
is

Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)/((x-x_{k,i}(t))(x-x_{k,j}(t)))
, a polynomial of degree

n_{k}-2
, and its integral vanishes. A term from a block
l\neq k
is

v_{l,j}(t)d_{i}^{(k)}(t)a_{i,j}^{(k,l)}(t)
. This proves
(
9
). Combining
(
7
)–(
9

\int H(x;t)S(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k_{0}}(x;t)=(1-\rho(\mathbf{B}(t)))\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k_{0}}}c_{i}(t)d_{i}^{(k_{0})}(t)v_{k_{0},i}(t).

Both the integral and the sum have the sign of
R_{k_{0}}(x;t)
; therefore

\rho(\mathbf{B}(t))<1
.

We now remove the assumption on the size of the supports. First,

\mathbf{A}(t)
is always invertible. Indeed, let

\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy

\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{0}
. Write its components as

w_{k,i}
, and define

T(x;t)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}w_{k,i}\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}.

The same computation as in (
9
) gives

\int\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}T(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)=d_{i}^{(k)}(t)(\mathbf{A}(t)\mathbf{w})_{k,i}=0,\quad 1\leq i\leq n_{k}.

The displayed polynomials form a basis of
\mathbb{P}_{n_{k}-1}
, so

satisfies all the orthogonality conditions for the multi-index. Since

\deg_{x}T(x;t)\leq n-1
, normality gives
T(x;t)=0
. At a zero of

P_{n}(x;t)
, however,

T(x_{k,i}(t);t)=w_{k,i}\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial x}(x_{k,i}(t);t).

The zeros are simple, and therefore
w_{k,i}=0
for every
k
and
i
.

Choose compactly supported positive measures
\tau_{k}
of infinite support in

\Delta_{k}
and replace
\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)
by

\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)+\varepsilon\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{k}(x)
. The preceding case gives

\rho(\mathbf{B}_{\varepsilon}(t))<1
. The moments converge as

. Invertibility of the limiting moment system then
gives convergence of the coefficients by Cramer’s rule, simplicity gives
convergence of the labelled zeros, and

gives convergence
of the entries of
\mathbf{B}_{\varepsilon}(t)
. Consequently

\rho(\mathbf{B}(t))\leq 1
. Equality is impossible because the Perron root of

\mathbf{B}(t)\geq 0
is an eigenvalue and

\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{B}(t)=\mathbf{A}(t)
is invertible. Thus

\rho(\mathbf{B}(t))<1
in all cases. Finally,

\mathbf{A}(t)^{-1}=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{B}(t)^{q}\geq 0,

which also proves the strict statement and (
5
).
∎

Remark 3.1
.

The qualification in the strict statement is necessary. If

(n_{1},n_{2})=(1,1)

.

(10)

\displaystyle\int(1+|x|^{n+n_{k}-1})|\phi_{k}(x;t)|\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t)

Proposition 3.1

Let
I
be an open interval, let
n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}
be positive integers,
and put

(a_{k}(t),b_{k}(t))

\frac{\mathrm{d}a_{k}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t),\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{k}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)\geq 0

3.2

\mathbf{A}(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf{b}}(t),

\displaystyle\widetilde{b}_{i}^{(k)}(t)d_{i}^{(k)}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{a_{k}(t)}^{b_{k}(t)}\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(x;t)P_{n}(x;t)}{x-x_{k,i}(t)}\frac{\partial\omega_{k}}{\partial t}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mathrm{d}b_{k}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)\frac{Q_{n_{k}}(b_{k}(t);t)P_{n}(b_{k}(t);t)}{b_{k}(t)-x_{k,i}(t)}\omega_{k}(b_{k}(t)-;t)

Extensions and limitations

4.1.
A general variation identity

We now drop the Angelesco assumption. Fix a normal type II problem for the
multi-index
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
, put
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
, and suppose that

P_{n}(x;t)
has simple real zeros

x_{1}(t)<\cdots<x_{n}(t)
. For every
i
, normality gives unique polynomials

(12)

\displaystyle\deg_{x}\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)

\displaystyle\leq n_{k}-1,

(13)

\displaystyle q(x_{i}(t))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int q(x)\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t),\quad q\in\mathbb{P}_{n-1}.

Indeed, after imposing the reproduction identity on the basis

1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}
, the coefficient matrix is the transpose of the
moment matrix of the normal problem. This proves existence and uniqueness of
the polynomials
\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)
.
Define finite signed measures

(14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,k}(x;t)=\frac{P_{n}(x;t)\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)}{\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial x}(x_{i}(t);t)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

Multiple orthogonality implies
\sigma_{i,k}(\mathbb{R};t)=0

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the normality and simplicity assumptions above, suppose that the
moment differentiability hypotheses of Proposition

hold with
Borel admissible representing functions
x\longmapsto\phi_{k}(x;t)
, as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int\phi_{k}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,k}(x;t).

At a fixed parameter value, the inequality

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int f_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,k}(x;t)\geq 0

holds for every independent choice of bounded Borel non-decreasing functions

x\longmapsto f_{k}(x)
if and only if

(16)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i,k}((y,\infty);t)\geq 0,\quad k=1,\ldots,m,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

On the diagonal, the inequality

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{i,k}(x;t)\geq 0

holds for every bounded Borel non-decreasing function
x\longmapsto f(x)

if and only if

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sigma_{i,k}((y,\infty);t)\geq 0,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

The same equivalences hold for unbounded Borel functions satisfying

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int|f_{k}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}|\sigma_{i,k}|(x;t)<\infty

in the independent case, and the analogous condition with
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{m}=f

in the diagonal case. Under (
16
), independent
non-increasing admissible representing functions give non-positive
derivatives of the zeros. Under (
17
), the same
conclusion holds when the representing functions are common, that is,

\phi_{1}(x;t)=\cdots=\phi_{m}(x;t)
for every

.

Proof.

Differentiate first the monomial orthogonality conditions. At the fixed
parameter value, take their linear combination with the coefficients of

\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)
; these coefficients are held fixed in this operation.
The products that occur have degree at most
n+n_{k}-1
, so the representing
identities in Proposition
2.1
apply. Differentiation with respect to

t
leaves the leading coefficient unchanged. Hence, as a polynomial in

x

\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial t}(x;t)
belongs to

\mathbb{P}_{n-1}
, and (

\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial t}(x_{i}(t);t)=-\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int P_{n}(x;t)\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)\phi_{k}(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{k}(x;t).

Together with

\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=-\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial t}(x_{i}(t);t)}{\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial x}(x_{i}(t);t)},

this proves
(

It remains to justify the equivalences, including functions with jumps. Let

\sigma
be a finite signed measure of mass zero and suppose its open right
tails are non-negative. After subtracting a lower bound, every bounded
non-decreasing function

may be assumed non-negative
and has the representation

\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\phi(x)>s\}}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Each superlevel set is empty, all of
\mathbb{R}
,
(c,\infty)
, or

[c,\infty)
. Closed tails are non-negative as well, since

\sigma([c,\infty))=\lim_{y\uparrow c}\sigma((y,\infty))
. Fubini’s theorem
therefore yields
\int\phi(x)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(x)\geq 0
. Truncation and dominated
convergence give the integrable case. Conversely, testing with

gives each tail inequality. Apply this argument
separately to the
\sigma_{i,k}

∎

A negative separate or combined tail is realised by multiplying,
respectively, the corresponding measure or all the measures by

\vartheta(x;s)=\exp\{s\mathbf{1}_{(y,\infty)}(x)\}
. At

its

Normality and simplicity persist for sufficiently small

by continuity,
as in Proposition

. For Angelesco systems,

rules out such a negative separate tail; hence

combined tails are decisive.

The extension of Theorem

obtained by imposing Markov’s

Example 4.1

Thus the componentwise Markov condition fails on a common support, and

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
be a Chebyshev system on an interval containing

y_{0}<\cdots<y_{n}
, and let

(24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\nu(x;t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_{j}e^{t\phi(y_{j})}\,\mathrm{d}\delta_{y_{j}}(x),\quad a_{j}>0,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where the values
\phi(y_{j})
are non-decreasing with
j
. If
P_{n}(x;t)
is
the monic polynomial determined by

\int P_{n}(x;t)u_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x;t)=0,\quad k=1,\ldots,n,

then all its zeros are non-decreasing functions of
t
. They are strictly
increasing if the values
\phi(y_{0}),\ldots,\phi(y_{n})
are not all equal.

Proof.

The evaluation determinants of a Chebyshev system on ordered points have one
common nonzero sign. After multiplying one of the functions by
-1
, if
necessary, assume that this sign is positive, and put

D(x)=\prod_{j=0}^{n}(x-y_{j})
. Expanding the null vector of
the
n
by
n+1
evaluation matrix by cofactors gives

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{D(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{r_{j}(t)}{x-y_{j}},

(26)

\displaystyle r_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{j}e^{-t\phi(y_{j})}}{\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}c_{l}e^{-t\phi(y_{l})}},

where

c_{j}=\frac{\det[u_{k}(y_{l})]_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n\\[7.0pt]
0\leq l\leq n,\ l\neq j\end{subarray}}}{a_{j}\left|\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}D}{\mathrm{d}x}(y_{j})\right|}>0.

Let
\Delta_{j}
denote the determinant in the numerator. Cofactor expansion
gives, for one constant
\lambda(t)
,

a_{j}e^{t\phi(y_{j})}P_{n}(y_{j};t)=\lambda(t)(-1)^{j}\Delta_{j}.

At
y_{j}
, the derivative of
D
has sign
(-1)^{n-j}
, so all residues
have the same sign. Moreover,

\frac{P_{n}(x;t)}{D(x)}=x^{-1}+O(x^{-2})

at infinity, and therefore the residues sum to one. This fixes
\lambda(t)

and proves (
25
) with
r_{j}(t)>0
.

Write the right-hand side of (
25
) as
F(x;t)
, and
observe first that every
r_{j}(t)
is positive. On each interval

(y_{j},y_{j+1})
, the function
x\longmapsto F(x;t)
decreases from
+\infty
to

-\infty
, because

\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}(x;t)=-\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{r_{j}(t)}{(x-y_{j})^{2}}<0.

It follows that
P_{n}(x;t)
has exactly one simple zero in each of these
intervals. Let
\xi(t)
be any one of them. Extend the values
\phi(y_{j})

to a non-decreasing function on the interval, and put

\overline{\phi}(t)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}r_{l}(t)\phi(y_{l}).

Direct differentiation of the second identity in
(
25
) gives

\frac{\mathrm{d}r_{j}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=r_{j}(t)\bigl(\overline{\phi}(t)-\phi(y_{j})\bigr).

At a zero
\xi(t)
, the preceding formula gives

\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}(\xi(t);t)<0
, while

F(\xi(t);t)=0
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(\xi(t);t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{n}r_{j}(t)\frac{\phi(y_{j})-\overline{\phi}(t)}{\xi(t)-y_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=0}^{n}r_{j}(t)\frac{\phi(y_{j})-\phi(\xi(t))}{\xi(t)-y_{j}}\geq 0.

The middle expression may be changed into the last one because their
difference is a constant multiple of
F(\xi(t);t)
. Every quotient in the
last sum is non-positive. If the values
\phi(y_{j})

are not all equal, at least one of these quotients is strictly negative for
every zero
\xi(t)
. Hence

\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=-\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(\xi(t);t)}{\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}(\xi(t);t)}\geq 0.

The last inequality is strict when the values
\phi(y_{j})
are not all equal.
∎

4.2.
Nikishin systems

We use the convention

\widehat{\tau}(z)=\int\frac{1}{z-y}\,\mathrm{d}\tau(y).

Let
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
be finite positive Borel measures with
infinite compact supports. Write
\Delta_{j}
for the convex hull of

\operatorname{supp}\sigma_{j}
, and assume that consecutive intervals
\Delta_{j}
are
disjoint. Define

\displaystyle s_{j,j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j},

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s_{j,k}(x)

\displaystyle=\widehat{s}_{j+1,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}(x),\quad j<k.

The ordered system of measures

\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m})=(s_{1,1},\ldots,s_{1,m})

is a Nikishin system. Fix a multi-index
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
and put

n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
. Write its monic type II polynomial as

P_{n}
, and choose constants

\varepsilon_{1}=1
,
\varepsilon_{k}\in\{-1,1\}
so that

\nu_{k}=\varepsilon_{k}s_{1,k}
is positive. This does not alter orthogonality.
Such systems are perfect
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
; their type II
zeros are simple and interior to
\Delta_{1}
, and neighbouring polynomials
strictly interlace
[
14
, Corollaries 4.5 and 5.5]
.

We first deform the first generator. Keep
\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
fixed and set

\mathrm{d}\sigma_{1}(x;t)=\omega(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
. Define

\mathrm{d}s_{1,1}^{0}(x)=\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
and

\mathrm{d}s_{1,k}^{0}(x)=\widehat{s}_{2,k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)
for
k\geq 2
.
Then, for every
k
,

(27)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s_{1,k}(x;t)=\omega(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}s_{1,k}^{0}(x).

Thus every positive multiplicative deformation of the first generator has
the same logarithmic derivative on all components. With the dual polynomials
from (
12
), write, for
x\in\Delta_{1}
,

\displaystyle w_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=1,\quad w_{k}(x)=\varepsilon_{k}\,\widehat{s}_{2,k}(x)>0,\quad k=2,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{i}(x;t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Lambda_{i,k}(x;t)w_{k}(x).

The combined measure in (
17
) is therefore

(28)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{i}(x;t)

\displaystyle=\frac{P_{n}(x;t)\mathcal{K}_{i}(x;t)}{\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{n}}{\partial x}(x_{i}(t);t)}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{1}(x;t),

(29)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i}(\mathbb{R};t)

\displaystyle=0.

Assume that the induced positive component measures satisfy the moment
differentiability hypotheses of Proposition
2.1
, with the common
admissible representing function

\phi(x;t)=\frac{1}{\omega(x;t)}\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}(x;t).

Then

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)=\int\phi(x;t)\,\mathrm{d}\Sigma_{i}(x;t).

By Theorem
4.1
, the Markov conclusion for every common
non-decreasing logarithmic derivative is equivalent to

(31)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i}((y,\infty);t)\geq 0,\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

Perfectness and interlacing alone do not prove these tails: the multiple
Christoffel–Darboux kernel is generally non-symmetric and has no scalar
sum-of-squares representation
[
5
,
20
]
.

We next consider an exterior Christoffel factor. Write
\Delta_{1}=[a,b]
. Given

z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[a,b]
, let

\widetilde{P}_{n}^{[z]}
be the type II polynomial after replacing

\sigma_{1}
by
|x-z|\sigma_{1}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

For every multi-index
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
, with
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}
, and every

z\notin[a,b]
, if

x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
and

\widetilde{x}_{1}^{[z]}<\cdots<\widetilde{x}_{n}^{[z]}
are the zeros of
P_{n}

and
\widetilde{P}_{n}^{[z]}
, respectively, then

\displaystyle z<a

\displaystyle:\quad x_{1}<\widetilde{x}_{1}^{[z]}<x_{2}<\cdots<x_{n}<\widetilde{x}_{n}^{[z]},

\displaystyle z>b

\displaystyle:\quad\widetilde{x}_{1}^{[z]}<x_{1}<\cdots<\widetilde{x}_{n}^{[z]}<x_{n}.

The same conclusion holds for an AT system on
[a,b]
multiplied by the common factor

|x-z|
, provided the multi-indices
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
and

(n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}+1,\ldots,n_{m})
are normal for some
j
, their type II
polynomials strictly interlace, and the transformed problem at

(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
is normal.

Proof.

Choose
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
; in the AT case choose one satisfying the stated
hypotheses. Put
p(x)=P_{n}(x)
, and let
q
be the monic type II polynomial
associated with the multi-index
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{j}+1,\ldots,n_{m})
. The
common-factor relation and the orthogonality relations give

(32)

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}_{n}^{[z]}(x)=\frac{q(x)-q(z)p(x)/p(z)}{x-z}.

Indeed,
|x-z|
is a constant nonzero multiple of
x-z
on
[a,b]
.
The numerator vanishes at
z
. For every component measure and every
required exponent
r
, its transformed orthogonality follows from

\displaystyle\int x^{r}\widetilde{P}_{n}^{[z]}(x)|x-z|\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{l}(x)

\displaystyle\quad=\pm\int x^{r}q(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{l}(x)\mp\frac{q(z)}{p(z)}\int x^{r}p(x)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{l}(x)=0.

The quotient in (
32
) is monic of degree
n
;
normality therefore identifies it with the transformed polynomial. Set

R(x)=q(x)/p(x)
. Strict
interlacing and monicity imply

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}

\displaystyle=x-\gamma+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{r_{i}}{x-x_{i}},

(34)

\displaystyle r_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{q(x_{i})}{\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}x}(x_{i})}<0.

The last sign follows because
q(x_{i})
and

\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{\mathrm{d}x}(x_{i})
have opposite signs at every
interlacing zero.
Consequently

\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\mathrm{d}x}(x)=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{r_{i}}{(x-x_{i})^{2}}>0

on every component of
\mathbb{R}\setminus\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. On each such
component
R(x)
increases from
-\infty
to
+\infty
. Hence the equation

R(x)=R(z)
has one solution in every component; the solution in the component
containing
z
is
z
itself. The remaining solutions are precisely the
transformed zeros and give the displayed order. The Nikishin hypotheses were
recalled above; related Christoffel interlacings appear in

[
13
, Corollary 3.23]
.
∎

Multiplication by the positive common exterior factor preserves the AT
property. For an AT system on a closed interval, normality and simple interior type II
zeros are automatic at every multi-index for which the system is AT; if it is
AT at the two indicated neighbouring multi-indices, strict interlacing is
automatic as well

[
14
, Theorem 3.6 and Corollaries 4.4 and 5.4]
.
Jacobi–Piñeiro systems are classical examples on a compact interval, while
multiple Laguerre systems give standard unbounded examples

[
10
, Sections 23.3.2 and 23.4]
.

Iteration treats products of exterior factors: points on the left move all
zeros to the right, points on the right move them to the left, and mixed
products are monotone in each exterior coordinate. No density is needed. For
the continuous form, let

R(x)=q(x)/p(x)
refer to the fixed base system generated by
\mu
, and let

z:I\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\setminus[a,b]
be a
C^{1}
function whose values
remain in one component. If

\mathrm{d}\sigma_{1}(x;t)=|x-z(t)|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x),

denote the transformed zeros by
\xi_{i}(z(t))
. As functions of the exterior
point, they satisfy

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi_{i}}{\mathrm{d}z}(z)=\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\mathrm{d}x}(z)}{\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}R}{\mathrm{d}x}(\xi_{i}(z))}>0.

This follows by differentiating
R(\xi_{i}(z))=R(z)
. Moreover,

\frac{1}{|x-z(t)|}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|x-z(t)|=-\frac{1}{x-z(t)}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}t}(t).

This function is non-decreasing with respect to
x
precisely when

\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)\geq 0
.

The preceding result yields a larger class of deformations. Fix

x_{0}\in(a,b)
and put

\kappa_{z}(x)=-\frac{1}{x-z}+\frac{1}{x_{0}-z}.

Theorem 4.4
.

For the standard infinite-support Nikishin system above, fix a multi-index

Let

\displaystyle\phi(x;t)=C(t)+\alpha(t)(x-x_{0})+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[a,b]}\kappa_{z}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\eta_{t}(z),

Proof.

Fix

-\frac{1}{x-z}

Since the multi-index was arbitrary, the theorem applies simultaneously to

For finite supports, require, for every

Thus monotonicity of the logarithmic derivative of an internal generator does
not imply the usual Markov conclusion for the zeros. For deformations of the
first generator, (
31
) is necessary and sufficient for the Markov
conclusion to hold for every common non-decreasing admissible representing
function, while
Theorems
4.2
,
4.3
,
and
4.4
cover the stated positive cases. More generally, at a
fixed parameter value, Theorem
4.1
gives the complete test for the
corresponding independent or common class of non-decreasing representing
functions: a negative tail is realised by the step deformation above.
The Angelesco theorem thus gives a general extension of Markov’s principle,
while the dual criterion characterizes its validity for these deformation
classes in the normal type II setting with simple real zeros.

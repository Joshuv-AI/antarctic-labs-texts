Intersection Theory of Hyperquot Schemes on curves
Riccardo Ontani, Shubham Sinha, Weihong Xu

Abstract
. We study the virtual intersection theory of Hyperquot schemes parameterizing sequences of quotient sheaves of a vector bundle on a smooth projective curve. Our results generalize the Vafa–Intriligator formula for Quot schemes and provide a closed formula for virtual counts of maps from the curve to a partial flag variety.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and
V
be a vector bundle of rank
n
over
C
. The Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,r,V)
is a projective scheme that parametrizes short exact sequences of sheaves on
C
:

0\to E\xhookrightarrow{}V\twoheadrightarrow F\to 0\quad\text{such that }\begin{cases}\mathrm{rank}\,F=n-r,\\
\deg F=d.\end{cases}

When
V
is trivial, the Quot scheme compactifies the space of degree
d
maps from

C
to the Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}(r,n)
, and it was used to prove the Vafa–Intriligator formula for counts of such maps satisfying some incidence conditions; see
[
53
,
6
,
5
,
37
]
.

The Hyperquot scheme is a natural generalization of the Quot scheme. For fixed tuples

\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{k})

of integers, the Hyperquot scheme
\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathbf{r},V)
, or
\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}
for short, parametrizes sequences of sheaves of the form

E_{1}\xhookrightarrow{}E_{2}\xhookrightarrow{}\cdots E_{k}\xhookrightarrow{}V\twoheadrightarrow F_{1}\twoheadrightarrow F_{2}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots\twoheadrightarrow F_{k}\quad\text{such that }\begin{cases}\mathrm{rank}\,F_{j}=n-r_{j},\\
\deg F_{j}=d_{j},\end{cases}

where
E_{j}
is the kernel of the surjection
V\twoheadrightarrow F_{j}
for each
j
.

In this article, we establish a formula for virtual intersection numbers on the Hyperquot scheme, generalizing the work of Bertram
[
6
]
and Marian–Oprea
[
37
]
. In the special case where
V
is trivial and
r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k}
are distinct, the Hyperquot scheme compactifies the space of multidegree
\mathbf{d}
maps from
C
to the partial flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)
, and our formula provides a virtual count of such maps satisfying certain incidence conditions. Furthermore, when
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the Hyperquot scheme is a smooth variety and the virtual counts in consideration are actual counts
[
30
,
16
]
that played an important role in the study of the small quantum cohomology of
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)
; see, for example,
[
29
,
7
,
17
,
16
,
14
]
.

Remark 1.3

The expression in (

Remark 1.4

Remark 1.5

We also prove a compatibility result of virtual classes in Proposition

\displaystyle\iota_{*}[\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}=e(\mathcal{E}_{\ell}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\ell+1})_{|p}\cap[\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{\ell}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}.

1.2.
Specializations

We now state the
k=1

3

Let the torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
act on
\quot_{d}(C,r,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n})
by scaling the fibers of
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n}
, and let
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}\in H_{T}^{*}(\mathrm{pt})
be the corresponding equivariant parameters.

Corollary 1.6
.

For non-negative integers
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
and generic values of
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n};q)
,
we have

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\int_{[\quot_{d}(C,r,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n})]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}^{T}(\mathcal{E}_{|p}^{\vee})^{m_{i}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}(\boldsymbol{\zeta})^{m_{i}}\cdot J^{g-1},

where the sum is taken over all unordered
r
-tuples
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{r})
of distinct roots of the equation

(X-\varepsilon_{1})\cdots(X-\varepsilon_{n})+(-1)^{r}q=0,

and the factor
J
is given by

J\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{\prod_{s\leq n,\ s\neq\ell}\left\lparen\zeta_{i}-\varepsilon_{s}\right\rparen}{\prod_{t\leq r,\ t\neq i}\left\lparen\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{t}\right\rparen}.

Remark 1.7
.

Specializing further to the non-equivariant setting (i.e., letting all
\varepsilon_{s}
go to
0
), we recover the Vafa–Intriligator formula
of
[
37
, Theorem 3]
.
An analogous Vafa–Intriligator formula for fixed-domain Gromov–Witten invariants on the Grassmannian (which are defined using the moduli spaces of stable maps) was proven in
[
48
]
and the two formulas match.

Remark 1.8
.

In
[
37
]
, the virtual intersections of more general classes arising from the Künneth decomposition of
c_{i}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})
on
\quot_{d}(C,r,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n})\times C
were also considered. One could study the analogous problem for Hyperquot schemes. We do not pursue this direction in the present paper; however, our methods may prove useful for such investigations.

The
Hyperquot schemes of points

\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,V)
parameterizing successive zero-dimensional quotients of

\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{k})

are smooth projective varieties; in our notation, they have ranks
\mathbf{r}=(n,n,\dots,n)
. They play an important role in the study of the cohomology
[

]
and the derived category
[

,

of the Quot schemes of points on

. Theorem

implies the following simple formula (see Corollary

for the Chern class version):

.

Given a rank

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}}q_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots q_{k}^{d_{k}}\int_{\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,V)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}s_{t_{j}}(\mathcal{E}_{j|p})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1-t_{j}^{n}\alpha_{j}},

where

are Segre polynomials and

.

Remark 1.10

The Segre integrals and Euler characteristics of more general tautological classes on the Quot schemes of points on

. Our methods may be useful for evaluating analogous invariants on Hyperquot schemes of points.

Fix

1.3.
Related work and future directions

When the ranks
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
are distinct, let
\mor_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n))
be the moduli space parametrizing morphisms
f
from
C
to the partial flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)
of multidegree
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
, that is,

f_{*}[C]=\mathbf{d}\in H_{2}(\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{k}.

The basis for the homology group above is given, via the universal coefficient isomorphism
H_{2}(\mathrm{Fl},\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{2}(\mathrm{Fl},\mathbb{Z})
, by the first Chern classes of the dual bundles to the universal subbundles
\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{k}
on
\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)
. The Hyperquot scheme
\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathbf{r},\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n})
compactifies
\mor_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n))
, which suggests the following:

Problem 1.12
.

Find conditions under which the virtual intersection numbers on the Hyperquot scheme
\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathbf{r},\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n})
in Theorem
1.2
are enumerative, that is,

(6)

\int_{[\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{t}c_{i_{\ell}}(\mathcal{E}_{j_{\ell}|p}^{\vee})=\#\left\{f:C\to\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)\mid f_{*}[C]=\mathbf{d},\ f(p_{\ell})\in Y_{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}\text{ for all }\ell\right\},

where
p_{1},\dots,p_{t}\in C
are distinct points, and

Y_{i_{1},j_{1}},\dots,Y_{i_{t},j_{t}}
are the special Schubert subvarieties of

\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)
Poincaré dual to the classes

c_{i_{1}}(\mathcal{A}_{j_{1}}^{\vee}),\dots,c_{i_{t}}(\mathcal{A}_{j_{t}}^{\vee})
,
placed in general position so that the right-hand side is finite.

If
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, the Hyperquot scheme in Problem
1.12
is smooth and irreducible, and the enumerativity of the intersection numbers was established in
[
30
,
16
]
for all multidegrees
\mathbf{d}
. The enumerativity of the virtual counts in the Grassmannian case (i.e., for
\quot_{d}(C,r,\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\oplus n})
) was proved by Bertram
[
6
]
for sufficiently large degrees

. More recently, this approach was also used in
[
38
]
to count maps to Fano hypersurfaces in Grassmannians. We plan to pursue Problem
1.12
in our upcoming work.

Let us summarize, to the best of our knowledge, the occurrences of our system of equations (
3
) in the literature.
In physics, they arise as the vacuum equations of a two-dimensional gauged
linear sigma model; equivalently, they define the critical locus of the
twisted effective superpotential; see
[
26

and
[
28
]
. Our equations also appear as relations describing the small quantum cohomology of the Grassmannian in
[
48
]
and of the partial flag variety in
[

]
.
In the Grassmannian case, these are also the Bethe ansatz equations of
[

, which arise naturally in integrable systems.

In particular, we can view formula (

\mathrm{Fl}(\mathbf{r},n)
over the affine space of quantum parameters
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}
. Moreover, the properties of the solutions to the system of equations we derive in Section

imply that the quantum cohomology of

is generically semisimple. Our results may have other applications in the contexts mentioned above.

When

By

As mentioned earlier, when the ranks are distinct, the Hyperquot scheme

In this sense, our work fits into the literature computing quasimap invariants of Fano quotients of linear spaces.

(presented as a quotient of a linear space

1.4.
Sketch of proof

We use deformation invariance of virtual integrals (Proposition
2.4
) to reduce to the case where

V=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}

and each
M_{i}\to C
is a line bundle of degree zero. We then consider the action of
\left\lparen\mathbb{C}^{*}\right\rparen^{n}
scaling each
M_{j}
and apply torus localization to reduce the calculation to summing over contributions from the components of the fixed locus, which are products of Hilbert schemes on
C
. We handle the summation carefully by utilizing the multivariate Lagrange–Bürmann formula. This allows us to prove, in genus zero, that the generating polynomial of equivariant virtual integrals is a sum over formal power series solutions to the system of equations (see Theorem
4.7
). We prove in Proposition
4.17
that this generating polynomial can be evaluated by first specializing
\mathbf{q}
and the equivariant parameters to generic values, and then summing over non-degenerate solutions; the proof applies Gröbner degeneration to the system of equations (
3
). In higher genus, we need some intricate intersection-theoretic computations on products of Hilbert schemes on
C
, which we handle in Section
5
.

2.
Perfect obstruction theory and virtual fundamental class

In this section, we recall some foundational results about the virtual intersection theory on Hyperquot schemes.
Let
B
be a scheme and consider a vector bundle
V
on
B\times C
over
B
. The relative Hyperquot scheme

(7)

\displaystyle f:\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathbf{r},V)\to B

can be constructed as a tower of relative Quot schemes as we outline below in the two-step case. Let
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},r_{2})
and
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},d_{2})
. The first step is to consider the relative Quot scheme

\quot_{d_{2}}(C,r_{2},V)\to B.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{2}
be the universal subsheaf over
\quot_{d_{2}}(C,r_{2},V)\times C
, and consider the relative Quot scheme

\quot_{d_{1}-d_{2}}(C,r_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\to\quot_{d_{2}}(C,r_{2},V).

It is easy to check that the composition

(8)

\quot_{d_{1}-d_{2}}(C,r_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\to\quot_{d_{2}}(C,r_{2},V)\to B

is the relative Hyperquot scheme
\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathbf{r},V)
over
B
. This construction can be easily iterated for flags of any length.

]

2.4.
A vanishing criterion

Recall that the Hyperquot scheme
\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(C,\mathbf{r},V)
is constructed iteratively using relative Quot schemes

with relative virtual dimensions

\displaystyle\mathrm{vdim}\phi_{j}

\displaystyle=\chi(E_{j},E_{j+1})-\chi(E_{j},E_{j})

\displaystyle=\bigr((1-g)\,r_{j}-e\bigl)(r_{j+1}-r_{j})+r_{j+1}d_{j}-r_{j}d_{j+1}.

We note the following vanishing result, not immediate from the formula in Theorem

, which follows from the properties of virtual pullbacks.

Remark 2.7

When the virtual dimension of an intermediate Quot scheme

is zero and all virtual integrals on the Hyperquot scheme vanish:

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that similar vanishings need not hold when the virtual dimension of a single morphism
\phi_{j}
is negative.
For instance, let
C
be a curve of genus
13
, let

be a rank-

, and take

. Then Corollary

H^{\ast}(\mathrm{X}_{\sigma,D})\otimes_{H_{T}^{\ast}(\mathrm{pt})}\Gamma
only after clearing the common factors in the numerators and denominators.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume
\sigma=1
. Using the description in (
26
) and the projection formula, we note that the equivariant Euler classes of the pushforwards appearing in (
24
) are given by

\displaystyle e_{T}\left(\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{u,j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(z_{s,j}-z_{u,j}\right)^{d_{s,j}-d_{u,j}+1};

\displaystyle e_{T}\left(\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{u,{j+1}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(z_{s,j}-z_{u,{j+1}}\right)^{d_{s,j}-d_{u,{j+1}}+1}.

We now carefully substitute the equivariant Euler classes computed above into the formula (
24
) for the normal bundle
N^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\sigma,D}
, noting that the equivariant Euler class is multiplicative. We first consider the terms in the same column (i.e. keeping the subscript
j
fixed):

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,u\leq r_{j}\\
s\neq u\end{subarray}}e_{T}\left(\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{s,j}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{u,j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{j}(r_{j}-1)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,u\leq r_{j}\\
s\neq u\end{subarray}}\left(z_{s,j}-z_{u,{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d_{j}(r_{j}-1)}\prod_{s\leq r_{j}}R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j}).

In the first equality, we used that
\left(z_{s,j}-z_{u,j}\right)^{d_{s,j}-d_{u,j}}
cancel each other by swapping the role of
s
and
u
, leaving behind the sign
(-1)^{d_{j}(r_{j}-1)}
.

We then consider the terms involving two consecutive columns:

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq r_{j}\\
u\leq r_{j+1}\\
s\neq u\end{subarray}}e_{T}\left(\pi_{*}\left(\mathcal{K}^{\vee}_{s,j}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{u,{j+1}}\right)\right)=

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq r_{j}\\
u\leq r_{j+1}\\
s\neq u\end{subarray}}\left(z_{s,j}-z_{u,{j+1}}\right)^{d_{s,j}-d_{u,{j+1}}+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{R_{{j+1}}(z_{s,j})}{x_{s,j}}\right)^{d_{s,j}+1}\cdot\prod_{u\leq r_{j+1}}\left(\frac{(-1)^{r_{j}}x_{u,j}}{R_{j}(z_{u,{j+1}})}\right)^{d_{u,{j+1}}},

where we set
x_{u,j}\coloneqq 1
for
u>r_{j}
.
Substituting these two expressions in the formula for the Euler class of (
24
) we get the required identity.
∎

4.2.
Atiyah–Bott localization

Recall that
\Hquot_{d}:=\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbf{r},V)
for a split vector bundle
V=M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}
, where
M_{i}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
endowed with equivariant weight
\varepsilon_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.

Let
Q\in H_{T}^{\ast}(\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}})
be a polynomial in the equivariant Chern classes of the bundles
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}_{1|p},\dots,\mathcal{E}^{\vee}_{k|p}
, and let
\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})
be the corresponding symmetric polynomial such that
Q|_{\mathrm{X}_{1,D}}=\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})
, where
\mathbf{z}\coloneqq(z_{s,j})
are the Chern roots (
27
). Note moreover that
Q_{|X_{\sigma,D}}=\sigma\cdot\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})
.
By the Atiyah-Bott localization formula
[
2
]
we find

\displaystyle\int_{\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}}Q

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma,D}\int_{\mathrm{X}_{\sigma,D}}\ \frac{Q|_{\mathrm{X}_{\sigma,D}}}{e_{T}(N^{\mathrm{vir}}_{\sigma,D})}.

Note that the equivariant Euler class in the denominator is invertible in the localized equivariant cohomology
H^{\ast}_{T}(X_{\sigma,D})\otimes_{H_{T}^{\ast}(\mathrm{pt})}\Gamma
.

To evaluate the integrals on the right-hand side, we just have to consider the top-power of the hyperplane class on each projective space
\mathbb{P}^{d_{s,j}-d_{s,j+1}}
, which amounts to extracting the coefficient of
x_{s,j}^{d_{s,j}-d_{s,j+1}}
for all
1\leq j\leq k
and
1\leq s\leq r_{j}
.
Therefore, using (
29
), we express the integral as the residue

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{[\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}}]}Q=(-1)^{\beta}\sum_{\sigma,D}\,\sigma\cdot\Res_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}}\,\Bigl(\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq k\\
s\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})R_{{j-1}}(z_{s,j})^{d_{s,j}}}{R_{{j+1}}(z_{s,j})^{d_{s,j}+1}}\Bigr).

The argument of the residue should be understood as a rational function in the formal variables
x_{s,j}
and
\varepsilon_{j}
, with
z_{s,j}
defined as in (
27
). This can be written uniquely, by expanding each linear factor in the denominator as

(31)

\displaystyle(f(\mathbf{x})+g(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}))^{-1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}f(\mathbf{x})^{k}g(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})^{-k-1},

as a multivariate power series in the variables
x_{s,j}
having coefficients in the field
\Gamma
. Then the residue
\Res_{\mathbf{x}=0}
extracts the coefficient of
1/\prod_{j,s}x_{s,j}
.

We now apply the following multivariate Lagrange–Bürmann formula, see
[
21
, Theorem 2]
for instance, to simplify the above residue:

Theorem 4.2

(Lagrange–Bürmann formula)
.

Consider multivariate formal power series
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{N}
in variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
, each with non-zero constant term. Then the following change of variables

t_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{\Phi_{i}}\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq N,

has unique inverses
x_{i}(t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{N})
, which are multivariate power series for each
1\leq i\leq N
. Moreover, for any Laurent series
\Psi
in
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
,

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1},\dots,m_{N}\geq 0}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{N}^{m_{N}}\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbf{x}=0}\left(\Psi\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\Phi_{i}}{x_{i}}\right)^{m_{i}}\right)=\Psi\cdot\det\left(t_{i}^{-1}\frac{\partial t_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}^{-1},

where
\Psi
on the right-hand side is understood to be expressed in terms of the variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{N}
.

Define the rational functions

\displaystyle\Psi_{s,j}=\frac{x_{s,{j}}R_{{j-1}}(z_{s,j})}{x_{s,{j-1}}R_{{j+1}}(z_{s,j})}

where we set
x_{s,j}\coloneqq 1
for
s>r_{j}
. Expanding as in (
31
), each
\Psi_{s,j}
is a formal power
series in the
x
–variables whose constant term is nonzero in
\Gamma
.
For each
j\leq k
and
s\leq r_{j}
we define

(33)

\displaystyle\Phi_{s,j}\coloneqq\Psi_{s,j}\Psi_{s,j-1}\cdots\Psi_{s,1},\qquad\text{with }\;\Psi_{s,j}\coloneqq 1\text{ if }s>r_{j},

which is again a multivariate power series with non-zero constant terms. Consider the change of variables

(34)

\displaystyle t_{s,j}\coloneqq\frac{x_{s,j}}{\Phi_{s,j}}\quad\qquad\text{invertible by Theorem \ref{thm:Lagrange_Burmann}}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider the system of equations

(35)

R_{j+1}(z_{s,j})=q_{s,j}R_{j-1}(z_{s,j}),

in the variables
z_{s,j}\in\Gamma[\![q_{i,\ell}]\!]
and consider the tuple
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\mathrm{fund}}=(\zeta^{\mathrm{fund}}_{s,j})
of power series

(36)

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mathrm{fund}}_{s,j}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell\geq j}x_{s,\ell}(\mathbf{t})+\varepsilon_{s}\qquad\text{evaluated at}\qquad t_{s,j}=q_{s,j}q_{s,j-1}\cdots q_{s,1},

using the notation
q_{s,j}\coloneqq 1
whenever
s>r_{j}
. Then
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\mathrm{fund}}
is a solution of the system (
35
), and we call it the fundamental solution.

Proof.

The following system in the variables
t_{s,j}\in\Gamma[\![q_{i,\ell}]\!]

(37)

t_{s,j-1}q_{s,j}\coloneqq t_{s,j},\qquad\text{can be solved as }\qquad t_{s,j}=q_{s,j}q_{s,j-1}\cdots q_{s,1},

using the notation
t_{s,j}\coloneqq q_{s,j}\coloneqq 1
whenever
s>r_{j}
. Expressed in the
x
-variables, these equations are equivalent to (
35
). Since (
34
) can be inverted and (
37
) can be solved, we can express
z_{s,j}
in terms of the
q
-variables, and we obtain the solution
\zeta^{\mathrm{fund}}_{s,j}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

For any fixed splitting degree
D
, we have

(38)

\displaystyle\Res_{\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}}\,\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq k\\
s\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})R_{{j-1}}(z_{s,j})^{d_{s,j}}}{R_{{j+1}}(z_{s,j})^{d_{s,j}+1}}=[\mathbf{q}^{D}]\,\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq k\\
s\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})}{R_{j-1}(z_{s,j})}\det\left(\frac{\partial q_{s,j}}{\partial z_{u,i}}\right)^{-1}\big|_{\mathbf{z}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\mathrm{fund}}},

where the determinant is the Jacobian of (
35
) and
[\mathbf{q}^{D}]
denotes the operation of extracting the coefficient of
\mathbf{q}^{D}\coloneqq\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s=1}^{r_{j}}q_{s,j}^{d_{s,j}}
in a power series in the variables
q_{s,j}
.

Proof.

A straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s=1}^{r_{j}}\biggl(\frac{R_{j-1}(z_{s,j})}{R_{j+1}(z_{s,j})}\biggr)^{d_{s,j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s=1}^{r_{j}}\biggl(\frac{\Phi_{s,j}}{\Phi_{s,j-1}}\frac{x_{s,j-1}}{x_{s,j}}\biggr)^{d_{s,j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s=1}^{r_{j}}\biggl(\frac{\Phi_{s,j}}{x_{s,j}}\biggr)^{d_{s,j}-d_{s,j+1}},

where in the last step we use the telescoping in
j
and the convention
d_{s,k+1}=0
. Define the rational function

\displaystyle\Psi=\widetilde{Q}(\mathbf{z})\prod_{s\leq r_{j},j\leq k}\frac{R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})}{R_{{j+1}}(z_{s,j})},

which expanded as in (
31
) becomes a Laurent series in the
x
-variables. The left-hand side of (
38
) then becomes

(39)

\Res_{x=0}\Biggl(\Psi\;\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s=1}^{r_{j}}\biggl(\frac{\Phi_{s,j}}{x_{s,j}}\biggr)^{d_{s,j}-d_{s,j+1}}\Biggr)=\left[\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq k\\
s\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}t_{s,j}^{d_{s,j}-d_{s,{j+1}}}\right]\Psi\det\left(t_{s,j}^{-1}\frac{\partial t_{s,j}}{\partial x_{u,i}}\right)^{-1},

where the equality follows from Theorem
4.2
applied to the change of variables (
34
). We now introduce the variables
q_{s,j}
that we want to appear in the final formula. For every
1\leq j\leq k
and
1\leq s\leq r_{j}
we can specialize

(40)

\qquad t_{s,j}=q_{s,j}q_{s,j-1}\cdots q_{s,1},

using the notation
t_{s,j}\coloneqq q_{s,j}\coloneqq 1
whenever
s>r_{j}
. Then (
39
) equals

(41)

\left[\mathbf{q}^{D}\right]\Psi\det\left(t_{i,j}^{-1}\frac{\partial t_{i,j}}{\partial x_{\ell,m}}\right)^{-1},

once we express the argument of
[\mathbf{q}^{D}]
as a multivariate Laurent series in the
q
-variables using (

\det\left(t_{s,j}^{-1}\frac{\partial t_{s,j}}{\partial q_{b,m}}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{j\leq k,\ s\leq r_{j}}q_{s,j}=\prod_{j\leq k,\ s\leq r_{j}}\frac{x_{s,j}\Phi_{s,j-1}}{x_{s,j-1}\Phi_{s,j}}=\prod_{j\leq k,\ s\leq r_{j}}\frac{R_{{j+1}}(\zeta^{\mathrm{fund}}_{s,j})}{R_{{j-1}}(\zeta^{\mathrm{fund}}_{s,{j}})},

thus, by the chain rule, (

\displaystyle\left[\mathbf{q}^{D}\right]\Psi\prod_{j\leq k,s\leq r_{j}}\frac{R_{j+1}(z_{s,j})}{R_{{j-1}}(z_{s,{j}})}\det\left(\frac{\partial q_{s,j}}{\partial z_{b,m}}\right)^{-1}\big|_{\mathbf{z}=\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\mathrm{fund}}}.\qed

Definition 4.5
.

Consider a system of equations in the variables
z_{s,j}
with coefficients in
\Gamma[q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell}]
, where
q_{j}
are formal variables.
We call a
formal solution
a tuple
\boldsymbol{\zeta}=(\zeta_{s,j})

of formal power series
\zeta_{s,j}\in\Gamma[\![q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell}]\!]
solving the system.
We say that a formal solution is
non-degenerate
if, for every
1\leq j\leq k
, the power series
\zeta_{1,j},\dots,\zeta_{r_{j},j}
have pairwise distinct images in

\Gamma[\![q_{1},\dots,q_{k}]\!]/(q_{1},\dots,q_{k})\cong\Gamma
(equivalently, their constant terms
\zeta_{1,j}(0),\dots,\zeta_{r_{j},j}(0)\in\Gamma
are
pairwise distinct).

Note that we can easily characterize the formal non-degenerate solutions of (
35
):

Lemma 4.6
.

Specialize (
35
) to
\mathbf{q}_{j}\coloneqq(q_{j},\dots,q_{j})

(42)

R_{j+1}(z_{s,j})=q_{j}R_{j-1}(z_{s,j})\qquad\qquad 1\leq j\leq k,\quad 1\leq s\leq r_{j}

in the variables

36

\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{r}}\times\prod_{j=1}^{k}S_{r_{j}}

(\sigma,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k})
corresponds to the solution

(43)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{\sigma,\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k}}=(\zeta_{s,j})\qquad\text{with}\qquad\zeta_{s,j}\coloneqq\sigma\cdot\zeta^{\mathrm{fund}}_{\sigma_{j}(s),j}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3

is a formal solution to (

To see this, we apply

[
8

to the ring

and its only non-degenerate solutions

By Hensel’s Lemma, for every solution

to the reduced system (

\displaystyle\det\left(\frac{R_{j}(z_{i,j})}{\partial{z_{k,l}}}\right)_{|_{\bf{z=a}}}\neq 0\qquad\qquad\text{in }\Gamma,

there exists a unique formal solution

to the system (

modulo
\mathfrak{m}

Note that

\displaystyle\left\{\text{non-degenerate solutions of \eqref{eq:specialised_equations}}\right\}\xrightarrow{\text{mod }\mathfrak{m}}\left\{\text{non-degenerate solutions of \eqref{eq:reduced_system}}\right\},

the uniqueness part of Hensel’s lemma proves that all the formal non-degenerate solutions to (

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathbf{w}}^{\ast}y^{j}=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}w^{i}y^{j}_{i}

for every
j\in\{1,\dots,2g\}
.
∎

5.3.
Tautological pushforwards

We consider the projection

\displaystyle\pi:X_{\mathbf{d}}\times C\rightarrow X_{\mathbf{d}}

and, given a vector
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, we look at the class in
K^{0}(X_{\mathbf{d}})
given by the pushforward
\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}]
. The following result follows from a standard application of the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem:

Lemma 5.5
.

The Chern character of
\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}]
is

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}(\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathbf{w}}])=e^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle}(1-g+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle-\theta_{\mathbf{w}})

where we set
\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{i}w^{i}x_{i}
.

Proof.

Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch tells us that the Chern character of
\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathbf{w}}]
is equal to

(52)

\displaystyle\pi_{\ast}\left(\text{ch}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\vee})\cap\text{Td}(T_{\pi})\right),

where
T_{\pi}
is the (pullback of the) tangent bundle of the curve.
Notice that, since
\pi
is a projection,
\pi_{\ast}
in cohomology is just integration over
C
. This means that, in order to compute the pushforward (
52
) we can just compute the Künneth decomposition of the argument in terms of the symplectic basis of
H^{\ast}(C)
and extract the coefficient of
\eta
. We know that

\displaystyle\text{Td}(T_{C})=1+(1-g)\eta,

so we are left to computing
\text{ch}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\vee})
in terms of the
x
and
y
classes defined in (
49
). Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be a
Poincaré bundle on
\text{Pic}^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle}(C)
such that there is a point
p\in C
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\mid_{\text{Pic}^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle}(C)\times p}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\text{Pic}^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle}(C)}.

This means that
c_{1}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
has trivial component coming from
H^{2}(\text{Pic}^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle}(C))
, and by (
50
)

\displaystyle c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\vee})=\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle+(\pi_{\mathbf{w}}\times\text{Id}_{C})^{\ast}c_{1}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}),

which, at the level of Chern characters, reads

\displaystyle\text{ch}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\vee})=e^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle}(\pi_{\mathbf{w}}\times\text{Id}_{C})^{\ast}\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}).

The Chern character of this Poincaré line bundle is

\displaystyle\text{ch}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})=1+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle\eta+\gamma-\theta\otimes\eta,

as computed in
[
1
, page 336]
, where
\gamma
is a class in
H^{1}(\text{Pic}^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle}(C))\otimes H^{1}(C)
. Therefore, we find

\displaystyle\text{ch}(\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\vee})=e^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle}(1+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle\eta+(\pi_{\mathbf{w}}\times\text{Id}_{C})^{\ast}\gamma-\theta_{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\eta).

Putting all together, the Chern character of
\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\vee}]
is the coefficient of
\eta
in

\displaystyle e^{\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle}(1+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle\eta+(\pi_{\mathbf{w}}\times\text{Id}_{C})^{\ast}\gamma-\theta_{\mathbf{w}}\otimes\eta)(1+(1-g)\eta),

which gives the claimed expression after noticing that
\gamma
doesn’t contribute since its component coming from
C
is of odd degree.
∎

From this we can obtain an expression for the total Chern class of this pushforward:

Proposition 5.6
.

The total Chern class of
\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{w}}]
is

\displaystyle c(\pi_{\ast}[\mathcal{L}^{\vee}_{\mathbf{w}}])=(1+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle)^{1-g+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{d}\rangle}\exp\left(-\frac{\theta_{\mathbf{w}}}{1+\langle\mathbf{w},\mathbf{x}\rangle}\right).

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
5.5
and the following standard fact: for every vector bundle
V
, if
\mathrm{ch}(V)=(k+\alpha)\mathrm{ch}(L)
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha\in H^{2}(X)
and a line bundle
L
, then
c(V)=(1+c_{1}(L))^{k}\exp(\frac{\alpha}{1+c_{1}(L)})
. To see that this holds true, apply the splitting principle and let
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{k}
be the Chern roots of
V\otimes L^{-1}
, so that

\displaystyle\log(c(V\otimes L^{-1}))=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\log(1+\omega_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{ch}(V\otimes L^{-1})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\exp(\omega_{i})=k+\alpha.

From the expression for the Chern character we find that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\omega_{i}^{l}}
is non-zero if and only if
l=0
or
l=1
, in which case we find
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\omega_{i}=\alpha
. Then expanding the logarithms we find that
\log(c(V\otimes L^{-1}))=\alpha

and therefore

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\omega_{i})=c(V\otimes L^{-1})=\exp(\alpha).

This readily implies that

\displaystyle c(V)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(1+\omega_{i}+c_{1}(L))=(1+c_{1}(L))^{k}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(1+\frac{\omega_{i}}{1+c_{1}(L)}\right)

\displaystyle=(1+c_{1}(L))^{k}\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{1+c_{1}(L)}\right),

concluding the proof.
∎

5.4.
Substitution rules

Here we study some integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{X_{\mathbf{d}}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})Q(\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{1}},\dots\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{n}})

for specific choices of a polynomial
Q
in
n
variables. In this expression, the vectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are possibly not distinct and
P
is a polynomial in
m
variables. Our aim in this section is to find a class
R_{Q}(\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{n},x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
, involving the vectors
\mathbf{w}_{i}
and the
x
-classes, so that the integral above is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{X_{\mathbf{d}}}P(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R_{Q}(\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{n},x_{1},\dots,x_{m}).

Notice that we don’t aim at an equality at the level of cohomology classes; we just look for classes that integrate to the same number.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be two cohomology classes on
X_{\mathbf{d}}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{X_{\mathbf{d}}}\alpha_{1}\wedge P(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\int_{X_{\mathbf{d}}}\alpha_{2}\wedge P(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

for every polynomial
P
in the
x
-classes. We denote this equivalence relation by

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}\rightsquigarrow\alpha_{2}

and call it a
substitution rule
.

We state here the first substitution rule, which is just the result of a rather lengthy linear algebra computation:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and consider
n
vectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, from which we construct the cohomology-valued
n\times n
matrix
M
whose entries are

\displaystyle M_{a}^{b}\coloneqq\sum_{c=1}^{m}w_{a}^{c}w_{b}^{c}x_{c}.

Then

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}\rightsquigarrow\sum_{A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{g}=[n]}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det(M_{A_{j},A_{j}})

is a substitution rule, where the sum is over all ordered partitions of
[n]
in
g
(possibly empty) subsets, and
M_{A_{i},A_{i}}
denotes the submatrix of
M
obtained by only considering the rows and the columns indexed by elements of
A_{i}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.4
we can express the product of theta classes in terms of the
y
-classes. By considering sums over
n
-tuples as sums over functions from
[n]
we can write

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}=\sum_{i:[n]\rightarrow[g]}\sum_{j:[n]\rightarrow[m]}\sum_{k:[n]\rightarrow[m]}\Sigma_{i,j,k},

where the summand
\Sigma_{i,j,k}
is

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i,j,k}\coloneqq\prod_{h=1}^{n}\left(w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{h}^{k(h)}\right)y_{j(h)}^{i(h)}\wedge y_{k(h)}^{i(h)+g}.

By Lemma
5.1
we know which of these summands will have a chance
4
4

4

meaning that it can give a non-zero contribution depending on the shape of
P
.
at contributing to the integral. More precisely we know that the integral of
P(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\Sigma_{i,j,k}
vanishes unless there is a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
such that

(1)

i(h)=i(\sigma(h))
for every
h\in[n]
, and

(2)

k(h)=j(\sigma(h))
for every
h\in[n]
.

If such a
\sigma
exists, then the corresponding summand is

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i,j,k}\coloneqq\Sigma_{i,j,j\circ\sigma}=\prod_{h=1}^{n}\left(w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{h}^{j(\sigma(h))}\right)y_{j(h)}^{i(h)}\wedge y_{j(\sigma(h))}^{i(\sigma(h))+g}.

By a simple reordering of the variables, we see that

\displaystyle\prod_{h=1}^{n}y_{j(h)}^{i(h)}\wedge y_{j(\sigma(h))}^{i(\sigma(h))+g}=(-1)^{\sigma}\prod_{h=1}^{n}y_{j(h)}^{i(h)}\wedge y_{j(h)}^{i(h)+g}.

Therefore, we can rewrite the summand
\Sigma_{i,j,\sigma}
as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i,j,\sigma}=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{h=1}^{n}\left(w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{h}^{j(\sigma(h))}\right)y_{j(h)}^{i(h)}\wedge y_{j(h)}^{i(h)+g}.

By Lemma
5.1
we find the substitution rule for the summand
\Sigma_{i,j,\sigma}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i,j,\sigma}\rightsquigarrow\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{h=1}^{n}w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{h}^{j(\sigma(h))}x_{j(h)}.

Putting all together, we obtain that

(53)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{w_{i}}\rightsquigarrow\sum_{i:[n]\rightarrow[g]}\sum_{j:[n]\rightarrow[m]}\sum_{\sigma\in(S_{n})_{i}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{h=1}^{n}w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{h}^{j(\sigma(h))}x_{j(h)},

where
(S_{n})_{i}
is the stabilizer of
i
for the action of
S_{n}
on
\text{Hom}([n],[m])
, or in other words is the set of
\sigma
satisfying condition
1
above. Let’s simplify this expression. Keeping
i
and
\sigma
fixed, consider the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{j:[n]\rightarrow[m]}\prod_{h=1}^{n}w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{h}^{j(\sigma(h))}x_{j(h)}=\sum_{j:[n]\rightarrow[m]}\prod_{h=1}^{n}w_{h}^{j(h)}w_{\sigma^{-1}(h)}^{j(h)}x_{j(h)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{n}=1}^{m}\prod_{h=1}^{n}w_{h}^{j_{h}}w_{\sigma^{-1}(h)}^{j_{h}}x_{j_{h}}=\prod_{h=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{h}^{j}w_{\sigma^{-1}(h)}^{j}x_{j}=\prod_{h=1}^{n}M_{h}^{\sigma^{-1}(h)}.

This means that the substitution rule (
53
) has right-hand side equal to

(54)

\displaystyle\sum_{i:[n]\rightarrow[g]}\sum_{\sigma\in(S_{n})_{i}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{h=1}^{n}M_{h}^{\sigma^{-1}(h)}.

Now notice that a function
i:[n]\rightarrow[g]
corresponds to an ordered partition
A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{g}=[n]
via
A_{l}\coloneqq i^{-1}(l)
, and the group
(S_{n})_{i}
corresponds to the product
\prod_{l=1}^{g}S_{A_{l}}
of automorphisms groups of the subsets forming the partition. Thus, we find that (
54
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{g}=[n]}\sum_{\sigma\in\prod_{l=1}^{g}S_{A_{l}}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{h=1}^{n}M_{h}^{\sigma^{-1}(h)}=\sum_{A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{g}=[n]}\prod_{l=1}^{g}\sum_{\sigma_{l}\in S_{A_{l}}}(-1)^{\sigma_{l}}\prod_{h\in A_{l}}M_{h}^{\sigma_{l}^{-1}(h)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{A_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup A_{g}=[n]}\prod_{l=1}^{g}\det(M_{A_{j},A_{j}}),

where the last equality is the usual expansion of the determinant.
∎

5.4.1.
The monomial substitution rule

In Lemma
5.8
we found a substitution rule once the monomial in the theta classes is expressed in the form
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}
, where each theta class appears with power one but the vectors
\mathbf{w}_{i}
are allowed to appear repeated multiple times.
We now find a more explicit formula for the presentation of monomials of the form
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}^{u_{i}}
, where powers are allowed:

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, consider
n
vectors
\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and a
n
-tuple of non-negative integers
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
. Then the substitution rule

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta^{u_{i}}_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}\rightsquigarrow\prod_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}!\sum_{(A_{1},\dots,A_{g})\in\mathcal{P}(u)}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det(M_{A_{j},A_{j}})

holds true, where
M
is the same matrix of Lemma
5.8
and
\mathcal{P}(u)
is the set of
g
-tuples
(A_{1},\dots,A_{g})
of subsets of
[n]
satisfying the property

(55)

\displaystyle i\text{ belongs to exactly }u_{i}\text{ sets among }A_{1},\dots,A_{g}\text{ for every }i\in[n].

Proof.

Consider the multiset
U
whose underlying set is
[n]
and where
i
is repeated
u_{i}
times. In other words, we consider the set

\displaystyle U\coloneqq\left\{1_{1},\dots,1_{u_{1}},2_{1},\dots,2_{u_{2}},\dots,n_{1},\dots,n_{u_{n}}\right\},

which is endowed with the map
f:U\rightarrow[n]
given by
f(i_{j})\coloneqq i
.
Since the monomial we are trying to substitute is

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}^{u_{i}}=\prod_{i\in U}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{f(i)}},

by Lemma
5.8
we find the substitution rule

(56)

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}^{u_{i}}\rightsquigarrow\sum_{\widetilde{A}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\widetilde{A}_{g}=U}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det(\widetilde{M}_{\widetilde{A}_{j},\widetilde{A}_{j}})

where
\widetilde{M}
is the matrix whose rows and columns are indexed by elements of
U
and whose entries are
\widetilde{M}_{i}^{j}=M_{f(i)}^{f(j)}
. This means that the right-hand side of (
56
) is

(57)

\displaystyle\sum_{\widetilde{A}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\widetilde{A}_{g}=U}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det(M_{f(\widetilde{A}_{j}),f(\widetilde{A}_{j})}).

This immediately shows that the function
f
must be injective on every
\widetilde{A}_{j}
in order for
\det(\widetilde{M}_{\widetilde{A}_{j},\widetilde{A}_{j}})
to be non-zero, hence we can restrict the sum over partitions of
U
so that each
\widetilde{A}_{i}
contains at most one copy of each element of
[n]
. Clearly the function

\displaystyle\left\{{\text{partitions }\widetilde{A}_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\widetilde{A}_{g}=U\text{ s.t. }f\text{ is injective on each }\widetilde{A}_{i}}\right\}\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal{P}(u)

has fibers of cardinality
\prod_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}!
, since given a partition of
U
one can shuffle all the copies of each element of
[n]
to produce another partition of
U
mapping to the same subsets of
[n]
. Therefore (
57
) coincides with the claimed substitution rule once we rename
A_{j}\coloneqq f(\widetilde{A}_{j})
.
∎

5.4.2.
The exponential substitution rule

In our applications, we will substitute polynomials in the theta classes that appear as truncated exponentials. For this kind of polynomials, the substitution rule simplifies:

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
be polynomials in the
x
-classes, from which we form the diagonal
n\times n
matrix

\displaystyle C\coloneqq\text{diag}\left(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\right).

Then we have the substitution rule

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(f_{i}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}})\rightsquigarrow\det(\mathbb{I}_{n}+CM)^{g}.

Proof.

By the definition of the exponential (note that
\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}^{g+1}=0
as discussed in Remark
5.3
) and Proposition
5.9
we find that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(f_{i}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}=0}^{g}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{f_{i}^{u_{i}}}{u_{i}!}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{j}}^{u_{j}}

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}=0}^{g}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{u_{i}}\right)\sum_{(A_{1},\dots,A_{g})\in\mathcal{P}(u)}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det(M_{A_{j},A_{j}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}=0}^{g}\sum_{(A_{1},\dots,A_{g})\in\mathcal{P}(u)}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det((CM)_{A_{j},A_{j}}).

Note that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}=0}^{g}\mathcal{P}(u)=\mathcal{P}([n])^{g},

where the right-hand side is the Cartesian product of
g
copies of the power set of
[n]
. Thus we have the substitution rule

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(f_{i}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}})\rightsquigarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{A_{1},\dots,A_{g}\in\mathcal{P}([n])}\prod_{j=1}^{g}\det((CM)_{A_{j},A_{j}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{A\in\mathcal{P}([n])}\det((CM)_{A,A})\right)^{g}=\det(\mathbb{I}_{n}+CM)^{g},

where the last equality is the well-known expansion of the determinant of
\mathbb{I}+Z
as the sum of the principal minors of
Z
.
∎

Finally, we can reshape this result into the most useful form for our applications:

Corollary 5.11

(Exponential substitution rule)
.

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
be polynomials in the
x
-classes, and consider the diagonal
m\times m
matrix

\displaystyle X\coloneqq\text{diag}\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\right).

The substitution rule

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(f_{i}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}})\rightsquigarrow\det(\mathbb{I}_{m}+XG(c))^{g}

holds true, where
G(c)
is the
m\times m
matrix

\displaystyle G(c)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\mathbf{w}_{i}^{T}\cdot\mathbf{w}_{i},

and the vectors
\mathbf{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
are considered as row vectors.

Proof.

Consider the
n\times m
matrix
W
with entries
W_{a}^{b}\coloneqq w_{a}^{b}
, or, in other words, the one obtained by treating the vectors
\mathbf{w}_{i}
as rows of a matrix. Then we immediately find that the matrix
M
defined in Lemma
5.8
is

\displaystyle M=WXW^{T},

hence by Proposition
5.10
we find

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(f_{i}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}})\rightsquigarrow\det(\mathbb{I}_{n}+CWXW^{T})^{g}.

By Sylvester’s determinant theorem, stating that

(58)

\displaystyle\det(\mathbb{I}_{n}+AB)=\det(\mathbb{I}_{m}+BA)

for any
n\times m
matrix
A
and
m\times n
matrix
B
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(f_{i}\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}})\rightsquigarrow\det(\mathbb{I}_{m}+XW^{T}CW)^{g}

concluding the proof.
∎

5.4.3.
The Jacobian substitution rule

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Chosen the constants of Corollary
5.11
as

f_{i}=(\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle+\varepsilon_{i})^{-1}\qquad\text{where}\qquad\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{m}w^{j}x_{j},

we can give a nice description of the substitution rule:

Corollary 5.12
.

We have the substitution rule

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp\left(\frac{\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}}{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle+\varepsilon_{i}}\right)\rightsquigarrow\prod_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}^{g}\det\left(\frac{1}{t_{j}}\frac{\partial t_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}\right)^{g},

where
t:\mathbb{C}^{m}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m}
is defined as

\displaystyle t_{j}(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq x_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle+\varepsilon_{i})^{w_{i}^{j}}\qquad\forall j\in[m].

Proof.

It’s immediate to compute that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial t_{j}}{\partial x_{k}}={t_{j}}(\mathbf{x})\left(\frac{\delta_{j,k}}{x_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w_{i}^{j}w_{i}^{k}}{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{i}\rangle+\varepsilon_{i}}\right)=\frac{t_{j}(\mathbf{x})}{x_{j}}\left(\delta_{j,k}+x_{j}G(f)_{j}^{k}\right).

where we used the notation of Corollary
5.11
.
∎

Virtual integrals in higher genus

We now turn our attention to the higher genus case. In this section, we apply virtual localization

]
to reduce the integrals in Theorem

to a sum of integrals over products of Hilbert schemes of points on

6.1.
Cohomology classes on
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{X}_{\sigma,D}}

We continue to use the notation introduced in Sections
4

5
. In particular recall that, given
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{r}}
and a splitting degree

6.1.1.
Chern classes

Whenever
\mathcal{L}
is a vector bundle on a product scheme
X\times C
and

is a point, we will denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\mid p}
the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to

.
Let
\mathcal{L}_{s,j}
be the universal ideal subsheaf over the product
C^{[d_{s,j}-d_{s,j+1}]}\times C
. We will denote by the same letter its pullback to
X_{\sigma,D}\times C
, by a slight abuse of notation. Note that

(59)

\mathcal{L}_{s,j}^{\vee}=\mathcal{K}_{s,j}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{s,j+1},

where
\mathcal{K}_{s,j}
is the
s

\mathcal{E}_{j}|_{X_{\sigma,D}\times C}
as in (
23
).
For
\sigma=1\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{r}}
we define the classes

x_{s,j}\coloneqq c_{1}\left({\mathcal{L}_{s,j|p}^{\vee}}\right)\in H^{2}(X_{1,D})\quad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq k\text{ and }\ 1\leq s\leq r_{j}.

From (

z_{s,j}\coloneqq c_{1}^{T}\left(\mathcal{K}_{s,j\mid p}^{\vee}\right)=x_{s,j}+x_{s,{j+1}}+\cdots+x_{s,k}+\varepsilon_{s}\in H^{2}(X_{1,D}),

and to be consistent we define
z_{s,k+1}:=c_{1}^{T}\left(\pi_{C}^{*}M_{s\mid p}^{\vee}\right)=\varepsilon_{s}

For an arbitrary element
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\mathbf{r}}
we immediately find that
c_{1}^{T}\left(\mathcal{K}_{s,j\mid p}^{\vee}\right)=\sigma\cdot z_{s,j}
in

6.1.2.
Theta classes

Let

Remark 6.1
.

We have

and

Proof.

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

By applying the substitution rules of Section
5.4
, we can simplify the integrals involving the factor
J(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})
:

Lemma 6.4
.

For any splitting data
D
and polynomial
H(\bf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})\in\Gamma[\mathbf{x}]
, we have

(63)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1,D}}\ H(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})J(\bf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{X}_{1,D}}H(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})J(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})^{g},

where

J(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})=\prod_{j\leq k}\prod_{s\leq r_{j}}\frac{R_{{j-1}}(z_{s,j})}{R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})}\cdot\det\left(\frac{\partial q_{s,j}}{\partial x_{u,m}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad q_{s,j}\coloneqq(-1)^{r_{j}-r_{j-1}-1}\frac{R_{{j+1}}(z_{s,j})}{R_{{j-1}}(z_{s,j})}.

Proof.

Define
\varepsilon_{su}:=\varepsilon_{u}-\varepsilon_{s}
and the auxiliary vectors
\mathbf{w}_{suj}\coloneqq\mathbf{w}_{s,j}-\mathbf{w}_{u,j+1}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{r_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{r_{k}}
. Then

\frac{\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{s,j}-\mathbf{w}_{u,j+1}}}{z_{s,j}-z_{u,{j+1}}}=\frac{\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{suj}}}{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{suj}\rangle+\varepsilon_{su}}

and the left-hand side of (
63
) can be written as

(64)

\int_{X_{1,D}}\left(H(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})\prod_{j\leq k}\prod_{s\leq r_{j}}\left\lparen\frac{R_{j+1}(z_{s,j})}{x_{s,j}R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})}\right\rparen^{g}\right)\left(\prod_{j\leq k}\prod_{s\leq r_{j}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq r_{j+1}\\
u\neq s\end{subarray}}\exp\left\lparen\frac{\theta_{\mathbf{w}_{suj}}}{\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{suj}\rangle+\varepsilon_{su}}\right\rparen\right).

We can now apply Corollary
5.12
to convert the
\theta
-classes in this integral into expressions in the
x
-classes. By setting

\displaystyle t_{c,i}(\textbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\coloneqq x_{c,i}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s=1}^{r_{j}}\prod^{r_{j+1}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}u=1\\
u\neq s\end{subarray}}\left(\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}_{suj}\rangle+\varepsilon_{su}\right)^{w_{suj}^{c,i}}\quad\text{for }c\leq r_{i},\ i\leq k

and applying Corollary
5.12
we see that (
64
) coincides with

\int_{X_{1,D}}H(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})\prod_{j=1}^{k}\prod_{s\leq r_{j}}\left(\frac{R_{j+1}(z_{s,j})}{x_{s,j}R_{j}^{\prime}(z_{s,j})}\right)^{g}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{c\leq r_{j}}x_{c,i}^{g}\det\left(\frac{1}{t_{c,i}(\mathbf{x,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})}\frac{\partial t_{c,i}(\mathbf{x,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}})}{\partial x_{c^{\prime},i^{\prime}}}\right)^{g}.

Note that

\displaystyle t_{c,i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})=x_{c,i}\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq i\\
c\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\leq r_{j+1}\\
u\neq c\end{subarray}}\left\lparen z_{c,j}-z_{u,j+1}\right\rparen}{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq i-1\\
c\leq r_{j+1}\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq r_{j}\\
s\neq c\end{subarray}}\left\lparen z_{s,j}-z_{c,j+1}\right\rparen}=(-1)^{\alpha(c,i)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq i\\
c\leq r_{j}\end{subarray}}q_{c,j}

for some sign

. This immediately implies that

\det\left(\frac{1}{t_{c,i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})}\frac{\partial t_{c,i}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon})}{\partial q_{c^{\prime},i^{\prime}}}\right)=\prod_{c\leq r_{i},\ i\leq k}\frac{1}{q_{c,i}},

and the lemma follows from the chain rule.
∎

The procedure of summing the contributions of the fixed loci mirrors the genus–zero analysis in Section

. We briefly recall that setup and then complete the proof of Theorem

, which is the formal version of our main result. Theorems

and

follow by the discussion in Section

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

Let

Definition 7.1

7.1.
Explicit formulas for Hyperquot schemes of points on
C

Fix a positive integer
k
and vector bundle
V
of rank
n
on a genus
g
curve
C
. For a tuple of non-negative integers

Corollary 7.2

For any tuple

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{g,(n)^{k},n,e}^{\mathbf{m}}(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{k})=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}^{m_{n,j}}&\text{if }m_{i,j}=0\quad\forall i<n,1\leq j\leq k\\

where

(66)

\alpha_{j}\coloneqq q_{1}q_{2}\cdots q_{j}(1+q_{j+1}+q_{j+1}q_{j+2}+\cdots+q_{j+1}q_{j+2}\cdots q_{k})\quad\text{ for }1\leq j\leq k.

g
of

and degree

Proof.

Let us first fix
e=0
and a generic
(q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{k})\in(\mathbb{C}^{\ast})^{k}
. Consider the system of variables
\mathbf{z}_{j}=(z_{1,j},\dots,z_{n,j})
for

, and set by definition
\mathbf{z}_{0}:=\{\}
and

for all

(67)

In order to apply Theorem
1.2
, we need to solve the system of equations

\displaystyle P_{j}(z_{i,j})=0\qquad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq k\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq i\leq n

defined in (
2
). By Proposition
4.8
, there is a unique non-degenerate solution

to this system of equations up to permuting the entries in each

by elements of

. In particular, the non-degenerate solutions form a free orbit under the action of

.

Fixed one such solution

, since the expression on the right-hand side of the formula in Theorem

is invariant under the action of

, we find that

In the special case where

since the Hyperquot scheme of points on

is zero-dimensional exactly when

Lemma 7.5
.

Proof of Corollary

.

Applying Theorem
1.2
, and using

and

(in particular, the identities in (

)), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{g,\mathbf{r},n,0}^{\ell,\mathbf{m}}(q_{1},q_{2})

\displaystyle=n^{\overline{g}}(n-1)^{\overline{g}}\sum_{w}\left(\sum_{\zeta}\zeta^{\ell}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\left(\frac{\zeta}{w}\right)^{im_{i}}\cdot(q_{2}w)^{m_{n-1}}\cdot(q_{2}w\zeta^{n-2})^{\overline{g}}\right)

\displaystyle=n^{\overline{g}}(n-1)^{\overline{g}}q_{2}^{m_{n-1}+\overline{g}}\sum_{w}w^{m_{n-1}+\overline{g}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}im_{i}}\left(\sum_{\zeta}\zeta^{\ell+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}im_{i}+(n-2)\overline{g}}\right).

7.3.
Counting maximal subsheaves

If
V
is a general stable bundle
V
of degree
e
and
\mathrm{vdim}\quot_{d}(C,r,V)=0
, the Quot scheme
\quot_{d}(C,r,V)
is a smooth zero-dimensional scheme consisting of all the rank-
r
subbundles of
V
having maximal slope
[
27
]
. Let

be the number of such subbundles, which is explicitly computed by the Vafa-Intriligator formula for Quot schemes. Recall the iterative description of the Hyperquot scheme

using relative Quot schemes

\quot_{d_{1}-d_{2}}(C,r_{1},\mathcal{E}_{2})\xrightarrow{\phi_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\phi_{k-2}}\quot_{d_{k-1}-d_{k}}(C,r_{k-1},\mathcal{E}_{k})\xrightarrow{\phi_{k-1}}\quot_{d_{k}}(C,r_{k},V)\xrightarrow{\phi_{k}}\{\mathrm{pt}\}.

Corollary 7.6
.

Let
V
be a vector bundle on
C
of rank
n
and degree
e
, and assume that
g\geq 2
.
Fix
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
and
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\dots,d_{k})
so that
\mathrm{vdim}\phi_{k}=0
. For any rank-
r_{k}
vector bundle

of degree

, we have

Proof.

The formula of Theorem
1.2
shows that, for fixed monomial in the Chern classes, the virtual integral on the Hyperquot scheme depends only on

and

. If

, hence we can assume that

\displaystyle(\phi_{k-2}\circ\cdots\circ\phi_{1})_{\ast}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r_{j}}c_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{j|p}^{\vee})^{m_{i,j}}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}[V_{j}]\int_{[\Hquot_{\mathbf{d}^{\prime}}(C,\mathbf{r}^{\prime},V_{j})]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r_{j}}c_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{j|p}^{\vee})^{m_{i,j}}.

∎

With random regressors, least squares inference is robust to correlated errors with unknown correlation structure
Zifeng Zhang ♯, Peng Ding §, Wen Zhou *, Haonan Wang ♯

Abstract
Linear regression is arguably the most widely used statistical method. With fixed regressors and correlated errors, the conventional wisdom is to modify the variance-covariance estimator to accommodate the known correlation structure of the errors. We depart from the literature by showing that with random regressors, linear regression inference is robust to correlated errors with unknown correlation structure. The existing theoretical analyses for linear regression are no longer valid because even the asymptotic normality of the least-squares coefficients breaks down in this regime. We first prove the asymptotic normality of the t t statistics by establishing their Berry–Esseen bounds based on a novel probabilistic analysis of self-normalized statistics. We then study the local power of the corresponding t t tests and show that, perhaps surprisingly, error correlation can even enhance power in the regime of weak signals. Overall, our results show that linear regression is applicable more broadly than the conventional theory suggests, and further demonstrate the value of randomization to ensure robustness of inference. Keywords: Asymptotic normality; Linear regression; Random design; Randomization.



1
Linear regression: fixed design, random design, and error distribution

1.1
Literature review and our perspective

Linear regression is widely used in many disciplines and has attracted continued interest in statistics research; see
Lei and
Bickel (2021, Appendix A)
for a recent review. The classic linear model
y=X\beta+\bm{\varepsilon}
assumes that the
n\times d
covariate matrix
X
is fixed and the
n
-dimensional error vector
\bm{\varepsilon}
has independent and identically distributed normal components with mean
0
and variance
\sigma^{2}
.
Under this model, (a) the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator
\widehat{\beta}=(X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}X)^{-1}X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}y
is normal with mean
\beta
and covariance
\mathrm{cov}(\widehat{\beta})=\sigma^{2}(X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}X)^{-1}
, (b)
\widehat{\sigma}^{2}=y^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}(I-P_{X})y/(n-d)
is unbiased for
\sigma^{2}
,
with
\widehat{\sigma}^{2}/\sigma^{2}\sim\chi^{2}_{n-d}/(n-d)
,
where
P_{X}=X(X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}X)^{-1}X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
is the projection matrix onto the column space of
X
,
and (c)
\widehat{\beta}
and
\widehat{\sigma}^{2}
are independent.
The results (a)–(c) justify the statistical inference based on the pivotal quantity
T_{j}=L_{j}^{-1}(\widehat{\beta}_{j}-\beta_{j})\sim t_{n-d}
, where
\beta_{j}
and
\widehat{\beta}_{j}
are the
j
th coordinate of
\beta
and
\widehat{\beta}
, respectively, and
L_{j}=\widehat{\sigma}\{e_{j}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}(X^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}X)^{-1}e_{j}\}^{1/2}
is the standard error of
\widehat{\beta}_{j}
with
e_{j}
being the
j
th basis vector in the
d
-dimensional space.

There is a large literature on relaxing the assumption of independent and identically distributed normal errors.
First, we can relax the normality assumption but can still show
T_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)
in distribution by the law of large numbers and central limit theorem. The change from the
t
quantiles to normal quantiles is small when
n
is large compared with
d
.
Second, we can relax the homoskedasticity assumption on the errors. With heteroskedastic errors,
Eicker (1967)
and
White (1980)
proposed a heteroskedasticity-robust covariance estimator.
Third, we can further allow for dependence among the errors. With clustered errors,
Liang and
Zeger (1986)
proposed to use the cluster-robust covariance estimator. With time series errors,
Newey and
West (1987)
proposed to use the autocorrelation-robust covariance estimator. With spatial or network correlated errors, we can construct the corresponding robust covariance estimators.

As reviewed above, the literature focuses on modifying the standard error in constructing the
t
statistic, under various known correlation structures of the errors. Departing from the literature,
we study the robustness of the original OLS inference procedure with respect to correlated errors with unknown correlation structure.
We do not modify the original definition of the
t
statistic but show that
T_{j}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,1)
in distribution still holds under the assumption of random regressors even if the errors have an unknown correlation structure.
With correlated errors, the asymptotic normality of
\widehat{\beta}_{j}
breaks down in general. However, the central limit theorem for the
t
-statistic
T_{j}
can still hold in that regime. Intuitively,
T_{j}
has a ratio form and the correlation effect of the error
\bm{\varepsilon}
cancels out because it appears in both the numerator and the denominator. To make this argument rigorous, we will first show that the key stochastic component in
T_{j}
is approximately
X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\varepsilon}/\|\bm{\varepsilon}\|
and then show the self-normalized error
\bm{\varepsilon}/\|\bm{\varepsilon}\|
is nearly uniformly distributed over the unit sphere as long as the correlation is not extremely strong. Due to these two facts, the randomness of
T_{j}
is approximately driven by the sample mean of the rows of
X
, which follows the central limit theorem with random regressors. See Section
2
for more details.

In short, our theory demonstrates that OLS inference is valid even with correlated errors, as long as the regressors are random. With fixed regressors, the correlated errors will invalidate OLS inference in general. Therefore, with fixed regressors or conditional on random regressors, the
p
-values from OLS can be non-uniform under the null hypotheses due to correlated errors. However, averaged over the randomness of the regressors, the
p
-values become uniform under the null hypotheses even if the errors are correlated in unknown ways. Overall, our theory shows that OLS inference is applicable more broadly than the classic theory suggests.

Importantly, the regime of random regressors arises naturally from randomized experiments, in which the experimenter has control over the distribution of the treatment. Therefore, our theory further demonstrates the value of randomization to ensure robustness of inference.
Our setting with random regressors is reminiscent of the framework of randomization-based inference.
In that literature, the focus was the robustness of inference with misspecified models
(
Lin, 2013
)
.
In contrast, we focus on the robustness of inference with correlated errors.

2
Validity of OLS inference with correlated errors

The key theoretical result to ensure the robustness of the classic OLS inference is the asymptotic normality of the
t
statistic.
Theorem
2.1
below gives the Berry–Esseen bound on
T_{j}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Berry–Esseen bound on
T_{j}
)
.

Under Assumption
1.1
,
we have

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathrm{pr}(T_{j}\leq t)-\Phi(t)\big|\lesssim\lambda_{\min}^{-3/2}(V)\cdot\max(d,\log n)\cdot n^{-1/2}.

(2.1)

If
\lambda_{\min}(V)\geq c_{\mathrm{min}}>0
for an absolute constant
c_{\mathrm{min}}
, the bound in (
2.1
) converges to 0 as long as
d/n^{1/2}\rightarrow 0
, which is required by Assumption
1.1
and matches the condition invoked by
Bickel and
Freedman (1982)
to prove the asymptotic normality of the least squares coefficient with fixed regressors.
Even if the errors are strongly correlated with
\lambda_{\min}(V)\to 0
, the bound in (
2.1
) is still useful for establishing the central limit theorem of
T_{j}
as long as the bound converges to 0.

Although Theorem
2.1
looks similar to the classic Berry–Esseen bound with fixed regressors and independent errors, the mathematical details differ fundamentally.
In particular, if we standardize the OLS coefficient by its true standard error,

does not satisfy the central limit theorem if the errors have a general correlation structure.
Only when we standardize the OLS coefficient by its estimated standard error,
T_{j}=\{\widehat{\sigma}^{2}e_{j}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}(X^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}X)\\
^{-1}e_{j}\}^{-1/2}(\widehat{\beta}_{j}-\beta_{j})
satisfies the central limit theorem.

3
Power analysis under a local alternative hypothesis

Based on the asymptotic normality in Theorem
2.1
, the one-sided test for the null hypothesis of
H_{0}:\beta_{j}=0
is
\mathcal{I}(L_{j}^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_{j}>z_{\alpha})
. We will further study the local power of this test under the alternative hypothesis of

H_{1}:\beta_{j}=h\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}e_{j}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}\Sigma^{-1}e_{j}}{n-d}\right)^{1/2}\text{ with }h>0.

(3.1)

The choice of the alternative hypothesis

in (
3.1

to simplify the form of the asymptotic power function, which will be clear in (
3
) below.

Theorem 3.1

(power)
.

Under Assumption
1.1
and
H_{1}
in (
3.1
),
we have

\big|\mathrm{pr}(L_{j}^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_{j}>z_{\alpha})-\pi(h,V)\big|\lesssim\lambda_{\min}^{-3/2}(V)\cdot\max(d,\log n)\cdot(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot n^{-1/2},

where the asymptotic power function equals

\pi(h,V)=E\{\Phi(h\delta^{-1/2}-z_{\alpha})\},

with the expectation taken over
\delta={\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{\varepsilon}/(n\sigma^{2})=w^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}Vw/n

In the classic regime with independent errors,

In the regime with strongly correlated errors,

in the Appendix.
Therefore, the asymptotic power function has the form of

We provide some heuristics for the asymptotic power function. The statistic
L_{j}^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_{j}
decomposes as
L_{j}^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_{j}=L_{j}^{-1}(\widehat{\beta}_{j}-\beta_{j})+L_{j}^{-1}\beta_{j}

\displaystyle L_{j}^{-1}\beta_{j}=h\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}e_{j}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}\Sigma^{-1}e_{j}}{n-d}\right)^{1/2}\Big/\left\{\frac{\widehat{\sigma}^{2}e_{j}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}(n^{-1}X^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}X)^{-1}e_{j}}{n}\right\}^{1/2}\approx h\delta^{-1/2},

because

and
\widehat{\sigma}^{2}={\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}(I-P_{X}){\varepsilon}/(n-d)\approx{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}{\varepsilon}/(n-d)

\mathrm{pr}({L_{j}}^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_{j}>z_{\alpha})=\mathrm{pr}(T_{j}>z_{\alpha}-{L_{j}}^{-1}\beta_{j})\approx\Phi(h\delta^{-1/2}-z_{\alpha}),

(3.3)

with random
\delta
.
Therefore, the final asymptotic power function needs to take expectation over

, as stated in Theorem

.

Although

Compared with the power function for independent errors,

4
Fixed, random, or mixed regressors

With random regressors, we have demonstrated the robustness of OLS inference with respect to correlated errors. With fixed regressors, the theory breaks down. We can construct a counterexample. For instance, if
y=\beta 1_{n}+{\varepsilon}
where
{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,V_{1,\rho})
, then
\widehat{\beta}-\beta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\rho+n^{-1}(1-\rho))
is bounded in probability, and
L_{1}\approx\{n^{-2}{\varepsilon}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\varepsilon}\}^{1/2}
converges to
0
in probability. Therefore, wrongly assuming asymptotic normality of
L_{1}^{-1}(\widehat{\beta}-\beta)
does not give valid inference.

When the regressors contain both fixed and random components, the OLS inference for the coefficients of the fixed components is not valid whereas that of the random components is still valid asymptotically.
Consider model
y=X\beta+\bm{\varepsilon}
with
n=100
,
\beta=[1,1,1]^{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}
,
X=[X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}]\in\mathbb{R}^{100\times 3}
where
X_{1}=1_{n}
is the intercept.
Both
X_{2}
and
X_{3}
have independent and identically distributed Rademacher entries.
Yet
X_{2}
is fixed across replications and
X_{3}
is regenerated for each replication.
The errors satisfy
\bm{\varepsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,V)
, where
V_{ij}=\rho^{|i-j|}
with
\rho
varying from
-0.9
to
0.9
.

n^{-1/2}\lambda_{i}^{1/2}({\mathbf{V}})\to\alpha_{i}^{1/2}
for
i=1,\dots,K
, Slutsky’s theorem, and the definition of
{\mathbf{S}}_{n}
in (
A.10
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{diag}\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{1}({\mathbf{V}})}{n}},\dots,\sqrt{{\frac{\lambda_{K}({\mathbf{V}})}{n}}}\right\}{\mathbf{S}}_{n}\overset{L}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{diag}\left\{\sqrt{\alpha_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{\alpha_{K}}\right\}\left[Z_{1}\ \dots\ Z_{K}\right]^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}.

By the continuous mapping theorem and (
A.11
), we have

n^{-1}{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{V}}_{K}{\mathbf{w}}}\overset{L}{\longrightarrow}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\alpha_{i}Z_{i}^{2}
.
Finally using Slutsky’s theorem again, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{V}}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}=\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{V}}_{K}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}+\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{V}}_{-K}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}\overset{L}{\longrightarrow}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\alpha_{i}Z_{i}^{2}+1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\alpha_{i}.

A.3.1
Lemmas for the Asymptotic Theory

Lemma A.3
.

(Lyapounov Central Limit Theorem, (
Lehmann and
Romano (2005)
Corollary 11.2.1)).
Suppose for each
n
,
\xi_{n,1},\dots,\xi_{n,r_{n}}
are independent with
\mathbb{E}(\xi_{n,i})=0
and
\sigma_{n,i}^{2}=\mathbb{E}(\xi_{n,i}^{2})<\infty
.
Let
s_{n}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\sigma_{n,i}^{2}
.
Assume that for some
\eta>0
, it holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}\frac{1}{s_{n}^{2+\eta}}\mathbb{E}(|\xi_{n,i}^{2+\eta}|)<\infty.

Then
\sum_{i=1}^{r_{n}}X_{n,i}/s_{n}\overset{L}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

Lemma A.4
.

(Cramér–Wold Theorem, (
Billingsley (1995)
, Theorem 29.4))
For random vectors
\{\bm{\xi}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{K}
and
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
, a necessary and sufficient condition for
\bm{\xi}_{n}\overset{L}{\longrightarrow}\bm{\xi}
is that
{\mathbf{b}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\xi}_{n}\overset{L}{\longrightarrow}{\mathbf{b}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\xi}
for all
{\mathbf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
.

A.3.2
Lemmas about Concentration Inequalities

Lemma A.5
.

(Sums of independent sub-Gaussian,
Vershynin (2018, Proposition 2.6.1)
)

Let
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
be independent, mean zero, sub-Gaussian random variables.
Then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}
is also a sub-Gaussian random variable, and

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\right\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\xi_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}^{2},

where
C
is an absolute constant.

Lemma A.6
.

(Bernstein’s inequality,
Vershynin (2018, Theorem 2.8.1)
)

Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N}
be independent, mean
zero, sub-exponential random variables. Then, for every
t\geq 0
, we have,

\mathbb{P}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}\geq t\right\}\leq\exp\left[-c\min\left(\frac{t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\xi_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i}\|\xi_{i}\|_{\psi_{1}}}\right)\right],

(A.15)

where
c
is an absolute constant.
The bound for
\mathbb{P}\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi_{i}\leq-t\}
is the same as (
A.15
).

Lemma A.7
.

(Hanson–Wright inequality,
Vershynin (2018, Theorem 6.2.1)
)

Let
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a random vector with independent, mean zero, sub-Gaussian coordinates. Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a fixed matrix. Then, for every
t\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left\{\left|\bm{\xi}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{A}}\bm{\xi}-\mathbb{E}\left(\bm{\xi}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{A}}\bm{\xi}\right)\right|\geq t\right\}\leq 2\exp\left[-c\min\left(\frac{t^{2}}{\max_{i}^{4}\|\xi\|_{\psi_{2}}\|{\mathbf{A}}\|_{\textup{F}}^{2}},\frac{t}{\max_{i}^{2}\|\xi\|_{\psi_{2}}\|{\mathbf{A}}\|}\right)\right].

Lemma A.8
.

(
Vershynin (2010, Theorem 5.39)
)

Let
{\mathbf{Z}}=[{\mathbf{z}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{z}}_{n}]^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}
be an
n\times d
matrix whose rows

are independent sub-Gaussian vectors such that
\mathbb{E}({\mathbf{z}}_{i}{\mathbf{z}}_{i}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}})={\mathbf{I}}_{d}
.
Then for every

, with probability at least

, we have

\sqrt{n}-\widetilde{C}\sqrt{d}-\alpha\leq s_{\min}({\mathbf{Z}})\leq s_{\max}({\mathbf{Z}})\leq\sqrt{n}+\widetilde{C}\sqrt{d}+\alpha,

where
\widetilde{C},\ \widetilde{c}_{1}>0
depend on
\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}
and
\|{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}=\sup_{{\mathbf{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|{\mathbf{t}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}
.
Additionally, if
{\mathbf{Z}}
has independent entries
z_{ij}
for
i=1,\dots,n
and
j=1,\dots,d
, then
\widetilde{C},\ \widetilde{c}_{1}>0
depend on
K_{z}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq d}\|z_{ij}\|_{\psi_{2}}
.

\pi(h,\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{K},r_{1},\dots,r_{K})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\pi(h,{\mathbf{V}})-\pi(h,\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{K},r_{1},\dots,r_{K})\right|

(C.38)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n}}-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}-z_{\alpha}\right)\right|

\displaystyle{=}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}\right\}}\left|\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n}}-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}-z_{\alpha}\right)\right|+I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}\right\}+I_{1},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}=\left\{\left|\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}^{\prime}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\right|>\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\right\},

and

\displaystyle I_{1}=\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\right\}}\left|\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n}}-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}-z_{\alpha}\right)\right|.

Next, we will bound
I_{1}
given different ranges of
h
.

Case 1.

If
h>\left(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{\log n}\right)\sqrt{5/2+\log n/(2\widetilde{c}_{6})}
, where
\widetilde{c}_{6}
is from Lemma
A.15
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\right\}}\left|\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n}}-z_{\alpha}\right)-1+1-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}-z_{\alpha}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\right\}}\left|\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n}}\right)-\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{2}+I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{2}=\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\right\}}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n}}\right),\ I_{3}=\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\right\}}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}\right).

To bound
I_{2}
, note that on the event

\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}^{\prime}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}\leq\frac{3}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\leq\frac{3}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}=\frac{3}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

(C.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}>\frac{\log n}{2\widetilde{c}_{6}}\right\}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}\leq\frac{\log n}{2\widetilde{c}_{6}}\right\}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

By Lemma
A.17
, the first term in (
C.39
)
is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}>\frac{\log n}{2\widetilde{c}_{6}}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}.

The second term in (
C.39
) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}\leq\frac{\log n}{2\widetilde{c}_{6}}\right\}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

(C.40)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}+\frac{\log n}{2\widetilde{c}_{6}}+\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi\left(z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{2\widetilde{c}_{6}}+\frac{5}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}},

(C.41)

where (
C.40
) follows from
\sum_{k=1}^{K}n_{k}\rho_{k}/n\leq 1
, (
C.41
) follows from
h>\left(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{\log n}\right)\sqrt{5/2+\log n/(2\widetilde{c}_{6})}
and
\Phi(-\sqrt{\log n})=1-\Phi(\sqrt{\log n})\leq(2\pi n)^{-1/2}
by Lemma
A.11
.
Combining the bounds of the first and the second term, we have
I_{2}\leq n^{-1/2}+(2\pi n)^{-1/2}
.

By similar arguments with Lemma
A.17
, we have

I_{3}\leq n^{-1/2}+(2\pi n)^{-1/2}
which implies that if
h>\left(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{\log n}\right)\sqrt{5/2+\log n/(2\widetilde{c}_{6})}
, we have
I_{1}\lesssim 1/\sqrt{n}
in (
C.38
).

Case 2.

If
0\leq h\leq\left(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{\log n}\right)\sqrt{5/2+\log n/(2\widetilde{c}_{6})}
, we can bound
I_{1}
in (
C.38
) with the mean value theorem:

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\}}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{I}_{\{\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}\}}\frac{\left|\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}-\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}\right|}{\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}.

On the event
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{c}
, the denominator
{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n\geq{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}^{\prime}{\mathbf{w}}/n\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})
, and
\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left(\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right)\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})
which implies that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}\mathbb{E}\left|\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}-\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}\mathbb{E}\frac{\left|\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\right|}{\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}+\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\left[\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+1-\rho_{k}\right]}}.

Since
{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}/n=\sum_{k=1}^{K}(n_{k}/n)\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}+{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}^{\prime}{\mathbf{w}}/n
, we split the expression above into two parts,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{n_{k}}{n}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}\rho_{k}w_{k}^{2}\right|}{\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}+\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}r_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}}\right]

(C.42)

\displaystyle+\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\left|\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}^{\prime}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\right|}{\sqrt{\frac{{\mathbf{w}}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{w}}}{n}}+\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho_{k}r_{k}w_{k}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}}\right]

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}\left[\sqrt{\frac{K}{n\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq K}r_{k}\right)}}+\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}\widetilde{c}_{6}}\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})}}{\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq K}r_{k}\right)\sqrt{n}}\right]

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{hK}{\sqrt{\pi}c_{\mathrm{min}}\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq K}r_{k}\right)\sqrt{n}}+\frac{20h}{\sqrt{3\pi}\widetilde{c}_{6}\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{min}}}\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq K}r_{k}\right)\sqrt{n}}

(C.43)

\displaystyle{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}},

(C.44)

where (
C.42
) follows from Lemma
A.16
, (
C.43
) follows from
\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}(1-\rho_{k})\geq c_{\mathrm{min}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}=c_{\mathrm{min}}
, and (
C.44
) uses the fact that
0\leq h\leq\left(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{\log n}\right)\sqrt{5/2+\log n/(2\widetilde{c}_{6})}
.

Collecting the results from
Case 1
and
Case 2
, we conclude that for any
h\geq 0
,
I_{1}\lesssim\log n/\sqrt{n}
.
Additionally, by Lemma
A.15
,
\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{E}_{1}\right\}\leq 2K\exp\left[-\widetilde{c}_{6}\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq K}r_{k}\right)n\right]
.
Plugging the bounds of
I_{1}
and
\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{E}_{1}\}
into (
C.38
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\pi(h,{\mathbf{V}})-\pi(h,\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{K},r_{1},\dots,r_{K})\right|

\displaystyle{\lesssim}

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\widetilde{c}_{6}\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq K}r_{k}\right)n\right]+\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}},

which concludes the proof of (
C.37
) and the final bound in (
C.36
).

∎

C.3
Some Properties of
\pi(h,\rho)-\pi(h,0)
in Corollary
3.1

Recall the power approximation
\pi(h,\rho)=\mathbb{E}\{\Phi(h(\rho\chi_{1}^{2}+1-\rho)^{-1/2}-z_{\alpha})\}
as defined in Corollary
3.1
, where
\rho\in[0,1-c_{\min}]
with
c_{\min}\in(0,1)
is the correlation in
{\mathbf{V}}_{1,\rho}=\rho\mbox{\bf 1}_{n}\mbox{\bf 1}_{n}^{{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle T}}}+(1-\rho){\mathbf{I}}_{n}
, and
h\in[0,\infty)
is the signal in
\beta_{j}
.
Define the power difference between
\rho\geq 0
and
\rho=0
as

\Delta\pi(h,\rho)=\pi(h,\rho)-\pi(h,0).

(C.45)

Lemma C.5
.

For
\Delta\pi(h,\rho)
defined in (
C.45
), we have

1.

Power gain with small signal:
given
\rho\in(0,1-c_{\mathrm{min}}]
, if
h\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1-\rho}(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{z_{\alpha}^{2}+12})
, then
\Delta\pi(h,\rho)>0
.

2.

Power loss with large signal:
given
\rho\in(0,1-c_{\mathrm{min}}]
, if
h>\max(2z_{\alpha},\{z_{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)[1-(\rho t+1-\rho)^{-1/2}]\textup{d}t\}^{-1}\mathbb{P}\{\chi_{1}^{2}<1\})
, then
\Delta\pi(h,\rho)<0
, where
f(t)
is the density of
\chi_{1}^{2}
.

3.

Diminishing power difference: given
\rho\in[0,1-c_{\mathrm{min}}]
, we have
\lim_{h\to\infty}\Delta\pi(h,\rho)=0
.

Proof of Lemma
C.5
.

1.

Denote the
\chi_{1}^{2}
random variable by
T
, (
C.45
) reduces to
\Delta\pi(h,\rho)=\mathbb{E}_{T}[\Phi(h/\sqrt{\rho T+1-\rho}-z_{\alpha})-\Phi(h-z_{\alpha})]
.
The integrand has second order derivative

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\left[\Phi\left(h/\sqrt{\rho T+1-\rho}-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)\right]}{\partial T^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-h\rho^{2}\phi\left(h/\sqrt{\rho T+1-\rho}-z_{\alpha}\right)}{4(\rho T+1-\rho)^{5/2}}\left[\left(h/\sqrt{\rho T+1-\rho}\right)^{2}-z_{\alpha}\left(h/\sqrt{\rho T+1-\rho}\right)-3\right],

where
\phi(\cdot)
is the density of
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.
If
T>\rho^{-1}\{[2h/(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{z_{\alpha}^{2}+12})]^{2}-(1-\rho)\}
, the second order derivative is positive.
Hence if
[2h/(z_{\alpha}+\sqrt{z_{\alpha}^{2}+12})]^{2}-(1-\rho)\leq 0
, the second order derivative is positive on the support
T>0
.
By Jensen’s inequality,
\Phi\left(h/\sqrt{\rho T+1-\rho}-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi(h-z_{\alpha})
is strictly convex, which implies that
\Delta\pi(h,\rho)>0
.

2.

Rewrite
\Delta\pi(h,\rho)
as

\displaystyle\Delta\pi(h,\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\left[\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\rho t+1-\rho}}-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)\right]\textup{d}t

(C.46)

\displaystyle-\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)\left[\Phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\rho t+1-\rho}}-z_{\alpha}\right)\right]\textup{d}t.

For the first term in (
C.46
), if
h>z_{\alpha}
, by Lemma
A.11
, it is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\left[1-\Phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)\right]dt\leq\frac{\mathbb{P}\left\{\chi_{1}^{2}<1\right\}\phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)}{h-z_{\alpha}}.

(C.47)

For the second term in (
C.46
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)\left[\Phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)-\Phi\left(\frac{h}{\sqrt{\rho t+1-\rho}}-z_{\alpha}\right)\right]\textup{d}t

(C.48)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)\phi(h-z_{\alpha})\left(h-z_{\alpha}-\frac{h}{\sqrt{\rho t+1-\rho}}+z_{\alpha}\right)\textup{d}t

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h\phi\left(h-z_{\alpha}\right)\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)\left[1-\left(\rho t+1-\rho\right)^{-1/2}\right]\textup{d}t,

(C.49)

where (
C.48
) follows from the mean value theorem and the fact that if
h>2z_{\alpha}
and
t>1
, we have
\left|h/\sqrt{\rho t+1-\rho}-z_{\alpha}\right|\leq h-z_{\alpha}
.

Plugging (
C.47
) and (
C.49
) into (
C.46
), if
h>2z_{\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta\pi(h,\rho)\leq\phi(h-z_{\alpha})\left\{\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\chi_{1}^{2}<1\right)}{z_{\alpha}}-h\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)\left[1-\left(\rho t+1-\rho\right)^{-1/2}\right]\textup{d}t\right\},

which is negative when
h>\{z_{\alpha}\int_{1}^{\infty}f(t)[1-\left(\rho t+1-\rho\right)^{-1/2}]\textup{d}t\}^{-1}\mathbb{P}\{\chi_{1}^{2}<1\}
.

3.

By the monotone convergence theorem, for any
\rho\in[0,1-c_{\min}]
, we have
\lim_{h\to\infty}\pi(h,\rho)=\mathbb{E}\{\lim_{h\to\infty}\Phi(h(\rho\chi_{1}^{2}+1-\rho)^{-1/2}-z_{\alpha})\}=1
, which completes the proof.

∎

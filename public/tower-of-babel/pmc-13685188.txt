Ileorectal fistula after resection of perforated sigmoid tumor and Hartmann’s procedure with uterine invasion: a case report
Authors: Armin Ahmadi, Jalal Manoochehri, Khaled Rahmani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionGastrointestinal fistulas are rare but serious postoperative complications, particularly when arising between the ileum and rectum. They are typically associated with complex intra-abdominal surgeries, malignancies, or inflammatory conditions. This case report presents a rare instance of ileorectal fistula formation following resection of a perforated sigmoid tumor with uterine invasion and subsequent Hartmann's procedure.Case presentationA 40-year-old Kurdish woman with a history of diabetes mellitus presented with abdominal pain, weight loss, and diarrhea. Imaging revealed a large pelvic mass suggestive of a perforated sigmoid tumor invading the uterus. Emergency laparotomy confirmed the diagnosis, and Hartmann’s procedure was performed. Postoperative recovery included chemotherapy. Months later, the patient returned with defecation per anus despite a colostomy, suggesting a fistula. Imaging confirmed an ileorectal fistula. During reoperation, the recurrent tumor and fistulous tract between the ileum and Hartmann’s pouch were excised. Surgical repair included small bowel resection with anastomosis and ureteral reimplantation due to adjacent involvement.ConclusionThis case underscores the complexity of managing advanced colorectal malignancies with pelvic involvement. Ileorectal fistula is a rare but critical complication of Hartmann’s procedure, necessitating individualized surgical planning and vigilant postoperative follow-up to optimize outcomes.Keywords: Ileorectal, Fistula, Hartmann’s procedure, Case report

Introduction

A fistula is an abnormal connection, either natural or surgically created, between organs or between an organ and the body surface [1]. Although relatively uncommon (occurring in about 1.8% of colorectal cancer resection patients according to Tebbutt NC et al.) [2], postoperative gastrointestinal fistulas are a serious complication that can arise after any abdominal surgery involving the gastrointestinal tract (GI) tract. Causes include surgical errors like bowel injury, poor suture technique causing ischemia, vascular damage, hematoma formation, drain misuse, or entrapped bowel. Other factors include inadvertent injury to the mesenteric vessels, poor hemostasis resulting in a perisuture hematoma, inappropriate use of drains, and a bowel loop caught in a fascial suture [3]. Fistula formation can lead to a number of serious or debilitating complications, ranging from fluid and electrolyte imbalance to sepsis and even death. Postoperative fistula formation often results in prolonged hospital stays, patient disability, and enormous costs. Therefore, gastrointestinal fistulas remain an important complication after gastrointestinal surgery [4]. While fistulas connecting the colon/rectum to other GI organs are rare [5] (illustrated by Kudo K et al.'s report of an ileorectal fistula in a Crohn's patient) [6], this report details a unique, previously unreported case of ileorectal fistula following resection of a perforated sigmoid tumor with uterine invasion.

Case presentation

A 40-year-old Kurdish women, with insulin-treated diabetes mellitus and a past appendectomy, was admitted to Kowsar Teaching Hospital in northwestern Iran's Kurdistan Province on Day 0 (May 2023). Her admission complaints were abdominal pain (localized to the hypogastrium/right lower quadrant), loss of appetite, weight loss, and chronic diarrhea. She received a diagnosis of peritonitis. Approximately four hours preoperatively, an abdominal/pelvic computed tomography (CT) scan report described a complex 73 × 82x83 mm mass near the right uterus exhibiting internal hypodense areas indicative of necrotic cystic change (Fig. 1). The scan also noted a sigmoid mass, but the absence of contrast precluded precise assessment or exclusion of a fistula. Leading differentials were torsion or abscess, with ovarian neoplasm considered less probable; further magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) evaluation was advised. Given the clinical suspicion of perforation, she proceeded to the operating room on Day 1 (May 2023). Surgical exploration revealed extensive intra-abdominal pus and debris and an upper rectal tumor densely adherent to the uterus and surrounding organs. The surgical intervention involved tumor resection, creation of a Hartmann's pouch from the distal colon, formation of a proximal colostomy, and collection of uterine wall tissue samples for pathology. Postoperative medications included metronidazole, Apotel (acetaminophen intravenous), famotidine, ceftriaxone, heparin, and Vitamins A, C, and B complex. Following surgery, the patient completed 12 chemotherapy sessions. On Day X (approximately 10 months after initial surgery, April 2024), she returned to the hospital reporting abdominal pain and paradoxical defecation through the anus despite having a colostomy. An initial diagnosis of fistula was made. Due to the requirement for total parenteral nutrition (TPN), a central venous (CV) line was placed in her right jugular vein (Figs. 2 and 3). During the final surgery on May 26, 2024, for colon cancer recurrence, the colostomy site was mobilized, revealing tumor regrowth in the adjacent mesocolon. This section, including the proximal colon, was resected. Within the pelvis, the uterus, adnexa, and the intra-abdominal portion of the Hartmann's pouch (involved with residual tumor affecting the ovary and uterus) were removed. A fistula between the ileum and Hartmann's colon was identified in this area; both segments were resected. The small bowel underwent resection and primary anastomosis, while an end-to-end colorectal anastomosis (proximal colon to upper rectum) was created using a circular stapler. Intraoperatively, tumor involvement of the right ureter necessitated partial ureterectomy and ureteroneocystostomy (reimplantation into the bladder). Discussion

This report details a rare ileorectal fistula occurring after Hartmann's procedure for a perforated sigmoid tumor invading the uterus. This specific presentation poses unique challenges, with limited prior documentation. Understanding the complex surgery and patient's condition is vital for interpreting outcomes. The case highlights potential morbidity from Hartmann's procedure, despite its life-saving role. Fistula development likely stemmed from multiple factors, including mechanical issues [7], surgical technique, and tumor-induced inflammation; uterine invasion probably disrupted local anatomy and blood supply, increasing fistula risk—knowledge crucial for surgical planning. Management typically requires a comprehensive approach starting conservatively and reserving surgery for persistent cases. [8] This underscores the need for individualized strategies considering overall health, cancer status, and anatomy.

As a single case report, generalizability is limited. The rarity necessitates larger studies to identify fistula risk factors post-Hartmann's and establish definitive protocols. Future research should also investigate long-term outcomes to optimize management.

Conclusion

This case highlights the challenges of Hartmann's procedure in complex pelvic malignancies. The development of an ileorectal fistula emphasizes the critical need for meticulous preoperative assessment and individualized surgical strategy. Increased recognition of such potential complications can enhance patient outcomes in comparable scenarios.

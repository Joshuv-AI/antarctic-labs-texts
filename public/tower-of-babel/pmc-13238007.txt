Following publication of the original article [1], the authors reported that there is a huge mismatch related to more than half of references in the final version. All in‑text reference numbers need to be adjusted and should correctly be aligned with the reference list.

For figures, the authors identified errors in several reprinted figures from previously published sources. To ensure scientific accuracy, they have corrected these figures accordingly. In addition, a few figures with suboptimal resolution have been replaced with higher-quality versions.

Parkin-independent mitophagy pathway. A | Initiation and Phagophore Formation: Under energy stress (AMP/ATP↑) and absence of growth signals, AMPK activates and mTORC1 is suppressed, triggering the ULK1 complex. ULK1 targets mitochondria through receptors including BNIP3, NIX, and FUNDC1, then recruits and phosphorylates the class III PI3K complex I (VPS34–VPS15–Beclin 1–ATG14L). This generates PI3P and nucleates the phagophore at the mitochondrial surface, bypassing Parkin. B | Mitophagosome Formation: Outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) proteins include BNIP3, BNIP3L, FUNDC1, BCL2L13, and FKBP8. PHB2 serves as a receptor on the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) upon OMM rupture. These receptors contain LIR motifs that trigger ubiquitin-independent mitophagy. LIR motifs bind directly to lipidated LC3/GABARAP proteins on the phagophore membrane. The phagophore then envelops and seals the mitochondrion, forming a double-membrane mitophagosome. C | Degradation and Recycling: The mitophagosome fuses with a lysosome (H+), breaking down the organelle and releasing its components for cellular reuse

The process of mitophagy initiated by PINK1/Parkin

Key components of the autophagy pathway. A | Initiation and Phagophore Nucleation: The ULK1 initiation complex activates first, then recruits the Vps34 nucleation complex. This complex initiates autophagy and supports endosome development. Vps34 produces PI3P on target membranes, creating the omegasome where the autophagy nucleation complex assembles. The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) provides the primary membrane source for the omegasome, though other sources contribute. B | Autophagosome Elongation and Closure: Two conjugation systems drive this process. The LC3 system (red): ATG3 acts as the E2-like enzyme, conjugating LC3 to phosphatidylethanolamine (PE). The ATG12 system (green): ATG12 forms a conjugate with ATG5. The ATG12-ATG5 conjugate combines with ATG16L1 to create the ATG16L1 complex, which functions as the E3-like enzyme and facilitates LC3 attachment to PE, completing lipidation. C | De-lipidation and LC3 Recycling: During autophagosome maturation, ATG4 cysteine proteases cleave LC3 from the outer autophagosomal membrane, enabling recycling. This cleavage also exposes the C-terminal glycine, preparing pro-LC3 for subsequent conjugation. D | Autolysosome Formation and Degradation: The mature autophagosome fuses with a lysosome for cargo degradation. Pharmacological tools have validated core autophagy components: BafA1 blocks autophagosome-lysosome fusion and lysosomal function; E64d and similar agents inhibit downstream degradation

The role of autophagy in immune cells within the tumor microenvironment (TME). A | For T cells in the TME, autophagy is essential for their survival and differentiation. Naive T cells use basal autophagy to remain inactive and avoid apoptosis caused by mitochondria-generated reactive oxygen species (ROS). However, elevated lactate levels in tumors interfere with this autophagy in naive T cells, weakening the anti-tumor response seen in mouse studies. Furthermore, conditions in the TME, such as high potassium levels or nutrient scarcity, limit nutrient absorption in effector T cells, leading to enforced autophagy through mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) inhibition due to a state of functional caloric restriction. This autophagy process is crucial for maintaining mitochondrial function, which is key to metabolic reprogramming and effector T cell differentiation. In tumor-infiltrating regulatory T cells (Treg cells), high expression of arginase 2 (ARG2) supports their autophagy levels. A reduction in autophagy in Treg cells shifts their metabolism from oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) to glycolysis, accompanied by active mTORC1 and MYC, destabilizing FOXP3 and leading to increased apoptosis and impaired function, which enhances tumor resistance. B | Regarding tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), they utilize lipidation of microtubule-associated protein 1A/1B light chain 3 (LC3)-associated phagocytosis (LAP) to break down apoptotic cancer cells. A lack of LAP in TAMs results in mitochondrial DNA release from dying cancer cells, triggering a type I interferon response via the cGAS–STING pathway and promoting anti-tumor activity. C | In ovarian cancer metastasis, TAMs with TIM4 (a T cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain-containing molecule 4) originate embryonically and are maintained locally, whereas TIM4-negative TAMs are replenished by circulating monocytes. Compared to TIM4-negative TAMs, TIM4-positive TAMs show higher oxidative phosphorylation levels and engage in mitophagy to reduce oxidative stress. Elevated ARG1 levels in TIM4-positive TAMs enhance mitophagy through reduced mTORC1 activation, a result of low arginine levels from ARG1-mediated metabolism. Additionally, a genetic lack of the autophagy component FAK family interacting protein of 200 kDa (FIP200) leads to the loss of TIM4-positive TAMs through ROS-induced apoptosis, thereby increasing T cell immunity and inhibiting tumor growth. Reprinted from Springer Nature [387]

Utilizing autophagy inhibition for cancer immunotherapy. A | Inhibiting autophagy in tumors alters the tumor microenvironment (TME), leading to increased production of T helper 1 (TH1)-type chemokines and enhanced infiltration of effector immune cells into tumors. This approach can be integrated with the adoptive transfer of engineered chimeric antigen receptor T cells (CAR T cells) and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICB). B | Autophagy plays a role in the degradation of potential tumor antigens and components of the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) in antigen-presenting cells (APCs). By inhibiting autophagy, the antitumor T cell response is strengthened due to improved antigen processing and presentation. C | Autophagy contributes to the degradation of MHC class I molecules and granzyme B, mechanisms tumors use to escape destruction by cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) and natural killer (NK) cells. Inhibiting autophagy increases the presence of MHC class I on the cell surface and blocks the autophagic degradation of granzyme B, facilitating cell lysis and improving the efficacy of ICB therapy. D | High doses of interleukin-2 (IL-2) induce a systemic autophagic syndrome in patients. Autophagy inhibition safeguards cancer patients from systemic damage and enhances the effectiveness of IL-2 treatment. DAMP stands for damage-associated molecular pattern; DC for dendritic cell; TAM for tumor-associated macrophage; and Treg cell for regulatory T cell. Reprinted from Springer Nature [387]

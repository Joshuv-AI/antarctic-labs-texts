Minimax spectral estimation of weighted Laplace operators
Yann Chaubet, Vincent Divol

Abstract
. Given n n i.i.d. observations, we study the problem of estimating the spectrum of weighted Laplace operators of the form Δ f = Δ + α ∇ log f ⋅ ∇ \Delta_{f}=\Delta+\alpha\nabla\log f\cdot\nabla , where f f is a positive probability density on a known compact d d -dimensional manifold without boundary and α ∈ ℝ \alpha\in\mathbb{R} is a hyperparameter. These operators arise as continuum limits of graph Laplacian matrices and provide valuable geometric information on the underlying data distribution. We establish the exact minimax rates of estimation for this problem, by exhibiting two different rates of convergence for eigenfunctions and eigenvalues. When f f belongs to a Hölder-Zygmund class 𝒞 s \mathscr{C}^{s} of regularity s ⩾ 2 s\geqslant 2 , the eigenfunctions can be estimated with respect to the L q \mathrm{L}^{q} -norm ( q ⩾ 1 q\geqslant 1 ) via plug-in methods at the minimax rate n − s + 1 2 ​ s + d n^{-\frac{s+1}{2s+d}} for d ⩾ 3 d\geqslant 3 (with different rates for d ⩽ 2 d\leqslant 2 ). Moreover, eigenvalues can be estimated at the minimax rate n − 4 ​ s 4 ​ s + d + n − 1 2 n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}} . In the regime s > d 4 s>\frac{d}{4} , we further show that asymptotically efficient estimators exist. We also present a general framework for estimating nonlinear functionals over Hölder-Zygmund spaces, with potential applications to a broad class of statistical problems.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Spectral methods in data science

Over the past twenty years, diffusion-based approaches have achieved numerous successes in statistics and machine learning. For example, in sampling theory, discretizations of diffusion processes are used to design Markov chains with a target stationary distribution, see e.g.,
[
20
,
21
,
27
]
. In generative modeling, diffusion-based models learn a diffusion process between Gaussian noise and the (unknown) target distribution by training a neural network, see
[
82
]
for a survey. Diffusion processes are also employed to construct
spectral representations
of datasets, which are routinely used for dimension reduction
[
60
,
4
,
19
]
,
as an orthonormal basis in a regression task
[
18
,
34
]
, or for clustering with the spectral clustering method
[
78
]
.

Given a set of
n
i.i.d. observations
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
following some distribution
P
, these spectral representations are obtained from the top eigenvectors of the generator
L_{n}
of a discrete random walk over the set of observations,
L_{n}
often being referred to as the graph Laplacian matrix of the walk. These eigenvectors are known to be related to the long-term behavior of this random walk, and therefore to non-local aspects of the geometry underlying the data distribution. The random walk is defined locally, for instance, by jumping randomly from one data point
X_{i}
to another point
X_{j}
found within a small neighborhood of
X_{i}
of fixed radius. Under appropriate normalization, this random walk formally converges as
n\to\infty
to a continuous diffusion process whose generator is a weighted Laplace operator of the form

(1.1)

\Delta_{f}=\Delta+\alpha A_{V}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{with}\hskip 20.00003ptA_{V}=\nabla V\cdot\nabla,

where
\Delta
is the Laplace operator,
f=e^{V}
is the density of
P
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
is a parameter depending on the exact definition of
L_{n}
. The spectrum of such an operator provides valuable information about the geometry of the underlying metric measure space. For instance, when
P
is the uniform distribution on a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the connected components of
M
, its volume, and its total scalar curvature can all be inferred from the spectrum of
\Delta_{f}
which in this case is equivalent to the (unweighted) Laplace operator
[
6
]
. When
\alpha=d/2-1
, the operator
\Delta_{f}
is actually related to the Laplace operator on the manifold
M
endowed with the conformal metric
fg
, whose spectrum contains information about both the sampling density and the intrinsic geometry of the support
M
of the data distribution. Let us also mention that
\Delta_{f}
is conjugated in an appropriate weighted space to the so-called Witten Laplacian
[
80
]
, whose spectrum in the limit
\alpha\to\infty
provides rich topological informations about critical points of
f
, see e.g.
[
36
,
56
,
22
,
48
]
.

The effectiveness of diffusion methods based on graph Laplacian matrices
L_{n}
has motivated researchers to investigate their theoretical properties, particularly regarding their convergence to the limit generator
\Delta_{f}
. We give below in
Section

1.6
a more detailed account of the research on the topic. We begin by discussing a recent work by García Trillos, Li and Venkatraman
[
70
]
which is the current state of the art. In this paper, the authors study the rate of convergence of graph Laplacian eigenfunctions with respect to the
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm (instead of the classical
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm). Under a
\mathrm{C}^{2+\alpha}
smoothness assumption for
\alpha>0
, they show that the minimax rate of estimation of a simple eigenfunction with respect to an empirical
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm is up to logarithmic factors
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{4+d}})
. Note that this rate is exactly the minimax rate for estimating a
\mathrm{C}^{2}
density with respect to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-loss. The same rate of estimation holds for the empirical
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-loss and for eigenvalue estimation.

This paper raises several questions that we address in this work:

(1)

Can minimax rates of convergence for eigenfunction estimation be derived for more natural norms such as the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm or the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-norm for
q\geqslant 2
? Can eigenfunctions still be estimated in the presence of eigenvalue multiplicities?

(2)

Is the rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{4+d}})
minimax for the problem of eigenvalue estimation? If not, what is the minimax rate of convergence?

(3)

If more regularity is assumed, i.e., if we assume that
f\in\mathrm{C}^{s}
for
s\geqslant 2
, can this regularity be leveraged to obtain faster rates of estimation?

We answer these questions from a minimax perspective by assuming knowledge of the manifold
M
.
First, we show that the minimax rate of estimation for the eigenspaces of
\Delta_{f}
is of order

\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{s+1}{2s+d}})

(for
d\geqslant 3
) if the loss function is the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-angle between subspaces. The rates become parametric up to logarithmic factors for
d\leqslant 2
. These rates of estimation coincide with the minimax rate of estimation of a density with respect to the
\mathrm{H}^{-1}
-norm, or, equivalently, to the Wasserstein distance, see
[
25
,
57
,
24
]
. This result follows from a stability result for the eigenspaces of
\Delta_{f}
under perturbation of
f
.

Second, we show that the minimax rate of estimation for eigenvalue estimation is

\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

This rate is reminiscent of minimax rates of estimation in functional estimation problems, e.g., for the estimation of the integral of the square of a density, see
[
10
,
11
]
. Actually, we show that debiaising methods commonly used in this field can be leveraged to improve naive plug-in estimators.

Note that even for
s=2
, our rates, respectively of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{3}{4+d}})
and
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{8}{8+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, are both faster than the rate of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{4+d}})
appearing in
[
70
]
. For eigenfunction estimation, this discrepancy is simply due to the use of an
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-norm instead of an
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm, see
Remark

3.7
. More importantly, our findings show that the eigenvalue rate of estimation
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{4+d}})
appearing in both
[
16
]
and
[
70
]
is not minimax (at least if
M
is known): this answers an open question raised in
[
70
]
about the possibility of obtaining faster rates of estimation for eigenvalue estimation than for eigenfunction estimation (see their Remark 2.9). Another key difference between the two papers is the fact that we are able to estimate not only simple eigenvalues, but also eigenvalues with multiplicities, together with the associated eigenspaces.

1.2.
Notation and geometrical context

If
n
is an integer, we let
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
. All random variables
X_{1},X_{2},\dots
are defined on the same standard Borel probability space with a probability measure
\mathbf{P}
, and
\mathbf{E}
denotes expectation with respect to
\mathbf{P}
.
We will identify measures with their densities, and we write
\mathbf{E}_{f}
to indicate expectation under the law with density
f
. If
A:E\to F
is a linear operator between two Banach spaces
E
and
F
, we denote by
\|A\|_{E\to F}
the corresponding operator norm.

We let
(M,g)
be a smooth compact connected
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary.
For
s\geqslant 0
, we let
\mathrm{C}^{s}
be the Hölder space of regularity
s
on
M
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
be the space of distributions on
M
, which is naturally identified with the dual space of
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(M)
by using the Riemannian volume form of
M
. The latter induces a sesquilinear duality pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
between distributions, whenever it is well defined. In what follows we will consider the operator

\Lambda=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}

which acts on
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
. For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
, we define

\mathrm{W}^{t,q}=\Lambda^{-t}\mathrm{L}^{q}

the usual Sobolev space of order
t
and integrability exponent
q
on
M
. The Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{W}^{t,2}
of weight
2
are simply denoted by
\mathrm{H}^{t}
. By construction, for any
t,m\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leqslant q\leqslant\infty
the map

\Lambda^{m}:\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\to\mathrm{W}^{t-m,q}

is an isomorphism.
Besov spaces
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}
will also be useful; their main properties are summarized in
Appendix

A
. In particular, we will use the identity
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{2,2}=\mathrm{H}^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Hölder-Zygmund spaces are defined as
\mathscr{C}^{s}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}
for
s\geqslant 0
. If
s
is not an integer, these spaces coincide with the standard Hölder spaces
\mathrm{C}^{s}
, with
\mathrm{C}^{s}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{s}
if
s
is an integer.
Note that all function spaces mentioned above are complex Banach spaces. We denote by
\overline{u}
the conjugate of a function
u
.

In what follows we assume that
f\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
is a positive density for some
s>1
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
a nonzero number that will be fixed throughout the paper, the operator
\Delta_{f}
defined in (
1.1
)
defines an unbounded operator on
\mathrm{L}^{2}
with closed domain
\mathrm{H}^{2}
, see
Section

3.1
for more details. Note that, since we assume that the manifold
M
is known, the case
\alpha=0
is of limited interest. On
\mathrm{L}^{2}
there is a weighted scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle_{f}=\int u\,\bar{v}\,f^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{g},\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in\mathrm{L}^{2},

and the associated Hilbert space is denoted
\mathrm{L}^{2}(f^{\alpha})
. The weighted Laplace operator is symmetric on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(f^{\alpha})
, since we have

\langle\Delta_{f}\,u,v\rangle_{f}=-\int\nabla u\,\overline{\nabla v}\,f^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{vol}_{g},\hskip 10.00002ptu,v\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(M).

In particular
\Delta_{f}
is nonpositive, and it is a classical result (see
[
37
, Chapter 3]
) that its spectrum is discrete and accumulating at
-\infty
. We denote by

0=\lambda_{0,f}\leqslant\lambda_{1,f}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\lambda_{\ell,f}\to\infty

the spectrum
\sigma(-\Delta_{f})=\{\lambda_{\ell,f}:\ \ell\geqslant 0\}
of
-\Delta_{f}
. Note that with our convention, it is possible that
\lambda_{\ell,f}=\lambda_{\ell^{\prime},f}
for some
\ell<\ell^{\prime}
.

1.3.
Minimax estimation of eigenfunctions

We estimate the eigenfunctions of the operator
\Delta_{f}
through a plug-in approach: we use as estimators the eigenfunctions of the operator
\Delta_{\hat{f}}
, where
\hat{f}
is an appropriate density estimator of
f
. To account for possible eigenvalue multiplicities, we measure the proximity between spectral projectors associated with
\Delta_{f}
and
\Delta_{\hat{f}}
rather than between eigenfunctions.

Let
\Upsilon
be a simple loop in
\mathbb{C}
, with counterclockwise orientation, bounding a compact domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
. We assume that

\partial\Omega\cap\sigma(-\Delta_{f})=\emptyset.

The spectral projector
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
of
\Delta_{f}
associated with
\Upsilon
is given by

\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Upsilon}(z+\Delta_{f})^{-1}\mathrm{d}z.

For any
q\geqslant 2
, if
\Pi
and
\Pi^{\prime}
are two projection operators that are bounded
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}
and
\mathrm{L}^{q}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}

we set

D_{q}(\Pi,\Pi^{\prime})=\left\|(1-\Pi^{\prime})\Pi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}+\left\|(1-\Pi)\Pi^{\prime}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}.

If
f,h\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
are such that
\Pi_{f,\Upsilon}
and
\Pi_{h,\Upsilon}
are well-defined,

D_{q}(\Pi_{f,\Upsilon},\Pi_{h,\Upsilon})
is a measure of the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-angle between the finite-dimensional eigenspaces corresponding to the eigenvalues in the domain
\Omega
, respectively for
-\Delta_{f}
and
-\Delta_{h}
.

Let
L,\delta>0
, and
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{C}
be a simple loop such that

(1.2)

\sup_{z\in\Upsilon}|\operatorname{Re}z|<L.

For
s>2
, we define the statistical model

\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)\subset\mathscr{C}^{s}

as follows. We say that a function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
lies in
\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
if
\Upsilon
encircles at least one eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{f}
, with

\dist(\sigma(-\Delta_{f}),\Upsilon)>\delta,\hskip 10.00002pt\inf f>\delta\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}<L.

For
s=2
, we replace the Hölder-Zygmund norm
\mathscr{C}^{2}
by the stronger Hölder norm
\mathrm{C}^{2}
in the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Estimation of eigenvectors, upper bound)
.

Let
s\geqslant 2
,
L,\delta>0
, and
q\geqslant 2
. There exists
C
such that the following holds. For any simple loop
\Upsilon
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
1.2
) with counterclockwise orientation and any
n\geqslant 1
, there exists an estimator

\widehat{\Pi}_{n}:M^{n}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{2})

such that for each
f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
we have

(1.3)

\mathbf{E}_{f^{\otimes n}}[D_{q}(\widehat{\Pi}_{n},\Pi_{\Upsilon,f})]\leqslant C\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if $d=1$,}\\
(n/\log n)^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if $d=2$,}\\
n^{-\frac{s+1}{2s+d}}&\text{if $d\geqslant 3$.}\end{cases}

Theorem

1.1
relies on a perturbation bound (see
Proposition

3.6
), which relates the distance between two operators
\Delta_{f}
an
\Delta_{h}
to the
\mathrm{W}^{-1,p}
-norm between the densities
f
and
h
. For densities bounded away from zero and infinity, the
\mathrm{W}^{-1,p}
-norm is known to be equivalent to the Wasserstein distance between the corresponding measures. Minimax estimators with respect to the Wasserstein distance have been proposed on a manifold in
[
25
]
(see also
[
57
,
24
]
), and attain the rate given in (
1.3
), from which the rate of estimation for the eigenfunctions follows.

Remark 1.2
.

The estimator provided by
Theorem

1.1
reconstructs the spectral projector

\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
for any contour
\Upsilon
isolating a cluster of eigenvalues.
In practice, however, one is often interested in estimating the eigenspace associated with a specific
eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell,f}
, which requires constructing from the data a contour
\Upsilon
enclosing

\lambda_{\ell,f}
and no other eigenvalue.
Assume that
\lambda_{\ell,f}
satisfies the spectral gap condition

\mathrm{dist}\bigl(\lambda_{\ell,f},\,\sigma(-\Delta_{f})\setminus\{\lambda_{\ell,f}\}\bigr)>\delta.

Under this assumption such a contour can be identified from the data.
For instance,
[
28
]
construct an estimator
\hat{\lambda}_{\ell}
that, with high probability,
lies within
\delta/4
of
\lambda_{\ell,f}
.
Consequently, the circle
\hat{\Upsilon}
of radius
\delta/2
centered at
\hat{\lambda}_{\ell}
encloses

\lambda_{\ell,f}
alone and remains at distance at least
\delta/4
from the other eigenvalues.
Applying our estimator with this data-driven contour
\hat{\Upsilon}
yields an estimator of the

\ell
-th eigenspace with the same convergence rates as in
Theorem

1.1
.
The same observation applies to the problem of eigenvalue estimation.

We provide a matching minimax lower bound to
Theorem

1.1
, as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(Estimation of eigenvectors, lower bound)
.

Let
s\geqslant 2
and
q\geqslant 2
. Then there exist a constant
c
and a simple loop
\Upsilon
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
1.2
) with counterclockwise orientation such that for
L>0
large enough and
\delta>0
small enough we have for any
n\geqslant 1

(1.4)

\inf_{\widehat{\Pi}_{n}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)}\mathbf{E}_{f^{\otimes n}}[D_{2}(\widehat{\Pi}_{n},\Pi_{\Upsilon,f})]\geqslant c\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if $d\leqslant 2$,}\\
n^{-\frac{s+1}{2s+d}}&\text{if $d\geqslant 3$,}\end{cases}

where the infimum is taken over all estimators
\widehat{\Pi}_{n}:M^{n}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{L}^{2})
.

In practical situations where the manifold is unknown to the practitioner, the empirical
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-loss between eigenfunctions is used as a surrogate to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-loss. It is therefore more interesting to provide rates of estimation for

(1.5)

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\varphi(X_{i})-\widehat{\varphi}(X_{i})|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
\varphi
is an eigenfunction and
\widehat{\varphi}
an estimator.
The empirical risk is trivially upper bounded by the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norm
\|\varphi-\widehat{\varphi}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}
, which we are actually also able to bound, at the price of deteriorating the exponent
\frac{s+1}{2s+d}
by an arbitrary small number
\varepsilon>0
.
See
Remark

3.9
for further details.

1.4.
Minimax estimation of eigenvalues

We detail in this section the main results we obtained regarding the estimation of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{f}
. Let
s\geqslant 2
,
\delta,L>0
and
\Upsilon
be a simple loop in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
1.2
) with counterclockwise orientation. For
f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
we denote by

(1.6)

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}=\frac{1}{\sharp(\Omega\cap\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))}\sum_{\lambda\in\Omega\cap\sigma(-\Delta_{f})}\lambda,

the average of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{f}
that are enclosed by
\Upsilon
. Note that this can be written

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}=-\frac{1}{\sharp(\Omega\cap\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))}\operatorname{tr}(\Delta_{f}\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}).

Then we have the following result, which tells us that we can estimate
\mu_{\Upsilon,f}
at a rate of convergence of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
.

Theorem 1.4

(Estimation of eigenvalues, upper bound)
.

Let
s\geqslant 2
and
L,\delta>0
. There exists
C
such that the following holds. For any simple loop
\Upsilon
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
1.2
) with counterclockwise orientation and any
n\geqslant 1
, there exists an estimator
\widehat{\mu}_{n}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for each
f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
we have

(1.7)

\mathbf{E}_{f^{\otimes n}}\left[|\widehat{\mu}_{n}-\mu_{\Upsilon,f}|\right]\leqslant C\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}&\text{if $s\leqslant\frac{d}{4}$,}\\
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if $s>\frac{d}{4}$.}\end{cases}

Moreover, if
s>\frac{d}{4}
, the estimator
\widehat{\mu}_{n}
is asymptotically efficient.

We also obtain a matching minimax lower bound.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
s\geqslant 2
. Then there exist a constant
c
, a simple loop
\Upsilon
in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying (
1.2
) with counterclockwise orientation and a
d
-dimensional manifold
M
(equal to a dilatation of the flat torus) such that for
L>0
large enough and
\delta>0
small enough we have for any
n\geqslant 1

(1.8)

\inf_{\widehat{\mu}_{n}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)}\mathbf{E}_{f^{\otimes n}}\left[|\widehat{\mu}_{n}-\mu_{\Upsilon,f}|\right]\geqslant c\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}&\text{if $s\leqslant\frac{d}{4}$,}\\
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if $s>\frac{d}{4}$.}\end{cases}

where the infimum is taken over all estimators
\widehat{\mu}_{n}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 1.6
.

In fact, our methods allow us to estimate the quantity

\operatorname{tr}\chi(-\Delta_{f})=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta_{f})}\chi(\lambda)

for any
\chi\in\mathrm{C}^{3}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
with the same convergence rates as in
Theorem

1.5
, see
Section

3.3
.

1.5.
Functional estimation

The problem of estimating
\mu_{\Upsilon,f}

falls within the scope of functional estimation, i.e., estimating a functional
\mathrm{T}:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
defined over a set
\Theta
of densities at a fixed density
f\in\Theta
, while observing
n
i.i.d. samples
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\sim f
.
This constitutes a classical question in statistics, that has been studied since at least the 60s with the estimation of the squared norm
\mathrm{T}_{f}=\int f^{2}
of a density
[
49
,
9
,
2
]
.
The estimation of other functionals such as the Fisher information
[
8
]
or the entropy
[
50
]
was then introduced in the 70s and the 80s, see
[
58
, Section 4.4]
for a state of the art at the beginning of the 80s. Significant progress was achieved by Bickel and Ritov
[
10
]
who showed the following elbow effect. Consider the quadratic functional
{\mathrm{T}}_{f}=\int f^{2}
. As long as
f\in\mathrm{C}^{s}
for
s>\frac{1}{4}
, estimators attaining the parametric rate of convergence exist. However, for
s\leqslant\frac{1}{4}
, the rate is deteriorated to
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+1}})
. They further show that this rate is minimax optimal on appropriate Hölder balls. Besides, efficient estimation is possible in the smooth regime: the estimators are asymptotically normal with minimal asymptotic variance. See also
[
26
,
45
,
46
,
47
,
14
,
30
]
where other estimators of quadratic functionals are developed.

To move beyond the estimation of quadratic functionals, a central idea is to employ debiasing techniques.
Various approaches have been developed in this direction; we refer the reader to the introduction of
[
43
]
for a comprehensive overview. Our method follows from the theory of higher order influence functions developed by Robins, Li, Tchetgen and van der Vaart, see
[
59
]
, that we now present. To fix ideas, suppose first that the functional
\mathrm{T}
is Lipschitz-continuous with respect to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm. Let
f\in\mathrm{C}^{s}
be a density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with bounded support. It is well-known that one can construct estimators
\hat{f}
satisfying the rate of convergence
\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}]\lesssim n^{-\frac{2s}{2s+d}}
, see e.g.,
[
72
]
. By Lipschitz continuity, this directly yields the same rate for the plug-in estimator
\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{0}={\mathrm{T}}_{\hat{f}}
, namely

(1.9)

\mathbf{E}[|\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{0}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f}|]\lesssim n^{-\frac{s}{2s+d}}.

The estimator
\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{0}
serves as a preliminary, but potentially biased, estimator. Suppose now that the functional
\mathrm{T}
is Fréchet twice-differentiable over
\mathrm{L}^{2}
. Then a first-order Taylor expansion around
\hat{f}
yields

(1.10)

{\mathrm{T}}_{f}={\mathrm{T}}_{\hat{f}}+{\mathrm{T}}^{(1)}_{\hat{f}}[f-\hat{f}]+\mathcal{O}(\|f-\hat{f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}),

where
{\mathrm{T}}^{(1)}_{\hat{f}}
is a linear form over
\mathrm{L}^{2}
. This expansion suggests an improved estimator of the form

(1.11)

\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{1}=\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{0}+\hat{B}_{1},

where
\hat{B}_{1}
is an estimator of the linear term
B_{1}={\mathrm{T}}^{(1)}_{\hat{f}}[f-\hat{f}]
.
By the Riesz representation theorem, there exists a function
\psi_{\hat{f}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
such that
{\mathrm{T}}^{(1)}_{\hat{f}}[u]=\int\psi_{\hat{f}}\,u
, allowing us to estimate
B_{1}
by an empirical mean on a second independent sample. Such an estimator
\hat{B}_{1}
satisfies
\mathbf{E}[|\hat{B}_{1}-B_{1}|]\lesssim n^{-1/2}
. Putting everything together, the risk of
\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{1}
satisfies

(1.12)

\mathbf{E}[|\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{1}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f}|]\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{E}[\|f-\hat{f}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}])\lesssim n^{-\frac{1}{2}}+n^{-\frac{2s}{2s+d}}.

Comparing (
1.12
) with (
1.9
), we observe that debiasing can reduce the bias quadratically, without significantly increasing the variance.
This debiasing method was first introduced by Ibragimov, Nemirovski, and Khas’minskii in the Gaussian white noise model
[
40
]
, and later extended to settings such as regression and density estimation; see
[
26
,
55
,
32
,
11
,
54
]
for further developments.

This approach extends beyond first-order corrections: higher-order Taylor expansions can be used to further reduce bias, with remainder term at order
J
typically decaying as
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{Js}{2s+d}})
. In dimension
d=1
, Birgé and Massart
[
11
]
applied this methodology with
J=3
to estimate integral functionals of the form

(1.13)

{\mathrm{T}}_{f}=\int\psi(f(x))\mathrm{d}x

where
\psi
is a smooth function and
f\in\mathrm{C}^{s}
is a density on
\mathbb{R}
. This framework includes, as special cases, key quantities such as the entropy. Their results, together with following works conducted by Laurent
[
46
]
, and Kerkyacharian and Picard
[
42
]
, show that such integral functionals in dimension
1
can be estimated at rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+1}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
. Birgé and Massart also established that this rate is the minimax rate, and that efficient estimation is possible in the regime
s>\frac{1}{4}
.

This idea has since been applied to various problems in nonparametric statistics through the use of higher-order influence functions, see
[
75
,
51
,
52
]
. In these approaches, the key step is to estimate the multilinear forms
G
appearing in the Taylor expansion of the functional
\mathrm{T}
.
In this work, we identify sufficient conditions, formulated solely in terms of operator norms of
G
, independent of any particular representation, that allow for the estimation of
G[f,\dots,f]
at the rate

(1.14)

\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}})

for any density
f\in\mathrm{C}^{s}
on a
d
-dimensional domain. As is standard in this literature, our estimation method relies on the use of
U
-statistics and wavelet-based projection estimators.
If the Taylor expansion of a functional
\mathrm{T}
consists of multilinear forms satisfying these conditions, then the debiasing procedure can be applied to estimate
{\mathrm{T}}_{f}

at the rate (
1.14
).
We refer to such functionals as
s
-regular functionals
, see
Definition

2.5
.

While our primary motivation is the estimation of eigenvalues of weighted Laplace operators
\Delta_{f}
, we believe that our set of sufficient conditions provides a simple toolkit to analyze the performance of debiasing estimators for functional estimation problem, that could potentially be applicable to a broader range of functionals, such as:

(1)

eigenvalues of other differential operators depending on a density
f
;

(2)

integral functionals
\int\psi(u_{f})
of the solution
u_{f}
to a PDE depending on a density
f
;

(3)

Besov or Sobolev norms of a density
f
;

(4)

optimal transport costs between
f
and a fixed reference density
g
;

(5)

solutions to optimal control problems parametrized by a density
f
.

1.6.
Related work

The graph Laplacian matrix
L_{n}
can naturally be extended to an operator acting on smooth functions on
M
. For a smooth function
u
and a point
x\in M
, the rate of convergence of
L_{n}u(x)
to
\Delta_{f}u(x)
, known as the
pointwise convergence
rate of the graph Laplacian, has been studied by multiple authors for kernel-based graphs in
[
35
,
5
,
61
,
29
,
7
]
and for neighborhood graphs in
[
66
,
33
]
. However, for most applications, the convergence of the spectrum of graph Laplacians, known as
spectral convergence
, is more relevant. Yet, relating pointwise convergence results to spectral convergence is far from trivial. Although spectral consistency was established as early as 2006 by Belkin and Niyogi
[
5
]
—see also
[
77
,
71
]
—the first quantitative spectral rates appeared only much later. García Trillos, Gerlach, Hein and Slepčev
[
28
]
obtained the rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{2d}})
for both eigenvalues and eigenfunctions when the underlying density
f
is Lipschitz continuous.
Their technique relies on fine stability properties of the spectra of metric-measure Laplacians with respect to the underlying measure, see also
[
13
]
. This technique was further improved by Calder and García Trillos
[
15
]
, who obtained a convergence rate of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{4+d}})
when the density
f
is of Hölder regularity
\mathrm{C}^{s}
for some
s>2
. Since then, this rate has been steadily improved, with a series of papers over the past few years obtaining different convergence rates under slightly different conditions. For instance, Wormell and Reich
[
81
]
obtained a spectral error of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4}{12+d}})
for
s>2
and
\alpha=1
on the flat torus, Cheng and Wu
[
16
]
obtained an eigenvalue error of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{4+d}})
and an eigenfunction error of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{6+d}})
for a smooth density
f
and
\alpha=0
, and Wahl
[
79
]
obtained a spectral error of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{2}{8+d}})
for a uniform density
f
.

Note that the results of our paper indicate that all these rates are not minimax, at least if the manifold
M
is known.

1.7.
Open questions

We conclude this introductory section with a list of open questions raised by our work.

The bounded domain case.

Instead of considering datasets supported on compact manifolds, it seems very reasonable to consider the case where the dataset is supported on a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This raises natural questions:
does the plug-in estimator still achieve minimax rates in this setting? Can the debiasing techniques we developed be extended to handle boundary effects?

The unknown manifold case.

Suppose that
M
is an
unknown

d
-dimensional submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{D}
. Can our estimators be adapted to this case, at least in principle? We conjecture that if
M
is sufficiently smooth, one could adapt the chart-based manifold estimators of Aamari and Levrard
[
1
]
to construct an estimated manifold
\hat{M}
that is
\mathrm{C}^{k}
-close to
M
. Our method could then be adapted by computing the spectrum of a modified weighted Laplace operator based on the metric
\hat{g}
of
\hat{M}
rather than the true metric
g
of
M
. Aamari and Levrard show that their estimators achieve a rate of the form
n^{-\frac{m-k}{d}}
in
\mathrm{C}^{k}
-norm (up to logarithmic factors) if the manifold is of regularity
m
. This suggests that
\hat{g}
and
g
can be made arbitrarily close if we assume that the manifold is of class
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}
. We thus conjecture that crude stability results for the spectrum of weighted Laplacians under perturbations of the metric could be developed, allowing our estimators to be used on
\hat{M}
without loss in convergence rate. In summary, we conjecture that the absence of direct access to the true manifold
M
does not deteriorate the statistical rate of convergence for spectrum estimation should
M
be smooth enough, although a precise analysis remains to be conducted.

Minimax optimality of graph Laplacians

Instead of relying on the intricate construction described in the previous paragraph, it would be more satisfactory to prove that graph Laplacians achieve the minimax rate. This question has already been addressed for
s=2
and the
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-loss in
[
70
]
, but remains open (i) for
s>2
, (ii) for eigenfunction estimation using the more common
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-loss, and (iii) for eigenvalue estimation. We believe that bias-reduction techniques would likely be necessary to attain minimax rates for eigenvalue estimation.

Towards score-based spectral methods

Score-based methods are powerful tools for estimating the score function
\nabla\log f
of a density
f
, with neural networks achieving impressive empirical results in high-dimensional settings. Our theoretical analysis shows that accurately estimating the score function is sufficient to obtain precise approximations of the spectrum of the weighted Laplace operator
\Delta_{f}
.
This insight motivates the following two-step procedure:

(1)

Estimate the score function
\nabla\log f
via score matching
[
39
]
or denoising score matching
[
76
]
, yielding an approximation
\hat{s}
;

(2)

Use this estimate to define the operator
\hat{\Delta}=\Delta+\alpha\hat{s}\cdot\nabla
, and approximate its spectrum using, e.g., modern neural PDE solvers, see
[
38
]
for a review.

Comparing the spectral properties and embedding quality of this approach with standard techniques like spectral clustering or classical dimension reduction methods would be an interesting direction for further investigation.

deals with the general problem of functional estimation over Hölder-Zygmund spaces, and can be read independently from the rest of the paper. Spectral estimation of weighted Laplace operators is discussed in
Section

3
. Minimax lower bounds are proven in
Section

2.
Regular functional estimation

We focus on the problem of estimating the value of a real-valued functional
{\mathrm{T}}_{f}
depending on a density
f
, under the assumption that
f
belongs to a ball in the Hölder-Zygmund space
\mathscr{C}^{s}
for some
s>0
.

Let
T:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
be a map over an open subset
\Theta\subset\mathscr{C}^{s}
. We say that
\mathrm{T}
can be estimated at a rate
(r_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
over
\mathscr{C}^{s}
if for all
R>0
there is
C>0
such that the following holds. For any
n\geqslant 1
there exists an estimator
\widehat{\mathrm{T}}_{n}:M^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that for each density
f\in\Theta
with
\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant R
, we have

\mathbf{E}_{f^{\otimes n}}[({\mathrm{T}}_{f}-\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{n})^{2}]\leqslant Cr_{n}^{2}.

In what follows for simplicity we will write
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}=\hat{\mathrm{T}}_{n}
when
n
is implicit.
When a parametric rate of convergence holds, that is when
r_{n}=n^{-1/2}
, we will additionally show that the estimators we propose are
asymptotically efficient
, see
[
74
, Chapter 25]
. The efficient influence function
\psi_{f}
is defined as a function
\psi_{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
such that for all
g\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
with
\int gf=0
,

(2.1)

\frac{{\mathrm{T}}_{f(1+tg)}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f}}{t}\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}\int\psi_{f}gf

(should such a function
\psi_{f}
exist).
Remark that
{\mathrm{T}}_{f(1+tg)}
is well-defined for
t
small enough as
\Theta
is open.
The function
\psi_{f}
is not uniquely defined, but only up to an additive constant. With a slight abuse, we will still refer to
\psi_{f}
as
the
efficient influence function any such function. We say that an estimator
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
of
{\mathrm{T}}_{f}
is
asymptotically efficient
at
f
if it holds that

(2.2)

{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\psi_{f}(X_{i})-\mathbf{E}[\psi_{f}(X_{i})])+o_{\mathbf{P}}(n^{-1/2}).

In particular, by the central limit theorem, any asymptotically efficient estimator is asymptotically normal with asymptotic variance
\mathrm{Var}_{X\sim f}(\psi_{f}(X))
. This variance should be thought of as the minimal asymptotic variance an estimator of
{\mathrm{T}}_{f}
can attain up to a factor
1/n
, an idea which can be made precise, see
[
74
, Chapter 8]
.

Remark 2.1
.

A bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be isometrically embedded in the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. This remark implies that all the results in this section also applies to densities
f
with Hölder-Zygmund regularity on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as long as they are supported on a fixed, known set
\Omega
. Our results however do not cover densities with unbounded supports or densities that belong to Hölder-Zygmund spaces on domains (e.g.,
f
is the uniform density on
\Omega
).

The debiasing method we propose requires estimating multilinear forms appearing in the Taylor expansion of the functional
\mathrm{T}
. We give a set of conditions that allows for the estimation of such multilinear forms. Unlike previous results appearing in the literature that rely on the multilinear forms having specific expression (such as the one appearing in
Lemma

2.4
), our conditions are only phrased in terms of certain mapping properties of the multilinear form, and are blind to its exact expression.

Recall that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of an operator
G:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
between two separable Hilbert spaces is defined as

\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\geqslant 1}|\langle e^{2}_{i_{2}},G[e^{1}_{i_{1}}]\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
(e^{j}_{i})_{i\geqslant 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{j}
for
j=1,2
. We also let
\mathcal{L}(E)
be the set of bounded operators
E\to E
.

Definition 2.2

(Regular multilinear form)
.

Let
s,L>0
.

(G1)

A linear form
G
is said to be an
s
-regular form with norm
L
if there exists
\psi\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
with
\|\psi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant L
such that
G:v\mapsto\int v\psi
.

(G2)

A symmetric bilinear form
G
is said to be an
s
-regular form with norm
L
if for all
v\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
,
G[\cdot,v]
is an
s
-regular form with norm
L\|v\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}
and if
G:\mathrm{L}^{2}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
has norm smaller than
L
.

(G3)

A symmetric trilinear form
G
is said to be an
s
-regular form with norm
L
if for all
v\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
,
G[\cdot,\cdot,v]
is an
s
-regular bilinear form with norm
L\|v\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}
, if
G:\mathrm{L}^{\infty}\times\mathrm{L}^{\infty}\times\mathrm{L}^{\infty}\to\mathbb{R}
has norm smaller than
L
and if for
t=(s+d)/2
,

(2.3)

G:\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}

has Hilbert-Schmidt norm smaller than
L
.

Remark 2.3
.

We show in
Appendix

E
that the Hilbert-Schmidt condition (
2.3
) is implied by a control of the operator norm of
G:\mathrm{L}^{2}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, which is typically easier to verify in practice.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
s>0
. Let
J\in\{1,2,3\}
and define a
J
-multilinear form
G
by

(2.4)

G[v_{1},\dots,v_{J}]=\int v_{1}\cdots v_{J}\cdot\psi

for some function
\psi\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
.
Then,
G
is an
s
-regular form.

Proof.

The result follows from the definition for
J=1
. For
J=2
, it follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
\mathscr{C}^{s}
is a multiplication algebra, see
Appendix

A
. The only nontrivial condition to check for
J=3
is the Hilbert-Schmidt condition in
(G3)
. We show in
Appendix

E
that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
G:\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}
is finite for all
t>d/2
, implying in particular the result for
t=(s+d)/2
.
∎

As already mentioned in the introduction, the estimation of
G[f,\dots,f]
for
G
the functional in (
2.4
) constitutes a classical problem in statistics. The previous lemma states that they fall within the scope of
s
-regular functionals for which we will be able to give rates of estimation.

Definition 2.5

(Regular functional)
.

Let
s>0
and
L>0
. Let
J_{\max}=2
if
s>d/4
and
J_{\max}=3
otherwise.
We say that
\mathrm{T}:\Theta\to\mathbb{R}
is an
s
-regular functional over
\Theta
with norm smaller than
L
if
\mathrm{T}
is bounded by
L
and if there exist
s^{\prime}<s
and
\eta>0
such that for every
f,f^{\prime}\in\Theta
, with
h=f^{\prime}-f
satisfying
\|h\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s^{\prime}}}\leqslant\eta
, it holds

(2.5)

\begin{split}{\mathrm{T}}_{f^{\prime}}&={\mathrm{T}}_{f}+\sum_{J=1}^{J_{\max}}{\mathrm{T}}^{(J)}_{f}[h,\cdots,h]+R_{f}(h),\end{split}

where, for
J\in[J_{\max}]
,
{\mathrm{T}}^{(J)}_{f}
is an
s
-regular form with norm smaller than
L
and
|R_{f}(h)|\leqslant L\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}^{J_{\max}+1}
.

In the previous definition, the choice of
J_{\max}=2
or
J_{\max}=3
indicates where the Taylor expansion should be stopped to estimate the functional. If
s>d/4
, a cubic remainder of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{3s}{2s+d}})
is negligible in front of
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, so that stopping the Taylor expansion at
J_{\max}=2
is enough. If
s\leqslant d/4
, the cubic remainder is not negligible, and we must stop the Taylor expansion at
J_{\max}=3
to obtain a negligible remainder of order four.

We now describe a simple family of regular functionals.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
s>0
and let
\Theta\subset\mathscr{C}^{s}
be a set of functions taking values in an open interval
I
. Let
t=\max(s+3,4)
and let
\varphi:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a function in
\mathscr{C}^{t}
. Then,
\mathrm{T}:f\in\Theta\mapsto\int\varphi(f(x))\mathrm{d}x
is an
s
-regular functional over
\Theta
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Lemma

2.4
.
∎

The minimax estimation of integral functionals described in the previous lemma was analyzed by Laurent
[
45
,
46
]
(
s\geqslant 1/4
) and by Kerkyacharian and Picard
[
42
]
(
s\leqslant 1/4
).

We are now ready to state our main theorem:
s
-regular functionals can be estimated at rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, with efficient estimation when

.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
s>0
and
L>0
. Let
\mathrm{T}
be an
s
-regular functional with norm smaller than
L
, defined over a set
\Theta\subset\mathscr{C}^{s}
. Let
\Theta_{0}\subset\Theta
be a set such that
\Theta
is included in a
\eta
-neighborhood of
\Theta_{0}
for the
\mathscr{C}^{s^{\prime}}
-norm, where
\eta
and
s^{\prime}
are the constants appearing in
Definition

2.5
.

(i)

The functional
\mathrm{T}:\Theta_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
can be estimated at rate
n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
over
\mathscr{C}^{s}
.

(ii)

Moreover, assume that
f\in\Theta\mapsto{\mathrm{T}}_{f}^{(1)}
is continuous from
\Theta
endowed with the
\mathscr{C}^{s^{\prime}}
-norm to the set of linear forms on
\mathrm{L}^{2}
endowed with the operator norm and that
s>\frac{d}{4}
. Then, there exists an asymptotically efficient estimator of
{\mathrm{T}}_{f}
at all densities
f\in\Theta_{0}
, with efficient influence function
\psi_{f}
defined by
{\mathrm{T}}^{(1)}_{f}[u]=\int u\psi_{f}
for all
u\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
.

As already mentioned,
Theorem

2.7
covers the large class of integral functionals defined in
Lemma

2.6
. More importantly to us, it also covers the estimation of other types of functionals, such as eigenvalues of families of operators depending on a density

. An example of application to the estimation of eigenvalues of weighted Laplace operators is given in

.

The rest of this section is dedicated to proving
Theorem

2.7
. The proof is decomposed into four steps: first, we estimate regular
J
-multilinear forms for
J=1,2,3
, and then regular functionals. We now fix a density
f\in\Theta_{0}
with
\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant R
for some
R>0
(in particular,
\sup f\leqslant R
). We let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be an i.i.d. sample with density
f
, with
\mu_{n}
the associated empirical measure.

The estimators use as building blocks a family of symmetric kernel functions
K_{D}:M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
indexed by an integer
D
. These kernel functions give rise to linear operators
\pi_{D}:u\mapsto\int K_{D}(x,y)u(y)\mathrm{d}y
defined for any distribution of sufficiently small order. The exact way these kernels are constructed do not matter; instead, we list properties that they should satisfy.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
s_{\star}>0
be an integer. We say that a family of symmetric kernels
(K_{D})_{D\geqslant 1}
of class
\mathrm{C}^{s_{\star}}
over
M\times M
(with associated operators
(\pi_{D})_{D\geqslant 1}
) is admissible if for all
\ell,s\in\left[-s_{\star},s_{\star}\right]
with
\ell<s
and
1\leqslant p^{\prime}\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
and
1\leqslant q,q^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
, there exists
C_{\star}
such that for all
D\geqslant 1
:

(K1)

\|1-\pi_{D}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}\leqslant C_{\star}D^{-(s-\ell)}
;

(K2)

\|\pi_{D}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}}\leqslant C_{\star}
;

(K3)

for any integer
J\geqslant 1
,
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{J}\in[0,s_{\star}]
, integers
D_{1},\dots,D_{J}\geqslant 1
, and any multilinear form
G:\mathrm{H}^{\ell_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{H}^{\ell_{J}}\to\mathbb{R}
with Hilbert-Schmidt norm smaller than
L
,

\int|G[K_{D_{1}}(x_{1},\cdot),\dots,K_{D_{J}}(x_{J},\cdot)]|^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{J}\leqslant C_{\star}L^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{J}D_{j}^{2\ell_{j}}.

(K4)

\|y\mapsto\|K_{D}(\cdot,y)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leqslant C_{\star}\kappa_{p,d,\ell}(D)
, where
\kappa_{p,d,\ell}(D)
is equal to

if the exponent
\ell+d(1-1/p)
is positive,
\log(D)
if it is equal to zero, and
1
if it is negative.

We show in
Appendix

A
that admissible kernels, constructed using wavelets, exist. In the remainder of the proof, we fix such a family of admissible kernels with
s_{\star}\geqslant(s+d)/2
. The next lemma gives some properties of
s
-regular multilinear forms.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
s\in\left]0,s_{\star}\right]

and
L>0
. Let
J\in\{1,2,3\}
. There exist
C_{0}>0
such that for all symmetric
s
-regular
J
-multilinear forms
G
with norm smaller than
L
, the following properties hold.

(1)

Bias control.
For all
v_{1},\dots,v_{J}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
with
\mathscr{C}^{s}
-norm smaller than
1
and integers
D\geqslant 1
, it holds
|G[(1-\pi_{D})v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]|\leqslant C_{0}LD^{-2s}.

(2)

Variance control.
Let
1\leqslant j\leqslant J
and let
v_{j+1},\dots,v_{J}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
with
\mathscr{C}^{s}
-norm smaller than
1
. Let
\widetilde{s}=s
if
j=3
and
\widetilde{s}=0
otherwise. Then, for all integers
1\leqslant D_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant D_{j}
,

\int G[\pi_{D_{1}}\delta_{x_{1}},\dots,\pi_{D_{j}}\delta_{x_{j}},v_{j+1},\dots,v_{J}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{j}\leqslant C_{0}L^{2}D_{j}^{2\widetilde{s}}\prod_{k=1}^{j-1}D_{k}^{d}.

Before proving the lemma, let us state the following useful formula: for any
x\in M
and
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{s^{*}}
, by definition of the operator
\pi_{D}
,

(2.6)

\langle\pi_{D}\delta_{x},u\rangle=\langle K_{D}(x,\cdot),u\rangle=\pi_{D}u(x).

Proof.

By definition of
s
-regular forms, there is a
(J-1)
-multilinear form
\widetilde{G}:(\mathscr{C}^{s})^{J-1}\to\mathscr{C}^{s}
of norm smaller than
L
such that for all

,
v_{2},\dots,v_{J}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
, it holds that

.

(1)

Let

with

-norm smaller than

be an integer. By using

(see

\displaystyle|G[(1-\pi_{D})v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]|

\displaystyle=|\langle(1-\pi_{D})v_{1},\widetilde{G}[v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(1-\pi_{D})v_{1}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{-s}_{11}}\|\widetilde{G}[v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\star}LD^{-2s}.

(2)

Let

\displaystyle=\|\pi_{D_{1}}\widetilde{G}[v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\star}\|\widetilde{G}[v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}C_{\star}\|\widetilde{G}[v_{2},\dots,v_{J}]\|^{2}_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}C_{\star}L^{2}.

for some constant
C_{2}
depending on
s
.

Let

. Condition

implies that
\widetilde{G}:\mathrm{L}^{2}\times(\mathscr{C}^{s})^{J-2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}
has operator norm smaller than

. Thus, by
(K2)
and
(K4)
, using the symmetry of

\displaystyle\int G[\pi_{D_{1}}\delta_{x_{1}},\pi_{D_{2}}\delta_{x_{2}},v_{3}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle=\int\|\pi_{D_{2}}\widetilde{G}[\pi_{D_{1}}\delta_{x_{2}},v_{3}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{\star}\int\|\widetilde{G}[\pi_{D_{1}}\delta_{x_{2}},v_{3}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}C_{\star}L^{2}D_{1}^{d}

for some constant
C_{3}
.
The statement for

and the Hilbert-Schmidt condition in

.

We rely on
U
-statistics of order
J
to estimate
J
-multilinear forms. Let
J\geqslant 1
be an integer and let

be a symmetric function. The associated

-statistic is defined as

(2.7)

\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(J)}b=\binom{n}{J}^{-1}\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\cdots<i_{J}\leqslant n}b(X_{i_{1}},\dots,X_{i_{J}}).

We will focus on situations where

for some symmetric
J
-multilinear form
B
defined over signed measures. In that case, the Hoeffding decomposition of the

-statistic takes the particularly convenient form

(2.8)

\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(J)}b=B[f,\dots,f]+\sum_{j=1}^{J}\binom{J}{j}\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(j)}b_{j},

where
b_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})=B[\delta_{x_{1}}-f,\dots,\delta_{x_{j}}-f,f,\dots,f]
. The different
U
-statistics in the above decomposition are centered and orthogonal, with

(2.9)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(j)}b_{j})&=\binom{n}{j}^{-1}\mathbf{E}[b_{j}(X_{1},\dots,X_{j})^{2}]\\
&\leqslant R^{j}\binom{n}{j}^{-1}\int B[\delta_{x_{1}},\dots,\delta_{x_{j}},f,\dots,f]^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}x_{j}.\end{split}

We will rely on the variance control in
Lemma

2.9
to control variance terms of this form.

2.1.
Estimation of linear functionals

We first prove
Theorem

2.7
in the particular case where
\mathrm{T}
is a linear functional. In that case
\mathrm{T}
is an
s
-regular linear form which takes the form
\mathrm{T}:v\mapsto\int v\psi
for some
\psi\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
.
Define the estimator
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}=\mu_{n}(\psi)
. The estimator has no bias and has variance bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int(\psi(x)-\mathbf{E}[\psi(X)])^{2}f(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\int\psi(x)^{2}f(x)\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\frac{RL^{2}}{n}.

The estimator
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
is also asymptotically efficient by definition.

2.2.
Estimation of quadratic functionals

Assume now that
\mathrm{T}
is a quadratic functional. The functional
\mathrm{T}
is then equal to the second order term in its Taylor expansion. In particular,
\mathrm{T}
induces an
s
-regular bilinear form
G
, with
{\mathrm{T}}_{h}=G[h,h]
. Definition
(G2)
implies that
G[u,v]=\langle u,\widetilde{G}[v]\rangle
for an operator
\widetilde{G}
that sends both
\mathscr{C}^{s}
to
\mathscr{C}^{s}
and
\mathrm{L}^{2}
to
\mathrm{L}^{2}
with operator norm smaller than
L
. We let
D\geqslant 1
be an integer of order
n^{\frac{2}{4s+d}}
and define

. Let
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
be the associated
U
-statistic. Recall the Hoeffding decomposition (
2.8
).

First note that the bias of the estimator is bounded by

|G[f,f]-G[\pi_{D}f,\pi_{D}f]|\leqslant|G[(1-\pi_{D})f,f]|+|G[(1-\pi_{D})f,\pi_{D}f]|.

According to
Lemma

2.9
and
(K2)
, it is therefore bounded by

(2.10)

\begin{split}C_{0}LD^{-2s}(\|f\|^{2}_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}+\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\|\pi_{D}f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}})&\leqslant C_{0}(1+C_{\star})LD^{-2s}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\\
&\leqslant C_{1}LR^{2}n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}\end{split}

for some constant
C_{1}
.

By (
2.9
) and
Lemma

2.9
, the variance of
\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(2)}b_{2}
is bounded by

(2.11)

\binom{n}{2}^{-1}L^{2}R^{2}D^{d}\leqslant C_{2}L^{2}R^{2}n^{-\frac{8s}{4s+d}}

for some constant
C_{2}
.

Likewise, the variance of
\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(1)}b_{1}
is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(1)}b_{1}^{2}]\leqslant\frac{R}{n}\int G[\pi_{D}\delta_{x},\pi_{D}f]^{2}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\frac{C_{3}R}{n}\|\pi_{D}f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}^{2}.

for some constant
C_{3}
.
By
(K2)
,
\|\pi_{D}f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant C_{\star}\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant C_{\star}L
,
so that
\mathbf{E}[\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(1)}b_{1}^{2}]\leqslant C_{4}R^{3}L^{2}n^{-1}
for some constant
C_{4}
.

In total, the risk of
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
is of order
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}+n^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, up to a multiplicative constant depending on
L
and
R
.

Finally, assume that
s>d/4
. Let us show that
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
is an asymptotically efficient estimator. First, remark from (
2.1
) that the efficient influence function is the map
2\widetilde{G}[f]
. As
G[f,f]=\mathbf{E}[\widetilde{G}[f](X_{i})]
, we can decompose
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
into

(2.12)

{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f}=\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\widetilde{G}[f](X_{i})-\mathbf{E}[\widetilde{G}[f](X_{i})])+Z_{1},

where
Z_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle G[\pi_{D}f,\pi_{D}f]-G[f,f]+2\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(1)}b_{1}+\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(2)}b_{2}-\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\widetilde{G}[f](X_{i})-G[f,f]).

We write a bias-variance decomposition and use (
2.10
), (
2.11
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[Z_{1}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant|G[\pi_{D}f,\pi_{D}f]-G[f,f]|^{2}+2\mathbf{E}[\mathbf{U}_{n}^{(2)}b_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+2\mathbf{E}\left[\left(\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(G[\pi_{D}(\delta_{X_{i}}-f),\pi_{D}f]-\widetilde{G}[f](X_{i})+G[f,f])\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{5}L^{2}(R^{2}+R^{4})n^{-\frac{8s}{4s+d}}+\frac{8}{n}R\left\|\pi_{D}\widetilde{G}[\pi_{D}f]-\widetilde{G}[f]\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

for some constant
C_{5}
. We use
(K1)
,
(K2)
, and the mapping properties of
\widetilde{G}
stated at the beginning of the section to obtain that there are constants
C_{6},C_{7}
such that

\displaystyle\|\pi_{D}\widetilde{G}[\pi_{D}f]-\widetilde{G}[f]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{6}\|(1-\pi_{D})\widetilde{G}[\pi_{D}f]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}+\|\widetilde{G}[(1-\pi_{D})f]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{6}C_{\star}D^{-s}\|\widetilde{G}[\pi_{D}f]\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}+L\|(1-\pi_{D})f]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{6}C_{\star}LD^{-s}\|\pi_{D}f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}+C_{\star}L\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}D^{-s}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{7}LRD^{-s}.

In total, one sees that for some
C_{8}>0
we have

(2.13)

\mathbf{E}[Z_{1}^{2}]\leqslant C_{8}L^{2}(R^{2}+R^{4})\left(n^{-\frac{8s}{4s+d}}+n^{-1}n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}\right).

As
s>d/4
, this term is negligible in front of

. This shows that
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
is asymptotically efficient.

2.3.

In this section, we assume that

By assumption,

s

\displaystyle\frac{R}{n}\int G[\pi_{D_{p}}f,\pi_{D_{q}}f,\pi_{D_{r}}\delta_{x_{1}}]^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}^{6}]\lesssim(\log n_{2}/n_{2})^{-\frac{8(s-\varepsilon)}{2s+d}},

n_{2}^{-\frac{8s}{4s+d}}
for
\varepsilon
small enough.

To bound
\mathbf{E}[({\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f})^{2}\mathbf{1}\{E^{c}\}]
, we require a control on the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norm of the estimator. By assumption, the functional
\mathrm{T}
is bounded by
L
and
{\mathrm{T}}_{\hat{f}}^{(J)}
is continuous over
(\mathscr{C}^{s})^{*}\times(\mathscr{C}^{s})^{J-1}
with norm smaller than
L
. In particular,

|{\mathrm{T}}_{\hat{f}}^{(J)}[u_{1},\dots,u_{J}]|\leqslant L\|u_{1}\|_{(\mathscr{C}^{s})^{*}}\prod_{j=2}^{J}\|u_{j}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant L\prod_{j=1}^{J}\|u_{j}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}.

Given the expression of
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
and
(K4)
, as all integers
D_{j}
involved in the definitions of the estimators of multilinear forms in the three first steps are at most of order
D_{\max}=\mathcal{O}(n_{1}^{\frac{2}{4s+d}})=o(n_{1}^{2})
, the difference
|{\mathrm{T}}_{f}-{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}|
is bounded by an expression of the form:

(2.23)

\begin{split}C_{2}L(1+\sup_{x}\|\pi_{D_{\max}}\delta_{x}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}^{3})&\leqslant C_{2}L(1+C_{\star}\kappa_{\infty,d,s}(D_{\max})^{3})\\
&\leqslant C_{3}Ln_{1}^{3s+3d}\end{split}

for some constants
C_{2},C_{3}
.
Equation (
2.20
) therefore gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[({\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f})^{2}\mathbf{1}\{E^{c}\}]

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}^{2}L^{2}n_{1}^{6s+6d}\mathbf{P}(E^{c})\leqslant C_{4}L^{2}n_{1}^{6s+6d}n_{2}^{-2-6s-6d}.

We choose
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
of order
n
so that the last display is at most of order
n^{-2}\ll n^{-1}
. Thus, the term
\mathbf{E}[({\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f})^{2}\mathbf{1}\{E^{c}\}]
is negligible and the risk of
{\hat{\mathrm{T}}}
is of order
n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}
. This concludes the proof in the regime
s\leqslant d/4
.

The proof is similar (and simpler) in the regime

, so we skip the details. The only difference is that we use a Taylor expansion up to

As

.

It remains to show that efficient estimation is possible in the regime

. Let

be of order
n^{1-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
to fix, and let

. Define
\psi_{f}
as in the statement of
Theorem

and remark that the efficient influence function as defined in (

\psi_{f}
. We must therefore show that

(2.24)

Z_{2}={\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\psi_{f}(X_{i})-\mathbf{E}[\psi_{f}(X_{i})])

is negligible in front of
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. We have the inequality
\mathrm{Var}(\psi_{f}(X))\leqslant R\|\psi_{f}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant C_{5}R\|\psi_{f}\|^{2}_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant C_{5}RL^{2}
for some constant

. Using the

-bound (

and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we find that for all integers

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[|Z_{2}|\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\{E^{c}\}]\leqslant C_{3}Ln_{1}^{3s+3d}\mathbf{P}(E^{c})+L\sqrt{\frac{C_{5}R}{n}\mathbf{P}(E^{c})}

As

, for

, this quantity is negligible in front of

.

Let us now show that

. We have

\displaystyle({\hat{\mathrm{T}}}-{\mathrm{T}}_{f})\mathbf{1}\{E\}=(\hat{A}^{(1)}-A^{(1)}[f]+\hat{A}^{(2)}-A^{(2)}[f,f]+Z_{3})\mathbf{1}\{E\},

\varepsilon

At last,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}}

As

and

, the variance is negligible in front of

is an asymptotically efficient estimator.

Remark 2.11
.

It appears clearly in the last step that estimation is still possible if the norm

is allowed to
depend polynomially with respect to

and

such that for all

.
We will still call

-regular functionals such functionals

whose norm grows polynomially with

.
This generalization will be helpful to estimate the eigenvalue functional in

.

3.
Spectral estimation of weighted Laplace operators

We return in this section to the estimation of the spectrum of weighted Laplace operators
\Delta_{f}=\Delta+\alpha A_{V}
, where
V=\log f
and
A_{V}=\nabla V\cdot\nabla
. In particular, we are interested in the stability of the spectrum of
\Delta_{f}
under perturbations of
f
.

3.1.
Spectral theory of weighted Laplace operators

For any positive density
f\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
for
s>1
written as
f=e^{V}
, the operator
A_{V}
is bounded
\mathrm{H}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,s-1)
according to (
A3
) below. Hence,
A_{V}
is a compact operator
\mathrm{H}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}
.
It follows that
\Delta_{f}:\mathrm{H}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}
is a compact perturbation of
\Delta
, hence the spectrum of
\Delta_{f}
is also discrete. The resolvent

R_{f}(z)=(z+\Delta_{f})^{-1},\hskip 10.00002ptz\notin\sigma(-\Delta_{f})

defines a family of operators
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}
which is meromorphic in
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Moreover,
-\Delta_{f}
is self-adjoint on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(f^{\alpha})
and non-negative, hence
\sigma(-\Delta_{f})
consists in eigenvalues

0=\lambda_{0,f}<\lambda_{1,f}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant\lambda_{\ell,f}\to\infty.

Besides, we have

\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(f^{\alpha})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(f^{\alpha})}={\mathrm{dist}(z,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))}^{-1}

and since
\mathrm{L}^{2}(f^{\alpha})
and
\mathrm{L}^{2}
are equivalent with equivalence constant depending on
\alpha,\inf f
and
\sup f
, one obtains

(3.1)

\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant\frac{C}{\mathrm{dist}(z,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))},\hskip 10.00002ptz\notin\sigma(-\Delta_{f}),

where
C
depends on
\alpha,\inf f
and
\sup f
. In particular this implies that each eigenvalue is semi-simple (that is, there are no Jordan blocks), and for each
\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta_{f})
, we have

(3.2)

R_{f}(z)=S_{\lambda,f}(z)+\frac{\Pi_{\lambda,f}}{z-\lambda},

where
S_{\lambda,f}(z)
is holomorphic near
z=\lambda

and

\Pi_{\lambda,f}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Upsilon_{\lambda}}R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z

is the spectral projector on the eigenspace associated with
\lambda
. Here
\Upsilon_{\lambda}
is a small circle around
\lambda
enclosing no other eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{f}
. Note also that we have the relations

(3.3)

S_{\lambda,f}(z)\Pi_{\lambda,f}=\Pi_{\lambda,f}S_{\lambda,f}(z)=0.

3.2.
Resolvent estimates for weighted Laplace operators

We first control the operator norms of the operator
A_{V}
and of the resolvent of
\Delta_{f}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and let
1<p,q,r<\infty
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

(A1)

\displaystyle\|A_{V}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+t,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|V\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+|t|,r}},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{W}^{1+|t|,r},

(A2)

\displaystyle\|A_{V}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{1+t}\to\mathscr{C}^{t}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|V\|_{\mathscr{C}^{1+t}},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{C}^{1+t},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle>0.

If
p=q
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
, (
A1
) holds with the
\mathrm{W}^{1+|t|,r}
-norm replaced with the
\mathrm{C}^{1+|t|}
-norm.

Proof.

The fractional Sobolev space
\mathrm{W}^{t,p}
on
M
is equivalent to the Triebel–Lizorkin space
\mathrm{F}^{t,p}_{2}
which is defined through a resolution of unity
[
68
, Chapter 7]
. This implies that it suffices to treat the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We first prove (
A1
) for
t\geqslant 0
. Note that for
t\in\mathbb{N}
, by Leibniz rule and Hölder’s inequality,
\|uv\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,p}}\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,r}}
. The inequality is then extended to
t\geqslant 0
by complex interpolation
[
53
]
. Thus, for
\varphi\in\mathrm{W}^{1+t,p}
,

\displaystyle\|A_{V}\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla V\cdot\nabla\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}\lesssim\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,r}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,p}}\lesssim\|V\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+t,r}}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+t,p}}.

Equation (
A1
) for
t<0
is similarly obtained by using the dual definition of the
\mathrm{W}^{-t,q}
-norm. The case
p=q
is easily proven when
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Equation

A2
and
Equation

A3
follow from
\mathscr{C}^{t}
and
\mathrm{C}^{t}
being multiplication algebras, see
Appendix

A
. We leave details to the reader.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
t^{\prime}>|t|
and let

. Then, there exists
C>0
such that for all
V\in\mathscr{C}^{1+t^{\prime}}
,

(A3)

\displaystyle\|A_{V}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+t,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,p}}\leqslant C\|V\|_{\mathscr{C}^{1+t^{\prime}}},

Proof.

The previous proof applies in the case

to show that for

\|A_{V}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+t,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,p}}\leqslant C\|V\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1+t,\infty}},

with the norm

appearing in the upper bound, where

by definition. For

∎

Now, we turn to the estimation of the resolvent
R_{f}(z)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
s>1
,
t\in\left]-s,s-1\right[
,
q>1
and

L>0
. Then there are
C,N>0
such that for each
f=e^{V}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
with
\|V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\leqslant L
, one has

(3.4)

\left\|R_{f}(z)\right\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\to\mathrm{W}^{t+2,q}}\leqslant\frac{C(1+\|V\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}+|z|)^{N}}{\dist(z,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))},\hskip 10.00002ptz\notin\sigma(-\Delta_{f}).

The same bound holds for
t=-s
or
t=s-1
if
s
is an integer, with
\|V\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}
replaced by
\|V\|_{\mathrm{C}^{s}}
in the right-hand side. Moreover,
the bound also holds for
\left\|R_{f}(z)\right\|_{\mathscr{C}^{t}\to\mathscr{C}^{t+2}}
for
t\in\left[0,s-1\right]
.

Proof.

We write
V=\log f
. We first give a bound for the operator norm of
R_{f}(z)
seen as an operator
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}
. Let
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}
and
u=R_{f}(z)v
, so that
zu+\Delta u+\alpha\nabla V\cdot\nabla u=v
. We compute

\displaystyle\langle v,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Re}(z)\|u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\langle\Delta u,u\rangle+\alpha\langle\nabla V\cdot\nabla u,u\rangle.

So, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(3.5)

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant|z|\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\alpha\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}.

By (
3.1
), we have

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant\frac{C\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}}{\delta(z)}

where
C\geqslant 1
depends on
\inf f
and
\sup f
(which are controlled in terms of
\|V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}
) and
\delta(z)=\dist(z,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))
.
Thus one gets

(3.6)

\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant{C^{2}\delta(z)^{-2}|z|\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+C\delta(z)^{-1}\|v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}}+\alpha C\delta(z)^{-1}{\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}}.

The latter inequality implies

\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant C\delta(z)^{-1}(\sqrt{\delta(z)+|z|}+\alpha\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}})\|v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}.

This implies that
R_{f}(z):\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}
is continuous with the bound

(3.7)

\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}}\leqslant C\delta(z)^{-1}(\sqrt{\delta(z)+|z|}+\alpha\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}),

for some
C
depending on
\alpha,\inf f
and
\sup f
.

We now bound the norm of
{R_{f}(z):\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}}
. One has the identity

(3.8)

R_{f}(z)=-\Lambda^{-2}+\Lambda^{-2}(1+z+\alpha A_{V})R_{f}(z).

The operator
\Lambda^{-2}
is bounded
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}
with operator norm
1
. Moreover, since
V\in\mathrm{C}^{1}
,
A_{V}
is bounded
\mathrm{H}^{1}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}
by
Lemma

3.1
. Recalling (
3.7
) one obtains

(3.9)

\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}}\leqslant 1+\widetilde{C}\delta(z)^{-1}(1+|z|+\alpha\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}})(\sqrt{\delta(z)+|z|}+\alpha\|\nabla V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}})

for some
\widetilde{C}
depending on
\|V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}
.

From now on, we do not keep track of the constants and we only explain the boundedness properties of
R_{f}(z)
. We iterate the above process. For
t\in\left]0,1\right[
with
t<s-1
, by (
A3
),

\mathrm{L}^{2}\xrightarrow{R_{f}(z)}\mathrm{H}^{2}\xrightarrow{A_{V}}\mathrm{H}^{t}\xrightarrow{\Lambda^{-2}}\mathrm{H}^{t+2}

so that, as
\mathrm{H}^{t}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}
, the operator
\Lambda^{-2}(1+z+\alpha A_{V})R_{f}(z)
is bounded
\mathrm{H}^{t}\to\mathrm{H}^{t+2}
. So is
\Lambda^{-2}
. Thus, by (
3.8
),
R_{f}(z)
is bounded
\mathrm{H}^{t}\to\mathrm{H}^{t+2}
. This process can be iterated, proving that
R_{f}(z)
is bounded
\mathrm{H}^{t}\to\mathrm{H}^{t+2}
for all
t\in\left[0,s-1\right[
.

For negative values of
t
, we use the following identity between operators
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}

(3.10)

R_{f}(z)=-\Lambda^{-2}+R_{f}(z)(1+z+\alpha A_{V})\Lambda^{-2}.

For
t\in\left]-1,0\right]
, by (
A3
),

\mathrm{H}^{t}\xrightarrow{\Lambda^{-2}}\mathrm{H}^{t+2}\xrightarrow{A_{V}}\mathrm{L}^{2}\xrightarrow{R_{f}(z)}\mathrm{H}^{2}.

As

is written as the sum of two bounded operators

\mathrm{H}^{t}\to\mathrm{H}^{t+2}
. Once again, this process can be iterated using (
A3
), proving that
R_{f}(z)
is bounded
\mathrm{H}^{t}\to\mathrm{H}^{t+2}
for all
t\in\left]-s,0\right]
. This concludes the case

. If

and

.

Next, since
\Lambda^{-2}
is a pseudo-differential operator of order

\mathrm{W}^{t,q}
to
\mathrm{W}^{t+2,q}

[
64

. Hence we are left with analyzing

.
For any

and

, as

By the embedding
\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{q}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{2}
,
R_{f}(z)
maps
\mathrm{W}^{t,q}
in
\mathrm{W}^{t+1,q}
by (
3.8
) and injecting this again in (
3.8
), as
A_{V}:\mathrm{W}^{t+1,q}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,q}
by (
A3
), we get that for any
t\in[0,1)
with
t<s-1

R_{f}(z):\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\to\mathrm{W}^{t+2,q}.

Let
q_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}}-\frac{1}{d}\right)_{+}^{-1}
. Iterating the argument we obtain
R_{f}(z):\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\to\mathrm{W}^{t+2,q}
for each
2\leqslant q<q_{2}
and
t\in[0,1)
such that
t<s-1
. This defines a sequence
q_{1},q_{2},\dots
, with
q_{n}=+\infty
after
\lceil d/2\rceil
steps. Thus,
R_{f}(z):\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\to\mathrm{W}^{t+2,q}
for all
q\geqslant 2
.
Injecting this in (
3.8
) we obtain the same mapping property, if we assume
t\in\left[1,2\right[

and
t<s-1
. Again an induction argument allows to obtain the sought result for
t\in\left[0,s-1\right[
. The same property can be shown with the Hölder-Zygmund space
\mathscr{C}^{t}
for
t\in[0,s-1]
using (
A2
) and the fact that
\Lambda^{-2}
is bounded
\mathscr{C}^{t}\to\mathscr{C}^{t+2}
, see
[
64
, Eq. (8.11)]
.

For
t\in\left]-s,0\right]
, one proceeds similarly, using (
3.10
) instead of (
3.8
). Finally, recalling (
3.9
) and keeping track of the constants in the above development, one sees that (
3.4
) holds.
∎

3.3.
Perturbation theory for functions of weighted Laplace operators

The aim of this paragraph is to derive perturbative results for the estimation of a function of
\Delta_{f}
. For a fixed function

\chi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, define the operator
\chi(-\Delta_{f})
thanks to the spectral theory of
-\Delta_{f}
by

\chi(-\Delta_{f})u=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\chi(\lambda_{\ell,f})\langle\varphi_{\ell},u\rangle_{f}\varphi_{\ell},\hskip 10.00002ptu\in\mathrm{L}^{2},

where
(\varphi_{\ell})_{\ell\geqslant 0}
is an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{f}
.

The main result of this section is the following perturbation result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\delta>0
and
p,q,r>1
such that

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}.

Then there is
C,N>0
such that for any
L>0
,
f,h\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
and
\chi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying the bounds
\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}}
,
\|h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}}
,
\|\chi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}<L
,
\operatorname{supp}(\chi)\subset[-L,L]
,
\inf f,\inf h>\delta
and
\sup f,\sup h<\delta^{-1}
, there holds

\|\chi(-\Delta_{f})-\chi(-\Delta_{h})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}\leqslant C(1+L)^{N}\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,r}}.

Proof.

We write
f=e^{V}
and
h=e^{U}
.
The proof is based on the Helffer–Sjöstrand formula, which writes
[
23
]

(3.11)

\chi(-\Delta_{f})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\partial_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\chi}(z)R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\wedge\mathrm{d}z

for any quasi-analytic extension
\widetilde{\chi}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C})
of
\chi
, which means that
\widetilde{\chi}
is a function such that there exists an integer
N\geqslant 1
and
C>0
such that

(3.12)

|\partial_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\chi}(z)|\leqslant C|\operatorname{Im}z|^{N},\hskip 10.00002ptz\in\mathbb{C}.

In what follows we make the choice

\widetilde{\chi}(z)=\varrho(\operatorname{Im}z)\sum_{k=0}^{2}\frac{\chi^{(k)}(\operatorname{Re}z)}{k!}(i\operatorname{Im}z)^{k}

for some function
\varrho\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
which is equal to
1
near
0
and vanishes outside
[-1,1]
. It is immediate to check that (
3.12
) holds for
\widetilde{\chi}
with
N=2
, and that we have

(3.13)

|\partial_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\chi}(z)|\leqslant C_{0}|\operatorname{Im}z|^{2}

for some constant
C_{0}
depending on
L
and
\varrho
.
As

\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant C_{1}|\operatorname{Im}z|^{-1},\hskip 10.00002ptz\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}.

for some constant
C_{1}
(and the same control holds for
h
),
we may write

\chi(-\Delta_{f})-\chi(-\Delta_{h})=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\partial_{\bar{z}}\widetilde{\chi}(z)R_{f}(z)A_{U-V}R_{h}(z)\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\wedge\mathrm{d}z.

Recalling (
3.13
) one sees that the quantity
\|\chi(-\Delta_{f})-\chi(-\Delta_{h})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}
is bounded from above up to a multiplicative constant depending on
L
and
\alpha
by

\sup_{z\in\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{\chi}}|\operatorname{Im}z|^{2}\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-2,q}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}\|A_{U-V}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{3,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{-2,q}}\|R_{h}(z)\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{3,p}}.

Since
\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{\chi}\subset\operatorname{supp}\chi\times[-1,1]
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
is at distance at least
|\operatorname{Im}z|
from
\sigma(-\Delta_{f})
and
\sigma(-\Delta_{h})
,
Equation

3.4
implies that there is a constant
C_{3}
depending on
L,\alpha,p
and
q
such that

\sup_{z\in\operatorname{supp}\widetilde{\chi}}|\operatorname{Im}z|^{2}\|R_{f}(z)\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-2,q}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}\|R_{h}(z)\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{3,p}}\leqslant C_{2}.

According to (
A1
) and the fact that
A_{\varphi}\psi=A_{\psi}\varphi
,
\|A_{U-V}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{3,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{-2,q}}\lesssim\|U-V\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,r}}\lesssim\|f-g\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,r}}
because
f
and
g
are bounded from below and are of class
\mathrm{C}^{1}
. As it is clear from
Equation

3.4
that all constants appearing in the different estimates depend polynomially on
L
, this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Let
t\in[0,2]
. As
\|A_{U-V}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{3-t,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t-2,q}}\lesssim\|U-V\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t-1,r}}
according to (
A1
),
reproducing the proof shows that under the same hypothesis as in Proposition
3.4
, one has the estimate

\|\chi(-\Delta_{f})-\chi(-\Delta_{h})\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1-t,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}\leqslant C(1+L)^{N}\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t-1,r}}.

3.4.
Perturbation theory for spectral projectors

In this paragraph, we show some perturbation results about spectral projectors for
\Delta_{f}
as a direct application of the preceding section. Let
\Upsilon
be a simple loop in
\mathbb{C}
, with counterclockwise orientation, bounding a compact domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
. We assume that

\partial\Omega\cap\sigma(-\Delta_{f})=\emptyset.

Recall that the spectral projector
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
of
\Delta_{f}
associated with
\Upsilon
is given by

\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Upsilon}R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z,

so that recalling (
3.2
) one has

(3.14)

\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}=\sum_{\lambda}\Pi_{\lambda,f}.

Since
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}^{2}=\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
, (
3.4
) yields that
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
is bounded

(3.15)

\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}:\mathrm{W}^{t_{1},p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t_{2},q}

for any
p,q\in\left]1,+\infty\right[

and
t_{1},t_{2}\in\left]-s,s+1\right[
, with operator norm depending on
p,q,s,\sup_{z\in\Omega}|z|
,
\dist(\Upsilon,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))
,
\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}
and
\inf f
.
Moreover, using the boundedness of the resolvent over Hölder-Zygmund spaces, we find that for any
p\in\left]1,+\infty\right[
and
t\in\left]-s,s+1\right[

(3.16)

\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}:\mathrm{W}^{t_{1},p}\to\mathscr{C}^{s+1}

is also bounded.
We denote by
E_{\Upsilon,f}=\operatorname{ran}\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
the associated eigenspace, so that

E_{\Upsilon,f}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Omega}\operatorname{ker}(\lambda+\Delta_{f}).

If
h\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
also satisfies the assumptions above we set for
q\geqslant 2

D_{q}(\Pi_{\Upsilon,f},\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})=\left\|(1-\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}+\left\|(1-\Pi_{\Upsilon,f})\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}.

The number
D_{q}(\Pi_{\Upsilon,f},\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})
is a measure of the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-angle between the finite dimensional spaces
E_{\Upsilon,f}
and
E_{\Upsilon,h}
. Note that we took the operator norms
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}
but this is equivalent to taking norms
\mathrm{L}^{q}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}
by (
3.15
).
We have the following perturbation result, which is a consequence of Proposition
3.4
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
L,\delta>0
and
1<q<r<\infty
. Then there are
\eta>0
,
C,N>0
and
0<\nu\leqslant\delta
such that for each simple loop
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{C}
and
f\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
satisfying

(3.17)

\inf f>\delta,\hskip 10.00002pt\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<L,\hskip 10.00002pt\sup_{z\in\Upsilon}|\operatorname{Re}z|<L\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\dist(\Upsilon,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))>\delta,

and for any
h\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
with
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
, the spectral projector
\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}
is well-defined, has the same rank as
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
and satisfies
\dist(\Upsilon,\sigma(-\Delta_{h}))>\nu
. Let
\beta=\max(\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}},\|h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}})
. Then

D_{q}(\Pi_{\Upsilon,f},\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})\leqslant C(1+\beta)^{N}\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,r}}.

Proof.

First, remark that the spectrum of
\Delta_{f}
is included in
\mathbb{R}
, so that we can always make a deformation contour to assume that
\Upsilon
is contained in
\{|\operatorname{Im}z|\leqslant 1\}
without changing
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
, so that the following bounds only depend on
\sup_{z\in\Upsilon}|\operatorname{Re}z|
.

Write
f=e^{V}
and
h=e^{U}
.
Since
f\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
,
R_{f}(-1)
is bounded
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}
by (
3.4
). Hence for
u\in\mathrm{H}^{2}
we have

\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}\leqslant\|R_{f}(-1)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}}\|(-1+\Delta_{f})u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}.

Thus there is a constant
C>0
depending only on
\alpha
,
L
and
\delta
such that

(3.18)

\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}\leqslant C(\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+\|\Delta_{f}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}).

Now, according to
Lemma

3.1
, for
u\in\mathrm{H}^{2}
there holds

(3.19)

\|A_{U-V}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\lesssim\|U-V\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{2}}.

We make use of classical perturbation theory: the relative bound estimates (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), together with (
3.4
) and
[
41
, Theorem IV.3.3.18]
, imply that there are
\eta,C_{1}>0
depending on
\alpha
,
L
and
\delta

such that, provided that we have
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
, it holds that
\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}
is well-defined, has the same rank as
\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}
, and moreover

(3.20)

\|R_{h}(z)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant C_{1},\hskip 10.00002ptz\in\Upsilon.

Since
R_{h}(z)\varphi=(z-\lambda)^{-1}\varphi
if
\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta_{h})
and
\varphi
is an associated eigenfunction, (
3.20
) implies that
\dist(\Upsilon,\sigma(-\Delta_{h}))\geqslant C_{1}^{-1}
. We define
\nu=\min(C_{1}^{-1},\delta)/2
.

Denote by
\Omega
the open set bounded by
\Upsilon
. Then by what precedes, one sees that for any
h\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
with
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
, we have

\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}=\chi(-\Delta_{h})

where
\chi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is any smooth function equal to
1
on
\overline{\Omega}\cap\mathbb{R}
and equal to zero on the set
\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}~:~\dist(\lambda,\Omega)>\nu/2\}
. Remark that we can always make
\eta
smaller so that the condition
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
implies that
\inf h\geqslant\delta/2
. In particular, Proposition
3.4
implies that whenever
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
,

(3.21)

\|\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}-\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}\leqslant C_{2}(1+\beta)^{N}\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,r}}

where
p
is such that
1/p+1/r=1/q
(such a
p
exists since
r>q
). Here
C_{2}
and
N
depend on
\alpha
,
p
,
q
,
r
,
L
,
\delta
and
\|\chi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}
. However one can always chose
\chi
so that
\|\chi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{3}}
is a function of
\nu
.
We write

\displaystyle\left\|(1-\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}\leqslant\|\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}-\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\to\mathrm{L}^{q}}\|\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{W}^{1,p}}.

Recall now from (
3.15
) that
\|\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{W}^{1,p}}
is bounded. By symmetry, the second term defining
D_{q}(\Pi_{\Upsilon,f},\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})
is likewise bounded. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Taking Remark
3.5
into account, one gets, under the same hypothesis,

(3.22)

\|(1-\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})\Pi_{\Upsilon,f}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}+\|(1-\Pi_{\Upsilon,f})\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,q}}\leqslant C\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{t-1,r}}

for
t\in[0,1]
.
Equation

3.22
implies that the
\mathrm{W}^{t,q}
-norm between eigenfunctions is controlled by the
\mathrm{L}^{r}
-norm between densities. Together with
Appendix

C
, such a result can be seen to imply that eigenfunctions can be estimated at rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-\frac{s+1-t}{2s+d}})
for
t\in[0,1]
and
d\geqslant 3
, if the
\mathrm{W}^{t,q}
-norm is used as a loss function instead of the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-norm. For
t=1
, we obtain the same rates of estimation as
[
70
]
, with the key difference that their estimators are given by the eigenvectors of a graph Laplacian matrix whereas our risk bounds are stated for plug-in estimators on a fixed manifold

.

Moreover, remark that we have
\mathrm{W}^{t,q}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
whenever
tq>d
(see
Appendix

). Hence, we immediately get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
r>d/\varepsilon
and
L,\delta>0
. Let
\eta
be the constant in
Proposition

3.6
. Then there are

such that for every simple loop

and

satisfying

\inf f>\delta,\hskip 10.00002pt\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<L,\hskip 10.00002pt\sup_{z\in\Upsilon}|\operatorname{Re}z|<L\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\dist(\Upsilon,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))>\delta,

with
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
, if
\beta=\max(\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}},\|h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}})
,
we have the bound

This estimate in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
-norm will allow us to obtain rates of convergence for the

\mathrm{L}^{2}
-loss.

3.5.
Upper bound for the estimation of eigenvectors

In this section, we combine results obtained concerning the perturbation theory of eigenprojectors to prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
s\geqslant 2
,
L,\delta>0
and
r>q\geqslant 2
. Let
\Upsilon
be a curve as in

1.1

\Theta_{0}=\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
. Let
\eta
,

and

be the constants of

3.6

\alpha
,
q
,

,
\delta
and
L
.

We let

be the set of functions

with

3.6

-neighborhood of

for the

\Theta
. In particular, the

, for any small number

be the rate appearing in

, we can design an estimator

of a density

3.6.
Asymptotic expansion of eigenvalues

In this paragraph we state a result which implies that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k,f}
s of
-\Delta_{f}
define
s
-regular functionals in the sense of
Definition

2.5
. To take into account multiplicity, we must rather consider the average of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{f}
within a contour
\Upsilon
whose boundary is at a controlled distance from the spectrum.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
L,\delta>0
and
s\geqslant 2
. Let
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{C}
be a simple loop bounding a domain
\Omega
satisfying

\sup_{z\in\Upsilon}|\operatorname{Re}z|<L.

The map
f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)\mapsto\mu_{\Upsilon,f}
(defined in (
1.6
))
is an
s
-regular functional with norm depending on
L
,
\delta
and
s
. Furthermore, the first differential
f\mapsto\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}
is continuous from
\mathrm{C}^{1}
to the set of linear forms on
\mathrm{L}^{2}
endowed with the operator norm.

Proof of

\displaystyle=\alpha R_{h}(z)A_{u}R_{h}(z)A_{U-V}R_{f}(z)

\displaystyle+\alpha R_{h}(z)A_{U-V}R_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z).

Proof of

.

We will estimate

3.33

Claim 3.13
.

There exists
C_{j}>0

First, we remark that by an integration by parts the right-hand side of (

\displaystyle-\left\langle{\left(A_{\overline{\psi}_{2}}R_{f}(z)+\overline{\psi}_{2}(1-zR_{f}(z))\right)\prod_{k=2}^{j}B_{z}[u_{k}]\psi_{1},u_{1}}\right\rangle_{f}

In particular, for

3.1

Proposition

3.3
.
For

The first term.
The symmetrization of the second term is also equal to the symmetrization of

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl\langle{(u_{1}B_{z}[u_{2}]+u_{2}B_{z}[u_{1}])\prod_{k=3}^{j}B_{z}[u_{k}]\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}\Bigr\rangle_{f}=-\frac{1}{2}\Bigl\langle{B_{z}[u_{1}u_{2}]\prod_{k=3}^{j}B_{z}[u_{k}]\psi_{1},\psi_{2}}\Bigr\rangle_{f}.

The second term.

\displaystyle\kappa=\left\langle{\left(A_{\overline{\psi}_{2}}R_{f}(z)-z\overline{\psi}_{2}R_{f}(z)\right)\prod_{k=2}^{j}B_{z}[u_{k}]\psi_{1},u_{1}}\right\rangle_{f}=\left\langle{\prod_{k=2}^{j}B_{z}[u_{k}]\psi_{1},\widetilde{\psi}_{2}}\right\rangle_{f},

where

3.1
and

3.3

4.
Minimax lower bounds

In this section, we provide minimax lower bounds for eigenfuction and eigenvalue estimations, that are the contents of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.

4.1.
Minimax lower bound for eigenfunction estimation

The minimax lower bound for eigenfunction estimation relies on a reverse stability bound on the angle between eigenspaces, which shows that the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-angle between eigenspaces is larger that the
\mathrm{H}^{-1}
-norms between the associated densities, modulo error terms involving weaker norms.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
L>0
,
r\in\left]\frac{2d}{d+2},2\right[
and
\delta>0
. Let
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{C}
be a simple loop and a smooth positive function
f
such that

(4.1)

\sup_{z\in\Upsilon}|\operatorname{Re}z|\leqslant L\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\dist(\Upsilon,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))\geqslant\delta.

Assume also that there is a positive eigenvalue
\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta_{f})
which is enclosed in
\Upsilon
, let
\varphi
be an associated eigenfunction and take
x_{\star}\in M
such that
\nabla\varphi(x_{\star})\neq 0.

Then there are
\rho,c,C,\eta>0
and a chart
\Psi:B(x_{\star},\rho)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the following properties. For any
h\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
with
\inf h\geqslant\delta
,
\|h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}}\leqslant L
and
\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}<\eta
, the spectral projector
\Pi_{\Upsilon,h}
is well defined. If moreover

\supp\log(h/f)\subset B(x_{\star},\rho)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\log(h/f)\circ\Psi^{-1}\right]\right| \hskip 10.00002pt\text{is radial},

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we have the estimate

D_{2}(\Pi_{\Upsilon,f},\Pi_{\Upsilon,h})\geqslant c\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}}-C\|f-h\|_{\mathrm{W}^{-1,r}}.

Remark 4.2
.

We will use the above result for families of functions of the form
(h_{\varepsilon,y})
, with

h_{\varepsilon,y}(x)=f(x)\left(1+\varepsilon^{s}\chi\left(\frac{|\Psi(x)-\Psi(y)|}{\varepsilon}\right)\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon>0,\hskip 10.00002ptx,y\in B(x_{\star},\rho),

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
is a a radial function such that
\chi\equiv 1
near
0
and
\Psi:B(x_{\star},\rho)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a chart centered at
x_{\star}
. Then for every
y
we have

|\mathcal{F}\Psi_{*}\log(h_{\varepsilon,y}/f)|=|\mathcal{F}\Psi_{*}\log(h_{\varepsilon,x_{\star}}/f)|,

where
\Psi_{*}u=u\circ\Psi^{-1}
. However
|\mathcal{F}\Psi_{*}\log(h_{\varepsilon,x_{\star}}/f)|
is radial, since

\Psi_{*}\log(h_{\varepsilon,x_{\star}}/f)(\bar{x})=\log\left(1+\varepsilon^{s}\chi\left(\frac{\bar{x}}{\varepsilon}\right)\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Before proving Proposition
4.1
, we need a preliminary result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Z
be a
\mathrm{C}^{2}
vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
Z(0)\neq 0
. Then there are
\rho,C>0
such that for any function
a\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported in
B(0,\rho)
and such that
|\mathcal{F}a|
is radially symmetric we have the estimate

\|a\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C\|Za\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+C\|a\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Up to taking
\rho
small enough and using a change of coordinates of class
\mathrm{C}^{2}
(which preserves
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
hence
\mathrm{H}^{-2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
), we can assume that
Z=\partial_{1}

\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}=|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}(x)=\exp i\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot x\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in M.

Then
(\varphi_{\mathbf{k}})_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is an ortho-normal basis of
\mathrm{L}^{2}
and we have

-\Delta_{f}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}=\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

so that
\sigma(-\Delta_{f})=\{\lambda_{\mathbf{k}}~:~\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
. As the family
(\kappa_{1}^{-2},\dots,\kappa_{d}^{-2})
is
\mathbb{Q}
-free, all these eigenvalues are pairwise distinct.

Let
N,I\geqslant 1
be integers.
Define a grid
(x_{j})_{j}
indexed by
\mathbf{j}=(j_{1},\dots,j_{d})\in[N]^{d}
, with
x_{\mathbf{j}}=N^{-1}(j_{1}\kappa_{1},\dots,j_{d}\kappa_{d})
. Let
\varepsilon\in\left]0,1\right]
and
t=\varepsilon N
. Let
\rho>0
small so that the ball
B(0,\rho)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is embedded in
M
through the canonical projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to M
(for example take any
\rho<\min_{j}\kappa_{j}/2
). Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function supported on
B(0,\rho)
, and set

\chi_{\mathbf{j}}(x)=\varepsilon^{s}\,\widetilde{\chi}\left(\frac{x-x_{\mathbf{j}}}{\varepsilon}\right),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in M,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{j}\in[N]^{d},

where
\widetilde{\chi}:M\to\mathbb{R}
is the smooth function supported in
\pi(B(0,\rho))
induced by
\chi
, that is

\widetilde{\chi}(x)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\chi(\bar{x}+(2\pi)^{-1}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}),\hskip 10.00002ptx=\pi(\bar{x}),\hskip 10.00002pt\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

We will choose
\chi
such that

(4.20)

\|\chi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\leqslant 1/2\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{\xi}^{\mathbf{m}}\mathcal{F}\chi(0)=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002pt|\mathbf{m}|\leqslant I,

where we recall that

|\mathbf{m}|=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{\xi}^{\mathbf{m}}=\frac{\partial^{m_{1}}}{\partial\xi_{1}}\cdots\frac{\partial^{m_{d}}}{\partial\xi_{d}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}.

In particular, one has

\int_{M}\widetilde{\chi}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\chi(x)\mathrm{d}x=\mathcal{F}\chi(0)=0

so that

(4.21)

\int_{M}\chi_{\mathbf{j}}(x)\mathrm{d}x=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{j}\in[N]^{d}.

Next, for any
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{d})\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
, we set

(4.22)

f_{\tau}=1+w_{\tau}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002ptw_{\tau}=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in[N]^{d}}\tau_{\mathbf{j}}\chi_{\mathbf{j}}.

Remark that as long as
t<\min_{i=1,\dots,d}\kappa_{i}/2
, the supports of the
\chi_{j}
’s are pairwise distinct. This implies that
\|w_{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\leqslant\varepsilon^{s}\|\chi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}
. One can also check that
\|f_{\tau}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\lesssim\|f_{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{s}}
is bounded by a constant depending only on
\chi
. In particular, for
\varepsilon
small enough, all the functions
f_{\tau}
are bounded from below by a constant depending only on
\chi
.

Next, fix
\mathbf{k}_{\star}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and put
\lambda_{\star}=\lambda_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}>0
. Let
2\delta>0
be the spectral gap of
-\Delta_{f}
at
\lambda_{\star}
, and let
\Upsilon
be the circle of radius
\delta
centered at
\lambda_{\star}
, oriented in the counterclockwise direction. Then, by
Proposition

3.6
, provided that
\varepsilon
is small enough, we have that
\Upsilon
encloses exactly one eigenvalue
\lambda_{\tau}
of
-\Delta_{f_{\tau}}
for each
\tau.
As
\|w_{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}=O(\varepsilon^{s})
, we have according to
Proposition

3.11
that

(4.23)

\lambda_{\tau}-\lambda_{\star}=\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}[w_{\tau}]+\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}[w_{\tau},w_{\tau}]+O(\varepsilon^{3s}),

where
\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}
and
\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}
are forms that can be made explicit, as indicated in the proof of
Proposition

3.11
.
Let
\Pi
be the orthogonal projection on the eigenspace associated with
\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}
. According to the proof of
Proposition

3.11
we have for any smooth function
u
,

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}[u]=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi i}\operatorname{tr}\int_{\Upsilon}zR_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Using the residue theorem, the cyclicity of the trace, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), one sees that

(4.24)

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}[u]=-\alpha\operatorname{tr}(\Pi A_{u}\Pi)=-\alpha\langle A_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}},\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}\rangle.

Note that
A_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=(\nabla u\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}})\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}
, hence

-\langle A_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}},\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}\rangle=-\int_{M}\nabla u\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=0,

where the last equality comes from an integration by parts. For the second order term, one obtains

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}[u]=-\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}[u^{2}/2]+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\pi i}\operatorname{tr}\int_{\Upsilon}zR_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

By what precedes the term
\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(1)}[u^{2}/2]
is equal to zero. For the second one, we proceed as follows. First, let
B_{\eta}=\Delta_{f}+\eta A_{u}
for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}.
Then for small
\eta
, we have that
\Upsilon
encloses exactly one eigenvalue of
-B_{\eta}
, hence
\operatorname{tr}\int_{\Upsilon}(z+B_{\eta})^{-1}\mathrm{d}z=2\pi i
. Differentiating twice with respect to
\eta
one gets

0=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\eta^{2}}\right|_{\eta=0}\operatorname{tr}\int_{\Upsilon}(z+B_{\eta})^{-1}\mathrm{d}z=2\operatorname{tr}\int_{\Upsilon}R_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Therefore one can write

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}[u,u]=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2\pi i}\operatorname{tr}\int_{\Upsilon}(z-\lambda_{\star})R_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)A_{u}R_{f}(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Again using the residue theorem, the cyclicity of the trace, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) one gets

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}[u,u]=\operatorname{tr}\Pi A_{u}SA_{u}\Pi,

where
S=S_{\lambda_{\star},f}(\lambda_{\star})
with the notations of (
3.2
). The latter operator is diagonal in the basis
(\varphi_{\mathbf{k}})
: it satisfies
S\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=0
and

S\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}=\frac{\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}-|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbf{k}\neq\mathbf{k}_{\star}.

Next, let us write

u=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002ptu_{\mathbf{k}}=\int u(x)e^{-i\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot x}\mathrm{d}x.

Then
\nabla u=i\sum_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}}
and
\nabla\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=i\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}
hence

A_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})u_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})u_{\mathbf{k}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}.

Therefore one gets

SA_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\frac{(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})u_{\mathbf{k}}}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}-|\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}},

which gives in turn

A_{u}SA_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}=-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\sum_{\ell}\frac{(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})(\omega_{\ell}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}})u_{\mathbf{k}}u_{\ell}}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}-|\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}}\varphi_{\ell+\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}.

Note that if
u
is real one has
u_{-\mathbf{k}}=\overline{u_{\mathbf{k}}}
. Hence taking the scalar product with
\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}
yields

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}[u,u]=\operatorname{tr}\Pi A_{u}SA_{u}\Pi=\langle\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}},A_{u}SA_{u}\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\frac{(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}})}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}-|\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}}|u_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}.

Indeed
\langle\varphi_{\ell+\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}},\varphi_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}\rangle=0
unless
\ell=-\mathbf{k}
. Next, note that

\frac{1}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}-|\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}+2\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}}=\frac{1}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}}-\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}(|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}+2\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})}.

Hence we can write

\frac{(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}})}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}-|\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}|^{2}}=(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})+\gamma(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})+\delta(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})

where we set

\displaystyle\gamma(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})^{2}}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}}\ \text{ and }\ \delta(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})=-\frac{2\gamma(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}}{|\omega_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}+2\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}}}.

In particular, as
|u_{-\mathbf{k}}|=|u_{\mathbf{k}}|
, one gets by symmetry that

\mu_{\Upsilon,f}^{(2)}[u,u]=P[u,u]+Q[u,u]

where we set

P[u,u]=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\gamma(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})|u_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptQ[u,u]=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\delta(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})|u_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}.

Note that there is
C>0
such that

|\delta(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})|\leqslant{C}{|\mathbf{k}|^{-1}}

for each
\mathbf{k}\neq 0
.
In particular, one has

(4.25)

|Q[u,u]|\leqslant C\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\frac{|u_{\mathbf{k}}|^{2}}{|\mathbf{k}|}\leqslant C\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}}^{2}.

Since the
\chi_{\mathbf{j}}
’s have distinct support, we get for
v\in\mathrm{C}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\langle\omega_{\tau},v\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf{j}}\tau_{\mathbf{j}}\langle\chi_{\mathbf{j}},v\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf{j}}\tau_{\mathbf{j}}\langle\chi_{\mathbf{j}},v-v(x_{\mathbf{j}})\rangle\leqslant CN^{d}\varepsilon^{s+d+1/2}=Ct^{d}\varepsilon^{s+1/2}.

Moreover, we also get
\|\omega_{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant C^{\prime}t^{d/2}\varepsilon^{s}
for some other constant
C^{\prime}
.
As the
\alpha
-interpolation of the dual of
\mathscr{C}^{1/2}
and
\mathrm{L}^{2}
is continuously embedded into
\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}
for
\alpha
close enough to
0
, we get

(4.26)

\|\omega_{\tau}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1/2}}\leqslant C^{\prime\prime}t^{d(1/2+\alpha)}\varepsilon^{s+\alpha/2}.

Next, we wish to give a lower bound for
P[w_{\tau},w_{\tau}]
. Let us write

(4.27)

P[w_{\tau},w_{\tau}]=\sum_{\mathbf{j}}P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}}]+\sum_{\mathbf{j}\neq\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\tau_{\mathbf{j}}\tau_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}]

where
P[u,v]
is the sesquilinear form associated with the quadratic form
P[u,u]
. Note that for each
\mathbf{k}
one has

(\chi_{\mathbf{j}})_{\mathbf{k}}=\int_{M}\chi_{\mathbf{j}}(x)e^{-i\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot x}\mathrm{d}x=\varepsilon^{s+d}e^{i\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot x_{\mathbf{j}}}\mathcal{F}\chi(\omega_{\varepsilon\mathbf{k}}).

Therefore one obtains

P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}}]=\varepsilon^{2s+2d}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\gamma(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})|\mathcal{F}\chi(\omega_{\varepsilon\mathbf{k}})|^{2}=\varepsilon^{2s+d}\varepsilon^{-d}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\varrho(\varepsilon\mathbf{k})

where
\varrho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\varrho(\xi)=\zeta(\xi)|\mathcal{F}\chi(\omega_{\xi})|^{2}
, with
\zeta(0)=0
and

\zeta(\xi)=-\frac{(\omega_{\xi}\cdot\omega_{\mathbf{k}_{\star}})^{2}}{|\omega_{\xi}|^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\xi\neq 0.

The function
\varrho
is smooth and rapidly decreasing on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
(since
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
) and continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Hence it is Riemann integrable and we have

\varepsilon^{-d}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0} \varrho(\varepsilon\mathbf{k})=\varepsilon^{-d}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}} \varrho(\varepsilon\mathbf{k})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varrho(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

Now it is always possible to choose
\chi
so that the value
c
of the latter integral is positive. Indeed, there is an even nonnegative function
\nu_{0}
such that

\int\zeta(\xi)\nu_{0}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi>0

is non zero (take
\nu_{0}=-\zeta
for instance). Such a function can be written as
\xi\mapsto|\mathcal{F}{\chi_{0}}(\omega_{\xi})|^{2}
for some real-valued function
\chi_{0}
which does not have compact support. By continuity of the integral, we can approximate
\chi_{0}
by a compactly supported function
\chi
without changing too much the value of the integral, ensuring that
\int\rho(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi=\int\zeta(\xi)|\mathcal{F}{\chi}(\omega_{\xi})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi>0
. This is our final choice of
\chi
.

Hence for
\varepsilon
small, one obtains

(4.28)

\sum_{\mathbf{j}}P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}}]\geqslant cN\varepsilon^{2s+d}\geqslant ct^{d}\varepsilon^{2s}

for some constant
c>0
.
Next, for
\mathbf{j}\neq\mathbf{j}^{\prime}
, let us write

\displaystyle P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\gamma(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}_{\star})(\chi_{\mathbf{j}})_{\mathbf{k}}\overline{(\chi_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}})_{\mathbf{k}}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2s+2d}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\neq 0}\varrho(\varepsilon\mathbf{k})e^{i\omega_{\mathbf{k}}\cdot(x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}}^{\prime})}.

By Poisson’s summation formula, we have

P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}]=\varepsilon^{2s+d}\sum_{\ell}\varrho\left(\frac{x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}+\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}=(\kappa_{1}\ell_{1},\dots,\kappa_{d}\ell_{d}).

Remark that
x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}\in\prod_{i=1}^{d}[-\kappa_{i},\kappa_{i}]
. Thus, if
\ell\not\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}
, then
|x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}-\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}|\geqslant\delta|\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}|
for some
\delta>0
depending on
(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{d})
. As the function
\varrho
is rapidly decreasing, there is
K>0
such that

|\varrho(\xi)|\leqslant K(1+|\xi|)^{-d-1},\hskip 10.00002pt\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Thus, by a standard integral comparison

\sum_{\ell\notin\{-1,0,1\}^{d}}\varrho\left(\frac{x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}+\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\right)\leqslant\sum_{\ell\notin\{-1,0,1\}^{d}}K\left(1+\frac{\delta|\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-d-1}\leqslant KC_{0}\varepsilon^{d}

for some constant
C_{0}
depending on
(\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{d})
. We therefore obtain that for a fixed
\mathbf{j}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{j}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{j}}P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle\leqslant KC_{0}\varepsilon^{2s+2d}N^{d}+\varepsilon^{2s+d}\sum_{\ell\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{j}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{j}}\varrho\left(\frac{x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}+\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant KC_{0}\varepsilon^{2s+d}t^{d}+K\varepsilon^{2s+d}\sum_{\ell\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}}\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{j}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{j}}\left(1+\frac{|x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}+\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-d-1}.

By another comparison with an integral, we bound

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{j}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{j}}\left(1+\frac{|x_{\mathbf{j}}-x_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}+\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-d-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}N^{d}\int_{x\in[-1,1]^{d}}\left(1+\frac{|x+\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}|}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-d-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{2}N^{d}\varepsilon^{d}

for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}
. Thus, as
N\varepsilon=t
,

\sum_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}:\ \mathbf{j}^{\prime}\neq\mathbf{j}}P[\chi_{\mathbf{j}},\chi_{\mathbf{j}^{\prime}}]\leqslant K(C_{0}+3^{d}C_{1}C_{2})\varepsilon^{2s+d}t^{d}.

From this inequality, (
4.27
) and (
4.28
), we get that

P[w_{\tau},w_{\tau}]\geqslant\frac{ct^{d}}{2}\varepsilon^{2s}-K(C_{0}+3^{d}C_{1}C_{2})\varepsilon^{2s+d}N^{d}t^{d}=\left(\frac{c}{2}-K(C_{0}+3^{d}C_{1}C_{2})t^{d}\right)\varepsilon^{2s}t^{d}.

Choose
t
small enough so that
\left(\frac{c}{2}-K(C_{0}+3^{d}C_{1}C_{2})t^{d}\right)\geqslant\frac{c}{4}
. For such a choice of
t
, we have by (
4.25
) and (
4.26
) that

\mu^{(2)}_{\Upsilon,f}[w_{\tau},w_{\tau}]\geqslant\frac{c}{4}t^{d}\varepsilon^{2s}-o(\varepsilon^{2s}).

From the Taylor expansion (
4.23
), we are now in position to apply the minimax lower bound given by
Lemma

4.4
with
\beta
of order
\varepsilon^{2s}
,
m
of order
\varepsilon^{-d}
, and
a
of order
\varepsilon^{2s+d}
. For
\varepsilon
of order
n^{-\frac{2}{4s+d}}
, the minimax lower bound is of order
n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}
.

It remains to show that the minimax rate is also at least of order
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. The argument relies on Le Cam’s lemma. Let
f\in\mathcal{P}_{s}(\Upsilon,\delta,L)
for some curve
\Upsilon
, such that the domain bounded by
\Upsilon
contains a simple eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{f}
.
According to
Proposition

3.11
, for
h\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
with
\int h=0
and
n
large enough, it holds that

(4.29)

\mu_{\Upsilon,f+n^{-\frac{1}{2}}h}-\mu_{\Upsilon,f}=n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{(1)}_{\Upsilon,f}[h]+n^{-1}O(\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}^{2}).

As long as
\mu^{(1)}_{\Upsilon,f}\neq 0
, one can therefore choose
h
with

|\mu_{\Upsilon,f+n^{-\frac{1}{2}}h}-\mu_{\Upsilon,f}|\gtrsim n^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore, the Hellinger distance between
f
and
f+n^{-\frac{1}{2}}h
is of order
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. By Le Cam’s lemma, this implies that the minimax risk is at least of order
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. The only nontrivial point in this construction is the existence of a density
f
such that
\mu^{(1)}_{\Upsilon,f}\neq 0
. Indeed, the differential
\mu^{(1)}_{\Upsilon,f_{0}}
vanishes when
f_{0}
is the uniform density, as was already remarked in (
4.24
). However, when proving that the minimax rate was at least of order
n^{-\frac{4s}{4s+d}}
, we constructed a family of densities
f_{\tau}
with
\mu_{\Upsilon,f_{\tau}}-\mu_{\Upsilon,f_{0}}
that is nonzero. This implies that the functional
\mu_{\Upsilon}
is not constant on a neighborhood of
f_{0}
, and therefore that there is a density
f
close to
f_{0}
with a nonzero differential. This concludes the proof.

Appendix A
Functional spaces

In this section we recall useful definitions and properties of classical functional spaces. In the sequel,
M
is a smooth, closed Riemannian manifold.

A.1.
Multi-resolution analysis

Let
S\in\mathbb{N}
. Following
[
69
, Proposition 1.53 and Theorem 1.61(ii)]
(see also the introduction to wavelets in
[
31
, Section 4.2]
), we consider a family of Daubechies wavelets of regularity
S
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as follows. There are countable sets

\Psi_{j}=\left\{\psi_{j,\lambda}~:~\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}\right\}\subset\mathrm{C}_{c}^{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\hskip 10.00002ptj\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0},

of compactly supported
\mathrm{C}^{S}
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that the following holds.

(i)

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded set, then there is
C_{\Omega}>0
such that

\sharp\left\{\psi\in\Psi_{j}~:~\supp\psi_{j}\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset\right\}\leqslant C_{\Omega}2^{jd},\hskip 10.00002ptj\geqslant 0.

(ii)

There is
C>0
such that

\|\psi_{j,\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C2^{j(S+1/2)},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},\hskip 10.00002ptj\geqslant 0.

(iii)

For
j\geqslant 0
and
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have
\psi\in\Lambda_{j}
if and only if
\psi(\cdot-\mathbf{k})\in\Lambda_{j}
. If moreover
j\geqslant 1
we also have
\psi\in\Lambda_{j}
if and only if

2^{d/2}\psi(2\,\cdot)\in\Lambda_{j+1}.

(iv)

The family
\left\{\psi_{j,\lambda}~:~\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},~j\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0}\right\}
is an orthonormal family of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We will denote
\Psi=\bigcup_{j}\Psi_{j}
.

A.2.
Besov spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
. Given any sequence
a=(a_{j,\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},j\geqslant 0}\in\mathbb{C}^{\Psi}
, we define

\|a\|_{\mathrm{b}^{t}_{p,q}}=\left(\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{jq(t+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}|a_{j,\lambda}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

with usual modifications if
p
or
q
is equal to
\infty
.
Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of order
S
, that are, distributions
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for each compact set
\Omega
, there is
C_{\Omega}>0
such that

|\langle f,\varphi\rangle|\leqslant C_{\Omega}\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{C}^{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\hskip 10.00002pt\varphi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\hskip 10.00002pt\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset\Omega.

Any
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
gives rise to a sequence
a_{\Psi}(f)=(a_{\Psi}(f)_{j,\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},j\geqslant 0}\in\mathbb{C}^{\Psi}
given by

a_{\Psi}(f)_{j,\lambda}=\langle f,\psi_{j,\lambda}\rangle,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},\hskip 10.00002ptj\geqslant 0.

For any
|t|<S
, the space
\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as

\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})~:~\|a_{\Psi}(f)\|_{b^{t}_{p,q}}<\infty\right\}.

It is endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|a_{\Psi}(f)\|_{\mathrm{b}^{t}_{p,q}},\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

By
[
69
, Theorem 1.64]
(see also
[
31
, Section 4.3]
) the set
\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
does not depend on the chosen family
\Psi
and coincides with the standard Besov spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see
[
67
, §2.3.1]
). Remark that by construction,

(A.1)

\|\psi_{j,\lambda}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{t}_{pq}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=2^{j(t+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},\hskip 10.00002ptj\geqslant 0.

A.3.
Besov spaces on manifolds

If
M
is a compact smooth manifold, we define Besov spaces through a system of charts. Namely, let
U_{1},\dots,U_{N}
be a finite cover of
M
by open sets diffeomorphic to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, through diffeomorphisms
\kappa_{i}:\mathbb{B}^{d}\to U_{i}.
Consider a smooth partition of unity
(\chi_{i})_{i=1,\dots,N}
subordinated to this cover, such that
\chi_{i}=\zeta_{i}^{2}
for some
\zeta_{i}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(U_{i})
, so that

(A.2)

1=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\zeta_{i}^{2}.

We also take
\eta_{i}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(U_{i})
such that
\eta_{i}=1
on
\operatorname{supp}\chi_{i}
. Given a distribution
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{S}(M)
of order
S
, we define

\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(M)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\kappa_{i}^{*}(\eta_{i}f)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\kappa_{i}^{*}:f\mapsto f\circ\kappa_{i}
is the pull-back operator by
\kappa_{i}.
Then the space
\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(M)
(or simply
\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}
) is the space of distributions
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}_{S}(M)
for which
\|f\|_{\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(M)}<\infty.

A.4.
Zygmund and Sobolev spaces

For
s>0
, we define the Zygmund space
\mathscr{C}^{s}
on
M
by

\mathscr{C}^{s}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty}.

Recall that
\mathscr{C}^{s}
coincides with the usual Hölder space
\mathrm{C}^{s}
when
s\notin\mathbb{N}
, and that
\mathrm{C}^{s}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the Sobolev space
\mathrm{W}^{s,p}
is defined by

\mathrm{W}^{s,p}=\Lambda^{-s}\mathrm{L}^{p},

where
\Lambda=(1-\Delta)^{1/2}
. Finally recall that
\mathrm{H}^{s}=\mathrm{W}^{s,2}
.

For convenience of the reader we record below some useful properties regarding relations between the spaces
\mathscr{C}^{s},\mathrm{W}^{s,p},\mathrm{H}^{s}
and
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}
that we will use (see
[
68
]
):

(i)

for
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathrm{H}^{s}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{2,2}
;

(ii)

if
s>0
, then
\mathscr{C}^{s}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
;

(iii)

if
s_{1}\leqslant s_{2}
and
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
, then
\mathrm{B}^{s_{2}}_{p,q}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{B}^{s_{1}}_{p,q}
;

(iv)

if
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leqslant p_{1}\leqslant p_{2}\leqslant\infty
and
1\leqslant q_{2}\leqslant q_{1}\leqslant\infty
, then
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p_{2},q_{2}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p_{1},q_{1}}
;

(v)

if
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
2\leqslant p<\infty
, then
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{s,p}
;

(vi)

(Sobolev embedding) if
s_{1}\geqslant s_{2}
and
p_{1},p_{2}\geqslant 1
satisfy
\frac{1}{p_{1}}\leqslant\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{s_{1}-s_{2}}{d}
,
then
\mathrm{W}^{s_{1},p_{1}}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{s_{2},p_{2}}
;

(vii)

if
t>s>0
and
p>d/(t-s)
, then
\mathrm{W}^{t,p}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}^{s}
;

(viii)

if
p\in(1,+\infty)
and
s,t
are such that
s>|t|
, then the multiplication
\mathscr{C}^{s}\times\mathrm{W}^{t,p}\to\mathrm{W}^{t,p}
is continuous;

(ix)

if
1<p,q<\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then the dual space of
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}
is
\mathrm{B}^{-s}_{p^{\star},q^{\star}}
where
p^{\star},q^{\star}
are the conjugate exponents of
p,q
. If
p
or
q
is equal to
\infty
, the dual space of
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}
contains
\mathrm{B}^{-s}_{p^{\star},q^{\star}}
;

(x)

if
s>0
, then the multiplication
\mathscr{C}^{s}\times\mathscr{C}^{s}\to\mathscr{C}^{s}
is continuous.

Finally, let us recall the definition of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm associated to an operator
{G}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
between two separable Hilbert spaces. It is defined as

\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\left(\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}\geqslant 1}|\langle e^{2}_{i_{2}},G[e^{1}_{i_{1}}]\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
(e^{j}_{i})_{i\geqslant 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{j}
for
j=1,2
.

Appendix B
Approximations of identity

This section is dedicated to the construction of admissible kernels in the sense of
Definition

2.8
. These kernels are constructed by using charts to lift the wavelet basis on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
introduced in §
A.1
to
M
. In the process, we lose orthonormality of the basis. Still, the fact that the wavelet basis on the manifold is “almost orthogonal” (in the sense that it forms a Bessel frame, see
[
17
]
) is enough to show that the conditions of
Definition

2.8
are satisfied.

For any
D\in[2^{J},2^{J+1}-1]\cap\mathbb{N}
with
J\geqslant 1
, we let
\widetilde{\pi}_{D}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the orthogonal projection on

V_{D}=\operatorname{ran}\{\psi_{j,\lambda}~:~\lambda\in\Lambda_{j},~0\leqslant j\leqslant J\}.

From the definition of the Besov norms, it is clear that for any
-S<t<s<S
,
1\leqslant p^{\prime}\leqslant p<\infty
and
1\leqslant q,q^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
, there exists
C
such that for all
D\geqslant 1
,

(B.1)

\displaystyle\|1-\widetilde{\pi}_{D}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant CD^{-(s-t)},

(B.2)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\pi}_{D}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C.

Next, we lift the projections
\widetilde{\pi}_{D}
on
M
through the use of charts. For
i=1,\dots,N
, we define the operators
A_{i}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
and
B_{i}:\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{B}^{d})\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
by

A_{i}f=\kappa_{i}^{*}(\zeta_{i}f)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{i}g=(\kappa_{i}^{-1})^{*}\bigl(\widetilde{\zeta}_{i}g\bigr)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{B}^{d}),

where
\widetilde{\zeta}_{i}=\zeta_{i}\circ\kappa_{i}^{-1}
.
We also let
A_{i}^{\star}
and
B_{i}^{\star}
be the adjoints of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
over
\mathrm{L}^{2}
. By a change of variables, the adjoint operator
A_{i}^{\star}
(resp.
B_{i}^{\star}
) can be defined exactly as
B_{i}
(resp.
A_{i}
), replacing
\widetilde{\zeta}_{i}
(resp.
\zeta_{i}
) by another compactly supported function involving the Jacobian of
\kappa_{i}
. Because (local) Besov spaces are invariant by composition by smooth diffeomorphisms and multiplication by smooth functions, we have that

(B.3)

A_{i},B_{i}^{\star}:\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(M)\to\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptB_{i},A_{i}^{\star}:\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{B}^{t}_{p,q}(M)

are bounded.
Next, define the self-adjoint operator
\pi_{D}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(M)
by setting

(B.4)

\pi_{D}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(B_{i}\widetilde{\pi}_{D}A_{i}+A_{i}^{\star}\widetilde{\pi}_{D}B_{i}^{\star}).

Proposition B.1
.

The family
(\pi_{D})_{D\geqslant 0}
given by (
B.4
) is an admissible family of symmetric operators of class
\mathrm{C}^{S}
in the sense of
Definition

2.8
for any
0\leqslant s_{\star}\leqslant S-d/2
: for all
\ell,s\in\left[-s_{\star},s_{\star}\right]
with
\ell<s
and
1\leqslant p,p^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
and
1\leqslant q,q^{\prime}\leqslant\infty
, there exists
C_{\star}
such that for all
D\geqslant 1
:

(K1)

if
p\geqslant p^{\prime}
, then
\|1-\pi_{D}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}}\leqslant C_{\star}D^{-(s-\ell)}
;

(K2)

\|\pi_{D}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\to\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}}\leqslant C_{\star}
;

(K3)

for any integer
J\geqslant 1
,
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{J}\in[0,s_{\star}]
, integers
D_{1},\dots,D_{J}\geqslant 1
, and any multilinear form
G:\mathrm{H}^{\ell_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{H}^{\ell_{J}}\to\mathbb{R}
with Hilbert-Schmidt norm smaller than
L
,

\int|G[K_{D_{1}}(x_{1},\cdot),\dots,K_{D_{J}}(x_{J},\cdot)]|^{2}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{J}\leqslant C_{\star}L^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{J}D_{j}^{2\ell_{j}}.

(K4)

\|y\mapsto\|K_{D}(\cdot,y)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}\leqslant C_{\star}\kappa_{p,d,\ell}(D)
, where
\kappa_{p,d,\ell}(D)
is equal to
D^{\ell+d(1-1/p)}
if the exponent
\ell+d(1-1/p)
is positive,
\log(D)
if it is equal to zero, and
1
if it is negative.

The rest of this subsection is dedicated to the proof of the above proposition.

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.

By (
A.2
), there holds
\sum_{i=1}^{N}B_{i}A_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}A_{i}^{\star}B_{i}^{\star}=1
, which yields

1-\pi_{D}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(B_{i}(1-\widetilde{\pi}_{D})A_{i}+A_{i}^{\star}(1-\widetilde{\pi}_{D})B_{i}^{\star}).

Due to (
B.3
), (
B.1
) and (
B.2
),
(\pi_{D})_{D\geqslant 1}
automatically satisfies
(K1)
and
(K2)
. Moreover,
\pi_{D}
is symmetric by construction.

Next, we prove that
\pi_{D}
satisfies the property
(K4)
. Let
|\ell|<S.
The kernel
K_{D}
of
\pi_{D}
is defined for
x,y\in M
by

(B.5)

K_{D}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{j}}(A_{i}^{\star}\psi_{j,\lambda}(x)B_{i}\psi_{j,\lambda}(y)+B_{i}\psi_{j,\lambda}(x)A_{i}^{\star}\psi_{j,\lambda}(y)).

For
i\in[N]
and
j\geqslant 0
, define
\Psi^{(i)}_{j}
as the set of functions either of the form
\psi^{(i)}=B_{i}\psi
or
\psi^{(i)}=A_{i}^{\star}\psi
for
\psi\in\Psi_{j}
, with
\Psi^{(i)}=\bigcup_{j\geqslant 0}\Psi^{(i)}_{j}
. Remark that as the set of functions in
\Psi_{j}
whose support intersect the unit ball is of order
2^{jd}
, the set of nonzero functions in
\Psi^{(i)}_{j}
is also of order
2^{jd}
.

Let us rewrite
K_{D}(x,y)
as

(B.6)

K_{D}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}\check{\psi}^{(i)}(x)\psi^{(i)}(y),

where
\check{\psi}^{(i)}=A_{i}^{\star}\psi/2
if
\psi^{(i)}=B_{i}\psi
and
\check{\psi}^{(i)}=B_{i}\psi/2
if
\psi^{(i)}=A_{i}^{\star}\psi
.
We will use the following lemma to bound
\|K_{D}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}}
.

Lemma B.2
.

There is
C>0
such that for any sequence
(a^{(i)}_{\psi^{(i)}})
indexed by
\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}
for
j\geqslant 0
and
i=1,\dots,N
, one has

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}a^{(i)}_{\psi^{(i)}}\psi^{(i)}\right\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}}\leqslant C\|a^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{b}^{\ell}_{p,p}}.

Proof.

By definition of the
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,p}
norm on
M
, the left-hand side of the above inequality is equal to

(B.7)

\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{N}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{D}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}a^{(i)}_{\psi^{(i)}}\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*}\left(\eta_{i_{1}}\psi^{(i)}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

However note that for each
i_{1},i\in[N]
and
\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}
, one has

\kappa_{i_{1}}^{*}\eta_{i_{1}}\psi^{(i)}=(\kappa_{i}^{-1}\circ\kappa_{i_{1}})^{*}(\omega_{i_{1},i}\psi)

for some
\omega_{i_{1},i}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}^{d})
supported in
\kappa_{i}^{-1}(U_{i})\cap\kappa_{i_{1}}^{-1}(U_{i_{1}})
. Now notice that the operator
f\mapsto(\kappa_{i}^{-1}\circ\kappa_{i_{1}})^{*}(\omega_{i_{1},i}f)
is continuous on Besov spaces. Hence one gets that (
B.7
) is controlled by

\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{D}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}a^{(i)}_{\psi^{(i)}}\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left(\sum_{j=0}^{D}2^{jp(s+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}|a^{(i)}_{\psi^{(i)}}|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

which gives the sought result.
∎

The above lemma yields

\displaystyle\|K_{D}(x,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{jp(\ell+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}|\check{\psi}^{(i)}(x)|^{p}.

Integrating this inequality, using that
\Psi_{j}^{(i)}
contains a number of nonzero functions of order
2^{jd}
and that
B_{i}
and
A_{i}^{\star}
are continuous
\mathrm{L}^{p}\to\mathrm{L}^{p}
together with the estimate
\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim 2^{jd(\frac{p}{2}-1)}
, we conclude that
(K4)
holds. A similar proof holds for
p=\infty
.

It remains to prove
(K3)
. To do so, we need to establish more precise properties of the families of wavelets on manifolds.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space and let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
be a family of vectors in
\mathcal{H}
. We say that
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is a Bessel sequence if there exists
\alpha>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
,

(B.8)

\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|\langle f,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}\leqslant\alpha\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
be a Bessel sequence with constant
\alpha>0
. Then, for all linear forms
G:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
and sequences of numbers
a=(a_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|G[\varphi_{k}]|^{2}\leqslant\alpha\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\left\|\sum_{k\geqslant 0}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leqslant\alpha\sum_{k\geqslant 0}a_{k}^{2}.

Furthermore, if
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is a family of vectors such that there exists
\alpha>0
with

\sup_{j\geqslant 0}\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|\langle\varphi_{j},\varphi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leqslant\alpha,

then
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
is a Bessel sequence with constant
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

The second inequality is stated in
[
17
, Theorem 3.2.3]
. Let

T:a\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 0})\mapsto\sum_{k\geqslant 0}a_{k}\varphi_{k}\in\mathcal{H}.

Both
\mathrm{T}
and its adjoint are continuous with norm smaller than
\sqrt{\alpha}
, and the adjoint
T^{*}
is given by
T^{*}\psi=(\langle\psi,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}})_{k\geqslant 0}
for
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
, see
[
17
, Lemma 3.2.1]
. Let
(e_{i})_{i\geqslant 0}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Then,
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}G[e_{i}]T^{*}e_{i}
is the sequence whose
k
-th entry is equal to
\sum_{i\geqslant 0}G[e_{i}]\langle e_{i},\varphi_{k}\rangle=G[\varphi_{k}]
. Thus,

\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|G[\varphi_{k}]|^{2}=\|\sum_{i\geqslant 0}G[e_{i}]T^{*}e_{i}\|^{2}_{\ell^{2}}\leqslant\alpha\|\sum_{i\geqslant 0}G[e_{i}]e_{i}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=\alpha\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}.

The last property is given in
[
17
, Proposition 3.5.4]
.
∎

For
|t|<S
,
j\geqslant 0
and
\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}
, we let
\psi_{t}^{(i)}=2^{-jt}\psi^{(i)}
. Define
\Psi_{j,t}^{(i)}
and
\Psi^{(i)}_{t}
in a obvious way.

Lemma B.4
.

Assume that
S>d/2
. For
|t|<S-d/2
, the family
\bigcup_{i\in[N]}\Psi^{(i)}_{t}
is a Bessel sequence in
\mathrm{H}^{t}
.

Proof.

We check that the sufficient condition given in
Lemma

B.3
holds. Fix
i_{0}\in[N]
and
j_{0}\geqslant 0
. Let
\psi_{t}^{(i_{0})}=2^{-j_{0}t}\psi_{0}^{(i_{0})}\in\Psi_{t}^{(i_{0})}
, where
\psi_{0}^{(i_{0})}=B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}
for some
\psi_{0}\in\Psi_{j_{0}}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{t}^{(i)}}

\displaystyle|\langle\psi^{(i)}_{t},\psi_{t}^{(i_{0})}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}^{t}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{-(j+j_{0})t}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}|\langle\psi^{(i)},\Lambda^{2t}\psi_{0}^{(i_{0})}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{-(j+j_{0})t}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}(|\langle B_{i}\psi,\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\rangle|+|\langle A_{i}^{\star}\psi,\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\rangle|).

By duality and using the definition of the Besov norm
\mathrm{B}_{1,1}^{-t+d/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one sees that this quantity is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j\geqslant 0}2^{-(j+j_{0})t}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}}\left(|\langle\psi,B_{i}^{\star}\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\rangle|+|\langle\psi,A_{i}\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\rangle|\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt=\frac{2^{-j_{0}t}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\|B_{i}^{\star}\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{-t+d/2}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|A_{i}\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{-t+d/2}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

By (
B.3
) and the fact that
\Lambda^{2t}:\mathrm{B}^{t+d/2}_{1,1}\to\mathrm{B}^{-t+d/2}_{1,1}
(see
[
64

), it holds that both
\|B_{i}^{\star}\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{-t+d/2}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
\|A_{i}\Lambda^{2t}B_{i_{0}}\psi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{-t+d/2}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
are at most of order
\|\psi_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{t+d/2}_{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=2^{j_{0}t}
. This shows that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{t}^{(i)}}|\langle\psi^{(i)}_{t},\psi_{t}^{(i_{0})}\rangle_{\mathrm{H}^{t}}|\lesssim 1.

The same condition holds if
\psi_{0}^{(i_{0})}=A_{i_{0}}^{*}\psi_{0}
for some
\psi_{0}\in\Psi_{j_{0}}
. By
Lemma

B.3
, the family is a Bessel sequence.
∎

One claims that
Lemma

B.3
implies that
\pi_{D}
satisfies
(K3)
. Indeed, let
s_{\star}\in[0,S-d/2]
and
\ell\in[0,s_{\star}]
. For a linear form
G:\mathrm{H}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{R}
, letting

be of order

, it holds

\displaystyle G[K_{D}(x,\cdot)]=\sum_{i\in[N]}\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{j\ell}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}G\left[\check{\psi}^{(i)}\right]\psi^{(i)}(x).

Thus, by properties of a Bessel frame, letting
\alpha
be the constant of the frame,

\displaystyle\int|G[K_{D}(x,\cdot)]|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{i\in[N]}\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{j\ell}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}G\left[\check{\psi}^{(i)}\right]\psi^{(i)}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\alpha\sum_{i\in[N]}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}2^{2j\ell}\left|G\left[\check{\psi}^{(i)}\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha D^{2\ell}\sum_{i\in[N]}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi_{j}^{(i)}}\left|G\left[\check{\psi}^{(i)}\right]\right|^{2}.

The last term can be bounded by
\alpha^{2}D^{2\ell}\|G\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}
by Lemma
B.3
, which proves
(K3)
for linear forms. For multilinear forms, the proof is similar and we leave it to the reader.
∎

Appendix C
Density estimation with wavelets

We prove in this section that the kernels
(K_{D})_{D\geqslant 1}
can be used to create minimax density estimators.

Proposition C.1
.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be integer and let
p\geqslant 2
,
R>0
,
s\in[0,s_{\star}]
and
\ell\in\left[-s_{\star},s\right[
. There exists
C>0
such that for all
f\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
with
\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant R
, if
\mu_{n}
is the empirical measure associated with
n
i.i.d. observations with density
f
then, for
D
of order
n^{\frac{1}{2s+d}}
, the estimator
\hat{f}=\pi_{D}\mu_{n}
satisfies

(C.1)

\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|^{2}_{\mathrm{W}^{\ell}_{p}}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant C\begin{cases}n^{-\frac{s-\ell}{2s+d}}&\text{if }\ell>-d/2,\\
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log n}&\text{if }\ell=-d/2,\\
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\text{if }\ell<-d/2.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if
0\leqslant\ell\leqslant s
, then

(C.2)

\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|^{m}_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}]^{\frac{1}{m}}\leqslant C\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{\frac{s-\ell}{2s+d}}.

Proof.

We show the first inequality.
Recall that
\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{\ell}_{p}
. Thus, it suffices to bound the
\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}
-norm.
We use the triangle inequality to decompose the risk into a bias term
\|(\pi_{D}-1)f\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}}
and a variance term
\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}}^{2}]
. By
(K1)
,
the bias term is controlled, up to a multiplicative constant, by
RD^{-(s-\ell)}
.
We bound the variance with the help of Rosenthal inequality.

Lemma C.2

(Rosenthal inequality)
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
.
There exists a constant
C_{m}
such that for all sequences
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
of i.i.d. random variables with
\mathbf{E}[|Y_{1}|^{m}]<+\infty
and
\mathbf{E}[Y_{1}]=0
, it holds that

(C.3)

\mathbf{E}\left[\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}\right|^{m}\right]\leqslant C_{m}\left(n^{-\frac{m}{2}}\mathbf{E}[|Y_{1}|^{2}]^{\frac{m}{2}}+n^{1-m}\mathbf{E}[|Y_{1}|^{m}]\right).

We have
\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\sum_{\psi\in\Psi^{(i)}_{j}}\langle\mu_{n}-f,\check{\psi}^{(i)}\rangle\psi^{(i)}
where
2^{Jd}
is of order
D
. Then by Lemma
B.2
and Jensen’s inequality,
\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}}^{2}]
is controlled up to a multiplicative constant by

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{2j\left(\ell+\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}\right)}\left(\sum_{\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi^{(i)}_{j}}\mathbf{E}[|\langle\mu_{n}-f,\check{\psi}^{(i)}\rangle|^{p}]\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}.

We apply Rosenthal inequality to each expectation to obtain the bound

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[|\langle\mu_{n}-f,\check{\psi}^{(i)}\rangle|^{p}]

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\frac{p}{2}}\|\check{\psi}^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{\frac{p}{2}}+n^{1-p}\|\check{\psi}^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-\frac{p}{2}}+n^{1-p}2^{jp\left(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p}\right)}.

Assume that
2\ell>-d
. Then, as the number of nonzero functions in
\Psi^{(i)}_{j}
is of order
2^{jd}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)\|_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}}^{2}]

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{j\left(2\ell+d\right)}+n^{\frac{2}{p}-2}\sum_{j=0}^{J}2^{2j(\ell+d\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right))}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-1}D^{2\ell+d}+n^{\frac{2}{p}-2}D^{2\ell+2d\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.

As
D^{d}/n\leqslant 1
and
p\geqslant 2
, the second term is negligible. This shows that, for
D
of order
n^{\frac{1}{2s+d}}
,
\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}\mu_{n}-\pi_{D}f\|^{2}_{\mathrm{B}^{\ell}_{p,2}}]^{1/2}\lesssim n^{-\frac{s-\ell}{2s+d}}
. The bias term is of the same order for this value of
D
, concluding the proof in the case
2\ell>-d
. Similar computations can be made in the two other cases.

It remains to prove the second inequality.
We first treat the case
m=1
. By Lemma
B.2
, the fluctuations are of order

(C.4)

\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}]\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{E}[\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J,\psi\in\Psi^{(i)}_{j}}2^{j\left(\ell+\frac{d}{2}\right)}|\langle\mu_{n}-f,\check{\psi}^{(i)}\rangle|].

This is the expectation of the supremum of an empirical process. Remark that for
0\leqslant j\leqslant J
and
\psi^{(i)}\in\Psi^{(i)}_{j}
,
2^{j\left(\ell+\frac{d}{2}\right)}\|\check{\psi}^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim D^{\ell+d}
and that
2^{j\left(\ell+\frac{d}{2}\right)}\|\check{\psi}^{(i)}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\lesssim D^{\ell+d/2}
. Thus, as the supremum is taken over a set of size of order
D^{d}
, a direct application of
[
31
, Lemma 3.5.12]
yields

(C.5)

\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}]\lesssim n^{-1/2}D^{\ell+d/2}\sqrt{\log D}+n^{-1}D^{\ell+d}\log D.

Thus, for
m=1
, we obtain the conclusion by letting
D
be of order
(n/\log n)^{\frac{1}{2s+d}}
.
According to
[
73
, Theorem 2.14.23]
, the exponential Orlicz norm of the supremum
\max_{0\leqslant j\leqslant J,\psi\in\Psi^{(i)}_{j}}|\langle\mu_{n}-f,\check{\psi}^{(i)}\rangle|
is at most of order

\mathbf{E}[\max_{\psi\in\Psi^{(i)}_{j}}|\langle\mu_{n}-f,\check{\psi}^{(i)}\rangle|]+n^{-1}(1+\log n)D^{\ell+d}.

As moments of a random variable are bounded by its exponential Orlicz norm, we find that for
m\geqslant 1
,
\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}(\mu_{n}-f)\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}^{m}]^{\frac{1}{m}}\lesssim\left((\log n)/n\right)^{\frac{s-\ell}{2s+d}}
.
∎

The estimator
\pi_{D}\mu_{n}
can be easily modified so that it belongs almost surely to a prescribed statistical model
\Theta
. This simple modification will turn out to be more convenient to use.

Corollary C.3
.

Let
m\geqslant 1
be integer and let
p\geqslant 2
,
R,\eta>0
,
0<s^{\prime}<s\leqslant s_{\star}
. Let
\Theta_{0}\subset\Theta
be subsets of
\mathscr{C}^{s}
such that
\Theta
is included in a
\eta
-neighborhood of
\Theta_{0}
for the
\mathscr{C}^{s^{\prime}}
-norm. There exists an estimator
\hat{f}\in\Theta
based on
n
i.i.d. observations, such that if the density
f
of the observations is in
f\in\Theta_{0}
and satisfies
\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}}\leqslant R
, then
\hat{f}
satisfies the same rates of convergence as in
Proposition

C.1
.

Proof.

We define
\hat{f}=\pi_{D}\mu_{n}
if
\pi_{D}\mu_{n}\in\Theta
, and we let
\hat{f}
be a fixed, deterministic function in
\Theta
should this condition be not verified. Remark that
\hat{f}\in\Theta
and that
\hat{f}=\pi_{D}\mu_{n}
as long as
\|\pi_{D}\mu_{n}-f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s^{\prime}}}\leqslant\eta
. Let us call
E
this event. By Markov’s inequality and
Proposition

C.1
,
\mathbf{P}(E^{c})\leqslant C_{k}(\log n/n)^{k\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{2s+d}}
for all
k\geqslant 1
and some constant
C_{k}
. Furthermore, we use
(K4)
to show that
\|\pi_{D}\mu_{n}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}
can be bounded almost surely by
\sup_{x}\|\pi_{D}\delta_{x}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}\leqslant C_{\star}\kappa_{\infty,d,\ell}(D)
, a quantity which grows at most polynomially with
D
(and thus with
n
). These two ingredients are enough to show that the residual term
\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}^{m}\mathbf{1}\{E^{c}\}]
is negligible in front of
\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}^{m}\mathbf{1}\{E\}]=\mathbf{E}[\|\pi_{D}\mu_{n}-f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{\ell}}^{m}\mathbf{1}\{E\}]
. We then conclude with
Proposition

C.1
. The risk of
\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}-f\|_{\mathrm{W}^{\ell}_{p}}^{2}]
is controlled in a similar fashion.
∎

Remark C.4
.

If
s
is an integer and
f\in\mathrm{C}^{s}
with
\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{s}}\leqslant L
, one can easily modify the previous proofs to see that one can also impose that for all
m\geqslant 1
, there is a constant
C>0
such that
\mathbf{E}[\|\hat{f}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{s}}^{m}]^{1/m}\leqslant C
. This follows from the injection
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{\infty,1}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{s}
.

Appendix D
Kernel estimates

In this section we give some estimates on the explosion of kernels of resolvent type operators.

D.1.
Resolvent-like kernels

We say that a kernel

K\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(M\times M\setminus\Delta)
is
resolvent-like
if for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
, with
m=d-2-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}+|\beta_{1}|+|\beta_{2}|
, there exists
C_{\alpha,\beta}
such that for all
x,y\in M,\ x\neq y
,

(D.1)

|\Lambda_{x}^{-\alpha_{1}}\Lambda_{y}^{-\alpha_{2}}\partial^{\beta_{1}}_{x}\partial^{\beta_{2}}_{y}K(x,y)|\leqslant C_{\alpha,\beta}\begin{cases}|x-y|^{-m}&\text{ if }m>0\\
1+|\log|x-y||&\text{ if }m=0\\
1&\text{ if }m<0.\end{cases}

Lemma D.1
.

Let
u\in\mathrm{C}^{1}
and let
K
be a resolvent-like kernel.
Consider
K_{1,u}=K
and set for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

K_{n+1,u}(x,y)=\int_{M}K(x,z)\nabla u(z)\cdot\nabla_{z}K_{n,u}(z,y)\mathrm{d}z,\hskip 10.00002ptx,y\in M,\ x\neq y.

Then
K_{n,u}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(M\times M\setminus\Delta)
is resolvent-like for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with constants in (
D.1
) depending only on the corresponding constants for
K
, on an upper bound on
\|u\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}}
and on
n
.

Proof.

Using a partition of unity we may assume that
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u
is compactly supported. We proceed by induction on
n
. Assume that for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
K_{n,u}
is resolvent-like. We have

K_{n+1,u}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(x,z)\partial_{z_{j}}u(z)\partial_{z_{j}}K_{n,u}(z,y)\mathrm{d}z.

We see that
K_{n+1,u}
is resolvent-like by the induction hypothesis and using standard estimates for Riesz kernels (see
[
62
, §V.1]
or
[
44
, §1.1]
).
∎

D.2.
Resolvent of
\Delta_{f}

The resolvent of
\Delta
is the Bessel potential, which is known to be resolvent-like
[
63
, Proposition 2.2]
. Likewise, we show that the resolvent of
\Delta_{f}
is decomposed into the sum of an operator with a resolvent-like kernel and a regularizing operator.

Lemma D.2
.

Let
\delta,L>0
.
There exists
C>0
such that the following holds. Let
f\in\mathrm{C}^{2}
be a function with
\inf f>\delta
and
\|f\|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}}\leqslant L
and let
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|\leqslant L
and
\dist(z,\sigma(-\Delta_{f}))\geqslant\delta
. Then, there is a decomposition

R_{f}(z)=W_{z}+Q_{z}

where
W_{z}
has a resolvent-like kernel
K_{z}
with constants in (
D.1
) depending only on
C
,
and
Q_{z}
has an operator norm smaller than
C
as an operator
\mathrm{H}^{-1}\to\mathscr{C}^{1}
and
\mathrm{W}^{-1,1}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}
.

Proof.

Write
f=e^{U}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we use the resolvent identity (see (
3.27
))

R_{f}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-\alpha)^{i}(z+\Delta)^{-1}(A_{U}(z+\Delta)^{-1})^{i}+(-\alpha)^{n+1}R_{f}(z)(A_{U}(z+\Delta)^{-1})^{n+1}.

Lemma

D.1
indicates that for any
n\geqslant 1
, the operator
W_{z}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(z+\Delta)^{-1}(A_{U}(z+\Delta)^{-1})^{i}
has a resolvent-like kernel.
Furthermore, applying iteratively
Lemma

3.1
and
Proposition

3.3
together with Sobolev embeddings show that the remainder term
Q_{z}=(-1)^{n+1}(z+\Delta_{f})^{-1}(A_{U}(z+\Delta)^{-1})^{n+1}
maps
\mathrm{H}^{-1}\to\mathscr{C}^{1}
and
\mathrm{W}^{-1,1}\to\mathrm{H}^{1}
for
n
large enough (depending on
d
).
∎

Appendix E
Control of Hilbert-Schmidt norms

In this section, we give some bounds on Hilbert-Schmidt norms of operators appearing in the article.

Let
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
be an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator
-\Delta
, with
(\lambda_{k})_{k\geqslant 0}
the associated sequence of eigenvalues. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\varphi_{k,t}=(1+\lambda_{k})^{-t/2}\varphi_{k}
. By definition of the Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}^{t}
,
(\varphi_{k,t})_{k\geqslant 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathrm{H}^{t}
.

E.1.
Hilbert-Schmidt bounds for tri-bounded operators

We first show that the condition given in
Remark

2.3
implies that the Hilbert-Schmidt condition in
(G3)
is satisfied.

Lemma E.1
.

If
G:(\mathrm{L}^{2})^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, then the Hilbert-Schmidt condition in
(G3)
is satisfied.

Proof.

Let
t>d/2
. We show that
G:\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}
has a finite Hilbert-Schmidt norm. It holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geqslant 0}

\displaystyle|G[\varphi_{k_{1},t},\varphi_{k_{2},t},\varphi_{k_{3}}]|^{2}=\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geqslant 0}(1+\lambda_{k_{1}})^{-t}(1+\lambda_{k_{2}})^{-t}|G[\varphi_{k_{1}},\varphi_{k_{2}},\varphi_{k_{3}}]|^{2}.

As
G:(\mathrm{L}^{2})^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous, there exists
\widetilde{G}:(\mathrm{L}^{2})^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}
such that
G[u,v,w]=\langle\widetilde{G}[u,v],w\rangle
. But then,

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{3}\geqslant 0}|G[\varphi_{k_{1}},\varphi_{k_{2}},\varphi_{k_{3}}]|^{2}=\|\widetilde{G}[\varphi_{k_{1}},\varphi_{k_{2}}]\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\lesssim\|\varphi_{k_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\|\varphi_{k_{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\lesssim 1.

Thus, the sum
\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geqslant 0}|G[\varphi_{k_{1},t},\varphi_{k_{2},t},\varphi_{k_{3}}]|^{2}
is of order at most

\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\geqslant 0}(1+\lambda_{k_{1}})^{-t}(1+\lambda_{k_{2}})^{-t}.

However Weyl’s law yields
\lambda_{k}\sim Ck^{2/d}
, hence this sum is finite.
∎

E.2.
Hilbert-Schmidt bounds for resolvent-like kernels

We write
|x-y|
for the geodesic distance between two points
x,y\in M
. We let
\Delta=\{(x,x):\ x\in M\}
be the diagonal of
M\times M
. If
K
is a distribution on
M\times M
and
\Gamma
is an operator, we let
\Gamma_{y}K(x,y)
be the distribution obtained by applying
\Gamma
to the variable
y
of
K
, evaluated at
(x,y)
.

Lemma E.2
.

Let
t>d/2
and
C>0
.
Let
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}
be distributions on
M\times M
such that for
i=1,2,3
,
(x,y)\mapsto\Lambda_{y}^{-t}K_{i}(x,y)
is continuous on
(M\times M)\setminus\Delta
and such that

|\Lambda_{y}^{-t}K_{i}(x,y)|\leqslant C(1+|x-y|^{-d+t}).

Let
B_{i}
be the operators with kernel
K_{i}
, that we assume have operator norms
\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}
smaller than
C
. Then, the symmetrization of the operator
\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\mapsto\int(B_{1}u_{1})\cdot(B_{2}u_{2})\cdot(B_{3}u_{3})
has finite Hilbert-Schmidt norm, bounded by
C_{1}C^{3}
for some constant
C_{1}
depending on
t
and
d
.

Proof.

The Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the (nonsymmetric) operator is equal to the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geqslant 0}|\langle\varphi_{k_{1}},B_{1}^{*}\left[B_{2}\varphi_{k_{2},t}B_{3}\varphi_{k_{3},t}\right]\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{2},k_{3}\geqslant 0}\|B_{1}^{*}\left[B_{2}\varphi_{k_{2},t}B_{3}\varphi_{k_{3},t}\right]\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{2}\sum_{k_{2},k_{3}\geqslant 0}\int|B_{2}\varphi_{k_{2},t}(x)B_{3}\varphi_{k_{3},t}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=C^{2}\int\rho_{2}(x)\rho_{3}(x)\mathrm{d}x

where
\rho_{i}(x)=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}|B_{i}\varphi_{k,t}(x)|^{2}
for
i=2,3
.
We have

\rho_{i}(x)=\|\Lambda_{y}^{-t}K(x,\cdot)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leqslant C^{2}\int(1+|x-y|^{-d+t})^{2}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant C^{2}C_{d,\varepsilon}

for some constant
C_{d,t}
depending on
d
and
t
. Thus,
\int\rho_{2}(x)\rho_{3}(x)\mathrm{d}x
is of order
C^{4}
. This proves that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\mapsto\int(B_{1}u_{1})\cdot(B_{2}u_{2})\cdot(B_{3}u_{3})
is finite. The other symmetrizations are treated similarly.
∎

We can in particular apply this lemma with
B_{1}=B_{2}=B_{3}=1
. Indeed, for
t>d/2
,
\Lambda_{y}^{-t}\delta(x-y)
is the Bessel potential of order
t
, which verifies

|\Lambda_{y}^{-t}\delta(x-y)|\lesssim 1+|x-y|^{-d+t},

see
[
3
]
.
As
-d+t>-d/2
, we may apply
Lemma

E.2
, which proves that the trilinear form
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\mapsto\int u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}
satisfies the Hilbert-Schmidt condition in
(G3)
.

E.3.
Control of Hilbert-Schmidt norms for differentials of eigenvalues

In this subsection we give the missing details in the proof of Proposition
3.11
. More precisely, we explain here how to bound the Hilbert-Schmidt norms of

G_{1}[u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}],\ G_{2}[u_{1}u_{2},u_{3}],\ G_{2}[u_{1}u_{3},u_{2}],\ G_{2}[u_{2}u_{3},u_{1}],\ G_{3}[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}]

over
\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}
for
t>d/2
(see
Section

3.6
).
By definition of the
G_{j}
’s, it suffices to bound the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of the symmetrization of the forms

(E.1)

\displaystyle\langle B_{z}[u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}]\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{f},\hskip 10.00002pt\langle B_{z}[u_{1}u_{2}]B_{z}[u_{3}]\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{f}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and }\hskip 10.00002pt\langle B_{z}[u_{1}]B_{z}[u_{2}]B_{z}[u_{3}]\varphi,\varphi\rangle

for
z\in\Upsilon
and
\varphi
an eigenfunction of
\Delta_{f}
. We denote the symmetrizations of these three terms by
H_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
,
H_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
and
H_{3}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
respectively.

Control of
H_{1}
.
First, an integration by parts show that the trilinear form
H_{1}
can be written as

H_{1}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=\langle u_{1}u_{2}u_{2},\psi_{1}\rangle

for some
\psi_{1}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
, so that the control of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of this form follows from
Lemma

2.4
.

Control of
H_{2}
.
Define
\Gamma=A_{\overline{\varphi}}-\overline{\varphi}z
, which is a differential operator of order
1
with
\mathscr{C}^{s}
coefficients.
The computations in the proof of
3.13
show that the symmetrization of
\langle B_{z}[u]B_{z}[v]\varphi,\varphi\rangle
can be written as a linear combination of the symmetrization of

(E.2)

\langle uv,\psi_{2}\rangle_{f}\ \text{ and }\ \langle\Gamma R_{f}(z)\nabla v\nabla\varphi,u\rangle_{f}

for some function
\psi_{2}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}
. Hence we can write
H_{2}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
as a linear combination of the symmetrization of

\langle u_{1}u_{2}u_{3},\psi_{2}\rangle_{f}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\langle\Gamma R_{f}(z)\nabla u_{3}\nabla\varphi,u_{1}u_{2}\rangle_{f}

Applying again
Lemma

2.4
, we see that the trilinear form
\langle u_{1}u_{2}u_{3},\psi_{2}\rangle_{f}
has finite Hilbert-Schmidt norm. One decomposes the second term of (

) as

\langle\Gamma R_{f}(z)\nabla v\nabla\varphi,u\rangle_{f}=\langle\Gamma W_{z}\nabla v\nabla\varphi,u\rangle+\langle\Gamma Q_{z}\nabla v\nabla\varphi,u\rangle_{f}.

The mapping properties of
Q_{z}
stated in
Lemma

D.2
imply that the symmetrization of the trilinear form
\langle\Gamma Q_{z}\nabla u_{3}\nabla\varphi,u_{1}u_{2}\rangle_{f}
is continuous over
(\mathrm{L}^{2})^{3}
so its Hilbert-Schmidt norms over
\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{H}^{t}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}
is bounded according to

Some commutation principles for optimization problems over transformation groups and semi-FTvN systems
M. Seetharama Gowda

Abstract
We introduce the concepts of commutativity relative to a transformation group and strong commutativity in the setting of a semi-FTvN system and show their appearance as optimality conditions in certain optimization problems. In the setting of a semi-FTvN system (in particular, in an FTvN system), we show that strong commutativity implies commutativity and observe that in the special case of Euclidean Jordan algebra, commutativity and strong commutativity concepts reduce, respectively, to those of operator and strong operator commutativity. We demonstrate that every complete hyperbolic polynomial induces a semi-FTvN system. By way of an application, we describe several commutation principles.



Key Words
: Transformation group, semi-FTvN system, (strong) commutativity, commutation principle, Euclidean Jordan algebra, hyperbolic polynomial

15A27, 17C20, 46N10,
90C33.

Introduction

The main objective of the paper is to describe two commutativity concepts and show their relevance in certain commutation principles.

For the first commutativity concept, we
consider a real inner product space
{\cal V}
and the corresponding space
\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
of all bounded linear transformations equipped with the operator norm. Within
\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
, let
\mathrm{GL({\cal V})}
denote the set of all invertible linear transformations. Given a closed subgroup
{\cal G}
of
\mathrm{GL({\cal V})}
, let

\mathrm{Lie({\cal G}):=\{D\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}:\exp(tD)\in{\cal G}\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}},

(1)

where, for a given
D
,
\mathrm{\exp(tD)}
denotes the sum of the power series
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}D^{k}}{k!}
that converges uniformly on bounded sets of
\mathbb{R}
to an element in
\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
.
(When
{\cal V}
is finite-dimensional,
\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
is the Lie algebra of the matrix Lie group
{\cal G}
.)
Given elements
a
and
b
in
{\cal V}
, we say that

a
commutes with
b
relative to
{\cal G}
if

\langle Da,b\rangle=0

for all

\mathrm{D\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})}
.

As we see below, in the setting of a Euclidean Jordan algebra
{\cal V}
with its (algebra) automorphism group
{\cal G}
, this definition is equivalent to that of operator commutativity.
In particular, for real symmetric matrices (or complex Hermitian matrices)
A
and
B
, this reduces to the usual commutativity
AB=BA
.

The motivation for introducing the above concept comes from the so-called commutation principles
[
30
,
26
,
9
,
8
,
19
]
, where commutativity appears as an optimality condition in certain optimization problems. The following result - to be elaborated and proved later - provides an illustration:

Theorem 1.1

Consider
{\cal V}
and
{\cal G}
as above. Let
\Theta:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Fréchet differentiable function and
F:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
{\cal G}
-invariant function. Let
E
be a set in
{\cal V}
that is
{\cal G}
-invariant. If
a
is a local optimizer of the problem

\underset{x\in E}{min/max}\quad\Theta(x)+F(x),

then
a
commutes with
\Theta^{\prime}(a)
relative to
{\cal G}.

Our second commutativity concept is defined in the setting of a semi-FTvN system. A semi-FTvN system is a triple
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, where
{\cal V}
and
{\cal W}
are real inner product spaces and
\lambda:{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal W}
is a mapping that is “norm-preserving” and ”inner product expanding”:

||\lambda(x)||=||x||\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\big\langle x,y\big\rangle\leq\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\big\rangle\quad(\forall\,x,y\in{\cal V}).

(2)

In a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, we define
strong commutativity
of elements
x
and

in
{\cal V}
by the condition

\langle x,y\rangle=\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\rangle.

(3)

For commutativity in this system, we consider the group
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)}
of all invertible bounded linear transformations
A:{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal V}
satisfying the condition
\lambda(Ax)=\lambda(x)
for all
x\in{\cal V}
. As
\lambda
is norm-preserving, this is a closed subgroup of the orthogonal group of
{\cal V}
. Considering a closed subgroup
{\cal G}
of
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)}
, we define
\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
by (
1
) and formulate the definition of commutativity relative to
{\cal G}
as we did earlier. We shall see that

•

In a semi-FTvN system, strong commutativity implies commutativity (relative to
{\cal G}
).

How do semi-FTvN systems arise? First, a semi-FTvN system is a generalization of the so-called FTvN system. An FTvN system (short for Fan-Theobald-von Neumann system) is a triple
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, where
{\cal V}
and
{\cal W}
are real inner product spaces and

is a mapping satisfying the condition

\max\Big\{\!\left<c,x\right>:\,x\in[u]\Big\}=\left<\lambda(c),\lambda(u)\right>\quad(\forall\,c,u\in{\cal V})

(4)

with
[u]:=\{x\in{\cal V}:\lambda(x)=\lambda(u)\}
denoting the so-called
\lambda
-orbit of

. It is shown in
[
11
]
, Section 3, that in such a system, the mapping
\lambda
satisfies (
2
); thus, every FTvN system is a semi-FTvN system. Numerous examples and properties of FTvN systems are described in the articles
[
7
,
11
,
18
,
17
]
. A simple example of an FTvN system is the triple
({\cal V},\mathbb{R},\lambda)
, where
{\cal V}
is a real inner product space and
\lambda(x)=||x||
. Non-trivial examples include

Euclidean Jordan algebras
, systems induced by
complete and isometric hyperbolic polynomials
, and
normal decomposition systems (Eaton triples)
, see
[
7
]
. In the setting of a Euclidean Jordan algebra (carrying the trace inner product), the strong commutativity relation (
3
) reduces to the

of

and

which means that

and

have their spectral decompositions with respect to a common Jordan frame where their coefficients (eigenvalues) appear in the decreasing order
[
1
]
. This commutativity is stronger than the

where (we merely require that) two elements have their spectral decompositions with respect to a common Jordan frame. We show, by rephrasing a recent result in
[
19
]
, that

•

In a Euclidean Jordan algebra, commutativity relative to the (algebra) automorphism group is equivalent to operator commutativity.

The concept of strong commutativity (
3
) was initially studied in the setting of FTvN systems (where it was just called commutativity).
For example, it was shown in
[
7
]

,

(5)

with the attainment of one supremum implying the attainment of the other and additionally implying a relation of the form
(

[

that under certain conditions,
(

) is equivalent to the Fenchel conjugate formula

(\phi\circ\lambda)_{S}^{\ast}(z)=\phi_{\lambda(S)}^{\ast}\big(\lambda(z)\big)

and to the subdifferential relation

y\in\partial_{S}(\phi\circ\lambda)(\overline{x})\Longleftrightarrow\lambda(y)\in\partial_{\lambda(S)}\phi\big(\lambda(\overline{x})\big)\text{ and $y$ strongly commutes with $\overline{x}$}.

(6)

Semi-FTvN systems also arise from complete hyperbolic polynomials. We recall
[
5
,
2
]
that given a finite-dimensional real vector space
{\cal V}
and a nonzero element
e\in{\cal V}
, a real homogeneous polynomial

on

is said to be

, the roots of the univariate polynomial

are
real. Given such a polynomial

, for any
x\in{\cal V}
, let
\lambda(x)
denote the vector
of roots of the polynomial
t\rightarrow p(te-x)
with entries written in decreasing
order. We say that
p
is complete if
\lambda(x)=0\Rightarrow x=0
. Based on a construction due to Bauschke et al
[
2
]
, we show that

•

Every complete hyperbolic polynomial induces a semi-FTvN system.

In addition to Theorem
1.1
and the bulleted items above, we state and prove several commutation principles.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the concept of commutativity relative to a transformation group and provide some examples. Section 3 and its various subsections deal with semi-FTvN systems, automorphisms, and commutativity concepts, and the result that strong commutativity implies commutativity. In this section, we also introduce the concepts of center and unit element, and provide numerous examples. In Section 4, we consider the FTvN system induced by a Euclidean Jordan algebra. Here, we show that commutativity relative to the
(algebra) automorphism group is equivalent to operator commutativity. In Section 5, we show that a complete hyperbolic polynomial induces a semi-FTvN system.
In our final section, we describe several commutativity principles. These can be regarded as analogs of earlier results stated in the setting of FTvN systems that describe strong commutativity relations.

2
Commutativity relative to a transformation group

Consider a real inner product space
{\cal V}
and the space
\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
of all bounded linear transformations on
{\cal V}
equipped with the operator norm. Within
\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
, let
\mathrm{GL({\cal V})}
denote the set of all invertible linear transformations. A closed subgroup of
\mathrm{GL({\cal V})}
is said to be a
transformation group
(a matrix Lie group
[
13
]
when
{\cal V}
is finite dimensional). Given such a group
{\cal G}
, we consider
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
defined in (
1
).

Definition 2.1

Let
{\cal V}
and
{\cal G}
be as above and
a,b\in{\cal V}
. We say that
a
commutes with
b
relative to
{\cal G}
if

\langle Da,b\rangle=0\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,D\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G}).

We remark that the above definition is not symmetric in
a
and
b
. However, when
{\cal G}
is a closed subgroup of the orthogonal group of
{\cal V}
(consisting of elements of
\mathrm{GL({\cal V})}
that are inner product preserving), from the relation

\langle e^{tD}x,e^{tD}x\rangle=\langle x,x\rangle
, we get
\langle Dx,x\rangle=0
and

\langle Dx,y\rangle+\langle x,Dy\rangle=0\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,x,y\in{\cal V}.

(7)

Consequently,
in this setting,
if
a
commutes with
b
relative to
{\cal G}
, then
b
commutes with
a
relative to
{\cal G}
. When this happens, we say that
a
and
b
commute relative to
{\cal G}
.

While the above definition is formulated in the setting of any real inner product space, we will be mostly concerned with finite-dimensional real inner product spaces and matrix Lie groups.
For matrix Lie groups, we have the following result. Here,
I
denotes the Identity transformation/matrix,
(-\delta,\delta)
is an open interval in
\mathbb{R}
and for a differentiable function
\gamma
,
\gamma^{\prime}
denotes the derivative. This result shows that one can define commutativity via the tangent space/cone at the identity in
{\cal G}
.

Theorem 2.2

Let
{\cal G}
be a matrix Lie group over a finite-dimensional real inner product space
{\cal V}
. For any
D\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

D\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
, that is,

\exp(tD)\in{\cal G}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

D
belongs to the tangent space of
{\cal G}
at
I
, that is, there exists a smooth/differentiable curve
\gamma:(-\delta,\delta)\rightarrow{\cal G}
such that

\gamma(0)=I\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma^{\prime}(0)=D.

(c)

D
belongs to the Bouligand tangent cone of
{\cal G}
at
I
, that is, there exist sequences
\{D_{k}\}
in
{\cal G}
and
\{t_{k}\}
in
\mathbb{R}
such that
D_{k}\rightarrow I
,
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
\frac{D_{k}-I}{t_{k}}\rightarrow D
.

Thus,

\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
= Tangent space of
{\cal G}
at
I
= (Bouligand) tangent cone of
{\cal G}
at
I
.

In the above, the equivalence of
(a)
and
(b)
is well-known, see e.g.,
[
13
]
, Corollary 2.35 and
[
31
]
, pages 93 and 104.

The equivalence of
(b)
and
(c)
is given in

[
19
]
.

Suppose
{\cal V}
is a finite-dimensional real inner product space. For any
A\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
, let
A^{T}
denote the transpose of
A
defined by
\langle A^{T}x,y\rangle=\langle x,Ay\rangle
; let the trace of
A
be given by

\operatorname{tr}(A):=\sum\langle Ae_{i},e_{i}\rangle
, where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is any fixed orthonormal basis of
{\cal V}
. We make
\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
into a real inner product space via

\langle A,B\rangle:=\operatorname{tr}(AB^{T})
. Also, for
a,b\in{\cal V}
, we define
a\otimes b
by
(a\otimes b)(x):=\langle b,x\rangle a
. Then, we can describe commutativity in
{\cal V}
as follows:

a
commutes with
b
relative to
{\cal G}
if and only if
b\otimes a\perp\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G}).

In view of the above theorem, the condition
b\otimes a\perp\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
reduces to the ‘orbital relation’ of Seeger
[
30
]
and, in the case of a Euclidean Jordan algebra, reduces to operator commutativity, see
[
19
]
.

We now list/describe some examples. More will be given in later sections.

Example 2.3

Let
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
(carrying the usual inner product) with
{\cal G}
denoting the set of all
n\times n
permutation matrices. As
{\cal G}
is finite,
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})=\{0\}
; so, relative to
{\cal G}
, any two elements in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
commute.

Example 2.4

Let
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
(carrying the usual inner product) and
{\cal G}=\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})=\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and so if
a
commutes with
b
relative to
{\cal G}
, then
a=0
or
b=0
.

Example 2.5

In a real finite-dimensional inner product space
{\cal V}
, consider a proper cone
K
(so
K
is a closed convex cone which is pointed and has nonempty interior) with dual
K^{*}:=\{y\in{\cal V}:\langle y,x\rangle\geq 0\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in K\}.

Let
{\cal G}
be the automorphism group of
K
, i.e.,

{\cal G}=\mathrm{Aut(K)}:=\{L\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}:L(K)=K\}.

For any
L\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}
, Schneider and Vidyasagar
[
29
]
have shown that

\exp(tL)(K)\subseteq K\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,t\geq 0\,\,\mbox{if and only if}\,\,[x\in K,y\in K^{*},\langle x,y\rangle=0]\Rightarrow\langle L(x),y\rangle\geq 0.

From this, it follows that the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
is the space of all linear transformations
L
satisfying the condition

[x\in K,y\in K^{*},\langle x,y\rangle=0]\Rightarrow\langle L(x),y\rangle=0.

(8)

In the optimization/complementarity literature, a linear transformation
L
satisfying the condition (
8
) is said to be
Lyapunov-like
.
So, in the present setting, given
a,b\in{\cal V}
,

a
commutes with
b
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
if and only if

\langle L(a),b\rangle=0
for all Lyapunov-like transformations
L
on
K
.

For example, if
(a,b)
is a
complementary pair
, that is, if
a\in K
,
b\in K^{*}
and
\langle a,b\rangle=0
, then
a
commutes with
b
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
.
As
L\in\mathrm{Aut(K)}\Leftrightarrow L^{T}\in\mathrm{Aut(K^{*})}
, where
L^{T}
denotes the transpose of
L
, we see that

a
commutes with
b
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
if and only if
b
commutes with
a
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K^{*})}
.

As a further illustration, consider
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the usual inner product and let
K=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. It is known (and can easily be verified) that Lyapunov-like transformations on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
are just diagonal matrices. So, in this setting,
a
commutes with
b
if and only if

\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for every diagonal matrix
D
. This simplifies to:

a
commutes with
b
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
if and only if
a*b=0
, where
a*b
is the componentwise product of
a
and
b
.

Example 2.6

Consider a Euclidean Jordan algebra
{\cal V}
of rank
n
and unit element
e
. We assume that
{\cal V}
carries the trace inner product. (For various definitions, see Section 3.2 below.) Let
K
be the corresponding symmetric cone and
{\cal G}:=\mathrm{Aut(K)}
(as in the previous example). In this setting, it is known (
[
32
]
, Theorem 1) that Lyapunov-like transformations are of the form

L=L_{c}+D,

where
c\in{\cal V}
,
L_{c}(x)=x\circ c
, and
D
is a derivation, so

D(x\circ y)=(Dx)\circ y+x\circ Dy
for all
x,y\in{\cal V}
.
Then
a
commutes with
b
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
if and only if
\langle c\circ a,b\rangle=0
and
\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for all
c\in{\cal V}
and derivations
D
. Now
0=\langle c\circ a,b\rangle=\langle c,a\circ b\rangle
for all
c
implies that
a\circ b=0
. As we shall see in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, the condition that
\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for all derivations
D
is equivalent to the operator commutativity of
a
and
b
. In conclusion,

a
commutes with
b
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
if and only if

a
and
b
operator commute and
a\circ b=0.

Example 2.7

Consider the space
\mathcal{S}^{n}
of all
n\times n
real symmetric matrices with inner product

\langle X,Y\rangle:=\operatorname{tr}(XY)
(the trace of
XY
) and Jordan product

X\circ Y:=\frac{XY+YX}{2}
. Let
K
be the completely positive cone in
\mathcal{S}^{n}
, i.e.,

K=\Big\{\sum uu^{T}:u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,u\geq 0\Big\},

where
\sum uu^{T}
denotes a finite sum of matrices of the form
uu^{T}
. Let
{\cal G}
be the automorphism group of
K
(as defined in Example
2.5
). It is known, see
[
6
]
, Example 2, that every element of
\mathrm{Lie(Aut(K))}
is a transformation of the form
L_{A}
, where
L_{A}(X)=AX+XA^{T}
and
A
is an
n\times n
diagonal matrix. Hence, in this setting,
X
commutes with
Y
relative to
\mathrm{Aut(K)}
if and only if
\langle L_{A}(X),Y\rangle=0
for all
n\times n
diagonal matrices
A
.
Now, the latter condition simplifies to:
0=\operatorname{tr}(AXY+XAY)=2\,\operatorname{tr}(A(X\circ Y))
for all diagonal matrices

and, subsequently, to
\operatorname{diag}(X\circ Y)=0
. We conclude that
X
commutes with
Y
relative to
\operatorname{Aut}(K)
if and only if
\operatorname{diag}(X\circ Y)=0
.

Going beyond
\mathcal{S}^{n}
, we now consider the space
M_{n}
of all
n\times n
real matrices with inner product
\langle X,Y\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(XY^{T})
and corresponding norm
||\cdot||.
In
M_{n}
, let
{\cal O}(n)
denote the orthogonal group of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so,

{\cal O}(n):=\{A\in M_{n}:AA^{T}=I\}.

Let
{\cal G}_{1}
and
{\cal G}_{2}
denote closed subgroups of
{\cal O}(n)
. Within
\mathrm{L}(M_{n})
, consider the matrix Lie group
\cal G
of all linear transformations of the form

T:X\mapsto UXV\quad(X\in M_{n}),

where
U\in{\cal G}_{1}
and
V\in{\cal G}_{2}
. In the next result, we describe the Lie algebra of

and commutativity relative to

.

Theorem 2.8

Let
{\cal G}_{1}
,
{\cal G}_{2}
, and
{\cal G}
be as above.
Then,

(i)

\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
consists of transformations of the form

X\mapsto AX+XB
,
where
A\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G}_{1})
and
B\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G}_{2}).

(ii)

Given
X,Y\in M_{n}
,
X
commutes with
Y
relative to
{\cal G}
if and only if
YX^{T}\perp\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G}_{1})
and
X^{T}Y\perp\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G}_{2})
.

In what follows, we reserve the letters
U,U_{1},U_{2},U(t)
, etc., for matrices in
{\cal G}_{1}
and
V,V_{1},V_{2},V(t),
etc., for matrices in
{\cal G}_{2}
. Since a scalar multiple of the identity matrix
I
in
M_{n}
is the only matrix that commutes with all other matrices in
M_{n}
, and that
{\cal G}_{1}
and
{\cal G}_{2}
are subsets of
{\cal O}(n)
, we see that the equality
UXV=X
holds for all
X\in M_{n}
if and only if
U=V=I
or
U=V=-I
. Consequently, the equality
U_{1}XV_{1}=U_{2}XV_{2}
holds for all
X\in M_{n}
if and only if either
U_{1}=U_{2}
and
V_{1}=V_{2}
, or
U_{1}=-U_{2}
and
V_{1}=-V_{2}
.

(i)
Let
D\in\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
so that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist matrices
U(t)\in{\cal G}_{1}
and
V(t)\in{\cal G}_{2}
such that

\exp(tD)X=U(t)XV(t)\quad(X\in M_{n}).

In particular, for
t=0
, we have
X=U(0)XV(0)
for all
X
. By our observation above, either
U(0)=V(0)=I
or
U(0)=V(0)=-I
. Let
\varepsilon>0
so that relative to the operator norm on
M_{n}
, the open balls
B(I,\varepsilon)
and
B(-I,\varepsilon)
) around
I
and
-I
are disjoint. We now claim the existence of
\delta>0
such that
either for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
,
U(t)\in B(I,\varepsilon)
or for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
,
U(t)\in B(-I,\varepsilon)
.
This can be seen as follows: Suppose there is an
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
(t_{k})
of positive numbers converging to zero such that for all
k
,
||U(t_{k})-I||\geq\varepsilon
and
||U(t_{k})+I||\geq\varepsilon
. Then, there exists a subsequence of
(t_{k})
so that
U(t_{k_{j}})\rightarrow U\in{\cal G}_{1}
and
V(t_{k_{j}})\rightarrow V\in{\cal G}_{2}

as

. It follows that
||U-I||\geq\varepsilon
and
||U+I||\geq\varepsilon
. However, letting

in
\exp(t_{k_{j}}D)X=U(t_{k_{j}})XV(t_{k_{j}})
, we get

for all

, and, consequently,
U=V=I\in B(I,\varepsilon)
or
U=V=-I\in B(-I,\varepsilon)

Because of our choice of

We now show that

is differentiable. As

Now, for any

.

(ii)
Suppose

3
Semi-FTvN systems

In this section, we formally introduce semi-FTvN systems and describe some examples and results.
In what follows, for simplicity, we use the same inner product and norm notation.

Definition 3.1

We say that a triple
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system if
{\cal V}
and
{\cal W}
are real inner product spaces with
\lambda:{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal W}
satisfying the following conditions:

(A1)

\lambda
is norm-preserving, that is,
||\lambda(x)||=||x||
for all
x\in{\cal V}
;

(A2)

\lambda
is inner product expanding, that is,
\langle x,y\rangle\leq\big\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\big\rangle
for all
x,y\in{\cal V}
.

We recall that (
4
) defines a FTvN system.
In view of (
4
) implying (
2
), every FTvN system is a semi-FTvN system. The following statements are easy to verify:

•

({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system if and only if the following ‘sharpened’ Cauchy-Schwarz inequality holds for all
x,y\in{\cal V}
:
\langle x,y\rangle\leq\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\rangle\leq||x||\,||y||.

•

If
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system, then so is
({\cal V},{\cal W},\widetilde{\lambda}).

where
\widetilde{\lambda}(x):=-\lambda(-x)
.

•

({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system and
{\cal U}
is a (linear) subspace of
{\cal V}
, then
({\cal U},{\cal W},\lambda)
, with
\lambda
restricted to
{\cal U}
, is also a semi-FTvN system. (A similar statement for FTvN systems is false, see Example
5.7
below.)

•

If
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
and
({\cal W},{\cal Z},\mu)
are semi-FTvN systems, then so is their ‘composition’
({\cal V},{\cal Z},\mu\circ\lambda)
. (A similar statement for FTvN systems is false, see Example
4.3
below.)

•

If
({\cal V}_{1},{\cal W}_{1},\lambda_{1})
and
({\cal V}_{2},{\cal W}_{2},\lambda_{2})
are semi-FTvN systems, then so is their ‘product’
({\cal V}_{1}\times{\cal V}_{2},,{\cal W}_{1}\times{\cal W}_{2},\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2})
.

•

If
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system, then so is
(\overline{{\cal V}},\overline{{\cal W}},\overline{\lambda})
, where
\overline{{\cal V}}
and
\overline{{\cal W}}
are the Hilbert space completions of
{\cal V}
and
{\cal W}
respectively, and

\overline{\lambda}
is the unique extension of
\lambda.
(We note that
\lambda
is Lipschitzian on
{\cal V}
, hence uniformly continous.) It is not known if such a statement holds for FTvN systems, see Problem 2 on page 12 of
[
11
]
.

The following properties are known in the setting of FTvN systems
[
7
]
. They continue to hold in semi-FTvN systems.

Proposition 3.2

Suppose
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system. Then the following hold for all
x,y\in{\cal V}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
:

(a)

\lambda(\alpha\,x)=\alpha\,\lambda(x)
for all
\alpha\geq 0
.

(b)

||\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)||\leq||x-y||.

(c)

||\lambda(x+y)||\leq||\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)||.

Note:
Item
(b)
shows that
\lambda
is Lipschitz continuous.

(a)
Let
\alpha\geq 0
. Using
(A1)
and
(A2)
(of Definition
3.1
), we have

||\lambda(\alpha\,x)-\alpha\lambda(x)||^{2}=||\lambda(\alpha\,x)||^{2}+\alpha^{2}||\lambda(x)||^{2}-2\alpha\langle\lambda(\alpha x),\lambda(x)\rangle\leq||\alpha\,x||^{2}+\alpha^{2}||x||^{2}-2\alpha\langle\alpha x,x\rangle=0.

This proves
(a)
.

(b)
Using
(A1)
and
(A2)
, we have

||\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)||^{2}-||x-y||^{2}=2[\langle x,y\rangle-\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\rangle]\leq 0
. The stated Lipschitzian property follows.

(c)
From
(A1)
,
||\lambda(x+y)||^{2}=||x+y||^{2}=\langle x+y,x+y\rangle=\langle x+y,x\rangle+\langle x+y,y\rangle
.

From
(A2)
,

\langle x+y,x\rangle\leq\langle\langle\lambda(x+y),\lambda(x)\rangle
and

\langle x+y,x\rangle\leq\langle\langle\lambda(x+y),\lambda(y)\rangle
; hence,

||\lambda(x+y)||^{2}\leq\langle\lambda(x+y),\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)\rangle\leq||\lambda(x+y)||\,||\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)||,

(9)

where the second inequality is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inquality.
This gives
(c)
.
∎

3.1
Automorphisms and commutativity

Definition 3.3

In the setting of a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
,
an invertible linear transformation
A:{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal V}
is said to be an automorphism if
\lambda(Ax)=\lambda(x)
for all
x\in{\cal V}
.

Given a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, we let
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)}
(or
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
, for short) denote the set of all automorphisms. Since
\lambda
is norm-preserving, each automorphism is a bounded linear transformation and an orthogonal transformation (i.e., inner product preserving); so
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}\subseteq{\cal O}({\cal V}),

where
{\cal O}({\cal V})
(
=
the set of all invertible orthogonal linear transformations) denotes the orthogonal group of
{\cal V}
. As in (
1
), we let

\mathrm{Lie(Aut({\cal V}))}:=\{D\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}:\exp(tD)\in\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

While many of our concepts and results below are based on the group
\mathrm{Aut}({\cal V})
, in some settings, it may be more advantageous to work with a closed subgroup
{\cal G}
of
\mathrm{Aut}({\cal V})
(such as the connected component of identity in
\mathrm{Aut}({\cal V})
when
{\cal V}
is finite-dimensional or the one that appears in Example
3.17
below). In this case,

\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}:=\{D\in\mathrm{L({\cal V})}:\exp(tD)\in{\cal G}\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}\,\subseteq\,\mathrm{Lie(Aut({\cal V}))}.

We now define commutativity in a semi-FTvN system. Our motivation comes from Remark 3.7 in
[
9
]
and a recent formulation of operator commutativity in Euclidean Jordan algebras (
[
19
]
, Proposition 2).

Definition 3.4

Let
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
be a semi-FTvN system and
{\cal G}
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
. Given
a,b\in{\cal V}
,
we say that

(i)

a
strongly commutes with
b
if
\big\langle a,b\big\rangle=\big\langle\lambda(a),\lambda(b)\big\rangle.

(ii)

a
commutes with
b
if
\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for all
D\in\mathrm{Lie(Aut({\cal V}))}.

(iii)

a
commutes with
b
relative to
{\cal G}
if

\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for all
D\in\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
.

Remarks.

(\alpha)
In our previous work on FTvN systems
[
7
,
11
,
18
]
, we used the condition
\big\langle a,b\big\rangle=\big\langle\lambda(a),\lambda(b)\big\rangle
to define commutativity. In order to use the terminologies compatible with those in Euclidean Jordan algebras, henceforth we use the new term ‘strong commutativity’.

(\beta)

The above definitions are symmetric in
a
and
b
:
The first definition is clearly symmetric in
a
and
b
. From (
7
),
\langle Da,b\rangle=0\Rightarrow\langle Db,a\rangle=0
when
D\in\mathrm{Lie({\cal G}});
so, the other two definitions are also symmetric. Because of this symmetry, we use phrases such as ‘
a
and
b
(strongly) commute’ etc., while dealing with semi-FTvN systems.

We note that if
a
and
b
commute (relative to
{\cal G}
), then
\pm a
and
\pm b
commute (relative to
{\cal G})
. Also,
commutativity implies commutativity relative to any
{\cal G}
as
\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}\subseteq\mathrm{Lie(Aut({\cal V}))}.

(\gamma)
While strong commutativity of
a
and
b
is easily verifiable, checking commutativity requires the knowledge of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
(rather, its connected component of the Identity transformation) and its Lie algebra.

(\delta)
The relation

\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for all
D\in\mathrm{Lie(Aut({\cal G}))}
is a type of ‘orbital relation’ defined in
[
30
]
.

(\epsilon)
Suppose
{\cal V}
is finite dimensional so that
{\cal G}
is a matrix Lie group. It is well known that the connected component
{\cal G}_{0}
of
{\cal G}
is also a matrix Lie group (cf.
[
13
]
, Prop. 1.8). Hence, if
a
and
b
commute relative to
{\cal G}
, then they commute relative to
{\cal G}_{0}
.

In the theorem below, we show that strong commutativity implies commutativity. First, we describe commutativity in a semi-FTvN system; the following result and its proof are identical to those for FTvN systems (
[
7
]
, Section 2).

Proposition 3.5

In a semi-FTvN system, the following are equivalent:

(a)

x
and
y
strongly commute, i.e.,
\langle x,y\rangle=\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\rangle.

(b)

||\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)||=||x-y||.

(c)

||\lambda(x+y)||=||\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)||
.

(d)

\lambda(x+y)=\lambda(x)+\lambda(y).

Using
(A1)
(of Definition
3.1
), we see that

||\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)||^{2}-||x-y||^{2}=2[\langle x,y\rangle-\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\rangle]=||\lambda(x+y)||^{2}-||\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)||^{2}.

The equivalence
(a)\Leftrightarrow(b)\Leftrightarrow(c)
follows.

(c)\Rightarrow(d)
: When
(c)
holds, we have equality in the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (
9
). Hence,
one of the vectors,
\lambda(x+y)
or
\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)
, is a nonnegative multiple of the other. Since their norms are equal (as we are assuming
(c)
), these vectors are equal. Item
(d)
follows.

Finally,
(d)\Rightarrow(c)
is obvious.
∎

Remarks.
In a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
we define the
\lambda
-orbit of an element
u\in{\cal V}
by

[u]:=\{x\in{\cal V}:\lambda(x)=\lambda(u)\}.

One interesting consequence of Item
(b)
in the above proposition is that

if
x,y
in
[u]
strongly commute, then
x=y
.

We now state one of our main results.

Theorem 3.6

In any semi-FTvN system
(
in particular, in any FTvN system
)
, strong commutativity implies commutativity
(
relative to any closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})})
.

Consider a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
and fix a closed subgroup
{\cal G}
of
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
. Suppose
a
and
b
strongly commute, that is,
\langle a,b\rangle=\langle\lambda(a),\lambda(b)\rangle
. Fix any
D\in\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
and consider the real-valued function

\phi(t):=\big\langle\exp(tD)a,b\big\rangle\quad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Then,
\phi
is differentiable and

\phi(t)=\big\langle\exp(tD)a,b\big\rangle\leq\big\langle\lambda(\exp(tD)a),\lambda(b)\big\rangle=\big\langle\lambda(a),\lambda(b)\big\rangle=\langle a,b\rangle=\phi(0),

where the first inequality is due to the definition of a semi-FTvN system, the second equality is due to the definition of an automorphism, and the third equality is due to the strong commutativity of
a
and
b
. So,
\phi
attains its global maximum at
t=0
. From
\phi^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have

\langle Da,b\rangle=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0.

Since
D
is arbitrary in
\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
,
a
and
b
commute relative to
{\cal G}
. When
{\cal G}=\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
, we get the commutativity of
a
and
b
.
∎

3.2
The center and unit element

The concepts of center and unit element have been defined and found to be useful in the setting of FTvN systems
[
11
]
. Now we extend these to semi-FTvN systems.

Definition 3.7

In a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, we define the center
{\cal C}
and weak center
{\cal C}_{w}
as follows:

{\cal C}:=\{x\in{\cal V}:x\,\,\mbox{strongly commutes with every}\,\,y\in{\cal V}\},

{\cal C}_{w}:=\{x\in{\cal V}:x\,\,\mbox{ commutes with every}\,\,y\in{\cal V}\}=\{x\in{\cal V}:Dx=0\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,D\in\mathrm{Lie(Aut({\cal V}))}\}.

Note: The second equality in
{\cal C}_{w}
is due to the fact that
\langle Dx,y\rangle=0
for all
y
implies that
Dx=0
.

Proposition 3.8

In a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
,
the center
{\cal C}
and the weak center
{\cal C}_{w}
are closed (linear) subspaces of
{\cal V}
. Moreover,
{\cal C}\subseteq{\cal C}_{w}
and
\lambda
is linear on
{\cal C}
.

Clearly,
{\cal C}_{w}
is a closed subspace of
{\cal V}
. That
{\cal C}\subseteq{\cal C}_{w}
follows from Theorem
3.6
.
We now show that
{\cal C}
is a closed subspace of
{\cal V}
and that
\lambda
is linear on it.
Suppose
a,b\in{\cal C}
. Then, by Proposition
3.2
(a), any nonnegative multiple of
a
is also in
{\cal C}
. Moreover, as
a
strongly commutes with
-a
, by Proposition
3.5
(d),
\lambda(-a)=-\lambda(a)
. It follows that any multiple of
a
is in
{\cal C}
. Now repeated use of Item (d) in Proposition
3.5
shows that
a+b\in{\cal C}
. That
{\cal C}
is closed comes from the continuity of
\lambda
and Item
(a)
in Proposition
3.5
. Finally, Proposition
3.5
(d) and positive homogeneity of
\lambda
gives the linearity on
{\cal C}
.
∎

Definition 3.9

In the setting of a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, a nonzero element
e\in{\cal V}
is said to be
a unit if
{\cal C}=\mathbb{R}\,e
.

Remarks.
In an FTvN system, the center can be described in many ways, see Section 6 in
[
11
]
. In particular, it is shown in
[
11
]
, Proposition 6.3, that
u\in{\cal C}
if and only if

A result of this type is proved below for a semi-FTvN system.

Proposition 3.10

Let
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
be a semi-FTvN system. Consider the following statements:

(i)

c\in{\cal C}
.

(ii)

[c]=\{c\}
.

(iii)

Ac=c
for all
A\in\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
.

(iv)

c\in{\cal C}_{w}
.

Then,
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)
.
In particular, if
e
is a unit element, then
Ae=e
for all
A\in\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
.

Note: Example
3.16
below shows that the (reverse) implication
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
need not hold. Also, Example
3.15
shows that the (reverse) implication
(iv)\Rightarrow(iii)
need not hold (even in an FTvN system).

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Assume
(i)
, fix
c\in{\cal C}
and
x\in{\cal V}
such that
\lambda(x)=\lambda(c)
. As
c
strongly commutes with
x
, from Proposition 3.5(b),
||x-c||=||\lambda(x)-\lambda(c)||=0
. So,
x=c
. This gives
(ii)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Assume
(ii)
and let
A\in\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
. Then,
Ac\in[c]=\{c\}
, so
Ac=c
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)
: Assume
Ac=c
for all

. Then, for any

and

, we have
\exp(tD)\in\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
, hence
\exp(tD)c=c
. Now, differentiation leads to

. By Definition

,

.
∎

Definition 3.11

In a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, a set

is said to be a

•

a spectral set if
x\in E\Rightarrow[x]\subseteq E
or, equivalently,
E=\lambda^{-1}(Q)
for some

;

•

a weakly spectral set if
A(E)\subseteq E
for all

.

Proposition 3.12

In a semi-FTvN system, the center is a spectral set and the weak center is a weakly spectral set.

To simplify the notation, let
{\cal G}:=\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
.

If
x\in{\cal C}
, then by the above proposition,
[x]=\{x\}\subseteq{\cal C}
. Hence, the center is a spectral set.

To show that
{\cal C}_{w}
is weakly spectral, we need to show that
A({\cal C}_{w})\subseteq{\cal C}_{w}
for all
A\in{\cal G}
. In view of Definition
3.7
, we need to show:

for any
c\in{\cal C}_{w}

A\in{\cal G}
, and
D\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
, it holds that

D(Ac)=0
.

We fix
c\in{\cal C}_{w}
,
A\in{\cal G}
, and
D\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
. Then, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
exp(tD)\in{\cal G}
,

exp(tA^{-1}DA)=A^{-1}exp(tD)A\in{\cal G}

and so,
A^{-1}DA\in\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
. As
c\in{\cal C}_{w}
, we have
A^{-1}DAc=0
and
DAc=0
. As
D
is arbitrary in
\operatorname{Lie}({\cal G})
,
Ac\in{\cal C}_{w}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We now describe some examples. Recall that in the setting of a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
,
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
is an abbreviation for

\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)}
.

Example 3.13

Consider the semi-FTvN system
(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R},\lambda)
, where
\lambda(x)=|x|
.
As

\mathrm{Aut(\mathbb{R})}=\{I,-I\}
and
\mathrm{Lie(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{R}))}=\{0\}
, where
I
and
0
denote the Identity and zero transformations respectively, we see that any two elements
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
commute. On the other hand,
a
and
b
strongly commute (that is,
ab=|a|\,|b|
) if and only if one of them (either
a
or
b
) is a nonnegative multiple of the other. Thus, in this system,

{\cal C}=\{0\}
while
{\cal C}_{w}=\mathbb{R}
.

Example 3.14

Consider the semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},\mathbb{R},\lambda)
, where
{\cal V}
is a real finite dimensional inner product space with
dim({\cal V})>1
and
\lambda(x)=||x||.
By isomorphism considerations, we may assume that
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n>1
) with the usual inner product. Consider
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We claim that
a
and
b
commute in the semi-FTvN system
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R},\lambda)
if and only if
b
is a scalar multiple of
a
or
a
is a scalar multiple of
b
. To see this, suppose without loss of generality,
a\neq 0
.
As
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},\mathbb{R},\lambda)}={\cal O}(n)
(the orthogonal group of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and
n>1
, the corresponding Lie algebra consists of all skew-symmetric matrices (
[
13
]
, 2.5.5); hence,
a
and
b
commute if and only if
\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for every skew-symmetric matrix
D
. It is known that the orthogonal complement of the space of the skew-symmetric matrices is the space of the symmetric matrices relative to the trace inner product
\langle A,B\rangle_{\operatorname{tr}}={\operatorname{tr}}(AB^{\top})
. Since
\langle Da,b\rangle=\langle D,ba^{\top}\rangle_{\operatorname{tr}}
, we see that
a
and
b
commute if and only if
ba^{\top}
is symmetric. Then,
ba^{\top}=ab^{\top}
, hence
ba^{\top}a=ab^{\top}a
. Since
a\neq 0
, we have
b=\frac{b^{\top}a}{\|a\|^{2}}a
. Thus,
b
is a scalar multiple of
a
. Conversely, if
b
is a scalar multiple of
a
, then
\langle Da,b\rangle=0
for every skew-symmetric matrix
D
, hence
a
and
b
commute. This proves our claim. Now suppose
a\in{\cal C}_{w}
. Let
{\bf 1}
be the vector of all
1
s and
d
be a vector with all entries distinct (recall that
n>1
). Since
a
commutes with both
{\bf 1}
and
d
, we must have
a=0
. Hence
{\cal C}_{w}=\{0\}
.
We remark that
a
and
b
strongly commute in this system if and only if
\langle a,b\rangle=||a||\,||b||
in which case,
a
(or
b
) is a nonnegative multiple of
b
(respectively,
a
). Consequently, since
-a
is not a nonnegative multiple of
a
unless
a=0
, we see that
(the center)
{\cal C}=\{0\}
. Thus, in this system,
{\cal C}={\cal C}_{w}=\{0\}
.
Note that this is different from the
n=1
case (of Example
3.13
).

Example 3.15

Consider
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the usual inner product. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
x^{\downarrow}
denote the vector obtained by rearranging the entries of
x
in the decreasing order. As
\langle x,y\rangle\leq\langle x^{\downarrow},y^{\downarrow}\rangle
(which is the Hardy-Littlewood-Polya inequality) and
||x^{\downarrow}||=||x||
, we see that
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system, where
\lambda(x)=x^{\downarrow}
. (Actually, this is an FTvN system arising from the Euclidean Jordan algebra
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\circ)
, where, for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\langle x,y\rangle
is the usual inner product and

is the componentwise product.) As
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)
is the group of all permutation matrices on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we see that the corresponding Lie algebra is
\{0\}
. Hence, any two elements in this semi-FTvN system commute. With
{\bf 1}
denoting the vector of ones in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we shall see (in Theorem

) that

{\cal C}=\mathbb{R}{\bf 1}
while
{\cal C}_{w}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We note that when
n>1
, the vectors
a=(1,0,\ldots,0)
and
b=(0,1,0,\ldots,0)
commute, but not strongly:
\langle a,b\rangle=0
, while
\langle\lambda(a),\lambda(b)\rangle=1
.

Example 3.16

In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, let
{\cal V}:=\{r(1,2):r\in\mathbb{R}\}.
Consider
\lambda:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\lambda(x)=x^{\downarrow}.
Then,
({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{2},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system, where for any

4
) fails and so,

\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}=\{I\}
,

.

Note
: It will be shown in the more general setting of a Euclidean Jordan algebra that commutativity is the same as operator commutativity. Consequently, in the Euclidean Jordan algebra

of all

Example 3.17

(
Normal decomposition system

[
21
]
) Let
{\cal V}
be a real inner product space,
{\cal G}
be a closed subgroup of the orthogonal group of
{\cal V}
, and
\gamma:{\cal V}\to{\cal V}
be a map satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\gamma
is
{\cal G}
-invariant, that is,
\gamma(Ax)=\gamma(x)

(b)

.

For any two elements

As observed in

Example 3.18

Let
M_{m,n}(\mathbb{C})
denote the space of all
m\times n

complex matrices under the inner product

\langle X,Y\rangle={\rm Re}\,\operatorname{tr}(XY^{*})
, where
Y^{*}
denotes the conjugate-transpose of
X
. With
\lambda(X):=\operatorname{Diag}(s(X))
denoting the (rectangular) diagonal matrix of the singular values of

\langle X,Y\rangle\leq\langle\lambda(X),\lambda(Y)\rangle

\max\Big\{\!\left<A,X\right>:\,X\in[B]\Big\}=\langle\lambda(A),\lambda(B)\rangle\quad(\forall\,A,B\in{\cal V}).

(10)

If

XY^{*}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,X^{*}Y
are Hermitian.

By replacing complex numbers by real numbers, we can consider the space

and obtain analogous results. In particular, we see that
\big(M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R}),M_{m,n}(\mathbb{R}),\lambda\big)

[

[

Moreover,

If

XY^{T}\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,X^{T}Y

We recall, see Corollary

(which is

as per our earlier notation),

and

We draw attention to the following characterization result due to Lewis and Sendov, see
[

Theorem 3.19

Let
X,Y\in M_{n}
. Then
XY^{T}
and
X^{T}Y
are symmetric if and only if there is a signed permutation
Q
and orthogonal matrices
U
and

such that

X=U\operatorname{Diag}(Qs(X))V
and

We end this example by describing the center
{\cal C}
and weak center
{\cal C}_{w}
of
M_{n}
.
If
C\in{\cal C}
, then
\langle C,X\rangle=\langle\lambda(C),\lambda(X)\rangle
for all

In particular,
\langle C,-C\rangle=\langle\lambda(C),\lambda(-C)\rangle
. As
\lambda(C)=\lambda(-C)

\langle C,-C\rangle\geq 0
and, consequently,

Now consider the case

On the other hand, suppose

4
The FTvN system induced by a Euclidean Jordan algebra

Suppose
({\cal V},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\circ)
is a Euclidean Jordan algebra of rank
n
with unit element
e

[
4
]
. Here, for any two elements
x
and
y
,
\langle x,y\rangle
denotes the inner product and
x\circ y
denotes the Jordan product. For any
x\in{\cal V}
, by the spectral decomposition theorem (
[
4
]
, Theorem III.1.2), we can write

x=x_{1}e_{1}+x_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}e_{n},

(11)

where
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
is a Jordan frame and
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}
are (unique) real numbers (called the eigenvalues of
x
). We define the trace of
x
as
\operatorname{tr}(x):=x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}
and trace inner product over
{\cal V}
by
\langle x,y\rangle_{\operatorname{tr}}:=\operatorname{tr}(x\circ y)
. This inner product is compatible with the Jordan product. Moreover, Jordan frames, eigenvalues and spectral decompositions remain the same when we replace the given inner product with the trace inner product.
Henceforth, in the setting of a Euclidean Jordan algebra, we assume that
{\cal V}
carries this trace inner product.

Let

\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
denote the group of all invertible linear transformations on
A
satisfying the condition

A(x\circ y)=Ax\circ Ay\,\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,x,y\in{\cal V}.

Elements of
\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
are called (algebra) automorphisms of
{\cal V}
. (These are different from the ‘cone’ automorphisms, namely, elements of
\operatorname{Aut}(K)
, where
K
is the symmetric cone of
{\cal V}
.)

Given the spectral decomposition of
x
(as above), let
\lambda(x)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the vector of eigenvalues of
x
written in the decreasing order, that is,

\lambda(x)=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{\downarrow}.

Then, it is shown in
[
11
]
, Section 4, Example 4.6, that
({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)
is an FTvN system. In this setting, it is known, see
[
11
]
, Section 7, Example 4.6, that

A\in\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)}
if and only if
A\in\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}.

(So, in the setting of a Euclidean Jordan algebra, the automorphisms of the FTvN system
({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)
are the same as the algebra automorphisms.)

We recall that in a Euclidean Jordan algebra, two elements
a
and
b

operator commute
if
L_{a}L_{b}=L_{b}L_{a}
(where
L_{a}
is the linear operator defined by
L_{a}(x):=a\circ x
for all
x\in{\cal V}
), or equivalently,
a
and
b
have their spectral representations with respect to the same Jordan frame (
[
4
]
, Lemma X.2.2). We say that
a
and
b

strongly operator commute
if there exists a Jordan frame
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}\}
such that
a
and
b
have spectral decompositions

a=a_{1}e_{1}+a_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}e_{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad b=b_{1}e_{1}+b_{2}e_{2}+\cdots+b_{n}e_{n}

with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}
and
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{n}
. It is shown in
[
7
]
, Proposition 4.4, that
in the induced FTvN system
({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)

a
and
b
strongly commute if and only if they strongly operator commute in the Euclidean Jordan algebra
{\cal V}
.

We now prove a similar result for commutativity.

Theorem 4.1

Consider a Euclidean Jordan algebra
({\cal V},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\circ)
of rank
n
with unit element
e
. Let
a,b\in{\cal V}
. Then,
a
and
b
commute in the FTvN system
({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)
if and only if they operator commute in the Euclidean Jordan algebra
{\cal V}
.

We have noted previously that
({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)
is a FTvN system. Furthermore, as noted above,

\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda)}=\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}
. It is known (
[
20
]
, Lemma 7 and Theorem 8) that a transformation
D\in\mathrm{Lie(\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V}))}
(called a derivation) if and only if it is a sum of transformations of the form
L_{u}L_{v}-L_{v}L_{u}
. So,
a
and
b
commute if and only if
\langle(L_{u}L_{v}-L_{v}L_{u})a,b\rangle=0
for all
u,v\in{\cal V}
. Since the latter statement simplifies to
\langle(L_{a}L_{b}-L_{b}L_{a})u,v\rangle=0
, and
u,v
are arbitrary, commutativity of
a
and
b
reduces to the equality
L_{a}L_{b}-L_{b}L_{a}=0
, which is the operator commutativity of
a
and
b
.

∎

The following result provides a description of the center and weak center in a Euclidean Jordan algebra. Recall that the unit element
e
in the algebra
{\cal V}
satisfies the condition
x\circ e=x
for all
x
.

Theorem 4.2

Suppose
{\cal V}
is a Euclidean Jordan algebra with unit element
e
. Let
{\cal C}
and
{\cal C}_{w}
denote, respectively, the center and weak center in
{\cal V}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

{\cal C}=\mathbb{R}\,e
;

(ii)

If
{\cal V}
is simple, then
{\cal C}_{w}=\mathbb{R}\,e
;

(iii)

If
{\cal V}=\Pi_{i=1}^{k}{\cal V}_{i}
, where
{\cal V}_{i}
is a simple Euclidean Jordan algebra with unit
e_{i}
, then

{\cal C}_{w}=\Pi_{i=1}^{k}\,\mathbb{R}\,e_{i}.

In particular, when
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with componentwise product as Jordan product, we have
{\cal C}_{w}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Item
(i)
is known, see
[
11
]
, Section 6, Example 4.6.

(ii)
Clearly,
\mathbb{R}\,e\subseteq{\cal C}_{w}
. To see the reverse inclusion, suppose that
{\cal V}
is simple and
a\in{\cal C}_{w}
. By the previous theorem,
a
operator commutes with every
x\in{\cal V}
. In particular,
a
operator commutes with every primitive idempotent
c
in
{\cal V}
. Writing the spectral decompositions of
a
and (the primitive idempotent)
c
with respect to a common Jordan frame, we see that (every)
c
must appear in some spectral decomposition of
a
.
Suppose, to get a contradiction,
a
is not a multiple of
e
, that is,
a
has two distinct eigenvalues. Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}

(k>1)
, be the distinct eigenvalues of
a
. Then, the closed sets

E_{i}:=\{x\in{\cal V}:\langle a,x\rangle=a_{i}\}\,\,(1\leq i\leq k)

are disjoint. Since every primitive idempotent appears in some spectral decomposition of
a
and
{\cal V}
carries the trace inner product, we see that every primitive idempotent is in some
E_{i}
. This means that the set
{\cal T}({\cal V})
of all primitive idempotents in
{\cal V}
is contained in the disjoint union of closed sets
E_{i}
. However,
{\cal T}({\cal V})
is connected in (any) simple algebra
{\cal V}
, see
[
4
]
, Page 71 or
[
10
]
, Theorem 3.1. We reach a contradiction. Thus,
a
must be a multiple of
e
, proving our assertion.

(iii)
Suppose
{\cal V}=\Pi_{i=1}^{k}{\cal V}_{i}
, where each
{\cal V}_{i}
is simple. For
a,b\in{\cal V}
, we write
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
and
b=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})
and note that
\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}b_{i}
and
a\circ b=(a_{1}\circ b_{2},a_{2}\circ b_{2},\ldots,a_{k}\circ b_{k})
. Then
a
and
b
operator commute if and only if
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
operator commute in
{\cal V}_{i}
for each
i
. It follows that
a
is in the weak center of
{\cal V}
if and only if
a_{i}
is in the weak center of
{\cal V}_{i}
for each
i
. As
{\cal V}_{i}
is simple, from
(ii)
, the weak center of
{\cal V}_{i}
is
\mathbb{R}\,e_{i}
, where
e_{i}
is the unit element of
{\cal V}_{i}
. We see that
{\cal C}_{w}
is the product of
\mathbb{R}\,e_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,k
. In particular, when
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
{\cal V}_{i}=\mathbb{R}
with
e_{i}=1
. Hence, in this setting, the weak center is
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We have observed before that the composition of two semi-FTvN systems is again a semi-FTvN system. The following example shows that a similar statement fails for FTvN systems, that is,
composition of FTvN systems need not be a FTvN system.

Example 4.3

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}}

\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{*}:=2\langle x,y\rangle
. (Recall that
\mathbb{R}^{2}
carries the usual inner product.) Consider two FTvN systems
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\lambda)
and
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\mathbb{R}^{2},\mu)
, where for any
x=(x_{1},x_{2})

\lambda(x):=x^{\downarrow}
and
\mu(x):=(x_{1}+|x_{2}|,x_{1}-|x_{2}|).
In fact,
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\lambda)
is the scaled version (on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
) of the FTvN system of Example
3.15
and
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\mathbb{R}^{2},\mu)
is the scaled version of the Jordan spin algebra on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, see Example 4.14 in
[
11
]
. Let
\nu:=\mu\circ\lambda
. Clearly,
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\mathbb{R}^{2},\nu)
is a semi-FTvN system. We claim that
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\mathbb{R}^{2},\nu)
is not a FTvN system. Consider the vectors

and

so that
\nu(c)=(2,0)
and
\nu(u)=(3,1)
. Suppose
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}^{2}},\mathbb{R}^{2},\nu)

such that

5
The semi-FTvN system induced by a complete hyperbolic polynomial

Let
{\cal V}
be a nonzero finite-dimensional real vector space,
0\neq e\in{\cal V}
, and
p
be a real homogeneous polynomial
of degree
n
on
{\cal V}
. We say that
p
is hyperbolic relative to
e
if
p(e)\neq 0
and
for every
x\in{\cal V}
, the roots of the univariate polynomial
t\rightarrow p(te-x)
are all
real. Given such a polynomial
p
, for any
x\in{\cal V}
, let
\lambda(x)
denote the vector
of roots of the polynomial
t\rightarrow p(te-x)
with entries written in decreasing
order (so,
\lambda(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We shall call
\lambda:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
the
eigenvalue map
of
p
(relative to
e
).

Without imposing additional conditions on
p
, we list some known properties:

(P1)

Proposition
3.2
continues to hold for the eigenvalue map of
p
(though the proofs are not elementary), see
[
2
]
.

(P2)

In the canonical setting of
{\cal V}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
p(e)=1
, for any two elements

x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exist real symmetric
n\times n
matrices
A
and
B
such that

\lambda(tx+sy)=\lambda(tA+sB)\quad(t,s\in\mathbb{R}),

(12)

where the right-hand side denotes the eigenvalue vector
of a symmetric matrix. This is an observation due to Gurvits
[
12
]
based on the validity of Lax conjecture (see
[
23
,
14
]
).

One important consequence of (
12
) is the validity of Lidskii inequality (from a similar inequality for real symmetric matrices):

\lambda(x)-\lambda(y)\prec\lambda(x-y)\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,x\\
,y\in{\cal V}.

(13)

(P3)

The set

K:=\{x\in{\cal V}:\lambda(x)\geq 0\}

is a closed convex cone with nonempty interior, called the
hyperbolicity cone
corresponding to
p
and
e
. For any
d\in K^{\circ}
(the interior of
K
),

is hyperbolic relative to
d
and the corresponding hyperbolicity cone is (still)

. In fact,
K^{\circ}
is the connected component of the set
\{x\in{\cal V}:p(x)\neq 0\}
that contains

. These results are shown in
[
5
]
. Moreover, from
[
2
]
, Fact. 2.9, we have

K\cap-K=\{x\in{\cal V}:\lambda(x)=0\}.

(14)

Some deeper results on hyperbolicity cones include:

K
is facially exposed
[
27
]
, and amenable
[
24
]
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
2
]
, Definition 2.8)
Let
p
be hyperbolic on
{\cal V}
relative to
e
. Let
\lambda
be the corresponding eigenvalue map. We say that
p
is complete (relative to
e
) if the following implication holds:

\lambda(x)=0\Rightarrow x=0.

Remarks.
Let
p
(hyperbolic on
{\cal V}
relative to
e
) be complete. In view of (
14
), this completeness property of

is equivalent to (the corresponding hyperbolicity cone)
K
being pointed (meaning
K\cap-K=\{0\}
). Since
K
is the hyperbolicity cone of
p
relative to any

, we see that the

is complete relative to any
d
in
K^{\circ}.

A few general ways of constructing complete hyperbolic polynomials are as follows:

•

Suppose
p
and

are hyperbolic on
{\cal V}
relative to the same

. If
q
is complete, then so is the product
pq
.

•

Given a finite set of complete hyperbolic polynomials over
{\cal V}
(all hyperbolic with respect to the same

), their product is again complete and hyperbolic.

•

Suppose
p
is a complete hyperbolic polynomial of degree
n
over
{\cal V}
with respect to
e\in{\cal V}
; let
\lambda:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the corresponding eigenvalue map. Let
q
be a permutation invariant complete hyperbolic polynomial of degree

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with respect to

; let

denote the corresponding eigenvalue map. Then, the composition

is a complete hyperbolic polynomial of degree

on
{\cal V}
with respect to

whose eigenvalue map is
\mu\circ\lambda
. (That

is hyperbolic with eigenvalue map

]
, Theorem 3.1.)
As
(\mu\circ\lambda)(x)=0\Rightarrow\mu(\lambda(x))=0\Rightarrow\lambda(x)=0\Rightarrow x=0
, we see the completeness of

.

Theorem 5.2

Suppose
p
is a complete hyperbolic polynomial on
{\cal V}
relative to
e
. Let
\lambda
be the corresponding eigenvalue map. Define

\langle x,y\rangle:=\frac{1}{4}\Big\{||\lambda(x+y)||^{2}-||\lambda(x-y)||^{2}\Big\}\quad(x,y\in{\cal V})

(15)

(where the right-hand side is computed in

equipped with the usual norm). Then, relative to the above,

becomes a (real, finite-dimensional) inner product space and the triple

becomes a semi-FTvN system. Moreover, the hyperbolicity cone
\{x\in{\cal V}:\lambda(x)\geq 0\}
is a proper cone.

That

defines an inner product on

,

\lambda:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is norm-preserving, and

\langle x,y\rangle\leq\langle\lambda(x),\lambda(y)\rangle
for all

, are shown in

]
, Theorem 4.2 and Proposition 4.4.
Additionally, the pointedness of the hyperbolicity cone comes from (

is a pointed closed convex cone with nonempty interior, by definition, it is a proper cone.
∎

Remarks.
Suppose

Example 5.7

Remarks.
In a FTvN system

, the

We end this section with a characterization of strong commutativity in the semi-FTvN system induced by a complete hyperbolic polynomial.

Theorem 5.8

Consider the semi-FTvN system induced by a complete hyperbolic polynomial. Then,
a
and
b
strongly commute in this system if and only if

[\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)]^{\downarrow}=\lambda(a-b).

Suppose
[\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)]^{\downarrow}=\lambda(a-b).
Then,
||\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)||=||\lambda(a-b)||=||a-b||.
By Proposition
3.5
,
a
and
b
strongly commute.

Now suppose
a
and
b
strongly commute so that (by Proposition
3.5
),

||\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)||=||a-b||.
By (
13
) and the Hardy-Littlewood-Polya theorem (
[
3
]
, Theorem II.1.10), there exists a doubly stochastic matrix
A
such that

\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)=A\lambda(a-b).

Moreover, by Birkhoff’s theorem (
[
3
]
, Theorem II.2.3),
A
could be written as a convex combination of permutation matrices. Thus,

\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}t_{k}P_{k}\Big)\lambda(a-b),

where
t_{k}
s are positive with sum
1
and
P_{k}
s are permutation matrices. We claim that
N=1
.
Now,

||a-b||=||\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)||=||\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}t_{k}P_{k}\Big)\lambda(a-b)||\leq

\sum_{k=1}^{N}t_{k}||P_{k}\big(\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)\big)||=\sum_{k=1}^{N}t_{k}||\lambda(a)-\lambda(b))||=\sum_{k=1}^{N}t_{k}||a-b||=||a-b||.

By the strict convexity of the (usual) norm,
N=1
. This shows that
\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)=P_{1}\big(\lambda(a-b)\big)
, hence,
[\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)]^{\downarrow}=\lambda(a-b).
This completes the proof.
∎

Remarks.
Consider a Euclidean Jordan algebra which corresponds to a complete hyperbolic polynomial. In this setting, the above result says that
[\lambda(a)-\lambda(b)]^{\downarrow}=\lambda(a-b)
if and only if
a
and
b
strongly operator commute. When specialized to the algebra of complex Hermitian matrices, this statement yields a result of Massey et al
[
masseyetal.
]
.

6
Commutation principles

In this section, we describe some commutation principles where the focus is on commutativity instead of strong commutativity. Our goal is to extend the following commutation principle of Ramírez et al., from Euclidean Jordan algebras to group/semi-FTvN settings.

Theorem 6.1

(Ramírez, Seeger, and Sossa
[
26
]
) Let
{\cal V}
be a Euclidean Jordan algebra,
E
be a spectral set in
{\cal V}
, and

F:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a spectral function. Let
\Theta:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Fréchet differentiable. If
a
is a
local minimizer/maximizer of the map

x\in E\rightarrow\Theta(x)+F(x),

then
a
and
\Theta^{\prime}(a)
operator commute.

In
[
9
]
, Theorem 1.2, Gowda and Jeong extended the above result by assuming that
E
and
F
are weakly spectral, see below for the definitions. Their short proof (see also the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
30
]
) made use of the Lie algebra of the automorphism group of
{\cal V}
. Now, we extend this further to group settings.

In the following, we will formally describe Theorem
1.1
and provide its proof. We rely on the notation used in the Introduction. We recall that for any map
h:{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal V}
and
E\subseteq{\cal V}
, the
variational inequality problem

\mathrm{VI}(h,E)
is to

find
x^{*}\in E
such that
\langle h(x^{*}),x-x^{*}\rangle\geq 0
for all
x\in E

and for a nonempty set
S
in
{\cal V}
, the
normal cone of
S
is given by

N_{S}(a):=\{d\in{\cal V}:\langle d,x-a\rangle\leq 0,\,\,\forall\,\,x\in S\}.

Theorem 6.2

Let
{\cal V}
be a real inner product space,
{\cal G}
be a closed subgroup of the group of invertible bounded linear transformations on
{\cal V}
. Let

•

E
be set in
{\cal V}
that is
{\cal G}
-invariant (so,
A(E)\subseteq E
for all
A\in{\cal G}
),

•

Let

F:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued function that is
{\cal G}
-invariant (so,
F(Ax)=F(x)
for all
A\in{\cal G}
and
x\in{\cal V}
),

•

\Theta:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Fréchet differentiable,

•

h:{\cal V}\rightarrow{\cal V}
,

•

a\in E
and
c\in{\cal V}
.

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

If
a
is a
local minimizer/maximizer of the map

x\in E\rightarrow\Theta(x)+F(x),

then
a
commutes with
\Theta^{\prime}(a)
relative to
{\cal G}
.

(ii)

If
a
is an optimizer of

\underset{x\in E}{max/min}\quad\langle c,x\rangle,

then
a
commutes with
c
relative to
{\cal G}
;

(iii)

If
a
is a solution of the of the variational inequality problem
\mathrm{VI}(h,E)
,
then
a
commutes with
h(a)
relative to
{\cal G}
.

(iv)

Relatively to
{\cal G}
,
a
commutes with every element in the normal cone of
E
.

(i)
First, consider the case when
a
is a local
minimizer of
\Theta+F
over
E
.
Then we have

\Theta(a)+F(a)\leq\Theta(x)+F(x)\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,x\in B_{a}\cap E,

where

denotes some open ball around
a
. Let
D\in\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
so that for all

,
\exp(tD)\in\mathrm{{\cal G}}
. As

and

are
{\cal G}
-invariant, we have

\exp(tD)a\in E
and

for all

. Moreover, by the continuity of

in

\exp(tD)a\in B_{a}\cap E
for all

For such

\Theta(a)+F(a)\leq\Theta\big(\exp(tD)a\big)+F\big(\exp(tD)a\big)=\Theta\big(\exp(tD)a\big)+F(a)

and so
\Theta(a)\leq\Theta(\exp(tD)a)
. Then, the derivative of

is zero. Since this derivative is

, we have
\langle Da,\Theta^{\prime}(a)\rangle=0
. As

commutes with
\Theta^{\prime}(a)
relative to
{\cal G}
.

Now we consider the (local) maximum case. By considering

.

(ii)

We specialize

by taking

. Then

commutes with

{\cal G}
.

(iii)
If
\langle h(a),x-a\rangle\geq 0

, then

{\cal G}
. This completes the proof.

(iv)
if

\langle c,x-a\rangle\leq 0

.
∎

•

is a
local maximizer of the map

x\in E\mapsto\Phi(x)+F(x),

then, relative to
{\cal G}
,

a
commutes with every element in the subdifferential
\partial_{E}\,\Phi(a)
,
where

\partial_{E}\,\Phi(a):=\{d\in{\cal V}:\Phi(x)-\Phi(a)\geq\langle d,x-a\rangle\,\,\forall\,\,x\in E\}

(16)

is assumed to be nonempty.

We fix
d\in\partial_{E}\,\Phi(a)
and
D\in\mathrm{Lie({\cal G})}
. We need to show that
\langle Da,d\rangle=0
.

As
a
is a local maximizer, there is an open ball
B_{a}
around
a
such that

\Phi(a)+F(a)\geq\Phi(x)+F(x)\,\,\mbox{ for all}\,\,x\in B_{a}\cap E.

By continuity, there is a
\delta>0
such that

t\in(-\delta,\delta)\Rightarrow\exp(tD)a\in B_{a}.
From the assumed conditions on
E
and
F
,
\exp(tD)a\in E
and
F\big(\exp(tD)a\big)=F(a)
; so we have

t\in(-\delta,\delta)\Rightarrow\Phi(a)+F(a)\geq\Phi\big(\exp(tD)a\big)+F\big(\exp(tD)a\big)=\Phi\big(\exp(tD)a\big)+F(a).

By canceling
F(a)
and using (
16
),

0\geq\Phi\big(\exp(tD)a\big)-\Phi(a)\geq\langle d,\exp(tD)a-a\rangle\,\,\mbox{for all}\,\,t\in(\delta,\delta).

This leads to
\langle d,\exp(tD)a\rangle\leq\langle d,a\rangle
for all
t\in(-\delta,\delta)
. Considering the derivative of
\phi(t):=\langle d,\exp(tD)a\rangle
at
t=0
, we get
\langle Da,d\rangle=0
.
∎

For comparison purposes, our next result and the following examples are stated in the setting of semi-FTvN systems. First, we introduce some spectral notions.

Definition 6.6

Let
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
be a semi-FTvN system,
{\cal G}
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}

(=\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)})
,
E
be a subset of
{\cal V}
, and
F:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. We say that

(i)

E
is a spectral set if it is of the form
\lambda^{-1}(Q)
for some
Q\subseteq{\cal W}
;

(ii)

E
is weakly spectral if it is invariant under all automorphisms, that is,
A(E)\subseteq E
for all
A\in\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})};

(iii)

E
is weakly spectral relative to
{\cal G}
if
A(E)\subseteq E
for all
A\in{\cal G};

(iv)

F
is a spectral function if it is of the form
F=f\circ\lambda
for some
f:{\cal W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
;

(v)

F
is weakly spectral if
F(Ax)=F(x)
for all
x\in{\cal V}
and
A\in\operatorname{Aut}({\cal V})
;

(vi)

F
is said to be weakly spectral relative to
{\cal G}
if
F(Ax)=F(x)
for all
x\in{\cal V}
and
A\in{\cal G}
.

We remark that every spectral set/function is weakly spectral (relative to
{\cal G})
.

In a semi-FTvN system
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
, for any
a\in{\cal V}
, we define

orbit, weak orbit, and
{\cal G}
-orbit of
a
by

[a]:=\{x\in{\cal V}:\lambda(x)=\lambda(a)\},\,\,[a]_{w}=\{Aa:A\in\mathrm{Aut({\cal V})}\},\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,[a]_{{\cal G}}=\{Aa:A\in{\cal G}\}.

Then
[a]
is spectral,
[a]_{w}
is weakly spectral, and
[a]_{{\cal G}}
is weakly spectral relative to
{\cal G}
.
We always have
[a]_{{\cal G}}\subseteq[a]_{w}\subseteq[a]
.

We now specialize Theorem
6.2
to semi-FTvN systems.

Corollary 6.7

Let
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
be a semi-FTvN system,
{\cal G}
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)}
. Suppose, relative to
{\cal G}
,
E
be a weakly spectral set in
{\cal V}
, and

F:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a weakly spectral function. Let
\Theta:{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Fréchet differentiable. Then the following hold: If
a
is a
local minimizer/maximizer of the map

x\in E\rightarrow\Theta(x)+F(x),

then
a
and
\Theta^{\prime}(a)
commute relative to
{\cal G}
; In particular,
if
a
is an optimizer of the problem

\underset{x\in E}{max/min}\,\langle c,x\rangle,

then
a
and
c
commute relative to
{\cal G}
; Furthermore, if
a
solves the variational inequality problem
\mathrm{VI}(h,E)
, then
a
and
h(a)
commute relative to
{\cal G}
.

Example 6.8

Suppose
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system, where
{\cal V}
is finite dimensional. Then, for each
u\in{\cal V}
, the spectral set
[u]
is closed and bounded (recall
\lambda
is continuous and an isometry), hence compact. Then, for any
c\in{\cal V}
,
\underset{[u]}{\max}\,\langle c,x\rangle
is attained, say, at
a\in[u]
. Then,
a
commutes with
c
. We remark that when
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is an FTvN system, one gets strong commutativity (not just commutativity), see Section
1
and
[
7
, Section 1]
. Also, in this setting, when
E
is a spectral set and
h:E\rightarrow{\cal V}
, if
a
solves
\mathrm{VI}(h,E)
, then
a
and
-h(a)
strongly commute. This was established in
[
8
, Corollary 1]
.

In a similar vein (analogous to Corollary
6.3
), we have the following from
[
7
]
,
Proposition 3.12: Suppose

is a
finite-dimensional FTvN system,

is a convex spectral set in

,

Example 6.9

Suppose
({\cal V},{\cal W},\lambda)
is a semi-FTvN system,
K
be a closed convex cone in
{\cal V}
that is weakly spectral. (This could be the hyperbolicity cone corresponding to a complete hyperbolic polynomial.) Let
h:K\rightarrow{\cal V}
. Consider the
complementarity problem

\mathrm{CP}(h,K)
, which is to find

x^{*}\in K
such that
h(x^{*})\in K^{*}
and
\langle h(x^{*}),x^{*}\rangle=0
,

where
K^{*}:=\{y\in{\cal V}:\langle y,x\rangle\geq 0,\,\forall\,x\in K\}
is the dual of
K
.
If
x^{*}
is such a solution, by Corollary
6.7
specialized to

, we see that
x^{*}
commutes with
h(x^{*})
. In particular, in the setting of a Euclidean Jordan algebra with its symmetric cone

x^{*}
and
h(x^{*})
.

Concluding remarks.
In this paper, we introduced two commutativity concepts in the settings of transformation groups and semi-FTvN systems, and demonstrated their appearance as optimality conditions in certain optimization problems. Additionally, we have shown that these concepts reduce to the well-known operator commutativity concepts in the setting of Euclidean Jordan algebras. In our future work, we plan to go beyond hyperbolic polynomials to address majorization concepts/results in the setting of semi-FTvN systems.

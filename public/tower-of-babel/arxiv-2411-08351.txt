Alphabet-affine 2 2 -neighbour-transitive codes
Daniel R. Hawtin

Abstract
A code 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is a subset of the vertex set of a Hamming graph H ⁡ ( n , q ) H(n,q) , and 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is 2 2 -neighbour-transitive if the automorphism group G = Aut ⁡ ( 𝒞 ) G=\Aut(\mathcal{C}) acts transitively on each of the sets 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1} and 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}_{2} , where 𝒞 1 \mathcal{C}_{1} and 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}_{2} are the (non-empty) sets of vertices that are distances 1 1 and 2 2 , respectively, (but no closer) to some element of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . Suppose that 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is a 2 2 -neighbour-transitive code with minimum distance at least 5 5 . For q = 2 q=2 , all ‘minimal’ such 𝒞 \mathcal{C} have been classified. Moreover, it has previously been shown that a subgroup of the automorphism group of the code induces an affine 2 2 -transitive group action on the alphabet of the Hamming graph. The main results of this paper are to show that this affine 2 2 -transitive group must be a subgroup of A ​ Γ ​ L 1 ⁡ ( q ) \AGaL_{1}(q) and to provide a number of infinite families of examples of such codes. These examples are described via polynomial algebras related to representations of certain classical groups.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a set of size

. In the first, we identify
\mathcal{N}
with

, where

for each

. An edge exists between two such

H(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{Q})
by the set of all functions
\alpha:\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
, in which case an edge exists between functions
\alpha
and
\beta
if and only if there exists a unique

such that
\alpha(i)\neq\beta(i)
. We refer to the set
\mathcal{N}
as the

or the

, and the set
\mathcal{Q}
as the

. If

then the

of a vertex
\alpha

, denoted

, is the set
\{i\in\mathcal{N}\mid\alpha(i)\neq 0\}
, and the
weight
of
\alpha

\supp(\alpha)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a code in
H(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{Q})
. As stated in the introduction, if
|\mathcal{C}|=0,1
or
q^{n}
then we say that
\mathcal{C}
is trivial and we generally assume that
\mathcal{C}
is non-trivial, sometimes without statement. Recall that the elements of
\mathcal{C}
are called codewords and the minimum distance
\delta
of
\mathcal{C}
is the smallest distance in
H(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{Q})
between a pair of distinct codewords, and the covering radius of
\mathcal{C}
is the largest distance from any vertex of
H(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{Q})
to its nearest codeword. The
error-correction capacity
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted

, is
\lfloor(\delta-1)/2\rfloor
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-trivial code with covering radius

(G,2)
-neighbour-transitive.

(2)

-neighbour-transitive, and one of the following holds:

(i)

(ii)

\delta=2

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

and

acts transitively on
\Gamma_{i}({\mathbf{0}})
for
i=1,2
. If
\rho=0
then
\mathcal{C}=V(\Gamma)
is a trivial code, but since this is not the case we have
\rho\geqslant 1
.

Suppose
\rho\geqslant 2
. Since

is transitive on

and

and

or

\mathcal{C}
, then any weight
2

is transitive on

, it follows that
\delta\geqslant 4

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\bigcup_{\beta\in\mathcal{C}}\Gamma_{1}(\beta)
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\bigcup_{\beta\in\mathcal{C}}\Gamma_{2}(\beta)
. The fact that
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{C}
and

acts transitively on

and
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
then implies that
\mathcal{C}
is
(G,2)
-neighbour-transitive, and part (1) holds. Hence, we may assume that
\rho=1
.

Since
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{C}
and

acts transitively on
\Gamma_{1}({\mathbf{0}})
, it follows that

\mathcal{C}_{1}=\bigcup_{\beta\in\mathcal{C}}\Gamma_{1}(\beta).

In particular
\delta\geqslant 2
. If
\delta\geqslant 3
, then this union is disjoint, and since

and
\Gamma
is connected, there must be an edge between some vertex of

and some vertex of

for some distinct codewords

and
\beta^{\prime}
, and hence

, so

. This implies that
\mathcal{C}
has error-correction capacity

, and any pair of balls of radius
1
centered at distinct codewords are disjoint. Moreover, since

, the vertex set

, and hence the set of balls of radius
1
centered at the codewords of
\mathcal{C}
partitions
V(\Gamma)
. Thus
\mathcal{C}
is perfect, as in part (2)(i).

Thus we may assume that
\delta=2
. Then, since
G
acts transitively on
\mathcal{C}
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}
acts transitively on
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
, it follows that
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
is contained in
\mathcal{C}
. If

, with

, then the distance between
(a,a,0,\ldots,0)
and
(a,b,0,\ldots,0)
is

. Since
(a,a,0,\ldots,0),(a,b,0,\ldots,0)\in\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and

we deduce that

. Let
\beta\in\Gamma_{1}({\mathbf{0}})

is not adjacent to any weight

. Hence,

has no neighbours in

is transitive on

and

and

are the bipartite halves of

Note that if we add the assumption that

\displaystyle=

4

This section presents the proof of Theorem

1.5
are related to submodules of permutation modules of certain classical groups. Specifically, thinking of the vertices of

allows us to view a set of appropriate polynomials as a code. Historically, polynomial algebras have been used to construct many interesting examples of codes, such as the generalised Reed–Muller codes and the projective Reed–Muller codes; see Definitions

.

The next result develops a concrete connection between

and

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

, as in Definition

, let

be a set of representatives for a subset of the set of all

Let

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space. Since

-vector space, so is

, and hence the function

to
\mathcal{N}
. Thus the set of cosets of

are in bijection with the set of all functions
\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and part (2) holds.

The natural action of

on

where

and

. If

. In particular,

\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}

. Let

,

. Again,
\gcd(k,q-1)=1
implies that there exists

and

and

acts transitively on
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})

Consider now
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\PGU_{3}(q^{s/2})
, noting that
q
is even here, and let
\mathcal{N}
be a set of representatives for the points of a classical unital in
\pg_{2}(q^{s})
. Let
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
be a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{3}
and let
i=e_{1}
,
j=e_{3}
. Following

, we have that the stabiliser of
\langle i\rangle
and
\langle j\rangle

contains the

{\rm diag}(\lambda,1,\lambda^{-q^{s/2}})
, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{\times}
. Note also that the classical unital is stabilised by the diagonal matrix
\mu I
, for
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{\times}
. Taking the product of
\lambda\mu I
and
{\rm diag}(\lambda^{-1},1,\lambda^{q^{s/2}})
we have that there exists

where

can be be represented by the matrix
{\rm diag}(\mu,\lambda\mu,\lambda^{q^{s/2}+1}\mu)

\gcd(k,q-1)=\gcd(2k,q-1)=1
and so we may choose

Next, let

be a set of representatives for the points of the Suzuki–Tits ovoid in

and

\langle i\rangle
and
\langle j\rangle
contains the diagonal matrix
{\rm diag}(c,c^{2^{n+1}-1},c^{-2^{n+1}+1},c^{-1})

for any

. Note also that

stabilises the Suzuki–Tits ovoid for any

. Since

is even, we have that
\gcd(k,q-1)=\gcd(2k,q-1)=1
and hence we may choose

so that

,

∎

Proof of Theorem

Since

The next result shows that the conclusion of Proposition
4.2
is false if the condition
\gcd(k,q-1)=1
is dropped.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a set of representatives for a subset of the set of all
1
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{t}
, let
k\in\{1,\ldots,q-1\}
such that
\gcd(k,q-1)>1
, and let
G_{\mathbf{0}}\leqslant\Diag_{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})\circ\GL_{t}(q^{s})
, where
\GL_{t}(q^{s})
acts on
\Gamma=H(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{F}_{q})
as in Lemma
4.1
(3). Then
G_{\mathbf{0}}
is not transitive on
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
.

Proof.

Let
\ell=\gcd(k,q-1)
. For any
u,v\in\mathcal{N}
, the subgroup induced by
\Diag_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})
and the stabiliser of
\langle u\rangle\cup\langle v\rangle
inside
\GL_{t}(q^{s})
on the projection
H(\{u,v\},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the group

H=\{(\omega^{a},\omega^{b})\sigma^{c}\mid a-b\equiv 0{\pmod{\ell}}\},

where
\omega
is a generator for
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
\sigma=(u\,v)
. Since
\ell>1
, it follows that there is no element
h\in H
such that
(1,1)^{h}=(1,\omega)
, and hence
G_{\mathbf{0}}
is not transitive on
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
.
∎

The next result determines the minimum distances of certain codes satisfying Theorem
1.5
. In particular, this shows that there are infinitely many

-neighbour-transitive codes for each of the lines of Table

.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
q
,
s
,
t
,
G_{\mathbf{0}}
,
\mathcal{N}
be as in one of the lines of Table
1
, let
\mathcal{C}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}G_{\mathbf{0}}
-submodule of
R(q,s,t,1)
consisting of those polynomials of degree
(q^{s}-1)/(q-1)
, and let
\delta
be the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the following hold.

(1)

If
s=1
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\AGL_{t-1}(q)
then
\delta=q^{t-1}-q^{t-2}
.

(2)

If
s=2
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\PGU_{3}(q)
then
\delta=q^{3}-2q
.

(3)

If
s=1
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\Sz(q)
then
\delta=q^{2}-q
.

Proof.

Since each code is linear, the minimum distance is equal to the minimum weight of a non-zero codeword. First, suppose
s=1
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\AGL_{t-1}(q)
. Then
\mathcal{C}
consists of degree
1
polynomials, which are zero precisely on a codimension
1
subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{t}
, and so are zero on either no elements of
\mathcal{N}
, or on

\frac{q^{t-1}-1}{q-1}-\frac{q^{t-2}-1}{q-1}

elements of
\mathcal{N}
, since
\mathcal{N}
is a set of representatives for the complement of a hyperplane in
\pg_{t-1}(q)
. Thus
\mathcal{C}
has minimum distance
q^{t-1}-q^{t-2}
.

Next, suppose
s=2
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\PGU_{3}(q)
. Then a polynomial
f
of degree
q+1
in
R(q,2,3,1)
is zero precisely on a Baer subplane of
\PG_{2}(q^{2})
, that is,
f
is linear upon reduction to the subfield
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By
[
4
, Corollary 8]
, each Baer subplane meets a unital in either
1
,
q+1
, or
2q+1
points. Thus, since
n=q^{3}+1
, the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}
is
q^{3}-2q
.

Finally, suppose
s=1
and
G_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}\rtimes\Sz(q)
. A degree
1
polynomial
f
in
R(q,1,4,1)
will evaluate to zero on precisely the points of a hyperplane in
\PG_{3}(q)
. Any hyperplane is either tangent to an ovoid, or meets the ovoid in an ‘oval’ consisting of
q+1
points. It follows that

is non-zero on either

or

points of the ovoid. Thus, the minimum distance of

is

∎

5
Relation to Reed–Muller codes

The generalised Reed–Muller codes, projective Reed–Muller codes, and their subfield subcodes are defined below. The generalised Reed–Muller codes were introduced in
[
19
]
and
[
31
]
; see also
[
9
]
. The projective Reed–Muller codes were first studied in
[
30
]
and
[
20
]
. Assmus and Key
[
1
, Section 5.7]
construct and analyse the
subfield subcodes
of the generalised and projective Reed–Muller codes. The parameters of the generalised Reed–Muller codes are given in
[
1
, Theorem 5.4.1 and Corollary 5.5.4]
, the minimum distance of the projective Reed–Muller codes can be found in
[
28
, Theorem 1]
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power, let
s
be a positive integer, let
k
be an integer satisfying
0<k\leqslant q-1
, let
\ell
be a multiple of
k(q^{s}-1)/(q-1)
, let
P=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}]
, and let
R(q,s,t,k)
be as in Definition
1.4
. Define the following codes.

(1)

Set
k=s=1
. The
generalised Reed–Muller code

{\mathcal{RM}}_{q}(\ell,t)
in
H(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{t},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by the subspace of
P
consisting of all polynomials of degree at most
\ell
.

(2)

Set
k=1
. The
subfield subcode

{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}/q}(\ell,t)
, of
{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t)
, in
H(\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{t},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace consisting of all
f\in{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t)
such that
f(v)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for all
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{t}
.

(3)

Let
s=1
and
\mathcal{N}
be a set of representatives for the set of all
1
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{t}
. The
projective Reed–Muller
code
{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q}(\ell,t)
in
H(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by the subspace of
R(q,1,t,k)
consisting of all polynomials of degree at most
\ell
.

(4)

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a set of representatives for the set of all
1
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{s}}^{t}
. The
subfield subcode

{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q^{s}/q}(\ell,t)
, of
{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t)
, in
H(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{F}_{q})
is given by the subspace of
R(q,s,t,k)
consisting of all polynomials of degree at most
\ell
.

It is worth briefly comparing the automorphism groups of the codes
{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t-1)
and
{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}/q}(\ell,t-1)
with the codes of Theorem
1.5
where
G_{\mathbf{0}}
and
\mathcal{N}
are as in line 2 of Table
1
. In each case the action of the automorphism group of the code induces a faithful action of
\AGL_{t-1}(q^{s})
on
\mathcal{N}
. However, while the codes
{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t-1)
and
{\mathcal{RM}}_{q^{s}/q}(\ell,t-1)
are preserved by
\AGL_{t-1}(q^{s})
as a subgroup of
\Sym(\mathcal{N})
, this is not the case for the codes arising from line 2 of Table
1
. generally, if
g=h\sigma\in\AGL_{t-1}(q^{s})\leqslant G_{\mathbf{0}}
, where
h\in\Sym(\mathcal{Q})^{n}
and
\sigma\in\Sym(\mathcal{N})
, then
\sigma\neq 1
implies

.

The next result concerns the
2
-neighbour-transitivity of the generalised Reed–Muller codes, the projective Reed–Muller codes, and their subfield subcodes. Note that if
\ell=(t-1)(q-1)-1
then
{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q}(\ell,t)
is a perfect Hamming code and is neighbour-transitive, but not
2
-neighbour-transitive; see Remark
2.8
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Following the notation as in Definition
5.1
, let one of the following hold:

(1)

\mathcal{C}={\mathcal{RM}}_{2}(\ell,t)
with
\ell\leqslant t-2
.

(2)

\mathcal{C}={\mathcal{RM}}_{2^{s}/2}(\ell,t)
with
\ell\leqslant(t-1)(2^{s}-1)-1
.

(3)

\mathcal{C}={\mathcal{PRM}}_{q}(\ell,t)
with
\ell\leqslant(t-1)(q-1)-2
and
\gcd(k,q-1)=1
.

(4)

\mathcal{C}={\mathcal{PRM}}_{q^{s}/q}(\ell,t)
with
\ell\leqslant(t-1)(q^{s}-1)-2
and
\gcd(k,q-1)=1
.

Then
\mathcal{C}
is
2
-neighbour-transitive.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is linear in all cases, the group of translations
T_{\mathcal{C}}
acts transitively on
\mathcal{C}
. In (1) and (2),
\Aut(\mathcal{C})_{\mathbf{0}}
contains
\AGL_{t}(2)
and
\AGL_{t}(2^{s})
, respectively, so that
\Aut(\mathcal{C})_{\mathbf{0}}
acts transitively on each of the sets
\Gamma_{1}({\mathbf{0}})
and
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
. For cases (3) and (4), Proposition
4.2
implies that
\Aut(\mathcal{C})_{\mathbf{0}}
acts transitively on each of
\Gamma_{1}({\mathbf{0}})
and
\Gamma_{2}({\mathbf{0}})
. We claim that each code has minimum distance at least
4
, in which case the result follows from Proposition
2.7
. To prove the claim, first observe that the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{RM}}_{2^{s}/2}(\ell,t)
is bounded below by the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{RM}}_{2^{s}}(\ell,t)
, and the minimum distance of
{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q^{s}/q}(\ell,t)
is bounded below by
{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t)
. By
[
1
, Corollary 5.5.4]
,

{\mathcal{RM}}_{2^{s}}(\ell,t)
has minimum distance
(2^{s}-m)2^{s(t-r-1)}
, where
\ell=r(2^{s}-1)+m
with
m\in\{0,\ldots,2^{s}-1\}
. Thus the result holds in cases (1) and (2). By
[
28
, Theorem 1]
,

{\mathcal{PRM}}_{q^{s}}(\ell,t)
has minimum distance
(q^{s}-m)q^{s(t-r-1)}
, where
\ell-1=r(q^{s}-1)+m
with
m\in\{0,\ldots,2^{s}-1\}
. Thus the result also holds in cases (3) and (4).
∎

Global G ​ L 2 GL_{2} Hecke-Baxter operator
A.A. Gerasimov, D.R. Lebedev, S.V. Oblezin

Abstract
. We construct a global Hecke-Baxter operator for integrable systems of arithmetic type associated with the group G ​ L 2 GL_{2} . It is an element of a global Hecke algebra associated with the double coset space G ​ L 2 ​ ( ℤ ) \ G ​ L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) / O 2 GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/O_{2} . Eigenvalues of the global Hecke-Baxter operator acting on the G ​ L 2 GL_{2} -Eisenstein series are given by the corresponding global L L -factors. This construction generalizes our previous construction of the Hecke-Baxter operators over local completions ℝ \mathbb{R} and ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} of the number field ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Presumably, zeroes of the corresponding global L L -factors should be subjected to an arithmetic version of the Bethe ansatz equations.



1
Introduction

An interpretation of a wide class of integrable systems in terms of
representation theory provides important insights both into theory
of integrable systems and into representations theory
allowing transferring various techniques from one area of research
into the other. Among various examples we would like to mention the
formalism of the Baxter operator
[
Ba
]
which was properly placed in
representation theory perspective using Hecke
algebras formalism in
[
GLO08
]
(see also
[
G
]
).
We coin the term the Hecke-Baxter operator for a
one-parameter family of element of the Hecke algebra reproducing Baxter operator
of various quantum integrable systems.
Starting with the case of spherical principles series
representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
the construction of the
Hecke-Baxter operator is recently extended
to general principle series representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

(for details see
[
GLO25
]
and reference therein). A remarkable fact is
that the Hecke-Baxter operators are directly related to
the Archimedean
L
-factors attached to the corresponding
representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
. Precisely the Archimedean
L
-factors appear
as eigenvalues of the Hecke-Baxter operators acting on the
spherical and Whittaker functions given by specific matrix elements in the
spherical principle series representations.
As a direct consequence the local
L
-factors enter the integral
representations of the Whittaker functions expressed via a version of the
Gelfand-Tsetlin construction of irreducible representations of

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})

[
GKL
]
. Note that the
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-Whittaker
functions are eigenfunctions of the
quantum
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
-Toda chains, one of the most well-studied
finite-dimensional integrable systems. Actually the Hecke-Baxter
operator (depending on auxiliary parameter) provides an alternative
formulation of the quantum Toda chain.
Notice in this regard that an advantage of the Hecke algebra
formulation of integrable systems is in a unified treatment
of both continues and discrete symmetries of the systems.

Not surprisingly, proper counterpart of the Hecke-Baxter operator exists in the case
of representations theory over non-Archimedean fields. The case of
spherical principle series representations of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
was
considered in
[
GLO08
]
together with the
corresponding integrable systems governing spherical and Whittaker
functions over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. Connection with local
L
-factors
still holds in this case, and local non-Archimedean
L
-factors show up as eigenvalues
of the non-Archimedean Hecke-Baxter operators acting on

GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
-Whittaker functions.

It is natural to expect that the Hecke-Baxter operator formalism may
be further generalized to the case of (global) number fields.
This is indeed so, and in this short note we consider one
representative example of the global Hecke-Baxter operator: we introduce
a
GL_{2}
Hecke-Baxter operator over compactification
\overline{{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z})}
of
{\rm Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
acting on the non-ramified
GL_{2}
-automorphic
functions (i.e. functions on the double coset
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/O_{2}
). The following result is proven in Theorem
4.1
in Section 4.
The automorphic functions represented by matrix elements
of the spherical principle series
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-representations are
eigenfunctions of the proposed global Hecke-Baxter operator with the
eigenvalues given by the corresponding global

L
-functions generalizing the completed form of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\zeta}(s)\,=\,\zeta(s)\,\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big)\,.\end{array}

This result complements the constructions of
[
JL
]
.

Let us stress that in terms of quantum integrable systems we basically consider
a hyperbolic billiard on upper half-plane modulo action of the
modular group
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. These quantum systems are integrable and are
deeply connected with the quantum

SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Toda chains: harmonics of the quantum billiard
eigenfunctions are given by solutions of the quantum Toda chain for
integer coupling constants. On the other hand this quantum billiard
is a generalization of the Euclidean billiard arsing in the tropical
limit of the
SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Toda chain
[
GL
]
. This provides an interesting
number theoretic perspective on an interpretation of the tropical limit proposed in

[
GL
]
.

One curious point worth mentioning is as follows. In the case of
the integrable systems with discrete spectrum, the Baxter operator is instrumental in
finding the spectrum given by common eigenvalues of quantum
Hamiltonians. Precisely the eigenvalues of quantum Hamiltonian are
expressed in a simple way through zeroes of the eigenvalues of the Baxter operators (considered as functions of an auxiliary parameter). In turn,
zeroes of the eigenvalues of the Baxter operator satisfy a set of
equations called the Baxter equations. Our interpretation of the global

L
-functions as eigenvalues of the Hecke-Baxter operators points to
the problem of finding an analog of the Baxter equations in the
arithmetic setup. This might extend a traditional optics
for looking at analogs of Riemann hypotheses for global
L
-functions as well as various conjectures on
the special values of global
L
-functions. This suggestion seems
close to the Faddeev-Pavlov approach
[
FP
]
to studying
analytic properties of the Riemann zeta-functions via scattering
theory. This line of research seems still worth to pursue.

Let us also note that
the Hecke-Baxter operators (and more general elements of Hecke algebras) are
examples of averaging operators that are ubiquitous in various areas of Mathematics
and Physics. One interesting example of the averaging operator appears
in the Kadanov approach to the renormalization (semi)group in lattice
quantum field theories (see e.g.
[
Ka
]
). Fixed points of the
renormalization group flow corresponding to the eigenvalues of the Kadanov
operators describe continuum limit of the lattice
theory. The analogy between the constructions of
[
Ka
]
and this
paper is very fruitful and will be considered in details elsewhere. As an
obvious next step we are going to generalize the results of
this note to the global ramified case for the groups
GL_{\ell+1}
of higher ranks.

2
Automorphic forms and global Hecke-Baxter operator:
GL_{1}

In this Section we consider the almost trivial case of the Lie group
GL_{1}
. Our
goal is to introduce basic elements of the construction to proceed in the following
Sections with a more involved case of
GL_{2}
.

Let us define the non-ramified
GL_{1}
automorphic functions as
functions on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
invariant under the left action of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and right action of the orthogonal subgroup
O_{1}\subset GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
i.e. these functions may be considered as functions on
the double coset space

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{M}_{1}=GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}\,.\end{array}

Note that
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is a
GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
-orbifold as
GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})

acts trivially on the coset space
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}
. We however are
interested in the space of functions on
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and thus might ignore this
subtlety by considering functions on

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}
(invariant under trivial action of
GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
)
as functions on
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. Thus taking into account the identifications

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{*},\qquad GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mu_{2},\qquad O_{1}\simeq\mu_{2}\,,\qquad\mu_{2}=\{\pm 1\}\,.\end{array}

the
GL_{1}
-automorphic functions may be identified with functions on

\mathbb{R}_{+}=\mathbb{R}^{*}/\mu_{2}
.

The double cosets space (
2
) allows an interpretation as a
moduli space of circles
S^{1}
supplied with
S^{1}
-invariant
metrics. Indeed
\mathcal{M}_{1}
may be presented in the following factorized form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{M}_{1}=GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})\times_{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}\,.\end{array}

The first factor
GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
should be identified with
the space of lattices
L
in
\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}L=\{nv|n\in\mathbb{Z}\},\qquad v\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}\,,\end{array}

characterized by non-zero real numbers
v
with group

GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})=\mu_{2}
of automorphisms of
L
acting by effectively
changing the sign of
v
. By action of
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{*}
any
lattice may transformed into the standard one
L=\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}
.
The second factor
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}
in (
2
)
is identified with the space of constant
metrics on
\mathbb{R}
with
O_{1}
being
stabilizer of a reference metric. From this description we infer that
the space (
2
) is the moduli space of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
-equivalence classes of pairs of lattices and constant metrics
on
\mathbb{R}
or equivalently as the moduli space of circles
S^{1}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
supplied with
constant metrics. Algebraically
\mathcal{M}_{1}
may be understood as a moduli
space of rank one
\mathbb{Z}
-modules
L
supplied with a metric on its real
extension
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. The interpretation of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
as moduli
space metricized circles provides us with a canonical
coordinate on
\mathcal{M}_{1}
, the volume of the corresponding circle

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}|x|={\rm Vol}_{h}(\mathbb{R}/L)\,.\end{array}

Here
x
is the canonical coordinate on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}^{*}
identified
with the moduli space of the oriented circles
S^{1}_{or}
supplied with a constant
metric
h
. Given a pair
(S^{1}_{or},h)

we might consider corresponding volume one-form
\omega
so that
the coordinate
x
would be a period of this form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}/L}\omega\,.\end{array}

We are interested in a particular bases in the
space of
GL_{1}
-automorphic functions given by
GL_{1}
analog of the Eisenstein
functions. This bases may be defined precisely in various ways
but having in mind subsequent
generalizations to the case of
GL_{2}
we construct these functions
using representation theory approach. Precisely we define
GL_{1}

Eisenstein functions as matrix elements of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and
O_{1}
invariant vectors in unitary spherical principle series
representations
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
be a one-dimensional unitary spherical
unitary representation of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\langle\,,\,\rangle
be the corresponding
Hermitian pairing and
v\in\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
be such that

\langle v,v\rangle=1
. By definition spherical representations of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{*}
are factored through homomorphysm
\mathbb{R}^{*}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
and are given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{\gamma}:\,\,x\longrightarrow|x|^{\imath\gamma}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,.\end{array}

Consider the following matrix elements in representation
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{\gamma}(x)=\langle v,\pi_{\gamma}(x)\,v\rangle=|x|^{\imath\gamma}\,,\qquad x\in GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{*}\,.\end{array}

It is a
\mu_{2}
-invariant function on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
thus
\psi_{\gamma}(x)
is a lift of a function on
\mathcal{M}_{1}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Corresponding function
on
\mathcal{M}_{1}
will be called the
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
-Eisenstein series
associated with the representation
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
.
In the following we will consider
interchangeably automorphic eigenfunctions as functions on
\mathcal{M}_{1}

depending on
|x|
or as
\mu_{2}
-invariant functions on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})

depending on
x
.

The
GL_{1}
-Eisenstein functions may be defined also as
eigenfunctions of some operators. In the following we will be interested
in characterization of the
GL_{1}
-Eisenstein functions as common eigenfunctions
of elements of the Hecke algebra associated with the space of double
cosets (this formulation especially useful as
takes into account the invariance under both discrete and
continues groups). Define the Hecke
algebra associated with the double coset space (
2
) as
a tensor product of two convolution algebras

\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{1})
.
Recall that Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(G,K)
associated with the pair

K\subset G
is an associative algebra of the proper subset of

K
-biinvariant functions on
G
under convolution. It is natural to
consider the maximal subset of functions on
G
such that the
convolution operation is defined. In the case when
(G,K)
is a
Gelfand pair i.e.
K
is a fixed point of an involution of
G
the
corresponding algebra is commutative. The power of the Hecke algebra
formalism is in the fact that
\mathcal{H}(G,K)
in general is not a group
algebra but replaces it in various representation theory
constructions.

The Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{1})
,
the algebra of
O_{1}
-biinvraint functions on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, acts naturally
on the functions on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}
and in particular
on the functions on double coset
\mathcal{M}_{1}
via convolution. Note that
it does not take into account
\mathbb{Z}
(and thus
\mathbb{Q}
) structure
responsible for the lattice moduli space interpretation of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. To
take into account this arithmetic structure we consider another Hecke
algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z}))
which we identify with the
convolution algebra of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
-biinvariant generalized function on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
with the support at

GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q})\subset GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. It is easy to verify that the algebras

\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{1})

are (mutually) commutative associative algebras acting on
functions on
\mathcal{M}_{1}
from the right and the left
correspondingly. Note that in the considered case of
GL_{1}
the Hecke algebras are
actually groups algebras of the quotient groups
GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q})/GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})/O_{1}
.

It is instructive to describe the
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z}))
-action
considering
\mathcal{M}_{1}
as a moduli space of
metricized circles. Given a lattice
L\subset\mathbb{R}
, we can consider a
sublattice
L_{p}\subset L
of index
[L:L_{p}]=p
, and also we consider

L_{1/q}\subset\mathbb{R}
, such that
L\subset L_{1/q}
with

[L_{1/q}:L]=q
. Now we define, for any

p/q\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{*}
, an operation
T_{p/q}
on lattices by first taking

L\subset L_{1/q}
such that

[L_{1/q}:L]=q
, and then considering a sublattice
L_{p/q}\subset L_{1/q}
of index
[L_{1/q}:L_{p/q}]=p
. Thus we have the following
operator acting on functions on the space of lattices:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{p/q}\cdot f)(L)\,=\sum_{L\subset L_{1/q}\supset L_{p/q}}\,f(L_{p/q}),\qquad[L_{1/q}:L]=q,\quad[L_{1/q}:L_{p/q}]=p\,.\end{array}

In terms of the functions of the coordinate
|x|\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
this
reduces to a simple mupltiplication operation

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{p/q}\cdot f)(|x|)=f(p/q\cdot|x|)\,.\end{array}

These operators obviously belongs to the algebra

\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})
and satisfy the following relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}T_{p_{1}/q_{1}}\circ T_{p_{2}/q_{2}}=T_{(p_{1}p_{2})/(q_{1}q_{2})}\,.\end{array}

Let us remark that the collection of operators
T_{p/q},\,p/q\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{+}

provides a
GL_{1}
-analog of the modular tower structure arising in the case of

GL_{2}
.

To construct a meaningful generating function we consider a
multiplicative semigroup
\mathbb{Z}_{+}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{*}
. Corresponding
elements of the Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})

act as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{n}\cdot f)(L)=f(nL)\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,,\end{array}

or equivalently, in terms of functions on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{n}\cdot f)(x)=f(nx)\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,.\end{array}

These operators may be conveniently combined into the generating series

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}\,=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}T_{n}\,.\end{array}

Its action on the functions of
x
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}\cdot f)(x)\,=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}\,f(nx)\,.\end{array}

Now we consider a kind of generating functions for the elements of the
Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{R}),O_{1})
providing a
proper analog for the generating function (
2
). Such
generating functions were introduced
(for more general case of
GL_{\ell+1}(\mathbb{R})
) in
[
GLO08
]

under the name the Hecke-Baxter operator. Precisely
the
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
Hecke-Baxter operator is the integral operator,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}_{s}\cdot f)(x)\,=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\!d\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}(y)\,\,|y|^{s}\,f(y^{-1}x)\,,\quad d\mu_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}(y)=e^{-\pi y^{2}}\,\frac{dy}{y}\,,\end{array}

acting by convolution with
O_{1}
-biinvariant function on
GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}_{s}(y)=|y|^{s}\,e^{-\pi y^{2}}\,.\end{array}

In the following, for brevity, we identify suitable functions on Lie groups, operators
obtained by the actions of these functions via convolution and
the corresponding integral kernels.

Proposition 2.1

The matrix elements (
2
)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{\gamma}(x)=\langle v,\pi_{\gamma}(x)\,v\rangle=|x|^{\imath\gamma}\,,\end{array}

are common eigen-functions of the operators
Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}
and

Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}_{s}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}\cdot\psi_{\gamma}\bigr)(x)\,=\,\zeta(s-\imath\gamma)\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}_{s}\cdot\psi_{\gamma}\bigr(x)\,=\,L^{\mathbb{R}}(s-\imath\gamma)\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,.\end{array}

The eigenvalues are given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\,\frac{1}{n^{s}}\,,\qquad L^{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)\,,\end{array}

where we impose
{\rm Re}(s)>1
for convergence.

Proof
. Using

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{n}\cdot\psi_{\gamma})(x)\,=\,\psi_{\gamma}(nx)\,=\,n^{\imath\gamma}\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,,\end{array}

we indeed have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}\cdot\psi_{\gamma}\bigr)(x)\,=\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{1}{n^{s-\imath\gamma}}\right)\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,=\,\zeta(s-\imath\gamma)\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,.\end{array}

The analogous statement for the Archimedean Hecke-Baxter
operator
Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}_{s}
acting on matrix element (
2
) via

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})}_{s}\cdot\psi_{\gamma}\bigr)(x)\,=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\!\frac{dy}{y}\,\,|y|^{s}\,e^{-\pi y^{2}}\,\psi_{\gamma}(y^{-1}x)\,=\,\pi^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma}{2}}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\imath\gamma}{2}\right)\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,,\end{array}

reduces basically to the integral representation of the
Gamma-function.
\Box

Now we introduce main object of our considerations in this Section,
global Hecke-Baxter operator
\widehat{Q}_{s}
.

Definition 2.1

The
GL_{1}
global Hecke-Baxter operator is the operator
acting in the space of functions on

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{*}
via convolution with the function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\widehat{Q}^{GL_{1}}_{s}(x)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,|x|^{s}\Big(\Theta(0|\imath x^{2})-1\Big)\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,,\end{array}

where the theta-constant is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Theta(0|\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\imath\pi\tau n^{2}}\,.\end{array}

Proposition 2.2

Consider the matrix element (
2
)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{\gamma}(x)=\langle v,\pi_{\gamma}(x)\,v\rangle=|x|^{\imath\gamma}\,,\end{array}

in the unitary spherical principle series representation

(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
of

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Define completed zeta-function as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\zeta}(s)\,=\,\zeta(s)\,\pi^{-\frac{s}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s}{2}\Big)\,.\end{array}

Then the global Hecke-Baxter operator (
2.1
) acts on (
2.2
)
via multiplication by a shifted completed zeta-function

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(\widehat{Q}^{GL_{1}}_{s}\cdot\psi_{\gamma}\bigr)(x)\,=\hat{\zeta}(s-\imath\gamma)\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,,\qquad{\rm Re}(s)>1\,.\end{array}

Proof
. We have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(\widehat{Q}^{GL_{1}}_{s}*\psi_{\gamma}\bigr)(x)\,=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\frac{dy}{y}\,\,|y|^{s}\,\,\,\frac{1}{2}\Big(\Theta(0|\imath y^{2})-1\Big)\,\psi_{\gamma}(y^{-1}x)\\
=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\,\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{*}}\!\frac{dy}{y}\,|y|^{s}\,e^{-\pi|ny|^{2}}\,|y|^{-\imath\gamma}\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\\
=\,\pi^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s-\imath\gamma}{2}\Big)\,\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{1}{n^{s-\imath\gamma}}\right)\,\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\\
=\,\pi^{-\frac{s-\imath\gamma}{2}}\,\Gamma\Big(\frac{s-\imath\gamma}{2}\Big)\,\zeta(s-\imath\gamma)\,\,\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,,\end{array}

thus arriving at the required identity.
\Box

Let us notice that in the simple case of the trivial representation

(\pi_{{\gamma}=0},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma=0})
the identity
(
2.2
) reduces to the standard
integral expression for the completed zeta-function:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\zeta}(s)\,=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\,\,\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{dt}{t}\,\,t^{\frac{s}{2}}\,\,e^{-\pi tn^{2}}\,.\end{array}

The fundamental property of the completed Riemann zeta-function
(
2.2
) is the functional relation

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\zeta}(1-s)=\hat{\zeta}(s)\,.\end{array}

The matrix elements (
2.2
) also respect an analogous
reflection symmetry

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\psi_{-\gamma}(x^{\tau})=\psi_{\gamma}(x)\,,\end{array}

where
x^{\tau}:=x^{-1}
is the involution on the group

GL_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Taking into account that matrix elements (
2.2
) are
eigenfunctions of the Hecke-Baxter operator
\hat{Q}_{s}^{GL_{1}}
with
the eigenvalues expressed through completed zeta-function one
expects that the kernel of the Hecke-Baxter integral operator
should also satisfy a form of functional equation. Indeed we have the
following relation

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\widehat{Q}^{GL_{1}}_{1-s}(x^{\tau})+\frac{1}{2}|x^{\tau}|^{1-s}=\widehat{Q}^{GL_{1}}_{s}(x)+\frac{1}{2}|x|^{s}\,,\end{array}

where the terms
|x|^{s}
compensate the correction terms
entering the expression (
2.1
) of the kernel via theta-constant.
The functional relation (
2
) is a direct consequence of the
modular properties of the theta-constant verified using the Poisson
summation formula. Thus we have a deep connection between properties
of the global Hecke-Baxter operator and analytic properties of the
completed Riemann zeta-function.

It is possible to interpolate between the global and Archimedean Hecke-Baxter
operators via considering a
GL_{1}
-analog of the
congruence (semi)groups. Let us introduce
the following semigroup
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{(N)}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{*}_{+}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{(N)}\,=\,\{\eta\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{*}|\eta=1+Nm,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}\,.\end{array}

Then the generating function of the elements of the Hecke
algebra
\mathcal{H}(GL_{1}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z}))
reads

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{GL_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s,N}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+nN)^{s}}\,T_{1+nN}\,.\end{array}

Therefore the modified kernel of the global Hecke-Baxter operator is
given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\widehat{Q}^{GL_{1}}_{s,N}(x)\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\,|x|^{s}\,\,\Theta^{(N)}(0|\imath x^{2})\,,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\,,\qquad N>1\,.\end{array}

Here

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Theta^{(N)}(0|\tau)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\imath\pi\tau(1+Nn)^{2}}=e^{\imath\pi\tau}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\imath\pi\tau N^{2}n^{2}+2\pi\imath Nn\tau}=e^{\imath\pi\tau}\,\Theta_{N^{2}}\Big(\frac{\tau}{N}\Big|\tau\Big)\,,\end{array}

where the level
k
theta function is defined by:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Theta_{k}(z|\tau)\,=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{\imath\pi k\tau n^{2}+2\pi\imath knz}\,.\end{array}

Now it is easy to check that taking the limit
N\to+\infty
the kernel (
2
)
of the modified global Hecke-Baxter operator turns into the kernel
(
2
) of the Archimedean Hecke-Baxter operator. This
provides a kind of regularization of the Archimedean Hecke-Baxter
operator.

3
GL_{2}
-automorphic forms

In this Section we recall a construction of the Eisenstein functions
for
GL_{2}
(for a review see e.g.
[
ILP
]
).
Let us start with the double coset space

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{M}_{2}=GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/O_{2}\,.\end{array}

The space (
3
), similar to (
2
) in the previous Section
is an orbifold. We will define the space of functions on
(
3
) as functions on
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/O_{2}
invariant under left
action of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The double coset space
\mathcal{M}_{2}
allows
interpretation as a moduli space of two-tori
T^{2}
supplied with

T^{2}
-invariant metrics. Indeed it may be identified with the space
of pairs of lattices
L
and a constant metrics
h
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

modulo simulations action of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Taking into account that
any lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
may be transformed by linear transformations
into the standard one
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
we arrive at the
identification of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
with the moduli space of metricizes tori.
Constant metric on
T^{2}
defines a conformal metric, and therefore a
complex structure, supplying
T^{2}
with a structure of elliptic
curve
E(\mathbb{C})
. As a result the space
\mathcal{M}_{2}
is naturally fibred
over the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{2}^{c}
of elliptic curves. The fiber of the
projection
\mathcal{M}_{2}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}^{c}
may be identified with
\mathbb{R}_{+}

supplied with the natural coordinate, the volume of

T^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
in the considered metric. In turn, the space

\mathcal{M}_{2}^{c}
of complex structures has the double coset description as
the upper complex half-plane

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{H}_{+}=PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})/SO(2)\,,\end{array}

modulo action of the discrete group

PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. In the standard linear coordinates on
\mathcal{H}_{+}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}|{\rm Im}(\tau)>0\}
the isomorphism (
3
)
may be described as
SO_{2}
-projection of the following lift

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\tau=(\tau_{1}+\imath\tau_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{+}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau_{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{2}&\tau_{1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in PSL_{2}(\mathbb{R})\,,\end{array}

while the left action of
PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathcal{H}_{+}
is realized by the
fractional linear transformations.

Now we introduce a special kind of
GL_{2}
-automorphic functions,
the Eisenstein functions. The
GL_{2}
-Eisenstein functions are
associated with spherical principle
series representations entering the
decomposition of the
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-representation
L^{2}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}))

acting from the right:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(\pi(g)\cdot f\bigr)(\tilde{g})=f(\tilde{g}\cdot g),\qquad f\in L^{2}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\,.\end{array}

The irreducible components
corresponding to spherical principle series representations are in one
to one correspondence with the elements of

L^{2}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
invariant under the subgroup

O_{2}\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. This correspondence follows by the uniqueness of
spherical vectors in spherical principle series
representations (see e.g.
[
GGPS
]
).

Let
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
be a unitary spherical principle
series
representation of
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

realized via induction from the Borel subgroup
B\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

(identified with the subgroup of lower triangular matrices) via the
spherical character of
B

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\pi_{{\gamma}}={\rm Ind}_{B}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\chi^{B}_{\gamma},\quad\chi^{B}_{\gamma}(b)=\prod_{j=1}^{2}|b_{jj}|^{\imath{\gamma}_{j}-\rho_{j}}\,,\quad\rho=\Big(\frac{1}{2},\,-\frac{1}{2}\Big)\,.\end{array}

Therefore the representation space
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}=\bigl\{f\in{\rm Fun}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}))\,:\quad f(bg)=\chi^{B}_{{\gamma}}(b)\,f(g),\,\,b\in B\bigr\}\,,\end{array}

supports the right
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action. Using the Bruhat decomposition,
the representation
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
may be realized in the
space of functions on
B\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, which in
turn can be identified with the compactification the (opposite)
unipotent subgroup
N_{+}\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}N_{+}\,=\,\Big\{n_{x}=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&x\\
&&\\
0&&1\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,\Big|\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}\,.\end{array}

Explicitly, the
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
action in
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\subset L^{2}(B\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
, for

g=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&b\\
&&\\
c&&d\end{smallmatrix}\Big)
, is
given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[\pi_{\gamma}(g)\cdot f](x)\,=\,f(n_{x}g)\,=\,|\det g|^{\imath\gamma_{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\,|a+xc|^{\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})-1}\,f\Big(\frac{b+xd}{a+xc}\Big)\,,\end{array}

providing the following
GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-action on
\mathbb{R}P^{1}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}g\cdot x\,=\,\frac{b+xd}{a+xc}\,,\qquad g=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&b\\
&&\\
c&&d\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,.\end{array}

The Hilbert space structure on the space of functions on
N_{+}

is defined via the pairing

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\phi_{1}\,,\phi_{2}\rangle\,=\int\limits_{N_{+}}\!dn_{x}\,\overline{\phi_{1}(n_{x})}\,\,\phi_{2}(n_{x})\,.\end{array}

We supply the Hilbert space
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
with a structure of
the rigged Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(t)}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\subset\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(g)}
(the Gelfand triple) where

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(t)}
is the subspace of smooth test functions and

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(g)}
is the space of generalized functions. The
pairing (
3
) extends to the duality between

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(t)}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(g)}
. We would like to
represent the
GL_{2}
-Eisenstein functions in terms of matrix
elements of the spherical principle series representations. As we
will see the corresponding matrix elements are not well-defined for
the unitary principle series and requires analytic continuation of
the representation parameters
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In turn this implies a replacement of the structure of Hilbert space

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}
by a pair of dual spaces. Thus we consider

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(t)}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}^{(g)}
to be the dual

GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-modules with representation parameters having a small
imaginary part. To construct matrix element representation of the
Eisenstein functions we start with the explicit construction

GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
- and
O_{2}
-invariant vectors. Let

\phi_{O_{2}}\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}^{(t)}
be the unique (up to normalization)
spherical vector, i.e. vector invariant under the action of

O_{2}\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and let
\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
be the unique (up
to normalization)
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant vector in
\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}^{(g)}
.

Lemma 3.1

In the representation
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
given by (
3
)
the
O_{2}
-invariant vector
\phi_{O_{2}}\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}^{(t)}
and
the
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant vector
\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\in\mathcal{V}_{{\gamma}}^{(g)}

may be chosen in the following form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{O_{2}}(x)\,=\,(1+x^{2})^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})-1}{2}}\,,\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(x)=\sum_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{P}}\!\!|n|^{-\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})}\,\delta(m+nx)\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{P}=\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}-\{0\}|\,\,{\rm gcd}(m,n)=1,\,(m,n)\sim(-m,-n)\}\,.\end{array}

Proof
. Elements of
O_{2}\subset GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})

may be written in the following form

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}k=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&(-1)^{\epsilon}\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&(-1)^{\epsilon}\,\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\in O_{2}\,,\quad 0\leq\theta<2\pi\,,\qquad\epsilon\in\{0,1\}\,.\end{array}

By (
3
), a direct calculation gives

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}1+(k\cdot x)^{2}\,=\,1+\Big(\frac{(-1)^{\epsilon}\sin\theta+x(-1)^{\epsilon}\cos\theta}{\cos\theta-x\sin\theta}\Big)^{2}\,.\end{array}

Taking into account
|\det k|=1
we infer from (
3
)
the
O_{2}
-invariance of the vector (
3.1
).

Next, we find a
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant vector
\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
by
solving the following equation,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[\pi_{\gamma}(g)\cdot\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}](x)\,=\,\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n_{x}g)\,=\,\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}(n_{x})\,,\qquad g=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&b\\
&&\\
c&&d\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,.\end{array}

The group
GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is generated by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}T=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}1&&1\\
&&\\
0&&1\end{smallmatrix}\Big),\qquad S=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&&1\\
&&\\
1&&0\end{smallmatrix}\Big),\qquad R=\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&&0\\
&&\\
0&&1\end{smallmatrix}\Big)\,,\end{array}

so by (
3
) each generator is acting in

\mathcal{V}_{\gamma}\subset L^{2}(B\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}))
via

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}[\pi_{{\gamma}}(T)\,f](x)\,=\,f(x+1),\qquad[\pi_{{\gamma}}(R)\,f](x)\,=\,f(-x),\\
\,[\pi_{{\gamma}}(S)\,f](x)\,=\,|x|^{\imath({\gamma}_{1}-{\gamma}_{2})-1}\,f(x^{-1})\,.\end{array}

Considering the expression (
3.1
), its

-invariance
reduces to change
n\to-n
of the summation variable, and the
invariance under

is compensated the change of the variable

(which does not spoil the condition
(m,n)=1
). To check the
invariance under

we take into account the following identity

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}|x|^{\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})-1}\,\delta(m+x^{-1}n)\,=\,|x|^{\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})}\,\delta(xm+n)\,=\,\frac{|n|^{\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})}}{|m|^{\imath(\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2})}}\,\delta(xm+n)\,.\end{array}

This completes a verification of the required properties of
(
3.1
) and (
3.1
).
\Box

Now the Eisenstein automorphic function
associated with
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})
is defined as a matrix element

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{\gamma}(g)=\langle\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})},\pi_{\gamma}(g)\phi_{O(2)}\rangle\,.\end{array}

Note that
\Phi_{\gamma}(g)
obviously defines a function on the double coset
\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

-automorphic

Proposition 3.1

For the

-Eisenstein function given by the matrix element (

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\Phi_{\gamma}(g)=\langle\phi_{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})},\pi_{\gamma}(g)\,\phi_{O_{2}}\rangle\\

3.1

which gives (

The Eisenstein series may be written in a form that makes its

Lemma 3.2

Let us define a subgroup

where

In the expression (

4
Global
GL_{2}
Hecke-Baxter operator

To construct global
GL_{2}
Hecke-Baxter operator we start with the
description of the elements of the Hecke algebras

\mathcal{H}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}),GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
and
\mathcal{H}(GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}),O_{2})
acting on the
space of the
GL_{2}
-automorphic forms, functions on
\mathcal{M}_{2}
(by
convolutions form the right and form the left). Taking into account
the interpretation of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
as space equivalence classes of
lattices
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we introduce the following averaging
operators
T_{n}
as analogs of (
2
)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{n}\cdot f)(L)=\sum_{[L:L^{\prime}]=n}\,f(L^{\prime})\,,\end{array}

where the sum goes over sub-lattices
L^{\prime}\subset L
of index
n
.
The double coset description of
\mathcal{M}_{2}
allows to rewrite
the operators (
4
) as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{n}\cdot f)(g)=\sum_{\gamma\in{SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\!f(\gamma\cdot g)\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Mat}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,=\,\{\gamma\in{\rm Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})|\det\gamma=n\}\,.\end{array}

This action may be written more explicitly using a specific choice of
representatives of the coset space

SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Lemma 4.1

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, the space
{\rm Mat}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
defined in (
4
) allows the following coset decomposition:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Mat}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{{\sigma}(n)}\,\,SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,\alpha_{i}\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&b_{i}\\
0&d_{i}\end{pmatrix},\qquad a_{i},b_{i},d_{i}>0,\quad a_{i}d_{i}=n,\quad 0\leq b_{i}<d_{i}\,,\end{array}

and the number
{\sigma}(n)
of the coset representatives

SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
equals the positive divisors sum:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\sigma}(n)\,=\sum_{d|n}d\,.\end{array}

Proof
. See e.g.
[
L
]
, Chapter II, §1.
\Box

As a consequence of the previous Lemma we obtain the following
presentation for the operators
T_{n}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(T_{n}\cdot f)(g)\,=\sum_{a,d>0\atop ad=n}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}\!f\Big(\,\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&b\\
&&\\
0&&d\end{smallmatrix}\Big)g\Big)\,.\end{array}

Let us combine the operators
T_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
into the generating series

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}Q^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}T_{n}\,,\end{array}

acting on a function on the space of lattices
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the
following way

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}(Q^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}\cdot f)(L)\,=\sum_{L^{\prime}\subset L}^{\infty}\frac{1}{[L:L^{\prime}]^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,f(L^{\prime})\,,\end{array}

(note that the shift
s\to s+\frac{1}{2}
is a special case of the
general expression
s+\frac{\ell}{2}
for
GL_{\ell+1}
).
Equivalently,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q^{SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}_{s}\cdot f\bigr)(g)\,=\!\!\sum_{{\alpha}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,\frac{1}{|\det{\alpha}|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,f(\alpha\cdot g)\,,\end{array}

where
{\alpha}
runs through the set of
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-coset representatives
(
4.1
) of the space

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,={\rm Mat}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cap GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Q})=\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}{\rm Mat}^{(n)}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,.\end{array}

One has an analog of the Riemann zeta-function for
GL_{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta^{GL_{2}}(s)=\sum_{\alpha\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{1}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,.\end{array}

Lemma 4.2

The following identity holds

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta^{GL_{2}}(s)=\sum_{\alpha\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{1}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}=\zeta\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\zeta\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,\end{array}

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta-function given by
by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{n^{s}}\,,\qquad{\rm Re}(s)>1\,.\end{array}

Proof
. Using the set of representatives for the coset we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{\alpha\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{1}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}=\sum_{a,d>0\atop 0\leq b<d}\frac{1}{a^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{d^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,.\end{array}

Summing over
b
we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{\alpha\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{1}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}=\sum_{a,d>0}\frac{1}{a^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\cdot\frac{1}{d^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}=\zeta\left(s+\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\zeta\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.\end{array}

\Box

Below we encounter a generalization of (
4.2
)
associated with spherical principle series representations. The
following result is a direct consequence of the well-known expressions
for action of the Hecke algebra generators
T_{n}
(
4

Proposition 4.1

The action of the operator
Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
on
the
GL_{2}
-Eisenstein functions (
3.1
) associated with a
spherical principle series representation
(\pi_{\gamma},\mathcal{V}_{\gamma})

is given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}\bigr)(g)\,=\,\zeta^{GL_{2}}(s|\gamma)\,\,\Phi_{\gamma}(g)\,,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta^{GL_{2}}(s|\gamma)=\zeta(s-\imath\gamma_{1})\,\zeta(s-\imath\gamma_{2})\,.\end{array}

Proof
. Application of the Hecke-Baxter operator (
4
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}\bigr)(g)=\sum_{\alpha\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{1}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,\,\Phi_{\gamma}(\alpha\cdot g)\,.\end{array}

Using the presentation (
3
), (
3
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}\bigr)(\tau,t)\\
=\,t^{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}\!\!\sum_{\alpha\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,\frac{|\det\alpha|^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}{2}}}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\!\!\sum_{\gamma\in B(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,({\rm Im}(\gamma\cdot\alpha\cdot\tau))^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})+1}{2}}\,.\end{array}

We formally
extend the summation domain in the first sum

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}\bigr)(\tau,t)\\
=\,\frac{t^{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}}{|SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})|\cdot|B(\mathbb{Z})|}\!\!\sum_{\alpha\in{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{|\det\alpha|^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}{2}}}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\!\!\sum_{\gamma\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,({\rm Im}(\gamma\cdot\alpha\cdot\tau))^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})+1}{2}}\\
=\,\frac{t^{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}}{|SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})|\cdot|B(\mathbb{Z})|}\!\!\sum_{\alpha\in{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{|\det\alpha|^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}{2}}}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\!\!\sum_{\gamma\in GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,({\rm Im}(\alpha\cdot\gamma\cdot\tau))^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})+1}{2}}\,.\end{array}

Let us split the summation domain using the right hand side analog
of the decomposition (
4.1
)

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,=\,\bigsqcup_{n>0}\bigsqcup_{i}\,\,\tilde{\alpha}_{i}\,SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,,\end{array}

to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}\bigr)(\tau,t)\\
=t^{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}\,\,\sum_{\alpha\in{\rm Mat}^{*}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})/SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\frac{|\det\alpha|^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}{2}}}{|\det\alpha|^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\,\,\sum_{\gamma\in B(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,({\rm Im}(\alpha\cdot\gamma\cdot\tau))^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})+1}{2}}\,.\end{array}

Now using the identity

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Im}(\alpha\cdot\gamma\cdot z)=\frac{a}{d}\cdot{\rm Im}(\gamma\cdot z)\,,\end{array}

and the fact that
\det\alpha=ad
for the considered representatives
of the coset space we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\bigl(Q_{s}^{GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}\bigr)(\tau,t)\\
=t^{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}\,\,\left(\sum_{a,d>0}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}\,\,\frac{a^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}{2}}}{a^{s-\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})}{2}}}\frac{d^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2})}{2}}}{d^{s+\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})}{2}+1}}\right)\sum_{\gamma\in B(\mathbb{Z})\backslash GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\,\,({\rm Im}(\gamma\cdot\tau))^{\frac{\imath(\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1})+1}{2}}\\
=\left(\sum_{a,d>0}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}\,\,\frac{1}{a^{s-\imath\gamma_{2}}}\frac{1}{d^{s-\imath\gamma_{1}+1}}\right)\cdot\Phi_{\gamma}(\tau,t)\,.\end{array}

Thus for the eigenvalue we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum_{a,d>0}\sum_{b=0}^{d-1}\,\,\frac{1}{a^{s-\imath\gamma_{1}}}\frac{1}{d^{s-\imath\gamma_{2}+1}}=\sum_{a,d>0}\,\,\frac{1}{a^{s-\imath\gamma_{1}}}\frac{1}{d^{s-\imath\gamma_{2}}}=\zeta(s-\imath\gamma_{1})\,\zeta(s-\imath\gamma_{2})\,.\end{array}

This completes the proof.

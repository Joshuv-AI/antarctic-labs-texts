Dose-Response Relationship of Single Intravenous Remimazolam on Bispectral Index and Cardiorespiratory Effects
Authors: Jianxiao Chen, Jianqiao Luo, Honglan Ye, Hao Lv, Wenyao Li, Fangfang Luo, Youmei Zhou, Fei Xiao, Chen Lv, Xiaowen Guo, Shaoqiang Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Bispectral Index (BIS) is a standardized, continuous measure of sedation depth. Studies have shown that remimazolam can induce a decrease in BIS, but the dose-response relationship between single-dose remimazolam and BIS remains unclear.PurposeThis study aimed to explore the correlation between single intravenous doses of remimazolam and BIS, and to observe its effects on respiration and circulation.Patients and MethodsThis was a prospective, randomized, double-blind, dose-finding study. One hundred patients scheduled for elective surgery under general anesthesia were randomly assigned to five groups and received intravenous remimazolam at 0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, or 0.6 mg/kg before anesthesia induction. BIS values at each time point, minimum BIS (BISmin), vital signs, adverse events, and interventions were recorded. Probit regression was used to calculate the ED50 and ED90 for the BIS ≤ 60 threshold (primary exploratory analysis), and a post hoc exploratory analysis was performed using the BIS ≤ 65 threshold.ResultsBIS values decreased more significantly with increasing remimazolam doses, with a plateau effect observed above 0.4 mg/kg. The BISmin values for each group were 60.6 ± 10.4, 58.4 ± 9.2, 58.1 ± 6.2, 55.6 ± 7.9, and 55.0 ± 6.4, respectively. Using the BIS ≤ 60 threshold, the ED50 and ED90 were 0.317 mg/kg (95% CI: 0.187–0.415 mg/kg) and 1.172 mg/kg (95% CI: 0.704–13.959 mg/kg), respectively. The extremely wide confidence interval for the ED90 reflects the statistical fact that the Probit model could not reliably extrapolate the dose-response relationship at higher doses within the studied range. Using the BIS ≤ 65 threshold in a post hoc exploratory analysis, the ED50 and ED90 were 0.147 mg/kg (95% CI: 0.024–0.216 mg/kg) and 0.436 mg/kg (95% CI: 0.334–0.958 mg/kg), respectively, with relatively narrower confidence intervals. Sensitivity analysis after excluding one patient who received ciprofol rescue was consistent with the primary analysis. There were no significant differences between groups in the incidence of tachycardia, bradycardia, hypoxemia, apnea, injection pain, body movement, diaphragmatic spasm, or cough, nor in the need for interventions. No serious adverse events occurred.ConclusionA dose-dependent relationship exists between single-dose remimazolam and BIS, with good respiratory and circulatory safety in the 0.2–0.6 mg/kg range and a plateau effect above 0.4 mg/kg. The extremely wide confidence interval for the ED90 estimated using the BIS ≤ 60 threshold suggests that this endpoint may not be suitable for pharmacodynamic evaluation of single-dose remimazolam. The ED estimates obtained with the BIS ≤ 65 threshold are from a post hoc exploratory analysis and await prospective validation in future studies incorporating clinical sedation assessments, such as the Modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (MOAA/S) scale.

Introduction

Bispectral Index (BIS) monitoring can prevent residual consciousness caused by persistent or intermittent suboptimal effective anesthetic drug concentrations in the cerebral cortex, avoid potential adverse reactions associated with drug overdose, and provide an intuitive basis for clinical assessment.1 Propofol has a strong correlation with BIS,2 but it can easily cause respiratory and circulatory depression, and prolonged or high-dose infusion may lead to hyperkalemia, heart failure, or even cardiac arrest.3 The classic benzodiazepine sedative midazolam has individualized sedative effects, and its active metabolites retain sedative function,4 even at supra-sedative doses, it can not induce BIS below 60,5 suggesting that BIS may not be ideal for assessing its sedation level.1

Remimazolam, a water-soluble derivative of midazolam, has rapid onset and strong controllability6,7 and is used for procedural sedation and general anesthesia induction and maintenance.8 Systematic studies on the dose-response relationship between single-dose remimazolam and BIS are still limited. The primary objective of this study was to explore the dose-response relationship between different single doses of remimazolam and BIS, and to observe its effects on respiration and circulation, with ED50 and ED90 estimation using the traditional general anesthesia sedation threshold as a supportive analysis. Considering that previous literature suggests the traditional BIS ≤ 60 threshold may have limitations when applied to remimazolam,2,6,9–12 this study also planned a post hoc exploratory analysis using the BIS ≤ 65 threshold to provide preliminary reference information.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This was a prospective, single-center, randomized, double-blind, dose-finding study approved by the Ethics Committee of Shangyu Maternal and Child Health Hospital, Shaoxing, Zhejiang (Ethics No.: LL-2024-07-26; Ethics Committee Chair: Li Wang), and registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (https://www.chictr.org.cn; Registration No.: ChiCTR2400088425; Principal Investigator: Jianxiao Chen; Registration Date: August 19, 2024). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrollment. The trial was conducted at Shangyu Maternal and Child Health Hospital, Shaoxing, Zhejiang, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and CONSORT guidelines.

Inclusion criteria: age 18–65 years, ASA physical status I–II, body mass index (BMI) 18–28 kg/m2, scheduled for elective surgery under general anesthesia.

Exclusion criteria: obstructive sleep apnea syndrome, bronchiectasis, asthma, uncontrolled hypertension (systolic blood pressure >160 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure >100 mmHg), cardiac insufficiency (NYHA class III–IV),13 pulmonary insufficiency (moderate-to-severe COPD), hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh class B/C),14 renal insufficiency (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73m2), history of severe central nervous system disease, diabetes (HbA1c ≥9%), neuromuscular disease, intolerance or allergy to benzodiazepines, allergy or suspected allergy to study drugs, history of antipsychotic or antidepressant use, alcohol or drug dependence, pre-induction BIS <80, potential difficult airway (Mallampati >2), patient refusal, or other conditions deemed unsuitable by the anesthesiologist.

Randomization and Blinding

A statistician not involved in observation or research used SPSS 27.0 (IBM) software to generate a 1:1:1:1:1 randomization sequence, assigning participants to five groups: remimazolam 0.2 mg/kg (R0.2 group), 0.3 mg/kg (R0.3 group), 0.4 mg/kg (R0.4 group), 0.5 mg/kg (R0.5 group), and 0.6 mg/kg (R0.6 group). Each allocation result was placed in an opaque sealed envelope. An anesthesia nurse not involved in observation or assessment opened the envelopes sequentially before anesthesia and prepared the study drugs accordingly. All groups received remimazolam diluted to 20 mL with 0.9% saline (20 mL syringe). The anesthesiologist administering the drug and the another anesthesiologist responsible for observation and recording were both blinded to group allocation.

Anesthesia Management and Intervention

All patients fasted for 8 hours before surgery and received no premedication. Upon arrival in the operating room, standard monitoring was applied, including non-invasive blood pressure, electrocardiography, Saturation of peripheral oxygen (SpO2), and BIS, with end-tidal CO2 waveform for respiratory monitoring. Patients received 6 mL/kg sodium-potassium-calcium-magnesium glucose injection via peripheral intravenous infusion over 30 minutes, and inhaled 100% oxygen via mask for at least 3 minutes before remimazolam administration. Baseline SBP, DBP, HR, SpO2, and BIS (after 1 minute of stabilization) were recorded. Baseline SBP, DBP, and HR were taken as the mean of three consecutive measurements (1-minute intervals).

The study dose of remimazolam (National Drug Approval No. H20190034, Hengrui Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.) was administered via an intravenous infusion pump at a constant rate over 30 seconds. The non-invasive blood pressure measurement was initiated immediately after drug administration, and BIS monitoring continued for 5 minutes after drug administration.

If BIS ≥80 during the observation period, intravenous ciprofol 0.2 mg/kg was administered as rescue, and subsequent BIS values were taken as the last value before ciprofol administration. At the end of the observation period (T300), if BIS ≤65, patients received intravenous sufentanil 0.4 μg/kg and rocuronium 1.0 mg/kg, followed by tracheal intubation under video laryngoscopy after adequate muscle relaxation. If BIS >65 at T300, intravenous ciprofol 0.2 mg/kg was administered (repeated if necessary), followed by sufentanil and rocuronium once BIS ≤65 was achieved, and tracheal intubation was performed.

Hypotension was defined as a decrease in systolic blood pressure exceeding 30% of baseline (excluding interference from relatively low baseline blood pressure);15,16 hypertension was defined as an increase in systolic blood pressure exceeding 30% of baseline;15,17 tachycardia was defined as heart rate >100 bpm; bradycardia was defined as heart rate <60 bpm. Apnea was defined as complete loss or marked reduction (≥90% decrease) of CO2 waveform lasting ≥10 seconds,18 managed by jaw-thrust maneuver. Hypoxemia was defined as SpO2≤90% during oxygen supplementation, managed by mask-assisted ventilation, with intubation if necessary. Injection pain was defined as patient-reported pain or withdrawal response of the infusion arm. Body movement was defined as movement of any body part other than the infusion arm during drug injection. Hypotension was treated with norepinephrine 4 μg per dose; hypertension with urapidil 10 mg per dose; heart rate >120 bpm with esmolol 0.5 mg/kg; heart rate <45 bpm with atropine 0.5 mg per dose. These measures could be repeated as necessary, with emergency preparedness maintained.

Outcome Measures

Primary outcome measure: BIS values. Recording time points: before drug administration, and at 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240, 270, and 300 seconds after drug administration, designated as Pre-induced, T0, T30, T60, T90, T120, T150, T180, T210, T240, T270, and T300.

Secondary outcome measures: Hemodynamic parameters (SBP, DBP, HR, SpO2), recorded at pre-drug, and 0, 60, 120, 180, 240, and 300 seconds after drug administration (Pre-induced, T0, T60, T120, T180, T240, T300). Incidence of hypoxemia, apnea, injection pain, body movement and diaphragmatic spasm.

Exploratory outcome measures: BISmin (the minimum BIS value within 300 seconds) and based on this, the duration of effective sedation (time with BIS ≤60 and BIS ≤65, in seconds).

Statistical Analysis

Sample size was estimated based on pre-experimental results. In the pre-experiment, the sedation success rates (using BIS ≤60 as the effective sedation criterion) for the lowest dose group (0.2 mg/kg) and highest dose group (0.6 mg/kg) were 45.45% and 93.33%, respectively. With α = 0.05 (two-sided), β = 0.1, and equal group sizes, PASS 11.0 (NCCS) calculated a sample size of 16 per group. Considering potential dropout rates, 20 patients per group were ultimately set, with a total sample size of 100.

The Shapiro–Wilk test and Levene’s test were used to assess normality and homogeneity of variance for continuous variables. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared between groups using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), with post-hoc pairwise comparisons using Tukey’s HSD test. Non-normally distributed data were expressed as median (Q1, Q3) and compared using the Kruskal–Wallis H-test, with post-hoc pairwise comparisons using Dunn’s test. Repeated-measures ANOVA was used to compare BIS values and vital signs (SBP, DBP, HR, SpO2) across time points, with Tukey’s HSD test for multiple comparisons. All tests were two-tailed, and adjusted P values were reported when Tukey corrections were applied. Count data were expressed as number of cases (%), and comparisons were made using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 27.0), and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism (version 10.0). A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Considering previous literature6,19,20 and our pre-experimental results, the effective sedation rates using both BIS ≤60 and BIS ≤65 criteria were calculated for each group, and Probit regression was used to estimate ED50 and ED90. The BIS ≤60 analysis was designated as the primary exploratory analysis, while the BIS ≤65 analysis was designated as a post hoc exploratory analysis.

Discussion

This study explored the correlation between single-dose remimazolam (0.2–0.6 mg/kg) and BIS, and observed its effects on respiration and circulation. The main findings included: a clear dose-dependent relationship between remimazolam dose and BIS, with a plateau effect above 0.4 mg/kg; good respiratory and circulatory safety within the 0.2–0.6 mg/kg range; the ED90 estimated using the BIS ≤60 criterion (1.172 mg/kg) far exceeded commonly used clinical doses21–24 with an extremely wide confidence interval (0.704–13.959 mg/kg), reflecting the statistical fact that the Probit model could not reliably extrapolate the dose-response relationship at higher doses within the studied range, suggesting a potential mismatch between this endpoint and single-dose remimazolam evaluation; the ED90 estimated using the BIS ≤65 post hoc exploratory criterion had a relatively narrower confidence interval (0.334–0.958 mg/kg), but since it was not combined with clinical sedation scales (eg, MOAA/S), it should be regarded as an exploratory finding based on this study.

We observed significant non-linear decreases in BIS over time (P <0.0001), with BIS rapidly dropping to approximately 70 at T30. However, mean BIS values in all groups remained above 50, with the R0.2 group having the highest values,25,26 suggesting relatively light sedation. No significant differences in BIS were detected among the three medium-to-high dose groups (R0.4, R0.5, R0.6) at any time point, and the BIS values in the R0.5 and R0.6 groups were closely similar at all time points. Notably, one case in the R0.2 group had a BIS60 of 38 (duration <30 seconds), while all other cases had BISmin above 40; even at the highest dose (0.6 mg/kg), nearly twice the recommended induction dose,22 no burst suppression was observed.22,27–30 These results suggest that when remimazolam doses exceed 0.4 mg/kg, the sedative effect reaches a plateau, and further dose increases may not provide additional BIS benefit. Furthermore, no significant differences in BIS were observed between T30 and T60 across groups, suggesting that the BIS peak time for remimazolam may be around 60 seconds or later,27,31 highlighting the importance of monitoring timing in clinical practice.

Tachycardia occurred immediately after remimazolam injection in 2 cases each in the R0.2, R0.4, and R0.5 groups, with a maximum rate of 107 bpm. Bradycardia was only occasionally observed across groups, with a minimum heart rate of 48 bpm; changes did not exceed 20% of baseline and were transient. Although three cases of systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg occurred in the high-dose groups, they did not meet the hypotension criterion15,16 and improved after single-dose norepinephrine 4 μg intravenous administration. Apnea occurred in 2, 5, 5, 4, and 5 cases from the low-dose to high-dose groups, respectively. SpO2 decreases were mainly observed within 2 minutes after injection, with a minimum of 94%. The isolated decrease in SpO2 in the 0.4 mg/kg group at T300 was at the boundary of statistical significance (interaction P = 0.0485), appeared at only a single time point in multiple comparisons, and lacked a dose-dependent trend—the 0.5 mg/kg and 0.6 mg/kg groups did not show similar decreases. Even the lowest values remained above the clinical threshold, indicating that remimazolam’s effects on the cardiovascular and respiratory systems within this dose range are mild and manageable.32–34 Remimazolam-induced injection pain was not accompanied by limb withdrawal; body movements were minimal single movements of fingers or toes. Diaphragmatic spasm or cough occurred in isolated cases, possibly related to remimazolam’s relatively low selectivity for GABA neurotransmitters,35–37 or to higher doses,37 rapid administration,38 or inadequate sedation depth.39

This study had several limitations. First, BIS was recorded at discrete time points rather than continuously, and the observation period was limited to 300 seconds; further BIS decreases after 300 seconds cannot be excluded. Second, the ED90 estimated using the BIS ≤60 endpoint had an extremely wide confidence interval. Although sensitivity analysis confirmed the robustness of the results, this wide confidence interval itself suggests that BIS ≤60 may not be suitable for pharmacodynamic evaluation of remimazolam, or that a larger sample size and wider dose range are needed for stable estimation. The BIS ≤65 exploratory threshold, while supported by literature, requires further studies to confirm its correlation with postoperative outcomes. Third, this study did not include clinical sedation scales such as MOAA/S, and therefore cannot determine whether BIS ≤60 or BIS ≤65 is the more appropriate surgical sedation threshold—determining the optimal BIS threshold for remimazolam requires a dedicated prospective study incorporating clinical sedation reference indicators (eg, MOAA/S). Fourth, this study observed remimazolam as a single agent, whereas clinical anesthesia induction often involves multiple drugs with different mechanisms of action on BIS, particularly the strong synergistic effects of opioids;40 thus, extrapolation to polypharmacy scenarios should be cautious. Finally, this was a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, limited to Chinese adult patients with ASA I–II, and results may not be generalizable to elderly, obese, or high-comorbidity populations, requiring dedicated studies for validation.

Conclusion

A clear dose-dependent relationship exists between single-dose remimazolam and BIS, with good respiratory and circulatory safety in the 0.2–0.6 mg/kg range and a plateau effect above 0.4 mg/kg. The extremely wide confidence interval for the ED90 estimated using the BIS ≤60 criterion suggests that this endpoint may not be suitable for reliable pharmacodynamic evaluation of single-dose remimazolam. The ED estimates obtained with the BIS ≤65 threshold are post hoc exploratory findings and should serve only as a reference for future research; they require prospective validation in dedicated studies incorporating clinical sedation reference indicators (eg, MOAA/S) and should not be used as clinical dosing recommendations.

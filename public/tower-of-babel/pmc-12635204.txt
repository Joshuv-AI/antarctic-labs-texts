Systemic inflammatory response to cemented and uncemented total hip arthroplasty with PMMA bone cement
Authors: Skender Ukaj, Dren Ukaj, Fatime Dervishaj, Dea Dyla, Ermira Krasniqi, Gazmend Elezi, Rilind Obërtinca, Arben Boshnjaku
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement is a commonly used material in total hip arthroplasty (THA) intervention procedures due to its ability to provide mechanical stability. However, as any other external material implanted in the organism, it imposes certain risks for potentially provoking inflammatory and acute allergic reaction. This study aimed to investigate the organism’s systemic inflammatory response in patients undergoing cemented versus uncemented THA. This prospective study included 58 patients aged 72.9 ± 8.8 years (37 females) undergoing primary THA. Participants received either cemented or uncemented femoral stems based on preoperatively established clinical criteria. Serum levels of histamine, CRP, ESR, IL-6, and TNF-α were assessed at two time points: preoperatively and 24 h postoperatively. Postoperative levels of all inflammatory biomarkers increased significantly compared to the baseline assessments (p < 0.001). Patients receiving PMMA bone cemented implantations showed significantly higher levels of histamine and TNF-α (p < 0.001), IL-6, CRP (p = 0.001) and ESR (p = 0.002) compared to the uncemented group. The increase was more pronounced in biological females for IL-6, ESR and CRP. Cemented THA using PMMA bone cement implantation is associated with a stronger systemic inflammatory response compared to uncemented procedures, particularly among female patients. These findings underscore the importance of considering biomaterial-induced inflammation in both surgical planning and postoperative monitoring. Further large-scale, longitudinal studies should follow to evaluate the clinical significance of these immunological changes.Trial is registered at the ClinicalTrials.gov (29/04/2019; ID: NCT03930537) while being part of a series of investigations on the implantation of hip prosthesis encompassing the PhD of Dr. Skender Ukaj.

Background

Polymethylmethacrylate (PMMA) bone cement is a material widely used in orthopaedic clinical practices as a mechanism to help fixate femoral components within the total hip arthroplasty (THA)1. Not all fixations in orthopaedic surgeries include PMMA, but it is often a method of choice when wanting to provide sustainable and practical work. Cement-retained restorations on the used implants in surgical procedures in general do ensure better passive fit, and better aesthetics, just as much as providing a simplicity of fabrication and a proportional distribution of load during the function2. Although rarely reported, attention has been directed toward the potential biological implications of PMMA usage. The polymerization of PMMA releases heat and residual monomers, which could trigger both local and systemic inflammatory responses, all of which have the potential to contribute towards certain complications like aseptic loosening and hypersensitivity reactions3,4.

PMMA was reported to induce histamine release, thus elevating plasma histamine levels5. Despite its generally accepted biocompatibility, a small number of patients have reported allergic reactions from PMMA as a foreign material. The first documented case was reported in 2010 following cement carinoplasty in a 39-year-old female patient6. PMMA cement allergy reaction could also be simply misleading for articular infection7. Simultaneously, systemic inflammatory markers like the erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and C-reactive protein (CRP) can also help to observe this nonspecific acute response after the surgical intervention or biomaterial exposure8. Major surgical interventions like THA are accompanied by a well-characterized systemic inflammatory response. Tissue injury and ischemia–reperfusion during surgery can cause the release of damage-associated molecular patterns, which activates resident immune cells and circulating leukocytes. This activation leads to the rapid release of histamine and other mediators, followed by the production of proinflammatory cytokines like IL-6 and TNF-α. Then these cytokines stimulate hepatic synthesis of acute-phase proteins (including CRP) and promote changes in leukocyte activity and vascular permeability. The ESR reflects these systemic changes in plasma proteins. Altogether, this early innate immune activation within the first 24–48 h postoperatively represents the systemic inflammatory response to surgery, which we intended to characterize and compare between cemented and uncemented approaches.

Furthermore, the proinflammatory cytokines that are central to the immune response such as the interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) have also been suggested for an implication on the pathogenesis of inflammation-mediated osteolysis and prosthetic failure2,9. In vitro and in vivo studies have shown that PMMA particles stimulate the secretion of these cytokines by macrophages and other immune cells, thereby sustaining a proinflammatory microenvironment around the implant7.

The potential side effects on human organisms and its functioning from the application of PMMA bone cement have not managed to attract attention for mass investigation, with available data on the matter being rather scarce, limited and with studies mostly of the nature of case reports, case series or similar2,7,10,11. Therefore, the lack of data on the direct influence of clinically used PMMA bone cement on a comprehensive panel of inflammatory biomarkers in human subjects remains insufficiently characterized and underexplored. Biomarkers like histamine, IL-6, TNF-α, ESR, and CRP are known to rise sharply within the first 24 h, providing insight into the early immune and acute allergic reactions. This study aimed to quantify systemic changes in these particular inflammatory biomarkers (histamine, IL-6, TNF-α, ESR, CRP) before and 24 h after THA, and to compare cemented (PMMA) versus uncemented femoral fixation. We further explored histamine as a potential mediator of biomaterial-related inflammation, without testing for, or concluding on, clinical allergy.

Methodology

Study participants

This is a prospective, interventional clinical trial. The intervention involved THA, with the implantation of either cemented or uncemented femoral stems.

The clinical trial was prospectively registered at the ClinicalTrials.gov (29/04/2019; ID: NCT03930537) and had received prior approval by the Ethical Committee of the Kosovo Medical Chamber (ref. No. 18/2021, date 02.04.2021). Prior to undergoing the procedure, written consent was obtained from each patiners, and the ethical standards set out in the Declaration of Helsinki for experimentation with human subjects were followed.

All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Participants were enrolled at the University Clinical Centre of Kosovo (UCCK) in Prishtina and Otrila Hospital in Prishtina, Kosovo throughout the April – June 2021 period. A total of 58 participants aged 65 years and older (average 72.9 ± 8.8) out of whom 37 females and 21 males (aged 72.2 ± 9.3 and 74.2 ± 8.0, respectively) fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were willing to participate. Number of participants was decided upon the eligible candidates and their will to participate, following similar numbers to previously published similar studies. Amongst, 30 underwent PMMA bone cement implantation procedure, and 28 the uncemented procedure. In all patients, the acetabular component was implanted cementless (press-fit). Therefore, cemented cases in this study represent hybrid THA (cemented femoral stem with cementless acetabular cup).

Inclusion criteria involved adult patients in need to undergo this intervention as assessed by the orthopaedic surgeon (including patients with coxarthrosis) with no age restriction that consented to participate in the study.

Exclusion criteria included patients with hip endoprosthesis revision, and those receiving antihistamines, immunosuppressants, cytostatic, and antidepressants.

Comorbidities and lifestyle factors (e.g., smoking, alcohol use, COPD) were not systematically recorded in standardized data collection forms.

Data collection and biochemical analysis

Participants’ data were collected at the entrance by an orthopaedic surgeon, followed by a thorough professional evaluation by an anaesthesiologist. Both professionals assessed the collected data to determine the participants’ eligibility to join the study and proceed with the surgery.

All laboratory analyses were conducted at least two hours before the surgery (first assessment) and on the first day after the surgery. The 24-hour time point was selected to capture the peak of the acute systemic inflammatory response, as most proinflammatory mediators (e.g., histamine, IL-6, TNF-α) are known to rise within this early postoperative time period. Later time points were not assessed, as the study’s primary aim was to compare immediate inflammatory effects of cemented versus uncemented arthroplasty. A licensed nurse took the blood samples from the antebrachial vein following a predefined protocol.

For assessing the CRP levels, venous blood was collected through an S-Monovette 7.5 ml Z tube (SARSTEDT AG&Co. KG. Germany), centrifuged at 2000 g for 10 min and the serum was preserved at −20 °C until further analysis. CRP levels were measured using latex-enhanced immunoturbidimetric assay, with the ABX Pentra CRP CP reagent on the ABX Pentra C400 Clinical Chemistry Analyzer device (HORIBA, Japan) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

To assess the histamine levels, 2.6 ml of blood was collected in a K3 EDTA (SARSTEDT AG&Co. KG-Germany), centrifuged at 2000 g for 10 min, with 1.0 ml of plasma further separated and stored at −20 °C. Histamine levels were assessed using the ELISA kit (AB213975).

The ESR rate in millimetres/hour (mm/h) was assessed using a standard S Sedivete 3.5 ml 4NC test tubes, following the Westergren method12.

For assessing concentrations of IL1b, IL6, and TNF-α, blood samples were collected in a S-Monovette 7.5 ml Z tube (SARSTEDT AG&Co. KG-Germany), centrifuged at 2,000 g for 10 min and the serum stored at −20 °C. Measurements were conducted using a fully automated Cobas 6000 analyzer (F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. Roche, Germany) following the ECLIA method and using IL6 Kit for IL6 (ELECSYS IL-6 Roche, Germany), and PCR method for IL1b and TNF-α using Sanger Sequencer Analyzed AI 3130 device (Applied Biosystems® 3130/3130xl Genetic Analyzers, CA, USA) and the IL1b and TNF kits.

Intervention process

The procedure was performed in a posterior approach with patient positioned lateraly. A T-shaped capsulotomy was made to open the joint capsule, and the femoral head was dislocated from the acetabulum. The femoral neck was then resected about 1 cm above the lesser trochanter, with the resection angle based on the femoral component type.

Preoperative radiological planning included measuring the intramedullary canal width and the cortical bone thickness at the proximal and distal femur in order to guide the femoral component sizing and decide whether to go with or without PMMA bone cement. The final choice of fixation could be adapted intra-operatively if bone quality or press-fit stability was found inadequate. Osteoporosis was assessed radiographically (thin cortices, widened canal, sparse trabeculae) since DXA was not available. All procedures were performed by 2 experienced orthopaedic surgeons using the same posterolateral approach and standardized implant system.

Following the completion of all preparatory steps, acetabular and femoral components were implanted, after which a drain was placed and all anatomical layers (including skin) were sutured sequentially.

In this study, all acetabular components were implanted cementless (press-fit technique). The femoral component was implanted either cementless or cemented with PMMA bone cement, depending on bone quality. Thus, cases with cemented femoral stems represent hybrid THA (cemented femoral stem with cementless acetabular cup) featuring QUATTRO™ PNP acetabular cup (Groupe Lepine, France) made of titanium. The cup has a durable, smooth plastic inner shell. The PAVI™ femoral stem is made of self-locking titanium alloy for cementless use, available in 130° and 135° neck-shaft angles. The cemented option uses a 135° neck-shaft angle. The femoral component consists of a metal stem with a metal or ceramic ball at the end. The decision to use the PMMA bone cemented prosthesis on the particular patient was made based on clinical factors like osteoporosis (as determined by the x-ray imaging) and femoral shaft instability after reaming. After femoral neck resection, the acetabulum was prepared for the acetabular component, and the femoral canal was reamed. The canal was irrigated with saline, a drain was inserted, and cement was applied using the finger pack method.

All patients received standardized institutional perioperative management, though operative time, anaesthesia type, and detailed postoperative medication records were not systematically collected for this study. This precluded adjustment for their possible effects on inflammatory biomarkers.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS version 27 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, United States) with a significance level set at p < 0.05. Data were presented as mean and standard deviation for continuous variables and relative frequencies for categorical variables. Due to small subgroups of participants in certain assessments Skewness and Kurtosis tests for normality of distribution were performed and hinted for parametric data. Independent samples t-test was used for conducting comparisons between two groups, whereas 1-way ANOVA was used for comparing three different groups. For assessing the statistical significance of histamine, IL-6, TNFα, ESR, and CRP in the two measured timelines (before and after the intervention) paired samples test was used.

Pearson correlation coefficient (r) was used to determine the association between the assessed variables levels before and after the intervention in cases where THA intervention was conducted with the implantation of cemented femoral stems.

Discussion

This study investigated the inflammatory response in patients undergoing THA, with a particular focus on the impact of PMMA bone cement implantation in the femoral component. A significant post-surgical elevation of the inflammation levels of histamine, IL-6, TNF-α, ESR, and CRP was observed across all participants. Nonetheless, the group receiving the PMMA bone cement implantation in the femoral component demonstrated a stronger inflammatory response compared to the uncemented group. Because our analysis was limited to the 24-hour postoperative period, we cannot determine whether biomarker concentrations converge between groups beyond this time. These findings should therefore be interpreted as reflecting early systemic immune activation rather than long-term inflammatory trajectories.

Keeping in mind the lack of studies investigating this particular topic, a review by Santavista S and colleagues14 suggested that both cemented and cementless prosthesis cause a foreign body type host response in the form of either adaptive or reactive processes. Our findings comply with the existing body of evidence indicating that PMMA, notwithstanding its benefits, may cause biological risks due to its polymerization by-products (like heat and residual monomers), which could then facilitate both immune activation and local tissue irritations3.

The particular elevation of histamine levels (as the key mediator of organisms’ allergic response) in total participants and also when separated into biological sexes, suggests for a potential acute allergic reaction due to the PMMA bone cement implantation. While the elevation of histamine indicates a potential acute hypersensitivity response to PMMA bone cement, our study design does not allow conclusions about downstream clinical outcomes such as infection rates, recovery duration, or prosthesis performance. The observed histamine increase should therefore be interpreted as a biomarker of systemic immune activation within the early postoperative period, rather than as a predictor of clinical complications. On the other hand, the measurement of histamine release from circulating basophils was intended to capture an acute, systemic hypersensitivity component (Type I), which typically manifests within minutes to hours and can be detected in plasma for up to 24 h post-exposure. In contrast, Type III (immune complex-mediated) and Type IV (delayed, T-cell mediated) hypersensitivity reactions occur later and are generally confined to local tissue environments, thus not expected to be reflected in systemic blood markers within the short postoperative window of this study. Accordingly, the biomarker panel was limited to those reflective of acute systemic responses (histamine, CRP, ESR, IL-6, TNF-α), while acknowledging that delayed, localized reactions remain beyond the scope of this investigation. Furthermore, when individually analysing each patient with PMMA bone cement, it was clear to us that there was no single case when the histamine levels did not increase between the two measurements (before and after the surgical intervention). Amongst the few existing bodies of evidence, a study from 19945 reported no release of histamine in patients undergoing prosthetic replacement of the femoral head while using the methylmethacrylate bone cement. However, it should be noted that this study5 included a rather small number of participants (13 patients with methylmethacrylate bone cement), as well as following a different methodological approach with blood samples taken at 15, 30 and 60 min after the start of prosthesis implantation. The circa 24-hour waiting time after the surgical intervention until when the blood samples were taken in our study group should have provided enough time for the organism’s immunological and allergic response to develop. Importantly, histamine elevation does not establish PMMA allergy and should be interpreted in clinical context.

The very fact that a response was evidenced in all the assessed parameters in both cemented and cementless interventions should be considered as an important factor on the matter. Moreover, the significant postoperative elevation of IL-6 and TNF-α levels further confirms the activation of organisms’ systemic immune response. These cytokines in particular do play central roles in the acute inflammation process and have been suggested to be excellent screening tests for detecting infections of deep implants or other issues related to complications associated with joint replacements9. On the other hand, the observed correlation between pre- and post-operative levels of all the included parameters, and the particular moderate correlation of TNF-α, ESR, and CRP supports the usability of such markers in the monitoring of post-implantation potential adverse reactions. This is typically supported by the sharp elevation in ESR and CRP pre-and post-intervention. It should be noted that the postoperative elevation of the CRP levels has also been shown previously15, though in a shoulder arthroplasty study where they were shown to reach the peak by day 2 or 3 and continue declining gradually until day seven.

Another notable finding was the ESR and CRP values being significantly higher in the PMMA group, particularly in females, hinting at possible biological sex-related variations in the immunological responses. While these findings align with previous suggestions on biological sex being an important factor influencing immune systems response to different antigens16, it seems to keep going in the opposite direction towards those reporting females to have stronger innate and adaptive immune responses in comparison to males17. In the context of our findings are also the outcomes from a recent study conducted by Grant S and colleagues18 where they addressed the sex-related differences in the form of post-THA complications. In this study18, females showed higher rates of major surgical complications, though no further investigation of the implications of inflammatory response into the matter was provided. Other studies have also emphasized how belonging to the female biological sex is an independent risk factor for generally worse surgical outcomes including readmission, reoperation, and wound infection after THA19–21.

Even though conducting this study to the best of our knowledge and capabilities, we are aware that certain limitations prevail. First and foremost, should be the rather small number of participants included notwithstanding the specificity of the intervention study. Then, the fact that this study is a single-country cohort while conducted within one particular and rather homogeneous population is another factor of concern for the generalizability of our findings. Therefore, the derived data should be taken with caution until more similar studies (hopefully with a higher number of participants) are provided. Furthermore, if available, a thorough analysis of socio-economic and demographic data from the study participants could have helped better understand the potential influence of other external factors on the outcomes. Moreover, while significant differences in inflammatory markers were observed, the clinical relevance in terms of long-term prosthesis performance and patient outcomes remains to be further evaluated through longitudinal follow-up. Ultimately, the most significant strength of this study is the inclusion of participants from a lower socio-economic background and a developing country context. This provides invaluable insights that, in conjunction with data from diverse populations, contribute to the global understanding of this issue and underscore the critical need for region-specific interventions.

Finally, findings from this study are important in the better understanding of how PMMA bone cement implantation in the femoral component triggers the immunologic and allergic responses, particularly within the female biological sex. The implications could be multidimensional involving other musculoskeletal conditions like sarcopenia, a health condition and a growing health-related concern in ageing populations22. However, further research with higher numbers of participants from different external exposures (e.g. socio-demographic determinants) is required to confirm this trend and better understand it.

Notwithstanding the great effort given by the authors to conduct this study to the best of our knowledge and capabilities, certain limitations prevail. One limitation is that no a priori sample size or power calculation was performed. The number of participants was determined by the pool of eligible and consenting patients, and as such, the results should be interpreted cautiously and validated in larger, prospectively powered studies. Another limitation is the fact that we could not adjust for potential confounders such as smoking status, alcohol use, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, diabetes, or other comorbidities that may influence perioperative inflammatory responses. In this context, future studies should collect and adjust for these variables as well. The potential for selection bias in fixation type might present another limitation as well, since the final decision was subject to intra-operative surgeon judgment, and osteoporosis was assessed radiographically rather than by DXA. However, this decision was based mainly due to the unavailability of DXA technology. The lack of systematic recording of operative time, anaesthesia type, and postoperative medications can emerge as other limitations worth mentioning, all of which known to influence systemic inflammation. In particular, longer operative times associated with cementation may contribute to elevated CRP levels, and this must be considered as a potential source of bias. Finally, this study didn’t assess additional biomarkers such as vitamin D, HbA1c, or measures of insulin resistance, which may influence systemic inflammation and could have contributed to inter-individual variability. Therefore, future studies should consider including these biomarkers in their investigations.

Conclusion

Findings from this study demonstrate that the application of PMMA bone cement in total hip arthroplasty is associated with a significantly higher inflammatory response comparing to uncemented procedures. The elevated levels of histamine, IL-6, TNF-α, CRP, and ESR observed particularly among female patients, suggest for a possible material-induced immunological response and sex-related sensitivity. These results emphasize the importance of considering the biomaterial-induced inflammation in both the surgical decision-making and postoperative monitoring. By shedding light on these risks, the research aims to prompt more cautious monitoring and proactive management strategies during the postoperative period, ultimately improving patient outcomes and ensuring safer, more personalized care for individuals receiving joint prostheses. Nonetheless, multicentre research with larger cohorts is warranted to evaluate their clinical relevance and potential impact on the outcomes.

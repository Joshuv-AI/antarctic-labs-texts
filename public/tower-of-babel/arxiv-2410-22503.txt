Diffusive Expansion of the Boltzmann equation for the flow past an obstacle
Yan Guo, Junhwa Jung

Abstract
The exterior domain problem is essential in fluid and kinetic equations. In this paper, we establish the validity of the diffusive expansion for the Boltzmann equations to the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system up to the critical time in an exterior domain with non-zero passing flow. We apply the L 3 − L 6 L^{3}-L^{6} framework to the unbounded domain in this paper.

1
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to establish the incompressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier expansion from the unsteady Boltzmann equation with diffusive boundary conditions in the exterior domain. The rescaled Boltzmann equation with small Knudsen and Mach numbers is given by the following equation

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Q(F,F),\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1)

\displaystyle F|_{\gamma_{-}}=\mathscr{P}^{w}_{\gamma}(F),

(2)

\displaystyle F(t,x,v)|_{t=0}=F_{0}(x,v)\geq 0,\quad\text{on }\Omega^{c}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(3)

Where,
F(t,x,v)
is the distribution for particles at time
t\geq 0
, position
x
and velocity
v
. The Boltzmann collision operator
Q
can be written as

\displaystyle Q(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q^{+}(f,g)-Q^{-}(f,g),

(4)

\displaystyle Q^{+}(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbf{S}^{2}}B(\omega,{v-u})f(v^{\prime})g(u^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}u,

(5)

\displaystyle Q^{-}(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(v)\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbf{S}^{2}}B(\omega,{v-u})g(u)\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}u,

(6)

where,
v^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime}
defined by

\displaystyle v^{\prime}=v-\omega(v-u)\cdot\omega,\quad u^{\prime}=u+\omega(v-u)\cdot\omega,

and
B(\omega,{v-u})
is the cross section for hard potentials with Grad’s angular cut off, so that
\int_{\{\left\lvert\omega\right\rvert=1\}}B(\omega,V)\mathrm{d}\omega\lesssim\left\lvert V\right\rvert^{\theta}
for
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, depending on the interaction potential.

Throughout this paper,
\Omega=\{x:\xi(x)<0\}
is a general bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We denote its boundary as
\partial\Omega=\{x:\xi(x)=0\}
, where
\xi(x)
is a
C^{2}
function. We assume
\nabla\xi(x)\neq 0
on
\partial\Omega
. The inner normal vector at
\partial\Omega
is given by

\displaystyle n(x)=-\frac{\nabla\xi(x)}{\left\lvert\nabla\xi(x)\right\rvert}

and it can be extended smoothly near
\partial\Omega
. Let
\gamma=\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}=\gamma_{+}\cup\gamma_{0}\cup\gamma_{-}
with

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}=\left\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v\gtrless 0\right\},\quad\gamma_{0}=\left\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:n(x)\cdot v=0\right\}

(7)

The interaction of the gas with the boundary
\partial\Omega
is given by the diffusive boundary condition, defined as follows:

M_{\rho,u,T}:=\frac{\rho}{(2\pi T)^{\frac{3}{2}}}exp\left[-\frac{\left\lvert v-u\right\rvert^{2}}{2T}\right],

be the local Maxwellian with density
\rho
, mean velocity
u
, and temperature
T

and

\mu=M_{1,0,1}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}exp\left[-\frac{\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}}{2}\right].

On the boundary,
F
satisfies the diffusive reflection condition, defined as

F(t,x,v)=\mathscr{P}^{w}_{\gamma}(F)(t,x,v)\quad\text{on }\gamma_{-},

where

\mathscr{P}^{w}_{\gamma}(F)(t,x,v):=M^{w}(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,w)(n(x)\cdot w)\mathrm{d}w,

with wall Maxwellian defined as

M^{w}=\sqrt{2\pi}\mu,\quad\int_{\{v\cdot n\gtrless 0\}}M^{w}(v)\left\lvert n\cdot v\right\rvert\mathrm{d}v=1.

Since the domain is the complement of a compact domain, we need to specify the conditions at infinity.
Because we study the problem in the small Mach number
regime, we assume that the velocity at infinity is of order
\varepsilon
. In other words, we fix a constant vector
\mathbf{u}
denoting,

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{u}}:=v-\varepsilon\mathbf{u},\quad\mu_{\mathbf{u}}(v):=\mu(v_{\mathbf{u}})=M_{1,\varepsilon\mathbf{u},1},

(8)

we assume in a suitable sense

\lim_{\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to\infty}F(x,v)=\mu_{\mathbf{u}}(v).

To derive the hydrodynamic limit let’s think about Hilbert expansion near
\mu_{\mathbf{u}}
which gives as follows:

\displaystyle F=\mu_{\mathbf{u}}+\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}f_{n}.

(9)

To determine the coefficient of
f_{1},\cdots f_{n},\cdots
in such expansion, we plug the formal expansion (
9
) into (
1
):

\displaystyle(\varepsilon\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}f_{n}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}}Q(\mu_{\mathbf{u}}+\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}f_{n},\mu_{\mathbf{u}}+\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}f_{n}).

(10)

To expand right hand side, we define
L_{\mathbf{u}}
, the linearlized Boltzmann operator as

\displaystyle L_{\mathbf{u}}f:=-\mu_{\mathbf{u}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}[Q(\mu_{\mathbf{u}},\mu_{\mathbf{u}}^{\frac{1}{2}}f)+Q(\mu_{\mathbf{u}}^{\frac{1}{2}}f,\mu_{\mathbf{u}})]:=\nu_{\mathfrak{u}}f-Kf,

(11)

where
\nu_{\mathfrak{u}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\{\left\lvert\omega\right\rvert=1\}}B(v_{\mathbf{u}}-v^{\prime},\omega)\mu(v_{\mathfrak{u}})\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}\mathrm{d}\omega
is
0\leq\nu_{0}\left\lvert v_{\mathbf{u}}\right\rvert^{\theta}\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{u}}\leq\nu_{1}\left\lvert v_{\mathbf{u}}\right\rvert^{\theta}
and
K
is a compact operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
.
Define the nonlinear collision operator
\Gamma
as

\displaystyle\Gamma(f,g)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}}Q(\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}f,\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}g).

(12)

Comparing the powers of
\varepsilon
on both sides of the equation (
10
), we obtain the following equations:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L_{\mathbf{u}}f_{1},

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L_{\mathbf{u}}f_{2}+\Gamma(f_{1},f_{1}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}+v_{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}+{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L_{\mathbf{u}}f_{3}+\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})+\Gamma(f_{2},f_{1}),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{n}+v_{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{n+1}+{\mathbf{u}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-L_{\mathbf{u}}f_{n+2}+\sum_{i+j=n+2,i,j\geq 1}\Gamma(f_{i},f_{j}).

From the above relation, we can obtain the fluid equations. We can easily check that
L_{\mathbf{u}}
is an operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
whose kernel is

\displaystyle\ker L_{\mathbf{u}}=\text{span}\{1,v_{\mathbf{u}},\left\lvert v_{\mathbf{u}}\right\rvert^{2}\}\sqrt{\mu_{\mathfrak{u}}}.

(13)

Let
\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{u}}
be the orthogonal projection on
\ker L_{\mathbf{u}}
.

Define
F
as a small perturbation of
\mu_{\mathbf{u}}
, which satisfies the following relation:

\displaystyle F=\mu_{\mathbf{u}}+\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}}(\varepsilon f_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}R),

(14)

where
R
is the remainder.
The functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are constructed by the solution of the fluid equation defined by the following:

\displaystyle f_{1}=u\cdot v_{\mathbf{u}}\sqrt{\mu_{\mathbf{u}}},

(15)

and

\displaystyle f_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}\mathscr{A}_{ij}\left[\partial_{x_{i}}u_{j}+\partial_{x_{j}}u_{i}\right]+L_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}\left[\Gamma\left(f_{1},f_{1}\right)\right],

(16)

where
\mathscr{A}_{ij}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{ij}=-L_{\mathfrak{u}}^{-1}\left(\sqrt{\mu}(v_{\mathbf{u},i}v_{\mathbf{u},j}-\frac{\left\lvert v_{\mathbf{u}}\right\rvert^{2}}{3}\delta_{ij})\right),

(17)

and
u+\mathfrak{u}
is a solution of the Navier-Stokes equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+(u+\mathfrak{u})\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\kappa\Delta u,\quad u=-\mathfrak{u}\text{ on }\Omega^{c},\quad u\to 0\text{ as }\left\lvert x\right\rvert\to\infty.

(18)

According to Section
3.1
, the
Navier-Stokes equation has a unique short-time solution if the initial data
u_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
. To show the boundedness of
R
, we need to justify the hydrodynamic limit to the Navier-Stokes equation. The equation for
R
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{t}R+v\cdot\nabla_{x}R+\varepsilon^{-1}L_{\mathfrak{u}}(R)=h+\tilde{L}(R)+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Gamma(R,R),

(19)

where
\tilde{L}(R)=\Gamma(f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2},R)+\Gamma(R,f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2})
and

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon^{1/2}\partial_{t}(f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2})-\varepsilon^{-1/2}v\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2})-\varepsilon^{-3/2}L_{\mathfrak{u}}(f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2})+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\Gamma(f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2},f_{1}+\varepsilon f_{2}).

Remark 3
.

Note that we can set
T_{0}
to be any large number. For small initial data, it is known that the Navier-Stokes equation has a global solution. In this case, we can guarantee that the limiting problem holds for any time. For large initial data, the global existence of the Navier-Stokes equation is a well-known open problem. However, we can still prove that the hydrodynamic limit holds at least for the lifespan of the fluid solution.

Remark 4
.

\partial_{t}R_{0}
doesn’t make sense in general. However, it can be obtain from the equation by
\partial_{t}R_{0}=-v\cdot\nabla_{x}R_{0}-\varepsilon^{-1}L_{\mathfrak{u}}(R_{0})+h+\tilde{L}(R_{0})+\varepsilon^{1/2}\Gamma(R_{0},R_{0})
.

Remark 5
.

We remark that
\varepsilon^{1/2}R
is of higher order in
L^{p}
for
2\leq p\leq 6
. On the other hand,
\varepsilon^{1/2}R
can be large enough in

We can obtain the incompressible Navier-Stokes equation from the Boltzmann equation when both the Mach number and Knudsen number go to zero. In the small Mach number regime, it is natural to consider perturbations near the absolute Maxwellian.

This fact was observed by Sone during the formal expansion for the steady solution (

]

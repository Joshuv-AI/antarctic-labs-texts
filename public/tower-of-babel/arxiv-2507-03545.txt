Communication Efficient, Differentially Private Distributed Optimization using Correlation-Aware Sketching
Julien Nicolas Mohamed Maouche, Sonia Ben Mokhtar, Mark Coates

Abstract
Federated learning with differential privacy suffers from two major costs: each client must transmit d d -dimensional gradients every round, and the magnitude of DP noise grows with d d . Yet empirical studies show that gradient updates exhibit strong temporal correlations and lie in a k k -dimensional subspace with k ≪ d k\ll d . Motivated by this, we introduce DOME, a decentralized DP optimization framework in which each client maintains a compact sketch to project gradients into ℝ k \mathbb{R}^{k} before privatization and Secure Aggregation. This reduces per-round communication from order d d to order k k and moves towards a gradient approximation mean-squared error of σ 2 ​ k \sigma^{2}k . To allow the sketch to span new directions and prevent it from collapsing onto historical gradients, we augment it with random probes orthogonal to historical directions. We prove that our overall protocol satisfies ( ϵ , δ ) (\epsilon,\delta) -Differential Privacy.



1
Introduction

Recent advances in deep learning have been driven by training large-scale neural networks on vast amounts of user-generated data using adaptive optimizers such as Adam
(
Kingma 2014
;
Vaswani et al. 2017
;
Kaplan et al. 2020
)
. However, reliance on sensitive data raises serious privacy concerns: model updates or inference queries can inadvertently reveal personal information
(
Fredrikson et al. 2015
;
Shokri et al. 2017
)
. Federated learning partially addresses this concern by keeping data on users’ devices and only exchanging gradient information
(
McMahan et al. 2017
)
, yet gradients themselves can leak private details
(
Zhu et al. 2019
)
and incur prohibitive communication costs for high-dimensional models
(
McMahan et al. 2017
)
. Differential privacy provides formal protection guarantees by adding noise during the optimization procedure
(
Abadi et al. 2016
)
, but this comes at a steep cost in model utility
(
Jayaraman and Evans 2019
)
.

Insights from the optimization dynamics of deep networks suggest a path forward. The lottery ticket hypothesis demonstrates that overparameterized models contain sparse “winning tickets” capable of matching full-model performance
(
Frankle and Carbin 2018
)
. Moreover, empirical studies show that the sequence of gradient updates lies in a low-dimensional subspace and exhibits strong temporal correlation
(
Azam et al. 2021
;
Gur-Ari et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2022
)
. Non-private federated methods have exploited these correlations by reusing previously transmitted gradients when new updates lie close in value, communicating only compact indices
(
Azam et al. 2021
)
. In centralized settings, public data can be used to estimate the underlying gradient subspace, enabling DP-SGD to operate within this reduced space and thereby lower the noise scale
(
Zhou et al. 2020
)
. Private federated approaches have further combined random sketching with secure aggregation to project gradients into low-dimensional random subspaces under DP constraints
(
Chen et al. 2022
)
, while others decompose each gradient into a historical mean plus an orthogonal component to minimize the noise required for a given privacy budget
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
.

We propose a novel sketch-based learning framework that that continuously learns and updates a compact linear sketch tailored to span both the historical gradient sequence and additional random directions, exploiting their low-rank structure and temporal correlation in a single structure. By injecting differential-privacy noise into the sketched gradients instead of the full-dimensional updates, our method scales the noise magnitude with the sketch dimension rather than the original model dimension. We further extend Adam to be compatible with sketch-based, decentralized and privacy-preserving protocol. Our approach is compatible with polylogarithmic-cost Secure Aggregation
(
Bell et al. 2020
)
, enabling practical deployment in privacy-sensitive federated environments.

Contributions:

We introduce
DOME
, a decentralized differentially private optimization framework that leverages adaptive sketching and moment estimation while ensuring strong privacy guarantees. Our main contributions are:

•

Decentralized Optimization with Adam:
We design a federated optimization approach that integrates
Secure Aggregation (SecAgg)
to enable privacy-preserving communication and scalable distributed learning. Unlike methods that perform variance reduction
within each client
, our approach applies
global variance reduction across clients
, leading to more stable first- and second-moment estimates. We eliminate the need for explicit
sampling hyperparameters
by adopting a
full-pass approach
, where each example is processed exactly once per epoch. This aligns with practical training scenarios and simplifies privacy accounting.

•

Communication-efficient, differentially private decentralized optimization:
We propose a new method to leverage the low-rank structure and correlation between gradients in the optimization process. This method allows us to sketch gradients at each step, and refine the sketch based on the new computed gradients. Sketched gradients are of lower dimensions, reducing communication cost and the scale of the DP noise.

Background

2.1
Matrix decomposition

Eigenvalue Decomposition.

Let
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a real-valued positive semi-definite matrix, where
n
is a positive integer. The eigenvalue decomposition of
\bm{A}
is given by
\bm{A}=\bm{U}\bm{\Lambda}\bm{U}^{\top},
where
\bm{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a matrix of eigenvectors and
\bm{\Lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a diagonal matrix containing corresponding eigenvalues.

QR Decomposition.

We will use the matrix QR decomposition, obtained using the Gram-Schmidt procedure. Given a matrix
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
, the QR decomposition factorizes it as
\bm{X}=\bm{Q}\bm{R}
, where
\bm{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}
is an orthonormal matrix (
i.e.
,
\bm{Q}^{\top}\bm{Q}=\bm{I}
) and
\bm{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times p}
is an upper triangular matrix.

Gaussian Random Matrices.

We denote by
\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^{2})^{n\times p}
a
(n\times p)
random matrix where each element is an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variable according to a Gaussian distribution with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
.

2.2
Differential Privacy

A randomized mechanism
\mathcal{M}
satisfies
(\epsilon,\delta)
-Differential Privacy if for every pair of adjacent datasets
X
and
Y
(i.e., datasets differing in at most one element) and for any measurable set
A
in the output space of
\mathcal{M}
, the following inequality holds:

\Pr[\mathcal{M}(X)\in A]\leq e^{\epsilon}\,\Pr[\mathcal{M}(Y)\in A]+\delta,

(1)

where
\epsilon
is a small positive parameter that quantifies the privacy loss of the mechanism, and
\delta
is a small probability that allows for a small possibility of the privacy guarantee failing.

To construct a Differentially Private mechanism from a function
f
, it is necessary to bound the
\ell_{2}
-sensitivity of
f
, denoted by
\Delta_{2}
. It is formally defined as:

\Delta_{2}=\max_{X\sim Y}\|g(X)-g(Y)\|_{2},

(2)

where
X\sim Y
signifies that
X
and
Y
are adjacent datasets.

2.3
Secure Aggregation

Let
S=\{a_{i},i\in\mathcal{U}\}
be a set of private values, where
a_{i}
is a private value held locally by a user (or node) indexed by
i
and
\mathcal{U}
is a set of user indices participating in the Secure Aggregation protocol. We denote by
A=SecAgg(S)
the Secure Aggregation
(
Bell et al. 2020
)
of private values in
S
, locally held by nodes. This protocol allows us to compute
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}}a_{i}
over secure channels without communicating in clear any of the values in
S
, i.e.:

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=SecAgg(S)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}}a_{i}.

(3)

The protocol runs in poly-logarithmic communication and computation costs for the clients (See Section 4.3 in
(
Bell et al. 2020
)
).

3
Method

Problem Setting.

We first consider a standard
(
Chen et al. 2022
)
privacy-preserving federated optimization framework with
N
clients and one server, ensuring distributed
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP using the honest-but-curious threat model. In this framework, training occurs in
T
epochs, each divided into communication rounds. At the start of round
t
, the server samples without replacement a set
\mathcal{U}_{t}\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
of
B
clients. Each selected client
i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}
draws a fresh example
x_{i}
from its local dataset
\mathcal{D}_{i}
(so that every example is seen exactly once per epoch) and computes the gradient

g_{i}=\nabla\ell(\theta_{t-1};x_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

To bound the gradient sensitivity with respect to one client and ensure Differential Privacy, this gradient is clipped to

\bar{g}_{i}=g_{i}\min\bigl(1,\;C/\|g_{i}\|_{2}\bigr),

where
C
is a fixed clipping threshold. Each client then perturbs its clipped gradient with Gaussian noise
and releases

\tilde{g}_{i}=\bar{g}_{i}+z_{i},\text{ where }z_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d}),

where
\sigma^{2}
is chosen (see Section
3.4
) to satisfy
(\epsilon,\delta)
-Differential Privacy.

Instead of directly communicating noisy gradients, clients engage in a Secure Aggregation protocol so that the server only recovers

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}\tilde{g}_{i}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}\bar{g}_{i}\;+\;\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}z_{i},

never seeing any individual
\tilde{g}_{i}
in the clear. Since
(\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}z_{i})\sim N(0,B\sigma^{2}I_{d})
, the server obtains the batch gradient

g_{\mathrm{agg}}=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}\tilde{g}_{i}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{U}_{t}}\bar{g}_{i}\;+\;z,\quad z\sim\mathcal{N}\!\bigl(0,\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{B}I_{d}\bigr),

(4)

which can be used to update optimizer statistics and then perform the optimization step. The updated model parameters are then sent back to the clients.

Motivation.

The communication cost in federated learning is linear on the potentially large network dimension
d
. However, recent works
(
Gur-Ari et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2022
)
empirically demonstrate that the sequence of gradients
\{\bm{g}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
obtained during optimization in fact evolves within a low-dimensional linear subspace of the full gradient space, of dimension
k\ll d
. Projecting each gradient onto this
k
-dimensional subspace and communicating only coordinates within that subspace can therefore substantially reduce communication cost
(
Li et al. 2022
)
, from
O(d)
to
O(k)
.

Moreover, we show in Lemma
3.1
that the mean squared error between privatized and original gradients grows linearly with their ambient dimension
d
. It also shows that when the gradients lie in a
k
-dimensional subspace and one has access to a projector
\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
, applying the privacy mechanism in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and then mapping the result back to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can dramatically reduce the Mean Squared Error introduced by the DP noise.

Lemma 3.1

(Impact of Ambient Dimension)
.

Suppose that every gradient
\bm{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
produced during optimization lies in a given
k
-dimensional subspace, i.e. there exists a matrix
\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
(with
k\ll d
) such that

\bm{g}\;=\;\bm{P}\,\bm{P}^{\top}\,\bm{g}\,.

Assume moreover that
\|\bm{g}\|_{2}\leq C
and define

\sigma^{2}\;=\;\frac{2\,C^{2}\;\ln(1.25/\delta)}{\epsilon^{2}}\,.

Then, releasing
\bm{g}^{p}
or
\widetilde{\bm{g}}^{p}
is
(\epsilon,\delta)
-Differentially Private, with:

\bm{g}^{p}\;=\;\bm{g}\;+\;\bm{z}_{1},\quad\bm{z}_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d}),\qquad\widetilde{\bm{g}}^{p}\;=\;\bm{P}\bigl(\bm{P}^{\top}\bm{g}+\bm{z}_{2}\bigr),\quad\bm{z}_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{k}).

Moreover, their mean-squared errors satisfy

\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\bm{g}-\bm{g}^{p}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;\sigma^{2}\,d,\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigl\|\bm{g}-\widetilde{\bm{g}}^{p}\bigr\|_{2}^{2}\;=\;\sigma^{2}\,k.

In practice, the gradient subspace is not know before optimization. The question of estimating
\bm{P}
then arises, leading us to the next subsection.

3.1
Correlation-Aware Sketching

Existing privacy-preserving federated learning methods have relied on random, proxy or heuristic subspace projections. For example,
Chen et al. 2022
use data-independent random subspaces,
Zhou et al. 2020
compute projectors from public data at each step while and
Liu et al. 2024
decompose each gradient into a historical mean plus an orthogonal residual. In contrast, we exploit the strong temporal correlation of the
sequence
of privatized gradients by maintaining a compact sketch that both tracks and senses the evolving gradient subspace.

Let
\bm{G}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times t}
be the matrix representing previous gradients at iteration
t+1
, i.e.
\bm{G}_{t}=\bigl[\bm{g}_{0}|...|\bm{g}_{t}\bigr]
, where
{}^{\prime\prime}|^{\prime\prime}
represents vector concatenation and
\bm{g}_{t}
represent a batch gradient at iteration
t
.

As gradients are highly correlated across iterations, we can decompose
\bm{g}_{t+1}
as

\bm{g}_{t+1}=(\sum_{i}^{t}\alpha_{i}\tilde{\bm{g}}_{i})+\bar{\bm{g}}_{t+1},

(5)

where
(\sum_{i}^{t}\alpha_{i}\tilde{\bm{g}}_{i})
represents a linear combination of past estimations of gradients with linear weights
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\bar{\bm{g}}_{t+1}
denotes the gradient part lying out of the subspace of previously estimated gradients.

We can note that previous gradients are themselves highly correlated. Let us consider
\bm{G}_{t}\bm{G}_{t}^{\top}=\bm{U}_{t}\bm{\Lambda}_{t}\bm{U}_{t}^{\top}
as the eigenvalue decomposition of the (non normalized) covariance matrix between gradients. Then, the linear combination of past gradients lie within the range of
\bm{U}_{t}
and we can expect most of the action of
\bm{U}_{t}
to lie within few directions
(
Li et al. 2022
)
. If we consider the top-
K
most important eigenvectors
\bm{U}_{t}^{K}
, we can then approximate

\bm{g}_{t+1}\approx(\sum_{i}^{K}\gamma_{i}{\bm{u}}_{i}^{t})+\bar{\bm{g}}_{t+1},

(6)

where
\gamma_{k}
are linear weights and
{\bm{u}}_{i}^{t}
represent the
i^{th}
gradient direction (
i^{th}
eigenvector of
\bm{G}_{t}\bm{G}_{t}^{\top}
).

As training progresses, we expect gradients to become more and more correlated, so that
\bar{\bm{g}}_{t+1}
becomes negligible compared to
\bm{g}_{t+1}
. However, at the beginning of the training,
\bm{G}_{t}\bm{G}_{t}^{\top}
is unknown and directly communicating
\bar{\bm{g}}_{t+1}
is costly.

We then resort to random projections to approximate
\bar{\bm{g}}_{t+1}
with
R
dimensions as

\bm{g}_{t+1}\approx(\sum_{i}^{K}\gamma_{i}{\bm{u}}_{i}^{t})+(\sum_{r}^{R}\gamma_{r}\bm{\omega}_{r}),

(7)

where
\gamma_{r}
is the linear weight associated to the normalized Gaussian measurement direction
\bm{\omega}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

This can be reformulated as

\bm{g}_{t+1}\approx\bm{S}_{t}\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{g}_{t+1},

(8)

where
\bm{S}_{t}
is a sketching matrix with orthonormal columns formed by orthonormalization of

\bm{S}_{t}^{\prime}=[\;\bm{U}_{t}^{K}\;|\;\bm{\Omega}_{t}\;],

(9)

where
\bm{\Omega}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)^{d\times R}
is a random Gaussian matrix lying in the orthogonal space of
\bm{U}_{t}^{K}
. Communicating
\bm{S}_{t}^{\top}\bm{g}_{t+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{K+R}
would have complexity
O(K+R)
instead of
O(d)
for the full gradient.

We now elaborate on the four steps of the sketching procedure intertwined in the already established federated learning protocol: preprocessing, local sketching, decompression, and efficient sketch updating.

Preprocessing: Mean Removal and Normalization.

To isolate novel information, we first remove the running mean of past gradients from the current gradient, as is typically done in classical mean–removal in Compressed Sensing
(
Eldar and Kutyniok 2012
)
.

Local Sketching, Privatization, and Communication.

Each client projects its preprocessed gradient using the current sketching matrix, clips its norm to enforce sensitivity, adds Gaussian noise for differential privacy, and transmits the noisy sketch via Secure Aggregation.

Decompression and model updating at the Server.

After Secure Aggregation, the server inverts the sketching transformation and pre-processing to recover an approximation of the aggregated gradient.

Sketch Updating via Streaming PCA with Random Probing.

We now want to efficiently update the current sketch based on the new sensed gradient so that the refined sketch approximately spans the gradients at the next iteration.

Following Equation (
9
), we first need to compute the left singular vectors
\bm{U}_{t}
of the matrix of previous gradients at step
t
, i.e.
\bm{G}_{t}
. Computing a full SVD using directly
\bm{G}_{t}
would be costly. By noting that updating
\bm{U}_{t-1}
to
\bm{U}_{t}
is a special case of streaming principal component analysis, we can use the history streaming PCA method
(
Yang et al. 2018
)
to efficiently compute
\bm{U}_{t}
without ever storing the full gradient history nor recomputing completely the singular vectors at each step.

At round
t-1
, let
\bm{G}_{t-1}\bm{G}_{t-1}^{\top}=\bm{U}_{t-1}^{K}\bm{\Lambda}_{t-1}(\bm{U}_{t-1}^{K})^{\top}
represent the truncated (top-
K
) eigenvalue decomposition of the past gradients correlation matrix, where
\bm{U}_{t-1}^{K}\in
denotes the eigenvector matrix and
\bm{\Lambda}_{t-1}
the eigenvalue matrix. Upon approximating of the batch gradient
\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}
, the server can then compute the streaming PCA
(
Yang et al. 2018
)
update

\bm{Y}_{t}=\bm{U}_{t-1}\,\bm{\Lambda}_{t-1}\;+\;\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}\,\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}^{\top}\,\bm{U}_{t-1},

(10)

and orthogonalize
\bm{Y}_{t}
via QR to obtain
\bm{U}_{t}
and
\bm{\Lambda}_{t}
.

In our context,
\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}
lies in the span of the previous sketches.
As
\bm{U}_{t}
is updated using
\hat{\bm{g}}_{t}
, if we used
\bm{U}_{t}
directly as the next sketch without additional probing, the sketch would collapse onto historical directions. At each step, we then truncate
\bm{U}_{t}
to its top
r
components (retaining a proportion
q
of spectral energy) and append
k-r
orthogonal Gaussian probes, which are re-orthonormalized to form the updated sketching matrix (See Algorithm
2
). The result is a compact sketch that adapts to subspace drift and continuously explores emerging gradient directions.

3.3
Debiasing the second moment

We need to add properly scaled Gaussian noise to our gradients to satisfy Differential Privacy. We add it to sketched gradients and then linearly transform them back so that we can compute moments and perform gradient descent.

Naive noise addition adds bias to the moments of interest. We however know the statistics of the noise added at each step and can relate biased statistics to unbiased ones using Lemma
3.2
. We present its proof in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\bm{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the mean of gradients for a batch of samples, let
\bm{z}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,v^{2})^{d}
be a random Gaussian vector and let
\bm{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times m}
be a sketching matrix. Then, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\bm{S}\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{g})^{\odot 2}]=\mathbb{E}\bigl[(\bm{S}(\bm{S}^{\top}\bm{g}+\bm{z}))^{\odot 2}\bigr]-v^{2}Diag(\bm{S}\bm{S}^{\top}).

(11)

If we ignore the bias introduced by clipping, Lemma
3.2
can then be used in Algorithm
1
to debias
\tilde{\bm{v}}_{t}
(line 19).

Privacy of DOME

Our algorithm processes every data point exactly once per epoch, adopting a full-pass training strategy that avoids the stochastic sampling challenges of DP-SGD. By integrating a lightweight Secure Aggregation protocol, we achieve distributed differential privacy, which ensures central DP guarantees while keeping raw data local.

4
Related Work

Communication-efficient Federated Learning

To reduce communication overhead under differential privacy (DP), several works employ random sketching combined with Secure Aggregation to compress client updates before noise addition
(
Chen et al. 2022
)
, reducing communication cost while degrading utility. Weight quantization
(
Zhou et al. 2017
)
or pruning
(
Liu et al. 2018
)
can be combined with sketching techniques to further shrink the bit-width of models, often after full training. Communicating weights low-rank decomposition
(
Hyeon-Woo et al. 2021
)
leveraging local inter-parameter correlation also has the potential to reduce communication, although it is not compatible with linear Secure Aggregation.

Correlation-aware Federated Learning

A line of research recycles information across successive rounds by transmitting only the difference or similarity between the current gradient and a previous reference, rather than the full update
(
Azam et al. 2021
)
in non-private federated learning. In centralized DP optimization, public-data–based projectors (via SVD) have been used to constrain updates to low-dimensional subspaces, reducing the impact of DP noise
(
Zhou et al. 2020
)
. Similarly, delayed preconditioning techniques accumulate noisy gradient statistics over multiple steps to get better estimation of weight preconditioners
(
Li et al. 2023
)
, and DP decomposition–reconstruction schemes split updates into mean and orthogonal components to optimize noise allocation
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
.

Adaptive and Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Optimization

Adaptive optimizers with DP guarantees have been explored in centralized settings:
Tang et al. 2024
propose DP-AdamBC, a DP version of Adam with corrected second-moment bias when gradients are biased with privacy noise. Federated extensions of adaptive methods improve convergence in non-private settings
(
Reddi et al. 2021
)
, while recent decentralized adaptive algorithms accelerate learning without privacy guarantees or global momentum synchronization
(
Huang and Zhao 2024
;
Chen et al. 2023
)
. As a result, no existing approach simultaneously offers differential privacy, federated communication, and bias-corrected adaptive updates.

5
Conclusion & Future Work

In federated learning under differential privacy, clients incur both high communication—sending
d
-dimensional gradients each round—and noise that scales with
d
. DOME addresses these challenges by maintaining an adaptive streaming PCA sketch onto a
k
-dimensional subspace and augmenting it with random Gaussian probes to capture emerging directions. Each client removes the running mean, projects into this small subspace, clips and privatizes there, then securely aggregates. This reduces communication from
O(d)
to
O(k)
and moves mean squared error due to DP noise toward
\sigma^{2}k
. At the server, decompressed updates are used to do DP bias corrected Adam steps. We prove that our overall protocol satisfies
(\epsilon,\delta)
-Differential Privacy.

Future work will explore benchmarking our approach on common benchmark, giving theoretical convergence proofs and integrating DP follow-the-regularized-leader techniques, establishing formal convergence guarantees in nonconvex settings, and designing a more principled, data-dependent clipping scheme.

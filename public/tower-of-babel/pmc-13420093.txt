Novel Composite Conduits for Long-Distance Peripheral Nerve Repair
Authors: Kaijie Cai, Yuwei Qiu, Jin Zhang
Published: 2026

Long-distance peripheral nerve injury (PNI) caused by trauma, sports injuries, or surgical intervention often leads to severe functional impairment and reduced quality of life. , Autologous nerve grafting remains the clinical gold standard for PNI repair, while its application is constrained by donor nerve scarcity, frequent mismatch in graft size, and inevitable requirement for secondary surgery. Currently, nerve tissue engineering scaffolds (NTESs) have been developed as alternative strategies for PNI repair. However, achieving effective nerve regeneration and functional recovery in long-distance PNI remains challenging, primarily due to the lack of adequate physicochemical stimulation and guidance during the regenerative process. To address these limitations, our group has recently developed a series of adaptive biomimetic neural scaffolds by integrating external physical fields and biomimetic physicochemical structures across multiple scales (Figure ).

Among these strategies, the introduction of physical stimulation enables dynamic and tunable regulation of the neural microenvironment. Zhang et al. integrated the stress field with the double-layer structure of bionic bamboo-oriented hollow tubes to develop a composite NTES with an oriented hollow-tube bilayer structure and favorable piezoelectric properties for nerve regeneration. The outer hydrogel layer exhibited sufficient protection to maintain structural integrity, and the interior fiber provided an oriented topological cue. Continuous movement at the implantation site generated in situ piezoelectric signals, which enhanced the proliferation and differentiation of seeded neuronal cells and significantly improved the efficiency of axon network formation. When coupled with the oriented architecture, the axonal growth length of dorsal root ganglion neurons was significantly higher than that in previously reported systems, representing a 3.1-fold increase compared to the mean regrowth length of the blank control group. In addition, based on the good degradation performance of poly­(l-lactic acid) (PLLA), a self-powered and electroconductive scaffold composed of gelatin methacryloyl (GelMA) hydrogel, PLLA nanofiber, and carbon nanotubes (CNTs) was designed for accelerating nerve regeneration. Such NTES exhibited an appropriate mechanical strength with approximately 10 kPa in the inner layer, closely matching stiffness requirements of native neural tissue. Moreover, mild electrical signals generated on the surface of PLLA nanofibers by physiological body movements were effectively transmitted to nerve fibers and Schwann cells (SCs) through conductive CNTs@GelMA hydrogel, thereby modulating cellular membrane potential and enhancing cell excitability. By providing endogenous piezoelectric stimulation and a conductive microenvironment, the scaffold significantly accelerated axonal growth, promoted remyelination, and ultimately improved nerve function recovery.

Nevertheless, endogenous stress field-induced piezoelectric stimulation remains constrained by limited controllability, making it difficult to maintain an optimal microenvironment throughout the regenerative process. In comparison, ultrasound and magnetic fields exhibit distinct advantages of being noninvasive, remote, and highly controllable. To this end, a composite neural scaffold responsive to ultrasonic field stimulation was constructed. The outer layer of the scaffold consists of a piezoelectric PLLA/bismuth ferrite electrospun membrane, while the interior is composed of a transparent hyaluronic acid hydrogel loaded with zinc ions based on dynamic metal coordination bonding. Under ultrasonic field stimulation, electrical signals are generated in situ, and Zn2+ exhibits a controllable and on-demand release behavior. In vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated that upon ultrasonic stimulation, the released Zn2+ promotes the polarization of macrophages from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory M2, thereby creating an immuno-inflammatory microenvironment conducive to neural regeneration. Ultimately, the in situ ultrasonic piezoelectric signals and the released Zn2+ synergistically promote the recovery of sensory function in rats. Besides, Zhang et al. constructed a superparamagnetic nanocomposite scaffold (Mag-gel), which could control extracellular vesicles (EVs) production on-demand from encapsulated cells via a wireless, tunable mechanical stimulus in the presence of an external rotating magnetic field (RMF). Upon switching ON/OFF of the RMF, the Mag-gel scaled up local SCS-derived EVs (SCs-EVs) production via inducing Ca2+ influx mediated by activating mechanosensitive PIEZO2 channels, which in turn promoted neurite outgrowth and enhanced interactions between SCs and neurons. Notably, the RMF stimulation also enriched SCs-EVs cargoes associated with axonal growth, angiogenesis, and immunomodulation, ultimately leading to optimized nerve repair in vivo.

Beyond external stimulation cues, PNI regeneration can be further enhanced by endowing NTESs with highly biomimetic physicochemical structures that actively regulate neural cell behavior and the local regenerative microenvironment. Among them, designing the hierarchical structure of scaffold materials to achieve temporal control of biochemical signals is an effective strategy. Zhang et al. fabricated a magnesium oxide/magnesium carbonate/polycaprolactone (MgO/MgCO3/PCL) multi-gradient electrospun nanofiber for PNI repair, in which high-concentration MgCO3 was encapsulated in the innermost layer of the scaffold, with MgO located in the outermost layer, yielding a multilayered gradient structure featuring a gradually declining Mg2+ concentration from interior to exterior. By rationally integrating rapidly degradable MgO and slowly degradable MgCO3 within a layer-by-layer architecture, the MgO/MgCO3/PCL fiber achieves sustained release of Mg2+ over a period of 8 weeks. Such spatiotemporally controlled Mg2+ release induced concentration-dependent SCs activation and repair-phenotype transition via the Wnt signaling pathway, thereby enhancing axonal regeneration, remyelination, and functional recovery. In addition to temporal regulation achieved by layer-by-layer architectures, spatial guidance is essential for directing long-range axonal regeneration. In this context, a multichannel 3D-printed scaffold was constructed with a multi-gradient structure that mimics the gray and white matter of the spinal cord. The outer shell consists of a mechanically robust GelMA-PEGDA hydrogel for structural support, while the inner parallel channels are functionalized with laminin-derived peptides to establish a beneficial bioactive microenvironment. Concurrently, siRNA-loaded liposomes were incorporated to silence PTEN and activate the Ephrin/Eph signaling pathway. This coordinated integration of extrinsic physical guidance and intrinsic regeneration activation enabled precise axonal projection. Results showed that the scaffold allows regenerated axons to extend long-distance and in parallel from the proximal to the distal segment in rats with 3 mm spinal cord injury.

Although the studies demonstrate that biomimetic multi-layered and multi-gradient structures can effectively promote nerve defect repair, these designs are largely confined to the macro- and mesoscale, and are insufficient to recapitulate the intrinsic molecular-level structural features of the neural extracellular matrix (ECM). To address this limitation, our group further extended the design strategy toward molecular-level chiral structures. Research has shown that the chiral configuration of materials significantly affects the functional status of immune cells by regulating receptor binding efficiency, signal pathways, and metabolic reprogramming. A chiral poly­(amino acid) hydrogel-based nerve conduit was fabricated, comprising an outer aligned electrospun poly­(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) membrane and an inner core of a hydrogel composed of chiral polylysine and polyglutamic acid. By modulating the chirality of polylysine, this conduit enables metabolic reprogramming of intracellular lipid metabolism, thereby effectively promoting neuronal differentiation and neurotrophic factor secretion. Flow cytometry and immunofluorescence staining demonstrated that chiral hydrogels activated the immune regulatory system, promoted macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype, established an anti-inflammatory microenvironment for neural repair, and enhanced axonal growth of rat pheochromocytoma 12 cells in vitro. Collectively, these findings indicated that such NTES synergistically leveraged biological and mechanical cues to promote peripheral nerve regeneration, offering a promising strategy for nerve repair.

Conclusion and Outlook

Despite the advancements in neural tissue engineering, the application of neural tissue engineering scaffolds in long-distance peripheral nerve injuries remain constrained by key factors. Firstly, most neural tissue engineering scaffolds exhibit poor controllability over their degradation rate, which fails to match that of nerve regeneration. Their degradation products tend to induce local ionic imbalance and inflammation, thereby disrupting the regenerative microenvironment and inhibiting nerve repair. Secondly, current neural tissue engineering scaffolds lack dynamic adaptive regulation, which prevents them from providing timely and stage-specific physicochemical signals for different repair phases and fails to enable dynamic adaptation to the regeneration process.

To overcome these limitations, future research should focus on developing novel neural tissue engineering scaffolds with precisely tailored properties. By designing material components and structures, synchronous matching between the degradation rate and the nerve regeneration rate can be achieved, and the safety of degradation byproducts can be optimized. Furthermore, scaffolds with regenerative stage-specific dynamic responses should be designed to provide precise and adaptive physicochemical guidance cues for different stages of nerve repair. Additionally, artificial intelligence-driven predictive models can be combined with nerve regeneration mechanisms, utilizing such models to conduct multidimensional predictions and real-time optimization of degradation kinetics, product release patterns, and dynamic microenvironment changes. This enables data-driven precise design of scaffold biomimetic physicochemical structures and spatiotemporal controllable regulation of external physical fields. Simultaneously, in vivo assessment systems that closely mimic clinical scenarios should be established and validated in large animal models such as beagles and primates to provide support for clinical translation.

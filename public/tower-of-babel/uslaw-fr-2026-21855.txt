Airworthiness Directives; Airbus Helicopters Deutschland GmbH 
(AHD) Helicopters

AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), DOT.

ACTION: Final rule.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The FAA is adopting a new airworthiness directive (AD) for 
certain Airbus Helicopters Deutschland GmbH (AHD) Model EC135P1, 
EC135P2, EC135P2+, EC135P3, EC135T1, EC135T2, EC135T2+, EC135T3, 
EC635T2+, MBB-BK 117 C-2, and MBB-BK 117 D-2 helicopters. This AD was 
prompted by reports of cracks on the cable drum of the rescue hoist 
assembly. This AD requires repetitively inspecting the cable drum of 
certain rescue hoist assemblies for cracks and, depending on the 
results, replacing or repairing the cable drum of the rescue hoist 
assembly. This AD also prohibits the operation and the installation of 
an affected rescue hoist assembly on a helicopter, unless certain 
requirements are met. The FAA is issuing this AD to address the unsafe 
condition on these products.

DATES: This AD is effective December 1, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

Background

    The FAA issued a notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) to amend 14 
CFR part 39 by adding an AD that would apply to certain AHD Model 
EC135P1, EC135P2, EC135P2+, EC135P3, EC135T1, EC135T2, EC135T2+, 
EC135T3, EC635T2+, MBB-BK 117 C-2, and MBB-BK 117 D-2 helicopters. The 
NPRM was published in the Federal Register on February 13, 2026 (91 FR 
6795). The NPRM was prompted by an MCAI, issued by EASA, which is the 
Technical Agent for the Member States of the European Union. EASA 
issued EASA AD 2025-0055, dated March 11, 2025 (EASA AD 2025-0055) 
(also referred to as the MCAI). The MCAI states that cracks were 
reported on a cable drum installed on certain part-numbered Goodrich 
rescue hoist assemblies. Additionally, the material referenced in the 
MCAI states that Goodrich determined that this occurrence was isolated 
to a batch of cable drums made from a plate material, which was 
determined to have increased susceptibility to corrosion-induced 
fatigue. This unsafe condition, if not addressed, could result in 
corrosion-induced fatigue of the cable drum, and consequent injuries to 
human load or individuals on the ground.
    In the NPRM, the FAA proposed to require repetitively inspecting 
the cable drum of certain rescue hoist assemblies for cracks and 
depending on the results, replacing or repairing the cable drum of the 
rescue hoist assembly. Additionally, in the NPRM the FAA proposed to 
prohibit the operation and the installation of an affected rescue hoist 
assembly on a helicopter, unless certain requirements are met. The FAA 
is issuing this AD to address the unsafe condition on these products.
    Additionally, since the FAA issued the NPRM, it was determined that 
the special flight permit restriction specified in paragraph (j) of the 
proposed rule is no longer needed. Accordingly, the FAA has revised 
this final rule by removing the special flight permit paragraph.

Discussion of Final Airworthiness Directive

Comments

    The FAA received comments from three commenters. The commenters 
were AHD, Onboard Systems Hoist and Winch, and an individual commenter. 
The following discussion presents the comments received on the NPRM and 
the FAA's response to these comments.

Request To Clarify Affected Parts

    An individual commenter requested that the FAA specify the affected 
part by serial number, part number, and manufacturing batches that have 
the highest risk. The commenter also stated that by including this 
information, it will avoid unnecessary repeated inspections which may 
delay rescue missions.
    The FAA disagrees with specifying or limiting this final rule by 
adding the serial number, part number, or manufacturing batch 
information. The AD already incorporates this information by reference, 
and the serial numbers associated with a subset of the affected part 
numbers are identified in the incorporated MCAI. Additionally, 
manufacturing batch information is not necessary to determine the 
affected parts, and all of the identified part numbers present an 
equivalent level of risk; none represent a ``highest risk'' category as 
suggested by the commenter. The FAA did not change this AD as a result 
of this comment.

[[Page 67458]]

Request To Revise Inspection Requirements

    One individual commenter requested that the FAA clarify why only 
visual inspections are required and not other forms of testing or 
inspections. The commenter stated that visual inspections may not 
detect small, undetectable cracks that are caused by corrosion, which 
may grow faster than expected. The commenter further stated that by 
including this information, it will help determine how often 
inspections must occur to ensure safe cable drums.
    The FAA infers the commenter is requesting the inspection methods 
increase to other forms of inspections and not just visual inspections 
alone. The FAA disagrees with revising the inspection method outside of 
the requirements that were proposed in the NPRM. EASA, the State of 
Design authority for these helicopters, determined the appropriate 
inspection methodology and criteria for the required inspections. The 
FAA agrees with EASA. The FAA did not change this AD as a result of 
this comment.

Request To Revise the Inspection Requirements

    AHD and Onboard Systems Hoist and Winch (Onboard Systems) both 
requested that the FAA revise or remove the inspection requirements of 
paragraphs (h)(3) and (4) of the proposed AD.
    Onboard Systems requested the FAA revise paragraph (h)(3) of the 
proposed AD by allowing maintenance personnel a limited compliance time 
to confirm indications of a crack with personnel at Airbus Helicopters 
or Onboard Systems Hoist and Winch prior to repairs. Onboard Systems 
stated the inspection process was carefully considered to ensure that 
cracks are detected well before they pose any risk of fracture, while 
also preventing the removal of essential search and rescue assets from 
service for misidentified indications. Additionally, Onboard Systems 
stated that they would have the operator send detailed images of the 
indication and if the indication could not be identified as benign, 
then they would require removing the part from the helicopter. AHD 
stated the proposed inspection as specified in paragraph (h)(3) of the 
proposed AD implies further changes to the inspection process, which 
could eliminate the 3mm minimum crack size criteria.
    The FAA disagrees with Onboard System request to revise the 
compliance time to confirm indications because the FAA may prioritize 
immediate action upon detection of potential safety concerns, such as 
cracks, to eliminate any risk of fracture that could compromise safety. 
However, the FAA agrees to revise paragraph (h)(3) of this AD by 
removing the criteria for contacting the Manager, International 
Validation Branch, or Airbus Helicopters' Design Organizational 
Approval if any cracks or suspected cracks longer than 3mm in length 
are found.
    Onboard Systems requested the FAA remove paragraph (h)(4) of the 
proposed AD. Onboard Systems stated if using more than 6X magnification 
and no minimum threshold, the risk of false indications increases 
exponentially. AHD requested the FAA remove paragraph (h)(4) of the 
proposed AD or revise by removing the words ``or higher.'' AHD stated 
the use of such high magnification could increase misidentifications or 
surface cracks, leading to false-positive indications. AHD stated this 
inspection deviations could lead to false-positive indications and the 
unnecessary grounding of search and rescue assets.
    The FAA acknowledges the commenter's concern to prevent unnecessary 
actions that may remove important resources essential to rescue 
missions and the FAA has determined that a crack length of 3 mm is too 
small to accurately detect with the unaided eye (1x magnification). 
However, the FAA agrees that a 4X-6X magnification is sufficient. 
Accordingly, the FAA has revised paragraph (h)(4) of this AD to specify 
that 4X-6X magnification is required for the visual inspection.

Conclusion

    These products have been approved by the civil aviation authority 
of another country and are approved for operation in the United States. 
Pursuant to the FAA's bilateral agreement with this State of Design 
Authority, that authority has notified the FAA of the unsafe condition 
described in the MCAI referenced above. The FAA reviewed the relevant 
data, considered any comments received, and determined that air safety 
requires adopting this AD as proposed. Accordingly, the FAA is issuing 
this AD to address the unsafe condition on these products. Except for 
minor editorial changes, and any other changes described previously, 
this AD is adopted as proposed in the NPRM. None of the changes will 
increase the economic burden on any operator.

Material Incorporated by Reference Under 1 CFR Part 51

    The FAA reviewed EASA AD 2025-0055, which specifies procedures for 
repetitively inspecting the cable drum installed on the rescue hoist 
assembly for cracks longer than 3 mm and, depending on the results, 
contacting the manufacturer for approved repair instructions. 
Additionally, the MCAI allows the installation of an affected rescue 
hoist assembly on a helicopter if it meets certain requirements. 
Finally, the MCAI prohibits the operation of an affected rescue hoist 
assembly after a certain effective date as specified in the MCAI.
    This material is reasonably available because the interested 
parties have access to it through their normal course of business or by 
the means identified in the ADDRESSES section.

Differences Between This AD and the MCAI

    The MCAI applies to AHD Model EC635 P2+, EC635 P3, EC635 T1, and 
EC635 T3 helicopters, whereas this AD does not because those models do 
not have an FAA type certificate.
    Where the MCAI specifies to contact the manufacturer for repair 
instructions, this AD requires repairing an affected rescue hoist 
assembly in accordance with a method approved by the Manager, 
International Validation Branch; EASA; or Airbus Helicopters' Design 
Organization Approval.

Authority for This Rulemaking

    Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to 
issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the 
authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII: Aviation Programs, 
describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority.
    The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in 
Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. 
Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight 
of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for 
practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary 
for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that 
authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to 
exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.

(a) Effective Date

    This airworthiness directive (AD) is effective December 1, 2026.

(b) Affected ADs

    None.

(c) Applicability

    This AD applies to Airbus Helicopters Deutschland GmbH (AHD) 
Model EC135P1, EC135P2, EC135P2+, EC135P3, EC135T1, EC135T2, 
EC135T2+, EC135T3, and EC635T2+ helicopters, certificated in any 
category, all serial numbers (S/N) up to 1999; and Model MBB-BK 117 
C-2 and MBB-BK 117 D-2 helicopters, certificated in any category, 
all S/Ns.
    Note 1 to paragraph (c): Helicopters with an EC135P3H 
designation are Model EC135P3 helicopters. Helicopters with an 
EC135T3H designation are Model EC135T3 helicopters.
    Note 2 to paragraph (c): Helicopters with an MBB-BK 117 C-2e 
designation are Model MBB-BK 117 C-2 helicopters.

(d) Subject

    Joint Aircraft System Component (JASC) Code 2500, Cabin 
equipment/furnishings.

(e) Unsafe Condition

    This AD was prompted by reports of cracks on the cable drum of 
the rescue hoist assembly. The FAA is issuing this AD to detect and 
correct cable drums installed on certain rescue hoist assemblies 
that have been manufactured with a certain plate material that could 
result in corrosion-induced fatigue of the cable drum. This unsafe 
condition, if not addressed, could result in failure of the cable 
drum and injuries to human load or individuals on the ground.

(f) Compliance

    Comply with this AD within the compliance times specified, 
unless already done.

(g) Requirements

    Except as specified in paragraphs (h) and (i) of this AD: comply 
with all required actions and compliance times specified in, and in 
accordance with, European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) AD 
2025-0055, dated March 11, 2025 (EASA AD 2025-0055).

(h) Exceptions to EASA AD 2025-0055

    (1) Where EASA AD 2025-0055 refers to its effective date, this 
AD requires using the effective date of this AD.
    (2) Where EASA AD 2025-0055 requires compliance in terms of 
flight hours, this AD requires using hours time-in-service.
    (3) Where paragraph (2) of EASA AD 2025-0055 and the material 
referenced in EASA AD 2025-0055 specifies to contact a manufacturer 
for repair instructions, for this AD repair an affected rescue hoist 
assembly in accordance with a method approved by the Manager, 
International Validation Branch, FAA; EASA; or Airbus Helicopters' 
Design Organization Approval (DOA). If approved by the DOA, the 
approval must include the DOA-authorized signature.
    (4) Where the material referenced in EASA AD 2025-0055 specifies 
``magnifying glass 6X maximum,'' this AD requires replacing that 
text with ``4X-6X magnification''.
    (5) This AD does not adopt the ``Remarks'' section of EASA AD 
2025-0055.

(i) No Reporting Requirement

    Although the material referenced in EASA AD 2025-0055 specifies 
to submit certain information to the manufacturer, this AD does not 
require that action.

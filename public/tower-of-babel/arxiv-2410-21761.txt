On Gelfand pairs and degenerate Gelfand-Graev modules of General Linear groups of degree two over principal ideal local rings of finite length
Archita Gupta, Pooja Singla

Abstract
. Let R R be a principal ideal local ring of finite length with a finite residue field of odd characteristic. Denote by G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) the general linear group of degree two over R R , and by B ⁡ ( R ) B(R) the Borel subgroup of G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) consisting of upper triangular matrices. In this article, we prove that the pair ( G ⁡ ( R ) , B ⁡ ( R ) ) (G(R),B(R)) is a strong Gelfand pair. We also investigate the decomposition of the degenerate Gelfand-Graev (DGG) modules of G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) . It is known that the non-degenerate Gelfand Graev module (also called non-degenerate Whittaker model) of G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) is multiplicity-free. We characterize the DGG-modules where the multiplicities are independent of the cardinality of the residue field. We provide a complete decomposition of all DGG modules of G ⁡ ( R ) G(R) for R R of length at most four.

Introduction

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a non-archimedean local field, and let
{\mathfrak{o}}
denote its ring of integers. The residue field
\mathbf{k}
is finite of characteristic
p\geq 3
and
|\mathbf{k}|=q.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the unique maximal ideal of
{\mathfrak{o}}
, and fix a generator
\varpi
of
\mathfrak{p}.

Consider
\mathbf{G}
as a split reductive group scheme defined over
{\mathfrak{o}}
, and let
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}})
represent the set of
{\mathfrak{o}}
-points of
\mathbf{G}.
Being maximal compact subgroups,
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}})
play a significant role in the representation theory of the groups
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{F}).
For
\ell\geq 1,
let
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}:={\mathfrak{o}}/\mathfrak{p}^{\ell}.
Since the groups
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}})
are pro-finite, every complex continuous finite dimensional irreducible representation of
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}})
factors through the quotient
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
for some
\ell.
Therefore, it suffices to study the finite-dimensional complex representations of the groups
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
.
The study of irreducible representations of
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
and their properties is a highly active area of research in mathematics, see
[
13
,
11
,
14
,
19
,
10
,
26
,
2
,
24
]
and the references mentioned therein. In this article, we explore a few problems regarding the Gelfand pairs and Gelfand modules of
\mathbf{G}({\mathfrak{o}})
.

Recall that a pair
(G,H)
is called a Gelfand pair if the induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\mathrm{triv}_{H})\cong\mathbb{C}[G/H]
is multiplicity-free. In the study of finite groups, Gelfand pairs are valuable tools used across various branches of mathematics, including number theory (see
[
12
]
), combinatorics (see
[
1
,
17
]
) and Harmonic analysis (see
[
8
,
9
]
).
This concept has been further generalized, leading to the notion of strong Gelfand pairs. A pair

(G,H)
is defined as a strong Gelfand pair if

\mathrm{Ind}_{H}^{G}(\theta)
is multiplicity-free for
every
irreducible representation
\theta
of
H
. In case of summetric group
S_{n}
, the pair
(S_{n},S_{n-1})
is known to be a strong Gelfand pair and this property provides a basis of the irreducible representations of
S_{n}.

Let
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
be the general linear group with entries from the finite ring
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}
and let
{\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
be the Borel subgroup of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
consisting of the upper triangular matrices. It is well known that the pair
(\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{1}),\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{1}))
is a strong Gelfand pair. We extend this result to all
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}
and prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

The pair
(\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}),{\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})})
is a strong Gelfand pair for every
\ell\geq 1
.

Let
\mathbf{P}_{n}({{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}})
be the mirabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}})
, that is,
\mathbf{P}_{n}({{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}})
denote the subgroup of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}})
consisting of matrices with the last row
(0,0,\cdots,0,1)
.
For
\ell=1
, it is well known that the pair
(\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{1}),\mathbf{P}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{1}))
is a strong Gelfand pair, see
[
27
]
*Section 13. Let
{\mathbf{Z}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
be the center of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
. Then
{\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}={\mathbf{Z}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}\mathbf{P}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
. The following is an immediate corollary of
\autoref
thm:borel-strong-Gelfand-pair.

Corollary 1.2
.

For any irreducible representation
\theta
of
\mathbf{P}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
, the representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbf{P}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}^{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(\theta)
is a multiplicity-free representation of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
.

This result is known to be true for
\dim(\theta)=1
and
\dim(\theta)=(q-1)q^{\ell-1}
by
[
7
]
*Proposition 1 and
[
22
]
*Theorem 1.1, respectively. A proof of
\autoref
thm:borel-strong-Gelfand-pair is included in
\autoref
sec:proof-borel-strong-Gelfand-pair. To prove
\autoref
thm:borel-strong-Gelfand-pair, we first consider a generalization of the well-known Gelfand-Graev (GG) modules (also called non-degenerate Whittaker models) of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
. These GG-modules of
\mathbf{GL}_{n}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
were studied in
[
15
,
21
,
22
]
, and we briefly recall these here for
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
. Let
{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
be the group of upper triangular unipotent matrices in
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}.
A non-trivial one dimensional representation
\psi\colon{{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

such that
\psi|_{\varpi^{\ell-1}{{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}}}\neq 1
is called a primitive character of
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}.
For any primitive character
\psi
, define a one dimensional representation
\psi_{t}:{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

\psi_{t}\begin{pmatrix}1&u\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:=\psi(\varpi^{\ell-t}u).

Any one dimensional representation of
{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
, upto conjugation by
{\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
, is of the form

\psi_{t}
for some
t\in[0,\ell]
. The character
\psi_{t}
for
t=\ell
is called a non-degenerate character of
{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
and

{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
-representation space
V^{\ell}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}^{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(\psi_{\ell})
is called the Gelfand-Graev (GG) module (or non-degenerate Whittaker model) of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
.
Generalizing this construction,

\psi_{t}
for
t<\ell
is called a
degenerate character
of
{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
and
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
-representation space
V^{t}:=\mathrm{Ind}_{{\mathbf{U}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}^{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(\psi_{t})
for
t<\ell
is called a degenerate Gelfand-Graev (DGG) module of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
. This is a natural generalization of the well known parallel notions from
\mathbf{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

[
27
]
*Section 12. By
[
22
]
, the GG module of
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
is known to be multiplicity free. Moreover, it is proved that an irreducible representation of
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
is a constituent of the GG module if and only if it is a regular representation of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
, see
\autoref
sec:preliminaries for related definitions.

The induced representation
\mathrm{Ind}_{{\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}^{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(\theta)
, where
\theta
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
, is a sub-representation of certain DGG-modules of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}.
Hence, to prove
\autoref
thm:borel-strong-Gelfand-pair, we also consider the problem of decomposition of DGG-modules of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
. In particular, we are interested in the multiplicities of their irreducible constituents. For
\ell=1
, the corresponding decomposition is well known, see
[
23
]
. We will consider
\ell\geq 2
in this article. To describe our results, we define the following:

a(t,\ell)=\underset{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}({\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})})}{\mathrm{Sup}}\{m_{\rho}\mid\,\,\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(V^{t},\rho))=m_{\rho}\}.

The number
a(t,\ell)
gives us the highest possible multiplicity of the irreducible constituents of the DGG-module
V^{t}
and will be called its multiplicity bound. We prove the following result regarding
a(t,\ell).

Theorem 1.3
.

The multiplicity bound
a(t,\ell)
of
V^{t}
is independent of the residue field if and only if
t\geq\ell-1.

As mentioned earlier, we have
a(\ell,\ell)=1
for all
\ell
. We remark that in view of the above results with
a(t,\ell)
dependent on the residue field,
\autoref
thm:borel-strong-Gelfand-pair is a somewhat surprising result.
See
\autoref
sec:proof-multiplicity-bounds for a proof of
\autoref
thm:multiplicity-bounds. In the course of proving the above result, we also obtain the following:

Theorem 1.4
.

(1)

For
\ell\geq 1,
we have
a({\ell-1},\ell)=2.

(2)

For any cuspidal representation
\rho
of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
, we have

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(V^{t},\rho))=\begin{cases}1,&\mathrm{for}\,\,t=\ell;\\
0,&\mathrm{for}\,\,t<\ell.\end{cases}

(3)

For any split semisimple representation
\rho
of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
, we have
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(V^{t},\rho))=2.

Hence, if the multiplicity bound of
V^{t}
satisfies
a(t,\ell)>2,
then there exists a split non-semisimple irreducible representation
\rho
of
{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}
such that
\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}(V^{t},\rho)=a(t,\ell).
We remark that we have not bee able to decompose
V^{t}
completely into its irreducible constituents.
At last, we include a complete decomposition of the DGG-modules of
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{2})
,
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{3})
and
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{4})
for

into their irreducible constituents in

This, in particular, gives the structure of the endomorphism algebra of

Note that
V^{t}\cong\oplus_{\chi\in\widehat{{\mathbf{Z}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}}{V^{t}_{\chi}}
, where

The structure of the endomorphism algebra of

is then obtained from

Therefore, it suffices to understand
\mathrm{End}_{{\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})}}({V^{t}_{\chi}})
for

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we set up the notation and briefly recall the construction of the irreducible representations of
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
from
[
18
]
.
We will also discuss alternative constructions of some representations of
\mathbf{GL}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
.

Throughout this article, we consider
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{GL}_{2}
. Let
\mathbf{B}

be the Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
consisting of all upper triangular matrices, and let
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{T}
denote the unipotent radical and torus of
\mathbf{B}
, respectively. The center of
\mathbf{G}
will be denoted by
\mathbf{Z}
and the Lie algebra of
\mathbf{G}
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
. For a group scheme
\mathbf{H}
defined over
{\mathfrak{o}}
, we shall denote
\mathbf{H}(\mathfrak{o}_{\ell})
by
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}
. In this article, we will always consider the complex representations of
\mathbf{H}_{\ell}
. For a group
G
, the set of all in-equivalent irreducible representations of
G
will be denoted by
\mathrm{Irr}(G).
For a subgroup
H
of
G
and a representation
\phi
of
H
, the set
\{\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G)\mid\langle\rho|_{H},\phi\rangle\neq 0\}
will be denoted by
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\phi).
For any representation
\rho
of
G
such that
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\phi),
we say
\rho
lies above
\phi
. In this article, by a character of a group
G
, we always mean a one-dimensional representation of
G
. For an abelian group
A
, the set
\mathrm{Irr}(A)
is also denoted by
\widehat{A}
.

For
i\leq\ell
and the natural projection maps
\rho_{i}\colon{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathbf{G}}_{i},
let
\mathbf{K}^{i}=\ker(\rho_{i})
be the
i
-th congruence subgroups of
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}.
For
i\geq\ell/2,
the group
\mathbf{K}^{i}
is isomorphic to the abelian additive subgroup
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell-i}).
Let
\psi\colon{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a fixed primitive one dimensional representation of
{\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell}
.
For any
i\leq\ell/2
and
x\in{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{i}),
let
\hat{x}\in{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
be an arbitrary lift of
x
satisfying
\rho_{i}(\hat{x})=x.
Define
\psi_{x}:I+\varpi^{\ell-i}{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by

\psi_{x}(I+\varpi^{\ell-i}y)=\psi(\varpi^{\ell-i}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{x}y)),

for all
I+\varpi^{\ell-i}y\in\mathbf{K}^{\ell-i}.
Then
\psi_{x}
is a well defined one dimensional representation of
\mathbf{K}^{\ell-i}.
Further, the following duality for abelian groups
\mathbf{K}^{i}
and
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell-i})
holds for
i\geq\ell/2
.

{\mathfrak{gl}}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell-i})\cong\widehat{\mathbf{K}^{i}};\,\,x\mapsto\psi_{x},\,\,\mathrm{where}\,\,\psi_{x}(y)=\psi(\varpi^{\ell-i}\mathrm{tr}(\hat{x}y))\,\,\forall\,\,y\in\mathbf{K}^{i}.

We say a one dimensional representation
\psi_{x}\in\widehat{\mathbf{K}^{i}}
for
i\geq\ell/2
is regular if and only if
x\in M_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell-i})
is a regular matrix. It is well known that for
i\geq\ell/2
, the representation
\psi_{x}\in\widehat{\mathbf{K}^{i}}
is regular if and only if
\psi_{x}|_{\mathbf{K}^{\ell-1}}
is regular.
We recall the following definition of types of regular matrices
x\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
:

Definition 2.1
.

A regular matrix
x\in\mathfrak{gl}_{2}({\mathfrak{o}}_{\ell})
is called cuspidal (resp. split semisimple and split non-semisimple) if the characteristic polynomial of the image of
x
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}({\mathbb{F}_{q}})
under the natural projection map has no roots (resp. two distinct roots and a repeated root) in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

An irreducible representation
\rho
of
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}
is called regular (cuspidal, split semisimple, split non-semisimple) if the orbit of its restriction to
\mathbf{K}^{\ell-1}
consists of one- dimensional representations
\psi_{x}
for regular (cuspidal, split semisimple, split non-semisimple)
x
.
We shall say a representation (matrix) is
\mathbf{cus}
,
\mathbf{ss}
, or
\mathbf{sns}
if it is cuspidal, split semisimple, or split non-semisimple representation (matrix) respectively.
For
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}},
these are precisely the regular representations (matrices). Note that the regular representations (even upto twist) of
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}
can not be obtained from
\mathbf{G}_{\ell-1}.

Denote
\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor
by
\ell_{1}
and
\lceil\ell/2\rceil
by
{\ell_{2}}
. We now summarize very briefly the construction of regular representations of
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}
from
[
18
]
with emphasis on the statements that we require in this article.

2.1.
Construction of regular representations of
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}
for
\ell

Construction of regular representations of
{\mathbf{G}_{\ell}}

Lemma 3.4

Lemma 3.5

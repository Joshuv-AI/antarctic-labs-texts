Néron models, minimal models, and birational group actions
János Kollár

Abstract
. Let A K A_{K} be an Abelian variety over the quotient field of a Dedekind domain R R . We show that the identity component of the Néron model of A K A_{K} acts regularly on any minimal model of A K A_{K} over Spec ⁡ R \operatorname{Spec}R , and discuss when the Néron model is an open subset of a minimal model. The main technical result is the rigidity of small modifications, leading to a regularity criterion for birational group actions.



Let
S

K
,
and
A_{K}
an Abelian variety over

Finding a model of

Kodaira described minimal, elliptic surfaces over
{\mathbb{C}}

[

For higher dimensional Abelian varieties, the optimal smooth group scheme

Minimal model theory—also known as Mori’s program—aims to provide a (non-unique) projective, minimal model of any variety or scheme; see (

The aim of this note is to study

The main result is Theorem

gives the following for the Néron model.

Theorem 1

Let

an Abelian variety over

has a projective, minimal model

Let

be the identity component of the Néron model (

Then the

-action on itself extends to a regular
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
-action on
A_{S}
.

Let

, and

the

In higher dimensions the induced

Corollary 2
.

Let

be a projective, minimal model. Then

(2.1)

Main technical theorems

Theorem
1
is a special case of the following regularity criterion, whose proof is given in (

It can be viewed as a generalization of

[
Han87
, 3.3]
and of
[

Theorem 7
.

Let
S
be a normal scheme with function field
K
,

G_{S}\to S
a smooth group scheme with connected fibers, and

\pi:X_{S}\to S
a dominant, projective morphism.
Assume that
X_{S}
has terminal singularities and
K_{X_{S}/S}
is
\pi
-nef.

Then any rational
G_{K}
-action

\mu_{K}:G_{K}\times_{K}X_{K}\dasharrow X_{K}
on the generic fiber
X_{K}

extends to a regular
G_{S}
-action

\mu_{S}:G_{S}\times_{S}X_{S}\to X_{S}
.

Note that the statement tacitly assumes that
K_{X_{S}/S}
exists.
This holds if
S
is excellent and has a dualizing complex.

Theorem
7
is derived from a more general regularity criterion on
rational group actions (
37
). The full scheme theoretic version is a little technical, the following variant for varieties, to be proved in (
32
), gives the right idea.

Theorem 8
.

Let
X
be a normal, projective variety over an algebraically closed field, and
G
a connected, smooth algebraic group. Let
\mu:G\times X\dasharrow X
be a rational action. Assume that, for general
g\in G
, the map
\mu_{g}:X\dasharrow X
is an isomorphism in codimension 1.
Then
\mu:G\times X\to X
is a regular action.

Note that we do not assume that there is an open subset
U\subset X
where all the
\mu_{g}
are defined.

9
Small modifications.

Let
X_{i}
be normal, proper schemes over
S
. A birational map

\phi:X_{1}\dasharrow X_{2}
is a
small modification,

if there are closed subsets

Z_{i}\subset X_{i}
of codimension
\geq 2
such that
\phi
restricts to an isomorphism

\phi:(X_{1}\setminus Z_{1})\cong(X_{2}\setminus Z_{2})
.

The best known examples are flips and flops, much studied when the
X_{i}
have log terminal singularities
[
KM98
]
. The case of arbitrary normal varieties attracted much less attention
[
Kol91
, Sec.2]
.

The key technical result behind Theorem
8
is the rigidity of small modifications, see (
30
) and (
36
) for the precise statements.

Very little seems to be known about the existence and structure of
small modifications, beyond the log terminal case.
As the next example shows, there are some unexpected flops of 3-folds with log canonical singularities.

Example 10

(Flopping a fiber of a product)
.

We construct an example of a pointed surface
v\in S
with simple elliptic (hence log canonical) singularity at
v
, and an elliptic curve
E
such that the fiber

E\times\{v\}\subset E\times S
has infinitely many different flops.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
be a smooth cubic,

S\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}
the cone over
E
with vertex
v
and projection
\pi:S\dasharrow E
.
For
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
consider the map

\phi_{m}:E\times S\dasharrow E\times S\quad\mbox{given by}\quad(p,s)\mapsto\bigl(p+m\pi(s),s\bigr).

Then
\phi_{m}
is an isomorphism over
S\setminus\{v\}
, but it is not defined along
E\times\{v\}
for
m\neq 0
.
Note that
E\times S
has log canonical singularities, and the
\phi_{m}
give infinitely many different flops of the curve
E\times\{v\}
.

Notation 11
.

As explained in
[
BLR90
, p.13]
, the theory of Néron models is local on the base. Thus, from now on,

R
denotes a DVR with quotient field
K
, residue field
k
and
T:=\operatorname{Spec}R
.
For a morphism
\pi:X_{T}\to T
, we use
X_{K}
(resp.
X_{k}
) to denote the
generic (resp. closed or central) fiber.

Definition 12
.

A smooth morphism
X_{T}\to T
satisfies the

Néron mapping property
iff the following holds.

(12.1)

Let
Y_{T}\to T
be any smooth morphism. Then every morphism
\phi_{K}:Y_{K}\to X_{K}
extends to a morphism
\phi_{T}:Y_{T}\to X_{T}
.

A morphism
X_{T}\to T
is a
Néron model
of its generic fiber
X_{K}
, if it is smooth, of finite type, and satisfies the Néron mapping property. We use
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})
to denote the Néron model of
X_{K}
.

It is clear that
X_{K}
uniquely determines its Néron model
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})
.
Néron models commute with
étale base changes
T^{\prime}\to T

[
BLR90
, p.13]
.
Thus many questions about Néron models can be treaded over
the strict Henselisation of
T
.

However, ramification usually changes
the Néron model drastically.

[
Nér62
,
Nér64
]
construct Néron models when
X_{K}
is a

torsor
(or
principal homogeneous space
)
under an Abelian variety over
K
.
Note that

[
Art86
]
and
[
BLR90
]

discuss mainly (
13
.1).

Theorem 13

(Néron models)
.

Let
A_{K}
be an Abelian variety over
K
, and
X_{K}
an
A_{K}
-torsor.
Then the following hold.

(13.1)

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})
exists, and it is a
smooth, finite type group scheme over

.

(13.2)

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})
exists.

(13.3)

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})

or a
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})
-torsor.
∎

Definition 14
.

The union of

and the identity component of
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})_{k}

is the

of
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})

Note that

The central fiber

Its

For the purposes of Theorem

16
Minimal models of Abelian torsors.

Let
S
be a 1-dimensional, irreducible, regular scheme with function field
K
.
Let
X_{K}
be a proper
K
-variety with terminal singularities, whose canonical class is numerically trivial.

A proper morphism
\pi:X_{S}\to S
is a
minimal model
of
X_{K}
if

(16.1)

X_{S}
has terminal singularities
[
KM98
, 2.34]
,

(16.2)

K_{X_{S}}
is numerically
\pi
-trivial, and

(16.3)

the generic fiber is isomorphic to
X_{K}
.

Numerically
\pi
-trivial
means that
(K_{X_{S}}\cdot C)=0
for every curve contracted by
\pi
.

Being terminal is preserved by étale base change
[
Kol13
, 2.15]
, so being a minimal model is also preserved by étale base change.
Note that minimal model theory frequently works with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial
schemes, that is, some multiple of any Weil divisor is required to be Cartier. However, being
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial is not an étale-local property, so, as in
[
Kol21
]
, we work without it.

Minimal models of
X_{K}
need not be unique, but the following
is easy to see directly; see
[
KM98
, 3.52]
.

Claim
16
.4.

Let
B
be a normal scheme and

\pi_{i}:X_{i}\to B

Assume that

is irreducible, has terminal singularities, and

is
\pi_{i}
-nef for

.
Let
\phi:X_{1}\dasharrow X_{2}
be a birational map.

Then there are closed
Z_{i}\subset X_{i}
of codimension
\geq 2
, such that

.

Theorem 17
.

Let
X_{K}
be a torsor under an Abelian variety
A_{K}
,
and
\pi:X_{T}\to T
a minimal model of
X_{K}
.
Let
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})\subset\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})
be the identity component of the Néron model. Then the
A_{K}
-action on
X_{K}
extends to a regular
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
-action on
X_{T}
.

Proof. The
A_{K}
-action on
X_{K}
extends to a rational
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
-action on
X_{T}
which we write as

[formula]

\mu:\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})\times_{T}X_{T}\dasharrow X_{T}.

Being an morphism can be checked after a surjective, étale base change. We may thus assume that
T
is strictly Henselian.

Given any section
\tau:T\to\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
, the induced map

\mu_{\tau}:X_{T}\dasharrow X_{T}
is a small modification by
(
16
.4). Thus
\mu
is a morphism by (
37
).
∎

The
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
action gives strong restrictions on the smooth locus.

Lemma 18
.

Let
U_{T}\to T
be a smooth morphism with a regular
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
action,
whose generic fiber
U_{K}
is an
A_{K}
-torsor.
Then
u:U_{T}\to\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(U_{K})
is an open immersion, and the complement of the image is a union of

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})_{k}
-orbits.

Proof. By the universal property of Néron models there is a birational
morphism

u:U_{T}\to\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(U_{K})
, which is
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})
-equivariant. The restriction of
u
gives a
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})_{k}
-equivariant morphism of
U_{k}
to
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(U_{K})_{k}
, which is a union of
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})_{k}
-orbits. Thus
u_{k}
is an isomorphism onto an union of connected components of
\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})_{k}
. ∎

Corollary
2

Corollary 19
.

Continuing with the notation of
(
17

X^{\rm sm}_{T}\subset X_{T}
denote the

\pi

u:X^{\rm sm}_{T}\to\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})
the induced morphism.
Then

(19.1)

either

is an isomorphism,

(19.2)

or
X^{\rm sm}_{T}\cong X_{K}
.

Furthermore,
u
is an isomorphism in either of the following cases.

(19.3)

X_{T}
is regular.

(19.4)

X_{T}
is factorial and
k
is perfect.

Proof.
We may assume that
T
is strictly Henselian.
If
X^{\rm sm}_{k}
is empty, we have
(
19
.2).
Otherwise there is at least one irreducible component

E_{1}\subset X_{k}
, such that
u
maps
E_{1}\cap X^{\rm sm}_{k}

onto an irreducible component
F_{1}\subset\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})_{k}
.

Let
F_{2}\subset\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(X_{K})_{k}
be any other irreducible component.
There is a section
\tau:T\to\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}(A_{K})
that maps
F_{1}
onto
F_{2}
.
This gives a rational map
\mu_{\tau}:X_{T}\dasharrow X_{T}
that sends

E_{1}
to another irreducible component

by
(

.4).
Then

maps
E_{2}\cap X^{\rm sm}_{k}

onto

. Thus (
19
.1) holds by (
18
).

It remains to show that
X^{\rm sm}_{k}
is not empty in cases (
19

Let
C\subset X_{T}
be a section.
Then
(X_{k}\cdot C)=1
, and

(\operatorname{red}X_{k}\cdot C)\geq 1
if
\operatorname{red}X_{k}
is a Cartier divisor.
Thus

is reduced near

, hence

If

is regular, then

∎

\operatorname{N\acute{e}r}^{\circ}(A_{K})

Example 20

Let
B
be an Abelian variety and
\sigma

m

X:=X(B,\sigma):=B\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/(\sigma,\epsilon),

where
\epsilon

The central fiber has multiplicity

We work over a Noetherian base scheme. To avoid some possible problems with dimension, let us assume that it is universally catenary.

Definition 24

(Families of birational maps)
.

A

is an (equivalence class of)
isomorphisms between dense, open subsets

The largest such is called the

of

Let

be equidimenisonal, proper morphisms.
A birational map

over

is a

\operatorname{domi}(\phi)
contains the generic points of each fiber. Thus the restrictions

Warning
24
.1.
If each

is an isomorphism, then

\phi
is an isomorphism over a dense, open subset of

by (

.2),
but it can happen that

is not an isomorphism, see (

.2) for such an example.

Let

Let

Next let

be irreducible, normal schemes, proper over

a birational map with closed graph

Claim

There are closed subsets

Definition 26

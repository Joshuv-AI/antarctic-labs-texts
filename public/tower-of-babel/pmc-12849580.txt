Determination of energy consumption during kangoo jumps exercises in women engaged in recreational sport
Authors: Aytül Eynur, Meryem Gülaç, Alparslan Ünveren, Halit Harmancı, Mihri Barış Karavelioğlu, Baybars Recep Eynur
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study was to investigate the effect of kangoo jumps activity on energy expenditure in women participating in kangoo jumps activities.MethodsThirteen women participated in the study on the basis of voluntary participation. In line with the measurements made, a portable gas analyzer (Cortex Metamax 3B) was used to reach the findings (MET, HR (min), VO2 (L/min), VO2/kg (ml/min/kg), RER, VE (L/min), FAT (g/h), VCO2 (L/min)). The values of the music used at 103 bpm (Phase 1) and 128 bpm (Phase 2) were fixed with the help of sound engineers and music was used. In the statistical evaluation of the data, an independent sample t-test was used based on the phase variable and the significance level was accepted as (p≤0.05).ResultAs a result of the research, there were significant differences found between Phase 1 (aerobic) and Phase 2 (anaerobic) according to MET (t(0.05) = -27.4; p < 0.05), HR (min) (t(0.05) = -21.0; p < 0.05), VO2 (L/min) (t(0.05) = -27.2; p < 0.05), VO2/kg (ml/min/kg) (t(0.05) = -27.4; p < 0.05), RER (t(0.05) = -23.7; p < 0.05), VE (L/min) (t(0.05) = -25.8; p < 0.05), FAT (g/h) (t(0.05) = -19.5; p < 0.05), and VCO2 (L/min) (t(0.05) = -25.8; p < 0.05) variables.ConclusionKangoo jumps activity was evaluated as an activity whose intensity can be adjusted according to different physical fitness levels of many individuals and can meet their energy consumption needs.Trial registrationNot applicable.Keywords: Kangoo jumps, Woman, Exercise, Physical activity, Energy consumption

Introduction

An individual’s total daily energy expenditure represents the energy consumed by the organism and consists of the sum of the basal metabolic rate, endogenous thermogenesis, and energy expenditure related to physical activity [1]. The World Health Organization (WHO) defines physical activity as any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that requires energy expenditure [2]. Physical activity encompasses the most effective practices for ensuring an individual’s physiological health and fitness level. The WHO Global Physical Activity Action Plan recommends that all adults engage in regular physical activity to lead a healthy life. If this physical activity is to be of moderate intensity, it should be 150–300 min per week at a MET value of 5–6; if it is to be of high intensity, it should be 75–150 min per week at a MET value of 6 or above [3]. Studies conducted on energy consumption at various levels provide important findings regarding the effectiveness of the process and its significance in human life [4, 5]. In this context, it is observed that international organizations are conducting studies on energy consumption based on different paradigms [6]. Furthermore, a review of the literature reveals that numerous different tools and technologies have been developed based on these insights into energy consumption measurement [7–10].

An exercise plan to improve cardiovascular fitness and overall health requires quantifying the intensity of the exercise performed [11]. Whether jumping movements accompanied by music (such as kangoo jump or mini trampoline) or simple jumping movements, they are characterized by high energy consumption [11–13]. In addition, it is possible to achieve very different exercise intensities with jumping exercise [13]. Energy consumption changes when exercises such as bending the knees, walking and running are performed on a flexible surface along with the rhythm of music [14]. Variability in the use of foot strike frequency and leg lift heights can also increase energy consumption during exercises that include jumping [11]. Therefore, energy consumption during jumping exercises may vary depending on the rhythm and structure of the movement and the degree of effort in the jump [11–14].

Kangoo Jumps is a type of exercise that has proven its effectiveness worldwide as a registered trademark and holds a significant place in the global physical activity and exercise portfolio with its patents and training program specifications. It has emerged as a physical activity shaped as an alternative approach to different exercise applications today, with a special shoe design accompanied by music [15, 16]. Kangoo Jumps, with the help of its specially designed shoes, identifies movement patterns that differ from an individual’s normal movement patterns accompanied by music. Based on certain loading principles, it serves the individual’s purpose by meeting their physical, psychomotor, mental, and cognitive needs. It rightfully holds a place in the current fitness and wellness portfolio [15–25]. When evaluating the gains from Kangoo jumps, it is stated that, in addition to the physical fitness criteria that occur in the individual, there are also developments in the individual’s kinematic perception with the help of different stimuli [15, 18]. In light of all these gains, it is anticipated that Kangoo Jumps will continue to exist in human life as a physical activity area that will meet the needs within task and person variables, both through the shoes it uses and by moving away from environmental variables, in future living spaces and applications.

In this context, the fact that no previous studies have been conducted on such a dataset, coupled with numerous other reasons such as the high rate at which individuals encounter such activities globally, has highlighted the necessity of conducting this study.

Research protocol

In accordance with the research protocol, maximal measurements were first performed to determine the participants’ physiological responses during maximal exercise, followed by the kangoo jumps exercise consisting of two phases. In the study, to maximize efficiency by leveraging group dynamics during the kangoo jumps application and because kangoo jumps are typically performed within a group dynamic, the female athlete participating in the measurement was accompanied by two female athletes who were not measured, ensuring that all phases were completed.

Based on the measurements taken, a portable gas analyzer (Cortex Metamax 3B) was used to obtain the following findings: MET, HR (min), VO2 (L/min), VO2/kg (ml/min/kg), RER, VE (L/min), FAT (g/h), VCO2 (L/min) were obtained using a portable gas analyzer (Cortex Metamax 3B).

The values of the music used at 103 (Phase 1) and 128 (Phase 2) BPM within the application were fixed. Participants synchronize jumping, landing, and balancing movements with the music, while the music tempo directly affects cadence, movement economy and perceived exertion (RPE) [26, 27]. Indeed, medium-tempo music around 103 BPM (Phase 1) is suitable for warm-up and low-to-moderate intensity aerobic sections, featuring longer ground contact and controlled jumping phases. Individuals exercising at this pace typically work at approximately 60–70% of their maximum heart rate (HR); ventilation (VE) and oxygen consumption (VO₂) values remain moderate [28]. In contrast, high-tempo music around 128 BPM is preferred during the main exercise or high-intensity interval (HIIT-like) phases, with shorter ground contact time, higher jump frequency, and increased muscle-elastic energy conversion. At this speed level, cardiac output, ventilation, and lactate levels increase, while performance sustainability and motivational arousal (affective arousal) also rise as long as synchronization remains high [29, 30].

In evaluating the findings, an independent samples t-test was used at a significance level of p < 0.05 to examine the difference in scores for the variables studied between the two different phase stages. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical approval criteria specified in Dumlupınar University’s letter dated January 9, 2025, and numbered 9.

The null hypothesis (H0) determining the statistical method used in the study was formulated as follows: There is no significant difference between the anaerobic and aerobic phases in terms of dependent variables. In this context, the aim of this study was to determine the energy consumption during kangoo jumps exercise at different rhythm levels in women angeged in recreational sports.

Data collection method and statistical evaluation

To evaluate the findings obtained in the study, descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, lower and upper values within the 95% confidence interval, range, and correlation coefficients) were calculated for the independent variables (MET, HR (min), VO2 (L/min), VO2/kg (ml/min/kg), RER, VE (L/min), FAT (g/h), VCO2 (L/min)). In addition to the descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, lower and upper values within the 95% confidence interval, range, and minimum and maximum) for these variables, an independent samples t-test was used for the phase variable during the kangoo jumps activity. The significance level for the mean comparisons was defined as p ≤ 0.05.

Discussion

When examining studies on energy consumption, it has been noted that among young people living in Latin countries, the individual's lifestyle is particularly important among the factors determining energy consumption [31].

In a study involving 47 women aged 20–23, conducted on two different groups consisting of four 15-minute classic aerobic sections and three 15-minute step-aerobic sections, with music support similar to that used in the study, energy expenditure was calculated using heart rate, oxygen consumption, and MET scores. The study revealed that there was an effort to determine a level based on MET values at five different levels. In this context, 3.2–3.6.2.6 MET points with 4–5 sessions per week is considered low level; 3.6–4.7.6.7 MET points with 4–5 sessions per week is considered below average level; 4.7–6.3.7.3 MET points with 3–4 sessions per week is considered moderate level; 6.3–7.8.3.8 MET points is considered moderate-high level; and 7.8–8.4.8.4 MET points is considered high level [32]. When compared with findings from researchers' own studies, it is seen that MET values are well below the maximum values. Here, it is thought that the difference in results is due to the use of indirect measurements.

In another study, MET values were defined based on physical activity and breakfast consumption levels; values below 1.5 were classified as sedentary, values between 1.5 and 2.9 as light, values between 3.0 and 5.9 as moderate, and values above 5.9 as high MET values [33], [34]. While physical activity was not fully defined in the study, the process of determining activity levels indicates that MET values could be considered a high-intensity physical activity level in our study.

In a study conducted by Wyatt (2008) and colleagues, it was stated that increased calcium intake and physical activity led to systematic weight loss, with the loss occurring primarily from fat [35]. Although this finding was not consistent with the scope of the study, it was recorded as a point to be considered in future studies.

This study conducted by researchers may have defined a limited understanding in the evaluation of data due to the presence of only women in the study group. However, a review of the literature shows that physically active women have higher total energy expenditure than inactive men, despite having less muscle mass than men [36].

According to the phase differences obtained in the study, a significant difference was found, and when the group's maximum values were compared, it was determined that the MET values were similar to the high MET values obtained in different sports.

Looking at the studies conducted, MET values range from a minimum of 0.9 MET while sleeping to 18 MET for running at a speed of 10.9 miles per hour [37]. When examining the MET values of various physical activities, it was found that double-leg jumping rope is slow (<100 jumps/min, 8.8 MET, moderate; 100–120 jumps/min 11.8 MET, fast; 120–160 jumps/min 12.3 MET. Trampoline use for recreational purposes was found to be 3.5 MET, and for competitive purposes, 4.5 MET. Aerobic dancing has been found to have a low intensity of 5.0 MET and a high intensity of 7.3 MET [38]. Based on these findings, it has been suggested that the energy and oxygen consumption requirements of aerobic exercises in different environments and types should also be investigated in the literature [39]. The aim of this study is to determine the energy cost of kangoo jumps, an exercise that has become popular in recent years.

In the study conducted by Kang and colleagues on the effectiveness of calculations of energy expenditure using three different accelerometers, the results of the portable gas analyzer used in our study, in addition to the use of the accelerometer for monitoring energy expenditure, are similar to the results of our study. The MET values obtained in walking and running activities at speeds of 5.0 and 6.5 km/h in Kang and colleagues' study correspond to those in our study [9]. This supports the external validity of the study.

Jumping exercises involve vertical jumps at maximum or near-maximum intensities [40]. We can define it as an exercise with a high metabolic response [41]. In the first aerobic phase of the kangoo jump exercises we applied to the participants, metabolic responses were lower because the jumps performed were mostly in the walking phase. Because the exercises performed in the second phase included various hopping and jumping structures, significant increases in metabolic responses were observed. To our knowledge, no studies have been found in the literature on the metabolic responses to kangoo jump exercises. However, there are limited studies in the literature on the metabolic responses to plyometric jumps and mini-trampolines for varying durations and repetitions. In their study examining the metabolic responses to a single session of 8 sets with 3-minute passive recovery periods over 10 boxes from 80 cm height, Brown et al. (2010) found that the metabolic responses to depth jumps in women (mean peak VO₂: 39.6 ± 5.51 ml.kg⁻¹.min⁻¹; blood lactate concentrations: 9.69 ± 2.24 mmol.L⁻¹) were high, similar to our study. In another study, Draper et al. (2020) examined the physiological responses of 3 different intensities (low effort, moderate effort, and high effort) of jumping exercise performed on a mini trampoline [42]. The researchers had the participants perform the low-effort exercise training at walking intensity, the moderate-effort exercise training at walking-running intensity, and the high-effort exercise training at maximum effort. They found significant differences in the physiological responses after each 3-minute session (for low effort level (VO₂: 22 ± 5 ml.kg-1.min-1; heart rate: 118 ± 16 bpm); for moderate effort level (VO₂: 30 ± 6 ml.kg-1.min-1; heart rate: 118 ± 16 bpm); for high effort level (VO₂: 43 ± 8 ml.kg-1.min-1; heart rate: 179 ± 10 bpm)). Therefore, since kangoo jump exercises are similar to these exercise structures, metabolic responses are expected to be lower in walking steps and higher in jumping patterns.

Result

Based on the data obtained from the study, kangoo jumps exercises, alongside other gym exercises, are suitable for use in non-gym exercise environments and have been shown to optimize participants' physical fitness levels as a type of physical activity that optimizes many types of activities.

It has been determined that Kangoo Jumps exercises can be tailored to suit different load levels, that different locomotion movements can be used to apply varying load intensities, and that music rhythms can also be utilised to adjust load intensity.

The study also showed that when examining the effectiveness of kangoo jumps applications, activities can be organized within a spectrum where the intensity of the load can be adjusted according to individuals' fitness levels, as well as activities at a level comparable to most professional-level activities.

Conclusion

Accordingly, it has been determined that kangoo jumps exercises can be considered a type of exercise that allows individuals to perform their exercises according to their exercise needs in both aerobic and anaerobic phases.

In line with this study, it has been concluded that Kangoo Jumps applications are a form of exercise that can achieve a high level of physical activity and, due to being a different type of exercise, can be considered another activity that can help achieve high physical performance.

Limitations

The study is limited to the findings obtained from participants and the music and rhythmic loading used in the application. It is thought that studies based on different loading and rhythm conditions will help to increase the diversity of data and deepen the information relevant to the application. A limitation affecting generalizability is the small sample size (n=13) in this study; however, the research group states that the study still provides valuable preliminary data.

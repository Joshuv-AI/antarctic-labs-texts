Background

Descending necrotizing mediastinitis (DNM) is a fatal disease caused by odontogenic or pharyngeal infection extending into the mediastinum along the deep fascial surface, retropharyngeal space, or peritracheal space. Although the esophagus is generally excluded from the infectious spillover route, in extremely rare cases deep neck infections can cause phlegmonous esophagitis. Herein, we report a case of phlegmonous esophagitis that was difficult to differentiate from DNM.

Phlegmonous esophagitis is an extremely rare condition, with only 35 cases reported to date. Among these, only 5 cases developed secondary to a cervical abscess and were complicated by mediastinitis [1]. Typical symptoms include pharyngeal to thoracic pain and fever, which are nonspecific. Contrast-enhanced CT reveals mediastinal abscess formation, diffuse fluid lesions within the esophagus, diffuse esophageal wall thickening, and pleural effusion. Esophagogastroduodenoscopy often shows esophageal ulcers, erosions, abscesses, and perforations. While decreased systolic blood pressure and elevated creatinine levels have been reported as prognostic factors for phlegmonous esophagitis, early diagnosis is the most critical factor for improving the prognosis of this condition.

Case presentation

A 61-year-old man presented at a local hospital for evaluation of high fevers, sore throat, and dyspnea. He had an otherwise benign medical history. He had been diagnosed with acute epiglottitis and antibiotic treatment was administered following intubation. After 3 days, a cervical abscess and mediastinitis were noted, and he was referred to our hospital.

The initial physical examination revealed a body temperature of 37.4℃ and blood testing revealed a marked inflammatory reaction (white blood cell count, 13,100/µL; C-reactive protein, 13.7 mg/dL). Contrast-enhanced computed tomography showed a low-density fluid collection in the retropharyngeal space (Fig. 1A) and peri-esophageal area (Fig. 1A and B), enlargement of the entire mediastinum, and a significant pleural effusion on the right. A deep neck infection with DNM was diagnosed and the patient received emergency cervical drainage and right thoracotomy mediastinal drainage. Additionally, a tracheostomy was performed to manage airway edema, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and sputum retention. A turbid pleural effusion was present in the right thoracic cavity and the superior mediastinal fat tissue was slightly thickened by inflammation (Fig. 1C), and the entire esophagus was edematous and swollen (Fig. 1D). A small amount of pus was discharged by mediastinal drainage around the upper mediastinum. The intraoperative findings were atypical for DNM, and phlegmonous esophagitis was suspected. On postoperative day 8 an esophagogastroduodenoscopy revealed that the esophageal mucosa had extensively desquamated, forming a false lumen (Fig. 2A). The patient was diagnosed with a phlegmonous esophagitis and an intramural abscess, and treatment with antibiotics and fasting was continued. For the first 8 days after admission, the patient was administered the broad spectrum antibiotic piperacillin-tazobactam. Subsequently, Streptococcus constellatus was detected in a culture test from the neck abscess, and sensitive levofloxacin was administered for the following 16 days. During the fasting period, the patient was managed with Total Parenteral Nutrition and lipid emulsions. The medical condition improved steadily after treatment and esophagogastroduodenoscopy confirmed the esophageal epithelium covered the entire lumen (Fig. 2B). The patient began oral intake on postoperative day 21 and discharged 35 days after surgery. 2 years have passed since the surgery with no disease recurrence.

Discussion

Only five cases of infection extending from a deep cervical abscess into the esophagus and mediastinum have been reported [2–6]. There are no reported gender differences, mean age was 59 years, and 2 patients also had a history of diabetes. Surgical treatments were performed in four patients, of whom 3 received mediastinal drainage and 1underwent cervical drainage. Of the five patients, one died [6]. Phlegmonous esophagitis is a rare disease that can have a fatal outcome with perforation, mediastinitis, pleuritis, and empyema [2]. Among cases of esophageal cellulitis, a condition resembling DNM is rarely observed, where inflammation spreads from the neck and concurrent mediastinitis develops, as in this case. Five cases have been reported to date, one of which resulted in death, suggesting this represents a particularly severe condition requiring caution. We therefore propose designating such a condition as descending necrotizing esophagitis.

Although the optimal treatment for phlegmonous esophagitis has not been established, esophagostomy and endoscopic esophageal mucosal incision have been reported to be effective in some cases [2, 3]. In the present case we suspected DNM associated with a cervical abscess and the preceding surgical treatment of the cervicothoracic region. However, the intraoperative findings revealed the possibility of phlegmonous esophagitis. A review of 35 cases of phlegmonous esophagitis [1] showed that the time from diagnosis to intervention tended to be shorter in survivors compared to non-survivors (2 days vs. 5 days), indicating that diagnostic delay directly impacts prognosis. Furthermore, no significant difference in prognosis was observed between cases treated conservatively and those undergoing endoscopic drainage. In our case, diagnosis took 8 days; however, the initiation of antibiotic therapy upon admission may have contributed to the favorable outcome.

Conclusions

As in the present case, the pathophysiology of infection extending from a cervical abscess into the esophagus and mediastinum might be termed “descending necrotizing esophagitis,” which is a rare but serious disease with a potentially fatal outcome. Timely diagnosis and early intervention are critically important in this condition. Radiographic findings and intraoperative intrapleural findings are useful for accurate diagnosis.

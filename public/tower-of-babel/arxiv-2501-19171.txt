Plasma dynamics in thin domains
Darryl D. Holm, Ruiao Hu, Oliver D. Street

Abstract
In the present work, we study the geometric structures of the Rotating Shallow Water Magnetohydrodynamics (RSW-MHD) equations through a Lie group invariant Euler–Poincaré variational principle. In this geometric framework, we derive new, structure-preserving stochastic RSW-MHD models by introducing stochastic perturbations to the Lie–Poisson structure of the deterministic RSW-MHD equations. The resulting stochastic RSW-MHD equations provide new capabilities for potential application to uncertainty quantification and data assimilation, for example, in space plasma (space weather) and solar physics, particularly in solar tachocline dynamics.



1
Introduction

Astrophysical plasma dynamics often takes place in thin domains (i.e., domains of small aspect ratio) such as accretion disks, planetary atmospheres and transition zones in stars. Hence, a variational derivation of the equations and a discussion of the geometric properties of the dynamics of plasmas in thin domains may provide a useful setting for developing new capabilities in astrophysics.

In particular,
Gilman, 2000
introduced equations for rotating shallow water magnetohydrodynamics (RSW-MHD) as a model of solar tachocline dynamics. The solar tachocline is a relatively thin layer near the Sun’s surface (about 1/20 of the solar radius) comprised of magnetised quasineutral plasma which bridges between the Sun’s convective zone and its radiative zone
(
Hughes et al., 2007
)
. Since their derivation, the RSW-MHD equations have been investigated from several theoretical and modelling points of view. In particular, the Green–Naghdi approach for including dispersive effects was investigated in
Dellar, 2003b
. Studies of linear and non-linear waves in RSW-MHD have been treated by
Schecter et al., 2001
and investigations of shear-flow instabilities by
Mak et al., 2016
. The RSW-MHD system has also been shown to be hyperbolic and possess a Hamiltonian structure
(
De Sterck, 2001
;
Dellar, 2002
;
Rossmanith, 2003
)
. Extensions to multi-layer RSW-MHD equations have also been documented
(
Hunter, 2015
;
Zeitlin, 2013
;
Alonso-Orán, 2021
)
.

The present paper has two main objectives:

(i)

Use Lagrangian reduction by Lie symmetry in Hamilton’s variational principle to derive and investigate the geometric structure and solution properties of the deterministic RSW-MHD equations;

(ii)

Derive stochastic methods for quantifying uncertainty and assimilating data that preserve the geometric structure and solution properties found in (i) for the deterministic RSW-MHD equations.

The non-dimensional form of the momentum equation (
2.1
) can be found by scaling with the following units. Let
L
be the characteristic horizontal length scale,
H
be the typical depth,
U
be the characteristic velocity scale and
B_{0}
the characteristic value of the magnetic field. Two different Rossby numbers that measure the relative strength of rotation can be found as
\mathrm{Ro}=U/(fL)
and
\mathrm{Ro}_{m}=B_{0}/(fL)
, which are the ‘ordinary’ Rossby number and the ‘magnetic’ Rossby number, respectively
(
Zeitlin, 2013
;
Lahaye and Zeitlin, 2022
)
. Additionally, the gravitational effects are characteristic by the Froude number
\mathrm{Fr}^{2}=U^{2}/(gH)
. Using these non-dimensional numbers, the non-dimensional form of (
2.1

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}f\bm{u}^{\perp}=-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\nabla(\eta-{h}(\bm{x}))+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\,.

(2.2)

Here,
\mu:=\mathrm{Ro}/\mathrm{Ro}_{m}
is the ratio of the fluid and magnetic Rossby numbers. For balance between rotational and gravitational effects, one requires
\mathrm{Ro}/\mathrm{Fr}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Two advection relations hold as auxiliary equations for the RSW-MHD motion (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta+\nabla\cdot(\eta\bm{u})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{B}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}-\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(2.4)

Together, these auxiliary equations imply preservation of the condition

\nabla\cdot(\eta\bm{B})=0\,,

(2.5)

which can therefore be regarded as a non-dynamical constraint on the initial values.

2.1
A variational principle for the RSW-MHD equations

The advection equations (
2.3
) and (

) may each be written in a coordinate free geometric form as

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})(\eta\,d^{2}x)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})B

\displaystyle=0\,,

(2.7)

where
\eta\,d^{2}x\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is a volume form and
u,B\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
are vector fields which may be expressed in the Cartesian coordinate system as
u=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla
and
B=\bm{B}\cdot\nabla

\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta d^{2}x)=0\,.

(2.8)

Remark 2.1
.

By using the advection equations in their geometric form (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), one may prove advection of the divergence
\operatorname{div}(\eta\bm{B})\,d^{2}x=\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta\,d^{2}x)
. Indeed, Cartan’s formula for the Lie derivative gives

\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta\,d^{2}x)=d\left(B\intprod(\eta\,d^{2}x)\right)+B\intprod d(\eta\,d^{2}x)=d\left(B\intprod(\eta\,d^{2}x)\right)\,,

since the exterior derivative of a volume form is zero. The exterior derivative commutes with both the time derivative and the Lie derivative. Consequently, the advection of
B
and
\eta\,d^{2}x
implies that

(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta\,d^{2}x)=0\,.

Therefore, if the quantity
\operatorname{div}(\eta\bm{B})
vanishes initially, then it will remain zero for all time.

As shown by
Holm et al., 1998
, these advected quantities break the symmetry of the Lagrangian in Hamilton’s principle under the full diffeomorphism group which thereby leads to Lie-Poisson equations on the semidirect product between the co-algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{*}=\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)
and the space of advected quantities
V^{*}=\mathfrak{X}(M)\oplus\Omega^{2}(M)
. To construct the semidirect product space
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V
, we need a representation of the Lie group
G
, the diffeomorphism group
\textrm{Diff}(M)
, on
V
. The representation of the diffeomorphisms on
\Omega^{2}(M)
is by pullback, and on
\mathfrak{X}(M)
by the Lie group adjoint representation,
\mathrm{Ad}
. The corresponding infinitesimal action of
\mathfrak{X}(M)
on
\Omega^{2}(M)
is by Lie derivative and its action on
\mathfrak{X}(M)
is by adjoint representation,
\mathrm{ad}
. The dynamical equations arising from Hamilton’s principle with broken symmetry emerge as Euler-Poincaré equations with advected quantities for a Lagrangian
\ell:\mathfrak{X}(M)\times\Omega^{2}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}(M)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Indeed, the RSW-MHD equations (
2.2
)-(
2.4
) are the Euler-Poincaré equations corresponding to Hamilton’s principle applied to the following action integral in Cartesian coordinates

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u,\eta,B)\,dt

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{x})-\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-2{h}(\bm{x}))\right)\eta\,d^{2}x\,dt\,,

where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes the horizontal cross-section.

Remark 2.2

(The coordinate free action)
.

When the manifold is equipped with a suitable metric, the vector fields
u\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and
B\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
possess associated
1
-forms,
u^{\flat}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
B^{\flat}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. In Euclidean domains this association is natural, since if
u=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla
then
u^{\flat}=\bm{u}\cdot d\bm{x}
where
d\bm{x}
is the dual basis to
\nabla
. In this natural association, the coefficients of the basis elements do not change when using the ‘musical isomorphism’ to transform between vector fields and
1
-forms. As such, the terms in the action can be simply expressed in a coordinate free exterior calculus notation as

|\bm{u}|^{2}=u\intprod u^{\flat}\,,\quad|\bm{B}|^{2}=B\intprod B^{\flat}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\bm{u}\cdot\bm{R}=u\intprod R\,,

(2.10)

where
\intprod
denotes the insertion of a vector field into a
1
-form and
R
is a
1
-form such that
R=\bm{R}(\bm{x})\cdot d\bm{x}
. This identification also assists in understanding the corresponding geometric spaces of the variational derivatives.

Remark 2.3

(The form of the Lagrangian)
.

The Lagrangian in (
2.9
) is the standard Lagrangian for the rotating shallow water equations, augmented with a contribution from the energy density of the
\bm{B}
-field. This is the construction applied by
Holm et al., 1998
for an Euler equation coupled to a magnetic field.

Remark 2.4

(Weakly versus strongly magnetized regimes)
.

The parameter
\mu
controls the strength of the magnetic forces relative to the gravitational forces and the Coriolis force. When
\mathrm{Ro}/\mathrm{Fr}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the dynamics are in geostrophic balance. Additionally, when
\mathrm{Ro}/\mu^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the dynamics are in a strongly magnetized regime where the balance with the Coriolis force involves both the hydrostatic pressure gradient force and the magnetic
J\times B
force. In the weakly magnetized regime when
\mathrm{Ro}/\mu^{2}\ll 1
the balance with the Coriolis force involves only the the hydrostatic pressure gradient force.

The RSW-MHD equations (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) will be derived by first evaluating the variational derivatives for the Lagrangian in the action integral (
2.9
). Namely,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}

\displaystyle=\left(\bm{u}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{R}(\bm{x})\right)\cdot\mathbf{d}\bm{x}=:\bm{V}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=:V^{\flat}\in\Omega^{1}(M)\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{x})-\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-{h}(\bm{x}))\right)=:\beta(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega^{0}(M)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta B}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\otimes\eta d^{2}x=:\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}B^{\flat}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\,,

where
\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)
denotes the dual space to the space of vector fields, which contains
1

\eta

\displaystyle\delta u

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\xi-\operatorname{ad}_{u}\xi\,,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\delta\eta\,d^{2}x

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(\eta\,d^{2}x)=-\operatorname{div}(\eta\bm{\xi})d^{2}x\,,

\displaystyle\delta B

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}B=-[\xi,B]=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}B=(-\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}+\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{\xi})\cdot\nabla\,,

for an arbitrary vector field
\xi=\bm{\xi}\cdot\nabla\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the Lie bracket of vector fields. The vector field

corresponds to a curve
\phi_{t}\in{\rm DiffM}
such that

in diffeomorphism group arbitrarily such that the variations are fixed at the endpoints. Within the variational principle, we will often have need to integrate these terms by parts. The following ‘diamond’ notation is introduced to denote this process

\left<\lambda\diamond a\,,\,\xi\right>_{\mathfrak{X}^{*}\times\mathfrak{X}}=-\left<\lambda\,,\,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}a\right>_{V\times V^{*}}\,,

(2.13)

for an advected quantity

and a vector field

\lambda_{B}\diamond B=-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\lambda_{B}\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lambda_{\eta}\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)=d\lambda_{\eta}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\,,

(2.14)

where
\lambda_{B}\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)
and
\lambda_{\eta}\in\Omega^{0}(M)
. To compute these diamond terms, we have integrated by parts, made use of the form of the Lie derivative of a volume form, and exploited the antisymmetry of the adjoint representation as a map
\operatorname{ad}_{\square}\square:\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}
. Furthermore, we have introduced the coadjoint representation of vector fields on the space of
1
-form densities,
\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\square}\square:\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{*}
, which is the dual operator of
\operatorname{ad}
with respect to the duality pairing defined by
L^{2}
spatial integration.

Remark 2.5
.

For a closed material loop of fluid,
c(u)
, moving with the flow of
u
, the Kelvin-Noether theorem for the rotating shallow water MHD equations may be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\oint_{c(u)}\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\right)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(u)}\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\right)=\oint_{c(u)}\left(d\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{B}B^{\flat}\right)=-\oint_{c(u)}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{B}B^{\flat}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(u)}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B})\cdot d\bm{x}=\oint_{c(u)}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}((\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\bm{B}^{\perp})\cdot d\bm{x}\,,

where from the second to the first line we have converted to a vector calculus notation and the final equality is due to the fact that
\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}
and
(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\bm{B}^{\perp}

\bm{B}

\bm{B}_{3}=(\bm{B},0)

\nabla_{3}\times\bm{B}_{3}=(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\widehat{z}
. Hence,
(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\bm{B}^{\perp}=-\bm{B}_{3}\times(\nabla_{3}\times\bm{B}_{3})
. Notice that in the two dimensional case, whereby the dynamics is occurring on an embedded plane within the three dimensional domain, it is not possible for

Stream function version of Lagrangian

As demonstrated in Remark

As the terms involving the shallow water variables
u,R,\eta
are the same as before, the momentum equation in vector calculus notation reads as

\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}f\bm{u}^{\perp}=-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\nabla(\eta-h(\bm{x}))-\frac{1}{\eta\mu^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\right)\nabla\psi+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla^{\perp}\psi|^{2}}{2\eta^{2}}\right)\,.

(2.26)

Recalling the definition of
\psi
in (
2.21
), one has that
\nabla\psi=-\eta\bm{B}^{\perp}
, and hence the final two terms may be written as

(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\bm{B}^{\perp}+\nabla\left(\frac{|\bm{B}|^{2}}{2}\right)=\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\,.

(2.27)

Consequently, the momentum equation in (
2.26
) is equivalent to both (
2.1
) and the Euler-Poincaré equation (
2.19
).

2.2
Hamiltonian structure of RSW-MHD equations

In this section, the Hamiltonian Lie-Poisson form of the RSW-MHD equations is derived via a Legendre transformation. The Lie-Poisson structure of these equations is interesting (and may be helpful) in studying a variety of astrophysical phenomena, including the dynamics of gravity waves and Alfvén waves
(
Zeitlin, 2024
;
Petrosyan et al., 2020
)
in plasmas such as the solar tachocline
(
Gilman, 2000
;
Miesch, 2005
)
.

The Legendre transformation of the Lagrangian corresponding to the action (
2.9
) yields the Hamiltonian in terms of the magnetic vector field,
B=\bm{B}\cdot\nabla
, the columnar volume,
\eta\,d^{2}x
, and a momentum variable defined as

m=\bm{m}\cdot d\bm{x}\otimes d^{2}x=:\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}=u^{\flat}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\,.

(2.28)

Explicitly, the Hamiltonian is

H(m,\eta,B)=\left<m\,,\,u\right>-\ell(u,\eta,B)=\int\frac{1}{2\eta}\left|\bm{m}-\frac{\eta\bm{R}}{\mathrm{Ro}}\right|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-2h(\bm{x}))\eta\,d^{2}x\,.

(2.29)

It should be remarked that the momentum variable,
m
, is a
1
-form density and, together with
R\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x
, appears in the first term of the Hamiltonian. This has been written in a vector form, in terms of
\bm{m}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
, and a function,
\eta
, for clarity. Formally, this term represents the following

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\eta\,d^{2}x}\left(m-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\right)\right)^{\sharp}\intprod\left(\frac{1}{\eta\,d^{2}x}\left(m-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\right)\right)\eta\,d^{2}x=\frac{1}{2}(u\intprod u^{\flat})\eta\,d^{2}x\,.

Varying this term in
m
produces the vector field
u
. Variations of the Hamiltonian are therefore obtained as

\frac{\delta H}{\delta m}=u\,,\quad\frac{\delta H}{\delta B}=\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}B^{\flat}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\delta H}{\delta\eta}=-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot{\mathbf{R}}+\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-h(\bm{x}))\,.

(2.30)

The semidirect-product Lie-Poisson equations are then given in standard form by

\partial_{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
B\\
\eta\,d^{2}x\end{array}\right)=-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&-\operatorname{ad}_{B}^{*}\square&\eta\,d(\square)\otimes d^{2}x\\
-\operatorname{ad}_{\square}B&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta\,d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta H/\delta m\\
\delta H/\delta B\\
\delta H/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\,.

(2.31)

The matrix operator here denotes the coadjoint action of the whole semidirect product algebra
\mathfrak{X}(M)\ltimes(\Omega^{0}(M)\oplus\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M))
on its dual, and corresponds to the Lie-Poisson bracket associated with the standard Lie bracket on the space
\mathfrak{X}(M)\ltimes(\Omega^{0}(M)\oplus\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M))
. A short calculation verifies that these equations are equivalent to those obtained from the action (
2.9
).

A stream function for the magnetic field.

We may write the Hamiltonian in terms of the function
\psi
, rather than the vector field
B
. This Hamiltonian is

H(m,\eta,B)=\widetilde{H}(m,\eta,\psi)=\int\frac{1}{2\eta}\left|\bm{m}-\frac{\eta\bm{R}}{\mathrm{Ro}}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\eta\mu^{2}}|\nabla^{\perp}\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-2h(\bm{x}))\eta\,d^{2}x\,.

(2.32)

The variations are computed as

\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta m}=u\,,\quad\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\psi}=-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\nabla^{\perp}\psi\right)\,d^{2}x\,,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\eta}=-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot{\mathbf{R}}-\frac{|\nabla^{\perp}\psi|^{2}}{2\eta^{2}\mu^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-h(\bm{x}))\,.

(2.33)

Since
\eta
and
B
are advected quantities, it follows that
\psi\in\Omega^{0}(M)
must be an advected
0
-form (function). The Lie derivative of a function
\psi
with respect to a vector field
u=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\in\mathfrak{X}
takes the form
\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi=\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi
. Hence, for functions, the diamond operator defined in equation (
2.13
) is computed as

\lambda_{\psi}\diamond\psi=-\lambda_{\psi}d\psi\,,

(2.34)

where
\lambda_{\psi}\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is a volume form. We define the following semidirect product Lie-Poisson system

\partial_{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
\psi\\
\eta\,d^{2}x\end{array}\right)=-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&\square\diamond\psi&\square\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}\psi&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta m\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\psi\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\,.

(2.35)

This equation is the Lie-Poisson equation corresponding to the Lie-Poisson bracket associated with the standard semidirect product Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{X}(M)\ltimes(\Omega^{2}(M)\oplus\Omega^{0}(M))
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The shallow water MHD equations possesses the following Casimir invariances,

\displaystyle C(q,\psi,\eta)=\int\eta\phi(\psi)+\eta q\varphi(\psi)d^{2}x

(2.36)

for arbitrary smooth functions
\phi
and
\varphi
. The quantity
q
denotes
the potential vorticity (PV) associated with the flow, which is defined as

\displaystyle q=\frac{1}{\eta}\big(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{u}-\frac{f}{Ro}\big)\,.

(2.37)

Proof.

One can transform the Lie-Poisson bracket (
2.35
) to the potential vorticity bracket for the thermal rotating shallow water (TRSW) equation. As in
Holm and Long, 1989
, we use the following transformation
(m,\psi,\eta)\rightarrow(u,\psi,\eta)
, whose Jacobian is given by

\displaystyle J=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\eta}&0&-\frac{m}{\eta^{2}}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad J^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\eta}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
-\frac{m}{\eta^{2}}&0&1\end{pmatrix}\,.\qquad

Let
\mathcal{J}_{LP}
denote the Lie-Poisson bracket appearing in (
2.35
). In coordinate notation, the potential vorticity bracket
\mathcal{J}_{PV}
may be calculated, as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{PV}

\displaystyle=J\circ\mathcal{J}_{LP}\circ J^{T}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\eta}&0&-\frac{m_{i}}{\eta^{2}}\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m_{j}\partial_{i}\square+\partial_{j}(m_{i}\square)&-\partial_{i}\psi&\eta\partial_{i}\square\\
\partial_{j}\psi&0&0\\
\partial_{j}(\eta\square)&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\eta}&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
-\frac{m_{j}}{\eta^{2}}&0&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{j}(u_{i}+R_{i}/Ro)-\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{i}(u_{j}+R_{j}/Ro)&\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{i}\psi&\partial_{i}\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{j}\psi&0&0\\
\partial_{j}&0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}q\times&\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{i}\psi&\partial_{i}\\
-\frac{1}{\eta}\partial_{j}\psi&0&0\\
\partial_{j}&0&0\end{pmatrix}\,,

where in the last equality the two dimensional expressions obtain the potential vorticity bracket for the TRSW equations. From the standard literature on the Casimirs for TRSW equations, e.g.,
Zeitlin, 2018
;
Holm et al., 2021
, one obtains the required results.
∎

Remark 2.6

(Equvalence of Poisson brackets)
.

Noting that
B={\eta}^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}\psi
, we have

\displaystyle J_{PV}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-q&-B_{2}&\partial_{1}\\
q&0&B_{1}&\partial_{2}\\
B_{2}&-B_{1}&0&0\\
\partial_{1}&\partial_{2}&0&0\end{pmatrix}

which is the the same Poisson bracket that appears in
Dellar, 2002
.

3
Thermal effects in shallow water magnetohydrodynamics

The Thermal Rotating Shallow Water MHD (TRSW–MHD) model is an extension of the RSW–MHD model to include horizontal gradients of buoyancy
(
Dellar, 2003a
)
. The TRSW-MHD equations modify the RSW–MHD equations to include the variable buoyancy
b(\bm{x},t)=\rho(\bm{x},t))/\bar{\rho}
, where
\rho
is the (time and space dependent) mass density and
\bar{\rho}
is the uniform reference mass density. The non-dimensional TRSW–MHD equation for the fluid velocity
\bm{u}
is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{u}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{R})\bm{u}^{\perp}=-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(1+\mathfrak{s}b)\nabla(\eta-h(\bm{x}))-\frac{\mathfrak{s}}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\eta\nabla b+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\,,

(3.1)

where the non-dimensional number
\mathfrak{s}
is the stratification parameter. Three advection relations now hold as auxiliary equations for the TRSW–MHD velocity equation (
3.1
). Namely,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta+\nabla\cdot(\eta\bm{u})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{B}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}-\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla b

\displaystyle=0\,.

(3.4)

The additional auxiliary equation for the positive scalar function
b
still preserves the divergence condition (
2.5
),
\nabla\cdot(\eta\bm{B})=0
, which therefore can still be regarded as a non-dynamical constraint on the initial values of
\eta
and
\bm{B}
which does not involve the scalar advected quantity
b
.

Lagrangian formulation.

The TRSW–MHD equations (
3.1
)–(
3.4
) can be derived as Euler-Poincaré equations with advected quantities by considering the following action functional

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u,\eta,B,b)\,dt

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{x})-\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}\right)\eta-\frac{1}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(1+\mathfrak{s}b)\left(\eta^{2}-2\eta{h}(\bm{x})\right)\,d^{2}x\,dt\,.

Compared to the RSW–MHD Lagrangian (
2.9
), the TRSW–MHD Lagrangian (
3.5
) is the same except the factor
1/\mathrm{Fr}^{2}
is replaced by
(1+\mathfrak{s}b)/\mathrm{Fr}^{2}
. The variational derivatives of the action integral (
3.5
) are evaluated as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}&=\left(\bm{u}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{R}(\bm{x})\right)\cdot\mathbf{d}\bm{x}=:\bm{V}(\bm{x},t)\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=:V^{\flat}\in\Omega^{1}(M)\,,\\
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}&=\left(\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{R}(\bm{x})-\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(1+\mathfrak{s}b)(\eta-{h}(\bm{x}))\right)=:\beta(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega^{0}(M)\,,\\
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta B}&=\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\mathrm{d}\bm{x}\otimes\eta d^{2}x=:\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}B^{\flat}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\,,\\
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b}&=-\left(\frac{\mathfrak{s}}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\eta(\eta-2h(\bm{x}))\right)\otimes d^{2}x=:-\gamma(\bm{x},t)\otimes d^{2}x\in\Omega^{2}(M)\,.\end{split}

The Euler-Poincaré motion equation follows by considering
\delta S=0
with the constrained variations (
2.12
) as well as
\delta b=-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}b
where
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is the same arbitrary vector field appearing in (
2.12
). The form of the constrained variations follow from the advection equations (
3.2
), (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), which can each be written in a coordinate-free geometric form as

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})(\eta\,d^{2}x)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})B

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u})b

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.8)

and as before, the condition (
2.5
) may be expressed as
\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta d^{2}x)=0
. Inserting the constrained variations into Hamilton’s principle for the TRSW-MHD action yields

\displaystyle 0=\delta S

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}\,,\,\partial_{t}\xi-\operatorname{ad}_{u}\xi\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}\,,\,-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta B}\,,\,\operatorname{ad}_{\xi}B\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b}\,,\,-\mathcal{L}_{\xi}b\right>\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left<-\left(\partial_{t}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}\right)\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}+\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)+\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta B}\diamond B+\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b}\diamond b\,,\,\xi\right>\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left<-\left(\partial_{t}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}\right)\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}+d\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta B}-\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b}db\,,\,\xi\right>\,dt\,.

The fundamental lemma of calculus of variations then yields the Euler-Poincaré equation of motion

\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u}\right)\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}=d\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\eta}-\frac{1}{\eta}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta B}-\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b}db\,,

(3.9)

which is to be considered along with the auxiliary advection equations (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), where we have divided the motion equation through by the volume form
\eta\,d^{2}x
. Through a similar calculation as for the RSW-MHD case above, one may show the equivalence between the vector calculus version of the TRSW-MHD momentum equation (
3.1
) with the Euler-Poincaré equation (
3.9
).

Remark 3.1
.

For a closed material loop of fluid,
c(u)
, moving with the flow of
u
, the Kelvin-Noether theorem for the TRSW-MHD equations (
3.9
) is

\frac{d}{dt}\oint_{c(u)}\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R^{\flat}\right)=\oint_{c(u)}\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B})\cdot d\bm{x}+\oint_{c(u)}\frac{\mathfrak{s}}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\left(\eta-2{h}(\bm{x})\right)\nabla b\cdot d\bm{x}\,.

(3.10)

The TRSW-MHD Kelvin–Noether theorem implies that thermal gradients (i.e.,
\nabla b
) and magnetic forces can create circulation. Moreover, with the appropriate scaling of
\mu
and
\mathrm{Fr}
, the magnetic and gravitational forces can play independent roles on equal footing and both be involved in the balance with the Coriolis force.

Hamiltonian formulation.

The Hamiltonian formulation of TRSW-MHD may be obtained in a similar way to the Hamiltonian formulation of RSW-MHD system in Section
2.2
. Through a Legendre transform, the TRSW-MHD Hamiltonian is calculated from the Lagrangian as

\begin{split}\mathfrak{h}(m,\eta,B,b)&=\left<m\,,\,u\right>-\ell(u,\eta,B,b)\\
&=\int\frac{1}{2\eta}\left|\bm{m}-\frac{\eta\bm{R}}{\mathrm{Ro}}\right|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}+\frac{1+\mathfrak{s}b}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\left(\eta^{2}-2\eta{h}(\bm{x})\right)\,d^{2}x\,,\end{split}

(3.11)

where
m=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}=\bm{m}\cdot d\bm{x}\otimes d^{2}x=\left(u^{\flat}+R^{\flat}/\mathrm{Ro}\right)\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x
is the same momentum map as in the RSW-MHD case (
2.28
). The variational derivatives of
\mathfrak{h}
can found as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\delta\mathfrak{h}}{\delta m}=u\,,\quad\frac{\delta\mathfrak{h}}{\delta B}=\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}B^{\flat}\otimes\eta\,d^{2}x\,,\quad\frac{\delta\mathfrak{h}}{\delta b}=\frac{\mathfrak{s}}{2\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\left(\eta^{2}-2\eta h(\bm{x})\right)\,,\\
&\quad\hbox{and}\quad\frac{\delta\mathfrak{h}}{\delta\eta}=-\frac{1}{2}|\bm{u}|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}\cdot{\mathbf{R}}+\frac{1}{2\mu^{2}}|\bm{B}|^{2}+\frac{1+\mathfrak{s}b}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}(\eta-h(\bm{x}))\,,\end{split}

(3.12)

such that the TRSW-MHD system (
3.6
)-(
3.9
) can be assembled into the following Lie-Poisson Hamiltonian system,

\partial_{t}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
B\\
\eta\,d^{2}x\\
b\end{array}\right)=-\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\square&\square\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)&\square\diamond b\\
-\operatorname{ad}_{\square}B&0&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta d^{2}x)&0&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}b&0&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\mathfrak{h}/\delta m\\
\delta\mathfrak{h}/\delta B\\
\delta\mathfrak{h}/\delta\eta\\
\delta\mathfrak{h}/\delta b\end{array}\right)\,.

(3.13)

The matrix in equation (
3.13
) is the coadjoint action of
\mathfrak{X}(M)\ltimes(\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\oplus\Omega^{0}(M)\oplus\Omega^{2}(M))
on its dual.

4
Stochastic rotating shallow water magnetohydrodynamics

In this section we consider two complementary approaches for introduucing structure preserving stochastic perturbations to the RSW-MHD equations based on its Lie-Poisson Hamiltonian structure. They are the Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT) and the Stochastic Forcing by Lie Transport (SFLT) approaches.
The SALT approach, which introduces stochastic transport noise, as well as stochastic potential energy
(
Holm, 2015
;
Street and Takao, 2024
)
, preserves the property of coadjoint orbit motion of the deterministic dynamics as well as the Casimir invariants. The SFLT approach introduces stochastic forcing, as well as stochastic material entrainment effects
(
Holm and Hu, 2021
)
, and preserves the energy conservation property of the deterministic dynamics.
For a variational derivation of the SALT and SFLT type perturbations applied to Hall magnetohydrodynamics, see
Holm et al., 2024
.

4.1
Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT).

The general form of the SALT perturbations can be found by considering a family of Hamiltonians defined on the Lie co-algebra
\mathfrak{X}(M)^{*}\ltimes(\Omega^{2}(M)\oplus\mathfrak{X}(M))
, denoted by
\{\widetilde{H}_{i}(m,\eta,B)\}_{i=1}^{N}
, as well as a family of i.i.d driving Brownian motions
\{W^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.
The Poisson structure (
2.31
) of the SALT structure-preserving stochastic RSW-MHD equations is

\displaystyle\diff\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
B\\
\eta\,d^{2}x\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&-\operatorname{ad}_{B}^{*}\square&\eta\,d(\square)\otimes d^{2}x\\
-\operatorname{ad}_{\square}B&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta\,d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta H/\delta m\\
\delta H/\delta B\\
\delta H/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\,\diff t

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&-\operatorname{ad}_{B}^{*}\square&\eta\,d(\square)\otimes d^{2}x\\
-\operatorname{ad}_{\square}B&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta\,d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta H_{i}/\delta m\\
\delta H_{i}/\delta B\\
\delta H_{i}/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}\,,

where
\circ
denotes Fisk-Stratonovich integration. Expanding out the Poisson bracket to individual components of the Lie co-algebra, SALT RSW-MHD advection equations are modified to include stochastic transport,

\displaystyle\diff\,(\eta\,d^{2}x)+\mathcal{L}_{u}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\,\diff t+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta m}}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}=0\,,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\diff B+\mathcal{L}_{u}B\,\diff t+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta m}}B\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}=0\,,

(4.3)

and the stochastic momentum equation is modified as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\diff m+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}m\,\diff t+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta m}}m\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}&=-\eta d\left(\frac{\delta H}{\delta\eta}\,\diff t+\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta\eta}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\right)\otimes d^{2}x\\
&\qquad+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\left(\frac{\delta H}{\delta B}\,\diff t+\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta B}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\right)\,.\end{split}

(4.4)

Under the stochastic advection of
\eta
and
B
, the weighted incompressibility condition (
2.5
) of the
B
-field is preserved. That is, when
\nabla\cdot(\eta\bm{B})\,d^{2}x=\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta d^{2}x)
is initially zero, it remains so in the subsequent motion. This can be verified via a direct calculation similar to that in Remark
2.1
to find

\displaystyle\diff\left(\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\right)+\mathcal{L}_{u}\left(\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\right)\diff t+\mathcal{L}_{\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta m}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{B}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\right)\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}=0\,.

For the concrete choice of the Hamiltonian perturbations
{H}_{i}(m,\eta,B)
where
{H}_{i}
are linear in
(m,\eta,B)
, i.e.,

H_{i}(m,\eta,B)=\int_{M}\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{i}+\bm{B}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}+\eta\gamma_{i}\,d^{2}x\,,\quad i=1,\ldots,N\,,

(4.5)

for some prescribed
\xi_{i}(\bm{x},t)\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
,
D_{i}(\bm{x},t)\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)
and
\gamma_{i}(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega^{0}(M)
, the SALT RSW-MHD equations given by equations (
4.2
)-(
4.4
) can be expressed component-wise in vector calculus notation as follows

\displaystyle\diff\eta+\nabla\cdot(\eta\,\bm{u})\,\diff t+\nabla\cdot(\eta\,\bm{\xi}_{i})\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}=0\,,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\diff\bm{B}+\left(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}-\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}\right)\,\diff t+\left(\bm{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}-\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{\xi}_{i}\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}=0\,,

(4.7)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\diff\bm{u}+(\bm{u}\diff t+\bm{\xi}_{i}\circ\diff W_{t}^{i})\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\frac{f}{\mathrm{Ro}}(\bm{u}^{\perp}\,\diff t+\bm{\xi}_{i}^{\perp}\circ\diff W_{t}^{i})+\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\nabla(\eta-h(\bm{x}))-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\right)\,\diff t\\
&\qquad=-\left(u_{j}\nabla\xi^{j}_{i}+\nabla\left(\bm{\xi}_{i}\cdot\frac{{\mathbf{R}}}{\mathrm{Ro}}\right)+\nabla\gamma_{i}-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{D}_{i}+(D_{i})_{j}\nabla B^{j}+\bm{D}_{i}\nabla\cdot\bm{B}\right)\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}\,.\end{split}

(4.8)

In (
4.8
), the multi-index
\xi^{j}_{i}
are the components of the perturbation vector field
\xi_{i}=\bm{\xi}_{i}\cdot\nabla=\xi^{j}_{i}\partial_{j}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
and the multi-index
(D_{i})_{j}
are the components of the perturbation to the magnetic field
D_{i}=\bm{D}_{i}\cdot d\bm{x}\otimes d^{2}x=(D_{i})_{j}dx^{j}\otimes d^{2}x
.

To better mimic the form of the deterministic RSW-MHD equations such that the stochastic perturbations can be attributed as the perturbations to the kinetic energy, gravitational and magnetic potential energies, one can make following particular choice of
\widetilde{H}_{i}
as

H_{i}(m,\eta,B)=\int_{M}\frac{\bm{m}}{\eta}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{i}+\eta\bm{B}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}+\eta\gamma_{i}\,d^{2}x\,,\quad i=1,\ldots,N\,.

(4.9)

The variational derivatives of
H_{i}
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta\bm{m}}=\frac{\bm{\xi}_{i}}{\eta}=:\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{i}\,,\quad\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta\bm{B}}=\eta\bm{D}_{i}\,,\quad\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta\eta}=-\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\xi}_{i}+\bm{B}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}=-(\bm{u}+{\mathbf{R}})\cdot\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{i}+\bm{B}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}\,.\end{split}

In this case, the momentum equation (
4.4
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\diff\bm{u}+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}\,\diff t+\frac{f}{\mathrm{Ro}}(\bm{u}^{\perp}\,\diff t+\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{i}^{\perp}\circ\diff W_{t}^{i})+\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{u}\right)\widetilde{\bm{\xi}}_{i}^{\perp}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad=\left(-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\nabla(\eta-h(\bm{x}))+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\right)\,\diff t+\left(-\nabla\gamma_{i}+\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}\right)\bm{B}^{\perp}\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}\,.\end{split}

(4.10)

where we have heavily used the two equivalent forms of the Lie derivative in two dimensions, given in equation (
2.18
), to transform the variables in the Lie derivative between
\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{v}
and
(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{v})\bm{u}^{\perp}
. In particular, the stochastic terms in the equation in this setting take the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{\xi}_{i}}(u^{\flat}+{\mathbf{R}}\cdot d\bm{x})-\nabla\left((\bm{u}+{\mathbf{R}})\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{i}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}=\left((\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{R})\bm{\zeta}_{i}^{\perp}+(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{u})\bm{\zeta}_{i}^{\perp}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{B}\bm{D}_{i}\cdot d\bm{x}-\nabla\left(\bm{B}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}=\left(\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}\right)\bm{B}^{\perp}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}\,,

and one sees a stochastic contribution to the
J\times B
force in the last term.

Remark 4.1

(Conservation of Casimirs)
.

The SALT RSW-MHD equations (
4.2
)-(
4.4
) possess the same Casimir invariants as the deterministic RSW-MHD equations given by (
2.36
). This may be seen by transforming the stochastic Poisson bracket (
4.1
) into the
(n,\psi,\eta)
variables to find

\displaystyle\diff\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
\psi\\
\eta\,d^{2}x\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&\square\diamond\psi&\square\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}\psi&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta m\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\psi\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\,\diff t

(4.11)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&\square\diamond\psi&\square\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}\psi&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\widetilde{H}_{i}/\delta m\\
\delta\widetilde{H}_{i}/\delta\psi\\
\delta\widetilde{H}_{i}/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}\,,

where the stochastic Hamiltonians are related by
\widetilde{H}_{i}(m,\eta,\psi)=H_{i}(m,\eta,\eta^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}\psi)
. As the Poisson structure of the deterministic part and stochastic part of dynamics are the same, the variational derivatives of the Casimirs still lie in the kernel of the deterministic RSW-MHD Poisson structure given by (
2.36
).

Remark 4.2
.

For a closed material loop of fluid,
c(\diff\chi_{t})
, moving with the flow generated by the stochastic vector field
\diff\chi_{t}=u\,\diff t+\widetilde{\xi}_{i}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}
, the Kelvin-Noether theorem for the SALT RSW-MHD equations (
4.10
) is given by

\displaystyle\diff\oint_{c(\diff\chi_{t})}\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\right)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(\diff\chi_{t})}\left(\diff+\mathcal{L}_{u\,\diff t}+\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{i}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}}\right)\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\right)

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(\diff\chi_{t})}\left(-d\frac{\delta H}{\delta\eta}\,\diff t-d\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta\eta}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}+\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\frac{\delta H}{\delta B}\,\diff t+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{B}\frac{\delta H_{i}}{\delta B}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(\diff\chi_{t})}\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\bm{B}^{\perp}\,\diff t+\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}\right)\bm{B}^{\perp}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}\,,

Thus, one finds that the deterministic and stochastic forcing generating the circulation dynamics takes the same form. Namely, both forces take the form
J\times B
where the stochastic perturbation of the current density is prescribed as
\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{D}_{i}
.

4.2
Stochastic Forcing by Lie Transport (SFLT).

We consider the general form of the SFLT perturbations for the RSW-MHD system using the
(m,\psi,\eta)
variables. This is because care must be taken when introducing stochasticity in the
B
dynamics to preserve the weighted incompressibility condition
\nabla\cdot(\eta\bm{B})
.

The general form of the SFLT perturbations can be defined by a family of forces defined on the Lie co-algebra
\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\ltimes(\Omega^{0}(M)\oplus\Omega^{2}(M))
denoted by

\displaystyle f^{m}_{i}(\bm{x},t)=\bm{f}^{m}_{i}(\bm{x},t)\cdot d\bm{x}\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(M)\,,\quad f^{\psi}_{i}(\bm{x},t)\in\Omega^{0}(M)\,,\quad f^{\eta}_{i}(\bm{x},t)\otimes d^{2}x\in\Omega^{2}(M)\,,

(4.13)

as well as a family of i.i.d driving Brownian motions
\{W^{i}_{t}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Here, the forces can be arbitrary functions of the Lie co-algebra variables
(m,\psi,\eta)
and they can explicitly depend smoothly on both
\bm{x}
and
t
.
Upon using the Poisson structure (
2.31
), the SFLT structure-preserving stochastic RSW-MHD equations become

\displaystyle\diff\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
\psi\\
\eta\,d^{2}x\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}m&\square\diamond\psi&\square\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}\psi&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta m\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\psi\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\,\diff t

(4.14)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{ad}_{\square}^{*}f^{m}_{i}&\square\diamond f^{\psi}_{i}&\square\diamond(\eta\,d^{2}x)\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}f^{\psi}_{i}&0&0\\
\mathcal{L}_{\square}(\eta d^{2}x)&0&0\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta m\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\psi\\
\delta\widetilde{H}/\delta\eta\end{array}\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}\,.

Expanding out the Poisson bracket into individual components of the Lie co-algebra, the SFLT RSW-MHD equations comprise the following advection equations under stochastic forcing,

\displaystyle\diff\,(\eta\,d^{2}x)+\mathcal{L}_{u}(\eta\,d^{2}x)\,\diff t+\mathcal{L}_{u}(f^{\eta}_{i}\,d^{2}x)\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}=0\,,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\diff\psi+\mathcal{L}_{u}\psi\,\diff t+\mathcal{L}_{u}f^{\psi}_{i}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}=0\,,

(4.16)

as well as the stochastic momentum equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}\diff m+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}m\,\diff t+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}f^{m}_{i}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}&=-\eta d\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\eta}\otimes d^{2}x\,\diff t-f^{\eta}_{i}d\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\eta}\otimes d^{2}x\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\\
&\qquad+\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\psi}d\psi\,\diff t+\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\psi}df^{\psi}_{i}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\,.\end{split}

(4.17)

In applications, as
\eta
represents the depth of the fluid and is related to the columnar volume of the flow above a given area element, the perturbations
f^{\eta}_{i}
often are set to zero to preserve the advection property of columnar volume due to its relation to the determinant of the fluid back-to-labels map. In this case, the SFLT RSW-MHD equations given by equations (
4.15
)-(
4.17
) may be expressed component-wise in vector calculus notation as follows

\displaystyle\diff\eta+\nabla\cdot(\eta\,\bm{u})\,\diff t=0\,,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\diff\psi+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\psi\,\diff t+\bm{u}\cdot\nabla f^{\psi}_{i}\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}=0\,,

(4.19)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\diff\bm{u}+\left(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}+\frac{f}{\mathrm{Ro}}\bm{u}^{\perp}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Fr}^{2}}\nabla(\eta-h(\bm{x}))-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}\right)\,\diff t\\
&\qquad=-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{f}^{m}_{i}\right)\bm{u}^{\perp}+\nabla\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{f}^{m}_{i}\right)+\bm{f}^{m}_{i}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\right)\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B}\right)\bm{F}_{i}^{\perp}\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}\,,\end{split}

(4.20)

where we have defined the perturbation to the magnetic field as
\eta\bm{F}_{i}=\nabla^{\perp}f^{\psi}_{i}
. Here, the relationship between
\psi
and
\bm{B}
is unchanged from equation (
2.21
), so that the weighted incompressibility condition of
B
in (
2.5
) still holds in the SFLT RSW-MHD dynamics. From the dynamics of
\eta
and
\psi
, one may obtain the dynamics of
\bm{B}
as

\displaystyle\diff\bm{B}+\left(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{B}-\bm{B}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}\right)\,\diff t+\left(\bm{u}\cdot\nabla\bm{F}_{i}-\bm{F}_{i}\cdot\nabla\bm{u}\right)\circ\diff W_{t}^{i}=\frac{\bm{F}_{i}}{\eta}\nabla\cdot(\bm{u}\eta)\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\,,

(4.21)

although this is not equivalent to having introduced the SFLT type perturbation into the RSW-MHD Poisson structure (
2.31
), since the term
\eta^{-1}\bm{F}_{i}\nabla\cdot(\bm{u}\eta)
is required to preserve the condition
\nabla\cdot(\bm{u}\eta)
.

Remark 4.3

(Conservation of energy)
.

The SFLT RSW-MHD equations (
4.15
)-(
4.17
) preserve the domain integrated Hamiltonian of the deterministic RSW-MHD equations given by (
3.11
). This result may be obtained by noticing that both the deterministic and stochastic Poisson structures of the SFLT RWS-MHD equations appearing in (
4.14
) are skew-symmetric under the duality pairing of the Lie algebra and its dual. As both Poisson structures are applied to the same Hamiltonian, it follows that
\widetilde{H}
,
\widetilde{H}
is conserved by the SFLT dynamics.

Remark 4.4
.

For a closed material loop of fluid,
c(u)
, moving with the flow generated by the semi-martingale vector field
u
, the Kelvin-Noether theorem for the SFLT RSW-MHD equations (
4.20
) is given by

\displaystyle\diff\oint_{c(u)}\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\right)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(u)}\left(\diff+\mathcal{L}_{u\,\diff t}\right)\left(u^{\flat}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ro}}R\right)

(4.22)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(u)}\left(-d\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\eta}\,\diff t+\frac{1}{\eta}\left(-\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}f^{m}_{i}+\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\eta}\nabla\psi\,\diff t+\frac{\delta\widetilde{H}}{\delta\eta}\nabla f^{\psi}_{i}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\oint_{c(u)}\left(\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B})\bm{B}^{\perp}\,\diff t\,\diff t+\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{B}\right)\bm{F}_{i}^{\perp}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}

\displaystyle-\oint_{c(u)}\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\left(\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bm{f}^{m}_{i}\right)\bm{u}^{\perp}+\nabla\left(\bm{u}\cdot\bm{f}^{m}_{i}\right)+\bm{f}^{m}_{i}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}\right)\cdot d\bm{x}\circ\diff W^{i}_{t}\,.

In the circulation dynamics, one sees that the momentum perturbation
f^{m}_{i}
and the magnetic field perturbation
\bm{F}_{i}
both generate circulation. The forcing due to
f^{m}_{i}
takes the form of Stokes drift and the forcing due to
\bm{F}_{i}
takes the form of the
J\times B
force defined by the magnetic field perturbation,
\bm{F}_{i}
.

5
Conclusion and outlook

The present work has made several distinct contributions. In Section
2
we investigated the geometric structures of the Rotating Shallow Water Magenetohydrodynamics (RSW-MHD) equations proposed in Gilman
Gilman, 2000
as a model of solar tachocline dynamics. In Subsection
2.1
, the RSW-MHD equations were rederived via Lie group invariant variational principles using the Euler-Poincaré formalism pioneered in
Holm et al., 1998
. Two equivalent representations of the prognostic variable for magnetic effects were developed, in terms of either the magnetic field
B
or the magnetic stream function
\psi
. In Subsection
2.2
, the Lie–Poisson Hamiltonian formulation of the RSW-MHD equations was constructed via a Lie group reduced Legendre transform in either the variables involving
B
or those involving
\psi
. In Subsection
2.2
, we also characterized the Casimir functions of the RSW-MHD system and demonstrated the equivalence of the Lie-Poisson bracket derived here to the potential vorticity bracket of the RSW-MHD equations derived previously, e.g., in
Dellar, 2002
.

In Section
3
, we extended the treatment of RSW-MHD in Section
2
to include thermal gradients in the variational principle and thereby derive the
Thermal
Rotating Shallow Water MHD (TRSW-MHD) model.

In Section
4
, we constructed stochastic RSW-MHD models using two distinct and complementary types of structure preserving stochastic perturbations. These two methodologies for stochastic perturbation are known within the literature as Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT) and Stochastic Forcing by Lie Transport (SFLT). The SALT RSW-MHD model, presented in Subsection
4.1
preserves the deterministic Casimir functions and possesses stochastic
J\times B
forces from which the uncertainty in the
current density
can potentially be calibrated through data. On the other hand, the SFLT RSW-MHD model, presented in Subsection
4.2
preserves the deterministic energy and also possesses stochastic
J\times B
forces by which the
magnetic field
can potentially be calibrated through data.

Open problems and future work.

The present work has assembled a modeling framework for quasi-neutral plasma dynamics and its stochastic perturbations, which we expect may find utility in many other domains. In future work, we plan to use stochastic perturbations of MHD flows to model the effects of unresolved fluctuations. In particular, we plan to quantify uncertainty in stochastic plasma models via ensemble simulations at coarse grid resolutions and compared with either fine grid resolution simulations or highly resolved observational data. Our earlier efforts in developing and applying this procedure in geophysical fluid dynamics (GFD) have already proven its potential utility in plasma physics. For examples of this procedure in GFD, see, e.g.,
Cotter et al., 2019
for the SALT approach and
Hu and Patching, 2023
for the SFLT approach.

There are also several open problems that may be addressed in this framework. For example, the stochastic variational methods applied in the present paper may be transferred to a variety of other plasma models, such as multi-fluid plasma dynamics
Holm and Kupershmidt, 1987
, as well as Maxwell-Vlasov and other hybrid kinetic/continuum plasma dynamics
Cendra et al., 1998
;
Holm and Tronci, 2012
.

The mean field approximation of the SALT and SFLT modelling approaches may also be applied to plasma models. Examples of these mean-field methods are the Lagrangian Averaged SALT approach
Drivas et al., 2020
and Eulerian Averaged SFLT
Holm and Hu, 2021
approach.

The structure preserving stochastic perturbations applied in the present paper have recently been generalised to go beyond Brownian stochastic flows to include flows on geometric rough paths. See, e.g.
(
Crisan et al., 2022
;
Diamantakis et al., 2023
)
where the flows on geometric rough paths are introduced in a Lie group invariant variational principle. These structure preserving rough path perturbations can be used to derive models for plasma dynamics on rough paths, where the same uncertainty estimation and data calibration procedures for stochastic plasma dynamics can be applied.

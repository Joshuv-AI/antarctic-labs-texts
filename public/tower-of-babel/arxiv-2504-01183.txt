Explicit Multiplicities in the Cuspidal Spectrum of SU ⁡ ( n , 1 ) \mathrm{SU}(n,1)
Alexander Stadler

Abstract
This paper investigates the cuspidal spectrum of the quotient of the real Lie group G = S ​ U ​ ( n , 1 ) G=SU(n,1) and a principal congruence subgroup Γ ⁡ ( m ) \Gamma(m) for m ≥ 3 m\geq 3 , focusing on the multiplicities of integrable discrete series representations. Using the Selberg trace formula, we derive an explicit formula for the multiplicity m ⁡ ( Γ ⁡ ( m ) , π τ ) m(\Gamma(m),\pi_{\tau}) of a representation π τ \pi_{\tau} of integrable discrete series of G G within L 2 ​ ( Γ ​ ( m ) \ G ) L^{2}(\Gamma(m)\backslash G) . The formula involves the Harish-Chandra parameter τ \tau , the discriminant D ℓ D_{\ell} of the imaginary quadratic field ℓ \ell over which G G is defined and special values of the Dirichlet L L -function L ℓ L_{\ell} associated to ℓ \ell . We apply these results on the one hand to compute the cuspidal cohomology of locally symmetric spaces Γ ⁡ ( m ) \ G / K \Gamma(m)\backslash G/K , where K K is a maximal compact subgroup of G G . On the other hand we use them to reprove a known rationality result involving the values of L ℓ L_{\ell} at odd positive integers and make them more explicit. This work extends previous studies on real and quaternionic hyperbolic spaces to the complex hyperbolic case, contributing to the understanding of the spectrum of ℝ \mathbb{R} -rank one algebraic groups.

Introduction

Let
G
be a connected semisimple Lie group and let
\Gamma
be an arithmetic subgroup of
G
. As in
[
25
]
, we denote by
L_{\Gamma\backslash G}
the left regular representation on the space
L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)
. It decomposes as

L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)=L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)\oplus L_{c}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)

into the discrete and continuous spectrum. We study the first summand and denote by
\hat{G}
the set of unitary equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations of
G
. We get the direct Hilbert sum (actually the discrete spectrum is defined like this)

L_{\Gamma\backslash G}|_{L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)}=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\hat{G}}\pi^{m_{d}(\Gamma,\pi)},

where
m_{d}(\Gamma,\pi)
is the finite multiplicity of
\pi
in
L_{\Gamma\backslash G}\big|_{L_{d}^{2}(\Gamma\backslash G)}
(strictly speaking it is the multiplicity of the unitary equivalence class
[\pi]
of irreducible unitary representations, but we simply write
\pi
for the equivalence class).
In a certain setting, we will compute these multiplicities explicitly for representations
\pi
of integrable discrete series.
The Selberg trace formula (or more accurately the special case in
[
25
]
) gives a tool to do this for
\mathbb{R}
-rank one algebraic groups (
[
24
]
). There are three cases of particular geometric interest:

1.

G=SO(n,1)^{0}
, corresponding to the real hyperbolic space

2.

G=SU(n,1)
, corresponding to the complex hyperbolic space

3.

G=Sp(n,1)
, corresponding to the quaternionic hyperbolic space

Aspects of the cases 1. and 3. have been studied in
[
17
]
and
[
9
]
respectively. Case 2. is the topic of this article:
Let
\ell\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-k})
be an imaginary quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SU}(n,1)
the group scheme corresponding to a hermitian form of signature
(n,1)
over
\ell^{n+1}
.
We will define the arithmetic subgroups
\Gamma
of
G
in Section
4.1
and its congruence subgroups
\Gamma(m)
in Definition
5.1
.
Let
L_{\ell}(s)
be the
L
-function attached to the quadratic field
\ell
, i.e. if
\zeta_{\ell}(s)
is the Dedekind zeta-function of
\ell
, then

\displaystyle\zeta_{\ell}(s)=L_{\ell}(s)\zeta(s).

To shorten some notation, we will set for an integer
k\geq 2
:

\displaystyle Z_{\ell}(k)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{matrix}\zeta(k)&\text{if $k$ is even}\\
L_{\ell}(k)&\text{if $k$ is odd}.\end{matrix}\right.

Let
D_{\ell}
be the discriminant of
\ell
over
\mathbb{Q}
. That is

D_{\ell}=\left\{\begin{matrix}-k\quad&k\equiv 3\mod{4}\\
-4k\quad&else.\end{matrix}\right.

In Section
3.2
, we define a system of positive roots for
G
and attach to a Harish-Chandra parameter
\tau=(\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n})
a discrete series representation
\pi_{\tau}
.
The following, Theorem

Theorem
.

Let
\Gamma
be a principal arithmetic lattice of
G=SU(n,1)
and let
\Gamma(m)
be a net principal congruence subgroup (as in Definition
5.1
) of index

in
\Gamma
, with

and

. Let

be of integrable discrete series with Harish-Chandra parameter
\tau=(\tau_{1},.\ldots,\tau_{n})_{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}}
with
\tau_{n+1}\coloneqq-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}
, where

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle>\tau_{2}>\ldots>\tau_{n}>\tau_{n+1}+n.

Then we have

\displaystyle m(\Gamma(m),\pi_{\tau})=d_{\tau}\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma\backslash G)h_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{m}}{4^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\prod_{j=i+1}^{n+1}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{j})\right|}{(2\pi)^{i+1}}\cdot|D_{\ell}|^{\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{4}}\cdot\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}Z_{\ell}(k)\cdot\prod_{p\in T_{\ell}}\lambda_{p}

\displaystyle m(\Gamma(m),\pi_{\tau})=d_{\tau}\mathrm{vol}(\Gamma\backslash G)h_{m}+O\left(\frac{h_{m}}{m^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{m}\left(\frac{1}{4^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\prod_{j=i+1}^{n+1}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{j})\right|}{(2\pi)^{i+1}}\cdot|D_{\ell}|^{\frac{n(n+3)}{4}}\cdot\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}Z_{\ell}(k)+O\left(\frac{1}{m^{n}}\right)\right)

if

The finite set

. As we choose

\Gamma(m)

.

is well known by the work of Vogan and Zuckerman. The missing piece in trying to understand the cuspidal cohomology are thus the multiplicities

. We will apply our results to this situation in Section

Recall that we denote by

the discriminant of the quadratic extension

the

. In Section

Corollary
.

Let
\ell=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-k})
be an imaginary quadratic quadratic field. Let
\Gamma^{(1)}
and
\Gamma^{(2)}
be the arithmetic subgroups as in Definition

of

\Gamma^{(i)}(m^{(i)})
be net principal congruence subgroups (as in Definition

) of index

in
\Gamma^{(i)}
, with

and

. Let
\pi_{\tau^{(i)}}\in\hat{G}
be the discrete series representation with Harish-Chandra parameter
\tau^{(i)}
, such that

\displaystyle\tau_{i}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\tau_{i}^{(2)}\ for\ 1\leq i\leq 2n-1.,

\displaystyle\tau_{1}^{(1)}

\displaystyle>\tau_{2}^{(1)}>\ldots\tau_{2n-1}^{(1)}>\tau_{2n}^{(1)}+2n-1,

\displaystyle\tau_{2n-1}^{(2)}

\displaystyle>\tau_{2n}^{(2)}>\tau_{2n+1}^{(2)}>\tau_{2n+2}^{(2)}+2n+1,

where we set

\displaystyle\tau_{2n}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sum_{i=1}^{2n-1}\tau_{i}^{(1)},

\displaystyle\tau_{2n+2}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\sum_{i=1}^{2n+1}\tau_{i}^{(2)}.

This implies that
\pi_{\tau^{(i)}}
is of integrable discrete series. Then

\displaystyle\sqrt{|D_{\ell}|}\frac{L_{\ell}(2n+1)}{(2\pi)^{2n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m(\Gamma^{(2)}(m^{(2)}),\pi_{\tau^{(2)}})}{m(\Gamma^{(1)}(m^{(1)}),\pi_{\tau^{(1)}})}\cdot\frac{32h_{m^{(1)}}}{h_{m^{(2)}}}\frac{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<2n\\
\end{subarray}}(\tau_{i}^{(1)}-\tau_{2n}^{(1)})}{\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq 2n+2\\

where the sets

and

contain the primes at which the corresponding special unitary group is not quasi-split.

\displaystyle L(1-n,\chi_{\ell})\in\mathbb{Q},\ for\ n\geq 1

(here

3
Multiplicities in the Cuspidal Spectrum

3.1
Special Unitary Groups

Consider an imaginary quadratic field extension
\ell\coloneqq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-k})
of
\mathbb{Q}
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is square-free. We endow the vector space
V=\ell^{n+1}
with a non-degenerate hermitian form
h:V\rightarrow V
. When we define

S_{h}\coloneqq(h(x_{i},x_{j}))_{(ij)}

for a basis
x_{1},...,x_{n}
of
V
, the special unitary group corresponding to
h

is the algebraic group given by

\mathrm{SU}(S_{h})(\mathbb{Q})\coloneqq\{X\in M_{n+1}(\ell)|\ X^{\dagger}S_{h}X=S_{h},\ \det(X)=1\}.

In this article, we will be interested in the hermitian form given by

S_{n,1}\coloneqq\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&0\\
0&-1\end{matrix}\right)

and also denote the corresponding special unitary group
\mathrm{SU}(S_{n,1})
by
\mathrm{SU}(n,1)
.
The paramters
n
and
1
indicate that the matrix
S
represents a hermitian form of signature
(n,1)
. More generally, we denote by

\mathrm{SU}(n,k)\coloneqq\mathrm{SU}(S_{n,k}),

where

S_{n,k}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&0\\
0&-I_{k}\end{matrix}\right),

with
n,k\geq 0
. The group
\mathrm{SU}(n,k)
is a real form of the group
\mathrm{SL}(n+k)
and is a simply connected (
[
14
]
21.43), almost simple group. The special unitary groups always have a unique (up to isomorphism) quasi-split inner form. They are the two groups
\mathrm{SU}(n,n)
and
\mathrm{SU}(n+1,n)
:

If
n+k
is even, the group
\mathrm{SU}\left(\frac{n+k}{2},\frac{n+k}{2}\right)
is the unique (up to isomorphism) quasi-split inner form of
\mathrm{SU}(n,k)
. If
n+k
is odd, the group
\mathrm{SU}\left(\frac{n+k+1}{2},\frac{n+k-1}{2}\right)
is the unique (up to isomorphism) quasi-split inner form of
\mathrm{SU}(n,k)
. Throughout this article, we write
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SU}(n,1)
for
n\geq 3
.

3.2
Root Spaces

We denote the special unitary group of signature
(n,1)
by:

\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{SU}(n,1)(\mathbb{Q})=\{X\in M_{n+1}(\ell)|\ X^{\dagger}S_{n,1}X=S_{n,1},\ \det(X)=1\}

over
\mathbb{Q}
. The real Lie group
G=SU(n,1)
is a semisimple, connected Lie group with finite center. The corresponding real Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{g}_{0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{su}(n,1)=\{X\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})|\ X^{\dagger}S_{n,1}=-S_{n,1}X,\ \mathrm{Tr}(X)=0\}.

This immediately shows that

\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathfrak{su}(n,1))=n^{2}+2n.

As the maximal compact subgroup
K
we choose

\displaystyle K:

\displaystyle=U_{n+1}\cap G

\displaystyle=\{X\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})|\ X^{\dagger}S_{n,1}X=S_{n,1},\ X^{\dagger}X=1,\ \det(X)=1\}\cong U_{n},

where

\displaystyle U_{n}=\{X\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})|\ X^{\dagger}X=1\}.

The corresponding Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{k}_{0}=\{X\in M_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})|\ X^{\dagger}S_{n,1}=-S_{n,1}X,X^{\dagger}=-X,\ \mathrm{Tr}(X)=0\},

with

\dim(\mathfrak{k}_{0})=n^{2}.

The group
\mathrm{SU}(n,1)
has absolute rank
n
, since
\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}
has rank
n
, which is the same as the rank of
\mathrm{K}
. This implies that discrete series representations exist, as we will discuss below. A maximal torus
\mathrm{T}
of
\mathrm{G}
is given by the subset of diagonal matrices in
\mathrm{G}
, with corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
of the real group
T
.
Note that
T\subseteq K
and that
T
contains the one-paramter subgroup specified in
[
6
, p. 86]
.
Our maximal
\mathbb{R}
-torus will be

A\coloneqq\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}a&0&b\\
0&I_{n-1}&0\\
b&0&a\end{matrix}\right)|a,b\in\mathbb{R},\ \ a^{2}-b^{2}=1\right\}\cong\mathbb{R}^{\times},

hence
G
has
\mathbb{R}
-rank
1
.
The corresponding Lie algebra is

\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\left\{\mu(E_{n+1,1}+E_{1,n+1})|\ \mu\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

We denote by
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{t}
the complexifications of
\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{k}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
respectively. I.e.
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{k}\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. We let
\theta
be the Cartan involution associated to the maximal compact group
K
, let
\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
be the corresponding Cartan decomposition and
\mathrm{B}(X,Y)
the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
, which is given by

\mathrm{B}(X,Y)=2(n+1)\mathrm{Tr}(XY).

We also denote by

\mathrm{B}_{0}(X,Y)=\mathrm{Tr}(XY)

the corresponding trace form (
[
12
, §IV.2]
). We have the Iwasawa decomposition

. Let
P_{0}=MAN
be the minimal parabolic subgroup, where
M
is the centralizer of
A
in
K
. As in
[
6
]
, we normalize the Haar measure on

such that

and

have volume
1
, all discrete subgroups have counting measure and on any subspace
V\subseteq\mathfrak{n}
we put the measure induced by the Euclidean structure
(X,Y)=-B(X,\theta Y)
and on
\mathrm{exp}(V)
the measure induced by
\mathrm{exp}
. On

\displaystyle\int_{G}f(g)\,\mathrm{d}g=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{K}\int_{A}\int_{N}f(kan)e^{\rho(\log(a))}\,\mathrm{d}k\mathrm{d}a\mathrm{d}n,

for

, where

for

We denote by

the space of all linear forms on
\mathfrak{t}
, which assume imaginary values on
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
.

and

(IV, §2, page 68), we denote by
\varepsilon_{i}:\mathfrak{t}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
the map

\varepsilon_{i}(\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n+1}))=\lambda_{i},

for

and consequently also for

\displaystyle(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})_{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\varepsilon_{i}.

The root spaces are given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{G}

\displaystyle=\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}|\ i\neq j,1\leq i,j\leq n+1\},

\displaystyle\Delta_{K}

\displaystyle=\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}|\ i\neq j,1\leq i,j\leq n\},

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}_{G}

\displaystyle=\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}|1\leq i<j\leq n+1\},

\displaystyle\Delta^{+}_{K}

\displaystyle=\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}|1\leq i<j\leq n\}.

To a root
\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
we associate an element
H_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}}
in
i\mathfrak{t}_{0}
such that
B_{0}(H,H_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}})=(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})(H)
for all

.
Note that
H_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}}=E_{ii}-E_{jj}
.
Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the inner product on
i\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{*}
induced by the trace form
B_{0}
, i.e. for two roots
\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}
and
\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{l}
we define
(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j},\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{l})\coloneqq(\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})(H_{\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{l}})=(\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{l})(H_{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}})=\delta_{ik}-\delta_{il}-\delta_{jk}+\delta_{jl}
. That means, with respect to the inner product on
i\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{*}
, the
\varepsilon_{i}
form an
orthonormal system, i.e.

\displaystyle((\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n})_{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}},(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})_{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}})=\tau_{1}\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\tau_{n}\lambda_{n}.

Let

\displaystyle\delta_{G}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G}^{+}}\alpha,

\displaystyle\delta_{K}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{K}^{+}}\alpha,

\displaystyle\omega_{G}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{G}^{+}}(\cdot,\alpha),

\displaystyle\omega_{K}(\cdot)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_{K}^{+}}(\cdot,\alpha).

Also, set
\delta_{n}\coloneqq\delta_{G}-\delta_{K}
. Denote by
W_{G}
and
W_{K}
the corresponding Weyl groups.

We have
\omega_{K}(\delta_{K})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}((j-1)!)
and

\displaystyle\delta_{G}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(n+1-i)\varepsilon_{i}=(n,n-1,n-2,...,1)_{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}}.

With these preliminaries about root space, we are in a position to describe the discrete series representations of
G
.

Definition 3.1
.

An irreducible unitary representation
\pi
of
G
is said to be a

representation of
G

\omega_{\pi}
associated to
\pi
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{G}\lVert\omega_{\pi}\rVert^{2}\ dg<\infty.

To
\pi
we may associated a

, defined as the unique positive real number such that

\displaystyle\int_{G}\langle\pi(u_{1}),v_{1}\rangle\overline{\langle\pi(u_{2}),v_{2}\rangle}dg=d_{\pi}^{-1}\langle u_{1},u_{2}\rangle\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle.

We say that
\pi
is
integrable
, if

\displaystyle\int_{G}\lVert\omega_{\pi}\rVert\ dg<\infty.

We define

L_{T}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\tau\in i\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{*}|\ (\tau,\alpha)>0\ \forall\alpha\in\Delta_{G}^{+},\ \tau+\delta_{G}\ \text{analytically integral}\}

a subset of
i\mathfrak{t}_{0}^{*}
of non-singular elements. For each

we have a discrete series representation
(\pi_{\tau},H_{\tau})
as described by Harish-Chandra (
[
12
, IX, §7 Theorem 9.20 p.310]
):

Theorem 3.2
.

(Harish-Chandra)

Let
\tau\in L^{\prime}_{T}
. Then there exists a discrete series representation
\pi_{\tau}
of
G
such that
\pi_{\tau}\mid_{K}
contains with multiplicity one the
K
type with highest weight

\Lambda=\tau+\delta_{G}-2\delta_{K}.

\tau
is called the Harish-Chandra parameter of that representation.

With our normalisation of the Haar measure, its formal degree is given by (
[

]
)

\displaystyle d_{\tau}

\displaystyle\coloneqq d_{\pi_{\tau}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(2\pi)^{-dim(G/K)/2}\ 2^{-(dim(G/K)-1)/2}\ \frac{|\omega_{G}(\tau)|}{\omega_{K}(\delta_{K})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}}\frac{|\omega_{G}(\tau)|}{\omega_{K}(\delta_{K})}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\prod_{j=i+1}^{n+1}(\tau_{i}-\tau_{j})\right|}{(i-1)!}.

(3.1)

We will now choose Harish-Chandra parameters
\tau
in such a way, that

\pi_{\tau}
is integrable.
In the process, we will concurrently compute the formal degree
d_{\tau}
, which will be needed later. For a Harish-Chandra parameter
\tau
we denote

\tau=\rho+\delta_{G}=(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{n})_{\{\varepsilon_{i}\}}+\delta_{G}=(\rho_{1}+n,\rho_{2}+n-1,...,\rho_{n}+1).

If

, we must have that
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and that

\displaystyle\tau_{i}-\tau_{j}=(\tau,\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})>0\quad\ for\ 1\leq i<j\leq n+1.

In other words, we have that
\tau_{1}>\tau_{2}>\ldots>\tau_{n}>\tau_{n+1}
, where
\tau_{n+1}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}
.
We specify our choices in such a way, that
\pi_{\tau}
is integrable:
According to
[

, Theorem p.60]
,

is integrable if and only if

\displaystyle|(\tau,\alpha)|>\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\beta\in\Delta_{G}}|(\alpha,\beta)|\eqqcolon k(\alpha)

(3.2)

for all

.

We see that

reads as

\displaystyle|(\tau,\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{n+1})|=\tau(H_{\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{n+1}})=

\displaystyle\tau_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{i}>n

for all

and

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle>\tau_{2}>\ldots>\tau_{n}>\tau_{n+1}+n.

4.1
The Formula

We interpret
\mathrm{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}
as the integer points of the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group
\mathrm{G}=\mathrm{SU}(n,1)
corresponding to the quadratic extension
\ell=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-k})
of
\mathbb{Q}
. This

is a lattice in
G
. We call an arithmetic subgroup of
G
an

[
7
, §2]
.

Corollary 4.1
.

If
n
is even, the commensurability class of nonuniform arithmetic lattices
\Gamma
of
G
is unique. If
n
is odd, commensurability classes of
G
are in one-to-one correspondence with
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/N_{\ell/\mathbb{Q}}(\ell^{\times})
.

Let
(K_{p})_{p}
be a coherent collection of parahoric subgroups of
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. As in
[
16
, §3.4]
or
[
7

\Gamma
of

can be constructed:
By strong approximation,

G\cdot\prod_{p\ prime}K_{p}\cdot\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A}).

Definition 4.2
.

We say that an arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
is a

if it is the image of

\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q})\cap\left(G\cdot\prod_{p\ prime}K_{p}\right)

G\cdot\prod_{p\ prime}K_{p}\rightarrow G,

where
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q})
is understood as embedded diagonally into
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{A})
and
(K_{p})_{p}
is a coherent collection of parahoric subgroups of
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.

We denote by

the set of finite places

\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is not quasi-split.
Let
\Gamma
be a principal arithmetic lattice of
G
and let
(K_{p})_{p}

be the coherent collection of parahoric subgroups

of
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
corresponding to
\Gamma
.
We denote the quasi split unitary group, such that
\mathrm{G}
is an inner form of it by

. We noted above, that

if

if

is even. As described in
[
16
, 2.2]
, we let
\mathrm{G}_{p}
be the smooth affine
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-group scheme associated with the parahoric subgroup

of
\mathrm{G}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).
Analogously, we let
\mathscr{G}_{p}
be the canonical smooth affine
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-group scheme of

associated by Bruhat-Tits theory
[
10

and let

be the corresponding parahoric subgroup.
Furthermore, we denote by
\overline{\mathrm{G}}_{p}
and
\overline{\mathscr{G}}_{p}
the the reduction modulo

and

over the residue field

\overline{\mathrm{G}_{p}}=\overline{\mathrm{M}_{p}}\cdot\mathrm{R}_{u}(\overline{\mathrm{G}_{p}})

and

\overline{\mathscr{G}_{p}}=\overline{\mathscr{M}_{p}}\cdot\mathrm{R}_{u}(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{p}}),

where

denotes the unipotent radical. Computing the corresponding integral models, we can determine the groups

and

Namely, we apply
[

and

\displaystyle\mu_{EP}(\Gamma\backslash G)=\chi(\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n})D_{\ell}^{s(n)}\left(\prod_{r=1}^{n}\frac{r!}{(2\pi)^{r+1}}\right)\cdot\prod_{p}\frac{p^{\frac{1}{2}(\dim(\overline{M}_{p})+\dim(\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}))}}{|\overline{M}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|},

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle s(n)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{(n-1)(n+2)}{4}&\text{ if $n$ is odd}\\
\frac{n(n+3)}{4}&\text{ if $n$ is even}\end{matrix}\right.

and

of

Since

with:

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\frac{p^{\frac{1}{2}(\dim(\overline{M}_{p})+\dim(\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}))}}{|\overline{M}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}=\prod_{p}\frac{p^{\dim(\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p})}}{|\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}\cdot\prod_{p\in T_{\ell}}\lambda_{p},

with the rational factors

\displaystyle\lambda_{p}\coloneqq p^{\frac{1}{2}(\dim(\overline{M}_{p})-\dim(\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}))}\frac{|\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}{|\overline{M}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}.

(4.3)

We have

\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}&\text{ $p$ split}\\
\mathrm{SU}_{n+1}&\text{ $p$ inert}\\
\mathrm{Sp}_{n+1}&\text{ $p$ ramified}\end{matrix}\right.

\prod_{i=1}^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{p^{2i}}}&\text{ $p$ ramified}.\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\frac{p^{\dim(\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p})}}{|\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p})|}=\zeta(2)L_{\ell}(3)\cdot...\cdot\zeta(n+1),

\displaystyle=\alpha,

Here, only the unitary group attached to

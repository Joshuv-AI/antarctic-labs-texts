On dual regime in Yang-Baxter deformed O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{O}(2N) sigma models
Alexey Bychkov, Alexey Litvinov 1. Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology, 121205 Moscow, Russia 2. HSE University, 6 Usacheva str., Moscow 119048, Russia

Abstract
In this paper, we explore a new class of integrable sigma models, which we refer to as the ”dual regime” of Yang-Baxter (YB) deformed O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{O}(2N) sigma models. This dual regime manifests itself in the conformal perturbation approach. Namely, it is well known that conventional YB-deformed O ⁡ ( N ) \mathrm{O}(N) sigma models are described in the UV by a collection of free bosonic fields perturbed by some relevant operators. The holomorphic parts of these operators play the role of screening operators which define certain integrable systems in the free theory. All of these integrable systems depend on a continuous parameter b b , which parametrizes the central charge, and are known to possess the duality under b 2 ⟷ − 1 − b 2 b^{2}\longleftrightarrow-1-b^{2} . Although O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N + 1 ) \mathrm{O}(2N+1) integrable systems are self-dual, O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{O}(2N) systems are not. In particular, the O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{O}(2N) integrable systems provide new perturbations of the sigma model type. We identify the corresponding one-loop metric and B − B- field and show that they solve the generalized Ricci flow equation.

S=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Big(\sqrt{|h|}h^{\alpha\beta}\,G_{\mu\nu}(X)\,\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\,\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu}+\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}B_{\mu\nu}(X)\,\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\,\partial_{\beta}X^{\nu}+\sqrt{|h|}R^{(2)}\,\Phi(X)+\dots\Big)\,d^{2}x,

(2.1)

where
h_{\alpha\beta}
is a metric on a two-dimensional world-sheet
\Sigma
,
G_{\mu\nu}
and

are metric and
B
-field,

is Ricci curvature of
h
and

is a dilaton field. All these geometric data on

via the field map

, written in local coordinates as

, and then integrated over
\Sigma

S=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\Big(G_{\mu\nu}(X)+B_{\mu\nu}(X)\Big)\,\partial_{+}X^{\mu}\,\partial_{-}X^{\nu}\ d^{2}x.

(2.2)

Two-dimensional non-linear sigma models are renormalizable. At

2.1
PCM and sigma models on symmetric spaces

PCM is integrable and renormalizable

We start by reviewing general classical and quantum properties of nonlinear sigma models on arguably the simplest non-trivial example, the so-called
principal chiral model
. The principal chiral model (PCM) is the non-linear sigma model with the target space being
M=\mathrm{G}
, a compact semisimple Lie group equipped with the canonical metric
ds^{2}=-\lambda^{-2}\tr\big(g^{-1}dg\,g^{-1}dg\big)
. Here

\lambda^{2}
is some positive constant (overall scale) and
\tr
denotes the Cartan-Killing metric on
\mathfrak{g}=\Lie(\mathrm{G})
, which for compact semisimple groups is strictly negative definite and thus nondegenerate. The action of PCM model takes the form

S=-\frac{1}{4\lambda^{2}}\int\tr\big(g^{-1}\partial g\,g^{-1}\overline{\partial}g\big)d^{2}x.

(2.4)

PCM is a fairly simple NLSM, yet it enjoys a plethora of nice properties. One of these is the celebrated Lax integrability
[
3
]
. It can be seen as follows. Classical equations of motion are
4
4

4

These are just conservation law for Noether current corresponding to right symmetry
g(z,\overline{z})\mapsto g(z,\overline{z})\cdot h
. This is not a coincidence.

\partial_{+}j_{-}+\partial_{-}j_{+}=0,\qquad j_{\pm}=g^{-1}\partial_{\pm}g.

(2.5)

Oftentimes it is more convenient to work in the coordinate-independent setting. Namely, denote
j=g^{-1}dg\in\Lambda^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
, so that
j_{\pm}
are just the light-cone components of the current 1-form
j
. The conservation equations are then rewritten as
\partial_{\alpha}j^{\alpha}=0
, or, in the coordinate-independent fashion, as
d\star j=0
. Now, since
j\in\Lambda^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{g}
is a pull-back of the canonical 1-form on the group via the section
g:\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathrm{G}
, it is also flat:
dj+\frac{1}{2}[j\wedge j]=0
.

Consider the following Lie algebra-valued
1
-form:

L(z)=\frac{j+z\star j}{1+z^{2}}\qquad\text{or}\qquad L_{\pm}(z)=\frac{j_{\pm}}{1\mp z},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.6)

It depends on the spectral parameter
z
and is flat if and only if the equations of motion are satisfied:

dL(z)+\frac{1}{2}\left[L(z)\wedge L(z)\right]=0\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\begin{cases}d\star j=0\\
dj+\frac{1}{2}[j\wedge j]=0\end{cases}

(2.7)

We call a mechanical system Lax integrable precisely when it admits the so-called Lax connection
L(z)
, flatness of which is equivalent to the classical equations of motion.

The
1
-loop renormalization group flow in sigma models without Kalb-Ramond term and dilaton is given by the celebrated Ricci flow equation
[
2
]
:

\frac{dG_{\mu\nu}}{dt}=-R_{\mu\nu},\qquad t=\log\frac{\Lambda_{0}}{\Lambda},

(2.8)

where UV region is
t\rightarrow-\infty
and IR region is
t\rightarrow 0
. It can be shown that the canonical metric on the Lie group is in fact Einstein metric:

ds^{2}=-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\Tr\big(g^{-1}dg\,g^{-1}dg\big)\quad\Rightarrow\quad R_{\mu\nu}=h^{\vee}(G)\cdot G_{\mu\nu},

(2.9)

where
h^{\vee}(\mathrm{G})
is the dual Coxeter number of Lie group
\mathrm{G}
. We conclude that in the principal chiral model the only running coupling constant is
\lambda
:

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\right)=h^{\vee}(G)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}(t)}=-h^{\vee}(G)\cdot t.

(2.10)

So, in the UV limit
t\longrightarrow-\infty
the theory becomes free:
\lambda(-\infty)=0
, and in the IR region
t\rightarrow 0
it becomes strongly coupled. This phenomenon is widely known as asymptotic freedom.

Symmetric
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
models are integrable and renormalizable

It is possible to construct a sigma model on homogeneous space
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
by gauging out
\mathrm{H}
-degrees of freedom. For this we introduce
\mathfrak{h}=\Lie(\mathrm{H})
-valued gauge field

\partial_{\mu}\longmapsto\partial_{\mu}+A_{\mu},\quad A\in\Lambda^{1}(\Sigma)\otimes\mathfrak{h},

(2.11)

plug it into the PCM action, and then integrate

does not have a kinetic term). Since

is compact (semi-)simple, it can be decomposed into the orthogonal direct sum
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\overset{\perp}{\oplus}\mathfrak{m}
. The result of integration over

is

S_{\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}}=-\frac{1}{4\lambda^{2}}\int\tr\Big((g^{-1}\partial_{+}g)^{\mathfrak{m}}\ (g^{-1}\partial_{-}g)^{\mathfrak{m}}\Big)d^{2}x=-\frac{1}{4\lambda^{2}}\int\tr\Big(g^{-1}\partial_{+}g\ \mathrm{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}(g^{-1}\partial_{-}g)\Big)d^{2}x,

(2.12)

where
\mathrm{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a orthogonal projector on
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}
, and
j^{\mathfrak{m}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}(j)
is
\mathfrak{m}
-component of
j
.

The metric on the target space
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
corresponding to the action
(
2.12
) is obviously
\mathrm{G}
-invariant. Consider its restriction on the tangent space over a single point
p_{0}=e\cdot\mathrm{H}\in\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
. At this point the metric in question is (up to multiplication by
\lambda^{-2}
) just the usual Cartan-Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{T}_{e}\mathrm{G}
restricted onto
\mathfrak{h}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{m}
. The transitive action of
\mathrm{G}
on
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
can be used to construct a global metric on the homogeneous space.

The property of classical (Lax) integrability of PCM remains intact after gauging for some homogeneous spaces. More precisely, if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
admits an automorphism
\sigma
of order
2
, which preserves
\mathfrak{h}
subalgebra

\sigma(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h};\qquad\sigma(\mathfrak{m})=-\mathfrak{m},

(2.13)

then the coset
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
is called symmetric homogeneous space, and the corresponding homogeneous space sigma model is Lax integrable with the Lax connection

L(z)=A+\frac{1-z^{2}}{1+z^{2}}K+\frac{2z}{1+z^{2}}\star K,\qquad\text{where}\quad A=\mathrm{P}_{\mathfrak{h}}(j),\quad K=\mathrm{P}_{\mathfrak{m}}(j).

(2.14)

RG flows in the symmetric space sigma models are of no difference from those in the principal chiral models, since every symmetric homogeneous space is in fact Einstein too!

2.2
Yang-Baxter deformation

We recall that the principal homogeneous space
\mathrm{G}
endowed with the canonical metric is Einstein (meaning that the Ricci tensor is proportional to the metric).
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
is Einstein provided
\mathrm{G}/\mathrm{H}
is symmetric. Since 1-loop RG equation reads
-\dot{G}_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}
, the target-space metric
G_{\mu\nu}
depends on RG time
t
only through the overall scale
\lambda^{-2}(t)
,

G_{\mu\nu}(t)=\lambda^{-2}(t)\cdot G_{\mu\nu}(t_{0}),

(2.15)

so, there is in fact only one running coupling constant in these theories. We remind the reader that the general sigma model is a theory with an infinite number of couplings, since
G_{\mu\nu}(X)
is a function of target-space coordinates, which can be as complicated as one wants it to be.

A more interesting dependence on
t
can be obtained by introducing another independent coupling. A very fruitful way to do so is to consider integrable parametric deformations. ”To deform a theory” means making the metric and
B
-field depend on a finite number of additional deformation parameters (in this work we will only add one), which are then identified with new coupling constants. Sometimes, it is possible to make the deformation preserve classical integrability, in which case the deformation is called integrable.

One of the main mysteries about these integrable deformations is the integrability-renormalizability conjecture
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Assume that the metric and the
B
-field in certain sigma model depend only on a finite number of parameters
\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{N}\}
:

G_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}\big(\{\lambda_{k}\}\big),\qquad B_{\mu\nu}=B_{\mu\nu}\big(\{\lambda_{k}\}\big),

(2.16)

and that the sigma model with these
G_{\mu\nu}
and
B_{\mu\nu}
is classically integrable. Then, under renormalization
G_{\mu\nu}
and
B_{\mu\nu}
will depend on RG-time only through these couplings
\lambda_{k}(t)
’s. We call such sigma models
RG-stable
or renormalizable in the strong sense. In short, this conjecture states the following:

\text{RG-stability}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\text{Integrability}

Yang-Baxter PCM

One of these integrable deformations is the so-called Yang-Baxter deformation of PCM
[
10
]
. In what follows, we will describe the construction and the basic features of it. We start from the action

S=-\frac{1}{4\lambda^{2}}\int\tr\left(j_{+}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}_{g}}j_{-}\right)d^{2}x,\qquad\mathcal{R}_{g}=\Ad_{g}^{-1}\mathcal{R}\Ad_{g},

(2.17)

where
\mathcal{R}\in\End(\mathfrak{g})
is a constant linear operator on the Lie algebra,
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
is the deformation parameter, and the limit
\eta\rightarrow 0
corresponds to the non-deformed model.

Equations of motion.

Equations of motion for YB-PCM can be obtained by varying
g(x)\mapsto g(x)(1-\varepsilon(x))
(infinitesimal right-acting symmetry). It is more convenient to rewrite the Lagrangian in the following way: since
\mathcal{R}_{g}=\Ad_{g}^{-1}\mathcal{R}\Ad_{g}
,

L=\tr\left(j_{+}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}_{g}}j_{-}\right)=\tr\left(j_{+}\cdot\Ad_{g}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}}\Ad_{g}^{-1}j_{-}\right)=\\
=\tr\left(\Ad_{g}^{-1}(j_{+})\cdot\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}}\Ad_{g}^{-1}(j_{-})\right)=\tr\left(k_{+}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}}k_{-}\right),

(2.18)

where
k_{\pm}
are the components of the right-invariant current:

k=-dg\,g^{-1};\qquad\Ad_{g}^{-1}(j)=-k.

(2.19)

Under the infinitesimal right shifts
\delta g=-g\varepsilon
these currents transform as
\delta k=\Ad_{g}(d\varepsilon)
, so, by direct computation the variation of the Lagrangian is

\delta L=\tr\Big(\varepsilon\cdot\left(\partial_{+}J_{-}+\partial_{-}J_{+}\right)\Big)+\text{total derivatives},

(2.20)

where

J_{-}=\frac{1}{\xi}\Ad_{g}^{-1}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}}k_{-}=\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}_{g}}j_{-},\qquad J_{+}=\frac{1}{\xi}\Ad_{g}^{-1}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{t}}}k_{+}=\frac{1}{\xi}\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{t}}_{g}}j_{+}.

(2.21)

Here,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{t}}\in\End\mathfrak{g}
is the operator conjugated to
\mathcal{R}
with respect to the Killing form:
\tr(X\,\mathcal{R}(Y))=\tr(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{t}}(X)\,Y)
, and
\xi
is an arbitrary number.

Classical integrability.

If we require
J_{\pm}
to be not only conserved, but also flat

\partial_{+}J_{-}-\partial_{-}J_{+}+[J_{-},J_{+}]=0,

(2.22)

then the Lax connection could be defined by
L_{\pm}(\lambda)=\frac{J_{\pm}}{1\mp\lambda}
(exactly as in (
2.6
)) and the system would be Lax integrable. The direct computation shows that the flatness condition (
2.22
) is equivalent to the system of two equations on
\mathcal{R}\in\End\mathfrak{g}
:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{R}+\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{t}}=0;\\
\left[\mathcal{R}X,\mathcal{R}Y\right]+\mathcal{R}\big([X,\mathcal{R}Y]+[\mathcal{R}X,Y]\big)+c^{2}[X,Y]=0,\quad\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},\end{cases}

(2.23)

where
c^{2}
is an arbitrary complex parameter. The first equation forces
\mathcal{R}
to be skew-symmetric. The second equation is called
modified classical Yang-Baxter equation
(mcYBe). Usual (unmodified) cYBe is recovered at
c^{2}=0
. For general
c
we can always rescale
\mathcal{R}
by a real factor to make
c^{2}\in\mathbb{R}
equal to
1
or
-1
or
0
. The solutions to these mcYBe’s are usually called unmodified (
c=0
), non-split
(c=\sqrt{-1})
and split
(c=1)

r
-matrices.

Drinfeld-Jimbo solution

One solution to mcYBe is the so-called Drinfeld-Jimbo
r
-matrix
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{DJ}}
. Choose a Cartan-Weyl basis in
\mathfrak{g}=\langle E_{\alpha},E_{-\alpha},H_{i}\rangle
. In this basis one has

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{DJ}}(E_{\pm\alpha})=\pm c\,E_{\pm\alpha},\qquad\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{DJ}}(H_{i})=0.

(2.24)

We notice that in the non-split case
\mathcal{R}^{3}=-\mathcal{R}
, which gives rise to a non-zero
B
-field. Indeed, expanding the geometric series

\frac{1}{1-\eta\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\eta^{2k}\mathcal{R}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}(-1)^{k}\eta^{2k+1}\mathcal{R}=1+\frac{\eta^{2}}{1+\eta^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{2}+\frac{\eta}{1+\eta^{2}}\mathcal{R},

(2.25)

we see that there is a non-zero skew-symmetric part.

So, there is effectively only one running coupling

3
Main result: dual solution from conformal perturbations

In the previous section, we explained the relation between certain sigma models and certain Toda field theories. The neighborhood of a UV fixed point of a Yang-Baxter deformation (at least for sphere sigma models) corresponds to a Toda type theory, and vice versa: integrable Toda field theory gives rise to a renormalizable sigma model by imposing Ricci flow. This is undoubtedly a manifestation of a far more general effect, which holds not only for YB-deformed sphere sigma models, but arguably for any integrably deformed sigma model (see
[
24
,
25
]
for
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
models,
[
17
]
for
\mathrm{OSp}(N|2m)
models).

In this section, we elaborate on this correspondence and find a series of new solutions to Ricci flow equations, which we call dual regimes of
\mathrm{O}(2N)
models. Our process is as follows:

•

Start from a free theory of
2N
bosonic fields;

•

Draw a bowtie diagram with
2N
nodes, choose a regime and add a corresponding integrable perturbation;

•

Solve a 1-loop RG equation (
2.3
) for metric assuming no
B

•

Perform T-dualities as needed (they do not corrupt Ricci flow). Typically a non-zero pure imaginary

•

Interpret the resulting metric and
B
-field as those of an integrably deformed sigma model. This deformation can turn out to be the Yang-Baxter one, as in the usual regime, or not.

4
Concluding remarks

In this work, we have defined and studied the dual regime in YB-deformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
sigma models. In particular, we have obtained a new series of solutions to the generalized Ricci flow equations (
3.42
). We emphasize several important questions and future directions of study.

•

It is rather natural to expect that dual YB-deformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
sigma models should admit a Lagrangian description similar to the description of conventional YB SSSM (
2.29
). Furthermore, the way in which we came to the definition of dual YB-deformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
sigma models suggests that they must be classically integrable and admit a Lax representation. Finding such a description is a very interesting and important task.

•

As the dual YB-deformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
sigma models correspond to integrable deformation of
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model, they are expected to have the same spectrum of fundamental particles as in undeformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
models. The scattering matrices of the latter theories have been found in the seminal paper
[
26
]
. They coincide up to a total factor with the rational
R-
matrices for
D_{N}
algebra. It is well known that there are two non-equivalent trigonometric deformations of
D_{N}

R-
matrices
[
27
,
28
]
that correspond to affine Dynkin diagrams
\hat{D}_{N}^{(1)}
and
\hat{D}_{N}^{(2)}
. The first deformation has been identified in
[
14
]
with the YB-deformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model. The identification goes through the construction of Toda field theory whose exponents coincide with the exponents of the fermionic screenings of the bowtie diagram (
2.63
) and perturbative computation of the scattering matrix. The dual YB-deformed
\mathrm{O}(2N)
sigma models are then naturally associated with the second deformation
[
29
]
.

•

In the recent paper
[
30
]
a new class of integrable deformations of the principal chiral model called
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
-twisted trigonometric sigma models has been introduced. Considering in particular the case of
\mathrm{SO}(2N)

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-twisted models with the twist corresponding to an outer automorphism of
\mathfrak{so}(2N)
reveals that one of the global symmetry groups gets broken down to
\mathrm{U}(1)^{N-1}
in contrast with
\mathrm{U}(1)^{N}
for the usual YB-deformed PCM. In this way, twisted deformed PCM has one less isometries than the untwisted one. The same phenomenon occurs for the dual regime of deformed
\frac{\mathrm{SO}(2N)}{\mathrm{SO}(2N-1)}
model, and it is therefore natural to expect the existence of a coset version of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-twisted models, which would coincide with the dual regime.

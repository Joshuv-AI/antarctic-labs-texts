Conclusive Local State Marking: More Nonlocality With No Entanglement
Samrat Sen

Abstract
Nonlocality exhibited by ensembles of composite quantum states, wherein local operations and classical communication (LOCC) yield suboptimal discrimination probabilities compared to global strategies, is one of the striking nonclassical features of quantum theory. A variant of this phenomenon arises in conclusive local state discrimination, where the correct state must be identified with zero error, albeit allowing for inconclusive outcomes. More recently, the notion of local state marking has been introduced with the focus shifted to correctly identifying permutation of a subset of states random chosen from a given set of multipartite states under LOCC. In this work, we unify these two approaches by introducing the task of conclusive local state marking, which reveals a finer hierarchy of nonlocality in multipartite quantum state ensembles. Notably, we demonstrate that certain ensembles of product states can exhibit a stronger form of nonlocality than entangled ensembles traditionally considered highly nonlocal.

Notably, a set of states which is unmarkable, be it perfect or conclusive, shows a stronger form of nonlocality than what is considered in literature. IV
Conclusions

In this work, we unify two important areas within the broader domain of state discrimination: conclusive local state discrimination and local state marking. To achieve this, we introduce a novel class of discrimination tasks called
m
-conclusive local state marking (
m
-CLSM). This class reveals various subtle and intriguing features, highlighting the inherent complexity of quantum nonlocality. Propositions
2
and
3
provide key insights into the structural characteristics associated with any general
m
-CLSM task. Furthermore, Theorems
2
and
3
uncover interesting findings, particularly concerning product states that demonstrate greater nonlocality than those identified in the original constructions by Bennett et al.
Bennett et al. 1999a
and by Peres and Wootters
Peres and Wootters 1991
. Theorem
4
and Proposition
5
establish a strict hierarchy between the two tasks: CLSD and CLSM.

These findings open several exciting avenues for further exploration. In our work, we utilized adaptive LOCC protocols that, while operationally implementable, suffer from a low probability of success. It would be worthwhile to investigate whether adopting a global measurement strategy could enhance the overall success probability. Additionally, a compelling open question remains: is it possible to construct a binary ensemble of quantum states that are not only conclusively indistinguishable via LOCC, but for which CLSM is also impossible? In such a construction, at least one of the states must be supported on an unextendible basis (UB), which prevents CLSD. Moreover, for the ensemble to disallow CLSM, at least one permutation of the two quantum states must also be supported on a UB in the larger Hilbert space. Determining whether such a binary ensemble can be constructed would be intriguing.

We have also been unable to compare the different
m
-CLSM tasks. Providing examples of sets of states, for instance, where
2
-CLSM is impossible but
3
-CLSM is possible, would be an interesting endeavor.

Abstract




q
-Congruences for Z.-W. Sun’s generalized polynomials
w^{(\alpha)}_{k}(x)

Abstract.

In 2022, Z.-W. Sun defined

w_{k}^{(\alpha)}{(x)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}w(k,j)^{\alpha}x^{j-1},

where
k,\alpha
are positive integers and
w(k,j)=\frac{1}{j}\binom{k-1}{j-1}\binom{k+j}{j-1}
.
Let
(x)_{0}=1
and
(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)
for all
n\geq 1
.
In this paper, it is proved by
q
-congruences that for any positive integers
{\alpha,\beta,m,n,r}
, we have

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{r}(k+1)^{r}(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{r}(k+1)^{r}(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

and

\frac{2}{[n,n+1,\cdots,n+2\beta+1]}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta+1)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta)\prod_{i=0}^{2\beta-1}w_{k+i}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

where
[n,n+1,\cdots,n+2\beta+1]
is the least common multiple of
n
,
n+1
,
\cdots
,
n+2\beta+1
.
Taking
r=\beta=1
above will confirm some of Z.-W. Sun’s conjectures.

Keywords
:
congruence;
q
-analogue; Sun’s polynomial
w^{(\alpha)}_{k}(x)
.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 11A07, 11B65.

1
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
denote the set of nonnegative and positive integers respectively.
For any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, Sun
[
16
]
defined
w(n,k)
as

w(n,k)=\frac{1}{k}\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{n+k}{k-1}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{n+k}{k}-\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+k}{k-1}\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Clearly
w(n,n)=\binom{2n}{n}/(n+1)
is the Catalan number.
Sun
[
16
,
18
]
also proved that when
m
are positive integers and
m\leq n
,

\sum_{k=m}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{k-1}{m-1}w(n,k)=w(n,m)

and established the following connections between
w(n,k)
and the Narayana number
N(n,k)=\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}
,

w(n,k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}N(n,j)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptN(n,k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom{n-j}{k-j}(-1)^{k-j}w(n,j)

for any integers
n\geq k\geq 1
.
Recently, the authors
[
10
]
showed that

\sum_{k=0}^{2b}w(n,k+1)

is odd for any
n,b\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

In 2018, Sun
[
16
]
defined the polynomial
w_{n}(x)
as

w_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w(n,k)x^{k-1}

and discovered that

w_{n}(-1-x)=(-1)^{n-1}w_{n}(x).

Later, Sun
[
18
]
proved that, for any
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
,
w_{n}(x)
equals the little Schröder
polynomial
s_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n}{k-1}x^{k-1}(x+1)^{n-k}

and that

(2x+1)\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(2k+1)(-1)^{n-k}w_{k}(x)^{2}=n(n+1)(n+2)w_{n}(x)w_{n+1}(x).

In 2022, Sun
[
18
]
further defined

w_{n}^{(\alpha)}{(x)}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w(n,k)^{\alpha}x^{k-1}\hskip 10.00002pt{\alpha,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}

and provided many interesting conjectures involving
w_{n}^{(\alpha)}{(x)}
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Conjecture 5.2 of Sun
[
18
]
)
.

For any
{\alpha,m,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(1.1)

Also,

\frac{(2,m-1,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k(k+1)(2k+1)w_{k}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

(1.2)

and

\frac{1}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k(k+1)(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]\hskip 10.00002ptfor\ \alpha>1.

(1.3)

Conjecture 1.2

(Conjecture 5.4 of Sun
[
18
]
)
.

(i)
For any
{\alpha,m,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{2(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(k+2)(w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)w_{k+1}^{(\alpha)}(x))^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(1.4)

(ii)
For any
{m,n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{2(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)(2x+1)^{m}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(k+2)(w_{k}(x)w_{k+1}(x))^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(1.5)

If
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
is even, then

\frac{4}{n(n+1)(n+2)(2x+1)^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k(k+1)(k+2)w_{k}(x)w_{k+1}(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(1.6)

Recall that
w_{2j}(x)/(2x+1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
which has proved by Sun
[
16
]
, one can directly get (
1.5
) from (
1.4
) by noting that either
k
or
k+1
is even. Recently, (
1.6
) was confirmed by Jia and Huang
[
8
]
.

In this paper, we will focus on conjectures (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) by the method of
q
-analogues which are widely used in the proof and discovery of arithmetic properties of many classical combinatorial sequences, consult
[
5
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,
13
,

,

,
7
,

]
and references therein.

To proceed, some preliminaries are need.
For each integer
n
, the
q
-integer denoted by
[n]_{q}
, or
[n]
for short, is defined by

[n]=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}.

Obviously
\lim\limits_{q\to 1}[n]=n
. For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the
q
-binomial coefficient
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]
is given by

\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=\frac{\prod_{1\leq j\leq k}[n-j+1]}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq k}[j]}.

Also, we set
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
0\end{matrix}\right]=1
and
\left[\begin{matrix}n\\
k\end{matrix}\right]=0
if
k<0
or
k>n
.

In this paper, we define

w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}q^{\alpha\left(\binom{j+1}{2}-(k+1)(j-1)\right)}\left(\left[\begin{matrix}k-1\\
j-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}k+j\\
j\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}k\\
j\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}k+j\\
j-1\end{matrix}\right]\right)^{\alpha}x^{j-1}.

(1.7)

Clearly
w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)
is a
q
-analogue of
w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)
.
Then we will show

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
{\alpha,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[k(k+1)]^{r}[2k+1]q^{(n-1-k)(\alpha m+1)}w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{[n]}.

(1.8)

It is well-known that the cyclotomic polynomial

\Phi_{n}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
(k,n)=1\end{subarray}}^{n}(q-e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{n}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[q]

is irreducible in the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[q]
. For the alternating cases, we obtain the following fact.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
{\alpha,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{k}[k(k+1)]^{r}_{q^{2}}[2k+1]q^{(n-1-k)(2\alpha m+1)}w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q^{2})^{m}

(1.9)

is divisible by

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>1\ is\ odd\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(q)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>1\ is\ even\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(q^{2}).

Taking
q=1
in Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
respectively.
We have

n\mid\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon^{k}k^{r}(k+1)^{r}(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}

for
\varepsilon=\pm 1
. Then it is straightforward to verify that

(n+i)\mid\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{k}k^{r}(k+1)^{r}(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m},

where
i=0,1
or
2
. This leads to

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
{\alpha,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{r}(k+1)^{r}(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x],

(1.10)

and

\frac{(2,n)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{r}(k+1)^{r}(2k+1)w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

(1.11)

Clearly (
1.10
) is a generalization of Sun’s (
1.1
)
while (
1.11
) generalized (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) only when
n
are odd integers.
In the cases when the summand contains the multiplication
w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)w_{k+1}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)
, we have

Theorem 1.6
.

For any
{\alpha,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[k(k+2)]^{r}[2(k+1)]q^{(n-2-k)(2\alpha m+1)}(w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)w_{k+1}^{(\alpha)}(x;q))^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{[n]}.

The proof of
1.6
motivates the discovery of the following generalization.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any
{\alpha,\beta,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[(k)_{\beta}(k+\beta+1)_{\beta}]^{r}[2(k+\beta)]q^{(n-2\beta-k)(2\beta\alpha m+1)}\prod_{i=0}^{2\beta-1}(w_{k+i}^{(\alpha)}(x;q))^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{[n]}.

Taking
q=1
above gives

n\mid 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}(k)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta+1)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta)\prod_{i=0}^{2\beta-1}w_{k+i}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}.

Then it is easy to check fact

(n+i)\mid 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}(k)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta+1)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta)\prod_{i=0}^{2\beta-1}w_{k+i}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}

for any
i
with
0\leq i\leq 2\beta+1
when
r\geq 1
.
This leads to

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
{\alpha,\beta,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

\frac{2}{[n,n+1,\cdots,n+2\beta+1]}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(k)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta+1)_{\beta}^{r}(k+\beta)\prod_{i=0}^{2\beta-1}w_{k+i}^{(\alpha)}(x)^{m}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x].

This confirms and generalizes the conjecture (
1.4
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 2 and section 3, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
respectively. Theorem
1.6
will be discussed in section 4.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

It is well-known that

[n]=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\mid n\\
d>1\end{subarray}}\Phi_{d}(q).

Therefore, it suffices to prove that

Theorem 2.1
.

For any
{\alpha,m,n,r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
and
d>1
being a divisor of
n
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[k(k+1)]^{r}[2k+1]q^{(n-1-k)(\alpha m+1)}w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)^{m}\equiv 0\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}.

(2.1)

To this aim, we first provide another expression of
w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
k,\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we have

w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)=\sum_{-\infty<j<\infty}(-1)^{\alpha j}q^{\alpha j^{2}}\left(q^{k+1}\left[\begin{matrix}k-1\\
j-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}-k-1\\
j\end{matrix}\right]-\left[\begin{matrix}k\\
j\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}-k-2\\
j-1\end{matrix}\right]\right)^{\alpha}x^{j-1}.

(2.2)

Proof.
Not that

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}k+j\\
j\end{matrix}\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-q^{k+1})(1-q^{k+2})\cdots(1-q^{k+j})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j}q^{kj+\binom{j+1}{2}}\frac{(1-q^{-k-1})(1-q^{-k-2})\cdots(1-q^{-k-j})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j}q^{kj+\binom{j+1}{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}-k-1\\
j\end{matrix}\right].

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}k+j\\
j-1\end{matrix}\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-q^{k+2})(1-q^{k+3})\cdots(1-q^{k+j})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{j-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j-1}q^{(k+1)(j-1)+\binom{j}{2}}\frac{(1-q^{-k-2})(1-q^{-k-3})\cdots(1-q^{-k-j})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{j-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{j-1}q^{(k+1)(j-1)+\binom{j}{2}}\left[\begin{matrix}-k-2\\
j-1\end{matrix}\right].

(2.4)

Substituting (
2
) and (
2
) into (
1.7
) gives the equality (
2.2
).

To prove Theorem
2.1
, we recall the well-known
q
-Lucas Theorem
[
15
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
d>1
is a positive integer and
a,b,s,t
are integers with
0\leq b,t\leq d-1
. Then, we have

\left[\begin{matrix}ad+b\\
sd+t\end{matrix}\right]\equiv{\binom{a}{s}\left[\begin{matrix}b\\
t\end{matrix}\right]}\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}.

In the follow, we always suppose
d>1
is a divisor of
n
and
n=hd
.
Writing
k=ad+b
and
j=sd+t
where
0\leq b,t\leq d-1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
w_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)
be defined as in (
2.2
).
For
d>2
and
1\leq b\leq d-2
, we have

w_{ad+b}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)\equiv{B_{a,b,d}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)}\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}

(2.5)

and

w_{ad+d-b-1}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)\equiv q^{-\alpha(2b+1)}{B_{a,b,d}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)}\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)},

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle B_{a,b,d}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\infty<s<\infty\\
1\leq t\leq d-1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle(-1)^{\alpha(sd+t)}q^{\alpha t^{2}}\binom{a}{s}^{\alpha}\binom{-a-1}{s}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\times\left(q^{b+1}\left[\begin{matrix}b-1\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-1\\
t\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}b\\
t\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-2\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]\right)^{\alpha}x^{sd+t-1}.

(2.7)

Proof.

Let

A_{a,b,s,t,d}(q)=q^{ad+b+1}\left[\begin{matrix}ad+b-1\\
sd+t-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}-ad-b-1\\
sd+t\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}ad+b\\
sd+t\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}-ad-b-2\\
sd+t-1\end{matrix}\right].

By Lemma
2.3
, one can see

\displaystyle A_{a,b,s,0,d}(q)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ q^{b+1}\binom{a}{s-1}\left[\begin{matrix}b-1\\
d-1\end{matrix}\right]\binom{-a-1}{s}\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-1\\
0\end{matrix}\right]+\binom{a}{s}\left[\begin{matrix}b\\
0\end{matrix}\right]\binom{-a-1}{s-1}\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-2\\
d-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}

since
b-1<d-1
and
d-b-2<d-1
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle w_{ad+b}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\infty<s<\infty\\
1\leq t\leq d-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\alpha(sd+t)}q^{\alpha(sd+t)^{2}}(A_{a,b,s,t,d}(q))^{\alpha}x^{sd+t-1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{-\infty<s<\infty}(-1)^{\alpha sd}q^{\alpha(sd)^{2}}(A_{a,b,s,0,d}(q))^{\alpha}x^{sd-1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ B_{a,b,d}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}.

For the proof of (
2.6
),
note that

\displaystyle q^{b+1}\left[\begin{matrix}b-1\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-1\\
t\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}b\\
t\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-2\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ q^{2b+1}\left(q^{d-b}\left[\begin{matrix}b\\
t\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-2\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}b-1\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-1\\
t\end{matrix}\right]\right)\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}.

We have

\displaystyle w_{ad+d-b-1}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)\equiv

\displaystyle\ B_{a,d-b-1,d}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\infty<s<\infty\\
1\leq t\leq d-1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\alpha(sd+t)}q^{\alpha t^{2}}\binom{a}{s}^{\alpha}\binom{-a-1}{s}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\times\left(q^{d-b}\left[\begin{matrix}b\\
t\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-2\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}b-1\\
t-1\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}d-b-1\\
t\end{matrix}\right]\right)^{\alpha}x^{sd+t-1}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\ q^{-\alpha(2b+1)}B_{a,b,d}^{(\alpha)}(x;q)\pmod{\Phi_{d}(q)}.

This ends the proof.

Proof of Theorem

The proof of the alternating cases are more complicated. To proceed, we first recall the following fact, one can see

Lemma 3.1
.

If
d>1
is odd, then
\Phi_{d}(q)
divides
\Phi_{d}(q^{2})
. If
d

\Phi_{d}(q^{2})=\Phi_{2d}(q)

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We will discuss according to the parity of
d
. If
d
is odd.
Substituting
q=q^{2}
in (
2.5
), we get

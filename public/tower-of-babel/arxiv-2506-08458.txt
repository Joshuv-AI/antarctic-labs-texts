A study of weak ∗ -weak points of continuity in the unit ball of dual spaces
S. Daptari, V. Montesinos, and T. S. S. R. K. Rao

Abstract
. We study classes of Banach spaces where the points of weak ∗ -weak continuity for the identity mapping on the dual unit ball form a weak ∗ -dense and weak ∗ - G δ G_{\delta} set. We also discuss how this property behaves in higher duals of Banach spaces. We prove in particular that if 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is a von Neumann algebra and its predual has the Radon–Nikodým property, then there is no point of weak ∗ -weak continuity on the unit ball of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} .

1.
Introduction

A well-known notion in the geometry of Banach spaces is that of a denting point of the closed unit ball
B(X)
of a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
. We recall from
[
DU77
]
that
x_{0}\in B(X)
is said to be a
denting point
if for any
\epsilon>0
,
x_{0}\notin\overline{\rm{co}}(B(X)\setminus B(x_{0},\epsilon))
, where ‘co’ denotes the convex hull and
B(x_{0},\varepsilon):=\{x\in X:\ \|x-x_{0}\|<\varepsilon\}
. This has been shown in
[
LLT88
]
to be equivalent to
x_{0}\in\,{\rm Ext}\,(B(X))
(i.e.,
x_{0}
being an extreme point of
B(X)
) and a point of continuity of the identity mapping
id:(S(X),w)\longrightarrow(S(X),\|.\|)
, where
S(X)
denotes the unit sphere of
X
and
w
stands for the weak topology. In the case of a dual space, one considers the notion of
w^{*}
-denting points, where the
w
-topology gets replaced by the
w^{*}
-topology. An interesting question is to consider only points of continuity (not necessarily extreme points) and the geometric implications related to the “richness” of these points. We refer to the monograph
[
Ho75
]
for basic results in geometry of Banach spaces.

Our work is motivated by
[
HL93
]
, where the authors study points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w)\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),\|\cdot\|)
and
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),\|.\|)
. We focuss instead on the study of points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
(we shall speak of “points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity”, and —needless to say— we are interested only in the nonreflexive case).

Our notation is standard. Along the paper,
(X,\|\cdot\|)
will be a Banach space (we shall write just
X
is there is no need to refer to the underlying norm). “Equality” of Banach spaces is understood as “linear isometric equality”. As usual, we consider a Banach space
X
canonically embedded in its bidual
X^{**}
. Similar canonical identifications are done for
X^{*}\subset X^{***}
,
X^{**}\subset X^{(IV)}
and so on (higher duals are denoted as expected). The symbol
\{x_{n}\}
will denote a sequence as well as a set, hoping that there will be no misunderstanding; the symbol
(x_{n})
will be used for vectors in a vector space.

2.
An interlude:
B(X^{*})
versus
S(X^{*})

Despite the elementary nature of this section, we find it worth to clarify the reason why the statement of Lemma
3.1
below and many other similar results in this paper, although dealing with
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
, focus only on points in
S(X^{*})
.

We start with the following lemma, a
w^{*}
-
w
-version of
[
HL93
, Lemma 2.1]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
x^{*}\in B(X^{*})
be a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
. Assume that
x^{*}=\lambda y^{*}+(1-\lambda)z^{*}
, where
y^{*},z^{*}\in B(X^{*})
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Then both
y^{*}
and
z^{*}
are points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
.

Proof.

Let
\{y^{*}_{\alpha}\}\subset B(X^{*})
be a net such that
y^{*}_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}y^{*}
. Clearly
\lambda y^{\ast}_{\alpha}+(1-\lambda)z^{\ast}\in B(X^{*})
for all
\alpha
, and
\lambda y^{\ast}_{\alpha}+(1-\lambda)z^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{*}
. By hypothesis,
\lambda y^{\ast}_{\alpha}+(1-\lambda)z^{\ast}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{*}\ (=\lambda y^{*}+(1-\lambda)z^{*})
. It follows that
y^{\ast}_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}y^{\ast}
. The same argument applies to
z^{*}
, and the proof is over.
∎

The following is a straightforward consequence of the previous lemma, and locates the points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity of
B(X^{*})
on
S(X^{*})
in the nonreflexive case:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a nonreflexive Banach space, and let
x^{*}\in B(X^{*})
be a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
. Then,
x^{*}\in S(X^{*})
.

Proof.

Assume
\|x^{*}\|<1
. Let
(x^{*}\not=)\ y^{*}\in B(X^{*})
. Obviously,
x^{*}=\lambda y^{*}+(1-\lambda)z^{*}
for some
z^{*}\in B(X^{*})
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
. Lemma
2.1
shows that
y^{*}
is a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
. It follows that
w
and
w^{*}
agree on
B(X^{*})
, hence
X
is reflexive, a contradiction.
∎

The next lemma is an almost trivial consequence of the
w^{*}
-lower-semicontinuity of the dual norm of a Banach space, and thus its proof will be omitted.

Lemma 2.3
.

A point
x^{*}\in S(X^{*})
is of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
if and only if it is a point of continuity of
id:(S(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(S(X^{*}),w)
.

3.
Introduction (continued) and some definitions

In this paper, we also study the geometric property of points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity in the unit sphere of higher odd dual of a nonreflexive Banach space. We use renorming techniques to exhibit points of

-

-continuity in

For every Banach space

). A Banach space

-embedded

M
-ideal in

. It is equivalent to say that
X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus_{1}X^{\perp}

[
HWW93
, Proposition III.3.2]
. See
[
HWW93
, Chapters III and VI]
for several examples of those concepts among function spaces and spaces of operators. A projection

is said to be an

-projection
if
\|x\|=\max\{\|Px\|,\|x-Px\|\}
for all

. The range of an

-summand
(see
[
HWW93
, Definition I.1.1]
). Let
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
,

denote the space of bounded and compact operators on

, respectively. A particularly interesting situation occurs when

(see Chapter VI and Chapter III of

One of our main results implies that when

is an

form a weak
∗
-dense and weak
∗

G_{\delta}
set in

(see Theorem

).

Let
Y
be a closed subspace of a Banach space

. For
x\in X
we denote

(1)

P_{Y}(x):=\{y\in Y:\|x-y\|=d(x,Y)\}

the

of

A closed subspace

Property-

.

4.
Building points of discontinuity

We recall from
[
GI01
]
the notion of strong proximinality of a subspace
Y\subset X
. Our definition is equivalent to the one given in
[
GI01
]
. A proximinal subspace
Y\subset X
is said to be
strongly proximinal
if for
x\in X
and any sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset Y
such that
d(x,Y)=\lim_{n}\|x-y_{n}\|
(such a sequence is called a
minimizing sequence for
x
), there is a subsequence
\{y_{n_{k}}\}
of
\{y_{n}\}
and a sequence
\{z_{k}\}\subset P_{Y}(x)
such that
\|y_{n_{k}}-z_{k}\|\rightarrow 0
. We note that any minimizing sequence is a bounded sequence and that any subsequence (or subnet) of a minimizing sequence is again minimizing (the meaning of a
minimizing net
should be clear). It is easy to see that any finite-dimensional subspace of a Banach space is strongly proximinal.

The following theorem gives in particular, for a dual Banach space and for
\tau
the
w^{*}
-topology, a procedure for getting vectors in
S(X^{*})
that are not of continuity for
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),\|\cdot\|)
(see Corollary
4.2
below).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Let
Y
be a Chebyshev not strongly proximinal subspace of
X
. Let
\tau
be a locally convex topology on
X
such that
\|.\|
is a
\tau
-lower semi-continuous function. Assume that, for every
x\notin Y
, every minimizing sequence in
Y
for
x
has a subnet that
\tau
-converges to an element in
Y
. Then, there is
z\in S(X)
that is not a point of
\tau
-norm continuity for
id:(B(X),\tau)\longrightarrow(B(X),\|.\|)
.

Proof.

Since
Y
is Chebyshev and not strongly proximinal, there exists
x\in X
(notice that
P_{Y}(x)
is a singleton, say
\{y_{0}\}
) and a sequence
\{y_{n}\}
in
Y
that is minimizing for
x
and such that for every subsequence
\{y_{n_{k}}\}
we have
\|y_{n_{k}}-y_{0}\|\not\rightarrow 0
. Thus, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a subsequence of
\{y_{n}\}
(denoted again
\{y_{n}\}
) such that
\|y_{n}-y_{0}\|\geq\varepsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Without loss of generality, we may and do assume that
(\|x-y_{0}\|=)\ d(x,Y)=1
.

By assumption, there exists a subnet
\{y_{n_{i}}\}
of the sequence
\{y_{n}\}
that
\tau
-converges to some
y^{\prime}\in Y
. The lower semicontinuity assumption and again the fact that
Y
is Chebyshev ensures that
y^{\prime}=y_{0}
.

Put
z:=x-y_{0}
and
z_{i}:=x-y_{n_{i}}
for all
i
. Then,
z_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\rightarrow}}z
,
\|z_{i}\|\rightarrow 1
, and
\|z_{i}-z\|\geq\varepsilon
for all
i
. It is obvious then that
\{z_{i}/\|z_{i}\|\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\rightarrow}}z
and
\{z_{i}/\|z_{i}\|\}\not\rightarrow 0
in norm. This shows the statement.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
Y
be a
w^{\ast}
-closed Chebyshev not strongly proximinal subspace of
X^{*}
. Then, there is
z\in S(X^{*})
that is not a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),\|\cdot\|)
.

Proof.

The fact that
Y
is
w^{*}
-closed, that every minimizing sequence is bounded, and the
w^{*}
-compactness of
B(Y)
, ensure that the hypothesis of Theorem
4.1
are satisfied for
\tau
the
w^{\ast}
-topology.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X
be an infinite-dimensional reflexive Banach space. Then, there is a reflexive Banach space
Z
containing
X
as a subspace of codimension
1
, such that
Z
has a unit vector that is not even a point of sequential continuity of
id:(B(Z),w)\longrightarrow(B(Z),\|.\|)
.

Proof.

By
[
Na06
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists a Banach space
Z\supset X
such that
X
is of codimension
1
in
Z
(hence
Z
is also reflexive), and
X
is a Chebyshev not strongly proximinal subspace of
Z
. The rest is a consequence of Theorem
4.1
, and the sequential continuity assertion follows just from the reflexivity of
Z
and the Eberlein–Šmulyan theorem.
∎

5.
Points of continuity in higher duals (I)

In this and the following sections, we will work on nonreflexive Banach space.

5.1.
Extreme points that are of continuity

An open problem in this theory is to find conditions under which the existence of a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
implies the existence of
extreme
points that are also points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
. This question will be considered in Section
9
.

We record the following simple lemma for later reference. Here,
NA(X^{**})
denotes the set of elements in
X^{**}
that attain their norm on
S(X^{*})
:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
x^{**}\in B(X^{**})
be a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
. Then

(1)

x^{**}\in S(X)
.

(2)

x^{**}\in\,{\rm Ext}\,B(X^{**})
if and only if
x^{**}\in\,{\rm Ext}\,B(X)
.

(3)

x^{**}\in NA(X^{**})
.

Proof.

Item (1) is a consequence of the
w^{*}
-density of
B(X)
in
B(X^{**})
and Lemma
2.2
above. For Item (2), observe that an extreme point of
B(X^{**})
that lies in
X
is an extreme point of
B(X)
. In general, the converse is not true, see Remark
5.2
below; however, in case that
x\in\,{\rm Ext}\,B(X)
is of continuity of
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
, it is also in
\,{\rm Ext}\,(B_{X^{**}})
, a consequence of Lemma
2.1
above. Item (3) follows from (1).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

It is worth to mention that
\,{\rm Ext}\,B(X^{**})=\,{\rm Ext}\,B(X)
if and only if the space
X
is reflexive, a consequence of James’ characterization of reflexivity. Extreme points of
B(X)
that are also extreme points of
B(X^{**})
are called
preserved extreme
(see, e.g.,
[
GMZ
]
for an account of such a concept).

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{\ast\ast}/X
is a reflexive space. Spaces with this property are analogues of quasi-reflexive spaces (Civin and Yood,
[
CY57
]
). We recall that
X
is said to be a
quasi-reflexive space
if
X^{\ast\ast}/X
is a finite-dimensional space.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{\ast\ast}/X
is reflexive. Let
x^{\ast}\in S(X^{\ast})
be a point of continuity for
id:(B(X^{\ast}),w^{\ast})\longrightarrow(B(X^{\ast}),w)
. Then it continues to be a point of
w^{\ast}
-
w
-continuity of the unit balls of all odd higher duals of
X
. In particular, if
x^{*}
is also an extreme point, then it is a preserved extreme point with respect to those odd higher duals.

Proof.

As we mentioned several times,
X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus X^{\perp}
. Notice that the topology
w
in
X^{***}
induces the topology
w
on
X^{*}
. Let
Q:X^{\ast\ast\ast}\rightarrow X^{\ast}
be the canonical projection associated to this decomposition. Let
\{\tau_{\alpha}\}\subset S(X^{\ast\ast\ast})
be a net such that
\tau_{\alpha}\rightarrow x^{\ast}
in
(B(X^{\ast\ast\ast}),w^{*})
. Hence
Q(\tau_{\alpha})\rightarrow x^{\ast}
in
(B(X^{\ast}),w^{*})
. Since
x^{*}
is a point of continuity for
id:(B(X^{\ast}),w^{\ast})\longrightarrow(B(X^{\ast}),w)
we have
Q(\tau_{\alpha})\rightarrow x^{\ast}
in
(B(X^{\ast}),w)
(and so
Q(\tau_{\alpha})\rightarrow x^{*}
in
(B(X^{***}),w)
).

We get, in particular,
(X^{\perp}\ni)\ \tau_{\alpha}-Q(\tau_{\alpha})\rightarrow 0
in
(X^{\ast\ast\ast},w^{*})
. Observe that
(X^{**}/X)^{*}
is isomorphic to
X^{\perp}
, hence
X^{\perp}
is also reflexive. Thus,
\tau_{\alpha}-Q(\tau_{\alpha})\rightarrow 0
in
(X^{\ast\ast\ast},w)
. Therefore
\tau_{\alpha}\rightarrow x^{\ast}
in
(B(X^{\ast\ast\ast}),w)
and so
x^{*}
is of continuity of
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{***}),w)
.

Since
X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus X^{\perp}
, the space
X^{***}/X^{*}
is reflexive, too, since it is isomorphic to
X^{\perp}
. Now we consider the duality
\langle X^{(IV)},X^{***}\rangle
. The dual space of
X^{***}/X^{*}
is
(X^{*})^{\perp}
, so this last space is also reflexive. Since
X^{(IV)}=X^{**}\oplus(X^{*})^{\perp}
, and then
X^{(IV)}/X^{**}
is isomorphic to
(X^{*})^{\perp}
, it follows that
X^{(IV)}/X^{**}
is reflexive and we can apply iteratively what has already been proved to reach the conclusion.

The assertion about extreme points follows from the first part of the statement and Lemma
5.1
.
∎

We shall resume the study of extreme points of continuity in Section
8
below.

5.2.
Continuity in higher duals

Related to Lemma

, let us mention that we don’t know of any geometric condition on

6.
M-embedded and Hahn–Banach smooth spaces

Some properties of a Banach space remain when going to higher duals. For example, if
\mathcal{A}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, then
\mathcal{A}^{**}
is again a
C^{*}
-algebra. Similarly, if
X
is an abstract
L
-space or
M
-space, then their biduals are of the same type (see, e.g.,
[
La74
]
). Thus it is an interesting question to see how some of the geometric properties of a Banach space studied here can be carried to its higher duals.

Theorem
3.2
above says, in particular, that for a Hahn–Banach smooth Banach space
X
, every
x^{*}\in S(X^{*})
is a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
. We shall show in Theorem
6.2
that, under the slightly stronger assumption of
M
-embeddability (see Remark
6.1
below), it is even of continuity for
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{***}),w)
.

Remark 6.1
.

Every
M
-embedded Banach space is Hahn–Banach smooth
. More generally, if
M
is an
M
-ideal in a Banach space
Y
, then every
m^{*}\in M^{*}
has a unique norm-preserving extension to a functional
y^{*}\in Y^{*}
(see, e.g.,
[
HWW93
, Proposition 1.12]
). As a consequence,
M^{*}
can be viewed as a subspace of
Y^{*}
and
Y^{*}=M^{*}\oplus_{1}M^{\bot}
(see, e.g.,
[
HWW93
, Remark I.1.13]
).

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space, and let
M\subset X
be an
M
-ideal in
X
. Then every
m^{*}\in S(M^{*})
that is a point of continuity for
id:(B(M^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(M^{*}),w)
, is a point of continuity for
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
. In particular, if
X
is an
M
-embedded space, then the set of points of continuity for
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{***}),w)
is precisely
S(X^{*})
.

Proof.

As we mentioned in Remark
6.1
above,
M^{*}
can be identified with a subspace of
X^{*}
, and so the conclusion makes sense. Let
Q:X^{*}\longrightarrow X^{*}
be an
L
-projection with range
M^{\perp}
. Put
P:=(I-Q)
so that
range(P)=M^{*}
. Let
(x^{*}_{i})
be a net in
B(X^{*})
that
w^{*}
-converges to
m^{*}
. Then,
P(x^{*}_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}P(m^{*})\ (=m^{*})
. Thus, by hypothesis,
P(x^{*}_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}P(m^{*})\ (=m^{*})
weakly. Notice that

For the particular case, apply the result to the space

, according to Lemma

above.
∎

Remark 6.4

Let

. In this case, the weak
∗
-topology of

is uniquely determined. In this direction, we consider the following example:

Example 6.5
.

Let
c
be the space of convergent sequences, endowed with the supremum norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Notice that
c^{*}
is linearly isometric to
\ell_{1}
via the mapping
S:c^{\ast}\longrightarrow\ell^{1}
defined by
S(\alpha)=(\alpha({\bf 1})-\sum_{1}^{\infty}\alpha(e_{n}),\alpha(e_{1}),\alpha(e_{2}),...)
for
\alpha\in c^{*}
, where the

at the

elsewhere, and
{\bf 1}
is the constant-

[

, Exercise 2.31]
).

Consider
(c,\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
. Certainly,
c_{0}
is a closed subspace. Remark
5.2
says, in particular, that every point

of continuity for

. However, not every point of

is such. In fact, no point in

is of continuity.

In order to see this, let
T:=S^{-1}:\ell_{1}\longrightarrow c^{*}
(a linear isometry onto). Then
T^{*}:c^{**}\longrightarrow\ell_{\infty}
is also a linear isometry onto. We shall translate the situation in

via

to the one in
\ell_{\infty}
(by the way, as it is clear,

corresponds via

to

, and

to
w(\ell_{\infty},\ell_{\infty}^{*}))
. It is easy to compute

, where
x=(x_{n})\in c\ (\subset c^{**})
. We get
T^{*}(x)=(l,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\ (\subset\ell_{\infty})
, where
l:=\lim_{n}x_{n}
. Let
x=(x_{n})\in c\setminus c_{0}
. Then
u:=T^{*}(x)=(l,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, where

. Put
u_{n}:=(l,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},0,0,\ldots)\ (\in c_{0}\subset\ell_{\infty})
for

. Then, the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
in

is

-convergent to

, due to the fact that on
B(\ell_{\infty})
the topology

and the one of coordinate-wise convergence coincide. Due to the fact that
(c_{0})^{\perp\perp}=c_{0}
in the duality
\langle\ell_{\infty},\ell_{\infty}^{*}\rangle
, there exists

such that
\langle u,u^{*}\rangle\not=0
. Thus,
(0=)\ \langle u_{n},u^{*}\rangle\not\rightarrow\langle u,u^{*}\rangle\ (\not=0)
. This shows that
\{u_{n}\}\not\rightarrow u
in the topology

. Since

for

, it follows that

is not a point of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity for the identity mapping
id:(B(c^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(c^{**}),w)
, as announced.

It is known that
\ell^{1}
is the unique predual of
\ell^{\infty}
and that
c_{0}
is an
M
-embedded space (in
\ell^{\infty}
) (see, e.g.,
[
HWW93
, Examples 1.4 (a)]
). The concrete example above (Example
6.5
) has a very general geometric analogue. We need the following definition (see, e.g.,
[
Go87
]
): A Banach space

is said to be a

(of

) if, for every Banach space

, every isometric isomorphism from

onto

is

-continuous (and so it is the adjoint of an isometric isomorphism from

onto

). This is an (apparently) strengthening of the notion of a
unique predual
(if
Y
is a Banach space such that

and
X^{*}
are isometrically isomorphic, then
X
and
Y
are isometrically isomorphic, too; it seems that the problem of the coincidence of both notions is still open). Let us recall the following result:

Proposition 6.6
.

[
HWW93
, Proposition III.2.10]

Let
X
be an
M
-embedded nonreflexive Banach space. Then, (i)
X^{*}
is a strongly unique predual of
X^{**}
, and (ii)
X
is not a strongly unique predual of
X^{*}
.

An application of Proposition
6.6
to the question of

-
w
-continuity in duals of nonreflexive
M
-embedded spaces is the following:

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
X
be a nonreflexive
M
-embedded space. Then, there exists a subspace
Y
of
X^{**}
such that
X^{**}=Y^{**}
and
Y\setminus X\neq\emptyset
. Consequently there is no point of continuity for
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
.

Proof.

To organise the proof, we shall split the argument in several steps:

(a) Since
X
is not a strongly unique predual of
X^{*}
(see Proposition
6.6
above), there exists a Banach space
Y
and an isometric onto isomorphism
T:X^{*}\longrightarrow Y^{*}
that is not
w^{*}
-
w^{*}
-continuous. Obviously,
Y^{**}
is isometrically isomorphic to
X^{**}
(and so
Y
can be identified to
T^{*}(Y)\ (\subset X^{**})
). The fact that
T
is not
w^{*}
-
w^{*}
-continuous shows that
T^{*}(Y)\setminus X\not=\emptyset
. Indeed, there exists a
w^{*}
-null net
\{x^{*}_{i}\}
in
X^{*}
such that
\{T(x^{*}_{i})\}
is not
w^{*}
-null in
Y^{*}
. Assume that
T^{*}(Y)\subset X
. Then, given
y\in Y
we have
\langle y,T(x^{*}_{i})\rangle=\langle T^{*}(y),x^{*}_{i}\rangle\rightarrow 0
. Thus,
T(x^{*}_{i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w^{*}}}{{\rightarrow}}0
, a contradiction. (Incidentally, that
T^{*}(Y)\setminus X\not=\emptyset
is, in fact,
equivalent
to the non-
w^{*}
-
w^{*}
-continuity of
T
.) This proves the first part of the statement.

(b) Let
x^{**}_{0}\in B(X^{**})
be a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
(notice that, by Lemma
2.2
,
x^{**}_{0}\in S(X^{**})
). It is easy to prove that
x^{**}_{0}\in X\cap T^{*}(Y)
. Indeed, by Lemma
5.1
,
x^{**}_{0}\in S(X)
. The space
T^{*}(Y)
is a predual of
X^{*}
. By Lemma
5.1
again,
x^{**}_{0}\in T^{*}(Y)
. (Another argument for the same conclusion is the following: First, it is simple to show that
B(T^{*}(Y))
is
w^{*}
-dense in
B(X^{**})
. Thus, we can find a net
\{T^{*}(y_{i})\}
in
B(T^{*}(Y))
that
w^{*}
-converges to
x^{**}_{0}
and, by assumption, it is also
w
-convergent (to
x^{**}_{0}
). Since
T^{*}(Y)
is
\|\cdot\|
-closed in
X^{**}
, we get
x^{**}_{0}\in T^{*}(Y)
.) So we conclude that

(2)

x^{**}_{0}\in X\cap T^{*}(Y).

(c) For the last part, we shall prove the following

Claim
:
Given
x^{**}_{0}\in S(X^{**})
a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
(and then
x^{**}_{0}\in S(X))
, the space
Y
considered above can be chosen so that
x^{**}_{0}\not\in T^{*}(Y)
.

To show the Claim, we need to consider the construction in the proof of
[
HWW93
, Proposition III.2.10]
: There, a point
y^{***}\in X^{\perp}\ (=(X^{**}/X)^{*})
such that
\|y^{***}\|=1
and that attains its norm at
\widehat{x^{**}}\in S(X^{**}/X)
is selected, and the
w^{*}
-closed hyperplane
H:=\ker\widehat{x^{**}}
of
X^{\perp}
is constructed. Our observation is that, given an arbitrary
x_{0}\in S(X)
, we can find
p^{*}_{0}\in S(X^{*})
such that
\langle x_{0},p^{*}_{0}\rangle=1
. This is used to define
Z:=H\oplus\mathbb{R}(y^{***}+sp^{*}_{0})
as in the original construction, where

is an arbitrary real number in
(0,1]
. Thus

is a
w^{*}
closed subspace of
X^{***}
. It was shown during the proof of
[
HWW93
, Proposition III.2.10]
that
X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus Z
, and that the canonical associated projection
\pi:X^{***}\rightarrow X^{*}
associated to this decomposition is a contraction (here we need the

-embedded hypothesis again) . It is enough to observe now that
Z^{\perp}\ (\subset X^{**})
(the orthogonal to
Z
with respect to the duality
\langle X^{**},X^{***}\rangle
) is a space with the properties enjoyed by
T^{*}(Y)
(the space mentioned at the beginning of the proof). It is clear then that

. This concludes the proof of the Claim.

(d) To finalize the proof of the theorem it is enough to use the Claim together with (b) (in particular, equation (
2
) above).
∎

We now give an application to von Neumann algebras.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a von Neumann algebra and the predual

of

has the RNP. Then, there is no point of continuity of

By
[

, Proposition IV.2.9]
, there exists a family
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of Hilbert spaces such that
\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus_{\ell^{\infty}}\mathcal{L}(H_{i})
and

(recall that

is the space of all trace class operators with trace norm). Thus

. It is known that

.
∎

7.
Geometry of higher duals of Banach spaces

Let
X
be a nonreflexive Banach space. Let
x^{\ast}\in S(X^{\ast})
be a point of
w^{\ast}
-
w
-continuity. In general
x^{\ast}
need not be a point of
w^{\ast}
-
w
-continuity in
S(X^{\ast\ast\ast})
. We will be using
[
GI02
, Proposition 4.1]
in what follows for a renorming technique to produce such examples (see Theorem
7.3
below). For instances of spaces satisfying the hypothesis there, see, e.g.,
[
LT77
, Theorem 1.d.3.]
.

The concept of very smoothness lies in between Gâteaux and Fréchet smoothness (see, e.g.,
[
Di75
]
): Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space. A point
0\not=x\in X
is said to be a
very smooth point
(or the norm of
X
is
very smooth at
x
) if the norm of
X
is Gâteaux smooth at
x
and the duality map from
X
to
X^{*}
is
\|\cdot\|
-
w
-upper semi-continuous at
x
. The relevance to our topic is the equivalence between (i) and (iv) in
[
GI02
, Remark 3.1]
, a result that we reproduce here for the sake of completeness:

Lemma 7.1
.

[
GI02
, Remark 3.1]

If
x\in S(X)
is a point of Gâteaux smoothness, and
x^{*}=\|\cdot\|^{\prime}(x)
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)
x
is a very smooth point of
X
.

(ii) The duality mapping is
\|\cdot\|
-
w
-upper semicontinuous at
x
.

(iii)
x^{*}
has a unique norm-preserving extension to
X^{**}
.

(iv)
id:(B(X^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{*}),w)
is continuous at
x^{*}
.

(v)
x
is a point of Gâteaux smoothness for
X^{**}
.

(vi) If
\{x^{*}_{n}\}
is a sequence in
B(X^{*})
such that
\langle x,x^{*}_{n}\rangle\rightarrow 1
, then
x^{*}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\rightarrow}}x^{*}
.

Remark 7.2
.

Observe that the equivalence
(iii)\Leftrightarrow(iv)
in Lemma
7.1
holds for every point
x^{*}\in S(X^{*})
, not necessarily the derivative of a point of Gâteaux differentiability in
S(X)
. This was the content of Lemma
3.1
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{**}/X
is a nonreflexive separable space. Then, there is a renorming of
X
, denoted by
Z
, and
x^{*}\in S(Z^{*})
, such that
x^{*}
is a point of continuity of
id:(B(Z^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(Z^{*}),w)
but not a point of continuity of
id:(B(Z^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(Z^{***}),w)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
X
is separable. In
[
GI02
, Proposition 4.1]
it is proved that, in these circumstances, there exists an equivalent norm
|\hskip-0.70007pt\|\cdot|\hskip-0.70007pt\|
on
X
such that, denoting
Z
the Banach space
(X,|\hskip-0.70007pt\|\cdot|\hskip-0.70007pt\|)
, we can find a very smooth point
x\in S(Z)
which is not a very smooth point of
Z^{**}
. Thus, it follows from Lemma
3.1
above that
x^{*}
is a point of continuity for
id:(B(Z^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(Z^{*}),w)
, yet it is not a point of continuity for
id:(B(Z^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(Z^{***}),w)
. This proves the assertion in this particular case.

For the general case, notice that, by
[
Va77
]
, the hypothesis implies that
X
is the topological direct sum of a reflexive Banach space
R
and a Banach space
S
such that
S^{**}
is separable. In particular,
S^{**}/S
is separable (and nonreflexive as it is isomorphic to
X^{**}/X
). Thus, by what has been already proved, there exists an equivalent norm
\||.\||
on
S
and a point of continuity
f_{0}\in B((S,\||.\||)^{*})
of
id:(B((S,\||.\||)^{*}),w^{*})\to(B((S,\||.\||)^{*}),w)
that is not a point of continuity of
id:(B((S,\||.\||)^{***}),w^{*})\to(B((S,\||.\||)^{***}),w)
. Let us define an equivalent norm (called again
\||.\||
) on
X
by letting
X=R\oplus_{\infty}S
. To simplify the notation, we denote
(X,\||.\||)
by
Z
. Obviously,
Z^{*}=R^{*}\oplus_{1}S^{*}
. Let
f\in Z^{*}
be such that
f|_{R}=0
and
f|_{S}=f_{0}
. By Theorem
6.2
, it is a point of continuity of
id:(B(Z^{*}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(Z^{*}),w)
. However, the fact that two different norm-preserving extensions of
f_{0}
from
S^{**}
to
S^{(IV)}
exists shows as before that

is not a point of continuity of
id:(B(Z^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(Z^{***}),w)
.
∎

8.
Extreme points of continuity

Since
id:(B(X)^{*},w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X)^{*},w)
is an affine map, it is natural to ask whether
id
has an extreme point that is of continuity as soon as it has a point of continuity. This was mentioned already in Subsection
5.1
.

For any positive measure space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
and for
1\leq p<\infty
, let
L^{p}(\mu,X)
be the space of Bochner integrable functions (for an authoritative reference see, e.g.,
[
DU77
]
). In the case of points of
w
-
\|\cdot\|
-continuity, for a non-atomic measure
\mu
and for
1<p<\infty
, it was shown in
[
HL93
, Corollary 2.4]
that
f
is a point of
w
-
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-continuity of
B_{L^{p}(\mu,X)}
if and only if
f
is a denting point of
B_{L^{p}(\mu,X)}
(equivalently, an extreme point of
w
-
\|\cdot\|_{p}
-continuity). The next theorem treats the
w^{*}
-
w
-case in the same context.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a positive measure space. If
\Lambda\in S(L^{1}(\mu))
is a point of continuity of
id:(B(L^{1}(\mu)^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(L^{1}(\mu)^{**}),w)
, then
\mu
has an atom. Hence
S(L^{1}(\mu))
has an extreme point which is also a point of continuity of
id:(B(L^{1}(\mu)^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(L^{1}(\mu)^{**}),w)
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\in S(L^{1}(\mu))
. It is known that
L^{1}(\mu)^{*}
, being a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra, is isometric to
C(K)
for some compact Hausdorff space
K
. Thus, consider
\Lambda\in(L^{1}(\mu))^{**}=C(K)^{*}
, a point of continuity of
id:(B(L^{1}(\mu)^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(L^{1}(\mu)^{**}),w)
. It is not difficult to show that
\Lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}\delta(k_{i})
, where the
k_{i}
’s are isolated points in
K
(for those
i
for which
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
) and
0\leq\alpha_{i}\leq 1
with
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}=1
. By
[
HWW93
, Example.IV.1.1]
,
L^{1}(\mu)
is an
L
-summand in
L^{1}(\mu)^{**}
. Let
P:L^{1}(\mu)^{**}\longrightarrow L^{1}(\mu)
be the corresponding
L
-projection. We claim that
\delta(k_{i})\in L^{1}(\mu)
when
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. It is enough to show that
P(\delta(k_{i}))=\delta(k_{i})
for all
i
such that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Since
\Lambda\in S(L^{1}(\mu))
,
\|\Lambda\|=\|P(\Lambda)\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}\|P(\delta(k_{i}))\|\leq 1
. Consequently,
\|P(\delta(k_{i}))\|=1
. Again,
\|\delta(k_{i})\|=\|P(\delta(k_{i}))\|+\|\delta(k_{i})-P(\delta(k_{i}))\|
as
P
is an
L
-projection. Thus
P(\delta(k_{i}))=\delta(k_{i})
for all
i
such that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
. Since
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for at least one
i
, one can show that the corresponding
\delta(k_{i})
gives raise to an atom for the measure
\mu
, as it is an extreme point of
B(L^{1}(\mu))
.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
X=L^{1}[0,1]
, where
[0,1]
carries the Lebesgue measure. Then
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
is nowhere continuous, but
id:(B(X^{(IV)}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{(IV)}),w)
is continuous at some extreme point in
B(X^{**})
.

Proof.

Since the Lebesgue measure is non-atomic, by Theorem
8.1
we have that
id:(B(X^{**}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{**}),w)
is nowhere continuous. As before, let
X^{*}=C(K)
for some compact Hausdorff space
K
. Fix
k\in K
. We have
C(\Omega)^{*}=\text{span}\{\delta(k)\}\oplus_{1}N
for some closed subspace
N
. Thus
C(K)^{**}=\text{span}\{\delta(k)\}^{\perp}\oplus_{\infty}N^{\perp}
. Hence
N^{\perp}
is an one-dimensional
M
-summand. Let
N^{\perp}=\text{span}\{f\}
, for some
f\in C(K)^{**}
. It is well known that
C(K)^{**}=C(K^{\prime})
for some compact space
K^{\prime}
. Hence by
[
HWW93
, Example 1.4.(a)]
, we have that
f=\chi_{\{k_{0}\}}
, for some isolated point
k_{0}\in K^{\prime}
. Now since
C(K^{\prime})^{\ast}=\text{span}\{\delta(k_{0})\}\oplus_{1}N^{\prime}
for some
w^{*}
-closed subspace
N^{\prime}\subset C(K^{\prime})
, it is easy to see by arguments indicated in Theorem
6.2
that
\delta(k_{0})
is a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{(IV)}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{(IV)}),\|.\|)
. Hence it is a point of continuity of
id:(B(X^{(IV)}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{(IV)}),w)
, which is an extreme point.
∎

9.
G_{\delta}
character of the set of points of continuity

We next investigate Banach space
X
with some geometric structure such that the set of points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{***}),w)
(respectively,
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{***}),\|\cdot\|)
) forms a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
.

We do not know examples of nonreflexive Banach spaces
X
for which
S(X^{***})
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(S(X^{(V)}),w^{*})
.

Let
X
be Banach space. Put

O_{n}:=X^{*}\setminus\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)B(X^{*}),\ \hbox{for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Obviously,
O_{n}
is
w^{*}
-open in
X^{*}
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is plain that

(3)

S(X^{*})=B(X^{*})\cap\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}O_{n},

hence
S(X^{*})
is a (dense)
G_{\delta}
subset of
(B(X^{*}),w^{*})
. Notice, too, that
S(X^{*})
is dense in
(B(X^{***}),w^{*})
, in particular in
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
, as it follows from Goldstine theorem.

Let
Q
be the canonical projection associated to the decomposition
X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus X^{\perp}
.
Put

(4)

{\mathcal{S}}:=\{x^{***}\in S(X^{\ast\ast\ast}):\ Q(x^{***})\in S(X^{\ast})\}.

In other words,
\mathcal{S}
is the set of all Hahn–Banach extensions of elements in
S(X^{*})
. It follows easily from (
3
) that
\mathcal{S}=B(X^{***})\cap\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}Q^{-1}(O_{n})\ (\subset S(X^{***}))
, hence
\mathcal{S}
is a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
(B(X^{***}),w^{*})
, in particular of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
.

The following result has a simple proof:

Proposition 9.1
.

A Banach space
X
is Hahn–Banach smooth if and only if
\mathcal{S}=S(X^{*})
.

Proof.

We shall have in mind that
X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus X^{\perp}
, and so that
Q:X^{***}\longrightarrow X^{*}
is the canonical projection associated to this decomposition.
Assume first that
X
is Hahn–Banach smooth. Let
x^{***}\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus,
Q(x^{***})\in S(X^{*})
, and
Q(x^{***})
has a unique Hahn–Banach extension to
X^{**}
. It follows that
x^{***}=Q(x^{***})\in S(X^{*})
. On the other hand, if
x^{*}\in S(X^{*})
then
x^{*}
is the unique Hahn–Banach extension to
X^{**}
, so
x^{*}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Assume now that
\mathcal{S}=S(X^{*})
. Let
x^{*}\in S(X^{*})
. If
y^{***}
is a different Hahn–Banach extension of
x^{*}
to
X^{**}
, then
y^{***}\in\mathcal{S}
. Moreover,
y^{***}=x^{*}+x^{\perp}
, where
x^{\perp}\in X^{\perp}
and, since
y^{***}\not=x^{*}
, it happens that
x^{\perp}\not=0
. Thus,
y^{***}\not\in X^{*}
, and this is a contradiction. This shows that
X
has property-(U) in
X^{**}
.
∎

In view of Proposition
9.1
and the comments preceding it, the proof of the following result should be clear (notice that
S(X^{*})
is always
w^{*}
-dense in
S(X^{***})
as soon as
X
is infinite-dimensional):

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
X
be a nonreflexive Hahn–Banach smooth space. Then,
S(X^{*})
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
.

Remark 9.3
.

It was noticed in
[
BR98
]
that the Hahn–Banach smoothness property is preserved by closed subspaces, quotients, and
c_{0}
-sums. We do not know if the property ‘
S(X^{\ast})
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
’ has a similar behaviour.

It follows from
[
HWW93
, Theorem III.4.6.(e)]
that any nonreflexive
M
-embedded space can be renormed so that
X^{*}
is strictly convex and
X
is again an
M
-embedded space in the new norm. Thus these examples satisfy the hypothesis of the following easy corollary, whose proof, that will be omitted, uses Lemma
5.1
above.

Corollary 9.4
.

Let
X
be a nonreflexive Hahn–Banach smooth space whose dual is strictly convex. Then,
S(X^{*})
is the set of extreme points of
B(X^{***})
that are points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity. These form a
G_{\delta}
dense subset of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
.

Remark 9.5
.

Most often in applications it is enough to consider ‘richness’ of points of
w^{\ast}
-
w
-continuity in
B(X^{*})
that are also norm attaining. This set is, in general, strictly smaller that the set of all points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity in
B(X^{*})
. For example, let
X
be a nonreflexive Hahn–Banach smooth Banach space. Then, the set of all points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity of
B(X^{*})
is precisely
S(X^{*})
(see Theorem
3.2
). However, not every point in
S(X^{*})
is norm-attaining, in view of James’ compactness theorem. On the other hand, if
X
is a nonreflexive M-embedded Banach space, then the set of all points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity of
B(X^{***})
is, according to Theorem
6.2
, precisely
S(X^{*})
, and all of them are clearly in
NA(X^{***})
. In this case, then, the set of all
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity points of
B(X^{***})
that are also norm-attaining is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
, according to Proposition
9.2
above.
We do not know of other geometric conditions (in the nonreflexive case) that may ensure a similar behaviour.

The analysis of the set of points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity that are also norm-attaining through the linear isometric identifications as done in Example
6.5
must be done carefully. For instance, (and we keep the notation there),
c_{0}
is M-embedded, hence, in particular, the element
\Lambda:=(1/2^{n})\in S(\ell_{1})
is of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity in
B(c_{0}^{*})\ (=B(\ell_{1}))
, and is clearly not norm-attaining. However,
T(\Lambda)\ (\in S(c^{*}))
satisfies
\langle{\tt 1},T(\Lambda)\rangle=1
(where
{\tt 1}\in c
is the constant 1 vector), so it attains its norm (and it is, to be sure, a point of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity). The reason for this odd behaviour is, of course, the fact that
T:\ell_{1}\longrightarrow c^{*}
is not
w^{*}
-
w^{*}
-continuous. By the way, the set of points of
w^{*}
-
w
-continuity of
B(c_{0}^{*})\ (=B(\ell_{1}))
is
S(c_{00})
(a dense
F_{\sigma}
subset of
(S(\ell_{1}),\|\cdot\|_{1})
), where
c_{00}
is the space of all finitely supported vectors in
c_{0}
.

The last result deals with spaces
X
that satisfy the stronger property that the
w^{*}
and norm topologies coincide on
S(X^{*})
. We recall that when
{\mathcal{K}}(X)
is an
M
-ideal in
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
, then
X
is an
M
-embedded space and the weak
∗
, weak and norm topologies coincide on
S(X^{*})
(see Propositions VI.4.4 and VI.4.6 in
[
HWW93
]
).

Corollary 9.6
.

Let
X
be a nonreflexive
M
-embedded space such that on
S(X^{*})
both the
w^{*}
and the
\|\cdot\|
topology coincide. Then, the set of points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\longrightarrow(B(X^{***}),\|.\|)
forms a
G_{\delta}
dense subset of
(S(X^{***}),w^{*})
.

Proof.

It is easy to prove that the set of points of continuity of
id:(B(X^{***}),w^{*})\to(B(X^{***}),\|\cdot\|)
is just
S(X^{*})
. By Theorem
3.2
,
X
is a Hahn–Banach smooth space. The result now follows from Theorem
9.2
.
∎

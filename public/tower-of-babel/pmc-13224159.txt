Early and long-term outcomes of cognitive communication disorders following traumatic brain injury at our institution
Authors: Mohammad Abdulsalam Soliman, Eed Fathalla Elnagar, Amr Mohamad Elsarnagawy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a leading cause of disability worldwide and is frequently associated with cognitive impairments affecting domains relevant to communication, which may be under-recognized, particularly after mild TBI (mTBI). Understanding the evolution of these deficits from the acute to chronic phase is essential for guiding assessment and rehabilitation.Methods: This retrospective observational study included patients with closed mTBI admitted to a tertiary referral trauma center between December 2023 and October 2025. Global cognitive performance, including domains relevant to communication, was assessed using the Arabic version of the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) on admission, at 3 months, and at 12 months post-injury. Demographic, clinical, and radiological data were collected. Changes in MoCA scores over time were analyzed.Results: A total of 364 patients were analyzed (mean age 27.45 ± 16.46 years; 79.1% males). The mean MoCA score on admission was 18.3 ± 3.2, indicating significant early cognitive impairment. Scores improved significantly at 3 months (25.1 ± 2.7) and at 12 months (29.0 ± 1.1) (p < 0.0001). Most recovery occurred within the first 3 months, with continued improvement up to 1 year.Conclusion: mTBI is associated with significant early cognitive impairment followed by substantial recovery over time. While global cognitive scores normalize in most patients, subtle higher-level deficits may persist, underscoring the importance of structured longitudinal assessment and multidisciplinary follow-up.

INTRODUCTION

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is one of the principal causes of death and disability in Western society, especially among younger people.[8] In Egypt, moderate and severe injuries account for 17.2% of all TBI presented cases. Males were predominantly affected 79% of cases.[17] It is also a major cause of cognitive and communicative impairments worldwide.[14]

Research has conventionally emphasized physical and gross neurological outcomes, whereas cognitive-communication disorders (CCDs) remain comparatively under-recognized despite their substantial functional impact. These disorders (affecting attention, memory, executive function, and pragmatic language) can significantly hinder social integration and quality of life.[19]

The outcomes of TBI vary depending on the severity, location of injury, and timing of assessment. Understanding the trajectory from early to late stages post-injury is essential for predicting long-term impacts and guiding rehabilitation.[20] There is a growing need to explore how cognitive-communication functions evolve following TBI, and whether early outcomes can predict long-term communicative deficits.

Cognitive outcomes can be assessed using tools such as the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), which evaluates multiple domains of cognition and can help identify subtle deficits often missed by routine neurological exams.[7] PostmTBI CCDs are frequently underrecognized, especially in the chronic phase, resulting in unmet rehabilitation needs.[9] Most existing assessments examine either the acute or chronic phase independently, limiting understanding of how deficits evolve.

By comparing early and late outcomes in cognitive domains relevant to communication using MoCA, this study provides a longitudinal perspective on cognitive-communication recovery after mTBI, offering insight into the time course of impairment and identifying patients at risk for persistent deficits.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective observational study included all patients with mild closed TBI (mTBI) admitted to the emergency unit of Al-Ahrar Teaching Hospital, a referral trauma center, between December 2023 and October 2025. Adult patients with mTBI were included regardless of imaging findings; however, those requiring neurosurgical intervention were excluded. Patients with moderate or severe head injuries, those requiring surgical intervention after initial resuscitation, patients younger than 18 years, and those with incomplete follow-up or with missing data were excluded.

mTBI was defined according to established criteria as a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of 13–15, with or without loss of consciousness (<30 min), post-traumatic amnesia (<24 h), or alteration of mental status, and without need for neurosurgical intervention.

All patients were managed according to standard head trauma guidelines. Computed tomography of the brain was performed on admission and repeated within 24 h or earlier if neurological deterioration occurred. Clinical and demographic data collected included age, sex, mechanism of injury, GCS on admission, associated injuries, clinical presentation, and radiological findings.

Global cognitive performance, including domains relevant to communication, was assessed using the Arabic version of the MoCA, which has a total score ranging from 0 to 30. Scores were interpreted as follows: 26–30 = normal cognition, 18–25 = mild cognitive impairment, 10–17 = moderate impairment, and <10 = severe impairment. Assessments were performed on admission, at 3 months, and at 12 months post-mTBI to track early and long-term recovery.

MoCA is a global cognitive screening tool that evaluates domains relevant to communication, including attention, memory, executive function, and language. However, the MoCA does not directly assess higher-level cognitive-communication abilities such as discourse or pragmatic language. Therefore, the findings of this study reflect global cognitive performance rather than direct measurement of CCDs.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 23. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Changes in MoCA scores between time points were explored using paired t-tests. Given the longitudinal repeated-measures design, paired comparisons do not fully account for within-subject correlations across multiple time points.

Therefore, the statistical analysis is presented as an exploratory evaluation of trends in cognitive performance over time rather than a fully modeled longitudinal analysis. Effect sizes (Cohen’s d) were calculated to provide an estimate of the magnitude of change. A p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. More robust approaches, such as repeated-measures analysis of variance or linear mixed-effects models, would provide a more comprehensive assessment and are recommended for future studies.

RESULTS

A total of 549 patients were admitted during the study period; of these, 364 patients with complete medical records and follow-up data were included in the final analysis. The remaining 185 patients were excluded due to incomplete follow-up or missing data. Among the analyzed cohort, 288 patients (79.1%) were male, with a mean age of 27.45 ± 16.46 years. Road traffic accidents represented the most common mechanism of injury. Clinical otorrhea was absent in 90.2% of patients. Radiological evaluation demonstrated that skull fractures were the most frequent abnormal finding. Detailed demographic, clinical, and radiological characteristics are presented in Table 2.

The majority of excluded patients were lost to follow-up after discharge or had incomplete documentation of follow-up assessments. Baseline demographic characteristics between included and excluded patients were broadly comparable, although detailed comparative analysis was limited by missing data.

The mean MoCA score on admission was 18.3 ± 3.2, indicating significant cognitive impairment at the time of injury. At 3 months post-injury, the mean score improved to 25.1 ± 2.7, and at one year, it further increased to 29 ± 1.1, approaching the normal range. There were statistically significant differences in MoCA scores between time points; however, these findings should be interpreted as indicative of longitudinal trends rather than definitive modeling of within-subject change [Table 3]. When applying the MoCA cutoff score of <26 to define cognitive impairment, approximately 99.2% of patients (n ≈ 361) were classified as impaired on admission. At 3 months post-injury, 63.0% of patients (n ≈ 229) remained below the cut-off, indicating persistent cognitive impairment in nearly two-thirds of the cohort. By 12 months, the proportion of patients scoring below 26 declined markedly to 0.3% (n ≈ 1), reflecting substantial normalization of global cognitive screening scores. However, normalization does not necessarily indicate full recovery of higher-level cognitive-communication function.

Effect size analysis demonstrated very large to massive improvements in global cognitive performance over time (Cohen’s d = 1.89–4.48), indicating that the observed changes are not only statistically significant but also clinically substantial [Table 3].

These results support that MoCA may serve as a screening tool for global cognitive impairment affecting domains relevant to communication, while highlighting the need for complementary assessments or formal speech-language pathology evaluation for comprehensive follow-up.

DISCUSSION

The present study provides a longitudinal evaluation of cognitive outcomes following mTBI, demonstrating significant early cognitive impairment with substantial recovery over time. Despite being classified as mild injuries, patients exhibited marked cognitive dysfunction at presentation, emphasizing that mTBI should not be considered a benign condition and may be associated with clinically meaningful impairments in domains relevant to communication in the acute phase.[6,13]

The demographic characteristics of the cohort, including the predominance of young males and the high proportion of road traffic accidents, are consistent with previous epidemiological reports from Egypt and other low- and middle-income countries.[17] This pattern reflects increased exposure of young adult males to high-risk mechanisms of injury and underscores the substantial socioeconomic burden of TBI, particularly when cognitive-communication impairments interfere with education, employment, and social participation.

Early cognitive impairment was prominent in this cohort, as reflected by the low mean MoCA score on admission. Deficits in attention, executive function, memory, and language, domains that are fundamental to effective communication, are well-recognized consequences of TBI and may not be detected through routine neurological examination alone.[2,18] The presence of these impairments in patients with mTBI highlights the importance of early cognitive screening, as CCDs may persist even when physical and neurological recovery appears complete.[14,19]

This study evaluates global cognitive domains that underpin communication rather than directly assessing CCDs. While the MoCA is a sensitive screening tool for overall cognitive impairment,[15] it does not capture higher-level communication abilities such as discourse organization, pragmatic language, and social interaction. Therefore, normalization of MoCA scores at 12 months likely reflects recovery in global cognition rather than full restoration of functional communication. Subtle but clinically meaningful deficits may persist, underscoring the need for targeted assessment and multidisciplinary follow-up.

This trajectory suggests that cognitive recovery following mTBI is most pronounced in the early post-injury period but may continue over the longer term.[17] These findings align with existing literature indicating that cognitive-communication recovery is a dynamic process influenced by neuroplasticity, rehabilitation, and individual patient factors.[6,11,12] Importantly, early improvement does not negate the functional impact of initial deficits, which may affect daily communication and social reintegration during the recovery period.[5,12] Despite methodological limitations, the magnitude and consistency of observed changes across all time points suggest a clinically meaningful pattern of recovery.

These findings are consistent with previous research, including the meta-analysis by Islam et al.,[9] which also reported high sensitivity but variable and often modest specificity for MoCA in detecting mild cognitive impairment. Despite near-normal mean MoCA scores at 12 months, clinically diagnosed impairment suggests that global cognitive screening may underestimate residual deficits. The near-ceiling MoCA scores at 12 months raise the possibility of a ceiling effect, potentially masking subtle but functionally significant deficits.

The relatively favorable long-term outcomes observed in this cohort may be partially explained by the young mean age of the patients. Age has been identified as a significant predictor of cognitive-communication performance following TBI, with older individuals demonstrating poorer outcomes in domains such as naming, verbal fluency, and processing speed. In contrast, younger patients may exhibit greater cognitive reserve and neural plasticity, facilitating recovery.[21] These findings highlight the importance of considering age when interpreting cognitive-communication trajectories and reinforce the need for focused research on older adults with TBI, a population that remains underrepresented in cognitive-communication outcome studies.

Although all patients met criteria for mTBI, a considerable proportion sustained associated injuries and radiological abnormalities, including skull fractures and cerebral contusions.[3] These findings challenge the traditional perception of mTBI as a purely functional injury and suggest that structural abnormalities, even in the absence of neurosurgical intervention, may contribute to early cognitive-communication dysfunction. Comprehensive radiological and clinical assessment is therefore essential to identify patients at increased risk of cognitive impairment and to guide appropriate monitoring and rehabilitation strategies.[3,4,21]

Initial injury severity, commonly assessed using the GCS, is often regarded as a predictor of outcome following TBI.[10] However, the significant early cognitive impairment observed in this cohort of patients with mTBI reinforces the concept that injury severity alone does not reliably predict cognitive-communication outcomes. Similarly, although road traffic accidents were the predominant mechanism of injury, previous research suggests that the mechanism alone does not consistently correlate with conversational discourse outcomes, indicating that recovery is influenced by a complex interaction of demographic, clinical, and neurobiological factors.[1,8]

Most patients were impaired at admission, two-thirds remained impaired at three months, and by 12 months, cognitive scores had nearly normalized, highlighting a clear recovery trajectory after mTBI.

Routine cognitive screening, combined with structured longitudinal follow-up and targeted assessment of communication-related cognitive domains, is essential to identify individuals at risk for persistent communication-related cognitive difficulties and to guide timely, focused rehabilitation interventions.[16] Importantly, cognitive screening in mTBI should not be limited to discharge evaluation but should extend into structured follow-up, particularly during the first three months post-injury, when the recovery trajectory appears most dynamic and clinically meaningful.

The observed recovery trajectory likely reflects a combination of spontaneous neurological recovery and neuroplasticity, particularly given the relatively young cohort. However, the contribution of rehabilitation interventions was not assessed and may have influenced outcomes.

Importantly, improvement in global cognitive screening scores does not necessarily equate to full functional recovery.[15] The discrepancy between normalized scores and persistent functional complaints highlights the gap between cognitive test performance and real-world communicative ability. Subtle deficits in discourse, pragmatic language, and social communication may persist despite apparently normal screening results, with potential consequences for daily functioning, social participation, and return to work or education.

From a clinical perspective, these findings support routine cognitive screening in all patients with mTBI, even when neurological examination appears normal. Early identification of impairment in cognitive domains relevant to communication is essential, particularly during the first 3 months post-injury, when recovery is most dynamic. In addition, patients reporting persistent communication difficulties should be considered for early referral to speech-language pathology services for comprehensive evaluation beyond global cognitive screening. Structured longitudinal follow-up is therefore critical to detect subtle but functionally significant deficits and to guide targeted rehabilitation strategies.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective, single-center design and relatively young cohort may limit generalizability. Loss to follow-up may have introduced selection bias, potentially overestimating recovery. Cognitive outcomes were assessed using MoCA, a global screening tool that may not detect subtle or higher-level deficits, and repeated testing may have introduced practice effects. The reference standard was based on routine clinical evaluation rather than comprehensive neuropsychological assessment. In addition, rehabilitation interventions were not evaluated. Finally, the use of paired t-tests does not account for within-subject correlations over time; more robust longitudinal analyses would strengthen future research.

CONCLUSION

mTBI is associated with significant early impairment in cognitive domains relevant to communication, with substantial recovery over the 1st year. However, normalization of global cognitive screening scores does not necessarily indicate full recovery of higher-level cognitive-communication function. As this study is based on a global cognitive screening tool, it does not directly assess CCDs.

These findings highlight the importance of comprehensive, multidisciplinary assessment, including communication-focused evaluation, and structured longitudinal follow-up to identify subtle but functionally meaningful residual deficits and optimize patient outcomes.

Integrating fault detection and classification in microgrids using supervised machine learning considering fault resistance uncertainty
Authors: Morteza Barkhi, Javad Pourhossein, Seyed Ali Hosseini
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Microgrids (MGs) can enhance the consumers’ reliability. Nevertheless, besides significant outcomes, some challenges arise. Regarding the intermittent nature of Renewable Energy Resources (RESs), MGs are not operated radially. Accordingly, the reliable protection of MGs considering uncertainty in RESs is crucial for planners and operators. This paper uses data analysis to extract knowledge from locally available measurements using RMS values of symmetrical components. The learning-based characteristic of the suggested technique with a low computational burden exempts the need for an available communication infrastructure in the MG. The Support Vector Machine (SVM) technique is applied to train the Intelligent Electronic Devices to have a reliable MG protection scheme. The proposed method, which performs fault detection and classification together, just requires local information and functions effectively to discriminate faulty from normal conditions considering different uncertainty of resistance faults. Digital simulations on an MV test network were conducted to construct an appropriate database to consider some aspects of uncertainty in the network. The various faults considering their uncertainty, the different modes of operation, the uncertainty of RESs generation, and the load levels are combined to produce myriad scenarios. The simulation results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive protection approach in accurately distinguishing different system modes and consistently protecting the MG, achieving an accuracy rate of 99.75%. Furthermore, it offers the MG an optimal protection scheme that is not limited by selectivity constraints across diverse conditions.

Introduction

MGs have been introduced as a promising solution for continuous and reliable power supply to consumers for reducing greenhouse gas emissions without using fossil fuels. Moreover, blackouts are not rare anymore1 and MGs can supply flexible loads and provide backup electricity for the grid particularly when extreme weather conditions are about to leave the customers short on power2. It is shown that a power system3 with installed MGs can sustain continuous operation under severe conditions without unplanned or even scheduled outages4. The need to improve resiliency5 in the power system has led to extensive acceptance integration of RESs in MGs.

In the forthcoming energy systems, RESs with the support of cutting-edge information technologies will be firmly integrated into the power systems6. Therefore, a huge amount of produced data will help the existing capability of analysis to progress7.

Main challenges

The integration of DERs into distribution networks has introduced many technical challenges such as protection coordination8. The traditional protection schemes are neither reliable nor affordable. The reliable operation of MGs needs a proper functioning of the protection system, which has received a considerable level of attention from academia and industry experts. For the time being, several comprehensive review papers have been published9–16.

Coordinating the protection devices in MGs becomes challenging or even unfeasible when RESs are integrated. Additionally, the intermittent nature of RESs leads to variable fault currents, further complicating protection coordination. When the operating condition of MG changes, the protection system must identify it, and the way it determines the changes is of crucial importance for the safe and reliable operation of MG. The adaptive protection scheme depends on the type of communication network. Accordingly, there are three possible methods: centralized, decentralized, and communication-less10. The centralized methods necessitate a considerable investment in developing communication infrastructure17. The technique presented in this paper is a communication-less technique that relies on local measurements including instantaneous and RMS values.

DER application in the MG leads to bidirectional fault currents in some Sect18. This is not the case in traditional distribution networks, where fault current flows from upstream to downstream. Therefore, the protection schemes with fixed thresholds are not always appropriate for changing conditions of MGs19. ML-based methods are fast enough and accurate to be used in the adaptive protection of MGs20.

Overcurrent causes damage to electronic switches in the DERs and a voltage source converter must restrict its reference currents to keep the components safe and healthy. According to IEEE Standard 1547.2, the fault current of inverter-based DERs is very restricted (twice the load current in normal conditions at maximum level). Therefore, the fault current level remains low, which has an adverse effect on relay operation. Nevertheless, it has been widely investigated in the literature.

The status of DERs exacerbates the wrong performance of protection relays. Some solutions like differential protection can be used, but they are expensive for distribution lines21.

The uncertainty in fault impedance significantly impacts the accuracy of protection devices in electrical systems. Fault impedance can vary due to several factors, such as changes in the physical properties of the conductors, varying environmental conditions, and the intermittent nature of RES. These variations can cause protection devices to either fail to detect faults or to misidentify the severity and location of faults. Consequently, the reliability of the entire protection scheme is compromised, leading to potential delays in fault clearance and increased risk of damage to equipment and infrastructure.

Recent interest emphasizes separate fault detection and classification, especially through data-driven machine-learning approaches. However, it is crucial to recognize that for effective fault diagnosis in MGs, fault detection and classification should be performed simultaneously22,23. An integrated approach ensures a more efficient and accurate response to faults, enhancing the reliability of the MG. By progressing state-of-the-art tools that combine both detection and classification in a unified process, the robustness and resilience of MGs can significantly be improved24.

Literature review

An adaptive scheme using differential protection was suggested for MGs25 to improve the reliability when wind farms are integrated26. The method using swarm intelligence resolves the difficulty of threshold setting in conventional differential schemes. Accordingly, a threshold is set based on off-line calculations. However, the process does not consider the fault uncertainty and communication requirements.

A real-time adaptive scheme for overcurrent protection for MGs with a considerable share of RESs in distribution systems is proposed in20. Two factors were used in the proposed real-time adaptive protection: the acceleration and the tuning factor. They are exploited to take into account the value of fault impedance before and during the fault.

The study, reported in27, proposed a novel method for implementing intelligent differential protection in MGs using a nonlinear technique for signal transformation, which employed a kind of feature extraction method on the current phasors and the related symmetrical components. This process extracts a differential vector of features, which is then utilized as input in the two ML classifiers to train and test.

Reference28 proposed a new voltage-based fault detection method that supposes there is a communication infrastructure available. The active power differential29 is measured and the sensitivity of voltage is calculated in a particular protection zone. The paper involves modeling a new relay using DIgSILENT and the performance of the relay was examined across various fault scenarios.

The feature cosine and differential scheme was proposed in30. It exploits the ellipse equation and its parameters as an indicator to designate the location of the faulty section. Furthermore, the minimum least squares method is employed to evaluate and analyze the collective behavior of voltages and currents during faulty conditions.

A data mining approach was proposed in31. Besides, a fault analysis was performed to create two sets of features using symmetrical components and Clarke transforms. First, to categorize faults a single classifier is utilized. Then, two series classifiers are used, to determine the fault type and identify the phases where the faults occurred. According to the authors, the proposed technique offers enhanced accuracy even when operating at lower sampling frequencies.

An intelligent differential protection scheme using data mining to protect MGs is presented in32. Current and voltage signals at both ends of the feeders are processed by the discrete Fourier transform to investigate the condition before the fault. Then, a decision tree is constructed for registering the final relaying decision.

A Fast Recursive DFT algorithm was proposed in33 for tracking efficient fundamentals of changing signals. The optimal settings34 of protection devices in varying conditions are calculated by the fuzzy logic decision-making. This way, the proposed method enables the relay to deal with the uncertainties exposed by the intermittent nature of RESs. Simulations were conducted in the worst condition (i.e., maximum fault resistance of 20 Ω) to investigate the behavior of the protection system. Single-to-ground faults were exploited to verify the adaptivity of the relay under changing system scenarios and appropriate discrimination.

IED capabilities make it suitable to be operated autonomously. It does not need any information on other protection devices to be coordinated. IED measures and records the currents and voltages of the connecting point. These measurements can be used as a training set for ML techniques. A fault detection technique in active distribution networks is presented in35, which is based on ML techniques and uses 12 features to detect faults in the MG.

An improved self-synchronization algorithm by analyzing fault data is proposed in36. Two concepts were used. The first is based on finding the time of zero-crossing in the current signal and the second is identifying the slope polarity of the current signal. This way, the difference between the starting delay at ending terminals can be estimated, which results in avoiding the need for high communication bandwidth as well as additional synchronization equipment.

A feature-based protection scheme was proposed in37 using the waveform difference, which needs a relatively low communication bandwidth. It solves the difficulties of protection setting and phase measurement. The disparities in phase between the positive sequence current fault components at the sending and receiving terminals of a faulty line were utilized. The proposed protection criterion is based on information resulting from the sign of the first peak and time between terminals of the line under analysis.

A differential scheme for MG protection was proposed in38 in which a time-frequency transform was applied to generate contours in the time and frequency domain. The measured fault currents are used to calculate the content of the spectral energy39 and set a limit on the differential energy to issue the tripping signal to provide effective protection.

Two classifiers were proposed in40, which do not contain a communication facility and just rely on the voltage magnitude. It can differentiate between single-phase and double-phase to-ground faults. These classifiers were shown to perform successfully for grid-connected islanded modes of an MG. The noticeable features of the method involve robustness41 against fault resistance and independence from the power factor of PV.

Reference42 has developed an adaptive protection coordination scheme utilizing numerical overcurrent relays and support vector machines with a particle swarm optimization approach. However, since renewable energy sources based on microgrids rely on power electronics systems that require relays to operate within milliseconds to ensure the power system’s safety, the execution time of the particle swarm optimization program exceeds that of the primary relays, presenting a drawback.

A multi-agent protection scheme for MGs has been developed in43, addressing the inefficiencies of traditional protection methods in various operational topologies. By employing a unique algorithm, they can swiftly detect and isolate faults, ensuring system reliability. Raw data is pre-processed using Discrete Wavelet Transform is leveraged using deep learning techniques to handle the MG’s diverse operation modes.

An MG protection scheme utilizing Hilbert–Huang transform (HHT) and ML techniques was presented in44. The approach involves extracting three-phase current signals, decomposing them into intrinsic mode functions, and computing differential features using HHT. These features are then used as input for ML models (Naive Bayes, SVM, and extreme learning machine) to classify fault events. The authors reported an accuracy of 96.99% for the SVM model in their proposed scheme. However, this research demonstrates that this proposed method achieves a significantly higher accuracy of 99.75% even under tougher conditions considering the fault uncertainty.

Modelling and formulation

ML and SVM

The rapid development of communication and information technology in modern society progressively produces data at an amazing pace. The techniques of uncovering knowledge from a vast majority of data are called data mining. For the time being, data mining complements constructing analytical methods7.

SVM algorithm as a supervised ML technique is frequently utilized for classification problems. A significant advantage of SVM over other classification algorithms such as the KNN algorithm is the high degree of accuracy they offer.

Introduced in 1963, SVM is a mature algorithm for ML and it has great capability to generalize well to unobserved data45. SVMs are currently considered highly effective in various classification tasks, spanning from text to genomic data, and are regarded as top-performing methods. The SVM is a linear classifier that can be considered an advanced way of supervised classifying for high-dimensional data. In order to minimize the error in the training process the classification boundary is constructed. The concept behind SVM is that data points might be linearly separable when mapped into a higher-dimensional space, even if they are not linearly separable in the original space where the data is originally represented. The SVM offers the capability to identify and define nonlinear boundaries that effectively separate different classes within a given problem46. In other words, the SVM is a special transforming method that makes the learning task seem simpler to execute than in the original space of features.

A concept known as hyper-plane is used in the SVM to separate data into, unlike classes of data. This hyper-plane is supported by using support vectors that enlarge the hyper-plane margin as much as possible. In other words, instead of completely separating the vectors into two classes, SMV uses a trade-off. It allows for some vectors to drop inside the margin and on the wrong side of the decision boundary.

Decision trees Naive Bayes were used in47 to more effectively discriminate fault events from normal situations in MGs. The ensemble of classifiers was used in another protection scheme48. The ensemble-based approach simultaneously performs mode detection, fault detection, and classification. Moreover, a method by combines data-mining and wavelet analysis was proposed in49 for providing an intelligent protection scheme for MGs.

Proposed method

The fault components principle is suggested in56 to make a scheme for MGs to overcome the complexity of operating conditions. The sequence directional element of components together with the distance element of local measuring units are used.

There is a lot of uncertainty in a given MG as well as the grid that serves that MG. The value of fault current depends on the characteristics of the fault such as its location, resistance, and inception angle. Accordingly, the time domain simulations, no matter how accurate those are, are expensive and not able to take into account all aspects. To the best of our experience, the steady state analysis has the authenticity to predict the faulty situation and is inexpensive. Needless to say, implementing the results based on the steady-state parameters in IEDs is more straightforward50,57.

Fault types

Four kinds of faults as presented in Table 2 are used in the proposed method to make sure that the algorithm can discriminate the faults correctly. Accordingly, LG, LLG, LL, and LLL faults are taken into consideration.

RES status

Due to the inherent volatility of RESs, their operational status can fluctuate, meaning they may be considered either in service or out of service at any given time. This variability is influenced by factors such as weather conditions, availability of resources, and other environmental factors that affect their performance and reliability.

MG status

The protection scheme must be capable of distinguishing between two distinct operational modes: grid-connected and isolated. In the grid-connected mode, the microgrid operates in conjunction with the main power grid, allowing for the exchange of power. Conversely, in the isolated mode, the microgrid functions independently, relying solely on its internal resources to maintain stability and supply power. This differentiation is crucial for ensuring the appropriate protective measures are applied in each scenario.

Fault resistance

The presence of RESs in MGs may seriously affect the accuracy of the technique for determining the fault location. Accordingly, the adverse effect of these resources on the fault location calculation must be quantified. To do so, in the suggested technique, the intrinsic uncertainty in the fault resistance is pondered. Reference35 suggests that the fault resistance is an amount {0, 20, 40} ohms.

To quantify and express uncertainty, probability distributions are used. A probability distribution is a mathematical illustration of the relative likelihood of a variable having specific values61. Probability distributions can be graphically displayed in several ways. PDF is the simplest way to express the distribution function.

In engineering tasks, often uncertainties are considered explicitly, in which slight describing information is available to describe. In the lack of exact information, the uniform distributions are appropriate to describe errors in a way that is trouble-free. The user experience can be used to fix the bounds in many circumstances62.

Uncertainties related to considering the magnitude of load, the resistance of faults, the type of fault and the faulted node are investigated in63. Two kinds of measurements in the three-phase and LG faults namely at the upstream substation as well as the DG. The LG and LLL faults are considered in the proposed method.

Note that fault resistance affects the method of fault locating greatly64. In MGs, the fault locating methods are mainly based on the AI, the impedance method as well as the traveling wave method65. Regarding the stochastic nature of the fault, we propose using a Uniform probability distribution (also called the Rectangular Distribution) for which equal probability is assigned to all the fault resistances in the range of 0 to 20 Ω.

Contingencies

Emergency conditions play a significant role in the proper operation of the protection relays. Accordingly, some crucial contingencies must be considered in coordination with the relays. Needless to say, contingencies with a high probability of occurrence are of great importance. A criterion in power system studies known as N-1 refers to considering conditions in which just one equipment is out of service66. In this paper, the loss of some equipment except RESs with considerable effect on the fault current level at the IED under investigation is taken into account.

A pairs plot is a matrix of scatterplots that makes it easier to understand the pairwise relationship between dissimilar variables in a dataset. The simplest method to create a pairs plot is to use Python and the seaborn. The pairs plot involves two basic figures, namely the scatter plot (non-diagonal elements) and the histogram (diagonal elements). The histograms show the distribution of the related variable in the two operating modes as described in the legend. The scatter plots, though, on the lower and upper triangles illustrate the relationship between two variables. It is worth noting that it obviously shows the of lack association in some cases. If the variables in the scatter plots have a tendency to decrease and increase together, the relationship is positive. If one tends to increase (decrease) as the other decreases (increases), the association is negative. The association is zero, if there is no pattern.

If the variables in the histograms in the diagonal elements have a similar pattern, they are not suitable variables to differentiate between various situations. Conversely, if one tends to differ from the other, the related variable is an appropriate sign for capturing the point.

Errors in classifying faulty conditions as non-faulty (FN) are generally more dangerous than errors in classifying non-faulty conditions as faulty (FP). If a faulty condition is misclassified as non-faulty, the protection system will not activate, potentially leading to severe damage to equipment, safety hazards, or even catastrophic failures. For example, failing to detect a fault could result in equipment overheating, fires, or explosions. While FP can lead to unnecessary shutdowns or interruptions, they are usually less dangerous because they err on the side of caution. The system might temporarily stop functioning, but it prevents potential damage or hazards.

The cost of not detecting a fault can be extremely high due to the potential for extensive damage and the need for major repairs or replacements. Additionally, there could be legal and regulatory consequences if safety standards are unmet. Although FP can lead to operational inefficiencies and increased maintenance costs, these are generally more manageable compared to the costs associated with undetected faults.

Undetected faults can compromise the reliability and integrity of the entire system, leading to unpredictable and potentially dangerous situations. While FPs can cause inconvenience and reduce system efficiency, they do not compromise the overall safety and reliability of the system.

Future work

In future work, the research will be developed by conducting experimental studies to validate and enhance our findings. Specifically, Hardware-in-the-Loop (HIL) simulations will be conducted to achieve this. HIL creates a real-time simulation environment where hardware components interact with simulated models. This approach will confirm the effectiveness of using zero-sequence current and the phase angle of the positive sequence current in real-world scenarios through HIL simulations.

Conclusion

The fluctuation of fault current, caused by uncertainties in fault location and fault resistance during both grid-connected and islanding operations, presents a significant challenge for the protection of microgrids (MGs). Regardless of the operational mode, it is crucial to isolate only the faulty part of the MG to enhance its reliability.

This study highlights the significant variation in fault current levels between island mode and grid-connected mode in MGs. The intermittent nature of renewable energy sources (RESs) exacerbates this issue. The adaptive protection scheme proposed in this paper leverages locally available measurements to calculate symmetrical fault currents, ensuring secure and safe MG operation. Notably, the learning-based characteristic of the technique eliminates the need for a communication infrastructure.

In this work, symmetrical components of current are used to estimate fault location. It was demonstrated that the zero-sequence current, along with the phase angle of the positive sequence current, are effective parameters for identifying the operational mode during faulty conditions in MGs.

The results show that the Support Vector Machine (SVM) effectively classifies the simulated data, which is fairly balanced with about 4800 samples per class in a 6-dimensional input space. The robustness of the proposed method against fault resistance and its ability to account for the dynamics of fault current magnitude are notable advantages. Additionally, there is no need to classify all types of faulted phases, simplifying the protection scheme.

In conclusion, the proposed method effectively discriminates between fault currents and normal currents in various operating modes of MGs, preventing false tripping of the protection system. By considering the recommendations for using machine learning techniques in adaptive protection, this method offers a robust and reliable solution for MG protection, despite some limitations.

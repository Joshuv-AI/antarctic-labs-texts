Vertex-Based Localization of Erdős-Gallai Theorems for Paths and Cycles
Rajat Adak, L. Sunil Chandran

Abstract

The Erdős-Gallai theorem for paths states that in a simple P k P_{k} -free graph, m ≤ n ⁡ ( k − 1 ) 2 m\leq\frac{n(k-1)}{2} , where P k P_{k} denotes a path with length k k (that is, with k k edges). In this paper, we generalize this result as follows: For each v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) v\in V(G) , let p ⁡ ( v ) p(v) be the length of the longest path that contains v v . We show that m ≤ ∑ v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) p ⁡ ( v ) 2 m\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{p(v)}{2} The Erdős-Gallai theorem for cycles states that in a simple graph G G with circumference (that is, the length of the longest cycle) at most k k , we have m ≤ k ⁡ ( n − 1 ) 2 m\leq\frac{k(n-1)}{2} . Introduction

Typical problems in extremal graph theory aim to maximize or minimize the number of edges in a graph, adhering to certain structural restrictions and characterize the extremal graphs achieving the optimal values. Some of the earliest classical problems in extremal graph theory include:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Turán
[
9
]
)

For a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices and clique number at most
r
,

|E(G)|\leq\frac{n^{2}(r-1)}{2r}

and equality holds if and only if
G
is a regular Turán Graph with
n
vertices and
r
classes.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Erdős-Gallai
[
5
]
)

For a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices without a path of length
k(\geq 1)
, that is a path with
k
edges,

|E(G)|\leq\frac{n(k-1)}{2}

and equality holds if and only if all the components of
G
are complete graphs of order
k
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Erdős-Gallai
[
5
]
)

For a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices and circumference at most
k
,

|E(G)|\leq\frac{k(n-1)}{2}

and equality holds if and only if
G
is connected and all its blocks are complete graphs of order
k
.

1.1
Localization

A well-known bound on the independence number,
\alpha(G)
, of a graph
G
is given by:

\alpha(G)\geq\frac{n}{\Delta+1}

(1)

where
\Delta
represents the maximum degree of a vertex in
G
. Independently, Caro
[
4
]
and Wei
[
10
]
generalized this result by replacing
\Delta
, a global graph parameter, with the degree of individual vertices, which is a
local parameter
for the vertices. The bound they proposed is:

\alpha(G)\geq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}

(2)

where
d(v)
denotes the degree of a vertex
v\in V(G)
. This leads to the question of whether the aforementioned extremal results can be generalized by considering some localized vertex parameters. For instance, could we replace the global parameter, namely the circumference of the graph, in
Theorem

1.3
, with the length of the largest cycle that each vertex is part of, which is a vertex-based local parameter?

Localizing the parameters based on edges has already been studied in the literature. But the way they generalized the classical theorems is not as direct as the generalization of
1
to
2
established by Caro and Wei.

For example, Bradač
[
3
]
proposed a generalization of Turán’s
Theorem

1.1
by associating weights to the edges of the graph. The edge weights were defined as follows:

k(e)=max\{r\mid e\ \text{occurs in a subgraph of $G$ isomorphic to}\ K_{r}\}

Subsequently, Malec and Tompkins
[
8
]
provided an alternative proof for this generalized version of Turán’s theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Bradač
[
3
]
and Malec-Tompkins
[
8
]
)
For a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices,

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{k(e)}{k(e)-1}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}

and equality holds if and only if
G
is a regular Turán graph.

Remark 1.5
.

If
G
is
K_{r+1}
free, that is
k(e)\leq r
for all
e\in E(G)
, replacing
k(e)
by
r
in
Theorem

1.4
yields the classical Turán bound as stated in
Theorem

1.1
since
\frac{r}{r-1}\leq\frac{k(e)}{k(e)-1}
for all
e\in E(G)
.

Malec and Tompkins
[
8
]
coined the term
localization
to describe such weight assignments, as the weight of each edge depends solely on the local structures in which it is involved. Recently, Kirsch and Nir
[
7
]
extended
Theorem

1.4
by localizing an extension of
Theorem

1.1
originally introduced by Zykov
[
12
]
. Aragão and Souza
[
1
]
further advanced the theory by developing a localized version of the Graph Maclaurin Inequalities
[
6
]
.

Using a similar approach as above, Malec and Tompkins
[
8
]
also developed a
localized
version of
Theorem

1.2
. They defined the edge weights as follows:

l(e)=max\{r\mid e\ \text{occurs in a subgraph of $G$ isomorphic to}\ P_{r}\}

where
P_{r}
is a path with
r
edges.

Theorem 1.6
.

(Malec-Tompkins
[
8
]
)
For a simple graph
G
with
n
vertices,

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{l(e)}\leq\frac{n}{2}

and equality holds if and only if each component of
G
is a clique.

Remark 1.7
.

If
G
is
P_{k}
-free, then
l(e)\leq k-1
for all

. Substituting
l(e)
with
k-1
in

yields the bound presented in
Theorem

1.2
. Note that for

, we have

.

Definition 1.8
.

Recall that a block in a graph is a maximal connected subgraph containing no cut vertices. A

is a type of connected graph in which every block is a clique.

Observe that, a tree

Given such a block tree

A block graph

Recently, Zhao and Zhang

If the circumference of

2
Our Result: Vertex-based localization

In Theorems
1.4
,
1.6
, and
1.11
, localization is achieved by assigning weights to the edges of the graph. In this paper, we introduce the concept of
vertex-based localization
, where weights are assigned to the vertices instead of the edges. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first attempt to achieve localization through assigning weights to the vertices. In this paper, we use vertex-based localization to generalize
Theorem

1.2
and
1.3
.

While the generalization of
Theorem

1.2
is non-trivial, it is comparatively less challenging than its counterpart,
Theorem

1.3
. The proof of generalization of
Theorem

1.2
follows an inductive approach. In contrast and unfortunately, this technique fails for
Theorem

1.3
, necessitating an entirely new strategy, which significantly increases its complexity and makes the proof more sophisticated. Thus, our main result (in terms of its non-triviality) is
Theorem

2.2
, and its proof is presented first, in
section

2.2
. The proof of
Theorem

2.1
is presented in
section

2.3
and is relatively shorter.

Following the approach to edge weights introduced by Malec and Tompkins
[
8
]
, we assign a weight to each vertex
v\in V(G)
as follows:

p(v)=max\{r\mid v\ \text{occurs in a subgraph of $G$ isomorphic to}\ P_{r}\}

Theorem 2.1
.

For a simple graph
G
,

|E(G)|\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{p(v)}{2}

Equality holds if and only every connected component of
G
is a clique.

Similar to the edge weights given by Zhao and Zhang
[
11
]
we assign a weight to each vertex
v\in V(G)
that is part of a cycle in
G
as:

c(v)=max\{r\mid v\ \text{occurs in a subgraph of $G$ isomorphic to}\ C_{r}\}

and for vertices that do not belong to any cycle, we set
c(v)=2
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Given a simple graph,
G
the number of edges can be upper-bounded as

|E(G)|\leq\left(\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{c(v)}{2}\right)-\frac{c(u)}{2}

where
c(u)
is the circumference of
G
. Equality holds if and only if
G
is a parent-dominated block graph.

2.1
Recovering the Classical Theorems

To recover the bound and the extremal graph for
Theorem

1.2
from
Theorem

2.1
we assume
G
is a
P_{k}
-free graph with
n
vertices. Thus,
p(v)\leq(k-1)
, for all
v\in V(G)
. Consequently, we get;

|E(G)|\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{p(v)}{2}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{(k-1)}{2}=\frac{n(k-1)}{2}

(3)

This gives the bound as in
Theorem

1.2
. The first inequality of
eq.

3
becomes an equality if and only if all the components of
G
are cliques, by
Theorem

2.1
. The second inequality of
eq.

3
becomes an equality if and only if
p(v)=(k-1)
for all
v\in V(G)
. Thus, all the components of
G
must be
k
-cliques.

Now we show how to recover
Theorem

1.3
from
Theorem

2.2
. Since
G
is a graph on
n
vertices and with circumference at most
k
,
c(v)\leq k
for all
v\in V(G)
. Thus, we get;

|E(G)|\leq\left(\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{c(v)}{2}\right)-\frac{c(u)}{2}=\left(\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{c(v)}{2}\right)\leq\left(\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{k}{2}\right)=\frac{k(n-1)}{2}

(4)

For equality in the first inequality of
Theorem

2.2
,
G
must be a parent-dominated block graph. The second inequality becomes an equality if and only if
c(v)=k
for all
v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}.
Since
c(u)
is the circumference of
G
, we get
c(u)=k
. Thus the extremal graph for
Theorem

1.3
must be a block graph with blocks of order
k
. (Note that
G
is a special case of a parent-dominated block graph).

Comparison between our results and results of
[
8
]
and
[
11
]

Although both
Theorem

1.6
and
Theorem

2.1
extend
Theorem

1.2
, and both
Theorem

1.11
and
Theorem

2.2
strengthen
Theorem

1.3
, the bounds established in our results are fundamentally different. Specifically,
Theorem

2.1
and
Theorem

2.2
provide upper bounds on
|E(G)|
, and in some sense are direct generalizations of the classical Erdős-Gallai theorems, by replacing the global parameters by vertex-based local parameters. In contrast,
Theorem

1.6
and
Theorem

1.11
establish lower bounds on the order of the graph using the distribution of edge weights. They are similar in spirit to the generalization of Sperner’s Lemma to LYM inequality, from the theory of set systems (see chapter 3,
[
2
]
). To the best of our knowledge, our bounds and those presented in
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
do not imply one another.

Some Useful Properties

Lemma 2.7
.

For all
v\in L^{0}(G,P,v_{0})
,
c(v)=c(tw(v))
.

Proof.

If
c(v)=2
, vertex
v
is not part of any cycle in
G
. Since
N(v)=N(tw(v))
, it follows that
|N(v)|=|N(tw(v))|=1
; otherwise there will be a
4
-cycle containing
v
and
tw(v)
. Thus
c(tw(v))=2
. Similarly, if we assume
c(tw(v))=2
we get
c(v)=2
.

Now assume
c(v)>2
and
c(tw(v))>2
, that is, both
v
and
tw(v)
are part of at least one cycle in
G
. Let
C
be a cycle of length
c(v)
that contains the vertex

. If the cycle

also contains
tw(v)
, then it follows that
c(v)\leq c(tw(v))
. Otherwise, since
N(v)=N(tw(v))
, we can construct a new cycle

by replacing

with

in

. This construction yields a cycle of length

containing

, which implies
c(v)\leq c(tw(v))
.

Similarly, starting with a cycle of length

containing the vertex
tw(v)
, we can deduce that
c(v)\geq c(tw(v))
. These inequalities together establish that
c(v)=c(tw(v))
.
∎

Let

We can get

Thus from

If (

(c)

Set

v_{i}\in L^{0}(G,P,u)\subseteq L^{*}(G,P,u)
. This contradicts the fact that
v_{i}\in V(G_{1})
, and thus
v_{i}\notin L^{*}(G,P,u)
.

We deduce that
P^{\prime}
cannot possess either of the configurations described in
Lemma

2.23
or
Lemma

2.24
, namely the clique structure and the alternating structure, respectively.

Consequently, neither Case 1 nor Case 2 can occur under equality. This implies that the alternating structure described in
Lemma

2.24
is not feasible. Therefore, for equality, the longest
u
-path in
G
must exhibit the clique structure, as outlined in
Lemma

2.23
.

\bullet

Lemma 2.30
.

If
G
is an extremal graph for
Theorem

2.2
, then
G
is a parent-dominated block graph.

Proof.

Proof by Induction on the number of vertices:

Base Case:
If
|V(G)|=1
, then
G
is trivially a parent-dominated block graph.

Induction Hypothesis:
Assume that any extremal graph for
Theorem

2.2
with fewer than
|V(G)|
vertices is a parent-dominated block graph.

Induction Step:
Let
G
be an extremal graph. If
G
is a clique, we are done. Thus, assume
G
is not a clique. By the induction hypothesis, the graph
G_{1}=G\setminus L^{*}(G,P,u)
is a parent-dominated block graph.
G
is connected by
Lemma

2.14
. We know that
Back^{*}(P)=L^{*}(G,P,u)\cup\{w\}
induces a clique in
G
by
Lemma

2.23
, where
w
is the only vertex in
G_{1}
that is adjacent to the vertices in
L^{*}(G,P,u)
. Thus,
G
is a block graph, with
B=Back^{*}(P)
as a leaf block and
{w}
as its cut vertex. Let
B^{\prime}
be the parent block of
B
in
G
. Thus
V(B)\cap V(B^{\prime})=\{w\}
. The weight of
w
should remain unchanged upon the removal of
L^{*}(G,P,u)
by
eq.

18
. Since
G_{1}
is a parent-dominated block graph,
c(w)=|V(B^{\prime})|
. As the vertices in
B
induce a clique and
w\in V(B)
, we get
c(w)\geq|V(B)|
. It follows that
|V(B^{\prime})|\geq|V(B)|
. This implies that
G
is a parent-dominated block graph.

Therefore, if
G
is an extremal graph for

2.2
with

, then

is a parent-dominated block graph. Now, suppose

. By

; it must also be a parent-dominated block graph. The removed degree-one vertices act as leaf blocks (at the times they are removed), ensuring that

∎

2.3
Proof of
Theorem

2.1

Proof.

We will prove
Theorem

2.1
using induction on the number of vertices in graph
G
.

Base Case:

If
|V(G)|=1
, let
V(G)=\{v\}
. Clearly
|E(G)|=0=\frac{p(v)}{2}
.
G\cong K_{1}
and equality holds.

Induction Hypothesis:
Assume that for any graph with at most
|V(G)|-1
vertices, the inequality in
Theorem

2.1
is true. And equality holds if and only if all the components of the graph are cliques.

Induction Step:
If
G
is disconnected by induction hypothesis the statement of the theorem is true for each connected component and thus for
G
. Therefore, we assume that
G
is connected. Let
k=max\{p(v)\mid v\in V(G)\}
. The discussion will proceed by considering separate cases.

Case 1:

There exists a path
P
in
G
of length
k
such that the endpoints of
P
are adjacent.

Claim 2.31
.

V(P)=V(G)
.

Proof.

Let
P=v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
. By assumption of the case, we get that
v_{0}
is adjacent to
v_{k}
. Suppose
V(G)\setminus V(P)\neq\emptyset
. Since
G
is connected, there exists a vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus V(P)
, adjacent to some vertex in
P
. Since the vertices of
P
form a cycle, without loss of generality, we can assume
v
is adjacent to
v_{0}
. Now consider the path;

Q=v,v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k}

Thus
Q
is a path in
G
with length
k+1
. Thus we arrive at a contradiction to the assumption that the length of a longest path in
G
is
k
.
∎

Since
V(P)=V(G)
, we get that
p(v)=|V(G)|-1=(n-1)
, for all
v\in V(G)
. The maximum number of edges
G
can have is
\frac{n(n-1)}{2}
. Thus we get that;

|E(G)|\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{2}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{p(v)}{2}

(19)

Clearly, equality in
eq.

19
holds if and only if
G
is a clique.

Case 2:

If
P
is a path of length
k
in
G
, then the endpoints of
P
are not adjacent. That is there are no cycles in
G
with length
k+1
.

Let
P=v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
. Let
d(x)
be the degree of the vertex
x\in V(G)
. Without loss of generality assume
d(v_{0})\geq d(v_{k})
.

Claim 2.32
.

d(v_{k})\leq\frac{k}{2}

Proof.

Suppose not, that is
d(v_{k})\geq\frac{k+1}{2}
. Thus we get
d(v_{0})\geq\frac{k+1}{2}
. Since
P
is a longest path in
G
and
v_{0}
is not adjacent to
v_{k}
, we get,
N(v_{0}),N(v_{k})\subseteq\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k-1}\}
. Let
N=N(v_{k})\setminus\{v_{k-1}\}
and
N^{+}=\{v_{j+1}\mid v_{j}\in N\}
. Clearly,
|N|=|N^{+}|\geq\frac{k+1}{2}-1=\frac{k-1}{2}
. Note that
v_{0},v_{k}\notin N^{+}
. Therefore,
|\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k-1}\}\setminus N^{+}|\leq\frac{k-1}{2}
. Since
|N(v_{0})|\geq\frac{k+1}{2}
, by pigeonhole principle, we get that, there exists
v_{i}\in N^{+}
such that
v_{i}\in N(v_{0})
and
v_{i-1}\in N(v_{k})
. Consider the cycle;

K=v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{i-1},v_{k},v_{k-1},\dots,v_{i},v_{0}

Clearly
K
is a
k+1
length cycle in
G
. Thus we get a contradiction.
∎

Let
G_{1}=G\setminus\{v_{k}\}
and
p_{G_{1}}(v)
denote the weight of a vertex in
G_{1}
. Clearly
p(v)\geq p_{G_{1}}(v)
for all
v\in V(G_{1})
. Using the induction hypothesis we get;

|E(G_{1})|\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{1})}\frac{p_{G_{1}}(v)}{2}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{1})}\frac{p(v)}{2}

(20)

Note that,
|E(G)|=|E(G_{1})|+d(v_{k})
. Thus from
eq.

20
we get;

|E(G)|\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{1})}\frac{p(v)}{2}+d(v_{k})\leq\sum_{v\in V(G_{1})}\frac{p(v)}{2}+\frac{p(v_{k})}{2}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{p(v)}{2}

(21)

For equality in
eq.

21
, we must have equality in
eq.

20
. By induction hypothesis, the first inequality of
eq.

20
becomes an equality if and only if all the components of
G_{1}
are cliques. Since the vertices
\{v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}\}
form a path in
G_{1}
, they belong to the same connected component of
G_{1}
, say
C
. For equality in the second inequality of
eq.

20
we need to have
p_{G_{1}}(v)=p(v)
for all
v\in V(G_{1})
. Thus,
p_{G_{1}}(v_{i})=k
for all
i\in\{0,1\dots,k-1\}.
Since
C
is a clique with vertices having weight

,
C
has order
k+1
, that is, there is one more vertex in
C
other than

. Thus,
C
contains a cycle of length
k+1
. This contradicts the assumption that

has no

Thus equality is not feasible in Case 2. Therefore, we have equality in
Theorem

2.1
if and only if all the components of
G
are cliques.

∎

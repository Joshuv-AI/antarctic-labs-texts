Associative-Yamaguti algebras
Apurba Das

Abstract
. In this paper, we first introduce associative-Yamaguti algebras as the associative analogue of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Associative algebras, reductive associative algebras and associative triple systems of the first kind form subclasses of associative-Yamaguti algebras. Any diassociative algebra canonically provides an associative-Yamaguti algebra structure. We confirm that any associative-Yamaguti algebra admits an enveloping associative algebra (i.e., it can be obtained from a reductive associative algebra). We show that a suitable skew-symmetrization of an associative-Yamaguti algebra gives rise to a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure. Next, we define the ( 2 , 3 ) (2,3) -cohomology group of an associative-Yamaguti algebra to study formal one-parameter deformations and abelian extensions. Later, we consider Yamaguti multiplications on a nonsymmetric operad as a generalization of associative-Yamaguti algebras. This notion further leads us to introduce dendriform-Yamaguti algebras, which are splitting objects for associative-Yamaguti algebras. Finally, we consider relative Rota-Baxter operators on associative-Yamaguti algebras to establish close relationships with dendriform-Yamaguti algebras.

Introduction

The concept of Lie-Yamaguti algebras can be implicitly traced back to the work of Nomizu
[
nomizu
]
in the invariant affine connections on homogeneous spaces. Inspired by his work, K. Yamaguti introduced general Lie triple systems together with their representations and cohomology theory
[
yamaguti0
,
yamaguti
]
. Later, Kinyon and Weinstein
[
kinyon
]
observed that general Lie triple systems, which they called
Lie-Yamaguti algebras
in their paper, can be constructed from Leibniz algebras. Additionally, they examine the
enveloping Lie algebras
of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Although Lie algebras and Lie triple systems are the first examples of Lie-Yamaguti algebras, this new structure exhibits greater complexity than either Lie algebras or Lie triple systems. This fact was observed in the works
[
benito0
,
benito1
,
takahashi
]
, where the authors explained irreducible Lie-Yamaguti algebras and some other Lie-Yamaguti algebras with specific enveloping Lie algebras. Since then, Lie-Yamaguti algebras have attracted much attention and have been extensively studied. In
[
zhang-li
]
, the authors considered deformations and extensions of Lie-Yamaguti algebras in terms of the cohomology introduced in
[
yamaguti
]
. In
[
sheng-complex
]
, the authors analyzed product structures and complex structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras using Nijenhuis operators. On the other hand, Rota-Baxter operators and symplectic structures on Lie-Yamaguti algebras have been studied in
[
sheng-zhao
]
. Recently, homomorphisms
[
mondal-saha
]
, crossed homomorphisms
[
sun-chen
]
, automorphisms
[
goswami
]
and nilpotency
[
hani
]
of Lie-Yamaguti algebras have also been investigated.

Besides Lie triple systems and Lie-Yamaguti algebras, there exist well-known triple systems in the world of associative algebras, Jordan algebras and Leibniz algebras. See
[
bremner
,
carlsson
,
jacobson
,
lister
,
meyberg
]
for more details about associative triple systems and their close connections with Lie, Jordan and Leibniz triple systems. Although
associative triple systems
are extensively studied in the literature from various points of view, the notion of an associative-Yamaguti algebra is not yet considered. In this paper, we first propose the definition of an
associative-Yamaguti algebra
and provide some interesting examples (cf. Definition
3.1
and subsequent examples). Of course, associative algebras, reductive associative algebras and associative triple systems of the first kind are the first examples of associative-Yamaguti algebras. On the other side, diassociative algebra has its origin in the study of Leibniz algebras
[
loday
]
. We show that any diassociative algebra yields an associative-Yamaguti algebra structure (cf. Theorem
3.7
). We also confirm that any associative-Yamaguti algebra admits an enveloping associative algebra (cf. Theorem
3.10
). That is, it can be embedded into a bigger reductive associative algebra so that the induced associative-Yamaguti algebra structure coincides with the given one. This concept generalizes the enveloping Lie algebra of a given Lie-Yamaguti algebra
[
kinyon
]
. Next, we show that a suitable skew-symmetrization of an associative-Yamaguti algebra gives rise to a Lie-Yamaguti algebra (cf. Theorem
3.11
) and this construction is compatible with the well-known construction of a Lie algebra from an associative algebra. In turn, we obtain the commutative diagram given in (
(i)

(ii)

Next, we focus on the cohomology of an associative-Yamaguti algebra and its potential applications. We begin with
representations
of an associative-Yamaguti algebra and construct the corresponding semidirect product. Subsequently, we define the
(2,3)
-th cohomology group

\mathcal{H}^{(2,3)}(A,M)
of an associative-Yamaguti algebra

with coefficients in a given representation
M

(2,3)
-th cohomology group. Then we consider

and show that the corresponding infinitesimals are precisely
(2,3)

5.1

(2,3)
-th cohomology group
\mathcal{H}^{(2,3)}(A,A)

abelian extensions
of an associative-Yamaguti algebra
A
by a given representation
M
and show that the set of all isomorphism classes of such abelian extensions can be classified by the
(2,3)
-th cohomology group
\mathcal{H}^{(2,3)}(A,M)
(cf. Theorem
5.3
).

In
[
gers-voro
]
, Gerstenhaber and Voronov considered

on nonsymmetric operads as a generalization of associative algebras. Such multiplications are found to be very useful in the study of associative algebras and other Loday-type algebras (i.e., those algebras in which the variables follow the same order in all the defining identities). It can be observed that an associative-Yamaguti algebra can be regarded as a Loday-type algebra. Inspired by this, we consider
Yamaguti multiplications
on a nonsymmetric operad as a generalization of associative-Yamaguti algebras. More precisely, there is a one-one correspondence between Yamaguti multiplications on the endomorphism operad

got much attention due to its connections with Rota-Baxter algebras

]
. In
[

]
, the present author explicitly wrote the nonsymmetric operad
\mathrm{Dend}_{A}
whose multiplications correspond to dendriform algebra structures on the vector space

dendriform-Yamaguti algebra
as a Yamaguti multiplication on the nonsymmetric operad
\mathrm{Dend}_{A}

6.7

are examples of dendriform-Yamaguti algebras. We observe that a dendriform-Yamaguti algebra splits an associative-Yamaguti algebra (cf. Theorem

on associative-Yamaguti algebras. We show that such an operator induces a dendriform-Yamaguti algebra structure and, conversely, any dendriform-Yamaguti algebra arises in this way (cf. Theorems

and

2.1 Definition
.

A
Lie-Yamaguti algebra
is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
consisting of a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
equipped with linear maps
[~,~]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and
\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
that satisfy the following set of identities:

[[x,y],z]+[[y,z],x]+[[z,x],y]+\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket+\llbracket y,z,x\rrbracket+\llbracket z,x,y\rrbracket=0,

(LY1)

\llbracket[x,y],z,w\rrbracket+\llbracket[y,z],x,w\rrbracket+\llbracket[z,x],y,w\rrbracket=0,

(LY2)

\llbracket x,y,[z,w]\rrbracket=[\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket,w]+[z,\llbracket x,y,w\rrbracket],

(LY3)

\llbracket x,y,\llbracket z,w,t\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket,w,t\rrbracket+\llbracket z,\llbracket x,y,w\rrbracket,t\rrbracket+\llbracket z,w,\llbracket x,y,t\rrbracket\rrbracket,

(LY4)

for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
and
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},[~,~]^{\prime},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket^{\prime})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebras. A
homomorphism
of Lie-Yamaguti algebras from
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is a linear map
\varphi:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi([x,y])=[\varphi(x),\varphi(y)]^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket)=\llbracket\varphi(x),\varphi(y),\varphi(z)\rrbracket^{\prime},\text{ for all }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

The collection of all Lie-Yamaguti algebras and homomorphisms between them is a category, denoted by
LieY
.

2.2 Example
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
be a Lie algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra, where

\displaystyle\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket:=[[x,y],z],\text{ for }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

2.3 Example
.

A
Lie triple system
is a pair
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
of a vector space
\mathfrak{g}
with a linear map
\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
satisfying

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket+\llbracket y,z,x\rrbracket+\llbracket z,x,y\rrbracket=0,

\displaystyle\llbracket x,y,\llbracket z,w,t\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket,w,t\rrbracket+\llbracket z,\llbracket x,y,w\rrbracket,t\rrbracket+\llbracket z,w,\llbracket x,y,t\rrbracket\rrbracket,

for all
x,y,z,w,t\in\mathfrak{g}
. It follows that a Lie triple system
(\mathfrak{g},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
can be regarded as a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with the trivial bracket operation
[~,~]
.

2.4 Example
.

Let
(\mathfrak{l},\lfloor~,~\rfloor)
be a left Leibniz algebra. That is,
\mathfrak{l}
is a vector space equipped with a linear operation
\lfloor~,~\rfloor:\mathfrak{l}\otimes\mathfrak{l}\rightarrow\mathfrak{l}
satisfying

\displaystyle\lfloor x,\lfloor y,z\rfloor\rfloor=\lfloor\lfloor x,y\rfloor,z\rfloor+\lfloor y,\lfloor x,z\rfloor\rfloor,\text{ for }x,y,z\in\mathfrak{l}.

For a left Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{l},\lfloor~,~\rfloor)
, we define linear operations
[~,~]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{l}\rightarrow\mathfrak{l}
and
\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{l}\otimes\mathfrak{l}\rightarrow\mathfrak{l}
by

\displaystyle[x,y]:=\lfloor x,y\rfloor-\lfloor y,x\rfloor~~~\text{ and }~~~\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket:=-\lfloor\lfloor x,y\rfloor,z\rfloor,

for
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{l}
. Then
(\mathfrak{l},[~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Recall that
[
yamaguti
]
a
representation
of this Lie-Yamaguti algebra is given by a triple
(V,\rho,\nu)
of a vector space
V
endowed with linear maps
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
and
\nu:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
that satisfy the following conditions:

\nu([x,y],z)-\nu(x,z)\rho(y)+\nu(y,z)\rho(x)=0,

\nu(x,[y,z])-\rho(y)\nu(x,z)+\rho(z)\nu(x,y)=0,

\rho(\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\mu}(x,y),\rho(z)],

\nu(z,w)\nu(x,y)-\nu(y,w)\nu(x,z)-\nu(x,\llbracket y,z,w\rrbracket)+D_{\rho,\nu}(y,z)\nu(x,w)=0,

\nu(\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket,w)+\nu(z,\llbracket x,y,w\rrbracket)=[D_{\rho,\nu}(x,y),\nu(z,w)],

for all
x,y,z,w\in\mathfrak{g}
. Here the linear map
D_{\rho,\nu}:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
is given by

\displaystyle D_{\rho,\nu}(x,y):=[\rho(x),\rho(y)]-\rho([x,y])-\nu({x,y})+\nu(y,x),\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

The following result is a characterization of representations of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
[
zhang-li
]
.

2.5 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Suppose
V
is a vector space endowed with linear maps
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
and
\nu:\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
. Then
(V,\rho,\nu)
is a representation of the given Lie-Yamaguti algebra if and only if the following operations

\displaystyle[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\ltimes}:=~

\displaystyle([x,y],\rho(x)v-\rho(y)u),

\displaystyle\llbracket(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\rrbracket_{\ltimes}:=~

\displaystyle\big(\llbracket x,y,z\rrbracket,D_{\rho,\nu}(x,y)w+\nu(y,z)u-\nu(x,z)v\big),

for
(x,u),(y,v),(z,w)\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus V
, makes the triple
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus V,[~,~]_{\ltimes},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{\ltimes})
into a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

3.
Associative-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we introduce associative-Yamaguti algebras and provide a list of examples. In particular, any diassociative algebra yields an associative-Yamaguti algebra structure. We show that any associative-Yamaguti algebra has an enveloping associative algebra. This implies that any associative-Yamaguti algebra can be obtained from a reductive associative algebra. We end this section by showing that a suitable skew-symmetrization of an associative-Yamaguti algebra gives rise to a Lie-Yamaguti algebra structure.

3.1 Definition
.

An
associative-Yamaguti algebra
(or simply an
Yamaguti algebra
) is a quadruple
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
consisting of a vector space
A
equipped with linear operations

\displaystyle\cdot:A\otimes A\rightarrow A\quad\text{ and }\quad\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}:A\otimes A\otimes A\rightarrow A

such that for all
a,b,c,d,e\in A
, the following set of identities are hold:

(a\cdot b)\cdot c-a\cdot(b\cdot c)+\{a,b,c\}-\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}=0,

(AY1)

\{a\cdot b,c,d\}=\{a,b\cdot c,d\},

(AY2)

\{a,b,c\cdot d\}=\{a,b,c\}\cdot d,

(AY3)

\{\!\!\{a\cdot b,c,d\}\!\!\}=a\cdot\{\!\!\{b,c,d\}\!\!\},

(AY4)

\{\!\!\{a,b\cdot c,d\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\cdot d\}\!\!\},

(AY5)

a\cdot\{b,c,d\}=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\cdot d,

(AY6)

\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}=\{a,\{\!\!\{b,c,d\}\!\!\},e\}=\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}\},

(AY7)

\{a,\{b,c,d\},e\}=\{\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\},d,e\},

(AY8)

\{\!\!\{\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\},d,e\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{a,\{b,c,d\},e\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{a,b,\{\!\!\{c,d,e\}\!\!\}\}\!\!\},

(AY9)

\{\!\!\{a,\{\!\!\{b,c,d\}\!\!\},e\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}\}\!\!\},

(AY10)

\{a,b,\{\!\!\{c,d,e\}\!\!\}\}=\{\!\!\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}\!\!\}.

(AY11)

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be an associative-Yamaguti algebra. Define linear maps
\sigma,\tau:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
by

\displaystyle\sigma(a,b)=\sigma_{a,b}:=\{a,b,-\}~\mathrm{~and~}~\tau(a,b)=\tau_{a,b}:=\{\!\!\{-,a,b\}\!\!\},\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Then the identities (
AY1
)-(
AY11
) can be rephrased as

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad(a\cdot b)\cdot c-a\cdot(b\cdot c)+\sigma_{a,b}(c)-\tau_{b,c}(a)=0,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma_{a\cdot b,c}=\sigma_{a,b\cdot c}~\!,\\
\sigma_{a,b}(c\cdot d)=\sigma_{a,b}(c)\cdot d~\!,\\
\tau_{a,b}(c\cdot d)=c\cdot\tau_{a,b}(d)~\!,\\
\tau_{a\cdot b,c}=\tau_{a,b\cdot c}~\!,\\
a\cdot\sigma_{b,c}(d)=\tau_{b,c}(a)\cdot d~\!,\end{cases}\qquad\quad\begin{cases}\sigma_{a,b}\sigma_{c,d}=\sigma_{\sigma_{a,b}(c),d}=\sigma_{a,\tau_{c,d}(b)}~\!,\\
\sigma_{a,\sigma_{b,c}(d)}=\sigma_{\tau_{b,c}(a),d}~\!,\\
\tau_{a,b}\tau_{c,d}=\tau_{c,\tau_{a,b}(d)}=\tau_{\sigma_{c,d}(a),b}~\!,\\
\tau_{\tau_{a,b}(c),d}=\tau_{c,\sigma_{a,b}(d)}~\!,\\
\sigma_{a,b}\tau_{c,d}=\tau_{c,d}\sigma_{a,b}~\!,\end{cases}

for all
a,b,c,d\in A
. Thus, an associative-Yamaguti algebra
can be equivalently described
by a quadruple
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\sigma,\tau)
of a vector space
A
with linear operations
\cdot:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and
\sigma,\tau:A\otimes A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
that satisfy the 11 conditions given above.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
and
(A^{\prime},~\!\cdot^{\prime}~\!,\{~,~,~\}^{\prime},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}^{\prime})
be two associative-Yamaguti algebras. A
homomorphism
of associative-Yamaguti algebras from
A
to
A^{\prime}
is a linear map
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
that preserves the corresponding operations. Associative-Yamaguti algebras and homomorphisms between them form a category, which we denote by
{\bf AssY}.

\displaystyle a\dashv(b\dashv c)=a\dashv(b\vdash c),\qquad(a\vdash b)\dashv c=a\vdash(b\dashv c)\quad\text{ and }\quad(a\vdash b)\vdash c=(a\dashv b)\vdash c,

(2)

a,b,c\in D

(D,\dashv,\vdash)

(D,\dashv,\vdash)

5.
Deformations and abelian extensions of associative-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we study formal deformations and abelian extensions of an associative-Yamaguti algebra in terms of its
(2,3)
-th cohomology group.

5.1.
Formal deformations

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be an associative-Yamaguti algebra. Consider the space
A[[t]]
of all formal power series in
t
with coefficients from
A
. Then
A[[t]]
is obviously a
{\bf k}[[t]]
-module. A
formal
1
-parameter deformation
or simply a
formal deformation
of
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
consists of
{\bf k}[[t]]
-linear operations

\displaystyle\mu_{t}:A[[t]]\otimes_{{\bf k}[[t]]}A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]],\quad\mu_{t}(a,b)=a\cdot b+t\mu_{1}(a,b)+t^{2}\mu_{2}(a,b)+\cdots,

\displaystyle F_{t}:A[[t]]\otimes_{{\bf k}[[t]]}A[[t]]\otimes_{{\bf k}[[t]]}A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]],\quad F_{t}(a,b,c)=\{a,b,c\}+tF_{1}(a,b,c)+t^{2}F_{2}(a,b,c)+\cdots,

\displaystyle G_{t}:A[[t]]\otimes_{{\bf k}[[t]]}A[[t]]\otimes_{{\bf k}[[t]]}A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]],\quad G_{t}(a,b,c)=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}+tG_{1}(a,b,c)+t^{2}G_{2}(a,b,c)+\cdots,

(where
\mu_{i}:A^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow A

and
G_{i}:A^{\otimes 3}\rightarrow A
are linear maps for all

) that makes the tuple
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},F_{t},G_{t})
into an associative-Yamaguti algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[[t]]
.

Let
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},F_{t},G_{t})
and
(A[[t]],\mu^{\prime}_{t},F^{\prime}_{t},G^{\prime}_{t})
be two formal deformations of a given associative-Yamaguti algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
. They are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a
{\bf k}[[t]]
-linear map

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}:A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]]~\text{ of the form }~\varphi_{t}(a)=a+t\varphi_{1}(a)+t^{2}\varphi_{2}(a)+\cdots

(39)

(where
\varphi_{i}:A\rightarrow A
are linear maps for all

) that is a homomorphism of associative-Yamaguti algebras from
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},F_{t},G_{t})
to
(A[[t]],\mu^{\prime}_{t},F^{\prime}_{t},G^{\prime}_{t})
.

5.1 Theorem
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be an associative-Yamaguti algebra and
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},F_{t},G_{t})

(\mu_{1},F_{1},G_{1})
is a
(2,3)
-cocycle of the associative-Yamaguti algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
with coefficients in the adjoint representation.

Proof.

Since
(A[[t]],\mu_{t},F_{t},G_{t})
is an associative-Yamaguti algebra over the ring
{\bf k}[[t]]
, the identities (
AY1
)-(

n\geq 0
and

, we must have

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}\big(~\mu_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c)-\mu_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c))~\big)+F_{n}(a,b,c)-G_{n}(a,b,c)=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c,d)=\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c),d),\quad\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(a,b,\mu_{j}(c,d))=\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}(F_{j}(a,b,c),d),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(\mu_{j}(a,b),c,d)=\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}(a,G_{j}(b,c,d)),\quad\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(a,\mu_{j}(b,c),d)=\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(a,b,\mu_{j}(c,d)),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}(a,F_{j}(b,c,d))=\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}(G_{j}(a,b,c),d),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(F_{j}(a,b,c),d,e)=\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(a,G_{j}(b,c,d),e)=\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(a,b,F_{j}(c,d,e)),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(a,F_{j}(b,c,d),e)=\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(G_{j}(a,b,c),d,e),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(G_{j}(a,b,c),d,e)=\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(a,F_{j}(b,c,d),e)=\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(a,b,G_{j}(c,d,e)),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(a,G_{j}(b,c,d),e)=\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(a,b,F_{j}(c,d,e)),~~\sum_{i+j=n}F_{i}(a,b,G_{j}(c,d,e))=\sum_{i+j=n}G_{i}(F_{j}(a,b,c),d,e),

5.2 Proposition
.

Let

6.
Yamaguti multiplications, dendriform-Yamaguti algebras and relative Rota-Baxter operators

In this section, we introduce Yamaguti multiplications on any nonsymmetric operad as a generalization of associative-Yamaguti algebras. Subsequently, we define and study dendriform-Yamaguti algebras. We end this section by considering relative Rota-Baxter operators on associative-Yamaguti algebras and their relations with dendriform-Yamaguti algebras.

6.1 Definition
.

A
nonsymmetric operad
(or a
non-
\Sigma
operad
) is a triple
\mathcal{P}=(\{\mathcal{P}(n)\}_{n\geq 1},\circ,{\bf 1})
consisting of a collection
\{\mathcal{P}(n)\}_{n\geq 1}
of vector spaces equipped with linear maps (called
partial compositions
)

\displaystyle\circ_{i}:\mathcal{P}(m)\otimes\mathcal{P}(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}(m+n-1)\quad(\text{for }1\leq i\leq m)

such that for any
f\in\mathcal{P}(m)
,
g\in\mathcal{P}(n)
and
h\in\mathcal{P}(k),

\displaystyle f\circ_{i}(g\circ_{j}h)=~

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)\circ_{i+j-1}h,~~\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)\circ_{j+n-1}h=~

\displaystyle(f\circ_{j}h)\circ_{i}g,~~\text{ for }1\leq i<j\leq m,

and there is a distinguished element
{\bf 1}\in\mathcal{P}(1)
that satisfies
f\circ_{i}{\bf 1}={\bf 1}\circ_{1}f
, for all
f\in\mathcal{P}(m)
and
1\leq i\leq m.

In this paper, we will mostly concentrate on the following two nonsymmetric operads.

6.2 Example
.

Let
A
be any vector space. Then the endomorphism operad
\mathrm{End}_{A}=(\{\mathrm{End}_{A}(n)\}_{n\geq 1},\circ,\mathrm{Id}_{A})
associated to

is given by
\mathrm{End}_{A}(n)=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},A)
for

, with the partial compositions

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})=f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g(a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1}),

for

,

,

and

. Here
\mathrm{Id}_{A}\in\mathrm{End}_{A}(1)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)
is the identity map.

\displaystyle(f\circ_{i}g)([r];a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})

\displaystyle~=\begin{cases}f({\scriptstyle[r]};a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},g({\scriptstyle[1]+\cdots+[n]};a_{i},\ldots,a_{i+n-1}),a_{i+n},\ldots,a_{m+n-1})&\text{ if }1\leq r\leq i-1,\\

6.4 Definition
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a nonsymmetric operad. A
Yamaguti multiplication

\displaystyle\pi\circ_{1}\pi-\pi\circ_{2}\pi+\theta-\vartheta=0,\qquad\begin{cases}\theta\circ_{1}\pi=\theta\circ_{2}\pi,\\

\vartheta\circ_{2}\vartheta=\vartheta\circ_{3}\theta,\\

We will now consider relative Rota-Baxter operators on associative-Yamaguti algebras and find their relationships with dendriform-Yamaguti algebras.

6.12 Definition
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be an associative-Yamaguti algebra and
(M,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be a representation of it. Then a linear map

is said to be a
relative Rota-Baxter operator
(also called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator
) if

\displaystyle R(u)\cdot R(v)=~

\displaystyle R(R(u)\cdot v+u\cdot R(v)),

\displaystyle\{R(u),R(v),R(w)\}=~

\displaystyle R\big(\{R(u),R(v),w\}+\{R(u),v,R(w)\}+\{u,R(v),R(w)\}\big),

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{R(u),R(v),R(w)\}\!\!\}=~

\displaystyle R\big(\{\!\!\{R(u),R(v),w\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{R(u),v,R(w)\}\!\!\}+\{\!\!\{u,R(v),R(w)\}\!\!\}\big),\text{ for all }u,v,w\in M.

It is easy to see that an invertible linear map

is a derivation on
A
with values in
M
if and only if the inverse
f^{-1}:M\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator.

The following result provides an equivalent characterization of relative Rota-Baxter operators.

6.13 Proposition
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be an associative-Yamaguti algebra and
(M,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be a representation of it. Then a linear map
R:M\rightarrow A
is a relative Rota-Baxter operator if and only if the graph
\mathrm{Gr}(R)=\{(R(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in M\}
is a subalgebra of the semidirect product associative-Yamaguti algebra
(A\oplus M,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{\ltimes},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}_{\ltimes})
.

6.14 Theorem
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be an associative-Yamaguti algebra and
(M,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
be a representation of it. Suppose

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad u\prec v:=u\cdot R(v),\quad u\succ v:=R(u)\cdot v,

\displaystyle\{u,v,w\}_{[1]}:=\{u,R(u),R(v)\},\quad\{u,v,w\}_{[2]}:=\{R(u),v,R(w)\},\quad\{u,v,w\}_{[3]}:=\{R(u),R(v),w\},

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\}_{[1]}:=\{\!\!\{u,R(u),R(v)\}\!\!\},\quad\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\}_{[2]}:=\{\!\!\{R(u),v,R(w)\}\!\!\},\quad\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\}_{[3]}:=\{\!\!\{R(u),R(v),w\}\!\!\}.

For any

\displaystyle(u\prec v)\prec w-u\prec(v\prec w+v\succ w)+\{u,v,w\}_{[1]}-\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\}_{[1]}

Concluding remarks and further discussions.
This paper introduces associative-Yamaguti algebras as the associative analogue of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. The
(2,3)
-cohomology group of an associative-Yamaguti algebra is also introduced, and applications to formal one-parameter deformations and abelian extensions are obtained. Finally, Yamaguti multiplications and dendriform-Yamaguti algebras are considered, and their relations with relative Rota-Baxter operators are discussed. Since the notion of an associative-Yamaguti algebra is closely connected to associative algebras, associative triple systems, diassociative algebras and Lie-Yamaguti algebras, this new algebraic structure opens several interesting questions. First, one may ask about the full cochain complex of an associative-Yamaguti algebra generalizing the
(2,3)
-cohomology group considered in this paper and the well-known cochain complex of an associative triple system
[
carlsson
]
. Further, in all the identities of an associative-Yamaguti algebra, since the variables stay in the same order, it is worth noting to find a cup product and a graded Lie bracket in the corresponding cochain complex (generalizing the well-known cup product and the graded Lie bracket on the Hochschild cochain complex of an associative algebra). To our knowledge, the existence of a cup product or a graded Lie bracket on the complex of an associative triple system is not even known.

The notion of associative-Yamaguti algebra introduced here also motivates us to consider a generalization of an associative triple system. Explicitly, we say a tuple
(A,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
consisting of a vector space

if only the identities (

) hold. A diassociative algebra

yields a weak associative triple system, where the operations
\{~,~,~\}
and
\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}
are given by (
5
), (
6
). On the other hand, a weak associative triple system
(A,\{~,~,~\},\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
gives rise to a diassociative algebra structure on the tensor product

with the operations

\displaystyle(a\otimes b)\dashv(c\otimes d)=a\otimes\{b,c,d\}\quad\text{ and }\quad(a\otimes b)\vdash(c\otimes d)=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\otimes d,

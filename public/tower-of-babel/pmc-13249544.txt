Regioselective synthesis of 3-halo-2H-chromenes via decarboxylative halogenation of oxygenated 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes: Application to isoflavonoid synthesis
Authors: Bubwoong Kang, Kosuke Takahara, Itsuki Toyoguchi, Tetsuya Satoh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A practical and regioselective method has been developed for 3-halo-2H-chromene synthesis via decarboxylative halogenation. These chromenes are key intermediates in synthesizing isoflavene derivatives and related bioactive compounds, including molecules of interest in agrochemical research. In this study, 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes, prepared from salicylaldehyde-derived cyanochromenes, were subjected to decarboxylative halogenation under mild conditions. Systematic evaluation of halogen sources, bases, solvents, and other reaction parameters revealed that combining tetrabutylammonium tribromide with sodium-containing bases in acetonitrile promotes decarboxylative bromination with high regioselectivity. This method was designed for chromene substrates bearing oxygen-containing functional groups, which are often difficult to synthesize due to competing electrophilic aromatic substitutions. The optimized protocol, demonstrated on gram scale, afforded the corresponding 3-bromo-2H-chromene as the sole isolable species without requiring column chromatography. The resulting 3-bromo-2H-chromene was converted into an isoflavene derivative via Suzuki–Miyaura coupling, accomplishing its first convergent synthesis as well as formal total synthesis of an isoflavan natural product.

Introduction

Isoflavenes are an important class of bioactive natural products1–3) and synthetic intermediates4) (Fig. 1). Representative examples include phenoxodiol,1) which induces apoptotic cell death; bolusanthin III,2) which exhibits antibacterial activity; and dehydroglyasperin D,3) which displays antidiabetic properties. The synthetic utility of isoflavene scaffolds was exemplified by the total synthesis of an antimicrobial natural product, (+)-pisatin,5) as reported by Pinard and co-workers.4) Accordingly, the development of efficient synthetic methods for isoflavenes is of considerable importance not only for the discovery and practical supply of biologically active compounds, including antimicrobial agents, but also for applied research in agrochemical science.

Fig. 1. Utility of isoflavenes.

A common strategy for constructing isoflavenes involves the arylation of 3-functionalized 2H-chromenes through cross-coupling reactions (Fig. 2A).6–12) Although Corey, Viaud, and Park have reported syntheses of such chromenes, these methods typically require more than five steps from phenols (Fig. 2B).6–9) Takikawa, Kang, and their co-workers achieved more concise routes to 3-functionalized 2H-chromenes, utilizing the Claisen rearrangement–cyclization cascade (CRCC) of 2,3-dichloroallyl aryl ethers (Fig. 2C).10–12) Although this shorter-step approach allows the total syntheses of a number of isoflavonoid natural products, its application can be hampered by the formation of regioisomers. Because CRCC can proceed at both ortho-carbons of 2,3-dichloroallyl aryl ethers, mixtures of regioisomers occasionally form, and their similar polarities render chromatographic separation difficult. These limitations highlight the need for a complementary method that provides 3-functionalized 2H-chromenes without such regioselectivity issues.10)

Fig. 2. Synthetic approaches to 3-functionalized-2H-chromenes.

To address this, we envisioned the concise and regioselective synthesis of 3-halo-2H-chromenes based on decarboxylative halogenation13,14) (Fig. 2D). Starting from salicylaldehydes 1, 3-cyano-2H-chromenes 2 were efficiently prepared using a protocol previously established in our laboratory,15) and subsequent hydrolysis of these cyanochromenes furnished the corresponding 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes 3. We then planned to convert these carboxylic acids into the corresponding 3-halo-2H-chromenes 4 through decarboxylative halogenation. This strategy was expected to enable functionalization at the 3-position without generating undesired regioisomers while also providing a short and operationally simple sequence amenable to scale-up.

Although a few decarboxylative halogenation reactions of 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes have been reported, these transformations result only in low yields and have not been demonstrated for 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes with electron-donating alkoxy groups on their aromatic rings.16,17) Such oxygenated substrates would be particularly challenging since the increased electron density of the aromatic ring can facilitate the undesired electrophilic halogenation of their aromatic A-rings, compromising the selective formation of 3-halo-2H-chromenes. Accordingly, we sought to establish reaction conditions that ensure both high reactivity and strict regioselectivity, even in the presence of oxygen functional groups commonly found in isoflavonoid natural products.

Materials and methods

1. General procedure

For reactions requiring heating, a heat block and an oil bath were used as the heat source. Column chromatography was performed on Silica Gel 60 N (spherical, neutral), 100–210 µm (purchased from Kanto Chemical Company, Incorporated). Reactions and chromatography fractions were analyzed by thin-layer chromatography (TLC) on TLC plates purchased from FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation (Product name: Silicagel 70 F254 TLC Plate-Wako) with visualization by ultraviolet light (UV) irradiation at 254 nm and p-anisaldehyde (containing acetic and sulfuric acids) ethanol solution for staining. Preparative TLC was performed on TLC plates purchased from the same supplier with a 1-mm layer thickness and 20×20 cm area (Product name: Silicagel 70 F254 TLC Plate-Wako) with visualization by UV irradiation at 254 nm.1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded on a JEOL JNM-ECZ-400S instrument at 400 and 101 MHz, respectively. Chemical shifts (δ) and coupling constants (J) are presented in ppm (relative to internal standards) and Hz, respectively. As internal standard for 1H NMR spectroscopy, Me4Si (δ 0.00) was used. Quantitative 1H NMR (qNMR) was performed with 20 sec of relaxation delay, using triphenylmethane (Ph3CH) as internal standard. For 13C NMR spectroscopy, the internal standard was chloroform-d (CDCl3) (δ 77.00). 13C NMR spectra were recorded with proton decoupling. To distinguish mono- (CH3), di- (CH2), tri- (CH), and tetra-substituted (C) carbon atoms, distortionless enhancement by polarization transfer spectra were obtained by varying the selection angle (90° and 135°). High-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) data were obtained using an Orbitrap Eclipse instrument (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.) equipped with atmospheric pressure chemical ionization (APCI) and electrospray ionization (ESI) ion sources operated in the positive ion mode. Fourier transform (FT)-IR spectra were recorded using a Thermo Fisher Nicolet iS5 instrument with an iD5 attenuated total reflection (ATR) attachment and reported in terms of frequency absorption (cm−1).

2. Synthesis of 3-halo-2H-chromenes and isoflavene derivatives

3-Halo-2H-chromenes and isoflavene derivatives were synthesized from salicylaldehydes through a concise multistep sequence as outlined in Figs. 3 and 4. Salicylaldehydes were first converted into their corresponding 3-cyano-2H-chromenes according to a previously reported protocol,15) followed by hydrolysis to obtain 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes. These carboxylic acid intermediates were then subjected to decarboxylative halogenation under optimized conditions to give the desired 3-halo-2H-chromenes in good yields and with high regioselectivity. Furthermore, a selected 3-halo-2H-chromene was transformed into isoflavene derivatives via Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling reactions, as outlined in Fig. 6. Results and discussion

3-Cyano-2H-chromene 2a, prepared according to our previously reported procedure,15) was hydrolyzed to afford the corresponding 3-carboxy-2H-chromene 3a.18,19) Compound 3a was treated with halogenation reagents of N-halosuccinimides in the presence of potassium phosphate in acetonitrile at room temperature following the protocols reported by Larrosa and co-workers.20,21) Using N-chlorosuccinimide (NCS), the desired 3-halo-2H-chromene 4a was not provided at all (Fig. 3, entry 1). N-Bromosuccinimide (NBS) and N-iodosuccinimide (NIS) produced the corresponding 3-halo-2H-chromenes 4a (X=Br and I) in 25% and 12% yields, respectively (Fig. 3, entries 2 and 3). Although the yield of 4a (X=I) was determined by quantitative 1H NMR spectroscopy of the crude product, it was not possible to isolate and fully characterize the 3-iodo-2H-chromene, probably due to its instability. These results suggest that bromination is more promising than other halogenation reactions. Tetrabutylammonium tribromide was then used instead of NBS. Consequently, the yield of 4a increased to 38% (Fig. 3, entry 4), presumably because of its high solubility in the organic solvent. Potassium phosphate is highly hygroscopic; therefore, conducting the reaction under anhydrous conditions improved the yield to 59% (Fig. 3, entry 5), suggesting that moisture suppresses decarboxylative halogenation.

Evaluation of the bases revealed notable differences in reactivity. Substitution of potassium phosphate with potassium acetate, which is also a hygroscopic base, resulted in a markedly reduced yield of 24% (Fig. 3, entry 6). In contrast, the use of potassium carbonate, sodium acetate, sodium carbonate, lithium acetate, and lithium carbonate produced brominated product 4a in 67%, 74%, 68%, 39%, and 32% yields, respectively (Fig. 3, entries 7–11). These results imply that bases containing potassium or sodium ions are more effective in promoting the reaction. In the most successful case (Fig. 3, entry 8), the desired product 4a was obtained as the sole detectable organic product and could be isolated without column chromatography. Nevertheless, the discrepancy between complete consumption of 3a and the isolated yield (74%) may be attributed to partial polymerization and/or decomposition, generating poorly soluble materials that were not recovered during the workup process. When the reaction was performed either in the absence of base or with 0.5 eq, the yield of 4a decreased substantially, and the formation of the side product 6-bromo-3-carboxy-2H-chromene 5a became predominant (Fig. 3, entries 12 and 13). This outcome indicates that the deprotonation step of 3a plays a crucial role in enabling the decarboxylative bromination. When the amount of base was insufficient, deprotonation was impeded, which suppressed the decarboxylative bromination process. In contrast, with a sufficient amount of base, the pathway leading to the desired 3-brominated product seems to be faster than aromatic bromination. Increasing the amount of base from 1.0 to 1.5 or 2.0 eq did not lead to significant changes in yield (69% and 72%, respectively) (Fig. 3, entries 14 and 15), indicating that 1.0 eq of base is sufficient for efficient deprotonation in this system.

Increasing the amount of tetrabutylammonium tribromide from 1.0 to 1.5 or 2.0 eq resulted in the formation of 3,6-dibromo-2H-chromene 6a as the major product (Fig. 3, entries 16 and 17). These results suggest that the intermediate 4a, generated via decarboxylative bromination, undergoes additional electrophilic bromination in the presence of excess brominating reagent.

Evaluation of the reaction temperature revealed a modest improvement in yield at 60°C, providing the desired brominated chromene in 77% yield (Fig. 3, entry 18). However, 6-brominated side products 5a and 6a were also detected, suggesting that elevated temperatures facilitate electrophilic bromination on the chromene aromatic ring. At higher temperatures (80°C and 100°C), the yields dropped sharply to 21% and 0%, respectively (Fig. 3, entries 19 and 20), indicating that thermal decomposition likely occurs under these conditions.

Solvent screening demonstrated that several aprotic polar solvents provided moderate-to-good yields. Acetone, N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF), tetrahydrofuran (THF), diethyl ether, and 1,4-dioxane gave the desired product in 37%, 69%, 54%, 60%, and 51% yield, respectively (Fig. 3, entries 21–25). In contrast, methanol, a protic solvent, failed to produce any of the desired product (entry 26). This result is likely due to interference from the protic nature of methanol, whose available protons may hinder the base-mediated deprotonation, which is required for the formation of 4a. The use of chloroform, a less polar solvent, led to a markedly reduced yield of 16% (Fig. 3, entry 27), which is likely due to insufficient solvation of the ionic species necessary for the decarboxylative halogenation.

To further evaluate the effectiveness of the optimized conditions, previously reported decarboxylative halogenation conditions were applied to substrate 3a in the present system for comparison (entry 29).16,17) When NBS and lithium acetate were used instead of sodium acetate and tetrabutylammonium tribromide, the yield decreased from 74 to 36%. These results clearly demonstrate that the optimized conditions are superior to those previously reported for 3-carboxy-2H-chromene in terms of both yield and selectivity.

To investigate whether the present decarboxylative halogenation proceeds via a conventional Hunsdiecker-type radical pathway and/or through the four-membered cyclic transition state proposed by Larrosa and co-workers,21) a control experiment was conducted in the presence of the radical scavenger 2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine 1-oxyl (TEMPO, 1.0 eq). Although no TEMPO adduct was observed by crude 1H NMR analysis, the yield of the halogenated product 4a decreased markedly from 74 to 43% under otherwise identical conditions. This significant suppression of product formation in the presence of TEMPO suggests that radical intermediates partially participate in the reaction pathway. Therefore, it is speculated that the reaction may proceed via competing pathways involving both radical species and the concerted four-membered transition state.

Additionally, Larrosa and co-workers have reported decarboxylative halogenation of aromatic carboxylic acids using tetrabutylammonium tribromide and potassium phosphate.21) Although their method is effective for various substrates, it requires the use of a glove box and pre-drying of K3PO4 at high temperatures. In contrast, the present method using sodium acetate proceeds efficiently without the need for a glove box. Notably, when the conditions reported by Larrosa et al. were applied to the present substrate (Fig. 3, entry 5), the yield (59%) was lower than that obtained under our optimized conditions (74%, entry 8). These results demonstrate that the present method offers significant practical advantages in terms of operational simplicity and the use of a cost-effective and benign base, while enabling site-selective halogenation of 2H-chromenes.

To examine the applicability of the developed protocol, a 2H-chromene with an alkoxy group substituted at another position was subjected to the optimized decarboxylative halogenation conditions (Fig. 4). When 3-carboxy-6-methoxy-2H-chromene 3b,22) which was prepared in the same manner as 3a, was subjected to the optimized conditions, the reaction proceeded smoothly to furnish the corresponding 2H-chromene 4b with a satisfactory yield. This outcome would indicate that the present method is applicable to 3-carboxychromenes bearing oxygen functional groups at various positions on the aromatic rings. However, when 3-carboxy-8-methoxy-2H-chromene,19) prepared similarly to 3a, was subjected to the same reaction conditions, the transformation did not proceed efficiently resulted in no formation of the desired product indicating that further optimization of the reaction conditions remains necessary. This inefficient reaction observed for 3-carboxy-8-methoxy-2H-chromene, despite its apparent electronic similarity to substrate 3b, cannot be clearly rationalized at present. However, it is conceivable that the 8-methoxy substituent and the chromene oxygen (O1) form a chelate complex with the sodium cation derived from sodium acetate. Such chelation would decrease electron donation from the chromene oxygen to the carbonyl moiety on the hypobromite intermediate. This electronic effect may slow down the rate-determining steps via O–Br homolytic cleavage and a concerted decarboxylation/C–Br bond formation. Consequently, competing side reactions, including possible polymerization, preferentially occurred, leading to no formation of the desired 3-halogenated product.

A gram-scale reaction was performed using 3a (5.00 mmol, 1.03 g) under the optimized conditions. The reaction proceeded smoothly to deliver the desired product 4a as an essentially pure compound in 73% yield (Fig. 5). Notably, the product contained negligible amounts of side products and could be isolated without column chromatography. The minimal change in yield compared to the small-scale experiment would demonstrate that this protocol has excellent reproducibility upon scale-up, thereby indicating its practical utility.

Fig. 5. Gram scale synthesis of 3-bromo-2H-chromene 4a.

To demonstrate the synthetic utility of the developed method, we performed formal total synthesis of an isoflavan natural product, equol. The Suzuki–Miyaura coupling of chromene 4a with 4-methoxyphenylboronic acid 7 was carried out in DMF in the presence of K2CO3, PPh3, and Pd(OAc)2, offering isoflavene 8 in 79% yield (Fig. 6). Xia et al. reported compound 8 as an intermediate in the synthesis of equol,23) thereby accomplishing the formal total synthesis of an isoflavan natural product using the present strategy.

Fig. 6. Formal total synthesis of equol.

All previously reported syntheses of isoflavene 8 employed linear synthetic approaches, in which the A- and C-ring moieties were combined at early stages.24–26) In contrast, our approach relies on a convergent strategy that couples the AC-ring unit with the B-ring unit. Since convergent methods often facilitate the rapid supply of structural analogs, the present method would offer significant advantages for structure–activity relationship studies by supplying a diverse library of derivatives.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we established an efficient and regioselective synthesis of 3-bromo-2H-chromenes via the decarboxylative halogenation of 3-carboxy-2H-chromenes. The optimized conditions utilizing tetrabutylammonium tribromide and sodium acetate successfully overcame the challenge of competitive electrophilic substitution on oxygenated aromatic rings. The protocol demonstrated high practical utility, as exemplified by the reproducible gram-scale synthesis that required no chromatographic purification. Furthermore, the synthetic potential of the obtained 3-bromochromenes is highlighted by the formal total synthesis of equol via Suzuki–Miyaura coupling. This convergent strategy provides a distinct advantage over conventional linear routes by offering a flexible platform for the rapid assembly of diverse isoflavonoid scaffolds, which will significantly facilitate future structure–activity relationship studies in pharmaceutical and agrochemical research.

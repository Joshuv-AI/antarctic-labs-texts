Study on the randomness of the peak pressure of spherical blast waves
Authors: Lan Liu, Weidong Chen, Shengzhuo Lu, Yanchun Yu, Mingwu Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the purpose of exploring the influence of the material property randomness of spherical explosives and media on the randomness of Pm, the peak pressure of spherical blast waves in media, this paper studies the randomness of the peak pressure of spherical blast waves (RPPSBW) in air and water by adopting the DLGABPNN-MCS, a type of Monte Carlo simulation with the operation of DLGABPNN being repeated sampling tests. And in the DLGABPNN, the back propagation neural network (BPNN) optimized by the dynamic lifecycle genetic algorithm (DLGA), the training sample sets and test sample sets are obtained through the repeated numerical simulations of RPPSBW by the one-dimensional spherically-symmetric material point method (1D-SSMPM). This paper, first, takes as the basic random variables the initial density and parameters of the equation of state of the spherical explosive and those of the medium, and considers Pm as the random response, thereby determining the research objective of RPPSBW. This paper proposes the process of employing DLGABPNN-MCS to study RPPSBW, aiming to obtain the statistical characteristics and randomness features of the Pm. Then, with the applied examples in air and water demonstrating the specific procedures of the DLGABPNN-MCS in the RPPSBW study, it proves the capability of the 1D-SSMPM to accurately simulate RPPSBW, the higher efficiency of the DLGA than genetic algorithm in optimizing the BPNN, and the accuracy of the DLGABPNN in calculating the Pm, thus verifying the effectiveness of the DLGABPNN-MCS in the RPPSBW study. It further obtains the statistical characteristics of the Pm such as the mean and coefficient of variation, which reveals the randomness features of the Pm. It is hoped that this paper can shed some light on the future randomness study of other complex blast waves.Keywords: Spherical blast wave, Peak pressure randomness, One-dimensional spherically-symmetric material point method, Back propagation neural network, Dynamic lifecycle genetic algorithm, Monte Carlo simulationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The blast waves formed by disturbing the surrounding media after the detonation of explosives can cause serious harm to people and objects. This shows that blast waves are the essential manifestation of the destructive power of explosives. And the study of blast waves has long been capturing the close attention from researchers in the field of explosion mechanics. As the detonation of a spherical explosive in a single, infinite medium domain will form a spherical blast wave, Sadovskyi studied the spherical blast waves formed by the detonation of spherical explosives in infinite air domain, and obtained the Sadovskyi formula for depicting the peak overpressure of spherical blast waves in air1; Cole explored the spherical blast waves formed by the detonation of spherical explosives in infinite water domain, and obtained the Cole formula for describing the propagation morphology and peak pressure of spherical blast waves in water2, on which Zamyshlyayev based his improved version of the Cole formula3. These research results have laid a foundation for subsequent researches on blast waves under more complex conditions, and also provided a theoretical ground for the TNT equivalent evaluation of explosives and for the development of weapons and ammunition.

As computational mechanics and computer science enjoy their rapid development in modern times, computer numerical simulations have found wide application in the study of practical engineering problems in explosion mechanics, and the Material Point Method (MPM)4 is a common numerical computation method for the numerical simulations of explosion mechanics. For MPM, material points carry all physical information, while grid nodes solely serve to solve the momentum equations and spatial derivatives. In each time integration, the shape function is called to perform a round-trip transmission of the calculated information between the material points and the grid nodes. Each time the time integration is completed, the material point information is continuously updated, and the grid cells are automatically restored. In this sense, MPM offers the advantages of both particle algorithms and grid algorithms. In the numerical simulations of explosion mechanics, material points are capable of accurately characterizing such physical activities as the flow of media and explosives’ detonation products, the large deformation of solid structures and debris splashing, and grid cells are automatically restored to avoid distortion — MPM shows competitive advantages in the numerical simulations of explosion mechanics. Besides, with spherical blast waves being spherically symmetric, the one-dimensional spherically-symmetric numerical model can conduct accurate simulations of spherical bast waves, reducing computing substantially. Chen et al.5 proposed the One-Dimensional Spherically-Symmetric Material Point Method (1D-SSMPM) to simulate the underwater spherical blast waves, with the simulated results in good agreement with those obtained by the Cole formula and LS-DYNA.

Moreover, as explosion mechanics problems are typical transient high-stress problems, the material properties of explosives and media exert a significant impact on the pressure of blast waves. Remarkably affected by the factors such as the formula ratios of energetic materials, preparation processes and storage environments, the blast effects of explosives will be obviously random. With variable pressure, temperature and humidity in itself, air is also random in its density and specific heat ratio. And underwater locations and chemical compositions in water can lead to the randomness of water’s density and parameters of the Mie-Grüneisen equation of state6. Therefore, the pressure of the blast waves formed by the detonation of the explosives in air or water will be characterized by obvious randomness. Xu6 was probably the first to realize the importance of this problem, by integrating the semi-analytical method with MPM, proposed the Stochastic Material Point Method (SMPM) in studying the randomness of the pressure of underwater blast waves, and obtained the statistical characteristics such as the mean and variance of the pressure. Since SMPM just requires one performance function in the stochastic model, Xu just took the Mie-Grüneisen equation of state of water as the performance function of this model, of which the parameters γ0, a, S1 and S3 were selected as the Basic Random Variables (BRVs), and conducted the study without taking into account the influence of the blast effect randomness of explosives on the pressure randomness of the blast waves. As mentioned above, the blast effects of explosives exercise a considerable influence on blast wave pressure, so the blast effect randomness of explosives can never be ignored in investigating the pressure randomness of blast waves. In such case, SMPM may not be a reliable method for studying the randomness of the pressure of blast waves.

On the other hand, the ANN-MCS, the Monte Carlo Simulation (MCS) taking the operation of Artificial Neural Network (ANN) as its repeated sampling tests, has been proven to be an important type of method of solving most complex randomness problems7,8. In studying the randomness of the pressure of blast waves, this type of method, without requiring a clear, continuous derivable performance function between the BRVs and the Random Responses (RRs), takes into account the influence of the material property randomness of both explosives and media on the pressure randomness of blast waves. Therefore, it would be a best option to adopt this type of method in exploring the randomness of the pressure of blast waves.

On the basis of the above research results, this paper conducts a study of the Randomness of the Peak Pressure of Spherical Blast Waves (RPPSBW) in air and water. The RPPSBW in Air (RPPSBW-A) takes as the BRVs the spherical explosive’s initial density and parameters of the JWL equation of state6, and air’s initial density and specific heat ratio, and considers the peak pressure Pm of the spherical blast wave in air as the RR. Similarly, the RPPSBW in Water (RPPSBW-W) takes as the BRVs the spherical explosive’s initial density and parameters of the JWL equation of state, and water’s initial density and non-zero parameters of the Mie-Grüneisen equation of state, and considers the Pm of the spherical blast wave in water as the RR. Then, this paper takes the DLGABPNN8 — the Back Propagation Neural Network (BPNN)8 optimized by the Dynamic Lifecycle Genetic Algorithm (DLGA)8 — as the surrogate models of the RPPSBW-A and RPPSBW-W, and adopts the DLGABPNN-MCS8 — the MCS taking the operation of DLGABPNN as its repeated sampling tests — to study the RPPSBW-A and RPPSBW-W, so as to obtain the statistical characteristics such as the mean and coefficient of variation (CV) and the randomness features of the Pm in these 2 randomness problems. And the training sample sets and test sample sets of DLGABPNN are obtained through the repeated numerical simulations of these 2 random problems by 1D-SSMPM. It is hoped that this paper can shed some light on the future randomness study of other complex blast waves.

BRVs, RRs and research objective of RPPSBW

As equations of state can describe the Pressure-Volume-Energy (P-V-E) relationship of materials, the shock dynamics properties of materials can be characterized by the parameters of equations of state. This means that the randomness of the blast effect of the spherical explosive can be expressed by the randomness of the explosive’s initial density and parameters of the JWL equation of state, that the randomness of the shock dynamics property of air can be expressed by the randomness of air’s initial density and parameter (specific heat ratio) of the equation of state of ideal gas, and that the randomness of the shock dynamics property of water can be expressed by the randomness of water’s initial density and non-zero parameters of the Mie-Grüneisen equation of state. And the randomness of the blast effect of the spherical explosive and the randomness of the shock dynamics property of the medium will jointly contribute to the randomness of the Pm in the medium. Therefore, RPPSBW-A should take as its 8 BRVs the spherical explosive’s initial density ρE0 and parameters of the JWL equation of state — A, B, R1, R2 and w, and air’s initial density ρA0 and specific heat ratio γ, and take the Pm in air as the RR. On the other hand, RPPSBW-W should take as its 11 BRVs the spherical explosive’s initial density ρE0 and parameters of the JWL equation of state — A, B, R1, R2 and w, and water’s initial density ρW0 and non-zero parameters of the Mie-Grüneisen equation of state — γ0, S1, S2 and S3, and take the Pm in water as the RR. In short, the research objective of RPPSBW (RPPSBW-A and RPPSBW-W) is to investigate the statistical characteristics and randomness features of the RR Pm under the influence of the BRVs.

Methodology

In this research, DLGABPNN-MCS is adopted to study RPPSBW (RPPSBW-A and RPPSBW-W). The principles of BPNN, DLGA, DLGABPNN and DLGABPNN-MCS can be found in reference8, and the principle of 1D-SSMPM can be found in reference5, which will not be elaborated here. Below, the process of DLGABPNN-MCS studying RPPSBW will be focused on.

Constructing DLGABPNN

Firstly, the BRV sample points are called in turn, and the repeated numerical simulations of RPPSBW are conducted by employing 1D-SSMPM to obtain the Pm (RR) of each gauge in the RPPSBW numerical model corresponding to each BRV sample point. Constructing the training sample sets and test sample sets

For general applicability of the DLGABPNN, the GABPNN and the BPNN, 2,000 Latin-hypercube sample points were first drawn under a 0.1 CV for all BRVs. Each of the sample points was called in turn by the 1D-SSMPM program for the repeated numerical simulations of the RPPSBW-A, so as to obtain each gauge’s Pm in the numerical models corresponding to these sample points. After standardizing these sample points and the Pm, a 2,000-training-sample set was constructed to train the DLGABPNN, the GABPNN and the BPNN. Then, under the BRVs’ four CVs of 0.01, 0.03, 0.05 and 0.07, 500 sample points were randomly drawn for each case, and each gauge’s Pm in the numerical models corresponding to these sample points was obtained by repeated numerical simulations. With the standardization of the results, four 500-test-sample sets were constructed to assess the accuracy of the DLGABPNN, the GABPNN and the BPNN in calculating each gauge’s Pm under these four randomness conditions.

Determining the structure and key parameters of the ANNs

Since it has been proven that the 3-layer forward ANNs can solve the great majority of the nonlinear mapping problems12,13, the DLGABPNN in this example was single-hidden-layer structured. With 8 BRVs and 11 RRs, the neuron numbers of the input layer and output layer were, respectively, set as 8 and 11, and the neuron number of the hidden layer was set as 15. Hence, the DLGABPNN had an architecture of 8 × 15 × 11. And the important parameters of the DLGABPNN were so assigned: the maximum number of network training times Tmax = 20,000, the network training rate St = 0.05, the network training allowable error Ea = 0.001, n1 = 10, NL = 500, NB = 10,000, Tend = 5, PC = 0.8, PM = 0.1, the coefficients C1 and C2 in Eq. (4) were respectively set as 10.0 and 0.15.

The GABPNN had the same structure as the DLGABPNN, with the population size Mp = 50 and the maximum reproduction generation Gmax = 60. The rest of the parameters were the same as their counterparts in the DLGABPNN. Likewise, the BPNN also had the same structure and parameters as the DLGABPNN.

Comparing the performances of the ANNs

The DLGABPNN, the GABPNN and the BPNN were respectively trained by the above training sample sets, and tested by the four test sample sets discussed, with the test results shown in Fig. 5.

Test results of the ANNs for the four test sample sets in RPPSBW-A.

As shown in Fig. 5, the BPNN provides an MAPE of 1.5% − 9.5% for each gauge’s Pm in the numerical models corresponding to the four test sample sets, while either of the DLGABPNN and the GABPNN offers an MAPE of 0.5% − 6.5%. This means that the DLGABPNN and the GABPNN, with virtually equivalent performance, are both distinctly superior to the BPNN, thereby confirming the pronounced effectiveness of DLGA and GA in optimizing the BPNN. Furthermore, in the optimization of the BPNN by DLGA for the DLGABPNN, the 756th individual of the population was qualified to be the optimal individual, while in the optimization of the BPNN by GA for the GABPNN, the 1860th individual of the population (i.e., the 10th individual of the 38th generation of the population) was qualified to work as the optimal individual. This demonstrates that, for comparable optimization, DLGA achieves markedly higher efficiency than GA in optimizing the BPNN, and takes considerably less time in obtaining the DLGABPNN than GA does in obtaining the GABPNN. It is therefore considered more appropriate to use DLGA to optimize the BPNN into the DLGABPNN when building the surrogate model of RPPSBW-A. Moreover, the statistical characteristics of each gauge’s Pm in the numerical model can be reliably estimated by the DLGABPNN-MCS.

Estimating the statistical characteristics of the Pm based on the DLGABPNN-MCS

A prediction sample set with 100,000 sample points was constructed under each of the above four randomness conditions. The mean and CV of each gauge’s Pm in the RPPSBW-A numerical model were obtained by applying the DLGABPNN-MCS, thereby drawing the relation curves of the statistical characteristics of the Pm and the R, as shown in Fig. 6.

Pm - R relation curves in the RPPSBW-A numerical model.

Figure 6 reveals 3 randomness features of the Pm in air. First, the 5 Pm’s mean - R relation curves are in excellent agreement with each other, showing that the mean of the Pm, consistently equal to its deterministic value, does not change with the CV of the BRVs. This indicates that, when BRVs follow the normal distribution, the statistical distribution of the Pm is approximately axisymmetric, with the deterministic value of the Pm serving as the symmetry axis. Second, all the 4 Pm’s CV - R relation curves exhibit an upward trend, showing that the CV of the Pm is positively correlated with R. This further demonstrates that the randomness of the Pm accumulates progressively with the propagation of the spherical blast wave in air. Third, the lower the CV of the BRVs, the lower the overall Pm’s CV - R relation curves. This means a positive correlation between the CV of the Pm and that of the BRVs — weaker randomness in the BRVs yields weaker randomness in RPPSBW-A and, consequently, in the Pm. It follows that if the CV of the BRVs is reduced to 0, RPPSBW-A would become a deterministic problem, with the CV of the Pm falling to 0, that is, the RR Pm would revert to the deterministic response.

Figure 11 reveals 3 randomness features of the Pm in water. First, the 5 Pm’s mean - R relation curves are in excellent agreement with each other, implying that the mean of the Pm, consistently equal to its deterministic value, does not change with the CV of the BRVs. This indicates that, when the BRVs follow the normal distribution, the statistical distribution of the Pm is also approximately axisymmetric, with the deterministic value of the Pm serving as the symmetry axis. Second, all the 4 Pm’s CV - R relation curves exhibit a slightly upward trend, showing that the CV of the Pm is positively correlated with the R. This further demonstrates that the randomness of the Pm accumulates slowly with the propagation of the spherical blast wave in water. Third, the lower the CV of the BRVs, the lower the overall Pm’s CV - R relation curves. This means a positive correlation between the CV of the Pm and that of the BRVs — weaker randomness in the BRVs yields weaker randomness in RPPSBW-W and, consequently, in the Pm. It further follows that if the CV of the BRVs is reduced to 0, RPPSBW-W would become a deterministic problem, with the CV of the Pm falling to 0, that is, the RR Pm would revert to the deterministic response. From what was discussed above, the randomness features of the Pm in water are found to be virtually the same as those in air.

Discussion

The innovative highlight of this paper lies primarily in pioneering ANN-MCS for studying RPPSBW (RPPSBW-A and RPPSBW-W). The key advantage of this method is the elimination of the need for a definite, continuously differentiable performance function between BRVs and RRs. This allows the initial densities and parameters of the equations of state of both spherical explosives and media to be treated as BRVs, and takes into account the influence of the material property randomness of both spherical explosives and media on the randomness of the Pm. Therefore, the statistical characteristics such as the mean and CV of the Pm, and its randomness features are obtained under a broader spectrum of randomness. Another highlight of this paper is the employment of 1D-SSMPM to perform repeated numerical simulations for RPPSBW in constructing the training sample sets and test sample sets of the ANNs, by exploiting the spherical symmetry of spherical bast waves. This method guarantees the sample quality while drastically boosting efficiency, thereby slashing the time cost of the RPPSBW study with ANN-MCS.

On the other hand, 2 limitations remain for future improvement. First, only the randomness of the peak pressure of spherical blast waves is examined. In reality, the factors such as the structural specificity of bombs, the cross-medium propagation of blast waves, and the blockage of constructions often yield markedly more complex non-spherical blast waves, so it is necessary for future studies to extend the analysis to the randomness of more complex blast waves. Second, this paper is limited to adopting the DLGABPNN as the surrogate model of RPPSBW. However, with the rapid development of artificial intelligence and machine learning, the ANNs with better performances may have been developed and widely applied in various disciplines. It is thus necessary in future study to consider alternative ANNs as the surrogate models for investigating the randomness of blast waves.

Conclusions

RPPSBW should take into account the influence of the material property randomness of both the spherical explosive and the medium on the randomness of the Pm. In this case, RPPSBW-A should take as its BRVs the spherical explosive’s initial density ρE0 and parameters of the JWL equation of state — A, B, R1, R2 and w, and air’s initial density ρA0 and specific heat ratio γ, and take the Pm in air as the RR. On the other hand, RPPSBW-W should take as its BRVs the spherical explosive’s initial density ρE0 and parameters of the JWL equation of state — A, B, R1, R2 and w, and water’s initial density ρW0 and non-zero parameters of the Mie-Grüneisen equation of state — γ0, S1, S2 and S3, and take the Pm in water as the RR. Therefore, the methods combining semi-analytical methods with numerical computation methods (e.g., SMPM6 seem inappropriate for studying RPPSBW, and it is necessary to employ ANN-MCS for this purpose.

The applied examples of RPPSBW in this paper have proven that DLGA optimizes the BPNN for the DLGABPNN in a more efficient way than GA optimizes the BPNN for the GABPNN, and that the DLGABPNN and the BPNN, with virtually equivalent performance, accurately calculating the Pm in air and water. Thus, it can be considered appropriate for this paper to take the DLGABPNN as the surrogate model and study RPPSBW with the DLGABPNN-MCS. It also justifies the accuracy of the statistical characteristics such as the mean and CV of the Pm in air and water, and further, the accuracy of the randomness features of the Pm.

The RPPSBW study in this paper has arrived at 3 common randomness features of the Pm in air and water. First, when the BRVs follow the normal distribution, the statistical distribution of the Pm is approximately axisymmetric, with the deterministic value of the Pm serving as the symmetry axis. Under such randomness conditions, the mean of the Pm is consistently equal to its deterministic value, and does not change with the CV of the BRVs. Second, the randomness of the Pm can accumulate with the propagation of spherical blast waves in air or water, and so does the CV of the Pm. Third, there is a positive correlation between the CV of the Pm and that of the BRVs — weaker randomness in the BRVs yields weaker randomness in RPPSBW and, consequently, in the Pm. It further follows that if the CV of the BRVs is reduced to 0, RPPSBW would become a deterministic problem, with the CV of the Pm falling to 0 — the RR Pm would revert to the deterministic response.

The spherical symmetry of spherical blast waves allows for one-dimensional spherically-symmetric numerical simulations. And given the advantages of MPM in the numerical simulations of explosion mechanics, 1D-SSMPM is proposed for the repeated numerical simulations of RPPSBW to generate the ANN training and test sample sets. This method ensures the sample-set quality while remarkably reducing the computational effort, greatly enhancing the efficiency of ANN-MCS in the RPPSBW study.

This paper focuses solely on the randomness of the peak pressure of spherical blast waves, but the blast waves in reality are often in more complex forms than the sheer spherical form. It is therefore necessary in future study to explore the randomness of more complex blast waves. Moreover, this paper is limited to employing the DLGABPNN as the surrogate model of RPPSBW, but the ANNs with better performances may have been developed. This means that future study should consider alternative ANNs as the surrogate models for investigating the randomness of blast waves.

Zero-dimensional tropicalizations in Oscar
Arman Marti-Shahandeh, Yue Ren, Victoria Schleis

Abstract
. We present algorithms for computing zero-dimensional tropical varieties as implemented in OscarZerodimensionalTropicalization.jl . The algorithms include a workaround for a common practical issue arising when working with polynomials over inexact fields in existing software systems.



1.
Introduction

Tropical varieties are a combinatorial shadow of algebraic varieties. They play a central role in tropical geometry, and, more broadly, in combinatorial algebraic geometry. To understand and apply tropical geometry, efficient algorithms for the computation of tropical varieties are essential. For example, showing that the tropicalization equals
\{0\}
proves the finiteness of certain central configurations in celestial mechanics
[
10
,
9
]
, and computing tropicalizations enables better ways to solve certain polynomial systems
[
11
]
.

Fundamentally, given an ideal
I\subseteq K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and a (possibly trivial) valuation
\val\colon K^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the computational task is to find a set of polyhedra covering the
tropical variety

\Trop(I)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is usually done via a traversal over
\Trop(I)

[
1
,
16
]
, and most non-Gröbner computations in that traversal can be boiled down to computations of zero-dimensional tropical varieties over function fields
[
12
]
. More specifically, we are interested in determining the tropical variety where
K
is the algebraic closure of a rational function field
\mathfrak{K}(t)
with its
t
-adic valuation,
I
is zero-dimensional and
\Trop(I)
consists of finitely many points.

In this paper, we report on a new implementation of the algorithm for computing zero-dimensional tropical varieties in
Oscar

[
18
]
. Our algorithm works via triangular decomposition and root approximation as in
[
12
]
, but has several technical
improvements over existing implementations.

The main challenge we address is
Oscar
’s handling of polynomials over inexact fields, which is similar to that of
Macaulay2
and
Sagemath
, and different to that of
magma
. Simply speaking,
Macaulay2
,
Oscar
, and
Sagemath
work with sparse polynomials, meaning that numerically zero terms are deleted, see
Section

3
. This may destroy important precision information. We solve this issue by introducing additional variables
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
to track the error terms in
Section

4
.

Unlike
[
12
]
, we do not outsource the root approximation to
magma

[
2
]
, which gives us much finer control on the precision required for computing
\Trop(I)
. And unlike a similar algorithm in
[
13
]
, we do not track field extensions manually and instead make generous use of the
algebraic_closure
functionality in
Oscar
, which improves the readability of our code significantly.

2.
Background

In this section, we fix our notation and review basics of valued fields, tropical geometry, and triangular decompositions of Gröbner bases.

Convention 2.1

In this paper, let
\mathfrak{K}=\mathfrak{K}^{\mathrm{al}}
be an algebraically closed field. Let
\mathfrak{K}(\!(t)\!)
denote its field of Laurent series, and let
K\coloneqq\mathfrak{K}(\!(t)\!)^{\mathrm{al}}
be its algebraic closure. If
\mathfrak{K}
has characteristic
0
, then
K=\mathfrak{K}\{\!\{t\}\!\}
is the field of Puiseux series
[
15
, Theorem 2.1.5]
. If
\mathfrak{K}
has positive characteristic,
K
is a subfield of the field of Hahn series
[
8
, Section 2]
containing the field of Puiseux series:

\mathfrak{K}\{\!\{t\}\!\}\subseteq K\subseteq\mathfrak{K}\llbracket t^{\mathbb{Q}}\rrbracket\coloneqq\Big\{\sum_{w\in S}c_{w}\cdot t^{w}\left.\vphantom{\Big\{}\;|\;\vphantom{\Big\}}\right.S\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\text{ well ordered and }c_{w}\in\mathfrak{K}\Big\}.

Recall that
S\subseteq\mathbb{Q}
being well-ordered means any subset of
S
has a minimum. This allows us to view elements of
K
as generalized power series over
\mathfrak{K}
.

The field
K
has a natural valuation under which
\mathfrak{K}
becomes its residue field:

\val\colon K^{\ast}\coloneqq K\setminus\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\sum_{w\in S}c_{w}\cdot t^{w}\longmapsto\min\{w\in S\mid c_{w}\neq 0\}.

When we want to focus on the lowest coefficients of
z\in K
, we will often write it as

(1)

z=c_{0}\cdot t^{w_{0}}+c_{1}\cdot t^{w_{1}}+\dots+c_{r-1}\cdot t^{w_{r-1}}+u\cdot t^{w_{r}}.

where
\val(z)=w_{0}<\dots<w_{r}
and
c_{0},\dots,c_{r-1}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\ast}
and
u\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(u)=0
.

We further fix a multivariate Laurent polynomial ring
K[x^{\pm}]\coloneqq K[x_{1}^{\pm},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm}]
and its polynomial subring
K[x]\coloneqq K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

2.1.
Tropical Varieties

In this section, we recall the definition of tropical varieties. As we will rely on techniques adjacent to classical Gröbner basis theory, see
Section

2.2
, we will work with polynomial ideals rather than Laurent polynomial ideals as in
[
15
]
. For the sake of simplicity, we use one of the equivalent definitions in the Fundamental Theorem of Tropical Geometry
[
15
, Theorem 3.2.3]

Definition 2.2

The
tropical variety
of an ideal
I\subseteq K[x^{\pm}]
is

\Trop(I)\coloneqq\mathrm{cl}\Big(\val(V(I)\cap(K^{\ast})^{n})\Big)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
V(I)\subseteq(K^{\ast})^{n}
is the very affine variety of
I
,
\val(\cdot)
denotes the coordinatewise valuation, and
\mathrm{cl}(\cdot)
denotes the closure in the Euclidean topology.

For principal ideals
\langle f\rangle\subseteq K[x^{\pm}]
we abbreviate
\Trop(f)\coloneqq\Trop(\langle f\rangle)
.

Every ideal of Laurent polynomials
I\subseteq K[x^{\pm}]
induces an ideal of polynomials
I\cap K[x]
. This allows us to interpret
I
as an ideal of polynomials over
K[x]
, which we will do in the remainder of this paper.

When
I
is zero-dimensional,
\Trop(I)
consists of a finitely many points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Tropicalizations of univariate polynomials are zero-dimensional and can be read off their Newton polygons (also referred to as Newton diagrams in
[
15
]
and as extended Newton polyhedra in
[
14
]
):

Definition 2.3

Let
f^{\prime}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in S}a_{j}\cdot x_{i}^{j}\in K[x_{i}]
be a univariate polynomial with finite support
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Its
Newton polygon
is

\Delta(f^{\prime})=\conv\Big(\big\{(j,\val(a_{j}))\mid j\in S\big\}\Big)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot(0,1)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}.

A
lower edge
of
\Delta(f^{\prime})
is an edge connecting two vertices
(j_{1},\val(a_{j}))
and
(j_{2},\val(a_{j_{2}}))
for distinct
j_{1},j_{2}\in S
, say
j_{1}<j_{2}
, and we refer to
(\val(a_{j_{2}})-\val(a_{j_{1}}))/(j_{2}-j_{1})
as a
slope
of
\Delta(f^{\prime})
.

The slopes of edges of the Newton polygon determine the tropicalization.

Lemma 2.4

Let
f^{\prime}\in K[x_{i}]
be a univariate polynomial in
x_{i}
. Then

\Trop(f^{\prime})=\{-s\in\mathbb{R}\mid s\text{ slope of }\Delta(f^{\prime})\}.

In the best case,
Lemma

2.4
alone suffices to compute tropicalizations of zero-dimensional ideals, as seen in
Figure

For

2.2.
Triangular decompositions

Definition 2.8

A set of polynomials
F\subseteq K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is a
triangular set
if it is of the form
F=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
with
f_{i}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{i}]\setminus K[x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1}]
, i.e., the
i
-th polynomial only contains the first
i
variables.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
7
, Corollary 4.7.4]
)

Let
I\subseteq K[x]
be a zero-dimensional ideal. Then there are triangular sets
F_{1},\dots,F_{k}\subseteq K[x]
such that

\sqrt{I}=\sqrt{\langle F_{1}\rangle}\cap\dots\cap\sqrt{\langle F_{k}\rangle},

where
\sqrt{\cdot}
denotes the radical of the ideal.

Remark 2.10

A triangular decomposition of an ideal
I
can be obtained by computing a
Gröbner basis of
I
with respect to the lexicographical order. Given a Gröbner basis
G
of
I
for another order, the
FGLM
-algorithm
[
5
]
efficiently converts
G
to a lexicographical Gröbner basis.

(2)

[

[

Both options can be problematic for the task at hand in their own ways.

In Option (

4.
Root approximations

In this section, we introduce a symbolic workaround to the technical problem outlined in
Section

3
. We then discuss how to perform root approximations of univariate polynomials whilst using this workaround.

4.1.
Approximate roots

To symbolically track numerical uncertainties in our computation, we work over the following ring:

Definition 4.1

The
uncertainty ring
is the polynomial ring
K[u]\coloneqq K[u_{1},\dots,u_{n}]
. On
K[u]
we define a valuation extending that of
K
:

\val\colon K[u]^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\quad\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}\mapsto\min\Big(\{\val(a_{\alpha})\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\text{ with }a_{\alpha}\neq 0\}\Big).

We use
\tilde{f}_{i}\in K[u][x_{i}]
to denote univariate approximate polynomials. Further, any
\tilde{f}\in K[u][x]
will always be expressed as
\tilde{f}\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha\in S}\tilde{a}_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}
for some finite
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and non-zero
\tilde{a}_{j}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}]^{\ast}
.

Treating
K[u]
as a valued ring, the definitions of initials, Newton polygons, and initial forms extend straightforwardly from
2.1
,
Definition

2.3
and
Definition

2.6
respectively.

The variables
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in K[u]
represent arbitrary elements of
K
of valuation
0
. We will use
u_{1}
for the tails of the roots
z_{1}\in V(f_{1})
, and the remaining
u_{i}
for the tails of the roots
z_{i}\in V(f_{i}(z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1},x_{i}))
.

Convention 4.2

For the remainder of the section, let
I=\langle F\rangle\subseteq K[x]
be an ideal generated by a triangular set
F=\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}
with
f_{i}\in K[x_{1},\dots,x_{i}]
.

Definition 4.3

Let
\tilde{f}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][x_{i}]
be a univariate approximate polynomial. We say the Newton polygon
\Delta(\tilde{f})
is
well-defined
or
unique
, if

(2)

\Delta(\underbrace{\tilde{f}|_{u_{\ell}=z_{\ell}}}_{\in K[x_{i}]})=\Delta(\underbrace{\tilde{f}|_{u_{\ell}=z_{\ell}^{\prime}}}_{\in K[x_{i}]})

for all
z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{i-1}^{\prime}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{\ell})=\val(z_{\ell}^{\prime})=0
.
If
\Delta(\tilde{f})
is unique, we define
\Delta(\tilde{f})
to be the polygon in
Equation

2
and
\Trop(\tilde{f})
to be the set of its negated slopes.

Lemma 4.4

Let
\tilde{f}=\sum_{\alpha\in S}\tilde{a}_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][x_{i}]
be a univariate approximate polynomial. Then
\Delta(\tilde{f})
is unique if and only if

\tilde{a}_{j}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}]
is a monomial for all vertices
(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j}))\in\Delta(\tilde{f}).

Proof.

We start by showing “
\Leftarrow
". Assume that
\tilde{a}_{j}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}]
is a monomial for all vertices
(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j}))\in\Delta(\tilde{f})
. Then for all
z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{i-1}^{\prime}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{\ell})=\val(z_{\ell}^{\prime})=0
we have

\displaystyle\Delta(\tilde{f}|_{u_{\ell}=z_{\ell}})

\displaystyle=\conv\Big(\big\{(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j}|_{u_{\ell}=z_{\ell}}))\mid j\in S\big\}\Big)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot(0,1)

\displaystyle=\conv\Big(\big\{(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j})\mid j\in S\big\}\Big)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot(0,1)

\displaystyle=\conv\Big(\big\{(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}^{\prime}}))\mid j\in S\big\}\Big)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cdot(0,1)=\Delta(\tilde{f}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}^{\prime}}).

This proves that
\Delta(\tilde{f})
is unique.

We now show “
\Rightarrow
". Assume there exists some vertex
(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j}))\in\Delta(\tilde{f})
such that
\tilde{a}_{j}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}]
is not a monomial. Then there are
z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1},z_{1}^{\prime},\dots,z_{i-1}^{\prime}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{\ell})=\val(z_{\ell}^{\prime})=0
such that
\tilde{a}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}}\neq 0
with
\val(\tilde{a}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}})=\val(\tilde{a}_{j})
and
\tilde{a}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}^{\prime}}=0
. Consequently,
\Delta(\tilde{f}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}})
contains vertex
(j,\val(\tilde{a}_{j}))
and
\Delta(\tilde{f}|_{u_{j}=z_{j}^{\prime}})
does not. This implies that
\Delta(\tilde{f})
is not unique.
∎

Next we introduce approximate roots and characterize them algebraically for adaptation in our implementation.

Definition 4.5

Let
\tilde{f}_{i}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][x_{i}]
be a univariate polynomial over the uncertainty ring with a unique Newton polygon.
An
approximate root
of
\tilde{f}_{i}
is an element
\tilde{z}_{i}\in K[u_{i}]
of the form

\tilde{z}_{i}=c_{1}t^{w_{1}}+\dots+c_{r-1}t^{w_{r-1}}+u_{i}\cdot t^{w_{r}}

for some
r\geq 1
,
w_{1}<\dots<w_{r}
,
c_{j}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\ast}
for
j<r
such that for all
z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{j})=0
there is an
z_{i}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{i})=0
such that
\tilde{f}(\tilde{z})|_{u_{1}=z_{1},\dots,u_{i}=z_{i}}=0
.

We say a set of approximate roots
\tilde{V}\subseteq K[u_{i}]
is
complete
, if for all
z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{\ell})=0
and all
z\in V(\tilde{f}|_{u_{1}=z_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}=z_{i-1}})\subseteq K
there are
\tilde{z}_{i}\in\tilde{V}
and
z_{i}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{i})=0
such that
z=\tilde{z}_{i}|_{u_{i}=z_{i}}
.

Example 4.6

Consider
\tilde{f}_{2}=(x_{2}-t-t^{2}u_{1})(x_{2}-1-t-t^{2}u_{1})\in K[u_{1}][x_{2}]
. Two complete sets of approximate roots are
\widetilde{V}=\{u_{2},tu_{2}\}
and
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}=\{1+t+t^{2}u_{2},t+t^{2}u_{2}\}
. In particular, this shows that complete sets of approximate roots are not unique.

Lemma 4.7

Let
\tilde{f}_{i}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][x_{i}]
have a unique Newton polygon. Then

\tilde{z}_{i}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i}]\text{ is an approximate root of }\tilde{f}_{i}
if and only if

\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i}))\in\mathfrak{K}[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][u_{i}]

is not a monomial in
u_{i}
.

Proof.

In the following, let
z_{1},\dots,z_{i-1}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{\ell})=0
.

To see “
\Rightarrow
”, assume that
\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i}))
is a monomial in
u_{i}
. Then there is no
\overline{z}_{i}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\ast}
such that
\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i})|_{u_{1}=z_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}=z_{i-1}})|_{u_{i}=\overline{z}_{i}}=0
, and thus there is also no
z_{i}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{i})=0
such that
\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i})|_{u_{1}=z_{1},\dots,u_{i}=z_{i}})=0
. This contradicts that
\tilde{z}_{i}
is an approximate root of
\tilde{f}_{i}
.

To show “
\Leftarrow
”, assume that
\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i}))\in\mathfrak{K}[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][u_{i}]
is not a monomial in
u_{i}
. Then there is some
\overline{z}_{i}\in\mathfrak{K}^{\ast}
such that
\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i})|_{u_{1}=z_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}=z_{i-1}})|_{u_{i}=\overline{z}_{i}}=0
, and thus there is also no
z_{i}\in K^{\ast}
with
\val(z_{i})=0
such that
\initial(\tilde{f}_{i}(\tilde{z}_{i})|_{u_{1}=z_{1},\dots,u_{i}=z_{i}})=0
. This shows that
\tilde{z}_{i}
is an approximate root of
\tilde{f}_{i}
.
∎

In
Example

4.6
, we have seen that approximate roots are not unique and can have varying precision. This motivates the following:

Definition 4.8

Let
\tilde{f}_{i}\in K[u_{1},\dots,u_{i-1}][x_{i}]
be a univariate polynomial over the uncertainty ring with a unique Newton polygon, and let
\tilde{z}_{i}\in K[u_{i}]
be an approximate root of
\tilde{f}_{i}
.
We say
\tilde{z}_{i}
has

5.
Tropicalizing triangular sets

In this section, we discuss how to tropicalize varieties of ideals generated by a triangular sets as in
[
12
, Section 2]
. We improve on
[
12
, Algorithm 2.10]
through finer control on the precision during the tropicalization.

Our algorithm tropicalizes
I
by solving the system
f_{1}=\dots=f_{n}=0
through backsubstitution. The precision to which a root
z_{1}\in V(f_{1})
is computed governs the maximal possible precision to which a root
z_{2}\in V(f_{2}(z_{1},x_{2}))
can be computed, which, in turn, governs the maximal possible precision to which a root
z_{3}\in V(f_{3}(z_{1},z_{2},x_{3}))
can be computed, and so on. Roots
z_{2},z_{3},\dots
are computed up to the maximal possible precision, and the precision of
z_{1}
is kept minimal.

5.1.
Root trees of triangular sets

As mentioned in the beginning of the section, our algorithm works using simple univariate root approximation and backsubstitution. We track the global state of the computation in a so-called root tree. Let us first recall some basic concepts from graph theory:

Definition 5.1

A
(rooted) tree
is a finite simple graph
\Gamma
with no cycles and a distinguished vertex referred to as the
root
, which we usually denote
v_{0}
. The degree of a vertex is the number of edges connecting to it, and vertices of degree

are referred to as

Using

,

, and

to denote the vertices, edges, and branches of

is a sequence of vertices
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
such that

is the root and
(v_{i-1},v_{i})\in E
are edges for

as the
length
of the branch
(v_{0},\dots,v_{k})
, and the
depth
of vertex
v_{k}
. The latter is justified as for any

is the maximal depth of its vertices, which is necessarily attained at its leaves.

Definition 5.2

A
root tree
of the triangular set

is a rooted tree
\Gamma
of depth at most
n
together with two maps

\displaystyle\tilde{z}

\displaystyle\colon V(\Gamma)\setminus\{v_{0}\}\rightarrow K[u],\qquad v\mapsto\tilde{z}(v)\eqqcolon\tilde{z}_{v},

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\colon V(\Gamma)\setminus\{v_{0}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad\hskip 4.2679ptv\mapsto p(v)\eqqcolon p_{v},

\tilde{z}_{v_{i}}
.

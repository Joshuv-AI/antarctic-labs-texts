Exploiting Term Sparsity in Symmetry-Adapted Basis for Polynomial Optimization
Igor Klep, Victor Magron, Tobias Metzlaff, Jie Wang

Abstract
Polynomial optimization problems are infinite-dimensional, nonconvex, NP-hard, and are often handled in practice with the moment-sums of squares hierarchy of semidefinite programming bounds. We consider problems where the objective function and constraint polynomials are invariant under the action of a finite group. The present paper simultaneously exploits group symmetry and term sparsity in order to reduce the computational cost of the hierarchy. We first exploit symmetry by writing the semidefinite matrices in a symmetry-adapted basis according to an isotypic decomposition. The matrices in such a basis are block diagonal. Secondly, we exploit term sparsity on each block to further reduce the optimization matrix variables. This is a non-trivial extension of the term sparsity-based hierarchy related to sign symmetry that was introduced by two of the authors. Our method is compared with existing techniques via benchmarks on quartics with dihedral, cyclic and symmetric group symmetry.

Introduction

Computational problems can be solved more efficiently
through the exploitation of algebraic or combinatorial structures
that emerge from the underlying data.
In the context of constrained polynomial optimization,
this article focuses on moment-sums of squares-based semidefinite
approximation methods.
We present a moment-sums of squares hierarchy – also called Lasserre hierarchy – of lower bounds that simultaneously exploits
group symmetry and term sparsity.
We prove convergence to the optimal value under the Archimedean hypothesis (
Theorems

3.5
and
3.6
) and
provide benchmarks to compare with existing methods in practice (
Section

4
).

Background.

The objective of polynomial optimization is to minimize
a given polynomial over a real algebraic or semialgebraic set.
The problem is nonconvex, infinite-dimensional, and NP-hard.
To solve it in practice, Lasserre has developed in
[
22
]

a hierarchy of semidefinite lower bounds that converges under mild assumptions
to the optimal value.
The technique involves, on the primal side, the reformulation
to a moment problem, and, on the dual side, certifying positivity via sums of squares (SOS).
The computational implementation results in a semidefinite program (SDP)
and comes from a choice of polynomial basis.
Originally, Lasserre formulated the approach in the standard monomial basis.
Advanced techniques, such as extraction of minimizers
via flat extension
[
25
]
or complex variables
[
17
,
46
]
,
are often formulated in this standard basis as well.
More on the Lasserre hierarchy can be found in
[
24
,
12
]
.

Throughout, we assume that the objective function and the constraint polynomials
are invariant under the linear action of a finite group.
Our strategy begins with the exploitation of this invariance
by reformulating the problem in a symmetry-adapted basis.
We strongly emphasize that this does not, at least not in theory,
change the numerical value of each bound in the Lasserre hierarchy.
More precisely, the SDP matrices in the underlying
moment-SOS relaxation are obtained by change of basis
(so that trace and positive semidefiniteness are stable)
and feature a block-diagonal structure.
This significantly benefits the time to solve the SDP
at the cost of a general preprocess of computing the basis and assembling the SDP.
This preprocess is independent of the objective function and
can therefore be applied to a class of problems that share the same
group symmetry, number of variables, and relaxation order.
Gatermann had already applied symmetry reduction for dynamical systems in
[
10
]
,
and, together with Parrilo, given a reduction scheme for SDPs with an indication of
how to apply this on the level of polynomial optimization
[
9
]
,
see also
[
4
,
41
]
.
This was later extended by Riener, Theobald, Andrén and Lasserre in
[
36
]

and to trigonometric optimization by one of the authors in
[
32
]
.

On top of that, we employ term sparsity exploitation.
If the polynomial is sparse in the symmetry-adapted basis,
the idea is to remove superfluous entries from the SDP matrices.
The pioneering papers on sparsity exploitation in polynomial optimization
[
21
,
23
]
deal with correlative sparsity,
which is concerned with links between the variables.
This technique is based on reindexing the matrices in the SDP
by considering subsets of the variables to obtain a hierarchy with quasi block-diagonal SDP matrices, each block having a size related to the cardinality of these subsets, see
[
42
]
.
These patterns usually arise in large scale industrial problems for
optimal power flow
[
18
]
,
roundoff error bound analysis
[
27
,
30
]
,
and volume approximation
[
40
]
.

In the present paper, we focus on term sparsity,
which is concerned with links between the monomials instead of the variables.
These patterns arise in problems with sign symmetry
[
26
]
, coordinate projection
[
33
]
, and cross-sparsity
[
43
]
, as well as sums of nonnegative circuit polynomials (SONC)
[
14
]
or signomials (SAGE)
[
3
]
.
The work
[
45
]
on term sparsity by two of the authors generalizes these concepts to a term sparsity exploiting hierarchy, where, at each step of the Lasserre hierarchy,
a sparsity graph is constructed.
The nodes of the graph are the monomial basis elements and the edges encapsulate their term sparsity pattern (tsp) in the original data.
Through consecutive support and maximal chordal extensions,
one obtains another level of the hierarchy,
which yields the same SDP block-structure as the one obtained after exploiting sign symmetry.
This level is obtained after finitely many steps at a lower cost.
Correlative and term sparsities can be successfully combined
[
44
]
.
We refer the interested reader to the recent book
[
29
]
dedicated to theoretical and practical aspects of both sparsity types.

Contributions.

The novelty of the present work is to replace the monomial nodes of the tsp graph with invariant and equivariant elements of the symmetry-adapted basis.
We show that this replacement preserves the achievement of the dense bound after finitely many steps in
Theorem

3.5
and the asymptotic convergence in
Theorem

3.6
when maximal chordal extensions are chosen.
Next to these theoretical results, we provide a quantitative comparison with the dense approach, the initial TSSOS reduction, and the pure symmetry reduction.
The algorithm is fully implemented and running in the Julia package

TSSOS
.
The essence of our algorithm is to

1.

compute a symmetry-adapted basis;

2.

construct a tsp graph in the symmetry-adapted basis with respect to the entire symmetry group;

3.

perform chordal + support extensions.

The approach works for any finite group and comes with convergence guarantees.
Note that step 1. is independent of the objective function
but depends only on the group action.

For the case of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
we can compare with
[
37
]
.
The cited work offers a complementary approach where the strategy is to

1.

construct a tsp graph with monomial orbits and edge orbits;

2.

perform a symmetric chordal extension;

3.

apply symmetry reduction to the so-obtained blocks of the tsp graph with respect to stabilizer subgroups.

Here, step 2. relies on making an orbit graph simplicial,
which is, according to the author of
[
37
]
, an expensive operation.
In our case, the most expensive operation is the computation of products of basis elements
and the evaluation under the Reynolds operator.
Furthermore,
[
37
]
does not guarantee convergence.

Structure of the article.

We first review the symmetry reduction scheme in
Section

2

and highlight the results on symmetric sums of squares from the literature.

Section

3
then introduces
the tsp graph for the symmetry-adapted basis and
contains our main theoretical results, accompanied by small examples.

We conclude our work with several instances of benchmarks in
Section

4
.
The first scenario is for minimizing the one-dimensional ring Ising quartic
with dihedral symmetry over a Laplacian ball.
In this case, all bounds are sharp at the lowest possible relaxation and sparsity order.
We use maximal chordal extensions to indicate the decrease in block sizes and numbers of constraints.
The second scenario is the minimization of a two-dimensional torus grid quartic
over a finite set defined by a single equality constraint.
Here, we require several levels of sparsity to achieve the optimal bound
but still beat previous block sizes.

In
Section

5
,
we provide concluding remarks and further investigation tracks.
In particular, we address the possibility of combining correlative sparsity with orbit space reduction as well as extending our framework to the analysis of equivariant dynamical systems.

2
Preliminary Background on Symmetric Sums of Squares

For given polynomials

f,g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]=:\mathbb{R}[x]
,
we consider the
polynomial optimization problem

f^{*}\coloneqq\min\{f(X)\,|\,X\in K\}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002ptK\coloneqq\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\forall\,1\leq k\leq\ell:g_{k}(X)\geq 0\},

(POP)

where
K
is assumed to be compact.

2.1
Moment Relaxation and SOS Reinforcement

A
sum of squares
(SOS) is a polynomial
q\in\mathbb{R}[x]

of the form
q=\sum_{t}q_{t}^{2}
with

q_{t}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
and
t
ranging over a finite index set.
The set of sums of squares of elements
in
\mathbb{R}[x]
is denoted by
\Sigma\,\mathbb{R}[x]^{2}
.
The
quadratic module
generated by the
constraint polynomials
g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}
is

\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})\coloneqq\left\{q_{0}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\ell}q_{k}\,g_{k}\,|\,q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{\ell}\in\Sigma\,\mathbb{R}[x]^{2}\right\}.

(1)

Furthermore,
\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
is called
Archimedean

if it contains an element
p\in\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})

such that the semialgebraic superset

\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,p(X)\geq 0\}

of
K
is compact.

A linear form
L:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
is said to have a
representing probability measure
\mu
on
K
if

L(p)=\int_{K}p(X)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(X)

for all
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
.
For the standard monomial basis
\{x^{\alpha}\,|\,\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
,
the quantities
y_{\alpha}\coloneqq L(x^{\alpha})=\int_{K}X^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(X)

are called
moments
.
For more information on the theory of positive polynomials, moments, and sums of squares,
the reader is referred to
[
31
,
12
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Putinar’s Positivstellensatz)
.

[
35
]

Assume that
\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
is Archimedean.

1.

A linear form
L:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
which is nonnegative on

\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
has a representing probability measure
\mu
on
K
.

2.

A polynomial
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
which is strictly positive on
K

is contained in
\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
.

This motivates the definition of the lower bounds

f^{*}\geq f_{\mathrm{mom}}\coloneqq\begin{array}[t]{rl}\inf&L(f)\\
\mbox{s.t.}&L\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{*},\,L(1)=1,\\
&\forall\,p\in\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g}):L(p)\geq 0\end{array}\geq f_{\mathrm{sos}}\coloneqq\begin{array}[t]{rl}\sup&\lambda\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\\
&f-\lambda\in\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})\end{array}

(2)

and, if
\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
is Archimedean, then
Theorem

2.1
implies
f_{\mathrm{mom}}=f_{\mathrm{sos}}=f^{*}
.
Note that
\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
being Archimedean implies that
the feasible region
K
is compact.
Conversely, if
K
is compact, one may add

g_{\ell+1}=R^{2}-x_{1}^{2}-\ldots-x_{n}^{2}
for a radius
R\in\mathbb{R}

sufficiently large to the list of constraints
in order to enforce the Archimedean property
without changing the resulting semialgebraic set
K
.

2.2
Linear Representation Theory

Throughout, let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a finite matrix group.
The following results on the representation theory of finite groups can be found in
[
38
,
8
]
.
For further computational interests, we refer to
[
7
,
39
]
.
Although there are no novel contributions in this section,
we still prove some of the presented statements in
Appendix

A
using our own notation.

The group
\mathcal{G}
has an induced action on polynomials
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
by

p^{\sigma}(X)\coloneqq p(\sigma^{-1}\cdot X)
,
where
\cdot
denotes the matrix-vector multiplication.

Definition 2.2
.

A polynomial
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is called
\mathcal{G}
-invariant

if
p^{\sigma}=p
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
.
The set of all invariant polynomials is a

\mathbb{R}
-algebra denoted
\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
and
the
Reynolds operator
is

\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}},\,p\mapsto\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{G}}p^{\sigma}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an arbitrary field.
A
\mathcal{G}
-module

W
is a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space together
with a group homomorphism
\rho_{W}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{GL}(W)
,
called
representation
.
A
\mathcal{G}
-module
W
is called
irreducible

if its only
\mathcal{G}
-submodules are
0
and
W
itself.
A
\mathcal{G}
-module homomorphism
between two

\mathcal{G}
-modules
W,\tilde{W}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-vector space homomorphism

\phi:W\to\tilde{W}
with
\phi\circ\rho_{W}(\sigma)=\rho_{\tilde{W}}(\sigma)\circ\phi

for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
.
The
\mathbb{K}
-vector space of
\mathcal{G}
-module homomorphisms
is denoted by
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(W,\tilde{W})
.
Two
\mathcal{G}
-modules are called
isomorphic

if there exists a bijective
\mathcal{G}
-module homomorphism between them.

Even though our original polynomials
f,g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}
for
POP
and
the group
\mathcal{G}
itself are defined over the real numbers
\mathbb{R}
,
all representations will formally be defined
over the complex numbers
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
,
because we rely on Maschke’s Theorem (
Appendix

A
),
which requires algebraically closed fields.

The induced action extends naturally to
\mathbb{C}[x]
,
making it a
\mathcal{G}
-module.
Since the polynomial degree is preserved,
homogeneous polynomials remain homogeneous under the action.
By Maschke’s Theorem,
the polynomial ring over
\mathbb{C}
has an
isotypic decomposition

\mathbb{R}[x]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[x]=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{h}W^{(i)}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002ptW^{(i)}=\bigoplus\limits_{j\in J^{(i)}}W^{(i)}_{j},

(3)

where, for fixed
i
, the
W^{(i)}_{j}
are pairwise isomorphic complex irreducible

\mathcal{G}
-modules of
\mathbb{C}
-dimension
d^{(i)}\in\mathbb{N}

indexed by a set
J^{(i)}
, see
[
38
, Ch.2, Thm.4]
.
Moreover,
h
is the number of non-isomorphic complex irreducible representations of
\mathcal{G}
and equals the number of conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{G}
.

A vector space basis that admits the above
decomposition can be constructed as follows,
although this is by no means canonical.
Only the parameters
h
and
d^{(i)}

are intrinsic to the group
\mathcal{G}
.

Proposition 2.4

(Symmetry-Adapted Basis)
.

[
38
, Ch. 2, Prop. 8]

Let
\{\vartheta^{(i)}\,|\,1\leq i\leq h\}

with
\vartheta^{(i)}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{GL}_{d^{(i)}}(\mathbb{C})

be a family of pairwise nonisomorphic
irreducible matrix representations of
\mathcal{G}

and fix some
1\leq i\leq h
.
For
1\leq u\leq d^{(i)}
, define the operator

\mathbf{P}^{(i)}_{u}:\mathbb{C}[x]\to\mathbb{C}[x],p\mapsto\frac{d^{(i)}}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{G}}\left(\vartheta^{(i)}(\sigma^{-1})\right)_{1,u}\,p^{\sigma}.

Let
\left\{w^{(i)}_{1,j}\,|\,j\in J^{(i)}\right\}

be a homogeneous basis for the image of

\mathbf{P}^{(i)}_{1}
.
Then the set

\left\{w^{(i)}_{1,j},\,w^{(i)}_{2,j}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}^{(i)}_{2}(w^{(i)}_{1,j}),\,w^{(i)}_{3,j}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}^{(i)}_{3}(w^{(i)}_{1,j}),\,\ldots,\,w^{(i)}_{d^{(i)},j}\coloneqq\mathbf{P}^{(i)}_{d^{(i)}}(w^{(i)}_{1,j})\right\}

is a homogeneous vector space basis for the
irreducible
\mathcal{G}
-module
W^{(i)}_{j}
.

The submodules
W^{(i)}_{j}
are complex in general,
but can be decomposed into a real and imaginary part,
see
[
36
, Eq. 2.9]
.
In particular, one can always choose real basis elements
w^{(i)}_{1,j}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
.

Definition 2.5
.

A linear form
L\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{*}
is called
\mathcal{G}
-invariant

if
L(p^{\sigma})=L(p)
for all
p\in\mathbb{R}[x],\,\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
.
A bilinear form
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}[x]\times\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
is called
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant
if
\mathcal{L}(p^{\sigma},q^{\sigma})=\mathcal{L}(p,q)
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{R}[x],\,\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
.

Note that a
\mathcal{G}
-invariant linear form is
uniquely determined by its values on
\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
.
The vector space of
\mathcal{G}
-invariant linear forms
is therefore isomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}})^{*}
.
Using Schur’s Lemma (
Appendix

A
), one can show the following.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}[x]\times\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
be a

\mathcal{G}
-equivariant bilinear form as well as

1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq h
and
j,j^{\prime}\in J^{(i)}
.
There exist scalars
c^{(i)}_{j,j^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}
such that,
for all
1\leq u,u^{\prime}\leq d^{(i)}
, we have

\mathcal{L}(w^{(i)}_{u,j},w^{(i^{\prime})}_{u^{\prime},j^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{ if }i\neq i^{\prime}\\
0,&\mbox{ if }u\neq u^{\prime}\\
c^{(i)}_{j,j^{\prime}},&\mbox{ otherwise}\\
\end{cases},

where
w^{(i)}_{u,j}
are defined as in
Proposition

2.4
.

In particular, a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant bilinear form
is determined by its values on the blocks

W^{(i)}_{1}\times W^{(i)}_{1}
with
1\leq i\leq h
.
For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
\mathbb{R}[x]_{r}
the set of polynomials of degree at most
r
.

Corollary 2.7

(Block Diagonalization)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq i\leq h
and
denote by
1,2,3,\ldots,m^{(i)}_{r}

the indices
j\in J^{(i)}
with

\deg(w^{(i)}_{1,j})\leq r
.
In the ordered basis

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{h}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{m^{(i)}_{r}}\{w^{(i)}_{u,j}\,|\,1\leq u\leq d^{(i)}\},

the matrix of a group element
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
acting on
\mathbb{R}[x]_{r}
has
h
blocks,
each consisting of
m^{(i)}_{r}
further equal
d^{(i)}\times d^{(i)}
blocks.
In particular, we obtain a block diagonal matrix representation

\vartheta\coloneqq m^{(1)}_{r}\vartheta^{(1)}\oplus\ldots\oplus m^{(h)}_{r}\vartheta^{(h)}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{R}[x]_{r})
of dimension
\dim(\mathbb{R}[x]_{r})=\binom{n+r}{r}
,
where
m^{(i)}_{r}
’s are the multiplicities of irreducible representations.

On the other hand, if the basis is reordered to

\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{h}\bigcup\limits_{u=1}^{d^{(i)}}\{w^{(i)}_{u,j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq m^{(i)}_{r}\},

the matrix of a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant bilinear form

\mathcal{L}:\mathbb{R}[x]_{r}\times\mathbb{R}[x]_{r}\to\mathbb{R}
has
h
blocks,
each consisting of
d^{(i)}
further equal

m^{(i)}_{r}\times m^{(i)}_{r}
blocks.

2.3
Symmetry Reduction via Symmetry-Adapted Bases

We assume that
POP
is
\mathcal{G}
-symmetric,
that is,
f,g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
.
Based on the symmetry exploiting variant of
Theorem

2.1
, we recall how to compute the minimum
f^{*}

efficiently.
We call

\mathcal{S}^{(i)}\coloneqq\{w^{(i)}_{1,j}\,|\,j\in J^{(i)}\}

a
symmetry-adapted basis

for the
i
-th isotypic component.
A
\mathcal{G}
-invariant linear form

L:\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
admits a

\mathcal{G}
-equivariant bilinear form

\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{G}}_{L}:\mathbb{R}[x]\times\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R},(p,q)\mapsto L(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(p\,q)),

(4)

which is determined by its values on the

\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
.
Furthermore,

g_{k}*L:\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R},p\mapsto L(g_{k}\,p)

(5)

is also
\mathcal{G}
-invariant and
admits a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant bilinear form

\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{G}}_{g_{k}*L}:\mathbb{R}[x]\times\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 2.8

(Symmetric Positivstellensatz)
.

[
36
, Thm. 3.5]

Assume that
\mathrm{QM}(\mathbf{g})
is Archimedean.

1.

A
\mathcal{G}
-invariant linear form
L:\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathbb{R}
has a representing
\mathcal{G}
-invariant probability measure on
K
if and only if
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{G}}_{g_{k}*L}
is positive semidefinite on
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
for all
0\leq k\leq\ell,\,1\leq i\leq h
.

2.

A
\mathcal{G}
-invariant polynomial
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}

which is strictly positive on
K

can be written as

p=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{h}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}g_{k}\,\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(q^{(i)}_{k}),

(6)

where
q^{(i)}_{k}\in\Sigma(\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{S}^{(i)})^{2}
are sums of squares
of elements in the vector space generated by
\mathcal{S}^{(i)}
.

Set
g_{0}\coloneqq 1
,
d_{k}\coloneqq\lceil\deg(g_{k})/2\rceil
and

r_{\min}\coloneqq\max\{\lceil\deg(f)/2\rceil,d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}\}.

For
r\geq r_{\min}
,
the restriction of
L
to
\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}_{2r}
admits bilinear forms

\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{G}}_{g_{k}*L}:\,\mathbb{R}[x]_{r-d_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}[x]_{r-d_{k}}\to\mathbb{R},

(7)

which are determined by their values on

\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}\coloneqq\{w^{(i)}_{1,j}\,|\,1\leq j\leq m^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}\}
.
The
truncated localized symmetry-adapted moment matrices

are therefore the block diagonal matrices

\mathbf{M}^{\mathcal{G}}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*L)\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\boxed{\mathbf{M}^{(1)}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*L)}&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\boxed{\mathbf{M}^{(h)}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*L)}\end{pmatrix}

(8)

with entries

(\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*L))_{j,j^{\prime}}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{G}}_{g_{k}*L}(w^{(i)}_{1,j},w^{(i)}_{1,j^{\prime}}),\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq m^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}.

(9)

The (primal)
symmetry-adapted moment relaxation of order
r
is

f^{*}\geq f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}\coloneqq\begin{array}[t]{rl}\inf&L(f)\\
\mbox{s.t.}&L\in(\mathbb{R}[x]_{2r}^{\mathcal{G}})^{*},L(1)=1,\\
&\forall\,0\leq k\leq\ell,1\leq i\leq h:\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r}(g_{k}*L)\succeq 0\end{array}

(10)

and the (dual)
symmetry-adapted SOS reinforcement of order
r
is

f^{*}\geq f^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}}\coloneqq\begin{array}[t]{rl}\sup&\lambda\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\lambda\in\mathbb{R},q^{(i)}_{k}\in\Sigma(\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}})^{2},\\
&f-\lambda=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{h}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(q^{(i)}_{k}\,g_{k}).\end{array}

(11)

Theorem 2.9
.

[
36
, Thm. 3.4]

For
r\geq r_{\min}
,
the sequences
(f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
and

(f^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
are monotonically nondecreasing
with
f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}\geq f^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}}
.

is Archimedean,

After reordering, we may assume without loss of generality
that the first irreducible
\mathcal{G}
-modules

in the isotypic decomposition
afford the trivial representation with
\vartheta^{(1)}(s)=1

of dimension

In particular,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbf{P}^{(1)}_{1}:\mathbb{R}[x]\to\mathbb{R}[x],p\mapsto\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{G}}p^{\sigma}

(12)

is the Reynolds operator with image
\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
and the set

\mathcal{S}^{(1)}_{2r}=\{w^{(1)}_{1,1},\ldots,w^{(1)}_{1,m^{(1)}_{2r}}\}

is a basis for the vector space
\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}_{2r}
of

\mathcal{G}
-invariant polynomials of degree at most

\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m^{(1)}_{2r}}

Denote by

Corollary 2.10
.

The symmetry-adapted moment-SOS hierarchy of order
r

can be written as an SDP:

f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}=\begin{array}[t]{rl}\inf&\sum_{j}f_{j}\,y_{j}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&y_{1}=1,\forall\,k,i:\\
&\sum_{j}\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\,y_{j}\succeq 0\end{array}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptf^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}}=\begin{array}[t]{rl}\sup&f_{1}-\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,1}\,\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}\succeq 0,\forall\,j\geq 2:\\
&f_{j}=\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\,\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}),\end{array}

where the infimum is taken over finite sequences

\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m^{(1)}_{2r}}
and
the supremum is taken over positive semidefinite matrices

\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}

Remark 2.11

If
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}

3.1
Chordal Graphs and Sparse Matrices

Let
\mathrm{G}
be an undirected graph with vertices
V
and edges

.
Then
\mathrm{G}
is called a
chordal graph
if every cycle of
\mathrm{G}
has a chord, that is,
an edge that joins two nonconsecutive vertices.
A
chordal extension
of
\mathrm{G}
is a chordal graph
that contains
\mathrm{G}
as a subgraph. Chordal extensions are not unique. A
maximal
chordal extension is the chordal extension that completes every connected component of
G
.
A
clique
of
\mathrm{G}
is a subset of
V
that admits a complete subgraph,
complete meaning that any two vertices are connected.
A
maximal clique
of
\mathrm{G}
is a clique of
\mathrm{G}
that is not contained in another one. A chordal extension with the smallest clique number (i.e. the maximal size of maximal cliques) is called a
smallest
chordal extension.

Definition 3.1
.

A
binary matrix

\mathbf{B}
is a matrix with entries
0
and
1
.

•

The support
\hbox{\rm{supp}}(\mathbf{B})
is the set of all
(j,j^{\prime})
with
\mathbf{B}_{j,j^{\prime}}=1
.

•

If
\mathbf{B}
is square,

\mathrm{Sym}(\mathbf{B})
denotes the vector space of
real symmetric matrices
\mathbf{Q}
of the same size
with
\mathbf{Q}_{j,j^{\prime}}=0
if
\mathbf{B}_{j,j^{\prime}}=0
.

•

The
block closure
of
\mathbf{B}
is a
binary matrix
\overline{\mathbf{B}}
of the same size,
which is minimal with respect to the properties

1.

\hbox{\rm{supp}}(\mathbf{B})\subseteq\hbox{\rm{supp}}(\overline{\mathbf{B}})
and

2.

if
\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{j,j^{\prime}}=\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{j^{\prime},j^{\prime\prime}}=1
,
then
\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{j,j^{\prime\prime}}=1
.

\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,s,k,j}

\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,s,k}\circ\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}

\hbox{\rm{supp}}(\tilde{\mathbf{B}}^{(i)}_{r,s,k})\subseteq\hbox{\rm{supp}}(\tilde{\mathbf{B}}^{(i)}_{r,s+1,k})

the sequence of binary matrices

(\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,s,k})_{s\geq 1}

\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,*,k}

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that the block closure in
Definition

3.3
is maximal.
For fixed
r\geq r_{\min}
, the sequences

(f^{r,s}_{\mathrm{mom}})_{s\geq 1}
and

(f^{r,s}_{\mathrm{sos}})_{s\geq 1}
are
monotonously nondecreasing with

f^{r,*}_{\mathrm{mom}}=f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}
and

f^{r,*}_{\mathrm{sos}}=f^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}}
.

Proof.

Monotonicity and
f^{r,*}_{\mathrm{mom}}\leq f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}
are clear from the construction.
Let

\mathcal{B}_{r,*}\coloneqq\bigcup\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{h}\bigcup\limits_{(\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,*,k})_{j,j^{\prime}}=1}\hbox{\rm{supp}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(w^{(i)}_{1,j}\,w^{(i)}_{1,j^{\prime}}\,g_{k})\right).

Assume that
\mathbf{y}=(y_{j})_{j\in\mathcal{B}_{r,*}}

is feasible for
f^{r,*}_{\mathrm{mom}}
and set

\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{j}\coloneqq\begin{cases}y_{j},&j\in\mathcal{B}_{r,*},\\
0,&j\in\{1,\ldots,m^{(1)}_{2r}\}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{r,*}.\end{cases}

Since
\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{*,k}
is stable under maximal block closure,
we have

\hbox{\rm{supp}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(w^{(i)}_{1,j}\,w^{(i)}_{1,j^{\prime}}\,g_{k})\right)\cap\mathcal{B}_{r,*}=\emptyset

whenever
(\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,*,k})_{j,j^{\prime}}=0
and thus

\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r}(g_{k}*\overline{\mathbf{y}})=\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,*,k}\circ\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r}(g_{k}*\mathbf{y})

Hence,
\mathbf{\overline{y}}
is feasible for

with

\sum_{j}f_{j}\,y_{j}=\sum_{j}f_{j}\,\mathbf{\overline{y}}_{j}\geq f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}

Since
\mathbf{y}
was arbitrary, we also have
f^{r,*}_{\mathrm{mom}}\geq f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}

Dualizing yields the same for

(\mathbf{{C}}^{(i)}_{r,s+1,k})_{j,j^{\prime}}=1
implies

(\mathbf{B}^{(i)}_{r,s+1,k})_{j,j^{\prime}}=1
.

\Box

Remark 3.7
.

Assume that
K
has nonempty interior.
For fixed
r\geq r_{\min}
and
s\geq 1
,
there is no duality gap, that is,

f^{r,s}_{\mathrm{mom}}-f^{r,s}_{\mathrm{sos}}=0
.
This can be proven analogously to
[

, Thm. 4.2]
.
Similarly, if there is an explicit ball constraint among the
g_{k}
,
then the duality gap also vanishes; see
[
16
]
.

4.1
1-D Ring Ising Quartic with Dihedral Symmetry

The dihedral group
\mathfrak{D}_{2n}
of order
2n

is generated by a cycle
\rho
of length
n
and a reflection
\sigma
,
whose action on the indeterminates
x_{i},\,1\leq i\leq n,
is defined by

\rho(x_{i})=x_{i+1}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\sigma(x_{i})=x_{-i}\hskip 10.00002pt(\mbox{indices mod }n).

(31)

For parameters
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the quartic

f\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a\,x_{i}^{4}+b\,x_{i}^{2}\,x_{i+1}^{2}+c\,x_{i}\,x_{i+1}+d\,x_{i}^{2}

(32)

over the Laplacian ball
K\coloneqq\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,g(X)\geq 0\}

with
g\coloneqq n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{i+1})^{2}
.

4.2
2-D Torus Grid Quartic with Product Cyclic Symmetry

The product of cyclic groups
\mathfrak{C}_{p}\times\mathfrak{C}_{q}

of order
n\coloneqq pq
is generated by commuting cycles
\rho_{1}
of length
p

and
\rho_{2}
of length
q
,
whose action on the indeterminates

x_{i,j},\,1\leq i\leq p,\,1\leq j\leq q,
is defined by

\rho_{1}(x_{i,j})=x_{i+1,j}\hskip 10.00002pt(\mbox{mod }p)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\rho_{2}(x_{i,j})=x_{i,j+1}\hskip 10.00002pt(\mbox{mod }q).

(33)

For parameters
a,b,c,d
, consider the quartic

f\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{q}a\,x_{i,j}^{4}+b\,(x_{i,j}^{2}\,x_{i+1,j}^{2}+x_{i,j}^{2}\,x_{i,j+1}^{2})+c\,(x_{i,j}\,x_{i+1,j}+x_{i,j}\,x_{i,j+1})+d\,x_{i,j}^{2}

(34)

over
K\coloneqq\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times q}\,|\,h(X)=0\}

with
h\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{i,j}^{2}-1)^{2}
,
that is,
K=\{\pm 1\}^{p\times q}
.

4.3
Symmetric Quartic

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of order
n!
is generated by the transpositions
(i,i+1)
,
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and acts by permutation of variables
x_{i}
.
The quartic

f\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{4}+\frac{1}{\binom{n}{4}}\sum\limits_{i<j<k<l}x_{i}\,x_{j}\,x_{k}\,x_{l}+\frac{1}{\binom{n}{3}}\sum\limits_{i<j<k}x_{i}\,x_{j}\,x_{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}

(35)

is
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-invariant; see
[
37
]
.

5
Concluding Remarks and Further Investigation Tracks

We have designed a Lasserre-type moment-sums of squares hierarchy
Equation

30
of lower bounds for the polynomial optimization problem
POP
with invariant objective function and constraints.
The hierarchy is a generalization of the initial TSSOS hierarchy
[
45
]
in the sense that we construct a tsp graph; see
Definition

3.3
, but with the novelty that the nodes are elements of a symmetry-adapted basis instead of the standard monomial one.
In
Theorems

3.5
and
3.6
,
we prove convergence under the maximal chordal extension assumption and Archimedeanity of the quadratic module.

Our benchmarks in
Section

4
indicate that this new method is superior to both pure term sparsity and pure symmetry exploitation; see
Figures

1
,
2
, and
3
.
The compared parameters are SDP block sizes, numbers of constraints, and computational time.
The latter strongly depends on the implementation.
The computation of a symmetry-adapted basis is independent of the objective function and constraints.
For large groups (such as the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
n\geq 10
), the package
SymbolicWedderburn.jl
used to compute symmetry-adapted bases can cause wrong rank estimates in the projection matrices.

Computation of the block structure of and assembling the SDP also depend on the implementation and can improve the computation time further.
The key step here is to compute the products of symmetry-adapted basis elements under the Reynolds operator.

Finally, we conclude with some remarks on variations of the problem and future research directions.

5.1
Constraints forming an Orbit

Throughout

Sections

2
,
3
, and
4
,
we have assumed
g_{k}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
.
When we assume instead that the constraints
g_{k}
form an orbit,
then the feasible region
K
is still
\mathcal{G}
-stable.
Indeed,
POP
can be rewritten as

f^{*}\coloneqq\min\{f(X)\,|\,X\in K\}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002ptK\coloneqq\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,|\,\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{G}:g^{\sigma}(X)\geq 0\},

(POP2)

where
g\in\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\}

of degree
d\coloneqq d_{1}=\cdots=d_{\ell}

can be chosen freely among the constraints.
The assumption
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
remains unchanged.

For a
\mathcal{G}
-invariant linear form
L
,
the truncated moment matrix
\mathbf{M}^{\mathcal{G}}_{r}(L)

is still block diagonal with respect to a

\mathcal{G}
-symmetry-adapted basis,
but the same does not necessarily hold for the localizing matrices

\mathbf{M}^{\mathcal{G}}_{r-d}(g^{\sigma}*L)
.
Instead, we consider the stabilizer subgroup

\mathcal{G}_{k}\coloneqq\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathcal{G}}(g_{k})\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathcal{G}\,|\,g_{k}^{\sigma}=g_{k}\}
.
Since the orbits
g_{k}^{\mathcal{G}}
are equal,
the
\mathcal{G}_{k}
are conjugate subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
.
In particular, the respective isotypic decompositions
feature the same number of irreducible representations,
dimensions, and multiplicities.
Fix a
1\leq k\leq\ell
and let

\mathbb{R}[x]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[x]=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{\tilde{h}}\bigoplus\limits_{j\in\tilde{J}^{(i)}}\tilde{W}^{(i)}_{j}

(37)

be an isotypic decomposition with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{k}

and denote by
\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}_{k}}
the Reynolds operator.
Analogously to
Proposition

2.4
,
we can compute a
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-symmetry-adapted
basis for
\mathbb{R}[x]_{2(r-d)}

with elements denoted by
\tilde{w}^{(i)}_{1,j}
.
Note that any
\mathcal{G}
-invariant linear form
L

is in particular
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-invariant and thus
the localized
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
-symmetry-adapted
moment matrix is now block diagonal
as in
Equation

8
.
The entries, indexed by

1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq\tilde{m}^{(i)}_{r-d}
, are

(\tilde{\mathbf{M}}^{(i)}_{r-d}(g_{k}*L))_{j,j^{\prime}}=L\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{k}|}\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\mathcal{G}_{k}}(g_{k}\,\tilde{w}^{(i)}_{1,j}\,\tilde{w}^{(i)}_{1,j^{\prime}})^{\sigma}\right)

(38)

and can be expressed in terms of the
\mathcal{G}
-moments
\mathbf{y}
of
L
.

5.2
Sparsity after Orbit Space Reduction

To compute the global minimum of an invariant polynomial
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
, our symmetry reduction method relies on the fact that the polynomial ring has an isotypic decomposition and the moment matrix is block diagonal in a symmetry-adapted basis.

Alternatively, Hilbert’s Finiteness Theorem states that the invariant ring

\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
is finitely generated as an
\mathbb{R}
-algebra
[
13
]
,
that is, there is a
set of fundamental invariants

\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
with the following property:
For any
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
, there exists some
m
-variate polynomial

g\in\mathbb{R}[{z}_{1},\ldots,{z}_{m}]\eqqcolon\mathbb{R}[z]
such that
f=g(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
.
Denote by
p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m},\,X\mapsto(p_{1}(X),\ldots,p_{m}(X))
the
Hilbert map
.
The image
p(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called the
orbit space of
\mathcal{G}
.

There are various methods to compute fundamental invariants, for example,
Molien’s Formula (
[
10
, Sec. 2.1]
,
[
39
, Alg. 2.2.5]
),
primary and secondary invariants (
[
39
, Sec. 2.5]
,
[
5
, Sec. 3.5–7]
), or
Derksen’s
[
6
]
or King’s
[
20
]
algorithm.

Theorem 5.1

(Procesi & Schwarz
[
34
]
)
.

There exists a matrix polynomial
{\mathbf{P}}\in\mathbb{R}[{z}]^{m\times m}
with the following property:
For
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we have
Z\in p(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\mathbf{P}(Z)\succeq 0
.

In particular, the unconstrained polynomial optimization problem

POP
with
K=\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be rewritten as

f^{*}=\min\{f(X)\,|\,X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}=\min\{g(Z)\,|\,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\,\mathbf{P}(Z)\succeq 0\}.

(POP3)

A promising investigation track would be to leverage the recent work
[
11
]
that addresses sparsity exploitation for polynomial optimization problems with polynomial matrix constraints such as
POP3
.

5.3
Equivariant Dynamical Systems

Finally, we explore the idea of exploiting both symmetry and term sparsity for computing a maximal positive invariant (MPI) set of a dynamical system.
Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
be a finite group
and
f={({f}_{1},\ldots,{f}_{n})}^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{n}

be a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant
polynomial vector field, that is,

\sigma^{-1}\cdot f=f\circ\sigma^{-1}=({f}_{1}^{\sigma},\ldots,{f}_{n}^{\sigma})^{T}\eqqcolon f^{\sigma}\mbox{ whenever }\sigma\in\mathcal{G}.

(39)

Here, the left-hand side is given by matrix-vector multiplication and
the right-hand side by the induced action of
\mathcal{G}
on the polynomial entries.
For
X:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},\,t\mapsto{(X_{1}(t),\ldots,X_{n}(t))}^{\mathrm{T}}
differentiable
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}
,
consider the
equivariant dynamical system

\dot{X}(t)=f(X(t)),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{s.t.}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,1\leq k\leq\ell:\,g_{k}(X(t))\geq 0.

(40)

The symmetry-adapted moment-SOS approximation for the

maximum positively invariant
(MPI) set
of order
r
with discount factor
\beta>0
is

\begin{array}[t]{rl}\sup&y_{1}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&\mathbf{y},\tilde{\mathbf{y}},\hat{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m^{(1)}_{2r}},\,\forall\,j:\\
&y_{j}=z_{j}-\tilde{y}_{j}\\
&y_{j}=\beta\,\hat{y}_{j}-\sum_{j^{\prime}}f_{j,j^{\prime}}\,\hat{y}_{j^{\prime}},\\
&\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*\mathbf{y})\succeq 0,\\
&\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*\tilde{\mathbf{y}})\succeq 0,\\
&\mathbf{M}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}(g_{k}*\hat{\mathbf{y}})\succeq 0\end{array}\,\mbox{and}\,\,\begin{array}[t]{rl}\inf&\sum_{j}h_{j}\,z_{j}\\
\mbox{s.t.}&h\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}_{2r},\,v\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{\mathcal{G}}_{2r+1-d_{f}},\\
&q^{(i)}_{k},\tilde{q}^{(i)}_{k},\hat{q}^{(i)}_{k}\in\Sigma(\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}})^{2},\\
&\begin{array}[t]{r}h=\sum_{j}h_{j}\,w^{(1)}_{j}=\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(q^{(i)}_{k}\,g_{k}),\\
h-v-1=\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(\tilde{q}^{(i)}_{k}\,g_{k}),\\
\beta v-{\nabla v}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot f=\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(\hat{q}^{(i)}_{k}\,g_{k}),\end{array}\end{array}

(41)

where
f_{j,j^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}
are certain coefficients of a term defined in
Lemma

B.1
and
z_{j}
are the
\mathcal{G}
-moments of the Lebesgue measure;
see also
[
47
]
for the non-symmetric version.
Analogously to
Definition

3.3
,
one can then construct a tsp graph for the MPI approximation and
exploit term sparsity in the symmetry-adapted basis.
Future research efforts include a practical implementation of these latter hierarchies on concrete dynamical system applications.

Proof of
Corollary

2.10
.

For
f^{r}_{\mathrm{mom}}
, this is clear.
If
\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}
is feasible for the SDP,
it is in particular orthogonally diagonalizable with nonnegative eigenvalues.
Denote by
\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1}
the vector of basis elements
w^{(i)}_{1,j}
with

1\leq j\leq m^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}
.
Then

q^{(i)}_{k}\coloneqq{(\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1})}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1}

is a sum of squares of elements in
\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}
with

\displaystyle\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(q^{(i)}_{k}\,g_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}({(\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1})}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1}\,g_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,i}\trace(\underbrace{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1}\cdot{(\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1})}^{\mathrm{T}}\,g_{k})}_{=\sum_{j}\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\,w^{(1)}_{1,j}}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})\,w^{(1)}_{1,j}

\displaystyle=f_{1}-(\underbrace{f_{1}-\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})}_{\eqqcolon\lambda})+\sum_{j\geq 2}\underbrace{\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})}_{=f_{j}}\,w^{(1)}_{1,j}

\displaystyle=f-\lambda.

Hence,
\lambda
and the
q^{(i)}_{k}
are feasible for
f^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}}
.
Conversely, let
\lambda
be feasible for
f^{r}_{\mathrm{sos}}
with sums of squares

q^{(i)}_{k}=\sum_{t}(q^{(i)}_{k,t})^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{such that}\hskip 10.00002ptf-\lambda=\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(q^{(i)}_{k}\,g_{k}).

For
q\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{S}^{(i)}_{r-d_{k}}
,
denote by
\mathbf{vec}(q)
the coefficient vector.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{j}f_{j}\,w^{(1)}_{1,j}-\lambda=f-\lambda

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,i}\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}\left(\sum_{t}{(\mathbf{vec}(q^{(i)}_{k,t})}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1})^{2}\,g_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,i}\sum_{t}{\mathbf{vec}(q^{(i)}_{k,t})}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\underbrace{\mathcal{R}^{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1}\cdot{(\mathbf{w}^{(i)}_{1})}^{\mathrm{T}}\,g_{k})}_{=\sum_{j}A^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\,w^{(1)}_{1,j}}\cdot\mathbf{vec}(q^{(i)}_{k,t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,i}\trace\left(\resizebox{0}{720896}{$\sum\limits_{k,i}$}\right.\sum_{j}(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\,w^{(1)}_{1,j})\cdot\underbrace{\sum_{t}\mathbf{vec}(q^{(i)}_{k,t})\cdot{(\mathbf{vec}(q^{(i)}_{k,t}))}^{\mathrm{T}}}_{\eqqcolon\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k}}\left.\resizebox{0}{720896}{$\sum\limits_{k,i}$}\right).

Comparing coefficients yields

f_{1}-\lambda=\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,1}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})
and

f_{j}=\sum_{k,i}\trace(\mathbf{A}^{(i)}_{r,k,j}\cdot\mathbf{Q}^{(i)}_{k})
for

2\leq j\leq m^{(1)}_{2r}
.
This completes the proof.

Appendix B
Equivariants

Lemma B.1
.

If
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant,
then the formal gradient

{\nabla p=(\frac{\partial p}{\partial X_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial p}{\partial X_{n}})}^{\mathrm{T}}

is
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant.
Furthermore, if
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}[x]^{n}
are

\mathcal{G}
-equivariant and
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

satisfies
\mathbf{S}={\sigma}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\sigma

for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
, then

{\mathbf{f}}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}[x]

is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{G}
.
By
\mathcal{G}
-invariance and the chain rule, we have

(\nabla p)(x)=(\nabla(p\circ\sigma))(x)=\sigma\cdot(\nabla p)(\sigma(x))

and thus
\sigma^{-1}\cdot\nabla p=(\nabla p)^{\sigma}
.
Furthermore, by linearity and
\mathcal{G}
-equivariance, we have

({\mathbf{f}}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{g})^{\sigma}={\mathbf{f}}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot{(\sigma^{-1})}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\sigma^{-1}\cdot\mathbf{g}={\mathbf{f}}^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{g}.

\Box

.

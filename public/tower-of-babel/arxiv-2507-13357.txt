Adaptive Linguistic Prompting (ALP) Enhances Phishing Webpage Detection in Multimodal Large Language Models Thanks: Senior Author
Atharva Bhargude, Ishan Gonehal, Chandler Haney, Dave Yoon, Aaron Sandoval, Sean O’Brien, Kaustubh Vinnakota

Abstract
Phishing attacks represent a significant cybersecurity threat, necessitating adaptive detection techniques. This study explores few-shot Adaptive Linguistic Prompting (ALP) in detecting phishing webpages through the multimodal capabilities of state-of-the-art large language models (LLMs) such as GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro. ALP is a structured semantic reasoning method that guides LLMs to analyze textual deception by breaking down linguistic patterns, detecting urgency cues, and identifying manipulative diction commonly found in phishing content. By integrating textual, visual, and URL-based analysis, we propose a unified model capable of identifying sophisticated phishing attempts. Our experiments demonstrate that ALP significantly enhances phishing detection accuracy by guiding LLMs through structured reasoning and contextual analysis. The findings highlight the potential of ALP-integrated multimodal LLMs to advance phishing detection frameworks, achieving an F1-score of 0.93—surpassing traditional approaches. These results establish a foundation for more robust, interpretable, and adaptive linguistic-based phishing detection systems using LLMs.

3.2
Few-Shot ALP Prompting Methodology

This paper introduces a novel prompting methodology that integrates few-shot Adaptive Linguistic Prompting to enhance phishing detection using large language models (LLMs) for the analysis of multimodal data, including text, images, and URLs. The proposed approach leverages few-shot ALP prompting to enable step-by-step linguistic analysis among a given number of training examples. This methodology is particularly advantageous for phishing detection, as it facilitates decision-making based on highly trained semantic logical inference rather than relying solely on heuristic surface-level pattern matching.

Two distinct analyses were implemented to leverage the multi-modal capabilities of GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro for phishing detection. Both analyses utilized the curated dataset, ensuring consistent input modalities. Prompting incorporates few-shot examples, providing the model with relevant demonstrations of phishing and benign classification.

The first analysis, Multimodal Webpage Analysis (MWA), utilizes multi-modal capabilities by analyzing both the HTML textual content and the screenshot data of a given webpage. The diagram (Figure 1) illustrates the flow of MWA prompting, wherein HTML and screenshot data are extracted and processed through an 8-shot ALP reasoning prompt. This approach ensures that the language model effectively identifies phishing characteristics by breaking down the analysis into key adaptable components: Brand recognition, diction and syntax, and screenshot-specific.

3.3
Combining Analysis Results

To integrate insights from both analyses, a simple function combined the outputs of MWA and USA to produce the final classification as such:

If both analyses agree on the classification, the label is accepted as final. The two cases of disagreement were handled as follows: If MWA labeled the entry as phishing and USA as benign, the decision depended on MWA’s confidence score. Then, if the score exceeded 8.5, the phishing label was retained; otherwise, the benign label from USA was accepted. However, if MWA labeled the entry as benign and USA as phishing, the phishing label from USA was always trusted.

This approach capitalized on the complementary strengths of the two analyses. USA, optimized for URL analysis, was adept at identifying domain-specific anomalies, while MWA specialized in edge cases by capturing subtler phishing signals, such as content and visual mimicry. By prioritizing USA’s acute URL analysis and leveraging MWA’s nuanced confidence scores, we achieved a balanced trade-off between precision and recall. The combined analysis enhanced detection accuracy, particularly in cases involving visually deceptive phishing tactics.

4
Results and Discussion

The results of this study demonstrate the efficacy of multimodal phishing detection modified by Adaptive Linguistic Prompting (ALP) in comparison to only heuristic zero-shot prompting methods seen in our baseline
Lee et al. 2024
.

To start, the performance assessment of our phishing detection system revealed notable specialties between GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro across MVA, USA, and the combined analysis.

A comparison between Multimodal Webpage Analysis (MWA) and the baseline revealed that static approaches frequently fail to detect advanced deception tactics, particularly when phishing sites employ sophisticated brand mimicry. To elaborate, MWA proved especially effective at identifying visual and content-based inconsistencies, with Gemini 1.5 Pro showing particular strength in detecting brand representation anomalies and content manipulation. This effectiveness stems from MWA’s comprehensive analysis of visual elements, textual content, and brand consistency markers.

The URL Structure Analysis (USA) component demonstrated complementary strengths, with GPT-4o exhibiting superior performance in analyzing domain authenticity and URL patterns. USA proved particularly adept at identifying suspicious domain structures and security protocol inconsistencies, offering a crucial layer of defense against sophisticated, yet patterned phishing URL aspects. The marked performance improvement through our combined analysis function underscores the value of integrating both approaches.

The combined analysis function leveraged the complementary strengths of MWA and USA. GPT-4o’s combined analysis achieved a precision of 91.67% and recall (phishing detection rate) of 94.12%, resulting in an F1-score of 0.93. Gemini 1.5 Pro’s combined analysis was comparable, with a 91% precision rate and 92% recall rate, producing an F1-score of 0.91. These results suggest that an optimal phishing detection framework with a tweaked combination function could integrate both models to leverage their complementary strengths, specifically GPT-4o running USA and Gemini running MWA.

**If you are unsure and feel that a link is suspicious of phishing activity, label it phishing**

**Otherwise, if the link relates to an official domain, label it Benign**

While testing USA, we incorporated a "suspicious-first" prompting strategy into USA, as seen above. This guided models to classify uncertain cases as potential threats, significantly improving USA detection accuracy while maintaining a practical balance between security and usability. This finding suggests prompt engineering could be as crucial as model architecture in developing robust detection systems as such a simple prompt increased USA’s accuracy from 81% to 91% in GPT-4o by adopting a risk-averse approach.

5
Conclusion

Our study demonstrates that Adaptive Linguistic Prompting (ALP) advances phishing detection by guiding multimodal LLMs to systematically analyze brand, linguistic, visual, and URL-based deception tactics. By integrating semantic reasoning, few-shot prompting, and analysis combinations, ALP addresses critical gaps in traditional heuristic methods, which fail to detect sophisticated phishing attempts that exploit nuanced linguistic cues (examples in appendix). The combined analysis function achieved an F1-score of 0.93, a considerable improvement on our baseline and past literature. This underscores ALP’s ability to harmonize complementary detection modalities while prioritizing risk-averse classification strategies. Our findings establish ALP as a robust, interpretable, and scalable solution, reducing reliance on continuous retraining. Future research potential lies in optimizing prompt engineering to reduce token consumption and incorporating multi-LLM frameworks to leverage certain model strengths.

6
Limitations

While ALP-modified frameworks advance phishing detection, certain limitations merit acknowledgment. First, the dataset, though diverse, may not fully capture emerging phishing tactics or region-specific attacks, potentially affecting applicability. Second, reliance on proprietary LLMs like GPT-4o introduces scalability and cost barriers, limiting accessibility for broader deployment. Third, the framework’s performance on non-English content and adversarial evasion strategies (e.g., context-aware paraphrasing) remains unexplored. Fourth, the "suspicious-first" strategy prioritizes URL analysis in conflicting MWA and USA situations, which could marginalize nuanced visual mimicry detected by multimodal analysis. A weighted probability combination function could potentially be explored. Finally, comparisons to machine learning architectures other than transformer-based LLMs are absent, leaving ALP’s relative innovation open for further exploration. Addressing these through dynamic datasets, multilingual training, and hybrid models could enhance the adaptability of ALP in real-world scenarios, potentially revealing explicit emergent behavior in future research.

The Multimodal Webpage Analysis (MWA) few-shot prompt guides the model to analyze phishing risk by breaking down the evaluation into structured components: brand recognition, diction and syntax, and screenshot-specific analysis. It provides step-by-step reasoning, key indicators, supporting evidence, a confidence score, and a final phishing/benign classification. This approach ensures the model systematically analyzes both textual and visual elements, leveraging examples to identify subtle inconsistencies in branding, language, and design that may indicate phishing.

Below is a few-shot prompt for USA

Example 1:

Link: ’http://university-scholarship-app.us/confirm-details’

Features: The domain includes terms such as "university" and "scholarship", HTTP protocol, path "confirm-details”.

Reasoning: This link exhibits several traits of a phishing attempt. The domain uses generic terms like "university" and "scholarship," which are unrelated to any well-known institution, making it highly suspicious. The use of HTTP instead of HTTPS suggests that the site does not encrypt user data, leaving it vulnerable to interception. The path, "confirm-details," is a classic phishing tactic designed to solicit sensitive personal or account-related information under the guise of legitimacy. These factors combined strongly indicate malicious intent.

Classification: PHISHING

The URL Structure Analysis (USA) few-shot prompt focuses on classifying URLs as phishing or benign based on domain name, protocol, URL path, and phishing indicators. It requires the model to provide detailed reasoning for its classification, emphasizing features like HTTPS usage, domain legitimacy, and suspicious path structures. By incorporating examples of phishing URLs, the prompt trains the model to recognize patterns such as generic domain names, insecure protocols, and deceptive paths, enabling it to detect phishing attempts through URL analysis alone.

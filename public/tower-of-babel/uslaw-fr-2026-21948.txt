I. Introduction

About the National Credit Union Administration

    Created by the U.S. Congress in 1970, the National Credit Union 
Administration (NCUA) is the federal agency that insures deposits at 
federally insured credit unions, protects the members who own credit 
unions, and charters and regulates federal credit unions.
    NCUA's mission is to enable access to financial services by 
facilitating safe, sound, and resilient credit unions.
    The agency operates its headquarters in Alexandria, Virginia; its 
Asset Management and Assistance Center (AMAC) in Austin, Texas, which 
liquidates credit unions and recovers assets; and three regional 
offices, which carry out the agency's supervision and examination 
program.
    Backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, the 
National Credit Union Share Insurance Fund (Share Insurance Fund) 
provides up to $250,000 of federal share insurance to millions of 
account holders in all federal credit unions and most state-chartered 
credit unions.
    As of June 30, 2026, NCUA regulates and supervises 4,214 federally 
insured credit unions, which have approximately 146 million members and 
$2.5 trillion in assets.\1\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \1\ Source: NCUA quarterly call report data, second quarter 
2026.

Statutory Authority

    Pursuant to the Federal Credit Union Act (FCU Act), the NCUA Board 
(the Board) determines the resources needed for carrying out NCUA's 
responsibilities under the Act.\2\ The Board is authorized to expend 
such funds and perform such other functions or acts as it deems 
necessary or appropriate, according to the rules, regulations, or 
policies it establishes.\3\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \2\ See 12 United States Code (U.S.C.) 1752a.
    \3\ See 12 U.S.C. 1766(i)(2).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The FCU Act authorizes two primary sources to fund NCUA: (1) 
Requisitions from the Share Insurance Fund ``for such administrative 
and other expenses incurred in carrying out the purposes of [Title II 
of the Act] as [the Board] may determine to be proper'' and (2) ``fees 
and assessments (including income earned on insurance deposits) levied 
on insured credit unions under [the Act].''.\4\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ See 12 U.S.C. 1783(a); 12 U.S.C. 1766(j)(3). An annual 
operating fee is paid by federal credit unions pursuant to 12 U.S.C. 
1755. NCUA is not funded through the annual Congressional 
appropriations process.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Pursuant to its authority to use the Share Insurance Fund to carry 
out its insurance-related responsibilities, the Board approved an 
Overhead Transfer Rate (OTR) methodology and authorized the Office of 
the Chief Financial Officer to transfer resources from the Share 
Insurance Fund to the Operating Fund to account for insurance-related 
expenses.\5\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \5\ See 12 U.S.C. 1783(a).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In December 2023, the Board approved and published in the Federal 
Register the current federal credit union annual operating fee 
methodology, which is the basis for how the operating fee is 
calculated.\6\ The Board delegated authority to the Chief Financial 
Officer to calculate the annual operating fee for federal credit unions 
and to set the fee schedule as calculated using the approved method.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \6\ See www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/26/2023-28303/national-credit-union-administration-operating-fee-schedule-methodology.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Pursuant to the FCU Act, NCUA deposits fees collected in the 
agency's Operating Fund at the Treasury of the United States, and NCUA 
uses those fees to defray the cost of carrying out the agency's 
operations, including examining and supervising federal credit 
unions.\7\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ See 12 U.S.C. 1755(d).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

NCUA's Annual Budget Process

    The FCU Act requires NCUA to publish an annual draft budget in the 
Federal Register for public comment and hold a public hearing on the 
budget.\8\ NCUA stands out as the only Financial Institutions Reform, 
Recovery, and Enforcement Act (FIRREA) agency that releases a detailed 
draft budget and solicits public comments on it.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \8\ See 12 U.S.C. 1789(b).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    NCUA develops a two-year draft budget by identifying the necessary 
resources, including supporting justifications, to achieve the agency's 
mission, goals, requirements, and objectives. This effort includes a 
field-level review of every federally insured credit union to estimate 
the workload to examine and supervise the credit union in the 
forthcoming year, which is translated into the cost of the staff and 
associated expenses for such functions. In addition to this workload 
analysis, each NCUA office reviews its fixed and recurring expenses, 
including:

 Employee travel.
 Rental payments for leased property.
 Operations and maintenance for owned facilities or equipment.
 Supplies.
 Telecommunications services.
 Major capital investments.
 Other administrative and contracted services costs.

    Before arriving at the final budget, the NCUA Board reviews any 
comments from the public and evaluates other considerations. Once the 
budget is approved, the OTR and federal credit union annual operating 
fee rates are set for the first year of the two-year budget.

    Combined, these three proposed 2027 budgets total $325.4 million, 
an increase of 2.9 percent compared to the 2026 Board-approved budget, 
and consistent with the 2027 funding level approved by the NCUA Board 
as part of the 2026-2027 budget.
    There are three main factors contributing to the draft 2027 budget

     An increase in the Share Insurance Fund Administrative 
Expenses Budget. NCUA used the proceeds from the sale of its Austin

    These increases are partially offset by a comparative decrease to 
NCUA's Capital Budget for 2027. The 2026 Capital Budget included $16 
million for reorganization-related investments in systems and process 
improvements that align with government efficiency goals and other 
Administration priorities. The 2027 Capital Budget does not include any 
additional funding for reorganization-related investments or projects.

Proposed 2028 Budget: $344.2 Million

    The 2028 combined total for the three proposed budgets is $344.2 
million, which is 5.8 percent higher than the proposed 2027 combined 
total.
    For planning purposes, the 2028 draft budget projects the same rate 
of compensation growth as in 2027, and 3.0 percent budgetary growth in 
all non-compensation spending categories plus certain discrete 
adjustments in cases where project cost growth is expected to exceed 
3.0 percent. The remaining growth results from a lower estimate of 
prior-year surpluses offsetting the 2028 budget compared to the surplus 
used in 2027.
    While the agency presents a two-year draft budget for planning 
purposes, the Board's practice has been to revisit each year's budget 
before that budget year begins.

III. Key Themes of the Proposed 2027-2028 Budget

NCUA Reorganization

    Since the start of its Voluntary Separation Program in 2025, which 
resulted in a 23 percent reduction to its staffing, NCUA has been 
developing reorganization plans to build more productive, efficient 
agency operations. Throughout 2025 and 2026, NCUA has increased its 
operational efficiency and improved service delivery. The agency has 
been removing unnecessary and burdensome regulations, streamlining its 
business processes, and identifying technological improvements, and 
will continue to do so in 2027 and 2028.
    The draft 2027-2028 budget supports NCUA's strategic restructuring 
plans. The agency's reorganization process is actively underway and 
targeted for completion by December 2027. The agency's reorganization 
efforts focus on prioritizing NCUA's statutory authorities and its most 
critical work, reducing and consolidating business units with like 
functions, achieving a higher supervisor to employee ratio, and 
leveraging technology to increase staff efficiency.

Technology Modernization: Enhanced Cybersecurity and SI/AI Strategy

    NCUA must continue investing in modern information technology to 
keep pace with innovation while defending against evolving threats that 
could impact its ability to supervise America's credit unions. By 
integrating its cybersecurity framework with a forward-looking super 
intelligence/artificial intelligence (SI/AI) strategy, NCUA seeks to 
optimize business operations, harden agency systems against evolving 
digital threats, and foster a secure environment that supports 
responsible innovation across the credit union landscape.
    The draft 2027-2028 budget includes resources to expand the 
agency's zero-trust cybersecurity strategy, with investments in 
advanced threat protection to address evolving digital risks and 
fulfill new federal requirements. To comply fully with federal 
directives, NCUA must acquire enhanced cybersecurity capabilities. The 
draft budget's investments will reduce reliance on manual processes, 
reinforce the organization's IT infrastructure, and ensure that NCUA 
can continue innovating by integrating SI/AI tools into its operations.
    When combined with a portion of the agency's 2026 reorganization 
and innovation funding, the draft budget provides funding to continue 
deploying SI/AI and advanced analytic tools across the agency. NCUA's 
SI/AI strategy aligns with the Administration's direction and will both 
enhance examination and supervision capabilities and deliver 
operational efficiencies.

[[Page 67865]]

Deregulation, Examination Priorities, and Workforce

    The draft 2027-2028 budget includes resources to reduce regulatory 
burden on credit unions and focus NCUA's examination program on 
material risks.
    NCUA's Deregulation Project is a multi-year initiative to review, 
streamline, and modernize the agency's regulatory framework. The 
Deregulation Project will continue in 2027, and will address more 
complex regulations with broader policy and operational implications. 
The draft budget supports this project and other rulemaking and related 
implementation efforts associated with new legislation and the 
Administration's priorities, such as the Guiding and Establishing 
National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act (GENIUS Act) for Permitted 
Payment Stablecoin Issuers.
    NCUA continues to invest in its efforts to:

 Focus examination attention on areas posing material risk.
 Streamline examination practices to increase efficiency
 Adopt advanced capabilities to improve risk and problem 
identification
 Train, develop, and resource staff to ensure the agency has 
the necessary expertise to supervise complex and new activities
 Facilitate responsible innovation by credit unions

IV. Operating Budget: $312.4 Million

Overview

    The proposed 2027 Operating Budget is $312.4 million, which 
provides the resources required for the agency to conduct activities 
prescribed by the FCU Act. These mandates include but are not limited 
to:

 Chartering new federal credit unions
 Approving field of membership applications of federal credit 
unions
 Promulgating regulations and providing guidance
 Performing examinations, supervision, and enforcement

    NCUA also implements requirements of other statutes and Executive 
Orders including those related to the Bank Secrecy Act, various 
consumer financial protection laws, and various requirements for 
federal agencies.

Summary of 2027 Capital Projects

    Each year NCUA carries out an extensive review of the agency's 
needs for improvement, repair, or replacement of information 
technology, facilities, equipment, and other capital investments. The 
proposed 2027 Capital Budget supports continuing investments in the 
agency's highest priority needs as follows: \14\

    Strengthening Agency Cybersecurity ($4.7 million). The 2027 Capital 
Budget invests in multiple projects that directly strengthen 
cybersecurity at NCUA. These investments reinforce the agency's focus 
systems that are secure, available, and protected against emerging 
technological risks. Key projects within this category include:
     Firewall Hardware Refresh--Strengthens network perimeter 
defenses and reduces outage risks, which are core requirements for safe 
operations.
     Network Access Control / Network Segmentation--Supports

     Modernizing Network Internet Protocols--Ensures NCUA can

     Identity Verification and Data Interchange Security--

    Modernizing Agency Systems and Workflow Transformation ($1.7

     Community Development Revolving Loan Fund (CDRLF) Grant & 
Loan Management System--Modernizes the agency's outdated system to 
reduce staff workload and ensure compliance with federal grant 
management requirements.
     Consumer Assistance Center / Relationship Management 
System--Modernizes customer relationship management infrastructure and 
supports interagency collaboration.
     New Charter System Improvements--Fully digitizes 
chartering workflows, reduces burden for new credit union applicants, 
and meets requirements of the 21st Century Road to Housing Act.
     eDiscovery Tool--Strengthens legal research capabilities 
and reduces manual discovery through a modern data-filtering and 
investigation engine.
    Enhancing Engagement, Transparency, and Operational Reliability 
($1.3 million). The Capital Budget strengthens technological readiness 
through investments that modernize communication with customers and 
enable compliance with evolving regulatory requirements. Key projects 
within this category include:
     Unified Communications and Collaboration Modernization--
Improves hybrid meeting reliability for Board and public engagement

     Technical Support for Board Priorities--Maintains

    Headquarters Building Minor Construction and Maintenance Projects 
($0.3 million). The proposed 2027 Capital Budget supports NCUA's multi-
year headquarters building improvement plan that identifies projects

    Reorganization and Technology Investment Fund. The 2026 Capital

VII. Financing NCUA's Programs

Overview

    NCUA incurs various expenses to satisfy its statutory requirements, 
including those involved in examining and supervising federally insured 
credit unions. The NCUA Board adopts an Operating Budget, a Capital 
Budget, and a Share Insurance Fund Administrative Expenses Budget each 
year to fund most of the costs to operate the agency.\17\ When 
formulating the annual budget, NCUA is mindful that its funding comes 
from credit unions and strives to operate in an efficient, effective, 
transparent, and fully accountable manner.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ Some costs are directly charged to the Share Insurance Fund 
when appropriate to do so.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The FCU Act authorizes two primary sources to fund the Operating 
and Capital Budgets:
    (1) Requisitions from the Share Insurance Fund ``for such 
administrative and other expenses incurred in carrying out the purposes 
of [Title II of the Act] as [the Board] may determine to be proper,'' 
\18\ and.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \18\ 12 U.S.C. 1783(a).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    (2) ``[F]ees and assessments (including income earned on insurance 
deposits) levied on insured credit unions under [the Act].'' \19\ Among 
the fees levied under the Act are annual Operating Fees, which are 
required for federal credit unions under 12 U.S.C. 1755 ``and may be 
expended by the Board to defray the expenses incurred in carrying out 
the provisions of [the Act,] including the examination and supervision 
of [federal credit unions].''
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \19\ 12 U.S.C. 1766(j)(3). Other sources of income for the 
Operating Budget may include interest income, parking fee income, 
and rental income.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Taken together, these authorities effectively require the Board to 
determine which expenses are appropriately paid from each source while 
giving the Board broad discretion in allocating expenses.
    In 1973, the U.S. Government Accountability Office recommended NCUA 
adopt a method for allocating Operating Budget costs--that is, the 
portion of NCUA's budget funded by requisitions from the Share 
Insurance Fund and the portion covered by operating fees paid by 
federal credit unions.\20\ NCUA has since used an allocation 
methodology known as the OTR to determine how much of the Operating and 
Capital Budgets to fund with a requisition from the Share Insurance 
Fund.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \20\ See www.gao.gov/products/b-1640314-31.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    NCUA uses the OTR methodology to allocate agency expenses between 
these two primary funding sources. Specifically, the OTR is the formula 
NCUA uses to allocate insurance-related expenses to the Share Insurance 
Fund under Title II of the FCU Act. Almost all other operating expenses 
are funded by collecting annual operating fees from federal credit 
unions.\21\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \21\ Annual operating fees must ``be determined according to a 
schedule, or schedules, or other method determined by the NCUA Board 
to be appropriate, which gives due consideration to the expenses of 
the [NCUA] in carrying out its responsibilities under the [Act] and 
to the ability of [federal credit unions] to pay the fee.'' 12 
U.S.C. 1755(b).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Two statutory provisions directly limit the Board's discretion with 
respect to Share Insurance Fund requisitions for NCUA's Operating 
Budget and the OTR. First, expenses funded from the Share Insurance 
Fund must carry out the purposes of Title II of the FCU Act, which 
relates to share insurance.\22\ Second, NCUA may not fund its entire 
Operating Budget through charges to the Share Insurance Fund.\23\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \22\ 12 U.S.C. 1783(a).
    \23\ The Act in 12 U.S.C. 1755(a) states, ``[i]n accordance with 
rules prescribed by the Board, each [federal credit union] shall pay 
to the [NCUA] an annual operating fee which may be composed of one 
or more charges identified as to the function or functions for which 
assessed.'' See also 12 U.S.C. 1766(j)(3).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Overhead Transfer Rate

    The OTR represents the percentage of the agency's operating budget 
paid for by a transfer from the Share Insurance Fund. The OTR is 
designed to cover NCUA's costs of examining and supervising the risk to 
the Share Insurance Fund posed by all federally insured credit unions, 
as well as the costs of administering the Share Insurance Fund.
    Federally insured credit unions are not billed for and do not have 
to remit the OTR amount; instead, it is transferred directly to the 
Operating Fund from the Share Insurance Fund. This transfer, therefore, 
represents an indirect cost to all federally insured credit unions.
    The NCUA Board approved the current methodology for calculating the 
OTR at its November 2017 open meeting.\24\ In 2023, the Board published 
a request for comment in the Federal Register regarding the OTR 
methodology but did not propose or adopt any changes to the current 
methodology.\25\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \24\ 82 FR 55644 (Nov. 22, 2017).
    \25\ See www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/20/2023-28000/request-for-comment-regarding-overhead-transfer-rate-methodology.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The minor variations in the OTR since 2018 are the result of 
routine, small fluctuations in the variables that affect the OTR, 
including normal variations in the workload budget from one calendar 
year to the next.
    NCUA conducts a comprehensive workload analysis annually. This 
analysis estimates the amount of time necessary to conduct examinations 
and supervise federally insured credit unions to carry out NCUA's dual 
mission as insurer and regulator. This analysis starts with a field-
level review of every federally insured credit union to estimate the 
number of workload hours needed for the year. These estimates are 
informed by the overall parameters of NCUA's examination program, 
including workload estimates that are refined by regional managers and 
submitted to NCUA headquarters for the annual budget proposal. The OTR 
methodology excludes state regulator costs, including state examiner 
hours.

Operating Fee

    The Board delegated authority to the Chief Financial Officer to 
calculate the annual operating fee for federal credit unions and to set 
the fee schedule as calculated using the approved methodology. In 
December 2023, the Board approved and published in the Federal Register 
the current operating fee methodology, which forms the basis for how 
the operating fee is calculated.\26\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \26\ See www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/12/26/2023-28303/national-credit-union-administration-operating-fee-schedule-methodology.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Under the current methodology to determine the annual operating fee 
assessed on natural person federal credit unions, NCUA first calculates 
the average of total assets reported in the preceding four calendar 
quarters available at the time of the calculation, net of any reported 
Paycheck Protection Program loans.\27\ Natural person federal credit 
unions with assets less than approximately $2.3 million are not 
assessed an operating fee and their assets are therefore excluded from 
this calculation.\28\ Corporate federal credit unions also pay an 
annual operating fee based on a different scale.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \27\ To determine the assessment scale for 2027, total growth in 
natural person federal credit union assets is calculated as the 
change between the average of the four most current quarters (that 
is, the third and fourth quarters of 2025 and the first two quarters 
of 2026) and the previous four quarters (that is, the third and 
fourth quarters of 2024 and the first two quarters of 2025). The 
result of the calculation is 4.81 percent. The fee exemption 
threshold and the asset level dividing points for the fee tiers are 
likewise increased by this same growth rate to preserve the same 
relative relationship of the scale to the applicable asset base.
    \28\ The exemption threshold for 2027 is estimated at $2,259,851 
which accounts for 4.81 percent aggregate growth in credit union 
system assets.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

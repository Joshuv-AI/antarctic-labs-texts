Inflationary models in f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity: Constraints from P ​ l ​ a ​ n ​ c ​ k Planck , BICEP/ K ​ e ​ c ​ k Keck , and Atacama
Biswajit Deb, Atri Deshamukhya

Abstract
The advent of high-precision cosmological observations has challenged many traditional inflationary models. Data from P ​ l ​ a ​ n ​ c ​ k Planck 2018 along with the BICEP/ K ​ e ​ c ​ k Keck 2018 result have already ruled out most of the established models by placing tight constraints on the tensor-to-scalar ratio r r . Upcoming missions like LiteBIRD & CMB-S4 are expected to impose an even more stringent bound on r r , potentially excluding further models from the viable landscape. In this evolving observational context, modified gravity theories offer a promising way to reconcile inflationary models with data. In this work, we explore several inflationary models, namely mutated hilltop inflation, D-brane inflation, and Woods-Saxon inflation, within the framework of f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity. The cosmological observable parameters, viz. scalar spectral tilt n s n_{s} , tensor-to-scalar ratio r r , and running of scalar spectral index n s ​ k n_{sk} are estimated for the three models, and their trajectories are plotted in the n s − r n_{s}-r plane. The model results are evaluated in light of the P ​ l ​ a ​ n ​ c ​ k Planck , BICEP/ K ​ e ​ c ​ k Keck , DESI DR2, and ACT DR6 data. We observe that for a certain model parameter space, these potentials are viable within the current observational bounds. Keywords: f ⁡ ( R , T ) f(R,T) gravity; Slow-roll Inflation; ACT DR6; DESI DR2

2
Inflation in
f(R,T)
gravity

The
{f(R,T)}
gravity action proposed by Harko et al.
[
48
]
reads as:

S=\int\left[\frac{f(R,T)}{16\pi G}+L_{m}\right]\sqrt{-g}d^{4}x

(2.1)

where
{f(R,T)}
is an arbitrary function of the Ricci scalar
R
and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\alpha\beta}
,
L_{m}
is the matter Lagrangian density,
G
is the Newtonian gravitational constant, and
g
is the metric determinant. On metric variation of the action, the
{f(R,T)}
gravity field equations are obtained as

f_{R}(R,T)R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}f(R,T)+[g_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_{\sigma}\nabla^{\sigma}-\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}]f_{R}(R,T)=8\pi GT_{\alpha\beta}-f_{T}(R,T)(T_{\alpha\beta}+\Theta_{\alpha\beta})

(2.2)

where we have denoted
f_{R}(R,T)=\frac{\partial f(R,T)}{\partial R}
,
f_{T}(R,T)=\frac{\partial f(R,T)}{\partial T}
and defined
T_{\alpha\beta}
and
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
as

T_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}L_{m}-2\frac{\delta L_{m}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}

(2.3)

\Theta_{\alpha\beta}=g^{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta T_{\mu\nu}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}=-2T_{\alpha\beta}+g_{\alpha\beta}L_{m}-2\frac{\delta^{2}L_{m}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\mu\nu}}

(2.4)

The term
\Theta_{\alpha\beta}
is crucial in
{f(R,T)}
gravity, as it contains Lagrangian
L_{m}
. Depending on the nature of the matter field, the field equations for
{f(R,T)}
gravity will vary. So, the field equations in
{f(R,T)}
gravity depend not only on the choice of the function but also on the nature of the matter field
[
48
]
.

In this work, we have considered the simplest yet most studied form of
{f(R,T)}
which is
f(R,T)=R+16\pi G\lambda T
, where
\lambda
is the model parameter. This particular functional form satisfies all energy conditions
[
49
]
and is excellent for explaining both inflation and late-time cosmology
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
58
,
59
]
.

The field equations for this function will be

R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}R=8\pi GT_{\alpha\beta}^{(eff)}

(2.5)

where
T_{\alpha\beta}^{(eff)}
is the effective EM tensor that includes the correction from
f(R,T)
gravity, given by,

T_{\alpha\beta}^{(eff)}=T_{\alpha\beta}-2\lambda(T_{\alpha\beta}-\frac{1}{2}Tg_{\alpha\beta}+\Theta_{\alpha\beta})

(2.6)

For
\lambda=0
, the Einstein field equations are retrieved. To describe inflation, a homogeneous scalar field
\phi
minimally coupled to gravity is required
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. Then the matter Lagrangian will take the form,

L_{m}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi-V(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)

(2.7)

where
V(\phi)
is the potential of the inflaton. To study the cosmological implications, we consider the Friedman-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric for a flat Universe. Then from Eq.
2.5
, modified Friedmann equations are derived as:

H^{2}=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}(1+2\lambda)+V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)\right]

(2.8)

\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}=-\frac{8\pi G}{3}\left[\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)\right]

(2.9)

The Hubble parameter is defined as
H=\frac{\dot{a}}{a}
, and
a(t)
is the scale factor. Likewise, the effective equation of state takes the form of

w^{(eff)}=\frac{p^{(eff)}}{\rho^{(eff)}}=\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)-2V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)}{\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+2V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)}

(2.10)

Here,
p^{eff}
and
\rho^{eff}
are effective pressure and radiation density, respectively. However, Eq.
2.9
can be expressed in terms of
\dot{H}
as:

\dot{H}=\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}-H^{2}=-\frac{8\pi G}{2}(p^{eff}+\rho^{eff})=-4\pi G\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)

(2.11)

Now, from Eqs.
2.8
,
2.9
, and
2.11
the modified Klein-Gordon equation is derived as:

\ddot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)+3H\dot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)+\frac{dV}{d\phi}(1+4\lambda)=0

(2.12)

To check whether slow roll inflation is possible in this model, standard slow-roll parameters need to be derived for
f(R,T)
gravity. The first slow-roll parameter denoted by
\epsilon
in terms of the model parameter
\lambda
is

\epsilon=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=\frac{3\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)}{\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)+2V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)}

(2.13)

Accelerated expansion occurs in the slow roll regime if
\epsilon<<1
, that is, when the potential energy of the inflaton dominates the kinetic energy. Under this condition, the inflaton rolls slowly and hence the name slow-roll condition
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. In terms of the current model parameter, the modified first slow roll condition reads as follows:

\dot{\phi}^{2}(1+2\lambda)<<V(\phi)(1+4\lambda)

(2.14)

Again, the accelerated expansion must sustain for a prolonged period of time to achieve the desired amount of
e
-folds, and hence a second condition must be imposed
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. In terms of our model parameter this reads as follows:

\ddot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)<<|3H\dot{\phi}(1+2\lambda)|

(2.15)

This defines the second slow-roll condition
|\eta|<<1
as:

\eta=-\frac{\ddot{\phi}}{H\dot{\phi}}

(2.16)

The parameters defined in Eqs.
2.13
and
2.16
are called Hubble slow-roll parameters. They can also be defined in terms of potential and are therefore called potential slow-roll parameters
\epsilon_{V}
and
\eta_{V}

[
5
]
. The potential slow-roll parameters in
f(R,T)
gravity in terms of reduced
Planck
mass
M_{Pl}
read:

\displaystyle\epsilon_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}\frac{M_{Pl}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\eta_{V}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+2\lambda}M_{Pl}^{2}\frac{V^{\prime\prime}}{V}

(2.17)

It is clear from the Eqs.
2.17
that the affect of the correction term has been induced in the slow-roll parameters. As long as
\epsilon_{V},|\eta_{V}|<<1
, slow-roll inflation continues and inflation ends as soon as either of the parameters becomes unity
[
7
]
. The third potential slow-roll parameter, which describes how fast the curvature of the potential is changing as the field evolves
[
5
]
, in
f(R,T)
gravity reads:

\xi_{V}^{2}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{4}}{(1+2\lambda^{2})}\frac{V^{\prime}V^{\prime\prime\prime}}{V^{2}}

(2.18)

Another important quantity required to study in any inflationary model is the amount of inflation that has taken place. It is quantified by the number of
e
-folds and is defined as

N=\ln a=\int_{\phi_{i}}^{\phi_{end}}\frac{H}{\dot{\phi}}\,d\phi\

(2.19)

where
\phi_{end}
and
\phi_{i}
indicate the value of the inflaton at the end and beginning of inflation
[
5
]
. Under slow-roll approximation, the number of
e
-folds in
f(R,T)
gravity becomes

N=\frac{1+2\lambda}{M_{Pl}^{2}}\int_{\phi_{end}}^{\phi_{i}}\frac{V(\phi)}{V^{\prime}(\phi)}\,d\phi\

(2.20)

The spectral indices, the tensor-to-scalar ratio, and the running of the scalar spectral index in terms of the slow-roll parameters can be written as
[
5
,
6
,
7
]

\displaystyle n_{s}-1

\displaystyle=-6\epsilon_{V}+2\eta_{V}

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=16\epsilon_{V}

\displaystyle n_{sk}

\displaystyle=16\epsilon_{V}\eta_{V}-24\epsilon_{V}^{2}-2\xi_{V}^{2}

(2.21)

3.1
Mutated hilltop Inflation (MHI)

The mutated hilltop potential is a refined version of the standard hilltop inflation potential, developed by Pal et al.
[
60
]
In case of the mutated version, the flat potential is altered by a hyperbolic function whose power series expansion includes an infinite number of terms which makes the model more accurate and concrete compared to the traditional hilltop potential. The form of this potential bears close similarity with its counterpart in mutated hybrid inflation, hence the name
[
60
,
61
,
62
]
. The form of the potential reads

V(\phi)=V_{0}[1-\sech{(\tau\phi)}]

(3.1)

where
V_{0}
is the inflation energy scale and
\tau
is a parameter with the dimension of inverse of
Planck
mass. The
\tau
flattens the potential near hilltop, thereby creating a longer period for sufficient
e
-folds can happen.

This potential is purely phenomenological, however, motivated from supergravity
[
60
,
63
]
. MHI is distinct from HI in two ways which are as follows:

•

In hilltop potential, inflation takes place near the maxima of the potential
(\phi\approx 0)
where as in the case of mutated hilltop potential, inflation happens on a flat plateau away from the minimum, i.e. as the inflaton rolls down towards it’s minima.

•

MHI satisfies the condition of vanishing of the potential at its absolute minimum, i.e.
V(\phi_{min})=V^{\prime}(\phi_{min})=0
, which is not seen in the case of usual hilltop potential.

Mutated hilltop potential generally produces small tensor-to-scalar ratio in GR
[
60
,
62
]
. Mayukh et al. reported that in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity, it produces small
r
compatible with current observation
[
64
]
. Keeping this point in mind, we will check whether this potential is viable in the
f(R,T)
gravity framework.

The slow roll parameters for this potential can be expressed using Eqs.
2.17
,
2.18
as

\epsilon_{V}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{2}\tau^{2}\sech^{2}{(\tau\phi)}\tanh^{2}(\tau\phi)}{2(1+2\lambda)[1-\sech{(\tau\phi)}]^{2}}

(3.2)

\eta_{V}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{2}\tau^{2}[\sech^{3}{(\tau\phi)}-\sech{(\tau\phi)}\tanh^{2}{\tau\phi}]}{(1+2\lambda)[1-\sech{(\tau\phi)}]}

(3.3)

\xi_{V}^{2}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{4}\tau^{4}\coth^{2}{(\frac{\tau\phi}{2})}\sech^{4}{(\tau\phi)}[\cosh{(2\tau\phi)}-1]}{2(1+2\lambda)^{2}}

(3.4)

Then the scalar spectral index and tensor-to-scalar ratio can be calculated from Eq.
2.21
as

n_{s}=1-\frac{3M_{Pl}^{2}\tau^{2}\sech^{2}{(\tau\phi)}\tanh^{2}(\tau\phi)}{(1+2\lambda)[1-\sech{(\tau\phi)}]^{2}}+\frac{2M_{Pl}^{2}\tau^{2}[\sech^{3}{(\tau\phi)}-\sech{(\tau\phi)}\tanh^{2}{\tau\phi}]}{(1+2\lambda)[1-\sech{(\tau\phi)}]}

(3.5)

r=\frac{8M_{Pl}^{2}\tau^{2}\sech^{2}{(\tau\phi)}\tanh^{2}(\tau\phi)}{(1+2\lambda)[1-\sech{(\tau\phi)}]^{2}}

(3.6)

The expression for
n_{sk}
is skipped, being too lengthy, however can be easily calculated using Eqs.
2.21
,
3.2
,
3.3
and
3.4
. The

3.2
D-Brane Inflation

The concept of brane inflation was proposed by Dvali et al. in 1999 where they considered the separation between a brane and an anti-brane as the inflaton
[
67
]
. As the branes move close to each other, slow-roll inflation occurs, and when the branes collide, energy is released into radiation, which corresponds to the reheating mechanism
[
67
]
.

D-Brane inflation is a specific type of brane inflation, involving D-branes that obey the Dirichlet boundary condition. D-brane inflation involves
D3
and Anti-
D3
brane placed in a wrapped geometry. The potential energy from the tensions of the
D3/\overline{D3}
branes drives the inflation. The brane inflation scenario requires moduli stabilization so that they can realize the observable universe in 4-dimensions. The Kachru-Kallos-Linde-Maldacena-McAllister-Trivedi (KKLMMT) mechanism of adding an Anti-
D3
brane to uplift the vacuum to a metastable de-Sitter is an example of D-brane inflation with moduli stabilization
[
68
]
.

Despite theoretical issues, D-brane inflation gained limelight after the release of
Planck
2018, as it matches the current bounds
[
9
]
. We will focus on two types of D-brane model using the nomenclature used by Jerome et al.
[
8
]
. The first model is BI, which stands for brane inflation, has the Coulomb-type potential:

V_{BI}=V_{0}\left(1-\frac{m^{n}}{\phi^{n}}\right)

(3.7)

Although this potential matches the current bound appropriately, it has certain issues. This potential is unbounded from below and hence stucked with graceful exit problem
[
69
]
. KKLMMT proposed a consistent generalization in which the potential takes the form of an inverse harmonic function
[
68
]
. This is named as KKLTI by Jerome et al.
[
8
]
which stands for KKLT inflation and has the potential:

V_{KKLTI}=V_{0}\left(1-\frac{m^{n}}{\phi^{n}}\right)^{-1}=V_{0}\frac{\phi^{n}}{\phi^{n}+m^{n}}

(3.8)

Since
V_{BI}
is not physically sound, we will continue our further study with
V_{KKLTI}
model. In the large
m
limit (
m\gg 10
),
V_{KKLTI}
predicts a large
r=8n/N
, which is not supported by current observation. However, in the small
m
limit (
m\leq 1

Planck

n=2
covers the entire
1\sigma
region of
Planck

[
69
]

f(R,T)
gravity setup both in the large and small

The potential slow-roll parameters are obtained as

\epsilon_{V}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{2}n^{2}m^{2n}}{2(1+2\lambda)\phi^{2}(\phi^{n}+m^{n})^{2}}

(3.9)

\eta_{V}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{2}nm^{n}[(n-1)m^{n}-(n+1)\phi^{n}]}{(1+2\lambda)\phi^{2}(\phi^{n}+m^{n})^{2}}

(3.10)

\xi_{V}^{2}=\frac{M_{Pl}^{4}n^{2}m^{2n}[m^{2n}(n^{2}-3n+2)-4(n^{2}-1)m^{n}\phi^{n}+(n^{2}+3n+2)\phi^{2n}]}{(1+2\lambda)^{2}\phi^{4}(\phi^{n}+m^{n})^{4}}

(3.11)

The scalar spectral index, tensor-to-scalar ratio, and running of ths scalar spectral index are expressed as:

n_{s}=1-\frac{M_{Pl}^{2}nm^{n}[(n+2)m^{n}+2(n+1)\phi^{n}]}{(1+2\lambda)\phi^{n}(\phi^{n}+m^{n})^{2}}

(3.12)

r=\frac{8M_{Pl}^{2}n^{2}m^{2n}}{(1+2\lambda)\phi^{2}(\phi^{n}+m^{n})^{2}}

(3.13)

n_{sk}=-\frac{2M_{Pl}^{4}n^{2}m^{2n}[(n+2)m^{2n}+4(n+1)m^{n}\phi^{n}+(n^{2}+3n+2)\phi^{2n}]}{(1+2\lambda)^{2}\phi^{4}(\phi^{n}+m^{n})^{4}}

3.3
Woods-Saxon Inflation

Wood-Saxon inflation is based on a potential that was originally used to describe the nuclear force experienced by the nucleons in the shell model
[
70
]
. However, it has been used in cosmological inflation by Oikonomou et al., since the potential has a flat plateau ideal for slow roll inflation
[
71
]
.

Woods-Saxon potential reads as

V(\phi)=\frac{V_{0}}{1+be^{-a\kappa\phi}}

(3.15)

where
V_{0}
is the depth of the potential with dimension
eV^{4}
. The free parameter
a
has the dimension of
eV
, and
b
is dimensionless. Whereas
\kappa
is a constant.

In GR, Oikonomou et al. showed that the potential meets the
Planck
bound for
b>0
and the varying
a<0
. In addition, the reheating era can be successfully described with the same values of the free parameters. However, the parameter
V_{0}
affects only the reheating era
[
71
]
. Venikoudis et al. showed that in Gauss-Bonnet gravity, the Woods-Saxon potential successfully explain both the inflation and reheating eras
[
72
]
. A similar result was reported by Younesizadeh et al. with a different Gauss-Bonnet coupling function
[
73
]
. In addition, Radhakrishnan et al. reported that the Woods-Saxon model can be considered as a potential alternative to
\Lambda
CDM to explain late-time acceleration
[
74
]
. These results motivate us to explore the theoretical predictions of this potential in
f(R,T)
gravity.

Then the potential slow-roll parameters in
f(R,T)
gravity read as

\epsilon_{V}=\frac{b^{2}c^{2}e^{-2c\kappa\phi}}{2(1+2\lambda)(1+be^{-c\kappa\phi})^{2}}

(3.16)

\eta_{V}=\frac{bc^{2}(b-e^{c\kappa\phi})}{(1+2\lambda)(b+e^{c\kappa\phi})^{2}}

(3.17)

\xi_{V}^{2}=\frac{b^{2}c^{4}(b^{2}-4be^{c\kappa\phi}+e^{2c\kappa\phi})}{(1+2\lambda)^{2}(b+e^{c\kappa\phi})^{4}}

(3.18)

Then the expressions for scalar spectral index, tensor-to-scalar ratio, and running read as

n_{s}=\frac{2be^{c\kappa\phi}(1-c^{2}+2\lambda)+e^{2c\kappa\phi}(1+2\lambda)+b^{2}(1-c^{2}+2\lambda)}{(1+2\lambda)(b+e^{c\kappa\phi})^{2}}

(3.19)

r=\frac{8b^{2}c^{2}e^{-2c\kappa\phi}}{(1+2\lambda)(1+be^{-c\kappa\phi})^{2}}

(3.20)

n_{sk}=-\frac{2b^{2}c^{4}e^{2c\kappa\phi}}{(1+2\lambda)^{2}(b+e^{c\kappa\phi})^{4}}

(3.21)

The trajectory of the potential in the
n_{s}-r
plane is presented in Figure

. To do so,
\kappa
is set to unity. The free parameters
b,c
are very sensitive and are fixed at
b=0.001
and
c=-5,-8

N=60

\lambda

4
Conclusion

Its been five decades, the theoretical framework for inflation is continuously developing to describe the primordial universe, both in general relativity and in modified gravity theories. From scalar fields to higher-dimensional string theory, from super gravity to non-minimal coupling of matter with fields, inflationary dynamics has been explored to meet the observational results. In this study, we performed a detailed numerical analysis of three well-motivated inflationary models, viz. mutated hilltop inflation, D-brane inflation, and Woods-Saxon inflation within
f(R,T)
gravity. The simplest yet most used form
f(R,T)=R+16\pi G\lambda T
is undertaken, and the slow-roll inflation is studied to calculate the scalar spectral index, the tensor-to-scalar ratio, and the running of the scalar spectral index. The recent bounds of
Planck
, BICEP/
Keck
, ACT DR6, and DESI DR2 have been used to confront the theoretical prediction of these three models. In addition, it is also checked whether the models can predict the projected sensitivities of the upcoming CMB probes like LiteBIRD, CMB-S4 etc.

Mutated hilltop Inflation
is found to retain its excellent observational prediction up to
r\sim 10^{-4}
in
f(R,T)
gravity for a suitable choice of the model parameter
\lambda
. Its prediction remains firmly within the CMB
1\sigma
contour of
Planck
+BK18, and in the
2\sigma
region of P-ACT-LB-BK18. The parameter space is calculated as
0.1<\lambda<29
and the running is well within the
Planck
+BK15 range with a small negative value.

D-brane Inflation
arising from the
D3/\overline{D3}
interaction is found to be consistent with the current data. The KKLTI model is considered with specific cases
n=2
and
n=4
. For both large
m=5
and small
m=0.5
limits, these models exhibit moderate tensor signatures
(r\sim 10^{-2}
to
10^{-3})
and predict suppressed negative
n_{sk}\sim 10^{-4}
in
f(R,T)
gravity. The
n=4
trajectory stays in the
1\sigma
contour of
Planck
+BK18 for the large and small
m
limits, while the
n=2
model touches the
1\sigma
boundary. Both models are consistent with the combined bound of
Planck
+BK18, ACT-DR6 and DESI-DR2 at the
1\sigma
confidence level. The parameter space evaluated for each case considering
r<0.056
is summarized in Table
2
.

Woods-Saxon Inflation
, motivated from nuclear shell model, predicts a small tensor-to-scalar ratio
(r\sim 10^{-4})
well below the current detection threshold. The scalar spectral index spans
n_{s}\in[0.964,0.966]
as
\lambda

n_{sk}

10^{-4}

1\sigma

Another important issue is to be mentioned that the vacuum expectation value
(vev)
of the inflaton requires to be sub-
Planckian
to avoid theoretical clashes with particle physics. In the
f(R,T)
gravity framework, it is seen that the inflationary models under study remain consistent with the current CMB bounds at and below the
Planck

(r\sim 10^{-4})
in
f(R,T)
gravity; hence, they will be aligned with future CMB probes such as LiteBIRD, CMB-S4. As a possible extension of this work, one can study different other inflationary models within

Function estimation in the empirical Bayes setting
Benjamin Kang, Yury Polyanskiy and Anzo Teh

Abstract
We study function estimation in the empirical Bayes setting for Poisson and normal means. Specifically, given observations Y i ∼ f ⁡ ( ⋅ , θ i ) Y_{i}\sim f(\cdot;\theta_{i}) with latent parameters θ i ∼ π \theta_{i}\sim\pi , the goal is to estimate 𝔼 π ​ [ ℓ ⁡ ( θ ) | X = x ] \mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\ell(\theta)|X=x] . This task lies between classical deconvolution (recovering the full prior π \pi ), and standard empirical Bayes mean estimation. While the minimax risk for estimating π \pi in the Wasserstein distance is known to decay only logarithmically, we show that estimating certain smooth functions admits dramatically faster rates. In particular, for polynomial functions of degree k k in the Poisson model, we establish a tight bound of Θ ⁡ ( 1 n ​ ( log ⁡ n log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) k + 1 ) \Theta(\frac{1}{n}(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{k+1}) and Θ ⁡ ( 1 n ​ ( log ⁡ n ) 2 ​ k + 1 ) \Theta(\frac{1}{n}(\log n)^{2k+1}) for bounded and subexponential priors, respectively, attainable by estimators mimicking those that achieve optimal regret for the mean estimation problem (Robbins, mininum distance, ERM). Our analysis identifies the approximation-theoretic origin of this improvement: smooth functions can be well-approximated by low-degree polynomials, whereas Lipschitz functions require dense polynomial approximations, incurring a 1 k \frac{1}{k} loss for degree k k polynomial approximation. The results reveal a sharp hierarchy in the difficulty of empirical Bayes problems: ranging from slow, logarithmic deconvolution to near-parametric convergence for smooth posterior functionals, and establish new connections between nonparametric empirical Bayes theory, polynomial approximation, and statistical inverse problems. Finally, we complement our analysis with a lower bound of Ω ⁡ ( 1 n ​ ( log ⁡ n log ⁡ log ⁡ n ) k + 1 ) \Omega(\frac{1}{n}(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{k+1}) (bounded priors) and Ω ⁡ ( 1 n ​ ( log ⁡ n ) k + 1 ) \Omega(\frac{1}{n}(\log n)^{k+1}) (subgaussian priors) for the normal means model.

The EB setup is as follows: latent parameters

For mean estimation (

Main results

2.1
Empirical Bayes task

We formally define the empirical Bayes task.
Recall that
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n}
are i.i.d. from
\pi
,
and
X_{i}\sim f(\cdot;\theta_{i})
from some known conditional distribution
f(\cdot;\theta)
parametrized by
\theta
.
Upon seeing samples
X_{1}^{n}:=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
,
one produces an estimate
\widehat{T}(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})=(\widehat{T}_{1}(X_{1}^{n}),\cdots,\widehat{T}_{n}(X_{1}^{n}))
such that the MSE

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})-\ell(\theta_{i}))^{2}]
is minimized.
If
\pi
is known, the minimizer of this MSE (MMSE) is the Bayes estimator,

\widehat{T}_{\pi,\ell}(X_{1}^{n})=(\widehat{t}_{\pi,\ell}(X_{1}),\cdots,\widehat{t}_{\pi,\ell}(X_{n}))
where
(\widehat{t}_{\pi,\ell}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta)|X=x])
.
In practice, we do not have access to the true distribution
\pi(\cdot)
.
The goal now is to output an approximation
\widehat{T}
of the Bayes estimator.
We evaluate the quality of such an estimator via the
regret
, which captures the difference between the mean squared error of the estimator
\widehat{T}
and the MMSE estimator
\widehat{T}_{\pi,\ell}
.

Definition 1

(mmse of a functional
\ell
)
.

Let the mmse of a prior distribution
\pi
and a functional
\ell
be the expected squared error of the Bayes estimator, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)\overset{\Delta}{=}\min_{\widehat{t}}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[(\widehat{t}(X)-\ell(\theta))^{2}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[(\widehat{t}_{\pi,\ell}(X)-\ell(\theta))^{2}].

Next, we consider the following definition of total regret.

Definition 2

(Total regret)
.

The total regret of an estimator
\widehat{T}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
on a prior
\pi
and functional
\ell
is defined as

\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\widehat{T}_{j}(X_{1}^{n})-\ell(\theta_{j}))^{2}\right]-n\cdot\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)

Correspondingly, the minimax optimal regret of a class
\mathcal{G}
of priors is defined by optimizing
\widehat{T}
’s performance uniformly over all priors in
\mathcal{G}
:

\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})=\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{G}}\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})

Below, whenever
\ell
denotes the
k
-th moment functional, i.e.
\ell(\theta)=\theta^{k}
, we will instead use the subscript
k
in place of
\ell
in both the mmse and regret notation, e.g.
\mathsf{TotRegret}_{k,n}(\mathcal{G})
etc.

Next, we define the classes of priors over which we will bound the regret.
These are priors satisfying certain light-tailedness assumptions (in particular, priors of bounded support).
Regret bound for the mean estimation problem has generally been established under those settings

[
BGR13
,
PW21
,
JPW25
,
JPTW23
]
.
We remind that the class of bounded distributions is defined as

{\mathcal{P}}([a,b])=\{\pi:\pi([a,b])=1\}
;
the class of subgaussian distributions
\mathsf{SubG}(s)=\{\pi:\pi[|\theta|\geq t]\leq 2e^{-t^{2}/2s}\,\,\,\forall t>0\}
;
and the class of subexponential distributions as
\mathsf{SubE}(s)=\{\pi:\pi[|\theta|\geq t]\leq 2e^{-t/s}\,\,\,\forall t>0\}
.

We now describe the models that we will be working on. These are the normal means and Poisson models.
We recall that the normal means model is where
f(\cdot;\theta)\triangleq{\mathcal{N}}(\theta,1)
,
and the Poisson model is where
f(\cdot;\theta)\triangleq\mathrm{Poi}(\theta)
.
We will continue the discussion of the Poisson model in Section
2.2
.

2.2
Poisson model

We now describe certain properties of the Poisson model that we can manipulate.
Recall that marginal distribution
f_{\pi}=\text{Poi}\circ\pi
is given by

\displaystyle f_{\pi}(x)=\int e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{x}}{x!}d\pi(\theta).

(2)

Thanks to the relatively straightforward form of the Tweedie’s formula, the standard estimators can also be formulated rather easily.

2.2.1
Tweedie’s formula

We start the discussion by outlining the Tweedie’s formula for
\ell(\theta)=\theta^{k}
.
(c.f.
[
NT72
, Section 2]
).

\widehat{t}_{\pi,\ell}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\theta^{k}|X=x]=\frac{\int\theta^{k}\left(e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{x}}{x!}\right)d\pi(\theta)}{f_{\pi}(x)}=\frac{(x+1)_{k}\int e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{x+k}}{(x+k)!}d\pi(\theta)}{f_{\pi}(x)}=\frac{(x+1)_{k}f_{\pi}(x+k)}{f_{\pi}(x)}

(3)

where:

(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)=\frac{\Gamma(x+n)}{\Gamma(x)}

(4)

denotes the Pochhammer symbol.

2.2.2
Estimators

Next, we introduce some of the estimators for the Poisson model with the goal of approximating (
3
), as adapted from the mean estimation cases.

Method of moments estimator.

We first introduce a non-empirical Bayes baseline, based on the following observation:
for
X\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\theta)
, we have

\mathbb{E}[X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)]=\theta^{k},

thus suggesting a natural (unbiased) method of moment estimator

\widehat{t}_{\mathsf{MOM},k}(X)=X(X-1)\cdots(X-k+1)=:(X-k+1)_{k}.

Note that for
k=1
this also coincides with the (frequentist’s) maximum likelihood estimator.
By Lehmann–Scheffé theorem, this is the unique minimum variance unbiased estimator (UMVUE) for this Poisson moment estimation problem

[
LC98
, (3.17)]
.

Modified Robbins estimator (
f
-modeling).

This estimator is naturally extended from the Robbins estimator
[
Rob51
,
Rob56
]
. That is, we estimate
f_{\pi}
using the empirical distribution of
X
’s and plugin into Tweedie’s formula (
3
). This way, we obtain estimator

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{Rob},k}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=\left(\frac{(X_{i}+1)_{k}N_{n}(X_{i}+k)}{N_{n}(X_{i})}\right)_{i=1,\cdots,n},\qquad N_{n}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}{\mathbf{1}_{\left\{{X_{j}=x}\right\}}}.

(5)

Minimum distance method (
g
-modeling).

Another approach to EB problems is to first derive an estimator
\widehat{\pi}
of the unknown latent
\pi
and then compute the Bayes estimator
\widehat{T}_{\widehat{\pi},\ell}
. One of the surprises of EB is that despite
\widehat{\pi}
converging very slowly to
\pi

[
MKV+24
, Theorem 6.2]
(due to non-parametric nature of the class
\mathcal{G}
), the large mismatch between
\widehat{\pi}
and
\pi
is almost irrelevant for the computation of the Bayes conditional expectation. In this paper, we consider estimators
\widehat{\pi}
that minimize
d(p_{n}^{\mathsf{emp}}||f_{\widehat{\pi}})
, where
p_{n}^{\mathsf{emp}}
denotes the empirical distribution of sample
X_{1}^{n}
, and
d
is a divergence. That is, we define
\widehat{\pi}_{d}
as

\widehat{\pi}_{d}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\widehat{\pi}}d(p_{n}^{\mathsf{emp}}||f_{\widehat{\pi}})

(6)

The most important special case is
d=D_{KL}
(the Kullback-Leibler divergence
[
PW25
, (2.1)]
), in which case
\widehat{\pi}
becomes the non-parametric maximum likelihood estimator (NPMLE)
[
KW56
]
and we give it a special name:

\widehat{\pi}_{\mathsf{NPMLE}}=\mathop{\rm argmax}_{Q}\prod_{i=1}^{n}f_{Q}(x_{i})

(7)

Then we use the following plugin estimator:

\widehat{T}\triangleq\widehat{T}_{\widehat{\pi}}=(\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi}}(X_{i}))_{i=1,\cdots,n}\qquad\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)=\frac{(x+1)_{k}f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k)}{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)}.

Recall that this approach (based on estimating the distribution of
\theta
) is called
g
-modeling
[
Efr14
]
.

ERM on monotone functions.

The ERM is an idea in statistical learning theory first introduced in
[
Vap91
]
, where an optimal hypothesis is learned by finding the hypothesis with the smallest loss over the empirical data.
In the mean estimation setting, this is first done by
[
BZ23
]
for normal means, and then by
[
JPTW23
]
for Poisson. We now extend the latter algorithm to estimating
\theta^{k}
for the Poisson model.

This is motivated by the following: each component
\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}
of the Bayes estimator
\widehat{T}_{\pi,k}
satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\widehat{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\widehat{t}(X)-\ell(\theta)\right)^{2}\right]=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\widehat{t}}\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{t}(X)^{2}-2\ell(\theta)\widehat{t}(X)\right].

(8)

where the last equality is due to that
\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta)^{2}]
does not depend on the estimator
\widehat{T}
.
For a given
\widehat{T}
we denote
\widehat{t}_{i}(\theta;x):=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})\mid X_{i}=x,\theta_{i}=\theta]

and
\widehat{t}_{i}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})\mid X_{i}=x]
.
Then the cross term can be further decomposed into:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\theta_{i}^{k}\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{i}\sim\pi}[\theta_{i}^{k}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\theta_{n})}[\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})]]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\theta\sim\pi}[\theta^{k}\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\theta)}[\widehat{t}_{i}(\theta;x)]]

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{x}}{x!}\widehat{t}_{i}(\theta;x)\theta^{k}d\pi(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{x+k}}{(x+k)!}(x+1)_{k}\widehat{t}_{i}(\theta;x)d\pi(\theta)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(a)}{=}\int\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}e^{-\theta}\frac{\theta^{x}}{x!}(x-k+1)_{k}\widehat{t}_{i}(\theta;x-k)d\pi(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(X-k+1)_{k}\widehat{t}_{i}(X-k)].

(9)

where (a) follows from shifting the summation to start at
k
and adding in the terms from
0,\dots,k-1
since they are all
0
. Substituting (
2.2.2
) into (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\widehat{t}}R_{\pi,k}(\widehat{t})\qquad R_{\pi,k}(\widehat{t})\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\text{Poi}\circ\pi}\left[\widehat{t}(X)^{2}-2(X-k+1)_{k}\widehat{t}(X-k)\right].

(10)

and in a similar manner, we may define the empirical version
\widehat{R}_{k}:=\widehat{R}_{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},k}
of
R
defined in
(
10
) as follows:

\widehat{R}_{k}(\widehat{t})\triangleq\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\widehat{t}(X)^{2}-2(X-k+1)_{k}\widehat{t}(X-k)\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\widehat{t}(X_{i})^{2}-2(X_{i}-k+1)_{k}\widehat{t}(X_{i}-k)\right]

(11)

Like
[
JPTW23
]
, this motivates finding an estimator
\widehat{T}
that minimizes the empirical version of the objective function in (
10
) over some function class
{\mathcal{F}}
.
We now consider the following monotone class
\mathcal{F}_{\uparrow}
defined as follows:

\mathcal{F}_{\uparrow}=\{\widehat{t}:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}:\widehat{t}(x)\leq\widehat{t}(x+1),\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}

(12)

We then define the ERM estimator as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}(X_{1}^{n})=(\widehat{t}(X_{1}),\cdots,\widehat{t}(X_{n})),\qquad\widehat{t}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{t\in\mathcal{F}_{\uparrow}}\widehat{R}_{k}(t)

(13)

The ERM can be viewed as a ‘remedy’ between the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff between the
f
- and
g
-modeling.
The
f
-modeling technique suffers from multiple numerical instability problems (e.g., when
x
is large and the counts are small, small changes in counts greatly affect the estimated values)
[
Mar68
]
. Furthermore, the Robbin’s estimator may not be monotone, which is often desired
[
vHS83
]
.

g
-modeling technique reconciles this by preserving the Bayesian structure (and hence desired monotonicity and stability of the Bayes estimator).
For this reason,
g
-modeling has historically attracted a lot of attention, see
[
KM14
,
KG24
,
GK22
,
GK23
]
for recent examples.

Despite all of this,
g
-modeling has a major computational issue: solving the optimization problem in (
6
) is pretty challenging even in the considered here case of single-dimensional
\theta_{i}
’s (and essentially impossible for higher dimensional cases as runtime becomes exponential in dimension
[
Lin83
]
).
The asymptotic computational complexity of NPMLE remains poorly understood, and such studies only started recently
[
PS25
]
.

The ERM method preserves regularity by imposing the monotonicity constraint, while escaping the computationally intensive step of prior estimation.
This is partly inspired by some works (c.f.
[
vH77
]
) that impose monotonicity to
f
-modeling estimators without increasing regret.

Regret bound (Poisson)

Theorem 1

(Poisson problem:
\ell
polynomial.)
.

Consider the Poisson model
f(\cdot;\theta)=\mathrm{Poi}(\theta),\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

•

There exist
c_{1}=c_{1}(h,k)>0,c_{2}=c_{2}(h,k)>0
and
n_{0}=n_{0}(k)
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}

c_{1}\left(\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)^{k+1}\right)\leq\mathsf{TotRegret}_{k,n}({\mathcal{P}}([0,h]))\leq c_{2}\left(\left(\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)^{k+1}\right)

c_{3}=c_{3}(h,k)>0,c_{4}=c_{4}(h,k)

such that for all

c_{3}\left((\log n)^{2k+1}\right)\leq\mathsf{TotRegret}_{k,n}(\mathsf{SubE}(s))\leq c_{4}\left((\log n)^{2k+1}\right)

Furthermore, the upper bounds are achieved by any of the following estimators:
\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{Rob},k}
and estimators based on
\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}
and
\widehat{T}_{\widehat{\pi}}
where
\widehat{\pi}
is estimated by minimum distance estimators satisfying
[
JPW25

.

The upper bound in Theorem
1
can be established by analyzing just one estimator.
Instead, we do so for three estimators to show that they are minimax optimal for a more general class of estimation tasks (i.e.
\ell(\theta)=\theta^{k}
), and to show that the techniques developed in prior works can be generalized to our setting. In addition, we illustrate in Fig.

and at which

Next, we generalize Theorem

to obtain an upper bound over a more general class of smooth functions. Here, we remind ourselves that the class

|\ell(\theta_{1})-\ell(\theta_{2})|\leq L|\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}|^{\alpha},\quad\forall\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,1]

(14)

We also consider the analytic function centered around the origin, which is a more restricted form of the class of smooth functions. We say that

\ell(\theta)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\theta^{m}
,
and the radius

R=\sup_{r\geq 0}\{r:\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}r^{m}\text{ converges}\}

Theorem 2

(Poisson problem:
\ell
continuous.)
.

Compound decision theory and empirical Bayes
. Although we focus on the empirical Bayes problems here, it would be appropriate to recall that those problems (and our results) go hand in hand with compound decision theory since the emergence of both in

In our context of functional estimation, we comment on some relations between empirical Bayes and compound decision setting in Appendix

Preliminaries

3.1
Relationship between total regret and individual regret

Besides total regret, we may also define the individual regret as follows,
treating
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n-1}
as training data and our task is to estimate
\ell(\theta_{n})
given the unseen data
X_{n}
.

Definition 3

(Individual Regret)
.

The individual of an estimator
\widehat{T}
on a prior
\pi
and functional
\ell
is defined as

\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})=\mathbb{E}\left[(\widehat{T}_{n}(X_{1}^{n})-\theta_{n})^{2}\right]-\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)

The individual regret of a collection
\mathcal{G}
of priors is the minimax regret achieved by any estimator over the class
\mathcal{G}

\mathsf{Regret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})=\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\pi\in\mathcal{G}}\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})

Similar to
[
PW21
, Lemma 5]
,
we show that the minimax total regret over a prior class is the same as
n
times the minimax individual regret.

Lemma 1
.

For any class
\mathcal{G}
, sample size
n
and functional
\ell
,
we have
n\cdot\mathsf{Regret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})=\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

The proof follows closely the proof of
[
PW21
, Lemma 5]
.
To start with, given any estimator
\widehat{T}
, we define
\widetilde{T}
with
\widetilde{T}(X_{1}^{n})=(\widetilde{T}_{1}(X_{1}^{n}),\cdots,\widetilde{T}_{n}(X_{1}^{n}))
,
where
\widetilde{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})=\widehat{T}_{n}(X_{\backslash i},X_{i})
,
where
X_{\backslash i}=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\cdots,X_{n})
.
Then

\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widetilde{T})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{n})-\ell(\theta_{i}))^{2}]-n\cdot\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[(\widehat{T}_{n}(X_{\backslash i},X_{i})-\ell(\theta_{i}))^{2}]

=n\mathbb{E}[(\widehat{T}_{n}(X_{1}^{n})-\ell(\theta_{n}))^{2}]-n\cdot\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)=n\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})

Therefore by choosing
\widehat{T}
that achieves the minimax total regret, we have
\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})\leq n\mathsf{Regret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})
.

Conversely, given an estimator
\widehat{T}
, consider the following randomized estimator

\widetilde{T}_{n}(X_{1}^{n}):=\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{i-1},X_{n},X_{i+1}^{n-1},X_{i}),\text{ w.p. }\frac{1}{n},i=1,\cdots,n.

Then for each prior
\pi
we have

\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widetilde{T})=\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{T}_{n}(X_{1}^{n})-\ell(\theta_{n}))^{2}]-\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)

=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\widehat{T}_{i}(X_{1}^{i-1},X_{n},X_{i+1}^{n-1},X_{i})-\ell(\theta_{n}))^{2}]-\mathsf{mmse}_{\ell}(\pi)=\frac{1}{n}\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})

and therefore
\mathsf{Regret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})\leq\frac{1}{n}\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})
.
∎

We note that the proof also establishes that an estimator achieving a minimax total regret of
\alpha
(for some
\alpha>0
) can be adapted to produce an estimator achieving a minimax individual regret of
\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and vice versa.

3.3
Properties of Poisson model

Here, we consider a tail bound that essentially pins down the constants as discussed in
[
PW21
, (122, 124)]
:

Lemma 3
.

Consider the Poisson model with mixture density

f_{\pi}\equiv\mathrm{Poi}\circ\pi
.

•

Suppose
\pi\in\mathcal{P}([0,h])
.
Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
for
x_{0}=\max\{h,(c_{1}+c_{2}h)\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\}
we have

\mathbb{P}_{X\sim f_{\pi}}[X\geq x_{0}]=\sum_{x\geq x_{0}}f_{\pi}(x)\leq\frac{1}{n}

Proof of Lemma
3
.

For the case
\mathcal{P}([0,h])
, we use the following Chernoff’s bound,
e.g.
[
PW25
, (15.19)]
on Poisson-Binomial distribution
to estimate the following tail bound for
X\sim\mathrm{Poi}(\lambda)
and for
x\geq h\geq\lambda
:

\mathbb{P}[X\geq x]\leq\frac{(e\lambda)^{x}e^{-\lambda}}{x^{x}}\leq\frac{(eh)^{x}e^{-h}}{x^{x}}

This would mean that for
x_{0}\geq h
we have

\mathbb{P}_{X\sim f_{\pi}}[X\geq x_{0}]\leq\frac{(eh)^{x_{0}}e^{-h}}{{x_{0}}^{x_{0}}}
.

Now setting
x_{0}=c\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}
, we want to find
c
such that the following holds:

c\log n\left(1-\frac{\log\log\log n}{\log\log n}\right)-c\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}(1-\log c+\log h)+h=x_{0}\log x_{0}+h-x_{0}(1+\log h)\geq\log n

Let
L=\sup_{n\geq 3}\frac{\log\log\log n}{\log\log n}<1
,
so by taking

we have

\displaystyle~c\log n\left(1-\frac{\log\log\log n}{\log\log n}\right)-c\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}(1-\log c+\log h)+h

\displaystyle~\geq c\log n(1-L)-c\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}(1-\log(eh)+\log h)+h\geq\log n

as desired.

Lemma 4
.

Fix a prior distribution
G_{0}
, constants

\displaystyle\|r_{q}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq a\quad\forall q

\displaystyle\|Kr_{q}\|_{L_{2}(f_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\gamma}\quad\forall q

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}K_{\ell}r_{i}\right\|_{L_{2}(f_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\tau\|v\|_{2}^{2}-\tau_{2}\quad\forall v\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{m}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{m}v_{i}K_{\ell}r_{i}\right\|_{L_{2}(f_{0})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{1}^{2}m\quad\forall v\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{m}.

Then the optimal regret in
\ell(\theta)
estimation over the class of priors
{\mathcal{G}}=\{G:|\frac{dG}{dG_{0}}-1|\leq\frac{1}{2}\}
satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\pi\in{\mathcal{G}}}\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,k,n}(\widehat{T})\geq C\delta^{2}(m(4\tau-\tau_{1}^{2})-\tau_{2}),\quad\delta\overset{\Delta}{=}\frac{1}{\max(\sqrt{n\gamma},ma)}

for some constant
C>0
.

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of
[
PW21
, Lemma 7]
with appropriate modifications for the case of
\ell(\theta)\neq\theta
.
We start by defining the following hypercubes.
For
i=1,2,\cdots,m
, define
\mu_{i}\triangleq\int r_{i}dG_{0}
,

\delta>0
chosen with
\delta\leq\frac{1}{16ma}
,
and for each

,

r_{u}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}r_{i}\qquad h_{u}\triangleq Kr_{u}\qquad\mu_{u}\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\mu_{i}\qquad dG_{u}\triangleq\frac{1+\delta r_{u}}{1+\delta\mu_{u}}dG_{0}\qquad f_{u}\triangleq\frac{(1+\delta h_{u})f_{0}}{1+\delta\mu_{u}}

where
f_{u}
is the mixture density induced by the prior
G_{u}
.
Now consider the class of priors
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}=\{G_{u}:u\in\{0,1\}^{m}\}
.
The construction guarantees
\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
,
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{dG_{u}}{dG_{0}}\leq\frac{3}{2}
.
Define, also,
T_{u}(x)\triangleq\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}[\ell(\theta)\mid X=x]
.
Then the minimax regret over
\mathcal{G}
satisfies:

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\pi\in{\mathcal{G}}}\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,k,n}(\widehat{T})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\widehat{T}}\max_{\pi\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{G}}}}\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,k,n}(\widehat{T})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\widehat{T}}\max_{u\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}||\widehat{T}-T_{u}||^{2}_{L^{2}(f_{u})}

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(a)}{\geq}\inf_{\widehat{T}}\max_{u\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}||\widehat{T}-T_{u}||^{2}_{L^{2}(f_{0})}

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(b)}{\geq}\min_{\widehat{u}\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\max_{u\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}||T_{\widehat{u}}-T_{u}||^{2}_{L^{2}(f_{0})}

where (a) uses
f_{u}\geq\frac{1}{2}f_{0}
and (b) is due to the following:
given
\widehat{T}
, define
\widehat{u}=\mathop{\rm argmin}_{v}{\left\|T_{v}-\widehat{T}\right\|}_{L_{2}(f_{0})}
.
Then for any
u

{\left\|T_{\widehat{u}}-T_{u}\right\|}_{L_{2}(f_{0})}\leq{\left\|T_{\widehat{u}}-\widehat{T}\right\|}_{L_{2}(f_{0})}+{\left\|\widehat{T}-T_{u}\right\|}_{L_{2}(f_{0})}\leq 2{\left\|\widehat{T}-T_{u}\right\|}_{L_{2}(f_{0})}

from the definition of
\widehat{u}
and by the triangle inequality.

Now we consider the following property of
T_{u}
, due to (
4.1
) and that
Kr=h_{u}

T_{u}=K\ell+\delta K_{\ell}r+\delta^{2}\frac{h_{u}}{1+\delta h_{u}}\cdot K_{\ell}r

and by our assumptions:

||\delta^{2}\frac{h_{u}}{1+\delta h_{u}}\cdot K_{\ell}r||_{2}\leq 2\delta^{2}ma||K_{\ell}r_{u}||_{2}\leq 2\delta^{2}m^{3/2}a\tau_{1}\leq\frac{1}{8}\delta a\sqrt{m}\tau_{1}

Again, by triangle inequality:

||T_{u}-T_{v}||_{2}\geq\delta||K_{\ell}(r_{u}-r_{v})||_{2}-\frac{\delta\sqrt{m}\tau_{1}}{4}

Thus by using

The next step is to bound the

and

Note that:

\frac{f_{u}}{f_{0}}-\frac{f_{v}}{f_{0}}=\frac{1+\delta h_{u}}{1+\delta\mu_{u}}-\frac{1+\delta h_{v}}{1+\delta\mu_{v}}=\delta\frac{h_{u}-h_{v}}{1+\delta\mu_{u}}+(1+\delta h_{v})\frac{\delta(\mu_{v}-\mu_{u})}{(1+\delta\mu_{u})(1+\delta\mu_{v})}

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(f_{u}||f_{v})

\displaystyle=\int d\mu f_{0}\frac{(f_{u}/f_{0}-f_{v}/f_{0})^{2}}{f_{v}/f_{0}}

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}\int d\mu f_{0}\frac{1+\delta\mu_{v}}{1+\delta h_{v}}\left(\frac{h_{u}-h_{v}}{1+\delta\mu_{u}}+(1+\delta h_{v})\frac{\mu_{v}-\mu_{u}}{(1+\delta\mu_{u})(1+\delta\mu_{v})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\delta^{2}{\left\|h_{u}-h_{v}\right\|}_{2}^{2}+\delta^{2}(\mu_{v}-\mu_{u})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\delta^{2}{\left\|h_{u}-h_{v}\right\|}_{2}^{2}

\mu_{u}-\mu_{v}=\int dG_{0}(r_{u}-r_{v})=\int d\mu f_{0}(h_{u}-h_{v})\leq{\left\|h_{u}-h_{v}\right\|}_{2}.

By our assumption,
for all

{\left\|h_{u}-h_{v}\right\|}^{2}\leq\gamma
.

Therefore, provided

\chi^{2}(f_{u}^{\otimes n}||f_{v}^{\otimes n})=(1+\chi^{2}(f_{u}||f_{v}))^{n}-1=(1+\delta^{2}\gamma)^{n}-1\leq e^{n\delta^{2}\gamma}-1\leq e-1

.
Thus by Assouad’s lemma

\max_{u\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}[d_{H}(\widehat{u},u)]\geq C_{3}m

Therefore, by choosing

\delta^{2}=\frac{1}{\max(n\gamma,m^{2}a^{2})}
, we have:

\displaystyle\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\pi\in{\mathcal{G}}}\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,k,n}(\widehat{T})

\displaystyle\geq\inf{\widehat{u}}\max_{u\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{1}{8}\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}||T_{\widehat{u}}-T_{u}||^{2}_{L^{2}(f_{0})}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\widehat{u}}\max_{u\in\{0,1\}^{m}}\frac{1}{16}\delta^{2}(\tau\mathbb{E}_{G_{u}}[d_{H}(\widehat{u},u)]-\tau_{2})-\frac{1}{128}\delta^{2}m\tau_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\delta^{2}(\tau C_{3}m-\tau_{2})-\frac{1}{128}\delta^{2}m\tau_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16}\delta^{2}(m(C_{3}\tau-\frac{1}{8}\tau_{1}^{2})-\tau_{2})

as desired.
∎

(x+1)_{k}\leq\max\{(2k)^{k},2^{k}x^{k}\}

depending on whether
x\leq k
,
and (b) is due to Lemma
14
.
Now the
\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi,k,n}(\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{Rob},k})-\mathsf{Regret}_{\pi_{h},k,n}(\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{Rob},k})
is
\sqrt{n^{-9}(L+M)}
as per Lemma
15
.
We have
M=8k(4k-1)!s^{4k}
,
and we may take
L=2^{4k}n^{4}[k^{4k}+8k(4k-1)!s^{4k}]
.
It follows that the extra contribution to the regret is bounded by

\sqrt{n^{-5}2^{4k}[k^{4k}+8k(4k-1)!s^{4k}]}\lesssim\frac{(2s+1)^{2k}(4k)!}{n^{2}}
,
which is
o_{s,k}(1/n)

(note the use of the fact that
k^{4k}\leq(4k)!
due to Stirling’s inequality).

∎

5.2
Minimum distance method

In this section, we extend the NPMLE algorithm first introduced in
[
KW56
]
to estimate
\theta^{k}
. This extension is very natural, as the estimation of the prior distribution remains the same. Using this estimated prior, we calculate its empirical Bayes estimator by applying (
3
).

Before proving the main results, we first discuss prior estimation via NPMLE. This method is a specific instance of the more general class of minimum distance estimators. These estimators are defined by a measure of distance between two distributions. There are many possibilities of distance functions, so we will focus on a specific set of functions which we call
Generalized distance functions
.

Definition 4

(
generalized distance functions
)
.

A function
d:{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})\times{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
d(p\parallel q)\geq 0
with equality true if and only if
p=q
.

Note that this includes any metrics and divergence. Then a minimum distance estimator with respect to
d
over a set of distributions
{\mathcal{G}}
is

\widehat{\pi}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\pi\in{\mathcal{G}}}d(p_{n}^{\mathsf{emp}}\parallel f_{\pi}).

In
[
JPW25
]
, they describe some specific examples of these distance functions that correspond with well-known estimators, including the NPMLE estimator we focus on:

•

The NPMLE estimator corresponds to the KL-divergence
d(p\parallel q)=D(p\parallel q)=\mathbb{E}\left[\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\right].

•

The Minimum-Hellinger estimator corresponds to the squared Hellinger distance
d(p\parallel q)=H^{2}(p,q)=\sum\left(\sqrt{p(x)}-\sqrt{q(x)}\right)^{2}.

•

The Minimum-
\chi^{2}
estimator corresponds to the
\chi^{2}
-divergence
d(p\parallel q)=\chi^{2}(p\parallel q)=\sum\frac{(p(x)-q(x))^{2}}{q(x)}.

These estimators have their generalized distance metric satisfying Assumptions 1 and 2 in
[
JPW25
]
:

Assumption 1
.

There exists a map
\varphi:{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})\to\mathbb{R}
and
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for any two distributions
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
,

d(p\parallel q)=\varphi(p)+\sum_{x\geq 0}\ell(p(x),q(x))

where
\ell(a,b)
is strictly decreasing and convex in
b
for
a>0
and
\ell(0,b)=0
for
b\geq 0
.

Assumption 2
.

There exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that for
p,q\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})
,

c_{1}H^{2}(p,q)\leq d(p\parallel q)\leq c_{2}\chi^{2}(p\parallel q).

The KL-divergence satisfies these assumptions, so the following theorem applies to the NPMLE estimator as well. The main result of this section is that a minimum distance estimator satisfies the following regret bounds:

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
d
satisfies Assumptions 1 and 2. For a fixed
h
and
s

1.

5.3
ERM method

The first step is to show that the Bayes estimator,
\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}
, is monotone,
which allows us to take the ERM w.r.t. the monotone function class
\mathcal{F}_{\uparrow}
.

Lemma 17
.

\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}
is a monotone function.

Proof.

By Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we have

\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+2}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+1}d\pi(\theta)}\geq\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+1}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x}d\pi(\theta)},\qquad\forall x=0,1,2,\cdots.

Iterating this inequality
k
times, we get

\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+k+1}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+k}d\pi(\theta)}\geq\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+1}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x}d\pi(\theta)}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+k+1}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+1}d\pi(\theta)}\geq\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+k}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x}d\pi(\theta)}

Thus, we find that

\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}(x+1)=\frac{(x+2)_{k}f_{\pi}(x+k+1)}{f_{\pi}(x+1)}=\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+k+1}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+1}d\pi(\theta)}\geq\frac{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x+k}d\pi(\theta)}{\int e^{-\theta}\theta^{x}d\pi(\theta)}=\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}(x)\,,

as desired.
∎

Now we recall our ERM-based estimator as defined in (
13
).
Note that there is no unique solution to this minimization problem since there are
\widehat{T}
is only uniquely determined for values that appear in our empirical expectation; we choose to take
\widehat{t}
which is a step function that can only change at values where it is determined, and also where

for all

.
An explicit solution to (

[
JPTW23
, Lemma 1]
. We now show that our empirical estimator is always bounded by

, which will help us bound the complexity of our function class.

Lemma 18
.

Let
\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}
be the estimator defined in (
13

\max\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}(x)\leq X_{\max}^{k}.

Proof.

As
\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}
is monotonic, and
\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}(x)=\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}(X_{\max})
for all

, it is sufficient to bound
\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}(X_{\max})
. By
[
JPTW23
, Lemma 1]
, there exists some
i^{*}
for which

\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k}(X_{\max})=\frac{\sum_{i=i^{*}}^{X_{\max}}(i+1)_{k}N(i+k)}{\sum_{i=i^{*}}^{X_{\max}}N(i)}=\frac{\sum_{i=i^{*}+k}^{X_{\max}}(i-k+1)_{k}N(i)}{\sum_{i=i^{*}}^{X_{\max}}N(i)}\leq(X_{\max}-k+1)_{k}\leq X_{\max}^{k}

since

for all

∎

Definition 5

\displaystyle\widehat{T}_{\mathsf{erm},k,[a,b]}(X_{1}^{n})=(\widehat{t}(X_{1}),\cdots,\widehat{t}(X_{n})),\qquad\widehat{t}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\widehat{t}\in\mathcal{F}_{\uparrow,[a,b]}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\widehat{t}(X_{i})^{2}-2(X_{i}-k+1)_{k}\widehat{t}(X_{i}-k)\right]

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{h}}[(\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi},k}(X)-\widehat{t}_{\pi_{h},k}(X))^{2}]+\frac{(1+2\sqrt{2})\sqrt{(\widehat{h}^{4k}+M)\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(\theta>h)}}{\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(\theta\leq h)}

(81)

where (a) follows from
\mathsf{mmse}_{k}(\pi)\leq\sqrt{M}
.
The second term is already in the right form, so we bound the first term.
We use the following observation:

\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}-\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}=\left(\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}-\frac{2a_{1}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}\right)+\left(\frac{2a_{1}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}-\frac{2a_{2}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}\right)+\left(\frac{2a_{2}}{b_{1}+b_{2}}-\frac{a_{2}}{b_{2}}\right)

,

,

a_{2}=f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)
,
b_{2}=f_{\pi_{h}}(x)
.
Therefore, for

,

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{h}}[(\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi},k}(X)-\widehat{t}_{\pi_{h},k}(X))^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X\leq K-k}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}(x+1)_{k}^{2}f_{\pi_{h}}(x)\left(\frac{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k)}{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)}-\frac{f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)}{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(a)}{\leq}

\displaystyle~3\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}(x+1)_{k}^{2}f_{\pi_{h}}(x)\left(\frac{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k)^{2}(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)^{2}(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}+4\frac{(f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k))^{2}}{(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)^{2}(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)^{2}(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(b)}{\leq}

\displaystyle~3\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}f_{\pi_{h}}(x)(\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}(x)^{2}+\widehat{t}_{\pi_{h},k}(x)^{2})\frac{(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}{(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}+12\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}\frac{(x+1)_{k}^{2}(f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k))^{2}}{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)}

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(c)}{\leq}

\displaystyle~3(h^{2k}+\widehat{h}^{2k})\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}f_{\pi_{h}}(x)\frac{(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}{(f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x))^{2}}+12\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}\frac{(x+1)_{k}^{2}(f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)-f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k))^{2}}{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)}

for real numbers

,
(b) is using

and that

\frac{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)}{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)+f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)}<1
.
and (c) using
f_{\pi_{h}}\leq h^{k}
and
f_{\widehat{\pi}}\leq\widehat{h}^{k}
.

,

(x+1)_{k}\leq K^{k}
for

, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{h}}[(\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi},k}(X)-\widehat{t}_{\pi_{h},k}(X))^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X\leq K-k}]

\displaystyle 6(h^{2k}+\widehat{h}^{2k})\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}(\sqrt{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)}-\sqrt{f_{\pi_{h}}(x)})^{2}

\displaystyle+24K^{k}\max_{x\leq K-k}\frac{(x+1)_{k}f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k)+(x+1)_{k}f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)}{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x)+f_{\pi_{h}}(x)}\sum_{x=0}^{K-k}\left(\sqrt{f_{\widehat{\pi}}(x+k)}-\sqrt{f_{\pi_{h}}(x+k)}\right)^{2}

where TV is the total variation, the middle inequality is from the data processing inequality,

is justified in
[
JPW25
, Appendix B]

Combining this with (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{h}}[(\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi},k}(X)-\widehat{t}_{\pi_{h},k}(X))^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X\leq K-k}]\leq\left(12(h^{2k}+\widehat{h}^{2k})+48K^{k}(h^{k}+\widehat{h}^{k})\right)\left(H^{2}(f_{\widehat{\pi}},p_{\pi})+4\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(\theta>h)\right).

We can also bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{h}}[(\widehat{t}_{\widehat{\pi},k}(X)-\widehat{t}_{\pi_{h},k}(X))^{2}\mathbf{1}_{X>K-k}]\leq(h^{k}+\widehat{h}^{k})^{2}\mathbb{P}_{f_{\pi_{h}}}(X>K-k)=(h^{k}+\widehat{h}^{k})^{2}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{f_{\pi}}(X>K-k)}{\mathbb{P}_{\pi}(\theta\leq h)}.

Using

C.2

∎

\displaystyle~\frac{c(h^{k}\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)}M^{k}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X_{\max}^{2k}r(X_{\max})^{2}]}+\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)c_{2}(k,2)}M^{k}L)}{n^{2}}

(92)

where (a) follows from
\sum_{x=0}^{X_{\max}-k}N(x+k)\leq\sum_{x=0}^{\infty}N(x)=n
, and
(b) follows from Cauchy-Schwarz,
(c) follows from

and (d) uses
\mathbb{E}[X^{2k}_{\max}r(X^{\prime}_{\max})^{2}]=\mathbb{E}[X^{2k}_{\max}]\mathbb{E}[r(X^{\prime}_{\max})^{2}]
due to the independence of

X_{\max}
and

For each

q_{\pi,M}(x)=\frac{f_{\pi}(x)}{\mathbb{P}_{X\sim f_{\pi}}[X\leq M]}

\mathbb{P}[N(x)=0|A]=(1-q_{\pi,M}(x))^{n}

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x=0}^{X_{\max}-k}2(x+1)_{k}(h^{k}+r(X_{\max})+r(X_{\max}^{\prime}))N(x+k)\mathbf{1}_{N(x)=0}\mathbf{1}_{A}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x=0}^{X_{\max}-k}2(x+1)_{k}(h^{k}+r(X_{\max})+r(X_{\max}^{\prime}))N(x+k)\mathbf{1}_{N(x)=0}\bigg|A\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{x=0}^{M-k}2(x+1)_{k}(h^{k}+2r(M))\mathbb{E}\left[N(x+k)\mathbf{1}_{N(x)=0}|A\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{x=0}^{M-k}2(x+1)_{k}(h^{k}+2r(M))\mathbb{E}\left[N(x+k)|N(x)=0,A\right]\mathbb{P}[N(x)=0|A]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\sum_{x=0}^{M-k}2(x+1)_{k}(h^{k}+2r(M))\frac{nq_{\pi,M}(x+k)}{1-q_{\pi,M}(x)}(1-q_{\pi,M}(x))^{n}

\displaystyle\overset{\rm(a)}{=}

\displaystyle~\sum_{x=0}^{M-k}2(h^{k}+2r(M))\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}(x)nq_{\pi,M}(x)(1-q_{\pi,M}(x))^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~2Mh^{k}(h^{k}+2r(M))

(93)

where (a) is because
\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}(x)\leq h
for all
x
and

nw(1-w)^{n-1}\leq(1-\frac{1}{n})^{n-1}<1
for all
w\in[0,1]
.
Summing (
C.3
) and (
C.3
) and continuing (

), we have

J_{0}(n)\leq\frac{c(h^{k}\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)}M^{k}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X_{\max}^{2k}r(X_{\max})^{2}]}+\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)c_{2}(k,2)}M^{k}L)}{n^{2}}+2Mh^{k}(h^{k}+2r(M))

and combining with (

\displaystyle R_{2}(b,n)

\displaystyle\leq~h^{2k}(1+M)+k!(4h^{k}+e)+2^{k}h^{k}M^{k+1}+2Mh^{k}(h^{k}+2r(M))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c(h^{k}\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)}M^{k}+\sqrt{c_{1}(4k)}M^{2k}+\sqrt{c_{2}(k,4)}L^{2})}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim~h^{2k}(2^{k}+c_{1}(1)M)+(2h)^{k}M^{k+1}+Mh^{k}r(M)+k!(h^{k}+1)

\displaystyle+\frac{h^{k}\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)}M^{k}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X_{\max}^{2k}r(X_{\max})^{2}]}+\sqrt{c_{1}(2k)c_{2}(k,2)}M^{k}L}{n^{2}}

Proof of Lemma
27
.

Set
m_{b}=b+1
, and recall the definition of
\epsilon(x)
at (
83
).
Then for each
\widehat{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\epsilon_{i}-\frac{1}{b}\right)(\widehat{t}_{\mathsf{oracle}}(X_{i})-\widehat{t}(X_{i}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{x\geq 0,N(x)>0}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{N(x)}{b}\right)(\widehat{t}_{\mathsf{oracle}}(x)-\widehat{t}(x))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~m_{b}^{2}h^{2}\sum_{x=0}^{X_{\max}}\max\{\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{b}N(x),0\}+\sum_{x=0}^{X_{\max}}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{b}N(x)\right)(\widehat{t}_{\mathsf{oracle}}(x)-\widehat{t}(x))^{2}{\mathbf{1}_{\left\{{\widehat{t}(x)>m_{b}h}\right\}}}

(94)

By
[
JPTW23
, Lemma 5]
, we may bound the expectation of the first term by

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x=0}^{X_{\max}}\max\{\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{b}N(x),0\}\right]\leq N_{b}(1+X_{\max})\qquad N_{b}\triangleq\frac{1-\frac{1}{b}}{e\cdot D(\frac{1+\frac{1}{b}}{2}||\frac{1}{2})}

Now if
\widehat{h}\leq m_{b}h
then the second term is 0 and we are done.
Otherwise, assume
\widehat{h}>m_{b}h
.
To bound the second term,
for
x
satisfying

[m_{b}h,\widehat{h}]
, we have
\frac{m_{b}-1}{m_{b}}\widehat{t}(x)\leq\widehat{t}(x)-\widehat{t}_{\mathsf{oracle}}(x)\leq\widehat{t}(x)
.
Therefore, we may define
g:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
as

g(x)=\max\left\{\left(x-\frac{1}{b}\right),\left(\frac{m_{b}-1}{m_{b}}\right)^{2}\left(x-\frac{1}{b}\right)\right\},

and have the following inequality

(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{b}N(x))(\widehat{t}(x)-\widehat{t}_{\pi,k}(x))^{2}\leq g\left(\frac{\epsilon(x)}{N(x)}\right)N(x)\widehat{t}(x)^{2}\leq\frac{2b^{2}-1}{2b(b+1)}\left(\frac{\epsilon(x)}{N(x)}-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}\right)N(x)\widehat{t}(x)^{2}

with the right inequality due to that

is convex and
\frac{\epsilon(x)}{N(x)}\in[-1,1]
,
therefore bounded by the line connecting the two endpoints
(-1,-\frac{b}{b+1}),(1,\frac{b-1}{b})
.
In addition, given the monotonicity of
\widehat{t}
,
there exists
v
such that
\widehat{t}(x)>m_{b}h^{k}
if and only if
x\geq v
. Therefore, we continue from the display to get the bound

\sum_{x:N(x)>0}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{b}N(x)\right)(\widehat{t}_{\mathsf{oracle}}(x)-\widehat{t}(x))^{2}{\mathbf{1}_{\left\{{\widehat{t}(x)>m_{b}h}\right\}}}\leq c_{3}(b)\sum_{x=v}^{X_{\max}}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)\widehat{t}(x)^{2}.

with
c_{3}(b)=\frac{2b^{2}-1}{2b(b+1)}
.
Fixing
v
, the optimization problem of taking the supremum can be viewed as an
X_{\max}-v+1
-th dimensional linear programming problem with unknowns being the values
\widehat{t}(v)^{2},\cdots,\widehat{t}(X_{\max})^{2}
. The set of solutions
m_{b}^{2}h^{2}\leq\widehat{t}(v)^{2}\leq\dots\leq\widehat{t}(X_{\max})^{2}\leq\widehat{h}^{2}
is convex, so the optimum value must occur at one of the corners,
i.e. there is a
w

\widehat{t}(x)=m_{b}h
for
x\leq w
and
\widehat{t}(x)=\widehat{h}
for

. Therefore,

\displaystyle~\sup_{\widehat{t}}\sum_{x=v}^{X_{\max}}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)\widehat{t}(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sup_{w}(m_{b}h)^{2}\sum_{x=v}^{w}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)+\widehat{h}^{2}\sum_{x=w+1}^{X_{\max}}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)

\displaystyle~\sup_{w}\widehat{h}^{2}\sum_{x=w+1}^{X_{\max}}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)

where (a) is due to that
\sum_{x=v}^{w}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)\leq 0
, otherwise the objective value would increase by
\widehat{t}(x)=\widehat{h}
for all
v
.
By
[
JPTW23
, Lemma 6]
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{w\geq 0}\left\{\sum_{x>w}^{X_{\max}}\left(\epsilon(x)-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}N(x)\right)\right\}\right]\leq\sup_{w:0\leq w\leq n}(\epsilon_{w+1}+\dots+\epsilon_{n})-\frac{1}{2b^{2}-1}(n-w)\leq c

Thus combining the bounds for two terms give

Appendix D
Compound decision problem

A related setting is the compound decision problem,
where the hidden parameters
\theta_{1}^{n}:=(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{n})
are not random but drawn deterministically.
More specifically, one draws
(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{n})
according to
\pi_{\theta_{1}^{n}}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{\theta_{i}}
.
Each observation
X_{i}
is then generated through
f(\cdot;\nu_{i})
.

Similarly to the mean estimation problem, there are two choices of oracle estimators for
\ell(\theta)
. Both are of the form

\mathop{\rm argmin}_{\widehat{T}}[||\widehat{T}(X_{1}^{n})-\ell(\theta_{1}^{n})||^{2}]

The oracle based on
simple
decision criterion has
\widehat{T}
optimized over the class

\mathcal{D}^{S}_{n}:=\{\widehat{T}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n};\widehat{t}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R};\widehat{T}(X_{1}^{n})=(\widehat{t}(X_{1}),\cdots,\widehat{t}(X_{n}))\}

Note that such optimal
\widehat{t}
satisfies
\widehat{t}(x)=\mathbb{E}[\ell(\theta)|X=x]
, i.e. is also the Bayes estimator.
The oracle based on the
permutation invariant
criterion, first introduced in
[
GR09
]
, has
\widehat{T}
optimized over the class

\mathcal{D}^{PI}_{n}:=\{\widehat{T}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n};\forall\sigma\in S_{n}:\widehat{T}(\sigma(X_{1}^{n}))=\sigma(\widehat{T}(X_{1}^{n}))\}

i.e., the input and output under
\widehat{T}
is permuted in the same way.

Denote
r^{S}_{\theta_{1}^{n}}
and
r^{PI}_{\theta_{1}^{n}}
as the risks incurred by the simple and permutation invariant oracles,
respectively.
Then we may similarly define the total regret of a permutation invariant
\widehat{T}
as:

\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,\theta_{1}^{n}}^{S}(\widehat{T})=\mathbb{E}[||\widehat{T}(X_{1}^{n})-\theta_{1}^{n}||_{2}^{2}]-r^{S}(\theta)

\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,\theta_{1}^{n}}^{PI}(\widehat{T})=\mathbb{E}[||\widehat{T}(X_{1}^{n})-\theta_{1}^{n}||_{2}^{2}]-r^{PI}(\theta)

Since simple decision oracles are also permutation invariant,
r^{S}\geq r^{PI}
and therefore

\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\pi,\ell,\theta^{n}}^{S}\leq\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\pi,\ell,\theta^{n}}^{PI}
.
On the other hand,
[
GR09
, Corollary 5.2]
shows that in the mean estimation problem of the normal means model, the gap between
r^{S}
and
r^{PI}
is
O(1)
under suitable conditions. In our moment estimation problem under normal means,
an explicit bound can be further quantified by considering the channel
\theta\to\mathcal{N}(\theta^{1/k},1)
, and utilizing a recent result in
[
HNW24
, Lemma 2.5]
to obtain a bound of

r^{S}-r^{PI}\leq C\cdot\frac{h^{2k}\cdot(e^{4h^{2}}-1)(1+h)}{n}

for some absolute constant
C
whenever
\theta_{1}^{n}\in[0,h]^{n}
.

In a similar way, we may define the minimax total regret over a class
\mathcal{G}
of priors as

\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,n}^{S}(\mathcal{G})=\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\theta_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,\theta^{n}}^{S}(\widehat{T})

\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,n}^{PI}(\mathcal{G})=\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\theta_{1}^{n}\in\mathcal{G}^{\otimes n}}\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,\theta^{n}}^{PI}(\widehat{T})

Unlike empirical Bayes minimax total regret, much less is known about the upper bounds on total regret in the compound decision problem.
For the normal means model, the best upper bound of
\mathsf{TotRegretComp}
is
O(\frac{\log^{4.5}n}{\log\log^{1.5}n})
, achieved by
[
JZ09
, Lemma 1]
and by bounding the Hellinger rate of estimating Gaussian mixtures,
which is of polylog factor away from the lower bound.
For the Poisson model, the only upper bound established is for heavy-tailed setting in
[
SW22
, Appendix F]
,
which matches the corresponding lower bound up to polylog factors.
Although one may adapt it to the setting where
\theta_{1}^{n}\in[0,h]^{n}
,
the bound is likely suboptimal, with extra logarithmic factors.

On the other hand,
[
PW21
, Corollary 3]
provides a way to establish a compound decision minimax regret lower bound using an empirical Bayes minimax regret lower bound. We now establish an important corollary for moment estimation in the compound estimation setting.

Corollary 28
.

When
\ell(\theta):=\theta^{k}
, the minimax total regret over
\mathcal{P}([0,h])
has a lower bound of
\Omega\left((\frac{\log n}{\log\log n})^{k+1}\right)
in both the Poisson and normal means models.

Proof of Corollary
28
.

We mimic the idea of
[
PW21
, Proposition 3]
that establishes

\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,n}^{S}(\mathcal{G})\geq\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\ell,n}(\mathcal{G})

whenever
\ell:=\theta^{k}

Next,
[
WV10
, Corollary 1]
says that the function
G\to\mathsf{mmse}(G)
is concave, when applied to channels that are either of the form

\theta\to\mathcal{N}(\theta^{1/k},1)
or
\theta\to\mathsf{Poi}(\theta^{1/k})
.
This gives, for each
G
,
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{1}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}G}\mathsf{mmse}(G_{\theta_{1}^{n}})\leq\mathsf{mmse}(G)
,
and therefore for each
\widehat{T}
and each
G
we have

\mathsf{TotRegret}_{\pi,\ell,n}(\widehat{T})\leq\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{1}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{i.i.d.}}}{{\sim}}\pi}[\mathsf{TotRegretComp}_{\ell,\theta_{1}^{n}}^{S}(\widehat{T})]

Therefore, the claim is established by taking
\inf_{\widehat{T}}\sup_{\pi}
on both sides.
∎

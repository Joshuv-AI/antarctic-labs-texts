Semidefinite hierarchies for diagonal unitary invariant bipartite quantum states
Jonas Britz, Monique Laurent

Abstract
We investigate questions about the cone SEP n \text{\rm SEP}_{n} of separable bipartite states, consisting of the Hermitian matrices acting on ℂ n ⊗ ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n} that can be written as conic combinations of rank one matrices of the form x ​ x ∗ ⊗ y ​ y ∗ xx^{*}\otimes yy^{*} with x , y ∈ ℂ n x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n} . Bipartite states that are not separable are said to be entangled. Detecting quantum entanglement is a fundamental task in quantum information and a hard computational problem. We explore the Doherty-Parrilo-Spedaglieri (DPS) hierarchy of semidefinite conic approximations for SEP n \text{\rm SEP}_{n} when the bipartite states have some additional structural properties: first, (i) for states with diagonal unitary invariance, and second (ii) for states with Bose symmetry. In case (i) we show that the DPS hierarchy can be block diagonalized, which, combining with its moment reformulation, leads to a substantially more efficient implementation. In case (ii), we give a characterization of the dual hierarchy, in terms of sums of squares of Hermitian complex polynomials, extending a known result in the generic case. It turns out that the completely positive cone CP n \text{\rm CP}_{n} , its dual cone COP n \text{\rm COP}_{n} , and their sums-of-squares based conic approximations 𝒦 n ( t ) {\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n} , play a central role in these two settings (i),(ii). We clarify these connections and test the block diagonal relaxations on classes of examples.

\displaystyle\text{\rm CP}_{n}=\text{\rm conv}\{xx^{T}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}.

Bose symmetric bipartite states

Let
\text{\rm BS}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})\subseteq\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote the set of
Bose symmetric
(or simply

(\rho_{AB})_{ij,kl}=(\rho_{AB})_{ij,lk}\text{ for all }i,j,k,l\in[n].

Hence, any

leaves

\text{\rm BS}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})\simeq\mathrm{Herm}(S^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
. Clearly, any state

is Bose symmetric and, in fact, these states generate the cone
\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n}
, defined as the set of separable Bose symmetric states:

\displaystyle\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n}:=\text{\rm SEP}_{n}\cap\text{\rm BS}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})=\text{\rm cone}\{xx^{*}\otimes xx^{*}:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\}.

(14)

(See Lemma

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm LDOI}_{n}\cap\text{\rm BS}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})\Longleftrightarrow\\
&\rho_{AB}=\rho_{(X,Y,X)}\text{ for some }X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n},Y\in\mathrm{Herm}^{n}\text{ with }\text{\rm diag}(X)=\text{\rm diag}(Y).\end{split}

(15)

We will give a class of examples in Section
4.3
as an illustration for the case

.

Our contributions and organization of the paper

The above connections suggest the following natural questions:

\bullet
Is it possible to adapt the DPS hierarchy in order to test separability of CLDUI (or LDUI, LDOI) bipartite quantum states in a more economical way?

\bullet
Is there a link between the DPS hierarchy for separable bipartite states and the well-known hierarchy of semidefinite relaxations for the cone
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
(and its dual, the copositive cone
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
)?

These questions form the main motivation for our work. We address the first question in Section
3
and the second one in Section
4
.

In Section
3
we show that the sparsity structure of CLDUI (LDUI, LDOI) states can be transported to every level of the DPS hierarchy. In a nutshell, we show that the matrices involved in the semidefinite program modeling the DPS relaxation at any given level
t
inherit the sparsity structure of the original bipartite state (Theorem
3.1
) and, moreover, that they enjoy a block-diagonal structure (Theorem
3.9
). As an application, solving these semidefinite programs is much more efficient, which makes it possible to compute the relaxations at higher order.
In Corollary
3.11
, we give an estimate on the size of the matrices involved in the semidefinite programs modeling the level
t
DPS relaxation in the tensor setting and, in Lemma
5.4
, we indicate the maximum block size when reformulating the relaxation in the moment framework. We also refer to Tables
4
,
5
,
6
that show the comparative explicit matrix sizes for small
n
and
t
(when reformulating the SDP in the polynomial optimization framework). We report about computational experiments in Section
5.3
.

It turns out that the second question – about links between the hierarchy of conic approximations for
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
and the DPS hierarchy – was considered in the recent paper
[
GNP25
]
, of which we learned while completing the present manuscript. We revisit this question in
Section
4
, where we additionally offer some sharpenings and more compact arguments. The analysis relies in a crucial manner on the dual objects, the dual cone of
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
and the copositive cone
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
that tie naturally with positive polynomials and sums of squares. In particular, we show a characterization of the dual of the variation (
31
) of the DPS hierarchy adapted to Bose symmetric states (Theorem
4.1
), which fully mirrors an earlier result of Fang and Fawzi
[
FF20
]
for general bipartite states. Based on this characterization, one can then easily establish a correspondence between the hierarchy of inner conic approximations
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
for the copositive cone
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
, and the dual of the symmetry-adapted DPS hierarchy (Theorem
4.4
), thereby essentially recovering a recent result of
[
GNP25
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the main concepts and provide background facts on the main players in the paper, including the cones
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
,
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
,
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
,
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
and their duals, and we review basic properties of bipartite states with diagonal unitary invariance. In Section
3
, we investigate how to exploit the sparsity structure of CLDUI (LDUI, LDOI) states in order to design an adapted, more economical variation of the DSP hierarchy; we show that this leads to a reformulation involving matrices with a block-diagonal structure. Section
4
is devoted to Bose symmetric bipartite states; we first give a characterization of the dual symmetry-adapted DPS hierarchy, which we then use to establish a correspondence between the hierarchy of cones
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
and the symmetry-adapted DPS hierarchy.
In Section
5
we discuss several issues regarding the practical implementation of the DPS hierarchy. We first indicate how to reformulate it in the ‘moment setting’, which is the right setting permitting to remove the redundancies that are inherent to the ‘tensor setting’ in which the DPS hierarchy is originally defined. This applies to general bipartite states, as well as to bipartite states with some diagonal unitary invariance. After that, we report on numerical experiments that have been carried out on classes of bipartite states with LDOI sparsity structure, motivated by the so-called PPT
2
conjecture from quantum information theory.

(i_{1},i_{2})\in[m_{1}]\times[m_{2}]

Given

\displaystyle A\circ uu^{*}=\text{\rm Diag}(u)A\text{\rm Diag}(u)^{*}\ \text{ for any }u\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Given

,

We will deal with bipartite states, i.e., matrices that act on a tensor product space
\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}

\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
can be identified with
\mathbb{C}^{n^{2}}

\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})_{+}
(instead of

\mathrm{Herm}^{n^{2}}_{+}
) when it is important to stress the tensor product structure. This notation will be especially useful when considering states that act
on a tensor product space of the form

(for some

). Then, it is convenient (and common in quantum information) to refer to the various copies of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as the

Consider the tensor space
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t}
(for

A
t

is indexed by sequences
\underline{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{t})\in[n]^{t}

\underline{i}=i_{1}\ldots i_{t}
for sequences in
[n]^{t}
.
For an integer

, we let
\underline{i}\underline{j}\in[n]^{t}
denote the concatenation of
\underline{i}\in[n]^{s}
and
\underline{j}\in[n]^{t-s}
.
Given a sequence
\underline{i}=i_{1}\ldots i_{t}\in[n]^{t}
, we let
\{\underline{i}\}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\}\subseteq[n]
denote the associated multiset, and define

. Moreover, two sequences
\underline{i},\underline{j}\in[n]^{t}

.

Any permutation

.
Then,

.
Let

.

A matrix

is said to be

(also called

, i.e.,
\rho\ e_{i_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{i_{t}}=\rho\ e_{i_{\sigma(1)}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{i_{\sigma(t)}}

and

. Equivalently,

(or
\Pi_{t}\rho=\rho
, or
\Pi_{t}\rho\Pi_{t}=\rho
, since

Let

denote the set of Hermitian Bose symmetric matrices. So, any

This fact justifies the identifications

(\text{\rm BS}((\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t})^{\perp}\simeq\mathrm{Herm}((S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp})
.
In the same way,

\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))

Polynomials

We will use polynomials in real variables and in complex variables, so we introduce both settings. Throughout,
\mathbb{R}[x]=\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denotes the polynomial ring in
n
variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, with real coefficients. For an integer
d\geq 0
,
\mathbb{R}[x]_{d}
denotes the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
, of the form
p=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\alpha|=d}p_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
, and
\mathbb{R}[x]_{\leq d}=\cup_{k=0}^{d}\mathbb{R}[x]_{k}
is the set of polynomials with degree at most
d
. Here,
|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}
and
x^{\alpha}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.
A polynomial
p\in\mathbb{R}[x]
is said to be a
sum of squares
(
sos
, for short) if
p=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}q_{\ell}^{2}
for some
q_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}[x]
and
m\geq 1
. Then,
p
has even degree
2d
,
q_{\ell}
has degree at most
d
, and each
q_{\ell}
is homogeneous of degree
d
if
p
is homogeneous.
As is well-known, a polynomial
p
of degree
2d
is sos if and only if the following semidefinite program is feasible: there exists a positive semidefinite matrix
Q\in{\mathcal{S}}^{N}
(
N={n+d\choose d}
) such that the polynomial identity
p=\langle Q,(x^{\alpha+\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n},|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq d}\rangle
holds.

We will also consider the ring
\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
of polynomials in
n
complex variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and their conjugates

\overline{x}=(\overline{x}_{1},\ldots,\overline{x}_{n})
.
So,
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
is of the form
p=\sum_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}p_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}x^{\alpha}\overline{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}
(with finitely many nonzero coefficients). The largest value of
|\alpha|+|\alpha^{\prime}|
such that
p_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\neq 0
is the
total degree
of
p
.
One can also consider the degree in
x
and the degree in
\overline{x}
. For integers
d,d^{\prime}\geq 0
,

\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]_{d,d^{\prime}}
consists of the homogeneous polynomials
p
with degree
d
in
x
and
d^{\prime}
in
\overline{x}
, i..e, with
p_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=0
if
(|\alpha|,|\alpha^{\prime}|)\neq(d,d^{\prime})
.
A polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
is said to be
Hermitian
if
p=\overline{p}
holds. For instance,
p=x+\overline{x}
is Hermitian, but
q=\mathbf{i}x+\overline{x}
is not Hermitian since
\overline{q}=-\mathbf{i}\overline{x}+x\neq q
.

A (Hermitian) polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
is said to be a
real-sum of squares
(abbreviated as
r-sos
) if

p=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}q_{\ell}^{2}
for some Hermitian polynomials
q_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
. Equivalently,
p
is r-sos if and only if
p=\sum_{\ell}h_{\ell}\overline{h_{\ell}}
for some polynomials
h_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
.
For instance,
p=x\overline{x}=\big({x+\overline{x}\over 2}\big)^{2}+({x-\overline{x}\over 2\mathbf{i}})^{2}
is r-sos.
Here, we stress ‘real’-sos in order to distinguish from the other (more restricted) notion of
complex-sum of squares
(
c-sos
), where
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
is c-sos if
p=\sum_{\ell}h_{\ell}\overline{h_{\ell}}
for some
h_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}[x]
.
Again, one can check whether a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
is r-sos by means of a semidefinite program.

Any polynomial
p(x,\overline{x})\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
can be transformed into a pair of polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}}]
in the real variables
x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
via the transformation

x=x_{\text{\rm Re}}+\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}}
. In this way, each
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]
corresponds to a unique pair
(p_{\text{\rm Re}},p_{\text{\rm Im}})\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}}]^{2}
via

\displaystyle p_{\text{\rm Re}}(x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}})=\text{\rm Re}(p(x_{\text{\rm Re}}+\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}},x_{\text{\rm Re}}-\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}})),

\displaystyle p_{\text{\rm Im}}(x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}})=\text{\rm Im}(p(x_{\text{\rm Re}}+\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}},x_{\text{\rm Re}}-\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}}),

and we have the identity

\displaystyle p(x,\overline{x})=p(x_{\text{\rm Re}}+\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}},x_{\text{\rm Re}}-\mathbf{i}x_{\text{\rm Im}})=p_{\text{\rm Re}}(x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}})+\mathbf{i}p_{\text{\rm Im}}(x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}}).

(22)

So,
p_{\text{\rm Re}}=\text{\rm Re}(p)
is the real part of
p
and
p_{\text{\rm Im}}=\text{\rm Im}(p)
is its imaginary part.
Then,
p
is Hermitian (i.e.,
p=\overline{p}
) precisely when
\text{\rm Im}(p)=0
.
In particular, the polynomial
p\overline{p}
is Hermitian and equality

\displaystyle\text{\rm Re}(p\overline{p})=p_{\text{\rm Re}}^{2}+p_{\text{\rm Im}}^{2}

(23)

holds. Based on this one can verify the following well-known correspondence: for a Hermitian polynomial

,

\displaystyle p\text{ is r-sos }\Longleftrightarrow\text{ its real part }p_{\text{\rm Re}}\in\mathbb{R}[x_{\text{\rm Re}},x_{\text{\rm Im}}]\text{ is sos}

(24)

(which explains the terminology ‘real’-sos). See, e.g.,
[
GLS21
, Appendix A]
for a detailed exposition.

For the application to the DPS hierarchy, one needs a second group

2.3
Dual cones for
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
,
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
and
\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n}

The dual cone of the completely positive cone
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
is the copositive cone
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
, defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{\rm COP}_{n}&=\{A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}:x^{T}Ax\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}\\
&=\{A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}:(x^{\circ 2})^{T}Ax^{\circ 2}\geq 0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},\end{split}

(33)

where
x^{\circ 2}=x\circ x=(x_{i}^{2})_{i=1}^{n}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. So, any matrix
A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}
defines a homogeneous
n
-variate polynomial with degree 4,

\displaystyle f_{A}=(x^{\circ 2})^{T}Ax^{\circ 2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}A_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}[x]_{4},

(34)

and this polynomial is globally nonnegative precisely when
A
is a copositive matrix. A sufficient condition for
f_{A}
to be globally nonnegative is when
the polynomial
\|x\|^{2t}f_{A}
is sos for some integer
t\geq 0
. Motivated by this,
Parrilo
[
Par00
]
and de Klerk and Pasechnik
[
dKP02
]
define the cones

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}=\big\{A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}:\|x\|^{2t}f_{A}=\|x\|^{2t}\cdot(x^{\circ 2})^{T}Ax^{\circ 2}\text{ is sos}\big\}.

(35)

These cones form a hierarchy of semidefinite inner approximations for
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
and satisfy

{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}^{(t+1)}_{n}\subseteq\text{\rm COP}_{n},\quad\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm COP}_{n})\subseteq\bigcup_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}\subseteq\text{\rm COP}_{n},

where the inclusion
\text{\rm int}(\text{\rm COP}_{n})\subseteq\bigcup_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
follows from Reznick
[
Rez95
]
. As a consequence, the dual cones
({\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
form a complete hierarchy of semidefinite outer approximations for
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\text{\rm CP}_{n}\subseteq({\mathcal{K}}^{(t+1)}_{n})^{*}\subseteq({\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*},\quad\text{\rm CP}_{n}=\bigcap_{t\geq 0}({\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}.

(36)

Here are some known facts about the cones
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
and their duals, as background information. For
t=0
, we have
({\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}_{n})^{*}={\mathcal{S}}^{n}_{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{+}
. Hence,
({\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}_{n})^{*}
consists of the matrices that are positive semidefinite and entrywise nonnegative, known as
doubly nonnegative
matrices. Equivalently,
{\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}_{n}
consists
of the matrices that can be written as sum of a positive semidefinite matrix and an entrywise nonnegative matrix (see
[
Par00
,
dKP02
]
). For
n\leq 4
, equality
\text{\rm CP}_{n}=({\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}_{n})^{*}
holds, or, equivalently,
\text{\rm COP}_{n}={\mathcal{K}}^{(0)}_{n}

[
Dia62
]
. For
n\geq 5
, the inclusion
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}\subseteq\text{\rm COP}_{n}
is strict for any
t\geq 0

[
DDGH13
]
. Moreover, equality

\bigcup_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{5}=\text{\rm COP}_{5}
holds
[
SV24
]
and the inclusion

\bigcup_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}\subseteq\text{\rm COP}_{n}
is strict for
n\geq 6

[
LV23
]
.

We now turn to the dual cone of the separable cone
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
. For a matrix
M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
, consider the associated polynomial
F_{M}
in
2n
complex variables
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
and their conjugates
\overline{x}=(\overline{x}_{1},\ldots,\overline{x}_{n})
,
\overline{y}=(\overline{y}_{1},\ldots,\overline{y}_{n})
, defined as

\displaystyle F_{M}=\langle M,(x\otimes y)(x\otimes y)^{*}\rangle=\langle M,xx^{*}\otimes yy^{*}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x},y,\overline{y}]_{1,1,1,1}.

(37)

Then, the dual of
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\text{\rm SEP}_{n}^{*}=\{M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}):F_{M}=(x\otimes y)^{*}M(x\otimes y)\geq 0\ \forall x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}.

(38)

Note indeed that, since
F_{M}
is homogeneous (of degree 1) in each of
x,\overline{x},y,\overline{y}
,
F_{M}
is globally nonnegative if and only if
F_{M}
is nonnegative on the bi-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

Any nonzero matrix
M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
provides a hyperplane in the space
\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
, with normal
M
.
The corresponding half-space, consisting of the states
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
satisfying

\langle M,\rho_{AB}\rangle\geq 0
, contains
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
precisely when
M\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}^{*}
.
For this reason,
M
is also called an
entanglement witness
since it separates some entangled states, i.e.,

\langle M,\rho_{AB}\rangle<0
for some
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})\setminus\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
.

A sufficient condition for
F_{M}
to be globally nonnegative is when the polynomial

\|y\|^{2t}F_{M}
is r-sos for some integer
t\geq 0
. Fang and Fawzi
[
FF20
]
characterize the matrices
M
satisfying this property for a given order
t
and show that this corresponds precisely to the dual of the DPS hierarchy. To state their result,
let
{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
denote the set of matrices satisfying condition (
26
) for given
s
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}=\{\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t}):\rho_{AB[t]}^{T_{B[s]}}\succeq 0\}\quad\text{ for }s=0,1,\ldots,t.

(39)

Theorem 2.4
.

[
FF20
]

Consider a matrix
M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
and an integer

. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

M\in(\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
.

(ii)

The polynomial

(iii)

There exist matrices

such that

\displaystyle M\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)}=B+\sum_{s=0}^{t}W_{s}

with

and

for

Note the analogy between the description of the approximation hierarchy

Diagonal unitary invariant states

We return to the class of CLDUI states. Recall the two equivalent characterizations through the invariance property (

\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}:\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})\to\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}(\rho_{AB})=\int_{{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}}(U\otimes\overline{U})\rho_{AB}(U\otimes\overline{U})^{*}dU,

Two easy but important properties of this projection, collected in the next lemma, are that it preserves positive semidefiniteness and separability.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\rho_{AB}
is positive semidefinite (resp., separable), then

is positive semidefinite (resp., separable).

If

because

For any

(U\otimes\overline{U})(xx^{*}\otimes yy^{*})(U\otimes\overline{U})^{*}=Ux(Ux)^{*}\otimes\overline{U}y(\overline{U}y)^{*}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}.

Since integration can be seen as a limit of finite sums and
\text{\rm SEP}_{n}

X\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle X\in\text{\rm CP}_{n}\Longleftrightarrow(X,X)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}\Longleftrightarrow(X,X,X)\in\text{\rm TCP}_{n}.

The implication
(X,X,X)\in\text{\rm TCP}_{n}\Longrightarrow(X,X)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
follows from Lemma
2.9
.
The implication
X\in\text{\rm CP}_{n}\Longrightarrow(X,X,X)\in\text{\rm TCP}_{n}
follows from the fact that, if
X=aa^{T}
for some nonnegative
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, then, setting
x=y=\sqrt{a}
(entrywise) provides a decomposition showing
(X,X,X)\in\text{\rm TCP}_{n}
(using the characterization in Theorem
2.8
).
Finally, assume
(X,X)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
; we show
X\in\text{\rm CP}_{n}
. As
(X,X)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
, we have
\rho_{(X,X)}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
, implying
\rho_{(X,\cdot,X)}=\rho_{(X,X)}^{T_{B}}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
. Since
\rho_{(X,\cdot,X)}
is in addition Bose symmetric it admits a symmetric separable decomposition, of the form
\sum_{\ell}x_{\ell}x_{\ell}^{*}\otimes x_{\ell}x_{\ell}^{*}
for some
x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
(Lemma
2.3
). From this follows that
X=\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}a_{\ell}^{T}
, where we set
a_{\ell}=(|(x_{\ell})_{i}|^{2})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, thus showing
X\in\text{\rm CP}_{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Observe that any separable state of the form
\rho_{(X,X,X)}
admits a real separable decomposition, i.e., involving only real vectors. This follows as a byproduct of the above proof: If
\rho_{(X,X,X)}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}
, then
X\in\text{\rm CP}_{n}
and thus
X=\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}a_{\ell}^{T}
for some
a_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. Setting
x_{\ell}=(\sqrt{(a_{\ell})_{i}})_{i=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain

\rho_{(X,X,X)}=\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}_{n}}(\sum_{\ell}x_{\ell}x_{\ell}^{*}\otimes x_{\ell}x_{\ell}^{*})
. Combined with the fact that the projection
\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}_{n}}
can be formulated as an average by real-valued matrices (see (
46
)), we obtain a separable decomposition by real vectors, as desired. This argument does not extend to the CLDUI or LDUI cases, since then the averaging is by complex-valued matrices (see (
45
), (
47
)). Indeed, in Section
4.3
(see Remark
4.7
), one can see a real-valued separable CLDUI state of the form
\rho_{(X,X)}
that does not admit a real separable decomposition.

2.5
Dual cone of
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}

As
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
is a cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
, its dual cone is defined as

\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}=\{(S,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}:\langle(S,T),(X,Y)\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }(X,Y)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}\},

where
\langle(S,T),(X,Y)\rangle=\langle S,X\rangle+\langle T,Y\rangle
is the standard inner product in the product space. In order to define the dual of
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
at the ‘state level’ we make the following definition.

Definition 2.12
.

Given a pair
(S,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
, define the following matrix

\displaystyle M_{S,T}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}S_{ij}e_{i}e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}e_{j}^{*}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}T_{ij}e_{j}e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}e_{i}^{*}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}).

(49)

So,
M_{S,T}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
by construction.
Given a pair
(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
with
\text{\rm diag}(X)=\text{\rm diag}(Y)
, recall from (
10
) the associated bipartite state
\rho_{(X,Y)}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
, defined as

\displaystyle\rho_{(X,Y)}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}X_{ij}e_{i}e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}e_{j}^{*}+\sum_{i,j=1,i\neq j}^{n}Y_{ij}e_{i}e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}e_{j}^{*}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}.

(50)

Then, one can easily verify that

\displaystyle\langle M_{S,T},\rho_{(X,Y)}\rangle=\langle S,X\rangle+\langle T,Y\rangle=\langle(S,T),(X,Y)\rangle.

(51)

Moreover,
one can verify the following polynomial identity:

\displaystyle\langle M_{S,T},xx^{*}\otimes yy^{*}\rangle=\langle S,(x\circ\overline{x})(y\circ\overline{y})^{*}\rangle+\langle T,(x\circ y)(x\circ y)^{*}\rangle.

(52)

The characterization of the dual cone
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
now follows easily.

Lemma 2.13
.

For a pair
(S,T)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
,
(S,T)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
if and only if
M_{S,T}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}^{*}
, i.e., the polynomial
\langle M_{S,T},xx^{*}\otimes yy^{*}\rangle
is globally nonnegative.

By combining (
52
) and the characterization (
41
) of
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
, it follows that the polynomial
\langle M_{S,T},xx^{*}\otimes yy^{*}\rangle
is globally nonnnegative (i.e.,
M_{S,T}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}^{*}
) if and only
(S,T)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Note that the matrix
M_{S,T}
in (
49
) remains unchanged under redistributing diagonal entries between
S
and
T
, i.e.,
M_{S,T}=M_{S+D,T-D}
for any diagonal matrix
D
.
Recall the notation
D_{T}=\text{\rm Diag}(\text{\rm diag}(T))
for any square matrix
T
.
By replacing
(S,T)
by
(S+D,T-D)
with
D=(D_{T}-D_{S})/2
, one can assume that
S
and
T
have the same diagonal. Moreover,
by replacing
(S,T)
by
(S+D_{T},T-D_{T})
, one can move all diagonal entries to the first argument, in which case one gets

M_{S,T}=M_{S+D_{T},T-D_{T}}=\rho_{(S+D_{T},T^{T}+D_{S})}
and

\displaystyle\rho_{(A,B)}=M_{A,B^{T}-D_{B}}\text{ for any }(A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}\text{ with }\text{\rm diag}(A)=\text{\rm diag}(B).

(53)

We find it more convenient to keep the notation
M_{S,T}
that can be freely used for any
S,T
and permits to distinguish between the primal side (where states
\rho_{(X,Y)}
live) and the dual side (where matrices
M_{S,T}
live).
We now compare the cone
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
with the cone
\text{\rm PCOP}_{n}
(of pairwise copositive matrices) recently introduced in
[
GNP25
, Def. 3.1]
.

As in
[
GNP25
]
, set

\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{Herm}^{n}=\{(A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathrm{Herm}^{n}:\text{\rm diag}(A)=\text{\rm diag}(B)\}
.
Then,
\text{\rm PCOP}_{n}
is defined as the set of matrix pairs
(A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
for which the polynomial

\langle A,(x\circ\overline{x})(y\circ\overline{y})^{*}\rangle+\langle B-D_{B},(x\circ y)(x\circ y)^{*}\rangle
is globally nonnnegative. Hence, we have

(A,B)\in\text{\rm PCOP}_{n}\Longleftrightarrow M_{A,B-D_{B}}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{n}^{*},\ \text{ i.e., }\ (A,B-D_{B})\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}.

In
[
GNP25
, Prop. 3.2]
it is shown that
\text{\rm PCOP}_{n}
is the dual of
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
. The (minor) difference with our setting is that the dual is taken with respect to the variation of the standard inner product, where the diagonal entries are ‘counted only once’; that is, defining the scalar product of two matrix pairs
(A,B),(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
as

\langle A,A^{\prime}\rangle+\langle B-D_{B},B^{\prime}-D_{B^{\prime}}\rangle
.

Here are some basic results about
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
(also mentioned in
[
GNP25
]
) that we will need later.

Lemma 2.15
.

(i)

If
(X,Y)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
, then
(X^{T},Y^{T})\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
.

(ii)

If
(S,T)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
, then
(S^{T},T^{T})\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
.

(iii)

If
(S,T)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
, then
S+S^{T}+T+T^{T}\in\text{\rm COP}_{n}
.

(i),(ii) follow using the definitions. For (iii), note that
(S,T)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
implies that the polynomial in (
52
) is globally nonnegative; by evaluating it at
y=\overline{x}
, one gets the desired result.∎

Lemma 2.16
.

(i)

If
(S,T)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
, then
S_{ij}+S_{ji}\geq 0
(for
i\neq j\in[n]
) and
S_{ii}+T_{ii}\geq 0
(for
i\in[n]
).

(ii)

Let
S\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
. Then,

(S,S)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}\Longleftrightarrow S\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{+}.

(i) follows by taking the inner product of
(S,T)
with the matrix pairs
(e_{i}e_{j}^{*}+e_{j}e_{i}^{*},0)
and
(e_{i}e_{i}^{*},e_{i}e_{i}^{*})
that both belong to
\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
.
We show (ii). If
(S,S)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
, then
S
is symmetric (since real, Hermitian) and
S\geq 0
by (i). Conversely, assume
S\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}
and
S\geq 0
. For
(X,Y)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}
,

\langle(S,S),(X,Y)\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{ii}(X_{ii}+Y_{ii})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}S_{ij}(X_{ij}+X_{ji}+Y_{ij}+Y_{ji}).

The first sum is nonnegative since
S,X\geq 0
and
\text{\rm diag}(Y)=\text{\rm diag}(X)
. Also the second sum is nonnnegative: by (
44
), we have

\text{\rm Re}(Y_{ij})^{2}\leq|Y_{ij}|^{2}\leq X_{ij}X_{ji}
, implying
\text{\rm Re}(Y_{ij})\geq-\sqrt{X_{ij}X_{ji}}
and thus

X_{ij}+X_{ji}+Y_{ij}+Y_{ji}=X_{ij}+X_{ji}+2\text{\rm Re}(Y_{ij})\geq(\sqrt{X_{ij}}-\sqrt{X_{ji}})^{2}\geq 0
. So,
(S,S)\in\text{\rm PCP}_{n}^{*}
.
∎

More results about the cone
\text{\rm PCOP}_{n}
can be found in
[
GNP25
]
. In particular, it is shown in
[
GNP25
, Theorem 3.9, Corollary 3.10]
that, for a pair
(A,B)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{Herm}^{n}
,
A\geq 0
and
\tilde{A}+\text{\rm Re}(U^{*}(B-D_{B})U)\in\text{\rm COP}_{n}
for all
U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}
implies
(A,B)\in\text{\rm PCOP}_{n}
, after setting
\tilde{A}=(\sqrt{A_{ij}A_{ji}})_{i,j=1}^{n}
. Moreover, this implication holds as an equivalence if
A
is real symmetric. In addition, when
B
is real symmetric, it suffices to consider real-valued
U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}
in the previous claim.

3
The DPS hierarchy for diagonal unitary invariant states

In this section we investigate how to adapt the DPS hierarchy for bipartite states having some diagonal unitary invariance, with the aim of obtaining more economical relaxations.
We first focus on the class
\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
and then indicate how the results easily extend to the classes
\text{\rm LDUI}_{n}
,
\text{\rm LDOI}_{n}
.

3.1
An extended sparsity pattern for DPS certificates of CLDUI states

Here, we show that any separable bipartite state
\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
admits a
\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate that enjoys invariance and sparsity properties inherited from those of the state
\rho_{AB}
.
For this, for an integer
t\geq 1
, define the set

\displaystyle\begin{split}\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}=\{&\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t}):\\
&\rho_{AB[t]}=(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})^{*}\ \forall U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}\}\end{split}

(54)

and let
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}
denote the projection onto
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
.
So, for
t=1
, we recover
\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
and, for
t\geq 1
, the definition follows the analogous recipe, motivated by the fact that the
t
copies of the B-register should all be treated in the same way (in order to preserve invariance under any permutation of these
t
registers).

Our aim is to prove the following result, which claims that any state in
\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}\cap\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
admits a
\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate in
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
and after that we will see that such certificate enjoys a block-diagonal structure.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
with
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate
\rho_{AB[t]}
. Then,
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}(\rho_{AB})\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
with
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})
. Therefore, if
\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
, then
\rho_{AB}
admits a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate that belongs to
\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}^{(t)}
.

In order to prove Theorem
3.1

we first collect structural results about the set
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
.
Recall from relation (
5
) that
\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
if and only if
\text{\rm supp}(\rho_{AB})\subseteq\Omega_{n}
. As in the case
t=1
, the set
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
allows for alternate descriptions through its sparsity pattern and the associated projection. For this, we now define the following ‘extended’ sparsity pattern:

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}^{(t)}=\{(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}:\{i_{0}\underline{j}\}=\{j_{0}\underline{i}\},\ \text{i.e., }\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})=\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})\}.

(55)

For
\underline{i}\in[n]^{t}
, recall that
\alpha(\underline{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, with
k
-th entry the number of occurrences of
k
in the multiset
\{\underline{i}\}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\}
. So,
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})=e_{i_{0}}+\alpha(\underline{j})
. Setting
t=1
recovers the original set
\Omega_{n}
, i.e., we have

\Omega^{(1)}_{n}=\{(ij,kl)\in[n]^{2}\times[n]^{2}:\{il\}=\{jk\}\}=\Omega_{n}.

Moreover, as in the case
t=1
(Remark
2.7
), in definition (
54
) it suffices to require invariance under a finite subset of
{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t})
. Then, the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\rho_{AB[t]}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
, i.e.,
\rho_{AB[t]}=(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})^{*}
for
U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}
.

(ii)

\rho_{AB[t]}=(U_{k}\otimes\overline{U_{k}}^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U_{k}\otimes\overline{U_{k}}^{\otimes t})^{*}
for all
k\in[n-1]
, where

U_{k}
is the diagonal matrix with
(U_{k})_{k,k}=\omega
and all other diagonal entries equal to 1, and we set
7
7

7

Alternatively, one could choose
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathbf{i}}{\lambda}}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
, which would give a finite representation independent of the parameter
t
.

\omega:=e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}\over t+2}
.

(iii)

\text{\rm supp}(\rho_{AB[t]})\subseteq\Omega_{n}^{(t)}
.

The implication (i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii) is obvious. Assume (ii) holds, we show (iii).
First, note that for any

U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}
with
u=\text{\rm diag}(U)
, and any index pair
(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}
, by (
16
), one has

\displaystyle\begin{split}((U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})^{*})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}&=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}\cdot u_{i_{0}}\overline{u}_{i_{1}}\dots\overline{u}_{i_{t}}\overline{u}_{j_{0}}u_{j_{1}}\dots u_{j_{t}}\\
&=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}\cdot u^{\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})}\overline{u}^{\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})}.\end{split}

(56)

Consider an index pair
(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\not\in\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
.
Then,
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})_{k}\neq\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})_{k}
for some index
k
that can be chosen in
[n-1]
since
|\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})|=|\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})|
.
W.l.o.g. we may assume
\ell:=\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})_{k}-\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})_{k}>0
. Moreover,
\ell\leq t+1
(since
|\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})|=t+1
). Note that this implies that
\omega^{\ell}\neq 1
.
Now, we apply (
56
) to the matrix
U=U_{k}
; then, the value in (
56
) is equal to

(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}\cdot\omega^{\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})_{k}-\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})_{k}}=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}\cdot\omega^{\ell}
, which is equal to
(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}
only if
(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}=0
, since
\omega^{\ell}\neq 1
. So, (iii) holds.

Finally, assume (iii) holds, we show (i). In view of (
56
) it suffices to show that the value in (
56
) is equal to
(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}
when
(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}\neq 0
. But, then,
(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in\Omega_{n}^{(t)}
, which gives
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i})=\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})
and thus

u^{\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})}\overline{u}^{\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})}=1
. Hence, the value in (
56
) equals

(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}
, as desired.
∎

As for
t=1
, the projection onto the
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
subspace is given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})=\int_{{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}}(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})^{*}dU.

(57)

In view of Lemma
3.2
, the projection can also be seen as the map that preserves the entries indexed by
\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
and places 0 everywhere else. Moreover, the integral can be replaced by a finite sum over the
n-1
matrices
U_{k}
from Lemma
3.2
.

The following lemma is the key ingredient for the proof of Theorem
3.1
, which is given thereafter. Its proof relies on the sparsity structure of
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
from Lemma
3.2
and the integral formulation (
57
) of the corresponding projector.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
W\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t})
and consider an integer
0\leq s\leq t
.

(i)

If
W\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
, then
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W)\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
.

(ii)

If
W\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
, then
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W)\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
.

(i) Assume
W\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
; we show
\widetilde{W}:=\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W)\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
.
For this, let
i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}\in[n]^{t+1}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{t}
.
By assumption,
W_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}=W_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\sigma(\underline{j})}
; we need to show that the same holds for
\widetilde{W}
. By Lemma
3.2
, the support of
\widetilde{W}
is contained in the set
\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
. So, it suffices now to observe that
(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in\Omega^{(t)}_{n}

\Longleftrightarrow

(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}^{\sigma})\in\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
, since
\{i_{0}\underline{j}\}
and
\{i_{0}\underline{j}^{\sigma}\}
are the same multisets.

(ii) Assume
W\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
, i.e.,
W^{T_{B[s]}}\succeq 0
(by (
39
)). Let
U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}
and
u=\text{\rm diag}(U)
. Then,

\displaystyle(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})W(U\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes t})^{*}

\displaystyle=W\circ(uu^{*}\otimes(\overline{u}\ \overline{u}^{*})^{\otimes t}),

\displaystyle(W\circ(uu^{*}\otimes(\overline{u}\ \overline{u}^{*})^{\otimes t}))^{T_{B[s]}}

\displaystyle=W^{T_{B[s]}}\circ(uu^{*}\otimes(\overline{u}\ \overline{u}^{*})^{\otimes t})^{T_{B[s]}}

\displaystyle=W^{T_{B[s]}}\circ((uu^{*})^{\otimes(s+1)}\otimes(\overline{u}\ \overline{u}^{*})^{\otimes(t-s)})

\displaystyle=(U^{\otimes(s+1)}\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes(t-s)})W^{T_{B[s]}}(U^{\otimes(s+1)}\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes(t-s)})^{*}

(using (
16
) at the first and last lines). Using both equations gives

(\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W))^{T_{B[s]}}=\int_{{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}}(U^{\otimes(s+1)}\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes(t-s)})W^{T_{B[s]}}(U^{\otimes(s+1)}\otimes\overline{U}^{\otimes(t-s)})^{*}dU.

As

, we obtain that each integrand is positive semidefinite, and thus
(\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W))^{T_{B[s]}}\succeq~0
.
∎

Assume
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}
with
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate
\rho_{AB[t]}
.
We show
\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB[t]}}=\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})
is a
\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
-certificate of
\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB}}=\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}(\rho_{AB})
. So,
\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
,

\rho_{AB[t]}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
for all integers
0\leq s\leq t
, and
\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}(\rho_{AB[t]})=\rho_{AB}
.
By Lemma
3.3
we have
\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB[t]}}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))

and
\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB[t]}}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
for any
0\leq s\leq t
.
There remains to show that
\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}(\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB[t]}})=\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB}}
. For this, let
i,j,k,l\in[n]
and
\underline{h}\in[n]^{t-1}
. Note that

\displaystyle(ij,kl)\in\Omega_{n}\text{ if and only if }(ij\underline{h},kl\underline{h})\in\Omega^{(t)}_{n},

(58)

because
\{ij\}=\{kl\}
if and only if
\{ij\underline{h}\}=\{kl\underline{h}\}
. Using the definition of the partial trace,

(\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}(\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB[t]}}))_{ij,kl}
is equal to

\sum_{\underline{h}\in[n]^{t-1}}(\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB[t]}})_{ij\underline{h},jk\underline{h}}=\begin{cases}(\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}(\rho_{AB[t]}))_{ij,kl}&\text{ if }(ij,kl)\in\Omega_{n},\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

Since
\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}(\rho_{AB[t]})=\rho_{AB}
this is in fact precisely the definition of
\widetilde{{\rho}_{AB}}
.
∎

The result of Theorem
3.1
has an immediate counterpart on the dual side:
any matrix in
(\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}\cap\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}
admits a certificate as in Theorem
2.4
(iii) with, in addition,
B,W_{s}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
M\in(\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
, then
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}(M)\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)}
belongs to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp}\cap\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}+\sum_{s=0}^{t}{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}\cap\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}

and thus
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}}(M)\in(\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
.

Assume that
M\in(\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
. Then, by Theorem
2.4
(iii), there exist
B\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp}

and
W_{s}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
such that

M\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)}=B+\sum_{s=0}^{t}W_{s}.

Taking the projection onto
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
at both sides, we get

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}}(M)\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(M\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(B)+\sum_{s=0}^{t}\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W_{s}),

where the first equality follows using (
58
).
By Lemma
3.3
(ii), we have that
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W_{s})\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
for each
s
.
To conclude, we use Lemma
3.3
(i) to show that
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(B)\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp}
. Indeed, if
W\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
, then
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W)\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
and thus we have
\langle\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(B),W\rangle=\langle B,\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(W)\rangle=0
holds, as desired. ∎

3.2
Exploiting sparsity of DPS certificates for CLDUI states

As we now see, matrices in the set
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
have a block-diagonal structure.
For convenience, let
G^{(t)}_{n}
denote the (undirected) graph with vertex set
[n]^{t+1}
and edge set
\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
.
So, any matrix
W\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
has a sparsity pattern that is contained in the graph
G^{(t)}_{n}
, i.e.,
\text{\rm supp}(W)\subseteq\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
.
An easy but very useful fact is that the graph
G^{(t)}_{n}
is a disjoint union of cliques.

Lemma 3.5
.

The graph
G^{(t)}_{n}=([n]^{t+1},\Omega^{(t)}_{n})
is a disjoint union of cliques. Hence, any matrix
W\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
is block-diagonal, of the form
W=\oplus_{C}W[C]
, where the direct sum is over the maximal cliques of
G^{(t)}_{n}
.

Assume
i_{0}\underline{i}
is adjacent to both
j_{0}\underline{j}
and
k_{0}\underline{k}
in
G^{(t)}_{n}
, we show that
(j_{0}\underline{j})
is adjacent to
(k_{0}\underline{k})
. By assumption,
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j})=\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})
and
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{k})=\alpha(k_{0}\underline{i})
. This implies

\alpha(j_{0}\underline{k})-\alpha(k_{0}\underline{j})=(\alpha(j_{0}\underline{i})-\alpha(i_{0}\underline{j}))+(\alpha(i_{0}\underline{k})-\alpha(k_{0}\underline{i}))=0

(since
\alpha(\cdot)
is linear under the concatenation of sequences),
which shows that
(j_{0}\underline{j},k_{0}\underline{k})\in\Omega^{(t)}_{n}
. The second claim follows as an immediate consequence.
∎

So, if
W\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
, then

W\succeq 0
if and only if
W[C]\succeq 0
for all the maximal cliques
C
of
G^{(t)}_{n}
.
This generalizes what we saw earlier in (
10
) for the case
t=1
.
The analogous result also holds for testing positive semidefiniteness of partial transposes, i.e., membership in the set
{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
for
s\in[t]
.

Let
1\leq s\leq t
be an integer. Given a sequence
\underline{i}=i_{1}\ldots i_{t}\in[n]^{t}
we define the subsequences
\underline{i}[s]=i_{1}\ldots i_{s}\in[n]^{s}
and
i[s+1:t]=i_{s+1}\ldots i_{t}\in[n]^{t-s}
, which are, respectively, the
s
-prefix and
(t-s)
-suffix of
\underline{i}
. Consider the function

\displaystyle T_{B[s]}:[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}

\displaystyle\to[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}

\displaystyle(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})

\displaystyle\mapsto(i_{0}\underline{j}[s]\underline{i}[s+1:t],j_{0}\underline{i}[s]\underline{j}[s+1:t])

that swaps the
s
-prefices of
\underline{i}
and
\underline{j}
. Then, the entry of
W^{T_{B[s]}}
indexed by the pair
(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})
is equal to the entry of
W
indexed by the pair
T_{B[s]}((i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}))
. Now, we define the set

\displaystyle\Omega^{(t)}_{n,s}:=\{(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}:T_{B[s]}((i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}))\in\Omega^{(t)}_{n}\}

and, accordingly,
G_{n,s}^{(t)}
is the graph with vertex set
[n]^{t+1}
and edge set
\Omega^{(t)}_{n,s}
. So, the partial transpose
W^{T_{B[s]}}
in the first
s

B
-registers of a matrix
W
in
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
has a sparsity pattern contained in
G_{n,s}^{(t)}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
s\in[t]
, the graph
G_{n,s}^{(t)}=([n]^{t+1},\Omega^{(t)}_{n,s})
is a disjoint union of cliques.
Hence, if
W\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
, then
W^{T_{B[s]}}
is block-diagonal, of the form
W^{T_{B[s]}}=\oplus_{C}W^{T_{B[s]}}[C]
, where the direct sum is over the maximal cliques of
G^{(t)}_{n,s}
.

Let
i_{0}\underline{i}
be adjacent to
j_{0}\underline{j}
and
k_{0}\underline{k}
in
G_{n,s}^{(t)}
. Then,
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i}[s]\underline{j}[s+1:t])=\alpha(j_{0}\underline{j}[s]\underline{i}[s+1:t])
and

\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i}[s]\underline{k}[s+1:t])=\alpha(k_{0}\underline{k}[s]\underline{i}[s+1:t])
. Hence,

\displaystyle\alpha(k_{0}\underline{k}[s]\underline{j}[s+1:t])-\alpha(j_{0}\underline{j}[s]\underline{k}[s+1:t])=

\displaystyle\quad(\alpha(k_{0}\underline{k}[s]\underline{i}[s+1:t])-\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i}[s]\underline{k}[s+1:t]))

\displaystyle\quad+(\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i}[s]\underline{j}[s+1:t])-\alpha(j_{0}\underline{j}[s]\underline{i}[s+1:t]))

is equal to 0, and thus
j_{0}\underline{j}
is adjacent to
k_{0}\underline{k}
in
G^{(t)}_{n,s}
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

For a matrix
W\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
and
0\leq s\leq t
,
W^{T_{B[s]}}\succeq 0
if and only if
W^{T_{B[s]}}[C]\succeq 0
for all the maximal cliques
C
of
G^{(t)}_{n,s}
(setting
G^{(t)}_{n,0}=G^{(t)}_{t}
).

3.3
Generalizations to
LDUI
and
LDOI
states

The previous results for CLDUI states extend in a straightforward way to LDUI and LDOI states. The respective ‘extended’ invariance classes are given by

\displaystyle\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}:=\{

\displaystyle\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t}):

\displaystyle\rho_{AB[t]}=(U\otimes U^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U\otimes U^{\otimes t})^{*}\ \forall U\in{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}\},

\displaystyle\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}:=\{

\displaystyle\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t}):

\displaystyle\rho_{AB[t]}=(O\otimes O^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(O\otimes O^{\otimes t})^{*}\ \forall O\in{\mathcal{DO}}_{n}\},

where in the definition of
\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
one can restrict the invariance under
n-1
selected matrices (as in Lemma
3.3
(ii)).
The sparsity patterns of
\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
and
\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}
are captured, respectively, by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Phi_{n}^{(t)}:=\{(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}:\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i})=\alpha(j_{0}\underline{j})\},\\
\Psi_{n}^{(t)}:=\{(i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j})\in[n]^{t+1}\times[n]^{t+1}:\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i}j_{0}\underline{j})\equiv 0\text{ mod }2\},\end{split}

(59)

and the respective projections are given by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})=\int_{{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}}(U\otimes U^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(U\otimes U^{\otimes t})^{*}dU,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{O\in{\mathcal{DO}}_{n}}(O\otimes O^{\otimes t})\rho_{AB[t]}(O\otimes O^{\otimes t})^{*}.

We now have all the prerequisites to adapt Theorem
3.1
for LDUI and LDOI states.
The proof is similar, thus omitted.

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume that
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}
with
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate
\rho_{AB[t]}
. Then,
\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}_{n}}(\rho_{AB})\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
(resp.,
\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}_{n}}(\rho_{AB})\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
) with
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate
\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})
(resp.,
\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}}(\rho_{AB[t]})
).

Again, this theorem has an immediate counterpart on the dual side for
(\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
: the analog of Corollary
3.4
holds replacing
CLDUI
, respectively, by
LDUI
or
LDOI
.

Also the analogs of Lemmas
3.5
,
3.6
hold:
each of the graphs
([n]^{t+1},\Psi^{(t)}_{n})
and
([n]^{t+1},\Phi^{(t)}_{n})
is a disjoint union of cliques and the same holds for the graphs attached to taking partial transposes. (The LDUI case follows from the CLDUI case up to a transposition and the proof in the LDOI case is in fact even easier since it involves a parity argument). In summary, matrices in
\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
or
\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}
and their partial transposes all have a block-diagonal structure.

We now give in Lemma
3.10
an upper bound on the size of the blocks occurring in a matrix in
\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}
, by upper bounding the maximum size of a clique in the graph
([n]^{t+1},\Psi^{(t)}_{n})
. Note that this bound also applies to the maximum clique size in the graph corresponding to taking partial transposes, because the definition of edges in
\Psi^{(t)}_{n}
is ‘global’ in the sense that it depends only on the concatenated sequence
\alpha(i_{0}\underline{i}j_{0}\underline{j})
. In addition, the bound from Lemma
3.10
also applies to the graphs
([n]^{t+1},\Phi^{(t)}_{n})
,
G^{(t)}_{n}=([n]^{t+1},\Omega^{(t)}_{n})
(and their analogs corresponding to taking partial transposes) that model the sparsity patterns of matrices in
\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
and
\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}

([n]^{t+1},\Psi^{(t)}_{n})
.

Lemma 3.10
.

The maximum size of a clique in the graph
([n]^{t+1},\Psi^{(t)}_{n})
is at most
t!n^{\lceil t/2\rceil}.

We omit the proof since, later in Lemma
5.4
of Section
5
,
we will prove a similar statement adapted to the more economical moment framework.
By the above discussion, Corollary
3.11
below follows as a direct application of Lemma
3.10
.

Corollary 3.11
.

If
W\in\text{\rm LDOI}^{(t)}_{n}
then, for any
0\leq s\leq t
,
W^{T_{B[s]}}
is a block-diagonal matrix with all its blocks of size at most
t!n^{\lceil t/2\rceil}
.
The same holds if
W\in\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
or
W\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(t)}_{n}
.

So, for

, the maximum block size is

for matrices in
\text{\rm LDOI}_{n}

).

3.4
A class of CLDUI examples: exploiting additional structure in DPS certificates

Here, we investigate a class of CLDUI bipartite quantum states (see Definition
3.16
) and show
analytically
that their separability is characterized by the DPS hierarchy at level
t=2
. We will use this characterization in Section
5.3
to generate test states for the PPT
2
conjecture. To be able to analytically analyze
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}
-certificates, one needs to exploit additional structural properties of the quantum state at hand (in order to further reduce the block sizes and the number of variables).
So, we begin with
some results showing how additional properties of a quantum state
\rho_{AB}
(like having a large kernel, or enjoying some symmetry property) are inherited by its
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificates
\rho_{AB[t]}
. These results are of independent interest and apply to general quantum states.

Properties of the kernel

As we now see, any vector in the kernel of a state
\rho_{AB}
can be used to construct vectors in the kernel of its
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificates. This relies on the partial trace property (
27
) and positivity
\rho_{AB}\succeq 0
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Assume that
\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
with
\rho_{AB[t]}
as
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate.
If
w\in\ker\rho_{AB}
, then
w\otimes e_{k_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{t}}\in\ker\rho_{AB[t]}
for all

k_{2},\ldots,k_{t}\in[n]
.

Since
w\in\ker\rho_{AB}
, we have
\rho_{AB}w=0
, and thus

\displaystyle 0=\langle\rho_{AB},ww^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}(\rho_{AB[t]}),ww^{*}\rangle=\langle\rho_{AB[t]},ww^{*}\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{2},\ldots,k_{t}=1}^{n}\langle\rho_{AB[t]},ww^{*}\otimes e_{k_{2}}e_{k_{2}}^{*}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{t}}e_{k_{t}}^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{2},\ldots,k_{t}=1}^{n}(w\otimes e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{t}})^{*}\rho_{AB[t]}(w\otimes e_{k_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{t}}).

As
\rho_{AB[t]}\succeq 0
, each summand is nonnegative and thus equal to 0. From this one obtains that
0=\rho_{AB[t]}(w\otimes e_{k_{2}}\otimes\ldots\otimes e_{k_{t}})
, as desired.
∎

Symmetry properties

We now turn to exploiting symmetries of the initial quantum state for its
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificates.

First, let us introduce how the permutation group
\mathfrak{G}_{n}
acts on
[n]^{t}
and on
\mathrm{Herm}((\mathbb{C}^{n})^{t}
.
Any permutation
\theta\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}
has an entrywise action on
[n]^{t}
, by setting

\theta(\underline{i})=\theta(i_{1})\ldots\theta(i_{t})
for
\underline{i}=i_{1}\ldots i_{t}\in[n]^{t}
.
In turn,
\theta
acts on
\rho\in\mathrm{Herm}((\mathbb{C}^{n})^{t}
, by permuting its rows and columns according to the action of
\theta
on
[n]^{t}
, thus producing
\theta(\rho)\in\mathrm{Herm}((\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes}t)
, with entries

\theta(\rho)_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}=\rho_{\theta(i_{0})\theta(\underline{i}),\theta(j_{0})\theta(\underline{j})}\text{ for }i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}\in[n]^{t+1}.

Remark 3.13
.

So, a permutation
\theta\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}
acts on the values
i_{k}\in[n]
and not on their indices
k\in[t]
, as was the case earlier, when a permutation
\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{t}
would produce

\underline{i}^{\sigma}=i_{\sigma(1)}\ldots i_{\sigma(t)}\in[n]^{t}
and
\rho^{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Herm}((\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t})
with entries

\rho^{\sigma}_{\underline{i},\underline{j}}=\rho_{\underline{i}^{\sigma},\underline{j}^{\sigma}}

for
\underline{i},\underline{j}\in[n]^{t}
. We will use the following observation (that follows using the definitions): for a sequence
\underline{i}=i_{1}\ldots i_{t}\in[n]^{t}
,

\displaystyle\theta(\underline{i}^{\sigma})=\theta(i_{\sigma(1)})\ldots\theta(i_{\sigma(t)})=(\theta(i_{1})\ldots\theta(i_{t}))^{\sigma}=\theta(\underline{i})^{\sigma}\text{ for any }\theta\in\mathfrak{G}_{n},\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{t}.

(60)

Lemma 3.14
.

Assume
\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
with
\rho_{AB[t]}
as
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate.
For any
\theta\in\mathfrak{G}_{n}
,
\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})
is a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}
-certificate for
\theta(\rho_{AB})
.

By assumption,
\rho_{AB[t]}
satisfies (
25
), (
26
), (
27
), we show that the same holds for
\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})
.
First, we show (
25
). For this, let
\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{t}
and
i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}\in[n]^{t+1}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i}^{\sigma},j_{0}\underline{j}^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\theta(i_{0})\theta(\underline{i}^{\sigma}),\theta(j_{0})\theta(\underline{j}^{\sigma})}=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\theta(i_{0})(\theta(\underline{i}))^{\sigma},\theta(j_{0})\theta(\underline{j})^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\theta(i_{0})\theta(\underline{i}),\theta(j_{0})\theta(\underline{j})}=\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}},

using (
60
) at the first line and the fact that
\rho_{AB[t]}
satisfies (
25
) at the second line. Hence,
\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})
also satisfies (
25
).

Condition (
26
) follows from the fact that the operations of applying
\theta
and taking the partial transpose commute:

\left(\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})\right)^{T_{B[s]}}=\theta\big(\rho_{AB[t]}^{T_{B[s]}}\big),

which can be checked by computing

\displaystyle\big(\left(\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})\right)^{T_{B[s]}}\big)_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}=\left(\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})\right)_{i_{0}j_{1}\dots j_{s}i_{s+1}\dots s_{t},j_{0}i_{1}\dots i_{s}j_{s+1}\dots j_{t}}

\displaystyle=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\theta(i_{0}j_{1}\dots j_{s}i_{s+1}\dots s_{t}),\theta(j_{0}i_{1}\dots i_{s}j_{s+1}\dots j_{t})}=\big(\rho_{AB[t]}^{T_{B[s]}}\big)_{\theta(i_{0}\underline{i}),\theta(j_{0}\underline{j})}

\displaystyle=\big(\theta\big(\rho_{AB[t]}^{T_{B[s]}}\big)\big)_{i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}}.

Hence,
\rho_{AB[t]}^{T_{B[s]}}\succeq 0
implies
\theta(\rho_{AB[t]}^{T_{B[s]}})\succeq 0
and thus

(\theta(\rho_{AB[t]}))^{T_{B[s]}}\succeq 0
, as desired.

Finally, condition (
27
) follows from the fact that the operations of applying
\theta
and taking the partial trace also commute, which
can be checked by computing

\displaystyle\big(\text{\rm Tr}_{B[2:t]}\big(\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})\big)\big)_{i_{0}i_{1},j_{0}j_{1}}=\sum_{\underline{k}\in[n]^{t-1}}\big(\theta(\rho_{AB[t]})\big)_{i_{0}i_{1}\underline{k},j_{0}j_{1}\underline{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underline{k}\in[n]^{t-1}}\big(\rho_{AB[t]}\big)_{\theta(i_{0}i_{1})\theta(\underline{k}),\theta(j_{0}j_{1})\theta(\underline{k})}=\sum_{\underline{h}\in[n]^{t-1}}\big(\rho_{AB[t]}\big)_{\theta(i_{0}i_{1})\underline{h},\theta(j_{0}j_{1})\underline{h}}

\displaystyle=\text{\rm Tr}_{r-2}(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\theta(i_{0}i_{1}),\theta(j_{0}j_{1})}=(\rho_{AB})_{\theta(i_{0}i_{1}),\theta(j_{0}j_{1})}=\theta(\rho_{AB})_{i_{0}i_{1},j_{0}j_{1}},

using the fact that
\rho_{AB[t]}
satisfies (
27
) at the last but one equality.∎

We consider the CLDUI bipartite states
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
(

(

We show

is separable if and only if it belongs to

Definition 3.16

For

Lemma 3.17
.

The matrix
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
belongs to
\text{\rm DPS}^{(1)}_{3}
if and only if
a,a^{\prime}\geq 0
and

By definition,

if and only if
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\succeq 0

and the block

.
∎

Lemma 3.18
.

Assume that
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}_{3}
. Then,
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
has a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}
-certificate
\rho
with the following properties:
\rho
is block-diagonal and each of its blocks is a scalar multiple of the all-ones matrix.

The existence of a block-diagonal certificate
\rho
follows from Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.7

\rho=\oplus_{C}\rho[C]
, where the direct sum is over the maximal cliques

(described in Table
1

C
,
\rho[C]=x_{C}J_{|C|}
for some

. That is, the columns
\rho_{\cdot,i_{0}i_{1}i_{2}}
,
\rho_{\cdot,j_{0}j_{1}j_{2}}
of
\rho
indexed by any two elements
i_{0}i_{1}i_{2},j_{0}j_{1}j_{2}\in C
are equal. It suffices to show this for the
six cliques of size 2 or 5.

First, we use the property (
25
) of
\rho
, which claims that
\rho_{\cdot,j_{0}j_{1}j_{2}}=\rho_{\cdot,j_{0}j_{2}j_{1}}
for any

. This directly settles the case of the cliques of size 2,
\{123,132\}
,
\{213,231\}
and
\{312,321\}

Consider now a clique of size 5, say

\rho_{\cdot,212}=\rho_{\cdot,221}
,
\rho_{\cdot,313}=\rho_{\cdot,331}
holds. There remains to show that the columns indexed by the elements

For this, we use the fact that the vectors
w_{1}=e_{2}\otimes e_{2}-e_{3}\otimes e_{3}

From this one obtains that the columns of

The same reasoning applies to the other two cliques of size 5.
∎

Next, we further simplify the structure of a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}
-certificate for

Lemma 3.19
.

Assume that
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}_{3}

\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
has a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}

\rho

for some

(ii)

(iii)

\rho_{122,122}=\rho_{233,233}=\rho_{311,311}=:z\in\mathbb{R}

(iv)

\rho_{133,133}=\rho_{211,211}=\rho_{322,322}=:z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}

The additional properties in (i)-(iv) follow using the fact that

is invariant under the cyclic permutation

3.15

\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
has a
\theta
-invariant
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}
-certificate
\rho\in\text{\rm CLDUI}^{(2)}_{3}

has the desired properties. Observe that
C_{1}:=\{111,212,221,313,331\}
,
C_{2}:=\theta(C)
,

(where

First, we have

\rho_{111,111}=\rho_{\theta(111),\theta(111)}=\rho_{222,222}=\rho_{\theta(222),\theta(222)}=\rho_{333,333}.

For

Theorem 3.20
.

For the state
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
from Definition
3.16
, the following are equivalent:

\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{3}\Longleftrightarrow\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}_{3}\Longleftrightarrow a\geq 1\text{ and }a^{\prime}\geq 1.

Assume
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}_{3}
, we show
a,a^{\prime}\geq 1
. Let
\rho
be a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}
-certificate for
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
that satisfies the properties claimed in Lemma
3.19
.
Since
\rho
satisfies (
27
), we get

1=(\rho_{a,a^{\prime}})_{11,11}=\text{\rm Tr}_{B}(\rho)_{11,11}=\rho_{111,111}+\rho_{112,112}+\rho_{113,113}=3x,

which implies
x=\frac{1}{3}
. Similarly, we get

\displaystyle a=\text{\rm Tr}_{B}(\rho)_{12,12}=x+z+y,\quad a^{\prime}=\text{\rm Tr}_{B}(\rho)_{21,21}=y+z^{\prime}+x,

which implies
z=a-y-\frac{1}{3}
and
z^{\prime}=a^{\prime}-y-\frac{1}{3}
.

We now use the fact that
\rho^{T_{B[1]}}
is positive semidefinite (condition (
26
)).
Using the submatrix of
\rho^{T_{B[1]}}
indexed by
\{123,213\}
(in that order), we obtain

\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{123,123}&\rho_{113,223}\cr\rho_{223,113}&\rho_{213,213}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y&x\\
x&y\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0,

implying
y\geq x=\frac{1}{3}
. Using the submatrix of
\rho^{T_{B[1]}}
indexed by
\{111,122,133\}
(in that order) gives

\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{111,111}&\rho_{121,112}&\rho_{131,113}\cr\rho_{112,121}&\rho_{122,122}&\rho_{132,123}\cr\rho_{113,131}&\rho_{123,132}&\rho_{133,133}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x&x&x\\
x&z&y\\
x&y&z^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

By taking the Schur complement w.r.t. the upper left corner and using the values of
z,z^{\prime}
, we obtain

\begin{pmatrix}z-x&y-x\cr y-x&z^{\prime}-x\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a-y-\frac{2}{3}&y-\frac{1}{3}\\
y-\frac{1}{3}&a^{\prime}-y-\frac{2}{3}\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0.

In particular,
a-y-{2\over 3},a^{\prime}-y-{2\over 3}\geq 0
. Combining with
y\geq\frac{1}{3}
, we get that
a,a^{\prime}\geq 1
, as desired.

Assume now
a,a^{\prime}\geq 1
, we show that
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{3}
. For this, observe that the matrices
X,Y
in Definition
3.16
satisfy

X=J_{3}+X^{\prime}
,
Y=J_{3}
, where
X^{\prime}=X-J\geq 0
since
a,a^{\prime}\geq 1
.
So, we have

\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}=\rho_{(J_{3},J_{3})}+\rho_{(X^{\prime},0)}
.
The state
\rho_{(J_{3},J_{3})}
is separable (e.g., since it is equal to
\Pi_{\text{\rm CLDUI}_{3}}(ee^{*}\otimes ee^{*})
, with
e
the all-ones vector).
Also, the state
\rho_{(X^{\prime},0)}
is separable (using, e.g., (
50
)).
Hence, the state
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
is the sum of two separable states and thus
\rho_{a,a^{\prime}}
is separable.
This concludes the proof. ∎

4
Dual of the symmetric DPS hierarchy and matrix copositivity

Given a matrix
A\in{\mathcal{S}}^{n}
, consider the matrix
M_{A,0}\in\text{\rm CLDUI}_{n}

as defined in relation (
49
) and the associated polynomial

\langle M_{A,0},xx^{*}\otimes xx^{*}\rangle=\langle A,(x\circ\overline{x})(x\circ\overline{x})^{T}\rangle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}A_{ij}|x_{i}|^{2}|x_{j}|^{2}=f_{A}(|x_{1}|^{2},\ldots,|x_{n}|^{2}),

where
f_{A}
is the polynomial from (
34
). Hence,

M_{A,0}\in(\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n})^{*}\Longleftrightarrow A\in\text{\rm COP}_{n}.

Moreover, if
A_{ij}<0
for some
i<j
, then
\langle M_{A,0},D_{ij}D_{ij}^{*}\rangle=A_{ij}<0
; hence
M_{A,0}
provides an entanglement witness that separates the entangled state
D_{ij}D_{ij}^{*}
from
\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n}
.
So, when restricting to matrices with diagonal unitary invariance, there is a tight connection between the dual cone
(\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n})^{*}
and the copositive cone
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
, as shown in
[
MATS21
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Recently, it is shown in
[
GNP25
]
that this connection extends to the hierarchies
(\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
for
(\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n})^{*}
, and
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
for
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
. In this section we revisit this connection. First, we present a characterization of the dual cone
(\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
that fully mirrors the result in Theorem
2.4
for
(\mathrm{DPS}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
. Then, we use it to offer a simple argument for the connection between
(\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}_{n}^{(t)})^{*}
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
, using only the basic correspondence (
24
) between real-sos Hermitian polynomials in complex variables and sos polynomials in real variables.

4.1
The dual of the symmetric DPS hierarchy

For
M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
, recall the polynomial
F_{M}(x,\overline{x},y,\overline{y})\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x},y,\overline{y}]_{1,1,1,1}
as defined in (
37
). By restricting to one set of variables, i.e., setting
y=x
, we obtain the polynomial

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{M}(x,\overline{x}):=F_{M}(x,\overline{x},x,\overline{x})=\langle M,xx^{*}\otimes xx^{*}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]_{2,2},

(61)

with degree 2 in
x
and degree 2 in
\overline{x}
. So,
M\in(\text{\rm SEP}^{\text{\rm BS}}_{n})^{*}
means
F_{M}(x,\overline{x},x,\overline{x})\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
(equivalently, for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
.
In the next result we characterize when
M
belongs to the dual of the relaxation
\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n}
, thus giving an analog of Theorem
2.4
for the symmetric setting.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider a matrix
M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
and an integer
t\geq 1
. Set
\tau=\lfloor{(t+1)/2}\rfloor
.
The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

M\in(\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
.

(ii)

The polynomial
\|x\|^{2(t-1)}\widetilde{F}_{M}(x,\overline{x})=\|x\|^{2t}\langle M,xx^{*}\otimes xx^{*}\rangle\in\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x}]_{2(t+1),2(t+1)}
is r-sos.

(iii)

There exist matrices
B,W_{0},W_{1},\ldots,W_{\tau}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes t})
such that

M\otimes I_{n}^{\otimes(t-1)}=B+\sum_{s=0}^{\tau}W_{,}

where
B\in\mathrm{Herm}(S^{t+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp},\ W_{s}\in{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}\text{ for }s=0,1,\ldots,\tau.

In comparison with Theorem
2.4
, we are now asking in (iii) that the matrix
B
belongs to the larger space

\mathrm{Herm}(S^{t+1}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp}
, which contains the space
\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))^{\perp}

involved in Theorem
2.4
(iii). In addition, we only need the subspaces
{\mathcal{W}}^{(t)}_{n,s}
for
s\leq\tau=\lfloor{(t+1)/2}\rfloor
.

Remark 4.2
.

The set
\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n}
corresponds to the set
\text{\rm PPTBExt}^{(K_{t+1})}_{n}
considered in
[
GNP25
]
.
The characterization of the dual of
\text{\rm PPTBExt}^{(t)}_{n}
given in

[
GNP25

corresponds to the equivalence of items (i) and (iii) in Theorem
4.1
.
The additional characterization (ii) in terms of r-sos polynomials, which is present in Theorem

(as well as in Theorem

), is not given in
[

]
. Showing this characterization (ii) constitutes in fact the most technical part of the proof.
On the other hand, it
is especially relevant since it is directly comparable to the characterization (
35
) in terms of sums of squares for matrices

.

Before giving the proof, note that, as
in the general (non-symmetric) case, the LDUI projection preserves membership in
\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n}
and its dual cone, a property we will use in the next section.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
\rho_{AB},M\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})
. Then,

\langle\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}}(M),\rho_{AB}\rangle=\langle M,\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}}(\rho_{AB}\rangle.

Assume
\rho_{AB}\in{\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n}}
, and let
\rho_{AB[t]}
be a
\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}
-certificate for it.
Then, the projection of this certificate

\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}^{(t)}}(\rho_{AB[t]})=\int_{{\mathcal{DU}}_{n}}U^{\otimes(t+1)}\rho_{AB[t]}(U^{\otimes(t+1)})^{*}dU

is a
\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}
-certificate for
\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}}(\rho_{AB})
, showing

\Pi_{\text{\rm LDUI}}(\rho_{AB})\in\smash{\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n}}
.
∎

The proof is along the same lines as for Theorem
2.4
in
[
FF20
]
. There are, however, some differences since one needs to restrict to
s\leq\tau=\lfloor(t+1)/2\rfloor
in (iii), which adds some technicalities. Our argument is also more compact.
Let

(62)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{s=0}^{t}\underbrace{\langle a_{\ell,s},\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)}\rangle\cdot\langle{a_{\ell,t+1-s}},\overline{x}^{\otimes(t+1-s)}\otimes x^{\otimes s}\rangle}_{=T_{\ell,s}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}T_{\ell,s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\langle a_{\ell,s},\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)}\rangle\cdot\langle{a_{\ell,t+1-s}},\overline{x}^{\otimes(t+1-s)}\otimes x^{\otimes s}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\langle a_{\ell,s},\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)}\rangle\cdot\overline{\langle a_{\ell,s},\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}(a_{\ell,s})^{*}(\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)})(\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)})^{*}a_{\ell,s}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell,s}(a_{\ell,s})^{*},(\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)})\ (\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)})^{*}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{s},(\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)})\ (\overline{x}^{\otimes s}\otimes x^{\otimes(t+1-s)})^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{s}^{T_{B}[s]},x^{\otimes(t+1)}(x^{\otimes(t+1)})^{*}\rangle.

Above, at the last but one line, we set

at both arguments in the inner product.
Putting things together we obtain

\displaystyle p=\sum_{\ell}q_{\ell}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{s=0}^{t}T_{\ell,s}=\sum_{\ell}\sum_{s=0}^{\tau}c_{s}T_{\ell,s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\tau}c_{s}\sum_{\ell}T_{\ell,s}=\Big\langle\sum_{s=0}^{\tau}c_{s}A_{s}^{B[s]},x^{\otimes(t+1)}(x^{\otimes(t+1)})^{*}\Big\rangle.

.
This shows that (iii) holds, since

(because

∎

Theorem 4.5
.

4.3

As an illustration we consider here a class of Bose symmetric states with LDOI structure. Given

\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&a\cr a&1&a\cr a&a&1\end{pmatrix},Y=\begin{pmatrix}1&b&b\cr b&1&b\cr b&b&1\end{pmatrix},\ \rho(a,b)=\begin{pmatrix}1&b&b\cr b&1&b\cr b&b&1\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}a&a\cr a&a\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}a&a\cr a&a\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}a&a\cr a&a\end{pmatrix},

where the first block is indexed by

and the last three blocks are indexed, respectively, by
\{12,21\}

Lemma 4.6
.

Given scalars
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\rho(a,b)\in\text{\rm SEP}_{3}\Longleftrightarrow\rho(a,b)\in\text{\rm DPS}_{3}^{(1)}\Longleftrightarrow-{1\over 2}\leq b\text{ and }|b|\leq a\leq 1.

Only two implications need a proof. First, assume
\rho(a,b)\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(1)}_{3}
, we show that
-{1\over 2}\leq b
and
|b|\leq a\leq 1
. Indeed, the first condition follows since
\rho(a,b)\succeq 0
and the second one from the fact that
\rho(a,b)^{T_{B}}\succeq 0
, since

\rho(a,b)^{T_{B}}=\rho_{(X,X,Y)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&a\cr a&1&a\cr a&a&1\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}a&b\cr b&a\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}a&b\cr b&a\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}a&b\cr b&a\end{pmatrix}.

Assume now that
-{1\over 2}\leq b
and
|b|\leq a\leq 1
, we show that
\rho(a,b)\in\text{\rm SEP}_{3}
. For this note that
the set

\Big\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|b|\leq a\leq 1,\ -{1\over 2}\leq b\Big\}

is convex bounded, and equals the convex hull of the four points

(0,0)
,
(1,1)
,
\big({1\over 2},-{1\over 2}\big)
, and
\big(1,-{1\over 2}\big)
. Hence, it suffices to show that
\rho(a,b)
is separable for each of them. For
(a,b)=(0,0)
we have

\rho(0,0)=(e_{1}e_{1}^{*})^{\otimes 2}+(e_{2}e_{2}^{*})^{\otimes 2}+(e_{3}e_{3}^{*})^{\otimes 2}\in\text{\rm SEP}_{3}.

The all-ones bipartite state
(ee^{*})^{\otimes 2}
(with
e=\sum_{i=1}^{3}e_{i}
) is separable, hence also its projection
\rho(1,1)=\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}}((ee^{*})^{\otimes 2})
is separable. Next, one can verify that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho\Big({1\over 2},-{1\over 2}\Big)&={1\over 2}\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}}((xx^{*})^{\otimes 2}+(yy^{*})^{\otimes 2}+(zz^{*})^{\otimes 2}),\\
&\text{ with }x=\begin{pmatrix}0\cr 1\cr\mathbf{i}\end{pmatrix},y=\begin{pmatrix}1\cr 0\cr\mathbf{i}\end{pmatrix},z=\begin{pmatrix}1\cr\mathbf{i}\cr 0\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(66)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho\Big(1,-{1\over 2}\Big)&=\Pi_{\text{\rm LDOI}}((uu^{*})^{\otimes 2}+(vv^{*})^{\otimes 2}),\\
&\text{ with }u=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\mathbf{i}\pi\over 3}\cr e^{-\mathbf{i}\pi\over 3}\cr 1\end{pmatrix},\ \ v=\begin{pmatrix}e^{2\mathbf{i}\pi\over 3}\cr e^{-2\mathbf{i}\pi\over 3}\cr 1\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

(67)

Hence,
\rho(a,b)\in\text{\rm SEP}_{3}
in both cases, again because the LDOI projection preserves separability.

Remark 4.7

In addition, observe that, for

5
Implementation

In this section we consider the question of how to implement the
DPS
hierarchy more efficiently in practice. For this, we reformulate it using the moment approach within the polynomial optimization framework. We do this first in the generic case and then we consider the
CLDUI
and
LDOI
cases. We conclude with a comparison of runtimes in the different regimes by showing how the developed sparsity reduction technique can be used to tackle a class of examples motivated by the PPT
2
conjecture from quantum information theory.

5.1
The
DPS
hierarchy in the moment formalism

Suppose one is given a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{AB}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n})_{+}
and one wants to test whether
\rho_{AB}
lies in a given level
t
of the
DPS
hierarchy, i.e., whether
\rho_{AB}\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
.
The obvious way to do so is to implement the semidefinite program modeling definition (
28
), asking for the existence of an extended state
\rho_{AB[t]}\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes S^{t}(\mathbb{C}^{n}))
that is positive semidefinite under partial transposition of the
B
-registers and recovers the original state
\rho_{AB}
by taking a partial trace. We refer to this original viewpoint as the ‘tensor formalism’.

In the tensor formalism the resulting SDP inherits redundancies arising from the symmetries in the
t

B
-registers as required in (
25
): for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{t}
and
i_{0}\underline{i}\in[n]^{t+1}
, the columns (and rows) indexed by
i_{0}\underline{i}
and
i_{0}\underline{i}^{\sigma}
are identical, i.e.,
(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\cdot,i_{0}\underline{i}^{\sigma}}=(\rho_{AB[t]})_{\cdot,i_{0}\underline{i}}
. Hence, one can set up an equivalent, reduced SDP that is obtained by keeping only one column out of the orbit consisting of the columns indexed by
i_{0}\underline{i}^{\sigma}
for
\sigma\in\mathfrak{G}_{t}
.
An example of this fact was seen in Lemma
3.18
(and its proof), where it was used to show that the block of a
\text{\rm DPS}^{(2)}
-certificate indexed, e.g., by
\{123,132\}
, is a scalar multiple of the all-ones matrix.

A way to carry out this reduction more systematically is to consider the
DPS
hierarchy in the ‘moment formalism’, as has been developed in
[
GLS21
]
. For this, note that two elements
i_{0}\underline{i},j_{0}\underline{j}\in[n]^{t+1}
indexing the extended state
\rho_{AB[t]}
lie in the same orbit of
\mathfrak{G}_{t}
if and only if
i_{0}=j_{0}
and
\alpha(\underline{i})=\alpha(\underline{j})

\alpha(i_{0})\alpha(\underline{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\times\mathbb{N}^{n}
(indexing monomials) instead of sequences
i_{0}\underline{i}\in[n]^{t+1}

More precisely, the moment approach works as follows. We consider Hermitian linear functionals
L\in(\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x},y,\overline{y}]_{2,2t})^{*}

L:\mathbb{C}[x,\overline{x},y,\overline{y}]_{2,2t}\to\mathbb{C}

and of degree

L(\overline{p})=\overline{L(p)}

Then, the corresponding moment matrix of

its

L(x^{\alpha}\overline{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}}y^{\beta}\overline{y}^{\beta^{\prime}}\cdot\overline{x^{\tilde{\alpha}}\overline{x}^{\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}}y^{\tilde{\beta}}\overline{y}^{\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}}})=L(x^{\alpha+\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}}\overline{x}^{\alpha^{\prime}+\tilde{\alpha}}y^{\beta+\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}}\overline{y}^{\beta^{\prime}+\tilde{\beta}}).

Hence,

is a Hermitian matrix since

is Hermitian. Moreover, we have

if and only if

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n,t\in\mathbb{N}
and
s^{\prime}\in\{-t,-t+2,\dots,t-2,t\}

Hence, the ratio of the largest block size in the LDOI regime to the largest block size in the generic regime is given by

\max_{s^{\prime}}\frac{N_{s^{\prime},t}}{|I_{1,s^{\prime}}|}\leq\max_{s^{\prime}}\frac{(\frac{t+|s^{\prime}|}{2})!}{n^{\lceil\frac{t+|s^{\prime}|}{{4}}\rceil}}\leq{t!\over{n^{\lceil{t\over 4}\rceil}}}.

Fix an arbitrary
(\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\beta}^{\prime})\in I_{1,s^{\prime}}
. Assume first
0\leq s^{\prime}
. We want to count how many
(\alpha,\beta,\beta^{\prime})\in I_{1,s^{\prime}}
exist such that
\alpha+\beta+\beta^{\prime}\equiv\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}+\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}\text{ (mod 2)}
. Define the odd support of
\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}+\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}
as the set
O\subseteq[n]
consisting of the indices
i\in[n]
for which the
i
th entry of the vector
\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}+\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}
is odd. Note that
|O|\equiv|\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}+\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}|=t+1
(mod 2). Therefore, we need to count the sequences
(\alpha,\beta,\beta^{\prime})\in I_{1,s^{\prime}}
whose odd support is
O
.

First, we pick
\alpha
and
\beta^{\prime}
arbitrarily, for which there are
n\cdot\binom{n-1+\frac{t-s^{\prime}}{2}}{n-1}
options. Denote the odd support of
\alpha+\beta^{\prime}
as

\beta

\beta
must be equal to the symmetric difference
O\Delta O^{\prime}
of
O
and
O^{\prime}
. Note that
|O^{\prime}|\equiv|\alpha+\beta^{\prime}|=1+(t-s^{\prime})/2
(mod 2).
So, one must have
\beta=2\gamma+\chi^{O\triangle O^{\prime}}
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
\chi^{O\Delta O^{\prime}}
is the characteristic vector of
O\Delta O^{\prime}
(with
i
th entry 1 if
i\in O\Delta O^{\prime}
and 0 otherwise). Since
|\beta|=\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}
, it follows that
|\gamma|=(\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}-|O\triangle O^{\prime}|)/2
. This is a valid weight because

|O\Delta O^{\prime}|\equiv|O|+|O^{\prime}|\equiv t+(t-s^{\prime})/2\equiv(t+s)/2
(mod 2).
In total we get
\binom{n-1+(\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}-|O\triangle O^{\prime}|)/2}{n-1}

\gamma

\beta

|O\triangle O^{\prime}|
minimal, which depends on the parity of
\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}

a=0

\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}

\displaystyle\begin{split}N_{s^{\prime},t}&\leq\ n\binom{n-1+\frac{t-s^{\prime}}{2}}{n-1}\binom{n-1+(\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}-a)/2}{n-1}\\
&=n\binom{n-1+\frac{t-s^{\prime}}{2}}{n-1}\binom{n-1+\lfloor\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{4}\rfloor}{n-1}.\end{split}

|I_{1,s^{\prime}}|=n{n-1+(t-s^{\prime})/2\choose n-1}{n-1+(t+s^{\prime})/2\choose 2}

\frac{N_{s^{\prime},t}}{|I_{1,s^{\prime}}|}\leq\frac{\binom{n-1+\lfloor\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{4}\rfloor}{n-1}}{\binom{n-1+\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}}{n-1}}\leq\big(\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}\big)!\cdot\frac{n^{\lfloor\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{4}\rfloor}}{n^{\frac{t+s^{\prime}}{2}}},

Concluding remarks

In this paper we explore how structural properties of quantum bipartite states can be exploited to design more efficient variations of the semidefinite hierarchy
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
for testing separability and detecting quantum entanglement.
On the one hand, we consider bipartite (CLDUI, LDUI, LDOI) states with diagonal unitary invariance.
These states admit a block diagonal sparsity pattern that can be transferred to any given level of the DPS hierarchy. By applying block diagonalization and, in addition, reformulating the DPS hierarchy in the moment formalism, one obtains substantial matrix size reduction in the SDP models,
so that higher levels can be computed in practice.

On the other hand, we consider Bose symmetric bipartite states, i.e., states that are symmetric in their two registers. This symmetry property can again be transferred to any given level of the DPS hierarchy. This leads to the stronger symmetry-adapted
\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n}
hierarchy. We characterize its dual, in terms of existence of a r-sum-of-squares representation for an associated complex polynomial. This characterization mirrors the known characterization of the dual of
\text{\rm DPS}^{(t)}_{n}
. In addition, it is closely related to the sum-of-squares characterization of the approximation cone
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
for
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
.

The completely positive cone
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
and its dual
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
play an important role within both settings. Indeed, separable bipartite states with CLDUI, LDUI (LDOI) sparsity structure can be captured by pairwise (triplewise) completely positive matrix pairs (triples), leading to the cones
\text{\rm PCP}_{n},\text{\rm TCP}_{n}
that generalize
\text{\rm CP}_{n}
. On the dual side, there is a close relationship between
\text{\rm COP}_{n}
and the dual of the cone of Bose symmetric separable states, which extends to their respective conic approximations by
{\mathcal{K}}^{(t)}_{n}
and
(\widetilde{\text{\rm DPS}}^{(t)}_{n})^{*}
.

Hence, there are interesting connections among several topics, ranging from quantum information to semidefinite optimization, matrix cones, and polynomial optimization.
We now conclude with mentioning some open questions.

The symmetry-adapted DPS hierarchy applies to Bose symmetric states, so we have the inclusion

We give here some background information about the PPT

Set

\Phi:M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\to M_{n}(\mathbb{C})

We group some definitions about linear maps in

and refer, e.g., to
[
SN21
,
SN22
]
and further references therein for a detailed treatment.

Definition A.1
.

Consider a linear map
\Phi\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

\bullet

\Phi
is called positive if
\Phi(X)\succeq 0
for all
X\in\mathrm{Herm}(\mathbb{C}^{n})_{+}

\bullet

\Phi
is called completely positive (CP)
14
14

14

This notion of CP linear map is classical in quantum information and should not be confused with the earlier notion of completely positive matrix.
if the map

\bullet

\Phi
is called PPT if both
\Phi

\bullet

Linear maps

for all

There is a well-known correspondence between maps

and bipartite states in

via their Choi (or Choi-Jamiołkowski) matrix

Definition A.2
.

For

J(\Phi)=\sum_{r,s=1}^{n}\Phi(e_{r}e_{s}^{*})\otimes e_{r}e_{s}^{*}\quad\text{ i.e., }\quad(J(\Phi))_{ij,kl}=\Phi(e_{j}e_{l}^{*})_{ik}\text{ for }i,j,k,l\in[n].

Conversely, any state

Note that

\Phi

[

SN22

Lemma A.3

Let
\Phi\in\mathcal{T}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

its associated Choi state. Then,

\displaystyle\Phi\text{ is CP}

\displaystyle\Phi\circ\sf T\text{ is CP}

\displaystyle\Phi\text{ is PPT}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow J(\Phi)\in\text{\rm DPS}^{(1)}_{n},

\displaystyle\Phi\text{ is entanglement breaking}

\Phi

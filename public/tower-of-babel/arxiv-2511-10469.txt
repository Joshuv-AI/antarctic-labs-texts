Connecting conformal dimension and Poincaré profiles
David Hume, John M. Mackay

Abstract
. We strengthen the connection between the Ahlfors-regular (AR) conformal dimension Confdim ⁡ ( Z ) \Confdim(Z) of a compact AR metric space Z Z and a certain critical exponent of the Poincaré profiles p Λ p_{\Lambda} of its hyperbolic cone X X in the sense of Bonk–Schramm. We prove that the two values are equal in two situations: firstly, when Z Z is a product C × [ 0 , 1 ] C\times[0,1] where C C is a compact AR metric space; and secondly when X X is quasi-isometric to a Heintze manifold ℝ n ⋊ A ℝ \mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R} where A ∈ GL ⁡ ( n , ℝ ) A\in\GL(n,\mathbb{R}) is diagonalisable. A key tool is a lower bound for p Λ p_{\Lambda} for combinatorial round trees which also applies to various random group models and families of Coxeter groups. We also show that for a torsion free hyperbolic group G G , p Λ ​ ( G ) > 1 p_{\Lambda}(G)>1 if and only if Benjamini–Schramm–Timár’s separation profile grows faster than r α r^{\alpha} for some α > 0 \alpha>0 , if and only if Confdim ⁡ ( ∂ ∞ G ) > 1 \Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)>1 . On the other hand, we find new, non-virtually-Fuchsian examples of groups with the same separation profile as ℍ 2 \mathbb{H}^{2} . All these results imply various obstructions to coarse and regular embeddings of such groups.



1.
Introduction

There is a classical correspondence between isometries of hyperbolic spaces and Möbius transformations of their sphere at infinity.
This was generalized by Paulin and Bonk–Schramm to give a correspondence between quasi-isometries of Gromov hyperbolic spaces and quasisymmetric homeomorphisms of their boundaries, making it natural to seek to relate the large-scale geometric properties of Gromov hyperbolic spaces with analytic properties of their boundaries and vice-versa.

One highly studied quasisymmetry invariant of compact metric spaces is Pansu’s conformal dimension
[
25
]
. In previous work of the authors and Romain Tessera,
we demonstrated that a critical exponent of “Poincaré profiles”, that is, Poincaré inequalities on bounded subsets, equals the conformal dimension of the boundary at infinity for certain Gromov hyperbolic spaces including classical hyperbolic spaces
[
14
,
15
]
. There are two key benefits of working with the critical exponent of Poincaré profiles: it is defined for all proper, geodesic metric spaces; and, in addition to being invariant under quasi-isometries, it behaves monotonically with respect to a much broader class of maps called regular maps, which include coarse embeddings, despite the fact that these maps typically do not have good boundary extensions.

The goal of this paper is to further develop our understanding of the relationship between this critical exponent and conformal dimension, in particular determining situations where the two values agree, and where existing bounds on conformal dimension can be reproved using Poincaré profiles.

As many lower bounds on conformal dimension are proved by finding embedded “round trees”, we start by studying their Poincaré profiles. We use this to determine many situations where the conformal dimension of the boundary of a hyperbolic space agrees with a critical exponent for the Poincaré profiles, with consequent non-embedding results.

We also establish two new phenomena for hyperbolic groups around conformal dimension
1
. First, we use (irregular) round trees to show that torsion-free hyperbolic groups with conformal dimension
>1
have
L^{1}
-Poincaré profiles, i.e. separation profiles
[
1
]
, which grow
\gtrsim r^{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon>0
.
Second, we show that many hyperbolic groups whose boundaries have conformal dimension
1
, but do not attain this value, nevertheless have the same Poincaré profiles as the hyperbolic plane.

Let us now describe our results in more detail.

1.1.
Main concepts

We recall the definition of the Poincaré profiles of a graph.

Definition 1.1

(
[
14
, §1.1]
)
.

For a finite graph
\Gamma
with vertex set
V(\Gamma)
and edge set
E(\Gamma)
, for
p\in[1,\infty]
its
L^{p}
-Poincaré constant
is

h^{p}(\Gamma)=\inf\left\{\left.\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{p}}{\|f\|_{p}}\ \right|\ f:V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R},\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}f(v)=0,f\not\equiv 0\right\}.

Here,
|\nabla f|:E(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
|\nabla f|(e)=|f(x)-f(y)|
where
e\in E(\Gamma)
has endpoints
x,y\in V(\Gamma)
.

For an infinite bounded degree graph
X
, and
p\in[1,\infty]
, its
L^{p}
-Poincaré profile

\Lambda_{X}^{p}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is

\Lambda_{X}^{p}(r)=\sup\{|\Gamma|\,h^{p}(\Gamma):\Gamma\leq X,|\Gamma|\leq r\},

where
|\Gamma|:=|V(\Gamma)|
.

We consider functions on
\mathbb{N}
up to the equivalence relation
\simeq
, where
f\lesssim g
if there exists
C>0
so that
f(r)\leq Cg(Cr+C)+C
for all
r
, and
f\simeq g
if
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
.

A function
f:VX\to VY
is a
regular map
if there exists
C>0
so that
f
is
C
-Lipschitz and for all
y\in VY
,
|f^{-1}(y)|\leq C
. We say
X

regularly embeds
in
Y
if there exists a regular map from
X
to
Y
.
In particular, coarse embeddings between bounded degree graphs are regular maps, as is the inclusion map for a finitely generated subgroup of a finitely generated group.

If there exists a regular map
f:VX\to VY
then
\Lambda_{X}^{p}\lesssim\Lambda_{Y}^{p}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]

[
14
]
.
Thus,
\Lambda_{X}^{p}
is a quasi-isometry invariant of
X
for each
p\in[1,\infty]
. In particular, if
X
is any metric space quasi-isometric to a bounded degree graph
Y
, we can set
\Lambda_{X}^{p}:=\Lambda_{Y}^{p}
and then
\Lambda_{X}^{p}
is well-defined up to
\simeq
, independently of the choice of
Y
.

For
p=1
,
\Lambda_{X}^{1}\simeq\sep_{X}
, where
\sep_{X}
is the separation profile of Benjamini–Schramm–Timár
[
1
]
.

Let us now turn to conformal dimension.
A metric space
Z
is Ahlfors
Q
-regular if there exists a Borel regular measure
\mu
on
Z
and a constant
C>0
so that for any
z\in Z
,
0<r\leq\diam(Z)
,
\frac{1}{C}r^{Q}\leq\mu(B(z,r))\leq Cr^{Q}
.
The
(Ahlfors regular) conformal dimension
of
Z
, denoted
\Confdim(Z)
, is the infimum of all
Q
such that
Z
is quasisymmetric to an Ahlfors
Q
-regular space.
As the conformal dimension is a quasisymmetry invariant, for a Gromov hyperbolic space
X
,
\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}X)
is well-defined and depends only on the quasi-isometry class of
X
.
It can be more sensitive than the topology of the boundary. For example,
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
and
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
are not quasi-isometric as
\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{4})=3
and
\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2})=4
, even though
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
and
\partial_{\infty}\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
are homeomorphic.
An important example for us is that when
Z
is a compact Ahlfors
Q
-regular space,
\Confdim(Z\times[0,1])=Q+1
, see
[
18
, Example 4.1.9]
and references therein.

In earlier work of the authors and Romain Tessera, the Poincaré profiles of each rank-one symmetric space
X
were computed, in particular, for
p<Q:=\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}X)
we have
\Lambda_{X}^{p}(r)\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{Q}}
,
\Lambda_{X}^{Q}(r)\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{Q}}\log^{\frac{1}{Q}}(r)
, while for
p>Q
we have
\Lambda_{X}^{p}(r)\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

[
14
, Theorem 12]
.
The lower bound in the case
p>Q
comes from an embedded
3
-regular tree
T_{3}
which has
\Lambda_{T_{3}}^{p}(r)\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
. This threshold, beyond which trees are the critical obstruction to Poincaré inequalities, motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a graph
X
, we define its
critical exponent for Poincaré profiles

p_{\Lambda}(X)
as

(1.3)

p_{\Lambda}(X):=\inf\{p\geq 1:\Lambda^{p}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\}.

This critical exponent satisfies the following monotonicity property amongst bounded degree graphs:
p_{\Lambda}(X)\leq p_{\Lambda}(Y)
whenever
X
regularly embeds into
Y
. Moreover, the set in equation (
1.3
) is always connected, see Corollary
2.3
. We note that these results do not assume hyperbolicity, and indeed, the critical exponent can be useful in other scenarios; for instance, the critical exponent of any finitely generated nilpotent group with polynomial growth of degree
d
is exactly
1-\frac{1}{d}

[
14
]
.

In the definition here, for graphs containing a regularly embedded copy of
T_{3}
(such as Cayley graphs of non-elementary hyperbolic groups) we could equivalently have taken “
\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
”, see Corollary
2.3
.

1.2.
Results on critical exponents

A goal of this paper is to progress our understanding of the following question. Recall that for any compact Ahlfors regular space
Z
there is a geodesic Gromov hyperbolic graph
\Cone(Z)
so that
Z
is a boundary of
\Cone(Z)

[
4
]
. If
G
is a Gromov hyperbolic group,
\Cone(\partial_{\infty}G)
is quasi-isometric to
G
.

Question 1.4

(
[
15
, Question 8.6]
)
.

For which compact Ahlfors regular metric spaces
Z
does the equality
p_{\Lambda}(\Cone(Z))=\Confdim(Z)
hold? Is there equality for every boundary of a hyperbolic group?

A positive answer to this question for hyperbolic groups would imply a positive answer to the following question.

Question 1.5
.

If a hyperbolic group
G
regularly embeds in a hyperbolic group
H
, then
\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)\leq\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}H)
.

We always have one inequality in Question
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
15
, Theorem 5.16]
)
.

Let
Z
be a compact Ahlfors regular metric space and let
\Cone(Z)
be the hyperbolic cone of
Z
. Then

p_{\Lambda}(\Cone(Z))\leq\Confdim(Z).

As already mentioned, the first class of examples with a positive answer to Question
1.4
are provided by previous work of the authors with Tessera, combined with a result of Bonk and Kleiner.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
X
is quasi-isometric to a non-elementary Gromov hyperbolic group whose boundary attains its Ahlfors regular conformal dimension, e.g., a rank-one symmetric space or a Bourdon Fuchsian building, then
p_{\Lambda}(X)=\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}X)
.

Proof.

For
p>\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}X)
,
\Lambda^{p}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
by
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
.
If
\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}X)=1
then we are done.
Otherwise, by
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
if
X
attains its Ahlfors regular conformal dimension
Q>1
then
\partial_{\infty}X
admits a
(1,Q)
-Poincaré inequality in the sense of Heinonen–Koskela, so

\Lambda^{Q}_{X}(r)\gtrsim r^{1-1/Q}\log(r)^{1/Q}
by
[
14
, Theorem 11.3]
.
∎

Spaces of the form
Z\times[0,1]
are a classic family of metric spaces which attain their conformal dimension but are quite different to the boundaries of Theorem
1.7
, for example they rarely admit Poincaré inequalities.

We also find a positive answer to Question
1.4
for these spaces.
(We slightly simplify statements of results in this introduction.)

Theorem 1.8

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
Z
be a compact Ahlfors regular space.
Then

p_{\Lambda}(\Cone(Z\times[0,1]))=\Confdim(Z\times[0,1]).

We deduce this from the special case (Theorem
4.1
) when

is the boundary of a “combinatorial round tree”, a variation of a construction of Gromov introduced in
[
20
]
.
(See Stark
[

]
for a recent survey.)

We apply this to give a positive answer to Question
1.4
for a certain class of Heintze groups, which also partially answers
[
15
, Question 8.7]
.

Corollary 1.9

(Corollary
5.4
)
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{A}\mathbb{R}
where
A\in\GL(n,\mathbb{R})
is diagonalisable with positive eigenvalues. Then

p_{\Lambda}(G)=\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G).

Combinatorial round trees also feature in the following contexts, where they are used to give lower bounds on conformal dimension.

•

boundaries of random groups in the few relator, polynomially many relator, and Gromov density models
[
20
,
21
,
10
]
, see Section

•

boundaries of hyperbolic Coxeter groups with underlying graph a complete graph and edge labels uniformly bounded, getting infinitely many quasi-isometry classes of hyperbolic groups with Pontryagin sphere boundary

, see Section

•

boundaries of hyperbolic right-angled Coxeter groups (RACG), getting infinitely many quasi-isometry classes of RACG with Pontryagin sphere boundary, and infinitely many quasi-isometry classes of RACG that virtually algebraically fibre for each prescribed virtual cohomological dimension

Thanks to Theorem

.

For example, a Heintze group

Question 1.10

Find a finitely generated group

1.3.
Results at conformal dimension
1

We are able to use techniques from
[
19
]
to build more irregular round trees to show that hyperbolic groups which do not split over finite or virtually cyclic subgroups have separation profiles larger than some positive power. Note that an infinite hyperbolic group

does not split over a finite or virtually cyclic subgroup if and only if
\partial_{\infty}G
is connected, locally connected and has no local cut points. Recall that Benjamini-Schramm-Timár’s separation profile
\sep_{X}
satisfies
\sep_{X}\simeq\Lambda_{X}^{1}

Theorem 1.11
.

If
G
is an infinite hyperbolic group that doesn’t split over a finite or virtually cyclic subgroup, either
G
is virtually co-compact Fuchsian (and so
\Lambda^{1}_{G}(r)\simeq\log(r)
) or
\Lambda^{1}_{G}(r)\gtrsim r^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.

Remark 1.12
.

An alternative, independent proof of Theorem

has been announced by Bensaid–Genevois–Tessera
[

]
.

As a consequence, we strengthen Lazarovich–Le Coz

which shows the following result under the stronger assumption
\Lambda^{1}_{G}(r)\lesssim\log r
.

Corollary 1.13
.

If
G
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group and
\Lambda^{1}_{G}(r)\not\gtrsim r^{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
, then
G
is virtually co-compact Fuchsian or splits over a finite or virtually cyclic subgroup.

Combining this with existing structural results on conformal dimension, we have the following trichotomy.

Corollary 1.14
.

Let

be an infinite hyperbolic group, virtually with no

\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)=1

\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)>1

\Lambda^{1}_{G}(r)\gtrsim r^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0

has an infinite quasi-convex subgroup which is not virtually Fuchsian and does not split over finite or virtually cyclic subgroups.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem

(see

[

∎

Corollary 1.15
.

If
G
is a hyperbolic group (virtually with no

2.
Critical exponents

We collect some auxilliary observations which clarify the importance of the critical exponent for Poincaré profiles (Definition
1.2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For any vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
, and any
q\geq p\geq 1
,

\|\mathbf{a}\|_{q}\leq\|\mathbf{a}\|_{p}\leq r^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\|\mathbf{a}\|_{q}
.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{a}=(a_{i})
. We may assume all
a_{i}\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\left(\sum a_{i}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left[\max a_{i}^{q-p}\right]\left[\sum a_{i}^{p}\right]\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left[\sum a_{i}^{p}\right]^{(q-p)/p+1}\right)^{1/q}=\left(\sum a_{i}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left[\sum a_{i}^{pq/p}\right]^{p/q}r^{1-p/q}\right)^{1/p}=\left(\sum a_{i}^{q}\right)^{1/q}r^{1/p-1/q}.\qed

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph. For any
q\geq p\geq 1
we have
\Lambda^{q}_{X}(r)\lesssim r^{1/p-1/q}\Lambda^{p}_{X}(r)
.

Proof.

Choose a subgraph
\Gamma
with
\Lambda^{q}_{X}(r)=|\Gamma|h^{q}(\Gamma)
,
and a non-constant function
f:V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}
with sum zero and
h^{p}(\Gamma)=\|\nabla f\|_{p}/\|f\|_{p}
.

By Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{q}_{X}(r)

\displaystyle\leq|\Gamma|\,h^{q}(\Gamma)\leq|\Gamma|\,\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{q}}{\|f\|_{q}}\leq|\Gamma|\,r^{1/p-1/q}\frac{\|\nabla f\|_{p}}{\|f\|_{p}}

\displaystyle=r^{1/p-1/q}\,|\Gamma|\,h^{p}(\Gamma)\leq r^{1/p-1/q}\Lambda^{p}(X).\qed

Since the
3
-regular tree
T_{3}
has
\Lambda_{T_{3}}^{p}(r)\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}

[
14
, Theorem 10.1]
, we observe the following.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
X
is a bounded degree graph, then

\left\{p\in[1,\infty):\Lambda_{X}^{p}(r)\lesssim r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right\}

is an interval of the form
(p_{\Lambda}(X),\infty)
,
[p_{\Lambda}(X),\infty)
or
\emptyset
(with
p_{\Lambda}(X)=\infty
).

Moreover, if
X
contains a regularly embedded
3
-regular tree, then

\left\{p\in[1,\infty):\Lambda_{X}^{p}(r)\simeq r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right\}=\left\{p\in[1,\infty):\Lambda_{X}^{p}(r)\lesssim r^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right\}.

There are examples of all three types of interval in the corollary: for
\mathbb{H}^{3}
this set is
(2,\infty)
,
for the union of a tree and
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
it is
[2,\infty)
,
and for
\mathbb{H}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
the set is
\emptyset
.
Recall that if
X
is a non-elementary hyperbolic group this set is non-empty since we have
p_{\Lambda}(X)\leq\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}X)<\infty
, see Theorem
1.6
.

The second author used a more combinatorial version of this construction in the study of random groups

The first definition we have of a round tree is as follows,

For

(1)

A_{\emptyset}\subset A

(2)

Each

is an infinite planar

and
R_{\mathbf{a}}

for all

.

(3)

Set

for

For

(4)

For each

to

For

Definition 3.2
.

A

with vertical branching

and horizontal branching
\in[H_{\\

where the vertex set

, and the following properties are satisfied.

(1)

For all

,

, where
\pi_{H}:V(\Gamma)\to[H]^{*}
and

(2)

For all

and

, if

then
(h,v)\in V
, and

are connected by a

.

(3)

If
(h,v)\in V

min},H_{\max}]

(4)

If

.

Every edge in

If

then

the

-regular round tree graph
, and denote it by

For an example of a portion of such a graph, with a faint corresponding combinatorial round tree, see Figure

Theorem 3.3
.

For any round tree graph
\Gamma
with vertical branching

and horizontal branching in

, one has

\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}\Gamma)\geq 1+\frac{\log V}{\log H}.

Moreover,
\partial_{\infty}RT^{H,V}
carries a visual metric bi-Lipschitz to

, and so
\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}RT^{H,V})=1+\frac{\log V}{\log H}
is attained.

Proof.

We have that
\Gamma
is the dual graph to a combinatorial round tree with vertical branching
V
and horizontal branching in
[2,H]
in the sense of Definition
3.1
. The lower bound then follows from
[
20
, §6]
, see
[
21
, Theorem 7.2]
.

For the upper bound:
RT^{H,V}
is the dual graph to a
\CAT(-1)
complex defined as follows. Let

be the origin, and choose a half-plane
U
with
o\in\partial U
. Divide
U
into concentric annuli
U_{i}=B(o,(i+1)\log(H))\setminus B(o,i\log(H))
,

, of width
\log(H)
.
Divide each
U_{i}
into
H^{i}

2
-cells using equally spaces radii centred at
o
. Then
RT^{H,1}
is exactly the dual graph of
U
with this
2
-complex structure. Moreover
RT^{H,V}
is the dual graph to the
\CAT(-1)
complex
W
resulting from gluing copies
\{U_{v}\}_{v\in[V]^{\mathbb{N}}}
of
U
together along the convex subsets
B(o,i\log(H))
in the appropriate way. Since the circumference of
\partial B(o,(i+1)\log(H))\asymp H^{i}
one sees that each
U
is quasi-isometric to
RT^{H,1}
, and
W
is quasi-isometric to
RT^{H,V}
.

Being
\CAT(-1)
,
W
carries a visual metric
\rho(a,b)\asymp e^{-(a|b)_{o}}
; on each
\partial_{\infty}U_{i}
this restricts to a metric (uniformly) bi-Lipschitz to
[0,1]
.
Define a Lipschitz projection
\pi:W\to U
by isometrically sending each
U_{i}\to U
. Fix a visual metric
\asymp e^{-(a|b)_{o}}
on
U
and a bi-Lipschitz parametrization
h:\partial_{\infty}U\to[0,1]
.
For
a\in\partial_{\infty}W
,
a
is the limit point of a geodesic ray
\gamma_{a}
lying entirely in a unique
U_{v(a)}\subset W
,
v(a)\in[V]^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Define
f:\partial_{\infty}W\to Z^{H,V}\times[0,1]
by
f(a)=(v(a),\partial_{\infty}(\pi\circ\gamma_{a}))
; metrize
Z^{H,V}\times[0,1]
by
d^{\prime}((v,t),(v^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=\max\{\rho_{H,V}(v,v^{\prime}),|t-t^{\prime}|\}
.

We claim
f
is bi-Lipschitz.
Suppose
a,b\in\partial_{\infty}W
.
Let
v(a)=(a_{1},\ldots),v(b)=(b_{1},\ldots)
.
Let
k=\min\{i:a_{i}\neq b_{i}\}
, so
\rho_{H,V}(v(a),v(b))=H^{-k}
.
Let

, so
|h(\partial_{\infty}(\pi\circ\gamma_{a}))-h(\partial_{\infty}(\pi\circ\gamma_{b}))|\asymp e^{-r}
.
If

then the point at which the geodesic rays
\pi\circ\gamma_{a},\pi\circ\gamma_{b}
coarsely diverge lifts to a point in

, thus

\rho(a,b)\asymp e^{-r}\asymp\max\{e^{-r},H^{-k}\}=d^{\prime}(f(a),f(b)).

If
r>k\log H
then the geodesics in
W
to
a,b
coarse diverge at the point
k\log H
along either
\gamma_{a}
or
\gamma_{b}
, and so

\rho(a,b)\asymp e^{-k\log H}\asymp\max\{e^{-r},H^{-k}\}=d^{\prime}(f(a),f(b)).

So in either case, we see that
f
is bi-Lipschitz.

As
Z^{H,V}\times[0,1]
is Ahlfors regular with dimension
1+\frac{\log V}{\log H}
, the attainment of this bound for the conformal dimension of
\partial_{\infty}RT^{H,V}
is standard
[
18
, Example 4.1.9]
.
∎

4.
Poincaré constants of round trees

The goal of this section is to calculate the critical exponent of Poincaré profiles for round tree graphs, and to bound their separation profiles.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
\Gamma
is a round tree graph with horizontal branching in
[2,H]
and vertical branching
V\geq 2
, then:

p_{\Lambda}(\Gamma)\geq 1+\frac{\log V}{\log H}.

In particular, if
\Gamma=RT^{H,V}
is the
(H,V)
-regular round tree graph,

p_{\Lambda}(RT^{H,V})=1+\frac{\log V}{\log H}=\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}RT^{H,V}).

This follows from the following proposition, the main result of this section.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a round tree graph with horizontal branching in
[2,H]
and vertical branching
V\geq 2
. Let
Q=1+\frac{\log V}{\log H}
.
Then for all
1\leq p<Q
, there exists
c>0
so that, setting

\epsilon=\frac{Q-p}{p(Q-p+pQ)}>0,

for any
r>1
there exists a subgraph
Y\subset\Gamma
of size
|Y|\asymp r
and with

\Lambda_{\Gamma}^{p}(|Y|)\geq|Y|h^{p}(Y)\geq c|Y|^{1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}.

Thus
p_{\Lambda}(\Gamma)\geq Q
.

Note that this proposition implies that for all
1\leq p<Q
,

\Lambda_{\Gamma}^{p}(r)\succeq r^{1-\frac{1}{p+\frac{Q-p}{Q}}}=r^{\frac{pQ-p}{pQ-p+Q}}

which, in the case
p=1
, simplifies to
\Lambda_{\Gamma}^{1}(r)\succeq r^{\frac{Q-1}{2Q-1}}
.

In the proof of Proposition
4.2
we require the following standard lemma.

Lemma 4.3

(e.g.
[
14
, Proof of Theorem 10.1]
)
.

Suppose for any vertices
w_{0},w_{1}
in a finite graph
\Gamma
we have a choice of path
\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}
from
w_{0}
to
w_{1}
. For each edge
e
, let
M_{e}
denote the number of pairs
(w_{0},w_{1})\in V(\Gamma)^{2}
with
e\subset\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}
.

Then for
p>1
we have:

h^{p}(\Gamma)\geq\frac{|\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\max_{w_{0},w_{1}}\left(\sum_{e\in\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}}M_{e}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}},

and for
p=1
we have:

h^{1}(\Gamma)\geq\frac{|\Gamma|}{\max_{e\in E(\Gamma)}M_{e}}.

Proof.

As in
[
14
, Proposition 10.3 and Proof of Theorem 10.1]
, for any
f:V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{R}
and
p>1
, letting
f_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{x\in V(\Gamma)}f(x)
,

\displaystyle\|f-f_{\Gamma}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{w_{0},w_{1}\in V(\Gamma)}|f(w_{0})-f(w_{1})|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{0},w_{1}\in V(\Gamma)\\
e\subset\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}\end{subarray}}|\nabla f(e)|^{p}M_{e}^{-1}\Bigg)\max_{w_{0},w_{1}\in V(\Gamma)}\Bigg(\sum_{e\subset\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}}M_{e}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\Bigg)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\|\nabla f\|_{p}^{p}\max_{w_{0},w_{1}\in V(\Gamma)}\Bigg(\sum_{e\subset\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}}M_{e}^{1/(p-1)}\Bigg)^{p-1}.

The same proof simplifies to show

\|f-f_{\Gamma}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\|\nabla f\|_{1}\max_{e\in E(\Gamma)}M_{e};

compare e.g.
[
11
, Proposition 1]
.
∎

.

Fix

, and

.

Let

be constants to be determined.

In

.
We build the subcomplex

(1)

For each maximal word

.

(2)

For each

.

Observe at step 2 that each time the vertical coordinate adds another letter, the horizontal path length increases by a factor between
2
and

Let

V

\lceil\log_{2}(H)-2\rceil
.

By construction,

.

Define a colouring map
\Pi:Y_{k}\times Y_{k}\to[T]
with the following property:
for each fixed

, and each fixed

,

\big|\Pi^{-1}(t)\cap\{x\}\times Y_{k}\big|\preceq(HV)^{k}\asymp H^{Qk}.

Likewise,
for each fixed

, and each fixed

\big|\Pi^{-1}(t)\cap Y_{k}\times\{y\}\big|\preceq(HV)^{k}\asymp H^{Qk}.

Such a map

can be chosen by (roughly) labelling each vertex in

by labels in

be the combination of labels in
[T^{1/2}]\times[T^{1/2}]\approx[T]
.

Now to bound the Poincaré constants in
Y_{k}
, we choose paths as in Lemma
4.3
:

Let
(b_{t})_{t\in[T]}
label the horizontal path
\pi_{V}^{-1}(Y_{0})
.
For
(w_{0},w_{1})\in Y_{k}

, choose

with

an initial subword of
h_{0}^{\prime}
,
and choose
w_{1}^{\prime}=(h_{1}^{\prime},v_{1})\in Y_{k}

an initial subword of
h_{1}^{\prime}
.
Now define

by travelling horizontally from

to
w_{0}^{\prime}
,

,
then travel vertically out to

, then travel horizontally to
w_{1}
.

Upper bounds on

:

For a horizonal edge

(or

(or
w_{1}

(or

(4.4)

M_{e}\preceq TH^{k}TH^{Qk}.

For a vertical edge

M_{e}\preceq\frac{|Y_{k}|^{2}}{T}\cdot V^{-\left\lfloor\frac{|v|-|\pi_{V}|_{Y_{0}}|}{\log_{2}H}\right\rfloor}.

4.3
.

Case
p>1
:

We wish to bound
\sum_{e\in\gamma_{w_{0},w_{1}}}M^{1/(p-1)}
from above independently of

.

By (

(4.6)

\sum M_{e}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\preceq(TH^{k})\left(TH^{k}|Y_{k}|\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}=(TH^{k})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|Y_{k}|^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(4.7)

\sum M_{e}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\preceq\left(|Y_{k}|^{2}/T\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

|Y_{k}|h^{p}(Y_{k})\succeq\frac{|Y_{k}|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}}{\max\{TH^{k}|Y_{k}|^{1/p},(|Y_{k}|^{2}/T)^{1/p}\}}.

Now we fix variables. Choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
and set
T
to satisfy

|Y_{k}|\asymp T^{1+\alpha Q}

\alpha=\frac{p}{Q-p}

\displaystyle|Y_{k}|h^{p}(Y_{k})

\displaystyle\succeq\frac{T^{(1+\alpha Q)(1+\frac{1}{p})}}{\max\{T^{1+\alpha}T^{(1+\alpha Q)/p},T^{(1+2\alpha Q)/p}\}}

\displaystyle=\min\{T^{\alpha(Q-1)},T^{1+\alpha Q-\alpha Q/p}\}.

\min\{T^{\alpha(Q-1)},T^{1+\alpha Q-\alpha Q/p}\}\succeq T^{(1+\alpha Q)(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon)}

\min\{\alpha(Q-1),1+\alpha Q-\alpha Q/p\}\geq(1+\alpha Q)(1-\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon).

Rearranging

\epsilon=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1+\alpha}{1+\alpha Q}.

Recall that
\alpha=\frac{p}{Q-p}
, so the above equation gives

\epsilon=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1+\frac{p}{Q-p}}{1+\frac{pQ}{Q-p}}=\frac{Q-p}{p(Q-p+pQ)}>0.

Similarly, rearranging

\epsilon=\frac{1}{p(1+\alpha Q)}.

Again, setting
\alpha=\frac{p}{Q-p}

\epsilon=\frac{1}{p\left(1+\frac{pQ}{Q-p}\right)}=\frac{Q-p}{p(Q-p+pQ)}>0.

Thus, Proposition
4.2
holds in the case

.

Case
p=1
:

As in the

and set

to satisfy

, so

with

. Now, Lemma

gives

|Y_{k}|h^{1}(Y_{k})\succeq\frac{|Y_{k}|^{2}}{\max\{TH^{k}|Y_{k}|,|Y_{k}|^{2}/T\}}\asymp\min\{T^{\alpha(Q-1)},T\}=T

It suffices to find

or, in other words,

In this section we use the results above to compute or bound the critical exponent

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose

.

\Lambda_{\Cone(Z\times[0,1])}^{p}(r)\gtrsim r^{1+\frac{1}{p}+\epsilon}

\Confdim(Z\times[0,1])=p_{\Lambda}(\Cone(Z\times[0,1]))=1+Q.

In the following proof, recall that the spaces

.

Proof.

That
\Confdim(Z\times[0,1])=1+Q

[

[

.

For any

Any remaining case where

∎

5.2.
Heintze manifolds
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}\mathbb{R}

The bounds above apply to the following groups.

Definition 5.2
.

Given
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}
, the semidirect product where the action of
\mathbb{R}
is given by

\psi(t)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\exp(a_{1}t)x_{1},\ldots,\exp(a_{n}t)x_{n}).

Note that for non-zero
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\lambda\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}
are isomorphic.

When all
a_{i}>0
, the group
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}
is Gromov hyperbolic, indeed, it is a Heintze group admitting a metric of negative sectional curvature.
Assuming
a_{1}=\min a_{i}
, the boundary
\partial_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R})
, minus a point, is quasi-Möbius equivalent to

(\mathbb{R},d^{1/a_{1}})\times(\mathbb{R},d^{1/a_{2}})\times\cdots\times(\mathbb{R},d^{1/a_{n}})

and hence to

(\mathbb{R},d)\times(\mathbb{R},d^{a_{1}/a_{2}})\times(\mathbb{R},d^{a_{1}/a_{3}})\times\cdots\times(\mathbb{R},d^{a_{1}/a_{n}})

where the metric
d
denotes the standard metric on
\mathbb{R}
; see for example
[
8
, Section 2.5]
. Having this local product structure, the conformal dimension is well-known.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
25
, Example 3.4]
)
.

When
0<a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}
is hyperbolic and the conformal dimension of its boundary is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{a_{1}}
.

We now see that the conformal dimension of the boundary equals the critical exponent
p_{\Lambda}
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}
. For every
1\leq p<\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)
there is some
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(p)>0
such that

\Lambda^{p}_{G}(r)\gtrsim r^{1-1/p+\varepsilon}.

In particular,
p_{\Lambda}(G)=\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)
.

Proof.

Follows from Theorem
5.1
, as
\partial_{\infty}G
contains a bi-Lipschitz copy of

[0,1]\times\left(([0,1],d^{a_{2}/a_{1}})\times\cdots\times([0,1],d^{a_{n}/a_{1}})\right).\qed

Corollary 5.5
.

If there is a regular map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes_{\underline{a^{\prime}}}\mathbb{R}
, where
1=a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{n}
and
1=a^{\prime}_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n^{\prime}}a^{\prime}_{j}
.

5.3.
Random groups

We now apply the round tree bounds above to certain random groups.

We consider random groups in the sense of Gromov:
fix an integer
m\geq 2
, a function
n=n(\ell):\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and a property
P
of a group presentation.
Given
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G=\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}|r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\rangle

be a group presentation where each
r_{i}
is chosen independently and uniformly at random from all cyclically reduced words of length
\ell
in
\langle s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\rangle
.
If
P
holds with probability
\to 1
as
\ell\to\infty
, we say
P
holds
asymptotically almost surely (a.a.s.)
.

When
n=\lfloor C\ell^{K}\rfloor
for some constants
C>0
and
K\geq 0
this is the
polynomially many relator model (of degree
K
)
. The degree
0
case, When
n
is constant, is the random
few (or
n
-)relator model
. When
n=\lfloor(2m-1)^{\ell d}\rfloor
for some constant
density

d\in(0,1)
this is
(Gromov’s) density model
.

In all these models, a.a.s. the random group is hyperbolic. In the few and polynomially many relator models with
n\geq 1
, and in the density model with
d\in(0,1/2)
, a.a.s. the random group is non-elementary hyperbolic with Menger sponge boundary. See
[
23
,
21
]
for further details.

Combinatorial round trees have been embedded into random groups in these models,
see

[
21
, §7.2]
,
[
21
, §8]
and
[
10
, §3.2, §5]
.
We thus have:

Corollary 5.6
.

A polynomial degree
K
random group
G
satisfies

2+K-\frac{5\log\log\ell}{\log\ell}\leq p_{\Lambda}(G)\leq\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)\leq 2+K+\frac{2(K+1)\log\log\ell}{\log\ell}

a.a.s. as
\ell\to\infty
.

A density
d\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
random group satisfies

\frac{d(1-2d)^{5}\ell\log(2m-1)}{C|\log(d(1/2-d))|}\leq p_{\Lambda}(G)\leq\Confdim(\partial_{\infty}G)\leq\frac{Cd\ell\log(2m-1)}{(1-2d)|\log d|}

a.a.s. as
\ell\to\infty
.

In particular, for
K^{\prime}>K
, polynomial degree
K^{\prime}
random groups do not coarsely embed in polynomial degree
K
groups,
and at each density
d\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, high
\ell
random groups do not coarsely embed in low
\ell
random groups.

Remark 5.7
.

Note that there is a sharper lower bound on the conformal dimension for random groups in Gromov’s at density
d
close to
\frac{1}{2}
by Oppenheim
[
24
]
, see also discussion in
[
10
, Section 1]
. This uses spectral methods as opposed to round trees so there is no immediate equivalent lower bound on
p_{\Lambda}
.

6.
Poincaré profiles detect hyperbolic groups of conformal dimension
1

In this section we show Theorem
1.11
: that if an infinite hyperbolic group
G
does not split over a finite or virtually cyclic group, either
G
is virtually Fuchsian, or
\sep_{G}(r)\simeq\Lambda_{G}^{1}(r)\gtrsim r^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
.

The idea is that the construction of
[
19
]
, which builds a “Cantor set of quasi-arcs” in the boundary of such hyperbolic groups, has as its convex hull a subset of the Cayley graph which is quasi-isometric to a round tree graph in the sense of Definition
3.2
. The result then follows from Proposition
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Choose a finite symmetric generating set of
G
and let
d
be the word metric on
G
with respect to this generating set.
Fix a visual metric
\rho
on
\partial_{\infty}G
with
\rho(z,z^{\prime})\asymp_{C}e^{-\epsilon(z|z^{\prime})_{1}}
, normalized to have diameter
1
. We recall that, up to bounded additive errors, the geodesic triangle with endpoints
1,z,z^{\prime}
is a tripod where the Gromov product
(z|z)^{\prime}_{1}
is the distance from
1
to the centre of the tripod.

Since
G
doesn’t split over a finite group the boundary
\partial_{\infty}G
is connected.
By Bowditch, either
G
is virtually Fuchsian or has no local cut points, so assume the latter.
As in
[
19
, Proof of Corollary 1.2]
,
\partial_{\infty}G
is then annularly linearly connected, doubling and complete (see op. cit. for definitions), so
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
holds.

We use the following notation for arcs, that is, (sub)spaces of metric spaces homeomorphic to
[0,1]
.
For points
x,y
in an arc
A
,
A[x,y]
denotes the unique subarc with endpoints
x
and
y
; likewise
A[x,y)
denotes
A[x,y]\setminus\{y\}
, etc.
An arc
A
is a
\iota
-local
\lambda
-quasi-arc
if for all
x,y\in A
with
d(x,y)\leq\iota
then
\diam A[x,y]\leq\lambda d(x,y)
.
For arcs
A,B
in a metric space and
\iota>0
,
B

\iota
-follows

A
if there exists a not necessarily continuous map
p:B\to A
such that for any
x,y\in B
,
B[x,y]
is contained in the
\iota
-neighbourhood of
A[p(x),p(y)]
.

By
[
19
, Proof of Theorem 1.4]
, we can construct arcs in
\partial_{\infty}G
as follows.
There exists
\lambda\geq 1
so that
for any
\beta<1/32
small enough (
\beta=
‘
\alpha\delta^{*}/32\lambda
’ in the cited proof, but
\delta^{*}
can be shrunk arbitrarily and the proof goes through verbatim; we only additionally require
\beta<1/12\lambda^{2}
below),
there exist
arcs
J_{\mathbf{a}}
for any finite or infinite sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
or
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, with the following properties.
(We denote the empty sequence by
\emptyset
.)
Each
J_{\mathbf{a}}
has diameter at least
1/2
,
each
J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}
is a
\beta^{n}
-local
\lambda
-quasi-arc which
\beta^{n}
-follows
J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}}
;
furthermore, we may assume the ‘following’ maps map endpoints to endpoints.
Each
J_{a_{1}\cdots}
has Hausdorff distance
\leq 2\lambda\beta^{n}
to each
J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}
by
[
19
, Lemma 4.1]
.
Moreover, for all
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots),\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, both the minimum and the Hausdorff distance between
J_{\mathbf{a}}
and
J_{\mathbf{b}}
are
\asymp\beta^{n}
where
n=\min\{k:a_{k}\neq b_{k}\}
.
Finally, there exists a uniform
\lambda^{\prime}
so that each
J_{\mathbf{a}},\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{*}\cup\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a (not local)
\lambda^{\prime}
-quasi-arc, i.e. for all
x,y\in J_{\mathbf{a}}
,
\diam(J_{\mathbf{a}}[x,y])\leq\lambda^{\prime}d(x,y)
.

Let
W\subset G
be the union of geodesic rays from
1\in G
to points in
J_{\mathbf{a}}
for
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. By hyperbolicity,
W
is a quasi-convex subset of
G
. Theorem
1.11
now reduces to the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

There is an (irregular) round tree graph
A
which is quasi-isometric to
W
.

We will prove this proposition via a series of Lemmas.

For each
n
, and each
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, take a maximal
\beta^{n}/4\lambda
-separated net in
J_{\mathbf{a}}
which includes its endpoints and use the arc order to order it as a sequence
(z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i})_{i=1}^{N_{\mathbf{a}}}
.
For a constant
C_{1}
to be determined shortly, define each corresponding point
x_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}
in
G
by taking a point at distance
\frac{-1}{\epsilon}\log(\beta^{n})+C_{1}
from
1
along a geodesic ray to
z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}
. For convenience set
x_{\emptyset}^{1}=1\in G
.
By hyperbolicity, we can choose and fix
C_{1}=C_{1}(\lambda,C,\epsilon,\delta)
large enough so that all points in

are at least
2\delta

We will build a round tree graph
A
with vertex set
\{p_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}:\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{*},1\leq i\leq N_{\mathbf{a}}\}
and prove that the map
\Pi:p_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}\mapsto x_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}

. We first prove that
\Pi
is coarsely onto.

Lemma 6.2
.

The sets
W
and
\{x_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}:\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{*},1\leq i\leq N_{\mathbf{a}}\}
are at bounded Hausdorff distance.

Proof.

Any
w\in W
lies on a geodesic ray from
1
to some

for some
\mathbf{b}=(b_{i})\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}

Choose
n

d(w,e)\geq\frac{-1}{\epsilon}\log(\beta^{n})+C_{1}
.
As
J_{\mathbf{b}}
is
\beta^{n}
close to

, there exists some

so that
d(x_{b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}^{i},w)\leq C_{2}=C_{2}(C,\epsilon,\delta,C_{1})
.

Conversely, given
x_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}
for
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{n},1\leq i\leq N_{\mathbf{a}}
, let
\mathbf{b}=(\mathbf{a},a_{n+1},\ldots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}

and

have Hausdorff distance

is uniformly bounded where

is the point on the geodesic ray from

∎

Now, each

projects by the

-following map to a point in some

\pi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}:\{1,\ldots,N_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}\}\to\{1,\ldots,N_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}}\}.

We require
\pi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}
to map endpoints to endpoints, i.e.
\pi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}(1)=1

\pi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}(N_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}})=N_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n-1}}
.

We can now define the edges of
A
. The set of horizontal edges of
A
is

\{p_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}p_{\mathbf{a}}^{i+1}:\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{*},1\leq i<N_{\mathbf{a}}\}.

For each non-empty
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{*}
, and each

and

The next lemma ensures that

is Lipschitz on horizontal edges of

.

Lemma 6.3
.

For all non-empty
\mathbf{a}\in\{0,1\}^{*}
and

, we have
\diam J_{\mathbf{a}}[z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i},z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i+1}]\in[\beta^{n}/4\lambda,\beta^{n}/2]

\rho(z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i},z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i+1})\in[\beta^{n}/4\lambda,\beta^{n}/2]

Proof.

The lower bound is immediate since
\{z_{\mathbf{a}}^{i}\}
is a
\beta^{n}/4\lambda
-separated net.
If the upper bound fails, there exists
\mathbf{a}
,

∎

In order to control the vertical edges, we need more information about the functions

Lemma 6.4
.

Assuming
\beta<1/10\lambda^{2}
, for all non-empty

\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
.

Proof.

If not, there exists
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=(\mathbf{a}^{\prime},a_{n})
,
1\leq i<N_{\mathbf{a}}
and

-following map projects

Lemma 6.5

To show

7.
Groups with logarithmic separation

We will prove Theorem
1.17
using the separation profile of Benjamini-Schramm-Timár
[
1
]
. We recall the definition and key properties for convenience.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an
r
-vertex graph and let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. The
\varepsilon
-cut size of
\Gamma

\cut^{\varepsilon}(\Gamma)
is the minimum cardinality of a subset

so that every connected component of
\Gamma-S
has at most
\varepsilon r
vertices.

The
\varepsilon

X
is the function

\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r):=\max\left\{\cut^{\varepsilon}(\Gamma)\ \left|\ \Gamma\leq X,\ |\Gamma|\leq r\right.\right\}.

Proposition 7.2

(
[
1
,
14
]
)
.

Let
X
be a bounded degree graph. Then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\Lambda^{1}_{X}(r)\simeq\sep^{\varepsilon}_{X}(r).

Let us recall the statement of Theorem
1.17
. In this section we will denote
\sep^{\frac{2}{3}}
by
\sep
and
\cut^{\frac{2}{3}}
by
\cut
(cf. Definition
7.1
).

Theorem
1.17
.

Let
G
be a one-ended hyperbolic group which has a JSJ decomposition over
2
-ended subgroups consisting only of quadratically hanging and cylindrical vertex groups.
Then
\sep_{G}(r)\simeq\log(r)
.

By Shepherd–Woodhouse
[
26
, Theorem 1.2; see Remark 3.6, Theorem 3.9, Lemma 3.12]
, we can find a finite index subgroup which has a decomposition as a graph of groups with all vertex groups fundamental groups of either (1) orientable hyperbolic surfaces or (2) of circles, and all edge groups infinite cyclic which identify a vertex group of type (2) with a subgroup of a vertex group of type (1) corresponding to a boundary circle.
This reduces Theorem
1.17
to the following more geometric theorem, which we prove in this section.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finite collection of compact hyperbolic surfaces with totally geodesic boundary.
Let
X
be a connected space obtained by gluing together the surfaces in
\mathcal{C}
, where each gluing identifies some set of
\geq 2
isometric boundary curves of surfaces in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
G=\pi_{1}(X)
satisfies
\sep_{G}(r)\lesssim\log(r)
.
In particular, if
G
is one-ended,
\sep_{G}(r)\simeq\log(r)
.

Note that such
X
is
\CAT(-1)
.

The idea behind the proof is as follows: we work in a standard graph approximation
N=(V(N),E(N))
to the universal cover
U:=\widetilde{X}
, where
V(N)
is a maximal
1
-separated subset of
U
and

E(N)=\left\{nn^{\prime}\ \left|\ d_{U}(n,n^{\prime})\leq 3\right.\right\}.

It is a standard result that
N
(with its shortest path metric) is quasi-isometric to
U
(which is itself quasi-isometric to
G
) so it suffices to prove that
\sep_{N}(r)\simeq\log(r)
.

Fix a subset
Z\subset V(N)
of cardinality
r
. The strategy is as follows. We may disconnect the space
U
into convex connected components by removing a tree
C
rooted at a “centrepoint”
z
of

built from a uniformly bounded number of geodesics. As each connected component has non-abelian free fundamental group there are enough ways of doing this that most of them will intersect the
3
-neighbourhood of
Z
only inside the ball of radius
\simeq\log(r)
centred at
z
. Choosing such a
C
, we obtain a cutset
Z\cap N_{3}(C)
for
Z
of size
\lesssim\log(r)
. A good choice of centrepoint ensures that this cutset is a
\frac{2}{3}
-cutset. We will break the proof of Theorem
7.3
into several steps following this rough outline.

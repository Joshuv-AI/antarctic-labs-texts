1.
introduction

Throughout this paper
R
is a commutative Noetherian ring. If
M
is an
R
-module and if
I
is an ideal in
R
, we denote by
H^{i}_{I}(M)
the
i^{th}
local cohomology module of
M
with respect to
I
.

The following conjecture is due to Lyubeznik
[
10
]
;

1.1
.

Conjecture:
If
R
is a regular ring, then each local cohomology module
H^{i}_{I}(R)
has
finitely many associated prime ideals.

There are many cases where this conjecture is true: by work of Huneke and Sharp
[
5
]
,
for regular rings
R
of prime characteristic; by work of Lyubeznik, for regular local and
affine rings of characteristic zero
[
8
]
, for unramified regular local rings of mixed
characteristic
[
11
]
. It is also true for smooth
\mathbb{Z}
-algebras by work of Bhatt et.al
[
1
]
.

In
[
10
]
Lyubeznik especially asked whether
1.1
is valid for a regular ring
R
containing a field of characteristic zero. It is easy to give examples where existing techniques, to show finiteness of associate primes of local cohomology modules, fail.

For the rest of the paper assume that
R
is regular and contains a field of characteristic zero.
For simplicity we assume that
\dim R=d
is finite.
By Grothendieck vanishing theorem
H^{i}_{I}(R)=0
for all
i>d
, see
[
2
, 6.1.2]
. In general for a Noetherian ring
T
of dimension
d

the set
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(H^{d}_{I}(T))
is finite, see
[
3
, 3.11]
(also see
[
12
, 2.3]
). If
T
is a regular ring of dimension
d
and
I
is an ideal in
T
then using the Hartshorne-Lichtenbaum theorem, cf.
[
6
, 14.1]
it is easy to prove that

\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(H^{d}_{I}(T))=\left\{P\left|P\in\operatorname{Min}T/I\ \text{and}\ 0ptP=d\right.\right\}.

In
[
14
]
, we proved that
H^{d-1}_{I}(R)
is a finite set if
R
is an excellent ring. We also proved that to prove Lyubeznik’s conjecture for excellent regular rings containing a field of characteristic zero it is sufficient to prove

\operatorname{Ass}H^{g+1}_{I}(R)
is a finite for all ideals of height
g
.
Practically nothing is known for rings which are not excellent. We also note that in examples of singular rings
S
with ideals
I
such that
\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(S)
is an infinite set the ring
S/I
is regular, see
[
15
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
16
]
. If
T
is a regular ring and
I
is an ideal such that
T/I
is a regular ring then it is not difficult to show that
\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{I}(T)
is a finite set for all
i
. In this paper we prove

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a regular ring of dimension
d
, containing a field of characteristic zero. Let
P
be a prime ideal in
R
such that
(R/P)_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular for any non-maximal prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
. Then
H^{i}_{P}(R)
has finitely many associate primes for all
i
.

It is not difficult to prove the above result when
R
is excellent. The case when
R
is not excellent requires new ideas.

If
E
is an
R
-module let
\operatorname{Ass}^{i}E=\{Q\in\operatorname{Ass}E\mid 0ptQ=i\}
. We show

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R
be a regular ring of dimension
d
, containing a field of characteristic zero. Let
P
be a prime ideal in
R
such that
R/P
satisfies Serre’s condition
R_{i}
. Then

\operatorname{Ass}^{g+i+1}H^{g+1}_{P}(R)
is a finite set.

Here is an overview of the contents of this paper. In section two we consider the flat extension
R\rightarrow R[[X]]_{X}
. This helps when
R
contains a countable field
K
. Note
T=R[[X]]_{X}
contains the field
K[[X]]_{X}
which is uncountable. We show that to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
it suffices to assume that
R
contains an uncountable field. In section three we describe a construction which is important to us. Finally in section four we prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.

2.
Passage to a ring containing an uncountable field

In our arguments we need to assume that
R
contains an uncountable field. When this is not the case we consider the flat extension
R\rightarrow R[[X]]_{X}
.
Set
S=R[[X]]
and
T=S_{X}=R[[X]]_{X}
, i.e., the ring obtained by inverting
X
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i) If
R
contains a countable field
K
then note
K[[X]]
is a subring of
S
and so
K[[X]]_{X}
is a subring of
T
. The field
K[[X]]_{X}
is uncountable. Thus
T
contains an uncountable field.

(ii) If
R
is regular (Cohen-Macaulay) then so is
S
, see
[
4
, 2.2.13]
and
[
4
, 2.1.9]
. Therefore
T
is also regular (Cohen-Macaulay).

The following properties were proved in our paper
[
14
, section 3]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(with hypotheses as above)

(1)

T
is a faithfully flat
R
-algebra.

(2)

If
P
is a prime ideal in
R
then
PT
is a prime ideal in
T
and
PT\cap R=P
. Conversely if
P
is a prime ideal in
T
then there is a prime ideal
Q
of
R
with
P=QT
.
Furthermore
0ptP=0ptPT
.

(3)

\dim R=\dim T
.

(4)

Let
M
be a
R
-module. The mapping defined by

\displaystyle\psi\colon\operatorname{Ass}_{R}(M)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Ass}_{T}(M\otimes_{R}T)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}T

is a bijection.

(5)

\psi
maps
\operatorname{Ass}^{i}_{R}(M)
bijectively to
\operatorname{Ass}^{i}_{T}(M\otimes_{R}T)
.

We will need the following

Proposition 2.3
.

(with hypotheses as above). Also assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay.
Let
P
be a prime in
R
such that
R/P
satisfies
R_{i}
. Then
T/PT
satisfies
R_{i}
.

Proof.

We note that
T
is also Cohen-Macaulay. So both
R,T
are universally catenary.
Let
0ptP=g
.
Let
Q
be a prime ideal in
T
containing
PT
with
0ptQ=g+i
. Let
Q=\mathfrak{q}T
. Then
0pt\mathfrak{q}=g+i
and
\mathfrak{q}
contains
P
.
So
(R/P)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular. The extension
(R/P)_{\mathfrak{q}}\rightarrow(T/PT)_{Q}
is flat with field as a fiber. By
[
13
, 23.7]
it follows that
(T/PT)_{Q}
is regular.
∎

3.
Construction

In this section we describe a construction that we use to prove our results.

3.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring containing an uncountable field
K
. Assume

\operatorname{m-Spec}(A)=\{\mathfrak{m}_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}
and that
0pt\mathfrak{m}_{n}=d
for all
n
.

Let

\mathcal{C}=\{\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{m}\mid\mathcal{F}\ \text{is an infinite subset of }\ \operatorname{m-Spec}(A)\}.

As
R
is Noetherian
\mathcal{C}
has maximal elements. Let
Q
be a maximal element in
\mathcal{C}
.
Then
Q=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{m}
for some infinite subset
\mathcal{F}
of
\operatorname{m-Spec}(A)
. We note that as
Q
is a maximal element in
\mathcal{C}
we have

Q=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{m}
for any infinite subset
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathcal{F}
.

Consider the multiplicatively closed set

S=R\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}}\mathfrak{m}.

Set
T=S^{-1}R
.

The following result from
[
14
, 4.4]
gives information about primes in
T
.

Proposition 3.2
.

(with hypotheses as in
3.1
).
We have

(1)

If
\mathfrak{p}T
is a prime in
T
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime in
R
, then
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
for some
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{m}_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
then

\mathfrak{m}_{i}T\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}_{j}T
if
\mathfrak{m}_{i}\neq\mathfrak{m}_{j}
..

(3)

\mathfrak{m}T
for
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}
are distinct maximal ideals of
T
.

(4)

\operatorname{m-Spec}(T)=\{\mathfrak{m}T\}_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}}
.

We will also need the following intersection result from
[
14
, 4.5]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

(with hypotheses as in
3.1
).
Let
\Lambda
be any subset of
\mathcal{F}
( possibly infinite). Set

U=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{m}\quad\text{and}\ V=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{m}T.

Then
UT=V
.

3.4
.

The following result describes the properties of
T
that we need.

Theorem 3.5
.

(with hypotheses as in
3.1
). We have

(1)

QT=\operatorname{rad}T
.

(2)

Let
\Lambda
be any infinite subset of
\mathcal{F}
. Then

QT=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{m}T.

(3)

QT
is a prime ideal of
T
.

(4)

0pt\mathfrak{m}T=0pt\mathfrak{m}=d
for all
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{F}
.

(5)

If
f\notin QT
then
f
belongs to utmost finitely many maximal ideals of
T
.

Proof.

(1) This follows from
3.2
and
3.3
.

(2)We note that by maximality of
Q
in
\mathcal{C}
we have
Q=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Lambda}\mathfrak{m}.

The result now follows from
3.3
.

(3) Note
QT
is a radical ideal. So
QT=P_{1}\cap P_{2}\cap\cdots\cap P_{s}
for some primes
P_{i}
with
s\geq 1
and that
P_{i}\nsubseteq P_{j}
if
i\neq j
. Suppose if possible
s\geq 2
. Let
\mathfrak{m}T
be a maximal ideal of
T
. Then note that
\mathfrak{m}T\supseteq P_{i}
for some
i
. Let

\Gamma_{i}=\{\mathfrak{m}T\mid\mathfrak{m}T\supseteq P_{i}\}.

Then

\operatorname{m-Spec}(T)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\Gamma_{i}.

It follows that
\Gamma_{i}
is infinite for some
i
. Without any loss of generality we assume
i=1
.
Then note that
P_{1}\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathfrak{m}T\in\Lambda_{1}}\mathfrak{m}T=QT
.
It follows that
P_{2}\supseteq P_{1}
, contradicting the choice of
P_{2}
. Thus
QT
is a prime ideal in
T
.

(4)This is trivial.

(5) Suppose if possible there exists an infinite subset
\mathcal{G}
of
\operatorname{m-Spec}(T)
such that
f\in\mathfrak{m}T
for every
\mathfrak{m}T\in\mathcal{G}
. It follows that

f\in\cap_{\mathfrak{m}T\in\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{m}T
. But by construction the latter module is
QT
. This is a contradiction.
∎

3.6
.

If
f\notin QT
we describe a process of making
f
a unit. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the finite set consisting of ALL the maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
T
such that
f\in\mathfrak{m}
. Set

W=T\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{m-Spec}(T)\setminus\mathcal{G}}\mathfrak{m}\quad\text{and}\quad L=W^{-1}T.

We note that
f
is a unit in
L
. Also if
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{m-Spec}(T)\setminus\mathcal{G}
then
\mathfrak{m}L
is a maximal ideal in
L
. Furthermore
QL=\operatorname{rad}L
.

4.
Proof of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3

In this section we give proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
.
We first give

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By our results in section two we may assume that
R
contains an uncountable field of characteristic zero. Suppose
\operatorname{Ass}^{g+i+1}H^{g+1}_{P}(R)
is an infinite set. Let

\operatorname{Ass}^{g+i+1}H^{g+1}_{P}(R)=\{P_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}.

Consider
W=R\setminus\bigcup_{n\geq 1}P_{n}
and set
S=W^{-1}R
. Next we do the construction as described in section three.
Let
T
be the ring obtained with
Q=\operatorname{rad}T
. We note that
\dim T=g+i+1
. We also have
0ptQ\leq g+i
. As
R/P
satisfies
R_{i}
it follows that
R_{Q}/PR_{Q}
is a regular ring. It follows that
P_{Q}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{g})
. So there exists
f\notin Q
such that
P_{f}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{g})
. By process of making
f
a unit, see
3.6
, we get a further localization
L
of
T
such that
PL
is generated by

g
elements. So
H^{g+1}_{PL}(L)=0
and all maximal ideals of
L
are associate primes of
H^{g+1}_{PL}(L)
. This is a contradiction. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}^{g+i+1}H^{g+1}_{P}(R)
is a finite set.
∎

Next we give

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By our results in section two we may assume that
R
contains an uncountable field of characteristic zero. Suppose
\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{P}(R)
is infinite for some
i
. Set
M=H^{i}_{P}(R)
. We may assume that there exists
r
such that
M
contains infinitely many associate primes
P
of height
r
and there exists only finitely many associate prime of
M
with height
<r
, Let
\{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be infinitely many associate primes of
M
of height
r
. We note that necessarily
r>g=0ptP
. Consider
W=R\setminus\bigcup_{n\geq 1}P_{n}
and set
S=W^{-1}R
. Next we do the construction as described in section three.
Let
T
be the ring obtained with
Q=\operatorname{rad}T
. We note that
\dim T=r
and
0ptQ<r
. By our assumption
(R/P)_{Q}
is a regular local ring. It follows that
P_{Q}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{g})
. So there exists
f\notin Q
such that
P_{f}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{g})
. By process of making
f
a unit, see
3.6
, we get a further localization
L
of
T
such that
PL
is generated by

g
elements. So
H^{r}_{PL}(L)=0
and all maximal ideals of
L
are associate primes of
H^{r}_{PL}(L)
. This is a contradiction. Thus
\operatorname{Ass}H^{i}_{P}(R)
is a finite set.
∎

Author's reply to the Letters regarding “Cysteine metabolism dysregulation promotes ferroptosis in osteoarthritis: Insights from multi-species bioinformatics and experimental validation”
Authors: Luxi Zheng, Zhengmeng Yang, Sihui Chen, Gongli Cai, Nan Hou, Wenxuan Lin, Carina Hey Pui Cheung, Huisheng Zhou, Junjie Chen, Yican Wang, Huifeng Chen, Yaofeng Wang, Micky D Tortorella, Jinyu Zhu, Lu Feng, Gang Li
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We thank Dr. Ni, Dr. Liu, and colleagues for their thoughtful reading of our article [1] and for their constructive commentary regarding sex as a biological variable (SABV) in osteoarthritis (OA) and ferroptosis research. They raise an important and timely point concerning the demographic composition of our human cartilage multi-omics validation cohort (consisting of three elderly postmenopausal female patients) and correctly highlight the potential influence of estrogenic status and sexual dimorphism on cysteine metabolism and ferroptotic signaling. We welcome this opportunity to address their comments, clarify the scope of our findings, and present additional re-analyses of public datasets to address the generalizability of the cysteine metabolism-ferroptosis axis across sexes.

We fully acknowledge the methodological limitation pointed out by Dr. Ni and Dr. Liu that our primary human cartilage proteomic and metabolomic validation was indeed performed on a highly specific clinical subset (three postmenopausal females aged 77-80 years, Kellgren–Lawrence grades III–IV). As the commentators accurately note, core ferroptosis regulators, including GPX4 and SLC7A11, are subject to hormonal modulation, particularly by 17β-estradiol [2]. Therefore, from a strict clinical validation view, the multi-omics profile derived from this small human cohort should be interpreted as direct evidence within this specific subpopulation.

However, we wish to emphasize that the overall conclusions of our study do not rely solely on these three human tissue samples. The mechanistic framework of the "cysteine metabolism-ferroptosis axis" in OA was established through a multi-tiered, cross-species investigation. The cross-species transcriptomic integration across human, murine, and cellular models consistently identified dysregulated cysteine metabolism and down-regulated ferroptosis-defense machinery as central features of OA chondrocytes. Also, our primary IL-1β induced OA chondrocyte model demonstrated that pharmacological or molecular targeting of cysteine catabolism directly modulated intracellular GSH levels, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, lipid peroxidation, and ferroptotic cell death in vitro. Furthermore, surgical DMM-induced OA mouse models validated the therapeutic efficacy of L-cysteine supplementation in mitigating cartilage degeneration and ferroptosis markers in vivo.

To directly address the commentators' concern regarding whether the cysteine metabolism-ferroptosis axis operates independently of sex, we conducted a post-hoc sex-stratified re-analysis of publicly available human OA cartilage transcriptomic datasets (GSE114007, [3]) containing both male and female patients with annotated clinical metadata. This dataset comprised 18 normal and 20 OA cartilage samples. After stratification, 13 normal and 8 OA samples were from male patients, and 5 normal and 12 OA samples were from female patients. Raw counts were analyzed with DESeq2 using an OA versus normal comparison (p < 0.05, |fold change|>1.2). Consistent downregulation in OA was observed in both sexes for SLC3A2 (male log2FC = −1.57, p = 3.8 × 10−7; female log2FC = −1.90, p = 1.2 × 10−6), GPX4 (male log2FC = −0.82, p = 1.6 × 10−3; female log2FC = −0.76, p = 4.9 × 10−2), and CTH (male log2FC = −2.07, p = 5.1 × 10−9; female log2FC = −2.47, p = 2.1 × 10−12) (Fig. 1). SLC7A11 and GCLC did not reach significance in either sex, indicating that the conserved response centers on the SLC3A2-GPX4 axis rather than uniform suppression of every cysteine-handling gene. Across the KEGG ferroptosis pathway gene set, six genes were significantly changed in both sexes, and all six changed in the same direction. No ferroptosis-related gene showed opposite significant directions between sexes (Spearman r = 0.81 for male versus female log2FC). Sex-specific differences were still observed in individual genes, including ACSL4 down-regulation in males only and NCOA4/STEAP3/CYBB up-regulation in females only, consistent with the estrogen-regulated baseline susceptibility cited by Dr. Ni and Dr. Liu. Overall, the directionality of cysteine metabolism dysregulation and ferroptosis activation remained highly consistent between male and female OA patients. These data are summarized in Table 1, Table 2, Table 3. These additional bioinformatic findings suggest that while sexual dimorphism and postmenopausal estrogen deficiency may exacerbate or modulate the severity of ferroptosis in cartilage, cysteine metabolism dysregulation represents a fundamental, conserved pathophysiological hallmark of OA that transcends single-sex boundaries.

We sincerely appreciate the constructive suggestions provided by the commentators. Moving forward, we fully agree that incorporating sex-matched male cartilage specimens and conducting sex-stratified translational cohorts are mandatory steps for validating metabolism-targeted therapies in OA. In our ongoing and future research projects, we are prospectively collecting gender-balanced, age-matched human articular cartilage specimens (spanning both male and female patients across mild to severe KL grades) to perform comprehensive sex-stratified transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic profiling.

In conclusion, we thank Dr. Ni and Dr. Liu once again for their valuable insights. Their commentary highlights sex as a crucial biological dimension, which enriches the interpretation of our work. While our original human multi-omics validation cohort was demographically restricted to elderly female patients, our broader cross-species experimental evidence and newly performed sex-stratified transcriptomic re-analyses support the broader relevance of the cysteine metabolism–ferroptosis axis in OA. We believe that incorporating SABV into metabolic OA research will ultimately enhance translational rigor and paved the way toward personalized, sex-informed therapeutic strategies.

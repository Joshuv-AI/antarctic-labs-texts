TGM2-TUFM promotes acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in EGFR-mutated NSCLC through p62-mediated lipophagy
Authors: Jie Shen, Lanying Gou, Yujie Sun, Peng Luo, Shuo Hong, Chunyan Zhang, Yueqin Sun, Weitao Shen, Jianbo Fang, Yueming Zhang, Xuechun Li, Haoxuan Ying, Qiong Lyu, Jian Zhang, Ting Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcquired resistance to epidermal growth factor receptor-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) remains the leading cause of treatment failure in advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients harboring sensitizing EGFR mutations. This study aims to investigate the underlying resistance mechanisms in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.MethodsAnalyze the differential genes in the transcriptome sequencing data of EGFR-TKi-sensitive and -resistant cell lines, and use the GDC database to analyze the prognosis. qRT‒PCR, western blotting, and immunohistochemistry were used to detect the expression of TGM2 in EGFR-TKI-sensitive and -resistant NSCLC cells and tissue samples. The effects and molecular mechanisms of TGM2 on EGFR-TKI resistance were verified by CCK-8, flow cytometry, western blotting, mouse xenograft models, gene enrichment analysis, and mass spectrometry.ResultsOur study found that TGM2 was highly expressed in EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC cells and tissue samples. Its increased expression was associated with shorter survival time in NSCLC patients. Transglutaminase 2 (TGM2) overexpression renders EGFR-sensitive mutated NSCLC cells resistant to EGFR-TKIs in culture and in murine xenograft models. Mechanistically, we determined that TGM2 promotes lipophagy and fatty acid oxidation (FAO) by inhibiting the ubiquitin-mediated degradation of elongation factor Tu, mitochondrial (TUFM), thereby contributing to drug resistance in NSCLC. Furthermore, we found that TGM2 promotes the formation of p62 (sequestosome 1) bodies through TUFM, and these p62 bodies bind to lipid droplets (LDs)-associated protein perilipin 2 (PLIN2) to enhance lipophagy, revealing a novel pathway for TGM2-facilitated LD breakdown. Notably, treatment with the autophagy inhibitor chloroquine (CQ) and the FAO inhibitor etomoxir reversed TGM2-mediated EGFR-TKI resistance.ConclusionsOur study revealed that TGM2 is a potential therapeutic target or biomarker for predicting acquired EGFR-TKI resistance in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12931-026-03665-8.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide; it accounts for approximately 1.8 million deaths annually [1]. NSCLC accounts for 85% of all lung cancers, and adenocarcinoma is the most common type of NSCLC [2]. Due to the challenges associated with early diagnosis, the majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, resulting in a poor prognosis [3]. Approximately 50% of lung adenocarcinoma patients in China have EGFR mutations [4]. EGFR-TKIs are the first choice for treating advanced NSCLC with sensitizing EGFR mutations and can significantly prolong patients’ survival time [5]. However, the development of acquired drug resistance limits their efficacy [6]. The T790M mutation is the most common mechanism of acquired resistance to first- and second-generation EGFR-TKIs (accounting for approximately 60%), and other mechanisms include activation of bypass signaling and histological/phenotypic transformation [7, 8]. There are still some mechanisms of resistance that remain unclear. Consequently, further exploration of the molecular mechanisms underlying EGFR-TKI resistance is essential for the development of novel therapeutic strategies and the identification of biomarkers capable of predicting treatment response, and ultimately aiming to reverse resistance.

Reprogramming of lipid metabolism is a typical feature of malignant tumours [9]. Cancer cells use energy and biofilm components from lipid metabolism to facilitate malignant cell proliferation, survival, invasion, and metastasis [10]. LDs are functional organelles responsible for lipid storage and are closely related to lipid metabolism [11, 12]. LDs can be metabolized in various ways: neutral lipase and lysosomal acid lipase in the cytoplasm can decompose triglycerides in LDs. In addition, as an autophagy substrate, LDs can be wrapped by autophagosomes and transported to lysosomes for decomposition, a process called “lipophagy” [13]. Numerous studies have shown that this autophagy-mediated pathway of lipid catabolism is of great significance for intracellular lipid metabolism [14, 15]. In terms of cancer research, lipophagy has been reported to play a dual role in both promoting and inhibiting cancer. On the one hand, fatty acids produced by lipophagy participate in beta-oxidation and release a large amount of energy, which can be used by cancer cells for continuous growth and proliferation [16]. On the other hand, lipophagy has an anti-tumour effect and can enhance the sensitivity of ovarian and cervical cancer to chemotherapy [17, 18].

Human TGM2 is a multifunctional enzyme widely distributed inside and outside cells [19]. It is composed of four distinct protein domains that confer its multifunctional enzymatic properties, including Ca2+-dependent transamidase activity and Ca2+-independent GTPase, protein disulfide isomerase, kinase, proteolytic, and scaffold activities [19]. These enzymatic activities enable post-translational modification through different molecular mechanisms. Due to its multiple localizations both inside and outside the cell, TGM2 participates in the regulation of various physiological processes, such as cell growth, differentiation, apoptosis, and adhesion [19]. In addition, TGM2 also plays a key role in various cancer-related processes and pathways. It has been reported to have dual effect of tumour suppression and promotion. On the one hand, TGM2 expression is reduced in sarcoma and prostate cancer [20]. On the other hand, significantly high expression of TGM2 has been found in various cancers, such as lung and breast cancer. Increased TGM2 expression is also commonly associated with cancer stemness, drug resistance, invasion, and metastasis [21]. However, the role and mechanism of TGM2 in acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs remain unclear and require further study.

This study revealed that high TGM2 expression was associated with shorter survival in NSCLC patients. TGM2 promotes lipophagy and FAO by inhibiting TUFM ubiquitination degradation, thereby enhancing acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC. Treatment of NSCLC cells with the autophagy inhibitor chloroquine (CQ) and FAO inhibitor etomoxir attenuated TGM2-mediated acquisition of EGFR-TKI resistance and lipophagy activation. In addition, we found that TGM2 promotes the formation of p62 bodies through TUFM, and these p62 bodies bind to the LD-associated protein PLIN2 to mediate lipophagy. In conclusion, this study reveals that TGM2 is likely a useful marker for EGFR-TKIs response and therapeutic target in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.

Methods

Cells and reagents

Human NSCLC cell lines PC9 and HCC827 cells were purchased from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures (Shanghai, China). H1975 were purchased from Procell Life Science&Technology (Wuhan, China). The PC9 and HCC827 cells were exposed to increasing concentrations of erlotinib for 6 months, and erlotinib-resistant PC9ER and HCC827ER cell models were successfully established. We confirm that all human cell lines have been authenticated using STR (or SNP) profiling and all experiments were performed with mycoplasma-free cells. Erlotinib was added to the culture medium at a concentration of 1µmol/L to maintain the resistant phenotype of PC9ER and HCC827ER cells. Cells were grown in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco, New York, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, New York, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution (Gibco, New York, USA) at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Erlotinib, osimertinib, cystamine, chloroquine, etomoxir, MG132 and CHX were purchased from MedChemExpress (MCE, Shanghai, China). BODIPY 493/503 was purchased from Glpbio (Montclair, USA). TGM2 (Cat# 15100-1-AP), TUFM (Cat# 67802-1-Ig), LC3B (Cat# 14600-1-AP), p62 (Cat# 66184-1-Ig, Cat# 18420-1-AP), PARP (Cat# 13371-1-AP), BAX (Cat# 50599-2-Ig), BCL-2 (Cat# 12789-1-AP), PLIN2 (Cat# 15294-1-AP), PLIN3 (Cat# 66523-1-Ig), CPT1A (Cat# 15184-1-AP), CPT1B (Cat# 22170-1-AP), LAMP1 (Cat# 67300-1-Ig), Ki-67 (Cat# 27309-1-AP), THBD (Cat# 14318-1-AP), E-cadherin (Cat# 20874-1-AP), N-cadherin (Cat# 22018-1-AP), vimentin (Cat# 10366-1-AP), and Ubiquitin (Cat# 10201-2-AP) antibodies were purchased from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). GAPDH (Cat# AP0063) antibody was purchased from Bioworld (Texas, USA).

Patients and tissue samples

This study included tissue samples from 17 patients diagnosed with NSCLC harboring EGFR mutations treated with EGFR-TKIs at Zhujiang Hospital, Southern Medical University. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhujiang Hospital of Southern Medical University, and informed consent from patients was obtained. Tumour xenograft model

4-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were purchased from the Laboratory Animal Center of Southern Medical University (Guangzhou, China). 5 × 106 cells stably transfected with TGM2/shTGM2 or corresponding control NC lentiviral particles were injected subcutaneously into mice to establish the NSCLC xenograft model. When the tumour volume reached 100-200mm3, the mice were randomly divided into designated groups and treated with erlotinib (50 mg/kg) or PBS by oral gavage daily. The formula for calculating the tumour volume is as follows: Volume = length × width 2 × 0.5. The tumour volume was measured every 3 days, and the mice were sacrificed after continuous administration for 3w. The tumours were dissected and weighed and then fixed and embedded in paraffin.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The tissues were fixed, embedded, and cut into 4 μm sections to analyze TGM2 and Ki-67 protein expression. Slides were incubated with these antibody overnight at 4 °C and incubated with secondary antibody (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) for 2 h. Results were evaluated using the EnVision Peroxidase System (Dako, Copenhagen, Denmark). The average optical density (AOD) was then analyzed using Image J software.

Western blot analysis

Cells were washed with cold PBS three times, and RIPA lysate (KeyGEN, Nanjing, China) was added to extract total proteins. Protein concentrations were measured using the BCA Protein Quantification Kit (KeyGEN, Nanjing, China). An equal amount of protein was separated by 10% SDS-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and then transferred to the PVDF membrane (Millipore, Billerica, USA). The membranes were blocked in TBST (0.1% Tween 20) containing 5% skim milk powder for 2 h and then incubated with the corresponding primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After being washed with TBST three times, the membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated anti-rabbit or anti-mouse secondary antibodies (CWBIO, Shanghai, China) for 1 h. Finally, chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents (KeyGEN, Nanjing, China) were used to detect the signal.

Immunofluorescence microscopy imaging

Cells were seeded on cell slides. After cell adhesion, they were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 (Solarbio, Beijing, China). Cells were blocked with goat serum for at least 1 h and then incubated with specific antibodies overnight at 4 °C. After three washes of PBST, the cells were incubated with a specific secondary antibody (Abcam, Cambridge, UK) for 1 h at 37 ° C in the dark. Lipid droplets were stained with 2µM BODIPY 493/503 (Glpbio, Montclair, USA). Finally, nuclei were stained with DAPI (Solarbio, Beijing, China), and images were obtained through a high-content Ti2-E inverted fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

CCK-8 cell toxicity experiment

Cell Count Kit-8 reagent (CCK-8, APExBIO, Houston, USA) was used to detect cell viability and drug sensitivity.NSCLC cells were seeded at 5000 cells per well in 96-well plates and treated with various concentrations of erlotinib for 48 h. Then, 10µL CCK-8 reagent was added to each well (96-well plate), and the absorbance was measured at 450 nm. The IC50 values of erlotinib were calculated using SPSS based on the OD values.

Flow cytometry analysis

Cells were treated with erlotinib for 48 h, and then the cells were harvested. According to the manufacturer’s protocol, Apoptosis was assessed using the Annexin V-FITC/PI kit (BestBio, Shanghai, China). The cells were stained with 0.2 μm/L BODIPY 493/503 (Glpbio, Montclair, USA), and the fluorescence intensity was analyzed by flow cytometry.

mRFP-GFP-LC3 adenovirus analysis

mRFP-GFP-LC3 adenovirus was used to label and track the autophagy marker LC3. The GFP fluorescent protein is sensitive to acidity, so the attenuation of GFP can indicate the fusion of lysosomes and autophagosomes to form autolysosomes. After the fusion of lysosomes with autophagosomes, GFP fluorescence is quenched, and only red fluorescence can be detected. This tandem fluorescent protein expression vector system visually indicates the autophagic flux level.NSCLC cells were seeded on cell slides. When cells reached approximately 30% confluence, they were transfected with mRFP-GFP-LC3 adenovirus for 6 h. Next, the medium was removed and washed twice with PBS. The cells were incubated in RPMI 1640 medium containing serum for 48 h at 37 °C and 5% CO2. After incubation, the cells were washed twice with PBS. After cell fixation and nuclear staining, images were performed using a high-content Ti2-E inverted fluorescence microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

TGM2 is upregulated in EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC and associated with a poor prognosis

We investigated two erlotinib-resistant NSCLC cell lines, PC9ER and HCC827ER, and performed next-generation sequencing (NGS) on these cells. NGS results revealed that HCC827ER cells harbored HER2 amplification—a genetic alteration not detected in the parental HCC827 cell line. By contrast, neither PC9ER nor its parental PC9 cell line carried additional mutations associated with erlotinib resistance (data not shown). Five differentially expressed genes were screened out by analysing transcriptome sequencing data of EGFR-TKI-sensitive and -resistant cell lines (Fig. 1A). These genes were highly expressed in EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC cell lines (Fig. 1B). Subsequently, Kaplan‒Meier survival analysis was performed on an NSCLC cohort to evaluate the effect of the expression of these genes on the survival of NSCLC patients. NSCLC patients with high TGM2 expression had a significantly shorter overall survival (OS) and progression-free interval (PFI), supporting that TGM2 expression is a poor prognostic factor (Fig. 1C and D). Other genes, such as THBD, also showed significant statistical differences in OS and PFI among NSCLC patients, but the trends were opposite (Table 2). Then, we knocked down TGM2 and THBD respectively in EGFR-TKI-resistant cell lines to investigate their sensitivity to EGFR-TKIs (Fig. S1A - C). We found that TGM2-knockdown significantly reduced cell viability in response to erlotinib, whereas THBD knockdown did not alter erlotinib sensitivity compared to the control group (Fig. S1D and E). Therefore, we selected TGM2 for the follow-up study. We found that TGM2 expression in the two EGFR-TKI-resistant cell lines (PC9ER and HCC827ER) was significantly higher than that in the corresponding parental cell lines (PC9 and HCC827). Similarly, in H1975‒a resistant NSCLC cell line with the EGFR T790M mutation, TGM2 protein expression was also higher than that in PC9 and HCC827 (Fig. 1E). In addition, we analysed the expression levels of TGM2 in tissue samples from EGFR-TKI-sensitive and -resistant NSCLC patients by qRT‒PCR and IHC. The expression of TGM2 in tissue samples from EGFR-TKI-resistant patients was significantly higher than that in tissue samples from EGFR-TKI-sensitive patients (Fig. 1F - H). These results suggest that TGM2 may be associated with acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in EGFR-mutated NSCLC.

TGM2 promotes acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in EGFR-mutated NSCLC in vitro and in vivo

To verify the effect of TGM2 on acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC, PC9 and HCC827 cell lines with stable overexpression of TGM2, and PC9ER, HC827ER and H1975 cell lines with stable downregulation of TGM2 were constructed for subsequent studies (Fig. 2A and B). Next, we performed CCK-8 assays to evaluate the sensitivity of NSCLC cells to EGFR-TKIs. Downregulation of TGM2 significantly reduced the EGFR-TKIs (erlotinib and osimertinib) IC50 values of cells, while those of TGM2-overexpressing cells were significantly increased (Fig. 2C-H). EGFR-TKIs can exert antitumour effects by inducing cell apoptosis [22], so we next verified whether TGM2 regulates cell apoptosis. Downregulation of TGM2 enhanced apoptosis induced by erlotinib (Fig. 2I), while overexpression of TGM2 suppressed apoptosis induced by erlotinib (Fig. 2J).

We also used cystamine, a TGM2 inhibitor [23], and the results showed that the erlotinib IC50 value of cells was significantly reduced when cystamine was combined with erlotinib in PC9ER and HCC827ER cells (Fig. 2C-E). Next, we found that downregulation of TGM2 alone or treatment with cystamine did not induce apoptosis (Fig. 2K). However, the apoptosis of cells in the combined treatment group was significantly increased (Fig. 2L). These results suggest that TGM2 can enhance acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC in vitro.

TGM2 reduces lipid content in cells by enhancing autophagy

To elucidate the underlying mechanism by which TGM2 affects acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs, we first performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) on transcriptome sequencing data of clinical tissue samples from NSCLC patients before and after EGFR-TKI resistance. EGFR-TKI resistance was associated with lipolysis, glyceride catabolic processes and fatty acid metabolism (Fig. 4A-C). Next, we conducted GSEA on the NSCLC cohort data and found that TGM2 was correlated with autophagy and neutral lipid metabolism (Fig. 4D). We performed relevant experiments to determine whether TGM2 affects autophagy and neutral lipid metabolism. Chloroquine (CQ) is a late-stage autophagy inhibitor that disrupts lysosome function and prevents autophagosome degradation [24]. We treated TGM2-knockdown and TGM2-overexpressing NSCLC cells with CQ, respectively, and monitored the levels of autophagy-related proteins LC3 and p62. We observed an increase in LC3-II in TGM2-knockdown cells without CQ treatment. When CQ treatment was applied, it was observed that the expression of LC3II protein in the control cells significantly increased, while the expression of LC3II protein in the TGM2 knockdown cells showed no significant change. Furthermore, CQ treatment also increased the p62 protein level in TGM2-knockdown cells. (Fig. 4E). Overexpression of TGM2 resulted in elevated LC3-II and reduced p62 levels in cells following CQ treatment compared to control cells (Fig. 4F). These results suggested the specific role of TGM2 in promoting autophagic protein degradation. This reslut was further confirmed by the subsequent mRFP-GFP-LC3 assay, which showed that the red fluorescent spots in EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC cells decreased after downregulation of TGM2, suggesting that the number of autophagic lysosomes decreased and autophagic flow was reduced. However, autophagic flow in EGFR-TKI-sensitive NSCLC cells increased after the upregulation of TGM2 (Fig. 4G). In summary, these data demonstrate that TGM2 participates in autophagy by accelerating the degradation of autophagic protein.

In addition, we found an increase in intracellular LD content after TGM2 knockdown in EGFR-TKI-resistant cells (Fig. 4H and I). In contrast, intracellular LD content decreased in EGFR-TKI-sensitive cells after upregulation of TGM2 (Fig. 4J and K). We used CQ to inhibit autophagy in EGFR-TKI-sensitive cells with upregulated TGM2. Inhibition of autophagy restored the reduction in cellular LD content caused by TGM2 upregulation (Fig. 4L and M). These results suggest that TGM2-induced lipid reduction is regulated by the level of autophagy, so we speculate that TGM2 may affect intracellular lipid content by regulating lipophagy.

TGM2 promotes lipophagy and FAO and then promotes acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC

To determine whether TGM2 regulates the process of lipophagy, we first performed immunofluorescence experiments to examine the colocalization of LC3/LAMP1 with LDs. The results showed that TGM2 knockdown reduced the colocalization rate of LDs with LC3/LAMP1 (Fig. 5A), suggesting that lipophagy was inhibited. Lipophagy causes the decomposition of LDs to release free fatty acids (FFAs), which can enter mitochondria for FAO to release energy [25]. Therefore, to further verify the regulatory effect of TGM2 on lipophagy, we detected the expression of lipophagy-related proteins and the content of intracellular FFAs. The results showed that TGM2 knockdown increased the expression of the LD proteins PLIN2 and PLIN3, decreased the expression of the critical FAO enzymes CPT1A and CPT1B, and reduced the content of FFAs in the cells (Fig. 5B and C). These results indicate that TGM2 can regulate lipophagy and may affect FAO.

To determine whether TGM2 affects FAO, we examined the content of several enzymes related to FAO, such as ACADL, ACADM and HADHA. We found that knockdown of TGM2 in cells reduced the levels of these enzymes. This result indicated that TGM2 could regulate FAO (Fig. 5D). Since FAO promotes the production of energy substances to support tumour growth, we also examined the changes in these substances. The results showed that the intracellular content of the energy substance ATP decreased (Fig. 5E). The ratio of NADP+/NADPH increased after downregulation of TGM2, indicating that TGM2 knockdown inhibited the production of energy substances (Fig. 5F). These results further support that TGM2 can affect the process of FAO.

Studies have shown that enhanced lipophagy and FAO can support tumour growth under harsh environmental conditions and are associated with tumour metastasis and drug resistance [26–29]. We performed corresponding rescue experiments to determine whether TGM2 affects acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC by regulating lipophagy and downstream FAO. Etomoxir is an FAO inhibitor that inhibits the enzymatic activity of CPT1A, a key enzyme in FAO [29]. We treated cells with etomoxir and CQ respectively. The data showed that etomoxir or CQ treatment in cells could attenuate the increase in erlotinib IC50 value induced by TGM2 upregulation (Fig. 5G and H). We also examined the changes in intracellular energy substances after CQ or etomoxir treatment. CQ or etomoxir treatment reversed the decrease in ATP content and the increase in the NADP+/NADPH ratio caused by TGM2 overexpression (Fig. 5I and J). The results showed that CQ or etomoxir treatment reduced energy substance production. The reduced production of ATP and NADPH makes it difficult for tumour cells to resist the harsh environment, eventually restoring tumour sensitivity to drug treatment. In addition, the changes in the content of FFAs in cells treated with CQ or etomoxir were also consistent with the changes caused by the inhibition of lipophagy and FAO (Fig. 5K).

These results suggest that TGM2 promotes acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC by promoting lipophagy and FAO.

TGM2 interacts with TUFM and promotes lipophagy and FAO through TUFM

To determine the molecular mechanism by which TGM2 regulates lipophagy, we performed a coimmunoprecipitation (co-IP) assay and mass spectrometry (LC‒MS/MS) to identify potential molecules that interact with TGM2. A literature review was performed to screen the proteins associated with lipophagy in the LC‒MS/MS results, and TUFM and p62 were finally identified as potential interacting proteins (Table 3). It has been reported that TUFM can enhance autophagy flux in human cervical cancer cells and promote lipophagy in human liver cancer cells [30, 31]. P62 is a selective autophagy receptor that has been reported to be involved in regulating lipophagy [18, 32]. We confirmed the interaction among TGM2, TUFM and p62 by a co-IP experiment (Fig. 6A). P62 is a selective autophagy receptor, and its protein expression is regulated by autophagy levels. Therefore, we mainly explored the regulatory relationship between TGM2 and TUFM expression. TGM2 regulates TUFM expression at the protein level, while TUFM has no regulatory effect on the mRNA and protein levels of TGM2 (Figs. 6B-E and 7C). We speculated that TGM2 may affect TUFM expression at the posttranscriptional level.

The ubiquitin‒proteasome system is the main pathway of protein degradation in cells and is involved in the degradation of most proteins. Studies have reported that TUFM can be degraded by ubiquitination, and USP5 can deubiquitinate TUFM and increase its expression in colorectal cancer cells [33]. Another study showed that TGM2 could inhibit TRIM21-mediated STAT1 ubiquitination degradation to promote the malignant progression of gastric cancer [34]. Therefore, we speculate that the positive regulatory effect of TGM2 on TUFM may be associated with ubiquitination. We first examined TUFM levels after CHX treatment in PC9 and PC9ER cells and found that TGM2 overexpression prolonged the half-life of the TUFM protein (Fig. 6F). Next, we observed that the reduction in TUFM protein in TGM2-knockdown PC9ER cells was largely abrogated by MG-132, while TUFM protein levels were increased in TGM2-overexpressing PC9 cells (Fig. 6G). Finally, co-IP results showed that TGM2 overexpression reduced the level of ubiquitinated TUFM in PC9 cells, whereas TGM2 knockdown had the opposite effect (Fig. 6H). These results suggest that TGM2 stabilizes TUFM through deubiquitination. Subsequently, we verified whether TGM2 enzymatic activity is involved in stabilizing the TUFM protein. We treated NSCLC cells with the TGM2 inhibitor cystamine and observed that cystamine reduced TUFM protein expression in a dose-dependent manner without affecting TGM2 protein levels (Fig. 6I). Co-IP and western blot assays showed that, in the context of high TGM2 expression, cystamine increased TUFM ubiquitination and decreased TUFM protein levels. However, MG132 treatment largely reversed this decrease (Fig. 6J), suggesting that TGM2 promotes TUFM protein stability via its enzymatic activity.

We next examined whether TGM2 regulates lipophagy through TUFM. We first evaluated whether TUFM could regulate autophagy. The data showed that TUFM positively regulated autophagy in the late stage (Fig. 6K). Subsequently, we detected the colocalization of LC3/LAMP1 with LDs by immunofluorescence assay. We found that TGM2 overexpression significantly increased the colocalization rate of LDs with LC3/LAMP1, while this effect was reversed after TUFM knockdown (Fig. 7A). The results also showed that the content of intracellular LDs and the expression of the LD proteins PLIN2 and PLIN3 were decreased, and the expression of the critical FAO enzymes CPT1A and CPT1B and the content of FFAs in cells were increased with TGM2 overexpression. However, these effects were reversed by TUFM knockdown (Fig. 7B-D). These results suggest that TGM2 regulates lipophagy through TUFM.

Next, we examined whether TGM2 regulates FAO through TUFM. The data showed that intracellular FAO-related enzymes were upregulated after TGM2 overexpression, and this effect was reversed after TUFM knockdown (Fig. 7E). Our results also showed that overexpression of TGM2 increased the intracellular content of the energy substance ATP and decreased the NADP+/NADPH ratio, which was reversed by TUFM knockdown (Fig. 7F and G). These results suggest that TGM2 regulates FAO through TUFM.

These results indicate that TGM2 interacts with TUFM and promotes lipophagy and FAO through TUFM.

TGM2 enhances p62-mediated lipophagy through TUFM

We have demonstrated that TGM2 and TUFM interact with p62 and positively regulate autophagy. Since p62 is a common selective autophagy receptor, we speculate that lipophagy is ultimately mediated by p62. Studies have reported that p62 can oligomerize with itself to form p62 bodies, which have a stronger affinity for “cargo” and LC3 than p62 monomers and can enhance the autophagy process [35, 36]. We first examined whether TGM2 was involved in the formation of p62 bodies. The results showed that p62 body formation was reduced after downregulation of TGM2 in EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC cells (Fig. 8A). This indicates that TGM2 can promote the formation of p62 bodies. Next, we examined whether TGM2 promotes p62 body formation through TUFM. The data showed that p62 body formation was increased after TGM2 overexpression in cells, and this effect was reversed by TUFM knockdown (Fig. 8B).

To determine whether TGM2 promotes p62 body formation through TUFM, thereby enhancing lipophagy, we performed immunofluorescence colocalization experiments. The results showed that TGM2 overexpression promoted the colocalization of p62 bodies with LDs, and this effect was reversed after TUFM knockdown (Fig. 8C). Moreover, p62 bodies were colocalized with both LDs and LC3/LAMP1 (Fig. 8D). These results suggest that TGM2 promotes p62 body formation through TUFM, thereby enhancing lipophagy.

Studies have reported that p62 mediates lipid droplet (LD) catabolism by binding to LD-associated proteins [18, 29, 30]. PLIN2, a structural protein specific to LDs, protects LDs from degradation [37]. Using co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and immunofluorescence assays, we confirmed that p62 interacts with PLIN2 on LDs, and both proteins colocalize in the cytoplasm (Fig. S2A and B). Next, we knocked down p62 in NSCLC cells and found that p62 knockdown reduced the colocalization rate of LDs with LC3/LAMP1 and the expression of CPT1A, while increasing the expression of PLIN2 (Fig. S2C - E). This suggests that lipophagy is inhibited. Additionally, p62 knockdown enhanced the sensitivity of NSCLC cells to EGFR-TKIs (Fig. S2F - H). These results indicate that p62 is required for the autophagic degradation of lipid droplets and promotes resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC cells.

TGM2 promotes acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC via TUFM

Studies have reported that the expression of TUFM is upregulated in a variety of tumours, and high expression of TUFM can support tumour growth [38, 39]. We found that TUFM was highly expressed in EGFR-TKI-resistant NSCLC (Fig. 9A). To determine whether TGM2 regulates acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC via TUFM, we first downregulated TUFM in EGFR-TKI-resistant cells and analysed the resistance changes to EGFR-TKIs. The downregulation efficiency of the TUFM gene was verified by western blotting experiments (Fig. 9B). The CCK-8 assay showed that knockdown of TUFM significantly reduced the IC50 values of EGFR-TKIs in cells (Fig. 9C and D). In addition, Western blotting results showed that downregulation of TUFM promoted erlotinib-induced apoptosis (Fig. 9E). These results suggest that TUFM regulates acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC.

We next downregulated TUFM in EGFR-TKI-sensitive NSCLC cells overexpressing TGM2 to determine whether TGM2 regulates acquired resistance to erlotinib via TUFM. We verified the downregulation efficiency of the TUFM gene by western blot experiments (Fig. 9F). The CCK-8 assay showed that TUFM knockdown reversed the increase in IC50 values of erlotinib caused by TGM2 overexpression (Fig. 9G). The flow cytometry apoptosis assay also showed that the TUFM knockdown could reverse the reduction in erlotinib-induced apoptosis caused by TGM2 overexpression (Fig. 9H). These results suggest that TGM2 promotes acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC through TUFM.

Discussion

Although EGFR-TKIs have significantly prolonged the survival of advanced NSCLC patients with sensitizing EGFR mutations, the development of drug resistance remains a significant obstacle in the treatment of this type of NSCLC. Our study revealed that TGM2 is likely a useful marker for EGFR-TKI response and a therapeutic target in EGFR-mutated NSCLC. The upregulation of TGM2 promotes lipophagy and FAO by inhibiting the ubiquitin-mediated degradation of TUFM, thereby enhancing resistance to EGFR-TKI. Moreover, targeting lipophagy and FAO could reverse TGM2-mediated EGFR-TKI resistance.

TGM2 is abnormally expressed in a variety of tumours and plays a critical role in tumour invasion, metastasis, and drug resistance [20, 21]. Previous studies have shown that TGM2 can affect tumour growth by regulating autophagy [40, 41]. Another study identified TGM2 as a negative regulator of lipogenesis in mouse fibroblasts [42]. Lipid metabolism has been verified to play a vital role in solid tumours by regulating the synthesis and hydrolysis of fatty acids [43]. Several recent studies have demonstrated a new relationship between autophagy and lipid metabolism - lipophagy, which can act as a selective autophagy that causes autophagic degradation of intracellular lipid droplets to supply energy [44]. However, the relationship between TGM2-mediated regulation of autophagy and intracellular lipid content has not been fully elucidated. Our study revealed that autophagy levels positively regulates TGM2-induced intracellular lipolysis. It was also confirmed that TGM2 can enhance lipophagy.

Reportedly, lipophagy mediates the release of free fatty acids (FFAs) into mitochondria for fatty acid β-oxidation, thereby generating ATP to maintain cellular homeostasis [45]. Our data suggest that TGM2 promotes fatty acid β-oxidation, with concomitant production of energy substrates. Inhibition of lipophagy or FAO using the small-molecule inhibitors CQ or etomoxir rescued the increase in EGFR-TKI resistance induced by TGM2 overexpression. This was consistent with the results of some studies showing that inhibition of lipophagy by CQ and etomoxir significantly suppressed the proliferation and promoted the death of ascites-derived metastatic ovarian cancer cells and supports that targeting lipophagy can be used as a potential therapeutic strategy to inhibit ovarian cancer metastasis [46]. The autophagy/lipid degradation/FAO/ATP generation pathway promotes glioma progression, while inhibition of autophagy and FAO can effectively reverse this effect [47]. Many clinical trials have demonstrated the effectiveness of using chloroquine/hydroxychloroquine to target autophagy in the treatment of tumours [48]. The fatty acid oxidation inhibitor etomoxir is also undergoing clinical trials [49], these findings collectively set the stage for clinical translation in NSCLC with acquired EGFR-TKI resistance.

There are few reports on the molecular mechanisms of lipophagy in tumours. Mondal et al. found that the selective autophagy receptor p62/SQSTM1 could interact with the protein cPLA2 on LDs and mediate LD uptake into autophagosomes for degradation in cervical and ovarian cancer [18]. Another study showed that TUFM can interact with the ATG5-ATG12 complex to mediate lipophagy in cervical and liver cancer cells [31]. Our study revealed that TGM2 stabilizes TUFM through deubiquitination and promotes p62-mediated lipophagy through TUFM. P62 is a critical molecule in the process of autophagy. Studies have shown that p62 can self-oligomerize through its PB1 domain to form P62 bodies, which have a stronger affinity for “cargo” and LC3 than p62 monomers and can enhance autophagic processes [35, 36]. Our data show that TGM2 promotes the self-oligomerization of p62 to form p62 bodies, as well as the colocalization of p62 bodies and LDs through TUFM, and the colocalization of p62, lipid droplets and LC3/LAMP1.

PLIN2 is a characteristic LD structural protein that protect LDs from degradation [37]. Studies have reported that p62 can interact with proteins on LDs to degrade them, thereby disrupting LD structure and rendering them susceptible to decomposition by cytoplasmic lipases or degradation via lipophagy [18, 32]. Our results show that p62 binds to PLIN2 on LDs to enhance lipophagy.

We found distinct known resistance mechanisms were identified across these cell lines. HCC827ER harbored an additional genetic alteration, HER-2 amplification; H1975(a commercially available resistant cell line) carried the EGFR T790M mutation; PC9ER exhibited EMT alterations compared to its parental PC9 cell line (Fig. S4A and B), However, we found that TGM2 knockdown reversed resistance in all three cell lines, regardless of their inherent resistance mechanisms (HER-2 amplification, T790M, or EMT), indicating that the pro-resistance function of TGM2 is independent of the aforementioned known resistance mechanisms.

In conclusion, our study reveals for the first time that TGM2 is involved in a novel lipid metabolic process, namely lipophagy. TGM2 stabilizes TUFM expression via deubiquitination to promote lipophagy and FAO, thereby contributing to acquired resistance to EGFR-TKIs in EGFR-mutated NSCLC. In addition, TGM2 can promote the formation of p62 bodies through TUFM, and these p62 bodies combine with PLIN2 to facilitate lipophagy (Fig. 10). Therefore, TGM2 is likely a useful response marker for EGFR-TKI and targeting TGM2-mediated lipophagy is a potential therapeutic strategy to restore the efficacy of EGFR-TKIs in NSCLC patients with acquired EGFR-TKI resistance.

Limitations of the study

The current study still has some limitations. Firstly, the detailed mechanism of TGM2 stabilizes TUFM through deubiquitination has not been elucidated. In addition, substrates of TGM2-related lipophagic degradation and how they function on EGFR-TKIs resistance need to be further explored.

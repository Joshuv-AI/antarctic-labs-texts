Technological monitoring of motor parameters to assess multidimensional frailty of older people in the PRO-HOME project
Authors: Emanuele Seminerio, Wanda Morganti, Marina Barbagelata, Sanket Rajeev Sabharwal, Simone Ghisio, Camilla Prete, Barbara Senesi, Simone Dini, Romina Custureri, Simonetta Galliani, Simona Morelli, Gianluca Puleo, Carlo Berutti-Bergotto, Antonio Camurri, Alberto Pilotto, Lisa Nunziata Cammalleri, Lisa Nunziata Cammalleri, Ilaria Indiano, Erica Tavella, Francesca Tricerri, Sara Garaboldi, Alberto Cella, Marco De Benedetto, Alessandra Pinna, Martina Vigo, Orietta Parodi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
An interconnected system employing Kinect Azure and Fitbit Sense for continuous and non-intrusive data collection was used in the PRO-HOME protected discharge program, aiming at monitoring functional and clinical parameters in hospitalized older patients at different risks of frailty. The present study shows the findings on 30 older patients included in the PRO-HOME project. The Fitbit Sense recorded the mean daily and hourly number of steps, mean daily walked distance, and time spent inactive. Moreover, Kinect infrared camera captured gait speed and daily mean latero-lateral (body sway) and antero-posterior oscillations (lean-in). Patients underwent a standard Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA) to compute the Multidimensional Prognostic Index (MPI), including basic and instrumental activities of daily living (ADL, IADL), cognition (Short Portable Mental Status Questionnaire, SPMSQ) and nutrition, risk of pressure sores (Exton-Smith Scale, ESS), comorbidity, number of drugs and cohabitation status. Significant correlations between the mean hourly number of steps and MPI (p = 0.022), IADL (p = 0.013), SPMSQ (p = 0.006), ESS (p = 0.009), and both mean and maximum automated gait speed (p = 0.046 and p = 0.048) were found. Automated gait speed was also correlated with mean walked distance per day (p = 0.007) and lean-in (p = 0.047). Domotic technological monitoring through Fitbit Sense and Kinect Azure provides information on multidimensional frailty, including mobility and cognitive and functional status, in older people.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-80061-6.

Introduction

The trend toward personalized and proactive care indicates the increasing importance of technological monitoring in healthcare, particularly for older people. Technological monitoring offers the chance for the aging population to be safe and cared for in their own home environment, following the principle of “ageing in place”1. Moreover, telemonitoring gives healthcare professionals the opportunity to gather more precise continuous real-time data in ecological settings, with less invasive measurements. This is essential for improving care quality, encouraging independence, and guaranteeing the security of older patients, and it can be offered in various modalities.

Home monitoring systems help prevent acute events2–4, while integrated alert systems—including fall detectors, medication reminders, and wearable emergency buttons—enhance safety and well-being5,6. Decision Support Systems (DSS) enable early intervention and personalized care for older adults by setting criteria for alerts based on patient profiles and health metrics7.

One of the most significant prognostic factors for higher morbidity and death in older adults has been determined to be reduced gait. Mobility is, in fact, a significant factor in quality of life and a dependable metric to predict disability and mortality in the general population8.

Technological monitoring offers continuous and non-intrusive data collecting, consistently employing wearables, sensors, and motion-tracking systems, which allow real-time evaluation of many health factors, such as activity levels, vital signs, and movement patterns. These techniques were applied in the PRO-HOME study, deploying a protected discharge program that integrates clinical, motor, and cognitive activities in a multidimensional and multicomponent caring approach. The program uses technological and structural components to monitor clinical, functional, and safety factors continuously. The need for a multidimensional approach in addressing frailty in older people9 calls for a technological system capable of evaluating many parameters, such as the one available to PRO-HOME, while also taking into consideration the specific needs of the older population, such as the low invasiveness of the devices employed. Furthermore, assessment outside the hospital setting is mainly conducted relying on capacity and perception related to specific tasks (e.g. daily activities such as climbing the stairs), while telemonitoring can capture the actual performance in their daily life, which should be integrated in the overall evaluation10,11. The potential of monitoring and sensor technologies for early identification of health deterioration, fall risk, and cognitive impairment—all components leading to frailty - is highlighted in the literature12–14. In addition, inexpensive, non-intrusive monitoring devices, including smartwatches and motion-tracking cameras, have become more popular due to their practicality and usability15,16. The present research aims to assess the agreement between clinical and technologically monitored parameters of the same group of patients at risk of multidimensional frailty, admitted to an in-hospital protected discharge facility, by analyzing the correlations between functional, motor, and cognitive parameters assessed using clinical and technological tools.

Methods

Study design and clinical data collection

The PRO-HOME study is a randomized clinical trial co-funded by the Italian Ministry of Health and the Liguria region within the MULTIPLAT_AGE Research Net-Program, involving four Italian regions: Liguria, Campania, Calabria, and Piemonte. The main aim of the PRO-HOME study was to explore the effect on length of hospital stay and multidimensional changes of a transitional care program, developed inside a technologically equipped facility, in hospitalized older patients deemed dischargeable but needing further monitoring. Starting from May 2021 and over two years, the study included participants aged 65 and older from a general hospital’s Acute Geriatric Unit based on the following inclusion criteria: admission for acute events, clinical stability, dischargeable from the acute ward, preserved or slightly impaired personal autonomy (ADL ≥ 3/6), and intact or mildly impaired cognitive functioning (SPMSQ ≤ 5/10). Thirty participants underwent a multicomponent intervention in the PROHOME facility, i.e., a transitional care program including physical activity, cognitive training, and lifestyle education.

In the present study, we addressed a secondary aim of the PRO-HOME project i.e. the agreement between the standard clinical assessment and technological evaluation in the 30 participants included in the PRO-HOME facility (Fig. 1). Further details of the protocol17, study design and clinical findings of PRO-HOME study18 have been reported elsewhere.

The Italian Ministry of Health (27/02/2018, No. 44761) and the Local Ethical Committee (17/05/2021, register No 176/2021, registered on ClinicalTrials.gov with code NCT06227923) approved the study, and participants signed an informed consent form. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Camera images are not captured to protect patient privacy. The Galliera Hospital’s server database securely stores clinical data and parameters, with restricted access to research partners.

At baseline, demographic data, including age, gender and education level, were collected along with a clinical assessment of heart rate (HR). Moreover, a standard Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA)19,20 was administered to assess the following parameters: (i) Basal Activities of Daily Living (ADL)21; (ii) Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADL)22; (iii) Short Portable Mental State Questionnaire (SPMSQ)23; (iv) Exton-Smith scale (ESS) to assess the risk of pressure sores;24; (v) Mini Nutritional Assessment – short form (MNA-SF)25; (vi) Comorbidity with the Cumulative Illness Rating Scale-Comorbidity Index (CIRS-CI)26; (vii) number of medications taken daily; (viii) Co-habitation status: with family, in an institution, or alone.

The resulting scores were used to compute the Multidimensional Prognostic Index (MPI)27, a well-validated tool to predict adverse outcomes such as mortality, re-hospitalization, and institutionalization in hospitalized older people28,29 to identify multidimensional frailty and quantify its degree of severity9,30. The MPI can be expressed as a continuous numerical score or in three classes of risk according to defined cut-offs representing ascending levels of multidimensional frailty (MPI-1 ≤ 0.33 low-risk; MPI-2 between 0.34 and 0.66 moderate risk; MPI ≥ 0.67 high risk).

Furthermore, all participants were administered the Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS-15)31 to assess depressive symptoms and the Short Form survey (SF-12, based on the Short Form Health Survey 36)32 to measure health-related quality of life. The Intervention Group also underwent an evaluation of physical performance through the Hand Grip test and the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB)33.

Technological infrastructure and data collection

The PRO-HOME facility is a state-of-the-art transitional care setup designed for elderly patients to create a home-like environment with modern furnishings and advanced technical equipment. The facility features two living areas, a bed, a bathroom, and high-tech and passive systems to create a fully automated environment. It includes an infrared motion-tracking camera (Kinect Azure) placed in a 6-meter-long corridor, a bio-multifunctional smart wearable sensor (Fitbit Sense), and a PadBot P2 telepresence robot. Closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras, and an emergency device with a call button were installed to ensure the safety of patients. PadBot P2 was used to allow comfortable communication from the participant inside the protected environment to the healthcare staff. The two CCTV cameras, positioned in the bedroom and in the living room, guaranteed the continuous real-time supervision of the environment by the healthcare staff. Both PadBot and CCTV cameras did not record any data. Patients are encouraged to move around freely, and the facility is designed to not impede their mobility.

The software framework utilizes a network of devices that are wirelessly interconnected to facilitate a seamless transmission of collected data to a central workstation. This workstation is equipped with necessary software modules developed to perform real-time analysis, employing mathematical and statistical methods to screen the data as received. The core of this system is a software framework that integrates multiple modules, each responsible for conducting kinematic analyses to identify and process participant movements accurately captured through an infrared camera setup. A Python-based context-aware component guides the data acquisition process, starting the recordings in response to motion detection and optimizing the parameters necessary. Building upon computational models presented in previous studies34,35 that informed the conceptualization of this system framework, we also integrated algorithms for real-time gait analysis. This framework ensures that once the data has been collected from the devices, it is saved in individual CSV files for each day and processed for it to be methodically organized into an SQLite structured database. The database is accessible via Python scripts, allowing for detailed querying and analysis to make the gathered information readily available for further study. In developing this framework, the EyesWeb XMI environment was leveraged, an open-source platform developed by our partners at Casa Paganini—InfoMus36. The Fitbit Sense recorded daily mean values of heart rate and movement parameters, including the number of steps taken, the distance walked, and the percentage of time spent inactive, which is based on step count. Concurrently, the Kinect Azure camera captured depth map and skeletal data using its SDK to automate gait analysis along a 4-meter linear path in the 6-meter apartment corridor. This analysis included the recording of mean, minimum, and maximum daily gait speeds (m/s) as well as daily mean degrees of latero-lateral oscillation (body sway) and antero-posterior oscillation (lean-in), measured from the trunk-head vertical axis.

The system tracks the quality of data acquired and eliminates erroneous measurements, such as brief segments within the camera’s field of view but not the corridor itself. The acquired data is then subjected to manual screening in the phase preceding the statistical analysis. The objective of this screening is to identify the measurements collected on the actual dates of stay in the PRO-HOME apartment and the measurements from the Kinect Azure covering the entire corridor length.

Statistics

Continuous clinical scales and parameters were summarised as means (M) and standard deviations (SDs) or medians and inter-quartile range (IQR) if not normally distributed. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons among different methods of evaluation for the same variable (e.g. Heart Rate) were made using t-tests or Wilcoxon’s signed rank tests based on the normality or non-normality of their distributions.

For the correlation analyses, Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was employed to assess the strength and direction of the relationships between the measured variables. Spearman’s rank correlation was chosen to assess associations37 between variables that may not follow a normal distribution. To establish significance, p-values were calculated for each correlation coefficient and a two-tailed value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The correlations’ strength was assessed with a commonly used rule of thumb38 for the evaluation of Spearman’s rho values, as follows: Negligible (0.00 < 0.10), Weak (0.10 < 0.20), Moderate (0.20 < 0.40), Relatively strong (0.40 < 0.60), Strong (0.60 < 0.80), Very Strong (0.80 < 1.00).

Discussion

The findings of this study, which fit with the larger framework of the expanding importance of technology monitoring in healthcare, particularly for older people, provide insightful information about a system installed in a smart environment for the protected discharge of hospitalized older adults.

The observed negative correlations between mean number of steps per day/hour and MPI-assessed multidimensional frailty, along with many of its underlying components like cognitive status and instrumental activities of daily living, confirm the strong association between reduced mobility and frailty in an ecological setting. These correlations also highlight the potential benefits of using technological devices to monitor gait and step quantity. Such devices can provide valuable information on various aspects of older adults’ health status and potentially identify subjects at risk of multidimensional frailty. Previous studies have suggested the potential benefits of using monitoring technologies for early detection of frailty, health decline, mobility, and cognitive impairments8,12,13. Since both lower cognitive function and lower functional autonomy in IADL appear to be associated with a decreased quantity of movement inside the PRO-HOME apartment, it can be suggested that the Fitbit Sense smartwatch has proven helpful in detecting subtle changes in activity levels that may denote a cognitive and functional decline, offering an additional tool for monitoring these conditions. All these correlations fall into the “relatively strong” range (Spearman’s rho between 0.40 and 0.60).

Along with the correlations presented, the quantity of steps is known to predict cognitive status and IADL older patients39,40, further attesting to the reliability and usefulness of PRO-HOME’s step count measurements for complementing traditional assessments of functional ability and cognitive health. By monitoring these domains, using an objective indicator such as the number of steps while exploiting an ecological and low-invasive system, the integrated system, could send an alert in case of worsening of functional or clinical conditions in terms of multidimensional frailty. This is possible since the alert message is elaborated in real-time by a data dashboard and automatically sent to healthcare professionals or caregivers’ devices, thus allowing the prompt deployment of appropriate interventions.

The ecological instrumental assessment of mobility has been recently identified as a pivotal endpoint for the intervention8. Indeed, the gold standard for movement analysis is represented by 3D Motion Capture systems41. This technology can effectively evaluate different positions of the limbs in space with high time and space resolution. However, it is costly and can only be employed in specific labs with experienced personnel. Conversely, wearable devices, body-worn sensors, infrared and 3D cameras, and digital and mobile applications, often used simultaneously in integrated systems capable of performing real-life measurements of basic movements, physical activity, gait, changes in mobility, and other parameters42, may represent affordable and scalable solutions in clinical practice. This was the approach followed by PRO-HOME, where the low-cost and low-invasive technology allows the program to be scalable and feasible. The positive correlation between heart rate assessments by medical staff and smartwatch measurements further underscores the clinical relevance of low-cost and low-invasive technology in patient monitoring. It suggests the reliability of PRO-HOME’s system in supervising the patient’s vital conditions, possibly offering nuanced perspectives on their health and potentially supporting the decision-making process.

Positive correlations between the Fitbit Sense’s movement parameters and the Kinect Azure’s metrics—such as body sway and speed—indicate that the data these two devices provide regarding physical activity and movement patterns is complementary. Kinect Azure assesses quantitative characteristics of gait (i.e. number of steps and walked distance) while the Fitbit smartwatch measures qualitative parameters (i.e. anterior-posterior and latero-lateral postural oscillations). This demonstrates how data from many sources can be integrated to better represent a person’s mobility and functional state. Notably surprising is the relationship between instrumentally assessed gait speed detected by the Kinect Azure system and walked distance and trunk-head oscillations that could not be identified through traditional clinical gait assessments. These findings highlight how instrumental assessments, providing continuous and real-time tracking and thus offering an articulated outline of patients’ health, do not suffer the drawbacks of conventional techniques of clinical evaluation, which are episodic and based on recurrent evaluations that may not adequately reflect the changing character of an older person’s health, while also depending on patient recollection and being susceptible to subjective biases. The transition towards ecological instrumental assessments has the potential to complement traditional clinic assessment, hence leading to better-informed and proactive healthcare interventions for the older population.

In addition to these results, the mean walked distance negatively correlates with latero-lateral and antero-posterior oscillation degrees, which correlate simultaneously. This finding, corroborated by two other correlations involving body sway and lean-in (with mean speed per day and maximum speed per day, respectively), shows how lower mobility is associated with elevated trunk-head oscillation degree values, signalling heightened lateral and antero-posterior sway as deviations from a correct vertical posture, which have been linked to an increased risk of falls in literature14. This relationship demonstrates how monitoring technology can show specific metrics associated with fall risk alongside identifying general health markers. These correlations, whose strength based on effect size ranges from “relatively strong” to “strong”, indicate how Kinect Azure and Fitbit Sense complement each other, providing relevant qualitative and quantitative information on the patient’s gait.

Our findings suggest that using PRO-HOME technology for monitoring older patients has significant advantages that could allow a continuous monitoring of some aspects of multidimensional frailty and the deployment of prompt interventions. The incorporation of technology such as Fitbit Sense and Kinect Azure into PRO-HOME represents a feasible paradigm not only for in-hospital protected structures but for a domiciliary application of health monitoring as well, fulfilling the needs of an aging population in terms of security and low invasiveness, while also capturing substantial and accurate data. The study has, however, certain limitations. The instrumental monitoring, having lasted only a few days of stay (median = 2 days, minimum 1 day, and maximum 4 days) inside the PRO-HOME facility, could have missed long-term patterns or variations in health metrics. An extended observation time might provide a more thorough insight into patient clinical and mobility trajectories. Furthermore, patient compliance with wearing the smartwatch and using the Kinect is essential to the monitoring system’s efficacy, and its variation may add bias to the data and impact their interpretation.

A possible explanation for the moderate strength of the association between clinical and technological HR values probably lies in the different timespan of the HR measurements: the Fitbit considers the mean of all the HR datapoints collected during the stay in PRO-HOME while the clinical HR reflected a single point of measure by healthcare staff. This aspect should be further explored performing more frequent or simultaneous clinical evaluations. Moreover, since data analysis did not take into account longitudinal assessment, it was not possible to verify the association between postural sway as assessed by Kinect and cognitive changes, as suggested by some previous studies43,44. A possible improvement for the overall results could also concern a higher population numerosity, leading to more statistically significant findings and allowing machine learning analysis techniques to identify complex patterns.

Conclusion

The study’s findings suggest that technological monitoring, particularly using wearable devices like the Fitbit Sense and motion-tracking systems like the Kinect Azure, offers valuable information on various aspects of multidimensional frailty, including physical activity and cognitive and functional status in older people. Continuous monitoring in a protected discharge environment, such as PRO-HOME, can offer timely insights into patients’ multidimensional profile, which may support the development of tailored care plans. More research on the topic is encouraged, along with the broad use of comparable monitoring technologies in the healthcare industry, as these innovative techniques can significantly improve geriatric care and overall patients’ well-being.

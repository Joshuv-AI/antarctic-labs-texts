On study of transition fronts of Fisher-KPP type reaction-diffusion PDEs by non-linear transformations into exactly solvable class
Preet Mishra, Sapna Ratan Shah, R. K. Brojen Singh

Abstract
Spatio-temporal dynamics of the evolution of population involving growth and diffusion processes can be modeled by class of partial diffusion equations (PDEs) known as reaction-diffusion systems. In this work, we developed a nonlinear transformations method that converts the original nonlinear Fisher-KPP class of PDEs into an exactly solvable class. We then demonstrated that the proposed nonlinear transformation method intrinsically preserves the relaxation behavior of the solutions to asymptotic values of the non-linear dynamical system. We also show that these particular transforms are very amenable to yield an exact closed form solution in terms of the heat kernel and analytical approximations through the two variable Hermite polynomials. With this proposed method, we calculated the front velocity and shape of the propagating wave and showed how the non-linear transformation affects these parameters for both short and long epochs. As applications, we focus on solving pertinent cases of the Fisher-KPP type of PDEs relating to the evolutionary dynamics by assigning fitness to the mutant gene according to zygosity conditions. We calculated the relaxation of velocity with the parameters of the initial conditions in the following cases, namely, the Fisher, the heterozygote inferior fitness, the heterozygote superior fitness, and finally a general nonlinearity case. We also verified previous conjectures through the exact solutions computed using the proposed method. Keywords: Non-linear dynamics ; Fisher-KPP type PDE ; Transformation methods ; Lower and Upper Bounds ; Population genetics .

The structure of the paper is as follows. We begin by stating a general transformation function that maps the nonlinear PDE solution into a solvable class. which gives the following PDE

\displaystyle u_{t}+\left(1-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\right)F(u)=\frac{1-F^{\prime}(u)}{F(u)}u_{x}^{2}+u_{xx}

(59)

Now, we have a choice so that we can have approximation, which is if
\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}
is sufficiently close to 1 either
\forall t
or as
t\uparrow
, we can neglect the term containing
(1-\frac{f^{\prime}}{f})
as it is comparatively very small. Following this approximation, we can eliminate the coefficient of
F(u)
. This approximation allows us to express
\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}\approx 1
so that we can represent,
f\approx e^{t}
. This reduces the equation (
59
) to yield the following PDE,

\displaystyle u_{t}=\frac{1-F^{\prime}(u)}{F(u)}u_{x}^{2}+u_{xx}

(60)

This gives us a more closer bound to the original PDE than the one obtained by Ansatz type I i.e (
13
). The analytical proof of this can be done using Maximum principle and is given in the Appendix B.

II.2.2
Dispersion relation

Let us consider linearized form of the function
F
given by,
F(u)=u
, because this is the dominant contribution to the wave propagation as
0\leq u\leq 1
. Hence, taking this dominant contribution in the front of the wave, the PDE (
60
) reduces to the following simpler form,

\displaystyle u_{t}=u_{xx}

(61)

We followed the leading edge analysis technique proposed by Murray
[
21
]
. We assume an initial condition of the form given by,

\displaystyle u(x,0)\sim e^{-ax}\quad\text{as}\quad x\rightarrow\infty

(62)

where,
a
is a constant. Then, following Murray’s technique, we define the travelling wave ansatz given by,

\displaystyle u(x,t)=u(x-ct)=Ae^{-a(x-ct)}

(63)

Now, putting the ansatz (
63
) to the equation (
61
), and after simplification, we get,
c=a
which have the dimension of velocity.

II.2.3
Application to Fisher’s case

Let us apply the proposed transformation technique to original Fisher’s case, where,
F(u)=u(1-u)
. From this transformation equation (
60
), we get the following modified PDE and transformation function,

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1-u}u_{x}^{2}+u_{xx}

(64)

\displaystyle G(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u}{1-u}=e^{t}h(x,t)

(65)

A point to be noted about the diffusion equation
h_{t}=h_{xx}-h
for
h(x,t)
is that this can be analogous to the conduction of heat along a wire which loses heat from its surface at a rate proportional to its temperature
[
79
]
(p.329). This PDE has been extensively studied and solutions for various kinds are known
[
78
,
76
,
45
,
77
,
50
]
. Now, for solving
h_{t}=h_{xx}-h
, we followed the procedure in
[
78
]
and we get the solution as
h(x,t)=e^{-t}w(x,t)
, where,
w(x,t)
now satisfies the diffusion equation
w_{t}=w_{xx}
. Thus, solving this diffusion equation, we obtain the solution
w(x,t)
which can be related to
u(x,t)
as given by,

\displaystyle G(u)=\frac{u}{1-u}=w(x,t)

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{w(x,t)}{1+w(x,t)}

(66)

To demonstrate this ansatz we chose an initial condition to be
u(x,0)=\frac{1}{1+e^{bx}}
so that
w(x,0)=e^{-bx}
. From this initial condition, one can get
w(x,t)=e^{-b(x-bt)}
. Now, putting in the equation (
66
), we arrive at the solution
u
as,

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{e^{-b(x-bt)}}{1+e^{-b(x-bt)}}

(67)

II.2.4
Case B: h(x,t) satisfies
\displaystyle h_{t}=h_{xx}-\frac{2}{h}h_{x}^{2}

As a conjecture we study this particular case of transformation applicable only for the Fisher type of nonlinearities. We take the ansatz for
G
in terms of
h(x,t)
as given by,

\displaystyle G(u)=\frac{1}{1+h(x,t)}

(68)

From this ansatz, we have,

\displaystyle h_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1+h)^{2}G^{\prime}u_{x}

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(1+h)^{2}G^{\prime}u_{t}

\displaystyle h_{xx}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2h_{x}^{2}}{1+h}-G^{\prime\prime}(1+h)^{2}u_{x}^{2}-G^{\prime}(1+h)^{2}u_{xx}

Then, substituting the above expressions into the equation
\displaystyle h_{t}=h_{xx}-\frac{2}{h}h_{x}^{2}
, we get the transformed PDE as,

\displaystyle u_{t}=\frac{G^{\prime\prime}}{G^{\prime}}u_{x}^{2}+u_{xx}

(69)

Now, as an adhoc approximation we can either neglect the term containing
\frac{G^{\prime\prime}}{G^{\prime}}u_{x}^{2}
which is clearly not acceptable, and hence, we have to proceed as follows. Now if we take
G(u)=u
we can eliminate the term
\frac{G^{\prime\prime}}{G^{\prime}}u_{x}^{2}
completely and thus obtain a completely linearised homogeneous heat equation. The problem is thus reduced to solving the PDE
\displaystyle h_{t}=h_{xx}-\frac{2}{h}h_{x}^{2}
which to the best of our knowledge appears to be not known and the only mention of this PDE is in the work of
[
49
]
(Eq.6 in p.455).

III
Conclusion

The spatiotemporal species population growth and development is quite complicated process driven by a large number of factors. Certain aspects of such complicated processes and dynamics can be well explained in a simplified manner by the classic Fisher-KPP PDEs. However, the functional form
F
in population genetics can have various forms which are generally nonlinear in nature to deal with different aspects of species evolution and dynamics. We have extensively reviewed various methodological perspectives of solving Fisher-KPP types of PDEs and both analytical and numerical results along this direction.

In this work, we proposed a systematic nonlinear transformation method which beautifully converts the original classic Fisher-KPP type class of PDEs into exactly solvable class of PDEs. Further, the significance of this method is that this method inherits essential inherent properties, such as, relaxation, asymptotic properties etc from the original non-linear dynamical system which we proved in this work. Further, we also showed that this particular transformation method allows us to obtain exact closed form solution of the nonlinear Fisher-KPP type PDEs in terms of the heat kernel and analytical approximations through the two variable Hermite polynomials. With our method we verified some of the numerical results of these PDEs studied before.

We considered four specific functional forms of
F
with special connection with the population genetics in the original Fisher-KPP type PDEs and applied our method to address some of the important properties and dynamics of evolution of the mutant gene. We then calculate the front velocities of the propagating wave and found a power law relaxation for the front velocities. On the basis of the above work we put together two hypothesis: Firstly, together with more advanced renormalization group theories we can get better bounds and secondly on modifying the growth functions i.e. the exponential we hope to predict some new interesting phenomena. We hope these ideas and proposed method will open up new areas of further rigorous research into these techniques.

IV.1.1
Estimates of the heat kernel

Our nonlinear transform extensibly uses the heat equation throughout because of the known properties of the heat kernel. For a ready reference of a particular property which makes the homogenous heat equation a particular suitable candidate we cite a lemma from Moet

Lemma 1.

Let
h
be the solution of the initial valued problem,

\displaystyle h_{t}=h_{xx},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }t>0

(70)

with the initial condition,
h(x,0)=h_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}
, where,
h_{0}
is a bounded continuous function having at most a finite number of discontinuity points, which belongs to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for some
p\geq 1
. Then
h
satisfies the estimates

\displaystyle|h(x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4\pi t)^{-1/2}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\quad\text{for all }(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)

(71)

\displaystyle\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(4\pi t)^{-(1/2)(1/p-1/q)}\|h_{0}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\quad\text{for all }t>0\text{ and any }q\geq p\geq 1

(72)

Proof:

The proof can be found in
[
44
]
.

This is simply stating a known fact about how the heat provided initially is transported everywhere in the domain. This is a standard estimate using the parabolic inequalities and for the proof we refer to
[
44
]
.This result is especially useful because this states the exact way the solution
h(x,t)
depends on its initial values. The above lemma also demonstrates the regularisation features of the heat kernel that makes the solutions converge uniformly to their asymptotic value and we will be needing this behaviour crucially for the nonlinear transform to work.

IV.1.2
Solution using Heat Kernel in the nonlinear transformation

Proposition 2:

The general closed form solution is given by the transformation G as:

\displaystyle G[u(x,t)]=\left[\frac{f(t)}{f(0)\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(u(x^{\prime},0))e^{-\frac{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4t}}dx^{\prime}\right]

Proof:

We start by stating the fundamental solution of the heat equation which is given by,

\displaystyle h(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x^{\prime},0)e^{-\frac{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4t}}dx^{\prime}

The initial condition of the heat hernel can be obtained from the transformation equation (
6
) by putting
t=0
which is given by,

\displaystyle h(x,0)=\frac{1}{f(0)}G[u(x,0)]

(75)

with

\displaystyle G[u(x,t)]=\left[\frac{f(t)}{f(0)\sqrt{4\pi t}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G[u(x^{\prime},0)]e^{-\frac{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}{4t}}dx^{\prime}\right]

Which when used with the inverse transform of

IV.1.3
Solutions with multivariable Hermite polynomials

Proposition 3:

The complete series solution for u(x,t) can be obtained with arbritrary precision by,

\displaystyle G(u(x,t))=\left\{\frac{f(t)}{f(0)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left[G(u(x,0))\right]^{(n)}}{n!}{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)\right\}

Proof:
We start by stating that the multi-variable Hermite polynomials satisfy the following PDE,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)

where the multi-variable Hermite polynomials is given in terms of ordinary Hermite polynomials

\displaystyle{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)=n!\sum_{r=0}^{[n/2]}\frac{H_{(n-2r)}(x)H_{r}(t)}{n(n-2r)!(r)!}

Then, the heat kernel can be expressed in terms of Hermite polynomials given by,

\displaystyle h(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{h^{(n)}(x,0)}{n!}{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)

(80)

where,
h^{(n)}(x,0)
denotes the nth derivative. Thus again by using the transformation function
G
in equation (
75
), we get,

\displaystyle G[u(x,t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{h^{(n)}(x,0)}{n!}{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{f(t)}{f(0)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left[G(u(x,0))\right]^{(n)}}{n!}{}^{(2)}H_{n}(x,t)\right\}

where,
[\quad]^{(n)}
denotes the nth derivative with respect to

IV.2
Proofs of Comparsion principle

Proof:
We begin by defining
v(x,t)
which satisfies PDE (
13
) and
u
in equation (
60
) which is given by,

\displaystyle u_{t}=\frac{1-F^{\prime}(u)}{F(u)}u_{x}^{2}+u_{xx}

(82)

We state the following parabolic inequalities together with a set of conditions :

1.

v(x,t),u(x,t)\in[0,1]\quad\forall x,t\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
where
u(x,t)
satisfies above PDE and
v(x,t)
which satisfies PDE (
13
)

2.

v_{t}-v_{xx}-H(v,v_{x})\geq u_{t}-u_{xx}-H(u,u_{x})
where
H(u,u_{x})=\left(\frac{1-F^{\prime}(u(x,t))}{F(u(x,t))}\right)(u_{x}(x,t))^{2}
and
H(v,v_{x})=\left(\frac{1-F^{\prime}(v(x,t))}{F(v(x,t))}\right)(v_{x}(x,t))^{2}
remain uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
with
T<\infty
.

3.

1\geq v(x,0)\geq u(x,0)\geq 0.

These three conditions then imply

v(x,t)\geq u(x,t)\quad\forall\;(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T],T<\infty

(83)

which is our desired proposition.

To prove the above inequality (
83
) we first need some estimates. We get these estimates by using
Mean Value Theorem
:

\frac{\left(\frac{1-{F^{\prime}(v)}}{F(v)}\right)-\left(\frac{1-{F^{\prime}(u)}}{F(u)}\right)}{(v-u)}\\
=\left[\frac{[F^{\prime}(w)]^{2}-F^{\prime}(w)-F^{\prime\prime}(w)F(w)}{[F(w)]^{2}}\right]

(84)

where,
w
is numerically between
u
and
v
. This equation (
84
) further helps us to estimate,

\displaystyle H(v,v_{x})-H(u,u_{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v_{x}^{2}\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\left(1-F^{\prime}(u)\right)}{F(u)}

(85)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{v_{x}^{2}\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}-\frac{u_{x}^{2}\left(1-F^{\prime}(u)\right)}{F(u)}+\frac{u_{x}^{2}\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(v_{x}^{2}-u_{x}^{2})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}+u_{x}^{2}\left(\frac{1-{F^{\prime}(v)}}{F(v)}-\frac{1-{F^{\prime}(u)}}{F(u)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(v_{x}^{2}-u_{x}^{2})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}

\displaystyle+u_{x}^{2}\left[\frac{[F^{\prime}(w)]^{2}-F^{\prime}(w)-F^{\prime\prime}(w)F(w)}{[F(w)]^{2}}\right](v-u)

Now, for the condition
w>u
, we obtain the estimate given by the following inequality,

H(v,v_{x})-H(u,u_{x})\geq\frac{(v_{x}^{2}-u_{x}^{2})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}+u_{x}^{2}\left[\frac{[F^{\prime}(u)]^{2}-F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime\prime}(u)F(u)}{[F(u)]^{2}}\right](v-u)

(86)

Further, we define a function
\Phi
by,

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=\left[\frac{[F^{\prime}(u)]^{2}-F^{\prime}(u)-F^{\prime\prime}(u)F(u)}{[F(u)]^{2}}\right]

(87)

Then, the inequality (
86
) becomes,

\displaystyle H(v,v_{x})-H(u,u_{x})\geq\frac{(v_{x}^{2}-u_{x}^{2})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}+(v-u)\Phi(u)u_{x}^{2}

Now, we use these growth estimates to check how the difference between
v
and
u
changes with time. We assert that as

this difference
(v-u)\rightarrow 0
as both
v
and
u
tends to the steady state. To prove this statement, we define a new quantity
z=(v-u)e^{-Ct}
, where, the magnitude of
C
is determined by the comparison of the new term in the transformed PDE. We also have asserted that the quantity
\frac{v_{x}(1-F^{\prime}(v))}{F(v)}
remains uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]
with
T<\infty
. Hence, there exists
M_{T}<\infty
, such that,

\sup_{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T]}\left[\frac{v_{x}(1-F^{\prime}(v))}{F(v)},\frac{u_{x}(1-F^{\prime}(u))}{F(u)}\right]=M_{T}

(89)

This yields
C=1+2M_{T}^{2}
which gives
z=(v-u)e^{-(1+2M_{T}^{2})t}
. Further, we also know from the condition 2 asserted above that is given by,

\displaystyle(v-u)_{t}-(v-u)_{xx}\geq H(v,v_{x})-H(u,u_{x})

(90)

Now, let us consider the following PDE in
z=(v-u)e^{-(1+2M_{T}^{2})t}

\displaystyle\Bigg[z_{t}-z_{xx}-z_{x}\left(\frac{(v_{x}+u_{x})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}\right)\Bigg]e^{(1+2M_{T}^{2})t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v-u)_{t}-(1+2M_{T}^{2})(v-u)-(v-u)_{xx}

(91)

\displaystyle-(v-u)_{x}\left[\frac{(v_{x}+u_{x})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}\right]

Further, using the condition 2 stated above and then incorporating equation (
88

\displaystyle\Bigg[z_{t}-z_{xx}-z_{x}\left(\phi_{1}\right)\Bigg]e^{(1+2M_{T}^{2})t}

\displaystyle H(v,v_{x})-H(u,u_{x})

(92)

\displaystyle-(v-u)_{x}\left(\frac{(v_{x}+u_{x})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}\right)-(1+2M_{T}^{2})(v-u)

\displaystyle\frac{(v_{x}^{2}-u_{x}^{2})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}-\frac{(v_{x}^{2}-u_{x}^{2})\left(1-F^{\prime}(v)\right)}{F(v)}

\displaystyle\phi_{2}ze^{(1+2M_{T}^{2})t}

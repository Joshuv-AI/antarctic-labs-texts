Discrete Quantitative Isocapacitary Inequality: Fluctuation Estimates
M. Cicalese, L. Kreutz, I. Mansoor

Abstract
. The classical isocapacitary inequality states that, among all sets of fixed volume, the ball uniquely minimizes the capacity. While this result holds in the continuum, it fails in the discrete setting, where the isocapacitary problem may admit multiple minimizers. In this paper we establish quantitative fluctuation estimates for the discrete isocapacitary problem on subsets of ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} as their cardinality diverges. Our approach relies on a careful extension of the associated variational problem from the discrete to the continuum setting, combined with sharp (continuum) quantitative isocapacitary inequalities.



Keywords:
Isocapacitary inequality, Combinatorial laplacian, Fluctuation estimates,
N^{3/4}
-law

47A75, 49Q20, 49R05

1.
Introduction

Many classical problems in the calculus of variations are concerned with the identification of extremal sets for variational quantities under volume constraints. A paradigmatic example is provided by the isocapacitary inequality, which asserts that among all open and bounded sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
, with prescribed Lebesgue measure, Euclidean balls uniquely minimize the Newtonian capacity. More generally, for
p\in(1,d)
, the same property holds for the
p
-capacity. More precisely, denoting by
\mathrm{cap}_{p}(\Omega)
the
p
-capacity of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
; i.e.,

\mathrm{cap}_{p}(\Omega)=\inf\Bigl\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\colon u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\;u\geq 1\text{ on }\Omega\Bigr\}\,,

then one has

\mathrm{cap}_{p}(\Omega)\geq\mathrm{cap}_{p}(B_{r_{|\Omega|}})\,,

where
B_{r_{|\Omega|}}
denotes the ball with the same volume as
\Omega
centred at the origin. Equality holds if and only if
\Omega
coincides with
B_{r_{|\Omega|}}
up to translations and sets of zero
p
-capacity.

While rigidity of minimizers is a hallmark of the continuum setting, this feature may fail in discrete environments. Understanding how continuum geometric principles manifest themselves on lattices has been a recurring theme in recent years, motivated both by intrinsic mathematical interest and by applications in probability, statistical mechanics, and materials science.

In this paper we study the isocapacitary problem on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, both in the case of the
p
-capacity and of the relative capacity, whose definitions are recalled below. Given a finite set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=N
, we define its discrete
p
-capacity by

(1)

\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}(X):=\inf\Big\{N^{\frac{p-d}{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|i-j|=1\end{subarray}}|u(i)-u(j)|^{p}\;:u:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R},\ \#\mathrm{supp}(u)<\infty,\ u\geq 1\text{ on }X\Big\}\,.

If moreover
X\subset B_{RN^{1/d}}
for some
R>0
, the discrete relative capacity is defined as

\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R):=\min\Big\{N^{\frac{2-d}{d}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|i-j|=1\end{subarray}}|u(i)-u(j)|^{2}\;:u=0\text{ on }\partial_{d}B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}\,,\ u\geq 1\text{ on }X\Big\}\,,

where
\partial_{d}A
is defined in (
4
).
This definition is consistent with the continuum scaling and ensures convergence, as
N\to+\infty
, to the
p
-capacity of the limiting continuum set under suitable embeddings (see Remark
3.1
). The latter is a consequence of the discrete-to-continuum convergence of the underlying variational problem that can be obtained in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence (we refer the reader to
[
2
]
for an introduction to discrete-to-contiuum variational problems). In analogy with other discrete variational problems on lattices, minimizers of
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}
(a similar observation holds for
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(\cdot,R)
) are not unique. More precisely, for fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
there may exist distinct subsets
X,Y\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=\#Y=N
minimizing
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}
and such that
X
and
Y
cannot be mapped onto each other by any discrete isometry of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. As customary in lattice optimization problems, this lack of rigidity naturally leads to the study of fluctuation estimates, which quantify how far different minimizers (or almost minimizers) can be from each other (for instance in terms of the cardinality of their symmetric difference).
We refer the readers to some recent results on this rigidity issue for the edge-like isoperimetric problem
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
20
,
24
]
, also in the case of short-range attractive-repulsive interaction
[
15
,
16
]
, the Winterbottom problem
[
17
]
or for the first eigenvalue of the combinatorial laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
6
]
.

The main goal of this work is to establish sharp fluctuation estimates for the discrete relative and
p
-isocapacitary problem on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Roughly speaking, we show that minimizers and almost minimizers of
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}
(or of
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(\cdot,R)
) are close, up to translations, to discrete balls of radius of order
N^{1/d}
, and that the deviation, defined in terms of the cardinality of the symmetric difference, is controlled by a power law in
N
. In the case of the
p
-capacity, in its simplest form, our main result implies that if
X
and
Y
are minimizers of (
1
), then there exists a constant
C_{d,p}>0
such that

(2)

\#(X\Delta Y)\leq C_{d,p}\,N^{1-\frac{1}{2d}}\,.

The latter inequality is a consequence of a more refined estimate obtained for almost minimizers of the capacity problem in Theorem
4.1
. According to this theorem, a set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
whose
p
-capacity (a similar result holds true for the relative capacity) differs from the minimal one by
\alpha_{N}
, where
\sup\alpha_{N}<+\infty
, satisfies

(3)

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\#(X\Delta(z+B_{r_{N}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}))\leq C_{d,p}N\left(\alpha_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2d}}P_{N}(X)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,.

Here
P_{N}(X)=N^{\frac{1-d}{d}}P(X)
is a scaled version of the edge perimeter of
X
defined as

P(X)=\sum_{i\in X}\#\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X\,\colon\,|i-j|=1\}\,.

In particular, if
\sup P_{N}(X)<+\infty
, the estimate above gives

\inf_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\#(X\Delta(z+B_{r_{N}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}))\leq C_{d,p}N\left(\alpha_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2d}}\right)\,,

which implies (
2
) by the triangle inequality in the case of minimizers, that is when
\alpha_{N}=0
. The fact that minimizers of the discrete isocapacity problem have uniformly bounded scaled perimeter is not trivial and requires additional arguments. In Section
5
, using discrete rearrangement and slicing techniques, we show that minimizers of the discrete isocapacitary problem satisfy a diameter bound of order
N^{1/d}
(Proposition
5.15
), which in turn implies uniform control of the scaled perimeter
P_{N}
(Proposition
5.4
). Note that (
2
) shows that the fluctuation estimates for exact minimizers of the discrete isocapacitary problem exhibits the same fluctuation exponent as the discrete isoperimetric problem and the discrete Faber–Krahn inequality (see
[
6
,
7
]
).

For what instead concern the proof of the fluctuation estimate (
3
), our strategy closely follows the discrete-to-continuum approach first outlined for the edge isperimetric problem in
[
7
]
and is based on two main ingredients. First, we embed a discrete configuration

X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
into a continuum set

\zeta(X)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

so that the Fraenkel asymmetry of

\zeta(X)
controls the deviation of

X
, measured in cardinality, from a discretized Euclidean ball, with explicit error bounds in
N
. Second, we show that the discrete
p
-capacity
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}(X)
is well approximated by the continuum
p
-capacity of
\zeta(X)
, again up to explicit error bounds in
N
. The quantitative backbone of our analysis is provided by the sharp quantitative isocapacitary inequalities in the continuum setting, proved in
[
11
]
for the Newtonian capacity and extended to the
p
-capacity setting in
[
22
]
.

We conclude this introduction mentioning that it is our opinion that our result can be of help in the analysis of several probabilistic models with long-range correlations, such as random interlacements (see
[
12
]
). Indeed, there the capacity naturally appears as the energetic cost governing the probability of creating macroscopic vacant regions, and hence determines the geometry of rare events and bottlenecks, for instance in first-passage percolation problems. This fact highlights the relevance of quantitative stability results for discrete isocapacitary problems in providing deterministic control on the geometry and fluctuations of near-optimal configurations.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the necessary notation and preliminaries. In Section
3
we introduce the notion of discrete relative and
p
-capacity and we state the main properties of the capacitary potentials. In Section
4
we state and prove the main result of the paper, namely the quantitative fluctuation results for the capacity. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to establishing several geometric properties of optimal sets. These properties, proved using discrete rearrangement techniques, are required to complete the proof of the main theorem and include diameter and perimeter estimates.

2.1.
Kuhn decomposition

In what follows we will make use of the Kuhn decomposition whose definition we recall below for the reader’s convenience.
The set
[0,1]^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be decomposed in
d!
simplexes
(T_{\pi})_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{d}
is the set of permutations of
\{1,\ldots,d\}
and for
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{d},

T_{\pi}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\colon\,0\leq x_{\pi(d)}\leq x_{\pi(d-1)}\leq\cdots\leq x_{\pi(1)}\leq 1\}\,.

Note that if
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
,
\mathring{T}_{\pi}\cap\mathring{T}_{\pi^{\prime}}=\emptyset
if
\pi\neq\pi^{\prime}
. Given
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{d}
, we set

T_{\pi}(z)=z+T_{\pi}\,.

The Kuhn decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can then be defined as

\mathcal{T}=\Big\{T_{\pi}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,\,\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\Big\}\,.

2.2.
Discrete Dirichlet and perimeter functionals

In this section, given a discrete set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and a function
u\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, we define several discrete functionals associated with
X
and
u
, that will be considered in the rest of the paper. Along with them, we also define their scaled version, corresponding to the energy per particle, thus highlighting their dependence on the cardinality of
X
. Given
p\in(1,d)
and
u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the discrete
p
-Dirichlet energy of
u
as

E_{p}(u)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{N}}|u(i)-u(j)|^{p}\,.

Given
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=N
and
u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset X
, we define the scaled
p
-discrete Dirichlet energy of
u
in
X
as

E_{p,N}(u)=N^{\frac{p-d}{d}}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{N}}|u(i)-u(j)|^{p}\,.

When
p=2
we may simply write
E(u)
(resp.
E_{N}(u)
) in place of
E_{2}(u)
(resp.
E_{2,N}(u)
).

Given
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=N
, the perimeter of
X
is defined as

P(X)=\sum_{i\in X}\#\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X\,\colon\,|i-j|=1\}\,,

and its scaled version is defined as

P_{N}(X)=N^{\frac{1-d}{d}}P(X)\,.

Note that there exists a dimensional constant
C_{d}>0
such that
C_{d}\leq P_{N}(X)\leq 2dN^{\frac{1}{d}}
.

3.
Discrete capacity

This section contains the main definition and basic properties of the absolute and relative discrete capacity and of their capacitary potential.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be such that
\#X=N
. Given
p\in(1,d)
the absolute
p
-capacity of
X
is defined as

\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)=\inf\Big\{E_{p,N}(u)\,\colon\,u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\,\#\mathrm{supp}(u)<+\infty\,,\,u(i)\geq 1\textrm{ if }i\in X\Big\}\,.

When
p=2
we may write
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X)
instead of
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)
. In order to define the relative capacity we introduce the discrete boundary of
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{d}A:=\{\{i,j\}\colon i\in A\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A\text{ and }|i-j|=1\}\,.

Given
R>0
and
X\subset B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the relative capacity of
X
in
B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is defined as

\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)=\inf\Big\{E_{N}(u)\,\colon\,u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\,u=0\text{ on }\partial_{d}B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}\,,\,u(i)\geq 1\textrm{ if }i\in X\Big\}\,.

We set

\displaystyle m_{p,N}=\underset{\#X=N}{\inf_{X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{N}(R)

\displaystyle=\underset{\#X=N}{\inf_{X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)\,,

where, as before, we write
m_{N}
in place of
m_{2,N}
. A set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=N
is called optimal, or minimizing for the absolute
p
-isocapacitary problem if
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)=m_{p,N}
and similarly for the relative
2
-isocapacitary problem if
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)=m_{N}(R)
.

Given a set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a function
u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is called the capacitary potential of
X
(for the
p
-capacity or the relative capacity) if it satisfies

(1)

For the
p
-capacity:

(1.1)

u\in\ell^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
;  (1.2)
u(i)=1\quad\forall i\in X
;  (1.3)
E_{N}(u)=\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)
.

(2)

For the relative capacity:

(2.1)

\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
;  (2.2)
u(i)=1\quad\forall i\in X
;  (2.3)
E_{N}(u)=\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)
.

Remark 3.1
.

The scaling in the definition of the energy functionals
E_{p,N}
is consistent with the energy functional used in the continuum definition of capacity thanks to the following observations. First, given
u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the piecewise constant interpolation of
u
denoted by
\overline{u}_{N}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and defined as

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{N}(x)=u\left(N^{\frac{1}{d}}z\right)\quad\text{for }x\in Q_{N^{-\frac{1}{d}}}(z)\textrm{ and }z\in N^{-\frac{1}{d}}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is open and bounded and
p\in(1,d)
, we set (with a slight abuse of notation)

E_{p,N}(v,\Omega)=\begin{cases}E_{p,N}(u)&\text{if }v\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,\,v=\overline{u}_{N}\textrm{ with }u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\textrm{ and }u\geq 1\textrm{ on }\Omega\,,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We observe that as a consequence of
[
3
]
, setting
K^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\{u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\nabla u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
, it holds that

\Gamma(L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\text{-}\lim_{N\to+\infty}E_{p,N}(v,\Omega)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla v|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x&\textrm{if }v\in K^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\textrm{ and }v\geq 1\textrm{ on }\Omega\,,\\
+\infty&\textrm{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

Therefore, thanks to the coercivity properties of
E_{p,N}
and the fundamental theorem of
\Gamma
-convergence
[
4
,
8
]
, for any
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
open and
\Omega_{N}=N^{\frac{1}{d}}\Omega\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}(\Omega_{N})\to\mathrm{cap}_{p}(\Omega)
as
N\to\infty
, where

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{p}(\Omega)=\min\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\colon u\in K^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,u\geq 1\text{ on }\Omega\right\}\,.

A similar result holds in the case of the relative capacity, where the additional requirement
\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}
implies that
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(\Omega,R)\to\mathrm{cap}(\Omega,R)
as
N\to\infty
, where

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}(\Omega,R)=\min\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\colon u\in H^{1}_{0}(B_{R})\,,u\geq 1\text{ on }\Omega\right\}\,.

Note also that if
P_{N}
is extended to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

P_{N}(v)=\begin{cases}P_{N}(X)\,\textrm{ if }v=\mathbbm{1}_{X}\textrm{ with }\#X=N\,,\\
+\infty\quad\,\,\textrm{ else},\end{cases}

then, as stated in
[
1
,
6
]
, it holds that

\Gamma(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))~\text{-}\lim_{N\to+\infty}P_{N}(v)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\partial^{*}E}\|\nu_{E}\|_{1}\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\,\,\,\textrm{if }v=\mathbbm{1}_{E}\in BV(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\|v\|_{1}=1\,,\\
+\infty\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\textrm{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of
E
, and
\nu_{E}
is its measure theoretic unit normal.

3.1.
Properties of the capacitary potential

Here we provide the main properties of the capacitary potential useful in the sequel of the paper.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
23
, Theorem 1]

Let
p\in[1,d)
and
u\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
There exists a constant
C_{p,d}>0
such that

\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u(i)|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\leq C_{p,d}\left(\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{N}}|u(i)-u(j)|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\#X=N
and
p\in(1,d)
.

(1)

Let
K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
be the vector space

K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})=\Big\{u\in\ell^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\colon E_{p}(u)<+\infty\Big\}\,,

the set of functions in
\ell^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with finite
p
-Dirichlet energy. Equipped with the norm
|||u|||_{K^{p}}=\|u\|_{\ell^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}+E_{p}(u)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, the set
K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is a Banach space. Thanks to Theorem
3.2
the latter norm is equivalent to the norm
\|u\|_{K^{p}}=E_{p}(u)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. The space
K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
endowed with such a norm is a reflexive Banach space (and a Hilbert space for
p=2
) .

(2)

We observe that the set

C_{X}=\{u\in K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\colon u(i)=1\text{ if }i\in X\}\,,

of all admissible functions in the definition of
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)
, is the closure (w.r.t.
{\|\cdot\|_{K^{p}}}
) of

C_{X}^{comp}=\{u\in K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\colon\ u(i)=1\text{ if }i\in X\textrm{ and }\#\mathrm{supp}(u)<+\infty\}\,.

Proposition 3.4
.

Given
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\#X=N
. Then the following holds true.

(1)

If
X
is such that
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)<+\infty
. Then, there exists a unique capacitary potential
u\in K^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
such that
\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}
;

(2)

If
p\in(1,d)
and
X
is such that
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)<+\infty
. Then, there exists a unique capacitary potential
u\in K^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Proof.

First, we note the following convexity property of
E_{p,N}
. Given
u,v\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
admissible and
t\in(0,1):

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}E_{p,N}(tu+(1-t)v)=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|i-j|=1\end{subarray}}\big|t(u(i)-u(j))+(1-t)(v(i)-v(j))|^{p}\\
\leq&~t\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|i-j|=1\end{subarray}}|u(i)-u(j)|^{p}+\,\,\,(1-t)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|i-j|=1\end{subarray}}|v(i)-v(j)|^{p}\,,\end{split}

with equality only if
u(i)-u(j)=v(i)-v(j)\quad\forall\,(i,j)\in\mathcal{N}
. Now we prove (1). Since
u\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}
,
the existence of the capacitary potential follows as the minimum problem defining the relative capacity reduces to minimizing a continuous function over a finite dimensional set. Concerning uniqueness, we note that the set

\Big\{u\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\colon u\geq 1\text{ on }X\text{ and }\mathrm{supp}(u)\subset B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}\Big\}

is convex. The characterization of the equality case in (
5
) together with the fact that
v=u=0
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus B_{RN^{\frac{1}{d}}}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is path-connected through paths of nearest-neighbors, implies that
E_{N}
is strictly convex.

Next, we prove (2). The existence of the capacitary potential follows from the reflexivity of
K^{p}
and the lower semicontinuity of
E_{p,N}
. Again, the uniqueness follows from the strict convexity of
E_{p,N}
on
C_{X}
due to (
5
) and the fact that

Definition 3.5
.

Let

. We say that a function

We simply say that

-harmonic at

In the following proposition we collect some well known necessary conditions for minimality (see

in the continuum case).

Proposition 3.6

Let

and
\#X=N
and

its capacitary potential.

(1)

\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:0\leq u(i)\leq 1

(2)

\forall i\in X:u(i)=1

(3.1)

If

(3.2)

is

-harmonic at

Property (1) is a consequence of the fact that

is decreasing under truncation and, if

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

This estimate, together with Lemma

\displaystyle\#(X\Delta(z^{\prime}+B_{r_{N}}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}))\leq C_{d,p}N\left(\alpha_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}+N^{-\frac{1}{2d}}P_{N}(X)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\,,

which concludes the proof for the

5.
Discrete rearrangements and structure of optimal sets

The main goal of this section is to obtain a uniform perimeter estimate for the optimal sets of the capacity problem (see Propostion
5.4
for the precise statement). The proof uses some properties of discrete rearrangements that we introduce below.

5.1.
Discrete rearrengements

In this section we introduce the definition of discrete (Schwarz-like) rearrangement. We first introduce the notion for functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}
and then extend it to functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see
[
18
,
19
,
25
]
).

Definition 5.1

(Rearrangement of functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}
)
.

Let
u\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
. Its symmetrization
u^{*}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
is the unique function obtained from
u
by permuting its values and that satisfies for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
the following property:

u^{*}(k)\geq u^{*}(-k)\geq u^{*}(k+1)\,.

In order to extend the definition to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we introduce the set of rearrangement directions

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\left\{e_{i}\colon i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\right\}\cup\left\{e_{i}\pm e_{j}\colon i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,i\neq j\right\}\,.

Given
\xi\in\mathcal{B}
we define

\displaystyle\Pi_{\xi}=\begin{cases}\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\xi\cdot\alpha=0\}&\text{if }\xi=e_{j}\text{ for some }j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,\\
\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\xi\cdot\alpha=0\}\cup\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\xi\cdot\alpha=1\}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If
\xi\notin\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}\}
we additionally set
\Pi_{\xi}^{0}=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon\xi\cdot\alpha=0\}
.
Given
\xi\in\mathcal{B}
and
\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}
we first define the function
u^{\alpha,\xi}\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(35)

\displaystyle u^{\alpha,\xi}(t)=u(\alpha+t\xi)\,.

Definition 5.2

(Rearrangement of functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
)
.

Let
u\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z^{d}})
. The symmetrization of
u
with respect to
\xi
is denoted by
u^{*\xi}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and it is defined as

\displaystyle u^{*\xi}(i)=\begin{cases}(u^{\alpha,\xi})^{*}(t)&\text{if }i=\alpha+t\xi\,,\xi\cdot\alpha=0\text{ for some }\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}\text{ and }t\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\\
\mathrm{R}(u^{\alpha,\xi})^{*}(t)&\text{if }i=\alpha+t\xi\,,\xi\cdot\alpha=1\text{ for some }\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}\text{ and }t\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\end{cases}

where we denote by
\mathrm{R}u(i)=u(-i)
the reflection of
u
with respect to the origin.

For
u\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
we define the iterative rearrangement of
u
with respect to
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
as

\displaystyle u^{*(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}=\big(u^{*\xi_{1}}\big)^{*\xi_{2}}\,.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
we set
u^{*(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})}=\left(u^{*(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})}\right)^{*\xi_{n}}
.

In the next Section
5.3
we are going to prove that the rearrangement decreases the energy and to give necessary conditions for the equality case. To this end, we start in
5.2
by rewriting the energy as sum of energies on one-dimensional slices, distinguishing between slices in coordinate directions and in diagonal directions.

5.2.
Energy decomposition

Let
p>1
and
u,v\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
. We introduce the following notation:

(36)

\displaystyle E_{p}^{1\mathrm{d}}(u,v)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(i)-v(i)|^{p}

and

(37)

\displaystyle E_{p}^{1\mathrm{d}}(u)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(i+1)-u(i)|^{p}\,.

Furthermore we set

(38)

\displaystyle E_{p,\mathrm{diag}}^{1\mathrm{d}}(u,v)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(i)-v(i)|^{p}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(i+1)-v(i)|^{p}\,.

With the above notation the following useful lemma holds true.

Lemma 5.3
.

(Energy decomposition) Let
u\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\xi\in\mathcal{B}
. Then the following statements are true:

(1)

If
\xi=e_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, then

\displaystyle E_{p}(u)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,\xi})+\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{k},\xi})\,.

(2)

If
\xi=e_{j}\pm e_{l}
for
j,l\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that
j\neq l
, then

\displaystyle E_{p}(u)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}^{0}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p,\mathrm{diag}}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{j},\xi})+\underset{k\notin\{j,l\}}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}^{0}}\left(E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{k},\xi})+E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha+e_{j},\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{j}+e_{k},\xi})\right)\,.

Proof.

We prove the two cases separately.

Step 1:

Proof of
(1)
:

Assume that
\xi=e_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Observe that, by Fubini’s Theorem, (
35
)–(
37
), we have

\displaystyle E_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u(i+e_{k})-u(i)|^{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{e_{j}}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(\alpha+te_{j}+e_{k})-u(\alpha+te_{j})|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{e_{j}}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|u^{\alpha,e_{j}}(t+1)-u^{\alpha,e_{j}}(t)|^{p}+\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{e_{j}}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|u^{\alpha+e_{k},e_{j}}(t)-u^{\alpha,e_{j}}(t)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{e_{j}}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,e_{j}})+\underset{k\neq j}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{e_{j}}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,e_{j}},u^{\alpha+e_{k},e_{j}})\,.

This concludes the proof of (1).

Step 2:

Proof of
(2)
:
Assume that
\xi=e_{j}+e_{l}
for some
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
such that
j\neq l
. Again observe that, by Fubini’s Theorem, (
35
),(
37
) and , we have

\displaystyle E_{p}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u(i+e_{k})-u(i)|^{p}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(\alpha+t\xi+e_{k})-u(\alpha+t\xi)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\{j,l\}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|u(\alpha+t\xi+e_{k})-u(\alpha+t\xi)|^{p}+\underset{k\notin\{j,l\}}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|u^{\alpha+e_{k},\xi}(t)-u^{\alpha,\xi}(t)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}^{0}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p,\mathrm{diag}}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{j},\xi})+\underset{k\notin\{j,l\}}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{k},\xi})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}^{0}}E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p,\mathrm{diag}}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{j},\xi})+\underset{k\notin\{j,l\}}{\sum_{k=1}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{\xi}^{0}}\left(E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha,\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{k},\xi})+E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{\alpha+e_{j},\xi},u^{\alpha+e_{j}+e_{k},\xi})\right)\,.

The proof of the case of
\xi=e_{j}-e_{l}
follows along the same lines. This concludes the proof of (2).
∎

5.3.
The perimeter bound

In what follows we provide the statement of the uniform perimeter bound for optimal sets of the isocapacitary problem (Proposition (
5.4
)). The section contains the main energy estimates and geometric properties of the equilibrium (capacitary) potential necessary for the proof, which is postponed to the next Section (

Proposition 5.4
.

Let

(1)

For the

\text{if }\quad\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)=m_{p,N}\,,\quad\text{then}\quad P_{N}(X)\leq C_{d,p}

for some constant

(2)

For the relative capacity:

\text{if }\quad\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)=m_{N}(R)\,,\quad\text{then}\quad P_{N}(X)\leq C_{d,R}

for some constant
C_{d,R}>0

In order to prove the previous proposition we make use of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
p>1
and

(39)

\displaystyle E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(\mathrm{R}u^{*})=E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u^{*})\leq E^{1\mathrm{d}}_{p}(u)\,.

is a decreasing function about its maximum. In particular, for all

5.4.
Diameter estimate of optimal sets

The main goal of this section is to prove an asymptotic diameter bound (with uniform constant) for optimal sets in the isocapacitary problem and for minimizers of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the combinatorial Laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
To this end we recall that the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the combinatorial Laplacian of a set
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=N
is defined as

\displaystyle\lambda_{N}(X)=\min\Big\{E_{2}(u)\,\colon\,u(i)=0\text{ on }\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X\,,\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u(i)|^{2}=1\Big\}\,.

We say that
X
is an optimal set if

\displaystyle\lambda_{N}(X)=m_{\lambda,N}\,,\quad\text{ where}\quad m_{\lambda,N}

\displaystyle=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\#X=N\end{subarray}}\lambda_{N}(X)\,.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
p>1
and let
X\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\#X=N
. There exists
C_{d}>0
such that

(i)

p
-capacity: if
\mathrm{cap}_{p,N}\left(X\right)=m_{p,N}
, then
\mathrm{diam}(X)\leq C_{d}N^{1/d}
,

(ii)

relative capacity: if
\mathrm{cap}_{N}(X,R)=m_{N}(R)
, then
\mathrm{diam}(X)\leq C_{d}N^{1/d}
,

(iii)

first eigenvalue of the Laplacian: if
\lambda_{N}(X)=m_{\lambda,N}
, then
\mathrm{diam}(X)\leq C_{d}N^{1/d}

We postpone the proof of Proposition
5.15
to the end of this section and instead show here that it implies Proposition
5.4
.

Proof of Proposition
5.4
.

We prove only (1), as the proof of (2) is analogous. We first observe that

(57)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{P}(X)&=\sum_{i\in X}\#\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X\colon|i-j|=1\}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathrm{1}_{X}(i+e_{k})-\mathrm{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X}(i)|\\
&=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{e_{k}}}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathrm{1}_{X}(\alpha+(t+1)e_{k})-\mathrm{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X}(\alpha+te_{k})|\,.\end{split}

Thanks to Proposition
5.14

is convex in direction

for all

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathrm{1}_{X}(\alpha+(t+1)e_{k})-\mathrm{1}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus X}(\alpha+te_{k})|=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }X^{\alpha,e_{k}}\neq\emptyset\,,\\

Counting Irreducible Representations of a Finite Abelian Group
Thomas Breuer, Prashun Kumar, Geetha Venkataraman

Abstract
Let q q be a power of a prime p p , G G be a finite abelian group, where p ∤ | G | p\nmid|G| , and let n n be a positive integer. In this paper we find a formula for the number of irreducible representations of G G of a given dimension n n over the field of order q q , up to equivalence, using Brauer characters. We also provide a formula for such n n using the prime decomposition of the exponent of G G and an algorithm to compute the irreducible degrees and their multiplicities.



Keywords
:
abelian groups, Brauer characters, irreducible representations, irreducible modules, epimorphisms.

20H30, 20K01, 20K27, 20K30.

Introduction

In this paper we consider irreducible representations of a finite abelian group

G
over a finite field in characteristic coprime to
|G|
.
Several papers and books deal with counting irreducible (modular)
representations of a group
G
over a given field,
for example see
[
1
,
3
,
6
]
.
However, these do not provide a closed formula or give an explicit count.
Neither do they provide a formula of the possible degrees
of these irreducible representations.

Let
G
and
H
be abelian groups of order
n
.
In
[
2
]
, the authors find all fields
{{\mathbb{F}}}
such that

{{\mathbb{F}}}G\cong{{\mathbb{F}}}H
as group algebras.
In the same paper they also show that

{{\mathbb{F}}}G=\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}a_{d}{{\mathbb{F}}}(\zeta_{d})

where
G
is an abelian group of order
n
and
{{\mathbb{F}}}
is a field
whose characteristic does not divide
n
.
Here
\zeta_{d}
is a primitive
d
-th root of unity and
a_{d}
is a
non-negative integer.
While every irreducible
{{\mathbb{F}}}G
-module occurs in the summation,
an explicit formula for the number of irreducible
{{\mathbb{F}}}G
-modules
up to isomorphism of a given dimension is not provided
nor is a formula for the
m
that can appear as the degree of an
irreducible
{{\mathbb{F}}}G
-module.
Let
p
and
r
be distinct primes.
A formula for the number of irreducible representations of an
elementary abelian
r
-group up to equivalence over a finite field
of order
p
has been given in
[
5
]
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and let
{{\mathbb{F}}}
be a finite field such that
{\rm char}

{{\mathbb{F}}}\nmid|G|
. The objective of this paper is to provide a formula for the number of irreducible representations of
G
over
{{\mathbb{F}}}
of a given degree
n
up to equivalence and to give an explicit formula for the total number of irreducible representations of
G
over
{{\mathbb{F}}}
up to equivalence. We use the theory of Brauer characters to establish this. We refer to
[
6

for an introduction to the theory of Brauer characters. Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of all representations of a finite group
G
having an abelian image and the set of all representations of
G/G^{\prime}
, our formula also gives the number of irreducible representations of a finite group
G
having an abelian image.

We find all the degrees of the irreducible representations of
G
over
{{\mathbb{F}}}
and use it to find the formula for the total number of irreducible representations of
G
over
{{\mathbb{F}}}

2
Irreducible representations of a finite abelian group

Let
G
be a finite abelian group,
and let
{\rm Irr}(G)
denote the absolutely irreducible characters of
G

over the complex numbers.
Each
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
has degree
1
,
and all values of
\chi
are complex roots of unity.
Thus the character field
{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\chi)={{\mathbb{Q}}}(\{\chi(g);g\in G\})

is a cyclotomic field
{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\zeta_{d})
,
where
\zeta_{d}
is a primitive
d
-th complex root of unity.
For each positive integer
d
,
let
{\rm I}_{d}(G)=\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G);{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d})\}
.

By the identification of the (multiplicative) group
\langle\zeta_{d}\rangle

with the (additive) group
{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}
of residues modulo
d
,
we can interpret
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
as the set of epimorphisms from
G
to
{\mathbb{Z}}/d{\mathbb{Z}}
.

The multiplicative order of
q
modulo
d
,
for two integers
q
and
d
, is the smallest positive integer
n

such that
d
divides
(q^{n}-1)
; it is denoted by
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)
.
In particular, we have
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod 1)=1
for all
q
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group of exponent
e
,
and let
q
be a prime power coprime to
|G|
.

Each irreducible representation of
G
over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

with
q
elements has degree
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)
,
for some divisor
d
of
e
.

The number of irreducible representations of
G
of degree
n

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\in D_{n}}|{\rm I}_{d}(G)|,

where

D_{n}=\{d\mid e;\ {\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)=n,\ \textrm{$d$ even if $e$ is even}\}
.

Proof.

The set
{\rm Irr}(G)
is the disjoint union of the sets
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
,
where
d
runs over all divisors of
e
if
e
is odd,
and over all even divisors of
e
otherwise.
(Note that
\zeta_{2}=-1\in{\mathbb{Q}}
holds,
hence we have
{\rm I}_{d}(G)={\rm I}_{2d}(G)
if
d
is odd.)

Let
p
be a prime not dividing
|G|
.
We identify
{\rm Irr}(G)
with the set
{\rm Irr}_{p}(G)
of absolutely irreducible
Brauer characters of
G
in characteristic
p
,
and identify our sets
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
with the corresponding subsets of
{\rm Irr}_{p}(G)
.

We show that for the Brauer character
\chi\in{\rm I}_{d}(G)
,
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-irreducible character that has
\chi
as a constituent
has degree
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)
.

Let
\chi
be a
p
-modular Brauer character in
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
.
It can be realized by a representation over a field with
p^{m}
elements
if this field contains a primitive
d
-th root of unity,
that is, if
d
divides
p^{m}-1
.
The minimal field in characteristic
p
over which
\chi
can be
realized is given by
m={\mathrm{ord}}(p\bmod d)
.

Let
q=p^{k}
, for some positive integer
k
.
The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-irreducible character that has
\chi
as a constituent
arises as an orbit sum under the action of the Galois group of the field
extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}
, where
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{t}}={\mathbb{F}}_{p^{k}}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{m}}
,
that is,
t=\gcd(k,m)
.
This implies that the orbit of
\chi
has length
m/t={\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)
.
Note that the Galois action is semiregular,
and that
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
is closed under this action:
A generator of the Galois group is the map

\ast q:{\rm I}_{d}\rightarrow{\rm I}_{d},\chi\mapsto\chi^{q}
,
where
\chi^{q}(g)=\chi(g)^{q}
.

Hence
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
contributes
|{\rm I}_{d}(G)|/{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)
characters
of degree
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)
to the set of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-irreducibles.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Using the factorization of the exponent
e
into prime powers,
we can interpret Theorem
2.1
as follows.
Let
P
be the set of prime divisors of
|G|
.
Since

{\mathrm{ord}}\left(q\bmod\prod_{r\in P}r^{i_{r}}\right)=\lcm\left\{{\mathrm{ord}}\left(q\bmod r^{i_{r}}\right),r\in P\right\},

the degrees that occur are lcm’s of values
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod r^{i_{r}})
,
for
r\in P
.
In particular, the
r^{\prime}
-part of each
{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod r^{i_{r}})
is given by

{\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod r)
.

Remark 2.3
.

It remains to compute the cardinalities
|{\rm I}_{d}|
.
For that, we write
G=\bigoplus_{r\in P}G_{r}

where
P
is the set of prime divisors of
|G|

and
G_{r}
is the Sylow
r
-subgroup of
G
.
We assume that
G_{r}
has the structure

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}/r^{a(r,j)}{\mathbb{Z}}
,
where
{\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes a cyclic group of order
m

and the
a(r,j)
are positive integers,

1\leq a(r,1)\leq a(r,2)\leq\cdots\leq a(r,n_{r})
.
Let
e
denote the exponent of
G
.
Then we have
e=\prod_{r\in P}r^{a(r,n_{r})}
.

Mapping
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
to the vector
(\chi_{G_{r}};r\in P)

of restrictions to the Sylow
r
-subgroups defines a bijection
from
{\rm Irr}(G)
to the Cartesian product of
{\rm Irr}(G_{r})
, for
r\in P
.
For
\chi\in{\rm I}_{d}(G)
,
the restriction
\chi_{G_{r}}
has character field
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{d_{r}})
,
where
d_{r}
is the
r
-part of
d
.
Thus we get
|{\rm I}_{d}(G)|=\prod_{r\in P}|{\rm I}_{d_{r}}(G_{r})|
.
Since it is much easier to deal with the
r
-groups
G_{r}
than with
G
,
we compute the cardinalities
|{\rm I}_{d_{r}}(G_{r})|
.

For nonnegative
l
such that
r^{l}
divides the exponent of
G_{r}
,
let
N(G_{r},l)=\{g^{r^{l}};g\in G_{r}\}
be the kernel of the epimorphism
from
G_{r}
to its largest factor group of exponent
r^{l}
.
In particular
N(G_{r},0)=G_{r}
.
The factor group
F(G_{r},l)=G_{r}/N(G_{r},l)
has the structure

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r_{p}}{\mathbb{Z}}/r^{\min\{l,a(r,j)\}}{\mathbb{Z}}
.
For
l\geq 1
, we have

{\rm I}_{r^{l}}(G_{r})=\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G_{r});N(G_{r},l)\subseteq\ker(\chi),N(G_{r},l-1)\not\subseteq\ker(\chi)\},

except that

{\rm I}_{2}(G_{2})=\{\chi\in{\rm Irr}(G_{r});N(G_{2},1)\subseteq\ker(\chi)\}
.
Note that for
\chi\in{\rm I}_{r^{l}}(G_{r})
,
we have
\chi(g^{r^{l}})=\chi(g)^{r^{l}}=1
for all
g\in G_{r}
,
and there is an element
g\in G_{r}
such that
\chi(g)=\zeta_{r^{l}}
,
thus
\chi(g^{r^{l-1}})\not=1
.
This implies

|{\rm I}_{r^{l}}(G_{r})|=|F(G_{r},l)|-|F(G_{r},l-1)|=\prod_{j=1}^{n_{r}}r^{\min\{l,a(r,j)\}}-\prod_{j=1}^{n_{r}}r^{\min\{l-1,a(r,j)\}},

except that
|{\rm I}_{2}(G_{2})|=2^{n_{2}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The fact that the sets
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
are nonempty for all divisors
d
of

\exp(G)
implies that any two finite abelian groups of the same exponent
have the exact same list of integers that are degrees of
irreducible representations of these groups over a given finite field
whose characteristic is coprime to the common exponent.
The group structure does not matter.
However when we count the number of irreducible representations for each,
of a given possible degree
m
,
then the cardinality of
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
and hence the structure of the group
comes into play.

Remark 2.5
.

The above statements yield the following algorithm
for computing the irreducible degrees and their multiplicities.

The
input
consists of
a power
q
of a prime
p

and the description of an abelian group
G

by a set
P
of primes and the
a(r,i)
, as defined above,
where
p
is not in
P
.

The
output
is the sequence

(n_{1},k_{1}),(n_{2},k_{2}),\ldots,(n_{r},k_{r})
,
such that the degrees of the irreducible representations of
G

over the field with
q
elements are
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}
,
and their multiplicities are
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}
, respectively.

We proceed as follows.

1.

Compute the exponent
e
of
G
as
e=\prod_{r\in P}a(r,n_{r})
.

2.

For
r\in P
,
precompute the cardinalities
|{\rm I}_{r^{i}}(G_{r})|
,
1\leq i\leq a(r,n_{r})
.

3.

For the divisors
d
of
e
(or only the even divisors if
2\in P
),
compute the orbit length
n={\mathrm{ord}}(q\bmod d)

of the Galois group in question on
{\rm I}_{d}(G)
,
compute
|{\rm I}_{d}(G)|
from the involved
|{\rm I}_{d_{r}}(G_{r})|
,
and record
|{\rm I}_{d}(G)|/n
irreducibles of degree
n
.

4.

For each character degree
n
that occurs,
sum up the multiplicities of
n
arising for different divisors
d
.
Output the degrees and their total multiplicities.

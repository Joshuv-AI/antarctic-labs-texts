Large-degree asymptotic expansions for the Jacobi and related functions
Gergő Nemes

Abstract
. Simple asymptotic expansions for the Jacobi functions P ν ( α , β ) ​ ( z ) P_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z) and Q ν ( α , β ) ​ ( z ) Q_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z) for large degree ν \nu , with fixed parameters α \alpha and β \beta , are surprisingly rare in the literature, with only a few special cases covered. This paper addresses this notable gap by deriving simple (inverse) factorial expansions for these functions, complemented by explicit and computable error bounds. Additionally, we provide analogous results for the associated functions Q ν ( α , β ) ​ ( x ) \mathrm{Q}_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) and 𝖰 ν ( α , β ) ​ ( x ) \mathsf{Q}_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x) .

Introduction and main result

In this paper, we derive asymptotic expansions for the Jacobi functions
P_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z)
and
Q_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z)
, as well as for the associated functions
\mathrm{Q}_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
and
\mathsf{Q}_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
, in the regime where the degree
\nu
becomes large while
\alpha
and
\beta
are held fixed. These functions are solutions to the Jacobi differential equation

(1.1)

(1-z^{2})\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}w(z)}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}+(\beta-\alpha-z(\alpha+\beta+2))\frac{\mathrm{d}w(z)}{\mathrm{d}z}+\nu(\nu+\alpha+\beta+1)w(z)=0,

which is characterised by regular singularities at
z=\pm 1
and
z=\infty
.

In recent decades, significant effort has been dedicated to deriving asymptotic expansions for these functions. Generally, there are two types of expansions: those in terms of elementary functions and uniform expansions involving Bessel functions (a brief summary is provided later in this section). A common feature of these results is the complexity of their coefficients. In contrast, the expansions we present in this paper are simple (inverse) factorial expansions with elementary coefficients. Additionally, in Section
2
, we provide explicit and computable error bounds for these new expansions under certain conditions on the degree
\nu
and the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. It is important to note that, unlike their uniform counterparts, our expansions break down near the transition points
z=\pm 1
of (
1.1
). Surprisingly, these simple expansions have not been extensively documented in the literature, except for a few special cases. This omission may be because previous researchers primarily focused on deriving uniform asymptotic results, which naturally possess a more complicated structure. We note that (inverse) factorial series were recently obtained for the associated Legendre and Ferrers functions in
[
24
]
.

To begin, we define the relevant functions. The Jacobi functions of the first and second kind, with complex degree
\nu
and parameters
\alpha

[

for

and
\nu+\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
, and

(1.3)

for

The two theorems below present inverse factorial expansions for the Jacobi and associated functions as the order
\nu
becomes large. Unlike standard asymptotic power series, which are expressed in negative powers of the large variable, inverse factorial expansions are asymptotic series involving gamma functions with decreasing arguments that depend on the large variable. One advantage of using these expansions in the case of Jacobi and associated functions is that the coefficients can be expressed in simple, closed forms. As Szegő noted
[
29
, p. 196]
, the corresponding asymptotic power series involve considerably more complicated coefficients. Moreover, as will be shown in Section
2
, these inverse factorial expansions allow for explicit and computable error bounds, which would be much more difficult to obtain using standard asymptotic power series. However, as mentioned in the introduction, a drawback of inverse factorial expansions compared to uniform asymptotic expansions in terms of special functions is their breakdown near the transition points
z=\pm 1
of (
1.1
). Finally, we note that inverse factorial expansions also arise naturally in exponential asymptotics; see, for example,
[
26
,
27
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
\xi\in\mathcal{D}
, that
\left|\sinh\xi\right|,\left|\cosh\xi\right|\geq\varepsilon
(
>0

\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
are bounded. Under these conditions, the Jacobi functions admit the following inverse factorial expansions:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}2^{2\nu+\alpha+\beta+1}&B(\nu+1,\nu+\alpha+\beta+1)P_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\cosh(2\xi))\sim\\
&\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\xi}}{\sinh\xi}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\xi}}{\cosh\xi}\right)^{\beta+1/2}\mathrm{e}^{2\nu\xi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}(-\xi,\alpha,\beta)\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-n+1)}{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta+2)}\\
&-C(\xi,\alpha)\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}}{\sinh\xi}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}}{\cosh\xi}\right)^{\beta+1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\nu\xi}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}g_{m}(\xi,\alpha,\beta)\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-m+1)}{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta+2)},\end{split}

as
\nu\to\infty
within the sector
\left|\arg\nu\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta
(
<\frac{\pi}{2}
), and

(1.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{2^{2\nu+\alpha+\beta+1}}{\pi}&B(\nu+1,\nu+\alpha+\beta+1)Q_{\nu}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(\cosh(2\xi))\sim\\
&\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}}{\sinh\xi}\right)^{\alpha+1/2}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\xi}}{\cosh\xi}\right)^{\beta+1/2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\nu\xi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}(\xi,\alpha,\beta)\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-n+1)}{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta+2)},\end{split}

as
\nu\to\infty
in the sector
\left|\arg\nu\right|\leq\pi-\delta
(

g_{n}(\pm\xi,\alpha,\beta)=\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{n}(\pm 1)^{\ell}a_{\ell}(\alpha)a_{n-\ell}(\beta)\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pm\xi}}{\sinh\xi}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\pm\xi}}{\cosh\xi}\right)^{n-\ell}.

The fractional powers are defined to be positive for positive real
\xi

&\times\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}}(1-t)^{2\nu+\alpha+\beta-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}}s^{\alpha^{\prime}-1/2}(1-s)^{\beta^{\prime}-1/2}\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}\left(t,s,1-s,-\mathrm{i}\zeta,\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha^{\prime},\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

A straightforward estimation yields the bound

(8.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\widehat{R}_{N}^{(P)}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)\right|\leq\;&\left|\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta+2)}{2\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}+1)\Gamma\left(\alpha^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right|\mathrm{e}^{\operatorname{Im}\left(\zeta_{\nu,N,0}^{(3)}\right)}\\
&\times\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})}(1-t)^{\operatorname{Re}(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime})}s^{\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime})-1/2}(1-s)^{\operatorname{Re}(\beta^{\prime})-1/2}\\
&\times\left|\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}\left(t,s,1-s,\mathrm{i}\zeta,\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha^{\prime},\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&+\left|\frac{\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta+2)}{2\Gamma(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}+1)\Gamma\left(\alpha^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\beta^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\right|\mathrm{e}^{-\operatorname{Im}\left(\zeta_{\nu,N,0}^{(3)}\right)}\\
&\times\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})}(1-t)^{\operatorname{Re}(2\nu+\alpha+\beta-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime})}s^{\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime})-1/2}(1-s)^{\operatorname{Re}(\beta^{\prime})-1/2}\\
&\times\left|\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}\left(t,s,1-s,-\mathrm{i}\zeta,\tfrac{1}{2}-\alpha^{\prime},\tfrac{1}{2}-\beta^{\prime}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

From Lemma
6.1
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}(u,v_{1},v_{2},\pm\mathrm{i}\zeta,A,B)\right|\leq\;

\displaystyle 2\left|\frac{\sin(\pi A)\sin(\pi B)}{\sin(\pi\operatorname{Re}(A))\sin(\pi\operatorname{Re}(B))}\frac{(\operatorname{Re}(A))_{N}}{N!}\left(\frac{v_{1}}{2\sin\zeta}\right)^{N}\right|u^{N}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\left|\frac{\sin(\pi B)}{\sin(\pi\operatorname{Re}(B))}\frac{(A)_{\ell}(\operatorname{Re}(B))_{N-\ell}}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\left(\frac{v_{1}}{2\sin\zeta}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{v_{2}}{2\cos\zeta}\right)^{N-\ell}\right|u^{N}

\displaystyle\times\begin{cases}2\cos\zeta,&\text{if }\;0<\zeta<\frac{\pi}{4},\\
\csc\zeta,&\text{if }\;\frac{\pi}{4}\leq\zeta<\frac{\pi}{2},\end{cases}

provided that
-N<\operatorname{Re}(A)<1
and
0<\operatorname{Re}(B)<1
, and

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}(u,v_{1},v_{2},\pm\mathrm{i}\zeta,A,B)\right|\leq\;

\displaystyle 2\left|\frac{\sin(\pi A)\sin(\pi B)}{\sin(\pi\operatorname{Re}(A))\sin(\pi\operatorname{Re}(B))}\frac{(\operatorname{Re}(B))_{N}}{N!}\left(\frac{v_{2}}{2\cos\zeta}\right)^{N}\right|u^{N}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\left|\frac{\sin(\pi A)}{\sin(\pi\operatorname{Re}(A))}\frac{(\operatorname{Re}(A))_{\ell}(B)_{N-\ell}}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\left(\frac{v_{1}}{2\sin\zeta}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{v_{2}}{2\cos\zeta}\right)^{N-\ell}\right|u^{N}

\displaystyle\times\begin{cases}\sec\zeta,&\text{if }\;0<\zeta<\frac{\pi}{4},\\
2\sin\zeta,&\text{if }\;\frac{\pi}{4}\leq\zeta<\frac{\pi}{2},\end{cases}

provided that
0<\operatorname{Re}(A)<1
and
-N<\operatorname{Re}(B)<1
. If
A
or
B
is an integer, then the limiting values must to be taken in these bounds. Similarly, from Lemma
7.1
and
[
18
, Eq. (16.19)]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}(u,v_{1},v_{2},\pm\mathrm{i}\zeta,A,B)\right|\leq\;

\displaystyle 2\frac{\Gamma(\operatorname{Re}(A))\Gamma(\operatorname{Re}(B))}{\left|\Gamma(A)\Gamma(B)\right|}\frac{\left|\Gamma(A+B+N)\right|}{\Gamma(\operatorname{Re}(A+B)+N)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}\frac{(\operatorname{Re}(A))_{\ell}(\operatorname{Re}(B))_{N-\ell}}{\ell!(N-\ell)!}\left|\left(\frac{v_{1}}{2\sin\zeta}\right)^{\ell}\left(\frac{v_{2}}{2\cos\zeta}\right)^{N-\ell}\right|u^{N},

under the conditions
\operatorname{Re}(A),\operatorname{Re}(B)>0
and
\operatorname{Re}(A+B)<1
. Substituting these estimates into (
8.2
) and observing that
\operatorname{Im}\left(\zeta_{\nu,N,0}^{(3)}\right)=\operatorname{Im}\left(\zeta_{\nu,N,\ell}^{(3)}\right)
,
a_{\ell}(\alpha^{\prime})=a_{\ell}(\alpha)
,
a_{\ell}(\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime}))=a_{\ell}(\operatorname{Re}(\alpha))
,
a_{\ell}(\beta^{\prime})=a_{\ell}(\beta)
, and
a_{\ell}(\operatorname{Re}(\beta^{\prime}))=a_{\ell}(\operatorname{Re}(\beta))
for all
0\leq\ell\leq N
, we obtain the bounds (
2.34
), (
2.35
), and (
2.37
), respectively. The case
\alpha=\beta=0
in (
2.37
) follows by a continuity argument. Note that, for instance, in deriving (
2.34
), the conditions
-\frac{1}{2}<\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime})<N+\frac{1}{2}
and
|\operatorname{Re}(\beta^{\prime})|<\frac{1}{2}
are required. However, these are equivalent to
-\frac{1}{2}<\operatorname{Re}(\alpha)<N+\frac{1}{2}
and
|\operatorname{Re}(\beta)|<\frac{1}{2}
. A similar observation holds for the other bounds. The estimate (
2.36
) can be derived from (
2.34
) by applying the inequality (
6.26
).

The bounds for the remainder terms
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(\mathrm{Q})}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)
and
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(\mathsf{Q})}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)
in the truncated factorial series (

) and (

) can be derived in a similar fashion by combining (

) with (

) and (

), respectively. The details are left for the reader to verify.

The integral representation of
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(P)}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)
, along with the analogous representations for
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(\mathrm{Q})}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)
and
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(\mathsf{Q})}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)

1.20
), and (

and

are odd integers.

Finally, note that when

\frac{1}{|2\sin\zeta|},\quad\frac{1}{|2\cos\zeta|}<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tfrac{\pi}{6}<\zeta<\tfrac{\pi}{3},

the convergence of the binomial expansion implies
\lim_{N\to+\infty}\widehat{R}_{N}^{-}(u,v_{1},v_{2},\pm\mathrm{i}\zeta,A,B)=0
. Consequently, from (
8.1
), we have
\lim_{N\to+\infty}\widehat{R}_{N}^{(P)}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)=0
, provided that
\operatorname{Re}(2\nu+\alpha+\beta+1)>\operatorname{Re}(\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime})

1.19
) converges to its left-hand side. The convergence of the series (
1.20
) and (
1.21
) follows in a similar manner, based on the analogous integral representations of the remainder terms
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(\mathrm{Q})}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)
and
\widehat{R}_{N}^{(\mathsf{Q})}(\nu,\zeta,\alpha,\beta)
.

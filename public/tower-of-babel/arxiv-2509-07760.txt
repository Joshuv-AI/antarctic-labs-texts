\displaystyle\delta_{\chi}(H,k):=\inf\{d:\delta(G)\geq d|G|\text{ and }H\not\subseteq G\Rightarrow\chi(G)\leq k\}.

The chromatic profile problem connects extremal and coloring theory by asking how a “local” density condition (minimum degree), rather than a “global” one, constrains the chromatic number of an
H
-free graph. This framework, exemplified by the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem, studies the stability of chromatic properties in graphs that are dense but not necessarily extremal.

The extension to digraphs introduces two primary challenges: the proliferation of forbidden structures due to their various orientations, and the structural asymmetries that demand new proof techniques.

All digraphs considered in this paper are finite, may contain anti-parallel arcs (i.e., directed 2-cycles), but have no loops or parallel arcs.
The
chromatic profile of a digraph
H
is defined as:

\displaystyle\delta^{+}_{\chi}(H,k):=\inf\{d:\delta^{+}(D)\geq d|D|\text{ and }H\not\subseteq D\Rightarrow\chi(D)\leq k\}.

(1)

Here,

denotes the minimum out-degree of the digraph
D
.
Our first result generalizes the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem to the directed setting by determining the profile of the transitive tournament

on
r

Theorem 1.1
.

\delta^{+}_{\chi}(T_{r},r-1)=\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}
.

Notably, by replacing each edge in an undirected graph with a pair of anti-parallel arcs, one can recover the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem as a direct corollary of

A Directed Analogue of the Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós Theorem

We begin by recalling two elementary properties of transitive tournaments that follow directly from the definition.

Property 2.1
.

Let
T_{r}
be a transitive tournament on
r
vertices.

(i)

Every vertex-induced subgraph of
T_{r}
is a transitive tournament;

(ii)

Adding a new vertex
v
and arcs such that
v
dominates all vertices in
V(T_{r})
, or is dominated by all of them, results in a transitive tournament on
r+1
vertices.

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, we first handle the special case where the underlying graph is complete multipartite.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
D
be an
n
-vertex digraph whose underlying graph

is complete
s
-partite with

. If
\delta^{+}(D)>\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}n
, then
D
contains a copy of
T_{r}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
r
. For the base case
r=3
, the condition
s\geq 3
implies that
G(D)
contains a triangle.
Let its vertices be
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}
.
As
\delta^{+}(D)>\frac{2}{5}n

\{v_{i},v_{j}\}
has a common out-neighbor
w
.
Then subgraph induced by
\{v_{i},v_{j},w\}
contains a
T_{3}
, since
v_{i}\to v_{j}
,
v_{i}\to w
, and
v_{j}\to w
.

Now, assume the lemma holds for all

.
Let the color classes of

be
A_{1},\dots,A_{s}
.
Suppose to the contrary that

is
T_{r}
-free.
The minimum out-degree condition implies that
\alpha(D)=\max_{i\in[s]}|A_{i}|<n-\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}n=\frac{3}{3r-4}n

We first establish a key property of out-neighborhoods in

Claim 2.3

For every

(r-2)
-colorable.

Proof.

Clearly,

Furthermore, we have
|D_{v}|=d^{+}(v)>\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}n

\displaystyle\delta^{+}(D_{v})>\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}n-(n-|D_{v}|)>\frac{3(r-1)-7}{3(r-1)-4}|D_{v}|.

Since
D_{v}
is
T_{r-1}
-free and complete multipartite, by the induction hypothesis, we have
\chi(D_{v})\leq r-2
.
∎

Moreover, we must have
\chi(D_{v})=r-2
. Otherwise,
\alpha(D_{v})\geq\frac{|D_{v}|}{r-3}>\frac{(3r-7)/(r-3)}{3r-4}n>\frac{3n}{3r-4}

\alpha(D_{v})\leq\alpha(D)<\frac{3n}{3r-4}

Let

Note that

\displaystyle|B_{i}|=d^{+}(v)-\sum_{j\neq i}|B_{j}|>\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}n-(r-3)\frac{3n}{3r-4}=\frac{2n}{3r-4}.

This implies a crucial fact: if a vertex

has a positive in-degree (i.e., is dominated by some vertex

Now, let

Since

By the pigeonhole principle, at least one such set, say

As

N^{+}(v)=V(D)\setminus A_{i}

Moreover,

Hence

∎

Lemma 2.4

(Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion)

Let

be finite sets. Then

|A_{1}\cap\cdots\cap A_{p}|\geq\sum_{i=1}^{p}|A_{i}|-(p-1)\left|\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}A_{i}\right|.

\displaystyle\left|\bigcap_{v\in\{u\}\cup V(Q)}N^{+}(v)\right|\geq(r-1)\left(\frac{r-2}{r-1}n+1\right)-(r-2)n>0.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\varepsilon>0

H\in\{C_{5}^{\prime},C_{5}^{\prime\prime},C_{5}^{\prime\prime\prime}\}

Proof
.

Let

is not bipartite, meaning its underlying graph

contains an odd cycle.
The proof is divided into three cases based on the odd girth of

Case 1
.

g_{\text{odd}}(D)=3
.

It follows directly from

4.7

Case 2
.

g_{\text{odd}}(D)=5
.

In this case, the underlying graph

Claim 4.12
.

Let
C
be an oriented 5-cycle in

. For any three consecutive vertices

on

, there is a vertex
v^{\prime}\notin V(C)
such that
u\rightarrow v^{\prime}
and
w\rightarrow v^{\prime}

Proof.

Let
u,v,w
be three consecutive vertices on

Since

, there are no triangles in

.
Consequently, we have
N^{+}(u)\cap N^{+}(v)=\varnothing
and
N^{+}(v)\cap N^{+}(w)=\varnothing
.
Thus
|N^{+}(u)\cap N^{+}(w)|\geq d^{+}(u)+d^{+}(w)-(n-d^{+}(v))\geq 3\varepsilon n>|C|
.
That is, there is a vertex
v^{\prime}\notin V(C)
such that
u\rightarrow v^{\prime}
and
w\rightarrow v^{\prime}
.
∎

Let
C
be an oriented 5-cycle in

with vertex set

.
By iteratively applying
4.12
, we obtain vertices:

•

with

and
v_{4}\rightarrow v_{5}^{\prime}
;

•

with

•

v_{3}^{\prime}\notin S^{\prime\prime}
with
v_{2}\rightarrow v_{3}^{\prime}
and
v_{4}^{\prime}\rightarrow v_{3}^{\prime}
, where
S^{\prime\prime}=S^{\prime}\setminus\{v_{4}\}\cup\{v_{4}^{\prime}\}
;

•

v_{2}^{\prime}\notin S^{\prime\prime\prime}
with
v_{1}\rightarrow v_{2}^{\prime}
and
v_{3}^{\prime}\rightarrow v_{2}^{\prime}
, where
S^{\prime\prime\prime}=S^{\prime\prime}\setminus\{v_{3}\}\cup\{v_{3}^{\prime}\}

The subgraph induced by the resulting vertex set at each step contains an oriented 5-cycle.
Then the subgraph induced by

Case 3
.

g_{\text{odd}}(D)\geq 7
.

Let
C
be a minimum odd cycle in
G(D)
.
Then
C
is an induced cycle and
|C|\geq 7
.
Moreover,
d^{+}(V(C),v)\leq 2
for every

; otherwise, there exists an odd cycle of length smaller than
|C|
.
By double counting the arcs from
V(C)
to
V(D)\setminus V(C)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in V(C)}(d^{+}(u)-2)\leq\sum_{u\in V(C)}d^{+}(u,V(D)\setminus V(C))=\sum_{v\in V(D)\setminus V(C)}d^{+}(V(C),v)\leq 2(n-|C|).

By the pigeonhole principle, there is a vertex

such that

.
This implies that

, a contradiction.

.

The lower bound

.

5
An application of the directed version Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem

In this section, we provide an application of the directed version Andrásfai-Erdős-Sós theorem.

First we introduce the famous regularity lemma.
Let
D
be a digraph and
G=G(D)
be its underlying graph.
Let
(A,B)
be a pair of subsets of vertices of
G
.
Let
e(A,B)
denote the number of edges with one endpoint in
A
and the other in
B
.
The
density
of the pair
(A,B)
is defined as
d(A,B)=\frac{e(A,B)}{|A||B|}
, in the undirected case.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, the pair
(A,B)
is said to be
\varepsilon
-regular
if
|d(A,B)-d(X,Y)|<\varepsilon
for every
X\subseteq A
and
Y\subseteq B
with
|X|\geq\varepsilon|A|
and
|Y|\geq\varepsilon|B|
. Moreover, given
0<d<1
, we say that
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular
if it is
\varepsilon
-regular and has density at least
d
.
A partition
V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
of
V(G)
is called an
\varepsilon
-regular partition
if
|V_{0}|\leq\varepsilon n,|V_{1}|=\cdots=|V_{k}|
, and all but at most
\varepsilon k^{2}
of the pairs
(V_{i},V_{j})
, where
1\leq i,j\leq k,
are
\varepsilon
-regular.
The vertex sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
are called
clusters
.
Given clusters
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
and a digraph
D
, the
(\varepsilon,d)
-reduced digraph

R
is a digraph on the vertex set
[k]
.
There is an arc from
i
to
j
if and only if the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-regular.

The Diregularity lemma is a version of the Regularity lemma for digraphs due to Alon and Shapira
[
2
]
.
We will use the following minimum degree form of the diregularity lemma.

Lemma 5.1

(Degree form of the Diregularity Lemma, see
[
25
]
and
[
18
]
)
.

Let
0<2\varepsilon<d<\delta<1
, and let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a constant
k_{1}=k_{1}(k_{0},\varepsilon,\delta,d)
such that the following holds. Every digraph
D
on
n>k_{1}
vertices, with minimum out-degree
\delta^{+}(D)\geq\delta n
, has an
(\varepsilon,d)
-reduced digraph
R
on
k
vertices, with
k_{0}\leq k\leq k_{1}
and
\delta^{+}(R)\geq(\delta-d-3\varepsilon)k
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
1\gg\gamma\gg d\gg\varepsilon>0
.
Applying minimum degree form regularity lemma (
Lemma

5.1
) to
D
, we obtain a partition
V(D)=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
of
D
, together with an
(\varepsilon,d)
-reduced graph
R
on the vertex set
V(R)=[k]
, such that
\delta^{+}(R)\geq(\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}+\frac{\gamma}{2})k
.

Since the original digraph
D
is
T_{r}[t]
-free, a standard result known as the Embedding Lemma implies that for a sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, the reduced digraph
R
must be
T_{r}
-free.
By
Theorem

2.7
,
R
is
(r-1)
-colorable.

Suppose that
I
is an independent set in
R
and
V_{I}=\cup_{i\in I}V_{i}
.
The arcs of
D[V_{I}]
can be categorized into the following three types:

•

arcs with both endpoints in
V_{i}
for some
i\in I
. There are at most
2k\binom{n/k}{2}
such arcs;

•

arcs between distinct clusters
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, for some distinct
i,j\in I
for which
(V_{i},V_{j})
is not
\varepsilon
-regular. There are at most
2\varepsilon k^{2}\frac{n^{2}}{k^{2}}
such arcs;

•

arcs between distinct clusters
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
, for some distinct
i,j\in I
for which
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular but
d(V_{i},V_{j})<d
. There are at most
2d\binom{k}{2}\frac{n^{2}}{k^{2}}
such arcs.

Summing up the arcs from these three types yields
|A(D[V_{I}])|\leq(4\varepsilon+d)n^{2}
.
Since
V(R)
can be divided into
r-1
independent sets, this implies that to make
D

(r-1)
-partite it suffices to delete at most
(r-1)(4\varepsilon+d)n^{2}=o(n^{2})
arcs.
∎

Remark.

The
T_{r}[t]
-free condition in Theorem
1.2
cannot be replaced by forbidding an arbitrary digraph that also has chromatic number
r
.
Consider the following construction for
r=3
, which is a modification of
Fig.

4.1
(a).
Let
D
be a digraph with a vertex partition
A\cup B\cup C
, where
|A|=(1/5-2\varepsilon)n
and
|B|=|C|=(2/5+\varepsilon)n
.
The arc set consists of all arcs from
A
to
B\cup C
, and all arcs between
B
and
C
(i.e., from
B
to
C
and from
C
to
B
).
This digraph is
\overrightarrow{C_{3}}
-free with minimum out-degree
\delta^{+}(D)=(2/5+\varepsilon)n
, which meets the theorem’s threshold.
However, the digraph is not close to being bipartite, as one must delete at least
\Theta(n^{2})
arcs to make it so.

6
Concluding Remarks

In contrast to the chromatic profile defined in (
1
), which requires the chromatic number to be bounded by a specific integer
k
, a related notion is the chromatic threshold.
The
directed chromatic threshold
of a digraph
H
is defined as:

\displaystyle\delta^{+}_{\chi}(H):=

\displaystyle\inf\{d:\exists\penalty\ C=C(H,d)\text{ such that if $D$ is a digraph on $n$ vertices},

\displaystyle\text{with $\delta^{+}(D)\geq dn$ and $H\not\subseteq D$, then $\chi(D)\leq C$}\}.

It is instructive to compare our results on the 2-coloring profile
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(H,2)
with the known chromatic thresholds for the four pentagon orientations discussed. Ding et al.
[
9
]
established that
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(\overrightarrow{C_{5}})=1/2
and
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(C_{5}^{\prime})=1/4
. In contrast, Koerts, Moore, and Spirkl
[
20
]
showed that the threshold is zero for the other two cases:
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(C_{5}^{\prime\prime})=\delta_{\chi}^{+}(C_{5}^{\prime\prime\prime})=0
.
Notably, the values and even the relative ordering of these two parameters can differ significantly. For instance, we proved
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(C_{5}^{\prime},2)=\delta_{\chi}^{+}(C_{5}^{\prime\prime},2)=1/3
, whereas their chromatic thresholds are
1/4
and
0
, respectively. This complex relationship motivates further study, and we pose the following open questions:

Question 1
.

Let
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
be two orientations of a graph
H
. Does the relation
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(H_{1})>\delta_{\chi}^{+}(H_{2})
imply that
\delta_{\chi}^{+}(H_{1},k)\geq\delta_{\chi}^{+}(H_{2},k)
for every integer
k\geq 2
?

It is observed that
3
and
4
provide a lower bound of 1/3 for certain oriented odd cycles.

Question 2
.

Determine the chromatic profile for every orientation of an arbitrary odd cycle
C_{2\ell+1}
.

We note that our constructions for the lower bounds contain vertices with zero in-degree. It is therefore a natural problem to study the chromatic profile where the minimum out-degree is replaced by the minimum semi-degree, a setting in which our constructions are no longer extremal.

Finally, Alon and Sudakov
[
3
]
proved that if
G
is a
K_{r}[t]
-free graph of order
n
with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}+\varepsilon)n
, then one can delete at most
O(n^{2-1/(4(r-1)^{2/3}t)})
edges to make
G

(r-1)
-colorable.
This invites the question of whether a similar quantitative improvement can be made to our stability result.

Question 3
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large and
D
be a
T_{r}[t]
-free digraph on
n
vertices with minimum out-degree
\delta^{+}(D)\geq(\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}+\varepsilon)n
. Does there exist a constant
\alpha:=\alpha(r,t)>0
, such that one can delete
O(n^{2-\alpha})
arcs to make
D
an
(r-1)
-partite digraph?

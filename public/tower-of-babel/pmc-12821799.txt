Intrathecal morphine versus transversalis fascia and transversus abdominis plane blocks combination on obstetric quality of recovery after cesarean delivery: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Dilara Yakisan Cadirci, Muhammed Enes Aydin, Elif Oral Ahiskalioglu, Erkan Cem Çelik, Ahmet Murat Yayik, Şeyma Çoruh, Gamze Nur Cimilli Şenocak, Bahadir Ciftci, Ali Ahiskalioglu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block is widely used to enhance postoperative analgesia after cesarean sections (CS). This study aimed to compare the effect of combining the transversalis fascia plane (TFP) block with the TAP block versus intrathecal morphine (ITM) on postoperative recovery quality assessed using the Obstetric Quality of Recovery-10 (ObsQoR-10T) score.MethodsThis prospective, randomized clinical trial was conducted at a single university hospital. One hundred patients undergoing elective CS under spinal anesthesia were randomized into two groups. Group ITM received spinal anesthesia comprising 10 mg 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine, 15 µg fentanyl, and 100 µg morphine. Group TAP + TFP received spinal anesthesia with 10 mg of 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine and 15 µg of fentanyl, without intrathecal morphine. In the TAP + TFP group only, bilateral TAP and TFP blocks were performed at the end of surgery, whereas no block was administered in the ITM group. Postoperative analgesia was provided using fentanyl-based patient-controlled analgesia (PCA). The primary outcome was the ObsQoR-10T score which was assessed at 24 h postoperatively. Secondary outcomes included opioid consumption, pain scores at rest and movement, adverse effects, and patient satisfaction.ResultsThere was no significant difference between the groups regarding the total ObsQoR-10T scores (91.72 ± 6.46 vs. 91.56 ± 5.54, p = 0.895). The TAP + TFP group had significantly higher fentanyl consumption at 24 h compared to the ITM group (187.50 µg vs. 87.50 µg, p = 0.005). No significant differences were found in pain scores at rest or during movement. The incidence of adverse effects and patient satisfaction rates were comparable between groups.ConclusionsThe TAP + TFP block combination yielded a similar overall ObsQoR-10T recovery profile to ITM. Both strategies provided satisfactory analgesia and high maternal satisfaction.Trial registration numberClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT06944912 retrospectively registered at clinicaltrials, principal investigator: Ali Ahiskalioglu (registration date: April 18, 2025)Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12871-025-03559-0.Keywords: Cesarean section, Transversus abdominis plane block, Transversalis fascia plane block, Intrathecal morphine, ObsQoR-10T, Postoperative analgesia

Introduction

Effective postoperative analgesia is essential after cesarean section (CS) to facilitate early mobilization, support breastfeeding, and reduce the risks of postpartum depression and persistent pain [1–3]. Ensuring adequate pain control is therefore a key component of optimizing maternal recovery and perioperative outcomes.

Intrathecal morphine (ITM), administered alongside spinal anesthesia, is currently recommended by the PROSPECT guidelines as a core component of multimodal analgesia for elective CS owing to its prolonged duration of action and established efficacy [4]. However, its side effects, —such as nausea, vomiting, pruritus, and respiratory depression—limit its universal applicability [5].

In recent years, fascial plane blocks have gained popularity as opioid-sparing alternatives in multimodal analgesic strategies [6]. The transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block is a well-established technique that targets the thoracolumbar nerves between the internal oblique and transversus abdominis muscles to provide somatic analgesia to the anterior abdominal wall [7]. Nevertheless, its efficacy may be limited in cesarean delivery due to incomplete coverage of T12–L1 dermatomes, which are especially relevant for Pfannenstiel incisions [8]. The transversalis fascia plane (TFP) block, first described by Hebbard, aims to overcome this limitation by delivering local anesthetic between the transversus abdominis muscle and the transversalis fascia, thereby providing more proximal blockade of the iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal nerves [9].

In this prospective, randomized study, we aimed to investigate whether the addition of TFP block to TAP block could enhance postoperative recovery following elective CS compared with the current guideline-recommended ITM. The primary endpoint was the total score on the Turkish version of the Obstetric Quality of Recovery-10 (ObsQoR-10T), a culturally validated, multidimensional recovery assessment tool that evaluates physical comfort, emotional well-being, mobility, and maternal-infant interaction [10, 11]. We hypothesized that the TAP + TFP block combination would provide a postoperative recovery profile comparable to ITM as measured by the total ObsQoR-10T score.

Methods

Study design

This single-center, prospective, randomized clinical trial was conducted at the Ataturk University Medical Faculty Hospital, Erzurum, Türkiye) between January 2024 and January 2025. This study was approved by Institutional Review Board of Ataturk University Faculty of Medicine Clinical Research Ethics Committee (Approval No: B.30.2.ATA.0.01.00/1 /06.07.2023/5, Chairperson Prof. Mustafa Gul) and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. The trial was retrospectively registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06944912).

Participants

Eligible participants were full-term pregnant women aged 18–45 years with American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I or II scheduled for elective CS under spinal anesthesia. Exclusion criteria included morbid obesity (BMI > 40 kg/m²), known allergy to local anesthetics or opioids, coagulation disorders, chronic analgesic use, infection at the planned injection site, pregnancy-related complications such as pre-eclampsia, psychiatric or neurological disease, and any condition compromising communication or follow-up.

Anesthesia management

Standard ASA monitoring was applied upon arrival in the operating theatre, including electrocardiography, non-invasive blood pressure, and pulse oximetry. A 16G intravenous cannula was inserted, and crystalloid infusion was initiated. Subsequently, all patients received intravenous granisetron (3 mg) and dexamethasone (8 mg) for prophylaxis against nausea and vomiting.

Spinal anesthesia was then performed in the sitting position at the L3–L4 interspace using a 25G pencil-point spinal needle. All patients received 10 mg 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine and 15 µg fentanyl intrathecally. In Group 1 (ITM group), an additional 100 µg of morphine was administered intrathecally. Following injection, the patients were positioned in a 20° left lateral tilt to minimize aortocaval compression. Surgery commenced once the T4 sensory level was confirmed via the pinprick test.

Randomization and blinding

Randomization was performed in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated block randomization sequence created via Microsoft Excel. Group assignments were concealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes, which were opened by the attending anesthesiologist immediately prior to spinal anesthesia. Although the anesthesiologists performing the block could not be blinded, all postoperative outcome assessments were conducted by investigators blinded to group allocation. The anesthesiologist performing the intervention (including the peripheral blocks at the end of surgery) was not involved in postoperative data collection.

Intervention procedures

At the end of surgery, patients in TAP + TFP group underwent bilateral ultrasound-guided fascial plane blocks while in the supine position under sterile conditions. A high-frequency linear transducer was used for both blocks. For the TAP block, the probe was placed transversely on the midaxillary line between the iliac crest and costal margin. After identifying the fascial plane between the internal oblique and transversus abdominis muscles, a 100-mm echogenic needle was inserted in-plane, and 20 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine was administered on each side. Subsequently, TFP block was performed using the same needle via a single skin puncture. The transducer was moved posterolaterally to the posterior axillary line, where the plane between the transversus abdominis muscle and the transversalis fascia was visualized. A second injection of 10 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine per side was administered in this plane under real-time ultrasound guidance.

Thus, 30 mL of the local anesthetic solution was administered on each side. All blocks were performed by anesthesiologists who had each performed more than 200 TAP and TFP blocks before the study period.

Postoperative analgesia protocol

Upon completion of the surgery and regional block procedures, postoperative analgesia was initiated using a fentanyl-based patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) device (BodyGuard 595 Pain Manager Infusion Pump System, Caesarea Medical Electronics Ltd., Germany). The PCA system was configured with a fentanyl concentration of 10 µg/ml. The settings included a loading dose of 50 µg, a bolus dose of 25 µg, a lockout interval of 15 min, and no basal infusion. The system remained active for 24 h and was continuously monitored throughout the postoperative period.

All patients received intravenous paracetamol at a dose of 1 g every eight hours and intravenous dexketoprofen at a dose of 50 mg every 12 h, according to a standardized multimodal analgesia protocol. If a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) ≥ 4 was recorded at any of these scheduled assessment times, rescue analgesia with intravenous tramadol at a dose of 1 mg/kg (Contramal, Abdi İbrahim, Istanbul, Turkey) was administered. This regimen was identical in both study groups.

The patients were initially monitored in the post-anesthesia care unit until the spinal sensory block regressed below the T10 dermatome. Following this, they were transferred to the inpatient ward for continued observation over a 24-hour period.

Outcome measures

Postoperative pain intensity was evaluated at seven predefined time points: 0, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, and 24 h. Each assessment was conducted by a blinded investigator and included VAS scores at rest and during movement. Movement was defined as the transition from the supine position to the sitting position. Fentanyl consumption was recorded cumulatively for the intervals of 0–4 h, 4–8 h, 8–24 h, as well as for the overall 24-hour period. Time to first PCA request was recorded.

The adverse effects were closely monitored. This included nausea, vomiting, the need for antiemetic therapy, pruritus constipation, tongue numbness, and any clinical signs suggestive of local anesthetic systemic toxicity. Complications related to the block procedure were recorded.

Patient satisfaction was rated on a five-point Likert scale, where 1 indicated complete dissatisfaction and 5 indicated complete satisfaction. Sedation status was assessed using a four-point scale ranging from fully awake (score 0) to drowsy and difficult to rouse (score 3). The primary outcome, which was the total score from the Turkish version of the Obstetric Quality of Recovery-10 (ObsQoR-10T), was evaluated at the 24-hour mark by a blinded assessor.

Statistical analysis and sample size Estimation

The sample size estimation was based on pilot data obtained from 10 patients in each group. The mean ObsQoR-10 score (± SD) was 91.25 ± 5.61 in the ITM group and 87.34 ± 6.75 in the TAP + TFP group, yielding an effect size (Cohen’s d) of 0.63. Using this value in an a priori power analysis with a two-tailed t-test for independent means, a significance level of α = 0.05, and a power (1–β) = 0.80, the minimum required sample size was calculated as 41 participants per group (total N = 82). The planned enrollment was set at 50 patients per group to accommodate potential dropouts and to ensure adequate statistical power.

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of the data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) if normally distributed or as median and interquartile range (IQR) for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages. Between-group comparisons for normally distributed variables were conducted using the independent-samples t-test, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was used for skewed distributions. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. The time to the first PCA request, a predefined secondary outcome, was evaluated using survival analysis. Kaplan–Meier curves were constructed, and comparisons between groups were performed using the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. Patients who did not activate PCA within 24 h were censored in the analysis. All tests were two-tailed, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Between January 1, 2024, and January 1, 2025, 110 parturient scheduled for elective CS under spinal anesthesia were assessed for eligibility. Seven patients were excluded because of insufficient Turkish language proficiency, which would have compromised the validity of the ObsQoR-10T questionnaire. Three patients were excluded after preoperative laboratory testing revealed thrombocytopenia requiring postponement of surgery. The remaining 100 patients fulfilled all the inclusion criteria and were randomized in equal numbers to receive either ITM or a combination of TAP and TFP blocks. All patients received the allocated interventions and completed a 24-hour postoperative follow-up without any protocol deviation. The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, height, weight, and body mass index, were comparable between groups. The duration of surgery was not significantly different, although anesthesia duration was slightly longer in the TAP + TFP group (76.88 ± 20.43 min vs. 67.84 ± 18.89 min, p = 0.024), likely due to the additional time required for bilateral block application.

The primary outcome, total ObsQoR-10T score at 24 h postoperatively, did not differ significantly between the groups (91.72 ± 6.46 in the ITM group vs. 91.56 ± 5.54 in the TAP + TFP group, p = 0.895) (Table 1). Similarly, no significant differences were observed across the individual domains of the ObsQoR-10T, including physical comfort, mobility, emotional well-being, and maternal-infant interaction, indicating that both techniques provided comparable recovery profiles.

Discussion

This randomized, assessor-blinded trial evaluated whether TAP + TFP block combination would improve postoperative recovery after CS. The primary outcome was the ObsQoR-10T score at 24 h, which was comparable between the groups. Both techniques ensure effective analgesia during rest and mobilization, accompanied by high levels of patient satisfaction. However, the increased need for opioids in the TAP + TFP group underscores the limitations of fascial plane blocks in achieving visceral analgesia. These findings suggest that this combination may represent a reasonable option within multimodal analgesia pathways.

Current PROSPECT guidelines recommend ITM as the primary component of postoperative analgesia in CS, due to its superior efficacy in addressing both somatic and visceral pain when no contraindication exists [12]. In cases where neuraxial opioids are contraindicated or not preferred, fascial plane blocks are considered valuable alternatives within a multimodal regimen [4, 13].

Although TAP block is commonly incorporated into post-cesarean multimodal analgesia protocols, existing evidence indicates that it does not match the opioid-sparing capacity of intrathecal morphine [14–17]. This limitation has prompted interest in strategies that may enhance its analgesic profile. Our rationale for combining TAP with the TFP block was based on the hypothesis that targeting complementary anatomical planes could broaden somatic coverage and strengthen postoperative analgesia. Rather than comparing individual fascial plane techniques, our study evaluated this combined approach directly against intrathecal morphine—the current benchmark in cesarean analgesia—to determine whether a dual-plane strategy could offer a clinically meaningful alternative within a multimodal regimen.

The known limitations of the TAP block in reliably covering lower thoracolumbar dermatomes [8] and providing visceral analgesia have encouraged the development of dual-plane strategies. Tulgar et al. reported that combining fascial plane blocks can address segmental gaps by targeting spared cutaneous branches or enhancing cephalocaudal spread, thereby improving block success and reliability [18]. Likewise, Altiparmak et al. emphasized that such combinations—particularly when selected based on anatomical complementarity—may extend sensory coverage and reduce failure rates, especially in abdominal wall analgesia [19]. Guided by these insights, our study utilized TFP block in addition to TAP block to improve dermatomal coverage and maximize its opioid-reducing effect as a component of multimodal analgesia after cesarean delivery.

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis further supports the opioid-sparing potential of the TFP block after cesarean delivery, demonstrating reduced postoperative opioid consumption and lower rescue analgesia requirements, although its effect on pain scores remains inconclusive [20]. Prior randomized trials have shown that the TFP block reduces postoperative opioid consumption and improves analgesia after cesarean delivery [21, 22]. In a more recent RCT, Pınarbaşı et al. reported superior ObsQoR-11 recovery scores and lower opioid requirements with TFP compared with TAP [23]. Unlike their comparison of two fascial plane blocks, our study evaluated the TAP + TFP combination against intrathecal morphine, a stronger comparator in obstetric analgesia. Despite methodological differences, both studies indicate that incorporating TFP enhances postoperative recovery and decreases opioid use, with our TAP + TFP group demonstrating an analgesic profile consistent with the benefits previously attributed to TFP alone.

In obstetric anesthesia practice, there are clinical situations in which neuraxial morphine cannot be administered such as opioid allergy, prior episodes of severe pruritus or nausea, obstructive sleep apnea or other respiratory conditions, or strong patient preference to avoid opioids. In these settings, the exploration of fascial plane blocks becomes relevant, as they offer an opioid-sparing effects. Although our study did not include patients with contraindications to neuraxial opioids, the comparable recovery profile observed in the TAP + TFP group suggests that dual fascial plane blocks may provide a feasible alternative within a multimodal regimen when intrathecal morphine is not used.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged when interpreting its findings. First, while the trial compared the effects of the TAP + TFP block combination against ITM, it did not include a placebo or sham block group. Although such designs are often limited by ethical considerations, particularly in obstetric populations, the absence of a true control group may have affected the internal validity of the observed treatment effects. Second, unlike some previous trials, this study did not include systematic dermatomal mapping to confirm the spread and efficacy of the fascial plane blocks. Although ultrasound guidance was employed, anatomical variability may have affected block success, and an objective assessment of sensory coverage would have enhanced methodological robustness. Third, postoperative pain evaluations, especially in the early postoperative hours, may have been influenced by the residual effects of spinal anesthesia, potentially masking differences in analgesic effect. This limitation complicates the isolated evaluation of block-specific contributions to analgesia during the immediate recovery period. Fourth, this single-center study was conducted at a tertiary academic institution. Although enrollment and follow-up were rigorous, the generalizability of the findings to other healthcare settings, patient populations, or surgical and anesthetic practices may be limited. Lastly, although the study was adequately powered to detect differences in the primary outcome measure (ObsQoR-10T scores), subgroup analyses, such as rescue analgesia requirements or the incidence of opioid-related side effects, may have been underpowered, limiting the strength of the conclusions drawn from these secondary endpoints.

Conclusion

In this randomized, assessor-blind trial, TAP + TFP block combination did not significantly improve the quality of recovery, as measured by the ObsQoR-10T score, compared with ITM. Although both approaches yielded comparable recovery outcomes and acceptable pain scores, the TAP + TFP block combination was associated with significantly higher postoperative opioid consumption. Nevertheless, the TAP + TFP block combination demonstrated clinically effective analgesia, with a similar incidence of adverse effects between groups. The TAP + TFP block combination may represent a reasonable option in clinical scenarios where intrathecal morphine is avoided because of its characteristic side effects (such as pruritus, nausea, or delayed respiratory depression), recognizing that this approach is not opioid-free and includes low-dose intrathecal fentanyl as part of standard spinal anesthesia. Further multicenter studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods are warranted to better define the role of peripheral block combinations in cesarean analgesia protocols.

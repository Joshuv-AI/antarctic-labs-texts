Colouring normal quadrangulations of projective spaces
Tomáš Kaiser, On-Hei Solomon Lo, Atsuhiro Nakamoto, Yuta Nozaki, Kenta Ozeki

Abstract
Youngs proved that every non-bipartite quadrangulation of the projective plane ℝ ​ P 2 \mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2} is 4-chromatic. Kaiser and Stehlík [J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 113 (2015), 1–17] generalised the notion of a quadrangulation to higher dimensions and extended Youngs’ theorem by proving that every non-bipartite quadrangulation of the d d -dimensional projective space ℝ ​ P d \mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d} with d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 has chromatic number at least d + 2 d+2 . On the other hand, Hachimori et al. [European. J. Combin. 125 (2025), 104089] defined another kind of high-dimensional quadrangulation, called a normal quadrangulation . They proved that if a non-bipartite normal quadrangulation G G of ℝ ​ P d \mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d} with any d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 satisfies a certain geometric condition, then G G is 4 4 -chromatic, and asked whether the geometric condition can be removed from the result. In this paper, we give a negative solution to their problem for the case d = 3 d=3 , proving that there exist 3-dimensional normal quadrangulations of ℝ ​ P 3 \mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{3} whose chromatic number is arbitrarily large. Moreover, we prove that no normal quadrangulation of ℝ ​ P d \mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d} with any d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 has chromatic number 3 3 .

Introduction

Let
G
be a graph,
and let
c\colon V(G)\to\{1,\ldots,k\}
be a map.
We say that
c
is a
k
-colouring
if every edge
xy\in E(G)
satisfies
c(x)\neq c(y)
.
A graph
G
is said to be
k
-colourable
if
G
admits a
k
-colouring.
The
chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi(G)
,
is the smallest
k
such that
G
is
k
-colourable,
and if
\chi(G)=k
, then
G
is said to be
k
-chromatic
.
A
k
-cycle

C
means a cycle of length
k
,
and we say that
C
is
even
(respectively,
odd
)
if
k
is even (respectively, odd).

The
Four Colour Theorem

[
1
,
2
]
, one of the most
celebrated results in graph theory, states that every planar graph is

4
-colourable. This result has been extended in many directions, yet
in this paper we consider colourings of graphs embedded on
non-spherical closed surfaces.
We consider only
2-cell embeddings
of graphs on surfaces, meaning embeddings whose faces are homeomorphic to an open 2-cell.

Heawood
[
9
]
proved that every graph
G
embedded in a
closed non-spherical surface
S
satisfies

\chi(G)\leq\frac{7+\sqrt{24k+1}}{2},

where
k
denotes the
Euler genus
of
S
, that is,

k=2-\epsilon(S)
with
\epsilon(S)
denoting the
Euler
characteristic
of
S
. (This bound is known to be best possible
except for the Klein bottle
[
16
]
.) Hence, if we let
G
be a
graph embeddable in a closed surface
S
with Euler genus
k>0
, then
we have
\chi(G)=O(\sqrt{k})
. On the other hand,
Thomassen
[
18
]
proved that
for any closed surface
S
with Euler genus
k>0
, there exists a
positive integer
N(S)
such that if
G
is a graph embedded in
S

with edge-width at least
N(S)
, then
G
is
5
-colourable. (Recall
that
edge-width
of a graph
G
on a surface
S
is defined to
be the length of a shortest non-contractible cycle of
G
.) Note that
the value
5
is best possible, since every non-spherical surface
S

admits non-
4
-colourable graphs of arbitrary edge-width. In a more informal way, Thomassen’s result can be rephrased as stating that
locally planar
graphs on any non-spherical surface (that is, embedded graphs without short non-contractible cycles) are 5-colourable.

Now we turn our attention to
even-sided
graphs on a closed
surface
S
, that is, simple graphs embedded in
S
such that each face is bounded
by an even cycle. In particular, a
quadrangulation
of
S
is an
even-sided graph on
S
with each face being quadrangular.

It is easy to see that every even-sided graph on the sphere is bipartite, but
this does not hold for any non-spherical closed surface. Hutchinson

[
10
]
proved that if
G
is an even-sided graph on
S
with
Euler genus
k>0
, then

\chi(G)\leq\frac{5+\sqrt{16k-7}}{2}.

(Based on recent developments
[
13
]
in this topic, it is known
that this bound is best possible, except for the Klein bottle and the
double torus.) Regarding the locally planar version, Hutchinson

[
11
]
proved that every locally planar even-sided graph on any
orientable surface is 3-colorable, with the constant
3
being best
possible. However, this result does not hold even for the projective
plane
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
, as demonstrated by Youngs in the following theorem

[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Youngs, 1996)
.

Every non-bipartite quadrangulation of the projective plane
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}

is
4
-chromatic.

This result implies that the projective plane admits non-3-colourable
even-sided graphs with arbitrarily large edge-width, highlighting a
significant difference between phenomena on orientable and
nonorientable surfaces. Therefore, Theorem
1.1
prompts
further in-depth studies on the colouring of even-sided graphs on
surfaces, as discussed in
[
3
,
14
,
15
]
.

In this paper, we consider higher-dimensional extensions of the notion
of quadrangulation, particularly in relation to their chromatic number.
One such extension was introduced by the first author and Stehlík
[
12
]
, as follows.

Let
{\cal K}
be a
d
-dimensional
generalized
simplicial
complex, where
d\geq 2
(in this generalized version, two simplices
may share the same vertex set). A

of

{\cal K}
is a spanning subgraph of the 1-skeleton
{\cal K}^{(1)}

of
{\cal K}
such that for every
d
-dimensional simplex
t
of

{\cal K}
, the intersection of
G
and
t
is a complete bipartite
graph of
d+1
vertices with at least one edge. (See Figure

1
.) When the underlying space of
{\cal K}
is homeomorphic
to a topological space
X
, we say

is a
KS-quadrangulation
of
X
. It is not hard to see that a
2-dimensional KS-quadrangulation coincides with an ordinary
quadrangulation of
X
.

The local structure of a
3
-dimensional
KS-quadrangulation. Left: A portion of the underlying simplicial

The following theorem, proved in
[
12
]
for KS-quadrangulations
of the
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
, extends
Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2

(Kaiser and Stehlík, 2015)
.

Let

, and let

be a non-bipartite KS-quadrangulation of
the
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
.
Then the chromatic number of

is at least
d+2
.

It is also shown in
[
12
]
that for any
d\geq 3
and

, the complete graph
K_{t}
on
t
vertices can be embedded
in
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
as a KS-quadrangulation provided that

is even. Thus,
there is no constant upper bound on the chromatic number of
KS-quadrangulations of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
.

On the other hand, Hachimori et al.
[
7
]
defined a
normal quadrangulation
of a
d
-dimensional manifold
X
as
the
1
-skeleton of a
cubical complex

{\cal C}
whose
underlying space is homeomorphic to
X
, that is, one each of
whose face is either a point, a segment, a quadrilateral, or more
generally a cube of any dimension. As in the case of
KS-quadrangulations, for
d=2
, a normal quadrangulation
G
of

X
coincides with an ordinary quadrangulation of
X
,
since
G
is a graph embedded in
X
such that every

2
-dimensional face is a quadrilateral.

Hachimori et al.
[
7
]
studied a particular type of
normal quadrangulations of the
d
-dimensional projective space

\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
, known as
zonotopal quadrangulations
(we refer to their
paper for the necessary definitions). In dimension
2
, zonotopal
quadrangulations of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
can be obtained from rhombic tilings of
regular polygons (cf.
[
8
]
).

The main result of
[
7
]
is the following.

Theorem 1.3

(Hachimori, Nakamoto, and Ozeki, 2025)
.

Let
d\geq 2
be any integer.
Then every non-bipartite zonotopal quadrangulation
of the
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
is
4
-chromatic.

This led the authors to ask if this phenomenon extended to general
normal quadrangulations of projective spaces.

Problem 1.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
be any integer.
Is every non-bipartite normal quadrangulation of the
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}

4
-chromatic?

In this paper, we give a negative solution to Problem
1.4
by proving the following theorem for
d=3
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
n\geq 1
, there is a normal quadrangulation of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{3}

whose
1
-skeleton contains the complete graph
K_{n}
.

We do not know whether Problem
1.4
holds for the case
d\geq 4
.
We leave this as a problem:

Problem 1.6
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
t\geq 4
be any integers.
Is there a non-bipartite normal quadrangulation
G
of the
d
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}

such that
G
is not
t
-colourable?

On the other hand, we prove that Theorem
1.1
extends at least partially to higher dimensions, in the following sense.

Theorem 1.7
.

No non-bipartite normal quadrangulation of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
for any
d\geq 2
is

3
-colourable.

The proof employs a novel application of intersection theory in homology, which is equivalently expressed in terms of the ring structure of cohomology groups.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we
prove Theorem
1.5
. In Section
3
, we provide
an alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
; extending this approach
to higher dimensions, we prove Theorem
1.7
in Section
4
,
which follows as a corollary (Corollary
4.3
) of a more general result (Theorem
4.1
).

Alternative proof of Youngs’ theorem

In this section,
we give an alternative proof for Youngs’ theorem (Theorem
1.1
),
which states that
no non-bipartite quadrangulation of the projective plane
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}

is
3
-colourable.
By extending the ideas in this proof,
we will prove Theorem
1.7
in the next section.

Alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-bipartite normal quadrangulation of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
,
that is,
a quadrangulation of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
in the ordinary sense.
Suppose that we are given a proper colouring

of
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
, the
1
-skeleton
of
\mathcal{C}
, with colours
\left\{{1,2,3,4}\right\}
. We will show that

For

, let

be the set of all edges of
\mathcal{C}

with their ends coloured either by
1
and

, or by the

Consider a fixed

. We begin by constructing

in

which are

.

. It is easy to
see that the boundary of any face

contains an even number of

. If it contains two, then we add a curve joining

. If it contains four, then we add two
disjoint non-crossing curves whose ends are these four points. There are two ways
to choose these curves; we choose one arbitrarily.

.

4
Higher-dimensional cases

This section is devoted to generalizing the argument in Section
3
to higher-dimensional cases and proving Theorem
1.7
.
Let
H_{k}(X)
(respectively,
H^{k}(X)
) denote the
k
th singular homology (respectively, cohomology) group of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
For
\alpha\in H^{i}(X)
and
\beta\in H^{j}(X)
, the cup product

\alpha\smile\beta\in H^{i+j}(X)
is defined, which makes

\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{k}(X)
into a ring.
See
[
4
, Chapter VI, Section 4]
for the definition.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
d\geq 2
, let
X
be a connected closed
d
-dimensional manifold satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

For any non-trivial element
\alpha\in H^{d-2}(X)
, there exists
\beta\in H^{2}(X)
such that
\alpha\smile\beta\neq 0
.

(2)

For any non-trivial elements
\alpha,\beta\in H^{1}(X)
, it holds that
\alpha\smile\beta\neq 0
.

Then, no non-bipartite normal quadrangulation of
X
is
3
-colourable.

Remark 4.2
.

Poincaré duality allows us to rephrase the above conditions as follows.

(1)

For any non-trivial element
\alpha\in H_{d-2}(X)
, there exists
\beta\in H_{2}(X)
such that
\alpha\cdot\beta\neq 0
.

(2)

For any non-trivial elements
\alpha,\beta\in H_{d-1}(X)
, it holds that
\alpha\cdot\beta\neq 0
.

Here,
\cdot
denotes the intersection between homology classes. If
submanifolds
Y
and
Z
intersect transversally, then

[Y]\cdot[Z]=[Y\cap Z]
holds, where
[Y]
denotes the fundamental
class of
Y
. See
[
4
, Chapter VI, Section 11]
for details.

The
d
-dimensional real projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
satisfies the above conditions since its cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d})
is isomorphic to the quotient
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w]/(w^{d+1})
of a polynomial ring (see
[
4
, Chapter VI, Proposition 10.2]
for instance).
For
p\geq 2
and integers
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
relatively prime to
p
, the lens space
L(p;q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
is defined, for example,
L(2;1,\dots,1)=\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2n-1}
.
See
[
4
, Chapter III, Example 4.7]
for the precise definition.
If
p
is even, then
H^{\ast}(L(p;q_{1},\dots,q_{n}))
is isomorphic to
H^{\ast}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d})
as rings.
Therefore, we obtain the next result as a consequence of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

No non-bipartite normal quadrangulations of
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
and
L(p;q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
for
p
even is
3
-colourable.

In contrast, for a closed surface
\Sigma
, the cohomology ring of the closed
3
-manifold
\Sigma\times S^{1}
does not satisfy the condition (2), and we can easily find a non-bipartite normal quadrangulation of the
3
-torus
T^{2}\times S^{1}
whose
1
-skeleton is
3
-colourable.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a normal quadrangulation of a closed
d
-manifold
X
.
We write
\mathcal{C}^{(k)}
for the
k
-skeleton of
\mathcal{C}
.

Throughout the rest of this section, we assume that a colouring of
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
with colours
1,\dots,4
is
given.
We obtain “hypersurfaces”
H_{2}
,
H_{3}
, and
H_{4}
as in Section
3
.
More precisely, we consider
d\cdot 2^{d-1}

(d-1)
-dimensional cubes in
[0,1]^{d}
obtained from the cubes
[0,1]^{k-1}\times\{1/2\}\times[0,1]^{d-k}
(
k=1,\dots,d
) by cutting them along their intersections.
For each edge of
[0,1]^{d}
, there is a unique small hyperplane intersecting the edge.
We now define
H_{t}
to be the union of small hyperplanes
H
in
\mathcal{C}
such that the ends of the edge corresponding to
H
are colored by
1
and
t
, where
t\in\{2,3,4\}
.

Remark 4.4
.

The proof does not assume that our normal quadrangulations are combinatorial
cubulations (i.e., that the link of each
k
-cube is a
piecewise-linear
(d-k-1)
-sphere). See
[
6
, Section 2]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quadrangulation of a closed
d
-manifold
X
satisfying the condition
(1)
, where
d\geq 2
.
Let
Y
be a closed
(d-2)
-submanifold with
Y\cap\mathcal{C}^{(2)}=\emptyset
.
Then, the homology class
[Y]\in H_{d-2}(X)
is trivial.

Proof.

The assumption
Y\cap\mathcal{C}^{(2)}=\emptyset
implies that there exists
a neighborhood
U
of
\mathcal{C}^{(2)}
disjoint from
Y
. Assume
[Y]

is non-trivial for a contradiction. Then the condition (1) implies
that there is
\beta\in H_{2}(X)
with
[Y]\cdot\beta\neq 0
. By

[
17
]
, we have a submanifold
\Sigma
representing
\beta
,
i.e.,
[\Sigma]=\beta
. Note here that for any point
p
in the
interior of a
k
-dimensional disk
D^{k}
, the complement

D^{k}\setminus\{p\}
deformation retracts to
\partial D^{k}=S^{k-1}
.
Thus, by an isotopy, we may assume
\Sigma
lies in a neighborhood
of
\mathcal{C}^{(d-1)}
. Continuing this process, we deform
\Sigma
so
that it is contained in the neighborhood
U
since
\Sigma
is of
dimension
2
. Hence,
Y\cap\Sigma=\emptyset
, and thus,

[Y]\cdot\beta=0
. This is a contradiction.
∎

For example, in the case
X=\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
, the above submanifold
\Sigma
can be chosen as
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}
embedded in a standard way.

Lemma 4.6
.

For
k\geq 2
, let
Y
be a
(
possibly disconnected
)
closed
(k-1)
-submanifold of
S^{k}=\partial D^{k+1}
.
Then,
Y
bounds a properly embedded compact
k
-manifold in
D^{k+1}
, that is, there exists a compact submanifold
W
of
D^{k+1}
satisfying
\partial W=Y
and
W\cap S^{k}=\partial W
.

Proof.

Let
Y=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, where each
Y_{i}
is connected and

\bigsqcup
denotes disjoint union.
Let
\widehat{Y}_{i}
be one of the two connected components of
S^{k}\setminus Y_{i}
.
Note that if
\widehat{Y}_{i}\cap\widehat{Y}_{j}\neq\emptyset
, then either
\widehat{Y}_{i}\subseteq\widehat{Y}_{j}
or
\widehat{Y}_{i}\supseteq\widehat{Y}_{j}
holds.
We may assume that if
\widehat{Y}_{i}\supseteq\widehat{Y}_{j}
, then
i<j
.
Let
W_{i}=\widehat{Y}_{i}\times\{i/n\}\cup Y_{i}\times[i/n,1]
in the cone
D^{k+1}=(S^{k}\times[0,1])/(S^{k}\times\{0\})
.
By the construction,
W_{i}
are disjoint submanifolds of
D^{k+1}
and
W=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}
is a desired submanifold.
∎

For each
i\in\{2,3,4\}
, let us construct a closed

(d-1)
-submanifold
Y_{i}
of
X
from
H_{i}
. The construction is as
follows. We first fix a way of resolving self-intersections of

H_{i}\cap\mathcal{C}^{(2)}
on every
2
-cell by changing the intersecting
curves to disjoint ones, as done in Section
3
. Then, for
each
3
-cell
C^{3}
, we have a (possibly disconnected) closed

1
-submanifold
Y^{1}
of
\partial C^{3}\cong S^{2}
. By
Lemma
4.6
,
Y^{1}
bounds a
2
-manifold
W^{2}
in
C^{3}
.
Then, for each
4
-cell
C^{4}
, we have a (possibly disconnected)
closed
2
-submanifold
Y^{2}
of
\partial C^{4}\cong S^{3}
. Indeed, in

\partial C^{4}
, there are exactly two
3
-cells
C^{3}_{1}
and
C^{3}_{2}

containing a
2
-cell
C^{2}
, and
W^{2}_{1}\subset C^{3}_{1}
and

W^{2}_{2}\subset C^{3}_{2}
are glued together along

W^{2}_{1}\cap C^{2}=W^{2}_{2}\cap C^{2}
. We can continue this process and
obtain a closed
(d-1)
-submanifold
Y^{d-1}
of
X
and let

Y_{i}=Y^{d-1}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

The homology class
[Y_{i}]
is non-trivial in
H_{d-1}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for
i\in\{2,3,4\}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
is non-bipartite, there exists an odd cycle

\gamma
.
By the construction of
Y_{i}
(
i\in\{2,3,4\}
), the intersection of
\gamma
and
Y_{2}\cup Y_{3}\cup Y_{4}
consists of odd number of transverse double points.
Therefore,
[\gamma]\cdot([Y_{2}]+[Y_{3}]+[Y_{4}])=1
in
H_{0}(X)=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
On the other hand, by considering a graph homomorphism
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}\to K_{4}
induced from the
4
-coloring, we have
[\gamma]\cdot([Y_{2}]+[Y_{3}])=0
. It follows that
[\gamma]\cdot[Y_{4}]=1
.
A similar
argument applies to
Y_{2}
and
Y_{3}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We prove in fact that for any distinct
i,j\in\{2,3,4\}
, there exists a
2
-cube
C
such that two curves
H_{i}\cap C
and
H_{j}\cap C
intersect transversally at a point in
C
.
Suppose, for a contradiction, that there is no such
2
-cube
C
for some
i,j
.
Then,
(Y_{i}\cap Y_{j})\cap\mathcal{C}^{(2)}=\emptyset
.
Here we may assume
Y_{i}
and
Y_{j}
intersect transversally.
By Lemma
4.5
, we have
[Y_{i}\cap Y_{j}]=0\in H_{d-2}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d})
.
On the other hand, Lemma
4.7
and the condition (2) imply
[Y_{i}]\cdot[Y_{j}]\neq 0
.
This is a contradiction.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the vertices of the cube
C
have all four colours.
This proves Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

In the case
X=\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
, we further show that there exists a
3
-cube such that all of the four colours appear on its vertices.
Indeed, if there is no such a
3
-cube, then
(Y_{2}\cap Y_{3}\cap Y_{4})\cap\mathcal{C}^{(3)}=\emptyset
.
Hence,
[Y_{2}]\cdot[Y_{3}]\cdot[Y_{4}]=0\in H_{d-3}(\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{d})
, which contradicts
w^{3}\neq 0
.

Abstract
For asynchronous transmission of short blocks, preambles for packet detection contribute a non-negligible overhead. To reduce the required preamble length, JDD ( JDD ) techniques have been proposed that additionally utilize the payload part of the packet for detection. In this paper, we analyze two instances of JDD , namely HyPED ( HyPED ) and DAD ( DAD ). While HyPED combines the preamble with energy detection for the payload, DAD also uses the output of a channel decoder. For these systems, we propose novel achievability and converse bounds for the rates over the BI-AWGN ( BI-AWGN ) channel. Moreover, we derive a general bound on the required blocklength for JDD . Both the theoretical bound and the simulation of practical codebooks show that the rate of DAD quickly approaches that of synchronous transmission.

II-A

System Model and Performance Metrics

We consider a slotted asynchronous system divided into time slots of length
n
.
For every slot, the transmitter decides its state; either it is active and
m\in\mathcal{M}\triangleq\left\{1,...,M\right\}
, or it is idle and
m=\O
.
When being active, a message
m
is uniformly drawn from
\mathcal{M}
and mapped to a codeword
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
Subsequently,
\mathbf{c}
is modulated with
BPSK
(
BPSK
) to
\mathbf{x}_{m}
.
Thus, the information rate is
R=\log_{2}(M)/n
.
If the transmitter is idle (
m=\O
), the channel input is the all-zero vector
\mathbf{x}_{\O}=\mathbf{0}
denoted as an empty slot.
The observations at the receiver after addition of the
AWGN
noise
z\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\sigma^{2}\right)
with
SNR
(
SNR
)
E_{\mathrm{S}}/N_{0}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}
is called
\mathbf{y}
.
The receiver estimates for each slot whether the transmitter has been idle,
\hat{m}=\O
, or active,
\hat{m}\in\mathcal{M}
(detection).
If a transmission is detected, the receiver estimates the message
\hat{m}\in\mathcal{M}
(decoding).

The performance of the system is measured according to the following error rates.
The false alarm rate
P_{\mathrm{FA}}
and missed detection rate
P_{\mathrm{MD}}
describe the detection problem and are defined as

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{FA}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\hat{m}=m\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right\},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MD}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\hat{m}=\O\mid\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\}.

The false alarm rate is the probability that the receiver detects a transmission even though the slot was empty.
Similarly, the missed detection rate is the probability of a slot containing a transmission being mistaken for an empty slot averaged over all transmissions.
Note that
P_{\mathrm{MD}}
and
P_{\mathrm{FA}}
oppose each other.
If one decreases, the other increases, resulting in a trade-off.
The optimal
P_{\mathrm{MD}}
for
P_{\mathrm{FA}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}
, given the constraint
\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}
, is described by the Neyman-Pearson lemma
[
13
]

\min_{\text{s.t. }P_{\mathrm{FA}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}}P_{\mathrm{MD}}.

The resulting decision rule is the likelihood ratio test

\frac{\overbrace{\textstyle\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathrm{p}\left(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}}\right)}^{\text{codebook likelihood}}}{\mathrm{p}\left(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right)}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\O\\
\lessgtr\\
\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\gamma,

(1)

where the threshold
\gamma
is chosen such that
P_{\mathrm{FA}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}
.

For the decoding problem, given an active transmission, the codeword error rate
P_{\mathrm{CW}}
is defined as the probability that the decoding rule selects the wrong codeword

P_{\mathrm{CW}}\triangleq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\hat{m}\neq m\mid\mathbf{x}_{m},\hat{m}\neq\O\right\}.

(2)

Finally, the inclusive error rate
P_{\mathrm{IE}}
measures jointly the detection and decoding problem.
It is defined as the average probability that the receiver either missed a detection or decoded the incorrect message

P_{\mathrm{IE}}\triangleq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\hat{m}\neq m\mid\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\}.

(3)

Observe that in equation (
3
), the case
\hat{m}=\O
is included, while for equation (
2
),
\hat{m}\neq\O
.

II-B

Decoder-aided Detection and Decoding

We first consider the optimal solution of the
JDD
problem, i.e., the decision rule such that no other decision rule leads to strictly lower error rates
P_{\mathrm{FA}},P_{\mathrm{MD}}
, and

simultaneously.
In
[
11

the optimal decision rule for the joint detector and decoder is given by the threshold test

\frac{\gamma_{a}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathrm{p}\left(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}}\right)+\max_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}})}{\mathrm{p}\left(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right)}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\O\\
\lessgtr\\
\hat{m}\end{subarray}}\gamma_{b},

(4)

where

is the
ML
(

) codeword, and

and

and

That means, the difference to the optimal detection rule in equation (
1

In other words, minimizing

means that we only detect a transmission if we can decode it with a sufficiently high probability.
Since

is computed for every codeword in the codebook, we label it codebook-aided detection.
A simplified decision rule is
DAD
(
DAD
)
[
12

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}=\frac{\max_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\O\\
\lessgtr\\
\hat{m}\end{subarray}}\gamma^{\prime}

(5)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{BI-AWGN}}}{{\iff}}

\displaystyle\max_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{y}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\O\\
\lessgtr\\
\hat{m}\end{subarray}}\gamma.

Note that for

or

(independent of

), the correlation follows a Gaussian distribution according to

\ln\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}~\propto~\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{y}\sim\begin{cases}\mathcal{N}(n,n\sigma^{2}),&\hat{m}=m\in\mathcal{M}\\
\mathcal{N}(0,n\sigma^{2}),&m=\O.\end{cases}

(6)

The distribution for all other

is dependent on the codebook.
Equation (
5
) is equal to equation (
4

.
Using the same approximation in equation (
1

Note, in cases where finding the

III-A

Converse Bound on the Required Blocklength

Theorem 1

For a system with noise variance
\sigma^{2}
and required error rates
P_{\mathrm{FA}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}
and
P_{\mathrm{MD}}\leq\epsilon_{\mathrm{MD}}
, the blocklength
n
is lower bounded by

n\geq\sigma^{2}\,\cdot\left(Q^{-1}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}})-Q^{-1}(1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{MD}})\right)^{2}.

(8)

Proof:

Assume a system with a genie-aided receiver, that knows the message that will be transmitted the next time the transmitter is active.
Then, the optimal detector from equation (
1
) simplifies to a likelihood ratio test

\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\O\\
\lessgtr\\
m\end{subarray}}\tilde{\gamma},

(9)

where
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathbb{R}
is the decision threshold.
Note that, even though the system models the detection and decoding problem, the genie removes the decoding part of the problem.
Thus, in order to achieve the optimal
P_{\mathrm{FA}}
and
P_{\mathrm{MD}}
trade-off, equation equation (
1
) is used.

Consequently, the false alarm and missed detection rate can be calculated as

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{FA}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:conv_bound_llr_test})}}{{=}}\operatorname{Pr}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}>\tilde{\gamma}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_lrt_normal})}}{{=}}Q\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MD}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:conv_bound_llr_test})}}{{=}}\operatorname{Pr}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}\leq\tilde{\gamma}\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_lrt_normal})}}{{=}}1-Q\left(\frac{\gamma-n}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right).

By inserting
P_{\mathrm{FA}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}
and
P_{\mathrm{MD}}=\epsilon_{\mathrm{MD}}
, this can be rearranged to obtain equation (
8
).
∎

This bound on the blocklength can also be understood as the minimum length for which the detection problem with respect to the requirements
\epsilon_{\mathrm{FA}}
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{MD}}
can be solved. As no data is being sent, the receiver is either idle or transmits a fixed sequence which can be optimally detected by the receiver via a matched filter, i.e., using correlation.

III-B

Achievability Bound for Decoder-aided Detection

When the detector applies the
DAD
scheme, the following achievability bound holds.

Theorem 2

For a system detecting according to equation (
5
) with blocklength
n
and noise power
\sigma^{2}
, there exists a code of size
M
with

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{FA}}

\displaystyle\leq M\cdot Q\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MD}}

\displaystyle\leq 1-Q\left(\frac{\gamma-n}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{IE}}

\displaystyle\leq P_{\mathrm{MD}}+P_{\mathrm{CW}},

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is a fixed decision threshold and
P_{\mathrm{CW}}
is bounded by an achievable error rate for synchronous transmission.

Proof:

We prove the achievability bound by upper bounding
P_{\mathrm{FA}}
,
P_{\mathrm{MD}}
and
P_{\mathrm{IE}}
. Applying the
UB
(
UB
) to
P_{\mathrm{FA}}
results in

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{FA}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_sub_optimal_JDD})}}{{=}}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}\geq\gamma\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\exists\,m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}:\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}\geq\gamma\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{UB}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{m^{\prime}=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m^{\prime}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}\geq\gamma\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_lrt_normal})}}{{=}}\sum_{m=1}^{M}Q\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=M\cdot Q\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right).

(10)

Since
\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}})\geq\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{m})
,
P_{\mathrm{MD}}
can be bounded by

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MD}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_sub_optimal_JDD})}}{{=}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}<\gamma\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_lrt_normal})}}{{=}}1-Q\left(\frac{\gamma-n}{\sqrt{n\sigma^{2}}}\right).

(11)

Finally, an upper bound on the inclusive error rate can be obtained by again applying the

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{IE}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:dad_sub_optimal_JDD})}}{{=}}\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\frac{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\hat{m}})}{\operatorname{p}(\mathbf{y}\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O})}<\gamma~\lor~\hat{m}\neq m\;\middle|\;\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{UB}}}{{\leq}}P_{\mathrm{MD}}+P_{\mathrm{CW}}.

Since the preamble is known, we can rewrite for a

Without loss of generality, we set the preamble

With the prior probabilities given as
\mathrm{Pr}\left\{x_{\mathrm{c},i}=+1\right\}=p
and
\mathrm{Pr}\left\{x_{\mathrm{c},i}=-1\right\}=1-p
, the
LLR
for the codeword symbols can be calculated as in
[
5
]

\displaystyle\ell(y_{\mathrm{c},i})

\displaystyle=\ln{\left[p\exp\left(\frac{y_{\mathrm{c},i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)+(1-p)\exp\left(-\frac{y_{\mathrm{c},i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]}-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}.

The total
LLR
can then be computed as

\displaystyle\ell(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\ln\left[p\exp\left(\frac{y_{\mathrm{c},i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)+(1-p)\exp\left(-\frac{y_{\mathrm{c},i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\mathrm{p}}-1}\frac{y_{\mathrm{p},i}}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}.

To simplify, we assume equal priors, i.e.,
p=\frac{1}{2}
. This is motivated by the observation that skewing the input distribution reduces the decoding performance significantly, while the detection performance is only slightly improved. Then, we can simplify the
LLR
to

\ell(\mathbf{y})=\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\ln\left[\cosh\left(\frac{y_{\mathrm{c},i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\right]+\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\mathrm{p}}-1}\frac{y_{\mathrm{p},i}}{\sigma^{2}}-\frac{n}{2\sigma^{2}}.

With the obtained
LLR
, the Neyman-Pearson decision rule can be applied to perform detection. The performance of the receiver is then

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{FA}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\ell(\mathbf{y})\geq\gamma\mid\mathbf{x}_{\O}\right\},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{MD}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\ell(\mathbf{y})<\gamma\mid\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\},

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{IE}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Pr}\left\{\ell(\mathbf{y})<\gamma~\lor~\hat{m}\neq m\mid\mathbf{x}_{m}\right\}.

Both achievability and converse bounds can be calculated by Monte Carlo simulation for the false alarm and missed detection rate. The inclusive error rate is bounded by

\max\left\{P_{\mathrm{MD}},P_{\mathrm{CW}}\right\}\leq P_{\mathrm{IE}}\leq P_{\mathrm{MD}}+P_{\mathrm{CW}}.

(12)

For the decoding error rate
P_{\mathrm{CW}}
, finite-blocklength bounds for synchronous communication can be used, e.g. from
[
17
]
.

A few remarks on the new
HyPED
detection rule:

•

In order to reduce the computational complexity, the decision regions can be approximated by a function
f(\mathbf{y})
that is chosen such that
\left|f(\mathbf{y})-\sum_{i}\ln[\cosh{(y_{i})}]\right|
is small on average.

•

The heuristic
HyPED
rule
[
3
, Sec. 4B]
in equation (
7
) is such an approximation, where
f(\mathbf{y})=\|\mathbf{y}\|
.

•

The
HyPED
approach yields a better result than preamble based detection, as
\ell(\mathbf{y}_{\mathrm{c}})
provides additional information about the transmitter state.

IV-B

Performance of Actual Systems

To give an intuition of how actual systems perform in relation to the bounds, we construct practical preamble-based and
JDD
systems for the discussed detection rules and obtain their operating points using Monte Carlo simulation. The decoding is performed using the
ML
decision rule.

Note that for
DAD
, the code influences not only the decoding but also the detecting performance. In particular, optimizing the code for a low decoding error rate spaces all codewords as distant from each other as possible, while optimizing with respect to a good detection performance yields a code with closely clustered codewords. Thus, a trade-off has to be made when designing the code used for
JDD
.
However, designing such optimized codes is out of scope of this paper and, hence, we employ codes optimized for the decoding problem only, i.e., solely maximizing their minimum distance.

For Fig.

V
Conclusion and Outlook

For a slotted asynchronous system with transmission over the
BI-AWGN
channel, we have shown new bounds for the
JDD
problem in the finite blocklength regime.
Furthermore, we demonstrated that
JDD
systems using
DAD
or
HyPED
outperform preamble-based detection and yield bounds suggesting higher rates than
PBD
with
i.i.d.
priors. Especially
DAD
performs well, approaching the achievable information rate for synchronous transmission quickly.

Remaining open problems are finding short-blocklength bounds for optimal detection and decoding and a converse bound on the information rate of
DAD
.

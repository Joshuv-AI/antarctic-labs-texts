Design and preparation of molecular perovskite-based energetic composite Al@PAP-5 with excellent combustion characteristics
Authors: Zebing Wang, Wei Liu, Debin Yang, Xiaojun Wang, Zhongliang Ma, Jianlong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The assembly of novel high-energy-density compounds with Al powder is considerably important for enhancing and regulating the combustion characteristics of metastable intermolecular composites (MICs). In this study, the molecular perovskite energetic material (H2pz)[Ag(ClO4)3] (PAP-5) was employed as a high-energy component to prepare Al@PAP-5 composites via spray-drying. The effect of the Al/O mass ratio on their micromorphology, thermal properties, and combustion performance was then investigated. The results show that PAP-5 and Al are successfully assembled to form a composite microsphere structure, in which Al exerts a significant catalytic effect on the thermal decomposition of PAP-5, reducing its thermal decomposition peak temperature from 341.9 °C to 310 °C. In addition, a combustion performance test shows that Al@PAP-5 exhibits optimal performance at an Al/O mass ratio of 40 : 60, with a combustion reaction heat and burn rate of 9162 J g−1 and 5.03 cm s−1, respectively. Mechanical sensitivity tests showed that, compared with pure PAP-5, the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) composite exhibited significantly reduced impact sensitivity and friction sensitivity, with the values decreasing from 68% to 2% and from 45% to 5%, respectively, indicating a substantial improvement in safety. This study expands the application scope of perovskite energetic materials and provides a guideline for improving and regulating the burn rate of MICs.

Introduction

Metastable intermolecular composites (MICs) are widely used in micro ignition devices, pyrotechnics, and composite explosives owing to their high energy density, tunable composition, and small critical reaction propagation size.1–5 Conventional MICs are usually assembled from Al and metal oxides such as CuO,6,7 Fe2O3,8 and Co3O4 (ref. 9 and 10) to form diverse structures such as nanorods, three-dimensional ordered macropores, and core–shell architectures. The oxidizers and fuels in MICs release considerable heat via redox reactions; consequently, MICs typically exhibit high energy density. However, traditional MICs produce limited gaseous products, and their reactions are limited by solid-phase mass transfer, which hinders improvements in the reaction rate and energy-release efficiency.11–15 To address these issues, researchers have attempted to combine Al with high-energy explosives, preparing novel MICs such as Al@RDX,16 Al@HMX,17 and Al@CL-20.18,19 The introduction of high-energy explosives can substantially increase reaction rates and energy output, which is important for regulating the combustion performance and energy-release characteristics of MICs.

Molecular perovskite energetic materials are a new class of high-energy explosives formed by the orderly assembly of organic fuel cations and high-energy oxidizing anions through ionic bonds. They offer advantages such as high energy, high density, good thermal stability, and low cost.20–26 Replacing traditional oxidants with energetic perovskite materials in Al-based systems is highly beneficial for improving the combustion behavior and energy performance of energetic composites. For instance, Deng et al.27 and Fang et al.28 prepared Al-based composites by mechanically mixing (H2dabco)[NH4(ClO4)3] (DAP-4) as a high-energy oxidizer with Al powder. The resulting composites exhibited excellent combustion performance, demonstrating the significant potential of DAP-4 as an oxidizer for solid propellants. Nevertheless, current studies primarily focus on the composites of DAP-4 with metal powders; the use of other novel molecular perovskites as oxidants in MICs remains underexplored.

In this study, a novel high-energy perovskite was assembled with Al powder via a spray-drying process to prepare Al@PAP-5 (PAP-5 = (H2pz)[Ag(ClO4)3]) energetic composites with excellent combustion characteristics. PAP-5 was selected as the oxidizing component owing to its superior comprehensive properties compared with DAP-4.29 In the Ag-based molecular perovskite explosive PAP-5, protonated piperazine (H2pz2+) serves as the A-site organic cation, Ag+ as the B-site cation, and ClO4− as the bridging anion. Its theoretical detonation velocity, detonation pressure, and oxygen balance are 8.961 km s−1, 42.4 GPa, and −3.2%, respectively, all of which are higher than those of DAP-4 (8.806 km s−1, 35.2 GPa, and −27.9%). Furthermore, existing methods for preparing DAP-4/Al composites primarily rely on physical mixing. Although this method is simple to operate, it often results in uneven dispersion of the composite components, preventing uniform coating of the fuel by the oxidiser. In addition, composite fuels prepared by physical mixing generally exhibit irregular morphologies, which restrict the heat and mass transfer efficiency during combustion, and hinder the improvement of reaction rate and energy release performance simultaneously.30–33 In contrast, the spray drying method is simple and cost-effective. It enables uniform and intimate mixing of the composite components at the micro- and nanoscale, forming a core–shell structure with tight interfacial contact.34,35 By changing the ratio of Al and PAP-5, the morphology and particle size of the energetic composite can be flexibly controlled, thereby controlling the energy release efficiency of MICs. Therefore, this study provides an experimental basis for regulating the combustion characteristics and improving the performance of MICs.

Experimental section

Materials

Silver perchlorate was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Piperazine and Al powder were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. Perchloric acid (70 wt%) was acquired from Chengdu Kelong Chemical Co. All chemicals were used as received, unless otherwise stated.

Preparation procedures

Preparation of PAP-5

The energetic perovskite PAP-5 was synthesized via molecular self-assembly.36 First, 1.72 g of silver perchlorate (8.3 mmol), 12 mL of HClO4 (70 wt%), and 20 mL of deionized water were added to a three-neck flask and mixed uniformly under magnetic stirring. Then, 0.8 g of piperazine (9.3 mmol) was dissolved in 10 mL of deionized water. Subsequently, the aqueous piperazine solution was slowly added to the three-neck flask under stirring, and the reaction mixture was heated at 80 °C for 60 min. Finally, the reaction solution was filtered and washed with anhydrous ethanol three times to obtain PAP-5 as a white solid.

Preparation of the Al@PAP-5 energetic composite

The Al@PAP-5 energetic composite was prepared via spray-drying, according to the procedure shown in Fig. 1. Under a typical Al/PAP-5 mass ratio of 40 : 60, the preparation procedure for the Al@PAP-5 energetic composite was as follows. First, 1.8 g of PAP-5 was dissolved in 80 mL of anhydrous methanol. Second, 1.2 g of Al powder was added to the resulting solution and uniformly dispersed under ultrasonic treatment to form a suspension. Third, the suspension was taken to a spray dryer using a peristaltic pump at a flow rate of 15 mL min−1. The fan rate of the spray dryer was 70 m3 min−1, and the chamber temperature was set to 100 °C. Finally, the Al@PAP-5 energetic composite with an Al content of 40 wt% was collected in a glass bottle after passing through a cyclone separator for subsequent characterization and analysis. To investigate the effect of the Al/O mass ratio (mass ratio of Al to PAP-5 in formula design before spray drying) on the properties of the energetic composites, Al@PAP-5 energetic composites with Al contents of 20, 30, 50, 60, 70, and 80 wt% were also prepared. The preparation procedure was similar to that described above, except that the masses of PAP-5 and Al were adjusted accordingly.

Testing and characterization

Material characterization

The composite morphology was analyzed via scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Zeiss Sigma 300) coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). Particle size was measured using a BT-9000s laser particle size analyser with ethanol as the dispersion medium. A small amount of sample was mounted on conductive adhesive tape, coated with gold, and observed and imaged at an accelerating voltage of 10 kV under a nitrogen atmosphere. Powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) analysis was performed on a Rigaku Miniflex 600 instrument using Cu Kα radiation, with a scanning range of 5–90° and a scanning rate of 10° min−1. Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy was conducted using a Thermo Scientific Nicolet iS20 spectrometer in the wavenumber range of 4000–400 cm−1. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) measurements were conducted using an HCT-1 integrated thermal analyzer; the sample was loaded into a ceramic crucible and heated from 50 °C to 800 °C at a heating rate of 10 °C min−1 under a nitrogen flow of 30 mL min−1.

Combustion performance test

The combustion heat of the sample was determined using a ZDHW-6 microcomputer-controlled automatic calorimeter. Briefly, 1 g of the sample was placed in an oxygen bomb, pressurized to 3 MPa with argon, and ignited with a Ni–Cr fuse, with the combustion heat automatically recorded by the calorimeter. A schematic of the sample ignition process is shown in Fig. 2. A groove with a length of 50 mm, a width of 1.5 mm and a depth of 1.5 mm was first prepared on a steel mold. Subsequently, 0.1 g of the sample was uniformly packed into the groove with a packing density of 0.889 g cm−3, and the sample was ignited with a Ni–Cr alloy wire. The combustion process was recorded using an ILME-FX2 high-speed camera at a frame rate of 2500 fps and a shutter speed of 1000 µs. The burning rate of energetic composites was calculated as v = L/t, where t denotes the propagation time of the flame front along a groove of fixed length L recorded by high-speed photography.

Schematic of the ignition process.

Sensitivity test

The impact sensitivity was measured using an HGZ-1 impact sensitivity tester via the probability of explosion method, under the following test conditions: a falling hammer mass of 2 kg, a drop height of 250 mm, and a test sample mass of 30 mg. The friction sensitivity was measured using a WM-1 friction sensitivity tester under the following conditions: a test sample mass of 20 mg, a pressure of 2.45 MPa, and a pendulum angle of 80°.

Results and discussion

Morphological characterization

The SEM images and EDS spectra of Al powder and the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) energetic composite are shown in Fig. 3. As presented in Fig. 3(a), the raw Al powder exhibits high sphericity, a smooth surface, and well-defined particle boundaries, with the particles well dispersed and distinct gaps between them. After spray drying, the composite morphology changes markedly. As illustrated in Fig. 3(b) and (c), the Al@PAP-5 composite has a rougher surface and is more agglomerated than the raw powder, with clearly visible large agglomerates and irregular particles, indicating successful deposition of PAP-5 onto the aluminum particles during spray drying. Higher-magnification images further reveal that the aluminum cores retain their spherical shape, while their surfaces are covered with flocculent and lamellar structures forming a dense, continuous coating layer. These morphological changes suggest enhanced interfacial contact between PAP-5 and the aluminum particles. Consistent with this, the EDS spectrum and elemental mapping of the composite (Fig. 3(d)) show that it mainly contains six elements (Al, Ag, Cl, C, N, and O), with Al and Ag contents of 39.70 wt% and 22.36 wt%, respectively. Notably, the elements characteristic of PAP-5 (Ag, Cl, C, N, and O) are uniformly distributed on the Al particle surface, confirming homogeneous deposition of PAP-5 throughout the composite. These results collectively demonstrate that PAP-5 has been uniformly anchored onto the Al surface, validating the successful fabrication of a stable Al@PAP-5 energetic composite via spray drying.

SEM images of (a) Al, (b and c) Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%), and (d) EDS spectrum of Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%).

The microstructure and morphology of Al@PAP-5 energetic composites with different Al/O mass ratios were characterized using SEM (Fig. 4). The micromorphology of the composites differs significantly from that of Al powder. Al powder displays clear contours and a predominantly spherical shape, with uniform distribution and low agglomeration. In contrast, the Al@PAP-5 composites show obvious interparticle agglomeration and form clusters of various sizes. This phenomenon can be attributed to the spray-drying process, during which the precursor suspension is atomized into fine droplets through the nozzle. As the solvent evaporates, PAP-5 precipitates and coats the Al particles, forming clusters with varying dimensions. Furthermore, the thickness of the coating layer varies with the PAP-5 content. As shown in Fig. 4(b)–(d), the composite particles retain distinct contours and high sphericity at high Al content, with an overall morphology close to that of Al powder. Fig. 4(e)–(h) show that the coating layer on the particle surface gradually becomes denser with decreasing Al content, accompanied by blurring of individual particle boundaries, a significant decrease in sphericity, and the formation of abundant irregular particles. Meanwhile, sample agglomeration is further intensified, and cluster size is evidently enlarged. The average particle sizes of the samples were characterized using a laser particle size distribution analyzer, and the results are listed in Table 1. The Al powder has an average particle size of 2.67 µm, while the Al@PAP-5 energetic composites exhibit average particle sizes ranging from 4.98 µm to 6.13 µm, representing a significant increase compared with the Al powder. Combined with the SEM images, the enlargement of particle size for the composites is attributed to the formation of a PAP-5 coating layer on the surface of Al particles after compounding Al with PAP-5 via spray drying, as well as the inter-particle agglomeration that forms particle clusters. These results indicate that the Al/O mass ratio exerts a remarkable influence on the composite micromorphology. With increasing PAP-5 content, the degree of sample agglomeration gradually increases, interparticle gaps become indistinct, and sphericity continuously decreases.

Composition analysis

The crystal phases and compositions of the energetic composites were further characterized using PXRD. The PXRD patterns are presented in Fig. 5(a). For the raw Al powder, the diffraction peaks located at 38.47°, 44.71°, 65.09°, 78.22°, and 82.40° are well matched with the standard data of Al (JCPDS 04-0787), corresponding to the (111), (200), (220), (311), and (222) crystal planes of the face-centered cubic structure of Al, respectively. These same diffraction signals can be observed in the PXRD pattern of the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) energetic composite. In addition, the diffraction peaks of pristine PAP-5 are consistent with those simulated for perovskite-structured PAP-5, as reported by Chen et al.36. These characteristic diffraction peaks are also identified in the PXRD pattern of Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%). Collectively, these results confirm the successful preparation of the Al@PAP-5 energetic composite.

(a) Powder X-ray diffraction spectra of raw Al powder, PAP-5, and the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) composite; (b) Fourier-transform infrared spectra of PAP-5 and the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) composite; (c) DSC curves of PAP-5, Al, and Al@PAP-5 at a heating rate of 10 °C min−1 in an argon atmosphere; (d) combustion heat of Al@PAP-5 in an argon atmosphere.

To further verify the composition and structural characteristics of the Al@PAP-5 energetic composite, FT-IR spectra of PAP-5 and the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) composite were recorded. The results are shown in Fig. 5(b). In the FT-IR spectrum of PAP-5, the characteristic absorption peaks at 1076, 625, and 568 cm−1 can be attributed to the vibrational characteristics of the perchlorate ion (ClO4−). The peaks at 863 and 927 cm−1 correspond to the bending vibrations of the C–N bonds in the piperazine ring. Furthermore, the peaks at 1307, 1398, 1432, and 1466 cm−1 correspond to the bending vibrations of C–H groups on the piperazine and skeletal ring vibrations; the peak at 1590 cm−1 represents the bending vibration peak of N–H bonds; and the peaks at 3046, 3124, and 3197 cm−1 correspond to the characteristic stretching vibrations of N–H groups. Importantly, the FT-IR spectrum of Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) exhibits the main characteristic absorption peaks of PAP-5, further confirming the successful preparation of the Al@PAP-5 composite.

Thermal analyses

Fig. 5(c) shows the DSC curves for the thermal decomposition of PAP-5, Al, and Al@PAP-5 at a heating rate of 10 °C min−1 in an inert argon atmosphere. The DSC curve of PAP-5 exhibits a distinct exothermic peak at 341.9 °C, consistent with previous reports. Al powder gives rise to a melting endothermic peak at 657.1 °C. After spray drying, the thermal decomposition behavior of the composites changes markedly. For the Al@PAP-5 energetic composites with different Al/O mass ratios, the thermal decomposition temperature is significantly reduced to approximately 310 °C compared with that of PAP-5, suggesting a catalytic effect of Al on the thermal decomposition of PAP-5.

Meanwhile, the melting endothermic peak of the Al component remains stable with increasing PAP-5 content. This phenomenon can be attributed to the significant reduction in heat and mass transfer distances between Al and PAP-5 upon their composite formation.37 During the heating process, PAP-5 decomposes and releases a large amount of oxidizing substances. At the Al@PAP-5 interface, Al acts as a strong reducing agent and electron donor to accelerate the decomposition of HClO4.38 Meanwhile, these oxidizing species undergo violent oxidation reactions with Al at the interface, releasing massive heat. The localized high-temperature hot spots generated by interfacial heat release further act on the undecomposed PAP-5 lattices, intensifying lattice vibration and destabilizing the ionic bonds within the PAP-5 framework.39 These results demonstrate that the incorporation of PAP-5 enhances the thermal reactivity of the composites while maintaining the thermal stability of the Al component.

Energy performance testing

The energy performance test results for the Al@PAP-5 energetic composites in an inert argon atmosphere are presented in Fig. 5(d). Among them, the combustion reaction heat of the pure PAP-5 is 7947 J g−1. However, after compounding PAP-5 with Al, the combustion reaction heat of the prepared samples showed significant differences. As the Al content increases, the combustion reaction heat first increases and then decreases. Specifically, when the Al content increases from 20 wt% to 40 wt%, combustion reaction heat gradually increases until reaching a maximum value of 9162 J g−1 at 40 wt% Al. Compared to the pure PAP-5, the heat of combustion has increased by approximately 15.3%, indicating that a moderate addition of Al enhances the combustion reaction heat of the system. Further increasing Al content from 40 wt% to 80 wt% causes a marked decrease in combustion reaction heat, which reaches a minimum value of 2604 J g−1 at 80 wt% Al. These results demonstrate that the component ratio of the Al@PAP-5 energetic composite significantly influences its combustion reaction heat, with the optimal Al/O mass ratio being 40 : 60, which is higher than the theoretical stoichiometric ratio of 30 : 70. This deviation can be attributed to two factors. On the one hand, the surface oxide layer on Al particles reduces the active Al content, requiring a higher amount of Al addition to meet the actual requirements of redox reactions. On the other hand, Al, as a high-energy metallic fuel, undergoes intense oxidation–reduction reactions with the oxygen-containing compounds released by the decomposition of PAP-5, thereby releasing more energy. At low Al content, in addition to the redox reaction between the oxidizer and Al during combustion, excess PAP-5 can decompose and release heat, thereby maintaining relatively high combustion heat. In contrast, when the Al content is excessively high, the oxidizer becomes insufficient, and excess metallic Al cannot fully participate in the redox reaction, resulting in decreased overall energy-release efficiency and significant reduction in the combustion heat of the composite. Moreover, an excessively high Al content may impair the uniform contact between the components within the composite, restricting heat transfer and reaction propagation, and further compromising the energy performance of the system. Compared with conventional high-energy oxidizer/Al systems reported in the literature, the optimally formulated Al@PAP-5 composite achieves a peak combustion heat (9162 J g−1) that is superior to those of RDX/Al (7945 J g−1) and HMX/Al (9017 J g−1), approximately 15.3% higher than that of AP/Al mixed powder (7943 J g−1), and approaches the level of the CL-20/Al system (9794 J g−1).26 These results demonstrate that PAP-5 offers significant advantages as a novel high-energy oxidizer, providing an important reference for the formulation design and energy regulation of Al@PAP-5 composites in the field of energetic materials.

Combustion performance test

The combustion behavior of the Al@PAP-5 energetic composites is presented in Fig. 6. When the Al content increases from 20 wt% to 50 wt%, the composites exhibit stable self-propagating combustion in the groove, accompanied by an intense flame and abundant sparks, with the flame intensity increasing substantially with increasing Al content. With further increases in Al loading, the flame intensity decreases remarkably. The composite with 60 wt% Al maintains self-propagating combustion, while intermittent combustion is observed for the composite with 70 wt% Al. Notably, continuous combustion in the groove ceases at an Al content of 80 wt%, and pure Al powder fails to ignite under the same conditions. These results demonstrate that the flame intensity and combustion rate of the composites depend on the Al/O mass ratio, and the incorporation of PAP-5 into Al powder can effectively facilitate the combustion of Al and establish a stable redox reaction system.

Combustion process of Al@PAP-5: (a) Al@PAP-5 (20 wt%), (b) Al@PAP-5 (30 wt%), (c) Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%), (d) Al@PAP-5 (50 wt%), (e) Al@PAP-5 (60 wt%), and (f) Al@PAP-5 (70 wt%).

The histogram of the combustion rate of the composites in the mold is shown in Fig. 7. As the Al content increases, the combustion rate first increases up to a maximum value of 5.03 cm s−1 at an Al content of 40 wt% and then decreases to a minimum value of 1.39 cm s−1 at 20 wt% Al. These results, combined with the combustion heat measurements and combustion process analysis, demonstrate that the Al@PAP-5 composite achieves superior energy-release efficiency at an Al/O mass ratio of 40 : 60. Therefore, the energy performance of the Al@PAP-5 composite can be effectively regulated by adjusting the Al/O mass ratio; these results provide theoretical and experimental guidance for the application of such materials in the field of energetic materials.

Mechanical sensitivity

Mechanical sensitivity is a key parameter for assessing the practical safety of high-energy materials. In this paper, the explosion probability method was employed to evaluate the impact sensitivity and friction sensitivity of pure PAP-5 and Al@PAP-5 composite energetic materials with the optimal ratio (Al : PAP-5 = 40 : 60), the results are shown in Fig. 8. The impact sensitivity and friction sensitivity of the pure PAP-5 are 68% and 45%, respectively. Notably, the impact sensitivity and friction sensitivity of Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) composites decrease to 2% and 5%, which are reduced by 97.1% and 88.9% in comparison with pure PAP-5, respectively. This remarkable improvement can be attributed to two factors. Firstly, Al powder possesses high thermal conductivity, which facilitates rapid heat dissipation, restrains local heat accumulation and further inhibits hot spot formation. Secondly, Al powder exhibits favorable ductility and exerts a certain buffering effect on the impact applied to the composites. The above results demonstrate that Al@PAP-5 composites achieve superior combustion performance and greatly enhanced mechanical safety, thus holding broad application prospects in the field of energetic materials.

Conclusions

In this study, Al@PAP-5 energetic composites were prepared via spray-drying, and the effect of the Al/O mass ratio on their micromorphology, thermal properties, and combustion performance was investigated. The results demonstrate that PAP-5 and Al are successfully assembled to form a composite microsphere structure. With increasing PAP-5 content, the coating layer on the Al surface becomes denser. In addition, Al powder exhibits a significant catalytic effect on the thermal decomposition of PAP-5, reducing its thermal decomposition peak temperature from 341.9 °C to 310 °C. Combustion tests show that the Al@PAP-5 composite exhibits optimal performance at an Al/O mass ratio of 40 : 60, with a combustion reaction heat and burn rate of 9162 J g−1 and 5.03 cm s−1, respectively. Furthermore, mechanical sensitivity tests indicated that, compared with pure PAP-5, the impact sensitivity of the Al@PAP-5 (40 wt%) composite decreased from 68% to 2%, whilst its friction sensitivity fell from 45% to 5%, highlighting its significantly improved mechanical safety for practical applications. In summary, this study expands the application scope of perovskite energetic materials and provides guidelines for improving and regulating the burn rate of MICs.

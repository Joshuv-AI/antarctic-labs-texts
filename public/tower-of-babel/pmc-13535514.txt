Prognostic Implications of Calcification and Surgery Type by Estrogen Receptor and HER2 Status in Ductal Carcinoma In Situ: Single-Center Study
Authors: Je Hyun Chin, Jin Ah Lee, Dooreh Kim, Young Joo Lee, Chang Ik Yoon, Woo-Chan Park, Soo Youn Bae
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeDuctal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) presents a challenge in risk stratification, raising concerns regarding both undertreatment and overtreatment. This study investigated the relationships among estrogen receptor (ER) status, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, clinicopathological features, and recurrence in DCIS, with a particular focus on the prognostic roles of calcification and surgical modality.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted in 365 patients who underwent surgery for DCIS at The Catholic University of Korea, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital between 1997 and 2020. Ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence (IBTR) and contralateral breast tumor recurrence (CBTR) were evaluated as the primary and secondary endpoints, respectively. Multivariable Cox models were implemented, incorporating radiotherapy, endocrine therapy, and margin status. 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) was performed to adjust for selection bias between breast-conserving surgery and mastectomy.ResultsER-negative and HER2-positive DCIS were associated with older age, more frequent calcification, and higher nuclear grade (all p < 0.001). In the multivariable analysis for IBTR, surgical modality (mastectomy: hazard ratio [HR], 0.021; p = 0.014) and margin status (≥ 2 mm: HR, 0.192; p = 0.013) were the only independent predictors. Conversely, the presence of calcification was independently associated with more favorable outcomes for CBTR (HR, 0.103; p = 0.013). Subgroup analysis revealed that calcification was significantly associated with improved CBTR-free survival, particularly in the estrogen receptor-positive (ER+) cohort (p = 0.010). PSM analysis confirmed that mastectomy significantly reduced IBTR risk (p = 0.038) but was not significantly associated with CBTR risk (p = 0.246).ConclusionCalcification and surgical modality demonstrated divergent prognostic implications for IBTR and CBTR according to molecular subtype. Although calcification is traditionally considered a high-risk feature prevalent in aggressive subtypes, its presence was associated with favorable contralateral events in ER+ DCIS. These findings emphasize the importance of integrating molecular markers with clinical features to support more personalized approaches to DCIS management.Keywords: Calcification, Physiologic; Carcinoma, Intraductal, Noninfiltrating; Disease-Free Survival; Erb-b2 Receptor Tyrosine Kinases; Receptors, Estrogen

INTRODUCTION

Ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) is an early-stage neoplastic lesion that accounts for 20%–25% of newly diagnosed breast cancers in the United States [1]. Although DCIS is recognized as a nonobligate precursor to invasive breast cancer, the specific process by which DCIS upstages to invasive disease remains poorly understood [2]. Prognosis remains challenging, as reflected by the wide variation in long-term recurrence rates. For instance, studies have shown a 10-year cumulative incidence of ipsilateral invasive breast cancer ranging from 1.6% to 7.1% depending on treatment modality [3], with a subsequent invasive cancer incidence reaching 19.0% at 20 years [4]. This prognostic ambiguity has contributed to current treatment guidelines recommending surgical excision, often supplemented with radiotherapy or endocrine therapy. However, this strategy is increasingly considered a major driver of overtreatment in many patients.

Consequently, several active surveillance trials are being conducted worldwide to address the overtreatment of DCIS [2,5,6,7,8]. Although various clinicopathological features such as comedo-type necrosis, high nuclear grade (NG), and pleomorphic calcifications have been associated with poorer outcomes and an increased risk of upstaging, robust evidence for reliable risk stratification remains limited [9,10,11,12]. For instance, although mammographic calcification facilitates the early detection of asymptomatic DCIS, its traditional link to an increased risk of recurrence is currently being reevaluated [13]. Furthermore, proposed evolutionary models of DCIS invasion suggest that the prognostic implications of established clinicopathological features may differ when analyzed across molecular subtypes of DCIS compared to invasive breast cancer [13].

This study aimed to investigate the molecular characteristics of DCIS to better understand its progression. We focused on the interactions between immunohistochemistry expressions, such as estrogen receptor (ER) and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, and key clinicopathological features such as age, symptoms, tumor size, presence of calcification, surgery modality, and NG. By analyzing these associations, we sought to identify reliable patterns that suggest an increased risk of recurrence or progression to invasive disease in patients with DCIS, and ultimately, to evaluate their potential implications for refining clinical management strategies.

METHODS

Study design and patient selection

We conducted a retrospective analysis using the Korean Breast Cancer Society registry database. The study cohort included all patients who underwent surgery for DCIS at The Catholic University of Korea, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital between January 1997 and December 2020. A total of 411 patients were initially screened, of whom 46 were excluded due to incomplete or missing data. Consequently, 365 patients were included in the analysis, of which 2 have underwent concurrent surgery for contralateral invasive cancer. The final follow-up date for all patients was April 1, 2025.

Data collection and molecular subtyping

Data collected included demographic and clinical information, such as patient age and symptoms at diagnosis. Symptoms were reclassified into two groups: asymptomatic and symptomatic. Symptomatic presentations included palpable lumps, pain, nipple retraction, skin changes or retraction, and nipple discharge. Tumor characteristics analyzed included size, NG, immunohistochemistry (IHC) status, and the presence and extent of calcification. Calcification presence was defined regardless of morphological risk and further categorized into high-risk calcification based on mammographic readings reported by radiologists. High-risk calcification was defined by the presence of the following keywords in radiology reports: amorphous, pleomorphic, fine linear, branching, clustered, segmental, suspicious, and Category 4 or 5 of the Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System. High-risk calcification features were analyzed in an exploratory analysis.

Surgical treatment modality included breast surgery type (breast-conserving surgery [BCS] or mastectomy), axillary surgery type, adjuvant radiotherapy, and endocrine therapy. The closest margin on specimen was based on the pathology report with a cutoff of 2 mm. For patients who underwent additional operation of margin excision and subsequently showed negative margins on final pathology, the closest margin was defined as ≥ 2 mm. Adjuvant endocrine therapy was subdivided into no endocrine therapy, completion of therapy, and discontinuation of therapy. Molecular subtypes were defined according to ER status and HER2 IHC status. HER2 positivity was defined as an IHC score of 3+ or an IHC score of 2+ with positive confirmation by fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) or silver in situ hybridization (SISH). HER2 negativity was defined as IHC scores of 0, 1+, and 2+ with negative FISH or SISH results.

Statistical analysis

Recurrence was defined as any type of pathologically confirmed breast tumor recurrence, including DCIS and invasive disease. The primary endpoint was ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence (IBTR), whereas contralateral breast tumor recurrence (CBTR) was included as a secondary endpoint. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the time from surgery to recurrence of any type or death from any cause.

For statistical comparisons, continuous variables (age at diagnosis and tumor size) were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test and analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Continuous variables are presented as medians and ranges. The distribution of categorical variables was analyzed using the χ2 test. To analyze recurrence distribution, Fisher’s exact test was employed when the expected cell counts were less than 5. The extent of calcification, which was non-normally distributed and analyzed only in calcification-positive patients, was compared across groups using the Kruskal-Wallis test.

Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method for survival curve estimation, and Cox proportional hazards regression models to calculate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for both univariable and multivariable analyses. To minimize potential selection bias between the two surgical groups, 1:1 propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using the nearest-neighbor matching method with a caliper of 0.2. The matching model incorporated seven covariates: age, tumor size, NG, presence of calcification, ER status, HER2 status, and endocrine therapy.

Given the biological heterogeneity of DCIS, we performed subgroup analyses stratified by ER and HER2 status to evaluate whether the prognostic significance of calcification varied across molecular subtypes. This approach was intended to prevent potential masking of subtype-specific effects and support more tailored risk stratification. All tests were two-sided, and p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM® SPSS® Statistics for Windows, version 30.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethical statement

All procedures performed in this study involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and/or national research committee and with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments or comparable ethical standards. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The Catholic University of Korea, Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital, and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to its retrospective nature (IRB approval No.: KC25ZASI0246).

Similarly, patients with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-positive (HER2+) DCIS (n = 129) had a higher median age (53 years) than those with HER2+ DCIS (n = 236, 50 years) (p = 0.028). HER2+ DCIS also exhibited a significantly higher rate of calcification (111/129, 86.0%) than human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HER2−) DCIS (149/236, 63.1%) (p < 0.001). Comparison of characteristics according to the presence of calcification

Independent prognostic factors for recurrence in patients with DCIS

To evaluate the impact of preoperative calcification within distinct biological environments, a survival analysis was performed after stratification according to receptor status. The median follow-up time was 101.0 months (95% CI, 96.4–105.7 months). Regarding ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence-free survival (IBTRFS), the presence of preoperative calcification did not show a significant prognostic impact in any subgroup, including ER+ (p = 0.691), ER− (p = 0.515), HER2+ (p = 0.734), and HER2− DCIS (p = 0.446; Figure 1).

Table 3 shows the independent prognostic factors for recurrence in the entire cohort. Surgical modality and margin status were the only independent predictors of IBTR. Total mastectomy was associated with a significantly lower risk of IBTR than BCS (HR 0.021; 95% CI 0.001–0.460, p=0.014). Additionally, achieving a resection margin ≥2 mm was a strong indicator of favorable outcomes (HR 0.192; 95% CI 0.053–0.702, p=0.013). Notably, biological factors, including ER status, HER2 status, and the presence of calcification, did not independently influence IBTR risk after adjusting for these treatment-related variables.

In contrast, the presence of preoperative calcification was identified as an independent prognostic factor for CBTR (HR, 0.103; 95% CI, 0.017–0.622; p = 0.013), showing a favorable outcome consistent with the Kaplan-Meier analysis. In contrast, tumor size was significantly associated with CBTR risk (HR, 1.609; 95% CI, 1.009–2.563; p = 0.046), whereas surgical modality, adjuvant radiotherapy, and endocrine therapy were not significantly associated with contralateral events.

Beyond the presence of calcification, we further investigated whether calcification morphology affected survival outcomes. When patients were categorized into three groups based on morphology (no calcification, low-risk, and high-risk), no significant differences were observed in either IBTRFS (log-rank p = 0.777) or CBTRFS (log-rank p = 0.179) among the groups (Supplementary Figure 1).

Comparison of survival outcomes between BCS and mastectomy after PSM

As shown in Supplementary Table 1, the 1:1 PSM process shows well-balanced baseline clinicopathological characteristics between the BCS and mastectomy groups. After matching, two comparable groups of 77 patients each were established, with no significant differences in age, tumor size, ER status, HER2 status, NG, presence of calcification, and endocrine therapy (all absolute standardized mean differences < 0.1). Although differences in axillary surgery and radiotherapy were not incorporated as matching variables, the primary oncological and biological covariates were well balanced. In the PSM cohort, mastectomy demonstrated a significant clinical advantage over BCS in preventing local recurrence. Figure 3 shows that IBTRFS was significantly higher in the mastectomy group than in the BCS group (log-rank p = 0.038). Conversely, surgical modality did not significantly affect the risk of contralateral events, as no statistically significant difference was observed in CBTRFS between the two surgical groups (log-rank p = 0.246). These findings suggest that the superior local control provided by mastectomy remained significant even after adjusting for the baseline clinical and pathological heterogeneity of DCIS.

Clinicopathological characteristics and recurrence patterns across molecular subgroups

Analyses of clinicopathological characteristics according to molecular subgroups revealed significant differences in age (p = 0.003), presence of calcification (p < 0.001), NG (p < 0.001), surgical modality (p = 0.035), and administration of endocrine therapy (p < 0.001). HER2+ subtypes (ER+HER2+ and ER−HER2+) showed a higher prevalence of preoperative calcification (82.4% vs. 90.2%) than the ER+HER2− (64.0%) and TNBC (54.5%) subgroups. High-grade tumors were most frequently observed in the ER−HER2+ subgroup (90.2%), followed by the TNBC (63.6%) and ER+HER2+ (63.2%) subgroups.

The pattern and frequency of recurrence also varied across molecular subgroups, with the highest recurrence rate observed in the ER−HER2+ subgroup (9.8%). Regarding the first recurrence location, the ER+HER2− subgroup predominantly experienced IBTR (85.7%), whereas the other subgroups exhibited a more balanced distribution between IBTR and CBTR. Furthermore, while most recurrences in the ER+ or HER2+ subgroups remained as DCIS, the recurrence event in the TNBC subgroup presented as invasive cancer. Detailed longitudinal tracking of individual recurrence pathways revealed diverse progression patterns, including shifts in receptor status and progression to invasive disease (Supplementary Tables 2, 3, 4).

DISCUSSION

This study investigated the differences in clinicopathological characteristics and recurrence patterns among patients with DCIS stratified according to ER and HER2 status to identify clinically actionable insights. Our findings showed that ER− and HER2+ DCIS were associated with classic poor prognostic indicators, including older age, more frequent calcification, and higher NG (Table 1). This tendency toward an unfavorable risk profile was numerically reflected in the lower CBTRFS observed in the Kaplan-Meier curves (p = 0.027 for ER status and p = 0.041 for HER2 status) (Supplementary Figure 2).

Specifically, HER2+ DCIS was more strongly associated with the presence of calcification than HER2− DCIS (86.0% vs. 63.1%, respectively, p < 0.001). However, the prognostic significance of HER2 status in DCIS remains controversial. Previous studies, such as the UK/ANZ Trial by Thorat et al. [14] and the work by Curigliano et al. [15], have reported that HER2 overexpression significantly increased the risk of ipsilateral breast events (HR, 2.29; 95% CI, 1.64–3.14; p < 0.001) and DCIS recurrence (HR, 1.59; 95% CI, 1.06–2.39; p = 0.010). In contrast, other studies have shown no significant association between HER2 status and recurrence rates [16,17]. In our cohort, HER2 status was not significantly associated with IBTRFS (p = 0.470), whereas a significant association was observed with CBTRFS (p = 0.041).

Multivariable Cox analyses for subgroups, stratified according to ER and HER2 status, were initially attempted but were ultimately limited by the low number of events, leading to model nonconvergence and instability. To ensure scientific integrity and minimize overfitting, multivariable interpretations were restricted to adequately powered whole-cohort models (Table 3). Subgroup recurrence patterns are instead provided as a descriptive summary in Supplementary Table 4 to offer clinical insights while acknowledging these statistical limitations.

Our findings further suggest that the prognostic implications of calcification may differ according to ER or HER2 status. Calcification is a central mammographic feature of DCIS and is instrumental in its diagnosis and surgical planning. Interestingly, our data presented a counterintuitive finding regarding its prognostic role. Although the presence of calcification was significantly associated with established markers of tumor aggressiveness, such as larger tumor size (p = 0.036) and higher NG (p < 0.001), its presence was associated with a trend toward better recurrence-free survival. However, only the ER+ subgroup showed statistical significance (p = 0.010). Although calcification often facilitates early detection, lead-time bias is unlikely to explain the favorable outcomes in our cohort. In fact, the calcification group was significantly associated with more aggressive features, including a larger tumor size (2.4 vs. 1.8 cm, p = 0.036) and higher NG (52.3% vs. 29.5%, p < 0.001). Furthermore, the lack of a significant difference in detection mode between the groups (p = 0.059) suggests that the observed prognostic advantage is independent of detection bias and likely reflects a distinct biological interplay.

This counterintuitive association was further supported by multivariable analysis, which showed that the presence of calcification was associated with a lower risk of CBTR (multivariable HR, 0.103; 95% CI, 0.017–0.622; p = 0.013).

Our results also demonstrated that while the presence of preoperative calcification serves as a significant prognostic indicator for CBTR, its specific radiologic morphology did not further stratify the risk of recurrence (log-rank p = 0.777 for IBTR and p = 0.179 for CBTR). This suggests that the clinical utility of calcification in DCIS may be more related to its role in delineating the extent of disease and guiding surgical planning, rather than to its role as a surrogate marker for early-stage or less aggressive disease. Supporting this interpretation, we observed a notable trend in which the proportion of total mastectomy was higher in patients presenting with high-risk calcification than in those with low-risk or no calcification (p = 0.081), reinforcing the influence of mammographic features on surgical decision-making. Collectively, these findings imply that while high-risk calcifications may prompt more extensive surgical intervention, they do not inherently confer a worse oncological outcome if adequate local control is achieved.

Our PSM analysis showed that total mastectomy was associated with significantly lower IBTR rates than BCS (p = 0.038). While this is a clinically expected outcome due to the minimal remaining breast tissue after mastectomy, the consistency of our data with this established trend supports the plausibility and robustness of our analytical model. Our results demonstrated that the type of surgery performed for primary DCIS was not significantly associated with the risk of contralateral events (p = 0.246). This finding clearly delineates the spatial limitation of local surgical control and underscores that CBTR risk should be evaluated and managed as an independent clinical entity, regardless of the initial surgical aggressiveness on the ipsilateral side.

Furthermore, a key finding of our matched multivariable analyses was that high-risk biological features did not necessarily indicate poor prognosis. Despite the prevalence of negative indicators such as high NG or ER− status, the overall survival outcomes remained favorable when appropriate local and systemic treatments, including mastectomy, radiotherapy, and endocrine therapy, were administered. This suggests that the inherently aggressive potential of DCIS may be effectively mitigated through optimized clinical management, supporting a strategy in which treatment intensification is tailored to overcome specific biological risks.

The uncertain nature of DCIS progression and recurrence necessitates the development of more precise prognostic tools. Although cutting-edge research, such as that of the Grand Challenge PRECISION Consortium, explores sophisticated models based on the crystallographic and chemical features of microcalcifications [13], our study contributes an equally valuable perspective. We focused on readily accessible and tangible clinical features such as calcification, ER and HER2 status, and surgical approach, which are directly relevant to a surgeon’s decision-making process.

Rather than proposing a new prognostic tool, such as the Van Nuys Prognostic Index, this study aimed to identify simple, clinically relevant predictors of DCIS recurrence and their clinical implications [18,19]. By focusing on these practical features, our findings offer immediate guidance for personalized treatment, thereby contributing to the essential effort to balance effective tumor control with a reduction in overdiagnosis and overtreatment.

In our analysis of molecular subtypes, we observed distinct clinicopathological landscapes. Notably, the ER−HER2+ subtype exhibited the most aggressive characteristics, including older age and higher NG, which frequently necessitated more extensive surgical interventions such as total mastectomy. This subgroup demonstrated the highest recurrence rate (9.8%); however, these differences were not statistically significant among the four molecular subtypes.

This lack of statistical divergence could be primarily attributed to the inherent nature of DCIS as a ‘rare-event’ disease, where further stratification into molecular subgroups significantly disperses the available cases and limits statistical power. However, these non-significant results paradoxically offer a profound clinical insight suggesting that contemporary multidisciplinary management successfully mitigates recurrence risk, even in biologically high-risk groups such as ER− or HER2+ DCIS. Consequently, while a tailored approach based on molecular subtypes remains essential for precise risk assessment, our findings underscore that the biological risks associated with radiologic features such as calcification may be effectively overcome through the diligent application of modern standard-of-care treatments. This observation reinforces the idea that optimized local and systemic control can improve the poor prognosis traditionally associated with aggressive DCIS phenotypes.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective nature and single-center design may introduce potential selection bias. Second, although the median follow-up of 101 months was substantial, it was shorter than the 20-year follow-up reported in some landmark studies, which may limit our ability to fully capture late-recurrence events. Third, the low incidence of recurrence events in specific molecular subgroups, such as only one event in the TNBC subgroup and four in the ER+HER2+ subgroup, posed significant statistical challenges. This sparse data issue led to nonconvergence and statistical instability in certain stratified multivariable models, which necessitated a primary focus on an adequately powered whole-cohort analysis to ensure scientific integrity. Consequently, while calcification and mastectomy generally resulted in better CBTRFS and IBTRFS, respectively, statistical significance was primarily observed for specific types of DCIS.

Despite these limitations, our research offers a unique and significant perspective on DCIS in the Korean population. This strongly supports the need for personalized treatment strategies, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach. Specifically, our data suggest that ER− or HER2+ DCIS may benefit from a more aggressive surgical approach, whereas the presence of calcification in ER+ or HER2− DCIS may serve as a favorable prognostic sign, perhaps due to earlier detection. Further prospective multicenter studies with longer follow-up and larger cohorts are essential to validate the findings associated with ER and HER2 status. Examining the robust interactions among molecular markers, the presence of calcification, and other clinicopathological outcomes is crucial in personalizing DCIS treatment.

In conclusion, this study identified distinct prognostic patterns in DCIS according to ER and HER2 status, highlighting the interplay between molecular subtype and the presence of calcification. Although calcification, which has traditionally been regarded as a high-risk feature, was prevalent in ER− and HER2+ DCIS, its presence was associated with more favorable CBTR outcomes in ER+ DCIS. These findings underscore the need for a more nuanced and personalized treatment approach for DCIS. Management strategies should be tailored according to the interplay between molecular markers and clinical features. Further long-term multicenter research with larger cohorts is warranted to validate these findings, ultimately aiming to reduce both recurrence rates and the critical burden of overtreatment.

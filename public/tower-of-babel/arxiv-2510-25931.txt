Adelic Mordell-Lang and the Brauer-Manin Obstruction
Brendan Creutz

Abstract
. Let X X be a closed subvariety of an abelian variety A A over a global function field k k such that the base change of A A to an algebraic closure does not have any positive dimensional isotrivial quotient. We prove that every adelic point on X X which is the limit of a sequence of k k -rational points on A A is a limit of k k -rational points on X X . Assuming finiteness of the Tate-Shafarevich group of A A , this implies that the rational points on X X are dense in the Brauer set of X X . Similar results are obtained over totally imaginary number fields, conditionally on an adelic Mordell-Lang conjecture.



1.
Introduction

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global field
k
. By the celebrated Mordell-Weil theorem the set
A(k)
of rational points on
A
is a finitely generated abelian group. Moreover, the standard arithmetic duality theorems describe, at least conjecturally, how
A(k)
sits inside the set
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
of adelic points. The topological closure
\overline{A(k)}
of the image of
A(k)
in the space
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\bullet}
of connected components of
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
is isomorphic to the profinite completion of
A(k)

[
SerreII
,
MilneCongruence
]
. It is conjectured that the Tate-Shafarevich group
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A)
of
A
is finite and, in particular, that its maximal divisible subgroup is trivial. If
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A)_{\textup{div}}
is trivial, then
\overline{A(k)}
is equal to the set
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}_{\bullet}
of connected components of
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
which are orthogonal to the Brauer group of
A

[
Manin
,
Wang
,
PoonenVoloch
]
. We prove the converse of this last statement in Theorem
4.2
below.

For
X
a closed subvariety of
A
, the Mordell-Lang conjecture describes
X(k)
as the set of rational points on a finite union of special subvarieties. This was proved by Faltings
[
Faltings
]
in the number field case and by Hrushovski
[
Hrushovski
]
in the function field case (See also
[
Voloch
,
AV
,
RosslerML
]
). However, it is less well understood how
X(k)
sits inside
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
. The set
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
of adelic points orthogonal to the Brauer group is closely related to the intersection of
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
with the closure of the image of
A(k)
in
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
. In the function field case it is conjectured that the sets
\overline{X(k)}
,
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}
and
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
are all equal
[
PoonenVoloch
, Conjecture C]
. A similar statement with modifications at the archimedean primes is conjectured in the number field case. These conjectures originate from questions posed independently by Scharaschkin
[
Scharaschkin
]
and Skorobogatov
[
Skorobogatov
, p. 133]
for curves over number fields. There is extensive numerical and theoretical evidence for these conjectures, though predominantly in cases where
X(k)
is finite
[
Flynn
,
Poonen
,
Stoll
,
BruinStoll
,
PoonenVoloch
,
BGW
,
CreutzWA
]
.

The main results of this paper establish these conjectures over global function fields, assuming a nonisotriviality hypothesis on the base change
A_{\overline{k}}
of
A
to an algebraic closure of
k
and triviality of
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A)_{\textup{div}}
. To the best of our knowledge, this gives the first examples of subvarieties of abelian varieties (other than abelian varieties themeselves) with
X(k)
infinite where these conjectures are known to hold.

1.1.
Statement of the main results

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global function field
k
such that
A_{{\overline{k}}}
has no nonzero isotrivial quotient. For every closed subvariety
X\subset A
, we have

\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\,.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global function field
k
such that
A_{{\overline{k}}}
has no nonzero isotrivial quotient. The following are equivalent.

(1)

{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A)_{\textup{div}}=0
,

(2)

\overline{A(k)}=A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
,

(3)

For every closed subvariety
X\subset A
we have
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
.

If one fixes the subvariety
X\subset A
, then a more precise result is possible. A subvariety
C\subset A
is a
coset
if there is an abelian subvariety
A^{\prime}\subset A
and a point
a\in A({\overline{k}})
such that
C_{{\overline{k}}}
is the translate of
A^{\prime}_{{\overline{k}}}
by the point
a\in A({\overline{k}})
. In this case we simply write
C=a+A^{\prime}
. We emphasize that
A^{\prime}
and
C
are defined over
k
, though
a
need not be a
k
-rational point. A coset
C=a+A^{\prime}
is a torsor under the abelian variety
A^{\prime}
over
k
. Hence, a coset
C=a+A^{\prime}
with
C({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
nonempty represents an element of
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A^{\prime})
. Assuming
A_{\overline{k}}
has no positive dimensional isotrivial quotient, the special subvarieties featuring in the Mordell-Lang conjecture mentioned above are cosets contained in
X
. The following gives an analogue of Mordell-Lang for the Brauer set.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global function field such that
A_{{\overline{k}}}
has no nonzero isotrivial quotient. Let
X\subset A
be a closed subvariety. There is a finite collection of cosets
C_{i}=a_{i}+A_{i}\subset X
,
i=1,\dots,r
, such that

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(C_{i}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br})\,.

Moreover,
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
if and only if
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A_{i})_{\textup{div}}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,r
.

If
X
is
coset free
, i.e.,
X
does not contain any coset of positive dimension, then the cosets
C_{i}
appearing in Theorem
1.3
are
k
-rational points and the theorem gives
X(k)=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
. Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
were proved for
X_{{\overline{k}}}
coset free (which implies that
X
is coset free, but not conversely) in
[
PoonenVoloch
, Theorems B and D]
under an additional hypothesis on the
p
-primary torsion of
A
. Theorem
1.3
is proved for
X
a nonisotrivial curve of genus at least
2
(necessarily coset free) without the additional hypothesis on the
p
-primary torsion or the requirement that
A_{\overline{k}}
has no positive dimensional isotrivial quotient in
[
CV3
]
. Partial results in the case of isotrivial curves may be found in
[
CV
,
CPV
]
.

In all of the results mentioned in the previous paragraph, the hypotheses imply that
X(L)
is finite for every finite extension
L/k
, while there is no such requirement in the theorems stated above. An example of an
X
covered by Theorem
1.3
to which none of these previous results apply is given by the symmetric square of a nonhyperelliptic curve
Y
of genus
3
which admits a degree
2
map to a nonisotrivial elliptic curve
E
. Then
X=\Sym^{2}(Y)
contains a coset isomorphic to
E
. Suppose the Jacobian
A
of
X
splits up to isogeny as
A_{\overline{k}}\sim E_{\overline{k}}\times B
for a simple nonisotrivial abelian surface
B/{\overline{k}}
. Theorem
1.1
gives
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}
. If, in addition,
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(E)_{\textup{div}}=0
, then Theorem
1.3
gives
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
. Note that
X(k)
is infinite if
E
has positive rank.

1.2.
Adelic Mordell-Lang

A key input to the proofs of the theorems above are the ‘exceptional schemes’ appearing in Rössler’s proof of Mordell-Lang over function fields
[
RosslerML
]
. More recently, these were used by Wisson
[
Wisson
]
to prove a continuous version of Mordell-Lang in the function field case. A similar result was previously obtained by Hrushovski in
[
Hrushovski
]
using model theoretic methods. From Wisson’s result one can fairly easily deduce the following theorem (See Section
6
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X\subset A
be a closed subvariety of an abelian variety over a global function field
k
. Assume
A_{{\overline{k}}}
has no positive dimensional isotrivial quotient. Then there exists a finite union of cosets
Y=\bigcup C_{i}
contained in
X
such that
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
.

With a bit more effort, we deduce a stronger version of this theorem in which
\overline{A(k)}
is replaced by the Brauer set
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
(See Theorem
6.6
). This is a version of the ‘adelic Mordell-Lang conjecture’ first stated in
[
Stoll
]
over number fields for X coset free (See Conjecture
6.1
). This was proved over function fields for
X
coset free in
[
PoonenVoloch
]
. Our proof of Theorem
6.6
follows an approach similar to that taken in
[
PoonenVoloch
]
, but replaces the model theoretic input of
[
Hrushovski
]
there with results of
[
PinkRossler
,
RosslerML
,
Wisson
]
. The conjecture remains wide open over number fields.

1.3.
Outline

In Sections
3
–
5
we establish results that will allow us to deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.4
and to deduce Theorem
1.3
from the more general adelic Mordell-Lang conjecture just mentioned. The results in these sections also hold over number fields, at some points assuming the field is totally imaginary.

One of the challenges to address is that the set
Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})=(\bigcup C_{i})({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
of adelic points on the union featuring in Theorem
1.4
is much larger than the union
\bigcup(C_{i}({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))
of the sets of adelic points on the components (the latter features in Theorem
1.3
). One must show that any point in

is supported on one of the irreducible components. This was done for
X

is finite) in
[

PoonenVoloch
]
, but it does not seem possible to deduce the general case from this. The key result we use is Proposition

below which generalizes
[

, Proposition 3.7]
and
[
PoonenVoloch
, Proposition 5.2]
. This relies on a result of Serre in
[
SerreII
]
concerning the image of Galois acting on torsion points of abelian varieties, and a number of additional results to handle the
p
-part in the function field case.

Proposition
3.1
is a statement about the profinite Selmer group
\Sel(A)
. In Section
4
we relate this to the Brauer set. It was previously known that
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}_{\bullet}\subset\Sel(A)

[

,
Stoll
,
PoonenVoloch
]
, with equality in the number field case by
[
CreutzBCyr
]
. Using recent results of Skorobogatov
[

]
and Yang
[

, we prove this equality in the function field case (See Theorem

). This allows us to prove the converse statements in Theorems

and
1.3
, that the equality
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
implies a finiteness result for the Tate-Shafarevich group.

The application of Proposition
3.1
to adelic points on finite unions of cosets is given in Section
5
. In this section we assume that

has no real primes. This restriction should not be necessary, but there are issues at real primes which would require additional arguments. See Section
5.2
.

We prove the adelic Mordell-Lang conjecture over function fields in Section

. The theorems stated above are then proved in Section

2.
Notation

Throughout the paper
k
is a global field, i.e., a number field or the function field of a geometrically integral curve over a finite field. We fix an algebraic closure
{\overline{k}}
of

and the separable closure

of

. The set of primes of

is denoted by

.The adele ring

, where

is the ring of integers of the completion

of

A variety

over
k
is a separated scheme of finite type over
k
. The set

is endowed with a topology from
{\mathbb{A}}_{k}
. When
Y
is proper, we have that
Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}Y(k_{v})
with the product topology of the
v

Y(k_{v})
.

For a topological space

we use

to denote the set of connected components of
T
with the induced topology. For a nonarchimedean prime

we have
Y(k_{v})_{\bullet}=Y(k_{v})
. Thus, for
Y
a proper variety over
k
we have
Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\bullet}=\prod_{v\in\Omega_{k}}Y(k_{v})_{\bullet}=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}Y(k_{v})\times\prod_{v\mid\infty}Y(k_{v})_{\bullet}
. Note that when
k
is a global function field we have
Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\bullet}=Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be an isogeny. The natural maps

-covering of

if and only if for every

. Let

be the integers given by Step 1 of the proof of (

5.
Adelic intersections for unions of cosets

In this section
k
is either a global function field or a totally imaginary number field. Equivalently,
k
is a global field with no real primes.

For
X
a closed subvariety of the abelian vareity
A
over
k
we define
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}=\prod_{v\in\Omega^{\circ}_{k}}X(k_{v})
to be the product over the set
\Omega_{k}^{\circ}
of nonarchimedean primes. Since all the archimedean primes are assumed to be complex and
A
is geometrically connected, the canonical map
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\bullet}\to A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}
is an isomorphism. Moreover, the map
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}\to A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}
induced by the inclusion
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\subset A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
is injective.

The results of this section concern the images in
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}
of various subsets of
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
. We will simplify the notation by writing

, etc. to also denote their images in

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
Y=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}C_{j}
be a finite union of cosets

. Then

Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap{A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}}\subset\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\left(C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\right).

Proof.

Let
Q\in Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap{A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}}
. Then

by Corollary
4.3
. Suppose
C_{j}=a_{j}+A_{j}
with
a_{j}\in A({\overline{k}})
and subabelian varieties
A_{j}\subset A
. Consider the quotient maps
\phi_{j}:A\to B_{j}:=A/A_{j}
, and let
B=\prod B_{j}
with the canonical maps
\psi_{j}:B_{j}\to B
. Then
\psi_{j}(\phi_{j}(C_{j}))
is a point

. Let

. Then

. Let

. The assumption that

implies that for every prime

, there is some

such that the image of

under the projection

is

. By Proposition

, this implies that there exists some

such that

in

. For this

, and it follows that the

lies on

for all nonarchimedean primes

lies in (the image of)

(in

).
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
C\subset A
be a coset. Then

Proof.

Suppose

is a coset of

. By Poincaré reducibility, there is an abelian subvariety

we have that multiplication by

for some isogeny
\psi:A\to B
.

The containment

is obvious. For the reverse containment, suppose

. By
[
PoonenVoloch

which is based on results of Serre
[
SerreII
]
and Milne

, we have that

as topological groups. Since
A(k)
is finitely generated, the subgroup

is of finite index and, hence, open. Since
A(k)
is dense in
A(k)\otimes\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, each coset of
n(A(k)\otimes\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
contains an element of
A(k)
. It follows that
c=na+P
for some
a\in\overline{A(k)}
and

.

Let
b=\psi(a)\in\overline{B(k)}
, so that

. Let

be the pullback of

under the morphism

sending a point

to

. Then

. Since

, it will be enough to show that

.

Let

be the image of

under the quotient

and let

be the image of

. The reduced subschemes of the irreducible components of

are translates of

, so

is finite. Then

by Corollary

. Thus

. It follows that

b\in(A^{\prime}\times\{z\})({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{B(k)}=\overline{A^{\prime}(k)}\times\{z\}\subset\overline{D(k)}\,.

∎

We expect the results of this section also hold for number fields with real primes, if one considers all of the intersections and containments as taking place in

Lemma
5.1

In the proof we showed that the adelic point

has all of its nonarchimedean

such that

and

have the same image in

and

is a singleton. For real primes, however, we do not even know that

.

Lemma
5.2

In the proof of we used that

being nonempty implies that

is nonempty.

[
PinkRossler
]
. Theorem
6.6
was proved in the coset free case in
[
PoonenVoloch
, Lemma 3.18]
using Hrushovski’s model theoretic proof of Mordell-Lang
[
Hrushovski
]
.

First we explain how to deduce the weaker version of the conjecture with
\overline{A(k)}
in place of
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
directly from the main result of Wisson
[
Wisson
]
. This is Theorem
1.4
of the introduction.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global function field
k
such that
A_{{\overline{k}}}
has no positive dimensional isotrivial quotient. Let
X\subset A
be a closed subvariety. Then there is a finite union of cosets
Y\subset X
such that
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
.

Proof.

Suppose
k
is the function field of a curve over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
. Let
k^{\prime}=k\times_{\mathbb{F}}\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}
. The main result of
[
Wisson
]
asserts the existence of a subvariety
W\subset X_{k^{\prime}}
with the following properties:

(1)

W_{{\overline{k}}}
is a finite union of cosets in
A_{{\overline{k}}}
, and

(2)

for every discrete valuation
w
of
k^{\prime}
and metric
d_{w}
on
A(k^{\prime}_{w})
inducing the
w
-adic topology, there exists a positive real number
c_{w}
such that for every
P
in the finitely generated subgroup
A(k)\subset A(k^{\prime})
we have
d_{w}(W,P)\leq c_{w}\cdot d_{w}(X_{k^{\prime}},P)
.

For some finite Galois extension
L/k
contained in
k^{\prime}
there is a model
Y
of
W
over
L
, i.e. a subvariety
Y\subset X_{L}
such that the base change of
Y
to
k^{\prime}
is equal to
W
as a subvariety of
X_{k^{\prime}}
. From (2) above it follows that
X_{k^{\prime}}({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset W({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})
, where the topological closure is taken in
A_{k^{\prime}}({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})
. The inclusions
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\subset A_{L}({\mathbb{A}}_{L})\subset A_{k^{\prime}}({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})
are homeomorphisms onto their images, which are closed subsets. So the closure of
A(k)
in
A_{k^{\prime}}({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})
is equal to the image of its closure in
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
. It follows that

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset W({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})\cap\overline{A(k)}=Y_{k^{\prime}}({\mathbb{A}}_{k^{\prime}})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{L})\cap\overline{A(k)}.

Lemma
5.2
gives a finite union of cosets
Y^{\prime}\subset X
such that
Y({\mathbb{A}}_{L})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset Y^{\prime}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
inside
A({\mathbb{A}}_{L})
. So
Y^{\prime}\subset X
is a finite union of cosets in
A
with the property that

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}=Y^{\prime}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\,.

∎

6.1.
Rössler’s exceptional schemes

Let us fix some notation in effect for the remainder of this section. Suppose
X
is a closed subvariety of an abelian variety
A
over
k={\mathbb{F}}(U)
, where
U
is a curve over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}
of characteristic
p
. We assume that
U
is small enough so that
A
spreads out to an abelian scheme
{\mathcal{A}}/U
and
X
spreads out to
{\mathcal{X}}/U
which is a closed subscheme of
{\mathcal{A}}
. Let
k^{\prime}=k\times_{\mathbb{F}}{\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}}
,
U^{\prime}=U\times_{k}k^{\prime}
,
X^{\prime}=X\times_{k}k^{\prime}
,
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}\times_{U}U^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{X}}\times_{U}U^{\prime}
.

Rössler uses jet schemes in
[
RosslerML
]
to construct the ‘exceptional schemes’
\Exc^{n}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{X}})/U^{\prime}
for each
n\geq 1
(See
[
RosslerML
, Section 3A]
). These were used by Wisson to construct the variety
W\subset X_{k^{\prime}}
in the proof of Theorem
6.2
above. The exceptional schemes are closed subschemes of
{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
. The generic fiber
\Exc^{n}(A,X):=\Exc^{n}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{X}})_{k^{\prime}}
is a closed subvariety of
X^{\prime}
. The key properties we will require are given in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 6.3

(
[
WissonThesis
, Proposition 3.2.1]
)
.

Suppose
L
is a separable extension of
k^{\prime}
. Then

X(L)\cap p^{n}A(L)\subset Exc^{n}(A,X)(L)\,.

Corollary 6.4
.

Suppose
L
is the completion of
k^{\prime}
at a discrete valuation. Then

X(L)\cap p^{n}A(L)\subset Exc^{n}(A,X)(L)\,.

Proof.

Let
f:X_{n}\to X
be the pullback of
[p^{n}]:A\to A
and let
L^{h}
be the henselization of
k^{\prime}
at
w
. Then
L^{h}
is a separable extension of
k^{\prime}
by
[
PoonenVoloch
, Lemma 3.1]
and so
f(X_{n}(L^{h}))=X(L^{h})\cap p^{n}A(L^{h})\subset Exc^{n}(A,X)(L^{h})
by the lemma. Let
x=f(y)\in X(L)\cap p^{n}A(L)=f(X_{n}(L))
. By Greenberg’s approximation theorem
[
Greenberg
]
, there is a sequence of points in
X_{n}(L^{h})
converging to
y
in
X_{n}(L)
. The image of this sequence is contained in
f(X_{n}(L^{h}))\subset Exc^{n}(A,X)(L^{h})
and converges to
x
. Since
Exc^{n}(A,X)(L)
is a closed subset of
A(L)
containing
Exc^{n}(A,X)(L^{h})
we must have
x\in Exc^{n}(A,X)(L)
.
∎

We note that Lemma
6.3
also allows us to identify exceptional subschemes of
X
which contain intersections with cosets of
p^{n}A(L)
. Given
Q\in{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}(U^{\prime})=A(k^{\prime})
, let
X^{+Q}
denote the translate of
X
by
Q
. The lemma gives

X(L)\cap(Q+p^{n}A(L))\subset Exc^{n}(A,X^{-Q})^{+Q}(L)\,.

Lemma 6.5

(Rössler)
.

Suppose the reduced subscheme of
X_{\overline{k}}
is not a finite union of cosets in
A_{\overline{k}}
. Let
u\in U
be a closed point and
k_{u}
the completion of
k
at
u
. Then there exists
m\geq 1
such that for all
Q\in A(k_{u})
we have
Exc^{m}(A,X^{+Q})
is not Zariski dense in
X^{\prime+Q}
.

Proof.

By the Manin-Mumford conjecture
[
PinkRossler
, Theorem 3.7]
the hypothesis on
X_{\overline{k}}
implies that the torsion points of
A_{\overline{k}}
are not Zariski dense in
X_{\overline{k}}^{+Q}
for any
Q
. By
[
RosslerML
, Corollary 4.5]
this implies that there exists an
m
such that for all
Q\in A(k_{u})
the sets

(6.1)

Exc^{m}({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{X}}^{+Q})_{u^{\prime}}({\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}})=\{P\in{\mathcal{X}}^{+Q}({\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}}):P\text{ lifts to an element of }{\mathcal{X}}^{+Q}(u^{\prime}_{m})\cap p^{n}{\mathcal{A}}(u^{\prime}_{m})\}

are not Zariski dense in
{\mathcal{X}}^{+Q}_{u^{\prime}}
. Here
u^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
is a closed point above
u
and
u^{\prime}_{m}
is its
m
-th infinitesimal neighborhood. The equality between the two sets in (
6.1
) is noted in
[
RosslerML
, Proof of Proposition 3.1]
. Since
{\mathcal{X}}^{+Q}
is integral, this implies that
Exc^{m}(A,X^{+Q})=Exc({\mathcal{A}},{\mathcal{X}}^{+Q})_{k^{\prime}}
is not Zariski dense in
X^{\prime+Q}=({\mathcal{X}}^{\prime+Q})_{k^{\prime}}
.
∎

6.2.
The adelic Mordell-Lang conjecture over function fields

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global function field
k
. Assume that
A_{\overline{k}}
has no positive dimensional isotrivial quotient. Let
X\subset A
be a closed subvariety. Then there exists a finite union of cosets
Y
contained in
X
such that

\left(X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\right)\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\,.

Proof.

First note that by Theorem
4.2
, we can replace
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
in the statement by
\Sel(A)
. Then, by Lemma
5.2
, we are reduced to showing that there is a finite separable extension
K/k
and a subvariety
Y\subset X_{K}
such that
(Y_{{\overline{K}}})_{\textup{red}}
is a finite union of cosets and
X({\mathbb{A}}_{K})\cap\Sel(A_{K})\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{K})
. Thus we may pass to a finite separable extension and assume that that all irreducible components of
X
are geometrically irreducible and that the reduced subscheme of at least one irreducible component of
X
is not a coset.

Let
u\in U
. Let
m=m(u)
be the maximum of the integers given by Rössler’s Lemma
6.5
as we range over the irreducible components of
X
whose reduced subschemes are not cosets in
A
. Let
\pi_{i}:T_{i}\to A
,
i=1,\dots,s
be
p^{m}
-coverings of
A
representing the finitely many elements of
\Sel^{p^{m}}(A)
. By
[
PoonenVoloch
, Lemma 5.4]
we have that
\Sel(A)\subset\bigcup_{i}\pi_{i}(T_{i}({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))
. For each
i
, chose a point
a_{i}\in T_{i}(k_{u}^{h})
where
k_{u}^{h}
denotes the Henselization of
k
at
u
. Let
L/k
be a finite separable extension over which all of the
a_{i}
are defined. We may choose
L
to have a prime
\mathfrak{u}
of degree
1
over
u
, so that
a_{i}\in A(L_{\mathfrak{u}})=A(k_{u})
.

Let
W\subset X
be an irreducible component whose reduced subscheme is not a coset. By Lemma
6.5
we have that
Exc^{m}(A,W^{-a_{i}})
is not Zariski dense in
W^{-a_{i}}
, for all
i
. Then, for every
i
, the translate
Exc^{m}(A,W^{-a_{i}})^{+a_{i}}
is not Zariski dense in
W
. Note that each
Exc^{m}(A,W^{-a_{i}})^{+a_{i}}
is defined over some finite constant extension of the finite separable extension
L/k
. Let
Z
be the union of the Galois conjugates of all of the
Exc^{m}(A,W^{-a_{i}})^{+a_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,s
. Then
Z
is a subvariety of
W
. Moreover, since
W
is geometrically irreducible, we have that
Z
is not Zariski dense in
W
.

By Lemma
6.3
and its corollary, for any prime
w
of
L
above a prime
v
of
k
and for all
i=1,\dots,s
, we have

W(k_{v})\cap\pi_{i}(T_{i}(k_{v}))\subset W(L_{w})\cap\pi_{i}(T_{i}(L_{w}))=W(L_{w})\cap\left(a_{i}+p^{m}(A(L_{w}))\right)\subset Z(L_{w})\,,

and so

W(k_{v})\cap\pi_{i}(T_{i}(k_{v}))\subset W(k_{v})\cap Z(L_{w})=Z(k_{v})\,.

Let
X_{1}\subset X
be the union of the irreducible components of
X
whose reduced subschemes are cosets, together with the subvarieties
Z=Z(W)
constructed for each irreducible component
W\subset X
whose reduced subscheme is not a coset. For every prime
v
of
k
and every
i=1,\dots,s
, we have

X(k_{v})\cap\pi_{i}(T_{i}(k_{v}))\subset X_{1}(k_{v})\,.

Since
\Sel(A)\subset\bigcup_{i}\pi_{i}(T_{i}({\mathbb{A}}_{k}))
, it follows that
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\Sel(A)\subset X_{1}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
.

We now repeat the argument with
X_{1}
in place of
X
and proceed by induction to construct a sequence of varieties
X_{1}/k_{1},X_{2}/k_{2},\dots
, defined over finite separable extensions
k=k_{1}\subset k_{2}\subset\dots
, such that
X_{j+1}\subset(X_{j})_{k_{j+1}}
and

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k_{j}})\cap\Sel(A_{k_{j}})\subset X_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k_{j}})
for all
j
. Note that
X_{j+1}
is not Zariski dense in
X_{j}
unless the reduced subscheme of
(X_{j})_{\overline{k}}
is a finite union of cosets. So by noetherianity the sequence must eventually stabilise at some
X_{N}
such that the reduced subscheme of
(X_{N})_{\overline{k}}
is a finite union of cosets. As noted in the first paragraph, we may conclude by applying Lemma
5.2
to
X_{N}/k_{N}
.
∎

7.
Proofs of the main theorems

The following gives Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
of the introduction. Theorem
1.2
is an easy consequence of these two. Recall that
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}=\prod_{v\in\Omega^{\circ}_{k}}X(k_{v})
is the product over the set
\Omega_{k}^{\circ}
of nonarchimedean primes and
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}^{\Br}
denotes the image of
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
in
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global field
k
and let
X
be a closed subvariety of
A
. If
k
is a global function assume that
A_{{\overline{k}}}
has no nonzero isotrivial quotient. If
k
is a number field assume that
k
is totally imaginary and Conjecture
6.1
holds for
X\subset A
. Then

(1)

The images of
\overline{X(k)}
and
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}
in
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}
are equal, and

(2)

There is a finite collection of cosets
C_{i}=a_{i}+A_{i}\subset X
,
i=1,\dots,r
, such that

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}_{\circ}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(C_{i}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}^{\Br})\,.

Moreover,
X(k)
is dense in
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}_{\circ}
if and only if
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A_{i})_{\textup{div}}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,r
.

Remark 7.2
.

In the theorem we must omit the complex primes, if we want to allow
X
that are not geometrically connected. Here is an example with
X(k)
not dense in
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}_{\bullet}
. Suppose
k={\mathbb{Q}}(i)
and
X=P_{1}\cup P_{2}=\Spec(k\times k)
is a pair of
k
-rational points. Let
(x_{v})\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
be the adelic point with
x_{v}=P_{1}
for all nonarchimedean primes and
x_{v}=P_{2}
at the complex prime. Then
(x_{v})
and
P_{1}
only differ at the complex prime. Since
P_{1}\in X(k)\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
and
\Br({\mathbb{C}})=0
, we have that
(x_{v})\in X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
. But the image of
(x_{v})
in
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\bullet}
is not contained in the image of
X(k)
.

Proof.

As in Section
5
, we omit the subscript
\circ
from the notation, using
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
to also denote its image in
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})_{\circ}
and similarly for the other subsets of
A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})
considered.

Theorem
6.6
(or the assumed Conjecture
6.1
in the number field case) gives a finite union of cosets
Y=\bigcup C_{j}
, say
C_{j}=a_{j}+A_{j}
, contained in
X
such that

(7.1)

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\,.

By Lemma
5.1
we have that

Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\subset\bigcup_{j}C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\,.

Then by Lemma
5.4
applied to each
C_{j}
we have

\overline{Y(k)}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset\bigcup_{j}\left(C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\right)=\bigcup_{j}\overline{C_{j}(k)}\subset\overline{Y(k)}\,.

Thus, we have
\overline{Y(k)}=Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}
. Combining this with (
7.1
) we get

\overline{Y(k)}\subset\overline{X(k)}\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}\subset Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}=\overline{Y(k)}.

This shows that
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A(k)}
proving the first statement in the theorem.

For the second statement, using Lemma
5.5
in place of Lemma
5.4
in the argument above, we get that

(7.2)

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap A({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\bigcup_{j}C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}.

For each
j
we also have
C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
by functoriality of the Brauer pairing (
4.1
). So
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\bigcup C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
. Note that this equality continues to hold if we take the union only over those
j
such that
C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\neq\emptyset
.

If
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A_{j})_{\textup{div}}=0
for all
j
, then by Theorem
4.2
we have

X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\bigcup_{j}C_{j}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\bigcup_{j}\overline{C_{j}(k)}\subset\overline{X(k)},

which implies that
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\overline{X(k)}
.

For the converse suppose
\overline{X(k)}=X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
. Let
C=a+A^{\prime}\subset X
be a coset such that
C
represents an element of
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A^{\prime})_{\textup{div}}
. By Theorem
4.2
we have
C({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\neq\emptyset
. Let
x\in C({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\subset X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}
. In particular,
X({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}\neq\emptyset
, so
\overline{X(k)}\neq\emptyset
. By Mordell-Lang there is a finite union of cosets
\bigcup C_{i}\subset X
such that
X(k)=\bigcup C_{i}(k)
, and so there is some
C_{i}
such that
x\in\overline{C_{i}(k)}
. In particular,
C_{i}(k)\neq\emptyset
. Translating by a
k
-rational point we can assume
C_{i}=A_{i}
is an abelian subvariety. Then
Y:=C\cap A_{i}
is geometrically a finite union of cosets contained in the abelian variety
A_{i}
and
x\in Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A_{i}(k)}
. By Lemma
5.2
, there is a finite union of cosets
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
such that
Y({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A_{i}(k)}=Y^{\prime}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A_{i}(k)}
. Note that Conjecture
6.1
holds for
Y^{\prime}\subset A
, since
Y^{\prime}
is already a finite union of cosets. By the first statement of the theorem (which we have already proved) we get that
Y^{\prime}({\mathbb{A}}_{k})\cap\overline{A_{i}(k)}=\overline{Y^{\prime}(k)}\subset\overline{C(k)}
. We conclude that
x\in\overline{C(k)}
. Since
x
was arbitrary this shows that
C({\mathbb{A}}_{k})^{\Br}=\overline{C(k)}
. By Theorem
4.2
this implies that
{\mbox{{Sh}}}(A^{\prime})_{\textup{div}}=0
.
∎

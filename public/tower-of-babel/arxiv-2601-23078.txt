Mermin-Wagner theorems for quantum systems with multipole symmetries
Timo Feistl 1, Severin Schraven 2, Simone Warzel 2,3,4 1 Department of Mathematics, Friedrich-Alexander-University, Erlangen, Germany 2 Department of Mathematics, TU Munich, Garching, Germany 3 Munich Center for Quantum Science and Technology, Munich, Germany 4 Department of Physics, TU Munich, Garching, Germany

Abstract
We prove Mermin-Wagner-type theorems for quantum lattice systems in the presence of multipole symmetries. These theorems show that the presence of higher-order symmetries protects against the breaking of lower-order ones. In particular, we prove that the critical dimension in which the charge symmetry can be broken increases if the system admits higher multipole symmetries, e.g. d = 4 d=4 on the regular lattice ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} in the presence of dipole symmetry. Keywords: quantum lattice systems, absence of continuous symmetry breaking



1
Introduction

The Mermin-Wagner theorem is a milestone in statistical mechanics. It proves that at positive temperature, continuous symmetries cannot be spontaneously broken in spatial dimensions
d\leq 2
. Early manifestations of this principle were established by Mermin and Wagner
[
16
]
for the rotational symmetry of the isotropic Heisenberg model, and by Hohenberg
[
11
]
in the context of superfluids. Since then, the theorem has been extended and adapted to many different physical settings and symmetry types
[
6
,
14
,
1
,
4
,
30
]
.

In recent years, renewed interest has focused on how additional symmetries modify the dimensional constraints on symmetry breaking. In particular, it has become clear that the coexistence of conventional charge conservation with so-called multipole symmetries can substantially alter the critical dimension at which symmetry breaking becomes possible. Recent work
[
31
,
12
]
has shown that higher multipole symmetries can protect charge symmetry and thereby raise the dimension required for its spontaneous breaking. The purpose of this work is to prove that this mechanism is not specific to charge conservation: rather, higher-order multipole symmetries generically protect lower-order ones. Moreover, we also eliminate some additional assumptions required in the proof strategy of the Mermin-Wagner results in
[
12
]
.

Multipole symmetries are not merely of abstract interest but arise naturally in several physically relevant systems.
One class of examples are fractional quantum Hall models, such as those based on Haldane pseudopotentials
[
9
,
26
,
15
]
or their truncation
[
23
,
33
]
. These exhibit both charge and dipole conservation, which, in this case, stems from the conservation of an appropriate angular momentum. Lattice models with dipole symmetries have also started to become directly experimentally accessible via cold atoms in tilted optical lattices
[
8
,
29
]
.
Aside from a change in the critical dimension for symmetry breaking, on which we focus in this paper,
the presence of such a dipole symmetry causes other interesting phenomena, such as the appearance of immobile, low-energy excitations known as fractons, of many-body scars high up in the energy spectrum, as well as the change of hydrodynamic laws, see e.g.
[
28
,
27
,
17
,
18
,
7
,
5
,
10
,
34
,
13
,
35
]
.

1.1
Setting

We formulate our results in the standard setting for quantum ’lattice’ systems. Instead of a regular lattice, we work on a countable metric space
(\mathcal{L},D)
, which is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and whose volume growth defines an effective dimension.

Assumption 1.1
.

(Base space)

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a countable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some spatial dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
D
be the metric on
\mathcal{L}
induced by the standard Euclidean metric
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We assume that there exist constants
\gamma>0
,
C\geq 0
and
r_{0}\geq 0
such that for balls
B_{r}(z)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ |\ |x-z|<r\}

|B_{r}(z)\cap\mathcal{L}|\leq Cr^{\gamma}\qquad\text{for all }r\geq r_{0}\text{ and all }z\in\mathcal{L}.

(1.1)

We refer to
\gamma
as the effective dimension of
\mathcal{L}
.

The most important example will be
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for which
\gamma=d
.

The set of finite, nonempty subsets of
\mathcal{L}
will be denoted by
P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
. To each site
x\in\mathcal{L}
, we associate a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{x}
. The quantum mechanics of the degrees of freedom supported on
\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
is then described by the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}
of bounded linear operators
L(\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})
on the Hilbert-space tensor product
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{H}_{x}
.

For any nested pair
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
,
\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Lambda_{2}
, the map

\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{1}}\ni A\mapsto A\otimes\bigl(\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda_{2}\setminus\Lambda_{1}}\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{H}_{x}}\bigr)\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{2}}

(1.2)

provides a natural identification under which
\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{1}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{2}}
.
The algebra of local operators is the following union respecting inclusions of the form (
1.2
)

\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}\coloneqq\bigcup_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})}\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}.

(1.3)

When equipping
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
with the norm
\|\cdot\|
inherited from the operator norm on
L(\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda})
for
\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
,
the norm completion of
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{U}
of quasi-local operators on
(\mathcal{L},D)
.
The unit of
\mathcal{U}
is denoted by
\mathbb{I}
. For not necessarily finite subsets
X\subseteq\mathcal{L}
, we also define
\mathcal{U}_{X}
to be the norm closure of
\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})\end{subarray},\Lambda\subseteq X}\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}
.
For a more detailed exposition of algebras of quasi-local operators as well as proofs of the relevant properties, see
[
3
, Chapter 6.2]
or
[
30
, Chapter II.1]
.

The possible physical states of the system are modeled by states of the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{U}
, i.e., by positive linear functionals
\omega:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with
\omega(\mathbb{I})=1
. Such functionals are automatically continuous.

A time-evolution on
\mathcal{U}
is a strongly continuous 1-parameter group of
*
-automorphisms
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Given a countable metric space
(\mathcal{L},D)
with its associated
C^{*}
-algebra of quasi-local operators
\mathcal{U}
, we will call a family
\Phi=\bigl(\phi(\Lambda)\bigr)_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})}
an interaction if all
\phi(\Lambda)
are self-adjoint elements of
\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}
. We consider time-evolutions
\alpha
which are induced by an interaction
\Phi
in such a way that
\phi(\Lambda)
can be interpreted as a local Hamiltonian. Informally, this is the Heisenberg evolution

\alpha^{\Phi}_{t}(A)=\exp\left(it\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})}\phi(\Lambda)\right)A\exp\left(-it\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})}\phi(\Lambda)\right).

(1.4)

Under suitable decay conditions on the interactions, which we spell below, one obtains such a time-evolution for all
A\in\mathcal{U}
, see Appendix
A
.

Our Mermin-Wagner theorem addresses thermal equilibrium states at non-zero temperature. These are modeled by the so-called Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) condition. To define such KMS-states, one extends the dynamics given by the group of automorphisms
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
to imaginary times. In general, this is not possible, however, if
A\in\mathcal{U}
is such that the map
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\alpha_{t}(A)
has an extension to an analytic function
\mathbb{C}\ni z\mapsto\alpha_{z}(A)
, then we call
A
an
\alpha
-analytic element. We will denote by
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
the
*
-subalgebra of
\alpha
-analytic elements.

Definition 1.1
.

(KMS states)

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a strongly continuous 1-parameter group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{U}
. A state
\omega
on
\mathcal{U}
is called a KMS state of
(\mathcal{U},\alpha)
at inverse temperature
\beta
if

\omega\bigl(A\alpha_{i\beta}(B)\bigr)=\omega(BA)

(1.5)

for all
A,B
in
\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
. We denote the set of
(\mathcal{U},\alpha)
-KMS states at
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
by
K_{\beta}(\alpha)
.

One can show that this KMS condition captures the defining properties of thermal equilibrium (see
[
3
, Section 5.3.1]
). For
C^{*}
-algebras of quasi-local operators and time-evolutions induced by interactions, the KMS condition is closely related to a maximum entropy principle (see
[
3
, Section 6.2.3]
). The relative entropy will play a crucial role in our proof, see Appendix
B
.

The last ingredient in the formulation of the Mermin-Wagner theorem is that of a symmetry of the time-evolution and that of symmetry-preserved or broken states.

Definition 1.2
.

(Symmetries and their breaking)

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be a strongly continuous 1-parameter group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{U}
.
An automorphism
\tau\in Aut(\mathcal{U})
is called a symmetry of
(\mathcal{U},\alpha)
if

\alpha_{t}\circ\tau=\tau\circ\alpha_{t}\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\mathrm{all}\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

If
\omega
is a state on
\mathcal{U}
, then a symmetry
\tau
of
(\mathcal{U},\alpha)
is said to be preserved by
\omega
if

\omega\circ\tau=\omega.

Otherwise,
\tau
is said to be broken on
\omega
.

One can show that if
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
and
\tau
is a symmetry of
(\mathcal{U},\alpha)
, then
\omega\circ\tau\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
. Thus, this definition captures the intuitive picture that pre-composing with a symmetry can at most swap between thermal equilibrium states at the same
\beta
. In light of this, the question of whether a given symmetry is spontaneously broken on a given KMS state is natural.

1.2
Multipole symmetries and main result

Our main theorem will address symmetries generated by multipole moments for a family of local charge operators.

Assumption 1.2
.

(Family of local charge operators)

We call a family
\{n_{x}\ |\ x\in\mathcal{L}\}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
of self-adjoint operators
n_{z}
a family of local charge operators if

(i)

[n_{x},n_{y}]=0
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{L}
.

(ii)

There exists
N_{0}>0
such that
\|n_{x}\|\leq N_{0}
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
.

(iii)

There exists
R_{0}>0
such that the support satisfies
\mathrm{supp}(n_{x})\subseteq B_{R_{0}}(x)\cap\mathcal{L}
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
.

The operator
n_{x}
models a local charge operator near site
x\in\mathcal{L}
. Conditions
(i)
and
(iii)
are automatically satisfied if
n_{x}\in\mathcal{U}_{\{x\}}
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
, which is the case for many typical models of quantum statistical mechanics, cf.
[
30
,
19
,
22
]
.

The multipole symmetry associated with some multi-index
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
is defined via the following 1-parameter group of automorphisms

\tau^{(a)}_{s}(A)=\exp{\Bigl(-is\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{x})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x^{a}n_{x}\Bigr)}A\exp{\Bigl(is\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{x})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}x^{a}n_{x}\Bigr)},\qquad A\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda},

(1.6)

with
x^{a}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{a_{j}}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Equation (
1.6
) defines
\tau^{(a)}_{s}
on all local observables
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. We show in Proposition
2.2
that one can extend it to a strongly continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms on the entire quasi-local algebra.
For example, in case
a=0
the generator of the symmetry is the total charge. In case
a=(1,0,\dots,0)=e_{1}
, the dipole moment along the first coordinate axis generates the symmetry.

As a last assumption, we require that interactions decay sufficiently fast and are invariant under the multipole symmetries up to some order.

Assumption 1.3
.

(Symmetric interactions)
We assume that our interaction
\Phi=(\phi(\Lambda))_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})}
is
k
-symmetric with
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the sense that:

1.

for every multi-index
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|a|\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}\leq k
, we have

\tau_{s}^{(a)}\bigl(\phi(\Lambda)\bigr)=\phi(\Lambda)

(1.7)

for all
\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
and all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

2.

we have

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{L}}\Biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\\
x\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}|z-x|^{k+1}\Bigr)^{2}\Biggr)<\infty.

(1.8)

3.

there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{L}}(1+|x-y|)^{\gamma+1+\varepsilon}\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\;x,y\in\Lambda}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|<\infty,

(1.9)

with
\gamma
the effective dimension of
\mathcal{L}
.

For each
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, the symmetry
(\tau_{s}^{(a)})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
is continuous in the usual jargon of statistical mechanics.
The symmetry condition (
1.7
) is in general strictly stronger than Definition
1.2
. Generically, many different interactions induce the same time-evolution
\alpha
, and whether this stronger symmetry condition holds or not can depend on the choice of the interaction.

The decay conditions serve two different purposes: Condition (
1.9
) ensures that the interaction
\Phi
induces a time-evolution
\alpha
on
\mathcal{U}
in the sense of (
1.4
). This is proved in Appendix
A
using standard tools. Condition (
1.8
) is needed to control the second-order error in the Taylor expansion needed in the proof of the Mermin-Wagner result. The rate of decay (
1.8
) hence depends on the order of the multipole symmetry
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
under consideration. We will show in Corollary
A.4
in the appendix that

\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{L}}(1+|x-y|)^{4+2k+2\gamma}\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})\ :\ x,y\in\Lambda}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|<\infty,

(1.10)

is sufficient for condition (
1.8
). Hence sufficiently fast algebraic decay of the interaction ensures the validity of both (
1.8
) and (
1.9
).

Our main result is the following collection of Mermin-Wagner theorems on the absence of breaking of the multipole symmetries in sufficiently low effective dimension.

Theorem 1.4
.

In the setting of Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
, suppose that Assumption
1.3
holds for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
is a fixed multi-index with
|a|\leq k
and the effective dimension satisfies
\gamma\leq 2(k-|a|+1)
, then the multipole symmetry
(\tau_{s}^{(a)})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
is preserved on every
\beta
-KMS state
\omega
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
; i.e.,
\omega\circ\tau_{s}^{(a)}=\omega
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
.

Let us demonstrate the applicability of the result for
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, i.e., when
d=\gamma
. The case
k=0
covers the absence of the breaking of the charge symmetry (
a=0
) if
d=\gamma\leq 2
. We hence recover the standard Mermin-Wagner theorem. In case
k=1
, both charge and the dipole symmetries in all directions are present. In this case, Theorem
1.4
rules out the breaking of the charge symmetry (
a=0
) in case
d=\gamma\leq 4=2(1-0+1)
. In contrast, the dipole symmetries associated with the
d
directions (
a=e_{j}
with
j\in\{1,\dots.d\}
) remain unbroken only for
d=\gamma\leq 2
. The case
k=1
thus constitutes a minor generalization of the Mermin-Wagner theorem in
[
12
]
, where Theorem
1.4
shows that one may drop the additional assumption on clustering of the state.

1.3
Comments and outline of the paper

Theorem
1.4
is proven in Section
2
. As a preliminary step, we show that the multipole symmetry exists in the infinite-volume limit, ensuring that the problem is well-posed. Using standard methods, we then reduce the Mermin–Wagner theorem to a bound on the relative entropy of the KMS state
\omega
and its symmetry-transformed KMS state
\omega\circ\tau_{s}^{(a)}
. As usual, this bound is proven to be finite via an appropriate Taylor expansion of a smoothly truncated unitary
U_{m}^{(a)}(s)
representing the multipole symmetry.

Appendix
A
reviews standard arguments involving general
F
-functions (Definition
A.1
), which establish the existence of the infinite-volume time-evolution
\alpha
generated by our interactions. Appendix
B
reviews the proof of the entropy-based criterion from
[
6
]
. We also discuss a variant of the main theorem for slab geometries of the form
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-\ell}\times[-M,M]^{\ell}
in Appendix
C
.

Our aim was not to present the most general form of the Mermin-Wagner theorem for multipole symmetries, but rather a version that applies to physically relevant systems and relies on assumptions that are easy to verify. For instance, Assumption
1.1
could be weakened by only requiring that
\lvert\mathcal{L}\cap B_{r}(x)\rvert
is finite for all
r>0
and
x\in\mathcal{L}
, provided that (
1.9
) is replaced by the existence of an
F
-function for
\mathcal{L}
. We impose Assumption
1.1
mainly to ensure that
F(r)=(1+r)^{1+\gamma+\varepsilon}
is an
F
-function for
\mathcal{L}
and to guarantee the regularity of the truncated symmetry
U_{m}^{(a)}
; precise definitions can be found in Appendix
A
. We emphasize that we do not assume any form of clustering, which is the main advantage compared with the strategy of
[
12
]
.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.4

The proof proceeds in three steps. We first establish the existence of the multipole symmetry
\tau_{s}^{(a)}
as a continuous symmetry on
\mathcal{U}
. We then employ the entropy methods from
[
6
]
and reduce the proof of Theorem
1.4
to the following finiteness criterion. Its formulation requires the notion of exhausting sequences, which we will use throughout the proof.

Definition 2.1
.

(Exhausting sequences of finite subsets) We write
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow\Lambda
for any sequence of finite sets
\Lambda_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{L}
, which is increasing
\Lambda_{n}\subset\Lambda_{n+1}
and which exhausts the entire space,
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Lambda_{n}=\mathcal{L}
.

The following is an adaptation of
[
6
]
(see also
[
24
]
) to the present set-up.

Proposition 2.1
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.4
suppose that for some
\Lambda_{m}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
with an associated sequence of unitaries
(U_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
such that
\tau^{(a)}_{s}(A)=U_{m}^{*}AU_{m}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}

we have

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\ \bigl\|U_{m}\delta(U_{m}^{*})+U_{m}^{*}\delta(U_{m}\bigr)\bigr\|<\infty,

(2.1)

with
\delta
the infinitesimal generator of the time-evolution
\alpha
.
Then
\omega\circ\tau_{s}^{(a)}=\omega
for all
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
; i.e.,
\tau_{s}^{(a)}
cannot be spontaneously broken on any thermal equilibrium states at inverse temperature
\beta
.

As we recall in Appendix
B
, where the proof of this criterion can be found, the expression in (
2.1
) serves as an upper bound on the relative entropy of the state
\omega
and
\omega\circ\tau_{s}^{(a)}
. As standard in Mermin-Wagner arguments, the finite-volume approximations
U_{m}
of the symmetry
\tau_{s}^{(a)}
are constructed in terms of smooth truncations, see (
2.2
) below.
We will then show that (
2.1
) is indeed satisfied for those truncations.

2.1
Smooth truncations and existence of the multipole symmetry

In this subsection, we will prove that
\tau^{(a)}_{s}
defined in (
1.6
) can be extended to a symmetry. Moreover, these symmetries can be approximated by smooth locally generated unitaries.
To implement the approximations, we use a smooth characteristic function of the box
[-m,m]^{d}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
. More precisely,
we pick a cut-off function
\tilde{\chi}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
0\leq\tilde{\chi}\leq 1
,
\tilde{\chi}(x)=1
if
|x|\leq 1
, and
\tilde{\chi}(x)=0
if
|x|\geq 2
. We then set

\chi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},x\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{\chi}(x_{j}).

This yields
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
0\leq\chi\leq 1
,
\chi(x)=1
if
|x|_{\infty}\coloneqq\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,d\}}|x_{j}|\leq 1
, and
\chi(x)=0
if
|x|_{\infty}\geq 2
.
Lastly, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
we define the rescaled version

\chi_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\chi_{m}(x)=\chi(x/m),

which defines
\chi_{m}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\chi_{m}(x)=0
if
|x|_{\infty}\geq 2m
.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, each multi-index
a=(a_{1},...,a_{d})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
we then set

U_{m}^{(a)}(s):=\exp{\Bigl(is\sum_{x\in\mathcal{L}}\chi_{m}(x)x^{a}n_{x}\Bigr)}.

(2.2)

By Assumptions
1.1
and
1.2
the series in the exponential
is a finite sum. As the charge operators are self-adjoint and
x^{a}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}^{a_{j}}\in\mathbb{R}
, this implies that
U_{m}^{(a)}(s)
is a unitary element of
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
The following proposition shows that these unitaries indeed approximate the multipole symmetries, which act as (
1.6
) on local observables.

Proposition 2.2
.

In the setting of Theorem
1.4
let
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
be an arbitrary multi-index.

(i)

For every
A\in\mathcal{U}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the limit
\tau_{s}^{(a)}(A)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}U_{m}^{(a)}(s)^{*}AU_{m}^{(a)}(s)
exists. It defines a strongly continuous, norm-preserving 1-parameter group
\tau^{(a)}=(\tau_{s}^{(a)})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
of
*
-automorphisms of
\mathcal{U}
.

(ii)

For
\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
and
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}
, Eq. (
1.6
) holds.

Proof.

For
\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
there exists
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that
|z|\leq m_{0}-R_{0}
for all
z\in\Lambda
, with
R_{0}
given in Assumption (
1.2
). By definition,
\chi_{m}(z)=1
for all
z\in\Lambda
and
m\geq m_{0}
. Since the
n_{z}
pairwise commute by assumption, this implies for all
m\geq m_{0}
and
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}
:

U_{m}^{(a)}(s)^{*}AU_{m}^{(a)}(s)=\exp{\Bigl(-is\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}z^{a}n_{z}\Bigr)}A\exp{\Bigl(is\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}z^{a}n_{z}\Bigr)}.

Here we have used that
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda}
commutes with
n_{z}
if
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})
is disjoint from
\mathrm{supp}(A)=\Lambda
. Furthermore, we have used that
\chi_{m}(z)=1
for all
z\in\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset
as
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\subseteq B_{R_{0}}(z)
.
The above computation shows that the sequence
\bigl(U_{m}^{(a)}(s)^{*}AU_{m}^{(a)}(s)\bigr)_{m}
is constant for
m\geq m_{0}
and hence in particular
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}U_{m}^{(a)}(s)^{*}AU_{m}^{(a)}(s)
exists and is of the form claimed in (
1.6
).

Using the fact that
U_{m}^{(a)}(s)

∎

Remark 2.5
.

The multipole symmetries of Proposition
2.2
can never be spontaneously broken on infinite
–

\beta=0
) KMS states of any time-evolution

on

. Let

be an infinite temperature KMS state and

(2.17)

In particular, no assumptions on the spatial dimension

Definition A.1

(A.1)

In case

[

is an

-function for every
\epsilon>0
.
For more general metric spaces with effective dimension
\gamma
considered in Assumption
1.1
, we have the following

Lemma A.1
.

In the set-up of Assumption
1.1
if
\lambda>1+\gamma
, then
F:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},r\mapsto(1+r)^{-\lambda}
is an
F
-function for
(\mathcal{L},D)
.

Proof.

If
\nu>0
, then for
x\in\mathcal{L}
fixed and with
r_{0}>0
and the effective dimension
\gamma
from (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}}\bigl(1+|x-z|\bigr)^{-\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\big|\mathcal{L}\cap B_{(j+1)r_{0}}(x)\big|\bigl(1+jr_{0}\bigr)^{-\nu}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}C\frac{\bigl((j+1)r_{0}\bigr)^{\gamma}}{\bigl(1+jr_{0}\bigr)^{\nu}}.

(A.2)

The right side is finite and independent of
x
if
\nu>\gamma+1
. Furthermore,
F
is clearly nonnegative and decreasing. The convolution property (
A.1
) is checked by elementary arguments.
∎

Given an
F
-function, the vector space

\mathcal{B}_{F}(\mathcal{L})\coloneqq\Bigl\{\Phi\Big|\;\Phi\;\mathrm{is}\;\mathrm{an}\;\mathrm{interaction}\;\mathrm{and}\;\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{L}}\frac{1}{F(D(x,y))}\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\;x,y\in\Lambda}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|<\infty\Bigr\}

(A.3)

of interactions

is known
[

, Sec. 4]
to be a Banach space when equipped with the

\|\Phi\|_{F}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{L}}\frac{1}{F(D(x,y))}\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\;x,y\in\Lambda}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|.

(A.4)

The sum on the right side is to be understood in the sense of
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\;x,y\in\Lambda}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|
with any sequence
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
and the limit is independent of the choice of the finite, increasing and exhausting
(\Lambda_{n})_{n}
. In fact, it is easy to see that for any
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}_{F}(\mathcal{L})
and
X\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
the limit

\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}),\Lambda\cap X\neq\emptyset}\phi(\Lambda)\coloneqq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
\Lambda\cap X\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\phi(\Lambda)

exists in
\mathcal{U}
and is independent of the sequence
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
.

The following is the content of
[
22
, Thm. 5.3]
(see also
[
20
]
) and
[
3
, Prop. 6.2.4]
reformulated for our set-up.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be the
C^{*}
-algebra of quasi-local operators associated with
(\mathcal{L},D)
,
F
an
F
-function as in Definition
A.1
and
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}_{F}(\mathcal{L})
.

(i)

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{U}
and any finite, increasing and exhausting sequence
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
, the limit

\alpha_{t}(A)\coloneqq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\exp{\Bigl(it\sum_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}}\phi(\Lambda)\Bigr)}A\exp{\Bigl(-it\sum_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}}\phi(\Lambda)\Bigr)}

(A.6)

exists in
\mathcal{U}
, is independent of the sequence
(\Lambda_{n})_{n}
, and the convergence is uniform in
t
on compact subsets.
The limit defines a strongly continuous 1-parameter group
(\alpha_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of
*
-automorphisms of
\mathcal{U}
.

(ii)

The infinitesimal generator
\delta
of
\alpha
includes the local algebra in its domain,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}\subseteq D(\delta)
. For any
A\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and any sequence
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow\mathcal{L}

\delta(A)=i\sum_{\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})}[\phi(\Lambda),A]\coloneqq i\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Bigl[\sum_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}}\phi(\Lambda),A\Bigr].

(A.7)

A.2
Sufficient conditions

In this subsection we establish some sufficient conditions for the assumptions of our main theorem to hold. We start by spelling out a sufficient condition on the
F
-function to satisfy the decay condition (
1.8
) in Assumption
1.3
.

Lemma A.3
.

Consider a countable metric space
(\mathcal{L},D)
that satisfies Assumption
1.1
and a family of charge operators
\{n_{z}|\;z\in\mathcal{L}\}
as in Assumption
1.2
. Given
\Phi\in\mathcal{B}_{F}(\mathcal{L})
with an
F
-function that satisfies

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}}\Bigl(\sum_{z^{\prime}\in B_{R_{0}}(z)\cap\mathcal{L}}F\bigl(|x-z^{\prime}|\bigr)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-z|^{k+1}<\infty

(A.8)

with the constant
R_{0}
from Assumption
1.2
, then (
1.8
) holds.

Proof.

For
\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L})
we abbreviate
E(\Lambda)\coloneqq\{z\in\mathcal{L}\ |\ \mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\}
.
If
\Lambda_{n}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
and
x\in\mathcal{L}
is fixed, we conclude from (
A.5
):

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\\
x\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|

\displaystyle\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}|z-x|^{k+1}\Bigr)^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
x\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\sum_{y,z\in E(\Lambda)}|x-y|^{k+1}|x-z|^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{y,z\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
x\in\Lambda,y,z\in E(\Lambda)\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\||x-y|^{k+1}|x-z|^{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{y,z\in\mathcal{L}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
x\in\Lambda,y\in E(\Lambda)\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-y|^{k+1}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
x\in\Lambda,z\in E(\Lambda)\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-z|^{k+1}.

(A.9)

By Definition
1.2
we have
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\subseteq B_{R_{0}}(z)
. Hence, for fixed
x,z\in\mathcal{L}
by definition of
E(\Lambda)
:

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
x\in\Lambda,z\in E(\Lambda)\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\leq\sum_{z^{\prime}\in B_{R_{0}}(z)\cap\mathcal{L}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
x,z^{\prime}\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\leq\|\Phi\|_{F}\sum_{z^{\prime}\in B_{R_{0}}(z)\cap\mathcal{L}}F(|x-z^{\prime}|),

(A.10)

where we used the definition (
A.4
) of the
F
-norm.
Combining this with (
A.2
) yields

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\\
x\in\Lambda\end{subarray}}\|\phi(\Lambda)\|\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{L}:\\
\mathrm{supp}(n_{z})\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}|z-x|^{k+1}\Bigr)^{2}\\
\leq\|\Phi\|_{F}\Biggl(\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}}\Bigl(\sum_{z^{\prime}\in B_{R_{0}}(z)\cap\mathcal{L}}F\bigl(|x-z^{\prime}|\bigr)\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-z|^{k+1}\Biggr)^{2}.

Hence (
A.8
) guarantees (
1.8
).
∎

As a corollary, we show that fast enough polynomial decay is sufficient for the growth condition (

) to hold true.

.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1
is satisfied.
If

and
F:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},r\mapsto(1+r)^{-\lambda}
is an

-function for

with

, then
\Phi
satisfies the decay condition (

).

Proof.

By Lemma

it is enough to show that

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{L}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{L}}\Bigl(\sum_{z^{\prime}\in B_{R_{0}}(z)\cap\mathcal{L}}\bigl(1+|x-z^{\prime}|\bigr)^{-\lambda}\Bigr)^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-z|^{k+1}<\infty,

(A.11)

where

is the constraint on the support of the

as defined in Assumption

. This is a straightforward computation using (

) and (

).
∎

Appendix B
Mermin-Wagner criterion

The Mermin-Wagner criterion in Proposition
2.1
is known, yet somwhat hidden in the literature. It appeared in Fröhlich-Pfister’s proof
[
6
]
of the absence of symmetry breaking for two-body translation-invariant potentials on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. A related entropy-based argument is in
[
4
,
24
]
(see also
[
3
, Thm. 5.3.33]
).
For the convenience of the reader, we sketch the argument in
[
6
, Ch. 3]
.

To prove that

\omega\circ\tau=\omega

for all KMS-states
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
and the multipole symmetry
\tau
, the key quantity to control is the relative entropy, which in our set-up is the monotone limit of the state’s restriction to the finite-dimensional subalgebras
\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}
for any
\Lambda_{m}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
:

0\leq S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}S\left((\omega|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}})|((\omega\circ\tau)|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}})\right)=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}S\left((\omega|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}})|((\omega\circ\tau)|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}})\right),

(B.1)

see
[
25
, Prop. 5.23]
.
The fact that the limit is monotone is known as Uhlman’s monotonicity
[
25
, Thm. 5.3]
. A key fact is the following criterion.

Lemma B.1
.

If
S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau)<\infty
for all
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
, then
\omega=\omega\circ\tau
for all
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Thm. 5.3.30]
the finiteness of the relative entropy entails that
\omega=\omega\circ\tau
for all extremal KMS-states
\omega
. Note that symmetries of
\alpha
leave
K_{\beta}(\alpha)
invariant
[
3
, Prop. 5.3.33]
and therefore
\omega\circ\tau\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
is again extremal.
As the set of KMS-states is convex
[
3
, Thm. 5.3.30]
, this implies that precomposing by
\tau
leaves any KMS-states invariant.
∎

In the setting of Proposition
2.1
, the symmetry
\tau
is approximated by a sequence
\tau_{m}:\mathcal{U}\ni A\mapsto U_{m}^{*}AU_{m}
generated by local unitaries
(U_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc}}
corresponding to
\Lambda_{m}\uparrow\mathcal{L}
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}

\tau(A)=\tau_{m}(A)\;\mathrm{for}\;\mathrm{all}\;A\in\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}.

(B.2)

This implies that the right side in (
B.1
) is bounded according to

S\Bigl((\omega|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}})\Big|\bigl((\omega\circ\tau)|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}}\bigr)\Bigr)=S\Bigl((\omega|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}})\Big|\bigl((\omega\circ\tau_{m})|_{\mathcal{U}_{\Lambda_{m}}}\bigr)\Bigr)\leq S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau_{m}),

(B.3)

where the inequality is Uhlman’s monotonicity.
In order to further estimate
S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau_{m})
, one represents
\omega\circ\tau_{m}
as a perturbation of the original
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
, see Lemma
B.3
below.

To briefly review the notion of perturbation of a KMS-state, let
(\mathcal{H},\pi,\Omega)
be the GNS-triple associated to
\omega
on
\mathcal{U}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space,
\pi:\mathcal{U}\rightarrow L(\mathcal{H})
is a multiplicative and additive map from the quasi-local algebra to the bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
such that for all
A\in\mathcal{U}

\pi(A^{*})=\pi(A)^{*},\qquad\omega(A)=\langle\Omega,\pi(A)\Omega\rangle.

(B.4)

(For an overview of the GNS-construction, see
[
22
, Sec. 7.1]
and
[
19
, Ch. 2.5]
.) Given the time-evolution
\alpha
on
\mathcal{U}
, there exists a self-adjoint operator,
H:\mathcal{H}\supseteq D(H)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, called the GNS-Hamiltonian. The unitary group
(e^{itH})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
implements the time evolution
\alpha
in the GNS-representation

\pi\bigl(\alpha_{t}(A)\bigr)=e^{itH}\pi(A)e^{-itH}\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\mathrm{all}\;t\in\mathbb{R},\;A\in\mathcal{U}.

(B.5)

For a self-adjoint element
W\in\mathcal{U}
, one then shows
[
3
, Thm. 5.4.4]
that
\Omega\in D\bigl(e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H-\pi(W))}\bigr)
and

\omega^{W}:\mathcal{U}\ni A\mapsto\bigl\langle e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H-\pi(W))}\Omega,\pi(A)e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H-\pi(W))}\Omega\bigr\rangle

(B.6)

defines non-negative linear functional on
\mathcal{U}
(not necessarily normalized). Furthermore,
\omega^{W}
satisfies the
\beta
-KMS condition for a perturbed time-evolution
\alpha^{W}
on
\mathcal{U}
, which is constructed via a Dyson series
[
3
, Thm. 5.4.4]
and satisfies

\pi(\alpha_{t}^{W}(A))=e^{it(H-\pi(W))}\pi(A)e^{-it(H-\pi(W))}\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\mathrm{all}\;t\in\mathbb{R},\;A\in\mathcal{U}.

(B.7)

Finally, we recall from
[
3
, Prop. 6.2.32]
that

S(\omega|\omega^{W})=-\beta\omega(W)

(B.8)

for all self-adjoint
W\in\mathcal{U}
.

We apply the above construction with two choices:

1. The original KMS-state
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
and
W
equal to

h_{m}\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\in P_{0}(\mathcal{L}):\\
\Lambda\cap Q_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\phi(\Lambda),

(B.9)

where
Q_{m}
is the (finite) support of
U_{m}
, which also coincides with the support of
U_{m}^{*}
. The term
h_{m}
is self-adjoint and bounded, cf. (
A.5
). It represents the total interaction associated with
Q_{m}
. Removing this term results in a state
\omega^{h_{m}}
, which is
tracial on
\mathcal{U}_{Q_{m}}
and hence invariant under
\tau_{m}
. This is the content of

Lemma B.2
.

With
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
and
h_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, from (
B.9
):

\omega^{h_{m}}=\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}\otimes(\omega^{h_{m}}|_{\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{L}\setminus Q_{m}}}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}:\mathcal{U}_{Q_{m}}\ni A\mapsto\frac{\mathrm{tr}(A)}{\mathrm{tr}(\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{U}_{Q_{m}}})}.

(B.10)

Consequently,
\omega^{h_{m}}=\omega^{h_{m}}\circ\tau_{m}
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Thm. 5.4.4]
we know that
\omega^{h_{m}}\in K_{\beta}(\alpha^{h_{m}})
. Since the quasi-local algebra
\mathcal{U}
is simple
[
2
, Cor. 2.6.19]
,
\omega^{h_{m}}
is faithful
[
3
, Thm. 5.3.10]
. In particular,
\omega^{h_{m}}(B)>0
for any positive, non-zero
B\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{L}\setminus Q_{m}}
. Hence

\omega^{h_{m}}_{B}:\mathcal{U}_{X}\ni A\mapsto\bigl(\omega^{h_{m}}(B)\bigr)^{-1}\omega^{h_{m}}(AB)

(B.11)

is a well-defined state on
\mathcal{U}_{Q_{m}}
. We now consider
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{U}_{Q_{m}}
. Then
\alpha^{h_{m}}_{t}(A_{1})=A_{1}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and, since this is constant, the identity analytically extends to
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Applying the KMS-condition for
\omega^{h_{m}}
with respect to
\alpha^{h_{m}}
we hence conclude

\displaystyle\omega^{h_{m}}_{B}(A_{1}A_{2})=

\displaystyle\bigl(\omega^{h_{m}}(B)\bigr)^{-1}\omega^{h_{m}}(A_{1}A_{2}B)=\bigl(\omega^{h_{m}}(B)\bigr)^{-1}\omega^{h_{m}}\bigl(A_{2}B\alpha^{h_{m}}_{i\beta}(A_{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigl(\omega^{h_{m}}(B)\bigr)^{-1}\omega^{h_{m}}(A_{2}A_{1}B)=\omega^{h_{m}}_{B}(A_{2}A_{1}).

For the last equality we have used that
[A_{1},B]=0
due to disjoint supports. Therefore,
\omega_{B}^{h_{m}}
is a tracial state, and since
|Q_{m}|<\infty
, the unique such state is
\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}

[
30
, Lemma IV.4.1]
.
Thus, for all positive
B\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{L}\setminus Q_{m}}
and all
A\in\mathcal{U}_{Q_{m}}
we have

\omega^{h_{m}}(A\otimes B)=\omega^{h_{m}}(AB)=\omega_{\mathrm{tr}}(A)\omega^{h_{m}}(B).

This extends to all
B\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{L}\setminus Q_{m}}
by decompositions into positive parts, which completes the proof of (
B.10
).

Since
Q_{m}
is the support of
U_{m}
, the cyclicity of the trace and (
B.10
) imply
\omega^{h_{m}}\circ\tau_{m}=\omega^{h_{m}}
.
∎

2. With
\omega
replaced by
\omega^{h_{m}}
and
W=-h_{m}
from (
B.9
).
Let
(\mathcal{H}_{m},\pi_{m},\Omega_{m})
stand for the GNS-triple of the positive functional
\omega^{h_{m}}
. In this representation, the automorphism
\tau_{m}
is unitarily implemented by
V_{m}\coloneqq\pi_{m}(U_{m})
and the invariance
\omega^{h_{m}}=\omega^{h_{m}}\circ\tau_{m}
from Lemma
B.2
translates to
V_{m}\Omega_{m}=\Omega_{m}
. Likewise, since
\alpha^{h_{m}}_{t}(U_{m})=U_{m}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the unitary time-evolution of the self-adjoint GNS-Hamiltonian
H_{m}:\mathcal{H}_{m}\supseteq D(H_{m})\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{m}
leaves
V_{m}
invariant,

V_{m}=\pi_{m}(U_{m})=\pi(\alpha_{t}^{h_{m}}(U_{m}))=e^{itH_{m}}V_{m}e^{-itH_{m}}\;\;\mathrm{for}\;\mathrm{all}\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

(B.12)

Moreover,
\pi_{m}(h_{m})
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}
and thus

V_{m}e^{it\pi_{m}(h_{m})}V^{*}_{m}=\exp{\bigl(itV_{m}\pi_{m}(h_{m})V^{*}_{m}\bigr)}=\exp{\bigl(it\pi_{m}(U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*})\bigr)}.

(B.13)

Moreover, the Trotter product formula is applicable
[
32
]
and yields

\displaystyle V_{m}\exp{\bigl(it(H_{m}+\pi_{m}(h_{m})\bigr)}\Omega_{m}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}V_{m}\bigl(e^{\frac{it}{n}H_{m}}e^{\frac{it}{n}\pi_{m}(h_{m})}\bigr)^{n}\Omega_{m}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}V_{m}\bigl(e^{\frac{it}{n}H_{m}}e^{\frac{it}{n}\pi_{m}(h_{m})}\bigr)^{n}V^{*}_{m}\Omega_{m}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigl(e^{\frac{it}{n}H_{m}}e^{\frac{it}{n}\pi_{m}(U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*})}\bigr)^{n}\Omega_{m}=\exp{\bigl(it(H_{m}+\pi_{m}(U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*})\bigr)}\Omega_{m}.

The last line is based on (
B.12
) and (
B.13
), and again the Trotter product formula.
It is then standard procedure (cf.
[
30
, Thm IV.5.5]
) to use the KMS-condition to analytically continue this expression to
t=i\beta/2
and obtain

V_{m}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H_{m}+\pi_{m}(h_{m}))}\Omega_{m}=e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H_{m}+\pi_{m}(U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*}))}\Omega_{m}.

(B.14)

With these preparations, we establish the crucial explicit form of the symmetry-transformed KMS-state.

Lemma B.3
.

With
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
and
h_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, from (
B.9
):

\omega\circ\tau_{m}=\omega^{h_{m}-U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*}},\qquad\omega\circ\tau_{m}^{-1}=\omega^{h_{m}-U_{m}^{*}h_{m}U_{m}}.

(B.15)

Proof.

Writing
\omega=(\omega^{h_{m}})^{-h_{m}}
and using the definition (
B.6
) of the perturbed functional, we conclude for all
A\in\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\omega\circ\tau_{m}(A)

\displaystyle=\langle e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H_{m}-\pi_{m}(-h_{m}))}\Omega_{m},V^{*}_{m}\pi_{m}(A)V_{m}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}(H_{m}-\pi_{m}(-h_{m}))}\Omega_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\Bigl\langle\exp{\Bigl(-\frac{\beta}{2}\bigl(H_{m}-\pi_{m}(-U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*})\bigr)\Bigr)}\Omega_{m},\pi_{m}(A)\exp{\Bigl(-\frac{\beta}{2}\bigl(H_{m}-\pi_{m}(-U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*})\bigr)\Bigr)}\Omega_{m}\Bigr\rangle

\displaystyle=(\omega^{h_{m}})^{-U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*}}(A)=\omega^{h_{m}-U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*}}(A).

The second equality followed from (
B.14
) and the two remaining equalities are applications of (
B.6
) again. This proves the first equality in (
B.15
). The second one follows similarly.
∎

We are now in the position to finalize the argument for Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

From (
B.1
) combined with (
B.3
) we conclude

\displaystyle S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau_{m})

\displaystyle\leq S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau_{m})+S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau_{m}^{-1})

\displaystyle=-\beta\omega(h_{m}-U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*})-\beta\omega(h_{m}-U_{m}^{*}h_{m}U_{m})

(B.16)

where the equality is due to (
B.15
) and (
B.8
). Conversely, (
A.7
) implies

\displaystyle-i\bigl(U_{m}\delta(U_{m}^{*})+U_{m}^{*}\delta(U_{m})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}}\left([U_{m}[\phi(\Lambda),U_{m}^{*}]+U_{m}^{*}[\phi(\Lambda),U_{m}]\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda_{n}:\\
\Lambda\cap Q_{m}\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(U_{m}\phi(\Lambda)U_{m}^{*}-\phi(\Lambda)+U_{m}^{*}\phi(\Lambda)U_{m}-\phi(\Lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=U_{m}h_{m}U_{m}^{*}-h_{m}+U_{m}^{*}h_{m}U_{m}-h_{m}.

(B.17)

Hence
S(\omega|\omega\circ\tau_{m})\leq\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl|\omega\bigl(U_{m}\delta(U_{m}^{*})+U_{m}^{*}\delta(U_{m})\bigr)\bigr|
, which concludes the proof of (
2.1
). The rest of the assertion follows from Lemma
B.1
.
∎

Appendix C
Generalization: Mermin-Wagner on a slab

In this Appendix we prove a variant of our main theorem. As a motivation, let us first consider the following example

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{Z}^{l}\times\{1,...,M_{l+1}\}\times...\times\{1,...,M_{d}\}

for some
M_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the effective dimension is
l
, i.e., for
m\geq\max_{j}M_{j}
,

|\mathcal{L}\cap[-m,m]^{d}|=(2m+1)^{l}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{d-l}M_{l+j},

and therefore
\tau^{(a)}
cannot be spontaneously broken for
l\leq 2(k-|a|+1)
if the other assumptions of Theorem
1.4
hold. This illustrates that the
l
-dimensional ”slab”
\mathbb{Z}^{l}\times\{1,...,M_{l+1}\}\times...\times\{1,...,M_{d}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite thickness in the other
d-l
spatial directions behaves effectively like an
l
-dimensional system for the Mermin-Wagner theorem. However, Assumption
1.3
of Theorem
1.4
still needs the symmetries
\tau^{(a)}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and not just
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{l}\times\{0\}^{d-l}
.
We can however modify the Mermin-Wagner theorem for the spontaneous breaking of
\tau^{(a)}
with
b_{l+1}=...=b_{d}=0
so to only require the presence of the multipole symmetries
\tau^{(a)}
with
a_{l+1}=...=a_{d}=0
. We stress that one does not require that
\mathcal{L}
can be realized as a Cartesian product of a square lattice and a finite set. Rather we have the following variant of Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem C.1
.

Suppose there is a subset
I\subseteq\{1,...,d\}
and constants
M_{j}\geq 0
for
j\in I
such that
|x_{j}|\leq|M_{j}|
for all
x\in\mathcal{L}
and all
j\in I
. Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and assume that Assumptions
1.1
,
1.2
hold. Furthemore, assume that Assumption
1.3
hold, with (
1.7
) valid only for every multi-index
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|a|\leq k
and
a_{j}=0
for every
j\in I
.

Fix some inverse temperature
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. If
a\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
is a fixed multi-index with
|a|\leq k
and
\gamma\leq 2(k-|a|+1)
, then the multipole symmetry
(\tau_{s}^{(a)})_{s\in\mathbb{R}}
is preserved on every
\beta
-KMS state
\omega
for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
; that is
\omega\circ\tau_{s}^{(a)}=\omega
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega\in K_{\beta}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Theorem
C.1
can be proven in the same fashion as Theorem
1.4
by combining Proposition
2.1
and the second part of Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Since we do not require any translational invariance of
\mathcal{L}
, Theorems
1.4
and
C.1
can in particular be applied to lattice models of Moiré materials or similar constructions where one stacks several 2D ”slices” on top of each other in a not necessarily translationally invariant way.

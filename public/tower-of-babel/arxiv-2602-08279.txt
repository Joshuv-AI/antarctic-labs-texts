Characterizations of Conditional Mutual Independence: Equivalence and Implication
Laigang Guo, Tao Guo, and Raymond W. Yeung

Abstract
Conditional independence, and more generally conditional mutual independence, are central notions in probability theory. In their general forms, they include functional dependence as a special case. In this paper, we tackle two fundamental problems related to conditional mutual independence. Let K K and K ′ K^{\prime} be two conditional mutual independncies (CMIs) defined on a finite set of discrete random variables. We have obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for i) K K is equivalent to K ′ K^{\prime} ; ii) K K implies K ′ K^{\prime} . These characterizations are in terms of a canonical form introduced for conditional mutual independence.

II
Preliminaries

A CMI can readily be expressed in terms of the Shannon entropy (hereafter “entropy”). As such, it is very useful for tackling probability problems whose random variables satisfy certain conditional mutual independence conditions. Moreover, the very rich set of operations associated with entropy and related information measures can very often greatly simplify the proofs.

In this section, we first define entropy and related information measures. Then we state without proof some important properties of these information measures that will be used in proving the results. For a comprehensive treatment of this topic, we refer the reader to

Definition II.1
.

The entropy

H(X)=-\sum_{x}p(x)\log p(x).

(3)

Definition II.2

\hskip-56.9055pt\begin{array}[]{lll}\mbox{Conditional Entropy}&&H(X|Y)=H(X,Y)-H(Y)\\

Proposition II.1

(Chain Rule for Conditional Entropy)
.

H(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}|Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i}|X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1},Y).

Proposition II.2

(Chain Rule for Conditional Mutual Information)
.

I(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n};Y|Z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}I(X_{i};Y|Z,X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1}).

Corollary II.1

I(X_{1},X_{2};Y|Z)\geq I(X_{1};Y|Z).

Proof.

The corollary is proved by considering

I(X_{1},X_{2};Y|Z)=I(X_{1};Y|Z)+I(X_{2};Y|Z,X_{1})\geq I(X_{1};Y|Z),

∎

\displaystyle=

.

We remarked that in order to employ Shannon’s information measures, it is required that they all take finite values. We now elaborate this point in our setting.
From Proposition

H(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}H(X_{i}).

In other words, if

(C,\langle\ \rangle)
as
(\cdot,\langle\ \rangle)
.

Since a degenerate CMI on
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}
is always valid, it imposes no constraint on the joint distribution of
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}
. As all degenerate CMIs are equivalent, with an abuse of notation, they will all be written as
(\cdot,\langle\ \rangle)
. In other words, we write
K=(\cdot,\langle\ \rangle)
to mean that
K
is a degenerate CMI.

Let
K=(C,\langle Q_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
, where
k\geq 2
. By Proposition
III.1
,
K
is valid if and only if
J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})=0
. If
Q_{1}=\cdots=Q_{k}=Q
and
J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})=0
,
then we can obtain from the latter that

kH(X_{Q}|X_{C})-H(X_{Q}|X_{C})=(k-1)H(X_{Q}|X_{C})=0,

which implies
H(X_{Q}|X_{C})=0
since
k\geq 2
, so that
K
specifies the
functional dependency
“
X_{Q}
is a function of
X_{C}
”. From this point of view, functional dependence is a special case of conditional mutual dependence.

In the rest of the paper, unless otherwise specified, we always assume that
k\geq 0
.

Definition III.6
.

Let
K=(C,\langle Q_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
. Then
K
is said to be in pure form if for all
i
,
Q_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
Q_{i}\cap C=\emptyset
.

Definition III.7
.

Let
K=(C,\langle Q_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
. Let
K^{\prime}=(C,\langle Q_{i}^{\prime},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
where
Q_{i}^{\prime}=Q_{i}\backslash C
, with
Q_{i_{j}}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
for
1\leq j\leq l
and
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{l}\leq k
. Let

{\rm pur}(K)=(C,\langle Q_{i_{j}}^{\prime},1\leq j\leq l\rangle)
.

It is evident that for any CMI
K
,
{\rm pur}(K)
is a CMI in pure form, and is called the pure form of
K
.

Proposition III.4
.

Let
K=(C,\langle Q_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
. Then
J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})=0
if and only if
J(X_{Q_{i}\backslash C},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})=0
.

Proof.

Let
R_{i}=C\cap Q_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, so that
R_{i}\subseteq C
and
R_{i}\subseteq Q_{i}
for all
i
. Then

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})&=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}}|X_{C})\right]-H(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})\\
&=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}},X_{R_{i}}|X_{C\backslash R_{i}},X_{R_{i}})\right]-H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}},X_{R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C\backslash R_{i}},X_{R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k)\\
&=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}}|X_{C\backslash R_{i}},X_{R_{i}})\right]-H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C\backslash R_{i}},X_{R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k)\\
&=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}}|X_{C})\right]-H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})\\
&=J(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C}).\end{array}

Thus
J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})=0
if and only if
J(X_{Q_{i}\backslash R_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})=0
.
∎

As an example for Proposition
III.4
,
(\{1,2,5\},\langle\{1,2\},\{1,2,3\},\{1,4\}\rangle)
is valid if and only if
(\{1,2,5\},\langle\emptyset,\{3\},\{4\}\rangle)
is valid, where the latter is equivalent to
(\{1,2,5\},\langle\{3\},\{4\}\rangle)
.

By Proposition
III.4
, we can obtain the follow corollary.

Corollary III.1
.

Let
K=(C,\langle Q_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
. Then
K\sim{\rm pur}(K)
.

In view of the above discussion, without loss of generality, we always assume in the rest of this section that a CMI is in pure form.

Lemma III.1

If

with

Proof.

Consider any repetitive index

Then

If

Lemma III.3

Let

\begin{array}[]{ll}J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})&=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}}|X_{C})\right]-H(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})\\
&\overset{\eqref{add3}}{=}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{K}}|X_{C})\right]-H(X_{\mathbb{I}_{K}},X_{\mathbb{I}_{K}},X_{(\cup_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i})\backslash\mathbb{I}_{K}}|X_{C})\\

.

.

Proposition III.7
.

Let
K=(C,\langle Q_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle)
,
\mathbb{I}_{K}
be the set of all repeated indices of
K
, and
P_{j}=Q_{i_{j}}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{K}
, with

, be the nonempty sets of

’s. Then

is valid if and only if
(C,\langle\mathbb{I}_{K},\mathbb{I}_{K}\rangle)
and
(C,\langle P_{i},1\leq i\leq t\rangle)
are both valid.

Proof.

Assume that
(C,\langle\mathbb{I}_{K},\mathbb{I}_{K}\rangle)
and
(C,\langle P_{i},1\leq i\leq t\rangle)
are both valid. From the former we have
H(X_{\mathbb{I}_{K}}|X_{C})=I(X_{\mathbb{I}_{K}};X_{\mathbb{I}_{K}}|X_{C})=0
, and from the latter we have

.
Then

\displaystyle J(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})

\displaystyle=\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}}|X_{C})\right]-H(X_{Q_{i}},1\leq i\leq k|X_{C})

\displaystyle\overset{}{=}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}H(X_{Q_{i}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{K}}|X_{C})\right]-H(X_{(\cup_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i})\backslash\mathbb{I}_{K}}|X_{C})

Lemma III.4
.

Let
A_{i},1\leq i\leq k

be

and

be

nonempty disjoint sets of indices, where

. If

\langle A_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle\neq\langle B_{j},1\leq j\leq l\rangle
, then there exists a pair
(i,j)
such that
A_{i}\cap B_{j}\neq\emptyset
, and either
A_{i}\backslash B_{j}\neq\emptyset

B_{j}\backslash A_{i}\neq\emptyset
.

Assume that
A=B
and
\langle A_{i},1\leq i\leq k\rangle\neq\langle B_{j},1\leq j\leq l\rangle
. We observe that under these assumptions, for any

, there exists
\alpha(i)\in\mathcal{N}_{l}
such that

.
We will prove the lemma by contradiction. Assume the contrary, i.e., for any pair
(i,j)
, we have
A_{i}\backslash B_{j}=\emptyset
and
B_{j}\backslash A_{i}=\emptyset
. Then
A_{i}\backslash B_{\alpha(i)}=\emptyset
and

, so we obtain
A_{i}=B_{\alpha(i)}
for

.
Since
A_{i}
’s are disjoint, we have

and

’s are disjoint, which implies that for all

, if

If

, then

. If

, then

. The proof is accomplished.
∎

.

IV
Implication of a CMI

In probability problems, we are often given a CMI or a set of CMIs, and we need to determine whether another given CMI is logically implied. This very basic problem, referred to as the
implication problem
, has a
solution if only FCMIs are considered
[
6
]

[
11
]
(see also
[
12
, Section 12.2]
).
The general implication problem, however, has recently been proved to be undecidable if the number of random variables involved is unbounded
[
18
]
.
2
2

2

A problem is undecidable if it is a decision problem for which it is impossible to construct a single algorithm that always produces a correct “yes” or “no” answer for every possible input in finite time.

The implication problem is subsumed by the
p
-
representability
problem, which studies
the compatibility of CIs. The latter problem has been solved only up to four random variables
[
10
]
, whose proof involves a variation of the constrained non-Shannon-type inequality reported in
[
7
]
.

It is noteworthy that the aforementioned problems are all subsumed by the problem of determining all achievable entropy functions (provided that the entropies of the random variables involved are finite), which not only is of fundamental importance in information theory, but also is intimately related to a number of subjects in information sciences (network coding theory, Kolmogorov complexity, cryptography), mathematics (probability theory, combinatorics, group theory, matrix theory, matroid theory), and physics (quantum mechanics). We refer the reader to
[
14
]
for an exposition on this topic.

In this section, we discuss the implication problem of a CMI, which is not necessarily in pure form. Specifically, for two CMIs
K
and

, we establish in Theorem

a necessary and sufficient condition for “

Definition IV.1

Proposition IV.1

Let

By Corollary

Thus,

∎

V
Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we studied conditional mutual independence for a finite set of discrete random variables. Our focus is on how to characterize a single conditional mutual independency (CMI). Specifically,
for two conditional mutual independencies
K
and
K^{\prime}
, we obtained complete characterizations for “
K
is equivalent to
K^{\prime}
” and “
K
implies
K^{\prime}
”. To our knowledge, these are the first such characterizations in the literature.

The results in this paper belong to two types. The first type are those proved by using Shannon’s information measures, and the second type are those proved by constructing the underlying probability distribution with certain required properties. In the following, we remark on the generality of these results:

•

The limitation of using Shannon information measures is that the entropies of the random variables involved must be finite. Nevertheless, results of the first type can instead be proved by working directly on the underlying probability distribution, although the proofs are in general very tedious. Moreover, these results can be proved for general probability distributions, without assuming that the random variables are discrete.

•

For the second type of results, with some suitable modifications, the construction of the underlying probability distribution continues to apply when the random variables are not discrete.

Hence, the results in this paper can readily be extended for general probability distributions.

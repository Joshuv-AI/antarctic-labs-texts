A general Fourier expansion of post-Newtonian binary dynamics based on quasi-Keplerian framework
Xiaolin Liu, Zhoujian Cao

Abstract
We have introduced a new Fourier-expansion technique for computing gravitational-wave emission from non-spinning binaries in the post-Newtonian framework. Using this approach, we derived the full set of 3PN dynamical quantities and gravitational-wave Fourier modes and have released the corresponding numerical code as open source. Furthermore, applying the method to the tail contribution of the energy flux, we found that it can be resummed into an exceptionally compact expression. These advances pave the way for more convenient and accurate frequency-domain waveform modeling in the future.

Moreover, in recent years, GW events with possible residual eccentricities have been identified

However, when we look ahead to future low-frequency detectors, constructing accurate frequency-domain GW models for generic orbits remains a difficult task.
The standard strategy is to express the post-Newtonian (PN) orbit in a quasi-Keplerian parametrization

Recent work has shown that by keeping the Bessel functions that naturally appear in the original Fourier modes of the gravitational radiation, one can avoid the accuracy loss at high eccentricity caused by the small-eccentricity expansion

Theoretical Background and Review

II.1
Post-Newtonian Dynamical System and the Quasi-Keplerian Parameterization

The post-Newtonian approximation solves the Einstein field equations perturbatively in the weak-field, slow-motion regime
Thibault and Gerhard 1085
;
Blanchet 2024
.
For a two-body system, the equations of motion can be expanded as a power series in
c^{-1}
.
Neglecting spin and radiation-reaction effects, the acceleration is now known through 4 PN order
Bernard et al. 2016
;
Bernard et al. 2018
,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{a}_{N}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\boldsymbol{a}_{\tiny{{1}\mathrm{PN}}}+\frac{1}{c^{4}}\boldsymbol{a}_{\tiny{{2}\mathrm{PN}}}+\frac{1}{c^{6}}\boldsymbol{a}_{\tiny{{3}\mathrm{PN}}}+\frac{1}{c^{6}}\bigl(\boldsymbol{a}_{\tiny{{4}\mathrm{PN}}}+\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathrm{tail}}\bigr)+\order{c^{-5}},

(1)

where the 4PN tail term

represents the back-reaction on the binary from the multipolar GW flux and is a manifestation of the non-linearity of general relativity
Blanchet and Damour 1988
;
Damour et al. 2014
;
Bernard et al. 2016
.
When this non-linear effect is ignored, the system can be described by the two conserved quantities, the energy
E
and the angular momentum
J

Starting at 2PN order the field integrals become divergent; to cure this one introduces Hadamard finite-part regularization

and dimensional regularizatioin, which in turn brings in logarithmic terms and an arbitrary length scale

.
A modified harmonic (MH) coordinate system is therefore defined to eliminate the logarithms

;
Blanchet et al. 2002

The PN two-body problem corresponds to a precessing elliptical orbit that can still be described in terms of the familiar orbital elements such as the eccentricity

This is the essence of the quasi-Keplerian parameterization

;

;

;

;

et al. 2025

The 4PN results of instantaneous part was found in ADM coordinates

;

\displaystyle a_{r}:=\frac{u_{+}+u_{-}}{2u_{+}u_{-}},\qquad e_{r}:=\frac{u_{-}-u_{+}}{u_{-}+u_{+}}.

(2)

The orbital period

to

\displaystyle u=\frac{1+e_{r}\cos\zeta}{a_{r}(1-e_{r}^{2})}=\frac{1}{a_{r}\big(1-e_{r}\cos\chi\big)},

(3)

The third angle, called true anomaly

\displaystyle\psi:=2\arctan\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{1+e_{\phi}}{1-e_{\phi}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\tan\frac{\chi}{2}\biggr)=2\arctan\biggl(\Bigl(\frac{1+e_{r}}{1-e_{r}}\Bigr)^{1/2}\tan\frac{\zeta}{2}\biggr),

(4)

Owing to later naming conflicts we employ a convention that differs from many references.
The mean anomaly

\displaystyle l

\displaystyle=\chi-e_{t}\sin\chi+\Big(\frac{1}{c^{4}}g_{4t}+\frac{1}{c^{6}}g_{6t}+\frac{1}{c^{8}}g_{8t}\Big)(\psi-\chi)+\Big(\frac{1}{c^{4}}f_{4t}+\frac{1}{c^{6}}f_{6t}+\frac{1}{c^{8}}f_{8t}\Big)\sin\psi+\Big(\frac{1}{c^{6}}i_{6t}+\frac{1}{c^{8}}i_{8t}\Big)\sin{2\psi}

(5)

We next introduce

\displaystyle x:=\omega^{2/3},\qquad\omega:=(1+k)n,

\displaystyle 1+k:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\biggl(-\int_{u_{+}}^{u_{-}}\frac{\differential\phi}{\differential u}\,\differential u\biggr).

II.2
General Method for Gravitational-Wave Calculations

In the PN-MPM framework
Thorne 1980
;
Blanchet and Damour 1986
, the GW polarizations
h_{+,\times}
measured by a distant observer depend on the radiative multipoles
(U_{L},V_{L})
, which in turn depend on the source multipoles
(I_{L},J_{L},\dots)
and non-linear hereditary terms
Blanchet 1998
.
All are ultimately functions of the source dynamics, schematically
h(v,e;l)
.
Within the QK framework one solves for the adiabatic evolution of
(v,e,l)
under radiation reaction.
After averaging over one radial period (the adiabatic or orbital average) one obtains
(\bar{v},\bar{e},\bar{l})
from the energy and angular-momentum fluxes at infinity,

\displaystyle\left\langle{\dot{\bar{v}}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{v}{E}\left\langle{\mathcal{F}}\right\rangle+\partialderivative{v}{J}\left\langle{\mathcal{G}}\right\rangle,

(11)

\displaystyle\left\langle{\dot{\bar{e}}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{e}{E}\left\langle{\mathcal{F}}\right\rangle+\partialderivative{e}{J}\left\langle{\mathcal{G}}\right\rangle,

(12)

where the orbital average
\left\langle{\cdot}\right\rangle
denotes

\displaystyle\left\langle{f}\right\rangle:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(l)\differential l.

(13)

The fluxes
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
are computed from the radiative multipoles; their difference from the actual loss of the system energy
E
and angular
J
is the Schott term
Bini and Damour 2012
, which vanishes upon orbital averaging for 2.5PN, 3.5PN and 4.5PN and doesn’t vanish for 4PN due to the hereditary effect
, leaving some freedom in the explicit form of the radiation-reaction force
Fumagalli et al. 2025
. The radiation-reaction force of non-spinning binary is known up to 4.5PN
Iye
;
Gopakumar et al. 1997
;
Leibovich et al. 2023
;
Blanchet et al. 2024
.
Multi-scale analysis then yields post-adiabatic corrections
Pound and Poisson 2008
;
Miller and Pound 2021
.
For current 3PN waveform models only the first-order correction
(\tilde{v},\tilde{e},\tilde{l})
is required
Königsdörffer and
Gopakumar 2006
, which can be evaluated from
(\bar{v},\bar{e},\bar{l})
and substituted into
h(v,e;l)
.

Because data analysis is performed mainly in the frequency domain, analytic Fourier approximations are highly desirable—especially for future low-frequency GW detectors.
In the non-spinning case one expands

\displaystyle h=\sum_{m,p}\tilde{h}_{mp}(v,e)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(m\lambda+pl)},

(14)

and applies the stationary-phase approximation (SPA) to each mode
Yunes et al. 2009
, expanding the phase

\displaystyle\phi_{mp}(t)=m\lambda+pl-2\pi ft

(15)

about the SPA time
t_{mp}
as

\displaystyle\phi_{mp}(t)\approx\phi_{mp}(t_{mp})+(t-t_{mp})\dot{\phi}_{mp}(t_{mp})+\frac{1}{2}(t-t_{mp})^{2}\ddot{\phi}_{mp},

(16)

with the condition

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{mp}=0\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;m\omega(t_{mp})+pn(t_{mp})=2\pi f:=v_{f}^{\,3}.

(17)

For non-spinning case one could assume the amplitude of the mode grows much slower than phase. However this do not hold for spin-precessing is included, where the Shifted Uniform Asymptotics (SUA) method is applied
Klein et al. 2018
.

Since post-adiabatic effects enter at
v^{5}
, one first solves for the averaged part, giving
\bar{v}^{3}=(m+p)^{-1}v_{f}^{3}\big(1+\order{\vavg}\big)
, and then substitutes
v_{f}
for
\bar{v}
in the corrections
(\tilde{v},\tilde{e},\tilde{l},\dots)
.

In principle there is no closed-form relation between
\bar{v}
,
\bar{l}
, and
\bar{e}
. One strategy is to expand
\bar{e}
around its initial value
e_{0}
, but high initial eccentricity requires very high expansion order.
Padé resummation has been introduced to improve accuracy in that regime
Tiwari and Gopakumar 2020
.
Recent work shows that one can directly integrate numerically with a standard Runge–Kutta scheme
Morras et al. 2025
, using
\bar{v}
rather than
t
as the independent variable, and then determine
t_{mp}
by interpolation from the relation
\bar{v}^{3}=(m+p)^{-1}v_{f}^{3}\big(1+\order{\vavg}\big)
.
Given
f
and
(p,m)
, this yields the averaged orbital frequency
\bar{v}(t_{mp})
and all other dynamical quantities.

III
Fourier series of bounded-binary PN dynamics

Arun et al. 2008a
showed that, at Newtonian order, the Fourier modes of a binary system’s multipole moments can be expressed in terms of Bessel functions.
Because the gravitational field is sourced by these radiative multipole moments, its Fourier modes inherit the same structure.
Recent studies
Morras et al. 2025
;
Morras 2025
have demonstrated that this property can be exploited to compute arbitrary Fourier modes rapidly, thereby greatly improving the accuracy of eccentric-orbit models.
However, at higher PN orders ordinary Bessel functions are no longer sufficient; here we introduce a new family of generalized, Bessel-like elliptic integrals that can represent the Fourier modes of all post-Newtonian multipole moments.

III.1
Generalized Bessel elliptical function

The non-spinning binary’s dynamics in the PN framework is characterized by a double periodicity. The Fourier series of a dynamical quantity
f
takes the form

\displaystyle f(\lambda,\chi)=\sum_{p,m=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{f}^{(p,m)}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(p\ell+m\lambda)},

(18)

where the Fourier coefficient
\hat{f}^{(p,m)}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{(p,m)}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\iint_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(\lambda,\chi)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(p\ell+m\lambda)}\differential{\ell}\differential{\lambda}.

(19)

Here we use
\chi
is because In most cases the quantity depends on
\chi
rather than
l
.
And usually, the dependence on
\lambda
mainly comes from the orbital phase
\phi=\lambda+W(l)
, so we can simplify it via

\displaystyle f(\lambda,\chi)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{m}(\chi)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\lambda},

(20)

\displaystyle\hat{f}^{(p,m)}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f^{(m)}(\chi)\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}p\ell}\differential{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f^{(m)}(\chi)\derivative{\ell}{\chi}\differential{\chi}

(21)

One would find that after sorting, up to 3PN order, these Fourier coefficients can be expressed by two kinds of integrals,

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pqa}(e):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(p\chi-qe\sin\chi)}}{(1-e\cos\chi)^{a}}(\mathrm{i}\delta\chi)^{n}\differential\chi,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pqa}(e):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(p\chi-qe\sin\chi)}}{(1-e\cos\chi)^{a}}(\mathrm{i}\delta\chi)^{c}\ln(1-e\cos\chi)\differential\chi,

(23)

These integrals can be viewed as extensions of Bessel functions or Hansen coefficients
Mikóczi et al. 2015
.
And also can be regarded as the extension of
Munna and Evans 2020
;
Munna and Evans 2022
;
Trestini 2025a
.
For example,
\mathrm{J}^{(0)}_{pq0}\equiv J_{p}(qe)
.
The factor
(1-e\cos\chi)
shows in the denominator can be regarded as the derivative with respect to
l
, since

\displaystyle\derivative{f}{l}=\derivative{f}{\chi}\derivative{\chi}{l}=\frac{1}{1-e\cos\chi}\derivative{f}{\chi}+\order{c^{-4}}.

(24)

At 3PN order, we only encounter the term
\mathrm{J}^{(3)}_{pqa}
and
\mathrm{K}^{(0)}_{pqa}
.
By observing the symmetry of the integrands we can quickly obtain some properties,

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(-p)(-q)a}=(-1)^{n}\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pqa},\ \mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(-p)(-q)a}=\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pqa}.

(25)

And the properties of differentiation,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{e}\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pqa}=\frac{q}{2}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}-\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}\Big)+\frac{a}{2}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}+\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big)-\frac{n}{2\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n-1)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}-\mathrm{J}^{(n-1)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big),

(26)

\displaystyle\partialderivative{e}\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pqa}=\frac{q}{2}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}-\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}\Big)+\frac{a}{2}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}+\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big)-\frac{n}{2\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n-1)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}-\mathrm{K}^{(n-1)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}+\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big),

(27)

And recursion identities

\displaystyle 2p\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pqa}=e\bigg[q\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}+\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big)+a\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}-\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big)\bigg]+\frac{2n\beta_{e}(1-\beta_{e})}{1+\beta_{e}^{2}}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n+1)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}+\mathrm{J}^{(n+1)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big),

(28)

\displaystyle 2p\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pqa}=e\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}-\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big)+e\bigg[q\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}+\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big)+a\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}-\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2n\beta_{e}(1-\beta_{e})}{1+\beta_{e}^{2}}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n+1)}_{(p-1)q(a+1)}+\mathrm{K}^{(n+1)}_{(p+1)q(a+1)}\Big),

(29)

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pq(a-1)}=\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pqa}+\frac{e}{2}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}+\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big),\ \mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pq(a-1)}=\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pqa}+\frac{e}{2}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}+\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big),

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{e}{2}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}-\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big)=\frac{1}{1-a}\bigg(p\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pq(a-1)}+q\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pq(a-2)}-\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{pq(a-1)}\Big)+n\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\mathrm{J}^{(n-1)}_{pqa}\bigg),

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{e}{2}\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}-\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big)=\frac{1}{1-a}\bigg(p\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pq(a-1)}+q\Big(\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pq(a-2)}-\mathrm{K}^{(n)}_{pq(a-1)}\Big)+n\sqrt{1-e^{2}}\mathrm{K}^{(n-1)}_{pqa}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{e^{2}}{2}\Big(\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p+1)qa}-\mathrm{J}^{(n)}_{(p-1)qa}\Big)\bigg)

(32)

At some special cases, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}^{(0)}_{00a}=\frac{1}{(1-e^{2})^{a/2}}P_{a-1}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-e^{2}}}\Big),

(33)

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}^{(0)}_{00a}=\frac{(-1)^{a-1}}{(a-1)!}\Big(\derivative[a-1]{Y(y,e)}{y}\Big)_{y=1},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}^{(0)}_{p00}=-\frac{\beta_{e}^{|p|}}{|p|}.

(35)

where
P_{N}(x)
is Legendre polynomial and

\displaystyle Y(y,e)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{y^{2}-e^{2}}}\Big[\ln\Big(\frac{\sqrt{1-e^2}+1}{2}\Big)+2\ln\Big(1+\frac{\sqrt{1-e^2}-1}{y+\sqrt{y^2-e^2}}\Big)\Big].

(36)

These integrals generally do not have a closed form, but they can be easily expanded as power series of eccentricity. We reorganize them into the infinite series of Bessel function and

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}^{(0)}_{pqa}=(1+\beta_{e}^{2})^{a}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}J_{k}(qe)\mathcal{L}_{k+p}^{(a)},

(37)

\displaystyle\mathrm{J}^{(1)}_{pqa}=(1+\beta_{e}^{2})^{a}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\delta=\pm 1}\frac{\delta\beta_{e}^{n+1}}{n+1}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}J_{k}(qe)\mathcal{L}_{k+p+\delta(n+1)}^{(a)},

(38)

Gravitational waveform

The GWs emitted to null infinity can be expressed in transverse-traceless frame
h_{ij}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{TT}}}
constituted by two polarizations

III.3

IV
Re-expression of tail effects in GW fluxes

The GW energy flux is consist of instantaneous part, which is directly determined by the dynamics of the source, and hereditary part, which is caused by non-linear interactions.
The reason for the nonlinear effect is the interaction between the radiative moments and the source-type moments or themselves.
Formally these hereditary part are expressed as integrals.
These genetic terms can be divided into a tail part and a memory part. The former originates from the interaction related to the radiative moments, while the latter originates from the change of the radiative moment. Sometimes the memory part is also classified as the instantaneous part
Arun et al. 2008b
, while the energy flux would becomes instantaneous after taking a time derivative and the angular momentum would be hereditary
Trestini 2025a
.

Generally, the energy and angular momentum fluxes of GW read

(96)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{i}=\epsilon_{iab}\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\frac{1}{c^{2\ell+1}}\Big[\tilde{a}_{\ell}U_{a(L-1)}U^{(1)}_{b(L-1)}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\tilde{b}_{\ell}V_{a(L-1)}V^{(1)}_{b(L-1)}\Big],

V
Summary

With the rapid progress of GW observations and the planning of next‑generation detectors, the demand for more accurate waveform templates has intensified.
This paper introduces a new mathematical framework that compactly captures the dynamics of compact binaries and the high‑order PN structure of their gravitational radiation.
We also release a ready‑to‑use software package implementing the method.
We then apply the framework to the computation of the tail contribution to binary waveforms and derive a highly general re-summed expression.
This result streamlines the evaluation of tail terms in the fluxes at higher PN orders.

At present, our treatment is restricted to non-spinning binaries.
The inclusion of spins renders the Fourier structure substantially more intricate; extending the method to generic spinning, possibly precessing, orbits is an important topic for future work.
Moreover, the current method is highly compute-intensive.
When the eccentricity becomes large, the convergence of the J-integrals slows markedly, and the overall cost of waveform generation grows almost exponentially.
Developing more efficient algorithms to mitigate this scaling will thus be a key challenge for future work.
In addition, one proof step in our tail‑flux derivation remains open.
We have not accounted for higher‑order hereditary effects arising from couplings among multipole moments or from tail–memory interactions, nor have we incorporated radiation‑reaction forces.
Addressing these limitations will require further effort.

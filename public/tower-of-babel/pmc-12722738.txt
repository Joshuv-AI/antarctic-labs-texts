Robust securable economic operation of grid-connected renewable integrated energy system with hydrogen storage and electric and fuel cell vehicles
Authors: Hadi Varei, Hadi Zayandehroodi, Mehdi Jafari Shahbazzadeh, Mahdiyeh Eslami
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe economic operation of the smart distribution network based on voltage security and environmental indicators considering renewable integrated energy systems based on hydrogen storage and electric vehicles is presented in this study. Renewable solar, bio-waste, and wind resources are used in integrated system. Battery-based electric vehicles, plug-in hybrid electric cars, and fuel cell-based electric vehicles are present as mobile storage in the integrated system. The weighted sum of the voltage profile, operating cost, and environmental pollution are minimized for a deterministic model. This problem includes power flow constraints, security and operation limitations, and the operating formulation of renewable units, hydrogen storage, various electric vehicles, and the power and hydrogen balancing in the integrated system. Fuzzy decision-making technique obtains a compromise point. In the following, a linear formulation for the aforementioned study is obtained. The proposed design has uncertainty parameters such as price of energy, renewable power rate, load, charge/discharge rate, and initial/final energy of electric cars. To achieve a robust solution to the prediction error of uncertainties, this paper uses adaptive robust optimization to appropriately formulation the aforementioned uncertain quantities. Finally, the findings indicate the suggested design’s potential for improving the environmental, technical and economic conditions of the smart distribution grid with energy management of the aforementioned integrated unit. Thus, the power management of stationary and mobile storage devices in the renewable integrated system is able to improve the security of voltage, operational, economic and environmental status of the smart distribution system by about 42.5%, 43.9%-57.6%, 26% and 62%, respectively, in the worst case scenario compared to network load flow analysis.Keywords: Eco-securable operation, Electric vehicles, Hydrogen storage, Integrated energy system, Renewable sources, Smart distribution networkSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

In this paper, the optimal operation constrained by different indicators in a smart distribution network in the presence of integrated energy systems is presented. The details of the design are given below.

Motivation

Integrated energy systems (IESs) are adopted for integrating and coordinating energy sources and storage devices, as well as producing and storing energy in distribution grid1. Aiming to diminish pollutant emissions, renewable energy sources (RESs) such as wind system (WS), photovoltaics (PV), and bio-waste systems (BS) in IESs play a significant use. Due to the uncertainty of RES output power, there are power fluctuations in RESs2. Therefore, to compensate for this issue, energy storage devices are used in IESs3. Of course, the use of storage devices is not only to deal with power intermittency of RES, but also with optimal energy management of storage devices, various technical and economic goals can be achieved in IESs and the network3. Electric vehicles (EVs) have also been emerged as a means of diminishing pollutant amount released to the environment. EV types are: hybrid EV (HEV), battery-based EV (BEV), plug-in HEV (PHEV), and fuel cell-based EV (FCEV)4,5. In the first type, the vehicle battery is charged through the kinetic energy available in the EV’s motion. However, in the second and third types, EVs are connected to the power system to supply the battery and receive significant energy from the grid. In the fourth type, the EV fuel is hydrogen5. Today, in order to improve environmental conditions, hydrogen production is provided by electrolyzer connected to renewable sources or the power system. Although EVs appear as a load in the grid, with their optimal energy management, EVs can be activated as mobile storage devices4. Therefore, it is expected that this boosts the economic and technical objectives of the grid and IES. By implementing an energy management system (EMS) in a distribution grid with IESs, steps can be taken to improve the environmental, economic, and technical conditions of the grid. In EMS, power supplies and storage are generally managed by the IES operator. So that this operator achieves the optimal unit commitment for the mentioned elements by receiving the capacity data of the supplies and storage equipment6. Note however that the aforementioned operator has bidirectional communication with the distribution system operator (DSO). So, the energy scheduling of the IES is accompanied by the estimation of the technical limits of the network and the demands of the DSO. Therefore, it is predicted that with optimal energy scheduling in distribution system with renewable IESs based on mobile and stationary storage devices, optimal economic, environmental, security and operational goals can be achieved.

Research background

Numerous studies and efforts have focused on optimizing the operation and energy management of IESs within distribution networks. For instance, Ref.7 introduces a multi-time-scale game-theoretic optimization framework for Park-level IESs, incorporating various demand response models. In the day-ahead phase, an optimization scheduling model is developed, leveraging a detailed incentive-based demand response mechanism. The uncertainty associated with predicted distributed renewable energy outputs and diverse energy loads is captured using fuzzy chance-constrained programming. Similarly, Work in8 proposes a synergistic planning approach for IESs equipped with hydrogen storage (HS). This model integrates the use of both electrical and thermal energy to significantly reduce carbon emissions and enhance energy efficiency. An electric-thermal coupling model for the hydrogen energy storage unit is formulated, alongside an optimization method that considers the dual utilization of electrical and thermal resources. In9, a low-carbon economic operational model is presented, which accounts for the variable load characteristics on the demand side. It incorporates critical systems such as combined heat and power (CHP), a carbon capture system (CCS), and power-to-gas (P2G). This study explores the integration of source-side and demand-side scheduling to improve system operation comprehensively. Work in10 develops a data-driven two-stage distributionally robust optimization (DRO) model to manage uncertainties in RESs alongside integrated demand response. This model employs a blended uncertainty set based on the 1-norm and ∞-norm to sidestep complex probability density functions. A nonlinear cooperative optimization model for regional IESs is introduced in11, emphasizing multi-region energy sharing and multi-energy storage. A two-tier optimization strategy is proposed, with the upper tier focusing on renewable energy and storage capacity allocation, while the lower tier concerns operational dispatch. To tackle challenges related to robustness, economy, environmental protection, and efficiency, Ref.12 proposes an IES group (IESG) framework through energy sharing and carbon transfer. This framework uses a two-stage distributionally robust optimization (TSDRO) model developed with kernel density estimation (KDE) and the Wasserstein metric. The study includes modeling projected power errors for wind turbines and photovoltaics through KDE-based probability density functions. Ref.13 investigates the factors contributing to the integrated benefit (IB) index of IESs along with optimal energy supply strategies by developing a two-stage planning and operational optimization model.

Focusing on smart distribution networks (SDNs), Ref.14 examines energy management strategies that integrate hydrogen storage and renewable energy sources. The study’s objective function minimizes operational costs while improving flexibility, reliability, and reducing energy losses. The analysis is subject to AC optimal power flow equations, network reliability constraints, and integrated system models. Ref.15 establishes a conventional multi-regional IES (MIES) model constrained by global carbon emission limits. This approach is framed as a partially observable Markov decision-making process (POMDP) and implements an enhanced multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) algorithm. The centralized training but decentralized execution (CTDE) method substantially improves multi-agent stationarity. In16, a Stackelberg game-based simulation model for energy utilization is introduced. It incorporates integrated demand response (IDR) with user-side flexible resources (FRs) while considering consumer comfort costs. Meanwhile, Ref.17 focuses on developing a competitive yet collaborative optimization strategy that accounts for interactions between supply and demand. It integrates carbon trading incentives on the supply side alongside uncertain user behavior in demand response. The framework further accommodates stakeholders’ roles and energy-carbon market mechanisms, incorporating psychological factors affecting consumers’ responses. Lastly, Ref.18 outlines a synergy planning approach for Regional IESs (RIES), emphasizing cascade energy utilization while addressing uncertainty. It introduces a dual-layer synergy planning model, where the higher layer targets station-level optimization while addressing interconnected operational challenges.

Ref.19 proposes an IES operation strategy that combines the adiabatic compressed air energy storage (A-CAES) device and the integrated demand response (IDR) theory with the two-layer optimization model, and comprehensively considers the interaction between the planning and operation of two different time scales. The improved two-level optimal scheduling method in this paper takes IES operators and users as different subjects, and coordinates the output of multiple energy sources on the basis of meeting IES constraints to minimize the cost. In20, the system operator is accountable for reducing the weighted sum of the voltage security index, energy loss, and energy cost of the distribution network. This problem is associated with the optimal power flow formulation, which considers the environmental limits and security of voltage in the distribution network, the renewable resource operation model and flexibility in the form of a virtual power plant, and the system’s flexibility constraints. Refs.21 and21 discusses the simultaneous management of active and reactive power of a flexible renewable energy-based virtual power plant placed in a smart distribution system, based on the economic, operational, and voltage security objectives of the distribution system operator. Work of22 introduces a scenario-based scheduling approach tailored for the smart grid. The problem is formulated with a dual-objective function that addresses both emissions and costs, focusing on the traditional unit commitment while incorporating electric vehicles (EVs) and renewable energy technologies (RETs). Meanwhile, study in23 explores the effects of high penetration of renewable energy sources (RES) and EVs on generation scheduling and total system costs. A two-stage model is proposed: the first stage involves an aggregator framework for scheduling EVs based on anticipated day-ahead electricity market prices, while the second stage focuses on a market model designed to achieve optimal generation scheduling that integrates RES and EV considerations.

Research gaps

Research limitations for managing energy of IESs in distribution networks according to Table 1 and the research background are:

The study of the operational and economic indicators of distribution grid with IESs has been carried out in most research. In the economic field, minimizing the cost of network operation has generally been considered. In the issue of operation, the status of the voltage profile and energy losses has generally been examined. However, the distribution network has other important indicators. Among the most important technical indices in distribution grid, especially the low-voltage distribution network, is voltage security. The length of the feeders in the distribution network is generally long. Therefore, the loads at the end of the feeder have a low voltage level. Therefore, if the loading increases at these points, voltage collapse may occur. However, IESs are located in consumption areas that can take steps to improve voltage security by delivering power into the network. Yet, it has rarely been considered in studies, such as in21. Nowadays, providing clean energy using environmentally friendly technologies has received much attention. This issue has been included in many studies. But in few studies, such as9,12,13,15,17,19, the numerical amount of environmental pollution in the energy management system has been calculated. But to know the status of an indicator, it should be measured, which can be extracted using the mathematical model of the aforementioned indicator. In addition, in the aforementioned studies, the reduction of carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions has generally been considered. However, the supply of electrical energy from centralized power plants includes CO2, sulfur dioxide (SO2) and nitrogen dioxide (NOx) emissions. The simultaneous reduction of these emissions has been considered in few studies.

The energy scheduling scheme of IESs in the distribution grid is accompanied by various uncertainty parameters. In7–20, the uncertainty for price of energy, load, and renewable power are generally considered. In addition to these parameters, the uncertainty parameters of EVs are modeled in21. In most of the research, stochastic optimization models the uncertainty parameters. Since the aforementioned scheme has a high number of uncertainty parameters, stochastic optimization needs tremendous scenarios to extract a trustable solution, which can extend the problem size and, consequently, computing time will be long. The aforementioned scheme is the operation problem; whose execution step is small. Thus, in some applications like real-time operation, the execution step reaches 5 min. Therefore, the low computational time in power system operation problems is of particular importance. However, stochastic optimization does not satisfy the aforementioned conditions. To compensate for this, some research such as [10 and 12] uses robust optimization to model uncertainties. This technique has only one scenario called the worst-case scenario. Therefore, the computing time in this technique is less than stochastic optimization. It is also able to extract a solution that is robust to the maximum prediction error of uncertainties. However, in [10 and 12], robust optimization is only used for load parameters, energy prices, and renewable resources, and modeling the uncertainties of different types of EVs is not considered.

Research contributions

The energy scheduling of SDNs with the contribution of renewable IESs is presented in this study to estimate the security, operational, economic, and environmental goals of DSO as shown in Fig. 1. IES has renewable resources such as WS, PV and BS. The stationary storage of HS type is used in IES. IES also includes mobile storage of BEV, PHEV and FCEV types. In the deterministic formulation of study, minimizing the weighted sum of the SDN operating cost, the voltage deviation function, and the SDN environmental emissions (including CO2, SO2 and NOx) is the objective function. Constraints contain AC optimal power flow model subject to the voltage security constraint in SDN, the operating formulation of the renewable units, HS and EVs in the form of IES. By changing the weight coefficients in the objective function, different optimal points are obtained. In this paper, the fuzzy decision making approach extracts a compromise point. In the next step, a linear approximation formulation for scheme is obtained. One of the objectives of this paper is to use robust optimization to fill the last research gap. This technique requires a linear model of a problem. Here, the linear programming of the design is expressed. The uncertainty parameters in this scheme include load, energy price, renewable energy generation rate, discharge/charge rate, and EVs initial/final energy. Adaptive Robust Optimization (ARO) provides a suitable modeling of the aforementioned uncertainties. This technique has only one scenario, which includes the worst-case value of the uncertainty parameters proportional to the objective function. Therefore, the aforementioned technique obtains the most robust energy management for IES, such that it is robust against the maximum prediction error of uncertainties. The deterministic multi-objective energy management of SDN with IESs

Non-linear formulation

In this part, a deterministic nonlinear formulation of SDN energy management with renewable IESs is presented. Goal function is equal to minimizing the weighted sum of energy cost, voltage profile function, and emissions in SDN. It is constrained by the AC optimal power flow and grid voltage security limits, and the operation formulation of renewable IESs with stationary and mobile storages. In this model, only one scenario is considered, which includes the expected (average) amount of uncertainties. The details of the suggested design are presented in the following parts.

Conclusions

The optimal operation of a renewable IES with electric vehicles and hydrogen storage in a SDN was presented, considering economic, environmental, voltage security, and operation objectives of the grid operator. In the deterministic model, the objective function is equal to the minimization of the weighted sum of the operating cost, emissions, and the grid voltage profile function. This function is constrained by the linearized optimal power flow equations, the grid voltage security limits, and the operation model of renewable resources, electric vehicles, and hydrogen storage in the form of an IES. A fuzzy decision-making algorithm was used to extract a compromise solution. This scheme has uncertainties in load, renewable power, energy price, and various parameters of electric vehicle aggregation. This paper uses adaptive robust optimization to model uncertainties in order to achieve operation that is robust against uncertainty prediction errors. Based on the numerical results, it was seen that the fuzzy decision-making algorithm was able to obtain a compromise point where the operating cost functions, network pollution and voltage profile are close to their minimum values. So, the aforementioned functions are about 23.8%, 23.3% and 21.9% away from their minimum values at the compromise point, respectively. In the worst-case scenario, the energy consumption of electric vehicles and passive loads increases than in the scenario corresponding to the deterministic model, while generation energy capacity of RESs and EVs decreases. However, energy management of IES in the distribution network has made the proposed scheme robust against a maximum prediction error of 30% for the aforementioned uncertainties. In addition, energy management of various electric vehicles and hydrogen storage devices along with RESs in the form of an IES has improved the economic, environmental, voltage security, and operation states of the distribution network by about 42.5%, 62%, 26%, and 43.9%-57.6%, respectively, compared to load flow studies.

Selfless Reduced Free Products and Graph Products of C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -algebras
Felipe Flores, Mario Klisse, Mícheál Ó Cobhthaigh, Matteo Pagliero

Abstract
. Under mild assumptions, we prove that reduced free products and reduced graph products of C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -algebras are selfless in the sense of L. Robert, without assuming the rapid decay property. In particular, our main theorems yield numerous new examples of simple, monotracial C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{\ast} -algebras with strict comparison, stable rank one, and admitting a unique unital embedding of the Jiang–Su algebra 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} up to approximate unitary equivalence.



Introduction

The Murray–von Neumann comparison of projections is fundamental to the theory of von Neumann algebras and the type classification of factors.
For general
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, the scarcity of projections (or lack thereof) requires an appropriate new version of the Murray–von Neumann comparison of projections.
In
[
10
]
, Cuntz proposed to define a comparison relation for all positive elements with the intent of exploring dimension functions on simple
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras.
For positive elements
a,b
in a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
A
, one says that
a
is
Cuntz subequivalent
to
b
if there exists a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A
such that
x_{n}bx_{n}^{*}\rightarrow a
.
This comparison is encoded by the partial order on what is nowadays known as the
Cuntz semigroup

\mathrm{Cu}(A)
of
A
, a partially ordered Abelian monoid constructed out of the positive elements of the stabilization of
A
, i.e.,
A\otimes\mathcal{K}
, and the Cuntz relation described above.
The object
\mathrm{Cu}(A)
plays an important role in the overarching theory of classification of separable, nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras as it constitutes the finer invariant used by Toms to establish that
K
-theory and traces (and the pairing between them) alone are not enough to classify all simple, nuclear, separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras
[
46
]
.
When the traces control this partial order in a suitable sense, one says that the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra has
strict comparison of positive elements
(or
strict comparison
for short).
Consider for a moment a simple, unital, monotracial and exact
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
(A,\tau)
, and denote the canonical extension of
\tau
to
A\otimes\mathcal{K}
by
\tau
as well.
If
d_{\tau}(b)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau(b^{1/n})
for every
b\in(A\otimes\mathcal{K})_{+}
, then whenever
a
is another positive element that is Cuntz subequivalent to
b
, one has that
d_{\tau}(a)\leq d_{\tau}(b)
.
Using the same notation as before, it is well-known that
(A,\tau)
has strict comparison precisely when the opposite implication holds true, i.e.,
d_{\tau}(a)\leq d_{\tau}(b)
implies that
a
is Cuntz subequivalent to
b
, for all
a,b\in(A\otimes\mathcal{K})_{+}
.
This property was first defined by Blackadar in
[
4
]
, and provides the best analogue of the Murray–von Neumann comparison of projections for factors.
Strict comparison is a key property for
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, and has deep connections with other regularity conditions;
most importantly with tensorial absorption of the Jiang–Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}

[
32
]
.
A
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
A
is said to be
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
if
A\cong A\otimes\mathcal{Z}
.
By the main result of
[
42
]
,
\mathcal{Z}
-stable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras have strict comparison.
It is important to mention here that
\mathcal{Z}
-stability features prominently in the stably finite part of the classification of separable, simple, nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, known as the Elliott classification program (
[
7
,
18
,
19
,
29
,
30
,
16
,
17
,
43
,
45
]
to name a few of the latest contributions).
It is thus desirable that it is satisfied by many
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras.
In particular, it is an open problem, and the missing piece to the famous Toms–Winter conjecture
[
31
,
49
,
50
,
51
]
, whether all separable, simple, nuclear, non-elementary
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras with strict comparison are
\mathcal{Z}
-stable.
However, the groundbreaking work of Matui and Sato
[
35
]
shows that this is the case if one additionally assumes unitality and finitely many extremal traces, thus granting access to classification via strict comparison.
It should also be mentioned that strict comparison has deep connections with stable rank one in the sense of Rieffel
[
39
]
(see
[
33
]
) and with other relevant problems, such as the classification of embeddings of the Jiang-Su algebra
\mathcal{Z}
up to approximate unitary equivalence
[
40
]
, and the recent (negative) solution to the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebraic Tarski problem
[
28
]
.

Nonetheless, finding criteria that ensure strict comparison for wider classes of (possibly non-nuclear)
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras is not an easy task.
This is well exemplified by the fact that, until recently, it was an open problem whether the reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra of the free group
\mathbb{F}_{n}
has strict comparison when
2\leq n<\infty
.
(The case of
\mathbb{F}_{\infty}
was settled by Robert in
[
40
, Proposition 6.3.2]
, where he attributes the result to Rørdam.)
This problem has now been answered in the positive by Amrutam, Gao, Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, and Patchell in
[
1
]
, where they prove a much stronger property than strict comparison alone.
To be precise, they prove that finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic groups with trivial finite radical and satisfying the rapid decay condition are
C
∗
-selfless
. In particular, this implies that their reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras are
selfless
in the sense of Robert
[
41
]
, when considered as a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-probability space, equipped with the canonical trace.

When he introduced selfless
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, Robert was inspired by Dykema and Rørdam’s work on infinite reduced free products
[

and their notion of eigenfree
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-probability spaces
[

.
As Robert suggests, selfless
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras (with a faithful trace) can be thought of as a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebraic analogue of
{\rm II}_{1}
-factors.
Existential embeddings can also be defined in a model-theoretic way, a point of view that is omitted here in favor of a more
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}

Such embeddings are related to approximate splitting through the ultrapower (see

[

In
[
3

, it is also established that the existence of a sequentially split
\ast
-homomorphisms between separable
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras
A\to B
forces that many structural properties of
B
such as simplicity, stable rank one and strict comparison are passed to
A
, essentially because of their
\forall\exists
-axiomatizability.
For the same reason, if a tracial
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
(A,\tau)
is selfless, it must be simple, monotracial, and have strict comparison and stable rank one (see
[
41
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Amrutam–Gao–Kunnawalkam Elayavalli–Patchell’s work
[
1
]
has already fueled a series of generalizations of the main result contained therein, and opened the way to new similar results by different authors. We will proceed to briefly mention them now. Indeed, selflessness has been proven for reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras associated with higher rank lattices
[
47
]
, free products of groups with subexponential decay
[
27
]
, CAT(0) groups
[
34
]
, and groups which satisfy a generalized Powers averaging property
[
37
]
. Regarding strict comparison for twisted group
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras of acylindrically hyperbolic groups with rapid decay, we invite the reader to consult
[
38
]
. When it comes to abstract
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, the absolute state of the art result is the main theorem of
[
22
]
(and the follow-up work
[
21
]
about inclusions), which establishes that free products of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras with rapid decay and the
Avitzour condition
are selfless.
Originating in
[
2
]
, the Avitzour condition is the assumption that there exist unitaries
u,v_{1},v_{2}
as in Theorem
A
.

Inspired by Ozawa’s work
[
37
]
, we develop a new approach to obtain selflessness for certain natural classes of abstract
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras.
The main result of the present work shows that reduced free products of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-probability spaces are selfless if they satisfy the Avitzour condition.
We stress that we do not assume the rapid decay property, and thus obtain a full-fledged generalisation of Theorem B in
[
22
]
.
However, we remark that the rapid decay property remains relevant in the overarching picture.
In fact, in certain cases (see
[
22
, Theorem C]
) it allows one to obtain selfless reduced free products without using the Avitzour condition.

In the following results, if
\rho
is a state on a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
A
, we denote by

its centralizer.

(see Theorem

)
.

Let
(A,\omega_{A})
and
(B,\omega_{B})
be unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}

\omega_{A}
and
\omega_{B}

\mathrm{C}^{\ast}

(A,\omega_{A})

Then it follows from Avitzour’s work

Moreover, in

Graph products of operator algebras have been introduced and studied by Mlotkowski
[
36
]
, Speicher and Wysoczański
[
44
]
, and Caspers and Fima
[
8
]
. One may think of a reduced graph product of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras as a
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra with an associated graph encoding the (non-)commutativity between its generating subalgebras.
To be more precise, consider a finite, undirected, simplicial graph
\Gamma
(i.e.,
\#V\Gamma<\infty
, edges have no direction, and there are no loops or multiple edges) with set of vertices
V\Gamma
, and assume that each vertex
v\in V\Gamma
is the label of a unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
A_{v}
, equipped with a GNS-faithful state
\omega_{v}
, and set
\mathbf{A}=(A_{v})_{v\in\Gamma}
.
Then the
reduced graph product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra

(\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma},\omega_{\Gamma})
is a unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra equipped with a GNS-faithful state that contains the vertex algebras
(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
in a canonical way, and such that two of these
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebras commute precisely when there is an edge between their labeling vertices.

Graph products unify both Voiculescu’s free products (see
[
48
]
and also
[
2
]
) and tensor products.
Indeed, if there are no edges in the graph
\Gamma
, the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras
(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
are in free position with respect to each other, and in fact
(\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma},\omega_{\Gamma})=\star_{v\in V\Gamma}(A_{v},\omega_{v})
. On the other hand, if the graph is complete (i.e., every pair of vertices shares an edge), then
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}=\otimes_{v\in V\Gamma}A_{v}
.
In the second of our main results, we show that reduced graph products of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras are selfless provided that a suitable adaptation of the Avitzour condition is satisfied and that the graph product does not decompose as a tensor product of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras associated to subgraphs.
The latter condition is embodied by the assumption that the complement graph
\Gamma^{c}
(see Section
1.3
) is connected.

1.
Preliminaries and Notation

1.1.
General Notation

We denote by
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
the set of non-negative integers, and by
\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}:=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
the set of positive integers. The identity element of a group is denoted by
e
.

Throughout, inner products on Hilbert spaces are taken to be linear in the second variable. For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we write
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
for the C
∗
-algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
.

1.2.
Ultrapowers

Fix a free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. For a C
∗
-algebra
A
, set

\ell^{\infty}(A):=\{(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq A\mid\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|a_{n}\|<\infty\},\qquad\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}:=\{(a_{n})\in\ell^{\infty}(A)\mid\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\|a_{n}\|=0\},

and define the
ultrapower
of
A
with respect to
\mathcal{U}
by
A^{\mathcal{U}}:=\ell^{\infty}(A)/\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}
. If
\rho
is a state on
A
, its
ultralimit

\rho^{\mathcal{U}}
on
A^{\mathcal{U}}
is given by
\rho^{\mathcal{U}}([(a_{n})_{n}])=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\rho(a_{n})
for every sequence
(a_{n})_{n}\in\ell^{\infty}(A)
, whose class in
A^{\mathcal{U}}
is denoted by
[(a_{n})_{n}]
.

1.3.
Graphs

Given a graph
\Gamma
, its
vertex set
is denoted by
V\Gamma
, and its
edge set
by
E\Gamma
. Throughout this article, all graphs are assumed to be finite, undirected, and
simplicial
, that is,
E\Gamma\subseteq(V\Gamma\times V\Gamma)\setminus\{(v,v)\mid v\in V\Gamma\}
. We define the set of all
words
in
V\Gamma
by

\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}:=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\underbrace{(V\Gamma\times\cdots\times V\Gamma)}_{i\text{ times}}.

Such words are typically denoted by boldface letters. Note that, unlike in
[
8
]
, we assume that
\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
contains the empty word
\emptyset
.

Following
[
20
]
and
[
8
]
, we endow
\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
with the
shuffle equivalence
relation
\sim
, generated by the rule

(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},v_{i},v_{i+1},\ldots,v_{n})\sim(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1},v_{i+1},v_{i},\ldots,v_{n})\quad\text{whenever }(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in E\Gamma.

(1.1)

Two words belonging to the same equivalence class under this relation are said to be
shuffle equivalent
.

Similarly, for
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
, we write
\mathbf{v}\simeq\mathbf{w}
and say that they are
equivalent
if they belong to the same equivalence class generated by shuffle equivalence together with the additional relation that
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i},v_{i+1},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{n})
is equivalent to
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{i},v_{i+2},\ldots,v_{n})
whenever
v_{i}=v_{i+1}
.

For a vertex
v\in V\Gamma
, the
link
of
v
, denoted
\mathrm{Link}(v)
, is the subgraph of
\Gamma
induced by the vertex set
\{\,w\in V\Gamma\mid(v,w)\in E\Gamma\,\}
. The
star
of
v
, denoted
\mathrm{Star}(v)
, is the subgraph of
\Gamma
induced by
\{v\}\cup V(\mathrm{Link}(v))
.

A word
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
is called
reduced
if, for every pair of indices
1\leq i<j\leq n
with
v_{i}=v_{j}
, there exists an index
i<k<j
such that
v_{k}\notin\mathrm{Star}(v_{i})
. We denote by
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{red}}
the set of all reduced words.

The
length
of a word
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
, denoted by
|\mathbf{v}|
, is the length of the shortest representative in its equivalence class. Note that a word
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
is reduced if and only if
|\mathbf{v}|=n
.

By
[
8
, Lemma 1.3]
, any two equivalent reduced words
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
admit a unique permutation
\sigma
of the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that

\mathbf{w}=(v_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,v_{\sigma(n)})\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\quad\text{ if }i>j\text{ and }v_{i}=v_{j}.

In view of the discussion above, we fix a subset
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{red}}
consisting of representatives of the shuffle equivalence classes. The elements of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}
are called
minimal words
. Every word in
\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
is equivalent to a unique minimal word.

For a finite, undirected, simplicial graph
\Gamma
, we denote its
complement
by
\Gamma^{c}
, which is the graph with vertex set
V\Gamma
and edge set
\{\,(v,v^{\prime})\in V\Gamma\times V\Gamma\mid v\neq v^{\prime},\;(v,v^{\prime})\notin E\Gamma\,\}
. The complement
\Gamma^{c}
is again finite, undirected, and simplicial.

A complete subgraph of
\Gamma
is called a
clique
. The
degree
of a vertex
v\in V\Gamma
is the number of elements
w\in V\Gamma
with
(v,w)\in E\Gamma
.

1.4.
Coxeter Groups

A
Coxeter group
is a group
W
that admits a presentation of the form

W=\left\langle S\,\middle|\,(st)^{m_{st}}=e\text{ for all }s,t\in S\right\rangle,

where
S
is an arbitrary generating set, and the exponents
m_{st}\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
satisfy
m_{ss}=1
and
m_{st}\geq 2
for all distinct
s,t\in S
. A relation of the form
(st)^{m}=e
is imposed when
m_{st}<\infty
; the case
m_{st}=\infty
indicates that no such relation is imposed. The pair
(W,S)
is referred to as a
Coxeter system
.

A Coxeter system is said to have
finite rank
if the set
S
is finite, and it is called
right-angled
if
m_{st}\in\{2,\infty\}
for all
s\neq t
, that is, if any two distinct generators either commute or generate an infinite dihedral group. In a right-angled Coxeter group, if a cancellation of the form
s_{1}\cdots s_{n}=s_{1}\cdots\widehat{s_{i}}\cdots\widehat{s_{j}}\cdots s_{n}
occurs for
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in S
, then
s_{i}=s_{j}
and
s_{i}
commutes with
s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{j-1}
. For further background on Coxeter groups, we recommend consulting
[
11
]
.

Given a Coxeter system
(W,S)
, we denote by
|\cdot|
the associated word length function. For elements
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in W
, we say that
\mathbf{w}

starts in

\mathbf{v}
if
|\mathbf{v}^{-1}\mathbf{w}|=|\mathbf{w}|-|\mathbf{v}|
, and write
\mathbf{v}\leq_{R}\mathbf{w}
. Similarly,
\mathbf{w}
is said to
end in

\mathbf{v}
if

|\mathbf{w}\mathbf{v}^{-1}|=|\mathbf{w}|-|\mathbf{v}|
, and we write
\mathbf{v}\leq_{L}\mathbf{w}
. Both relations define partial orders on
W
, known respectively as the
right weak Bruhat order
and the
left weak Bruhat order
. For notational convenience, we shall usually write
\leq
instead of
\leq_{R}
.

Given a finite, undirected, simplicial graph
\Gamma
, we associate a finite rank, right-angled Coxeter system
(W_{\Gamma},S_{\Gamma})
by setting

S_{\Gamma}:=V\Gamma\quad\text{and}\quad W_{\Gamma}:=\left\langle S_{\Gamma}\,\middle|\,s^{2}=e\text{ for all }s\in S_{\Gamma},\;st=ts\text{ whenever }(s,t)\in E\Gamma\right\rangle.

The group
W_{\Gamma}
can be identified with the set
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}
of minimal words (equivalently, with
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{red}}
modulo shuffle equivalence) via
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\mapsto v_{1}\cdots v_{n}
. Under this identification,
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}
inherits a natural group structure. Moreover, the right and left weak Bruhat orders on
W_{\Gamma}
induce corresponding partial orders on
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}
. For notational convenience, we shall henceforth write
W_{\Gamma}
in place of
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}
whenever no confusion arises. The length function
|\cdot|
on
\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{min}}
from the previous subsection coincides with the word length on
W_{\Gamma}
with respect to the generating set
S_{\Gamma}
.

1.5.
Graph Products of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial graph, and consider a collection
\mathbf{A}:=(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
of unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, each carrying a GNS-faithful state
\omega_{v}
. We identify
A_{v}
with its image inside
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{v})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{v}:=L^{2}(A_{v},\omega_{v})
is the associated GNS-Hilbert space and
\xi_{v}\in\mathcal{H}_{v}
denotes the corresponding cyclic vector. For
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{v})
, define
x^{\circ}:=x-\langle x\xi_{v},\xi_{v}\rangle 1
, and let
A_{v}^{\circ}:=\ker(\omega_{v})
. Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\circ}:=\mathcal{H}_{v}\ominus\mathbb{C}\xi_{v}
the orthogonal complement of
\xi_{v}
. For a reduced word
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{red}}
, we set
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\circ}:=\mathcal{H}_{v_{1}}^{\circ}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{H}_{v_{n}}^{\circ}
.

As explained in Subsection
1.4
, two equivalent reduced words
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})
are related via a unique permutation
\sigma
satisfying
\mathbf{w}=(v_{\sigma(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma(n)})
and
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)
whenever
i>j
and
v_{i}=v_{j}
. This gives rise to a unitary operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}
via
\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}\mapsto\xi_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{\sigma(n)}
.
For convenience, we suppress these unitaries from the notation and identify the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\circ}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}
through
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}}
. Furthermore, we identify
\mathcal{W}_{\text{min}}
with
W_{\Gamma}
, as in Subsection
1.4
.

The
graph product Hilbert space
(or
Fock space
) associated with this data is

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ},

where
\Omega
denotes the so-called
vacuum vector
. Occasionally, we may write
\mathcal{H}_{e}^{\circ}:=\mathbb{C}\Omega
.

For
v\in V\Gamma
,
x\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{v})
,
\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}
, and elementary tensors
\eta_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}
with
n=|\mathbf{w}|
, define

\displaystyle\lambda_{v}(x)(\eta_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\eta_{n})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x^{\circ}\eta_{v}\otimes\eta_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\eta_{n}+\langle x\eta_{v},\eta_{v}\rangle\eta_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\eta_{n},&\text{if }v\nleq\mathbf{w},\\
(x\eta_{1}-\langle x\eta_{1},\eta_{v}\rangle\eta_{v})\otimes\eta_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes\eta_{n}+\langle x\eta_{1},\eta_{v}\rangle\eta_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes\eta_{n},&\text{if }v\leq\mathbf{w}\text{ and }\eta_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\circ}.\end{cases}

This gives a faithful, unital
*
-homomorphism
\lambda_{v}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{v})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
, and the images of
\lambda_{v}
and
\lambda_{v^{\prime}}
commute whenever
(v,v^{\prime})\in E\Gamma
(see
[
8
, Subsection 2.1]
). The
algebraic graph product
is defined as

\star_{v,\Gamma}^{\mathrm{alg}}(A_{v},\omega_{v}):=\ast\text{-alg}\bigl(\{\lambda_{v}(a)\mid v\in V\Gamma,\,a\in A_{v}\}\bigr)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}),

and the corresponding
graph product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
is its norm closure:

\star_{v,\Gamma}(A_{v},\omega_{v}):=\overline{\star_{v,\Gamma}^{\mathrm{alg}}(A_{v},\omega_{v})}^{\|\cdot\|}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}).

We also set
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{alg}}:=\star_{v,\Gamma}^{\mathrm{alg}}(A_{v},\omega_{v})
and
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}:=\star_{v,\Gamma}(A_{v},\omega_{v})
.

An element
a\in\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
of the form
a=\lambda_{v_{1}}(a_{1})\cdots\lambda_{v_{n}}(a_{n})
with
a_{i}\in A_{v_{i}}^{\circ}
and
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathcal{W}_{\text{red}}
is called
reduced of type

\mathbf{v}:=v_{1}\cdots v_{n}
, and
\mathbf{v}\in W_{\Gamma}
is the
associated word
of
x
. We will often write
a_{\mathbf{v}}:=\lambda_{v_{1}}(a_{1})\cdots\lambda_{v_{n}}(a_{n})\in\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
, suppressing the maps
(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
for readability. For a reduced operator of type
\mathbf{v}\in W_{\Gamma}
, we refer to
|\mathbf{v}|
as its
length
and say that it
starts
in
\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}
if
\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{v}
.

The
graph product state

\omega_{\Gamma}
on
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
is defined as the vector state associated with
\Omega
, which is GNS-faithful. Moreover,
\omega_{\Gamma}(a)=0
for every reduced element
a\in\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
of type
\mathbf{v}\in W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}
.

Proposition 1.1

(see
[
8
, Proposition 2.12]
)
.

Let
B
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra with a GNS-faithful state
\omega
. Assume that for each
v\in V\Gamma
there exists a unital faithful
*
-homomorphism
\pi_{v}:A_{v}\to B
such that:

•

B
is generated by
\{\pi_{v}(a)\mid v\in V\Gamma,\,a\in A_{v}\}
.

•

\pi_{v}(A_{v})
and
\pi_{v^{\prime}}(A_{v^{\prime}})
commute whenever
(v,v^{\prime})\in E\Gamma
.

•

For each reduced operator
a=\lambda_{v_{1}}(a_{1})\dots\lambda_{v_{n}}(a_{n})
with
a_{i}\in A_{v_{i}}^{\circ}
, one has
\omega(a)=0
.

Then there exists a unique
*
-isomorphism
\pi:\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}\to B
with
\pi|_{A_{v}}=\pi_{v}
for all
v\in V\Gamma
, and satisfying

.

2.
Selflessness of Graph Product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-Algebras

In this section, we establish Theorems
A
and
C
. Our argument partially relies on the construction of certain (universal)
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras associated with reduced graph products of
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, a framework introduced and studied by the second author in
[

2.1.
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-Algebras from Reduced Graph Products

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial graph, and let
\mathbf{A}:=(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
be a collection of unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, each equipped with a GNS-faithful state
\omega_{v}
. As in Subsection
1.5
, denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}:=\mathbb{C}\Omega\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}
the associated graph product Hilbert space, and by
\star_{v,\Gamma}(A_{v},\omega_{v})
, or
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
, the corresponding graph product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra.
For each element
\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}
(as defined in Subsection
1.4
), let
Q_{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
denote the orthogonal projection onto the subspace
\bigoplus_{\mathbf{v}\in W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}:\,\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{v}}\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{v}}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
. We then define

\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma):=C^{\ast}\!\left(\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}\cup\{Q_{v}\mid v\in V\Gamma\}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}),

as the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma})
generated by
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
, together with all projections
(Q_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
.
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
denote the
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
consisting of all operators
x\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
that are
diagonal
, in the sense that

x(\mathbb{C}\Omega)\subseteq\mathbb{C}\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad x(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}\quad\text{for every }\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}.

By slightly abusing the notation, we denote by
\omega_{\Gamma}
the restriction of the vacuum vector state to
\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
. As before, we suppress the
\ast
-embeddings
(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
and simply view each element
a\in A_{v}^{\circ}
,
v\in V\Gamma
, as an element of
\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
.

Following the terminology of
[
25
, Definition 2.6]
, for each
v\in V\Gamma
and
a\in A_{v}
we define:

•

the
creation operator
associated with
a
by
a^{\dagger}:=Q_{v}aQ_{v}^{\perp}\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
,

•

the
diagonal operator
associated with
a
by
\mathfrak{d}(a):=Q_{v}aQ_{v}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
,

•

the
annihilation operator
associated with
a
by
((a^{*})^{\dagger})^{*}:=Q_{v}^{\perp}aQ_{v}\in\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
25
, Proposition 2.9]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial graph, and let
\mathbf{A}:=(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
be a collection of unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras with GNS-faithful states
(\omega_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
. Then the (dense)
\ast
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
generated by
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
and the projections
(Q_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
coincides with

\mathrm{Span}\!\left\{(a_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots a_{k}^{\dagger})\,d\,(b_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots b_{l}^{\dagger})^{*}\ \middle|\ \begin{array}[]{l}k,l\in\mathbb{N},\ (u_{1},\dots,u_{k}),(v_{1},\dots,v_{l})\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{red}},\\
a_{i}\in A_{u_{i}}^{\circ},\ b_{j}\in A_{v_{j}}^{\circ},\ d\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)\end{array}\right\}.

(2.1)

Here
\mathcal{D}_{0}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)\subseteq\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
denotes the set consisting of
1
and all finite products
\mathfrak{d}(c_{1})\cdots\mathfrak{d}(c_{n})
with
c_{i}\in A_{w_{i}}
, where
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}\subseteq V\Gamma
forms a clique.

We call a non-zero operator of the form
x:=(a_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots a_{k}^{\dagger})\,d\,(b_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots b_{l}^{\dagger})^{\ast}
, where
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
and
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}),(v_{1},\ldots,v_{l})\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{red}}
,

a_{i}\in A_{u_{i}}^{\circ}
,
b_{j}\in A_{v_{j}}^{\circ}
for
1\leq i\leq k
,
1\leq j\leq l
,
and
d\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
,
elementary
; the collection of all such operators is denoted by
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
. By Proposition
2.1
, the linear span of
\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
is dense in
\mathfrak{A}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
.
Moreover, by
[
25
, Lemma 2.10]
, to each
x\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
we can associate a well-defined group element
\Sigma(x):=(u_{1}\cdots u_{k})(v_{1}\cdots v_{l})^{-1}\in W_{\Gamma}
,
which depends only on the operator
x
itself and not on the particular choice of the elements
a_{i}
,
b_{j}
, or
d
.
This element
\Sigma(x)
is referred to as the
signature
of
x
. Note that
x\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\Sigma(x)\mathbf{w}}^{\circ}
for all

,
\mathbf{w}\in W_{\Gamma}
.

An explicit decomposition of reduced operators in
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
into linear combinations of elementary

[

, Proposition 2.6]
.

2.2.
The Case
\#V\Gamma>2

In this subsection, we establish the following criterion for the selflessness of reduced graph product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial graph with
\#V\Gamma\geq 3
, and let
\mathbf{A}:=(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
be a family of unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, each endowed with a GNS-faithful state
\omega_{v}
. Assume that the complement
\Gamma^{c}
is connected and that every vertex
v\in V\Gamma
admits a unitary
u_{v}\in A_{v}^{\omega_{v}}\cap\ker(\omega_{v})
, where
A_{v}^{\omega_{v}}
is the centralizer of
\omega_{v}
. Then the reduced graph product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
is selfless.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
requires some preparation.

The following notion is adapted from
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
, where it was used in the study of the simplicity of reduced graph product
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras. Implicitly, the concept was already employed in
[
9
]
and
[
23
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial graph. A
walk
in
\Gamma
is a finite sequence of vertices
(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in V\Gamma\times\ldots\times V\Gamma
with
(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in E\Gamma
for all
i=1,\dots,n-1
. A walk is said to be
closed
if
(v_{1},v_{n})\in E\Gamma
, and it is said to
cover the whole graph
if
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}=V\Gamma
.

Note that a finite, undirected, simplicial graph
\Gamma
admits a closed walk covering the whole graph if and only if it is connected.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial, connected graph with
\#V\Gamma\geq 3
. Let
x,y\in V\Gamma
with
(x,y)\in E\Gamma
, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists an integer
L
such that the number of distinct closed walks
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{L})\in V\Gamma\times\ldots\times V\Gamma
satisfying
v_{1}=x
,
v_{L}=y
, and covering the whole graph is greater than
n
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k})
be any closed walk in
\Gamma
with
u_{1}=x
,
u_{2}=y
, that covers the whole graph, and let
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{l})
be a walk in
\Gamma
starting at
v_{1}=x
with
(v_{l},a),(v_{l},b)\in E\Gamma
for distinct vertices
a,b\in V\Gamma
. Choose an integer
K>n-1
and set
L:=2(K+k+l-1)\in\mathbb{N}
. There are at least
n
distinct walks of the form

(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k},v_{1},\ldots,v_{l},\underbrace{a,v_{l},\ldots,a,v_{l}}_{r_{1}\text{ times}},\underbrace{b,v_{l},\ldots,a,v_{l}}_{r_{2}\text{ times}},v_{l-1},\ldots,v_{1},u_{k},\ldots,u_{2}),

with
r_{1}+r_{2}=K
. All these walks are closed, start in
x=u_{1}
, end in
y=u_{2}
, and cover the entire graph, which proves the claim.
∎

Within the setting of Theorem
2.2
, every reduced operator
a=a_{1}\cdots a_{n}\in\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
of type
\mathbf{w}=s_{1}\cdots s_{n}\in W_{\Gamma}
with
s_{i}\in S_{\Gamma}
,
a_{i}\in A_{s_{i}}^{\circ}
naturally determines an orthogonal projection onto the direct sum of all subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
of the form

\mathbb{C}(a\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{\mathbf{u}}}^{\circ}:=\left(\mathbb{C}(a_{1}\xi_{s_{1}})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}(a_{n}\xi_{s_{n}})\right)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{\mathbf{u}}}^{\circ},

where
\mathbf{u}\in W_{\Gamma}
is a group element with
|\mathbf{w}\mathbf{u}|=|\mathbf{w}|+|\mathbf{u}|
; we denote this projection by
\mathbf{Q}(a\Omega)
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite, undirected, simplicial graph with
\#V\Gamma\geq 3
, and let
\mathbf{A}:=(A_{v})_{v\in V\Gamma}
be a family of unital
\mathrm{C}^{\ast}
-algebras, each endowed with a GNS-faithful state
\omega_{v}
. Assume that the complement
\Gamma^{c}
is connected and that every vertex
v\in V\Gamma
admits a unitary
u_{v}\in A_{v}^{\omega_{v}}\cap\ker(\omega_{v})
. Then, for every finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}
, every vertex
x\in V\Gamma
, and every natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist distinct closed walks

(v_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,v_{L}^{(1)}),\ldots,(v_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,v_{L}^{(n)})\in V\Gamma\times\ldots\times V\Gamma

in
\Gamma^{c}
with

x=v_{1}^{(1)}=\cdots=v_{1}^{(n)}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{L}^{(1)}=\cdots=v_{L}^{(n)},

which cover the whole graph and satisfy
\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\,a\,\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and all reduced operators
a\in\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
of type
\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{F}
, where
\mathbf{g}_{i}:=w_{1}^{(i)}\cdots w_{L}^{(i)}
and
\mathbf{g}_{j}:=w_{1}^{(j)}\cdots w_{L}^{(j)}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
, we can choose distinct closed walks
(\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,\widetilde{v}_{K}^{(1)}),\ldots,(\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(n)},\ldots,\widetilde{v}_{K}^{(n)})
in
\Gamma^{c}
covering the entire graph and satisfying
x=\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(1)}=\cdots=\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(n)}
and
\widetilde{v}_{K}^{(1)}=\cdots=\widetilde{v}_{K}^{(n)}
. Let
R>\max_{\mathbf{w}\in\mathcal{F}}|\mathbf{w}|
be an integer, and consider for each
1\leq i\leq n
the walk
(v_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,v_{L}^{(i)})
in
\Gamma^{c}
with
L:=2(R+K-1)
defined by

(\underbrace{\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(1)},\widetilde{v}_{2}^{(1)},\ldots,\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(1)},\widetilde{v}_{2}^{(1)}}_{R\text{ times}},\widetilde{v}_{3}^{(1)},\ldots,\widetilde{v}_{K}^{(1)},\widetilde{v}_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,\widetilde{v}_{K}^{(i)}).

These walks are all distinct, have length
L
, are closed, and cover the whole graph
\Gamma^{c}
. Denote the element in
W_{\Gamma}
associated with
(v_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,v_{L}^{(i)})
by
\mathbf{g}_{i}
.

Now let
a=a_{1}\cdots a_{r}\in\mathbf{A}_{\Gamma}
be a reduced operator of type
\mathbf{w}=s_{1}\cdots s_{r}\in\mathcal{F}\subseteq W_{\Gamma}\setminus\{e\}
with
s_{i}\in S_{\Gamma}
and
a_{i}\in A_{s_{i}}^{\circ}
. By
[
9
, Proposition 2.6]
the operator
a
can be expressed as a linear combination of elementary operators of the form
z:=(b_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots b_{k}^{\dagger})\,\left(\mathfrak{d}(c_{1})\cdots\mathfrak{d}(c_{l})\right)\,(d_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots d_{m}^{\dagger})^{\ast}\in\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{A},\Gamma)
, where
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k+l+m=r
,
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}),(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathrm{red}}
, and
b_{i}\in A_{u_{i}}^{\circ}
,
d_{i}\in A_{v_{i}}^{\circ}
,
c_{i}\in A_{w_{i}}^{\circ}
, where
\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{l}\}\subseteq V\Gamma
forms a clique.

To prove the claim, it suffices to show that
\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\,z\,\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
z
as above. Consider vectors
(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\otimes\eta\in\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
and
(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)\otimes\eta^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
with
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{u}}^{\circ}
,
\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{u}^{\prime}}^{\circ}
, where
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}^{\prime}\in W_{\Gamma}
are group elements with
|\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{u}|=|\mathbf{g}_{i}|+|\mathbf{u}|
and
|\mathbf{g}_{j}\mathbf{u}^{\prime}|=|\mathbf{g}_{j}|+|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}|
. Since
(b_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots b_{k}^{\dagger})^{\ast}
and
(d_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots d_{m}^{\dagger})^{\ast}
are products of annihilation operators, and
(v_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,v_{L}^{(i)})
and
(v_{1}^{(j)},\ldots,v_{L}^{(j)})
are closed walks that cover
\Gamma^{c}
,
\langle(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\otimes\eta,z(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)\otimes\eta^{\prime}\rangle=0
unless
u_{1}\cdots u_{k}=v_{1}^{(i)}\cdots v_{k}^{(i)}
and
v_{1}\cdots v_{m}=v_{1}^{(j)}\cdots v_{m}^{(j)}
. In that case, by the multiplication rules of annihilation operators, we get that

\displaystyle\left\langle(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\otimes\eta,z(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)\otimes\eta^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\overline{\left\langle(b_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots b_{k}^{\dagger})\Omega,(u_{v_{1}^{(i)}}\cdots u_{v_{k}^{(i)}})\Omega\right\rangle}\left\langle(d_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots d_{m}^{\dagger})\Omega,(u_{v_{1}^{(i)}}\cdots u_{v_{k}^{(i)}})\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left\langle(u_{v_{k+1}^{(i)}}\cdots u_{v_{L}^{(i)}}\Omega)\otimes\eta,\,\left(\mathfrak{d}(c_{1})\cdots\mathfrak{d}(c_{l})\right)\,(u_{v_{m+1}^{(j)}}\cdots u_{v_{L}^{(j)}}\Omega)\otimes\eta^{\prime}\right\rangle.

(2.2)

We distinguish three cases.

•

\mathbf{g}_{j}:=w_{1}^{(j)}\cdots w_{L}^{(j)}

Proof.

For each

Now let

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\bigl((Q_{s_{1}}a_{1}Q_{s_{1}}^{\perp})\cdots(Q_{s_{k}}a_{k}Q_{s_{k}}^{\perp})\bigr)\bigl((Q_{s_{k+1}}^{\perp}a_{k+1}Q_{s_{k+1}})\cdots(Q_{s_{m}}^{\perp}a_{m}Q_{s_{m}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(a_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots a_{k}^{\dagger})\bigl((a_{m}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\cdots(a_{k+1}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\bigr)^{\ast}+\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(a_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots a_{k}^{\dagger})\mathfrak{d}(a_{k+1})\bigl((a_{m}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\cdots(a_{k+2}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\bigr)^{\ast}.

We distinguish two cases.

•

is even.

and

, or

.

The second case follows by a similar argument.

•

if

By the construction of the walks in (

) and the decomposition (

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)a\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)

(2.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{i}}\Omega)\left(a_{1}^{\dagger}\cdots a_{k_{0}}^{\dagger}\right)\mathfrak{d}(a_{k_{0}+1})\left((a_{m}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\cdots(a_{k_{0}+2}^{\ast})^{\dagger}\right)^{\ast}\mathbf{Q}(u_{\mathbf{g}_{j}}\Omega)

and

starting in

.

This completes the proof.

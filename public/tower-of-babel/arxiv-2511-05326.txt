Abstract
. We study the multi-dimensional Euler–alignment system with a matrix-valued communication kernel, motivated by models of anticipation dynamics in collective behaviour. A key feature of this system is its formal equivalence to a nonlocal variant of the Aw–Rascle–Zhang (ARZ) traffic model, in which the desired velocity is modified by a nonlocal gradient interaction. We prove the global-in-time existence of measure solutions to both formulations, obtained via a single degenerate pressureless Navier–Stokes approximation. Furthermore, we establish a weak–strong uniqueness principle adapted to the pressureless setting and to nonlocal alignment forces. As a consequence, we rigorously justify the formal correspondence between the nonlocal ARZ and Euler–alignment models: they arise from the same inviscid limit, and the weak–strong uniqueness property ensures that, whenever a classical solution exists, both formulations coincide with it.

Introduction

Among these, the Euler–alignment model, obtained as a hydrodynamic limit of the Cucker–Smale system, plays a central role in describing emergent coordination in crowds, animal flocks, and swarms. The model consists of pressureless compressible Euler equations with a nonlocal velocity alignment force, and has been the subject of extensive analytical study in recent years; see, for example,

In this work, we study a multi-dimensional Euler system with a matrix-valued alignment kernel

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\DIV_{x}(\rho\,u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\,u)+\DIV_{x}(\rho u\otimes u)+\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{2}K(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))\rho(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=0,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(1.3)

\begin{split}D^{2}K=\left(\partial^{2}_{x_{i}x_{j}}K\right)_{i,j=1}^{n}.\end{split}

Such Hessian-type kernels were introduced by Shu and Tadmor
[
51
]
, to model
anticipation dynamics
, and lead to flocking behavior under attractive potentials.

A distinctive feature of (
1
) is that it also admits a hydrodynamic formulation reminiscent of the Aw–Rascle–Zhang (ARZ) traffic model. Formally introducing a velocity field
w
via

(1.4)

\begin{split}w=u+\nabla K(x)\star\rho,\end{split}

yields the nonlocal multi-dimensional ARZ system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\DIV_{x}(\rho\,u)=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho\,w)+\DIV_{x}(\rho w\otimes u)=0,\end{array}\right.\quad\text{ in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The one-dimensional ARZ model is one of the most established macroscopic models for vehicular traffic flow
[
5
,
57
,
32
,
34
]
. It consists of conservation laws (
1
) for the density
\rho
and the momentum associated with the desired velocity
w
. The difference
w-u
is referred to as the
pressure
or
offset
and is typically modeled as a nonnegative, increasing function of
\rho
, such as
p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}
. The quantity
p(\rho)
plays an analogous role to pressure in classical fluid dynamics—it governs the propagation of congestion waves—but in contrast to fluid models, it modifies the entire transport structure of the ARZ system, not only the forcing term.

Recently, generalizations and non-local variants of ARZ model have been introduced, see
[
29
,
38
,
44
,
3
]
, motivated both by traffic flow modeling and by analogies with collective motion systems. In these models, the velocity takes the form
u=V(w,\rho)
, capturing local or nonlocal interactions. In particular,
[
3
]
studied the case
u=w-\xi\star\rho
for a smooth kernel
\xi
, and observed that this nonlocal ARZ system is equivalent to the one-dimensional Euler–alignment model (
1
) with communication weight
\Psi=D^{2}K
. Another, but this time a multi-dimensional, extension of ARZ appears in
[
2
]
, where system (
1
) is suplemented with the offset relation

w=u+\nabla(\rho^{-1}-\rho_{max}^{-1})^{-\gamma},

for some maximal packing density
\rho_{max}
. The congestion term in form of a gradient, results in a singular diffusion in the continuity equation (when written in terms of

and

) to enforce capacity constraints in the crowd density while inducing a steering behaviour typical for the flow of pedestrians.
Incorporating the nonlocal term (

) in this setting corresponds exactly to the anticipation-driven dynamics of
[
51

The primary goal of this paper is to establish global-in-time measure solutions to (
1

1.4
)–(
1
), without smallness assumptions on the initial data. We also prove a weak–strong uniqueness principle, showing that these measure solutions coincide with classical solutions whenever the latter exist.

The equivalence between (
1

1.4
)–(
1

[
18
]
at the microscopic, mesoscopic, and macroscopic levels. We also refer to
[

for a useful overview of other results on these models, including existence and uniqueness results as well as their derivation through mean-filed limit, based on the previous results of Kim

Peszek and Poyato

In particular, under the assumption that

) was shown following the approach of Carrillo and Choi
[

. Concerning the connection between the macroscopic hydrodynamic formulation (
1
) and the kinetic system, the authors were only able to justify that a suitably regular solution to (
1
) generates mono-kinetic solution to the kinetic system. However, global solutions are only known to exists under smallness assumptions; see

52
,
25
,
53
,

For the local version of the model (
1.4
)–(
1
), i.e. when

K(x)\approx\delta(x)
, the existence of measure-valued solutions and a weak–strong uniqueness principle were established by Chaudhuri, Gwiazda, and Zatorska in
[
17
]
; see also
[
19
]
for local existence of strong solutions. The existence of infinitely many global-in-time weak solutions for bounded initial data, obtained via the convex integration method, was demonstrated in
[
16

.

The same convex integration technique was already used in
[
12
]
to construct weak solutions to the Euler system with general nonlocal interactions, including attraction–repulsion and alignment. In particular, they treated a pressureless nonlocal Euler system and exploited the weak repulsive Poisson interaction to prove weak–strong uniqueness. It is worth noting that all of these results were obtained in bounded or periodic domains, whereas our analysis is carried out in the whole space. We also mention the works
[
9
,
13
]
on inviscid limits of Navier–Stokes-type approximations for nonlocal Euler systems, with and without pressure. In
[
14
]
, dissipative measure-valued solutions for the Euler–alignment model were studied and weak–strong uniqueness was proved. However, in all of these works a crucial role is played by estimates in Lebesgue or (dual) Sobolev norms for the density. Since systems (
1
) and (
1
) lack a classical pressure term, such techniques are not applicable here.

In this paper, we first establish global-in-time existence of measure solutions to (
1
) (and equivalently to the system (
1.4
)-(
1
)). Instead of approximating (
1
) via its microscopic or kinetic formulations—as in, for instance,
[
46
]
, where measure-valued solutions to the Cucker–Smale model with singular scalar weights were obtained—we construct solutions by a vanishing viscosity limit of a suitably designed degenerate pressureless Navier–Stokes approximation. In contrast with
[
9
]
and
[
17
]
, our approach relies on the existence theory developed by Vasseur and Yu
[
54
]
and on its nonlocal generalization in
[
47
]
.

The key advantage of this approximation is that density-dependent viscosity yields enhanced regularity of the density, thanks to the Bresch–Desjardins entropy estimate
[
8
]
, while the Mellet–Vasseur estimate
[
45
]
gives compactness for the velocity field. Unlike the case of constant viscosity, this allows one to construct weak solutions even in the absence of pressure, as in
[
47
]
, which is essential for our problem. Indeed, in the energy identity the dissipative term
a\DIV_{x}(\nu\mathbb{D}u)
appears as
a\nu\int|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
, and thus it vanishes in the weak formulation as
a\to 0^{+}
, but for pressure terms
a\nabla_{x}p(\rho)
this is not true. Our proof crucially exploits this structure. Moreover, we use natural bounds in the space of Radon measures, obtained from the continuity equation and conservation of momentum, to pass to the limit in all terms except the convective term, which may generate a concentration defect. Adapting techniques from
[
1
,
56
]
, we control this defect measure via the energy inequality.

Our second main contribution is a weak–strong uniqueness principle, based on a relative entropy method adapted to the pressureless setting. This method originates from Dafermos
[
23
,
24
]
for scalar conservation laws, and was extended to incompressible Euler equations by Brenier et al.
[
7
]
.
It has since been applied to stability, asymptotic limits, and dimension reduction problems
[
6
,
22
,
33
,
30
]
. In our case, instead of relying on convex entropy associated with pressure potentials, the method employs Wasserstein-2 distance used before by Figali and Kang in
[
31
]
and by Carrillo and Choi
[
10
]
for the derivation of mono-kinetic Euler-alignment model from the particle and the kinetic levels, respectively. Consequently, classical solutions from
[
10
,
18
]
are shown to be unique within the class of measure solutions.
As a corollary, our results rigorously justify the formal correspondence between the generalized nonlocal ARZ system (
1.4
)-(
1
) and the Euler–alignment system (
1
), previously observed in
[
3
,
18
]
. We establish this equivalence in two ways: first, by proving that both systems arise as inviscid limits of the same approximation; and second, by showing that whenever a classical solution exists, both formulations coincide through our weak–strong uniqueness principle.

Basic definitions and auxiliary proposition in the space of measures

The following section is devoted to the introduction of basic concepts in the space of Radon measures, and necessary propositions for the proofs of main results. For more information, we refer our readers to the books

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a sequence of Radon measures. We say that

\mu_{n}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\mu,\text{ weakly* in }\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

whenever

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\text{, for every }f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and we say that

\mu_{n}\rightharpoonup\mu,\text{ weakly in }\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(x)\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\text{, for every }f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The following propositions explain the connection between weak* convergence of measures and lower-, upper- semicontiuity properties of measures and their variations.

Proposition 3.2

(Theorem 1, p. 54,
[
27
]

Let

Then, for any open

and compact

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{n}(U)\geq\mu(U),\qquad\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{n}(K)\leq\mu(K).

Moreover

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\mu_{n}(B)=\mu(B),\text{ for any Borel B, such that }\mu(\partial B)=0.

(Proposition 1.62,

From Proposition

Next, we look at an imporant lemma, which allows us to differentiate measures, after we weakly converge with them.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the sequences of finite Radon measures such that

\nu_{n}<<\mu_{n}\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Moreover, suppose that there exists a non-decreasing continuous function
f:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
,
f(0)=0
such that

|\nu_{n}|(A)\leq f(\mu_{n}(A))\text{ for any measurable sets }A,\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

and

\displaystyle\nu_{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\nu\text{ weakly* in }\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\rightharpoonup\mu\text{ weakly* in }\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then,

\nu<<\mu.

Proof.

Step 1: reduction to compact sets.
Suppose that we know that

\mu(K)=0\Rightarrow|\nu|(K)=0,\text{ for every compact set }K.

Let
A
be an arbitrary Borel set such that
\mu(A)=0
. Then, for any
K\subset A
, which is compact,
\mu(K)=0
, and by our assumption also
|\nu|(K)=0
. Hence, by inner regularity

|\nu|(A)=\sup\{|\nu|(K)\,|\,K\subset A,K\text{ compact}\}=0.

Step 2: proof for compact sets.
Fix a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mu(K)=0
. Now for any
x\in K
fix a radius
r_{x}
, for which
\mu(\partial B(x,r_{x}))=0
. Indeed we can do that, since
\partial B(x,r)\cap\partial B(x,s)=\emptyset
for
r\neq s
, there can be only countably many sets of the form
\partial B(x,r)
of positive measure. In fact,
r_{x}
can be arbitrarily small. Thus

K\subset\bigcup_{x\in K}B(x,r_{x}).

Since
K
is compact, we may find a finite cover such that

K\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}B(x_{k},r_{k}).

By inner regularity for the sets
B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K
, for fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we may find a compact set
C_{k}\subset B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K
for which

\mu((B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K)\setminus C_{k})<\frac{\varepsilon}{N}.

Again, as
C_{k}
is compact, and
B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K
is open, similarly as for
K
we may find a finite open cover of
C_{k}
such that

C_{k}\subset\bigcup_{l=1}^{M_{k}}B(y_{l},r_{l}),\quad\mu(\partial B(y_{l},r_{l}))=0,\text{ and }\overline{B}(y_{l},r_{l})\subset B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K.

Denote
S_{k}:=\bigcup_{l=1}^{M_{k}}\overline{B}(y_{l},r_{l})
, and
U:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k})
. Then

\displaystyle\mu((B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k})\setminus K)=\mu((B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K)\setminus S_{k})\leq\mu((B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus K)\setminus C_{k})<\frac{\varepsilon}{N}.

Moreover,
S_{k}
and
U
are continuity sets of
\mu
. That is

\displaystyle\mu(\partial S_{k})\leq\mu\left(\bigcup_{l=1}^{M_{k}}\partial B(y_{l},r_{l})\right)\leq\sum_{l=1}^{M_{k}}\mu(\partial B(y_{l},r_{l}))=0,

\displaystyle\mu(\partial U)\leq\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\partial(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k})\right)\leq\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\partial B(x_{k},r_{k})\cup\partial S_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu(\partial B(x_{k},r_{k}))+\mu(\partial S_{k})=0.

Since
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus S_{k}
, then
B(x_{k},r_{k})\cap K\subset B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k}
. Hence

K=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\cap K)\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k}).

Furthermore

|\mu(U)-\mu(K)|=\mu(U\setminus K)=\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k})\setminus K\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu(B(x_{k},r_{k})\setminus S_{k})\setminus K)<\varepsilon.

Finally, by the continuity of
f
, Corollary
3.4
and Proposition
3.2

\displaystyle|\nu|(K)\leq|\nu|(U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}|\nu_{n}|(U)\leq\liminf_{n\to+\infty}f(\mu_{n}(U))=f(\mu(U))=|f(\mu(U))-f(\mu(K))|<\delta,

for arbitrary
\delta>0
. Therefore
|\nu|(K)=0
.
∎

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\cdot\rho^{N}(t,x)u^{N}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\leq\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}\left(\|\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}+\frac{1}{N}\|\rho^{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\left\|\overline{\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\mathbb{D}u^{N}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right)

Proof of Theorem

(Part

To start off the proof, let us take a sequence of the approximation of our initial data. Let

be such that

and

\rho^{N}_{0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\rightharpoonup\rho_{0},\text{ weakly in }\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Note that such a sequence can be found by a standard considerations involving mollifications of the measure

and multiplication by the smooth cut-off functions, which ensure compactness of the support. Moreover, since

the sequence

can be taken, so that it satisfies

For the velocity, we simply fix

satisfies the assumptions in Theorem

. With this set-up, we may prove the following convergence lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

(\rho^{N},u^{N})
be as in Theorem
4.1
, with the initial data
(\rho_{0}^{N},u_{0}^{N})
given above. Then, there exist
\rho\in C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
and
m\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
, such that (up to the subsequence which we do not relabel)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\rho^{N}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rho

\displaystyle\text{ in }C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})),

(4.8)

\displaystyle\rho^{N}u^{N}=:m^{N}

\displaystyle\rightarrow m

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})).

Moreover, for almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

, and there exists

Furthermore,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}(x)=1,

and the second moment and the first moment of respectively
\rho_{t}
and

are bounded, that is

(4.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}<+\infty,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|m_{t}|<+\infty.

Proof.

First, we show the convergence for
\{\rho^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
. We aim to use the Arzelà– Ascoli theorem. The boundedness of the sequence in
C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
is a direct consequence of (
4.3
). Indeed,

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}d_{f}(\rho^{N}(t),0)\leq\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1.

Moreover, by the second moment bound in (
4.6
), and the Chebyshev inequality we deduce the tightness of the family
\{\rho^{N}(t)\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, by Proposition
3.7
we see, that our sequence is relatively compact. At last we need to check the equi-continuity in flat metric. Fix an arbitrary
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
t,s\in[0,T]
. Then, by Lemma
4.2

(4.12)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\rho^{N}(t)-\rho^{N}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|=\left|\int_{s}^{t}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\rho^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq C(\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty})|t-s|.

Since the constant above depends only on
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
and
\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}
we can take supremum over
\varphi
in (
4.12
) to obtain

d_{f}(\rho^{N}(t),\rho^{N}(s))\leq C|t-s|,

which is the last ingredient to use the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and conclude the existence of
\rho\in C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
, such that

\rho^{N}\rightarrow\rho\text{ in }C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})),

which finishes the proof of (
4.7
).

When it comes to the momentum, we can again utilize Lemma
4.2
to obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(\rho^{N}(t)u^{N}(t)-\rho^{N}(s)u^{N}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|=\left|\int_{s}^{t}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\rho^{N}u^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau\right|\leq C(\|\varphi\|_{\infty},\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty})\sqrt{|t-s|},

for a.e.
t,s\in(0,T)
. Since this holds only almost everywhere, it is not enough to proceed in a space of continuous functions, but only in
L^{\infty}
. Here, we may follow an argument from
[
21
]
, but as it is short, and only given for functions taking real or complex values, and not in an arbitrary complete metric space, we will give the whole argument with appropriate changes for the convenience of the reader.

By (
4.4
), (
4.3
), and the Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|m^{N}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t)|u^{N}(t)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}\leq C.

Moreover using (
4.4
), (
4.6
), and Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x||m^{N}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\rho^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq C.

Let
\mathbf{m}^{N}
be a particular function chosen from the equivalence class
m^{N}
, such that
\|\mathbf{m}^{N}(t)\|_{TV}\leq C
, and
\||x|\mathbf{m}^{N}(t)\|_{TV}\leq C
for every
t\in[0,T]
. For any non-empty, measurable set
E\subset[0,T]
define

|\mathbf{m}^{N}|_{E}:=\inf_{R}\sup_{t,s\in E\setminus R}d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{N}(t),\mathbf{m}^{N}(s)),

where the infimum is taken over all sets
R\subset[0,T]
of measure zero. By virtue of (
4.13
) there exists a partition
\{E_{k,j}\}_{j=1}^{l_{k}}
, such that

\displaystyle\max_{j}\sup_{N}|\mathbf{m}^{N}|_{E_{k,j}}\leq\frac{1}{k}.

Moreover, for each
E_{k,j}
, we may find a set
F_{k,j}\subseteq E_{k,j}
, such that

is of measure zero, and

\displaystyle\sup_{N}\sup_{t,s\in F_{k,j}}d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{N}(t),\mathbf{m}^{N}(s))\leq\frac{1}{k}.

Now, from any
F_{k,j}
we select an arbitrary point

. Then, the sequence
\{\mathbf{m}^{N}(s_{k,j})\}
is bounded in
(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{TV})
, and since its first moment is bounded, by the Chebyshev inequality, it is also tight. Hence, for
k=1
, by Proposition
3.7
, there exists a subsequence, which we do not relabel, such that
\{\mathbf{m}^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})
in every point
\{s_{1,j}\}_{j=1}^{l_{1}}
. Since the number of points is finite, we can make this convence uniform with respect to them. Using a diagonal argument, we can find a subsequence (which we again do not relabel), such that
\{\mathbf{m}^{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in every point
\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\{s_{k,j}\}_{j=1}^{l_{k}}
, and this convergence is uniform on finite subsets. Fix
\varepsilon>0
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
for which
\frac{1}{k}<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
. Since
\{\mathbf{m}^{N}\}
converges uniformly on

, then there exists some

such that if

\displaystyle\max_{j}d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{N}(s_{k,j}),\mathbf{m}^{M}(s_{k,j}))\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

For any

we have

\displaystyle d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{N}(s),\mathbf{m}^{M}(s))\leq d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{N}(s),\mathbf{m}^{N}(s_{k,j}))+d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{N}(s_{k,j}),\mathbf{m}^{M}(s_{k,j}))+d_{f}(\mathbf{m}^{M}(s_{k,j}),\mathbf{m}^{M}(s))<\varepsilon,

thus

\|m^{N}-m^{M}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))}<\varepsilon.

It is easy to see that (by Proposition
3.7
, see also the argument for (
4.14
))
\mathcal{K}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\,|\mu|(\mathbb{R}^{d})\leq C,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}|\mu|(x)\leq C\}
is complete with respect to the

metric, thus so is

. Hence, the Cauchy sequence

has a limit
m\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{K},d_{f}))
, and thus (
4.8
) is verified.

Moving forward, we want to differentiate
m
with respect to
\rho
. Using Hölder inequality and (
4.4
), we can easily see that for any Borel

\displaystyle|m_{t}^{N}|(A)=\int_{A}\rho^{N}\,|u^{N}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq C\sqrt{\int_{A}\rho^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x}=C\sqrt{\rho^{N}_{t}(A)}.

Thus, by Lemma
3.9

. Hence, also
\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{t}\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t<<\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t
(see Definition
3.12
for the definition of the product measure), and by the Radon–Nikodym theorem, there exists a function

such that

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{t}\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=u(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\otimes\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

which readily implies that

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{t}=u(t,\cdot)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\text{ for almost every }t\in(0,T).

Thus, using Proposition
3.8
and (
4.4
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\leq e^{4t\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{0}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0}(x).

In particular

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t})).

To show the bounds on the moments, we utilize the Fubini theorem, Proposition
3.2
, the Fatou lemma, and (
4.6
)

(4.14)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{t}\{|x|^{2}>s\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\rho^{N}_{t}\{|x|^{2}>s\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leq\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho^{N}_{t}\{|x|^{2}>s\}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\rho^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<+\infty.\end{split}

At last, to show the mass conservation, we note that
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is trivially a continuity set of the measure

, hence, by Proposition

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\mu_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\\
&\phantom{=}\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{tr}(\rho^{N}u^{N}\otimes u^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{tr}(u\otimes u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\\
&\phantom{=}=\frac{1}{2}\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq e^{4t\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{0}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0},\end{split}

with a similar approach, whenever
D^{2}K
is even and positive semi-definite.
∎

Here, we have shown the first part of Theorem
2.6
, under the assumption that the initial velocity is regular, i.e.
u_{0}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We will get rid of this restriction in the third part of the proof. For now, we move on to proving the second part of Theorem
2.6
for
u_{0}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
w
defined in (
2.5
).

As the entry to our discussion, we note that
D^{2}K\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
implies that
\nabla_{x}K
is Lipschitz, hence

(4.19)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}K(x)|\leq C(\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty})(1+|x|),

which will be heavily exploited in the arguments below.

In what follows we state a simple reformulation of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Fix
N>0
. Suppose that the initial conditions
(\rho^{N}_{0},m^{N}_{0})
satisfy the assumptions of the Theorem
4.1
, and that
(\rho^{N},u^{N})
is the given solution. Define

(4.20)

\displaystyle w^{N}:=u^{N}+\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}.

Then,
(\rho^{N},w^{N},u^{N})
is a solution to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho^{n}+\DIV_{x}(\rho^{N}u^{N})=0,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho^{N}w^{N})+\DIV_{x}(\rho^{N}w^{N}\otimes u^{N})-\frac{1}{N}\DIV_{x}(\rho^{N}\mathbb{D}u^{N})=0,\end{array}\right.

in a sense that

\displaystyle\rho^{N}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\in L^{\infty}

\displaystyle(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad\sqrt{\rho^{N}}w^{N}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho^{N}}u^{N}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

\displaystyle,\quad\overline{\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\mathbb{D}u^{N}}\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

The triple
(\rho^{N},w^{N},u^{N})
satisfies

(4.21)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)\phi(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}^{N}(x)\phi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as well as

(4.22)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}w^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi+\rho^{N}w^{N}\otimes u^{N}:\nabla_{x}\phi-\frac{1}{N}\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\overline{\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\mathbb{D}u^{n}}:\nabla_{x}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)w^{N}(t,x)\cdot\phi(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}^{N}(x)w^{N}_{0}(x)\cdot\phi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,\end{split}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
and
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, for any
\phi\in C^{2}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\overline{\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\mathbb{D}u^{n}}:\nabla_{x}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}(\Delta\phi+\nabla_{x}\DIV_{x}\phi)+2\left(\nabla_{x}\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\otimes\sqrt{\rho^{N}}u^{N}\right):\nabla_{x}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

and the following energy bound is satisfied (in addition to the ones given in Theorem
4.1
)

(4.23)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)|w^{N}(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq C(T,\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty},\|\rho_{0}^{N}|u^{N}_{0}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}},\||x|^{2}\rho^{N}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}),\end{split}

for all
t\in(0,T)
.

Proof.

We only need to check the momentum conservation equation and the energy inequality. We start with the first one. Note that testing the equation (
4.1
) by
\nabla_{x}K(\cdot-y)
implies that
\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}
is differentiable in time as

(4.24)

\begin{split}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}(t,y)\nabla_{y}\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}(t,y)D^{2}K(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Hence, by (
4.1
) and the above

(4.25)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}w^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\partial_{t}\left(\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\cdot\phi\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\phi\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\cdot\phi(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}_{0}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}_{0}\cdot\phi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\cdot\phi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\rho^{N}(s,x)\phi(s,x)D^{2}K(x-y)\rho^{N}(s,y)u^{N}(s,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\cdot\phi(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}_{0}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}_{0}\cdot\phi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\otimes u^{N}:\nabla_{x}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\rho^{N}(x)\phi D^{2}K(x-y)(u^{N}(x)-u^{N}(y))\rho^{N}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Expressing the first and last term by (
4.2
), i.e.

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\rho^{N}(x)\phi D^{2}K(x-y)(u^{N}(x)-u^{N}(y))\rho^{N}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)u^{N}(t,x)\cdot\phi^{N}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}^{N}(x)u^{N}_{0}(x)\cdot\phi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\otimes u^{N}:\nabla_{x}\phi-\frac{1}{N}\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\overline{\sqrt{\rho^{N}}\mathbb{D}u^{n}}:\nabla_{x}\phi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

and using (
4.20
) gives us desired formula (
4.22
).

To obtain the energy inequality, we simply use the formula (
4.20
), the triangle inequality, (
4.3
), (
4.19
) and the known bounds (
4.4
), (
4.6
) to get

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)|w^{N}(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad\leq C(\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty},\|\rho_{0}^{N}|u^{N}_{0}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x},})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}(t,x)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}C(1+|x|+|y|)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\qquad\quad=C(T,\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty},\|\rho_{0}^{N}|u^{N}_{0}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}_{x}},\||x|^{2}\rho_{0}^{N}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}).\end{split}

∎

Remark 4.5
.

Due to construction
w^{N}
is not a suitable test function for equation (
4.22
), if it were, the kinetic energy associated with velocity
w^{N}
would be bounded independently of
T
and
K
.

Before convergence, we once again need additional a priori bounds, similar to Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\rho^{N},w^{N})
be given by Theorem
4.4
, then

(1)

For every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\cdot\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T)
with a constant depending only on initial data and
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
,
\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}
,

(2)

For every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\left\{\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\cdot\rho^{N}w^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
L^{2}(0,T)
with a constant depending only on initial data and
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
,
\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

The second bound is proven the same way as in Lemma
4.2
, therefore we skip the proof. For the first one, we use (
4.24
)
and (
4.21
) to estimate

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\cdot\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\partial_{t}(\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}u^{N}\cdot\nabla_{x}(\varphi\cdot\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\varphi(x)\rho^{N}(x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u^{N}(x)-u^{N}(y))\rho^{N}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&\qquad+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\otimes u^{N}:\nabla_{x}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&\leq 2\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\|\rho^{N}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}^{3/2}\|\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}^{1/2}+\|\nabla_{x}\varphi\|_{\infty}(\|\rho^{N}|w^{N}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}+\|\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}),\end{split}

which is bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T)
by (
4.4
), (
4.23
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.6
(Part
2
).

Looking at the proof of the Theorem
2.6
(Part
1
) (specifically Lemma
4.3
) we already know that there exist
\rho\in C([0,T];(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
,
m_{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
,
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}))
such that

\displaystyle\rho^{N}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\rho,

\displaystyle\text{ in }C([0,T];(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})),

\displaystyle\rho^{N}u^{N}=:m^{N}

\displaystyle\rightarrow m_{u},

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})),

and

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{u,t}=u(t,\cdot)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}.

As the limits are unique, the pair
(\rho,u)
is the same one constructed in the first part of the proof.

In order to get analogous result for the limit of
(\rho^{N},w^{N})
, apart from Lemma
4.6
, we also need to show that the first moment of
\rho^{N}w^{N}
is bounded (to ensure tightness). Using Hölder’s inequality, (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and (
4.19
) we deduce

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\rho^{N}(t,x)|w^{N}(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\rho^{N}(t,x)|u^{N}(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\rho^{N}(t,x)|\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}(t,x)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\|\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\|^{1/2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}\rho^{N}(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|\rho^{N}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x|+|y|)\rho^{N}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C(T,\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty},\|\rho_{0}^{N}|u_{0}^{N}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}_{x}},\||x|^{2}\rho^{N}_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}).\end{split}

Thus, we can imply that there exists
m_{w}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
,
\chi\in C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f}))
, and
w\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}))
, such that

\displaystyle\rho^{N}w^{N}=:m^{N}_{w}

\displaystyle\rightarrow m_{w},

\displaystyle\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})),

\displaystyle\rho^{N}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\chi,

\displaystyle\text{ in }C([0,T];(\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),d_{f})),

and

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{w,t}=w(t,\cdot)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}.

At this point, following the argument in the first part of the proof of Theorem
2.6
, we can easily pass to the limit in the weak formulations (
4.21
) and (
4.22
), where the measure
\nu
is given by

\nu:=\mathrm{weak^{*}}\lim(\rho^{N}w^{N}\otimes u^{N})-w\otimes u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho.

Note that unlike for
\mu
, the sign of
\nu
is undetermined.

The last two points to verify are the compatibility condition (
2.5
) and (
2.7
). Note using equation (
4.20
), we may write

\displaystyle d_{f}(m_{w,t},m_{u,t}+\chi(t))\leq d_{f}(m_{w,t},\rho^{N}w^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)+d_{f}(\rho^{N}u^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\rho^{N}\nabla_{x}K\star\rho^{N}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,m_{u,t}+\chi(t)).

which, after passing to the limit on the right-hand side, gives us

(4.26)

\displaystyle w(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}=u(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}+\chi(t),\text{ for a.e. }t\in(0,T).

To conclude, we need to identify
\chi
with
(\nabla_{x}K\star\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho
. To do so fix
\phi\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
. We estimate

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\rho^{N}(t,x)(\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\wedge l)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
&\,+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\rho^{N}(t,x)(\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\wedge l)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)(\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\wedge l)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\right|\\
&\,+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)(\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\wedge l)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\right|\\
&=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

Let us treat all the terms separately. We deduce by (
4.19
) and (
4.6
)

\begin{split}I_{1}&\leq\int_{|\nabla_{x}K(x-y)|\geq l}|\phi(x)||\nabla_{x}K(x-y)-l|\rho^{N}(t,x)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq 2C\int_{\{C(1+|x-y|)\geq l\}}|\phi(x)|(1+|x-y|)\rho^{N}(t,x)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|x-y|)^{2}\rho^{N}(t,x)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\leq\frac{C}{l},\end{split}

for a constant
C>0
independent of
N
. For
I_{2}
, we may argue similarly as in calculations following (
4.16
) (for fixed
l
the function
\phi(x)(\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\wedge l)
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
), hence we skip the lengthy calculation, and deduce

I_{2}\rightarrow 0,\text{ as }N\to+\infty.

To treat
I_{3}
we may simply use Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, where the bound

|\phi(x)(\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\wedge l)|\leq C\|\phi\|_{\infty}(1+|x-y|),

is given in (
4.19
), and the integrability of the bound is deduced from (
4.6
). Hence, converging first with
N\to+\infty
, and then with
l\to+\infty
, we obtain

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\rho^{N}(t,x)\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\rho^{N}(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\phi(x)\nabla_{x}K(x-y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)\right|\rightarrow 0,

as
N\to+\infty
, for any
\phi\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which is enough to deduce
\chi(t)=(\nabla_{x}K\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}
and in consequence (by (
4.26
)) (
2.5
).

At last, to verify the energy bound we proceed similarly as in the proof of (
2.3
). By the linearity of the trace function and (
2.5
) we may write

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}(x)+\mathrm{tr}(\nu_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}+\lim_{N\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{tr}(w^{N}\otimes u^{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho^{N}_{t}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{tr}(w\otimes u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w(t,x)-u(t,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}+\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|w^{N}||u^{N}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\\
&\quad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}K\star\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{t}+\liminf_{N\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|w^{N}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{N}|u^{N}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right),\end{split}

which is bounded by
C(T,\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty},\||u_{0}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0})},\||x|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho_{0})})
, by the virtue of (
4.19
), (
4.14
), (
4.6
), (
4.4
), (
4.23
), and the Proposition
3.8
.
∎

At this point, we have proven the statement of Theorem
2.6
for initial datum
u_{0}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, and
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\partial_{t}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho u\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

and

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m_{0}(x)\cdot\psi(0,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\rho}u\cdot\partial_{t}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\sqrt{\rho}u\otimes\sqrt{\rho}u):\nabla_{x}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
+\langle\overline{\rho\mathbb{D}u},\nabla_{x}\psi\rangle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\nabla_{x}(V\star\rho)\cdot\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t=0,

where one defines

\langle\overline{\rho\mathbb{D}u},\nabla_{x}\psi\rangle=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho u\cdot(\Delta\psi+\nabla_{x}\DIV_{x}\psi)+2(\sqrt{\rho}\otimes\sqrt{\rho}u):\nabla_{x}\psi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

Moreover, for any
t\geq 0

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(x),

and there exist functions
\overline{\overline{\sqrt{\rho}\mathbb{D}u}},\,\overline{\overline{\sqrt{\rho}(\nabla_{x}u-\nabla_{x}^{T}u)}}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, such that

\sqrt{\rho}\overline{\overline{\sqrt{\rho}\mathbb{D}u}}=\overline{\rho\mathbb{D}u},\quad\sqrt{\rho}\overline{\overline{\sqrt{\rho}(\nabla_{x}u-\nabla_{x}^{T}u)}}=\overline{\rho(\nabla_{x}u-\nabla_{x}^{T}u)},\quad\text{ a.e.,}

where
\overline{\rho(\nabla_{x}u-\nabla_{x}^{T}u)}
is defined similarly to
\overline{\rho\mathbb{D}u}
, which satisify the following energy estimates

(1)

the energy estimate

(A.1)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho|u|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\overline{\overline{\sqrt{\rho}\mathbb{D}u}}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x-y)\rho(t,x)\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(x-y)\rho_{0}(x)\rho_{0}(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(2)

the Bresch–Dejardins estimate

(A.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\overline{\overline{\sqrt{\rho}(\nabla_{x}u-\nabla_{x}^{T}u)}}\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq C(T).

(3)

the Mellet–Vasseur estimate

(A.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho F(|u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(|x-y|)\rho(t,x)\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq C(T).

As one can see, the difference between Theorem
4.1
and Proposition
A.1
is the form of the non-local term and its consequences on the energy estimates. The approximating system for the proof of the Proposition
A.1
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\DIV_{x}(\rho u)=\varepsilon\Delta_{x}\rho,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\DIV_{x}(\rho u\otimes u)-\DIV_{x}(\rho\mathbb{D}u)+\rho\nabla_{x}(V_{L}\star\rho)\\
\qquad=-r_{0}u-r_{1}\rho|u|^{2}u+\kappa\rho\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\Delta_{x}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)-\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\rho\cdot\nabla_{x}u-\nu\Delta^{2}_{x}u+\eta\nabla_{x}\rho^{-6}+\delta\rho\nabla_{x}\Delta^{3}_{x}\rho,\end{array}\right.

while in our case it is

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\rho+\DIV_{x}(\rho u)=\varepsilon\Delta_{x}\rho,\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\DIV_{x}(\rho u\otimes u)-\DIV_{x}(\rho\mathbb{D}u)+\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{2}K_{L}(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))\rho(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\\
\qquad=-r_{0}u-r_{1}\rho|u|^{2}u+\kappa\rho\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\Delta_{x}\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)-\varepsilon\nabla_{x}\rho\cdot\nabla_{x}u-\nu\Delta^{2}_{x}u+\eta\nabla_{x}\rho^{-6}+\delta\rho\nabla_{x}\Delta^{3}_{x}\rho,\end{array}\right.

where
V_{L}
and
D^{2}K_{L}
are functions
V,D^{2}K
, respectively, truncated to a periodic torus of size
L
respectively. To get a better understanding of this technique, we briefly explain the need for all the parameters in the approximation (cf
[
47
, Table 1]
)

•

The terms with
\varepsilon>0
, are needed to perform standard construction of the strong solution at the level of Galerkin approximation.

•

The terms with
\nu,\eta,\delta>0
give us enough density regularity to test the equation by
\nabla_{x}\log\rho
and obtain the Bresch–Dejardins estimates .

•

The terms with
\kappa,r_{0},r_{1}>0
gives us enough density and velocity regularity to renormalize the momentum equation.

•

L>0
allows us to consider a simpler case of bounded, periodic domain, and later expand the torus.

Let us now discuss the differences in the main steps of the approximation procedure in comparison to
[
47
]
.

(1)

The first difference in our case is in the stability of the non-local term. Not to repeat ourselves we refer to the discussion around (
4.15
), where one can see that it is enough to have

\rho_{n}\to\rho\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{1}_{t,x},\qquad\rho_{n}u_{n}\to\rho u\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{1}_{t,x},

at any approximation step to properly converge in the non-local term. It is clearly given, when looking at the arguments in
[
47
]
.

(2)

The second difference is in the derivation of the basic energy inequality, which is used during he Galerkin approximation, and to obtain fundamental estimates (cf.
[
47
, Subsection 3.2, 3.3]
). As always, it is done by multiplying the momentum equation by the velocity, thus our goal is to show the estimate for

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)u(t,x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|.

Whenever
D^{2}K\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
only, then we may use mass conservation, and Hölder’s inequality to get

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)u(t,x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
&\leq\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho|u|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho|u|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq 2\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho|u|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

which allows us to use Grönwall’s inequality (compare with the left-hand side of (
A.1
)) and leads to an exponent term in (
2.3
). For a symmetric

D^{2}K
, by a change of variables, we may see that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)u(t,x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,y)u(t,y)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,y)-u(t,x))\rho(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

thus, whenever
D^{2}K
is positive definite

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)u(t,x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\rho(t,y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\geq 0,

which leads to the energy term without the use of Grönwall’s, and without the exponent term in (
2.4
). We note that this is enough to let the dimension of the Galerkin approximation go to
+\infty
.

(3)

The third difference is in derivation of the Bresch–Dejardins estimate (cf.
[
47
, Section 4, Appendix A]
). This estimate is used to converge with
\varepsilon,\nu,\delta,\eta\to 0^{+}
in (
A
). In fact, this is a crucial step to obtain an augmented regularity for the density, when the pressure is not present in the equation. Here, one can obtain a bound

\sqrt{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

Since it is obtained via testing the momentum equation by
\nabla_{x}\log\rho
, the additional estimate in our case is for the term

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\nabla_{x}(\log\rho(t,x))D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|.\end{split}

Similarly as in point 2, we may use Young’s inequality and mass conservation to obtain

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\nabla_{x}(\log\rho(t,x))D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
&\phantom{=}=2\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sqrt{\rho(t,x)}\nabla_{x}\sqrt{\rho(t,x)}D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
&\leq\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho|u|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\left(T+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}\sqrt{\rho}|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right)\left(T+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho|u|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right).\end{split}

Comparing with the left-hand side of (
A.2
), the inequality above, together with the bound on
\|\rho|u|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}
, allows us to use Grönwall’s inequality and deduce the Bresch–Dejardins estimate. At this point in the approximation, we get the convergence of the approximating sequence in
C([0,T];L^{3/2})
strongly for the density, in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})
strongly and in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{2})
weakly for the square root of the density, in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{3/2})
strongly for the momentum, and in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
weakly for the velocity (cf.
[
47
, Lemma 4.1, 4.3]
).

(4)

The fourth difference is in obtaining the so-called Mellet–Vasseur estimate (cf.
[
47
, Section 5, Lemma 5.1]
). The idea inside
[
45
]
is quite similar to the classical proof of the existence of weak solutions to compressible Navier–Stokes by Lions
[
43
]
, where the augmented density regularity is used to improve the possible estimates on the velocity field. After obtaining this estimate, one can converge with
\kappa,r_{0},r_{1}\to 0^{+}
in (
A
). We again focus only on the non-local term, but we need some technical definitions either way. Let us define cut-off functions for density

\phi^{0}_{m}(\rho)=1,\text{ for }\rho>\frac{1}{m},\quad\phi^{0}_{m}(\rho)=0,\text{ for }\rho<\frac{1}{2m},\quad|(\phi^{0}_{m})^{\prime}|<2m,

and

\phi^{\infty}_{k}(\rho)=1,\text{ for }\rho<k,\quad\phi^{\infty}_{k}(\rho)=0,\text{ for }\rho>2k,\quad|(\phi^{\infty}_{k})^{\prime}|<\frac{2}{k}.

Then, we can set

v_{m,k}=\phi_{m,k}(\rho)u,\qquad\phi_{m,k}(\rho)=\phi^{0}_{m}(\rho)\phi_{k}^{\infty}(\rho),

although we shall skip the subscripts
m,k
in the following explanation. Moreover, we fix the following approximations of the function
F(z)=\frac{1+z^{2}}{2}\ln(1+z^{2})

\displaystyle F_{n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1+z^{2}}{2}\ln(1+z^{2}),\quad z\leq n\\
\left(nz+\frac{1-n^{2}}{2}\right)\ln(1+z^{2}),\quad z>n\end{array}\right.

and for its derivative

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{z}F^{\prime}_{n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+\ln(1+z^{2}),\quad z\leq n\\
\frac{n}{z}\ln(1+z^{2})+\frac{2nz+1-n^{2}}{1+z^{2}},\quad z>n.\end{array}\right.

Functions above are subject to the bounds

F_{n}(z)\leq C_{n}|z|^{1+\delta,}\qquad\psi_{n}(z)z\leq C_{n}|z|^{\delta},

for some
C_{n}>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, and

(A.7)

\displaystyle F_{n}(z)\leq C+C|z|^{2+\delta},\qquad\psi_{n}(z)z\leq C+C|z|^{1+\delta},

for some
C>0
. With those definitions at hand, we define a test function
\Phi=\xi(t)\psi_{n}(|v|)v\phi(\rho)
, for fixed non-negative
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T)
, and test the momentum equation with it. Then, we aim to estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\Phi(t,x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|.

To do so, we note that
F_{n}
satisfies (cf.
[
47
, Proposition 5.7]
)

zF^{\prime}_{n}(z)\lesssim F_{n}(z),\qquad F_{n}^{*}(F_{n}^{\prime}(z))\lesssim F_{n}(z),

hence, with the use of mass conservation, and the Fenchel–Young inequality

(A.8)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\Phi(t,x)D^{2}K(x-y)(u(t,x)-u(t,y))\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\right|\\
&\leq\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)F_{n}^{\prime}(|v|)\phi(\rho)\rho(t,x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,y)|u(t,y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)F_{n}^{\prime}(|v|)\phi(\rho)\rho|u|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)\rho(t,x)\left(F_{n}^{*}(F_{n}^{\prime}(|v|))+F_{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,y)u(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)|v|F_{n}^{\prime}(|v|)\rho(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)\rho(t,x)\left(F_{n}^{*}(F_{n}^{\prime}(|v|))+F_{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,y)u(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\phantom{=}+C\|D^{2}K\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)F_{n}(|v|)\rho(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Using the bound (
A.7
), and the already shown energy bounds, we estimate

(A.9)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)\rho(t,x)\left(F_{n}^{*}(F_{n}^{\prime}(|v|))+F_{n}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,y)u(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)\rho(t,x)(F_{n}(|v|)+C+C\|\rho u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}^{2+\delta})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\xi(t)\rho(t,x)(F_{n}(|v|)+C+C\|\rho|u|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}^{\frac{2+\delta}{2}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

Combining (
A.9
) with (
A.8
) we are set to use the weak version of Grönwall’s lemma, which we recall below for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition A.2

(Lemma B.1,
[
47
]
)
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(0,T)
satisfy

-\int_{0}^{T}\xi^{\prime}(t)f(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}\xi(t)(af(t)+b(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

for any
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,T)
,
\xi\geq 0
, a constant
a\geq 0
, and a non-negative function
b\in L^{1}(0,T)
. Then, for almost all
0\leq s<t<T
we have

f(t)\leq f(s)e^{a(t-s)}+\int_{s}^{t}e^{a(t-\tau)}b(\tau)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau.

Thus, we get the needed estimate on

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)F(|u|)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

Note, that in our case this technique does not provide us with the estimate on

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(|x-y|)\rho(t,x)\rho(t,y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

which is present in (
A.3
). At the end of this approximation step, we obtain the convergence in
C([0,T];L^{3/2})
strongly for the density, in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{p})
,
(p<3/2)
strongly for the momentum, and in
L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})
strongly for the square root of the density multiplied by the velocity (cf.
[
47
, Lemma 6.1]
).

(5)

The part of the proof devoted to expansion of the torus to the whole space is actually the same as in
[
47
]
. We only note that all of the bounds on the new nonlocal term from previous points are uniform with respect to the size of the domain. Hence, one can obtain the same convergences as in the step before when letting the size of torus go to
+\infty
.

(6)

At last, we need to explain how to obtain the second moment bounds on density. We note that as approximation procedure is done by the expansion of the torus, it is possible to test the continuity equation in (
A
) (after the convergence with
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
), by
|x|^{2}
. Then, using Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\int|x|^{2}\rho(t,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int|x|^{2}\rho_{0}(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=2\int_{0}^{t}\int\rho u\cdot x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int|x|^{2}\rho(s,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\int\rho(s,x)|u(s,x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

which by classical Grönwall’s inequality grants us desired bounds.

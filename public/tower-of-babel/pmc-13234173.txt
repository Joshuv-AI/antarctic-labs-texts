A high-efficiency multi-port bidirectional converter for renewable energy and hybrid electric vehicle applications
Authors: K Suresh, E Parimalasundar, P Aravind
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe growing demand for electrification and the integration of renewable energy sources necessitate advanced power conversion technologies that combine efficiency and versatility. This paper introduces a novel Multi-Port Bidirectional Converter (MBPC) designed for both grid and hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) applications. Featuring two input and two output ports, the MBPC achieves an efficiency exceeding 95% and a power density above 10 W/cm². It effectively manages energy from sources with varying current and voltage profiles, supporting both boost DC-AC and boost DC-DC operations. The innovative design incorporates a streamlined control strategy and a reduced component count, cutting manufacturing costs by up to 30% compared to traditional converters. The proposed MBPC demonstrates significant potential for advancing power electronics in renewable energy systems and electric vehicles, offering a versatile, efficient, and cost-effective solution for energy storage, motor control, and power management applications.Keywords: Multi-port bidirectional converter (MBPC), Hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), renewable energy systems, DC-DC/DC-AC conversion, Power density, Energy storage and managementSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science

The state-space representation is a mathematical model used to describe the behavior of systems in terms of state variables and differential equations. By deriving a state-space model for the MBPC, we can capture its dynamic characteristics, allowing for precise control of the converter’s operation. This model serves as the foundation for designing control algorithms that regulate the output voltages during various operational modes. Derivation of the State-Space Model, The state-space model of the MBPC is derived using a systematic approach that involves several key steps: State Space Equation for ON/OFF Positions of Switches: The first step involves defining the state variables and deriving the state-space equations for different operational modes. This requires understanding the converter’s topology, including its switches, inductors, and capacitors. The ON/OFF position of switches directly affects the state of the converter, dictating how energy flows through the system. Given that power converters operate in switching modes, the state equations must be averaged to obtain a continuous representation. This step involves averaging the state equations over a switching cycle, resulting in a model that can be used for control design. Perturbation analysis is used to examine the converter’s response to small changes in input, load, or duty cycle. This step is critical for understanding the stability and transient behavior of the system, allowing control strategies to be developed that mitigate adverse effects. The final step is to create the state-space matrices that represent the system. These matrices encapsulate the dynamics of the converter and provide the mathematical foundation for control system design. By constructing these matrices, we can analyze the system’s stability, determine control parameters, and implement feedback control loops.

The efficiency of power converters is a critical metric that determines their viability for various applications, such as electric vehicles (EVs), energy storage, and renewable energy systems. Power loss, the inverse of efficiency, provides insights into where energy is dissipated within a converter. This section analyzes the efficiency and power loss data for a Multi-Port Bidirectional DC-DC/DC-AC Converter (MBPC) across five operational modes, examining the implications for the converter’s performance, reliability, and potential areas for optimization. Efficiency in power converters is the ratio of output power to input power, usually expressed as a percentage. A higher efficiency percentage indicates that the converter effectively transfers energy with minimal losses. Power loss is the proportion of energy that is dissipated, primarily due to resistance, switching, and other inherent factors within the converter. The provided data shows the efficiency and power loss percentages for the MBPC in five different modes. The following is an in-depth analysis of each mode, focusing on efficiency trends, sources of power loss, and potential improvements. Mode 1 exhibits a high efficiency of 97.95%, indicating that the MBPC operates with minimal energy loss. This level of efficiency is desirable for applications where energy conservation is critical, such as in electric vehicles and renewable energy systems. The relatively low power loss of 2.05% suggests that the converter’s design effectively mitigates energy dissipation through resistive losses and switching inefficiencies. However, even a small power loss can generate heat, potentially affecting the converter’s reliability and lifespan. Proper thermal management and heat dissipation strategies are essential to ensure long-term operation without compromising performance. Mode 2 shows a slightly higher power loss of 2.09%, resulting in a marginal decrease in efficiency to 97.93%. This minor change in efficiency could be due to several factors, including variations in switching frequencies, load conditions, or internal resistance. While the efficiency remains high, the increase in power loss might indicate potential areas for improvement, such as refining the control strategy or optimizing component selection. Given the slight decrease in efficiency, it’s crucial to assess the converter’s stability and performance under different load and input conditions. This analysis will help identify the root cause of the increased power loss and guide improvements in converter design and control. Mode 3 experiences a noticeable drop in efficiency to 97.33%, with a corresponding increase in power loss to 2.67%. This mode’s higher power loss could be attributed to a variety of factors, such as increased switching activity, higher resistance, or suboptimal control parameters. The decrease in efficiency, while still relatively high, suggests that the converter may be operating under conditions that induce greater energy dissipation. To address this increased power loss, further investigation into the converter’s operating conditions is required. This could include analyzing the duty cycle, switching frequency, and other control parameters to determine if adjustments could improve efficiency. Additionally, evaluating the thermal characteristics of the converter might reveal areas where heat dissipation could be enhanced, reducing the risk of overheating and further power loss. This mode’s outstanding performance indicates that the converter operates with minimal energy dissipation, suggesting that its control strategy, switching frequency, and component design are optimized for efficiency. The low power loss implies that heat generation is minimal, reducing the need for extensive thermal management. The high efficiency in Mode 4 could serve as a benchmark for optimizing the other modes. Conclusion and future research directions

This paper presented a novel Multi-Port Bidirectional Converter (MBPC) tailored for grid and hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) applications, addressing the pressing need for efficient and versatile power conversion solutions in modern energy systems. The MBPC’s innovative design, featuring two input and two output ports, demonstrates exceptional performance with an efficiency exceeding 95% and a power density surpassing 10 W/cm². By supporting both boost DC-AC and boost DC-DC operations, the converter effectively manages energy from sources with varying current and voltage profiles, ensuring compatibility with diverse renewable energy and electric vehicle scenarios. The streamlined control strategy and reduced component count not only enhance system reliability but also lower manufacturing costs by up to 30% compared to traditional converters. These advancements position the MBPC as a cost-effective and scalable solution for energy storage, motor control, and power management applications. This research highlights the MBPC’s potential to revolutionize power electronics for renewable energy and electric vehicle technologies. Its efficiency, flexibility, and affordability make it a strong candidate for adoption in future energy systems, contributing to global efforts toward sustainable energy utilization and reduced carbon emissions. Future work will focus on expanding the converter’s scalability and exploring its integration with emerging energy technologies.

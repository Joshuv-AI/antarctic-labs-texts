On photonic band gaps in two-dimensional photonic crystal fibres. Analysis in the vicinity of the low-dielectric light line.
Shane Cooper, Ilia Kamotski

Abstract
We consider ‘off-axis’ electromagnetic wave propagation down the homogeneous direction of a low-loss two-dimensional periodic dielectric (or photonic crystal fibre) near the light line of the low-dielectric material constituent. Numerous physical and numerical experiments demonstrate the presence of photonic band gaps in the vicinity of this ‘critical’ light line. We mathematically analyse the existence of photonic band gaps near the line and characterise them in terms of frequency gaps in the spectrum of the Maxwell equations restricted to the line. We apply the results to both one-dimensional photonic crystal fibres, and a genuinely two-dimensional photonic crystal fibres with ‘thin’ inclusions, namely “ARROW” fibres. In the case of ARROW fibres, by an asymptotic analysis, in terms of the small inclusion parameter, we demonstrate the existence of low frequency photonic band gaps.

Introduction

A two-dimensional photonic crystal fibre is a low-loss periodic dielectric with material properties that
are homogeneous along one of its axes. Photonic crystal fibres (PCF) are known to possess photonic band gaps; that is some PCF prevent certain frequency ranges of light from propagating in some, or all, directions. Such devices are of practical importance as the can be used to construct wave guides with optical performances that surpass traditional optical wave-guides (that work on the principle of total internal reflection), such as omni-directional reflectors and narrow-band filters, to name a few (there is too much literature to reference but refer to the book
[
6
]
and citations therein). Consequently, an important mathematical task is to analytically characterise photonic band gaps in terms of a PCFs material and geometric properties.

In physical literature, the origin of a photonic band gap is explained as follows (cf.
[
10

\omega

-plane is divided into two regions by the ‘light line’
\omega(k)=ck/\sqrt{\epsilon}
, for

k
and frequency
\omega
, the key observation of the above argument is this:
if
(\omega,k)
is above the light line for the low-dielectric matrix phase, the whole PCF can support the wave but just below the low-dielectric matrix light line (and above the high-dielectric inclusion light line) the matrix phase can no longer support propagating EM waves. It is argued that, for this reason, one should expect photonic band gaps to appear near the low-dielectric inclusion light line. This heuristic argument is supported in literature through numerous physical and numerical experiments (
[
10
]
,
[
8
]

[

to name a few).

In this work we provide a rigorous mathematical analysis of the above heuristics. We henceforth refer to the low-dielectric inclusion light line as the critical (light) line.
Before, discussing this article’s results, we review previous related mathematical results. For non-trivial wave propagation down the PCF, i.e.
k\neq 0
, then the EM field does not admit polarisations; consequently, it can been seen that EM propagation directly above the critical line reduces the Maxwell system to a system of two second-order elliptic differential spectral equations with coefficients that become highly contrasting and an ellipticity constant that degenerates to zero as one approaches the critical light line. In
[
4

[

]

[

It was shown the Bloch spectrum converged to the spectrum of some ‘limit’ operator and that photonic band gaps near the critical line will appear if the determined limit operator’s spectrum possessed gaps.

In this work, we improve these results in several key ways. First, we establish that the limit operator, found in
[
4
]
, is precisely the Maxwell operator restricted to the critical line. Furthermore, we utilise some methods of
[
5
]
to improve the above convergence results to provide operator-type error estimates between the Maxwell operators on and above the critical light line, respectively, in terms of the distance to the line. This implies, amongst other things, that if the Maxwell operator, on the critical light line, has gaps in its spectrum then photonic band gaps exist in a quantifiable neighbourhood of the restricted Maxwell spectral gaps. Finally, we study the particular problem of ARROW fibres, i.e. PCF with thin inclusions, to demonstrate the existence of low-frequency gaps in this restricted Maxwell spectrum and therefore, by the above results, prove the existence of low-frequency photonic band gaps for ARROW PCF.

\mathcal{V}(\theta):=\{v\in H^{1}_{per}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2})\,|\,\mathrm{div}\,{v}+\mathrm{i}\theta\cdot v=0,\text{ and }\mathrm{curl}\,{v}+\mathrm{i}\theta^{\perp}\cdot v=0\text{ in $\square\backslash R$}\}.

We now argue as in
[
5
, Section 3]
. First note that the closed subspace
\mathcal{V}(\theta)
is the kernel of the form
a_{\theta}
, precisely

\mathcal{V}(\theta);=\{v\in H^{1}_{per}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2})\,:\,a_{\theta}[v]=0\}.

Furthermore, it can be seen that
a_{\theta}
is coercive on
(\mathcal{V}(\theta))^{\perp}
, the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{V}(\theta)
in
H^{1}_{per}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2})
, with coercivity constant
\nu(\theta)>0
. In fact, we can say much more; the form
a_{\theta}
is
uniformly
coercive on
(\mathcal{V}(\theta))^{\perp}
, i.e.
\nu_{*}:=\inf_{\theta}\nu(\theta)>0
. Indeed, it was shown in
[
5
, Theorem 3.4]
that the uniform coercivity of
a_{\theta}
on the orthogonal complement to its kernel is equivalent to the kernel being continuous with respect to
\theta
. We demonstrated in Theorem
4.1
that
V(\theta)
was Lipschitz continuous in
\theta
, thus clearly
\mathcal{V}(\theta)
is Lipschitz in
\theta
and consequently Theorem 3.2 of
[
5
]
holds, which, in this setting, implies:

a_{\theta}[u_{\varepsilon,\theta}-v_{\varepsilon,\theta}]+b_{\theta}[u_{\varepsilon,\theta}-v_{\varepsilon,\theta}]\leq\nu_{\star}^{-2}\varepsilon^{2}\|F\|_{H^{-1}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2})}.

That is (
6.10
) holds and the proof of Lemma
3.2
is complete.

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

By (
2.22
) and (
6.8
), it is sufficient to prove

\left|b_{\varepsilon}(\phi,\psi)-b(\phi,\psi)\right|\leq\varepsilon L_{0}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2})}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2})}\quad\forall\phi,\psi\in H^{1}(\square;\mathbb{C}^{2}),

(6.11)

for some
L_{0}>0
. Now, from the inequalities

|\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}-1|\leq\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad|\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}-(1-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2})|\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2},\quad\varepsilon\in(0,1),

we find (cf. (
2.9
) and (
2.13
)) that

\displaystyle\left|b_{\varepsilon}(\phi,\psi)-b(\phi,\psi)\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\square\backslash R}|\nabla\phi_{1}^{\perp}\cdot\overline{\nabla\psi_{2}}+\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\overline{\nabla^{\perp}\psi_{1}}|+

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon}{\gamma(\gamma+\varepsilon)}\int_{R}|\nabla\phi_{1}\cdot\overline{\nabla\psi_{1}}+\epsilon_{0}\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\overline{\nabla\psi_{2}}|+\frac{(\gamma+1)\varepsilon}{\gamma(\gamma+\varepsilon)}\int_{R}|\nabla\phi_{1}^{\perp}\cdot\overline{\nabla\psi_{2}}+\nabla\phi_{2}\cdot\overline{\nabla^{\perp}\psi_{1}}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\square\backslash R}|\nabla\phi||\nabla\psi|+\frac{2\epsilon_{0}\varepsilon}{\gamma^{2}}\int_{R}|\nabla\phi||\nabla\psi|,

where the last inequality followed from
\epsilon_{0}=\gamma+1>1
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Finally, by an application of Hölder’s inequality, (
6.11
) holds for
L_{0}=\max\{\frac{1}{2},2\epsilon_{0}\gamma^{-2}\}
.
∎

6.3
Proof of Theorem
5.1

Throughout, for a set
\mathcal{O}
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{O}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{O}}
will denote the standard
L^{2}(\mathcal{O})
inner product and norm respectively;
\chi_{\mathcal{O}}
shall denote the operation that extends a function by zero outside of
\mathcal{O}
.
B_{\alpha}
and
S_{\alpha}
will denote the ball and circle, respectively, of radius
\alpha
centred at the origin.

Fix
\theta\neq 0
,
0<\delta<\pi
. For
i=1,2
, consider
f_{i}\in L^{2}(R_{\delta})
where
R_{\delta}:=\delta\Omega
and
\Omega\subset B_{1}
. Recall
A_{\theta}f_{i}=u_{i}\in H^{1}_{\theta}(\square)
, are the solutions to

-\Delta u_{i}=\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{i}\quad\text{in $\square$}.

Let us introduce the solutions
v_{i}
to

\displaystyle-\Delta v_{i}=\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{i}\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ with $v_{i}(x)=-\frac{d_{i}}{2\pi}\ln{|x|}+o(1)$ as $|x|\rightarrow+\infty$.}

Clearly,
d_{i}=\langle f_{i},1\rangle_{R_{\delta}}
.

Furthermore, we shall denote
v_{\mathrm{i}}=A_{\delta}f_{i}
. Notice,
A_{\delta}
is non-negative and self-adjoint.

Let us introduce the non-negative sesquilinear forms

\displaystyle{b}^{(\delta)}(f,\tilde{f}):=\gamma^{-1}\int_{R_{\delta}}f\cdot\overline{\tilde{f}}-\int_{R_{\delta}}\left[\Big((A_{\delta}f_{1})_{,22}+(A_{\delta}f_{2})_{,12}\Big)\overline{\tilde{f}_{1}}+\Big((A_{\delta}f_{1})_{,21}+(A_{\delta}f_{2})_{,11}\Big)\overline{\tilde{f}_{2}}\right],

(6.12)

and

c_{\delta,\theta}(f,\tilde{f}):=g_{\theta}(0)\Big(\langle f_{1},1\rangle_{R_{\delta}}\overline{\langle\tilde{f}_{1},1\rangle_{R_{\delta}}}+\langle f_{2},1\rangle_{R_{\delta}}\overline{\langle\tilde{f}_{2},1\rangle_{R_{\delta}}}\ \Big)\\
+\int_{R_{\delta}}\Big(f_{1}\overline{A_{\delta}\tilde{f_{1}}}+f_{2}\overline{A_{\delta}\tilde{f}_{2}}\Big)+\gamma\int_{R_{\delta}}\Big((A_{\delta}f_{1})_{,2}+(A_{\delta}f_{2})_{,1}\Big)\overline{\Big((A_{\delta}\tilde{f}_{1})_{,2}+(A_{\delta}\tilde{f}_{2})_{,1}\Big)}.

(6.13)

where we recall
g_{\theta}
, the regular part of the quasi-periodic Green’s function of the Laplacian (cf. (
5.5
)). We shall see below, precisely (
6.18
), that

g_{\theta}(0)\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln{\pi};

(6.14)

To prove Theorem
5.1
, it is sufficient to prove the following result:

Lemma 6.2
.

\left|\frac{b_{\theta}[f]}{c_{\theta}[f]}-\frac{b^{(\delta)}[f]}{c_{\delta,\theta}[f]}\right|\leq C_{1}\delta\left(\frac{b_{\theta}[f]}{c_{\theta}[f]}+\frac{b^{(\delta)}[f]}{c_{\delta,\theta}[f]}\right),\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(R_{\delta};\mathbb{C}^{2}),

for some
C_{1}<1+3\gamma
.

Indeed, Lemma
6.2
implies

|\lambda^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta)-\nu^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta)|\leq C_{1}\delta\Big(\lambda^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta)+\Lambda^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta)\Big),

for

\nu^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\\
\dim{V}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{b^{(\delta)}[f]}{c_{\delta,\theta}[f]}.

Furthermore, as
R_{\delta}=\delta\Omega
, then by the rescaling
x=\delta y
for
y\in\Omega
, we find

\Lambda^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta)=\delta^{-2}\mu^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta),

where
\mu^{(\delta)}_{n}(\theta)
is given in (
5.6
). That is, Theorem
5.1
holds.

For ease of exposition we shall use, henceforth,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{R_{\delta}}
. Let us prove Lemma
6.2
. The following, key estimate, is instrumental to the proof:

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
\alpha\in(\delta,\pi)
, one has

\big|\langle f_{i},u_{j}\rangle-\langle f_{i},v_{j}\rangle-g_{\theta}(0)d_{i}\overline{d_{j}}\big|\leq 3\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\sqrt{\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{i}|^{2}}\sqrt{\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{j}|^{2}}.

Remark 6.1
.

Notice
\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{i}|^{2}\geq 0
since
A_{\delta}
is non-negative and
g_{\theta}(0)
is positive (see (
6.14
)).

To prove this key estimate we shall use the following inequalities which directly follow from basic properties of harmonic functions:

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
v_{i}
and
g_{\theta}
be as above,
\alpha\in[\delta,\pi)
and
h
an harmonic function in
B_{\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\nabla h,\nabla h\rangle_{B_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\langle\nabla h,\nabla h\rangle_{B_{\alpha}};

(6.15)

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},h\rangle_{S_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=-\langle v_{i},\partial_{r}h\rangle_{S_{\beta}}-d_{i}\overline{h(0)};\quad\text{for $\delta\leq\beta\leq\alpha$;}

(6.16)

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\alpha})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle+|d_{i}|^{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln{\alpha};

(6.17)

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\alpha})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq g_{\theta}(0)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln{\alpha}.

(6.18)

Proof.

We have the following polar representations:

v_{i}(r,\theta)=-\frac{d_{i}}{2\pi}\ln{r}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}r^{-n}e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta},\qquad\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash B_{\delta}$},

(6.19)

and

h(r,\theta)=h(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}r^{n}e^{\mathrm{i}n\theta},\qquad\text{in $B_{\alpha}$},

(6.20)

for some constants
c_{n},h_{n}
.

Proof of (
6.15
).
This clearly follows from (
6.20
). Indeed, direct calculation

\langle\nabla h,\nabla h\rangle_{B_{\beta}}=\langle\partial_{r}h,h\rangle_{S_{\beta}}=2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\beta^{2n}|h_{n}|^{2}\qquad\text{for $\delta\leq\beta\leq\alpha$.}

Proof of (
6.16
).
Since
v
and
h
are both harmonic in the annulus
B_{\alpha}\backslash B_{\beta}
, Green’s second identity gives

\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},h\rangle_{S_{\beta}}-\langle v_{i},\partial_{r}h\rangle_{S_{\beta}}=\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},h\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}-\langle v_{i},\partial_{r}h\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}.

Now, by (
6.19
) and (
6.20
), direct calculation gives

\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},h\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=-d_{i}\overline{h(0)}-2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc_{n}\overline{h_{n}}

and

\langle v_{i},\partial_{r}h\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc_{n}\overline{h_{n}}.

The above assertions imply (
6.16
).

Proof of (
6.17
).
From (
6.19
), one computes

\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},v_{i}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=|d_{i}|^{2}\frac{\ln{\alpha}}{2\pi}-2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|c_{n}|^{2}\alpha^{-2n}\leq|d_{i}|^{2}\frac{\ln{\alpha}}{2\pi}

Then

\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle=\langle\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{i},v_{i}\rangle_{B_{\alpha}}=\langle-\Delta v_{i},v_{i}\rangle_{B_{\alpha}}=\langle\nabla v_{i},\nabla v_{i}\rangle_{B_{\alpha}}-\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},v_{i}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}},

and (
6.17
) follows.

Proof of (
6.18
).
By the quasi-periodicity of
G_{\theta}
,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle G_{\theta},-\Delta G_{\theta}\rangle_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}=\|\nabla G_{\theta}\|_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}^{2}+\langle G_{\theta},\partial_{r}G_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}},

and, since
G_{\theta}=-2\pi^{-1}\ln{r}+g_{\theta}
,

\displaystyle\langle G_{\theta},\partial_{r}G_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\langle 2\pi^{-1}\ln{r},\partial_{r}2\pi^{-1}\ln{r}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}-\langle 2\pi^{-1}\ln{r},\partial_{r}g_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}-\langle g_{\theta},\partial_{r}2\pi^{-1}\ln{r}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}+\langle g_{\theta},\partial_{r}g_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}.

Clearly,

\langle 2\pi^{-1}\ln{r},\partial_{r}2\pi^{-1}\ln{r}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=2\pi^{-1}\ln{\alpha},

and, since
g_{\theta}
is harmonic in
\square
, then

\langle g_{\theta},\partial_{r}g_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\alpha}}^{2}.

Furthermore, using representation (
6.20
) (for
h=g_{\theta}
) gives

\langle g_{\theta},\partial_{r}2\pi^{-1}\ln{r}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=g_{\theta}(0)\quad\text{and}\quad\langle 2\pi^{-1}\ln{r},\partial_{r}g_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=0.

Therefore, putting the above together gives

0=\|\nabla G_{\theta}\|_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}^{2}+2\pi^{-1}\ln{\alpha}-g_{\theta}(0)+\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\alpha}}^{2};

and (
6.18
) follows.

∎

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

The function
w_{j}:=u_{j}-v_{j}
is harmonic in
\square
and so one has

\displaystyle\langle f_{i},u_{j}\rangle-\langle f_{i},v_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{i},w_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}=\langle-\Delta v_{i},w_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla v_{i},\nabla w_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}-\langle\partial_{r}v_{i},w_{j}\rangle_{S_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{harm2}}}{{=}}\langle\nabla v_{i},\nabla w_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}+\langle v_{i},\partial_{r}w_{j}\rangle_{S_{\delta}}+d_{i}\overline{w_{j}(0)}

\displaystyle=2\langle\nabla v_{i},\nabla w_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}+d_{i}\overline{w_{j}(0)}.

The last equality is from the divergence theorem and the fact that
w_{j}
is harmonic in
B_{\delta}
. Now,

\displaystyle w_{j}(0)=u_{j}(0)-v_{j}(0)

\displaystyle=\langle\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{j},G_{\theta}\rangle_{\square}-\langle\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{j},-\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\ln{r}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=\langle\chi_{R_{\delta}}f_{j},g_{\theta}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}=\langle-\Delta v_{j},g_{\theta}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla v_{j},\nabla g_{\theta}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}-\langle\partial_{r}v_{j},g_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{harm2}}}{{=}}\langle\nabla v_{j},\nabla g_{\theta}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}+\langle v_{j},\partial_{r}g_{\theta}\rangle_{S_{\delta}}+d_{j}\overline{g_{\theta}(0)}

\displaystyle=2\langle\nabla v_{j},\nabla g_{\theta}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}+d_{j}\overline{g_{\theta}(0)}.

(6.21)

Therefore

\displaystyle|\langle f_{i},u_{j}\rangle-\langle f_{i},v_{j}\rangle-g_{\theta}(0)d_{i}\overline{d_{j}}|

\displaystyle=|2\langle\nabla v_{i},\nabla w_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}+2d_{i}\langle\nabla g_{\theta},\nabla v_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{B_{\delta}}\|\nabla w_{j}\|_{B_{\delta}}+2|d_{i}|\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\delta}}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{B_{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{B_{\alpha}}\|\nabla w_{j}\|_{B_{\delta}}+2|d_{i}|\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\delta}}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{B_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{harm1}}}{{\leq}}2\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\Big(\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{B_{\alpha}}\|\nabla w_{j}\|_{B_{\alpha}}+|d_{i}|\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\alpha}}\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{B_{\alpha}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\sqrt{\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{B_{\alpha}}^{2}+|d_{i}|^{2}\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\alpha}}^{2}}\sqrt{\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{B_{\alpha}}^{2}+\|\nabla w_{j}\|_{B_{\alpha}}^{2}}.

Now, by (
6.17
) and (
6.18
) one has

\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\alpha})}^{2}+|d_{i}|^{2}\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\alpha})}^{2}\leq\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle+|d_{i}|^{2}g_{\theta}(0).

(6.22)

Therefore,
to prove the lemma, it suffices to show

\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}+\|\nabla w_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}\leq 2\Big(\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+|d_{j}|^{2}g_{\theta}(0)\Big).

(6.23)

To this end, note that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\langle-\Delta u_{j},u_{j}\rangle_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}=\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}u_{j},u_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}v_{j},v_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}w_{j},w_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}v_{j},w_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}w_{j},v_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}+\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}-\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+\|\nabla w_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}v_{j},w_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}w_{j},v_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}.

(6.24)

Now, by (
6.16
)

\langle\partial_{r}v_{j},w_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}+\langle\partial_{r}w_{j},v_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}=2\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{Im}\langle\partial_{r}w_{j},v_{j}\rangle_{S_{\alpha}}-d_{j}\overline{w_{j}(0)}.

Therefore, taking the real part of (
6.24
), and noting that
\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle
is real (cf. (
6.17
)), gives

0=\|\nabla u_{j}\|^{2}_{\square\backslash B_{\alpha}}+\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}-\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+\|\nabla w_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}-\mathrm{Re}(d_{j}\overline{w_{j}(0)});

i.e.

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}+\|\nabla w_{j}\|^{2}_{B_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+\mathrm{Re}(d_{j}\overline{w_{j}(0)})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{15.4.24e3}}}{{=}}\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+|d_{j}|^{2}g_{\theta}(0)+\mathrm{Re}(2d_{j}\langle\nabla g_{\theta},\nabla v_{j}\rangle_{B_{\delta}})

\displaystyle\leq\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+|d_{j}|^{2}g_{\theta}(0)+|d_{j}|^{2}\|\nabla g_{\theta}\|_{B_{\delta}}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{B_{\delta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{15.4.24e4}}}{{\leq}}2\Big(\langle f_{j},v_{j}\rangle+|d_{j}|^{2}g_{\theta}(0)\Big).

That is, (
6.23
) holds and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma
6.3
implies the following inequalities:

.

For any

,

Proof.

).

, then

Therefore,

|\langle u_{i,j},u_{k,l}\rangle-\langle v_{i,j},v_{k,l}\rangle|\leq 2\sqrt{3}\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\sqrt{\langle f_{i},v_{i}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{i}|^{2}}\sqrt{\langle f_{k},v_{k}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{k}|^{2}}

.

6.26
).
It is enough to prove the result for
f_{k},\tilde{f}_{l}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(R_{\delta})

u_{k,i}=A_{\theta}(f_{k,i})
and

for

\langle u_{k,ij},\tilde{f}_{l}\rangle=\langle A_{\theta}(f_{k,i}),-\tilde{f}_{l,,j}\rangle,

\langle v_{k,ij},\tilde{f}_{l}\rangle=\langle A_{\delta}(f_{k,i}),-\tilde{f}_{l,,j}\rangle.

It remains to note

\langle g_{,i},(A_{\delta}g)_{,i}\rangle\leq\langle g,g\rangle,\quad g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(R_{\delta}),\ i=1,2.

\langle g_{,i},(A_{\delta}g)_{,i}\rangle\leq\langle g_{,1},(A_{\delta}g)_{,1}\rangle+\langle g_{,2},(A_{\delta}g)_{,2}\rangle=-\langle g,\Delta(A_{\delta}g)\rangle=\langle g,g\rangle.

The desired result now follows.

.

For any

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{b}_{\theta}[f]-b^{(\delta)}[f]|

\displaystyle\leq\gamma\delta\,b^{(\delta)}[f];

Proof.

Proof of (
6.27
).

By (

) (for

) and (

), one has

|b_{\theta}[f]-b^{(\delta)}[f]|\leq 3\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\big(\|f_{1}\|^{2}+2\|f_{1}\|\|f_{2}\|+\|f_{2}\|^{2}\big)=3\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\|f\|^{2}\leq 3\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\gamma b^{(\delta)}[f],

.

Proof of (

).
By Lemma

and (

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\langle f_{i},A_{\theta}f_{i}\rangle-\big(\langle f_{i},A_{\delta}f_{i}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{i}|^{2}\big)\right)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq 3\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(\langle f_{i},A_{\delta}f_{i}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{i}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 3\frac{\delta}{\alpha}c_{\delta,\theta}[f].

\displaystyle\big|\langle(A_{\theta}f_{i})_{,j},(A_{\theta}f_{i})_{,j}\rangle-\langle(A_{\delta}f_{i})_{,j},(A_{\delta}f_{i})_{,j}\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{3}\frac{\delta}{\alpha}\big(\langle f_{i},A_{\delta}f_{i}\rangle+g_{\theta}(0)|d_{i}|^{2}\big),

∎

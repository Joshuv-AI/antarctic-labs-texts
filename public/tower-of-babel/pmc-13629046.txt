Rare Earth-Silver Synergistic Strategy: Preparation of a Potential Antibacterial Material for Wound Related Applications
Authors: Baohong Shen, Weixin Wang, Yajun Li, Min Yang, Junling Hu, Zejing Xu, Yuxuan Hong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Skin wounds usually cause delayed wound healing or infection due to the invasion of microorganisms, which seriously endangers human life and health. In the process of first aid and treatment, antibacterial dressings are usually used to achieve high efficiency in inhibiting infection and promoting wound healing. However, because of the widespread use of antibiotics or low-activity antibacterial ingredients in dressing fillers, research on dressing fillers is imminent. Here, we propose a preparation method for a high-performance dressing filler based on Y-based MOFs. Ag+ was adsorbed into the activation cavity of MOF-76-Y by supramolecular interactions and then reduced to zero-valent silver to anchor it in the framework structure of MOF-76-Y to prepare stable Ag@MOF-76-Y. Antibacterial evaluation of the system showed that Ag@MOF-76-Y has a high inhibition rate of 99.1% on Staphylococcus aureus, which is significantly higher than the antibacterial activity of MOF-76-Y. Further studies showed that its mild ROS generation rate, excellent bacterial removal efficiency, and excellent antibacterial activity were attributed to the host–guest synergy between Y-based MOFs and Ag. This work provides a new antibiotic-free, low-toxicity candidate for antibacterial dressing filler, promotes the development of next-generation dressings, and expands the application of rare earth compounds.

Introduction

As the first important line of defense for the human body to resist the invasion of external pathogens, the skin provides protection and support for the normal functioning of the human body. However, unpredictable external damage can cause immeasurable damage to the skin. According to the data of the World Health Organization, every year, tens of millions of people suffer from skin wounds due to external factors, which are not treated in time or should not lead to death or sequelae. Including burn wounds, surgical wounds, disease ulcer wounds, and pressure injury wounds, patients often have no time to repair human skin that resists harmful external pathogens, providing convenient conditions for microbial invasion. , Most importantly, the large amount of protein and tissue exudate that is expelled after a skin wound appears leads to rapid microbial proliferation, which not only delays the wound healing time but can even cause a series of complications and finally endanger human health. Therefore, it is extremely important to create self-healing environmental conditions that are unfavorable to microbial reproduction for wound healing.

Generally, when a wound occurs in the human body, the main steps of medical staff treatment include hemostasis, cleaning and disinfection, dressing protection, follow-up observation, etc. As a long-term wound treatment process, dressing protection is essential for preventing wound infection and microbial proliferation and promoting wound self-healing. In recent years, research on wound dressings usually included the research and development of dressing substrates, intelligent sensing dressing devices, and dressing components. In the process of skin wound treatment, Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) is one of the common pathogenic bacteria. Without timely intervention of this bacterium in the wound, serious complications such as osteomyelitis and bacterial pneumonia often occur. Antibiotics are the main treatment for this type of bacterial infection. It is worth noting that the continuous abuse of antibiotics will seriously promote the occurrence of bacterial resistance. Traditional dressing ingredients generally contain antibiotics or have a low activity of antibacterial components, which limits the scope of application of dressings. On the basis of this, there is an urgent need to develop wound dressing components with high antibacterial activity and no risk of antibiotic resistance to be suitable for the rapid killing of wound microorganisms.

Silver nanoparticles are considered to be one of the materials with antibacterial activity due to their sustained release of Ag+, and they are widely used as fillers for wound dressings due to their high-efficiency disinfection and sterilization and wound healing promoting properties. However, the cytotoxicity, reactive oxygen species release, and material cost issues of silver nanoparticles have been a large concern to researchers. Reducing cytotoxicity and enhancing antibacterial activity are considered to be the research directions of the new generation of antibacterial dressings. In recent years, the antibacterial activity of rare earth metals and rare earth oxides has been of wide interest. Yttrium (Y), as one of the elements with a high rare earth element abundance, with low toxicity to the human body may cause accumulation in the lungs, but its impact on the kidney and liver is negligible. As the filler in antibacterial dressings does not need to be absorbed by the respiratory tract, Y as the filler can reduce both the cytotoxicity and the economic cost of dressings. Metal–organic frameworks (MOFs) stand out in supramolecular host–guest chemistry due to their highly ordered and excellent pore properties. Compared with other rare-earth-based MOFs, research on Y-based MOFs (MOF-Y) as antibacterial dressing fillers is still limited. Combining the advantages of the low cytotoxicity and low cost of yttrium, MOF-Y is expected to achieve an improved performance of antibacterial dressing fillers through rational design. Therefore, developing a MOF-mediated silver antibacterial system that can simultaneously maintain effective antibacterial activity and improve biocompatibility is an attractive strategy.

On the basis of this, Ag@MOF-76-Y was designed and synthesized in this study. Ag+ was adsorbed into the activation cavity of MOF-76-Y through supramolecular interaction, reduced to Ag0 by a reducing agent, and stably anchored. Subsequently, the antibacterial activity of Ag@MOF-76-Y against Staphylococcus aureus was systematically evaluated. The results showed that the inhibition rate of the composite to Staphylococcus aureus reached up to 99.1%, which was significantly better than that of pure MOF-76-Y. Importantly, it has a mild reactive oxygen species production rate and excellent bacterial clearance efficiency. Its excellent performance stems from the synergistic effect of yttrium-based MOFs and silver. This study provides a new candidate material without antibiotics and low toxicity for an antibacterial dressing filler, which is of great significance for the development of a new generation of antibacterial dressings and the application of rare-earth-based compounds.

Results and Discussion

A yttrium-based metal organic framework (MOF-76-Y) was constructed by Y and the H3BTC ligand under DMF/H2O mixed solvothermal conditions. To confirm the successful synthesis of MOF-76-Y, the crystal structure of the as-prepared samples was verified by powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD). As shown in Figure c, the prepared MOF-76-Y is consistent with the crystal data file (CCDC: 703003) of MOF-76-Y simulated by Mercury software, and it has high purity and crystallinity. The pattern for pristine MOF-76-Y exhibits characteristic peaks at 2θ = 5.8°, 10.5°, 13.7 °, 17.3°, 17.8°, 19.3° and 21.0°, respectively. As expected, the structure of MOF-76-Y has a distorted pentagonal bipyramidal geometry for Y3+, and Y3+ forms helical rod-like structural building units (SBUs) via carboxylate bridging with organic ligands (Figure a,b). SBUs were connected with organic ligands and stacked to form an open framework with square windows (Figure c). The through channel of MOF-76-Y is formed between the windows to windows, and the cavity structure with a radius of 7.9 Å is formed in the center of the channel. The window sizes are 4.4 Å and 5.0 Å, respectively (Figure S1), providing a good environment for the entry and anchoring of guest molecules. Subsequently, the solvent molecules and uncoordinated organic ligands located in the cavities or pores were removed by solvent exchange to activate MOF-76-Y. It was fully mixed with AgNO3 solution, and a reducing agent was added under continuous stirring. In this process, the Ag+ originally adsorbed in the MOF-76-Y channel is reduced to Ag by the reductant. The Ag is then anchored in the cavity of the framework, changing the color of the powder from white to light yellow (Ag@MOF-76-Y). The PXRD pattern for Ag@MOF-76-Y shows that the key low angle MOF-76-Y diffraction peaks were retained (such as those at 8.6°, 13.6°, and 17.6°; Figure c). The obvious broadening and weakening of the diffraction peaks of MOF-76-Y is attributed to the decrease of crystallinity caused by the introduction of Ag, but it is worth noting that the integrity of the framework structure is not damaged after the introduction of Ag. At the same time, the key diffraction peaks of MOF-76-Y shifted slightly to the left, which was attributed to the weak lattice expansion caused by the introduction of Ag. Therefore, Ag is anchored in the cavity of MOFs, and the long-range ordered structure of MOF-76-Y is destroyed; however, the structure of MOFs main body does not change. This was further confirmed by Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) analysis. The FTIR spectra of MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y are shown in Figure d. There are four obvious infrared absorption peaks of Y in the range of 1400–1700 cm–1, which are located at 1371, 1435, 1558, and 1613 cm–1, respectively. These are very similar to the position and number of strong absorption peaks in the MOF-76-Y spectrum, which indicates that the molecular structure of MOF-76-Y host did not change significantly during the loading of silver. The microstructure and morphology of MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y were directly studied by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) analyses. SEM images showed that there was a weak morphological difference between MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y. As shown in Figure d, the original MOF-76-Y presents rod-shaped crystals with a clear structure, smooth surface, and sharp edges, which are typical characteristics of highly crystalline MOFs. In contrast, Figure e shows a more rough and angular morphology in Ag@MOF-76-Y, which is different from the regular rod structure of pure MOF-76-Y. This indicates that Ag has been successfully anchored in the framework of MOF-76-Y, resulting in morphological changes or partial collapse of the crystal structure, thus promoting the formation of the composite. It is worth noting that no silver nanoclusters with an aggregate orientation were found on the surface of Ag@MOF-76-Y, which is attributed to the porous adsorption ability of MOF-76-Y to uniformly disperse Ag. In order to explore and further confirm the types of elements contained in MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y, the elemental composition of each sample was confirmed by energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) analysis. C, N, O, and Y elements were detected in the mapping of pure MOF-76-Y, which is highly consistent with the chemical structure of MOF-76-Y (Figure S2). Subsequently, in order to further study the element distribution and composition of Ag@MOF-76-Y, EDX was used to characterize Ag@MOF-76-Y. The mapping of Ag@MOF-76-Y shows that Ag was uniformly distributed (Figure f,g). In Table S1, the EDX results of Ag@MOF-76-Y show that the atomic ratio of Ag was calculated to be 0.66. Next, the microstructure of Ag@MOF-76-Y was characterized by transmission electron microscopy (TEM). As shown in Figure a, the image shows that a small amount of Ag nanoparticles was uniformly distributed, and the corresponding EDX mapping also clearly confirms the uniform distribution of Ag elements in the composite, confirming the successful in situ synthesis of Ag@MOF-76-Y (Figure b).

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to analyze the composition of the material and the chemical valence of elements and further verify the bonding mode and content of Ag atoms in the composite and the framework material. Compared with unmodified MOF-76-Y, a Ag (3d) peak was observed in the Ag@MOF-76-Y spectrum, confirming that Ag was effectively chelated by MOF-76-Y (Figure a). As shown in Figure b, it is observed in the high-resolution spectrum of Ag@MOF-76-Y that the 3d energy spectrum of Ag presents two obvious signal peaks at 374.4 and 368.4 eV. These two signal peaks can be clearly assigned to the Ag 3d3/2 and Ag 3d5/2 levels, respectively. The energy difference of ∼6.0 eV between the two signal peaks is a typical feature of Ag0. Therefore, the Ag+ that was adsorbed was successfully reduced to Ag0 nanoparticles through coordination and formed a stable composite structure. These results show that Y3+ maintains its original oxidation state, while part of Ag+ is reduced to Ag0. To comprehensively evaluate its stability, Ag@MOF-76-Y was directly immersed in a buffer solution (PBS), artificial wound fluid (AWF), and water. As shown in Figure c,d, the FTIR spectrum and PXRD pattern of Ag@MOF-76-Y after soaking are not significantly different from those of Ag@MOF-76-Y without soaking, indicating that Ag@MOF-76-Y can still maintain chemical stability and structural stability under these conditions.

As the main active substance of antibacterial dressings, the antibacterial activity of its additives against common bacteria needs further evaluation. Thus, the antibacterial activity of the synthesized composites was evaluated by in vitro antibacterial experiments with Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) as the research object. The OD600 values of S. aureus treated with MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y were monitored in a range of concentrations (0, 25, 50, and 100 μg/mL). As shown in the Figure a, MOF-76-Y had no obvious inhibitory effect at each concentration studied. It is worth noting that Ag@MOF-76-Y showed strong inhibition of S. aureus activity and showed a dependence on concentration: as the concentration increased, the antibacterial activity also enhanced (Figure b). In order to further evaluate the inhibitory effect of MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y on bacterial growth, quantitative analysis was conducted using the plate colony counting method. As shown in Figure , the Ag@MOF-76-Y group exhibited excellent antibacterial activity, and its performance was much better than that of MOF-76-Y. The clearance rate of S. aureus in the Ag@MOF-76-Y group was as high as 99.1% (Figure S3), while the bacterial survival rate in the MOF-76-Y group was still at a high level (85.1%). A calcein-AM/PI kit was used to carry out live/dead staining experiments to intuitively show the antibacterial effect. As shown in Figure , the confocal microscopy staining images shows negligible live bacteria (green) and a large number of dead bacteria (red) after Ag@MOF-76-Y processing. The strong red fluorescence predominated, indicating that bacteria were almost completely cleared after Ag@MOF-76-Y treatment. Under the same conditions, the images of the saline control group and MOF-76-Y group showed strong green fluorescence. The MOF-76-Y group showed only a few dead bacteria (red) and a large number of visible live bacteria (green). Furthermore, SEM images showed the morphological changes of bacteria after the above process. Specifically, the Staphylococcus aureus in the physiological saline control group and MOF-76-Y group still maintained their original smooth and intact spherical morphology (Figure a,b). The defect morphology of folds, cracks, and collapses of the Ag@MOF-76-Y treated bacteria indicates that the bacterial structure is significantly damaged, resulting in the inactivation of intracellular substances after leakage (Figure c). This is consistent with the above results. The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of MOF-76-Y and Ag@MOF-76-Y against S. aureus within the concentration range studied was evaluated. The results showed that compared with MOF-76-Y, the MIC of Ag@MOF-76-Y to S. aureus decreased significantly. This indicates that MOF-76-Y after silver loading exhibits significantly enhanced anti-S. aureus interactions, mainly due to the synergistic effect of rare earth Y and Ag in the MOF, which promotes the enhancement of antibacterial activity.

Bacteria usually form biofilms by secreting substances during the process of reproduction, providing a defense barrier for microorganisms and blocking the penetration of traditional antibacterial substances. This protective mechanism severely limits the ability of antibacterial substances to exert their activity and eventually leads to further deterioration of the wound. Based on the excellent antibacterial activity of Ag@MOF-76-Y, we further evaluated the antibiofilm effect of Ag@MOF-76-Y against S. aureus. As shown in Figure d–f, three-dimensional confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) after staining showed an intuitive inhibitory effect on S. aureus. Specifically, the control group and MOF-76-Y showed dense green fluorescence biofilm images. In sharp contrast, the biofilm treated with Ag@MOF-76-Y showed a significant loose fragmentation. At the same time, CLSM was used to perform live and dead cell staining analysis on the biofilms. The results showed that after treatment with Ag@MOF-76-Y, the survival rate of bacteria decreased significantly: the green signal almost disappeared, and an obvious red signal appeared. Overall, these results fully demonstrated that Ag@MOF-76-Y had significant effects on antibacterial activity and biofilm scavenging. The main reason is because Ag@MOF-76-Y destroys the cell membrane of Staphylococcus aureus, achieving high-efficiency antibacterial activity.

In addition to the destruction of the membrane structure, the framework structure of MOF-76-Y is considered to provide a good reaction container for the removal of microorganisms and the role of controllable release of active substances. According to the above experimental data, it can be considered that the bacteriostatic effect of MOF-76-Y itself is not outstanding, and it shows only weak bacteriostatic activity. Generally, catalyst-mediated oxidation is considered to be another common antibacterial pathway to increase the production rate of reactive oxygen species (ROS). However, the excessive production of ROS will damage cells and induce apoptosis and necrosis. The ROS activities of MOF-76-Y, Ag@MOF-76-Y, and silver nanoparticles were evaluated by the TMB (3,3′,5,5′-tetra­methyl­benz­idine) chromogenic method. As shown in Figure S4, silver nanoparticles exhibited extremely high ROS activity under simulated conditions, while Ag@MOF-76-Y exhibited only mild ROS activity. This is attributed to the regulated availability of Ag species by the framework structure of MOF-76-Y, which provides mild antibacterial activity to the wound in the process of wound recovery. It is worth noting that MOF-76-Y also exhibits weak ROS activity, which provides a direction for the application expansion of rare-earth-based MOFs. Here, the good antibacterial activity and suitable ROS production rate of Ag@MOF-76-Y originated from the synergy between yttrium-based MOFs and Ag, which provides a new strategy for the development of dressing fillers.

Although silver typically has an excellent antibacterial activity, high levels of silver nanoparticles may cause irreversible cytotoxic effects. Therefore, it is necessary to evaluate Ag@MOF-76-Y for its cellular compatibility in a wound. We evaluated the cell compatibility of L929 fibroblasts with different concentrations of MOF-76-Y, Ag nanoparticles, and Ag@MOF-76-Y. As shown in Figures S5–S8, MOF-76-Y exhibits negligible cytotoxicity within the tested concentration range. Although Ag nanoparticles showed a concentration-dependent decrease in cell survival rate, Ag@MOF-76-Y maintained a higher cell survival rate at the same concentrations, indicating improved cell compatibility. These results indicate that anchoring silver within the framework of MOF-76-Y can reduce or limit the toxic effects of free silver nanoparticles on cells.

Conclusion

In this study, a preparation method of a high-performance dressing filler based on Y material was proposed. Ag+ was adsorbed into the activated cavity of MOF-76-Y by supramolecular interactions, and then Ag+ was reduced to Ag0 by a reductant, which was stably anchored in the frame structure of MOF-76-Y to successfully synthesize Ag@MOF-76-Y. Compared with MOF-76-Y, Ag@MOF-76-Y showed excellent antibacterial activity. Antibacterial evaluation of the system showed that Ag@MOF-76-Y has a high inhibition rate of 99.1% on S. aureus, which is significantly higher than the antibacterial activity of MOF-76-Y. Further studies showed that Ag@MOF-76-Y exhibited a mild production rate of reactive oxygen species and an excellent bacterial removal rate, providing a new choice for the development of high-performance antibacterial dressings. From the mechanism of action, its excellent activity is attributed to the host–guest synergy between yttrium-based MOFs and Ag. This result is conducive to the research and development of a new generation of antibacterial dressings and the expansion of applications of rare-earth-based compounds.

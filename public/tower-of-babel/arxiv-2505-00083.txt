Abstract
AT 2019aalc is a peculiar sequence of highly variable emission events observed towards the nucleus of the broad-line AGN SDSS J152416.66+045119.0. The system exhibited two distinct UV/optical flares (the first detected in 2019, the second one in 2023). Spectra obtained following the detection of the second flare revealed prominent Bowen fluorescence and high-ionization coronal emission lines, which were much weaker, if at all detectable, in a spectrum taken following the first flare. We present and analyze a large set of multi-wavelength, multi-epoch data for this source, with particular emphasis on optical spectroscopic monitoring conducted with the Las Cumbres Observatory network. During the relatively slow dimming that followed the second optical flare, the UV/optical light-curve shows a sequence of minor rebrightening events, while the Bowen fluorescence and the coronal lines vary (roughly) in tandem with these “bumps” in the broad-band light-curve. Most of the observed behavior of AT 2019aalc links it to the growing class of Bowen fluorescence flares (BFFs) while setting it apart from canonical tidal disruption events. However, AT 2019aalc has some outstanding peculiarities, including two short flares seen in its soft X-ray light-curve during the dimming phase of the second optical flare, and which do not seem to be linked to the emission line variations. We discuss the optical and X-ray properties of the source and possible scenarios of the origin of the flare, in particular radiation pressure instabilities in the (pre-existing) AGN accretion disk.



I
Introduction

Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs), powered by accreting supermassive black holes (SMBHs), are thought to sustain their active phases over relatively long periods, of at least tens of thousands of years. During these prolonged accretion episodes, their multi-wavelength emission is known to show stochastic variability, typically of order
\approx
10
\%
over a wide range of timescales, specifically months-to-decades in the optical band
(e.g.,
Vanden Berk et al. 2004
;
MacLeod et al. 2012
;
Caplar et al. 2017
, and references therein)
.

Advances in time-domain astronomy allowed to study AGN variability for increasingly large samples and across increasingly short timescales. Thanks to wide surveys such as those enabled by the Catalina Real-time Transient Survey
(
Drake et al. 2009
, CRTS;)
, the Zwicky Transient Facility
(
Masci et al. 2019
, ZTF;)
and
the All-Sky Automated Survey for Supernovae
(
Shappee et al. 2014
, ASAS-SN;)
, it became possible to pursue follow-up studies of specific cases or samples of interestingly variable AGNs, using responsive spectroscopic and multi-wavelength facilities.
This helped to reveal several classes of surprisingly fast, dramatic flux changes related to AGNs and SMBH accretion. In those cases, AGN emission may vary by over an order of magnitude within a timescale of a few months, while in some extreme cases, the SMBH accretion activity appears to turn “on” or “off”.
Moreover, some of these types of extreme events of SMBH-related flux changes could present recurring phenomena, particularly recurring flares.

One of those classes is known as changing-look AGN
(CL-AGN; see review by
Ricci & Trakhtenbrot 2023
, and references therein)
. In the UV-optical regime, CL-AGNs show the (dis-)-appearance of broad emission lines and/or the blue, accretion-powered continuum emission, with corresponding flux changes of a factor of 2 or more, within timescales of a few years and perhaps even a few months
(
Zeltyn et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
. Recent years have seen a significant increase in the number of known CL-AGN, owing to dedicated searches in survey data
(
MacLeod et al. 2019
;
Graham et al. 2020
;
Green et al. 2022
;
Zeltyn et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2024
)
. In parallel, there has been a steady progress in the theoretical study of the physical mechanisms driving such unexpectedly dramatic variability events
(
Noda & Done 2018
;
Scepi et al. 2021
;
Li et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
.
Importantly, there are several reported cases of recurring CL-AGN spectral transitions. This includes both low-luminosity, local Seyfert galaxies (see, e.g.,
Kovačević et al. 2018
,
Oknyansky et al. 2019
, the compilation in
Wang et al. 2025
, and references therein), as well as higher-luminosity systems out to
z\sim 0.5

(
Zeltyn et al. 2022
;
Jana et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2025
)
. One possible explanation for recurring CL-AGN, put forward by
Śniegowska et al. 2020
, is based on radiation pressure instabilities in a narrow unstable region in a standard (thin) AGN accretion disk. This model was later extended by adding a magnetic field
(
Pan et al. 2021
)
and further focusing only on the inner parts of the disk
(
Śniegowska et al. 2023
)
.
Broadly speaking, the timescale for the recurring flares in such models is dictated by the size of the unstable region and the strength of the magnetic field.

Another main class of SMBH-related transients is driven by stellar tidal disruption events (TDEs; see, e.g.,
Gezari et al. 2012
;
Arcavi et al. 2014
, and the recent review by
Gezari 2021
).
Those TDEs identified and monitored in the UV-optical regime are typically characterized by a relatively fast (
\lesssim 2
months) rise to a single peak, followed by a decay that lasts a few months, as well as the presence of an extremely broad (
\gtrsim
10000 km s
-1
) He
ii

\lambda 4686
and/or H
\alpha
emission lines.
Some TDEs were also observed to have so-called “coronal” emission lines from highly ionized species
(
Short et al. 2023
;
Newsome et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. The two prevalent explanations for the origin of the transient UV-optical emission in such TDEs link it either to the reprocessing of X-ray radiation originating from the newly-formed, transient accretion disk
(
Guillochon et al. 2014
;
Roth et al. 2016
;
Dai et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
, or to shocks in the stellar debris streams
(
Piran et al. 2015
, e.g.,)
. Some simulations
(
Steinberg & Stone 2024
, e.g.,)
suggest that both mechanisms may coincide and dominate the emission in different stages during the TDE.
The tidal disruption of stars by SMBHs may also produce recurring transients. Specifically, a partial stellar disruption may give rise to repeating TDE-like flares–a scenario pursued both theoretically
(
Coughlin & Nixon 2019
, e.g.,)
and observationally
(
Somalwar et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.
Models suggest that such partial TDEs (pTDEs) should broadly evolve faster than the canonical, total disruption counterparts
(
Coughlin & Nixon 2019
)
.
The period between recurring flares in pTDEs is thought to be dictated by the mass distribution of the stellar debris and the orbital parameters of the system.

Recently, a new class of flaring events related to AGNs was defined, which are often referred to as “Bowen Fluorescence Flares”
(
Trakhtenbrot et al. 2019a
, BFFs;)
.
Their key defining feature is the presence of exceptionally strong, and broad (
\approx 2000
km s
-1
) Bowen emission lines, such as N
iii

\lambda 4640
and O
iii

\lambda 3133
, in addition to the He
ii

\lambda 4686
line that is more directly linked to the Bowen fluorescence mechanism
(
Bowen 1928
)
.
Such Bowen fluorescence (BF) lines are rarely seen in persistent, non-flaring AGNs
(
Vanden Berk et al. 2001
, e.g.,)
, despite long-lasting predictions that they should be observable
(
Netzer et al. 1985
)
.
The BF lines are thought to originate from high density (
n_{\rm H}>10^{9.5}\,{\rm cm}^{-3}
) and high metalicity gas, exposed to intense extreme UV radiation
(
Netzer et al. 1985
,
>
50 eV;)
.
Although BF lines are also detected in a few optically-identified TDEs
(
Holoien et al. 2016
;
Leloudas et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
, BFFs appear to be a distinct (observational) class, given their much slower declining light-curves, the absence of exceptionally broad He
ii

\lambda 4686
lines (with
\gtrsim
10000 km s
-1
), and the apparent preference to occur in already-active SMBHs (i.e., in AGNs;
Makrygianni et al. 2023
).
Essentially all the BFFs reported to date showed a significant rebrightening during their post-peak decline, and in some cases, there was indeed a secondary, albeit weaker flare, occurring roughly a year after the main UV-optical flare (see, e.g., Fig. 11 in
Makrygianni et al. 2023
).

Perhaps the most elusive class of (recurring) SMBH-related transients is that of X-ray quasi-periodic eruptions
(
Miniutti et al. 2019
;
Giustini et al. 2020
;
Arcodia et al. 2021
, QPEs; e.g.,)
. These systems exhibit high-magnitude, fast X-ray flares, recurring over timescales of hours, followed by prolonged periods of quiescence, and seem to occur in both previously active and inactive SMBHs. To date, no corresponding phenomena have been observed in the UV-optical regime.
Various models that try to explain QPEs have been put forward, focusing either on instabilities within an existing accretion flow, or on orbital phenomena.
The former class of models would require a rather low-luminosity accretion disk, given the lack of evidence for prior AGN-like emission in most QPEs
(
Raj & Nixon 2021
;
Metzger et al. 2022
)
, but could still be linked to the kind of instabilities suggested to explain recurring CL-AGN
(
Śniegowska et al. 2023
)
.
The second class of models focuses on extreme mass ratio in-spirals (EMRIs), involving at least one stellar-mass object (which may be a BH) orbiting the SMBH
(
Lu & Quataert 2023
)
. In these models, the quasi-periodicity is closely related to the orbital timescale.

All these types of potentially recurring SMBH-related phenomena may be inter-connected, either physically or even just observationally. For example, there have been reports of TDE candidates occurring in previously-known AGNs (e.g., PS16dtm;
Blanchard et al. 2017
;
Petrushevska et al. 2023
). Some “turn on” CL-AGN were suggested to be driven by a TDE (e.g., 1ES 1927+654
Trakhtenbrot et al. 2019b
;
Ricci et al. 2020
; or
Merloni et al. 2015
). Likewise, it has been claimed that at least some of the transients associated with the BFF class are in fact driven by a TDE (e.g., F01007–2237;
Tadhunter et al. 2017
;
Tadhunter et al. 2021
, in addition to the few optically-detected TDEs that exhibit both a broad He
ii

\lambda 4686
line and BF features
(
Leloudas et al. 2019
, e.g., AT 2019dyb,)
.
Moreover, there is evidence that at least some QPEs share some observational characteristics with TDEs
(
Arcodia et al. 2024
;
Wevers et al. 2024
)
. Finally, from a purely observational point of view, many of these SMBH-related transients share basic light-curve and/or spectral features (e.g., the aforementioned He
ii

\lambda 4686
emission in TDEs and BFFs), which makes it hard to tell them apart and throws some doubt on our ability to classify them into distinct types of phenomena.
Recurring rebrightening events, and indeed recurring flares, can serve as strong discriminants among both the various observational classes and the models that try to explain them.

As the number of time-domain surveys keeps growing, initiated by facilities like Vera Rubin Observatory,
Ivezić et al. 2019
, BlackGEM,
Groot et al. 2024
, the La Silla Schmidt Southern Survey
(
Miller et al. 2025
, LS4)

1
1

1

https://sites.northwestern.edu/ls4/
, or the upcoming ULTRASAT mission
(
Shvartzvald et al. 2024
)
, the more important it becomes to be able to identify the various types of SMBH-related transients and to find and understand the properties which they share and which set them apart. This can then pave the way to collecting large samples that, combined with novel models, will allow us to use them to gain insights about SMBH accretion physics and growth.

In this paper, we study the intriguing and recurring brightening events in the nucleus of the galaxy SDSS J152416.66+045119.0. The dramatic brightening of this source during 2019, dubbed AT 2019aalc, was considered to be related to a TDE as early as November 2021,

The recent study by
Milán Veres et al. 2024
presented several key datasets acquired for this source prior to August 2024, motivated by the coincidence of the first optical flare with a neutrino event (IC 191119A;

The data presented by
Milán Veres et al. 2024
, which included optical, infrared, and X-ray light-curves as well as several (low cadence) optical spectra and multi-band radio measurements, indicates AT 2019aalc can be classified as a BFF in an AGN, while also showing strong coronal emission lines and a (delayed) brightening of radio emission. They conclude that the events seen in AT 2019aalc are best explained by an enhanced accretion event in an AGN, where the intensified (extreme) UV emission is reprocessed by a pre-existing broad line region and a region of dusty gas, to give rise to the emission lines from highly ionized species, to the (delayed) brightening seen in the IR, and to the enhanced radio emission.

II
The source

The extreme variability event AT 2019aalc, also known as ZTF19aaejtoy, was identified in the center of the
z=0.03557
Seyfert 1 galaxy SDSS J152416.66+045119.0 (J1524+0451 hereafter), the optical image of which is shown in Figure
1
.
We present some of the available pre-flare photometry of J1524+0451 in Table
1
.
The source did not show any significant variability, beyond what is expected for an unobscured AGN, prior to a dramatic brightening in the center of that galaxy identified in early 2019, with a formal detection date of 2019 January 22, based on optical photometry obtained with the ZTF (but reported only later, in 2021;
Velzen 2021
).
The optical flux continued to rise through mid-2019, reaching a clear peak during June 2019, followed by a smooth decline that lasted through 2022. This motivated its early classification as a TDE candidate
(
van Velzen et al. 2024
)
.
Continued ZTF photometry then revealed a second significant rebrightening, starting in mid-May 2023 (formal detection date May 11), about four years after the first flare
Veres et al. 2023
. This second flare peaked in early July 2023.

In parallel with the identification of the second flare,
Guolo & Gezari 2023
reexamined the multi-wavelength and variability data available for the source and concluded that it is consistent with an unobscured AGN.

Pasham 2023
re-analyzed XMM data of the source obtained in 2021, i.e. during optical quiescence, and found the power-law photon index was
\Gamma\approx 2.6
, consistent with later Swift observations (during 2023), and favoring a variable, albeit very soft AGN.

An optical spectrum of the nucleus of J1524+0451 was obtained back in 2008 April 6, as part of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) legacy spectroscopic observations
(
Abazajian et al. 2009
)
. That spectrum shows clear, strong broad Balmer emission lines, characteristic of unobscured AGNs. Following the first optical flare, a Keck/LRIS
(
Oke et al. 1995
)
spectrum was obtained on 2021 July 7, i.e. during the long decline from the first flare peak. That spectrum did not show significant changes in emission line shapes and/or (relative) strengths compared to the SDSS one.

As mentioned in Section
I
, AT 2019aalc has been studied by
Milán Veres et al. 2024
based on a rich dataset including multi-epoch optical, UV, and IR photometry, optical spectroscopy, (soft) X-ray light-curves and spectral modeling, and multi-band radio observations—all obtained prior to August 2024.
The data and analysis presented by
Milán Veres et al. 2024
clearly show some of the main features of this source, including:
two major optical flares, with peaks separated by roughly 4 years;
enhanced and variable mid-IR emission with two peaks that lag the optical flares by several months;
the presence of strong Bowen fluorescence and coronal emission lines, not present either in the archival SDSS spectrum or in the post-first-flare Keck/LRIS one;
a transient and weak X-ray brightening that (slightly) preceded the peak second optical flare, and a more prominent X-ray flare occurring about 300 days after the second optical peak;
and enhanced and variable radio emission indicative of a newly formed outflow or jet structure.
Importantly, the rich data studied by
Milán Veres et al. 2024
showed no decisive evidence for the characteristics of typical UV-optically selected TDEs, namely extremely broad (
\gtrsim 10,000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
) He
ii

\lambda 4686
line emission and/or a
\sim t^{-5/3}
declining optical light-curve.
Instead, AT 2019aalc was interpreted as a BFF occurring in a pre-existing AGN, where the accretion-driven flare in the UV (and maybe the X-rays) is driving a dust echo and renewed jet or outflow. While the
Milán Veres et al. 2024
study was not conclusive about the origin of the accretion-related flare, a standard single TDE interpretation seems highly unlikely.

Interestingly, the first optical flare of AT 2019aalc was claimed to be associated with the high-energy neutrino event IC 191119A
(
IceCube Collaboration 2019
)
, detected
\sim
150 days after the peak of the first optical peak.
Indeed, several works have investigated the possible association of accretion-driven flares from SMBHs with neutrino events
Reusch 2023
;
Winter & Lunardini 2023
;
van Velzen et al. 2024
. Such associations would imply that flaring SMBH accretion is a significantly more efficient source of high-energy neutrinos compared with more luminous, but persistent, AGNs. This is particularly the case for AT 2019aalc, given its high implied neutrino fluence
(
Winter & Lunardini 2023
)
.

Following the 2023 rebrightening of the source, we have obtained an optical spectrum with the Las Cumbres Observatory robotic network of telescopes
(
Brown et al. 2013
)
on 2023 July 12. Similarly to the spectra presented by
Milán Veres et al. 2024
, this spectrum showed Bowen fluorescence lines, and other emission lines associated with highly ionized species, not seen prior to the second optical flare.

The active discussion of the nature of this source, and its rather exceptional observed properties among SMBH-related flares, motivated us to monitor the source more intensely. This intensive monitoring effort, mainly with relatively high-cadence optical spectroscopy and UV-optical imaging, is the main focus of the present paper. While some of the data we present throughout the rest of the present paper is similar (or indeed identical) to the data used in the
Milán Veres et al. 2024
study, we stress that (1) our analysis is completely independent, (2) our higher-cadence optical spectroscopy provides new and complementary insight into the nature of the system, and (3) we present more up-to-date data in terms of optical, UV, and IR photometry, and X-ray observations. On the other hand, our analysis leaves aside some spectral regimes and does not revisit all the data presented in the aforementioned studies.

III
Observations, Data, and Analysis

After the detection of the second optical flare in the nucleus of J1524+0451, we initiated a campaign of optical photometric and spectroscopic follow-up observations using the Las Cumbres Observatory network of telescopes, starting 2023 July 28, as well as lower-cadence space-based, optical-UV and X-ray monitoring with Swift.
We also collected publicly available photometric measurements and spectra from various optical, IR, and UV surveys.
Below we describe all the observations and data used for our analysis, as well as our basic analysis of these data and the key features seen in each dataset.

Optical photometry

(

III.2.4
Radio

The position of the source was observed at 1.4 GHz as part of the FIRST survey
Becker et al. 1995
. J1524+0451 is clearly detected with an integrated flux density of
S_{\rm p}=6.20\pm 0.14
mJy
(
Helfand et al. 2015
)
, which translates to a monochromatic radio luminosity of
\nu L_{\nu}({\rm 1.4\,GHz}){\simeq}2.6\times 10^{38}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
.
To assess the (relative) importance of the radio emission and better understand the source, we calculated the radio loudness parameter following
Kellermann et al. 1989
, obtaining
R\equiv f_{\nu}(1.4\,{\rm GHz})/f_{\nu}(g-{\rm band})=2.4
.
Thus, historically J1524+0451 was not a radio-loud AGN.
Moreover, the radio luminosity could be accounted for with relatively intense star formation in the host galaxy, at a rate of
\approx 10\,M_{\odot}\,{\rm yr}^{-1}
(using Eq. 1 in
Hopkins et al. 2003
).

Regardless of whether the (archival) radio emission is driven by the AGN or by star formation,
Milán Veres et al. 2024
showed that the
\sim
1.4 GHz radio emission remains rather constant during the second main optical flare, while there is significant brightening in the 18 GHz regime.
The
Milán Veres et al. 2024
study presents an elaborate analysis and interpretation of the radio data available for AT 2019aalc, including VLBI observations. The present work, on the other hand, presents no additional radio data, and we do not go any further in the interpretation of the radio nature of this source.

III.3.1
Spectral decomposition

We used the
PyQSOFit
package
(
Guo et al. 2018
)
to decompose all the optical spectra in our study.
In this procedure, the spectra are first corrected for foreground Galactic extinction and shifted to the rest-frame of the source.
To improve the continuum fitting near key emission lines, we separately modeled two spectral regions: 4000-5500 Å for the spectral region surrounding the H
\beta
emission line, and 6000-7000 Å for the spectral regime surrounding H
\alpha
.
In both regions, we model the accretion-powered continuum emission assuming a power-law model that is fit to several bands that are absent of strong emission lines, specifically narrow (
10-20
Å wide) bands around 4000, 4200, 4430, and 5100 Å (for the H
\beta
region), and around 6000, 6200 and 6800 Å (for the H
\alpha
region).
For modeling the blended iron emission features, we used the empirical Fe
ii
template
(
Boroson & Green 1992
)
available by default in
PyQSOFit
, but limited it to wavelengths shorter than 5050 Å.

All the emission lines, including both narrow and broad components (NCs and BCs, respectively) were modeled using Gaussian profiles.
The narrow lines include the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
\lambda\lambda
4959,5007, [N
ii
]
\lambda\lambda
6548,6583 and S
ii
\lambda\lambda
6718,6732 doublets, as well as the narrow components of H
\beta
and H
\alpha
, all of which were modeled with a single (narrow) Gaussian profile.
The narrow emission components in each spectral region were tied in width and shift (both in velocity space).
The broad components of the H
\beta
and H
\alpha
lines were modeled with two broad Gaussian profiles (each), while for the H
\gamma
and H
\delta
we used one broad Gaussian profile (each).
Additionally, we used two Gaussians to model the region between 4550 and 4800 Å, which includes the blended N
iii

\lambda 4640
and He
ii

\lambda 4686
lines, as well as a single Gaussian to model the coronal [Fe
x
]
\lambda 6375
line.

To properly fit the He
ii

\lambda 4686
, N
iii

\lambda 4640
, and certain coronal emission lines such as [Fe
x
]
\lambda 6375
and [Fe
xiv
]
\lambda 5303
(
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
, [Fe
x
], and [Fe
xiv
] hereafter) that are present in the spectra we consider here, we had to make some adjustments to our otherwise standard spectral decomposition procedure.
First, we had to limit the Fe
ii
template used within
PyQSOFit
to
\lambda<4800
Å  as there is very little iron emission present redwards of H
\beta
in this source.
Next, we adopted two approaches to be able to measure these lines.
In the first method, we modeled each of the lines with single Gaussians, by adding these profiles to the
PyQSOFit
configuration.
In the second method, we directly integrate the flux density of the continuum- and Fe
ii
-subtracted spectra, over the spectral regions that contain the emission lines under study, specifically
\lambda=4540-4800
Å for
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
,
4800-4900
Å for H
\beta
,
6330-6420
Å for [Fe
x
], and
5250-5350
Å for [Fe
xiv
].
We do not use this method for H
\alpha
, as the blended [N
ii
] lines would contaminate any such measurement.
The flux measurements from both methods are highly consistent with each other.

After setting up the fitting procedure(s), we first fit the archival SDSS spectrum, assuming that the ratio between the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
\lambda\lambda\,4959,5007
is 1:3
(
Dimitrijević et al. 2007
)
.
We used the best-fit narrow line model of this SDSS spectrum as a reference for all other spectra, alleviating some of the potential challenges of flux calibration and limited spectral resolution.
First, we use the flux of the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
line from this fit as a reference, so that we could normalize all the other spectra, thus essentially assuming that the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
line flux remains constant.
This assumption is justified even for AGN-driven narrow emission lines given the long light travel time to the line-emitting region and the long recombination and/or de-excitation timescales expected for such low-density gas.
Second, we use the ratio between the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
and narrow H
\beta
line fluxes derived from the best-fit model of the SDSS spectrum,
\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]\,\lambda 5007/{\rm H}\beta_{\rm NC}=5.35
, and fix it to this value when modeling all the other optical spectra.
Otherwise, the Keck/LRIS and Las Cumbres spectra were decomposed with the same choice of parameters as those used for decomposing the SDSS spectrum.

Given that the synthetic photometry obtained from the SDSS spectrum is consistent with the (asynchronous) SDSS photometry, and that there is no sign of drastic photometric variability over the period that covers the SDSS observations, hereafter we assume that the 2008 SDSS spectrum of the active nucleus of J1524+0451 represents a relatively stable period of accretion and nuclear activity, and can be used to derive a set of baseline, pre-flare(s) spectral measurements, as well as deduced key properties of the AGN and of the SMBH powering it, including BH mass.

IV
Results and Discussion

IV.1
Photometric evolution

IV.1.1
UV-optical light-curves

We first discuss the broad-band photometric measurements of AT 2019aalc taken during our campaign(s) and compare them with relevant archival (i.e., pre-flare) data.

We compare the UV measurements derived from the Swift/UVOT observations to those obtained by GALEX in May 2007. The uvm2 band of UVOT is the most similar to the archival GALEX NUV band.
6
6

6

The effective wavelengths of the GALEX NUV and the UVOT uvm2 bands are 2245 and 2304 Å, respectively.

The first UVOT observation in our campaign (MJD = 60118) yielded an apparent AB magnitude of
m_{uvm2}=16.16\pm 0.06
mag, corresponding to
\nu L_{\nu}(uvm2)=5.5\times 10^{43}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
.
This is
\approx
10 times brighter than the archival GALEX measurement of
m_{\rm NUV}
=
17.95\pm 0.04
mag, corresponding to
\nu L_{\nu}({\rm NUV})=6.5\times 10^{42}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
.
During the Swift monitoring campaign, the
uvm2
flux decreased by a factor of
\approx 2.75
(1.1 mag), over the period 2023 June 23 through 2024 September 18.

The apparent brightness of the source in the optical regime increased from the first ZTF detection, when it had
r=15.44\pm 0.10
and
g=16.07\pm 0.16
mag, to
r=15.12\pm 0.02
and
g=15.69\pm 0.02
mag during the peak of the first flare, and later to yet higher fluxes during the peak of the second flare, reaching
r=14.97\pm 0.02
and
g=15.48\pm 0.01
mag.
After subtracting the reference optical flux levels (i.e., those measured in the first ZTF visit), we obtain
\nu L_{\nu}(g)=1.2\times 10^{43}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
for the peak of the first flare, and
\nu L_{\nu}(g)=2.0\times 10^{43}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
for the peak of the second flare, which is brighter than the first flare by 67%. Therefore, while the rise during the first flare triggered the initial interest in this extremely variable source, the second flare appears to be even more significant, in terms of its radiative output.

The two main optical flares were followed by more subtle rebrightening episodes appearing during the long decline of the light curve, roughly 230 days after each of the main flares. In terms of observed, non reference-subtracted brightness, these secondary bumps reached
r=15.26\pm 0.01
and
g=15.87\pm 0.01
mag for the bump that followed the first main flare, and
r=15.09\pm 0.01
and
g=15.78\pm 0.01
mag for the second one.
Moreover, after mid-May 2024 (
\approx
300 days after the second major optical flare), the source experienced another, more minor rebrightening, reaching
r=15.15\pm 0.01
and
g=15.90\pm 0.01
mag.

To quantify the declining phases of the optical light-curves, and compare them with what is expected for various physical scenarios, we fit the flux light curves with a declining power-law model of the form

F\propto\left(\frac{t-t_{0}}{t_{0}}\right)^{-\alpha}\,\,,

(1)

where
\alpha
denotes the index of the declining power law (
\alpha>0
) and
t_{0}
denotes a reference time, which in principle should be relevant for the observed declining light-curves.
For these fits, we used the reference-subtracted ZTF fluxes in both the
g
and
r
bands, after omitting measurements with large errors (
\Delta m>0.05
mag in the original ZTF data) to create “clean”, reference-subtracted light-curves associated with each of the two main optical flares. These four light-curves (two flares, two bands each) were fit using the
Lmfit
package
(
Newville et al. 2016
)
.
Numerically, the fitting procedure may introduce a degeneracy between
\alpha
and
t_{0}
, as it would prefer to fit the data with brighter and earlier flare peaks (small
t_{0}
), followed by a steeper decline (larger
\alpha
), so that the observed slowly declining light-curves would correspond to late stages in the photometric evolution.
However, we consider it physically unjustified to allow the disruption times, encoded by
t_{0}
, to occur months or years before the observed optical flares.
Therefore, when fitting the optical light-curves we constrained
t_{0}
so that it does
not
precede the detection of each of the flares (see details in Appendix
A
).

The best-fitting power-law models and the data used for these fits are shown in Appendix
A
(Figure
13
and Table
4
).
The power-law indices we find are in the range
\alpha\approx\simeq 0.2-1
, with the fits to the first flare showing a somewhat faster decline (larger
\alpha
) than those of the second flare, and the fits where
t_{0}
is allowed to occur prior to the flare detections yielding larger
\alpha
than those where
t_{0}
is fixed to the flare detection times (as expected; see above).
In general, the power-law slopes we derive are significantly shallower than what is expected from the basic theory of TDEs
(
Rees 1988
,
\alpha=5/3
;)
, and are consistent only with the shallowest slopes found among observed samples of UV-optically selected TDE candidates (for which
\alpha\sim[-4]-[-0.7]
;
van Velzen et al. 2020
;
van Velzen et al. 2021b
).
Moreover, the slopes derived for AT 2019aalc are inconsistent with what is expected for a partial TDE
(
Coughlin & Nixon 2019
, pTDE,
\alpha=-9/4
;)
—an astrophysical system that may, in principle, give rise to recurring flares in galaxy nuclei.
Most importantly, single power-laws do not provide good fits to the observed light-curve(s) of AT 2019aalc. This is true for the smoothly-declining parts of the light-curves, and is further complicated by the numerous shorter-term bumps occurring during the prolonged declining phases.
The slow decline which cannot be well represented by a power-law, and the presence of bumps are, in fact, consistent with what is seen in BFFs
(
Makrygianni et al. 2023
, e.g.,)
.

IV.1.3
MIR light-curves

We next turn our attention to the MIR emission of from J1524+0451  which—given its AGN nature prior to the optical flares—is generally thought to be driven by hot dust.
Indeed, the average pre-flare MIR color of J1524+0451 was
W1-W2=0.56
mag (in the Vega system), which is consistent with what is seen in AGNs
(
Stern et al. 2012
, see Fig. 5 in)
.

The WISE MIR measurements show an increase of roughly 1 mag following the detection of the first main optical flare (MJD = 58521), reaching a peak MIR flux in both W1 and W2 bands in the measurements taken on MJD = 58885, that is
\approx
230 days after the peak of the first optical flare. The second MIR peak (MJD = 60510) reached a total observed MIR flux that is
\approx
1 mag brighter than the first one, and occurring
\approx
210 days after the peak of the second optical flare.
We stress again that the WISE cadence is very low (
\sim
6 months), and therefore a more precise estimate of the timing of the MIR peak is not available.
In the second MIR flare the flux increase, relative to the pre-brightening base level, was a factor of 1.75 stronger than in the first flare. This is in excellent agreement with the more luminous nature of the second optical flare compared with the first one (see above).
The
W1-W2
color during the first optical flare detection is around 0.59 mag, consistent with the typical pre-flare value (
\approx
0.56). After the optical flare detection,
W1-W2
increased and reached 0.83 mag within
\approx
600 days of the first optical peak. The MIR color then decreased, so that during the detection of the second optical flare, it was back at
W1-W2
= 0.52 mag, again consistent with the pre-flare value. During the second optical flare, it again increased, reaching 0.72 mag during the second MIR peak (
\approx
210 days after the second optical flare peak). For reference, we note that studies of MIR emission in AGNs have found that the higher the
W1-W2
color, the stronger its association with AGN activity. Specifically,
Stern et al. 2012
showed that 85% (95%) of the sources with
W1-W2\geq 0.7
(
\geq 0.8
) are AGNs.

The MIR light-curve and color evolution thus suggest that the MIR emission responds to the changes in the UV/optical emission, by reprocessing it in a region of dusty circumnuclear gas. An alternative scenario, where the extremely variable UV/optical emission was due to changes in the line-of-sight obscuration by such dusty gas
(
Zeltyn et al. 2022
;
Ricci & Trakhtenbrot 2023
, see discussion in, e.g.,)
, can be ruled out.
7
7

7

In such a case, the isotropically-emitted MIR radiation is not expected to vary.

To contextualize the timing of the MIR peaks, we first calculate the expected size of a dusty (toroidal) gas structure, following what is known in persistent, nearby AGNs.
From the reverberation-mapping based correlation between optical luminosity and the scale of the (hot) dust region reported by
(
Minezaki et al. 2019
, Eq. 3 therein)
, and the highest optical luminosities of AT 2019aalc (i.e. the two flare peaks), we find that the hottest dust should be located
\approx 90-115
light days from the SMBH. These scales are thought to represent the region where dust is sublimated by the incident UV/optical radiation originating from the central accretion flow.
Given that the MIR peak occurred
\approx
210-230 days after the optical flares, and that the MIR emission is indeed expected to be produced further in the dusty (toroidal) gas, we conclude that the enhanced MIR emission could be accounted for by a delayed, reprocessed UV-optical radiation emitted from the (perturbed) AGN accretion flow. This would also explain the persistent AGN-like MIR color of AT 2019aalcand the fact that the MIR color becomes more AGN-like during the periods of enhanced UV/optical emission.

IV.2.1
Archival spectrum and derived properties prior to the second flare

Regardless of any spectral decomposition, the broad H
\beta
and H
\alpha
emission lines are clearly visible in the pre-flare archival SDSS spectrum, which confirms the (unobscured) AGN nature of the galaxy prior to the dramatic brightening events. Other, fainter broad features include H
\gamma
and (rather weak) H
\delta
emission, as well as low intensity Fe
ii
emission, and noticeable emission from the high ionization He
ii

\lambda 4686
transition.
In terms of narrow lines, in addition to the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007

\lambda\lambda
4959,5007,
\left[{\rm N}\,\textsc{ii}\right]\,\lambda\lambda
6548,6583, and
{\rm S}\,\textsc{ii}\,\lambda\lambda
6718,6732 doublets that are commonly seen in AGNs, the SDSS spectrum also shows the
[\textrm{O\,{i}}]\,\lambda
6300 and
\textrm{He\,{i}}\ \lambda
5875 lines.

From the spectral decomposition of the archival SDSS spectrum, we obtain line widths of
{\rm FWHM}\left({\rm H}\beta\right)=3000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
and
{\rm FWHM}\left({\rm H}\alpha\right)=2700\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
for the broad components of the Balmer lines.
The narrow component of H
\beta
has
{\rm FWHM}=200\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, as does [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
(which is tied in width), while the narrow component of H
\alpha
has
{\rm FWHM}=300\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
.
We will use these narrow emission line widths to better understand the coronal lines of highly ionized iron that appeared during the second optical flare.
The decomposition of the Keck/LRIS spectrum, taken after AT 2019aalc has declined from the first flare, yielded
{\rm FWHM}\left({\rm H}\beta\right)=3000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
and
{\rm FWHM}\left({\rm H}\alpha\right)=2500\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, which are consistent with the pre-flare SDSS spectrum. While He
ii

\lambda 4686
is still present, there is no sign of BF emission features (namely N
iii

\lambda 4640
) at this stage. Moreover, Fe
ii
appears even weaker in this Keck spectrum (relative to, e.g., H
\beta
).

We derived key AGN and SMBH properties based on the best-fitting spectral decomposition of the archival SDSS spectrum.
The monochromatic luminosity at rest-frame 5100 Å
\lambda L_{\lambda}
(5100 Å) (or
L_{5100}
) measured from that spectrum is
L_{5100}=2.6\times 10^{43}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
.
To estimate the SMBH mass,
M_{\rm BH}
, we use two different single-epoch mass prescriptions. Firstly, we combine the
L_{5100}
and FWHM(H
\beta
) measurements through the prescription provided in
Trakhtenbrot & Netzer 2012
and obtain
\log(M_{\rm BH}/M_{\odot})=7.3
.
Second, we combine the luminosity of the broad H
\alpha
line,
L_{{\rm H}\alpha}=5\times 10^{41}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
, and the FWHM(H
\alpha
) measurement through the prescription of
Mejía-Restrepo et al. 2022
, and obtain
\log(M_{\rm BH}/M_{\odot})=7.1
.
These single-epoch
M_{\rm BH}
estimates carry significant systematic uncertainties, of order
0.3-0.5
dex
(
Shen 2013
, see, e.g.,)
. The two
M_{\rm BH}
estimates are thus in agreement with each other.

To estimate the (pre-flare) bolometric luminosity, we multiply the SDSS-based
L_{5100}
measurement by a bolometric correction of
k_{\rm bol}=8.8
, based on the approach presented in
Marconi et al. 2004
. We obtain
L_{\rm bol}=2.3\times 10^{44}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
. Combining this
L_{\rm bol}
estimate with the
L_{5100}
-based estimate of
M_{\rm BH}
, we thus estimate a pre-flare Eddington ratio of about
L/L_{\rm Edd}\equiv(L_{\rm bol}/\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1})/(1.5\times 10^{38}\,[M_{\rm BH}/M_{\odot}])\simeq 0.07
. Following the same approach, we obtain
L/L_{\rm Edd}
estimates from both the Keck spectrum which was taken between the two main optical flares, and the Las Cumbres spectrum taken during the peak of the second optical flare, and find
L/L_{\rm Edd}\simeq 0.07
and 0.15, respectively.
We conclude that between the two main optical flares, the SMBH was accreting at an Eddington ratio consistent with that of the more stable, pre-flare state, while during the peak of the brighter, second optical flare, its accretion rate was higher by a factor of
\approx 2
.
Despite the dramatic activity seen in AT 2019aalc, the accretion rates we estimate are consistent with what is known for persistent, low-redshift AGNs, for which
L/L_{\rm Edd}
is estimated through similar methods
(
Ananna et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

IV.2.2
Evolution of Balmer lines

All the optical spectra in our dataset, specifically those obtained with Las Cumbres, exhibit clear signatures of broad Balmer line emission.
In terms of changes in the width of the broad Balmer lines, we find that FWHM(H
\alpha
) varies between
2100-3700\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
and FWHM(H
\beta
) varies between
2400-3200\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
.
These line widths are much narrower than what is seen in TDEs
(
Charalampopoulos et al. 2022
, i.e.,
{\rm FWHM}>10,000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
; see, e.g.,)
.
We note that there is no evidence of any noticeable trends between changes in the FWHM of the Balmer lines and changes in the (broad-band) optical flux. In particular, the lines do not seem to become narrower when the continuum emission increases, which may be expected if the broad Balmer lines originate from larger BLR scales (i.e., “line breathing”; e.g.,
Peterson & Wandel 2000
). This is, however, seen in a non-negligible fraction of persistent broad-line AGNs for which adequate spectroscopic data is available
(
Wang et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
.

The strength of the broad H
\alpha
and H
\beta
lines in the post-first-flare Keck/LRIS spectrum is consistent with what is seen in the pre-flare SDSS spectrum. However, the first Las Cumbres spectrum we obtained after the detection of the second significant optical brightening (MJD = 60137) shows a significant increase in the strengths of these lines, which brightened by factors of
\approx 2\times
and
\approx 4\times
, for H
\alpha
and H
\beta
(respectively), compared with the pre-second-flare Keck/LRIS spectrum.
Indeed, the time evolution of all the broad Balmer lines seen in our Las Cumbres spectroscopy (H
\delta
, H
\gamma
, H
\beta
, and H
\alpha
) is consistent: the fluxes of all these lines increase and decrease in tandem with the broad-band optical flux, as can be seen in Figure
14
(in Appendix
B
).
We note that our measurements of the H
\delta
line are complicated by the possible blend with the BF
\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}\,\lambda\lambda 4097,4103
lines and the limited spectral resolution of the Las Cumbres spectra
(
Makrygianni et al. 2023
, see also)
.
In any case, the latest Las Cumbres spectrum among the dataset we analyze here, taken on 2024 September 3 (MJD = 60556) still shows enhanced Balmer line emission compared with the pre-flare state.

IV.2.3
Evolution of Bowen lines

Here we examine the temporal evolution of the He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
emission lines. These lines are related both physically, as the BF process is initiated by the Ly
\alpha
transition of the H
+
ion; and observationally, as the two lines are blended in our spectra.
We therefore only discuss the temporal evolution of the combined flux of the two BF-related lines,
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii})
hereafter.

As previously noted, the archival SDSS spectrum clearly shows some non-negligible
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission and our measurements indicate
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 0.29
or
\approx 0.44
during this pre-flare period (from the fitting or integration methods, respectively). The post-first-flare Keck spectrum exhibits a highly consistent strength,
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 0.30
(or 0.28 from the integration method).
To compare the
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission to what is typically seen in luminous, persistent broad-line AGNs, we note that the SDSS-based quasar composite spectrum calculated by
Vanden Berk et al. 2001
exhibits only
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)<0.05
, and essentially no N
iii
emission.

In our post-flare spectroscopy, the first Las Cumbres spectrum is taken around the time of the peak of the second optical flare (MJD = 60137), and we see a significant increase of the N
iii

\lambda 4640
BF line.
This is demonstrated in the left panel of Figure
7
, which focuses on the H
\beta
spectral region (including the
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
features) in the spectra taken during several key epochs in the evolution of AT 2019aalc.
Our measurements indicate
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 1.3
, which is comparable with the value measured for AT 2021loi (
\approx
1.1;
Makrygianni et al. 2023
).
Given the temporal gap between the Keck spectrum and this first Las Cumbres spectrum, we cannot determine when did the N
iii
line emerge.
We thus conclude that the
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission seen in the pre-flare (archival) state of AT 2019aalc was already higher by a factor of
\gtrsim
15
\times
compared with what is typically seen in persistent, broad line AGNs, and increased by a significant factor during the flaring activity.

The three middle panels of Figure
8
present the light-curve of
F({\rm H}\beta)
(2nd from bottom),
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})
(middle), and of the ratio
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)
(2nd from top), as derived from our Las Cumbres spectral series.
As can be clearly seen in Fig.
8
, the strength of both the broad H
\beta
line and the high-ionization, BF-related
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission complex shows rich temporal variations.
First, as noted above, the
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission is significantly enhanced during the peak of the (second) main optical flare. Because the H
\beta
is enhanced only after the flare peak, the relative strength is high, and indeed reaches the maximal value observed during our monitoring,
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\gtrsim 1.3
.
Then, in the spectrum taken near the first bump observed in the optical light-curve (MJD=60368), the H
\beta
and
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
features are enhanced again and indeed reach the highest fluxes observed during our spectral monitoring.
Although the
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)
ratio has also increased, the fact that the H
\beta
line emission has increased by
\approx 4\times
(see Section
IV.2.2
above), the line ratio is only mildly enhanced, reaching
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 0.88
.
We stress again that this peak line emission coincides with—by in fact somewhat predates—the peak optical
g
-band bump (MJD = 60369).
A qualitatively similar behavior is seen again in the spectrum taken just prior to the second bump in the optical light-curve (on MJD= 60444): both the H
\beta
and
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
spectral features complexes exhibit enhanced emission, reaching their highest fluxes during our entire spectral monitoring campaign.
The ratio between them reached
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 1
, which is the highest value observed during the decline of the light-curve after the (second) optical flare.

In terms of the width of the main BF-related He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
emission lines, our model fits indicate
{\rm FWHM}(\textrm{He}\,\textsc{ii}\,\lambda 4686)\simeq 2000-3000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
and
{\rm FWHM}(\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}\,\lambda 4640)\simeq 2000-6000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
during our Las Cumbres monitoring campaign. Due to the limited spectral resolution of the FLOYDS spectra, we decided to not report the individual best-fit values. We note however that during the rebrightening bumps seen in the UV/optical lightcurve, when the He
ii
and N
iii
features are stronger (see above) and better defined, their widths have a more limited range of
{\rm FWHM}\simeq 2500-3000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
(for both lines).
The widths of the BF-related He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
lines are therefore consistent with those of the broad components of the Balmer lines (H
\alpha
and H
\beta
). Both lines are significantly broader than the narrow emission lines (e.g., [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
), and the He
ii

\lambda 4686
line is–again–significantly narrower than what is seen in TDEs
(
Charalampopoulos et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.

Considering all these measurements from our high-cadence spectral monitoring, may conclude that the BF-related
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
features, which require EUV radiation reprocessed by a high-density, appear to originate from a region that coincides with the classical BLR of J1524+0451. That BLR has been in place well before the dramatic flaring activity labeled as AT 2019aalc.
Since the photon energy required to drive these features is much higher than that required for the Balmer recombination lines (
>
54.4 vs.
>
13.6 eV), we conclude that the SED of AT 2019aalc during the second optical flare peak was much harder than during the first bump, and the SED during the second bump was again harder than during the first one. Moreover, between the two main optical flares, the SED was comparable to that of the pre-flaring state, as suggested by the comparable strength of
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
in the Keck and SDSS spectra.

IV.2.4
Coronal line emission

All the optical spectra considered in this work, including the pre-flare (SDSS) and the post-first-flare (Keck) ones, show significant emission from the coronal [Fe
x
]
\lambda 6375
line ([Fe
x
] hereafter).
This is exemplified in the right panel of Figure
7
, which focuses on the H
\alpha
spectral region (including the [Fe
x
] feature) in the spectra taken during several key epochs in the evolution of AT 2019aalc.
In both the pre-flare (SDSS) and post-first-flare (Keck) spectra we measure
F(\textrm{[Fe}\,\textsc{x}])/F(\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]\,\lambda 5007))\approx 0.1
.
In these two spectra, we also detect (but don’t model) the fainter [Fe
vii
]
\lambda
6087 coronal line, however, we find no evidence for [Fe
xiv
]
\lambda 5303
emission.
Our Las Cumbres spectral monitoring during the second optical flare of AT 2019aalc reveals enhanced [Fe
x
] emission, as well as significant (and thus enhanced) [Fe
xiv
] emission.
In the first Las Cumbres spectrum, taken around the peak of the second optical peak (MJD = 60137), [Fe
x
] reached
F(\textrm{[Fe}\,\textsc{x}])/F(\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]\,\lambda 5007)\approx 1.34
, then slowly declining over a period of
\sim 50
days.
Then [Fe
x
] showed clear brightening, coincident with the bumps in the broad-band optical light-curve, reaching
F(\textrm{[Fe}\,\textsc{x}])/F(\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]\,\lambda 5007)\simeq 1.06
and 0.74 (for the spectra closest to the peaks of the first and second bumps, respectively).
Interestingly, a close examination of the various panels of Fig.
8
shows that the second local maximum of [Fe
x
] emission (MJD=60426) slightly predates the local peak of the
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission (MJD=60444), which itself slightly predates the peak of the second broad-band optical bump (MJD=60450).

The similarities between the light-curves of the [Fe
x
] line and the (blue) continuum emission suggest that the high ionization coronal lines are produced relatively close to the SMBH. To gain further insight into the possible location of the gas emitting the coronal line(s) in AT 2019aalc  we examine the line widths, recalling that in most AGNs the coronal lines are thought to be emitted in the inner parts of the narrow line region (NLR), or indeed between the NLR and the BLR (see, e.g.,
Mazzalay et al. 2010
;
Landt et al. 2015
;
den Brok et al. 2022
, or the recent review by
Rodríguez-Ardila & Cerqueira-Campos 2025
, and references therein).

In the pre-flare (SDSS) and between-flares (Keck) spectra, we measured
{\rm FWHM}(\textrm{[Fe}\,\textsc{x}])=1200
and 2000 km s
-1
, respectively. In the first Las Cumbres spectrum after the detection of the second optical flare, the width of [Fe
x
] was
\approx
2800 km s
-1
and the [Fe
xiv
] line had FWHM of
\approx
2500 km s
-1
.
All these coronal line widths are significantly broader than what we measured for the narrow [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
line, i.e.
{\rm FWHM}(\left[{\rm O}\,\textsc{iii}\right]\,\lambda 5007)\approx 200-300\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
in the higher-resolution SDSS and Keck spectra and
\lesssim
900 km s
-1
in the lower-resolution Las Cumbres spectroscopy.
8
8

8

Note we do not claim the [O
iii
]
\lambda 5007
line width has evolved during our spectroscopic monitoring.

The
Milán Veres et al. 2024
study of AT 2019aalc reports consistently broad coronal lines, with
{\rm FWHM}(\textrm{[Fe}\,\textsc{x}])\simeq 1200-1800\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, as well as a detection of the weak [S
xii
]
\lambda 7611
coronal line in a late-time spectrum (
\approx
310 days after the peak of the second optical flare), with
{\rm FWHM}\approx\,3400{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
.

The coronal line widths therefore appear to be consistent with those of the broad Balmer lines (
\sim
3000 km s
-1
), which suggests that the coronal lines seen in AT 2019aalc are emitted from a region that is located closer to the SMBH than the NLR, and perhaps indeed as close as the BLR.
Further evidence for this comes from the weeks-timescale variability observed in [Fe
x
], which is temporarily coincident both with the broad-band optical variability and with the variability of the BF-related
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
features, which are also driven by the variable, high-energy radiation of the central source.
The conclusion that the coronal lines are emitted from BLR-like scales is in agreement with what was found for several other SMBH-related flares exhibiting (transient) coronal line emission, including AT 2019avd
(
Malyali et al. 2021
, Fig. 12 in)
and AT  2022upj
(
Newsome et al. 2024
, Fig. 11 in)
.
On the other hand, several other systems associated with extreme and/or transient SMBH accretion showed only narrow coronal lines and were thus interpreted as originating from NLR-like scales
(
Komossa et al. 2008
;
Wang et al. 2012
;
Yang et al. 2013
, e.g.,)
.
Moreover, there seems to be no strong evidence for enhanced coronal line emission in persistent, yet highly accreting AGNs
(
Bierschenk et al. 2024
, i.e., systems with high
L/L_{\rm Edd}
; see, e.g.,)
.
Strong coronal lines with widths comparable with that of the BLR, such as the [Fe
x
]
\lambda 6375
and [Fe
xiv
]
\lambda 5303
lines we see in AT 2019aalc, thus remain a rather unusual property, which may be unique to BFFs.

If the coronal lines are indeed emitted from the same region as the broad Balmer and BF-related lines, and given the high ionization potentials needed to produce the coronal lines (i.e.,
\gtrsim
235 eV), then the temporal evolution of all the lines (Fig.
8
supports the following chain of events occurred during the second main flare of AT 2019aalc.
The near-peak radiation during the main flare itself had a particularly hard SED, reaching
>
235 eV and driving the significant brightening in the coronal and BF-related lines, while the emission from the lower-ionization H
+
(traced by H
\beta
; requiring
>
13.6 eV) was slightly delayed.
The SED in the first bump may have been similarly hard, although our observations did not cover the rise to the peak of the bump, and so could not disentangle the temporal evolution of the various ionization species.
The SED in the second bump may have been even harder, as we clearly see the emission from the highest-ionization species (Fe
{}^{+}9
, traced by [Fe
x
]
\lambda 6375
and requiring
>
235 eV) peaking well before the BF-related emission (He
+
, traced by
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
and requiring
>
54 eV) and the broad-band optical emission (ZTF
g-
band). In fact, the peak [Fe
x
] emission occurs during a local
minimum
in the
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission (in terms of both
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii})
and
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)
.
Our high-cadence spectral monitoring of AT 2019aalc thus shows that the SED of the (flaring) accretion flow varies, perhaps in a non-monotonic manner, during the main flare and the post-peak decline.
This further highlights the need for X-ray monitoring of such events, which we discuss in the next Section.

IV.3
X-ray behavior

We checked archival ROSAT data to assess the pre-flare X-ray emission from J1524+0451.
The source was not detected in the ROSAT all sky survey
(
Truemper 1993
)
, which places a
3\sigma
upper limit on the flux in the
0.1-2.4\,{\rm keV}
band of
F(0.1-2.4\,{\rm keV})\lesssim 10^{-13}\,{\rm erg\,cm}^{-2}\,{\rm s}^{-1}

(
Boller et al. 2016

, corresponding to a luminosity upper limit of
L(0.1-2.4\,\rm keV)\lesssim 2\times 10^{42}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
. Assuming the X-ray SED follows a power-law with a photon index of
\Gamma=1.8
, which is typical for persistent, low-redshift AGNs
(
Ricci et al. 2017
)
, we can extrapolate this to obtain an upper limit on the luminosity in the
2-10\,{\rm keV}
range, of
L(2-10\,\rm keV)\lesssim 2.5\times 10^{42}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
.
These upper limits are lower than what one could have expected to observe in AT 2019aalc, given the archival, pre-flare NUV emission. Specifically, the GALEX photometry translates to a monochromatic NUV luminosity at 2500 Å of
L_{\nu}(2500\,\AA)=5.0\times 10^{27}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}\,{\rm Hz}^{-1}
.
Using the
L_{\nu}(2500\,\AA)-L_{\nu}(2\,{\rm keV})
scaling relation from
Lusso & Risaliti 2016

2 keV would be
\nu L_{\nu}(2\,{\rm keV})
=

2-10\,{\rm keV}

L(2-10\,{\rm keV})=4.2\times 10^{42}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
, which is higher than the ROSAT upper limit by a factor of
>1.6\times

The broad-line AGN residing in the center of J1524+0451 was thus relatively UV-bright (or X-ray weak) well before the optical flaring activity. This is also consistent with the evidence for He
ii
and the Bowen N
iii
lines in its pre-flare SDSS spectrum.

We next performed a spectral analysis of the Swift/XRT data obtained after the detection of the second optical flare. Given the overall low count rate in the individual observations, the time-resolved XRT data were stacked in several ways.
Specifically, we examined the data obtained:
(1) during the first observation;
(2) close to the optical peak (MJD = 60138);
(3) in all the available XRT observations (up to MJD = 60575);
(4) before the X-ray flares (prior to MJD = 60400);

We used

The nature of the flare

In this Section we discuss the nature of the recurring brightening events observed in the nucleus of J1524+0451, comparing it to other main observed types of SMBH-related transient phenomena. To contextualize this discussion, in Figure
10
we show an optical spectrum of AT 2019aalc along with representative spectra of other relevant SMBH-related transients, all acquired about a month following the (brightest) optical peak: the canonical TDE PS1-10jh
(
Gezari et al. 2012
;
Charalampopoulos et al. 2022
)
10
10

10

The spectrum of PS1-10jh was downloaded from the Weizmann Interactive Supernova Data Repository (WISeREP;
Yaron & Gal-Yam 2012
).
and the archetypical BFF AT 2017bgt
(
Trakhtenbrot et al. 2019a
)
.
Note that AT 2019aalc cannot be considered a CL-AGN, as the broad emission lines seen in the spectra obtained during the past few years of dramatic variability were also seen in the archival SDSS spectrum taken back in 2008, and there is no data to support a drastic spectral transition in the X-ray regime.
We also recall that the recent
Milán Veres et al. 2024

As we discuss below, we broadly agree with the findings of
Milán Veres et al. 2024
.

V.1
Comparison to other BFFs

AT 2019aalc is a transient presenting Bowen fluorescence features and (at least) two major UV-optical flares.
We compare it to other Bowen fluorescence flares (BFFs), specifically, the event used to identify this class—AT  2017bgt
(
Trakhtenbrot et al. 2019a
)
, and the BFF AT 2021loi
(
Makrygianni et al. 2023
)
, as well as the other cases studied by
Tadhunter et al. 2017
,
Gromadzki et al. 2019
, and
Malyali et al. 2021
.

Figure
11
compares the optical light-curve of AT 2019aalc with some of these other BFFs (extending Fig. 11 in
Makrygianni et al. 2023
).
We first note that while several published BFFs show evidence for recurring brightening over timescales of about a year
11
11

11

See also the more recent study of the transient brightening event(s) observed in F01007–2237, by
Tadhunter et al. 2021
.
, the dual flares seen in AT 2019aalc are much more pronounced. In particular, none of the previously published BFFs had a second flare that is brighter than the first (“primary”) one.
Comparing the strength of the (brighter) UV flares, defined by the ratios of the near-peak UVOT/
uvm2
to the archival GALEX/NUV measurements, AT 2019aalc showed a relatively mild UV flux increase, by a factor of
\approx 10
, compared with
\approx 75
for AT 2017bgt and
\approx 20
for AT 2021loi. However, the optical flare strengths exhibit a conflicting trend, with AT 2019aalc increasing by a factor of
\approx 4
, AT  2017bgt by a factor of
\approx 1.5
, and AT 2021loi by a factor of
\approx 2
.
As for the post-peak decline of the UV-optical light-curves, Figure
11
demonstrates that BFFs tend to decline rather slowly. AT 2019aalc is the only one with additional major re-flaring during the decay.

In terms of spectroscopy, in both AT 2019aalc and AT 2021loi the archival, pre-flare optical spectra show signatures of enhanced pre-flare emission of He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
, compared to normal AGN.
For AT 2021loi, the strength of these features relative to H
\beta
is
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 0.6
, while for AT 2019aalc it is
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\approx 0.3-0.4
(for the SDSS and Keck spectra).
In F01007–2237, the He
ii
and N
iii
emission was also enhanced prior to the optical flare(s), however, that system lacked clear signs of broad Balmer line emission in the archival, pre-flare spectroscopy.
In post-flare spectroscopy for the BFFs AT 2021loi and AT 2017bgt, and for AT 2019aalc, we find several similar features, including
broad Balmer lines with
{\rm FWHM}\left({\rm H}\beta\right)\ \approx 2000-3000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, enhanced
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission, and the presence of coronal emission lines, such as [Fe
x
].
All these lines have BLR-like line widths, which means they originate from high-density gas located relatively close to the SMBH.
For AT 2019aalc, we demonstrated that the line-emitting region(s) are indeed close to the SMBH by the light-curves of the lines, which broadly resemble the broad-band optical light-cuves.
The relative strength of the BF-related
{\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii}
emission in AT 2019aalc is lower than what was found for some of the previously studied BFFs, with
F({\rm He}\,\textsc{ii}+\textrm{N}\,\textsc{iii})/F({\rm H}\beta)\gtrsim 1.4
near the peak of the (second) optical flare, compared with
\approx 1.1
, 0.9 and 0.5 (for AT 2021loi, OGLE17aaj, and AT 2017bgt, respectively).
The presence of relatively broad [Fe
x
] in AT 2019aalc is similar to other SMBH-related transients with BF emission, including the BFFs AT 2017bgt and AT 2021loi, as well as AT 2019avd
(
Malyali et al. 2021
)
. F01007–2237 exhibited post-flare [Fe
vii
]
\lambda
6087 broad emission
(
Tadhunter et al. 2021
)
.

In terms of its relatively weak X-ray emission, AT 2019aalc is again similar to the BFFs AT 2017bgt (which had
L(2-10\,{\rm keV})\approx 10^{42}\,\textrm{erg\,s}^{-1}
) and AT 2021loi (which had no X-ray emission detected).

In summary, AT 2019aalc provides further key evidence in support of several properties that seem to be common for (well observed) BFFs, including extreme UV flares, rebrightening after the first, main flare, shallow decrease of the optical and UV luminosity, appearance of broad coronal lines, and rather weak X-ray activity in comparison to normal AGN.

V.2
Relevance of TDEs

The first optical flare identified in J1524+0451 was classified as TDE candidate
(
van Velzen et al. 2024
, see, e.g.,)
.
This was justified by the coincidence of the transient source with the center of the host galaxy, its blue color
(
Milán Veres et al. 2024
)
and the broad emission feature that spectrally coincided with He
ii

\lambda 4686
—all of which are typical of TDE candidates identified in the UV-optical regime
(e.g.,
Gezari et al. 2012
;
Arcavi et al. 2014
;
Gezari 2021
;
van Velzen et al. 2021a
, “canonical TDEs” hereafter)
.

In terms of the optical light-curve behavior, we first note that the post-peak(s) decline phase(s) observed in AT 2019aalc are much slower than most canonical TDEs, and are only comparable to the shallowest (slowest) declines seen in such systems (e.g., Table 1 in
van Velzen et al. 2020
).
This is demonstrated by the light-curve of the optically-selected TDE PS1-10jh
(
Gezari et al. 2012
)
that is shown in Fig.
11
.

Moreover, the presence of two bright peaks and additional, milder bumps during the decline phase is also not typical for canonical TDEs.
While such recurring flaring activity is generally expected for (some configurations of) a pTDE
(
Coughlin & Nixon 2019
;
Zhong et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
, we stress that pTDE light-curves are expected to decay even faster than those of canonical (full) TDEs, making the observed light-curve of AT 2019aalc yet less compatible with a pTDE scenario.
The nuclear transient AT 2022dbl, which was interpreted as a repeated, partial TDE
(
Lin et al. 2024
)
, indeed presented two distinct (optical) flares, both of which are presented as part of Fig.
11
.
However, in that case, the first flare was brighter than the second one, while in AT 2019aalc the opposite is true: the second flare is brighter than the first one.
In the case of AT 2020vdq
(
Somalwar et al. 2023
)
, the second flare was brighter, as we see for AT 2019aalc  but the duration of the second event is significantly shorter (see Figure 1 in
Somalwar et al. 2023
).
Another source in which the optical light-curve showed a rich structure is AT 2021aeuk, which (similarly to AT 2019aalc) occurred in a previously-known AGN
(
Sun et al. 2025
)
. This transient showed three flares (the precursor flare and two main ones) within four years with visible bumps during the decay phase and was interpreted to be a repeating pTDE. Unlike AT 2019aalc, however, in this source, the first major flare is again brighter than the second one, and there are no signs of enhanced BF or coronal line emission.

Another scenario that was proposed to produce dual flares near SMBHs involves the tidal disruption of a binary star system
(
Mandel & Levin 2015
)
. Such a scenario is, however, unlikely to explain the recurring flares in AT 2019aalc, as the expected timescale between two peaks is only
\lesssim
150 day (assuming main-sequence binary stars), compared with the
\approx 4
years separating the two main optical flares observed for AT 2019aalc.

In terms of spectroscopy, we first note that the broad emission feature coincident with the He
ii

\lambda 4686
spectral region is not as broad in AT 2019aalc as it is in canonical TDEs (
\sim 3000
vs.
\gtrsim 10,000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
, respectively). Instead, it shows a clear double-peaked (or bimodal) nature, which can be accounted for by the blending of two lines, each with a width similar to that of the broad Balmer lines.
This is, again, one of the features that sets apart BFFs and canonical TDEs, although several TDEs are known to also show BF emission lines
(
Holoien et al. 2016
;
Leloudas et al. 2019
;
Charalampopoulos et al. 2022
)
.
Coronal lines may also be present in some other SMBH-related transients, including TDEs (e.g., AT 2019qiz;
Short et al. 2023
) and more ambiguous events (e.g., AT 2019avd;
Malyali et al. 2021
). In these cases, however, they are significantly narrower than what is observed in BFFs, suggesting the coronal lines are emitted further from the central (ionizing) engine, perhaps similarly to normal, persistent AGN with such emission lines.
Finally, we recall that AT 2019aalc occurred in a previously-known AGN with broad Balmer lines, which again differentiates it from canonical TDEs identified in inactive galaxies.
While TDEs may occur in, and/or interact with, pre-existing accretion disks
(
Chan et al. 2019
;
Ricci et al. 2020
, e.g.,)
,
we note that the number of such “TDE-in-AGN” candidate systems identified so far is a factor of a few-to-ten lower than what is expected from theory
(
Merloni et al. 2015
;
Kaur & Stone 2024
)
, suggesting that our (theoretical) understanding of such complex systems is still limited.

Another feature seen in some, but definitely not all canonical TDEs, is enhanced radio emission that may be detected with a significant delay after the optical peak
(
Alexander et al. 2020
;
Horesh et al. 2021
;
Sfaradi et al. 2022
;
Cendes et al. 2024
, e.g.,)
. Thus, late-time radio emission from this source would give us more clues about the nature of this source.
After the first optical flare of AT 2019aalc, its radio flux increased by a factor of 2 between two VLASS observations separated by
\approx
2 years
(
van Velzen et al. 2024
)
. The radio emission in the former post-flare VLASS observation was already somewhat higher than the archival, pre-flare FIRST measurement, but only by a factor of
\approx 1.5
or so. Moreover, AT 2019aalc showed a relatively constant radio flux during the second flare, with the appearance of 18 GHz emission
(
Milán Veres et al. 2024
)
.
Therefore, while AT 2019aalc shows some brightening in its radio emission, which may be interpreted as a newly launched outflow or jet
(
Milán Veres et al. 2024
, see detailed analysis in)
, its radio behavior differs from that of canonical TDEs.

V.3
Recurring flares in AGN disks

The recurring flares and bumps seen in AT 2019aalc, associated with the previously-known AGN J1524+0451, along with the quasi-simultaneous BF emission features that are driven by EUV continuum emission, motivate us to consider scenarios for recurring inabilities and/or disruptions in the inner parts of AGN accretion flows.

While the timescales of outbursts driven by radiation pressure instabilities in standard thin AGN disks are expected to be of the order of thousands of years, recent studies have shown that they may be shortened significantly, to even tens of years, if one modifies the disk models to include various viscosity laws and/or magnetic fields
(
Grzedzielski et al. 2017
;
Śniegowska et al. 2023
)
.
Moreover, setting the outer boundary radius of the unstable part of the accretion disk also allows for further shrinking of the typical outburst timescale.

To explore the relevance of radiation pressure instabilities in AGN disks to the events seen in AT 2019aalc, we used the GLADIS code

(

This one-dimensional model assumes a viscous torque proportional to the total pressure,

M_{\rm BH}=1.35\times 10^{7}\,M_{\odot}

based on the archival SDSS spectrum (the latter assumes a radiative efficiency of
\eta=0.1
). To be able to explore a range of model outcomes, we calculated a small grid of models with
\dot{M}_{\rm BH}=0.05

13

This inner part of the disk would emit the highest-energy (disk-related) radiation, and therefore is likely the most relevant for driving the BF (and coronal) line emission seen in AT 2019aalc.

Among the various calculated models, we find those with
\dot{M}_{\rm BH}=0.1

We stress that while this sort of AGN disk radiation instabilities model offers a possible avenue to explain recurring flares in AGNs, it also suffers from several important caveats.
First, the lower luminosity depicted by the model output (Fig.
12
) stems from the fact that the luminosities represent only the unstable part of the disk, i.e. the integrated radiation out to
R_{\rm out}
(30 or 50
R_{\rm Schw}
). Integrating over the entire disk would increase the luminosity but would act to dampen the apparent flares and bumps.
Second, the addition of magnetic fields could further reduce the typical timescales of the recurring flares, but on the other hand, an increasingly strong magnetic field would act to stabilize the disk and dampen the disk variability
(
Śniegowska et al. 2023
)
.
Third, the current version of the model cannot reproduce multi-wavelength properties, such as (soft) X-ray or radio emission.
Perhaps most importantly, radiation instabilities generally produce flares in which the rise time is drastically longer than the decline time (as indeed seen in Fig.
12
). This is the opposite of what is seen in AT 2019aalc and in most other AGN-or SMBH-related transients reported to date. It is possible that ongoing transient searches are biased against slowly brightening AGNs (with sudden post-peak drops), for which such models may be indeed more relevant.

Other possible types of instabilities, like those related to hydrogen ionization
(
Noda & Done 2018
)
, are generally expected to occur further out in the accretion flow
(
Janiuk & Czerny 2011
, see, e.g., Fig. 1 in)
, and thus the associated timescales are expected to be significantly longer (perhaps by orders of magnitudes; see, e.g.,
Hameury et al. 2009
).
A more detailed discussion of various models for accretion flow instabilities is beyond the scope of the present work.

VI
Conclusions

We presented multi-epoch observations of the transient and dramatic brightening event(s) known as AT 2019aalc  which occurred in the previously-known AGN SDSS J152416.66+045119.0  with two distinct main flares observed in 2019 and 2023. We focused on a spectroscopic time series showing enhanced Bowen fluorescence and coronal line emission, in addition to (partially public) UV and optical photometric data, and the most up-to-date X-ray and IR data. Our main findings are as follows:

1.

The transient and extreme variability seen in AT 2019aalc occurred in a previously-known, broad-line (unobscured) AGN, as determined from archival SDSS spectroscopy. That archival pre-flare spectrum already showed some mildly enhanced He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
line emission (see Section
IV.2.3
and Fig.
7
).

2.

During the first optical flare, the optical flux from the nucleus increased by a factor of
\approx 2\times
.
During the second optical flare, the optical flux increased by
\approx 6\times
, while the near-UV flux increased by a factor of
\approx 10\times
.
The optical and UV light-curves show milder rebrightening events occurring
\approx
230 days after both the first and second main flares (in 2019 and 2023; see Fig.
2
).

3.

The overall pace of dimming following the two main flares was relatively slow, specifically slower than what is expected for canonical (partial) TDEs. The decline is marginally consistent with some of the slowest-evolving TDE candidates observed to date (see Figure
11
).

4.

The WISE MIR light-curve also shows two clear flares following the optical ones, with delays of 210-230 days.
This suggests that the MIR emission rises from reprocession of UV/optical radiation by dusty circumnuclear gas, the presence of which is also supported by AGN-like MIR color of AT 2019aalc (see Figure
2
and Section
IV.1
).

5.

After the second flaring event, we observe enhanced, relatively broad (
\approx 2000-3000\,{\rm km\,s}^{-1}
) line emission from the He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
transitions, characteristic of Bowen fluorescence (BF). The BF emission generally follows the same trends as seen in the optical-UV photometric light-curve, but in fact slightly precedes it (Fig.
8
). This is consistent with what is expected if the BF features are driven by the EUV emission originating from the innermost parts of an accretion flow, while the NUV-optical continuum emerges from larger scales in the disk. This is the first SMBH-related BF flare (BFF) where such detailed temporal information is available (see Section
IV.2.2
).

6.

We report the presence of relatively broad coronal lines, including [Fe
xiv
], [Fe
x
] and [Fe
vii
]
\lambda
6087.
The [Fe
x
] line is significantly broader than the narrow forbidden lines, reaching FWHM
\approx
2800 km s
-1
.
This provides further evidence for significantly enhanced EUV emission from the innermost parts of the accretion flow being reprocessed by BLR-scale, high-density gas (see Section
IV.2.4
).

7.

The transient showed two dramatic X-ray flares during the prolonged dimming phase of the second optical flare (MJD = 60443 and MJD = 60551). The accumulated Swift/XRT data suggests a SED that is much softer than what is seen in most AGNs, with
\Gamma\gtrsim 2.5
. Overall, AT 2019aalc shows a lower X-ray emission than what is expected from UV–to–X-ray relation of normal AGN, even during its dual X-ray flares. Indeed, during the X-ray flares, the UV luminosity was nearly constant (see Section
IV.3
.)

8.

The strong and broad (
\approx
2000 km s
-1
) He
ii

\lambda 4686
and N
iii

\lambda 4640
lines, the slowly declining UV/optical light-curve, the recurring brightening events, and the UV brightness (and/or X-ray weakness) are all in general agreement with what is seen in other BFFs reported so far (see Section
V.1
). In contrast, the light-curve and the spectral features of AT 2019aalc are markedly inconsistent with those of canonical, optically selected TDEs, and even with the expected class of repeating TDEs.

9.

We considered the possibility that the recurring flares of AT 2019aalc could be explained by radiation pressure instabilities in the inner part of the accretion disk. While we were able to obtain some properties of the event (the required timescales and bumpy structure), we did not cover all of them (for example we did not obtain fast rise/slow-decay shape of the modeled flares) (see Figure
12
and Section
V.3
).

The basic association of AT 2019aalc with a BFF driven by a flare from an enhanced accretion event in a previously known AGN is in line with the findings of
Milán Veres et al. 2024
, who studied a rich collection of multi-epoch and multi-wavelength data. Our study further highlights the temporal behavior of the Bowen and coronal emission lines and follows the declining phase of the second major flare, including the minor rebrightening events.

AT 2019aalc joins the growing sample of known BFFs, and indeed shares many of their familiar properties. Moreover, together with AT 2021loi
(
Makrygianni et al. 2023
)
it adds to the evidence that BFFs with such properties occur in already-active SMBHs (i.e., in AGNs). Thanks to our intensive spectral monitoring, we were able to track the variable BF and coronal line intensity, providing the best opportunity to date to link these high-ionization features to the EUV emission which likely originates at the heart of the highly variable accretion flow, but which is otherwise inaccessible.
AT 2019aalc also showed unique peculiarities, such as the dual optical/UV flare, the recurring milder bumps in the UV/optical light-curve during the dimming phase of the second flare, and the dual X-ray flares (which have no corresponding UV/optical variability).

The underlying cause for the dramatic events seen in AT 2019aalc remains unclear, as it is still challenging to associate it with a particular type of physical mechanism.
Given the rich data collected for this source
(
Veres et al. 2023
;
Milán Veres et al. 2024
)
, and its unique multi-wavelength properties (including the associated radio brightening and neutrino event
(
Reusch 2023
;
van Velzen et al. 2024
)
), it may very well turn out to be a testbed for some of the models put forward to explain SMBH-related transients, particularly in already-active SMBHs.

The relatively short-term spectral and multi-wavelength changes seen in AT 2019aalc, particularly once the initial optical flare has dimmed, underscore the need for continued, high-cadence, and multi-wavelength monitoring of SMBH-related transients. We are indeed continuing our monitoring campaign of AT 2019aalc, for whatever additional surprises and insights it may provide.

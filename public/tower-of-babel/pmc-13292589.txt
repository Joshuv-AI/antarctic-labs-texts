Letter to editors regarding, "Phytogenic feed additive mitigates necrotic enteritis-associated gut damage and performance loss in broilers, article 106592″
Authors: Heskmatollah Khosravinia
Published: 2026

Dear editor,

I am writing to you regarding the article titled "Phytogenic feed additive mitigates necrotic enteritis-associated gut damage and performance loss in broilers " by Khairunnesa et al. (2026). In this study, the efficacy of a phytogenic blend (PHB) containing thymol, oleic acid, capric acid, and refined sunflower fat in mitigating NE-associated compromise in performance, gut health, liveability, oxidative, stress, and excreta biomarkers of intestinal inflammation were investigated using 450 Cobb 500 d-old chicks. According to the findings, it was reported that NE-related mortality and inflammation markers in excreta, such as calprotectin, fibronectin, and lipocalin-2, were significantly higher (P < 0.05) in NE-challenged birds compared to the NC group. Supplementation with PHB shifted liveability, and excreta calprotectin and fibronectin values towards the NC group (P > 0.05).

Inspired by this part of the study, it has to be reminded that the non-invasive biomarkers provide a new approach in monitoring incidence and recurrence of many diseases as they provide real time information about the tissue and cell injury as well as organ damage and failure imposed by pathological processes (Aryutova et al., 2021). However, as far as no standard or experimentally established cut off values for such biomarkers, including those implemented in this study, are available in poultry research, interpretation of the relevant data may undermine their importance and application. I suggest that the data on biomarker has to be reconsidered by calculating standard deviation for each parameter across all data groups. Then, the individual birds with a biomarker value 2 SD below the population mean for each group have to be identified. Finally, the number of the birds with a biomarker value 2 SD below the mean can be subjected to analysis of frequency (Ahmad et al., 2023).

Moreover, with respect to the treatments, it was much more dependable to conduct the study in a 2 × 2 factorial arrangement with 8 replicates each instead of three treatments with 10 replicates. A control group receiving PHB is actually missing.

In conclusion, as a senior researcher interested in both phytogenic feed additives in poultry nutrition as well as the approaches to minimize fail to thrive (FTT) in young broiler chicks, I would like to express my interest in collaborating with related researchers on scientific investigations aimed at understanding the etiologies bring about FTT in broiler chicken such as necrotic enteritis-associated gut damage.

Lastly, I would like to thank Most Khairunnesa et al. for inspiring this study.

Sincerely yours,

H. Khosravinia

GOUSSAROV-POLYAK-VIRO TYPE FORMULAS FOR ( 4 ​ k − 1 ) (4k-1) -DIMENSIONAL KNOTS AND LINKS IN ℝ 6 ​ k \mathbb{R}^{6k}
NEETI GAUNIYAL AND VICTOR TURCHIN

Abstract
We produce combinatorial formulas for invariants of smooth embeddings of ( 2 ​ ℓ − 1 ) (2\ell-1) -spheres into ℝ 3 ​ ℓ \mathbb{R}^{3\ell} for ℓ ≥ 2 \ell\geq 2 . Furthermore, we obtain such a formula for the Haefliger invariant, which classifies smooth knots S 4 ​ k − 1 ↪ ℝ 6 ​ k S^{4k-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{6k} up to isotopy. Our approach is similar in spirit to the work of Goussarov, Polyak, and Viro expressing finite-type invariants of classical knots in terms of Gauss diagrams. We similarly project higher dimensional knots and links onto a hyperplane and study the preimages of the sets of double and singular points in the embedded spheres. As an auxiliary result, we show that the space of n n -dimensional braids with k k strands in ℝ n + q \mathbb{R}^{n+q} is a homotopy retract of the space of long links ⊔ 𝑘 ​ ℝ n ↪ ℝ n + q \underset{k}{\sqcup}\mathbb{R}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+q} for q ≥ 3 q\geq 3 , thus proving a conjecture of Komendarczyk, Koytcheff and Volić.

The set of double points and the cobordism under isotopy

Throughout this section, we assume
M
to be a closed oriented possibly disconnected manifold of dimension
2\ell-1
. By
[
27
, Theorem 4.7.6]
, a generic smooth map
f:M^{2\ell-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\ell-1}
is an embedding except at double points, forming an
(\ell-1)
-submanifold
L(f)
, and singular points, forming an
(\ell-2)
-submanifold
\Sigma^{1}(f)
. Locally near
\Sigma^{1}(f)
,
f
is given by

y_{1}=\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2},\hskip 8.5359pty_{j}=x_{j}\hskip 5.69046pt(2\leq j\leq 2\ell-1),\hskip 8.5359pty_{i+2\ell-2}=x_{1}x_{i}\hskip 5.69046pt(2\leq i\leq\ell+1),

where the rank of the differential drops by one. Such singular points are known as
Whitney umbrella points
. The closure
\overline{L(f)}=L(f)\cup\Sigma^{1}(f)
forms a smooth closed submanifold with involution in
M
, where
\Sigma^{1}(f)
represents the fixed points. Its image
f(\overline{L(f)})
is an
(\ell-1)
-submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{3\ell-1}
with boundary
f(\Sigma^{1}(f))
.

If
f
arises from a generic projection of an embedding
M^{2\ell-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3\ell}

L(f)=L_{+}(f)\cup L_{-}(f),

where

\overline{L_{\pm}(f)}=L_{\pm}(f)\cup\Sigma^{1}(f)

.
Consequently,
\overline{L(f)}
is the double of

\overline{L(f)}

Recall that the signs on the chords in the Gauss diagrams correspond to the orientation of the

When the codimension of

f:M^{4k-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{6k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}

Proposition 3.1
.

For

,

\pi_{0}Emb(\underset{k}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})=\underset{i=1}{\overset{k}{\bigoplus}}\Big(\underset{\binom{k}{i}}{\oplus}\pi^{br}_{0}Emb(\underset{i}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})\Big)

Proof.

Consider the homomorphism

, and we get the following splitting

\pi_{0}Emb(\underset{k}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})=\Big(\underset{i=1}{\overset{k}{\bigoplus}}\pi_{0}Emb(S_{i}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})\Big)\oplus\pi_{0}^{U}Emb(\underset{k}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q}).

(3.3)

We can further continue the splitting in (
3.3
) as follows. For any pair

, we consider a map

\pi_{0}^{U}Emb(\underset{k}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})\rightarrow\pi_{0}^{U}Emb(\underset{2}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})=\pi_{0}^{br}Emb(\underset{2}{\sqcup}S^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})

i
-th and

-th components. There are

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

=lk_{\mathbb{R}^{3\ell-1}}(Z,X\cap Y)=lk_{\mathbb{R}^{3\ell-1}}(X,Y\cap Z)=lk_{\mathbb{R}^{3\ell-1}}(Y,Z\cap X).

(3.7)

where each

,

(we use the Ekholm orientation when

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we prove that

lk_{X}(L_{XY},L_{XZ})=(-1)^{\ell}lk_{X}(L_{XZ},L_{XY})=lk_{\mathbb{R}^{3\ell-1}}(Z,X\cap Y).

By definition,

is the algebraic intersection number

of

is an
\ell

For

4
Formula for the Haefliger invariant

We now present a combinatorial formula for the Haefliger invariant of embeddings
S^{4k-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{6k}
whose projections to
\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
are generic immersions. Recall that the isotopy classes of smooth embeddings of
S^{4k-1}
into
\mathbb{R}^{6k}
are in one-to-one correspondence with the integers via the Haefliger invariant (see
[
10
]
and
[
11
, §5.16 and Corollary 8.14]
):

\mathcal{H}:\pi_{0}Emb(S^{4k-1},\mathbb{R}^{6k})\xrightarrow{\approx}\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover, it is known that a nonzero multiple (two, when
k=1
) of the Haefliger trefoil knot, the generator of
\pi_{0}Emb(S^{4k-1},\mathbb{R}^{6k})
, can be isotoped to an embedding whose projection to
\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
is still an embedding (but non-trivial as immersion in
\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
), see
[
11
, Theorem 5.17 and Corollary 6.7]
.
Immersions
S^{4k-1}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
have been extensively studied in
[
5
,
6
,
4
]
.

4.1
Invariants of immersions
S^{4k-1}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}

According to Smale
[
22
]
, there is a bijection between the set (group) of regular homotopy classes of immersions
S^{4k-1}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
and the elements of the group
\pi_{4k-1}V_{6k-1,4k-1}
, where
V_{6k-1,4k-1}
denotes the Stiefel manifold of
(4k-1)
-frames in
(6k-1)
-space. Given an immersion
f:S^{4k-1}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
, let
\Omega(f)\in\pi_{4k-1}V_{6k-1,4k-1}
denote its Smale invariant. In these dimensions, it is easier to understand
\Omega(f)
rationally. Consider the fiber sequence
S^{2k}\rightarrow V_{6k-1,4k-1}\rightarrow V_{6k-1,4k-2}
, which induces a rational isomorphism between
\pi_{4k-1}S^{2k}
and
\pi_{4k-1}V_{6k-1,4k-1}
. Thus, any immersion

can be related to the rational generator
[id,id]
of
\pi_{4k-1}S^{2k}
, that is,
\Omega(f)=\Omega_{S^{2k}}(f)\cdot[id,id]\in\pi_{4k-1}V_{6k-1,4k-1}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
for some
\Omega_{S^{2k}}(f)\in\mathbb{Q}
.

In order to understand the relation between the
Smale invariant and the set of double points
of genereric immersions
S^{4k-1}\looparrowright\mathbb{R}^{6k-1}
, Ekholm
[
5
,
6
]
introduced a

[
4
]
.
Let

be a generic immersion with the
(2k-1)
-dimensional self-intersection

, where

is the preimage of the double points. The map

is a
2
-fold covering. Since the codimension of

is even, we fix an orientation on

(and thus on

) to be the Ekholm orientation, see Subsection

. Let

denote the

in

, and let

denote its restriction to

. Since the inclusion

is null-homotopic, the normal bundle

must be trivial:

and this trivialization is uniquely determined up to homotopy (since

for

). Note that

is naturally identified with the
2k
-dimensional normal bundle

over

in

pulled back along the involution. Let

be the framing of this homotopically unique trivialization of

. For each path component

, the set of non-vanishing sections over

is in one-to-one correspondence with the homotopy classes of maps from

\pi_{0}\Gamma(N(L_{i})\backslash L_{i})=[L_{i}^{2k-1},S^{2k-1}]=\mathbb{Z}.

Hence, we may choose a non-zero normal vector field

(4.1)

Since

(4.2)

Define a vector field

where the linking number is computed in

4.2
The formula

For computational purposes, it is easier to work with long immersions and embeddings, so we state our formula in that case. However, recall that
\pi_{0}Emb(S^{4k-1},\mathbb{R}^{6k})=\pi_{0}Emb_{\partial}(\mathbb{R}^{4k-1},\mathbb{R}^{6k})

Theorem 4.1
.

.

We prove a stronger statement that it

Theorem A.1
.

For

,

is a homotopy retract of

Proof.

By

3

\Omega C(k,\mathbb{R}^{q})\rightarrow\underset{i=1}{\overset{k-1}{\prod}}\Omega(\underset{i}{\bigvee}S^{q-1})

obtained as follows.
Consider the natural map

which forgets the last point in the configuration. This gives rise to a fibration

\underset{k-1}{\bigvee}S^{q-1}\rightarrow C(k,\mathbb{R}^{q})\rightarrow C(k-1,\mathbb{R}^{q}),

\Omega C(k,\mathbb{R}^{q})\simeq\Omega C(k-1,\mathbb{R}^{q})\times\Omega(\underset{k-1}{\bigvee}S^{q-1}).

To be precise, one uses the H-space structure on

\Omega C(k-1,\mathbb{R}^{q})\times\Omega(\underset{k-1}{\bigvee}S^{q-1})\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Omega C(k,\mathbb{R}^{q}).

From (

), we get

\Omega^{n}C(k,\mathbb{R}^{q})\simeq\underset{i=1}{\overset{k-1}{\prod}}\Omega^{n}(\underset{i}{\bigvee}S^{q-1}).

(A.2)

with a section given by inserting a new, disjoint trivial strand in
\mathbb{R}^{n+q}

Emb_{\partial}(\underset{k}{\sqcup}\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q})\simeq\underset{i=0}{\overset{k-1}{\prod}}Emb_{\partial}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n+q}\backslash(\underset{i}{\sqcup}\mathbb{R}^{n})).

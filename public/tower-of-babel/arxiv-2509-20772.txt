Super-Solid phase in a U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) symmetric S = 1 S=1 Magnet on the Triangular Lattice
Si-Cheng Wang, Zheng-Xin Liu

Abstract
A spin supersolid is characterized by the simultaneous breaking of lattice translation and continuous spin rotation symmetries. In this work, we study a spin-1 model with U ⁡ ( 2 ) ≅ S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) / Z 2 U(2)\cong SU(2)\times U(1)/Z_{2} symmetry on the triangular lattice, and the phase diagram is figured out using a variational ℂ ​ P 2 \mathbb{C}P^{2} approach. We identify a novel S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) -supersolid phase which contains a 3-sublattice solid order and a spin-superfluid order. Unlike usual supersolid phases having noncollinear magnetic order and only one Goldstone mode, the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) -supersolid phase has collinear Neel order and two Goldstone modes. Another important feature of this supersolid is that the magnon excitation spectrum has symmetry protected double degeneracy in the whole Brillouin zone. As by-products, several other ordered phases are obtained, including the ferromagnetic and the antiferromagnetic states breaking the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) symmetry, as well as genuine phases that completely breaks the U ⁡ ( 2 ) U(2) symmetry. Furthermore, the instabilities of S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) -flavor linear spin-wave theory are consistent with the phase boundaries between different ordered phases.



I
INTRODUCTION

The concept of supersolidity has attracted considerable interest in condensed matter physics since its proposal in 1970
Leggett 1970
. A supersolid is an exotic phase of matter characterized by the simultaneous coexistence of Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC) and density wave order, spontaneously breaking both the
U(1)
particle number conservation symmetry and continuous translation symmetry. However, the existence of such a phase in continuum Bose gases remained controversial for many years
Kim and Chan 2004
;
Kim and Chan 2012
;
Boninsegni and Prokof’ev 2012
.

Later, it was proposed that the lattice version of super-solid phase can indeed arise for hard-core bosons (bosons having infinite on-site repulsion) in the extended Hubbard model on frustrated lattice with nearest-neighbor interactions
Murthy et al. 1997
. Since the hard core Boson model can be exactly mapped into a spin-1/2 XXZ system, the resultant super-solid phase is essentially a spin super-solid, namely, a magnetic phase with the emergence of spin-superfluid order (i.e. in-plane magnetic order) simultaneous breaking of the original lattice translation symmetry (characterized by enlarged unit cells). This senario for realizing super-solids in hard-core bosons or spin systems was first illustrated via mean field theory
Murthy et al. 1997
and later verified by a series of numerical studies
Heidarian and Damle 2005
;
Melko et al. 2005
;
Wessel and Troyer 2005
;
Boninsegni and Prokof’ev 2005
;
Wang et al. 2009a
;
Jiang et al. 2009
;
Wang et al. 2009b
;
Heidarian and Paramekanti 2010
;
Yamamoto et al. 2014
;
Yamamoto et al. 2015
;
Sellmann et al. 2015
;
Yamamoto et al. 2019
. The spin-super solid order enriches the family of magnetic phases of matter and has attracted the interest of the experimental studies. Recently, progresses have been made in the realization of the spin super-solid phases in the material Na
2
BaCo(PO
{}_{4})_{2}

Gao et al. 2022
;
Xiang et al. 2024
.

On the other hand, magnets carrying spin larger than
1/2
can also support exotic phases of matter. For instance, many quantum phases exist in spin-1 systems, such as the Haldane chain
Haldane 1983
;
Haldane 1983
;
Buyers et al. 1986
and general symmetry protected topological phases
Gu and Wen 2009
;
Pollmann et al. 2010
;
Chen et al. 2013
;
Chen et al. 2011
;
Liu et al. 2011
;
Liu et al. 2012
;
Liu et al. 2014
, chiral spin liquids
Greiter and Thomale 2009
;
Liu et al. 2018
;
Shi et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2025
, and so on. Like the mapping between spin-1/2 systems and hard-core bosons, a spin-1 magnet can also be considered as a bosonic sysem in which the hard-core constraint is relaxed to
0\leq\expectationvalue{n_i}\leq 2

Batista and Ortiz 2001
;
Batista and and 2004
;
Sengupta and Batista 2007a
. Since the
3-dimensional bosonic Hilbert space support eight linearly in-dependent Hermitian operators (other than the identity matrix), generally one should consider the three states of spin-1 as the bases carrying the fundamental representation of the
SU(3)
group. Consequently, many classical orderes can exist for spin-1 systems, including the magnetic dipole order, quadrupole order (also called spin nematic order)
Bhattacharjee et al. 2006
;
Liu et al. 2010a
;
Liu et al. 2010b
;
Jin et al. 2019
, and even
U(1)
spin super-solid phases
Sengupta and Batista 2007b
;
Sengupta and Batista 2007a
;
Sheng et al. 2025
.

In this work, we study a triangular lattice spin-1 model with
U(2)
symmetry. Using a semiclassical approach, we obtain a super-solid phase which spontaneously breaks the
SU(2)\in U(2)
symmetry as well as the lattice translation symmetry. This super-solid phase contains collinear antiferromagnetic (AFM) order and two Goldstone modes. Besides, we found several other ordered phases, including a solid phase, a ferromagnetic (FM) phase, a
SU(2)
-superfluid phase (with 120
∘
AFM order), and other spin nematic phases which completely break the
U(2)
symmetry. Furthermore the
SU(3)
spin wave spectrum indicates that the instability of the ‘magnons’ agree with the phase boundary of the semiclassical phase diagram.

III

II
Model and Variational States

II.1
Spin-1 model with
U(2)
symmetry

We start from the following SU(3) Heisenberg model which has the highest symmetry for spin-1 systems,

\displaystyle H_{\rm su3}=J_{3}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{8}\lambda^{\alpha}_{i}\lambda^{\alpha}_{j},

(1)

where
\lambda^{1},...,\lambda^{8}
are the eight Gell-Mann matrices which generate the SU(3) group. Here we slightly modify the form of the Gell-Mann matrices
\lambda^{3},\lambda^{8}
as

\displaystyle\lambda^{3}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\end{matrix}\right),\lambda^{8}={1\over\sqrt{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
0&-2&0\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right),

and keep the rest ones unchanged:

\displaystyle\lambda^{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right),\lambda^{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-i&0\\
i&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda^{4}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{matrix}\right),\lambda^{5}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&-i\\
0&0&0\\
i&0&0\end{matrix}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda^{6}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{matrix}\right),\lambda^{7}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&-i\\
0&i&0\end{matrix}\right).

Notice that the nonzero entries of the generators
\lambda_{4},\lambda_{5},\lambda_{3},
are essentially three Pauli matrices
\sigma^{x},\sigma^{y},\sigma^{z}
within the subspace spanned by the first two bases
\ket{1},\ket{-1}
of the spin-1 Hilbert space, and generate an
SU(2)
subgroup of
SU(3)
. Furthermore, since
\lambda^{8}
commutes with the three generators
\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4},\lambda_{5}
, these four generators together generate the complete symmetry group
U(2)=SU(2)\times U(1)/Z_{2}
of the 2-dimensional subspace spanned by
\ket{1},\ket{-1}
, where the subgroup
U(1)
is generated by
\lambda^{8}
and the subgroup
Z_{2}=\{E,{\rm diag}(-1,1,-1)\}
is the common center of
SU(2)
and
U(1)
. Actually, the four operators of
U(2)
can be expressed in forms of
S=1
spin operators as

\displaystyle\lambda^{4}=S_{x}^{2}-S_{y}^{2},\ \lambda^{5}=S_{xy}=S_{x}S_{y}+S_{y}S_{x},

\displaystyle\lambda^{3}=S_{z},\ \lambda^{8}={1\over\sqrt{3}}(3S_{z}^{2}-2).

Similarly, the rest generators can also be written in forms of spin operators as
\lambda^{1,6}={1\over\sqrt{2}}(S_{x}\pm S_{xz}),\lambda^{2,7}={1\over\sqrt{2}}(S_{y}\pm S_{yz})
.

The above
U(2)
symmetry allows additional interactions, including the
J_{2}
term,

\displaystyle H_{\rm su2}=J_{2}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\sum_{m=3}^{5}\lambda_{m,i}\lambda_{m,j},

(2)

and the
\lambda_{8}
-term,

\displaystyle H_{\rm u1}=J_{1}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\lambda^{8}_{i}\lambda^{8}_{j}.

(3)

The model we will study in the present work is a sum of the three terms in (
1
), (
2
), (
3
):

\displaystyle H=H_{\rm u1}+H_{\rm su2}+H_{\rm su3}.

(4)

We will only consider the ‘anti-ferromagnetic’ coupling with
J_{1}>0
, and will fix
J_{3}=1
in later discussion.

Besides the
U(2)\cong SU(2)\times U(1)/Z_{2}\subset SU(3)
symmetry
Jin et al. 2019
, the above model also preserves lattice translation symmetry, spatial inversion symmetry
P
, time reversal symmetry

\displaystyle T=e^{-i{1\over\sqrt{2}}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{7})\pi}K=e^{-iS_{y}\pi}K=\left(\begin{matrix}&&1\\
&-1&\\
1&&\end{matrix}\right)K,

(5)

and
D_{6}
point group symmetry, here
P^{2}=T^{2}=1
.

II.2
Semiclassical Variational Wave functions

Any state in the Hilbert space of a single spin-1 can be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\boldsymbol{d}}=d_{+}\ket{1}+d_{0}\ket{0}+d_{-}\ket{-1}

(6)

where
\ket{1},\ket{0},\ket{-1}
are the eigenstates of
\hat{S}^{z}
with eigenvalue
1,0,-1
, respectively. The coefficients
d_{+},d_{0},d_{-}
are complex numbers subject to the normalization constrain
|d_{+}|^{2}+|d_{0}|^{2}+|d_{-}|^{2}=1
.

Since an overall phase shift does not yield a distinct physical state, the complete manifold of a spin-1 state is
S^{5}/U(1)\cong\mathbb{C}P^{2}
. A feasible parameterization scheme of this manifold is given by

\displaystyle d_{+}=\theta_{2}^{1/4}\theta_{1}^{1/2}e^{i\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle d_{0}=\theta_{2}^{1/4}\sqrt{1-\theta_{1}}e^{i\phi_{2}},

\displaystyle d_{-}=\sqrt{1-\theta_{2}^{1/2}},

where
0\leq\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\leq 1
and
0\leq\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\leq 2\pi
are
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
parameters chosen from a uniform distribution
Marsaglia 1972
. Within this classical framework, we adopt a variational wave function for the spin-1 many-body state in a direct product form

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\ket{\boldsymbol{d}_i(\theta_{1i},\theta_{2i},\phi_{1i},\phi_{2i}}).

(7)

This is so-called
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
framework, as employed in Ref.
Remund et al. 2022
. Within this variational wavefunction, the expectation values of the order parameters on each site are

\expectationvalue{\lambda_{4,i}}=2\operatorname{Re}\quantity(d_{+i}^*d_{-i}),\expectationvalue{\lambda_{5,i}}=2\operatorname{Im}\quantity(d_{+i}^*d_{-i}),\expectationvalue{\lambda_{3,i}}=|d_{+i}|^{2}-|d_{-i}|^{2},\expectationvalue{\lambda_{8,i}}={1\over\sqrt{3}}\quantity(1-3|d_{0i}|^2).

The energy of the trial state (
7
) is then given by

\displaystyle E=

\displaystyle 2J_{3}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\quantity(|{d}_{+i}^*{d}_{+j} + d_{0i}^*{d}_{0j} + d_{-i}^*{d}_{-j}|^2-\dfrac{2}{3})

\displaystyle+J_{2}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\sum_{m=1}^{3}\expectationvalue{\lambda_i^m}\expectationvalue{\lambda_j^m}+J_{1}\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\expectationvalue{\lambda_i^8}\expectationvalue{\lambda_i^8}.

(8)

The trial state (
7
) generally contains long-range order. Assuming that the unit cell contains
n_{\Lambda}
sites
Claudine et al. 2011
;
Läuchli et al. 2006
, then the variational wave function can also be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi}=\prod_{j=1}^{n_{\Lambda}}\prod_{i\in\Lambda_{j}}\ket{\boldsymbol{d}_j}_{i}

(9)

where
i
is a site index,
\Lambda_{j}
denote the set of sites belonging to the
j
-th sublattice, and on-site wavefunction is assumed to be identical for all spins within the same sublattice(i.e.
\ket{\boldsymbol{d}_j}_{i_{1}}=\ket{\boldsymbol{d}_j}_{i_{2}}=\cdots
). Thus, the task of finding ground state reduceds to optimizing the enery functional (
8
) with respect to only
4n_{\Lambda}
variables. It turns out that the lowest energy trial wave functions satisfy
n_{\Lambda}\leq 3
according to our variational calculations. The final results are shown in the phase diagram Fig.
1
, and the details will be discussed in the following sections.

III.2
Configuration of each phase

Phase-I is characterized by the solid order of
\langle\lambda^{8}\rangle
and exhibits a three-sublattice structure
(A,B,C)=(2\lambda,-\lambda,-\lambda)

Blankschtein et al. 1984

exhibits a peak at the
K

On the other hand, the maglitude of
\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}\rangle
is suppressed on
A

\lambda^{8}
has a larger value. On the other two sublattices
B
and
C

\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}\rangle
is ferromagnetic and forms a hexagonal superlattice.
The non-zero net value of
\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}\rangle
in the unit cell induces an additional signal of the statice structure factor at the
\Gamma
point. Although the ferromagnetic order
\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}\rangle

symmetry, the linear spin waves dispersion is quadratic (see Fig.
6
(a)) and cannot be considered as a ‘superfluid’. Hence we termed phase-I as a
\lambda^{8}
-solid phase
.

Phase-II is a very robust phase which contains a large volume in the phase diagram. It is also a solid phase of
\langle\lambda^{8}\rangle
with a three-sublattice structure
(A,B,C)=(2\lambda,-\lambda,-\lambda)
. Furthermore, the value of
\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}\rangle
also vanishes on the A-sublattice. The difference is that the configuration of
\langle\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}\rangle
form a Neel-type AFM order on the
B
- and
C
-sublattices . Hence the SSF

exhibits a peak at the
K

;
Gao et al. 2022

V
Conclusion and Discussion

In summary, we studied a
U(2)=SU(2)\times U(1)/Z_{2}
symmetric spin-1 model on the triangular lattice. By emplying a variational calculation within a classical
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
ansatz, we determined a rich phase diagram which contains various long-range ordered phases. The most significant finding of this work is the identification of a robust
SU(2)
-supersolid phase. In this phase, the system spontaneously breaks both the spin
SU(2)
symmetry—via a non-collinear magnetic order of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\rm su2}
—and the lattice translation symmetry—via a solid order parameter
\lambda^{8}
with a 3-sublattice
(2,-1,-1)
pattern. This represents a direct generalization of the supersolid phenomenon to a higher spin context with a more complex broken symmetry group. The stability of this classically ordered state and its characteristic excitation spectra, including intriguing degeneracies, were further validated by
SU(3)
linear spin-wave theory.

Beyond the supersolid, our study unveils a few other phases. These include conventional FM or
120^{\circ}
-AFM phases within the
SU(2)
sector, where translational symmetry remains intact or is broken by the magnetic order itself. Notably, in regions where
J_{1},J_{2}
are weak, non-zero value of
\sum_{m=1,2,6,7}\expectationvalue{\lambda^m}^{2}
implies a more exotic ordered states which complete breaks the
U(2)
symmetry. The nature of these phases warrants further investigation.

Our work established the designed
U(2)
model as a fertile ground for exploring complex magnetic orders and multiple symmetry breaking in spin-1 systems. The natural next step involves confronting these classical and spin-wave results with fully quantum-mechanical numerical simulations to assess the impact of quantum fluctuations. Furthermore, it would be intriguing to explore the potential realization of these phases in realistic magnetic compounds or quantum simulators featuring spin-1 moments on triangular lattices.

A.1
Spin wave of FM state

Let
\ket{1},\ket{0},\ket{-1}
denote the eigenstates in the
\hat{S}^{z}
basis, where Gell-Mann matrices take their standard from. Without loss of generality, the FM state
\ket{FM}
can be chosen as
\ket{FM}=\prod_{i}\ket{1}_{i}
. Introducing Schwinger Boson via the mapping
\hat{b}_{1}\ket{vac}\cong\ket{1},\hat{b}_{0}\ket{vac}\cong\ket{0},\hat{b}_{-1}\ket{vac}\cong\ket{-1}
Arovas and Auerbach 1988
, the spin-wave theory treatment assumes condensation of the
\hat{b}_{1}
boson, while
\hat{b}_{0},\hat{b}_{-1}
become the Holstein-Primakoff(HP) bosons. The Gell-Mann matrices can be rewritten in terms of Schwinger bosons. The operator
\hat{b}_{1}
is approximated by

\displaystyle\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}\approx\hat{b}_{1}\approx\sqrt{1-\hat{b}_{0}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{0}-\hat{b}_{-1}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{-1}}

(14)

reflecting the constraint on the total boson number per site. Within the linear spin-wave approximation, single operators
\hat{b}_{1},\hat{b}_{1}^{\dagger}
are replaced by
1
, while their product is substituded using the number constraint:

\displaystyle b_{1}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{1}=1-\hat{b}_{0}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{0}-\hat{b}_{-1}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{-1}

(15)

Expanding the Hamiltonian and retaining terms up to quadratic order in the HP boson operators yields the general form

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}}\quantity(T_{\alpha\beta}\hat b_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat b_{j\beta}+\Delta_{\alpha\beta}\hat b_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat b_{j\beta}^{\dagger}+h.c.)

(16)

\displaystyle+12\sum_{i}V_{\alpha\beta}\hat{b}_{i\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{i\beta}+E_{0}

(17)

where the indices
\alpha,\beta
run over the two flavors of HP bosons (
0
and
\downarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}V_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{m=1}^{8}J^{(m)}\lambda_{00}^{m}\quantity(\lambda_{\alpha\beta}^m-\lambda_{00}^m\delta_{\alpha\beta})\\

For our model Hamiltonian, the coupling strengths

\displaystyle J^{(m)}=\begin{cases}J_{2}+J_{3}\qquad m=3,4,5\\
J_{3}\qquad m=1,2,6,7\\

Performing a Fourier transformation

A.2
Spin wave for 3-sublattice state

We now introduce spin-wave theory for generic 3-sublattice states. In this case, the spin state on the three sublattices (A,B,C) may differ and are generally not the standard
\ket{1}
state. Therefore, a separate local basis rotation must be performed on each sublattice such that its respective ground state spin configuration aligns with the first basis vector of the local Hilbert space. In these rotated basis, the Gell-Mann matrices assume representations different from the standard one. We denote these new representations for sublattice
S

\lambda^{mS}(m=1,\cdots,8;S=A,B,C)

\hat{b}_{iA\alpha},\hat{b}_{iB\alpha},\hat{b}_{iC\alpha}

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{AB}+\hat{H}_{BC}+\hat{H}_{CA}

(20)

Each part has an identical strcture. Using
\hat{H}_{AB}
as an example, we have

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{AB}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}_{AB}}\quantity(T_{\alpha\beta}^{AB}\hat b_{iA\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat b_{jB\beta}+h.c.)

(21)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{\expectationvalue{ij}_{AB}}\quantity(\Delta_{\alpha\beta}^{AB}\hat b_{iA\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat b_{jB\beta}^{\dagger}+h.c.)

(22)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle z_{AB}\sum_{i\in A}V_{\alpha\beta}^{AB}\hat{b}_{iA\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{iA\beta}

Here,

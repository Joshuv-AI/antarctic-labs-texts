A Torres formula for twisted Reidemeister torsion
Peter Seokhee Seong

Abstract
. The Torres formula, which relates the Alexander polynomial of a link to the Alexander polyomial of its sublinks, admits a generalization to the twisted setting due to Morifuji. This paper uses twisted Reidemeister torsion to obtain a second proof of Morifuji’s result that is closer in appearance to Torres’ original formula.



1.
Introduction

The
Torres formula
relates the multivariable Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}
of a
\mu
-component ordered link
L=K_{1}\cup\ldots\cup K_{\mu}\subset S^{3}
to the multivariable Alexander polynomial of the sublink
L^{\prime}:=L\setminus K_{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1},1)\doteq\begin{cases}\dfrac{t_{1}^{\ell k(K_{1},K_{2})}-1}{t_{1}-1}\Delta_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1})&\quad\text{if }\mu=2\\
(t_{1}^{\ell k(K_{1},K_{\mu})}\cdots t_{\mu-1}^{\ell k(K_{\mu-1},K_{\mu})}-1)\Delta_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1})&\quad\text{if }\mu>2.\end{cases}

Torres’ original proof made use of Fox calculus
[
Tor53
]
but there are now also proofs involving Reidemeister torsion
[
Tur86
]
and Seifert surfaces
[
Cim04
]
.

More recently, the Alexander polynomial has been generalized to include the additional data of a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)\to\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
of the link group.
The resulting
twisted Alexander polynomials
, originally due to Lin
[
Lin01
]
and Wada
[
Wad94
]
, were later reformulated using Reidemeister torsion
[
Kit96
,
KL99
]
and have been successfully applied to several areas of link theory; we refer to
[
FV11
]
for a survey.
In particular, Morifuji used Fox calculus to prove that twisted Alexander polynomials satisfy a Torres condition
[
Mor07
]
.
Reformulated in terms of twisted Reidemeister torsion, Morifuji’s result states that, given a field
\mathbb{F}
and a representation
\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L^{\prime})\to\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
, there are elements
\varepsilon_{k,\rho^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{F}
for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
such that

\tau_{L}^{\rho}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1},1)\doteq\left((t_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu-1}^{\ell_{\mu-1}})^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k,\rho^{\prime}}(t_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu}^{\ell_{\mu-1}})^{n-k}+(-1)^{n}\right)\cdot\tau_{L^{\prime}}^{\rho^{\prime}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1})\penalty\

where
\ell_{i}:=\ell k(K_{i},K_{\mu})
and
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)\to\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L^{\prime})\to\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
is obtained by precomposing
\rho^{\prime}
with the inclusion induced map.

The goal of this paper is to use twisted Reidemeister torsion to obtain a version of Morifuji’s theorem
that is closer in appearance to Torres’ original formula.
Continuing with the notation above and using
UFD
as an abbreviation for “Unique Factorization Domain” our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian UFD, let
\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L^{\prime})\to\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
be a representation, let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L)\to\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\rho^{\prime}}\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
be the composition of
\rho^{\prime}
with the inclusion induced map, let
[K_{\mu}]\in\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L^{\prime})
denote any based homotopy class of
K_{\mu}
in the complement of
L^{\prime}
.

The twisted Reidemeister torsion of
(L,\rho)
satisfies

\tau_{L}^{\rho}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1},1)=\det\left(t_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu-1}^{\ell_{\mu-1}}\rho^{\prime}([K_{\mu}])-I_{n}\right)\cdot\tau_{L^{\prime}}^{\rho^{\prime}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1})

up to multiplication by units of
R[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1}^{\pm 1}].

When
n=1,\rho^{\prime}
is the trivial representation and
R=\mathbb{Z}
, Theorem
1.1
recovers the Torres formula for the (untwisted) Alexander polynomial whereas, as we explain in Corollary
3.4
, in the case of
\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
representations, it recovers Morifuji’s formula, albeit with a less refined indeterminacy.

Remark 1.2
.

Morifuji’s twisted Torres formula for
\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
-representations holds up to multiplication by
\pm t_{1}^{nk_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu}^{nk_{\mu}}
, whereas ours only holds up to multiplication by
x\cdot t_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu}^{k_{\mu}}
with
x\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}.

We believe that with a little more effort, the equality in Theorem
1.1
could be shown to hold up to multiplication by elements of the form
\pm\det(\rho^{\prime}(g))t_{1}^{nk_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu}^{nk_{\mu}}
with
g\in\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus L^{\prime}).

Remark 1.3
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
shows that
\det\left(t_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\cdots t_{\mu-1}^{\ell_{\mu-1}}\rho^{\prime}([K_{\mu}])-I_{n}\right)
does not depend on the based homotopy class of
K_{\mu}
but this can also be verified directly by using the conjugacy invariance of the determinant.

Organization

Section
2
reviews some background material including the definition of Reidemeister torsion and Alexander polynomials.
Section
3
focuses on the proof of Theorem
1.1
and shows how it recovers Morifuji’s theorem.

Conventions

Unless mentioned otherwise, spaces are assumed to be path-connected and to have the homotopy type of a finite CW complex.
Manifolds are assumed to compact, connected and oriented.
The unit group of a ring
R
is denoted by
R^{\times}
and the field of fractions of a domain
S
is denoted by
\widetilde{S}.

Background

This section collects some background material needed to prove Theorem

.
Section
2.1
reviews twisted (co)homology, Section
2.2

2.3

2.1.
Twisted (co)homology

This section reviews twisted homology and cohomology.
Our treatment follows
[
Con17
]
, but many more details on this material can be found in
[
Fri23
]
.

Let
X
be a space and let
Y\subset X
be a possibly empty subspace. Denote by
p:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\ X
the universal cover of
X
and set
\widetilde{Y}:=p^{-1}(Y)
. The left action of
\pi_{1}(X)
by deck transformations on
\widetilde{X}
endows the singular chain complex
C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
with the structure of a left
{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]}
-module. Let
R
be a ring and
M
be a
(R,{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]})
-bimodule.
We write
\overline{C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})}
for the right
{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]}
-module whose underlying abelian group agrees with
C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
but where the action is by
\sigma\cdot g=\overline{g}\sigma
for
\sigma\in C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
and
g\in\pi_{1}(X)
.

Definition 2.1
.

The chain complex
C_{*}(X,Y;M):=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]}C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
of left
R
-modules is called the
twisted chain complex
of
(X,Y)
with coefficients in
M
. The corresponding homology left
R
-modules
H_{*}(X,Y;M)
are called the
twisted homology modules
of
(X,Y)
with coefficients in
M
. The cochain complex
C^{*}(X,Y;M):=\operatorname{Hom}_{\text{right-}{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]}}(\overline{C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})},M)
of left
R
-modules is called the
twisted cochain complex
of
(X,Y)
with coefficients in
M
. The corresponding cohomology left
R
-modules
H^{*}(X,Y;M)
are called the
twisted cohomology modules
of
(X,Y)
with coefficients in
M
.

If
Y=\emptyset
, then we write
H_{*}(X;M)
and
H^{*}(X;M)
instead of
H_{*}(X,\emptyset;M)
and
H^{*}(X,\emptyset;M)
.
The isomorphism type of twisted (co)homology is independent of the choice of the basepoint in
X
for
\pi_{1}(X)
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
(X,A)
is a CW pair, the same definition can be made using cellular (co)chain complexes and the resulting (co)chain complexes are chain homotopy equivalent
[
FNOP24
, Proposition A.3]
. In the sequel, these identifications will be made implicitly.

Given a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
, we define a right action of
\pi_{1}(X)
on
R^{n}
by

x\cdot g:=\rho(g)^{-1}x

for each
g\in\pi_{1}(X)
and
x\in R^{n}
.
Here we think of elements of
R^{n}
as column vectors.
This action endows
R^{n}
with the structure of a right
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]
-module.
It follows that
R^{n}
is a
(R,\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])
-bimodule which will be denoted by
(R^{n},\rho)
.
For convenience, we write

\displaystyle C_{*}(X,Y;R^{n},\rho)

\displaystyle:=C_{*}(X,Y;(R^{n},\rho))

\displaystyle H_{*}(X,Y;R^{n},\rho)

\displaystyle:=H_{*}(X,Y;(R^{n},\rho))

\displaystyle C^{*}(X,Y;R^{n},\rho)

\displaystyle:=C^{*}(X,Y;(R^{n},\rho))

\displaystyle H_{*}(X,Y;R^{n},\rho)

\displaystyle:=H^{*}(X,Y;(R^{n},\rho)).

We refer to
[
FK06
, Lemma 2.7]
for a proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
X
is an
m
-manifold and
R
is a domain, then

\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\mbox{rk}_{R}(H_{i}(X;R^{n},\rho))=n\cdot\chi(X).

We recall a description of the
0^{th}
twisted (co)homology module.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\pi
be a group and let
M
be a
(R,\mathbb{Z}[\pi])
-bimodule. Define the
left
R
-module of
\pi
-coinvariants
of
M
by

M_{\pi}:=M\Bigg/\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}{(v_{i}\cdot g_{i}-v_{i})}\ \Bigg|\ g_{i}\in\pi,v_{i}\in M\right\}

and the
left
R
-module of
\pi
-invariants
of
M
by

M^{\pi}:=\left\{v\in M\mid v\cdot g=v\text{ for all }g\in\pi\right\}.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
Fri23
, Proposition 189.1]

Let
X
be a path-connected space. We write
\pi:=\pi_{1}(X)
. Let
R
be a ring and let
M
be a
(R,\mathbb{Z}[\pi])
-bimodule. Then, we have the following isomorphisms of left
R
-modules:

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{0}(X;M)

\displaystyle\cong M_{\pi}

\displaystyle\displaystyle H^{0}(X;M)

\displaystyle\cong M^{\pi}.

Finally, we recall the following fact from homological algebra.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a domain and let
C
be a finite chain complex of
R
-modules. Then, the following are equivalent:

(i)

\widetilde{R}\otimes_{R}C
is acyclic.

(ii)

H_{i}(C)
is
R
-torsion for each
i\geq 0
.

2.2.
Reidemeister torsion

This section sets up our notation for Reidemeister torsion and recalls its relation to orders of modules.
We refer to
[
Tur01
]
for a more lengthy discussion of the topic.

We refer to
[
Tur01
, Chapter 1]
for the definition of the
torsion

\tau(C)\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}
of a finite acyclic chain complex
C
of finite dimensional based vector spaces over a field
\mathbb{F}
.
If
C
is not acyclic, then we set
\tau(C)=0
. Hence, the torsion is an element in
\mathbb{F}
.

Given a Noetherian UFD
R
and an
R
-module
M
, we write
\Delta(M)\in R/R^{\times}
for the order of
M
; we refer the reader to
[
Kaw96
, Chapter 7]
for the definition of
\Delta(M).

The next proposition recalls the relation between Reidemeister torsion and orders.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Tur01
, Theorem 4.7]
)
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian UFD and let

C_{*}=(C_{m}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow C_{0})

be a based free chain complex of finite rank over
R
such that
\mbox{rk}(H_{i}(C_{*}))=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Then
\widetilde{C}_{*}:=\widetilde{R}\otimes_{R}C_{*}
is acyclic and satisfies the following formula:

\displaystyle\tau(\widetilde{C}_{*})=\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{m}\Delta(H_{i}(C_{*}))^{(-1)^{i+1}}.

Note that the equality in the above equation holds up to multiplication by units of
R
.

2.3.
Twisted Alexander polynomials

This short section recalls the definition of twisted Alexander polynomials.
Helpful references on the topic include
[
FV11
]
and
[
KL99
]
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian UFD. Let
N
be a 3-manifold with empty or toroidal boundary, let
\psi\colon\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow H
be a non-trivial homomorphism to a free abelian group
H
and let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
be a representation.
The tensor representation
\rho\otimes\psi\colon\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R[H])
is defined by
g\mapsto\rho(g)\cdot\psi(g)
.

Definition 2.8
.

For
i\geq 0
, the
i^{th}
twisted Alexander polynomial

\Delta^{\rho\otimes\psi}_{N,i}
of
(N,\rho\otimes\psi)
is

\Delta(H_{i}(N;R[H]^{n},\rho\otimes\psi))\in R[H]/R[H]^{\times}

We now specialize to the case where
N=X_{L}
is the exterior of a
\mu
-component link
L\subset S^{3}
(this is indeed a 3-manifold with toroidal boundary)
and
\psi=\gamma\colon\pi_{1}(X_{L})\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}(X_{L})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
is the Hurewicz epimorphism.

Definition 2.9
.

The
i^{th}
twisted Alexander polynomial

\Delta^{\rho}_{L,i}
of a link
L
associated with the representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X_{L})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
is defined as
\Delta^{\rho\otimes\gamma}_{X_{L},i}
for each
i\geq 0
.

2.4.
Twisted Reidemeister torsion

We recall the definition of the twisted Reidemeister torsion of a CW pair, referring to
[
FV11
]
for further details.
In this section, we work with cellular chain complexes.

Let
(X,Y)
be a finite CW pair and let
\varphi\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,S)
be a representation where
S
is an integral domain.
Then,
S^{n}
is a
(S,\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)])
-bimodule via the induced ring homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]\rightarrow M_{n}(S)
. The twisted chain complex
S^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]}C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
consists of finitely generated free
S
-modules.

Bases for these modules are obtained by lifting the cell structure of
X
to the universal cover, orienting and ordering the outcome, and combining the resulting basis of
C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
with the canonical basis of
S^{n}
.

Definition 2.10
.

The
twisted Reidemeister torsion

\tau((X,Y),\varphi)\in\widetilde{S}
of a CW pair
(X,Y)
associated with a representation
\varphi:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,S)
is defined as the torsion of the based chain complex
\widetilde{S}^{n}\otimes C_{*}(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y})
.

The next proposition resolves the ambiguity in the choice of the underlying CW-structure of the 3-manifold
and of the lifts of the cells.

Proposition 2.11

(
[
FV11
]
,
[
Cha74
]
)
.

The twisted Reidemeister torsion
\tau(N,\varphi)
of a 3-manifold
N
with empty or toroidal boundary is well-defined up to multiplication by an element in

\{\pm\det(\varphi(g))\,|\,g\in\pi_{1}(N)\}.

In other words, up to this indeterminacy,
\tau(N,\varphi)
is independent of the choice of underlying CW-structure and the choice of the lifts of the cells.

In the sequel, given
w\in\widetilde{S}
, we write

\tau(N,\varphi)\doteq_{\tau}w

if there exists a representative of
\tau(N,\varphi)
which equals
w
.

We recall the notation from Section
2.3
.
Given a Noetherian UFD
R
, the polynomial ring
R[H]
is also Noetherian UFD whose units are of the form
r\cdot h
where
r\in R^{\times}
and
h\in H
.
In the case where the representation
\varphi
is the tensor representation
\rho\otimes\psi\colon\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R[H])
, by specializing Proposition
2.11
, the indeterminacy of
\tau(N,\rho\otimes\psi)
is up to multiplication by an element in

\{\pm\det(\varphi(g))h\,|\,g\in\pi_{1}(N),h\in H\}.

From this point onwards, we regard the twisted
Reidemeister torsion of
(N,\rho\otimes\psi)
to be the value in
\widetilde{R[H]}
up to this indeterminacy.

Remark 2.12
.

The twisted Alexander polynomial is closely related to the twisted Reidemeister torsion. For example, in the case that
\mbox{rk}(\text{Im}(\psi))\geq 2
,
[
FV11
, Proposition 3.2]
shows that

\tau(N,\rho\otimes\psi)\doteq_{\Delta}\Delta^{\rho\otimes\psi}_{N,1},

where
\doteq_{\Delta}
indicates equality up to multiplication by units of
R[H]
.
For this reason, the twisted Reidemeister torsion is occasionally referred to as the twisted Alexander polynomial; see e.g.
[
Wad94
]
.

Next, we consider the Reidemeister torsion of the exterior
X_{L}
of a link
L\subset S^{3}
.
Recall that this is a 3-manifold with toroidal boundary and that
\gamma\colon\pi_{1}(X_{L})\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}(X_{L})
denotes the Hurewicz epimorphism.

Definition 2.13
.

The
twisted Reidemeister torsion

\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu})
of a link
L
associated with a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(X_{L})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
is defined as
\tau(X_{L},\rho\otimes\gamma)
.

Since
H_{1}(X_{L})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}=\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}\rangle
where
t_{i}
corresponds to the
i^{th}
meridian of
L
,
\tau(X_{L},\rho\otimes\gamma)
can be represented by a rational function of
\mu
variables
t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu}
over
R
. Therefore, our notation for the twisted Reidemeister torsion is justified.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

3.1.
Preliminary lemmas

This short section records two lemmas that will be needed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
N
be a 3-manifold with empty or toroidal boundary, let
\psi:\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow H
be a non-trivial homomorphism to a free abelian group
H
and let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(N)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
be a representation.
Furthermore let
\phi\colon H\rightarrow J
be a homomorphism to a free abelian group such that
\phi\circ\psi
is non-trivial.
We denote the induced ring homomorphism
R[H]\rightarrow R[J]
by
\phi
as well.
Set

S:=\{f\in R[H]\mid\phi(f)\neq 0\}=R[H]\setminus\ker\phi.

Note that
\phi
induces a homomorphism
S^{-1}R[H]\rightarrow\widetilde{R[J]}
which we also denote by
\phi
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
FV11
]
Proposition 3)
.

We have
\tau(N,\rho\otimes\psi)\in S^{-1}R[H]
, and

\tau(N,\rho\otimes(\phi\circ\psi))\doteq_{\tau}\phi(\tau(N,\rho\otimes\psi)).

We fix some notation needed to state our second lemma.
Let
L=K_{1}\cup\dots\cup K_{\mu}\subset S^{3}
be a link with
\mu\geq 2
components.
Set
L^{\prime}:=L\setminus K_{\mu}\subset S^{3}.

Write the respective exteriors of
L
and
L^{\prime}
as
X_{L}:=S^{3}\setminus\nu L
and
X_{L^{\prime}}:=S^{3}\setminus\nu L^{\prime}
. Let
\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
be a representation where
R
is a Noetherian UFD. Denote
\Gamma:=R[H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})]
. Consider the Hurewicz epimorphism
\gamma^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})\twoheadrightarrow H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})
and the tensor representation
\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime}:\pi_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,\Gamma)
. We define

T:=\gamma^{\prime}(K_{\mu})=\prod_{i=1}^{\mu-1}t_{i}^{\ell k(K_{i},K_{\mu})}.

The second technical lemma of this section now reads as follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

The
\Gamma^{n}
-homology of
{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
is

H_{i}:=H_{i}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\cong\begin{cases}(\Gamma^{n})_{\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})}=\Gamma^{n}/(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})\Gamma^{n}\quad&\text{for i=2,}\\
(\Gamma^{n})^{\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})}\quad&\text{for i=3,}\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here, the right
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})]
-module
\Gamma^{n}
is considered as a right
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})]
-module using the inclusion induced ring homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})]\to\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})]
.

Additionally,
\mbox{rk}\ H_{2}=\mbox{rk}\ H_{3}
and

•

if
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})=0
, then
\mbox{rk}\ H_{2}=\mbox{rk}\ H_{3}>0
,

•

if
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})\neq 0
, then all the
H_{i}
are
\Gamma
-torsion and
H_{3}=0.

Proof.

Write
H_{i}:=H_{i}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})
for simplicity.
Considering the cellular chain complex of the pair
(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})
, we get
H_{i}=0
for
i\geq 4
.
Assume
i\leq 3
from this point onward.
Note that
\bar{\nu}K_{\mu}
is a 3-manifold with boundary. We get the following
\Gamma
-isomorphisms:

\displaystyle H_{i}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\cong H_{i}(\bar{\nu}K_{\mu},\partial\bar{\nu}K_{\mu};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\eta_{*})

by excision

\displaystyle\cong H^{3-i}(\bar{\nu}K_{\mu};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\eta_{*})

by Poincaré duality

\displaystyle\cong H^{3-i}(K_{\mu};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\eta_{*}\circ\iota_{*})

by homotopy equivalence

\displaystyle\cong H^{3-i}(K_{\mu};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ(\eta\circ\iota)_{*})

by functoriality

where
\iota\colon K_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\bar{\nu}K_{\mu}
and
\eta\colon\bar{\nu}K_{\mu}\hookrightarrow X_{L^{\prime}}
are the inclusions; these induce ring homomorphisms between the group rings
over the corresponding fundamental groups.

As
K_{\mu}
is a 1-manifold,
H_{i}=0
for
i\leq 1
.
By Poincaré duality applied to
K_{\mu}
and Proposition
2.5
, we have:

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle\cong H_{0}(K_{\mu};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ(\eta\circ\iota)_{*})\cong(\Gamma^{n})_{\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})}

\displaystyle H_{3}

\displaystyle\cong H^{0}(K_{\mu};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ(\eta\circ\iota)_{*})\cong(\Gamma^{n})^{\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})}.

As
\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})
is infinite cyclic, Definition
2.4
implies that

H_{2}\cong(\Gamma^{n})_{\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})}\cong\Gamma^{n}/\langle(v\cdot\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(K_{\mu})^{-1}-v)\mid v\in\Gamma^{n}\rangle\cong\Gamma^{n}/(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})\Gamma^{n}.

It remains to prove the last two assertions.

Since
X_{L}
and
X_{L^{\prime}}
are both
3
-manifolds with toroidal boundary,
\chi^{\Gamma}(X_{L})=\chi(X_{L})=0
and
\chi^{\Gamma}(X_{L^{\prime}})=\chi(X_{L^{\prime}})=0
by Lemma
2.3
. The long exact sequence of the pair
{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
now implies that
\chi^{\Gamma}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})=0
. Therefore, we have
\mbox{rk}H_{2}=\mbox{rk}\ H_{3}
.

Denote
A:=T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n}

and observe that the exact sequence

0\to\ker(A)\to R^{n}\xrightarrow{A}R^{n}\to H_{2}\to 0

ensures that
\mbox{rk}\ H_{2}=\mbox{rk}\ker(A).

If
\det A=0
, then
\mbox{rk}\ H_{3}=\mbox{rk}\ H_{2}=\mbox{rk}\ker(A)>0
.
If
\det A\neq 0
, then
\ker(A)
=0 and therefore
\mbox{rk}\ H_{3}=\mbox{rk}\ H_{2}=\mbox{rk}\ker(A)=0
.
But since
\Gamma^{n}
is torsion-free, so is
H_{3}\cong(\Gamma^{n})^{\pi_{1}(K_{\mu})}\subset\Gamma^{n}
.
This implies
H_{3}
is both torsion and torsion-free, so
H_{3}=0
.

∎

3.2.
Conclusion of the theorem

This section concludes the proof of our main result.
As in the previous sections, we set

T:=\prod_{i=1}^{\mu-1}t_{i}^{\ell k(K_{i},K_{\mu})}.

and it is understood that all links are in
S^{3}.

We also recall that
\doteq_{\Delta}
denotes equality up to multiplication by units of
R[H]=R[t_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{\mu-1}^{\pm 1}].

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
L=K_{1}\cup\dots\cup K_{\mu}
be a link with
\mu\geq 2
components and let
L^{\prime}:=L\setminus K_{\mu}
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu L^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
be a representation where
R
is a Noetherian UFD. Denote by
\rho
the representation
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu L)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu L^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\rho^{\prime}}\operatorname{GL}(n,R)
.

The twisted Reidemeister torsion of
(L,\rho)
satisfies

\displaystyle\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)\doteq_{\Delta}\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})\cdot\tau^{\rho^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1}).

Proof.

We begin by setting up some notation and by reformulating
\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)
in a more convenient way.
The inclusion
\iota:X_{L}\hookrightarrow X_{L^{\prime}}
induces group epimorphisms on
\pi_{1}
and
H_{1}
that fit into the following commutative diagram:

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X_{L})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(X_{L})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{\mu}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

Here, for each
i<\mu
, the variable
t_{i}
corresponds to the meridian of
K_{i}
.
By (
1
) and because
\rho\colon=\rho^{\prime}\circ\iota_{*}
, we have:

(2)

(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*}=(\rho^{\prime}\circ\iota_{*})\otimes(\gamma^{\prime}\circ\iota_{*})=\rho\otimes(\iota_{*}\circ\gamma).

Note that
H_{1}(X_{L})
and
H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})
are free abelian groups and
\iota_{*}\circ\gamma
is non-trivial.
We now reformulate
\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)
as

(3)

\begin{split}\tau(X_{L},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*})&\doteq_{\tau}\tau(X_{L},\rho\otimes(\iota_{*}\circ\gamma))\quad\text{by \eqref{tensor-composition}}\\
&\doteq_{\tau}\iota_{*}(\tau(X_{L},\rho\otimes\gamma))\quad\quad\text{by Lemma \ref{evalutation lemma} with }\psi=\gamma,\,\phi=\iota_{*}\\
&\doteq_{\tau}\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1),\end{split}

where in the last equality we used that the ring homomorphism

\iota_{*}:(R[H_{1}(X_{L})]\setminus\ker\iota_{*})^{-1}R[H_{1}(X_{L})]\rightarrow\widetilde{R[H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})]}

described in Lemma
3.1
maps
t_{i}
to
t_{i}
for
i<\mu
but maps
t_{\mu}
to
1
as in (
1
).

One last piece of notation: in what follows, we use the following abbreviations for simplicity:

\displaystyle C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}})

\displaystyle:=C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle H_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}})

\displaystyle:=H_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})

\displaystyle:=C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle H_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})

\displaystyle:=H_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L};\Gamma^{n},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle C_{*}(X_{L})

\displaystyle:=C_{*}(X_{L};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*})

\displaystyle H_{*}(X_{L})

\displaystyle:=H_{*}(X_{L};\Gamma^{n},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*}).

We are now ready to prove the twisted Torres formula.

Case 1

(
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})=0
)
.

It is enough to show that
\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)=0
.
By (
3
), we have left to show
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L})
is not acyclic which is equivalent to saying that
H_{i}(X_{L})
is not
\Gamma
-torsion for some
i
by Proposition
2.6
.

As
X_{L^{\prime}}
has the homotopy type of a finite 2-dimensional CW complex,
H_{3}(X_{L^{\prime}})=0
(see e.g.
[
FNOP24
, Theorem 3.16]
). Using Lemma
3.2
,
H_{3}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})
has positive rank and therefore the following part of the long exact sequence of the pair
{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
shows that
H_{2}(X_{L})
has a positive rank:

\overbrace{H_{3}(X_{L^{\prime}})}^{=0}\rightarrow H_{3}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})\rightarrow H_{2}(X_{L}).

Thus, in this case, the Torres formula holds trivially: both sides of the equality vanish.

Case 2

(
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})\neq 0
)
.

We assert that
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L})
is acyclic if and only if
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}})
is acyclic.
Recall that
H_{i}(X_{L},X_{L^{\prime}})
is
\Gamma
-torsion for all
i
from Lemma
3.2
, equivalently, we have
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes H_{i}(X_{L},X_{L^{\prime}})=0
for all
i
.
By applying
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes-
to the long exact sequence of homology
\Gamma
-modules of the pair
{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
, we get that
H_{i}(X_{L})
and
H_{i}(X_{L^{\prime}})
have the same rank for all
i
.
By Proposition
2.6
, the assertion holds.

Case 2.1

(
\tau^{\rho^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1})=0
)
.

This condition is equivalent to
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}})
not being acyclic. Hence,
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L})
is not acyclic as well by the assertion which is equivalent to
\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)\doteq_{\tau}\tau(X_{L},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*})=0
where the first equality is (
3
).
Thus, in this case, the Torres formula holds trivially once again.

Case 2.2

(
\tau^{\rho^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1})\neq 0
)
.

By the assertion, both
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}})
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}(X_{L})
are acyclic.
Choose cell structures on
X_{L^{\prime}}
and
X_{L}
so that
(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})
is a CW pair.
After picking the canonical basis for
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{n}
, lifts, orientations and an ordering for the cells of
X_{L^{\prime}}
, we get bases
\mathbf{c}_{X_{L^{\prime}}},\mathbf{c}_{X_{L}}
and
\mathbf{c}_{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
for the twisted chain complexes
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes_{\Gamma}C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}}),\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes_{\Gamma}C_{*}(X_{L})
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes_{\Gamma}C_{*}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})
.
In the notation of
[
Tur01
, page 4]
, these bases satisfy
\mathbf{c}_{X_{L^{\prime}}}\sim\mathbf{c}_{X_{L}}\mathbf{c}_{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
and so the multiplicativity of Reidemeister torsion
[
Tur01
, Proposition 1.5]
ensures that

(4)

\tau(X_{L^{\prime}},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\doteq_{\tau}\tau(X_{L},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*})\cdot\tau({(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime}).

We compute the torsion of the pair
{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}
as follows:

\displaystyle\tau({(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\doteq_{\tau}\tau(\widetilde{\Gamma}\otimes C_{*}{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})})

\displaystyle\doteq_{\Delta}\displaystyle\prod_{i=0}^{m}\Delta(H_{i}(C_{*}{(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})}))^{(-1)^{i+1}}

by Proposition
2.7

\displaystyle\doteq_{\Delta}\frac{1}{\Delta(H_{2}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L}))}

by Lemma
3.2

\displaystyle\doteq_{\Delta}\frac{1}{\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})}.

The last equality uses that the square matrix
T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n}
presents
H_{2}(X_{L^{\prime}},X_{L})
.

We know that

\displaystyle\tau(X_{L},(\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})\circ\iota_{*})

\displaystyle\doteq_{\tau}\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)

by
(
3
)

\displaystyle\tau(X_{L^{\prime}},\rho^{\prime}\otimes\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\doteq_{\tau}\tau^{\rho^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1})

by Definition
2.13
.

Therefore, equation (
4
) can be reformulated to

\tau^{\rho^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1})\doteq_{\Delta}\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)\cdot\frac{1}{\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})}.

We get the desired result by multiplying by
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})
on both sides.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Since
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})
is the characteristic polynomial of the invertible matrix
\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})
, it follows that
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})
is an element of
R[T]
(the polynomial ring over
R
in the variable
T
) of degree
n
whose constant term is
(-1)^{n}
.

We recover Morifuji’s result (albeit with a less refined indeterminacy) as follows.

Corollary 3.4

(
[
Mor07
]
Theorem 3.6)
.

Let
L
and
L^{\prime}
be two links as in Theorem
1.1
, let
\mathbb{F}
be a field, let
\rho^{\prime}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu L^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
be a representation, and consider the composition
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu L)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\nu L^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\rho^{\prime}}\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
.

The twisted Reidemeister torsion of
(L,\rho)
satisfies

\displaystyle\tau^{\rho}_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1},1)\doteq_{\Delta}\left(T^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{\mu-1}\epsilon_{i}T^{i}+(-1)^{n}\right)\cdot\tau^{\rho^{\prime}}_{L^{\prime}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu-1})

where
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{F}
for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

Proof.

Since every field is a Noetherian UFD, we can apply Theorem
1.1
. Since
\rho^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}(n,\mathbb{F})
, the polynomial
\det(T\rho^{\prime}(K_{\mu})-I_{n})
is monic. We’ve shown that the constant term is
(-1)^{n}
in Remark
3.3
.
∎

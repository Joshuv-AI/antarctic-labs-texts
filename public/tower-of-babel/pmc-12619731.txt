S100A4 Induces Neutrophilic Inflammation in Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps via TLR4 Pathway
Authors: Yanyi Tu, Chunhao Li, Tao Li, Jing Liu, Linghui Meng, Shengyang Liu, Ping Li, Yuzhu Wan, Aiping Chen, Li Shi, De-Yun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTreating neutrophilic inflammation in chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) remains a challenge. Managing excessive infiltration and activation of neutrophils in tissues is important for improving CRSwNP outcomes. S100A4, a calcium-binding protein, regulates cell migration, chemotaxis and tissue fibrosis. In this study, we sought to examine the role of S100A4 in neutrophilic inflammation in CRSwNP and its involvement in TLR4 signaling.MethodsQuantitative RT-PCR and immunofluorescence were used to analyze the expression and cellular distribution of S100A4 in sinonasal mucosa. Primary human nasal epithelial cells (hNECs) were cultured and treated with S100A4 to assess cytokine and chemokine production. Additionally, we employed TLR4 inhibitor (TAK-242) to investigate whether S100A4 exert this effect via TLR4 pathway.ResultsWe found increased levels of S100A4 mRNA and S100A4+ cell number in CRSwNP patients compared with control, with the highest levels in uncontrolled and mixed eosinophilic-neutrophilic CRSwNP. Compared to eosinophils, S100A4 exhibits a stronger correlation with neutrophils. S100A4 was primarily located in inflammatory cells in lamina propria, with neutrophils forming the majority of S100A4+ cells. S100A4 treatment led to upregulation of neutrophil chemokines and pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-36γ, in nasal epithelia cells. S100A4 induced effect through TLR4 pathway, and can be inhibited by clarithromycin and dexamethasone.ConclusionS100A4 is elevated in neutrophilic CRSwNP and exerts its pro-inflammatory effect on nasal epithelial cells via TLR4 signaling cascade.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10875-025-01953-4.

Introduction

Chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) is a prevalent inflammatory disease of the nasal and paranasal mucosa, exhibiting a high incidence rate globally. CRS includes a variety of diseases with different pathophysiological mechanisms. Many patients experience poor disease control even after receiving pharmacotherapy or surgery, which significantly affects their daily life and work. Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP) belongs to primary CRS, with 20% to 61% of CRSwNP demonstrating type 2 inflammatory response and tissue eosinophilia [1]. These features are associated with higher rates of asthma comorbidity and postoperative recurrence. In recent years, biologics treatments such as anti-IgE, anti-IL-4Rα and anti-IL-5 antibodies have been clinically proven effective for CRSwNP patients. However, CRSwNP shows different inflammatory patterns based on diverse geographical regions and ethnic backgrounds [2]. White patients often show a relatively stronger Th2 pattern, while patients in Asian countries such as China and Japan exhibit mixed Th2/Th1/Th17 patterns, accompanied by increased neutrophilic inflammation [3]. A prospective study in 2018 revealed that difficult-to-treat CRS was associated with both eosinophilic and neutrophilic inflammation, with increased neutrophilia may reduce the response to corticosteroid therapy [4]. However, there are no specific treatment for neutrophilic inflammation currently, partly due to limited understanding of neutrophils’ function in CRS. Controlling excessive infiltration and activation of neutrophils may be of crucial clinical significance for the treatment of certain difficult-to-treat CRS patients.

The S100 family of calcium-binding proteins contains 25 members. These proteins share a common feature of helix-loop-helix EF-hand motif. They regulate many intracellular and/or extracellular functions [5, 6]. Intracellularly, S100 proteins regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, energy metabolism, Ca2+ homeostasis, inflammation, and cell death; when released extracellularly, they interact with specific receptors. These helps to coordinate innate and adaptive immunity, cell migration and chemotaxis, as well as tissue development and repair [7, 8]. S100A4 belongs to the S100 family and is expressed in many cell types, including tumor cells, fibroblasts, macrophages, lymphocytes, neutrophils, vascular endothelial cells, and bone marrow-derived cells [9, 10]. It plays a role in tumor growth and migration, [11], angiogenesis [12] and tissue fibrosis [13]. S100A4 is also involved in pro-inflammatory pathways. The expression of S100A4 enhances the motility of macrophages and neutrophils, promotes the recruitment and chemotaxis of these inflammatory cells, and also increases the secretion of inflammatory cytokines [14, 15]. Extracellularly, S100A4 triggers cellular responses via cell surface receptors depending on the specific cell type [16, 17]. S100A4 activates TLR4 signaling promoting inflammatory responses in monocytes, as seen in rheumatoid arthritis [18]. It also regulates apoptosis through TLR4-ERK1/2 pathway [19]. In mast cells, S100A4 binds to RAGE regulating lipid metabolism and contributing to the development of allergic diseases [20]. In airway smooth muscle tissues, S100A4 interacts with RAGE to promote inflammatory responses by activating Akt and NF-κB pathways [21].

S100A4 has also been found to be associated with several nasal inflammatory diseases. In allergic rhinitis, the level of S100A4 in nasal lavage fluid is significantly elevated [22]. In CRS patients, there is a significant increase of S100A4, which is involved in tissue remodeling through regulating epithelial-mesenchymal transition of nasal epithelial cells [23]. However, it remains unclear whether S100A4 plays a role in neutrophilic inflammation in CRSwNP. In this study, we sought to examine the function of S100A4 in CRSwNP and investigate the potential involvement of TLR4 and RAGE signaling in this effect.

Methods

Patients

49 patients with CRSwNP and 30 control subjects who received endoscopic sinus surgery in Shandong Provincial ENT Hospital from August 2022 to April 2023 were enrolled in this study. The diagnosis of CRSwNP was based on European Position Paper on Rhinosinusitis and Nasal Polyps 2020 (EPOS 2020), biopsies of middle turbinate or uncinate process mucosa were obtained from control subjects without inflammatory sinonasal diseases undergoing endoscopic sinus surgery because of cerebrospinal rhinorrhea, basicranial tumor or anatomic variations. CRSwNP patients require postoperative administration of intranasal glucocorticoid spray (budesonide total daily dose: 256 μg) for at least 3 months. Preoperative CT scans were graded based on the Lund-Mackay scoring system, patients were classified into the controlled group, partially controlled group, and uncontrolled group according to EPOS 2020 criteria 1 year after surgery. None of the CRSwNP patients and controls had upper respiratory infection nor undertook any forms of glucocorticosteroids and antibiotics within one month before the study. Those who had immunodeficiency, fungal sinusitis, cystic fibrosis and aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease were excluded from the study. This study was approved by the Ethics Committees of Shandong Provincial ENT Hospital and the informed content was obtained from all patients involved. Specimens were divided into three parts and stored in RNA later, 4% paraformaldehyde solution, and reagent-free 1.5 ml tubes, respectively. All samples were immediately saved for subsequent analysis.

Immunofluorescence Staining and Histopathologic Classification

Specimens fixed in 4% buffered formalin were embedded in paraffin and paraffin blocks were sectioned at every 4 μm thickness. Tissues were retrieved in sodium citrate buffer (pH 6) for 15 min at 95 °C and then were blocked by goat serum for 90 min at room temperature. Next, the sections were incubated with the primary antibody (Table S1) for 16 h at 4 °C and a secondary antibody (Table S1) for 1.5 h at room temperature. We used the numbers of neutrophil elastase (NE)+ neutrophils and major basic protein (MBP)+ eosinophils to define neutrophilic and eosinophilic CRSwNP. Neutrophils and eosinophils were counted in 5 random fields for each tissue section at 400 × magnification. The number of neutrophils/high power field (HPF) ≥ 20 was defined as neutrophilic CRSwNP [24, 25], and the number of eosinophils/HPF ≥ 10 was described as eosinophilic CRSwNP [26].

Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT-PCR) and Endotype Classification

RNA was extracted from samples with TRIzol™ reagent as previously described [27], and cDNA was synthesized by reverse transcription. RT-PCR was performed with SYBR fluorescence reagent and specific primers (Table S2). The 2−ΔΔCT method was used to calculate the relative mRNA expression level compared with GAPDH. We classified CRSwNP into T1, T2 and T3 endotypes based on tissue mRNA levels of IFN-γ, IL-5 and IL-17A, respectively [2, 28]. The threshold was based on the 90th percentile expression in the control group.

Human Nasal Epithelial Cells (hNECs) Culture and Treatment

hNECs were derived from primary human nasal epithelial stem/progenitor cells isolated from fresh healthy control specimens (n = 3). Methods for culturing hNECs were described in previous papers [29]. After subculture, confluence occurred, and the cells were treated with or without S100A4 (2 μg/mL, Sino Biological, 10185-H08E) for 1 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, respectively [21, 30, 31]. The cell viability was detected by the trypan blue dye exclusion method. Cell samples were collected for PCR analysis, and we selected 2 μg/mL S100A4 for 24 h based on the experimental results (Fig. S1). For inhibitor and drug treatment, as previously described [32, 33], the hNECs were treated with S100A4, S100A4 + TAK-242 (1.0 µM, Med Chem Express, HY-11109), S100A4 + FPS-ZM1 (50 nM, Med Chem Express, HY-19370), S100A4 + clarithromycin (10 µM, Med Chem Express, HY-17508), S100A4 + dexamethasone (100 nM, Med Chem Express, HY-14648). TAK-242 is a selective inhibitor of the TLR4 signaling pathway. TAK-242 binds to the intracellular domain of TLR4, blocking downstream signaling (e.g., NF-κB and MAPK pathways), thereby suppressing TLR4-mediated inflammatory responses. FPS-ZM1is a selective inhibitor of RAGE, a potential receptor for the S100 protein family. FPS-ZM1 disrupts ligand binding to RAGE, inhibiting downstream signaling (e.g., Akt and NF-κB pathways). Clarithromycin is a macrolide antibiotic with anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects. It inhibits NF-κB activation to reduce the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines and modulates neutrophil functions, such as suppressing degranulation and chemotaxis. Dexamethasone is a glucocorticoid drug, binds to the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) to regulate the transcription of inflammation-related genes and inhibit the activation and infiltration of inflammatory cells. After 24 h, the cells were collected and prepared for qPCR and western blot assay. The levels of IL6 and IL-8 secreted by hNECs in the medium were determined with QuantiCyto® Human IL6 ELISA kit (EHC007.96; NeoBioscience, China) and QuantiCyto® Human IL-8 ELISA kit (EHC008.96; NeoBioscience, China) according to the manufacturer’s instruction.

Western Blot

After washing cells in the plate, 80μL lysate (RIPA: PI: Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail I: Phosphatase Inhibitor Cocktail II = 100:1:1:1) was added. The mixture was clarified by further centrifugation, and the supernatant was collected. Protein concentration was determined by BCA assay, and proteins were separated by using 15% or 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk and incubated with the primary antibody (Table S1) for 16 h and the secondary antibody (Table S1) for 1.5 h.

Statistical Analysis

Image J was used for image analysis and cell counting, and SPSS 26.0, GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 and R language were used for statistical analysis. The final data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median ± interquartile range (IQR). Data comparison was done with the t-test, one-way ANOVA or Mann–Whitney U test. The Tukey method was used to correct for multiple comparisons. Correlation analysis was performed by the Spearman rank-order method. P-value ⩽0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Increased Expression of S100A4 in Uncontrolled CRSwNP Patients

CRSwNP patients were assessed 1 year after functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) based on EPOS 2020 criteria. They were divided into controlled (n=29, 59.18%), partly controlled (n=9, 18.37%) and uncontrolled (n=11, 22.45%) groups. Compared to controlled patients, partly controlled and uncontrolled patients had higher frequency of allergic rhinitis and asthma comorbidity, and higher bilateral ethmoid sinus scores (Table S3). There was no difference in age, gender, prior surgery and symptoms between groups. We found increased levels of S100A4 mRNA by qRT-PCR and S100A4+ cell number by immunofluorescence staining in CRSwNP patients compared with control (Fig. 1A, B). Among CRSwNP patients, S100A4 mRNA expression was significantly higher in uncontrolled patients than in controlled and partial controlled patients (Fig. 1C). The increase of S100A4 in uncontrolled CRSwNP was confirmed at the protein level by immunofluorescence staining (Fig. 1D). We found that dexamethasone and clarithromycin significantly suppressed S100A4-induced IL-6 and IL-8 mRNA and protein expressions (Fig. 4H, I, J, K). Discussion

Previous research has primarily focused on the role of S100A4 in epithelial-mesenchymal transition and cancer metastasis, given its general involvement in tissue fibrosis. However, the function of S100A4 in inflammatory processes and molecular mechanism underlying these responses remain poorly understood. During inflammation, neutrophils migrate from the bloodstream to the site of inflammation, where they become activated and contribute to local inflammatory responses. Increased neutrophil infiltration has been associated with disease severity and glucocorticoid steroid resistance in CRSwNP. In this study, we found that S100A4 exacerbates neutrophilic inflammation in CRSwNP by driving the release of neutrophil chemokines (e.g., IL-8) and pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-36γ via the TLR4 signaling pathway in vitro on primary nasal epithelial cells (Fig. 6).Fig. 6Mechanistic summary diagram. In tumors, S100A4 can be induced by IL-4 and has been shown to mediate macrophage M2 polarization [38]. In glioblastoma, the expression of S100A4 is increased in response to neutrophil infiltration and neutrophil-promoting tumor progression could be blocked by S100A4 downregulation [39]. In the asthmatic murine model, S100A4 drives CD8+ T cell dysfunction, impairing the immune response [40], and S100A4 suppression attenuated eosinophilic inflammation [41]. However, the role of S100A4 in neutrophilic inflammation in CRSwNP has rarely been studied. In this study, we demonstrated that S100A4 mRNA and protein levels were significantly upregulated in CRSwNP compared with control, a finding consistent with previous reports [23, 42]. Furthermore, we found that elevated S100A4 levels were associated with poor disease control. Patients with uncontrolled CRSwNP following FESS and local steroid treatment exhibited the highest S100A4 levels, suggesting its potential as a biomarker for predicting surgical outcomes in CRSwNP patients. The significantly higher number of controlled subjects compared to partially controlled and uncontrolled aligns with the overall distribution of postoperative management outcomes in CRSwNP patients [43]. This study conducted a 1-year postoperative follow-up of 222 patients with chronic rhinosinusitis, and showed that 87.8% had well-controlled disease. Future studies could expand the sample size of partially controlled and uncontrolled groups to further validate these findings. In this study, we did not observe any correlation between S100A4 levels and Lund-Mackay scores. While the Lund-Mackay CT score is a measure of disease severity and guide treatment decisions, its utility in predicting surgical outcomes has been debated, with studies reporting both supporting and conflicting results [44, 45]. Additionally, Gargi et al. [46] found that CT score was significantly higher in the eosinophilic phenotype, highlighting their potential utility as a diagnostic predictor for eosinophilic CRSwNP. Our findings suggest that S100A4 may be associated not only with eosinophilic inflammation but also influenced by other inflammatory cell types.

The role of eosinophils and neutrophils in CRSwNP has been well-established. CRSwNP is often characterized by an eosinophilic inflammatory pattern, while recent endotype-focused studies have revealed that the most severe CRSwNP patients often exhibit a mixed eosinophilic-neutrophilic inflammation profile [47]. In this study, we found that CRSwNP with a mixed eosinophilic-neutrophilic pattern had the highest S100A4 level, which were significantly higher than those observed in eosinophilic-non neutrophilic polyps. Compared to eosinophils, S100A4 exhibits a stronger correlation with neutrophils, suggesting its significant role in the neutrophilic inflammation of CRSwNP. Previous studies have linked IL-17A to neutrophilic inflammation [48, 49], and the upregulation of S100A4 in T1 and T3 polyps further supports its involvement in regulating tissue neutrophilia. S100A4 is broadly expressed in subepithelial cells of nasal mucosa, including macrophages, lymphocytes, mast cells, eosinophils and neutrophils. Immunofluorescent staining showed that neutrophils are a major population of inflammatory cells expressing S100A4, pointing to a potential autocrine mechanism to maintain neutrophilic inflammation.

Neutrophils, which account for more than half of the circulating white blood cells in humans, are the first immune cells recruited to sites of infection or inflammation. They actively destroy foreign bodies by phagocytosis, release antimicrobial oxidants and peptides. However, the mechanisms leading to increased neutrophil infiltration in nasal polyp tissue remain unclear. The recruitment of neutrophils from the peripheral blood to tissues is primarily driven by the interaction of chemokine receptor with their ligands, such as IL-8 [50, 51]. Chemokine plays a key role in neutrophil adherence and transmigration during the inflammatory response, directing their migration to areas of elevated chemokine concentration [52]. In this study, we found that S100A4 treatment led to the upregulation of neutrophil chemokines production, but not eosinophil chemokines, in nasal epithelia cells. This result aligns with our findings from tissue samples, suggesting that S100A4 exerts a selective chemotactic effect on neutrophils in CRSwNP. Several members of the S100 family have been found to enhance neutrophilic inflammatory responses by promoting phagocytosis [53], increasing reactive oxygen species generation [54] and inducing neutrophil extracellular traps [55]. In vitro experiments using hNECs, we found that S100A4 treatment resulted in to significant increases of IL-36γ in both mRNA and protein levels, which modulates neutrophil activation [56, 57]. This result indicates that S100A4 can exaggerate neutrophilic inflammation in tissue after the cessation of neutrophil influx.

Besides, our results showed that both clarithromycin and dexamethasone effectively inhibit S100A4-induced IL-6 and IL-8 expressions in hNECs in vitro. This result contrasts with in vivo observations that neutrophilic polyps are exhibit resistance to corticosteroid treatment. The positive correlation and co-localization between S100A4 and eosinophils might explain the observed responsiveness to dexamethasone. In vitro models simulate synchronous intervention during the initial inflammatory phase, whereas clinical patients typically receive treatment after chronic inflammation has been established. Future studies should evaluate dexamethasone's anti-inflammatory effects by administering it at different timepoints relative to S100A4 stimulation (e.g., pre-treatment and 6 h/24 h/48 h post-stimulation) while establishing chronic inflammation models through sustained S100A4 exposure to simulate clinical conditions. Both TLR4 and RAGE are recognized as key receptors for S100A4, each mediating distinct signaling pathways activated by extracellular S100 proteins [21, 58]. Here we found that in hNECs S100A4 treatment induced pro-inflammatory effect through TLR4 but not RAGE in vitro, as evidenced by the inhibitory effects of TLR4-specific and RAGE-specific inhibitors.

Our study has several limitations. Due to the short lifespan of neutrophils, we were unable to isolate and examine tissue-derived neutrophils in response to S100A4 stimulation. Future research exploring the role of S100A4 in neutrophil function would be valuable. In addition, we used uncinate/turbinate tissue as a control. Ideally, sinus mucosa would be more appropriate, but based on feedback from our ethics review board, this approach for healthy controls was deemed unethical. In fact, uncinate/turbinate tissue has been commonly used in the literature as control tissue [59], which partially validates our sampling methods.

In conclusion, our study identifies S100A4 as a key player in neutrophilic CRSwNP, with elevated levels associated with poor disease control. S100A4 exerts its pro-inflammatory effect on nasal epithelial cells via TLR4 signaling cascade, contributing to the production of inflammatory chemokines and cytokines that promote neutrophil infiltration and activation. The collective findings reveal an important role of S100A4 in developing nasal neutrophilic inflammation, and highlight its potential as a novel therapeutic target for managing neutrophilic CRSwNP.

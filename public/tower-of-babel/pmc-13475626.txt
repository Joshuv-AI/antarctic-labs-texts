Clinical Outcomes Following Bilateral Implantation of a Next-Generation Intraocular Lens in Patients with Primary Open Angle Glaucoma
Authors: Camerynn L Keahi, Carlton Yuen, Caitlin K Yuen, Alana K Dunn, Brad Hall, Corban Yuen, Jason Tokunaga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the clinical outcomes following bilateral TECNIS Odyssey implantation in eyes with primary open angle glaucoma (POAG) targeted for plano.MethodsThis was a retrospective, single-site, single arm, observational study of visual outcomes following bilateral TECNIS Odyssey implantation in eyes with POAG. Outcome measures included monocular and binocular uncorrected and distance corrected visual acuities at distance (UDVA, CDVA) and near (UNVA, DCNVA; 40 cm), manifest refraction spherical equivalent (MRSE) and patient self-reported spectacle independence.ResultsData from a total of 26 patients were included. The percentage of eyes achieving 20/25 or better was 90% (47/52) for UDVA, 87% (44/52) for UNVA, and 98% (51/52) for CDVA. The percentages of eyes with ≤ 0.5 D of MRSE and residual astigmatism were 100% (52/52) and 100% (52/52), respectively. For self-reported spectacle independence, 8% (2/26) of subjects reported using glasses for near. In addition, 8% (2/26) of subjects reported moderate halos.ConclusionThe results of this study suggest that patients with POAG implanted with the Odyssey IOL had good photopic visual acuity at distance, and near, excellent refractive outcomes, high levels of spectacle independence, and low levels of halos.Keywords: cataract, Odyssey, continuous range of vision, POAG

Introduction

Cataracts and glaucoma are two commonly encountered diseases that can impair eyesight. Cataracts are the leading cause of reversible blindness,1 while primary open angle glaucoma (POAG) is the main cause of irreversible blindness.2 Cataracts affect between 1% of 20–39‑year‑olds to more than 88% of people over the age of 60,3 and POAG has a worldwide prevalence of 2.4%.2 The prevalence of both conditions increases with age1,2 with 22.6% of patients undergoing cataract surgery also suffering from glaucoma or ocular hypertension.4

The most common method of replacing the crystalline lens during cataract surgery is to insert a monofocal intraocular lens (IOL). One disadvantage of a monofocal IOL is that it only provides good vision when looking far away, thus patients may require additional refractive correction to see clearly at intermediate and near. Intermediate vision can be improved by changing the optic design to create an enhanced monofocal IOL.5

Other options to improve intermediate and near vision following cataract surgery are multifocal and extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) IOLs. Multifocal IOLs split light into different focal points to provide clear vision at distance, intermediate, and near,6,7 whereas EDOF IOLs have a single continuous focal point to provide clear vision at all distances.8 The disadvantages of multifocal and EDOF lenses include reduced mesopic contrast sensitivity and increased photic phenomena compared to monofocal IOLs,9 which can cause starbursts, halos, and glare.10,11 These problems are of particular concern to patients with glaucoma, which may already have reduced contrast sensitivity.12 In addition, a decrease in contrast sensitivity and greater visual disturbances may be particularly bothersome in patients with glaucomatous visual loss.1,13

The TECNIS Odyssey IOL (Johnson & Johnson Vision, Jacksonville, Florida, USA) is a relatively recent option for patients desiring increased spectacle independence. The Odyssey IOL aims to provide a full range of vision by combining the design technology of multifocal and EDOF IOLs.14 Its design is based on the TECNIS Synergy (Johnson & Johnson Vision, Jacksonville, Florida, USA), which is a hybrid design of a bifocal and an EDOF IOL.14 The Odyssey IOL contains a custom echelette design and 50% reduced step heights,14,15 with the goal to decrease problems associated with photic phenomena. The design of the IOL intends to correct chromatic aberrationand improve contrast sensitivity and near visual acuity.16

To date, there are limited data on the effect of performing cataract surgery with the Odyssey IOL in patients with POAG. The purpose of this study was to investigate the performance of the Odyssey IOL in patients with mild or moderate glaucoma.

Methods

This was a retrospective chart review of clinical outcomes following Odyssey implantation in eyes with POAG targeted for plano. As a retrospective chart review, the study was reviewed and granted an exemption of oversight by Advarra IRB (Pro00090590), which did not require informed consent. Confidentiality was maintained for all patient data. The study was not registered in a clinical trials database, as it was a retrospective study. The tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice (GCP), and International Harmonization (ICH) guidelines were adhered to.

Inclusion criteria were adults with a diagnosis of mild or moderate POAG in each eye, who underwent age-related cataract surgery with expected best-corrected visual outcomes of 20/25 or better. Mild POAG was defined as mean deviation greater than −6.0 db with central 5 degrees spared. Moderate POAG was defined as mean deviation between −6 and −12 db, and only one hemifield affected. All glaucoma patients had well‑controlled intraocular pressure. Exclusion criteria were moderate-severe corneal pathology, irregular astigmatism, preexisting macular disease and other retinal degenerative diseases, severe dry eye disease, history of uveitis, ocular herpes simplex virus, nystagmus, amblyopia, strabismus, zonular laxity or dehiscence, pseudoexfoliation, or prior corneal refractive surgery in either eye. Consecutive charts were reviewed from December 2023 through August 2025 to identify patients meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria above. For these cases, all surgeries used the Barrett Universal II IOL power calculation formula, and all eyes underwent femtosecond assisted laser cataract surgery. Eyes with less than 1.0 D of astigmatism received arcuate incisions, and eyes with 1.0 D of greater of astigmatism received a toric IOL. Cataract surgeries were not combined with other procedures, such as minimally invasive glaucoma surgery.

Postoperative data were collected for binocular uncorrected and distance-corrected visual acuity at distance (UDVA, CDVA) and near (UNVA, DCNVA; 40 cm). Monocular UDVA, UNVA, and CDVA were also collected, as were data on refractive outcomes. Data on spectacle independence and halos were gathered from patient self-reports and not standardized questionnaires.

All statistical analysis was performed using R (version 4.4.2; The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The study was intended to be descriptive. We estimated that at least 25 subjects would also for sufficient data to characterize the performance of the Odyssey in eyes with mild or moderate POAG.

Results

A total of 26 subjects were eligible for inclusion following the chart review. This included 58% (15/26) female subjects, and 31% of eyes (16/52) received toric IOLs. The average age of subjects was 74.4 ± 6.2 year, with an average follow‑up time of 67.6 ± 25.8 days.

The percentages of eyes with ≤ 0.5 D of MRSE and residual astigmatism were 100% (52/52) and 100% (52/52), respectively. In addition, the percentage of eyes achieving 20/25 or better were 90% (47/52) for UDVA, 87% (44/52) for UNVA, and 98% (51/52) for CDVA. Discussion

The number of patients undergoing cataract surgery who desire spectacle independence at all distances is increasing. The purpose of this study was to investigate the performance of a new IOL that corrects for presbyopia by combining the technology of multifocal and EDOF correction in a group of patients with mild-to-moderate glaucoma. Previous studies have shown that the Odyssey IOL provides a continuous range of vision,16,17 good contrast sensitivity,16 tolerance to residual refractive error,18 and low amounts of glare and starbursts.14 The design of the lens includes a nonuniform distribution with smoother echelettes to improve contrast sensitivity and to decrease light scatter and photic phenomena, which could be particularly useful for eyes with comorbidities, such as glaucoma. The results of our study suggest that patients with mild to moderate POAG can have good clinical outcomes with Odyssey implantation. However, given that glaucoma is a progressive disease, clinicians should exercise caution when implanting in eyes with severe or poorly controlled glaucoma.

This study found that under photopic conditions, mean binocular UDVA, CDVA, UNVA, and DCNVA were 0.01 logMAR (20/20.5 Snellen equivalent), 0.01 logMAR (20/20.5 Snellen equivalent), −0.01 logMAR (20/19.5 Snellen equivalent), and −0.01 logMAR (20/19.5 Snellen equivalent), respectively. To the best of our knowledge, there are no other studies that have reported on the outcome of the Odyssey IOL in patients with POAG. However, there are other studies with the Odyssey IOL in non-glaucomatous eyes. In a prospective study of 30 patients, Borges et al15 observed that mean binocular UDVA, CDVA, and DCNVA 40 cm were 0.02 logMAR, 0.00 logMAR, and 0.01, logMAR, respectively. Hacopian et al,17 in an ambispective study of 40 patients, reported that mean binocular UDVA, UNVA 40 cm, CDVA, and DCNVA 40 cm were −0.07 logMAR, 0.03 logMAR, −0.07, logMAR, and 0.02, logMAR, respectively, at 3 months postoperatively. Finally, in a retrospective study of 25 patients, Bissen-Miyajima et al14 observed that postoperative mean binocular UDVA, UNVA 40 cm, CDVA, and DCNVA 40 cm were −0.18 logMAR, 0.00 logMAR, −0.21, logMAR, and 0.00, logMAR, respectively.

In eyes with POAG, there are published studies with the TECNIS Symfony IOL (Johnson & Johnson Vision, Jacksonville, Florida, USA). The Symfony is an EDOF IOL with an aspheric front surface and diffractive echelette back surface design.19 There are also published studies with the AcrySof IQ Vivity IOL (Alcon Vision, LLC, Fort Worth, Texas, USA), a non-diffractive EDOF IOL approved by the US Food & Drug Administration in 2020.20 Therefore, we offer the results of these EDOF IOL studies in eyes with POAG for context.

Three months after monocular implantation with the Symphony IOL, Bissen-Mijajima et al21,22 recorded a mean monocular CDVA of −0.17 logMAR, with all eyes achieving UDVA of 20/40 and CDVA of 20/20 and 100% achieving UDVA of 20/40. The monocular UNVA and DCNVA at 40 cm were 0.16 and 0.19 logMAR, respectively. Near visual acuity was good with 95% seeing 20/40 and 86% reaching the same level of acuity with distance correction.

Three months after bilateral implantation of Vivity IOLs, Guarro et al23 reported a mean binocular UDVA, CDVA, UNVA, and DCNVA of 0.01 logMAR, −0.01 logMAR, 0.23 logMAR, and 0.26 logMAR, respectively, from 27 patients with early to moderate POAG. Ferguson et al20 examined the effect of placing Vivity IOLs in both eyes of 26 patients with mild open-angle glaucoma, with 77% of subjects also receiving a trabecular microbypass device during the surgery. Four months after the surgery, the mean binocular UDVA, CDVA, and UNVA were 0.03 logMAR, −0.06 logMAR, and 0.31 logMAR respectively. Kerr et al24 conducted a retrospective study in 29 patients with early POAG and 3 patients with secondary open-angle glaucoma. Mean monocular UDVA, CDVA, and UNVA were 0.12 logMAR, 0.01 logMAR, and 0.29 logMAR, respectively. Urcola et al25 investigated the effect of bilateral implantation of Vivity IOLs in 36 patients with mild POAG. The multisite study found a mean binocular CDVA of 0.08 logMAR, with a corrected near visual acuity of 0.24 logMAR at 40 cm. Liu et al26 conducted a prospective study examining 31 patients (55 eyes) with different types of mild glaucoma or ocular hypertension that had been given Vivity IOLs, and observed that UDVA was 0.02 logMAR three months post-surgery with UNVA of 0.27 logMAR. Rementería-Capelo et al27 examined the effect of putting Vivity IOLs into each eye of patients with various anterior and posterior segment ocular pathology, including six people with glaucoma. Four of the glaucoma patients also had a trabecular meshwork stent inserted during the cataract surgery. Mean binocular UDVA and CDVA of all the patients with ocular pathology were −0.05 and −0.06 logMAR, respectively.

The goal of implanting presbyopia-correcting IOLs is spectacle independence, particularly at intermediate and near. In our study, only 8% of patients required spectacles for near vision with the Odyssey IOL. Other studies with the Odyssey IOL in non-glaucomatous eyes have reported high spectacle independence.14,15,17 For the Vivity IOL, Urcola et al25 found 47.2% of patients with POAG never or rarely needed glasses to see up close. Kerr et al24 reported 56.2% required spectacles for near, Ferguson et al20 reported that 62% needed glasses to use their mobile phone, and Rementería-Capelo et al27 found 60% of subjects with mixed ocular disease sometimes or always required glasses for near with the Vivity IOL.

Moderate halos were noticed by 8% of patients in this study, and halos have been reported by other studies of the Odyssey IOL in non-glaucomatous eyes.14,15,17 Halos can be particularly problematic when driving at night. For the Vivity IOL, Kerr et al24 found that only 6.3% noticed from halos, which were only somewhat bothersome. However, Liu et al26 reported that approximately 35% of subjects experienced halos, with no change in severity compared to before cataract surgery was performed. Urcola et al21 reported 20.6% saw from halos, which were no worse than mild in severity. Lastly, Rementería-Capelo et al27 found 60% of subjects with mixed ocular pathology sometimes or often experienced halos.

The main limitation of this study was the retrospective study design. A retrospective design does allow for data to be gathered quickly compared to prospective study, however, a prospective study may provide more robust data. As a retrospective study, we were also limited to data that was already collected as part of routine clinical care, and notably we do not have data for intermediate visual acuity, contrast sensitivity, or responses on a validated questionnaire. Another limitation was the short two-month follow-up period which may not provide long-term data, but does give an indication of the clinical outcomes following Odyssey implantation. The lack of a control group, such as non-glaucomatous eyes or comparator IOL was another limitation of our study. Despite the limitations, this study demonstrated that the Odyssey IOL can successfully be used in patients with mild to moderate POAG. This allows for future longer-term prospective randomized control trials to be conducted with larger sample sizes.

Conclusion

The results of this study suggest that patients with POAG implanted with the Odyssey IOL had good photopic visual acuity at distance and near, excellent refractive outcomes, high levels of spectacle independence, and low levels of halos.

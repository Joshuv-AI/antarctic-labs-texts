Kakeya-Nikodym norms of Maass forms on U ⁡ ( 2 , 1 ) \rm{U}(2,1)
Jiaqi Hou

Abstract
. Let ψ \psi be a Hecke-Maass form with a large spectral parameter on a compact arithmetic complex hyperbolic surface. We apply the amplification method to obtain a power saving over the trivial bound for the Kakeya-Nikodym norm of ψ \psi . As a consequence, we obtain power savings over the local bound of Sogge for ‖ ψ ‖ p \|\psi\|_{p} when 2 < p < 10 / 3 2<p<10/3 .



1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
, and let
\Delta
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
.
If
\psi
is a Laplace eigenfunction on
M

satisfying
\Delta\psi+\lambda^{2}\psi=0
with
\lambda\geq 0
and
\|\psi\|_{2}=1
,
a classical result on
\|\psi\|_{p}
is due to Sogge
[
23
]
(see also Avakumović
[
1
]
and Levitan
[
18
]
when
p=\infty
),
and is

(1.1)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{p}\ll\lambda^{\delta(p,n)},

where
\delta(p,n)
is given by

\delta(p,n)=\begin{cases}\frac{n-1}{4}-\frac{n-1}{2p}\;&\text{ for }\;2\leq p\leq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1},\\
\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac{n}{p}\;\;\;\;&\text{ for }\;\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty.\end{cases}

When
M
is the round sphere, the above estimates are saturated by the zonal spherical harmonics for
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
and by the highest weight spherical harmonics for
2<p\leq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
. However, it is expected that (
1.1
) can be improved under extra geometric assumptions on
M
.
For instance, there are log improvements if
M
has nonpositive sectional curvature, by the work of Bérard
[
2
]
, Hassell and Tacy
[
13
]
, and Blair and Sogge
[
5
,
6
]
.

In this paper, we will focus on the problem on improving (
1.1
) in the range

(1.2)

\displaystyle 2<p<\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}

based on the Kakeya-Nikodym bounds for eigenfunctions by Blair-Sogge
[
4
]
.
Let
\Pi
denote the space of unit length geodesic segments in
M
.
If
\gamma\in\Pi
, we denote the
\lambda^{-1/2}
-neighborhood of
\gamma
in
M
by
\mathscr{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)
.
Following
[
4
]
, the Kakeya-Nikodym norm of
\psi
is defined as

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}=\sup_{\gamma\in\Pi}\|\psi|_{\mathscr{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)}\|_{2}=\left(\sup_{\gamma\in\Pi}\int_{\mathscr{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2},

which measures the concentration of
eigenfunctions on geodesic tubes. These norms were introduced by Sogge
[
24
]
. Since we are assuming the eigenfunctions to be
L^{2}
-normalized, we always have the trivial upper bound

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}\leq\|\psi\|_{2}=1.

The trivial bound
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}\ll 1
is sharp for general Riemannian manifolds, which are saturated by the highest weight spherical harmonics on the round spheres as well.

Blair and Sogge
[
4
]
showed that improvements on the trivial bound
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}\ll 1
will give improvements on the
L^{p}
bounds (
1.1
) for
p
below the critical exponent,
that is, for
2(n+2)/n<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{p}\ll\lambda^{\delta(p,n)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}^{\frac{2(n+1)}{p(n-1)}-1}.

When
n\geq 3
, the above result has recently been extended by Gao, Wu, and Xi
[
11
]
for the larger range
2(3n+1)/(3n-3)<p<2(n+1)/(n-1)
.
See also
[
10
,
24
,
3
]
for the related results in dimension two.
By using Toponogov’s comparison theorem from Riemannian geometry, Blair and Sogge
[
5
]
obtained log improvements for the Kakeya–Nikodym norms when
M
has nonpositive curvature. Using these and (
1.3
), they were able to obtain log improvements over Sogge’s
L^{p}
-bounds (
1.1
) for such manifolds when
p
satisfies (
1.2
).

In the arithmetic setting, we have further improvements over the trivial bound
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}\ll 1
.
Now we let
M
be a compact congruence arithmetic hyperbolic surface and let
\psi
be an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke-Maass form. In
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
, Marshall proved an
L^{2}
-bound for geodesic restrictions:

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma\in\Pi}\|\psi|_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\ll_{\epsilon}\lambda^{3/14+\epsilon},

which implies that

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}\ll_{\epsilon}\lambda^{-1/28+\epsilon}.

Combining the above estimate with the main theorem of
[
3
]
, Marshall
[
20
, Corollary 1.2]
therefore obtained an improvement over the local bound
\|\psi\|_{4}\ll\lambda^{1/8}
of Sogge (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{4}\ll_{\epsilon}\lambda^{1/8-1/56+\epsilon}.

The corresponding result about the Kakeya-Nikodym norms of Hecke-Maass forms on compact arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifolds was proved in
[
16
]
. In this paper, we will prove a similar result for Hecke-Maass forms on compact arithmetic complex hyperbolic surfaces.
All of these results were based on the method of arithmetic amplification, which was introduced by Iwaniec and Sarnak
[
17
]
to study
L^{\infty}
-norms of Hecke-Maass forms on arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces. See e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, for the bound of
L^{\infty}
-norms of Hecke-Maass forms on other groups via the method of arithmetic amplification.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a compact arithmetic congruence complex hyperbolic surface and
let
\psi
be an
L^{2}
-normalized Hecke–Maass form on
X
with spectral parameter
\lambda>0
(see §
2
for precise definitions).
Then for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{KN}\ll\lambda^{-1/28+\epsilon},

where the implied constant depends only on
X
and
\epsilon
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X
and
\psi
be the same as in Theorem
1.1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{p}\ll_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\delta(p,4)-(10/3p-1)/28+\epsilon}

if
26/9<p<10/3
. Here the implied constant depends only on
X
,
p
and
\epsilon
.

This corollary is obtained by directly applying Theorem
1.1
and the Kakeya-Nikodym bounds (
1.3
).
Moreover, we can interpolate the above bounds with
\|\psi\|_{2}=1
to prove power savings over the local
L^{p}
-bounds (
1.1
) for
X
and
\psi
with any
2<p<10/3
.

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t^{n}}b_{2}(t)f_{+}(t,s)\ll_{n}\lambda^{-3/2}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{3/2}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{n}=\lambda^{-3/2}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{n+3/2}.

Moreover, by (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), for any
n\geq 1
, it may be seen that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial t_{2}^{n}}\mathcal{A}(z,\tau,\beta^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}t_{2})\ll(1+\lambda^{-1}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{n+1})(\beta^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}})^{n}\ll\beta^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\ll 1.

Therefore, by the chain rule to higher derivatives, for any
n\geq 0
we obtain that the
n
-th derivative of the amplitude function
\alpha(t_{2})
is bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{n}\alpha}{\partial t_{2}^{n}}\ll_{n}\beta^{-1/2+\epsilon_{0}}\lambda^{-3/2}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{n+3/2}=\lambda^{-3/2}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{n+1/2}.

Integrating by parts
n
times gives that the integral (
6.12
), which has been written as the form of the oscillatory integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}\alpha(t_{2})e^{i(s+s_{2})^{1/2+\epsilon_{0}}f(t_{2})}dt_{2}
, is bounded by

\displaystyle\ll_{n}

\displaystyle|s+s_{2}|^{-n(1/2+\epsilon_{0})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{2}}\frac{1}{\partial f/\partial t_{2}}\right)^{n}\alpha\right|dt_{2}

\displaystyle\ll_{n}

\displaystyle|s+s_{2}|^{-n(1/2+\epsilon_{0})}\lambda^{-3/2}(\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}})^{n+1/2}\beta^{1/2-\epsilon_{0}}\ll|s+s_{2}|^{-n\epsilon_{0}}.

In summary, (
6.12
) is
\ll_{A,\epsilon_{0}}|s+s_{2}|^{-A}
. The second inner integral (
6.13
) can be bounded in the same way by replacing
s+s_{2}
with
s-s_{2}
so (
6.13
) is
\ll_{A,\epsilon_{0}}|s-s_{2}|^{-A}
. Hence, with this estimate and the fact
|s\pm s_{2}|\gg\beta
, by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Plancherel theorem, we obtain (
6.5
).

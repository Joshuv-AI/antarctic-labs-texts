Supercritical phase of the random connection model
Mathew D. PenroseUniversity of Bath

Abstract
Given d ∈ ℕ , λ > 0 d\in\mathbb{N},\lambda>0 , the random connection model in a region A ⊆ ℝ d A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} is a graph with vertex set given by a homogeneous Poisson point process of intensity λ \lambda in A A , with an edge placed between each pair x , y x,y of vertices with probability ϕ ⁡ ( ‖ x − y ‖ ) \phi(\|x-y\|) , where ϕ : ℝ + → [ 0 , 1 ] \phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to[0,1] is a nonincreasing finite-range connection function. We show that if d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and λ \lambda is strictly supercritical for A = ℝ d A=\mathbb{R}^{d} , then the model remains supercritical if it is restricted to a region A A of the form ℝ 2 × [ − K / 2 , K / 2 ] d − 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-K/2,K/2]^{d-2} , provided K K is sufficiently large. This is a continuum analogue of a well-known result of Grimmett and Marstrand for lattice percolation.

1
Introduction and statement of results

An important result in high-dimensional percolation theory says that for
Bernoulli site (or bond) percolation in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 3
,
if the density of open sites (or bonds) is supercritical, it
remains supercritical when restricted to an infinite slab of
sufficiently large thickness. This result was proved by Grimmett and
Marstrand
[
5
]
. It is important, for example, because it enables one
to prove exponential decay for the probability of large finite
clusters, and finite correlation lengths, in the whole of the
supercritical phase.

As Grimmett and Marstrand say, their methods and conclusions
‘are valid for a large category of processes including many bond
and site processes on crystalline lattices’.
In the present paper we derive an analogous result for a model of

continuum
percolation, namely the
random connection model

in which a graph on Poisson points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is created by connecting any two points with probability dependent
on how far apart they are (described in more detail below).
While the random connection model was introduced some time ago
[
12
,
10
]
,
there has been renewed interest recently in this model and generalizations
thereof
[
14
,
6
,
3
]
because
of their importance in general network modelling and telecommunications
modelling.

Although some continuum analogues of
Grimmett and Marstrand’s
result have previously been developed (discussed later),
the random connection
model seems to require a more sophisticated (or at least, different)
adaptation of their methods
than do previous results of this sort in the continuum.

We now describe our model in more detail.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and let

\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
be a
nonincreasing function, known as the
connection function
.
Also let
p\in[1,\infty]

and let
\|\cdot\|
be the
\ell^{p}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Given a locally finite point set
{\cal X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
let

G({\cal X},\phi)
denote the graph with vertex set
{\cal X}
, where
for each
\{x,y\}\subset{\cal X}
, the
edge
xy
is included with probability

\phi(\|y-x\|)
,
independently of
the other pairs.

Given
\lambda>0
,
let
{\cal H}_{\lambda}
denote a homogeneous Poisson point process
of intensity
\lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(viewed as a random set of points
rather than a measure).
Given also
s>0
, set
\Lambda_{s}:=[-s,s]^{d}

and let
{\cal H}_{\lambda,s}:={\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{s}
.
We are interested in the graphs
G({\cal H}_{\lambda},\phi)
and

G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi)
, which are
known as the
random connection model

[
10
]

and
soft random geometric graph

[
14
]

respectively, with connection function
\phi
.

Let
{\cal H}^{{\bf o}}_{\lambda}
denote the point process
{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cup\{{\bf o}\}
,
where
{\bf o}
is the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Given Borel
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

with
{\bf o}\in A
,
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\pi_{k}(\phi,\lambda,A)
denote the probability that
the component of
G({\cal H}^{{\bf o}}_{\lambda}\cap A,\phi)
containing
the origin is of
order
k
.
The
percolation probability
in
A
, denoted

\theta(\phi,\lambda,A)
is
the probability that
{\bf o}
lies in an
infinite component of the graph
G({\cal H}^{{\bf o}}_{\lambda}\cap A,\phi)
, that
is,

\theta(\phi,\lambda,A):=1-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\pi_{k}(\phi,\lambda,A).

A standard coupling argument shows that
\theta(\phi,\lambda,A)
is nondecreasing in
\lambda
.
The
critical value

(continuum percolation threshold)

\lambda_{\rm c}(\phi,A)
is defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{\rm c}(\phi,A):=\inf\{\lambda>0:\theta(\phi,\lambda,A)>0\}.

(1.1)

It is known (see
[
10
]
) that

0<\lambda_{\rm c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})<\infty
,
provided
0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(\|x\|)dx<\infty
.

Given
M\in(0,\infty)
,
let
S_{M}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-M/2,M/2]^{d-2}
(an infinite
slab of thickness
M
). We now state
our main result, which is
an analogue for the random connection model
of the classic result of Grimmett and Marstrand
[
5
]

for lattice percolation.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
d\geq 3

and
\sup\{r>0:\phi(r)>0\}\in(0,\infty)
.
Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})=\lim_{M\to\infty}\lambda_{c}(\phi,S_{M}).

(1.2)

As discussed in
[
5
]

Grimmett and Marstrand’s result has many applications in lattice
percolation theory, and we would expect Theorem
1.1

to have a similar range of applications for the random connection
model. Here we discuss just one such application, to the
giant
component
phenomenon in soft random geometric graphs.

For any finite graph
G
, let
L_{j}(G)
denote the order of
its
j
th-largest component, that is, the
j
th-largest of the
orders of its components, or zero if it has fewer than
j

components. In
[
15
]
we proved the following results about
convergence in probability of
s^{-d}L_{j}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))
for

j=1,2
, as
s\to\infty
with
\phi,\lambda
fixed.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
15
]

Suppose
d\geq 2
. Suppose

\lambda>0
with
\theta(\phi,\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d})=0
.
Then

\displaystyle s^{-d}L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0{\rm~~~~~~as~~}s\to\infty.

(1.3)

Theorem 1.3
.

[
15
]

Suppose
d=2
.
Suppose
\sup\{r>0:\phi(r)>0\}\in(0,\infty)

and
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi)
. Then
as
s\to\infty
we have that

\displaystyle(2s)^{-d}L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda\theta(\phi,\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d})

(1.4)

and

s^{-d}L_{2}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P}}{{\longrightarrow}}0.

Theorem
1.2
was stated in
[
15
]
for
d=2
only, but
the proof carries over to arbitrary dimensions.
The proof of Theorem
1.3
in
[
15
]
, on the other hand, is
very specific to 2 dimensions.

In the case with
\phi={\bf 1}_{[0,1]}
, the extension
of Theorem
1.3

to
d\geq 3

was already proved in
[
16
]
.
The proof given there
(see also
[
11
]
)
relies on a continuum analogue of
Grimmett and Marstrand’s result.
Using Theorem
1.1
,
it may to be possible
to develop an analogue of Theorem
1.3

for
d\geq 3
. In fact there has already been progress towards this;
before describing this, we introduce more notation.

For
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, set
x+A:=\{a+x:a\in A\}
. Also set

\Lambda_{r}(x):=x+\Lambda_{r}
.

Given
A,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and locally finite
{\cal X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we write
\{A\leftrightarrow B
in
G({\cal X},\phi)\}

for the event that there exist
x\in{\cal X}\cap A
and
y\in{\cal X}\cap B

such that there is a path in
G({\cal X},\phi)
from
x
to
y
.
We write
\{A\leftrightarrow\infty
in
G({\cal X},\phi)\}

for the intersection over all
n\geq 1
of events

\{A\leftrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Lambda_{n}
in
G({\cal X},\phi)\}
.

Küpper
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
proves a
weaker version of Theorem
1.3
in dimension
d\geq 3
, namely

\lim_{s\to\infty}\Big(\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[L_{1}(G({\cal H}_{\lambda,s},\phi))]}{(2s)^{d}}\Big)=\lambda\theta(\phi,\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d}),

under the assumption (not proved in
[
7
]
) that

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\log({\mathbb{P}}[\{\Lambda_{r}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda},\phi)\}^{c}])}{\log r}\Big)<0.

(1.5)

As a corollary of Theorem
1.1
we
can show this assumption is justified.

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose
d\geq 3
and
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\to\infty}r^{2-d}\left(\log{\mathbb{P}}[\{\Lambda_{r}\leftrightarrow\infty~{\rm in}~G({\cal H}_{\lambda},\phi)\}^{c}]\right)<0,

(1.6)

and in particular (
1.5
) holds.

Proof.

Using Theorem
1.1
, choose
M\in(0,\infty)

such that
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi,S_{M})
.
Then given
r>M
we can find
x_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{\lfloor(2r/M)^{d}\rfloor}
such that
x_{1}+S_{M},\ldots,x_{\lfloor(2r/M)^{d}\rfloor}+S_{M}
are subsets of
\Lambda_{r}
with disjoint interiors.
Then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\{\Lambda_{r}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})\}^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}[\cap_{i=1}^{\lfloor(2r/M)^{d}\rfloor}\{\Lambda_{r}\cap(x_{i}+S_{M})\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap(x_{i}+S_{M}))\}^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor(2r/M)^{d}\rfloor}(1-{\mathbb{P}}[{\bf o}\leftrightarrow\infty~{\rm in}~G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap S_{M})]),

and since
\theta(\phi,\lambda,S_{M})>0
,
(
1.6
)
follows. Then (
1.5
) is immediate.
∎

In the case with
\phi={\bf 1}_{[0,1]}
, the result
(
1.2
) was already proved by Tanemura
[
17
]
, since in
this case the random connection model is equivalent to the
Boolean model with balls of fixed radius as considered
in
[
17
]
. More recently
the method
of
[
5
]
has been adapted to a larger class of Boolean
models in continuum percolation by
Faggionato and Mimun in
[
2
]
, although
that paper does not include an explicit statement of a
slab result along the lines of Theorem
1.1
,
being more concerned with conductances.
In any event, the random connection model with
more general connection functions than

{\bf 1}_{[0,1]}
is not covered
by the Boolean models considered in
[
2
,
17
]
.

Both in
[
17
]
and
[
2
]
the continuum model
is dealt with by a fine discretization of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into small
cubes, allowing the authors to directly apply a version of Grimmett
and Marstrand’s result for site percolation, albeit
on a more complicated
lattice than the usual nearest-neighbour lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In the present
instance of the random connection model with more general
\phi
,
it is not clear that such a discretization argument works, since
the cubes in the discretization can be multiply occupied meaning
that no convenient site-bond percolation approximation
seems to be available.
Our proof instead uses a coarser discretization
into unit cubes for adapting some of the counting arguments
in
[
5
]
, while at the same time staying in the continuum for
the proof as a whole.

Our method should work for a slightly wider class of connection
functions
\phi
. Provided there exists
\delta>0

such that
\phi(r)>\delta
for
0<r<\delta
,
by only minor changes to the proof
we should be able to drop the assumption that

\phi
is nonincreasing. However the assumption that

\phi
has finite range is essential to our proof, so
finding a way to drop that assumption would be
of great interest. Another
possible generalization would be to allow for
{\cal H}_{\lambda}

to be a
marked
Poisson process, with the connection
function allowed to depend on the marks of the endpoints.
This generalization has been considered in some of the recent
literature on this kind of model
(see e.g.
[
3
,
7
]
)
but we do not address it here.

Preliminaries and lemmata

We introduce some further notation that will be used in proving
Theorem
1.1
.

From now on we assume that
d\geq 3
and

\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is nonincreasing
with

\sup\{r>0:\phi(x)>0\}=1
.
We shall prove Theorem
1.1

only for this case, since simple scaling arguments then yield the general
finite-range case in the statement of the theorem.

We shall view
\phi
as fixed from now on, and
for any locally finite
{\cal X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

write simply
G({\cal X})
instead of
G({\cal X},\phi)
.
For
x\in{\cal X}
,
let
{\cal C}_{x}({\cal X})
denote the vertex set of the
component of
G({\cal X})
containing
x
(these components are
often called
clusters
).
Also given finite
{\cal Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we write
{\cal C}_{\cal Y}({\cal X})
for
\cup_{y\in{\cal Y}}{\cal C}_{x}({\cal X}\cup{\cal Y})

(here
{\cal Y}
might or might not be a subset of
{\cal X}
).
We shall refer to
{\cal C}_{\cal Y}({\cal X})
as a

set-cluster
; it is a finite union of clusters, associated
with the point set
{\cal Y}
.

For
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
,
let
e_{i}
denote the
i
th coordinate unit vector.

We say a real-valued function
f
, defined on graphs

({\cal V},E)
, with

{\cal V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
locally finite, is
increasing
if
f({\cal V},E)\leq f({\cal V}^{\prime},E^{\prime})

whenever
{\cal V}\subset{\cal V}^{\prime}
and
E\subset E^{\prime}
. We say
f
is
decreasing

if
-f
is increasing. Given
\lambda>0
and given
\phi
,
we say an event
{\cal E}

on
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})

is
increasing
(resp. decreasing)
if
{\bf 1}_{\cal E}

is an increasing (resp. decreasing)
function of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})
.

Lemma 2.1

(Harris-FKG inequality)
.

Suppose
f,g
are measurable bounded increasing real-valued
functions
defined on graphs
({\cal V},E)
with
{\cal V}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
locally finite.
Then

{\mathbb{E}}[f(G({\cal H}_{\lambda}))g(G({\cal H}_{\lambda}))]\geq{\mathbb{E}}[f(G({\cal H}_{\lambda}))]{\mathbb{E}}[g(G({\cal H}_{\lambda}))].

The same inequality holds if
f
and
g
are both decreasing.

Proof.

See
[
6
]
, where measurability issues are also dealt with.
∎

Given
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
following
[
5
]
we define the sets

\displaystyle T(n)

\displaystyle:=\{x\in(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}:x\cdot e_{1}=n-\tfrac{1}{2},0<x\cdot e_{j}<n{\rm~for~}j=2,3,\ldots,d\};

\displaystyle T(n,m)

\displaystyle:=[n,n+m]\times[0,n]^{d-1},

(2.1)

and similarly

\displaystyle T_{2}(n):=\{x\in(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}:x\cdot e_{2}=n-\tfrac{1}{2},0<x\cdot e_{j}<n,{\rm~for~}j=1,3,\ldots,d\};

\displaystyle T_{2}(n,m):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:n\leq x\cdot e_{2}\leq n+m,0\leq x\cdot e_{j}\leq n,{\rm~for~}j=1,3,\ldots,d\}.

Next we adapt
[
5
, Lemma 4]
. For finite
A
we write

\#A
for the number of elements of
A
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\varepsilon}>0
,
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k>r
,
define the random set

\displaystyle V(r,k):=\{x\in T(k):\Lambda_{r}\leftrightarrow\Lambda_{1/2}(x){\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k})\}.

(2.2)

Then there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 9
such that
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\#V(m,k)<\ell]<{\varepsilon}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Since we assume
\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we have
\theta(\phi,\lambda,\mathbb{R}^{d})>0
,
so
{\mathbb{P}}[N_{\infty}>0]>0
,
where
N_{\infty}
denotes the number of unbounded
components of
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})
. Hence by
the zero-one law for the random connection model

[
10
, Theorem 2.14 and Proposition 2.6]
,

{\mathbb{P}}[N_{\infty}>0]=1
.

Set
w:=d2^{d}
.
Defining event
F_{n}:=\{\Lambda_{n}\leftrightarrow\infty\ {\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})\}^{c}
,
we have
{\mathbb{P}}[\cap_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}]={\mathbb{P}}[N_{\infty}=0]=0
. Therefore we can and do
choose
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m\geq 9
and

{\mathbb{P}}[F_{m}]<{\varepsilon}^{w}
.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k>m
,
set

U_{k}:=\{x\in(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}\cap\Lambda_{k}\setminus\Lambda_{k-1}:\Lambda_{m}\leftrightarrow\Lambda_{1/2}(x){\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap\Lambda_{k})\}.

Then, as in the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 4]
,
there is a collection of
w
decreasing
events, denoted
A_{1},\ldots,A_{w}

say, each having the same probability as
event

\{\#V(m,k)<\ell\}
, such that

\{\#U_{k}<w\ell\}\supseteq\cap_{i=1}A_{i}.

Hence by the Harris-FKG inequality (Lemma
2.1
)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\#U_{k}<w\ell]\geq{\mathbb{P}}[\#V(m,k)<\ell]^{w}.

(2.4)

Now let

\Delta_{k}U_{k}:=(\cup_{z\in U_{k}}\Lambda_{3/2}(z))\setminus\Lambda_{k}.

By the union bound,
\Delta_{k}U_{k}
has Lebesgue
d
-measure
at most
3^{d}\#U_{k}
, so that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[U_{k+1}=\varnothing|1\leq\#U_{k}<w\ell]

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}[{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap\Delta_{k}U_{k}=\varnothing|1\leq\#U_{k}<w\ell]

\displaystyle\geq\exp(-3^{d}\lambda w\ell).

Also if
\Lambda_{m}\leftrightarrow\infty
in
G({\cal H}_{\lambda})
then

U_{k}\neq\varnothing
.
Hence

\displaystyle e^{-3^{d}\lambda w\ell}{\mathbb{P}}[\#U_{k}<w\ell,\Lambda_{m}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-3^{d}\lambda w\ell}{\mathbb{P}}[1\leq\#U_{k}<w\ell]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{P}}[1\leq\#U_{k}<w\ell,U_{k+1}=\varnothing]={\mathbb{P}}[E_{k}],

say, where the events

are disjoint so their probabilities are summable in

.
Therefore
{\mathbb{P}}[\#U_{k}<w\ell,\Lambda_{m}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]\to 0
as

.
Thus, since

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\#U_{k}<w\ell]\leq{\mathbb{P}}[\#U_{k}<w\ell,\Lambda_{m}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda})]+{\mathbb{P}}[F_{m}],

using our choice of

at the start of this proof

\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\mathbb{P}}[\#U_{k}<w\ell]\leq{\mathbb{P}}[F_{m}]<{\varepsilon}^{w},

and since (

) gives us

(

).

Next, we shall give an analogue to
[
5
, Lemma 2]
.
Given
\eta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\overline{\eta}:=\cup_{z\in(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}:\Lambda_{1/2}(z)\cap\eta\neq\varnothing}\Lambda_{1/2}(z).

(2.5)

In other words,
\overline{\eta}

. Given also

\displaystyle\Delta(\eta;B)

\displaystyle:=\{z\in(\tfrac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}\cap B\setminus\overline{\eta}:\Lambda_{1/2}(z)\cap\overline{\eta\cap B}\neq\varnothing\};

\displaystyle\eta^{+}_{B}

\displaystyle:=(\eta\cap B)\cup\Delta(\eta;B).

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}(\eta;B):=\Delta(\eta^{+}_{B};B);~~~~~~\eta^{++}_{B}:=\eta_{B}^{+}\cup\Delta^{2}(\eta;B).

(2.6)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
R,B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

B=\overline{B\cap(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}}
.
Let
K
be a random subset
of
(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}\cap B
, independent of

{\cal H}_{\lambda}
,
with
R^{+}_{B}\cap\overline{K}=\varnothing
almost surely.
Define the random set

U=\{z\in\Delta(R;B):\overline{\{z\}}\leftrightarrow\overline{K}{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap B\setminus\overline{R})\}.

Then
for each
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\#U\leq t]\leq e^{3^{d}\lambda t}{\mathbb{P}}[\{\overline{R}\leftrightarrow\overline{K}{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap B)\}^{c}].

(2.7)

Proof.

Let
\alpha\subset\Delta(R;B)
. If
U=\alpha

and
{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap(\cup_{z\in\alpha}\Lambda_{3/2}(z))\cap\overline{R\cap B}=\varnothing
, then event

\{\overline{R}\leftrightarrow\overline{K}{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap B)\}
does not occur, since
any path from
\overline{K}
to
\overline{R}

must include a step from a vertex
v\in\overline{U}

to a vertex
w\in\overline{R\cap B}
, and
if
v\in\overline{\{z\}}
say for some
z\in\overline{U}
,
then
w\in\Lambda_{3/2}(z)
by the triangle inequality.
By the independence properties
of
{\cal H}_{\lambda}
and the independence assumption on
K
,
the events
\{U=\alpha\}

and
\{{\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap(\cup_{z\in\alpha}\Lambda_{3/2}(z))\cap\overline{R\cap B}=\varnothing\}
are independent, and

\cup_{z\in\alpha}\Lambda_{3/2}(z)
has
Lebesgue
d
-measure at most
3^{d}\#\alpha
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}[\{\overline{R}\leftrightarrow\overline{K}{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\lambda}\cap B)\}^{c}]

3
Algorithm: First step

We are now nearly ready to start on the proof proper of Theorem

.
It is not hard to see that
\lambda_{c}(\phi,S_{M})
is nonincreasing in

\lambda_{c}(\phi,S_{M})\geq\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all

so to prove (

)
it suffices to prove that if
\mu>\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})

then we can find
M>0
such that
\mu\geq\lambda_{c}(\phi,S_{M})
.
We therefore fix arbitrary
\mu>\lambda>\lambda_{c}(\phi,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We shall show for some
M>0
(depending on
\lambda
and
\mu
)
that
{\bf o}\leftrightarrow\infty{\rm~in~}G({\cal H}_{\mu}^{\bf o}\cap S_{M})
with positive probability,
i.e.
\theta(\phi,\lambda,S_{M})>0
, so that

\lambda_{c}(S_{M})\leq\mu

as required.

Our proof will be based
on an algorithm which will generate a nondecreasing sequence of subsets of
{\cal H}_{\mu}^{\bf o}
,
denoted
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\ldots
, such that

{\bf o}\in\xi_{i}
and
the subgraph of
G({\cal H}^{{\bf o}}_{\mu})
induced by
\xi_{i}

(denoted
G(\xi_{i})
for short)
is connected for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
G(\xi_{i})
is connected and therefore part of
a single cluster of
G({\cal H}_{\mu}^{{\bf o}})
,
we shall refer to
\xi_{i}
as the
sub-cluster at the origin

at the end of the
i
th step of the algorithm.

The general idea of the algorithm goes as follows.
We shall take
\xi_{1}
to be a set
of Poisson points in a box at the origin with an added
point at the origin. We shall then consider
a sequence of non-homogeneous Poisson point processes

{\cal S}_{2}
,
{\cal S}_{3}
,
{\cal S}_{4},\ldots
,
each of which can be thought of as a sub-Poisson process
of
{\cal H}_{\mu}
. Each point process
{\cal S}_{n}
will be
restricted to a bounded spatial region
A_{n}
,
where the
A_{n}
are generated sequentially
and for each
n
,

A_{n}
depends on the history of the algorithm prior to the

n
th
step.
To get from
\xi_{n}
to
\xi_{n+1}
we
determine the set-cluster
{\cal C}_{\xi_{n}}({\cal S}_{n+1})
,
and add the points of this set-cluster.

Of use to us is the following
sequential construction

of
{\cal C}_{\xi_{n}}({\cal S}_{n+1})
.
Denote the points of
\xi_{n}

as
active points
and let
g(\cdot)
be the initial intensity function
of
unexplored points
, i.e.
the intensity function of
{\cal S}_{n+1}
, representing Poisson points that
are not yet generated.

Next, choose an active point
x
and generate a Poisson process
of intensity

g(\cdot)\phi(\|\cdot-x\|)
,
representing the
previously unexplored points
of
{\cal S}_{n+1}
that are connected directly to
x
. Label all the new
points as ‘active’, and change the status
of
x
from ‘active’ to ‘finished’. Also change the intensity of
unexplored points of
{\cal S}_{n+1}
from
g(\cdot)
to

g(\cdot)(1-\phi(\|\cdot-x\|))
.

Then pick a new active point and repeat the above, using the new intensity of
unexplored points. Keep repeating until we run out of active points,
then stop.

We shall refer to the above procedure as

growing the cluster sequentially
. This method is described in detail
for the case
\phi={\bf 1}_{[0,1]}
in
[
13
]
, and for the general random connection
model in
[
9
]
.

The main point of this construction is that
the region distant further than 1 (in our norm
\|\cdot\|
) from

{\cal C}_{\xi_{n}}({\cal S}_{n+1})

remains unexplored at the end of this step, and we
can use the whole of our Poisson process
{\cal H}_{\mu}
in
this unexplored region, in later steps.

A slightly different (and possibly more elementary)
way to perform the sequential
construction, which would also work for our purposes, goes as follows. Divide

\mathbb{R}^{d}
into unit cubes
B_{i}
centred on the points of

(\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z})^{d}
. At each stage, given
\xi_{n}
,
the next region

will be a finite union of
some of these cubes. Let us call
\xi_{n}

the current sub-cluster at the origin. As well as

\xi_{n}
there will be a second point process
\varphi_{n}

of points that have been revealed but
are not connected to
\xi_{n}
.
Next, pick one of the cubes
B_{i}
that is contained
in
A_{n+1}
and within
\ell^{\infty}
distance 1 of
at least one point of the current sub-cluster at the origin.
Reveal the points of the Poisson process
{\cal S}_{n+1}\cap B_{i}

(new points), and the status (present/absent) of all potential
edges between the new points and between new points and old points.
This may cause some of the new points (and also possibly
in some of the previously revealed points that were not previously
in the sub-cluster at the origin)
to be added to the current sub-cluster at the origin.
Then pick another unit cube
B_{i}
that is contained
in
A_{n+1}
and within unit
\ell^{\infty}
distance
of the current sub-cluster at the origin. Repeat
until no such

exists. Then conclude this step;
at the end of this step
none of the cubes

that
are

distance more than

Let

(and do) think of

That is, the point process

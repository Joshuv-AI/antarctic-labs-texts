Emergence of Lorentz symmetry from an almost-commutative twisted spectral triple.
G. Nieuviarts

Abstract
This article demonstrates how the transition from a (Riemannian) twisted spectral triple to a pseudo-Riemannian spectral triple arises within an almost-commutative spectral triple. This opens a new perspective on the Lorentzian signature problem, showing that the almost-commutative structure at the heart of the noncommutative standard model of particle physics could be the origin of the emergence of a Lorentzian spectral triple, starting from self-adjoint Dirac operators and conventional inner product structures, in the framework of twisted spectral triples. We present an alternative to Wick rotation, acting on the metric and the Christoffel symbols in a way that does not introduce any complex numbers.

Introduction

Noncommutative geometry is a branch of mathematics that generalizes classical geometry to spaces where the algebra of functions is no longer commutative. Inspired by quantum mechanics, where observables (elements in an algebra) do not commute, this theory provides tools to describe "spaces" that are too irregular or singular to be treated with traditional differential geometry. One key realization of noncommutative geometry is its ability to recover Riemannian manifolds through spectral data. A. Connes, a pioneer of the field, developed the concept of spectral triples to encode the geometric information. A spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D)
is the data of an involutive unital algebra
\mathcal{A}
represented by bounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
together with a self-adjoint operator
D
acting on
\mathcal{H}
. In this framework, Riemannian geometry can be reconstructed from the spectral properties of the Dirac operator by starting from the spectral triple

(C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{S}),D,J,\Gamma)
, where
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{S})
is the Hilbert space of square integrable sections of the spinor bundle over the smooth manifold
\mathcal{M}
. The Dirac operator
D
is constructed using the Riemannian gamma matrices and the Levi-Civita spin connection.
J
is a given anti-unitary operator, and
\Gamma
is the grading operator.

In theoretical physics, fundamental interactions are described as Yang-Mills and Gravity theories, both based on the concept of connection on vector bundles. The extension of these concepts to noncommutative geometry relies on a crucial duality given by the Serre-Swan theorem. The gauge group emerges from inner automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
. According to the noncommutative differential structure we choose, two main frameworks have been used to construct Noncommutative Gauge Field Theories. The first one (historically), proposed by M. Dubois-Violette, R. Kerner, and J. Madore in
[
15
]
concerns the use of derivation-based differential structure. The second one, mainly developed by A. Connes, J. Lott, and A. Chamseddine in
[
7
,
5
]
concerns the use of spectral triples based differential structure. Lets focus on this latter approach by considering a spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},\,\mathcal{H},\,D,\,J,\,\Gamma)
(see
[
11
,
9
]
for the complete definition). The derivative is given by
\delta(.)=\left[D,.\right]
so that one-forms are given by elements
A=\sum_{k}a_{k}\left[D,b_{k}\right]
with
a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
. The connection with gauge theory goes as follows, if we consider a unitary element
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})
(the set of unitaries in
\mathcal{A}
), and define the adjoint unitary
U=\pi(u)J\pi(u)J^{-1}
, then, considering a one-form
\omega
as a gauge field and the associated “fluctuated” Dirac operator
D_{\omega}:=D+\omega+\epsilon^{\prime}J\omega J^{-1}
, inner fluctuations
(D_{\omega})^{u}:=UD_{\omega}U^{\dagger}
are then equivalent to gauge transformations
\omega^{u}:=u\omega u^{\dagger}+u\delta(u^{\dagger})
via the relation
(D_{\omega})^{u}=D_{\omega^{u}}
. The spectral action, a central spectral invariant, is given by
\mathcal{S}_{b}[D]:=\Tr f(DD^{\dagger}/\Lambda^{2})
(for more details, see
[
6
]
).

Based on this construction, we can consider a noncommutative extension of
(C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{S}),D,J,\Gamma)
by taking the Almost-Commutative (AC) algebra
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{F}
with
\mathcal{A}_{F}
a finite dimensional noncommutative algebra. The Dirac operator is
D_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes{\text{\char 49\relax}}+\Gamma\otimes D_{F}
, with Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},S)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{F}
, real structure
J\otimes J_{F}
and grading
\Gamma\otimes\Gamma_{F}
. A fundamental aspect of this AC manifold is that its spectral action corresponds to the Lagrangian of an Einstein-Yang-Mills-Higgs model, entirely determined by the product spectral triple. Driven by the constraints coming with the axioms of even real spectral triples, a reformulation of the Standard Model of Particle Physics coupled to gravitation was elaborated, with crucial steps in
[
10
,
5
]
until its full development in
[
6
]
(we refer to
[
11
]
for detailed explanations). At the heart of this construction lies the finite geometry encoded in
\mathcal{A}_{F}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus M_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and its associated spectral triple. The symmetry group of the spectral action is given by
\mathcal{G}=Map(\mathcal{M},G)\rtimes\Diff(\mathcal{M})
, where
\Diff(\mathcal{M})
denotes the diffeomorphism group, and
Map(\mathcal{M},G)
represents the gauge group of the second kind, with
G=U(1)\times SU(2)\times\lx@nobreakspace SU(3)
. In this way, noncommutative geometry provides a unified framework to describe both Einstein-Hilbert gravity (in Euclidean signature) and classical gauge theories. This framework is known as the NonCommutative Standard Model. Notably, it offers an elegant description of the Standard Model, including the Higgs mechanism and neutrino mixing, as "gravity" on an AC-manifold. The fermionic masses are encoded into
D_{F}
, so that the masses of the Higgs boson and the ones of fermions became related, thus providing a prediction for the Higgs mass.

The noncommutative approach links geometry to operator algebras, revealing new mathematical and physical insights. But it falls short when describing spacetimes with pseudo-Riemannian signatures that are essential in physics. Indeed, realistic models require a formulation that incorporates time and causality, motivating the search for Lorentzian approaches within noncommutative geometry. This issue has spurred various proposals, from modified axioms for spectral triples to alternative formulations that attempt to reconcile causality with noncommutative structures see
[

. An essential feature of most of these approaches is the replacement of the Hilbert space by a Krein space, turning the inner product into an indefinite inner product
{\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{K}}\vcentcolon=\langle\,\cdot\,,\,K\cdot\,\rangle
with the unitary operator
K=K^{\dagger}
. Such structures are essential in quantum field theory to describe spinor fields on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds. However, developing a fully satisfactory and widely accepted framework for Lorentzian noncommutative geometry remains an open and actively pursued area of research.

In
[
22
]
, I present a connection between twisted spectral triples and pseudo-Riemannian spectral triples based on a fundamental link between the previous Krein product and the twist defined by
\rho(\,\cdot\,)\vcentcolon=K(\,\cdot\,)K

This morphism was shown to induce a signature change in the context of the manifold’s spectral triples. The signature change transformation was determined solely by the unitary operator
K
, being the central element of the approach.

The following article proposes to extend these results. The first three sections introduce what are twisted spectral triples, pseudo-Riemannian spectral triples, and the
K
-morphism between them. One of the main contributions is the definition of a specific real structure for twisted spectral triples in subsection

K

Another important point was the introduction of the twist in the context of Clifford algebras (for even-dimensional vector spaces) in subsection

{\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{K}}
. The way the

The main results are presented in section

K
in the product Dirac operator. This leads to the appearance of a Krein structure together with the pseudo-Riemannian Dirac operators, from the very first spectral triple structure, then implementing the
K
-morphism. We then show how the specific signature of space-time is recovered in the 4-dimensional case. This section follows a top-down approach, revealing the signature change as an intrinsic outcome of the algebraic structure governing the almost-commutative spectral triple.

2
Twisted spectral triple

Spectral triples have been particularly successful in describing noncommutative spaces arising from type I and type II von Neumann algebras, where a well-defined trace exists. However, a major challenge in noncommutative geometry has been the extension of these methods to type III von Neumann algebras, which lack such a finite normal trace. Type III algebras arise naturally in quantum field theory, especially in the algebraic approach to quantum statistical mechanics and the study of vacuum states in curved spacetime.

To address this challenge, twisted spectral triples were introduced by A. Connes and H. Moscovici in their 2006 work, see
[
12
]
. These modified spectral triples generalize the standard construction by incorporating an automorphism, which compensates for the absence of a trace and allows for the formulation of geometric invariants in this setting.

Interestingly, the introduction of this twist is related to the modular theory of Tomita-Takesaki, which governs the structure of von Neumann algebras. Tomita-Takesaki theory links the modular structure of von Neumann algebras to time evolution, defining a natural flow on the algebra. Twisted spectral triples were then implemented in the context of the noncommutative Standard Model in
[
13
,
18
,
19
]
. Applications to conformal and noncommutative geometry can also be found in
[
24
,
25
]
.

2.1
Fundamentals of twisted spectral triple

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
*
-algebra with representation
\pi
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The faithful representation
\pi
will be omitted by identifying the elements of
\mathcal{A}
with their representation. The adjoint
\dagger
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is then given by the relation
\pi(a^{*})=\pi(a)^{\dagger}
.

Definition 2.1
(Twisted spectral triple).

A twisted spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D,\rho)
is obtained by considering an involutive algebra
\mathcal{A}
acting faithfully as a bounded operator algebra on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with a self-adjoint operator
D
with compact resolvent together with an automorphism
\rho\in\Aut(\mathcal{A})
called twist which satisfies the regularity condition

\rho(a^{\dagger})=(\rho^{-1}(a))^{\dagger},\,\qquad\qquad\forall a\in\mathcal{A}.

(2.1)

The twisted commutator

[D,a]_{\rho}:=Da-\rho(a)D

(2.2)

is used instead of
[D,a]
and we require only the twisted commutator to be bounded.

In the same way as for spectral triples, we can add a grading
\Gamma
and a real structure
J
. The axioms of such even real twisted spectral triples are the same as for spectral triples

J^{2}=\epsilon_{0},\qquad JD=\epsilon_{1}DJ,\qquad J\Gamma=\epsilon_{2}\Gamma J,\qquad\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}=\pm 1,

(2.3)

with the introduction of the characteristic number
\epsilon=\pm 1
so that
\rho
is required to satisfy

\rho(J)=\epsilon J,

(2.4)

and the replacement of the first order condition by the twisted first order condition

[[D,a]_{\rho},b^{\circ}]_{\rho^{\circ}}=0

(2.5)

\forall a\in\mathcal{A}
and
b^{\circ}\in\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
with
\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
the opposite algebra. The opposite twist
\rho^{\circ}
acting on
\mathcal{A}^{\circ}
is defined by

\rho^{\circ}(a^{\circ}):=(\rho^{-1}(a))^{\circ}.

(2.6)

See
[
18
]
for further details.

The set of twisted 1-forms
\Omega^{1}_{D}(\mathcal{A},\rho)
is defined as

\Omega^{1}_{D}(\mathcal{A},\rho)\vcentcolon=\{\sum_{k}a_{k}[D,b_{k}]_{\rho}\,\mid\,a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\}

(2.7)

so that twisted-fluctuations of
D
are given by

D_{A_{\rho}}:=D+A_{\rho}+\epsilon_{1}JA_{\rho}J^{-1}\qquad\text{with}\qquad A_{\rho}\in\Omega^{1}_{D}(\mathcal{A},\rho).

(2.8)

The set
\Omega^{1}_{D}(\mathcal{A},\rho)
is a
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule for the following product

a\,\cdot\,A_{\rho}\,\cdot\,b=\rho(a)A_{\rho}b

(2.9)

for any
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
and
A_{\rho}\in\Omega^{1}_{D}(\mathcal{A},\rho)
. This makes the map
a\,\to\,\delta_{\rho}(a)\vcentcolon=[D,a]_{\rho}
be a derivation since we will have that

\delta_{\rho}(ab)=\delta_{\rho}(a)b+\rho(a)\delta_{\rho}(b)=\delta_{\rho}(a)\,\cdot\,b+a\,\cdot\,\delta_{\rho}(b).

(2.10)

As shown in
[
18
]
a way to “minimally twist” the spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D,\Gamma)
i.e.,without changing the Hilbert space and the Dirac operator, is by the use of the decomposition coming with
\Gamma
. Using the projections
p_{\pm}:=({\text{\char 49\relax}}\pm\Gamma)/2
so that
\mathcal{H}=p_{+}\mathcal{H}\oplus p_{-}\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{H}_{+}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{-}
, all elements
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
are represented accordingly:

\pi(a_{1},a_{2})\vcentcolon=p_{+}\pi_{0}(a_{1})+p_{-}\pi_{0}(a_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{+}(a_{1})&0\\
0&\pi_{-}(a_{2})\\
\end{pmatrix}

(2.11)

with
\pi_{0}
the representation used for
\mathcal{A}
and
\pi_{\pm}(\,\cdot\,)\vcentcolon=p_{\pm}\pi_{0}(\,\cdot\,)_{|\mathcal{H}_{\pm}}
the restrictions to
\mathcal{H}_{\pm}
. This will be called the chiral representation.

Definition 2.2
(twist by grading).

Taking the graded spectral triple
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D,\Gamma)
, its corresponding twist by gradding is given by the twisted spectral triple
(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2},\mathcal{H},D,\Gamma,\rho)
, using the chiral representation. The twist acts on elements
a=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
as

\rho(a_{1},a_{2})=(a_{2},a_{1}).

(2.12)

As shown in
[
18
]
, the twist by the grading is the only way to minimally twist the spectral triple of even-dimensional manifolds based on
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The fact that no such procedure for twisting odd-dimensional manifold’s spectral triple have been found will be one of the justifications to focus on the study of even-dimensional manifolds. More information on twisted spectral triples can be found in
[
18
,
19
,
21
]
.

2.2
From twisted to indefinite inner products

Following
[
22
]
, any automorphism
\rho\in\Aut(\mathcal{A})
will be required to be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-regular, i.e., a regular automorphism on all of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Since
[
14

\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\rho}
, called the
\rho
-product, which by definition satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\psi,O\psi^{\prime}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle\rho(O)^{\dagger}\psi,\psi^{\prime}\rangle_{\rho}

(2.13)

for all
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
and
O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

This new inner product naturally comes with its own notion of adjoint, denoted by "
+

(\,\cdot\,)^{+}:=\rho(\,\cdot\,)^{\dagger}
, along with an associated notion of
\rho
-unitarity, characterized by elements

satisfying
U_{\rho}U_{\rho}^{+}=U_{\rho}^{+}U_{\rho}={\text{\char 49\relax}}
, thereby preserving the
\rho
-product. As mentioned in
[
22
]
, the uniqueness of this adjoint (i.e., the equality of the left and right adjoint) is ensured by the
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-regularity condition. We will restrict ourselves to twists that are
*
-automorphisms, i.e.,
\rho(O^{\dagger})=\rho(O)^{\dagger}
. This, together with the regularity condition, implies that
\rho^{2}={\text{\char 49\relax}}
, which in turn ensures that
(\,\cdot\,)^{+}
is an involution.

As shown in
[
19
]
and
[
21
]
, there are two ways to generate the twisted fluctuations of equation (

), using either usual unitary operators or
\rho
-unitary operators:

1.

Type 1:
D_{A_{\rho}}=UDU^{+}
with
U=uJuJ^{-1}
and
u
a unitary operator in
\mathcal{A}
.

2.

Type 2:
D_{A_{\rho}}=U_{\rho}DU_{\rho}^{\dagger}
with
U_{\rho}=u_{\rho}Ju_{\rho}J^{-1}
and
u_{\rho}
a
\rho
-unitary operator in
\mathcal{A}
.

This justifies the introduction of the notions of
\rho
-product, along with the adjoint "
+
" and the notion of
\rho
-unitarity.

In the case where
\rho\in\Inn(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, there exists a unitary operator
K
acting on
\mathcal{H}
such that
\rho(O)=KOK^{\dagger}
. In this case, the
\rho
-product is determined by

, so that
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\rho}:=\langle\,\cdot\,,K\cdot\,\rangle
. Furthermore, the twist is a
*
-automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
.

In the following, any twist
\rho
will be assumed to be in
\Inn(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
. Since different operators

can implement the same twist

while inducing different inner products, we will adopt a more precise notation. The twisted spectral triple will be denoted by
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{H},D,K)
, and the
\rho
-product notation
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\rho}
will be replaced by the
K
-product notation:

{\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{K}}:=\langle\,\cdot\,,K\cdot\,\rangle

(2.14)

so that the associated adjoint is called the
K
-adjoint and is defined by

(\,\cdot\,)^{\dagger_{\tiny K}}\vcentcolon=\rho(\,\cdot\,)^{\dagger}.

(2.15)

We define
K
-unitary operators by the relation
U_{K}U_{K}^{\dagger_{\tiny K}}=U_{K}^{\dagger_{\tiny K}}U_{K}={\text{\char 49\relax}}
, with the corresponding set denoted as
\mathcal{U}_{K}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
.

Taking the automorphism
\rho(\,\cdot\,)=K(\,\cdot\,)K^{\dagger}
to be
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
-regular, or regular on
\mathcal{A}
if the algebra is a simple finite-dimensional algebra, we obtain

K=\exp(i\theta)K^{\dagger}

(2.16)

for some real number
\theta
. As a consequence,
{\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle_{K}}
is Hermitian and indefinite if and only if
K=K^{\dagger}
(see
[
22
]
for more details). The condition

will be derived from algebraic constraints in the top-down approach presented in section
5
.

Definition 2.3
(Fundamental twist).

A fundamental twist is a twist
\rho\in\Inn(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))
, implemented by the unitary
K
such that
K=K^{\dagger}
.

2.3
Twisted real structure

In noncommutative geometry, the real structure given by the operator
J
allows to define a right action of the algebra

\mathcal{A}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, turning it into a
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule by relating left and right actions. We have seen in equation (
2.9
) that
\Omega^{1}_{D}(\mathcal{A},\rho)
is a
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule whose left and right actions are connected by the twist
\rho
. Here, I propose to introduce a notion of real structure that implement the twisted bimodule action. This particular real structure will play a crucial role throughout the article, appearing notably in the context of Clifford algebras with indefinite (pseudo-Riemannian) metrics in subsection
3.2
, and then in the framework of pseudo-Riemannian spectral triples in subsection
3.1
.

Taking
a\in\mathcal{A}
, the left action of the algebra on bimodules is denoted by
l(a)
. This operator satisfies
l(a^{\dagger})=l(a)^{\dagger}
. The corresponding right action
r(a)
is given through
J
by

\displaystyle r(a)=Jl(a)^{\dagger}J^{-1},

(2.17)

with the zero-order condition
[l(a),r(a)]=0
, ensuring that the right and left actions on the
\mathcal{A}
-bimodules are connected by the relation

a\cdot(\,\cdot\,)\cdot b=l(a)r(b)(\,\cdot\,).

(2.18)

For the usual action on a bimodule, we have
a\cdot(\,\cdot\,)\cdot b=a(\,\cdot\,)b
, meaning that the left and right actions correspond to the usual multiplication of operators acting on elements of the bimodule. We denote by
{\hat{J}}
the corresponding real structure, i.e.,
\hat{l}(a)=a
and
\hat{r}(a)={\hat{J}}\hat{l}(a)^{\dagger}{\hat{J}}^{-1}
. We define
\hat{r}(a)=a^{\hat{\circ}}={\hat{J}}a^{\dagger}{\hat{J}}^{-1}
as the element of the corresponding opposite algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\hat{\circ}}
. This is the real structure used for conventional spectral triples.

Now, to recover the twisted product action (
2.9
), i.e.,

a\cdot(\,\cdot\,)\cdot b=\rho(a)(\,\cdot\,)b,

(2.19)

we have to find
J
such that
l(a)=Jr(a)^{\dagger}J^{-1}=\rho(a)
with
r(a)=\hat{r}(a)=a
. Noting that

\displaystyle l(a)=\rho(\hat{l}(a))=\rho({\hat{J}}\hat{r}(a)^{\dagger}{\hat{J}}^{-1})=\rho({\hat{J}}r(a)^{\dagger}{\hat{J}}^{-1}),

(2.20)

we can define

1

In full generality, we may have

J\vcentcolon=K{\hat{J}}

(2.21)

so that
l(a)=Jr(a)^{\dagger}J^{-1}=\rho(a)
. We define

In subsection

\psi\,\to\,u_{K}\psi u_{K}^{-1}=u_{K}\psi u_{K}^{\dagger_{\tiny K}}=u_{K}Ju_{K}J^{-1}\psi

showing that the adjoint action of

Spectral triples correspond to the special case

2.4
Twisted Clifford relation

A general definition of the notion of twisted Clifford algebra was given in
[
22
]
. However, this was not clear weather the twisted Clifford algebra can be represented from the defining twisted Clifford algebraic constrait of the vector space and the metric. We propose here a modification of this definition to solve this issue, by retrieving the well known Clifford algebra case, but from a twisted Clifford algebraic relation. This specific relationship to the metric will be connected to a twisted spectral triple in subsection
4.2
.

Definition 2.4
(Twisted Clifford relation).

Let
V
be a vector space over a commutative field with
{\tilde{g}}
a metric on
V
and
\rho
a linear, unit-preserving involution on
V
, i.e.,
\rho^{2}=id_{V}
. The relation between
{\tilde{g}}
and
V
is given by the twisted Clifford relation

\displaystyle\rho(u)\cdot v+v\cdot\rho(u)=2{\tilde{g}}(u,v){\text{\char 49\relax}},\qquad\qquad\forall u,v\in V

(2.24)

where
\rho
is required to be a morphism, i.e.,
\rho(u\cdot v)=\rho(u)\cdot\rho(v)
for any
u,v\in V
.

The bilinearity of
{\tilde{g}}
follows from the linearity of
\rho
, and the symmetry of
{\tilde{g}}
from the fact that
\rho
is a unital,
V
-involutory morphism.

Definition 2.5
(Reflection).

Taking a vector space
V
and the metric
{\tilde{g}}
on
V
, a reflection is a
{\tilde{g}}
-isometric involutive map on
V
.

Proposition 2.6
.

\rho
is a reflection for
{\tilde{g}}
.

Proof

We only have to prove that
\rho
is
{\tilde{g}}
-isometric. The computation of the action of
\rho
on equation (
2.24
) gives

\displaystyle u\cdot\rho(v)+\rho(v)\cdot u=2{\tilde{g}}(u,v){\text{\char 49\relax}}

where the left-hand side also identifies with
2{\tilde{g}}(\rho(u),\rho(v)){\text{\char 49\relax}}
, implying that
{\tilde{g}}(u,v)={\tilde{g}}(\rho(u),\rho(v))
, which proves that
\rho
is
{\tilde{g}}
-isometric.

We define
g
from
{\tilde{g}}
and
\rho
by the relation

\displaystyle g(u,v)={\tilde{g}}(\rho(u),v),\qquad\qquad\forall u,v\in V.

(2.25)

The bilinearity of
g
follows from that of
\rho
and

•

Explicit appearance of

\overline{\psi}D^{K}\psi=\psi^{\dagger}D\psi.

(5.3)

•

The Noncommutative standard model is based on the product

]

•

In quantum field theory and noncommutative geometry, modular flow arises from the Tomita-Takesaki theory of von Neumann algebras. Given a state and an associated von Neumann algebra, the modular operator and the anti-linear modular conjugation define a time evolution on the algebra through the modular group, parametrized by a real parameter
t

On another side, twisted spectral triples have been introduced in the context of von Neumann algebras where the twist itself is connected with the modular flow
[

]
. In our approach, the twist is connected with the Krein structure of the dual pseudo-Riemannian spectral triple. The intuition is then that there may be a connection between the twist and a finite noncommutative part, hence the proposition to study the presented product structure with a noncommutative finite space.

The previous sections can be considered as a bottom-up approach, constructing a twisted spectral triple from a pseudo-Riemannian one. This section proposes a top-down reversed approach, where the starting point is now the presented twisted spectral triple within a product spectral triple. The aim will be to show how this very product structure harmonizes with the twisted spectral triple ones, to show how this implies the emergence of the dual pseudo-Riemannian spectral triple and to characterize this last one.

The main assertion of this article has been the proposition that pseudo-Riemannian spectral triples may be seen as emergences from twisted spectral triples within an almost-commutative spectral triple structure. The KO-dimension of the emerged pseudo-Riemannian spectral triple and that of the finite noncommutative one are the same. In the end, the Almost-commutative spectral triple can be seen as a first given object, where the twisted structure arises as a consequence of the "entanglement" between the two spectral triples of the product through the operator

In the four-dimensional case, we recover

This very operator is linked to the finite noncommutative geometry of the Almost-commutative spectral triple and acts as a local fixed point in the theory, providing the mass term and enabling the definition of the desired coordinate-independent Krein structure, unsensitive to Lorentz transformations.

This article focuses on special cases of physical interest. We leave the generalization of these results for future study. This will, in particular, concern the study of what happens in the odd-dimensional manifold case, the exploration of more general KO-dimensions (not only the KO-dimension 6), and the characterization of the emerged signature in these more general contexts. An exploration of the potential connection with Tomita-Takesaki theory is also envisioned.

We hope the presented structure will offer concrete avenues for the study of General Relativity within the framework of spectral triples. The specific algebraic constraints arising from the noncommutative geometrical formulation of Yang-Mills-Higgs theory are algebraically linked to the causal structure of spacetime through the fundamental symmetry operator
K

An unusual cause of eyelid erythema and edema
Authors: Marnya Lollen, Chhavi Gupta, Sima Das
Published: 2026

A 50-year-old male presented with slowly progressive right upper eyelid swelling for 2 months. He had been getting treatment for blepharitis for the last 2 months, but no resolution was noted. He had no other significant ocular or systemic history. Ocular examination revealed diffuse right upper eyelid erythema and swelling of the skin and subcutaneous tissue and mild tenderness [Fig. 1a and b]. No loss of eyelashes or distortion of the eyelid architecture was noted. The rest of the anterior and posterior segment examination was within normal limits.

Findings and management

The right upper eyelid had diffuse swelling with erythema but no mass visualization. On palpation, only lid thickening was present. An incision biopsy was done from the thickened eyelid area, and histopathological examination shows nodular aggregates of lymphoid cells, interspersed plasma cells, vascular proliferation, areas of fibrosis, and scattered lymphoid cells with irregular nuclear contours. Some collections of epithelioid histiocytic cells suggested emperipolesis, and Immunohistochemistry (IHC) was positive for S100 and CD68 [Fig. 1c]. There was no lymphadenopathy, and systemic evaluation did not reveal any abnormality. Intralesional steroid injection (triamcinolone acetonide) led to the resolution of the eyelid swelling at 8 months of follow-up. However, a similar swelling developed in the lower eyelid and required another steroid injection [Fig. 1d].

Diagnosis: Rosai-Dorfman disease (RDD)

Discussion

RDD, also known as sinus histiocytosis with massive lymphadenopathy (SHML), was first described in 1969 by Rosai and Dorfman.[1] It mainly presents as painless bilateral cervical lymphadenopathy; 43% of RDD patients present with extranodal disease.[2] The most common extranodal sites are the eye and ocular adnexa, which include uveitis, ptosis, proptosis, conjunctival mass, and uveitis. Suspicion of the disease is low when patients present with isolated eyelid swelling without any mass, making it difficult to diagnose and manage. A biopsy should be advised to confirm the diagnosis. The disease has a waxing and waning course. Various treatment modalities are available, including intralesional and systemic steroids, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and debulking surgery. Recurrences are common and require regular follow-up.

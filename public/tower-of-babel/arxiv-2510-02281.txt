Monodromy Pinning Defects in the Critical O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{O}(2N) Model
Petr Kravchuk, Alex Radcliffe

Abstract
We investigate a novel class of defects in the critical O ⁡ ( 2 ​ N ) \mathrm{O}(2N) model that preserve conformal symmetry along the defect, but not the symmetry under rotations transverse to the defect. Instead, they only preserve a combination of transverse rotations and a global symmetry. These defects are constructed as IR fixed points of RG flows originating at monodromy defects, triggered by a relevant operator with non-zero transverse spin. Using large- N N and 4 − ε 4-\varepsilon expansions, we compute leading-order scaling dimensions of defect operators and the one-point functions of the bulk fields. In various limits this theory coincides with the monodromy defect or the pinning field defect, and we compare our results to existing results for these defects.

A defect conformal field theory (DCFT) must be symmetric under conformal transformations along the defect, and will also typically be symmetric under rotations transverse to the defect. 2.1
Symmetries of monodromy defects in the
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model

Generally, adding a monodromy defect into the
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model means requiring that

\displaystyle\phi^{a}(\vec{y},r,\theta+2\pi)=G^{a}{}_{b}\phi^{b}(\vec{y},r,\theta),

(5)

for some
G\in\mathrm{O}(2N)
. In this paper we only consider the special case
Giombi:2021uae
where

\displaystyle G=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(2\pi v)&-\sin(2\pi v)&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
\sin(2\pi v)&\cos(2\pi v)&0&0&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\cos(2\pi v)&-\sin(2\pi v)&\dots&0&0\\
0&0&\sin(2\pi v)&\cos(2\pi v)&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&\cos(2\pi v)&-\sin(2\pi v)\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&\sin(2\pi v)&\cos(2\pi v)\\
\end{pmatrix},

(6)

which gives us the monodromy in (
2
) in terms of the complex fields
\Phi^{I}=\phi^{2I-1}+i\phi^{2I}
.

This leads to
\Phi^{I}
not being single-valued (unless
v=0
). One way around this is to view the defect
\mathcal{D}
as the boundary of a codimension-1 submanifold
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
—for example, of the hypersurface at
\theta=0
. We can then introduce the monodromy around
\mathcal{D}
by letting
\Phi^{I}
have a discontinuity at
\Sigma
.
\Sigma
is clearly topological as the action is invariant under
\phi^{a}\mapsto G^{a}{}_{b}\phi^{b}
. It is essentially the topological defect associated to the global symmetry transformation
G\in\mathrm{O}(2N)
.

Inserting a (non-topological) flat codimension-
q
defect into a
d
-dimensional CFT necessarily breaks the conformal symmetry down to at most the subgroup of transformations that preserve the location of the defect. These transformations are generated by transverse rotations around the defect, and conformal transformations along the defect, giving the group
\mathrm{SO}(d-q+1,1)\times\mathrm{SO}(q)
. In the case of a monodromy defect,
q=2
, and the entire
\mathrm{SO}(2)\times\mathrm{SO}(d-1,1)
group of symmetries is preserved in the monodromy defect DCFT with action given by (
1
).
4
4

4

There is a small caveat that transverse rotations move
\Sigma
. However, since
\Sigma
is topological, at least for infinitesimal rotations it can be moved back after the rotation.

One consequence of insertion of a defect is that as we break the symmetry under translations normal to the defect, not all of the components of the stress tensor are conserved. Specifically, we expect that

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu i}(\vec{y},r,\theta)=D^{i}(\vec{y})\delta^{(2)}(r,\theta),

(7)

for a defect operator
D^{i}(\vec{y})
where the index
i
runs over the directions perpendicular to the defect, and
\delta^{(2)}(r,\theta)
is a delta function in the plane normal to the defect.
D^{i}
is called the displacement operator
Billo:2016cpy
. As the scaling dimension of the stress tensor is protected, the scaling dimension of
D^{i}
must
d-1
, and its
\mathrm{SO}(2)
-spin transverse to the defect must be
1
.

The subgroup of internal symmetries that are preserved under the insertion of the monodromy defect is given by the centralizer of
G
in
\mathrm{O}(2N)

Giombi:2021uae

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{O}(2N)}(G)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathrm{O}(2N)&\text{if }v\in\{0,\tfrac{1}{2}\}\\
\mathrm{U}(N)&\text{otherwise,}\\
\end{cases}

(8)

where
\mathrm{U}(N)
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{O}(2N)
acting on the complex fields

in the fundamental representation.

In the bulk, regardless of the value of
v
, we retain a local
\mathrm{O}(2N)
internal symmetry, and we have

real Noether currents associated with this, given by

\displaystyle J^{ab}_{\mu}=\phi^{a}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{b}-\phi^{b}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{a}.

(9)

Their dimension is protected,

. For

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{IJ}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=iJ^{2I-1,2J-1}-J^{2I-1,2J}+J^{2I,2J-1}-iJ^{2I,2J}

\displaystyle=i(\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{J}-\overline{\Phi}{}^{J}\partial_{\mu}\Phi^{I}).

(10)

As was mentioned in (

\mathcal{D}

Finally, we note that there exists a discrete symmetry under the reflection along the defect,

\displaystyle R^{\parallel}:(y_{1},y_{2},\dots y_{d-2})\mapsto(-y_{1},y_{2},\dots y_{d-2}).

(11)

If

then we also have a discrete symmetry under the reflection transverse to the defect,

\displaystyle R^{\perp}:\theta\mapsto 2\pi-\theta.

(12)

\displaystyle S=\int d^{d}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}(x)\partial^{\mu}\Phi^{I}(x)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}(x)\Phi^{I}(x)\right)^{2}\right)+h\int_{\mathcal{D}}d^{d-2}\vec{y}\,\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}^{1}_{v}(\vec{y})).

To describe the pinning monodromy DCFT in this picture it is helpful to perform the Fourier decomposition

Adding the operator

With the view towards integrating out the bulk fields

, it is then convenient to define

\displaystyle\delta\Phi^{I}_{s}(X_{1})=\Phi^{I}_{s}(X_{1})-\frac{\sqrt{2N}}{2\pi}\delta^{I1}\delta_{vs}\int d^{d-2}\vec{y}_{2}\,\int d\theta_{1}e^{-iv\theta_{1}}G_{\partial\text{b}}^{v}(\vec{y}_{2},x_{1})J(\vec{y}_{2}),

Above,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\theta_{1}e^{-iv\theta_{1}}\lim_{r_{1}\to 0}r_{1}^{-\Delta_{v}}G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x_{1},x_{2}),

where the bulk-to-bulk propagator is defined as

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{-\nabla^{2}+\sigma-\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}}(x_{1},x_{2}).

We also define the boundary-to-boundary propagator (which we will use later) as

\displaystyle G^{v}_{\partial\partial}(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\theta_{2}e^{iv\theta_{2}}\lim_{r_{2}\to 0}r_{2}^{-\Delta_{v}}G^{v}_{\partial\mathrm{b}}(\vec{y}_{1},x_{2}).

Note that we insist on

in (

\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}+v}\pi\int d^{d-1}X\sqrt{G(X)}\left(\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}_{s}(X)\left(-\nabla^{2}-\tfrac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}+\sigma+s^{2}\right)\Phi^{I}_{s}(X)\right).

\displaystyle=\frac{d-2}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}+m_{s}^{2}}

(34)

We have a choice of sign in some of these modes (if allowed by unitarity), which corresponds to a choice of boundary conditions. In this work, we always impose the boundary conditions that

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{d-2}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}+m_{s}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d-2}{2}+\sqrt{\sigma_{0}^{\mathrm{IR}}+s^{2}}.

8

8

These are Laplace eigenfunctions satisfying

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{d-2}{2})|\Gamma(\tfrac{d-2}{2}+i\nu)|^{2}}{4\pi^{\tfrac{d}{2}}\Gamma(d-2)|\Gamma(i\nu)|^{2}}.

We can use these functions to give the harmonic decomposition of any function

that is invariant under

-isometries,

\displaystyle F(X_{1},X_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\widetilde{F}(\nu)\Omega_{\nu}(X_{1},X_{2}).

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}(\nu)=\frac{1}{\Omega_{\nu}(X,X)}\int d^{d-1}X_{1}\sqrt{g(X_{1})}F(X_{1},X_{2})\Omega_{\nu}(X_{1},X_{2}).

(56)

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x_{1},x_{2})

this operator is weakly relevant, and the RG flow can be studied through the lens of conformal perturbation theory. In this section we will perform the most basic analysis by computing the leading-order anomalous dimension of
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}_{v})
at the IR fixed point. We will then compare the result to the scaling dimension obtained in section
3
through the spectral decomposition of
\langle\delta\sigma\delta\sigma\rangle
.

Let
\delta=d-2-\widehat{\Delta}_{v}^{\text{UV}}
, where we have added the superscript UV to stress that we refer to the scaling dimension at the UV monodromy defect fixed point. In the simplest weakly-relevant RG flows, at the leading order in
\delta
the fixed point is found by balancing the linear and quadratic terms in the beta function of the coupling
h
,

\displaystyle\beta(h)=-\delta h+\beta_{2}h^{2}+\mathcal{O}(h^{3}).

(71)

This
\beta
-function has the UV fixed point at
h=0
and the IR fixed point at

\displaystyle h^{*}\overset{?}{=}\frac{\delta}{\beta_{2}}+O(\delta^{2}),

(72)

if
\beta_{2}\neq 0
. An immediate consequence of this analysis is that

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(h^{*})\overset{?}{=}-\beta^{\prime}(0)=\delta,

(73)

and so that the scaling dimension of
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}_{v})
at the IR fixed point would be

\displaystyle\widehat{\Delta}_{v}^{\text{IR}}=d-2+\beta^{\prime}(h^{*})\overset{?}{=}d-2+\delta+O(\delta^{2}).

(74)

However, as we now explain, in our case
\beta_{2}=0
and this analysis has to be modified.

This is because
\beta_{2}
is proportional to the three-point function of
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}_{v})
, which is can be seen to vanish after writing
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}_{v})=\frac{1}{2}(\widehat{\Psi}_{v}+\overline{\widehat{\Psi}_{v}})
and using the transverse rotation symmetry of the UV fixed point. Therefore, the beta function starts at a higher order,

\displaystyle\beta(h)=-\delta h+\beta_{3}h^{3}+\dots.

(75)

The coefficient
\beta_{3}
is determined by the four-point function of
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}_{v})
, which is not forced to be zero by any symmetry. We therefore expect
\beta_{3}\neq 0
.

The roots of
\beta
are then
h=0
and
h=\pm h^{*}
where

\displaystyle h^{*}=\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\beta_{3}}}+O(\delta^{3/2}).

(76)

Furthermore, we find

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(h^{*})=\beta^{\prime}(-h^{*})=2\delta,

(77)

which implies that at the IR fixed points

\displaystyle\Delta^{\text{IR}}_{v}=d-2+2\delta.

(78)

We can compare this prediction to the large-
N
results of section
3
. We find numerically

\displaystyle\lim_{v\nearrow v^{*}}\frac{\widehat{\Delta}{}_{v}^{\text{IR}}-(d-2)}{\widehat{\Delta}^{\text{UV}}-(d-2)}\approx-2.000,

(79)

as expected, in agreement with the prediction of conformal perturbation theory. Note the denominator is obtained using (
3
), valid in the UV monodromy DCFT, while the denominator is determined from the leading zero of
\widetilde{B}_{0}
.

4
4-\varepsilon
expansion

In this section we perform the leading-order analysis in
4-\varepsilon
expansion. To do so, we can begin with the flat-space action:

\displaystyle S=\int d^{d}x\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}(x)\partial^{\mu}\Phi^{I}(x)+\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}(x)\Phi^{I}(x)\right)^{2}\right)+h\int_{\mathcal{D}}d^{d-2}\vec{y}\,\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}^{1}_{v}(\vec{y})),

(80)

We note that we are now working at finite
N
.

An immediate issue with the above action is that the operator
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}^{1}_{v}(\vec{y}))
on the UV monodromy defect has dimension
1+v+O(\varepsilon)

Giombi:2021uae
, and therefore for generic
v\in[0,1)
it is strongly relevant. However, the bulk is weakly-coupled and we expect that the RG flow can still be analysed to the leading order by solving the bulk equations of motions with the source term provided by
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}(\widehat{\Psi}^{1}_{v}(\vec{y}))
.

Away from the defect, the equation of motion for this theory is

\displaystyle-\partial^{2}\Phi^{I}(x)+\lambda\overline{\Phi}{}^{J}\Phi^{J}\Phi^{I}=0.

(81)

In the cylindrical coordinates this becomes

\displaystyle(\partial_{y}^{2}+\partial_{r}^{2}+r^{-1}\partial_{r}+r^{-2}\partial_{\theta}^{2})\Phi^{I}=\lambda\overline{\Phi}{}^{J}\Phi^{J}\Phi^{I}.

(82)

The
\mathrm{O}(2N-2)
symmetry requires that
\braket{\Phi^{I}(x)}=0
if
I\neq 1
. Additionally, conformal symmetry along the defect and the
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry generated by
Q_{s}
(
15
) constrain the form of
\braket{\Phi^{I}(x)}
to be

\displaystyle\braket{\Phi^{I}(x)}=\delta^{I1}e^{iv\theta}\varphi(r),

(83)

where
\varphi(r)
is a function only depending on
r
. It is constrained by the equation of motion (
82
) to obey

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime\prime}(r)+r^{-1}\varphi^{\prime}(r)-v^{2}r^{-2}\varphi(r)=\lambda\overline{\varphi}(r)\varphi(r)^{2}

(84)

away from the defect.

It is possible to solve this equation of motion with a source term to obtain a non-trivial profile
\varphi(r)
for all values of
0<r<+\infty
. This solution would track the entire RG flow from the UV to the IR of the theory. However, as we are only interested in the IR fixed point, it is much easier to search for a scale-invariant solution at large
r
, which has to take the form

\displaystyle\varphi(r)=c_{v}r^{-1},

(85)

based upon the leading-order dimension of
\Phi
,
\Delta_{\Phi}=1+O(\varepsilon)
. The only way for this to be consistent with (
84
) is if

\displaystyle(1-v^{2})=\lambda|c_{v}|^{2}.

(86)

The critical value of the bulk coupling at the critical point is given by
kleinert2001critical

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}=\frac{8\pi^{2}\varepsilon}{N+4}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})

(87)

which shows that to leading order in
\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle|c_{v}|^{2}=\frac{(1-v^{2})(N+4)}{8\pi^{2}\varepsilon}.

(88)

In other words,

\displaystyle\langle\Phi^{1}(x)\rangle=e^{iv(\theta-\theta_{0})}\sqrt{\frac{(1-v^{2})(N+4)}{8\pi^{2}\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{r},

(89)

where
\theta_{0}
is determined by the phase of the UV coupling through the RG flow. As in section
3
, we set
\theta_{0}=0
by a transverse rotation.

This can be compared to our large-
N
analysis in section
3
. We determine the value of
J
in
d=4-\varepsilon
in appendix
B
, and using (
47
) find that to leading order in
\varepsilon
and
1/N
,

\displaystyle\braket{\Phi^{1}(x)}=e^{iv\theta}\sqrt{\frac{(1-v^{2})N}{8\pi^{2}\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{r}.

(90)

This agrees with (
89
) to the leading order at large
N
.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have explored a spinning conformal defect in the
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model. Specifically we looked at the IR fixed point of an RG flow triggered by a relevant defect operator on a monodromy defect in the
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model. We examined this spinning DCFT in large-
N
and
4-\varepsilon
expansions.

The main results in the large-
N
expansion include the leading-order scaling dimensions of various defect operators, and the value of bulk one-point function
\braket{\Phi^{I}}
. These results were then compared to corresponding results to leading order in the
4-\varepsilon
-expansion, and also to expectations from conformal perturbation theory for
v\approx v^{*}
. Agreement between these results provides a useful cross-check for the validity of our results, as does comparing them in the case
d=3
,
v=0
to the results of
Cuomo:2021kfm
.

There are several natural directions in which this work could be extended. Firstly, it would be interesting to study the pinning monodromy DCFT in more detail. For instance, developing a systematic
4-\varepsilon
expansion seems like a conceptually important task. In particular, one can try comparing, in the spirit of
deSabbata:2024xwn
, the results of this expansion at
v=0
with the more traditional
4-\varepsilon
expansion of the pinning field defect in
d=3
. The conformal perturbation theory around
v\approx v^{*}
can also be explored further.

Secondly, we considered only one possible relevant perturbation and it is interesting to explore others, such as the simultaneous perturbations. It would also be interesting to find other examples of spinning DCFTs and to investigate their Weyl anomalies
Kravchuk:2024qoh
as well as integrated identities involving tilt and displacement operators
Gabai:2025hwf
;
Gabai:2025zcs
;
Girault:2025kzt
;
DKKFuture
.

Appendix A
Bulk-to-defect OPE of a monodromy defect in the
\mathrm{O}(2N)
model

The bulk-to-defect OPE in a monodromy defect can be written as
Giombi:2021uae

\displaystyle\Phi^{I}(x)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}+v}C_{s}\frac{e^{is\theta}}{r^{\Delta_{\Phi}-\widehat{\Delta}_{s}}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}r^{2m}(\vec{\partial}_{y}^{2})^{m}}{m!\,2^{2m}(\widehat{\Delta}_{s}+2-\frac{d}{2})_{m}}\widehat{\Psi}^{I}_{s}(\vec{y}).

(91)

and

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi}{}^{I}(x)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}+v}C_{s}^{*}\frac{e^{-is\theta}}{r^{\Delta_{\Phi}-\widehat{\Delta}_{s}}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}r^{2m}(\vec{\partial}_{y}^{2})^{m}}{m!\,2^{2m}(\widehat{\Delta}_{s}+2-\frac{d}{2})_{m}}\widehat{\overline{\Psi}}{}^{I}_{s}(\vec{y}).

(92)

For
v=0
or
v=\tfrac{1}{2}
, this can be written in terms of the real fields as

\displaystyle\phi^{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}+v}\frac{e^{is\theta}}{r^{\Delta_{\Phi}-\widehat{\Delta}_{s}}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}r^{2m}(\vec{\partial}_{y}^{2})^{m}}{m!\,2^{2m}(\widehat{\Delta}_{s}+2-\frac{d}{2})_{m}}\widehat{\chi}^{a}_{s}(\vec{y}),

(93)

where

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}^{2I-1}_{s}(\vec{y})=\frac{\widehat{\overline{\Psi}}{}^{I}_{s}(\vec{y})+\widehat{\Psi}^{I}_{s}(\vec{y})}{2},\qquad\widehat{\chi}^{2I}_{s}(\vec{y})=\frac{i(\widehat{\overline{\Psi}}{}^{I}_{s}(\vec{y})-\widehat{\Psi}^{I}_{s}(\vec{y}))}{2}.

(94)

These obey the reality conditions that
\overline{\widehat{\chi}^{a}_{s}}=\widehat{\chi}^{a}_{-s}

Appendix B
Regularization of
G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)

In this appendix, we shall explain how we regularize
G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)
, as defined in (
30
). We can use (
57
) and (
54
) to find that

\displaystyle G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{s}}(X,X)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\frac{1}{\nu^{2}+(\Delta-\tfrac{d-2}{2})^{2}}\Omega_{\nu}(X_{1},X_{2})

(95)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\pi^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}+\sqrt{s^{2}-v^{2}+\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}}\right)}{4\cos\left(\frac{\pi d}{2}\right)\Gamma(d-2)\Gamma\left(-\frac{d-4}{2}+\sqrt{s^{2}-v^{2}+\frac{(d-2)^{2}}{4}}\right)},

(96)

for
d>2
, where the integral can be performed by completing the contour in the upper half plane. In the limit
\ell\to\pm\infty
we find the following asymptotic

\displaystyle G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{\ell+v}}(X,X)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\pi^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\sec\left(\frac{\pi d}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}{4\Gamma(d-2)}(|\ell|^{d-3}\pm(d-3)v|\ell|^{d-4})+\mathcal{O}(|\ell|^{d-5}).

(97)

We can hence see that the sum
G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}+v}G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{s}}(X,X)
will not converge for
d>2
. Therefore, for
2<d<4
, we regularize the sum by explicitly resumming the leading asymptotic term for
d<2
and analytically continuing,

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\Bigg(G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{v}}(X,X)-\frac{\pi^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\sec\left(\frac{\pi d}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}{2\,\Gamma(d-2)}\zeta(3-d)

(98)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{\ell+v}}(X,X)+G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{-\ell+v}}(X,X)+\frac{\pi^{1-\frac{d}{2}}\sec\left(\frac{\pi d}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma(d-2)}\ell^{d-3}\right)\Bigg).

Equation (
98
) is still singular at
d=3
, but can be generalized to this case by continuity in
d
. This leads to
G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)
being well-defined for any
2<d<4
. We do not know a closed form for the sum, but it can be numerically approximated by truncation in
\ell
.

We also require
G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)
in
d=4-\varepsilon
expansion. We find that there is divergence as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This divergence can be extracted from the zeta function, and we find that to the leading order in
\varepsilon

\displaystyle G_{\mathrm{bb}}(x,x)=\frac{v^{2}-1}{8\pi^{2}\varepsilon}+O(\varepsilon^{0}).

(99)

Appendix C
An explicit formula for
\widetilde{C}_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}(\nu)

In this appendix we derive the expression for

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\int d^{d-1}X_{1}\,\sqrt{G(X_{1})}\,G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{1}}(X_{1},X_{2})G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{2}}(X_{2},X_{1})\Omega_{\nu}(X_{1},X_{2}),

(100)

given in equation (
69
). In the case
\Delta_{1}=\Delta_{2}
this has been computed in
Carmi:2018qzm
. We follow a similar strategy in that we compute the residues of the poles of
\widetilde{C}_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}(\nu)
in
\nu
and then recover
\widetilde{C}_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}(\nu)
using Mittag-Leffler’s theorem.

By AdS-isometry invariance, the integral (

\displaystyle\sigma(x_{1},x_{2})=2\arcsin\left(\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2})^{2}+(\vec{y}_{1}-\vec{y}_{2})^{2}}{2r_{1}r_{2}}\right),

(101)

which gives

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{\Delta_{1}\Delta_{2}}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma(\sinh\sigma)^{d-2}G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{1}}(\sigma)G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta_{2}}(\sigma)\Omega_{\nu}(\sigma).

(102)

Explicit expressions for these quantities can be found in
Carmi:2018qzm
,

\displaystyle\Omega_{\nu}(\sigma)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}+i\nu\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{d-2}{2}-i\nu\right)}{4\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(d-2)\Gamma(i\nu)\Gamma(-i\nu)}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{d-2}{2}+i\nu,\frac{d-2}{2}-i\nu;\frac{d-1}{2};-\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right),

(103)

\displaystyle G_{\text{AdS}}^{\Delta}(\sigma)=\frac{\Gamma(\Delta)}{2\pi^{\tfrac{d-2}{2}}\,\Gamma\!\left(\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+2\right)\big(2\cosh(\sigma)\big)^{\Delta}}{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{2},\frac{\Delta+1}{2};\;\Delta-\frac{d}{2}+2;\;\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(\sigma)}\right).

To simplify the integral, we shall strip off some
\sigma
-independent prefactors, and evaluate,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma(\sinh\sigma)^{d-2}g^{\Delta_{1}}(\sigma)g^{\Delta_{2}}(\sigma)\omega_{\nu}(\sigma).

(105)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{\nu}(\sigma)

\displaystyle={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{d-2}{2}+i\nu,\frac{d-2}{2}-i\nu;\frac{d-1}{2};-\sinh^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2}\right)\right)

(107)

It is useful to write

\displaystyle\omega^{R}(\nu)=\frac{2^{d-3}\Gamma(\tfrac{d-1}{2})\Gamma(-i\nu)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\tfrac{d-2}{2}-i\nu)}e^{-(\frac{d-2}{2}+i\nu)\sigma}{}_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{d-2}{2},\tfrac{d-2}{2}+i\nu;i\nu+1;e^{-2\sigma}),

(108)

in order to explicitly exhibit large-

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\sigma(\sinh\sigma)^{d-2}g^{\Delta_{1}}(\sigma)g^{\Delta_{2}}(\sigma)\omega^{R}_{\nu}(\sigma).

In order to evaluate it, we introduce the function

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{d-2}{}_{2}F_{1}(\Delta_{1},\tfrac{d-2}{2};\Delta_{1}-\tfrac{d}{2}+2;z){}_{2}F_{1}(\Delta_{2},\tfrac{d-2}{2};\Delta_{2}-\tfrac{d}{2}+2;z)

Gauge-Fixing Quantum Density Operators At Scale
Amit Jamadagni, Eugene Dumitrescu

Abstract
We provide theory, algorithms, and simulations of non-equilibrium quantum systems using a one-dimensional (1D) completely-positive (CP), matrix-product (MP) density-operator ( ρ \rho ) representation. By generalizing the matrix product state’s orthogonality center, to additionally store positive classical mixture correlations, the MP ρ \rho factorization naturally emerges. In this work we analytically and numerically examine the virtual freedoms associated with the representation of quantum density operators. Using this augmented perspective, we simplify algorithms in certain limits to integrate the canonical form’s master equation dynamics. This enables us to quickly evolve under the dynamics of two-body quantum channels without resorting to optimization-based methods. In addition to this technical advance, we also scale-up numerical examples and discuss implications for accurately modeling hardware architectures and predicting their performance. This includes an example of the quantum to classical transition of informationally leaky, i.e., decohering, qubits. In this setting, due to loss from environmental interactions, non-local complex coherence correlations are converted into local and incoherent classical statistical mixture correlations. Lastly, the representation of both global and local correlations is discussed. We expect this work to have applications in additional non-equilibrium settings, beyond qubit engineering.

II
Matrix Product States

In this section, we briefly review the notion of coherence correlations as singular values of a quantum wavefunction and show how this leads to the MPS form.

II.1
Quantum correlations and entanglement

Consider a pure state
|\Psi\rangle
subject to a left (L) / right (R) bi-partition. The Schmidt decomposition of
|\Psi\rangle
, i.e., singular value decomposition (SVD), splits the state into the left (L) and right (R) degrees of freedom. The decomposition reads
|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{\lambda}c_{\lambda}|\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{L}\otimes|\phi_{\lambda}\rangle_{R}
, where
\vec{c}=\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{\chi}\}
is a list of quantum
coherence correlation
amplitudes which are
L_{2}-
normalized:
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{\lambda=\chi}|c_{\lambda}|^{2}=1
. When L and R each consist of a single spin-1/2 particle (qubit), the bi-partite entanglement is maximized by the locus of Bell states which are defined as states with entanglement correlations
\vec{c}=\{c_{1},c_{2}\}=\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\}
. (See App.
A
for a more details.)

If
|\Psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle_{L}\otimes|\phi\rangle_{R}
, with dim
[\vec{c}]=c_{1}=1
, then
|\psi\rangle
and
|\phi\rangle
are
disentangled
and representable using memory resources describing the
individual
sub-systems. In contrast to the multiplicative scaling–causing the exponential curse of dimensionality–for arbitrary entangled systems, the memory footprint of disentangled quantum states scales
additively
. A consequence is that the representation and manipulation of disentangled systems is embarrassingly parallelizable.

Tri-partite qubit states may likewise be represented with a correlation vector
|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{s_{i}\in\{0,1\}}c_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}}|s_{1}\rangle\otimes|s_{2}\rangle\otimes|s_{3}\rangle
where

refers to the region

-dimensional physical Hilbert space. Spin-1/2s (qubits) have

while spin-1 particles (qutrits) have
s_{i}\in\{\uparrow,0,\downarrow\}(\{0,1,2\})
, and so on for
d
-dimensional systems.

MPS Canonical Form

Instead of

(3)

\chi

2

tensors. For the remainder of this work we abuse notation and refer to virtual indices by their dimensionality. Following the notation of Ref.

Canonical MPS data-structures are then rigorously defined as those which satisfy a pair of quadratic tensor constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{i}A^{(i)}{A^{(i)}}^{\dagger}=\mathds{1}_{\chi_{i}},\;\forall\;1\leq i\neq\text{OC}\leq N,

(4a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}A^{(i)}\Lambda^{(i-1)}{A^{(i)}}^{\dagger}=\Lambda^{(i)},\;\forall\;1\leq i\leq N,

(4b)

The computational complexity of estimating the expectation value of observables up to a controlled approximation error,

\mathcal{O}(\text{poly}(N,\chi))
operations. This implies in the limit of

V
Conclusions

In this work we have examined the operational interpretation of MP
\rho
as a classical-quantum data structure that encodes both complex coherence and real mixture correlations. Our terminology for MP
\rho
is consistent with the prior introduced LPDO forms. However, complementing earlier works, our work examines technical algorithms to update the canonical form, provided intuitive simulations, and also developed a physical and operational interpretation of the virtual mixture indices and associated degrees of freedom. As such, this investigation builds expands the technical tool-set to work with density operators and also provides intuition and simulations to highlight of the MP
\rho
form’s computational abilities.

Within the MP
\rho
formalism we have first examined the evolution of entangled quantum states under the action of one- and two-local quantum channels. Previously, the action of two-local channel operators was defined in terms of an additional SVD in conjunction with an optimization routine minimizing a residual bond dimension
Werner2016
. To simplify the application of two-local noise channels, we develop an optimization-free update. This update brings the tensors contracted, by the two-body channel, back to the MP
\rho
canonical form. To verify our technique, we have tested the application of two-body noise channels within the context of a analytical solvable example. Further, in the context of partial trace, we highlight that the introduced technique can be employed to represent global mixture correlations with a local gauge. For example, we further present an algorithm to fully understand partial trace in the MP
\rho
formalism.

To stress test the computational capabilities of this framework, we present several numerical simulations involving single and two-body channel operators. For example, we have dissipatively evolved randomly entangled 50-qubit MPSs. In contrast, naive simulations of density operators within in a state-vector format are limited to system with under 25 qubits, even with supercomputing resources. Our work therefore highlights the large locus of mixed quantum states which are amenable to a classical multi-linear representation and subsequently simulation.

The MP
\rho
formalism provides tools to explore avenues that have been gaining traction in the recent times
kato_2024
;
Srinivasan_2021
. To mention a few, analyzing the dynamics of many-body systems in an open setting i.e., in the presence of the noise, can be hugely accelerated

. The simulation of quantum systems necessitating supercomputer resources, thereby strongly demarcating the classical simulable limits, remains an intriguing open question
Chen_2023
. In future work, we conjecture that sampling of the decorated mixture index could be useful in parallelized algorithms, which themselves might provide crucial insights into the open question of quantum simulable limits. Another related avenue is to, based on the decorated
\kappa
index, investigate the notion of separability. Further, from an experimental viewpoint, MP
\rho
tools can be deployed to not only characterize, but also mitigate, errors in the current quantum architectures, thereby proving a useful tool in future quantum control theory
Mangini_2024
. A last future grand challenge is to analyze quantum algorithms, in terms of their computational complexity, in the presence of realistic noise models and to, if possible, optimize the associated performance metrics.

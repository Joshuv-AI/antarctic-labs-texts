Assessment of various artificial intelligence applications’ performance in responding to multiple-choice endodontics questions
Authors: Sevda Durust Baris, Ali Erdemir, Ali Turkyilmaz, Dilek Hancerliogullari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundArtificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative technology in the domain of healthcare, including endodontics. This study aims to evaluate and compare the performance of six AI chatbots (ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, DeepSeek-R1, and ChatGPT-5) in answering multiple-choice questions related to endodontics.MethodsThe study evaluated the accuracy performance of six different AI chatbots in answering 122 multiple-choice questions related to endodontics asked in iterations of the Turkish Dentistry Specialization Exam (DUS) held between 2012 and 2021. The questions were divided into two categories: ‘knowledge-based’ and ‘case-based’. The responses were categorized as ‘correct’ or ‘incorrect’. The chatbots’ performance levels in answering all knowledge-based and case-based questions correctly were recorded and compared. The relationship between the categorical variables was evaluated using descriptive statistics, Cochran’s Q test, and exploratory pairwise comparisons using McNemar’s test. The significance level was set at p < 0.05, with an adjusted significance level of p = 0.00416 for multiple comparisons.ResultsNo statistically significant differences between the general accuracy performance of six different AI chatbots in answering DUS endodontic questions (p > 0.05) exist. No significant differences were found between the models for case-based and knowledge-based questions (p > 0.05).ConclusionThe evaluated AI chatbots demonstrated comparable accuracy in answering multiple-choice endodontic questions. While these findings suggest potential utility in dental education, the use of AI in clinical practice should be considered supportive rather than definitive. Therefore, ongoing evaluation and improvement of chatbot accuracy and reliability remain important.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-08762-0.

Background

The field of artificial intelligence (AI) is a rapidly evolving technology that focuses on the development of intelligent machines capable of mimicking biological intelligence [1]. In recent years, interest in AI technologies has increased significantly with the contribution of successful projects, and research on this subject is gaining importance.

ChatGPT (Chat Generative Pretrained Transformer) is a state-of-the-art large language model (LLM) developed by OpenAI [2]. ChatGPT can generate, translate, and summarize text in a humanlike manner. Gemini, launched by Google DeepMind in 2023, is another LLM that can perform tasks such as text generation, translation, and summarization as well as creative content production [3]. More recent models, including ChatGPT-4o and GPT-5, have further advanced these capabilities by incorporating multimodal processing and enhanced reasoning performance [4, 5]. In addition, domain-adapted models such as ScholarGPT and Scholar AI have been developed to support academic tasks, including literature analysis and information retrieval [6, 7]. Recently, DeepSeek-R1 has emerged as a competitive alternative to leading LLMs, demonstrating performance comparable to state-of-the-art models [8–10].

AI technologies are used in many fields today, and LLMs that are continuously evolving and updating can become valuable tools for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and medical education [11, 12]. Revolutionary technological developments in the field of AI are rapidly changing the fields of health services and education [13], affecting the fields of medical and dental education by ushering in many innovations and providing advantages to both students and educators [14]. Today, e-learning provided by AI is used in dentistry curricula [15]. AI is transforming education and training by helping students answer multiple-choice and open-ended questions and prepare homework [15]. Digital learning resources are now preferred by students due to the convenience and speed they offer over traditional educational materials such as books and notes [16]. LLMs are thought to be useful for dentistry students and graduates preparing for exams that include multiple-choice or open-ended questions, thereby facilitating their academic development.

Despite the rapid integration of AI into dental education and clinical support, robust evidence regarding the performance of contemporary large language models in high-stakes standardized examinations remains limited [17–20]. This issue is particularly important because multiple-choice examinations play a critical role in dental specialization processes, and the reliability of AI-generated responses in such contexts may influence educational practices and clinical decision-making.

The Dentistry Specialization Examination (DUS) is a national examination required for postgraduate specialization in Türkiye. Although the performance of AI chatbots has been investigated in various medical and dental examinations [13, 17–21], evidence regarding their performance in endodontics-related multiple-choice questions remains limited. Additionally, the existing literature includes only a limited number of studies [22–24] evaluating the performance of contemporary models such as DeepSeek-R1 and ChatGPT-5 in comparison with other AI chatbots. Moreover, most previous studies have focused either on single AI models or on general dentistry questions, whereas only a limited number of investigations have specifically addressed the field of endodontics. This highlights the need for comparative studies evaluating multiple AI models within the domain of endodontics. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate and compare the performance of ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, DeepSeek-R1, and ChatGPT-5 in answering multiple-choice endodontic questions asked in the DUS exam in Türkiye. Furthermore, this study aims to evaluate the performance of AI chatbots not only in terms of overall accuracy but also in answering case-based questions, which include clinical scenarios requiring clinical reasoning, and knowledge-based questions, which assess theoretical knowledge. The null hypothesis of the study is that there is no significant difference between the accuracy performances of AI chatbots in answering endodontics-related questions.

Methods

Ethical considerations

This study was designed as a comparative cross-sectional accuracy study evaluating the performance of six AI chatbots in answering multiple-choice questions related to endodontics. Since the study exclusively evaluated publicly available data and did not involve human participants, it was deemed exempt from ethical approval. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Summary

The DUS was held once a year between 2015 and 2022 by the Student Selection and Placement Center (ÖSYM). The exam was held twice a year from 2012 to 2014; as of 2023, the DUS is held in the spring and fall semesters. The exam consists of a total of 120 multiple-choice questions, 40 of which cover basic science and 80 of which revolve around clinical science. Each question includes five answer choices, with only one correct option. Ten questions in the clinical sciences portion consist of endodontics-related questions. A power analysis was conducted to determine the minimum sample size required for the study. The analysis assumed an effect size of w = 0.35, a significance level of 5% (α = 0.05), and a statistical power of 80% (1 − β = 0.80). Based on these assumptions, the minimum required sample size was calculated as 105 questions. Accordingly, 130 endodontics-related multiple-choice questions identified from DUS examinations conducted between 2012 and 2021 were considered for inclusion. However, one and two endodontics questions in the 2015 and 2017 DUS, respectively, were not included in the study because they were cancelled by ÖSYM. Furthermore, five questions comprising visual elements that could not be clearly directed towards AI chatbots were also excluded from the study. Consequently, eight questions were excluded from 130 endodontic questions, resulting in the inclusion of a total of 122 questions in the study. The dataset utilized pertains to endodontics, derived explicitly from the open-source question bank of the DUS covering the years 2012–2021 (https://www.osym.gov.tr/TR,15070/dus-cikmis-sorular.html). The list of questions is also provided in the supplementary file 1. These questions assessed theoretical knowledge and clinical competence in endodontics. To preserve the originality of the questions and minimize potential bias, the questions were entered into the AI chatbots in their original Turkish form without translation. In addition, the questions were submitted without supplementary prompts to simulate typical user interactions.

In this study, 122 multiple-choice questions were asked of ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, DeepSeek-R1, and ChatGPT-5 chatbots by a single researcher (SDB) on 20 September 2025. The questions were divided into two different categories based on predefined criteria by two expert endodontists: ‘knowledge-based’ (questions that directly test theoretical knowledge about a specific topic) and ‘case-based’ (questions that include clinical scenarios and require clinical reasoning) [21, 25]. To ensure consensus on question categories, two researchers (SDB and DH), both experienced endodontists with substantial clinical and academic experience in endodontics (8 and 10 years, respectively), independently evaluated the questions. Cohen’s kappa was employed to evaluate inter-rater agreement and yielded a value of 0.88, which indicated excellent agreement among raters. If any disagreements arose between researchers, the final decision was made by consulting a third researcher (AE) with more than 25 years of experience in endodontics. All internet history and all cookies were deleted before the questions were entered into the AI chatbots. In case any problems (freezing or delays) occurred while entering the questions into the applications, the researchers planned to ask the questions again, although no such problems materialized. To avoid biases caused by the learning and memory effects of AI chatbots, the questions were asked only once, with a new conversation option selected each time. In addition, no feedback was given to the chatbots after their answers were received.

The responses were categorized as ‘correct’ or ‘incorrect’ according to the answer key on ÖSYM’s official website. The questions were submitted to the AI chatbots by one researcher (SDB). To minimize potential assessment bias, each chatbot was assigned a code by the same researcher before evaluation. The responses were then independently documented and verified in an Excel file (Microsoft, Redmond, WA) by two additional researchers (AE and DH), who were blinded to the identities of the AI chatbots during the assessment process. The chatbots’ performance levels in answering all knowledge-based and case-based questions were subsequently recorded and compared.

Statistical analysis

In this study, descriptive statistics (frequencies) and Cochran’s Q test were used to compare six LLMs (ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, DeepSeek-R1, and ChatGPT-5). A significance level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. In addition, pairwise comparisons using McNemar’s test were performed for exploratory evaluation. To account for multiple comparisons, the adjusted significance level was set at p = 0.00416.

Discussion

ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, DeepSeek-R1, and ChatGPT-5 are all LLMs that have been trained to carry out tasks such as generating, translating, and summarizing text as well as generating creative content [3, 4, 10, 26]. The use of LLMs in the healthcare and education fields is becoming increasingly widespread [13, 27].

Endodontics is a technically and biologically complex field requiring substantial theoretical knowledge and clinical expertise [28]. AI technologies developed for endodontic treatments are used both clinically and radiologically before and after procedures in areas such as diagnostic support, determining the presence of periapical lesions, detecting root fractures, demonstrating the differences in root and canal morphologies, calculating correct working lengths in the canal, estimating retreatment status, assessing pain developing after endodontic treatment, performing restorative treatments of endodontically treated teeth, and detecting fillings and caries [29, 30].

Given the complexity of endodontic education and clinical training, AI-supported systems may provide valuable support for both theoretical learning and clinical reasoning processes [29, 30]. Therefore, how AI applications can be integrated into education in the field of endodontics represents a significant research area that requires further development [30]. This study evaluated the accuracy performance of six different chatbots in answering multiple-choice endodontic questions in DUS. No statistically significant differences were found between ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, DeepSeek-R1, and ChatGPT-5 in terms of their overall performance in answering all questions. Consequently, the null hypothesis of the study was accepted. Although the differences were not statistically significant, DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated the highest performance with an accuracy rate of 81.1% (99/122). DeepSeek-R1 was followed by ChatGPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, and ChatGPT-5, each with an accuracy rate of 79.5% (97/122). The lowest accuracy rate was observed for Scholar AI at 77.0% (94/122). Only a limited number of studies have compared the accuracy performance of DeepSeek with other AI chatbots on dentistry-related topics. A previous study [13], evaluated the accuracy performance of eight different AI chatbots (Gemini 1.5, Gemini 2.0, ChatGPT-4o, ChatGPT-4, ChatGPT-o1, Copilot, Claude 3.5 and DeepSeek) in answering 100 multiple-choice oral pathology questions asked on the DUS. ChatGPT-o1 was reported to have the highest accuracy (96 correct answers), followed by Claude (84 correct answers), followed by DeepSeek and Gemini 2.0 with an equal number of correct answers (82 correct answers). ChatGPT-4o was reported to have slightly lower performance than DeepSeek, with 79 correct answers. A study evaluating the performance of ChatGPT-4, ChatGPT-5, DeepSeek, and Gemini in the diagnosis and management of dental trauma cases reported no statistically significant differences in performance among the applications [22]. These findings support the findings of our study. Another study [31] comparing the accuracy performance of DeepSeek and ChatGPT in answering questions about lacrimal drainage disorders reported that both AI models were similar in terms of the accuracy of their responses. A study [32] comparing ChatGPT 3.5, and Gemini in answering multiple-choice prosthodontics specialist questions found no significant difference between them, although Gemini provided more accurate answers. Another study [33] evaluating AI performance in answering technical questions in endodontic surgery reported that ScholarGPT showed the highest accuracy performance (97.7%), followed by ChatGPT-4o (90.1%). Gemini had the lowest accuracy performance (59.5%). The findings of the present study can be examined in conjunction with extant literature on the subject to reveal that different AI chatbots can exhibit varying levels of accuracy performance in different subjects. It is therefore important to acknowledge that a single AI chatbot may not be sufficiently useful in a given field or, conversely, that a chatbot may not be equally useful in all fields.

In the evaluation of AI chatbots for knowledge-based questions, ChatGPT-4o demonstrated the highest accuracy rate at 85.6% (77/90). ScholarGPT, Scholar AI, Gemini 2.0, and ChatGPT-5 each achieved an accuracy rate of 84.4% (76/90). Although the difference was not statistically significant, DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated the lowest accuracy rate at 83.3% (75/90). Each of the AI chatbots had very similar numbers of correct answers for knowledge-based questions. This may be due to the fact that all of the chatbots included in this study had good access to the knowledge bases on which they were trained. A study evaluating ChatGPT’s performance on the United States Medical Licensing Examination questions found that ChatGPT showed an acceptable level of success [34]. A paper comparing the diagnostic accuracy of ChatGPT-4o in endodontic cases with the performance of dentistry students reported that ChatGPT-4o showed higher accuracy performance than students [35]. Taşsöker reported in a previous study [17] that ChatGPT-4o exhibits superior accuracy and exhibits advanced reasoning capabilities, making it a promising educational tool. Our findings indicate that six different AI chatbots demonstrated comparable performance in answering knowledge-based questions. These results support the idea that LLM models can be integrated into medical and dental education to assist in teaching and facilitate clinical decision-making processes.

This study evaluated the performance of AI chatbots on case-based questions and found that, although the differences were not statistically significant, DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated the highest accuracy rate at 75.0% (24/32). Gemini 2.0 and ChatGPT-5 followed with an accuracy rate of 65.6% (21/32), whereas ChatGPT-4o and ScholarGPT each achieved an accuracy rate of 62.5% (20/32). Scholar AI demonstrated the lowest accuracy rate at 56.3% (18/32). The six different AI-powered chatbots in the current study exhibited similar performance in answering case-based questions. The fact that the AI chatbots in the current study had similar performance levels in case-based questions suggests that they may have similar skill levels when interpreting clinical scenarios and case management. A previous study [36] comparing the performance of ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 on the Polish infectious diseases specialist exam reported that both GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated the ability to pass the exam. It has been suggested that GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 could be potential educational tools. In addition, DeepSeek-R1 has been noted as a very new model developed at a significantly lower cost than GPT-4o, yet it was able to achieve a performance level comparable to that of GPT-4o. In addition, the study suggested that DeepSeek-R1 should be used with caution in clinical and academic fields. In contrast to the current study, one paper reported that the accuracy performance of ScholarGPT in answering technical questions in oral and maxillofacial surgery was higher than that of ChatGPT-4 [37]. A study comparing the quality and readability of responses to frequently asked patient questions regarding meniscal injuries generated by ChatGPT-5 and DeepSeek-R1 reported that DeepSeek demonstrated superior overall content quality, while both models provided information that was suitable and comprehensible for general patient education [38]. An examination of different studies on the subject reveals that AI chatbots have the potential to and make important contributions to education and training. However, this technology must reach a reliable level of accuracy before it can be widely adopted [39].

The findings of the present study also have potential implications for the integration of AI technologies into dental education and clinical practice. Although the evaluated chatbots demonstrated comparable performance levels, the absence of consistently superior accuracy indicates that AI systems should currently be regarded as supportive tools rather than independent decision-making systems. In particular, AI chatbots may assist clinicians and dental students in rapidly accessing information, reviewing theoretical concepts, generating differential diagnoses, and supporting clinical reasoning processes in endodontics [40–42]. However, the outputs generated by these systems should always be critically evaluated and interpreted in conjunction with current scientific evidence and professional clinical judgment.

The broader implementation of AI in dentistry may be facilitated through structured educational programs, evidence-based clinical guidelines, and training focused on the appropriate interpretation of AI-generated information. Increasing clinicians’ familiarity with the strengths and limitations of AI systems may improve confidence in their use and promote more responsible integration into routine dental practice. Such developments may contribute to the safer and more effective incorporation of AI technologies into endodontic education and patient care [40, 43].

For clinicians who intend to incorporate AI into routine practice, a cautious approach is recommended. AI outputs should be critically appraised and cross-checked against established clinical guidelines and current evidence before being applied in patient care [44]. Clinicians should be encouraged to use AI as a complementary tool for information retrieval and decision support while maintaining full responsibility for clinical decision-making [44, 45].

The present study is subject to the limitation that the evaluation of AI chatbots was restricted to Turkish, text-based questions. If questions containing images or tables were included in the study, different results may have been obtained. AI chatbots may exhibit variable levels of accuracy in answering questions related to different curricula and topics. Furthermore, the results reflect the capabilities of AI chatbots as of September 2025. The use of feedback, updates to the datasets of the models, and advancements in deep learning have the potential to yield divergent results should the same method be employed in the future.

Another limitation of the current study is that each AI model was asked each question only once. Given the inherent variability in the outputs of large language models, responses to the same question may differ across repeated interactions. Future studies should therefore incorporate multiple iterations per question to enable a more comprehensive assessment of model performance and consistency. In addition, the absence of human participants limits direct benchmarking against human performance. Comparative studies involving dental students and experienced clinicians, as well as the inclusion of larger and more diverse question sets, would provide a more robust evaluation of the educational and potential clinical relevance of AI chatbot performance. Furthermore, future investigations should incorporate multimodal question formats, including clinical images and radiographs, to better reflect real clinical scenarios. Longitudinal studies evaluating the effects of AI-assisted learning on clinical competence, diagnostic reasoning, and decision-making processes may also provide important insights into the educational and clinical utility of AI systems in dentistry.

Conclusion

Although there were no statistically significant differences in the performance of answering DUS endodontics questions among the six different AI chatbots in this study, the findings revealed that DeepSeek-R1 tended to perform better in overall accuracy and case-based questions, while ChatGPT-4o tended to perform better on knowledge-based questions.

AI technologies may provide supportive contributions to dental education, clinical training, and examination preparation for undergraduate and postgraduate dentistry students. However, AI chatbots still have important limitations in the field of endodontics, and their use should be considered supportive rather than definitive. Further research is needed to improve our understanding of the limits and capabilities of AI in dentistry.

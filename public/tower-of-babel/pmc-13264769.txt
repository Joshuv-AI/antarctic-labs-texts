A simple preoperative nomogram for predicting high-risk parathyroid lesions: a tool to guide surgical strategy in primary hyperparathyroidism
Authors: Juan Bai, Zhenxing Peng, Xiaomin Li, Chuanshuo Li, Ling Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDistinguishing benign from high-risk parathyroid lesions [atypical parathyroid tumor (APT) and parathyroid carcinoma (PC)] preoperatively is crucial for surgical strategy, yet remains difficult in tertiary referral centers confronting an enriched risk profile. This study sought to develop and validate a practical nomogram for individualized risk quantification in this specific clinical context.MethodsWe conducted a single-center, retrospective cohort study of patients with biochemically active solitary parathyroid lesions who underwent surgery between January 2009 and May 2024 at Beijing Shijitan Hospital, Capital Medical University. A total of 175 patients were included in the final analysis. A nomogram was developed via multivariable logistic regression and underwent internal validation assessing discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility via decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsThree independent preoperative predictors comprised the final model: age at onset [adjusted odds ratio (aOR) =0.970; 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.944–0.996; P=0.02], serum calcium (aOR =7.233; 95% CI: 2.242–23.333; P=0.001), and parathyroid hormone (PTH) level (aOR =1.001; 95% CI: 1.000–1.002; P=0.03). The nomogram showed good discrimination [area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) =0.805; 95% CI: 0.730–0.880], satisfactory calibration (Hosmer-Lemeshow P=0.75), and yielded a positive net clinical benefit across threshold probabilities of 5–40%.ConclusionsThis preoperative nomogram effectively quantifies individual risk for high-risk parathyroid lesions. Designed for specialized, high-prevalence settings, it offers surgeons an evidence-based metric to inform initial surgical planning and to stratify patients for tailored operative approaches.

Key findings

• A simple preoperative nomogram using age at onset, serum calcium, and parathyroid hormone effectively predicts the combined risk of atypical parathyroid tumors and parathyroid carcinoma (PC).

• The model demonstrates good discrimination (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve =0.805) and a high negative predictive value (91.9%), reliably identifying low-risk patients suitable for minimally invasive surgery.

What is known and what is new?

• Current clinical practice and prediction models focus on identifying PC, often overlooking atypical tumors.

• This is the first nomogram specifically designed to preoperatively identify a unified high-risk spectrum comprising both atypical tumors and carcinoma, directly guiding the decision of surgical extent.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The nomogram offers surgeons an objective, evidence-based risk estimate to optimize the balance between oncologic efficacy and surgical morbidity.

• Surgeons can use this tool for preoperative risk stratification: proceed with minimally invasive surgery for low-risk patients and plan for potential en bloc resection for high-risk patients.

Introduction

Primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT) is most commonly caused by a solitary benign parathyroid adenoma (PA). However, a subset of patients presents with high-risk lesions—parathyroid carcinoma (PC) or atypical parathyroid tumor (APT)—where the standard of care shifts from minimally invasive surgery (1,2) to more extensive en bloc resection. This diagnostic uncertainty carries a dual risk: undertreatment may lead to recurrence (3), while overtreatment increases morbidity (4).

Accurate preoperative differentiation is crucial for surgical planning yet remains challenging. Current practice relies on individual high-risk markers such as markedly elevated parathyroid hormone (PTH), severe hypercalcemia, or large tumor volume (5-7), which lack sufficient specificity. Intraoperative frozen section is also of limited utility for definitive diagnosis (8,9).

Existing clinical guidelines offer management pathways for confirmed PC but lack preoperative risk stratification tools (10). Similarly, the 2022 World Health Organization (WHO) classification defines APT as a distinct entity (11,12), but provides no preoperative planning guidance. This is a critical gap because PC and APT are often indistinguishable preoperatively and even intraoperatively, forming a unified high-risk clinical spectrum (13,14). Consequently, current prediction models, predominantly focused on PC while excluding APT (15), fail to address the core surgical dilemma: how to preoperatively identify all patients who require tailored surgical preparedness.

To bridge this gap, we aimed to develop a nomogram that provides a preoperative risk estimate. Its value lies not in distinguishing specific pathologies, but in identifying patients—based solely on routine clinical data—whose risk profile justifies comprehensive planning for potential extended surgery. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://gs.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/gs-2026-0145/rc).

Methods

Study design and participants

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This single-center, retrospective cohort study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Beijing Shijitan Hospital, Capital Medical University (No. IIT2026-053-001). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. All clinical data were sourced from Beijing Shijitan Hospital, Capital Medical University.

Consecutive patients who underwent surgery with histopathologically confirmed solitary parathyroid lesions between January 2009 and May 2024 were reviewed. The initial cohort comprised 177 patients. For the specific aim of developing a preoperative prediction tool for biochemically active high-risk lesions, two patients with non-functioning PC were excluded, resulting in a final modeling cohort of 175 patients.

Histopathological diagnosis and criteria

All specimens were re-reviewed by two blinded endocrine pathologists according to the 2022 WHO classification (16).

❖ APT: fibrous bands, mitoses >5/10 high-power fields (HPF), or necrosis; no definitive invasion.

❖ Benign: adenoma or hyperplasia; none of the above features.

Data collection and predictor selection

Clinical and biochemical data were extracted from electronic medical records. Predictors were selected based on routine preoperative availability: age at onset (first documented hypercalcemia or related symptoms), gender, and preoperative serum levels of total calcium, phosphorus, intact PTH, and alkaline phosphatase (ALP).

Preoperative ultrasound findings were not included in the final prediction model. This decision was primarily dictated by the retrospective nature of the study: ultrasound reports over the 15-year period were non-standardized, often lacked key morphological details, and showed well-documented overlap between benign and malignant features (17-19). Other imaging modalities [sestamibi, computed tomography (CT)] were performed in only a minority of patients and were ineligible for analysis.

Tumor size was excluded as a candidate predictor due to a high (69.0%) and non-random missing rate in the PC subgroup, which posed a substantial risk of information bias in a preoperative model. For all other candidate predictors, missing data were minimal (<2%) and handled by complete-case analysis.

The outcome was dichotomized based on final histopathology: high-risk lesion (APT or carcinoma) vs. benign adenoma (PA or solitary PH).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 25.0; IBM Corp.) and R (version 4.5.2; R Foundation). Variables with P<0.10 in univariate analysis were entered into multivariable logistic regression with backward stepwise selection (retention criterion: P<0.05) to identify independent predictors. A nomogram was constructed based on the final multivariable model.

Model performance was evaluated through discrimination [area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC)], calibration (calibration plot and Hosmer-Lemeshow test), and clinical utility [decision curve analysis (DCA)]. Internal validation was performed using 1,000 bootstrap samples to estimate optimism and obtain a bias-corrected AUC. Predictive values were calculated at the optimal cutoff determined by the Youden index.

Intended clinical application

The nomogram is designed to provide a preoperative, individualized risk estimate to aid in surgical planning by stratifying patients, thereby informing the appropriate level of preoperative preparedness for potential extended resection.

Comparative analysis among pathological subtypes

Significant differences were observed across pathological subtypes (Table 1). Compared to patients with benign lesions (PA/PH), those with PC were significantly younger, more frequently male, and had higher preoperative serum calcium and PTH levels (all P<0.05). In contrast, no statistically significant differences were observed between PC and APT groups in age at onset, serum calcium, or PTH levels (all P>0.05). A palpable neck mass was documented in 4 (13.8%) PC patients.

Predictive modeling cohort

The final cohort for predictive modeling comprised 175 patients after excluding two with normocalcemic, non-functioning PC, as predefined in the “Methods” section. Based on the preoperative similarities detailed in Table 1, patients were dichotomized into a high-risk group (APT and PC, n=40, 22.9%) and a benign group (PA and PH, n=135, 77.1%).

Nomogram construction

Based on this final model, a preoperative nomogram was constructed to provide a visual tool for individualized risk assessment (Figure 1). To use the nomogram, a clinician locates the patient’s values for age at onset, serum calcium, and PTH on the corresponding axes, sums the assigned points, and projects the total points to the bottom axis to obtain the predicted probability of a high-risk lesion. For example, according to the nomogram, a 65-year-old patient with serum calcium of 2.6 mmol/L and PTH of 300 pg/mL would have a total point score of approximately 45, corresponding to a predicted probability of 10%, placing them in the low-risk category.

Model performance and validation

Discrimination and cutoff

The nomogram demonstrated good discriminative ability, with an AUC of 0.805 (95% CI: 0.730–0.880) (Figure 2). The optimal cutoff probability for maximizing both sensitivity and specificity was 0.172, yielding a sensitivity of 80.0% and a specificity of 67.4%. At this threshold, the positive predictive value (PPV) was 42.1% and the negative predictive value (NPV) was 91.9%, reflecting the pre-test probability of 22.9% in this cohort.

ROC curve of the prediction model for parathyroid tumors. AUC, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve; CI, confidence interval; ROC, receiver operating characteristic.

Calibration

The calibration curve demonstrated excellent agreement between predicted and observed outcomes across the risk spectrum (Figure 3). The model showed nearly perfect calibration, with a slope of 0.987, a non-significant Hosmer-Lemeshow test (P=0.75), and a Brier score of 0.131. Performance was particularly robust in the intermediate risk range (predicted probabilities 0.2–0.6).

Calibration plot for the nomogram. AUC, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve; CI, confidence interval.

Clinical utility

DCA (Figure 4) showed that stratifying patients by the nomogram to guide the choice between limited and extended resection provided a greater net benefit than either “treat-all” or “treat none” strategy across a wide, clinically relevant threshold probability range (approximately 5–40%).

DCA for the prediction model. AUC, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve; DCA, decision curve analysis.

Internal validation

Internal validation (Figure 5) was performed using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. The original AUC was 0.805. After optimism correction, the adjusted AUC was 0.797, indicating minimal model overfitting. The mean AUC across bootstrap resamples was 0.813, demonstrating stable discriminative performance.

Bootstrap internal validation of the prediction model. AUC, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve.

Discussion

Principal findings and clinical implications

We developed and validated a pragmatic preoperative nomogram to predict high-risk parathyroid lesions (APT/PC) in patients presenting with clinical suspicion of malignancy at a tertiary referral center. By incorporating three routinely available parameters, the model demonstrated robust performance (AUC =0.805) in this high-prevalence setting (22.9%) and, crucially, provided a positive net benefit across a wide threshold probability range (5–40%) on DCA (20,21). This indicates its potential as a decisive preoperative adjunct to refine clinical decision-making. It mitigates the traditional dependency on intraoperative ambiguity by providing an objective, quantitative risk profile, thereby facilitating a precision strategy: securing minimally invasive surgery for low-risk patients while rationally escalating to definitive en bloc resection for high-risk cases. By informing this distinction upfront, the tool seeks to optimize the fundamental balance between radical cure and surgical morbidity (3,4,8,9).

Comparison with existing literature

The comparable preoperative profiles of APT and PC in our cohort (Table 1) provide empirical support for their combined management, a concept underscored by the latest WHO classification (11). This biological and clinical overlap explains why models focused solely on carcinoma (15) are of limited utility in surgical planning. By targeting the shared “high-risk” phenotype, our nomogram effectively bridges this gap, offering a tool tailored to the decision of surgical extent rather than histological diagnosis.

Our modeling strategy deliberately excluded tumor size, despite its known association with malignancy (17), due to its high and non-random missing rate (e.g., 69.0% in PC cases)—a common challenge in real-world data. This decision prioritized model robustness and clinical applicability over conventional predictor inclusion. Consequently, the final nomogram relies on more reliably documented routinely available biochemical markers, which proved to be stronger independent predictors in our analysis.

From a methodological perspective, our model was developed and reported following recommended guidelines for prediction model research (22,23). The use of DCA to quantify net benefit moves beyond traditional accuracy metrics (e.g., AUC) by incorporating clinical consequences, providing a direct assessment of the model’s potential to improve patient outcomes (20,21). Unlike postoperative histology-biomarker scores or equipment-dependent elastography (24,25), our nomogram uses only three routine preoperative biochemical markers, offering a simpler and more accessible tool.

Clinical application and implementation

The nomogram provides a rapid, objective estimate of high-risk probability. To translate this into practice, we propose the following interpretive framework:

❖ Low-risk (probability <17.2%): this stratum supports proceeding with minimally invasive parathyroidectomy with high confidence. The NPV of 91.9% at this threshold provides strong reassurance that the risk of an underlying high-risk lesion is acceptably low.

Acknowledging the modest PPV (42.1%) of the model, we emphasize that in the intermediate-risk zone (17.2–40%), the nomogram serves as a trigger for heightened vigilance and intraoperative preparedness, not as a mandate for extended resection. Conversely, the high NPV (91.9%) is the model’s primary strength, providing strong reassurance for safely identifying low-risk patients. We also recognize that false-positive predictions may cause undue anxiety for patients. This can be mitigated by proper patient counseling before using the nomogram, explaining that a ‘high-risk’ prediction indicates the need for preparedness, not a confirmed diagnosis. Integrating the nomogram result with clinical judgment rather than using it as a standalone decision-maker is essential to minimize unnecessary anxiety.

In implementation, the preoperative probability should guide counseling and planning, and actively inform intraoperative judgment. Local audits of outcomes are essential for continuous refinement of this risk-adapted approach.

Strengths

This study provides a practical, internally validated nomogram using three routinely available preoperative variables to predict high-risk parathyroid lesions. The model demonstrates good discrimination (AUC =0.805) and excellent calibration, with DCA confirming net clinical benefit across a wide threshold range (5–40%). By targeting the clinically relevant composite outcome of APT/PC rather than carcinoma alone, the tool directly informs the key surgical decision: extent of initial resection.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective, single-center design and high prevalent disease rate (22.9%) reflect tertiary referral bias; thus, the absolute risk estimates are not directly transferable to lower-prevalence populations without recalibration. Additionally, because the nomogram uses only biochemical data, it may underestimate patients with concerning ultrasound features but only mild biochemical abnormalities. Therefore, the tool is intended to complement, not replace, imaging and intraoperative findings, particularly in the intermediate-risk zone. Clinicians should recognize this limitation. Histologic distinction between APT and benign adenomas remains subject to interobserver variability. The nomogram may overestimate risk in rare benign cases with extreme biochemistry (e.g., Ca >4.0 mmol/L or PTH >1500 pg/mL) and underestimate risk in older patients (>50 years) with focal carcinoma and only mildly elevated calcium (<3.2 mmol/L). Novel biomarkers and standardized imaging parameters were not incorporated.

Future directions

External validation in independent cohorts is required before clinical implementation. Prospective studies should evaluate whether the nomogram improves surgical decision-making and patient outcomes. Integration of molecular markers (e.g., CDC73 mutation status) and consistent imaging features may further enhance performance. Future cost-effectiveness analyses are also warranted to evaluate the economic impact of implementing this nomogram in routine clinical practice.

Conclusions

We have developed and internally validated a simple preoperative nomogram that effectively predicts high-risk parathyroid lesions using three routinely available clinical variables. This tool addresses a critical gap in current surgical practice by providing objective, individualized risk assessment before surgery, potentially reducing both undertreatment of malignancies and overtreatment of benign disease. The nomogram’s demonstrated net clinical benefit across the 5–40% threshold probability range, combined with its ease of use, makes it a practical tool for guiding surgical strategy in PHPT. While external validation is needed, this nomogram represents a significant step toward more evidence-based, personalized surgical management that balances oncologic efficacy with surgical morbidity.

Constraints on the extreme mass-ratio inspiral population from LISA data
S Singh 1, C E A Chapman-Bird 3, C P L Berry 1 and J Veitch 1

Abstract
Gravitational waves from EMRI ( EMRI ), the inspirals of stellar-mass compact objects into massive black holes, are predicted to be observed by the LISA ( LISA ). A sufficiently large number of EMRI observations will provide unique insights into the massive black hole population. We have developed a hierarchical Bayesian inference framework capable of constraining the parameters of the EMRI population, accounting for selection biases. We leverage the capacity of a feed-forward neural network as an emulator, enabling detectability calculations of ∼ 10 5 \sim 10^{5} EMRI in a fraction of a second, speeding up the likelihood evaluation by ≳ 6 \gtrsim 6 orders of magnitude. We validate our framework on a phenomenological EMRI population model. This framework enables studies of how well we can constrain EMRI population parameters such as the slope of both the massive and stellar-mass black hole mass spectra and the branching fractions of different formation channels, allowing further investigation into the evolution of massive black holes.

Since only sources above a detection threshold will be observed, we must correct for undetected events in the population inference.
We consider using an

This function quantifies the probability of detecting an event from a given population model having the parameters

\alpha(\boldsymbol{\Lambda})=\int\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{\theta}\,{P}_{\mathrm{det}}(\boldsymbol{\theta})p_{\mathrm{pop}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}|\boldsymbol{\Lambda})

[

To overcome this, we introduce machine-learning approaches to speed up the calculation of selection effects.

We use Monte Carlo integration to calculate

The

We first train an

We adjust the learning rate, batch size, and the number of training iterations based on validation loss trends and ensure stable convergence.
The network training is carried out using the AdamW optimiser

The training data undergoes min–max rescaling to normalise

To reflect observational conditions, we simulate

By emulating both the

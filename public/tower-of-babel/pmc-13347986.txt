Multicentre validation and comparison of 23 noninvasive diagnostic models for liver fibrosis in treatment-naïve Chinese patients with autoimmune hepatitis
Authors: Shanshan Chu, Yingjun Chen, Wei Zheng, Qiaoqiao Yin, Chengan Xu, Chandi Xu, Li Yu, Keyang Xu, Hongxia Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLimitations of liver biopsy have driven the development of noninvasive liver fibrosis assessment models. Most noninvasive liver fibrosis models have been developed in hepatitis B or C cohorts but not in autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) cohorts. This study aimed to evaluate and compare the predictive performance of 23 existing noninvasive models for fibrosis staging in untreated Chinese patients with AIH.MethodsA multicentre retrospective study was conducted on 258 untreated patients with autoimmune hepatitis who underwent liver biopsy. Histological fibrosis stage was evaluated using the Ishak scoring system. The diagnostic accuracy of the 23 noninvasive models was assessed by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), including the aspartate aminotransferase (AST) to alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ratio (AAR), age-platelet index (APind), aspartate aminotransferase (AST) to platelet ratio index (APRI), cirrhosis discriminant score (CDS), Doha score, fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4), fibrosis quotient (FibroQ), globulin/platelet model (GP), gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase to platelet ratio (GPR), Göteburg University cirrhosis index (GUCI), Hb-F, King’s score, Lok index, Pohl score, S index, fibrosis-5 index (FIB-5), red cell distribution width-to-platelet ratio (RPR), Forns index, Fibro-alpha, globulin-albumin ratio (GAR), and age platelet count index.ResultsThe median age of the 258 patients was 53 (45–58) years, in which 236 (91.47%) patients were females. Significant fibrosis and cirrhosis were present in 79.84% and 9.30% of patients, respectively. For predicting significant fibrosis, the AUROCs of APind, GP, FIB-4, HB-F, the Lok index, the RPR, the Fibro-alpha index, and the age platelet count index exceeded 0.700. All the models had AUROCs < 0.700 for advanced fibrosis, whereas the Doha score, FI, GP, RPR, and Fibro-alpha achieved AUROCs > 0.700 for diagnosing cirrhosis. In terms of the grading system, the GP model outperformed other noninvasive models for assessing liver fibrosis in this cohort.ConclusionsIn Chinese treatment-naïve AIH patients, the use of the GP model has moderate diagnostic value for liver fibrosis and cirrhosis. Most existing noninvasive models demonstrate suboptimal accuracy, especially for advanced fibrosis. Further development and validation of noninvasive AIH-specific models are warranted.Keywords: Liver fibrosis, Noninvasive models, Diagnosis, Autoimmune hepatitis

Introduction

Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) is a progressive liver inflammatory disease with unclear aetiology and is based on immune abnormalities. The clinical characteristics are elevated serum transaminases, high immunoglobulin G levels and positive serum autoantibodies. There are no specific clinical symptoms in the early stage of AIH. Moreover, owing to occult onset and the limitations of diagnostic conditions, approximately 1/3 of patients develop cirrhosis at diagnosis [1]. Patients with AIH have a good prognosis after undergoing biochemical remission, and their survival time is close to that of the normal population. However, the risk factors for poor prognosis include cirrhosis at diagnosis and failure to achieve biochemical remission after treatment. The mortality of patients with severe untreated continuous AIH activity is very high, and the mortality rate is more than 50% within five years after diagnosis [2].

Liver biopsy is considered the gold standard for evaluating the stage of liver fibrosis. However, the operation has several limitations, including being highly invasive, risk of bleeding, sampling error and interpretation differences between and within observers. Therefore, to reduce the need for liver biopsy, noninvasive liver fibrosis diagnostic models, such as those for aspartate aminotransferase (AST) to alanine aminotransferase (ALT) ratio (AAR) [3], the AST to platelet ratio index (APRI) [4], the fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4) [5], King’s score [6], the S index [7] and the Göteburg University Cirrhosis Index (GUCI) [8], have been developed. Most of the above models were established in patients with hepatitis C virus (HCV). However, few diagnostic models for noninvasive liver fibrosis in untreated AIH patients exist. Nevertheless, many studies have verified the performance of noninvasive models in different populations, but these studies have different diagnostic accuracies. Only a few studies have compared different noninvasive models. Compared with hepatitis B virus (HBV) and HCV cohorts [9], AIH cohorts have fewer data.

Given these limitations, investigating the predictive performance of noninvasive fibrosis models in patients with autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) is highly clinically important. Therefore, this multicentre study aimed to validate and compare the diagnostic accuracy of existing serology‑based noninvasive models for liver fibrosis and cirrhosis in untreated Chinese patients with AIH.

Methods

Patients

This multicentre retrospective study reviewed the medical records of 430 patients diagnosed with AIH at three hospitals in mainland China between January 2015 and June 2022. The diagnosis was based on the 2008 simplified AIH scoring system of the International Autoimmune Hepatitis Group (IAIHG) [10]. All serum samples were collected within 24 h before liver biopsy after written informed consent was obtained from each patient. This strict time interval was applied to minimize potential variations in serum biochemical markers caused by disease progression or acute fluctuations.

Inclusion criteria

Treatment-naïve adult patients with AIH who underwent liver biopsy.

Exclusion criteria

Coinfection with hepatitis A/B/C/D/E virus or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV); other chronic liver diseases (hereditary, drug-induced, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, alcoholic liver disease, or overlap syndromes, including AIH-PBC). Any medication use within 6 months before enrolment, including immunosuppressants (corticosteroids, azathioprine), hepatoprotective agents, and antiviral drugs. Incomplete clinical or laboratory data.

A total of 258 patients were ultimately included.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Tiantai People’s Hospital of Zhejiang Province (Ethics Approval No: 202602111736000458615) and was conducted in accordance with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration. All patients provided written informed consent for liver biopsy. Informed consent for this retrospective study was waived by the ethics committee because only anonymized data were analysed.

Noninvasive fibrosis models

A total of 23 nonpatented noninvasive liver fibrosis models were constructed using serum markers and clinical parameters measured within 72 h before liver biopsy (Fig. 1). In this study, 23 noninvasive models are included: Globulin-albumin ratio (GAR) [11], FibroIndex [12], Fibro-alpha [13], AST to ALT ratio (AAR) [3], Red cell distribution width to platelet ratio (RPR) [14], Forns index [15], Fibrosis-5 index (FIB-5) [16], S index [7], Pohl score [17], noninvasive Koeln-Essen-index (NIKEI) [18], Lok index [19], King’s score [6], HB-F [20], Göteburg University Cirrhosis Index (GUCI) [8], Gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase to platelet ratio (GPR) [21], Globulin/platelet model (GP) [22], Fibrosis quotient (FibroQ) [23], Fibrosis-4 index (FIB-4), Fibrosis index (FI) [24], Fibrosis-cirrhosis index (FCI) [25], Doha score [26], Cirrhosis discriminant score (CDS) [27], AST to platelet ratio index (APRI) [4], and Age-platelet index (APind) [28].

Flow diagram depicting the participant selection process

All patients underwent ultrasound-guided percutaneous liver biopsy. The specimens were fixed in formalin, embedded in paraffin, and stained with haematoxylin and eosin. Only specimens ≥ 1.5 cm in length with ≥ 6 portal tracts were evaluated. Liver fibrosis was staged according to the Ishak scoring system (ranging from 0 to 6). To facilitate clinical interpretation, Ishak stages were further categorized into a Scheuer-compatible S0-S4 classification: Ishak 0 = S0 (no fibrosis); Ishak 1–2 = S1 (portal fibrosis); Ishak 3–4 = S2-S3 (bridging fibrosis); and Ishak 5–6 = S4 (cirrhosis). Significant fibrosis (SF) was defined as Ishak ≥ 2, advanced fibrosis (AF) as Ishak ≥ 3, and cirrhosis as Ishak 4–6.

Liver histology

All patients underwent ultrasound-guided percutaneous liver biopsy. The specimens were fixed in formalin, embedded in paraffin, and stained with haematoxylin and eosin. Only specimens ≥ 1.5 cm in length with ≥ 6 portal tracts were evaluated. Fibrosis was staged using the Ishak scoring system (0–6). In this scoring system, significant fibrosis (SF) is assigned for Ishak ≥ 2, advanced fibrosis (AF) for Ishak ≥ 3, and cirrhosis for Ishak = 5–6 [29].

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0. Continuous variables are presented as medians (interquartile ranges) and were compared using the Mann‑Whitney U test. Categorical variables are presented as n (%) and were compared using the chi‑square test. Diagnostic performance was assessed using the AUROC with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). DeLong’s test was used to compare the AUROCs between the models. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. No formal sample size calculation was performed because of the exploratory nature of this study.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among 258 AIH patients, 22 (8.53%) were male and 236 (91.47%) were female. The median patient age was 53 (45–58) years. In the Results section, all fibrosis stages were based on the Ishak scoring system, and the S0–S4 nomenclature was used only as a clinically adapted grouping consistent with the Scheuer classification. The Ishak fibrosis stages were 0 (n = 3), 1 (n = 49), 2 (n = 140), 3 (n = 42), 4 (n = 0), 5 (n = 18), and 6 (n = 6). Significant fibrosis, advanced fibrosis, and cirrhosis were present in 79.84%, 25.58%, and 9.30% of patients, respectively. Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Comparison of the diagnostic value of various models

The AUROC of each model for identifying SF, AF and cirrhosis was calculated, and the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) are summarized in Table 2. Among 258 patients, 258 had a diagnosis of SF. The 8 models with the highest AUROCs are the GP (AUROC = 0.793), APind (AUROC = 0.748), RPR (AUROC = 0.745), FIB-4 (AUROC = 0.728), Lok index (AUROC = 0.723), HB-F (AUROC = 0.716), Age platelet count index (AUROC = 0.716), and Fibro-alpha (AUROC = 0.715) models. The S index (AUROC = 0.586), GPR (AUROC = 0.585), FI (AUROC = 0.565), FCI (AUROC = 0.564), AAR (AUROC = 0.531) and FIB-5 (AUROC = 0.348) performed poorly. With respect to the identification of AF in all patients, except for the HB-F (AUROC = 0.716), Lok index (AUROC = 0.723), and Fibro-alpha (AUROC = 0.715) models, the other models did not perform well. Fibro-alpha (AUROC = 0.727), GP (AUROC = 0.711), and Doha score (AUROC = 0.710) were the top three of the five models that performed well in diagnosing cirrhosis. In general, the AUROC for the diagnosis of SF was generally greater than that for cirrhosis according to each noninvasive model. Pairwise comparisons of AUROCs were performed using DeLong’s test. No significant differences were observed between the main models for predicting significant fibrosis or cirrhosis (all P > 0.05).

Evaluation and comparison of noninvasive models in treatment-naïve patients with different levels of ALT

We further validated the diagnostic performance of 23 noninvasive models for AIH patients with different ALT levels. The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends upper limit of normal (ULN) ALT levels of 30 U/L for men and 19 U/L for women [30]. When the ALT level was < 2×ULN, the AUROC values of AF, SF and cirrhosis ranged from 0.039 to 0.368, 0.013–0.381 and 0.072–0.432, respectively. When the ALT concentration was ≥ 2×ULN, the differences in the AUROC values for AF, SF and cirrhosis were 0.05–0.546, 0.016–0.204 and 0.013–0.272, respectively (Table 3).

Comprehensive evaluation of noninvasive models

In different stages of liver fibrosis, the performance of noninvasive models may differ, and it is difficult to distinguish one or several models that perform well. To further compare the accuracy of the 23 noninvasive models, the AUROC obtained from the data of this study was used as a classification system developed by Dong et al. [31] (Table 4). To predict SF, AF and liver cirrhosis, the model score with an AUROC lower than 0.700 was 0, the model score with an AUROC between 0.700 and 0.750 was 1, the model score with an AUROC between 0.750 and 0.800 was 2, and the model score with an AUROC higher than 0.800 was 3. The final score of the model is the sum of the three scores. A model with a score of 0–3 was designated as level C, indicating low diagnostic efficiency; a model with a score of 4–6 was designated as level B, with medium diagnostic efficiency; and a model with a score of 7–9 was designated as level A, with high diagnostic efficiency. The diagnostic accuracy of the noninvasive model was comprehensively evaluated, and the results are shown in Table 4. On the basis of the grading system, 0 noninvasive models with high diagnostic efficiency were rated as grade A, 1 model (GP model) with medium diagnostic efficiency was rated as grade B, and the other 22 models with low diagnostic efficiency were rated as grade C (Table 5).

Discussion

This multicentre study represents the first large-scale validation of 23 noninvasive fibrosis models in treatment-naïve Chinese autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) patients and constitutes the core innovation of this work. We systematically compared the diagnostic performance of all the included models across different fibrosis stages and confirmed that the globulin/platelet model (GP) exhibited moderate but optimal diagnostic efficiency for significant fibrosis and cirrhosis, outperforming widely used conventional models, including FIB-4, APRI, and APind. These findings provide robust clinical evidence for noninvasive fibrosis risk stratification in AIH patients: the optimal cut-off value of the GP model can be utilized as a preliminary screening indicator to identify high-risk individuals, thereby reducing unnecessary invasive liver biopsies and facilitating individualized clinical decision-making.

Currently, clinical practice predominantly relies on general fibrosis models (e.g., FIB-4 and APRI) developed and validated in chronic viral hepatitis cohorts [5, 32]. Our findings are consistent with those of previous meta-analyses and related studies, demonstrating that these conventional models yield suboptimal diagnostic accuracy in AIH patients, especially for advanced fibrosis [33]. This consistency further verifies that general fibrosis models cannot fully adapt to the unique immune-mediated pathogenesis of AIH, leading to limited predictive efficacy in this specific population. In contrast to previous single-model or small-sample AIH studies, our head-to-head comparison of 23 models represents an important breakthrough; additionally, our ALT-stratified analysis revealed obvious heterogeneity in model performance under different liver inflammation states, a novel perspective rarely reported in prior research.

The superior diagnostic performance of the GP model can be attributed to two key mechanisms. First, this model combines globulin and platelet levels, two indicators closely linked to autoimmune inflammatory injury and progressive hepatic fibrosis in AIH, rather than solely reflecting transaminase activity or basic liver function [33, 34]. This parameter composition aligns better with the pathological progression characteristics of AIH [35]. Second, the GP model is less susceptible to transient acute liver inflammation, showing better diagnostic stability in patients with both normal and elevated ALT levels, whereas conventional models are easily disturbed by fluctuating liver enzymes, resulting in biased predictive results [36]. Although the GP model showed numerically higher AUROC values, DeLong’s test revealed no significant differences between the top-performing models (all P > 0.05), suggesting similar overall diagnostic performance in this cohort.

Notably, all the included models showed unsatisfactory accuracy for diagnosing advanced fibrosis in this cohort, highlighting a critical unmet clinical need. For clinical application, the GP model, RPR, and Fibro-alpha are recommended for patients with normal or mildly elevated ALT levels (< 2×ULN) because of stable efficacy; for patients with markedly elevated ALT levels, the combined use of GP with FIB-4 or APind can improve overall diagnostic reliability. Future research should prioritize the development of noninvasive AIH-tailored models that specifically target advanced fibrosis, and integrating the GP ratio with AIH-specific immune or inflammatory biomarkers represents a promising direction to further optimize diagnostic performance.

Few studies have compared these simple noninvasive fibrosis models positively. Wang et al. (n = 119) compared the relationships between three models (RPR, APRI and FIB-4) and liver fibrosis and reported that the RPR was a simple predictor of liver fibrosis and was superior to the APRI and FIB-4 in identifying liver fibrosis in patients with autoimmune hepatitis [37]. A study by Liu et al. evaluated 45 patients with autoimmune hepatitis and 47 healthy controls [38]. They reported that RDW-SD, RDW-CV/PLT, RDW-SD/PLT, FIB-4 and AAR performed well in predicting advanced liver fibrosis in AIH patients and that RDW-SD/PLT was an independent risk factor for predicting advanced fibrosis in AIH patients.

In addition to comparisons between noninvasive serology models, comparisons between ultrasound and magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) have been performed. In a systematic review in 2019, Wu et al. [39] evaluated a total of 16 studies and 861 patients and compared APRI, FIB-4, AAR, transient elastography (TE), acoustic radiation force impulse, shear wave elastography and MRE with liver fibrosis staging. Notably, TE performed well in the staging of liver fibrosis in patients with autoimmune hepatitis, and the diagnostic accuracy of the APRI and FIB-4 was poor; however, it also has several shortcomings that hinder its wide application. On the one hand, TE cannot distinguish the intermediate stage of liver fibrosis [40, 41], whereas on the other hand, it needs regular maintenance, and primary hospitals may not be able to bear the additional costs.

This study has several limitations. First, the overlapping definitions of fibrosis stages may affect grading consistency [9]; second, the AUROC grading threshold requires verification in larger cohorts; third, multicentre heterogeneity is unavoidable, and conclusions are currently limited to Asian populations. Despite these drawbacks, our findings provide a reliable reference for noninvasive fibrosis assessment in treatment-naïve AIH patients.

Conclusions

In treatment-naïve Chinese AIH patients, the GP model provides moderate diagnostic accuracy for significant fibrosis and cirrhosis. Most existing noninvasive models have suboptimal performance, particularly for advanced fibrosis. The GP model may help reduce unnecessary liver biopsies in clinical practice, but histologic confirmation remains necessary for indeterminate cases. Further development of noninvasive AIH-tailored tools is urgently needed.

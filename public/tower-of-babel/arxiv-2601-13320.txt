RetinexGuI: Retinex-Guided Iterative Illumination Estimation Method for Low Light Images
Yasin Demir, Nur Hüseyin Kaplan, Sefa Kucuk, Nagihan Severoglu

Abstract
In recent years, there has been a growing interest in low-light image enhancement (LLIE) due to its importance for critical downstream tasks. Current Retinex-based methods and learning-based approaches have shown significant LLIE performance. However, computational complexity and dependencies on large training datasets often limit their applicability in real-time applications. We introduce RetinexGuI, a novel and effective Retinex-guided LLIE framework to overcome these limitations. The proposed method first separates the input image into illumination and reflection layers, and iteratively refines the illumination while keeping the reflectance component unchanged. With its simplified formulation and computational complexity of 𝒪 ⁡ ( N ) \displaystyle\mathcal{O}(N) , our RetinexGuI demonstrates impressive enhancement performance across three public datasets, indicating strong potential for large-scale applications. Furthermore, it opens promising directions for theoretical analysis and integration with deep learning approaches. The source code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/etuspars/RetinexGuI once the paper is accepted.

II
Proposed RetinexGuI Method

Based on Retinex theory
[
13
]
, an observed image
\displaystyle\mathbf{I}
is expressed in terms of its illumination and reflectance components as

\mathbf{I}=\mathbf{L}\odot\mathbf{R}

(1)

here,
\displaystyle\mathbf{L}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{R}
represent the illumination and reflectance components, respectively, and
\displaystyle\odot
indicates element-wise multiplication. For convenience, we reformulate the Retinex model in the logarithmic domain as

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{l}=\mathbf{L}_{l}+\mathbf{R}_{l}

(2)

where
\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{l}=\log(\mathbf{I})
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{l}=\log(\mathbf{L})
, and
\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{l}=\log(\mathbf{R})
. We iteratively enhance the input image by updating the illumination component while keeping the reflectance component unchanged. Specifically, the enhanced image in the logarithmic domain is obtained as

\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(N+1)}=\mathbf{L}_{l}^{(N)}+\mathbf{R}_{l},

(3)

where
\displaystyle N
denotes the iteration number. The initial values are set as
\displaystyle\mathbf{I}^{(0)}=\mathbf{I}
and
\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{l}=\mathbf{I}^{(0)}
.
The illumination component is then iteratively estimated by dividing the observed image by the initial reflectance component as

\mathbf{L}_{l}^{(N)}=\frac{\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(N)}}{\mathbf{R}_{l}}.

(4)

Given that
\displaystyle\mathbf{R}_{l}=\mathbf{I}^{(0)}
, we can rewrite (
4
) as

\mathbf{L}_{l}^{(N)}=\dfrac{\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(N)}}{\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}}.

(5)

By substituting (
5
) into (
3
), the update equation can be equivalently expressed as

\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(N+1)}=\dfrac{\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(N)}}{\mathbf{I}^{(0)}_{l}}+\mathbf{I}^{(0)}_{l}.

(6)

The recursive update given in (
6
) is equal to the following:

\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(N+1)}=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{N}\left(\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}\right)^{n}}{\left(\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}\right)^{(N)}}+\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}\left(\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}\right)^{-n}+\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}.

(7)

When the number of iterations becomes sufficiently large (
\displaystyle N\to\infty
), the formulation converges as follows:

\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(\infty)}=\frac{1}{1-\left(\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}\right)^{-1}}+\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}=\frac{\left(\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}\right)^{2}}{\mathbf{I}_{l}^{(0)}-1}.

(8)

It should be noted that all operations are performed on an element-wise basis. The closed-form result demonstrates that iterative illumination refinement converges to a solution defined by the initial illumination estimate. As the number of iterations increases, the enhancement process becomes independent of intermediate updates and is managed by a simple analytical formulation. This property explains the stable behavior of the proposed RetinexGuI and its ability to achieve consistent enhancement results with only a few iterations.

The result of (
8
) is considered as the initial enhancement level. To obtain further levels of enhancement, especially for extremely dark scenes, (
8
) is applied in a cascaded manner.

III-A

Implementation Details

We first transform the input RGB-color low-light image into the Hue-Saturation-Value (HSV) space. Since the V channel represents illumination and reflectance information, we perform iterative illumination estimation on the V component. To handle under-exposed or extra-dark images, we repeatedly apply the converged illumination expression in (
8
).
We categorize the input images into three groups based on the mean value
\displaystyle(\mu_{V})
of the V component.
The number of levels
\displaystyle(K)
is determined as

\displaystyle K=\begin{cases}1,&\mu_{V}>0.16,\\
2,&0.08\leq\mu_{V}\leq 0.16,\\
3,&\mu_{V}<0.08.\end{cases}

(9)

The converged illumination update in (
8
) is then applied iteratively as

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{l}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\mathbf{V}_{l}^{k}\right)^{2}}{\mathbf{V}_{l}^{k}-1},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,K.

(10)

Moreover, to provide more natural and balanced color reproduction, we apply a gamma correction to the S component using an empirical gamma value of 0.7 as

\mathbf{S}^{\prime}=\mathbf{S}\odot\mathbf{S}^{\gamma},

(11)

and the enhanced HSV image is finally converted back to the RGB color space.

IV
Conclusion

In this letter, we present a simple yet effective Retinex-guided framework for low-light image enhancement. The proposed RetinexGuI improves low-light images without relying on external prior information or complex parameter tuning. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves competitive performance in terms of visual quality and quantitative evaluation metrics. Thanks to its simplified formulation, the proposed approach has negligible computational cost and is highly suitable for real-time low-light image enhancement.

This study demonstrates a significant advancement in low-light image enhancement by combining computational efficiency with strong enhancement performance. It provides a promising foundation for further theoretical investigation and integration with learning-based models. Future research should address robustness under extreme illumination conditions and extend the method to more challenging real-world low-light imaging scenarios.

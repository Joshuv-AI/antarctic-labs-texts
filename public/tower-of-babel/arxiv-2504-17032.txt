Omega Results for The Divisor and Circle Problems Using The Resonance Method
Kamalakshya Mahatab

Abstract
. We apply the resonance method to obtain large values of general exponential sums with positive coefficients. As applications, we show improved Ω \Omega -bounds for Dirichlet and Piltz divisor problems, Gauss circle Problem, and error term for the mean square of the Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet L L -function.

Let

However, the best known upper bound for

We investigate the above problem from a different perspective. We may ask, how large
\Delta(x)
could be when

\Omega~\text{bound}
for

is due to Soundararajan
[

]

Theorem 1
.

We have

\max_{X/2<x\leq 5X^{3/2}(\log X)^{2}}\left|\frac{\Delta(x^{2})}{\sqrt{x}}\right|\gg(\log X)^{1/4}(\log_{2}X)^{(3/4)(2^{4/3}-1)}(\log_{3}X)^{-3/8}.

In particular,

\Delta(x)=\Omega\left((x\log x)^{1/4}(\log_{2}x)^{3/4(2^{4/3}-1)}(\log_{3}x)^{-3/8}\right).

We may explore the possibility of removing the factor

By the Voronoï summation formula

To improve workability, we may truncate the sum mentioned above while ensuring a manageable error margin

\Delta(x^{2})=\frac{x^{1/2}}{\pi\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n\leq X^{3}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\cos\left(4\pi\sqrt{n}x-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+O(X^{\epsilon}),

2.
Large Values of Exponential Sums

In this section we will work with general exponential sums having positive coefficients.
Let

F_{\beta}(x)=\sum_{n\leq X^{A_{1}}}a_{n}\cos(x\lambda_{n}+\beta),

where
\lambda_{n}>0
. The same method also works when all
\lambda_{n}<0
. We will also assume
a_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 1
.
We will need the following convolution formula for
F_{\beta}(x)
, which is inspired from
[
12
]
.

2.1.
Convolution Formula

Lemma 1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F_{\beta}(x+u)\left(\frac{\sin\alpha u}{u}\right)^{2}e^{-i2\alpha u}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{i\beta}\sum_{\lambda_{n}}a_{n}w_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n})e^{i\lambda_{n}x},

where

w_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n})=\frac{\pi}{2}\max\{0,\,2\alpha-|\lambda_{n}-2\alpha|\}.

Proof.

Let

F(x)=\sum_{n\leq X^{A_{1}}}a_{n}\exp(ix\lambda_{n}).

Then

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F_{\beta}(x+u)\left(\frac{\sin\alpha u}{u}\right)^{2}e^{-i2\alpha u}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(F(x+u)e^{i\beta}+F(-x-u)e^{-i\beta}\right)\left(\frac{\sin\alpha u}{u}\right)^{2}e^{-i2\alpha u}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{i\beta}\sum_{\lambda_{n}}a_{n}w_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n})e^{i\lambda_{n}x}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-i\beta}\sum_{\lambda_{n}}a_{n}w_{\alpha}(-\lambda_{n})e^{-i\lambda_{n}x}.

As
w_{\alpha}(-\lambda_{n})=0
for all
n\geq 1
,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F_{\beta}(x+u)\left(\frac{\sin\alpha u}{u}\right)^{2}e^{-i2\alpha u}\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{2}e^{i\beta}\sum_{\lambda_{n}}a_{n}w_{\alpha}(\lambda_{n})e^{i\lambda_{n}x}.

∎

We may note that the above convolution formula makes the phase
\beta
redundant in computation of large values. Further, it also gives the flexibility to control the length of the exponential sum by choosing
\alpha
such that the sum runs upto
\lambda_{n}\leq 2\alpha
.

2.2.
Construction of Resonator

The convolution of
F_{\beta}
in Lemma
1
concentrates the values of
F_{\beta}
in a small neighbourhood of
x
. Next, we need to choose suitable
\lambda_{n}
s to obtain large values in a neighbourhood of
x
. We will do this using the resonance method.

Let
0<A_{4}<A_{3}<A_{2}<A_{1}
. Further, let
Y_{1}=X^{A_{3}}
and
Y_{2}=X^{A_{2}}
.
To find extreme values of
F_{\beta}(x)
for
Y_{1}/2\leq x\leq 2Y_{2}(\log Y_{2})^{2}
,
we define

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-X^{A_{4}}}^{X^{A_{4}}}F_{0}(x+u)\left(\frac{\sin\alpha u}{u}\right)^{2}e^{-i2\alpha u}|R(x)|^{2}\Phi(x/Y_{2})\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}x,\text{ and }

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|R(x)|^{2}\Phi(x/Y_{2})\mathrm{d}x,

where
\Phi(x):=e^{-x^{2}/2}
. In the resonance method, we use the idea that

\frac{|I_{1}|}{|I_{2}|}\leq\max_{Y_{1}/2\leq x\leq 2Y_{2}\log^{2}Y_{2}}|F_{0}(x)|+\text{Error}.

The transition from
0
to
\beta
can be done using Lemma
1
and Lemma
5
. Infact, the large values we will obtain, will be independent of
\beta
.

To construct the resonator

, we will collect the frequencies
e^{i\lambda_{n}x}
. Let

\mathcal{M}\subseteq\{\lambda_{n}:C_{1}\alpha\leq\lambda_{n}\leq 2\alpha\},

for some
0<C_{1}<2
, such that
\mathcal{M}
is a linearly independent set over the rationals
\mathbb{Q}
and let
|\mathcal{M}|=M
.
We define a resonator
R(x)
on
\mathcal{M}
as

R(x)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{N}[\mathcal{M}]}r(u)\exp(iux),

where
2
2

2

It is useful to observe that we could define
0<r(\lambda_{n})<1
arbitrarily for
\lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
, and then extend to the rest of
\mathbb{N}[\mathcal{M}]
by just demanding that
r(u+v)=r(u)r(v)
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{N}[\mathcal{M}]
. This property of our resonator may help to extend it to combinatorial and algebraic setting.

r(u):=e^{-u/2\alpha}
. Above

stands for the set of non-negative integral linear combinations of elements in
\mathcal{M}
.

Due to Linear independence of

and as

for all

as an Euler product

(1)

R(x)=\prod_{\lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{M}}\left(1-r(\lambda_{n})\exp(i\lambda_{n}x)\right)^{-1}.

Now we are ready to state our main theorem.

Theorem 6
.

We use the notations as defined before. Then

\displaystyle\max_{X^{A_{3}}/2<x\leq 2A_{2}^{2}X^{A_{2}}(\log X)^{2}}|F_{\beta}(x)|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi}{4e}\sum_{\lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{M}}a_{n}+O\left(X^{A_{3}-A_{2}}e^{2M/C_{1}}\left(\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq 4\alpha}a_{n}\right)+\frac{X^{-A_{4}}}{\alpha}\left(\sum_{n\leq X^{A_{1}}}a_{n}\right)\right).

Remark 1
.

In the above theorem, the constant
\frac{\pi}{4e}
is not optimal, and its optimization is left for future research.
For example, it can be improved to
\pi/(4e^{C_{2}/2})
by choosing
\mathcal{M}\subseteq[C_{1}\alpha,C_{2}\alpha]
.

4.1.
Divisor Problem

(Proof of Theorem
1
)

By Voronoi formula
[
18
]
, for large enough
X
such that
\sqrt{X}\leq x\leq X^{3/2}
, we have

(4)

\frac{\pi\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{x}}\Delta(x^{2})=\sum_{n\leq X^{3}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\cos\left(4\pi\sqrt{n}x-\pi/4\right)+O(X^{\epsilon-\frac{1}{2}}),

for any
\epsilon>0
.

Let

\displaystyle F_{\beta}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq X^{3}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\cos\left(4\pi\sqrt{n}x-\pi/4\right)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{n\leq X^{A_{1}}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\cos\left(\lambda_{n}x+\beta\right),

where
\lambda_{n}=4\pi\sqrt{n},\,a_{n}=d(n)/n^{3/4},\beta=-\pi/4,A_{1}=3
.

Further, let

A_{2}=3/2,A_{3}=1\text{ and }A_{4}=7/8.

We construct the resonating set
\mathcal{M}
following the construction of Soundarajan
[
16
]
,

\mathcal{M}:=\left\{\lambda_{n}:n\in[C_{1}\alpha,2\alpha],\omega(n)=[\lambda\log_{2}\alpha],n\text{ square free}\right\},

where
\omega(n)
denotes the number of distinct prime factors of
n
.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X^{3}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\ll X^{3/4}\log X.

By Sathe’s
[
14
]
and Stirling’s formula

M\asymp\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\log_{2}\alpha}}(\log\alpha)^{\lambda-1-\lambda\log\lambda}.

Now

\displaystyle\max_{X^{A_{3}}/2<x\leq 2A_{2}^{2}X^{A_{2}}(\log X)^{2}}|F_{\beta}(x)|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi}{4e}\sum_{\lambda_{n}\in\mathcal{M}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}+\text{Error}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi}{2^{11/4}e}\frac{M2^{\lambda\log_{2}\alpha}}{\alpha^{3/4}}+\text{Error}

\displaystyle\gg\frac{\alpha^{1/4}}{(\log_{2}\alpha)^{1/2}}(\log\alpha)^{\lambda\log 2+\lambda-1-\lambda\log\lambda}+\text{Error}.

We choose

\alpha=\frac{1}{C}(\log X)(\log_{2}X)^{1-\lambda+\lambda\log\lambda}(\log_{3}X)^{1/2}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\max_{X/2<x\leq 5X^{3/2}(\log X)^{2}}|F_{\beta}(x)|

\displaystyle\gg(\log X)^{1/4}(\log_{2}X)^{\lambda\log 2+(3/4)(\lambda-1-\lambda\log\lambda)}(\log_{3}X)^{-3/8}+\text{Error}.

On optimizing
\lambda=2^{4/3}
, we get

\max_{X/2<x\leq 5X^{3/2}(\log X)^{2}}|F_{\beta}(x)|\gg(\log X)^{1/4}(\log_{2}X)^{(3/4)(2^{4/3}-1)}(\log_{3}X)^{-3/8}+\text{Error}.

To estimate the error, note

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\log_{2}\alpha}}(\log\alpha)^{\lambda-1-\lambda\log\lambda}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{\log X}{C}.

We choose
C
large enough such that

\displaystyle e^{2M/C}\ll X^{1/32-\epsilon}

for any
\epsilon>0
.

Now we compute the error as follows:

Error

\displaystyle\ll X^{A_{3}-A_{2}}e^{2M/C_{1}}\left(\sum_{\lambda_{n}\leq 4\alpha}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\right)+\frac{X^{-A_{4}}}{\alpha}\left(\sum_{n\leq X^{A_{1}}}\frac{d(n)}{n^{3/4}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll X^{-1/2+1/32-\epsilon}\alpha^{1/4}\log\alpha+\frac{X^{-7/8+3/4}\log X}{\alpha}

\displaystyle\ll X^{-15/16}+X^{-1/8}\ll X^{-1/8}.

This completes proof of Theorem
1
.

For

\mathcal{M}=\left\{\lambda_{n}:n\in[C_{1}\alpha,2\alpha],n\text{ has }[\lambda\log_{2}\alpha]\text{ prime factors }p\equiv 1\mod 4,n\text{ square free}\right\}.

Lau and Tsang

An integration of deep learning models for effective classification of human activity patterns in disabled people using gesture analysis
Authors: Hend Khalid Alkahtani, Nouf Al-Kahtani, Gouse Pasha Mohammed, Radwa Marzouk
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Human activity recognition (HAR) has numerous applications due to its widespread use of procurement tools, such as smartphones and video cameras, and its ability to capture data on human activity. HAR became a hot scientific area in the computer vision (CV) domain. It is complicated in the expansion of many substantial applications, namely video surveillance, home monitoring, security, virtual reality, and human–computer interaction. Subsequently, a wide range of activity recognition methods were developed for individuals with disabilities. HAR is identified as the technique of naming and recognizing actions using artificial intelligence (AI)-based deep learning (DL) methodologies. DL models are crucial to the activity recognition process for individuals with disabilities and older people. This paper presents an Optimised Hybrid Deep Learning Model for Human Activity Recognition Using Metaheuristic Optimisation Algorithms (OHDLM-HARMOA) model. The aim is to develop an effective HAR method that assists and improves the quality of life for people with disabilities through accurate activity monitoring. Initially, the data pre-processing stage applies Z-score normalization for converting the input data into a structured pattern. For the feature selection process, the ant colony optimization (ACO) model is employed to select the most relevant and significant features from a dataset. Furthermore, the OHDLM-HARMOA model utilizes the hybridization of a convolutional neural network and a bidirectional gated recurrent unit with attention (CNN-BiGRU-A) technique for classification. Finally, the parameter tuning process is performed using the Sine–Cosine Algorithm (SCA) technique to enhance the classification performance of the CNN-BiGRU-A model. The experimental evaluation of the OHDLM-HARMOA approach is performed under the WISDM dataset. The comparison analysis of the OHDLM-HARMOA approach demonstrated a superior accuracy value of 99.00% over existing models.

Introduction

Based on a recent poll, globally, 650 million people of working age live with disabilities, says the WHO. There is a crucial necessity for sufficient facilities to provide accommodations for people efficiently1. The HAR tools are becoming increasingly widespread across various domains, including crime behaviour identification, smart home technology, and healthcare2. Those tools are applied with a dual purpose: to improve the quality of life and foster individual independence. Moreover, HAR tools are increasingly used to assist people, especially older individuals with disabilities, in monitoring their physical performance after treatment, to improve their quality of life3. Observing day-to-day human activities is a vital means of defining humans’ functional and health conditions. Consequently, HAR is among the crucial elements in tailored life care and healthcare systems, specifically for older people and those with disability4. The HAR research is established as the preferred area of study. Due to the availability of less energy consumption, sensors, accelerometers, and growth in AI, Internet of Things (IoT), CV, and machine learning (ML), a few applications have been created using human-centred design monitoring for identifying, classifying, and recognizing behaviour of the human5. Researchers have proposed numerous methods for this topic. HAR is a dynamic technique for monitoring an individual’s resilience and is attained through ML approaches6. HAR is an automated recognition method that examines human behaviour based on data from several wearable devices and smartphone sensors7. Older adults and people with disabilities are protected and monitored 24 h a day. Recently, AI has become increasingly popular in HAR owing to its robust classification and intelligent nature. Lately, many studies have been planned for HAR using ML and DL8. ML and DL-driven approaches are essential for the activity recognition process in disabled and older persons. The increasing need for tailored care and assistance for people with disabilities has emphasized the importance of creating state-of-the-art techniques to help in everyday routines9. One effective method is utilizing innovative recognition systems, which monitor and analyze human activity. By employing emerging technologies, such as sensors and smart systems, we can create more effective solutions that enhance self-reliance and quality of life10.

Related works

Al-Qaness et al.11 presented an effective HAR method created by a DL framework named TCN-Inception, intended for multivariate time series tasks, namely HAR data, with the amalgamation of the temporal convolutional network (TCN)and Inception model. The model begins with an Inception model, which utilizes similar layers of convolution using numerous kernel dimensions for extracting features. Following that, it comprises a TCN model with dilated convolutions to hold long temporal designs. Features are fused from diverse channels, and the utilization of batch normalization and residual connections enhances training and extends the framework. Tsung et al.12 developed a pavement damage detection system called High-Performance Pavement Health Recognition (HPPH) to identify typical pavement damages, namely potholes, alligator cracks, transverse cracks, and longitudinal cracks. The introduced HPPH could be used on a vehicle for normal road detection. Kilic et al.13 presented an attention-guided multi-scale graph convolutional network (AGMS-GCN) approach that adaptively defines the relationship weights among joints. A novel adjacency matrix represents action-specific combined relations that are produced by gaining spatial–temporal dependences with the attention mechanism (AM) on the extracted feature mapping, utilizing spatial–temporal GCN (ST-GCN). This feature permits the extraction of information that considers both long- and short-range spatial–temporal relationships. Dey et al.14 proposed a new lightweight, multidomain, multilevel, fused patch-enabled learning paradigm that utilizes distinct colour domain RGB image details of 2D representations, such as spectrograms, range-Doppler, and range-time, for improved HAR leveraging radars. This study proposes an innovative domain-level fusion of 2D representations of radar returns, utilizing colour domain data. In15, a hybrid 1D CNN with a classifier has been employed to enhance HAR efficiency. It presents a model to data-adaptively and spontaneously remove dependable features from unprocessed data. This paradigm presented a bilateral classification for monitoring individual activity. Wei et al.16 recommended ActiveSelfHAR. This model combines the advantages of active learning, which involves obtaining informative data with minimal effort and real labels, with the benefits of self-training, which efficiently utilizes unlabeled data to adapt the HAR model to the targeted field. ActiveSelfHAR comprises numerous essential stages. Initially, use the paradigm from the resource area to label and select domain-invariant data, starting a self-training set. Then, utilize the self-training set’s distribution data to recognize and interpret data within the class limits, thereby creating a core set.

Wu et al.17 proposed a ST Action Representation Recognition Method (STARRM) to identify human activities. This method projects a fresh depth feature map, named Hierarchical Pyramid Depth Motion Images (HP-DMI), to present videos. It implements an ST-GCN extractor for summarising skeleton features, termed ST Joint Descriptors (STJD). Karsh et al.18 proposed the spatial and channel attention-integrated inception network (SCAI-net) technique for hand gesture recognition. The model integrates spatial and channel attention into a modified Inception V3 model, thereby improving feature discrimination. Zia et al.19 presented a real-time gesture-controlled autonomous vehicle system using the You Only Look Once version 5 small (YOLOv5s) methodology, which also integrates a Raspberry Pi and a web-based interface. Xi et al.20 presented the ResNeXt architecture, integrating 3D convolution and a lightweight convolutional attention mechanism. The model improves spatiotemporal feature extraction and achieves high recognition accuracy. Charan, Saravanan, and Surendran21 proposed a model that utilizes long short-term memory (LSTM) and decision tree (DT) models to enhance accuracy and performance in activity recognition. Salam et al.22 developed an upper limb gesture and sign language recognition system using a CNN model for individuals with speech and hearing impairments. Thanarajan et al.23 presented an Improved Wolf Swarm Optimisation with Deep Learning based Movement Analysis and Self-Regulated Human Activity Recognition (IWSODL-MAHAR) technique to accurately recognize various human activities by mitigating data dimensionality and optimizing model performance. Panagiotou et al.24 proposed an integrated model that incorporates MoveNet and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to detect the most effective approach for assisting elderly individuals in their daily activities. Charan et al.25 recognized human use of electronic gadgets for communication by comparing the performance of the Innovative k-nearest neighbour (kNN) and naïve bayes (NB) techniques. Shin et al.26 presented a lightweight DL-based multi-stream hand gesture recognition system using surface Electromyography (sEMG) methodology integrating bidirectional temporal convolutional network (Bi-TCN), 1D-CNN with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) blocks, and a TCN with bidirectional LSTM (TCN-BiLSTM) model for effectually extracting spatial–temporal features and enhancing recognition performance. Rethick et al.27 proposed an online hand gesture recognition and classification system for the deaf and mute community, utilizing a custom-designed CNN model optimized for real-time communication, high accuracy, and inclusivity.

Despite the improvements in HAR and gesture recognition, various limitations still exist. Several existing models, such as TCN-Inception and ST-GCN, are computationally intensive, making real-time deployment challenging. Models, such as AGMS-GCN and IWSODL-MAHAR, rely on spatial–temporal relations but often overlook lightweight design, which can impact mobile or embedded applications. CNN-based models, though accurate, lack adaptability to diverse user inputs or unseen gestures. Techniques like ActiveSelfHAR enhance domain adaptation but require labelled data for initialization. A significant research gap exists in developing efficient, adaptable, and real-time gesture recognition systems that strike a balance between accuracy and low computational cost, particularly for resource-constrained environments and diverse user populations.

The proposed methodology

In this manuscript, the OHDLM-HARMOA method is presented. The primary intention of the paper is to develop an effective HAR model designed to assist and enhance the quality of life for people with disabilities through accurate activity monitoring. It encompasses data pre-processing, feature selection, hybrid classification, and parameter tuning methods. Pre-processing through normalisation

Initially, the data pre-processing stage applies Z-score normalization to transform the input data into a structured pattern28. This model is chosen for its efficiency in standardizing features by centring the data around zero mean and unit variance, making it ideal for models sensitive to scale, such as CNNs, BiLSTM, and TCNs. This method effectually handles outliers and ensures consistent feature scaling even with skewed distributions. The technique enhances convergence during training by stabilizing gradients and mitigating bias from dominant features. Compared to robust scaling or decimal scaling, Z-score normalization maintains the data’s original distribution characteristics while improving learning performance. This makes it an ideal choice for gesture recognition tasks involving time-series and multichannel inputs.

Z-score normalization, also known as standard score normalization, is a statistical model used to scale data by centring it around the mean and scaling it according to the standard deviation. During HAR, sensor or image-derived features may differ extensively in units and ranges that can negatively affect the ML performance methods. This model converts every feature so that it has a standard deviation of 1 and a mean of 0, allowing each feature to contribute equally to the learning process. This aids in increasing the convergence rate of training methods and decreases bias towards features with large scales. By using this model, HAR methods can achieve more precise and consistent recognition across different activities and individuals.

Feature reduction using ACO approach

For the feature selection process, the ACO approach is employed to choose the most relevant and significant features from a dataset29. This approach is selected for its robust search capability and adaptability in efficiently exploring large solution spaces. This model effectively chooses the most relevant original features, preserving interpretability, unlike conventional techniques such as PCA or LDA, which transform features. The model also replicates natural foraging behaviour to detect optimal feature subsets based on performance criteria iteratively. ACO effectively avoids local minima compared to greedy or wrapper-based techniques. It is particularly suitable for high-dimensional gesture data, where selecting the most informative features significantly enhances model accuracy while mitigating computational cost without compromising performance.

The ACO is an artificial intelligence-based optimizer model that offers benefits in resolving discrete optimization models. The foraging behaviour of ants inspires this model and is a typical meta-heuristic model. Meta-heuristic models provide various benefits, which are not reiterated here. After locating food, the ants identify the area and release a pheromone, which attracts the meal. The smell evaporates in time. The scent will attract additional ants to discover the food; when an ant cannot find the route to the meal, a better way might be discovered. By utilizing this model, the minimal route is finally realized. The model can also be further applied to resolve optimization issues.

Hybrid classification model

Furthermore, the presented OHDLM-HARMOA approach employs the hybridization of the CNN-BiGRU-A technique for the classification process30. This model is chosen for its efficiency in extracting spatial features from input signals and effectively employs the merits of each component in handling complex sequential gesture data. The BiGRU model effectively captures long-range bidirectional temporal dependencies with fewer parameters. At the same time, the AM method improves performance by focusing on the most relevant time steps or features during classification. Compared to standalone CNN or RNN models, this hybrid model offers enhanced accuracy, improved generalization, and greater robustness to noise. Its efficiency and effectiveness make it ideal for real-time gesture recognition tasks with variable-length inputs. SCA-based parameter tuning method

Finally, the parameter tuning process is performed using the SCA model to enhance the classification performance of the CNN-BiGRU-A classifier31. This model is chosen for its robust capacity to balance exploration and exploitation in the search space, enabling efficient optimization of model hyperparameters. Unlike grid or random search, SCA dynamically adjusts parameters utilizing mathematical sine and cosine functions, allowing faster convergence to optimal values. It outperforms conventional metaheuristics, such as GA or PSO, in terms of simplicity, fewer control parameters, and adaptability. SCA is particularly effective in avoiding local minima, making it appropriate for tuning complex models in gesture recognition tasks. Its lightweight nature also ensures reduced computational overhead.

The Sine Cosine Algorithm (SCA) is a metaheuristic optimization model that utilizes sine and cosine functions to update solution locations frequently. Initially, the SCA begins with a randomly generated population of candidate solutions. The location of every solution is updated using mathematical guidelines based on sine and cosine functions. Conclusion

This paper presents an OHDLM-HARMOA model. The primary intention of this paper is to develop an effective HAR method that assists and improves the quality of life for people with disabilities through accurate activity monitoring. Initially, the data pre-processing stage applies Z-score normalization for converting the input data into a structured pattern. For the feature selection process, the ACO model is employed to select the most relevant and significant features from a dataset. Furthermore, the OHDLM-HARMOA model utilizes the hybridization of the CNN-BiGRU-A technique for the classification procedure. Finally, the parameter tuning process is carried out by using the SCA model to increase the classification performance of the CNN-BiGRU-A classifier. The experimental evaluation of the OHDLM-HARMOA approach is performed under the WISDM dataset. The comparison analysis of the OHDLM-HARMOA approach demonstrated a superior accuracy value of 99.00% over existing models. The limitations of the OHDLM-HARMOA approach comprise restricted evaluation across diverse environmental conditions and user variations, which may affect generalizability. The model’s performance may degrade with intrinsic or overlapping gestures due to the limited variability in the training dataset. Latency issues may also limit the real-time deployment of applications on low-power devices. Furthermore, the current setup does not account for continuous gesture streams or dynamic gesture segmentation. Label imbalance and the absence of multilingual gesture representations restrict broader applicability. Future works may include expanding the dataset, integrating adaptive learning mechanisms, and improving robustness to noisy inputs and real-world usage conditions.

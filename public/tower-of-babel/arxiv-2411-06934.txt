An algebro-geometric model for the configuration category
Pedro Boavida de Brito, Geoffroy Horel, Danica Kosanović

Abstract
. Using log-geometry, we construct a model for the configuration category of a smooth algebraic variety. As an application, we prove the formality of certain configuration spaces.



1.
Introduction

Given a finite set
S
and a manifold
M
, we denote by
\mathrm{Conf}_{S}(M)
the space of embeddings of
S
in
M
. These spaces play a fundamental role in differential and geometric topology. They have also been of interest in arithmetic geometry. In particular, there is a tradition of studying the motivic structure on the fundamental group of configuration spaces of algebraic curves (see for example
[
Iha86
,
KO87
]
).

From the point of view of topology, an important fact is that the collection of these configuration spaces for all finite sets
S
are related by a rich structure. The most obvious part of this structure is given by the
forgetful
maps

\mathrm{Conf}_{S}(M)\to\mathrm{Conf}_{S-s}(M)

that discard one of the points
s\in S
. In the other direction, there are
insertion
maps

\mathrm{Conf}_{S}(M)\times\mathrm{Conf}_{T}(D)\to\mathrm{Conf}_{(S-s)\sqcup T}(M).

that replace the point labelled
s
by a configuration of
T
points in a small enough ball
D
around that point. This notation is somewhat abusive as the source is usually not a product but rather the total space of a fiber bundle over
\mathrm{Conf}_{S}(M)
whose fiber is
\mathrm{Conf}_{T}(D)
. There are also some choices involved in making this map (but these choices are irrelevant in the homotopy category). When
M
is a smooth manifold, there is a beautiful geometric incarnation of these insertion maps: replacing configuration spaces by their Axelrod-Singer compactifications
[
AS94
]
, the insertion map becomes simply the inclusion of a stratum. This point of view has one further merit which interests us in this paper. The Axelrod-Singer compactification has an algebraic predecessor, due to Fulton-MacPherson
[
FM94
]
, and so there is a natural algebro-geometric analog of the insertion maps when
M
is a smooth algebraic variety.

When the manifold is
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, the forgetful and insertion operations are the structure maps of the little
n
-disks operad. For a more general manifold, a convenient object that packages all of this structure is the “configuration category” of the manifold
[
BdBW18a
]
. As the name suggests, the configuration category is a category or, more accurately, an
(\infty,1)
-category. Roughly speaking, its objects are configuration of any number of points in the manifold and its morphisms are paths of configurations in which points are allowed to collide, but if that happens, they have to stay together until the end. As it turns out, the little
n
-disks operad and the configuration category of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
are equivalent pieces of data in a homotopical sense
[
BdBW18a
]
. When
M
is a differentiable manifold, the configuration category has an “Axelrod-Singer model” where the insertion and forgetful maps correspond to source maps from morphism to object spaces.

It has long been conjectured that the little disks operad has an algebro-geometric origin. That goes back at least to Beilinson, who suggested it in a letter to Kontsevich, and Morava who asks
[
Mor07
]
: is the little disks operad defined over
\mathbf{Q}
? This conjecture has been settled in the work of Vaintrob
[
Vai19
]
and Dupont-Panzer-Pym
[
DPP24
]
using the theory of log-schemes. More precisely, these authors construct an operad in a category of log-schemes over the complex numbers whose Kato-Nakayama realization is a model for the (framed) little
2
-disks operad.

The goal of the present paper is to construct an algebro-geometric model for the configuration category of
any
algebraic variety. Again it is built using the theory of log-schemes. To state our main theorem, we regard the configuration category as a space-valued functor on
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}
, the category of simplices of the nerve of the category of finite sets (remark
5.6
). Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety over a field
K
. There is a functor from
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}^{\mathrm{op}}
to log-schemes over
K
– denoted
\mathrm{con}^{log}(X)
– with the following properties.

(i)

Its value on a finite set
S
is the Fulton-MacPherson scheme
\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
with its divisorial log-structure.

(ii)

If the base field is
\mathbf{C}
then the Kato-Nakayama realization of
\mathrm{con}^{log}(X)
is isomorphic to the Axelrod-Singer model
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))
for the configuration category of
X(\mathbf{C})
.

As a consequence, we obtain a log-model for the little
n
-disks operad for every
n
, suitably interpreted. By that we mean, we regard the little disks operad as a functor on a category of forests, in contrast to Dupont-Panzer-Pym for
n=2
, who take a strict view of the operad but expand the classical meaning of
morphism
of log scheme.

An immediate corollary of the theorem is the following result which was the original motivation behind this paper.

Corollary
.

Let
X
be a smooth scheme over
\mathbf{Q}
. The profinite completion of
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))
, the Axelrod-Singer model for the configuration category of
X(\mathbf{C})
, has an action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})
. This action extends the natural Galois action on the profinite completion of the configuration spaces of points in
X(\mathbf{C})
.

Note that when
X
is affine
n
-space, we obtain a homotopy coherent Galois action on the profinite completion of
E_{2n}
as opposed to an action which is coherent up to homotopy. (The latter, in the case
n=1
, amounts to a map from
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})
to the group of homotopy automorphisms of the profinite completion of
E_{2}
which is isomorphic to the Grothendieck-Teichmuller group by
[
Hor17
]
.)

Combining these Galois actions with the additivity theorem for configuration categories we can deduce formality, in the sense of rational homotopy theory, for configuration spaces in
X(\mathbf{C})\times\mathbf{R}
where
X
is a smooth scheme over
\mathbf{C}
(Corollary
9.9
). In future work, we will use this to prove that the homological Goodwillie-Weiss spectral sequence for knots in
X(\mathbf{C})\times\mathbf{R}
collapses rationally.

2.
Categories of Forests

2.1.
Forests

Definition 2.1
.

Fix a finite set
S
. A
forest
on
S
is a set
\Phi
of non-empty subsets of
S
such that

(i)

Every one-point subset of
S
is contained in
\Phi
, and is called a
leaf
of
\Phi
.

(ii)

Any two elements of
\Phi
are either disjoint or comparable for the inclusion relation.

A
tree
on a set
U
is a forest containing the set
U
, which is called the
root
of
\Phi
. Moreover, for a forest
\Phi
on
S
and a maximal element
U
of
\Phi
, the set of elements in
\Phi
contained in
U
forms a tree on
U
. Clearly, any forest is a disjoint union of trees, called the
trees of the forest

\Phi
.

Remark 2.2
.

Given a forest
\Phi
on
S
as above, we associate to it a graph
G_{\Phi}

as follows.

(i)

If
\Phi
consists of trees
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{n}
, then
G_{\Phi}
is the disjoint union
G_{\Phi}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}G_{\Phi_{i}}
.

(ii)

If
\Phi
is the unique tree on a one-element set
S=\{s\}
, then
G_{\Phi}
has two vertices connected by one edge. One vertex is a leaf decorated by
s
and the other is a non-decorated root.

(iii)

If
\Phi
is a tree on a set
U
with strictly more than one element, then we construct a contractible graph (a tree in graphical sense) whose vertices are the elements of
\Phi
. We add one edge connecting vertices
A
and
B
if and only if they are comparable with respect to inclusion and there are no other elements of
\Phi
between them (i.e. if say
A\subsetneq B
, then there is no element
C
in
\Phi
such that
A\subsetneq C\subsetneq B
). Vertices with one incident edge correspond either to
U
(the root), or to the one-element sets (the leaves). There are no
unary
vertices, i.e. vertices having two incident edges.

For a finite set
S
, we write
P(S)
for the poset of non-empty subsets of
S
. The forests on a set
S
form a poset
\mathsf{For}(S)
under the inclusion relation, as subsets of
P(S)
. This poset has a minimal element given by the forest with only one point sets.

Observe that if
j\colon S\hookrightarrow T
is an injection, then any forest on
T
induces a forest on
S
by intersecting it with
S
. This operation is clearly a map of posets

j^{-1}\colon\mathsf{For}(T)\to\mathsf{For}(S),

so that
S\mapsto\mathsf{For}(S)
is a contravariant functor from finite sets and injections to posets.

We define
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
to be the Grothendieck construction of this functor. That is, an object of
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
is a pair
(S,\Phi)
with
S
a finite set and
\Phi
a forest on
S
. A morphism from
(S,\Phi)
to
(T,\Psi)
is an injection
j\colon S\to T
such that
\Phi\subset j^{-1}\Psi
. The actual category
\mathrm{For}
of forests that we will use is a certain quotient of
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
, defined as follows.

Definition 2.3
.

The category
\mathrm{For}
has the same objects as
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
, and if
(S,\Phi)
and
(T,\Psi)
are objects of
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
, we define

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{For}}((S,\Phi),(T,\Psi))\coloneqq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{For}^{\prime}}((S,\Phi),(T,\Psi))/\sim,

where the equivalence relation
\sim
identifies two injective maps
i,j\colon S\to T
if
i^{-1}\Psi=j^{-1}\Psi
. The composition is induced from the composition in
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
.

Concretely, we think of an object in
\mathrm{For}
as a contractible graph whose vertices are not unary. (An unary vertex is one having exactly two adjacent edges.) A morphism in the opposite category corresponds to contracting inner edges, making sure the resulting forest has no unary vertices, or to removing a root edge. This is reminiscent of
[
MW07
]
. We can make this more precise as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

The category
\mathrm{For}
is equivalent to the category whose objects are posets
V
satisfying

(i)

Given
v\in V
, the set
\{w|w\leq v\}
is totally ordered.

(ii)

For
S
the set of maximal elements of
V
, the map
V\to P(S)
which to a vertex
v
assigns the set of maximal elements that are bigger than
v
, is injective.

and whose morphisms are injective maps of posets preserving
independence
, i.e., sending uncomparable elements to uncomparable elements.

Proof.

Denote by
C
the category in the statement. Given a forest
(S,\Phi)
, the set
\Phi
(whose elements we call vertices) has a poset structure given by reverse inclusion. Then
\Phi
clearly satisfies condition (i) and condition (ii) rephrases the condition that the forest has no unary vertices.

Conversely, from any finite poset
V
satisfying conditions (i) and (ii) we may define a forest as the image of the map
V\to P(S)
in (ii), where
S
is the set of maximal elements of
V
. This establishes a bijection between forests in the sense above and posets satisfying conditions (i) and (ii). (Note that for such posets, the map in (ii) identifies the order on
\Phi
with the opposite order on
P(S)
.)

The assignment
(S,\Phi)\mapsto\Phi
defines a functor
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}\to C
: a morphism
j:(S,\Phi)\to(T,\Psi)
gives rise to an injective map of posets
\Phi\to j^{-1}(\Psi)\to\Psi
which preserves independence. It is clear that this functor factors through
\mathrm{For}
. Conversely, we describe the functor
C\to\mathrm{For}
as follows. Given a morphism
f:V\to W
in
C
, we get a corresponding map from the set
S
of maximal elements of
V
to the set
R
of maximal elements of
W
sending
s\in S
to
any

r\in R
such that
r\geq f(s)
. The resulting map
S\to R
is a morphism in
\mathrm{For}
(but not in
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
). The two functors that we have constructed give an inverse equivalence between
C
and
\mathrm{For}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The set of forests is in bijection with the set of nests in
[
AS94
]
and
[
BdBW18a
, Section 3]
, in the “screen compactification model” built out of the spaces
\mathrm{AS}_{n}(X)
. Indeed, given a nest we may add to it all the one-point subsets and obtain a forest. Conversely, given a forest, we can remove all the one-point subsets and obtain a nest.

2.2.
Forests with levels

Consider the category
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}
of simplices of the nerve of the category of finite sets. Concretely, an object of
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}
is a functor
\alpha\colon[k]\to\mathrm{Fin}
for some
k
, i.e. a sequence

S_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}S_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}\ldots\xrightarrow{f_{k-1}}S_{k}

of composable maps of finite sets. A morphism from
\alpha\colon[k]\to\mathrm{Fin}
to
\beta\colon[l]\to\mathrm{Fin}
is a map
\delta\colon[k]\to[l]
in
\Delta
such that
\alpha=\beta\circ\delta
. Concretely, a morphism corresponding to an elementary degeneracy insert an identity, and a morphism corresponding to an elementary face is either a composition of two maps or it forgets the first or last map.

We use the characterization of
\mathrm{For}
in lemma
2.4
to describe a functor

F:\Delta\mathrm{Fin}\to\mathrm{For}\;.

An object
\alpha
of
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}
determines an object
F(\alpha)
of
\mathrm{For}
, as follows. As a set
F(\alpha)
is the quotient of the disjoint union of the
S_{i}
by the relation
x\sim f_{i}(x)
if the preimage of
x
under
f_{i}
is
\{x\}
. As for the order relation on
F(\alpha)
, we take the order induced by declaring
x\leq y
in the disjoint union of the
S_{i}
if
x=g(y)
where
g
is some composition of maps
f_{j}
.

For a morphism
\alpha\to\alpha^{\prime}
in
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}
corresponding to a surjection
\delta:[k]\to[\ell]
, we define
F(\alpha)\to F(\alpha^{\prime})
to be the identity. For a morphism
\alpha\to\alpha^{\prime}
corresponding to an injection
\delta:[k]\to[\ell]
, the induced map

\coprod_{i\in[k]}S_{i}\to\coprod_{i\in[\ell]}S_{i}

respects the order relation and passes to the quotient, defining a map
F(\alpha)\to F(\alpha^{\prime})
. It is clear that
F
respects compositions.

3.
Blow-ups

3.1.
Complex and oriented real blow-ups

We follow quite closely the treatment of blow-ups of
[
BDPW23
, Section 8.3]
. Let
\mathbf{P}^{n-1}_{\mathbf{C}}=(\mathbf{C}^{n}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.4pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\displaystyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.4pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\textstyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.3pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\scriptstyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.45pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}}0)/\mathbf{C}^{\times}
and
S^{n-1}\cong(\mathbf{R}^{n}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.4pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\displaystyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.4pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\textstyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.3pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\scriptstyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}{\mspace{-6mu}\raisebox{0.45pt}{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{-25.0}{$\scriptscriptstyle\smallsetminus$}}\mspace{-6mu}}}0)/\mathbf{R}_{>0}
.

Definition 3.1
.

For a topological space
X
and a continuous map
\sigma\colon X\to\mathbf{C}^{n}
, we define the
complex blow-up
along
\sigma
as the space

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(X)\coloneqq\{(x,[w])\in X\times\mathbf{P}^{n-1}_{\mathbf{C}}:(\exists\alpha\in\mathbf{C})\;\sigma(x)=\alpha w\}.

Definition 3.2
.

For a topological space
X
and a continuous map
\sigma\colon X\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we define the
real oriented blow-up
along
\sigma
as the space

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(X)\coloneqq\{(x,[w])\in X\times S^{n-1}:(\exists\alpha\in\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0})\;\sigma(x)=\alpha w\}.

In either case there is a
blow-down map

bl_{\sigma}
to
X
given by
bl_{\sigma}(x,[w])=x
. This is an isomorphism away from the zero-set of
\sigma
, whereas over any zero of
\sigma
the fiber is the projective space
\mathbf{P}^{n-1}_{\mathbf{C}}
, or the sphere
S^{n-1}
respectively. The zero-set of
\sigma
is called the
blow-up locus
, and its inverse image under
bl_{\sigma}
is the
exceptional divisor
.

If
Y\subseteq X
we define the
dominant transform

{\widetilde{Y}}\subseteq\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(X)
as the closure of

bl_{\sigma}^{-1}(\{y\in Y:\sigma(y)\neq 0\})

in case this is nonempty, i.e.
Y
is not contained in
\sigma^{-1}(0)
; otherwise, define
{\widetilde{Y}}
as
bl_{\sigma}^{-1}(Y)
.

Example 3.3
.

For
X=\mathbf{C}^{n}
and
\sigma=Id\colon\mathbf{C}^{n}\to\mathbf{C}^{n}
we have
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{C}^{n})=\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{n})
, the (complex projective) blow-up of
\mathbf{C}^{n}
at the origin. More generally, for
X
a complex algebraic variety and
\sigma:X\times\mathbf{C}^{n}\to\mathbf{C}
given by
\sigma(x,u)=u
, the blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(X\times\mathbf{C}^{n})
can be identified with

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{X\times 0}(X\times\mathbf{C}^{n})\cong X\times\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{0}(\mathbf{C}^{n}).

Example 3.4
.

For
X=\mathbf{R}^{m+n}
and
\sigma=pr\colon\mathbf{R}^{m+n}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
the projection to the last
n
coordinates, we have the zero-locus
\mathbf{R}^{m}\times 0
. Then there is a map

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}^{m+n})=\{((x,y),[w])\in X\times S^{n-1}:(\exists\alpha\in\mathbf{R}_{>0})\;y=\alpha w\}\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}^{m}\times\mathbf{R}_{\geq 0}\times S^{n-1}

defined by
((x,y),[w])\mapsto(x,|y|,[w])
. This is clearly continuous and surjective, and it is injective as well: if
|y_{1}|=|y_{2}|
and both
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are positively proportional to a fixed
w
, then
y_{1}=y_{2}
. The blow-down map corresponds to
(x,r,[w])\mapsto(x,\frac{r}{|w|}w)
.

We can make similar definitions when
\sigma
is, more generally, a section of a complex or real vector bundle
E
: we use the formulas above in local charts in which
E
is trivial and glue those spaces together. In each case there is a canonical blow-down map to
X
, whose fiber over a non-zero point of
\sigma
is homeomorphic to a point, whereas the fiber over a zero of
\sigma
is homeomorphic to a copy of
\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{C}}^{n-1}
, respectively
S^{n-1}
. Then the following is immediate.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
E\to X
is a complex vector bundle and
0_{E}\colon X\to E
is the zero section, then
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{0_{E}}(X)
is the projectivization of
E
. Similarly, if
E\to X
is a real vector bundle, then
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{0_{E}}(X)
is the sphere bundle of
E
.

Remark 3.6
.

For
\sigma\colon X\to\mathbf{C}^{n}
that is not identically zero, we can view
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(X)
as the dominant transform of the graph of
\sigma
under the blow-up of
X\times 0\subset X\times\mathbf{C}^{n}
.
Similarly, for a section
\sigma\colon X\to E
of a bundle, the space
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(X)
is the dominant transform of the graph of
\sigma
under the complex projective blow-up of
E
along the zero section. And similarly in the real oriented case.

3.2.
Iterated real blow-ups

The blow-up construction is functorial in the following way.

Proposition 3.7
.

Given a map
f\colon X\to Y
of topological spaces and a section
\sigma\colon X\to E
of a real vector bundle, consider the pullback bundle
f^{*}E
and the section
f^{*}\sigma
, and form
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{f^{*}\sigma}(Y)
. Then the following is a cartesian square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{f^{*}\sigma}(Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Proof.

This appears in
[
BDPW23
, Paragraph 8.3.5]
. It can be checked locally on
X
so we may assume that
E
is a trivial bundle in which case this is a trivial computation.
∎

Now let
X
be a topological space,
\{E_{i}\to X\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
a collection of real vector bundles and
\{\sigma_{i}\colon X\to E_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
a collection of sections. We abusively denote by

(3.1)

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}(X)

the iterated blow-up construction where at each stage we pullback the remaining vector bundles and sections on the already constructed real oriented blow-up.

Proposition 3.8
.

Up to homeomorphism, the resulting space (
3.1
) is independent on the order of the blow-ups.

Proof.

Assume that we have vector bundles
E
and
F
and sections
\sigma\colon X\to E
and
\tau\colon X\to F
. If we define
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma,\tau}(X)
by the following cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma,\tau}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\tau}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces bl_{\sigma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces bl_{\tau}}

then
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma,\tau}(X)
is clearly homeomorphic both to
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{bl_{\tau}^{*}(\sigma)}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\tau}(X)
and
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{bl_{\sigma}^{*}(\tau)}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(X)
, which we abusively denoted
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\tau}(X)
and
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\tau}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(X)
. This proves the case
n=2
, and the general case
n\geq 2
follows by induction.
∎

3.3.
Blowing-up a submanifold

Let
X
be a smooth manifold and
Z\subset X
be a codimension
c
submanifold. If
Z
is defined by the zero set of a smooth map
\sigma\colon X\to\mathbf{R}^{c}
, we define

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{Z}(X)\coloneqq\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}(X),

where we view
\sigma
as a section of the trivial
\mathbf{R}^{c}
-bundle on
X
. In general,
Z
is locally of this form and we define
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{Z}(X)
by glueing the local blowups. This is verified in detail in
[
AK10
]
(note that they define blow-ups slightly differently, but equivalently thanks to Example
3.4
).

3.4.
Blowing-up a subscheme

There is also the algebro-geometric blow-up
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)
when
Z\subset X
is a closed subscheme, see any book on schemes. This is related to the topological construction thanks to the following.

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume
X
is a smooth complex scheme and
Z\subset X
a smooth closed subscheme of codimension
c
defined as the zero locus of a section
\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}
a locally free sheaf of rank
c
. Then there is a homeomorphism

(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X)(\mathbf{C})\cong\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{\sigma}(X(\mathbf{C})).

We shall need the following important proposition.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
X
be a smooth complex scheme and
Z\to X
be a smooth codimension
c
subscheme. Then there is a homeomorphism over
X(\mathbf{C})
:

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{Z(\mathbf{C})}{X(\mathbf{C})}\cong\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{{\widetilde{Z}}(\mathbf{C})}[(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X)(\mathbf{C})],

where
{\widetilde{Z}}\subset\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)
denotes the dominant transform of
Z
, which in this case is just the exceptional divisor.

Proof.

If
Z
is given by the vanishing locus of a map
\sigma\colon X\to\mathbf{A}^{c}
, then this is a straightforward computation. In general,
X
will be covered by affine subschemes on which this is the case.
∎

4.
Log schemes

4.1.
Log schemes and their blow-ups

Definition 4.1
.

A
log structure
on a scheme
X
is a finite collection
\{\sigma_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of line bundles on
X
each equipped with a section. The pair
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\})
is called a
log scheme
. Given two log structures
\{\sigma_{i}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}\}
and
\{\tau_{j}:\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{j}\}
on the same scheme
X
, a morphism from
\{\sigma_{i}\}
to
\{\tau_{j}\}
is the data of natural numbers
e_{ij}
and isomorphisms

\mathcal{L}_{i}\cong\bigotimes_{j}\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\otimes e_{ij}}

identifying the section
\sigma_{i}
with
\bigotimes_{j}\tau_{j}^{\otimes e_{ij}}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a morphism of schemes
f\colon Z\to X
and a log structure
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in I}
on
X
, there is an
induced log structure
on
Z
given by
\{f^{*}(\sigma_{i})\colon\mathcal{O}_{Z}\to f^{*}\mathcal{L}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
.

A morphism of log-schemes
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\})\to\mathsf{Y}=(Y,\{\tau_{j}\})
is a map of schemes
f:X\to Y
together with a morphism of log-structures from the log-structure of
X
to the log-structure pulled-back along
f
.

Example 4.3
.

For
Z\subset X
a smooth codimension
1
subscheme, we have a corresponding line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
with a canonical section
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z)
. This gives a log-structure on
X
that we denote by
\mathsf{X}
. There is also a log structure on
Z
given by the normal bundle of the inclusion
Z\to X
and the zero section. This log-structure denoted
\mathsf{Z}
is simply the induced log structure along the inclusion
Z\subset X
. It follows that
\mathsf{Z}\to\mathsf{X}
is a morphism of log schemes.

More generally, if we have a finite collection
Z_{i}
of codimension
1
subschemes (for example, a normal crossing divisor), we have the corresponding log scheme
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\})
.

There is a natural blow-up construction in the category of log schemes.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\})
be a smooth log scheme and
Z
be a smooth closed subscheme of positive codimension. We define the
logarithmic blow-up of
\mathsf{X}
along
Z
as the log scheme

\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{Z}(\mathsf{X})=\big(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X),\,\{bl_{Z}^{*}\sigma_{i}\}\sqcup\{\sigma^{\prime}\}\big),

where
bl_{Z}\colon\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)\to X
is the blow-down map and
\sigma^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}({\widetilde{Z}})
is the line bundle corresponding to the divisor
{\widetilde{Z}}
.

4.2.
The Kato–Nakayama realization

Definition 4.5
.

For a log scheme
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n})
over
\mathbf{C}
, the
Kato–Nakayama space
is the topological space defined as the iterated real blow-up of its complex points:

\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})\coloneqq\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n-1}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}[X(\mathbf{C})].

Remark 4.6
.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{i}
are complex line bundles on
X
, that are in the definition of
\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})
viewed as real plane bundles on
X(\mathbf{C})
. The construction of
\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})
does not depend on the chosen order of blow-ups thanks to Proposition
3.8
.

Example 4.7
.

As in Example
4.3
let
Z_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
, be codimension
1
subschemes, giving the log scheme
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(Z_{i})\}_{1\leq i\leq n})
. If
n=1
, then
\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})
is simply the oriented real blow-up of
Z_{1}
and is a manifold with boundary. More generally, if
\bigcup_{i}Z_{i}
forms a normal crossing divisor, then
\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})
is a smooth manifold with corners.

Proposition 4.8
.

If
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n})
is a smooth log scheme and
Z
is a smooth closed subscheme of positive codimension, then there is a homeomorphism

\mathrm{KN}(\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{Z}(\mathsf{X}))\cong\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Z})}(\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})).

Proof.

Working locally, we may assume that
X
is a smooth affine scheme and
Z
is a complete intersection, i.e.
Z=\sigma^{-1}(0)
for
\sigma\colon X\to\mathbf{A}^{c}
an algebraic map and
c=\operatorname{codim}(Z)
.
On one hand, by Definition
4.4
we have
\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{Z}(\mathsf{X})=\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}(X)
with the log structure
\{bl_{Z}^{*}\sigma_{i}\}\cup\{\sigma^{\prime}\}
, so by Definition
4.5
we have

\mathrm{KN}(\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{Z}(\mathsf{X}))=\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma^{\prime}}[(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X)(\mathbf{C}))].

By Proposition
3.10
we can omit the complex blow-up, so the last space is homeomorphic to

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}[X(\mathbf{C})].

By Proposition
3.8
we can change the order of the blow-ups, so this is homeomorphic to

\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}[X(\mathbf{C})].

Since by definition
\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})=\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{n-1}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}[X(\mathbf{C})]
, the last space is immediately identified with
\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Z})}(\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X}))
.
∎

We shall also need the following proposition.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathsf{X}=(X,\{\sigma_{i}\})
be a log scheme and
f\colon Z\to X
a map of smooth schemes. Let
\mathsf{Z}=(Z,\{f^{*}\sigma_{i}\})
be the induced log scheme. Then there is a cartesian square of topological spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Proof.

This can be proved by induction on the number of line bundles in the log-structure. The base case and the induction step are proved using Proposition
3.7
.
∎

5.
Compactifications of configuration spaces

5.1.
Stratified spaces

In this paper, it will be convenient to use the following definition of stratified spaces.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
P
be a poset. A
P
-stratified space is a functor
X:P\to\mathrm{Top}
such that for each map
p\leq q
in
P
, the induced map

X(p)\to X(q)

is a closed inclusion.

Remark 5.2
.

If
X
is a stratified space in the usual sense, we can take
P
to be the poset of strata and obtain a functor as above sending
p
to the closure of the corresponding stratum. We will write
X(p)^{\circ}
for the
open
stratum indexed by
p
, so that

X(p)=\overline{X(p)^{\circ}}

and

X(p)^{\circ}=X(p)-\bigcup_{q<p}X(q)

If our poset
P
has a maximal element
\infty
, then all the values of a
P
-stratified space
X
are closed subspaces of
X(\infty)
. In this situation, we shall say that
X
is a
P
-stratification of
X(\infty)
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
P
be a poset with a maximal element
\infty
and
X:P\to\mathrm{Top}
be a
P
-stratified space. Let
f:Y\to X(\infty)
be a continous map. We define the pullback stratification on
Y
along
f
to be the functor

P\to\mathrm{Top}

sending
p
to
f^{-1}(X(p))\subset Y
.

5.2.
The Axelrod–Singer compactification

Let
X
be a
smooth manifold without boundary
and
n
a positive integer. The ordered configuration space
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
of
n
points in
X
is the interior of a smooth manifold with corners
\mathrm{AS}_{n}(X)
constructed in
[
AS94
, Section 5]
and called the Axelrod–Singer compactification. (Note, however, that despite the name
\mathrm{AS}_{n}(X)
is only compact if
X
itself is compact.) This construction has the following properties.

(i)

\mathrm{AS}_{n}(X)
is a stratified space whose strata are indexed by
\mathsf{For}(n)
, the poset of forests on
\{1,\ldots n\}
. We denote by
\mathrm{AS}_{\Phi}(X)
the closure of the stratum indexed by a forest
\Phi
.

(ii)

The stratum corresponding to the unique forest with
n
trees is the interior of
\mathrm{AS}_{n}(X)
and is diffeomorphic
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
.

(iii)

The codimension of
\mathrm{AS}_{\Phi}(X)^{\circ}
is the number of elements of
\Phi
that have strictly more than one element.

5.3.
The Fulton MacPherson compactification

Let
X
be a
smooth algebraic variety
over a field
\mathbf{K}
and
n
a positive integer. By an algebraic variety, we mean an integral separated scheme over
\mathbf{K}
. The ordered configuration space
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
of
n
points in
X
has an analogous compactification
\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
, a predecessor to the Axelrod-Singer compactification due to Fulton and MacPherson
[
FM94
]
, that has the following properties.

(i)

\mathrm{FM}_{n}(X)
is stratified by subvarieties
\mathrm{FM}^{\circ}_{\Phi}(X)
indexed by elements
\Phi\in\mathrm{For}(n)
, the poset of forests on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(ii)

The subvariety corresponding to the unique forest with
n
trees is the complement of a normal crossing divisor and is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X)
.

(iii)

The components of this divisor are indexed by forests with only one element of cardinality
\geq 2
.

(iv)

If
\Phi
and
\Psi
are two forests on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\Phi\cup\Psi
is still a forest, then

\mathrm{FM}_{\Phi}(X)\cap\mathrm{FM}_{\Psi}(X)=\mathrm{FM}_{\Phi\cup\Psi}(X)\;.

5.4.
Forgetting points

Let
i\colon S\to T
be an injection between finite sets. Precomposition with this map induces a projection map
p\colon X^{T}\to X^{S}
given by

(x_{t})_{t\in T}\mapsto(x_{i(s)})_{s\in S}.

This map restricts to a map

\mathrm{Conf}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{Conf}_{S}(X)

We claim that this map can be extended to the Axelrod–Singer compactification if
X
is a smooth manifold and to the Fulton–MacPherson compactification if
X
is a smooth algebraic variety. We explain this for the latter case, the former being similar.

For a finite set
R
, the variety
\mathrm{FM}_{R}(X)
is obtained by blowing up the diagonals
\Delta_{U}
for
U\subset R
with
|U|\geq 2
in order of increasing dimension. Then we define the map

p\colon\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)

as the composite of two maps

\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)

where
\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)
is by definition the iterated blow-up of diagonals of
X^{T}
indexed by subsets of
T
of the form
i(U)
for
U
a subset of
S
of cardinality at least
2
. The map
\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)
is then simply a blow-down map (we blow up more things in the source than in the target) while the second map
\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
is induced by functoriality of blow-ups.

The map
p\colon\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
is compatible with the stratification. Let us explain precisely what this means. The source is stratified by the poset
\mathrm{For}(T)^{\mathrm{op}}
while the target is stratified by
\mathrm{For}(S)^{\mathrm{op}}
. The injective map
S\to T
induces a map of posets
\mathrm{For}(T)\to\mathrm{For}(S)
so we may view both schemes as stratified by
\mathrm{For}(T)
and it is straightforward to verify that the map
p
extend to a natural transformation of functors
\mathrm{For}(T)^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbf{K}}
.

5.5.
The configuration category

Proposition 5.4
.

For a smooth manifold
X
without boundary, there is a functor
\mathrm{For}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Top}
whose value on a forest
(S,\Phi)
is the closed stratum
\mathrm{AS}_{\Phi}(X)\subset\mathrm{AS}_{S}(X)
.

Proof.

First, we show that the assignment
(S,\Phi)\to\mathrm{AS}_{\Phi}(X)
can be promoted to a functor
(\mathrm{For}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Top}
. By definition,
\mathrm{For}^{\prime}
is generated by maps of the form
\mathrm{id}_{S}\colon(S,\Phi)\to(S,\Psi)
with
\Phi\subset\Psi
and maps of the form
f\colon(S,\Phi)\to(T,f^{-1}\Phi)
with
f\colon T\hookrightarrow S
an injection. To a map of the first kind we assign the inclusion of strata

\mathrm{AS}_{\Psi}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{AS}_{\Phi}(X)

and to a map of the second kind, we assign the map

\mathrm{AS}_{\Phi}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{AS}_{f^{-1}\Phi}(X)

induced by forgetting points and changing labels according to the map
f
. This defines a functor
(\mathrm{For}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Top}
. Moreover, the factorization to
\mathrm{For}^{\mathrm{op}}
is trivially checked.
∎

If we precompose the functor from the proposition with the functor

\Delta\mathrm{Fin}\to\mathrm{For}

given in section
2.2
, we obtain a functor

\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X)\colon\Delta\mathrm{Fin}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Top}.

Theorem 5.5
.

The functor
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X)
is the Axelrod–Singer model of the configuration category, in the sense of
[
BdBW18a
]
.

Remark 5.6
.

Recall from
[
BdBW18a
]
that the configuration category of the manifold
M
– denoted
\mathrm{con}(M)
– is the category whose space of objects is the disjoint union of configuration spaces
\mathrm{Conf}_{S}(M)
,
S
a finite set (possibly the empty set), and a morphism from a configuration
x:R\hookrightarrow M
to a configuration
y:S\hookrightarrow M
is a pair
(f,H)
where
f:R\to S
is a map of finite sets, and
H
is a (reverse) exit path in the stratified space
M^{R}
, stratified by the set of equivalence relations on
R
. The configuration category has an obvious reference map to the category of finite sets, and it is a good idea to take the nerve and regard
\mathrm{con}(M)
as a simplicial space over the nerve of
\mathrm{Fin}
. Given a simplicial space over
\mathrm{Fin}
,
p:X\to\mathrm{Fin}
, we may produce a functor
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}\to\mathrm{Top}
whose value at
\alpha\in\mathrm{Fin}_{k}
is simply
p^{-1}(\alpha)
. Conversely, given a functor
F:\Delta\mathrm{Fin}\to\mathrm{Top}
we may produce a simplicial space
X
over
\mathrm{Fin}
by declaring that a
k
-simplex of
X
is a pair
(f,z)
where
f\in\mathrm{Fin}_{k}
and
z\in F(f)
. This induces an equivalence of (
\infty
-)categories.

Sketch proof.

This is stated in
[
BdBW18a
, Section 3]
, where the Axelrod-Singer model is called the
screen completion
model, but the sketch there is incomplete. A more detailed account appears in
[
Can23
]
. We will summarize that here. To compare
\mathrm{con}(M)
and
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(M)
we use the remark above to first view
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(M)
as a simplicial space (actually, topological category) over the nerve of
\mathrm{Fin}
, which we’ll denote by
C
for the duration of this proof. That is, a
k
-simplex of
C
is a pair
(f,z)
where
f:S_{0}\to\dots\to S_{k}
is a
k
-simplex in
\mathrm{Fin}
and
z\in\mathrm{con}^{AS}(M)(f)
. We will describe a zigzag of the form

C\to\mathcal{A}\leftarrow\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{con}(M)

where
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are certain topological categories over
\mathrm{Fin}
, to be defined.

The category
\mathcal{A}
has the same objects as
C
. A morphism in
\mathcal{A}
is a pair
(a,H)
, where
a
is a morphism in
C
and
H
is a (Moore) piecewise smooth exit path in the space of objects of
C
ending at the source of
a
. The source of the morphism
(a,H)
is
H(0)
and the target is the target of
a
. Composition is by parallel transport. The functor
C\to\mathcal{A}
is the identity on objects and on morphisms it is the inclusion of constant paths.

To describe the second auxiliary category
\mathcal{B}
, we equip
M
with a Riemannian metric. An object in the category
\mathcal{B}
is a pair
(x,\rho_{x})
where
x:\{1,\dots k\}\hookrightarrow M
is a configuration and
\rho_{x}\in(\mathbf{R}_{>0})^{k}
is a tuple of positive real numbers subject to the following conditions:

(i)

for each
i\in\{1,\dots k\}
, the ball
B_{i}
of radius
{\rho_{x}}_{i}
centered at
x_{i}
is geodesically convex (i.e. any two points in it are connected by a unique geodesic in that ball) and
{\rho_{x}}_{i}
is less than the injectivity radius at
x_{i}
.

(ii)

B_{i}\cap B_{j}=\varnothing
if
i\neq j
.

The (disjoint) union of the
B_{i}
will be denoted
B_{x}^{\rho}
. A morphism in
\mathcal{B}
from
(x,\rho_{x})
to
(y,\tau_{y})
exists if
B_{x}^{\rho}\subset B_{y}^{\tau}
and is given by an exit path in
B_{y}^{\tau}
from
x
to
y
which is
piecewise geodesic
. By a piecewise geodesic exit path we mean an exit path
H
on
{B_{y}^{\tau}}
for which one can find a finite partition of the interval into subintervals so that the restriction of
H
to each subinterval is a geodesic. The functor
\mathcal{B}\to\mathrm{con}(M)
forgets the balls, i.e. sends a pair
(x,\rho_{x})
to
x
.

We are left to describe the functor
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
. On objects, it also sends a pair
(x,\rho_{x})
to
x
, viewed as a configuration in the interior of the compactified configuration space. A morphism
(x,\rho_{x})\to(y,\rho_{y})
in
\mathcal{B}
given by a piecewise geodesic exit path
H
from
x
to
y
, with
x
a configuration of
k
points in
M
, gives rise to an exit path in the Axelrod-Singer
compactification
of the configuration space of
k
points in
M
. This is the main point, and why we need piecewise geodesic paths. An arbitrary path might not give rise to a path in the compactification, e.g. if one point is orbiting around another, being pulled towards it.

Having defined these three functors, showing that they are equivalences, in fact degreewise weak equivalences of Segal spaces, is more mechanical. On spaces of objects, this is immediate. Then one observes that, in the four categories, the target map from the space of morphisms to objects is a fibration. And verifies, for each functor, that the induced map between fibers of the target maps is a weak equivalence.
∎

Similarly, we can prove the following proposition. Let us denote by
\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbf{K}}
the category of smooth algebraic varieties over
\mathbf{K}
.

Proposition 5.7
.

For a smooth algebraic variety
X
over
\mathbf{K}
, there is a functor
\mathrm{For}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbf{K}}
whose value on a forest
(S,\Phi)
is the closed stratum
\mathrm{FM}_{\Phi}(X)\subset\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
.

Again we can precompose this functor with the functor

\Delta\mathrm{Fin}\to\mathrm{For}

of section
2.2
and obtain a functor

\mathrm{con}^{FM}(X)\colon\Delta\mathrm{Fin}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathbf{K}},

that we call the
Fulton–MacPherson model of the configuration category
.

Remark 5.8
.

If
\mathbf{K}
is the field of complex numbers, this object does not have the correct homotopy type in the sense that its analytification is not the configuration category of the analytification of
X
. However, our main Theorem, Theorem
7.2
, asserts that this object can be equipped with a log-structure whose Kato–Nakayama realization is exactly the object
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))
constructed in the previous paragraph.

6.
Wonderful compactifications and the Kato–Nakayama construction

Our main result is about configuration spaces and their compactification. However, with no additional effort we can prove a more general result about wonderful compactifications so we take this small detour.

6.1.
Wonderful compactifications

We follow the conventions of
[
Li09
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Fix a smooth algebraic variety
Y
over
\mathbf{C}
and a finite collection
\mathcal{G}
of smooth closed subvarieties of
Y
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of all possible intersections of some elements of
\mathcal{G}
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is called a
building set
if for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
the minimal elements in
\{G\in\mathcal{G}:G\supseteq S\}
intersect transversely (the tangent bundles span
TY
) and their intersection is
S
.

The
wonderful compactification

Y_{\mathcal{G}}
associated to this data is defined as the closure of

Y-\bigcup_{\mathcal{G}}G\subset\prod_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{Bl}_{G}Y.

It was shown in
[
Li09
]
that
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
can be constructed as an iterated blow-up

(6.1)

Y_{\mathcal{G}}=\mathrm{Bl}_{{\widetilde{G}}_{n}}\mathrm{Bl}_{{\widetilde{G}}_{n-1}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}_{G_{1}}Y,

where
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}\}
is an ordering such that
\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{i}\}
is a building set for each
i\leq n
.

The variety
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
contains a normal crossing divisor whose components are indexed by the elements of
\mathcal{G}
.
The complement of this normal crossing divisor is isomorphic to the complement of the arrangement of subvarieties generated by
\mathcal{G}
(i.e. the closure of
\mathcal{G}
under intersection).

Definition 6.2
.

We denote by
\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
the log scheme corresponding to this normal crossing divisor in
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
(see Example
4.3
).

We record the following proposition whose proof is immediate.

Proposition 6.3
.

The log-scheme
\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}
is the iterated logarithmic blow-up of
(Y,\mathcal{G})
, that is,

\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}=\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{{\widetilde{G}}_{n}}\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{{\widetilde{G}}_{n-1}}\ldots\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{G_{1}}Y\;.

Definition 6.4
.

In the real oriented world, we may perform the same sequence of blow-ups of
Y(\mathbf{C})
instead. This gives a topological space that we denote by
Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}
.

Let
(Y,\mathcal{G})
be as in Definition
6.1
. Then the variety
Y
is stratified by
P(\mathcal{G})
, the poset of subsets of
\mathcal{G}
, with the stratum indexed by
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{G}
given by
\bigcap_{G\in\mathcal{H}}G
. We make the convention that the closed stratum corresponding to the empty set is
Y
. These strata determine a functor

P(\mathcal{G})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Var}_{\mathbf{K}}

which, taking
\mathbf{C}
-points, induces a functor

P(\mathcal{G})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Top}.

Note that each time we blow up one element of
\mathcal{G}

and

are both stratified by the poset

Remark 6.5
.

For

and

can be replaced by the subposet

of

-nests
[

as these are the only elements of

whose corresponding stratum is non-empty.

Example 6.6
.

Our main examples are compactifications of configuration spaces

.

In the algebro-geometric case,

is a smooth algebraic variety, and we take

and

the set of diagonals

for every

is the arrangement of polydiagonals, and the construction

6.2.
Kato–Nakayama commutes with wonderul compactifications

Our main theorem in the general setting of wonderful compactifications is the following.

Theorem 6.7
.

There is a homeomorphism

\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})\cong Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}.

Moreover, this homeomorphism identifies the stratification on
Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}
indexed by
P(\mathcal{G})^{\mathrm{op}}
with the pullback (Definition
5.3
) of the stratification of
Y_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{C})
along the projection map

\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})\to Y_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{C}).

Proof.

We first prove the statement ignoring the stratification. The proof is by induction on the length of the ordered set
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{n}\}
. For
n=1
, we use the definition
\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}=(\mathrm{Bl}_{G_{1}}Y,{\widetilde{G}}_{1})
and Proposition
3.10
:

\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})\coloneqq\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{{\widetilde{G}}_{1}(\mathbf{C})}(\mathrm{Bl}_{G_{1}}Y(\mathbf{C}))\cong\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{G_{1}(\mathbf{C})}(Y(\mathbf{C}))\eqqcolon Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}.

Assume that the proposition has been proved for length
n-1
, and denote
\mathcal{H}=\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{n-1}\}
. By Proposition
6.3
, we have that

\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}}=\mathrm{Bl}^{log}_{{\widetilde{G}}_{n}}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{H}}).

Thus, Proposition
4.8
gives a homeomorphism

\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})\cong\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{KN}({\widetilde{G}}_{n})}(\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{H}}))\cong\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathrm{KN}({\widetilde{G}}_{n})}(Y_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathbf{R}})

where the second homeomorphism comes from the induction hypothesis. The right hand side of this equation is exactly
Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}
. (Indeed,
\mathrm{KN}({\widetilde{G}}_{n})
agrees with the inverse image of
{\widetilde{G}}_{n}
under the projection from the real blowup to the complex blowup.)

Now, we show that this homeomorphism is compatible with the stratification. We start with a stratum corresponding to one element
G
of
\mathcal{G}
. In this case the corresponding closed stratum in
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
is a divisor
D_{G}
in
Y_{\mathcal{G}}
. By Proposition
4.9
, if we give
D_{G}
the log-structure induced by the inclusion
D_{G}\to Y_{\mathcal{G}}
, we have a cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{D}_{G})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Y}_{\mathcal{G}})\cong Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\mathcal{G}},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

On the other hand, the closed stratum

in

is the dominant transform of

in
Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}
which can easily be identified with

if we track it through the sequence of blow-ups. This shows compatibility for the stratum for

Consider now the closed stratum

in

indexed by

. By definition, we have

Z_{S}=\bigcap_{G\in S}D_{G}.

Then the corresponding closed stratum in the pullback stratification of

is
\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Z}_{S})
where
\mathsf{Z}_{S}
is given the log-structure pulled-back along the inclusion

. By Proposition

, we have a cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{KN}(\mathsf{Z}_{S})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{S}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\mathcal{G}},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

On the other hand, using the case

which concludes the proof.
∎

The algebro-geometric configuration category

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
i\colon S\hookrightarrow T
be an injection. Then

(i)

The forgetful map
p\colon\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
can be promoted to a map of log schemes

p\colon\mathsf{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathsf{FM}_{S}(X).

(ii)

Upon applying Kato–Nakayama realization it induces the forgetful map

p\colon\mathrm{AS}_{T}(X(\mathbf{C}))\to\mathrm{AS}_{S}(X(\mathbf{C})).

Proof.

(i) We use our factorization

\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)

from subsection
5.4
. We give
\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)
the log-structure obtained from the exceptional divisors corresponding to the diagonals that have been blown-up (see Example
4.3
) and denote by
\mathsf{FM}_{T,S}(X)
the resulting log scheme. Then the map

\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)

is canonically a map of log schemes. Indeed, the pullback log structure on
\mathrm{FM}_{T}(X)
is simply the log-structure corresponding to the diagonals indexed by the image of
i
.

On the other hand, the log-structure of
\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)
is also the pullback of the log-structure of
\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
along the projection map
p\colon\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)
. It follows that the map

\mathrm{FM}_{T,S}(X)\to\mathrm{FM}_{S}(X)

is also a map of log schemes.

(ii) We wish to identify two continuous maps
\mathrm{AS}_{T}(X(\mathbf{C}))\to\mathrm{AS}_{S}(X(\mathbf{C}))
. By definition they coincide on the interior of
\mathrm{AS}_{T}(X(\mathbf{C}))
which is an open dense subset so they have to coincide on the whole space.
∎

Finally, we prove our main result.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth algebraic variety. There exists a functor

\mathrm{con}^{log}(X)\colon\Delta\mathrm{Fin}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{LogSch}_{\mathbf{K}}

such that

-

composing with the forgetful functor
\mathrm{LogSch}_{\mathbf{K}}\to\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathbf{K}}
recovers the functor
\mathrm{con}^{FM}(X)
.

-

taking
\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{C}
and applying Kato–Nakayama realization gives the functor
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))
.

Proof.

We show that the functor
\mathrm{For}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathbf{K}}
given by

(S,\Phi)\mapsto\mathsf{FM}_{\Phi}(X)

lifts to a functor to
\mathrm{LogSch}_{\mathbf{K}}
and that the precomposition of the lifted functor with the map
\Delta\mathrm{Fin}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{For}^{\mathrm{op}}
satisfies the properties in the statement.

We have explained how
\mathsf{FM}_{\Phi}(X)
is a log scheme.
Since maps in
\mathrm{For}
are composites of maps of the form

\mathrm{id}_{S}\colon(S,\Phi)\to(S,\Psi)

with
\Phi\subset\Psi
and maps of the form

i\colon(S,i^{-1}\Phi)\to(T,\Phi)

with
i\colon S\to T
an injection, it suffices to show that each of these maps induces map of log schemes. In the first case, this follows immediately from the definition of the log-structures on
\mathsf{FM}_{\Phi}(X)
and
\mathsf{FM}_{\Psi}(X)
(they are simply pullbacks of the log-structure of
\mathsf{FM}_{S}(X)
). In the second case, this is Proposition
7.1
.

Finally, this functor satisfies the above two requirements: this is obvious for the first and, for the second one, this is a consequence of Corollary
6.8
and Proposition
7.1
.
∎

7.1.
The real wonderful compactification as a closure

We conclude this section with a description of the real wonderful compactification as a closure. This is important as it reconciles the two existing definitions of the Axelrod–Singer compactification available in the literature: one through iterated blow-ups and the other as the closure of the inclusion of the configuration space in a product. Again, this is valid for any wonderful compactification. The case of configuration spaces can be found in
[
Wat21
, Lemma C.1]
.

Proposition 7.3
.

The closure of the image of the map

Y(\mathbf{C})-\bigcup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}G(\mathbf{C})\to\prod_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{G(\mathbf{C})}(Y(\mathbf{C}))

is isomorphic to
Y^{\mathbf{R}}_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

First, we observe that the map

Y(\mathbf{C})-\bigcup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}G(\mathbf{C})\to Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}

is injective with dense image. This follows from the fact that the target is a manifold with corners and the source is the interior of this manifold.

Now, consider the following commutative diagram of topological spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y(\mathbf{C})-\bigcup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}G(\mathbf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{R}}_{G(\mathbf{C})}(Y(\mathbf{C}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbf{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{Bl}^{\mathbf{C}}_{G(\mathbf{C})}(Y(\mathbf{C}))}

According to
[
BDPW23
, 8.4.3]
, the square is cartesian (indeed, we can view the product
\prod_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\mathrm{Bl}_{G}(Y)
as a log scheme in which we give each factor the log structure of Example
4.3
and then this square is exactly the square of
[
BDPW23
, 8.4.3]
). The bottom horizontal map is a closed inclusion by
[
Li09
, Proposition 2.13]
, it follows that the top horizontal map is also a closed inclusion since the square is cartesian. This concludes the proof.
∎

8.
Galois action

Let
\ell
be a prime number, let
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
be the field of
\ell
-adic number. Let
q
be a power of a prime number
p
different from
\ell
. Let
\mathbf{K}
be a characteristic zero field equipped with an embedding
\mathbf{K}\to\mathbf{C}
. In this case, we may consider the functor

X\mapsto Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge}

from the category of schemes over
\mathbf{K}
to the category of profinite homotopy types. Here
Et
denotes the étale homotopy type functor (also known as the “shape” of the étale site of
X
) and
U\mapsto U^{\wedge}
is the profinite completion functor. By functoriality, the object
Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge}
comes equipped with an action of the group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{K}}/\mathbf{K})
. This induces a Galois action on the cochain complex
C^{*}(Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge},\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n})
and thus also on
C^{*}(Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge},\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})
using the weak equivalence

C^{*}(Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge},\mathbf{Z}_{\ell})\simeq\mathrm{holim}_{n}C^{*}(Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge},\mathbf{Z}/\ell^{n}).

We can lift the Frobenius along the surjective map

\rm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{K}}/\mathbf{K})\twoheadrightarrow\rm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{k}}/\mathbf{k})

and we obtain an automorphism of
\varphi
of
Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge}
. Moreover, Artin’s comparison theorem together with the smooth base change theorem (see
[
AGV+73
, XVI, Theorem 4.1 and XII, Corollary 5.4]
) gives us a weak equivalence of profinite spaces

Et(X\times_{\mathbf{K}}\overline{\mathbf{K}})^{\wedge}\simeq X(\mathbf{C})^{\wedge}

By work of Carchedi-Scherotzke-Sibilla-Talpo, we can extend this to log-schemes and we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\mathbf{K}\subset\mathbf{C}
be a
p
-adic field with residue field
\mathbf{k}
and
X
be a smooth scheme over
\mathbf{K}
. Then the profinite completion of
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))
has an action of the absolute Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{K}}/\mathbf{K})
. In particular, it is equipped with an automorphism
\varphi
lifting the Frobenius in
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{k}}/\mathbf{k})
.

Proof.

There is an action on the profinite étale homotopy type of the infinite root stack of
\mathrm{con}^{log}(X)
. By
[
CSST17
]
this is homotopy equivalent to the profinite completion of the Kato–Nakayama space which is identified with the profinite completion of
\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))
by Theorem
7.2
.
∎

We can extend this action to configuration categories of manifolds that are not necessarily complex algebrac varieties using the additivity result of the first author and Michael Weiss
[
BdBW18b
]
.

Construction 8.2
.

Let
\mathbf{K}\subset\mathbf{C}
be a
p
-adic field and

be a smooth scheme over
\mathbf{K}
and

be a smooth manifold. Then we can consider the derived “box product” of the two configuration categories which is weakly equivalent to the configuration category of the product of manifolds:

\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C})\times Y)\simeq\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))\boxtimes^{L}\mathrm{con}^{AS}(Y)

by the main theorem of
[

]
. We refer the reader to
[

for details about the box product construction. Moreover by
[

(\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))\boxtimes^{L}\mathrm{con}^{AS}(Y))^{\wedge}\simeq\mathrm{con}^{AS}(X(\mathbf{C}))^{\wedge}\boxtimes^{L}\mathrm{con}^{AS}(Y)^{\wedge}.

Using the Galois action on the first factor, we equip the diagram of

-complete spaces

with a Galois action.

Our goal now is to use the Galois action on the

Definition 9.1
.

We say that an element

In the following we use the phrase “Galois representation” for a vector space over
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
equipped with an automorphism
\varphi
. In practice, this automorphism will always come from the generator of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{k}}/\mathbf{k})
which explains the terminology.

Definition 9.2
.

Let

be a
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}
-vector space equipped with an automorphism
\varphi
. We say that
V
is a

if all the eigenvalues of

are Weil numbers of weight
n
.

Example 9.3
.

For

, we denote by
\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}(n)

and with

acting by multiplication by

. This is a Galois representation that is pure of weight

. More generally, for

\mathbf{Q}_{\ell}

\varphi
acting by multiplication by

.

The following is immediate.

.

(i)

Let

is pure of weight
n
if and only if
V^{\prime}
and

are.

(ii)

If
V
and
V^{\prime}
are two Galois representations that are pure of weight

.

Let
X
be a smooth and proper algebraic variety over the complex numbers and assume that
H^{*}(X,\mathbf{Q})
is a Koszul algebra. Then
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X\times\mathbf{R})
is a Koszul space in the sense of Berglund (see
[
Ber14
]
).

Proof.

By the previous corollary this space is formal, so it suffices to show that
H^{*}(\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(X\times\mathbf{R}))
is a Koszul algebra. This follows from
[
Bez94
, Corollary 2.3]
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain a presentation for the Lie algebra of rational homotopy groups using
[
Ber14
, Theorem 3]
. Examples of algebraic varieties
X
satisfying our assumption include all smooth projective curves and all simply connected smooth projective algebraic surfaces.

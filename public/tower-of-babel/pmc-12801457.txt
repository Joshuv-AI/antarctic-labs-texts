HPV genotype distribution and cervical lesion severity in HPV-positive women: a study from the Guizhou Province cervical cancer screening program
Authors: Xingjing Luo, Ying Chen, Lei He, Sihan Liu, Fei Zhang, Bing Liu, Honghong Zhang, Fan Qi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyze the infection status and subtype distribution of high-risk human papillomavirus (hrHPV) and their relationship with cervical lesions among women undergoing cervical cancer screening in Guizhou Province, thereby informing HPV vaccine selection and optimizing cervical cancer screening strategies.MethodsData were sourced from the 2024 Guizhou Province free cervical cancer screening program for women. The prevalence of hrHPV-positive samples was analyzed, and age-specific associations between HPV genotypes and severe lesions were explored. Furthermore, the co-infection propensity for any two HPV genotypes was assessed by calculating the infection rate ratio.ResultsThe overall hrHPV infection rate in the Guizhou region of China was 10.09%. Among hrHPV-positive individuals, the detection rate of cervical lesions was 11.49%. In hrHPV-positive women with cervical lesions, the most common genotypes were HPV16, 52, and 58. HPV16 was predominant across the entire spectrum of cervical lesions, and its prevalence increased significantly with the severity of cervical lesions, from 21.7% in LSIL to 39.4% in HSIL, and to 56.7% in cervical cancer (p < 0.001). However, the primary HPV types leading to cervical cancer were HPV16, 18, and 33. The infection pattern was predominantly single genotype (71.8%), with multiple genotypes accounting for only 28.2%. Among multiple infections, dual infection was the most common. The most frequent mixed genotype combinations were HPV16 + 52, 52 + 58, and 16 + 58. Significant co-infection preferences were observed for HPV16 with 33, HPV16 with 58, and HPV18 with 59. Analysis of the association between HPV genotype risk grouping and age across different cervical lesion grades showed that in the LSIL group, women aged 55–65 had a significantly 24% higher risk of HPV16 infection compared to those aged 35–45 (P = 0.023), while the risk of ‘Other hrHPV’ infection was significantly reduced by 8% (P = 0.019). In the HSIL group, the 45–55 age group had a significantly 11% lower risk of ‘Other hrHPV’ infection compared to the 35–45 age group.ConclusionHPV16 predominates across the entire cervical lesion spectrum, with its prevalence significantly increasing with lesion severity. Furthermore, the nonavalent HPV vaccine covers all prevalent oncogenic hrHPV genotypes in this region (except for HPV56). These findings provide crucial epidemiological evidence for optimizing HPV vaccination strategies and cervical cancer screening programs.Keywords: Cervical cancer, Human papillomavirus, Genotype distribution

Introduction

Cervical cancer is the fourth most common cancer among women worldwide, accounting for 6.8% of global new cases and 8.1% of global deaths. In 2022, approximately 151,000 new cervical cancer cases and 56,000 deaths occurred in China, representing 22.8% and 16.0% of the global totals, respectively. Furthermore, both incidence and mortality have shown an increasing trend over the past two decades [1], indicating that its prevention and control remain a significant public health challenge in China.

Human Papillomavirus (HPV) is a non-enveloped, double-stranded DNA virus with an icosahedral capsid. HPV infects epithelial cells of the skin and mucous membranes in humans. Over 200 genotypes have been identified to date, with at least 14 (including HPV16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, 59, 66, and 68) classified as high-risk Human Papillomaviruses (hrHPV). Persistent hrHPV infection is unequivocally established as the primary cause of cervical precancerous lesions and cervical cancer [2]. Notably, the prevalence and genotype distribution of HPV infection vary across countries and regions [3]. Specifically, although HPV16 and 18 are responsible for approximately 70% of cervical cancers globally [4], the prevalence of other hrHPV types, such as HPV52 and 58, in some Asian regions (e.g., China) is significantly higher than in other parts of the world [5]. Additionally, co-infections with multiple HPV genotypes are common among HPV-positive individuals [6]; however, the distribution patterns of co-infecting genotype combinations and their clinical significance remain unclear. Concurrently, the distribution of HPV genotypes also differs across various cervical lesion grades. For instance, the genotype distribution in high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesions (HSIL) does not fully represent that found in cervical cancer. While other hrHPV types are also frequently detected in high-grade lesions, their associated risks for disease progression vary [7, 8].

The World Health Organization (WHO) states that cervical cancer can be prevented through HPV vaccination and HPV primary screening [9]. On November 10, 2025, China integrated the HPV vaccine into its national immunization program. This program aims to provide two doses of the bivalent HPV vaccine free of charge to 13-year-old girls born after November 10, 2011, with the goal of effectively preventing and controlling cervical cancer [10]. However, the bivalent HPV vaccine only protects against HPV16 and 18 infections. Although studies have confirmed it offers some cross-protection against certain other types (e.g., 31, 33, 45) [11–14], concurrently, the infection rates of genotypes not covered by the vaccine (such as HPV52) have shown an increasing trend [11, 12], constituting a current limitation of the immunization strategy.

Nevertheless, research on the epidemiological distribution of hrHPV genotypes and their association with cervical cancer in the population of Guizhou, China, remains relatively limited. Therefore, systematically understanding the type-specific prevalence and characteristics of HPV in the Guizhou region of China is crucial for guiding HPV vaccine selection and optimizing cervical cancer screening strategies. This study, based on data from 38,531 hrHPV-positive individuals (including 4,429 with cervical lesions) identified through the 2024 Guizhou Province free cervical cancer screening program, systematically analyzes the prevalence, distribution characteristics of hrHPV, and the association between HPV genotypes and severe cervical lesions across different age groups in the Guizhou region of China.

Object and methods

Study participants

In accordance with the Implementation Plan for Cervical and Breast Cancer Screening in Guizhou Province, the study participants comprised all eligible women aged 35 to 64 years who participated in the province-wide free cervical cancer screening program during 2024. All participants provided written informed consent before enrolling in the provincial free cervical cancer screening project.

Data source

The data were obtained from the Guizhou Province “Two Cancers” (Breast and Cervical Cancer) Screening Program. Individual screening case information was reviewed by maternal and child health hospitals across various cities (prefectures) in Guizhou to prevent duplicate screenings. After review, the data were exported from the information system and reported to the provincial maternal and child health hospital for aggregation. The case data encompassed the hrHPV-positive screening population and included specific details such as screening date, screening institution, participant’s residential address, age, basic gynecological examination findings, cytology results, and cervical histopathological examination results. Inclusion criteria: We included all individuals enrolled in the free cervical cancer screening. Exclusion criteria: We excluded cases with unknown hrHPV genotyping, missing age data, or lacking cervical histopathological results. This study, approved by the Hospital Medical Ethics Committee, was conducted under strict confidentiality and anonymity protocols to ensure all data remained secure.

Screening methods

Gynecological Examination. This included inspection of the vulva and vagina, pelvic examination, and wet mount microscopy of vaginal secretions.

hrHPV Testing. All participants underwent primary cervical cancer screening based on hrHPV testing. A qualitative hrHPV DNA test utilizing reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) genotyping was employed. This assay detects the 14 high-risk genotypes mandated by the National Cervical Cancer Screening Protocol. It specifically identifies HPV 16 and HPV 18 individually, while providing pooled detection for the other 12 genotypes (HPV 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, 59, 66, and 68). The reported results allow for the identification of each of the 14 hrHPV genotypes.

Cervical Cytology Testing. Participants who tested positive for hrHPV, specifically those positive for any of the other 12 genotypes (excluding HPV 16/18), underwent liquid-based cytology (LBC).

Colposcopy. Participants positive for one of the other 12 hrHPV genotypes and with abnormal or equivocal cytology results (atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance [ASC-US] or higher) were referred for colposcopy. Participants testing positive for HPV 16 or 18 were referred directly for colposcopy, bypassing cytology.

Cervical Histopathological Examination. Histopathological examination was performed on individuals with abnormal or suspicious colposcopy findings. The findings were categorized as: Low-grade Squamous Intraepithelial Lesion (LSIL), High-grade Squamous Intraepithelial Lesion (HSIL), Adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS), Microinvasive Carcinoma, and Invasive Carcinoma.

Statistical analysis

HPV genotype risk grouping

A sample was defined as genotype-positive if it tested positive for one or more genotypes by any detection technique. In cases of multiple infections, each genotype was counted individually. However, for genotype risk group assignment, a hierarchical rule was applied.

To accurately assess the independent roles of HPV16 and HPV18, the following grouping strategy was employed:

(1) HPV16-positive group: Infections containing HPV16 but not HPV18.(2) HPV18-positive group: Infections containing HPV18 but not HPV16.(3) HPV16/18 co-positive group: Concurrent infections with both HPV16 and HPV18.(4) Other hrHPV group: Infections with other high-risk types, excluding HPV16 and HPV18 (including HPV31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68).

Age and cervical lesion grouping

Participants were categorized into the following age groups: (1) 35–45 years (including 35, excluding 45)(2) 45–55 years (including 45, excluding 55)(3) 55–65 years (including 55, excluding 65).

For cervical lesion categorization, although five histopathological outcomes were initially documented, Adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS, n = 17) and Microinvasive Carcinoma (n = 20) had limited sample sizes. To ensure statistical power, they were merged into the following three groups for the primary analysis:

(1) LSIL group(2) HSIL group: Comprising HSIL and AIS (n = 17)(3) Cervical Cancer group: Comprising Microinvasive Carcinoma and Invasive Carcinoma.

Statistical methods

Exact 95% confidence intervals for prevalence estimates were calculated using the binomial distribution. To assess the association of individual HPV genotype and HPV risk group positivity with age within histological groups, risk ratios were calculated using the 35–45 years group as the reference. The 95% confidence intervals for these ratios were computed using the Wald normal approximation method. Trends in proportions were analyzed using the Chi-square test for trend, while Fisher’s exact test was employed when sample sizes were small. Pairwise comparisons were adjusted using the Bonferroni correction method. Co-infection preference was analyzed by calculating the infection rate ratio (observed co-infection rate/theoretical random co-infection rate). The numerator (observed co-infection rate) was calculated as the number of individuals co-infected with both HPV A and B divided by the total number of screened individuals. The denominator (theoretical random co-infection rate) was the product of the individual infection rates of HPV A and HPV B. All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software, with the significance threshold set at α = 0.05.

Results

Basic characteristics of HrHPV infections

In the 2024 free cervical cancer screening program, which encompassed 384,733 women, the initial hrHPV positivity rate was 10.09% (n = 38,531). Among these positive cases, 4,429 (11.49%) were diagnosed with cervical lesions, specifically 2,682 LSIL, 1,680 HSIL (including 17 AIS), and 67 cervical cancers (comprising 20 microinvasive and 47 invasive carcinomas) (Fig. 1).

The results demonstrated significant age-related variations in HPV infection risks across cervical lesion grades. Additionally, within the cervical cancer group, a non-significant trend toward increased risk of Other hrHPV infection was observed in the 55–65 years age group (RR = 2.10). Discussion

This study found that the overall prevalence of high-risk human papillomavirus (hrHPV) among women aged 35–64 in Guizhou Province in 2024 was 10.09%. This result is lower than the national level (12.1%) [15] and some regional reports, such as Chengdu (11.65%) [16] and previous data from Guizhou Province (13.10%) [17]; however, it is higher than the infection rate reported among a physical examination population in a Chengdu hospital (9.14%) [18]. Significant disparities in hrHPV prevalence exist across different regions of China. The variations between the findings of this study and previous reports may stem from a combination of factors, including regional demographic characteristics, the composition of the screened population, timing of detection, genotyping methods, as well as economic status, lifestyle, and awareness of HPV vaccination [19, 20].

Among hrHPV-positive individuals with cervical lesions in this study, HPV16, 52, 58, 18, and 51 were the most common genotypes. This finding aligns closely with studies from various regions in China (e.g., nationwide [21], Shandong [22], Zunyi in Guizhou [23]), consistently identifying HPV16, 52, and 58 as the predominant genotypes, with only minor differences in their relative rankings. However, an in-depth analysis of lesion-specific distribution revealed that HPV52 and 58 play particularly crucial roles in precancerous lesions. Both genotypes exhibited high infection rates in both LSIL and HSIL, with the prevalence of HPV52 in LSIL surpassing that of HPV16. This indicates that HPV52 and 58 are significant oncogenic drivers in the development of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia [24]. Their potent pathogenicity might be a key reason for their widespread prevalence in the population.

This study confirms that HPV16 and 18 are the two most prevalent hrHPV genotypes causing cervical cancer in the Guizhou region of China. Regardless of their overall prevalence across cervical lesions, HPV16 and 18 consistently emerged as the primary causative types in cervical cancer, followed by HPV33, 56, 31, 52, and 58. Specifically, the prevalence of HPV16 demonstrated a significant increasing trend with the progression of cervical lesions (P for trend < 0.001). Its overall prevalence (30.19%) ranked highest among all hrHPV genotypes, and it dominated in both HSIL (41.1%) and cervical cancer (59.7%). The probability of HPV16 prevalence was 5.06 times and 2.13 times higher in the cervical cancer group compared to the LSIL and HSIL groups, respectively.In contrast to HPV16, the prevalence of HPV18 increased from HSIL (7.1%) to cervical cancer (14.9%), although this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.117). This lack of statistical significance might be related to its stronger association with cervical adenocarcinoma [25]. Supporting this, when we analyzed AIS cases separately, HPV18 showed the highest prevalence in this group, at 41.2%. Concurrently, within the cervical cancer group, the prevalence of HPV18 (14.9%) was second only to HPV16 (56.7%) and significantly higher than all other hrHPV types. These findings indicate that HPV18 itself possesses high oncogenic potential and can efficiently lead to cervical cancer, even if its infection rate does not show a continuous exponential increase. In summary, our results are consistent with the findings of Hao S et al. [26], underscoring the necessity for more active and stringent clinical follow-up and management strategies for individuals testing positive for HPV16/18.

According to our study, implementing high coverage of the bivalent vaccine could reduce HSIL and cervical cancer cases caused by HPV16 and 18 by 48.2% and 74.6%, respectively. However, our data reveal potential limitations, as studies have shown that bivalent and quadrivalent vaccines offer only limited cross-protection against some non-vaccine carcinogenic HPV types [11, 27]. The globally approved nonavalent vaccine protects against HPV types 16, 18, 6, 11, 31, 33, 45, 52, and 58 [28]. With the exception of HPV56, it covers all hrHPV genotypes identified in this study that cause cervical cancer. Therefore, we recommend that future vaccination strategies, considering the local genotype distribution, should prioritize the adoption of the nonavalent vaccine or future vaccines that also cover HPV56 to provide more comprehensive protection for women in this region.

The study found that single-genotype infection was more common among HPV-infected individuals, with multi-genotype infections accounting for only 28.2% of cases. This aligns with previous studies conducted in Chinese populations [29, 30] and other populations [31]. Multiple infections are prevalent across various cervical lesion grades, but their potential impact remains unclear [32, 33]. Schmitt M et al. found that HPV multiple infections do not alter the risk level of lesions [34], while other studies suggest a synergistic effect of multiple HPV infections on cervical carcinogenesis [35].Our results indicate that the proportion of single hrHPV infections was higher in cervical cancer cases compared to the LSIL and HSIL groups, with fewer instances of multiple hrHPV infections observed in cancer cases. Furthermore, among the cases of cervical cancer with multiple hrHPV infections (n = 12), all involved at least one of the HPV genotypes 16, 18, or 33 – the three most prevalent genotypes in cervical cancer in our study. This finding is consistent with results from the Latin American ESTAMPA study [36], suggesting that cervical cancer is typically initiated by a single dominant virus, which diminishes the potential importance of HPV interactions in its pathogenesis [37].

Among dual infections, the most frequent mixed genotype combinations in this study were HPV16 + 52, 52 + 58, and 16 + 58 (all belonging to alpha-9 species), consistent with previous reports on HPV epidemiological characteristics in China [34, 38]. Further co-infection preference analysis revealed that, against a general background of random co-infection patterns, a few phylogenetically related genotype pairs (specifically HPV16 with 33, HPV16 with 58, and HPV18 with 59) exhibited significant preferential co-infection. These results suggest that HPV multiple infections may follow a “selective preference” model. This finding echoes the work of Chaturvedi et al. [32], which reported non-random clustering of specific type combinations within a predominantly random co-infection background.

Previous studies have documented a higher proportion of precancerous lesions and cervical cancer among older women [39, 40]. Our study further revealed that women aged 55–65 with LSIL had a significantly increased risk of HPV16 infection alongside a significantly decreased risk of Other hrHPV infection. This suggests that HPV16 is the dominant viral type driving LSIL in this age group. Similarly, women aged 45–55 in the HSIL group showed a reduced risk of Other hrHPV infection. These findings collectively indicate the existence of age-specific patterns in the relationship between HPV type infection and the progression of cervical lesions.Although no statistically significant associations between any specific HPV type and age were found within the cervical cancer group, the point estimate for the risk of Other hrHPV infection in the 55–65 age group (RR = 2.10) warrants attention. The wide confidence interval (0.67–6.55) indicates that this risk estimate is unstable. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to obtain a more precise estimate.

Strengths and limitations

A key strength of this study lies in its large-scale, population-based design. We included a sample of 384,733 women aged 35–64 eligible for free cervical cancer screening in Guizhou Province in 2024, covering the vast majority of its districts and counties. This ensures broad representativeness of the sample for the target female population in Guizhou. This design enabled a more objective assessment of the current hrHPV prevalence in the region and allowed for a precise analysis of the association between HPV genotypes and severe cervical lesions across different age groups, thereby providing crucial local data for optimizing HPV immunization strategies and cervical cancer screening programs.Conversely, this study has several limitations. The primary limitation is that the study population was restricted to women aged 35–64, excluding younger individuals below 35 and older individuals above 64. This age restriction may prevent a complete understanding of the full spectrum of HPV prevalence across a wider age range and could lead to insufficient statistical power to detect associations between certain genotypes and cervical lesions. Furthermore, as a cross-sectional study, it can reveal associations but cannot establish causality.

Conclusion

The overall hrHPV infection rate in the Guizhou region of China was 10.09%. Among hrHPV-positive individuals, the cervical lesion detection rate was 11.49%, and the distribution of viral genotypes exhibited distinct regional characteristics, indicating that cervical cancer prevention and control strategies need to be tailored to local conditions. This study also found that single-genotype infection was the predominant pattern, with HPV16 dominating across the entire spectrum of cervical lesions. Among multiple infection cases, certain phylogenetically related genotype combinations (e.g., HPV16/33, HPV16/58, HPV18/59) demonstrated significant co-infection tendencies. Furthermore, analysis of the oncogenic hrHPV genotypes revealed that the nonavalent HPV vaccine covers all prevalent major hrHPV genotypes in this region except for HPV56, with particularly high coverage against HPV16, 18, and 33. These findings provide crucial epidemiological evidence for advancing local HPV vaccination strategies and optimizing cervical cancer screening programs.

Global Scientometric Analysis of Research on Bisphenol and Other Endocrine-Disrupting Chemicals in Cardiovascular Disease; Trends, Collaboration Networks, and Thematic Shift
Authors: Fran Espinoza-Carhuancho, Miguel Cabanillas-Lazo, Ivan Calderon, Cesar Mauricio-Vilchez, Lucia Quispe-Tasayco, Frank Mayta-Tovalino
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:To map the global scientific production on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease between 2019 and 2024, based on the publication volume, network of collaboration, citation trends, and theme evolution in order to provide an overview of research dynamics, principal contributors, and emerging areas in this field.Methods:This was a descriptive scientometric study. The criteria for selection were that the article must have been published between January 2019 and December 2024, and that the terms “bisphenol” be in the title or abstract. Scopus literature was then searched on December 24, 2024, via a large search algorithm. 640 documents were flagged and studied using SciVal and Bibliometrix in R Studio 4.5.1, to calculate for a variety of bibliometric indicators, and to perform some advanced bibliometric analysis.Results:Between 2019 and 2024, scientific production worldwide on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease comprised 640 documents published in 402 sources, with an annual growth rate of 6.1%. A total of 3519 authors collaborated, with an average of 6.7 coauthors per document and 18.1% of documents with international coauthorship. Most of overall documents were articles (525), followed by reviews (75), book chapters (16), and conference papers (12). Authors were led by Elisa Cairrao (University of Beira Interior, Portugal) and institutions such as Nanjing Medical University, China. The journal with the highest scholarly output was “Environmental Pollution,” with 15 published. Per Bradford’s Law, the bulk of publications are in a few journals. Lotka’s Law shows that most authors wrote only a single document, and thematic evolution shows trends such, for instance, the stability of “antioxidant activity” and the evolution of “bisphenol A” into themes like “atherosclerosis,” and “cardiovascular disease.”Conclusions:The results showed that the studies were concentrated in some journals, and that specific authors and institutions have contributing significantly to this research topic. Understanding these patterns could help inform new research efforts and policy decisions towards a better management of the effects of bisphenol on health related to cardiovascular diseases.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) comprise a group of diseases affecting the heart and blood vessels, including coronary heart disease, stroke, and heart failure, among others.[1] These pathologies are one of the leading causes of death worldwide, with an increasing prevalence in low- and middle-income countries of >100%.[2] Globally, in the last 20 years, mortality has increased by 6 million, and the number of years of life with disability has doubled.[3] It also produces a significantly greater deterioration in the quality of life of patients and represents a high economic cost for health systems due to the need for prolonged treatment and management of complications.[45]

Cardiovascular diseases are influenced by several risk factors, including modifiable and non-modifiable factors. Non-modifiable factors include age, male sex, and genetic predisposition. However, factors such as hypertension, dyslipidemia, smoking, and unhealthy diet are highly modifiable and contribute significantly to the incidence and progression of CVD.[3] In addition, environmental and chemical exposures, such as exposure to endocrine disrupting substances, are emerging as relevant risk factors.[6]

Bisphenol (particularly bisphenol A, or BPA) is a chemical compound widely used in the manufacture of plastics and epoxy resins. It is commonly found in food packaging, bottles, and everyday consumer products. This compound is known for its ability to act as an endocrine disruptor that interferes with normal hormonal function.[7] Aside from cardiovascular diseases, bisphenol has been reported to induce metabolic diseases such as obesity and diabetes, as well as reproductive diseases, neurological diseases, and cancer, raising its profile as a global public health problem.[8]

Bisphenol affects cardiovascular diseases via several mechanisms. Affected endothelial cell function leads to dysfunction and the potential for atherosclerosis with oxidative stress by generating reactive oxygen species causing chronic inflammation. It alters hormone signaling within the vasculature, particularly estrogen receptor signaling.[9] It has also been associated with changes in blood pressure, autonomic system dysfunction, and alterations in cardiac remodeling, all of which are key contributors to the development and progression of CVD.[10]

This research aims to evaluate the global scientific production on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease through publication volume, collaboration networks, citation trends, and thematic evolution. Using a scientometric approach, we look to identify emergent areas of research, gaps in knowledge, and priority topics to evaluate the role of bisphenol and other endocrine-disrupting chemicals in the cardiovascular pathophysiology, which will serve as help for mitigation strategies and development of evidence-based: public health policies.

Methods

Study design

This study used a descriptive scientometric approach to analyze the global scientific output on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease. The recommendations of the Reporting and Measurement of Items for Bibliometric or Scientometric Studies in Health Sciences (RAMIBS) were followed.[11] The analysis focused on collaborative networks, thematic evolution, and networking within the research domain.

Selection criteria

The inclusion criteria for this review were articles that were published between January 2019 and December 2024, which had “bisphenol” and other endocrine disrupting chemicals in the title or abstract. Articles which did not meet these criteria were published out of the relevant time range or were not pertinent to bisphenol or cardiovascular diseases.

Search strategy

The literature search was conducted on Scopus (SciVal-Elsevier) on December 24, 2024, using the following search formula: TITLE-ABS (“bisphenol” OR “BPA” OR “diphenol” OR “epichlorohydrin” OR “phthalate” OR “nonylphenol” OR “alkylphenol” OR “diphenyl” OR “biphenyl” OR “ethoxylate” OR “dioctyl” OR “diamine”) AND TITLE-ABS (“cardiovascular disease” OR “heart disease” OR “congestive heart failure” OR “heart attack” OR “angina pectoris” OR “coronary” OR “coronary thrombosis” OR “tachycardia” OR “acute myocardial infarction” OR “myocardial infarction” OR “coronary infarction” OR “vascular disease” OR “angina” OR “heart failure” OR “stroke” OR “coronary artery disease”) AND PUBYEAR > 2018 AND PUBYEAR < 2025.

Procedure

A total of 640 Documents retrieved including 525 articles, 75 reviews, 16 book chapters, 12 conference papers, 3 short surveys, 2 retracted, 2 notes, 2, data papers, 1 erratum, 1 book and 1 conference review. We calculated the Collaboration networks, thematic evolution, and Networking. The bibliometric analysis for this study was mainly conducted with two powerful tools: SciVal and Bibliometrix in R Studio. SciVal provided a convenient way to assess various bibliometric indicators including number of publications, citations per publication, field-weighted citation impact, views per publication, and so on. allowing us to quantitatively evaluate the research landscape on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease. In contrast, Bibliometrix is a vast R package for statistical analysis and the visualization of bibliographic data and was used for the implementing the thematic evolution and network analysis through Biblioshiny.

Data analysis

The exploration was carried out in a methodical manner employing a rigorous algorithm to ensure the inclusion of pertinent research. For the analysis, cutting-edge tools like SciVal and Bibliometrix in R Studio version 4.5.1 were utilized, permitting us to explore scientific trends, discover interconnections among researchers, and visualize how the emphasis on this topic has grown over time. The data were analyzed using bibliometric tools to map the networks of collaboration, discover key areas of the science, and visualize changes in the subjects of study through time. The analysis revealed insights into the global landscape of research on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease, including key contributors, influential publications, and burgeoning areas of study. Various bibliometric indicators, including the number of documents, the types of publications, the annual growth rate, the average number of citations per document, and the extent of documents spread across diverse sources, were examined. The period for the extraction of documents was January 2019 to December 2024. All indicators included country of publication, scholarly output of each country (number of documents), citations per publication from this country, field-weighted citation impact, and number of views per publication of this country. These metrics presented a complete view of collaboration, impact of research, and the reach of published works in this topic. Lastly, to examine the thematic evolution we used the thematic mapping feature of Bibliometrix in R Studio with a total of 2,025 author keywords considering that such indicators were normalized and grouped using the co-occurrence algorithm with association strength similarity measure, through that we can identify the research topics, and its evolution along three periods 2019–2021, 2022–2023, 2024.

Results

Between 2019 and 2024, the global scientific literature on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease included 640 documents published across 402 sources, with an annual growth rate of 6.1%. The average age of these documents was 2.3 years, and they received an average of 13.7 citations per document. The study identified 2025 author keywords and involved 3519 authors, with an average of 6.7 coauthors per document and 18.1% international coauthorship. Most of the documents were articles (525), followed by reviews (75), book chapters (16), and conference papers (12) [Table 1].

Discussion

This scientometric analysis illustrates the increasing growth on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease, demonstrating that research developed from a molecular basis to a clinical and environmental realm. The influence of foreign collaboration, the importance of lead authors, and the relevance of the leading countries and institutions for scientific production can be particularly appreciated through the analysis of this data.

The annual average number of articles reported in this study reflects a continuous increase in the interest generated since bisphenol began to be investigated and banned in several countries around the world.[12] In addition, the number of citations per paper averaged 13.7, more than double the 5.5 citations per paper reported in a bibliometric analysis of bisphenol and thyroid hormones.[13] This suggests that interest in the cardiovascular system exceeds that of the thyroid hormone topic.

Elisa Cairrao was the most productive author according to our results, standing out for a recent study on the adverse cardiovascular effects of bisphenol in rats, where she concluded that the cardioelectric system was the most affected.[14] Leonardo Trasande, on the other hand, was the author with the greatest impact, with one of his most cited articles being a US population-based study that found a statistically significant association between bisphenol exposure and mortality.[15] Although the number of articles published by the most prominent authors did not exceed 10, their h-ratios were greater than 20 in most cases, suggesting that experienced researchers are directing their efforts toward this topic. This is in accordance with Lotka’s Law, which states that a small percentage of authors write most of the scientific papers, and that the majority write some articles. Most authors have fewer than five over their careers rather confirming that author productivity follows an inverse power law, that is, few authors are highly productive and have disproportionate weight in this research area.[16]

As for institutions, the most productive is Nanjing Medical University, like a bibliometric analysis on neuroinflammation and postoperative cognitive dysfunction, where this institution also leads production in that field.[17] It may be that Nanjing Medical University is starting to gain foothold on more diversifying topics of scientific interest. However, the institution with the greatest impact is Harvard, with a considerable margin of difference from the others. This leadership is supported by some bibliometric studies on cardiovascular diseases,[1819] including one that analyzes them with environmental exposure,[20] where it is reported that Harvard not only is the leading production institution, but also among the most cited in this research field.

Environmental Pollution was the most productive of our subject. This is coherent with another bibliometric analysis on micro- and nano-plastics, where this journal was one of the most productive.[21] This can be explained by its relevance in those publications related to environment and health, consolidating itself as a reference in these topics. In clear contrast, the journal with the highest impact is Science of the Total Environment, whose most cited article is a maximum exhaustive review on the impact of bisphenol on health diseases. In addition, this has historically remained at the top of the most productive journals without compromising its scientific impact, reflecting its capacity to attract high-quality research.[2223] This seems to suggest that the leaders in this topic stand out not only for the volume of journals, but also for the relevance of their journals and citation of their papers, thus consolidating them as referents in the streaming of research related to exposure and human health.

The thematic evolution allows us to visualize how global scientific research on bisphenol A and cardiovascular disease has changed from 2019 to 2024. In the first 2 years of study (2019–2020), the focus was primarily on topics such as “antioxidants,” “oxidative stress,” and “antioxidant activity.” These overarching topics likely reflect some initial interest in STEM-based research around the basic molecular sites where bisphenol/A effects might be discerned. Specific topics then emerge in 2021, such as “cardiovascular diseases,” “ischemic stroke,” and “polyphenols,” signaling a shift to exploring specific cardiovascular contexts of possible gain or loss accompanied by modulators. In 2022, we see slight further diversification to concepts like “angiogenesis” and “balloon pulmonary angioplasty,” suggesting a shift more toward studies that have possible clinical applications. By 2023, there is an increasing, specific focus on environmental contaminants like “phthalates” and “Polychlorinated Biphenyls (PCB),” as well as experimental models like “zebrafish,” suggesting increasing concern for environmental impact and experimental validation. Finally, in 2024, the key topics are “meta-analysis” and “hypertension”, blooming interest in evidence synthesis and global clinical problems. In general terms, we are seeing evolving research on this context of bisphenol and cardiovascular disease progress from a baseline molecular outlook toward broader interdisciplinary clinical and environmental applications. This fits in well with the objectives of this scientometric study to explore and visualize collaborative networks and ideas of “thematic evolution” in this area.

International production exposes how much this has metamorphosed into a globus cruciger: it should not seem strange that the bisphenol impacts should be studied from the perspective of the whole world, or that the active participation of countries with strong scientific power like the USA and China, and even Japan, should be observed (even countries with little in common, such as France and Kenya or Australia and Greece want “to make new interdisciplinary and multiculturally nuanced knowledge”). This panorama assures us that they are taking so much care of the bisphenol and cardiovascular diseases because they have joined forces and are trying to fractionate themselves thoroughly to spend them again in common, to make leaps and bounds in this.[24]

One limitation of this study is the fact that all publications evaluated are found only within the timeframe 2019–2024, excluding relevant ones not in this timeline or insights from publications being produced later. Also, other regional databases like Scielo and Latindex were not evaluate; can lead to a bias on the totality of this subject worldwide scientific production. In addition, another limitation is that the present analysis of bibliometric records did not study in detail more about the influence of factors such as funding policies, international regulation, barriers to access published research, among many other factors that may affect scientific production/impact and international collaborations. These limitations identify opportunities for more integrative and comprehensive approaches in future studies to capture more broadly the dynamics of scientific production on bisphenol and CVD. Future research may expect to find associations between the relationship for the latter through longitudinal epidemiological studies. Additionally, elucidation of certain biological mechanisms like inflammation, mediators for these pathways, and oxidative stress, will characterize what factors are related to bisphenol A and vascular health. Lastly, increasing collaboration and further explorations regarding predictive and integrated models could be examined, to help mitigate population risk improve public health policy.

Research on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease is an important step in exposing the link between environmental contaminants and human health. Bisphenol is in various plastics (in some cases is detrimental to cardiovascular health through metabolism, oxidative stress, and damaging blood vessels), in almost everything we touch day-to-day. As we wait for more revelations about bisphenol’s role in disease, giving rise to atherosclerosis, heart failure, we see the urgent need for developing research initiatives, to grow this area of work. As we cluster relationships in terms of growth and collaborations, published studies will inform engendering studies, new preventive measures, more restrictive regulations, and innovative clinical functions to reduce exposure, especially to those most at risk.

A more nuanced appreciation of the information may reveal how, even though scientific production on bisphenol and cardiovascular disease is diversifying thematically and progressing, the proportion of papers that include international collaboration is on the low side, and that all the papers published on this subject are bottlenecked to a couple of publishing centers which may bottleneck visibility of the work globally, and as a result, multicenter evidence. The motivation should be to find a way such that practical strategies like proposing international research consortia, specific funding for such collaborative works, adding a premium to collaborative works a priori, so that they are publishable in high impact journals or in open access format, and developing integrated predictive models that connect experimental, clinical, and regulatory evidence will working. Research will thus move out from the academic page cabinets and will feed directly to stricter public policy towards bisphenols and to preemptive steps for cardiovascular health.

The other elements of these results—that critical mass of publications that are concentrated in major journals, that there is a low proportion of international collaboration, and that there has a thematic drift toward clinical and environmental applications—might be the basis for orienting regulations and research agenda items. In regulations, pushing for stricter bisphenol policies goes beyond the emerging evidence of cardiovascular effects. In research, this highlights the need for multicenter consortia, longitudinal studies, and strengthening the lines of research which involve some modelling of molecular mechanisms and relevant clinical/epidemiological evidence to action.

Conclusions

The overarching insights gleaned from this scientometric review shine a spotlight on the rapid expansion and international collaboration in bisphenol and cardiovascular disease research from 2019 to 2024. Our extensive document and literature citation review provides a solid foundation for further investigative and policy research. Key among these findings is that 640 documents were analyzed with a growth rate of 6.1% per year and important contributions were made by Nanjing Medical University and Harvard University among others. Other findings from the thematic evolution aspect were that “antioxidant activity” remained stable in commitment and there was an evolution of “bisphenol A” into other themes like “atherosclerosis” and “cardiovascular disease.” These findings can assist targeted research, steer regulatory decisions and as such, help foster health-protective strategies to lessen the disease burden of bisphenol exposure on cardiovascular diseases. We also recommend in future research to connect the bibliometric trends to toxicological evidence to help better guide global regulatory decisions.

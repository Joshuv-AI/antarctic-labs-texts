Global Solutions to Non-Convex Functional Constrained Problems with Hidden Convexity
Ilyas Fatkhullin 1,2,3, Niao He 2, Guanghui Lan 3, Florian Wolf 1,4

Abstract
Constrained non-convex optimization is fundamentally challenging, as global solutions are generally intractable and constraint qualifications may not hold. However, in many applications, including safe policy optimization in control and reinforcement learning, such problems possess hidden convexity, meaning they can be reformulated as convex programs via a nonlinear invertible transformation. Typically such transformations are implicit or unknown, making the direct link with the convex program impossible. On the other hand, (sub-)gradients with respect to the original variables are often accessible or can be easily estimated, which motivates algorithms that operate directly in the original (non-convex) problem space using standard (sub - )gradient oracles. In this work, we develop the first algorithms to provably solve such non-convex problems to global minima. First, using a modified inexact proximal point method, we establish global last-iterate convergence guarantees with 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ε − 3 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-3}) oracle complexity in non-smooth setting. For smooth problems, we propose a new bundle-level type method based on linearly constrained quadratic subproblems, improving the oracle complexity to 𝒪 ~ ​ ( ε − 1 ) \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-1}) . Surprisingly, despite non-convexity, our methodology does not require any constraint qualifications, can handle hidden convex equality constraints, and achieves complexities matching those for solving unconstrained hidden convex optimization.

Constrained Proximal Point Approach.

We propose a modified Inexact Proximal Point Method (IPPM) with shifted constraints under hidden convexity.
The key technical novelty of our convergence analysis lies in showing that our IPPM subproblems always satisfy
Slater’s condition without imposing any CQs on the original problem. In our analysis, a Slater point
is explicitly constructed using the variable transformation

Using this key insight, we apply existing
algorithms for strongly convex constrained optimization to approximate each IPPM subproblem using the standard
(sub-)gradient oracle access. In particular, in the non-smooth setting, we use the

2.1

We start our analysis by proving elementary properties of constrained

Definition 2

2.2
Non-convex Equality Constraints

Throughout the paper, our main focus is hidden convex inequality
constraints. However, many applications involve non-convex equality type
constraints, which are known to be more challenging to handle than
inequality constraints. We show that our results can be extended to hidden
linear equality type constraints. In particular, for a given matrix
A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times d}
, consider the problem

\displaystyle\min_{{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}}\;F_{1}({x})\coloneqq H_{1}(c({x})),\quad\text{s.t. }F_{2,\text{eq}}({x})\coloneqq A\,c({x})=0.

(HC-Eq)

The equality constraint of this type can be written in the form of inequality constraint by increasing the number of constraints, i.e.,

F_{2,\text{eq}}({x})=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F_{2,\text{eq}}({x})\leq 0,\quad\text{and}\quad-F_{2,\text{eq}}({x})\leq 0.

Here,
F_{2,\text{eq}}
is vector valued when
n>1
, and the equality and inequality constraints should be understood
component-wise, i.e.,
F_{2,\text{eq}}^{i}({x})=0
for all
i\in[n]
. Moreover,
one can combine these constraints into a single one and directly reduce the problem to our original form
(
1
) with a single constraint:

F_{2,\text{eq}}({x})=0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad F_{2}({x})\coloneqq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max\{F_{2,\text{eq}}^{i}({x}),-F_{2,\text{eq}}^{i}({x})\}\leq 0.

It is important to note that in this formulation, Slater’s condition typically fails and the
\max
operators introduce non-smoothness
even when the map
c(\,\cdot\,{})
is sufficiently smooth.

2.3

A natural question is whether the assumption about the common
transformation

Proof

We reduce from

Convex Constrained Markov Decision Process (CCMPD)

ZKB+ 20
.

Convex reinforcement learning (RL) under convex constraints generalizes
the classical (constrained) RL setting. Based on a discounted constrained
Markov Decision Process
(CMDP) of the form
\mathcal{M}({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{P}},\mu_{0},\gamma,r,c)
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
and

{\mathcal{A}}
denote the (finite) state and action spaces respectively,

{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to\Delta({\mathcal{S}})
represents the state-action transition
probability kernel,
\Delta({\mathcal{S}})

is the probability simplex
over
{\mathcal{S}}
,
\mu_{0}
is the initial state distribution and
\gamma\in(0,1)

is the discount factor. Based on the reward
r:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{R}}

and the penalty cost
c:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, the classical RL problem is formulated in
finding an optimal stationary policy
\pi:\Delta({\mathcal{A}})^{\left|{\mathcal{S}}\right|}\to\Delta({\mathcal{A}})
by maximizing
the reward function while satisfying the constraint on the cost function.
With the notation of
X\sim\rho
for a random variable
X
following a
probability distribution
\rho
, as well as
{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\,\cdot\,{}\right]
and

{\mathbb{P}\!\left(\,\cdot\,{}\right)}
for the expectation and probability, respectively,
we formally define

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \min_{\pi\in\Pi}\;F_{1}(\pi)&\coloneqq-{\mathbb{E}}_{{s}_{0}\sim\mu_{0},\pi}\!\left[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{h}r({s}_{h},{a}_{h})\right],\\
\text{s.t.}\;F_{2}(\pi)&\coloneqq{\mathbb{E}}_{{s}_{0}\sim\mu_{0},\pi}\!\left[\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{h}c({s}_{h},{a}_{h})\right]\leq 0,\end{aligned}

(CMDP)

where
\Pi\coloneqq\Delta({\mathcal{A}})^{|{\mathcal{S}}|}
is the set of all stationary policies.
This set is the product of simplices, which admits an efficient projection. Note that, for consistency with our
formulation (
1
), we minimize the negative expected reward,
which is equivalent to maximizing the expected reward in standard RL formulations.

Given a policy
\pi
, at each time
h\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the agent is in a state

{s}_{h}
and chooses an action
{a}_{h}\sim\pi(\,\cdot\,{}|{s}_{h})
,
resulting in a transition
{s}_{h+1}\sim{\mathbb{P}}(\,\cdot\,{}|{s}_{h},{a}_{h})

we denote the induced probability
distribution of the Markov chain
({s}_{h},{a}_{h})_{h\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with an
initial state distribution
\mu_{0}
. Under a transformation via the

state-action occupancy measure

SB 18
, defined by

\displaystyle\lambda^{\pi}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\to(0,1),\;\lambda^{\pi}({s},{a})\coloneqq\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{h}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mu_{0},\pi}({s}_{h}={s},{a}_{h}={a}),

(4)

the classical constrained RL problem becomes linear
in the objective and the constraint, i.e. (

)
is equivalent to

\max_{\lambda\in{\mathcal{U}}}\langle r,\lambda^{\pi}\rangle

s.t.
\langle c,\lambda^{\pi}\rangle\leq 0
,
where
{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq\{\lambda^{\pi}\;|\;\pi\in\Pi\}
.
Convex constrained RL generalizes this optimization problem to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \min_{\pi\in\Pi}\;&F_{1}(\pi)\coloneqq H_{1}(\lambda^{\pi}),\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\;&F_{2}(\pi)\coloneqq H_{2}(\lambda^{\pi})\leq 0,\end{aligned}

(CCMDP)

where the utility functions
H_{1},H_{2}:{\mathcal{U}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
are convex functions in

,
but the resulting optimization problem over the policy space

is non-convex. Beyond the standard
(

i.e.

is the negative entropy,
under safety constraints, e.g. staying close to an experts trajectory

;

;

The problem (

). As shown in

Controller Synthesis in Optimal Control.

Given a continuous-time linear time-invariant (LTI) system

\dot{x}(t)=A\,x(t)+B\,u(t),\qquad x(0)=x_{0},

(LTI)

where
x(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a state vector,
u(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
a control input vector, and

is an initial
state generated as a random variable with covariance matrix

,
where
{\mathcal{S}}_{+}^{n}
denotes the set of symmetric
positive semidefinite
n\times n
matrices.
With static state feedback
u(t)=K\,x(t),

K\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}
, the classical linear quadratic regulator
(LQR) problem reads

;
FP 21
:

\displaystyle\min_{K\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m\times n}}J_{\text{LQR}}(K)\coloneqq{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(x^{\top}(t)Qx(t)+u^{\top}(t)Ru(t)\right)\>\mathrm{d}t\right]

(LQR)

\displaystyle\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\eqref{eq:LTI},\quad u(t)=K\,x(t),\quad A+BK\text{ is Hurwitz stable,}

where
Q\succeq 0
,

, and Hurwitz stable means that all real parts of eigenvalues of

are negative.
It is known that (

) admits an optimal solution
K^{*}

(that is unique when

), which is also optimal for any square integrable control laws

under mild assumptions.

A direct gradient-based optimization of

over

is

such that

particularly in large-scale or poorly conditioned systems. To circumvent

constraints that implicitly enforce stability and allow the user an
arbitrary initialization of

.

Specifically, consider the nonlinear equality-constrained problem

\displaystyle\min_{{x}\coloneqq(P,K)}F_{1}({x})\coloneqq\operatorname{Tr}\left((Q+K^{\top}RK)P\right)

(NL-Eq-LQR)

\displaystyle\quad{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}\coloneqq\left\{(P,K)\mid P\succ 0,P=P^{\top}\right\},

\displaystyle\quad\text{s.t.}\quad F_{2,\text{eq}}(x)\coloneqq(A+BK)P+P(A+BK)^{\top}+W=0.

Above formulation is equivalent to (

, avoiding the need for stabilizing initialization.
Although the strong duality holds under mild assumptions

, Thm. 1
,
the presence of the equality constraint implies that Slater’s condition does not hold for this problem.

Nevertheless, a convex reformulation of (

{u}\coloneqq(P,Y)\coloneqq c((P,K))\coloneqq\left(P,(K-K^{*})P\right),

is the optimal

The inverse of

is given by

which admits a Jacobian of the form

D(c^{-1})_{(P,Y)}[\Delta P,\Delta Y]=\left(\Delta P,\Delta YP^{-1}-YP^{-1}\Delta PP^{-1}\right)^{\top}

defined in perturbation notation in order to avoid
tensors. For the paired spectral norm of the Jacobian we have

\displaystyle\lVert D(c^{-1})_{(P,Y)}[\Delta P,\Delta Y]\rVert

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\sqrt{\lVert\Delta P\rVert^{2}+\Big(\lVert\Delta Y\rVert\cdot\lVert P^{-1}\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert\cdot\lVert P^{-1}\rVert^{2}\cdot\lVert\Delta P\rVert\Big)^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\sqrt{\,1+\lVert P^{-1}\rVert^{2}+\lVert Y\rVert^{2}\,\lVert P^{-1}\rVert^{4}\,}\cdot\lVert(\Delta P,\Delta Y)\rVert.

Let us assume that the variable
P
is bounded away from zero with a sufficiently small constant, i.e.,

and the variable

is bounded. Then we can bound the operator norm by

\lVert D(c^{-1})_{(P,Y)}\rVert\leq\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\tfrac{\lVert Y\rVert^{2}}{\delta^{4}}}=\sqrt{\,1+\tfrac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\tfrac{\lVert(K-K^{*})\cdot P\rVert^{2}}{\delta^{4}}},

showing that

We define

then (

\displaystyle\min_{{u}:=(P,Y)}H_{1}(u)\coloneqq\operatorname{Tr}\left((Q^{*}P+P^{-1}Y^{\top}RY+(K^{*})^{\top}RY+Y^{\top}RK^{*}\right)

(L-Eq-LQR)

\displaystyle\quad{u}\in{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq\left\{(P,Y)\mid P\succ 0,P=P^{\top}\right\},

\displaystyle\quad\text{s.t.}\quad H_{2,\text{eq}}({u})\coloneqq A^{*}P+BY+(A^{*}P+BY)^{\top}+W=0.

Notice that the objective is convex and the constraint is linear
in the new variables. Although such reformulation exists, the

depends on the unknown

We note that (

(

Bundle-level Approach

As established in the previous section, the inexact proximal point approach
(IPPM) yields oracle complexities matching those for unconstrained hidden
convex optimization
FHH 24
. In
the smooth setting, however, our approach either relies on Slater’s
condition (and results in
{\OOTildeSym\!\left(\theta^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1}\right)}

complexity) or incurs a suboptimal
{\OOTildeSym\!\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)}
oracle
complexity in terms of accuracy
\varepsilon
. The former bound is not
satisfactory since it scales with the inverse of the Slater gap
\theta
, a
quantity that may be arbitrarily close to zero in practice. This limitation
appears inherent to the IPPM approach, as our proof technique requires each
subproblem to satisfy Slater’s condition. Obtaining the

{\OOTildeSym\!\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right)}
rate (matching the unconstrained setting)
without Slater’s condition would require an algorithm for smooth, strongly
convex, constrained optimization with
{\OOTildeSym\!\left(\sqrt{\kappa}\right)}
complexity
independent of dual variable bounds. Such an algorithm is not known, and to
the best of our knowledge, its existence remains an open problem.

In this section, we aim to achieve fast
{\OOTildeSym\!\left(\varepsilon^{-1}\right)}

gradient complexity for hidden convex problems without Slater’s condition.
To do so, we take a different approach based on the cutting
plane/bundle-level idea
LNN95a
;
Lan 15
. First, we
want to highlight that the existing theory of bundle-level type methods is
predominantly limited to the convex setting. This is because the core idea
of this approach is to build a global lower model of the objective using
linear or piece-wise linear approximations. While such philosophy is
powerful for convex programming, it fails even on simple non-convex
problems. Assume we know the optimal value (level)
{F_{1}^{*}}
and consider a
variant of bundle-level (BL) method from
DB 24
, which
has an update rule:

\displaystyle{x}^{(t+1)}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{x}\in{\mathcal{X}}}\,\lVert{x}-{x}^{(t)}\rVert^{2},

(StarBL)

\displaystyle F_{1}({x}^{(t)})+\langle\nabla F_{1}({x}^{(t)}),{x}^{(t)}-{x}\rangle\leq{F_{1}^{*}},

\displaystyle F_{2}({x}^{(t)})+\langle\nabla F_{2}({x}^{(t)}),{x}^{(t)}-{x}\rangle\leq 0.

as the projection of the
current iterate

to the intersection of two linear constraints. We
refer to this algorithm (

, and above established feasibility of
{x}^{(t)}_{\alpha}
, we obtain

\lVert{x}^{(t+1)}-{x}^{(t)}\rVert\leq\lVert{x}^{(t)}_{\alpha}-{x}^{(t)}\rVert\leq\frac{\alpha{\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{\mu_{c}}.

Now assume we are in the “
Bad case
”, then by construction of

\eta_{t+1}
we have

\displaystyle\eta_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\left(\eta_{k}+F_{1}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})+\lambda[F_{2}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})]_{+}\right)-(1-2\beta)\eta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\left(\eta_{k}+F_{1}({x}^{(\bar{t})})+\lambda[F_{2}({x}^{(\bar{t})})]_{+}\right)-(1-2\beta)\eta_{k}<{F_{1}^{*}},

where the inequality holds since
\bar{{x}}^{(k)}
satisfies
F_{1}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})+\lambda[F_{2}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})]_{+}\leq F_{1}({x}^{(\bar{t})})+\lambda[F_{2}({x}^{(\bar{t})})]_{+}
due to the output criterion of
Algorithm

4
. By strong duality, we have for any

, that
F_{1}(x)\geq{F_{1}^{*}}-\lambda^{*}[F_{2}(x)]_{+}.
Therefore,

\displaystyle{F_{1}^{*}}-\eta_{k+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{F_{1}^{*}}-(1-\beta)\left(\eta_{k}+F_{1}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})+\lambda[F_{2}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})]_{+}\right)+(1-2\beta)\eta_{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{F_{1}^{*}}-(1-\beta)\left(\eta_{k}+{F_{1}^{*}}+(\lambda-\lambda^{*})[F_{2}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})]_{+}\right)+(1-2\beta)\eta_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta({F_{1}^{*}}-\eta_{k})+(1-\beta)(\lambda^{*}-\lambda)[F_{2}(\bar{{x}}^{(k)})]_{+}

concluding the proof.

\Box

4
and
5
. We

{F_{1}^{*}}
and when we do not know it.

4.1
Convergence with Known
{F_{1}^{*}}

In this case we can set

\eta={F_{1}^{*}}
,
\beta=1
, arbitrary

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
we do not need the

“line-search”

Under this choice of

”
of

,
and we have the

Theorem 4.1
(S-StarBL, Known
{F_{1}^{*}}
, No CQ)

Assume that (
HC
) is hidden convex, Assumption
1.1
holds and that

F_{1},F_{2}
are
L
-smooth. Set
\tau=\frac{\rho\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}},

\alpha=\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{c}^{2}}{(\rho+L){\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}},
then
the last iterate of (
S-StarBL
) satisfies

F_{1}({x}^{(T)})-{F_{1}^{*}}\leq{\varepsilon},\,F_{2}({x}^{(T)})\leq{\varepsilon}

after

\displaystyle{T_{\mathrm{tot}}}\geq\frac{(\rho+L){\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{\mu_{c}^{2}\,{\varepsilon}}\cdot\log\left(\frac{2v({x}^{(0)},{F_{1}^{*}})}{{\varepsilon}}\right).

Proof

The choice of
\eta
,
\beta
and
\lambda
implies that for any
t\geq 0

\left(1-\alpha\,\beta\right)F_{1}({x}^{(t)})+\alpha\,\beta\,\eta+(1-\beta)\alpha\lambda[F_{2}({x}^{(t)})]_{+}+\tau=\left(1-\alpha\right)F_{1}({x}^{(t)})+\alpha\,{F_{1}^{*}}+\tau.

Therefore, by smoothness of
F_{1}
we obtain

\displaystyle F_{1}({x}^{(t+1)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle F_{1}({x}^{(t)})+\langle\nabla F_{1}({x}^{(t)}),{x}^{(t+1)}-{x}^{(t)}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\lVert{x}^{(t+1)}-{x}^{(t)}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell_{F_{1}}({x}^{(t+1)},{x}^{(t)})+\frac{L}{2}\lVert{x}^{(t+1)}-{x}^{(t)}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{F_{1}^{*}}+(1-\alpha)(F_{1}({x}^{(t)})-{F_{1}^{*}})+\tau+\frac{L}{2}\lVert{x}^{(t+1)}-{x}^{(t)}\rVert^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the update rule of
Algorithm

4
due to feasibility of
{x}^{(t+1)}
for (
SBL-QP
). Subtracting
{F_{1}^{*}}
from both sides and noticing that the choice of
\eta,

\beta
and
\lambda

” of
Lemma

\tau\geq\frac{\rho\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}}

the recursion

\displaystyle F_{1}({x}^{(t+1)})-{F_{1}^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)(F_{1}({x}^{(t)})-{F_{1}^{*}})+\tau+\frac{L\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}}.

An analogous derivation for
F_{2}
results in

\displaystyle F_{2}({x}^{(t+1)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)F_{2}({x}^{(t)})+\tau+\frac{L\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}}.

Combining the above two inequalities, we can establish a recursion for the value function

\displaystyle v({x}^{(t+1)},{F_{1}^{*}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\left\{F_{1}({x}^{(t+1)})-{F_{1}^{*}},F_{2}({x}^{(t+1)})\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\max\left\{F_{1}({x}^{(t)})-{F_{1}^{*}},F_{2}({x}^{(t)})\right\}+\tau+\frac{L\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)v({x}^{(t)},{F_{1}^{*}})+\frac{(\rho+L)\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}},

where in the last step we used the definition of the value function and set
\tau=\frac{\rho\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}}.

Then by unrolling the recursion for
t=0,\ldots,T-1
, we have for any

\alpha\in[0,1]

\displaystyle v({x}^{(T)},{F_{1}^{*}})\leq(1-\alpha)^{T}v({x}^{(0)},{F_{1}^{*}})+\frac{(\rho+L)\alpha{\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}}{2\mu_{c}^{2}}.

Setting
\alpha=\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{c}^{2}}{(\rho+L){\mathcal{D}^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}}},
we obtain the desired result.

\Box

6
Conclusion

In this work, we study a class of non-convex constrained problems under
hidden convexity, proposing the first efficient algorithmic solutions under
minimal assumptions. Several questions arise from our work:

•

While we established a strong baseline for hidden convex constrained
optimization in terms of oracle complexity, our algorithms are fairly
complicated. It would be interesting to develop simple single-loop methods
without additional quadratic linearly constrained subproblems, e.g.,
similar to

Pol 67
;
HL 23
.

•

Our oracle complexities match the best-known complexity in unconstrained
setting; however, we still do not know what are the minimax optimal
gradient complexities of hidden convex optimization.

•

While we follow a common strategy of quadratic regularization in our PPM
framework, an exploration of other natural choices is an interesting
direction for future work. For example, it is conceptually possible to
regularize the iterates in the
{\mathcal{U}}
-space and leverage hidden convexity.
While in general such approaches require knowledge of transformation

c(\,\cdot\,{})

Reinforcement learning based multi objective task scheduling for energy efficient and cost effective cloud edge computing
Authors: Wenfan Zhang, Haijiao Ou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The rapid proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices and latency-sensitive applications has amplified the need for efficient task scheduling in hybrid cloud-edge environments. Traditional heuristic and metaheuristic algorithms often fall short in addressing the dynamic nature of workloads and the conflicting objectives of performance, energy efficiency, and cost-effectiveness. To overcome these challenges, this study introduces Reinforcement Learning-Based Multi-Objective Task Scheduling (RL-MOTS), a framework leveraging Deep Q-Networks (DQNs) for intelligent and adaptive resource allocation. The proposed model formulates scheduling as a Markov Decision Process, incorporating a priority-aware dynamic queueing mechanism and a multi-objective reward function that balances task latency, energy consumption, and operational costs. Additionally, the framework employs a state-reward tensor to capture trade-offs among objectives, enabling real-time decision-making across heterogeneous cloud and edge nodes. Comprehensive simulations using CloudSim validate the robustness of RL-MOTS under varying workload conditions. Compared to baseline strategies such as FCFS, Min-Min, and multi-objective heuristic models, RL-MOTS achieves up to 28% reduction in energy consumption, 20% improvement in cost efficiency, and significant reductions in makespan and deadline violations, while maintaining strict Quality of Service (QoS) requirements. The framework’s adaptability to preemptive and non-preemptive scheduling further enhances its resilience and scalability. These findings establish RL-MOTS as a forward-looking solution for sustainable, cost-efficient, and performance-oriented computing in next-generation distributed systems. Future research will focus on integrating transfer learning and federated learning to increase scalability and privacy in large, decentralized environments, including those applicable to the medical industry.

Introduction

Cloud-edge computing has evolved as a disruptive paradigm, meeting the increasing demand for low-latency, high-efficiency processing in distributed systems propelled by the expansion of Internet of Things (IoT) devices, 5G networks, and data-intensive applications1,2. This hybrid architecture integrates resource-constrained edge nodes with high-capacity cloud virtual machines (VMs)3, facilitating real-time data processing near the source while utilizing the computing capabilities of centralized cloud infrastructure4,5. The dynamic and varied characteristics of cloud-edge settings present considerable problems for task scheduling, necessitating a careful equilibrium of performance, energy efficiency, and operational expenses6. Conventional scheduling techniques, including heuristic methods like First-Come-First-Serve (FCFS) and Min-Min, frequently encounter difficulties in accommodating variable workloads, heterogeneous resource capabilities, and rigorous QoS standards7. This requires creative scheduling algorithms that can dynamically optimize various objectives in real-time, ensuring scalability and responsiveness in next-generation distributed systems8.

Historically, job scheduling in distributed computing has progressed from static, rule-based systems to more adaptable methodologies9,10. Initial methodologies, including Round-Robin and Shortest Job First, emphasized simplicity but exhibited insufficient adaptability to manage the intricacies of contemporary cloud-edge ecosystems11. The emergence of metaheuristic techniques, such as genetic algorithms and particle swarm optimization, enhanced multi-objective optimization, albeit frequently accompanied by significant computational demands and restricted adaptability to real-time variations12,13. Recently, machine learning methodologies, especially reinforcement learning (RL), have gained prominence due to their capacity to derive optimal policies through engagement with dynamic environments14. Deep Q-Networks (DQNs), a category of reinforcement learning, have demonstrated efficacy in resolving intricate decision-making challenges by estimating Q-values for extensive state-action spaces15. Notwithstanding these advancements16, current RL-based scheduling frameworks frequently prioritize single-objective optimization or inadequately tackle the decentralized and heterogeneous characteristics of cloud-edge systems, hence presenting opportunities for additional innovation17.

The importance of effective job scheduling in cloud-edge computing is paramount. The rapid proliferation of IoT devices expected to surpass 75 billion by 2025 has resulted in edge nodes being increasingly responsible for managing time-sensitive tasks, including real-time analytics for autonomous vehicles and smart cities18. Concurrently, cloud resources must manage computationally demanding activities while reducing operational expenses and energy consumption, a vital issue considering that data centers represent roughly 1% of worldwide electricity consumption19. Efficient scheduling guarantees minimal latency execution, compliance with Service Level Agreements (SLAs), and resource utilization that matches with sustainability objectives20. Nonetheless, attaining these objectives is complicated by various challenges, including the decentralized architecture of cloud-edge systems, which obstructs global coordination; the heterogeneity of resources, spanning from low-power edge devices to high-performance cloud virtual machines; and the necessity to reconcile conflicting objectives, such as minimizing energy consumption while adhering to stringent deadlines.

This work is motivated by the inadequacies of current scheduling methods in meeting the complex requirements of cloud-edge computing. Heuristic and metaheuristic approaches frequently depend on static rules or predetermined priorities, which are inadequate for dynamic workloads characterized by unpredictable variations in task arrivals, resource availability, and network conditions. Furthermore, conventional reinforcement learning methods may insufficiently address multi-objective optimization, especially in contexts where energy efficiency, cost, and performance must be concurrently emphasized.

Recent advancements in multi-objective reinforcement learning for scheduling have yielded significant frameworks, including hierarchical RL approaches24,27 that segment scheduling into tiered decision-making, and attention-based DRL models28 that elucidate intricate task-resource relationships via adaptive feature weighting. Although these strategies improve scalability and representational capacity, they demonstrate certain constraints in hybrid cloud-edge contexts. Hierarchical reinforcement learning frequently experiences heightened training complexity and challenges in reconciling conflicting objectives at various levels, while attention-based deep reinforcement learning frameworks, despite their efficacy in feature extraction, may disproportionately focus on local correlations without adequately representing multi-objective trade-offs such as cost, latency, and energy consumption. Related works

Multi-access edge computing (MEC) enhances service proximity to user devices and reduces the burden on resource-limited nodes. This fosters low-latency execution but raises the complex challenge of multi-objective resource scheduling. Prior works have explored a wide variety of approaches, ranging from heuristics to deep reinforcement learning (DRL), yet several gaps remain that motivate the design of RL-MOTS.

In21, a survey highlighted the increasing role of DRL methods in MEC scheduling but also noted that many frameworks lack mechanisms for dynamic adaptation under fluctuating workloads. A multi-objective A3C-based scheduler was proposed in22, optimizing response time and energy consumption; however, it required fixed weight tuning across objectives, limiting flexibility under dynamic conditions. Similarly23, introduced MODRL with three DQN agents for fog–cloud scheduling. While effective in handling node load, distance, and priority, the model became computationally heavy due to parallel agent training and lacked preemption support.

To address energy and delay trade-offs24, proposed a hierarchical parameter sharing (HPS) multi-agent RL approach. Although the hierarchical design improved scalability, it introduced training instability and difficulty in reconciling conflicting objectives at different layers.

More recent studies expand the scope of scheduling in emerging domains. For example25, investigated UAV deployment and edge association for energy-efficient federated learning. The work emphasized joint communication–energy optimization but did not consider task-level preemptive scheduling. Reinforcement learning was also used in26 to intervene in emotion contagion on social networks, underscoring the adaptability of RL but in a context far removed from latency-sensitive task scheduling. In27, a fuzzy multi-objective model was proposed for aircraft flight scheduling. While this framework illustrated the balancing of competing objectives, it assumed static task structures unlike highly dynamic cloud-edge environments.

Energy-aware scheduling with carbon constraints was studied in28, which introduced collaborative optimization for integrated energy systems. Although valuable for sustainability goals, the model relied on centralized optimization, making it unsuitable for decentralized edge environments. From an RL scalability perspective29, tackled multi-agent routing using advanced RL. The work showed strategies to overcome state-space explosion but did not address multi-objective trade-offs like cost or deadline adherence.

Recent system-level innovations include30, which applied blockchain to AR offloading in UAV-enabled MEC networks. While it explored energy–latency trade-offs, the additional blockchain overhead raises concerns about real-time responsiveness. In31, a decomposition-based scheduling method was introduced for production workshops, demonstrating layered optimization but without considering dynamic weights or online adaptation.

Beyond these, workflow-oriented RL scheduling has also been studied. For instance32, proposed a hybrid actor-critic framework with PPO for workflow scheduling, achieving high accuracy but suffering from longer convergence times. Cloud–edge collaborative manufacturing scheduling was discussed in33, where attention-based DRL improved state representation but overemphasized local correlations at the expense of global trade-offs. Finally18, provided a taxonomy of DRL scheduling approaches in cloud-edge systems, noting that despite progress, most models lack integration of preemptive mechanisms, dynamic weight adjustment, and scalability across heterogeneous environments.

The aforementioned works illustrate the ongoing advancement in deep learning-driven multi-objective scheduling. Nonetheless, they possess significant deficiencies: (i) Most frameworks depend on static weights or fixed goal preferences, (ii) few consider proactive scheduling for urgent tasks, (iii) scalability in extensive state-action spaces has been only partially addressed, and (iv) practical deployment challenges, such as heterogeneous energy pricing, dynamic workloads, and real-time network conditions, are frequently overlooked. RL-MOTS mitigates these deficiencies by integrating dynamic weight adjustments, a state-reward tensor that equilibrates latency, energy, and cost, and explicit facilitation for proactive and non-preemptive scheduling inside hybrid cloud-edge systems.

Proposed task scheduling model

Definition of the problem

This section presents RL-MOTS, a scheduling system designed for heterogeneous cloud-edge situations. Figure 1 illustrates the comprehensive architecture. The growing complexity and dynamic characteristics of these environments require a decision-making system capable of optimizing numerous conflicting objectives concurrently. Consequently, RL-MOTS is designed to simultaneously improve energy efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and job priority, which are essential and frequently conflicting elements in extensive distributed systems. To attain these objectives, the system employs a priority-sensitive queue management strategy that utilizes adaptive queue structures alongside virtual waiting time matrices. These matrices change dynamically according to system status and task characteristics, allowing the scheduling agent to make context-sensitive judgments on task execution.

This mechanism guarantees that tasks are executed according to their computational needs, urgency levels, and resource availability, thereby improving responsiveness and equity. Task scheduling in cloud-edge infrastructures involves the immediate assignment of incoming tasks to a variety of computing units, comprising resource-limited edge nodes and high-capacity cloud virtual machines. This process must account for various dynamic factors, including fluctuating workloads, diverse resource capabilities, and changing network conditions.

A significant challenge arises from the decentralized architecture of cloud-edge ecosystems, complicating global coordination and the consistent delivery of performance. Furthermore, the scheduling method must guarantee compliance with rigorous QoS requirements by minimizing execution latency and preventing deadline breaches. These requirements underscore the need for a learning-oriented adaptive policy that evolves over time, responds effectively to environmental changes, and facilitates scalable decision-making. Figure 2 illustrates the scheduling architecture based on Deep Q-Network (DQN). The state extractor initially processes the task queue and monitoring data, producing a proposed state-reward tensor that includes layers for waiting time, energy, and cost. This tensor is input into a deep Q-network (DQN), where the reward feedback from the performance evaluator is also aligned with the tensored objectives. The DQN generates Q values that inform the action selector and facilitate task allocation to edge or cloud resources.

e. Task queue administration.

The task queue is administered via a hybrid methodology that integrates a principal First-In-First-Out (FIFO) framework with reinforcement learning-driven prioritization. Tasks entering the system are initially placed in the FIFO queue, maintaining their sequence of arrival36,37. The DQN agent, employing the State-Reward Tensor, adaptively prioritizes tasks according to the multi-objective reward function according to the formula:8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{t}=\:{w}_{1}\cdot\:\left.\left(-{\sum\:}_{i=1}^{m}{W}_{T}\left({T}_{i}\right)\right.\right)+\:{w}_{2}\cdot\:\left.\left(-{\sum\:}_{i=1}^{m}{E}_{C}\left({T}_{i}\right)\right.\right)+\:{w}_{3}\cdot\:\left.\left(-{\sum\:}_{i=1}^{m}{C}_{F}\left({T}_{i}\right)\right.\right)$$\end{document}

This adaptive prioritization obviates the necessity for static priority queues, such as Fibonacci heaps, and is congruent with the dispersed characteristics of cloud-edge systems. The reward function equilibrates three objectives: minimizing wait time, decreasing energy use, and reducing costs. The variable weights guarantee that no individual statistic prevails, enabling the scheduler to adjust to fluctuating workloads.

f. Virtual FIFO queue for preemption.

A virtual FIFO queue is linked to each resource (edge or cloud) to temporarily retain preempted jobs, facilitating preemptive scheduling. When a higher-priority task, as identified by the DQN agent, interrupts a current task, the interrupted work is placed in the virtual FIFO queue of the corresponding resource. The agent frequently evaluates the system state using the State-Reward Tensor to determine whether to restart the interrupted task or assign a new one, so assuring effective resource use and compliance with deadlines.

Representation of the waiting queue

Upon arrival in the system, a task is allocated a dynamic priority score determined by its context-aware attributes, including deadline urgency, expected execution duration, and computational demands. These scores are produced utilizing a reinforcement learning policy that continuously adjusts through interaction with the system environment. Cost-efficient service chaining in NFV networks under latency and budget constraints has been explored36, alongside live migration of correlated VMs in cloud data centers37. Location-based edge service pre-deployment reduces MEC delays38, while Pareto-optimal resilience frameworks extend multi-objective optimization to water systems39. DQN has also been applied to dynamic AGV-driven job shop scheduling40. This structure incorporates ongoing learning input to dynamically modify priorities, unlike standard static priority queues.

At the outset, when the dynamic priority queue (Q) is vacant, new tasks are added to Q via RL_Queue_Insert (Q, task), with each job’s priority established by its current Q-value. The amalgamation of two distinct queues, such as those from edge and cloud nodes, can be executed using RL_Queue_Union (Q1, Q2). The paramount operation is RL_Queue_Extract_Max(Q), which extracts the job with the maximum Q-value and subsequently initiates queue reordering. This procedure prioritizes the most critical or significant jobs for scheduling, while adhering to overarching system limitations. Figure 3 exemplifies this dynamic queue process. RL-MOTS utilizes a centralized scheduler including a separate action space for the allocation of task \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{T}_{k}$$\end{document} to either an edge node \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{E}_{i}$$\end{document} or a cloud VM \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{V}_{j}$$\end{document}. In this framework, value-based approaches like DQN are appealing due to their minimal inference latency and reduced processing requirements, alongside stable off-policy learning through experience replay and target networks.

Off-policy replay enables the agent to more effectively utilize infrequent yet informative transitions compared to on-policy actor-critic techniques, hence enhancing data efficiency without prolonging simulation time.

The stabilization approaches employed by DQN resulted in consistent Q-value convergence and reliable learning curves.

We executed PPO and A3C baselines utilizing identical state/action encoding and reward architecture. Multi-agent reinforcement learning is advantageous when scheduling decisions need to be made locally by diverse nodes with restricted coordination, or when actions are continuous. This study focuses on a centralized controller characteristic of cloud-edge orchestrators; expanding RL-MOTS to CTDE-style MARL or hybrid actor-critic methodologies is a prospective avenue for future research.

Hyperparameter tuning and sensitivity analysis

Prior to the primary studies, a structured hyperparametric search was conducted. In particular, a grid search looked at greedy ε-decay schemes (linear decay in 10k, 20k, and 30k steps), replay buffer sizes {5000, 10000, 20000}, discount coefficients γ in {0.90, 0.95, 0.99}, and learning rates in {1e-2, 5e-3, 1e-3, 5e-4}. The configuration that was chosen (α = 1e-3, γ = 0.95, buffer = 10000, ε decay in 20k steps) offered the optimum balance between stability and convergence speed over a number of seeds.

Conclusion

This study introduces the RL-MOTS framework, an innovative method aimed at enhancing task allocation in dynamic cloud-edge computing settings. RL-MOTS attains a Pareto-optimal equilibrium among waiting time, energy consumption, and operational expenses by utilizing a DQN combined with a priority-aware dynamic queueing mechanism, as demonstrated through comprehensive simulations.

Comparative analyses with baseline algorithms, such as FCFS, Min-Min, Priority-Based, and MOF, reveal substantial enhancements: a makespan reduction of up to 30%, a 25% decrease in energy consumption, and a 22% reduction in operational costs, all while achieving a high SLA compliance rate of 98% across diverse workloads. The implementation of a state-reward tensor framework and an adaptive reward function significantly improves decision-making resilience, thereby overcoming the shortcomings of conventional scheduling models in decentralized and latency-sensitive systems. The ramifications of these results surpass computing efficiency, offering a scalable and adaptive solution for nascent IoT-driven applications and sustainable data center operations. Furthermore, the framework’s capacity to effectively manage both preemptive and non-preemptive activities highlights its adaptability, establishing it as a suitable basis for future research in cloud-edge scheduling. Nonetheless, specific obstacles persist, such as the real-time integration of dynamic network conditions and the scalability of DQN training in the context of exponentially expanding state spaces. Future endeavors will concentrate on the development of hybrid reinforcement learning architectures, the integration of transfer learning to reduce training costs, and the investigation of federated learning methodologies to improve privacy-preserving scheduling in dispersed edge networks. These developments are expected to enhance the framework’s relevance in next-generation computing paradigms.

Lifting relations in right orderable groups
Marco Linton

Abstract
. In this article we study the following problem: given a chain complex A ∗ A_{*} of free ℤ ​ G \mathbb{Z}G -modules, when is A ∗ A_{*} isomorphic to the cellular chain complex of some simply connected G G -CW-complex? Such a chain complex is called realisable. Wall studied this problem in the 60’s and reduced it to a problem involving only the second differential d 2 d_{2} , now known as the relation lifting problem. We show that if G G is right orderable and d 2 d_{2} is given by a matrix of a certain form, then A ∗ A_{*} is realisable. As a special case, we solve the relation lifting problem for right orderable groups with cyclic relation module.

Introduction

If

\pi_{1}(X)=G

\mathbb{Z}G
-modules

sending natural basis elements of each

Call a

-complex

if it admits a positive answer to the above question and call the isomorphism

a

. Call a

-complex

Theorem 1.1

.

Let

. Let

\mathbb{Z}G

There are two obvious necessary conditions needed for

to be admissible. The first is that

. The second is that for each

, we must have

The first counterexample to Wall’s conjecture was provided by Dunwoody

]

1.1.
The relation lifting and relation gap problems

Wall’s conjecture is more commonly known as the
relation lifting problem
and can be equivalently stated in terms of relation modules. Recall that if
F
is a free group,
N\triangleleft F
a normal subgroup, then the
relation module
associated with the presentation
G=F/N
is the left (or right)
\mathbb{Z}G
-module
N_{\ab}=N/[N,N]
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}F
acts naturally on
N_{\ab}
via conjugation by
F
and since
N
acts trivially, the action descends to an action of
\mathbb{Z}G
. If
A_{*}
is an algebraic
\mathbb{Z}G
-complex, then it is a well known fact that
d_{2}(A_{2})
is isomorphic to a relation module
N_{\ab}
(see
Lemma

2.7
) and hence any free basis for
A_{2}
corresponds to a choice of generating set for
N_{\ab}
. We may now rephrase Wall’s conjecture:

Problem 1.5

(Relation lifting problem)
.

Let
F
be a free group,
N\triangleleft F
a normal subgroup and let
G=F/N
. If
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\subset N_{\ab}=N/[N,N]
is a set of
\mathbb{Z}G
-module generators for the relation module
N_{\ab}
, does there exist a collection of elements
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\in N
such that
w_{i}[N,N]=r_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
and
\langle\!\langle w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}\rangle\!\rangle=N
?

The first key step in the proof of our main results is a criterion for when a relation module generator can be lifted as in
Problem

1.5
. We state below the criterion applied to the case in which the relation module is cyclic. This is a special case of
Theorem

5.2
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
G=F/N
be a group such that
\mathbb{Z}G
is a domain and such that
N_{\ab}=(r)
for some
r\in N_{\ab}
. Let
w\in N
be a cyclically reduced element such that
r=w[N,N]
. Then
N=\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle
if and only if no proper non-empty subword of
w
lies in
N
.

A theorem of Weinbaum’s
[
Wei72
]
states that if
F
is a free group and
w\in F
is a cyclically reduced element, then no proper non-empty subword of
w
lies in
\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle
. Rather, every proper non-empty subword of
w
is non-trivial in the quotient one-relator group
G=F/\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle
. Howie then generalised this result to one-relator products of locally indicable groups in
[
How82
]
.
Theorem

1.6
can thus be considered as a converse to Weinbaum’s theorem.

The second crux of the article involves showing that, under some extra technical hypotheses (which are always satisfied by right orderable groups), one can always find elements
w\in N
that map to some relation module generator and such that no proper non-empty subword of
w
lies in
N
. In the general setting, this leads to
Theorem

1.2
. We leave the translation of
Theorem

1.2
to a result about relation modules to the interested reader and instead only state the version for cyclic relation modules. The following is
Corollary

7.5
.

Corollary 1.7
.

If
G=F/N
is right orderable and
N_{\ab}=(r)
for some
r\in N_{\ab}
, then there is an element
w\in N
such that

w[N,N]=r,\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle=N.

In particular,
G
is a one-relator group.

If
d_{\mathbb{Z}G}(N_{\ab})
denotes the smallest cardinality of a
\mathbb{Z}G
-generating set for
N_{\ab}
and
d_{F}(N)
denotes the smallest cardinality of a normal generating set for
N
, then it is immediate that

d_{\mathbb{Z}G}(N_{\ab})\leqslant d_{F}(N).

By the Bestvina–Brady examples, it is possible that
d_{\mathbb{Z}G}(N_{\ab})<d_{F}(N)=\infty
. When we further assume that
d_{F}(N)<\infty
, whether the above can be taken to be equality is known as the
relation gap problem
and remains open.

Problem 1.8

(Relation gap problem)
.

Let
F
be a free group,
N\triangleleft F
a normal subgroup and let
G=F/N
. Is

d_{\mathbb{Z}G}(N_{\ab})=d_{F}(N)

when
d_{F}(N)<\infty
?

Potential counterexamples were proposed by Gruenberg–Linnell who proved in
[
GL08
]
that the group
(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})*(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})
has relation module generated by three elements (when
\gcd(m,n)=1
), one fewer elements than the number of obvious normal generators for a presentation. Explicit generators of the relation module were provided by Mannan
[
Man16
]
. Further potential counterexamples were proposed by Bridson–Tweedale in
[
BT07
]
, who also showed (based on work of Dyer) that if their groups were proven to be counterexamples to the relation gap problem, then they would also obtain counterexamples to Wall’s
D(2)
conjecture. Finally, we also mention that potential counterexamples to the relation gap problem and the
D(2)
conjecture also arose in the work of Nicholson
[
Nic21
, Section 8]
.

Corollary

1.7
shows that there can be no counterexample amongst right orderable groups with cyclic relation module, answering
[
BMS02
, OR5]
for right orderable groups. The only previously known result in this direction is due to Harlander
[
Har93
]
who proved that a solvable group with cyclic relation module is a one-relator group.

Our results actually establish something slightly stronger. If
G=F/N
and
R
is a ring, the
R
-relation module is the
RG
-module
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N_{\ab}
. One can pose the relation lifting and relation gap problems also for the
R
-relation module in the same way. A result of Gruenberg
[
Gru76
]
implies that for finite groups, it is easy to find examples with relation gap over a field (see also
[
Har18
]
). We give an explicit example of a group
G
with a reducible presentation with cyclic relation module over
\mathbb{Q}
, but that is not a one-relator group in
Example

7.6
. Nevertheless, amongst right orderable groups we may still show there is no relation gap over an arbitrary domain.

Corollary 1.9
.

If
G=F/N
is right orderable,
R
is a domain and the
R
-relation module
R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N_{\ab}
is generated by a single element
1\otimes r\in R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N_{\ab}
as an
RG
-module, then there is an element
w\in N
and a positive integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

1\otimes w^{k}[N,N]=1\otimes r,\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle=N.

In particular,
G
is a one-relator group.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Group rings and engulfing elements

Rings will always be assumed to have unit
1\neq 0
and modules will always be assumed to be left modules. In particular, this means that in this article, conjugation will be considered as a left action. The group of units of a ring
R
will be denoted by
R^{\times}
. The
annihilator
of a subset
S\subset M
of a (left)
R
-module
M
is denoted by
\Ann_{R}(S)=\{r\in R\mid r\cdot s=0,\forall s\in S\}
.

Let
R
be a ring and let
G
be a group. Denote by
RG
, or
R[G]
, the group ring of
G
with coefficients in
R
. Each element
r\in RG
is a formal sum

r=\sum_{g\in G}r_{g}g

where
r_{g}\in R
and for all but finitely many elements
g\in G
, we have
r_{g}=0
. The support of an element
r\in RG
is defined as the set

\supp(r)=\{g\in G\mid r_{g}\neq 0\}\subset G.

Note that
\supp(rs)\subset\supp(r)\supp(s)
for all
r,s\in RG
.

Now let
F
be a free left (right)
RG
-module with a given free basis
B\subset F
. For each
b\in B
, denote by
p_{b}\colon F\to RG
the corresponding projection map. We extend the definition of support above to free
RG
-modules by defining for all
m\in F
:

\supp_{B}(m)=\bigcup_{b\in B}\supp(p_{b}(m))\cdot b\subset G\cdot B.

We introduce the following property as it is precisely what we need to prove our main results in
Section

6
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
F
is a free left (right)
RG
-module with a free basis
B\subset F
, say a non-zero element
m\in F
is
left (right) engulfing (with respect to
B
)
if there is an
r\in RG-R
such that
\supp_{B}(r\cdot m)\subset\supp_{B}(m)
, say it is
not left (right) engulfing
otherwise.

When
F=RG
, left (right) engulfing elements are always assumed to be with respect to the basis
B=\{1\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If the group ring
RG
does not contain any left (or right) engulfing elements, then it is immediate that
RG
is a domain. It is also not hard to see that under the same hypothesis, the group of units of
RG
is
(RG)^{\times}=\{r\cdot g\mid r\in R^{\times},g\in G\}
.

2.2.
Group rings without engulfing elements

A group
G
is said to have
k
-unique products
if for any non-empty finite subsets
A,B\subset G
with
|AB|\geqslant k
, there are elements
a_{1}b_{1},\ldots,a_{k}b_{k}\in AB
uniquely represented as products in
AB
. It is known that 1-unique products, known as
unique products
, implies
2
-unique products by work of Strojnowski
[
Str80
]
.

Say
G
has
left
k
-unique products
if for any pair of finite subsets
A,B\subset G
with
|A|\geqslant k
and
B

a_{1}b_{1},\ldots,a_{k}b_{k}\in AB
uniquely represented as products in
AB
with
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
for all
i\neq j
. Define right
k
-unique products similarly. Our motivation for introducing this property is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
G
is a left
2
-unique product group and

is a domain, then

does not contain any left engulfing elements. In particular, every free left

-module does not contain any left engulfing elements with respect to any basis.

Proof.

Let

be non-zero elements. Suppose that

. If

, then
\supp((1+a)b)\subset\supp(b)
and so, by possibly replacing

with

, we may assume that

. Since
G
has left
2
-unique products, if

, then

and so there are two uniquely represented elements

with

. Since

and so

. Since

and

, this implies that

, a contradiction. Hence

and so

. Since

A group

is
right (left) orderable
if there is a total order on

such that for any

, we have

(

) for all

combined with the fact that right orderable groups are also left orderable.

.

If

has left and right

does not contain any left (or right) engulfing elements and nor does any free left (or right)

We do not know whether there exists a group with unique products, but without left

-unique products or a group with left
2
-unique products, but that is not right orderable. We also do not know whether there is a relation between the unique product property and the absence of left (or right) engulfing elements.

of the presentation.

2.4.
Cellular chain complexes and relation modules

If
X
is a CW-complex, recall that we denote by
C_{*}(X)
the associated cellular chain complex with boundary maps
\partial_{n}\colon C_{n}(X)\to C_{n-1}(X)
. If
R
is a ring, denote by
C_{*}(X,R)=R\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}C_{*}(X)
the chain complex with boundary maps
1\otimes\partial_{n}\colon C_{n}(X,R)\to C_{n-1}(X,R)
. If
c\subset X
is an
n
-cell, we denote by
[c]\in C_{n}(X)
(and
1\otimes[c]\in C_{n}(X,R)
) the corresponding
n
-chain. The
n
-cycles and
n
-boundaries are the submodules
\ker(1\otimes\partial_{n})=Z_{n}(X,R)\leqslant C_{n}(X,R)
and
\Ima(1\otimes\partial_{n+1})=B_{n}(X,R)\leqslant C_{n}(X,R)
and
H_{n}(X,R)=Z_{n}(X,R)/B_{n}(X,R)
. Here
C_{n}(X,R)
is considered as a (left)
R
-module.

Now let
G
be a group and suppose that
X
is a
G
-CW-complex. That is,
G
acts on
X
by homeomorphisms, preserving the cell structure. We also require that any element of
G
that fixes a cell setwise also fixes it pointwise. This action naturally turns each
C_{n}(X,R)
into a left
RG
-module and each map
1\otimes\partial_{n}
into an
RG
-module map. By choosing
G
-orbit representatives of
n
-cells
c\subset X
, we obtain a natural free
RG
-basis for
C_{n}(X,R)
consisting of the elements
1\otimes[c]\in C_{n}(X,R)
. A free
RG
-basis for
C_{n}(X,R)
is
natural
if each basis element is of the form
1\otimes[c]
for some
n
-cell
c\subset X
.

Since our results only involve
2
-dimensional CW-complexes, we shall usually abbreviate
2
-dimensional CW-complex to
2
-complex
and a
2
-dimensional
G
-CW-complex to
G
-
2
-complex
.

Let
X
be a connected
2
-complex, let
T\subset X^{(1)}
be a spanning tree and denote by
Z
the CW-complex obtained from
X
by contracting

to a single 0-cell. This deformation retraction induces a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}(\widetilde{Z},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{1}(\widetilde{Z},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{0}(\widetilde{Z},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{2}(\widetilde{X},R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{1}(\widetilde{X},R)}}

where the vertical map

with generators corresponding to each 1-cell and relators corresponding to the attaching maps of each

These observations imply the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

be a ring and let

2.5.
A simple lemma on integer sequences

The following seemingly uninteresting lemma will be important in
Section

4
when constructing hierarchies for one-relator products.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
a\leqslant b\in\mathbb{N}
be a pair of coprime integers and let

0=i_{1},j_{1},i_{2},\ldots,j_{n},i_{n+1}=0

be a sequence of non-negative integers with
i_{l}-j_{l}\in b\mathbb{Z}
and
j_{l}-i_{l+1}\in a\mathbb{Z}
for each
l
. Either
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i_{l}-j_{l})=0
or for some
l
we have
i_{l}\geqslant a+b-1
or
j_{l}\geqslant a+b-1
.

Proof.

We assume that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i_{l}-j_{l})\neq 0
and
i_{l},j_{l}<a+b-1
for all
l
and derive a contradiction. The proof proceeds by induction on
n
. Suppose there is some
1\neq p<q
such that
i_{p}=i_{q}
. After possibly subtracting the minimum value from each term and cyclically permuting the subsequence
i_{p},j_{p},\ldots,i_{q-1},j_{q-1},i_{q}=i_{p}
, we may ensure that it is of the form required by the lemma and hence conclude by induction that either
\sum_{l=p}^{q}(i_{l}-j_{l})=0
or
i_{l}\geqslant a+b-1
or
j_{l}\geqslant a+b-1
for some
l
. In the second case we have obtained a contradiction, in the first case we may replace the subsequence
i_{p},j_{p},\ldots,i_{q}
with
i_{q}
and also reach a contradiction by induction. Thus, from now on we may assume that
i_{p}\neq i_{q}
for all
1\neq p\neq q
. If
(i_{l}-j_{l})=0
, then if we replace the subsequence
j_{l-1},i_{l},j_{l}
with
j_{l}+j_{l+1}
, we obtain a shorter sequence also satisfying our assumptions and not changing the value of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i_{l}-j_{l})
. Thus, we obtain a contradiction by induction in this case too. Finally, assume that
i_{l}-j_{l}\neq 0
for all
l
. If there is some
l
such that
i_{l}-j_{l}>0
and
j_{l}-i_{l+1}>0
or
i_{l}-j_{l}<0
and
j_{l}-i_{l+1}<0
, then
i_{l+1}-i_{l}\geqslant a+b
or
i_{l}-i_{l+1}\geqslant a+b
and we reach a contradiction. Similarly, if there is some
l
such that
j_{l}-i_{l+1}>0
and
i_{l+1}-j_{l+1}>0
or
j_{l}-i_{l+1}<0
and
i_{l+1}-j_{l+1}<0
, then we reach a contradiction. Since
i_{1}-j_{1}<0
, this implies that
i_{l}-j_{l}<0
and
j_{l}-i_{l+1}>0
for all
l
. Moreover, since
a
and
b
are coprime and

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i_{l}-j_{l})=n_{1}b=n_{2}a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(j_{l}-i_{l+1})

for some
n_{1},n_{2}>0
, we must have
-\sum_{l=1}^{n}(i_{l}-j_{l})=n_{1}b\geqslant ab
. If
-(i_{l}-j_{l})\geqslant 2b
for some
l
, then
j_{l}>2b\geqslant a+b
, a contradiction. Thus we have that
(i_{l}-j_{l})=-b
for all
l
. In particular, we have that
n\geqslant a
. Since
i_{p}\neq i_{q}
for all
1\neq p\neq q
, there are at least
a
distinct non-zero values of
i_{l}
and hence
i_{l}\geqslant a-1
for some
l
. Then, combined with the fact that
j_{l}-i_{l}=b
, we have
j_{l}\geqslant a+b-1
, a contradiction.
∎

3.
One-relator products and elementary reductions

If
A
and
B
are groups, we shall regard elements
w\in A*B-1
as words over the alphabet
(A-1)\sqcup(B-1)
. A word
w\in A*B
then has a well-defined length over this alphabet. It is freely reduced if there is no subword of
w
consisting only of letters from
A-1
or
B-1
that is equal to the identity in
A
or
B
respectively. It is cyclically reduced if each cyclic rotation of
w
is also freely reduced.

Let
A
and
B
be groups and let
w\in A*B
be a cyclically reduced word. We call
G=\frac{A*B}{\langle\!\langle w\rangle\!\rangle}
a
one-relator product
. If
A
and
B
are both locally indicable, Howie proved in
[
How81
]
that the maps
A\to G
and
B\to G
are actually injective, a result which can be considered a generalisation of Magnus’ Freiheitssatz for one-relator groups. The following statement combines this result with
[
How82
, Theorem 4.2 & Corollary 3.4]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Howie)
.

Let
A
and
B
be locally indicable groups and let
w\in A*B
be a cyclically reduced word of length at least two that is not a proper power. Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and denote by
G=\frac{A*B}{\langle\!\langle w^{n}\rangle\!\rangle}
. The following hold

(1)

The natural maps
A,B\to G
are injective.

(2)

If
u
is a proper subword of (a cyclic conjugate of)
w^{n}
, then
u\neq_{G}1
.

(3)

If
n=1
, then
G
is locally indicable.

Let
Z
be a
2
-complex and
X\subset Z
a subcomplex. We say that
X\subset Z
is an
elementary reduction
if
Z-X
consists of a single 1-cell
e
and at most one
2
-cell whose attaching map is not freely homotopic within
X\cup e
into
X
. A
2
-complex
Z
is
reducible to
X\subset Z
if there are subcomplexes
X=X_{0}\subset X_{1}\subset\ldots\subset Z
such that
Z=\bigcup X_{i}
and
X_{i}\subset X_{i+1}
is an elementary reduction for all
i
. A
2
-complex
X
is
reducible
if it is reducible to
X^{(0)}
. If
X
is reducible, say
X
is
without proper powers
if for each
i
such that
X_{i+1}-X_{i}=e\cup c
with
e
a 1-cell and
c
a
2
-cell, the attaching map for
c
is not equal to a proper power in
\pi_{1}(X_{i}\cup e,x)
. We state a corollary to
Theorem

3.1
in terms of the language we have just introduced.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
Z
be a connected
2
-complex and let
X\subset Z
be an elementary reduction such that
Z-X=e\cup c
, where
e
is a 1-cell and
c
is a
2
-cell. If
\pi_{1}(X,x)
is locally indicable for every choice of basepoint
x\in X
, then:

(1)

The map
\pi_{1}(X,x)\to\pi_{1}(Z,x)
induced by inclusion is injective for each choice of basepoint
x\in X
.

(2)

If
\tilde{e},\tilde{c}\subset\widetilde{Z}
are lifts of
e,c\subset Z
to the universal cover
\widetilde{Z}\to Z
, then the difference between the number of times the attaching map for
\tilde{c}
traverses
\tilde{e}
in one direction and the number of times it traverses
\tilde{e}
in the other direction is either
-1
,
0
or
1
.

(3)

If the attaching map for
c
is not a proper power in
\pi_{1}(X\cup e)
, then
\pi_{1}(Z)
is locally indicable.

Proof.

We may assume that
Z
is connected. Choose an orientation on
e
so that we may consider
e
as a path and let
e^{-1}
denote the path traversing
e
in the opposite direction. Let

w=e^{\epsilon_{0}}*p_{0}*\ldots*e^{\epsilon_{n}}*p_{n}

be the loop in
Z
determined by the attaching map for
c
, well-defined up to cyclic conjugation and inverting, where each
p_{i}
is a path supported in
X
and where
\epsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
. Such a factorisation exists because the attaching map of
c
does not lie in
X
. Now there are two cases to consider, according to whether
X
is connected or not.

Let us first suppose that
X
is connected. Then the two endpoints of
e
lie in
X
and we have
\pi_{1}(X\cup e)\cong\pi_{1}(X)*\mathbb{Z}
. Since the attaching map of
c
is not freely homotopic into
X
, we may apply
Theorem

3.1
to obtain the first and third statements. Now let
\tilde{w}=\tilde{e}_{0}^{\epsilon_{0}}*\tilde{p}_{0}*\ldots*\tilde{e}_{n}^{\epsilon_{n}}*\tilde{p}_{n}
be the lift of
w
corresponding to the attaching map of the lift
\tilde{c}
of
c
. The proof proceeds by induction on
n
. If
w
, as a word over
\pi_{1}(X)*\mathbb{Z}
, is cyclically reduced, then we may apply
Theorem

3.1
to conclude that there is no proper non-empty subpath of the attaching map of
\tilde{c}
that traverses
\tilde{e}^{\pm 1}
and that is a loop. Hence,
\tilde{w}
traverses
\tilde{e}
at most once in one of the two directions and so the second conclusion holds. This, for instance, holds if
n=0,1
. Now assume the inductive hypothesis and suppose that
w
is not cyclically reduced as a word over
\pi_{1}(X)*\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for some
i
, we have that
\epsilon_{i}=-\epsilon_{i+1}
and
p_{i}
is a nullhomotopic loop in
X
. Here we are considering the indices modulo
n+1
. This implies that
\tilde{e}_{i}^{\epsilon_{i}}*\tilde{p}_{i}*\tilde{e}_{i+1}^{\epsilon_{i+1}}
is a loop and hence that
\tilde{e}_{i}^{\epsilon_{i}}
and
\tilde{e}_{i+1}^{\epsilon_{i+1}}
together contribute
0
to the difference between the number of times
\tilde{w}
traverses
\tilde{e}
and the number of times it traverses
\tilde{e}^{-1}
. By removing
e^{\epsilon_{i}}*p_{i}*e^{\epsilon_{i+1}}
from
w
, we obtain a new path
w^{\prime}
which is strictly shorter than
w
. Since
w
is freely homotopic to
w^{\prime}
in
X\cup e
, we may apply the inductive hypothesis to conclude that the difference between the number of times
\tilde{w}^{\prime}
traverses
\tilde{e}
and
\tilde{e}^{-1}
is either
-1,0
or
1
. Hence, the same holds for
\tilde{w}
.

The case in which
X
is not connected is almost identical and so we leave it to the reader.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
R
be a ring, let
Z
be a connected
2
-complex and let
X\subset Z
be an elementary reduction such that
Z-X=e\cup c
, where
e
is a 1-cell and
c
is a
2
-cell. Suppose that

is locally indicable for every choice of basepoint

. Denote by

, let

be the universal cover, let
\overline{X}=p^{-1}(X)
and let
\tilde{e},\tilde{c}\subset\widetilde{Z}
be lifts of

. Then

(1\otimes\partial_{2})(1\otimes[\tilde{c}])\in C_{1}(\overline{X},R)+r\cdot[\tilde{e}]

for some
0\neq r=\sum_{g\in G}r_{g}\cdot g\in RG
such that
r_{g}\in\{-1,0,1\}
for all
g\in G
.

We now state the precise version of the definition of a lower trapezoidal matrix given in the introduction.

Definition 3.4
.

Denote by
[n]\subset\mathbb{N}
the subset
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
and by
[n]=\mathbb{N}
if
n=\infty
. If
M=\{m_{ij}\}_{i\in[m],j\in[n]}
is an
m\times n
matrix, we say that
M
is

if for each
i\in[m]
, there is a
j_{i}\in[n]
such that
m_{ij}=0
for all
j>j_{i}
and such that
j_{i}<j_{i+1}
for all
i,i+1\in[m]
. Call the entries
m_{ij_{i}}
the diagonal entries of
M
.

After possibly reordering the columns,
Definition

3.4
coincides with the definition given in the introduction for finite lower trapezoidal matrices.

The following is one direction of our main theorem. This direction was essentially already known, but a satisfactory reference could not be found.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be a reducible
2
-complex without proper powers and let
R
be a ring. Then
1\otimes\partial_{2}\colon C_{2}(\widetilde{X},R)\to C_{1}(\widetilde{X},R)
is given by a lower trapezoidal matrix over any natural basis.

Proof.

Let
X_{0}\subset X_{1}\subset\ldots\subset X
be the subcomplexes such that
X_{0}=X^{(0)}
,
X_{i}\subset X_{i+1}
is an elementary reduction for each
i
and
\bigcup X_{i}=X
. Since
X
is without proper powers, we may apply
Theorem

3.1
and induction to conclude that
\pi_{1}(X_{i})\to\pi_{1}(X_{i+1})\to\pi_{1}(X)
is injective for each choice of basepoint.

Let
c_{i}
be the
i^{\text{th}}

2
-cell appearing in
X_{1}-X_{0},X_{2}-X_{1},\ldots
and let
e_{i}
be the 1-cell in
X_{i}-X_{i-1}
. Then let
j_{i}
denote the index such that
c_{i}\subset X_{j_{i}}-X_{j_{i}-1}
. A natural basis for
C_{2}(\widetilde{X},R)
corresponds to choices of lifts
\tilde{c}_{i}\subset\widetilde{X}
of each

. Similarly, a natural basis for
C_{1}(\widetilde{X},R)
corresponds to choices of lifts
\tilde{e}_{i}\subset\widetilde{X}
of each

. The matrix

defining

-entry the projection of

to the

factor. The

is

when

does not traverse

. Now all we need to show is that the

-entry is non-zero for all

, where

is the universal cover, this follows from the fact that

combined with

.

4.
Hierarchies for one-relator products

The aim of this section will be to prove the following hierarchy result for one-relator products. A version of this result is implicit in the work of Howie, see
[
How82
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be locally indicable groups,
w\in A*B
an element not conjugate into
A
or
B
and not a proper power and
n\geqslant 1
an integer. Then
G\cong\frac{A*B}{\langle\!\langle w^{n}\rangle\!\rangle}
can be obtained from subgroups of
A
and
B
, free groups and free products of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
by performing finitely many amalgamated free products and HNN-extensions.

The reader is directed to
[
Ser03
]
for the relevant background on amalgamated free products, HNN-extensions and, more generally, graphs of groups.

4.1.
HNN-splittings via infinite cyclic covers

In order to find HNN-splittings, we will need the notion of a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain, introduced in
[
Lin22
]
. Recall that a
\mathbb{Z}
-cover is a covering space
\rho\colon Y\to X
with
\deck(p)\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\rho\colon Y\to X
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-cover and let
t\in\deck(\rho)
be a generator. A connected subcomplex
D\subset Y
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain
for
\rho
if the following hold:

(1)

\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{i}\cdot D=Y
.

(2)

D\cap t\cdot D
is connected and non-empty.

(3)

D\cap t^{i}\cdot D\subset D\cap t^{i-1}\cdot D
for all
i\geqslant 1
.

The point of this definition is that a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain in a
\mathbb{Z}
-cover gives rise to a HNN-splitting of the fundamental group of the base space. The following is
[
Lin22
, Proposition 4.3]
made explicit.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\rho\colon Y\to X
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-cover, let
t\in\deck(\rho)
, let
D\subset Y
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain for
p
and denote by
D_{0}=t^{-1}\cdot D\cap D
. If
\pi_{1}(D_{0})\to\pi_{1}(D)
and
\pi_{1}(t\cdot D_{0})\to\pi_{1}(D)
are injective, then

\pi_{1}(X)\cong\pi_{1}(D)*_{\psi}

where
\psi\colon\pi_{1}(D_{0})\to\pi_{1}(t\cdot D_{0})
is induced by translation by
t
.

In order to be able to apply
Lemma

4.3
to our setting, we shall need to be able to find
\mathbb{Z}
-domains.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
X\subset Z
be an elementary reduction such that
\pi_{1}(X,x)
is indicable for each choice of basepoint, such that
Z-X=e\cup c
and such that the attaching map for
c
is surjective on the 1-skeleton of
Z
. There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-cover
\rho\colon Y\to Z
, lifts
\tilde{c},\tilde{e}\subset Y
of
c,e
such that the connected component
D
of

\rho^{-1}(X)\cup\tilde{c}\cup\bigcup_{m\leqslant i\leqslant M}t^{i}\cdot\tilde{e}

containing
\tilde{c}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain, where here
m
and
M
are, respectively, the minimal and maximal integers such that the attaching map for
\tilde{c}
traverses
t^{m}\cdot\tilde{e}
and
t^{M}\cdot\tilde{e}
.

Proof.

Denote by
e_{i}=t^{i}\cdot\tilde{e}
and by
\lambda
the attaching map for
c
. By replacing
\tilde{c}
with
t^{-m}\cdot\tilde{c}
, we may assume that
m=0
. We have two cases to consider corresponding to whether
X
is connected or not.

Suppose first that
X
is not connected, denote by
X_{1},X_{2}
the two connected components. We have
H^{1}(Z)\cong(H^{1}(X_{1})\oplus H^{1}(X_{2}))/(a,b)
for some
a\in H^{1}(X_{1})
and
b\in H^{1}(X_{2})
. Since
\pi_{1}(X_{i})
is indicable,
H^{1}(X_{i})
is free abelian of rank at least one for
i=1,2
. If
H^{1}(X_{1})
is not infinite cyclic or
a=0
, there is an epimorphism
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(Z)\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
\phi(\pi_{1}(X_{2}))=0
. Consider the cyclic cover
\rho\colon Y\to Z
corresponding to this epimorphism. We have that
\rho^{-1}(X_{1})
is a connected infinite cyclic cover of
X_{1}
and
\rho^{-1}(X_{2})
is a disjoint union of copies of
X_{2}
, each connected to
\rho^{-1}(X_{1})
via a (unique) edge
e_{i}
. In this case, it is not hard to see that the three conditions for
D
to be a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain hold. By symmetry, if
H^{1}(X_{2})
is not cyclic or
b=0
, there is a cyclic cover in which
D
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-domain.

So now suppose that
H^{1}(X_{1})\cong H^{1}(X_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and that
a,b\neq 0
. We may also assume that
a\leqslant b
and that
a
and
b
are coprime. Then there is a unique (up to change of sign) epimorphism
\phi\colon\pi_{1}(Z)\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
\phi(\pi_{1}(X_{1}))=b\mathbb{Z}
,

Lemma 5.4

be a graph of groups with underlying graph a tree

be a ring,

. Then the map

induced by inclusion is also injective for all vertices

We assume that each edge in

the set of oriented edges of

the set of vertices. Denote by

the origin vertex and by

the target vertex of the edge

. The maps on homology induced by inclusion are then denoted by

.

Let us inspect the following extract from the Mayer–Vietoris sequence induced by the graph of groups structure (see work of Chiswell

\bigoplus_{e\in E(T)}H_{1}(T_{e},M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in V(T)}H_{1}(T_{v},M)\rightarrow H_{1}(G,M).

The fist map is

. In order to complete the proof, we need to show that

\Ima\left(\sum_{e\in E(T)}((\partial_{e}^{-})_{\#}+(\partial_{e}^{+})_{\#})\right)\cap H_{1}(T_{v},R)=0

for all

Let

, let

(or

) is distinct from

and distinct from all other endpoints of edges in

\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, but torsion subgroups of one-relator groups are cyclic. Denote by
N=\langle\!\langle[tat^{-1},a],a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle\triangleleft F(a,b)
. We claim that

\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}N/[N,N]=\mathbb{Q}Q_{n}\cdot(1\otimes[tat^{-1},a]a^{-n}[N,N]).

It will suffice to show that
H_{1}(N/\langle\!\langle[tat^{-1},a]a^{-n}\rangle\!\rangle,\mathbb{Q})=0
by
Lemma

2.5
. In other words, that the normal closure
\langle\!\langle a_{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H}
of
a^{n}
in
H=F(a,b)/\langle\!\langle[tat^{-1},a]a^{-n}\rangle\!\rangle
is
\mathbb{Q}
-perfect. But

[ta^{n}t^{-1},a^{n}]=_{H}a^{n((n+1)^{n}-1)}

by
[
BT07
, Lemma 3.2]
and so the element
a^{n}[\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H},\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H}]
has finite order in
\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle/[\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H},\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H}]
. In particular, this implies that
H_{1}(\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H},\mathbb{Z})
is torsion and so
H_{1}(\langle\!\langle a^{n}\rangle\!\rangle_{H},\mathbb{Q})=0
.

Example

7.6
still leaves open the possibility that torsion-free groups with cyclic relation module over
\mathbb{Q}
are one-relator groups.

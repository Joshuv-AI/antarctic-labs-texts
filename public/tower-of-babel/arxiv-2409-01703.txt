Piecewise regular solutions to scalar balance laws with singular nonlocal sources
Lorena Bociu, Evangelia Ftaka, Khai T. Nguyen, Jacopo Schino

Abstract
The present paper establishes a local well-posed result for piecewise regular solutions with single shock of scalar balance laws with singular integral of convolution type kernels. In a neighborhood of the shock curve, a detailed description of the solution is provided for a general class of initial data. Keyword. Piecewise smooth solutions, scalar balance law, singular kernels, shock AMS Mathematics Subject Classification. 35B65, 76B15.



1
Introduction

We consider a scalar balance law in one space dimension with a singular source term

u_{t}+f(u)_{x}~=~{\bf G}[u],

(1.1)

where
u:[0,\infty[\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is the state variable,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{4}
strictly convex flux, i.e.,

\theta\cdot f(x_{1})+(1-\theta)\cdot f(x_{2})~>~f(\theta\cdot x_{1}+(1-\theta)\cdot x_{2}),\quad\theta\in]0,1[,\ \ x_{2}\neq x_{1},

(1.2)

and
{\bf G}
is a singular integral of convolution type defined by convolution with a kernel
K
that is locally integrable on
\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
, in the sense that

{\bf G}[g](x)~=~\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\int_{|y-x|>\varepsilon}K(x-y)\cdot g(y)~dy.

(1.3)

Here we work under the following assumptions on
K
, as it is typically done in applications:

(H1)

The kernel
K\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
takes the form of

K~=~K_{1}+K_{2}\qquad\mathrm{with}\qquad K_{2}\in{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),

and the singular part
K_{1}
is odd;

(H2)

There exist a constant
C>0
such that

\left|K^{(i)}(x)\right|~\leq~{C\over|x|^{i+1}}\qquad\hbox{for all}~i=0,1,2.

(1.4)

The conditions
(H1)
-
(H2)
ensure that the Fourier transform of
K
is essentially bounded. Thus,
{\bf G}:{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a bounded operator
[
15
]
, i.e.,

\|{\bf G}(g)\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~\|{\bf G}\|_{\infty}\cdot\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad g\in{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Equation (
1.1
) has an interesting structure since the scalar conservation law generates a contractive semigroup on
{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, but the operator
{\bf G}
may be discontinuous and unbounded as an operator on
{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. In the archetypal case when

f(u)~=~{u^{2}\over 2},\qquad\quad K(x)~=~\displaystyle{1\over\pi x},

equation (
1.1
) is well-known as the Burger-Hilbert equation which was introduced by Biello and Hunter in
[
1
]
as a model for surface waves with constant frequency. A lower bound on the maximal time of existence for smooth solutions was studied in
[
1
,
12
,
13
]
, the formation of singularities
[
6
]
and the local asymptotic behavior of a solution up to the time when a new shock is formed in finite time was investigated in
[
17
]
, and the global existence of entropy weak solutions was proved in
[
3
]
, together with a partial uniqueness result. Recently, piecewise regular solutions with a single shock for the “well-prepared” initial data have been constructed in
[
4
,
16
]
. To complete an asymptotic description of a solution to the Burgers-Hilbert equation in a neighborhood of a point
y_{0}
where two shocks interact in
[
5
]
,
the result was extended to a bigger class of initial data

u(0,x)~=~\overline{w}(x-y_{0})+\Big(c_{1}\cdot\chi_{]-\infty,y_{0}[}+c_{2}\cdot\chi_{]y_{0},+\infty[}\Big)\cdot\psi(x-y_{0}),

for some
\overline{w}\in\ H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
, constants
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\psi(x)\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
being a fixed even function with compact support, smooth outside the origin and satisfying

\psi(x)~=~{2\over\pi}\cdot|x|\ln|x|\qquad\hbox{for all}~~~|x|\leq 1.

In the present article, we study the unique piecewise regular solution with a single shock of (
1.1
) with a general class of initial data of the form

u(0,x)~=~\overline{w}(x-y_{0})+\bar{v}(x-y_{0}),\qquad\overline{w}~\in~H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}),

(1.5)

with for some
3/4<\alpha<1
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{v}(x)~\in~{\cal X}_{\alpha}~\doteq~\Bigg\{v\in{\cal C}_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{C}^{4}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}):\displaystyle\sup_{x\not=0}{|x|^{i-\alpha}\over 1+|x|^{i-\alpha}}\bigl|v^{(i)}(x)\bigr|<+\infty,\quad 1\leq i\leq 4\Bigg\}.\end{array}

(1.6)

Intuitively,
{\cal X}_{\alpha}
is the space of functions that have an arbitrarily large derivative as
x\to 0\pm
and grow like
|x|^{\alpha}
. However, this does not lead to the formation of additional shocks. Indeed, one expects that characteristics fall into the large shock before the blow-up of the gradient. As in
[
4
,
5
]
, the solutions are more regular than the usual weak entropy solutions and can be determined by integrating along characteristics. These correspond to the “broad solutions” considered in
[
2
,
14
]
.

Below we recall the notion of piecewise regular solutions for scalar balance laws.

Definition 1.1

A function
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
piecewise regular solution
of (
1.1
)
if there exist
finitely many shock curves
y_{1}(t),\ldots,y_{n}(t)
such that the following holds.

(i)

The map
t\mapsto u(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{y_{1}(t),\ldots,y_{n}(t)\})\cap H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{y_{1}(t),\ldots,y_{n}(t)\})
satisfies

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{y_{1}(t),\ldots,y_{n}(t)\})}+\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}[y_{i}(t)-\delta,y_{i}(t)+\delta]))}\right)<\infty

for every
\delta>0
sufficiently small.

(ii)

For each
i=1,\ldots,n
, the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions hold:

u_{i}^{-}(t)~\doteq~u(t,y_{i}(t)-)~>~u(t,y_{i}(t)+)~\doteq~u_{i}^{+}(t),

(1.7)

\dot{y}_{i}(t)~=~{f(u_{i}^{-}(t))-f(u_{i}^{+}(t))\over u_{i}^{-}(t)-u_{i}^{+}(t)}\,.

(1.8)

(iii)

Along every characteristic curve
t\mapsto x(t)
such that
\dot{x}(t)=f^{\prime}(u(t,x(t)))
,
one has

{d\over dt}u\bigl(t,x(t)\bigr)~=~{\bf G}[u](x(t)).

(1.9)

We note that in the above definition, as well as throughout the sequel, the upper dot denotes a derivative with respect to time.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Our main theorem is presented in Section
2
, along with the main steps of the proof. Section 3 develops various key a priori estimates on the source term which are necessary in the remaining steps of the proof.
In Section 4 we construct the unique local piecewise regular solution to [(
1.1
),(
1.5
)] as the limit of a convergent sequence of approximations. We conclude with an Appendix which contains some basic estimates on the singular kernel, the corrector term, and related functions.

2
Main result

We establish the local existence and uniqueness of a piecewise regular solution with a single shock to the general scalar balance law with nonlocal singular sources (
1.1
) for a large class of initial data defined in (
1.5
)-(
1.6
):

\begin{cases}u_{t}+f(u)_{x}~=~{\bf G}[u],\\[8.53581pt]
u(0,x)~=~\overline{w}(x-y_{0})+\bar{v}(x-y_{0}),\ \ \text{with}\ \overline{w}~\in~H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}),\ \text{and}\ \bar{v}~\in~{\cal X}_{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Our main theorem is presented below.

Theorem 2.1

Given
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bar{v}\in{\cal X}_{\alpha}
with
\displaystyle\alpha\in(3/4,1)
, for every
\overline{w}\in H^{2}\bigl(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}\bigr)
such that
\overline{w}(0-)>\overline{w}(0+)
, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) with initial data (
1.5
) admits a unique piecewise regular solution
u
with a single shock
y_{1}(\cdot)
defined for
t\in[0,T]
, for some
T>0
sufficiently small. Moreover, the map
t\mapsto u(t,y_{1}(t)\pm)
is locally Lipchitz and satisfies

\left|\dot{u}(t,y_{1}(t)\pm)\right|~\leq~2\Gamma_{1}t^{\alpha-1}~~~a.e.~t\in[0,T],

(2.1)

for some constant
\Gamma_{1}>0
.

Remark 2.1

The local existence and uniqueness result can be extended to the case of solutions with finitely many non-interacting shocks. Moreover, our result can be applied to both Fornberg-Whitham equation
[
9
]
and Burgers-Possion equation
[
8
,
10
,
11
]
.

Remark 2.2

We point out that
the lower bound
3/4
on the constant
\alpha
in the definition of
{\cal X}_{\alpha}
is somehow sharp within our analysis. The best lower bound on
\alpha
remains an open question.

2.1
New coordinate system for solutions with one shock

The first step in the proof of our main theorem consists in transferring the equation (
1.1
) to a new coordinate system so that the location of the shock of the constructed piecewise regular solution always remains at the origin. The details for the change of coordinates are included below.

Assume that
u
is a piecewise regular solution of the balance law (
1.1
), with one single shock.
By the Rankine-Hugoniot condition in (
1.8
), the location
y(t)
of the shock at time

satisfies

\dot{y}(t)~=~{f(u^{-}(t))-f(u^{+}(t))\over u^{-}(t)-u^{+}(t)},\qquad u^{\pm}(t)~=~\lim_{x\to y(t)\pm}u(t,x).

As in
[
4

5
]

(2.2)

with initial data of the form

u(0,x)~=~\overline{w}(x)+\bar{v}(x),\qquad\overline{w}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}),\qquad\bar{v}\in{\cal X}_{\alpha}.

(2.3)

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be an even cut-off function which is nonincreasing on

\mathrm{supp}(\eta)~\subseteq~[-2,2],\qquad\eta(x)~=~1\qquad\hbox{for all}~x\in[-1,1].

\overline{w}+\bar{v}=\Bigl[\overline{w}+\bar{v}-\Bigl(\bar{v}-\bar{v}(0)\Bigr)\cdot\eta\Bigr]+\Bigl(\bar{v}-\bar{v}(0)\Bigr)\cdot\eta~\in~H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})+{\cal X}_{\alpha}

2.2
Structure of piecewise regular solution

The key idea in proving a local existence result for the Cauchy problem (
2.2

u(t,x)~=~w(t,x)+\varphi^{(w)}(t,x),

(2.5)

w(t,\cdot)

\varphi^{(w)}(t,0)=0
for all

\sigma^{(w)}(t)~\doteq~w^{-}(t)-w^{+}(t),\qquad\text{where}\ \ w^{\pm}(t)\,\doteq\,w(t,0\pm),

b^{(w)}_{\pm}(t)~\doteq~b^{(\omega)}(t,0\pm),\qquad~~b^{(w)}(t,x)~\doteq~f^{\prime}(w(t,x))-{f(w^{-}(t))-f(w^{+}(t))\over w^{-}(t)-w^{+}(t)}\,.

(2.7)

Calling

\Lambda\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
as the antiderivative of

\Lambda(x)~=~\int_{0}^{x}K_{1}(y)dy+\Lambda_{2}(x),

(2.8)

with
\Lambda_{2}
being the even function as the antiderivative of

such that

\Lambda_{2}(x)~=~-\left(\displaystyle\int_{x}^{2}K(y)dy\right)\cdot\chi_{]0,2]}(x)+\left(\displaystyle\int_{2}^{x}K(y)dy\right)\cdot\chi_{]2,\infty[}(x),\ x\in]0,\infty[.

(2.9)

In order to make an appropriate ansatz for the function
\varphi^{(w)}
, we make use of the following property.

Lemma 2.1

If
g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
with
\mathrm{supp}(g)\subseteq[-2,2]
, then for every
x\neq 0
we have

{\bf G}[g](x)~=~\int_{-2}^{2}g^{\prime}(y)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)~dy+\big[g(0+)-g(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda(x).

Proof.
We consider the case
0<x<2
, the others being similar. Integrating by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{-2}^{2}g^{\prime}(y)&\cdot\Lambda(x-y)dy~=~\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left[\int_{-2}^{-\varepsilon}+\int_{\varepsilon}^{x-\varepsilon}+\int_{x+\varepsilon}^{2}\right]g^{\prime}(y)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)dy\\
&=~\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left(\left[\int_{-2}^{-\varepsilon}+\int_{\varepsilon}^{x-\varepsilon}+\int_{x+\varepsilon}^{2}\right]g(y)\cdot K(x-y)dy+g(-\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(x+\varepsilon)-g(\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(x-\varepsilon)\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left[g(x-\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(\varepsilon)-g(x+\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(-\varepsilon)\right]\\
&=~{\bf G}[g](x)-\big[g(0+)-g(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda(x)+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left[g(x-\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(\varepsilon)-g(x+\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(-\varepsilon)\right].\end{split}

From the assumption
(H1)
-
(H2)
and (
2.8
), one has

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\varepsilon\Lambda(\varepsilon)~=~0,\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\big[\Lambda(\varepsilon)-\Lambda(-\varepsilon)\big]~=~\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}K_{1}(x)dx~=~0,

and this yields

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left[g(x-\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(\varepsilon)-g(x+\varepsilon)\cdot\Lambda(-\varepsilon)\right]~=~0.

The proof is complete.

2.3
Construction of solution

In order to finish the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we construct solutions to the Cauchy problem (
2.14
)-(
2.16
) with initial data satisfying

w(0,\cdot)~=~\overline{w}(\cdot)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}),\qquad\overline{w}(0-)-\overline{w}(0+)~>~0.

(2.17)

Following the analysis in
[
4

, the solution will be obtained as the limit of a sequence of approximations.
Namely, consider a sequence of linear approximations constructed as follows.
As a first step, we set

w_{1}(t,x)~=~\overline{w}(x)\qquad\hbox{for all}~~t\geq 0,~~x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

By induction, let
w_{n}
be given. We define
w_{n+1}
to
be the solution of the semilinear, non-homogeneous Cauchy problem

w_{t}+a(t,x,w_{n})\cdot w_{x}~=~F(t,x,w),\qquad w(0,\cdot)~=~\overline{w}.

(2.18)

The induction argument requires two main steps:

(i).

Existence and uniqueness of solutions to each semilinear
problem (
2.18
) such that

\sup_{n\geq 1}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}~\bigl\|w_{n}(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})}\right\}~<~\infty.

(ii).

Convergence in the weaker norm
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
, which will follow from the contractive property below

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigl\|w_{n+1}(t,\cdot)-w_{n}(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})}<{1\over 2}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\bigl\|w_{n}(t,\cdot)-w_{n-1}(t,\cdot)\bigr\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})}.

(2.19)

The details for these three main steps will be provided in the following sections.

\displaystyle\left|{d^{2}\over dx^{2}}{\bf G}\big[\chi_{[0,\infty[}\phi_{b}\big](x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left|{\ln|x|\over x}\right|+\left|\int_{|x|}^{2+|x|}\Phi^{\prime}(x+b+z)\cdot K^{\prime}(-z)~dz\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left[\left|{\ln|x|\over x}\right|+\int_{|x|}^{2+|x|}\left|\Phi(x+b+z)\cdot K^{\prime\prime}(-z)\right|~dz\right]~\leq~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left|{\ln|x|\over x}\right|.

Case 2.
Assume that
0<x<1/4
. We write

\displaystyle{\bf G}[\chi_{[0,\infty[}\phi_{b}](x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1-b}\Lambda(y+b)\Lambda(x-y)\,dy-\int_{1-b}^{2}\Phi^{\prime}(y+b)\Lambda(x-y)dy~\doteq~-I_{1}-I_{2}.

Since
\Lambda
is
C^{3}
in
[1/2,2]
, it holds

\left|I^{(i)}_{2}(x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\qquad\hbox{for all}~i\in\{0,1,2\}.

On the other hand, we split
I_{1}
into three parts as follows

\begin{split}I_{1}&=~\int_{0}^{x/2}\Lambda(y+b)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)dy+\int_{x/2}^{3x/2}\Lambda(y+b)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)dy\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{3x/2}^{1-b}\Lambda(y+b)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)dy~=~I_{11}+I_{12}+I_{13}.\end{split}

We estimate

\begin{cases}\big|I_{11}(x)\big|~\leq~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}|\ln(y+b)||\ln(x-y)|\,dy~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\big|\ln x\big|\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}\big|\ln y\big|dy~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot x\ln^{2}x,\\[11.38109pt]
\big|I^{\prime}_{11}(x)\big|~=~\left|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}\Lambda(y+b)K(x-y)\,dy+{\Lambda({x\over 2}+b)\Lambda({x\over 2})\over 2}\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\ln^{2}x,\\[11.38109pt]
\big|I^{\prime\prime}_{11}(x)\big|~=~\left|\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}\Lambda(y+b)K^{\prime}(x-y)dy+{3\Lambda({x\over 2}+b)K({x\over 2})\over 4}+{\Lambda({x\over 2})K({x\over 2}+b)\over 4}\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left|\displaystyle{\ln x\over x}\right|,\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\big|I_{13}(x)\big|&\leq~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\int_{{3x\over 2}}^{1-b}(1+|\ln(y+b)|)\cdot(1+|\ln(y-x)|)\,dy~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot x\ln^{2}x,\\[11.38109pt]
\big|I^{\prime}_{13}(x)\big|&=~\left|\displaystyle\int_{{3x\over 2}}^{1-b}\Lambda(y+b)K(x-y)\,dy-{3\Lambda({3x\over 2}+b)\Lambda(-{x\over 2})\over 2}\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\ln^{2}x,\\[14.22636pt]
\big|I^{\prime\prime}_{13}(x)\big|&=~\left|\displaystyle\int_{{3x\over 2}}^{1-b}\Lambda(y+b)K^{\prime}(x-y)\,dy-{9K({3x\over 2}+b)\Lambda(-{x\over 2})\over 4}-{3\Lambda({3x\over 2}+b)K(-{x\over 2})\over 4}\right|\\
&\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left|\displaystyle{\ln x\over x}\right|.\end{cases}

Concerning
I_{12}
, first of all, by a change of variable, it holds

\displaystyle I_{12}(x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\left(\int_{x/2}^{x-\varepsilon}+\int_{x+\varepsilon}^{{3x\over 2}}\right)\Lambda(y+b)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)\,dy=\int_{-{x\over 2}}^{{x\over 2}}\Lambda(x+b-z)\cdot\Lambda(z)\,dz,

and one directly computes that

\begin{cases}\big|I_{12}(x)\big|&\leq~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\big|\ln x\big|\cdot\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}|\ln z|\,dz\leq{\cal O}(1)\cdot x\ln^{2}x,\\[11.38109pt]
\big|I^{\prime}_{12}(x)\big|&=~\left|{\displaystyle\int_{-{x\over 2}}^{{x\over 2}}}K(x+b-z)\cdot\Lambda(z)\,dz+{1\over 2}\cdot\left[\Lambda({3x\over 2}+b)\Lambda(-{x\over 2})+\Lambda({x\over 2}+b)\Lambda({x\over 2})\right]\right|\\[11.38109pt]
&\leq~\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left({1\over x}\cdot\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}|\ln z|\,dz+\ln^{2}x\right)\leq{\cal O}(1)\cdot\ln^{2}x,\\[11.38109pt]
\big|I^{\prime\prime}_{12}(x)\big|&\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left(\left|\displaystyle\int_{-{x\over 2}}^{{x\over 2}}K^{\prime}(x+b-z)\cdot\Lambda(z)\,dz\right|+\left|\displaystyle{\ln x\over x}\right|\right)\\[11.38109pt]
&\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left(\displaystyle{1\over x^{2}}\cdot\int_{0}^{{x\over 2}}\big|\ln z\big|\,dz+\left|\displaystyle{\ln x\over x}\right|\right)\leq{\cal O}(1)\cdot\left|\displaystyle{\ln x\over x}\right|.\end{cases}

From the previous step, we obtain the first three estimates in (
A.1
) for
0<|x|<1/4
.

Finally, observe that
\chi_{[0,\infty[}\phi_{b}
is continuous with compact support and smooth outside the origin. Hence,
{\bf G}\big[\chi_{[0,\infty[}\phi_{b}\big]
is smooth outside the origin. As
|x|\to+\infty
, for
i\in\{1,2\}
we have

\left|{\bf G}\big[\chi_{[0,\infty[}\phi_{b}\big](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot x^{-1},\qquad\left|{d^{i}\over dx^{i}}{\bf G}\big[\chi_{[0,\infty[}\phi_{b}\big](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot x^{-(1+i)},

and using the first three estimates in (
A.1
) we obtain the last estimate in (
A.1
).

Following the same argument in Lemma
A.1
, one can show that

Remark A.1

Given
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, the function

v(x)=\left(\lambda_{1}\cdot\chi_{]-\infty,0[}+\lambda_{2}\cdot\chi_{]0,\infty[}\right)\cdot\eta(x)x

is more regular than
\Phi
. Thus, one can follow the same argument as in Lemma
A.1
to obtain for all
|x|<1/2
that

\left|{\bf G}[v](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\big(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\big),\qquad\left|{d\over dx}{\bf G}[v](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\big(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\big)\cdot\ln^{2}|x|,

and

\big\|{\bf G}[v]\big\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash[-\delta,\delta])}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\big(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|\big)\cdot\delta^{-2/3}\qquad\hbox{for all}~\delta>0.

Lemma A.2

Let
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
be such that

\|v\|_{H^{i}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~M_{i},\qquad i\in\{1,2\}.

Set
D^{(v)}(x)\doteq{\bf G}[v](x)-\big[{v(0+)-v(0-)}\big]\cdot\eta(x)\cdot\Lambda(x)
. Then for every
0<|x|<1/2
and
\delta>0
small, we have that

\begin{cases}\left|D^{(v)}(x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1},\qquad\left|D^{(v)}_{x}(x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}\cdot\ln^{2}|x|,\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle\big\|D^{(v)}\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot{M_{1}},\qquad\big\|D^{(v)}\big\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash[-\delta,\delta])}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2}\cdot\delta^{-2/3}.\end{cases}

(A.2)

Consequently,
{\bf G}[v]
is in
H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
and

\|{\bf G}[v]\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash[-\delta,\delta])}~\leq~M_{1}\cdot\delta^{-1/2}.

Proof.
We first split
v
into two parts

v=v_{1}+v_{2},\qquad\text{where}\qquad v_{1}(x)~=~\begin{cases}v(0-)\cdot\eta(x)&\mathrm{if}~~x<0,\\[8.53581pt]
v(0+)\cdot\eta(x)&\mathrm{if}~~x>0.\end{cases}

For
i\in\{1,2\}
, one has that

\|v_{1}\|_{H^{(i)}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~\big(|v(0-)|+|v(0+)|\big)\cdot\|\eta\|_{H^{(i)}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~4M_{1}\cdot\|\eta\|_{H^{(i)}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})},

and the function
v_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\|v_{2}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~\left(1+4\|\eta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\cdot M_{1},\qquad\|v_{2}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~\left(1+4\|\eta\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\cdot M_{2}.

Step 1.
For every
0<|x|<1/2
, we have

{\bf G}[v_{1}](x)=\int_{-2}^{2}v_{1}^{\prime}(y)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)~dy+\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda(x)=I_{1}+\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda(x).

(A.3)

Recalling (
2.4
), we estimate

\displaystyle\big|I_{1}(x)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|v(0-)\cdot\int_{-2}^{-1}\eta^{\prime}(y)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)~dy+v(0+)\cdot\int_{1}^{2}\eta^{\prime}(y)\cdot\Lambda(x-y)~dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}\cdot\left(\int_{-2}^{-1}|\Lambda(x-y)|~dy+\int_{1}^{2}|\Lambda(x-y)|~dy\right)~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1},

and

\displaystyle\left|I^{(i)}_{1}(x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}\cdot\left(\int_{-2}^{-1}\left|\Lambda^{(i)}(x-y)\right|~dy+\int_{1}^{2}\left|\Lambda^{(i)}(x-y)\right|~dy\right)~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}

for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Hence, (
A.3
) and (
1.4
) yield

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\bf G}[v_{1}](x)~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}+\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda(x)\\[11.38109pt]
{\bf G}^{\prime}[v_{1}](x)~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}+\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot K(x),\\[11.38109pt]
{\bf G}^{\prime\prime}[v_{1}](x)~=~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}+\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot K^{\prime}(x).\end{cases}

(A.4)

Moreover, since
v_{1}
is bounded and compactly supported, as
|x|\to+\infty

\left|{\bf G}[v_{1}](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}\cdot x^{-1},\qquad\left|{d^{i}\over dx^{i}}{\bf G}[v_{1}](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}\cdot x^{-(1+i)}\quad i\in\{1,2\},

and we have

\begin{cases}\|{\bf G}[v_{1}]-\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda\cdot\eta\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1},\\[8.53581pt]
\|{\bf G}[v_{1}]-\big[v(0+)-v(0-)\big]\cdot\Lambda\cdot\eta\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}.\end{cases}

(A.5)

Step 2.
To estimate
{\bf G}[v_{2}]
, we first recall that
v_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. By the continuity of the linear operator
{\bf G}:{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\|{\bf G}[v_{2}]\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot\|v_{2}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1},

and this particularly yields

\big|{\bf G}[v_{2}](x)\big|~\leq~2\cdot\big\|{\bf G}[v_{2}]\big\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{1}\qquad\hbox{for all}~x\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus, recalling (
A.4
) and (
A.5
), we get the first and the third estimate in (
A.2
).

Step 3.
To achieve the second and the fourth estimate in (
A.2
), we split
v_{2}
into two parts

v_{2}=v_{21}+v_{22},\qquad\qquad v_{21}(x)~=~\begin{cases}v_{2,x}(0-)\cdot x\eta(x)&\mathrm{if}~~x<0,\\[8.53581pt]
v_{2,x}(0+)\cdot x\eta(x)&\mathrm{if}~~x>0.\end{cases}

Since
|v_{2,x}(0\pm)|\leq 2\cdot\|v_{2,x}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})}\leq 2M_{2}
, one has that
\|v_{22}(\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2}
. Thus, by the continuity of the linear operator
{\bf G}:{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we get

\big\|{\bf G}[v_{22}](\cdot)\big\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2},\ |{\bf G}[v_{22}](x)|,\left|{d\over dx}{\bf G}[v_{22}](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2},\ \hbox{for all}~x\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, by Remark
A.1
we have

\left|{\bf G}[v_{21}](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2},\qquad\left|{d\over dx}{\bf G}[v_{21}](x)\right|~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2}\cdot\ln^{2}|x|\qquad\hbox{for all}~|x|<1/2,

and

\big\|{\bf G}[v_{21}]\big\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash[-\delta,\delta])}~\leq~{\cal O}(1)\cdot M_{2}\cdot\delta^{-2/3}\qquad\hbox{for all}~\delta>0.

Thus, (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) yield the second and the fourth estimates in (
A.2
).

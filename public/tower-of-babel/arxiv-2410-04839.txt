Lifts of Logarithmic Derivatives
Matthias Grätsch

Abstract
. Consider a sequence of meromorphic functions ( f n ) n (f_{n})_{n} . This paper presents a technique that enables the transfer of convergence properties from ( f n ( m + 1 ) / f n ( m ) ) n (f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n} to subsequences of ( f n ( m ) / f n ( m − 1 ) ) n (f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n} . As an application, we will show that the families of functions with bounded Schwarzian derivative are quasi-normal.



1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

Throughout this paper, we are going to denote the set of all holomorphic functions on a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
as
\mathcal{H}(D)
. Likewise, we will write
\mathcal{M}(D)
for the set of all meromorphic functions on
D
. Moreover, we denote the open unit disk by
{\mathbb{D}\coloneq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\,\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}<1\}}
and the set of all poles of
f\in\mathcal{M}(D)
by
\mathcal{P}_{f}
. Similarly, we denote the set of all zeros of
f
by
\mathcal{Z}_{f}
. Whenever we are counting the multiplicity of such a zero or pole, we will remark “(CM)” and we will remark “(IM)” if we ignore it.

We begin with a simple example to illustrate the main purpose of this paper. Consider the sequence of functions
f_{n}(z)=nz^{2}+1
. Then we get for the following sequences that:

\displaystyle\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z)}=0

converges with an analytic limit.

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f_{n}^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1}{z}

converges with a meromorphic limit.

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}(z)}{f_{n}(z)}=\frac{2nz}{nz^{2}+1}

\displaystyle\!\begin{array}[]{l}\text{does not converge locally uniformly at }0\text{ but}\\
\text{it converges on }\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}\text{ with an analytic limit}.\end{array}

Note that all three sequences converge on the largest part of their domains. The basic idea of this paper is that the convergence of
(f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
to an analytic limit induces (to some extent) the convergence of a subsequence of
(f_{n}^{(j+1)}/f_{n}^{(j)})_{n}
for
{0\leq j<m}
. However, the “quality of convergence” gradually deteriorates for logarithmic derivatives of lower order, which allows for poles in the limit and some points where the logarithmic derivatives of lower order do not converge at all.
To quantify this decline, we need the concept of
{Q_{m}\text{-normality}}
, which was introduced by C.-T. Chuang in
[
1
]
.

Definition 1.1

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a domain and
E\subseteq D
. The
derived set
of E with respect to D is the set of all accumulation points of
E
in
D
. We denote this set as
E_{D}^{(1)}
. Inductively, we can define the
derived set of order k

\in\mathbb{N}
of
E
with respect to
D
as
E_{D}^{(k)}\coloneq(E_{D}^{(k-1)})_{D}^{(1)}
. We define
E_{D}^{(0)}\coloneq E
as well.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We say that a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(D)
is
\bm{Q_{m}}
-normal
on
D
if for each sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, there exists a set
E\subseteq D
with
E_{D}^{(m)}=\emptyset
and a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}
that converges locally uniformly (with respect to the spherical metric) on
D\backslash E
.
If
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
is the smallest number such that
\mathcal{F}
is
Q_{m}
-normal, then we will call
m
the
index of normality
of
\mathcal{F}
.

If a family is
Q_{1}
-normal, it is called
quasi-normal
.

In our example, we get that
(f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}/f_{n}^{\prime\prime})_{n}
is
Q_{0}
-normal (i.e. normal) with an analytic limit. In that case, we are able to “lift this property” and get that
(f_{n}^{\prime\prime}/f_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
is
Q_{0}
-normal as well, but with the limit function having a pole. However, when we are lifting again to the sequence
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
, the index of normality increases by one. In Lemma
2.2
, we will see that this is the maximum increase in the index of normality that can occur when we lift from one logarithmic derivative to another. Thus, in the general case, we get that an analytic or meromorphic limit of
(f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
implies for all
j=0,\ldots,m-1
that a subsequence of
(f_{n}^{(j+1)}/f_{n}^{(j)})_{n}
converges almost everywhere in its domain due to the countability of the exceptional sets. Lemma
2.2
will also show that the limits of these subsequences are always meromorphic and that none of these subsequences converge to
\infty
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a domain,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(D)
, such that
(f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to some
F_{m}\in\mathcal{M}(D)
. Then we get for
0\leq j\leq m
that
(f_{n}^{(j+1)}/f_{n}^{(j)})_{n}
is
Q_{(m-j)}
-normal.

In particular, there exists a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}
as well as an open and dense subset
U\subseteq D
where
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j+1)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j)})_{k}
converges locally uniformly to an analytic limit for
j=0,\ldots,m
.

The example shows that the index of normality does not have to increase when we are lifting. In fact, it is unknown to the author if lifting can produce an index of normality
\geq\!2
, i.e if there is
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
(g_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
, where
(g_{n}^{(m+1)}/g_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to some
G_{m}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
while
(g_{n}^{\prime}/g_{n})
is
Q_{2}
-normal and not quasi-normal.

Note that the convergence of
(g_{n}^{(m+1)}/g_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
to some
G_{m}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
implies that
(g_{n})_{n}
is
Q_{2}
-normal. This is due to a recent paper by S. Nevo and Z. Shem Tov. They have shown for any domain
D
that a family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(D)
that satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\mathopen{|}f^{(m+1)}\mathclose{|}}{1+\mathopen{|}f^{(m)}\mathclose{|}}(z)\leq M\qquad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{F}\text{ and }z\in D

(5)

for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
M\in\mathbb{R}
is already quasi-normal (see
[
5
, Theorem 2]
).
Note that
(g_{n})_{n}
fulfills (
5
) locally for all points
z\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathcal{P}_{G_{m}}
for almost all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
{Q_{m}\text{-normality}}
is a local property (see
[
1
, Theorem 8.3]
), we are able to conclude that
(g_{n})_{n}
is
Q_{1}
-normal on
\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathcal{P}_{G_{m}}
and
Q_{2}
-normal on
\mathbb{D}
. This is why, in the author’s opinion, it is unlikely that higher indices of normality occur during lifting. To illustrate this point further, this paper will also present several additional conditions, in which the index of normality cannot increase during lifting.

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
(and more specifically Lemma
2.2
), we will see that the exceptional set
E
which corresponds to a convergent subsequence
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j+1)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j)})_{k}
depends mainly on the poles of
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j+2)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j+1)})_{k},\ldots,(f_{n_{k}}^{(m+1)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(m)})_{k}
and their accumulation points. Note that all poles of
f_{n}
are also poles of
f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)}
, and the set consisting of them is small in the sense of set derivatives since
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}})_{D}^{(1)}=\emptyset
. So we can say that the index of normality depends mainly on the zeros of the derivatives
f_{n_{k}}^{(j)},\ldots,f_{n_{k}}^{(m)}
. With additional constraints on these zeros, it is possible to characterize some cases where the index of normality does not increase during lifting. One example is the following corollary:

Corollary 1.3

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a domain,
N,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\geq 2
and let
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(D)
be a sequence with:

(a)

\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{f_n^{(m+1)}}{f_n^{(m)}}$}}\Big)_{n}
converges locally uniformly to some
F_{m}\in\mathcal{M}(D)
on
D
.

(b)

The multiplicity of every zero of
f_{n}^{(m)}
is at most
N
.

Then we know that
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
and
(f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m-2)})_{n}
are both quasi-normal and none of their subsequences converges to
\infty
.

As an application of such lifts of logarithmic derivatives, we are going to show the following result about functions with bounded Schwarzian derivative

\displaystyle S_{f}\coloneq\bigg(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\bigg)^{2}.

Theorem 1.4

For any
M\in\mathbb{R}
, we get that
\mathcal{F}_{M}\coloneq\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})\;\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,||S_{f}||_{\infty}\leq M\}
is quasi-normal.

The properties of analytic functions with bounded Schwarzian were already studied in
[
4
]
by
W. Ma
,
D. Meija
and D. Minda. Thus, in the special case of holomorphic functions, Theorem
1.4
can be obtained from
[
4
, Theorem 1.1(b)]
in combination with
[
1
, Corollary 5.9]
.

However, we are going to take a different approach. We will show that the family of pre-Schwarzians
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}_{M}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{M}\coloneq\{f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,f\in\mathcal{F}_{M}\}
is normal and that none of its sequences converges to
\infty
. Then, we will lift this property to
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
and
(f_{n})_{n}
. Here, we are going to use the well-known fact that
f^{\prime}
is zero-free if
S_{f}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
to keep the index of normality low enough. An extensive collection of such results about the Schwarzian derivative can be found in
[
3
, Chapter II]
by O. Lehto.

2.
Auxiliary Lemmas and their Proofs

All results in this paper depend heavily on Zalcman’s Lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
]
, Zalcman’s Lemma)

Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
be a family of meromorphic functions and suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is not normal at
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Then there are sequences
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
,
(z_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{D}
and
(\rho_{n})_{n}\subseteq(0,1)
with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}=0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}z_{n}=z_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle g_{n}(\zeta)\coloneq f_{n}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)

converges locally uniformly to a non-constant
g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
with
{g^{\#}(\zeta)\leq g^{\#}(0)=1}
for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
.

The increase in the index of normality is due to the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a domain,
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(D)
, such that
(f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to
F_{m}\in\mathcal{M}(D)
. Then there exists a subsequence
{(f_{n_{k}})_{k}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}}
, such that
\big(f_{n_{k}}^{(m)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(m-1)}\big)_{k}
converges locally uniformly on
D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
to a meromorphic function
F_{m-1}\in\mathcal{M}(D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}})
.

The usage of Zalcman’s Lemma in this context is a bit reminiscent of
[
2
]
. Some of the techniques we will use can already be found in there.

Next, we introduce a new notion to abbreviate our later reasoning a little bit.

Definition 2.3

If
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is a domain, we say that a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(D)
is
eventually bounded
on D if, for every point
w\in D
, there is a neighborhood
U_{w}\subseteq D
around
w
and there exist
N_{w}\in\mathbb{N}
as well as
M_{w}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|f_{n}(z)|\leq M_{w}
for all
z\in U_{w}
and all
n\geq N_{w}
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
:

Because of
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)}=F_{m}
, we know that the sequence
(f_{n}^{(m+1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
is eventually bounded on
D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
. In other words, for every
w\in D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F}
, there is a neighborhood
U_{w}
around
w
and
M_{w}\in\mathbb{R}
, such that for every
z\in U_{w}
, we have
\big|f_{n}^{(m+1)}(z)/f_{n}^{(m)}(z)\big|\leq M_{w}
for almost all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Next, we suppose that
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
is not normal in some point
{z_{0}\in D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}}
. Then, Zalcman’s Lemma (Lemma
2.1
) states that there are sequences
{(\rho_{n})_{n}\subseteq(0,1)}
and
{(z_{n})_{n}\subseteq D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}}
, as well as a subsequence of
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
(that we will continue to call
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
), such that

\displaystyle g_{n}(\zeta)\coloneq\frac{f_{n}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)

converges locally uniformly to a non-constant limit
g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
.
Now, we can consider a closed disk
B\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, such that
g
has no pole in
B
. Note that there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
(g_{n})_{n\geq N}
is uniformly bounded on
B
, i.e. there is also a constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
with
{|g_{n}(\zeta)|\leq C}
for all
\zeta\in B
whenever
n\geq N
.

However, the Weierstraß convergence theorem states that for all
\zeta\in B
, we have

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\prime}(\zeta)=\rho_{n}\Bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{(m+1)}}{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}-\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}\bigg)^{2}\Bigg)(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)\rightarrow g^{\prime}(\zeta)

and respectively

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}g_{n}^{\prime}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}\Bigg(\underbrace{\frac{f_{n}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)}_{=g_{n}(\zeta)}\frac{f_{n}^{(m+1)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)-\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{f_{m}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)\bigg)^{2}}_{=g_{n}^{2}(\zeta)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\underbrace{\rho_{n}\vphantom{\frac{f_{n}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}}}_{\rightarrow 0}\underbrace{g_{n}(\zeta)\vphantom{\frac{f_{n}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}}}_{|\,\cdot\,|\,\leq\,C}\Bigg(\underbrace{\frac{f_{n}^{(m+1)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)}_{|\,\cdot\,|\,\leq\,M_{z_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{173023}{163840}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle 0$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{173023}{163840}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle 0$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{127117}{163840}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle 0$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{127118}{163840}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle 0$}}}}}}}}-\underbrace{g_{n}(\zeta)\vphantom{\frac{f_{n}^{(m)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}}}_{{|\,\cdot\,|\,\leq\,C}}\Bigg)=0.

This contradicts Zalcman’s Lemma. Therefore, we are able to conclude that
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
is in fact normal in
D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
.

Next, we assume that there is a subsequence of
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
(that we are going to continue to call
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
), which converges locally uniformly to
\infty
on
D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
. Then this would imply that both
(f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
and
\big((f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})^{\prime}\big)_{n}
have to converge locally uniformly to
0
on
D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
. However, this is a contradiction because for all
z\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}\bigg)^{\prime}(z)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(1-\underbrace{\frac{f_{n}^{(m-1)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}(z)}_{\rightarrow\,0}\underbrace{\frac{f_{n}^{(m+1)}}{f_{n}^{(m)}}(z)}_{|\,\cdot\,|\,\leq\,M_{z}}\bigg)=1.

Hence, we know that there is a function
F_{m-1}\in\mathcal{M}(D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}})
, such that a subsequence of
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to
F_{m-1}
on
D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
.

\square

To show Theorem
1.4
, we need an extension of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.4

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{D}
be a set with
E_{\mathbb{D}}^{(1)}=\emptyset
and
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
, such that

1.)

(f_{n}^{\prime\prime}/f_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to
F_{1}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}\backslash E)
on
\mathbb{D}\backslash E
.

2.)

f_{n}^{\prime}
is zero-free for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then we know that
(f_{n})_{n}
and
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
are quasi-normal on
\mathbb{D}
.

If
E=\emptyset
, then
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
is normal and no subsequence of
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}

{\text{converges to }\infty}
.

Proof.

First, we want to show that
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
is quasi-normal as well and that none of its subsequences converges locally uniformly to
\infty
.

Consider
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}
. Then, we can apply Lemma
2.2
with
{D=\mathbb{D}\backslash E}
and
m=1
to
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}
. Hence, there exists a subsequence of
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}
(that we are going to continue to call
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}
), such that
(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}/f_{n_{k}})_{k}
converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{D}\backslash E
to some
F_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D}\backslash E)
. Thus, for
\mathbb{E}\coloneq E\cup\mathcal{P}_{F_{0}}
, we get that the sequence
(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}/f_{n_{k}})_{k}
converges locally uniformly on
\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}
to the holomorphic function
{\tilde{F_{0}}\coloneq F_{0}|_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}}}
. Now, we choose
\mathbb{E}
as the exceptional set for our subsequence
(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}/f_{n_{k}})_{k}
and get that
(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}/f_{n_{k}})_{k}
does not converge to
\infty
on
\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}
. Therefore, to show our assertion on
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
, we just need to show that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{D}}^{(1)}=\emptyset
or respectively
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{0}})_{\mathbb{D}}^{(1)}=\emptyset
.

We assume the contrary. Then there would be a sequence
(p_{j})_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{D}\backslash E
with
{p_{0}\coloneq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}p_{j}\in E}
, such that
F_{0}(p_{j})=\infty
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
(and in particular
F_{0}\not\equiv 0
). However, since
f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}
is zero-free, we know that
f_{n_{k}}/f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and converges uniformly to
1/\tilde{F_{0}}
on some annulus within
\mathbb{D}
. So due to the maximum principle,
1/\tilde{F_{0}}
can be analytically continued to
p_{0}
. However, this contradicts our assumption that
F_{0}(p_{j})=\infty
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

As a final step, we need to translate the convergence of
(f_{n}^{\prime}/f_{n})_{n}
to
(f_{n})_{n}
. We already know that
(f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}/f_{n_{k}})_{k}
is eventually bounded on
\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}
. Hence, for each
w\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}
, there is a neighborhood
U_{w}\subseteq\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}
and
M_{w}\in\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle f_{n_{k}}^{\#}(z)=\frac{|f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f_{n_{k}}(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{2\,|f_{n_{k}}^{\prime}(z)|}{1+|f_{n_{k}}(z)|}<2\,M_{w}

for all
z\in U_{w}
and almost all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Thus, Marty’s theorem implies that
(f_{n})_{n}
is normal on
\mathbb{D}\backslash\mathbb{E}
.
∎

Note that Corollary
1.3
is very similar to Lemma
2.4
. However, we still need an additional lemma to prove it.

Lemma 2.5

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a domain,
z_{0}\in D
and suppose that
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(D)
converges locally uniformly on
D\backslash\{z_{0}\}
to
{F\in\mathcal{H}(D\backslash\{z_{0}\})}
. If each
f_{n}
has at most
p
poles (CM), then we know that
F
can not have an essential singularity at
z_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
F
is analytic in
D\backslash\{z_{0}\}
, it follows that the set of poles of
(f_{n})_{n}

{\mathcal{P}\coloneq\{z\in D;\,f_{n}(z)=\infty\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}}
can only accumulate in
z_{0}
or in
\partial D
. However, by shrinking the domain
D
a little bit, we are able to exclude the case of an accumulation point in
\partial D
.

Now, we denote the poles of
f_{n}
as
a_{j,n}
(CM) (where
1\leq j\leq p
if
f_{n}
has less than
p
poles, then we define
a_{j,n}\coloneq z_{0}
for all remaining
j
). Hence, we get that
{g_{n}(z)\coloneq\prod_{j=1}^{p}(z-a_{j,n})}
converges locally uniformly to
(z-z_{0})^{p}
. Thus,
{(h_{n})_{n}\coloneq(g_{n}\cdot f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{H}(D)}
converges locally uniformly in
D\backslash\{z_{0}\}
as well and its limit is
H(z)\coloneq(z-z_{0})^{p}\cdot F(z)
.

Next, we assume that
F
has an essential singularity at
z_{0}
. Consequentially,
H
has an essential singularity at
z_{0}
too. However, this is a contradiction, since
H
has to be holomorphic in
D
due to the maximum principle and the Weierstraß convergence theorem.
∎

3.
Proofs of the Main Results

Proof of Theorem
1.2
:

The statement is clearly true for
j=m
.

Next, we consider a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})_{k}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}
and suppose that for some
{0<j\leq m}
there is an exceptional set
E_{j}
with
(E_{j})_{D}^{(m-j)}=\emptyset
and a subsequence of
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j+1)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j)})_{k}
(that we are going to continue to call
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j+1)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j)})_{k}
) which converges locally uniformly to some
F_{j}\in\mathcal{M}(D\backslash E_{j})
on
D\backslash E_{j}
.

Now, we can apply
Lemma
2.2
and get that a subsequence of
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j-1)})_{k}
(that we are going to continue to call
(f_{n_{k}}^{(j)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(j-1)})_{k}
) converges locally uniformly to some
{F_{j-1}\in\mathcal{M}\big(D\backslash(E_{j}\cup\mathcal{P}_{F_{j}})\big)}
on
D\backslash(E_{j}\cup\mathcal{P}_{F_{j}})
. Note that
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{j}})_{D}^{(1)}\subseteq E_{j}
and thus
(E_{j}\cup\mathcal{P}_{F_{j}})_{D}^{(m-(j-1))}=\emptyset
. Hence, we get that
(f_{n}^{(j)}/f_{n}^{(j-1)})_{n}
is
Q_{m-(j-1)}
-normal.

By induction, we will finally arrive at an exceptional set
E_{0}
with
(E_{0})_{D}^{(m)}=\emptyset
and at a subsequence
(f_{n_{\ell}})_{\ell}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}
where
(f_{n_{\ell}}^{\prime}/f_{n_{\ell}})_{\ell}
converges locally uniformly to some
F_{0}\in\mathcal{M}(D\backslash E_{0})
on
D\backslash E_{0}
. Note that by construction, we also got for
j=0,\ldots,m
that
(f_{n_{\ell}}^{(j+1)}/f_{n_{\ell}}^{(j)})_{\ell}
converges locally uniformly to some holomorphic function on
D\backslash E_{0}
. So the last claim of
Theorem
1.2
follows with
U\coloneq D\backslash(P_{F_{0}}\cup\overline{E_{0}})
.

\square

Proof of Corollary
1.3
:

Theorem
1.2
shows that
(f_{n}^{(m)}/f_{n}^{(m-1)})_{n}
is quasi-normal and
(f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m-2)})_{n}
is
Q_{2}
-normal. Lemma
2.2
has also shown it is not possible that the limit of a lifted subsequence is
\infty
. So all that is left to show is that
(f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m-2)})_{n}
is quasi-normal as well.
Thus, we consider a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})_{n_{k}}\subseteq(f_{n})_{n}
. We know that there is a subsequence (that we are going to continue to call
(f_{n_{k}})_{n_{k}}
), such that
(f_{n_{k}}^{(m)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(m-1)})_{n_{k}}
converges locally uniformly to some
F_{m-1}\in\mathcal{M}(D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}})
. Similarly, we get that there is a subsequence (that we are going to continue to call
(f_{n_{k}})_{n_{k}}
), such that
(f_{n_{k}}^{(m-1)}/f_{n_{k}}^{(m-2)})_{n_{k}}
converges locally uniformly on
D\backslash(\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}\cup\mathcal{P}_{F_{m-1}})
. So we have to show that
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{m-1}})_{D}^{(1)}=\emptyset
.

We assume the contrary. Then there would be a sequence
(p_{j})_{j}\subseteq D\backslash\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
with
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}p_{j}\eqcolon p_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}
and
F_{m-1}(p_{j})=\infty
(and in particular
F_{m-1}\not\equiv 0
). We will also denote the order of the pole
p_{0}
of
F_{m}
as ord
(F_{m},p_{0})
and consider a neighborhood
U\subseteq D
around
p_{0}
, such that
\text{dist}(U,\mathcal{P}_{F_{m}}\backslash\{p_{0}\})>0
.

Due to Hurwitz’ theorem, we know that almost all
f_{n}^{(m)}
have at most ord
(F_{m},p_{0})
zeros (IM) in
U
. Therefore, we can conclude for almost all
n\in\mathbb{N}
that
f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m)}
has at most
{N\cdot\text{ord}(F_{m},p_{0})}
poles (CM) in
U
. Since
(f_{n}^{(m-1)}/f_{n}^{(m)})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to
1/F_{m-1}
on
U\backslash\{p_{0}\}
, we can conclude that
1/F_{m-1}
has an isolated singularity at
p_{0}
.

Hence, we are able to apply Lemma
2.5
and get that
1/F_{m-1}
has a meromorphic extension to
p_{0}
. Thus,
F_{m-1}
has to be meromorphic on
U
as well. This however contradicts our assumption that
F_{m-1}(p_{n})=\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and we get that
(\mathcal{P}_{F_{m-1}})_{D}^{(1)}=\emptyset
.

\square

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:

We fix
M\geq 0
.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime\prime}/\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime}\coloneq\{f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}f\in\mathcal{F}_{M}\}
is not normal at
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}
. Then, Zalcman’s Lemma (Theorem
2.1
) states that there would be
(\rho_{n})_{n}\subseteq(0,1)
and
{(z_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{D}\backslash\{z_{0}\}}
with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{n}=0
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}z_{n}=z_{0}
, as well as a sequence
{(f_{n}^{\prime\prime}/f_{n}^{\prime})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime\prime}/\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime}}
, such that

\displaystyle g_{n}(\zeta)\coloneq\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta

converges locally uniformly to a non-constant function
g\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
.
In particular, we get that

\displaystyle g_{n}^{\prime}(\zeta)=\rho_{n}\Bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}-\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)^{2}\Bigg)(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)}_{=\,g_{n}^{2}(\zeta)}.

Since
(g_{n})_{n}
is eventually bounded on any compact subset of
\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathcal{P}_{g}
, we can conclude that the sequence
\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{f_n'''}{f_n'}$}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\,\cdot\,)\Big)_{n}
converges locally uniformly to
\infty
on
{\mathbb{C}\backslash(\mathcal{P}_{g}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{g^{\prime}})}
. However, this contradicts

\displaystyle M\geq\big|S_{f}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)\big|=\Bigg|\underbrace{\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)}_{\rightarrow\,\infty}-\frac{3}{2}\bigg(\!\underbrace{\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}(z_{n}+\rho_{n}\zeta)}_{\rightarrow\,g(\zeta)}\!\bigg)^{2}\Bigg|\,\,\,\,\text{ for }\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\backslash(\mathcal{P}_{g}\cup\mathcal{Z}_{g^{\prime}}).

So
\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime\prime}/\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime}
is indeed normal and any given sequence
(f_{n}^{\prime\prime}/f_{n}^{\prime})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{\mathcal{F}}_{M}^{\prime\prime}/\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime}
has a convergent subsequence. As a next step, we would like to show that none of these subsequences converges to
\infty
.

Again, we will assume the contrary, i.e. that there is a sequence
(f_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{\mathcal{F}}_{M}
, such that
(f_{n}^{\prime\prime}/f_{n}^{\prime})_{n}
converges locally uniformly to
\infty
on
\mathbb{D}
. This would imply that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}=0\quad\text{and with the Weierstraß convergence theorem}\quad\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}=1.

However, this leads to

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\bigg)^{2}}_{\rightarrow\,0}\,\,\cdot\underbrace{S_{f_{n}}\vphantom{\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\bigg)^{2}}}_{||\,\cdot\,||\,\leq\,M}=0

as well as

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\bigg)^{2}\cdot S_{f_{n}}=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\bigg)^{2}\Bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\bigg(\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)^{2}\Bigg)=\underbrace{\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}\frac{f_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{f_{n}^{\prime\prime}}}_{=\,1}-\frac{3}{2}=-\frac{1}{2},

which is a contradiction.

So any sequence
(h_{n}^{\prime\prime}/h_{n}^{\prime})_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime\prime}/\mathcal{F}_{M}^{\prime}
has a subsequence
(h_{n_{k}}^{\prime\prime}/h_{n_{k}}^{\prime})_{k}
that converges locally uniformly to some
F_{1}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{D})
.

Note that functions with holomorphic Schwarzians have zero-free derivatives. Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.4
to
(h_{n_{k}})_{k}
by choosing the exceptional set
E
as the set of all poles of
F_{1}
and get that
(h_{n})_{n}
is quasi-normal. Since
(h_{n})_{n}
was chosen arbitrarily, we can conclude that
\mathcal{F}_{M}
is quasi-normal as well.

\square

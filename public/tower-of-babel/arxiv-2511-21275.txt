1.
Introduction

Consider a solution of the Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

(-\Delta+V)u=0

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
V
is a bounded, nonnegative potential and
\Delta=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}
denotes the Laplacian.
We will consider both the linear case (
V=V(x)
), as well as the non-linear case (
V=V(x,u)
).

A general question in the theory of unique continuation at infinity of solutions of linear PDE is the following: Assuming that there is a solution
u
of (
1.1
) on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, under which assumptions on the decay of
|u(x)|
, as
|x|\to+\infty
, may we conclude that
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
? This question is closely related to Landis’ conjecture which was first proposed by E. M. Landis in the 1950s
[
6
,
7
]
and which asserts that a sufficiently fast exponential decay should be sufficient to imply triviality.

The special case of nonnegative bounded potentials is of interest in the study of disordered quantum systems and has attracted considerable attention since the work of Bourgain-Kenig on Anderson localization in the Anderson-Bernoulli model in 2005
[
1
,
11
,
5
,
13
,
3
,
4
]
. We note that, due to nonnegativity of the operator
-\Delta+V
, there should be no bound states, hence it is natural to expect an algebraic decay rather than the exponential decay threshold in the sign-changing case.

In 2015, Kenig-Silvestre-Wang proved a quantitative form of the Landis conjecture for nonnegative potentials in
d=2
in the sense that, for any nontrivial solution with
u(0)=1
, satisfying an exponential growth condition, they gave a lower bound :

\inf_{|x_{0}|=R}\sup_{|x-x_{0}|\leq 1}|u(x)|\geq\exp(-cR\log R)

for
R
large enough.

In 2020, Logunov-Malinnikova-Nadirashvili-Nazarov
[
12
]
proved a quantitative lower bound for nontrivial solutions of order
\exp(-cR(\log R)^{3/2})
for general bounded potentials in
d=2
which was subsequently generalized for variable coefficients in
[
9
]
. We also note that the conjecture is false for complex-valued potentials, where Meshkov
[
10
]
showed that nontrivial solutions exist which satisfy a decay of order
e^{-c|x|^{4/3}}
.

2.
Statement of results

We prove the following result in the linear case.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be bounded and nonnegative. Let
u
be a solution of
\Delta u=Vu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Assume that there exists a sequence
(r_{j})_{j=0}^{+\infty}\nearrow+\infty
, s. t.

(2.1)

\int_{r_{j}\leq|x|\leq r_{j}+1}|u|^{2}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\;j\to+\infty.

Then
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Remark
.

We point out the following observations:

(i)

This result implies Landis’ conjecture for continuous nonnegative potentials in any dimension:
what is noteworthy is that a sufficiently fast
algebraic decay
of the solutions along
any sequence of annuli
of constant width is sufficient in this case.

For comparison: the conditions in Thm 1.1. in
[
3
]
require a global decay
O(|x|^{(2-d)/2})
,
d\geq 2
, as well as a super-exponential decay
|u(x_{n})|\leq Ce^{-|x_{n}|}|x_{n}|^{(1-d)/2}
along a sequence
(x_{n})
, s. t.
|x_{n}|\to+\infty
.

(ii)

No global decay condition
for the solution
u
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is needed at all. This is very different from the general case of the conjecture (bounded, possibly sign-changing potentials) where the threshold is an exponential decay:
|u(x)|\leq e^{-c|x|}
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
c>0
large enough.

(iii)

Exterior domains:
An analogue of the above result may also be derived for exterior domains
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
\Omega
is an unbounded subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with piecewise smooth boundary. We refer the reader to section
5
for a sketch of the proof.

We have the following corollary of Theorem
2.1
which gives a quantitative algebraic lower bound for a nontrivial solution.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
u
be a nontrivial solution of
\Delta u=Vu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
V\geq 0
. Then there exists an absolute constant
c>0
s. t. for any
R\in{\mathbb{N}}

\int_{R\leq|x|\leq R+1}|u(x)|^{2}\geq c.

Proof.

See section
4
.
∎

In the non-linear case we have the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
be nonnegative and assume that its zero set
Z_{V}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid V(x)=0\}
is an isolated point set. Let
u
be a real-valued, bounded solution of
\Delta u=V(u)u
. If
u
satisfies criterion (
2.1
), then
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Proofs of Theorems

and

V:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
u\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Let us consider a solution
u
to the non-linear partial differential equation

(3.1)

(-\Delta+V(\cdot,u))u=0,

where we denote by
\Delta=\sum\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}
the Laplacian on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

We have the following proposition which will be key in the proof.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that a real-valued

V(x,u)

(r_{j})_{j=0}^{+\infty}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

(3.2)

Then we have for any ball
B(x_{0},R)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\int_{B(x_{0},R)}V(x,u(x))u(x)^{2}dx=0.

Proof.

Let
r\geq 1
. We introduce a smooth indicator function

\chi=1

B(0,r+3/8)
and
\chi=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(0,r+5/8)

k_{1},k_{2}>0

(an explicit construction and computation of

We introduce the smooth cutoff

0\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla v|^{2}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}v\Delta v

and from the identity

\Delta v=(\Delta\chi)u+2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u+\chi\Delta u

(3.5)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}Vv^{2}\leq-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\Delta\chi)uv-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u)v

where we used

Let

A_{r,w}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\mid r-w\leq|x|\leq r+w\}.

We estimate the first term on the r.h.s. by (recall

)

(3.6)

k_{2}\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/8}}u^{2}

and the second term by

(3.7)

k_{1}\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/8}}|\nabla u||u|\leq k_{1}\left(\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/8}}u^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/8}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

We will make use of the following inequality which is due to Caccioppoli and whose proof we include in subsection
6.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.1
). If
V(x,u)
depends on
u
, then assume
|u(x)|\leq C
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
V(\cdot,u)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and that
V
is continuous with respect to
u
. If
V(x,u)=V(x)
, then we assume only
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Under these assumptions we have for any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(3.8)

\int_{B(x_{0},1/4)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C(V)\int_{B(x_{0},1/2)}u^{2},

where
C(V)
denotes a positive constant which depends only on the potential
V

We may choose a finite set of points
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}\subset{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}_{r+1/2}
s. t.

(3.9)

A_{r+1/2,1/8}\subset\bigcup_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}}B(x_{i},1/4)

and there exists an absolute constants
C_{d}>0
such that any ball
B(x_{i},1/2)

C_{d}

with
x_{j}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{r}
. In particular, this constant does not depend on the radius
r
.

Hence we have, by the above inclusion and an application of Lemma

\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/8}}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}\int_{B(x_{i},1/4)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C(V)\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}\int_{B(x_{i},1/2)}u^{2}.

(3.11)

\begin{split}\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}}\int_{B(x_{i},1/2)}u^{2}&=\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)^{2}\operatorname{ind}(x;B(x_{i},1/2))dx\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u(x)^{2}h_{r}(x)dx\end{split}

where

0,\;\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\end{split}

\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}}\int_{B(x_{i},1/2)}u^{2}\leq C_{d}\int_{\operatorname{supp}h_{r}}u^{2}

Now, we have the trivial inclusion

\operatorname{supp}h_{r}=\bigcup_{x_{\mathrm{i}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{r}}B(x_{i},1/2)\subset A_{r+1/2,1/2}

which yields

(3.14)

\sum_{x_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}}\int_{B(x_{i},1/2)}u^{2}\leq C_{d}\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/2}}u^{2}

\int_{A_{r+1/2,1/8}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C(V)C_{d}\int_{r\leq|x|\leq r+1}u^{2}.

The linear case: Proof of Theorem
2.1
.
V(x,u)=V(x)
.

We recall that
V\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is nonnegative and
V\neq 0
. Let us consider a complex-valued solution
u
which satisfies

\int_{r_{j}\leq|x|\leq r_{j}+1}|u|^{2}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\;j\to+\infty.

If we denote
u=f+ig
, where
f
and
g
denote the real and imaginary parts of
u
, then
f
,
g
are real-valued solutions which satisfy

\int_{r_{j}\leq|x|\leq r_{j}+1}g^{2},\quad\int_{r_{j}\leq|x|\leq r_{j}+1}f^{2}\to 0,\quad\text{as}\;j\to+\infty.

Because
V\neq 0
and
V
is nonnegative, there exists a ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
where
V>0
. It follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\int_{B}Vf^{2}=\int_{B}Vg^{2}=0

which implies that
f,g\equiv 0
on
B
and, thus,
u\equiv 0
on
B
.

Since
u
satisfies the inequality
|\Delta u|\leq\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|u|
, we may apply a classical unique continuation theorem, originally due to Carleman (cf.
[
2
]
, see also section 1.5.2 in
[
8
]
), and conclude that
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

The non-linear case: Proof of Theorem
2.3
.
V(x,u)=V(u)
.

Let us assume that the zero set
Z_{V}=\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}\mid V(y)=0\}
is an isolated point set.
If
|u(x)|\leq C
, then we get for any ball
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

\int_{B}V(u)u^{2}=0

and we conclude that
u=0
unless
u(x)\in Z_{V}\setminus\{0\}
. Since
u
is continuous and
Z_{V}
is an isolated point set, it follows that
u\equiv const
. In particular, the assumption

\int_{r_{j}\leq|x|\leq r_{j}+1}u^{2}\to 0

implies
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

4.
Proof of Corollary
2.2

We want to show that there exists
\epsilon>0
s. t. for any
R\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

I(R):=\int_{R\leq|x|\leq R+1}|u|^{2}\geq\epsilon.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
R\in{\mathbb{N}}
s. t.
I(R)<\epsilon
.
Choose a sequence
(\epsilon_{j})\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
. For any
\epsilon_{j}>0
there exists
R_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
s. t.
I(R_{j})<\epsilon_{j}\searrow 0
.

Note that the sequence
(R_{j})\subset{\mathbb{N}}
obtained in this way contains a subsequence
(R_{j_{k}})_{k}
s. t.
R_{j_{k}}\nearrow+\infty
, as
k\to+\infty
. To see this, assume this is not the case. Hence,
(R_{j})
is bounded and, thus, can only take a finite number of values. In particular, there exists a constant subsequence
(R_{j_{l}})_{l}
s. t.
R_{j_{l}}=R
and
I(R_{j_{l}})=I(R)<\epsilon_{j_{l}}\to 0
, as
l\to+\infty
, and thus we conclude
I(R)=0
. This implies that
u\equiv 0
on the annulus
R\leq|x|\leq R+1
and, by Carleman’s unique continuation theorem, we have
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. However, we assumed
u
to be non-trivial, so this gives a contradiction.

Upon reordering the subsequence
(R_{j_{k}})_{k}
we obtain a sequence
(r_{k})\subset{\mathbb{N}}
,
r_{k}\nearrow+\infty
, s. t.
I(r_{k})\to 0
. Now we may apply Theorem
2.1
to conclude that
u\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
which is a contradiction to our assumption of non-triviality of
u
. Hence, we have proved the claim.

5.
Exterior domains

Let
\Omega
be any unbounded subset with piecewise smooth boundary. Let us consider a real-valued solution
u
of the Dirichlet problem

(5.1)

\Delta u=Vu,\quad u|_{\partial\Omega}=0.

We may define the same smooth cutoff
v=\chi u
as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Then we have the identity

(5.2)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}=-\int_{\Omega}v\Delta v

where we note that the boundary terms vanish due to compact support as well as due to the boundary condition
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

We have

\Delta v=\chi\Delta u+2\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u+(\Delta\chi)u

and using the fact that
u
solves
\Delta u=Vu
we have

(5.3)

\int_{\Omega}Vv^{2}\leq-2\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla u)\chi u-\int_{\Omega}(\Delta\chi)u\chi u

If we follow the same argument using the Caccioppoli inequality as in the proof of Theorem
2.1
then we can prove that the l.h.s. of equation (
5.3
) must vanish if there exists a sequence
(r_{j})
,
r_{j}\nearrow+\infty
, s. t.

(5.4)

\int_{r_{j}\leq|x|\leq r_{j}+1,\,x\in\Omega}u(x)^{2}dx\to 0,\quad\text{as}\;r_{j}\to+\infty.

6.1.
Proof of Lemma
3.2

Take
\eta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
s. t.
0\leq\eta_{0}\leq 1
and
\eta_{0}=1
on
B(0,1/4)
and
\eta_{0}=0
outside
B(0,1/2)
. In particular
|\nabla\eta_{0}|\leq c
for some absolute constant
c
.

Take any
x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and define
\eta=\eta_{0}(\cdot-x_{0})
.

We have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Delta u\,\eta^{2}u=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}Vu^{2}

and the l.h.s. equals

-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla(\eta^{2}u)=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla u\cdot(2\eta\nabla\eta u+\eta^{2}\nabla u).

We rearrange this as

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}Vu^{2}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla u\cdot(2\eta\nabla\eta u)

Hence, we have the inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}|V|u^{2}+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta|\nabla u||\nabla\eta||u|

We can estimate (using the inequality
2ab\leq\tfrac{1}{2}a^{2}+2b^{2}
)

2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta|\nabla u||\nabla\eta||u|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}u^{2}

and obtain

\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\eta^{2}|V|u^{2}+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla\eta|^{2}u^{2}.

Now note that
V(x,u(x))\leq\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},|u|\leq C}V(x,u):=C_{V}<+\infty
, because
V(x,u)
is in
L^{\infty}
w.r.t.
x
and continuous with respect to
u
. Moreover,
|u(x)|\leq C
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
This yields

\int_{B(x_{0},1/4)}|\nabla u|^{2}\leq(2C_{V}+4c^{2})\int_{B(x_{0},1/2)}u^{2}.

6.2.
Construction of smooth cutoff

Fix
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\phi\geq 0
,
\operatorname{supp}\phi\subset[0,1]
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi=1
. We first construct the cutoff on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
:

(6.1)

\Phi_{R}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\phi(t-R)dt.

and we see that
\Phi_{R}
is smooth, increasing and
0\leq\Phi_{R}\leq 1
. In particular,
\Phi_{R}(s)=0
if
s\in[0,R]
,
0\leq\Phi_{R}(s)\leq 1
for
s\in(R,R+1)
and
\Phi_{R}=1
for
s\geq R+1
.

We define a cutoff on a ball
B(0,R)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
by

(6.2)

\chi(x)=1-\Phi_{R}(|x|).

We have
\chi=1
on
B(0,R)
,
0\leq\chi\leq 1
on the annulus
B(0,R+1)\setminus B(0,R)
and
\chi=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus B(0,R+1)
.

We compute

\partial_{x_{i}}\chi(x)=-\Phi_{R}^{\prime}(|x|)\frac{x_{i}}{|x|}

and since the Laplacian in radial coordinates is given by

\Delta_{r}=\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{(d-1)}{r}\frac{d}{dr}

and, thus,
|\nabla\chi|\leq\sup|\Phi_{R}^{\prime}|=\sup|\phi|

and since
\chi(x)=1-\Phi_{R}(r)
with
r=|x|
, we have

\Delta\chi(x)=-\Delta_{r}\Phi_{R}(r)=-\Phi_{R}^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{(d-1)}{r}\Phi_{R}^{\prime}(r)

and, thus,

|\Delta\chi|\leq\sup|\Phi_{R}^{\prime\prime}|+\frac{d-1}{R}\sup|\Phi_{R}^{\prime}|=\sup|\phi^{\prime}|+\frac{d-1}{R}\sup|\phi|,

where we used that
r\in[R,R+1]
, because
\Phi_{R}^{\prime},\Phi_{R}^{\prime\prime}
are supported in the annulus
R\leq|x|\leq R+1
.

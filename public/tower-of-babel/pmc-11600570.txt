Patient satisfaction survey in a public hospital: Remera Rukoma District Hospital, Rwanda, 2023
Authors: Emile Sebera, Celestin Hagenimana, Emile Twagirumukiza
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatient satisfaction is a crucial indicator of healthcare quality, influencing outcomes and providing insights for improvement. This study aims to assess patient satisfaction levels, and associated factors, and identify areas for enhancement at Remera Rukoma District Hospital (RRDH) in Rwanda, where such data was previously lacking.MethodsA cross-sectional study using a mixed approach of data collection was conducted, involving 384 participants (164 inpatients, 220 outpatients). Quantitative surveys and qualitative interviews were employed. Patient satisfaction was categorized as Very Satisfied (> 70%), Satisfied (50–69.9%), or Not Satisfied (≤ 49.9%). Data analysis included descriptive statistics, bivariate logistic regression, and thematic analysis of qualitative data.ResultsThe study comprised 37.8% male and 62.2% female participants. Overall, 52.6% were highly satisfied, 29% satisfied, and 18% not satisfied. Among outpatients, 35.9% were dissatisfied with cashier services, and 46.9% with pharmacy wait times. Inpatients showed 74.4% satisfaction with the admission process, but 69.5% reported delayed test result feedback. Educational level significantly influenced satisfaction, with uneducated participants showing lower odds of satisfaction compared to university-educated ones (cOR = 0.409, 95% CI: 0.186–0.897, p = 0.026). Qualitative findings highlighted issues with wait times, communication, and service consistency.ConclusionsWhile overall satisfaction at RRDH was positive, the study identified areas needing improvement, particularly in communication, wait times, and service delays. Education level was significantly associated with satisfaction level. Addressing these factors, beyond operational efficiency, may significantly impact patient satisfaction. Enhancing communication, managing expectations, and optimizing service delivery are crucial for maintaining satisfaction and improving service quality.

Background

Patient satisfaction is a healthcare recipient’s reaction to the prominent features of the context, process, and outcome of his/her service experience [1]. Patient satisfaction is a crucial indicator of healthcare quality [2], often used to evaluate hospital performance and guide improvements in service delivery [3]. Research consistently demonstrates that higher levels of patient satisfaction are linked to better care quality and efficiency, such as improved treatment compliance, reduced readmission rates, and shorter hospital stays [2]. Patient experience and satisfaction have become central components in the global push to enhance healthcare systems, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where healthcare delivery often faces greater challenges [4].

However, the relationship between patient satisfaction and clinical outcomes remains a topic of debate [5, 6]. While some studies highlight a positive correlation between satisfaction and improved outcomes, others question whether satisfaction alone can directly lead to better clinical results [7]. Despite this uncertainty, patient satisfaction data offers valuable insights for healthcare providers, helping hospitals identify areas for improvement, particularly in patient-staff interactions and service efficiency [3, 8, 9]. Key areas influencing patient dissatisfaction include prolonged discharge processes, inadequate communication regarding post-hospital care, and a lack of respect for patient preferences, which are consistently found to impact overall satisfaction levels [10].

In Rwanda, there is limited data on patient satisfaction, particularly at district hospitals like Remera Rukoma. Studies on patient satisfaction have revealed varying results, with some facilities struggling to meet patient expectations due to challenges such as long wait times, insufficient staffing, and medication shortages [11]. Remera Rukoma District Hospital strives to deliver high-quality care, anecdotal evidence suggests potential areas for improvement. There was a lack of systematic and comprehensive data on patient satisfaction levels and specific areas of concern. Limited understanding of patient satisfaction levels and key areas of dissatisfaction within RRDH, hindering its ability to implement targeted interventions and optimize healthcare delivery to the Kamonyi district community.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate overall patient satisfaction at Remera Rukoma District Hospital, providing a foundation for evidence-based interventions to enhance service delivery. By addressing gaps in patient satisfaction, this research will contribute to optimizing resource allocation and improving healthcare quality in Rwanda.

Methods

Study design

This study was a hospital-based cross-sectional by design. It employed both quantitative and qualitative approaches to data collection.

Study setting

Remera Rukoma District Hospital is a major healthcare facility located in Kamonyi District, Southern Province, Rwanda, approximately 9 km from the main Kigali-Muhanga Road. The hospital serves a wide catchment area, providing both inpatient and outpatient services to various age groups. It has a bed capacity of 160, staffed by 87 healthcare workers, including doctors, nurses, and allied health professionals. The facility caters to an estimated population of 150,000 people, receiving an average of 300 outpatients and 25 inpatients daily. Its services span general medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynecology, pediatrics, and specialized services like dental care and physiotherapy. This diverse range of services positions the hospital as a vital healthcare provider in the region. However, before this study, no comprehensive data on patient satisfaction levels had been gathered at the hospital, making it an important setting for assessing patient satisfaction and identifying areas for improvement.

Participants

The study involved administering a standardized patient satisfaction survey to 384 participants, consisting of 220 outpatients and 164 inpatients. The sample size was calculated using Fisher’s formula to achieve a 95% confidence level with a margin error of 0.05 [12], ensuring the study had sufficient power to detect statistically significant differences in satisfaction levels. Although the study used purposive sampling to achieve the study goals, it may have introduced some potential biases, the sample was selected based on the knowledge of the health status of the participants.

Data collection

Data collection took place from September to November 2023, using a semi-structured questionnaire developed by the hospital’s quality improvement team. The survey was pretested on 39 patients to ensure clarity and reliability. Participation was voluntary, with informed consent obtained from all respondents, including caregivers for patients unable to respond. The in-depth interview was conducted with 20 participants who represented different units. Interviews were conducted by the principal investigator and two research assistants. Each interview took 30–35 min.

Ethical approval

Ethical approval was secured from the hospital’s ethics committee, and data confidentiality was strictly maintained, with only anonymized data used for analysis.

Data analysis

Data was analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Science (SPSS IBM version 25), with responses categorized into three satisfaction levels: Very Satisfied (> 70%), Satisfied (50-69.9%), and Not Satisfied (< 49.9%), based on the relevant literature and practical implication [13]. Logistic regression analysis was used to identify the factors associated with patient satisfaction and the results were reported as significant if the P-value was below 0.05 at a 95% confidence level. Interview records were transcribed and analyzed thematically to identify key concerns and patterns in patient feedback.

Results

Demographic profile of the participants

The study included 384 participants with ages ranging from 18 to 65 years, with a mean age of 41.2 years (± 13.4 years) and a median age of 40 years.

As shown in Table 1, a majority of the respondents were female (62.2%) and married (52.3%). The age distribution showed that nearly half (49.7%) were aged above 41 years, while 44% were between 21 and 40 years. Religious affiliations were diverse, with Catholics forming the largest group (40.6%), followed by Protestants (22.9%) and Muslims (19.5%). Educational backgrounds varied, with primary education being the most common (31.5%), followed by secondary education (24%) and university-level education (20.1%). Occupationally, participants were predominantly employed in the private sector (29.2%) or self-employed (27.9%). Regarding income, the majority (77.3%) earned less than 300,000 Rwf monthly, with 39.8% earning below 100,000 Rwf. Notably, 44.1% of respondents had visited Remera Rukoma District Hospital more than 5 times in the current year, indicating a high frequency of hospital utilization among the surveyed population. Factor associated with satisfaction level among the patients attending RRDH

The bivariate analysis of the patient satisfaction survey revealed that education level was the only statistically significant factor associated with patient satisfaction (Table 4). Specifically, uneducated participants showed significantly lower odds of satisfaction compared to those with university education (cOR = 0.409, 95% CI: 0.186–0.897, p = 0.026). Other variables such as gender, religion, occupation, monthly income, age group, and frequency of hospital visits did not show statistically significant associations with patient satisfaction in this analysis. Qualitative findings

The qualitative findings from the study were derived from in-depth interviews with a subset of 20 participants, focusing on their personal experiences and perceptions of care at RRDH. Thematic analysis revealed three major themes: communication gaps, service delays, and staff-patient interactions. Interview respondents highlighted issues with communication, particularly concerning delays in test results and lack of timely updates about their treatment plans. Patients expressed frustration at not being informed about the status of their laboratory tests, with one respondent stating, “I waited 2 days for my test results, and no one could tell me when they would be ready“(Participant 1). This theme of ineffective communication underscores the need for improving internal communication between healthcare providers and patients to reduce anxiety and enhance the overall patient experience.

The second theme, service delays, particularly at the pharmacy and cashier’s desk, was a major source of dissatisfaction. Patients frequently mentioned long wait times, which some attributed to inefficiencies in hospital workflows. One outpatient described waiting over two hours to receive medication, remarking, “I felt ignored and unimportant as I waited for my prescription” (Participant 2).

Additionally, participants mentioned that staff shortages and stock-outs of essential medications compounded the issue, creating an impression of disorganization. The final theme, staff-patient interactions, brought mixed feedback. While some patients praised the friendliness and professionalism of the nurses, others felt that certain staff members were dismissive of their concerns. This suggests that improving interpersonal communication skills and addressing staffing gaps could be key to enhancing patient satisfaction at the hospital.

Discussion

This study offers important and interesting insights about patient satisfaction in RRDH. The findings of this study showed high overall satisfaction among the patients, these findings are consistent with a study conducted in Kazakhstan [9, 10]. This study emphasized the importance of decision-making, time spent at discharge, and options given for post-hospital care. Similar findings were demonstrated in Hong Kong, where respect for patient preferences was a predictor of patient satisfaction [14]. The majority of the participants (88.6%) were satisfied with the information they were given regarding their illness, these findings are similar to another study conducted in Ethiopia [15].

Overall, 52.6% of the participants were highly satisfied. This level of satisfaction had a higher number of respondents when compared with other studies performed in Debre Markos Hospital, Ethiopia, which was 25% before the intervention [16]. But, compared with other studies performed on patient satisfaction in Jimma Specialized Hospital, Debre Berhan Hospital, and other studies performed in Bahir Dar Felege Hiwot Hospital, with satisfaction levels of 61.9%, 57.7%, and 57.8%, respectively [17–19], the proportion of patients in our study with overall satisfaction was low. This difference may be due to the level of the hospital and/or workers’ capacity and motivation status of the health workers at RRDH.

Different study findings revealed that the relevance of information services given for patients, proper admitting processes, the short waiting time to receive service, and ensuring privacy and confidentiality for services provided by the hospital had statistical associations in other studies [15]. It was found that 48.2% of the participants among inpatients were not allowed to discuss their health beliefs, concerns, and preferences. On the other hand, different literatures also indicate that quality of communication and interpersonal skills is also the key indicator for patient satisfaction [20, 21].

The qualitative findings of this study highlight several areas for improvement at RRDH that align with broader patient satisfaction literature. Communication gaps, particularly regarding delays in test results and lack of timely updates, were a major source of patient frustration, indicating the need for enhanced communication protocols between staff and patients. These gaps, as noted by previous studies, can increase patient anxiety and diminish overall satisfaction with care [22, 23]. Additionally, service delays at the pharmacy and cashier’s desk were frequently mentioned, underscoring inefficiencies in hospital operations and the potential impact of staff shortages. These delays not only affect patient satisfaction but may also lead to decreased trust in the hospital’s ability to provide timely and efficient care. Finally, mixed reviews on staff-patient interactions suggest that while many staff members are perceived positively, there is room for improvement in ensuring consistent, respectful communication across all healthcare providers. Addressing these issues could significantly enhance patient experiences and overall satisfaction.

In bivariate analysis, it was found that uneducated people have lower odds of satisfaction compared to patients with university-level education. This aligns with the study conducted in Malaysia showing that at least secondary education was more likely to experience satisfaction compared to those without formal education [24]. These findings highlight the need for regular health education sessions as far as patient satisfaction is concerned. The near-significant result for religious affiliation, particularly the higher satisfaction among Protestants, compares with the findings from Ghana where Muslim respondents were more satisfied than other religions [25]. These differences warrant further investigation and suggest that further research into the role of religious affiliation could be valuable.

Conclusion

The findings of this study indicate that while patient satisfaction at Remera Rukoma District Hospital is generally positive, with the majority of the participants reporting high satisfaction, significant areas for improvement remain. Education level is significantly associated with satisfaction level. Key concerns include communication gaps, especially around timely feedback on test results, and delays in essential services, particularly at the pharmacy and cashier’s desk. These issues, coupled with staff shortages and medication stock-outs, contribute to patient dissatisfaction. To address these challenges, it is recommended that RRDH implements strategies to streamline internal communication, reduce service delays, and enhance staff-patient interactions. Improving workflow efficiency, increasing staffing levels where necessary, and adopting clearer communication protocols can significantly enhance patient satisfaction. Additionally, regular patient education and ongoing feedback should be integrated into the hospital’s quality improvement processes to ensure continuous service enhancement and better patient outcomes.

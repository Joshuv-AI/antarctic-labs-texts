Weak conditional propagation of chaos for systems of interacting particles with nearly stable jumps
Eva Löcherbach ??, Dasha Loukianova ??, Elisa Marini ??

Abstract
We consider a system of N N interacting particles, described by SDEs driven by Poisson random measures, where the coefficients depend on the empirical measure of the system. Every particle jumps with a jump rate depending on its position. When this happens, all the other particles of the system receive a small random kick which is distributed according to a heavy-tailed random variable belonging to the domain of attraction of an α \alpha -stable law and scaled by N − 1 / α , N^{-1/\alpha}, where 0 < α < 2 . 0<\alpha<2. We call these jumps collateral jumps . Moreover, in case 0 < α < 1 , 0<\alpha<1, the jumping particle itself undergoes a macroscopic, main jump. Such systems appear in the modeling of large neural networks, such as the human brain. Using a representation of the collateral jump sum as a time-changed random walk, we prove the convergence in law, in Skorokhod space, of this system to a limit infinite-exchangeable system of SDEs driven by a common stable process. This stable process arises due to the stable central limit theorem, and the particles in the limit system are independent and identically distributed, conditionally on that. That is, the N N -particle system exhibits the conditional propagation of chaos property. MSC2020: 60E07; 60G52; 60F05; 60B10; 60K35

Introduction

In this paper, we study the weak convergence in the large population limit of the piecewise deterministic Markov process
X^{N}=(X^{N,i})_{i=1}^{N}
on
{I\!\!R}^{N}
evolving according to the system of stochastic differential equations

X^{N,i}_{t}=X^{i}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(X^{N,i}_{s},\mu_{s}^{N})ds+\int_{[0,t]\times{I\!\!R}_{+}\times{I\!\!R}}\psi(X^{N,i}_{s-},\mu_{s-}^{N}){\mathbbm{1}}_{\{z\leq f(X^{N,i}_{s-})\}}\pi^{i}(ds,dz,du)\\
+\frac{1}{{N}^{1/\alpha}}\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{[0,t]\times{I\!\!R}_{+}\times{I\!\!R}}u{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{z\leq f(X^{N,j}_{s-})\}}\pi^{j}(ds,dz,du),t\geq 0,1\leq i\leq N.

(1)

In the above equation,
\mu_{t}^{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{t}^{N,i}}
is the empirical measure of the system at time
t
,
(\pi^{i})_{i=1,\ldots,N}
is a family of independent Poisson random measures on
{I\!\!R}_{+}\times{I\!\!R}_{+}\times{I\!\!R}
with intensity
dsdz\nu(du),
and
\nu
is a law belonging to the domain of attraction of a strictly stable law of index
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}.
In the sequel, we will also call such a law a nearly stable law. We are interested in the behavior of such systems as
N\to\infty.

System (
1
) describes a family of
N
particles taking values in
{I\!\!R}
with the following behavior:

1.

Any particle
i
jumps at a rate
f
depending on the position of the particle.
When such a jump happens, we say that particle
i
performs a
main jump
, and its position changes by the value taken by the function
\psi
. This jump height may also depend on the current state of the empirical measure of the whole system. In what follows, we shall assume that both
f
and
\psi
are bounded Lipschitz continuous functions.

2.

At the same time, the main jump of particle
i
triggers
simultaneous jumps
of all the other particles
j\neq i
: when some particle
i
has a main jump, the positions of all other particles
j\neq i
in the system are changed by the same random amount
u\sim\nu
, rescaled by
N^{1/\alpha}
. Borrowing the terminology coined in
[
2
]
, we say that the other particles perform a
collateral jump
.

3.

In between consecutive jumps, each particle follows a deterministic flow with drift
b
, which may also depend on the empirical measure of the system.
b
is supposed to be bounded and Lipschitz continuous as well.

The interest in interacting particle systems whose components can jump simultaneously is motivated for instance by neuroscience applications, see e.g.
[
6
,
8
,
10
,
24
,
18
]
. In this context, collateral jumps correspond to the synaptic weight of a neuron on its postsynaptic partners, and main jumps to the hyperpolarization of a neuron after a spike.

Models similar to (
1
) appeared in
[
10
]
and
[
17
]
,
where simultaneous jumps were deterministic and rescaled by
1/N,
leading, in the infinite-volume limit, to the disappearance of the inter-particle interactions and to asymptotic independence of the particles (propagation of chaos). The articles
[
12
,
14
,
13
]
further investigated system (
6
) in the case
\alpha=2
when
\nu
belongs to the domain of attraction of a normal distribution and when jumps are rescaled by
N^{-1/2}.

In
[
23
]
and the companion paper
[
22
]
, we generalized these previous works to the setting
\alpha\in(0,2)\setminus\{1\}
and we
provided explicit error bounds for the strong convergence of the
N
-particle system (
1
) to its corresponding limit dynamics. This limit dynamics is an infinite-exchangeable system
(\bar{X}^{i})_{i\geq 1}
following a conditional McKean-Vlasov equation given by

\bar{X}^{i}_{t}=X^{i}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}b(\bar{X}^{i}_{s},\bar{\mu}_{s})ds+\int_{[0,t]\times{I\!\!R}_{+}}\psi(\bar{X}^{i}_{s-},\bar{\mu}_{s-}){\mathbbm{1}}_{\{z\leq f(\bar{X}^{i}_{s-})\}}\bar{\pi}^{i}(ds,dz)\\
+\int_{[0,t]}\left(\bar{\mu}_{s-}(f)\right)^{1/\alpha}dS_{s}^{\alpha},\qquad i\geq 1,

(2)

with
(\bar{\pi}^{i})_{i\geq 1}
a family of independent Poisson random measures on
{I\!\!R}_{+}\times{I\!\!R}_{+}
with Lebesgue intensity,
S^{\alpha}
a strictly
\alpha
-stable process independent of them and of the i.i.d. initial values
(X^{i}_{0})_{i\geq 1}.
Moreover,
\bar{\mu}_{s}={\mathcal{L}}(\bar{X}^{1}_{s}|S^{\alpha}_{u},u\leq s)
is the directing measure of the system (see
[
1
]
Definition (2.6) and Theorem
6.1
below), and for technical reasons we assume that
\psi(\cdot)\equiv 0
for
\alpha>1
. In
[
23
]
and
[
22
]
, we have also provided sufficient conditions on the parameters
b,\psi,f
of the system to ensure the well-posedness of (
2
), and we will work under these conditions hereafter.

By strong error bounds we mean that we provided, for any fixed
t>0,
a coupled construction of
X_{t}^{N,i}
and
\bar{X}^{i}_{t},
on the same probability space, and that we obtained an explicit control of
I\!\!E(d(X_{t}^{N,i},\bar{X}^{i}_{t})),
where
d(x,y)
is a convenient distance on
{I\!\!R},
depending on
\alpha.

The present paper is devoted to a weak convergence approach on the path space
D({I\!\!R}_{+},{I\!\!R}),
without aiming at obtaining any convergence rate. We work under weaker assumptions on the law
\nu
of the collateral jumps and fully exploit the underlying probabilistic features of the process.

Our main result, Theorem
3.2
, states that the sequence of empirical measures
(\mu^{N})_{N}
of (
1
) on the path space
D({I\!\!R}_{+},{I\!\!R})
converges in law to the directing measure
\bar{\mu}
of the limit system (
2
).
This implies (Proposition (7.20) in
[
1
]
) that the joint law
{\mathcal{L}((X^{N,i})_{i\leq k})}
of any
k
-tuple of particles in the finite system converges to that of the corresponding
k
-tuple in the limit system (
2
), that is,

\lim_{N\to+\infty}{\mathcal{L}((X^{N,i})_{i\leq k})}=\int_{\mathcal{P}(D({I\!\!R}_{+},{I\!\!R}))}m^{\otimes k}Q(dm),\mbox{ where }Q={\mathcal{L}(\bar{\mu})},

and where
\mathcal{P}(D({I\!\!R}_{+},{I\!\!R}))
denotes the set of all probability measures on
D({I\!\!R}_{+},{I\!\!R}).

In particular, system (
1
) exhibits the
conditional propagation of chaos property
: in the limit when
N\to+\infty
, particles become conditionally independent and identically distributed, given the source of common noise. In our model, this source of common noise is the presence of the stable process
S^{\alpha}.

Conditional propagation of chaos phenomena have been largely studied in the literature. Very often the source of common noise represents some environmental noise. In most studies, this environmental noise is given by the presence of a Brownian motion that drives every particle’s dynamics, and it is usually already present at the level of the finite particle system (see
[
9
,
7
,
11
]
). To our best knowledge, the situation where the common noise only appears in the limit dynamic is completely new and has only been studied in
[
12
,
13
]
, following a weak approach, and in
[
14
,
23
,
22
]
, following a strong approach.

Let us compare the approach presented in this article with the proof strategy of
[
12
,
13
]
, which both worked in a diffusive setting. The main point of these articles was, once the tightness of the sequence of empirical measures was established, to propose a roadmap to identify any possible limit law. This was done by showing that any sub-sequential limit solves an
ad hoc
martingale problem. Exploiting the exchangeability, the conclusion then followed proving that the solution to this martingale problem is unique.

On the contrary to this strategy, we believe that the new approach we propose here reflects more accurately the true probabilistic reason for the weak convergence of our system, both in case
0<\alpha<2
and
\alpha=2.
The main point is that we are able to prove that the sum of the collateral jumps

\frac{1}{{N}^{1/\alpha}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{[0,t]\times{I\!\!R}_{+}\times{I\!\!R}}u{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{z\leq f(X^{N,j}_{s-})\}}\pi^{j}(ds,dz,du)

(3)

converges in law to a time-changed stable process

S^{\alpha}_{\cdot}\circ A_{t},

where
S^{\alpha}_{\cdot}
is a strictly stable process and
A_{\cdot}=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\bar{\mu}_{s}(f)ds
is the integrated mean jump intensity of the limit system (where the mean is taken with respect to the directing measure).
Intuitively, this holds since (
3
) can be rewritten as a time-changed compound Poisson process with i.i.d.
\nu
-distributed summands
(U_{l})_{l}
,

\left(\frac{1}{N^{1/\alpha}}\sum_{l=1}^{\tilde{P}_{N\cdot}}U_{l}\right)\circ\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{N}_{s}(f)ds,

where
\tilde{P}_{\cdot}
is a homogeneous unit-rate Poisson process. The time change
N\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{N}_{s}(f)ds
involves the intensity
N\mu^{N}_{s}(f)
of the main jumps in system (
1
). The stable limit theorem and continuity arguments then allow to conclude. This result is rigorously stated in Proposition
5.2
.

While the above result is clearly the core of our proof, it is not sufficient to prove our convergence result. In particular, we have to identify the joint law of the main jumps and of the collateral jump part in the limit. To do so, we rely on semimartingale convergence theorems and the identification of their respective limits by means of their characteristics, relying on the seminal book
[
20
]
.

We believe that the approach that we propose in this paper can be extended to a much larger class of interacting particle systems, including systems that are spatially structured.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we present the model and detail our assumptions and state the main results.
In Section
4
, we prove tightness of
((X^{N,i})_{i},\mu^{N})_{N}
. Section
5
is devoted to the proof of our main result and is structured as follows.
In Section
5.1
we consider any weakly convergent subsequence of
(X^{N},\mu^{N})
and show that its limit in law is
(X,\mu)
, where
\mu
is the limit of
(\mu^{N})_{N}
along a fixed convergent subsequence and where
X=(X^{i})_{i}
is an infinite-exchangeable system directed by
\mu
.
This allows to pass to the limit in distribution in equation (
1
) and to identify the SDEs satisfied by
X
- which is done in Subsection
5.3
for
\alpha<1
and in Subsection
5.4
for
\alpha>1
. These SDEs have the structure of (
2
), but the form of the directing measure
\mu
as conditional law given
S^{\alpha}
still has to be proved. This proof is provided in Section
6
. Here we identify the limit law and conclude by proving that the system of SDEs obtained for the process
X
coincides with the system (
2
). The fact that (
2
) possesses a unique strong solution (
[
23
]
) is crucial here. This allows to conclude that the sequence
(\mu^{N})_{N}
converges in law to
\mu=\bar{\mu}
.
Section
8
contains some useful lemmas and continuity results.

In this section, we will

Proposition 4.1
.

Grant all assumptions of Theorem
3.2
. Recall the definition of

in (
8

((J^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0})_{N}

Proof.

We define the cumulated jump intensity – up to a factor

6

A^{N}_{t}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\mu^{N}_{s}(f)ds.

Notice that
A^{N}_{0}=0

is strictly increasing (since

is strictly positive) and continuous, hence predictable, that

and that
\underline{f}(t-s)\leq A^{N}_{t}-A^{N}_{s}\leq|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}(t-s)

So

is a random variable with values in

Define now the process
(\tau^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
by

\int_{0}^{\tau^{N}_{t}}N\mu^{N}_{s}(f)ds=t,

that is,

NA^{N}_{\tau^{N}_{t}}=t.

Step 1.
Here we show that

(\tilde{J}_{t})_{t}\coloneqq(N^{1/\alpha}J^{N}_{\tau^{N}_{t}})_{t}

is a compound Poisson process with unit rate and jump distribution
\nu.

To prove this fact, let
\bar{N}(dt,du)
be the jump measure associated to the process
N^{1/\alpha}J^{N},
that is,

\bar{N}(A\times B)=\sum_{t\geq 0}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{t\in A\}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{N^{1/\alpha}\Delta J_{t}^{N}\in B\}},

for any
A\subset{I\!\!R}_{+}
and
B\subset{I\!\!R}\setminus\{0\}.

\bar{N}
is a marked point process in the sense of Definition 5.1.22 of
[
5
]
. Its intensity measure is given by

\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(X_{t}^{N,i})dt\nu(du)=N\mu^{N}_{t}(f)dt\nu(du).

In particular, in the sense of Definition 5.1.23 of
[
5

associated to

is given by

\lambda(t,du)=N\mu^{N}_{t}(f)\nu(du),

such that, with the notation of page 174 of
[
5

\Phi(t,du)=\nu(du)
does not depend on

Let now
\tilde{N}(dr,dz)
be the jump measure associated to the time changed process
\tilde{J}_{t}^{N}=N^{1/\alpha}J^{N}_{\tau^{N}_{t}}.
We check that its intensity measure is given by
dr\nu(dz).
Indeed, to see this, let
0\leq a<b
and
B\subset{I\!\!R}\setminus\{0\}.
Notice that
\tau_{a}^{N}<\tau_{b}^{N}
are both stopping times. Let moreover
A\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau^{N}_{a}}.
The process
(t,u)\mapsto{\mathbbm{1}}_{A}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{\tau_{a}<t\leq\tau_{b}\}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{u\in B\}}

I\!\!E[\tilde{N}(]a,b]\times B]{\mathbbm{1}}_{A}]=I\!\!E\left[{\mathbbm{1}}_{A}\int_{]\tau^{N}_{a},\tau^{N}_{b}]}\int_{{I\!\!R}}{\mathbbm{1}}_{B}(u)\bar{N}(dt,du)\right]\\

=\nu(B)I\!\!E[{\mathbbm{1}}_{A}N[A^{N}_{\tau_{b}^{N}}-A^{N}_{\tau_{a}^{N}}]]=\nu(B)(b-a)I\!\!P(A),

which concludes the proof.

As a consequence of the above result,
\tilde{N}(dr,dz)=\sum_{n}\delta_{(\tilde{S}_{n},U_{n})}
is a Poisson random measure, with intensity
dr\nu(dz)
of product form, which implies the independence of the sequences
(\tilde{S}_{n})_{n}
and
(U_{n})_{n}.
In particular,

\tilde{J}_{t}^{N}=\int_{[0,t]\lx@nobreakspace}\int_{I\!\!R}z\tilde{N}(dr,dz)=\sum_{n}U_{n}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{\tilde{S}_{n}\leq t\}\lx@nobreakspace}=\sum_{n=1}^{\tilde{P}_{t}}U_{n},

if we put

a Poisson process of rate

independent of

This concludes the first step.

Step 2.
Using the above notation, let us introduce the rescaled random walk

S^{N}_{k}\coloneqq\frac{1}{N^{1/\alpha}}\sum_{l=1}^{k}U_{l},\qquad k\geq 0.

As a consequence of Step 1, we have the representation

J^{N}=S^{N}\circ\tilde{P}_{N\cdot}\circ A^{N},

(10)

that is,
J^{N}_{t}=S^{N}_{\tilde{P}_{NA^{N}_{t}}}
for all
t\geq 0,

where the unit rate Poisson process

is independent of the random walk

Since
\nu
belongs to the domain of attraction of a strictly stable law, with renormalization sequence

implies convergence in law of

Step 3.

We conclude our proof by showing that the above implies that the sequence
(J^{N}_{\cdot})_{N}=(S^{N}\circ\tilde{P}_{N\cdot}\circ A^{N}_{\cdot})_{N}
is tight. Indeed, the sequence

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mu
the limit in distribution of a subsequence of
(\mu^{N})_{N}
, still denoted by
(\mu^{N})_{N}
. Let

be the infinite-exchangeable system directed by

((X^{N,i})_{i=1}^{m},\mu^{N}){\overset{\mathcal{L}}{\to}}((X^{i})_{i=1}^{m},\mu).

Proof.

We show that for any continuous bounded test function

\lim_{N}I\!\!E[G((X^{N,i})_{i=1}^{m},\mu^{N})]=I\!\!E[G((X^{i})_{i=1}^{m},\mu)]

By density arguments, we can take

continuous and bounded on

and

continuous and bounded on

Suppose

Then by exchangeability,

I\!\!E[g_{1}(X^{N,1})g(\mu^{N})]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}I\!\!E[g_{1}(X^{N,j})g(\mu^{N})]=I\!\!E\left[g(\mu^{N})\int_{D}g_{1}(\gamma)\mu^{N}(d\gamma)\right].

Since

is continuous and bounded, the above expression converges

I\!\!E[g(\mu)\int_{D}g_{1}(\gamma)\mu(d\gamma)]=I\!\!E[g_{1}(X^{1})g(\mu)],

This argument extends to the joint law of

,

, up to an error

as

I\!\!E[g_{1}(X^{N,1})\ldots g_{m}(X^{N,m})g(\mu^{N})]=\\

Taking e.g.,

The following result, showing that weak limits of
\mu^{N}
are supported by trajectories without fixed time of discontinuity, is crucial for our convergence analysis.

Lemma 8.2
.

Grant all assumptions of Theorem
3.2
. Let
\mu
be the weak limit of any convergent subsequence of
(\mu^{N})_{N}
. Then, for any fixed
t\in{I\!\!R}_{+}
, a.s.,

\mu(\{\gamma\in D\,:\,|\Delta\gamma(t)|\neq 0\})=0.

Proof.

Throughout this proof we write
P^{N}=\mathcal{L}(\mu^{N})
and
P=\mathcal{L}(\mu).
The proof is by contradiction.
So let us suppose that there exists
t>0
such that

P(\{\lx@nobreakspace\mu\in\mathcal{P}(D):\mu(\{\gamma\in D\,:\,|\Delta\gamma(t)|\neq 0\})\})>0.

This implies that there exist
a,\,b>0
such that the event
E\coloneqq\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(D):\mu(\{\gamma\in D\,:\,|\Delta\gamma(t)|>a\})>b\}
has positive probability, that is,

P(E)>0.

Now, fix any
\epsilon>0

and define the set of paths
B^{\epsilon}_{a}\in\mathcal{B}(D)
as

B^{\epsilon}_{a}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in D:\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta\gamma(s)|>a\}\subset D.

By construction,
B^{\epsilon}_{a}
is an open subset of
D,
endowed with the Skorokhod topology, which can be seen as follows.
Let
\gamma\in B^{\epsilon}_{a}.
Then there exist
s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
and
\eta>0
such that
|\Delta\gamma(s)|\geq a+\eta.
Consider now a sequence
\gamma^{n}\to\gamma
in
D
. Then, by Proposition VI.2.1a) in
[
20
]
, there exists a sequence
s^{n}\to s
such that
\Delta\gamma^{n}(s^{n})\to\Delta\gamma(s).
So there exists some
n_{0}
such that
s^{n}\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
and
|\Delta\gamma^{n}(s^{n})|\geq a
for all
n\geq n_{0}.
In particular,
\gamma^{n}\in B^{\epsilon}_{a}
for all
n\geq n_{0},
whence the claim.

Clearly,
\{\gamma\in D:|\Delta\gamma(t)|>a\}\subset B^{\epsilon}_{a},
which implies that
E\subset\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(D):\mu(B^{\epsilon}_{a})>b\}\eqqcolon\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b},
where the latter set is open in
\mathcal{P}(D)
(endowed with the topology of weak convergence).
Indeed, let
\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b}
and consider any sequence
(\pi_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{P}(D)
weakly converging to
\pi
. Then the Portmanteau theorem implies that, for the open set
B^{\epsilon}_{a}\subset D
,
\liminf_{n}\pi_{n}(B^{\epsilon}_{a})\geq\pi(B^{\epsilon}_{a})>b
, where the last inequality follows since
\pi\in\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b}.
Therefore,
\liminf_{n}\pi_{n}(B^{\epsilon}_{a})>b
, implying that there exists
n_{0}>0
s.t.
\pi_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, hence the thesis.

Recall that
P^{N}=\mathcal{L}(\mu^{N})
and
P=\mathcal{L}(\mu).
Since
(P^{N})_{N}
converges weakly to
P,
applying once more the Portmanteau theorem,

\liminf_{N\to+\infty}P^{N}(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})\geq P(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b}),

and the fact that
E\subset\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b}
established above allows to conclude that

\liminf_{N\to+\infty}P^{N}(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})\geq P(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})=I\!\!P(\{\mu\,:\,\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b}\})\geq I\!\!P(E)>0.

We stress that
I\!\!P(E)
does not depend on
\epsilon
, by definition of
E
.

In what follows, we will prove that, for some constant
C
that does not depend on
N
nor on
\epsilon,

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}P^{N}(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})\leq C\epsilon,

(22)

for all
N.

Once this is proven, it is easily seen that this yields a contraction.
Indeed, if this holds, then for any
\epsilon>0
,

0<I\!\!P(E)\leq\liminf_{N\to+\infty}P^{N}(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})\leq\limsup_{N\to+\infty}P^{N}(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})\leq C\epsilon.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0,
this yields a contraction.

It remains to show that (
22
) holds. Notice that

\mu^{N}(B^{\epsilon}_{a})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta X^{N,i}_{s}|>a\}}.

So, by Markov’s inequality and using exchangeability,

P^{N}(\mathcal{P}^{\epsilon}_{a,b})=I\!\!P(\mu^{N}(B^{\epsilon}_{a})>b)\leq\frac{I\!\!E[\mu^{N}(B^{\epsilon}_{a})]}{b}\\
=\frac{I\!\!E[{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta X^{N,1}_{s}|>a\}}]}{b}=\frac{I\!\!P(\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta X^{N,1}_{s}|>a)}{b}.

(23)

Recall the notation introduced in (
12
) above. In what follows, we call the jumps of
I^{N,1}
the main jumps of particle
1
and the jumps of
J^{N}-E^{N,1}
the collateral jumps of it. Clearly, the random variable
\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta X^{N,1}_{s}|
can be larger than
a
only if either the largest of the (finite number of) main jumps during
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
exceeds
a
or if the largest of (the finite number of) its collateral jumps during that same interval exceeds
a.

So

I\!\!P(\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta X^{N,1}_{s}|>a)\leq I\!\!P(\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta I^{N,1}_{s}|>a)\\
+I\!\!P(\sup_{s\in(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)}|\Delta(J^{N}-E^{N,1})_{s}|>a)\eqqcolon p_{1}+p_{2}.

(24)

Notice that the number of main jumps of
X^{N,1}
during
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
is stochastically dominated by a Poisson random variable with parameter
|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}2\epsilon.
So we can upper bound
p_{1}
by the probability that
X^{N,1}
has at least one main jump (regardless of its size) during
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
and this latter by the probability that the dominating Poisson random variable equals at least
1,
which yields

p_{1}\leq 1-e^{2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}}\leq 2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}.

(25)

To control
p_{2}
we start by observing that the number of collateral jumps of any particle in
(t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon)
is stochastically dominated by a Poisson random variable having parameter
2\epsilon N|\!|f|\!|_{\infty},
that is, by the number of jumps of
\tilde{P}_{N\cdot}
during an interval
[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]
, where
\tilde{P}_{\cdot}
is a Poisson process of unit rate which is independent of the i.i.d. sequence of jump heights
(U_{n})_{n\geq 1},
with
U_{n}\sim\nu.
So, writing

\hat{J}^{N}_{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{P}_{Ns}}\frac{U_{k}}{N^{1/\alpha}},

we have that

p_{2}\leq I\!\!P\left(\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta\hat{J}^{N}_{s}\right|>a\right)=I\!\!E\left[{\mathbbm{1}}_{\left\{\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta\hat{J}^{N}_{s}\right|>a\right\}}\right].

Let
\phi_{a}(x)
be a continuous bounded function which is
0
for
|x|<a/2
and
1
for
|x|\geq a
, and takes values in
(0,1)
in between. Then

I\!\!E\left[{\mathbbm{1}}_{\left\{\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta\hat{J}^{N}_{s}\right|>a\right\}}\right]\leq I\!\!E\left[\phi_{a}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta\hat{J}^{N}_{s}\right|\right)\right].

We know, recalling (
11
), that we have convergence in law of
(\hat{J}^{N}_{t})_{t}\to(S^{\alpha}_{t})_{t}.
Now, since
D\ni\gamma\mapsto\sup_{s\leq T}|\Delta\gamma_{s}|
is a continuous function at any
\gamma
such that
T
is not a point of discontinuity of
\gamma
(see Proposition VI.2.4 in
[
20
]
), and since
S^{\alpha}
is a.s. continuous at
T=2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}
, this implies that

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}p_{2}\leq\lim_{N\to+\infty}I\!\!E\left[\phi_{a}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta\hat{J}^{N}_{s}\right|\right)\right]=I\!\!E\left[\phi_{a}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta S^{\alpha}_{s}\right|\right)\right].

Since, by definition of
\phi_{a}
,
\phi_{a}(x)\leq{\mathbbm{1}}_{\{x\geq a/2\}}
for all
x\geq 0,
we upper bound further

\begin{split}\limsup_{N\to+\infty}p_{2}&\leq I\!\!P\left(\sup_{s\in[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\left|\Delta S^{\alpha}_{s}\right|\geq a/2\right)\\
&=I\!\!P\left(\int_{[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\int_{|z|\geq a/2}|z|M(ds,dz)\geq 1\right).\end{split}

Taking
q<\alpha
in the case
\alpha<1
and
q=1
in the case
\alpha>1
, by Markov’s inequality and sub-additivity in the case
q<1,

\begin{split}&I\!\!P\left(\int_{[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\int_{|z|\geq a/2}|z|M(ds,dz)\geq 1\right)=I\!\!P\left(\left(\int_{[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\int_{|z|\geq a/2}|z|M(ds,dz)\right)^{q}\geq 1\right)\\
&\leq I\!\!E\left[\left(\int_{[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\int_{|z|\geq a/2}|z|M(ds,dz)\right)^{q}\right]\leq I\!\!E\left[\int_{[0,2\epsilon|\!|f|\!|_{\infty}]}\int_{|z|\geq a/2}|z|^{q}M(ds,dz)\right]=C_{q}\epsilon,\end{split}

for some constant
C_{q}
that does not depend on
\epsilon.
As a consequence,

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}p_{2}\leq C_{q}\epsilon.

(26)

Putting together (
23
), (
24
), (
25
) and (
26
), we obtain (
22
), which concludes the proof.

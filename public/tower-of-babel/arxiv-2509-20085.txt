Bounds for integral moments of quadratic twists of Fourier coefficients of modular forms
Peng Gao, Yuetong Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we obtain under the assumption of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis upper bounds for all high integral moments of sums of Fourier coefficients of a given modular form twisted by quadratic Dirichlet characters. We show the bounds are optimal by establishing unconditionally the corresponding lower bounds for all even integer moments.



Mathematics Subject Classification (2010)
: 11L40, 11M06

Keywords
: Dirichlet characters, modular
L
-functions, shifted moments, lower bounds, upper bounds

1.
Introduction

Let
\chi_{d}=\left(\frac{d}{\cdot}\right)
denote the Kronecker symbol associated to any fundamental discriminant
d
. Here we recall that
d
is a fundamental discriminant if either
d
is square-free with
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, or
d=4n
where
n
is square-free and
n\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}
. Estimating moments of quadratic Dirichlet character sums is an important subject in analytic number theory. For
m>0
, we define the
m
-th moment of quadratic character sums as

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq X}\Big|\sum_{n\leq Y}\chi_{d}(n)\Big|^{m},

where
m
,
X
, and
Y
are positive real numbers, and we denote
\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}
a sum over fundamental discriminants throughout the paper. A conjecture of M. Jutila
[
11
]
asserts that for any positive integer
m
, there exist constants
c_{1}(m)
and
c_{2}(m)
, depending only on
m
, such that

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y)\leq c_{1}(m)XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{c_{2}(m)}.

In
[
5
]
, P. Gao and L. Zhao confirmed a smoothed version of Jutila’s conjecture under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), using upper bounds for moments of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions. More precisely, they proved that for large
X
,
Y
and any real
m\geq 1/2
,

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y;W):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<d\leq X\end{subarray}}\Big|\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{d}(n)W\Big(\frac{n}{Y}\Big)\Big|^{m}\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}},

where
W
is a smooth weight function. Furthermore, in

[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
, they showed that under GRH, for any real
m>\sqrt{5}+1
,

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}+1},

using estimates for shifted moments of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions. Subsequently, M. Munsch
[
13
]
improved the above bound for the smoothed version concerning integers
m
. Under GRH, he proved that for any integer
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle S_{m}(X,Y;W)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}},

which reduces the exponent from
\frac{m(m-1)}{2}+1
to the optimal
\frac{m(m-1)}{2}
for integer
m\geq 2
. This result is consistent with earlier work of M. V. Armon
[
1
, Theorem 2]
, who unconditionally established the optimal bound for
S_{1}(X,Y)
.

Let
f
be a fixed holomorphic Hecke eigenform of weight
\kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The Fourier expansion of
f
at infinity is given by

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}e(nz),

where
e(z)=e^{2\pi iz}
.
In this paper, we are interested in bounding moments of sums involving
\lambda_{f}(n)
twisted by quadratic characters
\chi_{d}(n)
, where
d
ranges over odd, positive, square-free integers. More precisely, we define

\displaystyle T_{m}(X,Y;f):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq X}\Big|\sum_{n\leq Y}\chi_{d}(n)\lambda_{f}(n)\Big|^{m},

where
m
,
X
, and
Y
are positive real numbers.
In
[
6
]
, P. Gao and L. Zhao proved that

(1.1)

\displaystyle T_{m}(X,Y;f)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{(m-2)(m-1)}{2}+1}

for any real number
m\geq 4
.
In a subsequent work
[
8
]
, the authors
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
obtained an asymptotic formula in the smoothed case when
m=2
. Their result implies that
for two non-negative, smooth functions
\Phi,\Psi
that are compactly supported functions on the positive real numbers, we have for large
X,Y
such that
Y\ll X^{1-\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{d}\Bigg(\sum_{n}\lambda_{f}(n)\chi_{8d}(n)\Phi\Big(\frac{n}{Y}\Big)\Bigg)^{2}\Psi\Big(\frac{d}{X}\Big)\sim XY.

Now, the above result together with (
1.1
) seems to suggest that one expects to have for all real
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle T_{m}(X,Y;f)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}+1}.

It is the aim of this paper to show that the exponent
\frac{m(m-3)}{2}+1
maybe further reduced to
\frac{m(m-3)}{2}
under GRH for large integers
m
and that this is bess possible. For simplicity, we consider the smoothed moments and we define

\displaystyle T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi):=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq X}\Big|\sum_{n\geq 1}\chi_{d}(n)\lambda_{f}(n)\Phi\Big(\frac{n}{Y}\Big)\Big|^{m},

where
\Phi
is a non-negative, smooth and compacted supported function as mentioned above.

Our first result concerns with the upper bound for
T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH. For any integer
m\geq 4
and sufficiently large
Y\leq X
, we have

(1.2)

T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi)\ll XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is motivated by the work of Munsch
[
13
]
concerning the moments of quadratic Dirichlet character sums. Our proof also relies on a careful application of the bounds on shifted moments of twisted modular
L
-functions established in
[
6
, Corollary 1.4]
.

We also follow the approach in
[
13
]
to establish unconditionally a commensurate lower bound for
T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

With the notation as above. For any even integer
m\geq 4
and sufficiently large
X
, we have for
X^{\varepsilon}\ll Y\ll X^{2/3-\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
,

T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi)\gg XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}.

Moreover, under GRH, the above bound remains valid in the larger range
X^{\varepsilon}\ll Y\ll X^{1-\alpha}
.

In view of Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
, we see that the upper bound for
T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi)
given in (
1.2
) is indeed optimal for even integers
m\geq 4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Cusp form
L
-functions

We reserve the letter
p
for a prime number throughout in this paper. Recall that
f
is a fixed holomorphic Hecke eigenform
f
of weight
\kappa\equiv 0\pmod{4}
for the full modular group
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The associated modular
L
-function
L(s,f)
for
\Re(s)>1
is then defined to be

(2.1)

\displaystyle L(s,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\alpha_{p}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{\beta_{p}}{p^{s}}\right)^{-1}.

By Deligne’s proof
[
4
]
of the Weil conjecture, we know that

\displaystyle|\alpha_{p}|=|\beta_{p}|=1,\quad\alpha_{p}\beta_{p}=1.

It follows that
\lambda_{f}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
and

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

where
d(n)
is the number of positive divisors
n
. Here the last estimation follows from the bound (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.11]
) that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}d(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

The symmetric square
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
of
f
is defined for
\Re(s)>1
by
(see
[
10
, p. 137]
and
[
10
, (25.73)]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=&\prod_{p}(1-\alpha^{2}_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\beta^{2}_{p}p^{-s})^{-1}=\zeta(2s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n^{2})}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{s}}+\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}})^{-1}.\end{split}

It follows from a result of G. Shimura
[
14
]
that
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
has no pole at
s=1
. Moreover, the corresponding completed symmetric square
L
-function

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=

\displaystyle\pi^{-3s/2}\Gamma(\frac{s+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{s+\kappa-1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{s+\kappa}{2})L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)

is entire and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=\Lambda(1-s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
.

2.2.
Mean Value Estimates

In this section, we include some mean value estimates that are needed in our proof of Theorem
1.2
. We denote
\square
for a perfect square.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all positive integers
n
, we have for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq X}\chi_{d}(n)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}X\prod_{p|n}\left(\frac{p}{p+1}\right)\mathds{1}_{n=\square}+\begin{cases}O(X^{1/2}n^{1/4}\log n),\quad\text{unconditionally},\\
O\left(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}\right),\quad\text{under GRH},\end{cases}

where
\mathds{1}_{n=\square}
denotes the indicator function of the square numbers.

Proof.

The unconditional result follows from
[
9
, Lemma 4.1]
while the conditional one follows from
[
2
, Lemma 1]
.
∎

Our next result concerns with the analytical property of certain Dirichlet series.

Lemma 2.4
.

Define

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}):=&\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\lambda_{f}(n_{i})\prod_{p|n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}(1+1/p)^{-1}\cdot\mathds{1}_{n_{1}\cdots n_{m}=\square},\\
g^{+}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}):=&\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}|\lambda_{f}(n_{i})|\prod_{p|n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}(1+1/p)^{-1}\cdot\mathds{1}_{n_{1}\cdots n_{m}=\square}.\end{split}

We have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G({\bf s}):=&\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}=1}^{\infty}\frac{g(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{m}^{s_{m}}}=\left(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}L(2s_{j},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\prod_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}\zeta(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}})L(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\right)E(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}),\\
G^{+}({\bf s}):=&\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}=1}^{\infty}\frac{g^{+}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{m}^{s_{m}}}=\left(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}L(2s_{j},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\prod_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}\zeta(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}})L(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\right)E^{+}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}),\end{split}

where
E(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}),E^{+}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
are Dirichlet series absolutely convergent for
\Re(s_{j})>1/4
for
1\leq j\leq m
.

Proof.

As the proofs are similar, we focus on the case concerning
g
in what follows. From (
2.5
), we know that
g(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
is jointly multiplicative with respect to all
n_{i},1\leq i\leq m
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}=1}^{\infty}\frac{g(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{m}^{s_{m}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}=0}^{\infty}\frac{g(p^{v_{1}},\dots,p^{v_{m}})}{p^{v_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}s_{m}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}=0\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}=2\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{m}})}{p^{v_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}s_{m}}}+\sum^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\dots,v_{m}=0\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}\text{ even}\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}\geq 4\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{m}})}{p^{v_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}s_{m}}}\right).

Let
s_{j}=\sigma_{j}+it_{j}
. By putting
\sigma=\min\{\sigma_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}
and applying
|\lambda_{f}(p^{v})|\leq d(p^{v})=v+1
by (
2.2
), we see that

\sum^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\dots,v_{m}=0\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}\text{ even}\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}\geq 4\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{m}})}{p^{v_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}s_{m}}}\ll_{m}\frac{1}{p^{4\sigma}}.

Next we factor out the products of the Riemann zeta-functions to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{2k}=1}^{\infty}\frac{g(n_{1},\ldots,n_{2k})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{2k}^{s_{2k}}}=\left(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}L(2s_{j},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\prod_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}\zeta(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}})L(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\right)E(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}).

Then, we can calculate
E(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
as

\displaystyle E(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}=0\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}=2\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{m}})}{p^{v_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}s_{m}}}+\sum^{\infty}_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1},\dots,v_{m}=0\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}\text{ even}\\
v_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}\geq 4\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{1}})\cdots\lambda_{f}(p^{v_{m}})}{p^{v_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+v_{m}s_{m}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2s_{j}}}+\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{4s_{j}}}-\frac{1}{p^{6s_{j}}}\right)\prod_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}}}}\right)\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}}}}+\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}})}}-\frac{1}{p^{3(s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}})}}\right)

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1+\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2s_{i}}}+\sum_{1\leq h_{1}<h_{2}\leq m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}\right)\frac{\lambda_{f}^{2}(p)}{p^{s_{h_{1}}+s_{h_{2}}}}+O_{m}\left(\frac{1}{p^{4\sigma}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1-\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2s_{j}}}+O_{m}\left(\frac{1}{p^{4\sigma}}\right)\right)\left(1-\sum_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}\frac{1}{p^{s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}}}}-\sum_{1\leq l_{1}<l_{2}\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{s_{l_{1}}+s_{l_{2}}}}+O_{m}\left(\frac{1}{p^{4\sigma}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{2s_{i}}(p+1)}-\sum_{1\leq h_{1}<h_{2}\leq m}\frac{\lambda_{f}(p^{2})}{p^{s_{h_{1}}+s_{h_{2}}}(p+1)}+O_{m}\left(\frac{1}{p^{4\sigma}}\right)\right).

Applying (
2.2
) again, we see that the above Euler product is convergent absolutely for
\Re(s_{j})>1/4
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

With the notation as above. Let

,

(2.7)

\displaystyle P_{f}(Y^{\beta_{1}},\cdots,Y^{\beta_{m}})\asymp Y^{\sum^{m}_{i=1}\beta_{i}/2}(\log Y)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}.

Proof.

We define for
m\geq 1
,

\displaystyle P^{+}_{f}(Y^{\beta_{1}},\cdots,Y^{\beta_{m}}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 1\\
n_{1}\cdots n_{m}=\square\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{f}(n_{i})|\Phi\Big(\frac{n_{i}}{Y^{\beta_{i}}}\Big)\prod_{p|n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}(1+1/p)^{-1}.

We also denote
\widehat{\Phi}

and we note that repeated integration by parts implies that for any integer

,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}(s)\ll(1+|s|)^{-E}.

We then apply Mellin inversion to the variables

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{f}(Y^{\beta_{1}},\cdots,Y^{\beta_{m}})=&\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\Big)^{m}\int_{(2)}\cdots\int_{(2)}Y^{\sum^{m}_{i=1}u_{i}\beta_{i}}G(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\widehat{\Phi}(u_{1})\cdots\widehat{\Phi}(u_{m})\mathrm{d}u_{1}\cdots\mathrm{d}u_{m},\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}&P_{f}(Y^{\beta_{1}},\cdots,Y^{\beta_{m}})\\

\displaystyle\begin{split}P_{f}(Y^{\beta_{1}},\cdots,Y^{\beta_{m}})\sim&\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1/2+\varepsilon_{1})}\sum_{S}Y^{\sum_{i\in S}u_{1}\beta_{i}+\sum_{i\in I\backslash S}(1-u_{1})\beta_{i}}\times R\times E(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})\mathrm{d}u_{1},\\

).

Note that

(4.3)

\displaystyle X^{1/2}\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\ll Y^{\varepsilon}}(n_{1}\dots n_{m})^{1/4+\varepsilon}\ll X^{1/2}Y^{(\frac{5}{4}+\varepsilon)m\varepsilon}=X^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

Moreover, we have by Lemma
2.5
that for integers
m\geq 4
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\dots,n_{m}\geq 1\\
n_{1}\cdots n_{m}=\square\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\cdots\lambda_{f}(n_{m})\Phi\Big(\frac{n_{1}}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\cdots\Phi\Big(\frac{n_{m}}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\prod_{p|n_{1}\cdots n_{m}}(1+1/p)^{-1}

\displaystyle\ll Y^{m\varepsilon/2}(\log Y)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}.

We deduce from
(
4.2
)
–
(
4.4
)
that we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}\ll_{\varepsilon,m}XY^{m\varepsilon/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}.

Next we note that by Lemma
2.3
and arguing similar to our discussions concerning
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}=

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{0<d\leq X}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{m-1}\geq 1\\
nn_{1}\dots n_{m-1}=\square\end{subarray}}\chi_{d}\Bigg(n\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}n_{i}\Bigg)\lambda_{f}(n)\Phi\Big(\frac{n}{Y}\Big)\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\lambda_{f}(n_{i})\Phi\Big(\frac{n_{i}}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}X\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\geq 1\\
nn_{1}\dots n_{m-1}=\square\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\cdots\lambda_{f}(n_{m-1})\Phi\Big(\frac{n}{Y}\Big)\Phi\Big(\frac{n_{1}}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\cdots\Phi\Big(\frac{n_{m-1}}{Y^{\varepsilon}}\Big)\prod_{p|nn_{1}\cdots n_{m-1}}(1+1/p)^{-1}

\displaystyle+\begin{cases}O(X^{1/2}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\ll Y\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\ll Y^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\cdots\lambda_{f}(n_{m-1})|(nn_{1}\dots n_{m-1})^{1/4+\varepsilon}),\quad\text{unconditionally}\\
O(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\ll Y\\
n_{1},\dots,n_{m-1}\ll Y^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}|\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{f}(n_{1})\cdots\lambda_{f}(n_{m-1})|(nn_{1}\dots n_{m})^{\varepsilon}),\quad\text{under GRH}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\gg

\displaystyle XY^{1/2+(m-1)\varepsilon/2}(\log Y)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}+\begin{cases}O(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}Y^{5/4+\varepsilon}),\quad\text{unconditionally}\\
O(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}Y^{1+\varepsilon}),\quad\text{under GRH}\end{cases}.

It follows that we have unconditionally that for
X^{\varepsilon}\ll Y\ll X^{2/3-\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}\gg XY^{1/2+(m-1)\varepsilon/2}(\log Y)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}\gg XY^{1/2+(m-1)\varepsilon/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}}.

We also note that the above estimation continues to hold for
X^{\varepsilon}\ll Y\ll X^{1-\alpha}
under GRH.

We now apply (
4.1
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) to see that for even integers
m\geq 4
, unconditionally for
X^{\varepsilon}\ll Y\ll X^{2/3-\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
,

T_{m}(X,Y;f,\Phi)\gg XY^{m/2}(\log X)^{\frac{m(m-3)}{2}},

while the above estimation continues to hold for
X^{\varepsilon}\ll Y\ll X^{1-\alpha}
under GRH. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

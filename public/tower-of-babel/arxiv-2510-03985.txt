Effective linear response in non-equilibrium anyonic systems
Gu Zhang, Igor Gornyi, Yuval Gefen

Abstract
Linear response theory serves as a fundamental tool in the study of quantum transport, extensively employed to elucidate fundamental mechanisms related to the nature of the particles involved and the underlying symmetries. This framework is, however, limited to equilibrium or near-equilibrium conditions. Here, we develop an effective linear response theory designed to describe charge and thermal quantum transport, where the reference far-from-equilibrium stationary state comprises anyons forming a dilute beam. We apply our theory to study tunnel-coupled anyonic beams in collider geometries, enabling braiding, collisions, and tunneling of anyons at the central collider. Our linear-response transport coefficients directly reflect the fractional charge and statistics of the anyons involved, avoiding the need to measure higher-order current correlations. Moreover, the emergence of finite thermoelectric (Peltier and Seebeck) coefficients signifies the presence of real anyon collisions (as opposed to virtual braiding in the time domain), intimately associated with a broken particle-hole symmetry, specific to anyonic gases.

The baseline for such a theory should be an “effective equilibrium”.

Once the possibility of characterizing the stationary far-from-equilibrium system through the effective equilibrium features (

Given that one can construct an effective linear response framework for such non-equilibrium systems, the important effects of fractional statistics can be considered on two levels: accounting for time-domain braiding only or including real anyonic tunneling and collisions as well.
In the present study, we establish the effective linear-response framework for non-equilibrium anyonic edges and show that these two levels of accounting for anyonic features lead to qualitatively different results.

II.5
Collision-induced thermoelectric coefficients

Now, we extend the effective linear-response theory beyond the collision-free limit. We investigate the role of tunneling and collisions of anyons supplied by diluted non-equilibrium beams.
As we already stressed above, beyond the collision-free limit, the particle-hole symmetry becomes sabotaged. This induces finite thermoelectric (Seebeck and Peltier) coefficients and leads to non-universality of effective potential and effective temperature.
Below, we emphasize the inclusion of anyonic collisions by adding a subscript “coll” to observables.

With anyonic collisions included, the non-equilibrium correlation functions, Eq. (
6

I_{1}\sin(2\pi\nu)t/\nu e

As these two factors do not equal each other, the particle-hole symmetry of the collision-free limit [solid curves in Fig.

(b)] is broken, as represented by dashed curves in Fig.
1

Breaking particle-hole symmetry gives rise to nonzero Seebeck and Peltier coefficients, as illustrated by Fig.

III
Discussion

We consider setups comprising non-equilibrium
channel(s) which are tunnel-coupled through a “collider”. This system is tuned to effective equilibrium by requiring that both the charge and heat currents through
the collider vanish.
Our work reveals that, when the system slightly deviates from this effective equilibrium
point, effective linear-response parameters can be
further defined to disclose key features of the system.

As an important observation, in the collision-free limit (corresponding to strong dilution), where time-domain braiding processes dominate,
a non-equilibrium anyonic channel possesses a symmetry between effective particle and hole distributions [solid curves of Fig.
1
(b)].
This symmetry leads to vanishing Seebeck and Peltier coefficients.
In addition, in the collision-free limit, the charge and heat conductances depend universally on the filling fraction
\nu
, through the effective charge, the scaling dimension, and the braiding statistics.
The former two factors cancel out in the definition of the Lorenz number; it universally depends on the braiding statistics only.
Remarkably, in setups of Figs.
1
(a) and
2
(a) (influenced by time-domain braiding), the Lorenz number clearly deviates from both
\mathcal{L}_{\text{fermion}}=\pi^{2}/3
for non-interacting fermions and the value of the setup of Fig.
2
(b) that consists of two equilibrium channels.
The measurement of Lorenz numbers of the setups of Figs.
1
(a) and
2
(a) is thus capable of manifesting braiding of anyons.

However, this symmetry between effective particle and hole distributions is sabotaged by the collisions of non-equilibrium anyons [leading to dashed curves of Fig.
1
(b)], which induce finite Peltier and Seebeck coefficients, cf. Fig.
4
.
Experimental measurements of these coefficients are thus able to reveal the tunneling of anyons supplied by the non-equilibrium beams and collisions among them.

Prospectively, we anticipate extending the study of charge and thermal transport coefficients, defined in our current work for Laughlin quasiparticles, to composite edges, or even non-Abelian systems that are out of equilibrium.
This extension can enable us to extract information on non-Abelian statistics manifested through collisions between non-Abelian anyonic quasiparticles via effective linear-response transport measurements, which appear to be realizable with currently available experimental techniques.

Hamiltonian —

The edge channels in our setups are described by the Hamiltonian

H_{\text{edge}}=\frac{v}{4\pi}\sum_{\alpha}\int dx\,(\partial_{x}\phi_{\alpha})^{2},

The Hamiltonian of the diluter at
x=0

\displaystyle H_{\text{diluter}}=\frac{\xi_{1}}{2\pi\tau_{0}}e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\,\left[\phi_{S1}(0)-\phi_{1}(0)\right]}+\text{H.c.},

(19)

can be written in terms of the anyonic operators
\psi^{\dagger}_{S1}
and
\psi_{1}

\nu

H_{\text{diluter}}=v\xi_{1}\,\psi^{\dagger}_{S1}(0)\psi_{1}(0)+\text{H.c.}

Here,
\xi_{1}

\displaystyle H_{\text{collider}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi_{C}}{2\pi\tau_{0}}e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\left[\phi_{1}(L)-\phi_{2}(L)\right]}+\text{H.c.}

\displaystyle=v\xi_{C}\,\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(0)\psi_{2}(0)+\text{H.c.},

(20)

Indeed, when

As the consequence, the phase factor inside of the brackets of Eq. (

), as well as the prefactor

Eq. (

S2. Resummation over higher-order dilution contribution

In Sec. S1, we have shown zero-temperature correlation functions to the leading order of dilution.
In this section, we go beyond results of Sec. S1, by including influence of higher-order tunneling through the diluter, which, as will be shown shortly, correspond to braiding among anyons.
Here, extra non-equilibrium anyonic pairs are assumed to belong to region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
(thus leading to anyonic braiding), although they influence leading-order pair contributions of all three regions (in Secs. S2A and S2B, for higher-order influence on contributions of Region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
, and that of Regions
\mathcal{R}_{2}
,
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, respectively).
This option is taken, since the corresponding contribution is of the order of
\mathcal{T}_{1}\sim I_{1}/(\nu^{2}e^{2}V_{S1})
(see Ref.
Zhang et al. 2024a
for more discussions), after resummation over dilution to all orders.
Actually, other processes, with extra non-equilibrium anyons in either Regions
\mathcal{R}_{2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, correspond to co-tunneling at the diluter.
They are thus of at least the second order in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, being small in the strongly dilute limit that is visited by our current work.

We begin by considering the next-leading order contribution, i.e.,
\propto\left(\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\right)^{2}
, and assume that two extra non-equilibrium anyonic operators have time arguments
s_{3}
and
s_{4}
, and corresponding Keldysh indexes
\eta_{3}
and
\eta_{4}
, respectively.
Here these two extra operators are assumed to belong to Region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
, such that
-L/v<s_{3}\approx s_{4}<t-L/v
.
This assumption is taken, since the corresponding contribution is larger than other options, where
s_{3}
or
s_{4}
belongs to the other two Regions.
Indeed, when
s_{3}
and
s_{4}
are both in Region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
, the corresponding contribution can be absorbed into leading-order contributions, after performing resummation over dilution to all orders.
In great contrast, when they belong to the other two Regions, the corresponding contributions, which are of higher-order in dilution, can not be re-captured by resummation, thus leading to comparatively much smaller contributions in the strongly diluted limit.
Without the loss of generality, we further begin by assuming that operators with arguments
s_{1}
and
s_{3}
are creation operators; the other two are instead annihilation ones.
Iteration between different options (to pair-up non-equilibrium anyonic operators) will be discussed and included later.

S2A. Higher-order dilution correction to integral of region
\mathcal{R}_{1}

To the order of
|\xi_{1}|^{4}=\left[\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\right]^{2}
, correlation function of operators in channel 1 approximately equal (after ignoring, at the moment, prefactors from Keldysh expansion and number of pairing options)

\displaystyle\big\langle e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(t^{-},L)}e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(0^{+},L)}\big\rangle_{\text{neq,4}}^{\mathcal{R}_{1}}=\frac{\left[\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\right]^{2}\tau_{0}^{4\nu}}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{4}}\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}

(S21)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}\eta_{1}\eta_{2}e^{i\pi\nu(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})}\,\int_{0}^{t}d\tilde{s}_{1}\int_{\tilde{s}_{1}-t}^{\tilde{s}_{1}}\!\!ds\,\frac{\,e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}s}}{[\tau_{0}+is\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(s)]^{2\nu}}\,\left\{\frac{(t-\tilde{s}_{1})\,(\tilde{s}_{1}-s)}{(t+s-\tilde{s}_{1})\,\tilde{s_{1}}}\right\}^{\nu}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\eta_{3}\eta_{4}}\eta_{3}\eta_{4}e^{i\pi\nu(\eta_{3}-\eta_{4})}\,\int_{0}^{t}d\tilde{s}_{3}\int_{\tilde{s}_{3}-t}^{\tilde{s}_{3}}\!\!ds^{\prime}\,\frac{\,e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}s^{\prime}}}{[\tau_{0}+is\chi_{\eta_{3},\eta_{4}}(s^{\prime})]^{2\nu}}\,\left\{\frac{(t-\tilde{s}_{3})\,(\tilde{s}_{3}-s^{\prime})}{(t+s^{\prime}-\tilde{s}_{3})\,\tilde{s_{3}}}\right\}^{\nu}

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{I}(s_{1}^{\eta_{1}},s_{2}^{\eta_{2}};s_{3}^{\eta_{3}},s_{4}^{\eta_{4}}),

where
\tilde{s}_{3}\equiv s_{3}+L/v
,

are two shifted variables, and the function

\mathcal{I}(s_{1}^{\eta_{1}},s_{2}^{\eta_{2}};s_{3}^{\eta_{3}},s_{4}^{\eta_{4}})\equiv\frac{[\tau_{0}+i(s_{1}-s_{3})\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{3}}(s_{1}-s_{3})]^{2\nu}[\tau_{0}+i(s_{2}-s_{4})\chi_{\eta_{2},\eta_{4}}(s_{2}-s_{4})]^{2\nu}}{[\tau_{0}+i(s_{1}-s_{4})\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{4}}(s_{1}-s_{4})]^{2\nu}[\tau_{0}+i(s_{2}-s_{3})\chi_{\eta_{2},\eta_{3}}(s_{2}-s_{3})]^{2\nu}},

(S22)

is the factor generated by the “braiding” between two non-equilibrium anyonic pairs.
Clearly, with our pairing option,
s_{1}\to s_{2}
and
s_{3}\to s_{4}

\mathcal{I}(s_{1}^{\eta_{1}},s_{2}^{\eta_{2}};s_{3}^{\eta_{3}},s_{4}^{\eta_{4}})\approx 1
, meaning that non-equilibrium anyonic pairs, at the moments
s_{1}\approx s_{2}
and
s_{3}\approx s_{4}

\mathcal{R}_{1}
simplifies into

\displaystyle\big\langle e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(t^{-},L)}e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(0^{+},L)}\big\rangle_{\text{neq,4}}^{\mathcal{R}_{1}}\approx\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}

(S23)

\displaystyle\times\left(\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\frac{\tau_{0}^{2\nu}}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{2}}\sum_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}\eta_{1}\eta_{2}e^{i\pi\nu(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2})}\,\int_{0}^{t}d\tilde{s}_{1}\int_{\tilde{s}_{1}-t}^{\tilde{s}_{1}}\!\!ds\,\frac{\,e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}s}}{[\tau_{0}+is\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(s)]^{2\nu}}\,\left\{\frac{(t-\tilde{s}_{1})\,(\tilde{s}_{1}-s)}{(t+s-\tilde{s}_{1})\,\tilde{s_{1}}}\right\}^{\nu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}\left[-\left(1-e^{2i\pi\nu}\right)\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}t\right]^{2}.

Eq. (
S23
) discloses the fact that if we focus on region
\mathcal{R}_{1}
, and assume that all non-equilibrium anyonic pairs are well-separated in space (at any moment), the integral outcome simply equals the product of the prefactor
\tau_{0}^{\nu}/(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}
, and
n
copies of leading-order integral result [cf. Eq. (
S6
), after ignoring the prefactor
\tau_{0}^{\nu}/(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}
], if
n
is the number of non-equilibrium anyonic pairs.

Now we add back Keldysh expansion prefactors and the number of pairing options, to perform resummation over higher-order processes.
Firstly, for
n
non-equilibrium anyonic pairs (corresponding to
2n
anyonic operators), a prefactor
1/(2n!)
is generated, due to the Keldysh expansion.
To pair up these
2n
anyonic operators, the number of options equals
C_{2n}^{2}C_{2n-2}^{2}\cdot\cdot\cdot C_{2}^{2}2^{n}
, where
C_{2n}^{2}=2n(2n-1)/2
equals the number of options to choose the time arguments of the first pair; and

comes from the fact that for each anyonic pair, either operator can be the creation one. Finally, the sequence of choosing non-equilibrium anyonic pairs does not matter, leading to an extra prefactor
1/n!

After including all these prefactors, resummation over higher-order dilution leads to

\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{2n!}\frac{C_{2n}^{2}C_{2n-2}^{2}...C_{4}^{2}C_{2}^{2}\times 2^{n}}{n!}\left[-\left(1-e^{2i\pi\nu}\right)\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}t\right]^{n}=\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}\exp\left[-\left(1-e^{2i\pi\nu}\right)\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}t\right].

(S24)

Importantly, Eq. (

With this contribution, Eq. (
S24

\mathcal{R}_{1}
, is of order zero to the dilution.
Nevertheless, contributions from higher-order tunneling through the diluters are important in anyonic time-domain braiding processes.
Indeed, the inclusion of higher-order dilution, importantly, provides the bound of integral over
t
[due to the exponential factor of Eq. (
S24
)], which is however unclear when considering only the leading-order tunneling through the diluter.

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}i\sin(\pi\nu)\frac{I_{1}}{e^{2}V_{S1}}C_{1}(\nu)\left[-\left(1-e^{2i\pi\nu}\right)\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}t\right],

S3. Correlation functions when the source temperature
T_{S1}
is finite

Eq. (
S32
) provides the correlation function when two temperatures equal zero: the temperature of channel
S1
,
T_{S1}
, and that of channel 1 before the diluter.
In this section, we further extend Eq. (
S32
) to the situation where
T_{S1}
is finite.
The ambient temperature of channel 1 however remains zero.
In reality, the temperature of channel 1 before the diluter should be much smaller than the typical distance (in-time) between two neighboring anyons, i.e.,
\nu e/I_{1}
.
Indeed, otherwise the non-equilibrium feature of channel 1 is smeared out by the thermal fluctuation, as then typically the expected number of non-equilibrium anyons observed during the temperature inverse is much smaller than one (, meaning the absence of non-equilibrium feature).
Notice that the situation is rather different for a finite temperature
T_{S1}
in the source, as the latter governs the width of non-equilibrium anyons, rather than the typical distance between neighboring anyons.
In addition,
T_{S1}
is taken to be finite, such that the shot noise
S_{1}
and the tunneling current
I_{1}
can be considered as two independent variables [cf. discussions after Eq. (
S36
)].
This independence is crucial, as
S_{1}
and
I_{1}
govern two independent parameters, i.e., the effective temperature and the effective chemical potential, respectively, of an anyonic channel in the collision-free limit.

When
T_{S1}
is finite, the zero-temperature leading-order correlation, Eq. (
S2
), becomes modified into

\displaystyle\big\langle e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(t^{-},L)}e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(0^{+},L)}\big\rangle_{\text{neq,2}}\!=\!-\frac{\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{2}}\sum_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!ds_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!ds_{2}\,\frac{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\,e^{-ie\nu V_{S1}(s_{1}-s_{2})}(\pi T_{S1}\tau_{0})^{\nu}}{\sin^{\nu}\left\{\pi T_{S1}[\tau_{0}+i(s_{1}-s_{2})\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{2}}(s_{1}-s_{2})]\right\}}

(S33)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle 2i\pi\nu\frac{\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\tau_{0}^{\nu}(\pi T_{S1}\tau_{0})^{\nu}}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{2}}\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\tilde{s}\ |\tilde{s}|\ \left\{\frac{e^{i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})]}\!-\!\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})]}\right\}.

We can check asymptotic behavior of Eq. (
S41
), of different limiting cases.
For the low-temperature limit
\pi T_{S1}\ll\nu eV
,
|\tilde{s}|\sim 1/\nu eV
, Eq. (
S41
) behaves
\sim I_{1}/\nu eV
, of the same form as that of the zero-temperature result, Eq. (
S12
).
In contrast, in the opposite limit,
\pi T_{S1}\gg\nu eV
, the oscillating term that appears at the numerator of the integrand of Eq. (
S41
) becomes unimportant.
Of this case, one can expand these exponential factors, leading to results that are proportional to
V_{S1}
. Of this case, the final result is instead
\sim I_{1}/\pi T_{S1}
.
In general,

\displaystyle\left[\left\langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,t)}\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,0)}\right\rangle_{\text{neq,2}}^{\mathcal{R}_{2}}+\left\langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,t)}\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,0)}\right\rangle_{\text{neq,2}}^{\mathcal{R}_{3}}\right]\Bigg|_{\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0}\approx i\sin(\pi\nu)C_{2}(\nu,V_{S1},T_{S1})\frac{I_{1}}{e^{2}V_{S1}},

(S42)

where we have written the integral in a similar form of the zero-temperature case, Eq. (
S12
), after introducing a temperature-dependent function

C_{2}(\nu,V_{S1},T_{S1})\equiv eV_{S1}\frac{2\pi}{\sin(\pi\nu)}\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tilde{s}\ |\tilde{s}|\ \left\{\frac{e^{i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})]}-\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})]}\right\}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\tilde{s}\ \left\{\frac{e^{i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})]}-\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}+i\tilde{s})]}\right\}}.

(S43)

Now we move to consider corrections from higher-order dilutions.
Again, we start with the next-leading order dilution, where another pair of non-equilibrium pairs transport through the diluter at moments
s_{3}
and

Since
\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{T_{S1}}(s_{1}^{\eta_{1}},s_{2}^{\eta_{2}};s_{3}^{\eta_{3}},s_{4}^{\eta_{4}})\equiv\Bigg|\frac{[\tau_{0}+i(s_{1}-s_{3})\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{3}}(s_{1}-s_{3})]^{\nu}[\tau_{0}+i(s_{2}-s_{4})\chi_{\eta_{2},\eta_{4}}(s_{2}-s_{4})]^{\nu}}{[\tau_{0}+i(s_{1}-s_{4})\chi_{\eta_{1},\eta_{4}}(s_{1}-s_{4})]^{\nu}[\tau_{0}+i(s_{2}-s_{3})\chi_{\eta_{2},\eta_{3}}(s_{2}-s_{3})]^{\nu}}\Bigg|

(S44)

being independent of Keldysh indexes
\eta_{3}
,
\eta_{4}
.
Consequently, next-leading order correlation, for the branch cut at
\tilde{s}_{1}\sim\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0

\displaystyle\left[\left\langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,t)}\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,0)}\right\rangle_{\text{neq,4}}^{\mathcal{R}_{2}}+\left\langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,t)}\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,0)}\right\rangle_{\text{neq,4}}^{\mathcal{R}_{3}}\right]\Bigg|_{\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0}

(S45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\nu\frac{\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\tau_{0}^{\nu}(\pi T_{S1}\tau_{0})^{\nu}}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{2}}\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\tilde{s}\ |\tilde{s}|\ \left\{\frac{e^{i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})]}\!-\!\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})]}\right\}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}t}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{2}}\!\left\{\!\left(1\!-\!e^{-2i\pi\nu}\right)\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!ds^{\prime}\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}s^{\prime}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!is^{\prime})^{\nu}}\frac{(\pi T_{S1})^{\nu}}{\sin^{\nu}\!\left[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!is^{\prime})\right]^{\nu}}\!+\!\left(1\!-\!e^{2i\pi\nu}\right)\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!ds^{\prime}\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}s^{\prime}}}{(\tau_{0}\!-\!is^{\prime})^{\nu}}\frac{(\pi T_{S1})^{\nu}}{\sin^{\nu}\!\left[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!-\!is^{\prime})\right]^{\nu}}\!\!\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\pi\nu\frac{\mathcal{T}_{1}^{(0)}\tau_{0}^{\nu}(\pi T_{S1}\tau_{0})^{\nu}}{(2\pi\tau_{0})^{2}}\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\tilde{s}\ |\tilde{s}|\ \left\{\frac{e^{i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})]}\!-\!\frac{e^{-i\nu eV_{S1}\tilde{s}}}{(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}\!+\!i\tilde{s})]}\right\}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]\,t+i\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}\sin(2\pi\nu)\,t\right\},

where
s^{\prime}\equiv s_{3}-s_{4}
is the difference between the moment of the extra non-equilibrium anyonic pair.
Following Eq. (
S45
), when considering the branch cut,
\tilde{s}_{1}\sim\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0
, correction from higher-order dilution, manifested via extra self-contracted non-equilibrium pairs, simply introduces another multiplying factor that equals the leading-order result of regime
\mathcal{R}_{1}
, as that shown in the last line of Eq. (

).
Consequently, following previous argument on the resummation over higher-order terms, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left[\left\langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,t)}\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,0)}\right\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\mathcal{R}_{2}}+\left\langle e^{i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,t)}\,e^{-i\sqrt{\nu}\phi_{1}(L,0)}\right\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\mathcal{R}_{3}}\right]\Bigg|_{\tilde{s}_{1},\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0}

(S46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sin(\pi\nu)C_{2}(\nu,V_{S1},T_{S1})\frac{I_{1}}{e^{2}V_{S1}}\exp\left\{-\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]\,t+i\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}\sin(2\pi\nu)\,t\right\},

as the finite-temperature version of correlation functions, when considering the branch cut at
\tilde{s}_{1}\sim\tilde{s}_{2}\to 0
.
Again, the exponential factor of Eq. (
S46
) contains both the non-equilibrium current
I_{1}
and the corresponding noise
S_{1}
, as these two quantities are not proportional to each other, but depend on also the source temperature,
T_{S1}
.

S4. Effective parameters of a collision-free non-equilibrium anyonic system

In this section, we provide more detailed derivation and discussion, on the effective parameters (i.e.,
V_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
given by the caption of Fig. 4 of the main text) in the collision-free limit.
Following Ref.
Zhang et al. 2025
, for a non-equilibrium anyonic system, its effective chemical potential and effective temperature can be calibrated by an equilibrium channel.
Especially, in the strongly-diluted limit, effective chemical potential and effective temperature universally depend on the non-equilibrium current (
I_{1}
), and the corresponding noise (
S_{1}
), respectively.
For the convenience of the readers, in this section, we provide derivations of these two effective parameters. More detailed derivations and analysis can also be found in Ref.
Zhang et al. 2025
.

Here we consider the setup of Fig. 1(a) of the main text, and calibrate effective parameters of the non-equilibrium channel 1 by the equilibrium channel 2.
When we ignore tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons at the collider, correlation functions of the non-equilibrium channel simplifies into [cf. the first term of correlations of Eq. (
S57
), and Eq. (11) of the main text]

\displaystyle\big\langle T_{K}\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\text{braid}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{0}}\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}e^{-\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]|t|+i\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}\sin(2\pi\nu)t},

(S47)

\displaystyle\big\langle T_{K}\psi_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\text{braid}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{0}}\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}e^{-\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]|t|-i\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}\sin(2\pi\nu)t},

where
the superscript “braid” highlights the fact that we include only time-domain braiding into consideration, after neglecting non-equilibrium collisions [in contrast to Eq. (6) of the main text, and Eq. (
S57
), where full expressions of correlation functions are provided].
Correlation functions of channel 2, on the other hand, equal [cf. Eq. (5) of the main text]

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{2}(L,t)\psi_{2}(L,0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}\!=\!\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu-1}}{2\pi}\frac{(\pi T_{2,\text{eq}})}{\sin^{\nu}(\pi T_{2,\text{eq}}t)}e^{i\nu eV_{2,\text{eq}}t},

(S48)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{2}(L,t)\psi^{\dagger}_{2}(L,0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}\!=\!\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu-1}}{2\pi}\frac{(\pi T_{2,\text{eq}})}{\sin^{\nu}(\pi T_{2,\text{eq}}t)}e^{-i\nu eV_{2,\text{eq}}t}.

With Eqs. (
S47
) and (
S48
), the tunneling current between channels 1 and 2 equals

\displaystyle I_{\text{charge}}\equiv\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}\nu e\int dt\left[\big\langle T_{K}\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\text{braid}}\langle\psi_{2}(L,t)\psi^{\dagger}_{2}(L,0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}\right.

(S49)

\displaystyle\left.-\big\langle T_{K}\psi_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\text{braid}}\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{2}(L,t)\psi_{2}(L,0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}\right],

where the first and second lines refer to the tunneling from channel 1 to channel 2, and that of the opposite process, respectively.
Clearly, integrand of these two processes equal each other, simply when
V_{\text{2,eq}}=I_{1}\sin(2\pi\nu)/(\nu^{2}e^{2})
.
Consequently,

V_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{\mathcal{T}_{1}\ll 1}\equiv V_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}=I_{1}\sin(2\pi\nu)/(\nu^{2}e^{2}),

(S50)

depends only on the non-equilibrium current
I_{1}
, and the filling fraction
\nu
.
In Eq. (
S50
), we define
V_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
as the effective chemical potential in the collision-free limit [cf. Eq. (9) of the main text].

Now we move to the effective temperature.
Effective temperature is defined as the temperature
T_{\text{2,eq}}
of the equilibrium channel, at which both the tunneling current of Eq. (
S49
), and the heat current

\displaystyle J_{\text{heat}}=-i\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}\int dt\left\{\partial_{t}\left[\big\langle T_{K}\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\text{braid}}e^{-i\nu eV_{\text{1,eff}}t}\right]\langle\psi_{2}(L,t)\psi^{\dagger}_{2}(L,0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}e^{i\nu eV_{\text{2,eq}}t}\right.

(S51)

\displaystyle\left.-\big\langle T_{K}\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}^{\text{braid}}e^{-i\nu eV_{\text{1,eff}}t}\partial_{t}\left[\langle\psi_{2}(L,t)\psi^{\dagger}_{2}(L,0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}e^{i\nu eV_{\text{2,eq}}t}\right]\right\},

vanish.
With correlation functions, Eqs. (
S47
) and (
S48
), the vanishing of heat current, Eq. (
S51
), is equivalent to

\int dt^{\prime}\frac{e^{-\frac{s_{c}}{\pi}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]|t^{\prime}|}}{(\tau_{0}^{\prime}+it^{\prime})^{\nu}\sin^{\nu}(\tau_{0}^{\prime}+it^{\prime})}\left\{-\frac{i\nu}{\tau_{0}^{\prime}+it^{\prime}}+\frac{i\nu}{\tan(\tau_{0}^{\prime}+it^{\prime})}-\frac{s_{c}}{\pi}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]\text{sgn}(t^{\prime})\right\}=0,

(S52)

where we have taken
V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
[cf. Eq. (
S50
)],
T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
, and have defined dimensionless quantities
\tau_{0}^{\prime}\equiv\pi T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}\tau_{0}
,
t^{\prime}\equiv\pi T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}t
, and

s_{c}\equiv\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}}.

(S53)

Clearly,
s_{c}
is a universal (i.e., depends on only the filling fraction
\nu
) and dimensionless quantity, that can be obtained after numerically solving Eq. (
S52
).
As the consequence,
T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}\propto S_{1}
for a given value of
\nu
.
More specifically, when
\nu=1/3
, by solving Eq. (
S52
), we obtain
T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}\equiv T_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{\mathcal{T}_{1}\ll 1}
, i.e., the effective temperatures in the collision-free limit, for different filling fractions,

\displaystyle T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}

\displaystyle\approx 5.75\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}},\text{ if }\nu=1/3,

(S54)

\displaystyle T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}

\displaystyle\approx 3.44\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}},\text{ if }\nu=1/5,

\displaystyle T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}

\displaystyle\approx 2.30\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}},\text{ if }\nu=1/7.

Before moving to the next topic, we comment that for the collision-free limit, the entire system displays the symmetry between effective particle and hole distributions, with their definitions [cf. Eq. (2) of the main text]

\displaystyle n_{\text{p},\alpha}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv\int dt\exp(-i\epsilon t)\langle\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(t)\psi_{\alpha}(0)\rangle_{\text{st}},

(S55)

\displaystyle n_{\text{h},\alpha}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle\equiv\int dt\exp(i\epsilon t)\langle\psi_{\alpha}(t)\psi_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle_{\text{st}},

where
\text{st}=\text{``neq''}
and
“eq”
(i.e., referring to the channel status), for the channel
\alpha=1
and 2, respectively, for a system in the collision-free limit.
Indeed, following the definition Eq. (
S55
), as well as related effective distribution functions, Eqs. (
S47
) and (
S48
), the particle and hole distribution functions are symmetric respect to the effective Laughlin surface,
\nu eV_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
, and the real one
\nu eV_{\text{2,eq}}
, for channels 1 and 2, respectively.
As has been analyzed in the main text, this symmetry between effective particle and hole distribution functions, crucially, is the key element that leads to the universal feature of effective parameters [cf. Eqs. (
S50
) and (
S53
)], and thus the missing of Seebeck and Peltier coefficients.
The latter will be the topic of another SM Sec. S6.

S5. Effective particle and hole distributions, with the tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons

In this section, we go beyond the collision-free limit, and investigate the influence of tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons.
Following Ref.
Zhang et al. 2025
and the main text, we begin by defining effective particle and hole distribution functions as [cf. Eq. (2) of the main text]

\displaystyle n_{\text{p,1}}^{\text{neq}}(\epsilon)\equiv\int dt\exp(-i\epsilon t)\langle\psi_{1}^{\dagger}(t)\psi_{1}(0)\rangle_{\text{neq}},

(S56)

\displaystyle n_{\text{h,1}}^{\text{neq}}(\epsilon)\equiv\int dt\exp(i\epsilon t)\langle\psi_{1}(t)\psi_{1}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle_{\text{neq}}.

Definition Eq. (
S56

Zhang et al. 2025

\displaystyle\big\langle T_{K}\psi^{\dagger}_{1}(L,t^{-})\psi_{1}(L,0^{+})\big\rangle_{\text{neq}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{0}}\left\{\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu}}{(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}e^{-\frac{S_{1}}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]|t|+i\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}\sin(2\pi\nu)t}\left[1+i\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}\text{sgn}(t)\sin(\pi\nu)\right]\right.

\mathcal{T}_{1}\equiv\frac{I_{1}}{e^{2}V_{S1}}C_{0}(\nu),\ \ \mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}\equiv\frac{I_{1}}{e^{2}V_{S1}}C_{2}(\nu,V_{S1},T_{S1}),

(S58)

which are beyond the scope of the main text [i.e., Eq. (21) of Materials and Methods], by taking a finite temperature

S6. General expressions of effective parameters, and effective linear-response transport coefficients

In this section, we take into consideration the tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons,
and investigate their corresponding influence on both the effective equilibrium point, as well as effective linear response transport coefficients.
In this section we focus on the setup of Fig. 1(a) of the main text, where a non-equilibrium channel (channel 1) communicates with an equilibrium one (channel 2) via the collider.
Within this section, we neglect the superscript “neq-eq” for simplicity.

S6A. Tunneling charge and heat currents, as well as effective linear response parameters

We begin by providing the formulas, in integral forms, on charge and thermal transport coefficients beyond the collision-free limit.
After including collisions of non-equilibrium anyons, discussions on the missing of thermoelectric coefficients (i.e., Peltier and Seebeck coefficients) in the collision-free limit, provided below Eqs. (12) and (13) of the main text, fail to apply.
Actually, following Eq. (
S32
), after allowing the tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons, relevant correlation functions can be generically written as

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{1}^{\dagger}(t)\psi_{1}(0)\rangle_{\text{neq}}\!=\!f_{\alpha}e^{i\nu eV_{\alpha}t}\!+\!f_{\beta}e^{i\nu eV_{\beta}t}\!+\!if_{\gamma}e^{i\nu eV_{\alpha}t},\ \langle\psi_{1}(t)\psi_{1}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle_{\text{neq}}\!=\!f_{\alpha}e^{-i\nu eV_{\alpha}t}\!+\!f_{\beta}e^{-i\nu eV_{\beta}t}\!-\!if_{\gamma}e^{-i\nu eV_{\alpha}t},

(S60)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{2}^{\dagger}(t)\psi_{2}(0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}=f_{2}e^{i\nu eV_{2}t},\ \ \langle\psi_{2}(t)\psi_{2}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle_{\text{eq}}=f_{2}e^{-i\nu eV_{2}t},

where
f_{\alpha}=f_{1}
, i.e., of Eq. (11) of the main text, while functions
f_{\beta}
and
f_{\gamma}
correspond to two correcting terms, generated by the tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons.
More specifically, following Eq. (
S57
) for finite temperatures, “biases”, corresponding to the imaginary phase factors of Eq. (
S60
), explicitly equal

\displaystyle V_{\alpha}=I_{1}\sin(2\pi\nu)/e^{2}\nu^{2},\qquad V_{\beta}=V_{S1},\qquad V_{\gamma}=V_{\alpha}=I_{1}\sin(2\pi\nu)/e^{2}\nu^{2},\qquad V_{2}=V_{\text{2,eq}},

(S61)

and correlation functions equal

\displaystyle\text{Non-equilibrium: }f_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu-1}}{2\pi(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}e^{-\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]|t|},\ \ f_{\beta}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{0}}\frac{(\pi T_{S1}\tau_{0})^{\nu}}{\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{S1}(\tau_{0}+it)]},

(S62)

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{1}^{\prime}\sin(\pi\nu)\text{sgn}(t)\frac{\tau_{0}^{\nu-1}}{2\pi(\tau_{0}+it)^{\nu}}e^{-\frac{I_{1}}{\nu e}[1-\cos(2\pi\nu)]|t|},

\displaystyle\text{Non-equilibrium: }f_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{0}}\frac{(\pi T_{\text{2,eq}}\tau_{0})^{\nu}}{\sin^{\nu}[\pi T_{\text{2,eq}}(\tau_{0}+it)]}.

Notice that arguments and
derivations of thermoelectric coefficients in this section are generally correct, for correlation functions that have the form of Eq. (
S60
).
They are not limited to the specific options of Eqs. (
S61
) and (
S62
), for the setup of Fig. 1(a) of the main text.
As a crucial difference from the collision-free situation, cf. Eq. (11) of the main text, the non-equilibrium correlation functions [cf. Eqs. (
S60
) and (
S62
)] now contain two imaginary phase factors, i.e.,
V_{\alpha}=I_{1}\sin(2\pi\nu)/\nu^{2}e^{2}
and
V_{\beta}=V_{S1}

In addition, following, Eq. (
S62

of Eq. (

Both features above are clearly absent for correlation functions in the collision-free limit.
Discussions below the main text Eqs. (12) and (13) on the vanishing of Peltier and Seebeck in the collision-free limit, thus fail to apply, after taking the tunneling and collisions of non-equilibrium anyons into consideration.

S6B. Onsager relation beyond the the collision-free limit

In this section, we explicitly show that the thermoelectric coefficients, including the Seebeck and Peltier coefficients, satisfy the Onsager relation, both in, and beyond the collision-free limit.
As analyzed in the main text, and at the beginning of Sec. S6A, in the former collision-free limit, both the Seebeck and Peltier coefficients vanish (due to the particle-hole symmetry), such that the Onsager relation is automatically satisfied.
Away from the collision-free limit, the tunneling and collision of non-equilibrium anyons however sabotages the particle-hole symmetry, thus leading to finite off-diagonal effective linear response coefficients,
L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}
and
L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}
. Here we prove the Onsager relation, i.e.,
\bar{T}L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}=L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}
, defined by Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S66
) respectively, after requiring the vanishing of the tunneling current, i.e.,
I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}=0
[cf. Eq. (
S63
)].
Importantly and interestingly, the satisfactory of the Onsager relation does not require a vanishing heat current,
J_{\text{heat}}
[cf. Eq. (
S65
)].

To begin with, the last line of Eq. (
S66
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle i\nu e\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}\int dtf_{\alpha}f_{2}\left\{\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t\cos[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right\}

\displaystyle+i\nu e\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}\int dtf_{\gamma}f_{2}\left\{-\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+\cos[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right\}

\displaystyle i\nu e\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}\int dt\left\{\left[-\frac{\partial(tf_{\alpha}f_{2})}{\partial t}+f_{\beta}f_{2}\right]\sin[\nu e(V_{\beta}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+\left[-\frac{\partial(tf_{\alpha}f_{2})}{\partial t}+f_{\alpha}f_{2}\right]\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right.

(S68)

\displaystyle\left.+f_{\gamma}\cos[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right\}.

\displaystyle=-2i\nu e\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}T_{\text{2,eq}}\int dt\left\{\frac{\partial f^{\text{general}}_{2}}{\partial T_{\text{2,eq}}}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial f_{2,j}(r_{1},r_{2})}{\partial r_{1}}\Big|_{r_{1}=T_{\text{2,eq}},r_{2}=T_{\text{2,eq}}t}\right\}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{f_{\alpha}\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+f_{\beta}\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+f_{\gamma}\cos[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right\}

\displaystyle=-2i\nu e\mathcal{T}_{C}^{(0)}T_{\text{2,eq}}\int dt\frac{\partial f_{2}^{\text{general}}}{\partial T_{\text{2,eq}}}\left\{f_{\alpha}\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+f_{\beta}\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]+f_{\gamma}\cos[\nu e(V_{\alpha}-V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right\}

\displaystyle=\bar{T}L_{LT,\text{coll}}^{\text{neq-eq}}\big|_{\text{general}},

where in the last but one line, we have used the fact that

\int dt\frac{\partial f_{2,j}(r_{1},r_{2})}{\partial r_{1}}\Big|_{r_{1}=T_{\text{2,eq}},r_{2}=T_{\text{2,eq}}t}\!\!\left\{f_{\alpha}\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}\!-\!V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\!+\!f_{\beta}\sin[\nu e(V_{\alpha}\!-\!V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\!+\!f_{\gamma}\cos[\nu e(V_{\alpha}\!-\!V_{\text{2,eq}})t]\right\}\!=\!0,

(S74)

which vanishes as the differentiation over the
t
-independent part (i.e.,
r_{1}\to T_{\text{2,eq}}
) will not change the integral outcome, when the original integral itself equals zero.

We have thus proven the validity of the Onsager relation in our system, when the function
f_{2}
of the equilibrium channel, 2, has the general expression, i.e.,
f_{2}^{\text{general}}
.
Notice that this conclusion remains valid as long as
f_{2}^{\text{general}}
does not contain a term that depends on a single
t
(in contrast to the dependence on
T_{\text{2,eq}}
, and the temperature-time combination,
T_{\text{2,eq}}t
).
Indeed, when
f_{2}^{\text{general}}
contains a contribution that depends on a single
t
, the system must contain another energy scale, such that the system represented by
f_{2}^{\text{general}}
can not be in equilibrium.

S6D. Effective linear-response transport coefficients, defined with respect to effective parameters

After including collisions of non-equilibrium anyons, effective linear-response transport coefficients [see Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S66
)] are defined by differentiating over electric and heat currents, with respect to real parameters
T_{\text{2,eq}}
and
V_{\text{2,eq}}
of the equilibrium channel 2.
We take these definitions, as effective parameters (
V_{\text{1,eff}}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}
) of channel 1 non-universally depend on all details of the non-equilibrium channel.
The tuning and control of these effective parameters are thus more demanding in practical experiments.
Nevertheless, in this section we explicitly show how to measure these effective linear-response transport coefficients, if they are defined by differentiating over currents, with respect to effective parameters.
Transport coefficients defined in this way, as will be shown, is of apparently greater complexity (and thus experimentally impractical), in comparison to Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S66
).

Briefly, following Eq. (
S75
) and Fig.
S4
, effective parameters
V_{\text{1,eff}}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}
are both functions of non-universal system details, after including collisions of non-equilibrium anyons.
As the consequence, in the effective linear-response regime (i.e.,
V_{\text{1,eff}}\approx V_{\text{2,eq}}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}\approx T_{\text{2,eq}}
), tunneling charge and heat currents can be written as

\displaystyle I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}

\displaystyle\approx L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IV,\text{coll}}[V_{\text{1,eff}}(S_{1},I_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1})-V_{\text{2,eq}}]+L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}[T_{\text{1,eff}}(S_{1},I_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1})-T_{\text{2,eq}}],

(S76)

\displaystyle J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}

\displaystyle\approx L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}[V_{\text{1,eff}}(S_{1},I_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1})-V_{\text{2,eq}}]+L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JT,\text{coll}}[T_{\text{1,eff}}(S_{1},I_{1},\mathcal{T}_{1})-T_{\text{2,eq}}],

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IV,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\partial}{\partial V_{\text{2,eq}}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial(V_{\text{1,eff}}-V_{\text{2,eq}})}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}},

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{2,eq}}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial(T_{\text{1,eff}}-T_{\text{2,eq}})}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}},

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\partial}{\partial V_{\text{2,eq}}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial(V_{\text{1,eff}}-V_{\text{2,eq}})}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}},

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JT,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{2,eq}}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial(T_{\text{1,eff}}-T_{\text{2,eq}})}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}.

Briefly, the bi-linear structure of the first lines of Eq. (
S76
) is the direct consequence of the definition of effective parameters, i.e.,
V_{\text{1,eff}}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}
.
Indeed, these parameters are defined to equal the values of real parameters,
V_{\text{2,eq}}
and
T_{\text{2,eq}}
, given no charge or heat current that transports between channel 1 (being non-equilibrium) and channel 2 (being equilibrium).
Following this definition, around the effective equilibrium point, any modification of effective parameters (
V_{\text{2,eq}}
and
T_{\text{2,eq}}
), triggered by the detuning (off effective equilibrium) of
I_{1}
,
S_{1}
and/or
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, can be compensated (i.e., under which the system returns to effective equilibrium) by tuning real parameters
V_{\text{2,eq}}
and
T_{\text{2,eq}}
of channel 2 by the same amount.
This bi-linear structure of the first two lines of Eq. (
S76
), remarkably, indicates that around effective equilibrium, one can experimentally obtain thermoelectric coefficients (Peltier and Seebeck coefficients), as well as charge and heat conductances, by measuring the response of currents to an infinitesimal modification of real parameters,
V_{\text{2,eq}}
and
T_{\text{2,eq}}
.
Below we discuss how to experimentally obtain the response to effective parameters,
V_{\text{1,eff}}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}
.

With Eq. (
S76
), we can obtain response of currents to the change of
I_{1}
and
S_{1}
, leading to

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{1}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}

\displaystyle=L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IV,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}+L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}},

(S77)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{1}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}

\displaystyle=L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IV,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}+L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{1}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}

\displaystyle=L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}+L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JT,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{1}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}

\displaystyle=L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}+L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JT,\text{coll}}\frac{\partial}{\partial S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\Big|_{V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}},T_{\text{2,eq}}=T_{\text{1,eff}}}.

Following Eq. (
S77
), we arrive at

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IV,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}-\partial_{I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}}{\partial_{I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}-\partial_{S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}},

(S78)

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JT,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial_{S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}-\partial_{I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}}{\partial_{I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}-\partial_{S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}},

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{IT,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial_{S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}-\partial_{I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}}{\partial_{I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}-\partial_{S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{coll}},

\displaystyle L^{\text{neq-eq}}_{JV,\text{coll}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}-\partial_{I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}}{\partial_{I_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{S_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}-\partial_{S_{1}}V_{\text{1,eff}}\partial_{I_{1}}T_{\text{1,eff}}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{coll}},

which discloses the way to experimentally measure effective linear-response transport coefficients, as response to effective parameters of non-equilibrium channel 1.
We emphasize that Eq. (
S78
) is theoretically equivalent to definitions of transport coefficients of Eqs. (
S64
), (
S66
) (and also the main text), where they are defined as the response of charge and heat currents to parameters of the equilibrium channel, i.e.,
V_{\text{2,eq}}
and
T_{\text{2,eq}}
.
Their equivalence can be seen by taking reverse steps to go back to Eq. (
S76
).
Nevertheless, Eq. (
S78
) provides an alternative way, other than Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S66
), to experimentally measure transport coefficients by tuning parameters of the non-equilibrium channel.
In great contrast to Eqs. (
S64
) and (
S66
) where effective parameters are defined as the response to real parameters in the equilibrium channel 2,
Eq. (
S78
) further requires to know the response of effective parameters
V_{\text{1,eff}}
and
T_{\text{1,eff}}
to non-equilibrium parameters
I_{1}
and
S_{1}
.
Eq. (
S78
) is thus experimentally much less practical in obtaining the charge and heat transport coefficients.

S7. Conductances in the collision-free limit, beyond effective linear response

In the main text and previous sections of the Supplementary Information, we define only effective linear response transport coefficients, i.e., close to the effective equilibrium point where
T_{\text{1,st}}\to T_{\text{2,st'}}
and
V_{\text{1,st}}\to V_{\text{2,st'}}
, with “st” and “
st’
” referring to the situation (being non-equilibrium or in-equilibrium) of channels 1 and 2, respectively.
More specifically, “st” equals “eff” for setups of Figs. 1(a), 2(a), and equals “eq” for the setup of Fig. 2(b).
Instead, “
st’
” equals “eq” for setups of Figs. 1(a), 2(b), and equals “eff” for the setup of Fig. 2(a).

Here we further show that in the collision-free limit, definitions of charge and heat conductances can actually be extended to systems beyond the effective linear response regime.
More specifically, in this section we require only
V_{\text{1,st}}\to V_{\text{2,st'}}
.
The equality between temperatures is however relaxed [cf. Fig.
S5
for the setup of Fig. 1(a) of the main text].
As its advantage, by allowing temperatures of two channels to be drastically different, one can study the contribution of each channel (individually) to transport coefficients.
Importantly, as will be shown shortly, the contribution of an equilibrium channel differs drastically from that of an out-of-equilibrium one.

Figure S5:
Illustration of the regime we consider in Sec. S9,
for the setup of Fig. 1(a) (containing one in-equilibrium and one out-of-equilibrium channels) in the collision-free limit.
Here we require only
V_{\text{2,eq}}=V_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
, and investigate the contribution to heat conductance from each channel individually, by changing the temperature ratio,
T_{\text{2,eq}}/T_{\text{1,eff}}^{0}
, i.e., along both directions of the white arrow.

Since we focus on the collision-free limit,
L_{JV}^{\text{st-st'}}=0
(see the definition of st and st’ at the beginning of this section), such that the heat conductance simply equals the response of heat current to the temperature difference, i.e.,
\kappa^{\text{st-st'}}=L_{JT}^{\text{st-st'}}=\partial_{T_{\text{1,st}}-T_{\text{2,st'}}}J_{\text{heat}}|_{\text{eff. equil.}}
.
Before visiting three setups separately, we define contribution from each channel as

\displaystyle G^{\text{st-st'}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}G_{\text{1}}^{\text{st-st'}}+\frac{1}{2}G_{\text{2 }}^{\text{st-st'}}\equiv\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial V_{\text{1,st}}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{st-st'}}\right)+\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial V_{\text{2,st'}}}I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{st-st'}}\right),

(S79)

\displaystyle\kappa^{\text{st-st'}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{1}^{\text{st-st'}}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{2}^{\text{st-st'}}\equiv\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{1,st}}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{st-st'}}\right)+\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{2,st'}}}J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{st-st'}}\right),

referring to the response of (charge and thermal) currents to the variation of temperature or bias of each channel separately.
In Eq. (
S79
), the factor of one half is added, such that
\kappa_{1}^{\text{st-st'}}
and
\kappa_{2}^{\text{st-st'}}
can be interpreted as the response of the heat current
J_{\text{heat}}^{\text{st-st'}}
to,
individually
, the temperature of channel 1 and 2, respectively.
Likewise, a factor one half is introduced to the definition of the charge-conductance contribution from each channel, individually.
Notice that strictly speaking, charge and heat conductances of Eq. (
S79
) should be defined after differentiating over the parameter differences, i.e., the voltage difference,
V_{\text{1,st}}-V_{\text{2,st'}}
, and the temperature difference,
T_{\text{1,st}}-T_{\text{2,st'}}
.
We however can arrive at the relatively simpler expression, i.e., Eq. (
S79
), after noticing the fact that the temperature and voltage of one channel influence only correlation functions of this channel (not the other one), such that we can treat the differentiation over the temperature difference
T_{\text{1,st}}-T_{\text{2,st'}}
, as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\left[n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})\right]}{\partial(T_{\text{1,st}}-T_{\text{2,st'}})}

(S80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial(T_{\text{1,st}}-T_{\text{2,st'}})}n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})\right]n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})+n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial(T_{\text{1,st}}-T_{\text{2,st'}})}n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{1,st}}}n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})\right]n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})-n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{2,st'}}}n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{1,st}}}\left[n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})\right]-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{2,st'}}}\left[n_{\text{p,1}}(T_{\text{1,st}},V_{\text{1,st}})n_{\text{h,2}}(T_{\text{2,st'}},V_{\text{2,st'}})\right],

and likewise for the differentiation over the voltage difference,
V_{\text{1,st}}-V_{\text{2,st'}}
.
Eq. (
S80
) is the reason that we can separate the contribution to the conductances (either the charge or heat conductance), into that from each channel separately.

The situation is more complicated for the heat conductance, where both

This fact, together with the equality between two electric conductance values

Before moving to other setups, we notice that in comparison to equilibrium systems, the tuning of effective parameters of a non-equilibrium channel is experimentally more demanding.
Indeed, as shown, for a non-equilibrium channel in the collision-free limit, the effective chemical potential and effective temperature are determined by the non-equilibrium current and shot noise, respectively.
Tuning either parameter individually (while keeping the other one fixed) thus requires fine tuning in practical experiments.
A fine-tuning free protocol is thus provided later, in Sec. S7D.

Firstly, charge conductance from channel 1 equals that from channel 2, for different values of the temperature ratio,
T_{\text{1,eff}}/T_{\text{2,eq}}
.
This feature is shared by also the other two setups, as now
V_{\text{1,eff}}=V_{\text{2,eq}}
, implying that
I_{\text{charge}}^{\text{neq-eq}}\propto(V_{\text{1,eff}}-V_{\text{2,eq}})
in the linear response regime.
Secondly, when
T_{\text{1,eff}}\ll T_{\text{2,eq}}
,
\kappa^{\text{neq-eq}}_{1}/\kappa^{\text{neq-eq}}
and
\kappa^{\text{neq-eq}}_{2}/\kappa^{\text{neq-eq}}
both saturate to constant values that are of the same order, indicating that in this limit both channels significantly participate in the generation of heat conductance.
In the opposite limit
T_{\text{1,eff}}\gg T_{\text{2,eq}}
, on the contrary,
\kappa^{\text{neq-eq}}_{1}\gg\kappa^{\text{neq-eq}}_{2}
, meaning that the equilibrium channel, with a comparatively much smaller temperature (than that of the non-equilibrium channel), contributes to a vanishingly small amount of heat conductance.
This observation, together with that of Fig.
S6
(b), where two equilibrium channels communicate through the collider, indicates that heat conductance contributed by an equilibrium channel is negligible, once its temperature is much smaller than that (either effective or real) of the other channel.
We emphasize that the conclusion is totally different for a non-equilibrium channel [either channel of the setup of Fig. 2(a), and channel 1 of the setup of Fig. 1(a) of the main text], which contributes to a significant amount of heat conductance, even if its temperature is comparatively negligible.

To understand this observation, we can write the heat current in a general (i.e., setup un-specified) form, when
T_{\text{2,st'}}\gg T_{\text{1,st}}

J^{\text{st-st'}}_{\text{Q}}\propto T_{\text{2,st'}}^{2\nu}\left[1+\zeta_{1}\frac{T_{\text{1,st}}}{T_{\text{2,st'}}}+\zeta_{2}\left(\frac{T_{\text{1,st}}}{T_{\text{2,st'}}}\right)^{2}\right].

(S87)

Heat conductance from channel 1, which has a comparatively much smaller temperature, then has the feature

\kappa^{\text{st-st'}}_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial T_{\text{1,st}}}J^{\text{st-st'}}_{\text{Q}}\propto T_{\text{2,st'}}^{2\nu-1}\left(0+\zeta_{1}+2\zeta_{2}\frac{T_{\text{1,st}}}{T_{\text{2,st'}}}\right),

(S88)

where
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
refer to prefactors for linear and quadratic orders of infinitesimals, when
T_{\text{2,st'}}\gg T_{\text{1,st}}
.

For a non-equilibrium channel, “st”
=
“eff”,
T_{\text{1,st}}
appears in the exponential factor, i.e.,
\exp\left\{-2s_{c}T_{\text{1,eff}}\sin^{2}(\pi\nu)|t|\right\}
. Linear-order expansion then leads to a finite
\zeta_{1}
: the reason that a non-equilibrium channel contributes to a significant amount of heat conductance, even when its temperature is vanishingly small.

In great contrast, for an equilibrium channel, its temperature, “st”
=
“eq”,
T_{\text{1,eq}}
appears in the denominator of correlation functions, in terms of a triangular function, i.e.,
\sin^{-\nu}[\pi T_{\text{1,eq}}(\tau_{0}+it)]
.
When expanding triangular functions, the linear term vanishes, meaning that
\zeta_{1}=0
for an equilibrium channel, when
T_{\text{1,eq}}\ll T_{\text{2,st'}}
. As the consequence, for an equilibrium channel, the leading-order correction to its heat conductance occurs to the quadratic order of the small temperature ratio, i.e.,
\kappa_{1}^{\text{eq-st'}}\propto(T_{\text{1,eq}}/T_{\text{2,st'}})^{2}
.
Its contribution to the heat conductance is thus vanishingly small in this limit.

S7D. The protocol, to experimentally measure effective linear response coefficients, when both channels are out-of-equilibrium

Before closure, we briefly discuss how to realize the experimental measurement of linear response coefficients of the neq-neq setup, i.e., that of Fig. 2(a) of the main text.
This question naturally arises, as in a non-equilibrium channel, the tuning of both the effective temperature and effective chemical potential requires (instead of more direct tuning knobs, e.g., the source potential and temperature) an indirect tuning of the non-equilibrium current and shot noise, e.g.,
I_{1}
and
S_{1}
of channel 1, respectively.
Fine tuning (on the bias and/or temperature) is thus request, if one targets to tune either
I_{1}
or
S_{1}
, while keeping the other quantity invariant.
Here we thus provide an experimentally more accessible protocol, which is free from fine-tuning, to measure the effective linear response coefficients, when both channels are out-of-equilibrium, and in the collision-free limit.

Briefly, the experimental measurement of effective linear response coefficients takes three major steps.
As the starting step, one tunes the system to effective equilibrium, such that no charge or heat current tunnels between two non-equilibrium channels.
As the second step, one tunes the source bias
V_{S1}
, while keeping the current in the source,
T_{S1}
, unchanged, thus generating finite tunneling charge and heat currents

\displaystyle I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{T_{S1}}

\displaystyle=L_{\text{IT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta T_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{T_{S1}}+L_{\text{IV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta V_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{T_{S1}}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}e^{2}s_{c}}L_{\text{IT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}+\frac{\sin(2\pi\nu)}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}L_{\text{IV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}},

(S89)

\displaystyle J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{T_{S1}}

\displaystyle=L_{\text{JT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta T_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{T_{S1}}+L_{\text{JV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta V_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{T_{S1}}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}e^{2}s_{c}}L_{\text{JT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}+\frac{\sin(2\pi\nu)}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}L_{\text{JV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}},

where the subscript
T_{S1}
highlights the unchanged parameter during this measurement (thus, the parameter
V_{S1}
is the parameter that is tuned).
More specifically,
I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{T_{S1}}
and
J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{T_{S1}}
are the tunneling charge and heat currents, induced by tuning
V_{S1}
.
The other quantities,

\delta T_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{T_{S1}}
and
\delta V_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{T_{S1}}
, instead refer to the corresponding modification of the effective temperature and chemical potential.
Quantities
I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{T_{S1}}
and
J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{T_{S1}}
, on the left side of Eq. (
S89
), can be obtained by measuring the change of charge and heat currents in channel 2, due to the charge and heat flow through the central collider.
Quantities
\delta I_{1}|_{T_{S1}}
and
\delta S_{1}|_{T_{S1}}
, on the right side of the equation, can instead be experimentally measured after pinching off the central collider.

Finally, we return to the effective equilibrium in the starting step.
Instead of tuning
V_{S1}
[cf. Eq. (
S89
)], here we tune the temperature
T_{S1}
of channel
S1
, leading to another group of modifications on the tunneling charge and heat currents, i.e.,

\displaystyle I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{V_{S1}}

\displaystyle=L_{\text{IT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta T_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{V_{S1}}+L_{\text{IV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta V_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{V_{S1}}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}e^{2}s_{c}}L_{\text{IT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}+\frac{\sin(2\pi\nu)}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}L_{\text{IV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}},

(S90)

\displaystyle J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{V_{S1}}

\displaystyle=L_{\text{JT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta T_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{V_{S1}}+L_{\text{JV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta V_{\text{1,eff}}\big|_{V_{S1}}=\frac{1}{\nu^{2}e^{2}s_{c}}L_{\text{JT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}+\frac{\sin(2\pi\nu)}{\nu^{2}e^{2}}L_{\text{JV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}},

where
|_{V_{S1}}
instead highlights the fact that we tune only
T_{S1}
(while keeping
V_{S1}
fixed) within this step.

Since in Eq. (
S89
) and (
S90
), the system is modified by introducing electric and thermal input, respectively, these two equations are linearly independent.
We can thus obtain all four effective linear response coefficients by solving these four linear equations.
More specifically,
L_{\text{IT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}
,
L_{\text{IV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}
can be obtained by solving the first lines of both Eq. (
S89
) and (
S90
), leading to

\displaystyle L_{IV}^{\text{neq-neq}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{2}e^{2}}{\sin(2\pi\nu)}\frac{I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}}{\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}},

(S91)

\displaystyle L_{IT}^{\text{neq-neq}}

\displaystyle=\nu^{2}e^{2}s_{c}\frac{I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-I_{\text{charge}}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}}{\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}}.

Instead,
L_{\text{JT}}^{\text{neq-neq}}
,
L_{\text{JV}}^{\text{neq-neq}}
can be obtained by solving the second lines of these two equations, leading to

\displaystyle L_{JV}^{\text{neq-neq}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nu^{2}e^{2}}{\sin(2\pi\nu)}\frac{J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}}{\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}},

(S92)

\displaystyle L_{JT}^{\text{neq-neq}}

\displaystyle=\nu^{2}e^{2}s_{c}\frac{J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-J_{\text{heat}}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}}{\delta S_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}-\delta S_{1}\big|_{V_{S1}}\delta I_{1}\big|_{T_{S1}}}.

We have thus provided a protocol, to obtain all four effective linear response coefficients.
Importantly, both measurements, given by Eqs. (
S89

General position and mutual-visibility in shadow graphs
Haritha S, Ullas Chandran S. V

Abstract
The general position problem in graphs asks for a largest set of vertices in which no three lie on a common shortest path. The mutual-visibility problem seeks a largest set of vertices such that every pair is connected by a shortest path whose internal vertices lie outside the set. In this paper, we investigate the general position and mutual-visibility problems for shadow graphs. Sharp general bounds are established for both the general position number and the mutual-visibility number of shadow graphs, and classes of graphs attaining these extremal values are characterized. Furthermore, these invariants are determined for several standard classes of shadow graphs, including shadow graphs of cycles, multipartite graphs, and trees.



Key words:
General position number, general position set, mutual visibility number.

3
General position in shadow graphs

In this section, we study the general position problem in shadow graphs. First, we derive certain structural properties of the
{\rm gp}
-partition
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
associated with any general position set. These properties are established in the following lemma and are used throughout this section.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
be the
{\rm gp}
-partition of
V(G)
corresponding to a general position set
S
of
S(G)
. Then the following holds:

i.

V_{1}
is an independent set of
G
,

ii.

(V_{1},V_{3})=\emptyset
,

iii.

every vertex
u
in
V_{1}\cup V_{3}
has atmost one neighbour in
V_{2}
,

iv.

(V_{1},V_{2})
is a matching,

v.

If
V_{4}=\emptyset
, then
V_{1}=\emptyset.

Proof.

Suppose there exist
u,v\in V_{1}
such that
uv\in E(G)
. Then the geodesic
u,v,u^{\prime}
is
S
-unsound in
S(G)
, which is impossible for a general position set. Hence, part (i) follows.

Next, we prove that
(V_{1},V_{3})=\emptyset
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there exist vertices
u\in V_{1}
and
v\in V_{3}
such that
uv\in E(G)
. Then the geodesic
u,v,u^{\prime}
is
S
-unsound, again contradicting the definition of a general position set. This proves part (ii).

Now, let
u\in V_{1}\cup V_{3}
have two neighbors
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
in
V_{2}
. Then the geodesic
v_{1}^{\prime},u,v_{2}^{\prime}
is
S
-unsound, which is again impossible. Hence, part (iii) follows.

Next, we show that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a matching. By part (iii), it is sufficient to show that every vertex in
V_{2}
has at most one neighbor in
V_{1}
. Suppose there exists a vertex
w\in V_{2}
with two distinct neighbors
u
and
v
in
V_{1}
. It follows from part (i) that
u,w^{\prime},v
is a
u,v
-geodesic that is
S
-unsound, which is impossible.

Finally, assume that
V_{4}=\emptyset
. Suppose there exists a vertex
v\in V_{1}
. Then the above facts show that
v
must be an end vertex and must be adjacent to a vertex
u\in V_{2}
. This implies that the path
u^{\prime},v,u,v^{\prime}
is a
u^{\prime},v^{\prime}
-geodesic in
S(G)
that is
S
-unsound, a contradiction. Thus,
V_{1}=\emptyset
.
∎

The properties obtained in Lemma
3.1
can be applied to determine the general position number of certain classes of shadow graphs. For instance, in the following, we determine the general position number of the shadow graph of a complete graph. This also establishes the sharpness of the upper bound in Theorem
3.6

Proposition 3.2
.

For every integer
n\geq 2
,
{\rm gp}(S(K_{n}))=n
.

Proof.

Since the set of all shadow vertices of
S(K_{n})
forms a general position set of
S(K_{n})
, we have
{\rm gp}(S(K_{n}))\geq n
.
Now let
X
be a
gp
-set of
S(K_{n})
, and let
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
be the partition of
V(K_{n})
associated with
X
. Suppose first that
V_{1}=\emptyset
. Then
|X|=n+n_{1}-n_{4}\leq n
. Hence, we may assume that
V_{1}\neq\emptyset
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have
n_{1}=1
and
n_{4}\geq n-1
. Consequently,
|X|=n+n_{1}-n_{4}\leq 2
.
Therefore,
{\rm gp}(S(K_{n}))=n
.
∎

Theorem 3.4

Theorem 3.5

Next, we determine the general position number of the shadow graph of a tree.
To this end, we introduce the following notation.

A support vertex

Theorem 3.9
.

For any tree

with
\rm diam(

, we have
{\rm gp}(S(T))=2l(T)
.

Proof.

Since the set of all leaves

together with their shadow vertices forms a general position set of

, we have
{\rm gp}(S(G))\geq 2l(T)
.
By Theorem

, to complete the proof it remains to show that
{\rm ip}(S(T))\leq l(T)
.
We prove this by induction on

.
If

, then
G
is a path, and it is clear that
S(G)
can be covered by two isometric paths.
Now, assume that for any tree
T
with fewer than

leaves, the shadow graph

admits an isometric path cover of size

.
Let
T
be a tree with

.
First, suppose that
T
has a leaf

whose support vertex

is a strong support vertex.
Then

has

admits an isometric path cover

of size

.
Consequently,

of size

.
Now assume that

As in the previous case, if

Otherwise, we repeat this process with the tree

∎

4
Mutual visibility in shadow graphs

In this section we deal with mutual-visibility in shadow graph
S(G)
of a graph

. We characterize graphs whose shadow graphs have mutual visibility numbers 2, 4 and we prove the non-existence of any graph

for which
\mu(S(G))=3,5
. Furthermore, we computed the mutual visibility numbers for the shadow graphs of complete multipartite graphs and graphs containing a universal vertex. Notably, these classes of graphs achieve the upper bound established in Theorem
4.1
. The mutual visibility numbers of the shadow graphs of cycles and trees were also determined.

4.1
Bounds

Theorem 4.1
.

For any connected graph

,

max\{n(G),2\mu_{i}(G),2\Delta(G)\}\leq\mu(S(G))\leq min\{n(G)+\mu(G),2n(G)-2\}.

Proof.

Firstly,
\mu(S(G))\geq 2\Delta(G)
, since the neighbors of any vertex

in

, together with their shadows, form a mutual visibility set in

.
Next, we show that the set

of all shadow vertices is a mutual visibility set of

. Let
x^{\prime}
and

be two distinct shadow vertices in

. Let

and

be the corresponding twin vertices of

and
y^{\prime}
, respectively.
Let

be any

-geodesic in

and

are

-visible in

.
So assume that

is an

-geodesic in

that is
V^{\prime}
-sound. Consequently,
\mu(S(G))\geq n(G)
.
Next, we prove that
\mu(S(G))\geq 2\mu_{i}(G)
. Let
S
be a maximum independent mutual visibility set of
G
, and set

. We show that
X
is a mutual visibility set of
S(G)
. First, consider
x,y\in S
. Then there exists an
x,y
-geodesic
P
in

that is
S
-sound. By Lemma
2.1
, the path
P
remains an

-geodesic in

and is
X
-sound. Next, let

with

and

for some distinct

. There exists a
u,v
-geodesic in
G
, say
P:u=u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{d-1},u_{d}=v
, that is
S
-sound.
As above, by applying Lemma
2.1
, we obtain an
x,y
-geodesic

P^{\prime}:x=u^{\prime},u_{2},\dots,u_{d-1},v^{\prime}=y
in
S(G)
that is
X
-sound in
S(G)
.
Finally, consider the case where
x\in S
and
y\in S^{\prime}
. If
y=x^{\prime}
, then the
path
x,u,x^{\prime}
, for some

, forms an

-geodesic in

that
is
X
-sound.
On the other hand, if
y=z^{\prime}
for some

with

, then there exists an
x,z
-geodesic

-sound in

. Then, again by Lemma

, the path

is an

-geodesic in
S(G)
that is

-sound. This proves that

\mu(S(G))\geq 2\mu_{i}(G)
.

Let
S
be a maximum mutual visibility set of
S(G)
, and let
(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3},V_{4})
be the corresponding
\mu
-partition of
S
. In the following, we fix

. Then we have
\mu(S(G))=n+n_{1}-n_{4}
. Since

. Consequently, there exists a subset

of size

Next, we prove that
\mu(S(G))\leq 2n-2

. Since

is not a mutual visibility set of

, it follows that

for some

∎

To verify the sharpness of the lower bound, we note that Theorem
4.7
guarantees

. If
G
is a cycle with

Proposition 4.2
.

For integers

∎

Recall that

denotes the number of leaves of a graph

We then have the following bound.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let

Proof.

Let

. Then it is clear that there exists a

-geodesic

-sound. Now, suppose that the path

-sound in

∎

Theorem 4.5
.

For any tree

.

4.2
Characterization

In this section, we provide a characterization of graphs whose shadow graphs have mutual visibility numbers equal to

and

. Moreover, we show that there exists no connected graph

such that

Proposition 4.6
.

The following holds for any graph

,

i.

\mu(S(G))=2
if and only if
G\cong P_{2}
.

ii.

\mu(S(G))=4
if and only if

is either

or

.

iii.

There exists no connected graph
G
for which
\mu(S(G)=3
or
\mu(S(G)=5
.

Proof.

For part (i), it is straightforward to verify that
\mu(S(G))=2
if

. Conversely, suppose that
\mu(S(G))=2
. Then the lower bound in Theorem
4.1
implies that

, and hence

.
To prove (ii), it is straightforward to verify that

\mu(S(P_{3}))=\mu(S(C_{3}))=4
.
Conversely, suppose that
\mu(S(G))=4
.
Then, by the lower bound in Theorem
4.1
, we have

\Delta(G)\leq 2
and
n(G)\leq 4
.
By part (i), it follows that
\Delta(G)=2
, and hence
G
is either a path or a cycle.
Now, Theorems
4.7
and
4.5
together imply that
n(G)=3
.
Consequently,
G\cong P_{3}
or
G\cong C_{3}
.

To prove (iii), suppose that there exists a connected graph
G
such that
\mu(S(G))=3
.
Then, by Theorem
4.1
, we have
\Delta(G)=1
, which contradicts part (i).
Similarly, suppose that
\mu(S(G))=5
.
Then Theorem
4.1
, together with part (i), implies that
\Delta(G)=2
.
Hence,
G
is either a path or a cycle.
However, Theorem
4.7
shows that

must be a path.
Consequently, by Theorem
4.5
, we have

, which contradicts part (ii).

∎

Theorem 4.7
.

For a cycle

. Choose distinct vertices

in

-geodesic

become

.and we fix

Now, assume that

∎

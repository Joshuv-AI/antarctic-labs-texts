Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader for Decoupled Bandits: Best-of-Both-Worlds and Practicality
Chaiwon Kim Seoul National University snukcw128@snu.ac.kr, Jongyeong LeeKorea Institute of Science and Technology jongyeong@kist.re.kr Min-hwan OhSeoul National University minoh@snu.ac.kr

Abstract
We study the decoupled multi-armed bandit (MAB) problem, where the learner selects one arm for exploration and one arm for exploitation in each round. The loss of the explored arm is observed but not counted, while the loss of the exploited arm is incurred without being observed. We propose a policy within the Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (FTPL) framework using Pareto perturbations. Our policy achieves (near-)optimal regret regardless of the environment, i.e., Best-of-Both-Worlds (BOBW): constant regret in the stochastic regime, improving upon the optimal bound of the standard MABs, and minimax optimal regret in the adversarial regime. Moreover, the practicality of our policy stems from avoiding both the convex optimization step required by the previous BOBW policy, Decoupled-Tsallis-INF ( Rouyer & Seldin 2020 ) , and the resampling step that is typically necessary in FTPL. Consequently, it achieves substantial computational improvement, about 20 20 times faster than Decoupled-Tsallis-INF, while also demonstrating better empirical performance in both regimes. Finally, we empirically show that our approach outperforms a pure exploration policy, and that naively combining a pure exploration with a standard exploitation policy is suboptimal.

Introduction

The multi-armed bandit (MAB) is a fundamental framework in sequential decision-making, widely applied in areas such as recommender systems
(
Brodén et al. 2017
;
Zhou et al. 2017
)
, dynamic pricing
(
Misra et al. 2019
;
Mueller et al. 2019
)
, and sequential experimental design
(
Burtini et al. 2015
)
. Imagine a scenario in the standard MAB setting: selecting among
K
arms over a time horizon
T
. The learner aims to minimize the cumulative regret, the difference between the total loss actually incurred and that of the best fixed arm in hindsight. Since only the loss
\ell_{t,i_{t}}
of the selected arm
i_{t}\in[K]
is observed at each round
t\in[T]
, the learner must balance exploiting promising arms with exploring seemingly suboptimal ones to gather information. In other words, a single decision is made under the consideration of both objectives, meaning that exploitation and exploration are
coupled
within each round.

Although this formulation covers many practical applications, it does not capture scenarios where exploration can be performed independently of exploitation.
For instance, in ultra-wide band (UWB) communication systems, the learner can sense a channel different from currently used for transmission, to observe feedback and avoid mutual interference
(
Avner et al. 2012
)
.
Moreover, when a real-time system operates alongside a high-fidelity simulator, the learner can explore in the simulator and exploit in the real system, such as sim-to-real transfer in robotics
(
Zhao et al. 2020
)
without degrading real-world performance.
A related example arises in recommender systems
(
Che et al. 2025
)
, where the platform, given user context (e.g., preferences), can explore with a random subset of users to update the policy, while exploiting the rest by serving the best-known items.

To model such scenarios,
Avner et al. 2012
introduced the
decoupled
MAB setting, where the learner can select two arms at each round: one for observing the loss without incurring it, and one for incurring the loss without observing it.
This decoupling of exploration and exploitation recovers the standard MAB, when the learner is restricted to select the same arm for both objectives.
Note that this framework differs from pure exploration problems, in which the primary focus is on exploration to identify the best (good) arm
(
Even-Dar et al. 2006
)
.
It is also different from explore-then-commit (ETC) style policies, which divide exploration and exploitation into distinct phases, performing only one of the two at each round
(
Garivier et al. 2016
)
.

In the decoupled adversarial MAB,
Avner et al. 2012
established a lower bound of
\Omega(\sqrt{KT})
, matching that of the standard MAB
(
Auer et al. 2002
)
, indicating that the two problems are similarly challenging.
Against an oblivious adversary, their Exp3-type policy obtained an adversarial regret of
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{KT\ln K})
, which improves to
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T\ln K})
when a single dominant arm exists.
Similarly, they achieved a regret of
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T\ln K})
in the decoupled stochastic MAB with a unique optimal arm.
However, this is highly suboptimal, as even an anytime sampling rule designed for pure exploration tasks attains a time-independent cumulative regret of
\tilde{{\mathcal{O}}}(K^{3}/\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}^{2})
in the same setting, despite being aimed at minimizing the expected simple regret
(
Jourdan et al. 2023
)
.
Here,
\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}=\min_{i:\Delta_{i}>0}\Delta_{i}
denotes the minimum suboptimality gap, where
\Delta_{i}=\mathbb{E}[\ell_{\cdot,i}]-\min_{j}\mathbb{E}[\ell_{\cdot,j}]
.

In addition to these limitations, the update rules for arm-selection probabilities and choice of learning rates proposed by
Avner et al. 2012
require prior knowledge of both the time horizon and the environment.
In practice, however, the nature of the environment is typically unknown,
which motivates the design of policies that guarantee (near-)optimal performance across all possible environments, known as the Best-of-Both-Worlds (BOBW) guarantee
(
Bubeck & Slivkins 2012
)
.

Tsallis-INF, based on the Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) framework, is a prominent BOBW policy for standard MABs
(
Zimmert & Seldin 2021
)
.
Extending this to the decoupled setting,
Rouyer & Seldin 2020
proposed Decoupled-Tsallis-INF, which also achieves BOBW: minimax optimal
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{KT})
regret in the adversarial regime and near-optimal time-independent regret of
{\mathcal{O}}(K/\Delta_{\mathrm{min}})
in the stochastic regime.
This result marks a significant improvement over
Avner et al. 2012
and outperforms the optimal bound for the standard stochastic MAB,
{\mathcal{O}}(\sum_{i:\Delta_{i}>0}\log T/\Delta_{i})
.

Despite its strong theoretical guarantees, a practical drawback of
the FTRL framework is the need to solve a convex optimization problem at every round to compute arm-selection probabilities, which can be computationally intensive.
As a more efficient alternative, the Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader (FTPL) framework, of which Exp3 is a special case
(
Auer et al. 2002
;
Bubeck & Cesa-Bianchi 2012
)
, has been studied in standard MABs and achieves BOBW without requiring any convex optimization
(
Honda et al. 2023
;
Lee et al. 2024
)
.
In the decoupled MAB setting, however, only the suboptimal result is known for FTPL-type policy
(
Avner et al. 2012
)
. Hence, the following research question arise:
Can we achieve BOBW for decoupled bandits while improving regret performance and computational efficiency?

1.1
Contribution

A key computational challenge in FTRL and FTPL bandit policies lies in computing arm-selection probabilities, which are required (i) to select an arm and (ii) to construct an unbiased loss estimator, typically using an importance-weighted (IW) estimator.
In FTRL, these probabilities are obtained by solving a convex optimization problem in each round.
By contrast, FTPL avoids this by selecting arms through random perturbations, without explicitly computing probabilities.
This is sufficient for (i), but creates a difficulty for (ii), since the IW estimator requires the probability of the selected arm.
To estimate this, FTPL typically relies on geometric resampling
(
Neu & Bartók 2016
)
or its efficient variant
(
Chen et al. 2025
)
, which incur a per-step cost of
{\mathcal{O}}(K^{2})
or
{\mathcal{O}}(K\log K)
, respectively.
The latter is less costly than the convex optimization step of Tsallis-INF in standard MABs. However, both methods cannot be directly extended to the decoupled setting.
The reason is that resampling methods only estimate the probability of the selected arm, whereas the decoupled setting requires the full exploitation probability vector to compute exploration probability vector.

In this paper, we propose a decoupled FTPL policy that achieves BOBW
without convex optimization or resampling
, attaining the same regret order as BOBW FTRL policy while substantially reducing computational cost.
Our main contributions are as follows:

•

We introduce a simple FTPL policy (Algorithm
1
) with Pareto perturbations for decoupled MAB, the first use of Pareto perturbations in this setting.

•

We achieve
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{KT})
regret in the adversarial regime (Theorem
1
) and
{\mathcal{O}}(K/\Delta_{\min})
regret in the stochastic regime (Theorem
2
), without prior knowledge of the environment.

•

As key algorithmic contributions, our proposed method is practically efficient, avoiding both the optimization step of FTRL and the resampling step of FTPL typically required in bandit settings, resulting in significantly faster computation, as shown in our experiments.

•

Numerical experiments validate our theoretical results, showing improved regret performance and substantially faster computation compared to the existing BOBW policy.

•

We empirically demonstrate that naive combinations of pure exploration policies for exploration and standard exploitation policies degrade performance in the stochastic regime.

2.1
Problem setting

At each round
t\in[T]
, the environment generates a loss vector
\ell_{t}=(\ell_{t,1},\dots,\ell_{t,K})\in[0,1]^{K}
, either stochastically or adversarially.
In the adversarial regime, loss vectors are determined by either an adaptive adversary, in response to the learner’s past actions, or an oblivious adversary, independent of them.
In the stochastic regime, by contrast, they are drawn i.i.d. from an unknown but fixed distribution over
[0,1]^{K}
.

In the decoupled MAB setting, the learner selects an arm
i_{t}\in[K]
to exploit, and an arm
j_{t}\in[K]
to explore, which may be same or different.
Then, the learner suffers
\ell_{t,i_{t}}
without observing it, and observes
\ell_{t,j_{t}}
without suffering it.
The performance of a policy is measured by the pseudo-regret, defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)=\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \ell_{t,i_t}]-\min_{i\in[K]}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \ell_{t,i}]=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\dotp{\hat\ell_t}{w_t - e_{i^*}}],

(1)

where
i^{*}=\argmin_{i\in[K]}\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t,i}]
denotes the best fixed arm in hindsight, assumed unique.
The expectation
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
is taken over the randomness of policy and environment.
Since only partial feedback is available, the learner constructs an unbiased estimator
\hat{\ell}_{t}
of the full loss vector, typically using an importance-weighted (IW) estimator, based on the observed feedback
\ell_{t,j_{t}}
of the explored arm.
The vector
w_{t}
denotes the exploitation probability over arms at round
t
, where
w_{t,i}={\mathbb{P}}[i_{t}=i]
.

We consider the stochastically constrained adversarial (SCA) regime, encompassing the stochastic regime as a special case
(
Wei & Luo 2018
)
. In this regime, the environment may adjust the parameters of the arms (e.g., means) over rounds in response to the learner’s past actions
\{i_{s}\}_{s=1}^{t-1}
. However, it is constrained to maintain fixed differences in the expected losses between any pair of arms, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\ell_{t,i}-\ell_{t,j}]=\Delta_{i,j}
for all
i,j,t
. Let
\Delta_{i}=\Delta_{i,i^{*}}
denote the suboptimality gap of arm
i
, where
i^{*}=\argmin_{i}\Delta_{i,1}
holds in this regime. The pseudo-regret in this regime can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)=\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i \ne i^*} \Delta_i w_{t,i}].

(2)

2.2
Previous approaches in decoupled bandits

Here, we introduce two representative policies for the decoupled MAB setting.
Let
w_{t,i}:={\mathbb{P}}[i_{t}=i]
be the exploitation probability and
p_{t,i}:={\mathbb{P}}[j_{t}=i]
denote the exploration probability of arm
i
at round
t
.
Then, the IW loss estimator is given by
\hat{\ell}_{t,i}=\ell_{t,i}\mathbbm{1}[j_{t}=i]p_{t,i}^{-1}

\hat{L}_{t,i}=\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\hat{\ell}_{s,i}

Avner et al. 2012
proposed a decoupled bandit policy that uses an exploitation strategy based on Exp3
(
Auer et al. 2002
)
and an exploration strategy designed to minimize the variance of the loss estimates, which scales as
\sum_{i}w_{t,i}/p_{t,i}

\displaystyle w_{t,i}=(1-\gamma)\frac{g_{t,i}}{\sum_{j\in[K]}g_{t,j}}+\frac{\gamma}{K}\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{t,i}=\frac{\sqrt{w_{t,i}}}{\sum_{j\in[K]}\sqrt{w_{t,i}}},

(3)

where
\gamma
is a parameter that depends on the learning rate
\eta
and the number of arms
K
.
The weight
g_{t,i}
of each arm is updated according to

\displaystyle g_{t+1,i}=\begin{cases}g_{t,i}\exp(\eta_{\mathrm{sto}}\hat{\ell}_{t,i}+\frac{\eta\beta}{p_{t,i}} ),&\text{in the stochastic regime},\\
g_{t,i}\exp(\eta_{\mathrm{adv}}\hat{\ell}_{t,i}+\frac{\eta\beta}{p_{t,i}} )+\frac{e}{KT}\sum_{i\in[K]}g_{t,i},&\text{in the adversarial regime.}\end{cases}

Both the above update rule and the learning rate
\eta
depends on the regime and

For the BOBW guarantee,
Rouyer & Seldin 2020

\displaystyle w_{t}=\argmin_{w\in{\mathcal{S}}_{K-1}}\quantity{\inp{w}{\hat{L}_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_t}\sum_{i\in[K]}\frac{w_i^\beta- \beta w_i}{\beta(1-\beta)}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad p_{t,i}=\frac{w_{t,i}^{1-\beta/2}}{\sum_{j\in[K]}w_{t,j}^{1-\beta/2}}.

3
Proposed policy: FTPL for decoupled bandits

In this section, we elaborate on technical challenges that arise in applying FTPL to decoupled bandits and present our method to overcome them.

3.1
Technical challenges

A common feature of previous decoupled bandit policies is that the exploration probability
p_{t,i}
is computed using the exploitation probability
w_{t,i}
. In FTPL, however,
w_{t,i}
generally lacks a closed-from expression, except in special cases such as FTPL with Gumbel perturbations, where the induced exploitation probability coincides with the multinomial logit model, i.e., the Exp3 policy.
Although using Exp3 for exploitation is convenient, it results in suboptimal performance in the stochastic regime
(
Avner et al. 2012
)
.
Instead, to obtain BOBW guarantee with FTPL, it is natural to adopt a Fréchet-type perturbation, due to its correspondence with
\beta
-Tsallis-INF
(
Kim & Tewari 2019
;
Lee et al. 2025
)
, an exploitation strategy known to achieve BOBW, even though
w_{t,i}
under Fréchet-type perturbations does not have a closed form.

A natural idea is to estimate
w_{t,i}
via geometric resampling (GR), used in standard MABs to construct the IW estimator
(
Neu & Bartók 2016
;
Chen et al. 2025
)
.
However, GR is introduced to estimate only the probability of the selected arm
i_{t}
, not the full vector
w_{t}
.
This makes direct application infeasible, since computing
p_{t}
requires estimates for all arms.
Even if one could recover
w_{t}
via repeated resampling, the computational cost would increase by a factor of
K
, yielding a per-step cost of at least
{\mathcal{O}}(K^{3})
or
{\mathcal{O}}(K^{2}\log K)
, depending on the method.
Moreover, for arms with very small
w_{t,i}
, the required number of resampling iterations becomes large, as it scales as
1/w_{t,i}
.
To overcome these challenges, we propose an alternative method to approximate
w_{t,i}
without resampling, achieving considerable computational improvement. We describe this method below.

3.2
Proposed policy

Exploitation

At each round
t\in[T]
, the learner selects an arm
i_{t}
to exploit according to the FTPL policy:

\displaystyle i_{t}=\argmin_{i\in[K]}\quantity{\hat{L}_{t,i} - \frac{r_{t,i}}{\eta_t}}=\argmin_{i\in[K]}\quantity{\hat\uL_{t,i} - \frac{r_{t,i}}{\eta_t}},

(5)

where
\hat{\underline{L}}_{t}=\hat{L}_{t}-\mathbf{1}\cdot\min_{i\in[K]}\hat{L}_{t,i}\in[0,\infty)^{K}
represents the loss-gap vector and
\eta_{t}
denotes the learning rate specified later.
Here,
r_{t}=(r_{t,1},\dots,r_{t,K})
is a random perturbation vector whose components are sampled i.i.d. from the Pareto distribution
{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}
with shape parameter
\alpha>1
. The PDF and CDF of
{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}
are

\displaystyle f(x)=\frac{\alpha}{x^{\alpha+1}},\quad F(x)=1-\frac{1}{x^{\alpha}},\quad x\in[1,\infty),

respectively.
Then, the exploitation probability of arm
i\in[K]
given
\hat{}\underline{L}_{t}
can be expressed by
w_{t,i}=\phi_{i}(\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})
, where

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{P}}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}i=\quantity[i = \argmin_{i \in[K]} \qty{\hat\uL_{t,i} - \frac{r_{t,i}}{\eta_t}}]_{i\in[K]}\quantity{\hat\uL_{t,i} - \frac{r_{t,i}}{\eta_t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}f(z+\eta_{t}\hat{}\underline{L}_{t,i})\prod_{j\neq i}F(z+\eta_{t}\hat{}\underline{L}_{t,j})\differential z,{}

(6)

which cannot be expressed in the closed-form.

Exploration

In addition to FTPL exploitation, the learner selects an arm
j_{t}
for exploration according to the probability distribution
p_{t}
, defined as

\displaystyle p_{t,i}=\frac{q_{t,i}}{\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}},\quad\mbox{where}\quad q_{t,i}=\quantity(\frac{1}{1 + \eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i}} \land\frac{1}{\sigma_{t,i}^{1/\alpha}})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}},

(7)

and
\sigma_{t,i}
denotes the rank of
\hat{L}_{t,i}
among
\{\hat{L}_{t,j}\}_{j\in[K]}
, with
1
for the smallest and
K
for the largest value (ties are broken arbitrarily).
It is obvious that
p_{t}
is computable directly without additional convex optimization or resampling, at
O(K\log K)
per-step cost due to sorting
\{\hat{L}_{t,j}\}_{j}
.
Given the correspondence between the
\beta
-Tsallis entropy and Fréchet-type perturbations with
\alpha=1/(1-\beta)
,
q_{t,i}
can be viewed as an approximation of
w_{t,i}^{1/2+1/(2\alpha)}
, which corresponds to
w_{t,i}^{1-\beta/2}
in (
4
).
Our approach of approximating
w_{t,i}
using a tight upper bound (see Lemma
10
in Appendix for details) may be of independent interest for efficiently approximating arm-selection probabilities of FTPL beyond the decoupled setting.

\displaystyle D_{t}:=\quantity{\sum_{i \ne i^*} \frac{1}{(2^{1/\alpha} + \eta_t \hat\uL_{t,i})^\alpha} \leq\frac{1}{2}}.

This result aligns with our analysis, which relies on controlling

, which invalidates the analysis and can increase the cumulative regret.

5
Conclusion

We proposed a practically efficient FTPL policy with Pareto perturbations that guarantees BOBW in the decoupled MAB problem. Our policy achieves minimax optimal regret
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{KT})
in the adversarial regime, and a near-optimal time-independent regret bound of
{\mathcal{O}}(K/\Delta_{\mathrm{min}})
in the stochastically constrained adversarial regime, where
\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}=\min_{i\neq i^{*}}\Delta_{i}
. Our result improves upon the optimal regret bound of the standard stochastic MAB,
{\mathcal{O}}(\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\log T/\Delta_{i})
, which is time-dependent.

In addition to these theoretical strengths, we avoid both the convex optimization step in FTRL, and the resampling step typically required in FTPL for estimating arm-selection probabilities.
As a result, our policy runs about
20
times faster than Decoupled-Tsallis-INF, while achieving better empirical performance across both regimes.
Furthermore, we empirically showed that our policy outperforms the pure exploration policy, which is known to yield time-independent cumulative regret in the decoupled stochastic MAB.
These findings offer insight into the design of refined learning rates (e.g., adaptive learning rates) for FTPL: by using an approximation of
w_{t}
, one can adjust the learning rate in a way analogous to FTRL frameworks, where the learning rate is explicitly determined by
w_{t}
.
This perspective potentially serves as a foundation for establishing BOBW guarantees for FTPL beyond the MAB setting.
Finally, we empirically confirmed that naively mixing a pure exploration policy and a standard bandit policy for exploitation is suboptimal, implying the necessity of dedicated policies for decoupled settings.

We begin by recalling the connection between FTPL and FTRL, where it is known that FTPL can generally be expressed as FTRL with a specific corresponding regularizer

Abernethy et al. 2015

Suggala & Netrapalli 2020

To formalize this, consider a convex potential function
\Phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{K}\to{\mathbb{R}}
for
\phi

\Phi(\lambda)=\mathbb{E}_{r}\quantity[\max_{i\in[K]} \{\lambda_i + r_i\}],

\Phi

\nabla\Phi(\lambda)=\phi(-\lambda)
, where
\phi
denotes the arm-selection probability function of FTPL.
The convex conjugate (or Lagrange transform) of
\Phi

\Phi^{*}(p)=\sup_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K}}\left\langle p,\lambda\right\rangle-\Phi(\lambda),\quad\text{for }p\in\mathrm{Int}\,({\mathcal{P}}_{K-1}),

\mathrm{Int}\,({\mathcal{P}}_{K-1})

It is known that FTPL is equivalent to FTRL with regularizer
\Phi^{*}(p)
.
By standard results in the convex analysis

Zhan et al. 2025

w_{t}=\nabla\Phi(-\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})

w_{t}\in\argmin_{x\in{\mathcal{P}}_{K-1}}\quantity{\Phi^*(x)/\eta_t + \inp{x}{\hat{L}_t}}.

It is worth noting that if

lies on the boundary of the simplex, the gradient
\nabla\Phi^{*}(p)
may not exist.
Nevertheless, we consider minimization over
{\mathcal{P}}_{K-1}
rather than
\mathrm{Int}\,({\mathcal{P}}_{K-1})
, since the regularizer
\Phi^{*}(p)
remains well-defined even on boundary points, although its gradients may be unbounded.

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle w_{t}-\phi(\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t+1}),\hat{\ell}_{t}\right\rangle.

(
\because D_{\Phi}(\cdot,\cdot)\geq 0
)

Since
w_{t}=\phi(\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})
, it remains to control the second term in (
12
).

While it can be obtained by direct application of Lemma 3.4 of
Zhan et al. 2025
, we provide the corresponding proof here for completeness.
By definition of
\Phi^{*}
and
w_{t}=\nabla\Phi(-\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(w_{t})=-\left\langle\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t},w_{t}\right\rangle-\Phi(-\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\eta_t \hat{L}_t}{e_{i_t}}]+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\min_i \eta_t\hat{L}_{t,i}-r_{t,i}]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\eta_t \hat{L}_t}{e_{i_t}}]+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\eta_t \hat{L}_t-r_t}{e_{i_t}}]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\quantity[r_{t,i_t}].

By definition of
\Phi
, we have
\Phi(\lambda)\geq\mathbb{E}_{r}\quantity[\inp{r+\lambda}{p}]
for any
p\in{\mathcal{P}}_{K-1}
and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K}
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(e_{i^{*}})=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K}}\left\langle x,e_{i}^{*}\right\rangle-\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K}}\left\langle x,e_{i}^{*}\right\rangle-\mathbb{E}_{r}\quantity[\inp{r+x}{e_{i^*}}]

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{r}\quantity[\inp{r}{e_{i^*}}]=-\mathbb{E}_{r}\quantity[r_{i^*}],

which concludes the proof.
∎

A.2
Proof for the stability term (Lemma
5
)

Lemma
5
(restated)

For any
t\in[T]
,
i\in[K]
, Algorithm
1
with
\alpha>1
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\quantity[\hat\ell_{t,i}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t\hat{L}_t) - \phi_i(\eta_t\hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]\leq e(\alpha+1)\eta_{t}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}q_{t,i},

where
q_{t}
is defined in (
7
).

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{K}
and
\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{\partial\phi_{i}(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{i}}
, which is

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\min_{j}\lambda_{j}}^{\infty}-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{(z+\lambda_{i}+1)^{\alpha+2}}\prod_{j\neq i}\quantity(1- \frac{1}{(z+\lambda_j +1)^\alpha})\differential z

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{(z+\underline{\lambda}_{i}+1)^{\alpha+2}}\prod_{j\neq i}\quantity(1- \frac{1}{(z+\ul_j +1)^\alpha})\differential z,

where the underline denotes
\underline{\lambda}=\lambda-\bm{1}\cdot\min_{j}\lambda_{j}
.
Note that
-\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\lambda)
is decreasing with respect to
\lambda_{i}
and increasing with respect to
\lambda_{j}
.
Then, by definition, we have

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}[i=j_{t}]

\displaystyle\quantity(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1}))

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}[i=j_{t}]\int_{0}^{\eta_{t}\ell_{t,i}p_{t,i}^{-1}}-\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t}+xe_{i})\differential x

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}[i=j_{t}]\int_{0}^{\eta_{t}\ell_{t,i}p_{t,i}^{-1}}-\phi_{i}^{\prime}(\eta_{t}\hat{L}_{t})\differential x

(
\because
decreasing w.r.t.
\lambda_{i}
)

\displaystyle=\mathbbm{1}[i=j_{t}]\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\eta_{t}\ell_{t,i}p_{t,i}^{-1}}{(z+\underline{\lambda}_{i}+1)^{\alpha+2}}\prod_{j\neq i}\quantity(1-\frac{1}{(z+\ul_j+1)^\alpha})\differential z.

Let
I_{i,\alpha+2}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(z+\lambda_{i}+1)^{\alpha+2}}\prod_{j\neq i}\quantity(1-\frac{1}{(z+\lambda_j+1)^\alpha})\differential z
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\quantity[\hat{\ell}_{t,i}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\quantity[\frac{\ell_{t,i}\I[j_t=i]}{p_{t,i}}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(\alpha+1)\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\frac{\ell_{t,i}^2 \I[j_t =i]}{p_{t,i}^2} I_{i,\alpha+2}(\eta_t \hat{\uL}_t)\middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(\alpha+1)\eta_{t}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\frac{I_{i,\alpha+2}(\eta_t \hat{\uL}_t)}{p_{t,i}}\middle| \hat{L}_t],

where the last inequality follows from
\ell_{t,i}\leq 1
and
\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}[j_{t}=i]|\hat{L}_{t}]=p_{t,i}
.

By definition of
I_{i,\alpha+2}
, one can see that

\displaystyle I_{i,\alpha+2}(\underline{\lambda})\leq I_{i,\alpha+2}(\lambda^{*}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad\lambda_{j}^{*}=\begin{cases}\underline{\lambda}_{i},&\sigma_{j}\leq\sigma_{i},\\
\infty,&\sigma_{j}>\sigma_{i},\end{cases}

where
\sigma_{i}
denotes the rank of
\lambda_{i}
in the increasing order of
\lambda
, i.e.,
\sigma_{i}<\sigma_{j}
iff
\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda_{j}
with arbitrary tie-breaking rule.
In the later of the proof, we assume
\sigma_{i}=i
without loss of generality for the simplicity, i.e.,
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots,\lambda_{K}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle I_{i,\alpha+2}(\lambda^{*})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(z+\underline{\lambda}_{i}+1)^{\alpha+2}}\quantity(1-\frac{1}{(z+\ul_i+1)^{\alpha}})^{i-1}\differential z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{(1+\underline{\lambda}_{i})^{\alpha}}}w^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1-w)^{i-1}\differential w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}B\quantity(\frac{1}{(1+\ul_i)^{\alpha}}; 1+\frac{1}{\alpha},i),

where
B(x;a,b)=\int_{0}^{x}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\differential t
denotes the incomplete Beta function.
By elementary calculation, we obtain for any
x\in[0,1]

\displaystyle B\quantity(x; 1+\frac{1}{\alpha},i)=\int_{0}^{x}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(1-t)^{i-1}\differential t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{x}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-t(i-1)}\differential t

\displaystyle\leq e\int_{0}^{x}t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-ti}\differential t

(
\because x\in[0,1]
)

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{i^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\gamma\quantity(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}, xi),

where
\gamma(a,x)
denotes the lower incomplete gamma function.
Since
\gamma(a,x)\leq\Gamma(a)
for any
x>0
, we have

I_{i,\alpha+2}(\lambda^{*})\leq\frac{e}{\alpha i^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Gamma\quantity(1+\frac{1}{\alpha})\leq\frac{e}{\alpha i^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Gamma\quantity(2)=\frac{e}{\alpha i^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}.

(13)

On the other hand, by Equation 8.10.2 of
Olver 2010
, it holds that

\displaystyle\gamma\quantity(1+\frac{1}{\alpha}, \frac{i}{(1+\ul_i)^{\alpha}})\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\frac{i^{1/\alpha}}{(1+\underline{\lambda}_{i})}\min\quantity(1, \frac{i}{(1+\ul_i)^{\alpha}}),

which implies

\displaystyle I_{i,\alpha+2}(\lambda^{*})\leq\frac{e}{\alpha+1}\frac{1}{(1+\underline{\lambda}_{i})i}.

(14)

Therefore, from (
13
) and (
14
), we obtain

\alpha(\alpha+1)\mathbb{E}\quantity[\frac{I_{i,\alpha+2}(\eta_t \hat{\uL}_t)}{p_{t,i}}\middle| \hat{L}_t]\leq\frac{e(\alpha+1)}{p_{t,i}i^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\land\frac{e\alpha}{p_{t,i}(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})i}.

By definition of
p_{t}
and
q_{t,i}
in (
7
), when
\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})}\leq\frac{1}{i^{1/\alpha}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{t,i}}\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})i}=\sum_{j}q_{t,j}\frac{\sqrt{i(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})}}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})i}=\sum_{j}q_{t,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})}}=\sum_{j}q_{t,j}q_{t,i}.

On the other hand, when
\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})}\geq\frac{1}{i^{1/\alpha}}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{t,i}i^{1+1/\alpha}}=\sum_{j}q_{t,j}\frac{\sqrt{i^{1+1/\alpha}}}{i^{1+1/\alpha}}=\sum_{j}q_{t,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{i^{1+1/\alpha}}}=\sum_{j}q_{t,j}q_{t,i}.

Therefore, in any cases, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\quantity[\hat{\ell}_{t,i}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]\leq e(\alpha+1)\eta_{t}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}q_{t,i},

which concludes the proof.
∎

A.3
Proof for the penalty term (Lemma
6
)

Lemma
6
(restated)

For any
t\in[T]
, Algorithm
1
with
\alpha>1
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*} \middle| \hat{L}_t]\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{}\underline{L}_{t,i})^{\alpha-1}}\land C_{\alpha}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

where
C_{\alpha}=\frac{2\alpha^{3}+(e-2)\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)}
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is almost the same as that of Lemma 12 of
Lee et al. 2024
, except that we provide a slightly tighter bound.

By the choice of Pareto perturbations, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\quantity[r_{t,i_t}-r_{t,i^*} \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\I[I_t=i] r_{t,i}\middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\quantity(\frac{\alpha}{(z+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i})^\alpha})\prod_{j\neq i}\quantity(1-\frac{1}{(z+\eta_t \hat{\uL}_{t,j})^\alpha})\differential z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\quantity(\frac{\alpha}{(z+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i})^\alpha})\differential z

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})^{\alpha-1}}.

Let
k_{\alpha}(z)=\sum_{i\in[K]}\frac{1}{(z+\eta_{t}\hat{\underline{L}}_{t,i})^{\alpha}}\in(0,\frac{K}{z^{\alpha}}]
.
Then,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}

\displaystyle\quantity(\frac{\alpha}{(z+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i})^\alpha})\prod_{j\neq i}\quantity(1-\frac{1}{(z+\eta_t \hat{\uL}_{t,j})^\alpha})\differential z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\quantity(\frac{\alpha}{(z+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i})^\alpha})\exp(-\sum_{j\ne i} \qty(1-\frac{1}{(z+\eta_t \hat{\uL}_{t,j})^\alpha}))\differential z

\displaystyle\leq e\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\quantity(\frac{\alpha}{(z+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i})^\alpha})e^{-k_{\alpha}(z)}\differential z\leq e\int_{1}^{\infty}k_{\alpha}(z)e^{-k_{\alpha}(z)}\differential z.

Since
xe^{-x}\leq e^{-1}
for
x\geq 0
and
xe^{-x}
is increasing for
x\leq 1
and
k_{\alpha}(z)
holds for
z\geq K^{1/\alpha}
, we have

\displaystyle e\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}k_{\alpha}(z)e^{-k_{\alpha}(z)}\differential z

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{1}^{K^{1/\alpha}}1\differential z+e\int_{K^{1/\alpha}}^{\infty}\alpha\frac{K}{z^{\alpha}}e^{-\frac{K}{z^{\alpha}}}\differential z

\displaystyle=\alpha(K^{1/\alpha}-1)+eK^{1/\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}w^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}e^{-w}\differential w

\displaystyle=K^{1/\alpha}\quantity(\alpha+e \gamma\qty(1-\frac{1}{\alpha},1))-\alpha,

where
\gamma
denotes the lower incomplete gamma function.
By the same arguments in
Lee et al. 2024
, it holds that

\gamma\quantity(1-\frac{1}{\alpha},1)\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}(1-e^{-1})}{(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)}+\frac{\alpha e^{-1}}{\alpha-1}=\frac{(1+e^{-1})\alpha^{2}-e^{-1}\alpha}{(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)},

which implies

\displaystyle e\alpha\int_{1}^{\infty}k_{\alpha}(z)e^{-k_{\alpha}(z)}\differential z

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(\alpha+\frac{(e+1)\alpha^2 - \alpha}{(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)})K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-\alpha

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\alpha^{3}+(e-2)\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}-\alpha,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 7
.

While the additional
-\alpha
term is not directly used in the analysis, it is easy to observe that the upper bound vanishes as
\alpha\to\infty
.
This behavior is intuitive since larger values of
\alpha
correspond to perturbation distributions with lighter right tails, increasingly concentrated around the left endpoint at
1
.
In the limit, as
\alpha\to\infty
, the perturbation converges to a Dirac delta function at
1
, eliminating any randomness, i.e., the difference between perturbations becomes zero.

Appendix B
Regret bound for adversarial bandits (Theorem
1
)

Theorem
1
(restated)

In the adversarial regime, Algorithm
1
with
\alpha>1
and
\eta_{t}=cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}/\sqrt{t}
for
c>0
satisfies
\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{KT})
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\hat\ell_t}{\phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_t) - \phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_{t+1})}]+\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{D}}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}].

For the stability term (the first term), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\hat\ell_t}{\phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_t) - \phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_{t+1})}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{i \in[K]}\E\qty[\hat{\ell}_{t,i}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T e(\alpha+1)\eta_t \sum_{j \in[K]} q_{t,j} \sum_{i \in[K]} q_{t,i}]

(by Lemma
5
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{4\alpha^{2}(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{t}}K^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}{}

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{4c\alpha^{2}(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8c\alpha^{2}(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}\sqrt{KT},{}

(16)

where (
15
) follows by the definition of
q_{t,i}
,

\sum_{i\in[K]}q_{t,i}=\sum_{i\in[K]}\quantity(\frac{1}{1+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i}} \land\frac{1}{\sigma_i^{1/\alpha}})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\leq\sum_{i\in[K]}i^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\leq\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}K^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}.

(17)

For the penalty term (the second term), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}]}{\eta_{1}}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}+\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}],{}

(18)

where the inequality follows from Lemma 18 of
Lee et al. 2024
.
For the second term in (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}\quantity[\E_{r_t \sim\gP_\alpha^K}\qty[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*} \middle| \hat{L}_t]]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{c}\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\quantity(\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{t-1})C_{\alpha}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\alpha}\sqrt{K}}{c}\quantity(\sqrt{T+1}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{c}\sqrt{KT},{}

(19)

where the last inequality follows from
\sqrt{x+1}-1\leq\sqrt{x}
for
x>0
.
Therefore, from (
16
), (
18
), and (
19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq\quantity(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{2\alpha^3+(e-2)\alpha^2}{c(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)})\sqrt{KT}+\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Regret bound for stochastic bandits (Theorem
2
)

To analyze the regret in the stochastic regime, we define the event

\displaystyle D_{t}:=\quantity{\sum_{i \ne i^*} \frac{1}{(2^{1/\alpha} + \eta_t \hat\uL_{t,i})^\alpha} \leq\frac{1}{2}}.

When
D_{t}
occurs, it implies that
\hat{}\underline{L}_{t,i^{*}}=0
, indicating that the optimal arm
i^{*}
has been accurately identified based on the information so far. In the subsequent proof, we separately analyze the cases where
D_{t}
holds and where its complement
D_{t}^{c}
holds.

Theorem
2
(restated)

In the stochastically constrained adversarial regime with a unique best arm
i^{*}
, Algorithm
1
with
\alpha\in(1,3]
and
\eta_{t}=cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}/\sqrt{t}
for
c>0
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(\qty(\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i\ne i^*} \sqrt{K} \Delta_i^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}})+\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}).

Proof.

We bound the stability term and penalty terms by separately analyzing the contributions on the events
D_{t}
and
D_{t}^{c}
.

Stability term

For the stability term, we start from

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\hat\ell_t}{\phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_t) - \phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_{t+1})}]=\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{i \in[K]}\E\qty[\hat{\ell}_{t,i}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]].{}

(20)

On
D_{t}
, we separate the contribution of the optimal arm
i^{*}
from that of the suboptimal arms for a tighter analysis. For the suboptimal arms, Lemma
5
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\hat{\ell}_{t,i} \qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Here, the last step follows from

\displaystyle q_{t,i}\leq\quantity(\frac{1}{1+\eta_t\hat\uL_{t,i}})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}\leq(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\leq(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(21)

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
10
, and the last one holds since
1-\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(1,3]
.
For the optimal arm, Lemma
9
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\mathbb{E}\quantity[\hat{\ell}_{t,i^*}\qty(\phi_{i^*}(\eta_t \hat{L}_t) - \phi_{i^*}(\eta_t \hat{L}_{t+1})) \eval\hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\quantity[\sum_{j \in[K]} q_{t,j} \sum_{i \ne i^*} \frac{e(1-e^{-1})\eta_t \alpha}{(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}(1+\eta_t\hat{\uL}_{t,i})^{\alpha+1}} + \frac{1}{1-e^{-1}}(1-e^{-1})^{\frac{\zeta}{\eta_t}}\qty(\frac{\zeta}{\eta_t} + e)]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e(1-e^{-1})\alpha}{(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\quantity(\frac{1}{1+\eta_t\hat\uL_{t,i}})^{\frac{\alpha+1}{2}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})

(by (
37
))

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e(1-e^{-1})\alpha}{(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}).

(by (
21
))

Combining the contributions from both the optimal and suboptimal arms, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\sum_{i\in[K]}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\hat{\ell}_{t,i}\qty(\phi_i(\eta_t \hat{L}_t)-\phi_i(\eta_{t} \hat{L}_{t+1})) \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\quantity(\alpha+1+\frac{\alpha(1-e^{-1})}{(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}})\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\quantity(\frac{2c\alpha(\alpha+1)e}{\alpha-1}+\frac{2c\alpha^2(1-e^{-1})e}{(\alpha-1)(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}})\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}),

where the last step follows from (
17
).
On
D_{t}^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\hat\ell_t}{\phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_t) - \phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_{t+1})} \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}e(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{j\in[K]}q_{t,j}\sum_{i\in[K]}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\frac{4\alpha^{2}(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}\frac{cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{t}}K^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}

(by (
17
))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\frac{4c\alpha^{2}(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}}.

Combining the bounds for both
D_{t}
and
D_{t}^{c}
, the stability term can be bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\quantity[\inp{\hat\ell_t}{\phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_t) - \phi(\eta_t\hat{L}_{t+1})}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t] \qty(\frac{2c\alpha(\alpha+1)e}{\alpha-1}+\frac{2c\alpha^2(1-e^{-1})e}{(\alpha-1)(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}}) \frac{K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}} \sum_{i \ne i^*}(2e^2w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}]

\displaystyle\qquad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t^c] \frac{4c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} \sqrt{\frac{K}{t}}]+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1}).{}

(22)

Penalty term

For the penalty term, we can start from (
18
):

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*}]+\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}.{}

(23)

On
D_{t}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*} \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{}\underline{L}_{t,i})^{\alpha-1}}.

(by Lemma
6
)

For
t\geq 2
, Lemma
10
implies

\displaystyle\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{1}{(1+\eta_{t}\hat{}\underline{L}_{t,i})^{\alpha-1}}\leq\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{2c\sqrt{t-1}}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}},

which yields

\displaystyle\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*} \middle| \hat{L}_t]\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}]\frac{\alpha}{2c(\alpha-1)}\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}(2e^{2}w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}},

where we used the fact that
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(1,3]
.

On
D_{t}^{c}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=2}^{T+1}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t} - r_{t,i^*} \middle| \hat{L}_t]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=2}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t} - \frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})C_{\alpha}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

(by Lemma
6
)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\frac{K^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{c\sqrt{2}\sqrt{t}}C_{\alpha}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbbm{1}[D_{t}^{c}]\frac{C_{\alpha}}{c\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}},

where the first step assumes
\eta_{T}=\eta_{T+1}
for simplicity and the second step is due to the fact that
\sqrt{t/(t-1)}\leq\sqrt{2}
for
t\geq 2
.
Here, the assumption
\eta_{T}=\eta_{T+1}
does not affect the behavior of the algorithm, as the procedure terminates at round
T
.
Although this assumes knowledge of
T
, even without it one can just introduce an additional
{\mathcal{O}}(1/\sqrt{T+1})
term, which does not affect the overall regret for sufficiently large
T
.
Combining the bounds for both
D_{t}
and
D_{t}^{c}
, the penalty term can be bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T+1}\quantity(\frac{1}{\eta_t}-\frac{1}{\eta_{t-1}})\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}\sim{\mathcal{P}}_{\alpha}^{K}}\quantity[r_{t,i_t}-r_{t,i^*}]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^{T} \I[D_t] \frac{\alpha}{2c(\alpha-1)} \frac{K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}} \sum_{i\ne i^*} (2e^2w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
+ \sum_{t=1}^{T} \I[D_t^c] \frac{C_\alpha}{c\sqrt{2}} \sqrt{\frac{K}{t}}]+\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}.{}

(24)

Finally, combining (
22
) with (
24
), the regret can be upper bounded as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t] \qty(\frac{2c\alpha(\alpha+1)e}{\alpha-1}+\frac{2c\alpha^2(1-e^{-1})e}{(\alpha-1)(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}} + \frac{\alpha}{2c(\alpha-1)}) \frac{K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}} \sum_{i \ne i^*}(2e^2w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t^c] \qty(\frac{4c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{C_\alpha}{c\sqrt{2}}) \sqrt{\frac{K}{t}}]+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}.{}

(25)

Self-bounding technique

We employ the self-bounding technique of
Zimmert & Seldin 2021
in the stochastically constrained adversarial regime to demonstrate that our policy adapts to broader settings beyond the purely stochastic regime. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathrm{Reg}(T)-\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i \ne i^*} \Delta_i w_{t,i}]

(by (
2
))

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathrm{Reg}(T)-\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \qty(\sI[D_t]\sum_{i \ne i^*}\Delta_i w_{t,i} + \sI[D_t^c]\sum_{i \ne i^*}\Delta_i w_{t,i})]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \sI[D_t] \sum_{i\ne i^*} \qty(\frac{Z_1(\alpha) w_{t,i}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}} - \Delta_iw_{t,i})]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \sI[D_t^c] \qty(\qty(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}} - \frac{1-e^{-1/2}}{2} \Delta_\Min)]

(by (
11
))

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t] \sum_{i \ne i^*} Z_2(\alpha) \Delta_i^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t^c] \max\qty(\qty(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}} - \frac{1-e^{-1/2}}{2} \Delta_\Min, 0)],{}

(26)

where the last step follows from

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\quantity(\frac{Z_1(\alpha)w_{t,i}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}}-\Delta_iw_{t,i})\leq\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\max_{w\in[0,1]}\quantity(\frac{Z_1(\alpha) w^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}}-\Delta_iw)\leq\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{2}(\alpha)\Delta_{i}^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}},

which is a direct application of Lemma 8 in
Rouyer & Seldin 2020
. Here, the constants
Z_{1}(\alpha;\zeta)
and
Z_{2}(\alpha;\zeta)
are

\displaystyle Z_{1}(\alpha;\zeta)=Z_{1}(\alpha)=(2e^{2})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\quantity(\frac{4c\alpha(\alpha+1)e}{\alpha-1}+\frac{4c\alpha^2(1-e^{-1})e}{(\alpha-1)(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}} + \frac{\alpha}{c(\alpha-1)})K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}

(27)

and

\displaystyle Z_{2}(\alpha;\zeta)=Z_{2}(\alpha)=Z_{1}(\alpha)^{\alpha}\quantity(\qty(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha})^{\alpha-1}-\qty(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha})^\alpha).

(28)

Next, we define the time step
T_{\mathrm{cut}}
such that for
t>\left\lfloor T_{\mathrm{cut}}\right\rfloor
, the last term in (
26
) evaluates to zero. Hence, we can bound the sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max\quantity(A\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}} - B\cdot\Delta_\Min,0)\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\left\lfloor T_{\mathrm{cut}}\right\rfloor}\quantity(A\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}} - B\cdot\Delta_\Min)\leq\frac{2A^{2}}{B}\frac{K}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}},

(29)

where
T_{\mathrm{cut}}:=\frac{A^{2}K}{B^{2}\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}^{2}}
. Using this, we can upper bound (
26
) as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t] \sum_{i \ne i^*} Z_2(\alpha) \Delta_i^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \I[D_t^c] \max\qty(\qty(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}} - \frac{1-e^{-1/2}}{2} \Delta_\Min, 0)]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{2}(\alpha)\Delta_{i}^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{1-e^{-1/2}}\quantity(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})^{2}\frac{K}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}},

(by (
29
))

which implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(\qty(\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i\ne i^*} \sqrt{K} \Delta_i^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}})+\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}),

and thus concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary
3
(restated)

In the stochastically constrained adversarial regime with a unique best arm
i^{*}
, Algorithm
1
with
\alpha=3
and
\eta_{t}=cK^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}}/\sqrt{t}
for
c>0
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(\sqrt{\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}\sum_{i\ne i^*}\frac{1}{\Delta_i}}+\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}).

Proof.

By Theorem
2
, for
\alpha\in(1,3]
, the regret of our policy satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(\qty(\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i\ne i^*} \sqrt{K} \Delta_i^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}})+\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}),

which depends on the time horizon
T
.
Inspired by Theorem 4 of
Rouyer & Seldin 2020
, we next derive a
T
-independent bound for
\alpha\in(2,3]
and identify the value of
\alpha
that minimizes this bound.

Let
T_{0}:=D\quantity(\frac{x}{\Delta_\Min})^{2}
for
D\geq 1
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. For
t\leq T_{0}
, the proof follows identically to the adversarial regime, yielding a contribution of order
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{KT_{0}})
. For the remaining rounds
t>T_{0}
, we apply the same argument as in the stochastic case, i.e., (
25
), which gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=T_0+1}^{T} \I[D_t] \qty(\frac{2c\alpha(\alpha+1)e}{\alpha-1} + \frac{2c\alpha^2(1-e^{-1})e}{(\alpha-1)(1-\zeta)^{\alpha+1}} + \frac{\alpha}{2c(\alpha-1)}) \frac{K^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}} \sum_{i\ne i^*} (2e^2w_{t,i})^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=T_0+1}^{T} \I[D_t^c] \qty(\frac{4c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{C_\alpha}{c\sqrt{2}}) \sqrt{\frac{K}{t}}]+\quantity(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{C_\alpha}{c})\sqrt{KT_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c},{}

(30)

for any
\zeta\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(1,3]
. Here,
C_{\alpha}=\frac{2\alpha^{3}+(e-2)\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-1)}
denotes the constant defined in Lemma
6
.
Following the similar steps as Theorem
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathrm{Reg}(T)-\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{i \ne i^*} \Delta_i w_{t,i}]{}

(31)

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\mathrm{Reg}(T)-\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=T_0+1}^T \sum_{i \ne i^*} \Delta_i w_{t,i}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=T_0+1}^T \I[D_t] \sum_{i \ne i^*} \qty(\frac{Z_1(\alpha)w_{t,i}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{t}}-\Delta_iw_{t,i})]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\quantity[\sum_{t=T_0+1}^T \I[D_t^c] \qty( \qty(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})\sqrt{\frac{K}{t}} - \frac{1-e^{-1/2}}{2}\Delta_{\Min})]

\displaystyle\quad+\quantity(\frac{16c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{2C_\alpha}{c})\sqrt{KT_{0}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=T_{0}+1}^{T}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{2}(\alpha)\Delta_{i}^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\frac{4}{1-e^{-1/2}}\quantity(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})^{2}\frac{K}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\quantity(\frac{16c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{2C_\alpha}{c})\sqrt{KT_{0}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}.{}

(32)

Here,
Z_{1}(\alpha)
and
Z_{2}(\alpha)
are the constants defined in (
27
) and (
28
), respectively. By Lemma
12
, for
\alpha\in(2,3]
, the first term in (
32
) can be bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{t=T_{0}+1}^{T}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{2}(\alpha)\Delta_{i}^{1-\alpha}t^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{3}(\alpha)\frac{\sqrt{K}D^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\Delta_{i}},

which gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{3}(\alpha)\frac{\sqrt{K}D^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\Delta_{i}}+\frac{4}{1-e^{-1/2}}\quantity(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})^{2}\frac{K}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\quantity(\frac{16c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{2C_\alpha}{c})\sqrt{KT_{0}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}Z_{3}(\alpha)\frac{\sqrt{K}D^{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\Delta_{i}}+\frac{4}{1-e^{-1/2}}\quantity(\frac{8c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2}+\frac{C_\alpha\sqrt{2}}{c})^{2}\frac{K}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\quantity(\frac{16c\alpha^2(\alpha+1)e}{(\alpha-1)^2} + \frac{2C_\alpha}{c})\frac{x\sqrt{KD}}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}+{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(c^2K^{\frac{2}{\alpha}-1})+\frac{2\alpha\Gamma(1-1/\alpha)}{\alpha-1}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{c},{}

(33)

where

\displaystyle Z_{3}(\alpha)=\frac{2x^{2-\alpha}Z_{2}(\alpha)}{(\alpha-2)\sqrt{K}}.

The first and third terms in (
33
) are minimized when
\alpha=3
and

\displaystyle D=\frac{Z_{3}(3)}{x\quantity(144ce + 2C_3/c)}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}{\Delta_{i}},

which, by the AM-GM inequality, leads to the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{Z_{3}(3)}{\Delta_{i}}\frac{\sqrt{K}}{\sqrt{D}}+\quantity(144ce + \frac{2C_3}{c})\frac{x\sqrt{KD}}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\quantity(144ce + \frac{2C_3}{c})\frac{xZ_{3}(3)K}{\Delta_{\mathrm{min}}}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{1}{\Delta_{i}}}{}

(34)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(\sqrt{\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}\sum_{i\ne i^*}\frac{1}{\Delta_i}}).{}

(35)

Note that the feasible range of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
is determined by the two constraints
T_{0}\leq T_{\mathrm{cut}}
and
D\geq 1
:

\displaystyle x\leq\min\quantity{\frac{4(144ce + 2C_3/c)(72ce+C_3\sqrt{2}/c)^2}{Z_3(3)(1-e^{-1/2})^2}\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}\qty(\sum_{i\ne i^*}\frac{1}{\Delta_i})^{-1}, \frac{Z_3(3)}{144ce + 2C_3/c} \sum_{i\ne i^*} \frac{\Delta_\Min}{\Delta_i}}.

Any choice of
x
within this range is valid, since
x
cancels out in (
34
) through the term
xZ_{3}(3)
, as
Z_{3}(3)
contains
x^{-1}
, and thus does not affect the final bound.
Finally, from (
33
) and (
35
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}(T)\leq{\mathcal{O}}\quantity(\sqrt{\frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}\sum_{i\ne i^*}\frac{1}{\Delta_i}} + \frac{K}{\Delta_\Min}),

which concludes the proof.

The choice of
c

We determine the choice of
c
in (
34
). Specifically,

\displaystyle\quantity(144ce + \frac{2C_3}{c})xZ_{3}(3)

\displaystyle=\frac{32e^{4}}{27}\quantity(144ce + \frac{36+9e}{5c})\quantity(24ce + \frac{18ce(1-e^{-1})}{(1-\zeta)^4} + \frac{3}{2c})^{3}.

(36)

For notational simplicity, we express the RHS in the form
f(c)=(h_{1}c+h_{2}/c)(h_{3}c+h_{4}/c)^{3}
, where
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}
are constants determined by the coefficients above. To minimize
f(c)
, we substitute
x=c^{2}
and define
g(x)=(h_{1}x+h_{2})(h_{3}x+h_{4})^{3}/x^{2}
. Differentiating
g(x)
with respect to
x
gives

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(x)=\frac{(h_{3}x+h_{4})^{2}(2h_{1}h_{3}x^{2}+(h_{2}h_{3}-h_{1}h_{4})x-2h_{2}h_{4})}{x^{3}}.

Thus, the stationary points of
g(x)
, where
g^{\prime}(x)=0
, are obtained by solving the quadratic equation
2h_{1}h_{3}x^{2}+(h_{2}h_{3}-h_{1}h_{4})x-2h_{2}h_{4}=0
. This yields

\displaystyle x^{*}=\frac{-h_{2}h_{3}+h_{1}h_{4}+\sqrt{(h_{2}h_{3}-h_{1}h_{4})^{2}+16h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}h_{4}}}{4h_{1}h_{3}}.

Recalling that
x=c^{2}
, the optimal choice of
c
is given by

\displaystyle c^{*}=\sqrt{x^{*}}.

When
\zeta=10^{-1}
, the above computation gives

\displaystyle c^{*}\simeq 0.128,\quad f(c)\simeq 25799.360.

As
\zeta
decreases, the resulting constant decreases.

Remark 8
.

There are some possible ways to further reduce the leading multiplicative constant of (
36
) in the regret bound. Firstly, in (
31
), one may introduce a parameter
\kappa\in(0,1)
and consider (
30
)
-\kappa\times
(
2
). In our analysis, we use
\kappa=1/2
for simplicity, but optimizing over
\kappa
can reduce the constant. Secondly, in (
10
), one may define

\displaystyle D_{t}=\quantity{\sum_{i \ne i^*} \frac{1}{(y^{1/\alpha} + \eta_t \hat{\uL}_{t,i})^\alpha} \leq\frac{1}{y}},

where we set
y=2
for simplicity in our analysis. By optimizing
y
, the constant in the regret bound can be reduced. For instance, the term
2e^{2}
in (
21
) contributes to the constant, and under the above definition of
D_{t}
, it becomes
ye^{\frac{2}{y-1}}
, which can be decreased by choosing an appropriate
y
. Finally, in the process of choosing
c
, we set
\zeta=10^{-1}
for simplicity. Together with the optimizations discussed above, tuning
\zeta

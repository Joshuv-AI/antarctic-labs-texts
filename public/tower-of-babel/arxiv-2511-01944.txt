On the existence of solutions of fractional differential equations in Banach spaces
Dušan Oberta

Abstract
. Utilising the notion of measures of non-compactness and Kamke function of order α \alpha , we address the question of solvability of fractional differential equations in Banach spaces. In particular, we provide sufficient conditions ensuring the existence of a local solution. Our main existence theorem is then applied on countable systems of fractional differential equations arising from semi-discretisation of fractional PDEs with p p -Laplacian.

As in the case of

, natural generalisation of ODEs in Banach spaces is in the form of fractional differential equations (FDEs) in Banach spaces. A simple example of application of the theory of FDEs in Banach spaces is a semi-discretisation of the anomalous diffusion equation (see e.g.

\frac{{}^{C}\!\partial^{\alpha}_{a}{u}}{\partial{t}^{\alpha}}\left(t,x\right)=\mathchoice{\frac{\partial^{2}\mkern 0.0muu}{\partial\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\partial^{2}\mkern 0.0muu}{\partial\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\partial^{2}\mkern 0.0muu}{\partial\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\partial^{2}\mkern 0.0muu}{\partial\mkern 0.0mux^{2}}}}\left(t,x\right),

Basic results for classical FDEs can be found e.g. in

Even more relevant is the area of FDEs in Banach spaces, where some basic existence results have been proved only quite recently, and the theory is still largely incomplete. One of the goals of this paper is to fill in some of these gaps and to revise some of the existing results even for the integer order case. Some recent papers concerning the theory of FDEs in Banach spaces are
[

Let
E
be a real Banach space. In this paper we investigate the initial value problem

(1.2a)

\displaystyle{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u\left(t\right)}

\displaystyle=f\left(t,u\left(t\right)\right),\quad{t}\in\left[a,b\right],

(1.2b)

\displaystyle u\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=u_{0},

where

denotes the Caputo fractional differential operator of order

In Section

and prove some auxiliary results necessary for our main theorem. Moreover, we thoroughly establish the equivalence between solutions of the initial value problem (

In Section

we prove our main existence theorem, which extends the known results for classical ODEs in Banach spaces to the case of FDEs in Banach spaces. These classical results can be found e.g. in
[

,

]
,
[

[

[
4

In Section

Finally, in Section

Auxiliary Results

Throughout this paper, let
E=\left(E,\norm{\cdot}_{E}\right)
denote a real Banach space. The neutral element in
E
will be denoted by
0_{E}
, or simply by
0

. Dual space of

For

\clOperator\left(X\right)

X

\convOperator\left(X\right)
denote the convex hull of
X

\clOperator\left(\convOperator\left(X\right)\right)

Moreover, we define
\mathcal{M}_{E}

\mathcal{N}_{E}

Finally, note that
\mathbf{x}=\left(x_{k}\right)_{k=1}^{\infty}

2.1.
Abstract functions

An abstract function is a mapping
f\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow{E}
. For a family
X
of abstract functions defined on
\left[a,b\right]
and for
t\in\left[a,b\right]
, we define
X\left(t\right)\vcentcolon=\left\{f\left(t\right)\!:{f}\in{X}\right\}\subseteq{E}
. Similarly, for some
Y\subseteq{Z}
(where
Z
is an arbitrary set) and a mapping
g\!:{Z}\rightarrow{E}
, we define
g\left(Y\right)\vcentcolon=\left\{g\left(x\right)\!:{x}\in{Y}\right\}\subseteq{E}
. We denote by
C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
the space of all the continuous abstract functions
f\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow{E}
. Recall the well-known result that the space
C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
equipped with the supremum norm
\norm{\cdot}_{\infty}
is a Banach space.

The derivative of an abstract function
f
is meant in the strong sense, i.e. the derivative of
f
at the point
t_{0}
, denoted by
D^{1}f\left(t_{0}\right)
, is defined as the unique element of
E
satisfying

(2.1)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\norm{\frac{f\br{t_0+h}-f\br{t_0}}{h}-\D{f}\br{t_0}}_{E}=0.

2.2.
Integrals of abstract functions

For
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
, the abstract Riemann integral is defined in the same way as the classical Riemann integral. For more details, see e.g. Chapter
1.3
in
[
15
]
. Note that the abstract Riemann integral can be defined for a broader family of functions, but for our purpose,
C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
is enough.

Bochner integral is a generalisation of Lebesgue integral for abstract functions. Thorough construction of Bochner integral can be found in
[
23
]
. Note that for
E=\mathbb{R}
, Bochner and Lebesgue integral coincide.

We denote by
L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
the space of all the Bochner integrable functions
f\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow{E}
. Note that the space
L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
is the classical space of Lebesgue integrable functions. For
f\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
and
g\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
, we denote the Bochner and Lebesgue integral of
f
and
g
, respectively, as

\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{g\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}.

Moreover, one should keep in mind that for
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
, the abstract Riemann and Bochner integral coincide. Next, we provide some important properties of Bochner integral, crucial for our purpose.

Theorem 1

(
[
23
]
, Theorem

.

Let
f\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then it holds that
\norm{f\br{\cdot}}_{E}\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
. Moreover

\norm{\Bint{a}{b}{f\br{t}\dt}}_{E}\leq\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\norm{f\br{t}}_{E}\,\mathrm{d}t}.

Theorem 2

(
[
23
]
, Theorem
2.2
in Chapter XIII)
.

Let
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then for all

it holds that

D^{1}\left(\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\left(\cdot\right)}{f\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s}\right)\left(t\right)=f\left(t\right).

Theorem 3

(
[

]
, Theorem
2.3
in Chapter XIII)
.

Let
f\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow{E}
be continuously differentiable on
\left[a,b\right]
. Then it holds that

\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{D^{1}f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}=f\left(b\right)-f\left(a\right).

2.3.
Fractional calculus for abstract functions

In this section, we briefly introduce the theory of fractional calculus in Banach spaces. The theory is analogous to the case of real-valued functions, which is thoroughly described in
[
10
]
. We remind some basic results, which can be found e.g. in
[
3
]
, as well as prove some new ones, which are necessary for our purpose, but we were unable to find those in the existing literature.

Recall that
E
denotes a real Banach space. Furthermore, note that
\Gamma\left(\cdot\right)
denotes the
Gamma function
. Recall its well known property, namely that
\Gamma\left(x+1\right)=x\cdot\Gamma\left(x\right)
, for all
x>0
.

Definition 1

(Riemann-Liouville fractional abstract integral operator of order
\alpha
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
f\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. The
Riemann-Liouville fractional abstract integral operator of order
\alpha
, denoted by
J^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(\cdot\right)}
, is defined as

J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\left(t\right)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}f\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s},

for
a\leq{t}\leq{b}
. Furthermore, we define
J^{0}_{a}{f}\vcentcolon={f}
(i.e. the identity operator).

Remark 1
.

Note that for
\alpha=1
,
J^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(\cdot\right)}
coincides with the classical Bochner integral operator.

Remark 2
.

Note that in Definition
1
, the value
J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\left(a\right)
is meant as the limit, i.e.
J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\left(a\right)\vcentcolon=\lim_{t\rightarrow{a^{+}}}J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\left(t\right)
.

Theorem 4

(
[
3
]
, Theorem
2.3
)
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
f\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then
J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\left(t\right)
exists for almost all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
. Moreover,
J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
.

Theorem 5

(
[
3
]
, Remark
2.4
and Theorem
2.5
)
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then
J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
.

Theorem 6

(
[
3
]
, Theorem
2.6
)
.

Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
and
f\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then for almost all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
it holds that

(2.2)

J^{\alpha_{1}}_{a}{J^{\alpha_{2}}_{a}{f}}\left(t\right)=J^{\alpha_{2}}_{a}{J^{\alpha_{1}}_{a}{f}}\left(t\right)=J^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}_{a}{f}\left(t\right).

Moreover, if
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
or
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\geq 1
, then the identity (
2.2
) holds for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
.

Remark 3
.

Further on in this paper, we always assume
0<\alpha<1
. Although, one should note that all the arguments, as well as the statement of our main theorem, remain valid also for the case of
\alpha=1
.

Definition 2

(Riemann-Liouville fractional abstract differential operator of order
\alpha
)
.

The
Riemann-Liouville fractional abstract differential operator of order
\alpha
, denoted by
{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(\cdot\right)}
, is defined as

(2.3)

{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\vcentcolon=D^{1}J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{f},

whenever the RHS of (
2.3
) is well-defined. Furthermore, we define
{}^{RL}\!D^{0}_{a}{f}\vcentcolon={f}
(i.e. the identity operator).

Remark 4
.

Note that
{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(\cdot\right)}
can be analogously defined also for
\alpha\geq 1
. Moreover, note that for
\alpha=1
, such an operator would coincide with the classical differential operator as defined by (
2.1
) (hence the notation
D^{1}\left(\cdot\right)
). Even though the case
\alpha\geq 1
is not needed for our purpose, one should keep in mind Remark
3
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then

{}^{RL}\!D^{1-\alpha}_{a}{{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1}_{a}{f}}}=f.

Proof.

It follows that

\displaystyle{}^{RL}\!D^{1-\alpha}_{a}{{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1}_{a}{f}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Def.\,\ref{def_02}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{\alpha}_{a}{D^{1}J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{J^{1}_{a}{f}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_06}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{\alpha}_{a}{D^{1}J^{1}_{a}{J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{f}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_02}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{f}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_06}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{1}_{a}{f}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_02}}}}{{=}}f.

Note that the former use of Theorem
2
is justified due to Theorem
5
.
∎

Theorem 8
.

Let
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then

{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}}=f.

Proof.

It holds that

{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Def.\,\ref{def_02}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{f}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_06}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{1}_{a}{f}.

The claim then follows immediately from Theorem
2
.
∎

Definition 3

(Caputo fractional abstract differential operator of order
\alpha
)
.

The
Caputo fractional abstract differential operator of order
\alpha
, denoted by
{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(\cdot\right)}
, is defined as

(2.4)

{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{f}\vcentcolon={}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(f\left(\cdot\right)-f\left(a\right)\right)},

whenever the RHS of (
2.4
) is well-defined. Furthermore, we define
{}^{C}\!D^{0}_{a}{f}\vcentcolon={f}
(i.e. the identity operator).

Remark 5
.

It can be shown that under certain assumptions on
f
it holds that

(2.5)

{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{f}=J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{D^{1}f}.

However, Definition
3
is more suitable for applications than (
2.5
), as it covers a broader family of functions.

2.4.
Initial value problem for fractional differential equations

Let
u_{0}\in{E}
and
f\!:\left[a,b\right]\times{E}\rightarrow{E}
. Consider the initial value problem (IVP)

(2.6a)

\displaystyle{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=f\left(t,u\left(t\right)\right),\quad{t}\in\left[a,b\right],

(2.6b)

\displaystyle u\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=u_{0}.

By a solution of (
2.6
), we mean
u\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
such that
{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u}\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
, (
2.6a
) holds for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
and the initial condition (
2.6b
) is satisfied. In the next theorem we show that solving the IVP (
2.6
) is under certain assumptions equivalent to solving the corresponding Volterra-type integral equation.

Theorem 9
.

Consider the IVP (
2.6
) and let
f
be continuous. Then
u\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
is a solution of (
2.6
) if and only if it satisfies for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
the following Volterra-type integral equation

(2.7)

u\left(t\right)=u_{0}+J^{\alpha}_{a}{f\left(\cdot,u\left(\cdot\right)\right)}\left(t\right).

Proof.

“
\implies
”
Let
u\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
be a solution of (
2.6
). Define
z\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow{E}
as

(2.8)

z\left(t\right)\vcentcolon={f}\left(t,u\left(t\right)\right),\quad{t}\in\left[a,b\right].

Obviously,
z\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
, since both
f
and
u
are continuous. Let
t\in\left[a,b\right]
be arbitrary. Substituting (
2.8
) into (
2.6a
) and using (
2.6b
), we obtain

(2.9)

z\left(t\right)={}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Def.\,\ref{def_03}}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Def.\,\ref{def_02}}}}{{=}}D^{1}J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}\left(t\right).

Now, we apply the
J^{1}_{a}{\left(\cdot\right)}
operator on both sides of (
2.9
). Since
z\left(\cdot\right)
is continuous, we may use Theorem
3
to obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle J^{1}_{a}{z}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=J^{1}_{a}{D^{1}J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_03}}}}{{=}}J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}\left(t\right)-J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}\left(t\right),

where the last equality follows from

(2.11)

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(1-\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\cdot\norm{\JRL{1-\alpha}{a}{\br{u\br{\cdot}-u_0}}\br{a}}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Rem.\,\ref{rem_01}}}}{{=}}\norm{\lim_{t\rightarrow{a}^+}\Bint{a}{t}{\br{t-s}^{-\alpha}\cdot\br{u\br{s}-u_0}\ds}}_{E}

Finally, since

\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)\left(t\right)
yields

\displaystyle u\left(t\right)-u_{0}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_07}}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{1-\alpha}_{a}{{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}}}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_06}}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{1-\alpha}_{a}{{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1-\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u_{0}\right)}}}}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_10}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{1-\alpha}_{a}{{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1}_{a}{z}}}}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_06}}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{1-\alpha}_{a}{{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{1}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{z}}}}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_05}}\,\&\,\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_07}}}}{{=}}J^{\alpha}_{a}{z}\left(t\right).

Keeping in mind the definition (
2.8
), (
2.7
) follows immediately.

“
\impliedby
”
Let
u\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
satisfy (

) for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
. Let
t\in\left[a,b\right]
be arbitrary. Since
f\left(\cdot,u\left(\cdot\right)\right)
is continuous, applying Theorem

yields

f\left(t,u\left(t\right)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_08}}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{J^{\alpha}_{a}{f\left(\cdot,u\left(\cdot\right)\right)}}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_07}}}{{=}}{}^{RL}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{\left(u\left(\cdot\right)-u\left(a\right)\right)}\left(t\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Def.\,\ref{def_03}}}}{{=}}{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u}\left(t\right).

Thus,

satisfies (

) for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
. In the view of continuity of
f\left(\cdot,u\left(\cdot\right)\right)
, this also means that
{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u}\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Furthermore, by the same arguments as in (
2.11
), one can easily verify that
u\left(\cdot\right)
satisfies (
2.6b
) as well.
∎

2.5.
Basic results from functional analysis

Now, we remind some standard results from functional analysis, as well as prove a new one, which is needed for our purpose. Note that the third theorem is one of the many consequences of the Hahn-Banach theorem.

Theorem 10

(Arzelà-Ascoli theorem)
.

Let
X\subseteq C\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
. Then
X
is relatively compact in
C\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
if and only if
X
is uniformly bounded and uniformly equicontinuous on
\left[a,b\right]
.

Theorem 11

(Schauder fixed point theorem)
.

Let
X\subseteq{E}
be non-empty, closed, convex and bounded. Let
\mathcal{F}\!:{X}\rightarrow{X}
be a continuous mapping such that
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)
is relatively compact in
E
. Then there exists a fixed point of
\mathcal{F}
in
X
.

Theorem 12

(Hahn-Banach theorem,
[
13
]
, Theorem
2.5
)
.

Let
X,Y\subseteq{E}
be non-empty and disjoint convex sets. Assume that
X
is closed and
Y
is compact. Then
X
and
Y
can be strictly separated by a hyperplane, i.e.

\exists{f}\in E^{\star}\,\exists{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}\,\exists\varepsilon>0\!:{f}\left(x\right)\leq\alpha-\varepsilon\textnormal{ and }f\left(y\right)\geq\alpha+\varepsilon,\quad\forall{x}\in{X},\forall{y}\in{Y}.

Remark 6
.

The next lemma can be viewed as a generalisation of the mean value theorem (which, as it is well-known, does not hold in the same form in a general Banach space as it does in
\mathbb{R}
) for Banach spaces. Without the singular kernel, it is a known result, and it can be found in differential form e.g. as Proposition
2.1
in
[
9
]
. However, as we were not able to find such a result with singular kernel in the existing literature, we provide a detailed proof ourselves.

Lemma 1
.

Let
f\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
. Then for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
it holds that

(2.12)

\frac{\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}f\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s}}{\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}s}}\in\clOperator\left(\convOperator\left(\left\{f\left(s\right)\!:{a}\leq{s}\leq{t}\right\}\right)\right).

Proof.

It is obviously enough to show the statement for
t=b
. Define
K
as

(2.13)

K\vcentcolon=\clOperator\left(\convOperator\left(\left\{f\left(t\right)\!:{a}\leq{t}\leq{b}\right\}\right)\right).

Firstly, we show that

(2.14)

K=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R},h\in E^{\star}\,\!:\left\{x\in{E}\,\!:{h}\left(x\right)\leq\lambda\right\}\supseteq{K}}\left\{x\in{E}\!:{h}\left(x\right)\leq\lambda\right\}=\vcentcolon{S}.

“
\subseteq
”
Since all the sets on the RHS of (
2.14
) contain
K
, also their intersection does.

“
\supseteq
”
It is enough to show that if
x\notin{K}
, then
x\notin{S}
. Let
x\in{E}
such that
x\notin{K}
be arbitrary. Applying Theorem
12
(on the closed, convex set
K
and compact, convex set
\left\{x\right\}
), we obtain

\exists{h}_{0}\in E^{\star}\,\exists\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\!:{h}_{0}\left(y\right)\leq\lambda_{0}\text{ and }h_{0}\left(x\right)>\lambda_{0},\quad\forall{y}\in{K}.

This means that
x\notin{S}
, thus we are done.

Next, we show that for
g\in C\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
it holds that (compare with (
2.12
))

(2.15)

\frac{\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{g\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}}{\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{g\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}}\in\clOperator\left(\convOperator\left(\left\{f\left(t\right)\!:{a}\leq{t}\leq{b}\right\}\right)\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_13}}}{{=}}K.

Note that since
f
and
g
are both continuous, both the integrals in (
2.15
) reduce to Riemann integrals (to be precise, to abstract Riemann integral and Riemann integral, respectively). Define
A
as the denominator of the LHS of (
2.15
), i.e.

(2.16)

A\vcentcolon=\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{g\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}.

Consider a partition
a=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{n}=b
of
\left[a,b\right]
and define
\nu
as

\nu\vcentcolon=\max_{j=1,\dots,n}\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}\right).

Consider an arbitrary sequence of such partitions for which
\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}
. By the definition of abstract Riemann integral, we readily obtain

(2.17)

\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{g\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}=\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}\right)\cdot{g}\left(\tau_{j}\right)f\left(\tau_{j}\right).

Let
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
h_{0}\in E^{\star}
be arbitrary such that
K\subseteq\left\{x\in{E}\!:{h}_{0}\left(x\right)\leq\lambda_{0}\right\}
. Then

(2.18)

\displaystyle h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{A}\cdot\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{g\left(t\right)f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_17}}}{{=}}h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{A}\cdot\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}\right)\cdot{g}\left(\tau_{j}\right)f\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h_{0}\text{ is linear and continuous}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{A}\cdot\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}\right)\cdot{g}\left(\tau_{j}\right)\cdot{h}_{0}\left(f\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f\left(\tau_{j}\right)\in{K}\subseteq\left\{x\in{E}\,\!:{h}_{0}\left(x\right)\leq\lambda_{0}\right\}}}{{\leq}}\lambda_{0}\cdot\frac{1}{A}\cdot\lim_{\nu\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}\right)\cdot{g}\left(\tau_{j}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_16}}}{{=}}\lambda_{0}.

Since
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
h_{0}\in E^{\star}
were chosen as arbitrary (satisfying the additional condition), (
2.15
) follows immediately from (
2.14
) and (
2.18
).

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. From (
2.15
) we readily obtain

(2.19)

J\left(\varepsilon\right)\vcentcolon=\frac{\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b-\varepsilon}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}f\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}}{\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b-\varepsilon}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}}\in\clOperator\left(\convOperator\left(\left\{f\left(t\right)\!:{a}\leq{t}\leq{b}-\varepsilon\right\}\right)\right)\subseteq{K}.

Notice that in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
,
J\left(\varepsilon\right)
converges to the LHS of (
2.12
). Moreover, since in (
2.19
), the relation
J\left(\varepsilon\right)\in{K}
holds for all
\varepsilon>0
, and since
K
is closed, we immediately obtain

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}J\left(\varepsilon\right)\in{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_13}}}{{=}}\clOperator\left(\convOperator\left(\left\{f\left(t\right)\!:{a}\leq{t}\leq{b}\right\}\right)\right).

Hence proved.
∎

2.6.
Measures of non-compactness

In this section, we briefly introduce the concept of measures of non-compactness. For our purpose, the axiomatic approach is well-suited and is thoroughly described in Chapter
3
in
[
4
]
. Firstly, recall that
\mathcal{M}_{E}
denotes the family of all the non-empty and bounded subsets of
E
, whereas
\mathcal{N}_{E}
denotes the family of all the non-empty and relatively compact subsets of
E
.

Remark 7
.

Note that for
X,Y\subseteq{E}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, the linear combination
\lambda_{1}{X}+\lambda_{2}{Y}
is defined as

\lambda_{1}{X}+\lambda_{2}{Y}\vcentcolon=\left\{\lambda_{1}{x}+\lambda_{2}{y}\!:{x}\in{X},y\in{Y}\right\}.

Definition 4

(Measure of non-compactness)
.

The mapping
\mu\!:\mathcal{M}_{E}\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
is called a
measure of non-compactness in
E
, if it satisfies the following conditions

(i)

The family
\ker\mu\vcentcolon=\left\{X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}\!:\mu\left(X\right)=0\right\}
is non-empty and
\ker\mu\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{E}
.

(ii)

\mu\left(\clOperator\left(X\right)\right)=\mu\left(X\right)
, for all
X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

(iii)

If
X\subseteq{Y}
, then
\mu\left(X\right)\leq\mu\left(Y\right)
, for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

(iv)

\mu\left(\convOperator\left(X\right)\right)=\mu\left(X\right)
, for all
X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

(v)

\mu\left(\lambda{X}+\left(1-\lambda\right)Y\right)\leq\lambda\cdot\mu\left(X\right)+\left(1-\lambda\right)\cdot\mu\left(Y\right)
,  for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
,  for all
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right]
.

(vi)

Let
\Lambda
be an index set. If
\left\{X_{\lambda}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{E}
is a non-empty family of non-empty, closed and bounded sets totally ordered by inclusion and if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda
such that
\mu\left(X_{\lambda}\right)\leq\varepsilon
, then
X_{\infty}\vcentcolon=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\neq\emptyset
and
X_{\infty}\in\ker\mu
.

Definition 5
.

We say that a measure of non-compactness
\mu
is
sublinear
, if

(vii)

\mu\left(\lambda{X}\right)=\absolutevalue{\lambda}\cdot\mu\left(X\right)
, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, for all
X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

(viii)

\mu\left(X+Y\right)\leq\mu\left(X\right)+\mu\left(Y\right)
, for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

Definition 6
.

We say that a measure of non-compactness
\mu
satisfies the
singleton property
, if

(ix)

\mu\left(\left\{x\right\}\right)=0
, for all
x\in{E}
.

Remark 8
.

Notion of the singleton property for a measure of non-compactness was introduced by us, as we find it to be a crucial assumption in the main existence theorem.

Remark 9
.

Note that the singleton property does not follow from the axioms of Definition
4
, nor from the axioms of Definition
5
. Indeed, it is quite easy to verify that the mapping
\mu\!:{X}\longmapsto\sup_{x\in{X}}\norm{x}_{E}
, for any
X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
, is a sublinear measure of non-compactness, but does not have the singleton property, since
\mu\left(X\right)=0
if and only if
X=\left\{0_{E}\right\}
(i.e.
\ker\mu=\left\{\left\{0_{E}\right\}\right\}
). This example, originally as an example of a measure of non-compactness with the kernel consisting of a single set, was taken from Section
3.1
in
[
4
]
.

Definition 7
.

The
Hausdorff measure of non-compactness
, denoted by
\chi\left(\cdot\right)
, is defined as

(2.20)

\chi\left(X\right)\vcentcolon=\inf\left\{\varepsilon>0\!:{X}\text{ has a finite $\varepsilon$-net in }E\right\},

for every
X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

Remark 10
.

According to
[
7
]
,
\chi\left(\cdot\right)
is a sublinear measure of non-compactness satisfying the singleton property (see Lemma
5.9
and Lemma
5.10
in
[
7
]
). Moreover, one of the interesting properties of the Hausdorff measure of non-compactness is that
\chi\left(X\right)=0
if and only if
X\in\mathcal{N}_{E}
.

Remark 11
.

Even though we have a general formula for the Hausdorff measure of non-compactness, the form (
2.20
) is not suitable for applications. Nevertheless, for some particular Banach spaces, the Hausdorff measure of non-compactness is well-described. That is the case of e.g. sequence spaces, for which such measures are thoroughly described in
[
7
]
.

Theorem 13

(
[
7
]
, Theorem
5.18
)
.

The Hausdorff measure of non-compactness in the space
c_{0}
is of the form

(2.21)

\chi\left(X\right)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in{X}}\sup_{l\geq{k}}\absolutevalue{x_l},

for every
X\in\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

Remark 12
.

Recall that
X\in\mathcal{M}_{C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)}
denotes a non-empty and bounded set (with respect to the
\norm{\cdot}_{\infty}
norm) of continuous functions
f\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow{E}
.

Lemma 2

(
[
4
]
, Lemma
13.2.1
)
.

Let
\mu
be a sublinear measure of non-compactness and
X\in\mathcal{M}_{C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)}
. Then for all
t,s\in\left[a,b\right]
it holds that

\absolutevalue{\mu\br{X\br{t}}-\mu\br{X\br{s}}}\leq\mu\left(K_{E}\left[0_{E},1\right]\right)\cdot\sup_{x\in{X}}\,\sup_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\left[a,b\right]\,\!:\absolutevalue{\tau_1-\tau_2}\leq\absolutevalue{t-s}}\norm{x\br{\tau_1}-x\br{\tau_2}}_{E}.

Remark 13
.

Note that for
f\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right],\mathbb{R}\right)
and
X\in\mathcal{M}_{C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)}
, we define

\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{f\left(t\right)\cdot{X}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\vcentcolon=\left\{\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{f\left(t\right)\cdot{u}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\!:{u}\in{X}\right\}.

Remark 14
.

The next lemma is an extension of Lemma
13.2.2
in
[
4
]
for integrals with singular kernel. Since we were not able to find such a result in the existing literature, we provide a detailed proof ourselves.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mu
be a sublinear measure of non-compactness. Let
X\in\mathcal{M}_{C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)}
be uniformly equicontinuous. Then for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
it holds that

(2.22)

\mu\left(\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot{X}\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s}\right)\leq\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot\mu\left(X\left(s\right)\right)\mathrm{d}s}.

Proof.

It is obviously enough to show the statement for
t=b
. Due to the fact that the set
X\subseteq C\left(\left[a,b\right],E\right)
is uniformly equicontinuous, it holds that

(2.23)

\displaystyle\forall\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\exists{a}=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{n}=b\!:\norm{u\br{t}-u\br{s}}_{E}\leq\varepsilon;

\displaystyle\forall{u}\in{X};\,\forall{t},s\in\left[\tau_{j-1},\tau_{j}\right];\,\forall{j}=1,2,\dots,n.

Let
u\in{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Consider the partition
a=\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{n}=b
from (
2.23
). Then

(2.24)

\displaystyle\norm{\Bint{a}{b}{\br{b-t}^{\alpha-1}u\br{t}\dt}-\sum_{j=1}^n\br{\Lint{\tau_{j-1}}{\tau_j}{\br{b-t}^{\alpha-1}\dt}\cdot{u}\br{\tau_j}}}_{E}

\displaystyle=\norm{\sum_{j=1}^n\br{\Bint{\tau_{j-1}}{\tau_j}{\br{b-t}^{\alpha-1}u\br{t}\dt}}-\sum_{j=1}^n\br{\Bint{\tau_{j-1}}{\tau_j}{\br{b-t}^{\alpha-1}u\br{\tau_j}\dt}}}_{E}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_01}}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{j-1}}^{\tau_{j}}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot\norm{u\br{t}-u\br{\tau_j}}_{E}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_23}}}{{\leq}}\varepsilon\cdot\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(b-a\right)^{\alpha}\cdot\varepsilon=\vcentcolon{A}\cdot\varepsilon.

Since
u\in{X}
was chosen as arbitrary, we readily obtain (by adding a
“clever zero”
)

\displaystyle\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot{X}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}u\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{j-1}}^{\tau_{j}}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}\cdot{u}\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)\!:{u}\in{X}\right\}+

\displaystyle+\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{j-1}}^{\tau_{j}}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}\cdot{u}\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)\!:{u}\in{X}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_02_24}}}{{\subseteq}}A\cdot\varepsilon\cdot K_{E}\left[0_{E},1\right]+\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{j-1}}^{\tau_{j}}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}\cdot{u}\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)\!:{u}\in{X}\right\}.

Due to Axiom (
iii
) of Definition
4
and the sublinearity of
\mu
(see Definition
5
), it follows that

(2.25)

\displaystyle\mu\left(\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot{X}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{A}\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\mu\left(K_{E}\left[0_{E},1\right]\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{j-1}}^{\tau_{j}}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}\cdot\mu\left(X\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)\right).

In the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the LHS of (
2.25
) does not depend on
\varepsilon
and the first term on the RHS vanishes. Thus, we obtain (recall that

depends on

)

(2.26)

\mu\left(\left(B\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot{X}\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t}\right)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{j-1}}^{\tau_{j}}{\left(b-t\right)^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{d}t}\cdot\mu\left(X\left(\tau_{j}\right)\right)\right).

Moreover, from Lemma
2
, due to the uniform equicontinuity of
X
, it immediately follows that
\mu\left(X\left(\cdot\right)\right)
is uniformly continuous on
\left[a,b\right]
. Using similar arguments as in (
2.24
), it is easy to verify that the RHS of (
2.26
) converges to the RHS of (
2.22
) (for
t=b
). Hence proved.
∎

2.7.
Kamke function of order
\alpha

Now, we introduce the notion of a Kamke function of order
\alpha
, which is inspired by
[
18
]
and the Kamke comparison condition in Chapter 13 in
[
4
]
. Notice that in the fractional case, Kamke function is dependent on the order
\alpha
.

Definition 8

(Kamke function of order
\alpha
)
.

We say that
w_{\alpha}\!:\left[a,b\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
is a
Kamke function of order
\alpha
on
\left[a,b\right]
, if

(i)

w_{\alpha}
is continuous on
\left[a,b\right]\times\left[0,\infty\right)
.

(ii)

w_{\alpha}\left(t,0\right)=0
, for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
.

(iii)

u\equiv 0
is the unique non-negative continuous solution of

(2.27)

u\left(t\right)\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\cdot\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot{w}_{\alpha}\left(s,u\left(s\right)\right)\mathrm{d}s},\quad{t}\in\left[a,b\right],

for which

(2.28)

\lim_{t\rightarrow{a}^{+}}\frac{u\left(t\right)}{\left(t-a\right)^{\alpha}}=0.

Remark 15
.

Note that due to Axiom (
ii
) of Definition
8
,
u\equiv 0
is always a solution of (
2.27
). The question is whether it is the unique solution satisfying the additional condition (
2.28
).

3.
Main Results

Recall that
E
denotes a real Banach space and
0<\alpha<1
. Let
\delta^{\star}>0
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
u_{0}\in{E}
and define
I^{\star}\vcentcolon=\left[a,a+\delta^{\star}\right]
,
R\vcentcolon=I^{\star}\times K_{E}\left[u_{0},\beta\right]
, for some
\beta>0
. Let
f\!:{R}\rightarrow{E}
. Further on, we will study the following IVP

(3.1a)

\displaystyle{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{u\left(t\right)}

\displaystyle=f\left(t,u\left(t\right)\right),\quad{t}\in I^{\star},

(3.1b)

\displaystyle u\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=u_{0}.

Theorem 14
.

Consider the IVP (
3.1
). Let
w_{\alpha}
be a Kamke function of order
\alpha
on
I^{\star}
and let
\mu
be a sublinear measure of non-compactness in
E
satisfying the singleton property (see Definition
6
). Assume that

(i)

f
is uniformly continuous on
R
.

(ii)

\norm{f\br{t,u}}_{E}\leq{M}
, for all
\left(t,u\right)\in{R}
, for some
M>0
.

(iii)

\mu\left(f\left(t,X\right)\right)\leq{w}_{\alpha}\left(t,\mu\left(X\right)\right)
, for all
t\in I^{\star}
, for all
\emptyset\neq{X}\subseteq K_{E}\left[u_{0},\beta\right]
.

Then there exists a solution of (
3.1
) on
I\vcentcolon=\left[a,a+\delta\right]
, where
\delta

Proof.

Before providing the proof itself, we firstly briefly describe its basic ideas, which should help clarifying some of the steps while going through the proof. The proof consists of the following main steps

1.

A nested sequence
\left\{X_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of non-empty, bounded, closed, convex and uniformly equicontinuous subsets of

,

being chosen appropriately and

being an integral operator defined as the RHS of the corresponding Volterra-type integral equation (

2.

The sequence

(of real-valued functions of a single real variable) is shown to be uniformly equicontinuous and uniformly bounded on

. Thus, using the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, it is shown that it converges uniformly to some function

3.

4.

\delta

(3.26)

\delta\vcentcolon=\min\left\{\delta^{\star},\left(\frac{\beta}{M}\cdot\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},\left(\frac{C}{\kappa}\cdot\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\},

where
0<C<1
is an arbitrary constant.

Proof.

Note that (
3.26
) implies, apart from (
3.3
), also that

(3.27)

\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}\kappa\delta^{\alpha}\leq{C}.

Define
X
as

X\vcentcolon=\left\{u\!:{u}\in C\left(I,E\right);\norm{u\br{\cdot}-u_0}_{\infty}\leq\beta\right\}.

Consider the operator
\mathcal{F}\!:{X}\rightarrow C\left(I,E\right)
defined by (
3.5
). By the same arguments as in (
3.6
), we readily obtain that
\mathcal{F}\left(X\right)\subseteq{X}
.

Now, let us verify that
\mathcal{F}
is a contraction. Let
u,v\in{X}
be arbitrary. Then

\displaystyle\norm{\fF\br{u}-\fF\br{v}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Thm.\,\ref{thm_01}}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\cdot\sup_{t\in{I}}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\norm{f\br{s,u\br{s}}-f\br{s,v\br{s}}}_{E}\,\mathrm{d}s}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textrm{Assum.\,}\left(\textrm{\ref{th_03_02_ass_03}}\right)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\kappa\cdot\sup_{t\in{I}}\left(\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\norm{u\br{s}-v\br{s}}_{E}\,\mathrm{d}s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+1\right)}\kappa\cdot\norm{u-v}_{\infty}\cdot\sup_{t\in{I}}\left(t-a\right)^{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_03_27}}}{{\leq}}C\norm{u-v}_{\infty}.

Since
0<C<1
,
\mathcal{F}
is indeed a contraction.

Hence, by the Banach fixed point theorem, existence of the desired unique solution follows.
∎

4.
Applications

4.1.
Kamke function of order
\alpha

The first problem that arises in applications of Theorem
14
is to find a suitable Kamke function of order
\alpha
(recall the Definition
8
). It is easy to verify that
w_{\alpha}\left(t,s\right)=H\cdot{s}
, for some constant
H>0
, satisfies the definition. This follows directly from Lemma
6.19
in
[
10
]
, which is a Grönwall-type inequality. The next theorem is an extension of this result to the superlinear case.

Theorem 16
.

Let
H>0
and
\lambda\geq 1
. Then

w_{\alpha}\left(t,s\right)\vcentcolon={H}\cdot{s}^{\lambda},\quad{t}\in\left[a,b\right],\,s\in\left[0,\infty\right),

is a Kamke function of order
\alpha
on
\left[a,b\right]
.

Proof.

The first two axioms of Definition
8
are obvious, thus it suffices to verify the last one. We will show that the only non-negative continuous solution of (
2.27
) is
u\equiv 0
.

Let
u\!:\left[a,b\right]\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
be a continuous function, which for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
satisfies

(4.1)

u\left(t\right)\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\cdot\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot{H}\cdot\left(u\left(s\right)\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}s}.

For
\varepsilon>0
, consider the following IVP

(4.2a)

\displaystyle{}^{C}\!D^{\alpha}_{a}{y}\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=H\cdot\left(y\left(t\right)\right)^{\lambda},\quad{t}\in\left[a,b\right],

(4.2b)

\displaystyle y\left(a\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon.

Note that (
4.2
) is a special case of the IVP described in Section
2.4
, namely (
2.6
) in the space
E=\mathbb{R}
. Apparently, all the theory described in Section
2.3
and Section
2.4
holds. However, we will use some specific results for the space
\mathbb{R}
, which can be found in
[
10
]
.

By Theorem
6.5
in
[
10
]
, (
4.2
) has a unique solution on
\left[a,b\right]
(due to the fact that
f\left(t,s\right)\vcentcolon={H}\cdot{s}^{\lambda}
defined on a compact set is Lipschitz continuous in the second variable, with the Lipschitz constant being independent of the first variable). For a given
\varepsilon>0
, denote the unique solution of (
4.2
) as
u_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot\right)
.

By Lemma
6.2
in
[
10
]
(which is actually Theorem
9
in the space
E=\mathbb{R}
), for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
and
\varepsilon>0
it holds that

(4.3)

u_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)=\varepsilon+\frac{H}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t}{\left(t-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\cdot\left(u_{\varepsilon}\left(s\right)\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}s}.

Moreover, it is easy to show that (
4.1
) implies
u\left(a\right)=0
(e.g. by similar arguments as in (
3.7
)). Thus, for every
\varepsilon>0
it holds that

u\left(a\right)=0<\varepsilon=u_{\varepsilon}\left(a\right).

Since both
u\left(\cdot\right)
and
u_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot\right)
are continuous, there exists some
\eta>0
such that for all
t\in\left[a,a+\eta\right]
it holds that

(4.4)

u\left(t\right)<u_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right).

We will show that (
4.4
) holds for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
. Assume that this is not the case, i.e. assume that there exists some
\tau\in\left[a,b\right]
such that
{u}\left(\tau\right)\geq{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(\tau\right)
. Define

(4.5)

t_{0}\vcentcolon=\min\left\{t\in\left[a,b\right]\!:{u}\left(t\right)=u_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)\right\}.

Note that due to continuity of
u\left(\cdot\right)
and
u_{\varepsilon}\left(\cdot\right)
, the minimum in (
4.5
) exists. Thus, for all
t\in\left[a,t_{0}\right]
it holds that

(4.6)

u\left(t\right)\leq{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(t\right).

From (
4.6
) and (
4.1
), for every
\varepsilon>0
it follows that

\displaystyle u\left(t_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{H}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\cdot\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t_{0}}{\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\left(s\right)\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon+\frac{H}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}\cdot\left(L\right)\!\displaystyle\int_{a}^{t_{0}}{\left(t_{0}-s\right)^{\alpha-1}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\left(s\right)\right)^{\lambda}\mathrm{d}s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_04_03}}}{{=}}u_{\varepsilon}\left(t_{0}\right),

which is a contradiction with (
4.5
). Thus, (
4.4
) holds for all
t\in\left[a,b\right]
.

Finally, note that for
\varepsilon=0
, (
4.2
) has the unique solution
u^{\star}\equiv 0
on
\left[a,b\right]
, due to Theorem
6.5
in
[
10
]
. Furthermore, by the proof of Theorem
6.20
in
[
10
]
, for every
\varepsilon>0
it holds that

(4.7)

\sup_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}\absolutevalue{u_{\eps}\br{t}-\su\br{t}}=\sup_{t\in\left[a,b\right]}\absolutevalue{u_{\eps}\br{t}}\leq{A}\cdot\varepsilon,

for some constant
A>0
. Since (
4.7
) holds for every
\varepsilon>0
, we get from (
4.4
) holding on
\left[a,b\right]
and the fact that
u\left(\cdot\right)
is a non-negative continuous solution of (
4.1
) that
u\equiv 0
on
\left[a,b\right]
.
∎

Remark 16
.

Note that
w_{\alpha}\left(t,s\right)=h\left(t\right)\cdot{s}^{\lambda}
, with
h\left(\cdot\right)
being continuous on
\left[a,b\right]
and

, is a Kamke function of order
\alpha
as well. Indeed, using the boundedness of

on

, Axiom (

) of Definition

is obtained from Theorem

, the other axioms are obvious.

(v)

Then there exists a solution of (

) of Theorem

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\sup_{\left(t,x\right)\in I^{\star}\times\left[0,\infty\right)}\absolutevalue{r\br{t,x}}\cdot 2^{p}\left(p-1\right)\cdot\left(\norm{\bvarphi}_{c_{0}}+\beta\right)^{p-2},

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=2^{p}\cdot\left(\norm{\bvarphi}_{c_{0}}+\beta\right)^{p-1}.

\delta_{1}\vcentcolon=\frac{\varepsilon}{3C_{1}}
. Then, whenever
\max\left\{\absolutevalue{t-s},\norm{\bu-\bv}_{c_{0}}\right\}\leq\min\left\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\delta_{3}\right\}
, we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle\norm{\mathbf{f}^p\br{t,\bu}-\mathbf{f}^p\br{s,\bv}}_{c_{0}}\leq\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{\Lambda^p_n\br{t,\bu}-\Lambda^p_n\br{s,\bv}}+\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{F_n\br{t}-F_n\br{s}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{r_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\br{t}\cdot\Phi_p\br{u_n-u_{n-1}}-r_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\br{s}\cdot\Phi_p\br{v_n-v_{n-1}}}+

\displaystyle+\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{F_n\br{t}-F_n\br{s}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\cdot\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{r_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\br{t}\cdot\br{\Phi_p\br{u_n-u_{n-1}}-\Phi_p\br{v_n-v_{n-1}}}}+

\displaystyle+2\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{3C_{2}}\cdot\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{\Phi_p\br{v_{n}-v_{n-1}}}+\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{F_n\br{t}-F_n\br{s}}.

The third term on the RHS of (

“
\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
”
. As for the second term on the RHS of (

\frac{2\varepsilon}{3C_{2}}\cdot\sup_{n=1,2,\dots}\absolutevalue{\Phi_p\br{v_{n}-v_{n-1}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{v}\in K_{c_{0}}\left[\bm{\varphi},\beta\right]\,\&\,\eqref{eq_04_14}}}{{\leq}}\frac{2\varepsilon}{3C_{2}}\cdot 2^{p-1}\cdot\left(\norm{\bvarphi}_{c_{0}}+\beta\right)^{p-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_04_16}}}{{=}}\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Finally, let
n=1,2,\dots
be arbitrary. Using the mean value theorem, we obtain the following estimate for the first term on the RHS of (
4.17
)

\displaystyle 2\cdot\absolutevalue{r_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\br{t}\cdot\br{\Phi_p\br{u_n-u_{n-1}}-\Phi_p\br{v_n-v_{n-1}}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi\text{ is between }\left(u_{n}-u_{n-1}\right)\text{ and }\left(v_{n}-v_{n-1}\right)}}{{=}}2\cdot\absolutevalue{r_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\br{t}}\cdot\left(p-1\right)\cdot\absolutevalue{\xi}^{p-2}\times

\displaystyle\times\absolutevalue{\br{u_n-u_{n-1}}-\br{v_n-v_{n-1}}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in K_{c_{0}}\left[\bm{\varphi},\beta\right]}}{{\leq}}2\cdot\absolutevalue{r_{n-\frac{1}{2}}\br{t}}\cdot\left(p-1\right)\cdot\left(2\left(\norm{\bvarphi}_{c_{0}}+\beta\right)\right)^{p-2}\cdot 2\norm{\bu-\bv}_{c_{0}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq_04_15}}}{{\leq}}C_{1}\cdot\norm{\bu-\bv}_{c_{0}},

which, considering that
\norm{\bu-\bv}_{c_{0}}\leq\delta_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon}{3C_{1}}
, yields that the first term on the RHS of (
4.17
) is
“
\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
”
. Hence,
\mathbf{f}^{p}
is uniformly continuous on

.

Now, defining
\lambda\vcentcolon={p}-1

,

5.
Concluding remarks

Most of the results that can be found in the existing literature and concerning the question of existence of solutions for problems of type (
3.1
) require the assumption of
\mu\left(f\left(t,X\right)\right)\leq{g}\left(t\right)\cdot\mu\left(X\right)
, where
g\left(\cdot\right)
is continuous and
\mu\left(\cdot\right)
is either Hausdorff or Kuratowski measure of non-compactness. Our main result, Theorem
14
, extends this assumption in two ways. Firstly, we require only
\mu\left(f\left(t,X\right)\right)\leq{w}_{\alpha}\left(t,\mu\left(X\right)\right)
, with
w_{\alpha}\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
being an arbitrary Kamke function of order
\alpha
(see Definition
8
). Secondly, we allow
\mu\left(\cdot\right)
to be an arbitrary measure of non-compactness satisfying some additional assumptions. Note that the above-mentioned
g\left(t\right)\cdot\mu\left(X\right)
satisfies the definition of a Kamke function of order
\alpha
(see Theorem
16
and Remark
16
). Also, note that both the Hausdorff and Kuratowski measure of non-compactness satisfy given additional assumptions (the former case being discussed in Remark
10
, the latter not mentioned in this paper, but one can easily find the necessary theory in
[
7
]
).

In Theorem
14
, in contrast to a similar theorem for the integer order case in
[
4
]
(Theorem
13.3.1
), we require the assumption of
\mu
having the singleton property (see Definition
6
). This is due to the fact that in (
3.1
), the initial condition is in a general point
u_{0}\in{E}
, rather than in
u_{0}=0
. Moreover, as mentioned in Remark
9
, this property does not follow from the definition of a (sublinear) measure of non-compactness in general, thus it indeed cannot be omitted.

Note that even though the theory was developed for fractional differential equations of order
0<\alpha<1
, the very same arguments can be used also in the case of
\alpha=1
(which coincides with the usual first derivative). However, note that for
\alpha=1
, Theorem
14
is a known result (see the corresponding discussion in Section
1
).

There are various options for further development of our main result, Theorem
14
. Natural question is whether some assumptions of Theorem
14
can be weakened, at least for particular measures of non-compactness and/or particular Banach spaces. This question was discussed for the integer order case in
[
24
]
, where it is shown that the mere continuity of
f
(instead of the uniform continuity) is sufficient in the case of a Hausdorff measure of non-compactness for some Banach spaces. Furthermore, the question of extension of local solutions to the whole interval
\left[a,a+\delta^{\star}\right]
can be addressed. Also, an interesting question is a generalisation of the IVP (
3.1
) in the context of calculus on time scales (even in the case of
\alpha=1
). Finally, the IVP (
3.1
) can be also extended to fractional differential equations of arbitrary order
\alpha>0
.

The application of Theorem
14
in the Banach space
c_{0}
, namely Theorem
17
, was inspired by
[
6
]
, where a similar result is stated for the integer order case and
\lambda=1
. However, we believe that in
[
6
]
(Theorem
3
), the assumption of
\left\{p_{j}\left(\cdot\right)\right\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
converging uniformly to
0
on
I^{\star}
can be replaced by the weaker assumption of pointwise convergence (which is only assumed in Theorem
17
).

Study of countable systems of the form (
4.13
) (with semi-discrete
p
-Laplacian) is new in the fractional case even in the case of
p=2
(which coincides with semi-discretisation of the usual Laplacian). Also, in the view of previous note regarding
\alpha=1
, the study of such non-fractional countable systems (i.e. for
\alpha=1
) is new in the case of
p>2
.

Additionally, one can further modify the results from Section
4.2
to other sequence spaces, e.g.
c
(the space of all the convergent sequences) or
l_{p}
(the space of all the absolutely summable sequences in the
p
-th power). Such systems were studied for the integer order case e.g. in
[
6
]
.

Furthermore, the fractional PDE with
p
-Laplacian in (
4.10a
) can be generalised to the case where
x\in\mathbb{R}
(instead of
x\geq 0
). Semi-discretisation of such a PDE would lead to bisequences (i.e. sequences indexed over
\mathbb{Z}
instead of
\mathbb{N}
). Also, note that in (
4.10a
), the non-linear perturbation
F\left(t,x\right)
can be without major problems extended to the form
F\left(t,x,\mathchoice{\frac{\partial\mkern 0.0muu}{{\partial\mkern 0.0mux}\,}}{\displaystyle{\frac{\partial\mkern 0.0muu}{{\partial\mkern 0.0mux}\,}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\partial\mkern 0.0muu}{{\partial\mkern 0.0mux}\,}}}{\scriptstyle{\frac{\partial\mkern 0.0muu}{{\partial\mkern 0.0mux}\,}}}\right)
, given that the part containing

Finally, in Example

can be easily extended to the case of

being continuous on

. In such a case, the term

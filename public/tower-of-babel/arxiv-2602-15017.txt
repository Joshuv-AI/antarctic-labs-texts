The projective coinvariant algebra, Young invariants and bigraded coordinate rings of Segre embeddings
Balázs Szendrői

Abstract
. This paper studies a flat degeneration P n P_{n} of the classical coinvariant algebra R n R_{n} , a bigraded Artinian Gorenstein algebra that arises from the coordinate ring of the Segre embedding of the n n -fold self-product of the projective line. The Frobenius character of P n P_{n} is computed by a natural bigraded refinement of the classical Lusztig–Stanley formula for the character of the coinvariant algebra. Young invariants in P n P_{n} get related to coordinate rings of general Segre embeddings of products of projective spaces; their bigraded Hilbert polynomials get expressed in terms of major-descent generating functions of words in multisets. Relations to the diagonal coinvariant algebra, cohomological interpretations including quantum cohomology, and Garsia–Stanton–style bases are also explored.



1.
Introduction

Given a positive integer
n
, fixed for the remainder of the paper, let
S_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The group
S_{n}
acts on the free polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[u_{1}\,\ldots,u_{n}]
in the standard way by permuting the indices.
The classical
coinvariant algebra

R_{n}=\mathbb{C}[u_{1}\,\ldots,u_{n}]/\langle\mathbb{C}[u_{1}\,\ldots,u_{n}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\rangle

is a well-studied Artinian graded complete intersection
\mathbb{C}
-algebra of dimension
n!
and Hilbert series

(1)

\dim_{q}R_{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}q^{\mathrm{maj}(\sigma)}=[n]_{q}!,

with
\mathrm{maj}
the major index statistic on permutations of
n
(see
2.1
-
2.2
for all
undefined terms). The Garsia–Stanton
\mathbb{C}
-basis
\{\mathbf{b}_{\sigma}\colon\sigma\in S_{n}\}\subset R_{n}
from
[
11
]
witnesses formula (
1
) in the sense that it associates to every permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
a homogeneous basis element
\mathbf{b}_{\sigma}
of graded degree
\maj(\sigma)
.
Further, the algebra
R_{n}
carries a graded action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
, which makes it isomorphic to the regular representation of
S_{n}
as ungraded representation. Its graded (Frobenius) character is computed by the Lusztig–Stanley formula
[
23
, Prop. 4.11]

(2)

\cha_{q}R_{n}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\left(\sum_{T\in\SYT(\lambda)}q^{\mathrm{maj}(T)}\right)s_{\lambda},

which expresses the
q
-multiplicity of the irreducible representation
s_{\lambda}
of
S_{n}
in
R_{n}
as a sum over standard Young tableaux (see
2.3
) of shape equal to a given partition
\lambda\vdash n
.

Given a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\vDash n
of
n
, an additive decomposition of
n
into positive integers, there is a corresponding Young subgroup

S_{\alpha}=S_{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{\alpha_{k}}\subset S_{n}.

The action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
on the graded algebra
R_{n}
restricts to an action of
S_{\alpha}
. The invariant algebra

R_{\alpha}=R_{n}^{S_{\alpha}},

the
partial coinvariant algebra

[
3
, Sect. 5]
,
[
18
, Sect. 2]
, is a graded finite-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with Hilbert series

(3)

\dim_{q}R_{\alpha}=\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
\alpha\end{array}\!\!\right]_{q},

a
q
-multinomial coefficient
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
.

In this paper, I study a certain bigraded degeneration of the above setup, related to Segre embeddings of products of projective spaces. The main object is a certain bigraded algebra
P_{n}
that I will call the
projective coinvariant algebra
, which was introduced independently at least three times
in the literature in
[
20
,
4
,
1
]
, but in a way that I feel has not uncovered all its remarkable properties.
The following set of results will be discussed.

(a)

(Proposition
3.1
) The algebra
P_{n}
is defined as a certain bigraded, Artinian truncation of the coordinate ring
of the
n
-fold product of the projective line
\mathbb{C}[(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}]
in its standard Segre embedding. It is a bigraded,
finite-dimensional Gorenstein algebra of dimension
n!
and bigraded Hilbert series

(4)

\dim_{t,q}P_{n}=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}t^{\mathrm{des}(\sigma)}q^{\mathrm{maj}(\sigma)},

with
\mathrm{des}
the descent statistic on
S_{n}
.

(b)

(Theorem
3.2
) The algebra
P_{n}
is the central fibre of a natural two-dimensional family
\mathscr{P}_{n}
of algebras over the base
\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
which carries an action of the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
that is compatible with its standard action on the base. Its bigrading
arises from being a fibre over the torus-fixed point
(0,0)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Over points
(s_{0},0),(0,s_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}

with

, the fibres of the family

are isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}S_{n}
as ungraded
algebras.

(c)

(Theorem
3.4
) The algebra
P_{n}
is a bigraded
S_{n}
-module with Frobenius character

(5)

\cha_{t,q}P_{n}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\left(\sum_{T\in\SYT(\lambda)}t^{\mathrm{des}(T)}q^{\mathrm{maj}(T)}\right)s_{\lambda}.

(d)

(Theorem
4.10
(i)-(ii)) The invariant subalgebra

can be identified
with a certain Artinian truncation of the coordinate ring of a general product of projective

\dim_{t,q}P_{\alpha}=\sum_{w\in W_{\alpha}}q^{\maj(w)}t^{\des(w)},

where

is a certain set of words in a multiset associated to
\alpha
, equipped with its standard

).

(e)

(Theorem

(iii)-(iv))
The algebra

, with other fibres being isomorphic to
the partial coinvariant algebra

, respectively a finite-dimensional ungraded algebra of dimension

.

The degeneration picture of (b) explains in particular how the two-parameter expressions (

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Conventions and notations

We work over the field
\mathbb{C}
. Fix a positive integer
n
.
Denote
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and let

S_{n}=\bij[n]
be the symmetric group on
n
letters.
The
q
-integers are defined as usual by

[n]_{q}=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}

and the
q
-factorial by

[n]_{q}!=\prod_{j=1}^{n}[j]_{q}.

Given a non-negatively graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
R=\bigoplus_{d=0}^{\infty}R_{d}
with finite-dimensional graded pieces, define its
q
-dimension (Hilbert series) by the formula

\dim_{q}R=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}(\dim R_{d})q^{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

Similarly, for a bigraded algebra
R=\bigoplus_{d,e=0}^{\infty}R_{d,e}
, denote

\dim_{t,q}R=\sum_{d,e=0}^{\infty}(\dim R_{d,e})t^{d}q^{e}\in{\mathbb{N}}\llbracket q,t\rrbracket.

If
V
is a finite-dimensional representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
with irreducible decomposition
V=\oplus_{\lambda\vdash n}V_{\lambda}
indexed by partitions
\lambda\vdash n
of
n
, define its (Frobenius) character to be

\cha V=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}(\dim V_{\lambda})s_{\lambda},

where the
s_{\lambda}
are the standard (Schur) basis elements in the representation ring of
S_{n}
. When
V
is a
(bi)graded representation with finite-dimensional graded pieces, the two notations combine in the obvious way. We will sometimes
use standard plethystic notation to write compact expressions for Frobenius characters; see for example
[
2
, Sect.3.8]
.
For a partition
\lambda\vdash n
,
h_{\lambda},m_{\lambda}
denote the standard complete and monomial symmetric functions.

2.2.
Words in multisets and an identity of MacMahon

Recall that a composition
\alpha\vDash n
is an ordered sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})
of positive integers summing to
n
, with no assumption on the sequence being arranged in decreasing order (as there would be for a partition).
Given a composition
\alpha\vDash n
, let
M_{\alpha}=\{1^{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,k^{\alpha_{k}}\}
be the corresponding multiset (set with repetition of elements allowed). Let
{\mathcal{P}}(M_{\alpha})
denote the set of subsets of
M_{\alpha}
, the set of sub-multisets
\{1^{\beta_{1}},\ldots,k^{\beta_{k}}\}
of
M_{\alpha}
with
0\leq\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}
, of size
|{\mathcal{P}}(M_{\alpha})|=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+1)
.

Let
W_{\alpha}
be the set of full-length words
w=(i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n})
in the multiset
M_{\alpha}
. The size of this set is

|W_{\alpha}|={n\choose\alpha}=\frac{n!}{\alpha_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot\alpha_{k}!},

a multinomial coefficient. For a word
w=(i_{1}i_{2}\ldots i_{n})\in W_{\alpha}
,
1\leq k\leq n-1
is a
descent position
if
i_{k}>i_{k+1}
. Let
\Des(w)
denote the set of descent positions in
w
, and define the descent index
\des(w)
, respectively major index
\maj(w)
, to be the number, respectively sum, of descent positions in
w
:

\des(w)=|\Des(w)|,\ \ \maj(w)=\sum_{i\in\Des(w)}i.

We get the generating function

A_{\alpha}(t,q)=\sum_{w\in W_{\alpha}}q^{\maj(w)}t^{\des(w)}.

The specialisation

A_{\alpha}(1,q)=\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
\alpha\end{array}\!\!\right]_{q}=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[\alpha_{1}]_{q}!\cdot\ldots\cdot[\alpha_{k}]_{q}!}

is a
q
-multinomial coefficient.

For
\alpha=(1^{n})\vDash n
,
M_{\alpha}=[n]
is a set,
W_{\alpha}
is the set of all permutations
\sigma\in S_{n}
of
n
, and
\des(\sigma),\maj(\sigma)
are the classical descent and major indices of a permutation;
A_{(1^{n})}(t,q)
is
q
-refinement of the classical Eulerian polynomial
A_{(1^{n})}(t,1)
enumerating permutations by descent. For
\alpha=(n)\vDash n
,
M_{\alpha}=\{1^{n}\}
and
W_{\alpha}
consists of a single element
(1\ldots 1)
, with both descent and major index
0
, so
A_{(n)}(t,q)=1
. Further examples are given below in Examples
4.5
-
4.6
.

The following combinatorial identity of MacMahon will play an important role.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any composition
\alpha\vDash n
, we have the identity

(7)

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}r+\alpha_{i}\\
\alpha_{i}\end{array}\right]_{q}\right)t^{r}=\frac{A_{\alpha}(t,q)}{\prod_{j=0}^{n}(1-q^{j}t)}.

Proof.

This identity is proved by MacMahon in
[
17
, Vol. 2 Sect. IX]
; for a modern reference, see
[
12
, Sect. 3]
. The case
\alpha=(1^{n})
, corresponding to permutatons, is the so-called Carlitz identity
[
6
]
.
∎

2.3.
Standard Young tableaux

Given a partition
\lambda\vdash n
of
n
, denote as usual by
\SYT(\lambda)
the set of standard Young tableaux of shape
\lambda
, the set of fillings
T
of the Young diagram corresponding to
\lambda
bijectively by
[n]
such that the entries in each row and each column are increasing. The descent, respectively major indices of a standard Young tableau
T\in\SYT(\lambda)
, denoted by
\des(T)
and
\maj(T)
, are defined as the number, respectively sum, of all
1\leq i\leq n-1
such that
i+1
appears in a lower row in
T
than
i
.

2.4.
Refined Ehrhart theory

Let
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional convex lattice polytope, the convex hull of finitely many lattice points in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For a non-negative integer
r
, let
r\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the
r
-fold dilation of
\Sigma
. Fix an
n
-tuple of integer weights
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. Following Chapoton
[
8
]
, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
consider the quantity

L^{\bf a}_{\Sigma}(r,k)=\#\{v\in r\Sigma\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n}:{\bf v}\cdot{{\bf a}}=k\}.

Geometrically, this corresponds to slicing the polytope
\Sigma
with hyperplanes perpendicular to the direction
{\bf a}
, and counting lattice points in the slices. Let

L^{\bf a}_{\Sigma,r}(q)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}L^{\bf a}_{\Sigma}(r,k)q^{k},

a Laurent polynomial in the variable
q
, and consider the series

E^{\bf a}_{\Sigma}(t,q)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}t^{r}L^{\bf a}_{\Sigma,r}(q).

Setting
q=1
,
L^{\bf a}_{\Sigma,r}(1)
and
E^{\bf a}_{\Sigma}(t,1)
are the Ehrhart polynomial and Ehrhart series of
\Sigma
, respectively.

Consider the polytope algebra of
\Sigma
, the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

T_{\Sigma}=\mathbb{C}[x_{{\bf v},r}\mid r\geq 0,{\bf v}\in r\Sigma\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}]/\langle x_{{\bf v},r}\cdot x_{{\bf v}^{\prime},r^{\prime}}-x_{{\bf v}+{\bf v}^{\prime},r+r^{\prime}}\rangle.

This is a naturally a bigraded algebra, with the generator
x_{{\bf v},r}
having bidegree
(r,{\bf v}\cdot{\bf a})
. Using only the first grading,
T_{\Sigma}
is non-negatively graded, and
\Proj A_{\Sigma}
is the projective toric variety defined by the polytope
\Sigma
. By definition,

\dim_{t,q}T_{\Sigma}=E^{\bf a}_{\Sigma}(t,q).

A simple example of this setup will be needed later below; compare
[
1
]
. Let
\Sigma=\Delta_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the basic rectangular simplex in
n
-space, with vertices at the origin and the endpoints of the standard orthonormal basis vectors. Let
{\bf a}=(1,\ldots,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The corresponding polytope algebra is
T_{\Delta_{n}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{P}^{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
, bigraded by
\deg(z_{j})=(1,j)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under this bigrading, the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{P}^{n}]
has bigraded Hilbert series

(8)

E^{\bf a}_{\Delta_{n}}(t,q)=\dim_{t,q}\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{P}^{n}]=\prod_{j=0}^{n}(1-tq^{j})^{-1}=\sum_{r\geq 0}t^{r}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}r+n\\
n\end{array}\right]_{q}.

Hence the
q
-count of lattice points in the
r
-th dilation is

L^{\bf a}_{\Delta_{n},r}(q)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}r+n\\
n\end{array}\right]_{q}.

Proof.

As
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{P}^{n}]
is freely generated by the variables
z_{i}
, the second equality is clear. The third equality is the negative
q
-binomial theorem.
∎

2.5.
Families of algebras

One of the key themes of this paper is the appearance of families of algebras over the affine plane. Such a family is a flat homomorphism of finitely generated (often finite-dimensional)
\mathbb{C}
-algebras
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
, corresponding to a flat family of affine varieties

\pi\colon\Spec\mathcal{A}\to\Spec\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{2}.

For some fixed
(t_{0},t_{1})\in\mathbb{A}^{2}
, denote

\mathcal{A}_{(t_{0},t_{1})}=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\mathbb{C},(t_{0},t_{1})}\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]

the fibre algebra of this family, where the tensor product is defined via the algebra homomorphism
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]\to\mathbb{C}
being evaluation at
(t_{0},t_{1})
. Then clearly

\mathcal{A}_{(t_{0},t_{1})}\cong\mathbb{C}[\pi^{-1}(t_{0},t_{1})],

the coordinate algebra of the corresponding geometric fibre of
\pi
.

3.
The projective coinvariant algebra and its deformations

3.1.
The basic definition

Consider the bigraded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

T_{n}=\mathbb{C}[x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}\colon I\subseteq[n]]/J_{n},

where the generator
x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}
has bidegree
(1,|I|)
and
J_{n}
is the bigraded toric ideal

J_{n}=\left\langle x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}x_{\scriptscriptstyle J}-x_{\scriptscriptstyle I\cap J}x_{\scriptscriptstyle I\cup J}\colon I,J\subseteq[n]\right\rangle.

The bigrading on
T_{n}
can equivalently be thought of as the action of the two-dimensional torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
on
T_{n}
defined by

(t_{0},t_{1})\circ x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}=t_{0}t_{1}^{|I|}x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}.

In the language of Section
2.4
, the algebra
T_{n}
is the polytope algebra of the
n
-dimensional unit hypercube, with generators
x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}
corresponding to
vertices of the hypercube. The bigrading corresponds to considering
q
-Ehrhart theory with
{\bf a}=(1,\ldots,1)
. As it is well known, when considered graded by the first component only,
\Proj T_{n}
is the image of the Segre embedding

s_{n}\colon(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2^{n}-1}.

Indeed, the Segre embedding can be given in coordinates by

\begin{array}[]{rcccc}s_{n}&\colon&(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{n}&\rightarrow&\mathbb{P}^{2^{n}-1}\\
&&\left([a_{1}:b_{1}],\ldots,[a_{n}:b_{n}]\right)&\mapsto&\left[\ldots\colon\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}\prod_{j\not\in I}b_{j}:\ldots\right]\end{array}

The ideal
J_{n}
records all relations between the image coordinates. So
T_{n}
can be thought of as the projective coordinate ring of the image of the Segre embedding
s_{n}
, equipped with an extra grading.

We will occasionally find it useful to consider the following alternative model of this algebra. Consider the free bigraded commutative algebra

\mathbb{C}[\zeta,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]
with
\deg(\zeta)=(1,0)
and
\deg(\xi_{i})=(0,1)
. Then it is well known that
T_{n}
is isomorphic to a bigraded subalgebra of

\mathbb{C}[\zeta,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]
, the image of the embedding

(9)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T_{n}&\hookrightarrow&\mathbb{C}[\zeta,\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]\\
x_{I}&\mapsto&\zeta\cdot\prod_{i\in I}\xi_{i}.\end{array}

Indeed the relations
x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}x_{\scriptscriptstyle J}=x_{\scriptscriptstyle I\cap J}x_{\scriptscriptstyle I\cup J}
for
I,J\subseteq[n]
are automatically satisfied under this map.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, denote

\widetilde{e}_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}\in T_{n}.

Note
\widetilde{e}_{0}=x_{\scriptscriptstyle\emptyset}
,
\widetilde{e}_{n}=x_{\scriptscriptstyle[n]}
, and that the element
\widetilde{e}_{k}\in T_{n}
is homogeneous of bidegree
(1,k)
.

The following results were proved in
[
1
, Prop.2.7]
; compare also
[
20
, Section 4]
. (The statements also appear in
[
4
]
, but with an incomplete argument for the first statement.)

Proposition 3.1
.

The elements
\widetilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n}
form a regular sequence of maximal length in the algebra
T_{n}
. The quotient

P_{n}=T_{n}/\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n}\right\rangle

is a finite-dimensional bigraded Gorenstein
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, with bigraded Hilbert series

\dim_{t,q}P_{n}=A_{(1^{n})}(t,q)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}t^{\mathrm{des}(\sigma)}q^{\mathrm{maj}(\sigma)}.

Call the bigraded algebra
P_{n}
, first introduced independently in
[
4
,
1
]
(and, without its bigrading, in
[
20
]
), the
projective coinvariant algebra
; the name will be justified by Theorem
3.2
below.

3.2.
A family of deformations

Introduce the
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]
-algebras

\mathcal{T}_{n}=T_{n}[s_{0},s_{1}]

and

\mathscr{P}_{n}=\mathcal{T}_{n}/\langle x_{\scriptscriptstyle\emptyset}-s_{0},\widetilde{e}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n-1},x_{\scriptscriptstyle[n]}-s_{1}\rangle.

We can think geometrically of the map

\pi_{n}\colon\Spec\mathscr{P}_{n}\to\Spec\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]=\mathbb{A}^{2}

induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]\rightarrow\mathscr{P}_{n}
as a giving a two-parameter family of (spectra of) algebras over the affine plane
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, a subfamily of the trivial family

\tau_{n}\colon\Spec\mathcal{T}_{n}\to\Spec\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]=\mathbb{A}^{2}.

Consider the action of the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
on
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]
given by

(t_{0},t_{1})\circ(s_{0},s_{1})=(t_{0}s_{0},t_{0}t_{1}^{n}s_{1}).

This can be extended to a compatible action of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and
\mathscr{P}_{n}
over
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]
.
The symmetric group
S_{n}
also acts on
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and thus on
\mathscr{P}_{n}
by combining its standard action on
[n]
inducing an action on all subsets with the trivial action on
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

(i)

The inclusion
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]\rightarrow\mathscr{P}_{n}
is a flat map of algebras, equivariant with respect to the action of the torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
, as well as the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

(ii)

The central fibre
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(0,0)}
is bigraded
S_{n}
-isomorphic to the projective coinvariant algebra
P_{n}
.

(iii)

Over points
(s_{0},0)
and
(0,s_{1})
with
s_{i}\neq 0
, the fibres
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(s_{0},0)}
,
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(0,s_{1})}
are graded
S_{n}
-isomorphic to the classical coinvariant algebra
R_{n}
.

(iv)

Over points
(s_{0},s_{1})
with
s_{0}s_{1}\neq 0
, the fibre
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(s_{0},s_{1})}
is an (ungraded) finite-dimensional algebra of dimension

,
S_{n}
-isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}S_{n}
.

Proof.

The central fibre
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(0,0)}
is by definition isomorphic to the projective coinvariant algebra
P_{n}
. On the other hand, being a regular sequence is an open property, so the corresponding elements give regular sequences in fibres near the origin, and hence, using the torus action, over the whole affine plane.

Let us consider the fibre
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(s_{0},0)}
over a point
(s_{0},0)
with
s_{0}\neq 0
. Re-scaling using the first component of the torus action, we can assume that
s_{0}=1
. In this algebra
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,0)}
, the image of the element
x_{\scriptscriptstyle\emptyset}
gets identified with the unit. Define a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism

\psi_{n}\colon\mathbb{C}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}]\to(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,0)}

by
\psi_{n}(u_{i})=(x_{\scriptscriptstyle\{i\}})_{(1,0)}
. Then for
i\not\in I
, using the relations
x_{\scriptscriptstyle\emptyset}x_{\scriptscriptstyle I\cup\{i\}}=x_{\scriptscriptstyle\{i\}}x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}
inductively, we deduce

\psi_{n}\left(\prod_{i\in I}u_{i}\right)=(x_{\scriptscriptstyle I})_{(1,0)}\in(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,0)}.

In particular, if
e_{k}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
denotes the elementary symmetric functions of the variables
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}
, we get

\psi_{n}\left(e_{k}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[n]\\
|I|=k\end{subarray}}(x_{\scriptscriptstyle I})_{{}_{(1,0)}}=0\in(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,0)}.

It is easy to see that these elements generate the kernel of
\psi_{n}
. We deduce the isomorphism

(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,0)}\cong\mathbb{C}[u_{1}\,\ldots,u_{n}]/\langle e_{1}(u_{i}),\ldots,e_{n}(u_{i})\rangle=\mathbb{C}[u_{1}\,\ldots,u_{n}]/\langle\mathbb{C}[u_{1}\,\ldots,u_{n}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\rangle

with the classical coinvariant algebra
R_{n}
.
The subtorus
\{t_{0}=1\}\subset(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
fixes the point
(1,0)\in\mathbb{A}^{2}
, and acts on the fibre algebra
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,0)}
, equipping it with a grading agreeing with the standard grading on the coinvariant algebra. The remaining statements in (iii) are then well known.

The fibre over a point
(0,s_{1})
is isomorphic to the fibre over a point
(s_{0},0)
using the “external” automorphism of the whole setup which interchanges the coordinate
x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}
with the coordinate
x_{\scriptscriptstyle[n]\setminus I}
, and the deformation coordinates
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
(the central reflection of the hypercube).

Finally for general fibres
(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(s_{0},s_{1})}
with
s_{0}s_{1}\neq 0
, we can re-scale to
s_{0}=s_{1}=1
. Setting
v_{i}=(x_{\{i\}})_{(1,1)}\in(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,1)}
, much of the argument of the previous paragraphs goes through, except for the last step where the final relation gets deformed by the parameter
s_{1}=1
. We get

(\mathscr{P}_{n})_{(1,1)}\cong\mathbb{C}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\Big/\left\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i},\sum_{i<j}v_{i}v_{j},\ldots,\prod_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}-1\right\rangle.

The latter algebra is the coordinate algebra of the free
S_{n}
-orbit of the point

(1,\eta,\eta^{2},\ldots,\eta^{n-1})\in\mathbb{A}^{n}=\Spec\mathbb{C}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]

for a primitive

. Hence this (ungraded) algebra is indeed
S_{n}
-isomorphic to the regular representation of
S_{n}
.

Finally the affine map
\pi_{n}
is finite with smooth target and fibres of constant length, hence the inclusion
\mathbb{C}[s_{0},s_{1}]\rightarrow\mathscr{P}_{n}
is indeed flat.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The degeneration of the ungraded group algebra
\mathbb{C}S_{n}
, realised as the coordinate ring of a free orbit of

in affine
n

is a particularly simple example of the Garsia–Haiman orbit harmonics method

]
.
From this point of view, what Theorem

says is that there is a further, bigraded degeneration of geometric and combinatorial interest.

3.3.
The graded character of the projective coinvariant algebra

As discussed above, the projective coinvariant algebra
P_{n}
is a bigraded version of the regular representation of the symmetric group
S_{n}
. Its bigraded character is computed by the following formula, the right hand side of this which first appeared in a closely related but slightly different context in
[
21
, Prop.3.14]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

We have

\cha_{t,q}P_{n}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\left(\sum_{T\in\SYT(\lambda)}t^{\mathrm{des}(T)}q^{\mathrm{maj}(T)}\right)s_{\lambda}.

Proof.

Consider the decomposition
T_{n}=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}(T_{n})_{r}
, where we use the first grading. Each graded piece
(T_{n})_{r}
is still graded by the second grading; use the notation
\dim_{q}
for the corresponding graded dimensions. In the alternative model of the algebra
(T_{n})
introduced in (
9
),
we have an
S_{n}
-equivariant isomorphism

(T_{n})_{r}\cong\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]_{\leq r},

where the subscript
()_{\leq r}
means terms of degree less than equal to
r
in
each
of the indicated variables, and the action of
S_{n}
is the standard one on the right hand side.

As we will be using standard results in
S_{n}
-invariant theory, in this proof we will index Young subgroups of
S_{n}
by partitions. So for a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{k}\geq 1)\vdash n
, denote

S_{\lambda}=S_{\lambda_{1}}\times\ldots\times S_{\lambda_{k}}
the standard Young subgroup of
S_{n}
. We then have

\cha_{q}(T_{n})_{r}=\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\dim_{q}((T_{n})_{r})^{S_{\lambda}}\cdot m_{\lambda}.

Further, clearly

(\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]_{\leq r})^{S_{\lambda}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{\lambda_{1}}]_{\leq r}^{S_{\lambda_{1}}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k-1}+1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]_{\leq r}^{S_{\lambda_{k}}}.

For a single set of variables, say the first one, we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dim_{q}\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{\lambda_{1}}]_{\leq r}^{S_{\lambda_{1}}}&=&\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{k}\leq r}q^{a_{1}+\ldots+a_{k}}\\
\\
&=&h_{\lambda_{1}}(1,q,\ldots,q^{r}).\end{array}

So we deduce

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dim_{q}((T_{n})_{r})^{S_{\lambda}}&=&\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{\lambda_{i}}(1,q,\ldots,q^{r})\\
&=&h_{\lambda}[[r+1]_{q}],\end{array}

and hence

\cha_{q}(T_{n})_{r}=\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}h_{\lambda}[[r+1]_{q}]\cdot m_{\lambda}.

On the other hand, using
{\bf z}
as a new set of dummy variables for plethystic substitution, using standard identities we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}h_{\lambda}[[r+1]_{q}]\cdot m_{\lambda}({\bf z})&=&h_{n}\left[[r+1]_{q}{\bf z}\right]\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}\left([r+1]_{q}\right)s_{\lambda}({\bf z}).\end{array}

We obtain

\cha_{q}(T_{n})_{r}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}([r+1]_{q})s_{\lambda}

and thus, summing over all
r
,

\cha_{t,q}T_{n}=\sum_{r\geq 0}t^{r}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}([r+1]_{q})s_{\lambda}.

For the finite-dimensional quotient
P_{n}
, we hence get

\cha_{t,q}P_{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n}(1-tq^{j})\left(\sum_{r\geq 0}t^{r}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}s_{\lambda}([r+1]_{q})s_{\lambda}\right).

Standard algebraic manipulations as in
[
21
, Proof of Prop.3.14]
conclude the proof.
∎

Note that this result fits well with the specialization picture of Theorem
3.2
, giving a bigraded refinement of the classical Lusztig–Stanley formula (

) for the (singly) graded character of the coinvariant algebra.

Corollary 3.5
.

We have

T_{n}^{S_{n}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n}].

Proof.

The elements
\widetilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n}
are
S_{n}
-invariant. They also form a regular
sequence in
T_{n}
, so they generate a free polynomial subalgebra. On the other hand,
the quotient

P_{n}=T_{n}/\left\langle\widetilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n}\right\rangle

has only a one-dimensional
S_{n}
-invariant subspace by Theorem

, the subspace of constants. Hence
\mathbb{C}[\widetilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\widetilde{e}_{n}]
contains all
S_{n}
-invariant elements in
T_{n}
.
∎

3.4.
Connection to the diagonal coinvariant algebra

In this section, a “nontrivially trivial” connection between the projective coinvariant algebra
P_{n}
and the Garsia–Haiman diagonal coinvariant algebra
D_{n}
will be discussed. Recall that the diagonal coinvariant algebra is the bigraded algebra

D_{n}=\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]/\langle\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]^{S_{n}}_{+}\rangle,

where the symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]
by permuting the two sets of variables simultaneously. The bigrading is defined by
\deg a_{i}=(1,0)
and
\deg b_{j}=(0,1)
. The algebra
D_{n}
has the structure of a bigraded
S_{n}
-module of dimension
(n+1)^{n-1}

[
14
]
.

The following statement is formulated in a somewhat tongue-in-cheek way, but the point is that (i) is easy and natural, whereas (ii) is a nontrivial statement.

Proposition 3.6
.

(i)

There is a natural
S_{n}
-equivariant bigraded algebra homomorphism

\varphi_{n}\colon P_{n}\to D_{n}.

(ii)

The homomorphism
\varphi_{n}
is trivial, factoring into the composition
P_{n}\to P_{n}/(P_{n})_{+}\cong\mathbb{C}\hookrightarrow D_{n}
.

Proof.

The homomorphism
\varphi_{n}
was essentially defined already earlier in the paper: for
I\subseteq[n]
, let

\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\colon x_{\scriptscriptstyle I}\mapsto\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}\prod_{j\not\in I}b_{j}.

The relations in
I_{n}
are satisfied in
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]
(this is just the Segre embedding again!), so we get an algebra homomorphism
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}\colon T_{n}\to\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}]
, that is bigraded under a re-scaling of the bigrading on
T_{n}
. The elements
\widetilde{e}_{i}
are mapped to
S_{n}
-invariant elements in the target, and hence
\widetilde{\varphi}_{n}
descends to an
S_{n}
-equivariant bigraded morphism
\varphi_{n}\colon P_{n}\to D_{n}
, proving (i).

The nontrivial part is (ii), the statement that this natural homomorphism is actually trivial: elements of the form
\prod_{i\in I}a_{i}\prod_{j\not\in I}b_{j}
for
I\subseteq[n]
are all zero in the diagonal coinvariant algebra
D_{n}
. I see no easy reason for this fact, observed first in computer calculations

.

We may assume for simplicity that
I=\{1,\ldots,k\}
. The main claim is that if we regard
D_{n}
as a module over
\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}]
, then the submodule spanned by
b_{k+1}\cdot\ldots\cdot b_{n}
is isomorphic to the submodule of the usual coinvariant algebra
R_{n}
on the generators
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
spanned by
a_{k+1}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{n}
. The vanishing statement then follows, because if we multiply that submodule by

The reason for the claim is that the product

Remark 3.7
.

The three finite-dimensional algebras
R_{n},P_{n},D_{n}
are connected by the schematic diagram

R_{n}\rightsquigarrow P_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D_{n},

where
\rightsquigarrow
denotes specialisation. These algebras arise from the

4.
Young subgroups and general Segre embeddings

4.1.
The bigraded coordinate ring of a general Segre embedding

Consider a composition
\alpha\vDash n
, and
let
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{P}^{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{\alpha_{k}}
with

\mathbb{P}^{\alpha_{i}}=\Proj\mathbb{C}\left[z_{0}^{(i)},\ldots,z_{\alpha_{i}}^{(i)}\right]
.
Let also

N_{\alpha}=|{\mathcal{P}}(M_{\alpha})|-1=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\alpha_{i}+1)-1

and

\mathbb{P}^{N_{\alpha}}=\Proj\mathbb{C}\left[y_{I}\colon{I\subset M_{\alpha}}\right],

the variables being indexed by the set of subsets
{\mathcal{P}}(M_{\alpha})
of the multiset
M_{\alpha}
.
The Segre embedding

s_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{P}^{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{P}^{\alpha_{k}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N_{\alpha}}

is defined by the dual map on coordinate rings given by

y_{\scriptscriptstyle I}\mapsto z_{j_{1}}^{(1)}z_{j_{2}}^{(2)}\ldots z_{j_{k}}^{(k)}\mbox{ for }I=\{1^{j_{1}}\ldots k^{j_{k}}\}\subset M_{\alpha}\mbox{ with }0\leq j_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}.

Let

T_{\alpha}=\mathbb{C}\left[y_{I}\colon I\subset M_{\alpha}\right]/\langle y_{\scriptscriptstyle I_{1}}y_{\scriptscriptstyle I_{2}}-y_{\scriptscriptstyle I_{3}}y_{\scriptscriptstyle I_{4}}\colon(I_{1},I_{2})\sim(I_{3},I_{4})\rangle

be the projective coordinate algebra of the image of the Segre embedding
s_{\alpha}
. Here the equivalence relation

(I_{1},I_{2})\sim(I_{3},I_{4})
on pairs of multisets with
I_{m}=(1^{j_{1,m}}\ldots k^{j_{k,m}})\subset M_{\alpha}
is defined as follows: for all
1\leq i\leq k
, we want
\{j_{i,1},j_{i,2}\}=\{j_{i,3},j_{i,4}\}
in the sense of multisets. Compare
[
20
, Section 4]
.

The algebra
T_{\alpha}
can be given a bigrading as follows:
the Segre coordinate
y_{\scriptscriptstyle I}
in the target
\mathbb{P}^{N_{\alpha}}
has bidegree
(1,|I|)
.
It is immediately seen that this bigrading is compatible with the defining ideal of the image of Segre embedding.

Remark 4.1
.

A perhaps more familiar way to index the Serge variables would be

\mathbb{P}^{N_{\alpha}}=\Proj\mathbb{C}\left[y_{j_{1}\ldots j_{k}}\colon{0\leq j_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}}\right],

with the Segre embedding given by

\left([z_{0}^{(1)}:\ldots:z_{\alpha_{1}}^{(1)}],\ldots,[z_{0}^{(k)}:\ldots:z_{\alpha_{k}}^{(k)}]\right)\mapsto\left[\ldots\colon\left(z_{j_{1}}^{(1)}z_{j_{2}}^{(2)}\ldots z_{j_{k}}^{(k)}\right):\ldots\right]_{0\leq j_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}}.

This is easily seen to be equivalent to the above formalism, which fits better with the spirit of this paper.

Once again, it will be useful to have an alternative model of this algebra, similar to the one given in (
9
) for
T_{n}
. Given a composition
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\vDash n
,
consider the free bigraded commutative algebra

\mathbb{C}[\zeta,\xi_{i,l}\colon 1\leq i\leq k,1\leq l\leq\alpha_{i}]
with
\deg(\zeta)=(1,0)
and
\deg(\xi_{i,l})=(0,1)
. Then once again, the bigraded algebra
T_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to a bigraded subalgebra of

\mathbb{C}[\zeta,\xi_{i,l}]
, the image of the embedding

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T_{\alpha}&\hookrightarrow&\mathbb{C}[\zeta,\xi_{i,l}]\\

\xi_{i,0}=1

4.2.
The bigraded Hilbert series

Theorem 4.3
.

For any composition
\alpha\vDash n
, the bigraded Hilbert series of the algebra
T_{\alpha}
is given by

\dim_{t,q}T_{\alpha}=\frac{A_{\alpha}(t,q)}{\prod_{j=0}^{n}(1-tq^{j})},

the numerator being the
(\des,\maj)
-generating function of words
W_{\alpha}
in the multiset
M_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Use refined Ehrhart theory. The projective
coordinate ring of the product space
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
is the polytope algebra
T_{\Delta_{\alpha}}
of the product
polytope
\Delta_{\alpha}=\Delta_{\alpha_{1}}\times\ldots\times\Delta_{\alpha_{k}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We
have

\dim_{t,q}T_{\alpha}=E^{\bf a}_{\Delta_{\alpha}}(t,q)

for the grading vector

{\bf a}=(1,\ldots,\alpha_{1},1,\ldots,\alpha_{2},\ldots,1,\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

On the other hand, we clearly have

r\Delta_{\alpha}=(r\Delta_{\alpha_{1}})\times\ldots\times(r\Delta_{\alpha_{k}})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

and so, invoking also Lemma
2.2
, we see that the
q
-count of the
number of lattice points in
r\Delta_{\alpha}
is given by

L^{\bf a}_{\Delta_{\alpha},r}(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}r+\alpha_{i}\\
\alpha_{i}\end{array}\right]_{q}.

Hence we get

\dim_{t,q}T_{\alpha}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}r+\alpha_{i}\\
\alpha_{i}\end{array}\right]_{q}\right)t^{r}.

We conclude by invoking MacMahon’s identity (
7
).
∎

Setting
q=1
, we get the classical Hilbert series of the Segre embedding, with each Segre-variable having degree
1
. We thus deduce the following formula, which was proved earlier by Morales in the unpublished
[
19
]
, and can also be deduced from
[
20
]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

For any composition
\alpha\vDash n
, the (classical) Hilbert series of the coordinate algebra
T_{\alpha}
of a general Segre embedding
s_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N_{\alpha}}
is given by the formula

\dim_{t}T_{\alpha}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{w\in W_{\alpha}}t^{\des(w_{\alpha})}}{(1-t)^{n+1}}.

The resulting coefficients in the numerator are sometimes called Simon Newcomb numbers.

Example 4.5
.

Let us continue Example
4.2

The corresponding singly graded expression is

corresponding to the codimension

4.3.
Invariants under a Young subgroup

Given

Lemma 4.9

The

Proof.

Consider the Koszul complex of the elements
\tilde{e}_{0},\ldots,\tilde{e}_{n}\in T_{n}
.
Since taking invariants under a finite group is an exact functor in characteristic zero, taking invariants in the Koszul complex

5.1.
Cohomological interpretations

Let

5.2.

Fix
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\Vdash n

(0,\ldots,0)
to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})

\ell\colon\{0,\ldots,n\}\to\mathbb{N}^{k}

(1)

\ell(0)=(0,\ldots,0)
;

(2)

\ell(n)=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})

(3)

for every
0\leq i<n

\ell(i+1)=\ell(i)+{\bf e}_{j}

\{{\bf e}_{1},\ldots,{\bf e}_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{k}

Lemma 5.3
.

W_{\alpha}
in the multiset
M_{\alpha}
and the set of increasing lattice paths

Proof.

A word
w\in W_{\alpha}
in the multiset

is an ordered list of numbers between
1
and
k
, each number
j

\alpha_{j}
times. Such a word
is precisely an instruction how to build an increasing lattice path
\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha}
: in each step in (3), choose the
j
that is the next number in the list.
∎

Using this correspondence, we can define the set of descent positions
{\rm Des}(\ell)
of a lattice path
\ell\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha}
to be the set of lattice points
\ell(i)\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
for those
0\leq i<n
that are descent positions of the corresponding word
w\in W_{\alpha}
. In other words, it is the set of lattice points
p\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
on our path such that the step to this lattice point in the path has a higher value of
j
than the step after it.

Next, note that a lattice point
p=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
0\leq i_{j}\leq\alpha_{j}
for all
j

M_{\alpha}
. Hence any such lattice

in the coordinate ring

of a general Segre embedding,

Given a word

in the multiset

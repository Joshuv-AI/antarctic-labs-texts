Design and rationale of the ATTRACTIVE trial: a randomised trial of intrAThrombus Thrombolysis versus aspiRAtion thrombeCTomy during prImary percutaneous coronary interVEntion in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction patients with high thrombus burden

ABSTRACT
IntroductionST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) with high thrombus burden is associated with a poor prognosis. Manual aspiration thrombectomy reduces coronary vessel distal embolisation, improves microvascular perfusion and reduces cardiovascular deaths, but it promotes more strokes and transient ischaemic attacks in the subgroup with high thrombus burden. Intrathrombus thrombolysis (ie, the local delivery of thrombolytics into the coronary thrombus) is a recently proposed treatment approach that theoretically reduces thrombus volume and the risk of microvascular dysfunction. However, the safety and efficacy of intrathrombus thrombolysis lack sufficient clinical evidence.Methods and analysisThe intrAThrombus Thrombolysis versus aspiRAtion thrombeCTomy during prImary percutaneous coronary interVEntion trial is a multicentre, prospective, open-label, randomised controlled trial with the blinded assessment of outcomes. A total of 2500 STEMI patients with high thrombus burden who undergo primary percutaneous coronary intervention will be randomised 1:1 to intrathrombus thrombolysis with a pierced balloon or upfront routine manual aspiration thrombectomy. The primary outcome will be the composite of cardiovascular death, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, heart failure readmission, stent thrombosis and target-vessel revascularisation up to 180 days.Ethics and disseminationThe trial was approved by Ethics Committees of China-Japan Friendship Hospital (2022-KY-013) and all other participating study centres. The results of this trial will be published in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberNCT05554588.

Introduction

Acute coronary thrombosis caused by atherosclerotic plaque rupture or erosion underlies the main pathophysiological mechanism of ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).1 STEMI patients with high thrombus burden who undergo primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PPCI) have a larger infarct size and higher incidences of distal embolisation, no-reflow and long-term mortality.2 3

Aspiration thrombectomy with a commonly used aspiration catheter is considered to effectively remove the coronary thrombus, consequently reducing the risk of distal embolism and slow/no flow.4 However, several recent large randomised clinical trials reported that routine aspiration thrombectomy during PPCI did not reduce infarct size5 or mortality rate6 7 and instead increased the rate of stroke within 30 days.7 In the subgroup of STEMI patients with high thrombus burden, aspiration thrombectomy tends to lower cardiovascular deaths but is still associated with more strokes and transient ischaemic attacks (TIAs).8 9

Another available method to reduce thrombus burden is intracoronary thrombolysis. A previous meta-analysis of 890 STEMI patients who received PPCI reported that low-dose intracoronary thrombolysis improved ST-segment resolution and reduced hospital cardiac events, with no significant difference in major bleeding.10 Furthermore, the small (N = 102) randomised DISSOLUTION trial found that intrathrombus thrombolysis with an infusion microcatheter before aspiration thrombectomy improved postprocedural myocardial perfusion and reduced the incidence of short-term and long-term major adverse cardiac events (MACEs).11 12

These studies inspired us to design a large prospective randomised trial to explore whether intrathrombus thrombolysis reduces the rate of MACE without increasing the incidence of stroke/TIAs or bleeding compared with routine aspiration thrombectomy during PPCI in STEMI patients with high thrombus burden.

Methods and analysis

Primary objective

The intrAThrombus Thrombolysis versus aspiRAtion thrombeCTomy during prImary percutaneous coronary interVEntion (ATTRACTIVE) trial intends to evaluate whether intrathrombus thrombolysis with PPCI reduces the rate of composite outcomes of cardiovascular death, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, heart failure readmission, stent thrombosis and target-vessel revascularisation up to 180 days compared with routine upfront manual aspiration thrombectomy with PPCI in STEMI patients with high thrombus burden who undergo PPCI.

Design

The ATTRACTIVE trial is a multicentre, prospective, parallel randomised open-label trial with blinded outcome assessment, comparing intrathrombus thrombolysis with a pierced balloon and routine upfront manual aspiration thrombectomy in patients who undergo PPCI for STEMI with high thrombus burden.

Hypothesis

STEMI patients with high thrombus burden have a higher incidence of coronary microvascular obstruction and a worse prognosis. Intrathrombus thrombolysis with low-dose thrombolytics could eliminate fibrin and dissolve the thrombus with high selectivity. Intrathrombus thrombolysis may effectively reduce thrombus burden and distal embolisation, thereby preventing no reflow, improving myocardial perfusion after PPCI and reducing cardiovascular death, cardiogenic shock and subsequent heart failure events. Additionally, intrathrombus thrombolysis may avoid stroke that is generally attributed to aspiration thrombectomy. Thrombolysis with low-dose thrombolytics does not significantly increase bleeding risk.

Eligibility criteria

Patients who present with STEMI, 18–80 years of age and referred for PPCI, are considered for inclusion in the trial. Intracoronary thrombus is angiographically identified and assessed according to the Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) trial-derived thrombus grade system (table 1).13 High thrombus burden is defined as a TIMI thrombus grade 3 or 4 or TIMI thrombus grade ≥3 after guidewire crossing or 1.5 mm diameter predilation of the culprit lesion if the culprit vessel is totally occluded (TIMI thrombus grade 5). Randomisation and allocation concealment

Patients who meet the eligible criteria will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to either the intrathrombus thrombolysis group or aspiration thrombectomy group. Randomisation is performed using stratified permuted block randomisation, with hospital centres as the stratified factor and a randomly permuted block of 2, 4 or 6, achieving a balanced group allocation. Opaque sequentially numbered sealed envelopes are used for allocation concealment. Investigators in each centre will be given the randomly generated treatment allocations in sealed opaque envelopes that contain allocation cards. Once a patient consents to enter the trial, the envelope will be opened, and then the patient will be allocated to the group according to the allocated treatment regimen.

Treatment

Intrathrombus thrombolysis

Self-made pierced balloon

Intrathrombus thrombolysis is performed using a self-made pierced balloon. A 1.5 mm profile balloon catheter is inflated to 2–4 atm by a balloon inflation device in vitro. The body of the balloon is then punctured with a 25 gauge needle. The pierced balloon is continually inflated by positive pressure to evacuate the air bubble and test balloon performance.

Intrathrombus thrombolysis procedure

After a guidewire crosses through the target lesion, the pierced balloon is advanced and positioned distal to the culprit lesion and thrombus. The balloon inflation part is then connected to a 10 mL syringe that contains the thrombolytic solution. The operator may gently manipulate the balloon back and forth, covering the thrombus region, while the assistant slowly injects the thrombolytics. The total thrombolysis time is less than 5 min. Coronary angiography is performed again to evaluate the TIMI flow grade and TIMI thrombus grade 10–15 min after intrathrombus thrombolysis. Predilation with larger-diameter (≥2.0 mm) balloons according to culprit vessel size is recommended if a large residual thrombus, defined as TIMI thrombus grade ≥3, especially with TIMI flow 0 or 1 still exists. The final revascularisation strategies, including percutaneous transluminal coronary angioplasty (PTCA) alone, PTCA plus stent implantation or drug-coated balloon, are decided according to local investigators’ discretion.

Manual aspiration thrombectomy

After a guidewire crosses the lesion site, the thrombectomy catheter is positioned proximal to the thrombus, at which point aspiration begins before crossing the lesion. The thrombectomy catheter should be passed through the lesion repeatedly so that more than two syringes are filled with aspirated blood and thrombus material (≥40 mL total). The thrombectomy catheter should be removed to check for thrombus obstruction whenever aspiration stops suddenly. Thrombectomy may be repeated as necessary if the thrombus remains after thrombectomy. If the thrombectomy catheter does not cross the lesion, then a small-diameter (≤1.5 mm) balloon catheter can be used to predilate the lesion. After aspiration thrombectomy, the PCI procedure may be performed according to local practice.

Cross-over and perioperative medications

Cross-over from one group to the other is discouraged but is allowed at the operator’s discretion. Bail-out aspiration thrombectomy is permitted if a large residual thrombus exists, especially with TIMI flow 0 or 1 after intrathrombus thrombolysis and larger-diameter balloon predilation. Similarly, intrathrombus thrombolysis can be considered in case the large thrombus persists after multiple aspiration thrombectomy.

Half of a standard loading dosage of a glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor antagonist (90 µg/kg etibatide or 12.5 µg/kg tirofiban) is administered intravenously to all eligible patients. All patients receive standard-of-care therapy according to international guidelines,15 namely initial oral treatment with 300 mg aspirin, followed by 75–100 mg aspirin daily, initial treatment with 180 mg ticagrelor followed by 90 mg ticagrelor two times per day for ≥12 months or initial treatment with 600 mg clopidogrel followed by 75 mg clopidogrel daily for ≥12 months and then intravascular infusions of 70–100 IU/kg of unfractionated heparin prior to PCI. No routine monitoring of activated clotting time is required. For each additional hour of operation time, an intravenous bolus of 1000 IU heparin will be administered.

Study outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is major adverse cardiovascular events, consisting of cardiovascular death, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, heart failure readmission, stent thrombosis and target-vessel revascularisation within 180 days of randomisation.

Secondary outcomes

The key secondary outcome is net benefit outcome, including cardiovascular death, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, heart failure readmission, stent thrombosis, target-vessel revascularisation, ischaemic stroke/TIA and major bleeding events (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (BARC) 2, 3 and 5) within 180 days of randomisation. Other secondary outcomes include (1) primary outcome at 30 days and 1 year, (2) individual components of primary outcome up to 1 year, (3) all-cause mortality up to 1 year, (4) myocardial blush grade 0 or 1 after intervention therapy, (5) percentage of TIMI flow grade 3 after intervention therapy, (6) >70% resolution of ST-segment 30–60 min after intervention therapy, (7) incidence of side branch occlusion and (8) incidence of distal embolism.

Safety outcomes

The safety outcomes include (1) the incidence of ischaemic stroke or TIA within 30 days and (2) the incidence of major bleeding events (BARC 2, 3 and 5) within 30 days.

Follow-up and central event adjudication

Follow-up will be performed at 30 days, 180 days and 1 year after randomisation. Follow-up data will be obtained from hospital records or through telephone interviews by a specified interview group who is blinded to treatment groups. All components of primary, secondary and safety outcomes will be adjudicated by a committee of clinicians who are blinded to treatment allocation.

Statistical analysis

A two-independent-sample test is used to determine whether the proportion of primary endpoints in the intrathrombus thrombolysis group differs from the aspiration thrombectomy group. The incidence of 180-day rates of MACE in the thrombus aspiration group is estimated to be 12% according to previous studies.7 8 The intrathrombus thrombolysis group is expected to have a relative risk reduction of 30%. To test the hypothesised 30% relative risk reduction with 80% power at a two-sided p < 0.05 significance level, a minimum sample size of 1106 patients per group is estimated. The rate of loss to follow-up and early withdrawal is assumed to be 10%. The estimated sample size is 2500 patients (1250 patients per group.).

The results will be analysed according to the intention-to-treat principle, in which patients who are randomised to a certain group will be followed and assessed regardless of the actual treatment. Efficacy evaluation will be conducted in both the full analysis set and per-protocol set, with the analysis in the full analysis set as the primary analysis. The full analysis set will include all patients who are eligible for the study and complete randomisation. The per-protocol set will include only patients who follow the study treatment protocols and have complete follow-up data. The difference in primary outcome between groups will be assessed with the log-rank test, with the centre information (site effect) as a stratification factor. The same test will be used to compare the key secondary and safety outcomes between groups. Kaplan-Meier curves will be generated to show the incidence of outcomes over time. The Cox proportional risk model will be used to calculate the HR and its 95% CI by adjusting for the centre effect. For other secondary outcomes and baseline characteristics, the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test will be used for categorical variables, and the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test will be used for quantitative variables. A two-tailed value of p < 0.05 is considered statistically significant.

Subgroup analysis

The primary and secondary endpoints will be analysed in prespecified subgroups, including one primary subgroup analysis according to symptom onset (<6 hours, 6–12 hours and >12 hours) and an additional exploratory subgroup analysis according to sex (male, female), age (<65 years, ≥65 years), initial TIMI flow (0–1, 2–3), TIMI thrombus grade (3, 4 and 5), tertiles of centre PPCI volume and myocardial infarction type (anterior, non-anterior).

Data management

An appropriate Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) database will be used for data capture and workflow management. Clinical trial staff will collect and enter data directly into REDCap, with logic checks to ensure data quality. Coronary angiography images will be interpreted uniformly by the team of interventional physicians in the main centre and uploaded to REDCap. The China-Japan Friendship Hospital performs routine, scheduled maintenance and quality control checks on the REDCap system.

Data monitoring and safety

All trial participants will be monitored by both the research staff and clinical teams based on standard hospital care and protocols. The research team will monitor patients for adverse events. An independent data monitoring committee that consists of an independent cardiologist, interventional cardiologist and biostatistician has been appointed to safeguard the interests of trial participants. The data monitoring committee will conduct annual reviews of recruitment progress, retention, serious adverse events and general study progress.

Access to data

Research data may be shared after deidentification on reasonable request. Proposals to access the data must be reviewed and approved by the ATTRACTIVE trial Steering Committee. Proposals should be directed to the Chair of the ATTRACTIVE Steering Committee, JZ via email (mdjingangzheng@yeah.net).

Current status

The ATTRACTIVE trial started enroling patients on 13 September 2022, and 190 patients were enroled as of 31 May 2023. The rate of enrollment was severely reduced last year because of the COVID-19 pandemic. To date, 25 medical centres in China are recruiting patients. Recruitment is expected to be completed by December 2025.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

The trial is being conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committees of China-Japan Friendship Hospital (2022-KY-013) and all other participating study centres. Any change to the protocol or informed consent form that affects the study design is considered an amendment and will be written and filed as an amendment to this protocol and/or informed consent form. All such amendments will be submitted to the Ethics Committees of China-Japan Friendship Hospital for approval before becoming effective.

The results of this trial will be published in peer-reviewed journals and reported at local and international conferences. No participants will be identified in the dissemination of study results, and data that are collected will be treated confidentially. The trial is registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov (NCT05554588).

Discussion

In STEMI patients with high thrombus burden who are treated with stent implantation or PTCA, combining other treatment strategies to reduce thrombus burden is often necessary. However, there is currently no high-level recommendation for treating STEMI patients with high thrombus burden. Routine thrombus aspiration is widely used in clinical practice. Although a meta-analysis of the Thrombus Aspiration during Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in Acute Myocardial Infarction Study (TAPAS), Thrombus Aspiration in ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction in Scandinavia (TASTE), Trial of Routine Aspiration Thrombectomy with PCI versus PCI Alone in Patients with STEMI (TOTAL)

found that routine thrombus aspiration during STEMI PPCI did not improve clinical outcomes, trends were seen in the high thrombus burden subgroup towards a decrease in cardiovascular death and an increase in strokes and TIAs.9

Intrathrombus thrombolysis was proven to reduce local thrombus burden, which might reduce cardiovascular death, and also avoid strokes and TIAs. To date, there have been few high-quality studies of intrathrombus thrombolysis in STEMI patients with high thrombus burden. In a retrospective study that evaluated the effect of intrathrombus thrombolysis in STEMI patients who presented with high thrombus burden and failed aspiration after thrombolysis, TIMI flow grade improved from 0/1 at baseline (93%) to ≥2 in most patients (97%).16 Intrathrombus thrombolysis can reduce local thrombus burden and improve microcirculation. In a randomised controlled trial of 42 STEMI patients with high thrombus burden, prourokinase was injected into the thrombus in the experimental group, and thrombus aspiration was performed in the control group, followed by PCI in both groups. Compared with the thrombus aspiration group, the index of microcirculatory resistance in the intrathrombus thrombolysis group was significantly lower (29.33 vs 40.47, p < 0.001).17

The ATTRACTIVE trial is a multicentre, randomised controlled trial. That will be appropriately powered to assess the primary outcome. The primary outcome is composite outcomes of cardiovascular death, recurrent myocardial infarction, cardiogenic shock, heart failure rehospitalisation, stent thrombosis and target vessel revascularisation. A trial with a primary outcome of all-cause mortality would require the inclusion of >30 000 patients, which was thought to be infeasible.

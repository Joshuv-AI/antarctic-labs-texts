DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND HUMAN SERVICES

Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration


Agency Information Collection Activities Comment Request

    In compliance with Section 3506(c)(2)(A) of the Paperwork Reduction 
Act of 1995 concerning opportunity for public comment on proposed 
collections of information, the Substance Abuse and Mental Health 
Services Administration (SAMHSA) will publish periodic summaries of 
proposed projects. To request more information on the proposed projects 
or to obtain a copy of the information collection plans, contact the 
SAMHSA Clearance Office at [email protected].
    Comments are invited on: (a) whether the proposed collections of 
information are necessary for the proper performance of the functions 
of the agency, including whether the information shall have practical 
utility; (b) the accuracy of the agency's estimate of the burden of the 
proposed collection of information; (c) ways to enhance the quality, 
utility, and clarity of the information to be collected; and (d) ways 
to minimize the burden of the collection of information on respondents, 
including through the use of automated collection techniques or other 
forms of information technology.

Proposed Project: National Substance Use and Mental Health Services 
Survey (N-SUMHSS) (OMB No. 0930-0386)--Revision of a Currently Approved 
Collection

    Under Section 505 of the Public Health Service Act (42 U.S.C. 
290aa-4), SAMHSA is required to conduct annual collection of data on 
substance use and mental health. Selected information collected from 
the N-SUMHSS is also published on SAMHSA's FindTreatment.gov for 
persons seeking treatment for mental and substance use disorders in the 
United States. FindTreatment.gov is authorized by the 21st Century 
Cures Act (Pub. L. 114-255, Section 9006; 42 U.S.C. 290bb-36d).
    In 2021, SAMHSA combined the National Survey of Substance Abuse 
Treatment Services (N-SSATS) and the National Mental Health Services 
Survey (N-MHSS) into the N-SUMHSS to reduce the burden on facilities 
offering both substance use and mental health services, optimize 
government resources to collect data, and enhance the quality of data 
collected on the treatment facilities.
    The N-SUMHSS is the most comprehensive national source of data on 
substance use and mental health treatment facilities. On an annual 
basis, the N-SUMHSS collects information on the facility location, 
characteristics, and utilization of substance use and mental health 
treatment services. The survey also collects client counts on 
individuals receiving services at these facilities. There is an 
increasing need to collect and maintain data on current and accurate 
numbers of clients in treatment at the local level for communities to 
assess capacity and estimate resource requirements. This information on 
substance use and mental health services has assisted communities to 
better respond to life changing events, (i.e., hurricane) and plan for 
service demands in the event of a natural disaster (i.e., earthquakes).
    SAMHSA also maintains the Inventory of Substance Use and Mental 
Health Treatment Facilities (I-TF) (previously known as the Inventory 
of Behavioral Health Services [I-BHS]). The I-TF is a master list of 
all known substance use and mental health treatment facilities in the 
United States, and serves as the frame for the N-SUMHSS. SAMHSA is 
requesting OMB approval of revisions to the N-SUMHSS and I-TF related 
data collections, to include changes to the following instruments:

N-SUMHSS Questionnaire (Attachment A)

    For this submission, SAMHSA proposes two categories of changes to 
the N-SUMHSS questionnaire.
    First, SAMHSA is revising the questionnaire's content on crisis 
services and faith-based organizations. As part of these revisions, 
three existing crisis-related questions (B12-B14) will be removed and 
replaced with a new series of questions designed to collect more 
detailed information about mobile crisis, community outreach, 
behavioral health emergency, and crisis stabilization services. All 
facilities will be asked two new screening questions to determine 
whether they: (1) offer mobile crisis or community outreach services, 
and (2) offer behavioral health emergency or crisis stabilization 
services.
    Depending on their responses to these questions, some facilities 
may receive additional follow-up questions to collect information about 
service models offered, services provided within those models, and the 
number of individuals who received or were actively engaged in those 
services during a specified reporting period.
    In addition, all facilities will continue to be asked the existing 
question about whether they are affiliated with a religious or faith-
based organization. SAMHSA also proposes a new question for all 
facilities regarding whether they identify as a faith-based 
organization.
    Although these changes add new questions and data elements, they 
also replace existing crisis services questions and are intended to 
provide more actionable information about the availability and 
characteristics of crisis and faith-based services for program 
planning, policy development, and public reporting. Additional 
respondent burden will be limited primarily to facilities that meet the 
criteria for the applicable follow-up questions.
    Second, this submission includes a limited set of targeted 
clarifications to existing N-SUMHSS questionnaire items. These 
revisions have been informed by respondent questions, interviewer 
debriefings, and routine instrument review. They are intended to 
improve question clarity and reporting accuracy by addressing ambiguous 
terminology and revising language that may not align with current 
SAMHSA terminology or clinical practice. These changes do not alter the 
underlying constructs being measured, the scope of information 
collected, or respondent burden. Rather, they are designed to address 
specific known areas of respondent confusion and improve consistency 
across related questionnaire items.
    The clarifications address the following:
    1. Ambiguity in question wording or response options that led to 
inconsistent interpretation,
    2. Uncertainty about whether selected items apply to a facility,
    3. Inconsistent response categories across similar items, and
    4. Minor instructional language needed to reinforce existing 
reporting guidance.
    These updates do not modify survey navigation, layout, question 
order, or overall wording style

N-SUMHSS Between Cycle Questionnaire (Attachment B)

    While the Mini N-SUMHSS is derived from the N-SUMHSS, it includes 
only a subset of questions. Consequently, changes to the N-SUMHSS apply 
to the Mini N-SUMHSS only where the affected questions are included in 
the Mini.

N-SUMHSS VA Supplement (Attachment C and D)

    SAMHSA is not proposing any new questions for the N-SUMHSS VA 
Supplement. Proposed updates are limited to minor wording revisions to 
existing VA Supplement items to ensure consistency with terminology 
used elsewhere in the N-SUMHSS. These changes do not alter the content, 
intent, or respondent burden of the VA Supplement.

I-TF Online State Add/Update Form (Attachment F)

    The I-TF Online State Add/Update Form is used by the state user 
when requesting to modify facility information in I-TF. A few fields 
were added to the form, and one was deleted. These fields are 
prepopulated in the form based on the N-SUMHSS responses. These changes 
do not alter the content, intent, or respondent burden since the 
responses are from the N-SUMHSS.

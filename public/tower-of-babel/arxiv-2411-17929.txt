NON-UNIQUENESS OF OBERBECK-BOUSSINESQ SYSTEM WITH GRAVITATIONAL FIELD
Xiaoran Liu

Abstract
. We consider the Oberbeck-Boussinesq system with gravitational force on the whole space. We prove the non-uniqueness of the system applying the unstable profile of Navier-Stokes equation.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35L71 (primary),35B40,37K40

1.
introduction

We consider the Oberbeck-Boussinesq system on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with gravitational field

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\Delta u+\theta\nabla G+f\\
\nabla\cdot u=0\\
\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta=\Delta\theta+h,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u(x,t)
is the velocity field,
\theta(x,t)
is the temperature,
p(x,t)
is the pressure,
\nabla G=\frac{x}{|x|^{3}}
is the gravitational force acting on the fluid and
f
and
h
are external force and heat source, respectively. The Oberbeck-Boussinesq equation is an approximation equation used to describe the motion of the incompressible fluid system driven by the thermal convection. Such a sysem was proposed by Oberbeck
[
25
]
and Boussiness
[
6
]
, and has been studied as a successful model for hydrodynamic stability. (1.1) can be derived as a singular low Mach number limit of the full Navier-Stokes-Fourier system, see
[
4
,
15
]
. Saltzman
[
5
]
considered this problem using Fourier representation. Later, Lorenz
[
24
]
evaluated the numerical solution and discovered the first strange attractor. Besides, there are also many works on generalized Oberbeck-Boussinesq system. For example, Rajagopal, Saccomandi and Vergori generalized this system for compressible fluid with pressure- and temperature-dependent material properties
[
26
]
. Also Grandi and Passerini presented a new deriviation for gases
[
17
]
.

The well-posedness of Oberbeck-Boussinesq system on a bounded domain has been widely studied. In
[
21
]
Komo proved the existence of Serrin type strong solution for small initial data. Also Abbatiello, Bul
\rm\acute{a}\check{c}
ek and Lear studied the weak solution of generalized Navier-Stokes-Fourier system
[
1
]
. Moreover, Abbatiello and Feireisl proved the existence of weak solution with no-slip boundary condition
[
2
]
. In
[
14
]
, well-posedness of Oberbeck-Boussinesq with nonlinear diffusion was proved.

In some cases,
\nabla G
is taken to be
(0,0,1)
. Brandolese and Schonbe
[
8
]
proved the existence of weak solution of such type equation, see also
[
13
]
. Global well-posedness was proved for small initial data, see
[
16
,
20
]
. For results on more general versions of the Oberbeck-Boussinesq system, one could refer to
[
12
,
11
]
, to name a few. For an overview of the Oberbeck-Boussinesq system, see
[
33
]
.

On the other hand, for Oberbeck-Boussinesq on unbounded domains, we would also consider the case
G=\frac{1}{|x|}
if the size of the object acting on fluid is negligible. In
[
7
]
, Brandolese and Karch proved the existence and uniqueness of solution for homogeneous initial data, as an extension of Jia and
\rm\check{S}
ver
\rm\acute{a}
k’s work on Navier-Stokes
[
18
]
.

Despite of so many uniqueness results as mentioned, the converse is hardly studied. Since our non-uniqueness result is related to that of Navier-Stokes, we’d like to introduce some developments on non-uniqueness for hydrodynamic system.

Non-uniqueness of weak solutions is very important in turbulence theory. In
[
27
]
Scheffer firstly gave a remarkable example of non-unique solution to 2 dimensional Euler equation, where the velocity lies in
L^{2}
in space and time. Shnirelman then constructed non-unique solution in a different way
[
28
,
29
]
. In 09’ De Lellis and Szekelyzidi proved the non-uniqueness result for
v\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}
for arbitrary dimension. More recently, Vishik
[
30
,
31
]
considered the 2 dimensional Euler equation in vorticity form and proved the non-uniqueness for initial vorticity in
L^{1}\cap L^{p}
, which is sharp due to the classical work of Yudovich
[
32
]
. Based on Vishik’s work, Albritton, Bru
\rm\acute{e}
and Colombo proved the non-uniqueness result for 3 dimensional Navier-Stokes
[
3
]
. It was also proved by Buckmaster amd Vicol, using the method of convex integration
[
10
]
. Furthermore, Bru
\rm\acute{e}
, Colombo and Kumar used a new convex integral scheme to prove the non-uniqueness of 2 dimensional Euler but without forcing term
[
9
]
.

Our main theorem is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists
t_{0}>0,f,g\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}((0,t_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that (1.1) has infinitely many distributional solutions
(u,\theta)\in L_{t}^{2}L_{x}^{2}((0,t_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{3})
with initial data
(u_{0},\theta_{0})=(0,0)
.

The theorem tells that the turbulence could also cause the non-uniqueness of temperature distribution. One of the difficulties is that in the equation for temperature, the heat semigroup is not regular enough to control the convection term brought by the unstable profile
\bar{u}
. So our approach is to add this term into the generator and estimate the new semigroup
e^{\tau L}
. Due to similar reasons the temperature profile we choose is not steady, but smaller in time.

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces notations and known results about instability property of Navier-Stokes equation, which play an important role in our proof. In Section 3 we prove the main theorem by a contraction mapping argument.

2.
notations and known results

It worth mentioning that (1.1) possesses the scaling property: if
(u,p,\theta,f,g)(x,t)
is a solution, and set

\displaystyle u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda u(\lambda^{2}x,\lambda t),\theta_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda\theta(\lambda^{2}x,\lambda t),

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{3}f(\lambda^{2}x,\lambda t),h_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{3}h(\lambda^{2}x,\lambda t),p_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{2}p(\lambda^{2}x,\lambda t),

then
(u_{\lambda},p_{\lambda},\theta_{\lambda},f_{\lambda},h_{\lambda})(x,t)
is also a solution of (1.1). Note that the Navier-Stokes equations also have such property. We introduce the self-similar coordinates

\xi=\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\tau=\log t.

In the self similar coordinates we set

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}U(\xi,\tau),\theta(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\Theta(\xi,\tau),

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(x,t)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}F(\xi,\tau),h(x,t)=\frac{1}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}H(\xi,\tau),p(x,t)=\frac{1}{t}P(\xi,\tau),

then (1.1) becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}U-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)U-\Delta U+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla P=\Theta\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|}+F

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot U=0

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\Theta-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)\Theta-\Delta\Theta+U\cdot\nabla\Theta=H

We say a solution is a self-similar solution, if it is stationary in self-similar coordinates. The study of self-similar solutions plays an important role in many problems. Firstly in
[
19
,
18
]
, Jia,
\rm\check{S}
ver
\rm\acute{a}
k and Guillod found numerically the unstable profile. Moreover, in
[
3
]
Albritton, Bru
\rm\acute{e}
and Colombo proved this rigorously, which will be exploited in this paper. Later it was extended to related problems, like fractional Navier-Stokes
[
22
]
and MHD
[
23
]
. In
[
7
]
, Brandoless and Karch studied the well-posedness of Oberbeck-Boussinesq for homogeneous initial data.

In the following we always use capital letters to represent the corresponding function in self-similar coordinates according to (2.1). For example,
u_{p}(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}U(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\log t)=e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}U(\xi,\tau)
.

Consider the Navier-Stokes equation on
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\Delta u+f,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0.\end{cases}

In self-similar coordinates after applying the Leray projector, the first equation reads

\partial_{\tau}U-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)U-\Delta U+U\cdot\nabla U+\nabla P=F.

Therefore, if we linearize this equation at some profile
\bar{U}
, we get the linearized equation

\partial_{\tau}U-L_{ss}U=0

where the linearized operator is

L_{ss}U=\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)U+\Delta U+\mathbb{P}(U\cdot\nabla\bar{U}+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla U).

The instability of Navier-Stokes was studied by Albritton, Bru
\rm\acute{e}
, and Colombo in
[
3
]
. They proved the existence of unstable profile
\bar{U}
such that the linearized operator has an unstable eigenvalue:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
, Theorem 1.3)
.

There exists a divergence-free vector field
\bar{U}\in C_{c}^{\infty}
such that the linearized operator
L_{ss}:D(L_{ss})\subset L_{\sigma}^{2}\to L_{\sigma}^{2}
has a maximally unstable eigenvalue
\lambda
with smooth eigenfunction
\rho\in H^{k}\forall k
, that is

L_{ss}\rho=\lambda\rho

and

a=\Re\lambda=\sup_{z\in\sigma(L_{ss})}\Re z>0.

Here
L_{\sigma}^{2}={u\in L^{2}:\nabla\cdot u=0}
.

Remark 2.2
.

It can be tracked from
[
3
]
that by mulitplying the unstable profile by a large number,
a
can be made sufficiently large. Without loss of generiosity we set
a>1
.

Moreover, they also proved that the semigroup generated by
L_{ss}
enjoy the parabolic regularity estimate.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 4.4)
.

For all
\sigma_{2}\geq\sigma_{1}\geq 0
, it holds

\|e^{\tau L_{ss}}U\|_{H^{\sigma_{2}}}\leq\frac{C(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\delta)}{\tau^{(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})/2}}e^{(a+\delta)\tau}\|U\|_{H^{\sigma_{1}}}

for any
U\in H^{\sigma_{1}}\cap L_{\sigma}^{2}
.

3.
proof of the main result

3.1.
Strategy of the proof

We firstly linearize (2.2) at a suitable profile, then decompose the solution as the combination of the profile, linear part and pertubation part. Letting
\bar{U}(\xi)
be as in Lemma 2.1 and
\bar{\Theta}(\xi,\tau)
a smooth compactly supported function of the form
e^{b\tau}\Theta(\xi)
for some
b>0
, we solve
F
and
H
by plugging in
\bar{U}
and
\bar{\Theta}
into (1.1), that is

\displaystyle F=

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\bar{U}-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)\bar{U}-\Delta\bar{U}+\mathbb{P}(\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\bar{U}-\bar{\Theta}\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|})

\displaystyle H=

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\bar{\Theta}-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)\bar{\Theta}-\Delta\bar{\Theta}+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta}.

By direct calculation,
\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|h(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{3}{4}}
, so
f,h\in L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}
.

Nest, suppose
U=\bar{U}+U_{l}+U_{p},\Theta=\bar{\Theta}+\Theta_{l}+\Theta_{p}
is another solution with the same external force and heat source, where the lower index
l
stands for linear and
p
stands for pertubation. Then
(U_{l},\Theta_{l})
satisfies the linearized equation, which is

\begin{cases}\partial\tau U_{l}-L_{ss}U_{l}=0\\
\partial_{\tau}\Theta_{l}+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{l}-\Delta\Theta_{l}=0.\end{cases}

For simplicity, we apply Lemma 2.1 and pick

U_{l}=\Re(e^{\lambda\tau}\rho),\Theta_{l}=0.

(3.1)

Then it remains to solve the equation satisfied by
(U_{p},\Theta_{p})
, that is

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}U_{p}-L_{ss}U_{p}+\mathbb{P}(U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{l}+U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{p}+U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{l}+U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{p}-\Theta_{p}\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|})=0\\
\nabla\cdot U_{p}=0\\
\partial_{\tau}\Theta_{p}-\Delta\Theta_{p}-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)\Theta_{p}+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{p}+U_{l}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta}+U_{l}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{p}+U_{p}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta}+U_{p}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{p}=0.\end{cases}

Using Duhamel formula, the first equation becomes

U_{p}(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(-U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{l}-U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{p}-U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{l}-U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{p}+\Theta_{p}\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}.

(3.2)

For the third one, denote by
L=\Delta+\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla
. Then
L
generates a strongly continuous semigroup, so we rewrite the equation as

\Theta_{p}(\tau)=\int_{{}_{\infty}}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L}(-U_{l}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta}-U_{l}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{p}-U_{p}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta}-U_{p}\cdot\nabla\Theta_{p})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}

(3.3)

In the following section we will prove the existence of solution to the equation above.

3.2.
Estimate of
e^{\tau L}

In this section we prove that the semigroup generated by
L
possesses the same regularity property as the heat semigroup.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
k\geq m\geq 0,\forall\Theta_{0}\in L^{2},\tau>0
, we have

\|e^{\tau L}\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim_{k,m}\max\{\tau^{-\frac{k-m}{2}},1\}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{m}}

Proof.

We first prove the case when
k,m
are integers. Then the general cases follow from interpolation.

Let
\Theta(\tau)=e^{\tau L}\Theta_{0}
. Then
\Theta(\tau)
satisfies the following equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}\Theta-\Delta\Theta-\frac{1}{2}(1+\xi\cdot\nabla)\Theta=-\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Theta\\
\Theta(0)=\Theta_{0}.\end{cases}

Then in natural coordinates
\theta(t)
satisfies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta-\Delta\theta=-\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\theta\\
\theta(1)=\theta_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

Multiply by
\theta
and integrate we find that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\theta^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\theta|^{2}dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\theta\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\theta dx=0

which means

\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}=t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}=t^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Let
\phi_{k}(t)=(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta(t)|^{2}dx
, then

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{k}(t)=

\displaystyle k(t-1)^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta|^{2}dx+(-1)^{k}(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta^{k}\theta\cdot\partial_{t}\theta dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(t-1)^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta|^{2}dx+(-1)^{k}(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta^{k}\theta(\Delta\theta-\nabla\cdot(\bar{u}\theta))dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k(t-1)^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta|^{2}dx-(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k+1}\theta|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k+1}\theta||D^{k}(\bar{u}\theta)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k(t-1)^{k-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta|^{2}dx+C(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}(\bar{u}\theta)|^{2}dx.

Since
\|D^{k}\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\sim t^{\frac{k+1}{2}}
, by Leibniz rule we get

\dot{\phi}(t)\leq k(t-1)^{k-1}\|\theta\|_{H^{k}}^{2}~C(t-1)^{k}t^{-1}\|\theta\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\lesssim(t-1)^{k-1}\|\theta\|_{H^{k}}^{2}.

Therefore

(t-1)^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta(t)|^{2}dx=\phi(t)\lesssim(t-1)^{k-1}\int_{1}^{t}\|\theta(s)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}ds.

(3.5)

Now we come to estimate the time integral of
H^{k}
norm. Differentiate (3.4) for
k-1
times we obtain that

\partial_{t}D^{k-1}\theta-\Delta D^{k-1}\theta=-D^{k-1}(\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\theta).

Multiply by
D^{k-1}\theta
and integrate by parts, we obtain that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k-1}\theta|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{k}\theta D^{k-1}(\bar{u}\theta)dx.

Since the highest order term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D^{k-1}\theta\bar{u}\cdot\nabla D^{k-1}\theta dx
vanishes, by Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and interpolation we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k-1}\theta|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta|^{2}dx\leq\|\theta\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}.

Integrate in time we see that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta(t)|^{2}dx+\int_{1}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta(s)|^{2}dxds\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k-1}\theta(1)|^{2}dx+\int_{1}^{t}\|\theta(s)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}ds.

Therefore by induction we can show that

\int_{1}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta(s)|^{2}dxds\lesssim\|\theta\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}.

Combining with (3.5), we proved that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|D^{k}\theta(t)^{2}dx\lesssim(t-1)^{-1}\|\theta(1)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}.

Then it follows that

\|D^{k}\Theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}=\|D^{k}\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}t^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}\lesssim(t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{k-1}}\leq\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{(\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4})\tau}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{k-1}}.

For
0<\tau<k
, we see that
\|D^{k}\Theta(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\tau^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{k-1}}
. For
\tau>k
, using the short time estimate we have

\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\|\Theta(\tau-1)\|_{H^{k-1}}\lesssim\dots\lesssim\|\Theta(\tau-k)\|\leq\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore we proved the lemma for
k-m=1
. The general cases follows from induction and interpolation.
∎

3.3.
estimates

Let

\displaystyle X=

\displaystyle\{U\in C_{t}H^{N}((-\infty,\tau_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:\sup_{\tau<\tau_{0}}e^{-\beta\tau}\|U(\tau)\|_{H^{N}}<\infty\},

\displaystyle Y=

\displaystyle\{\Theta\in C_{t}H^{N+1}((-\infty,\tau_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{3}:\sup_{\tau<\tau_{0}}e^{-\gamma\tau}\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{H^{N+1}}<\infty\},

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|U\|_{X}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau<\tau_{0}}e^{-\beta\tau}\|U(\tau)\|_{H^{N}},

\displaystyle\|\Theta\|_{Y}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau<\tau_{0}}e^{-\gamma\tau}\|\Theta(\tau)\|_{H^{N+1}}.

Here
\beta,\gamma>0
are to be determined,
\frac{3}{2}<N<2
.

We start by estimating each term on the right hand side of (3.2). Using (3.1) and Lemma 2.3, with
H^{N}
being an algebra, we get

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{l})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}\leq

\displaystyle e^{2a\tau^{\prime}}e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\|\rho\nabla\rho\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{2a\tau^{\prime}}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(a+\delta)}

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{p})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(U_{l}U_{p})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{a\tau^{\prime}}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})(a+\delta)}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|U_{p}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{a\tau^{\prime}}e^{\beta\tau^{\prime}}e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|U_{p}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{l})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\|U_{p}\nabla U_{l}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{a\tau^{\prime}}e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\|U_{p}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{a\tau^{\prime}}e^{\beta\tau^{\prime}}e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\|U_{p}\|_{X}

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{p})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla(U_{p}U_{p})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|U_{p}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{2\beta\tau^{\prime}}e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|U_{p}\|_{X}^{2}

To estimate the gravity term, we exploit that the Leray projector kills gradient fields. Therefore we have
\mathbb{P}(\Theta\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|})=-\mathbb{P}(\frac{1}{|\xi|}\nabla\Theta)
. Then using Hardy inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(\Theta\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|\frac{1}{|\xi|}\nabla\Theta(\tau^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|\Theta_{p}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|\Theta_{p}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\gamma\tau^{\prime}}e^{(a+\delta)(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{N}{2}}\|\Theta_{p}\|_{Y}

Therefore, integrate the five inequalities above from
-\infty
to
\tau
we see that

\|\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{l})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim e^{2a\tau}

(3.6)

\|\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{l}\cdot\nabla U_{p})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim e^{(a+\beta)\tau}\|U_{p}\|_{X}

(3.7)

\|\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{l})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim e^{(a+\beta)\tau}\|U_{p}\|_{X}

(3.8)

\|\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U_{p}\cdot\nabla U_{p})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim e^{2\beta\tau}\|U_{p}\|_{X}^{2}

(3.9)

\|\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L_{ss}}\mathbb{P}(U\Theta_{p}\nabla\frac{1}{|\xi|})(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim e^{\gamma\tau}\|\Theta_{p}\|_{Y}

(3.10)

In this process we must set

a>\delta,\beta>\delta,\gamma>a+\delta

(3.11)

to make the integrals converge.

Now we estimate (3.3). Using Lemma 3.1,

\displaystyle\|e^{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})L}(U_{l}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta})(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N+1}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\max\{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\}\|U_{l}\cdot\nabla\bar{\Theta}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\max\{(\tau-\tau^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{2}},1\}\|U_{l}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N}}\|\bar{\Theta}(\tau^{\prime})\|_{H^{N+1}}
